## Statement

For every even integer \(n\ge4\), put \(d=n^3\), \(\varepsilon=n^{-4}\), and \(K=n^6\). Define on all of \(\mathbb R\) the finite convex piecewise-affine function

\[
q_K(t)=\max_{k\in\{-K,\ldots,K-1\}}
\bigl((2k+1)t-k(k+1)\bigr),
\qquad
C_n=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\frac{\varepsilon^2}{d}\sum_{i=1}^n q_K(x_i)
+2\|\mathbf y\|_\infty\le1\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

The interpolation \(q_K\) restricted to \([-K,K]\) is exactly the function \(\phi_R\) in the earlier quadratic-simplex fractional/kernel example after substituting \(R=K\) in that function only. Here \(K\) is an interpolation cutoff, and the roof coefficient is \(\varepsilon^2/d=n^{-11}\), not \(K^{-2}\). Thus this is a different body from that example's simplex construction with \(d=R=n^2\); no simplex volume prefactor or simplex-kernel conclusion is imported.

Retain the locked \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C_n\cap\Lambda\), and \(h_S\). Use the positive-support convention of accepted facts `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` and `488f0a583886b5fc`: \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in C_n\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(D=\{\mathbf z:w_{\mathbf z}>0\}\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_d(S)\). This \(D\) means positive support, the role denoted by \(D_+\) for the different body in that earlier example. The archived source's \(D_n,W_n,S_n\) mean the present \(D,W,S\).

The set \(C_n\) is a compact, full-dimensional rational polytope. Its complete integer fiber table, including empty and zero-volume fibers, is

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathbf z}
&=\begin{cases}
[-a_{\mathbf z}/2,a_{\mathbf z}/2]^d,
&\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\le d,
\quad a_{\mathbf z}=1-\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2/d,\\
\varnothing,&\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2>d,
\end{cases}\\
w_{\mathbf z}
&=\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2}{d}\right)_+^d
\quad(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n),
\qquad
D=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2<d\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(t_+=\max\{t,0\}\). On \(D\), \(a_{\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d}>0\) is exactly the canonical root volume as well as the cube side length. Each equality fiber in (2) is the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\), has zero \(d\)-volume, and its sole mixed-integer point has depth zero. In particular \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\), \(0<W<\infty\), and

\[
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf0))=\frac W2.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(\mathbf J\in D\), allow every finite nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) and every positive vector \((\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in E}\) with \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). With natural logarithms, define the original root and logarithmic costs by

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{E,\lambda}^{\mathrm{root}}
&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}\right)^d},\\
L_{E,\lambda}^{\log}
&=\log w_{\mathbf J}
-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

For \(L\ge0\), let \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) be the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over \(F\subseteq D\) intersecting every such \(E\) for which some allowed coefficient vector has \(L_{E,\lambda}^{\mathrm{root}}\le L\). Define \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L)\) by replacing the root cost with the logarithmic cost. These are the integral hitting quantities of `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` and `488f0a583886b5fc`, respectively: allowing all finite barycentric supports gives the same values as their affinely independent support convention. Every selected fiber is charged its entire original weight once in \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\). There is no barycentric coefficient in this charge and no replacement of the original weights by truncated, fractional, or support-dependent capacities. The source's \(m^{\log}\) is the present \(m_{\log}\).

For every target \(\mathbf J\in D\) and every real \(L\ge0\), the closed ball of positive source indices
\(T_{\mathbf J}(L)=\{\mathbf I\in D:\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2\le\sqrt L/\varepsilon\}\)
hits every allowed root-cost support at cutoff \(L\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L)
&\le m_d(\mathbf J,L)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in T_{\mathbf J}(L)}w_{\mathbf I}
\le\#\left\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:
\|\mathbf z\|_2\le\frac{\sqrt L}{\varepsilon}\right\},\\
\frac{2^n}{W}\max_{\mathbf J\in D}m_d(\mathbf J,L)
&\le
2\left[8e\left(\sqrt{\frac Ln}+\frac1{2n^4}\right)^2\right]^{n/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Both costs are finite and nonnegative. At \(L=0\), the only admissible supports are \(\{\mathbf J\}\), and both hitting masses equal \(w_{\mathbf J}\). No cost, logarithm of a zero weight, or uniform law is assigned to targets outside \(D\); such an integer target has no barycentric representation using positive-volume source fibers.

Write \(\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}\) for the source's scalar cutoff fraction \(\alpha\). Consequently, for every fixed \(0<\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}<1/(8e)\) and every \(\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}\) with \(8e\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}<\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}<1\), all sufficiently large even \(n\) satisfy

\[
\frac{2^n}{W}
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf J\in D\\0\le L\le\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}} n}}
\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L}
\le2\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}^{n/2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

The same assertion holds with \(m_{\log}\). In contrast, writing
\(L_*=n+1/n\), \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,L\ge0}m_d(\mathbf J,L)/(1+L)\), and
\(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,L\ge0}m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L)/(1+L)\), one has

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min\{m_d(\mathbf0,L_*),m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L_*)\}
&\ge\frac{e^{-1/n}}4W,\\
\frac{2^n}{W}
\min\{\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w),\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\}
&\ge\frac{2^n e^{-1/n}}{4(1+n+1/n)}
\longrightarrow\infty
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

along the even integers. These score symbols retain the integral meanings of the two accepted hitting facts, not their fractional packing meanings.

## Proof

First verify the construction on its entire domain. The involution \(k\mapsto-k-1\) permutes the indices defining \(q_K\) and proves \(q_K(-t)=q_K(t)\). The terms with \(k=0,-1\) show \(q_K(t)\ge|t|\ge0\). For an integer \(k\) with \(-K\le k\le K-1\), its affine term is maximal on \([k,k+1]\): its difference from the term indexed by \(j\) is \((k-j)(2t-k-j-1)\), which is nonnegative there for both \(j<k\) and \(j>k\). Thus \(q_K\) is the linear interpolation between consecutive squared integer values on \([-K,K]\), and \(q_K(j)=j^2\) at every integer \(|j|\le K\). The term indexed by \(K-1\), together with evenness, also gives \(q_K(t)\ge K^2\) whenever \(|t|\ge K\).

All summands in (1) are nonnegative. Since \((\varepsilon^2/d)K^2=n>1\), every point of \(C_n\) has \(|x_i|<K\) for all \(i\), and \(\|\mathbf y\|_\infty\le1/2\). The defining function is continuous and convex, so the set is closed, bounded, and convex. Its value at the origin is zero, strictly below one, so it has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). The defining function is a maximum of finitely many rational affine functions: independently select one affine term in each \(q_K(x_i)\) and one of the \(2d\) terms \(2y_j,-2y_j\) in \(2\|\mathbf y\|_\infty\). Taking all these choices turns (1) into finitely many rational linear inequalities. Hence \(C_n\) is a full-dimensional rational polytope.

For any nonempty integer fiber, the preceding strict coordinate bound permits replacing every \(q_K(z_i)\) by \(z_i^2\), giving exactly its cube in (2). Conversely, if \(\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\le d\), then \(|z_i|\le\sqrt d/\varepsilon=n^{11/2}<K\), and substituting the integer values into (1) gives that same nonempty cube. If the quadratic inequality fails and some coordinate has absolute value at least \(K\), the fiber is empty by the preceding bound; if all coordinates are inside that range, substitution makes it empty. This proves (2) at every lattice point, including every zero of the table. The finite set \(D\) contains the origin, with weight one.

For a boundary index \(\mathbf z\) with \(\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2=d\), one has \(\mathbf z\ne\mathbf0\), and its fiber is \(\{\mathbf0\}\). The closed ambient halfspace \(\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):\mathbf z\cdot\mathbf x\ge\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\}\) contains that point. For every nonempty integer fiber indexed by \(\mathbf I\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\mathbf z\cdot\mathbf I\le\|\mathbf z\|_2\|\mathbf I\|_2\le\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\), with equality only if \(\mathbf I=\mathbf z\). Its intersection with \(S\) therefore has zero mixed-integer volume, proving the boundary-depth assertion.

Evenness of \(q_K\) and of the cube fibers makes both \(C_n\) and its mixed-integer measure invariant under \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\mapsto(-\mathbf x,-\mathbf y)\). Every closed halfspace containing the origin contains a closed halfspace through the origin. Reflection exchanges the two open sides of its boundary hyperplane, so the latter closed halfspace has measure \((W+\text{boundary measure})/2\ge W/2\). The particular halfspace \(\{y_1\ge0\}\) bisects every positive cube fiber. Its boundary has zero \(d\)-dimensional measure in every such fiber, and the remaining fibers already have volume zero. Its measure is exactly \(W/2\), proving (3).

We next check the hitting conventions, including arbitrary supports. For any allowed \((E,\lambda)\), set \(V_{E,\lambda}=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|_2^2\). The barycenter equations give the variance identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{E,\lambda}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I\|_2^2-\|\mathbf J\|_2^2,\\
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}
&=a_{\mathbf J}-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{d}V_{E,\lambda}>0,\\
L_{E,\lambda}^{\mathrm{root}}
&=-d\log\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^2V_{E,\lambda}}{d a_{\mathbf J}}\right)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The strict positivity comes from the positive roots and coefficients. In particular the argument of the logarithm belongs to \((0,1]\). If one starts instead with any integer barycenter \(\mathbf J\) of positive-volume sources, the same expansion gives
\(1-\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf J\|_2^2/d=\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}+\varepsilon^2V_{E,\lambda}/d>0\), so that target necessarily belongs to \(D\). This justifies restricting all scores and costs to positive-volume targets.

For completeness, passing to arbitrary finite representations does not change the accepted integral quantities. On a fixed finite source set, maximize the barycentric mean of the roots over all nonnegative coefficients of sum one and barycenter \(\mathbf J\). A maximizer exists on a compact probability polytope. Choose one with the smallest positive support. If that support were affinely dependent, a nonzero affine relation would give a coefficient perturbation preserving the sum and barycenter. Maximality forces its change in the objective to be zero, since sufficiently small perturbations of either sign are feasible. Moving until a coefficient first becomes zero then preserves the maximum and reduces the positive support, a contradiction. The maximizing support is therefore an affinely independent subset of the original support, has \(\mathbf J\) in its relative interior, and has root cost no larger than the original cost. The identical argument maximizing the mean of the finite numbers \(\log w_{\mathbf I}\) reduces logarithmic cost as well. Thus every all-support cutoff representation contains an admissible affinely independent one at the same weak cutoff. A set hits every affinely independent admissible support if and only if it hits every arbitrary admissible support. This proves the exact match to `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` and `488f0a583886b5fc`, with their original integral charges. Zero coefficients can be discarded and repeated occurrences of an index can be merged without changing a representation, cost, or its set of source fibers. There are finitely many affinely independent supports and hence finitely many relevant costs, proving attainment of the hitting minima and optimized cutoff maxima stated above.

Concavity of the logarithm gives \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log a_{\mathbf I}\le\log(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I})\). Using \(\log w_{\mathbf I}=d\log a_{\mathbf I}\) in (4) yields \(L_{E,\lambda}^{\log}\ge L_{E,\lambda}^{\mathrm{root}}\ge0\). The logarithmic cutoff family is therefore contained in the root cutoff family, and \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L)\le m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) at each target and cutoff, with unchanged weights. Formula (8) shows that root cost zero forces \(V_{E,\lambda}=0\), hence every source equals \(\mathbf J\). The same holds for logarithmic cost zero, and the singleton has both costs zero. This proves the assertions at \(L=0\).

If \(L_{E,\lambda}^{\mathrm{root}}\le L\), (8) implies

\[
V_{E,\lambda}
\le\frac{d a_{\mathbf J}}{\varepsilon^2}(1-e^{-L/d})
\le\frac{L}{\varepsilon^2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Here \(a_{\mathbf J}\le1\) and \(1-e^{-t}\le t\) for \(t\ge0\). If every source were outside the closed ball defining \(T_{\mathbf J}(L)\), every squared distance in the positive weighted average would be strictly greater than \(L/\varepsilon^2\), contradicting (9). Thus that ball hits every admissible support, regardless of its cardinality. Its original total weight is at most its cardinality because \(w_{\mathbf I}\le1\). Translation by the integer vector \(\mathbf J\) bounds this cardinality by the origin-centered lattice count in (5). This proves the first line of (5), with closed balls and weak cutoff inequalities throughout.

We give the denominator estimate with explicit constants. Write \(n=2m\), so \(m\ge2\), and let \(\kappa_n=\pi^m/m!\) be the volume of the Euclidean unit ball. Unit cubes centered at lattice points in a closed ball of radius \(r\ge0\) have pairwise disjoint interiors and lie in the concentric ball of radius \(r+\sqrt n/2\). Consequently

\[
\#\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|\mathbf z\|_2\le r\}
\le\kappa_n(r+\sqrt n/2)^n.
\tag{10}
\]

This includes \(r=0\). Define the continuous radial profile
\(\mathcal F(\mathbf u)=(1-\|\mathbf u\|_2^2/d)_+^d\), and put \(Z=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathcal F(\mathbf u)\,d\mathbf u\). The symbol \(\mathcal F\) is the source's radial function \(F\), distinguished from the hitting set \(F\) above. Polar coordinates and the substitution \(t=r^2/d\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z
&=n\kappa_n\int_0^{\sqrt d}r^{n-1}(1-r^2/d)^d\,dr\\
&=m\kappa_n d^m\int_0^1t^{m-1}(1-t)^d\,dt
=\pi^m d^m\frac{d!}{(d+m)!}\\
&=\pi^m\prod_{k=1}^{m}\left(1+\frac{k}{d}\right)^{-1}
\ge\pi^m\exp\left(-\frac{m(m+1)}{2d}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The integer beta integral here is \(\int_0^1t^{p-1}(1-t)^d\,dt=(p-1)!d!/(p+d)!\), obtained by integration by parts for positive integers \(p\); the last inequality uses \(\log(1+t)\le t\).

Set \(\delta=\varepsilon\sqrt n/2\). Partition \(\mathbb R^n\) into half-open cubes of side \(\varepsilon\) centered at \(\varepsilon\mathbf z\), assigning their measure-zero boundaries consistently. For every \(\mathbf u\) in the cube centered at \(\varepsilon\mathbf z\), one has \(\|\varepsilon\mathbf z\|_2\le\|\mathbf u\|_2+\delta\). Since \(\mathcal F\) is radially nonincreasing and \(w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal F(\varepsilon\mathbf z)\), integration over these cubes gives the exact comparison

\[
\varepsilon^nW
\ge\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\left(1-\frac{(\|\mathbf u\|_2+\delta)^2}{d}\right)_+^d\,d\mathbf u
=Z\,\mathbb E\left[\left(1-\frac{\delta}{R_{\mathrm{rad}}}\right)_+^{n-1}\right].
\tag{12}
\]

Here \(R_{\mathrm{rad}}\) is the norm under the probability density \(\mathcal F/Z\); it is the radial random variable denoted \(R\) in the source, not the base radius in the older simplex construction. To check the equality, write the integral in polar coordinates and substitute \(t=r+\delta\); its radial factor is \((t-\delta)^{n-1}\) on \(\delta\le t\le\sqrt d\). The value of the displayed integrand at \(R_{\mathrm{rad}}=0\) can be set to zero, since that event has probability zero.

The same beta integral, now with \(p=m-1\ge1\), yields

\[
\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{rad}}^{-2}]
=\frac1d\frac{\int_0^1t^{m-2}(1-t)^d\,dt}
{\int_0^1t^{m-1}(1-t)^d\,dt}
=\frac{m+d}{d(m-1)}.
\tag{13}
\]

Since \(d\ge m\) and \(m-1\ge m/2\), this is at most \(8/n\), hence less than \(9/n\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{rad}}^{-1}]\le3/\sqrt n\). For every \(x\ge0\) and integer \(k\ge1\), Bernoulli's inequality gives \((1-x)_+^k\ge1-kx\): the usual inequality applies for \(x\le1\), and the right side is nonpositive for \(x>1\). Applying it in (12) proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon^nW
&\ge Z\left(1-(n-1)\delta\,\mathbb E[R_{\mathrm{rad}}^{-1}]\right)\\
&\ge Z\left(1-\frac32(n-1)\varepsilon\right)
\ge(1-2n\varepsilon)Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

In our parameter range \(2n\varepsilon=2/n^3\le1/32\), so this denominator is strictly positive. Combining (9) through (14) with the weight bound in (5) gives, uniformly in \(\mathbf J\) and \(L\),

\[
\frac{2^n m_d(\mathbf J,L)}{W}
\le
\frac{\exp(m(m+1)/(2d))}{1-2n\varepsilon}
\frac{[2(\sqrt L+\varepsilon\sqrt n/2)]^n}{m!}.
\tag{15}
\]

There is no limiting choice of mesh in this estimate. Explicitly,
\(m(m+1)/(2d)=(n+2)/(8n^2)\le3/64\) for \(n\ge4\). The power-series bound \(e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}\) for \(0\le x<1\) therefore gives
\(\exp(m(m+1)/(2d))/(1-2n\varepsilon)\le(64/61)(32/31)=2048/1891<2\).
Also \(\log(m!)\ge\int_1^m\log t\,dt\ge m\log m-m\), so \(m!\ge(m/e)^m\). Since \(n=2m\), the remaining factor in (15) is at most

\[
\frac{[2(\sqrt L+\varepsilon\sqrt n/2)]^n}{(m/e)^m}
=\left[8e\left(\sqrt{\frac Ln}+\frac\varepsilon2\right)^2\right]^{n/2}.
\tag{16}
\]

Substitution of \(\varepsilon=n^{-4}\) proves the second line of (5). For fixed \(\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}\) in (6), the bracket in (16) is at most \(8e(\sqrt{\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}}+1/(2n^4))^2\), which tends to \(8e\alpha_{\mathrm{cut}}<\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}\). For sufficiently large even \(n\) it is at most \(\rho_{\mathrm{rate}}\). Since \(1+L\ge1\), this proves (6). The pointwise logarithmic comparison proves the same conclusion for \(m_{\log}\).

It remains to establish the unrestricted calibration using a centered Gaussian. Define \(Q(\mathbf z)=\varepsilon^2\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\), \(g_{\mathbf z}=e^{-Q(\mathbf z)}\), and \(\Theta_s=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(\mathbf z)}\) for \(s>0\), with \(\Theta=\Theta_1\). These Gaussian weights \(g_{\mathbf z}\), and their total \(\Theta\), are auxiliary; the body's weights and original hitting capacities remain \(w_{\mathbf z}\) and \(W\).

Apply the centered Poisson identity and its differentiated moment formula proved in accepted fact `9b58c05b730e2a1f`. Its matrix \(A\) is \(\varepsilon^2 I_n\), its shift \(a\) and nearest lattice point \(j\) are both \(\mathbf0\), and its quadratic \(Q\) is precisely the present \(Q\). Its untruncated Gaussian \(w_z,W\) are the present \(g_{\mathbf z},\Theta\); its \(\theta_s\) is \(\Theta_s\), and its \(m_s\) means the Gaussian expectation of \(Q\), not an integral hitting mass. The matrix is positive definite and the Gaussian sums and their derivatives converge, as required there. In this normalization that accepted identity reads

\[
\Theta_s
=\left(\frac{\pi}{s\varepsilon^2}\right)^{n/2}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n}
\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^2\|\mathbf k\|_2^2}{s\varepsilon^2}\right).
\tag{17}
\]

For clarity, every summand in the dual sum has a nonnegative derivative with respect to \(s\), and the derivative converges locally uniformly for \(s>0\). Its logarithmic derivative is therefore nonnegative. Taking the negative logarithmic derivative of (17) gives exactly

\[
\frac{\sum_{\mathbf z}Q(\mathbf z)e^{-sQ(\mathbf z)}}{\Theta_s}
\le\frac{n}{2s},
\qquad
\sum_{Q(\mathbf z)\le n}g_{\mathbf z}\ge\frac\Theta2.
\tag{18}
\]

The second inequality is Markov's inequality applied at \(s=1\), with the event \(Q>n\) as the complement; all equality atoms \(Q=n\) are retained. No assertion about realizing the infinite Gaussian table by a compact body is used.

On \(Q(\mathbf z)\le n\), the site belongs to \(D\), because \(n<d\). Here \(t=Q(\mathbf z)/d\le1/n^2\le1/2\), and
\(-\log(1-t)-t=\int_0^t u/(1-u)\,du\le t^2\). Hence

\[
-\log w_{\mathbf z}
=-d\log(1-Q(\mathbf z)/d)
\le Q(\mathbf z)+\frac{Q(\mathbf z)^2}{d}
\le Q(\mathbf z)+\frac1n
\le n+\frac1n.
\tag{19}
\]

Also \(\log(1-t)\le-t\) for \(0\le t<1\), and the body weight is zero when \(Q\ge d\). Thus \(w_{\mathbf z}\le g_{\mathbf z}\) at every lattice index and \(W\le\Theta\). Equations (18) and (19), with the original body weights, give

\[
\sum_{Q(\mathbf z)\le n}w_{\mathbf z}
\ge e^{-1/n}\sum_{Q(\mathbf z)\le n}g_{\mathbf z}
\ge\frac{e^{-1/n}}2\Theta
\ge\frac{e^{-1/n}}2W.
\tag{20}
\]

At target \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\), every nonzero \(\mathbf z\) in this finite core has an opposite pair \(\{\mathbf z,-\mathbf z\}\) with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\). The weights and roots at the two endpoints agree, while \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\), so both costs in (4) equal \(-\log w_{\mathbf z}\le L_*\). These are affinely independent two-point supports, and thus admissible under either support convention. The zero vector contributes the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) with cost zero. Every hitting set at cutoff \(L_*\) must contain the origin and at least one endpoint of every nonzero opposite pair in the core. Since the pairs are disjoint and equal endpoint weights are charged in full, its weight is at least

\[
w_{\mathbf0}
+\frac12\sum_{\substack{Q(\mathbf z)\le n\\\mathbf z\ne\mathbf0}}w_{\mathbf z}
=\frac12\left(\sum_{Q(\mathbf z)\le n}w_{\mathbf z}+w_{\mathbf0}\right)
\ge\frac{e^{-1/n}}4W.
\tag{21}
\]

This holds for every root hitting set and every logarithmic hitting set, proving the first line of (7). Evaluating either optimized score at this one target and cutoff proves its second line. The displayed lower bound tends to infinity because its numerator grows like \(2^n\) and its denominator grows linearly in \(n\). Together with the already established depth (3), this proves the stated obstruction and its unrestricted calibration with their original quantifiers.
