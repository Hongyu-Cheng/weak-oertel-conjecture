## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R_x^d$ be a convex body, $d\ge1$, with ordinary projection $K$ of volume $A$ and actual line sections $[a(x),b(x)]$. Let $g$ be the ordinary centroid of $K$, and suppose an integer $j$ belongs to the interval between the uniform means of $a,b$ on $K$. Put $y=(j,g)$, let $M$ be the mixed volume of $C$, and write $D_C(y)$ for raw closed-halfspace mixed depth. For every integer $k\ge0$, $C_k=C+[-k,k]e_t$ has mass $M+2kA$ and
$$
D_{C_k}(y)\ge D_C(y)+k\Gamma_d A,\qquad \Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d.
$$
The difference between this guaranteed lower bound and $(\Gamma_d/2)(M+2kA)$ equals $D_C(y)-(\Gamma_d/2)M$.

## Proof

The endpoint-mean interval theorem gives $y\in C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)$ and $|\pi_x(C\cap H)|\ge\Gamma_d A$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing $y$. Apply the single primitive generator case of a9b5e729b97a20f0 with generator $e_t$ and length $k$. Its quotient lattice has rank zero and the quotient measure is ordinary $d$-volume. It yields
$$
\mu(C_k\cap H)\ge\mu(C\cap H)+k|\pi_x(C\cap H)|\ge\mu(C\cap H)+k\Gamma_d A.
$$
This holds for the same $H$ on both sides, including zero integer component of its normal, where the accepted inequality can even double the shadow coefficient. Taking the infimum gives the depth inequality. The identity $C_k=(C+[0,2k]e_t)-ke_t$ and invariance of mixed measure under integer translation reduce the total mass to the one-sided identity 95b5a1ec678eeb67. Subtracting $(\Gamma_d/2)(M+2kA)$ cancels $k\Gamma_d A$ and gives the asserted difference.
