## Statement

Use the hypotheses and coefficients of the arithmetic zonotope polynomial. Set $Z=\sum_i[-k_i,k_i](v_i,0)$. For every closed ambient halfspace $H$,
$$
\mu_{n,d}((C+Z)\cap H)
\ge\sum_{I\ {\rm independent}}m(I)\mu_{n-|I|,d}(\pi_I(C\cap H))\prod_{i\in I} k_i .
$$
For a generator orthogonal to the integer component of the normal of $H$, replace its factor $k_i$ by $2k_i$ in the terms where it occurs. For a single primitive generator the coefficient one of $k\,\mu_{n-1,d}(\pi_v(C\cap H))$ is sharp even with a nonzero continuous normal and a nonzero pairing of the integer normal with $v$.

## Proof

Orient every nonorthogonal generator to have positive pairing with the same halfspace normal. Translating $C\cap H$ by any vector from the sum of the resulting one-sided segments remains within $H$ and within $C+Z$. For each orthogonal generator one may use both sides. The arithmetic zonotope formula on this contained set gives the lower bound, since generator signs change neither spans nor indices. To apply it to a two-sided orthogonal segment, write that segment as $[0,2k_i]v_i-k_iv_i$ and use translation invariance under the resulting integer vector. This works for empty or lower-dimensional caps with their original closed boundaries.

For sharpness take $C=[-2,2]\times[-1/2,1/2]$ and $H=\{(t,x):t+x\ge0\}$ with primitive integer direction $e_t$. For every $x\in[-1/2,1/2]$ the original cap has a nonempty section and projection length one. Symmetric extrusion captures precisely the $k$ new upper integer samples and no lower ones at each $x$. Its mass increment is exactly $k$, giving equality.
