## Statement

Let \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^N\) be a compact full-dimensional convex body, and let

\[
f(\mathbf x)=e^{L(\mathbf x)}\mathbf 1_Q(\mathbf x),
\]

where \(L:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R\) is affine and \(\int_{\mathbb R^N}f(\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x=1\). Thus \(f\) is a continuous-space log-affine probability density, with its values on \(\partial Q\) immaterial to all integrals. Define its actual continuous halfspace depth by

\[
\tau_f(\mathbf x):=
\inf\left\{
\int_H f(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf x\in H
\right\}.
\]

Then, for every real \(q>0\),

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^N} f(\mathbf x)\tau_f(\mathbf x)^q\,d\mathbf x
\ge e^{-q}[e(q+1)]^{-N}.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^N} f(\mathbf x)\tau_f(\mathbf x)^4\,d\mathbf x
\ge e^{-4}16^{-N}.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

For \(s>0\), set

\[
Z(s):=\int_Q f(\mathbf x)^s\,d\mathbf x,
\qquad
g(s):=\log Z(s),
\qquad
f_s(\mathbf x):=\frac{f(\mathbf x)^s}{Z(s)}.
\tag{3}
\]

Because \(L\) is bounded on the compact set \(Q\), differentiation under the integral is valid to every order on compact subintervals of \((0,\infty)\). Consequently

\[
g'(s)=\int_Q L(\mathbf x)f_s(\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x,
\qquad
g''(s)=\operatorname{Var}_{f_s}(L)\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]

We next obtain the dimension-dependent upper bound on this variance. Define the extended-real convex function \(V:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\) by \(V=-L\) on \(Q\) and \(V=+\infty\) outside \(Q\), so that \(f=e^{-V}\) almost everywhere. The perspective \((s,\mathbf z)\mapsto sV(\mathbf z/s)\) is convex on \((0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^N\). Hence

\[
\Phi(s,\mathbf z):=
\begin{cases}
\exp[-sV(\mathbf z/s)],&s>0,\\
0,&s\le0,
\end{cases}
\]

is a nonnegative jointly log-concave measurable function on \(\mathbb R^{N+1}\); its positive support is the convex cone \(\{(s,\mathbf z):s>0,\ \mathbf z\in sQ\}\). Every \(\mathbf z\)-section is integrable because \(Q\) is compact. Prékopa's marginal theorem, with the convex integration set \(\mathbb R^N\), therefore shows that

\[
s\longmapsto\int_{\mathbb R^N}\Phi(s,\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z
=s^N Z(s)
\]

is log-concave on \((0,\infty)\), where the equality follows from \(\mathbf z=s\mathbf x\). Thus \(s\mapsto N\log s+g(s)\) is concave. Combining this with (4) gives

\[
g''(s)\le \frac{N}{s^2}
\qquad(s>0).
\tag{5}
\]

Since \(Z(1)=1\), we have \(g(1)=0\). For \(0<t<1\), the relative entropy of \(f_t\) with respect to \(f\) is finite and equals

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(f_t\|f)
&:=\int_Q f_t\log\frac{f_t}{f}
=(t-1)g'(t)-g(t)\\
&=\int_t^1(1-s)g''(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The last equality is Taylor's formula with integral remainder applied to \(g(1)\). By (5),

\[
D(f_t\|f)
\le N\int_t^1\frac{1-s}{s^2}\,ds
=N\left(\frac1t-1+\log t\right).
\tag{7}
\]

Fix now \(q>0\), and put

\[
t:=\frac{q}{q+1},
\qquad
\lambda:=\frac1{q+1}=1-t.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(\mathbf m_t:=\int_Q\mathbf z f_t(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z\) be the centroid of \(f_t\). The density \(f_t\) is a compactly supported log-concave probability density and therefore has finite first moment. It is positive on the interior of the full-dimensional body \(Q\), so its centroid lies in \(\operatorname{int}Q\): if the centroid lay on a supporting hyperplane, the corresponding affine functional would be strictly smaller on a positive-measure subset of \(Q\), contradicting equality of that functional at its expectation. The log-concave centroid halfspace inequality recorded and checked in accepted predecessor \(5bd39b316ef3c194\), applied with its dimension \(d=N\), density \(g=f_t\), and centroid \(\mathbf c=\mathbf m_t\), gives

\[
\int_G f_t(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z\ge e^{-1}
\tag{9}
\]

for every closed halfspace \(G\) containing \(\mathbf m_t\).

We prove the required pointwise homothetic-cap estimate. Fix \(\mathbf y\in Q\) and set \(\mathbf x=t\mathbf m_t+\lambda\mathbf y\). In computing \(\tau_f(\mathbf x)\), it is enough to consider halfspaces whose boundary contains \(\mathbf x\): if a closed halfspace contains \(\mathbf x\) in its interior, translating its boundary parallel toward \(\mathbf x\) produces a smaller closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf x\). Accordingly, write such a halfspace as

\[
H=\{\mathbf w:\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf w-\mathbf x\rangle\ge0\}
\]

for some unit vector \(\mathbf u\), and define the parallel halfspace through the centroid by

\[
H_{\mathbf m_t}:=
\{\mathbf z:\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf z-\mathbf m_t\rangle\ge0\}.
\]

If \(\mathbf z\in Q\cap H_{\mathbf m_t}\), then convexity of \(Q\) gives \(t\mathbf z+\lambda\mathbf y\in Q\), while

\[
\langle\mathbf u,t\mathbf z+\lambda\mathbf y-\mathbf x\rangle
=t\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf z-\mathbf m_t\rangle\ge0.
\]

Therefore

\[
t(Q\cap H_{\mathbf m_t})+\lambda\mathbf y
\subseteq Q\cap H.
\tag{10}
\]

Log-affinity gives the exact identity

\[
f(t\mathbf z+\lambda\mathbf y)
=f(\mathbf z)^t f(\mathbf y)^\lambda
\qquad(\mathbf z,\mathbf y\in Q).
\tag{11}
\]

Using (10), the Jacobian \(t^N\), (11), and (9), we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_H f(\mathbf w)\,d\mathbf w
&\ge t^N f(\mathbf y)^\lambda
   \int_{Q\cap H_{\mathbf m_t}}f(\mathbf z)^t\,d\mathbf z\\
&=t^N Z(t)f(\mathbf y)^\lambda
   \int_{H_{\mathbf m_t}}f_t(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z\\
&\ge e^{-1}t^N Z(t)f(\mathbf y)^\lambda.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves

\[
\tau_f(t\mathbf m_t+\lambda\mathbf y)
\ge e^{-1}t^N Z(t)f(\mathbf y)^\lambda.
\tag{12}
\]

We now integrate (12), keeping the contribution only from the homothetic copy \(t\mathbf m_t+\lambda Q\subseteq Q\). Under \(\mathbf x=t\mathbf m_t+\lambda\mathbf y\), the Jacobian is \(\lambda^N\), and log-affinity gives \(f(\mathbf x)=f(\mathbf m_t)^t f(\mathbf y)^\lambda\). Since \((q+1)\lambda=1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^N}f(\mathbf x)\tau_f(\mathbf x)^q\,d\mathbf x
&\ge e^{-q}\lambda^N t^{qN}Z(t)^q f(\mathbf m_t)^t
   \int_Q f(\mathbf y)^{(q+1)\lambda}\,d\mathbf y\\
&=e^{-q}\lambda^N t^{qN}Z(t)^q f(\mathbf m_t)^t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Because \(L=\log f\) on \(Q\) is affine, (4) and the centroid identity give

\[
\log f(\mathbf m_t)=L(\mathbf m_t)
=\int_Q L(\mathbf y)f_t(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y
=g'(t).
\tag{14}
\]

Moreover, (6) and \(q(1-t)=t\) imply

\[
qg(t)+tg'(t)=-qD(f_t\|f).
\tag{15}
\]

Substitution of (14)--(15) into (13), followed by (7), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^N}f\tau_f^q
&\ge e^{-q}\lambda^N t^{qN}
   \exp[-qD(f_t\|f)]\\
&\ge e^{-q}\lambda^N t^{qN}
   \exp\left[-qN\left(\frac1t-1+\log t\right)\right]\\
&=e^{-q}\lambda^N
   \exp\left[-qN\left(\frac1t-1\right)\right]\\
&=e^{-q}\lambda^N e^{-N}
=e^{-q}[e(q+1)]^{-N},
\end{aligned}
\]

because \(1/t-1=1/q\). This proves (1). At \(q=4\), (1) gives the stronger bound \(e^{-4}(5e)^{-N}\). Finally, \(e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}1/k!<3\), so \(5e<15<16\), and hence \((5e)^{-N}>16^{-N}\). This proves (2).

## External sources

András Prékopa, [On logarithmic concave measures and functions](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/14411/), Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) 34 (1973), 335--343, Theorem 6, printed page 342. The theorem states that if \(F(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) is a nonnegative log-concave function on \(\mathbb R^{r+s}\) and \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) is convex, then \(\mathbf x\mapsto\int_A F(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y\) is log-concave (with zero values allowed). We apply it with \(r=1\), \(s=N\), \(A=\mathbb R^N\), and \(F=\Phi\). Joint log-concavity, measurability, and finiteness of every relevant marginal were checked above. The continuous centroid input from accepted predecessor \(5bd39b316ef3c194\) is exactly this: a log-concave probability density \(h\) on \(\mathbb R^N\) with finite first moment and centroid \(\mathbf c\) assigns mass at least \(e^{-1}\) to every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf c\). Its probability normalization, log-concavity, finite first moment, and centroid-containment hypotheses were matched for \(h=f_t\) in (9).
