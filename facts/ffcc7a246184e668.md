## Statement

Let $\phi$ be a nonnegative bounded compactly supported integrable function on $\mathbb R$, even and nonincreasing on $[0,\infty)$, with mass $M>0$. For $T>0$ put $v=M^{-1}\int_{s\le T}\phi(s)\,ds$. Then $1/2<v\le1$. For each real $p\ge2$, set $c_p=-2(1-v)/(pT)$. The denominator $1+c_p s$ is positive for $s\le T$, and
$$
\frac1M\int_{s\le T}(1+c_p s)^{-p}\phi(s)\,ds
\le\frac{2v-1}{v}.
$$

## Proof

Evenness gives $v=1/2+M^{-1}\int_0^T\phi(s)\,ds>1/2$, since a nonzero nonincreasing density on $[0,\infty)$ has positive mass on every interval $(0,T)$. If $v=1$ the claim is equality with $c_p=0$. Put $k=1-v\in(0,1/2)$ in the remaining case. For $s\le T$ the denominator is at least $1-2k/p\ge1-k>0$.

By layer cake, $\phi/M$ is a mixture of the uniform probability measures on centered intervals $[-r,r]$. A superlevel interval of radius $r$ has weight $2r/M$ times the level increment, so zero-length intervals have weight zero. Set $x=\min\{T/r,1\}$ on a layer. The captured probability is $(1+x)/2$, hence the layer average of $x$ is $2v-1$. For $p=2$ and $r\ge T$ the captured integral on the layer is
$$
\frac1{2r}\int_{-r}^T(1-ks/T)^{-2}\,ds
=I_k(x),\qquad I_k(x)=\frac{x(1+x)}{2(1-k)(x+k)}.
$$
For $r<T$ the full-layer integral is $1/[1-(kr/T)^2]\le I_k(1)$. Direct differentiation gives $I_k''(x)=-k/(x+k)^3$. Thus Jensen's inequality bounds the average by $I_k(2v-1)=(2v-1)/v$.

For each fixed $a>-2$, the logarithmic derivative of $(1+a/p)^{-p}$ in $p\ge2$ is $-\log(1+a/p)+(a/p)/(1+a/p)\le0$. In fact $\log(1+t)-t/(1+t)$ has minimum zero at $t=0$, by differentiating on $t>-1$. Take $a=-2ks/T>-2$ on the captured support. The integrand for exponent $p$ is bounded by the one for exponent two, giving the result. The pointwise comparison is strict when $p>2$, $k>0$ and $s\ne0$.
