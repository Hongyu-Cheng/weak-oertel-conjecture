## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite, and let $w_v\ge0$ have positive total $M$. Write $\delta_w(z)$ for the raw closed-halfspace depth of a real point $z$ under $\sum_{v\in P} w_v\delta_v$, and $f_w(z)$ for the full-occurrence fractional packing of groups whose convex hull contains $z$. With $w_z=0$ if $z\notin P$,
$$
\frac{\delta_w(z)+(n-1)w_z}{n}\le f_w(z)\le\delta_w(z).
$$
If $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, then $\max_{z\in P} f_w(z)\ge M/(n2^n)$. For unit weights the lower bound is $[\lceil |P|/2^n\rceil+n-1]/n$. On the line, $f_w(z)=w_z+\min(L_z,R_z)$, where $L_z,R_z$ are the strictly left and right masses, and some occupied source has value at least $M/2$. In dimension zero the only mark has packing value $M$.

## Proof

For every closed halfspace $H$ containing $z$, each qualifying group has at least one member in $H$, since otherwise its convex hull is in the open complement. Summing full charges over members in $H$ therefore bounds the total packing by $\sum_{v\in H}w_v$. Taking the infimum proves the upper bound.

Use an optimal packing over inclusion-minimal groups, as in the finite packing-dual theorem. If $z\in P$, the singleton $\{z\}$ is the only minimal group using $z$, and its mass in an optimum is $w_z$. The remaining positive residual capacities cannot have a hull containing $z$, since one could put additional positive mass on a qualifying group of such residual sites. If there are residual sites, strict separation gives a halfspace through $z$ containing none of them; a small perturbation avoids all other source points on its boundary while keeping strict separation. With no residual sites take any generic through-$z$ halfspace. Every source on the chosen side is saturated. In a nonsingleton minimal group, some convex representation of $z$ uses all its vertices with positive weights (otherwise the remaining vertices qualify). Because no nonsingleton vertex is on the generic boundary, the vertices occur on both sides. With at most $n+1$ vertices per group, the chosen side counts each nonsingleton at most $n$ times. Summing the saturated masses on that side gives at most $w_z+n(f_w(z)-w_z)$, and its mass is at least $\delta_w(z)$. This proves the lower bound, including $z\notin P$.

For $P$ lattice complete, apply 6d6fcf377e68c79c in integer dimension $n$ and continuous dimension zero to the finite atomic measure of mass $M$. Its chosen lattice point has depth at least $M/2^n$, so strict separation puts it in $\operatorname{conv}P$ and hence in $P$. The pointwise lower bound gives the weighted assertion. For unit weights its own mass is one and the infimum of finitely many integer halfspace masses is an integer, at least $\lceil |P|/2^n\rceil$, giving the refinement.

For $n=1$, the two rays through $z$ give $\delta_w(z)=w_z+\min(L_z,R_z)$ and the two bounds coincide. Take the first positive-weight source at which cumulative mass reaches $M/2$ from the left; both closed side masses are at least $M/2$. In dimension zero the singleton is the only qualifying group and uses all capacity.
