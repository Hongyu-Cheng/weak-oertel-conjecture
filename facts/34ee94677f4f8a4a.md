## Statement

Let \(k,n\ge1\) be integers, let \(j_1,\ldots,j_n\) be nonnegative integers, and let \(A=(a_{ri})\in\mathbb R^{k\times n}\). Let \(V_i\) be independent and uniform on \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Write \(a_r\) for row \(r\), and define \(\sigma_r^2=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}^2j_i(j_i+1)/3\). Then

\[
\Pr\bigl\{|a_r\cdot V|\le\sigma_r\text{ for every }1\le r\le k\bigr\}
\ge (8\sqrt{k})^{-32k}
\ge (k+1)^{-112k}.
\]

If \(\sigma_r=0\), the corresponding slab condition holds identically. In particular, the statement includes all deterministic coordinates and the all-zero box, and supplies the requested absolute constants for every positive real matrix \(A\).

## Proof

We first construct a one-coordinate coupling. For every integer \(j\ge1\), put \(m=j+1\), and use the state space \(\{0,\ldots,j\}\). Begin with joint mass \(1/(2m^2)\) at each ordered pair \((x,y)\). For each \(d\in\{-j,\ldots,j\}\), put

\[
w_d=\frac1{2j+1}-\frac{m-|d|}{2m^2}.
\]

These numbers are nonnegative, because \((m-|d|)/(2m^2)\le1/(2m)<1/(2m-1)=1/(2j+1)\). If \(j+d\) is even, add all mass \(w_d\) at \(((j+d)/2,(j-d)/2)\). If \(j+d\) is odd, add mass \(w_d/2\) at each of

\[
\left(\frac{j+d-1}{2},\frac{j-d-1}{2}\right),
\qquad
\left(\frac{j+d+1}{2},\frac{j-d+1}{2}\right).
\]

Both pairs are in the state space: the odd-parity case has \(|d|\le j-1\). Every added pair has \(x-y=d\). The baseline difference mass at \(d\) is \((m-|d|)/(2m^2)\), so the resulting joint law, denoted by \(\pi_j\), has difference uniform on \(\{-j,\ldots,j\}\). Its total mass is one: the baseline has mass \(1/2\), and summing the difference masses gives one.

The construction is unchanged by exchanging \(x,y\) and by replacing \((x,y)\) with \((j-x,j-y)\). Thus the two marginals coincide; denote this marginal by \(\mu_j\). It is symmetric about \(j/2\), and therefore has mean \(j/2\) and variance at most \(j^2/4\).

For a fixed state \(x\), residual mass can only come from \(d=2x-j\) and \(d=2x-j\pm1\). The first contribution is at most \(1/(2j+1)\), and each of the other two is at most \(1/[2(2j+1)]\). Consequently

\[
\frac1{2m}\le\mu_j(x)
\le\frac1{2m}+\frac2{2j+1}
\le\frac2m,
\qquad
\pi_j(x,y)\ge\frac1{2m^2}
\ge\frac18\mu_j(x)\mu_j(y).
\tag{1}
\]

For the second upper bound, \(2m/(2j+1)\le4/3\) when \(j\ge1\), so its coefficient is at most \(1/2+4/3<2\). When \(j=0\), let both variables be zero deterministically, and let \(\mu_0\) and \(\pi_0\) be the point masses. The final minorization in (1), the mean formula, and the variance bound also hold in that case.

We next prove the resampling inequality needed to tensorize (1). This proof is included to avoid a hidden dependence on the number of coordinates or the smallest marginal atom. Let \(\mu=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mu_i\) be any finite product probability law with positive atoms. All expectations in the next part are with respect to \(\mu\). Let \(J_i\) average only coordinate \(i\), and put \(L=\sum_i(I-J_i)\). For \(t\ge0\), the resampling operator is

\[
T_t=e^{-tL}
=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\bigl[e^{-t}I+(1-e^{-t})J_i\bigr].
\]

For positive \(h\), define \(\operatorname{Ent}(h)=\mathbb E(h\log h)-\mathbb Eh\log\mathbb Eh\), and define \(\mathcal D(f,g)=\mathbb E[fLg]\). The conditional covariance formula gives

\[
\mathcal D(f,g)
=\frac12\sum_i\mathbb E\bigl[(f(X)-f(X^{(i)}))(g(X)-g(X^{(i)}))\bigr],
\tag{2}
\]

where \(X^{(i)}\) replaces coordinate \(i\) by an independent sample and leaves all other coordinates fixed.

The entropy tensorization inequality is \(\operatorname{Ent}(h)\le\sum_i\mathbb E[\operatorname{Ent}_i(h)]\), where \(\operatorname{Ent}_i\) uses only coordinate \(i\). Here is a proof. Splitting off one coordinate gives the exact chain identity
\(\operatorname{Ent}(h)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Ent}_i(h)]+\operatorname{Ent}_{-i}(J_i h)\).
Entropy as a functional of a positive vector is convex: its second directional derivative in direction \(z\) is
\(\mathbb E(z^2/h)-(\mathbb Ez)^2/\mathbb Eh\ge0\) by Cauchy-Schwarz. Hence \(\operatorname{Ent}_{-i}(J_i h)\le\mathbb E_i[\operatorname{Ent}_{-i}(h)]\). Induction on the number of coordinates proves tensorization. Conditional Jensen gives \(J_i\log h\le\log J_i h\), so tensorization and the covariance formula imply

\[
\operatorname{Ent}(h)
\le\sum_i\mathbb E[\operatorname{Ent}_i(h)]
\le\mathcal D(h,\log h).
\tag{3}
\]

For every real \(s<1\), \(s\ne0\), (2) and (3) also give

\[
\operatorname{Ent}(h^s)
\le\mathcal D(h^s,\log h^s)
\le\frac{s^2}{s-1}\mathcal D(h^{s-1},h).
\tag{4}
\]

To verify the last inequality completely, for \(u>v>0\) the two functions \(z^{s-1}\) and \(z^{-1}\) are decreasing on \([v,u]\). Their nonnegative covariance under the uniform law on this interval gives

\[
\left(\int_v^u z^{s-1}\,dz\right)
\left(\int_v^u z^{-1}\,dz\right)
\le (u-v)\int_v^u z^{s-2}\,dz.
\]

That covariance is nonnegative because it is half the expectation of the product of the two corresponding differences at independent points. Multiplication by \(s^2\) yields
\((u^s-v^s)(s\log u-s\log v)\le [s^2/(s-1)](u^{s-1}-v^{s-1})(u-v)\).
The same inequality holds when \(u=v\), and exchanging \(u,v\) covers the remaining case. Applying it to each summand of (2) proves (4), including negative \(s\).

For positive \(h\), write \(\|h\|_s=(\mathbb E h^s)^{1/s}\) when \(s\ne0\), and \(\|h\|_0=\exp(\mathbb E\log h)\). These generalized means are continuous in \(s\). Fix \(0<\alpha<1\), let \(h_t=T_t h\), and let \(s(t)=1-(1-\alpha)e^t\). Away from \(s(t)=0\), differentiation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}\log\|h_t\|_{s(t)}
&=\frac{s'(t)}{s(t)^2}
\frac{\operatorname{Ent}(h_t^{s(t)})}{\mathbb E h_t^{s(t)}}
-\frac{\mathcal D(h_t^{s(t)-1},h_t)}{\mathbb E h_t^{s(t)}}
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed \(s'(t)=s(t)-1<0\); multiply (4) by \(s'(t)/s(t)^2\), reversing its inequality, and the two terms in (5) cancel. Continuity at \(s=0\) extends monotonicity across that point. Extra resampling cannot decrease \(\|h\|_s\) for any \(s<1\): this follows from Jensen applied to \(h^s\), with the inequality reversed upon taking the power \(1/s\) when \(s<0\), and from Jensen applied to \(\log h\) when \(s=0\). It follows that, for every \(q<\alpha<1\),

\[
\|T_t h\|_q\ge\|h\|_\alpha
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
t\ge\log\frac{1-q}{1-\alpha}.
\tag{6}
\]

Now suppose each coordinate pair has equal marginal \(\mu_i\), a symmetric joint law \(\pi_i\), and \(\pi_i(x,y)\ge\tfrac18\mu_i(x)\mu_i(y)\). Let \(K_i(x,y)=\pi_i(x,y)/\mu_i(x)\) be its Markov operator. Then
\(R_i=(K_i-\tfrac18J_i)/(7/8)\) is a self-adjoint Markov operator preserving \(\mu_i\). Set \(R=\bigotimes_iR_i\), \(K=\bigotimes_iK_i\), and \(t=\log(8/7)\). Since \(R_iJ_i=J_i\), one has \(K=RT_t\).

Take \(\alpha=1/16\) and its Hölder conjugate \(q=\alpha/(\alpha-1)=-1/15\). The required time in (6) is \(\log(256/225)\), which is at most \(t\), since \(8\cdot225\ge7\cdot256\). Reverse Hölder gives \(\mathbb E(uv)\ge\|u\|_\alpha\|v\|_q\) for positive \(u,v\). For completeness, ordinary Hölder applied to \((uv)^\alpha v^{-\alpha}\) gives
\(\mathbb E u^\alpha\le(\mathbb E uv)^\alpha(\mathbb E v^q)^{1-\alpha}\), which is exactly the claimed inequality after rearrangement. Also \(\|Rf\|_\alpha\ge\|f\|_\alpha\) by concavity of \(z^\alpha\), Jensen, and preservation of \(\mu\). Thus, for positive \(f,g\),

\[
\mathbb E[fKg]
=\mathbb E[(Rf)(T_tg)]
\ge\|Rf\|_\alpha\|T_tg\|_q
\ge\|f\|_\alpha\|g\|_\alpha.
\tag{7}
\]

Adding a positive constant and taking its limit down to zero extends (7) to indicators. In particular, for every set \(S\) in the product state space and independent coordinate pairs with these joint laws,

\[
\Pr\{X\in S,Y\in S\}\ge\mu(S)^{32}.
\tag{8}
\]

Apply (8) to the one-coordinate couplings \(\pi_{j_i}\) from (1), independently over \(i\), and put \(\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_{j_i}\). Then \(X-Y\) has exactly the required independent integer-uniform law of \(V\). Under \(\mu\), the coordinates of \(X\) are independent with means \(j_i/2\) and variances at most \(j_i^2/4\). For every row with \(\sigma_r>0\), define
\(Z_r(X)=a_r\cdot(X-j/2)/\sigma_r\), where \(j/2=(j_1/2,\ldots,j_n/2)\). Define \(Z_r(X)=0\) when \(\sigma_r=0\). The latter row has \(a_{ri}=0\) at each coordinate with \(j_i>0\), so \(a_r\cdot V=0\) identically. For all other rows,

\[
\mathbb E_\mu Z_r=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\mu Z_r
\le\frac{\sum_i a_{ri}^2j_i^2/4}{\sigma_r^2}
\le\frac34.
\]

Chebyshev and a union bound show
\(\mu\{|Z_r|\le\sqrt{3k}\text{ for every }r\}\ge3/4\).
Partition each interval \([-\sqrt{3k},\sqrt{3k}]\) into \(\ell=\lceil2\sqrt{3k}\rceil\) disjoint intervals of length at most one, using half-open intervals and a closed last endpoint. These give at most \(\ell^k\) product bins. Since \(\ell\le5\sqrt{k}\), one bin has preimage \(S\) with

\[
\mu(S)\ge\frac34\ell^{-k}
\ge\frac34(5\sqrt{k})^{-k}
\ge(8\sqrt{k})^{-k}.
\]

The last inequality uses \((5/8)^k\le5/8<3/4\). Whenever \(X,Y\in S\), every nonzero-variance row satisfies \(|a_r\cdot(X-Y)|=\sigma_r|Z_r(X)-Z_r(Y)|\le\sigma_r\). The zero-variance rows already hold identically. Therefore (8) proves
\(\Pr\{|a_r\cdot V|\le\sigma_r\ \forall r\}\ge(8\sqrt{k})^{-32k}\).
Finally \(8\le(k+1)^3\) and \(\sqrt{k}\le(k+1)^{1/2}\), so \(8\sqrt{k}\le(k+1)^{7/2}\), giving the stated exponent \(112k\). If all \(j_i=0\), the event has probability one, consistently with the same proof. Every tensor estimate above has constants independent of \(n\).

## External sources

The proof is self-contained. For comparison, Corollary 1.11 of Mossel, Oleszkiewicz, and Sen, [On reverse hypercontractivity, arXiv:1108.1210v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.1210), states that finite product resampling semigroups satisfy \(\|T_t h\|_q\ge\|h\|_p\) for positive \(h\), \(q<p<1\), and \(t\ge\log((1-q)/(1-p))\), without a smallest-atom restriction. The product law used here is finite with positive atoms. The particular resampling estimate required here was proved explicitly in (2)–(6), so this external result is not assumed as a premise.
