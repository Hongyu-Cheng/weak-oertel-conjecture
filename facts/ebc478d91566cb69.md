## Statement

Let \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be finite and nonempty, let \(m\in\operatorname{conv}E\), and, for each \(z\in E\), let \(D_z\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be nonempty, compact, and convex. Define
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{
\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,
\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m
\right\},
\]
\[
\mathcal K_m(D):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zD_z,
\]
and, for \(v\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
h_z(v):=\max_{y\in D_z}\langle v,y\rangle,
\qquad
\phi(v):=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zh_z(v).
\]
Then the following support-envelope identity holds:
\[
\mathcal K_m(D)
=
\left\{
x\in\mathbb R^d:
\langle v,x\rangle\leq\phi(v)
\text{ for every }v\in\mathbb R^d
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathcal K_m(D)=\varnothing\) if and only if there are an integer \(r\) with \(2\leq r\leq d+1\), nonzero vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\in\mathbb R^d\), and positive numbers \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\) such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i\phi(v_i)<0.
\tag{2}
\]
When the core is empty, the data can be chosen together with globally affine functions
\[
\ell_i(z)=\alpha_i+\langle g_i,z\rangle
\qquad(z\in\mathbb R^n)
\tag{3}
\]
such that
\[
\ell_i(z)\leq h_z(v_i)\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
\ell_i(m)=\phi(v_i)
\quad(1\leq i\leq r).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus their restrictions to \(\operatorname{aff}E\) are affine linear-programming minorants that are exact at \(m\).

Now let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for every real base point \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
Also put
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For \(p\in S\), write
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z),
\qquad
H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}.
\]
For lattice base points, write
\[
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n),
\]
and assume
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0.
\]
Put
\[
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
For every \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and every \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), define
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_Ky f(y)\,dy:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\
\int_Kf(y)\,dy=t
\right\}.
\]
There is \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\geq2^{-n}W
\tag{5}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\). For this \(m\), exactly one of the following two branches holds.

1. The half-mass metronoid core
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_{a/2})
:=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)
\tag{6}
\]
is nonempty. In that case every \(x\) in (6) gives \((m,x)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and
\[
h_S(m,x)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n-1}W.
\tag{7}
\]

2. The core (6) is empty. In that case there are \(r,v_i,\theta_i,\ell_i\) satisfying (2)-(4), with
\[
D_z=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\tag{8}
\]
for which the proper closed ambient halfspaces
\[
G_i:=
\left\{
(z,x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\langle v_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)
\right\}
\tag{9}
\]
have balanced continuous normals and are nonconcurrent in the target fiber:
\[
\sum_i\theta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\bigcap_{i=1}^r
\{x:\langle v_i,x\rangle=\ell_i(m)\}
=\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]
There is a proper closed base halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), with \(m\in Q\), such that
\[
Q\times\mathbb R^d\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^rG_i.
\tag{11}
\]
For real \(z\), set
\[
f_i(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(C_z\cap(G_i)_z\bigr),
\qquad
(G_i)_z:=\{x:\langle v_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\}.
\tag{12}
\]
For every \(z\in E\),
\[
f_i(z)\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z
\quad(1\leq i\leq r),
\tag{13}
\]
so the weighted geometric-mean overlap profile
\[
\Psi(z):=\prod_{i=1}^rf_i(z)^{\theta_i}
\tag{14}
\]
satisfies
\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z\leq\Psi(z)\leq a_z
\qquad(z\in E)
\tag{15}
\]
and carries target-scale mass on \(Q\):
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q}\Psi(z)
\geq\gamma_d2^{-n-1}W.
\tag{16}
\]

The profile in the second branch has the following exact common-body realization. Put
\[
A_i:=C\cap G_i,
\qquad
P_i:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(A_i),
\qquad
P:=\bigcap_{i=1}^rP_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Then \(P\) is a nonempty compact convex set containing \(\operatorname{conv}E\). On this common projection domain, define (12)-(14) for every \(z\in P\) and put
\[
g(z):=\Psi(z)^{1/d}
=\prod_{i=1}^rf_i(z)^{\theta_i/d}.
\tag{18}
\]
The function \(g\) is nonnegative, upper semicontinuous, and concave on \(P\). If \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is any convex body of volume one with \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\), then
\[
A_\Psi:=
\{(z,y)\in\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d:
z\in P,\ y\in g(z)K\}
\tag{19}
\]
is a convex body in the Euclidean affine space \(\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d\), and its fibers realize the profile exactly:
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((A_\Psi)_z)=g(z)^d=\Psi(z)
\qquad(z\in P).
\tag{20}
\]
Equivalently, after an affine identification of \(\operatorname{aff}(P)\) with \(\mathbb R^q\), where \(q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(P)\), (19) is an ordinary convex body in \(\mathbb R^{q+d}\). When \(P\) is full-dimensional, it is a convex body in the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

The second branch is not a mixed-depth conclusion. Its caps are necessarily nonconcurrent by (10), and (20) realizes their geometric-mean section-volume profile in an auxiliary body, not as a common intersection inside \(C\). The remaining transference gap is to convert (11), (15), and (16) into a point of \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) having depth \(c2^{-n}W\), or into a dimension-independent rank-reduction certificate, without a loss depending on \(r\), \(d\), \(n\), or the number of extracted levels. No such transference is asserted here, so this theorem is non-final for the locked weak Oertel target.

## Proof

The set \(\Lambda_m(E)\) is a nonempty compact polytope. For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), the Minkowski sum
\[
D(\lambda):=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zD_z
\]
is nonempty, compact, and convex, and its support function is
\[
h_{D(\lambda)}(v)=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zh_z(v).
\tag{21}
\]
The support-halfspace representation of a nonempty compact convex set therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
x\in\mathcal K_m(D)
&\Longleftrightarrow
\langle v,x\rangle\leq
\sum_z\lambda_zh_z(v)
\quad
\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)
\text{ and every }v\in\mathbb R^d\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\langle v,x\rangle\leq
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_z\lambda_zh_z(v)
\quad
\text{for every }v\in\mathbb R^d.
\end{aligned}
\]
The minimum is attained because its objective is continuous on the compact polytope. This proves (1).

For \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), write
\[
H_v:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle v,x\rangle\leq\phi(v)\}.
\]
We next show that emptiness of their intersection has a finite certificate. Choose \(R<\infty\) such that every \(D_z\) is contained in \([-R,R]^d\). For the standard coordinate vectors \(e_j\),
\[
\phi(e_j)\leq R,
\qquad
\phi(-e_j)\leq R.
\]
Hence the intersection of the \(2d\) coordinate halfspaces \(H_{e_j},H_{-e_j}\), if nonempty, is a closed subset of \([-R,R]^d\) and is compact. If \(\bigcap_vH_v=\varnothing\) but every finite subfamily together with these coordinate halfspaces had nonempty intersection, those intersections would form a family of closed subsets of one compact set with the finite-intersection property. Their total intersection would be nonempty, a contradiction. Thus some finite subfamily of the system is infeasible. Finite Helly in \(\mathbb R^d\) reduces it to an infeasible system
\[
\langle v_i,x\rangle\leq\phi(v_i)
\qquad(1\leq i\leq s)
\tag{22}
\]
with \(s\leq d+1\).

Finite-dimensional Farkas duality applied to (22) gives coefficients \(\eta_i\geq0\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_i\eta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i\eta_i\phi(v_i)<0.
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(\phi(0)=0\), indices with \(v_i=0\) may be removed without changing either expression in (23). Remove also every zero coefficient and normalize the remaining coefficients by their positive sum. This gives (2), with all \(v_i\neq0\) and all \(\theta_i>0\). At least two indices remain, since a positive multiple of one nonzero vector cannot be zero, and at most \(d+1\) remain by Helly.

Conversely, suppose (2) holds. If \(x\) belonged to the right side of (1), multiplying its inequalities in the directions \(v_i\) by \(\theta_i\) and summing would give
\[
0=
\left\langle\sum_i\theta_iv_i,x\right\rangle
\leq
\sum_i\theta_i\phi(v_i)<0,
\]
a contradiction. This proves the claimed equivalence.

It remains to obtain the affine minorants. For fixed \(v_i\), the definition of \(\phi(v_i)\) is the finite linear program
\[
\min\left\{
\sum_{z\in E}h_z(v_i)\lambda_z:
\lambda_z\geq0,
\ \sum_z\lambda_z=1,
\ \sum_z\lambda_zz=m
\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Its feasible set is the nonempty compact polytope \(\Lambda_m(E)\), so it has a finite attained optimum. The dual is
\[
\max\left\{
\alpha+\langle g,m\rangle:
\alpha+\langle g,z\rangle\leq h_z(v_i)
\quad(z\in E)
\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]
Strong finite-dimensional linear-programming duality supplies an optimizer \((\alpha_i,g_i)\) whose value is \(\phi(v_i)\). Defining \(\ell_i\) by (3) proves (4). This completes the general balanced-dual theorem.

We now specialize it to \(C\). Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes \(E\) finite, and \(W>0\) makes it nonempty. Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite measure
\[
\nu:=\sum_{z\in E}a_z\delta_z
\]
on \(\mathbb Z^n\). It gives \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfying (5). This \(m\) belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}E\): otherwise strict separation from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}E\) would produce a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\) and no point of \(E\), contradicting (5). Thus \(\Lambda_m(E)\neq\varnothing\).

For every \(z\in E\), the fiber \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives that
\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)
\tag{26}
\]
is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(C_z\), and that each \(q\in D_z\) has absolute fiber depth at least \(\gamma_da_z/2\). If the core (6) is nonempty, accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`, applied with the sets (26) and depth weights \(\gamma_da_z/2\), gives for every \(x\) in that core both \((m,x)\in C\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(m,x)
&\geq
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}\\
&\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is (7).

Suppose instead that (6) is empty. Apply the general balanced dual to the sets (26). It supplies \(r,v_i,\theta_i,\ell_i\) satisfying (2)-(4). In particular,
\[
\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(m)
=
\sum_i\theta_i\phi(v_i)<0.
\tag{27}
\]
The sets \(G_i\) in (9) are proper closed ambient halfspaces because \(v_i\neq0\). For every \(i\) and \(z\in E\), compactness of \(D_z\) supplies a point \(q_{i,z}\in D_z\) such that
\[
\langle v_i,q_{i,z}\rangle=h_z(v_i)\geq\ell_i(z).
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(q_{i,z}\in(G_i)_z\). The fiber halfspace \((G_i)_z\) is proper and closed, so the absolute-depth conclusion of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applied at \(q_{i,z}\) gives (13). Since also \(f_i(z)\leq a_z\), taking the weighted geometric mean and using \(\sum_i\theta_i=1\) proves (15).

The cap boundaries cannot be concurrent in the fiber over \(m\). Indeed, a common point \(x\) would satisfy \(\langle v_i,x\rangle=\ell_i(m)\) for every \(i\), whence
\[
0=
\left\langle\sum_i\theta_iv_i,x\right\rangle
=
\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(m)<0
\]
by (2) and (27), a contradiction. This proves (10).

Define the globally affine function
\[
L(z):=\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(z).
\tag{29}
\]
Equation (27) says \(L(m)<0\). If \(L\) is nonconstant, put
\[
Q:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:L(z)\leq0\}.
\tag{30}
\]
This is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\). If \(L\) is constant, it is a negative constant; in that case choose any proper closed base halfspace through \(m\), for example
\[
Q:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:z_1\leq m_1\}.
\tag{31}
\]
The latter choice is available because \(n\geq1\), and in both cases \(L(z)\leq0\) for every \(z\in Q\).

For \(z\in Q\) and \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), balance gives
\[
\sum_i\theta_i
\bigl(\langle v_i,x\rangle-\ell_i(z)\bigr)
=
\left\langle\sum_i\theta_iv_i,x\right\rangle-L(z)
=-L(z)\geq0.
\tag{32}
\]
All \(\theta_i\) are positive, so at least one summand in (32) is nonnegative. Thus \((z,x)\in G_i\) for at least one \(i\), proving (11). The special choice (31) repairs the otherwise improper all-space case while preserving both coverage and the centerpoint estimate. Finally, (5) and (15) give
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q}\Psi(z)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q}a_z
\geq
\gamma_d2^{-n-1}W,
\]
which proves (16).

It remains to prove the exact realization. Each \(A_i=C\cap G_i\) is compact and convex, so its projection \(P_i\) is compact and convex. By (13), every \(z\in E\) has a positive-volume section in every \(A_i\); hence
\[
E\subseteq P_i
\quad(1\leq i\leq r),
\qquad
\operatorname{conv}E\subseteq P:=\bigcap_iP_i.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus \(P\) is precisely a nonempty compact convex common domain on which all cap sections exist.

For \(z\in P_i\), put
\[
r_i(z):=f_i(z)^{1/d}.
\]
If \(z_0,z_1\in P_i\) and \(t\in[0,1]\), convexity of \(A_i\) gives
\[
(1-t)(A_i)_{z_0}+t(A_i)_{z_1}
\subseteq
(A_i)_{(1-t)z_0+tz_1}.
\]
Brunn-Minkowski in \(\mathbb R^d\), including zero-volume sections, yields
\[
r_i((1-t)z_0+tz_1)
\geq
(1-t)r_i(z_0)+tr_i(z_1).
\tag{34}
\]
Thus every \(r_i\) is nonnegative and concave on \(P_i\), and hence on \(P\).

The weighted geometric mean
\[
G(s_1,\ldots,s_r):=\prod_i s_i^{\theta_i}
\]
is coordinatewise nondecreasing and concave on \(\mathbb R_+^r\). For completeness, its concavity is the two-term generalized Hölder inequality
\[
\prod_i\bigl((1-t)s_i+tu_i\bigr)^{\theta_i}
\geq
(1-t)\prod_i s_i^{\theta_i}
+t\prod_i u_i^{\theta_i},
\tag{35}
\]
using \(\theta_i>0\) and \(\sum_i\theta_i=1\). Applying coordinatewise monotonicity to (34), followed by (35), shows that
\[
g(z)=G(r_1(z),\ldots,r_r(z))
\]
is concave on \(P\).

We also record the boundary regularity needed for compactness of (19). If \(z_k\to z\) in \(P_i\), compactness of \(A_i\) implies that, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
(A_i)_{z_k}\subseteq (A_i)_z+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d
\]
for all sufficiently large \(k\). Otherwise one could choose violating points and pass to a convergent subsequence in \(A_i\), contradicting the violation at the limit. Hence
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}f_i(z_k)
\leq
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl((A_i)_z+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d\bigr).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and using continuity from above for the decreasing compact parallel sets proves upper semicontinuity of \(f_i\), and hence of \(r_i\). The finite weighted product of these nonnegative bounded upper-semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, so \(g\) is upper semicontinuous and bounded on \(P\).

We now verify (19) directly. It is bounded because \(P\), \(K\), and \(g\) are bounded. It is closed: if \((z_k,y_k)\to(z,y)\) with \(y_k\in g(z_k)K\), then the gauge \(\rho_K\) satisfies
\[
\rho_K(y)=\lim_k\rho_K(y_k)
\leq\limsup_kg(z_k)
\leq g(z),
\]
so \(y\in g(z)K\). To prove convexity, take \(y_j\in g(z_j)K\), \(j=0,1\), and \(t\in[0,1]\). Put
\[
b:=(1-t)g(z_0)+tg(z_1).
\]
Convexity of \(K\) gives
\[
(1-t)y_0+ty_1\in bK
\]
when \(b>0\), and the assertion is immediate when \(b=0\). Concavity of \(g\) gives \(b\leq g((1-t)z_0+tz_1)\), and \(0\in K\) implies \(bK\subseteq g((1-t)z_0+tz_1)K\). Hence (19) is convex.

It has nonempty interior relative to \(\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^d\). Indeed, (15) gives \(g(z_0)>0\) for every \(z_0\in E\). If \(p\in\operatorname{relint}P\), the ray from \(z_0\) through \(p\) extends beyond \(p\) inside \(P\); concavity and nonnegativity therefore give \(g(p)>0\). A finite concave function is continuous on the relative interior of its domain, so \(g\) stays positive on a relative neighborhood of \(p\). Since \(0\in\operatorname{int}K\), (19) contains a relative product neighborhood there. Thus it is a convex body in the stated affine Euclidean space. Finally,
\[
(A_\Psi)_z=g(z)K,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_d((A_\Psi)_z)
=g(z)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)
=\Psi(z),
\]
which proves (20).

The construction has made no point of the original body concurrent with all caps. Indeed, (10) proves that such concurrency is impossible in the target fiber. Consequently, neither the union cover nor the auxiliary realization supplies a point of mixed depth in \(C\); the transference statement specified after (20) remains open. This establishes exactly the claimed non-final alternative and no final-target inference.
