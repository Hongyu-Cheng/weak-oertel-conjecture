## Statement

Let n,d be positive integers, let C be a convex body in R^n x R^d, and assume that (x,y) belongs to C if and only if (-x,y) belongs to C. This is simultaneous base negation; independent changes of the base-coordinate signs are not assumed. Put S=C intersect (Z^n x R^d), and use exactly the mixed-integer volume and closed-halfspace depth h_S of the locked problem.

Use the actual-fiber notation of accepted fact 3c78088dd84bbbdf:
\[
C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]
For every z in D_+, set
\[
b_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
\Sigma_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}(y-b_z)(y-b_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dy,
\qquad p_z=\frac{w_z}{W}.
\]
Thus b_z is the actual volume centroid and Sigma_z is the actual uniform-volume covariance, with precisely the same meaning as in that accepted fact. Write C_0 and w_0 for C_{0_n} and w_{0_n}.

Suppose M is a real number with M>=1 and w_z>=w_0/M for every z in D_+. Then W>0, and the original continuous marginal mean
\[
m=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z b_z
=\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}\int_{C_z}y\,dy
\]
is well-defined. The one fixed point (0_n,m) belongs to S and satisfies
\[
h_S((0_n,m))\ge\frac{W}{128M^3}.
\]
More precisely, if Z has the original probabilities p_z and conditional on Z=z the variable Y is uniform on the actual fiber C_z, then for every nonzero beta in R^d both original marginal caps obey
\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\bigr)\ge\frac1{64M^3},\qquad
\mathbb P\bigl(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\le0\bigr)\ge\frac1{64M^3}.
\]
The point m is chosen from the original law before beta or any mixed halfspace is chosen.

## Proof

Compactness of C makes the set of integer bases of its nonempty fibers finite. Every C_z is compact and convex, and the positive-volume fibers are full-dimensional convex bodies in R^d. Simultaneous base negation gives C_{-z}=C_z and w_{-z}=w_z. For every y in C_z, both (z,y) and (-z,y) lie in C, so their midpoint (0_n,y) lies in C. Hence C_z is contained in C_0 for every integer z, and D_+ is symmetric.

We check positivity, including the possible zero-volume convention of the locked problem. Because C is a convex body, there are (x_*,y_*) and r>0 for which the open ambient Euclidean ball of radius r centered at (x_*,y_*) is contained in C. For every v in R^d with norm less than r, the point (x_*,y_*+v) is in that ball and its reflected point (-x_*,y_*+v) is in C. Their midpoint (0_n,y_*+v) is in C. Thus C_0 contains a d-dimensional ball and w_0>0. Consequently 0_n belongs to D_+ and W>=w_0>0: the case W=0 cannot occur under the present hypotheses. In the locked problem, if W=0 is allowed for another instance, the depth inequality is automatic for any point in S; an original normalized marginal mean would then be undefined. Here positivity has been established rather than imposing a convention for that mean.

The centroid b_z belongs to C_z. Indeed, if it did not, strict separation from the compact convex set C_z would give a vector u with u dot b_z greater than the maximum of u dot y on C_z, contradicting its defining integral average. The inclusion C_z subset C_0 therefore puts every b_z in C_0. The coefficients p_z are nonnegative and sum to one, so convexity gives m in C_0. This proves the required point legality (0_n,m) in S.

We now identify and dominate the original continuous marginal. To distinguish a section count from the integer dimension n, define the capital-letter function
\[
N(y)=\#\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:y\in C_z\},\qquad y\in\mathbb R^d.
\]
This counts all original integer fibers, including any zero-volume fibers. It is a finite measurable sum of indicators. The union of the zero-volume C_z has d-dimensional measure zero, because only finitely many nonempty integer fibers occur. Thus, almost everywhere, N(y) equals the sum of the indicators over D_+. In particular, integral N(y) dy equals W, and the original continuous marginal density is
\[
f(y)=\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}\mathbf1_{C_z}(y)
=\frac{N(y)}W\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
This f is a probability density; it is the density of Y under the original joint law just defined. Its mean is exactly m. More generally, for every measurable ambient set F, the construction of (Z,Y) gives
\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in F)
=\frac1W\sum_{z\in D_+}\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in F\}
=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)}W.
\]
Zero-volume fibers contribute zero in the last identity.

Put k=#D_+. The volume comparison gives W>=k w_0/M. Since all positive fibers are contained in C_0, almost everywhere N(y)<=k times the indicator of C_0. Therefore
\[
f(y)\le\frac{k}{W}\mathbf1_{C_0}(y)
\le\frac{M}{w_0}\mathbf1_{C_0}(y)
\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\tag{1}
\]
The reference law used solely for this domination is uniform volume on the actual convex body C_0. Denote a variable with this law by U.

Fix a nonzero beta in R^d. Let q_beta be the density of beta dot Y, and let g_beta be the density of beta dot U. An invertible linear change of coordinates with first coordinate beta dot y, followed by integration over the other coordinates, shows that both projected laws have Lebesgue densities and that (1) implies q_beta<=M g_beta almost everywhere. For d=1 this is just the one-dimensional change of variables. Write
\[
\mu_\beta=\beta\cdot m,\qquad
\mu_{0,\beta}=\beta\cdot b_0,\qquad
\sigma_\beta^2=\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_0\beta.
\]
Because C_0 has nonempty interior and beta is nonzero, beta dot U is not constant on any full-measure subset of C_0. Hence sigma_beta>0. It is finite by compactness.

We use only the scalar projection result proved in accepted fact 3c78088dd84bbbdf: a nondegenerate real projection of uniform volume on a convex body has a log-concave density with essential supremum at most the reciprocal of its standard deviation. The normalization map is explicit: the actual fiber C_z in that scalar step is C_0 here, its centroid b_z is b_0, its projected centered density called f there is t mapped to g_beta(t+mu_{0,beta}) here, and its standard deviation s is sigma_beta. Translation of a real density preserves its essential supremum. Consequently
\[
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}g_\beta\le\frac1{\sigma_\beta},\qquad
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}q_\beta\le\frac{M}{\sigma_\beta}.
\tag{2}
\]
This invokes no approximate-centroid or rank conclusion of that fact.

Let X=beta dot Y under the original marginal. Its mean is mu_beta. Upper domination also gives the following variance bound at that original mean:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&\le\mathbb E\bigl[(X-\mu_{0,\beta})^2\bigr]\\
&=\int_{\mathbb R}(t-\mu_{0,\beta})^2q_\beta(t)\,dt\\
&\le M\int_{\mathbb R}(t-\mu_{0,\beta})^2g_\beta(t)\,dt
=M\sigma_\beta^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The first inequality follows from the identity E[(X-a)^2]=Var(X)+(mu_beta-a)^2. Thus no assumption that the two laws have the same mean has been made.

For completeness, we prove the own-mean cap bound using (2) and (3). Define L=P(X<mu_beta) and R=P(X>mu_beta). Since X has a density, L+R=1 and there is no mass at mu_beta. Put H_beta=M/sigma_beta, so that q_beta<=H_beta almost everywhere, and put
A=E[(mu_beta-X)_+] and B=E[(X-mu_beta)_+]. These first moments are finite by compactness, and E[X-mu_beta]=0 gives A=B.

For every t>=0, the interval (mu_beta-t,mu_beta) has probability at most H_beta t. Hence
P(mu_beta-X>t)>=max(L-H_beta t,0). Integration of this tail, justified by Tonelli's theorem for a nonnegative random variable, proves the elementary first-moment bathtub estimate
\[
A\ge\int_0^{L/H_\beta}(L-H_\beta t)\,dt
=\frac{L^2}{2H_\beta}
=\frac{L^2\sigma_\beta}{2M}.
\tag{4}
\]
On the other side, Cauchy-Schwarz and (3) yield
\[
B\le\sqrt{R\,\mathbb E[(X-\mu_\beta)^2\mathbf1_{\{X>\mu_\beta\}}]}
\le\sqrt{R\operatorname{Var}(X)}
\le\sigma_\beta\sqrt{RM}.
\tag{5}
\]
Combining A=B with (4)-(5), dividing by the positive sigma_beta, and squaring gives R>=L^4/(4M^3). If R<=1/2, then L>=1/2, so R>=1/(64M^3). If R>1/2, the same bound follows from M>=1. This proves the lower bound for the upper cap under the original law.

The other first-moment estimate follows from the interval (mu_beta,mu_beta+t): B>=R^2/(2H_beta), while Cauchy-Schwarz gives A<=sigma_beta sqrt(LM). Therefore L>=R^4/(4M^3). If L<=1/2, then R>=1/2 and L>=1/(64M^3); if L>1/2 the bound again follows from M>=1. Both caps in the statement follow, because the boundary event has zero probability. Only the reference density height and reference variance entered the comparison; every L, R, A, B, and cap probability in this argument belongs to the original marginal.

It remains to transfer this one fixed mean to arbitrary mixed normals. For beta in R^d, including beta=0, put B_beta={y:beta dot (y-m)>=0}. When beta=0 this is all of R^d and P(Y in B_beta)=1. For nonzero beta, the established original cap bound gives P(Y in B_beta)>=1/(64M^3).

Take any alpha in R^n and beta in R^d, not both zero, and consider the closed halfspace through (0_n,m)
H_0={ (x,y):alpha dot x+beta dot (y-m)>=0 }.
Partition D_+ minus {0_n} into its unordered pairs {z,-z}, choosing one representative of each pair. For any such representative z, the equality C_{-z}=C_z gives the exact pair contribution
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}
+\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_{-z}:-\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\\
&=\int_{C_z}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{-\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}\bigr)\,dy\\
&\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
=\frac12\bigl(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
+\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{-z}\cap B_\beta)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The indicator inequality is pointwise: when beta dot (y-m)>=0, at least one of alpha dot z+beta dot (y-m) and -alpha dot z+beta dot (y-m) is nonnegative. Closed boundaries, including the case both expressions vanish, are therefore covered. The singleton zero base contributes exactly vol_d(C_0 intersect B_beta), because alpha dot 0_n=0; this is at least half of its own original marginal-cap contribution. Summing (6) and this singleton contribution, with zero-volume fibers omitted only because they have zero measure, yields
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12\sum_{z\in D_+}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap B_\beta)
=\frac W2\mathbb P(Y\in B_\beta).
\tag{7}
\]
For nonzero beta, (7) is at least W/(128M^3). For beta=0, (7) is at least W/2 and thus also at least W/(128M^3). The calculation also includes alpha=0, arbitrary mixed normals, and every zero-base contribution.

Finally, every proper closed ambient halfspace H containing (0_n,m) can be written as { (x,y):alpha dot x+beta dot y>=t } for a nonzero pair (alpha,beta). Containment of the point gives t<=beta dot m. Consequently the parallel halfspace H_0 used above is contained in H, so monotonicity and (7) give the stated lower bound for H. If the whole ambient space is admitted as an improper closed halfspace, its mixed volume is W and it satisfies the same bound. An empty halfspace cannot contain the point. Taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves h_S((0_n,m))>=W/(128M^3). All choices of m and the original law preceded the choice of halfspace, and the constant involves neither n nor d.
