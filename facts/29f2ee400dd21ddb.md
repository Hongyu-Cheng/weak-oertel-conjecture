## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$, and $\mu$ a probability on $S$. For each parity $r$ put $p_r=\mu(S_r)$ and, when $p_r>0$, let $\mu^r=\mu|_{S_r}/p_r$. Define $Q\mu$ by choosing $r$ with probability $p_r$ and taking the midpoint of two independent samples from $\mu^r$. Then $Q$ maps probabilities on $S$ to probabilities on $S$, is weakly continuous, preserves the full ambient barycenter, and decreases every continuous convex integral on $C$. Its exact decrease of the squared-norm integral is $\tfrac12\sum_{r:p_r>0}p_r\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(\mu^r)$, which vanishes exactly when every positive-mass parity is a point mass. The full sequence $Q^k\mu$ converges weakly to a fixed law on $S$ with the original barycenter and at most one atom in each parity, hence at most $2^n$ atoms.

## Proof

The midpoint has integer base because its two bases have the same parity, and it belongs to $C$ by convexity. Unconditionally both endpoints have law $\mu$. Linearity preserves the mean and midpoint Jensen decreases the integral of each continuous convex function. Within each positive-mass parity, the mean of the midpoint is the conditional mean of either endpoint, and independence halves the conditional covariance. Multiplying the resulting squared-norm loss by $p_r$ and summing gives the claimed formula. The trace is zero precisely when the law is supported on its mean, by its squared-distance integral.

To see weak continuity, fix a continuous test on $S$. Each $S_r$ is relatively clopen because the integer base set is finite. Weak convergence therefore implies convergence of the restricted finite measures and their masses. When the limiting mass of a class is positive, the integral of the test on its midpoint contribution is the product integral against the two restricted measures divided by that class mass. Product integrals of continuous functions on the compact product converge, by uniform approximation by finite sums of products of continuous functions. The contribution of a class with mass tending to zero is bounded in absolute value by that mass times the uniform norm of the test. Thus each class contribution is continuous.

The convex integrals are bounded and monotone along the iteration, so converge. For any ambient polynomial $P$, compactness of $C$ gives a constant $A\ge0$ with $D^2(P+A|\cdot|^2)$ positive semidefinite on $C$. Restricting to every segment in $C$ shows $P+A|\cdot|^2$ is convex there. Thus the integral of $P$ converges as the difference of two convergent convex integrals. The restricted real polynomials contain constants and separate points of compact $S$, so Stone–Weierstrass gives uniform density in its continuous functions. Therefore all continuous integrals are Cauchy. Weak compactness of probabilities on the compact metric space $S$ supplies a limit, and the test limits identify it uniquely. The whole sequence converges to this law. Weak continuity of $Q$ makes it fixed, and the exact squared-norm loss forces its positive-mass parity restrictions to be point masses. The barycenter passes to the limit by continuity of the coordinates on compact $S$.
