## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the exact canonical objects and normalizations of accepted facts `0c5bcff6d676252e`, `344f13d853731558`, and `a00e54d30e71b49e`. In particular, put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\rm sel}(C)+M_{\rm off}(C)
 +R_{\rm cloud}(C)+D,
\tag{1}
\]
and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-dominant inequalities
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\ge O.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained product matching, let
\[
 J:=\{e\in\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B:s_e>0\},
 \qquad S:=\sum_{e\in J}s_e,
\tag{3}
\]
and let the load-minimal optimizer have edge loads \(\omega_e\), source loads
\[
 \beta_z:=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e,
\tag{4}
\]
source points \(p_z^\flat\), and common origin-symmetric full-dimensional body \(D_*\). Write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
 \qquad d_z(s):=\sum_{e\ni z}s_e.
\tag{5}
\]
For every loaded source used below, the accepted source conclusions give
\[
 d_z(s)\le\beta_z\le\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 p_z^\flat\in\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z).
\tag{6}
\]

Define the macroscopic overlap
\[
 \chi_e:=
 \begin{cases}
  \min\{\omega_e,s_e/16\},&e\in J,\\
  0,&e\notin J,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad X:=\sum_e\chi_e.
\tag{7}
\]
For every \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(\chi_e>0\), let
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 \ell_e:=\frac{x_{e,u}+x_{e,v}}2,
 \qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2},
\tag{8}
\]
where \(x_{e,u}\in\mathcal K_u\), \(x_{e,v}\in\mathcal K_v\) are the actual support contacts of accepted fact `0c5bcff6d676252e`. Thus both \((m_e,\ell_e)\) and \((m_e,F_e^\flat)\) belong to \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and
\[
 \|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_*}\ge\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed-volume measure of \(C\).

For every \(\alpha,\eta\ge0\) satisfying
\[
 \boxed{
 \eta\le16\gamma_d,
 \qquad
 \alpha+\frac{\eta}{16}\le2\gamma_d,}
\tag{10}
\]
the positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_{\alpha,\eta}:=
 \alpha\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}
 +\eta\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}}
\tag{11}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and is genuinely halfspace-dominated:
\[
 \boxed{\sigma_{\alpha,\eta}(H)\le\mu_C(H)}
\tag{12}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\). In particular, (12) is a statement about one sum measure and does not add two independent lower bounds for \(D\).

Within the coefficient region (10), the maximum of \(\alpha+\eta\) is \(17\gamma_d\), attained at
\[
 (\alpha,\eta)=(\gamma_d,16\gamma_d).
\tag{13}
\]
Accordingly, define
\[
 \tau_*:=
 \gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}
 +16\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 |\tau_*|=17\gamma_dX.
\tag{15}
\]
Let \(\rho_*\) and \(r_*\) be the intrinsic base rank and continuous residual rank of \(\tau_*\) in the sense of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\ge
 17\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_*}\frac{X}{r_*+1},}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 17\gamma_d\frac{2^{n-\rho_*}}{r_*+1}
 <\frac{128}{275},}
\tag{17}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 r_*+1>
 \frac{4675\gamma_d}{128}\,2^{n-\rho_*}.}
\tag{18}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\tau_*)
 =\rho_*+r_*\ge n+4.}
\tag{19}
\]

There is also a uniform tube-complement consequence using (9). The largest value of \(\min\{\alpha,\eta\}\) in (10) is
\[
 t_*:=\frac{32\gamma_d}{17},
\tag{20}
\]
attained by \(\alpha=\eta=t_*\). Thus
\[
 \zeta_*:=t_*\sum_e\chi_e
 \left(\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}+
       \delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}\right)
\tag{21}
\]
is halfspace-dominated. Put
\[
 A_\chi:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:\chi_e>0\}.
\tag{22}
\]
For every affine map \(a:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\), let \(\zeta_a^{\rm far}\) be the restriction
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \zeta_a^{\rm far}:=t_*\sum_e\chi_e\big(&
 \mathbf1_{\{\|\ell_e-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}\ge1/4\}}
       \delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}\\
 &+\mathbf1_{\{\|F_e^\flat-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}\ge1/4\}}
       \delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}\big).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
This is a nonzero halfspace-dominated measure, and
\[
 \boxed{|\zeta_a^{\rm far}|\ge t_*X.}
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(\rho_a^{\rm far},r_a^{\rm far}\) be its intrinsic ranks. Then, for every affine \(a\),
\[
 \boxed{
 r_a^{\rm far}+1>
 \frac{275\gamma_d}{68}\,
 2^{n-\rho_a^{\rm far}},}
\tag{25}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\zeta_a^{\rm far})
 =\rho_a^{\rm far}+r_a^{\rm far}
 \ge n+1.}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus no affine \(D_*/4\)-tube can leave only an affine-dimension-\(n\) remainder of the paired actual contact-edge support.

Finally, the overlap and calibration used above improve to
\[
 \boxed{X>\frac Q{160}.}
\tag{27}
\]
More generally, if \(\sigma\ne0\) is any positive finite measure carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\), and having intrinsic ranks \(\rho_\sigma,r_\sigma\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_\sigma}|\sigma|}
 {X(r_\sigma+1)}<\frac{128}{275}.}
\tag{28}
\]

## Proof

We first record the exact overlap calibration. Accepted fact `0c5bcff6d676252e` proves
\[
 X\ge\frac S{16}-R.
\tag{29}
\]
Accepted fact `a00e54d30e71b49e` proves the structural inequality
\[
 S+\frac{67}{8}R+\frac{307}{256}G
 \ge\frac{11}{32}Q.
\tag{30}
\]
Combining (29) and (30) gives
\[
 X\ge
 \frac{11}{512}Q-\frac{195}{128}R-\frac{307}{4096}G.
\tag{31}
\]
Equation (2) gives \(R+G<Q/100\). Since
\(195/128>307/4096\), equation (31) yields
\[
 X>
 \left(\frac{11}{512}-\frac{195}{12800}\right)Q
 =\frac Q{160},
\tag{32}
\]
which is (27).

We next prove simultaneous halfspace domination. Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\). For every edge whose contact atom \((m_e,\ell_e)\) belongs to \(H\), at least one of the two endpoint points \((u,x_{e,u})\), \((v,x_{e,v})\) belongs to \(H\); orient that occurrence toward one such endpoint. For every edge whose edge-point atom \((m_e,F_e^\flat)\) belongs to \(H\), independently orient that occurrence toward an endpoint whose source point \((z,p_z^\flat)\) belongs to \(H\). Both orientation rules are valid because the corresponding atom is the midpoint of its two displayed endpoint points.

For each source \(z\), let \(A_z\) be the total \(\chi\)-weight of contact occurrences oriented toward \(z\), and let \(B_z\) be the total \(\chi\)-weight of edge-point occurrences oriented toward \(z\). Equations (4), (6), and (7) give
\[
 A_z\le\sum_{e\ni z}\chi_e
 \le\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e=\beta_z,
\tag{33}
\]
\[
 B_z\le\sum_{e\ni z}\chi_e
 \le\frac1{16}d_z(s)
 \le\frac{\beta_z}{16}.
\tag{34}
\]

Suppose first that \(A_z>0\). Then the fiber section \(H_z\) contains at least one point \(x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\). The metronoid depth theorem in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}.
\tag{35}
\]
This also holds if \(H_z\) is the whole fiber. By (6), (10), (33), and (34),
\[
 \alpha A_z+\eta B_z
 \le\left(\alpha+\frac\eta{16}\right)\beta_z
 \le2\gamma_d\frac{a_z}{4}
 =\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}.
\tag{36}
\]
Suppose instead that \(A_z=0<B_z\). Then \(H_z\) contains the common source point \(p_z^\flat\in\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z)\). The same metronoid theorem and (10), (34) give
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \ge\gamma_d\beta_z
 \ge\eta B_z.
\tag{37}
\]
There is nothing to prove when \(A_z=B_z=0\). Summing (36)-(37) over the pairwise distinct source fibers proves
\[
 \sigma_{\alpha,\eta}(H)
 =\sum_z(\alpha A_z+\eta B_z)
 \le\mu_C(H),
\]
which is (12). This is a direct proof for the sum measure; it does not infer domination of a sum merely from domination of its two terms.

The coefficient optimizations are elementary. From (10),
\[
 \alpha+\eta
 \le2\gamma_d+\frac{15}{16}\eta
 \le17\gamma_d,
\tag{38}
\]
with equality at (13). If \(t:=\min\{\alpha,\eta\}\), then
\[
 \frac{17}{16}t
 \le\alpha+\frac\eta{16}
 \le2\gamma_d,
\]
so \(t\le32\gamma_d/17\), with equality at
\(\alpha=\eta=t_*\). This proves the asserted optimality within (10), as well as domination of (14) and (21).

We prove the general calibration (28). Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to \(\sigma\). With
\[
 \kappa:=
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_\sigma}|\sigma|}
 {X(r_\sigma+1)},
\tag{39}
\]
it gives
\[
 D\ge\kappa X.
\tag{40}
\]
Equations (2), (32), and (40) first imply
\[
 \kappa<\frac{Q/100}{Q/160}=\frac85.
\tag{41}
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(\kappa\ge128/275\). Equations (1), (2), (31), and (40) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac Q{100}
 &>R+G+D\\
 &\ge
 \frac{11\kappa}{512}Q
 +\left(1-\frac{195\kappa}{128}\right)R
 +\left(1-\frac{307\kappa}{4096}\right)G.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
By (41), the coefficient of \(G\) on the last line is positive. If
\(\kappa\le128/195\), the coefficient of \(R\) is also nonnegative, and the last line is at least
\[
 \frac{11}{512}\frac{128}{275}Q
 =\frac Q{100},
\]
a contradiction. If \(\kappa>128/195\), the coefficient of \(R\) is negative. Since \(R<Q/100\), the last line of (42) is strictly larger than
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{11\kappa}{512}Q
 +\left(1-\frac{195\kappa}{128}\right)\frac Q{100}
 &=\frac Q{100}
 +\kappa Q\left(\frac{11}{512}-\frac{195}{12800}\right)\\
 &=\frac Q{100}+\frac{\kappa Q}{160},
 \end{aligned}
\]
again a contradiction. Therefore \(\kappa<128/275\), proving (28).

Apply accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to the single dominated measure \(\tau_*\). Equations (15) and its dominated-measure bound give (16), and (28) gives (17). Rearrangement proves (18). The same accepted fact gives the intrinsic identity
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\tau_*)
 =\rho_*+r_*.
\tag{43}
\]
For every integer \(r\ge0\), \(r+1\le2^r\). Also
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 \le\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3,
\]
so \(\gamma_d>1/3\). Hence (18) gives
\[
 2^{\rho_*+r_*}
 \ge2^{\rho_*}(r_*+1)
 >\frac{4675}{384}\,2^n
 >2^{n+3}.
\]
The exponent on the left is an integer, proving (19).

It remains to prove the tube-complement assertions, which are where the paired separation (9) is used. Fix an affine map \(a:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\). For every \(\chi\)-positive edge, the triangle inequality and (9) give
\[
 \frac12
 \le\|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_*}
 \le\|\ell_e-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}
    +\|F_e^\flat-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}.
\tag{44}
\]
Thus at least one of the two indicators in (23) equals one. Because \(\zeta_a^{\rm far}\) is a positive submeasure of the halfspace-dominated measure \(\zeta_*\), it is halfspace-dominated, and (44) gives
\[
 |\zeta_a^{\rm far}|
 \ge t_*\sum_e\chi_e=t_*X.
\]
This proves (24) and also shows that the measure is nonzero.

Apply (28) to \(\zeta_a^{\rm far}\). Using (20) and (24),
\[
 \frac{32\gamma_d}{17}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_a^{\rm far}}}{r_a^{\rm far}+1}
 <\frac{128}{275}.
\]
Rearrangement is exactly (25). Finally,
\[
 \frac{275\gamma_d}{68}>\frac{275}{204}>1.
\]
Using again \(r+1\le2^r\), equation (25) yields
\[
 2^{\rho_a^{\rm far}+r_a^{\rm far}}>2^n.
\]
The intrinsic rank identity of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` and integrality of the affine dimension prove (26). This completes every claimed capacity, rank, and tube consequence while charging only one genuinely dominated measure at each application.
