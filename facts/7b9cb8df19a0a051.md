## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with nonempty interior. Suppose finitely many closed halfspaces $H_1,\dots,H_N$ cover $K$ up to a Lebesgue-null set. Write $J_+=\{j:|K\cap H_j|_d>0\}$. Then $K\subset\bigcup_{j\in J_+}H_j$. The same holds if some $H_j$ are the whole space or the empty set.

In particular, if $|K\cap H_i|_d=0$ and $F_i=K\cap\partial H_i$ is a supporting face, then for every nonnegative $\mathcal H^{d-1}$-measurable $w$ on $F_i$,
$$
\int_{F_i}w\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}
\le\sum_{j\in J_+}\int_{F_i\cap H_j}w\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}.
$$
For $d=1$ use counting measure. This applies separately on every positive fiber of a finite closed ambient mixed cover.

## Proof

The union of the halfspaces indexed by $J_+$ is closed. If it misses any point of $\operatorname{int}K$, it misses an open ball contained in $K$. Every omitted cap has zero volume in that ball, so the original finite family would leave a positive-volume part of $K$ uncovered. Thus the closed union contains $\operatorname{int}K$ and hence its closure $K$. This proof also allows full and empty traces. On $F_i$, multiply the resulting pointwise inequality $1\le\sum_{j\in J_+}\mathbf1_{H_j}$ by $w$ and integrate. A restriction of an ambient halfspace to a fiber is a closed halfspace, the whole fiber space, or the empty set, giving the fiberwise form.
