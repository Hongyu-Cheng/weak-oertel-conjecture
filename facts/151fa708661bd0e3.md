## Statement

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^m$ be compact and convex, with nonempty integer fibers $S_z$ over a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$. Suppose each $S_z$ has affine dimension at most $q$, with $q\ge0$, and that the midpoint of every distinct same-parity pair in $P$ belongs to $P$. Write $k$ for the number of occupied parities and $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times S_z)$. For every finite family of ambient convex sets whose intersection misses $S$, a subfamily of at most $(q+1)k$ already misses $S$. Consequently, for every nonzero finite positive Borel measure $\nu$ carried by $S$, some $y\in S$ satisfies $\nu(H)\ge\nu(S)/[(q+1)k]$ in every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing $y$.

## Proof

Take a finite family $C_i$ of ambient convex sets whose intersection misses $S$. At each integer base $z$, intersect the fibers of the $C_i$ with $S_z$ and work in $\operatorname{aff}(S_z)$. Ordinary Helly gives at most $q+1$ indices for which the intersection in $S_z$ is empty. In dimension zero this follows by choosing one set missing its unique point. For every nonempty index set $J$ of size at most $q+1$, let $E_J$ be the projection to the base of $D\cap\bigcap_{i\in J}C_i$. Each $E_J$ is convex and for every $z\in P$ at least one $E_J$ misses $z$.

The finite-set witness lemma in the proof of accepted fact bee3cc8e50a0dcea bounds the Helly number on $P$ by the largest cardinality of a subset of $P$ that is precisely the $P$-trace of its hull and consists entirely of vertices of that hull. Two different points of the same parity in such a subset would have a midpoint in $P$ in the hull but outside its vertex set. The cardinality is thus at most $k$. At most $k$ of the projected sets $E_J$ already miss $P$ jointly. The union of their index sets has at most $(q+1)k$ members. A point in $S$ satisfying all corresponding $C_i$ would project into each selected $E_J$, a contradiction.

The set $S$ is compact and carries exactly the original measure $\nu$. Apply the general closed-set Borel centerpoint theorem of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c with location set $S$ and measure $\nu$. Its finite $S$-Helly number is at most $(q+1)k$ by the preceding proof. Its bound concerns closed ambient halfspaces, including any atoms and hyperplane boundary mass.
