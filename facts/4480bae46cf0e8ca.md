## Statement

For the body on $[0,4]$ with widths $(13,11,9,7,5)$ and zero height centers, there is a continuous enlargement to its lifted hull with the singleton fibers $\{15/2\}$ at $-1$ and $\{0\}$ at $5$, keeping its original mixed mass and normalized optimized depth $11/30$ throughout. Now retain the old five intervals and replace the new fibers by $[15/2-u,15/2]$ and $[-u/4,u/4]$ for $0<u<3$. The resulting convex hull has exactly these seven fibers and optimized normalized depth at most
$$
B(u)=\frac{33/2+u/2}{45+3u/2}<\frac{11}{30}.
$$
The upper certificate satisfies $B'(u)=-9/[4(45+3u/2)^2]$ and $B(1)=34/93$, a strict improvement by at least $1/930$.

## Proof

The preceding five-site minimum gives baseline mixed mass $45$ and optimized raw depth $33/2$. Denote the baseline body by $C$ and the hull with the added singletons by $C_*$. The added points lie between the two affine extensions of the old roofs, so the hull remains between those roofs on $[0,4]$ and contains $C$. Thus $C_*$ preserves every old fiber exactly. The interpolation $(1-t)C+tC_*$ for $0\le t\le1$ stays between the two bodies by convexity, so it also preserves the old fibers. Its projection is $[-t,4+t]$. For $t<1$ this adds no new integer site, and at $t=1$ the two exposed new points carry zero length and have depth zero by their exposing base caps. The interpolation is continuous in Hausdorff distance by the defining Minkowski combination of two fixed compact bodies.

For the growth phase, the new upper slopes into $0$ and out of $4$ are $-1$ and $u/4-5/2\le-1$, and the new lower slopes are $-14+u\le1$ and $5/2-u/4\ge1$. The old upper and lower slopes are $-1$ and $1$. Linear interpolation of all endpoints therefore gives a concave upper and convex lower roof with the exact required fibers. The interpolated strips equal their lifted hull, because every section between consecutive integer bases is the Minkowski interpolation of the two endpoint intervals. The growth is continuous since all lifted endpoints vary continuously. Total mixed length is $45+3u/2$.

Use the two caps $x-2(z-1)\ge u/6$ and $-x-2(z-1)\ge-u/6$. For $0<u<3$ each continues to cut only the old fibers $0,1,2$ properly, excluding $3,4$ and the new right fiber. For example at site $3$ the upper threshold is $4+u/6>7/2$ and the lower threshold is $-4+u/6<-7/2$, the closest omitted old site. At the included sites, $|2(i-1)|+u/6<5/2$ for $i=0,1,2$, below each half length. At base $-1$ the first cap contains the new interval and the second excludes it since $15/2-u>4+u/6$. The old contributions are $33/2-u/2$ and $33/2+u/2$. After including the new left length $u$ in the first cap both budgets are $33/2+u/2$. The cap margins sum to $-4(z-1)$ and cover every line with $z\le1$.

The two caps $x+10(z-2)\ge0$ and $-x+10(z-2)\ge0$ retain half the old interval at $2$ and all of $3,4$ as before, and both miss the new left interval and contain the whole new right interval. At the new sites the base term is respectively $-30$ and $30$, strictly beyond the absolute height ranges there. Both budgets are $33/2+u/2$, and the sum of their margins is $20(z-2)$, so they cover every line at $z\ge2$. Together the four caps cover all candidates. Dividing by the total length gives the claimed bound, and its derivative and value at $1$ follow by direct substitution. The strict comparison is to the initial depth for each positive $u$.
