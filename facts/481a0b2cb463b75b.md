## Statement

Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) be finite and nonempty, \(n\ge0\), and \(d\ge1\). Let the convex bodies \(K_p\subset\mathbb R^d\) satisfy the anchored Minkowski inclusions at every base in \(P\), and let the summed \(d\)-volume be \(M>0\). Use the unweighted covariance-buffered depth of the preceding theorem, with multiplier one and parameter \(\epsilon\ge0\), and let \(b_0\) denote its zero-buffer depth. Write \(m=|P|\) and \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). Suppose \(L>0\) bounds the sum of maximal unit-normal fiber section volumes and \(G>0\) bounds \(\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}\) for unit \(u\).

At every buffered maximizing mark \((p,y)\),
$$
b_0(p,y)\ge\gamma_d M/m-\epsilon LG,\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial K_p)\ge b_0(p,y)/L .
$$
In particular, if \(\epsilon G<\gamma_d M/(4mL)\), every such mark lies in its own fiber interior and every shifted own cap with nonzero continuous normal cuts that fiber properly. The same \(\epsilon\) works uniformly on any collection with a common \(P,M,L,G\) and these hypotheses.

## Proof

The fixed-family case of the preceding continuity theorem gives attainment of the buffered maximum. The projective energy at an own mark is at most its fiber volume, by testing the constant denominator, so the unweighted buffer field satisfies \(0\le f\le1\). For a unit continuous normal, the buffered cap expands the ordinary cap by a slab of thickness at most \(\epsilon G\). Pure-base caps do not change. The slab estimate therefore gives
$$
b_0(p,y)\le b_\epsilon(p,y)\le b_0(p,y)+\epsilon LG.
$$
A largest fiber has volume at least \(M/m\). At its centroid every ordinary containing cap captures at least \(\gamma_d\) of its own volume by the ordinary centroid bound recorded in [c6b7777f667984de](c6b7777f667984de.md), equation (4), with the whole fiber paid for a pure-base cap. Hence the buffered maximum is at least \(\gamma_d M/m\). Combining the two inequalities at a maximizing mark proves the claimed lower bound on its ordinary depth.

At a fixed base \(p\), the same slab estimate shows that \(b_0(p,\cdot)\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz. At a boundary mark, an own supporting continuous direction and the anchored supporting slope from [155691fc3768c06f](155691fc3768c06f.md) give an ambient closed containing halfspace whose traces have zero \(d\)-volume. Thus the ordinary depth vanishes there. Comparing to a nearest own boundary point gives the stated distance bound.

Under the strict small-buffer condition, that distance is greater than \(3\gamma_d M/(4mL)\) and the normalized continuous threshold shift is at most \(\epsilon G<\gamma_d M/(4mL)\). The own cutting plane therefore meets the interior ball about \(y\). Each side has positive own volume, giving properness for every nonzero continuous normal. The estimates use the same constants for the entire stated collection.
