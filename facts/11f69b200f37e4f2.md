## Statement

For $j=1,\ldots,m$ with $m\ge1$, let $K_j\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty compact sets, $\mu_j$ probability measures on $K_j$, and $\lambda_j>0$ with $\sum_j\lambda_j=1$. For $y\in\mathbb R^d$, the minimum $D_y$ of $\int|\sum_j\lambda_jx_j-y|^2\,d\gamma$ over couplings of the prescribed $\mu_j$ is attained and vanishes exactly when one coupling has constant barycenter $y$ almost surely. For finitely supported atomic laws $\mu_j=\sum_k w_{jk}\delta_{x_{jk}}$ the minimization is a finite linear program with nonnegative tuple masses and equality constraints for the specified marginals. Its dual is
$$
\max_{f}\sum_{j,k}w_{jk}f_{jk}
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\sum_jf_{j,k_j}\le\left|\sum_j\lambda_j x_{j,k_j}-y\right|^2
\quad\text{for every tuple }(k_j),
$$
where the potentials $f_{jk}$ are unrestricted. Partitioning each $K_j$ into finitely many Borel cells and replacing each cell by a representative with its original mass changes the optimum by at most the maximum oscillation of the cost over product cells. In particular, cells of vanishing maximum diameter give converging values, and a finite dual bound larger than that oscillation certifies $D_y>0$.

For two equal-weight inputs this coupling exists exactly when $\mu_2=(x\mapsto2y-x)_\#\mu_1$. For $d+1$ equal-weight uniform inputs on $K_j=a_j+rT$, where $T$ is a full-dimensional simplex of centroid zero, $r>0$ and $\sum_ja_j=(d+1)y$, such a coupling exists. In the lifted setting it has barycenter $(p,y)$ whenever the attached bases obey $\sum_j\lambda_j z_j=p$.

## Proof

The product measure shows feasibility. Probability measures on the compact product form a weakly compact space, and the marginal constraints are closed because projections are continuous. The cost is continuous, so the minimum is attained. Its nonnegative integrand vanishes exactly on the required constant-barycenter set. For finitely supported atomic laws a coupling is given by one nonnegative mass per tuple; for each coordinate $j$ and index $k$ the sum over tuples with that index is $w_{jk}$. Finite linear programming strong duality gives the displayed inequalities and the unrestricted dual signs, since the marginal constraints are equalities.

To compare optimal values, send each coordinate in every cell to its representative. Marginals become exactly the discretized laws and the cost changes pointwise by at most the maximum product-cell oscillation. Conversely, given a discrete coupling of cell indices, replace each coordinate, conditional on the tuple, by an independent draw from its original law restricted to that cell and divided by its positive cell mass. Zero-mass cells have no positive tuple mass. The recovered marginals are the original laws and the same cost bound applies. These two comparisons give the claimed error; uniform continuity of the cost on the compact product gives convergence.

For two equal-weight inputs, a constant midpoint forces the second point to be the stated reflection of the first. Conversely this map provides the coupling whenever it pushes the first law to the second. In particular, positive-volume uniform convex bodies satisfy this criterion exactly when the bodies themselves are reflections, because each such uniform law has the entire closed body as support. For the simplex recipe, take one uniform point of $T$ and cycle its $d+1$ barycentric coordinates to generate $d+1$ points $X_j$. Each cyclic affine permutation preserves the simplex and its uniform law, and each barycentric coordinate sums to one across the $d+1$ points. Thus $\sum_j X_j$ is the sum of the vertices, which is zero. The tuple $(a_j+rX_j)_j$ has the desired marginals and constant barycenter. The assertion about the base follows from its given affine identity.
