## Statement

Let \(s\ge1\) and \(m\ge0\) be integers. Let \(J\) be a finite nonempty subset of \(\{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^s:\sum_i n_i=m\}\), satisfying symmetric exchange: for every \(\mathbf n,\mathbf n'\in J\) and index \(i\) with \(n_i>n'_i\), there is an index \(h\) with \(n_h<n'_h\) such that both \(\mathbf n-\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_h\) and \(\mathbf n'+\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_h\) belong to \(J\). Here \(\mathbf e_i\) denotes a coordinate unit vector.

Use the count-space notation of accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`. In its complete-composition specialization, the exact map is \(s=|I|\), \(J=\mathcal E\), \(\mathbf N=\mathbf N_I\), \(\mathbf j^+=\mathbf j_I^+\), with the same \(q_i\), \(\mathcal Z\), and \(\mathbf T\); coordinates indexed by \(I\) are simply enumerated from 1 to \(s\). The present \(\nu\) is a count-space law, equal in that specialization to the augmented active-count pushforward of the predecessor's lattice-point law \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\). For general \(J\), no simplex augmentation or maximum-entropy assertion is assumed. The assignment's normalizer \(Z\) and count mean \(\mathbf j\) are denoted here by \(\mathcal Z\) and \(\mathbf j^+\).

For arbitrary \(q_1,\ldots,q_s>0\), define \(q^{\mathbf n}:=\prod_i q_i^{n_i}\), \(\mathbf n!:=\prod_i n_i!\), and the law and its mean by
\[
\mathcal Z:=\sum_{\mathbf n\in J}q^{\mathbf n},
\qquad
\nu(\mathbf n):=\frac{q^{\mathbf n}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\mathbf N\sim\nu,
\qquad
\mathbf j^+:=\mathbb E_\nu\mathbf N.
\]
There are no factorial or multinomial coefficients in \(\nu\). Integrality of \(\mathbf j^+\) is not required for the conclusions below.

Given \(\mathbf N\), let the \(T_i\) be independent \(\operatorname{Gamma}(N_i+1,1)\) variables, where the second parameter is the rate. Set \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\) and \(f_J(\mathbf w):=\sum_{\mathbf n\in J}\mathbf w^{\mathbf n}/\mathbf n!\). Then the marginal density of \(\mathbf T\), with respect to \(s\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure, is exactly
\[
f_{\mathbf T}(\mathbf t)
=\frac{e^{-\sum_i t_i}}{\mathcal Z}
 f_J(q_1t_1,\ldots,q_st_s)
 \mathbf1_{\{t_i>0\ \text{for all }i\}}.
\]
This density is log-concave, and every nonzero linear projection of \(\mathbf T\) has a log-concave probability density. Its conditional moments and total covariance are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\mathbf T\mid\mathbf N]&=\mathbf N+\mathbf1,
&\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf T\mid\mathbf N)&=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf N+\mathbf1),\\
\mathbb E\mathbf T&=\mathbf j^++\mathbf1,
&\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf T)&=\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(\mathbf N)
 +\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf j^++\mathbf1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The posterior is also exact. For \(\mathbf t\) in the positive orthant, write \(w_i=q_it_i\). Then, for \(\mathbf n\in J\),
\[
\Pr(\mathbf N=\mathbf n\mid\mathbf T=\mathbf t)
=\frac{\mathbf w^{\mathbf n}}{\mathbf n!f_J(\mathbf w)}.
\]
In particular, with \(\delta_{ih}\) the Kronecker delta,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[N_i\mid\mathbf T=\mathbf t]
 &=w_i\partial_i\log f_J(\mathbf w),\\
\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,N_h\mid\mathbf T=\mathbf t)
 &=\delta_{ih}w_i\partial_i\log f_J(\mathbf w)
   +w_iw_h\partial_i\partial_h\log f_J(\mathbf w).
\end{aligned}
\]

A direction \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^s\) is a count-space coefficient vector. In the simplex specialization, the exact alias to the lattice-space direction of accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191` is its extension by \(a_{r+1}=0\), restricted to \(I\). Arbitrary count-space directions are allowed here. Define the space of coefficients constant on \(J\) and the least possible added variance by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U_J
 &:=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^s:
       \mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf n-\mathbf n')=0
       \text{ for every }\mathbf n,\mathbf n'\in J\},\\
\kappa_{J,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)
 &:=\min_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_J}
       \sum_{i=1}^s(a_i+u_i)^2(j_i^++1),
       \qquad \mathbf a\in\mathbb R^s.
\end{aligned}
\]
The minimum exists. For any \(A\ge0\) and direction \(\mathbf a\) satisfying the precise comparison
\[
\kappa_{J,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)
\le A\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N),
\]
accepted fact `8bb84543f325a261` and the lift imply
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\bigl|\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf N-\mathbf j^+)\bigr|^4
\le5072(1+A)^2
    \bigl(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N)\bigr)^2.
\]
Zero-variance directions satisfy the fourth-moment conclusion without imposing any comparison. A common numerical \(A\) independent of dimensions, support, and tilt would therefore give an absolute directional fourth-moment bound on any class on which the displayed comparison is proved. The lift alone does not prove such a common \(A\).

Indeed, for every integer \(L\ge0\), the support
\(J_L:=\{(L+k,L+2-k):k=0,1,2\}\), with \(q_1=q_2=1\), has integral mean \(\mathbf j^+=(L+1,L+1)\). For \(\mathbf a=(1,-1)\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N)=\frac83,
\qquad
\kappa_{J_L,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)=2(L+2),
\qquad
\frac{\kappa_{J_L,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)}
     {\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N)}
=\frac{3(L+2)}4.
\]
Thus no absolute \(A\) makes this comparison valid for all the supports in the statement, even under integral means. This is an obstruction to the specified lift comparison, not a disproof of an absolute fourth-moment assertion with additional hypotheses.

## Proof

For an integer \(k\ge0\), integration by parts gives \(\int_0^\infty e^{-t}t^k\,dt=k!\). The conditional density of \(T_i\) is therefore \(e^{-t_i}t_i^{N_i}/N_i!\) on \(t_i>0\). Multiplying these conditional densities by \(\nu(\mathbf n)\) and summing over \(\mathbf n\in J\) gives the asserted marginal density. Its integral is one, since each summand integrates to \(q^{\mathbf n}/\mathcal Z\). This calculation also shows exactly where factorials enter: they normalize the conditional gamma densities, not the original discrete law.

The support hypothesis is precisely the primary source's definition of an M-convex set. For \(m\ge1\), Theorem 3.10 of Brändén and Huh identifies \(f_J\) as a Lorentzian polynomial. It is homogeneous of degree \(m\), has nonnegative coefficients, is nonzero, and is strictly positive on \(\mathbb R_{>0}^s\). Theorem 2.30 identifies a homogeneous Lorentzian polynomial as strongly and completely log-concave. The order-zero case of strong log-concavity gives concavity of \(\log f_J\) on \(\mathbb R_{>0}^s\). Consequently
\(\log f_{\mathbf T}(\mathbf t)=-\sum_i t_i-\log\mathcal Z+\log f_J(q_1t_1,\ldots,q_st_s)\)
is concave on the positive orthant. This orthant is convex, so extension by zero is log-concave on all of \(\mathbb R^s\): if an endpoint has density zero, the defining inequality has zero right-hand side; otherwise both endpoints and their strict convex combination lie in the orthant. If \(m=0\), nonnegativity of the coordinates forces \(J=\{\mathbf0\}\), \(\mathcal Z=f_J=1\), and the product-exponential density is log-concave directly. No positive-degree theorem is needed in that case.

Here is the applicability check for scalar marginal log-concavity. The proof of accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191` establishes directly, using inverse cumulative distribution functions and weighted arithmetic-geometric mean, that integrating one coordinate of a finite nonnegative log-concave function preserves log-concavity whenever its sectional integrals are finite. Use that argument with the predecessor's \(h(\mathbf z,t)\) equal to the present density after an orthogonal change of coordinates, or to an intermediate coordinate marginal of that density. The original density satisfies a global bound
\(f_{\mathbf T}(\mathbf t)\le C(1+\|\mathbf t\|)^m e^{-\|\mathbf t\|}\)
for a finite constant \(C\) depending on \(J,q\); outside the positive orthant its value is zero. Indeed, each monomial is bounded by a constant times \((1+\|\mathbf t\|)^m\), and \(\sum_i t_i\ge\|\mathbf t\|\) in the orthant. Orthogonal changes preserve this bound.

Every partial-coordinate integral is finite at every value of its retained coordinates. More explicitly, split the rotated coordinates into \((\mathbf z,\mathbf v)\) and use
\(\sqrt{\|\mathbf z\|^2+\|\mathbf v\|^2}\ge(\|\mathbf z\|+\|\mathbf v\|)/\sqrt2\) and
\(1+\sqrt{\|\mathbf z\|^2+\|\mathbf v\|^2}\le(1+\|\mathbf z\|)(1+\|\mathbf v\|)\).
Integrating the preceding bound in \(\mathbf v\) bounds the marginal by a finite constant times \((1+\|\mathbf z\|)^m e^{-\|\mathbf z\|/\sqrt2}\). Thus the finiteness hypotheses hold at every intermediate integration, and the one-coordinate argument from `cfb2a3f2d408f191` applies successively. Rotating a nonzero direction to the first coordinate, integrating the others, and then scaling gives a log-concave density for every nonzero linear projection of \(\mathbf T\). This use of the accepted integration proof does not import the predecessor's special multinomial covariance identity.

The gamma integrals also give
\(\mathbb E[T_i\mid\mathbf N]=N_i+1\) and
\(\mathbb E[T_i^2\mid\mathbf N]=(N_i+1)(N_i+2)\), so the conditional variance is \(N_i+1\). Conditional independence makes the conditional covariance matrix diagonal. Total expectation and total covariance then give exactly the displayed formulas for \(\mathbb E\mathbf T\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf T)\). All these moments are finite, since \(J\) is finite.

Bayes' formula applied to the same joint density gives the stated posterior. Differentiating the finite polynomial sum shows that its conditional first moment is \(w_i\partial_i f_J/f_J=w_i\partial_i\log f_J\). Differentiating this conditional mean by \(w_h\partial_h\) gives the conditional covariance: differentiation multiplies each unnormalized posterior weight by \(n_h\), while the denominator derivative subtracts the product of the two conditional means. Expanding that derivative yields the displayed Kronecker-delta and Hessian terms. For a complete composition support, \(f_J(\mathbf w)=(\sum_iw_i)^m/m!\), recovering the predecessor's multinomial posterior. A general M-convex support need not have this polynomial form or that posterior.

The set \(\mathcal U_J\) is a closed linear subspace. The quadratic function minimized in \(\kappa_{J,\mathbf j^+}\) is strictly convex and coercive in \(\mathbf u\), because \(j_i^++1\ge1\). Its restriction to \(\mathcal U_J\) therefore attains a unique minimum. For any \(\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_J\), the scalar \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf N\) is constant, and its value equals \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf j^+\) by expectation. In particular, adding \(\mathbf u\) to the direction leaves its centered discrete projection unchanged.

Fix \(\mathbf a\), and write \(Y:=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf N-\mathbf j^+)\), using the predecessor's centered-projection notation. For \(\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_J\), set \(\mathbf b:=\mathbf a+\mathbf u\) and \(\widetilde Y:=\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf T-\mathbf j^+-\mathbf1)\). Here \(\mathbf b\) is the adjusted count-space direction. In the complete-composition specialization with \(m\ge1\), choosing \(u_i=-m^{-1}\sum_h a_hj_h^+\) gives exactly the \(b_i\) of `cfb2a3f2d408f191`; other admissible shifts are allowed here. Conditional expectation, conditional variance, and total variance give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\widetilde Y\mid\mathbf N]
 &=\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf N-\mathbf j^+)=Y,\\
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y\mid\mathbf N)
 &=\sum_i b_i^2(N_i+1),\\
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)
 &=\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\sum_i b_i^2(j_i^++1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\kappa_{J,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)\) is exactly the least added variance among all these admissible shifts. This explains the comparison in the statement; it is not replaced by individual coordinate-variance bounds.

If \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=0\), then \(Y=0\) almost surely, and the fourth-moment conclusion is immediate. Moreover, positivity of \(\nu\) on all of \(J\) implies in this case that \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf n\) is constant on \(J\), so \(\mathbf a\in\mathcal U_J\); choosing \(\mathbf u=-\mathbf a\) gives \(\kappa_{J,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)=0\). This also covers every singleton support.

Suppose instead that \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)>0\), and take the minimizing \(\mathbf u\). Then \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), since otherwise \(\mathbf a\) would be constant on \(J\). Hence \(\widetilde Y\) has a log-concave density by the projection argument and translation invariance of log-concavity. Its mean is zero, and the assumed comparison gives
\(\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)\le(1+A)\operatorname{Var}(Y)\).
Conditional Jensen for \(x\mapsto|x|^4\), followed by accepted fact `8bb84543f325a261`, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|Y|^4
 &\le\mathbb E|\widetilde Y|^4\\
 &\le5072\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)\bigr)^2\\
 &\le5072(1+A)^2\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(Y)\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The exact scalar alias to `8bb84543f325a261` is its \(X=\widetilde Y\), \(\mu=0\), and \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)\). Finite gamma mixtures have finite fourth moments, so all conditional expectations in this argument are finite.

Finally, verify the claimed obstruction. In \(J_L\), increasing the first coordinate by one decreases the second by one, and the allowed values of \(k\) form the interval \(\{0,1,2\}\). Whenever two points differ in the first coordinate, moving the larger \(k\) down by one and the smaller \(k\) up by one stays in this interval; the reversed move handles an excess in the second coordinate. This checks symmetric exchange. At \(q_1=q_2=1\), \(k\) is uniform on \(\{0,1,2\}\), giving the stated integral mean. All differences of support points span \((1,-1)\), so \(\mathcal U_{J_L}=\{(c,c):c\in\mathbb R\}\). For \(\mathbf a=(1,-1)\), the centered projection is \(2(k-1)\), of variance \(8/3\), and
\[
\kappa_{J_L,\mathbf j^+}(\mathbf a)
=\min_{c\in\mathbb R}(L+2)\bigl((1+c)^2+(-1+c)^2\bigr)
=2(L+2).
\]
The resulting ratio tends to infinity with \(L\), disproving a uniform comparison even for integral means. The same projection has fourth moment \(32/3\), and hence standardized fourth moment \(3/2\), independent of \(L\). In particular, failure of this lift comparison is not failure of its desired fourth-moment conclusion for this example.

## External sources

Petter Brändén and June Huh, “Lorentzian polynomials,” Annals of Mathematics 192 (2020). Primary PDF: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v192-n3-p04-s.pdf . The following statements and definitions were inspected in that primary PDF.

On printed p. 830, the second exchange condition defining M-convexity is: “For any \(\alpha,\beta\in J\) and any index \(i\) satisfying \(\alpha_i>\beta_i\), there is an index \(j\) satisfying \(\alpha_j<\beta_j\) and \(\alpha-e_i+e_j\in J\) and \(\beta-e_j+e_i\in J\).” This is exactly the hypothesis above, with the source's index \(j\) renamed \(h\) to avoid confusing it with the count mean.

Section 3.2, printed p. 855, defines \(f_J=\sum_{\alpha\in J}w^\alpha/\alpha!\), where \(\alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!\). Theorem 3.10, printed p. 856, states: “The following are equivalent for any nonempty \(J\subseteq\mathbb N^n\):” and includes “(4) The generating function \(f_J\) is a Lorentzian polynomial” and “(7) \(J\) is M-convex.” The source's \(n\) is the present \(s\), its \(\alpha\) is the present count index \(\mathbf n\), and its generating function is exactly the present factorial-normalized \(f_J\), not \(\sum_{\mathbf n\in J}\mathbf w^{\mathbf n}\). Nonemptiness and the exchange definition have been checked, and the present fixed total makes its degree exactly \(m\).

Theorem 2.30, printed p. 847, states: “The following conditions are equivalent for any homogeneous polynomial \(f\): (1) \(f\) is completely log-concave; (2) \(f\) is strongly log-concave; (3) \(f\) is Lorentzian.” On printed p. 846 the definition of strong log-concavity says that, for all \(\alpha\in\mathbb N^n\), “\(\partial^\alpha f\) is identically zero or \(\log(\partial^\alpha f)\) is concave on \(\mathbb R_{>0}^n\).” Taking \(\alpha=\mathbf0\) for the nonzero \(f_J\) yields precisely the needed concavity of \(\log f_J\). The zero-degree case was handled directly rather than inferred from a positive-degree statement.

No external marginal theorem is used: marginal log-concavity is obtained from the direct integration argument in accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`, with its sectional-integrability hypotheses verified in the proof above.
