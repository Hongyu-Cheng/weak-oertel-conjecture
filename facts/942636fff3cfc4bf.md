## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge4\), put
\[
 \mathcal A_n:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb N^n:2\le |\alpha|\le n,\
 |\alpha|\text{ even}\},
 \qquad d_n:=|\mathcal A_n|.
\]
Fix an integer \(L\ge n\) and a rational \(\Lambda>1\). For
\(s\in[-1,1]^n\), \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_n\), and
\(m_\alpha:=|\alpha|\), define
\[
 \ell_\alpha(s):=s^\alpha-
 \frac{m_\alpha-1}{2}
 \left(\Lambda m_\alpha-\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i s_i^2\right),
\]
\[
 u_\alpha(s):=s^\alpha+
 \frac{m_\alpha-1}{2}
 \left(\Lambda m_\alpha-\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i s_i^2\right),
\]
and
\[
 C=C_{n,L,\Lambda}:=
 \left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n}:
 s=x/L\in[-1,1]^n,\
 \ell_\alpha(s)\le y_\alpha\le u_\alpha(s)
 \ (\alpha\in\mathcal A_n)\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. The exact continuous dimension is
\[
 \boxed{d_n=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
 \binom{n+2j-1}{2j}.}
\tag{2}
\]
Every \(\ell_\alpha\) is convex on \([-1,1]^n\), every
\(u_\alpha\) is concave there, and every coordinate half-width obeys
\[
 \frac{m_\alpha-1}{2}
 \left(\Lambda m_\alpha-\sum_i\alpha_i s_i^2\right)
 \ge \frac{m_\alpha(m_\alpha-1)}2(\Lambda-1)>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body in
\(\mathbb R^{n+d_n}\).

2. Its positive lattice support is exactly
\[
 Z_L=[-L,L]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(z\in Z_L\), its fiber is the displayed coordinate box, its
centroid is
\[
 c_z=\bigl((z/L)^\alpha\bigr)_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n},
\tag{5}
\]
and its exact volume is
\[
 \boxed{
 a_z=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}
 (m_\alpha-1)
 \left(\Lambda m_\alpha-
 \sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i(z_i/L)^2\right).}
\tag{6}
\]
Put \(W:=\sum_{z\in Z_L}a_z\) and \(a_0:=a_{z=0}\), and let \(G\)
denote the canonical complete metronoid-core capacity of accepted facts
3d1f63abf09b2abb, 41c6cbb26797981c, and 6b09aae189415c70. Then
\[
 a_z\le a_0=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}
 (m_\alpha-1)\Lambda m_\alpha,
 \qquad
 a_0\le W\le(2L+1)^n a_0.
\tag{7}
\]

3. The genuine body has the global reflection symmetry
\[
 C_{-z}=C_z,\qquad a_{-z}=a_z,\qquad c_{-z}=c_z.
\tag{8}
\]
At the occupied midpoint \(0\), take one representative from every
nonzero unordered pair \(\{z,-z\}\), adjoin the central point \(c_0=0\),
and put
\[
 q_z:=\frac{c_z+c_{-z}}2=c_z.
\]
The affine rank of this central reflected centroid cloud is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\dim\operatorname{aff}
 \bigl(\{0\}\cup\{q_z:z/\!\pm\}\bigr)=d_n.}
\tag{9}
\]

4. To fix the canonical normalization, put
\(\gamma_k:=(k/(k+1))^k\). For every occupied midpoint \(\mu\), choose
one representative set \(\mathcal R_\mu\) from the nonzero reflected
pairs \(\{v,-v\}\) with \(a_{\mu+v}a_{\mu-v}>0\), put

## Proof

For a fixed \(\alpha\), write \(m=m_\alpha\) and choose a list
\(i_1,\ldots,i_m\) in which the index \(i\) occurs \(\alpha_i\) times.
Then \(s^\alpha=\prod_{p=1}^m s_{i_p}\). For every
\(v\in\mathbb R^n\), differentiation of this product gives
\[
 D^2(s^\alpha)[v,v]
 =\sum_{p\ne q}v_{i_p}v_{i_q}
 \prod_{r\ne p,q}s_{i_r}.
\tag{22}
\]
On \([-1,1]^n\), the triangle inequality and
\(2|ab|\le a^2+b^2\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |D^2(s^\alpha)[v,v]|
 &\le\sum_{p\ne q}|v_{i_p}v_{i_q}|\\
 &\le(m-1)\sum_{p=1}^m v_{i_p}^2
 =(m-1)\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i v_i^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
The Hessian of
\((m-1)\sum_i\alpha_i s_i^2/2\) has quadratic form equal to the last
quantity in (23). Hence \(D^2\ell_\alpha\) is positive semidefinite and
\(D^2u_\alpha\) is negative semidefinite. This proves the claimed
convexity and concavity. Since \(\sum_i\alpha_i s_i^2\le m\), (3)
follows. The set (1) is the intersection of the cube with the epigraphs
of the \(\ell_\alpha\)'s and the hypographs of the \(u_\alpha\)'s, after
the invertible base scaling \(x=Ls\), so it is convex and compact. At
\((s,y)=(0,0)\) every fiber inequality and every base inequality is
strict; continuity and (3) give an ambient open neighborhood contained
in \(C\). Thus \(C\) is full dimensional. This verifies a genuine
convex body before any rank calculation.

There are \(\binom{n+m-1}{m}\) weak compositions of \(m\) into \(n\)
parts. Summing this count over the positive even degrees at most \(n\)
proves (2). The interval in coordinate \(\alpha\) has length
\[
 (m_\alpha-1)
 \left(\Lambda m_\alpha-\sum_i\alpha_i(z_i/L)^2\right).
\]
It is positive by (3), and multiplication over the independent box
coordinates proves (4) and (6). The center of a coordinate box is its
centroid, proving (5). Every factor in (6) is at most its value at zero,
which proves (7).

All degrees in \(\mathcal A_n\) are even. Hence
\((-s)^\alpha=s^\alpha\), while every width depends only on the squares
\(s_i^2\). This proves (8). To prove (9), suppose that
\(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}\lambda_\alpha q_{z,\alpha}=0\) for all
representatives \(z/\!\pm\), and define
\[
 p(t):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}\lambda_\alpha t^\alpha.
\]
The polynomial has even total degree term by term, so \(p(-t)=p(t)\).
It therefore vanishes at every point of the Cartesian grid
\(L^{-1}Z_L\). Its degree in each variable is at most \(n\), whereas
that grid has \(2L+1>n\) distinct values in each variable. Repeated
univariate interpolation, one variable at a time, forces \(p\) to be
the zero polynomial. Thus every \(\lambda_\alpha=0\). The reflected
vectors span \(\mathbb R^{d_n}\), and because the cloud contains zero,
its affine rank is \(d_n\).

For the full cloud at zero, (8) gives \(\alpha_v^0=a_v\), and one
representative from each nonzero reflected pair has total volume
\((W-a_0)/2\). Equation (9) gives denominator \(d_n+1\) in (10),
proving the exact term (11). The elementary binomial bounds
\[
 \frac13<\gamma_k\le\frac12\qquad(k\ge1)
\tag{24}
\]
follow from \(2\le(1+1/k)^k<3\); the same numerical inequality is also
recorded in accepted fact 41c6cbb26797981c. In particular
\(\gamma_{d_n}-\gamma_{2d_n}/2>0\), so the numerator in (11) is at least
\(\gamma_{2d_n}W/2\), while it is at most \(W/2\). This proves (12).

If \(n\) is even, (2) gives
\[
 d_n\ge\binom{2n-1}{n}=\frac12\binom{2n}{n}
 \ge\frac{4^n}{2(2n+1)}.
\tag{25}
\]
If \(n\) is odd, it gives
\[
 d_n\ge\binom{2n-2}{n-1}
 \ge\frac{4^{n-1}}{2n-1}.
\tag{26}
\]
The last inequalities follow because the central binomial coefficient is
the largest of the \(2m+1\) coefficients whose sum is \(4^m\).
Equations (25)-(26) imply \(2^n/(d_n+1)\to0\), and hence the final claim
in (12). Since (10) is a supremum over all \(\mu,J\), no upper bound on
that supremum follows from this calculation.

We next prove (12a). Let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_{d_n})\) be uniform on
\([-1,1]^{d_n}\). The support function of the centered half-mass
metronoid of this cube is
\[
 h(u)=\mathbb E|\langle u,X\rangle|.
\tag{31a}
\]
Indeed, the maximizing half-volume density in direction \(u\ne0\) is
the indicator of the upper half-cube
\(\{\langle u,x\rangle\ge0\}\), and symmetry converts its normalized
first moment into the displayed absolute first moment. Put
\(Y:=\langle u,X\rangle\). Independence and
\(\mathbb EX_i^2=1/3\), \(\mathbb EX_i^4=1/5\) give
\[
 \mathbb EY^2=\frac13\|u\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb EY^4=
 \frac13\|u\|_2^4-\frac2{15}\sum_i u_i^4
 \le\frac13\|u\|_2^4.
\tag{31b}
\]
Hölder's inequality in the form
\[
 \mathbb EY^2
 \le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Y|^4)^{1/3}
\]
therefore yields \(h(u)\ge\|u\|_2/3\). Support-function containment
gives
\[
 \mathsf M_{2^{d_n-1}}([-1,1]^{d_n})
 \supseteq\frac13B_2^{d_n}.
\tag{31c}
\]
Each \(C_z\) is the affine image of this cube with diagonal semiaxes
equal to its coordinate half-widths. By (3), every such semiaxis is at
least \(\Lambda-1\). Affine covariance of metronoids and (31c) prove
(12a). Moreover \(\|c_z\|_2\le\sqrt{d_n}\), because every coordinate in
(5) has absolute value at most one. Thus every edge-center
\((c_u+c_v)/2\) has norm at most \(\sqrt{d_n}\), and (12a) proves that
zero belongs to every \(L_{\{u,v\}}\) under the displayed large-width
condition. The constant affine map proves (12b).

Finally, division of (6) by its value at zero gives, for \(s=z/L\),
\[
 \frac{a_z}{a_0}
 =
 \prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}
 \left(1-\frac{\sum_i\alpha_i s_i^2}
 {\Lambda|\alpha|}\right).
\tag{31d}
\]
Every factor lies in \((0,1]\). Using \(1-x\le e^{-x}\), permutation
symmetry of \(\mathcal A_n\), and
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}\frac{\alpha_i}{|\alpha|}
 =\frac1n\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}
 \frac{\sum_i\alpha_i}{|\alpha|}
 =\frac{d_n}{n},
\]
proves (12c). This upper profile alone does not supply the lower mass
or complete-core compatibility needed to bound \(G/W\); both are
provided next.

Assume (12d). Since \(r_*<1\), every \(z\in E_*\), with \(s=z/L\),
satisfies
\[
 \|c_z\|_2\le\sqrt{d_n}\,r_*^2.
\tag{31e}
\]
Indeed every monomial has degree at least two, so every coordinate of
\(c_z\) has absolute value at most \(r_*^2\). For
\(\alpha\in\mathcal A_n\), its coordinate half-width at such \(s\) is
at least
\[
 \frac{m_\alpha(m_\alpha-1)}2(\Lambda-r_*^2)
 \ge\Lambda-r_*^2
 =3\sqrt{d_n}\,r_*^2.
\tag{31f}
\]
The affine-cube argument proving (12a), now using the local lower bound
in (31f), shows that
\[
 \mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z
 \supseteq\sqrt{d_n}\,r_*^2 B_2^{d_n}.
\]
Together with (31e), this proves
\[
 0\in\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\qquad(z\in E_*).
\tag{31g}
\]
Use in the definition of \(G\) from accepted fact 3d1f63abf09b2abb
the symmetric support \(E_*\), center
zero, and masses \(t_z=a_z/2\). Every barycentric Minkowski sum occurring
in the complete core contains zero by (31g), so their intersection
contains zero. Also \(t_z=t_{-z}\). Every closed base halfspace
containing zero contains at least one point from each pair
\(\{z,-z\}\), and it contains the central point. Its selected mass is
therefore at least half the total selected mass, which is stronger than
the required \(2^{-n}\) balance. Hence
\[
 G\ge\sum_{z\in E_*}\frac{a_z}{2}=\frac{W_*}{2}.
\tag{31h}
\]

It remains to prove that \(E_*\) captures the mass uniformly. Put
\[
 T:=\frac{r_*}{\rho_*}
 =\sqrt{\frac{2d_n}{n(1+3\sqrt{d_n})}}.
\tag{31i}
\]
Equations (25)-(26) imply \(T\to\infty\), so for all sufficiently large
\(n\), \(\rho_*\le r_*<1\). Define the inner lattice box
\[
 I_*:=\{z\in Z_L:|z_i|\le L\rho_*\ \text{for all }i\}.
\]
For \(z\in I_*\), each number
\[
 x_\alpha:=
 \frac{\sum_i\alpha_i(z_i/L)^2}{\Lambda|\alpha|}
\]
is at most \(n/(2d_n)\le1/2\), and
\[
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}x_\alpha
 =\frac{d_n}{n\Lambda}\left\|\frac zL\right\|_2^2
 \le\frac n2.
\]
Since \(\log(1-x)\ge-2x\) on \([0,1/2]\), equation (31d) gives
\[
 a_z\ge e^{-n}a_0\qquad(z\in I_*).
\tag{31j}
\]

Write \(t:=L\rho_*>0\), \(N(t):=2\lfloor t\rfloor+1\), and
\[
 S(t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-k^2/(2t^2)}.
\]
Monotonicity and integration of the Gaussian give
\[
 S(t)\le1+\sqrt{2\pi}\,t\le4N(t)
\qquad(t>0).
\tag{31k}
\]
There are \(N(t)^n\) points in \(I_*\), so (31j) gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in I_*}a_z\ge a_0e^{-n}N(t)^n.
\tag{31l}
\]
For the one-dimensional tail, splitting the Gaussian exponent in half
gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U(t,T)
 &:=\sum_{|k|>Tt}e^{-k^2/(2t^2)}\\
 &\le e^{-T^2/4}
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-k^2/(4t^2)}
 =e^{-T^2/4}S(\sqrt2\,t)
 \le16N(t)e^{-T^2/4}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31m}
\]
For the last inequality, (31k) is applied at \(\sqrt2\,t\), and
\(N(\sqrt2\,t)\le4N(t)\). The profile upper bound (12c), a union bound
over the coordinate leaving \(E_*\), and (31k)-(31m) now yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W-W_*
 &\le a_0\,n\,U(t,T)S(t)^{n-1}\\
 &\le a_0\,4n\,4^nN(t)^n e^{-T^2/4}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31n}
\]
Since \(1+3\sqrt{d_n}\le4\sqrt{d_n}\),
\[
 \frac{T^2}{4}
 =\frac{d_n}{2n(1+3\sqrt{d_n})}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{8n}.
\tag{31o}
\]
Dividing (31n) by (31l), and using
\(\sum_{z\in I_*}a_z\le W_*\), proves
\[
 W-W_*\le
 4n(4e)^n
 \exp\left(-\frac{\sqrt{d_n}}{8n}\right)W_*
 =\eta_nW_*.
\]
Equations (25)-(26) show that the negative exponential term dominates
\(n\log(4e)+\log(4n)\), so \(\eta_n\to0\). Together with (31h), this
proves (12e)-(12f).

For all sufficiently large \(n\), take \(\eta_n\le3\). Under (12d),
(12e) gives \(G\ge W/8\), so assertion 1 of accepted fact
41c6cbb26797981c proves (12g). If (12d) fails and \(G\ge W/8\), the
same assertion applies. If (12d) fails and \(G<W/8\), accepted fact

accepted fact 41c6cbb26797981c is then at least \(Q>Q/100\), so that
assertion proves (12g). This proves the uniform asymptotic exclusion.

For \(n=4\), degrees two and four contribute respectively
\(\binom52=10\) and \(\binom74=35\) coordinates, proving (13).
Substitution of \(L=4\) in (6) gives (14), and substitution of \(z=0\)
gives (15). In the definition of the complete metronoid-core capacity,

\(t_0=a_0\). Its complete core is the nonempty full-mass metronoid, and

Hence \(G\ge a_0\). Conversely every admissible core tuple has
\(\sum_{z\in E}t_z\le\sum_{z\in E}a_z\le W\). Combining these facts
with (7) proves (16).

6b09aae189415c70 applies and gives
\[
 Q\ge\frac3{32}(W-4G)>\frac{3W}{64}.
\tag{27}

For each good parity class,

 \sum_{u<v}r_{\{u,v\}}
 =\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}
 \le\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
\tag{28}
\]
Summing proves \(Q\le W/8\). In one coordinate of
