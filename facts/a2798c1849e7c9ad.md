## Statement

All logarithms are natural except \(\log_2\). For each of the laws below, on its displayed finite support \(A\subset\mathbb Z^D\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace a\in A:2j-a\in A\rbrace,&
H_p(j)&=\sum_{a\in R_j}\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}},\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace a\in R_j:p_a=p_j\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{a\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}},\\
b_{\mathrm{equal}}(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2,&
b_{\mathrm{full}}(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\mathcal E(p)&=4^D b_{\mathrm{equal}}(p),&
\mathcal F(p)&=4^D b_{\mathrm{full}}(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For the affine height laws used here, the condition in \(\widehat R_j\) is equivalent to equality of the endpoint heights with the center height. The same equivalence holds for the selector mixtures below, because every reflected row remains within one selector fibre.

Let \(L\ge1\), \(m\ge64\), and \(B\ge1\) be integers, with \(2^{L+1}\) dividing \(m\). Let \(U\) be any nonempty subset of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), and put \(N=|U|\), \(n=m+1\). For \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(S_\ell\) be the first \(m/2^{\ell+1}\) coordinates, let \(s_\ell\) be its indicator, and put
$$
c_0=0,\qquad c_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}s_h,\qquad
v_\ell=(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell,0).
$$
Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B&=(U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)\cup(U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup(-U\times\lbrace2B\rbrace),\\
Q&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\mathcal B\cup\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L\rbrace\bigr),
&A&=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Assume only the parity occupancy condition
\(\bigl|A\cap(a+2\mathbb Z^n)\bigr|\le2\) for every \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\).
Let \(t>0\), \(\lambda=e^{-t}\), and \(\beta=e^{-tB}\le1/4096\). Retain the entire original law and its ambient and occupied capacities:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(x,z)&=z,&w_a&=e^{-tH(a)},&
Z&=\sum_{a\in A}w_a,&p_a&=w_a/Z,\\
K_{\mathrm a}&=2^n,&
K_{\mathrm o}&=\bigl|\lbrace a+2\mathbb Z^n:a\in A\rbrace\bigr|,&
W_3&=\sum_{a\in A}w_a^3,&
E&=\mathcal E(p),\quad F=\mathcal F(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
In particular \(E=K_{\mathrm a}^2b_{\mathrm{equal}}(p)\) and
\(F=K_{\mathrm a}^2b_{\mathrm{full}}(p)\); neither uses \(K_{\mathrm o}\).

Put \(D_{\mathrm{bound}}=L+4\cdot2^L\) and
\(\mathcal C=A\setminus(\mathcal B\cup\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L\rbrace)\).
Then the actual extra atoms satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal C|&\le4\cdot2^L,&
|A|&=3N+L+|\mathcal C|\le3N+D_{\mathrm{bound}},&
D_{\mathrm{bound}}&\le3m,\\
Z&=N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+L+\sum_{a\in\mathcal C}e^{-tH(a)}
\le N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+D_{\mathrm{bound}},\\
W_3&\ge N(1+\lambda^3),&
E_{\mathrm{at}}&:=K_{\mathrm a}^2W_3/Z^3\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
No assertion that all same-weight rows are singletons is needed in (4).
If \(E<1\), then
$$
\begin{gathered}
N>\frac{29}{32}2^m,\qquad m>\frac B4,\qquad
\frac{D_{\mathrm{bound}}}{N}<\beta^2,\qquad
\lambda>\frac{1023}{1024},\\
t<\min\lbrace1/1023,6\beta\rbrace,\qquad
B>\beta^{-1}\ge4096,\qquad Z<4N,\\
(1+2\beta^2)^{-3}\le E<1,\qquad
F\ge\frac{N\beta^3}{4}>512.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
$$

Fix any one such active law, meaning a choice (2)--(3) with \(E<1\), and denote it by \(Q_b,A_b,p^b\), with \(E_b=E\), \(F_b=F\). Let \(Q_g=[0,1]^n\), \(A_g=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), and give its entire support the same-slope law \(p^g_{(x,z)}=e^{-tz}/Z_g\). For an integer \(\nu\ge0\), put \(V=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^\nu\), \(J=2^\nu\), choose any map \(\tau:V\to\lbrace b,g\rbrace\), and choose any positive masses \(\alpha_v\) summing to one. Set
$$
\widetilde Q=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{v\in V}
(Q_{\tau(v)}\times\lbrace v\rbrace),\qquad
\widetilde A=\widetilde Q\cap\mathbb Z^{n+\nu}.
$$
Then
\(\widetilde A=\bigcup_{v\in V}(A_{\tau(v)}\times\lbrace v\rbrace)\),
so \(\widetilde p_{(a,v)}=\alpha_vp^{\tau(v)}_a\) is a probability law on the complete support. With \(E_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal E(\widetilde p)\) and \(F_{\mathrm{total}}=\mathcal F(\widetilde p)\), the exact identities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\mathrm{total}}&=J^2\sum_{v\in V}\alpha_v^3E_{\tau(v)},&
F_{\mathrm{total}}&=J^2\sum_{v\in V}\alpha_v^3F_{\tau(v)},\\
E_g&=F_g=4\,\frac{1+\lambda^3}{(1+\lambda)^3}>1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
For every choice of \(\nu,\tau,\alpha\), \(E_{\mathrm{total}}<1\) implies \(F_{\mathrm{total}}>9/8\). More precisely, when both types occur, put
\(\eta=|\tau^{-1}(b)|/J\) and \(x=\sum_{\tau(v)=b}\alpha_v\). Then
$$
E_{\mathrm{total}}<1
\ \Longrightarrow\
\eta>\frac{3t^2}{256\beta^2+3t^2}>
\frac{3t^2}{448\beta^2},\qquad
x>\frac23\eta,\qquad
F_{\mathrm{total}}>\frac8{27}\eta F_b>
\frac{Nt^2\beta}{2016}>\frac98.
\tag{7}
$$
If every block is of type \(b\), then \(F_{\mathrm{total}}\ge F_b>512\).
If every block is of type \(g\), then \(E_{\mathrm{total}}\ge E_g>1\).
These cases include \(\nu=0\). The arbitrary masses are a relaxation: the conclusion covers every globally affine placement with these conditional laws, without asserting that every mass vector has such a placement.

The original exact five-height clause also holds for all integers \(m\ge4\) divisible by four. Namely, let \(b\ge5\) be an integer, let \(s\) indicate the first \(m/4\) coordinates, and let \(U\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\setminus\lbrace0,s\rbrace\) be nonempty. Suppose
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_5&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)\cup(U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup(-U\times\lbrace4b\rbrace)\cup\lbrace v\rbrace\bigr),\\
v&=(2s,0),\qquad j_0=(0,2b),\qquad j_1=(s,b),\\
A_5&=Q_5\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1}
=(U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)\cup(U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup(-U\times\lbrace4b\rbrace)\cup\lbrace v,j_0,j_1\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
One may additionally require \(Q_5\) to be full-dimensional, as in the original test; the implication below does not need that extra assumption. Let \(t>0\), \(\beta_0=e^{-tb}\le1/4096\), \(\lambda=e^{-t}\), \(N=|U|\), and give \(A_5\) its full law \(p^5_a=e^{-tz_a}/Z_5\). Write \(E_5=\mathcal E(p^5)\), \(F_5=\mathcal F(p^5)\), \(W_{3,5}=\sum_{a\in A_5}e^{-3tz_a}\), and \(K_{\mathrm a}=2^{m+1}\). Its occupied count is \(K_{\mathrm o,5}=2N+2<K_{\mathrm a}\), every same-weight row is a singleton, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_5&=N(1+\lambda+\beta_0^4)+1+\beta_0+\beta_0^2,\\
W_{3,5}&=N(1+\lambda^3+\beta_0^{12})+1+\beta_0^3+\beta_0^6,\\
E_5&=K_{\mathrm a}^2W_{3,5}/Z_5^3,\\
F_5&=E_5+\frac{K_{\mathrm a}^2}{Z_5^3}
\bigl(4N(N+1)\beta_0^6+8\beta_0^3\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
If \(E_5<1\), then
$$
N>\frac{15}{16}2^m,\qquad m>b,\qquad
t<6\beta_0^2,\qquad b>\beta_0^{-2}\ge4096^2.
\tag{10}
$$
In particular \(m>64\), and (2)--(7) apply to this same law with
\(L=1\), \(B=2b\), \(s_1=s\), \(v_1=v\), and \(\beta=\beta_0^2\).
Thus \(F_5\ge N\beta_0^6/4>512\), and its same-slope cube dilution is also excluded from \(E_{\mathrm{total}}<1<F_{\mathrm{total}}\le9/8\).

The family in accepted fact 1872f0ebbf46aa8a is an explicit specialization of (2)--(7), under the parameter and capacity map proved below. Its band and special profile-height envelope are not hypotheses of the arbitrary-\(U\) conclusions.

## Proof

We first prove the support estimate for arbitrary \(U\). Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}(U)\). Any point of \(Q\) has a representation
$$
\begin{gathered}
x=\alpha u+\gamma u'-\theta w+
\sum_{\ell=1}^L\delta_\ell(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell),
\qquad z=\gamma+2B\theta,\\
u,u',w\in P,\qquad
\alpha,\gamma,\theta,\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_L\ge0,\qquad
\alpha+\gamma+\theta+\delta=1,\qquad
\delta=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\delta_\ell.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
$$
Unused choices from the nonempty set \(P\) may be made arbitrarily. All heights lie in \([0,2B]\).

If \(\delta=0\) and \(0<\theta<1\), every horizontal coordinate lies in \([-\theta,1-\theta]\), whose only integer is zero. Thus an integral point of this type has horizontal coordinate zero. If \(\delta=0\) and \(\theta=0\), an integral horizontal coordinate vector belongs to \(P\cap\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m=U\), and the integral height in \([0,1]\) is zero or one. To justify the equality \(P\cap\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m=U\), a convex combination of Boolean vectors can equal a Boolean vector only if every positively weighted vector agrees with it in every coordinate. If \(\delta=0,\theta=1\), the same argument gives \(x\in-U\) and \(z=2B\).

Partition \(S_1\) into the nonempty blocks
\(D_h=S_h\setminus S_{h+1}\) for \(h<L\), and \(D_L=S_L\).
All added horizontal vertices vanish outside \(S_1\). Their common value on \(D_h\) is
$$
V_\ell(h)=
\begin{cases}
h,&h<\ell,\\
\ell+1,&h\ge\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
$$
The block-value map identifies integer vectors constant on these blocks and zero elsewhere with \(\mathbb Z^L\).

The simplex spanned by \(V_1,\ldots,V_L\) has no integer points other than these vertices. This is immediate for \(L=1\). For \(L\ge2\), let \(d_h=V_h-V_{h+1}\), \(1\le h<L\). The first \(L-1\) coordinates of the matrix with columns \(d_h\) form a lower triangular integer matrix with diagonal entries one: \(d_h\) vanishes before coordinate \(h\), equals one at coordinate \(h\), and equals minus one thereafter. Consequently an integer vector in their real span has integer coefficients in this basis, by successive solution of its first \(L-1\) coordinates. If \(y=\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V_\ell\) is integral with \(\delta_\ell\ge0\) and \(\sum_\ell\delta_\ell=1\), then
\(y-V_L=\sum_{h=1}^{L-1}(\delta_1+\cdots+\delta_h)d_h\).
All cumulative coefficients are integers in \([0,1]\), so this nondecreasing sequence consists of zeros followed by ones. Exactly one \(\delta_\ell\) is one. This proves the assertion, including all boundary faces. In (11), the case \(\delta=1\) therefore gives only \(v_1,\ldots,v_L\).

Consider now an integral point represented with \(0<\delta<1\). Outside \(S_1\) its coordinates lie in \([-\theta,1-\theta-\delta]\), strictly between minus one and one, and thus vanish. On \(D_h\), write \(a_h=\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V_\ell(h)\). All coordinates in that block lie in
\([a_h-\theta,a_h+1-\theta-\delta]\), of length \(1-\delta<1\).
They therefore have one common integer value \(g_h\).
Since \(a_1=\delta+\delta_1\), its lower endpoint is at least \(2\delta-1>-1\), and its upper endpoint is \(1+\delta_1-\theta<2\). Hence \(g_1\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\).
For \(h<L\), (12) gives
\(a_{h+1}-a_h=2\delta_{h+1}+\sum_{\ell>h+1}\delta_\ell\in[0,2\delta]\).
Subtracting the two intervals gives
$$
-1<(a_{h+1}-a_h)-(1-\delta)
\le g_{h+1}-g_h
\le(a_{h+1}-a_h)+(1-\delta)<2.
$$
Thus every increment \(g_{h+1}-g_h\) is zero or one. With
\(e_1=g_1\) and \(e_h=g_h-g_{h-1}\), the horizontal coordinate is
\(x(e)=\sum_{h=1}^Le_hs_h\) for \(e\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L\).
This includes the zero profile found when \(\delta=0,0<\theta<1\).

We have proved that every lattice point is in \(\mathcal B\), is an added vertex, or has one of at most \(2^L\) staircase horizontal profiles \(x(e)\). For each fixed profile, the ambient parity has only two possibilities, according to the parity of the integer height. The occupancy hypothesis permits at most two atoms of each of those parities. There are therefore at most four actual heights at each fixed profile. This proves \(|\mathcal C|\le4\cdot2^L\); it does not presume that any optional profile or height occurs.
The \(3N\) atoms of \(\mathcal B\) are distinct, since \(2B\ge2\). The added vertices are distinct from them and from each other: they have a coordinate at least two, and their profiles (12) differ. Thus the exact cardinality and exact normalizer in (4) count every atom once. Because all heights are nonnegative, every weight in the remaining sum is at most one. Also \(L\le2^L\le m/2\), so \(L+4\cdot2^L\le(5/2)m\le3m\). The two lower Boolean layers prove the bound on \(W_3\).

For any reflected pair, affine height gives
\(w_aw_{2j-a}=w_j^2\), hence \(\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}}=p_j\).
In particular \(\widehat R_j\) contains \(j\), and
\(\widehat H_p(j)=|\widehat R_j|p_j\ge p_j\).
This proves \(E\ge E_{\mathrm{at}}\) in (4), without identifying the two energies.

Assume \(E<1\). Hölder's inequality on the complete probability law gives
\(1=(\sum_{a\in A}p_a)^3\le |A|^2\sum_{a\in A}p_a^3\).
Together with \(E\ge K_{\mathrm a}^2\sum p_a^3\), this implies \(|A|>K_{\mathrm a}=2^{m+1}\).
For all real \(m\ge64\), \(6m\le2^{m/2}\): it holds at 64, and the logarithmic derivative of \(2^{m/2}/m\) is \((\log2)/2-1/m>0\), since \(\log2>1/2\).
The latter strict inequality follows by integrating \(1/x>1/2\) over \(1\le x<2\).
In particular \(3m\le2^{m-1}\), so (4) gives
$$
3N>2^{m+1}-3m\ge3\cdot2^{m-1},\qquad
N>2^{m-1}.
\tag{13}
$$
Put \(\epsilon=D_{\mathrm{bound}}/N\), and
\(\delta_*=\beta^2+\epsilon\). Then
$$
0<\epsilon<6m\,2^{-m}\le2^{-m/2}\le2^{-32},\qquad
0<\delta_*<2^{-24}+2^{-32}<2^{-23}.
\tag{14}
$$
Since \(N\le2^m\), \(K_{\mathrm a}\ge2N\). Using (4) in \(E<1\) yields
\(4(1+\lambda^3)<(1+\lambda+\delta_*)^3\).
The exact identity
\(4(1+\lambda^3)-(1+\lambda)^3=3(1+\lambda)(1-\lambda)^2\)
therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
3(1+\lambda)(1-\lambda)^2
&<3(1+\lambda)^2\delta_*+
3(1+\lambda)\delta_*^2+\delta_*^3\\
&\le19\delta_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
The last bound uses \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(0<\delta_*<1\).
Thus
\((1-\lambda)^2<(19/3)2^{-23}<2^{-20}\),
so \(\lambda>1023/1024\), and
$$
t=\int_\lambda^1\frac{dx}{x}
\le\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
<\frac1{1023}<\frac{\log2}{16}.
\tag{16}
$$
For the last comparison, \(\log2>1/2\) and \(1/1023<1/32\) suffice.

The same atomic lower bound also yields density close to one. Put
\(\vartheta=N/2^m\), so \(K_{\mathrm a}=2N/\vartheta\). Then
$$
\vartheta^2>
\frac{4(1+\lambda^3)}{(1+\lambda+\delta_*)^3}
\ge\frac{(1+\lambda)^3}{(1+\lambda+\delta_*)^3}
\ge(1+\delta_*)^{-3}.
\tag{17}
$$
The middle inequality is the nonnegativity of the left side of the identity used in (15).
The convex function \((1+y)^{-3/2}\) lies above its tangent at zero for \(y\ge0\). Taking positive square roots in (17) therefore gives
\(\vartheta>(1+\delta_*)^{-3/2}\ge1-(3/2)\delta_*>29/32\).

We prove the geometric consequence of this density, for reuse also in (8).
For any \(U\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) of density greater than \(29/32\), its convex hull contains
\(x_\pm=(1/2\pm1/(4m))\mathbf1\), where \(\mathbf1\) is the all-one vector.
Let \(X\) be uniform on the Boolean cube, and fix a nonzero \(\xi\in\mathbb R^m\).
Put \(Y=\xi\cdot(X-\mathbf1/2)\) and
\(\sigma^2=\frac14\sum_i\xi_i^2>0\).
Independence of the uniform signs \(2X_i-1\), with every odd sign moment zero, gives
$$
\mathbb E Y^2=\sigma^2,\qquad
\mathbb E Y^4
=\frac1{16}\left(\sum_i\xi_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}\xi_i^2\xi_j^2\right)
=3\sigma^4-\frac18\sum_i\xi_i^4\le3\sigma^4.
\tag{18}
$$
On \(|Y|<\sigma/2\), \(Y^2\le\sigma^2/4\).
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\((3/4)\sigma^2\le
\mathbb E[Y^2\mathbf1_{\lbrace|Y|\ge\sigma/2\rbrace}]
\le(\mathbb E Y^4\,\mathbb P(|Y|\ge\sigma/2))^{1/2}\).
Hence \(\mathbb P(|Y|\ge\sigma/2)\ge3/16\).
The involution \(X\mapsto\mathbf1-X\) negates \(Y\), so
\(\mathbb P(Y\ge\sigma/2)\ge3/32\), including the equality boundary.
For either sign,
$$
\xi\cdot(x_\pm-\mathbf1/2)
\le\frac{\sum_i|\xi_i|}{4m}
\le\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt m}\le\frac{\sigma}{2}.
\tag{19}
$$
Every closed halfspace containing either \(x_\pm\) consequently contains at least \(3/32\) of the Boolean vertices. Indeed, writing it as \(\lbrace x:\xi\cdot x\ge a\rbrace\) with nonzero normal, its containing \(x_\pm\) means \(a\le\xi\cdot x_\pm\), and (19) puts every vertex with \(Y\ge\sigma/2\) in that halfspace.
If \(x_\pm\notin P\), choose a nearest point \(y\in P\) and let \(\xi=x_\pm-y\). Minimizing squared distance along each segment from \(y\) to \(u\in P\) gives \(\xi\cdot(u-y)\le0\), whereas
\(\xi\cdot(x_\pm-y)=\lVert\xi\rVert^2>0\).
A closed halfspace whose threshold is strictly between \(\xi\cdot y\) and \(\xi\cdot x_\pm\) contains \(x_\pm\) and is disjoint from \(P\). It would contain at least \(3/32\) of all Boolean vertices but no member of \(U\), contradicting density greater than \(29/32\). This proves the convex-hull assertion without any symmetry assumption on \(U\).

Apply it to the density in (17). The points \((x_\pm,0)\) and \((-x_\pm,2B)\) are in \(Q\). Their convex combinations give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac12-\frac1{4m}\right)(x_+,0)
+\left(\frac12+\frac1{4m}\right)(-x_-,2B)
&=(0,B+B/(2m)),\\
\left(\frac12+\frac1{4m}\right)(x_-,0)
+\left(\frac12-\frac1{4m}\right)(-x_+,2B)
&=(0,B-B/(2m)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Thus the entire closed segment
\(\lbrace0\rbrace\times[B-B/(2m),B+B/(2m)]\) lies in \(Q\).
If \(B/(2m)\ge2\), it contains the five lattice points \((0,B-2),\ldots,(0,B+2)\), three of which have the same ambient parity. This contradicts the occupancy hypothesis. Therefore \(m>B/4\), including exclusion of the equality boundary.

Combining this with (14) and (16) yields
$$
\epsilon<2^{-m/2}<2^{-B/8}<e^{-2tB}=\beta^2,\qquad
\delta_*<2\beta^2.
\tag{21}
$$
Use this improvement in (15):
\((1-\lambda)^2<(38/3)\beta^2<16\beta^2\), so \(1-\lambda<4\beta\).
Because \(\lambda>2/3\), (16)'s integral estimate gives \(t<6\beta\).
Also \(tB=-\log\beta\ge\log4096=12\log2>6\), and hence
\(B>1/\beta\ge4096\).
Finally, \(Z\le N(1+\lambda+\delta_*)<3N<4N\).

The hub \(j_0=(0,B)\) belongs to \(A\), as the midpoint of \((u,0)\) and \((-u,2B)\) for any \(u\in U\).
Its full reflected row contains its central atom and the \(2N\) distinct endpoints of these pairs.
Every corresponding geometric summand equals \(p_{j_0}=\beta/Z\).
Retaining this row in the original full energy gives
$$
F\ge K_{\mathrm a}^2(2N+1)^2(\beta/Z)^3
\ge\frac{(2N)^2(2N)^2\beta^3}{(4N)^3}
=\frac{N\beta^3}{4}>
\frac{2^{B/4}\beta^3}{8}.
\tag{22}
$$
Here (13) and \(m>B/4\) imply the final strict inequality. For every real \(u\ge16\), \(\log_2u\le\sqrt u\): equality holds at 16, and the derivative of \(\sqrt u-\log_2u\) is
\((\sqrt u\log2-2)/(2u\log2)>0\) on that interval.
For \(u\ge256\), \(u/4\ge4\sqrt u\).
Take \(u=\beta^{-1}\ge4096\). Since \(B>u\), (22) gives
$$
\frac{N\beta^3}{4}>
\frac{2^{B/4}}{8u^3}>
\frac{2^{u/4}}{8u^3}
\ge\frac{u^4}{8u^3}
=\frac u8\ge512.
\tag{23}
$$
This proves the claimed full-energy gap.

For the remaining lower bound on \(E\), the same atomic estimate and (21) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
E&\ge\frac{4(1+\lambda^3)}{(1+\lambda+\delta_*)^3}\\
&\ge\frac{4(1+\lambda^3)}{(1+\lambda)^3}
\left(1+\frac{2\beta^2}{1+\lambda}\right)^{-3}
\ge(1+2\beta^2)^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
The last inequality uses
\(4(1+\lambda^3)/(1+\lambda)^3
=1+3(1-\lambda)^2/(1+\lambda)^2\ge1\)
and \(1+\lambda\ge1\). This completes (5).

We next prove the selector assertion. The selector projection of \(\widetilde Q\) is \([0,1]^\nu\), so a lattice point has selector \(v\in V\). A convex combination of Boolean selectors can equal \(v\) only if every positively weighted selector equals \(v\), coordinate by coordinate. Thus the fibre at \(v\) is exactly \(Q_{\tau(v)}\), proving the stated complete lattice support. Likewise, if supported endpoints with selectors \(u,u'\) reflect about a point with selector \(v\), then \(u+u'=2v\). In each coordinate the bits must all agree, so \(u=u'=v\).

Multiplication of the conditional law in that fibre by \(\alpha_v\) multiplies every geometric row summand by \(\alpha_v\), and preserves equality of any endpoint probability with the center probability. Hence
$$
H_{\widetilde p}(j,v)=\alpha_vH_{p^{\tau(v)}}(j),\qquad
\widehat H_{\widetilde p}(j,v)=\alpha_v\widehat H_{p^{\tau(v)}}(j).
\tag{25}
$$
Within a fibre the probabilities still satisfy the affine midpoint identity, and \(t>0\) makes probability equality equivalent to height equality. The ambient capacity is \(2^{n+\nu}=JK_{\mathrm a}\). Substitution of (25) into (1) proves both exact identities (6), including all central atoms and all actual extra atoms of every fibre.
On the cube every reflected row is a singleton by the same bit argument.
There are \(2^m\) atoms at height zero and \(2^m\) at height one, so \(Z_g=2^m(1+\lambda)\); substitution gives the cube energy in (6).

We quantify how much type \(b\) is necessary for \(E_{\mathrm{total}}<1\).
From (24) and \(0<E_b<1\),
$$
0<E_b^{-1/2}-1
\le(1+2\beta^2)^{3/2}-1\le4\beta^2.
\tag{26}
$$
For the last inequality, if \(0\le y\le1\), then
\((1+2y)^2-(1+y)^3=y(1+y-y^2)\ge0\).

\tag{27}

Here \(3t^2<1\) follows from \(t<6/4096\), and the displayed denominator is at most \(2+\sqrt2<4\).
