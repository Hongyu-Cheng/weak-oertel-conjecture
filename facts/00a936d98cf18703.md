## Statement

Let $p\ge1$. Suppose a nonnegative density $f$ on $\mathbb R^p$ has finite positive integral, and $q=f^{-1/(p+1)}$, with $q=+\infty$ where $f=0$ and $q=0$ where $f=+\infty$, is extended-real convex with full-dimensional domain. Then $f$ is bounded, and it has a bounded upper-semicontinuous representative whose inverse power is convex and whose support is the same closed support.

## Proof

Take a closed ball of radius $r>0$ in the interior of the domain on which $q\le C$ for a finite $C>0$, using continuity of a finite convex function in the interior of its domain. At a point $x$ with $q(x)=\varepsilon\in(0,C]$, interpolate $x$ with this ball at weight $\varepsilon/C$ on the ball. The resulting ball has radius $r\varepsilon/C$ and $q\le2\varepsilon$ throughout. Writing $\omega_p$ for the unit-ball volume, its contribution to the mass is at least
$$
\frac{\omega_p r^p(\varepsilon/C)^p}{(2\varepsilon)^{p+1}}
=\frac{\omega_p r^p}{2^{p+1}C^p\varepsilon}.
$$
The finite total mass therefore bounds every such $\varepsilon$ uniformly away from zero. If $q(x)=0$, using weight $\lambda\in(0,1)$ on the ball gives $q\le\lambda C$ on a radius-$\lambda r$ ball and mass at least $\omega_p r^p/(C^{p+1}\lambda)$, impossible as $\lambda\downarrow0$. Values greater than $C$ also satisfy a positive lower bound. Thus $f$ is bounded.

Close the epigraph of $q$. It defines a lower-semicontinuous convex function with the same positive lower bound, equal to $q$ in the interior of its domain and infinite off the closure of that domain. The boundary of the full-dimensional convex domain is Lebesgue-null. Taking its negative $(p+1)$st power, with value zero at infinity, thus gives a bounded upper-semicontinuous representative. Both densities are positive and continuous in the interior of the original domain and vanish off its closure. The closure of that interior is the essential support for both.
