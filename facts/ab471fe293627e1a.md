## Statement

For $d\ge1$ let $A,C\subset\mathbb R^d$ be full-dimensional compact convex bodies, with volumes $0<a=|A|\le b=|C|$. Place their unnormalized volume measures at bases $-1,1$ in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$. Write $p(y)$ for the infimum of this source's mass over closed ambient halfspaces containing $(0,y)$ and $\Delta(A,C')=\sup_H\big||A\cap H|-|C'\cap H|\big|$, over closed ordinary halfspaces $H$ of $\mathbb R^d$. For every $y\in\mathbb R^d$,
$$
p(y)=b-\Delta(A,2y-C).
$$
The discrepancy as a function of $y$ is globally Lipschitz with constant $2L_C$, where $L_C$ is any uniform bound on the $(d-1)$-dimensional volumes of hyperplane sections of $C$ (take $L_C=1$ for $d=1$). The maximum of $p$ is positive and attained in $\operatorname{int}((A+C)/2)$, and its maximizers equal the discrepancy minimizers. The depth vanishes outside and on the boundary of $(A+C)/2$. For every $\beta>0$, $\{y:\Delta(A,2y-C)\le b-\beta\}$ is compact convex, allowing the empty set.

## Proof

Moving the boundary of a containing closed cap parallel until it passes through $(0,y)$ can only decrease its mass. A through-point cap with a nonzero continuous normal has the form $u\cdot x+t z\ge u\cdot y$ with $\lVert u\rVert=1$. Write $s=u\cdot y+t$ and $H=\{x:u\cdot x\ge s\}$. At base $-1$ its trace is $H$. At base $1$ the trace on $C$ is $u\cdot x\ge 2u\cdot y-s$. After reflection $x\mapsto 2y-x$ the latter is the opposite halfspace to $H$ on $C'=2y-C$. The boundary hyperplane has zero $d$-volume in each continuous fiber, so the cap mass is $b+|A\cap H|-|C'\cap H|$. All ordinary $H$ occur by choice of $(u,t)$.

For $v(H)=|C'\cap H|-|A\cap H|$ put $D=\sup_H v(H)$ and $T=b-a$. The closed opposite halfspace has discrepancy $T-v(H)$, and some halfspace contains both supports and has discrepancy $T$. Thus $D\ge T\ge0$, and every negative $v(H)$ satisfies $-v(H)\le D-T\le D$. Hence $D=\Delta(A,C')$, and the infimum over nonvertical caps is $b-\Delta$. Pure base caps through zero take a full endpoint of mass $a$ or $b$. Since $b-\Delta\le a$, they cannot reduce that infimum.

For a fixed $H$ with unit normal $u$, changing $y$ by $h$ changes the corresponding cap of $C$ by a slab of width at most $2|u\cdot h|$ and volume at most $2L_C\lVert h\rVert$. Taking the supremum of the absolute discrepancies preserves this Lipschitz bound. The section bound exists, for example by placing $C$ in a ball. If $y$ is outside or on the boundary of $(A+C)/2$, some unit $u$ satisfies $2u\cdot y\ge h_A(u)+h_C(u)$, where $h_K$ is the support function. Choose a threshold $s\in[h_A(u),2u\cdot y-h_C(u)]$. The nonvertical cap with this $s$ has zero mass on both endpoints, even if its traces touch support faces.

For $a_0\in\operatorname{int}A$ and $c_0\in\operatorname{int}C$, the midpoint of $(-1,a_0)$ and $(1,c_0)$ lies on the middle plane. Every containing cap contains at least one of those endpoints, since its defining affine function is nonnegative at their midpoint. Its trace at that endpoint takes at least half the volume of a fixed ball contained in the corresponding body and centered at the endpoint's continuous coordinate. The smaller of the two ball half-volumes is positive. Continuity, compactness of $(A+C)/2$ and zero boundary depth now give the claimed interior extrema. For convexity of a depth superlevel, any closed halfspace containing a point of the segment between two qualifying points contains at least one of them and thus has mass at least $\beta$. Closedness follows from continuity, and positive superlevels lie inside the compact Minkowski midpoint body.
