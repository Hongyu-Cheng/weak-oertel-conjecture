## Statement

Let \(r\geq1\) be an integer, let \(0<b_i\leq1\) for \(1\leq i\leq r\), and put \(B=\sum_i b_i\), \(q_i=2^{b_i-1}\), and

\[
D=\left\{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{r}:
\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_i k_i<B+2\right\}.
\]

The probability measure on \(D\) proportional to \(\prod_i q_i^{k_i}\) satisfies

\[
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}
\left(\sum_i b_i k_i\right)\prod_i q_i^{k_i}}
{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}\prod_i q_i^{k_i}}
\geq B.
\]

Equality holds if and only if all \(b_i=1\).

Consequently, for every integer \(r\geq0\), independent random variables \(K_i\) satisfying \(\mathbb P(K_i=k)=2^{-k-1}\) for \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), and coefficients \(0<b_i\leq1\), the centered sum \(Z=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_i(K_i-1)\), with the empty sum interpreted as \(0\), satisfies \(\mathbb E\min(2^Z,4)\geq1\).

Moreover, let \(m\geq1\), let \(0<a_i\leq1\), and let \(Y=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_iK_i\) for independent variables of the same geometric law. Put \(\mu=\sum_i a_i=\mathbb EY\) and \(c=\max_i a_i\). Then, for every real \(t\geq0\),

\[
\mathbb P(Y>\mu+ct)\geq2^{-t-2}.
\]

In particular, \(\mathbb P(Y\leq\mu)\leq3/4\) and \(\mathbb P(Y>\mu+1)\geq2^{-1/c}/4\).

## Proof

First, the function \(b\mapsto\log(2-b)+(b-1)\log2\) has second derivative \(-1/(2-b)^2<0\) on \([0,1]\), and its values at both endpoints are \(0\). Hence \((2-b)2^{b-1}\geq1\) for \(0\leq b\leq1\), with strict inequality for \(0<b<1\). In particular,

\[
\frac1{2-b_i}\leq q_i\leq1.
\tag{1}
\]

We prove the finite weighted-mean assertion using only (1). Thus the same finite assertion also holds for any positive weights \(q_i\) satisfying (1), although its equality characterization below concerns the specified weights \(q_i=2^{b_i-1}\).

Write \(z(\mathbf k)=\sum_i b_i k_i-B\), so that \(D=\{\mathbf k:z(\mathbf k)<2\}\). The set \(D\) is finite because every \(b_i>0\). For an integer vector \(\boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}^{r}\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
&=\prod_{i=1}^{r}
\binom{k_i+\alpha_i-1}{k_i}q_i^{k_i},
&A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}&=\sum_i\alpha_i b_i,\\
M_{\boldsymbol\alpha}
&=\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k),
&T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}&=\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}
z(\mathbf k)w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, \(M_{\boldsymbol\alpha}>0\) and \(A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\geq B\). The desired assertion is \(T_{\mathbf1}\geq0\), where \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\). We will prove \(T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\geq0\) for every positive integer shape vector, with the original \(B\) and \(D\) held fixed throughout.

We first establish eventual nonnegativity as the total shape increases. Put \(D_- =\{\mathbf k\in D:z(\mathbf k)<0\}\) and \(D_+ =\{\mathbf k\in D:z(\mathbf k)>0\}\). The set \(D_-\) contains \(\mathbf0\). The set \(D_+\) is nonempty: for any \(i\), the vector \((\lfloor B/b_i\rfloor+1)\mathbf e_i\) has its \(z\)-value in \((0,b_i]\subset(0,1]\). Consequently \(\eta=\min_{\mathbf k\in D_+}z(\mathbf k)>0\).

For \(\mathbf k\in D_-\) and any \(i\), let \(L_i(\mathbf k)=\lfloor-z(\mathbf k)/b_i\rfloor+1\). Then \(z(\mathbf k+L_i(\mathbf k)\mathbf e_i)\in(0,b_i]\), so this new vector belongs to \(D_+\). Writing \(L=L_i(\mathbf k)\), the ratio of its weight to the old weight is

\[
\frac{w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k+L\mathbf e_i)}
{w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)}
=q_i^L\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}
\frac{k_i+\alpha_i+\ell-1}{k_i+\ell}
\geq\alpha_i q_i^L\frac{k_i!}{(k_i+L)!}.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, the first numerator factor is at least \(\alpha_i\), and each remaining numerator factor is at least \(1\). Since there are only finitely many pairs \((i,\mathbf k)\) with \(\mathbf k\in D_-\), the number

\[
\gamma=\min_{1\leq i\leq r,\ \mathbf k\in D_-}
q_i^{L_i(\mathbf k)}
\frac{k_i!}{(k_i+L_i(\mathbf k))!}
\]

is strictly positive. For any fixed \(i\), summing (3), with multiplicities bounded by \(|D_-|\), gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf k\in D_-}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
\leq\frac{|D_-|}{\gamma\alpha_i}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in D_+}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k).
\]

Because \(-B\leq z(\mathbf k)<0\) on \(D_-\), it follows that

\[
T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}
\geq\left(\eta-\frac{B|D_-|}{\gamma\alpha_i}\right)
\sum_{\mathbf k\in D_+}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k).
\tag{4}
\]

Choose an integer \(N\geq r\) such that \(N/r\geq B|D_-|/(\gamma\eta)\). Whenever \(\sum_i\alpha_i\geq N\), some coordinate has \(\alpha_i\geq N/r\), and (4) proves \(T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\geq0\). This is an analytic injection argument for the arbitrary finite set \(D\); no enumeration or numerical verification of \(D\) is used.

We next derive an identity that propagates this nonnegativity to smaller shapes. Set \(Q_i=2q_i\), so that (1) gives \(Q_i\leq2\) and \((2-b_i)Q_i\geq2\). For the auxiliary weights

\[
u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
=\prod_{i=1}^{r}
\binom{k_i+\alpha_i-1}{k_i}2^{-k_i},
\]

an index shift gives, for every finitely supported function \(f\) on \(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{r}\),

\[
\sum_{\mathbf k}u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
(k_i-\alpha_i)f(\mathbf k)
=\sum_{\mathbf k}u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
(k_i+\alpha_i)
\bigl(f(\mathbf k+\mathbf e_i)-f(\mathbf k)\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]

To check (5), the shifted first term on its right is \(2\sum_{\mathbf k} k_i u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)f(\mathbf k)\), by the ratio of two consecutive binomial weights. Subtracting the unshifted term gives its left side. All sums involved in this calculation are finite.

Apply (5) with \(f(\mathbf k)=\prod_j Q_j^{k_j}(2-z(\mathbf k))_+\), where \(x_+=\max(x,0)\). This function has finite support \(D\). Moreover, \(f(\mathbf k)=f(\mathbf k+\mathbf e_i)=0\) whenever \(\mathbf k\notin D\). Multiplying (5) by \(b_i\), summing over \(i\), and using \(u_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\prod_j Q_j^{k_j}=w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
\bigl(z(\mathbf k)-(A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}-B)\bigr)
\bigl(2-z(\mathbf k)\bigr)\\
&\quad=\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
\sum_i b_i(k_i+\alpha_i)
\left[Q_i\bigl(2-z(\mathbf k)-b_i\bigr)_+
-\bigl(2-z(\mathbf k)\bigr)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For \(z<2\), the bracket in (6) equals
\((Q_i-1)(2-z)-b_iQ_i+Q_i(z+b_i-2)_+\).
Expand (6), use \(\sum_i b_i(k_i+\alpha_i)=z(\mathbf k)+B+A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\), and use
\((k_i+\alpha_i)w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)=\alpha_i w_{\boldsymbol\alpha+\mathbf e_i}(\mathbf k)\).
The quadratic terms in \(z\) cancel, leaving the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
2(A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}+1)T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}
&=\sum_i b_i(2-Q_i)\alpha_i
T_{\boldsymbol\alpha+\mathbf e_i}
+2(A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}-B)M_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\\
&\quad+\sum_i b_i\bigl((2-b_i)Q_i-2\bigr)\alpha_i
M_{\boldsymbol\alpha+\mathbf e_i}\\
&\quad+\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}w_{\boldsymbol\alpha}(\mathbf k)
\sum_i b_iQ_i(k_i+\alpha_i)
\bigl(z(\mathbf k)+b_i-2\bigr)_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Every coefficient and every term on the right of (7), except for the signed sums \(T_{\boldsymbol\alpha+\mathbf e_i}\), is nonnegative. We have already proved that all those signed sums are nonnegative when the total shape is at least \(N\). Descend through total shapes \(N-1,N-2,\ldots,r\). For a vector at each such level, its successor vectors in (7) have already been treated, so (7) and \(2(A_{\boldsymbol\alpha}+1)>0\) prove its nonnegativity. This proves \(T_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\geq0\) for every positive integer shape vector, in particular \(T_{\mathbf1}\geq0\).

For the specified weights \(q_i=2^{b_i-1}\), if some \(b_i<1\), the strict version of (1) gives \((2-b_i)Q_i-2>0\). At \(\boldsymbol\alpha=\mathbf1\), the corresponding term involving \(M_{\mathbf1+\mathbf e_i}>0\) in (7) is positive, and all other terms are nonnegative. Thus \(T_{\mathbf1}>0\). If all \(b_i=1\), then \(Q_i=2\), \(A_{\mathbf1}=B=r\), and \(z(\mathbf k)\) is an integer at most \(1\) on \(D\). Every term on the right of (7) is then zero at \(\boldsymbol\alpha=\mathbf1\), proving \(T_{\mathbf1}=0\). Division by the positive \(M_{\mathbf1}\) proves the finite weighted-mean statement and its equality characterization.

Now let \(K_i\) have the stated geometric law and put \(Z=\sum_i b_i(K_i-1)\). For \(r\geq1\), multiplying its original probability at \(\mathbf k\) by \(2^{z(\mathbf k)}\) gives

\[
\mathbb E[Z2^Z;Z<2]
=2^{-B-r}\sum_{\mathbf k\in D}
z(\mathbf k)\prod_i q_i^{k_i}
=2^{-B-r}T_{\mathbf1}\geq0.
\tag{8}
\]

The strict cut makes this a finite sum, including when some \(b_i=1\). For \(r=0\), the same expectation is \(0\).

Put \(\lambda=\log2\), \(\phi(z)=\min(2^z,4)\), and \(G(t)=\mathbb E\phi(tZ)\) for \(0\leq t\leq1\). The geometric series gives \(\mathbb EK_i=1\), hence \(\mathbb E|Z|<\infty\). Since \(\phi\) is globally Lipschitz with constant \(4\lambda\), the function \(G\) is Lipschitz and therefore absolutely continuous. At every \(0<t<1\), dominated right differentiation, with dominating random variable \(4\lambda|Z|\), gives

\[
G'(t+)
=\lambda\mathbb E[Z2^{tZ};tZ<2]
=\frac{\lambda}{t}\mathbb E[(tZ)2^{tZ};tZ<2]
\geq0.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, at an outcome with \(tZ=2\) one has \(Z>0\), so the right derivative of the capped value is zero; outcomes with \(tZ>2\) also contribute zero. This explains the strict cut in (9). Its last inequality is (8) applied to the admissible coefficients \(tb_i\). Absolute continuity and the nonnegative derivative almost everywhere imply \(G(1)\geq G(0)=1\). For the empty sum this is also immediate. This proves the cap \(4\) theorem.

Finally, relabel the coefficients in the scalar statement so that \(a_1=c\), and put \(R=\sum_{i=2}^{m}(a_i/c)K_i\) and \(Z=R-\mathbb ER\). This \(Z\) has exactly the centered-sum normalization just proved, with coefficients in \((0,1]\), and it is independent of \(K_1\). For every real \(x\), the exact geometric survival formula is

\[
\mathbb P(K_1>x)
=2^{-\max(0,\lfloor x\rfloor+1)}
\geq\min(1,2^{-x-1}).
\tag{10}
\]

The equality follows from \(\mathbb P(K_1\geq j)=2^{-j}\) for integers \(j\geq0\). For \(x<-1\) the inequality compares two values equal to \(1\); for \(x\geq-1\), it follows from \(\max(0,\lfloor x\rfloor+1)\leq x+1\).

Since \(Y/c=K_1+R\) and \(\mu/c=1+\mathbb ER\), conditioning on \(R\) and applying (10) gives, for \(t\geq0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(Y>\mu+ct)
&=\mathbb E\left[
2^{-\max(0,\lfloor1+t-Z\rfloor+1)}\right]\\
&\geq2^{-t-2}\mathbb E\min(2^Z,2^{t+2})\\
&\geq2^{-t-2}\mathbb E\min(2^Z,4)
\geq2^{-t-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking \(t=0\) and taking complements gives \(\mathbb P(Y\leq\mu)\leq3/4\). Taking \(t=1/c\) gives \(\mathbb P(Y>\mu+1)\geq2^{-1/c}/4\), as stated.
