## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let finitely many fixed positive fibers $K_z$ be $d$-simplices, possibly with different facet orientations. For a cap through $y=(z_0,x_0)$ assume every nonproper fiber is strictly full or empty, and each proper fiber has exactly one strictly excluded vertex and $d$ strictly included vertices. On such a proper fiber let $h_z>0$ be minus the excluded margin and $D_z$ the product of the $d$ positive differences between each included vertex's margin and the excluded one. The second derivative of mixed cap mass along an ambient base tilt $\delta a$, with the continuous normal fixed, is
$
-d(d-1)\sum_{\mathrm{proper}\ z}\frac{|K_z|h_z^{d-2}}{D_z}\bigl(\delta a\cdot(z-z_0)\bigr)^2 .
$
If any proper base differs from $z_0$, the cap cannot be a local minimum of mass over ambient caps through $y$. For triangular fibers in a strict sign chamber, proper traces with two included vertices have this one-excluded shape.

## Proof

A tilt by $t\delta a$ adds $t\delta a\cdot(z-z_0)$ to every vertex margin in a fiber, so differences between margins are fixed. For sufficiently small $|t|$ all signs persist. In each proper fiber the missing simplex has its excluded vertex and its $d$ intersections on the edges from that vertex to the included ones. The edge ratios are $[h_z-t\delta a\cdot(z-z_0)]$ divided by the respective old margin differences. Their product is the missing volume fraction. Subtracting it from one, multiplying by the fiber volume and differentiating twice gives the stated sum. The strictly saturated terms are locally constant. At an off-anchor proper base choose $\delta a$ with nonzero scalar product against $z-z_0$, so the second derivative is strictly negative. Along that line either the first derivative already excludes a local minimum or the strictly negative second derivative does.
