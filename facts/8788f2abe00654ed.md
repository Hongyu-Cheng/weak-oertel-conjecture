## Statement

Retain one common canonical hard-branch instantiation of accepted facts `0b93509e5294c8fe` and `c81f02314cb34f8b`, with their objects identified as follows: \(C\), its fiber volumes \(a_z\), total mixed volume \(W:=\sum_z a_z\), complete-core capacity \(G\), half-strength product reservoir \(r=(r_g)\), and reservoir mass \(Q:=\sum_g r_g>0\) are the same objects in both predecessors.  In particular,
\[
 G<\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{1}
\]
and there is a whole-label family \(\mathscr B\) of rescaled transverse-atlas labels
\[
 \sigma=(z(\sigma);e_\sigma,f_\sigma),
 \qquad e_\sigma\ne f_\sigma,
 \qquad \eta_\sigma>0,
\tag{2}
\]
whose mass is
\[
 M:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr B}\eta_\sigma
 >\frac{7Q}{800}.
\tag{3}
\]
The two parent edges of every label are original reservoir edges incident with its anchor \(z(\sigma)\), and the aggregate original-parent charges satisfy
\[
 q_g^{\mathscr B}
 :=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr B:\
 g=e_\sigma\ \mathrm{or}\ g=f_\sigma}}
 \eta_\sigma
 \leq\omega_g\leq r_g.
\tag{4}
\]

Let
\[
 \mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}
 :=\{z(\sigma):\sigma\in\mathscr B\},
 \qquad
 M_z:=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr B:\
 z(\sigma)=z}}\eta_\sigma
 \quad(z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}).
\tag{5}
\]
Then every anchor satisfies the strict canonical bound
\[
 \boxed{
 M_z<\frac{Q}{800\,2^n}.}
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}|>7\cdot2^n,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}}a_z
 \geq8M>\frac{7Q}{100}.}
\tag{7}
\]
If \(M_\xi:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B},\ z\equiv\xi}M_z\), then some parity class \(\xi\) satisfies
\[
 M_\xi>\frac{7Q}{800\,2^n}
\tag{8}
\]
and contains at least eight distinct anchors of \(\mathscr B\).  In particular, a canonical realization in which all labels in \(\mathscr B\) use one common original anchor is impossible under the hard ledger.

## Proof

We first verify that the fiberwise core bound from accepted fact `c81f02314cb34f8b` applies to the present canonical instance.  In a good parity class \(\xi\), put \(A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\).  The exact half-strength product formula gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\{u,v\}:u<v\\u,v\equiv\xi}}
 r_{\{u,v\}}
 =\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}
 \leq\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
\tag{9}
\]
The reservoir is zero on bad parity classes.  Summing (9) therefore yields
\[
 Q\leq\frac{W}{8}.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (1) and (10) imply
\[
 G<\frac{Q}{100}\leq\frac{W}{800}<\frac W2.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the hypothesis of the fiberwise conclusion in accepted fact `c81f02314cb34f8b` holds, and every positive fiber satisfies
\[
 a_z\leq2^{-n}G<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n}.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix an anchor \(z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}\).  For an original edge \(g\) incident with \(z\), define the portion of its charge owned specifically by labels anchored at \(z\):
\[
 q_{z,g}:=
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr B:\
 z(\sigma)=z,\\
 g=e_\sigma\ \mathrm{or}\ g=f_\sigma}}
 \eta_\sigma.
\tag{13}
\]
Every label in (2) has two distinct parent edges, both incident with its anchor.  Hence, before any aggregation across different anchors,
\[
 \sum_{g\ni z}q_{z,g}=2M_z.
\tag{14}
\]
For each edge, (13) is a subcharge of (4), so
\[
 0\leq q_{z,g}\leq q_g^{\mathscr B}\leq\omega_g\leq r_g.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, every anchor lies in a good parity class.  Summing (15) over its incident edges and using the exact product-reservoir degree gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2M_z
 &=\sum_{g\ni z}q_{z,g}
 \leq\sum_{g\ni z}r_g\\
 &=\frac{a_z}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_z}{A_{\xi(z)}}\right)
 \leq\frac{a_z}{4}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (12) and (16) proves
\[
 M_z\leq\frac{a_z}{8}
 <\frac{Q}{800\,2^n},
\tag{17}
\]
which is (6).

The anchor classes in (5) partition the labels, so
\[
 M=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}}M_z.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (3), (6), and (18) give
\[
 \frac{7Q}{800}<M
 <|\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}|
 \frac{Q}{800\,2^n},
\tag{19}
\]
and cancellation of \(Q/800>0\) proves the first assertion in (7).  Summing the first inequality in (17) instead gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}}a_z
 \geq8\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathscr B}}M_z
 =8M>\frac{7Q}{100},
\tag{20}
\]
which proves the second assertion in (7).

Finally, the \(2^n\) parity classes partition the anchor masses in (18).  Therefore one class \(\xi\) has
\[
 M_\xi\geq\frac{M}{2^n}
 >\frac{7Q}{800\,2^n},
\tag{21}
\]
proving (8).  By (6), each individual anchor in that class carries strictly less than \(Q/(800\,2^n)\).  Seven or fewer anchors would therefore carry strictly less than \(7Q/(800\,2^n)\), contradicting (21).  Hence that class contains at least eight anchors.  A one-anchor realization is a fortiori excluded.  This uses the actual product weights and original load ownership in (4); no arbitrary literal-parent substitute is involved.
