## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(I=\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\), and let P be any strict partial order on I. Define
$$
D_P=\bigl\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^{I}:
x_i=2,\ x_j=0\Longrightarrow i<_Pj\bigr\rbrace.
$$
Then \(D_P\) is a complete convex lattice census: \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\) is full-dimensional and \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^{I}=D_P\).

For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^{I}\), use the entire support and its original normalizer:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=\sum_{x\in D_P}e^{\ell\cdot x},&
p_x&=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z},\\
\tau_p(x)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{I}\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}p(H),&
M_r(p)&=\sum_{x\in D_P}p_x\tau_p(x)^r,
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(p(H)=\sum_{y\in D_P\cap H}p_y\). These are the original-law depth and moment normalizations of accepted fact ca7a06470b084cc4. Use its same parameters \(\delta=1/16\) and \(\rho=1+3\tanh^2(1/32)\), and define
$$
\eta=\rho^{-1/2},\qquad
c_{\mathrm{pos}}=\frac{(1-\eta)^4}{64}>0.
$$
Uniformly in n, P, and every field \(\ell\),
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{pos}}4^{-n},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{pos}}^{3/2}8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
$$
Every depth in (1) uses all original ambient containing closed halfspaces. No supported conditioning assignment or boundary point is omitted.

## Proof

We use the following auxiliary estimate proved as equation (5) in the proof of accepted fact ca7a06470b084cc4. For any strict partial order R on a finite label set J of cardinality m, any masks
\(A_i\in\lbrace\lbrace1\rbrace,\lbrace0,1\rbrace,\lbrace1,2\rbrace,\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\rbrace\), and any fields h with \(|h_i|\le\delta\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_R&=\lbrace y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^{J}:y_i=2,\ y_j=0\Longrightarrow i<_Rj\rbrace,&
E&=D_R\cap\prod_{i\in J}A_i,\\
Z_E&=\sum_{y\in E}e^{h\cdot y},&
q_E(y)&=\frac{e^{h\cdot y}}{Z_E}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(M_2(q_E)\ge4^{-m}/16\), with depth taken over all containing closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{J}\). For m=0 the support is the singleton empty assignment and its depth and moments are 1. This auxiliary estimate holds for arbitrary strict partial orders, independently of the interval-order hypothesis in that fact's concluding theorem. We use exactly its masked support, original exponential normalizer, ambient depth, and value \(\delta=1/16\). In its application below, J, R, h, E are respectively G, the induced order \(P|G\), \(\ell_G\), and \(E_\beta\).

First prove completeness. Define the bounded polytope
$$
K_P=\lbrace x\in[0,2]^{I}:
x_i-x_j\le1\text{ whenever }i\ne j\text{ and }i\not<_Pj\rbrace.
\tag{2}
$$
At an integer point of the box, one of the displayed difference inequalities fails exactly when \(x_i=2\) and \(x_j=0\). Thus \(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^{I}=D_P\). Since \(D_P\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\subseteq K_P\), this implies \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^{I}=D_P\). Moreover \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{I}\subseteq D_P\), so the convex hull is full-dimensional. The box and all inequalities in (2) are closed, and the census equality includes their integer boundary points.

Split the original coordinates into \(B=\lbrace i:|\ell_i|>\delta\rbrace\), \(G=I\setminus B\), and put \(k=|B|\), \(g=|G|\). The assignments on B that occur are exactly \(D_{P|B}\): every projection satisfies its internal constraints, and every assignment in \(D_{P|B}\) extends to D_P by setting all coordinates in G to 1. In particular, every such assignment has positive original marginal mass.

For \(\beta\in D_{P|B}\), obtain a mask \(A_{\beta,i}\) at each \(i\in G\) by starting with \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), removing 0 if there is \(j\in B\) with \(\beta_j=2\) and \(j\not<_P i\), and removing 2 if there is \(j\in B\) with \(\beta_j=0\) and \(i\not<_Pj\). All resulting masks contain 1 and belong to the four-mask family in the imported estimate. The exact conditional support on G is
\(E_\beta=D_{P|G}\cap\prod_{i\in G}A_{\beta,i}\).
Indeed, the fixed assignment \(\beta\) already satisfies the bad-bad constraints, these removals are exactly the bad-good constraints, and \(D_{P|G}\) is exactly the collection of good-good constraints. This proves the conditional support equality for every occurring assignment, including G empty.

Let \(\iota_\beta(y)\) insert \(\beta\) on B and y on G, and retain the exact normalizers
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_\beta&=\sum_{y\in E_\beta}e^{\ell_G\cdot y},&
q_\beta(y)&=\frac{e^{\ell_G\cdot y}}{Z_\beta},\\
\alpha_\beta&=\frac{e^{\ell_B\cdot\beta}Z_\beta}{Z},&
p_{\iota_\beta(y)}&=\alpha_\beta q_\beta(y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The positive \(\alpha_\beta\) sum to 1. Extend \(\alpha\) to all of \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^{B}\) by setting it to zero outside \(D_{P|B}\). Since \(|\ell_i|\le\delta\) on G, the imported estimate gives \(M_2(q_\beta)\ge4^{-g}/16\).

For every original closed halfspace H containing \(\iota_\beta(y)\), its inverse image under \(\iota_\beta\) is a closed halfspace containing y if the restricted normal on G is nonzero, and all of \(\mathbb R^{G}\) if it is zero. Therefore
\(p(H)\ge\alpha_\beta q_\beta(\iota_\beta^{-1}H)\ge\alpha_\beta\tau_{q_\beta}(y)\).
For G empty this is the singleton-atom inequality, with conditional depth 1. Taking the infimum over all original ambient containing halfspaces, and then summing the disjoint conditional strata, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\iota_\beta(y))&\ge\alpha_\beta\tau_{q_\beta}(y),\\
M_2(p)&\ge\sum_{\beta\in D_{P|B}}\alpha_\beta^3M_2(q_\beta)
\ge\frac1{16}4^{-g}\sum_{\beta}\alpha_\beta^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
We shall prove an absolute lower bound for the cubic atom sum of this original marginal.

Changing any coordinate of a point in D_P from 0 or 2 to 1 preserves membership in D_P, because it removes all constraints involving that coordinate and introduces none. Fix \(i\in B\) and an assignment \(\gamma\) on \(B\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace\). For \(a\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), define the possibly empty original good-coordinate fiber
\(F_a=\lbrace y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^{G}:(\gamma,a,y)\in D_P\rbrace\), with the entries placed at their own labels. The preceding observation gives \(F_0\subseteq F_1\) and \(F_2\subseteq F_1\). For every a, including unsupported assignments, its marginal mass is exactly
$$
\alpha_{(\gamma,a)}
=\frac{e^{\ell_{B\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace}\cdot\gamma+\ell_i a}}{Z}
\sum_{y\in F_a}e^{\ell_G\cdot y}.
$$
Consequently the following pointwise inequalities hold with the same original Z:
$$
\alpha_{(\gamma,1)}\ge e^{\ell_i}\alpha_{(\gamma,0)},\qquad
\alpha_{(\gamma,1)}\ge e^{-\ell_i}\alpha_{(\gamma,2)}.
\tag{5}
$$
They also hold when either source atom is unsupported, as its mass is then zero.

Choose a linear extension \(b_1,\ldots,b_k\) of \(P|B\), and abbreviate \(\beta_{b_t}\) by \(\beta_t\) in the following class definitions. For k=0 the list is empty. Let \(J_0=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{B}\). For \(1\le j\le k\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_j&=\lbrace\beta:\beta_t\in\lbrace1,2\rbrace\ (t<j),\
                 \beta_j=2,\
                 \beta_t\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\ (t>j)\rbrace,\\
J_j&=D_{P|B}\cap Q_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
The sets \(J_0,\ldots,J_k\) are disjoint and exhaust \(D_{P|B}\). To verify this, an assignment with no 2 lies in \(J_0\), and \(J_0\subseteq D_{P|B}\). Otherwise let j be its rightmost 2 in the chosen extension. A 0 at an index \(t<j\) would require \(b_j<_Pb_t\), contrary to the extension order. Thus all earlier values are 1 or 2, while all later values are 0 or 1 by the choice of j, proving membership in \(J_j\). Conversely every member of \(J_j\) has rightmost 2 at j. Each class is nonempty: for \(j\ge1\), the assignment with its sole 2 at \(b_j\) and every other bad coordinate 1 lies in \(J_j\); the all-1 assignment lies in \(J_0\). These assignments extend with all good coordinates 1.

Changing a nonpivot coordinate in \(J_j\), for \(j\ge1\), from its non-1 value to 1 preserves both membership in \(D_{P|B}\) and its rightmost 2 at j. The same is true of every coordinate in \(J_0\). Thus these changes preserve their classes. We make no product assertion about \(J_j\); its exact support is the intersection in (6).

Define the exact original class normalizers and probabilities by
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{J,j}&=\sum_{\beta\in J_j}e^{\ell_B\cdot\beta}Z_\beta,&
w_j&=\frac{Z_{J,j}}{Z},\\
\mu_j(\beta)&=\frac{e^{\ell_B\cdot\beta}Z_\beta}{Z_{J,j}}
\quad(\beta\in J_j).&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Here \(\mu_j\) is zero outside \(J_j\). The normalizer \(Z_{J,j}\) is precisely \(\sum_{x\in D_P,\ x_B\in J_j}e^{\ell\cdot x}\). Therefore every \(w_j\) is positive, \(\sum_{j=0}^k Z_{J,j}=Z\), \(\sum_{j=0}^k w_j=1\), and \(\alpha_\beta=w_j\mu_j(\beta)\) for \(\beta\in J_j\). For k=0 there is only the singleton class \(J_0\), with weight 1.

The class-preserving changes and (5) show that \(\mu_j\), considered on its full binary box with zero masses at unsupported atoms, obeys the same pointwise ratios for every nonpivot coordinate. In particular, on a low coordinate \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) with \(\ell_i>\delta\), the mass at 1 is at least \(e^\delta\) times the mass at 0 when all other coordinates are fixed. On a high coordinate \(\lbrace1,2\rbrace\) with \(\ell_i<-\delta\), the mass at 1 is at least \(e^\delta\) times the mass at 2. If a source atom is unsupported the inequality remains true.

We need a cubic atom bound that requires only these pointwise inequalities. Let \(\nu\) be any probability law on a binary box with m coordinates, allowing zero atom masses. Suppose a specified set F of f coordinates each has a preferred value such that, pointwise with every other coordinate fixed, the preferred mass is at least \(e^\delta\) times the other mass. Then
$$
\sum_z\nu(z)^3\ge\rho^f4^{-m}.
\tag{8}
$$
Here is a proof by successive marginalization. For nonnegative pair masses a,b, one always has \(a^3+b^3\ge(a+b)^3/4\). If a is the preferred mass and \(a\ge e^\delta b\), then, when \(a+b>0\),
$$
\frac{a^3+b^3}{(a+b)^3}
=\frac{1+3((a-b)/(a+b))^2}{4}
\ge\frac{1+3\tanh^2(\delta/2)}4=\frac\rho4.
\tag{9}
$$
Indeed, \((a-b)/(a+b)\ge(e^\delta-1)/(e^\delta+1)=\tanh(\delta/2)\ge0\). If \(a+b=0\), the corresponding cubic inequality holds with both sides zero.

Delete any coordinate i by summing its two masses to form a marginal \(\nu'\). Applying the pair inequality at every assignment of the remaining coordinates gives
\(\sum_z\nu(z)^3\ge c_i\sum_{z'}\nu'(z')^3\), where \(c_i=\rho/4\) if \(i\in F\), and \(c_i=1/4\) otherwise. Every remaining preferred-value inequality is preserved by this marginalization: sum its two original inequalities over the two values of i. Induction on m therefore proves (8), terminating at the unique probability law on the empty box, whose cubic atom sum is 1. This includes cases in which only the preferred value is supported.

For \(0\le j\le k\) define the following nonnegative integer counts using the original bad fields:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=\#\lbrace t\in\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace:\ell_{b_t}>0\rbrace,\\
f_j&=\#\lbrace t<j:\ell_{b_t}<0\rbrace
     +\#\lbrace t>j:\ell_{b_t}>0\rbrace\qquad(1\le j\le k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
Every bad field has nonzero sign and magnitude greater than \(\delta\). For \(J_0\), precisely the positive-field coordinates supply the preferred-value inequalities just described, so (8) with m=k gives
\(\sum_\beta\mu_0(\beta)^3\ge\rho^{f_0}4^{-k}\).
For \(J_j\) with \(j\ge1\), there are \(j-1\) high coordinates, \(k-j\) low coordinates, and a fixed pivot. The negative fields before j and positive fields after j give the \(f_j\) preferred-value inequalities. Apply (8) to the k-1 nonpivot binary coordinates. The fixed pivot contributes a factor 1 to the cubic atom sum, and hence
$$
\sum_\beta\mu_j(\beta)^3
\ge\rho^{f_j}4^{-(k-1)}
\ge\rho^{f_j}4^{-k}.
\tag{11}
$$
Thus the weaker last bound holds for every class \(0\le j\le k\), including k=0.

For any \(0\le j<l\le k\), we claim that
\(f_j+f_l\ge l-j-1\).
When \(j\ge1\), each index t strictly between j and l contributes at least once: a negative field contributes to \(f_l\), and a positive field contributes to \(f_j\). When j=0, every index \(1\le t<l\) contributes to \(f_0\) if its field is positive and to \(f_l\) if negative. This proves the claim in every case.

Consequently, if two indices j,l have \(f_j=f_l=r\), their distance is at most \(2r+1\). The set of all such indices therefore has cardinality at most \(2r+2\), since they are distinct integers in an interval of that length. This bound also holds for an empty or singleton level set. Since \(0<\eta=\rho^{-1/2}<1\), it follows that
$$
\sum_{j=0}^k\rho^{-f_j/2}
=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\#\lbrace j:f_j=r\rbrace\,\eta^r
\le\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(2r+2)\eta^r
=\frac{2}{(1-\eta)^2}.
\tag{12}
$$
For k=0 the left side is 1, so this case is covered as well.

The classes in (6) are disjoint, so (7) and (11) give the exact decomposition and lower bound
$$
\sum_\beta\alpha_\beta^3
=\sum_{j=0}^kw_j^3\sum_\beta\mu_j(\beta)^3
\ge4^{-k}\sum_{j=0}^kw_j^3\rho^{f_j}.
\tag{13}
$$
Hölder's inequality with exponents 3 and \(3/2\), applied to
\(w_j=(w_j\rho^{f_j/3})\rho^{-f_j/3}\), yields
$$
1=\left(\sum_{j=0}^kw_j\right)^3
\le\left(\sum_{j=0}^kw_j^3\rho^{f_j}\right)
   \left(\sum_{j=0}^k\rho^{-f_j/2}\right)^2.
$$
Combining this with (12)--(13) proves the original-marginal estimate
$$
\sum_\beta\alpha_\beta^3
\ge\frac{(1-\eta)^4}{4}\,4^{-k}.
\tag{14}
$$
Every factor in this argument concerns original marginal atoms or their normalized disjoint classes. In particular, (8) was applied without assuming independence.

Combining (4) and (14), and using g+k=n, proves
\(M_2(p)\ge(1-\eta)^4\,4^{-n}/64=c_{\mathrm{pos}}4^{-n}\).
If B is empty, its sole assignment has marginal mass 1, \(J_0\) is the law on the empty box, and all displayed bounds remain valid. If G is empty, each conditional law in (3) is a singleton with depth 1, so the same original halfspace transfer and subsequent argument remain valid.

Finally, Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(s\mapsto s^{3/2}\), under the entire original law p, gives
$$
M_3(p)=\mathbb E_p\bigl[(\tau_p(x)^2)^{3/2}\bigr]
\ge M_2(p)^{3/2}
\ge c_{\mathrm{pos}}^{3/2}8^{-n}.
$$
This proves (1) for every finite strict partial order, with the stated absolute coefficient.
