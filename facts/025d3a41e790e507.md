## Statement

Let a compact convex mixed family have one complete finite integer base interval $P$ and positive $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$. Suppose $K_z\subseteq y(z)+\ell(z)D$, where $D$ is a convex body of volume $V>0$ and $\ell$ is positive on $P$ and affine with slope $c>0$. Suppose equality holds on the sampled source interval $[a,b]$, $m=b-a\ge2$, and the maximal raw tied-buffered depth $Q$ is attained at a mark of base $a$. Use buffer parameter $\varepsilon\ge0$ as in 32418f8d50e792af, so a nonvertical through-mark cap is enlarged by $\varepsilon f(a,y)\Gamma(u)$ in its margin and $0\le f\le1$. If own sections perpendicular to unit $u$ have area bounded by $L$ and the pooled covariance norm has $\Gamma(u)\le G$ on unit covectors, put $C=LG$. Then
$$
\Gamma_d\ell(b)^d V\le Q\le \sum_{z<a}|K_z|+\tfrac12|K_a|+C\varepsilon,\qquad
Q\le\sum_{z\le a}|K_z|,
$$
with sums over $P$ and $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. In particular, with $t=\ell(a)/c$,
$$
\Gamma_d(t+m)^d\le\tfrac12t^d+\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil t\rceil-1}(t-k)^d+\frac{C\varepsilon}{Vc^d}.
$$
At $\varepsilon=0$ the scalar restriction alone allows an individual next weight to exceed the sum of the source weights: $(d,t,m)=(13,19,2)$ satisfies it strictly and $22^{13}>19^{13}+20^{13}+21^{13}$.

## Proof

The full-volume metronoid bound of 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives ordinary own depth at least $\Gamma_d|K_b|$ at the centroid of $K_b$. The buffered nonvertical caps only enlarge those cuts, and pure-base caps through that base include the whole own fiber. Equality of the envelope at $b$ gives the stated lower bound, irrespective of whether the centroid itself is globally deepest.

At a deepest mark $(a,y)$ choose any unit continuous covector $u$ and the two caps with through-mark margins $\pm u\cdot(X-y)+B(a-z)$, where the same $B>0$ is chosen large enough to include every predecessor fiber wholly and exclude every successor strictly even after buffering. This is possible since the other finite sampled bases have $|z-a|\ge1$ and the continuous fibers are bounded. In the own fiber the two buffered cuts cover it and overlap only in $|u\cdot(X-y)|\le\varepsilon f(a,y)\Gamma(u)$. The overlap volume is at most $2LG\varepsilon$ by integrating the section-area bound (use counting area in dimension one). The smaller of the two raw masses is therefore at most $\sum_{z<a}|K_z|+|K_a|/2+C\varepsilon$. At $\varepsilon=0$ the overlap is volume-null, including at a support plane. The separate pure-base closed cap $z\le a$ has exactly the displayed full-own mass for every buffer parameter.

By inclusion, $|K_z|\le V\ell(z)^d$. For each predecessor $a-k$ in the sampled interval, $k$ is a positive integer and $\ell(a-k)=c(t-k)>0$, so $k\le\lceil t\rceil-1$. Inserting the envelope and all potentially allowable predecessor terms, and dividing by $Vc^d$, gives the dimensionless bound. Its error must be controlled at that scale before taking a limit.

At $(d,t,m)=(13,19,2)$, clearing the positive denominator $2\cdot14^{13}$ in the unbuffered difference gives
$$
14^{13}\left(19^{13}+2\sum_{k=1}^{18}k^{13}\right)-2\cdot13^{13}21^{13}
=809166980250791721289725405278>0.
$$
Direct integer expansion gives $22^{13}-19^{13}-20^{13}-21^{13}=4364696681706232>0$. This demonstrates the logical insufficiency of the scalar restriction by itself. It does not realize this table as a deepest-source interval of an actual extremizer.
