## Statement

In a $C^2$ actual body/source and through-anchor normal chart for such a finite original cap menu, count each cap once in a weighted cap sum with specified nonnegative weights. Assume every positively weighted cap is stationary in all its through-anchor normal coordinates at the reference point. For a fixed body/source velocity $v$ and normal velocity $w$, suppose the retained weighted quadratic is $q(v,w)=v^{\mathsf T}Av+2b(v)^{\mathsf T}w+w^{\mathsf T}Kw$, with $b(v)=B^{\mathsf T}v$ and $K\succ0$. Include only genuinely objective-absent normal variables and cover-weight variables in the auxiliary velocity $\eta$. After substituting $v$, let the first-jet equalities and tight one-sided ownership inequalities be $Rw+S\eta=d(v)$, $Gw+H\eta\ge e(v)$ with linear right sides. Put $W(v)=\{w:\exists\eta,\ Rw+S\eta=d(v),\ Gw+H\eta\ge e(v)\}$.

If $W(v)$ is nonempty, the minimum of $q(v,\cdot)$ over it is attained at a unique $w$ and equals
$$
v^{\mathsf T}Av-b(v)^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}b(v)+\operatorname{dist}_K(-K^{-1}b(v),W(v))^2 .
$$
The value is piecewise quadratic in feasible $v$. At a feasible pair $(w,\eta)$ optimality is equivalent to the existence of $\lambda,\tau$ with $\tau\ge0$ such that
$$
\begin{aligned}
K(w-w_0)+R^{\mathsf T}\lambda-G^{\mathsf T}\tau&=0,\qquad
S^{\mathsf T}\lambda-H^{\mathsf T}\tau=0,\\
\tau_i(G_iw+H_i\eta-e_i(v))&=0\quad\text{for all }i,\qquad w_0=-K^{-1}b(v).
\end{aligned}
$$
For equalities alone with no auxiliaries, the added term is $(d(v)+RK^{-1}b(v))^{\mathsf T}(RK^{-1}R^{\mathsf T})^\dagger(d(v)+RK^{-1}b(v))$.

## Proof

The shared-cap jet lemma gives the linear equations and inequalities in the stated affine normal chart after inserting the body/source velocities. In particular the cross terms involving cover weights are present in those equations and the ownership rows use the derivative of the actual projected margins. At any actual path the retained normals are stationary at the reference, so no normal acceleration term is present in the weighted mass's second derivative. The body/source acceleration term need not vanish and is not part of $q$. Moving a zero-weight cap does not change the weighted objective, and a flat mode of a positive-weight cap can be put in $\eta$ only if it is genuinely absent from the quadratic, including the mixed force.

The projection of the finite polyhedron onto $w$ is a closed convex polyhedron by linear elimination, writing equalities as opposite inequalities. Completing the square gives
$$
q(v,w)=v^{\mathsf T}Av-b(v)^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}b(v)+(w-w_0)^{\mathsf T}K(w-w_0).
$$
The squared $K$-norm is coercive and strictly convex, so the minimizing $w$ exists and is unique, and a feasible auxiliary $\eta$ exists by the definition of $W(v)$. At a minimizing pair the first derivative of this squared norm is nonnegative on every feasible tangent direction. The tangent cone consists of the homogeneous equalities and the homogeneous nonnegative rows of the active inequalities; every such direction is realized by a sufficiently short feasible straight path. Its polar, by finite Farkas, gives the displayed multiplier equations, absorbing the factor two in the objective gradient. Conversely those equations make the objective derivative along any feasible displacement nonnegative by complementary slackness, so adding its nonnegative squared norm proves optimality.

For each subset of inequality rows prescribe equality there and zero multipliers on its complement. Keeping the full primal inequalities and multiplier signs, these systems are linear in $(v,w,\eta,\lambda,\tau)$. Elimination yields finitely many polyhedral cones in $(v,w)$ covering the graph of the minimizer. On each projected parameter cone the optimizer is unique; since the graph there is a convex cone, addition and nonnegative scaling show the solution is linear on that cone (and extends linearly to its span). Substitution gives the piecewise quadratic value. The equality-only expression is the feasible positive-definite equality case of 8c3661bf73c37d6f with the same $R,d(v)$; it does not require independent rows. A singular retained $K$ is outside this projection formula; ownership can constrain a mode in its kernel. The minimum is a bound on the normal cost of any actual path with that $v$. Actual attainment requires the second-jet and integration conditions, and a weighted bound does not imply all individual cap inequalities.
