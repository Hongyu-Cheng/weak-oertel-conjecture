## Statement

Use the admissibility and canonical notation of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. For a nonzero finite table \(w\), put \(W=\sum_z w_z\), \(M=\max_z w_z\), \(p_z=w_z/W\), \(\pi_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod 2}p_z\), \(q=\sum_b\pi_b^2\), and \(\rho=2^nM/W\). Use the same oriented reflection sums, including zero displacement: \(H_j=\sum_v\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\), \(H_*=\max_j H_j\), \(h=H_*/W\), and \(G=\log(2^nh)\). Let \(X,Y\) be independent with table \(p\), let \(\Gamma\) be their joint law conditioned on equal parity, and let \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\) be the law of \(J=(X+Y)/2\) under \(\Gamma\). These are exact notation and normalization identifications, with no legacy renaming.

For positive parity masses define \(\mu_b=\mathbb E[X\mid X\equiv b\pmod2]\), \(\Sigma_b=\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid X\equiv b\pmod2)\), \(\Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}_p(X)\), and the square-weighted within-parity covariance

\[
T_{\mathrm{sq}}=\frac1q\sum_b\pi_b^2\Sigma_b.
\]

This is the assignment's \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\). If the ordinary within-parity covariance is instead denoted by \(T=\sum_b\pi_b\Sigma_b\), then \(T=T_{\mathrm{sq}}\) when the parity masses are uniform, as they are in both families below. A complete positive support means \(\{z:w_z>0\}=\operatorname{conv}\{z:w_z>0\}\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Matrix inequalities use the positive-semidefinite order.

For every integer \(L\ge1\), take \(n=3\) and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_L&=\{(b_1,b_2,Lb_1b_2+k):b_1,b_2\in\{0,1\},\ k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\},\\
w_z&=\mathbf1_{A_L}(z),\qquad P_L=\operatorname{conv}(A_L),\qquad
\varepsilon_L=\frac{16}{L^2+20}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These tables have complete positive support and a finite-valued globally log-concave extension, including all lattice zeros. They are also realized as the fiber-volume tables of the convex bodies \(C_L=P_L\times[0,1]\subset\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\). They satisfy, for every \(L\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_b&=1/8\quad\text{for all }b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3,
&q&=1/8,\\
T_{\mathrm{sq}}&=e_3e_3^{\mathsf T}\preceq\varepsilon_L\Sigma,
&\varepsilon_L&\longrightarrow0,\\
\mathbb E_p\langle u,X-\mathbb E_pX\rangle^4
&\le3\bigl(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u\bigr)^2\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathbb R^3,
&\rho&=1/2,\\
\Gamma\{X=Y\}&=1/2,
&G&=\log(3/2),\\
\operatorname{TV}(p^{\mathrm{mid}},p)&=1/4,
&\operatorname{Cov}(p^{\mathrm{mid}})&=\Sigma-\tfrac12T_{\mathrm{sq}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(e_3=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(\operatorname{TV}(a,b)=\frac12\sum_z|a_z-b_z|\). Each conditional parity table also satisfies the directional fourth-moment bound with constant one. In particular, even with fixed \(n=3\), fixed kurtosis bound \(\kappa=3\), exact parity balance, complete support, and global log-concavity, \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\varepsilon\Sigma\) as \(\varepsilon\to0\) does not imply \((1-\rho)_+\to0\) or \(\Gamma\{X=Y\}\to1\).

For the prescribed sparse calibration, let \(n=1\), let \(L\ge3\) be odd, and let \(p\) be uniform on \(\{0,2,L,L+2\}\), with \(w=p\) and \(W=1\). It has \(\pi_0=\pi_1=1/2\), \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}=1\), \(\Sigma=L^2/4+1\), \(\rho=1/2\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p(X-\mathbb E_pX)^4
&=L^4/16+3L^2/2+1,\\
\frac{\mathbb E_p(X-\mathbb E_pX)^4}{\Sigma^2}
&=1+\frac{16L^2}{(L^2+4)^2}\le2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its support is incomplete, it is not admissible, and its canonical gain is exactly \(G=0\) for every such \(L\). Thus the claim that this particular sparse family's gain does not approach zero is false. The admissible three-dimensional family above supplies the positive-gain separation instead.

## Proof

First verify the support and extension in dimension three. The projection of \(P_L\) on its first two coordinates is \([0,1]^2\). An integer point of \(P_L\) therefore has first coordinates \(b_1,b_2\in\{0,1\}\). Each such base is a vertex of the projected square. More explicitly, in any convex combination producing this base, a coordinate equal to zero forces all positively weighted terms to have that coordinate zero, and a coordinate equal to one forces them all to have that coordinate one. Consequently only points from the column at that same base can contribute. The fiber of \(P_L\) at this base is exactly the interval \([Lb_1b_2,Lb_1b_2+3]\). Its integer points are precisely the four stated column points. This proves \(P_L\cap\mathbb Z^3=A_L\), including the absence of every other lattice point.

The extension \(f(x)=\mathbf1_{P_L}(x)\) is finite-valued and globally log-concave. If both endpoint values are one, convexity makes every intermediate value one; if either endpoint value is zero, the required right side is zero. It agrees with \(w\) at every lattice point, including zeros, so the admissibility definition of ab57fac07c0d3160 applies. The points \((0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,3)\) are affinely independent, hence \(P_L\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope. Thus \(C_L=P_L\times[0,1]\) is an actual convex body. Its one-dimensional fiber has length one at every integer point of \(A_L\) and is empty at every other integer base, establishing the claimed realization.

There are sixteen positive sites, all of weight one, so \(W=16\), \(M=1\), \(p_z=1/16\) on \(A_L\), and \(\rho=8/16=1/2\). At each fixed base \((b_1,b_2)\), exactly two of the four heights have either prescribed last-coordinate parity. Each of the eight parity classes therefore contains exactly two sites and has mass \(1/8\). The two sites in each class differ by \(2e_3\), and their conditional probabilities are both \(1/2\). Their conditional covariance is \(e_3e_3^{\mathsf T}\). In direction \(u\), their centered values are \(\pm u_3\), so their fourth moment equals the square of their variance, including when both are zero. Since \(\pi_b^2/q=1/8\), the asserted value of \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\) follows.

For the total covariance and all directional moments, sample independent uniform \(B_1,B_2\in\{0,1\}\) and uniform \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\); then \(X=(B_1,B_2,LB_1B_2+k)\) has table \(p\). Put \(\xi_i=2B_i-1\) and \(\eta=k-3/2\). The variables \(\xi_1,\xi_2\) are independent uniform signs, independent of \(\eta\), and \(\mathbb E\eta=\mathbb E\eta^3=0\), \(\mathbb E\eta^2=5/4\), \(\mathbb E\eta^4=41/16\). For any \(u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\), define coefficients \(a_1=u_1/2+Lu_3/4\), \(a_2=u_2/2+Lu_3/4\), and \(a_3=Lu_3/4\). Direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle u,X-\mathbb E X\rangle
&=a_1\xi_1+a_2\xi_2+a_3\xi_1\xi_2+u_3\eta,\\
u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u
&=a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2+\tfrac54u_3^2\\
&=\tfrac14(u_1+\tfrac L2u_3)^2
 +\tfrac14(u_2+\tfrac L2u_3)^2
 +(\tfrac{L^2}{16}+\tfrac54)u_3^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular \(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u\ge\varepsilon_L^{-1}u_3^2\), which proves \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\varepsilon_L\Sigma\) in every direction, not merely in trace. Equality holds for \(u_1=u_2=-Lu_3/2\), so this comparison is sharp for this family. Equivalently, the exact covariance matrix is

\[
\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/4&0&L/8\\
0&1/4&L/8\\
L/8&L/8&3L^2/16+5/4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

To verify the fourth moment for every direction, expansion over the two independent signs gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\langle u,X-\mathbb E X\rangle^4
={}&a_1^4+a_2^4+a_3^4
 +6(a_1^2a_2^2+a_1^2a_3^2+a_2^2a_3^2)\\
&+\tfrac{15}{2}(a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2)u_3^2
 +\tfrac{41}{16}u_3^4,\\
3(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u)^2
 -\mathbb E\langle u,X-\mathbb E X\rangle^4
={}&2(a_1^4+a_2^4+a_3^4)+\tfrac{17}{8}u_3^4\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, in the fourth power of \(a_1\xi_1+a_2\xi_2+a_3\xi_1\xi_2\), the only nonzero terms have all four factors equal or two equal factors of each of two types. Terms involving an odd number of \(\eta\) vanish. The remaining mixed term is six times the sign expression's second moment times \(u_3^2\mathbb E\eta^2\). This proves the displayed identities and the fixed bound \(\kappa=3\), including \(u=0\).

For reflection sums, an ordered endpoint pair contributing at an integer midpoint has equal parity. Since the first two coordinates of the support take only the values zero and one, equal parity forces the endpoints into the same column. At a column's outer heights \(k=0,3\), the only positive reflected pair is diagonal, and \(H_j=1\). At its inner heights \(k=1,2\), there are the diagonal pair and two oriented off-diagonal pairs, so \(H_j=3\). No other integer center contributes. Thus \(H_*=3\), \(h=3/16\), and \(G=\log(8\cdot3/16)=\log(3/2)\), independent of \(L\).

The diagonal endpoint pairs have unconditioned probability \(\sum_zp_z^2=16/256=1/16\). Conditioning on equal parity, whose probability is \(q=1/8\), gives \(\Gamma\{X=Y\}=1/2\). At an outer site of a column there is one ordered endpoint pair with that midpoint, and at an inner site there are three. Each has unconditioned probability \(1/256\). Consequently \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\) assigns mass \(1/32\) to each outer site and \(3/32\) to each inner site, while \(p\) assigns \(2/32\) to every site. There are sixteen differences of absolute size \(1/32\), giving TV distance \(1/4\).

For completeness, exact parity balance also gives the asserted covariance identity. Under \(\Gamma\), the common parity has probability \(\pi_b^2/q=\pi_b\); conditional on it, the endpoints are independent with conditional mean \(\mu_b\) and covariance \(\Sigma_b\). Their midpoint has mean \(\mu_b\) and covariance \(\Sigma_b/2\). The mixture therefore has the same mean as \(p\) and covariance \(\frac12\sum_b\pi_b\Sigma_b+\operatorname{Cov}_{b\sim\pi}(\mu_b)=\Sigma-\frac12T_{\mathrm{sq}}\). Hence even this exact relative covariance near-stationarity coexists with the fixed TV discrepancy and the fixed collision deficit.

The geometric source of the separation is not a missing lattice site. The centered height contains the non-affine sign term \((L/4)\xi_1\xi_2\), which survives every linear shear removing the terms proportional to \(\xi_1,\xi_2\). It contributes \(L^2/16\) to the residual total variance, while every within-parity covariance stays fixed. To contrast the mechanism exactly, replacing \(Lb_1b_2\) by any integer affine height \(r_0+r_1b_1+r_2b_2\), with \(r_0,r_1,r_2\in\mathbb Z\), in the same four-column construction leaves the direction \(u=(-r_1,-r_2,1)\) with total variance \(5/4\) and within-parity variance one. That affine variant must have \(\varepsilon\ge4/5\) in \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\preceq\varepsilon\Sigma\). Complete lattice support does not enforce this affine alignment. The false step is therefore the conversion of small relative covariance into high collision probability or distributional midpoint stationarity, even after imposing complete support and global log-concavity. No conclusion for tables with \(G\to0\) is drawn.

Finally verify the one-dimensional calibration separately. Its mean is \(L/2+1\). Its centered variable is the sum of independent uniform signs scaled by \(L/2\) and by one. Thus its variance is \(L^2/4+1\), and expansion of the fourth power gives \(L^4/16+3L^2/2+1\). Subtracting the variance squared from this fourth moment gives \(L^2\); division by the variance squared gives exactly \(1+16L^2/(L^2+4)^2\). This is at most two because \((L^2-4)^2\ge0\), and it tends to one. Both parity classes have two equiprobable sites separated by two, so their conditional variances equal one, their masses equal \(1/2\), and \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}=1\). The maximum probability is \(1/4\), giving \(\rho=1/2\).

The convex hull of this one-dimensional support is \([0,L+2]\), which contains the missing integer site one. A globally log-concave extension agreeing with all lattice values would require \(w_1\ge\sqrt{w_0w_2}=1/4\), whereas \(w_1=0\). It is therefore nonadmissible. Nevertheless its reflection sums are unambiguous: each of the four support sites has only its diagonal contribution \(H_j=1/4\); the two missing centers \(j=1,L+1\) each have two oriented contributions and \(H_j=1/2\); all other sums vanish. Opposite-parity endpoints have noninteger midpoints and cannot contribute. Hence \(H_*=1/2\) and \(G=\log(2H_*)=0\) exactly. This zero gain outside the admissible class does not contradict the admissible equality classification of ab57fac07c0d3160. The calibration's conditional midpoint table has masses \(1/8\) at the four original sites and \(1/4\) at each missing center, so its TV distance from \(p\) is \(1/2\); its zero gain cannot be used for the admissible midpoint-domination conclusions.
