## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a compact convex body with nonempty interior, $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, and $L(X)=1+a\cdot(X-y)$ the optimal positive projective denominator for $E=\int_K L^{-d-1}\,dX$. Write $n$ for its almost-everywhere outer unit normal, $dS$ for boundary area, and $h_y(X)=n(X)\cdot(X-y)$. Then $h_y L^{-d-1}\,dS/(dE)$ is a probability with barycenter $y$. With $g$ the ordinary centroid, $h_g\,dS/(d|K|)$ is a probability with barycenter $g$. These assertions use endpoint counting in $d=1$.

Given a nonempty finite family of positive fibers $K_z$ of volumes $V_z$ and centroids $g_z$, put $M=\sum_z V_z>0$. For finitely many marked source fibers $p_i$ with marks $y_i\in\operatorname{int}K_{p_i}$, take $\eta_i,\delta_i\ge0$, $\ell_z>0$, and $\Delta=\sum_i\eta_i\delta_i$. Lift source base weights $\eta_i\delta_i/\ell_{p_i}$ and target base weights $\Delta V_z/(M\ell_z)$ by the respective projective and ordinary cone probabilities. The base marginals are unchanged. The target-minus-source continuous first moment of these lifts is
$
\frac{\Delta}{M}\sum_z\frac{V_z}{\ell_z}g_z-\sum_i\frac{\eta_i\delta_i}{\ell_{p_i}}y_i .
$

## Proof

Set $N=d+1$. By d90ba05577427854, $\int_K(X-y)L^{-N-1}\,dX=0$ and $\int_K L^{-N-1}\,dX=E$. Gauss–Green on the convex body is applicable since its boundary is Lipschitz and the kernels are smooth near it. The divergence of $(X-y)L^{-N}$ is $dL^{-N}-N[a\cdot(X-y)]L^{-N-1}$, whose integral is $dE$. For coordinate $k$, the divergence of $(X_k-y_k)(X-y)L^{-N}$ is $N(X_k-y_k)L^{-N-1}$, since $a\cdot(X-y)=L-1$. Its integral is zero. The boundary traces of these vector fields give exactly the normalization and centered first moment of the asserted boundary measure. The support distance $h_y$ is positive almost everywhere because $y$ is interior. For $y=g$, the minimizing denominator is $1$ by d90ba05577427854, yielding the ordinary cone law. In dimension one Gauss–Green is the endpoint fundamental theorem of calculus. The base marginal and first-moment identities follow by substituting the mass and mean of each conditional probability.
