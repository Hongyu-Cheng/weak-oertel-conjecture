## Statement

For an irreducible row-stochastic zero-diagonal matrix P and a feasible missed-row cone point with exactly one positive surviving-root coordinate X and m>=1 positive missed coordinates Xu_j, its unweighted determinant norm defect is at least2X^2 sum_j u_j e_j, where e_j is the transition mass outside the point support, and at least2X^2(m+1)sum_j u_j log_+(Gamma_m/p_j), where p_j is the ordinary centroid vertex-cap fraction. Active cone vertices satisfy the exact resolvent identity1-u=(I-P_AA)^(-1)e. The proof derives an explicit local cap-excess tradeoff and an exact weighted source-family inequality retaining every repeated row incidence. No unproved overlap cancellation or sharp global score bound is asserted.

## Proof

# A source-vertex norm defect detects low-dimensional ordinary cap surplus

Research lemma, 6 November 2026. This connects two quantities previously
kept separate: escape of a stochastic sign row from a source-vertex
support, and that row's ordinary simplex-cap volume. It is a local exact
inequality. No summation over overlapping supports or global fan bound
is assumed.

Let N=d+1>=3. Let P be an irreducible row-stochastic N by N matrix with
zero diagonal. Fix a nonempty proper set J, put R=J^c, and let
H=(I-P_JJ)^(-1)P_JR be its row-stochastic absorption matrix. Write
lambda=(y,x) in the barycentric simplex and assume

    lambda_j<=(P lambda)_j for all j in J.

Suppose x has just one positive coordinate, at r in R. Put X=lambda_r,
A={j in J:lambda_j>0}, m=|A|, and suppose m>=1. Thus the support of
lambda is B=A union {r}. Define u_j=lambda_j/X for j in A and

    e_j=sum_(l outside B)P_jl,
    p_j=product_(l!=j)(1+P_jl)^(-1),
    Gamma_m=(m/(m+1))^m.

Here p_j is exactly the fraction of the ambient d-simplex in the
vertex-side cut lambda_j-(P lambda)_j>=0 through its centroid. The edge
ratio from vertex j to vertex l is 1/(1+P_jl), which proves this formula
even when some transition entries vanish.

Use the unweighted map and its defect from the stochastic capacity lemma:

    T_0(y,x)=(2y,x-H^T y),
    delta_0(lambda)=1-||T_0 lambda||_2^2.

Then the following exact lower bounds hold:

    delta_0(lambda)>=2 X^2 sum_(j in A)u_j e_j,                 (1)

    delta_0(lambda)>=2 X^2(m+1)
         sum_(j in A)u_j log_+(Gamma_m/p_j),                   (2)

    delta_0(lambda)>=2/(m+1)
         sum_(j in A)u_j log_+(Gamma_m/p_j),                   (3)

where log_+(t)=max(0,log t). In particular a small norm defect is
incompatible with large coordinate weights on rows whose ordinary caps
are substantially below the m-dimensional centroid constant.

To prove these statements, absorption gives y<=Hx, so 0<u_j<=1 and
X=1/(1+sum_A u_j)>=1/(m+1). Since every positive coordinate is at most X,
the original row constraint implies

    u_j<=sum_(l in B)P_jl (lambda_l/X)<=1-e_j.                 (4)

The exact nonnegative defect identity of the stochastic capacity lemma
contains 2[XY-||y||_2^2], with Y=sum y. Therefore

    delta_0(lambda)>=2 sum_A lambda_j(X-lambda_j)
        =2X^2 sum_A u_j(1-u_j)>=2X^2 sum_A u_j e_j,

which proves (1). This uses the actual original sign constraints, not
only the weaker absorbed-coordinate containment.

For the ordinary cap estimate assume first m<d. The m entries of row j
at the other coordinates in B have total 1-e_j, and the d-m entries
outside B have total e_j. Arithmetic-geometric mean gives

    p_j>=[(1+(1-e_j)/m)^m
           (1+e_j/(d-m))^(d-m)]^(-1).                       (5)

Taking logarithms relative to Gamma_m and using -log(1-t)>=t for
0<=t<1 and log(1+t)<=t for t>=0 yields

    log(p_j/Gamma_m)
      >=-m log(1-e_j/(m+1))
         -(d-m)log(1+e_j/(d-m))
      >=m e_j/(m+1)-e_j=-e_j/(m+1).

Consequently

    e_j>=(m+1)log_+(Gamma_m/p_j).                             (6)

If m=d, the support is all N coordinates, e_j=0, and ordinary
arithmetic-geometric mean gives p_j>=Gamma_d=Gamma_m. Thus (6) also
holds in that case without a division by d-m. Combining (1) with (6)
proves (2), and X>=1/(m+1) proves (3).

The restriction m>=1 only excludes the pure root lambda=e_r, where the
sum is empty and delta_0=0. This point supplies no cap-surplus charge.
All inequalities permit boundary coordinates and zero transitions
compatible with irreducibility. Their use in a global weighted fan
would additionally require an overlap-safe accounting of the distinct
source vertices and their support-dependent constants Gamma_m. The additional sections below give an exact accounting identity, but do
not prove that it is sufficient for a sharp global fan bound.

## Exact resolvent identity at active cone vertices

Suppose in addition that every original row constraint at j in A is an
equality. This holds for the vertices v(r,A) in the finite-vertex lemma
when P has strictly positive off-diagonal entries. Then

    u=(I-P_AA)^(-1)P_Ar,
    1-u=(I-P_AA)^(-1)e.                                     (7)

The inverse exists because A is a proper subset of an irreducible finite
chain. Its nonnegative Neumann series gives 1-u>=e. To verify the second
identity, subtract the first defining equation from

    (I-P_AA)1=P_Ar+e.

Thus the gap below the surviving root coordinate is exactly the total
probability of exiting A union {r} before reaching r. No probabilistic
interpretation is needed for the matrix identity. At a row where u is
maximal, the harmonic equation also gives

    max_A u_j <= max_(j in A) P_jr/(P_jr+e_j),                (8)

provided the denominators are positive. This is automatic for strictly
positive off-diagonal P; for general irreducible P, only rows with
positive denominator may be used, and (8) is not asserted without that
condition. Indeed, writing U=max u, the maximizing row satisfies
U<=P_jr+(1-P_jr-e_j)U.

Combining (1) with the stronger exact quantity in (7) also gives

    delta_0>=2X^2 sum_A u_j [(I-P_AA)^(-1)e]_j.               (9)

## An explicit cap-excess tradeoff

Let Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d and set c_j=log(p_j/Gamma_d)>=0. The argument
proving (6), without truncating the logarithm, gives

    delta_0 +2(m+1)X^2 sum_A u_j c_j
      >=2(m+1)X^2(sum_A u_j) log(Gamma_m/Gamma_d).            (10)

This is a local inequality, not an assertion that each row can be charged
only once. It holds for every feasible point with one surviving root
under the hypotheses of the first section, even if the row constraints
are not all active. If m=d, its right side is zero and the previous
proof treats this case without dividing by d-m. Pure root points have
empty sums and zero right side and are assigned that convention.

The derivation is direct: (6) implies

    e_j+(m+1)c_j >= (m+1)log(Gamma_m/Gamma_d).

Multiply by 2X^2 u_j, sum, and use (1). Also, by Jensen's inequality, the
same estimates yield, when U=sum_A u_j>0,

    (sum_A u_j p_j)/U
      >=Gamma_m exp[-delta_0/(2(m+1)X^2 U)].                 (11)

Thus small defect forces a definite ordinary cap contribution from the
rows used by that particular vertex.

## Exact accounting over a family of source simplices

For this section assume all off-diagonal P entries are strictly positive.
Let B be a d-simplex of volume vV, and let sources P_i subset B have
volume at least2^(-d)V, nonnegative weights e_i, and missed row sets J_i.
Write k_i=|J_i| and h_i=d+1-k_i. Zero weights may be omitted. A source
cannot miss all rows, since the full cone has dimension zero. If J_i is
empty, use the conventions below with its ordinary simplex vertices.

Within K_(J_i), choose a simplex Q_i of maximum relative volume. Compactness
and separate linearity of the determinant prove that it can be chosen
with DISTINCT vertices from the finite list v(r,A). Its volume ratio is
at least2^(-d)/v because the actual source is an admissible competitor.
This replacement is used solely for a necessary capacity inequality;
it does not replace any actual source or its hit contribution.

For every nonroot vertex l of Q_i, use the quantities

    A_il, m_il=|A_il|, X_il, u_il,j, delta_il

from the preceding sections. Pure root vertices contribute zero to all
quantities below. Define explicit row coefficients

    L_j=2sum_i e_i sum_(l:j in A_il)
              (m_il+1)X_il^2 u_il,j,

and the nonnegative support term

    R_*=2sum_i e_i sum_l
              (m_il+1)X_il^2(sum_(j in A_il)u_il,j)
                 log(Gamma_(m_il)/Gamma_d).                 (12)

Every repeated incidence is present in these sums. Summing (10) therefore
proves the overlap-accounted inequality

    sum_i e_i sum_l delta_il +sum_j L_j c_j >=R_*.            (13)

No bound on L_j or cancellation of repeated supports is assumed.
The unweighted determinant defect bound for Q_i yields

    k_i log2 +(1/2)sum_l[-log(1-delta_il)]
      <=d log2+log v.

Since -log(1-t)>=t for0<=t<1,

    sum_l delta_il<=2[(h_i-1)log2+log v].                    (14)

Consequently, writing E=sum_i e_i, equations(13)-(14) give

    sum_i e_i h_i
      >=E[1-log(v)/log2]
         +[R_*-sum_j L_j c_j]/(2log2).                      (15)

Equation(15) is an exact necessary inequality for the source family. It
makes the overlap cost visible rather than silently paying each row once.
When p_j is near Gamma_d, the cost c_j is small; when it is larger, that
same row has ordinary cap surplus. A universal argument combining these
row-dependent quantities with the fan's weighted scores is not proved
here. In particular, (15) is not asserted to imply Oertel's conjecture.
