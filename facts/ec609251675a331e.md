## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $d=m+1$, $a<0<b$, and let $r:[a,b]\to(0,\infty)$ be finite and concave. In the planar layer with density $r(z)^{m-1}/2$ on $a\le z\le b,\ |x|\le r(z)$, let $V=\int_a^b r(z)^m\,dz$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $\sigma_s$ be the pushforward to the $z$-coordinate of the section measure obtained by integrating the layer density against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$ on $z+sx=0$. Set $C(s)=\int d\sigma_s$ and $M(s)=\int z\,d\sigma_s$. Put $r_0=r(0)$ and take any affine majorant $r_0(1+b_0z)\ge r(z)$, which exists by concavity at the interior point $0$. Put $F(s)=C(s)+d b_0[P(s)-V/2]$. It is nonnegative and nonincreasing on $[0,\infty)$, with $\lim_{s\to\infty}F(s)=0$. In the chart $z'=z/(1+b_0z)$ with axial interval $[a',b']$, write $\widetilde r(z')=r(z)/(1+b_0z)$. For $s>0$, $|q|\le r_0$, set $e_s(q)=\widetilde r(sq)^{m-1}/2$ when $sq\in[a',b']$ and $|q|\le\widetilde r(sq)$, and zero otherwise. The power is evaluated only inside that support. Using right-continuous representatives in $s$ in the definition of the Stieltjes derivative, the nonnegative measure
$$
 d\Xi(s)=\int_{-r_0}^{r_0}(1-b_0sq)^{-d}[-d_s e_s(q)]\,dq
$$
on $(0,\infty)$ satisfies $\Xi((s,t])=F(s)-F(t)$ for $0<s<t$ and $\Xi((0,\infty))=F(0)=r_0^m+d b_0[P(0)-V/2]$. In particular $0\le F\le F(0)$ holds also after averaging over finite slopes.

## Proof

A finite concave profile can be replaced by its continuous one-sided interior limits at the two endpoints. These limits are finite and positive, and the replacement changes neither cap nor section records because for $s>0$ each endpoint meets the line in at most one point, while $s=0$ lies in the interior. The bounds follow from concavity, endpoint positivity and the finite support at zero. With these representatives $\widetilde r$ is concave by the perspective of $r$: the inverse has denominator $1-b_0z'$, and $\widetilde r(z')=(1-b_0z')r(z'/(1-b_0z'))$ on the positive denominator domain. The majorant makes its maximum $r_0$ at zero. Along each ray from zero the profile is nonincreasing up to its endpoint, so $e_s(q)$ is bounded, nonnegative and nonincreasing in $s$ at fixed $q$. Passing to right limits changes the integral at any fixed $s$ on a $q$-null set: endpoint equalities give at most two points, and the two equations $z=\pm sr(z)$ give at most one on each side by the strict monotonicity of $z/r(z)$.

Apply the inverse chart and $z'=s q$ to the raw section measure. Since $dz=dz'/(1-b_0z')^2$, this gives
$$
 C(s)=\int_{-r_0}^{r_0}(1-b_0sq)^{-d}e_s(q)\,dq,\qquad
 M(s)=\int_{-r_0}^{r_0}sq(1-b_0sq)^{-d-1}e_s(q)\,dq .
$$
The denominators on the support and its deletion points are bounded below by a positive constant because $1-b_0 z'=1/(1+b_0z)$ on the compact axial interval. On any compact interval of positive slopes, replace $t^{-d}$ below half that lower bound by a positive $C^1$ extension. The product rule for this smooth weight and the bounded monotone $e_s(q)$, followed by integration in $q$, gives $dC=d b_0(M(s)/s)\,ds-d\Xi$. The integrals are justified by the uniform bounds on the extended weight and its derivative there and by the uniform bound on the variation of $e_s(q)$. The extension has no effect where $e_s(q)$ or its variation is supported. The derivative $dP=-M(s)\,ds/s$ from the axial-tail lemma then gives $dF=-d\Xi$.

For $s>0$, $C$ is continuous by the axial coarea formula and local integrability of $wR$, and $P$ is continuous by the cap integral. As $s\downarrow0$, parameterizing the section by $x$ gives $C(s)=\int_{a\le-sx\le b,\,|x|\le r(-sx)}r(-sx)^{m-1}\,dx/2\to r_0^m$ by bounded convergence. The cap integral gives $P(s)\to P(0)$. As $s\to\infty$, the $z$-parametrized coarea is $O(1/s)$ and the clipped cap fraction tends to $1/2$ at every axial interior point, so $F(s)\to0$. These continuous representatives give the interval and total-mass identities and the asserted bounds.
