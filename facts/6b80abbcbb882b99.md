## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and let the full-dimensional compact convex fibers $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ be the actual sections at $P$ of $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$. Let $z\in P$ be a nonvertex of $\operatorname{conv}P$, and put
$$
C^-=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\ne z}(\{p\}\times K_p),\qquad B=(C^-)_z,\qquad K_z(t)=(1-t)B+tK_z\quad(0\le t\le1).
$$
Then $B$ has positive $d$-volume and $B\subseteq K_z$. The hull of the lifted $K_z(t)$ and all the unchanged $K_p$ for $p\ne z$ has exactly those integer fibers. These hulls are nested as $t$ increases, and their projection is $\operatorname{conv}P$ throughout.

## Proof

The other bases still span $\operatorname{conv}P$, and some convex combination of those bases equals $z$. The corresponding Minkowski combination lies in $B$ and contains a translate of a positive dilation of one full-dimensional fiber, by varying a term of positive coefficient and fixing the others. Also $C^-\subseteq C$, so $B\subseteq K_z$. By convexity $B\subseteq K_z(t)\subseteq K_z$. Each new hull is a subset of $C$ and contains each generating $K_p$ for $p\ne z$, so those sections are unchanged. A point in the new hull over $z$ is a convex combination of a point in the new $z$-generator and a point in $C^-$, allowing either weight to vanish. If the second weight is positive, the base equation forces the latter point to have base $z$; its continuous coordinate is in $B$. The new section at $z$ is therefore contained in $K_z(t)$, and the reverse inclusion is by generation. The projection is unchanged and lattice completeness of $P$ excludes any additional integer fiber.

For $0<t_2\le1$ and $0\le t_1\le t_2$, write $(1-t_1)b+t_1 x=(1-t_2)b+t_2[(1-t_1/t_2)b+(t_1/t_2)x]$. The bracket belongs to $K_z$, so $K_z(t_1)\subseteq K_z(t_2)$, giving nesting. At $t=0$ the hull is $C^-$ because the replacement is its already forced section.
