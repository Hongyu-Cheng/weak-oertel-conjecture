## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(z\) with \(a_z>0\), define
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{2}
\]
Use the absolute fiber depth \(\delta_C(z,q)\) and the residual-selector capacity \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) in the normalization of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Thus a nonempty finite set \(E\) of positive-volume integer bases, points \(q_z\in C_z\), weights
\[
 0<w_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z),
\tag{3}
\]
and an affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\) give the legal selector score
\[
 2^{n-\rho_E}
 \frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{r_s+1},
 \qquad
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
 \qquad
 r_s:=\dim\operatorname{span}\{q_z-s(z):z\in E\},
\tag{4}
\]
which is at most \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\).

Let \(Y\) be any nonempty finite set of distinct bases with \(a_y>0\), and let
\[
 0<\lambda_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in Y).
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
 \rho_Y:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(Y),
 \qquad
 r_Y^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(Y)\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s(y):y\in Y\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the centroids with the single family of depth weights
\[
 q_y=c_y,
 \qquad
 w_y=4\gamma_d\lambda_y
 \qquad(y\in Y)
\tag{7}
\]
form one legal selector, and
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 4\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_Y}
 \frac{\sum_{y\in Y}\lambda_y}{r_Y^c+1}.}
\tag{8}
\]

Now suppose, in addition, that \(C\) is in the canonical hard-branch setting of accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947`, and retain its endpoint-disjoint dyadic matching \(\mathcal M_2\), its positive weights \(t_e\), and the canonical fiber and stress notation of accepted facts `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` and `9c47a4888e8a2947`. Thus, for every edge \(e=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal M_2\),
\[
 u\ne w,
 \qquad
 u\equiv w\pmod 2,
 \qquad
 0<t_e\leq\frac{a_u}{4},
 \qquad
 0<t_e\leq\frac{a_w}{4}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(v=(v_e)_{e\in\mathcal M_2}\) be any dual-feasible stress, not necessarily optimal or load-minimal, and put
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 U_y:=\frac12v_{e(y)},
 \qquad
 \sigma_y:=h_{D_y}(U_y),
 \qquad
 T_*:=\sum_y\sigma_y.
\tag{10}
\]
Here global endpoint-disjointness makes \(e(y)\) unique. The capacity constraints and the lossless endpoint collapse give
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq t_e,
 \qquad
 \sigma_u+\sigma_w=\omega_e
 \quad(e=\{u,w\}),
 \qquad
 T_*=\sum_e\omega_e.
\tag{11}
\]

Let
\[
 F_+(v):=\{e\in\mathcal M_2:\omega_e>0\},
 \qquad
 k:=|F_+(v)|,
 \qquad
 Y_+(v):=\bigcup_{e\in F_+(v)}e.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(k>0\), define the centroid ranks
\[
 \rho_+:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(Y_+(v)),
\tag{13}
\]
\[
 r_+^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(Y_+(v))\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-s(y):y\in Y_+(v)\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Applying (8) first to the actual endpoint loads \(\lambda_y=\sigma_y\), and then to the larger independently legal calibration \(\lambda_y=\omega_{e(y)}\), gives respectively
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 4\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_+}
 \frac{T_*}{r_+^c+1},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 8\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_+}
 \frac{T_*}{r_+^c+1}.}
\tag{16}
\]
The weights in the second application are selector calibration weights, not a claim that the conserved endpoint stress masses equal \(\omega_{e(y)}\). Equation (16) is one selector on the single support \(Y_+(v)\); it is not a sum of selector scores.

For each \(e=\{u,w\}\in F_+(v)\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+w}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{17}
\]
Then \(a_{m_e}>0\), and the endpoint and midpoint singleton selectors give the strongest bound furnished by these three associated fibers:
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 2^n\gamma_d\max\{a_u,a_w,a_{m_e}\}
 \geq
 2^{n+2}\gamma_d t_e
 \geq
 2^{n+2}\gamma_d\omega_e.}
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, including the zero-stress case,
\[
 \boxed{
 T_*
 \leq
 \frac{k}{2^{n+2}\gamma_d}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).}
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore
\[
 k\leq\left\lfloor2^{n+4}\gamma_d\right\rfloor
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{T_*\leq4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).}
\tag{20}
\]
No conclusion in (8), (16), or (18)--(20) bounds arbitrary high-support stress, aggregates different low-rank subsets, supplies a common affine selector across different supports, or proves the locked weak Oertel statement.

## Proof

Accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` records Grünbaum's centroid-depth inequality in the present normalization:
\[
 \delta_C(y,c_y)\geq\gamma_da_y
 \qquad(a_y>0).
\tag{21}
\]
For the data in (5), define \(q_y,w_y\) by (7). Then
\[
 0<w_y=4\gamma_d\lambda_y
 \leq4\gamma_d\frac{a_y}{4}
 =\gamma_da_y
 \leq\delta_C(y,c_y).
\tag{22}
\]
Thus every point and weight in (7) is legal in (3). Choose an affine map attaining the integer-valued minimum in (6). Substitution of (7) into (4) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 &\geq
 2^{n-\rho_Y}
 \frac{\sum_{y\in Y}4\gamma_d\lambda_y}{r_Y^c+1}\\
 &=
 4\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_Y}
 \frac{\sum_{y\in Y}\lambda_y}{r_Y^c+1},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
which proves (8). This construction used exactly one support \(Y\) and one affine map.

We next apply (8) to a dual-feasible canonical stress. Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives that every \(D_y\) and \(D_e\) is an origin-symmetric convex body with the origin in its interior. Hence their support functions are norms. If \(\omega_e>0\), then \(v_e\ne0\), so both endpoint loads
\[
 \sigma_u=h_{D_u}(v_e/2),
 \qquad
 \sigma_w=h_{D_w}(v_e/2)
\tag{24}
\]
are strictly positive. Conversely, (11) and nonnegativity show that \(\omega_e=0\) forces both endpoint loads to vanish. Therefore \(Y_+(v)\) is exactly the set of positive endpoint stress loads and, by endpoint-disjointness, has \(2k\) distinct bases.

For \(y\in Y_+(v)\), equations (9) and (11) give
\[
 0<\sigma_y\leq\omega_{e(y)}\leq t_{e(y)}\leq\frac{a_y}{4}.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the actual endpoint choices \(\lambda_y=\sigma_y\) satisfy (5), and their sum is \(T_*\). Equation (8) gives (15).

The different choices
\[
 \lambda_y:=\omega_{e(y)}
 \qquad(y\in Y_+(v))
\tag{26}
\]
also satisfy (5) by (25). Every positive edge has two distinct endpoints and different edges have disjoint endpoints, so
\[
 \sum_{y\in Y_+(v)}\lambda_y
 =2\sum_{e\in F_+(v)}\omega_e
 =2T_*.
\tag{27}
\]
Substituting (26)--(27) into (8), with the same centroid ranks (13)--(14), proves (16). There is no capacity coupling between different weights in the definition (3)--(4), so using one legal depth weight at each endpoint does not assert or require conservation of the selector weights.

Fix a positive edge \(e=\{u,w\}\). Equations (9) and (11) imply
\[
 a_u\geq4t_e,
 \qquad
 a_w\geq4t_e,
 \qquad
 t_e\geq\omega_e>0.
\tag{28}
\]
For a singleton \(Y=\{z\}\), the affine map with value \(c_z\) has \(\rho_Y=r_Y^c=0\). Taking \(\lambda_z=a_z/4\) in (8) therefore gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq2^n\gamma_da_z
 \qquad(a_z>0).
\tag{29}
\]
Applying (29) to \(z=u\) and \(z=w\), then using (28), already proves
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq2^n\gamma_d\max\{a_u,a_w\}
 \geq2^{n+2}\gamma_dt_e
 \geq2^{n+2}\gamma_d\omega_e.
\tag{30}
\]

For completeness, we also prove the midpoint amplification in arbitrary continuous dimension. Same parity and distinctness in (9) make \(m_e\) an integer base distinct from both endpoints. Convexity of \(C\) gives the fiber containment
\[
 \frac12C_u+\frac12C_w\subseteq C_{m_e}.
\tag{31}
\]
Both endpoint fibers are compact convex bodies of positive \(d\)-volume. Brunn--Minkowski in \(\mathbb R^d\), applied to (31), gives
\[
 a_{m_e}^{1/d}
 \geq
 \frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_w^{1/d}}2
 \geq(4t_e)^{1/d}.
\tag{32}
\]
Consequently
\[
 a_{m_e}\geq4t_e>0.
\tag{33}
\]
The centroid \(c_{m_e}\) is therefore defined. Applying the singleton estimate (29) at \(m_e\) yields
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq2^n\gamma_da_{m_e}
 \geq2^{n+2}\gamma_dt_e.
\tag{34}
\]
Combining (29) at all three bases with (28) and (33) proves the full maximum chain in (18). Midpoints belonging to different labelled edges may coincide; (34) is applied separately to each edge and no scores are summed.

If \(k=0\), then every \(\omega_e=0\), and the lossless identity in (11) gives \(T_*=0\), so (19) holds. If \(k>0\), equation (18) gives
\[
 \omega_e
 \leq
 \frac{R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)}{2^{n+2}\gamma_d}
 \qquad(e\in F_+(v)).
\tag{35}
\]
Summing (35) over the \(k\) positive edges and using (11) proves (19). Finally, if the integer \(k\) satisfies the premise of (20), then
\[
 \frac{k}{2^{n+2}\gamma_d}
 \leq
 \frac{2^{n+4}\gamma_d}{2^{n+2}\gamma_d}
 =4.
\tag{36}
\]
Equations (19) and (36) prove (20), completing the proof.

## External sources

Richard J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355--405, Theorem 4.1, pp. 362--363, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. The exact form used is: for nonempty bounded measurable sets \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(\frac12A+\frac12B\right)^{1/d}
 \geq
 \frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(A)^{1/d}+\operatorname{vol}_d(B)^{1/d}}2.
\]
It applies in (32) because \(C_u,C_w\) are nonempty compact convex sets of positive \(d\)-volume, their Minkowski average is compact and measurable, and (31) supplies the required containment in \(C_{m_e}\).
