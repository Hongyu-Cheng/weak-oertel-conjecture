## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, and let $k,Q_i,\sigma_i>0$ for $d+1$ indices. Put $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=d k^{1/d}Q_i^{(d-1)/d}/(\sigma_i M)$, and $\Phi_s=f_s+(d-1)(\sum_{i\ne s}f_i/d)^{d/(d-1)}$. Fix $s$ and subtract the same $E>0$ from $k$ and $Q_s$, keeping all other $Q_i$ and all $\sigma_i$ fixed. If the resulting data stay positive and initially $\Phi_s\le d$, then finally $\Phi_s<d$.

## Proof

At a positive boundary point $\Phi_s=d$, write $x=f_s\in(0,d)$. The pairing of the row gradient with $f$ is $C=x+d(d-x)/(d-1)=(d^2-x)/(d-1)$, because the second term is homogeneous of degree $d/(d-1)$ in the other entries. For a unit loss, $\log f_i$ has derivative $-1/(dk)+1/(\sigma_sM)-\mathbf1_{i=s}(d-1)/(dQ_s)$. Thus the negative row derivative $\alpha_s$ obeys
$$dk\alpha_s=C+(d-1)xT^d-CxT^{d-1},\qquad T=(Q_s/k)^{-1/d}>0.$$
The last two terms are minimized at $T=C/d$, by the sign of their derivative $x(d-1)T^{d-2}(dT-C)$. Therefore $dk\alpha_s\ge C-x(C/d)^d>0$. Indeed $xC^{d-1}$ increases strictly to $d^d$ on $(0,d)$, as its logarithmic derivative is $1/x-(d-1)/(d^2-x)>0$. Along the linear loss path positivity holds throughout, and the row is differentiable. If initially tight, it immediately becomes strict. Any first subsequent contact with $d$ from below would have nonnegative left derivative, contradicting the strictly negative boundary derivative for the nonzero loss. This proves the endpoint claim.
