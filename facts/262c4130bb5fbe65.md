## Statement

Let $v_1,\ldots,v_m\ge0$ with $\sum_i v_i=1$ and put $s=\sum_i v_i^2$. Then $(1+s)\prod_i(1+v_i)\le3+s$. Equality holds exactly when one coordinate is positive.

## Proof

Let $e_j$ be the elementary symmetric coefficients of the $v_i$, with $e_0=e_1=1$, and extend the sequence by zero. The coefficients are log-concave with contiguous positive support. Indeed, successively convolve the initial sequence $(1)$ with $(1,v_i)$. If the current sequence is $a_j$ and the new one is $b_j=a_j+t a_{j-1}$, then
$$
b_j^2-b_{j-1}b_{j+1}
=(a_j^2-a_{j-1}a_{j+1})
+t(a_ja_{j-1}-a_{j-2}a_{j+1})
+t^2(a_{j-1}^2-a_{j-2}a_j)\ge0.
$$
The middle bracket is nonnegative by the decreasing consecutive ratios, and if its subtracted product is positive then all four entries are in the positive support. Contiguity persists, also when $t=0$.

Put $e=e_2=(1-s)/2$. The ratios $e_{j+1}/e_j$ for $j\ge2$ with positive denominator are at most $e$. Hence $\sum_{j\ge3}e_j\le e^2/(1-e)$, strictly if $e>0$ by finite support. Since $1+s=2(1-e)$, multiplying the bound $\prod_i(1+v_i)\le 2+e+e^2/(1-e)$ by $1+s$ gives $3+s$. If $e=0$, exactly one coordinate is positive and direct substitution gives equality.
