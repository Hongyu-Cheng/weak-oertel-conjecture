## Statement

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(3,-1),(-1,4)\}$ and put $Q(v)=|K\cap\{x:v\cdot x\ge0\}|$, $\sigma(v)=\mathcal H^1(K\cap\{x:v\cdot x=0\})/\|v\|$, and $h(v)=\sqrt{Q(v)}/\sigma(v)$ for $v\ne0$. For $v=(1,-6)$, $w=(0,-4)$, and $v+w$, respectively, $(Q,\sigma,h^2)$ equals $(395/102,25/51,4029/250)$, $(18/5,4/5,45/8)$, and $(1019/270,41/135,137565/3362)$. In these directions $h(v+w)>h(v)+h(w)$. The inequality remains strict when $Q$ is replaced by the normalized cap fraction.

## Proof

The triangle has area $10$ and its oblique edge lies on $5x_1+4x_2=11$. Its strict inequalities at zero show that zero is interior. For $(1,-k)$ with $k=6,10$, the line $x_1=kx_2$ meets the vertical edge at $(-1,-1/k)$ and the oblique edge at $(11k/(5k+4),11/(5k+4))$. A difference of $x_2$ coordinates along this line is a length divided by $\sqrt{1+k^2}$. Thus $\sigma(1,-k)=11/(5k+4)+1/k$. The negative cap is the triangle at the apex $(-1,4)$ of negative margin magnitude $1+4k$. Its two edge ratios are $(1+4k)/(5k)$ and $(1+4k)/(5k+4)$. The area on the other side is consequently $10[1-(1+4k)^2/(5k(5k+4))]$. For $(0,-4)$ the cut is $x_2=0$, with chord endpoints $(-1,0),(11/5,0)$. Its positive cap has area $10[1-(4/5)^2]=18/5$ and $\sigma=(16/5)/4=4/5$. Substitution gives the three asserted triples.

Write $a,b,c$ for the three respective values of $h^2$. Direct subtraction gives
$$
c-a-b=\frac{32235879}{1681000}>0,\qquad
(c-a-b)^2-4ab=\frac{14502698692641}{2825761000000}>0.
$$
Hence $c-a-b>2\sqrt{ab}$ and $\sqrt c>\sqrt a+\sqrt b$. Dividing $Q$ by $|K|$ multiplies every $h$ by $1/\sqrt{|K|}$.
