## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, $d\ge1$, and $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. Write $r=X-y$, $N=d+1$, $V=|K|$, $P(X)=1+a\cdot r$ for the attained positive denominator minimizing $\int_K P^{-N}\,dX$, and $E=\int_K P^{-N}\,dX$. At regular boundary points let $n$ be the outward unit normal and $h_y=r\cdot n$. With $q_H=P^{-N}/(NE)+1/(dNV)$ and all boundary integrals taken against $dS$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int n P^{-N}&=-NaE,& \int r\otimes n P^{-N}&=EI,\\
\int n q_H&=-a,& \int r\otimes n q_H&=I/d,& \int h_y q_H&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Define boundary probability measures
$$
d\nu_C=\frac{h_yP^{-d}}{dE}dS,\qquad
d\nu_E=\frac{h_yP^{-N}}{dE}dS,\qquad
d\nu_V=\frac{h_y}{dV}dS,\qquad d\nu_H=h_yq_H\,dS.
$$
For $D=\{r/P(X):X\in K\}$ put $L(\xi)=1-a\cdot\xi$, $c=E/V$, and let $\nu_D$ be the ordinary cone probability on $\partial D$ about the origin. Under $X\mapsto \xi=r/P(X)$, the pushforwards of $\nu_C,\nu_E,\nu_V,\nu_H$ have densities $1,L,cL^{-d},(dL+cL^{-d})/(d+1)$, respectively, against $\nu_D$. Every closed halfspace $H$ through $y$ satisfies $\nu_H(H)\ge \Gamma_d(E/V)^{1/(d+1)}$ with $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. With the sampled tied profile $f_H=(E V^{1/d})^{1/(d+1)}/M^{1/d}$ at total mixed volume $M>0$, the factor $(E/V)^{1/(d+1)}$ is $f_H/(V/M)^{1/d}$.

## Proof

The minimizing denominator theorem d90ba05577427854 applies to this positive body and interior mark. It gives $\int_K rP^{-N-1}=0$, $\int_K P^{-N-1}=E$, and the projective volume Jacobian $P^{-N}$. The centroid of $D$ is zero because $\int_D\xi\,d\xi=\int_K rP^{-N-1}\,dX=0$. The divergence theorem for $P^{-N}$ and for $r_iP^{-N}$ gives the first two identities componentwise. The same theorem for $1$ and $r_i$ gives $\int n=0$ and $\int r\otimes n=VI$. Substitute the density and take the trace to obtain the three $q_H$ identities. These formulas hold on convex Lipschitz boundaries, with the usual two endpoint fluxes in $d=1$. In particular $\nu_E,\nu_V,\nu_H$ are probabilities and $\nu_H=(d/N)\nu_E+(1/N)\nu_V$.

The cone law of a convex body centered about an interior origin gives a boundary set the volume of its radial sector divided by the body volume. Its surface expression is $h\,dS/(d\,\mathrm{volume})$ by radial integration. The projective map sends each radial sector to the sector with the same rays, and its Jacobian is $P^{-N}$. On a ray, a point at fraction $t$ of its original boundary endpoint has denominator $1+t(P-1)$. The radial Jacobian integral is
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{t^{d-1}}{[1+t(P-1)]^{d+1}}\,dt=\frac1{d P^d},
$$
as follows by differentiating $t^d/[1+t(P-1)]^d$. Thus $\nu_C$ is exactly the pullback of $\nu_D$, in particular a probability. Since $\nu_C=P\nu_E$ and $P^{-1}=L$ on the image, $\nu_E$ pushes to $L\nu_D$. The inverse map has denominator $L$ and the same radial calculation with $\xi$ at the boundary of $D$ shows that $\nu_V$ pushes to $cL^{-d}\nu_D$. The fourth density is the above convex combination.

Weighted AM–GM gives $(dL+cL^{-d})/(d+1)\ge c^{1/(d+1)}$. A halfspace through $y$ maps to the same signed homogeneous halfspace at zero because the denominator is positive. The mass under $\nu_D$ of its boundary portion equals the volume fraction of the corresponding cone cut, which is at least $\Gamma_d$ by ordinary Grünbaum at the centroid of $D$. This gives the lower bound. Substituting the definition of $f_H$ gives the final factor identity.
