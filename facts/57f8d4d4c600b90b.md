## Statement

For an integer $N\ge2$, let $v_i\ge0$ for $1\le i\le N$ and $k_{ij}\ge0$ for $1\le i<j\le N$. Put
$$
B=\max\left\{\max_i v_i,\max_{i<j}k_{ij},\frac34\max_{i<j}\bigl(k_{ij}+\min(v_i,v_j)\bigr)\right\},\qquad
M=\sum_i v_i+\sum_{i<j}k_{ij}.
$$
Then $M\le(3N^2-N+4)B/6$. The constant is sharp even subject to $k_{ij}\ge[(v_i^{1/d}+v_j^{1/d})/2]^d$ for every pair, for each integer $d\ge1$. When $N=4$ and $M=8B>0$, some edge term equals $B$.

## Proof

If $B=0$ all numbers vanish. Otherwise scale $B$ to one and order $v_1\ge\cdots\ge v_N$. For $i<j$ the edge number is bounded by $\min(1,4/3-v_j)$. Therefore
$$
M\le v_1+\sum_{j=2}^N\bigl[v_j+(j-1)\min(1,4/3-v_j)\bigr].
$$
For $0\le t\le1/3$ the bracket at $v_j=t$ equals $t+j-1\le j-2/3$. For $1/3\le t\le1$ it equals $4(j-1)/3-(j-2)t\le j-2/3$. The sum of these upper bounds with $v_1\le1$ is $(3N^2-N+4)/6$. When $N=4$ and equality holds, the brackets with $j=3,4$ force $v_3=v_4=1/3$. All edge bounds used in the sum must be equalities, so $k_{13}=k_{14}=1$ and their edge terms equal one. Finally $v_1=1$, $v_2=\cdots=v_N=1/3$, $k_{ij}=1$ attain the mass bound. Every input volume root is at most one, so this menu satisfies the midpoint inequalities in each $d$.
