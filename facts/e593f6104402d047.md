## Statement

For $n\ge2$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,(1,0),(0,1))$, $a=1+\varepsilon^2$, $A=\{x,y\ge0:\varepsilon\le x+y\le1\}$, $B=\varepsilon^2 T$, and $P=\{x,y\le a:x+y\ge\varepsilon\}$. Form a convex body by lifting $A,P/2,B$ at bases $(j,0)$, $j=0,1,2$, and three copies of $T$ at $(j,1)$, and taking the hull. Pad with $[0,1]^{n-2}$ in extra integer coordinates. These are exactly its $6\cdot2^{n-2}$ full-dimensional integer fibers.

If $W_n$ is its total mixed area, every fiber has area strictly below $(64/79)W_n/2^n$. The summed ordinary deficits relative to $4/9$ are positive and at most $2^{n-2}\cdot 2\varepsilon^2/9$. Taking any enclosing triangles for these fibers and convexifying at their bases adds at least $2^{n-2}\varepsilon/16$ in actual mixed area.

## Proof

Before padding, the projection is $[0,2]\times[0,1]$. Each of the two binary layers is an exposed section, so its convex combinations can use only points in that layer. At $k=0$ the source is the three-fiber hull of fact actual-planar-simplex-container-convexification-cost in the case $n=1$, which checks all three integer fibers. At $k=1$ the hull is $[0,2]\times T$, so its three integer fibers are $T$. Both layers contain relative interior in their parallel three-dimensional planes, and the hull has interior in $\mathbb R^4$. These are all six sampled fibers.

Every fiber area is at most $1/2$: in particular $2a-\varepsilon=2+2\varepsilon^2-\varepsilon\le2$, so $|P/2|\le1/2$. The total mixed area before padding is
$$
W=\tfrac32+\tfrac{1-\varepsilon^2}{2}+\tfrac{(2+2\varepsilon^2-\varepsilon)^2}{8}+\tfrac{\varepsilon^4}{2}
=\tfrac52-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}
+\varepsilon^2[(\varepsilon-\tfrac14)^2+\tfrac9{16}]>\tfrac{79}{32}.
$$
This implies $1/2<(64/79)W/4$. Each copy of $T$ has zero ordinary deficit by 03bc771bddd39a8a in dimension two. The lower layer has positive summed deficit bounded by $2\varepsilon^2/9$ by the cited three-fiber result.

The lifted hull of any enclosing triangles at the six bases has the same projection. Its $k=0$ section is precisely the hull of the lower-layer triangles by extremality of $k=0$. The cited three-fiber result yields its added area of at least $\varepsilon/16$ there, irrespective of choices in the top layer. The top layer contributes nonnegative excess since it contains every original upper fiber. Binary padding repeats these estimates in each of the $2^{n-2}$ exposed extra layers and keeps every individual fiber area at most $1/2$. In particular $W_n=2^{n-2}W$, giving the stated bound on each fraction. The cost divided by the summed deficit is again at least $9/(32\varepsilon)$.
