## Statement

Work in the original Euclidean lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\). Put \(\mathbf u=(1,0)\), \(\mathbf v=(-1,1)\), and \(\mathbf c=(0,1)\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=\{(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^2:
-1\le x_1+x_2\le2,\quad
0\le2x_1+3x_2\le3,\quad
-2\le x_2\le3\},\\
D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^2,\qquad
p_{\mathbf I}=\begin{cases}1/12,&\mathbf I\in D,\\0,&\mathbf I\notin D.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

The set \(D\) has exactly twelve points, \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), and \(p\) has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with every lattice value, including all zeros. The normalization to accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=1/12\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=0\) on \(D\).

Use exactly the arrays, target masses, Euclidean objective, and conditional assignment entropy of accepted fact `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\quad(\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2,\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},
\end{aligned}
\]

with \(0\log0=0\). The symbols \(\mathcal F_{\min}\), \(r^\star\), \(\mathcal T\), \(\mathcal J\), and \(D_{\mathbf J}\) retain their exact meanings from that fact: respectively the full minimizing face of \(V\), its entropy maximizer, the union of positive coordinates over that face, the target projection of that union, and its row supports.

Label four targets by \(\mathbf J_0=\mathbf0\), \(\mathbf J_1=\mathbf v\), \(\mathbf J_2=\mathbf u\), and \(\mathbf J_3=\mathbf c\). For a point \(\mathbf x=(x_1,x_2)\), use the auxiliary integer coordinate functions \(\xi(\mathbf x)=x_1+x_2\) and \(\zeta(\mathbf x)=2x_1+3x_2\). Define \(\tau(\mathbf I)=\mathbf J_{\zeta(\mathbf I)}\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\). Then the entire optimal face is a singleton, with

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{r\in\mathcal F}V(r)&=1,\qquad
r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=\frac1{12}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf J=\tau(\mathbf I)\}},\\
\mathcal T&=\{(\tau(\mathbf I),\mathbf I):\mathbf I\in D\},\qquad
\mathcal J=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf v,\mathbf u,\mathbf c\},\\
D_{\mathbf J}&=\{\mathbf J-(3,-2),\mathbf J,\mathbf J+(3,-2)\}
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J),\\
q^\star_{\mathbf J}&=\begin{cases}1/4,&\mathbf J\in\mathcal J,\\0,&\mathbf J\in D\setminus\mathcal J.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

Exactly twelve of the \(144\) array entries are positive; all other entries vanish throughout the full optimal face. Every positive target has \(p_{\mathbf J}=1/12<q^\star_{\mathbf J}=1/4\), and every positive row has three sources. Nevertheless, with natural logarithms and ordinary target entropy \(S(q^\star)=-\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}q^\star_{\mathbf J}\log q^\star_{\mathbf J}\), one has \(H(r^\star)=0\) and \(S(q^\star)=2\log2\).

Moreover, \(C=P\times[0,1/12]\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\) is a full-dimensional convex body whose complete original fiber-volume table is exactly \(p\), with total mixed-integer volume one. This realization does not assert any failure of the locked halfspace-depth conjecture.

## Proof

First enumerate the complete lattice support. The coordinate map \((x_1,x_2)\mapsto(\xi,\zeta)\) has determinant one and inverse \((x_1,x_2)=(3\xi-\zeta,-2\xi+\zeta)\). It therefore identifies the original lattice bijectively with integer pairs \((\xi,\zeta)\). The inequalities for \(P\) become \(-1\le\xi\le2\), \(0\le\zeta\le3\), and \(-2\le\zeta-2\xi\le3\). The full list of integer possibilities, including every boundary point, is

| \(\zeta\) | Allowed \(\xi\), in increasing order | Original lattice points in that order | Target \(\mathbf J_{\zeta}\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(-1,0,1\) | \((-3,2),(0,0),(3,-2)\) | \(\mathbf0\) |
| \(1\) | \(-1,0,1\) | \((-4,3),(-1,1),(2,-1)\) | \(\mathbf v\) |
| \(2\) | \(0,1,2\) | \((-2,2),(1,0),(4,-2)\) | \(\mathbf u\) |
| \(3\) | \(0,1,2\) | \((-3,3),(0,1),(3,-1)\) | \(\mathbf c\) |

This proves that \(D\) consists of twelve points, exactly three on each indicated parallel lattice line. In the auxiliary coordinates, the polygon's vertices are \((-1,0),(1,0),(2,2),(2,3),(0,3),(-1,1)\). For example, at fixed \(0\le\zeta\le3\) its interval of allowed first coordinates is
\(\max\{-1,(\zeta-3)/2\}\le\xi\le\min\{2,(\zeta+2)/2\}\); the two endpoint functions change slope only at \(\zeta=1,2\), giving exactly those six vertices. They all belong to the displayed lattice list, so \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). The polygon is compact and has nonempty interior.

The twelve positive masses sum to one. The function equal to \(1/12\) on \(P\) and zero off \(P\) is finite-valued and globally log-concave. If both endpoints lie in \(P\), their segment remains in \(P\) and the log-concavity inequality is equality; if either endpoint lies outside, its right-hand side is zero. The complete lattice enumeration gives agreement with every prescribed zero as well as every positive value. Thus the table satisfies the precise extension hypothesis of `488f0a583886b5fc` and `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`, with the stated normalization.

Each line's three sources are \(\mathbf J_{\zeta}-(3,-2)\), \(\mathbf J_{\zeta}\), and \(\mathbf J_{\zeta}+(3,-2)\). Routing all three source columns to their line's target gives a feasible array: each column has its required mass \(1/12\), and the two opposite displacement contributions cancel in every row moment. Its four target masses are \(1/4\). The squared norms at \(\mathbf0,\mathbf v,\mathbf u,\mathbf c\) are \(0,2,1,1\), so this array has objective one.

We now certify the entire optimal face, including every possible row indexed by \(D\). The coordinate chart above is used only for enumeration and evaluating affine functions. The objective remains the original Euclidean one, whose exact squared norm in this chart is
\(\|(3\xi-\zeta,-2\xi+\zeta)\|^2=13\xi^2-10\xi\zeta+2\zeta^2\).

Define four affine functions on the original plane by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\mathbf0}(\mathbf x)&=-2\xi(\mathbf x)+\tfrac52\zeta(\mathbf x),\\
\ell_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf x)&=-2\xi(\mathbf x)+\tfrac12+\tfrac32\zeta(\mathbf x),\\
\ell_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf x)&=-2\xi(\mathbf x)+2+\tfrac12\zeta(\mathbf x),\\
\ell_{\mathbf c}(\mathbf x)&=-2\xi(\mathbf x)+\tfrac92-\tfrac12\zeta(\mathbf x).
\end{aligned}
\]

Each satisfies \(\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf J)=\|\mathbf J\|^2\) at its own target. Set \(\alpha_{\mathbf I}=\min_{0\le j\le3}\ell_{\mathbf J_j}(\mathbf I)\). After adding the common term \(2\xi(\mathbf I)\), the four affine values depend only on \(\zeta(\mathbf I)\). Their complete evaluation is

| \(\zeta(\mathbf I)\) | \(\ell_{\mathbf0}+2\xi\) | \(\ell_{\mathbf v}+2\xi\) | \(\ell_{\mathbf u}+2\xi\) | \(\ell_{\mathbf c}+2\xi\) | Unique minimizing row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1/2\) | \(2\) | \(9/2\) | \(\mathbf0\) |
| \(1\) | \(5/2\) | \(2\) | \(5/2\) | \(4\) | \(\mathbf v\) |
| \(2\) | \(5\) | \(7/2\) | \(3\) | \(7/2\) | \(\mathbf u\) |
| \(3\) | \(15/2\) | \(5\) | \(7/2\) | \(3\) | \(\mathbf c\) |

In particular, among these four rows equality \(\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=\alpha_{\mathbf I}\) holds exactly for \(\mathbf J=\tau(\mathbf I)\). The Euclidean slack \(\|\mathbf I\|^2-\alpha_{\mathbf I}\), evaluated in the increasing \(\xi\) order of the first table, is respectively \((11,0,15)\), \((21,0,5)\), \((5,0,21)\), and \((15,0,11)\) on the four lines. To check all twelve values directly, subtract the line's minimum in the last table from \(13\xi^2-10\xi\zeta+2\zeta^2+2\xi\). The zeros occur precisely at the four designated targets, and all other slacks are strictly positive.

For each remaining row target \(\mathbf K\in D\setminus\{\mathbf J_0,\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2,\mathbf J_3\}\), define the affine function
\(\ell_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf x)=\ell_{\tau(\mathbf K)}(\mathbf x)+\|\mathbf K\|^2-\alpha_{\mathbf K}\).

Its value at \(\mathbf K\) is \(\|\mathbf K\|^2\), because \(\ell_{\tau(\mathbf K)}(\mathbf K)=\alpha_{\mathbf K}\). For every source \(\mathbf I\in D\), its slack satisfies
\(\ell_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf I)-\alpha_{\mathbf I}\ge\|\mathbf K\|^2-\alpha_{\mathbf K}>0\).
Consequently, with all twelve row functions now included,

\[
\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)-\alpha_{\mathbf I}
\begin{cases}
=0,&\mathbf J=\tau(\mathbf I),\\
>0,&\mathbf J\ne\tau(\mathbf I),
\end{cases}
\qquad(\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D).
\]

These are explicit strict dual equality cells in the normalization of `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`: each \(\ell_{\mathbf J}\) is affine and takes the required value \(\|\mathbf J\|^2\) at its row target. To verify the dual certificate without assuming dual attainment or any rank condition, let \(r\in\mathcal F\) be arbitrary. Affineness and the row moment identities give

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(r)
&=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\alpha_{\mathbf I}
+\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\bigl(\ell_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)-\alpha_{\mathbf I}\bigr)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\alpha_{\mathbf I}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last equality, the sums of \(\alpha_{\mathbf I}\) on the four lines are \(0,6,3,3\), respectively, and each original mass is \(1/12\). The feasible array already constructed attains this bound. Equality for any minimizing array forces \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=0\) at every strictly positive dual slack. Exactly one permissible row remains for each source column, namely \(\tau(\mathbf I)\); its column sum then forces that entry to be \(1/12\). This proves the singleton optimal face, all twelve positive coordinates, and all \(132\) forced zeros. The argument includes the polygon's boundary lines and all other boundary sources, and uses no Euclidean invariance under a unimodular transformation.

Because the minimizing face has one element, its entropy maximizer is precisely that array. Every positive assignment share \(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I}\) is one, so every nonzero entropy summand vanishes and \(H(r^\star)=0\). The four row masses are exactly \(1/4\), yielding \(S(q^\star)=\log4=2\log2\). Their residues modulo two are \((0,0),(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)\), respectively, so all four parity classes are present. Directly, \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J)=\{a\mathbf u+b\mathbf v:0\le a,b\le1\}\), since \(\mathbf c=\mathbf u+\mathbf v\) and the integer matrix with columns \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) has determinant one. Its only lattice points are its four vertices. Thus the example has the full extremal target geometry of `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`, while each target source is strictly smaller than its row mass.

All original positive masses are equal, so every logarithmic barycentric cost of `488f0a583886b5fc` equals zero, including every component from any of these rows. The failure therefore concerns the proposed original-target-mass rigidity itself and does not rely on an averaged-energy assertion.

Finally, for \(C=P\times[0,1/12]\), every original integer fiber over \(D\) is exactly the interval \([0,1/12]\), and every integer fiber outside \(D\) is empty. The polygon has nonempty interior and the interval has positive length, so \(C\) is a full-dimensional compact convex body. Its complete fiber-volume table is \(p\), with \(W=12/12=1\). Hence the counterexample is also an actual complete table, rather than an approximation or a table with omitted lattice sources. No claim about insufficient actual halfspace depth follows from its absence of singleton target rows.
