## Statement

Let $C=[-1,2]\times[0,1]$, with mixed measure $\mu$ given by the sum of lengths on its four integer fibers. Consider pairs of positive-mass closed convex subbodies covering $C$ with $\mu$-null overlap, or a single such subbody covering $C$. Require the base coordinate $j_i$ of each actual mixed centroid to be integral. The minimum of $\sum_i\mu(D_i)j_i^2$ is $2$. Every minimizer, up to labels, consists of the two complementary closed caps of $C$ whose base-one piece is cut by either $x+z/5\ge3/5$ or $x-z/5\le2/5$. The two pairs of centroids are $\{(1,7/10),(0,3/10)\}$ and $\{(1,3/10),(0,7/10)\}$. Each centroid has a containing closed halfspace of original mass $9/10$, hence normalized depth at most $9/40<1/4$. In each case the intersection of that halfspace with $C$ lies in the centroid's own piece.

## Proof

The total mass is four and the total base first moment is two. The bound $j^2\ge j$ for integer $j$, with equality exactly for $j=0,1$, gives a lower bound of two for the objective. In accepted fact 196f1bcbb66bc3ee the first listed partition has two pieces of mass two with the specified integer centroids, so it attains the bound. At a minimizer, conservation of mass and first moment forces exactly two pieces, with masses two and base moments zero and two, respectively. Call them $D_0,D_1$.

Suppose $Q=D_0\cap D_1$ has ambient interior. Every section over a base strictly inside its projected interval has positive length: combine an interior ball in $Q$ with a point whose base is farther in the desired direction to obtain a segment of positive length at that base. Thus no integer lies in the interior of the projected interval, which is contained in one closed gap $[k,k+1]$ for $k\in\{-1,0,1\}$. Write $s_z$ for the length of the trace of $D_1$ at $z$. If $0<s_z<1$, the two closed interval traces have positive lengths, exhaust the unit interval and meet at a point. Hence every split fiber is over $k$ or $k+1$. The constraints $\sum_zs_z=2$ and $\sum_z zs_z=2$ imply that $s_k+s_{k+1}$ and $ks_k+(k+1)s_{k+1}$ are integers, because all other lengths are zero or one. Subtracting $k$ times the first quantity from the second shows that both lengths are integers as well. The only two distinct bases summing to two are $0,2$. Closedness puts the full fibers over $0,2$ in $D_1$, so convexity puts the entire intervening unit fiber over $1$ in $D_1$. This contradicts its mass two. The ambient interiors are therefore disjoint.

Separate the two open convex interiors by a line and take closures. The line meets $\operatorname{int}C$ because both pieces have ambient interior on opposite sides. On either open side of the line the covering condition assigns every point of $C$ to the corresponding piece. Every point of $C$ on the line is a limit from both open sides by convex combinations with points on the respective sides, so both pieces contain the line trace. A vertical separator at an integer base would give mixed overlap of length one. A vertical separator between integer bases can leave mass two on a side only by retaining the first two or last two fibers, whose base moments are $-1$ and $3$, respectively. Neither equals the required $D_1$ moment two.

Thus $D_1$ is a nonvertical cap whose margin can be rescaled positively to $L=x+az+b$ or $L=-x+az+b$. For every dominated density $f$ of the same mass and base moment, the pointwise sign inequality $L(\mathbf1_{D_1}-f)\ge0$ on $C$ and cancellation of the base and mass terms show that $D_1$ is an extremizer of $\int xf\,d\mu$ with the appropriate sign. By accepted fact 196f1bcbb66bc3ee it agrees almost everywhere with the corresponding stated cap. Each of the four fiber traces of that cap has length strictly between zero and one, so the trace lengths and orientation determine the thresholds, and the thresholds at two distinct bases determine the separating line. The centroid pairs are those of the accepted fact.

For the upper cap at $(1,7/10)$ use $x+3z/10\ge1$. Its lengths are $(0,0,3/10,3/5)$ and its lower threshold exceeds that of the piece by $2/5-z/10\ge1/5$ on all of $C$. The measure-preserving map $(z,x)\mapsto(1-z,1-x)$ exchanges the two pieces and their centroids and preserves cap containment. Reflection $x\mapsto1-x$ then handles the two centroids of the second partition.

For comparison, the vertical cut $z=-1/2$ gives centroids $(-1,1/2)$ and $(1,1/2)$ with objective $1+3=4$. The three-fiber subsource at bases $0,1,2$ is centrally symmetric about the second centroid, so any closed containing cap takes at least $3/2$ from it. The cap $x+z/2\ge1$ takes exactly that mass from the full source, giving normalized depth $3/8$.
