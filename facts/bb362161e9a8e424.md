## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer and let \(\lambda\ge0\) and \(\eta\ge1\) be finite real numbers. The notation map to accepted facts 488f0a583886b5fc and 793210f8a4be3647 is \(N=n\); \(D,w_{\mathbf I},w_{\max},\phi_{\mathbf I},W,\mathbf J,\mathcal E_{\mathbf J},G_E,P_{\log},m_{\log},\mathcal B_{\log}\), and \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) retain their canonical meanings and normalizations. The scalar \(\lambda\) here is the penalty parameter, not a barycentric coefficient; barycentric coefficients retain the indexed notation \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). The scalar \(\eta\) is the correlation parameter, not the auxiliary submeasure called \(\eta\) in the proof of 793210f8a4be3647. Here \(r=e^{-\lambda}\) is a scalar weight parameter, not the affine rank denoted \(r\) in the realization proof of 488f0a583886b5fc.

Set \(p(t)=\max\{0,-t,t-1\}\), and define the complete table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4\}^{N},\\
\phi_{\mathbf I}
&=\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}p(I_i)
+\eta\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}2^{i-1}(I_i-1/2)\right)^2-1/4\right]
\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^{N}\setminus D),
\qquad W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},\\
r&=e^{-\lambda},\qquad \Gamma=1+r+r^2+r^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

For completeness, \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) consists of the nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}>0\) are their unique barycentric coefficients of \(\mathbf J\). Put \(G_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). The full-occurrence packing and its optimized score are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\max\left\{\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\\G_E\le L}}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\\G_E\le L,\ \mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D)\right\},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus every source in a support is charged the full component mass, as in 793210f8a4be3647, and all components share the original capacities. Define \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) to be the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over \(F\subseteq D\) intersecting every \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) with \(G_E\le L\); put \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,L\ge0}m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)/(1+L)\), as in 488f0a583886b5fc.

The table is globally extendable, \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N\), the potential is convex-admissible, and \(w_{\max}=1\). Its full total mass satisfies \(W\le3\Gamma^N\). At the center \(\mathbf J=(0,\ldots,0,1)\), the cutoff \(L=(5/2)\lambda N\) satisfies

\[
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
\ge\frac r2(2r+r^3)^{N-1}.
\]

If \(\Gamma\le2\), the singleton already gives \(2^N\mathcal B_{\log}(w)/W\ge1/3\). If \(\Gamma>2\), then \(r>1/2\), and the displayed reflected-pair reservoir gives \(2^N\mathcal B_{\log}(w)/W\ge1/150\). Consequently, for every parameter choice above,

\[
\frac{2^N\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}{W}
\ge\frac{2^N\mathcal B_{\log}(w)}{W}
\ge\frac1{150}.
\]

## Proof

First check admissibility and the complete support. The function on \(\mathbb R^N\) obtained from the formula for \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\) is convex: each \(p\) is a maximum of affine functions, the square of the displayed affine function is convex, and both coefficients are nonnegative. Call this real-valued function \(\Phi\), so that \(\Phi(\mathbf I)=\phi_{\mathbf I}\) on \(D\). Its convexity gives every required barycentric convex-admissibility inequality, not just midpoint inequalities. The polytope \(P=\operatorname{conv}D=[-3,4]^N\) satisfies \(P\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\). The function \(f=e^{-\Phi}\) on \(P\) and \(f=0\) off \(P\) is finite-valued and log-concave: convexity of \(\Phi\) proves the inequality when both endpoints lie in \(P\), and an endpoint outside \(P\) makes its right side zero. It agrees with the table at every lattice point, including every zero outside \(D\). Thus this is a globally extendable complete table in the sense of 488f0a583886b5fc.

For every integer vector \(\mathbf I\), the quantity \(\sum_i2^{i-1}(I_i-1/2)=\sum_i2^{i-1}I_i-(2^N-1)/2\) is a half-integer. Its squared value is therefore at least \(1/4\), and \(p(I_i)\ge0\). Hence \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\ge0\) on \(D\). At \(\mathbf J=(0,\ldots,0,1)\), every digit penalty is zero and the half-integer is \(1/2\), so \(\phi_{\mathbf J}=0\) and \(w_{\mathbf J}=1=w_{\max}\). In particular the displayed potential is exactly the canonical normalized potential \(\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\). The possible negativity of \(\Phi\) at noninteger real points does not affect either this normalization or the finite-valued log-concave extension.

We next bound all original mass, including all quadratic tails. For \(m\ge0\) and \(s\in\mathbb Z\), let \(Z_m(s)\) be the sum of \(\prod_{i=1}^m r^{p(I_i)}\) over all \(m\)-digit vectors in \(\{-3,\ldots,4\}^m\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^m2^{i-1}I_i=s\). Set \(Z_0(0)=1\) and \(Z_0(s)=0\) for \(s\ne0\). For \(m\ge1\), separating the first digit gives the exact recurrence

\[
Z_m(s)=\sum_{\substack{k\in\{-3,\ldots,4\}\\k\equiv s\pmod2}}
r^{p(k)}Z_{m-1}\left(\frac{s-k}{2}\right).
\]

For the even digits \(-2,0,2,4\), the penalties are respectively \(2,0,1,3\); for the odd digits \(-3,-1,1,3\), they are respectively \(3,1,0,2\). Both parity classes therefore have coefficient sum \(\Gamma\). Induction in the recurrence gives \(0\le Z_m(s)\le\Gamma^m\) for every \(s\). Using this bound and \(\eta\ge1\), with the nonnegative quadratic excess at half-integers, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z}Z_N(s)
\exp\left(-\eta\left[(s-(2^N-1)/2)^2-1/4\right]\right)\\
&\le\Gamma^N\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z+1/2}e^{-(t^2-1/4)}
=2\Gamma^N\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-k(k+1)}\\
&\le\frac{2\Gamma^N}{1-e^{-2}}
<3\Gamma^N.
\end{aligned}
\]

The geometric-series bound uses \(k(k+1)\ge2k\) for \(k\ge0\); the strict last inequality follows from \(e^2\ge1+2+2=5>3\). No site of \(D\) has been removed from \(W\).

The central singleton belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\), has gap zero, and accepts mass one. Consequently \(\mathcal B_{\log}(w)\ge1\). When \(\Gamma\le2\), the mass estimate proves \(2^N\mathcal B_{\log}(w)/W\ge(1/3)(2/\Gamma)^N\ge1/3\). Also \(\Gamma\) is strictly increasing as a function of \(r\ge0\), and \(\Gamma(1/2)=15/8<2\); hence \(\Gamma>2\) implies \(r>1/2\).

We now construct and check the reservoir. Take all chains with \(\xi_0=\xi_N=0\) and \(\xi_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\) for \(1\le i\le N-1\), and set \(v_i=2\xi_i-\xi_{i-1}\). The displacement \(\mathbf v=(v_1,\ldots,v_N)\) is the usual reflected-pair displacement; its two orientations correspond to the chains \(\xi\) and \(-\xi\). Telescoping gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N2^{i-1}v_i
=\sum_{i=1}^N2^i\xi_i-\sum_{i=1}^N2^{i-1}\xi_{i-1}
=2^N\xi_N-\xi_0=0.
\]

For \(i<N\), \(|v_i|\le3\), so both \(J_i+v_i=v_i\) and \(J_i-v_i=-v_i\) lie in \([-3,4]\). The last displacement is \(v_N=-\xi_{N-1}\in\{-1,0,1\}\), so the two last coordinates \(1\pm v_N\) lie in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Thus \(\mathbf J\pm\mathbf v\in D\), including the boundary possibilities. Both endpoints have the same half-integer \(1/2\) as \(\mathbf J\), so their quadratic contributions and the quadratic contribution to the barycentric gap are all exactly zero, for every \(\eta\).

The map from chains to displacements is injective, since \(\xi_i=(v_i+\xi_{i-1})/2\) reconstructs the chain from \(\xi_0=0\). A nonzero chain therefore gives \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0\), and the two-point set \(E_{\mathbf v}=\{\mathbf J+\mathbf v,\mathbf J-\mathbf v\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\), with both canonical barycentric coefficients equal to \(1/2\). Two such sets intersect only if their displacements are equal or opposite: an equality of endpoints says \(\varepsilon\mathbf v=\delta\mathbf u\) for signs \(\varepsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}\). Injectivity then identifies the chains up to sign. Choosing one representative of every nonzero sign orbit therefore gives pairwise disjoint two-point supports, none containing \(\mathbf J\).

For an integer \(t\) with \(|t|\le3\), \(p(t)+p(-t)=0\) if \(t=0\), and \(p(t)+p(-t)=2|t|-1\le5\) otherwise. For \(|t|\le1\), \(p(1+t)+p(1-t)\le1\). Hence every constructed pair has canonical logarithmic gap

\[
G_{E_{\mathbf v}}
=\frac\lambda2\sum_{i=1}^N\bigl[p(J_i+v_i)+p(J_i-v_i)\bigr]
\le\frac\lambda2\bigl[5(N-1)+1\bigr]
\le\frac52\lambda N=L.
\]

Assign mass \(\min\{w_{\mathbf J+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf J-\mathbf v}\}\) to each chosen pair and mass one to the central singleton. All used supports are disjoint, so each used source is charged once, at most its original capacity; every other source is charged zero. This checks the full-occurrence capacity constraints simultaneously on the entire \(D\), without dividing a source charge by its barycentric coefficient.

To lower-bound the mass, for each oriented chain observe that \(p(\pm v_i)\le|v_i|\) for \(i<N\), and \(p(1\pm v_N)\le1\). Since the endpoint quadratic terms vanish and \(0<r\le1\), its endpoint minimum satisfies

\[
\min\{w_{\mathbf J+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf J-\mathbf v}\}
\ge r\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}r^{|2\xi_i-\xi_{i-1}|}.
\]

For a previous state \(a\) and a next state \(b\), in the order \(-1,0,1\), the transition weights \(r^{|2b-a|}\) form the array

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
r&r&r^3\\
r^2&1&r^2\\
r^3&r&r
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first and last row sums are \(2r+r^3\). The middle row sum is at least this value, since \(1+2r^2-2r-r^3=(1-r)(r^2-r+1)\ge0\). Summing successively over the last free state, then the preceding free states, shows that the total product weight of the \(N-1\) free transitions starting from \(\xi_0=0\) is at least \((2r+r^3)^{N-1}\). This also holds for \(N=1\): there are no free transitions, and the empty product and the unique empty-chain sum both equal one.

The zero chain has endpoint minimum one and contributes the central singleton. Every nonzero sign orbit has two equal endpoint minima and contributes one pair. Thus the exact mass of the specified feasible packing is one half of one plus the sum of endpoint minima over all oriented chains. It is consequently at least \((r/2)(2r+r^3)^{N-1}\), proving the reservoir bound for every \(0<r\le1\).

It remains to turn that bound into an absolute coefficient in the second branch. For \(1/2\le r\le1\), direct factorizations give

\[
\begin{aligned}
4r-\Gamma&=(1-r)(r^2+2r-1)\ge0,\\
\frac{2(2r+r^3)}\Gamma-\frac65
&=\frac{2(2r-1)(r^2-r+3)}{5\Gamma}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(r^2+2r-1\ge1/4\) on the stated interval and \(r^2-r+3>0\). Also \(\lambda=-\log r\le\log2<1\). Therefore, whenever \(\Gamma>2\), the reservoir and the full mass bound yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^N\mathcal B_{\log}(w)}W
&\ge\frac{r}{3\Gamma}
\frac{\left(2(2r+r^3)/\Gamma\right)^{N-1}}
{1+(5/2)\lambda N}\\
&\ge\frac1{12}\frac{(6/5)^{N-1}}{1+(5/2)N}\\
&\ge\frac1{12}\frac{(N+4)/5}{1+(5/2)N}
\ge\frac1{150}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The penultimate inequality is the binomial bound \((1+1/5)^{N-1}\ge1+(N-1)/5\), valid also for \(N=1\). The last inequality is \(((N+4)/5)/(1+(5/2)N)\ge2/25\), which follows by cross-multiplication from \(25(N+4)\ge10+25N\). Thus the linear cutoff cost is paid once, and the coefficient does not depend on \(\lambda,\eta\), or \(N\).

Finally, for any hitting set \(F\) and any feasible packing at the same center and cutoff, every permitted support meets \(F\). Hence

\[
\sum_E x_E
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}\sum_{E:\,\mathbf I\in E}x_E
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}.
\]

Taking the packing maximum and hitting minimum proves \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\), and then optimization proves the claimed comparison of scores. All threshold and degenerate cases are included: \(\Gamma=2\) uses the singleton branch; \(r=1/2\) satisfies the reservoir algebra with equality in its ratio bound; \(\lambda=0\) has \(r=1\), cutoff zero, and the same disjoint packing; and \(N=1\) has only the central chain and singleton. The argument imposes no upper bound on \(\eta\).
