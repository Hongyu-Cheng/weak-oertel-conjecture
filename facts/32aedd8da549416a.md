## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(z\) with \(a_z>0\), define
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
 \qquad
 D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z,
\tag{2}
\]
and put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{3}
\]
Let
\[
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
 \qquad
 \mu_C(A):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}
\tag{3a}
\]
for Borel \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and define
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in S}
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \mu_C(H).
\tag{3b}
\]
For \(z\) with \(a_z>0\) and \(x\in C_z\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{proper\ closed\ halfspace}\\x\in J}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J).
\tag{3c}
\]

Part 1: exact midpoint metronoid stability. Let \(u,w\in\mathbb Z^n\) be distinct positive-volume bases of the same parity, put
\[
 m:=\frac{u+w}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 \bar c:=\frac{c_u+c_w}{2},
\tag{4}
\]
and define
\[
 D_{uw}:=\frac12(D_u+D_w),
 \qquad
 L_{uw}:=\bar c+D_{uw}
 =\frac12(\mathcal K_u+\mathcal K_w),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
 \beta_{uw}:=
 \left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_w^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\tag{6}
\]
Then \(a_m>0\), \(\beta_{uw}\leq a_m\), and
\[
 \boxed{\bar c\in\mathsf M_{\beta_{uw}}(C_m)},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\delta_C(m,\bar c)\geq\gamma_d\beta_{uw}},
\tag{7}
\]
where \(\delta_C\) is the absolute fiber depth in the normalization of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`. Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{L_{uw}\subseteq\mathsf M_{\beta_{uw}/2}(C_m)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
 \eta_{uw}:=\frac{\beta_{uw}}{2a_m-\beta_{uw}}\in(0,1].
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 c_m+\eta_{uw}(L_{uw}-c_m)
 \subseteq\mathcal K_m,}
\tag{10}
\]
equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \eta_{uw}(\bar c-c_m)+\eta_{uw}D_{uw}
 \subseteq D_m.}
\tag{11}
\]
For every \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), this gives the simultaneous support bound
\[
 \boxed{
 \eta_{uw}\left(
 h_{D_{uw}}(v)
 +\left|\langle v,\bar c-c_m\rangle\right|
 \right)
 \leq h_{D_m}(v).}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, if \(a_m\leq(1+\varepsilon)\beta_{uw}\) for some \(\varepsilon\geq0\), then
\[
 \eta_{uw}\geq\frac1{1+2\varepsilon}.
\tag{13}
\]
At exact Brunn--Minkowski equality \(a_m=\beta_{uw}\), equations (9)--(10) give
\[
 \boxed{L_{uw}\subseteq\mathcal K_m.}
\tag{14}
\]

Part 2: a collision-safe dominated midpoint-centroid measure. Let \(q=(q_e)\) be a finite nonzero endpoint-disjoint off-diagonal same-parity matching. Explicitly, every positive edge is an unordered pair \(e=\{u,w\}\) of distinct positive-volume bases with \(u\equiv w\pmod2\), every base is incident with at most one positive edge, and
\[
 0<q_e\leq\frac{a_u}{4},
 \qquad
 0<q_e\leq\frac{a_w}{4}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\},\ q_e>0).
\tag{15}
\]
For a positive edge put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+w}{2},
 \qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_w}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then the single positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \zeta_q:=4\gamma_d
 \sum_{e:q_e>0}q_e\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}}
\tag{17}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), has mass
\[
 |\zeta_q|=4\gamma_d|q|,
 \qquad |q|:=\sum_eq_e,
\tag{18}
\]
and is genuinely halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\):
\[
 \boxed{\zeta_q(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{19}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Equations (17)--(19) remain valid when different labelled edges have coincident midpoints, coincident atoms, or the same midpoint with different continuous atoms.

Let
\[
 P_q:=\{(m_e,\bar c_e):q_e>0\},
 \qquad
 \rho_q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}
 \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(P_q),
\tag{20}
\]
and let
\[
 r_q:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(P_q)
 \to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{x-s(z):(z,x)\in P_q\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{4\gamma_d|q|}{2^{\rho_q}(r_q+1)}.}
\tag{22}
\]

Let \(\rho_{\mathrm{cm}},r_{\mathrm{cm}}\) be the ranks (20)--(21) for the full dyadic midpoint-centroid atom set

 P_{\mathrm{cm}}:=

Then

 \boxed{
 D\geq

 r_{\mathrm{cm}}+1

Part 4: stress consequence. Let \(v=(v_e)_{e\in\mathcal M_2}\) be any dual-feasible stress in the normalization of accepted facts `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` and `9c47a4888e8a2947`, and put

 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 T_*:=\sum_e\omega_e.
\tag{27}

Here \(D_e=(D_u+D_w)/2\) for \(e=\{u,w\}\), and accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947` identifies this \(T_*\) with the total endpoint stress load. One has \(0\leq\omega_e\leq t_e\). Define

 \zeta_v:=4\gamma_d

Then \(\zeta_v\) is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\) and has mass \(4\gamma_dT_*\). If \(T_*>0\), let \(\rho_v,r_v\) be its ranks as in (20)--(21). Then

 D\geq
 4\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_v}

Under the canonical hard inequality in (23), this implies the exact load-sensitive rank bound

The measures in (17) and (28) are single globally dominated measures, not sums of numerical selector capacities. The conclusions force high continuous residual rank under the hard ledger. They do not extract one high-score low-rank centroid subset, aggregate different selectors or different midpoint fibers, prove the aligned centroid extraction condition `(CCE)`, prove the registered high-support stress-frame inequality `(HSF)`, or prove the weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

We prove Part 1. The constant density one on \(C_u\) has mass \(a_u\) and barycenter \(c_u\), and similarly at \(w\). Hence
\[
 c_u\in\mathsf M_{a_u}(C_u),
 \qquad
 c_w\in\mathsf M_{a_w}(C_w).
\tag{35}
\]
Apply the positive-root metronoid convexity of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` to the two triples
\[
 (u,c_u,a_u^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (w,c_w,a_w^{1/d}).
\]
Their midpoint has base \(m\), continuous point \(\bar c\), and positive root
\[
 \frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_w^{1/d}}2
 =\beta_{uw}^{1/d}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \bar c\in\mathsf M_{\beta_{uw}}(C_m).
\tag{36}
\]
In particular, the defining density has positive mass \(\beta_{uw}\) in \(C_m\), so \(a_m\geq\beta_{uw}>0\). The metronoid-depth theorem of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applied to (36) gives
\[
 \delta_C(m,\bar c)\geq\gamma_d\beta_{uw},
\]
which proves (7), including the asserted positivity and volume comparison.

Now take arbitrary \(x_u\in\mathcal K_u\) and \(x_w\in\mathcal K_w\). By definition,
\[
 x_u\in\mathsf M_{a_u/2}(C_u),
 \qquad
 x_w\in\mathsf M_{a_w/2}(C_w).
\tag{37}
\]
The corresponding positive roots are \(a_u^{1/d}/2^{1/d}\) and \(a_w^{1/d}/2^{1/d}\). Their arithmetic mean is
\[
 \frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_w^{1/d}}{2\,2^{1/d}}
 =\left(\frac{\beta_{uw}}2\right)^{1/d}.
\tag{38}
\]
Another application of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` gives
\[
 \frac{x_u+x_w}{2}
 \in\mathsf M_{\beta_{uw}/2}(C_m).
\tag{39}
\]
As \(x_u,x_w\) were arbitrary, the left side of (39) ranges over \(L_{uw}\). This proves (8).

We next prove the auxiliary mass-interpolation inclusion used to pass from (8) to the midpoint half-mass metronoid. Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body of volume \(a>0\) and centroid \(c\), and let \(0<s\leq a/2\). We claim
\[
 \boxed{
 c+\frac{s}{a-s}
 \bigl(\mathsf M_s(K)-c\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{a/2}(K).}
\tag{40}
\]
Fix \(p\in\mathsf M_s(K)\), and choose a representing density \(f\) satisfying
\[
 0\leq f\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int_Kf=s,
 \qquad
 \int_Kxf(x)\,dx=sp.
\tag{41}
\]
Put
\[
 A:=\frac{a}{2(a-s)},
 \qquad
 B:=\frac{a-2s}{2(a-s)},
 \qquad
 g:=Af+B\quad\text{on }K.
\tag{42}
\]
Since \(s\leq a/2\), one has \(A,B\geq0\), and a direct calculation gives \(A+B=1\). Hence \(0\leq g\leq1\). Its mass is
\[
 \int_Kg
 =As+Ba
 =\frac a2.
\tag{43}
\]
Using the centroid identity \(\int_Kx\,dx=ac\), its normalized first moment is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac2a\int_Kxg(x)\,dx
 &=\frac2a(As\,p+Ba\,c)\\
 &=c+\frac{s}{a-s}(p-c).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
Thus (44) belongs to \(\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)\), proving (40) without any external nesting assertion.

Apply (40) to \(K=C_m\), \(a=a_m\), and \(s=\beta_{uw}/2\). The coefficient in (40) is exactly
\[
 \frac{\beta_{uw}/2}{a_m-\beta_{uw}/2}
 =\frac{\beta_{uw}}{2a_m-\beta_{uw}}
 =\eta_{uw}.
\]
Combining (8) and (40) proves (10), and subtracting \(c_m\) gives (11).

Accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` proves that every centered half-mass metronoid \(D_z\) is origin-symmetric. Hence \(D_m=-D_m\) and \(D_{uw}=-D_{uw}\). Taking the support function of (11) in direction \(v\) gives
\[
 \eta_{uw}\langle v,\bar c-c_m\rangle
 +\eta_{uw}h_{D_{uw}}(v)
 \leq h_{D_m}(v).
\tag{45}
\]
Taking the support function in direction \(-v\), and using both symmetries, gives
\[
 -\eta_{uw}\langle v,\bar c-c_m\rangle
 +\eta_{uw}h_{D_{uw}}(v)
 \leq h_{D_m}(v).
\tag{46}
\]
The larger left side of (45)--(46) is the left side of (12), proving that inequality. If \(a_m\leq(1+\varepsilon)\beta_{uw}\), then
\[
 \eta_{uw}
 =\frac{\beta_{uw}}{2a_m-\beta_{uw}}
 \geq\frac1{1+2\varepsilon},
\]
which proves (13). If \(a_m=\beta_{uw}\), then \(\eta_{uw}=1\), and (10) is exactly (14). This completes Part 1.

We prove Part 2, paying particular attention to midpoint collisions. First, \((u,c_u)\in C\) and \((w,c_w)\in C\), since a convex body contains its centroid. Convexity of \(C\) therefore gives
\[
 (m_e,\bar c_e)
 =\frac12(u,c_u)+\frac12(w,c_w)
 \in C.
\tag{47}
\]
Same parity gives \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\), so every atom in (17) is carried by the mixed-integer set.

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace. Consider every labelled edge \(e=\{u,w\}\) whose atom \((m_e,\bar c_e)\) lies in \(H\). The defining affine functional of \(H\) takes at this atom the average of its values at \((u,c_u)\) and \((w,c_w)\). Hence at least one of those two endpoint centroid points belongs to \(H\). Orient \(e\) toward one such endpoint, making an arbitrary choice if both endpoints belong to \(H\).

The matching is endpoint-disjoint, so different labelled edges are oriented toward distinct endpoint bases. This remains true if several edges have the same midpoint, if several atoms coincide completely, or if one midpoint carries several different continuous atoms. For an oriented endpoint \(y\), let
\[
 H_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in H\}.
\tag{48}
\]
This fiber section contains \(c_y\). The constant density one realizes
\(c_y\in\mathsf M_{a_y}(C_y)\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied with metronoid mass \(a_y\), therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \geq\gamma_da_y
 \geq4\gamma_dq_e,
\tag{49}
\]
when \(H_y\) is proper. If \(H_y=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from \(a_y\geq\gamma_da_y\); an empty fiber section cannot contain \(c_y\). Summing (49) over the distinct oriented endpoint fibers gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\geq
 \sum_{\substack{e:q_e>0\\(m_e,\bar c_e)\in H}}
 4\gamma_dq_e\\
 &=\zeta_q(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{50}
\]
This proves (19) with all collision cases included. Equation (18) follows by summing the labelled weights, including weights of coincident atoms.

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to \(\zeta_q\). Its support and intrinsic ranks are exactly (20)--(21); coincident atoms are automatically aggregated by the measure and do not change the support, while different atoms at one midpoint remain different support points over the same base. That theorem yields
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{|\zeta_q|}{2^{\rho_q}(r_q+1)}
 =\frac{4\gamma_d|q|}{2^{\rho_q}(r_q+1)},
\]
which is (22).

We prove Part 3. The matching \(t=(t_e)\) of accepted fact `3016926516cc39df` is endpoint-disjoint and satisfies the quarter-capacity bounds in (23), so Part 2 applies with \(q=t\). Multiplying (22) by \(2^n\) gives the first inequality in (25). Substituting the strict mass lower bound in (23) gives the second inequality. Combining (25) with \(D<Q/100\) and cancelling \(Q>0\) yields
\[
 r_{\mathrm{cm}}+1
 >100\cdot\frac{59\gamma_d}{50}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}}
 =118\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}},
\]
which proves (26).

We prove Part 4. Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives
\[
 0\leq\omega_e=h_{D_e}(v_e)\leq t_e.
\tag{51}
\]
Thus \(\zeta_v\) is a positive submeasure of the already dominated full measure obtained from (17) with \(q=t\). It is therefore halfspace-dominated. Accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947` gives the lossless endpoint collapse
\[
 T_*=\sum_e\omega_e,
\tag{52}
\]
so \(|\zeta_v|=4\gamma_dT_*\). Applying accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` and multiplying by \(2^n\) proves (29). Combining (29) with \(D<Q/100\) gives
\[
 \frac Q{100}
 >4\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_v}
 \frac{T_*}{r_v+1},
\]
which rearranges to (30).

It remains to prove the load-minimal specialization. Apply accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` to the scalar matching \(t\). Its matching mass is \(S_2\), and its gauge-load lower bound gives
\[
 T_*\geq S_2-R_C(t),
\tag{53}
\]
where (52) supplies the exact alias from its gauge load to the present \(T_*\). By the definition of \(R_C(t)\) in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`, there is an affinely half-metronoid-realizable submatching of mass \(R_C(t)\). The affine matching certificate of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` therefore gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}R_C(t),
\]
which is (32) after multiplication by \(2^n\). The hard upper bound in (23) implies
\[
 R_C(t)<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}.
\tag{54}
\]
Combining (23), (53), and (54) proves (33). Substituting (33) into (30) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_v+1
 &>400\gamma_d
 \left(\frac{59}{200}
 -\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)
 2^{n-\rho_v}\\
 &=(118\gamma_d-4)2^{n-\rho_v},
 \end{aligned}
\]
which is (34). Every conclusion asserted in the statement has now been proved.
