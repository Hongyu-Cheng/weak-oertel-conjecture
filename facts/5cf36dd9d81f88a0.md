## Statement

For $m\ge2$ on the open domain $h<x_j<0$, set $\rho=\prod_j(1-x_j)$, $E=\rho^{-1}-1$, and
$$
L=\left(1-\frac{\sum_jx_j}{E}\right)\prod_j(1+x_j/E),\qquad
R=\rho(E+h)\left[1-\frac{(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)}{h^{m+1}}\right].
$$
Then $R/L$ is positive and has no local maximum. More explicitly, for $0<X_j<H<1$ define $\rho_+=\prod_j(1-X_j)$, $E_+=\rho_+^{-1}-1$, $C_+=\sum_j X_j/[(1-X_j)(1-\rho_+)]-1$, $Q_+=\prod_j(1+X_j/E_+)$, $W_+=(1-H)C_+Q_+$, and $R_+=\rho_+(E_++H)[1-(E_++H)\prod_j(H-X_j)/H^{m+1}]$. Under $x_j=-X_j/(1-X_j)$, $h=-H/(1-H)$ the ratio $R/L$ equals $R_+/W_+$.

## Proof

The coordinate substitution is a smooth bijection. It gives $\rho=1/\rho_+$, $E=\rho_+-1$, $1-\sum_jx_j/E=-C_+$, $1+x_j/E=(1+X_j/E_+)/(1-X_j)$, and $E+h=-\rho_+(E_++H)/(1-H)$. Also $h-x_j=-(H-X_j)/[(1-H)(1-X_j)]$. Thus the fraction $\Phi=(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)/h^{m+1}$ is unchanged by the substitution, $L=-C_+Q_+/\rho_+$, and $R=-R_+/[\rho_+(1-H)]$. In the positive variables, $C_+>0$ because $1-\rho_+\le\sum_jX_j<\sum_j X_j/(1-X_j)$. To see $0<\Phi<1$, take independent unit-rate exponentials with two positive coefficients $p=1$, $q=1-H$ and negative coefficients $-r_j=-qX_j/(H-X_j)$. Integrating the survival probability of $pY_p+qY_q$ against $\sum_jr_jY_j$ gives $\Phi=(A-qB)/H$, where $A=\prod_j(1+r_j)^{-1}$ and $B=\prod_j q/(q+r_j)$. Indeed $B/A=\rho_+$ and $B=\prod_j(1-X_j/H)$, yielding exactly the displayed expression for $\Phi$. This signed sum has strictly positive probabilities on both sides by unboundedness and independence of the phases. It follows that $L,R<0$ and the ratio is positive.

Suppose a local maximum has value $\Lambda>0$. Since $L<0$, the function $R-\Lambda L$ has a local minimum of zero. Fixing $h,E$ and the other coordinates, put $u=1-x_i$, $v=1-x_j$, $k=uv$ and $T_0=u+v$. With $S_o=\sum_{l\ne i,j}x_l$,
$$
L(T_0)=\frac{\prod_{l\ne i,j}(E+x_l)}{E^{m+1}}
 (E-S_o-2+T_0)[(E+1)^2-(E+1)T_0+k].
$$
Here $E\in(-1,0)$ and $E+x_l<0$, so the prefactor is negative and $L$ is strictly convex in $T_0$. The moving factor in $R$ is $(h-1)^2+(h-1)T_0+k$, so $R$ is affine in $T_0$. At unequal coordinates the derivative of $u+k/u$ is nonzero and yields a two-sided feasible interval. Strict concavity of $R-\Lambda L$ on that interval rules out a minimum. Hence all coordinates equal a common $x<0$.

For this family put $U=E-mx$, $Z=E((m+1)x-1)+mx$, $A_1=(E+x)^2-(E+1)U=Z+x(E+x)$, and $D=x[mh+(m+1)E]-Eh$, with $E=(1-x)^{-m}-1$. Strict convexity of $E(x)$ gives $U>0$ and $Z=(1-x)(xE'-E)>0$, using $E(0)=0$, $E'(0)=m$, and $E'=m(E+1)/(1-x)$. We also have $E+x<0$ and $A_1>0$. Stationarity in $h$ gives $R_h=0$, and differentiating $\Phi=(E+h)(h-x)^m/h^{m+1}$ gives
$$
1-\Phi=(E+h)\Phi_h,\qquad \frac{\Phi}{1-\Phi}=\frac{h(h-x)}D .
$$
Since $h<0$, $h-x<0$ and $0<\Phi<1$, this implies $D>0$. The equal-coordinate derivatives of $\log(-L)$ and $\log(-R)$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{L_x}{L}
&=\frac{E'-m}{U}+m\frac{E'+1}{E+x}-(m+1)\frac{E'}E
 =\frac{m(m+1)xZ}{(1-x)E U(E+x)},\\
\frac{R_x}{R}
&=\frac{E'}{E+h}-\frac{E'}{E+1}
-\frac{\Phi}{1-\Phi}\left(\frac{E'}{E+h}-\frac{m}{h-x}\right)
 =\frac{m(m+1)x(1-h)}{(1-x)D}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using $E'$ and $\Phi/(1-\Phi)$ gives the rightmost forms by common denominators. Stationarity of the ratio equates these derivatives, yielding $EU(E+x)(1-h)=DZ$. On collecting the $h$ terms, their coefficient after rearrangement is $(E+1)U^2>0$, so
$$
h=H_{\mathrm{val}}=\frac{EU(E+x)-(m+1)ExZ}{(E+1)U^2}.
$$

The feasible split $u=(1-x)e^s$, $v=(1-x)e^{-s}$ increases $T_0$ from its equal value at small nonzero $s$. It requires $R_{T_0}-\Lambda L_{T_0}\ge0$. Dividing by $R=\Lambda L<0$ gives $L_{T_0}/L\ge R_{T_0}/R$. The above affine factors and $\Phi/(1-\Phi)$ give
$$
\frac{L_{T_0}}L=\frac{A_1}{U(E+x)^2},\qquad
\frac{R_{T_0}}R=\frac{h(1-h)}{(h-x)D}.
$$
Inserting $EU(E+x)(1-h)=DZ$ and multiplying by the positive product of denominators $U(E+x)^2E(h-x)$ yields $E(h-x)A_1-h(E+x)Z\ge0$. The difference equals $x[h(E+1)U-EA_1]$. As $x<0$ this yields $h\le H_b=EA_1/[(E+1)U]$.

Subtracting these two thresholds over the common denominator gives
$$
H_{\mathrm{val}}-H_b
=\frac{E(E+x)[(1-x)U-Z]}{(E+1)U^2}
=\frac{E(E+x)(1-x)[2E-x(E'+m)]}{(E+1)U^2}.
$$
All factors outside the last bracket are positive. The function $E'(s)=m(1-s)^{-m-1}$ is strictly convex on $[x,0]$, so it lies strictly below its endpoint chord on $(x,0)$. Integrating the chord gives
$$
\frac{E(x)}x=\frac1{-x}\int_x^0 E'(s)\,ds<\frac{E'(x)+m}{2}.
$$
Multiplying by $x<0$ gives $2E>x(E'+m)$. Thus $H_{\mathrm{val}}>H_b$, contradicting stationarity and pair stability.
