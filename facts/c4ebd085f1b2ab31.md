## Statement

At bases $-1,0,1$ give the fibers $aT,cT,bT$ their summed planar area measure, where $a,b,c>0$ and $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$. Define raw depth as the infimum of mass in closed ambient halfspaces containing the point. Call the three continuous normals in the directions of $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$ canonical. Every minimizing halfspace at the middle centroid $(0,0)$ has a nonzero continuous normal in a canonical or opposite-canonical direction. Set
$$
f_r(k)=\begin{cases}0&k\le-2r,\\(2r+k)^2/2&-2r\le k\le r,\\(9/2)r^2&k\ge r.\end{cases}
$$
For $g(\alpha)=f_a(-\alpha)+f_b(\alpha)$ one has
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min g&=\min\{(a+b)^2,(9/2)a^2,(9/2)b^2\},\\
\max g&=\max\{(9/2)a^2+(2b-a)_+^2/2,\ (9/2)b^2+(2a-b)_+^2/2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The raw middle-centroid depth is the smaller of $2c^2+\min g$ and $(5/2)c^2+(9/2)(a^2+b^2)-\max g$.

If $c\ge(a+b)/2$, these are precisely the integer fibers of an actual convex body. At its middle centroid every minimizing continuous direction is canonical and the raw depth is $D_0=2c^2+\min g$. Each fiber's centroid maximizes depth within that fiber. At either endpoint base the depth at every point equals its ordinary planar depth in the endpoint triangle. The global raw maximum is therefore $\max\{2a^2,2b^2,D_0\}$, and the normalized maximum divides this by $(9/2)(a^2+c^2+b^2)$.

## Proof

Move any containing cap parallel to pass through the point. At the middle centroid a nonvertical cap containing it strictly loses positive own-fiber area under this move, because the centroid is interior to that triangle. A pure-base cap through the centroid contains its full middle fiber and at least one full endpoint. A sufficiently large finite integer tilt with a fixed nonzero continuous normal preserves the full/empty endpoint pattern and replaces the full middle fiber by a proper centroid cap. This gives strictly less mass. With a unit continuous normal, both endpoint traces saturate for sufficiently large $|\alpha|$ by boundedness, uniformly over the circle. Clamping the tilt at that bound preserves the traces. The mass is continuous in the resulting compact set of normals and tilts, because every nonvertical boundary has zero planar measure on all fibers. Thus a minimum is attained.

Apply the centered triangle mixed-state formulas. In the angular chart suppose $0<\theta<1$ and $Q$ is minimal. The case $\alpha=0$ is impossible because every centroid section there has $\ell_z>2r_z$, so $Q_\theta>0$. Reflect the base and interchange the endpoint names if necessary to take $\alpha>0$. Integer stationarity gives equal endpoint projected lengths $l$. The source length $L$ is $(2-\theta)c/(1-\theta)$ for $\theta\le1/2$ and $(1+\theta)c/\theta$ for $\theta\ge1/2$, hence $L>2c$. Angular stationarity implies
$$
l(a+b-l)=L(L-2c)/2>0.
$$
Thus $0<l<D:=a+b$. Both endpoints are proper. If both were in $I$, equal lengths would give $2(1-\theta)l=(2-\theta)D$ and $l>D$. If both were in $J$, one would have $2\theta l=(1+\theta)D$ and again $l>D$. Left $J$ requires $\theta>1/2$ from $-\alpha\ge(1-2\theta)a$, and right $I$ requires $\theta<1/2$ from $\alpha\le(1-2\theta)b$. Therefore the only possible pair is left $I$, right $J$, with transitions allowed provisionally. Their two width equations give
$$
3a=(1+\theta)D+(1-2\theta)l,\qquad
3b=(2-\theta)D-(1-2\theta)l.
$$
A transition at left would give $l=3a$ and then $D=2\theta l/(1+\theta)<l$. A transition at right would give $D=2(1-\theta)l/(2-\theta)<l$. Thus both states are strict.

For $\theta<1/2$, the $\alpha\alpha$ entry of the full Hessian is $1/(1-\theta)-1/\theta<0$. For $\theta>1/2$, the endpoint part is the difference of two rank-one forms in $(-1,l-a),(1,l-b)$, with determinant $-(D-2l)^2/[(1-\theta)\theta]$. The source is in $J$ and contributes only $-(L-c)^2/\theta$ to the angular diagonal. Since the $\alpha\alpha$ entry is now positive, the full determinant is strictly negative. At $\theta=1/2$, use the smooth cell to its right. The endpoint widths imply $a=b$, and the right Hessian has $\alpha\alpha$ entry zero and mixed entry $2(D-2l)$. If this is nonzero, a direction with positive angular component and suitably signed finite tilt component gives a negative quadratic value. If it is zero, $a=b=l$ and the endpoint angular contributions cancel, leaving the source term $-8c^2$. In either case a small forward step decreases $Q$, since the first derivatives vanish and the endpoint states remain strict. This excludes every interior angle. The two boundary angles are the canonical and opposite-canonical directions.

In the canonical direction $(1,0)$ the corner-area formula is $f_r(k)$ as displayed, and the middle contribution is $2c^2$. The opposite trace is its complement up to a null line, with reversed tilt. At the two tails of $g$ the values are $(9/2)a^2$ and $(9/2)b^2$. On the interval where both terms are proper, $g$ is a strictly convex quadratic with stationary tilt $\alpha=a-b$ and value $(a+b)^2$. This tilt is feasible exactly for $a/2\le b\le2a$. Outside that range the square exceeds the smaller plateau, so including it in the minimum makes no difference. A single proper term is strictly monotone except at its zero derivative at an empty transition. A full transition at $-a$ or $b$ jumps the derivative downward and cannot be a minimum. At an empty transition the derivative is continuous and can vanish only with the other term saturated or at a simultaneous proper quadratic endpoint. These cases are already among the plateaus or stationary point. This proves the minimum formula. The maximum on the both-proper interval is at an endpoint, and the single-proper intervals are monotone. The outer plateaus and the two full transitions suffice: any empty transition either meets a plateau or the monotone single-proper interval moves toward a larger value in the direction away from the empty term. Evaluating the full transitions gives the two displayed maxima, each at least its respective plateau. This proves the two middle formulas.

Compatibility of the three specified fibers is equivalent to $(aT+bT)/2\subset cT$, hence to $c\ge(a+b)/2$. For sufficiency use the positive piecewise affine concave radius $r(z)$ interpolating $a,c,b$ on $[-1,1]$ and the body with real fibers $r(z)T$. Its convexity follows from radius concavity and $sT+tT=(s+t)T$ for $s,t\ge0$, with $0\in T$. Its actual integer census consists exactly of the three specified fibers. Suppose $a\le b$. The second candidate for $\max g$ dominates the first: their difference is $3(b^2-a^2)$ for $b\le2a$, and $(5/2)b^2+2ab-5a^2>0$ for $b\ge2a$. Thus the opposite minimum is $(5/2)c^2+(9/2)a^2-(2a-b)_+^2/2$. Its difference from the canonical minimum is $c^2/2>0$ when $b\ge2a$. For $a\le b<2a$, substitute $c^2/2\ge(a+b)^2/8$ in that difference. Bounding $\min g$ by $(a+b)^2$ gives the lower bound $(a+b)(13a-11b)/8$. Bounding it by $(9/2)a^2$ gives $3(b-a)(5a-b)/8$. The first is positive for $b<13a/11$, and the second is positive from that threshold through $b<2a$. Thus only canonical directions minimize for actual compatible data.

Depth superlevel sets are convex within a fiber, since any closed halfspace containing a convex combination of two marks contains at least one of them. Averaging any point under the triangle's linear vertex symmetries therefore reaches its own centroid with depth at least as large. At either endpoint base every cap containing a point includes at least its ordinary depth on that fiber, and any ordinary cap can be isolated there by a sufficiently large signed base tilt excluding the two other bases. The endpoint depths therefore agree pointwise with ordinary planar depths. The ordinary centroid depth in $rT$ is $2r^2$: the mixed-state angular derivative for the one centroid trace is positive throughout $0<\theta<1$, since its section length exceeds $2r$, and the canonical endpoint value is $2r^2$. Combining the three centroid values gives the global formula.
