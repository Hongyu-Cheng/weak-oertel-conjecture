## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z,
\]
and assume \(W>0\). Let \(\mu\) be the mixed-volume measure carried by \(S\):
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}.
\]
Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfy \(w_{z_0}>0\) and
\[
\sum_{z\in Q_0\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_z\ge 2^{-n}W
\tag{1}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q_0\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z_0\). Let \(y_0\in\operatorname{int}C_{z_0}\) satisfy
\[
t:=h_S((z_0,y_0))
=\max_{y\in C_{z_0}}h_S((z_0,y)).
\]
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and write \(\delta:=t+\varepsilon\).

Suppose the conclusion of accepted fact `981a6c3aa5982216` supplies an integer \(r_0\), Euclidean unit vectors \(u_q=(a_q,b_q)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), positive numbers \(\theta_q\) summing to one, and closed halfspaces
\[
H_q:=\{(z,x):a_q^\top(z-z_0)+b_q^\top(x-y_0)\ge0\},
\qquad 1\le q\le r_0,
\]
such that
\[
\mu(H_q)\le\delta,
\qquad
\sum_{q=1}^{r_0}\theta_qb_q=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume the hard branch
\[
b_q\ne0\qquad(1\le q\le r_0).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose an inclusion-minimal subset of the vectors \(b_q\) whose convex hull contains zero. After relabeling the retained indices as \(1,\ldots,r\), there are replacement weights \(\lambda_i\), not obtained merely by restricting the original \(\theta_q\), such that
\[
2\le r\le r_0\le d+1,
\qquad
\lambda_i>0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i b_i=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) are affinely independent and
\[
0\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}\{b_1,\ldots,b_r\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
\bar a:=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i a_i.
\tag{6}
\]
There is a linear map \(L:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) such that the affine shear
\[
\Phi(z,x):=(z,x+L(z-z_0))
\tag{7}
\]
is invertible, fixes \((z_0,y_0)\), preserves each integer base fiber, has determinant one, preserves mixed volume and all halfspace depths, and transforms every retained halfspace into
\[
\Phi(H_i)
=\{(z,x):s_i(z,x)\ge0\},
\qquad
s_i(z,x):=\bar a^\top(z-z_0)+b_i^\top(x-y_0).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, every transformed cap still has mixed mass at most \(\delta\). In the remainder, replace \(C,S,H_i,\mu\) by their images under \(\Phi\) and drop the tildes. The fiber weights \(w_z\), the total mass \(W\), the anchor \((z_0,y_0)\), and its depth \(t\) are unchanged.

Define
\[
\sigma(z):=\bar a^\top(z-z_0),
\qquad
Q:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\sigma(z)\ge0\},
\qquad
P(z,x):=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\mathbf 1_{\{s_i(z,x)\ge0\}},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
R:=\max_{(z,x)\in S,\ 1\le i\le r}|s_i(z,x)|,
\qquad
\tau:=2^{-n}.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(\bar a=0\), then \(Q=\mathbb R^n\). In every case,
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_z\ge\tau W.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(R=0\), then every \(H_i\) contains \(S\) and \(\delta\ge W\). If \(R>0\), then the exact score-concentration inequalities are
\[
\delta\ge
\frac1R\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_z\sigma(z)
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\delta\ge
\frac1{2R}
\int_{S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)}
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i|s_i(z,x)|\,d\mu(z,x).
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
\delta\le\frac18\tau W,
\tag{14}
\]
then necessarily \(R>0\), and there is a measurable set
\(G\subseteq S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)\) such that
\[
\mu(G)\ge\frac12\tau W,
\tag{15}
\]
while every \((z,x)\in G\) satisfies
\[
0\le\sigma(z)<\frac{4\delta}{\tau W}R
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i|s_i(z,x)|
<\frac{8\delta}{\tau W}R.
\tag{17}
\]

For \(1\le i\le r\), define the strict exclusive region
\[
E_i:=C\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)
\cap\{s_i\ge0\}
\cap\bigcap_{k\ne i}\{s_k<0\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Because every \(b_i\ne0\), each cap boundary has zero \(d\)-volume on every integer fiber and hence
\[
\mu(\partial H_i)=0.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus changing strict to non-strict conventions on these boundaries does not change any mixed volume below. For distinct \(i,j\),
\[
u\in E_i,\quad v\in E_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{u+v}{2}\in C\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)\cap(H_i\cup H_j).
\tag{20}
\]

Let \(\pi(z,x):=z\), and for \(z\in\mathbb R^n\) define
\[
E_i(z):=\{x:(z,x)\in E_i\},
\qquad
e_i(z):=\mathcal H_d(E_i(z)),
\tag{21}
\]
\[
A_i(z):=\{x:(z,x)\in C\cap H_i\},
\qquad
h_i(z):=\mathcal H_d(A_i(z)).
\tag{22}
\]
For every pair \(i\ne j\), every
\[
z\in\pi(E_i)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
z'\in\pi(E_j)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
z\equiv z'\pmod 2,
\tag{23}
\]
the midpoint \(m:=(z+z')/2\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z^n\), and
\[
h_i(m)+h_j(m)
\ge
\left(
\frac{e_i(z)^{1/d}+e_j(z')^{1/d}}2
\right)^d
\ge
\sqrt{e_i(z)e_j(z')}.
\tag{24}
\]
The quantification in (23) means that both exclusive fibers are nonempty; (24) remains valid when either or both of their \(d\)-dimensional volumes are zero. No colored expansion, phase collapse, heavy-slice, or rank-drop conclusion follows here without an additional argument.

## Proof

The positive weights in (2) show that zero lies in the convex hull of the original \(b_q\). Choose an inclusion-minimal subset \(I\) with this property and choose convex coefficients \(\alpha_i\ge0\) on \(I\) representing zero. Minimality forces every \(\alpha_i>0\), since a zero coefficient would give a smaller balancing subset. If the retained \(b_i\) were affinely dependent, there would be real numbers \(c_i\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_i c_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i c_i b_i=0.
\tag{25}
\]
The coefficients \(c_i\) have both signs. Starting from the strictly positive vector \((\alpha_i)\), move a positive distance in the direction \((c_i)\) until the first coefficient becomes zero. Throughout this motion the coefficients sum to one and still represent zero; at the stopping time they are nonnegative and have smaller support. This contradicts minimality. Hence the retained \(b_i\) are affinely independent. Their convex representation of zero is therefore unique and strictly positive. Rename its coefficients \(\lambda_i\). These are replacement weights; there is no reason for them to equal normalized restrictions of the original \(\theta_q\). Assumption (3) rules out a one-element balancing family, so \(r\ge2\), while \(r\le r_0\le d+1\). Strict positivity of every barycentric coordinate gives (5). Every retained normal remains a unit vector and every retained cap keeps the mass bound in (2).

We construct the shear. Let
\[
V:=\operatorname{aff}\{b_1,\ldots,b_r\}.
\]
Because zero belongs to this affine hull, \(V\) is a linear subspace of \(\mathbb R^d\). Affine independence gives a unique affine map
\[
F:V\to\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
F(b_i)=a_i.
\]
Using (4) and affinity,
\[
F(0)=F\!\left(\sum_i\lambda_i b_i\right)
=\sum_i\lambda_iF(b_i)
=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i
=\bar a.
\tag{26}
\]
Therefore \(F(b)=\bar a+T_0b\) on \(V\) for a linear map \(T_0:V\to\mathbb R^n\). Extend \(T_0\) linearly to a map \(T:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^n\), and take
\[
L:=T^\top:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d.
\]
Then
\[
a_i=\bar a+Tb_i=\bar a+L^\top b_i.
\tag{27}
\]
Writing \(\widetilde x=x+L(z-z_0)\), so that
\(x=\widetilde x-L(z-z_0)\), gives the exact sign identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_i^\top(z-z_0)+b_i^\top(x-y_0)
&=(a_i-L^\top b_i)^\top(z-z_0)
  +b_i^\top(\widetilde x-y_0)\\
&=\bar a^\top(z-z_0)+b_i^\top(\widetilde x-y_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(\Phi(H_i)\) is exactly the closed halfspace in (8), with no reversal of its inequality.

The inverse shear is
\[
\Phi^{-1}(z,x)=(z,x-L(z-z_0)).
\]
The linear part of \(\Phi\) has block matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}I_n&0\\L&I_d\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is one. More specifically for mixed volume, on every fixed base fiber \(z\), \(\Phi\) is the translation
\(x\mapsto x+L(z-z_0)\), so it preserves \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure and maps \(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d\) onto itself. Hence \(\Phi(\Lambda)=\Lambda\), \(\Phi(S)=\Phi(C)\cap\Lambda\), every \(w_z\) is unchanged, and mixed volume is preserved fiber by fiber. The map is invertible affine, so it maps convex bodies to convex bodies and gives a bijection between closed halfspaces. It fixes \((z_0,y_0)\). Therefore, for every \(p\in S\), the correspondence \(H\leftrightarrow\Phi(H)\) between halfspaces containing \(p\) and halfspaces containing \(\Phi(p)\), together with fiberwise measure preservation, gives
\[
h_{\Phi(S)}(\Phi(p))=h_S(p).
\tag{29}
\]
This proves every asserted shear invariance and the transformed cap mass bound.

In the transformed coordinates, balance (4) gives the pointwise identity
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i s_i(z,x)
=\sigma(z)+\left(\sum_i\lambda_i b_i\right)^\top(x-y_0)
=\sigma(z).
\tag{30}
\]
The fiber weights are unchanged by the shear. If \(\bar a\ne0\), then \(Q\) is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(z_0\), so (1) gives (11). If \(\bar a=0\), then \(Q=\mathbb R^n\), its mass is \(W\), and (11) again holds.

Compactness of \(S\) and finiteness of the retained family make \(R<\infty\). If \(R=0\), every \(s_i\) vanishes on \(S\). Thus every closed cap \(H_i\) contains all of \(S\), so
\[
W=\mu(H_i)\le\delta.
\tag{31}
\]
This proves the stated zero branch and shows that (14) cannot hold there.

Now suppose \(R>0\). From the cap mass bounds and (4),
\[
\int_S P\,d\mu
=\sum_i\lambda_i\mu(H_i)
\le\delta.
\tag{32}
\]
At a point \((z,x)\in S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)\), equation (30) and \(\sigma(z)\ge0\) give
\[
\sigma(z)
=\sum_i\lambda_i s_i(z,x)
\le\sum_{s_i(z,x)\ge0}\lambda_i s_i(z,x)
\le RP(z,x).
\tag{33}
\]
Since \(\sigma\) is constant on each fiber, integration of (33), followed by (32), yields
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_z\sigma(z)
\le R\int_{S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)}P\,d\mu
\le R\delta,
\]
which is (12). On the same set,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\lambda_i|s_i|
&=2\sum_{s_i\ge0}\lambda_i s_i-\sum_i\lambda_i s_i\\
&=2\sum_{s_i\ge0}\lambda_i s_i-\sigma(z)\\
&\le2RP.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Integrating (34) and using (32) proves (13).

Assume (14), and set
\[
\eta:=\frac{4\delta}{\tau W}.
\]
Here \(\eta>0\), and (14) gives \(\eta\le1/2\). By (12),
\[
\mu\{(z,x)\in S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d):
\sigma(z)\ge\eta R\}
\le\frac{\delta}{\eta}
=\frac14\tau W.
\tag{35}
\]
By (13),
\[
\mu\left\{(z,x)\in S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d):
\sum_i\lambda_i|s_i(z,x)|\ge2\eta R\right\}
\le\frac{\delta}{\eta}
=\frac14\tau W.
\tag{36}
\]
Remove these two measurable bad sets from \(S\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)\) and call the remainder \(G\). Equations (11), (35), and (36) imply
\[
\mu(G)\ge\tau W-\frac14\tau W-\frac14\tau W
=\frac12\tau W.
\]
The use of \(\ge\) in the bad-set definitions gives the strict upper bounds (16) and (17) on \(G\), with thresholds \(\eta R\) and \(2\eta R\), respectively. This verifies the constants in the good-set claim.

We next record the boundary issue. On the integer fiber over \(z\), the boundary of \(H_i\) is contained in
\[
\{x\in\mathbb R^d:b_i^\top(x-y_0)=-\sigma(z)\}.
\tag{37}
\]
Because \(b_i\ne0\), this is an affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^d\) and has \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero. Only finitely many integer fibers meet the compact body \(C\), so summing the fiberwise zero measures proves (19). This justifies every claimed strict-versus-non-strict volume convention.

Fix distinct \(i,j\), take \(u\in E_i\) and \(v\in E_j\), and abbreviate score evaluation at a point in the evident way. At \(u\), equation (30), membership in \(Q\), and strict negativity of all scores except the \(i\)-th give
\[
0\le\sigma(u)
=\lambda_i s_i(u)+\lambda_j s_j(u)
 +\sum_{k\ne i,j}\lambda_k s_k(u)
\le\lambda_i s_i(u)+\lambda_j s_j(u).
\]
Hence
\[
-\lambda_j s_j(u)\le\lambda_i s_i(u).
\tag{38}
\]
Similarly,
\[
-\lambda_i s_i(v)\le\lambda_j s_j(v).
\tag{39}
\]
Put
\[
A:=\lambda_i s_i(u)\ge0,
\qquad
B:=\lambda_j s_j(v)\ge0.
\]
If \(A\ge B\), then affinity of \(s_i\) and (39) imply
\[
2\lambda_i s_i\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)
=\lambda_i s_i(u)+\lambda_i s_i(v)
\ge A-B\ge0,
\]
so the midpoint belongs to \(H_i\). If \(B\ge A\), affinity of \(s_j\) and (38) similarly put the midpoint in \(H_j\). Convexity of \(C\) and of \(Q\) also puts the midpoint in \(C\cap(Q\times\mathbb R^d)\). This proves (20), including the equality case \(A=B\).

Finally fix indices and base points as in (23), and set
\[
m:=\frac{z+z'}2.
\]
Coordinatewise equality of the parities makes \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\). Both \(E_i(z)\) and \(E_j(z')\) are nonempty bounded measurable subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\). Applying (20) pointwise and using convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac{E_i(z)+E_j(z')}2
\subseteq
C_m\cap\bigl(A_i(m)\cup A_j(m)\bigr).
\tag{40}
\]
Consequently,
\[
h_i(m)+h_j(m)
\ge
\mathcal H_d\!\left(\frac{E_i(z)+E_j(z')}2\right).
\tag{41}
\]
Write \(a:=e_i(z)\) and \(b:=e_j(z')\). If \(a,b>0\), the \(d\)-dimensional Brunn-Minkowski inequality gives
\[
\mathcal H_d\!\left(\frac{E_i(z)+E_j(z')}2\right)
\ge
\left(\frac{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\tag{42}
\]
If \(a=0<b\), choose any \(x_0\in E_i(z)\), which is possible by (23). Then
\[
\frac{x_0+E_j(z')}2
\subseteq
\frac{E_i(z)+E_j(z')}2
\]
has volume \(2^{-d}b\), exactly the right-hand side of (42) when \(a=0\). The case \(b=0<a\) is symmetric. If \(a=b=0\), the right-hand side is zero and (42) follows from nonnegativity. Thus (42), and therefore the first inequality in (24), holds in every zero-volume case allowed by (23). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
\frac{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}2\ge (ab)^{1/(2d)},
\]
and raising to the \(d\)-th power proves the second inequality in (24). This completes the structural package and makes no step toward the still-unproved colored expansion-or-rank-drop bridge.
