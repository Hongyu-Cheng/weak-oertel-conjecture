## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and write
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d.
\]
For every Borel set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define its mixed-integer volume by
\[
\mu(B):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(B_{\mathbf z}),\qquad
B_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in B\}.
\]
Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, let
\(\mathbf q_0=(\mathbf b,\mathbf v_0)\in C\), and let \(0<\alpha<1\).  Set
\[
D:=(1-\alpha)C+\alpha\mathbf q_0,\qquad
\mathbf p:=(1-\alpha)\mathfrak c(C)+\alpha\mathbf q_0,
\]
and assume
\[
\mathbf p\in\Lambda.
\]
For \(\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n\), define
\[
\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}:=
C\cap\left[
\left(\mathbf b+\alpha^{-1}(\mathbb Z^n-\mathbf t)\right)
\times\mathbb R^d
\right].
\]
If \(L:\mathbb R^{n+d}\to\mathbb R\) is affine, define the extended-real Borel function
\[
\Delta_L(\mathbf t):=
\begin{cases}
L(\mathbf q_0)-\max_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}}L(\mathbf q),
&\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}\ne\varnothing,\\
+\infty,&\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n\), let
\(\{\mathbf u\}:=\mathbf u-\lfloor\mathbf u\rfloor\in[0,1)^n\), with the floor taken coordinatewise.  Then
\[
\mu\bigl(C\cap\{L\ge0\}\bigr)
\ge
\operatorname{vol}_m\left\{
\mathbf w\in D:
L(\mathbf w)\ge
\alpha\Delta_L(\{\mathbf w_I\})
\right\},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathbf w_I\) denotes the first \(n\) coordinates of \(\mathbf w\).  The set on the right of (1) is Borel.

Suppose in addition that the phase-core condition
\[
\mathbf q_0\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}
\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n
\tag{2}
\]
holds.  Put
\[
\gamma_m:=\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m.
\]
Then \(\mathbf p\in C\cap\Lambda\), and every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p\) satisfies
\[
\mu(C\cap H)\ge
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)
\ge
\gamma_m(1-\alpha)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

We first construct a Borel maximizing anchor for every nonempty phase.  We use the following elementary compact-section selection observation.

Let \(X,Y\) be compact metric spaces, let
\(\Gamma\subseteq X\times Y\) be closed, and let
\(g:X\times Y\to\mathbb R\) be continuous.  On
\[
T:=\{t\in X:\Gamma_t\ne\varnothing\},
\qquad
\Gamma_t:=\{y\in Y:(t,y)\in\Gamma\},
\]
there is a Borel map \(s:T\to Y\) such that
\[
s(t)\in\Gamma_t,\qquad
g(t,s(t))=\max_{y\in\Gamma_t}g(t,y).
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a direct construction.  For every closed \(F\subseteq Y\), put
\[
M_F(t):=\max\{g(t,y):y\in\Gamma_t\cap F\},
\]
with value \(-\infty\) when \(\Gamma_t\cap F=\varnothing\).  Compactness and the closedness of \(\Gamma\) show by the usual convergent-subsequence argument that \(M_F\) is upper semicontinuous as an extended-real function, hence Borel.  Therefore
\[
\{t\in T:\operatorname{argmax}_{\Gamma_t}g\cap F\ne\varnothing\}
=\{t\in T:M_F(t)=M_Y(t)\}
\tag{5}
\]
is Borel.  For each \(j\ge1\), fix a finite cover of \(Y\) by closed balls of radius \(2^{-j}\).  Starting with \(F_0=Y\), and on each Borel cell determined by the preceding choices, choose the first ball in the \(j\)-th cover for which the set in (5), with \(F\) equal to its intersection with \(F_{j-1}\), contains the current parameter.  This refines the parameter space into finitely many Borel cells and gives nested closed sets \(F_j(t)\) meeting the argmax set, with
\(\operatorname{diam}F_j(t)\le2^{1-j}\).  The centers of the chosen balls are Borel finite-valued functions and converge to the unique point in
\[
\bigcap_{j\ge1}
\left(F_j(t)\cap\operatorname{argmax}_{\Gamma_t}g\right).
\]
Their pointwise limit is Borel and proves (4).

Apply this observation as follows.  Let
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C),\qquad
K:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^d}(C),
\]
which are compact, and let
\[
Z:=
\mathbb Z^n\cap
\bigl([0,1]^n+\alpha(P-\mathbf b)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
The set \(Z\) is finite.  It contains \(\mathbf0\), since
\(\mathbf b\in P\).  Regard the finite disjoint union \(Y:=Z\times K\) as a compact metric space, and define the closed set
\[
\Gamma:=
\left\{
\bigl(\mathbf t,(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\bigr)
\in[0,1]^n\times Y:
\left(\mathbf b+\alpha^{-1}(\mathbf z-\mathbf t),\mathbf x\right)\in C
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
For the fixed affine functional \(L\), the function
\[
g_L\bigl(\mathbf t,(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\bigr)
:=
L\left(\mathbf b+\alpha^{-1}(\mathbf z-\mathbf t),\mathbf x\right)
\tag{8}
\]
is continuous.  The compact-section construction therefore supplies, on every phase with a nonempty section, Borel functions
\(\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)\in Z\) and
\(\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t)\in K\) such that
\[
\mathbf a_L(\mathbf t):=
\left(
\mathbf b+\alpha^{-1}(\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf t),
\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t)
\right)
\in\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
L(\mathbf a_L(\mathbf t))
=\max_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}}L(\mathbf q).
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, (6) contains every integer label that can occur in
\(\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}\), so the sections in (7) parametrize exactly those anchor sets.  On phases with empty anchor set, define the two functions by
\[
\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t):=\mathbf0,\qquad
\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t):=\mathbf v_0.
\tag{11}
\]
The set of nonempty phases is compact in \([0,1]^n\), restricted to
\([0,1)^n\), and hence Borel.  Thus (9)--(11) define Borel functions on all of \([0,1)^n\).  They also prove that \(\Delta_L\) is Borel with values in
\(\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\).  Consequently the set on the right of (1) is Borel.

We now give the measure-preserving skew-product map.  Every
\(\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^{n+d}\) has unique coordinates
\[
\mathbf w=(\mathbf k+\mathbf t,\mathbf x),
\qquad
\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad
\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n,\quad
\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d.
\tag{12}
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal G_L(\mathbf k+\mathbf t,\mathbf x)
:=
\left(
\mathbf k+\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)+\mathbf t,\,
\mathbf x+\alpha(\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf v_0)
\right).
\tag{13}
\]
This is a Borel bijection.  Its inverse subtracts
\(\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)\) from the integer cell label and subtracts
\(\alpha(\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf v_0)\) from the continuous coordinate, while leaving the canonical phase \(\mathbf t\) unchanged, so the inverse is Borel as well.

The map (13) preserves \(m\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  Explicitly, for every nonnegative Borel function \(F\), Tonelli's theorem, translation invariance in \(\mathbb R^d\), and reindexing the sum over \(\mathbb Z^n\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^{n+d}}F(\mathcal G_L(\mathbf w))\,d\mathbf w
&=
\int_{[0,1)^n}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n}
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
F\left(
\mathbf k+\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)+\mathbf t,\,
\mathbf x+\alpha(\mathbf x_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf v_0)
\right)
d\mathbf x\,d\mathbf t\\
&=
\int_{[0,1)^n}
\sum_{\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb Z^n}
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
F(\boldsymbol\ell+\mathbf t,\mathbf u)
d\mathbf u\,d\mathbf t\\
&=
\int_{\mathbb R^{n+d}}F(\mathbf w)\,d\mathbf w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
E_L:=
\left\{
\mathbf w\in D:
L(\mathbf w)\ge\alpha\Delta_L(\{\mathbf w_I\})
\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(\mathbf w\in E_L\), then its phase
\(\mathbf t:=\{\mathbf w_I\}\) has a nonempty anchor set, because the inequality in (15) cannot hold when the right side is \(+\infty\).  Since \(\mathbf w\in D\), there is a unique \(\mathbf y\in C\) such that
\[
\mathbf w=(1-\alpha)\mathbf y+\alpha\mathbf q_0.
\tag{16}
\]
Define the actual mixed-integer image
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w)
:=
(1-\alpha)\mathbf y+\alpha\mathbf a_L(\mathbf t)
=
\mathbf w+\alpha(\mathbf a_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf q_0).
\tag{17}
\]
It belongs to \(C\) by convexity.  If
\(\mathbf w_I=\mathbf k+\mathbf t\), then (9) gives
\[
\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w)\bigr)_I
=
\mathbf k+\mathbf t+
\alpha\left[
\mathbf b+\alpha^{-1}(\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)-\mathbf t)-\mathbf b
\right]
=
\mathbf k+\mathbf z_L(\mathbf t)\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus (17), unlike the thickened image below, is an actual point of
\(C\cap\Lambda\).  Since \(L\) is affine, (10), (15), and (16) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w))
&=
L(\mathbf w)
+\alpha\bigl(L(\mathbf a_L(\mathbf t))-L(\mathbf q_0)\bigr)\\
&=
L(\mathbf w)-\alpha\Delta_L(\mathbf t)
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Hence
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w)
\in C\cap\{L\ge0\}\cap\Lambda.
\tag{20}
\]
The weak inequality in (19) retains points on the closed boundary.

For a Borel set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define its half-open unit-cell thickening by
\[
\widehat B:=
\left\{
(\boldsymbol\ell+\mathbf t,\mathbf x):
\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb Z^n,\
\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n,\
(\boldsymbol\ell,\mathbf x)\in B
\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The cells in (21) are disjoint, including on their boundaries because of the canonical half-open convention, and therefore
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(\widehat B)=\mu(B).
\tag{22}
\]
For \(\mathbf w\in E_L\), equation (13) is exactly
\[
\mathcal G_L(\mathbf w)
=
\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w)+(\mathbf t,\mathbf0).
\tag{23}
\]
The point in (23) is generally not in \(\Lambda\); rather, by (20), it lies in the unit cell corresponding to the actual mixed-integer point
\(\widetilde{\mathcal G}_L(\mathbf w)\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal G_L(E_L)
\subseteq
\widehat{\,C\cap\{L\ge0\}\,}.
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(\mathcal G_L\) is a Borel measure-preserving bijection, (22)--(24) yield
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(E_L)
=\operatorname{vol}_m(\mathcal G_L(E_L))
\le
\operatorname{vol}_m\left(
\widehat{\,C\cap\{L\ge0\}\,}
\right)
=
\mu\bigl(C\cap\{L\ge0\}\bigr).
\]
This proves (1), including empty phases, measurable maximizing anchors, closed boundaries, and the distinction between actual mixed-integer images and their thickened cells.

It remains to prove the phase-core corollary.  Condition (2) implies that every anchor set is nonempty and, for every affine \(L\),
\[
\max_{\mathbf q\in\mathcal A_{\mathbf t}}L(\mathbf q)
\ge L(\mathbf q_0).
\]
Hence
\[
\Delta_L(\mathbf t)\le0
\qquad\text{for every }\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n.
\tag{25}
\]
Let \(H=\{L\ge0\}\) be a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p\).  From (25),
\[
D\cap H\subseteq E_L,
\]
so (1) gives the first inequality in (3).

Since \(\mathbf q_0\in C\), convexity gives \(D\subseteq C\).  The centroid and volume of the homothetic copy \(D\) are
\[
\mathfrak c(D)
=(1-\alpha)\mathfrak c(C)+\alpha\mathbf q_0
=\mathbf p,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
=(1-\alpha)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\tag{26}
\]
Thus \(\mathbf p\in D\subseteq C\), and the assumption on its base coordinate gives \(\mathbf p\in C\cap\Lambda\).  Applying Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace theorem in dimension \(m\) to \(D\) and \(H\), then using (26), gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)
\ge
\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
=
\gamma_m(1-\alpha)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C).
\]
This proves the second inequality in (3) and completes the proof.

## External sources

Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace theorem: B. Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf.  The exact form used is: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) is a convex body with centroid \(\mathbf g\), then every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf g\) cuts from \(K\) at least
\(\left(m/(m+1)\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(K)\).  Here \(K=D\) is full-dimensional because \(0<1-\alpha<1\), its centroid is \(\mathbf p\) by (26), and \(H\) is a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p\).
