## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, with positive-volume compact convex bodies $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ at $z\in P$, $d\ge1$. Assume these are the actual positive-volume integer fibers of their lifted convex hull. Let $\mu$ be original mixed volume on the fibers, optionally multiplied by a common positive constant. Fix $j\in P$ and let $T$ be the maximum mass of a dominated source with base mean $j$. For $0<t\le T$ let $M_t(j)$ be its feasible continuous centroid section and $J(t)$ the largest inscribed ellipsoid volume, allowing zero. Let $J_K$ be the corresponding volume of $K_j$. Then $M_t(j)\subseteq M_s(j)$ for $0<s<t\le T$, the sections tend to $K_j$ in Hausdorff distance as $s\downarrow0$, and
$$
\lim_{s\downarrow0}J(s)=J_K,\qquad J(t)<J_K\quad(0<t\le T).
$$
Thus the supremum of $J(t)$ on $0<t<T$ equals $J_K$ and is unattained.

## Proof

Multiplying a mass-$t$ density by $s/t$ preserves its centroid and proves nesting. Compactness of the sections and existence of $T$ follow from the accepted raw-moment graph, dividing the raw moments by the fixed positive mass and scaling the entire graph first if $\mu$ includes a common factor. A barycenter of a source on the lifted hull belongs to that hull, so $M_s(j)\subseteq K_j$.

Write $w=\mu(\{j\}\times K_j)$ and $g$ for the centroid of $K_j$. For $0<s<w$ set $\theta=(s/w)^{1/d}$. The homothet $(1-\theta)p+\theta K_j$ at $j$ lies in that fiber for each $p\in K_j$, has source mass $s$, and has centroid $(1-\theta)p+\theta g$. Thus $(1-\theta)K_j+\theta g\subseteq M_s(j)$. The homothetic inclusion gives the Hausdorff limit, and by scaling an ellipsoid it gives $(1-\theta)^dJ_K\le J(s)\le J_K$.

For strictness, let $F$ be the minimal face of the base hull containing $j$. Every balanced source lies over $F$, since a functional exposing $F$ has nonnegative deficit everywhere and its integral under the source is zero. Write $C_F$ for the lifted hull of the fibers over $F$. Its affine hull is $\operatorname{aff}F\times\mathbb R^d$ since all the fibers have interior and their bases span $\operatorname{aff}F$. If a represented point $(j,x)$ had $x\in\partial K_j$, it would lie on the relative boundary of $C_F$, since a relative neighborhood there would give a continuous neighborhood in the $j$-fiber. A nonzero relative supporting functional must have nonzero continuous component: otherwise it supports the base hull $F$ at its relative-interior point $j$ and is constant on the relative ambient space. The deficit of the supporting functional is nonnegative on the source and its integral vanishes at the represented mean. This would concentrate a positive source on a plane cutting every fiber in a volume-null set, a contradiction. Hence $M_t(j)$ is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_j$. If $J(t)=0$, strictness follows from $J_K>0$. Otherwise a small dilation of its largest ellipsoid about its center stays inside $K_j$ and increases its volume.
