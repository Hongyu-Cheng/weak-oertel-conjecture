## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$ and take the actual planar fibers $K_j(a)=(1+aj)T$, $j=-1,0,1$, for sufficiently small $|a|$. Write $u_1=(1,0)$, $u_2=(0,1)$, $u_3=(-1,-1)$ in cyclic order. The middle group uses margins $(u_i+\theta_i u_{i+1})\cdot x+\alpha_i j$ with fixed equal cover weights and owns the whole planes at $\{-1,0\}$. A second group with fixed margins $u_i\cdot x+10(j-1)$ owns the plane at $1$, strictly isolates that endpoint and has each mass $2(1+a)^2<6$. In the common proper one-vertex chamber near the reference middle group, the minimum of its largest cap mass over the specified continuous balance and ownership is $6+4(\max\{-a,0\})^2$. For the radius velocity $Aj$ the extra second-derivative cost of ownership in the sum of middle cap masses relative to separate normal optimization is $24(\max\{-A,0\})^2$, or $18(\max\{-A,0\})^2$ with cap stress $3/4$. The same cost holds for smooth variable positive cover weights initialized equally. The velocity is neutral in each parity class and the total area curvature is $18A^2$. The true global raw depth is $6$ for all sufficiently small $|a|$; on $a>0$ the specified two-group cover attains this budget, while the same assigned cover costs more for $a<0$.

## Proof

For $|a|<1$ the real fibers $(1+az)T$ on $[-1,1]$ form the convex hull of the two lifted endpoint triangles: a Minkowski combination of positive homothets about zero is the homothet at the combined radius. The actual integer census is exactly the three given triangles. Their summed area is $27/2+9a^2$. In c4ebd085f1b2ab31 use left, middle and right radii $1-a,1,1+a$. The compatibility threshold is an equality. Its exact raw middle maximum is $2+\min\{4,(9/2)(1-a)^2,(9/2)(1+a)^2\}=6$ for all sufficiently small $|a|$, while the exposed endpoint maxima $2(1\pm a)^2$ are strictly below $6$. This calculation considers the full ambient family of containing caps.

The three canonical $u_i$ have the unique relation of equal coefficients, so fixed equal weights require $\theta_i=\theta$. They retain rank two nearby. The fixed-ownership whole-plane test of the shared-cap jet lemma then gives $\sum_i\alpha_i\le0$ for the left base and no constraint at the middle base. At its own base the second group is canonical through the right centroid, with cap area $2(1+a)^2$. The margins strictly exclude both other triangles for small $|a|$ since $\max_T u_i\cdot x=2$. Its weight balance gives a cover of its entire assigned plane. It thus carries genuine budget slack, with all three original fibers included in the total measure.

By 9de29e09d3ada6ce and the cyclic vertex permutation of $T$, each middle cap in its open one-vertex chamber has exact mass
$$
\Phi_i=6+\frac{3\theta_i^2}{2(1-\theta_i)}+\frac{(2a+\alpha_i-a\theta_i)^2}{1-\theta_i}.
$$
For $a\ge0$ choose $\theta_i=0$ and $\alpha_i=-2a$; this satisfies ownership and attains the universal individual lower bound $6$ in that chart. For $a<0$ let $k=a(2-\theta)<0$. Then the mean of $\alpha_i+k$ is at most $k$, hence the mean square is at least $k^2$. The largest mass is at least the mean and so at least
$$
(3/2+a^2)\frac{(2-\theta)^2}{1-\theta}
=6+4a^2+(3/2+a^2)\frac{\theta^2}{1-\theta}.
$$
Equality at $6+4a^2$ is attained by $\theta=\alpha_i=0$. Both optima preserve the actual cover and lie in the chamber for small $|a|$.

For a velocity $a'(0)=A$, differentiating the exact mass formula twice at the reference gives $\Phi_i''(0)=3(\theta_i')^2+2(2A+\alpha_i')^2$; the first derivatives vanish. The independent optimizing velocities have $\alpha_i'=-2A$ and $\theta_i'=0$. At the original $\alpha_i=0$ the derivatives of any smooth variable cover weights contribute zero to projected drift, so the required first-order condition remains $\sum_i\alpha_i'\le0$. For $A\ge0$ the independent minimizing velocity is allowed, while for $A<0$ its best feasible choice is $\alpha_i'=0$ by the same mean-square argument. Zero angular velocities achieve the lower bound in both cases and are integrable by the actual paths with constant equal weights just given. The sum adds $24(\max\{-A,0\})^2$, multiplied by $3/4$ when using that stress. The odd-base velocities cancel and the even-base one is zero; the total area polynomial gives curvature $18A^2$. Thus the chosen cover has a one-sided cost on a parity-neutral line. The normalized true depth $6/(27/2+9a^2)$ decreases from $4/9$, above the planar one-integer sharp target, and this exact depth does not depend on insisting on the specified assignment.
