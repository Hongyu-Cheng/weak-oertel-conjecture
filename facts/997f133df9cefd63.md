## Statement

Use the locked actual-fiber notation \(C_z\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\), \(W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z\), and the true uniform centroids \(b_z\) and covariances \(\Sigma_z\) of accepted fact \(\texttt{711f1969d2e61d1c}\). The interpolation error and its own-fiber covariance normalization are those of accepted fact \(\texttt{486fe778f838d99d}\). The new atomic graph law below is distinct from that fact's interpolated transport measure.

For every integer \(N\ge1\) and every \(0<\delta\le10^{-6}\), there is a convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\), hence \(n=2,d=1\), with exactly the positive integer fibers
\[
D_+=\{0,1\}\times\{-N,-N+1,\ldots,N\},\qquad
C_{(i,j)}=[ij,ij+w_{(i,j)}],\qquad
w_{(i,j)}=1+\delta\left(1-\frac{j^2}{N^2}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular all original positive fiber volumes lie in \([1,1+10^{-6}]\). They are not reweighted. Their true centroids and scalar covariances are
\[
b_{(i,j)}=ij+\frac12+\frac\delta2\left(1-\frac{j^2}{N^2}\right),\qquad
\Sigma_{(i,j)}=\frac{w_{(i,j)}^2}{12},\qquad
W=2\sum_{j=-N}^N\left[1+\delta\left(1-\frac{j^2}{N^2}\right)\right].
\tag{2}
\]
This family has the following properties.

For every finite convex combination of positive integer bases \(z_r\), with coefficients \(\lambda_r\ge0\), \(\sum_r\lambda_r=1\), whose barycenter \(z^\star=\sum_r\lambda_rz_r\) is integer, the barycenter belongs to \(D_+\), and
\[
\left|\beta\left(b_{z^\star}-\sum_r\lambda_rb_{z_r}\right)\right|
\le\sqrt3\,\delta\sqrt{\beta^2\Sigma_{z^\star}}
<\frac1{1000}\sqrt{\beta^2\Sigma_{z^\star}}
\quad(\beta\ne0).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\beta=0\) both sides of the non-strict inequality vanish. Thus the local estimates hold for every integer convex combination, with a constant strictly smaller than the \(10^{-3}\) transport calibration.

Nevertheless, if one fixed affine map \(Lz+b\), with \(L\in\mathbb R^{1\times2}\) and \(b\in\mathbb R\), satisfies the own-standard-deviation condition
\[
|b_z-Lz-b|\le\epsilon\sqrt{\Sigma_z}\qquad(z\in D_+),
\tag{4}
\]
then necessarily \(\epsilon\ge\sqrt3\,N\). In particular (3) does not supply (4) with \(\epsilon=10^{-3}\), the proposed input to accepted fact \(\texttt{711f1969d2e61d1c}\).

For each fixed \(0\le\theta<1\), define legal perturbed graph sites and their original-weight law by
\[
g_{\theta,(i,j)}=ij+\frac12+\frac{(1-\theta)\delta}{2}\left(1-\frac{j^2}{N^2}\right),\qquad
G_\theta=\{(i,j,g_{\theta,(i,j)}):(i,j)\in D_+\},\qquad
\eta_\theta=\frac1W\sum_{(i,j)\in D_+}w_{(i,j)}\delta_{(i,j,g_{\theta,(i,j)})}.
\tag{5}
\]
Every graph site belongs to \(C\), and \(|g_{\theta,z}-b_z|\le\sqrt3\,\delta\sqrt{\Sigma_z}\). The graphs themselves also satisfy the interpolation estimate (3), with \(b_z\) replaced by \(g_{\theta,z}\). Their relative Helly number, defined using intersections of ambient convex sets with \(G_\theta\), is exactly \(4N+2\), rather than \(2^n=4\). The halfspace depth of every site for \(\eta_\theta\) is exactly its own atom mass, so
\[
\max_{g\in G_\theta}\inf_{H\ni g}\eta_\theta(H)
=\frac{1+\delta}{W}
\le\frac{1+\delta}{4N+2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The infimum in (6) is over closed ambient halfspaces. Therefore no positive error tolerance in these local estimates alone preserves either the graph Helly bound or an absolute multiple of \(2^{-n}\) graph depth at a graph site.

In contrast, the single actual point \(\mathbf y^\star=(0,0,1/2)\in S\), fixed before all halfspaces, satisfies
\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{W}{8(1+\delta)}.
\tag{7}
\]
The family is consequently not a counterexample to the locked depth statement. A complete flattening, \(\theta=1\), gives an exactly integer-midpoint-closed legal graph in this example; (5)-(6) deliberately concern \(\theta<1\). No impossibility of every alternative compatible point selection is asserted.

## Proof

Use base coordinates \((x,t)\in\mathbb R^2\) and continuous fiber coordinate \(y\in\mathbb R\). For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), let
\[
K_i=\left\{(i,t,y):-N\le t\le N,\quad it\le y\le it+1+\delta\left(1-\frac{t^2}{N^2}\right)\right\},\qquad
C=\operatorname{conv}(K_0\cup K_1).
\tag{8}
\]
Each \(K_i\) is compact and convex: its lower boundary is affine and its upper boundary is concave. It has nonempty relative interior in the plane \(x=i\). Their convex hull is compact and convex. An open two-dimensional patch of \(K_0\), together with a point of \(K_1\), spans three affine dimensions, so \(C\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^3\). Thus it is an actual convex body of the required dimensions.

Its base projection is exactly \([0,1]\times[-N,N]\). A point of \(C\) with \(x=0\) can use only points of \(K_0\) in a convex representation, because all points in \(K_1\) have \(x=1\). Convexity of \(K_0\) then identifies that entire boundary sheet with \(K_0\). The same argument applies at \(x=1\). Hence the only integer bases are precisely the bases in (1), and their fibers are exactly the intervals stated there. Every interval has positive length. The elementary uniform-interval centroid and variance formulas give (2). This checks every integer fiber, including all boundary bases, and preserves the original mixed-integer volume.

For calibration, when two such bases interpolate to an integer base, ambient convexity gives the actual-fiber Minkowski inclusion required by accepted fact \(\texttt{486fe778f838d99d}\). With \(d=1\), its interpolated volume \(v\) is at least one, while the target volume is at most \(1+\delta\). Thus its \(M\) lies in \([1,1+\delta]\), and its conclusion gives the advertised local error at most \(\sqrt\delta\le10^{-3}\) times the target fiber's own directional standard deviation. We next verify the stronger estimate (3) directly, including arbitrary finite convex combinations.

Write \(z_r=(i_r,j_r)\) and \(z^\star=(i^\star,j^\star)\). Because all \(i_r\) are zero or one and \(i^\star\) is integer, every positive coefficient is attached to \(i_r=i^\star\). Also \(-N\le j^\star\le N\), so the integer barycenter is in \(D_+\). The affine term \(i^\star j+1/2\) cancels, giving the exact identity
\[
b_{z^\star}-\sum_r\lambda_rb_{z_r}
=\frac\delta{2N^2}\left(\sum_r\lambda_rj_r^2-(j^\star)^2\right)
=\frac\delta{2N^2}\sum_r\lambda_r(j_r-j^\star)^2.
\tag{9}
\]
The right side is nonnegative and at most \(\delta/2\), because \(\sum_r\lambda_rj_r^2\le N^2\). Since \(\sqrt{\Sigma_{z^\star}}=w_{z^\star}/\sqrt{12}\ge1/\sqrt{12}\), its standardized size is at most \(\sqrt3\delta\). Multiplication by \(|\beta|\) proves (3). The strict comparison uses \(\sqrt3\delta\le\sqrt3\cdot10^{-6}<10^{-3}\), with the latter inequality following, for example, by squaring. This is an exact inequality rather than a numerical feasibility premise.

For the affine obstruction, consider the four original bases \((0,-N),(0,N),(1,-N),(1,N)\). All four fibers have length one and standard deviation \(1/\sqrt{12}\). Their centroid alternating sum is
\[
b_{(1,N)}+b_{(0,-N)}-b_{(1,-N)}-b_{(0,N)}=2N.
\tag{10}
\]
Every affine map \(Lz+b\) has zero alternating sum on this rectangle. If (4) holds, the triangle inequality applied to its four residuals therefore gives \(2N\le4\epsilon/\sqrt{12}\), equivalently \(\epsilon\ge\sqrt3N\). Condition (4) is exactly the scalar \(d=1\) form of the all-direction hypothesis of accepted fact \(\texttt{711f1969d2e61d1c}\): multiplying it by \(|\beta|\) yields that fact's own-standard-deviation inequality, and taking \(\beta=1\) recovers (4). Thus the normalization and the failed implication are precise. Different affine slopes on the two sheets do not constitute the single fixed affine map required there.

We now prove all graph assertions. Write
\[
f_\theta(t)=\frac12+\frac{(1-\theta)\delta}{2}\left(1-\frac{t^2}{N^2}\right),\qquad
\kappa=\frac{(1-\theta)\delta}{2N^2}>0,\qquad B=N+1.
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(g_{\theta,(i,j)}=ij+f_\theta(j)\). It lies inside its actual interval since \(1/2\le f_\theta(j)\le w_{(i,j)}/2\le w_{(i,j)}\). Its difference from the true centroid is at most \(\delta/2\), which gives the asserted own-standard-deviation bound. Repeating (9) with \(\delta\) replaced by \((1-\theta)\delta\) proves the graph interpolation bound as well.

For each graph label \((i,j)\), define the affine tangent and an ambient affine functional by
\[
a_j(t)=f_\theta(j)-2\kappa j(t-j),\qquad
F_{i,j}(x,t,y)=y-it-a_j(t)-B\bigl((1-2i)x+i\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]
The quadratic identity \(f_\theta(k)-a_j(k)=-\kappa(k-j)^2\) gives
\[
F_{i,j}(i,k,g_{\theta,(i,k)})=-\kappa(k-j)^2,
\qquad
F_{i,j}(1-i,k,g_{\theta,(1-i,k)})=(1-2i)k-\kappa(k-j)^2-B\le-1.
\tag{13}
\]
At its own site, \(F_{i,j}=0\). At every different same-sheet site its value is at most \(-\kappa\), because distinct integer \(k,j\) differ by at least one. At every opposite-sheet site its value is at most \(-1<-\kappa\), because \(\kappa\le\delta/2<1\).

Consequently the closed halfspace \(H_{i,j}^-:=\{F_{i,j}\le-\kappa/2\}\) contains every site of \(G_\theta\) except its own labeled site. The family of all \(4N+2\) such halfspaces has empty intersection with \(G_\theta\), but every proper subfamily has nonempty intersection with \(G_\theta\): an omitted label's site belongs to every retained halfspace. This proves a relative Helly lower bound \(4N+2\). The matching upper bound holds for any finite location set of that cardinality: if an arbitrary family has empty intersection with the location set, choose, for each site, one member excluding it. Those at most \(4N+2\) members already have empty intersection with the set. Hence the relative Helly number is exactly \(4N+2\).

The opposite closed halfspace \(H_{i,j}^+:=\{F_{i,j}\ge-\kappa/2\}\) contains exactly its labeled graph site. Its \(\eta_\theta\)-mass is therefore \(w_{(i,j)}/W\). Every halfspace containing that site contains its atom, so no smaller mass is possible. This proves the exact graph depth assertion. The largest weight is \(1+\delta\), attained at \(j=0\), and \(W\ge4N+2\), proving (6). For any proposed fixed positive graph-depth coefficient, taking \(N\) sufficiently large contradicts its bound with \(n=2\) fixed. This failure persists for every \(\delta>0\) in the specified range and every partial correction \(\theta<1\), however small the remaining curvature. At \(\theta=1\), by contrast, the selected height is \(ij+1/2\). Two graph sites with an integer midpoint must have the same first base coordinate \(i\), and this height is affine in \(j\) on that sheet, so their midpoint is exactly another graph site. This verifies the stated limit of the obstruction.

Finally, \(\mathbf y^\star=(0,0,1/2)\) belongs to \(C\) and has integer base. Set \(W_0=\sum_{j=-N}^Nw_{(0,j)}=W/2\). Fix an arbitrary proper closed ambient halfspace containing this point. It contains a parallel closed halfspace through the point, of the form
\[
\alpha_1x+\alpha_2t+\beta(y-1/2)\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]
On sheet \(x=0\), choose the integer bases with \(\alpha_2j\ge0\). Their total original weight is at least \(W_0/2\), because \(w_{(0,j)}=w_{(0,-j)}\). For each such base, the event \(\beta(y-1/2)\ge0\) suffices for (14). In the actual interval \([0,w_{(0,j)}]\), its uniform probability is at least \(1/[2(1+\delta)]\): for \(\beta>0\) it is \(1-1/(2w_{(0,j)})\ge1/2\); for \(\beta<0\) it is \(1/(2w_{(0,j)})\ge1/[2(1+\delta)]\); and for \(\beta=0\) it is one. Summing precisely these original fiber volumes gives at least \(W_0/[4(1+\delta)]=W/[8(1+\delta)]\) in every containing halfspace. The whole-space case also satisfies this bound. Taking the infimum establishes (7) at the same fixed actual point for every halfspace and completes the verification.
