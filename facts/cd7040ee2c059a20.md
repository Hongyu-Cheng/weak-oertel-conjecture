## Statement

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, put \(n=2p-2\), \(Q=2^n-1\), and \(m=Q/p\), and choose any primitive element \(\alpha\) of \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\). Put \(\omega=\alpha^m\), and let \(c\in\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) be defined by \(\alpha^c=1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha\). The nonzero field expression and hence this exponent are guaranteed by accepted fact d6237f796d63f7f5.

Take exactly its labels \(X_{i,t}\), for \(i\in\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\), and original equations

\[
2X_{i,t}=X_{i-d_t,s}+X_{i-d_t,u},
\qquad
(d_0,d_1,d_2)=(1,m-1,c),
\qquad
\{s,u,t\}=\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Delete \(X_{jm,0}\) and \(X_{jm-1,1}\), for \(0\le j<p\), and every equation with a deleted endpoint. Use the identical aliases \(U_i=X_{i,0}\), \(V_i=X_{i-1,1}\), and \(Z_i=X_{i,2}\), and the identical sinks \(A_j=Z_{c-1+jm}\) and \(B_j=Z_{c+jm}\). Prescribe these \(2p\) sinks as the independent standard basis of \(\mathbb Q^{2p}\). Write \(\mu_Y(s)\) for the resulting harmonic coefficient at sink \(s\) of a retained label \(Y\).

Call a retained equation \(2Y=Y^-+Y^+\) comparable if, at every individual sink \(s\),

\[
0<\mu_{Y^-}(s)<3\mu_{Y^+}(s),
\qquad
0<\mu_{Y^+}(s)<3\mu_{Y^-}(s).
\]

Let \(\mathcal R\subseteq\mathbb Q^{2p}\) be the rational span of \(\mu_{Y^+}-\mu_{Y^-}\) over all comparable retained equations; changing a row's orientation does not change this span. Then the following conclusions hold.

For every \(i\not\equiv0\pmod m\), the \(p\) numbers
\(\mu_{V_i}(A_j)-\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(A_j)\), for \(0\le j<p\), are not all equal. In particular,
\(\dim_{\mathbb Q}\mathcal R\ge p-1\ge2\).

For every integer \(N\ge1\) and every assignment of positions in \(\mathbb Q^N\) to all \(2p\) sinks, realize the retained labels by their unique harmonic solution. Let \(P\) be their convex hull, let \(\mathcal L\) be the additive group generated by all their positions, and put \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\). For \(y\in B\), define
\(r_B^{\mathcal L}(y)=|\{v\in\mathcal L:y+v,y-v\in B\}|\), counting oriented displacements and zero once. If \(\max_{y\in B}r_B^{\mathcal L}(y)\le3\), then

\[
\dim\operatorname{aff}P\le p\le2p-3<2p-2=n.
\]

These assertions permit arbitrary affine dependencies and coincidences among sink positions and among other realized labels. The hull \(B\) is the complete hull in the generated additive group, not only the set of realized labels.

## Proof

We first record the precise accepted inputs. Accepted fact d6237f796d63f7f5 supplies the unique rational harmonic solution, its nonnegative coefficients with sum one, and one comparable retained equation \(2U_i=V_i+Z_{i-1}\), for every prime \(p\ge5\) and every permitted \(c\). The graph and all notation here agree exactly with that fact and with accepted fact ee2c0378f6772485. Their retained equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
2U_i&=V_i+Z_{i-1},&
2V_i&=U_{i-m}+Z_{i-m}
&& (i\not\equiv0\pmod m),\\
2Z_{i+c}&=U_i+V_{i+1}
&&&& (i\not\equiv0,-1\pmod m).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, combining the first two equations gives
\(4U_i-U_{i-m}=Z_{i-m}+2Z_{i-1}\) for every \(i\not\equiv0\pmod m\).

We need the field restriction \(c\not\equiv0\pmod m\). Here is its consequence from the field argument in the accepted facts. Put \(q=2^{p-1}\), so that \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{q^2}\setminus\mathbb F_q\) and \(\omega\in\mathbb F_q\). If \(\gamma=1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha\) belonged to \(\mathbb F_q\), the monic quadratic \(x^2+(1+\gamma)x+\omega\) would be the minimal polynomial of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its norm would have order \(p\), whereas the actual norm \(\alpha^{q+1}\) has order \(q-1>p\). Thus \(\gamma\notin\mathbb F_q\). If \(c\) were a multiple of \(m\), then \(\alpha^c\) would be a power of \(\omega\) and would belong to \(\mathbb F_q\), a contradiction. This argument includes \(p=5\).

Translation of every label's exponent by \(m\) is a graph automorphism and cyclically permutes both sink orbits. Uniqueness of the harmonic solution therefore makes the harmonic coefficient vectors equivariant under that permutation. We shall show, by finite integer arithmetic, that the difference
\(\delta_i=\mu_{V_i}-\mu_{Z_{i-1}}\) cannot be constant on its \(A\)-sink coordinates for any retained \(i\).

Choose a prime \(\ell\ne3\) dividing \(4^p-1\). Such a prime exists: since \(p\ge5\) is prime, \(p\) is not divisible by three, and \(4^p-1\) is congruent to either three or six modulo nine. It is therefore divisible by three but not by nine, and it is larger than three. The prime \(\ell\) is odd. The element four has order exactly \(p\) modulo \(\ell\), because its order divides \(p\) and order one would imply \(\ell\mid3\). Hence \(p\mid\ell-1\), so \(\ell\ne p\).

Let \(D_0\) be any positive common denominator of all coordinates of all the finitely many vectors \(\mu_Y\), and let \(k\ge0\) be the exact exponent of \(\ell\) in \(D_0\). Work in the finite ring \(R_\ell=\mathbb Z/\ell^{k+1}\mathbb Z\). There is a unit \(\theta\in R_\ell\) such that \(\theta\equiv4\pmod\ell\) and \(\theta^p=1\) in \(R_\ell\). To construct it, start with four modulo \(\ell\). If an integer \(u\) satisfies \(u^p\equiv1\pmod{\ell^a}\), replacing \(u\) by \(u+t\ell^a\) changes \(u^p-1\), modulo \(\ell^{a+1}\), by \(ptu^{p-1}\ell^a\). The coefficient \(pu^{p-1}\) is nonzero modulo \(\ell\), so one can choose \(t\) to lift the root to the next power. Iteration gives the stated \(\theta\). Since \(\theta-1\) is a unit and \(\theta^p=1\), the geometric sum \(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\theta^{-j}\) is zero in \(R_\ell\).

For every retained label \(Y\), define the following element of \(R_\ell\):

\[
F_Y=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\bigl(D_0\mu_Y(A_j)\bigr)\theta^{-j}.
\]

The coefficients in parentheses are integers. Thus this definition involves no division by a potentially nonunit element of the finite ring. Every retained midpoint equation holds for the \(F_Y\). Their boundary values are \(F_{A_j}=D_0\theta^{-j}\) and \(F_{B_j}=0\). Equivariance gives \(F_{U_{i-m}}=\theta F_{U_i}\), \(F_{V_{i-m}}=\theta F_{V_i}\), and \(F_{Z_{i-m}}=\theta F_{Z_i}\) whenever the corresponding labels occur. For example, shifting a starting label by \(-m\) sends its coefficient at \(A_j\) to the original coefficient at \(A_{j+1}\); reindexing the sum multiplies it by \(\theta\). The identity \(\theta^p=1\) handles the wraparound.

Not all \(F_Y\) can belong to the ideal \(\ell^kR_\ell\). Suppose that they did. Divide their representatives by \(\ell^k\) and reduce modulo \(\ell\), obtaining values \(\widehat F_Y\). This operation is well defined because the modulus was \(\ell^{k+1}\). It gives a solution of all the same midpoint equations over \(\mathbb F_\ell\), with phase relation \(\widehat F_{Y-m}=4\widehat F_Y\), with zero boundary on every \(B_j\), and with value \(D_0/\ell^k\ne0\) at \(A_0\). The combined cycle identity at \(i=c\) is available because \(c\not\equiv0\pmod m\). Its left side vanishes by the phase relation, while its right side is
\(4\widehat F_{Z_c}+2\widehat F_{Z_{c-1}}=2D_0/\ell^k\ne0\).
This is a contradiction.

Consequently \(k\ge1\). Let \(e\) be the largest integer such that all \(F_Y\) belong to \(\ell^eR_\ell\). The preceding paragraph gives \(0\le e<k\). Dividing by \(\ell^e\) and reducing modulo \(\ell\) produces a nonzero array \(\overline F_Y\) over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) that satisfies all retained midpoint equations and the phase relation
\(\overline F_{Y-m}=4\overline F_Y\). Every sink value in this array is zero, because \(e<k\).

We now determine this homogeneous array directly. The combined cycle identity gives
\(4\overline F_{Z_i}+2\overline F_{Z_{i-1}}=0\) for every \(i\not\equiv0\pmod m\). In representatives \(0\le i<m\), it follows that
\(\overline F_{Z_i}=(-1/2)^i\overline F_{Z_0}\).
The zero value at \(B_0=Z_c\), together with the phase relation, forces the value at the representative of \(c\) modulo \(m\) to be zero. All the displayed powers of \(-1/2\) are nonzero in \(\mathbb F_\ell\). Hence \(\overline F_{Z_0}=0\), all \(Z\)-values in these representatives vanish, and phase equivariance makes every \(Z\)-value zero.

The first retained equation now gives
\(\overline F_{V_i}=2\overline F_{U_i}\).
For \(1\le i\le m-2\), the retained type-two equation gives
\(\overline F_{U_i}+2\overline F_{U_{i+1}}=0\).
Therefore

\[
\overline F_{U_i}=(-1/2)^{i-1}\overline F_{U_1},
\qquad
\overline F_{V_i}=2(-1/2)^{i-1}\overline F_{U_1}
\qquad(1\le i<m).
\]

The array is nonzero and all its \(Z\)-values vanish, so \(\overline F_{U_1}\ne0\). Thus, for every retained \(i\), including all phase translates,
\(\overline F_{V_i}-\overline F_{Z_{i-1}}\ne0\).

If the \(A\)-coordinates of \(\delta_i\) were all equal, the integers
\(D_0(\mu_{V_i}(A_j)-\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(A_j))\) would have a common value. The definition of \(F\) and the vanishing geometric sum would then give \(F_{V_i}-F_{Z_{i-1}}=0\) in \(R_\ell\). Dividing by \(\ell^e\) and reducing modulo \(\ell\) would contradict the preceding nonzero difference. This proves the claimed nonconstancy on the \(A\)-orbit for every retained type-zero row. The argument uses only a finite modulus determined by an existing rational common denominator.

Let \(T\) be the linear permutation of sink coordinates induced by translation of labels by \(m\). It has order \(p\). Choose the comparable type-zero row supplied by accepted fact d6237f796d63f7f5, and write its parent difference as \(\delta_i\). All \(p\) phase translates of this row remain retained and comparable, because translation merely permutes the sinks in the strict comparison inequalities. Their differences are \(T^j\delta_i\), for \(0\le j<p\), and hence their rational span is contained in \(\mathcal R\).

For completeness, the needed rational orbit bound follows from elementary polynomial algebra. The polynomial
\(\Phi_p(x)=1+x+\cdots+x^{p-1}\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed, \(\Phi_p(x+1)=((x+1)^p-1)/x\) is monic, all its nonleading coefficients are divisible by \(p\), and its constant term is \(p\), not divisible by \(p^2\). If it factored over \(\mathbb Q\), clearing denominators and cancelling contents would give two monic integer factors of positive degrees. Modulo \(p\), their product is \(x^{p-1}\), so both reductions are monomials of positive degrees and both constant terms are divisible by \(p\). Their product could not have constant term \(p\). This proves the irreducibility.

Let \(g\) be the monic polynomial of least degree with \(g(T)\delta_i=0\). Polynomial division shows that \(g\) divides \(x^p-1=(x-1)\Phi_p(x)\). Also the orbit span has dimension \(\deg g\): the vectors \(\delta_i,T\delta_i,\ldots,T^{\deg g-1}\delta_i\) are independent by minimality, and polynomial division expresses every later power using them. The established nonconstancy implies \(T\delta_i\ne\delta_i\), and in particular \(\delta_i\ne0\). Thus \(g\) is neither one nor \(x-1\). Irreducibility of \(\Phi_p\) now gives \(\deg g\ge p-1\). Consequently \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}\mathcal R\ge p-1\), as asserted.

Finally assign arbitrary rational sink positions in \(\mathbb Q^N\). Every realized label is a convex combination of those positions, and the sinks themselves remain labels. Thus \(P\) is exactly the sink hull. All label positions are rational, so a common denominator puts \(\mathcal L\) inside a dilation of \(\mathbb Z^N\); the compact set \(P\) therefore has finite intersection \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\).

Consider any comparable equation \(2Y=Y^-+Y^+\) in this realization, and put \(v=Y^+-Y\in\mathcal L\). The two extrapolations

\[
Y+2v=2Y^+-Y=\frac{3Y^+-Y^-}{2},
\qquad
Y-2v=2Y^--Y=\frac{3Y^--Y^+}{2}
\]

belong to \(\mathcal L\) because they are integer combinations of realized labels. Each has strictly positive coefficients at every sink, by the comparable-parent inequalities, and those coefficients sum to one. They therefore also belong to \(P\). The points \(Y+tv\), for \(t=-2,-1,0,1,2\), all lie in \(B\). If \(v\ne0\), they are distinct, so \(r_B^{\mathcal L}(Y)\ge5\). Under the assumed reflection maximum at most three, every comparable row must therefore have \(Y^+=Y^-\).

Form the \(2p\)-by-\((N+1)\) matrix whose row for a sink \(s\) is \((1,b_s)\), where \(b_s\in\mathbb Q^N\) is its assigned position. Every comparable parent difference annihilates this matrix on the left: its coefficients sum to zero, and its weighted sum of sink positions is \(Y^+-Y^-=0\). The left kernel thus contains \(\mathcal R\), of dimension at least \(p-1\). The matrix has rank at most \(2p-(p-1)=p+1\), and its rational and real ranks agree. The affine dimension of its sink rows, and hence of \(P\), is one less than the rank of this augmented matrix. This gives \(\dim\operatorname{aff}P\le p\).

The last argument concerns the augmented position matrix and affine dimension. It does not identify affine dimension with the rank of the additive group \(\mathcal L\), and it does not replace \(B\) by the potentially smaller set of labels. Since \(p\ge5\), the inequalities \(p\le2p-3<2p-2\) finish the stated consumer.
