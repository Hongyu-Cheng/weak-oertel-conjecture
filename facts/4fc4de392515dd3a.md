## Statement

Under the hypotheses of finite-base-upward-max-support-erosion, fix finitely many marks $(p_i,y_i)$ with $y_i\in\operatorname{int}K_{p_i}$. Let $T_{a_i}(w)=w/(1+a_i\cdot w)$ be the centered positive projective normalization of $K_{p_i}-y_i$, and let $f_i$ equal $s^{-d}$ above the minimum denominator on this body, with its tangent extension below. Seed the upward construction with the maximum over the marked seeds at each base (or zero if none), where
$$
P_i(u,x)=h_{K_{p_i}-y_i}(u)f_i(1+a_i\cdot(x-y_i)),\qquad d_i=d|T_{a_i}(K_{p_i}-y_i)|.
$$
Every fixed ambient closed cap containing $(p_i,y_i)$ loses at least $\Gamma_d d_i$ to first order on the actual $p_i$ fiber, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. These lower bounds add across distinct bases. On a shared base their maximum is guaranteed by the individual inequalities.

## Proof

For each mark translate $K_{p_i}$ by $-y_i$. By d90ba05577427854 its attained projective parameter $a_i$ has positive denominator $L=1+a_i\cdot(x-y_i)$ on the original fiber, and the determinant and moment identities imply that $T_{a_i}(K_{p_i}-y_i)$ is a convex body of centroid zero. Convexity follows also because on every segment the projective image is the same segment between the images with positive reweighted barycentric coefficients. The inverse-power function $L^{-d}$ extended by its tangent at the positive minimum denominator is positive, finite, continuous, and convex on the real line. Its product with the strictly positive centered support gives an admissible seed.

For a through-mark cap and the old centered radial function $\rho$ about the mark, the boundary cone change of variables gives the integral of the seed as the integral of $(\rho/(1+\rho a_i\cdot v))^d$ over the unit ray directions $v$ of the cap. Indeed the surface-to-sphere Jacobian is $(\nu\cdot v)\,dS=\rho^{d-1}\,dv$, and $h_{K_{p_i}-y_i}(\nu)=\rho(\nu\cdot v)$. For $d=1$ this is the sum over the two rays. This equals $d$ times the corresponding volume of the projective image, and on the entire sphere it equals $d_i$. The ordinary Grünbaum centroid inequality on that full-dimensional image gives at least $\Gamma_d d_i$ in each through-mark cap.

The upward propagation theorem applies to the maxima of these seed fields at each base and gives $V_{p_i}\ge P_i$ pointwise and actual compatibility. In the first-loss formula, obtained by applying 84cd8a85417b0d7a on the singleton $\{p_i\}$, the selector for any containing ambient halfspace captures the boundary part of a parallel through-mark cap up to a surface-null set. This is because the mark is interior, so any nonvertical through-mark cut is proper. If the trace is full, the selector is $1$, including purely base halfspaces containing the marked base on their boundary. Consequently the same lower bound holds on the literal $p_i$ restriction. Distinct bases contribute disjoint summands. On one base the separate inequalities only imply their maximum without additional information about overlap. The seed integral $d_i$ is the one used in the lower bound.
