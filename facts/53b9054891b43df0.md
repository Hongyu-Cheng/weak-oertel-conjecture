## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For \(K\ge3\), define the new scalar constant \(c_{\exp}(K):=16+2560K^2\), distinguished from the locked problem's convex body \(C\).

First, let \(W,N\) be jointly finitely supported real random variables. Let \(\mathcal G\) be latent information with respect to which \(W\) is measurable, and assume that the conditional law of \(N\) given \(\mathcal G\) is symmetric about zero. Suppose that \(\mathbb EW=0\) and \(\mathbb EW^4\le K(\mathbb EW^2)^2\). Then, with \(G:=\mathbb E\exp(W+N)\),

\[
G=\mathbb E\bigl[e^W\cosh N\bigr]\ge\mathbb Ee^W\ge1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(W+N)\le c_{\exp}(K)G.
\]

In particular, \(G\ge c_{\exp}(K)^{-1/2}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(W+N)}\). These assertions include every zero-variance case. No independence between \(W\) and \(N\), and no fourth-moment bound on \(N\), is required.

Set \(K_{\mathrm{cen}}:=2^{18}(2400e^3)^2\), exactly the center constant of 1232ba9a1c0673db, and set \(c_{\mathrm{lik},2}:=c_{\exp}(K_{\mathrm{cen}})\). For every normalized original planar row in that fact, with coordinates \((S,Y)\), every \(a,b\in\mathbb R\) satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Var}(aS+bY)
\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}\,\mathbb E e^{aS+bY}.
\]

Consequently, let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\) be finite, nonempty, and complete: \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Write \(r:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\le2\). If \(r=2\), assume that its lattice width is at most two, meaning

\[
\min_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\left(\max_{\mathbf x\in A}\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x
      -\min_{\mathbf x\in A}\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\right)\le2.
\]

For an arbitrary finite tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^2\), let \(p_{\mathbf x}=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}/\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}\). Retain the definitions of \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\) and \(\mathcal B_2(p)\) from bf1ff94487296374. At each target \(\mathbf j\in A\), let \(F_{\mathbf j}\) be its minimal face, put \(E_{\mathbf j}:=A\cap F_{\mathbf j}\), and let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique mean-\(\mathbf j\) relative-entropy minimizer against \(p\). This \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is exactly the law \(r_{\mathbf j}\) of bf1ff94487296374 and its maximum-entropy law \(r_{E_{\mathbf j},\mathbf j}\); it is positive on \(E_{\mathbf j}\) and zero outside it. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathbf j}
&:=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
       \left[\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)}{p_{\mathbf X}}\right],\\
\omega_{\mathbf j}
&:=\frac{p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2}{\mathcal B_2(p)},
\qquad q_{\mathbf j}:=p(E_{\mathbf j}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\omega\) is the original capacity root-weight law, with the original source probabilities \(p_{\mathbf j}\).

There is a finite vector \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\in\mathbb R^2\) representing the row exponentially on its face:

\[
\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}}
       {\sum_{\mathbf z\in E_{\mathbf j}}
         e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)}}
\quad(\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf j}).
\]

Its restriction to the face direction is the only part that matters. Put \(T_{\mathbf j}:=(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\), with \(\mathbf X\) distributed as \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), and put \(G_{\mathbf j}:=\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}e^{T_{\mathbf j}}\). Then the exact face attenuation and the uniform price are

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf x}
&=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\,\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
       e^{(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})
             \cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}
       \quad(\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf j}),\\
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
&=\frac{q_{\mathbf j}}{G_{\mathbf j}}\le\frac1{G_{\mathbf j}},
\qquad G_{\mathbf j}\ge1,\\
V_{\mathbf j}&\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}G_{\mathbf j},\\
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2V_{\mathbf j}
&\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}\frac{q_{\mathbf j}^2}{G_{\mathbf j}}
 \le c_{\mathrm{lik},2},\\
\mathbb E_{\omega}V_{\mathbf j}
&\le4^r c_{\mathrm{lik},2}\le16c_{\mathrm{lik},2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For a rank-zero minimal face, \(V_{\mathbf j}=0\). For a rank-one minimal face, the directional fourth-moment bound from 075a3f88fa24d326, with its unchanged constant \(K_{\mathrm{row}}=20288\), suffices. For a rank-two minimal face on a width-two support, the row is an original width-two law of 1232ba9a1c0673db after the exact coordinate map given in the proof. A full-rank positive row with integral vector mean cannot occur on a full-rank width-one support.

## Proof

We first prove the scalar exponential-moment assertion. Conditional symmetry implies \(\mathbb E[N\mid\mathcal G]=0\), and, because \(W\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable, it also implies \(\mathbb EWN=0\) and

\[
\mathbb E e^{W+N}
=\mathbb E\left[e^W\mathbb E(e^N\mid\mathcal G)\right]
=\mathbb E\left[e^W\mathbb E(\cosh N\mid\mathcal G)\right]
=\mathbb E[e^W\cosh N].
\]

All expectations are finite. The inequalities \(\cosh N\ge1\) and Jensen's inequality give \(G\ge\mathbb Ee^W\ge e^{\mathbb EW}=1\). Also \(\mathbb E(W+N)=0\), so the variance identity is \(\operatorname{Var}(W+N)=\mathbb EW^2+\mathbb EN^2\).

Write \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb EW^2\). Suppose first that \(\sigma>0\). Hölder's inequality gives

\[
\mathbb EW^2
\le(\mathbb E|W|)^{2/3}(\mathbb EW^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|W|
\ge\frac{\sigma^3}{\sqrt{\mathbb EW^4}}
\ge\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt K}.
\]

Since \(W\) is centered, \(\mathbb EW_+=\mathbb E|W|/2\ge\sigma/(2\sqrt K)\), where \(W_+:=\max\{W,0\}\). Set \(t:=\sigma/(4\sqrt K)>0\). The part of \(W_+\) on \(\{W<t\}\) has expectation at most \(t\). Hence

\[
\frac{\sigma}{4\sqrt K}
\le\mathbb E[W_+\mathbf1_{\{W\ge t\}}]
\le\sigma\sqrt{\Pr(W\ge t)},
\qquad
\Pr(W\ge t)\ge\frac1{16K}.
\]

It follows that \(G\ge\mathbb Ee^W\ge e^t/(16K)\). Using \(e^t\ge t^2/2\), we obtain \(G\ge\sigma^2/(512K^2)\), or \(\sigma^2\le512K^2G\). If \(\sigma=0\), then \(W=0\) almost surely and this same inequality holds without using the positive-threshold argument.

To bound the noise, partition the probability space into \(B:=\{|N|\ge2|W|\}\) and its complement. Pointwise on \(B\),

\[
e^W\cosh N
\ge\frac12e^{W+|N|}
\ge\frac12e^{|N|/2}
\ge\frac{N^2}{16}.
\]

The last inequality follows from \(e^z\ge z^2/2\) for \(z\ge0\), with \(z=|N|/2\). On \(B^c\), \(N^2\le4W^2\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EN^2&\le16G+4\sigma^2,\\
\operatorname{Var}(W+N)
&=\sigma^2+\mathbb EN^2
\le16G+5\sigma^2
\le(16+2560K^2)G.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves the advertised constant. If \(\sigma=0\), the first line already gives \(\operatorname{Var}(W+N)\le16G\). If the total variance is zero, the variance identity forces \(W=N=0\) almost surely; then \(G=1\). Thus no zero-variance case required division by zero. Finally, \(\operatorname{Var}(W+N)\le c_{\exp}(K)G\le c_{\exp}(K)G^2\), because \(G\ge1\); taking square roots gives the stated weaker square-root bound.

Next apply this result to an original normalized row of 1232ba9a1c0673db. Retain that fact's symbols \(S,Y,Y_-,Y_+,Y_0,\lambda,C_{\mathrm{cen}}\) with their exact meanings. In particular, \(\lambda\) here is its scalar outer-branch probability, whereas \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\) below is a vector exponential parameter. On the middle branch, of probability \(1-\lambda\), sample \(Y_0\) and set \((S,Y)=(0,Y_0)\) and \(C_{\mathrm{cen}}=Y_0\). On the outer branch, of probability \(\lambda\), sample the independent pair \(Y_-,Y_+\) and an independent equiprobable sign \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), and set

\[
C_{\mathrm{cen}}=\frac{Y_-+Y_+}{2},
\qquad
(S,Y)=\left(\epsilon,
          \frac{Y_-+Y_+}{2}
          +\epsilon\frac{Y_+-Y_-}{2}\right).
\]

The accepted fact proves that this gives the exact original atom probabilities. It also proves \(\mathbb EC_{\mathrm{cen}}=0\) and \(\mathbb EC_{\mathrm{cen}}^4\le K_{\mathrm{cen}}(\mathbb EC_{\mathrm{cen}}^2)^2\).

Fix any real direction \((a,b)\). Take \(W=bC_{\mathrm{cen}}\). Define \(N=0\) on the middle branch and \(N=\epsilon(a+b(Y_+-Y_-)/2)\) on the outer branch. Then \(aS+bY=W+N\). Let \(\mathcal G\) consist of the branch and the sampled vertical coordinate or pair, with the outer sign omitted. This determines \(W\), and the conditional law of \(N\) is symmetric, including on the middle branch. Scaling the accepted center inequality gives \(\mathbb EW^4\le K_{\mathrm{cen}}(\mathbb EW^2)^2\), also when \(b=0\). The scalar theorem now proves the stated directional bound with \(c_{\mathrm{lik},2}=c_{\exp}(K_{\mathrm{cen}})\). In particular it controls every original direction, even when its variance comes entirely from the sign noise.

We now establish the row attenuation for the arbitrary source \(p\) in the statement. The entropy and face assertions of bf1ff94487296374 apply because \(A\) is finite and \(p\) is positive. In the log-affine case, every mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\eta\) satisfies

\[
D(\eta\|p)
=-H(\eta)-\log p_{\mathbf j},
\qquad
H(\eta):=-\sum_{\mathbf x}\eta(\mathbf x)\log\eta(\mathbf x).
\]

Indeed, the mean constraint evaluates the affine function \(\log p_{\mathbf x}\) at \(\mathbf j\). Thus the relative-entropy minimizer is also the maximum-entropy row, giving the aliases stated above; these laws are not replaced by a row with different weights.

The finite face multiplier formula in bf1ff94487296374 provides a vector \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\) in the direction space of \(F_{\mathbf j}\) with

\[
\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
=\frac{p_{\mathbf x}
          e^{\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}}
       {\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)}
\quad(\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf j}).
\]

Here \(\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\) is exactly that predecessor's face exponential multiplier \(\mathbf a\) for this target. Its face normalizer equals the capacity by the accepted entropy-capacity identity, even when the ambient infimum is attained only in a limit. Set \(\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}:=\boldsymbol\ell+\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\). Substituting the log-affine formula for \(p\) gives the claimed exponential representation for \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) on \(E_{\mathbf j}\). Rearranging the multiplier formula gives the exact attenuation identity with \(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}=-\mathbf a_{\mathbf j}\).

Summing that identity over the face gives \(q_{\mathbf j}=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)G_{\mathbf j}\). In particular, the numerator is the original source mass on the face, not a renormalized face law. Since \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) has mean \(\mathbf j\), \(\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}T_{\mathbf j}=0\), and Jensen gives \(G_{\mathbf j}\ge1\). As \(0<q_{\mathbf j}\le1\), this proves \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=q_{\mathbf j}/G_{\mathbf j}\le1/G_{\mathbf j}\). On the positive support of the row,

\[
\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)}{p_{\mathbf x}}
=-\log\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
 -(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})
      \cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j),
\qquad
V_{\mathbf j}=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}T_{\mathbf j}.
\]

It remains to verify the exponential-moment variance bound for every such row. If \(\dim F_{\mathbf j}=0\), the row is the point mass at \(\mathbf j\), so \(T_{\mathbf j}=0\), \(G_{\mathbf j}=1\), and \(V_{\mathbf j}=0\).

Suppose that \(\dim F_{\mathbf j}=1\). Completeness gives \(E_{\mathbf j}=F_{\mathbf j}\cap\mathbb Z^2\). An endpoint \(\mathbf x_0\) and the primitive integer direction \(\mathbf v\) of this lattice segment identify its support as \(\{\mathbf x_0+x\mathbf v: x=0,\ldots,m-1\}\). The intrinsic target index \(j\) in \(\mathbf j=\mathbf x_0+j\mathbf v\) is an integer. For a rank-one minimal face this index is interior, and the exponential row becomes the geometric law on \(D=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) with integral mean \(j\). This is exactly the interior maximum-entropy row \(\nu_j\) of 075a3f88fa24d326, under its scalar coordinate alias. It is also the same face row \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) under the map \(x\mapsto\mathbf x_0+x\mathbf v\).

That accepted fact gives \(\mathbb E(X-j)^4\le20288(\operatorname{Var}X)^2\) in these intrinsic coordinates. The directional scalar \(T_{\mathbf j}\) is \(((\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})\cdot\mathbf v)(X-j)\); hence it has mean zero and the same fourth-moment regularity constant, including when this coefficient vanishes. Apply the proved scalar theorem with \(W=T_{\mathbf j}\), \(N=0\), and \(K=20288\). We obtain \(V_{\mathbf j}\le c_{\exp}(20288)G_{\mathbf j}\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}G_{\mathbf j}\), since \(K_{\mathrm{cen}}\ge20288\) and \(c_{\exp}\) is increasing. This covers rank-one supports as well as all rank-one proper faces of planar supports.

Finally suppose that \(\dim F_{\mathbf j}=2\). Then \(r=2\), \(F_{\mathbf j}=\operatorname{conv}A\), and the row is positive on all of \(A\). If \(A\) has lattice width one, choose a primitive integer functional with values ranging from an integer \(h\) to \(h+1\). Positivity gives positive row mass on both extreme levels. Its expected functional value therefore lies strictly between \(h\) and \(h+1\). This is impossible because the row mean \(\mathbf j\) is integral, so that functional's expected value equals an integer. A full-rank integral-mean row cannot therefore occur in this case.

In the remaining case the full-rank support has lattice width two. Apply 1232ba9a1c0673db with its untransformed support \(D=A\), its original row law \(p=\nu_{\mathbf j}\), its original tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta=\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\), and its vector mean \(\mathbf j\). This is an explicit change of the predecessor's law label: its original \(p\) denotes our entropy row \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), not our source law \(p\). All its hypotheses hold: the support is complete and full rank, the row has a finite log-affine tilt and positive masses, and its mean is integral.

Let \(T\) be the predecessor's unimodular coordinate matrix and put \((S,Y)=T(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\). Its transformed support is exactly the three nonempty columns \(-1,0,1\), and its transformed row keeps every probability of \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\). The transformed tilt is \((T^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}\). The scalar \(T_{\mathbf j}\) is the original direction in these coordinates:

\[
T_{\mathbf j}=aS+bY,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=(T^{-1})^{\mathsf T}
       (\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j}).
\]

Thus the all-direction bound already proved for the exact normalized row gives \(V_{\mathbf j}\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}G_{\mathbf j}\). The matrix \(T\) and the scalar \(T_{\mathbf j}\) are distinct objects; the displayed equality specifies their entire relationship. Both the variance and exponential moment are unchanged when the coordinate transformation is undone. This completes the bound for every target and every minimal-face rank.

Combine it with attenuation to obtain

\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2V_{\mathbf j}
\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}\frac{q_{\mathbf j}^2}{G_{\mathbf j}}
\le c_{\mathrm{lik},2}.
\]

The capacity lower bound of bf1ff94487296374 applies to the original complete log-affine source in its intrinsic rank, so \(\mathcal B_2(p)\ge4^{-r}>0\). In particular, the original root weights are well defined and sum to one. Using these weights, and then \(\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}=1\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\omega}V_{\mathbf j}
&=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}
          \mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2V_{\mathbf j}}
        {\mathcal B_2(p)}\\
&\le\frac{c_{\mathrm{lik},2}}{\mathcal B_2(p)}
 \le4^r c_{\mathrm{lik},2}\le16c_{\mathrm{lik},2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(r=0\) this expectation is zero. For \(r=1\) the same argument even gives the displayed factor \(4\) instead of \(16\). Every face mass has been retained in the exact identity \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=q_{\mathbf j}/G_{\mathbf j}\), and no conditioning changes the target weights in this final average.
