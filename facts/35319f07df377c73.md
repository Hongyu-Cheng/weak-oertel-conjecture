For an empty sublevel this also holds. Because \(s_\star>0\) and \(B_i\ge2\), \(F_{\rm tail}(0)=0\). Ties among the \(B_i\) are included in the chosen largest index.

Now apply the new separation (5). With \(v_i=s_\star B_i\), one has \(e^{-\beta B_i}\le e^{-3v_i/4}=e^{-v_i/12}e^{-2v_i/3}\). Stieltjes summation and integration by parts give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i>j_\star}e^{-\beta B_i}
&\le\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-v/12}\,dF_{\rm tail}(v)\\
&=\frac1{12}\int_0^\infty e^{-v/12}F_{\rm tail}(v)\,dv\\
&\le\frac{K_\diamond}{12}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-v/12}(1+v^2)\,dv
=289K_\diamond.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
$$
The lower boundary is zero because \(F_{\rm tail}(0)=0\), and the upper boundary vanishes because this cumulative function is a finite step function. The final constant is exactly \(1+2\cdot12^2\): substitution \(v=12u\) and two integrations by parts give \(\int_0^\infty u^2e^{-u}\,du=2\). Equation (34) also includes an empty tail. No relation between \(s_\star\) and any active height was used.

We charge this whole tail once using original masses. Partition the full support into sectors by fixing disjoint subsets of indices \(i>j_\star\) at sites \(2\) and \(3\), with every other tail coordinate binary. Let \(E_{\rm fix}\) be the total fixed energy of a sector. If \(E_{\rm fix}>T\), that sector is empty. Otherwise shifting height from \(t\) to \(t'=t-E_{\rm fix}\) factors its unnormalized original mass into \(e^{-\beta E_{\rm fix}}\) times the remaining-coordinate law capped at \(T-E_{\rm fix}\). Appending zeros in the fixed coordinates injects that shifted support into \(A_T^{(j_\star)}\) with exactly its weights \(e^{-\beta t'}\). Thus the sector mass is at most \(e^{-\beta E_{\rm fix}}Z^{(j_\star)}\). Summing these bounds over all sectors gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac Z{Z^{(j_\star)}}
&\le\prod_{i>j_\star}
 (1+e^{-\beta B_i}+e^{-\beta C_{4,i}})\\
&\le\exp\left(2\sum_{i>j_\star}e^{-\beta B_i}\right)
\le\exp(578K_\diamond).
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
$$
Here \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge B_i\) and \(\beta\ge0\). Empty sectors only enlarge the stated product bound; no feasibility assumption on an individual expensive site is needed for (34)-(35).

Finally \(A_T^{(j_\star)}\subseteq A_T\) in the same ambient space, and on that subset the original probabilities equal \(Z^{(j_\star)}/Z\) times the prefix probabilities. Every containing ambient halfspace has at least this ratio times its prefix mass. Taking infima, cubing, and sampling only prefix atoms proves
$$
M_3(p)\ge
\left(\frac{Z^{(j_\star)}}Z\right)^4
M_3(p^{(j_\star)})
\ge
\frac{\exp(-2312K_\diamond)}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{36}
$$
There are exactly four powers in (36), one for the root and three for depth. In particular the single mass cost \(578K_\diamond\) incurs the exponent \(4\cdot578K_\diamond=2312K_\diamond\).

For \(k>N_\diamond\) the interior case is (36) and the upper endpoint is (12). For \(k\le N_\diamond\) use (10), including \(k=0\). The singleton zero-cap case was settled directly.
