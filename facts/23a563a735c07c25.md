## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^D$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body of volume $V$, with $D\ge1$. Let $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ be proper closed halfspaces covering $K$, where $m\ge1$, and write $A_i=|K\cap H_i|$. Put $\gamma_0=1$ and $\gamma_q=(q/(q+1))^q$ for integers $q\ge1$. For any fixed inclusion-minimal subcover indexed by $I$, let $j=|I|$ and let $\rho$ be the rank of its normals. For every $\alpha\ge0$,
$$
\sum_{i\in I}(A_i-\alpha)_+ + \frac{\alpha}{\gamma_{\min(\rho,j-1)}}\ge V,
\qquad x_+=\max(x,0).
$$
Consequently, with $s=\min(D,m-1)$,
$$
\sum_{i=1}^m(A_i-\alpha)_+ + \frac{\alpha}{\gamma_s}\ge V
\quad(\alpha\ge0),
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m A_i-V\ge (m-1/\gamma_s)\min_i A_i .
$$
For each pair $(D,m)$, $\gamma_s$ is the largest constant that can replace it in the all-cover positive-part profile.

## Proof

**Hereditary depth for the marginal densities.** Suppose an integrable density $f$ is positive on a nonempty full-dimensional open convex set $U\subset\mathbb R^q$, zero elsewhere, and $f^{-1/(q+1)}$ is finite and convex on $U$. For a closed convex set $L$ with positive $f$-mass, $U'=U\cap\operatorname{int}L$ is nonempty and open: $L$ has positive Lebesgue measure, and its boundary is Lebesgue-null. The restriction to $U'$ gives the same measure as $f\mathbf1_L$. Exhaust $U'$ by compact convex sets contained in it, for example by intersecting closed balls of increasing radii $n$ with the points of distance at least $1/n$ from its complement (if the complement is empty, use just the balls). Each truncation of positive mass is bounded and upper semicontinuous: the density is continuous on $U'$ by finiteness and positivity of its convex inverse power, and the truncation set is closed. The inverse power extended by infinity stays convex. Accepted fact d6a3cbae1da72277 gives a point of depth at least $\gamma_q$ times each truncated mass. The mass of every halfspace for $f\mathbf1_L$ dominates its truncated mass, so the supremum of the depth for $f\mathbf1_L$ is at least $\gamma_q\int_L f$ by monotone convergence.

This supremum is attained in $U'$. Indeed, depth is the minimum over unit normals of the mass of a halfspace through the candidate point. The mass is jointly continuous in point and normal by dominated convergence, since hyperplanes are null, so the compact unit sphere gives continuity of the minimum. At a point of large norm the outward halfspace has mass at most the vanishing tail beyond that norm. At every point outside $U'$, a separating or supporting halfspace through the point has mass zero. The positive maximum is therefore attained in $U'$. Taking $L$ within the closed convex support of $f(z)\,dz$ verifies the hereditary premise of accepted fact ac7ba1e1e0ebc955. These same arguments apply without assuming boundedness of the original support. Also, any full-dimensional log-concave density satisfies the inverse-power hypothesis on the positive interior: $f^{-1/(q+1)}=\exp(-\log f/(q+1))$ is convex there.

**Fix an inclusion-minimal subcover $I$.** If $j=1$, its cap mass is $V$ and $(V-\alpha)_++\alpha\ge V$. Let $j\ge2$, so $1\le\rho\le j$. If $\rho<j$, project orthogonally onto the $\rho$-dimensional normal span. Each selected halfspace is the inverse image of a proper closed halfspace, and the projected halfspaces cover the projected body. Its pushforward measure has the same total mass and selected cap masses. When $\rho=D$, the density is $\mathbf1_K$. Otherwise it is the section volume on the orthogonal complement. Brunn–Minkowski and the inclusion of Minkowski combinations of sections in the corresponding intermediate section make its $(D-\rho)$th root concave on the positive set. This implies log-concavity by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. The density is positive on the nonempty open projection of $\operatorname{int}K$, and every point in the interior of the projected body lies in that projection: extend a segment from a projected interior representative past the desired point within the projected body, and lift the resulting convex combination with the interior representative. Thus the measure may be represented by its density on the positive interior, discarding a null boundary. Its closed support is covered by the same projected halfspaces. Apply the hereditary depth conclusion in dimension $\rho$ and the trimming theorem ac7ba1e1e0ebc955 to this fixed projected family. This proves the subcover inequality with $\gamma_\rho$.

If $\rho=j$, write $\ell_i$ for the affine margin defining each selected $H_i=\{\ell_i\ge0\}$. The map of margins is an affine surjection onto $\mathbb R^j$. Its compact convex image of $K$ avoids the open negative orthant. Separation supplies a nonzero linear functional with coefficient vector $\lambda$ and with $\inf_{x\in K}\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i\ell_i(x)$ at least its supremum on the open negative orthant. Finiteness forces every $\lambda_i\ge0$, so that supremum is zero. If any coefficient were zero, the positive-coefficient caps alone would cover $K$, contrary to minimality. The affine coordinate $t=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i\ell_i$ is nonconstant by independence and nonnegative on $K$.

Complete $t$ to linear coordinates $(v,t)$ on the margin space, then to affine coordinates $(v,t,y)$ in $\mathbb R^D$. Every $\ell_i$ is a homogeneous linear form in $(v,t)$. The projective marginal lemma gives a density in $j-1$ dimensions with convex inverse power $-1/j$, preserving the total mass and all selected cap masses. The projected affine margins are $\ell_i$ expressed in the new coordinates at $(v,t)=(z,1)$ and satisfy $\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i\ell_i(z,1)=1$ identically. Their closed positive halfspaces thus cover all of $\mathbb R^{j-1}$. Their normals are nonzero: a constant projected margin would make its original affine margin proportional to $t$, contradicting independence and the positive coefficients of the other margins. Apply the hereditary depth conclusion in dimension $j-1$ and ac7ba1e1e0ebc955 to this fixed family. Its premise holds for every positive-mass closed convex remainder of the projected support; it requires no change of subfamily or projective coordinates. The null set $t=0$ in the original volume does not affect any cap masses.

The sequence $\gamma_q$ decreases with $q$: for $q>0$ the function $q\log(1+1/q)$ increases, since $\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)$ for $x>0$, and $\gamma_0=1$. Since $\min(\rho,j-1)\le s$, increasing the coefficient of $\alpha$ to $1/\gamma_s$ and restoring the omitted nonnegative terms proves the full profile. Evaluating it at $\alpha=\min_i A_i$, including when this minimum is zero, gives the overlap bound.

**Sharpness.** For $2\le m\le D+1$ take an $s$-simplex times a convex body of positive volume in the remaining dimensions, omitting the product factor if $s=D$. Use the $s+1$ caps given by one barycentric coordinate at least $1/(s+1)$, extended to the product. They cover because the coordinates sum to one. Each cap cuts out a copy of the simplex scaled by $s/(s+1)$ about its corresponding vertex, hence has volume $\gamma_s V$. At $\alpha=\gamma_s V$ all positive-part terms vanish, and the profile is an equality; a larger factor would fail. The overlap bound is also attained here with positive minimum. For $m>D+1$, use the $D+1$ canonical caps of a $D$-simplex and append disjoint-from-$K$ halfspaces; the same test gives profile sharpness. Those appended caps give zero minimum, so they do not furnish equality with a positive minimum in the overlap bound. If repeated halfspaces are allowed, repeating canonical caps instead preserves the common positive mass $\gamma_DV$ and gives equality in the overlap bound as well. When $m=1$, a cap of mass $V$ and $\alpha=V$ give sharpness of $\gamma_0=1$.
