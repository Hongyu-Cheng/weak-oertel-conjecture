## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(p_{\mathbf I}>0\) on \(D\), with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(p_{\mathbf I}=0\) off \(D\). Assume that a finite-valued log-concave function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agrees with \(p\) at every lattice point, including every zero. Thus, for \(x,x'\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\), require \(f((1-t)x+tx')\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(x')^t\), with zero on the right if either endpoint value is zero. Assume that the complete table is invariant under each coordinate reflection \(I_i\mapsto1-I_i\).

Use the normalization of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`: \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\). All logarithms are natural. Use exactly the feasible arrays, objective, and entropy of accepted fact `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\quad(\mathbf J\in D)
\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2,\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(0\log0=0\). Let \(r^\star\) be the unique maximizer of \(H\) among the arrays minimizing \(V\) on \(\mathcal F\). In particular, \(H(r^\star)\) is the conditional entropy of the target assignment given the source, with precisely the denominator \(p_{\mathbf I}\) displayed above.

Retain \(R_i(\mathbf I)=|I_i-\tfrac12|\), the joint radius vector \(\mathbf R\), and the expanded-axis set \(\mathcal X=\{i:\max_{\mathbf I\in D}R_i(\mathbf I)>\tfrac12\}\) of `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`. For each \(i\in\mathcal X\), let \(\theta_i>0\) be its unique finite solution to \(\mathbb E_p[R_i\tanh(\theta_iR_i)]=\tfrac12\). No multiplier is assigned to an unexpanded axis; its assignment is exactly \(J_i=I_i\). The accepted formula gives

\[
H(r^\star)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}
\left[\mathbb E_p\log\bigl(2\cosh(\theta_iR_i)\bigr)-\frac{\theta_i}{2}\right].
\]

For \(0\le u\le1\), define the auxiliary binary entropy \(\mathsf h(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)\), with both endpoint terms interpreted by continuity. Put \(b_i=\mathbb P_p(R_i-\tfrac12\text{ is an odd integer})\). Then \(0\le b_i\le\tfrac12\) and

\[
0\le\mathbb E_p\phi
\le2\sum_{i=1}^n\mathsf h(b_i)
\le4H(r^\star).
\]

For each \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\), retain the exact row support and row mass of `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`, namely \(D_{\mathbf J}=\{\mathbf I\in D:I_i=J_i\text{ for every }i\notin\mathcal X\}\) and \(q^\star_{\mathbf J}=2^{-n}\). Every entry \(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) on this support is positive. The row energy satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}}
\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q^\star_{\mathbf J}}
\log\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}}{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}
&=\mathbb E_p\phi+H(r^\star)
-\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}\log(1+e^{-\theta_i})\\
&\le\mathbb E_p\phi+H(r^\star)\le5H(r^\star).
\end{aligned}
\]

The assertions include all unexpanded axes and empty sums. Moreover, \(H(r^\star)=0\) holds if and only if \(D=\{0,1\}^n\). In that case \(p\) is uniform on this cube, \(\phi=0\) on \(D\), and every displayed energy is zero.

## Proof

Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives convex-admissibility of the original potential: every lattice point in a convex combination of points of \(D\) belongs to \(D\), and its potential is at most the corresponding average potential. Accepted fact `eb9a8fb910b2faf3` gives \(\{0,1\}^n\subseteq D\), \(p_{\mathbf J}=w_{\max}\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf J}=0\) on this cube, and equal masses at the \(2^n\) points of each reflection orbit. It also gives the stated entropy formula, row supports, row masses, row means, and conservation of the complete joint radius law.

Reindex the original joint radius law by its integer excess vector. Specifically, for \(\mathbf I\) with law \(p\), let \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf R(\mathbf I)-\tfrac12\mathbf1\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), and let \(\nu\) be its law. Its exact normalization is

\[
\nu_{\mathbf k}=2^np_{\mathbf1+\mathbf k},\qquad
\nu_{\mathbf0}=2^nw_{\max}>0,\qquad
\Phi_{\mathbf k}:=\phi_{\mathbf1+\mathbf k},\qquad
\nu_{\mathbf k}=\nu_{\mathbf0}e^{-\Phi_{\mathbf k}}
\quad(\nu_{\mathbf k}>0).
\]

Here \(\Phi\) is only a reindexing of the canonical potential \(\phi\), and \(\nu\) is only a reindexing of the canonical joint radius law, not a product of its marginals. Indeed, the positive-sign representative of the orbit with excess vector \(\mathbf k\) is \(\mathbf1+\mathbf k\). Every orbit has exactly \(2^n\) points and equal masses at these points. In particular, \(\nu\) is a finite probability law and \(\mathbb E_\nu\Phi=\mathbb E_p\phi\).

For a supported \(\mathbf k\), put \(\boldsymbol\eta=\mathbf k-2\lfloor\mathbf k/2\rfloor\in\{0,1\}^n\), where the floor is coordinatewise. The exact midpoint identity is

\[
\mathbf1+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathbf k}{2}\right\rfloor
=\frac12(\mathbf1+\mathbf k)+\frac12(\mathbf1-\boldsymbol\eta).
\]

The second endpoint is a cube vertex and has potential zero. Lattice convexity and convex-admissibility therefore prove that \(\lfloor\mathbf k/2\rfloor\) is supported by \(\nu\) and that

\[
\Phi_{\lfloor\mathbf k/2\rfloor}\le\frac12\Phi_{\mathbf k}.
\]

Thus this contraction uses supported lattice points of the original table and includes odd excess coordinates exactly. It needs no continuous rounding estimate.

Write \(\mathsf S(\mathbf U)=-\sum_{\mathbf u}\mathbb P(\mathbf U=\mathbf u)\log\mathbb P(\mathbf U=\mathbf u)\) for the ordinary Shannon entropy of a finite random vector \(\mathbf U\). This auxiliary entropy is distinct from the conditional assignment entropy \(H(r^\star)\). Let \(\mathbf Y=\lfloor\mathbf Z/2\rfloor\). The law of \(\mathbf Y\) is supported by \(\nu\), as just proved. Nonnegativity of relative entropy gives

\[
\mathsf S(\mathbf Y)+\log\nu_{\mathbf0}
\le\mathbb E\Phi_{\mathbf Y},\qquad
\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)+\log\nu_{\mathbf0}
=\mathbb E\Phi_{\mathbf Z}.
\]

For completeness, if \(\mu\) is any probability law supported where \(\nu>0\), summing \(a\log(a/b)-a+b\ge0\) with \(a=\mu_{\mathbf k}\) and \(b=\nu_{\mathbf k}\) gives \(\sum\mu_{\mathbf k}\log(\mu_{\mathbf k}/\nu_{\mathbf k})\ge0\). The scalar inequality follows by minimizing \(t\log t-t+1\) for \(t\ge0\), with its value at zero defined by continuity. Substituting \(\log\nu_{\mathbf k}=\log\nu_{\mathbf0}-\Phi_{\mathbf k}\) proves the first displayed inequality; taking \(\mu=\nu\) gives the second equality. Consequently the contraction yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\phi
&\le\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)-\mathsf S(\mathbf Y)
+\mathbb E\Phi_{\mathbf Y}\\
&\le\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)-\mathsf S(\mathbf Y)
+\frac12\mathbb E_p\phi,
\end{aligned}
\]

and hence \(\mathbb E_p\phi\le2[\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)-\mathsf S(\mathbf Y)]\).

Now let \(\boldsymbol\eta=\mathbf Z-2\mathbf Y\in\{0,1\}^n\) be the random parity vector. The map \(\mathbf Z\mapsto(\mathbf Y,\boldsymbol\eta)\) is a bijection onto its image, so \(\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)=\mathsf S(\mathbf Y,\boldsymbol\eta)\) by the entropy definition. Apply the same nonnegativity of relative entropy to the joint law of \((\mathbf Y,\boldsymbol\eta)\) and the product of the law of \(\mathbf Y\) with the individual marginal laws of \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_n\). Expanding its logarithm gives

\[
\mathsf S(\mathbf Z)-\mathsf S(\mathbf Y)
\le\sum_{i=1}^n\mathsf h(b_i),\qquad
b_i=\mathbb P(\eta_i=1).
\]

The reference product law is positive wherever the joint law is positive, including when a marginal is deterministic, so this relative-entropy application has no undefined positive-mass terms. This is an inequality for the actual correlated joint law. It does not assert independence of the radii or their parities. We have proved \(\mathbb E_p\phi\le2\sum_i\mathsf h(b_i)\).

We next show \(b_i\le\tfrac12\), using the original reflection and convexity assumptions. Suppose \(\nu_{\mathbf k}>0\) and \(k_i\ge1\). Set \(\mathbf I=\mathbf1+\mathbf k\), and let \(\sigma_i\mathbf I\) be its reflection in coordinate \(i\). Both endpoints belong to \(D\) and have equal potential. The exact convex combination

\[
\mathbf I-\mathbf e_i
=\left(1-\frac1{2k_i+1}\right)\mathbf I
+\frac1{2k_i+1}\sigma_i\mathbf I
\]

is a lattice point, so it lies in \(D\) and has potential at most \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\). Thus \(\nu_{\mathbf k-\mathbf e_i}\ge\nu_{\mathbf k}\). The map \(\mathbf k\mapsto\mathbf k-\mathbf e_i\) injects the supported vectors with odd \(k_i\) into those with even \(k_i\). Summing the last inequality gives \(b_i\le1-b_i\), as required.

To compare the parity entropies with the exact assignment entropy, put \(m_i=\mathbb E_p(R_i-\tfrac12)\ge0\) and \(\epsilon_i=m_i/(1+2m_i)\). These auxiliary quantities depend on the original marginal moments only; no marginal product replaces \(p\). Since an odd nonnegative integer is at least one, \(m_i\ge b_i\). Also \(m_i=0\) exactly on the unexpanded axes, and then \(b_i=\epsilon_i=0\).

For an expanded axis \(m_i>0\), so \(0<\epsilon_i<\tfrac12\). For every real \(x\) and \(0<\epsilon<1\), concavity of the logarithm gives the elementary binary inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(2\cosh x)
&=\log\left((1-\epsilon)\frac{e^x}{1-\epsilon}
+\epsilon\frac{e^{-x}}{\epsilon}\right)\\
&\ge(1-2\epsilon)x+\mathsf h(\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
\]

Apply this with \(x=\theta_iR_i\) and \(\epsilon=\epsilon_i\), and average under the original law. The identity \((1-2\epsilon_i)\mathbb E_pR_i=\tfrac12\) then yields

\[
\mathbb E_p\log(2\cosh(\theta_iR_i))-\frac{\theta_i}{2}
\ge\mathsf h(\epsilon_i).
\]

Here \(\theta_i\) is precisely the accepted multiplier satisfying \(\mathbb E_p[R_i\tanh(\theta_iR_i)]=\tfrac12\); the left side is its exact contribution to \(H(r^\star)\). Summing over expanded axes and inserting the zero contributions of the unexpanded axes proves \(H(r^\star)\ge\sum_{i=1}^n\mathsf h(\epsilon_i)\).

The function \(u\mapsto u/(1+2u)\) is increasing for \(u\ge0\). Because \(m_i\ge b_i\) and \(b_i\le\tfrac12\),

\[
\frac12\ge\epsilon_i
\ge\frac{b_i}{1+2b_i}\ge\frac{b_i}{2}.
\]

Binary entropy is increasing on \([0,\tfrac12]\) and concave on \([0,1]\): on the open interval its derivatives are \(\mathsf h'(u)=\log((1-u)/u)\) and \(\mathsf h''(u)=-1/u-1/(1-u)<0\), and it is continuous at the endpoints. Since \(\mathsf h(0)=0\), these properties give

\[
\mathsf h(\epsilon_i)\ge\mathsf h(b_i/2)
\ge\frac12\mathsf h(b_i).
\]

The inequality includes \(b_i=0\). Combining all the proved bounds gives
\(\mathbb E_p\phi\le2\sum_i\mathsf h(b_i)\le4\sum_i\mathsf h(\epsilon_i)\le4H(r^\star)\).
The lower bound \(\mathbb E_p\phi\ge0\) follows from the canonical normalization \(\phi\ge0\).

It remains to establish the exact row-energy formula. Use the assignment-share potential of `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`, with exactly its normalization \(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=-\log(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D_{\mathbf J}\). Its accepted explicit formula is

\[
\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)
=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}
\left[\log(2\cosh(\theta_iR_i(\mathbf I)))
-(2J_i-1)\theta_i(I_i-\tfrac12)\right].
\]

Each normalized row has mean \(\mathbf J\) and retains the complete original joint radius law. Therefore, averaging this formula in that row gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}}
\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q^\star_{\mathbf J}}
\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)
&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}
\left[\mathbb E_p\log(2\cosh(\theta_iR_i))
-(2J_i-1)\theta_i(J_i-\tfrac12)\right]\\
&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}
\left[\mathbb E_p\log(2\cosh(\theta_iR_i))
-\frac{\theta_i}{2}\right]
=H(r^\star).
\end{aligned}
\]

At the cube target itself, \(R_i(\mathbf J)=\tfrac12\), so
\(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf J)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}[\log(2\cosh(\theta_i/2))-\theta_i/2]=\sum_{i\in\mathcal X}\log(1+e^{-\theta_i})\).
Furthermore \(p_{\mathbf J}=w_{\max}\), and hence on the positive row support

\[
\log\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}}{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}
=\phi_{\mathbf I}+\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)-\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf J).
\]

The accepted conservation of the original potential distribution makes the row average of \(\phi\) equal to \(\mathbb E_p\phi\). Averaging the last identity proves the stated exact row energy. Every subtracted term \(\log(1+e^{-\theta_i})\) is nonnegative, so the proved \(C=4\) inequality gives its upper bound by \(\mathbb E_p\phi+H(r^\star)\le5H(r^\star)\). All averages use the original joint radius law, and the row-mean identity is used only after that law has been averaged.

Finally, if any axis is expanded, its \(m_i>0\) gives \(\mathsf h(\epsilon_i)>0\), and the established entropy lower bound forces \(H(r^\star)>0\). If no axis is expanded, all supported coordinates lie in \(\{0,1\}\); since the full cube is contained in \(D\), this means \(D=\{0,1\}^n\). Reflection invariance then gives the uniform table \(p_{\mathbf I}=2^{-n}\), and all potentials vanish. The accepted deterministic assignment on every axis is \(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf J=\mathbf I\}}\). Its conditional assignment entropy and every row energy are zero. This proves both directions of the zero-entropy assertion and completes all boundary cases.
