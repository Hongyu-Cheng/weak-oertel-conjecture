## Statement

Fix \(C\), a nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching \(q=(q_e)\), its finite positive support \(F\), and the data \(m_e,L_e,D_e\) from the weighted affine-defect dual of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d. In particular, every \(D_e\) is an origin-symmetric convex body with \(0\) in its interior, so \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm on the dual coordinate space. Let \(c=(c_e)_{e\in F}\) be any subcapacity with \(0\le c_e\le q_e\) for every \(e\); here \(c\) denotes edge capacities, not the fiber-centroid coordinates \(c_z\) of the accepted fact. Put
\[
\mathcal F(c):=\left\{v=(v_e)_{e\in F}:\sum_{e\in F}v_e=0,\ \sum_{e\in F}v_em_e^{\mathsf T}=0,\ h_{D_e}(v_e)\le c_e\ \text{for every }e\in F\right\}
\]
and
\[
\Delta_C(c):=\max_{v\in\mathcal F(c)}\Phi(v),
\qquad
\Phi(v):=-\sum_{e\in F}h_{L_e}(v_e).
\]
For a zero capacity \(c_e=0\), the convention is to retain the coordinate in \(F\); its constraint forces \(v_e=0\), equivalently one may delete that coordinate. If \(c=0\), set \(\Delta_C(0)=0\), consistently with the displayed dual, whose only feasible point is \(0\). For a nonzero subcapacity with some zero coordinates, deleting those coordinates leaves a nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching, so the displayed maximum is exactly the dual formula of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d for that restricted matching.

Let \(\operatorname{Opt}(c):=\{v\in\mathcal F(c):\Phi(v)=\Delta_C(c)\}\), choose any \(v^*\in\operatorname{Opt}(c)\) minimizing
\[
\lambda(v):=\sum_{e\in F}h_{D_e}(v_e)
\]
over \(\operatorname{Opt}(c)\), and define
\[
w_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e^*)\qquad(e\in F).
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_C(w)=\Delta_C(c).
\]
Moreover, every \(\widetilde v\in\operatorname{Opt}(w)\) satisfies
\[
h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)=w_e\qquad\text{for every }e\in F.
\]
Thus every positive reduced capacity is saturated, while a zero reduced capacity is saturated in the precise sense \(\widetilde v_e=0\) and \(h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)=0=w_e\). Every \(w\)-optimizer is consequently gauge-load-minimal for the \(w\)-problem, and applying the load operation again returns \(w\). If \(\Delta_C(c)=0\), then \(v^*=0\) and \(w=0\). No uniqueness of \(w\) across different chosen gauge-load-minimal optimizers for a positive-defect \(c\)-problem is asserted.

## Proof

Because \(F\) is finite and every \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm, the coordinate bounds in \(\mathcal F(c)\) make this feasible set bounded; it is closed and contains \(0\), hence it is compact and nonempty. The objective \(\Phi\) and the load \(\lambda\) are continuous. Thus \(\operatorname{Opt}(c)\) is nonempty and compact and a load-minimal optimizer \(v^*\) exists.

First suppose \(\Delta_C(c)>0\). Since \(v^*\in\mathcal F(c)\), one has \(0\le w_e\le c_e\) for every \(e\), and the definition of \(w\) also gives \(v^*\in\mathcal F(w)\). Since \(w\le c\) coordinatewise, \(\mathcal F(w)\subseteq\mathcal F(c)\). Therefore
\[
\Delta_C(c)=\Phi(v^*)\le\Delta_C(w)\le\Delta_C(c),
\]
which proves \(\Delta_C(w)=\Delta_C(c)\) and shows that \(v^*\in\operatorname{Opt}(w)\).

Let \(\widetilde v\in\operatorname{Opt}(w)\). The inclusion \(\mathcal F(w)\subseteq\mathcal F(c)\) and the equality of dual values show that \(\widetilde v\in\operatorname{Opt}(c)\). Gauge-load minimality of \(v^*\) on \(\operatorname{Opt}(c)\) gives
\[
\lambda(\widetilde v)\ge\lambda(v^*)=\sum_{e\in F}w_e.
\]
On the other hand, \(w\)-feasibility gives
\[
\lambda(\widetilde v)=\sum_{e\in F}h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)\le\sum_{e\in F}w_e.
\]
Hence equality holds. Each slack \(w_e-h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)\) is nonnegative, and their finite sum is zero, so every slack is zero. This proves \(h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)=w_e\) for every \(e\), including every positive-capacity coordinate. If \(w_e=0\), the same conclusion also follows directly from \(0\le h_{D_e}(\widetilde v_e)\le0\); since \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm, this is equivalent to \(\widetilde v_e=0\). Thus saturation holds on the entire reduced optimal face. It also gives \(\lambda(\widetilde v)=\sum_e w_e\) for every \(w\)-optimizer, so every such optimizer is load-minimal and its load vector is exactly \(w\).

It remains to make the zero-defect convention explicit. If \(\Delta_C(c)=0\), then \(0\in\mathcal F(c)\), \(\Phi(0)=0\), and hence \(0\in\operatorname{Opt}(c)\). Its load is \(\lambda(0)=0\). Because every summand of \(\lambda\) is nonnegative, any chosen load-minimal optimizer \(v^*\) has \(\lambda(v^*)=0\), and the norm property of every \(h_{D_e}\) forces \(v_e^*=0\) for all \(e\). Thus \(v^*=0\) and \(w=0\). The reduced feasible set is then \(\mathcal F(0)=\{0\}\), so \(\Delta_C(w)=0=\Delta_C(c)\), its unique optimizer saturates every coordinate as \(h_{D_e}(0)=0=w_e\), and the fixed-point conclusion remains valid. This also covers the case in which the original capacity vector itself is zero.

The argument begins with an arbitrarily chosen load-minimal \(v^*\) and proves the fixed-point property only for the resulting \(w\); it makes no comparison between the vectors arising from different choices of \(v^*\).
