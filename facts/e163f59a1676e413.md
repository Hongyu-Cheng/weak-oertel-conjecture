## Statement

Let $L=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:a_i\cdot z\le b_i,\ 1\le i\le r\}$ be a bounded full-dimensional polytope with irredundant unit-normal facets $F_i$, areas $A_i=\mathcal H_{n-1}(F_i)$ and centroids $c_i$. Let $K$ be compact and contained in $\operatorname{int}L$. Assume $\operatorname{int}L\cap\mathbb Z^n=\varnothing$ and that $L$ is a local volume maximum among bounded interior-lattice-free polyhedral containers of $K$ under small independent facet normal and offset perturbations. Then for $P_i=\operatorname{relint}(F_i)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ there exist nonnegative weights $\lambda_{ip}$ with
$$
\sum_{p\in P_i}\lambda_{ip}=A_i,\qquad
\sum_{p\in P_i}\lambda_{ip}p=A_i c_i.
$$
They satisfy
$$
\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ip}a_i=0,\qquad
\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ip}a_i p^{\mathsf T}=|L|I_n,\qquad
\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ip}a_i\cdot p=n|L|.
$$
A physical affine variation $z\mapsto z+t(Bz+q)$ has volume derivative $|L|\operatorname{tr}B=\sum_{i,p}\lambda_{ip}a_i\cdot(Bp+q)$. It is feasible for small $t>0$ if every boundary lattice point has at least one containing facet with strictly negative outward normal speed. If each has a containing facet of nonpositive outward normal speed, the first-order volume inequality is $|L|\operatorname{tr}B\le0$.

## Proof

The perturbed polytopes stay in a fixed ball: $\min_{|v|=1}\max_i a_i\cdot v>0$ by boundedness, and a fixed positive lower bound persists under small perturbations. The strict inequalities on the compact set $K$ persist. Initially exterior lattice points in the common ball form a finite set, and a violated inequality remains violated at each.

For a facet perturbation $(a_i,b_i)\mapsto(a_i+t\dot a_i,b_i+t\dot b_i)$ the signed volume derivative contributed by that facet is $\int_{F_i}(\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot z)\,d\mathcal H_{n-1}(z)$. To see this, project normally onto the old facet plane. Above a point $w$ of the plane, the moved cutoff is at signed distance $t(\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot w)/(1+t\dot a_i\cdot a_i)$. The signed length between the old and new cutoffs, restricted by the other inequalities, divided by $t$ tends to $\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot w$ on the relative interior of $F_i$ and to zero outside $F_i$. It is uniformly bounded on the projection of the common ball. The facet boundary has zero measure in the plane, and the same convergence holds if the other inequalities undergo their own small perturbations. Dominated convergence and telescoping over the facets give the total derivative $\sum_i A_i(\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot c_i)$. In dimension one the same calculation uses the two endpoint speeds and counting area one per endpoint.

In a one-facet variation only $P_i$ needs to be checked: all other boundary lattice points still lie on an unchanged facet. At $p\in P_i$ the exact feasibility condition for this variation is $\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot p\le0$. If $P_i$ were empty, a positive offset increment would be feasible and increase volume. If $c_i\notin\operatorname{conv}P_i$, strict affine separation gives an affine function $\dot b_i-\dot a_i\cdot z$ nonpositive on $P_i$ and strictly positive at $c_i$. This too yields a feasible increasing variation, contradicting local maximality. Thus $c_i\in\operatorname{conv}P_i$, and its convex coefficients multiplied by $A_i$ give the weights.

For the vector and matrix identities, integrate the constant and coordinate-linear fields by the divergence theorem on $L$. This gives $\sum_i A_i a_i=0$ and $\sum_i A_i a_i c_i^{\mathsf T}=|L|I_n$. Substitute the weights and take the trace. The determinant of the affine deformation gives its volume derivative, and the displayed stress gives the equal expression in contact speeds.

At a boundary lattice point $p$ the inverse affine deformation sends $p$ to $p-t(Bp+q)+O(t^2)$. A containing facet with $a_i\cdot(Bp+q)<0$ is therefore violated by the inverse image for all sufficiently small positive $t$. This works simultaneously for the finite set of all boundary lattice points, and the old exterior points and $K$ are controlled as above. To treat nonpositive selected speeds, replace $Bz+q$ by $Bz+q-\eta(z-z_*)$ for $z_*\in\operatorname{int}L$ and $\eta>0$. The speeds strictly decrease at every containing facet. Local maximality gives $|L|\operatorname{tr}(B-\eta I_n)\le0$, and letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives the asserted first-order inequality. Under an outward homothety the original relative-interior contacts lie in the new interior, as seen by applying the inverse map toward $z_*$.
