## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $\delta=d-1$, and let $\mu_-,\mu_+,\mu_0$ be finite positive measures supported on compact convex faces $F_-,F_+,F_0$ with common tangent space of dimension $0\le m\le\delta$ and $(F_-+F_+)/2\subset F_0$. Assume each measure is equivalent to intrinsic $\mathcal H^m$ on its face, and the three are relative to a common normal reference when they arise by disintegration. For all open balls $B(x,a),B(y,b)$ with positive endpoint masses suppose
$$
\mu_0(B((x+y)/2,(a+b)/2))\ge
\left[\frac{\mu_-(B(x,a))^{1/\delta}+\mu_+(B(y,b))^{1/\delta}}2\right]^\delta.
$$
For $m>0$ write $g_0^+$ for upper intrinsic ball density. At every pair of endpoint differentiation points of finite positive density $g_-(x),g_+(y)$, put $x_0=(x+y)/2$. If $0<m<\delta$, then $g_0^+(x_0)\ge[(g_-(x)^{1/(\delta-m)}+g_+(y)^{1/(\delta-m)})/2]^{\delta-m}$. For $m=\delta$ the lower bound is $\max(g_-(x),g_+(y))$. For $m=0$ the target atom mass is at least $[(g_-^{1/\delta}+g_+^{1/\delta})/2]^\delta$, with $g_\pm$ the endpoint atom masses.

A measure satisfying the same ball inequality with itself in all three slots has a unique finite positive continuous density representative on the relative interior of its face if $m>0$. For $m<\delta$ its $1/(\delta-m)$ power is concave there, and for $m=\delta$ it is constant there. When all three self-triples have the ball property, every relative-interior endpoint pair has a relative-interior midpoint and the bounds above apply with the finite continuous representatives there.

## Proof

For $m>0$ let $\omega_m$ be unit $m$-ball volume. Apply the ball inequality with radii $\varepsilon a,\varepsilon b$, divide by $\omega_m\varepsilon^m$ and take an upper limit as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The endpoint densities converge. This gives
$$
g_0^+(x_0)((a+b)/2)^m
\ge\left[\frac{g_-^{1/\delta}a^{m/\delta}+g_+^{1/\delta}b^{m/\delta}}2\right]^\delta .
$$
This holds for every positive choice $a,b$ at the same fixed endpoint pair. Set $a+b=2$. For $0<m<\delta$ write $U=g_-^{1/(\delta-m)}$ and $V=g_+^{1/(\delta-m)}$. Differentiating the strictly concave sum in brackets shows it is maximized at $(a,b)=(2U/(U+V),2V/(U+V))$. The value in brackets is $2^{-(\delta-m)/\delta}(U+V)^{(\delta-m)/\delta}$, giving the claimed power. If $m=\delta$, the sum is affine in $(a,b)$, and limits from positive radii to either endpoint of $a+b=2$ give the maximum bound. For $m=0$, the faces are points, so balls centered at them contain their whole atoms and the original inequality gives the stated formula.

For the self-triple write $O=\operatorname{relint}F$ and identify its affine space isometrically with $\mathbb R^m$. For each $\varepsilon>0$ set $f_\varepsilon(x)=\mu(B(x,\varepsilon))/(\omega_m\varepsilon^m)$ on this affine space and $h_\varepsilon=f_\varepsilon^{1/\delta}$ on $O$. Convolution with the normalized ball indicator is continuous by $L^1$ translation continuity of the intrinsic density. Each value on $O$ is positive by equivalence to volume. The equal-radius self-triple gives midpoint concavity of $h_\varepsilon$, and continuity gives concavity. If $B(x,2r)\subset O$, then $h_\varepsilon(y)\ge h_\varepsilon(x)/2$ for $y\in B(x,r)$ by concavity between $x$ and $2y-x$ and nonnegativity. Fubini gives $\int f_\varepsilon=\mu(F)$, so $\omega_m r^m(h_\varepsilon(x)/2)^\delta\le\mu(F)$. On compact subsets choose $r$ uniformly. The concavity bounds imply local equicontinuity: on any small ball a chord between two points can be extended a fixed positive distance in both directions inside a slightly larger ball, so its secant slope is bounded by the corresponding outer secants and the uniform bounds. Compact exhaustion and Arzelà–Ascoli yield locally uniform subsequential limits as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Lebesgue differentiation identifies every limit with the original density's $1/\delta$ power almost everywhere. Continuous limits on the open relative interior that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere, giving one limit and convergence there. That concave nonnegative limit is strictly positive on $O$, since a zero at an interior point forces its value at any other point to be zero by a chord extended past the interior point. Its $\delta$ power is the desired continuous representative, so all points in $O$ are differentiation points and their upper densities equal it.

Apply the first part to the self-triple on these points. When $m<\delta$ the sharper power is midpoint concave and continuous, hence concave. When $m=\delta$, the midpoint density is at least the maximum of the two endpoint densities. Nearby points with both reflected points still inside $O$ give both inequalities between the two densities. Thus it is locally constant, and constant on connected $O$. For a cross-triple, small relative balls at the two interior endpoint points give a relative ball at their midpoint in $F_0$ because all direction spaces agree. Therefore the target point is interior and the same bounds use its finite representative.
