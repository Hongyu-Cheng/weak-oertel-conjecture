## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer. Let \(p=(p_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be a nonnegative, finitely supported probability table. Assume it is globally extendable in the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`: there is a finite-valued function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing with \(p\) at every lattice point, including every zero, such that

\[
f((1-s)x+sx')\ge f(x)^{1-s}f(x')^s
\qquad(x,x'\in\mathbb R^n,\ 0<s<1),
\]

with zero on the right if either endpoint value is zero. Assume the complete table is invariant under each coordinate reflection \(I_i\mapsto1-I_i\).

Put \(D=\{\mathbf I:p_{\mathbf I}>0\}\). Then \(\{0,1\}^n\subseteq D\), and every cube vertex has weight \(w_{\max}=p_{\mathbf0}\). Use exactly the normalization \(w=p\), \(W=1\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D\) from `488f0a583886b5fc`. In particular, \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\).

At the original center \(\mathbf0\), use the full quantities of `280b299bc1e87c14`, with its weights here equal to \(p\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{p_v,p_{-v}\},
&\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\},\\
\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v)&=\log\frac{p_{\mathbf0}^2}{p_vp_{-v}}
\quad(v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}),
&B_{\mathbf0}^{\Delta}(L)&=
\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}\\\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v)\le L}}\alpha(v).
\end{aligned}
\]

Displacements are oriented, zero is included, and each \(\alpha(v)\) is the minimum of the two original capacities. With the notation \(K_{\mathbf0}(p)=\max_{L\ge0}B_{\mathbf0}^{\Delta}(L)/(1+L)\) from `488f0a583886b5fc`, one has

\[
K_{\mathbf0}(p)\ge\frac{1}{36\sqrt2}\,2^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]

The maximum is attained at a value in the finite set \(\{\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v):v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}\}\).

Consequently, let \(n,d\ge1\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{x:(\mathbf I,x)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf I}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf I})\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf I}>0\). Suppose its complete normalized table \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) is invariant under every stated coordinate reflection. For \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), there exists \(x^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) such that

\[
h_S((\mathbf0,x^\star))
\ge \frac{-\log(1-e^{-1})}{576\sqrt2}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{2}
\]

The point in (2) is chosen before the containing closed ambient halfspace. Thus the same point satisfies this lower bound for every such halfspace.

## Proof

First establish the lattice properties needed for the count. The log-concave extension implies the following finite convex-combination inequality: if the endpoints are in \(D\), their coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, and their barycenter \(\mathbf J\) is integral, then \(\mathbf J\in D\) and
\(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\).
Indeed, repeated application of the extension inequality gives a positive lower bound at the barycenter; agreement at every lattice point gives membership in \(D\), and taking logarithms gives the inequality. Terms with zero coefficient are omitted. This is also the convex-admissibility property of `488f0a583886b5fc` in the stated normalization.

For any \(\mathbf I\in D\), its coordinate-reflection orbit consists of the \(2^n\) points with coordinates \(\tfrac12\pm|I_i-\tfrac12|\). All have weight \(p_{\mathbf I}\). Their convex hull is the corresponding box, which contains \([0,1]^n\). The preceding convex-combination argument shows that every cube vertex belongs to \(D\) and has weight at least \(p_{\mathbf I}\). Reflection invariance makes all cube-vertex weights equal. This proves \(p_{\mathbf0}=w_{\max}>0\) and \(\phi=0\) on the cube. In particular every potential used below is nonnegative.

Retain the folded radii \(R_i(\mathbf I)=|I_i-\tfrac12|\) and integer excess vector \(\mathbf Z(\mathbf I)=\mathbf R(\mathbf I)-\tfrac12\mathbf1\). A folded excess vector \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\) has positive-sign representative \(\mathbf1+\mathbf z\). Write \(\Phi_{\mathbf z}=\phi_{\mathbf1+\mathbf z}\) whenever that representative is supported. Thus \(\Phi\) is only a reindexing of the canonical original potential. Every occurring excess vector has exactly \(2^n\) original sites of equal weight, and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\Phi_{\mathbf Z(\mathbf I)}\).

The folded weights are coordinatewise nonincreasing, including their zeros. To verify this, suppose \(\mathbf1+\mathbf z\in D\) and \(z_i\ge1\). Denote reflection in coordinate \(i\) by \(\sigma_i\). The exact lattice convex combination

\[
\mathbf1+\mathbf z-\mathbf e_i
=\left(1-\frac1{2z_i+1}\right)(\mathbf1+\mathbf z)
+\frac1{2z_i+1}\sigma_i(\mathbf1+\mathbf z)
\]

has two endpoints with the same positive weight. Therefore its weight is at least \(p_{\mathbf1+\mathbf z}\). Repeating coordinate decreases proves that \(0\le\mathbf u\le\mathbf z\) implies \(\mathbf1+\mathbf u\in D\) and \(\Phi_{\mathbf u}\le\Phi_{\mathbf z}\).

For \(t\ge0\), define the finite folded level set and its cardinality by
\(E_t=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:\mathbf1+\mathbf z\in D,\ \Phi_{\mathbf z}\le t\}\) and \(A(t)=|E_t|\).
Each \(E_t\) is a downset, meaning that it contains every nonnegative integer vector coordinatewise below any of its members. Also \(\mathbf0\in E_t\), so \(A(t)\ge1\).

The exact halving property is
\(\lfloor E_t/2\rfloor\subseteq E_{t/2}\), where floor is coordinatewise. For a supported \(\mathbf z\), let \(\boldsymbol\eta=\mathbf z-2\lfloor\mathbf z/2\rfloor\in\{0,1\}^n\). Then

\[
\mathbf1+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathbf z}{2}\right\rfloor
=\frac12(\mathbf1+\mathbf z)+\frac12(\mathbf1-\boldsymbol\eta).
\tag{3}
\]

The second endpoint is a cube vertex with potential zero. Convex-admissibility gives support at the midpoint and \(\Phi_{\lfloor\mathbf z/2\rfloor}\le\Phi_{\mathbf z}/2\), proving the property also for all odd-coordinate and zero-coordinate cases.

We next prove a Boolean counting inequality. For downsets \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq\{0,1\}^m\), let
\(Q_m(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)=|\{(a,b)\in\mathcal A\times\mathcal B:a_i b_i=0\text{ for every }i\}|\).
Then

\[
Q_m(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\ge(|\mathcal A|\,|\mathcal B|)^{3/4},
\qquad
Q_m(\mathcal A,\mathcal A)\ge|\mathcal A|^{3/2}.
\tag{4}
\]

The first inequality is proved by induction on \(m\); the second follows by taking the sets equal. Empty sets give zero on both sides, and \(m=0\) is immediate. For the induction step assume both sets are nonempty. Their last-coordinate sections satisfy \(\mathcal A_1\subseteq\mathcal A_0\) and \(\mathcal B_1\subseteq\mathcal B_0\), with all sections downsets in dimension \(m-1\). Put \(a_j=|\mathcal A_j|\) and \(b_j=|\mathcal B_j|\). Nonemptiness and the downset property give \(a_0,b_0>0\). The three possible disjoint last-coordinate choices give the exact recursion

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
&=Q_{m-1}(\mathcal A_0,\mathcal B_0)
+Q_{m-1}(\mathcal A_1,\mathcal B_0)
+Q_{m-1}(\mathcal A_0,\mathcal B_1)\\
&\ge(a_0b_0)^{3/4}
\left[1+(a_1/a_0)^{3/4}+(b_1/b_0)^{3/4}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

For completeness, the scalar inequality needed here is

\[
1+u^{3/4}+v^{3/4}\ge((1+u)(1+v))^{3/4}
\qquad(0\le u,v\le1).
\tag{5}
\]

For fixed \(u\), the derivative on \(0<v<1\) of
\((1+u^{3/4}+v^{3/4})/(1+v)^{3/4}\)
has the sign of \(v^{-1/4}-1-u^{3/4}\). This sign is decreasing, so the minimum of the continuous ratio on \([0,1]\) occurs at an endpoint. At \(v=0\), the required inequality is \(1+u^{3/4}\ge(1+u)^{3/4}\); its left side minus right side is zero at \(u=0\) and has nonnegative derivative for \(u>0\). At \(v=1\), the required inequality is
\((2+u^{3/4})/(1+u)^{3/4}\ge2^{3/4}\).
The derivative of this ratio has the sign of \(u^{-1/4}-2\), so its minimum also occurs at an endpoint. The endpoint values are \(2\) and \(3/2^{3/4}\), both at least \(2^{3/4}\), since \(3\ge2^{3/2}\). Continuity includes \(u=0\) and \(v=0\). This proves (5). Applying it with \(u=a_1/a_0\) and \(v=b_1/b_0\), which lie in \([0,1]\), completes the induction in (4).

Now count actual reflected pairs at a common endpoint-potential level. Define the full oriented count

\[
N(t)=\left|\{v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}:\phi_v\le t,\ \phi_{-v}\le t\}\right|.
\]

At this center \(\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v)=\phi_v+\phi_{-v}\). Every vector counted by \(N(t)\) therefore has \(\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v)\le2t\) and its original capacity satisfies \(\alpha(v)\ge p_{\mathbf0}e^{-t}\). Hence

\[
B_{\mathbf0}^{\Delta}(2t)\ge p_{\mathbf0}e^{-t}N(t).
\tag{6}
\]

Partition the actual folded level set into binary blocks. For \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), put
\(\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y}=\{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n:2\mathbf y+\boldsymbol\epsilon\in E_t\}\),
and put \(\mathcal Y_t=\{\mathbf y:\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y}\ne\varnothing\}\).
Every nonempty block is a Boolean downset. These blocks partition \(E_t\), and (3) gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf y\in\mathcal Y_t}|\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y}|=A(t),
\qquad
1\le|\mathcal Y_t|\le A(t/2).
\tag{7}
\]

For each \(\mathbf y\in\mathcal Y_t\) and each ordered pair
\((\boldsymbol\epsilon,\boldsymbol\eta)\in\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y}^2\)
with \(\epsilon_i\eta_i=0\) on every coordinate, construct one original displacement by

\[
v_i=
\begin{cases}
-(2y_i+1),& (\epsilon_i,\eta_i)=(1,0),\\
 2y_i+1,& (\epsilon_i,\eta_i)=(0,1),\\
 2y_i,& (\epsilon_i,\eta_i)=(0,0).
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

For an integer coordinate, its folded excess is
\(Z_i(v)=\max\{-v_i,v_i-1\}\). Directly in the three cases of (8),

\[
\mathbf Z(v)\le2\mathbf y+\boldsymbol\epsilon,
\qquad
\mathbf Z(-v)\le2\mathbf y+\boldsymbol\eta
\tag{9}
\]

coordinatewise. In the third case with \(y_i>0\), the two excesses are \(2y_i-1\) and \(2y_i\); with \(y_i=0\), both are zero. The first two cases give equality with the two corresponding source coordinates. Since \(E_t\) is a downset, (9) proves that both actual endpoints belong to \(D\) and have original potential at most \(t\). Thus every constructed displacement is counted by \(N(t)\).

The construction is injective across all blocks and ordered pairs. From any image vector recover \(y_i=\lfloor|v_i|/2\rfloor\). A negative odd coordinate gives \((\epsilon_i,\eta_i)=(1,0)\), a positive odd coordinate gives \((0,1)\), and a nonnegative even coordinate gives \((0,0)\). These are all the coordinate types in the image, including zero. Therefore no two distinct block-pair inputs have the same original displacement. This count retains positive and negative coordinates in the same vector. The block \(\mathbf y=\mathbf0\), with both Boolean vectors zero, also contributes the actual displacement zero.

Using this injection, (4), finite Hölder, and (7), one obtains

\[
\begin{aligned}
N(t)
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf y\in\mathcal Y_t}
Q_n(\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y},\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y})\\
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf y\in\mathcal Y_t}|\mathcal A_{t,\mathbf y}|^{3/2}\\
&\ge\frac{A(t)^{3/2}}{|\mathcal Y_t|^{1/2}}
\ge\frac{A(t)^{3/2}}{A(t/2)^{1/2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The finite Hölder step follows from
\(\sum_j a_j\le(\sum_j a_j^{3/2})^{2/3}|\mathcal Y_t|^{1/3}\).
All denominators in (7) and (10) are positive. Equation (10) is a lower bound for the full original count \(N(t)\); equation (6) uses the original endpoint minima for every vector in that full count.

It remains to select a cutoff. The set \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf0}\) is finite and contains zero. Its curvatures are finite and nonnegative, and the curvature of zero is zero. For any \(L\ge0\), replace \(L\) by the largest curvature value not exceeding it. The cutoff sum is unchanged and the denominator does not increase. Thus \(K_{\mathbf0}(p)\) is a finite attained maximum, with the asserted finite list of possible maximizing cutoffs.

Let

\[
T=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}A(t)\,dt
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\\\mathbf1+\mathbf z\in D}}
e^{-\Phi_{\mathbf z}}
=\frac{1}{2^n p_{\mathbf0}}.
\tag{11}
\]

The first equality with the sum follows by integrating the indicator of \(\Phi_{\mathbf z}\le t\) for each member of the finite support. The last equality uses all \(2^n\) sites in every reflection orbit and total original mass one. In particular \(0<T<\infty\).

Combining (6) and (10) with
\(B_{\mathbf0}^{\Delta}(2t)\le K_{\mathbf0}(p)(1+2t)\),
and raising the resulting inequality to the power \(2/3\), gives

\[
e^{-t}A(t)
\le\left(\frac{K_{\mathbf0}(p)}{p_{\mathbf0}}\right)^{2/3}
(1+2t)^{2/3}e^{-t/6}
\left(e^{-t/2}A(t/2)\right)^{1/3}.
\tag{12}
\]

Apply integral Hölder to (12), with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\). The two required integrals are exactly

\[
\int_0^\infty(1+2t)e^{-t/4}\,dt=36,
\qquad
\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}A(t/2)\,dt=2T.
\]

They are finite, so Hölder applies without a limiting or integrability assumption. It yields

\[
T\le
\left(\frac{K_{\mathbf0}(p)}{p_{\mathbf0}}\right)^{2/3}
36^{2/3}(2T)^{1/3},
\qquad
T\le36\sqrt2\,\frac{K_{\mathbf0}(p)}{p_{\mathbf0}}.
\tag{13}
\]

For the second inequality, cube the first, divide by the positive \(T\), and take the nonnegative square root. Substitution of (11) cancels \(p_{\mathbf0}>0\) and proves (1). All level conditions and cutoff conditions throughout the argument are weak inequalities, so atoms at a level or at a maximizing cutoff are included. The proof also applies when the folded support consists only of zero or when some coordinates are unexpanded; the associated blocks and downsets then simply have deterministic zero coordinates.

Finally consider the actual convex body in the statement. Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives global extendability of its complete positive-total-mass fiber-volume table; division by \(W\) preserves that property and every table zero. Thus the already proved reflection-class result applies to \(p=w/W\). In particular \(w_{\mathbf0}>0\). Returning to the actual weights multiplies each \(\alpha(v)\) and every cutoff sum by \(W\), and leaves every \(\Delta_{\mathbf0}(v)\) unchanged. Therefore

\[
K_{\mathbf0}(w)=W K_{\mathbf0}(p)
\ge\frac{1}{36\sqrt2}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

Use accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` at this fixed center \(j=\mathbf0\), with the actual fibers, actual endpoint capacities, and a maximizing cutoff. Its cutoff conclusion at a fixed center supplies one \(x^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) with
\(h_S((\mathbf0,x^\star))\ge[-\log(1-e^{-1})/16]K_{\mathbf0}(w)\).
This is the pointwise fixed-fiber conclusion proved there, with the point selected before every containing halfspace. Substitution gives (2). Its coefficient is an absolute positive number less than \(1/e\). This completes the conditional actual-body consequence while retaining the coordinate-reflection hypothesis.
