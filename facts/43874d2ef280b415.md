## Statement

Let $e,f,m$ be continuous real functions on a compact interval $I$, with $e>0$ and at least one probability law satisfying $\mathbb E m=0$. Every such law is a probability mixture of singletons at zeros of $m$ and centered two-point laws on pairs $(x,y)$ with $m(x)>0>m(y)$. Its ratio $\mathbb E f/\mathbb E e$ is an average of the component ratios with weights proportional to each component's expectation of $e$. Both extreme ratios over the centered laws are attained by laws on at most two points.

## Proof

Restrict a centered law $\mu$ to the sets $m>0$, $m<0$ and $m=0$, writing the restrictions $\mu_+,\mu_-,\mu_0$. Put $J=\int m\,d\mu_+=\int(-m)\,d\mu_-$. If $J=0$, $\mu=\mu_0$ and the singleton decomposition suffices. Otherwise let $d\nu_+=m\,d\mu_+/J$ and $d\nu_-=(-m)\,d\mu_-/J$. For $m(x)>0>m(y)$, the centered pair law $\pi_{xy}$ gives masses $-m(y)/(m(x)-m(y))$ and $m(x)/(m(x)-m(y))$ to $x,y$ respectively.

Mix the pairs against $J[1/m(x)+1/(-m(y))]\,d\nu_+(x)d\nu_-(y)$. Multiplication by the $x$-weight gives $J/m(x)$, and multiplication by the $y$-weight gives $J/(-m(y))$. Tonelli's theorem yields exactly $\mu_++\mu_-$ as the resulting measure. The total pair mixing mass is $\mu_+(I)+\mu_-(I)$, so adding the singleton mixture $\mu_0$ gives a probability decomposition. Since $e$ is bounded below by a positive constant, dividing the corresponding $f$ and $e$ averages gives the asserted positive reweighting.

The centered laws form a nonempty weakly compact set, as the probability laws on a compact metric space are weakly compact and $m$ is continuous. The objective is continuous because the denominator is bounded away from zero. At a maximizing law the component ratios are all bounded above by that attained maximum, so equality of the weighted average forces equality for almost every component. At least one component attains it. The same argument gives a minimizer of support size at most two.
