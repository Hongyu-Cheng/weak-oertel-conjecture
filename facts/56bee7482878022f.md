## Statement

Let $R\ge1$, $A=[-1/2,1/2]^2$, $B_R=[-R/2,R/2]\times[-1/(2R),1/(2R)]$, and $C_R=\operatorname{conv}((\{-1\}\times A)\cup(\{1\}\times B_R))$ in coordinates $(z,s,t)$. Its exact integer fibers at $-1,0,1$ are $A,M_R=(A+B_R)/2,B_R$. The middle area is $m_R=(R+2+1/R)/4$. In raw summed fiber area, the minimum over all $\alpha\in\mathbb R$ of the closed cap $s+\alpha z\ge0$ is
$$J_R=\frac R8+\frac34+\frac5{8R}
=\frac{3m_R}{2}-\sqrt{m_R(m_R-1)}>\frac{m_R+1}{3}.$$
It is attained at $\alpha=1/2$, minimized as a function of $R$ at $\sqrt5$ with value $(3+\sqrt5)/4$, and is less than $4/3$ exactly when $5/3<R<3$. The origin maximizes depth on the middle fiber and all extreme-fiber points have depth at most $1/2$. Hence every mixed point has raw depth at most $J_R$. At $R=2$, the total mixed area is $25/8$ and the cap at $\alpha=1/2$ has raw area $21/16$, including a nonempty null supporting edge at $z=-1$. This cap also realizes $J_{1/2}(f,g)=21/16$ for the centered, unit-mass, single-piece concave profiles $f=1$ on $[-1/2,1/2]$ and $g=1/2$ on $[-1,1]$, where $J_\alpha$ adds the positive axial midpoint area to the two upper endpoint tails at $\alpha,-\alpha$.

## Proof

At $z=0$ every hull representation has equal total weights at $-1$ and $1$, giving precisely the Minkowski average; the extreme levels force their own rectangles. The width and height of $M_R$ are $(1+R)/2$ and $(1+1/R)/2$. Its contribution to the cap is $m_R/2$. On $[-1/2,1/2]$ the endpoint contributions sum to $1-\alpha(1-1/R)$. For $\alpha\ge1/2$ the first cap has area zero and the second is nondecreasing in $\alpha$. For $\alpha\le-1/2$ the first contributes one. Therefore the least endpoint sum is $(1+1/R)/2$. Adding $m_R/2$ gives the first formula. The difference from $4/3$ is $(3R-5)(R-3)/(24R)$ and the derivative is $(R^2-5)/(8R^2)$. Solving $R+1/R=4m_R-2$ with $R\ge1$ gives $R=2m_R-1+2\sqrt{m_R(m_R-1)}$ and hence the area formula. Its strict comparison to $(m_R+1)/3$ follows from $7m_R-2>0$ and $(7m_R-2)^2-36m_R(m_R-1)=13m_R^2+8m_R+4>0$.

Depth is even on the middle fiber because $(z,s,t)\mapsto(z,-s,-t)$ preserves all three fibers and their measures. Depth is quasiconcave along any segment of feasible points since every halfspace containing a segment point contains at least one of its endpoints. In particular the midpoint of a middle point and its reflection has depth at least that point's depth. For a point at an extreme level $z_0\in\{-1,1\}$, choose the smaller of the two complementary closed ordinary halfplanes through the point in its own rectangle. Its area is at most $1/2$. If this halfplane is $u\cdot(x-x_0)\ge0$, extend it by the term $N z_0(z-z_0)$ on the left. The term vanishes on the own fiber and tends uniformly to minus infinity on the two other compact fibers as $N\to+\infty$. Thus one finite choice isolates the prescribed cap, establishing the extreme bound. Since $J_R>1/2$, the origin's axial cap upper-bounds every center.

For $R=2$, the left trace at $\alpha=1/2$ is $s=1/2$ in $A$, the right area is $3/4$, and the middle contribution is $9/16$. The transverse sections of the rectangles are exactly the profiles stated, with zero axial means. Their centered section bodies are precisely $A,B_2$ and their supremal midpoint profile is the section profile of $M_2$. Hence the profile calculation and the cap calculation both give $21/16=4/3-1/48$.
