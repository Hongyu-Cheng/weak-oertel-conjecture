## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers. Let \(0<\rho\le1\) and \(\beta\ge0\) be real. For every \(i\in[k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), let \(B_i,C_{4,i}\) be integers satisfying \(b\le B_i\le2b\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\). Let \(\Phi:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be nondecreasing with \(\Phi(0)=0\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
r(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k\max(x_i-1,0),&
\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i)+\Phi(r(\mathbf x)),\\
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^{T}e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}&
w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf x)&=\rho^{\sum_i x_i}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},\\
Q(\mathbf x)&=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,&
p_\rho&=\frac{\rho}{1+\rho},\\
Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf x),&
Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf x),\\
Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Every spatial vector has multiplicity one; its displayed weight is its original spatial weight times its complete original height tail. Every argument \(E\) of \(f_E\) is a nonnegative integer, and both height endpoints are included. Superscripts such as \(Q\le H\) or \(Q\ge H\) restrict the same original sum.

The objects \(k,b,T,\beta,B_i,C_{4,i},g_i,r,\Gamma_\Phi,f_E,Q\) have exactly the meanings in accepted fact `330a02ac07b88af9`. At \(\rho=1\), the three normalizers in (1) become respectively its \(Z_{4,\Phi},Z_{3,\Phi},Z_{\rm bin}\). At \(\Phi=0,\rho=1\), the full and binary normalizers also become \(Z_4,Z_{\rm bin}\) of accepted fact `deb724bb2b6dd465`. Thus the notation \(Z_{4,\rho}/Z_{3,\rho}\), when the penalty is suppressed, means exactly \(Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}/Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}\). The parameter \(L_{\rm cap}\) below is the cap parameter called \(L\) in the latter accepted fact. The locked objects \(n,d,C,S,c_0\) retain their locked meanings.

For every integer \(H\) with \(1\le H\le k/128\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_H^{\rm sp}&=\min\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac12\sqrt{\frac{k-H}{2H}}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace,\\
D_H^{\rm sp}&=\frac{100H^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}+2H\left(\frac23\right)^{h_H^{\rm sp}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Then \(h_H^{\rm sp}\ge1\), and the cap-free finite comparison is
$$
Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\le H}\le \exp(D_H^{\rm sp})Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}.
\tag{3}
$$
For every fixed \(a>0,A>0,s\ge0\), uniformly over the models in (1) with \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\) and integers \(1\le H\le A(1+\log k)^s\), the right-side factor in (3) is \(\exp(o(1))\) as \(k\to\infty\). In particular it is at most \(e\) for all sufficiently large \(k\) depending only on \(a,A,s\). This assertion is uniform in \(\rho,T,\beta,\Phi\), and the allowed individual costs.

Suppose additionally that \(a>0,L_{\rm cap}\ge0\), \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\), and \(T\le kp_\rho+L_{\rm cap}b\). Every integer
\(H\ge\max\lbrace1,4L_{\rm cap},(4/a^2)\log(2k)\rbrace\) satisfies
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\ge H}}{Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}}
\le\frac{(k+1)^2\exp(-a^2H^2/4)}{1-\exp(-a^2H/4)}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular, set
$$
K_a=4(1+a^{-2}),\qquad
H_0=\left\lceil K_a\bigl[L_{\rm cap}+\log(k+1)+1\bigr]\right\rceil.
\tag{5}
$$
Then \(Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\ge H_0}\le Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}/2\). If \(D\ge0\) and \(L_{\rm cap}\le D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\), then
$$
H_0\le K_{a,D}[1+\log^2(k+1)],\qquad K_{a,D}=K_a(D+2)+1.
\tag{6}
$$
Consequently, for every fixed \(a>0,D\ge0\) there is \(k_0(a,D)\) such that every model (1) with \(k\ge k_0(a,D)\), \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\), and
\(T\le k\rho/(1+\rho)+Db[1+\log^2(k+1)]\) satisfies
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}}{Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}}\le e+\frac12.
\tag{7}
$$
All logarithms are natural. These conclusions include \(\beta=0\), \(T=0\), arbitrary nondecreasing integer penalties, and every \(0<\rho\le1\), with no lower bound on \(\rho\) depending on \(k\).

## Proof

The zero vector at height zero contributes one, so \(Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}\ge Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}>0\). If \(E'\le E\) are nonnegative integers, then \(f_E\le f_{E'}\), including empty tails. In particular any energy-decreasing edge \(\mathbf x\to\mathbf y\) satisfies
$$
w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf x)
\le \rho^{\sum_i x_i-\sum_i y_i}w_{\Phi,\rho}(\mathbf y).
\tag{8}
$$
Multiplying (8) by any nonnegative edge kernel and summing is valid for the original masses. We extend the coupled exchange and conditioning framework of `330a02ac07b88af9`, tracking the new spatial factors explicitly.

First work on \(\nu\) coordinates with the same cost window, a nonnegative integer cap, and any nonnegative nondecreasing integer penalty \(\Psi\) with \(\Psi(0)=0\) on the local excess. All masses in this local argument have weight \(\rho^{\sum_i x_i}\) times their original height tail. For integers \(1\le u\le\nu/16\), let \(V_{1,\Psi,\rho}(u)\) be the mass with exactly one three and occupation at most \(u\), and \(V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\) the all-ternary mass with occupation at most \(u+1\). We claim
$$
V_{1,\Psi,\rho}(u)
\le\left[\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}
+u\left(\frac23\right)^{\widehat h(\nu,u)}\right]V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1),\qquad
\widehat h(\nu,u)=\min\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac12\sqrt{\frac\nu u}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace.
\tag{9}
$$

If \(\rho<4\sqrt{u/\nu}\), clip the unique three to two. The additive energy decreases, the excess decreases by one, and the label sum decreases by one. Each source has one target; each target has at most \(u\) inverse sources, one for each of its two-positions. Equation (8) therefore gives
\(V_{1,\Psi,\rho}(u)\le\rho u V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\le4u^{3/2}\nu^{-1/2}V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\). This proves (9) in this entire regime.

For the rest of the local proof assume \(\rho\ge4\sqrt{u/\nu}\). Order the coordinates by nondecreasing \(B_i\), breaking ties in a fixed way. Put \(m=\lceil\sqrt{\nu u}/\rho\rceil\), and let \(I_{\rm low}\) be the first \(m\) coordinates, with complement \(I_{\rm high}\). The hypotheses give
$$
\frac{\sqrt{\nu u}}\rho\le m\le\frac54\frac{\sqrt{\nu u}}\rho,
\qquad m\ge4u,\qquad m-u\ge\frac34m,\qquad m<\nu.
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed \(\sqrt{\nu u}/\rho\ge\sqrt{\nu u}\ge4\), while \(\rho\ge4\sqrt{u/\nu}\) gives \(\sqrt{\nu u}/\rho\le\nu/4\). The ceiling is at most \(\nu/4+1\le5\nu/16<\nu\).

For a source let \(i\) be the unique three-position, \(q\le u\) its occupation, and \(v\) its one-count. First take \(v\ge\rho\nu/4\) and \(i\in I_{\rm low}\). For each source one-position \(j\), change the three at \(i\) to zero and the one at \(j\) to two. The additive energy change is
\(B_j-1-C_{4,i}\le2b-1-(2B_i-1)\le0\). The excess drops by one. The label sum drops by two, so (8) has factor \(\rho^2\). There are \(v\ge\rho\nu/4\) distinct targets per source, all of occupation \(q\), and at most \(mu\) inverse sources per target: choose a zero in \(I_{\rm low}\) and a two. Summing the edges bounds this source mass by
\((4\rho mu/\nu)V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\).

Next take \(v\ge\rho\nu/4\) and \(i\in I_{\rm high}\). For every source one-position \(j\in I_{\rm low}\), change the three at \(i\) to two and the one at \(j\) to two. The additive energy change is at most
\((B_j-1)-(C_{4,i}-B_i)\le B_j-B_i\le0\). The excess and label sum both stay fixed. Targets have occupation \(q+1\le u+1\), and at most \((u+1)^2\le4u^2\) inverse sources each.

The needed outdegree bound here is an average with the actual source masses. Fix \(i\), the source two-position set \(I_2\), and \(v\ge\rho\nu/4\). All \(v\)-subsets of the \(\nu-q\) available coordinates are possible one-position sets. Throughout this class, the energy is
\(C_{4,i}+\sum_{j\in I_2}B_j+v+\Psi(|I_2|+2)\), and the spatial factor is \(\rho^{3+2|I_2|+v}\). Hence the original source masses, including zero masses, are equal throughout the class. Their average number of ones in \(I_{\rm low}\) is
$$
\frac{v\bigl(m-|I_2\cap I_{\rm low}|\bigr)}{\nu-q}
\ge\frac{\rho(m-u)}4.
\tag{11}
$$
The restrictions on occupation and one-count hold on every member of the class. Thus (11) also gives the source-mass-weighted average over all the present sources. Equation (8), whose spatial factor in this case is one, bounds their mass by \(16u^2/[\rho(m-u)]\) times \(V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\). Combining the two high-one cases and (10), their total coefficient is at most
$$
\frac{4\rho mu}{\nu}+\frac{16u^2}{\rho(m-u)}
\le\left(5+\frac{64}{3}\right)\frac{u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}
\le\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}.
\tag{12}
$$

Finally take all sources with \(v<\rho\nu/4\), irrespective of their three-position. Use the common integer
\(h=\min\lbrace b-1,\lfloor\rho\nu/(8u)\rfloor\rbrace\). Since \(\rho\ge4\sqrt{u/\nu}\),
\(\rho\nu/(8u)\ge\tfrac12\sqrt{\nu/u}\ge2\), so \(h\ge1\) and \(h\ge\widehat h(\nu,u)\). Also \(h\le b-1\) and \(h\le\rho\nu/8\). The source zero-count \(z=\nu-v-q\) obeys
$$
z>\frac{11\nu}{16},\qquad
z-h>\frac{9\nu}{16},\qquad
v+h<\frac{3\rho\nu}{8}.
\tag{13}
$$
Here use \(q\le\nu/16\), \(v<\rho\nu/4\), \(h\le\rho\nu/8\), and \(\rho\le1\). In particular there are at least \(h\) zeros.

Clip the unique three to two and promote any \(h\) source zeros to ones. The additive energy change is at most
\(h-(C_{4,i}-B_i)\le(b-1)-(B_i-1)\le0\), and the excess drops by one. Occupation stays fixed. The source label sum minus target label sum is \(1-h\), so the spatial factor in (8) is \(\rho^{1-h}\). Assign each of the \(\binom zh\) targets the kernel entry \(1/\binom zh\), giving row sums one. A target determines the source values \(v,z\) from its own one-count and zero-count, since \(h\) is common. Its inverse sources are specified by a two to restore to three and \(h\) ones to restore to zero. Thus the column load, after including the spatial factor, is at most
$$
\rho^{1-h}Q(\mathbf y)\frac{\binom{v+h}{h}}{\binom zh}
\le\rho^{1-h}u\left(\frac{v+h}{z-h+1}\right)^h
\le\rho u\left(\frac23\right)^h
\le u\left(\frac23\right)^{\widehat h(\nu,u)}.
\tag{14}
$$
The product formula for the binomial ratio and (13) prove the inequalities. This controls the entire low-one mass by the last coefficient times \(V_{0,\Psi,\rho}(u+1)\). The three source cases are disjoint and exhaustive; (12) and (14) prove (9).

We now retain the increased occupation cutoffs during conditioning and iteration. Let \(Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le J}^{(\ell)}\) be the original mass with exactly \(\ell\) threes and occupation at most \(J\). Fix \(1\le\ell\le H\) and \(J\ge\ell\) with \(u=J-\ell+1\le2H\). Fix a set \(F\) of \(\ell-1\) coordinates at three and define
$$
r_F=2|F|,\qquad E_F=\sum_{i\in F}C_{4,i}+\Phi(r_F),\qquad
T_F=T-E_F,\qquad \Phi_F(s)=\Phi(r_F+s)-\Phi(r_F).
\tag{15}
$$
These have the same meanings as the coupled conditioning quantities in `330a02ac07b88af9`. The residual penalty \(\Phi_F\) is nonnegative, nondecreasing, integer-valued, and zero at zero. There are \(\nu=k-\ell+1\) residual coordinates. For their vector \(\mathbf x'\), let \(E'\) be its energy with penalty \(\Phi_F\). The exact decomposition of the original weight is
$$
\rho^{3|F|+\sum_i x'_i}f_{E_F+E'}
=\rho^{3|F|}e^{-\beta E_F}\rho^{\sum_i x'_i}
\sum_{t'=E'}^{T_F}e^{-\beta t'}
\tag{16}
$$
when \(T_F\ge0\), with empty sums zero. It follows by translating every integer height by \(E_F\). If \(T_F<0\), every conditioned mass is zero.

Let \(U_1(F,J)\) be the original mass with these fixed threes, exactly one residual three, and total occupation at most \(J\). Let \(U_0(F,J+1)\) be the original mass with exactly the threes in \(F\), all residual coordinates ternary, and total occupation at most \(J+1\). Equation (16) leaves a common positive factor \(\rho^{3|F|}e^{-\beta E_F}\), and the residual source and target cutoffs are \(u,u+1\). Since \(H\le k/128\),
\(u/\nu\le2H/(k-H)\le2/127<1/16\), so (9) applies. Moreover
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat h(\nu,u)&\ge h_H^{\rm sp},\\
\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}+u\left(\frac23\right)^{\widehat h(\nu,u)}
&\le\frac{32(2H)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k-H}}+2H\left(\frac23\right)^{h_H^{\rm sp}}
\le D_H^{\rm sp},
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
using \(32\cdot2^{3/2}\sqrt{128/127}<100\). Also
\(\tfrac12\sqrt{(k-H)/(2H)}\ge\tfrac12\sqrt{127/2}>3\), so \(h_H^{\rm sp}\ge1\). We obtain \(U_1(F,J)\le D_H^{\rm sp}U_0(F,J+1)\), also when both vanish.

Summing over the sets \(F\) of size \(\ell-1\) gives the exact identities
$$
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_1(F,J)=\ell Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le J}^{(\ell)},\qquad
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_0(F,J+1)=Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le J+1}^{(\ell-1)}.
\tag{18}
$$
A source is counted once for each omitted three-position, while a target is counted only for its full set of three-positions. Each occurrence has its identical original weight. The factors in (16) may vary with \(F\); this does not change either identity. Thus
\(\ell Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le J}^{(\ell)}\le D_H^{\rm sp}Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le J+1}^{(\ell-1)}\).

For each fixed \(1\le\ell\le H\), iterate this recurrence with current third-count \(j=\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,1\) and current cutoff \(J_j=H+\ell-j\). Then \(J_j\ge j\) and \(1\le J_j-j+1\le2H-1\). Consequently
$$
Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le H}^{(\ell)}
\le\frac{(D_H^{\rm sp})^\ell}{\ell!}Z_{\Phi,\rho,\le H+\ell}^{(0)}
\le\frac{(D_H^{\rm sp})^\ell}{\ell!}Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}.
\tag{19}
$$
The all-ternary cutoff may reach \(2H\). Summing (19) over \(0\le\ell\le H\), with the zero-third term bounded by \(Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}\), proves (3).

For its uniform consequence, let \(M_k=A(1+\log k)^s\). For all sufficiently large \(k\), all eligible \(H\) satisfy \(H\le M_k\le k/128\), and \(b-1\ge(a/2)\sqrt{k}\). Put \(c_a=\min\lbrace a/2,1/8\rbrace>0\). The quantity
\(y=\tfrac12\sqrt{(k-H)/(2H)}\) is at least two and at least \(\tfrac14\sqrt{k/H}\), since \(k-H\ge k/2\). Thus \(\lfloor y\rfloor\ge y/2\ge\tfrac18\sqrt{k/H}\), and
\(h_H^{\rm sp}\ge c_a\sqrt{k/H}\). It follows that
$$
D_H^{\rm sp}\le\frac{100M_k^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}
+2M_k\left(\frac23\right)^{c_a\sqrt{k/M_k}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
$$
This proves the uniform assertion, including uniformity as \(\rho\) tends to zero. Increasing the threshold in \(k\) to make (20) at most one gives the factor \(e\).

It remains to prove the weighted original high-occupation tail. We extend the exact binary-height argument of `deb724bb2b6dd465`, proving its weighted versions rather than inserting weighted objects into the unweighted estimates. For an integer \(m\ge0\) and any integer \(U\), define
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\rho,\beta)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}
\rho^{\sum_i\epsilon_i}\sum_{t=\sum_i\epsilon_i}^{U}e^{-\beta t},&U\ge0,\\
0,&U<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{21}
$$
For \(m=0\) use the single empty binary vector. At \(\rho=1\) this is exactly the auxiliary \(Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\beta)\) of the latter accepted fact. In the current model, \(Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}=Z_{\rm bin}(k,T;\rho,\beta)\), because \(r=0\) and \(\Phi(0)=0\) on binary vectors. Define \(a_{m,j}=\binom mj\rho^j\) for \(0\le j\le m\), and define \(A_{m,\rho}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,t)}a_{m,j}\) for \(t\ge0\), with \(A_{m,\rho}(t)=0\) for \(t<0\). Exact counting at each height gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\rho,\beta)&=\sum_{t=0}^{U}e^{-\beta t}A_{m,\rho}(t),\\
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\rho,0)&=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,U)}a_{m,j}(U-j+1)
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
for \(U\ge0\). The factor \(U-j+1\) retains all original heights.

For every integers \(m,\delta,T\ge0\), one has the weighted height-tilt comparison
$$
\frac{e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,\beta)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,\beta)}
\le\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,0)}.
\tag{23}
$$
To prove it, the ratios \(a_{m,j+1}/a_{m,j}=\rho(m-j)/(j+1)\) decrease with \(j\). For \(1\le t<m\) this gives
\(a_{m,t+1}A_{m,\rho}(t-1)\le a_{m,t}\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}a_{m,j+1}\le a_{m,t}A_{m,\rho}(t)\), proving log-concavity of \(A_{m,\rho}\). At zero the preceding value is zero, at \(m\) the next value is unchanged, and beyond \(m\) the sequence is constant, so log-concavity holds at every nonnegative index. For \(m=0\) it is the constant one sequence there. Hence \(A_{m,\rho}(t-1)/A_{m,\rho}(t)\) is nondecreasing. For fixed \(\delta\), its consecutive-product expression shows that
\(R_\delta(t)=A_{m,\rho}(t-\delta)/A_{m,\rho}(t)\) is nondecreasing as well, including its initial zero values; for \(\delta=0\) it is one.

On \(0\le t\le T\), put \(w_t=A_{m,\rho}(t)>0\) and \(\alpha_t=e^{-\beta t}\). The sequence \(\alpha_t\) is nonincreasing. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\sum_t w_t\alpha_tR_\delta(t)\right)\left(\sum_t w_t\right)
-\left(\sum_t w_tR_\delta(t)\right)\left(\sum_t w_t\alpha_t\right)\\
&\qquad=\frac12\sum_{s,t}w_sw_t(\alpha_s-\alpha_t)(R_\delta(s)-R_\delta(t))\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
All sums here are over \(0,\ldots,T\). The numerator on the left side of (23) is exactly \(\sum_{t=0}^Te^{-\beta t}A_{m,\rho}(t-\delta)\), by the change of height index. Dividing (24) by positive denominators proves (23), including \(T<\delta\).

We need two bounds at \(\beta=0\). Let \(m\ge1\) and \(\delta\ge1\) be integers. If \(0\le T\le mp_\rho\), then
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,0)}
\le\exp(-\delta^2/m).
\tag{25}
$$
If \(T<\delta\), the numerator is zero. Otherwise reindex (22) as
\(Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,0)=\sum_{h=\delta}^{T}a_{m,h-\delta}(T-h+1)\). For \(\delta\le h\le T\le mp_\rho\le m/2\), the exact coefficient ratio is
$$
\frac{a_{m,h-\delta}}{a_{m,h}}=
\prod_{j=0}^{\delta-1}\frac{\rho^{-1}(h-j)}{m-h+j+1}.
\tag{26}
$$
Here \(\rho^{-1}h/(m-h)\le1\). Each factor in (26) is therefore at most
\((1-j/h)/(1+(j+1)/(m-h))\). Using \(\log(1-x)\le-x\) for \(0\le x<1\) and \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\) for \(x\ge0\), its logarithm is at most
\(-j/h-(j+1)/(m-h+j+1)\le-(2j+1)/m\). The last step uses \(h\le m\) and \(j\le h-1\), so \(m-h+j+1\le m\). Summing over \(j\) gives \(-\delta^2/m\). Multiplication by the nonnegative factors \(T-h+1\) and summation prove (25).

The second bound is: if \(T>mp_\rho\) and \(U=T-\delta\le mp_\rho-\delta/2\), then
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\rho,0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,0)}
\le(m+1)^2\exp(-\delta^2/(2m)).
\tag{27}
$$
Again \(U<0\) is immediate. For \(U\ge0\), put \(p=p_\rho\). The coefficient \(a_{m,j_*}\) with \(j_*=\lfloor(m+1)p\rfloor\) is a mode: the coefficient ratio is at least one precisely when \(j+1\le(m+1)p\). Also \(j_*\le\lceil mp\rceil\le T\). To check the first inequality, write \(mp\) as its integer part plus its fractional part; adding \(p<1\) increases the integer part by at most one, and by zero if the fractional part is zero. Since \(\sum_{j=0}^ma_{m,j}=(1+\rho)^m\), the mode is at least \((1+\rho)^m/(m+1)\). Its original height factor is at least one, whence
\(Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,0)\ge(1+\rho)^m/(m+1)\).

For completeness the finite weighted coefficient tail satisfies, for every real \(s\ge0\),
$$
(1+\rho)^{-m}\sum_{j\le mp-s}a_{m,j}\le\exp(-2s^2/m).
\tag{28}
$$
Indeed, \(a_{m,j}/(1+\rho)^m=\binom mjp^j(1-p)^{m-j}\). For \(\lambda\ge0\), the left side of (28) is at most
\(e^{-\lambda s}[(1-p)e^{\lambda p}+pe^{-\lambda(1-p)}]^m\). The logarithm \(F(\lambda)\) of the bracket has \(F(0)=F'(0)=0\); its second derivative is the variance of a two-point distribution on points a distance one apart, and is therefore at most \(1/4\). Explicitly that variance is \(q(1-q)\) for the tilted probability \(q\in[0,1]\). Integrating twice gives \(F(\lambda)\le\lambda^2/8\). Taking \(\lambda=4s/m\) proves (28). This is a finite coefficient calculation, with no assertion that an original capped law is a product law.

Since \(0\le U\le mp\le m/2\), every height factor in the numerator of (27) is at most \(m+1\). Equation (28) with \(s=mp-U\ge\delta/2\) bounds that numerator by
\((m+1)(1+\rho)^m\exp(-\delta^2/(2m))\). The mode lower bound for the denominator proves (27). This also proves both weighted count bounds uniformly in \(0<\rho\le1\), without a positive lower bound on \(p_\rho\).

Fix now an original sector with \(Q=q\ge1\), high-coordinate set \(I\) of size \(q\), and high labels \(\eta_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\). Let \(j\) be the number of its threes, \(m=k-q\), and \(\delta=bq\). The fixed high-coordinate energy is
\(E=\sum_{i\in I}g_i(\eta_i)+\Phi(\sum_{i\in I}(\eta_i-1))\ge bq\), because every high-site cost is at least \(b\). The remaining coordinates are binary, so their contribution to the excess is zero. The exact original sector mass is
$$
\rho^{2q+j}e^{-\beta E}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-E;\rho,\beta)
\le e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,\beta).
\tag{29}
$$
The equality is translation of every original height and the exact separation of the factor \(\rho^{2q+j}\). For the inequality use \(\rho^{2q+j}\le1\), and compare the tails at energies \(E+v\) and \(\delta+v\) for each residual binary one-count \(v\). Setting the coordinates of \(I\) to zero embeds the residual binary-height sum into the original binary sum with the identical spatial weights. Hence
\(Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}\ge Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,\beta)\). There are \(\binom kq2^q\) choices of \(I\) and its labels. Dividing (29) and using (23) gives
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q=q}}{Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}}
\le\binom kq2^q\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\rho,0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\rho,0)}.
\tag{30}
$$

If \(q\ge4L_{\rm cap}\), the cap assumption and \(p_\rho\le1/2\), \(b\ge2\) give
$$
T-\delta\le mp_\rho+qp_\rho+L_{\rm cap}b-\delta
\le mp_\rho+\delta/4+\delta/4-\delta
=mp_\rho-\delta/2.
\tag{31}
$$
For \(m=0\) this is negative, so the sector is empty. For \(m\ge1\), apply (25) if \(T\le mp_\rho\) and (27) if \(T>mp_\rho\). In both cases, since \(m\le k\), the ratio on the right of (30) is at most
\((k+1)^2\exp(-b^2q^2/(2k))\). The same upper bound holds for the empty sector. Using \(b^2/k\ge a^2\) and \(\binom kq2^q\le(2k)^q\), we obtain
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q=q}}{Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}}
\le(k+1)^2\exp\left(q\log(2k)-\frac{a^2q^2}{2}\right)
\quad(q\ge\max\lbrace1,4L_{\rm cap}\rbrace).
\tag{32}
$$
If \(q\ge H\ge(4/a^2)\log(2k)\), the positive term in the exponent is at most \(a^2q^2/4\). Summing (32) over the disjoint sectors and using \((H+j)^2\ge H^2+Hj\) proves (4) by a convergent geometric series. If \(H>k\), the original tail is empty and (4) still holds.

Finally write \(\xi=\log(k+1)\). The integer \(H_0\) in (5) meets the hypotheses of (4), since \(\log(2k)\le\xi+1\), \(K_a\ge4\), and \(K_a\ge4/a^2\). In particular \(\exp(-a^2H_0/4)\le(2k)^{-1}\le1/2\). Also \(aK_a=4(a+a^{-1})\ge8\), and hence
$$
\frac{a^2H_0^2}{4}\ge16(\xi+1)^2\ge2\xi+\log4.
\tag{33}
$$
The last inequality follows, for example, from \(16(\xi+1)^2-(2\xi+2)=16\xi^2+30\xi+14\ge0\) and \(\log4<2\). Equation (4) is thus at most \(2(k+1)^2e^{-a^2H_0^2/4}\le1/2\). The ceiling in (5), \(L_{\rm cap}\le D(1+\xi^2)\), and \(\xi+1\le2(1+\xi^2)\) give (6).

For fixed \(a,D\), (6) yields \(H_0\le2K_{a,D}(1+\log k)^2\). Apply the uniform low-occupation conclusion with \(A=2K_{a,D}\) and \(s=2\). For sufficiently large \(k\) depending only on \(a,D\), it gives \(Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\le H_0}\le eZ_{3,\Phi,\rho}\). The remaining mass has \(Q\ge H_0+1\), so
$$
Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}
=Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\le H_0}+Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}^{Q\ge H_0+1}
\le eZ_{3,\Phi,\rho}+\tfrac12Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}
\le\left(e+\tfrac12\right)Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}.
\tag{34}
$$
Taking \(L_{\rm cap}=D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\) proves (7) under its stated cap hypothesis. All estimates used the original weighted masses and complete height intervals. Conditional caps below zero were handled by empty sums, and no denominator vanished. At \(T=0\) only the zero spatial vector at height zero is feasible, giving ratio one. Weak energy inequalities include all cost endpoints and ties. No step requires \(\beta>0\), a selected-moment estimate, or any regularity of \(\Phi\) beyond its stated integer monotonicity.
