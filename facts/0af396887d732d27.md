## Statement

Let a convex body have centered Euclidean ball slices in $m\ge1$ perpendicular coordinates and let $R$ be their radius on its real projection. Suppose $R$ is positive, affine and nonconstant on a segment there. The entire projected volume measure, with density $f=\kappa_m R^m$ where $\kappa_m$ is the unit $m$-ball volume, cannot be the projected uniform volume of any convex body with the same real projected coordinate space and just $m-1$ perpendicular coordinates. Equality here is equality of Borel measures, so density versions may differ on null sets.

## Proof

For the projection of uniform volume on any full-dimensional compact convex body with $k\ge1$ perpendicular coordinates, Fubini gives the actual slice volume as a density. Its $k$th root is concave on the whole projection by Brunn–Minkowski and convexity's inclusion of interpolated slices. It is upper semicontinuous there, since closedness bounds the pointwise limsup of the indicators of slices by the indicator of the limiting slice, and all slices lie in a common bounded set. The density is positive on the interior of the projection. To see this, join a point in a projected ambient interior ball to a projected point beyond the target interior point on that line. A lifted convex combination with a point of the body produces an interior point above the target. Thus the closed support of the projected measure is the full projection.

If the two marginals are equal, their full projections coincide. Their canonical densities agree everywhere on its interior by continuity there of the concave roots, since they agree almost everywhere. They also agree at every boundary point: along a segment from a fixed interior point to that boundary point, concavity of each root gives a lower limit at least its boundary value and upper semicontinuity gives the reverse bound. The same argument applies if the proposed perpendicular dimension is zero by using its constant interior density one and its canonical value one on the projected body.

For $m\ge2$, the proposed root on the given segment would equal $\kappa_m^{1/(m-1)}R^{m/(m-1)}$. This is strictly convex there by positivity and nonconstancy of the affine $R$ and the exponent greater than one, contradicting the required concavity. For $m=1$, the alternative marginal is uniform volume on a set in the projected space itself. Its density is one on the interior of that projection and by the preceding continuity comparison the original density would also be one on the whole segment, contradicting nonconstancy.
