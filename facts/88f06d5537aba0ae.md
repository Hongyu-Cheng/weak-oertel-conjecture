## Statement

Use the uniform outward ramp, $h>0$, fixed positive compatible $d$-fibers on a finite complete lattice base $P$, the covariance norm $\Gamma$, and a nonnegative continuous concave buffer $b(Y)$ on the canonical hull. At a mark $Y=(p,y)$ take threshold $c=N\cdot Y-b(Y)\Gamma(u)$ for each nonvertical normal $N=(a,u)$, with ordinary pure-base caps unchanged. Let $Q$ be attained maximal raw depth. Assume each deepest mark has an attaining nonvertical cap with positive strip coarea. For any such cap normalize $\Gamma(u)=1$ and put $q=(N\cdot w-c)/h$, $S=\int\psi(q)\,d\mu/h$, $m=\int w\psi(q)\,d\mu/h$, and $A=\int q\psi(q)\,d\mu$. Then
$$
m_{\rm base}=S p,\qquad
m_{\rm cont}/S=y-[b(Y)-A/S]\nabla\Gamma(u).
$$
No convex combination with positive weights of two or more distinct deepest marks can have integer base. In particular each deepest base has one mark and the deepest bases are exactly the lattice points and vertices of their convex hull. They have distinct occupied parities, so there are at most $k(P)\le2^n$. This includes both $b=\varepsilon f$ and $b=0$.

## Proof

For a fixed mark $Y$ set $m_0=N\cdot(w-Y)$ and let $D=(a',v)$ be any full-normal direction. The first normalized margin derivative in a33339dae406ffd6, with its fixed scalar buffer $b=b(Y)$ and fixed $\Sigma$ at the mark, gives
$$
0=\int \psi(q)[D\cdot(w-Y)-m_0\nabla\Gamma(u)\cdot v]\,d\mu/h.
$$
The full direction is feasible because a finite normalized minimizing normal is also minimizing before normalization and $u\ne0$ is open. Since $m_0=hq-b(Y)$, the identity for all $D$ is exactly the two displayed moments.

For a fixed nonvertical normal its tail at arbitrary threshold is continuous and nonincreasing. Where the attaining coarea is positive its derivative is strictly negative, hence that raw mass is attained at a unique threshold on the full real line by monotonicity. For general normals the raw cap condition at level $Q$ is equivalent to the convex inequality $N\cdot Y-b(Y)\Gamma(u)\le c_Q(N)$ with the largest threshold of mass at least $Q$. This threshold exists since $Q>0$, supports are bounded, and the continuous ramp tail ranges from $M$ to zero. In this setting $Q>0$ because a fixed own interior ball gives positive depth to its center. For a pure-base halfspace through a convex combination of deepest marks, at least one positive-weight input lies in the halfspace. Its parallel translate through that input is a contained pure-base halfspace of mass at least $Q$. Therefore a convex combination with integer base is feasible by compatibility and is deepest.

At such a combination take one attaining nonvertical cap with positive coarea. Concavity makes its threshold at the combination at most the weighted thresholds at the contributing marks, and each of these is at most its unique $Q$ threshold, which is already its threshold at the combination. Hence all the positive-weight thresholds equal the same number, and the very same geometric cap attains at every contributing mark. Its $S,m,A$ are unchanged. The base moment gives the same base $p$ for every contributing mark. The strip barycenter $Z=m/S$ belongs to $C$, with base $p$, since the strip measure is positive and supported on $C$. Also $A<0$ because $q$ is strictly negative on the strip. With $g=\nabla\Gamma(u)$ the continuous coordinates of the contributing marks have the form $Z_{\rm cont}+t g$, where $t=b(Y)-A/S>0$. At each of these positions the buffer is $t+A/S$. If two positions are distinct, write $0<t_1<t_2$ for their line parameters. Concavity on the segment between $Z$ and the second position gives
$$
t_1+A/S\ge (1-t_1/t_2)b(Z)+(t_1/t_2)(t_2+A/S).
$$
It follows that $b(Z)\le A/S<0$, which contradicts nonnegativity. Thus all contributing marks coincide. This prohibits two deepest marks on a base, every integer site in the deepest-base hull other than its vertices, and every same-parity pair by its integer midpoint. The tied profile satisfies the buffer hypotheses by 32418f8d50e792af.
