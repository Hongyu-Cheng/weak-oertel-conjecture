## Statement

Use coordinates $(x_1,x_2,t,s)\in\mathbb R^4$, let $B$ be the closed planar unit disk, and let $C=B\times[0,3]^2$. Among all convex bodies, $C$ is uniquely determined by its volume $9\pi$ and the following twenty-two closed halfspace volumes.

- The eight caps $x_i\ge1$, $x_i\le-1$ for $i=1,2$, and $t\le0$, $t\ge3$, $s\le0$, $s\ge3$ have zero volume.
- The caps $t\le1$, $t\le2$, $s\le1$, $s\le2$ have volumes $3\pi,6\pi,3\pi,6\pi$, respectively.
- For $i=1,2$ and $j=0,1$, each of $t\ge j+1/2+x_i/10$ and $s\ge j+1/2+x_i/10$ has volume $3\pi(5/2-j)$.
- For $i=1,2$, the cap $t+s\le1/2+x_i/10$ has volume $101\pi/800$.

The total volume can instead be encoded by the halfspace $t\le4$ at volume $9\pi$. No polytope realizes these finite measurements, and no polytope preserves all open cell volumes of the corresponding arrangement when total volume is included.

## Proof

A zero exterior cap forces containment in its complementary closed halfspace. Otherwise the body contains a point strictly on the exterior side, and joining it toward an interior ball gives positive exterior volume. Thus any matching body $L$ lies in $[-1,1]^2\times[0,3]^2$. The total volume and untilted measurements make each of the three $t$ slabs and each of the three $s$ slabs have volume $3\pi$. The three-slab prism lemma applies in both directions.

The $t$-prism has $(t,s)$ projection $0\le t\le3$, $\alpha+a t\le s\le\beta+a t$, for $\alpha<\beta$. If $a\ne0$, at its least $s$ the $t$-section is a singleton. The $s$-prism has a $t$-interval of one fixed positive length at every level $s\in[0,3]$, including this endpoint, a contradiction. Thus $a=0$ and the projection is $[0,3]^2$. Its $s$-prism likewise has no $t$-shear, because a fixed positive $t$-interval translates to itself only under the zero shift. Applying both translations to the joint fiber over $(0,0)$ gives
$$
L=\{(y+tv+sw,t,s):y\in K,\quad 0\le t,s\le3\}
$$
for a planar convex body $K$ of area $\pi$, since the triangular shear has determinant one. The box bounds give $|v_i|,|w_i|\le2/3$ by comparing opposite slab endpoints at a fixed $y$ and fixed other coordinate.

Let $m_i$ be the centroid coordinates of $K$ and set $e=1/10$. For the upper $t$ cap at level $b=j+1/2$, the threshold in prism coordinates is $(b+e(y_i+sw_i))/(1-ev_i)$. The denominator is positive, and at $t=j,j+1$ the old margin $t-b-e x_i$ has strict opposite signs since $|x_i|\le1$. There is no truncation by the slab endpoints. Integrating the resulting interval length gives
$$
3\pi\left[3-\frac{b+e(m_i+3w_i/2)}{1-ev_i}\right]=3\pi(3-b).
$$
Hence $m_i+3w_i/2=-bv_i$. Its two distinct values of $b$ imply $v_i=0$ and $m_i+3w_i/2=0$. The corresponding $s$ equations imply $w_i=0$ and $m_i=0$.

Now $L=K\times[0,3]^2$ with centroid of $K$ zero. For each fixed $y$ the corner cap cuts a triangle of area $(1/2+e y_i)^2/2$ in $[0,3]^2$, since its threshold lies in $[2/5,3/5]$. Each such cap therefore has volume $\pi/8+(e^2/2)\int_K y_i^2\,dy$. For the disk the coordinate moments are $\pi/4$ by polar coordinates, giving the recorded $101\pi/800$. Matching these caps gives $\int_K |y|^2\,dy=\pi/2=\int_B |y|^2\,dy$. Since $|K|=|B|=\pi$,
$$
\int_K |y|^2\,dy-\int_B |y|^2\,dy
=\int_{K\setminus B}(|y|^2-1)\,dy+\int_{B\setminus K}(1-|y|^2)\,dy.
$$
The right-hand side vanishes only when the two sets agree up to null sets, hence exactly for compact convex bodies with interior by the exterior-point argument. Thus $K=B$. The disk prism is not a polytope because its projection onto the $x$-plane is a disk. The exterior zero caps force $t\le3$, so the suggested additional cap gives exactly total volume. Arrangement boundaries have Lebesgue measure zero, so matching all open cell volumes matches all halfspace volumes and the total.
