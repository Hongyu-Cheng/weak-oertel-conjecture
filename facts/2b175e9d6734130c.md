## Statement

Let \(G\) be a finite loopless undirected multigraph, allowing parallel edges, disconnected graphs, and isolated vertices. Count every nonloop edge once at each endpoint when computing degrees; parallel edges count separately. Assume that every vertex has degree at most 3 and that \(G\) has no two vertex-disjoint undirected cycles. Here two distinct parallel edges form a cycle of length two. When loops arise in a reduced graph, a loop forms a cycle of length one and contributes two to its vertex's degree.

Choose any orientation of the original edges. Use exactly the notation and normalization of accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8: \(V\) and \(E\) are the original vertex and edge sets, \(N:=|E|\), \(r:=|V|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G)\), and \(n:=N-r\), where components include isolated vertices. Thus \(n\) here is that fact's intrinsic cycle rank, not a new assertion about the dimension of an arbitrary mixed-integer convex body in the locked problem. For an edge oriented from \(u\) to \(v\), its column in \(\widehat A\) is \(\mathbf e_v-\mathbf e_u\); a reduced loop has column zero. For arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^E\), set
\[
D:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^E:\widehat A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},\qquad
Q:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}:=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}/Q.
\]
The symbol \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) denotes the minimum probability of a genuine closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^E\) containing \(\mathbf z\), and \(M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\). Retain the singleton convention of the accepted fact: normalized depth is 1, including in dimension zero.

There is a vertex set \(F\subseteq V\) with \(|F|\le4\) meeting every cycle of \(G\), and \(n\le12\).

Starting from \(G\), repeatedly apply the three operations of accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8: delete a bridge while retaining its endpoints, suppress a vertex incident with exactly two distinct nonloop edges and no other edge, or discard an isolated vertex. Retain every loop and follow the accepted orientation and tilt rules. Continue until no operation applies. Every such reduction terminates, preserves ordinary degree at most 3 and the absence of two vertex-disjoint cycles, and produces a graph \(G_*\) of exactly the original intrinsic rank \(n\). It has one of the following forms:

1. The empty graph, with \(n=0\).
2. One vertex carrying one loop, with \(n=1\).
3. A connected loopless cubic multigraph, with parallel edges allowed and no two vertex-disjoint cycles. If its vertex and edge sets are \(V_c,E_c\), and \(n_c:=|E_c|-|V_c|+1\), then \(n_c=n\), \(|V_c|=2(n_c-1)\le22\), and \(|E_c|=3(n_c-1)\le33\).

Consequently, in all cases \(|V_*|\le22\) and \(|E_*|\le33\). No uniqueness of the terminal graph is asserted or needed.

The exact alias map for the reduction is the identity map to the same-named objects of accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8: \(G_*,V_*,E_*,\widehat A_*,D_*,\boldsymbol\theta_*,Q_*,p^*,L,P,\Phi_*,\Phi,\widehat D,\widehat p\) have precisely its meanings. In particular, \(D_*:=\{\mathbf w\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E_*}:\widehat A_*\mathbf w=\mathbf0\}\), \(Q_*:=\sum_{\mathbf w\in D_*}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w}\), and \(p^*_{\mathbf w}:=e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w}/Q_*\). The chart objects are \(\Phi:=L\Phi_*\), \(\widehat D:=\Phi_*^{-1}(D_*)=\Phi^{-1}(D)\), and \(\widehat p_{\mathbf u}:=p_{\Phi\mathbf u}=p^*_{\Phi_*\mathbf u}\). There are an injective integer linear reconstruction map \(L:\mathbb R^{E_*}\to\mathbb R^E\) and a coordinate projection \(P:\mathbb R^E\to\mathbb R^{E_*}\) with \(PL=I\) satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(\ker\widehat A_*)&=\ker\widehat A,&
L(\ker\widehat A_*\cap\mathbb Z^{E_*})&=\ker\widehat A\cap\mathbb Z^E,&
L(D_*)&=D,\\
\boldsymbol\theta_*&=L^T\boldsymbol\theta,&
Q_*&=Q,&
p^*_{\mathbf w}&=p_{L\mathbf w},\\
\tau_p(L\mathbf w)&=\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w),&
\tau_p(\Phi\mathbf u)&=\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u),&
M_3(p)&=M_3(p^*)=M_3(\widehat p),
\end{aligned}
\]
for \(\mathbf w\in D_*\) and \(\mathbf u\in\widehat D\). The chart \(\Phi_*:\mathbb R^n\to\ker\widehat A_*\) is saturated, mapping \(\mathbb Z^n\) bijectively onto the integer kernel. Its support \(\widehat D\) is full-dimensional and satisfies \(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\). Every reduced loop is a retained free ternary coordinate, with its actual transferred tilt.

For completeness, the capacity notation also maps identically to that accepted fact. If \(c_{\mathbf z}=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\) for \(a>0\), put \(c^*_{\mathbf w}:=c_{L\mathbf w}\) and \(T:=\sum_Dc_{\mathbf z}\). For the capacity halfspace depth \(h_c\), namely the minimum capacity sum over closed halfspaces containing the point, the same \(T\) and \(a\) satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
c^*_{\mathbf w}&=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w},&
\sum_{D_*}c^*_{\mathbf w}&=T,&
h_c(L\mathbf w)&=h_{c^*}(\mathbf w)=T\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w),\\
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
&=\sum_{\mathbf w\in D_*}c^*_{\mathbf w}h_{c^*}(\mathbf w)^3
=T^4M_3(p^*).
\end{aligned}
\]
All these are equalities of actual depths, retaining boundary atoms, rather than bounds for a surrogate depth.

Let \(\mathcal K\) consist of the empty graph, the one-vertex one-loop graph, and the isomorphism classes of connected loopless cubic multigraphs on at most 22 vertices with no two vertex-disjoint cycles. Then \(\mathcal K\) is finite and every terminal kernel is represented in it. After standardizing vertex and edge labels and including all orientations, this gives a finite universal list of incidence supports \(D_*\), and, by fixing one accepted saturated chart per representative, a finite universal list of intrinsic supports \(\widehat D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) with \(n\le12\). The real tilts remain arbitrary; the finiteness concerns topologies and supports, not the collection of tilted probability laws.

## Proof

First apply Theorem 1.2 of Kang, Kwon, and Lee in its primary arXiv version 2, as stated and checked below. Take the source's root set to be the entire vertex set \(V\) of our graph. Every ordinary cycle, including a two-edge parallel cycle, is then an S-cycle in the source's notation. The hypothesis that there are no two vertex-disjoint cycles gives the source packing number at most one. The theorem supplies a set \(F\subseteq V\) of size at most 4 meeting all cycles. The source theorem assumes neither connectedness nor a degree bound, and explicitly works with finite graphs permitting loops and multiple edges. Thus this use includes disconnected graphs and isolated vertices.

Let \(B\subseteq E\) be the set of edges having at least one endpoint in \(F\). Since the original graph is loopless and degree counts edge multiplicity, counting incidences at vertices in \(F\) yields \(|B|\le\sum_{v\in F}\deg_G(v)\le3|F|\le12\). An edge with both endpoints in \(F\) is counted twice in the sum and only once in \(B\), which preserves the direction of this inequality. Isolated vertices contribute zero.

Delete all edges in \(B\), initially retaining every vertex. The resulting graph is the induced graph on \(V\setminus F\) together with the now-isolated vertices of \(F\). The induced graph has no cycle, since any such cycle would have avoided the hitting set. This is a forest in the ordinary multigraph sense: in particular, two surviving parallel edges would themselves have formed a cycle and cannot occur. Each nonempty connected acyclic component has one fewer edge than vertices. To see this, a singleton has zero edges. In a larger component an endpoint of a longest simple path has degree one: any extra incident edge either extends the path or creates a cycle, including a possible parallel two-edge cycle. Removing that leaf and its edge leaves a connected acyclic graph, so induction gives the asserted edge count. Summing over components, including all isolated vertices, proves that the graph after these edge deletions has cycle rank zero. The empty graph also has rank zero, with zero components.

For any intermediate graph \(G'\) in these deletions, removing one nonloop edge and retaining all vertices increases the component count by either zero or one. It increases it by one exactly when that edge is a bridge. The formula \(|E(G')|-|V(G')|+\#\operatorname{comp}(G')\) therefore decreases by either one or zero. Thus each of the \(|B|\) deleted edges lowers the rank by at most one. Since the final rank is zero, \(n\le|B|\le12\). The same argument covers an initially acyclic graph, a graph with isolated vertices, and a graph with no vertices. By the incidence-rank statement of accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8, this cycle rank is exactly \(\dim\ker\widehat A\), so it is the required intrinsic rank rather than the number of original edges or vertices.

We next analyze exhaustive accepted reductions. Every bridge deletion decreases \(|E|\) by one, every isolated-vertex deletion decreases \(|V|\) by one, and every series suppression decreases each by one. The nonnegative integer \(|V|+|E|\) therefore strictly decreases at every step. Consequently there can be only finitely many steps, and any procedure continuing whenever an allowed operation exists reaches a terminal graph.

The ordinary degree bound is preserved. Bridge deletion can only lower degrees. Discarding an isolated vertex affects no other degree. For a suppression at \(v\), let the two distinct nonloop edges join \(v\) to \(u\) and \(w\). If \(u\ne w\), each surviving endpoint loses one incident edge and gains one incident edge. If \(u=w\), the surviving vertex loses the two incidences of the two removed edges and gains the two incidences of the new loop. In both cases all surviving degrees are unchanged by suppression. In particular, parallel edges cause no degree-convention exception.

The absence of two vertex-disjoint cycles is also preserved. Edge or isolated-vertex deletion cannot create a cycle. For a series suppression, a reduced cycle avoiding the new edge was already a cycle before suppression. A reduced cycle using the new edge lifts by replacing that edge with the two-edge path through the removed vertex \(v\). If the new edge is a loop, its one-edge cycle lifts to the two-edge parallel cycle through \(u\) and \(v\). Each lift is a cycle. If two reduced cycles were vertex-disjoint, at most one could use the new edge. Their lifted cycles remain disjoint: the only added vertex is \(v\), which belongs only to the lift using that edge, and all other vertices were already vertices of the corresponding reduced cycles. This would contradict the previous graph's hypothesis. Induction proves the assertion throughout the reduction.

Accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8 gives rank preservation for exactly these operations, including loops produced by suppression. Directly, a bridge deletion lowers the edge count by one and raises the component count by one; suppression lowers both the edge and vertex counts by one without changing the component count; and deleting an isolated vertex lowers both the vertex and component counts by one. Each leaves \(|E|-|V|+\#\operatorname{comp}\) unchanged. Thus the terminal graph has exactly rank \(n\).

At termination there are no bridges and no isolated vertices. Suppose there is a loop at a vertex \(v\). It consumes two units of degree, so there cannot be a second loop at \(v\), and there is room for at most one other incident edge. If that other edge exists, it is nonloop. Removing it separates the vertex \(v\), with its loop, from all remaining vertices of its component, since no other nonloop edge leaves \(v\). It is therefore a bridge, contradicting terminality. Consequently every remaining loop is an isolated one-vertex one-loop component. It is not an isolated vertex and is not eligible for series suppression, so the loop must be retained as a free cycle coordinate.

Consider a terminal component containing no loop. No vertex has degree zero, because isolated vertices were discarded. A vertex of degree one would have its incident edge as a bridge. A vertex of degree two is incident with exactly two distinct nonloop edges and no other edge, even when the edges are parallel, and would be eligible for series suppression. Both possibilities contradict terminality. With degree at most 3, every vertex in this component consequently has degree exactly 3. Thus every terminal component other than an isolated loop is loopless and cubic.

Every nonempty terminal component contains a cycle. Indeed, a loop supplies one. Otherwise, take any edge of the component. It is not a bridge, so after removing it its endpoints are still joined by a path. A shortest such path has no repeated vertex; adding the removed edge gives a cycle. If that path consists of another parallel edge, the result is the permitted two-edge cycle. Two distinct nonempty components would therefore supply vertex-disjoint cycles, contrary to the preserved hypothesis. Since terminality also excludes isolated vertices, there is at most one component at all. This proves the three stated possibilities for \(G_*\).

For a connected loopless cubic component, ordinary multiplicity degree gives the handshake identity \(3|V_c|=2|E_c|\). Its rank is \(n_c=|E_c|-|V_c|+1\). Substituting the handshake identity gives \(n_c=|V_c|/2+1\), hence \(|V_c|=2(n_c-1)\) and \(|E_c|=3(n_c-1)\). There is only one component, so \(n_c=n\le12\), yielding the bounds 22 and 33. The isolated loop has one vertex, one edge, and rank one; the empty graph has zero of each. These satisfy the same universal size bounds. This reasoning makes no claim of a unique normal form and holds for every exhaustive sequence of the permitted operations.

The exact probability and depth assertions are an application, not a weakening, of accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8 to the original oriented loopless graph and this finite reduction sequence. Explicitly, a bridge coordinate reconstructs as zero, with the other tilts unchanged. At a suppressed vertex, if \(\varepsilon_e,\varepsilon_f\in\{-1,1\}\) are the incidence entries of its two edges and \(\sigma:=-\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f\), the surviving edge coordinate \(\xi\) reconstructs \(z_e=\xi\), \(z_f=\sigma\xi\), and has tilt \(\theta_g=\theta_e+\sigma\theta_f\). Coincident other endpoints produce a loop with zero incidence column, not a deleted coordinate. Thus each step uses precisely the accepted rules, with no arbitrary replacement of the original tilts.

The accepted reconstruction gives \(L(D_*)=D\) bijectively, exact equality of logarithmic weights through \(\boldsymbol\theta_*=L^T\boldsymbol\theta\), and \(Q_*=Q\). Its real- and integer-kernel isomorphisms have no lattice-index or multiplicity factor. Its restriction-and-lift argument for genuine closed halfspaces gives the displayed pointwise depth equalities in the reduced ambient space and in the saturated intrinsic chart; boundary atoms remain included. Its reindexing identities then give equality of cubic moments and, for the stated capacities, the same \(a\), total capacity \(T\), and capacity depths. In particular, bridge cleanup next to a loop cannot eliminate that loop's free ternary coordinate or its transferred tilt. When \(n=0\), all these supports are singletons and the accepted zero-dimensional convention gives depth and cubic moment 1.

Finally, \(\mathcal K\) is finite. For a loopless cubic graph on a fixed labeled vertex set of size \(m\le22\), the multiplicity of each unordered pair of distinct vertices is an integer between zero and three. Thus there are finitely many possible adjacency multiplicity arrays; requiring connectivity, degree three, and the stated cycle-packing property merely restricts this finite collection. Add the two exceptional topologies. Each cubic topology has at most 33 edges by the proved formula. Vertex labels may be standardized to \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and edge labels to \(\{1,\ldots,q\}\), where \(q\le33\). For fixed \(m,q\), there are at most \(m!q!\) relabelings and at most \(2^q\) orientations. A loop has zero incidence column; even if one separately records a choice of sign for its retained free coordinate, there are only two such choices. Therefore all standardized labeling and orientation choices remain finite. Arbitrary names on the original vertices and edges require only relabeling, not additional topologies.

For each such oriented representative, its ternary support is determined solely by its incidence matrix and is independent of the tilt. Choose once a saturated chart supplied by accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8 for each representative: a cubic representative is already a loopless original graph with no reduction needed, the isolated loop is obtained from a two-edge parallel cycle, and the empty representative is obtained from a forest. Fix the resulting chart, using the identity chart for the isolated loop and the zero-dimensional chart for the empty graph. The corresponding intrinsic support is then fixed as well, while its tilt varies through \(\Phi_*^T\boldsymbol\theta_*\). Coordinate relabelings are linear bijections and carry closed halfspaces bijectively, so they do not change the transported depths. Every original graph in the class therefore has its full law and actual depths represented on one member of this finite intrinsic support family. No constant loss, claim of finite possible tilts, or additional conclusion about the locked target is used.
