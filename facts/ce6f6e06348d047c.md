## Statement

Fix integers $n\ge2$, $d\ge1$ and $N\ge4$. Put $D=[-1,1]^d$, $v=e_1$, and write $z=(j,k)\in[-1,N]\times[-1,1]^{n-1}$. Use continuous fibers independent of $k$, given by $K_j=(j+2)D$ for $-1\le j\le1$ and $K_j=3D+(j-1)v$ for $1\le j\le N$. They define a compact convex body with positive volume in all its integer fibers. Each fiber satisfies $K_j\subset (j+2)D$, with equality throughout the full-dimensional source box $[-1,1]^n$ containing the integer point zero in its interior. For $A=3^{n-1}$ and $V=|D|=2^d$, the pushforward of the actual integer-fiber volumes under $\xi=x/(j+2)$ has density
$$
f_N(\xi)=A\left[(1+2^d+3^d)\mathbf1_D(\xi)+
 \sum_{j=2}^N(j+2)^d\mathbf1_{v+(3/(j+2))(D-v)}(\xi)\right]
$$
and mass $M=AV(1+2^d+N3^d)$. For $c\in D$ let $R_c$ be its Ball radial set about $c$, defined by $\rho_c(u)^d=d\int_0^\infty t^{d-1}f_N(c+tu)\,dt$ for unit $u$.

Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. At the centroid $0$ of the envelope $D$, the pointed cap $\xi_1\le0$ has mass $Q_0=AV[(1+2^d)/2+3^d]$. Every convex body $L\subseteq R_0$ with centroid zero satisfies $|L|\le Q_0/\Gamma_d$, which is below $M/2^n$ whenever $1+2^d+N3^d>(2^n/\Gamma_d)[(1+2^d)/2+3^d]$. In particular, for $n=d=2$ and $N=11$, one has $M=1248$, $Q_0=138$, and $|L|\le621/2<312=M/4$.

For $c\in D$ of normalized ordinary depth at least $\delta>0$ in $f_N$, let $D_\delta=\delta(1+2^d+N3^d)-(1+2^d)$. If $D_\delta>0$, then
$$
1-c_1\le\frac{6\,3^d}{D_\delta},\qquad
|c_l|\le\frac{3\,3^d}{D_\delta}\quad(2\le l\le d).
$$
These bounds apply at $\delta=\Gamma_d/2^n$ whenever $R_c$ contains a convex body of volume at least $M/2^n$ and centroid zero. They apply at $\delta=1/(d+1)$ to every actual deepest point of $f_N$. For fixed $n,d$ the corresponding centers tend to $v$ at rate $O(1/N)$.

## Proof

For each $u\in\mathbb R^d$ the support function of $K_j$ is the minimum of the affine functions $(j+2)h_D(u)$ and $3h_D(u)+(j-1)u\cdot v$. Indeed $u\cdot v\le h_D(u)$, which selects the first branch for $j\le1$ and the second for $j\ge1$. Concavity in $j$ of each support function gives $K_{(1-t)j+t j'}\supseteq(1-t)K_j+tK_{j'}$ for $0\le t\le1$. Hence the displayed family gives a convex body on the whole base box. It is bounded and closed by the two continuous piecewise-affine fiber formulas, and has an interior since interior base points with $j<1$ have uniform positive inballs nearby. Every integer fiber is positive and there are exactly $A$ copies at each integer $j$. The inclusion $K_j\subset(j+2)D$ for $j\ge1$ follows from $v\in D$ and $3D+(j-1)D=(j+2)D$. Equality for $-1\le j\le1$ follows from the fiber formulas.

The raw volumes at $j=-1,0$ are $V,2^dV$ per copy. For $1\le j\le N$ the raw volume is $3^dV$. In pointed coordinates $\xi=x/(j+2)$, the inverse Jacobian is $(j+2)^d$. The pointed supports at $j=-1,0,1$ are all $D$ and at $j\ge2$ are $v+(3/(j+2))(D-v)$. This yields the density and $M$ as stated. For any $c$, polar integration gives $|R_c|=M$ and
$$
|R_c\cap\{w:u\cdot w\le0\}|=\int_{\{u\cdot(\xi-c)\le0\}}f_N(\xi)\,d\xi\qquad (u\ne0).
$$
This is an equality of continuous volume and the pointed density's mass, with no convexity requirement on $R_c$. In dimension one the angular measure counts the two rays. For the source's general ray identity, a single summand before integrating has weight $\ell(z)^d$ and indicator $\mathbf1_{K_z}(y(z)+\ell(z)c+\ell(z)tu)$. The substitution $s=\ell(z)t$ cancels that weight against $t^{d-1}dt=s^{d-1}ds/\ell(z)^d$. Thus its ray integral is that of the unweighted physical indicator of $K_z-y(z)-\ell(z)c$, and the finite sum preserves the labels. Affinity of $y,\ell$ makes this a single affine shear, leaving every base unchanged.

For the cap $\xi_1\le0$, the physical condition in each fiber is $x_1\le0$. The two initial cubes give half their volumes. For $j\ge1$ the first-coordinate interval is $[j-4,j+2]$ of length six, and other coordinate lengths are fixed. Thus the fractions at $j=1,2,3$ are $1/2,1/3,1/6$ and all $j\ge4$ contribute zero volume. Their sum is one, giving $Q_0$. For a convex $L\subseteq R_0$ of centroid zero, the full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d applied to $L$ gives $\Gamma_d|L|\le|L\cap\{w:w_1\le0\}|\le Q_0$. The inequality on $N$ is precisely $Q_0<\Gamma_d M/2^n$. Substitution at $n=d=2,N=11$ gives $M=1248$, $Q_0=138$ and $Q_0/\Gamma_2=621/2$.

For $c\in D$, let $\eta=1-c_1>0$. In the cap $\xi_1\le c_1$, the physical threshold is $x_1\le(j+2)c_1$. At $j\ge1$ it misses the volume of the interval $[j-4,j+2]$ whenever $(j+2)\eta\ge6$. At most $6/\eta$ integer indices can contribute, each with at most $A3^dV$ mass. Adding the first two indices gives a cap mass at most $AV[1+2^d+(6/\eta)3^d]$. For $c_l\ne0$ with $l\ge2$, take the cap $\xi_l\ge c_l$ for positive $c_l$ and $\xi_l\le c_l$ for negative $c_l$. At $j\ge1$ the physical coordinate $x_l$ lies in $[-3,3]$, so at most $3/|c_l|$ indices contribute positive volume. The cap mass is at most $AV[1+2^d+(3/|c_l|)3^d]$. Comparing each to $\delta M$ gives the two bounds, with the zero-difference cases immediate.

If a centroid-zero convex $L\subseteq R_c$ has $|L|\ge M/2^n$, the same centroid inequality and polar identity give mass at least $\Gamma_d M/2^n$ in every closed cap through $c$, and hence in every containing closed halfspace. If $c$ is an actual ordinary deepest point, the continuous $n=0$ case of 6d6fcf377e68c79c applies to the nonzero finite Borel measure $f_N(\xi)\,d\xi$ on $\mathbb R^d$ and supplies a point of depth at least $M/(d+1)$. All deepest points belong to $D$ since the law is supported there and positive depth rules out any point outside by separation. The depth is attained because the minimum of through-mark cap masses over the unit sphere is continuous, by joint dominated convergence off null hyperplanes, and $D$ is compact. For each fixed positive $\delta$ and fixed $n,d$, the denominator in the asserted bounds grows linearly with $N$. The resulting coordinate bounds imply the claimed convergence to $v$.
