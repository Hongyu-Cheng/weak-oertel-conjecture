## Statement

For an integer \(n\ge1\) and a positive probability law \(p\) on a finite nonempty set \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), extended by zero off \(E\), retain the reflected quantities of accepted fact `ce8cccfdbee6f1cc` and the full geometric rows of accepted fact `445a9e668ee1a705`. Explicitly, for each \(j\in E\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in E:2j-x\in E\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},
&b(p)&=\sum_{j\in E}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)}\quad(x\in R_j),
&Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,
&q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
\qquad \nu(p)=\sum_{j\in E}q_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Thus \(\nu(p)\) is exactly the one-factor left side of (7) in accepted fact `445a9e668ee1a705`. This scalar is different from the comparison probability law denoted \(\nu\) in (6) of accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`. Every row in (1) is the entire available row; no pair is selected or discarded. The substitution \(x=j+v\) identifies each row sum with the oriented displacement sum, including \(v=0\) once and both orientations of every nonzero pair.

For each such law define \(K=2^n\), \(A_0=K^2\sum_{j\in E}p_j^3\), and \(A_{\mathrm{full}}=K^2b(p)\). All logarithms are natural. There is no finite absolute constant \(C\ge0\) for which
$$
\nu(p)\le C\log A_{\mathrm{full}}
\tag{2}
$$
holds for every positive globally affine Gibbs law \(p_x=W^{-1}\exp(c+\ell\cdot x)\), with \(c\in\mathbb R\), \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), and \(W=\sum_{x\in E}\exp(c+\ell\cdot x)\), on a complete convex lattice support \(E=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\), even if \(Q\) is a compact full-dimensional rational convex polytope and \(A_0<1\).

More precisely, for every integer \(k\ge8\), take the exact log-affine specialization \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) from accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`, also used in the final part of accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`. Its notation maps to the present family by
$$
E_k=\widehat A,\qquad n_k=N,\qquad h=t,\qquad
w_{k,(z,h)}=2^{-h},\qquad W_k=W,\qquad p_k=w_k/W_k.
\tag{3}
$$
Here \(\widehat A\), \(N\), \(t\), and \(W\) on the right are exactly the objects in accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`; \(E_k,n_k,W_k\) agree with the aliases in accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`. Set \(R_k=W_k/2^{n_k}\). At a fixed index \(k\), abbreviate \(p=p_k\), \(W=W_k\), \(n=n_k\), \(K=2^{n_k}\), and \(R=R_k=W/K\). This \(K\) is the parity scale, not the scalar \(K(w)=1\) of accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`.

For every member with \(R>1\), the canonical quantities in (1) satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
0<A_0&\le R^{-2}<1,\qquad A_{\mathrm{full}}\ge1,\\
2\sqrt{\nu(p)}&\ge\log A_{\mathrm{full}}+2\log R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Consequently, if \(\log A_{\mathrm{full}}>0\), then
$$
\frac{\nu(p)}{\log A_{\mathrm{full}}}
\ge\frac{(\log A_{\mathrm{full}}+2\log R)^2}
{4\log A_{\mathrm{full}}}
\ge2\log R.
\tag{5}
$$
If \(\log A_{\mathrm{full}}=0\), then \(\nu(p)\ge(\log R)^2>0\), so (2) already fails for every finite \(C\). Finally, writing \(H_k=\sum_{r=1}^k1/r\) for the harmonic number, this family has \(R_k\sim H_k/8\to\infty\). In particular \(A_0(p_k)\to0\), and for every \(C\ge0\), all sufficiently large members satisfy \(\nu(p_k)>C\log A_{\mathrm{full}}(p_k)\).

## Proof

First establish the full-row identities and the needed comparison for an arbitrary finite positive law. Since \(j\in R_j\), one has \(H_p(j)\ge p_j>0\), so \(b(p)>0\), every \(\kappa_j\) is a probability law, and \(q\) is a probability law. The involution \(x\mapsto2j-x\) preserves \(R_j\) and \(\kappa_j\), and changes the sign of \(Y_j\). Hence \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j=0\), and \(V_j\) in (1) is also its conditional variance. These are exactly the full-row normalizations in accepted fact `445a9e668ee1a705`; the normalizer \(b(p)\) remains the reflected moment of accepted fact `ce8cccfdbee6f1cc`.

Use a distinct symbol for the oriented minimum mass:
$$
\mathcal S_p(j)=\sum_{x\in R_j}\min\lbrace p_x,p_{2j-x}\rbrace
=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\lbrace p_{j+v},p_{j-v}\rbrace.
\tag{6}
$$
This is not the original row mass \(S_j=\sum_{x\in R_j}p_x\) used in the proof of accepted fact `445a9e668ee1a705`. Under the map (3), it is exactly \(S_j(w)/W\), where \(S_j(w)\) denotes the unnormalized oriented minimum sum in accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`, equivalently \(S_{\mathbf J}/W\) in accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`.

For positive \(u,v\), the identity \(\min\lbrace u,v\rbrace=\sqrt{uv}\exp(-|\log(u/v)|/2)\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_p(j)
&=H_p(j)\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}e^{-|Y_j|}\\
&\ge H_p(j)e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}|Y_j|}
\ge H_p(j)e^{-\sqrt{V_j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The first inequality is Jensen for the convex function \(u\mapsto e^{-u}\); the second is Cauchy--Schwarz. Both include a singleton row and \(V_j=0\).

Square (7), average with the original law \(p\), and only then introduce the auxiliary center law \(q\). Jensen for \(u\mapsto e^{-2u}\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q\), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_jp_j\mathcal S_p(j)^2
&\ge\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2e^{-2\sqrt{V_j}}\\
&=b(p)\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sqrt{V_j}}\\
&\ge b(p)e^{-2\mathbb E_q\sqrt{V_j}}
\ge b(p)e^{-2\sqrt{\nu(p)}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
All sums are finite. Equation (8) preserves the original reflected normalizer and uses the variance of every full row.

Now use precisely the accepted family (3). Accepted fact `604889294a17d7df` states that \(\widehat P\) is a compact full-dimensional rational convex polytope, that \(E_k=\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^{n_k}\), and that every supported height satisfies \(0\le h\le k\). Therefore \(0<w_{k,(z,h)}=2^{-h}\le1\), and the weight is the restriction of the single global affine exponential \(\exp(-(\log2)h)\). Thus the required completeness and global affineness hold simultaneously.

The same accepted fact, with the aliases already recorded in accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`, gives
$$
\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^{n_k}}S_j(w_k)=1,
\qquad \frac{W_k}{2^{n_k}}\sim\frac{H_k}{8}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{9}
$$
By (6), \(\mathcal S_p(j)\le1/W\) for every supported center. Since \(\sum_jp_j=1\), (8) and (9) imply
$$
A_{\mathrm{full}}e^{-2\sqrt{\nu(p)}}
\le K^2\sum_jp_j\mathcal S_p(j)^2
\le\frac{K^2}{W^2}=R^{-2}.
\tag{10}
$$
Taking logarithms of the positive quantities in (10) proves the second line of (4).

For completeness, verify directly the parity lower bound used in (4), which is also (3) of accepted fact `ce8cccfdbee6f1cc` and (12) of accepted fact `445a9e668ee1a705`. Write \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\). For any complete convex support with a global affine Gibbs law, every same-parity ordered pair \(x,y\in E\) has the integer midpoint \(j=(x+y)/2\in E\). Conversely every term in \(\chi(p)\) determines such a pair by \((x,y)=(j+v,j-v)\). Global affineness gives \(p_j=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(p)
&=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}
\left(\sum_{x\in E\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^n)}p_x\right)^2
\ge\frac1{2^n}=\frac1K,\\
b(p)&=\mathbb E_pH_p(j)^2
\ge(\mathbb E_pH_p(j))^2=\chi(p)^2\ge K^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz for the \(2^n\) parity masses summing to one, and the second is Jensen under the original law. Thus \(A_{\mathrm{full}}\ge1\).

The active condition follows separately from the actual original atom weights, without using \(b(p)\). Since \(0<w_j\le1\), one has \(w_j^3\le w_j\), and hence
$$
0<A_0=\frac{K^2}{W^3}\sum_jw_j^3
\le\frac{K^2}{W^3}\sum_jw_j
=\frac{K^2}{W^2}=R^{-2}.
\tag{12}
$$
This proves the first line of (4), its strict active inequality when \(R>1\), and \(A_0(p_k)\to0\).

To conclude quantitatively, suppose \(R>1\), and write \(g=\log A_{\mathrm{full}}\ge0\), consistently with the one-factor logarithmic surplus in accepted fact `ce8cccfdbee6f1cc`. By (4), \(2\sqrt{\nu(p)}\ge g+2\log R\). If \(g=0\), then \(\nu(p)\ge(\log R)^2>0\), whereas the right side of (2) is zero. If \(g>0\), square the nonnegative inequality and divide by \(4g\). The exact identity
$$
\frac{(g+2\log R)^2}{4g}-2\log R
=\frac{(g-2\log R)^2}{4g}\ge0
\tag{13}
$$
proves (5). For any proposed finite \(C\ge0\), (9) makes \(R_k>1\) and \(2\log R_k>C\) for all sufficiently large \(k\). The zero-logarithm case violates (2) directly, and the positive-logarithm case violates it by (5). Thus (2) cannot hold universally, even with both stipulated geometric premises and \(A_0<1\).

The quantity bounded above in (10) is the oriented minimum-mass moment from (6). It is not the actual halfspace-depth moment \(M_2(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2\) of accepted fact `ce8cccfdbee6f1cc`. The argument therefore establishes precisely the universal full-row variance-bridge obstruction and does not supply an upper bound on actual depth.
