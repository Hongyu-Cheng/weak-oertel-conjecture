## Statement

Let $T$ be a full-dimensional $d$-simplex of volume $V$, with $d\ge1$, barycentrics $\lambda_i$ indexed by $0\le i\le d$, $m=d+1$, and $\Gamma=(d/m)^d$. There is a dimension-only $\varepsilon_*>0$ such that for any $0\le\varepsilon_i\le\varepsilon_*$ the body $K=T\cap\{\lambda_i\le1-\varepsilon_i\ \text{for all }i\}$ has volume $V(1-t)$, where $t_i=\varepsilon_i^d$ and $t=\sum_i t_i$. On a fixed neighborhood of the centroid of $T$, the least $K$-volume in a closed halfspace containing $y$, divided by $V$, is
$$
\min_i\left\{d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(y)-t_i\right\}.
$$
There is a unique root $D$ in a fixed neighborhood of $\Gamma$ of
$$
\prod_i(D+t_i)\left(\sum_i(D+t_i)^{-1}\right)^d=d^d.
$$
The unique global maximizing point is interior to $K$ and has coordinates $\lambda_i^*=c/(D+t_i)$, where $c=(\sum_i(D+t_i)^{-1})^{-1}$. Its raw depth is $DV$ and its normalized depth is $D/(1-t)$. In dimension one the global root and midpoint formula holds throughout $\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1<1$, with $D=(1-t)/2$.

For one nonzero corner $t_0=t$ the other barycentrics are equal to $b$, the root near $1/m$ of $d^d b^{d-1}(mb-1)=t$, and $D=d^d(1-db)b^{d-1}$. For $d=2$ these specialize to $b=(1+\sqrt{1+3t})/6$ and $D=2(1+\sqrt{1+3t})/9-2t/3$. For a fixed nonnegative vector $(\tau_i)$ and $t_i=s\tau_i$, put $T_1=\sum_i\tau_i$, $\bar\tau=T_1/m$, and $B_2=\sum_i(\tau_i-\bar\tau)^2$. As $s\downarrow0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\Gamma-sT_1/m-s^2(d-1)B_2/(2m\Gamma)+O(s^3),\\
\lambda_i^*&=1/m+s(\bar\tau-\tau_i)/(m\Gamma)+O(s^2),\\
D/(1-sT_1)&=\Gamma+s(\Gamma-1/m)T_1
+s^2\left((\Gamma-1/m)T_1^2-(d-1)B_2/(2m\Gamma)\right)+O(s^3).
\end{aligned}
$$
The normalized linear coefficient is positive for $d\ge2$ and $T_1>0$. If all $t_i$ coincide, the maximizer is the centroid and $D=\Gamma-t/m$ exactly in the same regime.

## Proof

Affine equivalence allows a fixed standard simplex in each dimension. Write $g$ for its centroid. The deleted corners are disjoint for $\varepsilon_*<1/2$, since belonging to two would force two barycentric coordinates to sum to more than one. The $i$-th corner is homothetic to $T$ at vertex $i$ with ratio $\varepsilon_i$, and all cut boundaries are volume-null. This gives the volume formula. For $d=1$ the remaining interval has length $(1-t)V$, and its unique deepest point is its midpoint. For a nonempty remaining interval the positive root of the scalar equation is $(1-t)/2$ and the stated barycentrics give this midpoint.

A closed halfspace through $y$ suffices in the depth infimum, by translating the boundary of a containing halfspace toward $y$. For unit normal $u$ the $T$-volume of the cap through $y$ is jointly continuous in $(y,u)$ by dominated convergence off its limiting boundary. By 53fe12b742a7608d the only least-cap normals at $g$ point along $\nabla\lambda_i$, and each such cap has $T$-volume $\Gamma V$. Choose disjoint small open neighborhoods of these normals with compact closures on which the through-$g$ cuts strictly include only their designated vertex. The complement has a positive volume gap at $g$. Shrink to a fixed neighborhood $U$ of $g$ so that the strict vertex separation on the closures persists, and so that the gap on their complement remains bounded below by a positive constant relative to the corresponding canonical trials at $y\in U$. Deleting total volume $tV$ preserves the gap for sufficiently small $\varepsilon_*$. Uniform strict vertex separation on the compact closures, and the fact that corner diameters tend uniformly to zero, ensure every cap in the $i$-th neighborhood contains the entire $i$-th corner and misses all the others.

A through-$y$ cap that strictly isolates vertex $i$ has the form $\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\lambda_j(x)\le1$, with $a_j>1$ and $\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\lambda_j(y)=1$. Its $T$-volume fraction is $\prod_{j\ne i}a_j^{-1}$ by scaling the coordinates along the incident edges. AM–GM gives the lower bound $d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(y)$, attained only when $a_j=1/(d\lambda_j(y))$ for each $j\ne i$. These coefficients are $>1$ on a smaller $U$, and their cap normal tends to the designated canonical normal as $y\to g$. Thus the minimizing normal in each group is admissible in that group. Subtracting the known deletion $t_iV$ proves the exact formula for $y\in U$.

The $K$-depth is an upper semicontinuous function of $y$, being an infimum of continuous through-point cap volumes, so it attains its maximum on compact $K$. The centroid is retained and has depth at least $(\Gamma-t)V$. At any maximizer $y$ each canonical cap has $K$-volume at most $V(1-\lambda_i(y))^d$. Hence $\lambda_i(y)\le1-(\Gamma-t)^{1/d}$ for all $i$. These bounds tend uniformly to $1/m$, and their sum constraint gives the corresponding uniform lower bounds. Every maximizer is in $U$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon_*$.

At $t_i=0$ the left side of the root equation is $m^d D$, with derivative $m^d>0$. The analytic implicit function theorem gives the unique positive root near $\Gamma$, analytic in the $t_i$. The equation gives $d^d\prod_i\lambda_i^*=c$, so $d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j^*=D+t_i$. The point $\lambda^*$ tends to the centroid and thus lies in $U$ and in the interior of $K$. If another point with positive barycentrics satisfies all the product inequalities at level $D$, put $r_j=\lambda_j/\lambda_j^*$. Then $\sum_{j\ne i}\log r_j\ge0$ for all $i$. Multiply by the positive weights $1-d\lambda_i^*$, whose sum is one, and add. This yields
$$
0\le d\sum_j\lambda_j^*\log r_j\le d\log\!\left(\sum_j\lambda_j^*r_j\right)=0.
$$
Strict concavity of the logarithm and $\sum_j\lambda_j^*r_j=1$ give $r_j=1$ for every $j$. The localization above and exact equality at $\lambda^*$ prove the global assertion.

In the one-corner case the equal coordinates $b$ and $\lambda_0=1-db$ give $D+t=d^db^d$ and $D=d^d(1-db)b^{d-1}$. Subtracting gives the claimed equation for $b$; solving the quadratic when $d=2$ gives the explicit values. For the expansion, write $D=\Gamma+As+Bs^2+O(s^3)$ and take the logarithm of the root equation. Its linear coefficient is $(A+\bar\tau)/\Gamma$, so $A=-\bar\tau$. With this choice, the product-log term has quadratic coefficient $mB/\Gamma-B_2/(2\Gamma^2)$. The log of the inverse sum multiplied by $d$ has coefficient $-dB/\Gamma+dB_2/(m\Gamma^2)$. Their sum gives $B=-(d-1)B_2/(2m\Gamma)$. Expanding the coordinates and dividing the depth by $1-sT_1$ give the other two formulas. Bernoulli's inequality is strict for $(1-1/m)^d>1-d/m=1/m$ when $d\ge2$. Equal $t_i$ reduce the root to $m^d(D+t/m)=d^d$, and the coordinates are all $1/m$.
