## Statement

For a planar convex body $K$ containing $0$ in its interior and three nonzero spanning normals $v_1+v_2+v_3=0$, write
$$
k=|K|,\qquad Q_i=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge0\}|,\qquad
\sigma_i=\mathcal H^1(K\cap v_i^\perp)/\lVert v_i\rVert .
$$
Set $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_i M)$ and $\Phi_i=f_i+(f_j+f_l)^2/4$ where $\{i,j,l\}=\{1,2,3\}$. A triangle has strict distinct-vertex incidence if cap $i$ contains its vertex $P_i$ in its open halfplane and excludes both other vertices strictly.

Use the normals $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$. The canonical vertices for row $1$ are
$$
P_1=(2v+w,-v-w),\qquad P_2=(-x,x+s),\qquad P_3=(-x,-s-w),
\qquad x,s,v>0,\quad w\ge0.
$$

For each $0\le t\le x$ and sufficiently small $\delta>0$, the body
$K_\delta=\operatorname{conv}(P_1,P_2,P_3,(-x-\delta,t))$
is a quadrilateral containing the old triangle, with an ear of area $\delta B/2$ along $P_2P_3$, where $B=x+2s+w$. Its first row has strictly negative right derivative at $\delta=0$, and all three rows are strictly below $2$ for sufficiently small positive $\delta$. The cap $1$ and its cutting chord are unchanged.

## Proof

Write $b,c,e,L,H,B$ as in the canonical coordinate lemma. The base point $(-x,t)$ is in the relative interior of $P_2P_3$ because $-e<0\le t\le x<b$. The new point is outside that edge and remains inside the other two edge halfplanes for sufficiently small $\delta$, so the added region is the actual ear of area $\delta B/2$ and contains no cap $1$ area or chord extension.

For an ear triangle of area $E$ with base signed heights $a,-b$ for one cap, where $a,b>0$, and tip height $g\ge0$, the retained cap area and the coarea of its cut segment are
$$
E\left(1-\frac{b^2}{(a+b)(b+g)}\right),\qquad
\frac{2Eb}{(a+b)(b+g)} .
$$
The negative corner has edge fractions $b/(a+b)$ and $b/(b+g)$. Its volume is their product times $E$, and its Euclidean altitude from the cutting line is $b/\lVert v_i\rVert$, giving the segment formula. This argument also works for $g=0$, when the cutting segment ends at the tip.

Apply the formula first to cap $2$, with heights $(b,-e,t)$, and then to cap $3$, with heights $(c,-s,x+\delta-t)$ in the order of positive base vertex, negative base vertex, tip. In both cases the base height difference is $B$. It gives the exact data
$$
\begin{aligned}
k(\delta)&=k+\delta B/2,\
Q_2(\delta)&=Q_2+\frac{\delta}{2}\left(B-\frac{e^2}{e+t}\right),&
\sigma_2(\delta)&=\sigma_2+\frac{\delta e}{e+t},\
Q_3(\delta)&=Q_3+\frac{\delta}{2}\left(B-\frac{s^2}{b+\delta-t}\right),&
\sigma_3(\delta)&=\sigma_3+\frac{\delta s}{b+\delta-t}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular, the moving height at $t=x$ is $\delta$. All denominators are positive on the whole closed peak interval at $\delta=0$. With primes denoting right derivatives there, the quotient rule and $f_i'/f_i=k'/(2k)+Q_i'/(2Q_i)-\sigma_i'/\sigma_i-M'/M$ give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M'&=\frac{BH}{2L}\left(\frac{t}{b(e+t)}+\frac{x-t}{c(b-t)}\right),\
\Phi_1'&=(4-L/H)\left(\frac1{2L}-\frac{M'}H\right)
+\frac B{2L}\left(\frac{Bt-be}{b^2(e+t)}+\frac{B(x-t)-cs}{c^2(b-t)}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Set $u=t/x$ and $D=2b^2(b-t)(e+t)c^2 L H>0$. For the following three polynomials,
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=2s^{2}v+s^{2}w+s^{2}x+2svw+2svx+2sw^{2}+4swx+2sx^{2}\\
&\quad +vw^{2}+2vwx+vx^{2}+w^{3}+3w^{2}x+3wx^{2}+x^{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=4s^{4}v+2s^{4}w+2s^{4}x+8s^{3}vw+4s^{3}vx+6s^{3}w^{2}\\
&\quad +6s^{3}wx+8s^{3}x^{2}+6s^{2}vw^{2}+6s^{2}vwx+6s^{2}w^{3}\\
&\quad +7s^{2}w^{2}x+12s^{2}wx^{2}+11s^{2}x^{3}+2svw^{3}+2svw^{2}x\\
&\quad +2sw^{4}+3sw^{3}x+6sw^{2}x^{2}+11swx^{3}+6sx^{4}\\
&\quad +w^{3}x^{2}+3w^{2}x^{3}+3wx^{4}+x^{5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_2&=2s^{2}v+s^{2}w+s^{2}x+2svw+2svx+2sw^{2}+2sx^{2}\\
&\quad +vw^{2}+vx^{2}+w^{3}+x^{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

clearing the displayed denominators and collecting powers of $u$ gives
$$
-D\Phi_1'=e^2b^2P_0(1-u)^2+bcP_1u(1-u)+s^2c^2P_2u^2 .
$$
Each coefficient is strictly positive also when $w=0$, as each polynomial has a positive monomial independent of $w$. The three nonnegative terms have a positive sum for every $u\in[0,1]$. Thus $\Phi_1'<0$.

At the old triangle write $r=f_1$ and $z=f_2=f_3$. Then $r+z^2=2$, $0<z^2<2$, and
$$
4\bigl(2-z-(r+z)^2/4\bigr)=(4-z^2)(z-1)^2.
$$
If $z\ne1$, rows $2$ and $3$ are initially strict and remain so by continuity. If $z=1$, all $f_i=1$ initially and the three row derivatives all equal $f_1'+f_2'+f_3'$. Thus a strictly negative derivative in row $1$ gives all three strict inequalities for sufficiently small positive ear size in either case.
