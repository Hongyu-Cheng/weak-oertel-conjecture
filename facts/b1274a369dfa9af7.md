## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$ be integers, let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body, and put $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. For $z\in\mathbb Z^n$ set $K_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}$. Assume that the maximum affine dimension $q$ of the nonempty integer fibers satisfies $1\le q\le d$. Set $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$, and for
$$
P=\{z:\dim K_z=q\},\qquad w_z=\mathcal H_q(K_z),\qquad v_z=(1,z),
$$
write $Z=\sum_{z\in P}[0,w_z]v_z$. The set $Z$ is exactly the set of pairs $(\eta(S),\int_S z\,d\eta)$ for positive measures $0\le\eta\le\mu$.

Fix any $u\in\mathbb R^d$ such that $x\mapsto u\cdot x$ is nonconstant on $\operatorname{aff}K_z$ for every $z\in P$. Such $u$ exist, and if $q=d$ every nonzero $u$ qualifies. For every $r\in Z$ there are finite $b\in\mathbb R$ and $a\in\mathbb R^n$ such that the single closed halfspace
$$
H=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:b+a\cdot z+u\cdot x\ge0\}
$$
satisfies $\mu(\partial H)=0$ and $(\mu(H),\int_H z\,d\mu)=r$. This includes the zero and full-mass targets and arbitrary real first moments.

If the mass $t$ of $r$ is positive, $D=C\cap H$ is a convex body with maximum integer-fiber dimension $q$ and $\mathcal H_q|_{D\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}=\mu|_H$. If $r=(t,tj)$ with $j\in\mathbb Z^n$, the barycenter of this unweighted intrinsic measure belongs to $D\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ and $D_j=\{x:(j,x)\in D\}$ has positive $\mathcal H_q$ measure. In particular, for an integer $j$, if
$$
T_j=\max\left\{\sum_{z\in P}s_z:0\le s_z\le w_z,\quad \sum_{z\in P}(z-j)s_z=0\right\}>0 ,
$$
then $T_j$ is the maximum mass of a dominated submeasure with base barycenter $j$ and also the maximum retained mass of a convex subbody of $C$ with that base barycenter, measured using the original exponent $q$. A single cap with the prescribed direction $u$ attains both maxima.

## Proof

Compactness gives finitely many nonempty integer fibers. A compact convex set of affine dimension $q$ contains a $q$-simplex in its affine hull, so $0<w_z<\infty$ for $z\in P$. Each lower-dimensional fiber has zero $\mathcal H_q$ measure. The isometry $x\mapsto(z,x)$ identifies $\mu$ on the fiber over $z\in P$ with $\mathcal H_q|_{K_z}$. The mass and base moment of $\eta\le\mu$ therefore equal $\sum_{z\in P}s_zv_z$, where $s_z$ is its mass on that fiber and lies in $[0,w_z]$. Conversely, the density $s_z/w_z$ on each $K_z$ with $z\in P$ realizes any such list.

For each $z\in P$, the direction space $L_z=\operatorname{span}(K_z-K_z)$ has dimension $q\ge1$. The excluded directions form the finite union of the proper subspaces $L_z^\perp$, which has Lebesgue measure zero. If $q=d$, each excluded subspace is $\{0\}$. For a fixed admissible $u$, every level set of $u\cdot x$ in $K_z$ lies in an affine subspace of dimension at most $q-1$, so is $\mathcal H_q$-null. This proves $\mu(\partial H)=0$ for every threshold and base tilt under consideration, including when a lower-dimensional fiber lies entirely in the boundary.

We first solve relative-interior targets for any subfamily $J\subseteq P$. Put $Z_J=\sum_{z\in J}[0,w_z]v_z$ and $U_J=\operatorname{span}\{v_z:z\in J\}$. Since $Z_J$ contains zero and each $w_zv_z$, its affine hull is $U_J$. For nonempty $J$, $\dim U_J=1+\dim\operatorname{aff}J$, as is seen by subtracting one fixed $v_z$ from the others. For $J=\varnothing$, $Z_J=U_J=\{0\}$, and its only target is realized by the empty sum with parameter zero.

For nonempty $J$ and $\theta=(b,a)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}$, define
$$
\Psi_J(\theta)=\sum_{z\in J}\int_{K_z}(\theta\cdot v_z+u\cdot x)_+\,d\mathcal H_q(x).
$$
The hinge $s\mapsto s_+$ is 1-Lipschitz. Each coordinate difference quotient in the integrals is bounded by the corresponding absolute coordinate of $v_z$ and converges off the null threshold level. Dominated convergence gives the partial derivatives and, applied to the threshold indicators as $\theta$ varies, their continuity. Thus $\Psi_J$ is continuously differentiable with
$$
\nabla\Psi_J(\theta)=\sum_{z\in J}v_z\,\mathcal H_q\{x\in K_z:\theta\cdot v_z+u\cdot x\ge0\}.
$$
The weak inequality gives the same gradient as the strict one by nullity. Maximizing the linear functional $\theta$ over each generating segment gives the support function $h_{Z_J}(\theta)=\sum_{z\in J}w_z(\theta\cdot v_z)_+$. The Lipschitz bound gives
$$
|\Psi_J(\theta)-h_{Z_J}(\theta)|
\le\sum_{z\in J}\int_{K_z}|u\cdot x|\,d\mathcal H_q(x)<\infty ,
$$
uniformly in $\theta$.

Let $r_J\in\operatorname{relint}Z_J$. For some $\varepsilon>0$, the ball of radius $\varepsilon$ in $U_J$ centered at $r_J$ is contained in $Z_J$. It follows that $h_{Z_J}(\theta)-\theta\cdot r_J\ge\varepsilon\|\theta\|$ for $\theta\in U_J$. The uniform bound shows that the continuous function $\Psi_J(\theta)-\theta\cdot r_J$ tends to infinity as $\|\theta\|\to\infty$ in $U_J$, so it attains a minimum there. At a minimizer the derivative vanishes on $U_J$. Since both $\nabla\Psi_J(\theta)$ and $r_J$ belong to $U_J$, the gradient equals $r_J$. This realizes the target using the closed cap on all fibers in $J$.

Now take $r\in Z$. If it lies in $\operatorname{relint}Z$, use $J=P$. Otherwise let $F$ be the smallest face of $Z$ containing $r$, so $r\in\operatorname{relint}F$. A functional $e\in U_P$ exposes $F$. Indeed, write $Z$ by its facet inequalities in $U_P$ and sum the normals of the facets containing $F$. Equality in the resulting supporting inequality holds exactly when all those facet inequalities are equalities, which gives $F$. Maximization over the independent segments yields
$$
F=r_{\mathrm{fix}}+Z_J,\qquad
r_{\mathrm{fix}}=\sum_{z\in P:\,e\cdot v_z>0}w_zv_z,\qquad
J=\{z\in P:e\cdot v_z=0\}.
$$
The indices with $e\cdot v_z>0$ are fixed at full mass and those with $e\cdot v_z<0$ at zero. The residual $r-r_{\mathrm{fix}}$ lies in $\operatorname{relint}Z_J$. Choose $\theta_0$ from the relative-interior construction for that residual. For every $z\in P\setminus J$, the function $\theta_0\cdot v_z+u\cdot x$ is bounded on the compact $K_z$. Choose a single finite $T>0$ larger than each of these absolute bounds divided by $|e\cdot v_z|$. Then $\theta=\theta_0+Te$ retains each positive-sign fiber in the strict interior of the halfspace and excludes each negative-sign fiber strictly. On $J$ its trace is the one from $\theta_0$. Its moment vector is exactly $r$. At the two extreme targets $r=0$ and $r=\sum_{z\in P}w_zv_z$, one can also take $a=0$ and choose $b$ beyond the range of $-u\cdot x$ on $C$ to make $C\cap H$ empty or all of $C$, respectively.

For positive retained mass, nullity of $\partial H$ supplies a point of $C$ in the open halfspace. The interior of a full-dimensional convex body is dense in that body, so $\operatorname{int} C$ meets the open halfspace. Thus $D$ is compact, convex and has nonempty ambient interior. It has at least one fiber of positive $\mathcal H_q$ measure and all its fibers are subsets of the original fibers, which proves the dimension and measure assertions. The barycenter of its normalized measure lies in the compact convex $D$. When the first moment is $tj$, its base is $j$. Let $s_z=\mathcal H_q(D_z)$ for $z\in P$, so the positive weights $s_z/t$ sum to one and average the bases to $j$. By convexity the Minkowski sum $\sum_{s_z>0}(s_z/t)D_z$ lies in $D_j$. Keeping all but one summand fixed shows that $D_j$ contains a translate of a positive scaling of a positive $\mathcal H_q$ piece, which gives $\mathcal H_q(D_j)>0$ even if the contributing affine direction spaces differ.

The balanced box is compact and contains the zero row, so its maximum is attained. A maximizing row gives the moment vector $(T_j,T_j\,j)\in Z$. When $T_j>0$ its cap is a convex body with the claimed centroid and retained measure. Each competing dominated submeasure, including the restriction to any competing convex subbody measured by the original $\mathcal H_q$, has a feasible balanced box row and hence mass at most $T_j$.
