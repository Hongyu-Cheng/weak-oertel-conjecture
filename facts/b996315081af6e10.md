## Statement

Let \(n\ge6\) be an integer, put \(m=n^2\) and \(d=2n^4\), and define
\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
|x_i|\le nm\ (1\le i\le n),\quad
y_i\ge |x_i|/m\ (1\le i\le n),\quad
y_j\ge0\ (n<j\le d),\quad
\sum_{j=1}^d y_j\le d\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a full-dimensional compact rational convex polytope in the strict-positive fixed-normal simplex class. Its base is \(P=[-nm,nm]^n\), its concave facet functions are \(\psi_0=d\), \(\psi_i(x)=-|x_i|/m\) for \(1\le i\le n\), and \(\psi_i=0\) for \(n<i\le d\), and their sum is the canonical length \(L(x)=d-\Theta(x)>0\), where \(\Theta(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|/m\).

Let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), let \(\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:u_j\ge0,\ \sum_j u_j\le1\}\), and put \(t_z=(|z_1|/m,\ldots,|z_n|/m,0_{d-n})\) for \(z\in D\). With \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), the complete lattice fiber table is
\[
C_z=t_z+L(z)\Delta_d,\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=\frac{L(z)^d}{d!}>0
\quad(z\in D),\qquad
C_z=\varnothing,\quad w_z=0\quad(z\notin D).
\tag{2}
\]
Define \(\mu_z=\mathcal L^d|_{C_z}\) for every integer \(z\), using the zero measure for an empty fiber. Retain the locked definitions \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(W=\sum_z w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), and \(h_S\), and put \(T=2^{-n}W\). For a Borel ambient set \(A\), write \(\mu_C(A)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)\).

The following is the precise restricted kernel class. Let \(Q\) be uniform probability on \(\Delta_d\), and set \(F_z(u)=t_z+L(z)u\) for \(z\in D\). Choose any target \(J\in\mathbb Z^n\), any finite collection of ordered representatives \((z_a,z'_a)\) of pairs of distinct points in \(D\) with \(z_a+z'_a=2J\), any finite nonnegative Borel measures \(\eta_a\) on \(\Delta_d\), and a finite nonnegative Borel central measure \(\sigma\) on \(\mathbb R^d\). Require the globally shared actual source capacities
\[
\mathbf1_{\{z=J\}}\sigma
+\sum_{a:z_a=z}(F_z)_\#\eta_a
+\sum_{a:z'_a=z}(F_z)_\#\eta_a
\le\mu_z\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
Each component thus uses the same simplex coordinate \(u\) in its two source fibers and the coefficients \(1/2,1/2\). Repeated pairs are allowed, with all their usages in (3). Its output is
\[
B_a(u)=\frac{F_{z_a}(u)+F_{z'_a}(u)}2,
\qquad
\kappa_J=\sigma+\sum_a(B_a)_\#\eta_a.
\tag{4}
\]
The measures \(\eta_a\) need not be constant multiples of \(Q\). In particular (3)-(4) allow arbitrary measurable subcomponent retention under the common-coordinate coupling, in addition to arbitrary component masses. Source occurrence mass is charged in full, without a factor \(1/2\). No total-mass maximization is imposed.

The notation \(D,w_z,W,S\) agrees with accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`; its source indices \(\mathbf I\), target \(\mathbf J\), and uniform laws \(U_{\mathbf I}\) correspond to \(z,J\), and \((F_z)_\#Q=\mu_z/w_z\). Its scalar component mass \(x_a\) corresponds to \(|\eta_a|\), its central mass \(x_\circ\) to \(|\sigma|\), and its kernel mass \(M\), before retention, to \(|\kappa_J|\). Its logarithmic cost \(L_a\) is distinct from the fiber length \(L(z)\) in (2). Below, \(M\) is explicitly the mass of a newly specified retained uniform output, and \(M_f\) is the mass of the specified retained nonuniform output. The notation \(h_C\) in accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3` means the present \(h_S\), its \(D_+\) is the present \(D\), and its continuous variable is \(y\) here. Its continuous centroid inequality will be used in dimension \(k=d+n\), with constant \(\gamma_k=(k/(k+1))^k\). No affine-reference or logarithmic-cost hypothesis from either predecessor is assumed.

For every target \(J\), every kernel (3)-(4), every \(q\in\mathbb R^d\), every \(\ell_K>0\), and every \(M\ge0\), set \(K=q+\ell_K\Delta_d\) and let \(U_K\) be uniform probability on \(K\). If the retained measure \(\rho=M U_K\) satisfies \(\rho\le\kappa_J\), then
\[
M\le2\left(\frac45\right)^nT.
\tag{5}
\]
Domination here is an inequality of Borel measures. It permits arbitrary aggregate measurable retention of the output, not only deletion or downweighting of whole components. Every such retained measure can be realized by decreasing the component source measures in (3). A target outside \(D\) has only the zero feasible kernel.

There is nevertheless the following nonuniform construction in this same body. At target zero, retain \(\sigma=\mu_0\) and choose one representative from every nonzero antipodal pair in \(D\). For the pair \((z,-z)\), use \(\eta_z=w_zQ\). Then every source capacity is attained with equality, and the output is
\[
\kappa_0=\mu_0+\sum_{\{z,-z\}\subset D\setminus\{0\}}\mu_z,
\qquad |\kappa_0|=\frac{W+w_0}{2},
\tag{6}
\]
where the sum has one term for each unordered antipodal pair. Set \(\delta=1/(2m)\), \(\ell_*=d-n\delta\), and define
\[
f(y)=\frac{(2m)^n}{2}\prod_{i=1}^n(y_i-\delta)
\,\mathbf1_{\{y\in d\Delta_d,\ \delta\le y_i\le n\ (1\le i\le n)\}}.
\tag{7}
\]
The measure \(\rho_f=f\,d\mathcal L^d\) is dominated by \(\kappa_0\), is realizable by feasible retention in (3)-(4), and has mass \(M_f=|\rho_f|\ge3W/16\). The explicit rational point \(p^*=(0,y^*)\), defined by
\[
y_i^*=\delta+\frac{2\ell_*}{d+n+1}\quad(1\le i\le n),
\qquad
y_j^*=\frac{\ell_*}{d+n+1}\quad(n<j\le d),
\tag{8}
\]
belongs to \(S\) and satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_f(G)&\ge\frac W{48}
&&\text{for every proper closed continuous halfspace }G\ni y^*,\\
\operatorname{TD}_{\rho_f/M_f}(y^*)&\ge\frac1{12},
\qquad h_S(p^*)\ge\frac W{48}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{TD}_{\rho_f/M_f}\) is the infimum of normalized probabilities over all proper closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^d\) containing the point. The last assertion in (9) concerns every containing closed ambient halfspace, including vertical halfspaces, at this one fixed point.

## Proof

Resolving absolute values in (1) gives finitely many rational linear inequalities. They bound all base coordinates, and the continuous coordinates are nonnegative with sum at most \(d\), so the set is compact. The point \((0,\tfrac12\mathbf1_d)\) satisfies the defining inequalities strictly, which proves full dimensionality. The functions \(\psi_i\) in the statement are continuous concave rational piecewise-affine functions, and \(\Theta(x)\le n^2\) on \(P\). Hence \(L(x)\ge d-n^2>0\) on the entire base, as required by the strict-positive class. Translating each fiber by \(-t_z\) gives (2), including the simplex-volume factor \(1/d!\). The defining base box excludes every other lattice index. In particular the support is finite, all its fibers have positive volume, and \(W>0\).

We first check the capacity and retention statements without restricting source submeasures to uniform ones. Fix a target \(J\). Each source index other than \(J\) belongs to at most one unordered pair about this target, since its partner must be \(2J-z\). Repeated copies of the same unordered pair may therefore be merged, reversing the order of its representative when necessary; the common variable \(u\) and its output map do not change under this reversal. The pullback of \(\mu_z\) under the bijection \(F_z:\Delta_d\to C_z\) is \(w_zQ\). Consequently the merged measure for a pair \(\{z,z'\}\) satisfies \(\eta\le\min(w_z,w_{z'})Q\). This conclusion follows from the actual measure capacities in (3), including all repeated usages. The central measure satisfies \(\sigma\le\mu_J\). Thus all output measures have Lebesgue densities. Convexity of \(C\) puts each midpoint output in \(C_J\), and the same is true of the central measure.

If \(J\notin D\), no midpoint of two base points in \(D\) can equal \(J\), because the base box is convex and its integer points are precisely \(D\). In addition \(\mu_J=0\), so the central measure is zero. This proves the assertion about targets outside \(D\).

If \(\rho\le\kappa_J\) is any nonnegative retained output, its Radon--Nikodym derivative \(r=d\rho/d\kappa_J\) can be chosen in \([0,1]\). Replace each \(\eta_a\) by \((r\circ B_a)\eta_a\) and \(\sigma\) by \(r\sigma\). The resulting output is \(\rho\), and every source marginal only decreases. This proves the feasibility of arbitrary aggregate retention asserted after (5). In particular it includes every uniform-simplex retention considered there and the later nonuniform retention.

We also record the ambient transfer, using the source-occurrence convention of accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647` and proving it directly for (3)-(4). For a closed ambient halfspace \(H\), let \(H_z=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\). At each component coordinate \(u\),
\[
\mathbf1_{H_J}(B_a(u))
\le\mathbf1_{H_{z_a}}(F_{z_a}(u))
+\mathbf1_{H_{z'_a}}(F_{z'_a}(u)).
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, if both source scores in the linear inequality for \(H\) were strictly below its threshold, their average would be strictly below it. Integrating (10), adding the central contribution, and applying (3) yields
\[
\rho(H_J)\le\kappa_J(H_J)\le\sum_z\mu_z(H_z)=\mu_C(H)
\qquad(\rho\le\kappa_J).
\tag{11}
\]
This argument handles closed boundaries without a boundary-null hypothesis. It also verifies the normalization map to the predecessor: integrating (3) charges the whole mass \(|\eta_a|\) at each of its two sources, not its half.

Consider a merged noncentral pair \(\{z,z'\}\) about \(J\), where \(z'=2J-z\). Its output map is \(u\mapsto g+\ell u\), with
\[
g_i=\frac{|z_i|+|2J_i-z_i|}{2m}
    =\frac{\max(|J_i|,|z_i-J_i|)}m\quad(1\le i\le n),
\qquad g_j=0\quad(n<j\le d),
\qquad \ell=d-\sum_{i=1}^n g_i=\frac{L(z)+L(z')}2.
\tag{12}
\]
The scalar identity is \((|a+b|+|a-b|)/2=\max(|a|,|b|)\), applied with \(a=J_i\) and \(b=z_i-J_i\). The output support is \(g+\ell\Delta_d\). Its nonnegative facet gaps relative to \(C_J\) are \(g_i-|J_i|/m\) for \(i\le n\), zero for \(n<i\le d\), and zero at the common roof. Its output density is bounded by
\[
\frac{\min(w_z,w_{z'})}{\ell^d/d!}
=\left(\frac{\min(L(z),L(z'))}{(L(z)+L(z'))/2}\right)^d\le1,
\tag{13}
\]
because its underlying measure is dominated by \(\min(w_z,w_{z'})Q\). The central density is also at most one.

Suppose now \(\rho=M U_K\le\kappa_J\), with \(M>0\), \(K=q+\ell_K\Delta_d\), and \(\ell_K>0\). The zero-mass case of (5) is immediate. Since \(\rho\) is supported in \(C_J\), one has \(K\subseteq C_J\). To justify this at all boundary configurations, if the full-dimensional simplex \(K\) had a point outside the closed convex set \(C_J\), nearby interior points of \(K\) would give a positive-volume subset outside \(C_J\), contradicting domination. Therefore \(q_j\ge0\) for all \(j\), \(q_i\ge|J_i|/m\) for \(i\le n\), and \(\ell_K\le d-\sum_jq_j\).

Write \(b_i=q_i\) for \(i\le n\). A pair whose output reaches arbitrarily close to the lower vertex \(q\), from inside \(K\), must have \(g_i\le b_i\) for every \(i\le n\). By (12), all its sources lie in the same integer box \(|z_i-J_i|\le mb_i\) for \(1\le i\le n\). This box has
\(N_b=\prod_{i=1}^n(2\lfloor mb_i\rfloor+1)\)
integer points, is invariant under \(z\mapsto2J-z\), and has the one fixed point \(J\). It therefore contains at most \((N_b-1)/2\) unordered noncentral pairs. Bounds (13), together with the central density bound, give total density at most \((N_b+1)/2\le N_b\) near \(q\).

More precisely, there are only finitely many pairs, so choose a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(q\) such that every pair having some \(g_i>b_i\) stays excluded there. The neighborhood intersects the interior of \(K\) in positive volume. On that intersection the density of \(\rho\) is the constant \(M/\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) and is at most the stated almost-everywhere upper bound on the density of \(\kappa_J\). Put \(\Omega=d^d/d!\). It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
M&\le\frac{(d-\sum_{i=1}^n b_i)^d}{d!}
       \prod_{i=1}^n(2mb_i+1)\\
 &\le\Omega\prod_{i=1}^n\bigl((2mb_i+1)e^{-b_i}\bigr)
 \le\Omega\bigl(2m e^{-1+1/(2m)}\bigr)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The first inequality uses \(\sum_jq_j\ge\sum_{i=1}^n b_i\), and the second uses \((1-s/d)^d\le e^{-s}\) for \(0\le s<d\). The function \((2mb+1)e^{-b}\) on \(b\ge0\) has its unique maximum at \(b=1-1/(2m)>0\), where its value is \(2m e^{-1+1/(2m)}\). This proves the last inequality. All targets, source submeasures, retained submeasures, and facet thresholds have remained arbitrary.

We compare (14) with the actual total mass. For \(0\le u\le1/2\), differentiation proves \(-u-u^2\le\log(1-u)\le-u\). Since \(\Theta(z)\le n^2\), \(d=2n^4\), and \(\Theta(z)^2/d\le1/2\),
\[
w_z=\Omega e^{-\Theta(z)}r_z,
\qquad e^{-1/2}\le r_z\le1,
\qquad
W\ge\Omega e^{-1/2}
\left(\sum_{a=-nm}^{nm}e^{-|a|/m}\right)^n.
\tag{15}
\]
The one-dimensional sum is at least \(2m(1-e^{-n})-1\). Indeed, the decreasing function \(e^{-t/m}\) has \(\sum_{a=0}^{nm-1}e^{-a/m}\ge\int_0^{nm}e^{-t/m}\,dt=m(1-e^{-n})\). Restoring endpoints in the symmetric sum gives the stronger lower bound \(2m(1-e^{-n})-1+2e^{-n}\).

With \(\delta=1/(2m)=1/(2n^2)\), (14)-(15) and \(T=2^{-n}W\) give
\[
\frac MT\le e^{1/2}
\left(\frac{2e^{-1+\delta}}{1-e^{-n}-\delta}\right)^n
\le2\left(\frac45\right)^n.
\tag{16}
\]
Here are explicit checks on the numerical constants. For \(n\ge6\), \(e^{-n}\le1/(2n^2)=\delta\): verify the starting case using \(e>8/3\), and the induction uses \(e(n/(n+1))^2>1\). Hence \(1-e^{-n}-\delta\ge35/36\). Also \(e^\delta\le(1-\delta)^{-1}\le72/71\) and \(2/e<3/4\). The bracket in (16) is consequently at most \((3/4)(72/71)(36/35)=1944/2485<4/5\), and \(e^{1/2}<2\). This proves (5) with all its quantifiers.

We next construct and verify the complementary nonuniform output. For the antipodal pairs at target zero, \(C_z=C_{-z}\), so \(F_z=F_{-z}\). The measure \(w_zQ\) gives the full actual measure at each of its two sources and that same actual measure as output. Each nonzero source appears in exactly one pair, while the zero source is used centrally. Thus (3) is an equality at every source, and (6) follows by pairing equal weights.

At a continuous point \(y\in d\Delta_d\), the number of source fibers containing it is
\[
N(y)=\prod_{i=1}^n
\bigl(2\min(\lfloor my_i\rfloor,nm)+1\bigr).
\tag{17}
\]
This counts exactly the integers \(z_i\) with \(|z_i|\le nm\) and \(|z_i|\le my_i\), independently in each coordinate. The continuous marginal \(\sum_z\mu_z\) therefore has density \(N(y)\) on \(d\Delta_d\), and \(\kappa_0\) has density \((N(y)+1)/2\) there because \(\mu_0\) has density one. On the support specified in (7),
\(2\lfloor my_i\rfloor+1\ge2m(y_i-\delta)\)
for each \(i\le n\). The upper truncation \(y_i\le n\) means that the minimum in (17) does not reduce this bound. Thus \(f\,d\mathcal L^d\le\kappa_0\). Its feasible realization by source retention follows from the Radon--Nikodym pullback proved above.

For the quantitative depth, let \(k=d+n\), let \(\ell_*=d-n\delta\), and define \(I(a)=a^{d+n}/(d+n)!\) for \(a>0\). On the shifted simplex given by \(y_i\ge\delta\) for \(i\le n\), \(y_j\ge0\) for \(j>n\), and \(\sum_jy_j\le d\), the function
\[
\widetilde f(y)=\frac1{I(\ell_*)}\prod_{i=1}^n(y_i-\delta)
\tag{18}
\]
is a probability density. To see its normalization and geometry at once, take uniform probability on \(\ell_*\Delta_k\). Group its first \(2n\) coordinates into \(n\) pairs, replace each pair by its sum, and keep the remaining \(d-n\) coordinates. Add \(\delta\) to the first \(n\) resulting coordinates. For a pair of nonnegative coordinates with sum \(t_i\), integration over one member of the pair gives the factor \(t_i\); the change to that sum and that member has Jacobian one. The pushforward density is consequently (18), and its normalization is the volume \(\ell_*^{d+n}/(d+n)!=I(\ell_*)\).

The centroid of \(\ell_*\Delta_k\) has every coordinate \(\ell_* /(k+1)\). This follows, for example, by permuting its \(k+1\) barycentric coordinates, whose expectations are equal and sum to one. The mean of (18) is therefore exactly \(y^*\) in (8). Its coordinates are positive and sum to \(d-\ell_* /(d+n+1)<d\), so \(p^*\in S\). Also its first \(n\) coordinates are less than \(2+\delta<n\). In particular the specified point lies in the support region retained in (7).

Use the continuous centroid inequality recorded in the proof of accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3`: uniform probability on a full-dimensional convex body in dimension \(k\) assigns mass at least \(\gamma_k=(k/(k+1))^k\) to every closed halfspace containing its centroid. One may also obtain the required pure continuous specialization directly from its stated constant-profile consequence by taking an auxiliary body \([-1/4,1/4]\times(\ell_*\Delta_k)\), with one integer coordinate and the single positive integer fiber at zero. Its weighted lattice depth is that fiber's volume, and its actual mixed depth there is the continuous fiber depth. Thus no log-concavity theorem for nonuniform densities is needed. The grouping map used in (18) has rank \(d\). The inverse image of a proper continuous halfspace containing \(y^*\) is therefore a proper halfspace containing the centroid of \(\ell_*\Delta_k\). If \(\widetilde P\) denotes the probability in (18), it follows that
\[
\widetilde P(G)\ge\gamma_k>\frac1e>\frac13
\qquad\text{for every proper closed continuous halfspace }G\ni y^*.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict bound \(\gamma_k>1/e\) follows from \(\log(1+1/k)<1/k\). This application uses the dimension substitution \(k=d+n\) stated in the alias map, not the original continuous dimension \(d\).

We bound exactly the probability removed by the upper truncation \(y_i\le n\) in (7). Write \(t_i=y_i-\delta\), let \(b=d+n-1\), and set \(a=(n-\delta)/\ell_*\in(0,1)\). Under (18), \(t_i/\ell_*\) has density \(b(b+1)s(1-s)^{b-1}\) on \([0,1]\). This follows by integrating the remaining simplex coordinates in the paired-coordinate representation. Its tail probability at \(a\), by direct integration, equals \((1-a)^b(1+ba)\). We have \(n-\delta\le ba\le n+1\). The lower bound uses \(b\ge d\) and \(\ell_*\le d\). For the upper bound, it is enough that \(n^2+n(n+1)\delta\le d\); this follows from \(n(n+1)\delta=(n+1)/(2n)\le1\) and \(d=2n^4\). Therefore
\[
\widetilde P(y_i>n)\le2(n+2)e^{-n},
\qquad
r:=\widetilde P\bigl(\exists i\le n:y_i>n\bigr)
\le2n(n+2)e^{-n}<\frac14.
\tag{20}
\]
We used \(1-a\le e^{-a}\), \(e^\delta<2\), and the union bound. For the final strict inequality, \(2n(n+2)e^{-n}\) decreases for integer \(n\ge6\), since its consecutive ratio is \((n+1)(n+3)/(e n(n+2))<1\). At six its value is \(96/e^6<1/4\): the elementary exponential series gives \(e>65/24>27/10\), and \((27/10)^6>384\).

Set \(B=(2m)^n I(\ell_*)/2\). The retained measure with density (7) is \(B\widetilde P\) restricted by the truncation in (20). We compare \(B\) with \(W\) without changing the mixed-volume normalization. Formula (17) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
W&\le(2m)^n\int_{d\Delta_d}\prod_{i=1}^n(y_i+\delta)\,dy\\
 &\le(2m)^n I(d)\bigl(1+\delta(1+n/d)\bigr)^n
 \le\frac87(2m)^n I(d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
For the second line, expand the product. A term in which \(r\) of the \(n\) factors are replaced by \(\delta\) has integral \(\delta^r d^{d+n-r}/(d+n-r)!\). The elementary simplex monomial integral here follows by successive integration; it is also the paired-coordinate volume calculation above with only \(n-r\) pairs. Relative to \(I(d)\), this term is at most \([\delta(1+n/d)]^r\). There are \(\binom nr\) such terms, yielding the stated binomial. Finally, \(n\delta(1+n/d)\le1/8\), because \(n\ge6\), \(n\delta=1/(2n)\le1/12\), and \(1+n/d\le3/2\). Hence \((1+\delta(1+n/d))^n\le e^{1/8}\le8/7\), proving (21).

Bernoulli's inequality gives a corresponding lower bound on the shifted volume:
\[
\frac{I(\ell_*)}{I(d)}
=\left(1-\frac{n\delta}{d}\right)^{d+n}
\ge1-n\delta(1+n/d)\ge\frac78.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (21)-(22) show \(B/W\ge49/128>1/4\). From (20), the actual retained mass satisfies \(M_f\ge3B/4\ge3W/16\), while \(M_f\le B\). For any proper continuous closed halfspace \(G\ni y^*\), (19)-(20) give
\[
\rho_f(G)\ge B(\gamma_k-r)\ge\frac B{12}\ge\frac W{48},
\qquad
\frac{\rho_f(G)}{M_f}\ge\frac1{12}.
\tag{23}
\]
Taking the continuous halfspace infimum proves the first two assertions of (9).

Finally, let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace containing the already fixed \(p^*=(0,y^*)\). If its continuous normal is nonzero, its target section \(H_0\) is a proper closed continuous halfspace containing \(y^*\), so (11) and (23) give \(\mu_C(H)\ge W/48\). If its continuous normal is zero, the containing halfspace is vertical and \(H_0\) is all of \(\mathbb R^d\); (11) then gives \(\mu_C(H)\ge M_f\ge3W/16\). If the whole ambient space is admitted as an improper halfspace, its mass is \(W\). These exhaust all containing closed halfspaces and prove \(h_S(p^*)\ge W/48\).

The upper bound (5) is thus uniform over all targets and all permitted source and output retentions, and tends to zero relative to \(T\). The retained law (7) is nonuniform and satisfies (9) on the same actual body. No step bounds general nonuniform outputs from above, and no step treats midpoint coefficients as exhaustive of arbitrary barycentric choices. All statements are restricted to the exact family (1), so they assert neither a general-kernel obstruction nor a theorem for all fixed-normal simplex bodies.
