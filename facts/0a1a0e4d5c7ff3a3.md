## Statement

Let $X$ have a positive continuous nonincreasing density on a compact interval and mean zero. Then $\Pr(X\ge0)\le1/2$. Multiplying its density by a positive nonincreasing function with finite integral against the original law and renormalizing can only decrease this probability.

## Proof

The distribution function restricted to the interval of support is strictly increasing and concave, so its inverse quantile $Q$ is convex. The law of $Q(U)$ for uniform $U$ on $[0,1]$ is the original law. Jensen gives $Q(1/2)\le\int_0^1 Q(u)\,du=0$. Continuity of the law therefore gives $\Pr(X\ge0)\le1/2$.

Write $A=\{X\ge0\}$, let $\pi=\Pr(A)$, and let $r$ be the multiplier. For $0<\pi<1$, the conditional mean of $r(X)$ on $A$ is at most its conditional mean on $A^c$, since $r$ is nonincreasing. Writing these means as $r_+,r_-$ gives the tilted probability $\pi r_+/[\pi r_++(1-\pi)r_-]\le\pi$. If either side has probability zero, renormalization preserves it.
