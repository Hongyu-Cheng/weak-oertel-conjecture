## Statement

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite set in a Euclidean space, with weights $w_z>0$ of total $M$, and write $B=\operatorname{conv}P$. Fix an integer $h\ge1$ and set $\alpha=M/h$. Let $\mathcal F$ be the proper faces of $B$ of weight $w(F)=\sum_{z\in P\cap F}w_z=\alpha$. Exactly one of the following holds.

1. Some $v\in\mathbb R^P$ has $\sum_{z\in P}v_z=0$ and $\sum_{z\in P\cap F}v_z<0$ for every $F\in\mathcal F$. This holds vacuously for an empty face list.
2. There are numbers $\theta_F\ge0$ for $F\in\mathcal F$ such that $\sum_{F\in\mathcal F}\theta_F\mathbf1_{P\cap F}=\mathbf1_P$ and $\sum_{F\in\mathcal F}\theta_F=h$.

Suppose further that $h>1$ bounds the relative Helly number of $P$, and that the weights are the volumes of compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ with positive $d$-volume, $d\ge1$, whose centroids are given by one affine function on the base. In case 1 there is an actual centroid mark of raw mixed halfspace depth strictly greater than $\gamma_d M/h$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Thus if the maximum raw depth equals $\gamma_d M/h$, the critical proper faces have the fractional partition in case 2. The strict conclusion holds in particular if some site of $P$ lies outside every proper face of weight at most $\alpha$, including when $P$ meets $\operatorname{relint}B$.

## Proof

Write $V=\{v\in\mathbb R^P:\sum_z v_z=0\}$ and let $b_F$ be the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf1_{P\cap F}$ onto $V$. If the face list is empty, $v=0$ satisfies case 1 and the equation in case 2 is impossible because $P$ is nonempty. Otherwise the convex hull of the finitely many $b_F$ is compact. If it omits $0$, its nearest point $b$ to $0$ satisfies $b\cdot b_F\ge\|b\|^2>0$ for every $F$, by considering the segment from $b$ to $b_F$. Taking $v=-b\in V$ proves case 1.

If that convex hull contains $0$, there are nonnegative coefficients $\lambda_F$ of sum $1$ with $\sum_F\lambda_F b_F=0$. Therefore $\sum_F\lambda_F\mathbf1_{P\cap F}=c\mathbf1_P$ for some $c$. Pairing with the weights gives $\alpha=cM$, so multiplying the identity by $h$ gives case 2 with $\theta_F=h\lambda_F$. The two cases exclude each other because pairing the case 2 identity with $v$ in case 1 gives a strictly negative number on the left and zero on the right. The strict sign uses $\sum_F\theta_F=h>0$.

For the mixed consequence of case 1, put $w'_z=w_z+\varepsilon v_z$ with $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small. The total is $M$ and all weights stay positive. There are only finitely many subsets of $P$, so every subset of original weight strictly below $\alpha$ still has weight strictly below $\alpha$ for a single sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Every critical proper face also has new weight strictly below $\alpha$ by case 1. Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to the nonempty closed location set $P$ and its positive atomic measure with weights $w'_z$. Its relative Helly number is at most $h$, so there is a site $p$ of new weighted closed halfspace depth at least $M/h$. This is also an original center at level $\alpha$, since any trace of original weight below $\alpha$ stayed below it. No proper face of original weight at most $\alpha$ can contain $p$, since an exposing halfspace for that face would contradict the new depth. The full face has original weight $M>\alpha$. Thus the original minimal-face weight at $p$ is strictly greater than $\alpha$. By actual-centroid-graph-face-transfer in its affine case, $(p,g_p)$ has the required strict depth for the original fibers. This perturbation acts only on the auxiliary atomic weights.

If $q\in P$ lies outside every proper face of weight at most $\alpha$, take $v_z=\mathbf1_{\{q\}}(z)-w_z/M$. The sum of its coordinates is zero and its sum on every critical proper face is $-\alpha/M<0$. A point in $\operatorname{relint}B$ belongs to no proper face, giving the stated special case.

For a complete integer base $P=B\cap\mathbb Z^n$, $n\ge1$, the relative Helly bound $2^n$ follows from accepted fact bee3cc8e50a0dcea by taking the constant graph and lifting each base convex set to its product with the graph's continuous space. Same-parity midpoints belong to $P$ by completeness. For the positive-volume bases of a mixed-integer convex body, this completeness holds: at any lattice point in their convex hull, a convex combination of the positive fibers lies in its fiber and contains a translate of a positively scaled full-dimensional body. Consequently the strict conclusion applies at the threshold $\gamma_d M/2^n$ under the stated face and affine-centroid conditions.
