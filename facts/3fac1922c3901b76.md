## Statement

For $d\ge2$, let $\tau$ be positive and concave on a convex facet patch and $L$ positive affine there. Then $g=\tau L^{-d}$ has convex inverse power $g^{-1/(d-1)}$. For every real $\beta\ge d$ the inverse power $g^{-1/\beta}$ can fail to be convex, even for an unsaturated affine uniform-ramp selector from a constant shared roof. The example on $[0,1]$ is $L(x)=1+x$, $\tau(x)=(1+x/2)/2$.

## Proof

The map $(L,\tau)\mapsto L^{d/(d-1)}\tau^{-1/(d-1)}$ is the convex perspective $\tau(L/\tau)^{d/(d-1)}$ of the convex power, and decreases in $\tau>0$. With $L$ affine and $\tau$ concave, applying monotonicity at a convex combination and then joint convexity proves the first assertion. For the example, $\tau$ lies in $[1/2,3/4]$ and is exactly $\Psi(-1/2+x/4)$, with constant roof zero. Write $g^{-1/\beta}=2^{1/\beta}(1+x)^{d/\beta}(1+x/2)^{-1/\beta}$. Its first and second logarithmic derivatives at zero are $(d-1/2)/\beta$ and $(-d+1/4)/\beta$. Consequently
$$
(g^{-1/\beta})''(0)=2^{1/\beta}\frac{(d-1/2)^2+\beta(-d+1/4)}{\beta^2}<0.
$$
The numerator at $\beta=d$ is $(1-3d)/4<0$, and it decreases with $\beta$. The derivative is continuous on a neighborhood of zero, so it remains negative at nearby interior points. For a facet of dimension $d-1$, the same example may be extended constantly in its other $d-2$ coordinates. This is a scalar selector example with no full-menu stationarity assumption.
