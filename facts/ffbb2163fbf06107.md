## Statement

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard square instance of accepted facts `26568e8dd7d872e1` and `d5d5de35c3f25af9`. Thus \(r=(r_e)\) is the half-strength reservoir,
\[
 Q:=\sum_e r_e>0,
 \qquad
 \delta_z:=d_z(r)\leq\frac{a_z}{4},
\tag{1}
\]
\(b=(b_e)\leq r\) is the ancestry matching, and
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\quad(e=\{u,v\}),
 \qquad
 \lambda_m:=\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e,
 \qquad
 \Lambda:=\sum_m\lambda_m.
\tag{2}
\]
The degree-weighted target matching has degrees \(d_m\), mass \(P_{\mathrm{tar}}\), and
\[
 0\leq d_m\leq\lambda_m\leq\frac{a_m}{2},
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}:=\frac12\sum_m d_m
 >\frac{381}{800}Q.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(\mathcal L^\square\) be the full degree-weighted complete-product square-label family. For \(\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square\), write \(\theta_\sigma>0\) for its mass and \(\mathcal O_\sigma\) for its four-entry tagged source multiset, with every loop and repeated occurrence retained. Put
\[
 \Theta:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square}\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 R_z:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square}
 \theta_\sigma\operatorname{mult}_z(\mathcal O_\sigma),
 \qquad
 U_z:=a_z-R_z.
\tag{4}
\]
The accepted occurrence ledger gives
\[
 0\leq R_z\leq2d_z\leq a_z,
 \qquad
 \sum_zR_z=4\Theta.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(d_z\) in (5) is the degree-weighted target-matching degree, while \(\delta_z\) in (1) is the original reservoir degree.

Use the exact clipping and marked ancestry objects of `26568e8dd7d872e1`:
\[
 \varepsilon_z:=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{(2\delta_z-U_z)_+}{2\delta_z},&\delta_z>0,\\[2mm]
 0,&\delta_z=0,
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
\[
 \zeta_e:=r_e(1-\varepsilon_u-\varepsilon_v)_+,
 \qquad
 \eta_e:=\min\{\zeta_e,b_e\},
 \qquad
 h_m:=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\eta_e,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 \tau_m:=\min\{h_m,2d_m\}.
\tag{8}
\]
They satisfy
\[
 |\eta|:=\sum_e\eta_e\geq\Lambda-\Theta.
\tag{9}
\]

Define the exact whole-label synchronization LP
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Sync}(\tau):=\max\quad
 &\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square}q_\sigma\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 &0\leq q_\sigma\leq\theta_\sigma,\\
 &\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square}
 \operatorname{mult}_m(\mathcal O_\sigma)q_\sigma
 \leq\tau_m\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z^n).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Assume the degree-weighted square half-mass alternative
\[
 \Theta\geq\frac{P_{\mathrm{tar}}}{2}
\tag{11}
\]
and the zero-cost blocker phenomenon
\[
 \mathsf{Sync}(\tau)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Put
\[
 \mathcal B:=\{m:d_m>0,\ \tau_m=0\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then every positive whole square label has at least one tagged occurrence in \(\mathcal B\). Moreover, at each physical square target \(t\), positive direction orbits whose two tagged endpoints avoid \(\mathcal B\) can occur in at most one parity color.

The blocker masses obey the exact chain
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta
 \leq\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}R_m
 \leq2\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}d_m
 \leq2\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}\lambda_m
 \leq2\Theta.}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, with
\[
 \lambda_{\mathcal B}:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}\lambda_m,
\tag{15}
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_{\mathcal B}\geq\frac\Theta2
 \geq\frac{P_{\mathrm{tar}}}{4}
 >\frac{381}{3200}Q.}
\tag{16}
\]

There is an actual parent endpoint set \(\mathcal P\) and nonnegative charges \((\kappa_z)_{z\in\mathcal P}\), obtained from the ancestry edges targeting \(\mathcal B\), such that
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal P}\kappa_z=\lambda_{\mathcal B},
 \qquad
 0<\kappa_z\leq\delta_z\leq\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 U_z\leq\delta_z
 \quad(z\in\mathcal P).}
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal P}a_z\geq4\lambda_{\mathcal B},
 \qquad
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal P}R_z
 \geq3\lambda_{\mathcal B}.}
\tag{18}
\]
Let
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mathcal P}:=
 \{\sigma\in\mathcal L^\square:
 \mathcal O_\sigma\text{ has an occurrence in }\mathcal P\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_{\mathcal P}:=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal P}}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{19}
\]
Then every label in \(\mathcal F_{\mathcal P}\) also has a blocker occurrence, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{\mathcal P}
 \geq\frac34\lambda_{\mathcal B}
 \geq\frac{3\Theta}{8}
 \geq\frac{3P_{\mathrm{tar}}}{16}
 >\frac{1143}{12800}Q
 >\frac Q{100}.}
\tag{20}
\]

The blockers themselves carry one jointly feasible physical ancestry-target packet family with a nonnegative complement. For every positive ancestry edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},
\tag{21}
\]
and retain from `26568e8dd7d872e1` its positive-root mass \(\vartheta_e\) and the facts
\[
 \bar c_e\in L_e\subseteq\mathsf M_{\vartheta_e}(C_{m_e}),
 \qquad
 \sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{b_e}{\vartheta_e}\leq1.
\tag{22}
\]
There are densities \(H_m\) on \(C_m\), one for every \(m\in\mathcal B\), satisfying
\[
 0\leq H_m\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}H_m=\lambda_m,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_m}xH_m(x)\,dx
 =\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e\bar c_e.
\tag{23}

Define
\[
 V_m:=a_m-\lambda_m.
\tag{24}
\]
and let \(q_m\) be the barycenter of \(1-H_m\). Then
\[
 q_m\in\mathsf M_{V_m}(C_m),
 \qquad
 V_m\geq\lambda_m>0,
\tag{25}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 V_{\mathcal B}:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}V_m
 \geq\lambda_{\mathcal B}
 >\frac{381}{3200}Q
 >\frac Q{100}.}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus (23) is simultaneous target packing at the actual ancestry targets, and \((V_m,q_m)_{m\in\mathcal B}\) is its literal nonnegative physical complement.

This complement retains an exact parent-child first-moment identity. Put
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathcal B}:=
 \mathcal B\cup
 \{u,v:\ e=\{u,v\},\ b_e>0,\ m_e\in\mathcal B\}.
\tag{27}
\]
For every affine map
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal A_{\mathcal B})\to\mathbb R^d\)
and every \(m\in\mathcal B\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 V_m\bigl(q_m-s(m)\bigr)
 &=a_m\bigl(c_m-s(m)\bigr)\\
 &\quad-
 \frac12\sum_{e=\{u,v\}:m_e=m}b_e

 \end{aligned}}
\tag{28}
\]
Define
\[
 r_{\mathcal B}^{q}:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_m-s(m):m\in\mathcal B\},
\tag{29}
\]
\[
 r_{\mathcal A}^{c}:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal A_{\mathcal B})\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_z-s(z):z\in\mathcal A_{\mathcal B}\}.
\tag{30}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{r_{\mathcal B}^{q}\leq r_{\mathcal A}^{c}.}
\tag{31}
\]

Finally put \(\rho_{\mathcal B}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B)\) and \(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d\). Accepted facts `6769574fc1bd9f8d` and `be359f8a6c628483` give the legal selector lower bound
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal B}}
 \frac{\gamma_dV_{\mathcal B}}
 {r_{\mathcal B}^{q}+1}.}
\tag{32}
\]
Consequently either the right side of (32) is at least \(Q/100\), which is already a terminal accepted capacity, or, in the canonical hard branch \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<Q/100\),
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathcal A}^{c}+1
 \geq r_{\mathcal B}^{q}+1
 >\frac{381\gamma_d}{32}\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal B}}
 >\frac{381}{88}\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal B}}.}
\tag{33}

In particular, \(r_{\mathcal B}^{q}\leq3\), or the stronger condition \(r_{\mathcal A}^{c}\leq3\), forces the terminal alternative in (32). No assertion is made that the hard survivor in (33) is already absorbed, that the labels in (20) are synchronized inside the marked densities, or that the next square-output packets are jointly packed at their physical targets.

## Proof

We first characterize a zero synchronization optimum. Suppose one positive label \(\sigma\) has \(\tau_m>0\) at every source occurring in \(\mathcal O_\sigma\). Since the multiset has only four entries, there is a sufficiently small \(q_\sigma>0\) such that
\[
 q_\sigma\leq\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{mult}_m(\mathcal O_\sigma)q_\sigma
 \leq\tau_m
\]
at every one of its sources. Setting every other variable to zero is feasible in (10), contradicting (12). Thus every positive label has an occurrence with \(\tau_m=0\). Every square occurrence source has \(d_m>0\), by the degree-weighted construction in accepted fact `d5d5de35c3f25af9`, so this occurrence lies in \(\mathcal B\). Conversely, if every label has a zero-capacity occurrence, the constraint at that occurrence forces its nonnegative variable to vanish. Hence this blocker-cover condition is equivalent to (12), including loops and every repeated tag.

For the complete-product assertion, fix a physical target \(t\). In the notation of `d5d5de35c3f25af9`, call a positive orbit \((\xi,[u])\) over \(t\) unblocked when both occurrences \(t+u,t-u\) avoid \(\mathcal B\). A zero orbit is unblocked precisely when its repeated source \(t\) avoids \(\mathcal B\). If two distinct colors \(\xi<\eta\) had positive unblocked orbits \([u]\) and \([v]\), the complete cross-product coefficient would be
\[
 \theta_{t;\xi,[u];\eta,[v]}
 =\frac{2\beta_{\xi,t,[u]}\beta_{\eta,t,[v]}}
 {\alpha_t}>0.
\]
Its four tagged occurrences would all avoid \(\mathcal B\), contradicting the blocker cover. Thus at most one color over \(t\) has an unblocked positive orbit. This uses every cross pair, rather than an aggregate or diagonal-only coefficient table.

We prove (14). Choose one blocker occurrence from every positive label. Charging the full label mass to that occurrence, and then forgetting the choice, gives
\[
 \Theta\leq\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}R_m.
\tag{34}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) give
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal B}R_m
 \leq2\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}d_m
 \leq2\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}\lambda_m.
\tag{35}
\]
For \(m\in\mathcal B\), equation (8) and \(d_m>0\) imply \(h_m=0\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal B}\lambda_m
 =\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}(\lambda_m-h_m)
 \leq\sum_m(\lambda_m-h_m)
 =\Lambda-|\eta|
 \leq\Theta,
\tag{36}
\]
where the last inequality is (9). Equations (34)-(36) prove (14). Its two ends give \(\lambda_{\mathcal B}\geq\Theta/2\), and (3), (11) give the strict numerical bound in (16).

We now pull every blocker back through the actual ancestry edges. Fix \(m\in\mathcal B\) and an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(m_e=m\) and \(b_e>0\). Since
\[
 0=h_m=\sum_{f:m_f=m}\eta_f
\]
is a sum of nonnegative terms, \(\eta_e=0\). Equation (7) and \(b_e>0\) then force \(\zeta_e=0\). Also \(b_e\leq r_e\) makes \(r_e>0\), so
\[
 \varepsilon_u+\varepsilon_v\geq1.
\tag{37}
\]
Choose one endpoint \(\pi(e)\in\{u,v\}\) with
\(\varepsilon_{\pi(e)}\geq1/2\), resolving ties once. Define
\[
 \mathcal P:=\{\pi(e):b_e>0,\ m_e\in\mathcal B\},
 \qquad
 \kappa_z:=
 \sum_{\substack{e:m_e\in\mathcal B\\\pi(e)=z}}b_e.
\tag{38}
\]
Every relevant ancestry edge is assigned once, so
\[
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal P}\kappa_z
 =\sum_{m\in\mathcal B}\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e
 =\lambda_{\mathcal B}.
\tag{39}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 0<\kappa_z\leq d_z(b)\leq\delta_z\leq\frac{a_z}{4}.
\tag{40}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal P\), one has \(\delta_z>0\) and \(\varepsilon_z\geq1/2\). Equation (6) therefore yields
\[
 \frac{2\delta_z-U_z}{2\delta_z}\geq\frac12,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad U_z\leq\delta_z.
\tag{41}
\]
This proves (17). Since \(R_z=a_z-U_z\) and \(\delta_z\leq a_z/4\), equations (40)-(41) give
\[
 R_z\geq a_z-\delta_z\geq\frac{3a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 a_z\geq4\kappa_z.
\tag{42}
\]
Summing proves (18).

Every occurrence at a source in \(\mathcal P\) belongs to a label in \(\mathcal F_{\mathcal P}\). Each such label contributes at most four occurrences to \(\sum_{z\in\mathcal P}R_z\), with loops counted by their exact multiplicities. Hence
\[
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal P}R_z
 \leq4\Theta_{\mathcal P}.
\tag{43}
\]
Equations (18), (16), and (43) prove every inequality in (20). The blocker-cover property is inherited by the subfamily because it holds label by label.

We construct the physical blocker-target packets. The centroid \(c_z\) belongs to the half-metronoid \(\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\), represented by the constant half-density. Therefore \(\bar c_e\in L_e\). By (22), choose a density \(\phi_e\) on \(C_{m_e}\) of mass \(\vartheta_e\) and barycenter \(\bar c_e\). For \(m\in\mathcal B\), put
\[
 H_m:=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{b_e}{\vartheta_e}\phi_e.
\tag{44}
\]
Equation (22) gives pointwise
\[
 0\leq H_m
 \leq\sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{b_e}{\vartheta_e}
 \leq1.
\]
Its mass and first moment are exactly (23). Because \(d_m>0\) and \(d_m\leq\lambda_m\), one has \(\lambda_m>0\). The complement \(1-H_m\) has mass \(V_m=a_m-\lambda_m\) and barycenter \(q_m\), proving the metronoid membership in (25). Equation (3) gives
\[
 V_m=a_m-\lambda_m\geq\lambda_m.
\]
Summing and using (16) proves (26). This construction uses one summed density at each distinct physical target, so it is simultaneous target packing and not a collection of separately packable colliding labels.

The first-moment decomposition of \(1=H_m+(1-H_m)\) is
\[
 V_mq_m
 =a_mc_m-\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}.
\tag{45}
\]
For an affine map \(s\), the midpoint identity gives
\[
 s(m_e)=\frac{s(u)+s(v)}2.
\tag{46}
\]
Subtract \(V_ms(m)=(a_m-\lambda_m)s(m)\) from (45), use
\(\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e=\lambda_m\), and apply (46). This is exactly (28).

Choose an affine map on \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal A_{\mathcal B})\) attaining the minimum in (30). Equation (28) shows that every vector \(q_m-s(m)\) is in the span of the centroid residuals \(c_z-s(z)\), because \(V_m>0\). Restricting \(s\) to \(\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal B)\) proves (31).

Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives, from (25),
\[
 \delta_C(m,q_m)\geq\gamma_dV_m
 \qquad(m\in\mathcal B).
\tag{47}
\]
Use the points \(q_m\), weights \(\gamma_dV_m\), and an affine map attaining (29) in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. This proves (32).

If \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<Q/100\), equations (26) and (32) imply
\[
 r_{\mathcal B}^{q}+1
 >100\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal B}}
 \frac{V_{\mathcal B}}Q
 >\frac{381\gamma_d}{32}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathcal B}}.
\tag{48}
\]
Also
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1}>\frac4{11}.
\tag{49}
\]
For completeness, the first strict inequality follows from
\(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\), while
\[
 e
 <1+1+\frac12+\frac16
 +\frac1{24}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}4^{-j}
 =\frac{49}{18}<\frac{11}{4}
\]
proves the second. Combining (31), (48), and (49) proves (33). Since
\(381/88>4\), the hard branch has
\(r_{\mathcal B}^{q}+1>4\), even when
\(\rho_{\mathcal B}=n\). Thus a rank at most three is terminal, and (31) gives the same sufficient conclusion from
\(r_{\mathcal A}^{c}\leq3\). This proves every assertion and leaves only the explicitly stated high-rank absorption gap.
