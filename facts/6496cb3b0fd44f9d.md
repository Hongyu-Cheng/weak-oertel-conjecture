## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_z a_z.
\]
Assume \(W>0\), put \(Z:=\{z:a_z>0\}\), and set
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\qquad
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(q=(q_e)\) be a nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching in the sense of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5. Thus its positive support \(F:=\{e:q_e>0\}\) consists of unordered pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z\) with \(z\ne z'\) and \(z\equiv z'\pmod 2\), and
\[
d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\leq\frac{a_z}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Write \(Q:=|q|=\sum_eq_e\). For \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F\), define
\[
m_e:=\frac{z+z'}2\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
\bar c_e:=\frac{c_z+c_{z'}}2,
\qquad
D_e:=\frac12(D_z+D_{z'}),
\qquad
L_e:=\bar c_e+D_e=\frac12(\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'}).
\tag{3}
\]
The sets \(D_z\) and \(D_e\) are origin-symmetric convex bodies. Denote their Minkowski norms by \(\|\cdot\|_{D_z}\) and \(\|\cdot\|_{D_e}\), respectively, and put
\[
\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y,L_e)
:=\inf_{\ell\in L_e}\|y-\ell\|_{D_e}
=\bigl(\|y-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\bigr)_+.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the weighted affine defect and the maximum exactly affine-retainable mass by
\[
\Delta_C(q)
:=\min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\sum_{e\in F}q_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(s(m_e),L_e),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
R_C(q)
:=\max_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\sum_{\substack{e\in F\\s(m_e)\in L_e}}q_e.
\tag{6}
\]
Both extrema are attained, and
\[
R_C(q)
=\max\bigl\{|q'|:0\leq q'\leq q,\
q'\text{ is affinely half-metronoid realizable}\bigr\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The following exact weighted dual holds, with an attained maximum:
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_C(q)
=\max\left\{
-\sum_{e\in F}h_{L_e}(v_e):
\begin{array}{l}
\displaystyle\sum_{e\in F}v_e=0,\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\sum_{e\in F}v_em_e^{\mathsf T}=0,\\[1mm]
h_{D_e}(v_e)\leq q_e\quad(e\in F)
\end{array}
\right\}.}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, \(\Delta_C(q)=0\) if and only if one affine map hits every \(L_e\).

Choose a primal optimizer \(s_*\), and, among all dual optimizers, choose \(v=(v_e)_{e\in F}\) minimizing the gauge load defined below. With
\[
\omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
\qquad
\Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
\tag{9}
\]
one has
\[
0\leq\omega_e\leq q_e,
\qquad
\omega_e=q_e\quad\text{whenever }s_*(m_e)\notin L_e,
\tag{10}
\]
and consequently
\[
\boxed{Q-R_C(q)\leq\Omega\leq Q.}
\tag{11}
\]
There are points \(\ell_e\in L_e\) satisfying
\[
h_{L_e}(v_e)=\langle v_e,\ell_e\rangle
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_C(q)
=\sum_e\omega_e\|s_*(m_e)-\ell_e\|_{D_e}.}
\tag{13}
\]
If \(s_*(m_e)\in L_e\), one may take \(\ell_e=s_*(m_e)\). If \(s_*(m_e)\notin L_e\), \(\ell_e\) may be taken to be a nearest point in \(L_e\).

This load-minimal optimizer has a finite decomposition
\[
v_e=\sum_{j=1}^Nv_e^{(j)}
\tag{14}
\]
such that every \(v^{(j)}\) satisfies the two affine moment cancellations, is supported on at most \(d(n+1)+1\) edges, and obeys
\[
\sum_eh_{L_e}(v_e^{(j)})<0.
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover the gauge load is preserved exactly:
\[
\sum_{j=1}^N\sum_eh_{D_e}(v_e^{(j)})=\Omega.
\tag{16}
\]

For each \(e=\{z,z'\}\), choose \(x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z\) and \(x_{e,z'}\in\mathcal K_{z'}\) with
\[
\ell_e=\frac{x_{e,z}+x_{e,z'}}2.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(S_C:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let \(\mu_C\) be its mixed-volume measure. Then
\[

\tag{18}

\tag{19}

If the matching satisfies the stronger endpoint bounds

When \(\Omega>0\), let

\tag{22}
\]
and let
\[
r_q:=\min_{s:\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:\omega_e>0\}\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}

\tag{23}

\geq

\tag{24}
\]
Under (20), its application to (21) improves this to
\[
\boxed{

\geq
\frac{\Omega}{2^{\rho_q+1}(r_q+1)}.}
\tag{25}
\]

The same stress has the following corrected endpoint collapse. Put

U_z:=\frac12\sum_{e\ni z}v_e,
\qquad
\tau_z:=h_{D_z}(U_z),
\qquad
T:=\sum_z\tau_z.
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_zU_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}=0,
\tag{27}
\]
\[
0\leq\tau_z\leq d_z(q),
\qquad

\tag{28}

and

\boxed{
\sum_zh_{\mathcal K_z}(U_z)
\leq-\Delta_C(q).}
\tag{29}
\]
For every affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_z\tau_z
\bigl(\|c_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}-1\bigr)
\geq\Delta_C(q).}
\tag{30}
\]
If (20) holds and \(T>0\), define
\[
E_T:=\{z:\tau_z>0\},
\qquad
\rho_T:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E_T),
\tag{31}
\]
\[
r_T:=\min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(E_T)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}\{c_z-s(z):z\in E_T\}.
\tag{32}
\]
Then the residual-selector capacity of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 satisfies

\boxed{
R_{\rm sel}(C)
\geq
2^{n-\rho_T}\frac{T}{r_T+1}.}
\tag{33}

Consequently, suppose \(C_j\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_j}\times\mathbb R^{d_j}\) is any sequence with \(W_j>0\) and

For each \(j\), choose an exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching \(q_j\) supplied by be359f8a6c628483, and choose a primal-dual pair as above. Writing \(Q_j:=|q_j|\) and \(\Omega_j:=\sum_e\omega_{j,e}\), one has

The halfspace-dominated contact measure \(\sigma_{q_j}^{(1/2)}\) therefore has mass

## Proof

Accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 proves that \(D_z=\mathcal K_z-c_z\) is centrally symmetric. Accepted fact 107b9182522633f9 supplies a full-dimensional origin-symmetric covariance body contained in \(D_z\). Thus \(D_z\), and consequently every Minkowski average \(D_e\), is an origin-symmetric convex body with \(0\) in its interior. Their Minkowski functionals are genuine norms, their support functions are the corresponding dual norms, and (4) follows directly from \(L_e=\bar c_e+D_e\).

Let
\[
Y:=(\mathbb R^d)^F
\]
and let
\[
V:=\{(s(m_e))_{e\in F}:s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\text{ affine}\}\subseteq Y.
\tag{39}
\]
This is a linear subspace. Put \(L:=\prod_{e\in F}L_e\) and define the norm
\[
N_q((r_e)_e):=\sum_eq_e\|r_e\|_{D_e}.
\tag{40}
\]
Coordinatewise nearest-point minimization gives
\[
\Delta_C(q)=\min_{y\in V,\ \ell\in L}N_q(y-\ell).
\tag{41}
\]
The minimum is attained: the distance to the closed finite-dimensional subspace \(V\) is attained for each point of \(L\), and its quotient-norm value is continuous on the compact set \(L\).

The dual norm is
\[
N_q^*((v_e)_e)=\max_e\frac{h_{D_e}(v_e)}{q_e}.
\tag{42}
\]
Indeed, Hölder's inequality for each pair of primal and dual Minkowski norms gives the upper bound, and concentrating a norming vector in a coordinate attaining the maximum gives equality.

We prove the distance duality used in (8). For \(v\in V^\perp\) with \(N_q^*(v)\leq1\), \(y\in V\), and \(\ell\in L\),
\[
-h_L(v)
\leq-\langle v,\ell\rangle
=\langle v,y-\ell\rangle
\leq N_q(y-\ell).
\tag{43}
\]
Hence every dual value is at most (41). If the distance is zero, \(v=0\) gives equality. If its value is \(\Delta>0\), the convex set \(V-L\) is disjoint from the open \(N_q\)-ball of radius \(\Delta\). A separating functional \(v\ne0\), oriented toward \(V-L\), satisfies
\[
\inf_{r\in V-L}\langle v,r\rangle
\geq
\Delta N_q^*(v).
\tag{44}
\]
The set \(V-L\) is invariant under addition by \(V\); finiteness of the left side forces \(v\in V^\perp\). After normalizing \(N_q^*(v)=1\), (44) says \(-h_L(v)\geq\Delta\). Together with (43), this is equality. The feasible dual set is compact because every \(D_e\) contains a neighborhood of the origin, so the maximum is attained.

The product support function is \(h_L(v)=\sum_eh_{L_e}(v_e)\). If \(s(y)=Ay+b\), then \(v\perp V\) is equivalent to
\[
0=\sum_e\langle v_e,Am_e+b\rangle
\quad\text{for all }A,b,
\]
which is exactly the two moment equations in (8). Equation (42) turns \(N_q^*(v)\leq1\) into \(h_{D_e}(v_e)\leq q_e\). This proves (8), including attainment. It also proves \(\Delta_C(q)=0\) exactly when \(V\cap L\ne\varnothing\).

For (6)-(7), fix an affine map and retain the full weight \(q_e\) on every hit edge. The degree constraints remain valid, and membership \(s(m_e)\in L_e\) is exactly the existence of the endpoint witnesses in the affine-realizability definition of 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5. Conversely, the realizing affine map of any \(q'\leq q\) hits every edge on which \(q'_e>0\), so its mass is bounded by the right side of (6). There are finitely many edge subsets, and feasibility of a fixed subset is a closed finite-dimensional convex feasibility problem. Maximizing over the finitely many feasible subsets proves attainment and (7).

For one edge, the following support representation is immediate from the dual formula for a norm and minimization over \(L_e\):
\[
q_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y,L_e)
=\max_{h_{D_e}(v)\leq q_e}
\bigl(\langle v,y\rangle-h_{L_e}(v)\bigr).
\tag{45}
\]
Let \(y_e=s_*(m_e)\). Because \(v\in V^\perp\),
\[
\sum_e\langle v_e,y_e\rangle=0.
\tag{46}
\]
Primal-dual equality and (45) imply
\[
q_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y_e,L_e)
=\langle v_e,y_e\rangle-h_{L_e}(v_e)
\tag{47}
\]
for every edge separately, since every difference between the left and right sides is nonnegative and their sum is zero. If \(y_e\notin L_e\), the left side is positive. If \(h_{D_e}(v_e)<q_e\), scaling \(v_e\) upward until its dual norm is \(q_e\) would strictly increase the right side of (45), contradicting (47). Hence \(h_{D_e}(v_e)=q_e\) on every missed edge, proving (10).

The missed edges of \(s_*\) have \(q\)-mass at least \(Q-R_C(q)\). Summing (10) on them gives (11).

Choose a nearest point \(\ell_e\in L_e\) to \(y_e\). From (45)-(47),
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y_e,L_e)
&=\langle v_e,y_e\rangle-h_{L_e}(v_e)\\
&\leq\langle v_e,y_e-\ell_e\rangle\\
&\leq h_{D_e}(v_e)\|y_e-\ell_e\|_{D_e}\\
&\leq q_e\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y_e,L_e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Thus all inequalities are equalities. In particular, \(\ell_e\) exposes \(L_e\) in direction \(v_e\), and on a missed edge \(h_{D_e}(v_e)=q_e\) and
\[
\langle v_e,y_e-\ell_e\rangle
=\omega_e\|y_e-\ell_e\|_{D_e}.
\]
If \(y_e\in L_e\), take \(\ell_e=y_e\); (47) says it is also a support point in direction \(v_e\), and both sides of the last equality are zero. Summing proves (12)-(13).

We prove the circuit statement. The compact set of dual maximizers contains a minimizer of the continuous load \(\Omega\). For every edge with \(\omega_e>0\), put
\[
u_e:=\frac{v_e}{\omega_e},
\qquad
\Xi_e:=\bigl(u_e,\operatorname{vec}(u_em_e^{\mathsf T})\bigr)
\in\mathbb R^{d(n+1)}.
\tag{49}
\]
Then \(h_{D_e}(u_e)=1\) and
\[

\tag{50}
\]
Decompose the nonnegative vector \((\omega_e)_e\) into extreme rays of the polyhedral cone
\[
\{(a_e)_e:a_e\geq0,\ \sum_ea_e\Xi_e=0\}.
\tag{51}
\]
Writing the resulting nonnegative coefficients as \(\omega_e^{(j)}\), define \(v_e^{(j)}:=\omega_e^{(j)}u_e\). Every component satisfies both moment cancellations, the coefficients sum coordinatewise to \(\omega_e\), and therefore (14) and (16) hold. An extreme ray of (51) is a support-minimal positive dependence among vectors in \(\mathbb R^{d(n+1)}\), so its support has at most \(d(n+1)+1\) elements.

Divide (47) by \(\omega_e>0\). It gives
\[
h_{L_e}(u_e)-\langle u_e,y_e\rangle
=-\frac{q_e}{\omega_e}\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y_e,L_e)
\leq0.
\tag{52}
\]
Multiplying (52) by \(\omega_e^{(j)}\) and summing, the affine term vanishes by the moment cancellations of \(v^{(j)}\). Hence
\[

\tag{53}
\]

If it held, subtracting this component from \(v\) would preserve both moment equations and every capacity constraint, because the subtraction occurs along the same rays \(u_e\) with nonnegative remaining coefficients. It would preserve the dual objective while strictly decreasing \(\Omega\), contrary to load minimality. This proves (15).

e_+:=\{-e_2,e_2\},
\qquad
q_{e_-}=q_{e_+}=1.
\tag{71}
\]
Here \(d=1\), \(\gamma_1=1/2\), so (20) holds with equality. Direct integration for an interval \([c-1,c+1]\) gives
\[
\mathsf M_1([c-1,c+1])=[c-1/2,c+1/2].
\]
Both edge midpoints are \(0\), and
\[
L_{e_-}=[-1-\varepsilon/2,-\varepsilon/2],
\qquad
L_{e_+}=[\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2],
\qquad
D_{e_-}=D_{e_+}=[-1/2,1/2].
\tag{72}
\]
The intervals in (72) are disjoint, so one affine value at the common midpoint can hit exactly one of them and \(R_{C_\varepsilon}(q)=1\). Since \(\|t\|_{[-1/2,1/2]}=2|t|\), every point in the gap between the intervals has total weighted distance \(2\varepsilon\), and no point has smaller total distance. Hence \(\Delta_{C_\varepsilon}(q)=2\varepsilon\).

The dual stress
\[
v_{e_-}=2,
\qquad
v_{e_+}=-2
\]
satisfies both moment equations,
\[
h_{D_{e_-}}(v_{e_-})
=h_{D_{e_+}}(v_{e_+})=1=q_e,
\]
and
\[
h_{L_{e_-}}(2)+h_{L_{e_+}}(-2)=-2\varepsilon.
\]
Thus it is optimal by (8), has \(\Omega=2\), and proves (38). This completes the proof.
