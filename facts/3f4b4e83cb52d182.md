## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing\). For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), write \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), and \(W:=\sum_z w_z\). Assume that, whenever \(w_z>0\), the fiber \(C_z\) is centrally symmetric about a point \(c_z\in\mathbb R^d\), and that all such centers lie on one affine line. No assumption is made that \(c_z\) is affine in \(z\), and the integer bases need not have any sign symmetry.

For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define \(\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\})\), and define \(h_S(p):=\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }p\}\). Then there exists \(p\in S\) such that \(h_S(p)\geq 2^{-n-2}W\). Thus this full class satisfies the weak Oertel bound with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/4\in(0,1/e)\).

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes its set of nonempty integer fibers finite. Consequently all displayed fiber sums are finite and \(W<\infty\). If \(W=0\), any point of \(S\) proves the statement. Hence assume \(W>0\), and put \(\mathcal Z_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\).

We first establish the weighted centerpoint statement needed in the compressed space. Let \(B\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\) be a nonempty finite set, give each \(b\in B\) a positive weight \(\omega_b\), and write \(\nu:=\sum_{b\in B}\omega_b\delta_b\), \(T:=\nu(\mathbb R^{n+1})\), and \(h:=2^{n+1}\). There exists \(q\in(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\cap\operatorname{conv}(B)\) such that every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\) containing \(q\) satisfies \(\nu(J)\geq T/h\).

To prove this, consider every subset \(A\subseteq B\) with \(\nu(A)>(1-1/h)T\), and its compact convex hull \(K_A:=\operatorname{conv}(A)\). This is a finite nonempty family, since it includes \(A=B\). Given \(k\leq h\) members indexed by \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\), each complement has weight \(\nu(B\setminus A_i)<T/h\), so
\[
\nu\!\left(B\setminus\bigcap_{i=1}^k A_i\right)
\leq\sum_{i=1}^k\nu(B\setminus A_i)
<\frac{k}{h}T\leq T.
\]
There is therefore an element of \(B\cap\bigcap_i A_i\); this element belongs to \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\) and to every \(K_{A_i}\). The mixed-integer Helly theorem, whose Helly number for \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\) is \(2^{n+1}=h\), now gives a point \(q\in(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\cap\bigcap_A K_A\). If the family has fewer than \(h\) members, the same conclusion follows directly from the preceding common-element argument. Since \(K_B\) is one member, \(q\in\operatorname{conv}(B)\).

If a proper closed halfspace \(J\ni q\) had \(\nu(J)<T/h\), the subset \(A:=B\setminus J\) would have weight greater than \((1-1/h)T\). All points of \(A\) lie strictly in the opposite open halfspace. Every convex combination of these finitely many points also lies in that open halfspace, so \(q\notin K_A\), a contradiction. The whole-space case is immediate. This proves the weighted centerpoint statement, including halfspaces whose boundaries contain positive atomic mass.

Choose \(a\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\) so that the common affine line of the centers is \(a+\mathbb Rv\). This choice is also possible if all centers coincide. For every \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\), let \(t_z\in\mathbb R\) be the unique number such that \(c_z=a+t_zv\). Define the affine lift \(L:\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) by \(L(z,t):=(z,a+tv)\), and define the finite atomic measure \(\nu:=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+}w_z\delta_{(z,t_z)}\). Its mass is exactly \(W\). Applying the proved weighted centerpoint statement gives \(q=(m,t_*)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\) with
\[
q\in\operatorname{conv}\{(z,t_z):z\in\mathcal Z_+\},
\qquad
\nu(J)\geq 2^{-n-1}W
\quad\text{for every closed halfspace }J\ni q.
\]

Set \(p:=L(q)=(m,a+t_*v)\). Each center belongs to its fiber: if \(x\in C_z\), symmetry gives \(2c_z-x\in C_z\), and convexity then gives \(c_z\in C_z\). Hence \(L(z,t_z)=(z,c_z)\in C\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\). The affine nature of \(L\), the displayed convex-hull membership, and convexity of the actual body imply
\[
p=L(q)\in\operatorname{conv}\{(z,c_z):z\in\mathcal Z_+\}\subseteq C.
\]
Because \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), this proves \(p\in S\). In particular \(p\) lies in the actual fiber over \(m\); the proof does not assert that \(a+t_*v=c_m\).

Fix any proper closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni p\), written as \(H=\{(z,x):u\cdot z+b\cdot x\geq\gamma\}\), where \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), and \((u,b)\neq0\). Its pullback under \(L\) is
\[
J=L^{-1}(H)
=\{(z,t):u\cdot z+b\cdot a+t(b\cdot v)\geq\gamma\}.
\]
It contains \(q\), and is either a proper closed halfspace or the whole space. The weighted centerpoint estimate therefore applies.

For each \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\) such that \((z,t_z)\in J\), the point \((z,c_z)\) belongs to \(H\). Thus \(u\cdot z+b\cdot c_z\geq\gamma\), and the portion of \(H\) in that fiber contains \(K_z^+:=\{x\in C_z:b\cdot(x-c_z)\geq0\}\). Reflection \(x\mapsto2c_z-x\) preserves \(d\)-dimensional volume and exchanges \(K_z^+\) with \(K_z^-:=\{x\in C_z:b\cdot(x-c_z)\leq0\}\). Their union is \(C_z\), so
\[
2\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z^+)
=w_z+\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z^+\cap K_z^-)
\geq w_z.
\]
This also holds for \(b=0\), and retains all mass on the boundary. Summing over the selected fibers, while discarding only nonnegative contributions from other fibers, gives
\[
\mu_C(H)
\geq\frac12\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathcal Z_+\\(z,t_z)\in J}}w_z
=\frac12\nu(J)
\geq 2^{-n-2}W.
\]
For the whole-space ambient halfspace, the same bound follows from \(\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})=W\). The point \(p\) was chosen before \(H\), so taking the infimum over all admitted halfspaces proves the claim. The elementary inequality \(e<3<4\) gives \(1/4<1/e\).

## External sources

The sole external input is the mixed-integer Helly theorem: for integers \(r,s\geq0\), the Helly number of \(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb Z^s\) is \((r+1)2^s\). Equivalently, if every subfamily of at most \((r+1)2^s\) sets in a finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^{r+s}\) has a common point in \(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb Z^s\), the entire family has such a common point. Source: Gennadiy Averkov and Robert Weismantel, “Transversal Numbers over Subsets of Linear Spaces,” Theorem 1.1, equation (3), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0948. We apply it with \(r=1\) and \(s=n\), after permuting coordinates, to the finite family of compact convex hulls \(K_A\). The common-point hypothesis was verified above for every subfamily of at most \(2^{n+1}\) members. No measure regularity assumption or absolute continuity assumption is needed for this Helly application.
