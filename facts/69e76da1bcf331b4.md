## Statement

Fix a finite complete sampled base $P$, and consider actual compatible compact convex fibers $K_z\subseteq R B_d$, $d\ge1$, with summed volume $1$ and positive fiber-volume floor. Let $C$ be their canonical convex hull. Let $\Gamma(u)=(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u)^{1/2}$ for the pooled within-fiber covariance, with spectrum in a fixed $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]\subset(0,\infty)$. Use the nonnegative continuous concave normalized body-tied profile $f_H$ of 32418f8d50e792af, with its own-boundary value zero; the same conclusions hold for any nonnegative continuous concave profile with a uniform upper bound on sampled marks. Let $\Psi$ be the CDF of a smooth probability density $\psi$ supported on $[-1,0]$ and positive on $(-1,0)$, or use the uniform ramp $\Psi(t)=\min(1,\max(0,1+t))$ with $\psi=\mathbf1_{(-1,0)}$. For $\varepsilon\ge0,\delta>0$ the nonvertical cap mass at $(p,y)$ is the integral of $\Psi$ evaluated at
$
\frac{a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon f(p,y)\Gamma(u)}{\delta\Gamma(u)}
$
under summed fiber volume. Write $D_{\varepsilon,\delta}$ for the infimum of these through-mark cap masses and ordinary closed pure-base cap masses. Use the closed buffered cap at $\delta=0$, so $D_{0,0}$ is ordinary depth. For a smoothed cap let $S_0$ be the integral of $\psi$ of the displayed argument divided by $\delta\Gamma(u)$. There is a common constant $C_1$ such that for every sampled mark,
$$
0\le D_{\varepsilon,\delta}-D_{\varepsilon,0}\le C_1\delta,\qquad
0\le D_{\varepsilon,0}-D_{0,0}\le C_1\varepsilon.
$$
The corresponding capwise comparisons hold uniformly over all base tilts. At each fixed family and fixed parameters $\varepsilon\ge0,\delta>0$, exact replacement of base tilts after $\Gamma(u)=1$ puts all nonvertical cap profiles in one compact parameter domain. The raw depth is continuous on each sampled fiber and attains its maximum. For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon\ge0,\delta>0$, every deepest mark has a uniform own interior ball, has an attaining nonvertical cap and has a uniform positive lower bound on $S_0$ for each such attaining cap in $\Gamma$-unit normalization.

## Proof

The profile of 32418f8d50e792af is available because the input fibers are positive and compatible and their hull preserves them. It is continuous and concave with sampled values in $[0,1]$ at mass one. Denote a uniform sampled bound for the profile in use by $F$. If $|u|=1$, a nonvertical buffered cap adds to the ordinary cap only a slab of width at most $\varepsilon F\sqrt{\lambda_+}$. Smoothing is outward: the kernel equals one on nonnegative arguments and zero on arguments at most $-1$, so it adds only within another slab of width at most $\delta\sqrt{\lambda_+}$. The area of each Euclidean parallel section is at most $\omega_{d-1}R^{d-1}$, including counting area one for $d=1$. Integrating over the slab and summing across $P$ gives the two upper bounds, independent of the tilt. Pure-base masses are unchanged. Taking infima gives the claimed comparisons.

For exact clipping, take $\Gamma(u)=1$ and choose $B$ strictly larger than the uniform bound of $|u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon f(p,y)|+\delta$ for every sampled mark and every $X$ in a sampled fiber. Preserve each evaluation $a\cdot(z-p)$ in $[-B,B]$ exactly. Replace greater or smaller evaluations by the respective constraints $a'\cdot(z-p)\ge B$ or $a'\cdot(z-p)\le-B$. The old tilt is feasible, so the system has a minimum-Euclidean-norm solution. It lies in the span of its active rows, since a direction orthogonal to those rows is feasible locally in both signs. Independent active rows determine it in their row span by one of finitely many fixed linear inverses, and every right-hand side has magnitude at most $B$. Thus its norm is bounded uniformly over all marks and tilts at the fixed parameters. On the preserved rows the full old margin is unchanged, and on the outer rows both kernels are identically saturated, by the strict choice of $B$. The cap integral is jointly continuous in the mark, $a'$ and $u$ on the resulting compact domain, by dominated convergence. Its minimum is continuous on each compact sampled fiber. Pure-base through-mark masses depend only on the sampled base, so including them preserves continuity there. The finite union of fibers gives a maximum.

The ordinary maximum is at least $q_0=\gamma_d/|P|$: take the centroid of a largest fiber and use the full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d in its own trace. To bound ordinary depth near a sampled own boundary point $y_b\in K_p$, choose a unit supporting $u$ there. By the finite support LP dual in 7227bbfcd12ad3d2, the support heights of the sampled fibers have an affine majorant $g$ on the base with $g(p)=h_{K_p}(u)=u\cdot y_b$. Its hypotheses hold for this positive compact hull, and $g$ extends from its base affine span. The cap $u\cdot X\ge g(z)$ has zero mass because it meets each sampled fiber at most on a supporting hyperplane. Translating it to pass through $y\in K_p$ changes the threshold by at most $|y-y_b|$. The same slab-area bound gives $D_{0,0}(p,y)\le |P|\omega_{d-1}R^{d-1}\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial K_p)$. At any smoothed deepest mark its ordinary depth is at least $q_0-C_1(\varepsilon+\delta)$, since smoothing is outward. Taking the parameters small gives a uniform own ball of some radius $r>0$ at every such mark.

Decrease the parameters further so that $(\varepsilon F+\delta)\sqrt{\lambda_+}<r/2$. For each $\Gamma$-unit normal the two own kernel slab planes are within $r/2$ of the through-mark plane, so the whole slab has own-ball section area at least $\omega_{d-1}(\sqrt3 r/2)^{d-1}$. Integrating $\psi$ over its interval, with the coarea factor $1/|u|\ge\sqrt{\lambda_-}$, gives a positive uniform lower bound for $S_0$. To exclude any pure-base cap through the mark, multiply its base normal by a sufficiently large scalar and add a fixed $\Gamma$-unit continuous normal, with the prescribed buffer and smoothing. Nonzero base drifts retain their full or empty status by finite saturation. On all zero-drift fibers the mass is at most the original full mass, and the own ball gives a strict loss because it has positive mass below the kernel's full-capture plane. Thus each pure-base through-mark cap costs strictly more than some nonvertical cap. At a fixed sampled base, pure-base cap masses take only finitely many values, so they cannot lower the nonvertical minimum. Compact clipping gives its attainment and the own-slab estimate applies to each attaining normal.
