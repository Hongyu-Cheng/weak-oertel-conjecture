## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer and partition \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\) into \(g\in\{1,2\}\) nonempty blocks \(I_1,\ldots,I_g\). For every \(z\in\mathbb R^N\), define \(t_j(z):=\sum_{i\in I_j}z_i\) and \(t(z):=(t_1(z),\ldots,t_g(z))\). Let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R_{\ge0}^g\) be a nonempty compact convex set with \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^g\ne\varnothing\), and let \(F:Q\to\mathbb R\) be finite, continuous, and convex. No full-dimensionality assumption is imposed on \(Q\) or its lattice support. Fix arbitrary \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)\in\mathbb R^N\), with no sign restriction. Define the complete integer support and scalar table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&:=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N:t(z)\in Q\},\\
\widetilde w_z&:=\exp(-F(t(z))-a\cdot z)\quad(z\in D),
\qquad \widetilde w_z:=0\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^N\setminus D),\\
\widetilde W&:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^N}\widetilde w_z.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(J\in D\), let \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J:=\{\xi\in\mathbb Z^N:J+\xi,J-\xi\in D\}\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi)&:=\min\{\widetilde w_{J+\xi},\widetilde w_{J-\xi}\},\\
\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)&:=\log\frac{\widetilde w_J^2}
{\widetilde w_{J+\xi}\widetilde w_{J-\xi}}
\quad(\xi\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J),\\
\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)&:=
\sum_{\substack{\xi\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\\
\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)\le L}}\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi)
\quad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\]

All logarithms are natural. Every displacement sum is oriented and includes zero. The set \(D\) is finite and nonempty, every displayed curvature is nonnegative, the following maximum is attained, and

\[
\max_{\substack{J\in D\\L\ge0}}
\frac{\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)}{1+L}
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{(g+1)2^N}\widetilde W
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{3}\,2^{-N}\widetilde W.
\tag{1}
\]

For the actual-body consequence, let \(d\ge1\) and let \(\mathcal C\subset\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body. Write \(\mathcal C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in\mathcal C\}\) and \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(\mathcal C_z)\), with zero volume for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Suppose that the positive integer support is exactly \(\{z\in\mathbb Z^N:w_z>0\}=D\), and that some scale \(\mathsf K>0\) and comparison factor \(M\ge1\) satisfy

\[
\frac{\mathsf K}{M}\widetilde w_z\le w_z\le
\mathsf K M\widetilde w_z\qquad(z\in D).
\tag{2}
\]

Set \(S:=\mathcal C\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^N}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)\). For \(p\in S\), let \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{N+d}\). There exist \(J\in D\) and \(y^\star\in\mathcal C_J\), selected before the halfspace, such that

\[
h_S((J,y^\star))
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{12M^2(1+4\log M)(g+1)2^N}\,W
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{36M^2(1+4\log M)}\,2^{-N}W.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus for each fixed \(M\), the final coefficient in (3) is positive and independent of \(N,d,g,Q,F,a\), the block sizes, \(\mathsf K\), and all fiber shapes. The present \(N\) is the integer dimension denoted by \(n\) in the locked problem, and \(\mathcal C\) is its convex body \(C\). The scale \(\mathsf K\) and comparison factor \(M\) retain their meanings in accepted fact `ab4903f5a9a95d6c`; \(\mathsf K\) is not a scalar curvature score.

In particular, let \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_N>0\) take at most two distinct real values, let \(R\ge0\) be any real number, and set

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&:=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N:\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^N\beta_i z_i\le R\},\\
\widetilde w_z&:=e^{-a\cdot z}\quad(z\in D),
\qquad \widetilde w_z:=0\quad(z\notin D).
\end{aligned}
\]

For every real tilt vector \(a\), this complete signed-knapsack table satisfies

\[
\max_{J\in D}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb Z^N}
\min\{\widetilde w_{J+\xi},\widetilde w_{J-\xi}\}
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{3}\,2^{-N}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^N}\widetilde w_z.
\tag{4}
\]

Both orientations and the zero displacement occur in (4), with no division by two. The maximum may equivalently range over all \(J\in\mathbb Z^N\). The actual-body assertion (3) also applies to every convex body whose positive integer fiber volumes have the comparison (2) to this table. All statements retain boundary equalities, zero totals, zero budget, singleton blocks, \(N=g\), and zero-volume integer fibers outside \(D\).

## Proof

For each block choose one index \(p_j\in I_j\) minimizing \(a_i\) over that block. Such a pivot exists because the block is finite and nonempty; ties may be resolved arbitrarily. Put \(n:=N-g\). Retain every nonpivot coordinate as a horizontal coordinate \(x_i\), ordered by its original index, and append the \(g\) block totals. This defines the linear map

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi:\mathbb R^N&\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^g,
&\Phi z&=((z_i)_{i\notin\{p_1,\ldots,p_g\}},t(z)),\\
z_i&=x_i\quad(i\ne p_j\text{ for every }j),
&z_{p_j}&=t_j-\sum_{i\in I_j\setminus\{p_j\}}x_i
\quad(1\le j\le g).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

The second line is the inverse of the first: its block sums equal the prescribed totals, and its nonpivot coordinates equal the prescribed \(x_i\). Both maps have integer matrices. They are inverse on the whole real space, so their determinants are inverse integers and \(|\det\Phi|=1\). In particular, \(\Phi\) is a bijection of the entire lattice \(\mathbb Z^N\), with no omitted coset and no change of integer dimension.

For \(1\le j\le g\), write \(u_j(x):=\sum_{i\in I_j\setminus\{p_j\}}x_i\), using zero for an empty sum. Formula (5) proves the exact support identity

\[
D':=\Phi(D)
=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z^g:
t\in Q,\ x_i\ge0,\ u_j(x)\le t_j\ (1\le j\le g)\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, these inequalities are exactly nonnegativity of the nonpivot and pivot coordinates in the inverse formula. Compactness of \(Q\) bounds each total and hence every nonnegative original coordinate. Thus \(D\) is finite. Given any integer \(t\in Q\), set each pivot coordinate to \(t_j\) and every nonpivot coordinate to zero. This gives a point of \(D\), so \(D\) is nonempty and \(0<\widetilde W<\infty\).

For each nonpivot index \(i\in I_j\setminus\{p_j\}\), define \(a'_i:=a_i-a_{p_j}\ge0\), and set \(V(t):=F(t)+\sum_{j=1}^g a_{p_j}t_j\). This \(V\) is finite, continuous, and convex on \(Q\), irrespective of the signs of the pivot tilts. The inverse formula gives, for every real \((x,t)\) with \(t\in Q\),

\[
F(t)+a\cdot\Phi^{-1}(x,t)
=F(t)+\sum_{j=1}^g a_{p_j}t_j
+\sum_{j=1}^g\sum_{i\in I_j\setminus\{p_j\}}
(a_i-a_{p_j})x_i
=V(t)+a'\cdot x.
\tag{7}
\]

Define \(\widetilde w'_{(x,t)}:=e^{-V(t)-a'\cdot x}\) on \(D'\), and zero at every other point of \(\mathbb Z^N\). Equations (6) and (7) prove \(\widetilde w'_{\Phi z}=\widetilde w_z\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^N\), including zero weights outside the support. Consequently the transformed total mass \(\widetilde W':=\sum_I\widetilde w'_I\) equals \(\widetilde W\).

We next realize exactly \(D'\) by the two strictly positive resource rows required in `ab4903f5a9a95d6c`. Its height dimension \(k\) will be \(g\), and its horizontal dimension \(n\) will be the present \(N-g\); its total dimension \(n+k\) is therefore the original \(N\). Denote the auxiliary resource budgets by \(R_{\mathrm{aux}},T_{\mathrm{aux}}\), to distinguish them from the knapsack budget \(R\), and the auxiliary row vectors by \(b',c'\).

When \(g=1\), give both rows coefficient one at every horizontal coordinate, and let \(R_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)=T_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)=t_1\). Thus the two resource inequalities both say \(u_1(x)\le t_1\). These budgets are finite, continuous, affine, and nonnegative on \(Q\). Every row coefficient is strictly positive when there is a horizontal coordinate.

When \(g=2\), choose

\[
A:=1+\max_{t\in Q}\max(t_1,t_2),
\qquad \varepsilon:=\frac{1}{2(A+1)},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{aux}}(t):=t_1+\varepsilon A,
\quad T_{\mathrm{aux}}(t):=t_2+\varepsilon A.
\tag{8}
\]

The maximum exists by compactness, \(A\ge1\), every \(t_j\le A-1<A\), and \(0<\varepsilon A<1/2<1\). Give the first resource row coefficient one on nonpivot coordinates of block 1 and coefficient \(\varepsilon\) on those of block 2. Give the second row coefficient \(\varepsilon\) on block 1 and coefficient one on block 2. Every coefficient that occurs is strictly positive, even when one block has no nonpivot coordinate. The two row forms are then \(b'\cdot x=u_1(x)+\varepsilon u_2(x)\) and \(c'\cdot x=\varepsilon u_1(x)+u_2(x)\). The budgets in (8) are finite, continuous, affine, and positive on \(Q\).

In either case let

\[
P':=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^g:
t\in Q,\ x_i\ge0,\ b'\cdot x\le R_{\mathrm{aux}}(t),
\ c'\cdot x\le T_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)\}.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(g=1\), (6) immediately gives \(P'\cap\mathbb Z^N=D'\). For \(g=2\), if \((x,t)\in D'\), then \(u_j(x)\le t_j<A\), and hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1(x)+\varepsilon u_2(x)&\le t_1+\varepsilon t_2
\le t_1+\varepsilon A=R_{\mathrm{aux}}(t),\\
\varepsilon u_1(x)+u_2(x)&\le\varepsilon t_1+t_2
\le t_2+\varepsilon A=T_{\mathrm{aux}}(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Thus \(D'\subseteq P'\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Conversely, for an integer point \((x,t)\in P'\), nonnegativity of the horizontal coordinates and the resource inequalities imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1(x)&\le u_1(x)+\varepsilon u_2(x)
\le t_1+\varepsilon A<t_1+1,\\
u_2(x)&\le\varepsilon u_1(x)+u_2(x)
\le t_2+\varepsilon A<t_2+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Each \(u_j(x)\) and each \(t_j\) is an integer. Therefore (11) implies \(u_j(x)\le t_j\) for both blocks, proving the reverse inclusion in (6). We have proved \(P'\cap\mathbb Z^N=D'\) in all cases. This equality concerns the entire integer support; equality of the auxiliary real domain (9) with \(\Phi(\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^N:t(z)\in Q\})\) is unnecessary and is not asserted when \(g=2\).

If \(n=0\), both horizontal row vectors and all horizontal dot products are empty. The nonnegative budgets make both resource inequalities automatic, and (9) is simply \(Q\), as required by the predecessor's empty-horizontal convention. Formula (6) also reduces to \(D'=Q\cap\mathbb Z^g\). If just one horizontal block is empty, its \(u_j\) is zero and the same arguments apply without change. In particular, for an integer height with \(t_j=0\), (11) forces the corresponding nonnegative horizontal sum to be zero. Boundary equalities, zero original totals, and lower-rank height domains have not been removed at any stage.

All hypotheses of the scalar assertion (1) in accepted fact `ab4903f5a9a95d6c` now hold. Its \(Q\) and \(V\) are the present \(Q\) and \(V\); its horizontal tilt vector \(a\) is the present nonnegative vector \(a'\), not the original unrestricted tilt vector. Its \(b,c,R,T,P,D,\widetilde w,\widetilde W\) are respectively \(b',c',R_{\mathrm{aux}},T_{\mathrm{aux}},P',D',\widetilde w',\widetilde W'\). In particular, its requirement of complete integer support is the equality just proved. Source centers and displacements are the present transformed centers \(J'\) and transformed displacements \(\eta\). Define its pair quantities on \(D'\) with primes: \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}'_{J'}\), \(\widetilde\alpha'_{J'}(\eta)\), \(\widetilde\Delta'_{J'}(\eta)\), and \(\widetilde B_{J'}^{\prime\widetilde\Delta}(L)\), by the formulas in the statement with \(D',\widetilde w'\) in place of \(D,\widetilde w\).

For every original center \(J\in D\), linearity and whole-lattice invertibility of \(\Phi\) give a bijection \(\xi\mapsto\eta:=\Phi\xi\) from \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\) onto \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}'_{\Phi J}\). Indeed, \(\Phi(J\pm\xi)=\Phi J\pm\Phi\xi\), and (6) identifies both endpoint supports. The equality of all transported weights gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\alpha'_{\Phi J}(\Phi\xi)&=\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi),\\
\widetilde\Delta'_{\Phi J}(\Phi\xi)&=\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi),\\
\widetilde B_{\Phi J}^{\prime\widetilde\Delta}(L)
&=\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)
\qquad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The map preserves zero and sends the negative of a displacement to the negative of its image. Thus (12) is an equality of full oriented sums, without discarding or identifying either orientation. It applies to every original reflected pair and every cutoff, including equality at that cutoff.

For completeness, the curvature in the original coordinates is exactly

\[
\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)
=F(t(J)+t(\xi))+F(t(J)-t(\xi))-2F(t(J))\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]

The original affine tilt cancels. The two endpoint totals belong to \(Q\), and their midpoint is \(t(J)\), so convexity of \(F\) proves the inequality. Equivalently, the second difference of the transformed \(V\) equals (13), because its added linear term also cancels. For each \(J\), the pair support is finite and contains zero; consequently there are finitely many curvature values, all nonnegative and including zero. Between successive values the cutoff sum is constant and division by \(1+L\) cannot increase it. Hence the maximum over \(L\ge0\) is attained at a curvature value, and the maximum over the finite set of centers is attained too.

Apply the source scalar bound, using \(k=g\) and the unchanged total dimension \(N\). It gives a transformed center and cutoff with score at least \(e^{-600}\widetilde W'/[(g+1)2^N]\). Pulling this center back by \(\Phi^{-1}\), using (12) and \(\widetilde W'=\widetilde W\), proves the first inequality in (1). The second follows from \(g+1\le3\).

We now transfer the actual body, without any condition on its fiber shapes. Extend the base map by the identity in the continuous coordinates: \(\widehat\Phi(z,y):=(\Phi z,y)\), and set \(\mathcal C':=\widehat\Phi(\mathcal C)\). This is a convex body because \(\widehat\Phi\) is an invertible linear map of the full ambient space. For every \(I\in\mathbb Z^N\), its fiber satisfies \(\mathcal C'_I=\mathcal C_{\Phi^{-1}I}\) as an actual subset of \(\mathbb R^d\). Therefore the transformed actual volume \(w'_I:=\operatorname{vol}_d(\mathcal C'_I)\) equals \(w_{\Phi^{-1}I}\). Its positive integer support is exactly \(D'\), and (2) becomes

\[
\frac{\mathsf K}{M}\widetilde w'_I\le w'_I\le
\mathsf K M\widetilde w'_I\qquad(I\in D').
\tag{14}
\]

The values of \(\mathsf K\) and \(M\) are unchanged. Write \(S':=\mathcal C'\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(W':=\sum_Iw'_I\). Lattice bijectivity gives \(S'=\widehat\Phi(S)\) and \(W'=W\). The preservation of each fiber's volume uses the identity map on its continuous coordinates; it does not assume that \(\Phi\) is an ambient Euclidean isometry.

Apply the actual-body assertion (4) of `ab4903f5a9a95d6c` to \(\mathcal C'\). Its \(C,C_z,w_z,S,W,h_S\) are now \(\mathcal C',\mathcal C'_I,w'_I,S',W',h_{S'}\); its continuous dimension \(d\), scale \(\mathsf K\), and comparison factor \(M\) are unchanged. The preceding scalar notation map remains in force, with \(k=g\), \(n=N-g\), and total integer dimension \(N\). The source coefficient called \(c(M)\) is exactly \(e^{-600}/[12M^2(1+4\log M)]\). Thus that assertion supplies one \(J'\in D'\) and one \(y^\star\in\mathcal C'_{J'}\), chosen before every closed halfspace, with

\[
h_{S'}((J',y^\star))
\ge\frac{e^{-600}}{12M^2(1+4\log M)(g+1)2^N}\,W'.
\tag{15}
\]

Set \(J:=\Phi^{-1}J'\). Then \(J\in D\), \(y^\star\in\mathcal C_J\), and \(\widehat\Phi(J,y^\star)=(J',y^\star)\). Every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((J,y^\star)\) corresponds under \(\widehat\Phi\) to the closed halfspace \(H':=\widehat\Phi(H)\) containing \((J',y^\star)\). This correspondence is bijective, because an invertible linear image of a closed halfspace is again a closed halfspace. For each corresponding pair, relabeling the integer fibers and leaving the continuous coordinates fixed gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^N}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in\mathcal C_z:(z,y)\in H\}\\
&=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^N}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in\mathcal C'_I:(I,y)\in H'\}
=\mathcal H_d(S'\cap H').
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Taking infima over the corresponding closed halfspaces proves \(h_S((J,y^\star))=h_{S'}((J',y^\star))\). Substituting \(W'=W\) in (15) proves the first inequality in (3), and \(g+1\le3\) proves the second. The transported point was fixed before \(H\), as required. Equation (16) includes vertical halfspaces, halfspaces whose boundary contains an entire fiber, and all nonempty zero-volume integer fibers outside the positive support. Neither their presence in \(S\) nor any boundary contribution is suppressed.

Finally consider the signed-knapsack specialization. If the coefficients take two distinct positive real values \(B,C\), form the two nonempty blocks according to those values and take \(Q:=\{(t_1,t_2)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2:Bt_1+Ct_2\le R\}\), with \(F=0\). This \(Q\) is closed, convex, and bounded because \(B,C>0\); it contains the integer point zero even when \(R=0\). Its resulting original support is exactly the stated knapsack support, because \(\sum_i\beta_i z_i=Bt_1(z)+Ct_2(z)\). If there is just one coefficient value \(B>0\), use one block and \(Q=[0,R/B]\), again with \(F=0\). These choices meet every hypothesis of (1) and (3) for arbitrary real \(B,C,R\) of the stated signs.

In these specializations (13) is zero for every valid reflected pair. Consequently, for every \(J\in D\) and every \(L\ge0\),

\[
\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)
=\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(0)
=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb Z^N}
\min\{\widetilde w_{J+\xi},\widetilde w_{J-\xi}\}.
\tag{17}
\]

The terms outside \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\) are zero by the prescribed extension of the table. The best curvature score for this center is therefore attained at \(L=0\) and equals its full reflection sum. Equation (1) proves (4), with both orientations and the zero term retained. If \(J\in\mathbb Z^N\setminus D\), it cannot be the midpoint of two points in \(D\): the nonnegative real knapsack region is convex and \(D\) is its complete integer intersection. Hence every summand at such a center is zero, which also justifies maximizing over all integer centers. The actual-body specialization follows immediately from (3). No rationality, divisibility, coefficient-multiplicity restriction, or assumption on the signs of \(a_i\) entered the argument.
