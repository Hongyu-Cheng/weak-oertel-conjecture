## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty. Let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^2$, $z\in P$, be convex bodies with interior, with support functions $h_z$ satisfying every Jensen support inequality
$$
h_z\ge\sum_{w\in P}p_wh_w,\qquad p_w\ge0,\quad \sum_w p_w=1,\quad \sum_w p_ww=z.
$$
Fix a closed normal interval $I=[a,b]$ with $0<b-a<\pi$. Suppose on $I$ the curvature measures $R_z=h_z''+h_z$ satisfy $c\nu\le R_z\le C\nu$ for some finite nonzero nonatomic positive measure $\nu$ and constants $0<c\le C<\infty$. Both endpoints of $I$ are inward accumulation points of $\operatorname{supp}\nu$. Fix a finite list of prescribed caps for each fiber. For each such cap, given by a fixed halfplane, suppose the entire exposed boundary arc over $I$ lies on one strict side of its cap line, at a uniformly positive distance. Let $B_z$ be the homogeneous solution of $B_z''+B_z=0$ with the same two endpoint values as $h_z$, and let $f_z=h_z-B_z\in H_0^1(I)$. Assume the tensors $(1,z)\otimes f_z$ are linearly independent.

There are arbitrarily close simultaneous replacements by actual convex bodies whose supports are piecewise homogeneous on a common finite mesh on $I$ and equal the old supports outside $\operatorname{int} I$. They preserve every Jensen support inequality, every individual area and every prescribed cap area. Endpoint facets are allowed. Zero-curvature endpoint portions may first be trimmed. If $\nu=0$ all the original supports are already homogeneous on $I$.

## Proof

**Sine interpolation and area.** The quadratic form $Q(f,g)=\int_I(f'g'-fg)\,d\theta$ is an inner product equivalent to the $H_0^1(I)$ norm by Poincaré's inequality since $b-a<\pi$. On a cell $[u,v]$ of a mesh, write $\delta=v-u$. The nodal sine interpolant of a function $k$ is
$$
(\mathcal P k)(\theta)=\frac{\sin(v-\theta)k(u)+\sin(\theta-u)k(v)}{\sin\delta}.
$$
It fixes homogeneous solutions of $k''+k=0$. Restricted to $H_0^1(I)$, $\mathcal P$ is the $Q$-orthogonal projection onto the piecewise homogeneous functions with zero endpoint values. The error vanishes at the nodes and cellwise integration by parts against a homogeneous test gives zero. These projections converge strongly to the identity as the maximum cell length tends to zero. For a smooth function this follows from the cell Green formula below and its first derivative. For a general $H_0^1$ function, use smooth approximation and the norm bound of orthogonal projection.

For a globally periodic support $h$, area is $\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(h^2-h'^2)\,d\theta$. Thus if a new support agrees with $h$ outside $\operatorname{int} I$ and has residual $\widetilde f=\widetilde h-B$ on $I$, its area minus the old area is
$$
-\frac12\bigl[Q(\widetilde f,\widetilde f)-Q(f,f)\bigr].
$$
Indeed, the cross term with $B$ vanishes by $B''+B=0$ and the zero endpoint values of both residuals. In particular the uncorrected interpolation increases area by $\frac12 Q(f-\mathcal P f,f-\mathcal P f)$. The global area formula accounts for any facets introduced at $a,b$.

**Cell estimates and actual support functions.** If $k''+k=R$ on a cell with nonatomic finite signed measure $R$, then $k-\mathcal P k=-\int G_{uv}(\,\cdot\,,t)\,dR(t)$, where
$$
G_{uv}(s,t)=\frac{\sin(\min(s,t)-u)\sin(v-\max(s,t))}{\sin\delta}.
$$
This is the Dirichlet Green kernel of $-D^2-1$: it vanishes at the endpoints, is homogeneous off the diagonal and has a derivative jump of $-1$ there. It is strictly positive for both arguments interior. Also $\|G_{uv}\|_\infty=O(\delta)$ and its one-sided derivatives in the first argument are uniformly bounded as $\delta\to0$. The support and both one-sided derivatives of the interpolant therefore converge uniformly on the cells for any fixed nonatomic $R$ on $I$, since the maximum $|R|$-mass of a cell tends to zero. For bounded curvature density these two errors are $O(\delta^2)$ and $O(\delta)$, respectively. Differentiation at the left endpoint gives
$$
(\mathcal P k)'(u^+)-k'(u)=\int_{[u,v]}\frac{\sin(v-t)}{\sin\delta}\,dR(t).
$$
At the right endpoint, $k'(v)-(\mathcal P k)'(v^-)$ is the same integral with $\sin(t-u)$ in the numerator. Thus an interior derivative jump of $\mathcal P k$ is the sum of these weighted integrals from its two adjacent cells. At a global endpoint $a,b$, just the one inner cell contributes the derivative jump beyond the unchanged outer derivative of an originally differentiable support. The same formulas apply to a signed source in the interior, with absolute jumps bounded by the integrals for its total variation.

For an original support $h$, testing a maximizing point at $\theta$ in the two adjacent directions gives $h(\theta-t)+h(\theta+t)\ge2\cos(t)h(\theta)$. Dividing by $t^2$ and passing to distributions gives $h''+h\ge0$. Conversely, a continuous periodic candidate support with nonnegative distribution $h''+h$ is a support function. For a smooth function with positive $h''+h$, the curve $x(\theta)=h(\theta)u(\theta)+h'(\theta)u'(\theta)$, $u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, has $x'=(h''+h)u'$. For each $\alpha$, the derivative of $u(\alpha)\cdot x(\theta)$ has sign $\sin(\alpha-\theta)$, so the maximum is attained at $\alpha$ and is $h(\alpha)$. This proves the support criterion in the smooth case and gives the area formula by integrating $\frac12\det(x,x')$ and then integrating by parts. For the nonsmooth case, convolve periodically with nonnegative smooth approximate identities and add positive constants tending to zero. The smooth supports converge uniformly and their $H^1$ convergence gives the area formula by continuity of area for convex bodies under Hausdorff convergence. The uniform support limit is a support function by the closedness of sublinearity of its homogeneous extension. A support uniformly close to the original contains a smaller concentric copy of an original interior ball.

**Choose the end cells.** For a cell $[u,v]$ the $Q$-energy of the interpolation error equals
$$
\iint_{[u,v]^2} G_{uv}(s,t)\,dR_z(s)dR_z(t).
$$
Indeed $Q(e,e)=-\int e\,dR_z$ for the zero-endpoint error $e$, and substitute the Green representation. For the left end cell of length $\delta$ the reference endpoint jump for $\nu$ is
$$
\eta_a(\delta)=\int_{[a,a+\delta]}\frac{\sin(a+\delta-t)}{\sin\delta}\,d\nu(t).
$$
For the right end define $\eta_b(\delta)$ by reflection. Each is continuous for sufficiently small positive $\delta$ and tends to zero as $\delta\downarrow0$, using nonatomicity and boundedness of the kernel there. It is strictly increasing: for fixed $t>a$ in the old interval, the derivative of the weight with respect to $\delta$ is $\sin(t-a)/\sin^2\delta>0$, and the inward accumulation gives positive mass in every such interval. New mass has nonnegative weight. Choose first and last cell lengths $\delta_a,\delta_b$ with $\eta_a(\delta_a)=\eta_b(\delta_b)=\eta$ as $\eta\downarrow0$. They tend to zero. Every interpolated fiber has endpoint jumps at least $c\eta$.

On the first cell, restrict the Green double integral for $\nu$ to $s\le t$, use $\sin(s-a)\le\delta$, and bound the integral in $s$ by $\nu([a,a+\delta])$. By symmetry the reference energy is at most $2\delta\nu([a,a+\delta])\eta_a(\delta)$. The analogous estimate holds at $b$, and each $R_z$ energy is bounded by $C^2$ times the reference energy. The combined end-cell error is therefore $o(\eta)$. Refine the intervening cells until their combined error energy is at most $\eta^2$ for each fiber, and the maximum cell length tends to zero. For each fixed pair of end cells this is possible by the strong projection convergence applied to the interior interval after subtracting its own homogeneous endpoint solution. Write $F_z=\mathcal P f_z$ and $d_z=Q(f_z-F_z,f_z-F_z)/2$. The preceding estimates give $\max_z d_z=o(\eta)$.

**Correct all areas without changing the interpolated gaps.** Put $e(z)=(1,z)$ and
$$
A_{zw}=(e(z)\cdot e(w))Q(F_z,F_w),\qquad
\Psi_z=\sum_{w\in P}\xi_w(e(z)\cdot e(w))F_w.
$$
The Gram matrices $A$ converge to the positive definite Gram matrix of the assumed independent tensors, so $A^{-1}$ is uniformly bounded. The sine coefficients of interpolation are nonnegative, so all Jensen gaps of $B_z+F_z$ are nonnegative by their nodal values. Since $\Psi_z$ depends affinely on $z$, its contribution to every Jensen gap is identically zero. This includes gaps with any contact set.

The area formula requires $A\xi+q(\xi)=d$, where $q_z(\xi)=Q(\Psi_z,\Psi_z)/2$. Its quadratic coefficients are uniformly bounded since the $F_w$ are bounded in $Q$. If $d=0$ take $\xi=0$. Otherwise the map $\xi\mapsto A^{-1}(d-q(\xi))$ maps the closed Euclidean ball of radius $M\|d\|$ into itself for small enough $\|d\|$ and fixed $M>2\sup\|A^{-1}\|$. In fact the nonlinear term there is $O(\|d\|^2)$ and its Lipschitz constant is $O(\|d\|)$. The contraction theorem gives a solution $\|\xi\|=O(\|d\|)=o(\eta)$, restoring every area.

For each interior mesh node the jump of $F_w$ is that of the interpolated $h_w$, since $B_w$ is homogeneous. At a fixed node these nonnegative jumps are mutually comparable by a factor $C/c$, because they integrate the same nonnegative weights against $R_w$. The change of each jump due to $\Psi_z$ is bounded by a fixed constant times $\|\xi\|$ times the original interpolated jump of fiber $z$, even when the latter is zero. The endpoint derivatives of every $F_w$ are uniformly bounded by the cell estimates and the fixed $B_w$. Extending $\Psi_z$ by zero outside $I$ changes endpoint jumps by $O(\|\xi\|)=o(\eta)$, less than $c\eta$ for small enough $\eta$. The resulting piecewise homogeneous supports have nonnegative curvature globally. Their values and one-sided derivatives converge uniformly on $I$ to the old ones by the cell estimates, the boundedness of the interpolated derivatives, and $\xi\to0$. This gives actual planar bodies and polygonal arcs by the support criterion. If needed, trim to the first and last points of $\operatorname{supp}\nu$. There is no mass at the trimmed endpoints and the support is homogeneous on the removed portions. The tensor condition is invariant under trimming. In each affine coordinate a linear combination of the residuals with zero endpoint values vanishes exactly when its $D^2+1$ source vanishes, since the homogeneous zero-boundary problem on an interval of length less than $\pi$ has only the zero solution. Before and after trimming, the same linear combinations of the sources vanish. The trimmed arc remains in the original cap guards.

If all the original supports are $C^2$ on a neighborhood of $I$ and their curvature radii are strictly positive on $I$, take $\nu$ to be Lebesgue measure on $I$. Finite family size and compactness give a common positive lower bound and finite upper bound, and both endpoints have inward support accumulation.

**Caps and fixed endpoints.** At angle $\theta$ the exposed face consists of the points $h(\theta)u(\theta)+s u'(\theta)$ with $h'_-(\theta)\le s\le h'_+(\theta)$. The derivative extremes follow by taking the directional derivatives of the maximum over the body. Thus the uniform convergence of support values and one-sided derivatives on $I$ keeps the whole new arc, including any endpoint facets, on the same strict side of every prescribed cap line as the old arc. Faces at normals outside $I$ coincide, and the original arc endpoints are unchanged on the exterior sides of the endpoint faces, since the exterior derivatives and support values at $a,b$ are unchanged. Traverse the two boundaries counterclockwise. After cancelling their common exterior path, the difference is the closed path made of the new arc and the reversed old arc. For any point off the boundaries the difference of the two interior indicators is its winding number around this path. The path lies in one open halfplane of each cap line and can be contracted within it, so the indicator difference vanishes almost everywhere in the opposite halfplane. The cap area change is consequently zero if the arcs are outside the cap, and is the full area change if the arcs are inside. Both vanish.

**Placed fibers.** Support inequalities $h_z\ge\sum_w p_wh_w$ for every $p_w\ge0$ with $\sum_w p_w=1$ and $\sum_w p_w w=z$ are precisely the anchored Minkowski inclusions. With these inequalities and actual compact convex fibers established, the exact-hull-sections assertion of fact ba0f3db1bf4f62c6 applies to the finite real base $P$ with $d=2$. The hull of the placed new fibers has those exact sections at $P$. Its projection is $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, so if $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, it has exactly the prescribed integer fibers. The hull is full-dimensional in $\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^2$: a simplex of affinely independent bases gives each of its base sections a ball of a common positive radius, with its center depending affinely on the base, by combining interior balls from its vertices.
