## Statement

There exists a full-dimensional maximal lattice-free polyhedron $L\subset\mathbb R^4$ with sixteen facets such that its only lattice points are sixteen distinct relative-interior facet witnesses, one on each facet. Their convex hull is a full-dimensional empty lattice polytope of lattice width two. Here lattice-free means that $\operatorname{int} L$ contains no point of $\mathbb Z^4$, and maximality is by inclusion among convex lattice-free sets.

## Proof

Use the sixteen points $p_i$ and integer exposing functionals $n_i$ from the width-two hull predecessor. Put $Q=\operatorname{conv}\{p_i\}$ and $L=\{x:n_i\cdot(x-p_i)\le0\text{ for every }i\}$. The full-dimensional $Q$ is contained in $L$. For each $i$, $p_i$ is tight in row $i$ and strictly satisfies every other row by strict exposure. A neighborhood of $p_i$ inside its equality hyperplane therefore lies in $L$, while moving slightly along $n_i$ violates only row $i$. Each row is an irredundant facet inequality, and $p_i$ lies in its facet's relative interior. There are no other facets because a relative interior point of any facet must lie on an active defining hyperplane.

Let $x\in\mathbb Z^4$ and let $p_{[x]}$ be the point of the same parity. Apply the termination conclusion of abb6c0fd2ba74ca0 to the compact convex source $\operatorname{conv}(Q\cup\{x\})\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R$, with roots $p_i$ and all anchors zero. Every parity is represented and the root hull has exactly these lattice points, so its hypotheses hold. The iterates $x_{j+1}=(x_j+p_{[x_j]})/2$, starting at $x_0=x$, terminate at some $p_s$. Rearranging the accepted terminal barycenter gives
$$
x-p_s=\sum_{j<k}2^j(p_s-p_{[x_j]}).
$$
For $x\ne p_s$ at least one digit differs from $p_s$. Strict exposure then gives $n_s\cdot(x-p_s)>0$. Thus every lattice point of $L$ is one of the witnesses, each on the boundary.

If $x\notin L$, take a violated row $i$. For small $\alpha>0$, the point $r=(p_i-\alpha x)/(1-\alpha)$ satisfies every row strictly: row $i$ becomes strict in the inward direction and all other strict slacks persist near $p_i$. An interior ball around $r$ in $L$ gives an interior ball around $p_i=(1-\alpha)r+\alpha x$ in $\operatorname{conv}(L\cup\{x\})$. Any convex proper enlargement of $L$ contains such an $x$ and therefore fails lattice-freeness. This proves maximality.
