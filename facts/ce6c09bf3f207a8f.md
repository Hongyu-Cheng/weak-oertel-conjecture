## Statement

For each integer $N\ge1$, put $R_0=(N+1)^2$ and $w_i=R_0-i^2$ for $-N\le i\le N$. The centered interval body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ of width given by piecewise-linear interpolation of these samples is full-dimensional and has exactly these positive integer fibers. Its mixed centroid has base zero. Its sampled volume-root roof for $d=1$ has exactly $2N$ maximal affine patches, the consecutive unit intervals. Every affine global majorant of the roots contacts at most two sites, adjacent if there are two, whose total mass fraction is at most
$$
\frac{6(N+1)}{(2N+1)(2N+3)}\ \longrightarrow\ 0.
$$
Thus the mass of the largest single contact patch can be an arbitrarily small fraction of the total already for $n=d=1$.

## Proof

The successive slopes of the interpolated width are $-(2i+1)$, strictly decreasing. The width is positive even at the endpoints. Concavity of the width shows that the upper boundary given by half the width is concave and the lower boundary given by its negative is convex. Their region is compact, full-dimensional, and has precisely the displayed integer sections. Reflection in the base coordinate preserves all the widths, so the mixed centroid has base zero.

The width interpolation equals the sampled roof. Every mixture of the sample values is at most the interpolation by concavity, and the adjacent two-point representation attains the interpolation on each unit interval. Strictly decreasing slopes show exactly $2N$ maximal affine intervals. An affine majorant cannot touch nonadjacent samples, since an intervening sample is strictly above their affine chord. Its contact mass is thus at most $2R_0$. The total mass is
$$
\sum_{i=-N}^N(R_0-i^2)=(2N+1)(N+1)^2-\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}3
=\frac{(2N+1)(N+1)(2N+3)}3.
$$
Divide the contact mass bound by this total to obtain the claimed fraction.
