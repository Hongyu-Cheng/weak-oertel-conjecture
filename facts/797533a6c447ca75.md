## Statement

Put $A=(2,-1)$, $B=(-1,2)$, $C=(-1,-1)$, $T=\operatorname{conv}(A,B,C)$, $\sigma=1/100$, and $D_\sigma=(1-\sigma)A+\sigma T$. Consider the actual lifted convex hull with $T$ at zero and $D_\sigma$ at bases $\pm1$, with summed area and pooled within-fiber covariance norm $\Gamma$. For the outward uniform ramp of width $h>0$, a nonvertical cap through $Y=(0,y)$ with base coefficient $a$ and continuous normal $u\ne0$ captures density $\min(1,\max(0,1+(az+u\cdot(X-y))/(h\Gamma(u))))$. Pure-base caps are unchanged.

At the unique deepest mark for sufficiently small $h$, take any finite probability menu of actual minimizing ramps that balances their own-strip coarea times their unit-normalized continuous response, equivalently the vectors $k_u u$ in d1db032130242f2d. As $h\downarrow0$, the deepest marks have limiting barycentrics $(1-2t,t,t)$, where $t=(1+\sqrt{1-3\sigma^2})/6$. Within each of the three active vertex cones, the own cut converges to the corresponding unique section-centroid cap at that mark, and the three aggregate menu weights converge to
$$
\left(\frac{4t-1}{6t-1},\frac{t}{6t-1},\frac{t}{6t-1}\right).
$$
There is a nonnegative field continuous in the outer normal and convex in the physical coordinates for each fixed normal, with measured trace equal to a positive constant on the entire middle facet $x_1=-1$ and zero elsewhere. Its response-weighted capture efficiency, the field-weighted captured boundary area divided by total field-weighted boundary area with length measure on each fiber boundary, tends to
$$
\frac{4t^2}{6t-1}=\frac49+\frac{4(3t-1)^2}{9(6t-1)}>\frac49 .
$$
In particular it is strictly above $4/9$ for all sufficiently small positive $h$ at this fixed $\sigma$, uniformly over the balanced response menus.

## Proof

The actual fibers, unique deepest mark, active cone confinement, and endpoint masses and coareas follow from c3f4b82d42e475b2. In the scale $\max\lbrace u\cdot A,u\cdot B,u\cdot C\rbrace=2$ the other two vertex values are $-1\pm s$ with $|s|<1/4$. The deepest mark has both coordinates bounded by $2(v+4h)$, where $v=|T|\sigma^2$. Hence it stays in a compact region near the centroid. Each active cap in the $A$ cone captures exactly $v$ on the two endpoints together, and the $B,C$ cone caps capture zero there. All endpoint strip coareas are zero. The balanced response menus exist, have positive mass in every cone, and use the vectors $k_u u$ with $19/10<k_u<21/10$, by c3f4b82d42e475b2 and d1db032130242f2d.

The affine vertex permutation interchanging $B$ and $C$ and fixing $A$ preserves the centered triangle, both endpoint triangles and the base. It preserves volume since it permutes the vertices, and preserves the ramp law after covector transport since the within-fiber covariance transforms by congruence. Uniqueness of the deepest mark therefore gives equal $B,C$ barycentric coordinates at every $h$.

For an active cap, put $r=(az+u\cdot(X-y))/(h\Gamma(u))$. Keeping its base coefficient $a$ fixed, vary $u$ in its unnormalized open domain $u\ne0$. The actual minimizing cap is stationary there. Differentiating the ramp under the finite area integral, its kink lines being null, gives
$$
y=\frac{\int_{-1<r<0}(X-hr\nabla\Gamma(u))\,d\mu}{\int_{-1<r<0}d\mu}.
$$
This is the normal stationarity calculation in c3f4b82d42e475b2. Only the middle strip enters the integrals. In the stated cones and mark range, its entire width cuts just the top vertex, including along convergent sequences in the closed cone range. Indeed the other vertex values are at most $-3/4$, the top value is $2$, and $|u\cdot y|\le4(v+4h)$ with $h\Gamma(u)<2h$. The section is a segment whose two endpoints depend continuously on its threshold and normal, with positive limiting length. By slicing in the $u$ coordinate, the normalized strip mean tends to the segment centroid whenever $y$ and the cone representative converge. The term $hr\nabla\Gamma(u)$ tends uniformly to zero since $\Gamma$ is a fixed covariance norm and $|r|<1$ in the strip. Every limiting active own cut therefore has centroid exactly the limiting mark.

For a fixed limiting mark the section-centroid cap is unique in each cone. On the two edges from its top vertex to the other vertices $j,k$, the endpoints must have barycentrics $2y_j$ and $2y_k$ at their respective other vertices, because the section midpoint is $y$. This also gives its relative area $(2y_j)(2y_k)$. Along any sequence $h\downarrow0$, take a convergent subsequence of marks and at least one active normal per cone, using the compact representative ranges. The common ramp mass has the same limit for all three normals. The middle ramps converge in area to their proper sharp cuts, and the endpoint contributions remain the exact values above. Writing the limiting barycentrics as $(1-2t,t,t)$ and dividing by $|T|$ gives
$$
4t^2+\sigma^2=4t(1-2t).
$$
The core bound on the mark puts $t$ near $1/3$, excluding the smaller root. Thus every subsequential mark limit has the displayed $t$, and in each cone all active own cuts converge to its one section-centroid cap. Compactness gives convergence uniformly over choices of active cap in a cone.

The scalar coareas $k_u$ of d1db032130242f2d converge to the proper section coareas in the same vertex scale, either by their formula or by strip slicing. Any subsequential limit of the three aggregate weights has sum one and balances the three sharp response vectors. Since no two closed cones can balance, all three weights are positive. In Euclidean unit normalization their products with the normals are the same response vectors, since coarea rescales inversely to the normal. Apply the own-section formula of 000f61e3347cadf2 with $r_i=1-2y_i$, own fractions $q_A=4t^2$, $q_B=q_C=4t(1-2t)$ and weights proportional to $r_i/q_i$. Normalizing gives exactly the three displayed weights. Thus this is the only subsequential limit of any response menus.

Choose a continuous nonnegative bump $\chi$ on the normal circle with value one at $-e_1$ and zero at the two other facet normals of $T$. The field $\chi(n)[2-3\sigma-x_1]_+$ is convex in $(z,X)$ for each fixed $n$. The endpoints satisfy $x_1\ge2-3\sigma$, and the middle trace is zero off the chosen facet in boundary measure and constant $3-3\sigma$ on that facet. The limiting cap in the $A$ cone captures zero there. Each of the $B,C$ caps has relative captured length $2t$ there, by the edge-intercept formula. Ramp capture on the facet tends to the corresponding sharp lengths, by bounded convergence off the at most two intersections with each proper cut. The convergence is uniform over active choices in each cone by the preceding compactness and uniqueness. The resulting weighted capture is $2t$ times the sum of the $B,C$ weights, or $4t^2/(6t-1)$. Subtracting $4/9$ gives the displayed nonnegative square divided by $9(6t-1)$, with positive denominator and $t<1/3$ since $\sigma>0$. The uniform limit yields the strict small-width conclusion.
