## Statement

The random label \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab}}\) below is the object called \(\Lambda\) in the registered subgoal; it is unrelated to the mixed-integer lattice \(\Lambda\) in the locked problem. The letters \(W,V,N,T\) denote real scalar random variables, with \(T:=W+V+N\), rather than geometric objects or mixed-integer volumes.

Let \(K,K_0\ge1\) be finite. On a probability space, let \(Y\) be a latent random element, and let \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab}}\) be a random label with \(\mathcal G:=\sigma(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab}})\subseteq\sigma(Y)\). Suppose \(W,V\) are real, \(\sigma(Y)\)-measurable random variables, \(W\) is integrable, and the nonnegative conditional second moment \(v:=\mathbb E[W^2\mid\mathcal G]\) is finite almost surely. Assume
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[W\mid\mathcal G]&=0,\qquad
\mathbb E[W^4\mid\mathcal G]\le K_0v^2\quad\text{almost surely},\\
\mathbb E[N\mid\sigma(Y)]&=0,\qquad
\sigma^2:=\mathbb EN^2<\infty,\\
\mathbb EV&=0,\qquad
\mathbb EN^4\le K\sigma^4,\qquad
\mathbb EV^4\le K\sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The conditional second and fourth moments of \(W\) are understood as conditional expectations of nonnegative variables. No global second- or fourth-moment assumption on \(W\) is required. Then
\[
\min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}
\ge\frac{1}{2^{30}K^2K_0^4}.
\]
When \(\sigma=0\), the stronger bound \(1/(4K_0)\) holds.

For the independent-sum extension, let \(b\ge1\), let the full experiments \((Y_i,N_i)\), \(1\le i\le b\), be independent, and suppose that each row \((\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i},W_i,V_i,N_i)\) satisfies the preceding hypotheses with the same \(K,K_0\), with its label and \(W_i,V_i\) measurable in \(Y_i\), and with \(\sigma_i^2:=\mathbb EN_i^2\). Then
\[
\min\left\{
\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^b(W_i+V_i+N_i)\ge0\right),
\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^b(W_i+V_i+N_i)\le0\right)
\right\}
\ge\frac{1}{9\cdot2^{30}K^2\max\{K_0,3\}^4}.
\]
If \(\sum_i\sigma_i^2=0\), both probabilities are at least \(1/(4\max\{K_0,3\})\). If either supplied moment constant is any finite nonnegative number, all these assertions hold after replacing that constant by its maximum with \(1\).

## Proof

We first establish a conditional one-sided tail estimate. Conditional Hölder gives
\[
v\le\bigl(\mathbb E[|W|\mid\mathcal G]\bigr)^{2/3}
\bigl(\mathbb E[W^4\mid\mathcal G]\bigr)^{1/3}.
\]
Consequently, on \(\{v>0\}\),
\(\mathbb E[|W|\mid\mathcal G]\ge\sqrt v/\sqrt{K_0}\).
Write \(W_+:=\max\{W,0\}\) and \(W_-:=\max\{-W,0\}\). Conditional centering implies
\(\mathbb E[W_+\mid\mathcal G]=\mathbb E[W_-\mid\mathcal G]=\mathbb E[|W|\mid\mathcal G]/2\).
Splitting \(W_+\) at the \(\mathcal G\)-measurable threshold \(\sqrt v/(4\sqrt{K_0})\) and applying conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt v}{2\sqrt{K_0}}
\le\mathbb E[W_+\mid\mathcal G]
\le\frac{\sqrt v}{4\sqrt{K_0}}
+\sqrt v\,
\Pr\left(W>\frac{\sqrt v}{4\sqrt{K_0}}\,\middle|\,\mathcal G\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The same calculation for \(W_-\) proves, on \(\{v>0\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left(W>\frac{\sqrt v}{4\sqrt{K_0}}\,\middle|\,\mathcal G\right)&\ge\frac1{16K_0},\\
\Pr\left(W<-\frac{\sqrt v}{4\sqrt{K_0}}\,\middle|\,\mathcal G\right)&\ge\frac1{16K_0}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These conditional inequalities are valid even when global moments of \(W\) are infinite: they may first be applied on the \(\mathcal G\)-measurable events \(\{v\le j\}\), for positive integers \(j\), where the conditional fourth-moment hypothesis makes the restricted fourth moments finite, and then extended over their increasing union.

For the zero-noise case, \(\sigma=0\) implies \(N=V=0\) almost surely. Without truncating the positive part, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz yields, on \(\{v>0\}\),
\[
\frac{\sqrt v}{2\sqrt{K_0}}
\le\mathbb E[W_+\mid\mathcal G]
\le\sqrt v\,\Pr(W>0\mid\mathcal G)^{1/2},
\]
and similarly \(\Pr(W<0\mid\mathcal G)\ge1/(4K_0)\). On \(\{v=0\}\), \(W=0\) almost surely, so both conditional closed-sign probabilities equal \(1\). Integration proves both closed-cap bounds \(1/(4K_0)\).

Now assume \(\sigma>0\). Put \(R:=V+N\), and choose
\[
\delta:=\frac1{2^{20}K^2K_0^3},\qquad
\tau:=2^{19}K^{3/2}K_0^3,\qquad
A:=\{v\le\tau\sigma^2\},\qquad q:=\Pr(A^c),\qquad
\ell:=\frac{\sqrt\tau\,\sigma}{4\sqrt{K_0}}.
\]
The inequality \((a+b)^4\le8(a^4+b^4)\) gives \(\mathbb ER^4\le16K\sigma^4\). Since \(\ell^4=2^{30}K^3K_0^4\sigma^4\), Markov's inequality gives
\[
\Pr(|R|>\ell)\le\frac{16K\sigma^4}{\ell^4}
=\frac\delta{64K_0}.
\]
If \(q\ge\delta\), the conditional tail estimates imply
\(\Pr(W>\ell,A^c)\ge q/(16K_0)\) and
\(\Pr(W<-\ell,A^c)\ge q/(16K_0)\), because \(v>\tau\sigma^2\) on \(A^c\).
On \(\{W>\ell,|R|\le\ell\}\) one has \(T>0\), and on \(\{W<-\ell,|R|\le\ell\}\) one has \(T<0\). A union bound therefore gives, for either sign,
\[
\min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}
\ge\frac q{16K_0}-\frac\delta{64K_0}
\ge\frac\delta{32K_0}
=\frac1{2^{25}K^2K_0^4}.
\]
This proves more than required in this case.

It remains to treat \(q<\delta\). All subsequent moments of \(W\) are restricted to \(A\). Define
\(a_2:=\mathbb E[W^2\mathbf1_A]\),
\(M_2:=\mathbb E[T^2\mathbf1_A]\),
\(M_4:=\mathbb E[T^4\mathbf1_A]\),
\(M_1:=\mathbb E[|T|\mathbf1_A]\), and
\(m:=\mathbb E[T\mathbf1_A]\).
They are finite: conditional regularity gives \(\mathbb E[W^4\mathbf1_A]\le K_0\tau^2\sigma^4\), and the fourth moments of \(V,N\) are finite.

The event \(A\) lies in \(\mathcal G\subseteq\sigma(Y)\). Conditional centering of \(N\) gives
\(\mathbb E[(W+V)N\mathbf1_A]=0\). The coefficient \((W+V)\mathbf1_A\) is \(\sigma(Y)\)-measurable and square-integrable, so this identity follows first for bounded coefficients and then by truncation and Cauchy--Schwarz. Hence
\[
M_2=\mathbb E[(W+V)^2\mathbf1_A]+\mathbb E[N^2\mathbf1_A]
\ge\mathbb E[N^2\mathbf1_A].
\]
The lost noise variance satisfies
\[
\mathbb E[N^2\mathbf1_{A^c}]
\le(\mathbb EN^4)^{1/2}q^{1/2}
\le\sqrt{K\delta}\,\sigma^2
\le\frac{\sigma^2}{2},
\]
where \(\sqrt{K\delta}=2^{-10}K^{-1/2}K_0^{-3/2}\le1/2\).
Thus \(M_2\ge\sigma^2/2>0\).

The drift can cancel \(W\), but its second moment controls the cost of that cancellation. The pointwise inequality \(W^2\le2(W+V)^2+2V^2\) and \(\mathbb EV^2\le\sqrt K\,\sigma^2\) imply
\[
a_2\le2M_2+2\sqrt K\,\sigma^2\le6\sqrt K\,M_2.
\]
Using conditional regularity and \(v\le\tau\sigma^2\) on \(A\), we obtain
\[
\mathbb E[W^4\mathbf1_A]
\le K_0\mathbb E[v^2\mathbf1_A]
\le K_0\tau\sigma^2a_2
\le12\sqrt K\,K_0\tau M_2^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_4
&\le8\bigl(\mathbb E[W^4\mathbf1_A]+\mathbb ER^4\bigr)\\
&\le\bigl(96\sqrt K\,K_0\tau+512K\bigr)M_2^2\\
&\le128\sqrt K\,K_0\tau M_2^2
=D M_2^2,
\qquad D:=2^{26}K^2K_0^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate inequality uses \(\sqrt K\,K_0\tau=2^{19}K^2K_0^4\ge16K\). In particular \(M_4>0\), since \(M_2>0\).

Conditional centering of \(W\) and \(N\) on \(A\), together with \(\mathbb EV=0\), gives
\(m=\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_A]=-\mathbb E[V\mathbf1_{A^c}]\).
Hölder therefore gives \(|m|\le K^{1/4}\sigma q^{3/4}\le K^{1/4}\sigma\delta^{3/4}\).
Hölder on the restricted measure \(\mathbf1_A\,d\Pr\) also gives \(M_2\le M_1^{2/3}M_4^{1/3}\). Thus
\[
M_1\ge\frac{M_2^{3/2}}{\sqrt{M_4}}
\ge\frac{\sqrt{M_2}}{\sqrt D}
\ge\frac\sigma{\sqrt{2D}}
\ge2|m|.
\]
For the last inequality, the constants satisfy
\(2K^{1/4}\delta^{3/4}\sqrt{2D}=2^{-1/2}(KK_0)^{-1/4}\le1\).
Writing \(T_+:=\max\{T,0\}\) and \(T_-:=\max\{-T,0\}\), we have
\(\mathbb E[T_+\mathbf1_A]=(M_1+m)/2\ge M_1/4\) and
\(\mathbb E[T_-\mathbf1_A]=(M_1-m)/2\ge M_1/4\).
For each sign, Cauchy--Schwarz now implies
\[
\min\{\Pr(T\ge0,A),\Pr(T\le0,A)\}
\ge\frac{M_1^2}{16M_2}
\ge\frac{M_2^2}{16M_4}
\ge\frac1{16D}
=\frac1{2^{30}K^2K_0^4}.
\]
Taking the unrestricted events proves the scalar assertion. The cutoff uses the closed event \(A\) and its strict complement. All estimates hold with atoms, and no conditional or global symmetry was used.

For the sum extension, define \(W_\Sigma:=\sum_iW_i\), \(V_\Sigma:=\sum_iV_i\), \(N_\Sigma:=\sum_iN_i\), and \(\sigma_\Sigma^2:=\sum_i\sigma_i^2\). Take the product latent element \((Y_1,\ldots,Y_b)\) and the label vector \((\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},1},\ldots,\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},b})\). Write \(\mathcal G_\Sigma\) for the sigma-algebra generated by the latter and \(v_i:=\mathbb E[W_i^2\mid\sigma(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i})]\).

Independence of the full row experiments implies conditional independence of the latent rows given their label vector. This follows by testing against products of bounded functions of the individual labels, using independence to factor expectations, and extending to the sigma-algebra of the label vector by the monotone class theorem. Conditional centering and the fourth-power expansion therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[W_\Sigma\mid\mathcal G_\Sigma]&=0,\\
\mathbb E[W_\Sigma^2\mid\mathcal G_\Sigma]&=\sum_i v_i,\\
\mathbb E[W_\Sigma^4\mid\mathcal G_\Sigma]
&=\sum_i\mathbb E[W_i^4\mid\sigma(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i})]
+6\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j\\
&\le K_0\sum_i v_i^2+6\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j
\le\max\{K_0,3\}\left(\sum_i v_i\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the second- and fourth-moment identities, one can first restrict to label rectangles \(\{v_i\le j\}\) for all rows and positive integers \(j\), where every required moment is integrable, and then take their increasing union. Thus no global second or fourth moment of any \(W_i\) is being assumed. The sum \(W_\Sigma\) is integrable and its conditional second moment is finite almost surely.

Independence similarly implies \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid\sigma(Y_1,\ldots,Y_b)]=0\): for bounded measurable tests \(h_j\),
\[
\mathbb E\left[N_i\prod_jh_j(Y_j)\right]
=\mathbb E[N_ih_i(Y_i)]\prod_{j\ne i}\mathbb E[h_j(Y_j)]=0,
\]
and products of latent indicator tests extend to all bounded tests by the monotone class theorem. Hence \(N_\Sigma\) is conditionally centered and \(\mathbb EN_\Sigma^2=\sigma_\Sigma^2\). The centered independent variables \(N_i\) satisfy
\[
\mathbb EN_\Sigma^4
=\sum_i\mathbb EN_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2
\le3K\sigma_\Sigma^4.
\]
Each \(V_i\) is centered and \(\mathbb EV_i^2\le\sqrt K\,\sigma_i^2\). The analogous fourth-power expansion therefore gives
\[
\mathbb EV_\Sigma^4
\le K\sum_i\sigma_i^4+6K\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2
\le3K\sigma_\Sigma^4,
\qquad \mathbb EV_\Sigma=0.
\]
The scalar assertion applies with constants \(3K\) and \(\max\{K_0,3\}\), and gives exactly the stated sum bound. Its zero-noise assertion gives the stated bound when \(\sigma_\Sigma=0\). Finally, increasing either nonnegative supplied constant to its maximum with \(1\) preserves every hypothesis, proving the normalization assertion.
