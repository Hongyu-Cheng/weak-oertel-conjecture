## Statement

Let a positive finite Borel measure on $(z,u,x)$ be supported on at most $J$ coarse levels $z$, with $u\in\mathbb R^r$, $r\ge1$. Let $\eta_u$ be the largest mass fraction on $b\cdot u=c$ with $b\ne0$, and let $\eta_c$ be the largest on $a\cdot z+b\cdot u=c$ with $b\ne0$. Then
$$
\eta_u\le\eta_c\le\min(1,J\eta_u).
$$
For the pushed-forward normalized measure, $\eta_c$ is invariant under invertible affine changes with $z'=Uz+z_0$ and $u'=Au+Bz+u_0$, where $U,A$ are invertible. With uniformly bounded $J$, either fraction tends to zero exactly when the other does. The factor $J$ is attained by a full-dimensional mixed body for each integer $J\ge2$.

## Proof

The first family is the subfamily with $a=0$. On each fixed level $z$, a coupled hyperplane is contained in the corresponding fine-only hyperplane $b\cdot u=c-a\cdot z$. Its mass there is at most the mass on that fine-only hyperplane summed over all levels, bounded by $\eta_u$ times the total mass. Summation proves the upper bound; probability also gives the bound one.

For completeness the suprema in these families are attained. Take a maximizing sequence and normalize $\lVert b\rVert=1$. Pass to a subsequence so the normals converge and the thresholds $c-a\cdot z$ converge in the extended reals at each of the finitely many occupied levels. At a level with divergent threshold the indicators converge pointwise to zero. At a finite-limit level the limsup indicator is bounded by the indicator for the limiting normal and threshold. The vector of finite-limit thresholds remains in the closed finite-dimensional image of $(a,c)\mapsto(c-a\cdot z)_z$ on those levels, so it is realized by one coupled hyperplane. Reverse Fatou bounds the limiting masses by its mass. The same argument restricted to $a=0$ proves attainment for the fine-only family.

Pullback takes the fine coefficient $b$ of a coupled normal to $A^{\mathsf T}b$. The inverse change has the same triangular form, so these hyperplanes correspond bijectively and their pushed-forward fractions agree. In particular, this statement allows changes that send a common lattice coset to different offsets on different coarse levels.

For sharpness use $k=r=1$, any positive-dimensional compact convex body $K$ with nonempty interior in the $x$-coordinates, and the full-dimensional convex body $0\le z\le J-1$, $|u-z|\le\varepsilon$, $x\in K$, with $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. At integer $(z,u)$ there are exactly $J$ equal positive fibers, at $(j,j)$ for $j=0,\ldots,J-1$. Each fine-only hyperplane meets at most one of them, and $u-z=0$ contains all of them. Hence $\eta_u=1/J$ and $\eta_c=1$.
