## Statement

Let $d\ge1$. Let $P\subset\mathbb R^r$ be finite and affinely spanning, with the origin in the relative interior of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$. For each $p\in P$, let $K_p$ be a compact convex set of positive $d$-volume. Suppose the family is compatible, meaning that the convex hull of $S=\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$ has exactly $K_p$ as its fiber at $p$. Write $\mu=\sum_p\delta_p\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_p})$ and $M=\mu(S)$. For an affine $L$ positive on $P$, take the unweighted fiber-volume measure of the family obtained from $S$ by $(p,x)\mapsto(p/L(p),x/L(p))$. Assume its maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth is locally minimized at $L=1$ among positive affine denominators. Write $\beta$ for this maximum at $L=1$ and $Q=\beta M$.

Index the deepest bases by $p_i$. At each one choose any exact positive circuit of nonvertical closed caps
$$
H_{ij}=\{(p,x):a_{ij}\cdot p+u_{ij}\cdot x\ge b_{ij}\},\qquad
\|u_{ij}\|=1,\qquad \mu(H_{ij})=Q,
$$
whose boundaries pass through a common deepest mark $(p_i,x_i)$ and whose weights satisfy $\rho_{ij}>0$, $\sum_j\rho_{ij}=1$, and $\sum_j\rho_{ij}u_{ij}=0$. At every other base use a fixed circuit covering the full fiber plane with each cap of mass strictly below $Q$. Such choices exist.

For each selected critical cap, let $\theta^{\rm pr}_{ij}$, $\theta^{\rm in}_{ij}$ and $\theta^{\rm out}_{ij}$ be the sums of $(d-1)$-dimensional section area on its proper sections, inside supporting sections (the fiber is contained in the closed cap), and outside supporting sections (the cap meets the fiber in a supporting face of zero $d$-volume), respectively. These are measures on the lifted sections in the original coordinates, with zero-area sections contributing zero. For $d=1$ the section measure is counting measure.

For every such choice of circuits and every Borel system $\sigma^{\rm in}_{ij}\in[0,1]$ on the inside supporting sections, there exist outside densities $\sigma^{\rm out}_{ij}\in[0,1]$ and nonnegative multipliers $\lambda_{ij}$. Put
$$
\zeta_{ij}=\theta^{\rm pr}_{ij}+\sigma^{\rm in}_{ij}\theta^{\rm in}_{ij}
                     +\sigma^{\rm out}_{ij}\theta^{\rm out}_{ij},
\qquad S_{ij}=\zeta_{ij}(\mathbb R^{r+d})>0.
$$
One such choice satisfies the linear identity
$$
-d\int A(p)\,d\mu
=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}\left[-d\int_{H_{ij}} A(p)\,d\mu
                             +\int \ell_{ij}(p,x)\,d\zeta_{ij}\right]
\tag{1}
$$
simultaneously for every signed affine $A$ and every signed cap velocity $\ell_{ij}(p,x)=\delta a_{ij}\cdot p+\delta u_{ij}\cdot x-\delta b_{ij}$ satisfying, for every $i$,
$$
\sum_j\rho_{ij}\delta u_{ij}=0,\qquad
\sum_j\rho_{ij}(\delta a_{ij}\cdot p_i-\delta b_{ij})=0.
\tag{2}
$$
In particular, the same certificate gives
$$
\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}=\frac1\beta,\qquad
\int A(p)\,d\mu=\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}\int_{H_{ij}} A(p)\,d\mu
\quad\text{for every affine }A,\qquad
\lambda_{ij} S_{ij}=c_i\rho_{ij}\quad(c_i\ge0).
\tag{3}
$$
Consequently each group has either all multipliers positive or all zero. Equivalently, $\lambda_{ij}=\pi_i\kappa_{ij}/\beta$, where $\pi_i=\beta\sum_j\lambda_{ij}$ are nonnegative and sum to one, and $\kappa_{ij}=(\rho_{ij}/S_{ij})/\sum_l(\rho_{il}/S_{il})$. The same choice of outside allocations and multipliers works for all tests in (1)–(3) for the prescribed inside system.

## Proof

**Projective law and circuit choices.** Write $L(p)=b+a\cdot p$, where $b>0$ because the origin belongs to $\operatorname{conv}(P)$. The inverse map at image coordinates $(z,y)$ is $(z,y)\mapsto b(z,y)/(1-a\cdot z)$ on the chart $1-a\cdot z>0$. The map sends convex hulls in the positive chart to the convex hulls of the images: the image of a convex combination with coefficients $\eta_j$ uses coefficients proportional to $\eta_j L(p_j)$, and the inverse has the same property. The base map is injective by its inverse, so the image of the hull has the specified fibers $K_p/L(p)$ at its sampled bases. Dividing or multiplying an affine margin by the positive chart denominator takes halfspace traces to halfspace traces, with the trivial full or empty traces also realizable by halfspaces on the bounded sampled sets. Each individual $d$-fiber scales by $L(p)^{-1}$, so its unweighted image volume pulls back to $d\mu_L=L(p)^{-d}\,d\mu$. Thus local minimality is exactly local minimality of maximum normalized depth on the fixed sampled set $S$ under these weighted measures.

The circuit theorem applies to the finite compatible family and gives exact caps at each deepest base, and fixed cheaper full-plane covers at the nondeep bases by taking their local maximum depths. By the positive-depth theorem every deepest mark is interior: an interior ball in a positive fiber gives positive depth, so $Q>0$. A nonvertical cap through an interior mark cuts its own fiber properly, with strictly positive $(d-1)$-area. Thus every $\zeta_{ij}$ has $S_{ij}>0$, independently of both supporting allocations. If $r=0$, one can apply these two predecessors after putting the single base at $0$ in a dummy one-dimensional base coordinate. Restricting caps to the sampled plane gives the same depth since proper trace halfspaces extend and containing trivial traces have full mass.

**Signed density and cap variation.** Let $\mathcal V$ be the finite-dimensional linear space of the signed parameters in (1) subject to (2). For a direction in $\mathcal V$ and sufficiently small $t>0$, set $L_t=1+tA$ and move each critical cap by adding the margin $t\ell_{ij}$. Positivity of $L_t$ follows from finiteness of $P$. The moved caps remain nonvertical for small $t$. On the plane above $p_i$, the $\rho_{ij}$-weighted sum of the original margins vanishes by the common mark and the normal balance. Equations (2) make the weighted sum of the added margins vanish identically on that plane. Thus the moved closed caps cover it exactly, since at least one margin is nonnegative at each point. The nondeep cover caps stay fixed and keep strict slack at the budget $Q$ under $\mu_{L_t}$ by continuity of its finitely many weights.

For a fixed original cap with unit continuous normal $u$, write its threshold at $p$ as $\tau=b-a\cdot p$. On a line $x=v+su$, $v\in u^\perp$, the new threshold in $s$ is exactly
$$
\tau-\frac{t\,\ell(p,v+\tau u)}{1+t\,\delta u\cdot u}.
\tag{4}
$$
The shift is uniformly $O(t)$ on the projection of the bounded fiber. For a nonempty slice interval $[l,h]$, the retained length at cutoff $c$ is $[h-\max(l,c)]_+$. If $l<\tau<h$, its right derivative at $t=0$ with (4) is $\ell(p,v+\tau u)$. At $l=\tau<h$ it is $\min(0,\ell(p,v+\tau u))$, and at $l<\tau=h$ it is $\max(0,\ell(p,v+\tau u))$. For $\tau$ strictly outside the interval or a degenerate interval the derivative is zero. Difference quotients are uniformly bounded by (4) and the length function's Lipschitz bound.

At a proper cut, relative interior points of the section belong to the body interior: extend the segment from a body-interior point on the plane through a given relative interior point to another section point, and use convexity. At a supporting section, almost every point of a positive-area section has a nondegenerate normal slice. Indeed the projection of the full-dimensional fiber is full-dimensional in $u^\perp$, and every interior projection point is the projection of a body-interior point. For the latter assertion, extend in the projected body a segment from the projection of an interior point past the point in question and lift the far endpoint into the body. A section of positive area can intersect the boundary of the projection only on a set of projected measure zero. The projection of the cap plane in the fiber onto $u^\perp$ is an isometry. For $d=1$ this argument reduces to slicing the single nondegenerate interval, with its zero-dimensional projection given mass one. Fubini and dominated convergence therefore give the exact three section integrals.

In addition, $L_t(p)^{-d}=1-dtA(p)+O(t^2)$ uniformly over the bases, and the moving trace changes fiber volume by $O(t)$, by (4). The product of the changed density and the changed trace volume contributes $O(t^2)$. The full right derivative for each critical cap is consequently
$$
-d\int_{H_{ij}}A(p)\,d\mu
+\int\ell_{ij}\,d\theta^{\rm pr}_{ij}
+\int\min(0,\ell_{ij})\,d\theta^{\rm in}_{ij}
+\int\max(0,\ell_{ij})\,d\theta^{\rm out}_{ij}.
\tag{5}
$$
This holds for every signed direction of $\mathcal V$ with $t\downarrow0$. The total mass derivative is $L_0(A,\delta)=-d\int A(p)\,d\mu$.

**One certificate for the prescribed inside system.** Keep the given inside densities fixed. For each critical cap let $G_{ij}\subset\mathcal V^*$ consist of the forms
$$
g_{ij}(A,\delta)=-d\int_{H_{ij}}A(p)\,d\mu+\int\ell_{ij}\,d\zeta_{ij}
$$
as the outside density varies in $[0,1]$. This is a compact convex set. The admissible outside densities are a weak-star closed subset of the unit ball of $L^\infty(\theta^{\rm out}_{ij})$, and evaluation on a basis of $\mathcal V$ uses finitely many integrable test functions. Any admissible density has a Borel version up to a null set. The right derivative (5) is bounded above by $\sup_{g\in G_{ij}} g(A,\delta)$, since $\min(0,\ell)\le\sigma^{\rm in}_{ij}\ell$ pointwise for either sign and the outside branch is realized by the indicator of $\ell>0$. The inside system is fixed throughout this comparison.

Take $e\in\mathcal V$ with $A=1$ and every cap velocity zero. Then $L_0(e)=-dM$ and $g(e)=-dQ$ for every form in every $G_{ij}$. The cone of the union of these finitely many compact convex sets is closed: write a conic combination as $\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}g_{ij}$ with one form per group and cap index by convexity, then evaluation at $e$ bounds the sum of coefficients along any convergent sequence in the cone. Pass to subsequences of the finitely many bounded coefficients and compact form choices to obtain a conic representation of the limit.

If $L_0$ were outside this closed convex cone, separation would give a direction $v\in\mathcal V$ with $L_0(v)>0$ and $g(v)\le0$ for every form in the union. Replace $v$ by $v+\varepsilon e$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. Its total mass derivative remains strictly positive and every form now evaluates to at most $-\varepsilon dQ<0$. Thus every selected critical cap has strictly negative right derivative in this direction by (5). For sufficiently small positive $t$, the total mass is above $M$, the moved critical caps have mass below $Q$, and the unchanged nondeep caps retain their slack. These finitely many caps still cover every point of $S$. The new maximum normalized depth is therefore at most $Q/\mu_{L_t}(S)<\beta$, contradicting the local orbit minimum. Hence $L_0$ belongs to the cone. Choosing its representation as above and taking one outside density for each representing form proves (1) with the same multipliers and section measures for all directions.

Evaluate (1) at $e$ to obtain $\sum\lambda_{ij}=M/Q=1/\beta$. Take arbitrary affine $A$ with all cap velocities zero to get the bulk identities in (3). With $A=0$ and only threshold motions in group $i$, (2) reduces to $\sum_j\rho_{ij}\delta b_{ij}=0$. Equation (1) says $\sum_j\lambda_{ij} S_{ij}\delta b_{ij}=0$ on that subspace, so the vector $(\lambda_{ij}S_{ij})_j$ is $c_i(\rho_{ij})_j$ for some $c_i\ge0$. Positivity of each $S_{ij}$ and $\rho_{ij}$ gives the group conclusion, and normalizing the group sums gives the formulas for $\pi_i$ and $\kappa_{ij}$.
