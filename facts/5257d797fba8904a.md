## Statement

Let $T$ be a $4$-simplex with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$, $H=\lambda_0+\lambda_1$, $D_\delta=T\cap\lbrace H\ge l\rbrace $, $l=1-\delta$, $0<\delta<1$, and put $D_\delta,T,D_\delta$ at bases $-1,0,1$. Work at width zero with raw volumes divided by $|T|$. At a symmetric mark $y=(x,x,z,z,z)$, $x,z>0$, $2x+3z=1$, consider the zero-base-tilt low cap $\lambda_i+cH\ge z+2cx$ for $i=2,3,4$. Set $u=1-c>0$, $r=2z+2xu$, $t=r/u$, and assume $0<r<\min(1,u)$. Its own and each endpoint mass are respectively
$$
q_L=u^2t^4,\qquad q_D=u^2(t-l)_+^3(t+3l).
$$
The total is $Q_L=u^2 A(t)$, $A(t)=t^4+2(t-l)_+^3(t+3l)$. For fixed $c$, the endpoint mass is convex in the cut threshold throughout the strict vertex chamber; zero base tilt minimizes the sum over opposite endpoint shifts that stay in this chamber. An interior stationary zero-tilt cap satisfies $A'(t)(t-2x)=2A(t)$. For $t\le l$ this gives $t=4x$; for $t>l$ it gives
$$
3t^4-12xt^3+(24xl^2-8l^3)t-16xl^3+6l^4=0,\qquad
x=\frac{3t^4-8l^3t+6l^4}{4(3t^3-6l^2t+4l^3)}.
$$

Suppose a high cap is a strict vertex cap at its own-section-centroid direction and its base tilt saturates one endpoint and misses the other. Its own fraction is $q_H=256xz^3$, total mass $q_H+\alpha$, $\alpha=4\delta^3-3\delta^4$, and at zero base tilt each endpoint costs $q_H F(\delta/(4z))$, where $F(s)=4s^3-3s^4$ on $[0,1]$ and $F(s)=1$ on $[1,\infty)$. Thus an actual five-cap branch of the two saturated high and three zero-tilt low caps requires $\alpha\le2q_HF(\delta/(4z))$ and $Q_L=q_H+\alpha$ as well as the stationary equation.

If the five post-contact caps with $t>l$ are actual minima, their high caps use strict endpoint saturation and miss margins, and their symmetry-averaged balanced menu has positive per-cap weights $w_H,w_L$, $2w_H+3w_L=1$, then in the chosen low-cap affine scale the coarea is $S_L=u A'(t)=Q_L/z$ and
$$
\frac{w_H}{w_L}=\frac{S_L(1-3c)}{q_H(2/z-1/x)}.
$$
A field constant in physical coordinates at the normal of $F_0$ and zero at the other measured facet normals has efficiency
$$
R_H=\frac{w_H(4z)^3+3w_Lu^2t^3+2w_H\delta^3+6w_Lu^2(t-l)^3}{1+2\delta^3}.
$$
After retargeting both saturated high caps to base $-1$, the field supported in normal at the truncating facet and multiplied by the convex physical function $(-p)_+$ of the base coordinate has efficiency
$$
R_E=2w_H+3w_L\left(\frac{u(t-l)}{\delta}\right)^2\ge2w_H.
$$
A below-$1/e$ best-menu capacity on this branch requires both ratios strictly below $1/e$.

## Proof

The vertex deficit at $i$ is $r$ and the drops in $\lambda_i+cH$ along the incident full edges are $u,u,1,1$. Thus the intercepts are $t,t,r,r$, giving the own volume by a simplex determinant. In the normalized cap barycentrics $H=tU$ where $U$ is the sum of two coordinates and has density $12s(1-s)^2$ on $[0,1]$: the separated simplices of two and three coordinates give the factors $s$ and $(1-s)^2$. Its upper tail is $(1-s)^3(1+3s)$. This gives the endpoint formula, including its zero at $t=l$, and then the total using both endpoints. In terms of variable deficit $r$, the endpoint cost is $(r-ul)_+^3(r+3ul)/u^2$. Above $ul$ its second derivative is $12(r^2-u^2l^2)/u^2$ and below it is zero; the function and its first two derivatives join continuously. The strict vertex chamber is an interval in $r$, so convexity and midpoint Jensen apply to opposite shifts in that interval. No conclusion is drawn about a shift leaving the chamber.

Use $u=2z/(t-2x)$, so $Q_L=4z^2 A(t)/(t-2x)^2$ with chamber $2x/(1-2z)<t<1$ (equivalent to $r<1$ and $t<1$). Differentiation gives the stationary equation. For $t\le l$, $A=t^4$ with derivative $4t^3$ even at $l$, yielding $t=4x$. For $t>l$ expand $A=3t^4-12l^2t^2+16l^3t-6l^4$ and substitute. The coefficient denominator is positive there: after division by $l^3$, $3(t/l)^3-6(t/l)+4$ is one at $t/l=1$ and strictly increasing for $t/l\ge1$.

For the high cap the own-section-centroid rule makes its four incident intercepts $4x,4z,4z,4z$: each coordinate other than that of the retained vertex comes from exactly one section vertex. The product gives $q_H$. Its low-coordinate sum is $4z$ times the sum of three reference barycentrics. That sum has density $12s^2(1-s)$ by the same split, hence cumulative function $F$. Saturation is impossible as an actual minimum if zero tilt has strictly smaller cost, and equality with an active low cap gives the common-mass condition. Equality in the zero-tilt check means only a tie with that competitor.

Differentiation with respect to the low deficit at fixed $u$ gives its coarea $S_L=uA'(t)$ in the relative-volume scale, with both endpoint terms included. Stationarity and $u(t-2x)=2z$ give $S_L=Q_L/z$. The sum of the two high coarea response vectors in barycentric coordinates has coefficient $-q_H/x$ in each high coordinate and $-2q_H/z$ in each low coordinate: a cap at $i$ has response coefficients $-q_H/y_k$ for $k\ne i$ and zero at $i$ in the margin scale $1-\sum_{k\ne i}\lambda_k/(4y_k)$. The sum of the three low vectors has coefficients $3cS_L$ and $S_L$ respectively. Balance on the coordinate-sum-zero tangent space equates the two types after multiplication by their weights. The strict chamber $t<1$ implies $u>2/3$, using $1-2x=3z$, hence $1-3c>0$. Positive weights and the balance therefore force $2/z-1/x>0$, and division gives the ratio.

On $F_0$ the high cap at vertex $1$ has trace fraction $(4z)^3$, and the high cap at $0$ misses. Each low cap trace has intercepts $t,r,r$ and hence fraction $u^2t^3$. The endpoint patch of $F_0$ has relative area $\delta^3$ by a corner homothety at vertex $1$. Each high cap captures one full endpoint patch, for total weighted fraction $2w_H\delta^3$. For each low trace only one high coordinate survives, and its reference simplex barycentric tail is $(1-s)^3$; imposing $H\ge l$ gives relative area $u^2(t-l)^3$ in the whole $F_0$. The two patches and three low caps give the stated numerator; the denominator is the sum of all measured areas at that facet normal, divided by $|F_0|$.

At $H=l$ the truncation facet is an affine product of a high segment and a low triangle with total low coordinate $\delta$. Its normalized area has uniform low marginal. The inequality on $\lambda_i$ gives a corner triangle of linear ratio $u(t-l)/\delta$. This ratio is in $(0,1)$: if $u\le1$ use $t<1$, and if $u>1$ use $r<1$ to get $u(t-l)=r-ul<1-l$. The strict high endpoint margins leave zero outside coarea in this response calculation. Retargeting is allowed because finite sufficiently large signed tilts saturate either prescribed endpoint with the same active mass and with zero endpoint coarea, leaving the continuous response unchanged. The truncating facet normal is distinct from all $F_i$ normals. The two specified normal selectors can be taken continuous and nonnegative because there are only finitely many distinct measured normals. The first is constant in physical coordinates; the second isolates the left truncating facet since $(-p)_+$ is convex and vanishes at bases zero and one. The weighted fraction of its area captured is exactly $R_E$. Taking the two fields, with retargeting for the second, gives the necessary tests for best-menu capacity.
