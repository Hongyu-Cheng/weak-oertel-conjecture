## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
 \qquad
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 W:=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z|_d.
\]
Assume \(S\ne\varnothing\). For \(p\in S\), define
\[
 h_S(p):=\inf\left\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\right\},
 \qquad
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\]
If \(W=0\), every point of \(S\) has depth at least zero. Suppose henceforth that \(W>0\), and define
\[
 D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:|C_z|_d>0\},
 \qquad
 B:=\operatorname{aff}(D_+),
 \qquad
 \rho:=\dim B.
\]
Also put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 B_d:=d2^d\int_0^1\frac{x^{d-1}}{(1+x)^d}\,dx,
\]
and
\[
 \alpha_d:=\frac12\left[
 \gamma_d-(1-\gamma_d)\log\frac1{1-\gamma_d}
 \right].
\]
Then there exists \(p\in S\) satisfying the following strongest applicable bound:

1. If \(\rho=0\), then
\[
 h_S(p)\ge \gamma_d W.
\tag{1}
\]

2. If \(\rho=1\), then
\[
 h_S(p)\ge
 \max\left\{\frac1{2(d+1)},\alpha_d\right\}W.
\tag{2}
\]

3. If \(\rho=2\), then
\[
 h_S(p)\ge
 \max\left\{
 \frac1{4(d+1)},
 \frac{\gamma_d}{256},
 \frac{\gamma_d}{144B_d}
 \right\}W.
\tag{3}
\]

4. If \(\rho\ge3\), then
\[
 h_S(p)\ge
 \max\left\{
 \frac1{2^\rho(d+1)},
 \frac{\gamma_d}{B_d\rho2^{2\rho-1}(\rho+1)^\rho},
 \frac{\gamma_d}{B_d2^{2\rho}(\rho+2)^\rho}
 \right\}W.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, for every integer \(R\ge0\), define
\[
 c_R:=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac1{2e},&R=0,\\[4pt]
 \dfrac1e-\left(1-\dfrac1e\right)\log\dfrac e{e-1},&R=1,\\[7pt]
 \dfrac1{64e},&R=2,\\[7pt]
 \displaystyle
 \max\left\{
 \dfrac1{eR2^R(R+1)^R},
 \dfrac1{e2^{R+1}(R+2)^R}
 \right\},&R\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(0<c_R<1/e\), and every instance with \(\rho\le R\) has a point \(p\in S\) such that
\[
 h_S(p)\ge c_R2^{-n}W.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, if \((C_j)\) is a sequence of positive-mass instances with integer dimensions \(n_j\) and
\[
 \frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j}\longrightarrow0,
\]
then
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(D_{+,j})\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{7}
\]
The constants \(c_R\) in (5) tend to zero as \(R\to\infty\); therefore (6) does not supply one absolute constant for arbitrary \(\rho\), and this theorem is not a final resolution of the locked problem.

## Proof

Because \(C\) is compact, only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty. Since \(W>0\), the finite set \(D_+\) is nonempty. We first construct and audit an intrinsic lattice chart because the absence of an index, Jacobian, or codimension loss is essential to all four bounds.

Choose affinely independent points
\[
 z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_\rho\in D_+.
\]
Every \(C_{z_i}\) is a compact convex set of positive \(d\)-volume, hence has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^d\). Choose \(x_i\in\operatorname{int}(C_{z_i})\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 x_0+\varepsilon e_j\in C_{z_0}
 \qquad(1\le j\le d).
\]
The \(\rho+d\) differences from \((z_0,x_0)\) to
\[
 (z_i,x_i)\quad(1\le i\le\rho),
 \qquad
 (z_0,x_0+\varepsilon e_j)\quad(1\le j\le d)
\]
are linearly independent: their horizontal components first force the coefficients of the first \(\rho\) vectors to vanish, and their vertical components then force the remaining coefficients to vanish. Thus
\[
 K:=C\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)
\tag{8}
\]
is a compact convex body of relative dimension \(\rho+d\) in \(B\times\mathbb R^d\).

The group
\[
 \Gamma:=(B-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n
\tag{9}
\]
is discrete and contains the \(\rho\) linearly independent vectors \(z_i-z_0\). Since it lies in the \(\rho\)-dimensional vector space \(B-z_0\), it is a full lattice there of rank exactly \(\rho\). Moreover, directly from (9),
\[
 B\cap\mathbb Z^n=z_0+\Gamma.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_\rho\) of \(\Gamma\), with the empty basis when \(\rho=0\), and set
\[
 Uu:=\sum_{i=1}^\rho u_i g_i,
 \qquad
 \Phi(u,x):=(z_0+Uu,x).
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(\Phi\) is an affine bijection from \(\mathbb R^\rho\times\mathbb R^d\) onto \(B\times\mathbb R^d\), and (10) gives the exact lattice identity
\[
 \Phi(\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^d)
 =(B\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{12}
\]
Define
\[
 K':=\Phi^{-1}(K),
 \qquad
 S':=K'\cap(\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{13}
\]
The relative full-dimensionality of \(K\) proves that \(K'\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{\rho+d}\).

For every \(u\in\mathbb Z^\rho\), the restriction of \(\Phi\) to the continuous fiber over \(u\) is the identity map on \(\mathbb R^d\). Thus it preserves the \(d\)-dimensional volume of that fiber exactly. There is no determinant involving \(U\), because mixed-integer measure does not integrate in the discrete directions. Every positive-volume lattice fiber of \(C\) is indexed by a point of \(D_+\subseteq B\), while every lattice fiber indexed outside \(B\) has zero \(d\)-volume. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal H_d(S')
 =\sum_{z\in B\cap\mathbb Z^n}|C_z|_d
 =W.
\tag{14}
\]

We next audit transfer of an intrinsic depth point back to the ambient instance. Suppose \(p'\in S'\) has depth at least \(\beta W\) in \(S'\), and put \(p:=\Phi(p')\in S\). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any closed halfspace containing \(p\), and put
\[
 J:=\Phi^{-1}\bigl(H\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
The affine functional defining \(H\), restricted to \(B\times\mathbb R^d\) and pulled back by \(\Phi\), is either nonconstant or constant. In the first case \(J\) is a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{\rho+d}\); in the second case it is the whole space or empty. It cannot be empty because it contains \(p'\). Hence the intrinsic depth bound applies when \(J\) is proper, while the whole-space case has mass \(W\ge\beta W\) for every coefficient used below. Fiberwise volume preservation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
 &\ge
 \mathcal H_d\!\left(
 C\cap H\cap((B\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d)
 \right)\\
 &=\mathcal H_d(S'\cap J)
 \ge\beta W.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The first inequality only discards nonnegative contributions from fibers outside \(B\); all such fibers have zero full-fiber mass in any event. Equations (12), (14), and (16) prove that neither a lattice index, a continuous Jacobian, ambient codimension, nor closed-halfspace boundary mass is lost.

It remains to apply the strongest accepted intrinsic theorem in each rank. For every \(\rho\ge1\), accepted fact \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\) applies to the nonzero finite mixed-volume measure of the convex body \(K'\subseteq\mathbb R^{\rho+d}\), supported on \(\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^d\). Its total mass is exactly \(W\) by (14), so it supplies a point in \(S'\) of intrinsic depth at least
\[
 \frac{W}{2^\rho(d+1)}.
\tag{17}
\]
The applicability clause in that accepted fact also ensures that the point belongs to \(K'\), not merely to the ambient mixed-integer space. We will choose, in each rank, the point supplied by whichever accepted bound has the largest coefficient and then use (16).

If \(\rho=0\), then \(K'\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body of volume \(W\). Apply accepted fact \(6769574fc1bd9f8d\) with metronoid mass \(m=W\). The only admissible density is one almost everywhere, so the full-mass metronoid consists of the centroid of \(K'\), and the accepted metronoid-Grunbaum inclusion says that every closed halfspace containing that point has volume at least \(\gamma_dW\). Transfer by (16) proves (1).

If \(\rho=1\), accepted fact \(758103b1282d6b91\), applied to the convex body \(K'\subseteq\mathbb R^{1+d}\), supplies a point of intrinsic depth at least \(\alpha_dW\). Choosing between this point and the point from (17), then transferring by (16), proves (2).

If \(\rho=2\), accepted fact \(daef2580cdfec07b\) supplies a point of intrinsic depth at least
\[
 \frac{\gamma_d}{256}W.
\tag{18}
\]
Independently, accepted fact \(70fe7ecc186e568c\), specialized to integer dimension two, supplies a point of intrinsic depth at least
\[
 \frac{\gamma_d}{B_d}
 \max\left\{\frac1{2\cdot2^3\cdot3^2},
 \frac1{2^4\cdot4^2}\right\}W
 =\frac{\gamma_d}{144B_d}W.
\tag{19}
\]
Choose the point given by whichever of (17), (18), and (19) has the largest coefficient, and transfer it by (16). This proves (3).

If \(\rho\ge3\), apply accepted fact \(70fe7ecc186e568c\) to \(K'\) with integer dimension \(\rho\). Choosing between its point and the point from (17), then transferring by (16), gives exactly (4).

We now prove the uniform-rank-cap consequence. For \(R=0\), (1), \(\gamma_d>1/e\), and \(n\ge1\) imply (6) with \(c_0=1/(2e)\). For \(R=1\), accepted fact \(758103b1282d6b91\) gives the uniform inequality
\[
 \alpha_d\ge\frac12\left[
 \frac1e-\left(1-\frac1e\right)\log\frac e{e-1}
 \right]=\frac{c_1}{2}.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(n\ge1\), (1), (2), and (20) prove (6). For \(R=2\), (3) gives in particular
\[
 h_S(p)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{256}W>\frac1{256e}W.
\tag{21}
\]
Because \(n\ge\rho\), (1), (2), and (21) imply (6) with \(c_2=1/(64e)\). The exact expression for \(c_1\) lies in \((0,1/e)\) by accepted fact \(758103b1282d6b91\), and the same property is immediate for \(c_0,c_2\).

For an integer \(q\ge3\), put
\[
 \widetilde c_q:=
 \max\left\{
 \frac1{eq2^q(q+1)^q},
 \frac1{e2^{q+1}(q+2)^q}
 \right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
The strict universal consequence in accepted fact \(70fe7ecc186e568c\), applied intrinsically and then transferred by (16), gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)>\widetilde c_q2^{-q}W
 \qquad(\rho=q\ge3).
\tag{23}
\]
Both denominators in (22) strictly increase with \(q\), so \(\widetilde c_q\) is nonincreasing. Hence, if \(3\le q\le R\), then \(\widetilde c_q\ge\widetilde c_R=c_R\), while \(n\ge q\), and (23) gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)>c_R2^{-n}W.
\tag{24}
\]
By the definition of the supremum, (24) supplies a point whose depth is strictly larger than the right-hand side, and hence supplies the non-strict assertion (6).
For \(R\ge3\), one has
\[
 c_R\le c_3=\frac1{1536e}<\frac1{64e}<c_1<\frac1e,
\]
so the already proved ranks zero, one, and two also satisfy (6) with this \(c_R\). This proves (5) and (6) for every \(R\ge0\).

Finally, if the ranks in a sequence satisfying the hypothesis before (7) did not tend to infinity, some subsequence would have \(\rho_j\le R\) for a fixed finite \(R\). Equation (6) would then give
\[
 \frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j}\ge c_R>0
\]
on that subsequence, contradicting convergence to zero. This proves (7). The formula in the last branch of (5) visibly tends to zero with \(R\), so it does not furnish an all-rank constant and cannot be a final proof of the locked statement.
