## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), and put \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). Write \(C_i=\{\mathbf x:(i,\mathbf x)\in C\}\), \(a_i=\mathcal L^2(C_i)\), \(r_i=\sqrt{a_i}\), and \(W=\mathcal H_2(S)=\sum_i a_i\). Assume exactly four integer fibers have positive area. There exists one \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge2W/9\).

The following complete scalar alternative is also proved. Let \(\mathbf v=(u,1,b,v)\) at indices \(0,1,2,3\), and zero elsewhere, where

\[
u,v\ge0,\qquad \frac12\le b\le1,\qquad u+b\le2,\qquad v\le2b-1.
\tag{1}
\]

Put \(U=u^2\), \(B=b^2\), \(V=v^2\), and \(T=T_{\mathbf v}=U+1+B+V\). For \(j\in\{1,2\}\), \(0\le s\le v_j^2\), \(A>0\), and \(\kappa>2/9\), use exactly the retained-area envelope, quadratic test, and rational tests of accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)
&=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\sum_i
\min\{v_i^2,(\sqrt s+\beta(i-j))_+^2\},\\
\mathrm Q(\mathbf v,j):\quad
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)
&\le\frac{5T}{9}+\frac{9s^2}{4v_j^2}
\quad\left(0\le s\le\frac{2(\sqrt{10}-1)}9v_j^2\right),\\
\mathrm R_\kappa(\mathbf v,j):\quad
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)
&\le\frac{5T}{9}+P_{\kappa,v_j^2}(s)
\quad\left(0\le s\le\frac{\kappa v_j^2}{3\kappa/2+2/3}\right),\\
P_{\kappa,A}(s)
&=\frac{s\bigl(s+(\kappa/2-1/3)A\bigr)}{\kappa A-s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Either some entry has square at least \(T/2\), or an interior anchor satisfies one of these tests. More specifically, in the absence of such a heavy entry:

- If \(u\ge b\), then \(\mathrm R_{8/9}(\mathbf v,1)\) holds.
- If \(u\le b\) and \(4(U+1-B)\le5V\), then \(\mathrm Q(\mathbf v,2)\) holds.
- If \(u\le b\) and \(4(U+1-B)>5V\), then \(\mathrm R_{2/3}(\mathbf v,1)\) holds when \(b\le19/20\), and \(\mathrm R_{8/9}(\mathbf v,1)\) holds when \(b\ge19/20\).

These conclusions include all zero-entry, equality, and clipping boundaries in (1). The scalar area at anchor one is one, the area at anchor two is \(B\), and no scalar area is identified with an unnormalized actual area without the explicit scaling at the end of the proof.

## Proof

We first prove the scalar alternative, and then use the exact normalization and fixed-fiber transfer in accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`. All auxiliary inequalities below are proved directly. In particular, no finite enumeration or computational certificate is a premise.

We will use the following elementary neighboring-pair bound. Suppose the terms at the two neighboring indices \(j-1\) and \(j+1\) have root caps \(m\le M\), and the cap line has root \(x\ge0\) at the anchor. Their sum of retained areas is at most

\[
M^2+\min\{m^2,(2x-M)_+^2\}.
\tag{3}
\]

If one retained root is zero, their sum of squares is at most \(M^2\). Otherwise their retained roots have sum at most \(2x\). When \(2x\le M\), their square sum is at most \(4x^2\le M^2\). When \(2x\ge M+m\), the two caps prove (3). In the remaining interval, maximization along the root-sum boundary is a convex quadratic, so one of the two cap endpoints maximizes it. The difference between saturating the larger cap and saturating the smaller cap is \(2(M-m)(M+m-2x)\ge0\). This proves (3), including zero and equal caps. It applies to every slope and therefore to envelope upper bounds.

For \(A>0\) and \(0\le s\le A\), a second elementary inequality is

\[
s+(2\sqrt s-\sqrt A)_+^2-\frac{9s^2}{4A}\le\frac A9.
\tag{4}
\]

To prove it, set \(x=\sqrt{s/A}\). The right side minus the left side, divided by \(A\), equals \((3x^2/2-1/3)^2\) for \(0\le x\le1/2\). For \(1/2\le x\le1\), it equals \((3x-2)^2(9x^2+12x-8)/36\). The last quadratic is increasing and has value \(1/4\) at \(x=1/2\). Both expressions are nonnegative.

For unit anchor area, abbreviate the two rational functions by their parameters in subscripts:

\[
P_{2/3,1}(s)=\frac{s^2}{2/3-s}
\quad(0\le s\le2/5),\qquad
P_{8/9,1}(s)=\frac{s(9s+1)}{8-9s}
\quad(0\le s\le4/9).
\tag{5}
\]

All their denominators are positive on these ranges. The following bounds will be used repeatedly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{8/9,1}(s)&\ge\frac94s^2\ge s-\frac19,\\
P_{8/9,1}(s)&\ge2s-\frac5{12},\\
P_{2/3,1}(s)&\ge s-\frac7{60}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For the first line, subtracting the quadratic gives \(s(9s-2)^2/[4(8-9s)]\ge0\), and \(9s^2/4-s+1/9=(3s/2-1/3)^2\). For the second line, set \(\omega=8-9s>0\). Polynomial division gives
\(P_{8/9,1}(s)-(2s-5/12)=(4\omega^2-39\omega+96)/(12\omega)>0\), because the numerator has discriminant \(-15\) and positive leading coefficient. For the last line, multiplication by the positive denominator gives \(2s^2-(47/60)s+7/90\), whose discriminant is \(-31/3600\). This proves (6).

We next establish the one-variable inequalities needed when \(u\le b\). Define two explicit lower bounds

\[
\zeta_1(b)=\frac{2-b^2}{9},\qquad
\zeta_2(b)=\frac{10}{9}-b^2-(2b-1)^2
=\frac19+4b-5b^2.
\tag{7}
\]

We claim

\[
\begin{aligned}
s+(2\sqrt s-b)_+^2-P_{2/3,1}(s)
&\le\zeta_2(b)
&&\left(\frac12\le b\le\frac{63}{80},\ 0\le s\le\frac25\right),\\
s+(2\sqrt s-b)_+^2-P_{2/3,1}(s)
&\le\zeta_1(b)
&&\left(\frac{63}{80}\le b\le\frac{19}{20},\ 0\le s\le\frac25\right),\\
s+(2\sqrt s-b)_+^2-P_{8/9,1}(s)
&\le\zeta_1(b)
&&\left(\frac{19}{20}\le b\le1,\ 0\le s\le\frac49\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Here is a complete analytic verification. Write \(x=\sqrt s\). On a region with \(b\le2x\), the difference between the right side and left side of any line of (8) is a concave quadratic in \(b\). Its quadratic coefficient is \(-6\) for \(\zeta_2\), and \(-10/9\) for \(\zeta_1\). For each fixed \(x\), its minimum on the allowed interval of \(b\) therefore occurs at an endpoint. The endpoints are the displayed fixed values of \(b\), or \(b=2x\). This reduction preserves the exact clipping boundary.

Consider first the \(P_{2/3,1}\) lines. In the nonclipped region \(2x\le b\), (6) bounds the left side by \(7/60\). Throughout \([63/80,19/20]\),
\(\zeta_1(b)\ge\zeta_1(19/20)=439/3600>7/60\).
On \([1/2,63/80]\), the concavity of \(\zeta_2\) bounds it below by its endpoint minimum. At the left endpoint \(\zeta_2(1/2)=31/36\), and at the right endpoint

\[
\zeta_2(63/80)-\zeta_1(63/80)=\frac{101}{14400}>0.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus \(\zeta_2\) is also greater than \(7/60\) on that interval. This handles every nonclipped point and every variable endpoint \(b=2x\).

At the fixed endpoint \(b=1/2\), in the clipped region, \(x\le\sqrt{2/5}<2/3\) and (6) give
\(s+(2x-1/2)^2-P_{2/3,1}(s)\le25/36+7/60=73/90<31/36=\zeta_2(1/2)\).

For the other two fixed endpoints of these lines, define the polynomial obtained by multiplying the desired \(\zeta_1\) difference by its positive denominator:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr N_b(x)
&=(2-3x^2)
\left(P_{2/3,1}(x^2)-x^2-(2x-b)^2+\zeta_1(b)\right)\\
&=18x^4-12bx^3+\frac{10b^2-32}{3}x^2+8bx
+\frac{4-20b^2}{9}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

At \(b=63/80\), put \(y=x-1/2\). Direct expansion gives

\[
\mathscr N_{63/80}(x)
=\frac{47}{4608}-\frac{743}{1920}y
+\frac{8113}{1920}y^2+\frac{531}{20}y^3+18y^4.
\tag{11}
\]

For \(y\ge0\), the last two terms are nonnegative, and the first three form a strictly positive quadratic: its negative discriminant is equivalent to
\(3\cdot743^2=1656147<1906555=235\cdot8113\).
For \(b/2\le x\le1/2\), the derivative \(\mathscr N_b'\), with \(b=63/80\) fixed, is convex because \(\mathscr N_b'''(x)=432x-72b>0\). Its endpoint values are
\(\mathscr N_b'(b/2)=b(10b^2-8)/3<0\) and \(\mathscr N_b'(1/2)=-743/1920<0\).
It is therefore negative throughout that interval. The polynomial decreases to its positive value \(47/4608\) at \(x=1/2\). This proves \(\mathscr N_{63/80}(x)>0\) for all required \(x\ge b/2\). It also proves the \(\zeta_2\) endpoint inequality there by (9).

At \(b=19/20\), the value of \(\mathscr N_b\) at \(x=b/2\) is positive by the already checked nonclipped inequality. Moreover
\(\mathscr N_b'(b/2)=b(10b^2-8)/3>0\),
\(\mathscr N_b''(b/2)=(74b^2-64)/3>0\), and
\(\mathscr N_b'''(x)=432x-72b>0\) for \(x\ge b/2\).
The polynomial is consequently increasing and positive on the required interval. Concavity in \(b\), with all fixed and variable endpoints now checked, proves the first two lines of (8).

For the last line of (8), the nonclipped region and the variable endpoint \(b=2x\) follow from \(s-P_{8/9,1}(s)\le1/9\le\zeta_1(b)\). At \(b=1\), (4) and the first line of (6) give the full inequality. It remains to check \(b=19/20\) in the clipped region. The polynomial for this endpoint is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr M_b(x)
&=(8-9x^2)
\left(P_{8/9,1}(x^2)-x^2-(2x-b)^2+\zeta_1(b)\right)\\
&=54x^4-36bx^3+(10b^2-41)x^2+32bx
+\frac{16-80b^2}{9}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

With \(b=19/20\) and \(y=x-1/2\), it expands as

\[
\mathscr M_{19/20}(x)
=\frac{89}{1440}-\frac9{40}y-\frac{91}{40}y^2
+\frac{369}{5}y^3+54y^4.
\tag{13}
\]

If \(-1/40\le y\le0\), the linear and quartic terms are nonnegative, and the remaining terms give
\(\mathscr M_{19/20}(x)\ge89/1440-103/40000>0\).
If \(0\le y\le1/6\), use \(-91/40\ge-3\), \(369/5\ge72\), and

\[
72y^3-3y^2+\frac1{1296}
=\frac{(36y-1)^2(72y+1)}{1296}\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]

It follows that
\(\mathscr M_{19/20}(x)\ge89/1440-3/80-1/1296=61/2592>0\).
These two intervals are exactly \(19/40\le x\le2/3\). The denominator in (12) is positive there. The fixed endpoint is proved, and concavity in \(b\) proves the last line of (8). This completes the proof of all the inequalities in (8).

We now handle the case \(u\le b\). By (1), \(v\le1\). At anchor two, the opposite neighboring root caps are \(v\) and one, and the remaining root cap is \(u\). Equations (3) and (4), using \(s\le B\le1\), give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,2}(s)
&\le U+1+s+(2\sqrt s-1)_+^2\\
&\le U+1+\frac B9+\frac{9s^2}{4B}
\qquad(0\le s\le B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

In the second line, replace one in the positive part by \(b\le1\) and apply (4) with \(A=B\). Thus \(4(U+1-B)\le5V\) implies \(U+1+B/9\le5T/9\), proving the claimed quadratic test, in fact on its entire anchor-area range.

Suppose instead that \(4(U+1-B)>5V\) and no entry is heavy. Since the anchor-one square is one, \(T>2\), or \(U+B+V>1\). Put \(\zeta=5T/9-(B+V)=(5+5U-4B-4V)/9\). These two strict inequalities give

\[
\begin{aligned}
9\bigl(\zeta-\zeta_1(b)\bigr)
&=(U+B+V-1)+\bigl(4(U+1-B)-5V\bigr)>0,\\
\zeta
&>\frac{10}{9}-B-V
\ge\frac{10}{9}-B-(2b-1)^2=\zeta_2(b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

At anchor one, the neighboring caps satisfy \(u\le b\). Equation (3) therefore gives, for every \(0\le s\le1\),
\(\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,1}(s)\le B+V+s+\min\{U,(2\sqrt s-b)_+^2\}\le B+V+s+(2\sqrt s-b)_+^2\).
Combine this with (8) and (16). It gives \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,1}(s)\le5T/9+P_{2/3,1}(s)\) on \([0,2/5]\) for \(b\le19/20\), and the corresponding \(P_{8/9,1}\) inequality on \([0,4/9]\) for \(b\ge19/20\). These are precisely the rational tests in (2). This proves all asserted alternatives when \(u\le b\).

We next prove \(\mathrm R_{8/9}(\mathbf v,1)\) when \(u\ge b\) and no entry is heavy. In the rest of the scalar proof, set \(x=\sqrt s\), so \(0\le x\le2/3\), and use \(P_{8/9,1}\) on its full required range.

First assume \(b\le u\le1\). Applying (3) with larger neighboring cap \(u\) gives
\(\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,1}(s)\le U+V+s+\min\{B,(2x-u)_+^2\}\).
The deficit above \(U+V\) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5T}{9}-(U+V)
&=\frac{5+5b^2-4u^2-4v^2}{9}\\
&\ge\frac{1-4u^2-11b^2+16b}{9}\\
&\ge\min\left\{\frac{25/4-4u^2}{9},
\frac{1+16u-15u^2}{9}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The first inequality uses \(v\le2b-1\), with both sides nonnegative before squaring that constraint. The expression in its right side is concave in \(b\in[1/2,u]\), so its minimum is at one of those two endpoints. This proves the last line.

If \(u\le3/5\), (6) and \(B\le U\) bound
\(s+\min\{B,(2x-u)_+^2\}-P_{8/9,1}(s)\le U+1/9\).
This is at most each of the two quantities in (17): the first comparison is \(13U\le21/4\), and the second is \(24U\le16u\). Both hold for \(1/2\le u\le3/5\).

For \(3/5\le u\le1\), when \(2x\le u\), the same left side is at most \(1/9\) by (6). Each quantity in (17) is at least \(1/9\) on this interval. When \(2x\ge u\), we can discard the cap and use the second line of (6). The resulting upper bound is

\[
3x^2-4ux+U+\frac5{12}
\le\max\left\{\frac5{12}-\frac U4,
U-\frac{8u}{3}+\frac74\right\},
\tag{18}
\]

by convexity in \(x\in[u/2,2/3]\). Each quantity on the right is at most each quantity in (17). Subtracting the first bound in (18) from the first and second bounds in (17), and then doing the same for the second bound in (18), gives

\[
\frac{10-7U}{36},\qquad
\frac{-11+64u-51U}{36},\qquad
\frac{-19+48u-26U}{18},\qquad
\frac{-59+160u-96U}{36}.
\tag{19}
\]

The first is positive for \(U\le1\). Each of the other three numerators is a concave quadratic in \(u\). At \(u=3/5\), their values are \(226/25,11/25,61/25\); at \(u=1\), their values are \(2,3,5\). They are positive throughout the interval. This proves the rational test for \(b\le u\le1\).

It remains to handle \(u\ge1\). We explicitly account for the remote-cap clipping here. For fixed \(x\), the envelope at anchor one is the supremum over \(\beta\) of

\[
x^2+\min\{U,(x-\beta)_+^2\}
+\min\{B,(x+\beta)_+^2\}
+\min\{V,(x+2\beta)_+^2\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Its cap-saturation slopes are \(x-u\), \(b-x\), and \((v-x)/2\). Between consecutive saturation slopes, the set of saturated terms is constant and all other terms are squared positive parts of affine functions. The sum is convex on each such interval. Therefore its supremum occurs at a saturation slope or at one of the limits as \(\beta\to\pm\infty\). This argument includes coincident slopes, and a zero cap is an identically zero term. The two limits are \(x^2+U\) and \(x^2+B+V\), which are bounded by the first and second expressions below.

At the first saturation slope, \(3x-2u\le0\), because \(x\le2/3\) and \(u\ge1\). At the second, \(2b-x\ge2b-1\ge v\), because \(x\le1\). At the third slope, dropping both other caps and using \(z_+^2\le z^2\) gives an upper bound. Consequently (20) is at most the maximum of

\[
\begin{aligned}
&U+s+(2x-u)_+^2,\\
&B+V+s+(2x-b)_+^2,\\
&\frac72s-vx+\frac32V.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

We verify the rational test for each line separately. For the first line, (4), (6), and \(u\ge1\) bound it by \(U+1/9+P_{8/9,1}(s)\). Since the endpoint square \(U\) is not heavy, \(T>2U\), and therefore \(5T/9-U>U/9\ge1/9\). This line satisfies the required bound.

For the second line, \(U\ge1\) and \(V\le(2b-1)^2\) imply

\[
\frac{5T}{9}-(B+V)
\ge\frac{6+16b-20b^2}{9}.
\tag{22}
\]

For \(3/5\le b\le1\), the reasoning in (18)–(19), with \(u=b\), used no cap in the region \(2x\ge b\). Together with the nonclipped bound \(s-P_{8/9,1}(s)\le1/9\), it proves
\(s+(2x-b)_+^2-P_{8/9,1}(s)\le(1+16b-15b^2)/9\).
This is at most the right side of (22), since their difference is \(5(1-b^2)/9\ge0\). For \(1/2\le b\le3/5\), (6) instead bounds this expression by \((4/3-b)^2+1/9\le29/36\). The concave quadratic on the right of (22) is at least the minimum of its endpoint values, namely \(14/15>29/36\). This proves the second line's bound.

For the third line of (21), the second line of (6) and convexity in \(x\in[0,2/3]\) give

\[
\frac72s-vx+\frac32V-P_{8/9,1}(s)
\le\frac{13}{12}-\frac{2v}{3}+\frac32V.
\tag{23}
\]

Indeed, after using (6), the expression is at most \((3/2)x^2-vx+(3/2)V+5/12\). Its larger endpoint value is at \(x=2/3\), since \(0\le v\le1\). On the other hand, \(u\ge1\) and \(b\ge(1+v)/2\) give
\(T\ge9/4+v/2+5V/4\). Subtracting the right side of (23) from the resulting lower bound on \(5T/9\) yields \((6+34v-29v^2)/36>0\). Its numerator is concave on \([0,1]\), with endpoint values six and eleven. This proves the third line's bound. All possible cap maxima in (20) have been handled, so \(\mathrm R_{8/9}(\mathbf v,1)\) holds also for \(u\ge1\). The scalar alternative is now complete.

Finally consider the actual convex body in the statement. By accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`, its positive-area integer indices are consecutive, and their area roots form a concave sequence. Translate their indices to \(0,1,2,3\), and, if needed, reflect those indices so that \(r_1\ge r_2\). Integer translation and reflection preserve the mixed measure and all containing closed-halfspace inequalities. Divide all four roots by \(r_1>0\). The resulting roots \((u,1,b,v)\) satisfy (1): the two concavity inequalities give \(u+b\le2\) and \(1+v\le2b\), and the chosen orientation gives \(b\le1\). In the actual four-positive-area case, \(u,v>0\), but the scalar proof has already included its entire boundary.

The exact scaling is \(a_1=r_1^2\), \(T=W/a_1\), and
\(\mathcal E_{\mathbf r,j}(a_1s)=a_1\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)\) for \(j=1,2\). Also \(P_{\kappa,a_1A}(a_1s)=a_1P_{\kappa,A}(s)\), and the quadratic correction and its interval have the same scaling. Thus every scalar test proved above transfers to the same actual interior anchor, with its actual area and the original total \(W\).

If the heavy-entry alternative holds, the corresponding actual fiber has area at least \(W/2\), and the centroid clause of accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` gives a point of depth at least \(2W/9\). Otherwise, its fixed-fiber criterion applied to the transferred quadratic or rational test gives one point in the selected actual fiber whose depth, in the locked closed-halfspace convention, is at least \(2W/9\). The point is selected before the halfspace is quantified. Any other original integer fibers have area zero and contribute zero to every halfspace-intersection area; the accepted transfer already applies to the original mixed set, including such fibers and closed boundaries containing entire fibers. Transport the selected point back by the initial lattice translation or reflection. It belongs to the original \(S\) and satisfies the asserted inequality for every containing closed ambient halfspace.
