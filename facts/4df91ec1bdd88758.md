## Statement

Let \(b\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(1\le i\le b\), let \(m_i,h_i,L_i\ge1\) be integers, put \(K_i:=\lfloor L_i/h_i\rfloor\ge2\), and let \(\beta_i\ge0\) and \(\theta_i\in\mathbb R\) be finite. Take independent original rows \((\mathbf X_i,Y_i)\) with support and mass
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{L_i,h_i,m_i}
&:=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m_i+1}:
                    \sum_{r=1}^{m_i}x_r+h_i y\le L_i\right\},\\
R^{(i)}(\mathbf x,y)
&:=\frac{\exp\left(\beta_i\sum_{r=1}^{m_i}x_r+\theta_i y\right)}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{(\mathbf z,v)\in E_{L_i,h_i,m_i}}
              \exp\left(\beta_i\sum_{r=1}^{m_i}z_r+\theta_i v\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Suppose \(\mathbb E(\mathbf X_i,Y_i)=(j_i\mathbf1,1)\), where \(j_i\in\mathbb Z\). Define the concatenated original random vector \(\mathbf Z:=((\mathbf X_i,Y_i))_{i=1}^b\) and its full mean \(\mathbf z^\star:=((j_i\mathbf1,1))_{i=1}^b\). Then \(j_i\ge1\) and \(\mathbf z^\star\in\prod_{i=1}^b E_{L_i,h_i,m_i}\).

For every \(\mathbf a_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i}\) and \(t_i\in\mathbb R\), put
\(T:=\sum_{i=1}^b[\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-j_i\mathbf1)+t_i(Y_i-1)]\). With the absolute constants
\[
\mathcal K:=36\cdot2^{55},\qquad K_0:=236844,\qquad
c_\star:=\frac{1}{9\cdot2^{30}\mathcal K^2K_0^4},
\]
one has \(\min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}\ge c_\star>2^{-226}\). Consequently every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{\sum_i(m_i+1)}\) containing \(\mathbf z^\star\) has original product probability at least \(c_\star\).

The conclusions include \(\beta_i=0\), \(m_i=1\), zero final budgets, zero normal coefficients, and zero directional noise variance. More precisely, write \(\mu_{i,y}:=\mathbb E[X_{\Sigma,i}\mid Y_i=y]\), where \(X_{\Sigma,i}:=\sum_rX_{i,r}\), and set \(N_i:=\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbb E[\mathbf X_i\mid Y_i])\). If \(\sum_i\mathbb EN_i^2=0\), both closed-sign probabilities are at least \(1/(4K_0)\).

## Proof

All expectations until the labels are introduced use the original rows. Each mass \(R^{(i)}\) is positive on its entire finite support. In particular \((\mathbf e_r,0)\) has positive mass, so \(\mathbb EX_{i,r}=j_i>0\), and integrality gives \(j_i\ge1\). The original nonnegative slack \(L_i-X_{\Sigma,i}-h_iY_i\) is strictly positive at the positive-mass origin. Thus
\(L_i-m_i j_i-h_i>0\); this is an integer, so it is at least one. Therefore \((j_i\mathbf1,1)\) is in \(E_{L_i,h_i,m_i}\), proving the claimed legality of the full mean. Also \(B_{i,1}:=L_i-h_i\ge m_i+1\).

The notation \(R^{(i)}\), \(X_{\Sigma,i}\), \(B_{i,y}:=L_i-h_i y\), \(\mu_{i,y}\), and \(v_{i,1}:=\operatorname{Var}(X_{\Sigma,i}\mid Y_i=1)\) is the row-indexed notation \(R,X_\Sigma,B_y,\mu_y,v_1\) of accepted fact `128749b1a260f222`. Its \(s\) is \(s_i:=\mu_{i,1}-\mu_{i,0}\). The same original law is called \(p\) in accepted fact `fc05e99e54e6cdad`; its \(v_{B_1}\) is the present \(v_{i,1}\). Below, \(V_i\) is \(\bar a_i\) times the drift variable \(V\) in `128749b1a260f222`, and \(N_i\) is exactly the noise \(N\) in `fc05e99e54e6cdad` for \(\mathbf a=\mathbf a_i\). The height bounds \(K_i\) are distinct from the moment constant \(\mathcal K\).

Write \(p_{i,y}:=\Pr(Y_i=y)\). The original-height computation in the proof of `fc05e99e54e6cdad` establishes that \(p_i\) is log-concave on its contiguous positive support \(\{0,\ldots,K_i\}\). Its applicability uses the original partition
\(Z_{m_i}(B,u)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m_i},\,\sum_r x_r\le B}\exp(u\sum_r x_r)\): the sequence \(B\mapsto Z_{m_i}(B,u)\) is log-concave, and \(p_{i,y}\) is proportional to \(e^{\theta_i y}Z_{m_i}(L_i-h_i y,\beta_i)\). Concavity of \(\log Z_{m_i}\) sampled at steps of length \(h_i\) gives the required height log-concavity, including its zero extension at the endpoints.

We record the height-one mass bound explicitly. Temporarily omit the row index. The mean identity gives \(p_0=\sum_{y=2}^K(y-1)p_y\ge p_2\), and log-concavity gives \(p_1^2\ge p_0p_2\ge p_2^2\), hence \(p_2\le p_1\). Put \(\rho=p_1/p_0\). If \(\rho\ge1\), then \(p_0\le p_1\). Otherwise decreasing successive mass ratios imply \(p_{y+k}\le\rho^k p_y\) whenever both indices are in the support. Summing, with zero terms beyond the support, gives \(\Pr(Y\ge k)\le\rho^k\). Thus \(1=\mathbb EY\le\rho/(1-\rho)\), so \(\rho\ge1/2\). In either case \(p_0\le2p_1\). Finally \(\Pr(Y\ge3)\le1/3\), whence \(2/3\le p_0+p_1+p_2\le4p_1\). We have proved \(p_{i,1}\ge1/6\) for every row, using its original heights.

Fix arbitrary normal coefficients as in the statement, and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar a_i&:=\frac1{m_i}\sum_{r=1}^{m_i}a_{i,r},\\
W_i&:=(t_i+\bar a_i s_i)(Y_i-1),\\
V_i&:=\bar a_i\bigl(\mu_{i,Y_i}-m_i j_i-s_i(Y_i-1)\bigr),\\
N_i&:=\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbb E[\mathbf X_i\mid Y_i]),
\qquad \sigma_i^2:=\mathbb EN_i^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Permutation invariance of each original transverse kernel gives
\(\mathbb E[\mathbf X_i\mid Y_i]=\mu_{i,Y_i}\mathbf1/m_i\). Consequently the centered original row projection is exactly \(W_i+V_i+N_i\), and \(T=\sum_i(W_i+V_i+N_i)\). The two original full-mean identities give \(\mathbb EV_i=0\).

To compare drift and noise, put \(\mathbf b_i:=\mathbf a_i-\bar a_i\mathbf1\) and \(U_i:=X_{\Sigma,i}-\mu_{i,Y_i}\). Given \((Y_i,X_{\Sigma,i})=(y,q)\), the transverse vector is uniform on the complete compositions of \(q\) into \(m_i\) parts: all of them have the same original exponential weight. Therefore \(\mathbb E[\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf X_i\mid Y_i,X_{\Sigma,i}]=0\), since \(\sum_r b_{i,r}=0\). As \(N_i=\bar a_i U_i+\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf X_i\), its second moment is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_i^2
&=\bar a_i^2\sum_{y=0}^{K_i}p_{i,y}
       \operatorname{Var}(X_{\Sigma,i}\mid Y_i=y)
       +\mathbb E(\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf X_i)^2\\
&\ge\bar a_i^2p_{i,1}v_{i,1}
\ge\bar a_i^2v_{i,1}/6.
\end{aligned}
\]
The cross term vanishes by the displayed conditional expectation. For \(m_i=1\), \(\mathbf b_i=0\), so the same argument applies.

Accepted fact `128749b1a260f222` now gives
\(\mathbb EV_i^4\le2^{55}\bar a_i^4v_{i,1}^2\le36\cdot2^{55}\sigma_i^4=\mathcal K\sigma_i^4\), where \(\sigma_i^4=(\sigma_i^2)^2\). This uses multiplication of the variance comparison, not division by \(\bar a_i\) or \(\sigma_i\), and therefore includes their zero cases. Accepted fact `fc05e99e54e6cdad` gives \(\mathbb EN_i^4\le1{,}200{,}000{,}000{,}000\,\sigma_i^4\le\mathcal K\sigma_i^4\). The last numerical inequality follows from \(1{,}200{,}000{,}000{,}000<2^{41}<\mathcal K\). All these moments are finite because the original support is finite.

Apply accepted fact `3a60a638a17036a8` to these original height marginals. Its product-row count \(k\) is the present \(b\), its transverse dimension \(r_i\) is \(m_i\), and its scalar coefficient \(c_i\) is \(t_i+\bar a_i s_i\). Let \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}\) denote its label \(\Lambda_i\); this label is unrelated to the mixed-integer lattice \(\Lambda\) in the locked problem. Write its positive weights and mean-one height components as \(\pi_{i,\ell}\) and \(\nu^{(i,\ell)}\). Thus \(p_{i,y}=\sum_\ell\pi_{i,\ell}\nu^{(i,\ell)}_y\), and attach the labels to the original independent rows using
\(\Pr(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}=\ell\mid\mathbf X_i,Y_i=y)=\pi_{i,\ell}\nu^{(i,\ell)}_y/p_{i,y}\), with conditionally independent label draws across rows. Every denominator on the original support is positive.

Let \(K_{i,y}\) be the original conditional kernel of \(\mathbf X_i\) given \(Y_i=y\), exactly as in the kernel-preserving clause of `3a60a638a17036a8`. Its mass at \(\mathbf x\) with \(\sum_r x_r\le B_{i,y}\) is \(\exp(\beta_i\sum_r x_r)/Z_{m_i}(B_{i,y},\beta_i)\), and is zero otherwise. The coupling has the exact product identity
\[
\Pr(\mathbf X_i\in A_i,Y_i=y_i,\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}=\ell_i
       \text{ for all }i)
=\prod_{i=1}^b\pi_{i,\ell_i}\nu^{(i,\ell_i)}_{y_i}K_{i,y_i}(A_i).
\]
Summing over labels recovers precisely the original joint product law. Conditional on each height and label the transverse kernel remains \(K_{i,y}\), so all original means, drift and noise moments above remain unchanged after the coupling. In particular `3a60a638a17036a8` gives \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid Y_i,\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]=0\). Each height component has mean one, and its conditional regularity gives
\(\mathbb E[W_i\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]=0\) and
\(\mathbb E[W_i^4\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]\le K_0(\mathbb E[W_i^2\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}])^2\).
The conditional second moment here equals the conditional variance because of this centering. These assertions include singleton components \(\delta_1\), zero coefficients, and zero conditional variance. They require only the scalar height means of the components; they impose no equality between their conditional transverse means.

For the application of accepted fact `828c61552093316c`, its latent random element \(Y_i\) is now explicitly the augmented element \(\widehat Y_i:=(Y_i,\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i})\). Its label is \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}\), and its constants \(K,K_0\) are the present \(\mathcal K,K_0\). Thus \(\sigma(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i})\subseteq\sigma(\widehat Y_i)\), both \(W_i,V_i\) are measurable in \(\widehat Y_i\), and \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid\sigma(\widehat Y_i)]=0\). The product identity proves independence of the full coupled rows, hence of the full experiments \((\widehat Y_i,N_i)\). Every remaining row hypothesis of that fact was established above: conditional centering and regularity of \(W_i\), finite conditional second moments, global centering of \(V_i\), and its and \(N_i\)'s common fourth-moment bound \(\mathcal K\sigma_i^4\).

Apply the independent-sum conclusion of `828c61552093316c` once to all rows. Since \(K_0\ge3\), it yields
\(\min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}\ge1/(9\cdot2^{30}\mathcal K^2K_0^4)=c_\star\). These are the original product probabilities because deleting the labels preserves the original product law. If \(\sum_i\sigma_i^2=0\), the zero-noise conclusion of that same fact gives both closed-sign probabilities at least \(1/(4K_0)\). Equivalently, in this case the row fourth-moment bounds give \(N_i=V_i=0\) almost surely, leaving precisely the conditionally centered height sum.

The numerical simplification is exact: \(9\cdot36^2=11664<2^{14}\) and \(236844<2^{18}\), so \(9\cdot2^{30}\mathcal K^2K_0^4=11664\cdot2^{140}\cdot236844^4<2^{226}\). Hence \(c_\star>2^{-226}\).

Finally, any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z^\star\) contains the closed halfspace with the same inward normal and boundary through \(\mathbf z^\star\). The latter event has the form \(T\ge0\) for coefficients already allowed above, proving the asserted halfspace bound. With all normal coefficients zero, \(T=0\) and both probabilities equal one. At \(\beta_i=0\), `128749b1a260f222` gives its drift identically zero, so \(V_i=0\) for every normal; every other argument and bound still applies. The zero final budget has its unchanged point-mass kernel and causes no division by a slice variance. Mixed rows with some zero noise variances are included by the common row inequalities and the independent-sum bridge. This covers all the stated degeneracies.
