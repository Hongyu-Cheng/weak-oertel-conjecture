## Statement

There is a compact full-dimensional convex body in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R$ with the nine integer bases of $[0,2]^2$, all positive. For each $0<t<1$ every original fiber has length strictly greater than $t$. For every choice of length-$t$ subintervals of all nine original fibers, the lifted centroid graph $T_t$ fails $\operatorname{conv}(T_t)\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)=T_t$. Even arbitrary one-point selectors on the four original corner fibers force distinct points over the central integer base in the convexified graph.

## Proof

Take the lifted hull of the four corner intervals $[1/2,3/2]$ at $(0,0),(2,2)$ and $[-3/2,-1/2]$ at $(2,0),(0,2)$. Its projection is $[0,2]^2$, and the four lifted interval centers have absolute affine-difference determinant $16$, so it is full-dimensional. For a base $(2a,2b)$ in the square, all corner representations have weights
$$
(\lambda_{00},\lambda_{10},\lambda_{01},\lambda_{11})
=(1-a-b+s,a-s,b-s,s),\qquad
\max(0,a+b-1)\le s\le\min(a,b).
$$
Their center height is $1-2a-2b+4s$. All four original intervals have offset interval $[-1/2,1/2]$ relative to their centers, so the weighted offsets give exactly that same interval. Thus all actual fibers are determined: at the corners they are as prescribed, at each edge midpoint they are $[-1/2,1/2]$, and at $(1,1)$ the fiber is $[-3/2,3/2]$. There are no other sampled bases.

The two high-corner selected interval centroids are each at least $1/2+t/2$. The two low-corner centroids are each at most $-1/2-t/2$. Averaging each diagonal produces a convex-hull point at the same integer base $(1,1)$, with distinct continuous values on the two sides of zero. The selected graph includes only one point over that base. The same separation for arbitrary corner points follows from their original bounds $1/2$ and $-1/2$, independently of the other five choices.

The mixed length of the original body is $11$. At the original central centroid $(1,1,0)$ a containing ambient halfspace includes either its whole fiber or a closed half-line containing its midpoint. Thus it pays at least $3/2$ from that fiber, giving normalized original depth at least $3/22>1/8$.
