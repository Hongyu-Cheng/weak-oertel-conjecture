## Proof

Accepted facts 6aa711c2cd5e2a52 and be7c5fa9a327d93a establish, in the original Euclidean coordinates, that \(D_6=P_6\cap\mathbb Z^2\) has six sites and \(D_{12}=P_{12}\cap\mathbb Z^2\) has twelve sites, with \(P_s=\operatorname{conv}D_s\) for \(s=6,12\). Both are probability tables with the exact global extensions required by 488f0a583886b5fc. They also establish that their entire variance-minimal faces are singletons, their minimum objectives are one, and

\[
r^{s,\star}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}
=p_{s,\mathbf L}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf K=\tau_s(\mathbf L)\}}
\quad(s=6,12).
\]

Each optimizer has four target masses \(1/4\). Its inverse-image supports are
\(\tau_6^{-1}(\{\mathbf0\})=\{\mathbf a,\mathbf0,\mathbf b\}\),
\(\tau_6^{-1}(\{\mathbf K\})=\{\mathbf K\}\) for
\(\mathbf K\in\{\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}\), and
\(\tau_{12}^{-1}(\{\mathbf K\})=\{\mathbf K-(3,-2),\mathbf K,\mathbf K+(3,-2)\}\)
for all four targets. These are the accepted one-factor inputs.

A tuple of convex combinations is a convex combination of tuples, using products of the coefficients. Hence
\(\operatorname{conv}(D^{(m)})=P_6^m\times P_{12}^m=P^{(m)}\).
Taking its complete lattice intersection gives exactly \(D_6^m\times D_{12}^m\).
All product masses are positive on this support and sum to one.
Taking products of the two accepted finite-valued log-concave extensions gives an extension of \(p^{(m)}\). Multiplication of their log-concavity inequalities proves the product inequality, including the case of a zero endpoint. The complete support identity ensures agreement at every lattice zero. The maximal factor masses are \(1/4\) and \(1/12\), so the maximal product mass is exactly \(48^{-m}\). This proves the normalization and all hypotheses needed to use the objects of 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc.

We prove optimality and uniqueness for arbitrary feasible arrays. For \(1\le h\le2m\), write \(\varepsilon_h=6\) if \(h\le m\), and \(\varepsilon_h=12\) otherwise. Given any \(r\in\mathcal F^{(m)}\), define the assignment marginal

\[
r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}
=\sum_{\mathbf J:\mathbf J_h=\mathbf K}
 \sum_{\mathbf I:\mathbf I_h=\mathbf L}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\qquad(\mathbf K,\mathbf L\in D_{\varepsilon_h}).
\]

Its column sums and vector row moments are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf K}r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I:\mathbf I_h=\mathbf L}p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}
=p_{\varepsilon_h,\mathbf L},\\
\sum_{\mathbf L}r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}(\mathbf L-\mathbf K)
&=\sum_{\mathbf J:\mathbf J_h=\mathbf K}
 \sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I_h-\mathbf J_h)
=\mathbf0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first identity uses unit total mass of every other factor. The second uses the \(h\)-th two-dimensional block of each full row moment equation. Thus this is a feasible array for the corresponding factor. The original Euclidean objective has the exact decomposition

\[
V^{(m)}(r)
=\sum_{h=1}^{2m}\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf J_h\|^2
=\sum_{h=1}^{2m}V_{\varepsilon_h}(r^{[h]})
\ge2m,
\]

where \(V_s\) is the one-factor Euclidean objective. No transformed norm is used.

The product array
\(\overline r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=\prod_{h=1}^{2m}r^{\varepsilon_h,\star}_{\mathbf J_h,\mathbf I_h}\)
is feasible. Its column sums factor into \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\), and the \(h\)-th block of its row moment is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I}\overline r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I_h-\mathbf J_h)
=\left(\sum_{\mathbf L}
 r^{\varepsilon_h,\star}_{\mathbf J_h,\mathbf L}
 (\mathbf L-\mathbf J_h)\right)
 \prod_{k\ne h}\left(\sum_{\mathbf L}
 r^{\varepsilon_k,\star}_{\mathbf J_k,\mathbf L}\right)
=\mathbf0.
\]

Its assignment marginals are the corresponding factor optimizers, so its objective is \(2m\).
For any minimizing \(r\), the objective decomposition forces every marginal to have objective one. The accepted singleton characterizations force
\(r^{[h]}=r^{\varepsilon_h,\star}\) for every block.
If \(\mathbf J\ne\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I)\), some block satisfies
\(\mathbf J_h\ne\tau_{\varepsilon_h}(\mathbf I_h)\); the corresponding zero marginal entry is a sum of nonnegative full entries including \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), which must therefore vanish.
Only one target remains possible in each source column, and its prescribed column sum forces its entry to be \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\).
This proves the entire singleton face, its entropy maximizer, and every stated forced zero.

The four factor inverse images each have mass \(1/4\). Factoring their masses proves the uniform positive target mass \(4^{-2m}=16^{-m}\), the exact product row supports, and the counts \(72^m\) and \(16^m\).
Every positive assignment share \(r^{(m),\star}_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\) is one. Hence \(H^{(m)}=0\), and the share potentials in 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc are zero on their complete positive supports.
Uniformity of the targets gives \(S(q^{(m),\star})=\log(16^m)=4m\log2\).

We next prove the exact body realization. All six sites lie on the boundary of the triangle with vertices \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\); the other three are its edge midpoints:

\[

\[

\ge\frac{1/16+1/4}{2}=\frac5{32}.

\((C_6)_{\mathbf I}=[0,p_{6,\mathbf I}]\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D_6\).
