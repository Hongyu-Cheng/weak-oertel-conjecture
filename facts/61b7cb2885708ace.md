## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex with positive summed integer-fiber $d$-volume $\mu$, $d\ge1$, and let $Q>0$ be the maximum raw mixed depth. Fix a source base $p$ with positive fiber volume, a nonzero continuous normal $u$, and $c>m(p)$ where $m(z)=\min_{x\in C_z}u\cdot x$. Let $\mathcal A$ be a closed polyhedron of base tilts $a$ including at least one fixed deepest-point anchor equation $a\cdot(z_*-p)=c-u\cdot x_*$, and suppose the menu $\mathcal M=\lbrace a\in\mathcal A:\mu(H_a)=Q\rbrace $ is nonempty for $H_a=\lbrace u\cdot x+a\cdot(z-p)\ge c\rbrace $. Fix positive-volume destinations $z_j$ with positive weights $\theta_j$ summing to one and $\sum_j\theta_j z_j=p$. Put $d_j=z_j-p$, $B=\operatorname{conv}\lbrace z_j\rbrace $, $E=\operatorname{span}\lbrace d_j\rbrace $, and $S=\operatorname{proj}_E\mathcal M$.

For $b\in E$ let $K_b=\lbrace z\in B:m(z)+b\cdot(z-p)\le c\rbrace $ and let $t_j(b)$ be the last fraction of the segment from $p$ to $z_j$ in $K_b$. The set $S$ is closed. The function $F(b)=\sum_j\theta_j/t_j(b)$ is finite, continuous and convex on $E$ and attains its minimum on $S$. A minimizing projection has a finite lift in $\mathcal M$ with Pareto-maximal exit fractions and inclusion-maximal $K_b$ among the projected menu. The reciprocals are also those of the exact strict-residual first exits.

More precisely, for $s\ge1$ put $h_j(s)=s[c-m(p+d_j/s)]$. Then $h_j$ is continuous concave and has increase at least $(c-m(p))(s_2-s_1)$ between $s_2>s_1$. The scalar reciprocal function $\phi_j$ is one on $x\le h_j(1)$ and $h_j^{-1}(x)$ above that value, with $1/t_j(b)=\phi_j(b\cdot d_j)$. Above the clipping value its subgradient interval at $x=h_j(s)$ is $[1/h'_{j,-}(s),1/h'_{j,+}(s)]$, at the clipping value it is $[0,1/h'_{j,+}(1)]$, and below it is $\lbrace 0\rbrace $. For every one-sided feasible curve $b(\epsilon)=b_*+\epsilon v+o(\epsilon)$ in $S$ from a minimizer, $\max_{g\in\partial F(b_*)}g\cdot v\ge0$. If $C$ is polytopal and $m(p+td_j)=\max_\ell(\alpha_{j\ell}+\beta_{j\ell}t)$ on $[0,1]$, the exact reciprocal is $\max\lbrace 1,\max_\ell[(b\cdot d_j+\beta_{j\ell})/(c-\alpha_{j\ell})]\rbrace $.

## Proof

Each menu cap has mass $Q$ and passes through its deepest anchor, so it is minimizing at that anchor. To prove closure, take $a_l\in\mathcal M$ whose projections tend to $b$. On a subsequence all evaluations $a_l\cdot(z-p)$ at the finitely many positive-volume bases tend to finite values or to signed infinity. Choose a common finite bound $L$ so that evaluations at least $L$ or at most $-L$ respectively capture or miss the entire bounded fiber. Restrict $\mathcal A$ by the corresponding saturation inequalities at the infinite-limit sites. Its image under the map to $\operatorname{proj}_E a$ and to the finite-limit evaluations is a closed polyhedron by finite elimination. It contains the image of every late $a_l$, and so realizes the limit. The finite lift it supplies has the correct limiting mass at each site by saturation and continuity of the nonvertical cap-tail function. Thus it lies in $\mathcal M$ and $b\in S$.

The profile $m$ is finite convex on $B$ by mixing section minimizers, and lower semicontinuous by compactness of $C$. Along each segment it is continuous: endpoint upper limits follow from convex interpolation with the other endpoint, and lower limits from lower semicontinuity. The set $K_b$ is the projection of the closed convex residual over $B$ and is compact. Mixing a strictly feasible source point at $p$ with each of the finitely many destinations yields a relative neighborhood of $p$ in $K_b$. Each segment intersection is an interval, so $t_j>0$. At every fraction strictly below its last closed residual point, mixing that point with the strictly feasible source gives a strict residual point. Fibers along the segment have positive volume by mixing the positive source and destination fibers, so a strict point gives positive residual volume by convexity. Thus the positive residual and the closed residual give the same exit fractions. These are exactly the factors in the stopped barycentric row: the weights $\theta_j/t_j$, after division by their sum, have mean $p$ at the stopped bases $p+t_jd_j$, since $\sum_j\theta_jd_j=0$.

The perspective $s\,m(p+d_j/s)$ is convex by applying convexity of $m$ with denominator-weighted mixing, giving concavity of $h_j$. For $s_2>s_1\ge1$, use $m(p+d_j/s_2)\le(s_1/s_2)m(p+d_j/s_1)+(1-s_1/s_2)m(p)$ to obtain the stated strict growth. The point $p+d_j/s$ belongs to $K_b$ exactly when $b\cdot d_j\le h_j(s)$, giving the formula and the convex epigraph $\lbrace (b,s):s\ge1,\ b\cdot d_j\le h_j(s)\rbrace $. The inverse slopes are bounded by $1/(c-m(p))$. One-sided difference quotients of the inverse give the asserted subgradient intervals, with the reciprocal of an infinite right endpoint slope interpreted as zero. The subgradient of $F$ is the sum of $\theta_j d_j\,\partial\phi_j(b\cdot d_j)$. Along a feasible one-sided curve, local Lipschitz continuity removes the $o(\epsilon)$ error and the convex directional derivative is the support function of this subgradient. At a minimum that derivative is nonnegative. In the polytopal case the profile is convex piecewise affine (it is the lower boundary of a projected polytope). Each $\alpha_{j\ell}\le m(p)<c$. Substituting the maximum-of-affines expression in $b\cdot d_j\le h_j(s)$ gives the simultaneous inequalities $s(c-\alpha_{j\ell})\ge b\cdot d_j+\beta_{j\ell}$ and the claimed formula.

If $E$ is zero-dimensional, the exits are all one and every lift suffices. Otherwise, because the $d_j$ span $E$ and positively average to zero, $\max_j b\cdot d_j\ge\kappa|b|$ for some $\kappa>0$ by compactness of the unit sphere in $E$. Let $D=c-\min_{(z,x)\in C,\ z\in B}u\cdot x>0$. Since $h_j(s)\le Ds$, one gets $F(b)\ge(\min_j\theta_j)\kappa|b|/D$. This coercivity and closedness give a compact nonempty minimizer set. The sets $K_b$ are Hausdorff-continuous: upper convergence follows by compactness of the witnessing points in $C$; for lower convergence mix each feasible point with the strictly feasible source pair and then take the mixing weight to zero. A finite net in $K_b$ makes the latter approximation uniform. All $K_b$ have relative interior in $E$ after translation, so their $E$-volumes are continuous. Select a minimizer maximizing this volume. Increasing any listed exit without decreasing another would strictly reduce $F$. Properly enlarging $K_b$ within the menu increases volume (a compact convex proper superset of a full-dimensional convex set adds an open subset), and would either decrease $F$ or contradict the secondary maximization. The selected projection has a finite menu lift by the definition of $S$.
