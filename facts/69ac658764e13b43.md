## Statement

Use the original-fiber notation of accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\): \(C_z\) is the actual fiber, \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\), \(W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z\), and \(\mu_C(H)=\mathcal H_d(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H)\). The true uniform fiber centroid is denoted \(b_z\), so it is exactly that predecessor's \(\mathbf c_z\); \(h_S\) is its \(h_C\) and the locked original halfspace depth. Its complementary-cap symbol \(b_z\) is not used here. The atomic graph law introduced below is a different measure from \(\mu_C\).

Fix integers \(A\ge2\) and \(N\ge4\), and put \(M=2N+1\), \(\delta=1/(100A)\), and \(c_N=(2N-1)/(3N)\). Let \(v_0=(0,0)\), \(v_1=(2,1)\), \(v_2=(1,2)\), \(p=(1,1)=(v_0+v_1+v_2)/3\), and \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,v_1,v_2\}\). For \(-N\le j\le N\), write \(a_j=A+(1-j^2/N^2)/100\). This is an alias for the central original capacities \(w_{(p,i,j)}\), not a reweighting. Define \(\ell_{v_k,j}=1\) and \(\ell_{p,j}=a_j\), and the rational polytope in \(\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R\) by

\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\left(
\bigcup_{\substack{u\in\{v_0,v_1,v_2,p\},\ i\in\{0,1\}\\j\in\{-N,\ldots,N\}}}
\bigl(\{(u,i,j)\}\times[ij,ij+\ell_{u,j}]\bigr)
\right).
\tag{1}
\]

It is a convex body with \(n=4,d=1\). Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^4\times\mathbb R)\) and \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mu_C(H)\), where the infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces. Its complete positive integer fibers, original centroids, and total volume are

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_+&=\{v_0,v_1,v_2,p\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{-N,\ldots,N\},\\
C_{(u,i,j)}&=[ij,ij+\ell_{u,j}],\qquad
w_{(u,i,j)}=\ell_{u,j},\qquad b_{(u,i,j)}=ij+\ell_{u,j}/2,\\
W&=2M(A+3+c_N/100).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The original volume midpoint hypothesis holds:

\[
w_{(z+z')/2}\le(1+\delta)\frac{w_z+w_{z'}}2
\quad\text{for every }z,z'\in D_+\text{ with }z\equiv z'\pmod2.
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(0<\delta<1/(4e)\), and there are distinct legal midpoint pairs on every one of the eight base columns.

Consider the affine line \(\mathcal A=\{(p,0,t):t\in\mathbb R\}\) and \(E=D_+\cap\mathcal A\). Its integer lattice has rank one. Take the positive convex reference \(\varphi_E=a_0=A+1/100\) on \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\), which majorizes every original capacity on \(E\). With \(\gamma_1=\theta_1=1/2\), its original certificate weights and total are

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_{(p,0,j)}&=(w_{(p,0,j)}-\theta_1\varphi_E)_+=a_j-a_0/2>0,\\
Q_E&=\sum_{z\in E}q_z
=M(A/2+c_N/100-1/200),\\
2^{-1}Q_E&>(\gamma_1-2\delta)2^{-4}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

In fact the one prescribed true centroid \(\mathbf y^\star=((p,0,0),a_0/2)\in S\) has the exact original depth \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)=Q_E\). Thus (4) is a weaker local profile certificate with unchanged capacities, and it does not require centroid-graph depth.

By contrast, every positive convex \(\varphi\) on \(\operatorname{conv}(D_+)=P\times[0,1]\times[-N,N]\) satisfying \(\varphi(z)\ge w_z\) on \(D_+\) has

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_\varphi:=\sum_{z\in D_+}(w_z-\theta_1\varphi(z))_+
&\le M(A+2+c_N/100)<(\gamma_1-2\delta)W,\\
\gamma_1\max_{z\in D_+}w_z
&<(\gamma_1-2\delta)2^{-4}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Hence the maximum of the full-support certificate \(2^{-4}\sup_\varphi Q_\varphi\) from accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\) and the largest-original-fiber certificate \(\gamma_1\max w_z\) is strictly below the threshold in (4).

Finally let \(G=\{(z,b_z):z\in D_+\}\) and let \(\eta=W^{-1}\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z\delta_{(z,b_z)}\) be its original-weight atomic law. For closed ambient halfspaces,

\[
\max_{g\in G}\inf_{H\ni g}\eta(H)
\le\frac{\max\{M,A+1/100\}}{W}.
\tag{6}
\]

Along the finite family \(A=2N+1\), the right side tends to zero and \(\delta\) tends to zero, while (4) and the exact actual-depth assertion continue to hold. Thus the local certificate survives both specified failures in the same actual bodies.

## Proof

All segment endpoints in (1) are rational, so their finite convex hull is a compact rational polytope. On the sheet \(i=0\), the vertex-base segments alone contain \(P\times[-N,N]\times[0,1]\) in the coordinates \((u,j,y)\). This has dimension four in the hyperplane \(i=0\). Adding any defining point with \(i=1\) gives affine dimension five. A convex polytope of full affine dimension has nonempty interior, proving that \(C\) is an actual convex body.

Its base projection is exactly \(P\times[0,1]\times[-N,N]\). The triangle \(P\) is described by \(2u_2-u_1\ge0\), \(2u_1-u_2\ge0\), and \(u_1+u_2\le3\), which also imply \(u_1,u_2\ge0\). Its integer points are exactly the four listed bases: \(u_1=0\) forces \(u_2=0\); \(u_1=1\) permits \(u_2=1,2\); \(u_1=2\) permits only \(u_2=1\); and \(u_1\ge3\) permits none.

At an integer sheet \(i=0\) or \(i=1\), a convex representation can use only defining segments with that same sheet coordinate. At a vertex \(u=v_k\), extremality in \(P\) further forces every contributing segment to have that same vertex base. Their lower and upper heights are respectively \(it\) and \(it+1\), so every claimed vertex fiber in (2) is exactly its stated interval.

The sequence \(a_j\) has constant second difference \(-1/(50N^2)\). Its piecewise affine interpolant on \([-N,N]\) is therefore concave. Every defining endpoint on a fixed sheet \(i\), including a vertex-base endpoint, satisfies \(0\le y-it\le a_t\) at its integer parameter \(t\), since \(a_t\ge A\ge2\). In a convex combination, concavity of the interpolant gives \(0\le y-it\le a(t)\). At \(u=p,t=j\), the defining central segment attains the full interval \([ij,ij+a_j]\), so this is exactly its fiber. This checks every integer base, not merely the defining vertices of the polytope. All capacities are positive. Uniform-interval centroids give (2), and

\[
\sum_{j=-N}^N\left(1-\frac{j^2}{N^2}\right)
=M-\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}{3N^2}
=Mc_N
\tag{7}
\]

gives its original total \(W\).

The four \(u\)-bases have distinct parity vectors, and the two possible \(i\)-coordinates also have distinct parity. Thus a same-parity pair in \(D_+\) has the same \(u\) and the same \(i\), with indices \(j,j'\) of the same parity. Its midpoint is another site on that column. Vertex lengths are constant. On a central column, every length lies between \(A\) and \(A+1/100=A(1+\delta)\), so (3) follows directly. The pairs with indices \(j-1,j+1\), for \(-N+1\le j\le N-1\), show nonvacuity on all eight columns. Also \(\delta\le1/200<1/12<1/(4e)\), using \(e<3\) as established in accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\).

We next prove the cap estimate directly in the original line of actual intervals. Put \(t_0=a_0/2\) and \(W_E=\sum_j a_j\). The lengths satisfy \(a_{-j}=a_j\), \(a_j\le a_0\), and \(a_j\ge t_0\). Consequently \(Q_E=W_E-Mt_0>0\). A containing ambient halfspace restricts to either the entire line-fiber plane or a halfspace in the \((t,y)\)-plane containing \((0,t_0)\). In the latter case it contains a parallel closed halfspace through that point, of the form \(\alpha t+\beta(y-t_0)\ge0\). It suffices to bound the original mass of this smaller halfspace.

If \(\beta=0\), one sign of the symmetric integer indices is included, together with the entire interval at zero, so its mass is at least \(W_E/2\ge Q_E\). If \(\beta>0\), pair indices \(j,-j\). For a common interval length \(a=a_j\), their cut thresholds are \(t_0+v,t_0-v\), where \(v\ge0\), and \(a/2\le t_0\le a\). Their total upper-cap length is at least \(2a-2t_0\). Indeed, for \(0\le v\le a-t_0\) it is exactly \(2a-2t_0\); for \(a-t_0\le v\le t_0\) it is \(a-t_0+v\); and for \(v\ge t_0\) it is \(a\). All these values have the asserted lower bound. The interval at zero contributes \(a_0/2=a_0-t_0\), so the total is at least \(Q_E\).

If \(\beta<0\), the paired lower caps have the same thresholds. Their total length is at least \(a\): for \(v\le a-t_0\) it is \(2t_0\ge a\), for \(a-t_0\le v\le t_0\) it is \(a+t_0-v\ge a\), and for \(v\ge t_0\) it is \(a\). With the half-interval at zero, the mass is at least \(W_E/2\ge Q_E\). Nonvertical boundaries have zero length in each actual interval; the vertical case already included its full boundary fiber. These calculations cover all closed containing halfspaces.

The original measure on the rest of \(D_+\) is nonnegative, so the same fixed actual centroid \(\mathbf y^\star\) has \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge Q_E\) in the whole body. For the opposite inequality, take the closed ambient halfspace \(y-t_0-(N+A+1)i\ge0\). On sheet zero it cuts each central interval in length \(a_j-t_0\) and misses every vertex interval, since \(t_0>1\). On sheet one every upper endpoint is at most \(N+a_0\), and its halfspace expression is at most \(N+a_0-t_0-(N+A+1)=t_0-A-1<0\). It therefore misses every sheet-one interval. This containing halfspace has original mass exactly \(Q_E\), proving the exact depth assertion.

The constant \(\varphi_E=a_0\) is a fixed positive convex majorant on the line. Its \(q_z\) agree exactly with the accepted original-cap weights of \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\), with \(d=1\). Formula (7) yields (4)'s expression for \(Q_E\). Since \(N\ge4\) implies \(c_N\ge1/2\), we have \(Q_E/2\ge MA/4\). On the other hand,

\[
(\gamma_1-2\delta)2^{-4}W
=\frac{M(A+3+c_N/100)}{16}\left(1-\frac1{25A}\right)
<\frac{M(A+3+1/100)}{16}
<\frac{MA}{4},
\tag{8}
\]

where the last strict inequality is \(3A>3+1/100\), valid for \(A\ge2\). This proves the local inequality without replacing any original site multiplicity.

For the global obstruction, fix an arbitrary admissible convex \(\varphi\). At each fixed sheet and integer index, convexity at \(p=(v_0+v_1+v_2)/3\) gives \(\sum_{k=0}^2\varphi(v_k,i,j)\ge3\varphi(p,i,j)\ge3a_j\). Thus some vertex has \(\varphi(v_k,i,j)\ge a_j\ge2\), and its excess weight is zero. Each of the other two vertex excess weights is at most \(1/2\), and the central excess weight is at most \(a_j/2\). Summing over both sheets and all original integer indices proves \(Q_\varphi\le M(A+2+c_N/100)\). The difference between the proposed profile threshold and this upper bound is

\[
(\gamma_1-2\delta)W-M(A+2+c_N/100)
=M\left(1-\frac{A+3+c_N/100}{25A}\right)>0,
\tag{9}
\]

because \(A+3+c_N/100<25A\) for \(A\ge2\). This proves the first assertion of (5) for the entire convex-majorant class.

For the largest-fiber assertion, the threshold in (8) is at least \(49MA/800\ge441A/800\), using \(A\ge2\) and \(M\ge9\). Meanwhile \(\gamma_1\max w_z=(A+1/100)/2\le201A/400=402A/800\). This proves its strict failure. Both comparisons concern the specified certificates, while the exact actual-depth calculation above succeeds.

It remains to check the graph failure in the same bodies. Write \(f(t)=A/2+(1-t^2/N^2)/200\), \(\kappa=1/(200N^2)\), and \(B=N+1\). A central centroid site has height \(ij+f(j)\), and a vertex centroid site has height \(ij+1/2\). For each central label \((p,i,j)\), define the affine tangent \(L_j(t)=f(j)-2\kappa j(t-j)\) and the ambient affine functional

\[
F_{i,j}(u,x,t,y)
=y-it-L_j(t)-B\bigl((1-2i)x+i\bigr).
\tag{10}
\]

Here \(x\) is the ambient sheet coordinate; the label \(i\) is fixed in this formula. At its own central site \(F_{i,j}=0\). At another central site on the same sheet its value is \(-\kappa(k-j)^2\le-\kappa\). At every vertex site on the same sheet, \(L_j(k)\ge f(k)\ge A/2\) gives \(F_{i,j}\le-1/2\). On the opposite sheet the added height term is at most \(N\) and the penalty is \(B\), giving \(F_{i,j}\le-1\). Since \(\kappa<1/2\), the closed halfspace \(F_{i,j}\ge-\kappa/2\) contains exactly that central centroid site. Its graph depth is therefore exactly \(a_j/W\), since every containing halfspace necessarily contains its own atom.

For a vertex \(v_k\), set \(R_0(u)=u_1+u_2\), \(R_1(u)=3-2u_1+u_2\), or \(R_2(u)=3-2u_2+u_1\), respectively. These affine functions vanish at the chosen vertex and are at least one at the other three integer \(u\)-bases. The closed halfspace \(R_k(u)+(1-2i)x+i\le1/2\) contains all centroid sites on the single column \((v_k,i,t)\) and no other graph sites. Its graph mass is \(M/W\). Every vertex centroid site thus has graph depth at most \(M/W\), proving (6).

Finally, along \(A=M=2N+1\), its right side is at most \((M+1/100)/(2M(M+3))\), which tends to zero, while \(\delta=1/(100M)\) also tends to zero. All bodies, all lattice-site capacities, every cap calculation, and the fixed centroid are those already checked in (1)-(2). The local rank-one profile inequality and graph failure therefore coexist without reweighting or selecting the actual point after a halfspace is known.
