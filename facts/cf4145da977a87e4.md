## Statement

Let $\alpha,\beta$ be finite positive measures on compact metric spaces $X,Y$. For $m>0$ let $\pi$ have mass $m$ and marginals dominated by $\alpha,\beta$. Suppose its integral of a bounded Borel cost $c$ is within $\varepsilon\ge0$ of the supremum over this partial-coupling class. Put $\ell=\alpha-\pi_X$, $r=\beta-\pi_Y$, $l=|\ell|$, and $s=|r|$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int[c(p',q)-c(p,q)]_+\,d\pi(p,q)\,d\ell(p')&\le\max(m,l)\varepsilon,\\
\int[c(p,q')-c(p,q)]_+\,d\pi(p,q)\,dr(q')&\le\max(m,s)\varepsilon,\\
\int[c(p',q')-c(p,q)]_+\,d\pi(p,q)\,d\ell(p')\,dr(q')&\le\max(ls,ms,ml)\varepsilon .
\end{aligned}
$$
At exact optimality each corresponding gain is nonpositive almost everywhere for the displayed measure. For $k\ge2$ and a permutation $\tau$ of $\{1,\ldots,k\}$, the integrated positive cyclic replacement gain is at most $k m^{k-1}\varepsilon$ under $\pi^{\otimes k}$.

## Proof

For the first inequality restrict $\pi\otimes\ell$ to the set of positive gain and project it onto the old pair and the proposed pair, obtaining $\eta_{\rm old},\eta_{\rm new}$. The old projection is at most $l\pi$, the new first marginal is at most $m\ell$, and the two second marginals agree. Thus subtracting the old and adding the new, both scaled by $1/\max(m,l)$, preserves nonnegativity, marginal capacities, and total mass. The gain in the objective is exactly the scaled integral, which is at most $\varepsilon$. Interchange the factors for the second inequality. For the third restrict $\pi\otimes\ell\otimes r$ to the positive-gain set and project onto $(p,q)$ and $(p',q')$. The old projection is at most $ls\pi$, and the new first and second marginals are at most $ms\ell$ and $mlr$. Scaling by the inverse of the maximum of these three coefficients gives a feasible exchange and the claimed bound. If either unused mass vanishes the third integral is zero. A zero optimization gap forces each nonnegative integrand to vanish almost everywhere.

For cyclic replacement, restrict attention to couplings of the selected marginals $\pi_X,\pi_Y$ of mass $m$. They are feasible partial couplings, so $\pi/m$ is within $\varepsilon/m$ of the best normalized cost among probability couplings of $\pi_X/m,\pi_Y/m$. Apply 310a8aa0829c7dd2 and multiply by $m^k$.
