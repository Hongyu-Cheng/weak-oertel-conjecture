## Statement

Let $r\ge2$ be an integer and $\beta>r+1$. Let $f$ be a nonnegative Lebesgue density on $\mathbb R^r$ of mass $M\in(0,\infty)$ with full-dimensional convex positive set. Assume $f^{-1/\beta}$, extended by $+\infty$ where $f=0$, is convex. Write $U=\operatorname{int}\{f>0\}$ and set $f=0$ on its boundary when taking the density representative. For $y\in U$ the radii
$$
\rho_{B_y}(u)^r=r\int_0^\infty t^{r-1}f(y+tu)\,dt,\qquad u\in\mathbb S^{r-1},
$$
define a compact convex body $B_y$ containing $0$ in its interior. For every Borel set $E\subseteq\mathbb S^{r-1}$, the $f$-mass of rays from $y$ with directions in $E$ equals the volume of $B_y$ in the corresponding rays from $0$. In particular $|B_y|=M$, and every closed through-$y$ halfspace has the same mass as the volume of its translate intersected with $B_y$.

If $B_y$ is a polytope, let $p_{B_y}$ denote its Minkowski gauge. There are $R\in(0,\infty]$ and a profile $F$ positive and continuous on $[0,R)$ and zero for $t>R$ such that $f(y+x)=F(p_{B_y}(x))$ almost everywhere. On $[0,R)$ the function $F^{-1/\beta}$ is convex and nondecreasing. The positive interior is $U=y+\{x:p_{B_y}(x)<R\}$, and $r\int_0^R t^{r-1}F(t)\,dt=1$. The value at a finite endpoint $R$ is immaterial.

## Proof

**Construction at every interior anchor.** Translate $y$ to $0$. The full-dimensional convex positive set has null boundary, and from an interior anchor each ray meets that boundary at most once. Thus discarding boundary values preserves the density measure and every radial integral in the statement. Put $g=f^{-1/\beta}$ on $U$. It is finite and continuous there by convexity. If infinite density values are permitted and $g$ vanished at an interior point, convexity and an upper bound on $g$ on a small ball about that point would give $g(x)\le C\lVert x-x_0\rVert$ on that ball, forcing a divergent density integral since $\beta>r$. Thus $g>0$ and $f$ is continuous on $U$.

For integers $j\ge2$ put $C_j=(1-1/j)(\overline U\cap j\overline{\mathbb B}^r)$, where $\overline{\mathbb B}^r$ is the closed unit ball about $0$. Each $C_j$ is a compact convex neighborhood of $0$ contained in $U$. Scaling a point of $\overline U$ towards the interior point $0$ by a factor less than one puts it in $U$. The sets increase by convexity and the increasing scaling factors. They exhaust $U$, since for $x\in U$, eventually $x/(1-1/j)\in U$ and $\lVert x/(1-1/j)\rVert\le j$. The functions $f_j=f\mathbf1_{C_j}$ are bounded and upper semicontinuous by continuity on $U$ and closedness of $C_j$. Their extended inverse powers $f_j^{-1/(r+1)}$ equal $g^{\beta/(r+1)}$ on $C_j$ and $+\infty$ outside. They are convex because the power is convex and increasing on the positive line and $C_j$ is convex.

Apply 3919a4dbe7ace166 with $p=r$ to $f_j$. Its radial gauge body dilated by $r^{1/r}$ is a convex body $B_j$ with $0$ in its interior and $\rho_{B_j}(u)^r=r\int_0^\infty t^{r-1}f_j(tu)\,dt$ for every $u$. The $B_j$ increase and contain a common centered ball of radius $a>0$ from $B_2$. Polar integration gives $|B_j|=\int f_j\le M$. For any nonzero $x\in B_j$, the convex hull of $x$ and the radius-$a$ disk in $x^\perp$ is contained in $B_j$ and has volume $\omega_{r-1}a^{r-1}\lVert x\rVert/r$, with $\omega_{r-1}$ the unit $(r-1)$-ball volume. Hence all $B_j$ lie in a fixed ball. Their closed union $B=\overline{\bigcup_j B_j}$ is a compact convex body containing the common ball.

To identify every radius of $B$, take $x\in B$ and $0<\theta<1$. Choose $x_j$ in the union with $\lVert x-x_j\rVert<(1-\theta)a/\theta$, so $\theta x$ is a convex combination, with weights $\theta$ and $1-\theta$, of $x_j$ and $\theta(x-x_j)/(1-\theta)$ in the common ball. Thus $\theta x$ belongs to the union. Applied to the outer point on each ray, this shows $\rho_B(u)=\sup_j\rho_{B_j}(u)$ for every direction. Monotone convergence on that ray gives the stated formula for $B=B_y$. Polar integration over any Borel set of directions $E$ gives $\int_E \rho_B(u)^r\,d\sigma(u)/r$ for both masses, where $\sigma$ is surface measure on $\mathbb S^{r-1}$.

**Equality on a facet.** Assume $B$ is a polytope and take distinct $x_1,x_2$ on one facet. For $0<\lambda<1$, their combination $z=\lambda x_1+(1-\lambda)x_2$ is on that facet. On every boundary ray parametrized by $tx$ with $x\in\partial B$, the radial formula and a change of scale give $\int_0^\infty t^{r-1}f(tx)\,dt=1/r$. For this part only, put $\tilde f=rf$ and $\tilde g=(rf)^{-1/\beta}$. The densities $t^{r-1}\tilde f(tx_i)$ have integral one. They are positive and continuous on the open intervals before their positive support endpoints, which can be infinite. Their increasing quantiles $R_i(v)$, $0<v<1$, are continuously differentiable there, with $R_i'=[R_i^{r-1}\tilde f(R_i x_i)]^{-1}$.

Put $T=(\lambda/R_1+(1-\lambda)/R_2)^{-1}$, $a_1=\lambda T/R_1$, $a_2=(1-\lambda)T/R_2$, and $X_i=\tilde g(R_i x_i)$. Then $a_1+a_2=1$, $T=a_1R_1+a_2R_2$, and $Tz=a_1R_1 x_1+a_2R_2 x_2$ lies in $U$. Differentiation gives $T'=T\sum_i a_iX_i^\beta/R_i^r>0$. With $s=r/(\beta-1)\in(0,1)$, convexity of $\tilde g$, weighted Hölder, and concavity of the $s$-th power give
$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{r-1}\tilde f(Tz)T'
&\ge T^r\frac{\sum_i a_i X_i^\beta/R_i^r}{(\sum_i a_i X_i)^\beta}\\
&\ge \frac{T^r}{(\sum_i a_i R_i^s)^{\beta-1}}\ge 1 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The Hölder step factors $X_i=(X_i^\beta/R_i^r)^{1/\beta}R_i^{r/\beta}$. The last inequality is strict whenever $R_1(v)\ne R_2(v)$, since both weights are positive. Such a difference persists on an interval of $v$. Integrating on compact subintervals of $(0,1)$ and increasing to $(0,1)$ changes variables through the increasing map $T$. Its image is contained in the positive ray from $0$ through $z$, so the full ray integral, equal to one, bounds the result from above even at an infinite endpoint. Strictness on any interval would contradict that bound. Therefore the two quantile functions, and hence the two ray laws, coincide. Their positive intervals have the same endpoint, and continuity of their densities gives equality of the original $f(tx_i)$ throughout the positive interval, with equality at $t=0$ by continuity.

**Propagation and profile.** The finite facet intersection graph is connected. Otherwise disjoint nonempty closed unions of facets would partition $\partial B$, which is homeomorphic by radial projection to the connected sphere in dimension $r\ge2$. Meeting facets share a ray, so every point of $\partial B$ has one common profile $F$ and common endpoint $R\in(0,\infty]$. Every nonzero $x$ is $p_B(x)$ times a point of $\partial B$, which proves the representation and positive interior. The original ray normalization gives $r\int_0^R t^{r-1}F(t)\,dt=1$. The inverse power $F^{-1/\beta}$ is convex by restriction to a ray. For $0\le s<t<R$ and $x\in\partial B$, the point $sx\in tB$ is a convex combination of vertices of $tB$, all having the same $g$-value $F(t)^{-1/\beta}$. Convexity gives $F(s)^{-1/\beta}\le F(t)^{-1/\beta}$.
