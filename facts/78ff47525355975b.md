## Statement

For $\varepsilon>0$ let $\mu_\varepsilon$ be normalized area on $C_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,0),(1,0),(0,\varepsilon)\}\subset\mathbb R^2$. Its maximal normalized ambient closed-halfspace depth is $4/9$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the measures converge weakly to the law $(1-|x|)\,dx$ on $[-1,1]\times\{0\}$, whose maximal ambient closed-halfspace depth is $1/2$.

## Proof

The triangle $C_\varepsilon$ is full dimensional, so its maximal ordinary depth on the triangle is $4/9$ by the simplex case of ea91e021ff1f4416 in dimension two. Points outside the triangle have depth zero by separation. Its area is $\varepsilon$, and its vertical section at $x\in[-1,1]$ is $[0,\varepsilon(1-|x|)]$. For every bounded continuous $f$ on the plane,
$$
\int f\,d\mu_\varepsilon
=\int_{-1}^1\int_0^{1-|x|} f(x,\varepsilon t)\,dt\,dx
\ \longrightarrow\ \int_{-1}^1 f(x,0)(1-|x|)\,dx
$$
by dominated convergence. The limit has total mass one and is atomless and symmetric about zero. A closed ambient halfspace through the origin restricts to a halfline based at zero or the whole supporting line, so its mass is at least $1/2$. For every point on the segment the left or right closed vertical halfplane through the point has mass at most $1/2$, using symmetry and the absence of point masses. Off the segment depth is zero by separation. This gives the exact maximum at the limit.
