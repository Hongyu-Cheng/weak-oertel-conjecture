## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^5$ be the simplex with vertices $(0,0),(4,0),(0,e_1),(0,e_2),(1,e_3),(3,e_4),(3,e_5)$, where $z$ is the first coordinate and $e_i$ are the continuous standard basis vectors. Its ambient volume is $V=1/180$. The positive five-volume integer fibers $K_1,K_2,K_3$ have normalized mixed weights $(139,144,9)/292$. The actual integer fibers at zero and four have zero five-volume, and $K_2=(K_1+K_3)/2$. For the ordinary fiber centroids $g_1,g_3$, the point
$$
y=\left(2,\frac{g_1+g_3}{2}\right)
=\left(2,\frac{1763}{15012},\frac{1763}{15012},\frac19,\frac{161}{1251},\frac{161}{1251}\right)
$$
is actual. The closed ambient cap $\{z-x_3-3x_4-3x_5\ge1\}$ contains $y$ and has normalized mixed mass $1677/9344<(5/6)^5/2$, an absolute deficit of $(48739/2239488)V$ relative to the raw target. The parallel cap through $y$ has normalized mass $4150356952177626013/28629870146058409344$.

## Proof

The determinant of the vertex differences has absolute value four. Put $h=(0,4,0,0,1,3,3)$ in the displayed order. If $\lambda_*$ is the barycentric coordinate of $(4,0)$, then $\lambda_*=(z-x_3-3x_4-3x_5)/4$, the last five barycentric coordinates are the $x_i$, and the first is $1-\lambda_*-\sum_i x_i$. Hence the simplex inequalities are $x_i\ge0$, $\sum_i x_i\le1$, and
$$
x_3+3x_4+3x_5\le z\le4-4x_1-4x_2-3x_3-x_4-x_5 .
$$
The base range is $[0,4]$. The closed endpoint fibers are respectively a two-dimensional triangle and a point, both null in five-volume. There is no original vertex of $C$ at height strictly between one and three. A vertex of the slab $C\cap\{1\le z\le3\}$ in that open height interval would be an original vertex, since the two additional inequalities are strict there. The slab is therefore the convex hull of its two boundary fibers, which gives $K_2=(K_1+K_3)/2$ and the admissibility of $y$.

Here is the slice calculation, including the normalization. For heights $h_0,\ldots,h_m$ and a uniform probability on the standard barycentric simplex with $m$ free coordinates, the height density in the interior of the projection is $m[h_0,\ldots,h_m](t-s)_+^{m-1}$ at $s$, where brackets denote divided differences in $t$. For distinct knots this follows by integrating the $m$th derivative of a smooth $\phi$ over the barycentric simplex to obtain $[h_0,\ldots,h_m]\phi$. Indeed, with $\lambda_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i$, integration of the $\lambda_m$ derivative of $\phi^{(m-1)}(\sum h_i\lambda_i)$ gives the difference of the two $(m-1)$-simplex integrals on $\lambda_0=0$ and $\lambda_m=0$ divided by $h_m-h_0$. This is exactly the divided-difference recursion, starting from evaluation for $m=0$. Smooth approximation with $\phi(t)=(t-s)_+^m$ gives the upper tail since the uniform density is $m!$ in free coordinates and the level set has zero probability. Differentiating in $s$ gives the density. Confluent knots follow by limits. In the tuples used here, the maximum knot multiplicity is four and the truncated power for the density has degree at least five, so its required knot derivatives and the density at interior knots are continuous. In physical coordinates the ambient height coordinate is exactly $z$, so the scalar density $f_h$ equals five-volume of the slice divided by $V$ at every interior height. This follows by Fubini and continuity of interior section volumes. At an interior height, compactness of the body gives outer Hausdorff convergence of nearby slices, and interpolating the whole given slice toward a fixed point on each side gives inner convergence. Thus volume and first moments of these full-dimensional continuous slices vary continuously at every interior height.

To compute the first moments at fixed height, add one copy of $h_i$ to the uniform barycentric simplex, then merge the two coordinates with that height. The two merged coordinates split a fixed $\lambda_i$ over an interval of length $\lambda_i$. Thus the induced law on the original simplex has density $7\lambda_i$ relative to its uniform law, with $7$ from the ratio $7!/6!$. Height is unchanged. Therefore the mean barycentric coordinate on the $s$ slice is $f_{h\uplus\{h_i\}}(s)/(7 f_h(s))$ when $f_h(s)>0$.

For explicit arithmetic, on a sorted list of knots start a divided-difference row at $(h_i-s)_+^p$. Replace each consecutive subrow by the difference between the upper and lower order-$(j-1)$ values divided by the difference of the two endpoint knots, to obtain order $j$. When the endpoint knots coincide, the entry is the $j$th derivative at that knot divided by $j!$. It equals $\binom pj(h_i-s)^{p-j}$ if $h_i>s$ and is zero at the inactive knots in these evaluations. Using this rule with $p=5$ gives
$$
f_h(s)=\frac{(4-s)_+^5}{32}-\frac59(3-s)_+^4+\frac{(3-s)_+^5}{18}-\frac{(1-s)_+^5}{2}
\quad (0\le s\le4).
$$
It is zero outside the range, and the endpoint values agree with the actual null fibers. Thus $f_h(1),f_h(2),f_h(3)$ are $139/288,1/2,1/32$, and $M=(73/72)V$. This gives the stated nonheavy normalized weights. The duplicated-knot recursion with $p=6$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_1&=(5801/30024,5801/30024,1/6,227/2502,227/2502),\\
g_3&=(1/24,1/24,1/18,1/6,1/6).
\end{aligned}
$$
Their midpoint is the stated $y$. At the lifted centroids the $\lambda_*$ coordinates are $241/3336$ and $35/72$, so $\lambda_*(y)=1397/5004>1/4$.

The cap $\{\lambda_*\ge c\}\cap C$ for $0\le c<1$ is the homothet $c(4,0)+(1-c)C$. Its mixed mass divided by $V$ is $(1-c)^5\sum_{j=0}^4 f_h((j-4c)/(1-c))$. For $c=1/4$ the only positive terms are the original heights $4/3$ and $8/3$ at integer bases two and three. The terms at one and four evaluate at the null endpoint slices. Substitution gives $559/3072$ and hence normalized mass $1677/9344$. Subtracting the cap mass from $(5/6)^5M/2$ gives the stated positive deficit. For $c=1397/5004$ the same two integer bases contribute, and the rational formula with the displayed $f_h$ gives the stated through-$y$ mass.
