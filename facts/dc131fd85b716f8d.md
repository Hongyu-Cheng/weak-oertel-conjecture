## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be actual positive-area convex fibers at the only positive-area integer sites $-1,0,1$, respectively. Assume $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $0\in\operatorname{int}K$. Set $v_1=(1,0),v_2=(0,1),v_3=(-1,-1)$ and $T=\{X:v_i\cdot X\le1,\ 1\le i\le3\}$, of area $9/2$. Assume $B\subseteq T$ and each closed cap $v_i\cdot X\ge z$ through $(0,0)$ attains its actual raw mixed area depth $Q$, with proper $A$ cut and zero $B$ area. Write $a,k,b$ for the three fiber areas and $u\le v\le w$ for the ordered $A$ cap areas, with $v_\ell$ the normal of a largest one. Assume $b\le a+k$ and $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$. Set
$$
q=Q-w,\quad H=h_A(v_\ell)+1,\quad r=(a-u-v)/w,\quad s=\sqrt{1-r}.
$$
Then $H\ge3/s>3$ and $w\le2q$. For every $t\in[-2,1]$ put $x=H-1+t$, $\eta=(t+2)/3$, and $\ell(t)=\mathcal H^1(B\cap\{v_\ell\cdot X=t\})/\lVert v_\ell\rVert$, with value zero on empty sections. Then $b=\int_{-2}^1\ell(t)\,dt$, $0\le\ell(t)\le t+2$, and
$$
4q\ge x\ell(t)+w\max\{(x/H)^2,[\eta+(1-\eta)\sqrt r]^2\}.
$$
In particular, with $A_0=H-3$,
$$
b\le\int_{A_0}^H\min\{x-A_0,\ 4q/x-wx/H^2\}\,dx.
$$
This integral is $9/2$ when $4q\ge3H+w$. Otherwise it equals $2q-A_0(x_0-A_0)/2-w/2+4q\log(H/x_0)$, with
$$
x_0=\frac{A_0+\sqrt{A_0^2+16q(1+w/H^2)}}{2(1+w/H^2)}.
$$
The following further bounds hold:
$$
\begin{aligned}
b&\le 2q[1+\log((9+w)/(4q))]-w/2,\\
b&\le \frac{8s}{2-s}q-\frac{s(2-s)}2 w,\\
b&\le \frac{2s}{2-s}\left[4q-\frac w4(1+\sqrt r)^2\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular $(41s-18)q\ge2(2-s)(s^2+2s+3)w$ and $r<1357/1681$. The preceding upper bounds and levelwise inequality remain valid when $r$ is replaced by $r_*=(a-v-u/4)/w$ and $s$ by $\sqrt{1-r_*}$; the final coupled lower bound on $(41s-18)q$ is stated for the original $r,s$.

## Proof

Fact 077e154ea4666171 has exactly these assumptions on the actual fibers, caps, areas and bounds on $Q,b$. Its proof gives $a\le w+v+u/4$, $k\le9Q/4-u-v-w/4$, and $1/4\le r<r_*<1$. Its shifted-cap inequality gives $|A\cap\{v_\ell\cdot X\ge2\}|\ge r_*w$ and its homothety bound gives $H\ge3/\sqrt{1-r_*}\ge3/s$. Thus $H>3$ and both fractions give valid lower bounds on this same tail. Also $h_B(v_i)=1$ for each $i$. If one value were below $1$, slightly decrease the positive tilt coefficient in the threshold $v_i\cdot X\ge\alpha z$ from $\alpha=1$, keeping the $B$ cap empty. The middle cap stays fixed and the $A$ threshold $-\alpha$ strictly increases within the interior support interval. A positive-area slab is lost, contradicting that the cap attains $Q$.

For the largest normal, vary $\alpha$ over the real line. The endpoint capture areas at thresholds $-\alpha,\alpha$ are continuous, with opposite order at the two saturated limits, so they equalize at some finite $\alpha$ with common area $m$. The average of these two captured convex sets is inside the original middle cap by midpoint compatibility and opposite thresholds. For $m>0$ planar Brunn–Minkowski gives its area at least $m$, so $m\le q$ (which also holds if $m=0$). Criticality gives $Q\le q+2m$ and hence $w\le2q$.

For each normal $v_i$, the normalized section length of $T$ at level $t\in[-2,1]$ is $t+2$. For $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ this is the coordinate interval from $-1-t$ to $1$, and for $(-1,-1)$ the same interval parameterizes the line with arclength factor $\sqrt2=\lVert v_3\rVert$. This proves the section upper bound; coarea gives the integral identity. Put $A_t=A\cap\{v_\ell\cdot X\ge-t\}$. A homothetic copy of the original largest cap about a point at height $h_A(v_\ell)$ with factor $x/H$ lies in $A_t$, giving $|A_t|\ge w(x/H)^2$. Also planar Brunn–Minkowski applied to the original largest cap at height $-1$ and its far tail at height $2$ gives $|A_t|^{1/2}\ge\eta\sqrt w+(1-\eta)\sqrt{rw}$, since the interpolated threshold is $-t$. The same proof allows $r_*$ in place of $r$.

When the section of $B$ at level $t$ is nonempty, the average of that section and $A_t$ is inside the original middle cap. In orthogonal coordinates with longitudinal coordinate $v_\ell\cdot X/\lVert v_\ell\rVert$, adding that section adds its transverse interval length $\lVert v_\ell\rVert\ell(t)$ to every nonempty transverse interval of $A_t$. The longitudinal length of $A_t$ is $x/\lVert v_\ell\rVert$ since $h_A(v_\ell)>2$ and its original cut is proper. Fubini gives the Minkowski-sum area $|A_t|+x\ell(t)$, and the average scales it by $1/4$. For an empty section pair $A_t$ instead with a point of $B$ at height $1$, whose existence was proved above; their average still lies in the original middle cap. This gives the same inequality with $\ell(t)=0$. Combining the two lower bounds on $|A_t|$ proves the levelwise claim.

The homothety part gives $\ell(t)\le\min\{x-A_0,4q/x-wx/H^2\}$. The first capacity increases on $[A_0,H]$, and the second decreases and is strictly positive by $w\le2q$. If the first stays lower at $H$, its integral is $9/2$. Otherwise the unique crossing is the given positive root of $(1+w/H^2)x_0^2-A_0x_0=4q$. The integral in that case is $(x_0-A_0)^2/2+4q\log(H/x_0)-w(H^2-x_0^2)/(2H^2)$. Substitution of the crossing equation gives the displayed value.

For the simpler logarithm, $x=H-3+3\eta\ge3\eta$ and $x/H\ge\eta$ give $\ell(3\eta-2)\le\min\{3\eta,(4q-w\eta^2)/(3\eta)\}$ for $\eta>0$. The two ordinary cap profiles, $b\le9/2$, and $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$ give $Q\le 2+w/3$ by substituting $a+k\le9Q/4+3w/4$. Thus $4q<9+w$. Split the transformed integral with $dt=3d\eta$ at $\eta=\sqrt{4q/(9+w)}$; the claimed logarithm follows by integrating $9\eta$ below and $4q/\eta-w\eta$ above.

The projection of $B$ is $[1-L,1]$ for $0<L\le3$. Its section lengths are concave on that interval by convexity and additivity of interval lengths under Minkowski averaging. Hence $b\le L\ell(1-L/2)$. The homothety estimate at the midpoint yields $b\le L[4q/(H-L/2)-w(H-L/2)/H^2]$. Its derivative in $L$ is $4qH/(H-L/2)^2-w/H+wL/H^2>0$ by $4q>w$. At $L=3$ its derivative in $H$ is $-12q/(H-3/2)^2+3w(H-3)/H^3<0$ by $w\le2q$. Set $L=3$ and then use $H\ge3/s$ to obtain the second upper bound. The interpolated tail estimate at the same midpoint gives instead
$$
b\le\frac{L}{H-L/2}\left[4q-w\bigl(1-(L/6)(1-\sqrt r)\bigr)^2\right].
$$
Both factors are positive and nondecreasing in $L\in(0,3]$ by $4q>w$ and $0\le(L/6)(1-\sqrt r)\le1/2$. The expression decreases in $H$. Again $L=3$ and $H\ge3/s$ yield the third upper bound. These uses of the tail fraction and the height work with $r_*$ and its corresponding $s$ as stated.

Finally the profile of $K$ and the bound on $Q$ imply $b\ge9Q/2-a-k\ge9q/4+(5/2-r)w$. Compare this with the second upper bound and substitute $r=1-s^2$. Multiplication by $4(2-s)>0$ gives the stated coupled inequality. Its right side is strictly positive, so $s>18/41$ and $r<1357/1681$.
