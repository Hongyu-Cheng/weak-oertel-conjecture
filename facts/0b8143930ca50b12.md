## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^k$ be nonempty and finite with affine rank $k$. Let $L(P)$ be the real scalar functions on $P$ that preserve every anchored convex representation $z=\sum_w\lambda_w w$ of $z\in P$ by points in $P$, and let $\operatorname{Aff}(P)$ be the ordinary affine restrictions to $P$. Assume the hypergraph joining the target and positive-weight sources of each nontrivial anchored representation is connected. Fix $d\ge1$ and $0<\eta\le1/|P|$.

Then $L(P)=\operatorname{Aff}(P)$ if and only if there exist $R,v>0$, depending only on $P,d,\eta$, with the following property. For every family of compact convex positive-volume fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ satisfying all inclusions $\sum_w\lambda_wK_w\subseteq K_z$ for anchored representations and $|K_z|/\sum_w|K_w|\ge\eta$ for every $z$, there are one invertible linear map $T$ on $\mathbb R^d$ and one affine map $z\mapsto Bz+c$ such that $TK_z+Bz+c\subseteq R\mathbb B^d$ and $|TK_z+Bz+c|\ge v$ for every $z$.

## Proof

Suppose $L(P)=\operatorname{Aff}(P)$. First scale all continuous coordinates by $M^{-1/d}$, where $M=\sum_w|K_w|$, so all volumes are in $[\eta,1]$. The common determinant-one affine normalization of aa4df3d20ca63976 applies to this positive compatible family. Its connectedness hypothesis holds because the hypergraph defined by all anchored representations has one component, and the chosen positive representation at each nonvertex using its whole minimal face gives that same component. Hence there is a common diameter bound $D$ depending only on $P,d,\eta$. The normalization preserves compatibility and the lower volume bound. The centroid projection of 89563f7ef7a4762f, with this diameter bound, gives a recentering array in $L(P)\otimes\mathbb R^d$ whose subtraction puts every fiber in one fixed ball. By hypothesis this array is affine on the base. The initial scale and the linear part of the determinant-one map compose into $T$. Its constant translation and the recentering combine into the affine map $Bz+c$. Translations preserve the lower volume bound $\eta$.

Conversely, choose $g\in L(P)\setminus\operatorname{Aff}(P)$ if it exists. There is a real array $a_z$ orthogonal to all affine restrictions with $b=\sum_z a_z g(z)\ne0$. In particular $\sum_z a_z=0$ and $\sum_z a_z z=0$. For $t>0$ take $K_z=t g(z)e_1+\mathbb B^d$. These fibers obey every anchored inclusion with equality and have common relative mass $1/|P|$. They are exact sections of their placed hull: grouping any convex combination in the hull by base yields an anchored representation at each sampled target, and the projected hull has no other integer sites by completeness.

Suppose the asserted maps $T_t$ and affine translations exist for these families. Comparing opposite points in any transformed ellipsoid inside the radius-$R$ ball gives $\|T_t\|\le R$. Its volume bound gives $|\det T_t|\ge v/\kappa_d$ with $\kappa_d=|\mathbb B^d|$, so its least singular value is at least $v/(\kappa_d R^{d-1})$. If $c'_{t,z}$ are the transformed centers, the affine translation cancels in their combination:
$$
\sum_z a_z c'_{t,z}=t b T_t e_1.
$$
The left side has norm at most $R\sum_z|a_z|$, while the right side has norm at least $t|b|v/(\kappa_d R^{d-1})$. Letting $t$ increase proves the necessity.
