## Statement

Let $B=[0,1]^d$, $d\ge3$, with center $g$. For positive weights $w_i$ summing to one, take the opposite coordinate halfcaps $x_i\ge1/2$ and $x_i\le1/2$, each with weight $w_i/2$. The center is the unique ordinary deepest point, each cap is critical there with mass $1/2$, and every section has area one and centroid $g$. The combined old-boundary selector is $1/2$ almost everywhere. The full cap aggregate $A$ and section body $J$ are boxes with facet area at each of $\pm e_i$ equal to $a_i=(1+w_i)/2$ and $r_i=w_i/2$, respectively. If $w_d=t$ and $w_i=(1-t)/(d-1)$ for $i<d$, the $d$th side of $J$ tends to infinity as $t\downarrow0$, while the $d$th side of $A$ tends to $(d/(d-1))\,2^{-1/(d-1)}>1$. Consequently no fixed dilate of the cube contains a translate of $J$ uniformly over these cap laws, and $A$ itself need not have a translate in the cube.

In $d=3$ the weights $(12/25,12/25,1/25)$ give sides $(\sqrt{13}/5,\sqrt{13}/5,37/(10\sqrt{13}))$ for $A$ and $(\sqrt2/10,\sqrt2/10,6\sqrt2/5)$ for $J$, and volumes $37\sqrt{13}/250$ and $3\sqrt2/125$ respectively.

## Proof

Reflection in $g$ shows that any containing closed halfspace captures at least half the cube, by first moving its boundary to $g$. Any coordinate cap through $g$ attains one half. At any other point a coordinate differs from $1/2$ and its smaller coordinate halfspace through the point captures strictly less. The coordinate sections are proper centered unit $(d-1)$-cubes. Their inward normals cancel pairwise with the same weights. Outside the section hyperplanes the two indicators for each coordinate pair sum to one. Those hyperplanes meet the old boundary in area-null sets, so the total old-boundary selector is exactly $1/2$ almost everywhere, also after any signed normal-dependent test.

Apply the one-body formula in c3835529a10c63ef with $\beta=1/2$. Its section measure has mass $w_i/2$ at each of $\pm e_i$, because each cap contributes the single atom at its opposite inward normal with section area one. Adding the selected old normal marginal gives mass $(1+w_i)/2$ at each of $\pm e_i$ in the aggregate. For positive prescribed masses $b_i$ at both signs, put $V=(\prod_i b_i)^{1/(d-1)}$ and side lengths $\ell_i=V/b_i$. Then $\prod_i\ell_i=V$ and the facet orthogonal to axis $i$ has area $V/\ell_i=b_i$. These positive boxes are the stated bodies up to translation by Minkowski uniqueness. [Gardner, The Brunn–Minkowski inequality, §6](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/jaw/COURSES/520s/523/HO.523.20/Gardner.BullAMS.02.pdf).

For the specified weights, the $d$th side of $J$ is
$$
\frac{1-t}{2(d-1)}(t/2)^{-(d-2)/(d-1)},
$$
which tends to infinity. The other sides are $(t/2)^{1/(d-1)}$, so its volume tends to zero. The $d$th side of $A$ is
$$
\frac{d-t}{2(d-1)}\bigl((1+t)/2\bigr)^{-(d-2)/(d-1)}.
$$
Its limit is strictly greater than one, since raising the comparison to the power $d-1$ gives $(1+1/(d-1))^{d-1}>2$ by the binomial theorem for $d-1\ge2$. A translation does not change the length of the projection on this coordinate, so both claimed containment failures follow. Reflection has no effect up to translation on the boxes.

For the finite three-dimensional certificate the facet masses for $A$ are $(37/50,37/50,13/25)$ and for $J$ are $(6/25,6/25,1/50)$. Inserting these in $\ell_i=V/b_i$ gives the sides and volumes in the statement. In particular the third side of $A$ has square $1369/1300>1$ and the third side of $J$ has square $72/25$, which exceeds both $(1/2)^2$ and $1/2$.
