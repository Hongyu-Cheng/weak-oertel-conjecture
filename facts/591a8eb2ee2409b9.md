## Statement

Let $\varepsilon=1/10$, $K_0=[-1,1]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$, $K_1=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\times[-1,1]$, and $C=\operatorname{conv}((\{0\}\times K_0)\cup(\{1\}\times K_1))$. The normalized mixed area on its two actual integer fibers is their equal uniform mixture. The unrestricted maximum of closed-halfspace depth is attained at $g=(1/2,0,0)$ and is at most $11/40<4/13=1/(1+1/\gamma_2)$, where $\gamma_2=4/9$.

## Proof

The base projection is $[0,1]$, and its two endpoints force each integer fiber to equal its generating rectangle. The hull is compact and full-dimensional, since a rectangle at zero gives two independent continuous directions and a point at one supplies an independent base direction. Both rectangles have area $4\varepsilon$.

For a closed halfspace containing a convex combination of marks, at least one mark lies in the halfspace by affineness of the defining margin. Consequently each depth superlevel set is convex. An affine bijection preserving the mixture preserves its depth, since it permutes closed halfspaces. The two continuous sign reflections preserve both fiber laws. Averaging their four images of any mark $(z,x,y)$ gives $(z,0,0)$ in the same depth superlevel set. The symmetry $(z,x,y)\mapsto(1-z,y,x)$ then yields $g$ in that set by one more average. This proves unrestricted maximality, including for marks outside the mixed support.

The closed cap $x-2\varepsilon z\ge-\varepsilon$ has boundary through $g$. Its trace at zero is $x\ge-\varepsilon$, with area $2\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)$, and its trace at one is $x\ge\varepsilon$, a boundary edge of area zero. Its normalized mass is $(1+\varepsilon)/4=11/40$. The difference $4/13-11/40=17/520$ is positive.
