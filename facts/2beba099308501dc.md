## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $N\ge1$, and let $\mu_z$ be nonzero finite positive Borel measures of compact support $S_z\subset\mathbb R^D$ indexed by a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$. Suppose every positive-weight combination $z=\sum_i\theta_i z_i\in\mathbb Z^n$, with $z_i\in P$ and $\sum_i\theta_i=1$, has $z\in P$ and satisfies
$$
\mu_z\!\left(\sum_i\theta_i E_i\right)^{1/N}
\ \ge\ \sum_i\theta_i\mu_{z_i}(E_i)^{1/N}
$$
for all compact $E_i$ with $\mu_{z_i}(E_i)>0$. For any such incidence, any input index $i$, and any choices $x_l\in S_{z_l}$ for $l\ne i$, put $F_i(x)=\theta_i x+\sum_{l\ne i}\theta_l x_l$. Then the finite measures satisfy $\mu_z\ge\theta_i^N(F_i)_\#\mu_{z_i}$.

Each $S_z$ is convex, and each $\mu_z$ is absolutely continuous in its own affine hull, with counting measure at a point. For positive support dimension $q$, writing $|\cdot|_q$ for Lebesgue measure in the support affine hull, its density is at most $2^N\mu_z(S_z)/|S_z|_q$ almost everywhere. At an incidence every input and the output have the same support affine dimension and tangent space. Partition $P$ into strata $P_c$ by the pair of these invariants. Every $P_c$ is lattice-complete, different strata use disjoint original parity classes, and hence $\sum_c k(P_c)=k(P)\le2^n$, where $k$ counts occupied classes modulo $2\mathbb Z^n$. The convex hull of the placed supports in any stratum has exactly those supports as its integer fibers. The same exact fiber assertion holds for the hull of all the placed supports.

## Proof

Fix an incidence and the indicated points $x_l$. For a compact $E$ of positive $\mu_{z_i}$-mass use $E$ in input $i$ and $S_{z_l}\cap\overline B(x_l,\varepsilon)$ for each other input. All input masses are positive by the support property. Dropping the other summands from the right side gives a lower bound $\theta_i^N\mu_{z_i}(E)$ for the mass of the resulting compact Minkowski sum. These sums decrease to $F_i(E)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, as follows by taking convergent subsequences of the compact input tuples. Finiteness gives $\mu_z(F_i(E))\ge\theta_i^N\mu_{z_i}(E)$. The compact inequality holds also for zero input mass. Applying it to inverse images under the invertible homothety $F_i$ and using compact inner regularity proves the measure inequality on every Borel set.

A weighted average of input support points lies in the output support. For any neighborhood of the average, small compact balls intersected with the input supports have positive mass and a Minkowski sum in that neighborhood. Applying the assumed inequality gives positive output mass there. Taking all input bases equal proves convexity of each support.

To see intrinsic absolute continuity, fix one measure $\mu$ with support $S$ of affine dimension $q>0$, and write $|\cdot|_q$ for Lebesgue measure on its affine hull. The compact convex set $S$ has $|S|_q>0$. The midpoint instance of domination at this same base gives $\mu((E+x)/2)\ge 2^{-N}\mu(E)$ for every compact $E\subset S$ and every $x\in S$. Integrating in $x$ and applying Fubini gives
$$
2^{-N}|S|_q\,\mu(E)
\ \le\ \int_S |S\cap(2y-E)|_q\,d\mu(y)
\ \le\ \mu(S)|E|_q.
$$
The integrand arises from the condition $2y-x\in E$. Inner regularity gives the same upper bound for $\mu$ relative to $|\cdot|_q$ on Borel sets. A support of dimension zero consists of a single atom.

Domination implies $F_i(S_{z_i})\subseteq S_z$, so its dimension is at most that of $S_z$. A strict inequality would make $F_i(\operatorname{aff}S_{z_i})$ null for $\mu_z$, contrary to the positive mass of the dominated measure there. The dimensions therefore agree, and the homothety preserves directions, so the support tangents agree as well.

An integer point of $\operatorname{conv}(P_c)$ is an output of some combination of sites in $P_c$, hence it lies in $P_c$ by incidence rigidity. Two same-parity sites have an integer midpoint, and both inputs share the midpoint's dimension and tangent. Thus different strata cannot share a parity. Finally any point of a placed support hull at an integer base is a finite combination of the placed support points. Its base is an admissible output and its continuous coordinate belongs to the output support by the support inclusion proved above. The reverse fiber inclusion follows from the definition of the hull.
