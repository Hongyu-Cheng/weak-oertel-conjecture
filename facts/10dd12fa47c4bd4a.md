## Statement

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,-3),(4,4),(2,3),(-3,0)\}\subset\mathbb R^2$, of area $9$. The function $R_K(a)=|K\cap(2a-K)|$ on $\mathbb R^2$ has the unique maximizer $c=(-182/233,123/233)$, with $R_K(c)=1440/233$. The closed halfplane $3x-y\ge-669/233$ passes through $c$ and captures the fraction $70285/162867<4/9$ of the area of $K$. In particular $c$ fails the sharp ordinary planar depth bound and is not a global maximizer of ordinary halfspace depth in $K$.

## Proof

The four lower inequalities of $K$ are $-7x+8y\ge4$, $x-2y\ge-4$, $3x-5y\ge-9$, and $3x-y\ge-9$, in that order. The shoelace formula on the listed counterclockwise vertices gives area $9$. Write $L_i$ for the $i$th supporting line $\nu_i\cdot(x,y)=b_i$, indexed from zero, and $U_i$ for its reflected line $\nu_i\cdot(x,y)=2\nu_i\cdot(s,t)-b_i$ for a center $(s,t)$. The reflected intersection is bounded by the four lower and four upper constraints. The following are its successive active pairs and intersections:
$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &x&y\\
(U_0,L_3)&(-76-14s+16t)/17&(-75-42s+48t)/17\\
(U_1,L_3)&(-22-2s+4t)/5&(-21-6s+12t)/5\\
(U_1,U_2)&2s-2&2t-3\\
(L_0,U_2)&(-92-48s+80t)/11&(-75-42s+70t)/11\\
(L_0,U_3)&(76+48s-16t)/17&(75+42s-14t)/17\\
(L_1,U_3)&(22+12s-4t)/5&(21+6s-2t)/5\\
(L_1,L_2)&2&3\\
(U_0,L_2)&(92+70s-80t)/11&(75+42s-48t)/11
\end{array}
$$
Substituting $c$ gives two active constraints at each listed point and all other slacks at least $12/233$. Each of the eight bounding lines supports exactly the two adjacent listed points on that line, and they are distinct. The convex polygon formed by them thus has those eight halfplanes as its facet description. Strictness of the remaining slacks and distinctness of the vertices persist on a neighborhood of $c$. Applying shoelace there gives
$$
R_K(s,t)=\frac{-9516s^2+21644st-13864t^2-26292s+31544t-12816}{935}.
$$
Its value at $c$ is $1440/233$, its gradient there vanishes, and its Hessian has negative first diagonal entry and determinant $3728/55$. Thus $c$ is a strict local maximizer.

For $D_a=K\cap(2a-K)$ and $a,b\in K$, convexity of $K$ gives $(1-\lambda)D_a+\lambda D_b\subseteq D_{(1-\lambda)a+\lambda b}$ for $0\le\lambda\le1$: the corresponding reflected point is the same combination of the reflected points in $K$. Brunn–Minkowski gives concavity of $R_K^{1/2}$ on $K$. The intersections are compact convex and nonempty for centers in $K$, so the inequality also applies to zero-area intersections. A center outside $K$ has empty overlap because it would otherwise be the midpoint of two points of $K$. Concavity and strict local maximality exclude both a larger value and a second equal global value, by moving from $c$ on the segment to the alleged center.

The line $3x-y=-669/233$ contains $c$ and meets the relevant edges at $(-260/233,-111/233)$ and $(-104/233,357/233)$. The retained polygon has these two vertices and $(4,4),(2,3)$, in cyclic order when the intersections come first and last. Its shoelace area is $210855/54289$, giving $70285/162867<4/9$ after division by $9$. Apply 6769574fc1bd9f8d to the full-area centroid of this $K$ with $d=2$, obtaining normalized ordinary depth at least $4/9$ at that centroid. This strictly separates $c$ from a global depth maximizer.
