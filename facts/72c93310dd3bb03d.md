## Statement

Let $b$ be an interior barycentric point of a tetrahedron with $b_1>1/3$ and $b_2,b_3,b_4\le1/3$. Any three distinct strict quadrilateral sections with actual area centroid $b$ and the common split $\{1,2\}\mid\{3,4\}$ have linearly independent normals. If $b_3=b_4$, at most two such section planes exist.

## Proof

Orient toward the pair $\{1,2\}$ and normalize the fourth height to $-1$. In normalized reciprocal coordinates $(x,y,z,w)$, put $K=xy-zw$. The centroid formulas of 1462fa9ce6ced54e give
$$
3b_1-1=(Kx-zw)/(x-K),\qquad
3b_3-1=(-Kz-xy)/(z+K),\qquad
3b_4-1=(-Kw-xy)/(w+K).
$$
All three denominators are positive products of adjacent pair sums. Thus $b_1>1/3$ gives $K>0$ and $b_3,b_4<1/3$. In the conic coordinates $A>0$, $-1<B\le0$, and $GD>0\ge AB$. If $G\ne D$ its determinant is nonzero. Three linearly dependent normalized normals would give three distinct collinear $(p,r)$ points, since the fourth coordinate is fixed and $q$ is affine in $(p,r)$. A line through three points on this nondegenerate conic would be a factor of its homogeneous quadratic, which would make the matrix singular.

If $G=D$, fact 238906fb08a2b14c with the two equal central coordinates gives equal negative heights. Write the margins as $(p,q,-1,-1)$, put $u=(1+p)^{-1}$, $v=(1+q)^{-1}$, $t=2-u-v$, and let $B_0=b_3=b_4$. Substitution into the centroid formulas gives $3b_1=u(1+t-u)/t$ and $3b_2=v(1+t-v)/t$. Since $u,v<1$, the signs of $3b_1-1$ and $3b_2-1$ are the signs of $u-t$ and $v-t$, respectively. Consequently $u>t$ and $v\le t$, so $1/2<t<1$ by $u+v=2-t$ and $u<1$. The sum and difference of the centroid equations give
$$
uv=t^2-(1+3B_0)t+1,\qquad
3(b_1-b_2)t=(u-v)(2t-1).
$$
Squaring the latter and substituting $(u-v)^2=(2-t)^2-4uv=3t(4B_0-t)$ yields $(4B_0-t)(2t-1)^2=3(b_1-b_2)^2t$. Distinct sections have distinct $t$, since the displayed formulas recover both $u+v$ and $u-v$. If there were three, their $t$ values would be the three roots of this cubic. Its leading coefficient is $-4$ and Vieta gives $\sum_i t_i=1+4B_0$ and $\prod_i t_i=B_0$, so
$$
t_3=\frac{t_1+t_2-1}{4t_1t_2-1}<\frac12.
$$
The denominator is positive and the inequality is equivalent to $-(2t_1-1)(2t_2-1)<0$. This contradicts the range of actual roots and completes the same-split assertion, including $b_2=1/3$.
