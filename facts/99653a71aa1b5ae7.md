## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $T$ be a volume-one $d$-simplex with vertex $0$, centroid $g$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ with $\lambda_0(0)=1$. Set $b=1/(d+1)$, $\Gamma=(1-b)^d$, $c=(1-\Gamma)^{1/d}$, and let $\rho_d$ be the reflected simplex profile minimum. In the actual lift of $A=\varepsilon T,T,T$ at $-1,0,1$ assume $\varepsilon>0$ and $a=\varepsilon^d<\rho_d$. Use the finite cover of the mixed source by $\lambda_i(x)-2z\ge b$ and $\lambda_i(x)+2(z-1)\ge b$ for $0\le i\le d$. The one-cap residual-root obstacles are $(0,c,c)$, the unique sampled convex heights minimizing their sum of $d$th powers are $(c,c,c)$, and an exact KKT terminal row from $0$ is uniform on $-1,0,1$.

For each critical cap $\lambda_i(x)-Lz\ge b$ through $(0,g)$, define the correction cost to be the mean of the reciprocals of the first-exit fractions on this row, with fraction one for a destination retaining positive residual. The exact cost is $[2+(d+1)L]/3$ if $i\ge1$, and $[2+(d+1)(L+1-\varepsilon)]/3$ if $i=0$. The minimum over the full critical menu is $(d+2)/3$, attained at $L=1-b$ for every $i\ge1$. The corresponding closed and positive residual projections are respectively $[-1/d,1]$ and $(-1/d,1]$. Any such cap can be added to the cover or substituted for its corresponding same-direction left cap while retaining the residual program and the KKT row.

For $0<a\le\rho_d$ and each $L\ge2$, the caps $\lambda_i(x)-Lz\ge b$ are critical, with the same residual program and row when used in place of the left cover. For $i\ge1$ their costs are unbounded as $L$ grows even at fixed $d,a$, cap mass, source residual root, and original row. In the strict range the full-menu optimum also rules out a dimension-free bound, and for each fixed $d$ its excess over one cannot be bounded by a fixed multiple of the captured destination volume $a$.

## Proof

The accepted three-fiber theorem 98603e266680b22d applies for $0<a\le\rho_d$ because $\rho_d<1$ and $A\subset T$. It gives the unique maximizer $(0,g)$ and raw maximum $Q=\Gamma+a$. Its displayed cover is exactly the one in the statement. The left caps have trace volumes $(a,\Gamma,0)$ and the right caps $(0,0,\Gamma)$, hence the asserted obstacles.

For sampled heights $h_z$ use constraints $h_z\ge e_z$ with obstacles $e_z$, and support constraints $h_z+g_z(y-z)\le h_y$ for every ordered pair $z\ne y$. The two constraints out of $0$ give $h_{-1}+h_1\ge2h_0$. All heights are nonnegative, and $h_0\ge c$. Strict convexity of the $d$th power gives objective at least $3c^d$ with equality only for all heights equal to $c$. They are feasible with $g_z=0$. At these heights put $s=dc^{d-1}$, let the convexity multipliers be $\mu_{0,-1}=\mu_{0,1}=s$ and zero otherwise, and let obstacle multipliers be $\alpha_0=3s$, $\alpha_{-1}=\alpha_1=0$. The support inequalities are tight, the positive obstacle multiplier is at a contact, and the outgoing displacement sum is $s(-1)+s=0$. The height stationarity equation $\alpha_z=s+\sum_y\mu_{zy}-\sum_y\mu_{yz}$ holds at every site. In the associated absorbing chain each state stops with probability $s/(s+\sum_y\mu_{zy})$ and moves to $y$ with probability $\mu_{zy}/(s+\sum_y\mu_{zy})$. Thus the source stops at $0$ with probability $1/3$ and otherwise moves to either endpoint with probability $1/3$ each, where it stops immediately.

In the strict range the full-menu theorem for the tiny simplex endpoint shows that all critical caps have the displayed form with $L\ge1-b$. Each such cap has the left cap trace volumes $(a,\Gamma,0)$, so adding it or replacing the corresponding left cap leaves the obstacles unchanged. The cover is retained because each left cap contains $A$, the middle canonical directions are retained, and the original right caps still cover the right fiber. The right trace of a new left cap may be a vertex when $L=1-b$. For $0<a\le\rho_d$ and $L\ge2$, the same trace and coverage checks hold by $0\le\lambda_i\le1$. Those caps have mass $\Gamma+a$ and are critical by the accepted depth formula. This argument at $a=\rho_d$ needs no exhaustive equality classification.

For $z=-t$ with $0\le t\le1$, let $w_-,w_0,w_+$ be the convex weights at the three lifted generators. Then $w_-=t+w_+$, and the resulting continuous set for these weights is $[1-(1-\varepsilon)w_-]T$. The homothetic sets are nested about $0$, and the largest occurs at $w_+=0$. Therefore $C_{-t}=[1-(1-\varepsilon)t]T$. For $0\le z\le1$ the two large generators already supply all of $T$. The minimum of $\lambda_i$ on $C_{-t}$ is zero for $i\ge1$ and $(1-\varepsilon)t$ for $i=0$. The threshold for the left trace of a cap at $-t$ is $b-Lt$, so positive residual ends at $t=b/L$ for $i\ge1$ and at $t=b/(L+1-\varepsilon)$ for $i=0$. At either exit the closed residual is a supporting face of volume zero. These fractions are below one for $d\ge2$. The self and right destinations have fraction one because the rightward threshold is $b+Lt\ge b$ on a fixed $T$. The first-exit correction formula thus gives the asserted costs. Since $L\ge1-b$, the minimum uses $i\ge1$ and $L=1-b$, and the maximal closed projection begins at $-b/(1-b)=-1/d$. Only that left endpoint is omitted for the positive residual projection.

The optimum grows with $d$, and for $d\ge7$ is at least $3>1/\Gamma$ since $(1+1/d)^d<e<3$. At any fixed $d$ its excess $(d-1)/3$, the captured destination's row weight $1/3$, and the excess multiplied by the source root $c$ stay positive as $a\downarrow0$. Meanwhile the normalized depth exceeds $\Gamma/2$ by exactly $a(2-\Gamma)/[2(2+a)]$. The original row has roots $(0,c,1)$ and satisfies $(c+1)/3<c$ because $c>1/2$, which follows from $\Gamma<1/2$ for $d\ge2$. For the exact planar calibration take $\varepsilon=1/10$. The profile formula $\rho_2=2(\sqrt{10}-3)/9$ gives $1/100<\rho_2$ since $(3+9/200)^2<10$. Here $Q=409/900$, total mass $201/100$, $c^2=5/9$, optimal $L=2/3$, exit fraction $1/2$ and cost $4/3$.
