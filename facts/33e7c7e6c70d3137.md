## Statement

Let \(n,d,L\) be positive integers, let \(m=n+1\), and set \(P_L=\lbrace \mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i\le L\rbrace\) and \(D_+=P_L\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Define \(p_0(\mathbf x)=1-L^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\) and \(p_i(\mathbf x)=x_i/L\) for \(1\le i\le n\). Write \(\mathbf p(\mathbf x)=(p_0(\mathbf x),\ldots,p_n(\mathbf x))\), \(k_i(\mathbf z)=Lp_i(\mathbf z)\), and \(H(\mathbf p)=-\sum_{i=0}^n p_i\log p_i\), with natural logarithms and \(0\log0=0\). Thus the vectors \((k_0(\mathbf z),\ldots,k_n(\mathbf z))\) range over all weak compositions of \(L\), each exactly once.

Let \(A\) be any affine function positive on \(P_L\), put \(a_{\min}=\min_{P_L}A>0\), and let \(\delta\ge0\). Set \(r=A+\delta H(\mathbf p)\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=r(\mathbf z)^d\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}w_{\mathbf z}\). Suppose \(\varepsilon=(1+\delta\log2/a_{\min})^d-1\le1/10\).

Define the finite-constraint set
$$
\mathcal B=\left\lbrace \mathbf b\in\mathbb R^m:
\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf p(\mathbf z)\ge H(\mathbf p(\mathbf z))
\ \text{for every }\mathbf z\in D_+\right\rbrace
$$
and \(\mathcal J(\mathbf b)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}(A(\mathbf z)+\delta\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf p(\mathbf z))^d\) for \(\mathbf b\in\mathcal B\). This set is nonempty, and \(\mathcal J\) attains its minimum on it. Every \(\mathbf b\in\mathcal B\) has nonnegative coordinates.

Choose any minimizer \(\mathbf b^\star\), and set \(\varphi=A+\delta\,\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbf p\). If \(\delta=0\), then \(\varphi=r=A\) and the aggregate error is zero. If \(\delta>0\), define
$$
T=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}\varphi(\mathbf z)^{d-1},
\qquad
\nu(\mathbf z)=\frac{\varphi(\mathbf z)^{d-1}}{T},
\qquad
\mu_i=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}\nu(\mathbf z)k_i(\mathbf z).
$$
The conditional premise is precisely the following \(m\) inequalities for this chosen optimal \(\varphi\):
$$
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}\nu(\mathbf z)k_i(\mathbf z)(k_i(\mathbf z)-1)
\le2\mu_i^2
\qquad(0\le i\le n).
\tag{M}
$$
No assertion that (M) always holds is part of this lemma.

If (M) holds, then \(\varphi\) is positive and affine on \(P_L\), dominates \(r\) at every integer site, and, with \(\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}=\log3/\log2\), satisfies
$$
0\le
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}\bigl(\varphi(\mathbf z)^d-w_{\mathbf z}\bigr)
\le
\frac{\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}\varepsilon}{1-\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}\varepsilon}\,W
\le2\varepsilon W.
\tag{1}
$$
All sums use the full original integer support and its original unit multiplicities.

Consequently, for any convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) whose positive integer fiber support and actual fiber volumes are exactly \(D_+\) and \(w_{\mathbf z}\), accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\) gives one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), fixed before the containing halfspace is selected, with
$$
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge
[\gamma_d-2\theta_d\varepsilon]_+\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\quad
\theta_d=1-\gamma_d.
\tag{2}
$$
Here \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(h_S\) are exactly the actual mixed-integer set and locked halfspace depth of that accepted fact.

## Proof

First, \(\mathcal B\) is nonempty. At every integer site, \(\mathbf p\) is a probability vector, so \(H(\mathbf p)\le\log m\): Jensen's inequality for \(\log\), with weights \(p_i\) on its positive coordinates, gives \(H(\mathbf p)=\sum_{p_i>0}p_i\log(1/p_i)\le\log|\lbrace i:p_i>0\rbrace|\le\log m\). Therefore the vector having every coordinate equal to \(\log m\) is feasible. Each simplex vertex is an integer site, has barycentric vector \(\mathbf e_i\), and has entropy zero. Its constraint gives \(b_i\ge0\) for every \(\mathbf b\in\mathcal B\). It follows that \(A+\delta\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf p\ge A\ge a_{\min}\) on all of \(P_L\).

When \(\delta=0\), the objective is constant on the nonempty feasible set, every feasible vector is a minimizer, and \(\varphi=r=A\). Henceforth assume \(\delta>0\). The feasible set is closed. A nonempty objective sublevel is bounded: if \(\mathbf v_i\) is the vertex with barycentric vector \(\mathbf e_i\), its contribution implies \(\mathcal J(\mathbf b)\ge(A(\mathbf v_i)+\delta b_i)^d\), and \(b_i\ge0\). Thus every coordinate is bounded on a fixed sublevel. A nonempty sublevel is consequently compact, so continuity of \(\mathcal J\) gives a minimizer \(\mathbf b^\star\).

We establish its exact contact condition. Let
\(\mathcal I=\lbrace \mathbf z\in D_+:\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbf p(\mathbf z)=H(\mathbf p(\mathbf z))\rbrace\)
be its contact set, and put
\(\mathbf v=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}\varphi(\mathbf z)^{d-1}\mathbf p(\mathbf z)\).
Then \(\nabla\mathcal J(\mathbf b^\star)=d\delta\,\mathbf v\). We claim that \(\mathbf v\) lies in the cone generated by the vectors \(\mathbf p(\mathbf z)\) for \(\mathbf z\in\mathcal I\).

For completeness, that cone is closed: in a convergent sequence of its elements, the sum of the nonnegative coefficients in any generating representation equals the sum of the element's coordinates, since every generator has coordinate sum one. Those coefficient sums are bounded, so a convergent subsequence of coefficient vectors supplies a representation for the limit. The empty generated cone means \(\lbrace \mathbf0\rbrace\).

If \(\mathbf v\) did not belong to the cone, let \(\mathbf u\) be a nearest cone point. Euclidean projection onto a closed convex cone gives a vector \(\mathbf h=\mathbf u-\mathbf v\) such that \(\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf p(\mathbf z)\ge0\) for every contact and \(\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf v=-\lVert\mathbf v-\mathbf u\rVert^2<0\). These assertions follow directly by differentiating squared distance along segments from \(\mathbf u\) to cone points and then taking the cone points \(\mathbf0\) and \(2\mathbf u\). For sufficiently small \(t>0\), \(\mathbf b^\star+t\mathbf h\) is feasible: contact constraints cannot decrease, while the finitely many noncontact constraints have positive slack. But the directional derivative of \(\mathcal J\) along \(\mathbf h\) is \(d\delta\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf h<0\), contradicting optimality. This proves the claim.

There are therefore coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf z}\ge0\), indexed by the contacts, such that
$$
\mathbf v=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal I}\lambda_{\mathbf z}\mathbf p(\mathbf z),
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal I}\lambda_{\mathbf z}=T.
\tag{3}
$$
The second equality follows by summing coordinates in the first. In particular, \(\rho(\mathbf z)=\lambda_{\mathbf z}/T\) is a probability law on the contacts. Write \(\mathbb E_\nu\) and \(\mathbb E_\rho\) for expectations under these two laws, with \(\rho\) zero outside its contact set. Equation (3) implies that the barycentric means agree. Thus \(\mathbb E_\rho k_i=\mathbb E_\nu k_i=\mu_i\) for each \(i\), and contact equality yields
$$
\mathbb E_\nu[\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbf p]
=\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbb E_\nu\mathbf p
=\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbb E_\rho\mathbf p
=\mathbb E_\rho H(\mathbf p).
\tag{4}
$$
Each \(\mu_i\) is positive, since the corresponding vertex has positive \(\nu\)-weight, and \(\sum_i\mu_i=L\).

We now use (M) to compare the two entropy averages. Define \(g:[0,L]\to\mathbb R\) by affine interpolation between the values \(g(j)=j\log j\) at the integers \(0\le j\le L\), using \(0\log0=0\). The function \(t\log t\), continuously extended at zero, is convex. Hence its consecutive secant slopes are nondecreasing, \(g\) is convex, and \(g(t)\ge t\log t\). Its integer values are nonnegative, so \(g(t)\ge0\) as well. In particular,
\(g(t)\ge\max\lbrace 0,t\log t\rbrace\) for every \(0\le t\le L\).

The entropy identity on the integer support is
\(H(\mathbf p(\mathbf z))=\log L-L^{-1}\sum_i k_i(\mathbf z)\log k_i(\mathbf z)\).
Since \(g(k_i)=k_i\log k_i\), Jensen's inequality under the contact law gives
$$
\mathbb E_\rho H(\mathbf p)
\le\log L-\frac1L\sum_{i=0}^n g(\mu_i).
\tag{5}
$$
Under \(\nu\), size bias by \(k_i\) defines a probability law with probabilities \(k_i(\mathbf z)\nu(\mathbf z)/\mu_i\), supported where \(k_i>0\). Jensen's inequality for \(\log\) under precisely this law, followed by (M), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\nu[k_i\log k_i]
&\le\mu_i\log\left(\frac{\mathbb E_\nu k_i^2}{\mu_i}\right)\\
&\le\mu_i\log(1+2\mu_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Consequently,
$$
\mathbb E_\nu H(\mathbf p)
\ge\log L-\frac1L\sum_{i=0}^n\mu_i\log(1+2\mu_i).
\tag{7}
$$
For \(0<\mu\le1\), nonnegativity of \(g\) gives
\(\mu\log(1+2\mu)-g(\mu)\le\mu\log3\).
For \(\mu\ge1\), the bound \(g(\mu)\ge\mu\log\mu\) gives
\(\mu\log(1+2\mu)-g(\mu)\le\mu\log(2+1/\mu)\le\mu\log3\).
Using (4), (5), (7), and \(\sum_i\mu_i=L\), we obtain
$$
0\le
\mathbb E_\nu[\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbf p-H(\mathbf p)]
\le
\frac1L\sum_{i=0}^n
\bigl(\mu_i\log(1+2\mu_i)-g(\mu_i)\bigr)
\le\log3.
\tag{8}
$$
The left inequality is the integer majorant constraint itself. This derivation includes \(L=1\), when \(g=0\) throughout its domain; no real-profile entropy majorant has been substituted for the integer constraints.

Write \(J^\star=\mathcal J(\mathbf b^\star)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\varphi(\mathbf z)^d\). Sitewise, \(\varphi\ge r>0\), and convexity of \(t\mapsto t^d\) gives
\(\varphi^d-r^d\le d\varphi^{d-1}(\varphi-r)\).
Since \(\varphi-r=\delta(\mathbf b^\star\cdot\mathbf p-H)\), equation (8) and \(\varphi\ge a_{\min}\) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
0\le J^\star-W
&\le d\delta\,T\log3\\
&\le\frac{d\delta\log3}{a_{\min}}J^\star.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The binomial expansion of \((1+\delta\log2/a_{\min})^d\), all of whose terms are nonnegative, shows that \(d\delta\log2/a_{\min}\le\varepsilon\). Therefore (9) implies \(J^\star-W\le \mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}\varepsilon J^\star\). The exact inequality \(3^3=27<32=2^5\) gives \(\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}<5/3\), so \(\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}\varepsilon<1/6<1\). Rearrangement proves the first upper bound in (1). Finally,
\(\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}/(1-\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}\varepsilon)\le \mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}/(1-\mathsf c_{\mathrm{ent}}/10)<(5/3)/(1-1/6)=2\),
which proves the second. If \(\varepsilon=0\), then \(\delta=0\), already treated.

The function \(\varphi\) is affine and at least \(a_{\min}\) throughout \(P_L\), and its feasibility gives the required integer domination. Since the simplex vertices belong to \(D_+\), \(\operatorname{conv}(D_+)=P_L\). Thus \(\varphi\), the original support \(D_+\), the actual volumes \(w_{\mathbf z}\), and the aggregate error \(2\varepsilon W\) meet exactly the aggregate-majorant hypotheses of accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\). Its actual fiber-centroid conclusion gives (2), with the same \(n,d,W\), original integer-site multiplicities, and locked depth. This last application introduces no new comparison weights into \(W\).
