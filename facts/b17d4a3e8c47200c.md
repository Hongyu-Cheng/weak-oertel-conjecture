## Statement

The invertible affine maps preserving $\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$ bijectively are precisely $(z,x)\mapsto(Uz+k,Ax+Bz+b)$ with $U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$, $k\in\mathbb Z^n$, $A\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb R)$, and $B,b$ arbitrary. In a continuous such family through the identity the base map is the identity on the connected parameter component containing it. In particular the projected volume of a fixed set cannot vary under that family.

## Proof

On each fixed integer fiber the base coordinate of its affine image is a connected subset of $\mathbb Z^n$, so it is constant and the block from $x$ to the base vanishes. The remaining block matrix is triangular and invertible, hence its two diagonal blocks are invertible. Since the integer bases are mapped onto all integer bases, $k$ is integral and $U\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z^n$, giving the unimodular condition. Conversely the displayed form is invertible and bijects the mixed lattice. The entries of $U,k$ vary continuously in a discrete set and so are constant on the indicated component. They equal $I_n,0$ at the identity.
