## Statement

For any full positive three-fan through a triangle centroid with exactly one strictly positive vertex value per row, middle area vV, qualifying source triangles of areas>=V/4, and nonnegative weights totaling1-v, an explicit middle-volume increase to max(v,3/4), source replacement preserving every hit pattern, and normal interpolation to canonical cuts never increase the maximum fan score. The proof allows unequal cap costs and arbitrary weights and proves the exact fixed-fan surplus4/9+2(1-v)/9. The theorem is restricted to this centroid sign cone; no all-halfspace center theorem is inferred.

## Proof

# A monotone canonical reduction for all triangle-centroid vertex-cap fans

Research draft, 6 November 2026. This proves a normal and volume deformation for a full cone of non-symmetric planar fans, with arbitrary source weights. It uses a common uniform centroid but does not require cyclic equality of cap volumes. It excludes fans containing a cap with two positive original vertex values, and it makes no claim for nonuniform centers or arbitrary all-oblique fans.

## 1. Precise family

Let B be a triangle with |B|=vV, 1/4<=v<=1, and let g be its centroid. Let F be the3 by3 matrix of vertex values of three affine functions vanishing at g. Suppose its rows have rank2 and a strictly positive left kernel, so the cuts form a full positive fan. Assume every row has exactly one strictly positive vertex value and its other two vertex values are strictly negative.

Each column must have a positive entry, because a strictly positive combination of the rows is zero and all entries are nonzero. There are exactly three positive entries in total. After permuting the rows, the positive entries therefore occupy the diagonal. Write the two negative magnitudes of row i as a_i,b_i>0; its diagonal is r_i=a_i+b_i because its value at g is zero. Its positive cap contains exactly one vertex of B and has fraction

    p_i=r_i^2/[(r_i+a_i)(r_i+b_i)]
       =4/[9-delta_i^2],       delta_i=(a_i-b_i)/r_i.

Since |delta_i|<1,

    4/9<=p_i<1/2.                                    (1)

Let finitely many source triangles P_m subset B have areas at least V/4 and weights e_m>=0 summing to E=1-v. A source hits row i when it contains a point of strictly positive row value. Touching the cutting line alone is a miss. Scores are

    S_i=v p_i+sum_m e_m 1{P_m strictly hits row i}.

The theorem below preserves this strict convention exactly.

## 2. No qualifying source can hit only one row

Consider the region missing rows1 and2. Use coordinates X=lambda_1,Y=lambda_2,lambda_3=1-X-Y. Since row1 is negative at vertices2,3 and row2 at vertices1,3, their common negative region has vertices

    (0,0), (alpha,0), (1/3,1/3), (0,beta),            (2)

where0<alpha,beta<1/2 are the respective zero crossings on edges13 and23. The rows meet at g. There is no feasible point on edge12: row3 is negative along that whole edge, whereas positive dependence would force a positive weighted sum of rows1 and2 there. This proves the exhaustive quadrilateral description(2).

Its four vertex-triangle area ratios to B are

    alpha/3, beta/3, alpha beta,
    (alpha+beta)/3-alpha beta.                       (3)

Maximum inscribed triangle area is attained at polygon vertices, by successive maximization of the absolute affine determinant. The first two terms in(3) are less than1/6, the third is less than1/4, and the fourth is at most1/6. For the last assertion, the bilinear function(alpha+beta)/3-alpha beta has maximum1/6 at a corner of[0,1/2]^2. Thus the exact maximum among(3) is strictly below1/4.

Every source has ratio at least1/(4v)>=1/4. It cannot miss two rows. Missing all rows is also impossible, since a full positive fan's three closed negative halfplanes intersect only at g. Therefore every source hits at least two rows. If E>0, define the source hit fractions

    q_i=E^(-1)sum_m e_m 1{P_m strictly hits row i}.

Then0<=q_i<=1 and sum_i q_i>=2. The possible patterns are precisely single misses and the all-hit pattern, subject initially to their individual area feasibility.

## 3. Increasing the middle volume to three quarters never increases the maximum

Suppose first that v<3/4. Keep all barycentric source shapes and fan rows fixed while increasing the middle ratio to w in[v,3/4]. Scale the physical triangle and its sources homothetically so their area ratios remain fixed. Source areas can only increase, so the V/4 lower bound persists. Rescale each source weight by(1-w)/(1-v), preserving q_i and giving total weight1-w.

Let

    M(w)=max_i [w p_i+(1-w)q_i].

By(1) and sum_i q_i>=2,

    M(w)>=w(4/9)+(1-w)(2/3)>=1/2,
                                         v<=w<=3/4. (4)

For w_1<=w_2 in this interval, a row with q_i>p_i has nonincreasing score. A row with q_i<=p_i has score at most p_i<1/2, hence cannot exceed M(w_1) by(4). Therefore every row at w_2 is at most M(w_1), and

    M(w_2)<=M(w_1).                                 (5)

This is the transferable invariant: an active maximum is at least1/2, while every ordinary cap endpoint p_i is below1/2. Equal cap volumes are unnecessary. If the initial v is at least3/4, skip this phase. Put v_bar=max(v,3/4).

## 4. Universal fixed sources realize the same patterns

At volume v_bar V, for each row i define the sector triangle

    Q_i=conv(g, the two original vertices other than vertex i).

It has ratio1/3 to B, hence area v_bar V/3>=V/4. Its row-i values are zero at g and negative at its other vertices, so it misses row i exactly in the strict-support convention. It hits each other row because it contains that row's positive original vertex. Thus Q_i realizes the single-miss-i pattern for EVERY fan in the stated sign cone, independently of the unequal off-diagonal magnitudes. The all-hit pattern is realized by B itself.

Replace each original source by the appropriate Q_i or by B while retaining its rescaled weight. This preserves every q_i and therefore every score. No source count or weight equality is needed. At v_bar=3/4 the missed support at g is on the boundary and is deliberately counted as a miss; the source-volume equality is valid.

## 5. A genuine positive-fan deformation to canonical directions

Multiply each row of F by its corresponding strictly positive left-kernel coefficient. This changes neither cap nor hit pattern, and produces a matrix with both row and column sums zero. Its diagonal remains positive and all off-diagonal entries negative. Let

    F_s=(1-s)F+s(3I-11^T),       0<=s<=1.

Both kernels remain fixed. Every F_s has rank2: if F_s x=0, choose an index where x_i is largest. The row equation is a sum of strictly positive multiples of x_i-x_j, so all coordinates must equal. Thus its kernel is precisely span{1}; the positive left kernel is1, and all three cuts remain a full positive fan through g.

For a row with original negative magnitudes a_i,b_i and r_i=a_i+b_i, its normalized imbalance along this deformation is

    delta_i(s)=(1-s)(a_i-b_i)/[(1-s)r_i+2s].

Its absolute value decreases monotonically to zero. For example the ratio(1-s)/[(1-s)r_i+2s] has derivative-2/[(1-s)r_i+2s]^2<0. Formula(1) therefore implies that every own-cap fraction p_i(s) is nonincreasing. The fixed sector sources from Section4 preserve their exact hit patterns throughout the deformation, so all q_i remain unchanged. With v_bar fixed, EVERY score is nonincreasing during this phase.

At s=1 the fan consists of the three canonical positive facet orientations, at the same centroid. The source weights and their exact qualification remain valid. Combined with(5), this is a continuous parameter deformation, with a score-preserving source replacement between phases, whose maximum score never increases.

## 6. Scope and resulting bound

The mechanism proves a canonical reduction for all full positive3-fans through the uniform triangle centroid whose rows each have exactly one positive original vertex value. It includes arbitrary unequal off-diagonal entries, arbitrary positive balancing coefficients, and arbitrary source weights. The key properties are the exact singleton-capacity exclusion, the uniform interval4/9<=p_i<1/2, and the volume-independent sector sources of ratio1/3.

As a separate immediate consequence already visible before deformation,

    max_i S_i >=(1/3)sum_i S_i
       >=(4/9)v+(2/3)(1-v)
       =4/9+(2/9)(1-v).

This fixed-fan surplus is not a global center theorem. The deformation does not address nonuniform barycentric centers, rows with two positive vertex values, or the compatibility of several fans. In those settings the active-score threshold and fixed sector-source construction must be re-established rather than assumed.
