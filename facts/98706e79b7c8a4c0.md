## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), \(a_i>0\), \(R\ge0\), \(\theta_i\le0\), and \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) with at least one coordinate nonzero modulo three. For \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), suppose that
\[
D_r=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot z\le R,\ u\cdot z\equiv r\pmod3\}
\]
is nonempty, and define its complete original law by \(Z_r=\sum_{z\in D_r}e^{\theta\cdot z}\) and \(p_r(z)=e^{\theta\cdot z}/Z_r\). For \(j\in D_r\), let \(\tau_{p_r}(j)\) be the infimum of \(p_r(H\cap D_r)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j\), and put \(M_3(p_r)=\sum_{j\in D_r}p_r(j)\tau_{p_r}(j)^3\). These objects have exactly the meanings and normalizations of accepted fact `cbe047e54dd24a26`.

Retain that fact's absolute constants \(\alpha=(8/27)^{3/2}\), \(c_*\), \(B_*\), and \(\delta=\log(8\alpha)>0\), with no rescaling. Define the new absolute constants
\[
N_{\mathrm{all}}=\left\lceil\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{B_*+8}{\delta}\right)^2\right\}\right\rceil,
\qquad
\kappa_{\mathrm{all}}=\min\left\{c_*,\frac1{64\cdot8^{N_{\mathrm{all}}}}\right\}>0.
\]
Then every stated input satisfies
\[
M_3(p_r)\ge c_*\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2\right)^{-4}e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n
\ge c_*n^{-8}e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n,
\qquad
M_3(p_r)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{all}}8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]

More precisely, for each \(r\in\{1,2\}\), let \(e_i\) be the standard coordinate vectors, set \(I_+=\{i:u_i\equiv r\pmod3\}\) and \(I_-=\{i:u_i\equiv-r\pmod3\}\), and form the seed set
\[
\mathcal B_r=\{e_i:i\in I_+\}\ \cup\ \{e_i+e_h:i,h\in I_-,\ i\le h\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Delete a seed \(b\) only when \(a\cdot b>R\), and call the remaining set \(\mathcal B_r'\). This set is nonempty, has cardinality at most \(n(n+1)/2\), and gives the exact cover
\[
D_r=\bigcup_{b\in\mathcal B_r'}C_b,
\qquad
C_b=b+D_0^{(b)},
\qquad
D_0^{(b)}=\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot y\le R-a\cdot b,\ u\cdot y\equiv0\pmod3\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Overlaps in this cover are permitted. Define \(Z_0^{(b)}=\sum_{y\in D_0^{(b)}}e^{\theta\cdot y}\), \(q_b(y)=e^{\theta\cdot y}/Z_0^{(b)}\), and \(\pi_b=p_r(C_b)\). Then
\[
\pi_b=\frac{e^{\theta\cdot b}Z_0^{(b)}}{Z_r},
\qquad p_r(b+y)=\pi_bq_b(y),
\qquad M_3(p_r)\ge\pi_b^4M_3(q_b)
\tag{4}
\]
for every \(b\in\mathcal B_r'\), where \(M_3(q_b)\) uses the complete zero-coset support \(D_0^{(b)}\) and ambient closed-halfspace depth. In particular some \(b\) has \(\pi_b\ge2/[n(n+1)]\). Neither \(Z_r\) nor the original root probabilities are replaced in the conclusion (1).

## Proof

Positive resource coefficients make every \(D_r\) finite. Its nonemptiness makes \(Z_r\) finite and positive. First suppose \(r\in\{1,2\}\). The three residue classes of coefficients modulo three are exactly zero, \(r\), and \(-r\); the last two are distinct because \(r\ne0\pmod3\).

Every seed in (2) is nonnegative and has \(u\cdot b\equiv r\pmod3\). This is immediate for \(e_i\) with \(i\in I_+\). For a pair from \(I_-\), including a repeated index, its residue is \(-2r\equiv r\pmod3\). Thus, for each retained seed, translation by \(b\) bijects \(D_0^{(b)}\) with
\(C_b=\{z\in D_r:z_i\ge b_i\text{ for every }i\}\). Indeed, subtracting \(b\) preserves integrality, changes residue \(r\) to zero, and changes the resource capacity from \(R\) to \(R-a\cdot b\); coordinatewise nonnegativity is equivalent to \(z\ge b\). The converse follows by adding \(b\). Its translated capacity is nonnegative, and \(D_0^{(b)}\) contains zero.

To prove that these sets cover, fix any \(z\in D_r\). If \(z_i\ge1\) for some \(i\in I_+\), choose \(b=e_i\). Then \(b\le z\) coordinatewise. If no such coordinate exists, all coordinates indexed by \(I_+\) vanish. Put \(K=\sum_{i\in I_-}z_i\), a nonnegative integer. Coordinates with coefficients zero modulo three contribute nothing to the residue, so
\(r\equiv u\cdot z\equiv-rK\pmod3\). Since \(r\) is invertible modulo three, this implies \(K\equiv2\pmod3\), hence \(K\ge2\). There are consequently two coordinate units among the \(I_-\) coordinates of \(z\). If some such coordinate is at least two, choose the corresponding repeated-index seed \(2e_i\). Otherwise choose two distinct positive coordinates and their seed \(e_i+e_h\), ordered so that \(i<h\). Again \(b\le z\).

In either case positivity of the resource coefficients gives \(a\cdot b\le a\cdot z\le R\), so this seed is retained. Subtracting it shows \(z\in C_b\). Since each \(C_b\subseteq D_r\), this proves (3) and the nonemptiness of \(\mathcal B_r'\).

If \(k=|I_+|\) and \(l=|I_-|\), the seeds in (2) are distinct and their number is \(k+l(l+1)/2\). Since \(k+l\le n\) and \(0\le l\le n\),
\[
|\mathcal B_r'|\le k+\frac{l(l+1)}2
\le n+\frac{l(l-1)}2
\le\frac{n(n+1)}2.
\tag{5}
\]
The last inequality holds also for \(l=0\); for integer \(0\le l\le n\), one has \(l(l-1)\le n(n-1)\).

For every retained seed, the exact score identity is \(\theta\cdot(b+y)=\theta\cdot b+\theta\cdot y\). Summation over its support proves the first two identities in (4), always dividing by the original \(Z_r\). Thus \(q_b\) is precisely the pullback of the conditional law on \(C_b\), with all original atoms in that cone retained. Its coefficients \(a_i\) remain positive, its scores \(\theta_i\) remain nonpositive, its capacity is \(R-a\cdot b\ge0\), and its congruence vector is the same nonzero vector \(u\) modulo three. It therefore satisfies every hypothesis of the zero-residue estimate in accepted fact `cbe047e54dd24a26`, with only that fact's resource capacity \(R\) aliased to \(R-a\cdot b\), its zero-coset support to \(D_0^{(b)}\), its original zero-coset normalizer to \(Z_0^{(b)}\), and its original law to \(q_b\). In particular,
\(M_3(q_b)\ge c_*e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n\).

For clarity, the transfer to original depths does not presume any lower bound on the mass of a nonzero residue under an unrestricted law. If \(y\in D_0^{(b)}\) and \(H\) is any closed halfspace containing \(b+y\), its translate \(H-b\) is a closed halfspace containing \(y\). Using only the atoms in \(C_b\) gives
\[
p_r(H\cap D_r)\ge p_r(H\cap C_b)
=\pi_bq_b((H-b)\cap D_0^{(b)})
\ge\pi_b\tau_{q_b}(y).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves \(\tau_{p_r}(b+y)\ge\pi_b\tau_{q_b}(y)\). Translation is invertible on the entire ambient space, so there is no change of the halfspace convention. Keeping the original root weights and discarding only nonnegative terms outside this one cone, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p_r)
&\ge\sum_{y\in D_0^{(b)}}p_r(b+y)\tau_{p_r}(b+y)^3\\
&\ge\sum_{y\in D_0^{(b)}}\pi_bq_b(y)\bigl(\pi_b\tau_{q_b}(y)\bigr)^3
=\pi_b^4M_3(q_b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This proves the last inequality in (4), including its fourth power.

Because the cones cover \(D_r\), their original masses satisfy \(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_r'}\pi_b\ge1\), regardless of overlaps. Select one with maximal \(\pi_b\). Equation (5) gives \(\pi_b\ge1/|\mathcal B_r'|\ge2/[n(n+1)]\). Applying (6) and the accepted zero-residue estimate proves the first inequality in (1) for \(r=1,2\). For \(r=0\), accepted fact `cbe047e54dd24a26` gives the stronger estimate without the polynomial factor, and \(n(n+1)/2\ge1\). Thus the same inequality holds for all three residues. Since \(n(n+1)/2\le n^2\) for \(n\ge1\), its second inequality follows as well.

It remains to absorb the polynomial loss into the absolute \(8^{-n}\) estimate. For \(x\ge1\), the function \(\log x/\sqrt x\) has maximum \(2/e<1\), as follows by differentiation; hence \(\log n\le\sqrt n\). The first part of (1) therefore implies
\(M_3(p_r)\ge c_*e^{-(B_*+8)\sqrt n}\alpha^n\). If \(n\ge N_{\mathrm{all}}\), its definition gives \((B_*+8)\sqrt n\le\delta n\). Since \(e^{-\delta n}\alpha^n=8^{-n}\), we obtain \(M_3(p_r)\ge c_*8^{-n}\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{all}}8^{-n}\) in this range.

For \(1\le n<N_{\mathrm{all}}\), use the original-law intrinsic-parity baseline \(M_3(p_r)\ge64^{-n}/64\) for every nonempty residue support, also proved in accepted fact `cbe047e54dd24a26`. Dividing it by \(8^{-n}\) gives \(8^{-n}/64\ge1/(64\cdot8^{N_{\mathrm{all}}})\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{all}}\). This proves the final assertion of (1). Nonzero supports of translated capacity zero are singletons and are already included in the accepted zero-coset estimate. All comparisons above retain the weak resource inequality, all boundary atoms, and the original law on the complete \(D_r\).
