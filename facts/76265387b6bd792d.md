## Statement

Under the hypotheses of the one-noncentral two-branch inverse, suppose there are two actual roots $K_-<K_+$. Normalize their signed vertex values at the noncentral vertex to one, writing $n_\pm=(1,q_\pm,-r_\pm,-s_\pm)$. Then $q_->q_+>1$, $r_->r_+>0$, and $s_->s_+>0$. If a third actual strict quadrilateral section with the same centroid lies on the line through these points in this chart and has a different split, its representative is $n_++t(n_+-n_-)$ with $t>0$. If it captures vertex 3 and excludes vertex 4 in this orientation, then
$$
\max\left\{\frac{q_+}{q_--q_+},\frac{r_++1}{r_--r_+}\right\}<t<\frac{s_+}{s_--s_+}.
$$
The symmetric interval interchanges $r,s$. In any positive dependence of the three actual margins, the larger-$K$ section is the unique one oriented away from the noncentral vertex after a common reversal if necessary.

## Proof

The inverse proof gives that $y=K+J/(\delta-2K)$ is strictly increasing on $I$ because $J$ is positive and increasing. Also $z'=-1+(1+R')/(G-K)+(K+R)/(G-K)^2>0$ since $R'>0$ and $0<G-K<1$; the same reasoning gives $w'>0$. At actual roots $x=1-y-z-w$, so $x$ strictly decreases between the two roots. Therefore the positive ratios $x/y,x/z,x/w$ strictly decrease from $K_-$ to $K_+$. At each root the actual centroid equations and $A>0\ge B$ give $Kx>N\ge Ky$ with $K>0$, so $x/y>1$. This inequality applies after relabeling any strict quadrilateral section with the same one noncentral coordinate, since its other captured coordinate is at most $1/3$ and its excluded coordinates are strictly below $1/3$ by the inverse proof.

On the segment between $n_-,n_+$, and on the ray beyond $n_-$ away from $n_+$, all three parameters $q,r,s$ stay strictly positive, so those points have the original split. A different split is therefore on the other ray as stated. Its parameter must make the second margin negative, the third margin greater than one by the relabeled inequality, and the fourth margin negative. These three strict inequalities give exactly the displayed necessary interval. Finally, $n_+$ is strictly between the other two distinct affine points, so any dependence of their chart representatives has the middle coefficient of opposite sign from the two outer coefficients. Every oriented raw margin with those same planes is a nonzero scalar multiple of its chart representative, and its sign at vertex 1 is the sign of that scalar. With positive dependence coefficients, the unique differing sign is therefore the middle section's; global reversal gives the claimed orientation.
