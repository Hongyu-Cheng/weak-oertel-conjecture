## Statement

Let $A,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be planar convex bodies of equal area $a>0$. Put $M=(A+B)/2$, $r=|M|/a$, and let $g_M$ be its ordinary centroid. In the convex hull of the lifted endpoint bodies at bases $-1,1$, the only integer fibers are $A,M,B$ at $-1,0,1$. For every closed ambient halfspace containing $(0,g_M)$ the fraction of total mixed area it captures is at least
$$
 G(r)=\frac{4r/9+\max\{1/9,\;4\sqrt{5r}/3-20r/9\}}{r+2}.
$$
In particular the normalized depth at that mark is at least
$$
 \frac{4(22+12\sqrt 2)}{9(57+12\sqrt 2)}>\frac29 .
$$

## Proof

The hull has section $M$ at base zero because the endpoint weights there are both $1/2$. Its extreme integer sections are the two given endpoint bodies. Its mixed area is $a(r+2)$, and $(0,g_M)$ belongs to the middle fiber. Brunn–Minkowski gives $r\ge1$.

Move a containing closed halfspace inward to pass through $(0,g_M)$. With a nonzero continuous normal its endpoint thresholds have the opposite shifts $u\cdot g_M\pm t$, and the middle cap is the ordinary centroid cap with threshold $u\cdot g_M$. The latter has area at least $4|M|/9$ by the full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d. The direction-free supporting-height bound gives paired area at least $a/9$. Apply the scalar complementary-cap lemma in f0399a8f0bae48c6 with equal endpoint areas $a$, midpoint $M$ as its middle body, and the same directional thresholds. The opposite middle area is at most $5|M|/9$ by the full-mass bound, so the lemma gives paired area at least
$$
 a[1-(2\sqrt{5r/9}-1)^2]_+
  =a[4\sqrt{5r}/3-20r/9]_+ .
$$
The inner positive part in the lemma is redundant since $r\ge1$. Thus the total captured fraction is at least $G(r)$. With a purely integer normal the closed through-source cap contains the entire middle fiber and one endpoint. Its fraction $(r+1)/(r+2)$ is at least $G(r)$ because the paired lower-bound term in $G$ is at most one and $4r/9\le r$.

Put $F(r)=4\sqrt{5r}/3-20r/9$. It strictly decreases for $r\ge1$ since $F'(r)=2\sqrt5/(3\sqrt r)-20/9<0$. The equation $F(r)=1/9$ is equivalent to $12\sqrt{5r}=20r+1$ and has the unique solution $r_*=(17+12\sqrt2)/20$ on $[1,\infty)$, by squaring the two positive sides. On $[1,r_*]$ the derivative of $G(r)=(12\sqrt{5r}-16r)/(9(r+2))$ has the sign of $6\sqrt5(2-r)/\sqrt r-32$, which is negative for $r\ge1$. On $[r_*,\infty)$, $G(r)=(4r+1)/(9(r+2))$ is increasing. Its minimum is therefore
$$
 G(r_*)=\frac{4r_*+1}{9(r_*+2)}=\frac{4(22+12\sqrt 2)}{9(57+12\sqrt 2)} .
$$
The difference from $2/9$ has numerator $2(12\sqrt2-13)>0$ over the common positive denominator $9(57+12\sqrt2)$.
