## Statement

All lattices and finite sets in this lemma concern the integer base. We use \(\Gamma\) for a discrete lattice and \(T\) for a finite symmetric set, leaving the locked problem's mixed-integer notation \(\Lambda\) and \(S\) unchanged.

Let \(r\geq0\) be an integer, let \(L\) be an \(r\)-dimensional real vector space, and let \(\Gamma\subseteq L\) be a rank-\(r\) lattice spanning \(L\). Let \(T\subseteq\Gamma\) be finite and nonempty, with \(T=-T\), \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}T=L\), and \(K\cap\Gamma=T\), where \(K:=\operatorname{conv}T\).

When \(r\geq1\), choose any inclusion-maximal subset \(V\subseteq T\) such that \(v-v'\notin\tfrac12K\) for every two distinct \(v,v'\in V\). When \(r=0\), take \(V=\{0\}\). With \(m:=|V|\) and \(P:=\operatorname{conv}V\), one has
\[
m\leq5^r,\qquad
\tfrac12K\subseteq P\subseteq K,\qquad
|P\cap\Gamma|=|P\cap T|\geq4^{-r}|T|.
\]
More precisely, if \(B\) is any coset of \(4\Gamma\) in \(\Gamma\) for which \(|T\cap B|\geq4^{-r}|T|\), and \(a\in T\cap B\), then \(x\mapsto(x-a)/4\) is an injection from \(T\cap B\) into \(\tfrac12K\cap\Gamma\subseteq P\cap T\). Such a coset exists.

The symmetric-set hypotheses can be obtained by the following parity selection. Let \(n\geq1\) and let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, with \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). For each \(\epsilon\in\mathbb Z^n/2\mathbb Z^n\), let \(N_\epsilon\) be the number of points of \(A\) in that parity class. There is \(j\in A\) such that, on putting \(T:=(A-j)\cap(j-A)\), \(L:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}T\), \(r:=\dim L\), \(\Gamma:=L\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and \(K:=\operatorname{conv}T\), all hypotheses above hold and \(|T|\geq(\sum_\epsilon N_\epsilon^2)/|A|\geq2^{-n}|A|\).
Consequently the resulting \(V\) and \(P\) satisfy \(j+V\subseteq A\), \(j+P\subseteq\operatorname{conv}A\), and \(|(j+P)\cap\mathbb Z^n|\geq2^{-n}4^{-r}|A|\), with at most \(5^r\) selected generators.

## Proof

First suppose \(r=0\). Then \(L=\Gamma=T=K=\{0\}\), so \(V=P=\{0\}\) gives all assertions of the first part. The sole coset modulo \(4\Gamma\) is \(\{0\}\), and its indicated map fixes zero.

Suppose henceforth that \(r\geq1\). The set \(K\) is compact, convex, symmetric about zero, and full-dimensional in \(L\). Indeed, choose \(r\) linearly independent vectors in \(T\); the convex hull of these vectors and their negatives is contained in \(K\) and contains a neighborhood of zero in \(L\). Thus \(K\) is the closed unit ball of a norm on \(L\), and \(v-v'\notin\tfrac12K\) means that their distance in this norm is strictly greater than \(1/2\).

A maximal set \(V\) can be constructed by starting with any point of \(T\), then repeatedly adjoining a point \(x\in T\) for which \(x-v\notin\tfrac12K\) for every point \(v\) already selected. Finiteness of \(T\) makes this process terminate. The terminal set is inclusion-maximal. The arguments below apply to every inclusion-maximal set, regardless of this choice of ordering.

Symmetry and convexity give \(K-K=2K\). For distinct \(v,v'\in V\), the closed translates \(v+\tfrac14K\) and \(v'+\tfrac14K\) are disjoint: an intersection would imply \(v-v'\in\tfrac14(K-K)=\tfrac12K\), contradicting the strict separation. Each translate is contained in \(K+\tfrac14K=\tfrac54K\). Let \(\operatorname{vol}_r\) be Lebesgue measure on \(L\) in any linear coordinates. The volume \(\operatorname{vol}_r(K)\) is finite and positive. Finite additivity, containment, and the scaling rule give
\[
|V|\,4^{-r}\operatorname{vol}_r(K)
=\operatorname{vol}_r\!\left(\bigcup_{v\in V}(v+\tfrac14K)\right)
\leq(5/4)^r\operatorname{vol}_r(K).
\]
Dividing proves \(|V|\leq5^r\).

Maximality gives a closed covering of the finite set: for every \(x\in T\), some \(v\in V\) satisfies \(x-v\in\tfrac12K\). Otherwise \(x\) could be adjoined to \(V\). For a linear functional \(u\) on \(L\) and a nonempty compact set \(D\subseteq L\), write \(h_D(u):=\max_{x\in D}u(x)\). The covering implies \(u(x)\leq h_P(u)+\tfrac12h_K(u)\) for every \(x\in T\). Since \(K=\operatorname{conv}T\), taking the maximum over \(T\) yields \(h_K(u)\leq h_P(u)+\tfrac12h_K(u)\), hence \(h_P(u)\geq\tfrac12h_K(u)\) for every \(u\).

These inequalities imply \(\tfrac12K\subseteq P\). For completeness, equip \(L\) with an inner product. If \(x\in\tfrac12K\) were outside \(P\), compactness of \(P\) would give a closest point \(p\in P\). Minimizing squared distance along the segment from \(p\) to any \(y\in P\) gives \(\langle x-p,y-p\rangle\leq0\). For \(u(y):=\langle x-p,y\rangle\), this implies \(h_P(u)=u(p)<u(x)\leq\tfrac12h_K(u)\), a contradiction. The inclusion \(P\subseteq K\) follows directly from \(V\subseteq T\).

Choose a lattice basis of \(\Gamma\). Its integer coordinates identify \(\Gamma/4\Gamma\) with \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^r\), so there are exactly \(4^r\) cosets. Some coset \(B\) has \(|T\cap B|\geq4^{-r}|T|>0\). Fix \(a\in T\cap B\). If \(x\in T\cap B\), then \(x-a\in4\Gamma\), so \((x-a)/4\in\Gamma\). Also \(x,a\in K\), whence \(x-a\in K-K=2K\) and \((x-a)/4\in\tfrac12K\). The affine map \(x\mapsto(x-a)/4\) is injective. Therefore \(|\tfrac12K\cap\Gamma|\geq|T\cap B|\geq4^{-r}|T|\). Finally \(\tfrac12K\subseteq P\subseteq K\) and \(K\cap\Gamma=T\) give \(P\cap\Gamma=P\cap T\) and the claimed cardinality. This proof uses closed sets throughout, including lattice points on their boundaries.

It remains to establish the parity selection and its application. For each \(j\in A\), put \(T_j:=(A-j)\cap(j-A)\). Ordered pairs \((a,b)\in A^2\) in the same parity class correspond bijectively to pairs \((j,t)\) with \(j\in A\) and \(t\in T_j\): set \(j=(a+b)/2\) and \(t=(a-b)/2\), with inverse \((a,b)=(j+t,j-t)\). Same parity makes \(j,t\) integral, and lattice convexity of \(A\) places the midpoint \(j\) in \(A\). Thus
\[
\sum_{j\in A}|T_j|=\sum_\epsilon N_\epsilon^2
\geq\frac{(\sum_\epsilon N_\epsilon)^2}{2^n}
=\frac{|A|^2}{2^n}.
\]
The inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz over all \(2^n\) parity classes, including empty ones. Choosing \(j\) with \(|T_j|\) at least its average gives both asserted lower bounds for \(|T|\), where \(T=T_j\).

The set \(T\) is finite, symmetric, and contains zero. With \(L=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}T\) and \(r=\dim L\), the group \(\Gamma=L\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is discrete and contains \(r\) linearly independent vectors spanning \(L\); hence it is a rank-\(r\) lattice in \(L\). To verify \(K\cap\Gamma=T\), inclusion of \(T\) is immediate. Conversely, let \(x\in K\cap\Gamma\). Every \(t\in T\) satisfies \(j+t,j-t\in A\), so convexity gives \(j+x,j-x\in\operatorname{conv}A\). These two points are integral. Lattice convexity of \(A\) therefore places both in \(A\), and consequently \(x\in(A-j)\cap(j-A)=T\).

Apply the first part to this \(T\) and \(\Gamma\), including the case \(r=0\). Since \(V\subseteq T\), every point of \(j+V\) lies in \(A\), and \(j+P\subseteq\operatorname{conv}A\). Because \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(P\subseteq L\), translation by \(j\) identifies \(P\cap\Gamma\) with \((j+P)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The first part and the parity lower bound now give \(|(j+P)\cap\mathbb Z^n|\geq4^{-r}|T|\geq2^{-n}4^{-r}|A|\), as required.
