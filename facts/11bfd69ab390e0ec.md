## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be compact and convex, and define
\[
A_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in A\},
\qquad
a(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
Fix \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\), \(0<p<1/n\), and \(0<\rho<2^n\). Set
\[
M_\epsilon:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}a(2k+\epsilon),
\qquad
T:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a(z).
\]
There is a positive integer \(N=N_{n,\rho,p}\), depending only on \(n,\rho,p\), such that at least one of the following alternatives holds:
\[
M_\epsilon\leq \frac{T}{2^n-\rho};
\tag{1}
\]
or there are \(N\) parallel affine hyperplanes in \(\mathbb R^n\) whose union contains \(F\)-mass strictly greater than
\[
\left(1-2^{\,n-1/p}\right)M_\epsilon,
\qquad
F(k):=a(2k+\epsilon).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, the hyperplanes in the second alternative may be taken to have one common primitive integer normal: there exist a primitive \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{0\}\) and integers \(m_1,\ldots,m_N\) such that
\[
\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z^n\\
v^{\mathsf T}k\in\{m_1,\ldots,m_N\}}}
a(2k+\epsilon)
>
\left(1-2^{\,n-1/p}\right)M_\epsilon.
\tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, on the original parity class the captured points lie in the parallel primitive integer-normal hyperplanes
\[
v^{\mathsf T}z=v^{\mathsf T}\epsilon+2m_i
\qquad(1\leq i\leq N).
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof

Because \(A\) is compact, its projection to \(\mathbb R^n\) is compact. Hence only finitely many \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) have nonempty \(A_z\), so all functions and sums below have finite support.

Define functions \(F,G,h:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}\) by
\[
F(k)=G(k):=a(2k+\epsilon),
\qquad
h(s):=a(s+\epsilon).
\]
Then \(\sum F=\sum G=M_\epsilon\), while translation by \(\epsilon\) permutes \(\mathbb Z^n\), so
\[
\sum h=T.
\tag{5}
\]

We verify the pointwise hypothesis of van Hintum's theorem. Fix \(k,\ell\in\mathbb Z^n\). If \(F(k)G(\ell)=0\), the source convention for a negative power mean gives
\[
M_{-p,1/2}(F(k),G(\ell))=0,
\]
and therefore \(h(k+\ell)\geq M_{-p,1/2}(F(k),G(\ell))\) follows from nonnegativity. Suppose instead that both values are positive. Convexity of \(A\) gives
\[
\frac12 A_{2k+\epsilon}+\frac12 A_{2\ell+\epsilon}
\subseteq A_{k+\ell+\epsilon}.
\]
The \(d\)-dimensional Brunn--Minkowski inequality therefore yields
\[
h(k+\ell)^{1/d}
\geq
\frac12F(k)^{1/d}+\frac12G(\ell)^{1/d},
\]
or equivalently
\[
h(k+\ell)\geq M_{1/d,1/2}(F(k),G(\ell)).
\]
The power means are increasing in their order on positive inputs, and \(1/d>-p\), so
\[
h(k+\ell)\geq M_{-p,1/2}(F(k),G(\ell)).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (6) holds for every \(k,\ell\), including the zero-support cases.

Apply Theorem 1.2 of the source in lattice dimension \(n\), with its approximation parameter equal to \(\rho\). It supplies an integer \(N=N_{n,\rho,p}\) such that, if every family of \(N\) parallel affine hyperplanes satisfies
\[
\sum_{k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^N H_i}F(k)
\leq
\left(1-2^{\,n-1/p}\right)\sum F,
\tag{7}
\]
then
\[
\sum h\geq(2^n-\rho)\sum F.
\tag{8}
\]
By (5), (8) is exactly \(T\geq(2^n-\rho)M_\epsilon\), which is (1).

If (1) fails, then \(T<(2^n-\rho)M_\epsilon\). Consequently (7) cannot hold for every family of \(N\) parallel hyperplanes, by the contrapositive of the source theorem. Hence some such family satisfies the strict reverse inequality, which is (2). Notice that \(p<1/n\) implies \(0<1-2^{\,n-1/p}<1\).

It remains to justify the primitive integer-normal formulation. Write the hyperplanes obtained above as
\[
H_i=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:u^{\mathsf T}x=c_i\},
\qquad u\neq0,
\]
with common real normal \(u\). Let \(V\) be the rational span of all differences
\[
x-y,\qquad x,y\in H_i\cap\mathbb Z^n
\]
where \(i\) may vary but \(x,y\) belong to the same \(H_i\). Every such difference lies in \(u^\perp\), so \(\dim V\leq n-1\). Since \(V\) is a rational subspace, there is a nonzero rational vector orthogonal to \(V\). Clearing denominators and dividing by the greatest common divisor of its coordinates gives a primitive \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{0\}\) orthogonal to \(V\).

For every \(i\) with \(H_i\cap\mathbb Z^n\neq\varnothing\), choose \(k_i\) in that intersection and put \(m_i=v^{\mathsf T}k_i\in\mathbb Z\). For every \(k\in H_i\cap\mathbb Z^n\), the difference \(k-k_i\) belongs to \(V\), so
\[
v^{\mathsf T}k=m_i.
\]
Thus each \(H_i\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is contained in a hyperplane \(\{k:v^{\mathsf T}k=m_i\}\). Removing duplicate or empty members and, if necessary, adjoining unused parallel integer-offset hyperplanes gives exactly \(N\) parallel hyperplanes with primitive common normal \(v\), without decreasing the captured \(F\)-mass. This proves (3).

Finally, if \(z=2k+\epsilon\), then \(v^{\mathsf T}k=m_i\) is equivalent to
\[
v^{\mathsf T}z=v^{\mathsf T}\epsilon+2m_i.
\]
This proves (4) and completes the alternative.
