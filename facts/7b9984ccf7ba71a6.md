## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, with $k$ occupied parities modulo $2\mathbb Z^n$, and write $B=\operatorname{conv}P$. Let $K_z$ be positive compact convex $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$, satisfying $\sum_z\lambda_z K_z\subseteq K_p$ for every convex representation $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$. Set $V_z=|K_z|_d$, $M=\sum_z V_z$, and let $\Sigma$ be the mass-normalized pooled within-fiber covariance. Use invariant buffered depth at fixed $\varepsilon>0$, with marked projective energy $E$ extended by zero to own boundaries, positive affine multiplier $\ell$ on $B$ satisfying $\sum_zV_z\log\ell(z)=0$, and closed cap margins
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon(\ell(p)E_{K_p}(y)/M)^{1/(d+1)}
 \sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}
$$
at a mark $(p,y)$, for $(a,u)\ne(0,0)$. Thus pure-base caps are unshifted. Let $Q>0$ be the global raw buffered maximum on the actual sampled marks, and $I$ the set of bases attaining it. Denote the smallest face containing $p$ in $B$ by $F_p$ and let $b_{\mathrm{base}}(p)$ be the raw closed-halfspace depth of $p$ for the base masses $V_z$.

Assume every $p\in I$ satisfies $b_{\mathrm{base}}(p)=\sum_{z\in P\cap F_p}V_z=Q$ and every such $F_p$ is a simplex. If $Q/M<\gamma_d/k$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, let $U=P\cap\bigcup_{p\in I}F_p$ and $M_U=\sum_{z\in U}V_z$. The union has $M_U\le kQ<M$. There are interior centers $c_z\in\operatorname{int}K_z$ for $z\in U$, affine on each selected face, such that simultaneously contracting the $U$-fibers about these centers by $1-t$, with $0<t<1$, gives exact positive compatible fibers and leaves all other sampled fibers fixed. For the updated mass, covariance and energy, and the scalar-renormalized affine multiplier, the new global normalized buffered maximum satisfies
$$
\frac{Q(t)}{M(t)}\le\frac{rQ}{M-M_U(1-r)}<\frac QM,\qquad r=(1-t)^d,
$$
for all sufficiently small $t>0$. The same result holds for nonsimplex faces when coherent affine maps taking their sampled bases to the respective fiber interiors are given. In particular the simplex assumption is automatic when $\dim B=2$.

## Proof

The convex-superlevel and same-parity midpoint argument in the proof of f5e6736b5b1877c7 applies with the identical cap model: $P$ is lattice-complete, the affine array is positive and sampled-concave, $K_z$ are actual by anchored compatibility, and the positive covariance defines the norm there. Concretely the profile $(\ell E/M)^{1/(d+1)}$ on the canonical hull is a weighted geometric mean of the positive affine $\ell$ and the nonnegative concave energy root. The exact closed quantile constraint in each direction is convex in the mark. Taking two maximizing marks at coordinatewise same-parity bases yields a maximizing midpoint by convexity and completeness. This uses only the superlevel portion of that proof, independently of its later hinge deformation. It also works for parity in the inherited lattice since differences of integer points of the affine span have integral half-difference whenever they are coordinatewise even.

Each selected face has mass $Q<M$, so it is proper. Distinct selected faces form an antichain because a proper inclusion adds a vertex of $B$ of positive mass. Choose for each distinct face $F_i$ a maximizing base $p_i\in\operatorname{relint}F_i$ whose smallest face is $F_i$. If $p_i,p_j$ were in the same parity, their midpoint would be in $I$. The smallest face containing that midpoint is the smallest face containing both $F_i$ and $F_j$, since a face containing a strict midpoint contains both inputs. This face strictly contains $F_i$ and hence has mass greater than $Q$, a contradiction. There are therefore at most $k$ selected faces. Counting overlaps only once yields $M_U\le kQ<\gamma_d M<M$.

Choose a single $c_v\in\operatorname{int}K_v$ at each vertex belonging to the union of selected faces. On each simplex $F_i$ interpolate its vertex values affinely. At every sampled $z\in F_i$, the convex combination of interior balls at those vertices gives an interior ball in $K_z$ by compatibility, proving that the interpolated point is interior. The intersection of two faces, when nonempty, is a common face with precisely the common vertices. Affine interpolation agrees there. This defines a coherent $c_z$ on $U$. For nonsimplex faces use the supplied maps.

Define $K_z(t)=c_z+(1-t)(K_z-c_z)$ for $z\in U$ and $K_z(t)=K_z$ otherwise. Each changed body is nested in its old one. If an anchored target $p$ is outside $U$, its inclusion follows from nesting and the old compatibility. If $p\in U$, choose any selected face $F_i$ containing $p$. Every positive-weight input to its anchored representation lies in $F_i$, since $F_i$ is a face, and the centers are affine there. Thus
$$
\sum_z\lambda_z K_z(t)
 =c_p+(1-t)\left(\sum_z\lambda_z K_z-c_p\right)\subseteq K_p(t).
$$
This verifies every anchored inclusion, including targets on overlaps. The convex hull of the lifted new fibers has exactly those fibers at sampled bases, by grouping a convex combination by base and applying the inclusion, and has no extra integer bases by completeness of $P$.

Every volume in $U$ is multiplied by $r$, so $M(t)=M-M_U(1-r)$ and the mass on each selected face is $rQ$. An exposing closed pure-base halfspace with trace exactly that face bounds the buffered depth of every mark over it by $rQ$. Renormalize using
$$
\ell_t=\exp\left[-\frac{\sum_z V_z(t)\log\ell(z)}{M(t)}\right]\ell .
$$
The scalar tends to one and restores the geometric-mean condition. All fibers converge in Hausdorff distance on the same finite base, with positive limits. The multiplier is constant in the mark variable at each base and converges there. Thus the matched-mark estimate in f2ead43a963c7026 applies with actual updated $M(t),\Sigma(t)$ and energy. For each base outside $U$ the old maximum is attained and strictly below $Q$. The finite collection of outside maxima stays below $rQ$ for small positive $t$ since $rQ\to Q$. Consequently $Q(t)\le rQ$, and the displayed ratio is strictly smaller since $(1-r)(M-M_U)>0$.

If a full ambient dimensional realization is desired when $B$ is deficient, let $L=\operatorname{span}(P-P)$ and thicken the canonical hull by a small closed ball in $L^\perp$ in the base variables only. Integer linear forms annihilating $L$ can be chosen with common kernel $L$ because $P$ is integral. Take the ball small enough that the magnitude of each such form on it is less than one. An integer base of the thickened hull differs from a fixed $p_0\in P$ by an integer vector, on which the forms take integer values. They must all vanish, so its transverse displacement is zero. This leaves the sampled fibers and the objective exactly as proved. In dimension two all nonempty proper faces of $B$ are vertices or segments. The proof therefore includes every specified atomic maximizing base paid by its own minimal edge or vertex in that case.
