## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(r>0\), and \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n\).  Let
\[
f:\mathbf u+r[-1,1]^n\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
be multi-affine, meaning affine in each coordinate separately when the other coordinates are fixed.  Then there is a Borel map
\(\mathcal R_f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb Z^n\) such that, for every
\(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_i\in
\{\lfloor q_i\rfloor,\lceil q_i\rceil\}
\quad(1\le i\le n),
\]
\[
f\!\left(\mathbf u+r(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)-\mathbf q)\right)
\ge f(\mathbf u),
\]
and, for every nonnegative function
\(\phi:\mathbb Z^n\to[0,\infty]\),
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\phi(\mathbf z).
\]
Moreover, the construction is phase-equivariant:
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q+\mathbf k)=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)+\mathbf k
\qquad
(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n,\ \mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
Consequently every fiber \(\mathcal R_f^{-1}(\mathbf z)\) is Borel and has Lebesgue measure exactly \(1\).

## Proof

A multi-affine real-valued function on the box is continuous: its usual repeated one-dimensional affine interpolation expresses it as a finite linear combination of its vertex values with continuous product weights.  We construct \(\mathcal R_f\) recursively in the coordinate order \(1,\ldots,n\).

Fix \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\).  Suppose that
\(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_1,\ldots,\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_{i-1}\)
have already been defined.  Put
\[
\delta_j(\mathbf q):=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_j-q_j
\quad(j<i)
\]
and, for \(s\in[u_i-r,u_i+r]\), set
\[
g_{i,\mathbf q}(s):=
f\!\left(
u_1+r\delta_1(\mathbf q),\ldots,
u_{i-1}+r\delta_{i-1}(\mathbf q),
s,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_n
\right).
\]
All displayed arguments lie in the domain box.  Since \(f\) is multi-affine,
\(g_{i,\mathbf q}\) is affine.  Denote its slope by
\[
A_i(\mathbf q):=
\frac{g_{i,\mathbf q}(u_i+r)-g_{i,\mathbf q}(u_i-r)}{2r}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_i:=
\begin{cases}
\lceil q_i\rceil,&A_i(\mathbf q)\ge0,\\
\lfloor q_i\rfloor,&A_i(\mathbf q)<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular a zero slope, even on a set of positive measure, is resolved by the fixed ceiling rule.  If \(q_i\) is an integer, its floor and ceiling agree, so (1) is unambiguous on all cell boundaries.

We first verify measurability.  At the first stage \(A_1\) is constant.  Inductively, if the first \(i-1\) coordinates of \(\mathcal R_f\) are Borel, then the preceding \(\delta_j\)'s are Borel.  Continuity of \(f\) makes the two compositions defining \(A_i\) Borel.  The set \(\{A_i\ge0\}\) is therefore Borel, and (1), together with the Borel floor and ceiling maps, makes the \(i\)-th coordinate Borel.  Thus \(\mathcal R_f\) is Borel.

For monotonicity, define
\[
\mathbf x^{(i)}(\mathbf q):=
\bigl(
u_1+r\delta_1(\mathbf q),\ldots,
u_i+r\delta_i(\mathbf q),
u_{i+1},\ldots,u_n
\bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf x^{(0)}(\mathbf q):=\mathbf u.
\]
Every \(\delta_i(\mathbf q)\) lies in \([-1,1]\), because a floor or ceiling differs from its argument by at most \(1\).  Hence every \(\mathbf x^{(i)}(\mathbf q)\) belongs to the domain box.  If \(A_i(\mathbf q)\ge0\), then (1) gives
\(\delta_i(\mathbf q)=\lceil q_i\rceil-q_i\ge0\), so
\[
f(\mathbf x^{(i)}(\mathbf q))
=g_{i,\mathbf q}(u_i+r\delta_i(\mathbf q))
\ge g_{i,\mathbf q}(u_i)
=f(\mathbf x^{(i-1)}(\mathbf q)).
\]
If \(A_i(\mathbf q)<0\), then
\(\delta_i(\mathbf q)=\lfloor q_i\rfloor-q_i\le0\), and the same inequality follows because the affine slope is negative.  Iterating gives
\[
f\!\left(\mathbf u+r(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)-\mathbf q)\right)
=f(\mathbf x^{(n)}(\mathbf q))
\ge f(\mathbf x^{(0)}(\mathbf q))
=f(\mathbf u).
\]

The construction is phase-equivariant.  We prove by induction on \(i\) that, for every \(\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n\),
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q+\mathbf k)_j
=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_j+k_j
\quad(j\le i).
\tag{2}
\]
Before the first stage there is nothing to prove.  If (2) holds through \(i-1\), then
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q+\mathbf k)_j-(q_j+k_j)
=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)_j-q_j
\quad(j<i).
\]
Thus the two slope calculations at stage \(i\) have exactly the same arguments, so
\(A_i(\mathbf q+\mathbf k)=A_i(\mathbf q)\).  Also
\(\lfloor q_i+k_i\rfloor=\lfloor q_i\rfloor+k_i\) and
\(\lceil q_i+k_i\rceil=\lceil q_i\rceil+k_i\).
The identical sign decision in (1) now proves (2) at stage \(i\).  This proves phase equivariance in all coordinates.

It remains to prove the exact pushforward identity.  Every
\(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\) has a unique representation
\[
\mathbf q=\mathbf k+\mathbf t,\qquad
\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad
\mathbf t\in[0,1)^n,
\]
including points on cell boundaries.  Put
\(\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t):=\mathcal R_f(\mathbf t)\).
The coordinatewise floor-or-ceiling property gives
\(\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t)\in\{0,1\}^n\), with
\(\epsilon_i(\mathbf t)=0\) whenever \(t_i=0\).  Phase equivariance gives
\[
\mathcal R_f(\mathbf k+\mathbf t)
=\mathbf k+\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t).
\tag{3}
\]
For a nonnegative \(\phi:\mathbb Z^n\to[0,\infty]\), Tonelli's theorem and the half-open unit-cell decomposition yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q
&=\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n}
\int_{[0,1)^n}
\phi\!\left(\mathbf k+\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t)\right)
\,d\mathbf t\\
&=\int_{[0,1)^n}
\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n}
\phi\!\left(\mathbf k+\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t)\right)
\,d\mathbf t\\
&=\int_{[0,1)^n}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\phi(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf t\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\phi(\mathbf z).
\end{aligned}
\]
The third equality holds pointwise in \(\mathbf t\), because translation by the fixed lattice vector \(\boldsymbol\epsilon(\mathbf t)\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb Z^n\).  This argument includes arbitrary tie sets and all cell boundaries; no null-set deletion is used.  Finally, each fiber is Borel because \(\mathcal R_f\) is Borel, and taking
\(\phi=\mathbf 1_{\{\mathbf z\}}\) in the pushforward identity gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_n(\mathcal R_f^{-1}(\mathbf z))=1\).
