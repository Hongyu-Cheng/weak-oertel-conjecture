## Statement

All logarithms are natural. There is one absolute constant \(c>0\) with the following property. Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For each \(i\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, with positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) on all of \(A_i\). Assume every coordinatewise parity class contains at most two atoms. For every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\), require
$$
j=(x+y)/2\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{1}
$$
For each factor there is a finite list of positive numbers \(T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,\ell_i}\), possibly empty, satisfying \(T_{i,b+1}\ge4T_{i,b}\). Every nonzero score magnitude of a distinct same-parity pair satisfies
$$
\frac12\left|\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\in[0,1]\ \cup\ \bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},3T_{i,b}/2].
\tag{2}
$$
There is no positive lower bound on the listed bases, and no bound on their number, maximum, or total width. Empty bands and equal-weight pairs are allowed.

Keep the original normalizers, laws, occupied parity counts, and product:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i,\\
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,&
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,\qquad N=\sum_i n_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Extend these laws by zero off their supports. Define the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its moments by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,\\
M_r(P)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\qquad(r=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}.
\tag{5}
$$
These assertions include singleton and lower-dimensional supports and arbitrary numbers of pairs sharing a midpoint.

Here is an explicit absolute choice. Set \(U=48\), \(a_U=[2(1+e^{2U})]^{-1}\), and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_U&=\lbrace(s,t)\in[a_U,2]^2:
\tfrac12\le s+t\le2,\ s\le e^{2U}t,\ t\le e^{2U}s\rbrace,\\
f_{(h_1,h_2)}(s,t)&=(s+t-1)^2+s(h_1s-1)^2+t(h_2t-1)^2,\\
\delta_-&=\min\lbrace\tfrac14,\min_{\mathcal K_U}f_{(1,1)}\rbrace,\\
\delta_0&=\min\lbrace\tfrac14,\min_{\mathcal K_U}f_{(1,3)},\min_{\mathcal K_U}f_{(3,1)}\rbrace,\\
C_s&=U^2(2/\delta_-+1/\delta_0),\qquad
C_c=4U^2+16C_s,\\
C_0&=4C_c,\qquad C_1=4096C_c+2U^2+32,\qquad C_{\mathrm{dis}}=48C_1^2,\\
c&=\tfrac14\exp(-C_0-C_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{dis}}}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
The proof establishes that all compact minima defining \(\delta_-\) and \(\delta_0\) are strictly positive, so this formula specifies a finite positive constant independent of all input data.

## Proof

We use the original-law and reflection conventions of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. The occupied counts and the scaled masses \(\rho_x=Kp_x\) are those of 0539ec4ffa895fed and 8a51b83b22717cf1; \(\rho_x\) equals \(t_x\) in 31c9b0b9219d18d9. Our \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\) below is the quantity \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) in the first two of these score-product facts, and equals \(K\mathcal D\) in 31c9b0b9219d18d9. The full geometric mass \(H_p\), full energy \(F=K^2b(p)\), actual depth, and original moments keep their predecessor meanings.

The actual core rows selected later need not be the same-weight rows denoted by hats in 0539ec4ffa895fed and 8a51b83b22717cf1. They contain those rows and may contain nonzero-score pairs. Their energy is denoted \(E_c\), not the predecessor same-weight energy \(E\). Likewise, the multiplier \(h_j\) and deviation \(V_c\) below refer to a selected core; they agree with the full-row multiplier and \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9 only when the core is the full row system. We prove the more general selected-core estimate first, allowing arbitrary whole-pair selections, even ones omitting zero-score pairs. The later band choice and source/target arguments use the mechanisms of 0539ec4ffa895fed and 8a51b83b22717cf1 with the actual core, and every changed identity is proved below.

Fix one factor and suppress its index. Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of unordered distinct same-parity pairs. Each occupied class has either one atom or exactly one such pair. For \(e=\lbrace x,y\rbrace\in\mathscr Q\), write \(j_e=(x+y)/2\) and \(s_e=\tfrac12|\log(p_x/p_y)|\). Normalization cancels in this score and in (1). For every original center \(j\in A\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
Y_j(x)&=\tfrac12\log(p_x/p_{2j-x}),\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
\chi(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\qquad
b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,\quad F=K^2b(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Every noncentral reflection orbit is one pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(j\). Distinct such pairs at a fixed center are disjoint because an endpoint has a unique reflected mate. The central atom occurs once. By (1), every geometric-mean summand at center \(j\) equals \(p_j\).

We first prove a bounded-score lemma for every finite nonempty lattice support with positive weights, parity occupancy at most two, and midpoint closure and product equality (1). This lemma assumes no spectral condition (2). For the lemma alone, \(U\ge0\) is arbitrary, and \(a_U,\mathcal K_U,\delta_-,\delta_0,C_s,C_c\) are defined by the corresponding formulas in (6) with that value of \(U\). Choose any \(\mathscr Q_c\subseteq\mathscr Q\) whose pairs have \(s_e\le U\), without restricting the scores of unselected pairs. The selection retains entire pairs. At every original center, retain its central atom and both orientations of every selected pair centered there; the occupied count and all class masses remain those of the original support and law. Denote this row by \(R_{c,j}\), and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{c,j}&=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_c:j_e=j\rbrace|,&
h_j&=1+2r_{c,j},&H_c(j)&=h_jp_j,\\
\chi_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j),&b_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j)^2,&E_c&=K^2b_c,\\
\kappa_{c,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_c(j)}\quad(x\in R_{c,j}),&
q_{c,j}&=\frac{p_jH_c(j)^2}{b_c},\\
v_c(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{c,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_c&=\sum_jq_{c,j}v_c(j),&
V_c&=\sum_jp_j(KH_c(j)-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Each row contains its positive-mass center, so every denominator is positive. The row law is uniform on its \(h_j\) entries, and the pair scores occur as \(s_e,-s_e\). The auxiliary laws in (8) do not change the original law or remove any center.

For each occupied class \(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\), let \(m_\eta=|\mathcal C_\eta|\in\lbrace1,2\rbrace\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_x&=Kp_x,&R_\eta&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\rho_x,&
D_{\mathrm{un}}&=\sum_\eta(R_\eta-1)^2,\\
\Psi_x&=\rho_x(h_x\rho_x-1)^2,&
f_\eta&=(R_\eta-1)^2+\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
In particular, \(\sum_x\Psi_x=KV_c\) and \(\sum_\eta f_\eta=D_{\mathrm{un}}+KV_c\). Let \(e_\eta=1\) if the class's pair belongs to \(\mathscr Q_c\), and \(e_\eta=0\) otherwise, including singleton classes. The modified integer excess is
$$
F_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}h_x-(m_\eta+2e_\eta),\qquad
\sum_\eta F_\eta=\sum_xh_x-|A|-2|\mathscr Q_c|=0.
\tag{10}
$$
The last identity holds because every retained pair contributes exactly two to its midpoint's row cardinality, even when many pairs have that midpoint.

For a singleton class, \(F_\eta=h_x-1\ge0\). For a discarded-pair class, \(F_\eta=h_x+h_y-2\ge0\). For a retained-pair class, \(F_\eta=h_x+h_y-4\). As all \(h_x\) are odd positive integers, a negative excess is exactly \(-2\), occurring precisely for \((h_x,h_y)=(1,1)\). A retained-pair class has zero excess precisely for \((1,3)\) or \((3,1)\). Every positive excess is an even integer at least two. Thus, if \(N_-\) is the number of negative-excess classes and \(N_+\) the number of all positive-excess classes, (10) gives \(2N_+\le\sum_{F_\eta>0}F_\eta=2N_-\), hence \(N_+\le N_-\).

Call a class balanced if \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). In a balanced retained-pair class its two masses \(s,t\) have ratio at most \(e^{2U}\), and each is at least \(R_\eta/(1+e^{2U})\ge a_U\). Thus \((s,t)\in\mathcal K_U\) from (6). That set is closed and bounded, and is nonempty because it contains \((1/2,1/2)\). Each polynomial in (6) is continuous and nonnegative. A zero on this set would force
\(s+t=1\), \(s=1/h_1\), and \(t=1/h_2\), since both coordinates are positive. For the three displayed multiplier vectors, the reciprocal sum is respectively \(2,4/3,4/3\), never one. None of the three polynomials vanishes on \(\mathcal K_U\); each attains a strictly positive minimum there. This proves \(\delta_-,\delta_0>0\). An unbalanced class has \((R_\eta-1)^2\ge1/4\). Consequently every negative-excess class has \(f_\eta\ge\delta_-\), and every zero-excess retained-pair class has \(f_\eta\ge\delta_0\), including the balanced interval's endpoints.

Let \(N_{0,c}\) count zero-excess retained-pair classes. There is at most one retained pair per class, every retained score is at most \(U\), and the number of positive-excess retained-pair classes is at most \(N_+\le N_-\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_c}s_e^2
&\le U^2(2N_-+N_{0,c})\\
&\le U^2\left(\frac2{\delta_-}\sum_{F_\eta<0}f_\eta
+\frac1{\delta_0}\sum_{\substack{F_\eta=0\\e_\eta=1}}f_\eta\right)\\
&\le C_s(D_{\mathrm{un}}+KV_c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
Equal-weight retained pairs cause no problem: their squared scores are zero, while the counting bound remains valid. No score of any discarded pair is bounded or charged in (11).

The exact selected-score numerator, with both orientations counted, is
$$
v_c(j)=\frac2{h_j}\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}s_e^2,
\qquad
E_c\nu_c=\frac2K\sum_jh_j\rho_j^3
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}s_e^2.
\tag{12}
$$
For a center with \(h_j\rho_j\notin[1/2,2]\), its entire numerator before division by \(K\) satisfies
$$
2h_j\rho_j^3\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}s_e^2
\le U^2h_j(h_j-1)\rho_j^3
\le U^2\rho_j(h_j\rho_j)^2
\le4U^2\Psi_j.
\tag{13}
$$
The last inequality uses \(a^2\le4(a-1)^2\) for \(a>0\) outside \([1/2,2]\). This charges a shared midpoint once. At each remaining center, \(h_j\rho_j^3=(h_j\rho_j)^3/h_j^2\le8\), so each pair there contributes at most \(16s_e^2\) before division by \(K\). Equations (11)--(13) prove the selected-core price lemma
$$
E_c\nu_c\le C_c\left(V_c+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}\right).
\tag{14}
$$
This proof also covers a selection with no noncentral pairs: all rows still contain their centers, and \(\nu_c=0\).

We now return to the theorem, set \(U=48\) as in (6), and make the actual core selection. Retain every pair of score zero or score at most one, and every pair lying in a listed band whose base is less than 32. A pair in overlapping parts of this description is retained only once. Every retained score is at most \(U\). All remaining pairs belong to exactly one high band with base \(T_b\ge32\), because the listed bands are disjoint. The high bases remain factor-four-separated. Let \(\mathscr Q_H=\mathscr Q\setminus\mathscr Q_c\), and set
\(\Lambda_H=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_H}\rho_{j_e}^2\), with the original pair multiplicities.

The bijection \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) takes full oriented row entries to ordered same-parity pairs, including the diagonal. Its inverse midpoint is in \(A\) by (1). Its contribution to \(\chi(p)\) is the product of the endpoint probabilities. Since \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), this gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(p)&=\sum_\eta\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x\right)^2
=\frac1K+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K^2},&F&\ge K^2\chi(p)^2\ge1,\\
K\chi_c&=1+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_H}{K},&
E_c&\ge(K\chi_c)^2,\\
E_c&=1+V_c+\frac{2D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}-\frac{2\Lambda_H}{K}.&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
Both energy inequalities are Jensen under the original \(p\); the final identity expands \(V_c\). Because \(|A|\le2K\) and every center is retained, Hölder also gives
$$
E_c\ge K^2\sum_jp_j^3\ge K^2/|A|^2\ge\tfrac14.
\tag{16}
$$
Combining (14)--(15) yields
$$
E_c\nu_c\le C_c\left(E_c-1+\frac{2\Lambda_H}{K}\right).
\tag{17}
$$
Indeed \(V_c+D_{\mathrm{un}}/K=E_c-1-D_{\mathrm{un}}/K+2\Lambda_H/K\). If \(\mathscr Q_H\) is empty, \(E_c=F\ge1\) and (17) gives \(\nu_c\le C_c\log E_c\), using \((E_c-1)/E_c\le\log E_c\). Such a factor will use its core with no label. This includes empty lists and lists with no actual high pair.

Assume for the next estimates that some high pair exists. Empty high bands may be deleted when indexing the following sums; deleting them preserves separation and does not change any pair. Let \(T_{\min}\ge32\) be the least remaining high base and \(\epsilon_H=8e^{-2T_{\min}}\le1/2\). Define
$$
B=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_H\\\text{source class balanced}}}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\qquad
B_b=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_H\\\text{source class balanced}\\s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]}}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\qquad B=\sum_bB_b.
\tag{18}
$$
Every source class in these sums has just one pair. Put \(\beta=B/K\), including when \(B=0\). This is the scalar denoted by \(b=B/K\) in the seed, distinct from the reflected moment \(b(p)\) in (7).

For an unbalanced high source class of mass \(R_\eta\), orient its pair so the larger endpoint has mass \(\alpha\). Its midpoint has scaled mass \(\alpha e^{-s_e}\), whence its squared mass is at most \(R_\eta^2e^{-2T_{\min}}\). Also \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\) outside \([1/2,2]\). Counting each such source class once gives
$$
\Lambda_H\le2B+\epsilon_H D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{19}
$$

Every midpoint of a balanced high source has an unbalanced own class. To prove this, take a source in band \(b\), with larger endpoint mass \(\alpha\in[1/4,2]\), score \(s\in[T_b,3T_b/2]\), and midpoint mass \(\rho_j=\alpha e^{-s}\le2e^{-T_b}<1/4\). If the midpoint's own class were balanced, its largest atom would have mass \(\alpha'\in[1/4,2]\). It would be distinct from \(j\), so these two atoms would form a same-parity pair of positive score
$$
s'=\frac{s}{2}+\frac12\log\frac{\alpha'}{\alpha}
\in[T_b/2-\tfrac12\log8,\,3T_b/4+\tfrac12\log8]
\subset(3T_b/8,T_b).
\tag{20}
$$
The final inclusions are strict: \(\log8<3\) and \(T_b\ge32\) give \(\tfrac12\log8<3/2<T_b/8\) and \(\tfrac12\log8<T_b/4\). Every earlier listed band, including every earlier band used by the core, ends at or before \(3T_b/8\). The current and later bands start at or above \(T_b\). Moreover, \([0,1]\) lies below \(3T_b/8\). Thus (20) violates (2). This proves the assertion and treats closed band endpoints as well.

Let \(J_b\) be the distinct midpoints of balanced high source pairs in band \(b\), and let \(r_{b,j}\) count such pairs at midpoint \(j\). Every class meeting \(J_b\) is unbalanced and contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\). There are at most \(4D_{\mathrm{un}}\) such distinct classes and at most \(8D_{\mathrm{un}}\) distinct atoms in \(J_b\). Therefore
$$
\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\le16e^{-T_b}D_{\mathrm{un}},
\qquad B_b=\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}\rho_j^2.
\tag{21}
$$
Only distinct targets are charged to class defect here. Source pairs sharing a target remain in \(r_{b,j}\). A target class may also be a source class in (19); the two bounds are not added as disjoint contributions to defect.

At every original center, add to the entire core all pairs in high band \(b\), including pairs from unbalanced source classes. Let \(r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) count all such added pairs. Denote the resulting row by \(R_{b,j}\), its geometric mass by \(H_b(j)\), its energy by \(F_b=K^2\sum_jp_jH_b(j)^2\), and its score price by \(\nu_b\), defined by the same construction as (8) on these rows. These selected energies do not redefine the full energy \(F\). The exact gain \(G_b=F_b-E_c\) is
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_b&=\frac1K\sum_j\rho_j^3
\left((h_j+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})^2-h_j^2\right)\\
&\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz on the distinct centers in (21), followed by (21)--(22), gives
$$
B_b^2\le\left(\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\right)
\le4KG_be^{-T_b}D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{23}
$$
This is a gain for one alternative row system; gains across different bands have not been added.

When \(B>0\), one band satisfies \(B_b\ge e^{-T_b/4}B>0\). Indeed, separation implies \(T_b\ge T_{\min}+3T_{\min}(b-1)\). With \(q=e^{-T_{\min}/4}<1/2\),
\(\sum_b e^{-T_b/4}\le q/(1-q^3)<4/7<1\), so the strict reverse inequalities for all bands cannot sum to \(B\). For a chosen band put
\(\theta=T_b/2\ge16\) and \(G=G_b>0\). There are at most \(K\) balanced high source pairs in a band, each with midpoint mass at most \(2e^{-T_b}\). The choice and (23) consequently give
$$
\beta\le e^{T_b/4}B_b/K\le4e^{-7\theta/2},
\qquad
\beta^2\le4e^{-\theta}G\,D_{\mathrm{un}}/K.
\tag{24}
$$
Strict positivity of \(G\) also follows directly from (22), since the chosen band contains a pair.

First suppose \(E_c<1\). By (15) and positivity of \(\chi_c\), one has \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_H\). Equation (19) then implies
\(B>0\), \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<4B\), \(\Lambda_H\le4B\), and \(\Lambda_H-D_{\mathrm{un}}\le2B\). For example, \(B=0\) would give \(D_{\mathrm{un}}<\epsilon_HD_{\mathrm{un}}\), which is impossible. Put \(d=-\log E_c>0\). With \(a=K\chi_c\in(0,1)\), (15)--(16) yield
$$
1-E_c\le1-a^2\le2(1-a)=2(\Lambda_H-D_{\mathrm{un}})/K\le4\beta,
\qquad d\le(1-E_c)/E_c\le16\beta.
\tag{25}
$$
Select the band just described. Equations (24)--(25) and \(D_{\mathrm{un}}/K<4\beta\) give
$$
G\ge e^\theta\beta/16,
\qquad d\le256e^{-\theta}G,
\qquad d\le64e^{-7\theta/2}.
\tag{26}
$$
The core price (17), \(E_c\ge1/4\), and \(\Lambda_H\le4B\) also give
$$
\nu_c\le32C_c\beta\le512C_ce^{-\theta}G,
\qquad \nu_c\le128C_ce^{-7\theta/2}.
\tag{27}
$$
Since \(e>2\), \(256e^{-\theta}\le256e^{-16}<1/64\). If \(0<G\le1\), then \(1-E_c\le d\le G/64\), hence \(F_b=E_c+G\ge1+G/2>1\), and \(g=\log F_b\ge G/4\). If \(G\ge1\), then \(F_b\ge5/4\) by (16), so \(g\ge\log(5/4)\ge1/5\). These logarithmic inequalities follow by integrating \(1/t\) over the indicated interval and using its minimum there. In the first case use the gain-dependent bounds in (26)--(27); in the second use the bounds with \(e^{-7\theta/2}\). Both cases give
$$
F_b>1,
\qquad d\le1024e^{-\theta}g\le g/4,
\qquad \nu_c\le2048C_ce^{-\theta}g.
\tag{28}
$$
For the second case, the intermediate coefficients are \(320e^{-7\theta/2}g\) and \(640C_ce^{-7\theta/2}g\), which are bounded by the displayed ones. The bound \(d\le g/4\) follows from \(1024e^{-16}<1/4\). The shared boundary \(G=1\) is covered by either argument.

Now suppose \(E_c\ge1\). Write \(a_+=\log E_c\ge0\), \(u=\sqrt{E_c}-1\ge0\), \(\lambda_H=\Lambda_H/K\), and \(\delta=D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\). The first-moment inequality in (15) gives \(\delta\le\lambda_H+u\). Combining it with (19), and using \(\epsilon_H\le1/2\), gives
$$
\delta\le4\beta+2u,
\qquad \lambda_H\le4\beta+2\epsilon_Hu.
\tag{29}
$$
If \(B=0\), (17) and (29) give
$$
E_c\nu_c\le C_c(E_c-1+4\epsilon_Hu)
\le2C_c(E_c-1),
\qquad \nu_c\le2C_ca_+,
\tag{30}
$$
because \(4\epsilon_Hu\le2u\le E_c-1\). Use just the core with no label in this case, even if unbalanced high pairs exist. The case \(E_c=1\) is included; (30) then forces \(\nu_c=0\).

If \(B>0\), make the weighted band choice from (24). The gain estimate there and (29) imply
\(\beta^2\le e^{-\theta}G(16\beta+8u)\). Put \(v=e^{-\theta}G\). Solving this quadratic and using nonnegativity gives
$$
\beta\le8v+\sqrt{64v^2+8vu}
\le16v+\sqrt{8vu}\le20v+u/2.
\tag{31}
$$
The last inequality is \(\sqrt{8vu}\le4v+u/2\). From (17), (29), and \(4\epsilon_Hu\le E_c-1\),
$$
\nu_c\le2C_ca_++8C_c\beta/E_c
\le4C_ca_++160C_ce^{-\theta}G/E_c.
\tag{32}
$$
Here \(u/E_c\le(E_c-1)/(2E_c)\le a_+/2\) pays the \(u/2\) term in (31). Independently, the first bound in (24) gives
$$
\nu_c\le4C_ca_++32C_ce^{-7\theta/2}.
\tag{33}
$$
Set \(g=\log(F_b/E_c)=\log(1+G/E_c)>0\). If \(G/E_c\le1\), then \(G/E_c\le2g\), so (32) applies. If \(G/E_c\ge1\), then \(g\ge\log2\ge1/2\), so (33) applies. Thus
$$
\nu_c\le4C_ca_++320C_ce^{-\theta}g.
\tag{34}
$$
This includes \(E_c=1\) and the shared boundary \(G/E_c=1\). In this branch the baseline logarithmic energy \(a_+\) is retained in full.

It remains to price the selected-band alternative when the core already contains nonzero scores. At a center \(j\), abbreviate \(r=r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) and let \(S_c(j)\) and \(S_b(j)\) be the sums of squared unordered pair scores in its core and added band. Then \(S_c(j)\le U^2r_{c,j}\), \(2r_{c,j}\le h_j\), and \(S_b(j)\le9\theta^2r\). The exact numerator on the alternative row is
\((2/K)\sum_j\rho_j^3(h_j+2r)(S_c(j)+S_b(j))\). Its extra core part satisfies
$$
\frac4K\sum_j\rho_j^3rS_c(j)
\le\frac{2U^2}{K}\sum_j\rho_j^3rh_j
\le\frac{U^2}{2}G.
\tag{35}
$$
For its high-score part use \(2r(h_j+2r)\le(h_j+2r)^2-h_j^2\), obtaining at most \(9\theta^2G\). Hence
$$
F_b\nu_b\le E_c\nu_c+(U^2/2+9\theta^2)G.
\tag{36}
$$
All midpoint multiplicities appear in these exact rowwise expansions, without an assumption that selected rows form a new midpoint-closed support.

In the deficient branch, \(G/F_b=1-E_c/F_b\le\log(F_b/E_c)=g+d\le5g/4\); in the nondeficient branch it is at most \(\log(F_b/E_c)=g\). Also \(E_c/F_b\le1\). Equations (28), (34), and (36), with \(\theta\ge16\), now give the following uniform labels. For every factor put \(a_+=(\log E_c)_+\) and \(d=(-\log E_c)_+\), where \(t_+=\max\lbrace t,0\rbrace\). For a labeled factor there are two genuine row choices:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{row choice}&\text{logarithmic scaled energy}&\text{score price bound}\\ \hline
\text{core}&a_+-d&C_0a_++C_1e^{-\theta}g\\
\text{core and selected high band}&a_++g&C_0a_++C_1\theta^2g
\end{array}
\qquad d\le C_1e^{-\theta}g.
\tag{37}
$$
Here \(g=\log F_b\) when \(E_c<1\), and \(g=\log(F_b/E_c)\) otherwise. To check the constants in (6) explicitly, \(C_c\ge4U^2\ge1\), so \(C_1\) dominates \(1024\), \(2048C_c\), and \(320C_c\). In the deficient branch (36) bounds the alternative price by
\([2048C_c+(5/8)U^2+45/4]\theta^2g\), since \(e^{-\theta}\le1\le\theta^2\). In the other branch it bounds that price by
\(C_0a_++[320C_c+U^2/2+9]\theta^2g\).
Both bracketed coefficients are at most \(C_1=4096C_c+2U^2+32\). This proves every assertion in (37). Factors without actual high pairs, or with \(E_c\ge1\) and \(B=0\), are unlabeled and use the core, with logarithmic energy \(a_+\ge0\) and price at most \(C_0a_+\), by (17) or (30). No deficient factor is unlabeled, because (19) forces \(B>0\) whenever \(E_c<1\).

Return to the full finite product. For each integer \(m\ge0\), let \(I_m\) consist of labeled factors with \(\theta_i\in[m,m+1)\), and set \(G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i\). Retain this bin exactly when
\(G_m\ge4C_1(m+1)^2\): in that event choose the core and its selected band for every factor in the bin. Otherwise choose the core for every factor in the bin. Unlabeled factors also use the core. For a retained bin define \(W_m=C_1(m+1)^2G_m\). Its excess price in (37) is at most \(W_m\), and the retention cutoff implies \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\), including equality.

For the discarded labels define their total deficit and residual core-price budget by
$$
D_{\mathrm{dis}}=\sum_{m\text{ discarded}}\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i,
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{dis}}=C_1\sum_{m\text{ discarded}}\sum_{i\in I_m}e^{-\theta_i}g_i.
\tag{38}
$$
Equation (37) gives \(D_{\mathrm{dis}}\le V_{\mathrm{dis}}\). For each discarded bin its contribution to \(V_{\mathrm{dis}}\) is at most \(C_1e^{-m}G_m<4C_1^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2\). Since \(e>2\) and
\(\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m}(m+1)^2=12\), obtained from \(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\),
$$
D_{\mathrm{dis}}\le C_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad V_{\mathrm{dis}}\le C_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad C_{\mathrm{dis}}=48C_1^2.
\tag{39}
$$
Thus discarded core variance costs one global constant through the same summable budget as the energy deficit, not one constant per factor.

Use a star for the final chosen rows and define, with the original \(p_i\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&
q_{i,j}^*&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2}{b_i^*},\\
v_i^*(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&
\nu_i^*&=\sum_jq_{i,j}^*v_i^*(j),\\
S_g&=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*),&A_{\log}&=\sum_i a_{+,i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
$$
The sum defining \(A_{\log}\) includes all labeled and unlabeled factors, and is nonnegative. In (40) each original score is restricted to the chosen row; all centers remain. From the exact logarithmic energies and price bounds in (37),
$$
S_g=A_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-D_{\mathrm{dis}},
\qquad
\sum_i\nu_i^*\le C_0A_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m+V_{\mathrm{dis}}.
\tag{41}
$$
There are finitely many nonempty bins. Subadditivity of the square root, the retained-bin inequality, and \(A_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_0A_{\log}}\ge-C_0\) imply
$$
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}
\ge-C_0-D_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{dis}}}
\ge-C_0-C_{\mathrm{dis}}-2\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{dis}}}.
\tag{42}
$$
This covers no labels, no retained bins, and an empty collection of noncentral core pairs.

We finish with the actual original-product conversion, using the reflection argument of 445a9e668ee1a705 directly on the selected rows. Every \(R_{i,j}^*\) is symmetric about \(j\) and contains its central atom. Reflection preserves its geometric law and negates its score, so \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}=0\). For every selected endpoint, direct substitution gives the exact identity and normalization
$$
p_{i,x}=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}=w_{i,x}/Z_i,
\qquad
b_i^*=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{43}
$$
Thus no original law or normalizer has been conditioned or replaced.

For a supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), form
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_i v_i^*(j_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
$$
Finite geometric sums show that \(H_P^*(\mathbf j)\) is the geometric mass of this product row. Independence and zero score means give
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\).
Multiplying (43) gives \(P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}\), where \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,X_i}/\prod_iZ_i\) is the original product probability.

Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2\mathbf j-X\rbrace\): an affine inequality strictly excluding both endpoints would strictly exclude their midpoint. It contains the central atom as well. Reflection preserves (44) and negates its score. Summing the smaller original mass on every noncentral orbit and using half the central mass, then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
$$
The oriented sum counts every noncentral orbit twice before the factor \(1/2\), and the central atom once. Boundary atoms in a closed halfspace cause no exception, and the argument requires no full-dimensionality or positive variance.

Finally let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\). The exact identities from (40) and (44) are
\(P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2=(\prod_i b_i^*)q_{\mathbf j}^*\) and
\(\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*\).
Squaring (45), summing under the original \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under this auxiliary center law yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)
\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
$$
by (6) and (42). Under that same original law, Jensen for \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\) gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the second inequality in (5).
