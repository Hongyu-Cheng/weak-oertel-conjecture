## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex set, and let \(g:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]\) be a log-concave probability density that vanishes almost everywhere outside \(K\). Let \(\rho:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty]\) be measurable and integrable and also vanish almost everywhere outside \(K\). No positive lower bound on \(g\) is assumed. For \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d\), use exactly the depth notation of accepted fact `d4a3be98ad8645b7`,
\[
\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
:=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^d\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
                  \mathbf y\in H}}
\int_H\rho(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z.
\]
Then \(\tau_\rho\) attains its maximum on \(K\). For every \(0<\alpha<1\), writing
\[
\kappa_\alpha:=
\left(3^{1-\alpha}-2^{1-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha},
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\max_{\mathbf y\in K}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
\ge
\kappa_\alpha
\left(\int_K \rho(\mathbf z)^\alpha
                 g(\mathbf z)^{1-\alpha}\,d\mathbf z\right)^{1/\alpha}.}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\rho^\alpha g^{1-\alpha}=0\) wherever \(g=0\); this convention is immaterial on the null set where an integrable \(\rho\) might be infinite.

In particular, if \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body, \(g=|T|^{-1}\mathbf1_T\), and \(\rho\) is supported on \(T\), then
\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in T}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
\ge
\kappa_\alpha
\frac{\left(\int_T\rho^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}}
     {|T|^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the two source exponents give
\[
\max_T\tau_\rho
\ge
\left(3^{1/4}-2^{1/4}\right)^{4/3}
\frac{\left(\int_T\rho^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}}{|T|^{1/3}},
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\max_T\tau_\rho
\ge
\left(3^{1/5}-2^{1/5}\right)^{5/4}
\frac{\left(\int_T\rho^{4/5}\right)^{5/4}}{|T|^{1/4}}.
\tag{4}
\]
For a general \(g\) as above, the source's log-concave-reference case is
\[
\max_K\tau_\rho
\ge
\left(3^{1/4}-2^{1/4}\right)^{4/3}
\left(\int_K\rho^{3/4}g^{1/4}\right)^{4/3}.
\tag{5}
\]

## Proof

Put \(p:=1-\alpha\in(0,1)\) and
\[
I_\alpha:=\int_K\rho^\alpha g^p.
\]
This quantity is always finite. Indeed, Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(1/\alpha\) and \(1/p\) gives
\[
I_\alpha
\le
\left(\int_K\rho\right)^\alpha
\left(\int_Kg\right)^p
=
\left(\int_K\rho\right)^\alpha<\infty.
\tag{6}
\]
This calculation neither requires \(\rho\ll g\) nor discards regions on which \(g=0\).

We first establish attainment. Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y\) contains a parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf y\). Therefore
\[
\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
=
\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}
\int_{\{\mathbf z:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y)\le0\}}
\rho(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z.
\tag{7}
\]
For fixed \(\mathbf u\), the integral in (7) is continuous in \(\mathbf y\): along a convergent sequence of points, the indicators converge away from the limiting boundary hyperplane; that hyperplane is Lebesgue-null, and dominated convergence applies with dominating function \(\rho\). The infimum of these continuous functions is upper semicontinuous. Since \(K\) is compact, there is \(\mathbf y^\star\in K\) such that
\[
\delta:=\tau_\rho(\mathbf y^\star)
=\max_{\mathbf y\in K}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y).
\tag{8}
\]
This also covers \(\rho=0\) almost everywhere, in which case \(\delta=I_\alpha=0\) and (1) is immediate. We henceforth give the cutting argument without assuming that \(\delta\), \(I_\alpha\), or \(g\) on the support of \(\rho\) is positive.

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \(A_0:=K\) and \(q_0:=\int_{A_0}g=1\). Suppose that the convex Borel set \(A_j\subseteq K\) has
\[
q_j:=\int_{A_j}g>0.
\]
The conditional density
\[
g_j:=q_j^{-1}g\mathbf1_{A_j}
\tag{9}
\]
is log-concave: the indicator of a convex set is log-concave in the extended-valued convention, and products and positive rescalings preserve log-concavity. It is a probability density. It has finite first absolute moment because it is supported in the compact set \(K\). Let
\[
\mathbf c_j:=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\mathbf z g_j(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z
\]
be its centroid. Since \(K\) is closed and convex and supports \(g_j\), one has \(\mathbf c_j\in K\).

The log-concave centroid theorem used and recorded in the accepted proof of `5bd39b316ef3c194` states exactly that every closed halfspace containing the centroid of a log-concave probability density with finite first absolute moment has mass at least \(e^{-1}\). By (7), (8), and \(\mathbf c_j\in K\), choose a proper closed halfspace \(H_j\) whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf c_j\) and such that
\[
\int_{H_j}\rho\le\delta+\varepsilon.
\tag{10}
\]
The cited centroid theorem applied to the restricted density (9) gives
\[
\int_{A_j\cap H_j}g
=q_j\int_{H_j}g_j
\ge e^{-1}q_j
>\frac13q_j.
\tag{11}
\]
Set
\[
D_j:=A_j\cap H_j,
\qquad
A_{j+1}:=A_j\setminus H_j,
\qquad
q_{j+1}:=\int_{A_{j+1}}g.
\tag{12}
\]
The new remainder is a convex Borel set, being the intersection of \(A_j\) with the open halfspace complementary to \(H_j\). Equations (11) and (12) imply
\[
0\le q_{j+1}\le(1-e^{-1})q_j<\frac23q_j,
\qquad
\int_{D_j}g=q_j-q_{j+1},
\qquad
\int_{D_j}\rho\le\delta+\varepsilon.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(q_{j+1}=0\), stop; otherwise repeat. Thus every restricted reference to which the centroid theorem is applied is explicitly normalized, log-concave, and has a finite first moment.

The sets \(D_j\) are pairwise disjoint. If the construction does not stop, (13) gives \(q_j\le(2/3)^j\). For the residual set \(A_\infty:=\bigcap_{j\ge0}A_j\), continuity from above of the finite measure \(g(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z\) gives
\[
\int_{A_\infty}g=\lim_{j\to\infty}q_j=0.
\tag{14}
\]
In a finite construction the terminal remainder likewise has zero \(g\)-mass. Hence \(g=0\) almost everywhere on the residual remainder and consequently \(\rho^\alpha g^p=0\) almost everywhere there, even if that remainder has positive Lebesgue measure and positive \(\rho\)-mass. Therefore the strips cover all of the mixed moment:
\[
I_\alpha=\sum_j\int_{D_j}\rho^\alpha g^p.
\tag{15}
\]
This is the step that handles zero-reference regions without assuming that \(g\) is bounded away from zero.

For \(0\le t\le2/3\),
\[
\frac{(1-t)^p}{1-t^p}
\le
\frac1{3^p-2^p}.
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, on \((0,1)\) the logarithmic derivative of the left side has the sign of \(t^{p-1}-1\), so the ratio is increasing, and its value at \(2/3\) is \((3^p-2^p)^{-1}\). The endpoint \(t=0\) follows by continuity. Taking \(t=q_{j+1}/q_j\) in (16), using (13), and then telescoping yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j(q_j-q_{j+1})^p
&\le
\frac1{3^p-2^p}\sum_j(q_j^p-q_{j+1}^p)\\
&=\frac1{3^p-2^p}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
For an infinite construction, this identity follows first for finite partial sums and then from \(q_j\to0\) and monotone convergence. The scalar constant in (16) is attained at \(t=2/3\).

Apply Hölder's inequality on each strip and then use (13), (15), and (17):
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_\alpha
&\le
\sum_j
\left(\int_{D_j}\rho\right)^\alpha
\left(\int_{D_j}g\right)^p\\
&\le
(\delta+\varepsilon)^\alpha
\sum_j(q_j-q_{j+1})^p\\
&\le
\frac{(\delta+\varepsilon)^\alpha}{3^p-2^p}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and recalling \(p=1-\alpha\), we obtain
\[
\delta
\ge
\left(3^{1-\alpha}-2^{1-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}
I_\alpha^{1/\alpha},
\]
which is (1), including the case \(\delta=0\).

Finally, for the uniform density \(g=|T|^{-1}\mathbf1_T\),
\[
I_\alpha=|T|^{-(1-\alpha)}\int_T\rho^\alpha,
\]
so (1) becomes (2). Substitution of \(\alpha=3/4\) and \(\alpha=4/5\) gives (3) and (4), respectively. Substitution of \(\alpha=3/4\) in (1) gives (5). All maximizing points in these consequences are supplied by the attainment argument before any halfspace is chosen.
