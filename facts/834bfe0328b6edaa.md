## Statement

For an integer $m\ge1$ and $0\le x_j\le h<1$ with some $x_j>0$, put $\rho=\prod_j(1-x_j)$, $E=\rho^{-1}-1$, and
$$
L=\left(1-\frac{\sum_j x_j}{E}\right)\prod_j(1+x_j/E),\qquad
R=\rho(E+h)\left[1-\frac{(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)}{h^{m+1}}\right].
$$

Suppose $m\ge2$ and $x_1=\cdots=x_m=x$ with $0<x<h<1$ is a local minimum of $L-R$ in the full open domain and $L-R\le0$. In particular $E=(1-x)^{-m}-1$. Put $Z=E((m+1)x-1)+mx$, $A=Z+x(E+x)$, $h_c=x+Z/E$, and $h_b=EA/[(E+1)(E-mx)]$. Then
$$
h\ge h_b>h_c>x,\qquad h_b-h_c=\frac{Z^2}{E(E+1)(E-mx)}.
$$

## Proof

On the equal-coordinate family let $U=E-mx$ and $E'=m(E+1)/(1-x)$. The function $E(x)$ is strictly convex with $E(0)=0$ and $E'(0)=m$, so $U>0$ and $Z=(1-x)(xE'-E)>0$. Write $\Phi=(E+h)(h-x)^m/h^{m+1}$ and $D=x[mh+(m+1)E]-Eh$. Since $R\ge L>0$ and $R=\rho(E+h)(1-\Phi)$ with $\Phi>0$, we have $0<\Phi<1$. At the minimum, the $h$ derivative of $R$ is zero because $L$ is independent of $h$. Using $\Phi_h/\Phi=(E+h)^{-1}+m/(h-x)-(m+1)/h$ gives
$$
1-\Phi=(E+h)\Phi_h,\qquad \frac{\Phi}{1-\Phi}=\frac{h(h-x)}D,\qquad D>0 .
$$
The two logarithmic derivatives along the equal-coordinate variation are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{L_x}{L}
&=\frac{E'-m}{U}+m\frac{E'+1}{E+x}-(m+1)\frac{E'}E
=\frac{m(m+1)x Z}{(1-x)E U(E+x)},\\
\frac{R_x}{R}
&=\frac{E'}{E+h}-\frac{E'}{E+1}
-\frac{\Phi}{1-\Phi}\left(\frac{E'}{E+h}-\frac{m}{h-x}\right)
=\frac{m(m+1)x(1-h)}{(1-x)D}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last equalities use the formulas for $E'$ and $\Phi/(1-\Phi)$ and put the fractions over a common denominator. Both are positive, and $L_x=R_x$ gives $L/R=E U(E+x)(1-h)/(DZ)$.

To split two coordinates at fixed product, set their two complementary variables to $(1-x)e^{s}$ and $(1-x)e^{-s}$. Their sum $T_0$ increases from $2(1-x)$ for nonzero small $s$ and all coordinates remain interior. At fixed $h,E$, the changing factor $E-\sum_j x_j$ has derivative one in $T_0$; the pair factor in $\prod_j(E+x_j)$ has value $(E+x)^2$ and derivative $-(E+1)$ at the equal point. Hence $L_{T_0}/L=A/[U(E+x)^2]$, since $(E+x)^2-(E+1)U=A$. The pair factor in $\Phi$ has value $(h-x)^2$ and derivative $h-1$, so
$$
\frac{R_{T_0}}R=\frac{\Phi}{1-\Phi}\frac{1-h}{(h-x)^2}
=\frac{h(1-h)}{(h-x)D}.
$$
The feasible split requires $L_{T_0}-R_{T_0}\ge0$. Using $L/R$ above and cancelling positive factors gives $E(h-x)A-h(E+x)Z\ge0$. Expanding and factoring gives
$$
E(h-x)A-h(E+x)Z=x[h(E+1)U-EA],\qquad
\frac{EA}{(E+1)U}-x-\frac ZE=\frac{Z^2}{E(E+1)U}.
$$
All factors in the final denominator are positive. Since $x>0$ and $Z>0$, these identities prove the claimed threshold.
