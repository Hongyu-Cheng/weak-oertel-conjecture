## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex
body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\]
Fix \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(x_0\in C_{z_0}\) with absolute fiber depth
\[
\delta_0:=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{z_0}\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x_0\in J\}>0.
\]
Put
\[
\beta:=\frac{\delta_0}{a(z_0)},
\qquad
q:=(1-\beta)^{1/d},
\qquad
R:=a(z_0)^{1/d},
\qquad
S_0:=2qR.
\]
For \(A,B\geq0\), define
\[
\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B):=
A^d+B^d-
\max\{s^d+t^d:0\leq s\leq A,\ 0\leq t\leq B,\ s+t\leq S_0\}
\]
and
\[
\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B):=
\min\{\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B),A^d,B^d\}.
\]

More generally, let \(\sigma\geq0\), \(M:=\max\{A,B\}\), and
\(m:=\min\{A,B\}\). The capped maximum has the exact endpoint formula
\[
\max_{\substack{0\leq s\leq A,\ 0\leq t\leq B\\s+t\leq\sigma}}
(s^d+t^d)
=
\begin{cases}
A^d+B^d,
&\sigma\geq A+B,\\[2mm]
\sigma^d,
&0\leq\sigma<A+B\ \text{and}\ \sigma\leq M,\\[2mm]
M^d+(\sigma-M)^d,
&M<\sigma<A+B.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\Phi_{d,\sigma}(A,B)
=
\begin{cases}
0,
&\sigma\geq A+B,\\[2mm]
A^d+B^d-\sigma^d,
&0\leq\sigma<A+B\ \text{and}\ \sigma\leq M,\\[2mm]
m^d-(\sigma-M)^d,
&M<\sigma<A+B.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
These formulas include \(d=1\), zero endpoint volumes, and all boundary
equalities.

Let \(\mathcal P\) be any family of pairwise disjoint symmetric pairs
\[
\{z_0+v,z_0-v\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad v\neq0.
\]
Then
\[
h_{C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}((z_0,x_0))
\geq
\delta_0+
\sum_{\{z_0+v,z_0-v\}\in\mathcal P}
\Psi_{d,S_0}\!\left(
a(z_0+v)^{1/d},a(z_0-v)^{1/d}
\right).
\tag{3}
\]

For comparison, if a pair with endpoint roots \(A,B\) satisfies the flatness
hypothesis of accepted fact `92b530e0a2281d6e`,
\[
2R-A-B\leq(1-q)R,
\tag{4}
\]
then
\[
\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B)\geq
\beta\min\{A^d,B^d\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (3) recovers the accepted flat-pair bound on every pair to which that
fact applies and can give a positive contribution when (4) fails.

The truncation in \(\Psi\) is indispensable. For every
\(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), the set
\[
C_\varepsilon:=
\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2:
-1\leq z\leq1,\
2|x|\leq1-(1-\varepsilon)z\}
\tag{6}
\]
is a compact full-dimensional convex body. For \(z_0=0\), \(x_0=0\), and the
pair \(\{-1,1\}\), it has
\[
\delta_0=\frac12,\quad
\beta=q=\frac12,\quad
R=S_0=1,\quad
A=a(1)=\varepsilon,\quad
B=a(-1)=2-\varepsilon,\quad
\Phi_{1,1}(A,B)=1.
\tag{7}
\]
Nevertheless, the vertical halfspace \(H=\{(z,x):z\geq0\}\), whose boundary
contains \((0,0)\), satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_1\!\left(
C_\varepsilon\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)\cap H
\right)
=1+\varepsilon
<\frac32
=\delta_0+\Phi_{1,1}(A,B).
\tag{8}
\]
Hence the universal estimate obtained by replacing \(\Psi\) with \(\Phi\) in
(3) is false. Reflecting (6) in \(z=0\) shows that each of the two endpoint
truncations is individually necessary.

## Proof

Since \(\delta_0>0\), one has \(a(z_0)>0\). Choose a nonzero vector
\(w\in\mathbb R^d\). The two fiber halfspaces through \(x_0\) with normals
\(w\) and \(-w\) have a common affine-hyperplane boundary of
\(d\)-dimensional volume zero, and their intersections with \(C_{z_0}\) have
volumes summing to \(a(z_0)\). Each volume is at least \(\delta_0\).
Therefore
\[
0<\beta\leq\frac12,\qquad
0<q<1,\qquad
1-q^d=\beta.
\tag{9}
\]

We first prove (1). If \(\sigma\geq A+B\), the point \((A,B)\) is feasible
and monotonicity gives the first case. Suppose \(\sigma<A+B\). At a maximizer
the budget is tight, because otherwise at least one coordinate is below its
cap and can be increased. Thus \(t=\sigma-s\), with
\[
\max\{0,\sigma-B\}\leq s\leq\min\{A,\sigma\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The function
\[
s\longmapsto s^d+(\sigma-s)^d
\]
is convex on (10), including the constant-slope case \(d=1\), so its maximum
is attained at an endpoint. Relabel \(A,B\) so that \(A=M\geq B=m\). If
\(\sigma\leq M\), concentrating the entire budget in the first coordinate is
feasible and gives \(\sigma^d\). Also
\(s^d+t^d\leq(s+t)^d=\sigma^d\), proving the second case of (1).

It remains that \(M<\sigma<M+m\). The two endpoints in (10) give the vectors
\[
(M,\sigma-M)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(\sigma-m,m).
\tag{11}
\]
Every coordinate of the second vector is at most \(M\): this is clear for
\(m\), and \(\sigma-m<M\) follows from \(\sigma<M+m\). The first vector has
largest coordinate \(M\). For two nonnegative two-vectors with the same sum,
\(u^d+v^d\) is nondecreasing in the larger coordinate when \(d>1\), as follows
from differentiating \(u^d+(\sigma-u)^d\) for \(u\geq\sigma/2\); for \(d=1\)
it is constant. Hence the first vector in (11) is a maximizing endpoint and
gives \(M^d+(\sigma-M)^d\). This proves (1), and subtracting (1) from
\(A^d+B^d\) proves (2).

We next prove the local pair estimate underlying (3). Fix
\(v\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{0\}\), put
\[
z_+:=z_0+v,\qquad z_-:=z_0-v,\qquad
A:=a(z_+)^{1/d},\qquad B:=a(z_-)^{1/d},
\tag{12}
\]
and fix a proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \((z_0,x_0)\):
\[
H=
\{(z,x):
u^\top(z-z_0)+\ell^\top(x-x_0)\geq0\},
\qquad
(u,\ell)\neq(0,0).
\tag{13}
\]

Suppose first that \(\ell=0\). Then \(u\neq0\), and the values of the defining
linear form on \(z_+\) and \(z_-\) are \(u^\top v\) and \(-u^\top v\).
According to whether this number is positive, negative, or zero, \(H\)
contains the entire \(z_+\) fiber, the entire \(z_-\) fiber, or both.
Therefore the pair contributes at least
\[
\min\{A^d,B^d\}\geq\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B).
\tag{14}
\]
The anchor fiber lies in the boundary of \(H\), so it contributes
\(a(z_0)\geq\delta_0\). This proves the required estimates for every vertical
halfspace.

Now suppose \(\ell\neq0\), and define the opposite cut
\[
H^-:=
\{(z,x):
u^\top(z-z_0)+\ell^\top(x-x_0)\leq0\},
\qquad
D:=C\cap H^-.
\tag{15}
\]
For every \(z\), write \(H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}\), and define \(H^-_z\)
similarly. Their common boundary is an affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^d\),
hence has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Set
\[
b(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(D_z),
\qquad
r(z):=b(z)^{1/d}.
\tag{16}
\]
The volume retained by \(H\) in fiber \(z\) is exactly \(a(z)-b(z)\).
In particular, the retained anchor section is a fiber halfspace through
\(x_0\), so it has volume at least \(\delta_0\), and
\[
b(z_0)\leq a(z_0)-\delta_0=q^dR^d,
\qquad
r(z_0)\leq qR.
\tag{17}
\]

There are two logically separate endpoint cases. If \(b(z_+)=0\) or
\(b(z_-)=0\), then the corresponding original endpoint fiber is retained in
full. The total retained pair volume is therefore at least
\[
\min\{A^d,B^d\}\geq\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B).
\tag{18}
\]
No concave profile is extended by zero in this support-loss case.

Suppose instead that \(b(z_+)>0\) and \(b(z_-)>0\). Convexity of \(D\) gives
\[
\frac12D_{z_+}+\frac12D_{z_-}\subseteq D_{z_0}.
\]
Brunn-Minkowski, applied only to these two positive-volume endpoint sections,
then gives
\[
r(z_+)+r(z_-)\leq2r(z_0)\leq2qR=S_0.
\tag{19}
\]
Also \(0\leq r(z_+)\leq A\) and \(0\leq r(z_-)\leq B\), because
\(D_z\subseteq C_z\). Formula (1), with \(\sigma=S_0\), bounds the removed
endpoint volume by the capped maximum in the definition of \(\Phi\).
Consequently, the retained pair volume is at least
\[
A^d+B^d-r(z_+)^d-r(z_-)^d
\geq\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B)
\geq\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B).
\tag{20}
\]
This is the positive-support case. Equations (18) and (20) exhaust the
nonvertical possibilities without asserting root concavity outside the
positive support.

If the convention for closed halfspaces includes the whole space, its anchor
contribution is \(a(z_0)\geq\delta_0\), and every pair contributes
\(A^d+B^d\geq\Psi_{d,S_0}(A,B)\). Thus it also satisfies the asserted bound.

Fix now a boundary-through-\((z_0,x_0)\) halfspace and apply the appropriate
local estimate to every member of \(\mathcal P\). The anchor is distinct from
every pair endpoint, and pairwise disjointness ensures that no endpoint fiber
is counted twice. The compact base projection of \(C\) meets
\(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points, so only finitely many summands
can be nonzero. Ignoring all other nonnegative fiber contributions yields
\[
\mathcal H_d\!\left(
C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H
\right)
\geq
\delta_0+
\sum_{\{z_0+v,z_0-v\}\in\mathcal P}
\Psi_{d,S_0}\!\left(a(z_0+v)^{1/d},a(z_0-v)^{1/d}\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` permits the defining depth infimum to be
restricted to boundary-through-point halfspaces. Taking the infimum in (21)
proves (3).

It remains to verify the comparison (5). Let
\(M=\max\{A,B\}\) and \(m=\min\{A,B\}\). Condition (4) is equivalent to
\[
M+m\geq(1+q)R.
\tag{22}
\]
Because \(q<1\), (22) implies
\[
S_0=2qR<(1+q)R\leq M+m.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(S_0\leq M\), formula (2) gives
\[
\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B)
=M^d+m^d-S_0^d
\geq m^d.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(S_0>M\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
M+qm-S_0
&\geq
M+qm-\frac{2q}{1+q}(M+m)\\
&=
\frac{1-q}{1+q}(M-qm)
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(S_0-M\leq qm\), and the last case of (2) gives
\[
\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B)
=m^d-(S_0-M)^d
\geq(1-q^d)m^d
=\beta m^d.
\tag{26}
\]
Together, (24) and (26) give
\(\Phi_{d,S_0}(A,B)\geq\beta m^d\). Since
\(A^d,B^d\geq m^d\geq\beta m^d\), their minimum with \(\Phi\) also satisfies
(5).

The extension beyond accepted fact `92b530e0a2281d6e` can be strict. For
\[
\widetilde C:=
\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2:
-1\leq z\leq1,\
2|x|+\tfrac25|z|\leq1\},
\tag{27}
\]
take \(z_0=x_0=0\). Then \(d=1\),
\[
\delta_0=q=\frac12,\qquad R=S_0=1,\qquad A=B=\frac35.
\]
The old flatness condition fails because
\[
2R-A-B=\frac45>\frac12=(1-q)R,
\]
whereas (2) gives
\[
\Psi_{1,1}\!\left(\frac35,\frac35\right)
=\Phi_{1,1}\!\left(\frac35,\frac35\right)
=\frac15>0.
\tag{28}
\]

Finally, (6) is the intersection of the slab \(-1\leq z\leq1\) with the
sublevel set of the convex function
\((z,x)\mapsto2|x|+(1-\varepsilon)z\). It is compact, convex, and has
nonempty interior. Its fiber length is
\[
a(z)=1-(1-\varepsilon)z
\qquad(-1\leq z\leq1),
\tag{29}
\]
which proves all parameter identities in (7). For \(d=1\), the capped
objective is \(s+t\). Since \(A+B=2\) and \(S_0=1\), its maximum is \(1\),
so \(\Phi_{1,1}(A,B)=1\). The only integer fibers of (6) are at
\(-1,0,1\), while \(H=\{z\geq0\}\) contains exactly the fibers at \(0\) and
\(1\). Their lengths sum to \(1+\varepsilon\), proving (8) and the claimed
failure of the untruncated formula.
