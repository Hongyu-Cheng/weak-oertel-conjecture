## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For each integer \(n\ge32\), define \(\kappa(i)=\max\{-i,0,i-1\}\) for integer \(i\), \(A_n=2-2^{-n}\), \(Z_n=2A_n\), and the rational scalar probabilities \(\pi_i=2^{-\kappa(i)}/Z_n\) for \(-n\le i\le n+1\). Put

\[
D=\{-n,-n+1,\ldots,n+1\}^{n},\qquad
p_{\mathbf I}=\prod_{t=1}^{n}\pi_{I_t}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad p_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D).
\]

The alias map to accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=Z_n^{-n}\), \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=(\log2)\sum_t\kappa(I_t)\), and \(B=n^2\log2\). Use its original families \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\), original costs \(G_E\), and original integral masses \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;p)\). These tables are globally extendable, with complete lattice support \(D\).

Use exactly \(\mathcal F,V,H,r^\star,q^\star,\mathcal J,D_{\mathbf J}\), and \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\) of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc for this \(D,p\). There is a unique real \(b_n>0\) satisfying

\[
\theta_i=\frac1{1+\exp(b_n(i-1/2))},\qquad
\sum_{i=-n}^{n+1}i\pi_i\theta_i=0.
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(b_n\) is a new scalar logistic slope, distinct from the canonical potential range \(B\) and from any halfspace normal. The entropy-selected array is exactly

\[
r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
p_{\mathbf I}\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{n}
\theta_{I_t}^{\,1-J_t}(1-\theta_{I_t})^{J_t},
&\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^{n},\ \mathbf I\in D,\\
0,&\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{0,1\}^{n},\ \mathbf I\in D.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal J=\{0,1\}^{n}\), \(q^\star_{\mathbf J}=2^{-n}\) at every positive row, \(D_{\mathbf J}=D\), and both \(\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal J\) and \(\dim\operatorname{aff}D_{\mathbf J}\) equal \(n\). The target entropy is exactly \(-\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}q^\star_{\mathbf J}\log q^\star_{\mathbf J}=n\log2\). Writing \(\nu_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/q^\star_{\mathbf J}\), the original-potential energy gap at every selected target is

\[
g_{\mathbf J}^{p}:=
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\nu_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf J}
=n\log2\,
\frac{2-(n+2)2^{-n}}{2-2^{-n}}
\in\left[\frac n3\log2,n\log2\right].
\tag{3}
\]

Now consider any nonnegative scalar multipliers \(a=(a_{\mathbf J})_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}\) satisfying the original shared-capacity constraint

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}a_{\mathbf J}r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\le p_{\mathbf I}\qquad(\mathbf I\in D).
\tag{4}
\]

This is the specified proportional source-capacity reallocation rule. Define its integral hitting mass at one row by

\[
m_{\log}^{\mathrm{prop}}(\mathbf J,L;a)
=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}a_{\mathbf J}r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing
\text{ for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

The cutoff family in (5) is always the original-potential family of \(p\), not the family obtained by recomputing costs from the reallocated row weights. For every \(n\ge32\) and every allocation (4),

\[
\sup_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J,\ L\ge0}
\frac{m_{\log}^{\mathrm{prop}}(\mathbf J,L;a)}{1+L}
\le\frac{48}{n}\,2^{-n}.
\tag{6}
\]

In contrast, for the full original capacities, every \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J\) satisfies

\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,(3n/2)\log2;p)}{1+(3n/2)\log2}
\ge\frac1{12}\,2^{-n}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus there is no absolute \(c>0\) for which the proportional rule (4)-(5) always produces a row and cutoff with score at least \(c2^{-n}\). The same failure holds when only one row is multiplied and its feasibility is merely \(a_{\mathbf J}r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\le p_{\mathbf I}\) for every source.

The combined row-energy hypotheses are retained. For the application of accepted fact b8b036fb81da12b7 at each row, the exact alias map is its weights \(w_{\mathbf I}=\nu_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), total \(W=1\), potential \(\Phi_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=-\log\nu_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), original mean \(\mathbf J_0=\mathbf J\), and intrinsic rank \(r=n\). That theorem's energy variable is the gap for \(\Phi_{\mathbf J,\cdot}\), distinct from the original-table gap \(g_{\mathbf J}^{p}\) in (3).

## Proof

For each \(0\le k\le n\), the two scalar sites \(-k\) and \(1+k\) have \(\kappa=k\). Their weights sum to \(2\cdot2^{-k}\), proving the normalization \(Z_n=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-k}=2A_n\). Reflection \(i\mapsto1-i\) preserves \(\pi\), so its mean is \(1/2\). The function \(\kappa(x)=\max\{-x,0,x-1\}\) is convex on \(\mathbb R\). Hence the function

\[
f(x)=Z_n^{-n}\exp\left(-\log2\sum_t\kappa(x_t)\right)
\quad\text{on }[-n,n+1]^n,
\qquad f(x)=0\quad\text{off that box}
\]

is finite-valued and globally log-concave. It agrees with every lattice value and every lattice zero of \(p\). The box has exactly \(D\) as its lattice intersection. This verifies the globally extendable-table hypotheses of 488f0a583886b5fc and 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc, with the stated normalization.

For a real \(b\ge0\), set \(\theta_i(b)=(1+e^{b(i-1/2)})^{-1}\). Reflection gives \(\theta_{1-i}(b)=1-\theta_i(b)\), so \(\sum_i\pi_i\theta_i(b)=1/2\) for every \(b\). If \(x_i=i-1/2\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i i\pi_i\theta_i(b)
&=\frac14+\sum_i x_i\pi_i\theta_i(b),\\
\frac{d}{db}\sum_i x_i\pi_i\theta_i(b)
&=-\sum_i x_i^2\pi_i\theta_i(b)(1-\theta_i(b))<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

At zero slope the first expression is \(1/4\). As \(b\to\infty\), it tends to \(\sum_{i\le0}i\pi_i<0\), since at least the site \(-1\) has positive probability. Thus continuity and strict monotonicity establish the unique \(b_n>0\) in (1), without a numerical approximation. The scalar laws \(\nu_0(i)=2\pi_i\theta_i\) and \(\nu_1(i)=2\pi_i(1-\theta_i)\) have total mass one and means zero and one, respectively.

The displayed array (2) has the prescribed column sums because summing its Boolean conditional factors over \(\mathbf J\) gives one. Each row is the product of the scalar laws just identified, with mass \(2^{-n}\) and barycenter \(\mathbf J\). It is therefore feasible. For every feasible array, the source and row identities imply \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}J_t=1/2\) at each coordinate. Every integer target satisfies \(\|\mathbf J\|^2-\sum_tJ_t=\sum_tJ_t(J_t-1)\ge0\), with equality precisely at Boolean targets. Hence \(V\ge n/2\), and its entire minimum face consists exactly of feasible arrays supported on Boolean targets. Array (2) attains this value.

To check entropy selection on that entire face, denote (2) temporarily by \(\widehat r\). Its log share is

\[
\log\frac{\widehat r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
=-\sum_t\log\left(1+e^{-b_n/2+b_nI_t}\right)
+\sum_tJ_t(-b_n/2+b_nI_t).
\tag{9}
\]

For an arbitrary array on the minimum face, \(\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}J_tI_t=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}J_t^2=1/2\), by the row moments and Boolean targets. The expectation of (9) is consequently fixed on this entire face: its first sum uses only column sums, and its second sum uses these identities and \(\sum qJ_t=1/2\). Therefore

\[
H(\widehat r)-H(r)
=\sum_{\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n,\mathbf I\in D}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}
{\widehat r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}\ge0.
\tag{10}
\]

The inequality follows by applying \(u\log u-u+1\ge0\) to each ratio and using equal total masses. Its equality case forces equality of all coordinates, since \(\widehat r\) is positive on the whole Boolean-target/source rectangle. Thus (2) is exactly the unique entropy selection of 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc. All asserted targets, masses, complete row supports, ranks, and target entropy follow.

For each \(k\), reflection and the identity \(\theta_{-k}+\theta_{1+k}=1\) give
\(\nu_0(-k)+\nu_0(1+k)=\nu_1(-k)+\nu_1(1+k)=2^{-k}/A_n\).
Consequently under every normalized product row law, the variables \(\kappa(I_t)\) are independent with the same distribution \(\Pr(\kappa(I_t)=k)=2^{-k}/A_n\), regardless of \(\mathbf J\). Their mean is \([2-(n+2)2^{-n}]/[2-2^{-n}]\), by summing the finite differentiated geometric series. This mean lies between \(1/3\) and one: truncating the nonnegative geometric law at \(n\) gives an expectation increasing from its value \(1/3\) at \(n=1\) and bounded by the infinite-law mean one. Since every Boolean target has \(\phi_{\mathbf J}=0\), this proves (3).

The share potentials are explicitly sums of the convex functions \(\log(1+e^{b_n(i-1/2)})\) for zero target coordinates and \(\log(1+e^{-b_n(i-1/2)})\) for one target coordinates. Thus \(\Phi_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=-\log\nu_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) is convex-admissible on the complete \(D\). Its strictly positive weights have the original integer mean \(\mathbf J\) and full intrinsic rank \(n\). This verifies every hypothesis for the stated use of b8b036fb81da12b7; in particular that theorem yields its row-potential energy bound \((4\log18/3)n\) for these exact normalized rows. The original-potential gap (3) also lies below that row-potential gap by the convex share-potential identity in 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc. No claim that any selected row mean is deep is used.

We next bound the possible proportional amplification. Equation (8) and \(\theta(1-\theta)\le1/4\) give

\[
\frac14
=\int_0^{b_n}\sum_i x_i^2\pi_i\theta_i(b)(1-\theta_i(b))\,db
\le\frac{b_n}{4}\sum_i x_i^2\pi_i.
\tag{11}
\]

Under \(\pi\), \(|x_i|=k+1/2\) and the law of \(k\) is the geometric distribution just computed, truncated at \(n\). Since \((k+1/2)^2\) increases with \(k\), its truncated expectation is at most its infinite-law expectation. The infinite law \(\Pr(k)=2^{-(k+1)}\) has \(\mathbb E k=1\) and \(\mathbb E k^2=3\), by differentiating the geometric series. Hence \(\sum_i x_i^2\pi_i\le17/4\), and (11) implies \(b_n\ge4/17>1/5\).

Fix a Boolean target \(\mathbf J\), and choose the source \(\mathbf I^{\mathbf J}\) whose coordinate is \(-n\) when \(J_t=0\) and \(n+1\) when \(J_t=1\). With \(\delta_n=e^{-b_n(n+1/2)}\), its conditional share is
\(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I^{\mathbf J}}/p_{\mathbf I^{\mathbf J}}=(1+\delta_n)^{-n}\).
For \(n\ge32\), \(n\delta_n\le ne^{-n/5}\le32e^{-32/5}<1/2\). The middle inequality follows because \(x e^{-x/5}\) decreases for \(x\ge5\). For the last, the terms through degree three of \(e^{32/5}\) already sum to more than \(64\). Since \((1+\delta)^{-1}\ge1-\delta\) and \((1-\delta)^n\ge1-n\delta\) for \(0\le\delta\le1\), this conditional share is at least \(1/2\). Constraint (4), applied to this positive original source and dropping the other nonnegative rows, forces \(a_{\mathbf J}\le2\). The same argument proves this bound under the single-row constraint stated after (7). These rare extreme sources cannot be removed from a source-preserving scalar multiplication rule.

For any \(L\ge0\), the closed horizontal cap \(F_L=\{\mathbf I\in D:\phi_{\mathbf I}\le L\}\) hits every original low-cost support at every Boolean target. Indeed, such a support has barycentric average potential at most \(L\); all its site potentials cannot be strictly greater than \(L\). Thus (5) is bounded by the weight of this explicit integral hitting set, including all equality sites:

\[
m_{\log}^{\mathrm{prop}}(\mathbf J,L;a)
\le a_{\mathbf J}2^{-n}
\Pr_{\nu_{\mathbf J}}\left(\sum_t\kappa(I_t)\le L/\log2\right).
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(Y_t=\mathbf1_{\{\kappa(I_t)\ge1\}}\). These are independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(1-1/A_n\ge1/3\). If \(L\le n\log2/12\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-\sum Y_t}\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left(\sum_t\kappa(I_t)\le L/\log2\right)
&\le\Pr\left(\sum_tY_t\le n/12\right)\\
&\le e^{n/12}\left(\frac23+\frac1{3e}\right)^n
\le\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^n
\le\frac{10}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Here \(e>2\), so the parenthesized expectation factor is at most \(5/6\); the power-series bound \(e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}\) for \(0\le x<1\) gives \(e^{1/12}\le12/11\); and Bernoulli's inequality \((11/10)^n\ge1+n/10\) gives the final bound. If instead \(L>n\log2/12\), the probability in (12) is at most one and \((1+L)^{-1}\le12/(n\log2)\le24/n\), since \(\log2\ge1/2\). Using \(a_{\mathbf J}\le2\) in these two cases proves (6), uniformly over every selected row, every cutoff, and every shared feasible allocation. This is an upper bound on an integral hitting mass using an actual hitting set; no fractional certificate is substituted.

Finally we prove (7) with original capacities counted once. Fix any Boolean \(\mathbf J\), and use the displacement set \(\mathcal V=\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^{n}\). Both \(\mathbf J+v\) and \(\mathbf J-v\) belong to the complete original support for every \(v\in\mathcal V\). The singleton at \(v=0\) has cost zero. For \(v\ne0\), the two-point support is affinely independent and has midpoint \(\mathbf J\), with original logarithmic cost

\[
G_{\{\mathbf J-v,\mathbf J+v\}}
=\frac{\log2}{2}\sum_t\bigl(\kappa(J_t-v_t)+\kappa(J_t+v_t)\bigr)
\le\frac{3n}{2}\log2.
\tag{14}
\]

At either Boolean scalar target, the two endpoint exponents have sum zero for displacement zero, sum one for displacement of absolute value one, and sum three for displacement of absolute value two. Their unnormalized endpoint minima are respectively \(1,1/2,1/4\). For positive numbers, the minimum of two products is at least the product of the coordinatewise minima. Therefore the retained oriented minimum sum obeys

\[
\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\min\{p_{\mathbf J-v},p_{\mathbf J+v}\}
\ge Z_n^{-n}\left(1+2\cdot\frac12+2\cdot\frac14\right)^n
=\left(\frac{5}{2Z_n}\right)^n
\ge\left(\frac58\right)^n.
\tag{15}
\]

A set hitting every original support at the cutoff in (14) must contain \(\mathbf J\) and hit each of these noncentral reflected pairs. Distinct pairs indexed modulo \(v\sim-v\) have disjoint source sets: equality of their endpoints forces their displacements to differ only by sign. Thus their original source weights may be summed without any repeated capacity. Its weight is at least one half of the sum in (15), with the central term in fact counted in full. Taking the minimum gives \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,(3n/2)\log2;p)\ge(5/8)^n/2\).

Bernoulli's inequality gives \((5/4)^n\ge1+n/4\). Also \(\log2<1\), and \(6(1+n/4)\ge1+3n/2\). Consequently

\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,(3n/2)\log2;p)}{1+(3n/2)\log2}
\ge2^{-n}\frac{(5/4)^n}{2(1+(3n/2)\log2)}
\ge\frac1{12}2^{-n},
\]

proving (7). All sources in this argument retain their original weights, every low-cost pair has the required positive barycentric coefficients, and both the selected target and cutoff precede the arbitrary hitting set. Letting \(n\to\infty\) in (6) proves the claimed impossibility for the specified proportional reallocation rule. Equation (7) shows why that obstruction cannot be promoted to an obstruction to the full original-capacity target.
