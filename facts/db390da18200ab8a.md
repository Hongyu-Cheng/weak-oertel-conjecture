## Statement

Use the notation \(\mathscr R_n\), \(\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\), and \(\Lambda_m(E)\) from accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f. Thus \(D_0\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) is finite, nonempty, and lattice-convex, the endpoint data satisfy the realizability conditions (R), and

\[
h_z:=u_z-\ell_z>0,\qquad
H:=\sum_{z\in D_0}h_z.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be an affine subspace spanned by integer points. For \(E\subseteq D_0\cap A\), \(m\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E)\), and \(0<\tau_z\le h_z\), put \(S:=\sum_{z\in E}\tau_z\). For \(\beta\in(0,1]\), call \((E,m,\tau)\) an \((A,\beta)\)-core tuple if

\[
\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\left(\ell_z+\frac{\tau_z}{2}\right)
\le
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\left(u_z-\frac{\tau_z}{2}\right)
\tag{2}
\]

and every proper relative closed halfspace \(J_A\subsetneq A\) containing \(m\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J_A}\tau_z\ge\beta S.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(A\) is zero-dimensional, (3) is vacuous; below we use \(\beta=1\) in that case.

1. Bounded endpoint-support rank. Put

\[
q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_0),\qquad
\alpha_0:=1,\quad \alpha_1:=\frac14,\quad \alpha_2:=\frac1{64}.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), there is an
\((\operatorname{aff}(D_0),2^{-q})\)-core tuple of total scalar mass at least \(\alpha_qH\). Consequently,

\[
q=0\Longrightarrow \Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)=H,
\qquad
q=1\Longrightarrow \Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\ge\frac14H,
\qquad
q=2\Longrightarrow \Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\ge\frac1{64}H.
\tag{5}
\]

For a geometric prismatic body represented by these data as in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f, with factor volume \(V\), total mixed mass \(W=VH\), and complete-core capacity \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)=V\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\), (5) becomes

\[
q=0\Longrightarrow \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)=W,
\qquad
q=1\Longrightarrow \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\ge\frac14W,
\qquad
q=2\Longrightarrow \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\ge\frac1{64}W.
\tag{6}
\]

2. Exact one-sided augmentation. Let \((E_0,m,\tau)\) be an \((A,\beta)\)-core tuple with total mass \(S>0\), where

\[
2^{-n}\le\beta\le1.
\tag{7}
\]

Write

\[
H_A:=\sum_{z\in D_0\cap A}h_z,\qquad
H_{\mathrm{out}}:=H-H_A.
\tag{8}
\]

If \(H_{\mathrm{out}}>0\), there is an affine functional \(\varphi\) such that

\[
\varphi=0\text{ on }A,\qquad
\varphi(z)\ne0\text{ for every }z\in D_0\setminus A.
\tag{9}
\]

Identifying \(\sigma\in\{+,-\}\) with \(+1\) or \(-1\), define

\[
D_\sigma:=\{z\in D_0:\sigma\varphi(z)>0\},\qquad
H_\sigma:=\sum_{z\in D_\sigma}h_z.
\tag{10}
\]

If \(H_{\mathrm{out}}=0\), set \(H_+=H_-=0\) and make no augmentation.

For either sign \(\sigma\), set

\[
R_\sigma:=\min\left\{H_\sigma,(2^n\beta-1)S\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]

There are \(F_\sigma\subseteq D_\sigma\) and masses \(0<\rho_z\le h_z\) for \(z\in F_\sigma\), with \(F_\sigma=\varnothing\) when \(R_\sigma=0\), such that

\[
\sum_{z\in F_\sigma}\rho_z=R_\sigma.
\tag{12}
\]

For the enlarged support \(E_\sigma:=E_0\cup F_\sigma\), every representation of \(m\) ignores every added point, exactly:

\[
\Lambda_m(E_\sigma)
=
\left\{\widetilde\lambda:
\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_0),
\widetilde\lambda|_{E_0}=\lambda,
\widetilde\lambda|_{F_\sigma}=0
\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]

Consequently the two extrema in (2) are unchanged. In every geometric realization, each representation-dependent weighted Minkowski sum and therefore the whole set \(\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\) are exactly unchanged. The enlarged tuple satisfies the ambient \(2^{-n}\) halfspace-balance condition and has total scalar mass exactly

\[
S+R_\sigma
=
\min\left\{S+H_\sigma,2^n\beta S\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]

It follows that

\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge
\min\left\{S+\max(H_+,H_-),2^n\beta S\right\}
\ge
\min\left\{S+\frac{H_{\mathrm{out}}}{2},2^n\beta S\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]

No endpoint is added or changed in this operation. Thus all convex-concave realizability inequalities (R) remain literally unchanged. By the converse realization theorem in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f, the same augmented selected tuple occurs in a full-dimensional prismatic realization with \(d=2\), \(U=[0,1]\), and \(V=1\). For any geometric realization with factor volume \(V\), multiplying all scalar masses in (11)-(15) by \(V\) gives the identical geometric statement.

If \(F\) is an exposed face of \(Q:=\operatorname{conv}(D_0)\), \(A:=\operatorname{aff}(F)\), and \(D_F:=D_0\cap F\), then one may choose \(\varphi=0\) on \(F\) and \(\varphi>0\) on \(D_0\setminus F\). Hence an \((A,\beta)\)-core tuple supported on \(D_F\) satisfies the stronger exposed-face bound

\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge
\min\left\{S+H-H_F,2^n\beta S\right\},
\qquad
H_F:=\sum_{z\in D_F}h_z.
\tag{16}
\]

In particular, if \(r:=\dim F\) and \(\beta=2^{-r}\), then

\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge
\min\left\{S+H-H_F,2^{n-r}S\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

3. Rank-at-most-two slice consequence. Let \(A\) be an affine lattice subspace such that

\[
D_A:=D_0\cap A\ne\varnothing,\qquad
A=\operatorname{aff}(D_A),\qquad
q:=\dim A\le2,\qquad
H_A:=\sum_{z\in D_A}h_z.
\tag{18}
\]

For a functional satisfying (9), define \(H_+\) and \(H_-\) by (10). Then

\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge
\min\left\{\alpha_qH_A+\max(H_+,H_-),
2^{n-q}\alpha_qH_A\right\}
\tag{19}
\]

and therefore

\[
\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)
\ge
\min\left\{\alpha_qH_A+\frac{H-H_A}{2},
2^{n-q}\alpha_qH_A\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]

If there is an exposed face \(F\) of \(Q:=\operatorname{conv}(D_0)\) such that \(A=\operatorname{aff}(F)\), then \(D_A=D_0\cap F\), and the first entry in (19) improves to

\[
\alpha_qH_A+H-H_A.
\tag{21}
\]

Equations (19)-(21) multiply by \(V\) without any change of constants in every geometric realization. In particular, with \(W_A:=VH_A\), (20) gives

\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\ge
\min\left\{\alpha_qW_A+\frac{W-W_A}{2},
2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]

In particular, uniformly over \(q\le2\),

\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\ge
\frac1{64}\min\left\{W,2^{n-q}W_A\right\}.
\]

4. Exact two-sided endpoint limitation. For every \(n\ge2\), let

\[
D_0:=\{-1,0,1\}^2\times\{0\}^{n-2}\subseteq\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{23}
\]

and, writing \(z=(x,y,0,\ldots,0)\), prescribe

\[
u_z:=1-\frac12|x-y|,
\qquad
\ell_z:=-1+\frac12|x+y|.
\tag{24}
\]

These are restrictions of a concave upper endpoint and a convex lower endpoint on
\(Q=[-1,1]^2\times\{0\}^{n-2}\), so \((D_0,\ell,u)\in\mathscr R_n\). Moreover,

\[
1\le h_z=2-\frac12\bigl(|x-y|+|x+y|\bigr)\le2.
\tag{25}
\]

At \(m=0\), the singleton tuple \(E_0=\{0\}\), \(\tau_0=2\), is a \((\{0\},1)\)-core tuple. Let \(E_c\) be the four corner points \((\pm1,\pm1,0,\ldots,0)\). For any \(0<\tau\le1\), add scalar mass \(\tau\) at every point of \(E_c\). The enlarged tuple still satisfies the ambient \(2^{-n}\) halfspace-balance inequality, but its complete core is empty.

Indeed, the two corners \((1,1,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((-1,-1,0,\ldots,0)\) have interval \([0,1]\), whereas the other two corners have interval \([-1,0]\). The representation of \(m\) by the first antipodal pair gives the averaged shrunken interval

\[
I_+(\tau)=\left[\frac\tau2,1-\frac\tau2\right],
\tag{26}
\]

while the representation by the other antipodal pair gives

\[
I_-(\tau)=\left[-1+\frac\tau2,-\frac\tau2\right].
\tag{27}
\]

They are disjoint for every \(\tau>0\). The corner-only tuple with uniform scalar mass \(\tau\) is itself base-balanced at \(m\) and also has empty complete core. Thus the strict one-sided hypothesis in part 2 cannot be replaced by arbitrary two-sided addition, even when the balance budget is respected. The same example shows that a prescribed base-balanced uniform-mass construction need not have a positive complete core at the global width-ratio endpoint \(2\). It does not assert that \(\Phi_n(D_0,\ell,u)\) is small and is not a counterexample to the prismatic capacity inequality. Accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f realizes this datum by a full-dimensional prismatic body with \(d=2\).

## Proof

We prove the four parts in order.

For part 1, the case \(q=0\) consists of one lattice point \(z_0\). Taking \(\tau_{z_0}=h_{z_0}=H\) makes the two sides of (2) equal to \((\ell_{z_0}+u_{z_0})/2\). The selected mass has relative depth one. Since no tuple can select more than \(H\), this gives \(\Phi_n=H\).

Assume \(q\in\{1,2\}\). Use the exact convex and concave extensions supplied by accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f on \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(D_0)\), and work intrinsically in

\[
A:=\operatorname{aff}(D_0).
\]

The affine lattice \(A\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is a translate of a rank-\(q\) lattice and hence is affinely isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z^q\). Define the nonnegative concave function

\[
g:=\frac{u-\ell}{2}.
\tag{28}
\]

At every \(z\in D_0\), \(g(z)=h_z/2\), so

\[
\sum_{z\in D_0}g(z)=\frac H2.
\tag{29}
\]

Apply accepted fact 62f2ec156b347dfe intrinsically with integer dimension \(q\) and exponent one. There is \(t>0\) such that, for

\[
D_t:=\{z\in D_0:g(z)\ge t\},\qquad N_t:=|D_t|,
\tag{30}
\]

one has

\[
tN_t\ge2^{-q-1}\sum_{z\in D_0}g(z)
=2^{-q-2}H.
\tag{31}
\]

The real superlevel set \(K_t:=\{y\in Q:g(y)\ge t\}\) is compact and convex. Since \(D_0=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\), one has \(D_t=K_t\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and therefore

\[
P_t:=\operatorname{conv}(D_t)
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad
P_t\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_t.
\tag{32}
\]

If \(q=1\), take \(T=P_t\). Then \(T\) is a lattice segment or a point and

\[
|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|=N_t.
\tag{33}
\]

Suppose \(q=2\). If \(P_t\) has intrinsic rank at most one, again take \(T=P_t\). Otherwise choose a maximum-area triangle \(T\subseteq P_t\) whose vertices are vertices of the lattice polygon \(P_t\). This is also maximal among all triangles in \(P_t\): with two vertices fixed, triangle area is the absolute value of an affine function of the third vertex and attains its maximum over a polygon at a vertex. If \(v_0,v_1,v_2\) are the vertices of \(T\) and \(x\in P_t\) has barycentric coordinates \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\), replacing \(v_i\) by \(x\) gives a triangle in \(P_t\) of area \(|\alpha_i|\operatorname{area}(T)\). Maximality gives \(|\alpha_i|\le1\) for every \(i\). The region

\[
\alpha_0+\alpha_1+\alpha_2=1,\qquad |\alpha_i|\le1
\]

has area four times the barycentric simplex. Hence

\[
\operatorname{area}(P_t)\le4\operatorname{area}(T).
\tag{34}
\]

Use normalized area in the intrinsic lattice. Pick's formula gives, for every two-dimensional lattice polygon \(R\),

\[
|R\cap\mathbb Z^2|
=\operatorname{area}(R)+\frac{B_R}{2}+1
\le2\operatorname{area}(R)+2.
\tag{35}
\]

Equations (34)-(35), followed by Pick's formula for \(T\), give

\[
N_t
\le8\operatorname{area}(T)+2
\le8|T\cap\mathbb Z^2|.
\tag{36}
\]

Thus in every rank-two case we have a lattice simplex \(T\subseteq P_t\), possibly of lower rank, such that

\[
|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge\frac{N_t}{8}.
\tag{37}
\]

Let \(v_0,\ldots,v_r\), \(r\le q\), be the vertices of \(T\), and set

\[
c_z:=\frac{\ell_z+u_z}{2}.
\]

Let \(s\) be the affine function on \(\operatorname{aff}(T)\) interpolating the values \(c_{v_i}\), extended affinely to \(A\). For \(z=\sum_i\alpha_iv_i\in T\), convexity of \(\ell\), concavity of \(u\), and \(g(v_i)\ge t\) give

\[
s(z)-\ell(z)
\ge\sum_i\alpha_i(c_{v_i}-\ell_{v_i})
=\sum_i\alpha_i\frac{h_{v_i}}2
\ge t,
\tag{38}
\]

and similarly

\[
u(z)-s(z)\ge t.
\tag{39}
\]

Put

\[
E:=T\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad \tau_z:=2t\quad(z\in E).
\tag{40}
\]

Equations (38)-(39) ensure \(0<\tau_z\le h_z\). Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c, in its \(d=0\) weighted lattice form, to the equal masses \(2t\) on the intrinsic rank-\(r\) affine lattice \(\operatorname{aff}(T)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). It gives an integer point \(m\) for which every proper relative closed halfspace contains at least \(2^{-r}\) of the selected mass. Strict separation shows that \(m\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\), since otherwise a proper relative closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E\) would have zero mass.

For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), affineness of \(s\) and (38)-(39) give

\[
\sum_z\lambda_z(\ell_z+t)
\le s(m)
\le\sum_z\lambda_z(u_z-t).
\tag{41}
\]

Taking the maximum on the left and the minimum on the right proves (2). A proper relative closed halfspace of \(A\) containing \(m\) intersects \(\operatorname{aff}(T)\) either in all of \(\operatorname{aff}(T)\) or in a proper relative closed halfspace containing \(m\). Hence (3) holds with \(2^{-r}\ge2^{-q}\). The same restriction argument for ambient halfspaces, together with \(q\le n\), shows that this tuple is admissible for \(\Phi_n\).

When \(q=1\), equations (31), (33), and (40) give

\[
\sum_{z\in E}\tau_z
=2tN_t
\ge\frac14H.
\tag{42}
\]

When \(q=2\), equations (31), (37), and (40) give

\[
\sum_{z\in E}\tau_z
\ge2t\frac{N_t}{8}
\ge\frac1{64}H.
\tag{43}
\]

This proves (5). Equation (6) follows from the exact scaling identity in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f.

For part 2, assume first that \(H_{\mathrm{out}}>0\). Write \(A=a_0+L\). The linear functionals vanishing on \(L\) form \(L^\perp\). For each \(z\in D_0\setminus A\), the condition

\[
v\cdot(z-a_0)=0
\]

cuts out a proper linear hyperplane in \(L^\perp\). A finite union of proper linear hyperplanes cannot cover \(L^\perp\). Choose \(v\) outside their union and set

\[
\varphi(x):=v\cdot(x-a_0).
\]

This proves (9).

Fix a sign \(\sigma\). Since the finite capacities \(h_z\) on \(D_\sigma\) sum to \(H_\sigma\), any total \(R_\sigma\in(0,H_\sigma]\) can be obtained by taking full masses successively and, if necessary, one final partial mass. Omit every zero remainder. This proves (12), including strict positivity. If \(R_\sigma=0\), take \(F_\sigma=\varnothing\).

Let \(\widetilde\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_\sigma)\). Because \(m,E_0\subseteq A\),

\[
0=\sigma\varphi(m)
=\sum_{z\in F_\sigma}\widetilde\lambda_z\,\sigma\varphi(z).
\tag{44}
\]

Every term is nonnegative and every \(\sigma\varphi(z)\) is strictly positive. Thus \(\widetilde\lambda_z=0\) for every \(z\in F_\sigma\). Conversely, every representation in \(\Lambda_m(E_0)\) extends by zeros. This proves the exact identity (13). Both representation extrema in (2) are therefore unchanged. In a geometric realization, the coefficient of every added metronoid is zero in every representation, so every weighted Minkowski sum and the full complete core are exactly the old ones.

Let \(J\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\). The intersection \(J\cap A\) is either all of \(A\) or a proper relative closed halfspace containing \(m\). The old mass in \(J\) is therefore at least \(\beta S\), using \(\beta\le1\) in the first case. The new mass can only increase this quantity. By (11),

\[
S+R_\sigma\le2^n\beta S,
\]

and hence

\[
\sum_{z\in E_\sigma\cap J}\tau'_z
\ge\beta S
\ge2^{-n}(S+R_\sigma).
\tag{45}
\]

Thus the enlarged tuple is admissible and has the exact mass in (14). Choosing the heavier sign gives the first inequality in (15), and \(H_++H_-=H_{\mathrm{out}}\) gives the second. If \(H_{\mathrm{out}}=0\), (15) reduces to the already admissible old tuple and needs no functional.

If \(F\) is an exposed face, a supporting affine functional is constant on \(F\) and strictly larger on every point of the finite set \(D_0\setminus F\), after changing sign if needed. Also \(D_0\cap\operatorname{aff}(F)=D_F\). Thus all outside width lies on one strict side, so \(H_+=H-H_F\) and \(H_-=0\). Equations (16)-(17) follow. Since only the selected masses have changed, not \(D_0,\ell,u\), realizability (R) is unchanged. The geometric assertions follow from the identity and converse realization in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f.

For part 3, the restricted datum \((D_A,\ell|_{D_A},u|_{D_A})\), in intrinsic lattice coordinates on \(A\), is lattice-convex and inherits every condition (R). The case \(q=0\) is the singleton argument from part 1; for \(q=1,2\) this is a datum in \(\mathscr R_q\). Thus part 1 supplies an \((A,2^{-q})\)-core tuple of mass

\[
S\ge\alpha_qH_A.
\tag{46}
\]

Apply part 2 with \(\beta=2^{-q}\). Both entries of the minimum in (15) are nondecreasing in \(S\), so (46) gives (19)-(20). Under the additional hypothesis \(A=\operatorname{aff}(F)\) for an exposed face \(F\), one has \(D_A=D_0\cap F\), so the exposed-face version (16) gives (21). Multiplying by \(V\) and using the exact capacity identity gives (22). For \(q=1,2\), \(\alpha_q\le1/2\), so the first entry in (22) is at least \(\alpha_qW\); for \(q=0\), it is at least \(W/2\). Since every \(\alpha_q\ge1/64\), while the second entry is at least \(2^{n-q}W_A/64\), the final uniform bound follows.

For part 4, the functions

\[
a(x,y):=1-\frac12|x-y|,\qquad
b(x,y):=1-\frac12|x+y|
\]

are nonnegative and concave on \([-1,1]^2\). Thus \(u=a\) is concave and \(\ell=-b\) is convex. The identity

\[
|x-y|+|x+y|=2\max\{|x|,|y|\}
\]

proves (25), and the restrictions satisfy (R). At the origin the interval is \([-1,1]\); selecting its full scalar mass \(2\) leaves the singleton shrunken interval \(\{0\}\), proving the original core assertion.

After the four corner masses are added, every ambient halfspace containing the origin contains the origin mass \(2\). The total selected mass is \(2+4\tau\le6\), so, for \(n\ge2\),

\[
2\ge2^{-n}(2+4\tau).
\tag{47}
\]

Thus balance is not the obstruction. The corner-only uniform tuple is also base-balanced: a closed halfspace containing the origin contains at least one point from each antipodal corner pair, hence at least \(2\tau\), which is half of its total mass \(4\tau\) and is at least the required \(2^{-n}\) fraction. The exact prismatic interval formula in accepted fact c49b4366d3d2977f gives (26) and (27) from the two antipodal representations. Since

\[
\max I_-(\tau)=-\frac\tau2
<\frac\tau2=\min I_+(\tau),
\]

the complete intersection is empty. This proves precisely the stated two-sided and factor-two limitations, without making any claim about the globally optimized value of \(\Phi_n\).
