## Statement

Let a finite compatible family of positive-area planar convex fibers at fixed sampled bases locally minimize the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth, with value $\beta>0$ and total area $M$. Compatibility is given by finitely many Jensen inequalities $g_a=\sum_z q_{az}h_z\ge0$ for the support functions. Fix finitely many nonvertical closed ambient caps whose traces cover every sampled continuous plane and whose masses are at most $\beta M$. Index the equality caps by $j$.

Write $R_z=h_z+h_z''$, $R=\sum_z R_z^{\rm diff}$ and $r_z=dR_z^{\rm diff}/dR$. At an angle $\theta$ set $J=\lbrace z:r_z>0\rbrace$, $A=\lbrace a:g_a=0\rbrace$, and $L(\theta)=\lbrace v:v_z=0\ (z\notin J),\ q_a\cdot v=0\ (a\in A)\rbrace$. The bit $I_{jz}(\theta)$ is one when the whole exposed face is strictly inside cap $j$, and zero when it is strictly outside. Assign either bit at a coincidence. There are weights $\lambda_j\ge0$ with $\sum_j\lambda_j=1/\beta$, common to all angles, such that, with $W_z=1-\sum_j\lambda_jI_{jz}$,
$$
\sum_zW_zv_z^2\ge0\quad(v\in L(\theta)),\qquad
r(\theta)\in L(\theta),\qquad
\sum_z W_z r_z(\theta)v_z=0\quad(v\in L(\theta))
$$
for $R$-almost every $\theta$. In every guarded pattern with positive source mass the same quadratic form is nonnegative on the corresponding fixed kernel. For every compactly supported Green-potential acceleration within such a guard, with only the two endpoint moments imposed, the common weights annihilate its first-order area and cap response in the combination $\delta M-\sum_j\lambda_j\delta M_j$.

If the $R_z$ have continuous strictly positive densities $\rho_z$ with respect to angle, these weights satisfy $\sum_zW_z\rho_z^2=0$ almost everywhere in angle. More generally, for any fixed caps and weights satisfying this last identity under the same smoothness, every angle $\theta_0$ at which a strict bit changes satisfies
$$
\sum_{j,z}\lambda_j\bigl(I_{jz}(\theta_0+)-I_{jz}(\theta_0-)\bigr)\rho_z(\theta_0)^2=0.
$$
A transverse crossing of any cap with positive weight has a simultaneous crossing of another cap with positive weight and opposite sign.

## Proof

Use the guarded-source and area-variation fact 6ae2eb173ed91aee, including its assertion that $r\in L$ and that the relevant exposed faces have strict signs at $R$-almost every angle. Its countable exhaustion uses positive multiples of restrictions of $R$ to the exact patterns. Each nonatomic source $\nu$ is dominated by every $R_z$ for $z\in J$ in its pattern, and is supported on a compact guard of length less than $\pi$. On the guard all inactive Jensen gaps have a positive lower bound. In every affected fiber the whole exposed face has the guard's strict cap signs with uniform margins. We may restrict each source to compact intervals strictly inside its guard and exhaust its mass, since it has no atoms.

For a compact interval inside a guard, use the scalar potential
$$
\phi(\theta)=\int_{[a,\theta]}\sin(\theta-s)\psi(s)\,d\nu(s),\qquad
\int\psi\sin\,d\nu=\int\psi\cos\,d\nu=0
$$
with the source restricted to $[a,b]$. Extend by zero, so $\phi$ is $C^1$, with $\phi''+\phi=\psi\nu$ for bounded $\psi$. For $v$ in the guard's pattern kernel, the change $v\phi$ is two-sided feasible. The changed curvature measures are dominated by a bounded multiple of the respective original curvature measures. The active rows in the pattern annihilate $v$ identically and the other rows have their guard margins. The area formula is $\frac12\int(h_z^2-h_z'^2)\,d\theta$. On a single guard the cap area change on a fiber is exactly its fixed inside bit times the area change there. These are the feasibility and localization arguments of 6ae2eb173ed91aee.

First impose also the zero first-area conditions of that fact and normalize $\int(\phi^2-\phi'^2)=-1$. The direction $v\phi$ then has first area and cap changes zero and second changes $B_0=-\sum_z v_z^2$, $B_j=-\sum_zI_{jz}v_z^2$. For any finite list of such generators from possibly different source patterns, choose different support points, one for each source, strictly inside their guards. Nonatomicity and positive source masses permit the choices even for repeated sources. Choose disjoint compact intervals about the chosen points, each with positive mass for its source, and construct the normalized potentials on those intervals. Multiplying each potential by the square root of its nonnegative coefficient realizes the desired sum of the $B$ vectors by a single direction $G$, since the area cross terms for disjoint supports vanish. Cap cross terms also vanish, by localization on these disjoint intervals.

For accelerations $E$, allow any finite linear combination of $v\phi$ from all the guards with only the two endpoint moments imposed. Let $T_0(E)$ and $T_j(E)$ be their first total and cap area changes. For one potential the fiber's first area change is $\int v_z\phi\,dR_z$, and its cap change is that quantity times the guard's bit. These first responses are linear on the whole acceleration space. To see this even for overlapping intervals, fix a fiber and a cap. The strict signs on overlapping guards in that fiber must agree. Group the compact support intervals for this fiber and cap into their connected components, allowing also the disjoint intervals of $G$ when both are used. On each component every original exposed face lies on the same strict side. For all sufficiently small changes the face endpoints shift by the support increment times the unit normal plus its derivative times the unit tangent, so the new faces still have that sign. Old and new boundary arcs outside the changed intervals coincide. In each component the difference of their oriented arcs is a closed path on its strict side, with zero winding number on the opposite side. Thus its cap area change is the area change from that component times the common bit. The area integral is additive across disjoint components. If the intervals cover the entire circle in this fiber, their connected overlap forces one common sign everywhere and the whole body is on that side. This proves the asserted linearity for the first responses and, by expanding the area quadratic form on the same components, the exact second coefficients for the path $h+tG+t^2E/2$. That path is feasible for small $|t|$ because only finitely many bounded curvature densities and positive Jensen margins are involved.

At least one cap is an equality cap, since a finite covering by strictly slack caps gives a strict upper bound on the maximum depth. For the equality caps form the cone of all finite nonnegative sums of the vectors $(B_j-\beta B_0)_j$, plus the linear subspace of vectors $(T_j(E)-\beta T_0(E))_j$. This cone is disjoint from the open strictly negative orthant. Otherwise realize the corresponding finite choices by the preceding path. Its first changes vanish, and each equality cap has second coefficient $B_j+T_j(E)-\beta(B_0+T_0(E))<0$ after subtracting $\beta$ times the total. The slack caps stay slack by continuity, the fixed traces still cover every sampled plane, and the total stays positive. The new maximum normalized depth is at most the largest new cap fraction, hence strictly below $\beta$. This contradicts local minimality. Separation from the open orthant gives a nonzero vector $\pi\ge0$ nonnegative on the cone. Normalize $\sum_j\pi_j=1$ and put $\lambda_j=\pi_j/\beta$. On each quadratic generator the separator is $\beta\sum_zW_zv_z^2$. It vanishes on the acceleration subspace, giving $T_0(E)-\sum_j\lambda_jT_j(E)=0$. In particular the same weights work for every guarded source.

Fix one source, one compact restriction, and $v$ in its kernel. On the guard consider $\mu=\sum_zW_zv_z R_z$, where only affected fibers enter the sum and their $W_z$ are constant there. Enlarge the potential interval slightly within the guard so the compact source support is in its interior. The acceleration identity says $\int\phi\,d\mu=0$ for every bounded $\psi$ satisfying the two moments. Fubini writes this as $\int\psi(s)\int_{[s,b]}\sin(\theta-s)\,d\mu(\theta)\,d\nu(s)=0$. The potential-locality fact 887add153a7b936b implies that $\mu^{\rm diff}$ vanishes on the actual source support. For a finite basis of the kernel this gives $\sum_zW_zv_zr_z=0$ almost everywhere on that support with respect to $R$, wherever the pattern holds. The countable source exhaustion gives the claimed almost-everywhere identities. Since the guards exclude all cap coincidences for affected fibers, no choice of bit at a coincidence enters the argument.

Under the smooth hypothesis $r_z=\rho_z/\sum_w\rho_w$. Test the annihilation identity with $v=r$ and clear this positive normalization to obtain $\sum_zW_z\rho_z^2=0$. A strictly positive continuous curvature radius gives the support-point parametrization $X_z=h_z u+h_z'u'$ with derivative $\rho_z u'$, where $u$ is the unit normal. Each strictly convex fiber's boundary meets a fixed cap line at at most two points, so strict bits are constant between finitely many crossing normals. The last identity extends by continuity of the densities to both one-sided limits at each crossing. Subtracting those limits gives the displayed jump relation, also for any other weights satisfying the same identity. For one cap with continuous normal $b_j$, the derivative of its margin at a crossing is $\rho_z b_j\cdot u'$. Thus the transverse signs at a given angle are identical for that cap. A support tangency gives no bit jump. With positive densities and nonnegative weights, any nonzero positive-weight transverse contribution to the jump relation requires an opposite contribution from another positive-weight cap.
