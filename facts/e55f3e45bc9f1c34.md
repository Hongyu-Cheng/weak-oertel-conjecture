## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(0<t\leq a_z\), put
\[
 \mathsf M_t(C_z):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_{C_z}x f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_{C_z}f=t
 \right\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z).
\tag{2}
\]

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard system of accepted facts
6b09aae189415c70, cb96640c07ffd5fa, and dcca4b6d66d80dd4.
For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z,
 \qquad
 M_\xi:=\max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z,
\tag{3}
\]
where \(M_\xi:=0\) when the class is empty. A positive-mass class is
good when \(M_\xi\leq A_\xi/4\). The half-strength canonical product
reservoir is
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_u a_v}{4A_\xi}
 \quad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi,\ \xi\text{ good}),
\tag{4}
\]
and is zero otherwise. Its total mass is \(Q:=\sum_e r_e>0\). Write
\(G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\). The retained hard inequalities
and classwise maximum estimate are
\[
 G<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 \sum_\xi M_\xi\leq G.
\tag{5}
\]
For every positive reservoir edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 \vartheta_e:=2^{-(d+1)}
 \left(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}\right)^d.
\tag{6}
\]

Extend the self-saturated capacity of accepted fact
cb96640c07ffd5fa by zero to every reservoir edge and denote it by
\(q^{\mathrm{sat}}=(q_e^{\mathrm{sat}})_e\). Thus
\[
 0\leq q_e^{\mathrm{sat}}\leq r_e,
 \qquad
 \Omega_b:=\sum_e q_e^{\mathrm{sat}}>\frac{97}{100}Q,
\tag{7}
\]
and its source and physical-target budgets are
\[
 \sum_{e\ni z}q_e^{\mathrm{sat}}\leq\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 \sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{q_e^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\vartheta_e}\leq1.
\tag{8}
\]
Its positive support is the label set \(I\) of accepted fact
dcca4b6d66d80dd4. Under the exact alias, label \(j\in I\) is its
underlying reservoir edge, its endpoints are \(z_j^-,z_j^+\),
\[
 m_j:=\frac{z_j^-+z_j^+}{2},
 \qquad
 L_j:=\frac12(\mathcal K_{z_j^-}+\mathcal K_{z_j^+}),
\tag{9}
\]
and its load is \(q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\).

By accepted fact dcca4b6d66d80dd4, an affine map whose source-body hit
mass is at least \(Q/100\) would give \(G\geq Q/100\), contrary to
(5). Fix any affine map and retain its two-nonhit label family \(J_N\).
Put
\[
 \Omega_N:=\sum_{j\in J_N}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}>
 \frac{93}{100}Q,
 \qquad
 \eta_m^N:=\sum_{\substack{j\in J_N\\m_j=m}}
 q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\tag{10}
\]
For each parity class define the incoming target load
\[
 \Lambda_\xi:=\sum_{m\equiv\xi}\eta_m^N.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(\mathcal G_N\) be the good classes with \(\Lambda_\xi>0\), and
let \(\mathcal B_N\) be the bad classes with \(\Lambda_\xi>0\).
For a good class, define its full canonical reservoir mass and its load
under the same global optimizer by
\[
 Q_\xi^{\mathrm{res}}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}\\u,v\equiv\xi}}r_e,
 \qquad
 \Omega_\xi^{\mathrm{sat}}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}\\u,v\equiv\xi}}
 q_e^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\tag{12}
\]

The restricted joint target packing of accepted fact
dcca4b6d66d80dd4 has, at every target \(m\), mass
\(\eta_m^N/2\) and density at most \(1/2\). Consequently
\[
 0\leq\eta_m^N\leq a_m,
 \qquad
 \sum_\xi\Lambda_\xi=\Omega_N,
\tag{13}
\]
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}\Lambda_\xi
 \leq4G<\frac Q{25},
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Lambda_\xi>
 \frac{89}{100}Q.}
\tag{14}
\]
For every \(\xi\in\mathcal G_N\),
\[
 \boxed{
 Q_\xi^{\mathrm{res}}
 =\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}
 \geq\frac{3}{32}A_\xi
 \geq\frac{3}{32}\Lambda_\xi.}
\tag{15}
\]
Hence the good target classes have the aggregate canonical and globally
loaded lower bounds
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}Q_\xi^{\mathrm{res}}
 >\frac{267}{3200}Q,}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Omega_\xi^{\mathrm{sat}}
 >\frac{171}{3200}Q.}
\tag{17}
\]
The family counted in (17) is a restriction of the actual canonical
reservoir and of the same one global load-minimal optimizer, so it retains
both inequalities in (8). Moreover, at least one
\(\xi\in\mathcal G_N\) satisfies the dimension-free relative bound
\[
 \boxed{
 \Omega_\xi^{\mathrm{sat}}>
 \frac{171}{3200}\Lambda_\xi.}
\tag{18}
\]

There is also a finite labelwise parity escape which does not use a new
optimizer. Fix \(j\in I\), put
\[
 \delta_j:=z_j^+-z_j^-\ne0,
 \qquad
 s_j:=\min_{h:\,\delta_{j,h}\ne0}
 v_2(|\delta_{j,h}|)\geq1,
\tag{19}
\]
where \(v_2(k)\) is the exponent of \(2\) dividing a nonzero integer
\(k\). For \(t=0,\ldots,s_j-1\), set
\[
 \lambda_t^-:=\frac12-\frac1{2^{t+1}},
 \qquad
 \lambda_t^+:=\frac12+\frac1{2^{t+1}},
\tag{20}
\]
\[
 z_{j,t}^-:=(1-\lambda_t^-)z_j^-+\lambda_t^-z_j^+
 =m_j-\frac{\delta_j}{2^{t+1}},
\tag{21}
\]
\[
 z_{j,t}^+:=(1-\lambda_t^+)z_j^-+\lambda_t^+z_j^+
 =m_j+\frac{\delta_j}{2^{t+1}}.
\tag{22}
\]
All these bases are integral, and
\[
 z_{j,t}^+-z_{j,t}^-=\frac{\delta_j}{2^t}.
\tag{23}
\]
Define
\[
 \mathcal K_{j,t}^\pm:=(1-\lambda_t^\pm)
 \mathcal K_{z_j^-}+\lambda_t^\pm\mathcal K_{z_j^+},
\tag{24}
\]
\[
 \theta_{j,t}^\pm:=
 \left(
 (1-\lambda_t^\pm)(a_{z_j^-}/2)^{1/d}
 +\lambda_t^\pm(a_{z_j^+}/2)^{1/d}
 \right)^d.
\tag{25}
\]
Then accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal K_{j,t}^\pm
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\theta_{j,t}^\pm}(C_{z_{j,t}^\pm})
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}
 (C_{z_{j,t}^\pm}).}
\tag{26}
\]
Here the second inclusion follows by scaling every representing density
from mass \(\theta_{j,t}^\pm\) to mass \(2q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\).
At every stage,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12(\mathcal K_{j,t}^-+\mathcal K_{j,t}^+)=L_j,
 \qquad
 \frac{z_{j,t}^-+z_{j,t}^+}{2}=m_j.}
\tag{27}
\]
At the terminal stage \(t=s_j-1\), the two bases have the same parity,
but their common parity differs from that of \(m_j\) in at least one
coordinate. Thus every individual parity-stalling label admits a finite,
pair-body-preserving, no-load-loss contraction to actual convexity-created
fibers.

Equations (13)--(18) do not show that one target parity class carries a
fixed fraction of the global load, and (19)--(27) are labelwise rather
than simultaneous. In particular, no claim is made that coincident
contracted endpoints have total occurrence load within one fiber budget,
that the contracted pairs have their required weight in the canonical
product reservoir, or that the original global circuit decomposition
restricts to them. Those are exactly the remaining simultaneous descent
interfaces.

## Proof

We first prove the global child-reservoir estimates. The restricted joint
packing in accepted fact dcca4b6d66d80dd4 is obtained by summing its
atomwise densities over \(j\in J_N\). At a fixed physical target \(m\),
the sum has mass
\[
 \frac12\sum_{\substack{j\in J_N\\m_j=m}}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
 =\frac{\eta_m^N}{2}
\tag{28}
\]
and is bounded pointwise by \(\mathbf1_{C_m}/2\). Integration gives
\(\eta_m^N\leq a_m\). Summing the definition of \(\eta_m^N\) over
distinct physical targets counts each label once, so
\[
 \sum_m\eta_m^N=\Omega_N.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (13).

If \(\mathcal B_N=\varnothing\), then
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}\Lambda_\xi=0<\frac Q{25}
\tag{30a}
\]
because \(Q>0\). Suppose instead that \(\mathcal B_N\ne\varnothing\).
For every \(\xi\in\mathcal B_N\), one has \(A_\xi<4M_\xi\).
The family is finite and nonempty, so summing these strict inequalities,
then using (11), (13), and the retained class-maximum estimate in accepted
fact 6b09aae189415c70, gives
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}\Lambda_\xi
 \leq\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}A_\xi
 <4\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}M_\xi
 \leq4G<\frac Q{25}.
\tag{30b}
\]
Thus in either case
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}\Lambda_\xi
 \leq4G<\frac Q{25}.
\tag{30}
\]
Together with \(\Omega_N>93Q/100\), this gives
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Lambda_\xi
 =\Omega_N-\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal B_N}\Lambda_\xi
 >\frac{93}{100}Q-\frac4{100}Q
 =\frac{89}{100}Q,
\tag{31}
\]
which proves (14).

For a good class, summing the exact product weights (4) gives
\[
 Q_\xi^{\mathrm{res}}
 =\frac1{4A_\xi}\sum_{u<v}a_u a_v
 =\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}.
\tag{32}
\]
Goodness implies
\[
 \sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2
 \leq M_\xi A_\xi\leq\frac{A_\xi^2}{4},
\tag{33}
\]
while (13) gives \(A_\xi\geq\Lambda_\xi\). Substitution into
(32) proves (15), and summing (15) followed by (14) proves (16).

Accepted fact cb96640c07ffd5fa gives
\[
 \sum_e(r_e-q_e^{\mathrm{sat}})
 =Q-\Omega_b<\frac3{100}Q.
\tag{34}
\]
Restrict (34) to the reservoir edges in the classes
\(\mathcal G_N\). Equations (12), (16), and (34) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Omega_\xi^{\mathrm{sat}}
 &\geq
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}Q_\xi^{\mathrm{res}}
 -(Q-\Omega_b)\\
 &>\left(\frac{267}{3200}-\frac3{100}\right)Q
 =\frac{171}{3200}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
This proves (17). Restricting a nonnegative load family preserves both
budgets in (8), so this is the actual global optimizer on actual canonical
edges, not a separately feasible comparison load.

Put \(\Lambda_G:=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Lambda_\xi\).
It is positive by (14), and
\[
 \Lambda_G\leq\Omega_N\leq\Omega_b\leq Q.
\tag{36}
\]
If every \(\xi\in\mathcal G_N\) violated (18), summing those
inequalities would give
\[
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal G_N}\Omega_\xi^{\mathrm{sat}}
 \leq\frac{171}{3200}\Lambda_G
 \leq\frac{171}{3200}Q,
\]
contradicting the strict inequality (17). Hence (18) holds for at least
one class.

We finish with the finite dyadic contraction. Since \(j\) is a positive
reservoir edge, its endpoints are distinct and have the same parity.
Thus \(\delta_j\in(2\mathbb Z)^n\setminus\{0\}\), so \(s_j\) in
(19) is a finite positive integer. For \(0\leq t\leq s_j-1\), every
coordinate of \(\delta_j/2^{t+1}\) is integral. This proves the
integrality in (21)--(22), and direct subtraction gives (23).

Apply the positive-root convexity of accepted fact
dfd25e72c1f45a4e to arbitrary points of
\(\mathcal K_{z_j^-}=\mathsf M_{a_{z_j^-}/2}(C_{z_j^-})\) and
\(\mathcal K_{z_j^+}=\mathsf M_{a_{z_j^+}/2}(C_{z_j^+})\), with
coefficient \(\lambda_t^\pm\). It gives the first inclusion in (26)
with the exact root mass (25). A convex combination of two positive
numbers is at least their minimum, so
\[
 \theta_{j,t}^\pm\geq
 \frac12\min\{a_{z_j^-},a_{z_j^+}\}.
\tag{37}
\]
The source class of \(j\) is good, and (4), (7) give
\[
 q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\leq r_j
 =\frac{a_{z_j^-}a_{z_j^+}}{4A_\xi}
 \leq\frac14\min\{a_{z_j^-},a_{z_j^+}\}.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus \(2q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\leq\theta_{j,t}^\pm\). Scaling a
representing density of mass \(\theta_{j,t}^\pm\) by
\(2q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}/\theta_{j,t}^\pm\) preserves its barycenter and
proves the second inclusion in (26).

The two coefficients in (20) average to \(1/2\). Minkowski linearity
therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac12(\mathcal K_{j,t}^-+\mathcal K_{j,t}^+)
 &=\frac12\mathcal K_{z_j^-}
 +\frac12\mathcal K_{z_j^+}=L_j.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
The base identity in (27) follows directly from (21)--(22).
At \(t=s_j-1\), equation (23) says that the endpoint difference is
\(2w\), where
\[
 w:=\frac{\delta_j}{2^{s_j}}\in\mathbb Z^n
\tag{40}
\]
has at least one odd coordinate. Hence the terminal endpoints have the
same parity, while
\[
 m_j-z_{j,s_j-1}^-=w
\tag{41}
\]
is odd in at least one coordinate. Their common parity therefore differs
from the target parity. This proves the finite parity escape and all
claims.
