## Statement

Fix an integer \(N\geq4\). The base dimension is \(n=3\), the continuous dimension is \(d=N\), and \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_N\) denotes the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^N\). For \(1\leq i\leq N\), put
\[
 z_i^-:=(2i,2i^2,0),
 \qquad
 z_i^+:=(2i,2i^2,2),
 \qquad
 m_i:=\frac{z_i^-+z_i^+}{2}=(2i,2i^2,1).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(K:=B_2^N\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be the Euclidean unit ball, put
\[
 a:=\operatorname{vol}_N(K)>0,
 \qquad
 t:=\frac a4,
\tag{2}
\]
and define
\[
 C^{(N)}:=
 \operatorname{conv}
 \bigcup_{i=1}^N
 \left(
 \{z_i^-,z_i^+\}\times(K+3\mathbf e_i)
 \right)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^N.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(C^{(N)}\) is a full-dimensional convex body.
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^3\), write
\[
 C^{(N)}_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:(z,x)\in C^{(N)}\}.
\tag{3a}
\]

For every \(i\), its three relevant fibers are exactly
\[
 \boxed{
 C^{(N)}_{z_i^-}
 =C^{(N)}_{z_i^+}
 =C^{(N)}_{m_i}
 =K+3\mathbf e_i.}
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently all three volumes equal \(a\), all three centroids equal \(3\mathbf e_i\), and
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12\left(
 C^{(N)}_{z_i^-}+C^{(N)}_{z_i^+}
 \right)=C^{(N)}_{m_i}.}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the Brunn--Minkowski lower volume on every displayed edge is exactly the midpoint-fiber volume and
\[
 \boxed{
 c_{m_i}=\frac{c_{z_i^-}+c_{z_i^+}}2=3\mathbf e_i.}
\tag{6}
\]

Put
\[
 \mathcal K^0:=\mathsf M_{a/2}(K),
 \qquad
 D^0:=\mathcal K^0.
\tag{7}
\]
The centroid of \(K\) is zero, so \(D^0\) is the centered half-mass metronoid. Translation covariance gives
\[
 \mathcal K_{z_i^-}
 =\mathcal K_{z_i^+}
 =\mathcal K_{m_i}
 =3\mathbf e_i+D^0.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, for the edge
\[
 e_i:=\{z_i^-,z_i^+\},
\tag{9}
\]
its edge body in the normalization of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` is
\[
 L_i:=\frac12(
 \mathcal K_{z_i^-}+\mathcal K_{z_i^+})
 =3\mathbf e_i+D^0
 =\mathcal K_{m_i}.
\tag{10}
\]
Hence every matching edge has exact half-metronoid Jensen equality, not merely an inclusion.

Give every edge \(e_i\) the scalar matching weight \(t_i:=t\). The resulting matching is endpoint-disjoint, off-diagonal, and same-parity, and it satisfies the stronger quarter-capacity equality
\[
 t_i=\frac14
 \operatorname{vol}_N(C^{(N)}_{z_i^-})
 =\frac14
 \operatorname{vol}_N(C^{(N)}_{z_i^+}).
\tag{11}
\]
It is therefore a legal matching for the weighted affine-frustration theorem of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`.

Define its maximum exactly affine-retainable mass by
\[
 R_{C^{(N)}}(t):=
 \max_{s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^N\ \mathrm{affine}}
 t\,\bigl|\{i:s(m_i)\in L_i\}\bigr|.
\tag{11a}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{R_{C^{(N)}}(t)=3t.}
\tag{12}
\]
Define the corresponding weighted affine defect by
\[
 \Delta_{C^{(N)}}(t):=
 \min_{s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^N\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{i=1}^N
 t\,\operatorname{dist}_{D^0}(s(m_i),L_i),
\tag{12a}
\]
where \(\operatorname{dist}_{D^0}\) is the Minkowski distance in the normalization of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`. Let \(s_*:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^N\) be any attained minimizer in (12a), and define its fixed missed-edge set by
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}(s_*):=
 \{i:s_*(m_i)\notin L_i\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}(s_*)|\geq N-3.}
\tag{14}
\]
Here a dual optimizer means a maximizer of
\[
 -\sum_{i=1}^Nh_{L_i}(v_i)
\tag{14a}
\]
under the exact constraints
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^Nv_i=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^Nv_im_i^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad
 h_{D^0}(v_i)\leq t\quad(1\leq i\leq N).
\tag{14b}
\]
For every such dual optimizer \(v=(v_i)_{i=1}^N\), not only a chosen load-minimal optimizer, the edge loads
\[
 \omega_i:=h_{D^0}(v_i)
\tag{15}
\]
satisfy the simultaneous fixed-block saturation
\[
 \boxed{
 \omega_i=t
 \qquad(i\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}(s_*)).}
\tag{16}
\]

For a dual optimizer, define the two endpoint loads on edge \(i\) by
\[
 \sigma_{z_i^-}:=h_{D^0}(v_i/2),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{z_i^+}:=h_{D^0}(v_i/2),
\tag{17}
\]
and put
\[
 Y_+(v):=\{z_i^\pm:\sigma_{z_i^\pm}>0\},
 \qquad
 T_*(v):=\sum_{y\in Y_+(v)}\sigma_y.
\tag{18}
\]
Then
\[
 \sigma_{z_i^-}=\sigma_{z_i^+}=\frac{\omega_i}{2},
 \qquad
 T_*(v)=\sum_{i=1}^N\omega_i,
\tag{19}
\]
and every dual optimizer satisfies
\[
 \boxed{T_*(v)\geq(N-3)t.}
\tag{20}
\]

For a nonempty subset \(A\subseteq Y_+(v)\), define
\[
 \rho_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A),
\tag{21}
\]
\[
 r_A^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^N\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-s(y):y\in A\},
\tag{22}
\]
and define its aligned stress-centroid score by
\[
 \mathcal S_v(A):=
 2^{3-\rho_A}
 \frac{4\gamma_N
 \sum_{y\in A}\omega_{e(y)}}{r_A^c+1},
 \qquad
 \gamma_N:=\left(\frac N{N+1}\right)^N,
\tag{23}
\]
where endpoint-disjointness makes \(e(y)\) unique. Then, for every dual optimizer and every nonempty \(A\subseteq Y_+(v)\),
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal S_v(A)\leq64\gamma_Nt.}
\tag{24}
\]

Define the stress-frame score as follows. For every affine basis
\[
 B=\{y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3\}\subseteq Y_+(v),
\]
order the basis so that
\[
 M:=\sigma_{y_0}=\max_{0\leq j\leq3}\sigma_{y_j}.
\]
For every nontrivial partition \(I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,2,3\}\), put
\[
 F_*(B;I,J):=
 \frac{
 \left(M+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}\right)
 \left(M+\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}\right)}M.
\tag{25}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak F_*(v)\) be the maximum of (25), with value zero when there is no affine basis. Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak F_*(v)\leq3t\leq6t.}
\tag{26}
\]

A gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer is a dual optimizer minimizing \(\sum_i\omega_i\) over the dual optimizer set; accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives its existence. For this theorem, the aligned centroid extraction condition `(CCE)` is the assertion that such an optimizer \(v\) and a nonempty \(A\subseteq Y_+(v)\) exist such that
\[
 \mathcal S_v(A)
 \geq
 \frac{T_*(v)-8\mathfrak F_*(v)}4.
\tag{27}
\]
For every \(N\geq308\), condition (27) fails for every dual optimizer, and hence in particular for every gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer:
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal S_v(A)
 <\frac{T_*(v)-8\mathfrak F_*(v)}4
 \quad
 \text{for every nonempty }A\subseteq Y_+(v).}
\tag{28}
\]

This is an arbitrary legal matching. No assertion is made that its weights arise from the canonical pooled endpoint weights, the canonical dyadic bins, a transverse atlas, or the canonical hard branch. Equation (28) shows that exact edgewise Brunn--Minkowski equality, exact edgewise half-metronoid Jensen equality, quarter capacity, endpoint-disjointness, and a large optimizer-independent saturated block do not by themselves imply `(CCE)`. It does not show that the full residual-selector capacity of \(C^{(N)}\) is small; other selector witnesses or other capacities may be large. It therefore proves neither failure of `(HSF)` nor failure of the weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

We first verify the base geometry. For \(q_i:=(i,i^2)\in\mathbb R^2\), the affine functional
\[
 \ell_i(x_1,x_2):=x_2-2ix_1
\tag{29}
\]
has, on the finite set \(\{q_1,\ldots,q_N\}\), the values
\[
 \ell_i(q_j)=j^2-2ij=(j-i)^2-i^2.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus it has a unique minimum at \(q_i\). Every \(q_i\) is therefore an exposed vertex of the polygon
\[
 Q_N:=\operatorname{conv}\{q_1,\ldots,q_N\}.
\]
After scaling the first two coordinates by two, the base polytope
\[
 P_N:=\operatorname{conv}\{z_i^-,z_i^+:1\leq i\leq N\}
\tag{31}
\]
is the prism over \(2Q_N\). It is three-dimensional because \(Q_N\) is two-dimensional for \(N\geq3\). The functional obtained from (29) after this scaling, and made independent of the third coordinate, exposes exactly the vertical edge
\[
 [z_i^-,z_i^+]
\tag{32}
\]
of \(P_N\). These vertical edges are pairwise endpoint-disjoint.

The set in (3) is compact and convex by construction. Its affine hull is all of \(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^N\). Indeed, differences inside any one generating fiber span \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^N\), while the base differences among the vertices of the three-dimensional prism span \(\mathbb R^3\); after subtracting their continuous components using the already available continuous direction space, the latter give \(\mathbb R^3\times\{0\}\). A compact convex set with full affine hull has nonempty interior. Thus \(C^{(N)}\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

We prove the exact fibers. Fix \(i\). Since \(z_i^-\) and \(z_i^+\) are extreme vertices of \(P_N\), any convex representation of a point of \(C^{(N)}\) with either of these base coordinates uses only generating points with that same base coordinate. Hence
\[
 C^{(N)}_{z_i^-}=C^{(N)}_{z_i^+}=K+3\mathbf e_i.
\tag{33}
\]
The midpoint \(m_i\) lies in the relative interior of the exposed edge (32). Any convex representation of a point of \(C^{(N)}\) with base coordinate \(m_i\) must use only generating points whose base coordinates lie in that exposed face: otherwise the exposing functional would take a value strictly larger than its minimum. Among the generating bases, these are exactly \(z_i^-\) and \(z_i^+\). Their third coordinates force coefficient \(1/2\) at each endpoint. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C^{(N)}_{m_i}
 &=\frac12(K+3\mathbf e_i)
 +\frac12(K+3\mathbf e_i)\\
 &=K+3\mathbf e_i,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
because \((K+K)/2=K\) for the convex set \(K\). Equations (33)--(34) prove (4). Translation preserves volume and translates the centroid; the unit ball has centroid zero. This proves (5)--(6).

We verify the half-metronoids directly. If \(p\in\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)\) is represented by a density \(f\) on \(K\), then translating both the set and density by \(3\mathbf e_i\) gives a density of the same mass and barycenter \(p+3\mathbf e_i\) on \(K+3\mathbf e_i\), and translating back gives the converse. Hence
\[
 \mathsf M_{a/2}(K+3\mathbf e_i)
 =3\mathbf e_i+\mathcal K^0.
\tag{35}
\]
The metronoid theorem of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives \(\mathcal K^0\subseteq K\). Accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` gives the origin symmetry of the centered half-mass metronoid, so \(D^0=\mathcal K^0=-D^0\). Equations (4) and (35) prove (8)--(10), including exact half-metronoid equality.

All coordinates of \(z_i^-\) and \(z_i^+\) are even, so the endpoints of every edge have the same parity and all matching endpoints belong to one parity class. The endpoints within an edge are distinct, different edges have disjoint endpoints, and (4) and (2) give the exact quarter-capacity identities (11). In particular, the degree of an endpoint in this matching is \(t=a/4\leq a/2\), so the matching satisfies the legal degree condition of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` and the hypotheses of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`.

We compute the affine-retainable mass. First note that no three of the points \(m_i\) are collinear. Indeed, their first two coordinates lie on the strictly convex parabola \((2x,2x^2)\), and the determinant of three distinct such points is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant. Thus, for any three indices, there is an affine map from the plane containing all \(m_i\) to \(\mathbb R^N\) sending those three midpoints to their three centers \(3\mathbf e_i\); extend it affinely to \(\mathbb R^3\). Since \(3\mathbf e_i\in L_i\), an affine map hits any prescribed three edge bodies. Therefore
\[
 R_{C^{(N)}}(t)\geq3t.
\tag{36}
\]

Suppose an affine map \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^N\) hits four distinct edge bodies, indexed by a set \(I\) of cardinality four. The four points \(m_i\) lie in the affine plane with third coordinate one, so they have a nonzero affine dependence. Choose coefficients \((\alpha_i)_{i\in I}\) such that
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i m_i=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i\in I}|\alpha_i|=1.
\tag{37}
\]
Since \(s(m_i)\in L_i=3\mathbf e_i+D^0\), write
\[
 s(m_i)=3\mathbf e_i+d_i,
 \qquad d_i\in D^0\subseteq K.
\tag{38}
\]
Affineness and (37) give
\[
 0=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_is(m_i)
 =3\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i\mathbf e_i
 +\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_id_i.
\tag{39}
\]
The standard basis vectors are orthonormal, every \(d_i\) has Euclidean norm at most one, and an at-most-four-coordinate vector of \(\ell_1\)-norm one has \(\ell_2\)-norm at least \(1/2\). Hence (39) implies
\[
 \frac32
 \leq3\left(\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_i^2\right)^{1/2}
 =\left\|\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_id_i\right\|_2
 \leq\sum_{i\in I}|\alpha_i|\|d_i\|_2
 \leq1,
\tag{40}
\]
a contradiction. Thus every affine map hits at most three edge bodies. Since all edge weights equal \(t\), this and (36) prove (12).

Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` proves attainment of the primal minimum, so an optimizer \(s_*\) in (13) exists. The preceding hit bound applies to every affine map, including \(s_*\), and proves (14).

We now prove simultaneous saturation for every dual optimizer rather than assuming it. Let \(v=(v_i)\) be an arbitrary dual optimizer in the attained dual formula of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`. Put
\[
 d_i^*:=\operatorname{dist}_{D^0}(s_*(m_i),L_i).
\tag{41}
\]
For each \(i\), the dual norm inequality and feasibility give
\[
 \langle v_i,s_*(m_i)\rangle-h_{L_i}(v_i)
 \leq h_{D^0}(v_i)d_i^*
 =\omega_i d_i^*
 \leq t d_i^*.
\tag{42}
\]
The affine moment cancellations for the dual field give
\[
 \sum_i\langle v_i,s_*(m_i)\rangle=0.
\tag{43}
\]
Therefore the sum of the left sides in (42) is the dual optimum. The sum of the right sides is the value of the attained primal optimizer. Primal-dual equality in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` says both sums equal the common optimum. Every gap in (42) is nonnegative, so equality holds edge by edge. In particular,
\[
 (t-\omega_i)d_i^*=0.
\tag{44}
\]
If \(i\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}(s_*)\), then \(d_i^*>0\), and (44) gives \(\omega_i=t\). The dual optimizer was arbitrary, so (16) holds simultaneously over the entire dual optimizer set.

Positive homogeneity of the support function gives
\[
 \sigma_{z_i^-}=\sigma_{z_i^+}
 =h_{D^0}(v_i/2)
 =\frac12h_{D^0}(v_i)
 =\frac{\omega_i}{2}.
\tag{45}
\]
Summing (45) edge by edge proves (19). Equations (14) and (16) then give (20) for every dual optimizer.

We prove the aligned-score bound. Fix a nonempty \(A\subseteq Y_+(v)\), and let \(p\) be the number of distinct edge labels represented in \(A\). The continuous coordinates of the corresponding centroid graph points include the \(p\) distinct points
\[
 3\mathbf e_i,
\]
whose affine hull has dimension \(p-1\). Hence
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{(y,c_y):y\in A\}\geq p-1.
\tag{46}
\]
For completeness, fix any affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^N\), and let \(V_s\) be the span of its residual vectors \(c_y-s(y)\). The affine bijection
\[
 (y,x)\longmapsto(y,x-s(y))
\tag{47}
\]
preserves the affine dimension of the graph in (46) and sends it into \(\operatorname{aff}(A)\times V_s\). Therefore
\[
 p-1
 \leq\rho_A+\dim V_s.
\tag{48}
\]
Minimizing over \(s\), and also using \(r_A^c+1\geq1\), yields
\[
 r_A^c+1\geq\max\{1,p-\rho_A\}.
\tag{49}
\]
Every represented edge contributes at most two endpoints to \(A\), and dual feasibility gives \(\omega_i\leq t\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{y\in A}\omega_{e(y)}\leq2pt.
\tag{50}
\]
Since \(0\leq\rho_A\leq3\), equations (23), (49), and (50) give
\[
 \mathcal S_v(A)
 \leq
 2^{3-\rho_A}
 \frac{8\gamma_Npt}{\max\{1,p-\rho_A\}}.
\tag{51}
\]
If \(p\leq\rho_A\), then \(p\leq3\) and the right side of (51) is at most \(32\gamma_Nt\). If \(p>\rho_A\), then
\[
 \frac{p}{p-\rho_A}\leq\rho_A+1,
\]
and
\[
 2^{3-\rho_A}(\rho_A+1)\leq8
 \qquad(\rho_A=0,1,2,3).
\]
The right side of (51) is then at most \(64\gamma_Nt\). This proves (24) in all cases.

We prove the frame bound. Equation (45) and dual feasibility give
\[
 0<\sigma_y\leq\frac t2
 \qquad(y\in Y_+(v)).
\tag{52}
\]
For a basis and partition in (25), one of \(I,J\) has one member and the other has two. Since every displayed endpoint load is at most the maximum \(M\),
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}\leq |I|M,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}\leq |J|M.
\]
Consequently
\[
 F_*(B;I,J)
 \leq(1+|I|)(1+|J|)M
 =6M
 \leq3t.
\tag{53}
\]
Taking the maximum, including the empty-basis convention, proves (26).

Finally assume \(N\geq308\). For every dual optimizer, equations (20) and the weaker consequence \(\mathfrak F_*(v)\leq6t\) of (26) give
\[
 \frac{T_*(v)-8\mathfrak F_*(v)}4
 \geq\frac{N-51}{4}t
 \geq\frac{257}{4}t
 >64t.
\tag{54}
\]
Since \(0<\gamma_N<1\), equation (24) gives
\[
 \mathcal S_v(A)\leq64\gamma_Nt<64t.
\tag{55}
\]
Equations (54)--(55) prove the strict failure (28) for every dual optimizer and every nonempty positive endpoint subset. Accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` supplies at least one gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer, so the stated failure for every such optimizer is nonvacuous. All asserted conclusions are proved.
