## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be planar convex bodies at the three integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and total area $M$. Let $v_1,v_2,v_3$ span the plane with $\sum_i v_i=0$. Write $h_i=h_B(v_i)$ and choose $y_0\in\operatorname{int}K$. Suppose the caps
$$
v_i\cdot x\ge v_i\cdot y_0+z(h_i-v_i\cdot y_0)
$$
all have raw mass $Q$, the global maximum of raw mixed depth. Each cut on $A$ is proper or its threshold is strictly outside the support interval. Let $k_i$ and $\sigma_i$ be the section lengths divided by $\|v_i\|$ on $K$ and $A$ respectively, taking $\sigma_i=0$ in the strict saturation cases. Put $s_i=k_i+2\sigma_i$, $\pi_i=s_i^{-1}/\sum_j s_j^{-1}$, and $W_K(x)=\sum_i\pi_i\mathbf 1_{\{v_i\cdot x\ge v_i\cdot y_0\}}$.

For a whole nondegenerate supporting segment $F$ of $K$ with outward unit normal $n$, take $p\in\operatorname{relint}F$. Enlarge just $K$ to $K_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(K\cup\{p+\varepsilon n\})$. Write $T_p$ for the piecewise linear tent on $F$ with height one at $p$ and zero at the endpoints. The resulting hull has exactly the three prescribed integer fibers. For every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the same three caps with $y_0$ replaced by one $y_\varepsilon=y_0+O(\varepsilon)$ have common raw mass
$$
q_\varepsilon=Q+\varepsilon\int_F T_p W_K\,ds+o(\varepsilon)>Q .
$$
This bounds the new global raw depth from above. The difference between its normalized upper bound and the old normalized maximum is
$$
\frac{\varepsilon}{M}\int_F T_p(W_K-Q/M)\,ds+o(\varepsilon).
$$
A strictly negative integral yields strict descent. At a local minimum under these middle-fiber variations the integral is nonnegative for every such segment and peak.

## Proof

Containment of the old middle body preserves $(A+B)/2\subseteq K_\varepsilon$. The three-fiber hull lemma 2a8c2bc6d7992c5e, after translating the base index, gives the exact integer fibers. The planar bulge theorem 7514d7974e1bc9b7 gives $|K_\varepsilon|-|K|=\varepsilon|F|/2+o(\varepsilon)$. For the $i$th middle cap it gives added area $\varepsilon I_i+o(\varepsilon)$, where $I_i=\int_F T_p\mathbf 1_{\{v_i\cdot x\ge v_i\cdot y_0\}}\,ds$. The error is uniform for parallel threshold changes of size $O(\varepsilon)$ because these middle cuts are proper.

Use $\alpha_i=v_i\cdot y$ as the threshold on the middle fiber. The threshold is $2\alpha_i-h_i$ on $A$, and the threshold $h_i$ on $B$ always gives zero area. The sum of these three areas is continuous and locally strictly decreasing in $\alpha_i$, since a neighborhood of the old proper middle cut has sections of positive length in $K\subseteq K_\varepsilon$. Write $\alpha_i^0=v_i\cdot y_0$. At $\alpha_i=\alpha_i^0+h$ with $|h|=O(\varepsilon)$, the original cap mass is $Q-s_i h+o(\varepsilon)$ by coarea and continuity of the section length at a proper interior level. A strictly saturated $A$ trace stays constant. Adding the bulge area therefore gives $Q-s_i h+\varepsilon I_i+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly for bounded $h/\varepsilon$.

For $q=Q+\varepsilon u$ with $u$ in a fixed bounded interval, invert each local cap-mass function. Bracketing with $h=\pm C\varepsilon$ for sufficiently large fixed $C$ shows the inverse is defined there, and the expansion gives
$$
\alpha_i(q)=\alpha_i^0+\varepsilon(I_i-u)/s_i+o(\varepsilon).
$$
The sum of the three inverses is continuous and strictly decreasing in $q$. Since $\sum_i\alpha_i^0=0$, taking $u$ on opposite sides of $\sum_i\pi_i I_i$ gives opposite signs. Its unique local zero yields a common mass with $u=\sum_i\pi_i I_i+o(1)$. The three thresholds at that zero sum to zero, so they equal $(v_i\cdot y_\varepsilon)_i$ for one $y_\varepsilon=y_0+O(\varepsilon)$ by the rank and dependence of the $v_i$.

At every point of the middle plane, one of the three margins is nonnegative since they sum to zero. This proves the new depth bound for the whole middle fiber, including its added points. At either endpoint base, raw mixed depth equals the ordinary depth in its own fiber. For the lower direction, every ambient containing cap pays at least the own-fiber depth. For the upper direction, any own-fiber cut is realized by an ambient cap whose sufficiently steep base tilt misses the other two compact fibers. The endpoint depths are thus unchanged and at most $Q$. The original middle cap traces cover their plane, so $W_K\ge\min_i\pi_i$ there and $\int_F T_pW_K\,ds>0$. Hence $q_\varepsilon>Q$ for small $\varepsilon$, giving the bound at every integer fiber. Divide by $M+\varepsilon |F|/2+o(\varepsilon)$ and use $\int_F T_p\,ds=|F|/2$ to obtain the normalized formula and its consequences.
