## Statement

Let $U=\operatorname{conv}(U)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be nonempty and finite, and let $L(U)$ be the space of scalar functions preserving every anchored convex representation on $U$. There are integer-valued $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ whose span together with $1$ and the original coordinate functions is $L(U)$. Put $\Psi(u)=(u,f_1(u),\ldots,f_s(u))$, choose $u_0\in U$, and let $E_0=\operatorname{aff}(\Psi(U))-\Psi(u_0)$ and $\Lambda_0=E_0\cap\mathbb Z^{n+s}$. The integer graph is affinely equivalent to the canonical lift, $\Lambda_0$ is a full-rank lattice of $E_0$, and $\operatorname{conv}(\Psi(U))\cap(\Psi(u_0)+\Lambda_0)=\Psi(U)$. For every $u,v\in U$ and integer $m\ge1$,
$$
\Psi(u)-\Psi(v)\in m\Lambda_0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u-v\in m\mathbb Z^n.
$$
In particular, the number of occupied parity classes in the inherited affine lattice equals that of $U$, even if the lifted rank is larger. A nonempty closed minor of a complete base is complete, so this applies to successive closed minors as well.

## Proof

At each $u$ the anchored coefficient vectors form a bounded polytope defined by rational equalities and nonnegativity constraints. Its vertices are rational, since they are solutions of full-rank rational subsystems of the active constraints. Every coefficient vector is a convex combination of those vertices. Thus it suffices to impose the finite rational row equations for the vertices, so the kernel $L(U)$ has a rational basis. Add rational functions to the original coordinate functions and $1$ to span the kernel, and clear each added function's denominators. Both the graph coordinates and the canonical lift coordinates span $L(U)$ together with $1$. Each placement therefore has an affine map to the other. The compositions are the identity on the spanning points, hence on their affine hulls.

An ambient integer point of $\operatorname{conv}(\Psi(U))$ projects to an integer $u$ in $\operatorname{conv}(U)$, hence to a site of $U$. The convex combination defining the point now gives an anchored representation of $u$, which forces its added coordinates to equal $f_i(u)$ for every $i$. The graph is thus the full ambient integer trace of its hull. Its direction space $E_0$ is spanned by integer differences. The discrete subgroup $\Lambda_0$ contains a spanning set for that space and is therefore a full-rank lattice there. Intersecting in the affine hull uses precisely $\Psi(u_0)+\Lambda_0$.

Projection proves the forward congruence implication. If $m\ge2$ divides $u-v$ in $\mathbb Z^n$, the lattice point $w=v+(u-v)/m$ lies in $U$ by completeness. The anchored representation at $w$ yields $f_i(u)-f_i(v)=m(f_i(w)-f_i(v))$. The entire quotient graph difference is integral and belongs to $E_0$, so it is in $\Lambda_0$. For $m=1$ the equivalence follows from the definitions. For a closed minor $T$ of $U$, any integer point of $\operatorname{conv}(T)$ first belongs to $U$ by completeness and then to $T$ by closedness. The same argument applies in coordinates of any lattice basis of an inherited affine lattice.
