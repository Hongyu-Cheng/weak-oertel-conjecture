## Statement

Let $T$ be a regular $4$-simplex of volume $V$ with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_4$, $J=\lbrace0,1\rbrace$, $D_\delta=T\cap\lbrace\lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_4\le\delta\rbrace$, and $C_\delta$ the convex hull of $T$ at base zero and $D_\delta$ at each of bases $\pm1$. Use summed $4$-volume and the uniform outward ramp $\Psi(q)=\min(1,\max(0,1+q))$ with continuous width $h\Gamma_\delta(u)$, where $\Gamma_\delta$ is the actual pooled within-fiber covariance norm. Use ordinary closed pure-base caps. A response menu is a probability on the actual minimizing caps with mean of their continuous responses $S u$ equal to zero, where $S$ is raw threshold coarea and the continuous normals may be normalized by $\Gamma_\delta(u)=1$.

Take sufficiently small fixed disjoint cones around the five canonical normals $\nabla\lambda_i$. For all sufficiently small $\delta>0$, and then all sufficiently small $h>0$, the actual deepest mark is unique, at $(0,y_h)$ in a fixed interior core of $T$. Every actual minimizing cap is nonvertical, has positive own strip coarea, and strictly isolates vertex $i$ in $T$ for the index of its cone. A balanced response menu exists and every such menu uses all five cones. On the closed endpoint fibers, every active cap in a low cone $i\notin J$ has both ramps identically zero; every active cap in a high cone $i\in J$ has one endpoint ramp identically one and the other identically zero. The total outside mass is $\alpha V\,1_{\lbrace i\in J\rbrace}$, where $\alpha=4\delta^3-3\delta^4$, and both outside strip coareas vanish. Each low direction admits a strictly missed zero-base-tilt representative and each high direction admits a strictly saturated representative capturing either chosen endpoint, preserving its active mass and continuous response. On either endpoint boundary every menu profile is bounded by its total high-cone weight.

## Proof

The convex hull has exactly the three stated integer fibers, since $D_\delta\subset T$ and the endpoints are exposed. The low barycentric sum has density $12r^2(1-r)$ under uniform volume on $T$: at fixed $r$ the factors from the three low and two high coordinates are respectively $r^2$ and $1-r$, and the linear-coordinate Jacobian is constant. Integrating gives $|D_\delta|=\alpha V$. The actual covariance tends to that of $T$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, since the endpoint tensors are uniformly bounded and their masses tend to zero. Thus Euclidean sizes of covariance-unit normals have common positive lower and finite upper bounds.

The simplex centroid $g$ pays at least $\gamma V$, $\gamma=(4/5)^4$, from its own fiber by the ordinary centroid cap inequality used in ea91e021ff1f4416. Each endpoint mark admits an isolating pure-base cap of mass $\alpha V<\gamma V$. At any middle mark and in any continuous direction a finite large base tilt captures exactly one whole endpoint and misses the other, even for the ramp. The own ramp exceeds its ordinary cap by at most $Ch$, uniformly: a Euclidean slab of thickness $h\Gamma_\delta(u)/\lVert u\rVert $ in the fixed simplex has volume bounded by thickness times a fixed enclosing-ball section area. In particular the middle point depth is at most its ordinary own depth plus $\alpha V+Ch$, hence at most $\gamma V+\alpha V+Ch$ by the five canonical own caps of fractions $(1-\lambda_i(y))^4$. Every maximizing mark satisfies $(1-\max_i\lambda_i(y))^4\ge\gamma-\alpha-Ch/V$. This gives $\lambda_i(y)=1/5+O(\alpha+h)$ for every $i$, by the inverse of the fourth power near $4/5$ and the coordinate sum. The resulting marks are in a fixed interior core.

Here attainment and clipping are direct. Normalize $\Gamma_\delta(u)=1$; for $y\in T$ and $h$ bounded, the margins $u\cdot(X-y)$ on all three fibers are uniformly bounded. Clip the real base coefficient to a sufficiently large fixed closed interval, because beyond either end the opposite endpoint ramps are already strictly saturated and the own ramp is independent of that coefficient. The clipping preserves all ramp and strip data. The cap integrals are jointly continuous and their infimum over the compact parameter set and the two middle pure-base cap values is continuous; a maximizer exists on $T$. At a deepest mark pure-base mass is $V+\alpha V$ and is separated from the maximum by the preceding upper bound. Every active strip has positive own volume: the marks have a common own inball and the strip is a one-sided slab through its center. All hypotheses of 88f06d5537aba0ae now hold with buffer zero, so the deepest middle mark is unique.

For the ordinary simplex at $g$, equality in its centroid cap inequality is the apex-side pyramid case in ea91e021ff1f4416. Every pyramid apex of this simplex is a vertex, with the other vertices in the parallel base; thus precisely the five vertex directions attain equality. The ordinary centroid cap fraction has a strict compact gap outside the chosen cones. Since the active own ordinary cap costs at most $\gamma V+\alpha V+Ch$ and the source tends to $g$ as $(\delta,h)\to(0,0)$, the gap excludes every direction outside the cones. By choosing the cones and then the parameters small, every own cut strictly isolates its vertex. The canonical gradients have zero sum and any four are independent in the tangent space. Choose the cones small enough that the convex hull of any four cone direction sets on the unit sphere excludes zero, and that any four directions from distinct cones are independent.

At the interior source, each cap's mark gradient is $-Su$, continuous on the clipped parameter set: the two kink hyperplanes have zero $4$-volume on each fiber, so dominated convergence differentiates the ramp and its first derivative is continuous. If zero were outside the convex hull of the active $Su$, a separating displacement would strictly increase every active cap to first order. By continuity it also increases the caps near the active set by a common positive linear amount, and compactness gives a strict old mass gap for all other caps; pure-base caps retain their gap. This contradicts maximality. Carathéodory gives a balanced probability with at most five active caps, and the cone separation forces every balanced menu to use all five.

For a low direction in a sufficiently small cone, the continuous margin on $D_\delta$ is strictly below the ramp strip with zero base tilt: in the canonical scale it is near $\lambda_i-\lambda_i(y_h)$ and $\lambda_i\le\delta$. Thus every active tilt in that direction must have zero total outside mass. By continuity of the ramp and full support of volume on the closed bodies, both endpoint ramps vanish everywhere.

For a high direction, let $R$ swap $\lambda_0$ and $\lambda_1$ on $D_\delta$, preserving volume. The sum of continuous margins at $X$ and $RX$ for the canonical $\lambda_i$ direction is at least $1-\delta-2\lambda_i(y_h)$, uniformly positive. The positivity persists in its small cone. Pair points $(-1,X),(1,RX)$; their base terms cancel. The positive sum of margins makes at least one ramp one, and the sum of the ramps is strictly greater than one whenever one of them is strictly intermediate. Integrating gives outside cost at least $\alpha V$. A finite tilt can make either prescribed endpoint entirely captured with strict margin and the other strictly missed, giving cost exactly $\alpha V$. For an active tilt a strictly intermediate ramp at even one point would, by continuity and full support, make the integral strictly larger. Each endpoint is connected, so a continuous ramp taking only values zero and one is constant. Exactly one is one. These facts give zero outside strip coarea, leave the same continuous response when substituting the strict representatives, and give the pointwise menu bound.
