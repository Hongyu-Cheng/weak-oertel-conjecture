## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) and use the standard recursive symmetric chain partition and the same block objects as accepted facts 2117de3255444894 and a2c2d2b38e2fa409. Equivalently, scan a Boolean word from left to right, pairing each one with the last previously unmatched zero whenever one exists. Let \(D_m\) be the words with an unmatched zero, let \(i_m(c)\) be the first unmatched-zero position, and let \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), where \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector. Put
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,
\qquad T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m,
\qquad C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m.
$$
These are the block supports of 6fee4107100a027d, not earlier tree-coordinate supports.

For every integer \(Q\ge1\) and every sequence of integers \(m_1,\ldots,m_Q\ge1\), put \(\nu=\sum_{j=1}^Qm_j\) and use the explicit block aliases
$$
(B_j,D_j,F_j,i_j,E_j,T_j,C_j^{\mathrm{bool}})
=(B_{m_j},D_{m_j},F_{m_j},i_{m_j},E_{m_j},T_{m_j},C_{m_j}^{\mathrm{bool}}).
$$
Write \(x=(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(Q)})\in\mathbb R^\nu\) with \(x^{(j)}\in\mathbb R^{m_j}\), and define
$$
\Gamma=\bigcup_{s=0}^Q
 \left(\prod_{j\le s}E_j\right)\times\left(\prod_{j>s}B_j\right),
\qquad P^{\mathrm{ser}}=\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma),
\qquad A^{\mathrm{ser}}=P^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^\nu.
$$
Every empty Cartesian product has one element; every empty scalar product has value one. The exact heterogeneous completion and its disjoint last-spike strata are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{\mathrm{ser}}
 &=\bigcup_{s=0}^Q
   \left(\prod_{j\le s}T_j\right)\times\left(\prod_{j>s}B_j\right)
  =\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{r=0}^Q\mathcal L_r,\\
\mathcal L_0&=\prod_{j=1}^Q B_j,\\
\mathcal L_r&=\left(\prod_{j<r}T_j\right)\times E_r
                 \times\left(\prod_{j>r}B_j\right)\qquad(1\le r\le Q).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For \(x\in A^{\mathrm{ser}}\), write
\(\ell(x)=\max(\lbrace j:x^{(j)}\in E_j\rbrace\cup\lbrace0\rbrace)\).
Thus \(\mathcal L_r\) is exactly the stratum with last-spike index \(r\).

For every integer \(k\ge0\) and integer entrance schedule
\(0=a_0\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_Q\le k\), define
$$
\phi(x)=\max\left(0,
 \max_{\substack{1\le j\le Q\\1\le i\le m_j}}
 a_j(x_i^{(j)}-1)\right),
\qquad
\widehat P^{\mathrm{ser}}
=\lbrace(x,t):x\in P^{\mathrm{ser}},\ \phi(x)\le t\le k\rbrace.
$$
Its exact complete lattice support is
$$
\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}
=\widehat P^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^{\nu+1}
=\left\lbrace(x,t):x\in A^{\mathrm{ser}},\
 a_{\ell(x)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
\tag{2}
$$
For every independently chosen \(\gamma_j\in\mathbb R^{m_j}\) and every \(\zeta\in\mathbb R\), put
$$
w_{(x,t)}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^Q\gamma_j\cdot x^{(j)}+\zeta t\right),
\qquad W=\sum_{(x,t)\in\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}}w_{(x,t)},
\qquad p_{(x,t)}=w_{(x,t)}/W.
$$
The law is zero outside (2). Define its actual depth and cubic moment by
$$
\tau_p(I)=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^{\nu+1}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }I\rbrace,
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{I\in\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}}p_I\tau_p(I)^3.
$$
Let the following three quantities be absolute constants, with natural logarithms:
$$
g=1+\frac1{20736},\qquad
K=1+\frac{2\exp\left(\dfrac{2}{3\log g}\right)}{1-g^{-1/4}},
\qquad c_{\mathrm{ser}}=\frac1{16K^3}>0.
\tag{3}
$$
Then, for all the parameters just quantified,
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{ser}}\,8^{-(\nu+1)}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular, the coefficient is independent of all widths, their number, the height cutoff, the schedule, and every field. No original height atom or completed spatial point is omitted from the probability law in (4).

## Proof

We first prove (1) for the varying widths. Accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d supplies, separately for each block,
\(T_j=E_j\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_j^{\mathrm{bool}}\), nonemptiness of \(E_j\), and
\(|E_j|=2^{m_j}-\binom{m_j}{\lfloor m_j/2\rfloor}<2^{m_j}\).
Each element of \(E_j\) has one coordinate equal to two and all its other coordinates Boolean. The finite set \(\Gamma\) lies in \([0,2]^\nu\) and contains \(\prod_jB_j\). Thus its convex hull is compact, full-dimensional, and lies in that box.

Take an integer \(x\in P^{\mathrm{ser}}\), and express it as a convex combination of generators in \(\Gamma\), retaining only positive coefficients. If no coordinate of \(x\) equals two, every coordinate is zero or one, so \(x\in\prod_jB_j\). Otherwise let \(r\) be the last block containing a coordinate equal to two, and fix such a coordinate in block \(r\). All generating coordinates are at most two, so every positively weighted generator equals two there. For any representation of such a generator in the defining union for \(\Gamma\), its prefix length \(s\) must satisfy \(s\ge r\). Consequently each of its first \(r\) blocks belongs to the corresponding \(E_j\). The integer average in each of those blocks belongs to \(T_j\). Every later block of \(x\) is Boolean, by the choice of \(r\). This proves one inclusion in the union on the first line of (1).

Conversely, consider a point in
\((\prod_{j\le s}T_j)\times(\prod_{j>s}B_j)\).
Represent each of its first \(s\) blocks as a finite convex combination from \(E_j\), and multiply the convex coefficients across those blocks. Holding its Boolean suffix fixed gives a convex combination of points in
\((\prod_{j\le s}E_j)\times(\prod_{j>s}B_j)\subseteq\Gamma\).
The point is integer, so it belongs to \(A^{\mathrm{ser}}\). For \(s=0\) it is already a generator. This proves the union equality. Its block decomposition implies that every block of a support point belongs to \(B_j\cup E_j\). If its last spike is in block \(r\ge1\), the necessity argument puts its first \(r-1\) blocks in \(T_j\), its \(r\)-th block in \(E_r\), and its later blocks in \(B_j\). Conversely every point of that product belongs to the support by the union equality and has last-spike index exactly \(r\). The no-spike points are exactly \(\prod_jB_j\). This proves the disjoint equality in (1).

The function \(\phi\) is a maximum of finitely many affine functions. Since all spatial coordinates lie in \([0,2]\) and \(0\le a_j\le k\), it satisfies \(0\le\phi\le k\) on \(P^{\mathrm{ser}}\). Hence the lift is convex and compact and all its spatial columns are nonempty. At an integer spatial support point, a Boolean coordinate contributes at most zero to the maximum. The coordinate equal to two in a spiked block \(j\) contributes \(a_j\). Monotonicity of the schedule therefore gives \(\phi(x)=a_{\ell(x)}\), including the no-spike case. An integer point of the lift has its spatial part in \(A^{\mathrm{ser}}\), so this proves both inclusions in (2). It also proves the formula when some entrance heights coincide or \(k=0\); in the latter case every column has exactly its height-zero atom.

For the normalizers, use the block-sum convention of 6fee4107100a027d, now with the displayed varying-width aliases, and the height-tail convention of cdf4b723cb4dc8ec:
$$
Z_{X_j}(\gamma_j)=\sum_{z\in X_j}e^{\gamma_j\cdot z}
\quad(X_j\in\lbrace B_j,E_j,T_j\rbrace),
\qquad L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)=\sum_{t=a_r}^k e^{\zeta t}.
$$
All these sums are positive. Define \(W_r\) to be the full weight on the lifted stratum
\(\widehat{\mathcal L}_r=\mathcal L_r\times\lbrace a_r,a_r+1,\ldots,k\rbrace\).
The just-proved disjoint completion gives the exact identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_0&=L_0^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)\prod_{j=1}^QZ_{B_j}(\gamma_j),\\
W_r&=L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)
 \left(\prod_{j<r}Z_{T_j}(\gamma_j)\right)
 Z_{E_r}(\gamma_r)
 \left(\prod_{j>r}Z_{B_j}(\gamma_j)\right)\qquad(1\le r\le Q),\\
W&=\sum_{r=0}^Q W_r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Thus \(W\) is precisely the original lifted normalizer. In particular it includes Boolean points of completed prefix blocks and all allowed heights of every column. Put \(\mu_r=W_r/W\), so every \(\mu_r>0\) and \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\).

Next we establish a uniform reflected gain for a single standard block of any width. For a probability law \(q\) on \(T_m\), extended by zero outside it, retain the oriented definitions of 2117de3255444894 and dad8d1d7630b6923:
$$
H_q(x)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^m}\sqrt{q_{x+v}q_{x-v}},
\qquad b(q)=\sum_{x\in T_m}q_xH_q(x)^2.
\tag{6}
$$
The zero displacement occurs once. In particular \(H_q(x)\ge q_x\). If \(t_m=|T_m|\), finite Hölder gives
\(b(q)\ge\sum_xq_x^3\ge t_m^{-2}\).

Here is the exact small-width calculation from accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409. With out-of-range binomial coefficients zero, that fact gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{2u}&=2^{2u}-\binom{2u}{u}-2\binom{2u}{u+1} &&(u\ge1),\\
N_{2u+1}&=2^{2u+1}-2\binom{2u+1}{u+1}-\binom{2u+1}{u+2} &&(u\ge0),\\
t_m&=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}+N_m.
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting \(u=0,1,2\) in the odd formula and \(u=1,2,3\) in the even formula gives the following entries; the last row is the sum of the preceding two rows:

| \(m\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 22 | 44 |
| \(N_m\) | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 7 | 14 |
| \(t_m\) | 1 | 2 | 6 | 12 | 29 | 58 |

Thus \(t_m/2^m\le29/32\) for \(1\le m\le6\), and every probability law on these supports satisfies
$$
b(q)\ge\left(\frac{32}{29}\right)^2 4^{-m}
=\left(1+\frac{183}{841}\right)4^{-m}
\ge g4^{-m}.
\tag{7}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(183/841>1/20736\). For \(m\ge7\), accepted fact 2117de3255444894 gives exactly \(b(q)\ge g4^{-m}\) for every positive log-affine law
\(q_x=e^{\gamma\cdot x}/Z_{T_m}(\gamma)\).
Combining it with (7) proves, for every standard width \(m\ge1\) and every real field \(\gamma\),
$$
b\left(\frac{e^{\gamma\cdot x}}{Z_{T_m}(\gamma)}\right)\ge g4^{-m}.
\tag{8}
$$
The normalization and oriented reflection convention in this application are exactly those of (6).

For \(L\ge1\), let \(P_L\) be the product of the original independently tilted block laws on \(T_1,\ldots,T_L\):
\(P_L=\bigotimes_{j=1}^L q_j\), where
\(q_j(x)=e^{\gamma_j\cdot x}/Z_{T_j}(\gamma_j)\).
Define its depth using all closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{\sum_{j\le L}m_j}\). Accepted fact dad8d1d7630b6923, which permits different widths and fields in each block, gives its actual cubic-moment inequality
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(P_L)
&\ge\frac18\exp\left(-3\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)
          \prod_{j=1}^L b(q_j)^{3/2}\\
&\ge\frac18\exp\left(\frac32L\log g-3\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)
           8^{-\sum_{j\le L}m_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
It uses the original product normalizer \(\prod_{j\le L}Z_{T_j}(\gamma_j)\). For \(L=0\), take the empty product \(P_0\) to have one point, depth one, and third moment one. The second line of (9) then also holds, since its right side is \(1/8\). This convention agrees with a singleton fiber in any containing ambient space. Singleton or lower-dimensional nonempty block supports are allowed in the accepted product inequality.

We now give an exact reduction for exposed fibers. First, every site of \(E_m\) is exposed within that finite support. For \(v\in E_m\), let \(i\) be its coordinate equal to two and take
\(b_v=(2m+1)e_i+\sum_{h\ne i}(2v_h-1)e_h\).
If another \(v'\in E_m\) also has coordinate \(i\) equal to two, then \(b_v\cdot(v-v')\) is its positive Hamming distance from \(v\) on the other coordinates. If \(v'_i\le1\), the \(i\)-th contribution is at least \(2m+1\) and each other contribution is at least \(-2\); their sum is strictly positive. Thus \(v\) uniquely maximizes this functional. A Boolean site \(v\in B_m\) uniquely maximizes its sign functional \(2v-\mathbf1\). Adding the separate exposing functionals exposes every site of a Cartesian product of such factors. These assertions also cover a singleton factor, where the requirement off its only site is vacuous.

Let \(U,V\) be finite supports with positive laws \(P,q\), and suppose \(v\in V\) is exposed by a functional \(b\), with
\(b\cdot(v'-v)<0\) for every \(v'\ne v\) in \(V\).
Restriction of any ambient halfspace containing \((u,v)\) to the fiber \(U\times\lbrace v\rbrace\) gives a containing halfspace on \(U\), or the whole fiber. Hence its probability is at least \(q_v\tau_P(u)\).
For the converse, halfspaces on \(U\) whose boundary passes through \(u\) suffice: moving any containing boundary parallel to itself through the sample only decreases the halfspace. Write one as \(d\cdot(u'-u)\ge0\). For sufficiently large finite \(M>0\), the ambient halfspace
$$
d\cdot(u'-u)+M b\cdot(v'-v)\ge0
$$
has precisely that intersection with the fiber over \(v\), and contains no point of another fiber. Such \(M\) exists since the supports are finite and the second term is strictly negative off the fiber. Taking infima proves
$$
\tau_{P\otimes q}(u,v)=q_v\tau_P(u).
\tag{10}
$$
If \(U\) is the empty product, its only site has depth one and exposing \(v\) proves the same identity directly. If an exposing functional is zero because the second support is a singleton, there are no other fibers to remove. Equation (10) remains valid in this case too. Therefore, when every site of \(V\) is exposed,
\(M_3(P\otimes q)=M_3(P)\sum_v q_v^4\).
Only exposure of the second factor was used; no general product-depth identity is asserted.

Let \(\sigma_r\) be the spatial conditional law on \(\mathcal L_r\), with weights \(\exp(\sum_j\gamma_j\cdot x^{(j)})\). For \(r\ge1\), its exact product form is
\(\sigma_r=P_{r-1}\otimes q^{[r]}\), where \(q^{[r]}\) is the tilted law on
\(V_r=E_r\times\prod_{j>r}B_j\).
Every site of \(V_r\) is exposed by the preceding argument, and
\(|V_r|=|E_r|2^{\sum_{j>r}m_j}\le2^{\sum_{j\ge r}m_j}\).
Thus (10) and finite Hölder give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\sigma_r)
&=M_3(P_{r-1})\sum_{v\in V_r}(q^{[r]}_v)^4\\
&\ge M_3(P_{r-1})|V_r|^{-3}\\
&\ge 8^{-\nu}\frac18
 \exp\left(\frac32(r-1)\log g-3\sqrt{\frac{2(r-1)}3}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
This includes \(r=1\) by the checked empty-prefix case of (9). For \(r=0\), all \(2^\nu\) sites of \(\prod_jB_j\) are exposed, so
\(M_3(\sigma_0)=\sum_x\sigma_0(x)^4\ge8^{-\nu}\).
All the depths in (10)--(11) use the full ambient space of their product support, including any affine degeneracy.

We include the height and class-transfer arguments with their full normalizers. They are the closed-tail and fourth-power principles of accepted fact cdf4b723cb4dc8ec, and apply here without restrictions on \(\zeta\). For any probability law \(q\) on a finite ordered set, define
\(d_q(t)=\min(\sum_{s\le t}q_s,\sum_{s\ge t}q_s)\).
The heights with lower closed tail less than \(1/4\) form an initial segment of mass less than \(1/4\); those with upper closed tail less than \(1/4\) form a final segment of mass less than \(1/4\). Outside their union, of mass at most \(1/2\), both tails have mass at least \(1/4\). Consequently
$$
\sum_tq_t d_q(t)^3\ge\frac1{128}.
\tag{12}
$$
This includes a singleton height law.

For any spatial law \(\sigma\) and common independent height law \(q\), translate a containing ambient halfspace to have its boundary through the sample \((x,t)\), leaving a contained halfspace. It has the form
\(d\cdot(y-x)+b(s-t)\ge0\) and contains the product of the two events
\(d\cdot(y-x)\ge0\) and \(b(s-t)\ge0\).
Their probabilities are at least \(\tau_\sigma(x)\) and \(d_q(t)\), respectively. A zero spatial or height coefficient gives the whole corresponding factor and does not change the inequality. Independence followed by (12) proves
$$
\tau_{\sigma\otimes q}(x,t)\ge\tau_\sigma(x)d_q(t),
\qquad M_3(\sigma\otimes q)\ge\frac1{128}M_3(\sigma).
\tag{13}
$$
This argument tested every containing closed halfspace of the original spatial-plus-height ambient space.

Let \(p^{[r]}\) be the original law conditioned on the entire lifted stratum \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_r\). Formula (5) gives exactly
$$
p^{[r]}_{(x,t)}=\sigma_r(x)q_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(t),
\qquad q_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(t)=\frac{e^{\zeta t}}{L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)}
\quad(a_r\le t\le k).
$$
In particular no height-dependent comparison or deleted height atom enters this conditional factorization. Applying (13), (11), and the \(r=0\) estimate yields the following positive conditional coefficients:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p^{[r]})&\ge8^{-\nu}\vartheta_r,\\
\vartheta_0&=\frac1{128},\\
\vartheta_r&=\frac1{1024}
 \exp\left(\frac32(r-1)\log g-3\sqrt{\frac{2(r-1)}3}\right)
 \qquad(1\le r\le Q).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
Every conditional depth in this formula still ranges over all closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{\nu+1}\).

For an original sample \(I\) in class \(r\), every containing ambient closed halfspace has full probability at least \(\mu_r\) times its conditional probability. Cubing and averaging against the original law gives exactly the needed fourth-power transfer
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{r=0}^Q\mu_r^4M_3(p^{[r]})
\ge8^{-\nu}\sum_{r=0}^Q\vartheta_r\mu_r^4.
\tag{15}
$$
Here there is one factor of \(\mu_r\) from sampling and three from depth; the \(\mu_r=W_r/W\) are the full original class probabilities from (5). Hölder gives
$$
1=\sum_{r=0}^Q\mu_r
\le\left(\sum_{r=0}^Q\vartheta_r\mu_r^4\right)^{1/4}
   \left(\sum_{r=0}^Q\vartheta_r^{-1/3}\right)^{3/4}.
\tag{16}
$$

It remains to bound the last sum by an absolute number. From (14), putting \(L=r-1\), it is exactly
$$
\sum_{r=0}^Q\vartheta_r^{-1/3}
=128^{1/3}\left[1+2\sum_{L=0}^{Q-1}
 \exp\left(-\frac12L\log g+\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)\right].
\tag{17}
$$
For every real \(L\ge0\), nonnegativity of
\((\frac12\sqrt{L\log g}-\sqrt{2/(3\log g)})^2\)
gives
\(\sqrt{2L/3}\le\frac14L\log g+\frac{2}{3\log g}\).
Since \(g>1\), summing the resulting geometric series proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{L=0}^{Q-1}
 \exp\left(-\frac12L\log g+\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)
&\le\exp\left(\frac{2}{3\log g}\right)
       \sum_{L=0}^{\infty}g^{-L/4}\\
&=\frac{\exp\left(\dfrac{2}{3\log g}\right)}{1-g^{-1/4}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Equations (17)--(18) bound the capacity by \(128^{1/3}K\), with precisely the absolute \(K\) of (3). Combining (15)--(16) therefore yields
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-\nu}}{128K^3}
=\frac1{16K^3}\,8^{-(\nu+1)},
$$
which is (4). The argument includes all widths \(1,\ldots,6\), empty and singleton prefixes, \(Q=1\), \(k=0\), entrance plateaus, and every sign and magnitude of every field. The complete support (2), the full original normalizer (5), and the original ambient halfspaces were used throughout.
