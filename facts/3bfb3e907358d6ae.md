## Statement

Fix the following universal family \(\mathscr A\), independently of every graph to which the theorem will be applied. Consider all connected oriented loopless multigraphs \(\Gamma\) with vertex labels \(1,\ldots,v\) and edge labels \(1,\ldots,e\), where \(1\le v\le22\) and \(0\le e\le33\). Parallel edges are allowed. For each such labeled graph, choose once a spanning tree and an ordering of the remaining edges. Write \(\widehat A_\Gamma\) for its full signed incidence matrix and \(n_\Gamma:=e-v+1\) for its cycle rank. If \(n_\Gamma>0\), let \(\Phi_\Gamma:\mathbb R^{n_\Gamma}\to\ker\widehat A_\Gamma\) be the fundamental-circuit chart whose non-tree coordinates are the chart coordinates, and put
\[
\widehat D_\Gamma
:=\Phi_\Gamma^{-1}\!\left(
\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{e}:\widehat A_\Gamma\mathbf z=\mathbf0\}
\right)
\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\Gamma}.
\]
For \(n_\Gamma=0\), use the displayed singleton support \(\widehat D_\Gamma:=\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^1\), of intrinsic rank zero. Let \(\mathscr A\) contain all these supports and the free-loop support \(\{-1,0,1\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^1\), with duplicate supports removed. Thus it also contains the singleton convention.

Let \(G\) be any finite oriented loopless multigraph, including the possibilities of parallel edges, isolated vertices, and an empty graph. Suppose that some terminating sequence of bridge deletion, degree-two series suppression, and isolated-vertex deletion ends in a graph \(G_*\) whose components are either single isolated loops or connected loopless multigraphs with at most \(22\) vertices and at most \(33\) edges. Here a permitted series suppression replaces the two distinct nonloop edges incident with a vertex having no other incident edge by one edge between their other endpoints; a created loop is retained. The orientations and induced tilts are those of the exact reduction in accepted fact 20ff9a24dcb42eb8. This reduction property is an explicit hypothesis on \(G\), not an assertion about arbitrary graphs.

Use the canonical graphical notation of 20ff9a24dcb42eb8: \(V,E\) are the original vertex and edge sets, \(N:=|E|\), \(r:=|V|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G)\), and \(n:=N-r\), where isolated vertices count as components. For an edge directed from \(u\) to \(v\), its full incidence column is \(\mathbf e_v-\mathbf e_u\). For every original tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^E\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
D&:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E}:
                 \widehat A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},\\
Q&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},
\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Q},\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
&:=\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^E\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
                  \mathbf z\in H}}
              \sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Proper halfspaces have nonzero normals, and all boundary atoms are included. A singleton has normalized depth \(1\), including the zero-dimensional convention when \(E\) is empty; the same depth is obtained in every positive-dimensional ambient embedding of that singleton.

Let \(a_{\mathscr A}\) be the explicit positive finite-family constant of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, equations (11)--(15), for the single family just defined, with the finitely many choices in its equation (11) fixed once. Then \(\kappa:=a_{\mathscr A}^{\,3}>0\) is an absolute constant and, for every \(G\) satisfying the reduction hypothesis and every original tilt,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-n}.
\]
In particular, there is \(\mathbf z^\star\in D\) with
\(\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)\ge\kappa^{1/3}2^{-n}\).

For every \(a>0\), put \(c_{\mathbf z}:=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\), \(T:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}\), and let \(h_c(\mathbf z)\) be the minimum total capacity in a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\), with singleton capacity depth equal to its capacity. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_c(\mathbf z)&=T\tau_p(\mathbf z),\\
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
   &=T^4M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-n}T^4,\\
h_c(\mathbf z^\star)&\ge\kappa^{1/3}2^{-n}T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same \(\kappa\) is independent of total cycle rank, the number of components, the original numbers of vertices and edges, subdivisions, bridge attachments, the chosen terminating reduction, and all original tilts.

## Proof

First verify that \(\mathscr A\) is one finite family of complete lattice supports. For fixed \(v,e\), a labeled oriented loopless multigraph is specified by an ordered list of \(e\) ordered pairs of distinct vertices. There are finitely many such lists, and restricting to connected graphs preserves finiteness. The bounds \(v\le22\), \(e\le33\), and the fixed spanning-tree choices therefore give finitely many charts and supports. Vertex and edge relabelings of any bounded terminal component identify it with one of these listed oriented graphs.

For a connected \(\Gamma\), the chosen spanning tree has \(v-1\) edges and hence \(n_\Gamma=e-v+1\) remaining edges. The fundamental circulation associated with a remaining edge has coefficient \(1\) on that edge, zero on all other remaining edges, and coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) on the tree path closing it. These signs are determined by the edge orientations. This statement also covers a two-edge circuit formed by parallel edges.

For any real circulation, subtract the linear combination of these fundamental circulations with coefficients equal to its remaining-edge coordinates. The difference is a circulation supported on the tree. It vanishes: at a leaf, conservation forces the incident tree-edge coordinate to be zero, and deleting leaves proves the assertion inductively. It follows that \(\Phi_\Gamma\) is a linear isomorphism onto the real circulation kernel and that projection onto the ordered remaining edges is its left inverse. Since its columns are integer vectors, this also proves \(\Phi_\Gamma(\mathbb Z^{n_\Gamma})=\ker\widehat A_\Gamma\cap\mathbb Z^e\).
Indeed, an integer circulation has integer remaining-edge coordinates, and conversely an integer combination of fundamental circulations is an integer circulation. Thus the chart is saturated and describes the full ternary zero-flow section, with no missing points, multiplicities, or lattice-index factors.

For \(n_\Gamma>0\), the support \(\widehat D_\Gamma\) is finite because every chart coordinate is an edge coordinate and therefore belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\) on that support. It contains \(\mathbf0\) and each \(\pm\mathbf e_j\): the corresponding fundamental circulation is ternary. Consequently it is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^{n_\Gamma}\). It is also complete. To see this without any additional predecessor, the convex set
\(\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^{n_\Gamma}:\Phi_\Gamma\mathbf u\in[-1,1]^e\}\)
has integer points exactly \(\widehat D_\Gamma\). The convex hull of \(\widehat D_\Gamma\) is contained in that set, so every integer point of that convex hull belongs to \(\widehat D_\Gamma\). The reverse inclusion is immediate. Thus
\(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D_\Gamma)\cap\mathbb Z^{n_\Gamma}
=\widehat D_\Gamma\).
For rank zero, the tree argument gives a zero circulation kernel and a singleton support; its displayed realization \(\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^1\) is complete and has intrinsic rank zero. The free-loop support is complete and full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R\). All members of \(\mathscr A\) therefore satisfy the support hypotheses of bf1ff94487296374.

For clarity about the coefficient, use exactly the constants \(\eta,\rho,D_0,g\) in equations (11)--(14) of bf1ff94487296374 for this family. Its internal symbol \(\kappa\) in equation (13) is here denoted \(\kappa_{\mathrm{aux}}\), to distinguish it from our cubic coefficient. The admissible choice is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{\mathrm{aux}}&:=\max\{3,\rho^{-1}\},&
\alpha&:=\frac1{8\kappa_{\mathrm{aux}}},&
\beta&:=\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{aux}}}\,D_0,\\
a_{\mathscr A}&:=\alpha
        \exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta^2}{2\log g}\right),&
\kappa&:=a_{\mathscr A}^{\,3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The symbols \(\eta,\rho,D_0,g,\alpha,\beta,a_{\mathscr A}\) retain exactly their meanings in that accepted theorem, applied to \(\mathscr A\). In particular, these are finite-family constants, not parameters of the graph. Since the family contains the ternary interval with \(3>2^1\) points, the theorem's nontrivial branch applies and gives \(g>1\), \(\eta>0\), and \(\rho>0\). Hence \(0<a_{\mathscr A}\le1\) and \(0<\kappa\le1\). All choices defining them have been fixed on the universal family, before \(G\) and its tilts are chosen.

Now apply the exact reduction 20ff9a24dcb42eb8 to a sequence witnessing the stated hypothesis. The objects \(N,r,n,D,\boldsymbol\theta,Q,p,\tau_p,M_3,c,T,h_c\) have exactly the same meanings and normalizations as in that fact. Its reduced objects will be denoted \(G_*,E_*,\widehat A_*,D_*,\boldsymbol\theta_*,Q_*,p^*\), unchanged. In particular, the fact supplies an injective integer reconstruction map \(L:\mathbb R^{E_*}\to\mathbb R^E\) with
\[
\begin{aligned}
L(D_*)&=D,&
\boldsymbol\theta_*&=L^T\boldsymbol\theta,&
Q_*&=Q,\\
p^*_{\mathbf w}&=p_{L\mathbf w},&
\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w)&=\tau_p(L\mathbf w).
\end{aligned}
\]
It also proves that the cycle rank of \(G_*\) is the original \(n\). We use these exact reduction assertions; the component chart assertions needed here have just been proved directly.

If \(n=0\), the circulation kernel is zero, so \(D=\{\mathbf0\}\) and \(M_3(p)=1\), as also stated in 20ff9a24dcb42eb8. Because \(\kappa\le1\), the required bound holds. This covers forests, all singleton realizations, and the empty graph. A proper halfspace in any positive-dimensional ambient embedding that contains the only support point contains probability \(1\). The capacity conclusions then follow by multiplying that probability by \(T\). Henceforth suppose \(n>0\).

Index the positive-rank terminal factors by \(i=1,\ldots,k\). Each is either one free loop or a positive-rank loopless connected component \(\Gamma_i\). Write \(n_i\) for its cycle rank; for a loop \(n_i=1\). Zero-rank components have only their zero circulation and can be omitted from the probability table without loss. For a loop use the identity chart \(\Phi_i\) and support \(\widehat D_i=\{-1,0,1\}\); otherwise use the fixed chart of the labeled representative, writing \(\Phi_i=\Phi_{\Gamma_i}\) and \(\widehat D_i=\widehat D_{\Gamma_i}\). Every \(\widehat D_i\) belongs to the same \(\mathscr A\). The incidence equations are separate on the terminal components, and loops have zero columns. Thus the reduced edge-coordinate support is exactly the Cartesian product of their full circulation supports and any omitted singleton supports. The ranks add, and reduction preserves rank, so \(\sum_{i=1}^k n_i=n\).

The direct sum of the chosen charts, with zero coordinates on omitted rank-zero components, gives a saturated map \(\Phi_*:\mathbb R^n\to\ker\widehat A_*\). Let \(P_{\mathrm{chart}}:\mathbb R^{E_*}\to\mathbb R^n\) project onto the chosen non-tree coordinates and loop coordinates. Then \(P_{\mathrm{chart}}\Phi_*=I\) and
\(\widehat D:=\Phi_*^{-1}(D_*)=\prod_{i=1}^k\widehat D_i\).
With \(\boldsymbol\theta_{*,i}\) the tilt entries on factor \(i\), define
\(\boldsymbol\ell_i:=\Phi_i^T\boldsymbol\theta_{*,i}\) and the positive law
\[
\widehat p_i(\mathbf u_i)
 :=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf u_i}}
          {\sum_{\mathbf v_i\in\widehat D_i}
                         e^{\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf v_i}}.
\]
The unnormalized weight on \(\Phi_*\mathbf u\) is the product of the factor weights, so its partition is exactly the product of the factor partitions. A singleton omitted above contributes weight \(1\). Consequently the probability table is exactly
\[
\widehat p_{\mathbf u}
 :=p^*_{\Phi_*\mathbf u}
  =p_{L\Phi_*\mathbf u}
  =\prod_{i=1}^k\widehat p_i(\mathbf u_i)
 \qquad(\mathbf u\in\widehat D).
\]
This is equality of actual probability tables, not a conditional law or a bound involving separate component moments. Original bridge tilts and series tilts introduce no other weight or partition factor by 20ff9a24dcb42eb8.

We check actual depth for this particular choice of product chart. A proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{E_*}\) containing \(\Phi_*\mathbf u\) restricts under \(\Phi_*\) either to a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf u\), or to the whole chart space. In the latter case its restricted probability is \(1\): its restricted normal is zero and containment forces its constant inequality to hold. Conversely, a chart halfspace with nonzero normal \(\mathbf b\) lifts to a reduced halfspace with normal \(P_{\mathrm{chart}}^T\mathbf b\). This normal is nonzero because
\(\Phi_*^TP_{\mathrm{chart}}^T\mathbf b=\mathbf b\).
The lifted inequality has exactly the original chart inequality on every support point. Thus both comparisons retain every boundary atom and yield
\[
\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u)
   =\tau_{p^*}(\Phi_*\mathbf u)
   =\tau_p(L\Phi_*\mathbf u).
\]
On a finite support, only finitely many intersection subsets occur, so the infimum definition of depth in bf1ff94487296374 and the minimum definition used here agree. Reindexing therefore preserves every actual depth moment, in particular \(M_3(p)=M_3(\widehat p)\).

Here is the exact alias map for the fixed-family product theorem in bf1ff94487296374. Its support \(A_i\) is our \(\widehat D_i\); its law \(p_i\) is our \(\widehat p_i\); its intrinsic rank \(r_i\) is our component cycle rank \(n_i\), not the incidence rank \(r\) of the original graph. Its displayed support dimension \(n_{t(i)}\) equals \(n_i\) for these positive-rank charts. Its product law \(P\) is our \(\widehat p\), and its total intrinsic rank \(R\) is \(\sum_i n_i=n\). The introductory halfspace ambient dimension \(m\) in that fact is \(n\) for this product and \(n_i\) for factor \(i\); its earlier ambient lattice symbol \(n\) is \(n_i\) when used for a factor. The singleton member of the universal family is displayed in dimension \(1\) and has intrinsic rank \(0\), but is not needed as a factor when \(n>0\). Its product-law symbol \(P\) is unrelated to the reconstruction coordinate projection also called \(P\) in 20ff9a24dcb42eb8; that reconstruction projection is not used here.

For any probability table \(q\) in its indicated ambient realization, write \(M_2(q):=\sum_{\mathbf u}q_{\mathbf u}\tau_q(\mathbf u)^2\). Thus the quantity \(\mathcal M_2(P)\) in bf1ff94487296374 is exactly \(M_2(\widehat p)\), with the same actual closed-halfspace depth as above. No entropy capacity from that fact is being identified with the original capacities \(c_{\mathbf z}\) or capacity depth \(h_c\). Its fixed-family estimate (10), with arbitrary repetitions and arbitrary independent tilts, now gives directly
\(M_2(\widehat p)\ge a_{\mathscr A}^{\,2}4^{-n}\).
This is an estimate for the second moment of the entire product; it is not obtained by multiplying component estimates with their individual constants.

Finally apply convexity of \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\) on \([0,\infty)\) to the finite probability weights \(\widehat p_{\mathbf u}\) and the values \(\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u)^2\). This is the probability-form Lyapunov inequality and gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
 &=M_3(\widehat p)\\
 &=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\widehat D}
           \widehat p_{\mathbf u}
              \bigl(\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u)^2\bigr)^{3/2}\\
 &\ge\left(\sum_{\mathbf u\in\widehat D}
                 \widehat p_{\mathbf u}
                         \tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u)^2\right)^{3/2}\\
 &\ge\left(a_{\mathscr A}^{\,2}4^{-n}\right)^{3/2}
  =a_{\mathscr A}^{\,3}8^{-n}
  =\kappa\,8^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(p\) is a probability on the nonempty finite support \(D\), some support point has \(\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)^3\ge M_3(p)\). This proves the asserted actual depth bound.

For capacities, \(T=aQ>0\) and \(c_{\mathbf z}=Tp_{\mathbf z}\). Each closed halfspace's capacity sum is therefore exactly \(T\) times its probability sum, so minimizing gives \(h_c(\mathbf z)=T\tau_p(\mathbf z)\). Summing \(c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3\) then gives exactly \(T^4M_3(p)\), and the same \(\mathbf z^\star\) gives the claimed capacity depth. These normalizations also agree with the capacity transfer in 20ff9a24dcb42eb8.

All factors used above belong to a single family fixed by the universal numerical bounds \(22\) and \(33\). Neither the selected number of factors nor their tilts entered the definition of \(a_{\mathscr A}\). Exact reduction removes any dependence on subdivisions and bridge attachments. The class has unbounded total rank, for example by taking arbitrarily many disjoint cycles, which reduce to arbitrarily many isolated free loops; bridge attachments and subdivisions can also make the original graphs arbitrarily large. No assumption that cycles pairwise intersect was used or inferred. The bounded-core hypothesis remains essential to this application.
