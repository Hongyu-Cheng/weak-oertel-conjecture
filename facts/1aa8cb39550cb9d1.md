## Statement

Let $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body of area $a$ and set $u=|A\cap\{x\ge-1\}|$ and $v=|A\cap\{y\ge-1\}|$. Assume $u\le v$ and $0<v<a/2$. Write $t=-x-y$, let $[\ell,h]$ be its range on $A$, and let $W$ be the maximum length in the $x$ coordinate of a section at fixed $t$. Then
$$
|A\cap\{t\le2-2W\}|\le v,\qquad W\le\frac{2a}{h-\ell},\qquad |A\cap\{t\ge2\}|\ge a-v-2W^2.
$$
If the cut $t=-1$ meets the interior of $A$, put $H=h+1$. Then $W<2a/H$ and $|A\cap\{t\ge2\}|\ge a-v-8a^2/H^2$. The same conclusions hold if any two distinct covectors from $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$ are used as $x,y$, with $t$ given by the remaining covector.

## Proof

Join any two actual points of $A$ at the extreme levels $\ell,h$. Their segment supplies an affine selector $(X(t),Y(t))$ in every section, with $X(t)+Y(t)=-t$. Every point of that section satisfies $|x-X(t)|\le W$ and $|y-Y(t)|\le W$, since the $x$ coordinates are an interval of length at most $W$ and $y=-t-x$ at fixed $t$.

The lower $v/a$ quantile $t_*$ of the height under uniform area is unique and interior, because level lines have area zero and each interior interval of heights has positive area. Consider $X$ at $t_*$. If its slope is nonpositive and $X(t_*)>-1+W$, continuity and monotonicity give $X(t)>-1+W$ throughout all levels up to some $t_*+\varepsilon<h$. Those sections are entirely in $\{x\ge-1\}$ and have area greater than $v$, contrary to $u\le v$. If its slope is positive and $X(t_*)\ge-1+W$, every section above $t_*$ is captured, with area $a-v>v$. Thus $X(t_*)\le-1+W$. Apply the same argument to $Y$ using its cap area $v$. Adding gives $t_*\ge2-2W$, proving the first bound.

The map $(t,x)\mapsto(x,-t-x)$ has absolute determinant one, so $a$ is the integral of the section width. The width is concave by interpolation of interval sections. The maximum exists, since the set of pairs of points of $A$ of equal height is compact and the difference of their $x$ coordinates is continuous. Join the maximum-width point of the graph to zero at both extreme levels. Concavity places this piecewise linear tent below the graph, and its integral is $W(h-\ell)/2$, including a maximum at an extreme level. The strip between $2-2W$ and $2$ has area at most $2W^2$. Adding it to the first lower cap and taking the complement gives the stated upper-tail bound. Properness of the cut gives $\ell<-1<h$, hence $h-\ell>H>0$. Substituting the width bound yields the final estimate. Any pair of the three covectors gives absolute determinant one and the remaining covector is minus their sum.
