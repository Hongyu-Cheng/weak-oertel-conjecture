## Statement

Let \(d,m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let
\(p:\mathbb R^2\to[0,\infty)\) be a coercive polyhedral sublinear
function with maximal full-dimensional linearity cones
\(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_m\). Suppose
\[
p=p_0+\cdots+p_d,
\]
where every \(p_i:\mathbb R^2\to[0,\infty)\) is sublinear. For \(R>0\),
define
\[
C:=\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d:
x_i\ge p_i(z)\ (1\le i\le d),\quad
\sum_{i=1}^d x_i+p_0(z)\le R\right\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), let
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z.
\]
For \(x\in C_z\), define
\[
\delta_z(x):=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J\}.
\]
For every finite nonempty
\(E\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}\), put
\(q(E):=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)\). For every affine
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfying
\(s(z)\in C_z\) for \(z\in E\), define its score by
\[
2^{-q(E)}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}\delta_z(s(z)),
\]
and let \(\mathfrak A(C)\) be the supremum of these scores.

Then \(C\) is a convex body. Writing
\[
\Delta_d:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:y_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^d y_i\le1\},
\]
one has
\[
C_z=(p_1(z),\ldots,p_d(z))+(R-p(z))\Delta_d
\quad\text{whenever }p(z)\le R,
\]
while \(C_z=\varnothing\) when \(p(z)>R\), and
\[
w_z=\frac{(R-p(z))_+^d}{d!}.
\]
With
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
one has
\[
\mathfrak A(C)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4m}W.
\]
Moreover, \(\gamma_d>1/3\), so \(m\le5\) implies
\[
\mathfrak A(C)>\frac{W}{64}.
\]

## Proof

A finite sublinear function on \(\mathbb R^2\) is continuous. Indeed, if
\(r\) is such a function and
\[
L_r:=\max\{r(e_1),r(-e_1),r(e_2),r(-e_2)\},
\]
then subadditivity and positive homogeneity give
\[
r(u)\le L_r\lVert u\rVert_1.
\]
The two inequalities
\[
r(u)\le r(v)+r(u-v),\qquad
r(v)\le r(u)+r(v-u)
\]
therefore imply
\[
|r(u)-r(v)|\le L_r\lVert u-v\rVert_1.
\]
This applies to every \(p_i\) and to \(p\).

Each defining inequality of \(C\) is a convex inequality, so \(C\) is
convex, and continuity makes it closed. If \((z,x)\in C\), then
\[
p(z)=p_0(z)+\sum_{i=1}^d p_i(z)
\le p_0(z)+\sum_{i=1}^d x_i\le R.
\tag{1}
\]
Coercivity of \(p\) makes \(\{z:p(z)\le R\}\) compact. Also
\(p_i\ge0\), so feasibility gives
\[
0\le x_i\le\sum_{k=1}^d x_k\le R.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(C\) is bounded and hence compact. Every sublinear function
vanishes at the origin. At
\[
\left(0,\frac{R}{2d},\ldots,\frac{R}{2d}\right)
\]
all \(d+1\) defining inequalities are strict. Their continuity gives an
open neighborhood contained in \(C\). Hence \(C\) has nonempty interior
in \(\mathbb R^{2+d}\), and \(C\) is a convex body.

Fix \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), put
\[
a(z):=(p_1(z),\ldots,p_d(z)),\qquad t(z):=R-p(z),
\]
and substitute \(x=a(z)+y\). The fiber inequalities become
\[
y_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^d y_i\le t(z).
\tag{3}
\]
They are infeasible if \(t(z)<0\), and if \(t(z)\ge0\), (3) proves
\[
C_z=a(z)+t(z)\Delta_d.
\tag{4}
\]
The simplex \(\Delta_d=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_d\}\) has
volume \(1/d!\). Consequently,
\[
w_z=\frac{(R-p(z))_+^d}{d!}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, the fiber with \(p(z)=R\) is a singleton and has
\(d\)-volume zero.

We next compute the exact depth at the simplex centroid, with the stated
closed-halfspace convention. Let
\[
g:=\frac1{d+1}(1,\ldots,1),
\]
the centroid of \(\Delta_d\). If \(d=1\), then
\(\Delta_1=[0,1]\), \(g=1/2\), and every proper closed halfspace is a
closed ray. The least length of its intersection with \([0,1]\), among
rays containing \(g\), is \(1/2=\gamma_1\).

For \(d\ge2\), Grünbaum's centroid halfspace theorem, in its
dimension-dependent form for closed halfspaces, gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap J)
\ge\gamma_d\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)
\tag{6}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(J\) containing \(g\). The bound is
attained, not merely approached. Namely, the proper closed halfspace
\[
J_1:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:y_1\ge1/(d+1)\}
\]
contains \(g\), and direct substitution gives
\[
\Delta_d\cap J_1
=\frac1{d+1}e_1+\frac d{d+1}\Delta_d.
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore its volume is exactly
\(\gamma_d\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)\). Equations (6) and (7),
together with the direct \(d=1\) argument, prove
\[
\inf_{\substack{J\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\g\in J}}
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap J)
=\frac{\gamma_d}{d!}.
\tag{8}
\]
The closed boundary in (7) is included, so (8) also verifies the
boundary convention literally. Translation and a positive homothety
send proper closed halfspaces bijectively to proper closed halfspaces and
multiply all \(d\)-volumes by the \(d\)-th power of the homothety ratio.
It follows from (4) and (8) that, whenever \(p(z)<R\), the centroid
\[
c_z:=a(z)+\frac{R-p(z)}{d+1}(1,\ldots,1)
\tag{9}
\]
satisfies
\[
\delta_z(c_z)=\gamma_d w_z.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(p(z)=R\), both sides of (10) are zero as well.

We now prove simultaneous linearity of all the summands on every
total-gauge fan cell. A polyhedral sublinear function is the maximum of
finitely many linear functionals. Hence its maximal full-dimensional
linearity regions, taken with their closures, are closed convex
polyhedral cones which cover \(\mathbb R^2\); distinct cones may overlap
only on their boundary rays. Continuity preserves the relevant linear
formula on those boundaries.

Fix \(\Gamma_j\), and let \(\ell_j\) be the linear functional satisfying
\(p=\ell_j\) on \(\Gamma_j\). For \(u,v\in\Gamma_j\), convexity of the
cone gives \(u+v\in\Gamma_j\), and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=p(u)+p(v)-p(u+v)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^d
\bigl(p_i(u)+p_i(v)-p_i(u+v)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Every summand in the last line is nonnegative by subadditivity. Thus
\[
p_i(u+v)=p_i(u)+p_i(v)
\quad(0\le i\le d,\ u,v\in\Gamma_j).
\tag{12}
\]

Coercivity and positive homogeneity imply \(p(v)>0\) for every
\(v\ne0\). Hence \(\Gamma_j\) cannot contain a line: if
\(\{\lambda v:\lambda\in\mathbb R\}\subseteq\Gamma_j\), linearity of
\(p\) there and nonnegativity would give \(p(v)=p(-v)=0\).
Accordingly, the full-dimensional planar polyhedral cone \(\Gamma_j\)
is generated by two linearly independent rays, say by \(u_j,v_j\).
For \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), (12) and positive homogeneity give
\[
p_i(\alpha u_j+\beta v_j)
=\alpha p_i(u_j)+\beta p_i(v_j).
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(\ell_{ij}:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) be the unique linear
functional with
\[
\ell_{ij}(u_j)=p_i(u_j),\qquad
\ell_{ij}(v_j)=p_i(v_j).
\]
Equation (13) proves
\[
p_i=\ell_{ij}\quad\text{on }\Gamma_j
\tag{14}
\]
for every \(i=0,\ldots,d\), including both boundary rays. Moreover,
\[
\ell_j=\sum_{i=0}^d\ell_{ij}.
\tag{15}
\]
This proves the required simultaneous linearity.

Let
\[
Z_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}
=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:p(z)<R\}.
\tag{16}
\]
This set is finite because it lies in the compact set
\(\{p\le R\}\), and it is nonempty because \(0\in Z_+\). Thus
\(0<W<\infty\). Assign each \(z\in Z_+\) to the least index \(j\) for
which \(z\in\Gamma_j\), and let \(E_j\) be the set assigned to \(j\).
This convention assigns points on shared fan boundaries exactly once.
The \(E_j\) are pairwise disjoint and their union is \(Z_+\), so
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{z\in E_j}w_z=W.
\tag{17}
\]
Choose \(j_*\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in E_{j_*}}w_z\ge\frac Wm,
\tag{18}
\]
and put \(E:=E_{j_*}\). The set \(E\) is finite and nonempty. Let
\[
q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)\in\{0,1,2\}.
\tag{19}
\]

Write
\[
\ell:=\sum_{i=0}^d\ell_{i j_*}
\]
and define an affine map \(\widetilde s:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^d\) by
\[
\widetilde s_i(z)
:=\ell_{i j_*}(z)+\frac{R-\ell(z)}{d+1}
\quad(1\le i\le d).
\tag{20}
\]
Let \(s\) be its restriction to \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\). Since
\(\Gamma_{j_*}\) is convex,
\[
\operatorname{conv}(E)\subseteq\Gamma_{j_*}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(z=\sum_k\lambda_k z_k\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\), where
\(z_k\in E\), then linearity of \(p\) on \(\Gamma_{j_*}\) gives
\[
p(z)=\sum_k\lambda_kp(z_k)<R.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (9), (14), (15), and (20) now show that
\[
s(z)=c_z\in C_z
\quad\text{for every }z\in\operatorname{conv}(E).
\tag{23}
\]
In particular, \(s\) is admissible for \(E\). Notice that this argument
does not require \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\) itself to lie in the fan
cone: the globally affine formula (20) is restricted to that affine
hull, while feasibility is needed, and has been proved, on
\(\operatorname{conv}(E)\). It covers without change the cases in which
\(\operatorname{conv}(E)\) is a point or a segment.

Using (10), (18), (19), and (23), the score of this one admissible graph
is
\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{-q}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}\delta_z(s(z))
&=
2^{-q}\gamma_d
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\\
&\ge
2^{-q}\gamma_d\sum_{z\in E}w_z\\
&\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4m}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Here \(2^{-q}\) is respectively \(1\), \(1/2\), or \(1/4\) when
\(q=0,1,2\), so \(2^{-q}\ge1/4\) in every case. Taking the supremum over
all admissible pairs \((E,s)\) proves the global fan-cell bound.

For completeness, the centered-simplex smoke test is consistent with
the construction. Let
\[
B:=\operatorname{conv}\{e_1,\ldots,e_d,-\mathbf1\},
\qquad M:=I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top.
\]
Then \(B=-\mathbf1+M\Delta_d\), \(M^{-1}\mathbf1=\mathbf1/(d+1)\), and
the affine change \(x\mapsto M^{-1}(x+R\mathbf1)\) sends
\[
(R-p(z))B
\quad\text{to}\quad
\frac{p(z)}{d+1}\mathbf1+(R-p(z))\Delta_d.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus this fixed homothetic-simplex bundle has the displayed form with
\(p_i=p/(d+1)\) for every \(i=0,\ldots,d\). The graph selected above is
allowed to lie on any one fan cell and need not be anchored at the
origin, so (24) makes no anchored-simplex assertion.

Finally,
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
=\sum_{k=0}^d
\frac{d(d-1)\cdots(d-k+1)}{k!\,d^k}
\le\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3.
\tag{26}
\]
The last strict inequality follows from
\(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\), with strict inequality for \(k\ge3\);
the case \(d=1\) is immediate. Taking reciprocals in (26) proves
\(\gamma_d>1/3\). If \(m\le5\), then \(W>0\) and
\[
\mathfrak A(C)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4m}W
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{20}W
>\frac{W}{60}
>\frac{W}{64},
\]
as claimed.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pages 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body with centroid \(g(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(g(K)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap J)\ge(d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\). Applicability: \(\Delta_d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body, \(g\) in (8) is its centroid, and the theorem is invoked only for \(d\ge2\); equation (7) supplies an explicit proper closed halfspace attaining equality, while \(d=1\) is proved directly.
