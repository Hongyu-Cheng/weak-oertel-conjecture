## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb R_x^d$ be a convex body, $d\ge1$, with ordinary projection $K$ onto $x$ and line sections $[a(x),b(x)]$. Let $g$ be the centroid of $K$ and let $\bar a,\bar b$ be the uniform means of the endpoint functions on $K$. For every $t_0\in[\bar a,\bar b]$, the point $(t_0,g)$ belongs to $C$ and every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing it has $|\pi_x(C\cap H)|\ge\Gamma_d|K|$ for $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$.

Any integer in $[\bar a,\bar b]$ gives an actual mixed-integer point with that property. In particular, $|C|_{d+1}/|K|\ge1$ suffices. The interval can miss all integers even when the original mixed set has positive mass. In the planar body $0\le x\le1$, $x/2-1/10\le t\le11/10-x/2$, the endpoint-mean interval is $[3/20,17/20]$, the fibers at $t=0,1$ both equal $[0,1/5]$, and the line at $g=1/2$ has no integer $t$.

## Proof

By convexity of $C$, $a$ is convex and $b$ concave. Compactness makes $a$ lower semicontinuous and $b$ upper semicontinuous: converging subsequences of endpoint points stay in $C$, so limits of lower endpoints are at least the limiting fiber's minimum, and limits of upper endpoints at most its maximum. They are bounded because $C$ is compact. Jensen gives $a(g)\le\bar a\le\bar b\le b(g)$ and proves membership.

Translate a containing halfspace inward to its parallel position through $(t_0,g)$, with margin $\alpha(t-t_0)+u\cdot(x-g)$. If $\alpha>0$, maximizing over the line section at $x$ selects $b(x)$, and the projected condition is $-b(x)-(u/\alpha)\cdot(x-g)\le-t_0$. Its left side is convex lower semicontinuous and has mean $-\bar b\le-t_0$. If $\alpha<0$, the maximizing endpoint is $a(x)$ and the condition is $a(x)+(u/\alpha)\cdot(x-g)\le t_0$. The mean of the convex lower semicontinuous left side is $\bar a\le t_0$. If $\alpha=0$, apply the same mean-sublevel theorem to the affine function $-u\cdot(x-g)$ at zero. In all cases the closed projected cap contains the corresponding mean sublevel, so the ordinary convex-function mean-sublevel result gives the bound.

Fubini gives $\bar b-\bar a=|C|_{d+1}/|K|$. An interval of length at least one contains $\lceil\bar a\rceil$. In the example the integrals give $3/20,17/20$, substitution of $t=0$ or $1$ gives the stated two fibers, and the fiber in the $t$ direction at $g=1/2$ is $[3/20,17/20]$.
