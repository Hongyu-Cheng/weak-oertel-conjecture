## Statement

For a finitely supported probability law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^N\), extended by zero outside its support, define
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=
\inf\{p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf z\in H\},
\qquad
M_q(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q
\quad(q>0).
\]
For an integer \(r\ge2\), also define the final-integer-barycenter probability
\[
E_r(p):=
\mathbb P\!\left(\frac{\mathbf X_1+\cdots+\mathbf X_r}{r}
\in\operatorname{supp}p\right),
\]
where \(\mathbf X_1,\ldots,\mathbf X_r\) are independent with law \(p\). This event concerns only the final arithmetic mean. It imposes no integrality or support condition on intermediate averages. On a complete support \(D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\), the mean already lies in \(Q\), so this is equivalently the event that the final mean is integral. The symbol \(E_r\) here is not the pointwise capacity \(C_p\), the capacity notation \(\mathsf E_p\), a Hellinger quantity, or a hierarchical dyadic variable \(\beta_j\).

Fix an even integer \(r\ge4\). For every integer \(m\ge1\), put
\[
N_m=m+1,
\qquad K_m=2^m,
\qquad a_r=\frac{r-2}{2r-1},
\qquad B_m=K_m^{a_r},
\qquad Z_m=1+K_m(B_m+B_m^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_m&:=\left\{(t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^{m+1}:
-1\le t\le1,
\quad \frac{t-1}{2}\le x_i\le\frac{t+1}{2}
\ (1\le i\le m)\right\},\\
D_m&:=P_m\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1}\\
&=\{(1,\mathbf u):\mathbf u\in\{0,1\}^m\}
\cup\{\mathbf0\}
\cup\{(-1,-\mathbf u):\mathbf u\in\{0,1\}^m\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the entire support \(D_m\), with no omitted points, let
\[
p_m(t,\mathbf x)=\frac{B_m^t}{Z_m}.
\]
Thus every one of the \(2K_m+1\) support points has positive probability, and \(p_m\) is log-affine on the complete convex lattice support \(D_m=P_m\cap\mathbb Z^{N_m}\).

The objects just displayed are exactly those in part (C), equations (6)--(7), of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc under the aliases
\[
d_{\rm pred}=m,
\quad P_{d_{\rm pred}}=P_m,
\quad D_{d_{\rm pred}}=D_m,
\quad K_{\rm pred}=K_m,
\quad B_{\rm pred}=B_m,
\quad Z_{\rm pred}=Z_m,
\quad \pi_{d_{\rm pred},B_{\rm pred}}=p_m.
\]
In particular, its pointwise depth formulas apply without a change of normalization.

For every real \(q>0\), the exact weighted depth moment is
\[
M_q(p_m)=
\frac{(1+K_m/B_m)^q+K_m(B_m^{q+1}+B_m^{-(q+1)})}
{Z_m^{q+1}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Writing \(r=2s\), the final-barycenter probability satisfies
\[
E_r(p_m)\ge
\frac{\binom{r}{r/2}^{m+1}}{Z_m^r}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{E_r(p_m)}{M_{r-1}(p_m)}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{3}
\]
and hence no finite constant \(C_r\), depending only on the fixed even order \(r\), can make
\(E_r(p)\le C_rM_{r-1}(p)\) valid for all positive log-affine laws on complete convex lattice supports. At the same time,
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}2^{(r-1)N_m}M_{r-1}(p_m)=2^r.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(r=4\), the choices and exact formulas reduce to
\[
B_m=K_m^{2/7},
\qquad
Z_m=1+K_m^{9/7}+K_m^{5/7},
\]
\[
M_3(p_m)=
\frac{(1+K_m^{5/7})^3+K_m^{15/7}+K_m^{-1/7}}
{(1+K_m^{9/7}+K_m^{5/7})^4},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
E_4(p_m)\ge
\frac{6^{m+1}}{(1+K_m^{9/7}+K_m^{5/7})^4},
\qquad
\frac{E_4(p_m)}{M_3(p_m)}
\ge\frac35\left(\frac6{2^{15/7}}\right)^m\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
M_3(p_m)\sim2K_m^{-3}=16\,2^{-3N_m},
\qquad
\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf0)\sim K_m^{-4/7},
\qquad
\frac{\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf0)}{2^{-N_m}}\sim2K_m^{3/7}.
\tag{7}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc proves that the displayed \(P_m\) is a centrally symmetric parallelotope, that its complete lattice-point census is exactly \(D_m\), and that all its nonzero lattice points are vertices. Its equations (6)--(7), under the explicit aliases in the statement, give
\[
\tau_{p_m}(1,\mathbf u)=\frac{B_m}{Z_m},
\qquad
\tau_{p_m}(-1,-\mathbf u)=\frac{B_m^{-1}}{Z_m},
\qquad
\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf0)=\frac{1+K_m/B_m}{Z_m}.
\tag{8}
\]
There are \(K_m\) points of each nonzero type, having respective probabilities \(B_m/Z_m\) and \(B_m^{-1}/Z_m\), while the origin has probability \(1/Z_m\). Multiplying each point probability by the \(q\)-th power of its depth and summing all \(2K_m+1\) terms gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_q(p_m)
&=\frac1{Z_m}\left(\frac{1+K_m/B_m}{Z_m}\right)^q
+K_m\frac{B_m}{Z_m}\left(\frac{B_m}{Z_m}\right)^q
+K_m\frac{B_m^{-1}}{Z_m}\left(\frac{B_m^{-1}}{Z_m}\right)^q,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is exactly (1). Thus (1) is a genuinely probability-weighted moment formula and accounts for every support point.

We next prove (2) using only the final barycenter condition. Write \(r=2s\), and restrict to ordered \(r\)-tuples having exactly \(s\) positive vertices \((1,\mathbf u)\) and \(s\) negative vertices \((-1,-\mathbf v)\), with total sum zero. There are \(\binom{2s}{s}\) choices for the positive positions. In one Boolean coordinate, if exactly \(j\) of the positive bits are one, exactly \(j\) of the negative bits must be one; hence the number of assignments in that coordinate is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^s\binom{s}{j}^2=\binom{2s}{s}
\]
by Vandermonde's identity. The \(m\) Boolean coordinates are independent in this count. Therefore the number of these ordered zero-sum tuples is
\[
\binom{2s}{s}^{m+1}=\binom r{r/2}^{m+1}.
\]
Every such tuple has probability
\[
\left(\frac{B_m}{Z_m}\right)^s
\left(\frac{B_m^{-1}}{Z_m}\right)^s=Z_m^{-r},
\]
and its final mean is the origin, which belongs to \(D_m\). Summing this disjoint collection of ordered outcomes proves (2). No condition on a partial sum or an intermediate dyadic average was used.

Specialize (1) to \(q=r-1\). After cancelling the common factor \(Z_m^{-r}\) with (2),
\[
\frac{E_r(p_m)}{M_{r-1}(p_m)}
\ge
\frac{\binom r{r/2}^{m+1}}
{(1+K_m/B_m)^{r-1}+K_m(B_m^r+B_m^{-r})}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(0<a_r<1\), the normalizer obeys
\[
Z_m=1+K_m^{1+a_r}+K_m^{1-a_r}
\sim K_m^{1+a_r}.
\tag{10}
\]
The power \(a_r\) was chosen so that the origin contribution and the positive-vertex contribution in the numerator of \(M_{r-1}\) have the same leading exponent:
\[
(r-1)(1-a_r)=1+ra_r
=\frac{r^2-1}{2r-1}=:e_r.
\tag{11}
\]
The negative-vertex exponent \(1-ra_r\) is strictly smaller than \(e_r\). Hence
\[
(1+K_m/B_m)^{r-1}+K_m(B_m^r+B_m^{-r})
\sim2K_m^{e_r}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (1), (10), and (12) give
\[
M_{r-1}(p_m)
\sim2K_m^{e_r-r(1+a_r)}
=2K_m^{-(r-1)}.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(2^{N_m}=2K_m\), multiplication of (13) by \(2^{(r-1)N_m}\) proves (4).

For the ratio in (9), set
\[
\gamma_r:=\log_2\binom r{r/2}-e_r.
\]
Since \(K_m=2^m\), equation (12) shows that the right side of (9) is asymptotic to
\[
\frac12\binom r{r/2}K_m^{\gamma_r}.
\tag{14}
\]
It remains to check \(\gamma_r>0\) uniformly over the allowed even orders. Put \(r=2s\). The sequence
\[
\frac{\binom{2s}{s}\sqrt{s}}{4^s}
\]
starts at \(1/2\) and is increasing, because the square of the ratio of consecutive terms is
\((2s+1)^2/[4s(s+1)]>1\). Therefore
\[
\binom r{r/2}\ge\frac{2^r}{\sqrt{2r}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Also
\[
e_r=\frac{r^2-1}{2r-1}<\frac r2+\frac14,
\qquad
\log_2(2r)\le r-1\quad(r\ge4).
\tag{16}
\]
The second inequality follows from \(2r\le2^{r-1}\), with equality at \(r=4\) and induction thereafter. Combining (15)--(16) yields
\[
\gamma_r
\ge r-\frac12\log_2(2r)-e_r
>r-\frac{r-1}{2}-\frac r2-\frac14
=\frac14.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus (14) tends to infinity, proving (3) and the failure of every fixed-order comparison constant \(C_r\).

When \(r=4\), direct substitution gives (5) and the first inequality in (6). For \(K_m\ge2\),
\[
(1+K_m^{5/7})^3+K_m^{15/7}+K_m^{-1/7}
\le10K_m^{15/7}.
\]
Cancelling the common fourth power of \(Z_m\) and using \(K_m=2^m\) proves the ratio bound in (6); its base exceeds one because \(6^7>2^{15}\). Equation (13) gives the first two claims in (7), including \(2^{3N_m}M_3(p_m)\to16\). Finally, (8) gives
\[
\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf0)
=\frac{1+K_m^{5/7}}{1+K_m^{9/7}+K_m^{5/7}}
\sim K_m^{-4/7},
\]
and division by \(2^{-N_m}=(2K_m)^{-1}\) proves the last claim in (7).

The positive limit in (4) shows that this family remains at the natural \(2^{-(r-1)N_m}\) depth-moment scale. The argument therefore disproves only the proposed final-barycenter comparison. It neither disproves an \(H_q\)-type moment lower bound nor proves or disproves the locked mixed-integer weak Oertel conjecture.
