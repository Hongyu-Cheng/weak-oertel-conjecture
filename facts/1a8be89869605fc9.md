## Statement

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact and convex, let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ be nonempty, and put $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$. Fix an actual $Y\in S$ and $0<t<1$. Define $L_t=t(S-Y)\cap[-(1-t)(S-Y)]$, $G_t(Y)=\mathcal H_q(L_t)$. For the finite set $P$ of $q$-dimensional occupied bases set $D=\max_{z,w\in P}\lVert z-w\rVert_\infty$. A pair of distinct bases with a positive contribution to $G_t(Y)$ at an actual $Y$ can occur only at a reduced rational weight $t=r/m$ with $2\le m\le D$. In particular, only own-fiber terms occur at irrational $t$ and in a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of $1/2$. In the actual product $C=[-1,1]\times K$ with $d\ge1$ and $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ a full-dimensional centrally symmetric convex body centered at zero, at $Y=(0,0)$ one has $G_t(Y)=\min(t,1-t)^d|K|$ for $t\ne1/2$ and $G_{1/2}(Y)=3\cdot 2^{-d}|K|$.

## Proof

For a distinct pair $z,w$, choose a coordinate in which $z-w$ is nonzero. The matching relation $p=w+t(z-w)$ gives a ratio of integers in that coordinate. For a reduced rational weight the congruence of the residue pushforward forces $z-w$ to be a nonzero vector in $m\mathbb Z^n$, hence $m\le D$. There are only finitely many such rational weights, and for $n=0$ or a singleton positive base there are no distinct pairs. In the product at base zero, with base set $\{-1,0,1\}$, the only nonself possibilities are $(-1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$, both balanced. For the own pair, symmetry of $K$ and containment of its positive homothetic copies give $\min(t,1-t)^d|K|$. Each balanced nonself pair has the same intersection measure $2^{-d}|K|$ as the balanced own pair. Their scaled base displacements are distinct. This proves the jump over the punctured limit.
