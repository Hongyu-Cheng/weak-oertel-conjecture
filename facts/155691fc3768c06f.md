## Statement

Let $n\ge0$ and $d\ge1$ be integers. Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty and finite, fix $p\in P$, and for each $z\in P$ let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty, compact, and convex. Assume the anchored Minkowski inclusion
$$
\sum_{z\in P}\lambda_z K_z\subseteq K_p
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
\lambda_z\ge0,\qquad \sum_{z\in P}\lambda_z=1,\qquad \sum_{z\in P}\lambda_z z=p .
\tag{1}
$$
Use the given Euclidean base coordinates. The affine rank $r=\dim\operatorname{span}(P-p)$ may be any integer from $0$ to $n$. Write $h_z(u)=\max_{x\in K_z}u\cdot x$. For every $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ the inequalities
$$
h_z(u)\le h_p(u)+A\cdot(z-p)\qquad(z\in P)
\tag{2}
$$
have a unique minimum-Euclidean-norm solution $A_p(u)$. It lies in $\operatorname{span}(P-p)$, and the map $u\mapsto A_p(u)$ is continuous and bounded. The affine function $z\mapsto h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(z-p)$ yields a supporting inequality $u\cdot x\le h_p(u)+A_p(u)\cdot(z-p)$ on $\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, with equality at every point of the $u$-supporting face of $\{p\}\times K_p$.

## Proof

Let $V$ be the fixed matrix with rows $(z-p)^{\mathsf T}$ and let $c_z(u)=h_z(u)-h_p(u)$. If $VA\ge c(u)$ were infeasible, the finite Farkas alternative would give a vector $\eta\ge0$ with $V^{\mathsf T}\eta=0$ and $\sum_z\eta_z c_z(u)>0$. Dividing by $\sum_z\eta_z>0$ gives weights $\lambda$ as in (1). Since the support function of the weighted Minkowski sum is $\sum_z\lambda_z h_z(u)$, (1) implies $\sum_z\lambda_z c_z(u)\le0$, a contradiction.

Consider the feasible right-hand-side domain $\mathcal D=\{c\in\mathbb R^{P}: VA\ge c\text{ for some }A\}$. For each $c\in\mathcal D$ there is a unique minimum-norm feasible vector $A(c)$ by closedness, convexity, and strict convexity and coercivity of the squared norm. It belongs to the cone generated by the active row vectors. Indeed, each direction $w$ with $V_z w\ge0$ on the active rows is feasible for sufficiently small positive steps from $A(c)$, by finiteness and strict slack at every inactive row. Therefore $A(c)\cdot w\ge0$. The cone duality form of the finite Farkas alternative gives the stated conic representation. Conversely, any feasible vector in the cone of its active rows satisfies $A\cdot(B-A)\ge0$ for every feasible $B$, which implies that $A$ minimizes the squared norm.

A representation with the fewest positive coefficients uses independent rows. If those rows were dependent, choose a nonzero relation with a positive coefficient and subtract the largest positive multiple that keeps all coefficients nonnegative. At least one coefficient becomes zero without changing the represented vector. Thus for some independent active index set $I$ with $|I|\le r$ we have
$$
A(c)=V_I^{\mathsf T}(V_I V_I^{\mathsf T})^{-1}c_I,\qquad
(V_I V_I^{\mathsf T})^{-1}c_I\ge0 .
\tag{3}
$$
The empty set gives $A(c)=0$, in particular when $r=0$. The formula also proves that the solution lies in $\operatorname{span}(P-p)$.

Let $c^{(j)}\to c$ within $\mathcal D$. The finitely many linear formulas in (3) and the zero formula bound the vectors $A(c^{(j)})$. For any convergent subsequence we can take a further subsequence with a fixed $I$. At its limit feasibility, the equalities on $I$, and the nonnegative multipliers in (3) persist. For the empty set, the limit is feasible and zero. The sufficiency above identifies every subsequential limit as $A(c)$, so the whole sequence converges to $A(c)$. This treats active sets of every rank for the fixed matrix $V$, including limits where an active multiplier vanishes or further constraints become tight. Each support function is continuous by compactness of $K_z$, so $A_p(u)=A(c(u))$ is continuous and bounded on the compact sphere.

Finally, (2) implies the supporting inequality on each labeled fiber. Its affine dependence on $(z,x)$ extends it to their convex hull. At $z=p$ the affine right-hand side is $h_p(u)$ and the support maximum of $K_p$ is attained.
