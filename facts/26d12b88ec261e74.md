## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write

\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,
\]

\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]

For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put

\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]

and define

\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\]

Fix arbitrary

\[
L\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},\qquad m\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]

and define the shear, the sheared body, the raw pair body, and its volume by

\[
T_L(z,x):=(z,x-Lz),\qquad K:=T_L(C),
\tag{1}
\]

\[
D^0_{L,m}:=
\{(v,y):(m+v,y)\in K,\ (m-v,y)\in K\},
\qquad
V^0_{L,m}:=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D^0_{L,m}).
\tag{2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq
\frac{V^0_{L,m}}{e\,2^{n+1}}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

More precisely, if \(V^0_{L,m}>0\), there is
\(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) such that every closed
halfspace \(H\) containing \(p\), including the whole space if it is admitted
as a halfspace, satisfies

\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\frac{V^0_{L,m}}{e\,2^{n+1}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, for

\[
\mathfrak P_0(C):=
\sup_{L\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},\ m\in\mathbb Z^n}V^0_{L,m},
\tag{5}
\]

one has

\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\frac{1}{e\,2^{n+1}}\mathfrak P_0(C).
\tag{6}
\]

The following product-core form is an immediate corollary. Suppose that,
for some \(L,m\), there are compact convex sets

\[
B\subseteq\mathbb R^n,\qquad U\subseteq\mathbb R^d,
\qquad B=-B,
\]

such that

\[
(m+v,y),(m-v,y)\in T_L(C)
\quad\text{for every }(v,y)\in B\times U.
\tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(B)\operatorname{vol}_d(U)}
{e\,2^{n+1}}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Finally, let

\[
P:=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^2:
z_1\geq0,\ z_2\geq0,\ z_1+z_2\leq3\},
\qquad
C_{\triangle}:=P\times[-1,1].
\tag{9}
\]

For \(L=0\) and \(m=(1,1)\),

\[
V^0_{0,m}=6,
\qquad
h_{\max}(C_{\triangle})\geq\frac{3}{4e}>0.
\tag{10}
\]

In contrast, accepted fact 829297e4adb3d2ad proves that the eroded
capacity \(\mathfrak P(C_{\triangle})\) defined in accepted fact
6a9b0f7ac15a23be is zero. Thus (10) is a strict separation between the
raw and eroded certificates.

## Proof

Fix \(L,m\), abbreviate

\[
D:=D^0_{L,m},\qquad V:=V^0_{L,m},
\]

and first assume \(V>0\). The inverse of the shear in (1) is

\[
T_L^{-1}(z,y)=(z,y+Lz).
\tag{11}
\]

Both maps preserve the base coordinate, translate each continuous fiber,
have determinant one, map closed halfspaces bijectively to closed
halfspaces, and preserve \(d\)-dimensional volume on every lattice fiber.
In particular, \(K\) is a convex body and its mixed mass is \(W\). Write
\(\mu_K\) for the mixed-mass functional defined above with \(K\) in place
of \(C\).

The two affine bijections

\[
(v,y)\longmapsto(m+v,y),\qquad
(v,y)\longmapsto(m-v,y)
\]

show from (2) that \(D\) is compact and convex. It is invariant
under

\[
\rho(v,y):=(-v,y).
\tag{12}
\]

Positive \((n+d)\)-dimensional volume makes \(D\) full-dimensional. Let its
centroid be

\[
g_D=(\bar v,y_0):=\frac1V\int_D(v,y)\,d(v,y).
\]

The symmetry (12) gives \(\bar v=0\). The centroid of a full-dimensional
compact convex set lies in its interior, as proved in the centroid step of
accepted fact 6a9b0f7ac15a23be. Therefore

\[
(0,y_0)\in\operatorname{int}D\subseteq D.
\tag{13}
\]

At \(v=0\), either endpoint condition in (2) now gives

\[
(m,y_0)\in K.
\tag{14}
\]

For \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), define

\[
D_y:=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:(v,y)\in D\},
\qquad
f(y):=\operatorname{vol}_n(D_y).
\tag{15}
\]

The indicator of the compact convex set \(D\) is Borel and log-concave.
Prékopa's marginal theorem in the exact form recorded in accepted fact
6a9b0f7ac15a23be, applied by integrating out \(v\), shows that \(f\) is
log-concave. Fubini's theorem and the definition of \(g_D\) give

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(y)\,dy=V,
\qquad
\frac1V\int_{\mathbb R^d}y f(y)\,dy=y_0.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus \(f/V\) is a log-concave probability density with centroid \(y_0\).

For every \(y\), symmetry (12) makes \(D_y\) centrally symmetric about
the lattice point \(0\in\mathbb Z^n\). If \(D_y\) is nonempty, it is a
compact convex set, so the sampling theorem in accepted fact
252f1e9b01e7d3b0 gives

\[
f(y)\leq2^nN(y),
\qquad
N(y):=|D_y\cap\mathbb Z^n|.
\tag{17}
\]

If \(D_y\) is empty, both sides of (17) are zero, so (17) holds for every
\(y\), including lower-dimensional and zero-volume sections.

Let \(\widetilde H_0\) be a proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains
\((m,y_0)\). It can be written as

\[
\widetilde H_0=
\{(z,y):a^{\mathsf T}(z-m)+b^{\mathsf T}(y-y_0)\geq0\},
\tag{18}
\]

where \((a,b)\neq(0,0)\). Put

\[
Y_+:=
\begin{cases}
\{y:b^{\mathsf T}(y-y_0)\geq0\},&b\neq0,\\
\mathbb R^d,&b=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]

If \(b\neq0\), the log-concave Grünbaum inequality in the exact form
recorded in accepted fact 6a9b0f7ac15a23be, applied to \(f/V\), gives

\[
\int_{Y_+}f(y)\,dy\geq\frac Ve.
\tag{20}
\]

If \(b=0\), then \(Y_+=\mathbb R^d\), and (16) gives the stronger identity
\(\int_{Y_+}f=V\), so (20) also holds. This handles halfspaces whose
normal is purely in the lattice coordinates.

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define

\[
\iota_z(y):=
\mathbf1_{\{(z,y)\in K\cap\widetilde H_0\}}.
\tag{21}
\]

Fix \(y\in Y_+\) and \(k\in D_y\cap\mathbb Z^n\). By (2),

\[
(m+k,y)\in K,\qquad(m-k,y)\in K.
\tag{22}
\]

Their scores in (18) are

\[
a^{\mathsf T}k+b^{\mathsf T}(y-y_0),
\qquad
-a^{\mathsf T}k+b^{\mathsf T}(y-y_0).
\]

The sum of the two scores is nonnegative because \(y\in Y_+\). Hence at
least one score is nonnegative, and the closed boundary is retained when a
score is zero. Therefore

\[
\mathbf1_{D_y\cap\mathbb Z^n}(k)
\leq\iota_{m+k}(y)+\iota_{m-k}(y).
\tag{23}
\]

Summing (23) over \(k\in\mathbb Z^n\), and using that both
\(k\mapsto m+k\) and \(k\mapsto m-k\) are bijections of
\(\mathbb Z^n\), gives

\[
N(y)\leq2\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\iota_z(y).
\tag{24}
\]

Combining (17) and (24),

\[
f(y)\leq2^{n+1}\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\iota_z(y)
\qquad(y\in Y_+).
\tag{25}
\]

The compact base projection of \(K\) meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only
finitely many points. Thus the sum in (25) is finite, all its terms are
Borel measurable, and integration gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac Ve
&\leq\int_{Y_+}f(y)\,dy\\
&\leq2^{n+1}\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\int_{Y_+}\iota_z(y)\,dy\\
&\leq2^{n+1}\mu_K(\widetilde H_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mu_K(\widetilde H_0)\geq\frac{V}{e\,2^{n+1}}.
\tag{27}
\]

Now let \(\widetilde H\) be any proper closed halfspace containing
\((m,y_0)\). Move its boundary parallel toward \((m,y_0)\) until the
boundary passes through that point. The resulting closed halfspace
\(\widetilde H_0\) is contained in \(\widetilde H\). Monotonicity and
(27) therefore give the same bound for \(\widetilde H\). No boundary-null
assumption is used.

It remains to check the whole space. If \(k\in D_y\cap\mathbb Z^n\),
then the first endpoint in (22) belongs to \(K\). Since
\(k\mapsto m+k\) is a bijection,

\[
N(y)\leq\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathbf1_{K_z}(y).
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (16), (17), and (28) imply

\[
V\leq2^n\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(K_z)
=2^nW.
\tag{29}
\]

Thus the whole-space mixed mass satisfies

\[
W\geq\frac{V}{2^n}\geq\frac{V}{e\,2^{n+1}}.
\tag{30}
\]

Define

\[
p:=T_L^{-1}(m,y_0)=(m,y_0+Lm).
\tag{31}
\]

By (14), \(p\in C\), and its base coordinate is integral, so \(p\in S\).
If \(H\) is any closed halfspace in the original coordinates containing
\(p\), then \(T_L(H)\) is a closed halfspace containing \((m,y_0)\).
Fiberwise translation by \(T_L\) gives

\[
\mu_C(H)=\mu_K(T_L(H)).
\tag{32}
\]

Equations (27), (30), and (32) prove (4), hence (3), whenever \(V>0\).

If \(V=0\), positivity of \(W\) gives a lattice fiber of positive volume,
so \(S\neq\varnothing\). Mixed halfspace mass is nonnegative, and hence

\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq0
=\frac{V}{e\,2^{n+1}}.
\]

This proves (3) in all cases. The raw pair body injects into \(K\) under
\((v,y)\mapsto(m+v,y)\), so

\[
V^0_{L,m}\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(K)
=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C).
\]

Thus the supremum in (5) is finite, and taking the supremum of (3) over
\(L,m\) proves (6).

Under the hypothesis (7), one has

\[
B\times U\subseteq D^0_{L,m}.
\]

Therefore

\[
V^0_{L,m}
\geq\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(B\times U)
=\operatorname{vol}_n(B)\operatorname{vol}_d(U),
\]

and (8) follows from (3), including zero-volume product cores.

For the calibration, the positive lattice fibers of \(C_{\triangle}\) are
indexed by the ten nonnegative integer pairs with coordinate sum at most
\(3\), and each has length \(2\), so \(W=20\). At \(L=0\) and
\(m=(1,1)\), condition (2) is equivalent to

\[
D^0_{0,m}=B_{\triangle}\times[-1,1],
\]

where

\[
B_{\triangle}:=
\{(v_1,v_2):|v_1|\leq1,\ |v_2|\leq1,\
|v_1+v_2|\leq1\}.
\tag{33}
\]

The last constraint removes from the square \([-1,1]^2\) exactly two
right triangles, each of area \(1/2\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{vol}_2(B_{\triangle})=4-2\cdot\frac12=3,
\qquad
V^0_{0,m}=3\cdot2=6.
\tag{34}
\]

Substituting \(n=2\) and (34) into (3) gives

\[
h_{\max}(C_{\triangle})\geq
\frac{6}{e\,2^3}=\frac{3}{4e}.
\]

Accepted fact 829297e4adb3d2ad proves, with every shear, integer midpoint,
and unimodular fundamental cell quantified, that
\(\mathfrak P(C_{\triangle})=0\). The accepted eroded bound in
6a9b0f7ac15a23be therefore has right side zero on this body, while the
raw bound above is strictly positive. This proves the claimed strict
separation and completes the proof.
