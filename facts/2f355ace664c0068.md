## Statement

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)$ for a compact convex $C$. Fix an integer median $z_0$ of its base marginal, meaning both strict sides have mass at most $1/2$. Split the mass at $z_0$ proportionally between submeasures $\mu_-,\mu_+$ of mass $1/2$ supported at $z\le z_0$ and $z\ge z_0$, respectively, and with $\mu_-+\mu_+=\mu$. For every probability coupling $\sigma$ of $2\mu_-,2\mu_+$, send endpoints $p,q$ to the point $T(p,q)$ of their segment at base $z_0$, choosing their midpoint if both endpoints are at $z_0$. The image law $\nu_\sigma=T_\#\sigma$ is carried by the actual median fiber, and $\nu_\sigma(H)\le2\mu(H)$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$. In particular a target point of normalized depth $v$ in that fiber has original normalized depth at least $v/2$.

## Proof

Write $L=\mu\{z<z_0\}$ and $R=\mu\{z>z_0\}$. Assign masses $1/2-L$ and $1/2-R$ from the central measure to the left and right marginals, respectively, with a common constant density on the central measure for each assignment. Their sum is the central mass $1-L-R$, and when this is zero both assignments are zero. For endpoints $(i,x)$ and $(j,x')$ with $i<j$, the image is $(z_0,((j-z_0)x+(z_0-i)x')/(j-i))$. On the central-central stratum use $(z_0,(x+x')/2)$. The strata are clopen among the legal pairs and the map is continuous on each. The image belongs to $C$ by convexity. If both endpoints lie outside a closed halfspace, the whole segment lies in its open convex complement. Consequently $\mathbf1_H(T(p,q))\le\mathbf1_H(p)+\mathbf1_H(q)$ even on the boundary of $H$. Integration gives the claimed factor using the two probability marginals whose sum is $2\mu$. Taking the infimum over all original containing halfspaces gives the depth claim, since their traces on the target fiber are containing halfspaces or the whole fiber.
