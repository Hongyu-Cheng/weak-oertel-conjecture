## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put

\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]

Assume

\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0,
\qquad
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

Let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\) be the lattice point from
accepted fact ebc478d91566cb69, so that

\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\geq 2^{-n}W
\tag{1}
\]

for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing
\(m\). Suppose its half-mass metronoid core is empty. For \(z\in E\), define

\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\qquad
h_z(v):=\max_{q\in D_z}\langle v,q\rangle,
\]

and define

\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_z\lambda_z=1,
\ \sum_z\lambda_zz=m\right\},
\qquad
\phi(v):=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_z\lambda_zh_z(v).
\]

Among all finite families of nonzero directions and positive weights satisfying

\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i\phi(v_i)<0,
\tag{2}
\]

choose one having the smallest possible support cardinality \(r\). Such a
family exists, and it may be chosen with

\[
2\leq r\leq d+1.
\tag{3}
\]

There are globally affine functions
\(\ell_i(z)=\alpha_i+\langle g_i,z\rangle\) such that

\[
\ell_i(z)\leq h_z(v_i)\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
\ell_i(m)=\phi(v_i).
\tag{4}
\]

Put

\[
G_i:=\{(z,x):\langle v_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\},
\qquad
A_i:=C\cap G_i,
\]

and, for every real base point \(z\), put

\[
f_i(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d((A_i)_z),
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\prod_{i=1}^rf_i(z)^{\theta_i}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the same-base fiber product and its weighted image by

\[
\widehat B:=
\left\{(z,x_1,\ldots,x_r):(z,x_i)\in A_i
\text{ for every }i\right\},
\]

\[
B:=
\left\{\left(z,\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_ix_i\right):
(z,x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in\widehat B\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then \(B\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body in
\(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), \(B\subseteq C\), and, for every
\(z\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
B_z=\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i(A_i)_z,
\tag{7}
\]

where a Minkowski sum with an empty summand is empty. If
\(b_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z)\), then, for every \(z\in E\),

\[
a_z\geq b_z\geq\Psi(z)\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z>0.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:b_z>0\}=E
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
W\geq W(B):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}b_z
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}W.
\tag{10}
\]

For \(z\in E\), let \(c_z^B\) be the centroid of \(B_z\). Then
\((z,c_z^B)\in B\),

\[
c_z^B\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)
\subseteq\mathsf M_{\Psi(z)}(C_z),
\tag{11}
\]

and every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c_z^B\),
including one whose boundary contains \(c_z^B\), satisfies

\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H)
\geq\gamma_db_z
\geq\gamma_d\Psi(z)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d^2}{2}a_z.
\tag{12}
\]

The support-minimal choice in (2) has the following circuit structure. Put

\[
V:=\operatorname{span}\{v_1,\ldots,v_r\},
\qquad
L(z):=\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i\ell_i(z).
\tag{13}
\]

Then

\[
\ker[v_1\ \cdots\ v_r]=\operatorname{span}\{(\theta_i)_i\},
\qquad
\dim V=r-1,
\qquad
L(m)<0.
\tag{14}
\]

There is a unique affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to V\) such that

\[
\langle v_i,s(z)\rangle=\ell_i(z)-L(z)
\quad(1\leq i\leq r).
\tag{15}
\]

Thus

\[
G_i=\{(z,x):\langle v_i,x-s(z)\rangle\geq L(z)\}.
\tag{16}
\]

The set

\[
\mathcal R:=\{p\in V:\langle v_i,p\rangle\geq-1
\text{ for every }i\}
\tag{17}
\]

is a compact convex body in \(V\), and the common cap intersection has the
exact form

\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^r(G_i)_z
=s(z)+(-L(z))\mathcal R+V^\perp
\quad\text{when }L(z)\leq0,
\tag{18}
\]

with \(0\mathcal R=\{0\}\), while this intersection is empty when
\(L(z)>0\).

Finally, there is a proper closed base halfspace \(Q_m\) whose boundary
contains \(m\) and such that

\[
Q_m\times\mathbb R^d\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^rG_i.
\tag{19}
\]

It can be chosen as

\[
Q_m=\{z:L(z)\leq L(m)\}
\tag{20}
\]

when \(L\) is nonconstant. When \(L\) is constant, it is a negative
constant, the union in (19) covers every base fiber, and any proper closed
base halfspace through \(m\) may be used. For this choice,

\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q_m}\Psi(z)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q_m}b_z
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{21}
\]

## Proof

The empty-core certificate theorem in accepted fact ebc478d91566cb69
shows that the class in (2) is nonempty and contains a family of size at
most \(d+1\). Since all directions are nonzero, a positive balanced family
has at least two members. A family of minimum support cardinality therefore
exists and satisfies (3). For each of its directions, the finite linear
program defining \(\phi(v_i)\) and its affine dual give (4), exactly as in
the affine-minorant part of that accepted fact.

We first record what minimum support means. It is minimum among all negative
certificates satisfying all three conditions in (2), allowing both the
directions and the positive weights to vary. It is not merely the support of
one fixed positive dependence.

Let \(M:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R^d\) have columns \(v_i\), and write
\(\theta=(\theta_i)_i\). Suppose there were
\(\eta\in\ker M\) not proportional to \(\theta\). Then

\[
\delta:=\eta-\left(\sum_i\eta_i\right)\theta
\]

would be nonzero and would satisfy

\[
M\delta=0,
\qquad
\sum_i\delta_i=0.
\tag{22}
\]

Because every \(\theta_i>0\), the line
\(\theta+t\delta\) meets the boundary of the nonnegative simplex in both
directions. Let its two boundary points be \(\lambda^+\) and
\(\lambda^-\). Both have total mass one, both are balanced by the \(v_i\),
and each has support strictly smaller than \(r\). The vector \(\theta\) is a
strict convex combination of them. Hence the negative number
\(\sum_i\theta_i\phi(v_i)\) is the same convex combination of
\(\sum_i\lambda_i^+\phi(v_i)\) and
\(\sum_i\lambda_i^-\phi(v_i)\). At least one endpoint therefore has
negative value. Removing its zero coefficients gives a negative certificate
with smaller support, contrary to the choice in (2). Thus
\(\ker M=\operatorname{span}\{\theta\}\), proving the first two assertions
in (14). Equations (2) and (4) give

\[
L(m)=\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(m)
=\sum_i\theta_i\phi(v_i)<0,
\tag{23}
\]

which proves the last assertion.

We next prove the cap-volume bounds. Fix \(i\) and \(z\in E\). Compactness
of \(D_z\) gives \(q_{i,z}\in D_z\) with

\[
\langle v_i,q_{i,z}\rangle=h_z(v_i)\geq\ell_i(z).
\]

Thus the proper closed fiber halfspace \((G_i)_z\) contains \(q_{i,z}\).
Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, applied with selected mass \(a_z/2\), gives

\[
f_i(z)=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap(G_i)_z)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z.
\tag{24}
\]

Since also \(f_i(z)\leq a_z\), taking the weighted geometric mean yields

\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z\leq\Psi(z)\leq a_z.
\tag{25}
\]

The set \(\widehat B\) is naturally a closed subset of the compact product
\(A_1\times\cdots\times A_r\), cut out by equality of all base
coordinates. Hence it is compact. It is convex because the equal-base
conditions are linear and every \(A_i\) is convex. Its image in (6) is
therefore compact and convex, and its fibers are exactly (7). Every point
of \((A_i)_z\) belongs to the convex set \(C_z\), so every weighted average
in (7) belongs to \(C_z\). This proves \(B\subseteq C\).

We verify full dimensionality separately, since it does not follow merely
from \(E\subseteq\operatorname{proj}A_i\). Choose \(z_0\in E\). By (24),
each \((A_i)_{z_0}\) has positive \(d\)-volume. The boundary of
\((G_i)_{z_0}\) is a hyperplane of \(d\)-volume zero, so there is
\(x_i\in C_{z_0}\) such that

\[
\langle v_i,x_i\rangle>\ell_i(z_0).
\tag{26}
\]

Choose \(q=(z_*,y_*)\in\operatorname{int}C\). For \(0<t<1\), put

\[
p_i(t):=(1-t)(z_0,x_i)+tq.
\]

All \(p_i(t)\) have the same base coordinate
\(z_t=(1-t)z_0+tz_*\). For every \(t>0\), the point \(p_i(t)\) lies in
\(\operatorname{int}C\): an interior ball about \(q\), scaled by \(t\) and
convexly joined to \((z_0,x_i)\), gives an interior ball about \(p_i(t)\).
The cap slack is affine in \(t\) and is positive at \(t=0\) by (26).
Since there are finitely many indices, one sufficiently small common
\(t>0\) makes every \(p_i(t)\) a strict point of \(G_i\). Hence
\(p_i(t)\in\operatorname{int}A_i\) for every \(i\).

Write \(p_i(t)=(z_t,y_i)\). There is \(\rho>0\) such that the
\((n+d)\)-dimensional ball of radius \(\rho\) about every
\((z_t,y_i)\) is contained in \(A_i\). Put
\(\bar y:=\sum_i\theta_i y_i\). For every
\((u,w)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) with
\(\|(u,w)\|<\rho\), all points
\((z_t+u,y_i+w)\) belong to \(A_i\), and their weighted image is
\((z_t+u,\bar y+w)\). Thus \(B\) contains the ball of radius \(\rho\)
about \((z_t,\bar y)\). It is full-dimensional, and hence is a convex
body in the original ambient space.

For \(z\in E\), all cap fibers have positive volume. Brunn-Minkowski and
weighted arithmetic-geometric mean give

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_z^{1/d}
&\geq\sum_i\theta_i f_i(z)^{1/d}\\
&\geq\prod_i f_i(z)^{\theta_i/d}
=\Psi(z)^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

Together with \(B_z\subseteq C_z\) and (25), this proves (8). Inclusion
\(B\subseteq C\) shows that \(b_z>0\) implies \(a_z>0\), while (8) gives
the converse. This proves (9). Summing (8) over the finite lattice support
proves (10).

Fix \(z\in E\). The density \(\mathbf 1_{B_z}\) on \(C_z\) is admissible
for selected mass \(b_z\), and its normalized barycenter is \(c_z^B\).
The centroid of the convex body \(B_z\) belongs to \(B_z\), so
\((z,c_z^B)\in B\), and the density representation gives
\(c_z^B\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)\). Since
\(0<\Psi(z)\leq b_z\), multiplying any density admissible for mass
\(b_z\) by \(\Psi(z)/b_z\) preserves its normalized barycenter and makes
its mass \(\Psi(z)\). This proves the set inclusion and hence (11).
Applying accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d at selected mass \(b_z\), followed
by (8), proves (12), including halfspaces whose boundary passes through the
selector.

It remains to prove the circuit normal form. Define

\[
T:V\to\mathbb R^r,
\qquad
T(x):=(\langle v_i,x\rangle)_i.
\]

The map \(T\) is injective because the \(v_i\) span \(V\). Balance in (2)
shows that its image lies in

\[
\mathcal H_\theta:=\{u\in\mathbb R^r:\sum_i\theta_i u_i=0\}.
\]

Both spaces have dimension \(r-1\) by (14), so
\(T(V)=\mathcal H_\theta\). For every \(z\), the vector with coordinates
\(\ell_i(z)-L(z)\) belongs to \(\mathcal H_\theta\). It therefore has a
unique preimage \(s(z)\in V\). Since \(T^{-1}\) is linear and the
\(\ell_i-L\) are affine, \(s\) is affine. This proves (15), and (16)
follows by substitution.

The set \(\mathcal R\) is closed and convex, and \(0\) is in its relative
interior. It is bounded. Indeed, if it were unbounded, normalization of an
unbounded sequence would give a unit vector \(p\in V\) such that
\(\langle v_i,p\rangle\geq0\) for every \(i\). Positive balance would then
give

\[
0=\sum_i\theta_i\langle v_i,p\rangle,
\]

so every inner product would vanish. Since the \(v_i\) span \(V\), this
would force \(p=0\), a contradiction. Hence \(\mathcal R\) is a compact
convex body in \(V\).

Fix \(z\), decompose \(x-s(z)=p+w\) with \(p\in V\) and
\(w\in V^\perp\), and put \(t=-L(z)\). If \(t>0\), the inequalities in
all the caps are equivalent to
\(\langle v_i,p/t\rangle\geq-1\) for every \(i\), which is equivalent to
\(p/t\in\mathcal R\). If \(t=0\), those inequalities say that all
\(\langle v_i,p\rangle\geq0\); the same positive-balance argument forces
\(p=0\). This proves (18). If \(L(z)>0\), summing the cap inequalities
with weights \(\theta_i\) would give \(0\geq L(z)>0\), so the common
intersection is empty.

Finally, suppose \(L\) is nonconstant and define \(Q_m\) by (20). It is a
proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(m\), and (23) gives
\(L(z)\leq L(m)<0\) on \(Q_m\). If \((z,x)\) with \(z\in Q_m\) lay outside
every \(G_i\), then

\[
0=\left\langle\sum_i\theta_iv_i,x\right\rangle
<\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(z)=L(z)<0,
\]

a contradiction. This proves (19). If \(L\) is constant, (23) says that
it is negative, and the same argument proves that the cap union covers
every base fiber. Since \(n\geq1\), a proper closed base halfspace through
\(m\) exists. In both cases, (1), (8), and (25) give (21).

The centroids in (11) vary with the lattice fiber. Nothing above places one
of them at the anchor \(m\) or proves a mixed-depth lower bound for a single
point, so the locked weak Oertel target is not asserted.
