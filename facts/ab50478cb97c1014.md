## Statement

For the real-variable function $F(q)=[\sum_{j\ge0}(q-j)_+^2]^{1/3}$, the inequality $F'(q+1)\le F'(q)$ fails at $q=2$. In fact $F'(2)=2/5^{2/3}$ and $F'(3)=4/14^{2/3}$, with $F'(3)^3-F'(2)^3=8/1225>0$.

## Proof

At either integer, the finite sum is differentiable because a summand entering at zero has both value and derivative zero. The quadratic sum and its derivative at $2$ are $2^2+1^2=5$ and $2(2+1)=6$. At $3$ they are $3^2+2^2+1^2=14$ and $2(3+2+1)=12$. The chain rule gives the two stated derivatives. Cubing and subtracting gives $64/196-8/25=8/1225$, proving the strict reverse inequality.
