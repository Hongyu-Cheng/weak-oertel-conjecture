## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge 1$, a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb R^n$, and nonempty compact convex sets $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ for $z\in P$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_z})$, and $M=\mu(S)$. Let $h_\mu(z,x)$ be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(z,x)$. For each $z\in P$ and $(u,t)\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\times\mathbb R$ define
$$
c_z(u,t)=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R^n}\mu\{(w,y):a\cdot(w-z)+u\cdot y\ge t\}.
$$
The infimum is attained by a finite $a$, and $c_z$ is continuous on its entire parameter space. For every $x\in K_z$,
$$
h_\mu(z,x)=\min_{\|u\|=1}c_z(u,u\cdot x).
$$
In particular, the depth is continuous on each fiber, is attained by a nonvertical closed halfspace through the point, and attains its maximum over $S$.

## Proof

For fixed $(u,t)$ apply the finite affine-threshold compactification to a minimizing sequence with thresholds $t-a_j\cdot(w-z)$ on a ball containing all fibers. The evaluation at $z$ stays equal to $t$. The realizing affine threshold has that same value at $z$, hence has the form $t-a\cdot(w-z)$ for some finite $a$. Its mass equals the infimum. For $(u_j,t_j)\to(u,t)$, restrict to a subsequence realizing the lower limit of the capacities and take attaining $a_j$. Compactification keeps the finite limiting evaluation $t$ at $z$ and gives an admissible cap of limiting mass $\liminf_j c_z(u_j,t_j)$. Thus $c_z(u,t)\le\liminf_j c_z(u_j,t_j)$. For the upper limit, use an attaining $a$ for $(u,t)$ as a test at every $(u_j,t_j)$ and apply dominated convergence, since a nonvertical cap has $d$-volume-null boundaries within the fibers.

Moving a containing closed halfspace inward to pass through $(z,x)$ can only reduce its mass. A vertical through-point halfspace has the form $a_0\cdot(w-z)\ge0$ for some nonzero $a_0$. Fix a unit $u$. For a sufficiently large finite $L>0$, the nonvertical cap
$$
L a_0\cdot(w-z)+u\cdot(y-x)\ge0
$$
includes every strictly included original fiber and excludes every strictly excluded one. At bases where $a_0\cdot(w-z)=0$ it contains only a subset of the original vertical cap, and it passes through $(z,x)$. Thus its mass is at most that of the vertical cap. This comparison handles whole fibers on a vertical boundary. A nonvertical through-point cap has threshold $u\cdot x$ and some slope $a$. This proves the depth formula, with a minimum by compactness of the unit sphere and the established continuity. The continuous function $(u,x)\mapsto c_z(u,u\cdot x)$ is uniformly continuous on $\mathbb S^{d-1}\times K_z$, so its minimum over $u$ is continuous in $x$. Finitely many compact fibers give a global maximizing point.
