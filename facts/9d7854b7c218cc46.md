## Statement

Let $B$ be a positive-length interval, $k\ge1$ an integer, $0\le\eta\le\mathbf1_B\,dx$, and $\eta(B)=\alpha|B|$ with $\alpha\in[0,1]$. Let $g$ be the centroid of $\mathbf1_B\,dx+k\eta$. Denote by $\Psi_k(\alpha)$ the infimum of $(|B\cap H|+\eta(H))/|B|$ over all such $B,\eta$ and all closed line halfspaces $H\ni g$. Then $\Psi_1(\alpha)=(1+\alpha^2)/2$. For $k\ge2$ set $L_k=(\sqrt{(k+1)/3}-1)/k$ and $U_k=(\sqrt{k(k+1)}-1)/k$. Then
$$
\Psi_k(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \alpha+\frac{1+k\alpha^2}{2(1+k\alpha)},&0\le\alpha\le L_k,\\
\displaystyle \frac{2\sqrt{(1+k\alpha)^2+k+1}-3-k\alpha}{k},&L_k\le\alpha\le U_k,\\
\displaystyle \frac{k\alpha^2-(k-1)\alpha+k}{k+1},&U_k\le\alpha\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
The branches agree at the junctions and indicator bands attain the values. The endpoint values are $\Psi_k(0)=1/2$ and $\Psi_k(1)=1$. The minimum over $\alpha$ is $1/2$ uniquely at $\alpha=0$ for $k=1$, and $(\sqrt{3(k+1)}-2)/k$ uniquely at $\alpha=L_k$ for $k\ge2$. For $k\ge2$ an upper-end band of relative mass $L_k$ attains this minimum.

## Proof

Apply the fixed-mass one-copy program with $d=1$ and $q=2$. Its volume-one simplex is an interval, and the cost and containing-cap convention are exactly those of this statement. The scalar inequality is $s\ge S(r)$, where
$$
S(r)=\frac{1+k\alpha^2-2k\alpha r+2kr^2}{2(1+kr)}.
$$
The upper constraint $S(r)\le1-\alpha+r$ is equivalent to $r\ge r_0=(k\alpha^2+2\alpha-1)/(2(k+1))$. Thus every feasible cost is at least $F(r)=S(r)+r$ for some $r\in[\max(0,r_0),\alpha]$. This interval is nonempty because $r_0\le\alpha$, as $k\alpha^2-2k\alpha-1<0$. For $D=(1+k\alpha)^2+k+1$,
$$
F'(r)=\frac{4(1+kr)^2-D}{2(1+kr)^2},
$$
so the minimum of $F$ on that interval is at the projection of $r_*=(\sqrt D-2)/(2k)$ onto it.

For $k\ge2$, $r_*\ge0$. With $A=1+k\alpha$, the inequality $r_*\le\alpha$ is $\sqrt D\le2A$, equivalent to $\alpha\ge L_k$. Also $1+kr_0=D/[2(k+1)]$, so $r_*\ge r_0$ is equivalent to $\sqrt D\le k+1$, or $\alpha\le U_k$. The zero of $r_0$ is $A_k=(\sqrt{k+1}-1)/k$ and $0\le L_k\le A_k\le U_k<1$, with $L_k<U_k$. Hence the choices are $r=\alpha,r_*,r_0$ in the three ranges. To verify feasibility of $s=S(r)$, its numerator is $1+k(\alpha-r)^2+kr^2>0$ and the upper bound is already checked. In the first range $S(\alpha)\ge\alpha$ is $1-2\alpha-k\alpha^2\ge0$, true as $\alpha\le A_k$. In the middle range $S(r)\ge r$ is $r\le(1+k\alpha^2)/(2A)$, and the latter upper bound has $1+k(1+k\alpha^2)/(2A)=D/(2A)>\sqrt D/2=1+kr_*$. In the last range $S(r_0)=1-\alpha+r_0\ge r_0$. The scalar construction thus realizes the projected minima. With $y=1+kr$, one has $F(r)=(D/y+4y-6-2k\alpha)/(2k)$. Evaluating at $\alpha,r_*,r_0$ gives the three expressions, with agreement since the choices of $r$ coincide at both junctions.

For $k=1$ the signs of $r_0$ and $r_*$ change at $\sqrt2-1$. Below it the projection is $0$; at or above it the projection is $r_0$ by the same comparison $\sqrt D\le k+1$. Both are feasible: $S(0)\le1-\alpha$ when $r_0\le0$, and $S(r_0)=1-\alpha+r_0$. The two substitutions both give $(1+\alpha^2)/2$.

For $k\ge2$ the derivative of the first branch has the sign of $3(1+k\alpha)^2-(k+1)$, so that branch is decreasing until $L_k$ whenever it is not a singleton. The derivative of the middle branch is $2(1+k\alpha)/\sqrt D-1$, positive beyond $L_k$. The last branch has derivative $(2k\alpha-(k-1))/(k+1)>0$ for $\alpha\ge U_k$, because $\sqrt{k(k+1)}>(k+1)/2$. At $L_k$ put $m=\sqrt{(k+1)/3}$; the minimizing $r=L_k=(m-1)/k$ and $s=(2m-1)/k$ give the asserted minimum. The entire added band is the upper-end band because $r=\alpha$ in the attaining construction. For $k=1$ uniqueness in $\alpha$ follows from the single quadratic formula.
