## Statement

Retain the polytope \(P\), normalized facet slacks \(f_i\), original standard lattice, and complete support \(D\) of accepted 6f21a941612aa80e. Explicitly, for \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^{10}\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_i(\mathbf x)&=1+x_i &&(1\le i\le10),\\
f_{11}(\mathbf x)&=1-\frac{33}{32}x_1-\frac{55}{32}x_2
 -\frac54\sum_{i=3}^{10}x_i,\\
f_{12}(\mathbf x)&=1-\frac{55}{32}x_1-\frac{33}{32}x_2
 -\frac54\sum_{i=3}^{10}x_i,\\
P&=\{\mathbf x:f_i(\mathbf x)\ge0\text{ for }1\le i\le12\},
\qquad D=P\cap\mathbb Z^{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a full-dimensional bounded standard-lattice polytope with exactly twelve facets and \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^{10}=\{\mathbf0\}\). The unique maximum-absolute-determinant Gale pair is \(\{1,2\}\). Order the omitted indices as \(a=2\), \(b=1\), and define the selected slacks by \(y_i=f_{i+2}\) for \(1\le i\le10\). Thus \(y_i(\mathbf x)=1+\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x\), with independent gradients.

Let \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), write \(\mathbf X=\mathbf x+\mathbf1\), and define the complete affine line

\[
\ell=\left\{\mathbf x:
\mathbf X=(t,7-t,3,0,0,0,0,0,0,0),\ t\in\mathbb R\right\}.
\]

Let \(\mathbf p\) and \(\mathbf q\) be its standard lattice sites with \(t=3\) and \(t=4\), respectively. Then \(\mathbf v=\mathbf q-\mathbf p=\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_2\) is a primitive difference from an overweight selected-floor bin. In the omitted-slack representation \(f_a=1+\sum_i a_i(1-y_i)\), \(f_b=1+\sum_i b_i(1-y_i)\), all coefficients have absolute value at most one, and
\(A=\sum_i a_i(\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf v)=1\),
\(B=-\sum_i b_i(\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf v)=1\), and
\(\lvert\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf v\rvert<1\) for every \(i\).

For every complete affine line \(\ell'\) parallel to \(\mathbf v\) meeting \(D\), define its complete original demand and complete digital shadow by

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\ell'}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\cap\ell'}2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{10}y_i(\mathbf I)},\\
\mathcal N(\ell')&=\left\{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{10}:
\exists\mathbf x\in\ell'\ \
k_i\le y_i(\mathbf x)<k_i+1\text{ for every }i\right\},
\qquad c(\mathbf k)=2^{-\sum_i k_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The subfamily \(\mathcal A=\{\ell\}\) violates the proposed Hall inequality. More precisely, with \(\mathbf k=(3,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)\),

\[
\mathcal N(\ell)=\{\mathbf k\},
\qquad
\sum_{\ell'\in\mathcal A}d_{\ell'}
=2^{-11/4}
>2^{-3}
=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\bigcup_{\ell'\in\mathcal A}\mathcal N(\ell')}c(\mathbf j).
\]

## Proof

Accepted 6f21a941612aa80e proves the stated boundedness, full dimension, all twelve facets, standard-lattice vertex integrality, original unique interiority, and unique maximum-determinant pair for exactly the displayed \(P\). The present proof uses this accepted geometric certification with unchanged objects. It does not use any unverified handoff as a premise. The normalization \(f_i(\mathbf0)=1\) is explicit above.

For clarity, the determinant and original-interiority checks are also visible directly in these coordinates. The first ten facet gradients are the standard basis, and a two-row relation matrix has columns

\[
G_1=\begin{pmatrix}33/32\\55/32\end{pmatrix},\quad
G_2=\begin{pmatrix}55/32\\33/32\end{pmatrix},\quad
G_i=\begin{pmatrix}5/4\\5/4\end{pmatrix}\ (3\le i\le10),\quad
G_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\quad
G_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first pair has absolute determinant \(121/64\). Pairing the displayed column types shows that every other absolute determinant belongs to \(\{0,55/64,33/32,55/32,5/4,1\}\), each strictly smaller than \(121/64\). Any change of relation-row basis multiplies every determinant by the same nonzero factor. The selected gradients are independent because they include \(\mathbf e_3,\ldots,\mathbf e_{10}\), and the last two gradients have nonzero determinant on the first two coordinates. For original interiority, every interior standard lattice point must satisfy \(x_i\ge0\) from \(f_i>0\) for \(i\le10\). At a nonzero such point, \(f_{11}\le1-33/32<0\). The origin has every slack equal to one, proving the uniqueness directly.

The two omitted expressions in the selected slacks are

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_a=f_2&=5-\frac5{11}\sum_{i=1}^{8}y_i
 -\frac{10}{11}y_9+\frac6{11}y_{10},\\
f_b=f_1&=5-\frac5{11}\sum_{i=1}^{8}y_i
 +\frac6{11}y_9-\frac{10}{11}y_{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each coefficient row in constant-minus-sum form has eight entries \(5/11\) and the entries \(10/11,-6/11\) in the indicated order; its entries sum to four, as required for the constant five. Thus these are exactly the normalized forms in the statement, and every coefficient has absolute value at most \(10/11<1\).

The integral translation \(\mathbf X=\mathbf x+\mathbf1\) gives the original inequalities

\[
\mathbf X\ge0,\qquad
33X_1+55X_2+40\sum_{i=3}^{10}X_i\le440,\qquad
55X_1+33X_2+40\sum_{i=3}^{10}X_i\le440.
\]

On the entire affine line \(\ell\), the omitted slacks are \(f_1=t\) and \(f_2=7-t\), while the selected slacks are exactly

\[
y_1=3,\qquad y_2=\cdots=y_8=0,\qquad
y_9=\frac{22t-65}{32},\qquad
y_{10}=\frac{89-22t}{32}.
\]

Consequently the original line section is
\(P\cap\ell=\{\mathbf x\in\ell:65/22\le t\le89/22\}\).
Indeed, the two selected upper inequalities give precisely these two bounds, which imply \(0<t<7\); hence both omitted lower inequalities are strictly satisfied throughout the section. Every remaining original inequality is satisfied by the displayed constant coordinates. Since the translation preserves the standard lattice, \(\mathbf x\in\ell\) is integral exactly when \(t\) is integral. The inequalities \(2<65/22<3<4<89/22<5\) therefore prove that the complete original support on this line is exactly \(D\cap\ell=\{\mathbf p,\mathbf q\}\), including every boundary site.

At \(\mathbf p\), the last two selected slacks are \((1/32,23/32)\), and at \(\mathbf q\) they are \((23/32,1/32)\). Both points thus have selected floor vector \(\mathbf k\), and each has total selected fractional part \(3/4\). The weight of this actual occupied bin is at least \(2\cdot2^{-3/4}=2^{1/4}>1\). In particular, the direction used here is obtained from two points in an overweight original bin. Their difference \(\mathbf v=\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_2\) is already primitive.

Its selected slopes are \(\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf v=0\) for \(1\le i\le8\), \(\mathbf u_9\cdot\mathbf v=11/16\), and \(\mathbf u_{10}\cdot\mathbf v=-11/16\). Thus they all have absolute value less than one. Substitution in the two omitted coefficient rows gives \(A=(10+6)/16=1\) and \(B=(6+10)/16=1\). This is consistent with the original unique-interior restriction, rather than an imposed direction assumption: all selected slacks at each of the original lattice vectors \(\mathbf v\) and \(-\mathbf v\) are strictly positive, while \(f_2(\mathbf v)=0\), \(f_1(-\mathbf v)=0\), and the other omitted slack equals two in each case. The selected slopes sum to zero, so the prescribed exponential ratio along this direction is exactly one.

It remains to check the entire shadow, including all potential targets beyond the original omitted-facet endpoints. On the entire real line, the selected nonnegative orthant is reached exactly when \(65/22\le t\le89/22\), by the last two displayed slacks. On this whole closed interval, \(y_9+y_{10}=3/4\) and each summand lies in \([0,3/4]\). Hence both have floor zero, even at the interval endpoints. All other selected coordinates are the fixed nonnegative integers already displayed. The whole intersection of the real line with the selected orthant therefore lies in the single half-open unit cube with floor vector \(\mathbf k\), and it is nonempty. This proves \(\mathcal N(\ell)=\{\mathbf k\}\) exactly. There are no additional target bins beyond the omitted-facet endpoints, since here the selected constraints already give the shorter interval.

Finally, each of the two original sites has total selected slack \(3+3/4=15/4\). Its complete line demand is therefore \(d_\ell=2\cdot2^{-15/4}=2^{-11/4}=2^{1/4}c(\mathbf k)>c(\mathbf k)\). This is the claimed strict Hall violation for the one-line subfamily. The line lies in the selected facet hyperplanes \(f_4=\cdots=f_{10}=0\), so the unique-interior hypothesis does not prohibit its two lattice sites in the relative interior of this line section. The failure concerns the allowed destinations of this allocation. It gives no lower bound on \(B(P)\), and accepted 6f21a941612aa80e in fact proves \(B(P)<4^{10}\) for this same polytope.
