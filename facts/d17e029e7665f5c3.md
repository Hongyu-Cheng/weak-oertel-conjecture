## Statement

For a roof body
\[
C=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in T,\ -\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)\},
\quad S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
define
\[
a_i=\lfloor\ell_i^-\rfloor,\quad b_i=\lfloor\ell_i^+\rfloor,\quad
N_i=a_i+b_i+2,\quad
B=\prod_i(N_i-1),\quad M=\int_TB,
\]
\[
\mathbf m=(\mathbf1+\mathbf b-\mathbf a)/2,\quad
\mathcal K(\mathbf y)=\mathbf m(\mathbf y)+[-1/2,1/2]^n,
\quad
F=\prod_i\frac{N_i(N_i^2-1)}{N_i^2+2}.
\]
For a nonnegative base density \(\rho\), put
\[
\mathfrak D_\rho(\mathbf p)
=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\int_T\rho(\mathbf y)
\mathbf1_{\{(\mathcal K(\mathbf y)\times\{\mathbf y\})\cap H\ne\varnothing\}}
\,d\mathbf y.
\]
Let \(\nu\) be mixed measure on the anchor sections
\(\prod_i\{-a_i,\ldots,b_i\}\), and put
\[
\mathcal C(\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0)
=\inf_{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\ne0}
\nu\{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf k+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_0)
\ge\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf J-\sum_i(u_i)_+\}.
\]
All halfspaces are closed.

(a) There are thin roof bodies indexed by \(s\to\infty\), with \(\Delta_B=M/2\), such that
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0}
\mathfrak D_B((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))/\Delta_B\le2^{1-s},
\]
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0}
\mathfrak D_F((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))/\Delta_B
\le2(6/11)^s,
\]
while \(h_S((\mathbf1,0))\ge\Delta_B\).

If a nonnegative scalar function \(g\) admits a positive dimension-independent constant in the shifted-center box bound
\[
\#(H\cap\text{box})\ge c\prod_i g(N_i),
\]
for all integer boxes and all containing halfspaces through coordinatewise half-unit shifts of their centers, then on the same examples
\[
\sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0}
\mathfrak D_{\prod_i g(N_i)}((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))/\Delta_B
\le2(3/4)^s.
\]

(b) There are thin roof bodies with
\[
\Delta_B=M/2,\qquad
\sup_{\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n,\mathbf y_0\in T}
\mathcal C(\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0)\le2^{1-s}M,
\]
but
\[
\max_Sh_S/\Delta_B
\ge2^{-s}(3/2)^{2s^2}\to\infty.
\]
The supremum includes noncore integer vectors and base points on all section boundaries.

## Proof

Use \(T=[-1,1]^s\). For every sign word
\(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^q\), \(0\le q<s\), define
\[
\ell_{\boldsymbol\sigma}^{\pm}(\mathbf y)
=\min\left(
\{1,1\pm y_{q+1}\}
\cup\{1+\sigma_jy_j:1\le j\le q\}
\right). \tag{1}
\]
These are continuous concave functions with values in \([0,1]\).
Allow \(m\) identical copies of every word coordinate, so
\(n=m(2^s-1)\).

Away from the coordinate hyperplanes, write
\(\boldsymbol\varepsilon=\operatorname{sgn}\mathbf y\).
At level \(j\), precisely the word \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{<j}\) is active, and its active side has sign \(\varepsilon_j\). Thus there are exactly \(ms\) three-site coordinates and all remaining coordinates have two sites:
\[
B=2^{ms},\quad M=2^{ms+s},\quad\Delta_B=M/2. \tag{2}
\]

For (a), set \(m=1\). Then \(F=(24/11)^s\) almost everywhere. Write
\(\mathbf c=\mathbf m-\mathbf1/2\). In orthant \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\),
\[
c_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{<j}}=\varepsilon_j/2
\quad(j=1,\ldots,s),
\]
and all other components vanish.

Every central cube lies in \([-1/2,3/2]^n\). Hence a noncore integer vector has a coordinate halfspace missing every central cube, and its certificate is zero.

For a core vector \(\mathbf J\), define recursively
\(\tau_j=2J_{\boldsymbol\tau_{<j}}-1\), and choose a normal supported on this path:
\[
u_{\boldsymbol\tau_{<j}}=\tau_j3^{j-1}.
\]
Use no base component. Because every coefficient points outward from \(\mathbf J\), the containing halfspace intersects the central cube exactly when
\(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf c(\mathbf y)\ge0\).
This is true on orthant \(\boldsymbol\tau\). If another orthant first disagrees at level \(j\), the score is
\[
\tfrac12\left(\sum_{h<j}3^{h-1}-3^{j-1}\right)<0,
\]
and all later selected path coordinates are inactive. Thus exactly one orthant is counted, independently of \(\mathbf y_0\). Since each orthant has volume one, (2) proves the first two bounds in (a).

For actual depth, reflect both integer and base coordinates about \((\mathbf1,0)\). The number of surviving integer sites over almost every \(\mathbf y\) is \(2^s\). At the root, opposite activations give overlap count two. At each later level, the active nodes for \(\mathbf y\) and \(-\mathbf y\) are distinct; their two overlap counts are two and one, giving another factor two. All other coordinates contribute one. The resulting symmetric submeasure has mass \(M\), giving depth at least \(M/2=\Delta_B\).

For alternative scalar weights, a halfspace at a Boolean vertex can contain exactly one point in every dimension. Thus \(g(2)\le1\). The total number of points in a three-site box gives \(g(3)\le3\). On these examples the product density is at most \(3^s\), and the same single-orthant halfspace gives the bound \(2(3/4)^s\).

For (b), use \(m=2s\) copies in (1). Conditional on a base orthant, the normalized anchor coordinates are independent fair choices
\(k_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon_{<j},t}\in\{0,\varepsilon_j\}\), and all inactive coordinates equal zero.

First fix a core vector \(\mathbf J\). At each recursively chosen node, at least \(s\) of its \(2s\) copies have the same value of \(J\). Choose exactly \(s\), and let \(\tau_j=1\) if that value is one and \(\tau_j=-1\) otherwise. Assign their coefficients
\[
\tau_jw_j,\qquad w_j=(s+1)^{j-1},
\]
and assign zero elsewhere. Again use no base component. The shifted threshold defining \(\mathcal C\) is zero.

On the matching orthant, every anchor is counted. At a first disagreement at level \(j\), the score is
\[
\sum_{h<j}w_hX_h-w_jX_j,
\]
where \(X_h\) is a sum of \(s\) selected fair Bernoulli choices. Since
\(s\sum_{h<j}w_h=w_j-1\), a counted anchor must have \(X_j=0\), an event of probability \(2^{-s}\).
All orthants have equal anchor mass, so the cap mass is at most
\[
M[2^{-s}+(1-2^{-s})2^{-s}]\le2^{1-s}M. \tag{3}
\]

Now consider arbitrary noncore \(\mathbf J\). If a component lies outside
\(\{-1,0,1,2\}\), a coordinate halfspace gives certificate zero.
A component equal to \(-1\) or \(2\) forces, respectively, the negative or positive extra anchor site to be attained. Each requirement imposes a signed prefix on the base orthant.

If these prefix requirements are incompatible, outward coefficients on the noncore coordinates force impossible simultaneous anchor extremes, giving zero mass. If they are compatible, let \(r\) be the longest forced prefix length. They restrict the base to a fraction \(2^{-r}\) of its orthants. All deeper coordinates are core.

Apply the preceding majority construction to the remaining subtree of depth \(s-r\). Add a common coefficient magnitude \(\Lambda\) on every noncore coordinate, outward from \(\mathbf J\), larger than the maximum possible positive subtree score. Each such coordinate contributes at most \(\Lambda\) to a threshold contribution of \(\Lambda\). Missing its required extreme loses at least \(\Lambda\), which the subtree cannot offset. Thus every counted anchor must satisfy all noncore requirements and then the subtree test.

The resulting mass is at most
\[
M2^{-r}\left[
2^{-(s-r)}+(1-2^{-(s-r)})2^{-s}
\right]\le2^{1-s}M.
\]
This includes \(r=s\). The normals have zero base component, so the estimate is independent of the target base coordinate. Incompatible requirements can become feasible on boundary sections, but those sections have zero mixed mass; the estimate therefore also covers boundary target points.

Finally, over the positive base orthant \([0,1]^s\), the all-positive path has \(ms\) coordinates containing \(\{0,1,2\}\). Fix all other integer coordinates at one. This gives an original submeasure
\[
\{0,1,2\}^{ms}\times\{\mathbf1\}^{n-ms}\times[0,1]^s
\]
of mass \(3^{ms}\), symmetric about
\((\mathbf1,\tfrac12\mathbf1)\). Hence \(H_*\ge3^{ms}/2\).
Using (2) and \(m=2s\),
\[
H_*/\Delta_B\ge
3^{ms}/2^{ms+s}
=2^{-s}(3/2)^{2s^2}\to\infty.
\]
