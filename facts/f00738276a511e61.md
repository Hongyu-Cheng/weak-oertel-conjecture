## Statement

For uniform barycentric $U$ on $\Delta_d=\{u\ge0:\sum_j u_j=1\}$ let $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$ and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/d}$. Fix $d=3$ and $c_i>0$. No global maximizer of $H_c(X)=\sum_i c_i\phi_3(X_i)$ over nonnegative column-stochastic $4\times4$ matrices has exactly one strictly positive row with differentiable capacity and a unique proper regular minimizing threshold, exactly two surviving one-zero rows with distinct holes after same-hole mergers, and one identically zero row. Under these necessary maximizing conditions an actual nonnegative zero-diagonal column-stochastic matrix has strictly greater weighted off-diagonal geometric-mean objective than $H_c(X)$.

## Proof

Use the equal-low-heights lemma under these same hypotheses, normalize the multipliers, and relabel the surviving holes onto their diagonals. The positive row is $r$ and the zero row $s$. The strictly captured full-row entries at $r,s$ are both $\alpha$, with centroid coordinates both $A<1/3$. Write $\beta,\gamma$ for the two strictly excluded full-row entries and $C,D$ for their section centroid coordinates. Define $u=1-(t-\alpha)/(\beta-\alpha)$, $v=1-(t-\alpha)/(\gamma-\alpha)$, $S=u+v$, $\rho=uv/S^2$, and $V=(1-u)(1-v)$. Then $0<u,v<1$ and $0<\rho\le1/4$.

For the two excluded coordinates $z,w$ of a uniform tetrahedral point the marginal density is $6(1-z-w)$. The cap is $z/(1-u)+w/(1-v)\le1$. Integrating the marginal over this triangle gives $F=V(1+S)$. On the section line set $z=(1-u)\tau$, $w=(1-v)(1-\tau)$, $0\le\tau\le1$. In the affine section coordinates consisting of $\tau$ and the first low coordinate, the uniform area element is constant, and the first low coordinate ranges over $[0,v+(u-v)\tau]$. Writing $L=v+(u-v)\tau$, the first moments are consequently $\int_0^1 L^2/(2\int_0^1 L)$ for each low coordinate and the $L$-weighted means of $z,w$ for the high coordinates. Evaluation gives
$$
A=\frac{S(1-\rho)}3,\quad C=\frac{(1-u)(2u+v)}{3S},\quad
D=\frac{(1-v)(u+2v)}{3S},\quad CD=\frac{V(2+\rho)}9,\quad
C-D=\frac{(u-v)(1-2S)}{3S}.
$$
Also $C+D=1-2A$. Since $C,D\le1/3$, the respective inequalities simplify to $u+S\ge1$ and $v+S\ge1$. Thus $2/3\le S<1/(1-\rho)\le4/3$, the strict upper bound coming from $A<1/3$.

Restrict the zero-diagonal support to the three active rows and put $G=27A^2$. The transformed weights $W_i=c_i^{3/2}$ on those rows, in the order $r$, hole at the $\beta$ label, hole at the $\gamma$ label, are $(\sqrt F,\sqrt{GD},\sqrt{GC})$ by the normalized weight equations. For each of the three labels put $\eta_i=W_jW_k/(2W_i)$ for the other two labels $j,k$ and $H=\sum_i\eta_i$. These are positive and $W_i=2\sqrt{\eta_j\eta_k}$. The conditions $\eta_i\le H/2$ are exactly
$$
GCD\le F(C+D),\qquad F|C-D|\le GCD .
$$
The specified cap-product bound applies to the strict split, since the other captured coordinate is $A<1/3$. It gives $F>27ACD$. Also $C+D=1-2A>A$, yielding the first inequality strictly. For the second, insert the section formulas, use $|u-v|=S\sqrt{1-4\rho}$ and cancel $V/3$. The required inequality is
$$
(1+S)(2S-1)\sqrt{1-4\rho}\le S^2(1-\rho)^2(2+\rho).
$$
The factor $(1+S)(2S-1)/S^2=2+1/S-1/S^2$ increases for $0<S<2$. At $S=1/(1-\rho)$ its value is $2+\rho-\rho^2$. The squared difference of $(1-\rho)^2(2+\rho)$ and $(2+\rho-\rho^2)\sqrt{1-4\rho}$, both nonnegative, equals $\rho^2(\rho^4+4\rho^3-15\rho^2-6\rho+28)>0$; the bracket is at least $409/16$. This proves the second inequality.

The accepted zero-diagonal support dual, applied at $d=2$ with $\lambda=\eta$ and $T=H/2$, gives the attaining matrix $Z_{ij}=(H/2-\eta_i)/\eta_j$ for $i\ne j$, $Z_{ii}=0$, with weighted value $H$. In fact, its three column sums are one, and if $g_i$ is its off-diagonal mean, $W_i g_i=H-2\eta_i$. Append the column at the unmatched zero-row label $s$ with entries $\theta_i=W_i g_i/H$ on these active rows, keep row $s$ zero, and leave the three active columns unchanged. This is an actual nonnegative zero-diagonal column-stochastic $4\times4$ matrix. Its weighted geometric-mean objective is $\sum_i c_i g_i^{2/3}\theta_i^{1/3}=H^{2/3}$.

It remains to show $H>1$. Inserting the weights and section formulas gives
$$
H=\frac{F(C+D)+GCD}{2\sqrt{FCD}}
=\frac{N}{2\sqrt{(1+S)(2+\rho)}},\qquad
N=3+(1+2\rho)S-\rho(1-\rho^2)S^2 .
$$
Let $z=1-(1-\rho)S$, so $0<z<1$. The following exact polynomial identity follows on substituting $S=(1-z)/(1-\rho)$ and expanding:
$$
\begin{gathered}
(1-\rho)^2[N^2-4(1+S)(2+\rho)]=
\sum_{i=0}^4\binom{4}{i}B_i z^i(1-z)^{4-i},\\
B_0=\rho^4,\quad B_1=\rho^2(7-4\rho)/2,\quad
B_2=[(1-4\rho)^2+18\rho^2(1-\rho)]/6,\\
B_3=(1-\rho)(1-2\rho)(1-4\rho)/2,\qquad B_4=(1-\rho)^2(1-4\rho).
\end{gathered}
$$
All coefficients are nonnegative for $0<\rho\le1/4$, and the $i=0$ term is positive. Since $H=\sum_i\eta_i>0$, this proves $H>1$ and the asserted strict improvement over $H_c(X)=1$. To pass from geometric means to row capacities for the competitor, a row with positive off-diagonal entries and one zero has capacity equal to their geometric mean: projecting away the zero coordinate gives the positive orthant threshold tetrahedron of relative volume $t^3/\prod_{j\ne i}Y_{ij}$, which contains the actual cap and agrees with it for all sufficiently small positive $t$. If any off-diagonal entry is zero the geometric mean is zero and capacity is nonnegative. Thus the actual capacity objective is at least the strict competitor value. Undoing the common positive weight normalization preserves strictness.
