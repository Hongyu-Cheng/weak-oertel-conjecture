## Statement

If $D$ is an $m$-dimensional compact convex set, $m\ge1$, and $F:D\to[0,\infty)$ is concave with $F(x_0)\ge c>0$ at some $x_0\in D$, then
$$
|\{F<t\}|_m\le [1-(1-t/c)^m]|D|_m\le (m|D|_m/c)t\quad(0<t<c).
$$
In particular, for $B\ge0$ with $e^{-B}<c$, the integral of $(-\log F)_+$ over $\{(-\log F)_+>B\}$ is at most $(m|D|_m/c)(B+1)e^{-B}$.

Let full-dimensional compact convex $K_j\subset\mathbb R^d$ in a common ball converge in Hausdorff distance to full-dimensional $K$. Let $L_j$ be positive concave on $K_j$, with uniform convergence on the common compact range to a continuous $L$ that is nonnegative on $K$ and positive on its interior. Put $\Pi_j=\{u_j\cdot X=t_j\}$, with $(u_j,t_j)\to(u,t)$ and $|u_j|\ge b_*>0$, $D_j=K_j\cap\Pi_j$, and $0\le\lambda_j\le |u_j|^{-1}\mathcal H^{d-1}|_{D_j}$. Take a subsequence with $\lambda_j\rightharpoonup\lambda$ and $(-\log L_j)_+\lambda_j\rightharpoonup\mu$ finite. The section-log defect is $s=\mu|_{\{L=0\}}$. Uniformly positive section anchors imply $s=0$ and $\mu=(-\log L)_+\lambda$, as does a limiting plane $\Pi=\{u\cdot X=t\}$ meeting the interior of $K$. If $s\ne0$, every retained Hausdorff limit of the nonempty $D_j$ is contained in $\{L=0\}\cap K$.

If additionally $L$ on $K$ is a finite minimum of affine functions and $\sup_j\int_{K_j}1/L_j<\infty$, then each exposed affine zero face has dimension at most $d-2$ for $d\ge2$. A nonzero $s$ then forces $\mathcal H^{d-1}(D_j)\to0$ and $\lambda_j(D_j)\to0$ along its retained subsequence. For $d=1$ the section measure uses counting measure, a uniformly positive anchor suffices directly, and the finite-affine and inverse-integral hypotheses imply that $L$ has no zero in $K$.

## Proof

The homothetic copy $(t/c)x_0+(1-t/c)D$ lies in $\{F\ge t\}$ by concavity and nonnegativity. Its volume gives the first inequality, and Bernoulli's inequality gives the second. For $\ell=(-\log F)_+$ the tail integral is $B|\{\ell>B\}|_m+\int_B^\infty|\{\ell>s\}|_m\,ds$ by layer cake. Substitution of the sublevel bound proves the stated estimate.

For sections of full intrinsic dimension $m=d-1$, the outer radius bounds $|D_j|_m$ uniformly. Lower-dimensional sections have zero coarea. A uniform anchor therefore makes the tails uniformly small also for the allocated $\lambda_j$, since $|u_j|^{-1}\le b_*^{-1}$. The truncated weights $\min((-\log L_j)_+,B)$ converge uniformly as functions of $L_j$ using the continuous extension of the truncation by $B$ at nonpositive inputs. Thus the weak log limit equals $(-\log L)_+\lambda$, and bounded total mass implies $\lambda(\{L=0\})=0$ by testing each truncation. In dimension one each nonempty section is a point and the same conclusion follows directly from the anchor bound.

If $X$ lies in $\Pi\cap\operatorname{int}K$, a small ball about $X$ is contained in every sufficiently late $K_j$ by Hausdorff convergence of convex bodies. The orthogonal projections of $X$ onto $\Pi_j$ converge to $X$ and eventually lie in those balls, so $L_j$ there is bounded below by a positive constant. For any Hausdorff limit $D$ of nonempty $D_j$, a point $X\in D$ with $L(X)>0$ similarly gives uniform anchors along that subsequence. Since all retained subsequences have the same weighted weak limit, nonzero $s$ excludes such an $X$. If there were infinitely many empty sections, their zero measures would also force $s=0$.

For the dimension assertion, Fatou on $\operatorname{int}K$ gives $\int_K1/L<\infty$, as interior points eventually belong to $K_j$. Each limiting affine component is nonnegative on $K$ and is positive on its interior. If one zero face were a facet, take a positive relative-area patch in the facet and the cone joining it to an interior point. At interpolation distance $v$ from the patch the component is $v$ times its value at the interior point, and the section area tends to the patch area as $v\downarrow0$. The integral of the reciprocal of that component diverges like $\int_0^\epsilon dv/v$, so the inequality $L\le A$ for that component $A$ contradicts integrability. In $d=1$ the same argument at an endpoint uses the interval integral. The finite union of the exposed affine zero faces has no $(d-1)$-dimensional subset with positive area. If $s\ne0$, any Hausdorff limit of the convex sections lies in this union and has dimension at most $d-2$. Identify the moving hyperplanes with the limiting one by convergent rigid motions. Each subsequence has a further Hausdorff-convergent subsequence, and its sections eventually lie in an intrinsic slab of vanishing width and bounded diameter. This gives vanishing full area along the retained sequence and hence vanishing allocated coarea.
