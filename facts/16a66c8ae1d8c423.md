## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite set of at least two points in its affine span with arithmetic mean $0\in P$, and let $K$ be a centroid-zero $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$. Take the sum of uniform fiber volumes on $P\times K$, write $M=|P||K|$ and $\gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, and suppose its maximum normalized ordinary ambient depth equals $\gamma$. Then $(0,0)$ is its unique deepest point. Every other sampled plane has a finite cover by old nonvertical closed caps, each of mass strictly below $Q=\gamma M$. The maximum-depth hypothesis holds in particular for centrally symmetric $P$ about zero.

## Proof

Let $\lambda_j=1/(d+1)+l_j$ be the barycentric coordinates on $K$, with $l_j$ linear and $\sum_j l_j=0$. A cap $\lambda_j\ge t$ in $K$ has volume $|K|(1-t)^d$ for $0\le t\le1$, by homothety about vertex $j$. At $(p,x)$ with $x\ne0$, some $\lambda_j(x)>1/(d+1)$, so its horizontal cap is strictly below $Q$. At $(p,0)$ with $p\ne0$, choose a linear functional $a$ with $a(p)>0$. For small positive $t$ the cap $l_j(x)+t a(q-p)\ge0$ through that mark has proper cuts in every fiber $q\in P$. Writing $\alpha=d/(d+1)$, its mass is $|K|\sum_q(\alpha+t a(q-p))^d$, whose derivative at zero is $-d|K|\alpha^{d-1}|P|a(p)<0$ by the mean-zero condition. Thus all points other than $(0,0)$ have raw depth strictly below $Q$. By 1ec728c26c11b368 the depth is continuous on the compact fibers and attains its maximum. Hence only $(0,0)$ is deepest and the maximum on each other fiber is strictly below $Q$. Compatibility of $P\times K$ follows because every anchored combination of copies of $K$ equals $K$. Applying 755cb08c379113cc gives finite whole-plane covers with that local budget.

For centrally symmetric $P$, a through-origin nonvertical cap has shifts paired at $q,-q$. The own centered continuous marginal satisfies the paired-tail inequality of 6a7d56e450b3126f, including at shift zero, so the total is at least $\gamma M$. A pure-base cap through zero includes at least half of the uniform bases, and $\gamma\le1/2$ by $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$. Moving a containing cap to pass through the origin can only reduce mass. The horizontal canonical caps give a universal upper bound $\gamma$ on normalized depth since the largest barycentric coordinate at any mark is at least $1/(d+1)$. This proves the example hypothesis.
