## Statement

Let \(r,s,h_x,h_y\) be arbitrary positive integers, let \(n=r+s\), and let \(a\ge0\). Write lattice indices as \(\mathbf I=(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb Z^s\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,h_x]^r\cup\{-\mathbf1_r\}\bigr),
&Q&=[-h_y,h_y]^s,\\
P&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((R\times\{\mathbf0\})\cup(\{\mathbf0\}\times Q)\bigr),
&D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(V:R\to\mathbb R\) be any finite convex function with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\). This is an auxiliary potential on the first block. Define the whole-table potential, original weights, and total mass by

\[
\phi_{(x,y)}=V(x)+a\|y\|_\infty,
\qquad
w_{(x,y)}=e^{-\phi_{(x,y)}}\quad((x,y)\in D),
\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),
\qquad
W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}.
\]

Assume that the original weighted mean is \(\mathbf J_0=W^{-1}\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). All weights in this hypothesis and the conclusions are the displayed original weights.

Use the full original source capacities and all-support logarithmic hitting definition of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, a barycentric support at \(\mathbf0\) is a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) with positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) satisfying \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). Its gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}\). For \(L\ge0\), \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)\) is the minimum \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over subsets \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every such support whose gap is at most \(L\). Each selected source is charged its full original weight. The cited fact proves equivalence with its finite definition using affinely independent supports, and attainment of the cutoff maximum. The cutoff inequality is weak, and its equivalent closed lifted-cap formulation includes every equality atom.

The notation \(n,D,P,w_{\mathbf I},W\) has the same meaning as in `488f0a583886b5fc`. Our normalization is \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\): that fact's normalized potential is \(\phi-\min_D\phi\), and its \(w_{\max}\) is \(e^{-\min_D\phi}\). These substitutions give exactly the same gaps and original weights.

The exact complete support is

\[
D=\{(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}
\cup
\left\{(x,y):
x\in\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r,\quad y\in\mathbb Z^s,\quad
\frac{\|x\|_\infty}{h_x}+\frac{\|y\|_\infty}{h_y}\le1
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

The table is globally extendable, has affine rank \(n\), and has \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\) and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Write
\(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}w_{(\mathbf0,y)}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}\).
Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&\le 2^{r+1}Z_{\mathrm{cube}},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)&\ge \frac{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}{4},\\
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)}{1+L}
&\ge\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}
\ge\frac{W}{20\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

For the two-site set \(F=\{(\mathbf0,\mathbf0),(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\}\), the minimum mean potential over barycentric laws at zero supported in \(D\setminus F\) is exactly \(a\). This is an attained retained roof gap, since \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\). In particular, when \(a>0\), this deletion hits every support of gap below \(a\), but not every support at the closed cutoff \(L=a\). For every choice of \(r,s,h_x,h_y,a\), an explicit finite convex \(V\) satisfying the original stationarity hypothesis exists.

The actual-body consequence uses the same integer dimension \(n=r+s\) and any continuous dimension \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, put \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{z\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,z)\in C\}\), and suppose that for some \(\alpha>0\) its complete fiber-volume table satisfies

\[
w^C_{\mathbf I}:=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})=\alpha w_{\mathbf I}
\quad\text{for every }\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]

Thus this equality includes all zero-volume lattice fibers. Set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(W_C=\sum_{\mathbf I}w^C_{\mathbf I}=\alpha W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). There exists \(z^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) such that

\[
h_S((\mathbf0,z^\star))
\ge \frac{W_C}{120\,2^{r+s}}
=\frac{\alpha W}{120\,2^{r+s}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(h_S\) has the locked closed-halfspace definition. The point is selected in the original fiber over \(\mathbf0\) before the arbitrary containing closed ambient halfspace. The positive scalar multiple leaves the original mean zero. In applying accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, its weights and total mass are \(w^C\) and \(W_C\), its target \(\mathbf J\) is \(\mathbf0\), and its continuous dimension \(d\) is exactly this actual body's continuous dimension, independently of both discrete block dimensions \(r,s\). Its volume roots \(a_{\mathbf I}\) are \((w^C_{\mathbf I})^{1/d}\); they are distinct from the scalar potential coefficient \(a\) above. The notation \(m_d(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\) below is that predecessor's \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\), with the actual table argument made explicit.

## Proof

Represent a point of \(R\) as \(t u-(1-t)\mathbf1_r\), with \(0\le t\le1\) and \(u\in[0,h_x]^r\). Each coordinate is at least \(t-1\) and at most \((h_x+1)t-1\). A negative integer coordinate forces \(t=0\), so the whole point is \(-\mathbf1_r\). All other integer points of \(R\) lie in the nonnegative integer cube, and that whole cube is present. Hence
\(R\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r\).

Every point of \(P\) has a representation \((x,y)=(\lambda u,(1-\lambda)v)\), where \(u\in R\), \(v\in Q\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Its projection \(x\) belongs to \(R\), since \(\mathbf0\in R\). If the integer projection is \(-\mathbf1_r\), the coordinate lower bound \(x_i\ge-\lambda\) forces \(\lambda=1\) and \(y=\mathbf0\). Otherwise \(x\) is nonnegative, and the inequalities \(x_i\le\lambda h_x\) and \(\|y\|_\infty\le(1-\lambda)h_y\) give the inequality in (1). Conversely, if that inequality holds, set \(\lambda=\|x\|_\infty/h_x\). For \(0<\lambda<1\), the vectors \(x/\lambda\in[0,h_x]^r\) and \(y/(1-\lambda)\in Q\) give the required convex representation. The cases \(\lambda=0\) and \(\lambda=1\) lie in the two defining blocks directly. This proves (1), including every boundary and mixed lattice site.

The function equal to \(\exp(-V(x)-a\|y\|_\infty)\) on \(P\) and zero outside \(P\) is a finite-valued global log-concave extension. When both endpoints lie in \(P\), the extension inequality follows from convexity of its potential and of \(P\); if either endpoint lies outside \(P\), the right side is zero. By (1), it agrees with every lattice value, including all zeros. The origin and the coordinate unit vectors in both blocks belong to \(P\), so the affine rank is \(n\). The origin is a strictly positive barycenter of the affinely independent points \(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\) in \(R\), and hence is in its interior. It is also in the interior of \(Q\). Since \((R/2)\times(Q/2)\subseteq P\), the origin is in the interior of \(P\). In particular, the prescribed integer mean is an allowed site, and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Convexity of the displayed potential gives every required convex-admissibility inequality.

For the deletion assertion, every site retained after deleting \(F\) has a nonnegative first block. A barycentric law at the origin using only retained sites must therefore put all its mass on sites with \(x=\mathbf0\). Those retained sites have \(y\ne\mathbf0\), hence potential \(a\|y\|_\infty\ge a\). The pair \((\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1),(\mathbf0,-\mathbf e_1)\), with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), is present by (1) and attains mean potential exactly \(a\). This proves the retained roof statement. It also proves both hitting assertions, with the pair eligible at equality \(L=a\). Although opposite pairs of outer cube vertices have gap \(a h_y\), the nearer forced pair already has gap \(a\).

We next prove the first inequality in (2) directly from the original mean. On the countable set \(\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\), define an auxiliary probability by
\(\rho_x=2^{-(r+1)}2^{-(x_1+\cdots+x_r)}\).
Its mass at \(-\mathbf1_r\) is \(1/2\), and its total mass on the nonnegative orthant is
\(2^{-(r+1)}(\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j})^r=1/2\), so it is normalized exactly. On the product of this set with \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), define the auxiliary probability
\(q_{(x,y)}=\rho_x e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}/Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\).
The support census (1) places every original site in this product. These auxiliary probabilities are used only in the following log-sum inequality; source capacities remain \(w\).

Let \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) be the original law. Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives
\(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(q_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\le\log\sum_Dq_{\mathbf I}\le0\).
Substitution, together with the original equality \(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x=\mathbf0\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le \sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\log\frac{p_{(x,y)}}{q_{(x,y)}}\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
 +(r+1)\log2+\log Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Convexity of \(V\) and that same original stationarity give
\(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)\ge V(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x)=V(\mathbf0)=0\).
Consequently \(\log W\le(r+1)\log2+\log Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\), proving \(W\le2^{r+1}Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\). This argument assumes neither log-concavity of a marginal nor an energy-to-original-cap comparison. It evaluates both inequalities at the original law \(w/W\).

For every \(L\ge0\), the singleton at the origin and the mutually disjoint opposite pairs in the central cube with \(a\|y\|_\infty\le L\) give

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
\ge
1+\frac12
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s,\ y\ne\mathbf0\\a\|y\|_\infty\le L}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}.
\tag{4}
\]

Each pair is an affinely independent barycentric support of gap \(a\|y\|_\infty\), and its two original capacities are equal. A hitting set must pay at least one complete atom weight for each such pair. The singleton is disjoint from all of them. These supports are among the full family in the definition, so (4) also holds when every arbitrary barycentric support is included. The weak cutoff inequality retains equality cases.

An elementary map bounds the cube mass outside a fixed cutoff. For \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), set
\(\eta(y)_j=\operatorname{sgn}(y_j)\lfloor |y_j|/2\rfloor\).
Its image is still in the complete cube, \(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty\le\|y\|_\infty/2\), and every image has at most \(3^s\) preimages. In one coordinate the zero image has only the three possible preimages \(-1,0,1\); a positive or negative image has at most two, and truncation at the cube boundary only reduces that count. Therefore

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}
\le
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|\eta(y)\|_\infty}
\le3^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\]

At the cutoff \(L=4s\), it follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\\a\|y\|_\infty>4s}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}
&\le e^{-2s}
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}\\
&\le(3e^{-2})^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}
<\frac12Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The elementary inequality \(e^2>1+2+2+8/6>6\) justifies the last bound for every \(s\ge1\). When \(a=0\), the tail is empty and the same conclusion holds. Thus at least half the central-cube mass has pair gap at most \(4s\). By (4), \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)\ge Z_{\mathrm{cube}}/4\). Combining the two estimates gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)}{1+L}
&\ge\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}\\
&\ge\frac{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}{4(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{W}{2^{r+3}(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{W}{20\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last inequality, \(1+4s\le5\cdot2^{s-1}\). This holds with equality at \(s=1\); its induction step follows because the right side increases by at least five while the left side increases by four. This proves (2) with a specified cutoff and the complete original table.

We verify nonvacuity of original stationarity for every parameter choice by finite formulas. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac1{2h_x(h_x+1)^r(2h_y+1)^s},\\
c_-&=
\sum_{\substack{(x,y)\in D\\x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}}
x_1u^{x_1+\cdots+x_r}e^{-a\|y\|_\infty},
&\delta&=-\log(c_-u^r),\\
V(x)&=-(\log u)\sum_{i=1}^r x_i
+\delta\max\{0,-x_1,\ldots,-x_r\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

One has \(0<u<1\), and the term at \((\mathbf e_1,\mathbf0)\) makes \(c_->0\). Every nonzero term contributing to \(c_-\) has \(x_1\le h_x\), \(u^{x_1+\cdots+x_r}\le u\), and \(e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}\le1\). There are at most \((h_x+1)^r(2h_y+1)^s\) sites. Consequently \(c_-\le h_x(h_x+1)^r(2h_y+1)^s u=1/2\), so \(\delta>0\). The displayed \(V\) is a finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^r\), with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\).

At every nonnegative projected lattice site, its original weight is \(u^{x_1+\cdots+x_r}e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}\). At the unique negative projected site \((-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf0)\), the value \(V(-\mathbf1_r)=r\log u+\delta=-\log c_-\) gives weight exactly \(c_-\). The nonnegative part of (1) and these weights are invariant under permutations of the first-block coordinates. Its original weighted first moment is therefore \(c_-\) in every such coordinate, and the negative site cancels all those moments. Independent sign changes of the second-block coordinates preserve the whole table, so its original second-block moments vanish as well. Hence \(\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\) exactly. These finite formulas define the original table and prove its stationarity directly.

Finally, consider an actual body satisfying the fiber-volume hypothesis in the statement. Since \(\alpha>0\), its positive-volume support is exactly \(D\), its total mixed volume is \(W_C=\alpha W\), and its original normalized source law is \(w^C/W_C=w/W\). Its original mean therefore remains \(\mathbf0\). The target fiber has volume \(w^C_{\mathbf0}=\alpha>0\).

The common scaling leaves all logarithmic gaps unchanged: the normalized potential for the actual table in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` is
\(\log(w^C_{\max}/w^C_{\mathbf I})=\log(\alpha w_{\max}/(\alpha w_{\mathbf I}))=\phi_{\mathbf I}-\min_D\phi\).
Every possible hitting set has its weight multiplied by \(\alpha\). Consequently, at every cutoff,

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)=\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w).
\tag{5}
\]

For an affinely independent barycentric support \(E\) with its unique positive coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\), the root cost for this actual body's continuous dimension \(d\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_E^C
&=\log\frac{w^C_{\mathbf0}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}(w^C_{\mathbf I})^{1/d}\right)^d}\\
&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf0}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The cancellation is exact, since the denominator acquires the same factor \(\alpha\) as the numerator. The pointwise root-versus-logarithmic comparison in `488f0a583886b5fc`, applied with actual weights \(w^C\) and actual continuous dimension \(d\), states
\(m_d(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\).
Equivalently, every logarithmic eligible support is root eligible at the same cutoff, while the capacities are unchanged. This comparison includes weak cutoff inequalities.

The pointwise original-fiber conclusion (4) of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, with the exact notation map stated above, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{z\in C_{\mathbf0}}h_S((\mathbf0,z))
&\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf0,4s;w^C)}{6(1+4s)}\\
&\ge\frac{\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{6(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{\alpha W}{120\,2^{r+s}}
=\frac{W_C}{120\,2^{r+s}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last inequality is the specified-cutoff bound in (2). That accepted fact also proves attainment of the original-fiber depth maximum, so one point \(z^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) realizes the bound before the arbitrary closed halfspace is chosen. Its conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and all equality atoms. This proves (3).
