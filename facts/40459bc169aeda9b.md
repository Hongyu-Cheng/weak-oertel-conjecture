## Statement

For every $0<\varepsilon\le1/100$, there is a bounded full-dimensional source body in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2$ of mixed area $M=303/2500$ with six positive integer fibers and a four-group closed-cap certificate. The groups have rank-two positive circuits with three caps each and share a bounded full-dimensional mixed-lattice-free complementary polytope. Each group has an attained own contact, where all other groups' margins are at most $-9/100$. Expanded offsets have the four binary sites as strict exclusive witnesses and no interior integer point. Their own projected witness gaps are $\varepsilon$, their cross-witness slacks are $1/10$ or $1/5$, and every projected contact is at Euclidean distance $5\sqrt2\,\varepsilon$ from its witness.

For the root $(1,1)$ under the parity midpoint map, its group covers the whole basin, of mixed area $3/25$. In each group the three cap masses are $103/2500,100/2500,100/2500$, strictly below the common budget $104/2500$. The deficit from the proposed $\gamma_2$ basin mass bound is $(4/9)(3/25)-103/2500=91/7500$. Thus a bound of the form $\max_j\mu(H_{11,j})\ge \gamma_2\mu(\text{basin}_{11})-Mf(t)$, where $t$ is the largest own gap divided by the least cross-witness slack, would require $f(10\varepsilon)\ge91/909$ for every $0<\varepsilon\le1/100$. An error vanishing with projected-contact displacement alone fails for the same family. Normalizing the continuous normals by their support widths on the integer-fiber difference body preserves the ratio and the deficit.

## Proof

Use the actual six-fiber body of accepted fact ff8dd8bb1c333bbf. Its bases are $J=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)\}$. The squares have affine centers $b(z)=(11-4z_1-4z_2,4z_1-5)$ and infinity-norm radii $1/10$ at $(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)$ and $1/100$ elsewhere in $J$. Their hull has exactly these integer fibers by that fact; the three large squares are precisely the root-$(1,1)$ basin, since $z\mapsto\lceil z/2\rceil$ fixes the four binary sites and sends both nonroots to $(1,1)$. The common normal list is $u_1=(1,0),u_2=(0,1),u_3=(-1,-1)$, with normalized weights $1/3$.

Write $S_z=z_1+z_2$ and $D_z=z_1-z_2$. In groups $00,10,01,11$ respectively, take all three margins $u_j\cdot x+\ell_s(z)$, with
$$
(\ell_{00},\ell_{10},\ell_{01},\ell_{11})
=(-S_z/10,\ (D_z-1)/10,\ (-D_z-1)/10,\ (S_z-2)/10)
+\varepsilon(1,1,1,1).
$$
Writing $\ell_s(z)=a_s\cdot z-h_s$, the expanded offsets are $\beta_s=0,1/10,1/10,1/5$. Every binary witness $p_s$ gives $a_s\cdot p_s=\beta_s$, and direct evaluation gives $\beta_t-a_t\cdot p_s\in\{1/10,1/5\}$ for $s\ne t$. All own gaps are $\beta_s-h_s=\varepsilon$. The expanded region has $0\le S_z\le2$ and $|D_z|\le1$. An interior integer base would have $S_z=1$ and hence odd $D_z$, impossible.

The complementary polytope of all twelve weak reversed inequalities has projection
$10\varepsilon\le S_z\le2-10\varepsilon$, $|D_z|\le1-10\varepsilon$: the circuit sums are necessary, and $x=0$ supplies sufficiency. An integer base would again have sum one and odd difference, impossible. The projection is bounded, and the three normals positively span the continuous plane, so the continuous coordinates are uniformly bounded over it. The point $z=(1/2,1/2),x=0$ satisfies every reversed inequality strictly. Set $r_s=(1-10\varepsilon)p_s+5\varepsilon(1,1)$. At $(r_s,0)$ the own margins are zero, and the other margins are $-1/10+\varepsilon$ or $-1/5+2\varepsilon$ by substitution, hence at most $-9/100$. The base displacement is $5\sqrt2\,\varepsilon$. These contacts are outside the source body: its continuous points over any $r_s$ have second coordinate within $1/10$ of $4(r_s)_1-5\le-1$, since all generating centers are affine and their radii at most $1/10$.

On $J$ all $\ell_s$ lie in $(-3/10,11/100]$. At the three large squares the respective intended normal $u_1,u_2,u_3$ is at least $18/10$ throughout the square, and all unintended normals are at most $-9/10$, from their centers $(3,-1),(-1,3),(-1,-1)$ and radii. At the three small centers $(11,-5),(7,-1),(7,-5)$ the first normal is at least $7-1/100$ on the squares and the other two are at most $-1+2/100$. Thus adding the common terms preserves strict exclusive cap colors, with cap 1 containing one large and three small squares and caps 2 and 3 each containing one large square. This proves the masses, coverage of the basin, deficit, and inequality for $f$ after division by $M$.

The integer-fiber difference body is $D=[-1/5,1/5]^2$. Its support values on the normals are $1/5,1/5,2/5$. Divide each entire cap margin by its support value and use the new normalized circuit weights $1/4,1/4,1/2$. The weighted projected margin becomes $(15/4)\ell_s$, so the projected offsets and both types of witness slacks scale by the same factor. All caps and their masses are unchanged.
