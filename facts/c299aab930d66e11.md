## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, $b=\varepsilon^2$, $a=1+b$, and $\varepsilon/2\le s\le\varepsilon$. Consider the body on $0\le z\le2$ given by
$$
\begin{gathered}
x,y\le 1-(1-b)z/2,\qquad
x,y\ge\max\{-(a-s)z/2,-(a-s)(2-z)/2\},\\
s(1-z/2)\le x+y\le\min\{1+bz,2+b-z\}.
\end{gathered}
$$
It is bounded and full dimensional. Its actual fibers at $0,1,2$ are $A_s=\{x,y\ge0:s\le x+y\le1\}$, $P_s/2$ for $P_s=\{x,y\le a:x+y\ge s\}$, and $B=bT$. The nine shared plane inequalities have fixed nonzero continuous normals. Decreasing $s$ relaxes the five lower inequalities and preserves the other four.

If additionally $\varepsilon$ is smaller than the dimension-two radius in d2f95e21650e61cb, base 0 is the unique base attaining the global raw maximum. That maximum is $f(s)=(1+\sqrt{1+3s^2})/9-s^2/3$, at the unique mark with continuous coordinates both $(1+\sqrt{1+3s^2})/6$. For $M(s)=(1-s^2)/2+(2a-s)^2/8+b^2/2$, the global normalized maximum $f(s)/M(s)$ decreases along the shared-plane fill $ds/dt=-1$, with derivative at most $-1/[60M(s)^2]<-1/240$ throughout the interval.

## Proof

For $z=0$ the individual lower bounds give nonnegativity and the sum inequalities give $s\le x+y\le1$, which already imply the individual upper bounds. At $z=1$, the individual upper bounds $x,y\le a/2$ and sum lower bound $x+y\ge s/2$ imply the extra individual lower bounds and $x+y\le a$. At $z=2$ the nonnegative individual bounds and $x+y\le b$ give precisely $bT$. The $z$-range and individual bounds give compactness; the point with $z=1$ and $x=y=a/4$ satisfies every inequality strictly. The coefficients of $s$ on the right sides of the four individual lower inequalities and one sum lower inequality are $z/2,(2-z)/2$, or $1-z/2$, all nonnegative. Thus the motion is feasible throughout the full real height interval. The continuous coefficients of all nine inequalities are fixed, as claimed.

At the exposed base 0, 2acc8bc803acf1c0 identifies mixed and ordinary depth pointwise. Apply d2f95e21650e61cb with the single truncation of size $s$ at vertex zero in the triangle $T$. The deleted fraction is $s^2$, and ordinary raw depth is the accepted relative value multiplied by $|T|=1/2$, giving $f(s)$ and the stated mark. At base 2, isolation and the ordinary triangle bound give a raw maximum at most $2b^2/9$. For every point of the middle triangle $P_s/2$, one barycentric coordinate is at least $1/3$, so there is an apex-side cap through that point of area at most $4|P_s/2|/9$. A finite tilt preserving the middle trace excludes all of $A_s$ and pays at most the full area of $B$. Therefore its restricted raw maximum is at most
$$
U(s)=(2a-s)^2/18+b^2/2 .
$$
Both triangle-area formulas follow from the edge length in coordinate directions, $a-s/2$ for $P_s/2$ and $b$ for $B$.

Since $\sqrt{1+3s^2}\ge1$, we have
$$
f(s)-U(s)\ge 2s/9-7s^2/18-4b/9-13b^2/18
\ge\varepsilon/9-5\varepsilon^2/6-13\varepsilon^4/18
\ge\varepsilon/9-\varepsilon^2\ge7\varepsilon/144>0.
$$
Also $f(s)>1/5>2b^2/9$. Thus $f(s)$ is the exact global raw maximum with a unique active base. Adding the three fiber areas gives $M(s)$.

For $ds/dt=-1$, direct differentiation gives $df/dt=(s/3)[2-(1+3s^2)^{-1/2}]\le2s/3$ and $dM/dt=(2a+3s)/4\ge1/2$. Here $f(s)>1/5$ and $M(s)<2$, so the numerator of the normalized derivative is at most $4s/3-1/10\le-1/60$. Dividing by $M(s)^2$ proves the uniform strict bound.
