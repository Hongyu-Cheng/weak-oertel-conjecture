## Statement

Let a positive-area polytope facet $F$ coincide with the boundary of a cap, and use physical normal coordinate zero at the old facet with positive direction the outward unit normal. Under an affine body velocity with outward speed $\psi$ on $F$, the signed coincident-facet contribution to the right cap derivative at $t=0$ is $\int_F\min(\psi,-s)\,dS$ for an inside cap whose boundary moves to height $-ts$, and $\int_F(\psi-s)_+\,dS$ for an outside cap whose boundary moves to height $ts$. These expressions need not be finite piecewise-linear in the affine velocity and threshold coefficients. The same formulas apply after the quadratic actual hull repair for compatible common-normal simplex jets.

## Proof

Take $t\downarrow0$ and use the original outward unit facet normal and take its plane as signed normal coordinate zero. At a point in the relative interior of $F$, the moved body facet has normal height $t\psi(x)+O(t^2)$ in the old normal line through $x$, by the affine image formula and boundedness. For an inside coincidence the cap contains the inward side and its moved boundary is at height $-ts$. The captured region has outer height $\min(t\psi(x)+O(t^2),-ts)$, which gives the first displayed integral. For an outside coincidence the captured layer, if any, is between height $ts$ and the moved body height, yielding the positive part in the second integral. Almost every point in the facet projection is in the relative interior. A fixed tubular layer has thickness $O(t)$ uniformly, so division by $t$ and dominated convergence gives the formulas. Contributions near other facets' intersections with this face have zero limiting footprint and therefore do not affect the coincident terms. The uniform quadratic symmetric-difference estimate in regular-affine-simplex-cover-response-and-labeled-stress also transfers these formulas to its repaired paths; regularity of the chosen row is unnecessary for that estimate.

For example on a unit-length planar facet let $\psi(x)=x$ with $x$ the coordinate in $[0,1]$, a speed supplied by an affine field. The outside contribution for $0<s<1$ is $\int_s^1(x-s)\,dx=(1-s)^2/2$. The inside contribution for $-1<s<0$ is $\int_0^{-s}x\,dx+\int_{-s}^1(-s)\,dx=-s-s^2/2$. Neither restriction is piecewise linear on finitely many pieces. On a fixed side branch or when a linear allocation is supplied, the same selected local factor necessarily multiplies both terms of the expression being allocated: for the outside it multiplies $\psi-s$, and for the inside the mixture is $\alpha\psi+(1-\alpha)(-s)$. This computation does not supply such a choice or an interchange of circuit and allocation quantifiers.
