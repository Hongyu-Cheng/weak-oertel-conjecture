## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(k\ge2\) be an integer, let \(A,B\) partition \(\{1,\ldots,2k\}\) with \(|A|=|B|=k\), and use exactly the support and row notation of accepted fact `55b5ec7cf9276f99`:
\[
J_k=\left\{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2k}:
\sum_i n_i=2k,\quad k-1\le\sum_{i\in A}n_i\le k+1\right\},
\qquad S_A=\sum_{i\in A}N_i.
\]
For \(\mathbf j\in J_k\), \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is its minimal face, \(J_{\mathbf j}=J_k\cap F_{\mathbf j}\), \(I=\{i:j_i>0\}\), and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is the unique maximum-entropy law of mean \(\mathbf j\). Write \(H_J(\mathbf j):=H(\nu_{\mathbf j})\), where \(H(\nu)=-\sum_{\mathbf n:\nu(\mathbf n)>0}\nu(\mathbf n)\log\nu(\mathbf n)\).

For arbitrary positive source count weights \((q_i)_{i=1}^{2k}\), put
\[
p(\mathbf n)=\frac{\prod_iq_i^{n_i}}{Z},\qquad
Z=\sum_{\mathbf n\in J_k}\prod_iq_i^{n_i},\qquad
C_{\mathbf j}=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=p(\mathbf j)e^{H_J(\mathbf j)},
\]
\[
B_2=\mathcal B_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf j\in J_k}p(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^{2},\qquad
\omega_{\mathbf j}=\frac{p(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^{2}}{B_2},\qquad
V_{\mathbf j}=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)}{p(\mathbf N)}.
\]
Here \(\mathsf C_p\) and \(\mathcal B_2\) have exactly the normalization of accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374`. More precisely, delete count \(2k\) and let \(\pi(\mathbf n)=(n_1,\ldots,n_{2k-1})\), \(E=\pi(J_k)\), and \(R=2k-1\). The aliases to that fact are its support \(A=E\), ambient dimension \(m=R\), source law equal to the pushforward of \(p\), tilt \(\ell_i=\log(q_i/q_{2k})\) for \(1\le i\le R\), and \(r_{E,\pi(\mathbf j)}\) equal to the pushforward of \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\). The capacities displayed above are evaluated in this chart. The symbols \(C_{\mathbf j},B_2,\omega_{\mathbf j},V_{\mathbf j}\) have the same meanings as in accepted fact `81ad175d3c410508`, with its simplex support replaced by \(E\).

The source \(q_i\) are exponential count weights in the sense of accepted fact `128175cd66587a67`, not the face-conditioning probabilities in `bf1ff94487296374`. In the proof, \(w_i\) denote entropy-row count weights: these are the exact aliases of the \(q_i\) in the row representation of `55b5ec7cf9276f99`. Its composition partition sums \(h_T\) are those of `128175cd66587a67`, without multinomial coefficients, and are unrelated to the locked problem's halfspace-depth function \(h_S\).

There is one absolute constant \(C\), independent of \(k\), the source weights, and the root, such that
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\mathbf j\in J_k}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}\le C R.}
\]
One admissible choice is
\[
C_H=\log3+\frac43(\log7+\log2),\qquad
D_{\mathrm{num}}=256e^{2C_H},\qquad
C=90+\frac{704}{3}e^2+\frac{32}{27}\bigl((\log D_{\mathrm{num}})^2+2\bigr).
\]
All roots on proper faces are included. The chart \(E\) is complete in \(\mathbb Z^R\), and its affine lattice is saturated.

## Proof

The convex-hull description in `55b5ec7cf9276f99` identifies \(\operatorname{conv}J_k\) with the real nonnegative-count polytope given by the displayed total and band constraints. The all-one vector has strictly positive coordinates and strict band inequalities, so its affine hull is precisely \(\sum_i x_i=2k\), of dimension \(R=2k-1\). Deleting the last count bijects this affine lattice onto \(\mathbb Z^R\); the inverse restores that count as \(2k-\sum_{i=1}^{R}x_i\). This proves saturation. The integer points satisfying the real constraints are exactly \(J_k\), so \(E=\operatorname{conv}E\cap\mathbb Z^R\).

Log-affinity gives, for every mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\), \(D(\nu\|p)=-H(\nu)-\log p(\mathbf j)\). Thus the relative-entropy minimizer in `bf1ff94487296374` is \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), and its capacity identity proves the displayed formula for \(C_{\mathbf j}\), including boundary roots. Consequently \(\omega\) is a probability law whose unnormalized weights are \(p(\mathbf j)^3e^{2H_J(\mathbf j)}\).

We first record the real-mean profile and neighboring-total facts used below. For a finite support whose convex hull is one of the present composition simplices or capped-count polytopes, its maximum-entropy law exists and is unique at every real feasible mean. On the relative interior of a face it is positive on the entire face support and log-affine there. Indeed, compactness gives existence, strict concavity gives uniqueness, and a sufficiently small mixture of the uniform law on that face with a vertex-supported law realizes the mean with strictly positive masses. If a maximizer had a zero mass, mixing it with this positive feasible law would increase entropy by a positive term of order \(-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon\), contradicting maximality. The finite Lagrange multiplier equations then give the log-affine representation. In an affine chart of the face, differentiation of its log partition sum gives the mean and covariance. The covariance is positive definite: a score of variance zero is constant at every support point, and these points affinely span the face. The inverse function theorem therefore gives a smooth parameter as a function of the mean. Differentiating entropy yields \(dH=-\sum_i(\log w_i)\,dy_i\) for a tangent mean displacement. This is the same real-mean entropy-profile construction proved for simplices in `81ad175d3c410508`; the preceding argument also establishes it on the capped faces.

For later endpoint integrations, this profile is continuous on any closed feasible mean segment whose open segment is in a fixed face interior. The upper limit follows by taking convergent subsequences of maximizing laws and using continuity of entropy on the finite probability simplex. For the lower limit at either endpoint, mix the endpoint maximizer with any feasible law at the opposite endpoint. The resulting means approach that endpoint along the segment, and the laws and entropies converge to the endpoint maximizer. Smoothness supplies continuity at interior points. These arguments apply to every segment used below.

A transfer of \(r\ge1\) units from active coordinate \(i\) to active coordinate \(f\) within the same block is a bijection from the support points with \(N_i\ge r\) to those with \(N_f\ge r\). It preserves the total, the band, and any endpoint face. Under a log-affine row,
\[
\Pr(N_i\ge r)=(w_i/w_f)^r\Pr(N_f\ge r)\le(w_i/w_f)^r.
\]
The same identity holds for a complete-composition law, without the restriction to one of the two blocks. Summing tails shows that, at any positive real mean \(y_i\),
\[
\frac{w_i}{w_f}\ge\frac{y_i}{1+y_i}
\quad\hbox{if }w_i<w_f,
\qquad
-\log(w_i/w_f)\le\log(1+1/y_i)
\quad\hbox{in all cases}.
\]
In particular, after normalizing weights to have maximum one in a block, their negative logarithms lie between zero and \(\log(1+1/y_i)\).

For a fixed positive tuple \(\mathbf w\) with maximum one and length \(u\), denote its complete-composition coordinate means at total \(T\) by \(\mu_i^{(T)}\). The monotonicity \(\mu_i^{(T+1)}\ge\mu_i^{(T)}\) was proved in `55b5ec7cf9276f99` from the log-concavity of \(h_T\) in `128175cd66587a67`. The tail identity gives the additional exact recurrence
\[
\mu_i^{(T+1)}=w_i\frac{h_T(\mathbf w)}{h_{T+1}(\mathbf w)}
\bigl(\mu_i^{(T)}+1\bigr).
\]
Adding a unit at a maximal-weight coordinate injects the total-\(T\) compositions into the total-\(T+1\) compositions, so \(h_{T+1}\ge h_T\). The recurrence's factor is thus at most one. It follows that every increment \(\Delta_i=\mu_i^{(T+1)}-\mu_i^{(T)}\) belongs to \([0,1]\), and \(\sum_i\Delta_i=1\). Counting each total-\(T+1\) composition with its \(T+1\) units also gives
\[
(T+1)h_{T+1}(\mathbf w)
=\sum_{\sum n_i=T}\mathbf w^{\mathbf n}\sum_iw_i(n_i+1)
\le(T+u)h_T(\mathbf w).
\]
Thus \(1\le h_{T+1}/h_T\le2\) whenever \(T\ge u-1\). All these assertions allow a tuple of length one.

The marginal log-concavity and negative covariances of every complete-composition law are provided by `128175cd66587a67`. The scalar tail calculation in `81ad175d3c410508` applies at arbitrary real coordinate means and gives \(\operatorname{Var}N_i\le\mu_i(\mu_i+1)\). Explicitly, its log-concave marginal survival probabilities satisfy \(s_{a+b}\le s_as_b\), whence \(\mathbb E[N_i(N_i-1)]/2\le(\sum_{a\ge1}s_a)^2=\mu_i^2\). Subtracting \(\mu_i^2\) proves the asserted variance bound. Deterministic coordinates satisfy it as well.

We next prove the required polynomial entropy-numerator comparison. On compositions of total \(T\) into \(u\ge1\) coordinates, write \(H_T(\mathbf y)\) for the maximum-entropy profile. Its exact alias to the simplex profile in `81ad175d3c410508` is obtained by deleting any coordinate as slack: that fact's dilation is \(m=T\), its rank is \(r=u-1\), and its augmented mean is the full vector \(\mathbf y\). A one-coordinate composition has entropy zero. For \(T\ge\max\{1,u-1\}\), every integer mean \(\mathbf j\) of total \(T\), and every coordinate \(f\), we claim
\[
0\le H_{T+1}(\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f)-H_T(\mathbf j)\le\log16. \tag{1}
\]
The lower bound follows by shifting the entire mean-\(\mathbf j\) maximizing law by \(\mathbf e_f\); this is an injection preserving all its masses and entropy.

For the upper bound, take the entropy row of total \(T+1\) and mean \(\boldsymbol\ell=\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f\). Use just its active coordinates and normalize its weights \(\mathbf w\) to maximum one. Since all positive \(\ell_i\) are integers, the transfer bound gives \(1/2\le w_i\le1\). Use the same tuple at total \(T\), and call its mean \(\boldsymbol\mu\). Its law is the entropy maximizer at \(\boldsymbol\mu\). The preceding increments give \(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\mu=\boldsymbol\Delta\), with \(0\le\Delta_i\le1\) and \(\sum_i\Delta_i=1\). Directly from the two entropy formulas,
\[
H_{T+1}(\boldsymbol\ell)-H_T(\boldsymbol\mu)
=\log\frac{h_{T+1}(\mathbf w)}{h_T(\mathbf w)}
-\sum_i(\log w_i)\Delta_i\le\log4. \tag{2}
\]
Here the active tuple length is at most \(u\), so the partition-ratio bound applies.

Put \(t=\mu_f-j_f=1-\Delta_f\in[0,1]\). For \(i\ne f\), \(\mu_i=j_i-\Delta_i\), and \(\sum_{i\ne f}\Delta_i=t\). If \(t=0\), then \(\boldsymbol\mu=\mathbf j\). Otherwise follow the same-total segment from \(\mathbf j\) to \(\boldsymbol\mu\), increasing the \(f\)-mean by \(\tau\in[0,t]\) and decreasing the other means by \(\tau\Delta_i/t\). Normalize the row weights along its open segment to maximum one. The derivative of entropy along this segment is at most
\[
-\log w_f+\sum_{i\ne f}(\Delta_i/t)\log w_i
\le-\log w_f\le\log(1+1/(j_f+\tau)).
\]
All positive non-\(f\) means remain positive because the total-\(T\) law with tuple \(\mathbf w\) has positive means, and the \(f\)-mean is positive on the open segment. The established face continuity permits integration even if \(j_f=0\). For
\(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(x)=(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log x\), with \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(0)=0\), the resulting upper bound is
\[
H_T(\boldsymbol\mu)-H_T(\mathbf j)
\le h_{\mathrm{geom}}(j_f+t)-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(j_f)
\le h_{\mathrm{geom}}(1)=\log4.
\]
The last inequality uses \(t\le1\) and the decreasing positive derivative \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}'(x)=\log(1+1/x)\). Combining this with (2) proves (1), including old zero means and changes of active face.

For a positive source tuple \(\mathbf v=(v_1,\ldots,v_u)\) with \(\max_i v_i=1\), define
\[
F_T(\mathbf v)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{u}\\\sum_i j_i=T}}
\mathbf v^{3\mathbf j}e^{2H_T(\mathbf j)}.
\]
Here \(\mathbf v^{3\mathbf j}=\prod_i v_i^{3j_i}\). These \(F_T\) are entropy-weighted source numerators, not the minimal faces \(F_{\mathbf j}\). Choose \(f\) with \(v_f=1\). The injection \(\mathbf j\mapsto\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f\) and the lower bound in (1) give \(F_{T+1}\ge F_T\). For each root \(\boldsymbol\ell\) of total \(T+1\), applying the upper bound in (1) to any \(i\) with \(\ell_i>0\) gives
\(\mathbf v^{3\boldsymbol\ell}e^{2H_{T+1}(\boldsymbol\ell)}\le256v_i^3\mathbf v^{3(\boldsymbol\ell-\mathbf e_i)}e^{2H_T(\boldsymbol\ell-\mathbf e_i)}\).
Sum this bound over all such \(i\) as an overcount. For each fixed \(i\), \(\boldsymbol\ell\mapsto\boldsymbol\ell-\mathbf e_i\) bijects its domain onto all total-\(T\) roots. Therefore
\[
1\le\frac{F_{T+1}(\mathbf v)}{F_T(\mathbf v)}\le256u
\qquad(T\ge\max\{1,u-1\}). \tag{3}
\]
This proves the proposed polynomial mechanism directly.

We now compare capped entropy to the fixed-middle-level entropy. Let \(\sum_{i\in A}j_i=k\). The notation \(\alpha,\beta,\mu_i(s)\) is exactly that of `55b5ec7cf9276f99`: the row level probabilities are \((\alpha,\beta,\alpha)\), with \(\alpha\ge2/7\), \(\beta\ge1/3\), and hence \(\alpha\le1/3\). That fact's neighboring-total comparisons imply both
\(j_i/6\le\mu_i(s)\le6j_i\) for every active coordinate and level. Within each block, the adjacent conditional-mean increments for increasing block total are nonnegative and sum to one, as established above. Since the mean is the level mixture, this proves
\[
\|\boldsymbol\mu_A(k)-\mathbf j_A\|_1\le2\alpha,\qquad
\|\boldsymbol\mu_B(k)-\mathbf j_B\|_1\le2\alpha. \tag{4}
\]
For example in block \(A\), \(\mathbf j_A-\boldsymbol\mu_A(k)\) is \(\alpha\) times the difference of its two neighboring increment vectors, each nonnegative with sum one. The reversed block totals in \(B\) give the same bound.

Write \(H_{A,k}\) and \(H_{B,k}\) for the two fixed-total composition profiles, and put \(H_{\mathrm{same}}(\mathbf j)=H_{A,k}(\mathbf j_A)+H_{B,k}(\mathbf j_B)\). Conditional on the middle level, the blocks are independent composition laws, so their total entropy is \(H_{\mathrm{same}}(\boldsymbol\mu(k))\). Along the same-total segments in (4), every active coordinate mean is at least \(j_i/6\ge1/6\). Normalizing the weights to maximum one separately in each block, the entropy-gradient coefficients are between zero and \(\log7\). Integrating the gradient and using (4) gives
\[
\bigl|H_{\mathrm{same}}(\boldsymbol\mu(k))-H_{\mathrm{same}}(\mathbf j)\bigr|
\le4\alpha\log7\le\tfrac43\log7. \tag{5}
\]
This argument uses the real conditional means, not an integer-mean assertion about them.

For the full row weights \(w_i\), the within-block transfer estimate at its integer mean \(\mathbf j\) shows that their max-normalized negative logarithms are at most \(\log2\). The log normalizer and middle-level probability give the exact identity
\[
H_J(\mathbf j)=H_{\mathrm{same}}(\boldsymbol\mu(k))-\log\beta
+\sum_{i\in I}(\log w_i)(\mu_i(k)-j_i).
\]
The block gauges in the last sum cancel because each mean difference has block sum zero. Its absolute value is therefore at most \(4\alpha\log2\). By (5) and \(\beta\ge1/3\),
\[
H_J(\mathbf j)\le H_{\mathrm{same}}(\mathbf j)+C_H. \tag{6}
\]
Conversely, the product of the two fixed-total entropy maximizers at \(\mathbf j_A,\mathbf j_B\) is a feasible law on \(J_k\) of mean \(\mathbf j\). Thus \(H_J(\mathbf j)\ge H_{\mathrm{same}}(\mathbf j)\). At either endpoint block-total mean, \(S_A\) is forced to that endpoint and the maximizing law is exactly the product of the corresponding fixed-total maximizers, as in `55b5ec7cf9276f99`.

We next bound the row self variance and the internal source variance. For an interior block-total root, normalize its row weights by their block maxima \(M_A^{\mathrm{row}},M_B^{\mathrm{row}}\). For a block \(\mathcal C\in\{A,B\}\), write \(u_i=\log(M_{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{row}}/w_i)\) for \(i\in\mathcal C\cap I\), and put \(\delta=\log(M_A^{\mathrm{row}}/M_B^{\mathrm{row}})\). Here \(u_i\) are nonnegative and \(u_i\le1/j_i\), and at least one \(u_i\) per active block is zero. The row log density, up to a constant, is \(\delta S_A-\sum_{i\in I}u_iN_i\).

The two outer level probabilities are equal. For each block's normalized tuple, let \(r_{\mathcal C}=h_{k+1}/h_{k-1}\). The preceding partition-ratio estimates at \(k-1\) and \(k\) give \(1\le r_{\mathcal C}\le4\), since the active tuple length is at most \(k\). Equality of the outer unnormalized level weights gives \(e^{2\delta}=r_B/r_A\), so \(|\delta|\le\log2\).

Within each level the blocks are independent, and the count covariances inside each block are nonpositive by `128175cd66587a67`. Since the \(u_i\) have one sign, and since \(\mu_i(s)\le6j_i\) and \(j_i\ge1\) for active counts, the conditional self variance is bounded by
\[
\sum_{i\in I}u_i^2\mu_i(s)(\mu_i(s)+1)
\le42\sum_{i\in I:u_i>0}1\le42R.
\]
The conditional self-log-density means change between adjacent levels by at most \(|\delta|+\max_{A\cap I}u_i+\max_{B\cap I}u_i\le\log2+2\). Their three values thus have range at most \(2(2+\log2)\), and variance at most \((2+\log2)^2<9\). Total variance and \(R\ge3\) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)\le45R. \tag{7}
\]
For an endpoint root, `128175cd66587a67` applies to its two independent integer-mean composition factors. Deleting a slack in each active block identifies its simplex rank with that block's active coordinate count minus one. Its self variance is at most twice the sum of these ranks, hence at most \(2R\), and (7) still holds.

For the source, set \(M_{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{src}}=\max_{i\in\mathcal C}q_i\) for \(\mathcal C\in\{A,B\}\), \(\bar q_i=q_i/M_{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{src}}\) for \(i\in\mathcal C\), \(a_i=-\log\bar q_i\ge0\), and \(\gamma=\log(M_A^{\mathrm{src}}/M_B^{\mathrm{src}})\). There is a source-favored index \(f_{\mathcal C}\) in each block with \(a_{f_{\mathcal C}}=0\). The source log score, up to a constant, is
\(\gamma S_A-Q\), where \(Q=\sum_i a_iN_i\). Put \(K_{\mathbf j}=\sum_i(a_ij_i)^2\). Conditional negative covariance and the same coordinate moment bound give \(\operatorname{Var}(Q\mid S_A=s)\le42K_{\mathbf j}\). The adjacent conditional means of \(Q\) differ by at most \(b_A+b_B\), where \(b_{\mathcal C}=\max_{i\in\mathcal C\cap I}a_i\). Their range is at most \(2(b_A+b_B)\), so their variance is at most
\((b_A+b_B)^2\le2(b_A^2+b_B^2)\le2K_{\mathbf j}\), using \(j_i\ge1\) on \(I\). Thus \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Q\le44K_{\mathbf j}\). Endpoint rows satisfy the sharper conditional bound \(2K_{\mathbf j}\), so this estimate includes them.

For an interior root, \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}S_A=2\alpha\le2/3\); for an endpoint root it is zero. Applying \(\operatorname{Var}(X-Y)\le2\operatorname{Var}X+2\operatorname{Var}Y\), first to the source score and then to the likelihood, yields
\[
V_{\mathbf j}\le90R+176K_{\mathbf j}
+\frac83\gamma^2\mathbf1_{\{\sum_{i\in A}j_i=k\}}. \tag{8}
\]
Every negative-covariance application here is inside a fixed-total composition block. No negative covariance of the unconditioned capped row has been asserted.

The internal costs average uniformly. Fix \(i\ne f_{\mathcal C}\) in block \(\mathcal C\), condition under \(\omega\) on the root block total and on every root coordinate except \(j_i,j_{f_{\mathcal C}}\), and write \(j_i=t\), \(j_{f_{\mathcal C}}=M-t\). The possible integer \(t\) are precisely \(0,\ldots,M\). This transfer preserves the band, and the real entropy profile along this same-total line satisfies
\[
\frac{d}{dt}H_J(\mathbf y(t))
=-\log(w_i/w_{f_{\mathcal C}})\le\log(1+1/t)
\quad(0<t<M).
\]
Face continuity extends the resulting monotonicity of \(H_J(\mathbf y(t))-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)\) to both endpoints. The conditional unnormalized \(\omega\)-weights are therefore
\(e^{-3a_it+2h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)}g(t)\), where \(g(t)=e^{2[H_J(\mathbf y(t))-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)]}\) on \(0,\ldots,M\) and \(g(t)=0\) beyond \(M\). This \(g\) is bounded, nonnegative, nonincreasing, and positive at zero.

The one-dimensional monotone-weighting argument in `81ad175d3c410508` now applies without any geometric change. For clarity, weighting a probability law by a nonincreasing \(g\) decreases expectations of increasing functions, since for independent copies \(T,T'\), \((F(T)-F(T'))(g(T)-g(T'))\le0\). For \(a=a_i>0\), compare with the infinite law proportional to \(e^{-3at+2h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)}\). Since \(\log(t+1)\le h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)\le\log(t+1)+1\), its squared-cost expectation is at most
\[
e^2a^2\frac{\sum_{t\ge0}t^2(t+1)^2z^t}{\sum_{t\ge0}(t+1)^2z^t},
\qquad z=e^{-3a}.
\]
The denominator is \((1+z)/(1-z)^3\), and the numerator is \(4z(1+4z+z^2)/(1-z)^5\). Using \((1+4z+z^2)/(1+z)\le3\) and \(z/(1-z)^2=1/[4\sinh^2(3a/2)]\le1/(9a^2)\) gives the bound \(4e^2/3\). When \(a_i=0\) or \(M=0\), the cost is zero. Hence, for every coordinate,
\(\mathbb E_\omega(a_ij_i)^2\le4e^2/3\). At least two coefficients, one per block, vanish identically, so
\[
\mathbb E_\omega K_{\mathbf j}\le\tfrac43e^2(2k-2)\le\tfrac43e^2R. \tag{9}
\]

It remains to average the unrestricted block-total coefficient \(\gamma\). Apply (3) to each source-normalized block tuple \(\bar{\mathbf q}_A,\bar{\mathbf q}_B\) of length \(k\), at \(T=k-1,k\), and put \(L_k=256k\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_-&=F_{k-1}(\bar{\mathbf q}_A)F_{k+1}(\bar{\mathbf q}_B),\\
F_+&=F_{k+1}(\bar{\mathbf q}_A)F_{k-1}(\bar{\mathbf q}_B),\\
F_0&=F_k(\bar{\mathbf q}_A)F_k(\bar{\mathbf q}_B),\\
F_{\mathrm{mid}}&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf j_A,\mathbf j_B\ge0\text{ integer}\\
\sum_{i\in A}j_i=\sum_{i\in B}j_i=k}}
\bar{\mathbf q}^{3\mathbf j}e^{2H_J(\mathbf j)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The endpoint entropy identities show that \(F_-\) and \(F_+\) are exactly the source-normalized endpoint numerators. By (6), \(F_{\mathrm{mid}}\le e^{2C_H}F_0\). Writing each neighboring ratio in (3) as a number between one and \(L_k\) gives
\[
\left|\tfrac12\log(F_+/F_-)\right|\le\log L_k,
\qquad
\frac{F_0}{\sqrt{F_-F_+}}\le L_k.
\]
Indeed, \(F_+/F_-\) is the ratio of the two blocks' products of two neighboring ratios, while \(\sqrt{F_-F_+}/F_0\) is the square root of the product of the two upper neighboring ratios divided by the product of the two lower ones. In particular, with \(U=F_{\mathrm{mid}}/\sqrt{F_-F_+}\), we have \(0<U\le D_{\mathrm{num}}k\).

The total \(\omega\)-mass of interior block-total roots, denoted \(\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\), is exactly
\[
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}
=\frac{F_{\mathrm{mid}}}{e^{-3\gamma}F_-+F_{\mathrm{mid}}+e^{3\gamma}F_+}
=\frac{U}{2\cosh z+U},
\qquad
z=3\gamma+b_k,\quad b_k=\tfrac12\log(F_+/F_-).
\]
This follows directly from the unnormalized weights \(p(\mathbf j)^3e^{2H_J(\mathbf j)}\), whose block-total factor is \(e^{3\gamma\sum_{i\in A}j_i}\). Thus \(\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\le\min\{1,D_{\mathrm{num}}k e^{-|z|}\}\). Put \(t_k=\log(D_{\mathrm{num}}k)>2\). For \(x\ge t_k\), the function \(x^2e^{t_k-x}\) is nonincreasing, so
\(z^2\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\le t_k^2\). Also \(|b_k|\le\log L_k\le t_k\). Since \(3\gamma=z-b_k\),
\[
\gamma^2\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\le\frac49t_k^2
\le\frac49\bigl((\log D_{\mathrm{num}})^2+2\bigr)k. \tag{10}
\]
For the second inequality, \(t_k^2\le2(\log D_{\mathrm{num}})^2+2(\log k)^2\), \((\log k)^2\le k\) for \(k\ge1\), and \(k\ge2\) gives \(2(\log D_{\mathrm{num}})^2\le k(\log D_{\mathrm{num}})^2\). The elementary bound \(\log k\le\sqrt{k}\) follows by minimizing \(\sqrt{x}-\log x\) on \([1,\infty)\), whose minimum is \(2-\log4>0\).

Finally average (8), use (9) and (10), and note \(k\le R\). This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
