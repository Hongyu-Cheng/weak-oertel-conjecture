## Statement

Let $A=[-11/10,-9/10]\times[-3,3]$ and take the closed halfplanes $H_1=\{x\ge0\}$, $H_2=\{-x+y\ge0\}$ and $H_3=\{-x-y\ge0\}$. Their normals form a positive circuit and the halfplanes cover the plane. With $W=(2/5)\mathbf1_{H_1}+(3/10)\mathbf1_{H_2}+(3/10)\mathbf1_{H_3}$, the supremum of $\int_{\partial A}\Phi(n,x,y)W\,dS/\int_{\partial A}\Phi(n,x,y)\,dS$ over finite nonnegative normal-dependent potentials convex in the physical variables at each normal and with positive boundary integral is exactly $41/100<4/9$. A continuous normal multiplier constant in the physical variables attains the supremum.

## Proof

The positive dependence is twice the first normal plus the other two. If all three margins were negative, their positively weighted sum would be negative, whereas it is identically zero. The first cap misses the rectangle. On either horizontal edge exactly one of the other two caps captures every point, so the profile is $3/10$. On the vertical edge at $x=-c$, where $c=9/10$ or $11/10$, the profile is $3/10+(3/10)\mathbf1_{[-c,c]}(y)$.

The restriction $f$ of any admissible potential to one such edge is a nonnegative convex function of $y$. Its even symmetrization $g(t)=(f(t)+f(-t))/2$ is even and convex, hence nondecreasing for $t\ge0$. The mean of $g$ over $[0,c]$ is at most its mean over $[0,3]$, so $\int_{-c}^c f\le(c/3)\int_{-3}^3 f$. Thus the weighted vertical-edge integral is at most $(3/10)(1+c/3)$ times its unweighted integral. These bounds are $39/100$ on the right and $41/100$ on the left. The horizontal edge bound is $3/10$. Summing the nonnegative edge integrals gives the upper bound, also when some edge integrals vanish. Take a continuous nonnegative function of the unit normal that is one at the left outward normal and zero at the other three. This potential is constant in the physical variables and makes the only nonzero edge restriction constant on the left. There the mean bound is attained, so the global ratio is $41/100$.
