## Statement

Let \(\mu\) be a probability law with finite support \(\Omega\) in a
finite-dimensional real affine space whose translation space \(V\) is
equipped with any inner product.  Write
\[
 q_z:=\mu(\{z\}),\qquad
 \delta:=\max_{z\in\Omega}q_z\leq\frac14,\qquad
 \bar x:=\mathbb E_\mu X,\qquad
 \Sigma:=\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(X).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(X,Y\) be independent with law \(\mu\), put
\[
 E:=\{X\ne Y\},\qquad
 s_2:=\sum_{z\in\Omega}q_z^2,\qquad
 p:=\mathbb P(E)=1-s_2,
\tag{2}
\]
and use the conditional probability law
\(\mathbb P_E:=\mathbb P(\,\cdot\,\mid E)\).  Define
\[
 M:=\frac{X+Y}{2},\qquad S:=\frac{X-Y}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(s_2\leq\delta\), so \(p\geq3/4\), and the conditional means are
\[
 \mathbb E_E M
 =\bar x-\frac{\sum_{z\in\Omega}q_z^2(z-\bar x)}{p},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_E S=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The conditional covariance operators satisfy
\[
 \frac5{36}\Sigma
 \preceq\operatorname{Cov}_E(M)
 \preceq\frac23\Sigma,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_E(S)=\frac{\Sigma}{2p},
\tag{5}
\]
and hence
\[
 \frac12\Sigma
 \preceq\operatorname{Cov}_E(S)
 \preceq\frac23\Sigma.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, with cross covariance centered at the conditional means,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_E(M,S)
 :=\mathbb E_E\!\left[(M-\mathbb E_E M)\otimes
 (S-\mathbb E_E S)\right]=0.
\tag{7}
\]
No nondegeneracy assumption is needed, and
\[
 \ker\operatorname{Cov}_E(M)
 =\ker\operatorname{Cov}_E(S)=\ker\Sigma.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Cov}_E(M)
 =\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Cov}_E(S)
 =\operatorname{rank}\Sigma.
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a real Hilbert space and let
\(f:\Omega\to\mathcal H\).  For an affine map
\(a:\operatorname{aff}(\Omega)\to\mathcal H\), define
\[
 \mathcal D_\mu(f)
 :=\inf_a\mathbb E_\mu\|f(X)-a(X)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Under \(\mathbb P_E\), put
\[
 F:=\frac{f(X)+f(Y)}2,\qquad
 g(m):=\mathbb E_E[F\mid M=m],
\tag{11}
\]
let \(\nu\) be the law of \(M\), and define
\[
 \mathcal R_\mu(f):=\mathbb E_E\|F-g(M)\|_{\mathcal H}^2,\qquad
 \mathcal D_\nu(g):=\inf_a\mathbb E_E\|g(M)-a(M)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Here the affine maps in the second infimum may be taken on the same ambient
affine space.  Every affine map on the affine hull of the support of
\(\nu\) extends to that space, so this convention does not change the
infimum.

Both least-squares infima in (10) and (12) are attained.  One has the exact
conditional total-variance identity
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D_\nu(g)+\mathcal R_\mu(f)
 =\inf_a\mathbb E_E\|F-a(M)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.}
\tag{13}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \boxed{
 \left(\frac12-\delta\right)\mathcal D_\mu(f)
 \leq\mathcal D_\nu(g)+\mathcal R_\mu(f)
 \leq\frac{\mathcal D_\mu(f)}{2(1-\delta)}.}
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max\{\mathcal D_\nu(g),\mathcal R_\mu(f)\}
 \geq\left(\frac14-\frac\delta2\right)\mathcal D_\mu(f).
\tag{15}
\]

Equations (13)-(15) concern conditional expectations in an abstract finite
probability space.  They do not assert that \(g(m)\), or the conditional
barycenter defining it, belongs to a metronoid of a mixed-body fiber.  They
also do not provide a simultaneous admissible selection realizing the
values \(g(m)\).  Thus no mixed-body metronoid realization follows from
this theorem.

## Proof

The identity \(s_2\leq\delta\sum_zq_z=\delta\) proves \(p\geq3/4>0\),
so every conditional quantity above is well-defined.  Because
\(\mathbb E(X-\bar x)=0\), deleting the diagonal event gives
\[
 \mathbb E_E(M-\bar x)
 =-\frac{\sum_zq_z^2(z-\bar x)}p.
\tag{16}
\]
The conditional law of \((X,Y)\) is invariant under swapping its two
coordinates.  The swap fixes \(M\) and sends \(S\) to \(-S\), which proves
\(\mathbb E_E S=0\).  This establishes (4).

Fix \(u\in V\) and set
\[
 \xi_z:=\langle u,z-\bar x\rangle,\qquad
 \sigma^2:=\sum_zq_z\xi_z^2=\langle u,\Sigma u\rangle,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
 d_1:=\sum_zq_z^2\xi_z,\qquad
 d_2:=\sum_zq_z^2\xi_z^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Before conditioning,
\[
 \mathbb E\left(\frac{\xi_X+\xi_Y}{2}\right)^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\left(\frac{\xi_X-\xi_Y}{2}\right)^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]
On the diagonal \(X=Y=z\), the first squared variable equals
\(\xi_z^2\) and the second equals zero.  The off-diagonal numerator of the
first mean is \(-d_1\).  Division by the exact conditioning probability
\(p\) therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_E\langle u,M\rangle
 =\frac{\sigma^2/2-d_2}{p}-\frac{d_1^2}{p^2},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_E\langle u,S\rangle=\frac{\sigma^2}{2p}.
\tag{20}
\]
These formulas also hold when \(\sigma^2=0\), in which case
\(d_1=d_2=0\).

Since \(q_z\leq\delta\leq1/4\),
\[
 0\leq d_2\leq\delta\sigma^2\leq\frac{\sigma^2}{4}.
\tag{21}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz, followed by \(s_2\leq1/4\), gives
\[
 d_1^2
 \leq\left(\sum_zq_z^2\right)\left(\sum_zq_z^2\xi_z^2\right)
 =s_2d_2\leq\frac{\sigma^2}{16}.
\tag{22}
\]
As \(p\leq1\) and \(p\geq3/4\), equations (20)-(22) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_E\langle u,M\rangle
 \geq\frac{\sigma^2}{4}-\frac{\sigma^2/16}{(3/4)^2}
 =\frac5{36}\sigma^2,
\tag{23}
\]
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_E\langle u,M\rangle
 \leq\frac{\sigma^2}{2p}\leq\frac23\sigma^2,
\tag{24}
\]
and
\[
 \frac12\sigma^2
 \leq\operatorname{Var}_E\langle u,S\rangle
 =\frac{\sigma^2}{2p}\leq\frac23\sigma^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Because these inequalities hold for every \(u\), they are exactly the
Loewner inequalities (5)-(6), including the identity for
\(\operatorname{Cov}_E(S)\).

For (7), the centered midpoint \(M-\mathbb E_E M\) is fixed by the swap,
while \(S-\mathbb E_E S=S\) changes sign.  The conditional law is
swap-invariant, so the tensor expectation in (7) is its own negative and
is zero.

For positive semidefinite operators, zero quadratic form in a direction is
equivalent to membership in the kernel.  The upper bound
\(\operatorname{Cov}_E(M)\preceq(2/3)\Sigma\) and the lower bound
\((5/36)\Sigma\preceq\operatorname{Cov}_E(M)\) give both inclusions between
their kernels.  The same conclusion for \(S\) follows from
\(\operatorname{Cov}_E(S)=\Sigma/(2p)\).  This proves (8)-(9), also when
\(\Sigma\) has deficient rank or is zero.

We next justify Hilbert-valued least squares.  For any finite probability
law \(\lambda\) on a set \(\Lambda\), equip the space
\(\mathcal H^\Lambda\) with its weighted Hilbert norm.  Let
\(U\subseteq\mathbb R^\Lambda\) be the finite-dimensional space of
restrictions of scalar affine functions.  If
\(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r\) is a weighted-orthonormal basis of \(U\), the
restrictions of \(\mathcal H\)-valued affine maps are precisely
\[
 \left\{\sum_{j=1}^r\phi_j(\,\cdot\,)h_j:
 h_1,\ldots,h_r\in\mathcal H\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The coefficient map from \(\mathcal H^r\) to (26) is an isometry, so its
image is closed.  Orthogonal projection onto this image supplies a
least-squares affine minimizer.  This applies to both finite laws \(\mu\)
and \(\nu\).  Since constant functions lie in \(U\), the residual
\(r(z):=f(z)-a_*(z)\) of a minimizer for (10) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_\mu r(X)=0.
\tag{27}
\]

For any affine map \(a\), write \(r(z):=f(z)-a(z)\).  Affine maps commute
with midpoint averaging, so
\[
 F-a(M)=\frac{r(X)+r(Y)}2.
\tag{28}
\]
Independence gives the exact unconditioned identity
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|\frac{r(X)+r(Y)}2\right\|^2
 =\frac12\mathbb E_\mu\|r(X)\|^2
 +\frac12\|\mathbb E_\mu r(X)\|^2.
\tag{29}
\]
The diagonal contribution is exactly
\(\sum_zq_z^2\|r(z)\|^2\).  Removing it and dividing by \(p\) yields the
exact conditioned formula
\[
 \mathbb E_E\|F-a(M)\|^2
 =\frac{\frac12\mathbb E_\mu\|r(X)\|^2
 +\frac12\|\mathbb E_\mu r(X)\|^2
 -\sum_zq_z^2\|r(z)\|^2}{p}.
\tag{30}
\]

For every affine \(a\), the conditional expectation property in (11)
and the Hilbert-space inner product give the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 \mathbb E_E\|F-a(M)\|^2
 =\mathbb E_E\|F-g(M)\|^2
 +\mathbb E_E\|g(M)-a(M)\|^2.
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, after conditioning on \(M\), the cross term contains
\(\mathbb E_E[F-g(M)\mid M]=0\).  Taking the infimum over \(a\) in (31)
proves the exact identity (13).

For the lower bound, \(q_z^2\leq\delta q_z\) implies
\[
 \sum_zq_z^2\|r(z)\|^2
 \leq\delta\,\mathbb E_\mu\|r(X)\|^2.
\tag{32}
\]
Dropping the nonnegative mean term in (30), using \(p\leq1\), and then
taking the infimum over affine \(a\) gives
\[
 \inf_a\mathbb E_E\|F-a(M)\|^2
 \geq\left(\frac12-\delta\right)\mathcal D_\mu(f).
\tag{33}
\]
For the upper bound, use the least-squares minimizer \(a_*\).  Its residual
has mean zero by (27), and the diagonal term in (30) is nonnegative.
Therefore
\[
 \inf_a\mathbb E_E\|F-a(M)\|^2
 \leq\frac{\mathcal D_\mu(f)}{2p}
 \leq\frac{\mathcal D_\mu(f)}{2(1-\delta)},
\tag{34}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(p=1-s_2\geq1-\delta\).  Combining
(13), (33), and (34) proves (14).  Finally, the maximum of two
nonnegative numbers is at least half their sum, so (15) follows from the
lower bound in (14).
