## Statement

Let \(m\ge2\), \(h\ge1\), and \(L\) be integers with \(2h\le L<3h\). Let \(\beta<0\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) be finite, and give \((\mathbf X,Y)\) the strictly positive probability law proportional to \(\exp(\beta\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+\theta y)\) on
\[
 E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:
                       \sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\}.
\]
Suppose \(\mathbb E(\mathbf X,Y)=(j\mathbf1,1)\) for an integer \(j\ge1\). Define \(X_\Sigma=\sum_{i=1}^mX_i\), \(q=e^\beta\), \(B_y=L-hy\), and
\[
 Z_y=\sum_{s=0}^{B_y}\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}q^s,\qquad
 v_y=\operatorname{Var}(X_\Sigma\mid Y=y),\qquad
 U=X_\Sigma-\mathbb E[X_\Sigma\mid Y].
\]
These are the same original objects as in accepted fact \(5fa48a554f30f367\). In accepted fact \(43cb56218b18ec82\), its tilt variable \(r\) equals this \(q\), its partition function \(Z_m(B_y,\beta)\) equals this \(Z_y\), and its original support is this \(E_{L,h,m}\). Then
\[
 \mathbb E U^4
 \le 2^{101}\left(\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(X_\Sigma\mid Y)\right)^2
 =2^{101}(\mathbb E U^2)^2.
\]

## Proof

We first record the exact original-source constraints. The height support is \(\{0,1,2\}\). Its endpoint probabilities are equal because \(\mathbb EY=1\). Accepted fact \(5fa48a554f30f367\) gives
\[
 p_0=p_2=\varepsilon
     =\frac{\sqrt{Z_0Z_2}}{Z_1+2\sqrt{Z_0Z_2}},\qquad
 p_1=1-2\varepsilon\ge\frac13,\qquad
 \varepsilon v_0^2\le2^{74}v_1^2,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(p_y=\Pr(Y=y)\). Strict positivity makes the mean original slack \(L-mj-h=B_1-mj\) positive. It is an integer, so \(B_1\ge m+1\). Also \(h\le B_1<2h\), whence \(0\le B_2\le B_1/2\). Accepted fact \(5fa48a554f30f367\) gives \(1/2<q<1\), and accepted fact \(43cb56218b18ec82\) gives \(v_1\ge1/20\). In particular, with \(\overline v:=\sum_{y=0}^2p_yv_y=\mathbb EU^2\),
\[
 \overline v\ge\frac{v_1}{3}\ge\frac1{60}.
 \tag{2}
\]

We prove an elementary scalar estimate including variances smaller than the discrete lattice scale. If \(T\) has a log-concave probability mass function with finite contiguous integer support and variance \(v\), then
\[
 \mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^4\le2^{23}(v^2+v).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here log-concavity means that the successive positive mass ratios are nonincreasing. A one-point support has \(v=0\) and satisfies (3), so assume \(v>0\).

First let \(v\ge1/20\), put \(\sigma=\sqrt v\), and write \(a_i=\Pr(T=i)\), extended by zero outside the support. The survival sequence \(Q(k)=\Pr(T\ge k)\) is log-concave. Indeed, at an index in the positive support, the successive-ratio ordering gives \(a_ka_i\ge a_{k-1}a_{i+1}\) for every \(i\ge k\); this remains true if \(a_{k-1}=0\). Summing and using \(Q(k-1)=a_{k-1}+Q(k)\) and \(Q(k)=a_k+Q(k+1)\) gives
\[
 Q(k)^2-Q(k-1)Q(k+1)
 =a_kQ(k)-a_{k-1}Q(k+1)\ge0.
\]
Outside the positive support the same inequality follows from the constant-one or constant-zero survival values. The survival sequence of \(-T\) is also log-concave.

Let \(k_0=\lfloor\mathbb ET-2\sigma\rfloor\) and \(k_1=\lceil\mathbb ET+2\sigma\rceil\). Chebyshev's inequality gives \(Q(k_0)\ge3/4\) and \(Q(k_1)\le1/4\). The positive integer \(\ell=k_1-k_0\) satisfies \(\ell\le4\sigma+2<13\sigma\), because \(\sigma\ge1/\sqrt{20}\). If \(Q(k_1)>0\), concavity of \(\log Q\) on its positive indices implies
\[
 Q(k)\le\frac14\,3^{-(k-k_1)/\ell}\qquad(k\ge k_1).
\]
The bound also holds if \(Q(k)=0\). If \(Q(k_1)=0\), all these upper tails vanish instead. For \(u\ge7\) and \(k=\lceil\mathbb ET+u\sigma\rceil\), we have \(k-k_1\ge(u-2)\sigma-1\ge(u-7)\sigma\), since \(1/\sigma\le\sqrt{20}<5\). Applying the preceding bound to \(T\) and \(-T\) therefore gives
\[
 \Pr(|T-\mathbb ET|\ge u\sigma)
 \le\frac12\,3^{-(u-7)/13}\le4e^{-u/16}
 \qquad(u\ge7).
\]
The last inequality uses \(\log3>1\) and \(3^{7/13}<3\). For \(0\le u\le7\), the bound \(4e^{-u/16}>1\) makes the same estimate automatic. Tail integration now yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^4
 &=4\sigma^4\int_0^\infty u^3
           \Pr(|T-\mathbb ET|>u\sigma)\,du\\
 &\le16\sigma^4\int_0^\infty u^3e^{-u/16}\,du
   =96\cdot16^4v^2<2^{23}v^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]

Next let \(0<v<1/20\). For an independent copy \(T'\), integrality gives
\[
 2v=\mathbb E(T-T')^2\ge1-\sum_i a_i^2
       \ge1-\max_i a_i.
\]
Choose a mode \(z\), put \(a_*:=a_z\), and let \(\delta=1-a_*\). Then \(a_*\ge1-2v>9/10\) and \(\delta\le2v<1/10\). Each adjacent mass divided by \(a_*\) is at most \(\delta/a_*<1/9\). Log-concavity makes the outward ratios on each side no larger than its first outward ratio. Consequently, for every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
 a_{z+n}\le a_{z+1}9^{1-n},\qquad
 a_{z-n}\le a_{z-1}9^{1-n},
\]
with missing adjacent masses and their tails interpreted as zero. The elementary geometric-series derivative identity gives
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}n^4 9^{1-n}
 =\frac{1+11/9+11/81+1/729}{(1-1/9)^5}
 =\frac{17415}{4096}<8.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb E(T-z)^4\le8(a_{z-1}+a_{z+1})\le8\delta\le16v\). The inequality \(|x-y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) and Jensen's inequality then give
\[
 \mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^4
 \le8\bigl(\mathbb E(T-z)^4+|\mathbb ET-z|^4\bigr)
 \le16\mathbb E(T-z)^4\le256v.
\]
Together with (4), this proves (3) for all \(v\ge0\).

It remains to control the small-budget slice variance. Put \(t=B_1/m\ge1\) and \(q_*=B_1/(B_1+m)\). For \(q\le q_*\), equations (6) and (12) established in the proof of accepted fact \(5fa48a554f30f367\) give
\[
 v_2\le\sigma^2,\qquad
 v_1\ge\frac{\sigma^2}{7372800},\qquad
 \sigma^2=\frac{mq}{(1-q)^2}.
\]
Here \(\sigma^2\) is the same unconditioned variance of the sum of \(m\) independent geometric coordinates as in that fact. Hence \(v_2\le7372800v_1<2^{23}v_1\).

For \(q\ge q_*\), equation (11) established in accepted fact \(5fa48a554f30f367\) gives \(v_1\ge t^2/451584\). If \(m=2\), the slice-total support \([0,B_2]\cap\mathbb Z\) gives \(v_2\le B_2^2/4\le B_1^2/16\le t^2\). The bounded-support variance inequality follows directly from \(T^2\le B_2T\) and \(B_2\mathbb ET-(\mathbb ET)^2\le B_2^2/4\).

Suppose \(m\ge3\), and consider the original slack \(A_2=B_2-X_\Sigma\) in the slice \(Y=2\). Its unnormalized masses are \(H_a=\binom{B_2-a+m-1}{m-1}q^{B_2-a}\) for \(0\le a\le B_2\). For \(0\le a<B_2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{H_{a+1}}{H_a}
 &=\frac1q\frac{B_2-a}{B_2-a+m-1}\\
 &\le\frac{B_1+m}{B_1}\frac{B_1/2}{B_1/2+m-1}
   =:\rho=\frac{B_1+m}{B_1+2m-2}<1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(m-2\ge m/3\) and \(B_1+2m-2\le3B_1\), we have \(1-\rho\ge m/(9B_1)=1/(9t)\). Shifting the masses gives \(H_{a+k}\le\rho^kH_a\) whenever the two indices are supported, so \(\Pr(A_2\ge k\mid Y=2)\le\rho^k\). Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_2&=\operatorname{Var}(A_2\mid Y=2)
       \le\mathbb E[A_2^2\mid Y=2]\\
 &\le\sum_{k\ge1}(2k-1)\rho^k
   =\frac{\rho(1+\rho)}{(1-\rho)^2}
   \le162t^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
At \(B_2=0\), the same conclusion holds because \(A_2=0\). Combining this with the \(m=2\) case and the middle-slice lower bound proves \(v_2\le162\cdot451584v_1<2^{27}v_1\) in the upper chamber. Thus in both chambers,
\[
 v_2^2\le2^{54}v_1^2.
 \tag{5}
\]

For each height the conditional total law has masses proportional to \(\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}q^s\) on its finite integer interval. Its successive ratio is \(q(s+m)/(s+1)\), which is nonincreasing, so (3) applies to all three slice laws, including a one-point slice. Equations (1), (2), and (5) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{y=0}^2p_yv_y^2
 &=\varepsilon v_0^2+p_1v_1^2+\varepsilon v_2^2\\
 &\le(2^{74}+1+2^{54})v_1^2
 \le9(2^{74}+1+2^{54})\overline v^{\,2},\\
 \sum_{y=0}^2p_yv_y&=\overline v\le60\overline v^{\,2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Finally, conditional centering of \(U\), followed by (3), proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb EU^4
 &\le2^{23}\sum_{y=0}^2p_y(v_y^2+v_y)\\
 &\le2^{23}\bigl[9(2^{74}+1+2^{54})+60\bigr]
                      \overline v^{\,2}
 <2^{101}\overline v^{\,2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The power inequality is an exact numerical inequality, and all estimates used the exact original probabilities and budgets. This completes the asserted bound.
