## Statement

For every integer \(n\geq 2\), set
\[
d:=9n,\qquad m:=n+d=10n,\qquad
\alpha:=\frac{m}{m+1},\qquad R:=2^{1/d},
\qquad \Gamma:=\frac{(m+1)2^{d-1}}{d}.
\]
In coordinates
\[
(x,u,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d-1}\times\mathbb R,
\]
define
\[
D_n:=
\operatorname{conv}\left(
\bigl([-R,R]^n\times[-1,1]^{d-1}\bigr)\times\{-1\},
\{(0,0,m)\}\right),
\]
\[
H:=\{t\geq0\},\qquad A_n:=D_n\cap H.
\]
Then \(D_n\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body, its centroid is
\((0,0,0)\), the base coordinate of that centroid is in \(\mathbb Z^n\),
and \(\partial H\) contains the centroid. Its mixed masses are
\[
\mathsf M(D_n)=\frac{\Gamma}{2}(3^n+1),
\qquad
\mathsf M(A_n)=\Gamma\alpha^d.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\mathsf M(A_n)}{2^{-n}\mathsf M(D_n)}
=\frac{2^{n+1}\alpha^d}{3^n+1}
<2\left(\frac23\right)^n\longrightarrow0.
\]
For every \(1\leq r\leq n\), every rank-\((n-r)\) primitive sublattice
\(L\leq\mathbb Z^n\), and every \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\), one nevertheless has
\[
\sum_{z\in a+L}\operatorname{vol}_d((D_n)_z)
<2^{-r}\mathsf M(D_n).
\]
Thus no absolute \(c>0\) makes either alternative in the subgoal hold for
all \(n,d,D,H\).

Let
\[
g_A(x):=\operatorname{vol}_d((A_n)_x)^{1/d},
\qquad
L_s^A:=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:g_A(x)\geq s\}\quad(s>0).
\]
Every nonempty positive level is a centered cube. A positive-volume level
is lattice-bad, in the precise sense
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)>0
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
L_s^A+\mathbb Z^n\neq\mathbb R^n,
\]
if and only if its cube radius is strictly less than \(1/2\). All such bad
levels are certified by the one fixed rational quotient
\[
q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R,\qquad q(x)=x_1,
\]
and have the same quotient phase \(0\), whose lattice preimage is the
primitive coset
\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:z_1=0\}.
\]

Finally, with mixed measure given by counting measure in the first \(n\)
coordinates and Lebesgue measure in the last \(d\) coordinates, there is a
point
\[
y_\star=(0,0,t_\star)\in D_n\cap
(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)
\]
such that every closed halfspace \(G\) containing \(y_\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathsf M(D_n\cap G)\geq\frac14\mathsf M(D_n).
\]

## Proof

Put
\[
r=\frac{m-t}{m+1}.
\]
As \(t\) runs from the apex height \(m\) to the base height \(-1\), \(r\)
runs from \(0\) to \(1\), and
\[
D_n\cap\{t=m-(m+1)r\}
=r[-R,R]^n\times r[-1,1]^{d-1}. \tag{1}
\]
The convex hull of the compact base and the apex is compact. The base has
dimension \(m-1\) in the hyperplane \(t=-1\), and the apex is outside that
hyperplane, so \(D_n\) is full-dimensional.

The \((m-1)\)-volume of the section in (1) is a positive constant times
\(r^{m-1}\). Hence the mean of \(r\) under uniform \(m\)-dimensional
measure on \(D_n\) is
\[
\frac{\int_0^1 r^m\,dr}{\int_0^1 r^{m-1}\,dr}
=\frac{m}{m+1}=\alpha.
\]
Every section in (1) is centrally symmetric in \((x,u)\), while
\(t=m-(m+1)r\). Therefore the centroid is
\[
\left(0,0,m-(m+1)\alpha\right)=(0,0,0).
\]
Also, \(A_n\) consists exactly of the sections with \(0\leq r\leq\alpha\).
It is the apex-side homothetic copy of \(D_n\) with scale \(\alpha\), so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(A_n)=\alpha^m\operatorname{vol}_m(D_n).
\tag{2}
\]

For \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), write
\[
q(x):=\frac{\lVert x\rVert_\infty}{R}.
\]
At base coordinate \(x\), the allowable parameters for \(D_n\) are
\(q(x)\leq r\leq1\). At a fixed \(r\), the \(u\)-section has volume
\((2r)^{d-1}\), and \(\lvert dt\rvert=(m+1)\,dr\). Direct integration gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((D_n)_x)
=(m+1)2^{d-1}\int_{q(x)}^1r^{d-1}\,dr
=\Gamma\bigl(1-q(x)^d\bigr)_+, \tag{3}
\]
where \(v_+:=\max\{v,0\}\). Restricting instead to \(r\leq\alpha\) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((A_n)_x)
=\Gamma\bigl(\alpha^d-q(x)^d\bigr)_+. \tag{4}
\]

We next prove the strict inequality \(R\alpha<1\). For \(x>0\),
\(\log(1+x)\geq x-x^2/2\). Thus, using \(n\geq2\),
\[
d\log\left(1+\frac1m\right)
=9n\log\left(1+\frac1{10n}\right)
\geq\frac9{10}-\frac9{200n}
\geq\frac{351}{400}>\frac34.
\]
Moreover,
\[
e^{3/4}>1+\frac34+\frac12\left(\frac34\right)^2
=\frac{65}{32}>2,
\]
so \(\log2<3/4\). It follows that
\[
2^{1/d}=R<1+\frac1m=\alpha^{-1}, \tag{5}
\]
as claimed.

Since \(1<R<2\), the lattice points having positive \(D_n\)-fiber volume
are exactly
\[
S:=\{-1,0,1\}^n.
\]
Equation (3) gives mass \(\Gamma\) at \(z=0\). Every nonzero \(z\in S\)
has \(\lVert z\rVert_\infty=1\), and \(R^d=2\), so its mass is
\(\Gamma(1-R^{-d})=\Gamma/2\). Therefore
\[
\mathsf M(D_n)
=\Gamma+\frac{\Gamma}{2}(3^n-1)
=\frac{\Gamma}{2}(3^n+1). \tag{6}
\]
By (5), \(1/R>\alpha\). Thus every nonzero lattice fiber has zero
\(A_n\)-mass, whereas (4) gives mass \(\Gamma\alpha^d\) at \(0\).
Consequently,
\[
\mathsf M(A_n)=\Gamma\alpha^d, \tag{7}
\]
and (6)-(7) give
\[
\frac{\mathsf M(A_n)}{2^{-n}\mathsf M(D_n)}
=\frac{2^{n+1}\alpha^d}{3^n+1}
<\frac{2^{n+1}}{3^n}
=2\left(\frac23\right)^n\longrightarrow0. \tag{8}
\]
Given any proposed absolute \(c>0\), equation (8) makes the first
alternative fail for all sufficiently large \(n\).

It remains to rule out every slice in the second alternative. Fix
\(1\leq r\leq n\), a rank-\((n-r)\) sublattice \(L\leq\mathbb Z^n\),
and \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\). Put \(F=a+L\). There is a coordinate projection
onto \(n-r\) coordinates that is injective on
\(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}L\): choose a nonzero
\((n-r)\)-row minor of a matrix whose columns are a basis of that span.
The same projection is injective on \(F\). Its image sends \(F\cap S\)
into \(\{-1,0,1\}^{n-r}\), and hence
\[
\lvert F\cap S\rvert\leq3^{n-r}. \tag{9}
\]
This argument does not need primitivity, so it applies in particular to
all primitive slices in the statement.

If \(0\notin F\), every contributing fiber in \(F\) has mass
\(\Gamma/2\). Equations (6) and (9) yield
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in F}\operatorname{vol}_d((D_n)_z)}
{\mathsf M(D_n)}
\leq\frac{3^{n-r}}{3^n+1}
<3^{-r}<2^{-r}. \tag{10}
\]
If \(0\in F\), the central fiber has mass \(\Gamma\), and all other
contributing fibers have mass \(\Gamma/2\). Hence
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in F}\operatorname{vol}_d((D_n)_z)}
{\mathsf M(D_n)}
\leq\frac{3^{n-r}+1}{3^n+1}. \tag{11}
\]
The desired strict inequality in this case is equivalent to
\[
2^r(3^{n-r}+1)<3^n+1.
\]
The difference between the right side and the left side is
\[
3^{n-r}(3^r-2^r)-(2^r-1). \tag{12}
\]
For \(r=1\), (12) equals \(3^{n-1}-1>0\), because \(n\geq2\). For
\(r\geq2\), one has
\((3/2)^r\geq(3/2)^2>2\), and hence \(3^r>2^{r+1}\), so
\(3^r-2^r>2^r>2^r-1\); since \(3^{n-r}\geq1\), (12) is again positive.
This proves the strict dyadic bound for every proper affine lattice
slice.

We now separate positive levels from lattice-bad levels. From (4),
\[
g_A(x)^d
=\Gamma\left(\alpha^d-
\left(\frac{\lVert x\rVert_\infty}{R}\right)^d\right)_+.
\]
Put \(s_{\max}:=\Gamma^{1/d}\alpha\). For \(0<s\leq s_{\max}\),
\[
L_s^A=[-\ell_s,\ell_s]^n,\qquad
\ell_s:=R\left(\alpha^d-\frac{s^d}{\Gamma}\right)^{1/d}. \tag{13}
\]
For \(s>s_{\max}\), the level is empty. The levels with
\(0<s<s_{\max}\) have positive \(n\)-volume, while the top level
\(s=s_{\max}\) is the singleton \(\{0\}\) and has zero \(n\)-volume.
By (5), every nonempty positive level has
\[
0\leq\ell_s<R\alpha<1,
\]
and hence \(L_s^A\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{0\}\). This fact alone is not the
lattice-bad condition.

For any \(\ell\geq0\), the translates of
\([-\ell,\ell]^n\) by \(\mathbb Z^n\) cover \(\mathbb R^n\) if and only
if \(\ell\geq1/2\). Indeed, if \(\ell\geq1/2\), coordinatewise rounding
places every point of \(\mathbb R^n\) within \(\ell\) in
\(\ell_\infty\)-distance of an integer vector. If \(\ell<1/2\), the
point \((1/2,0,\ldots,0)\) is not in any integer translate of the cube.
Therefore a positive-volume level \(L_s^A\) is lattice-bad if and only if
\[
\ell_s<\frac12. \tag{14}
\]
Equality in (14) is deliberately excluded: the closed radius-\(1/2\)
cubes do cover.

Let \(E=\operatorname{span}(e_1)\), and identify \(E\) with
\(\mathbb R\). This is a fixed rational quotient direction, with
\[
P_E\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z e_1,\qquad
P_EL_s^A=[-\ell_s,\ell_s]e_1.
\]
For every bad level, (14) shows directly that
\[
P_EL_s^A+P_E\mathbb Z^n\neq E.
\]
Thus the same one-dimensional rational quotient certifies every bad
level. Since \(\ell_s<1/2\), the projected level meets the projected
lattice only at \(0\). Its full lattice preimage is always the same
phase coset
\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:z_1=0\}.
\]
The parent mixed mass of this coset is
\[
\frac{\Gamma}{2}(3^{n-1}+1)
=\frac{3^{n-1}+1}{3^n+1}\mathsf M(D_n)
<\frac12\mathsf M(D_n), \tag{15}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(n\geq2\). Hence exact synchronization
does not produce the dyadic rank-one threshold.

Finally, define the mixed measure \(\mu\) on \(D_n\) by
\[
\mu(B):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{(u,t):(z,u,t)\in B\}.
\]
Its total mass is \(\mathsf M(D_n)\). Let \(\nu\) be its \(t\)-marginal.
At height \(t=m-(m+1)r\), the conditional mixed section in \((z,u)\) is
\[
\left(\mathbb Z^n\cap[-Rr,Rr]^n\right)\times[-r,r]^{d-1}.
\]
Since \(1<R<2\), its counting-times-Lebesgue mass is
\[
w(t)=
\begin{cases}
(2r)^{d-1},&0\leq r<1/R,\\
3^n(2r)^{d-1},&1/R\leq r\leq1,
\end{cases}
\qquad r=\frac{m-t}{m+1}. \tag{16}
\]
Fubini gives
\[
\nu(J)=\int_Jw(t)\,dt
\]
for every Borel set \(J\subseteq[-1,m]\). Thus \(\nu\) is absolutely
continuous, in particular atomless. Its continuous distribution
function therefore has a median \(t_\star\in[-1,m]\) satisfying
\[
\nu([-1,t_\star])=\nu([t_\star,m])
=\frac12\mathsf M(D_n). \tag{17}
\]
The point \(y_\star=(0,0,t_\star)\) belongs to \(D_n\), by (1), and its
base coordinate is the lattice point \(0\).

It suffices first to consider a closed halfspace whose boundary contains
\(y_\star\). On the mixed support it has the form
\[
G=\{(z,u,t):
\langle a,z\rangle+\langle b,u\rangle+c(t-t_\star)\geq0\}. \tag{18}
\]
At every fixed \(t\), the conditional mixed section in (16), with its
counting-times-Lebesgue measure, is invariant under
\((z,u)\mapsto(-z,-u)\). Consequently, for every linear functional
\(\varphi(z,u)\),
\[
\mu_t\{\varphi\geq0\}\geq\frac12\mu_t(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{d-1}),
\tag{19}
\]
because the strict positive and strict negative parts have equal
conditional mass.

If \(c>0\), then for every \(t\geq t_\star\), the condition in (18) is
\[
\langle a,z\rangle+\langle b,u\rangle
\geq-c(t-t_\star),
\]
whose right side is nonpositive. The accepted set contains the
nonnegative half of the symmetric conditional section, so (19) and
(17) give
\[
\mathsf M(D_n\cap G)
\geq\frac12\nu([t_\star,m])
=\frac14\mathsf M(D_n). \tag{20}
\]
If \(c<0\), the same argument on \(t\leq t_\star\) gives (20). If \(c=0\),
(19) on every \(t\)-section gives the stronger lower bound
\(\mathsf M(D_n)/2\).

For an arbitrary closed halfspace containing \(y_\star\), move its
boundary parallel toward \(y_\star\) until the boundary contains
\(y_\star\). The resulting halfspace is a subset of the original one, so
the original halfspace has at least the mass in (20). Hence
\[
\inf_{\substack{G\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}\\y_\star\in G}}
\mathsf M(D_n\cap G)\geq\frac14\mathsf M(D_n),
\]
which proves the asserted absolute-constant mixed depth.
