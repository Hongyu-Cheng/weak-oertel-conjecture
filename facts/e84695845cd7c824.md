## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted fact ca061f9c44376a53.  In particular, \(a_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), the good parity classes \(\xi\) have masses
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}a_z,
\]
their canonical reservoir weights are
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi},
\]
and \(Q=\sum_er_e\).  For a good class \(\xi\) and an integer \(k\geq1\), define
\[
 \mathscr E_{k,\xi}:=
 \left\{\{u,v\}:u\ne v,\ a_ua_v>0,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2,
 \ v-u\in(2^k\mathbb Z)^n\right\},
\]
\[
 P_{k,\xi}:=\sum_{e\in\mathscr E_{k,\xi}}r_e,
 \qquad
 P_k:=\sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{good}}P_{k,\xi}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
 Q=P_1\geq P_2\geq P_3\geq\cdots\geq0,
 \qquad
 P_k=0\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }k.
\tag{2}
\]

There is an exact residue-class formula for every drop.  For
\(\eta\in(\mathbb Z/2^k\mathbb Z)^n\) with \(\eta\equiv\xi\pmod2\), put
\[
 A^{[k]}_{\xi,\eta}:=
 \sum_{\substack{z\equiv\xi\pmod2\\z\equiv\eta\pmod{2^k}}}a_z.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(\eta\mapsto\xi\) for reduction modulo \(2\), one has
\[
 \boxed{
 P_k-P_{k+1}
 =\sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{good}}\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \left(
 \sum_{\substack{\eta\bmod 2^k\\\eta\mapsto\xi}}
 (A^{[k]}_{\xi,\eta})^2
 -
 \sum_{\substack{\zeta\bmod 2^{k+1}\\\zeta\mapsto\xi}}
 (A^{[k+1]}_{\xi,\zeta})^2
 \right).}
\tag{4}
\]

For \(k\geq2\), let \(\mathscr P_{k,\xi}\subseteq\mathscr E_{k+1,\xi}\).  For
\(p=\{u,v\}\in\mathscr P_{k,\xi}\), put
\[
 T(p):=
 \left\{\frac{3u+v}{4},\frac{u+3v}{4}\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
If nonnegative weights \(A_p^{(k)}\) satisfy
\[
 0\leq A_p^{(k)}\leq r_{T(p)}-r_p,
\tag{6}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{good}}
 \sum_{p\in\mathscr P_{k,\xi}}A_p^{(k)}
 \leq P_k-P_{k+1}.}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently, for any finite set \(S\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}\), one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{k\in S}\sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{good}}
 \sum_{p\in\mathscr P_{k,\xi}}A_p^{(k)}
 \leq\sum_{k\in S}(P_k-P_{k+1})
 \leq P_2\leq Q.}
\tag{8}
\]
In the exact one-step normalization of accepted fact ca061f9c44376a53, the parents have difference in \((8\mathbb Z)^n\), the children have difference in \((4\mathbb Z)^n\), and
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak A\leq P_2-P_3.}
\tag{9}
\]

Now put
\[
 \mathcal H:=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{10}
\]
Retain from accepted fact ca061f9c44376a53
\[
 \mathcal H<\frac Q{100}\leq\Omega,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak J\geq\Omega-\mathfrak A-\mathfrak B,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B\leq R,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d\mathfrak B\leq D.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(K>e\).  If the proposed product-amplification absorption holds at factor \(K\), namely
\[
 \max\{R,G,R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C),M_{\mathrm{off}}(C),
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C),D\}
 \geq K\mathfrak A,
\tag{12}
\]
then the parent flux obeys the dimension-free estimate
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak J>
 \frac{\gamma_d-K^{-1}}{1+\gamma_d}\,\Omega
 >\frac{K-e}{K(e+1)}\,\Omega.}
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, factor \(4\) gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak J>\frac{4-e}{4(e+1)}\,\Omega.}
\tag{14}
\]

The threshold in (13) is sharp for deductions using only the scalar relations in (11) and (12).  More precisely, for every \(1<K<e\) those scalar relations admit data with \(\mathfrak J/\Omega\) arbitrarily small, and for \(K=e\) they admit a sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and \(\mathfrak J/\Omega\to0\).  Thus \(e\) is the infimum of the dimension-free absorption factors that force a positive parent-flux fraction from the accepted scalar ledger.  This scalar sharpness statement is not asserted to be a convex-body or canonical-KKT counterexample to (12).  Likewise, (4)-(9) are an amortized product charge, not a proof of a one-shot terminal absorption.

## Proof

The positive fiber support is finite.  For one good class \(\xi\), the condition
\(u\equiv v\pmod{2^k}\) partitions eligible pairs according to their common residue \(\eta\) modulo \(2^k\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{k,\xi}
 &=\frac1{4A_\xi}
 \sum_{\substack{\{u,v\}\subseteq Z_\xi\\u\ne v, u\equiv v\pmod{2^k}}}
 a_ua_v\\
 &=\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \left(
 \sum_{\substack{\eta\bmod 2^k\\\eta\mapsto\xi}}
 (A^{[k]}_{\xi,\eta})^2
 -\sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}a_z^2
 \right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
For \(k=1\), this is exactly the complete product-reservoir mass in that good class, so summing gives \(P_1=Q\).  The edge families decrease with \(k\), proving monotonicity.  Finiteness and \(u\ne v\) imply that no eligible difference is divisible by arbitrarily high powers of two, proving eventual vanishing.  Subtracting (15) at successive levels cancels the final sum of squares and proves (4).

Fix \(k\geq2\) and a good class \(\xi\).  If
\(p=\{u,v\}\in\mathscr E_{k+1,\xi}\), then (5) has integral endpoints.  Moreover, \((v-u)/4\in(2^{k-1}\mathbb Z)^n\subseteq(2\mathbb Z)^n\), so both child endpoints have parity \(\xi\), and their difference \((v-u)/2\) lies in \((2^k\mathbb Z)^n\).  Convexity gives positive child fibers.  Direct inversion,
\[
 u=\frac{3x-y}{2},
 \qquad
 v=\frac{3y-x}{2}
 \quad\text{when }T(p)=\{x,y\},
\tag{16}
\]
shows that \(T\) is injective.

Put \(U:=a_u^{1/d}\) and \(V:=a_v^{1/d}\).  Brunn-Minkowski at the two quarter points and weighted arithmetic-geometric mean give
\[
 a_x^{1/d}\geq\frac{3U+V}{4}\geq U^{3/4}V^{1/4},
 \qquad
 a_y^{1/d}\geq\frac{U+3V}{4}\geq U^{1/4}V^{3/4}.
\tag{17}
\]
Hence \(a_xa_y\geq a_ua_v\), and the common good-class denominator gives
\[
 r_{T(p)}-r_p\geq0.
\tag{18}
\]
Because all increments are nonnegative, enlarging \(\mathscr P_{k,\xi}\) to the full family \(\mathscr E_{k+1,\xi}\) can only increase their sum.  Injectivity and the inclusion
\(T(\mathscr E_{k+1,\xi})\subseteq\mathscr E_{k,\xi}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{p\in\mathscr P_{k,\xi}}A_p^{(k)}
 &\leq\sum_{p\in\mathscr E_{k+1,\xi}}
 (r_{T(p)}-r_p)\\
 &\leq P_{k,\xi}-P_{k+1,\xi}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Summing proves (7), and telescoping nonnegative drops proves (8).

For the actual weights of accepted fact ca061f9c44376a53, every child \(f\) has a distinct parent \(p(f)\in\mathscr E_{3,\xi}\), and its exact product formula is
\[
 0\leq A_f
 \leq\frac{\lambda_{m_f}\chi_{m_f}}{t_{m_f}}
 (r_f-r_{p(f)}).
\tag{20}
\]
Here \(0\leq\lambda_m\leq t_m\) and \(0<\chi_m\leq1\), so the coefficient in (20) is at most one whenever it is used.  Apply (7) with \(k=2\) and sum over the good classes.  This proves (9), including all source and target collisions because the estimate is on the original distinct edge coordinates.

It remains to prove the sharp scalar absorption threshold.  Let \(T\) be a terminal attaining the maximum in (12).  First suppose \(T=R\).  Equations (11)-(12) imply
\[
 \mathcal H\geq\max\{\mathfrak B,K\mathfrak A\}
 +\gamma_d\mathfrak B.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(K>\gamma_d\), the two regions
\(\mathfrak B\geq K\mathfrak A\) and
\(\mathfrak B\leq K\mathfrak A\) give, respectively,
\[
 \mathfrak A+\mathfrak B
 \leq\frac{1+K^{-1}}{1+\gamma_d}\,\mathcal H.
\tag{22}
\]
For completeness, in the first region use
\(\mathfrak A\leq\mathfrak B/K\).  In the second, the exact identity
\[
 (1+K^{-1})(K\mathfrak A+\gamma_d\mathfrak B)
 -(1+\gamma_d)(\mathfrak A+\mathfrak B)
 =(K-\gamma_d)\mathfrak A
 -(1-\gamma_d/K)\mathfrak B
\tag{23}
\]
is nonnegative because \(\mathfrak B\leq K\mathfrak A\).

If \(T=D\), then
\[
 \mathcal H\geq\mathfrak B+max\{\gamma_d\mathfrak B,K\mathfrak A\},
\]
and the analogous two-region calculation gives
\[
 \mathfrak A+\mathfrak B
 \leq\frac{1+\gamma_d/K}{1+\gamma_d}\,\mathcal H.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(T\) is one of the other four terminals, then
\[
 \mathcal H\geq(1+\gamma_d)\mathfrak B+K\mathfrak A.
\tag{25}
\]
The elementary bounds
\[
 \frac1e<\gamma_d\leq\frac12
\tag{26}
\]
follow from
\(2\leq(1+1/d)^d<e\).  Thus \(K>e\) implies
\(K>1/\gamma_d\geq1+\gamma_d\).  Equation (25) then gives
\[
 \mathfrak A+\mathfrak B\leq\frac1{1+\gamma_d}\mathcal H.
\tag{27}
\]
Among (22), (24), and (27), the coefficient in (22) is the largest.  Since \(\mathcal H<\Omega\), equation (11) now yields
\[
 \mathfrak J
 \geq\Omega-\mathfrak A-\mathfrak B
 >\left(1-\frac{1+K^{-1}}{1+\gamma_d}\right)\Omega
 =\frac{\gamma_d-K^{-1}}{1+\gamma_d}\Omega.
\tag{28}
\]
The last rational function is strictly increasing in \(\gamma_d\).  Using \(\gamma_d>1/e\) gives
\[
 \frac{\gamma_d-K^{-1}}{1+\gamma_d}
 >\frac{e^{-1}-K^{-1}}{1+e^{-1}}
 =\frac{K-e}{K(e+1)}.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (13), and substituting \(K=4\) proves (14).

Finally we verify scalar sharpness.  Normalize \(Q=100\) and \(\Omega=1\).  For \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), set
\[
 \mathfrak B:=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\gamma_d},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak A:=\frac{\mathfrak B}{K},
 \qquad
 R:=\mathfrak B,
 \qquad
 D:=\gamma_d\mathfrak B,
\tag{30}
\]
and set the other four terminals to zero.  Then
\[
 \mathcal H=1-\varepsilon<1=\frac Q{100}=\Omega,
 \qquad
 R=K\mathfrak A,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B=R,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d\mathfrak B=D.
\tag{31}
\]
Choose any nonnegative \(\mathfrak J\) strictly larger than
\[
 \left(
 1-\frac{(1+K^{-1})(1-\varepsilon)}{1+\gamma_d}
 \right)_+.
\tag{32}
\]
Then all scalar inequalities in (11), including the stronger consequence from accepted fact ca061f9c44376a53, are satisfied.  If \(1<K<e\), choose \(d\) large enough that \(K<1/\gamma_d\), then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); the right side of (32) becomes zero.  If \(K=e\), let first \(d\to\infty\), so \(\gamma_d\to1/e\), and then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); again (32) tends to zero.  This proves the claimed optimality of the threshold for the scalar ledger, while making no geometric realization claim.
