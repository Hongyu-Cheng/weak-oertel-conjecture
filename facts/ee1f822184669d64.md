## Statement

Let \(K_W,K_N\ge3\), and define the scalar constant
\[
c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)
:=2048K_N^2+512K_W^2(1+64K_N).
\]
This constant is distinct from the locked problem's convex body \(C\). The variables \(W,N\) below are real scalar random variables, not mixed-integer volumes. Latent information is denoted by the sigma-field \(\mathcal G\), and exponential moments by \(M_{\exp}\). In particular, \(W,N,\mathcal G\) have the scalar meanings used in 53b9054891b43df0; that fact's scalar exponential moment \(G\) is called \(M_{\exp}\) here, and its scalar regularity parameter \(K\) becomes \(K_W\). Its symmetric-noise theorem is not applied to the present noise.

Suppose \(W,N\) are jointly finitely supported, \(W\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EW&=0,&
\mathbb EW^4&\le K_W(\mathbb EW^2)^2,\\
\mathbb E[N\mid\mathcal G]&=0,&
\mathbb E[N^4\mid\mathcal G]
&\le K_N\bigl(\mathbb E[N^2\mid\mathcal G]\bigr)^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\end{aligned}
\]
No symmetry of the conditional noise law is assumed. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{\exp}:=\mathbb E e^{W+N}
&\ge\mathbb E e^W\ge1,\\
\operatorname{Var}(W+N)
&\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)M_{\exp},\\
\Pr(W+N\ge0)&\ge\frac1{16K_WK_N}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These conclusions include all zero-variance cases. For any finite collection of such representations carried by independent block experiments, their sums \(\sum_iW_i\) and \(\sum_iN_i\), conditioned on the joint latent information, satisfy the same hypotheses with the same constants. Consequently the same three bounds hold for \(\sum_i(W_i+N_i)\), independently of the number of blocks.

Here is the vector cap consumer. Let \(b\ge1\), let \(m_i\ge1\), and let \(\nu_i\) be probability laws on finite nonempty supports \(D_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{m_i}\), with fixed means \(\mathbf j_i\). Assume that for every \(\mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i}\) the scalar law of \(\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)\) under \(\nu_i\) admits a representation \(W_i+N_i\) satisfying the preceding hypotheses with the same \(K_W,K_N\). Each representation and its latent information may depend on its block direction. For
\(Q:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^b\nu_i\), \(J:=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_b)\), and
\(T_{\mathbf u}:=\sum_i\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q(T_{\mathbf u}\ge0)&\ge\frac1{16K_WK_N},\\
\operatorname{Var}_Q(T_{\mathbf u})
&\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)\mathbb E_Qe^{T_{\mathbf u}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every proper closed halfspace containing \(J\) has original product probability at least \(\delta:=1/(16K_WK_N)\). The symbols \(Q,J,\nu_i,D_i,m_i\) have the product-law and mean meanings of 558e5cac7133593e. In its actual-depth convention, \(\tau_Q(J):=\inf_{H\ni J}Q(H)\), with the infimum over proper closed halfspaces, satisfies \(\tau_Q(J)\ge\delta\). The halfspace inequality also holds when a mean is absent from its finite support.

For the likelihood consumer, let \(p\) be a positive probability law on a finite set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), let \(E\subseteq A\) be nonempty, and let \(\nu\) be positive on \(E\) with mean \(\mathbf j\). Suppose that a scalar function \(t:E\to\mathbb R\) and an attenuation factor \(\rho>0\) obey the exact identity \(p_{\mathbf x}=\rho\nu(\mathbf x)e^{t(\mathbf x)}\) on \(E\), and the law of \(T:=t(\mathbf X)\) under \(\nu\) admits the preceding scalar representation. Set \(q:=p(E)\), \(M:=\mathbb E_\nu e^T\), and \(V:=\operatorname{Var}_\nu[\log(\nu(\mathbf X)/p_{\mathbf X})]\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=q/M,&M&\ge1,\\
V&\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)M,&
\rho^2V&\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)q^2/M
\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the exact face-attenuation setting of 53b9054891b43df0, the aliases are \(E=E_{\mathbf j}\), \(\nu=\nu_{\mathbf j}\), \(\rho=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\), \(t(\mathbf x)=(\boldsymbol\ell-\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathbf j})\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)\), \(T=T_{\mathbf j}\), \(M=G_{\mathbf j}\), \(q=q_{\mathbf j}\), and \(V=V_{\mathbf j}\), with their unchanged source and row probabilities. The displayed price is therefore available whenever that row's scalar direction admits the present representation. No existence claim for such representations, and no application to other rows, is part of this theorem.

## Proof

We first record two scalar estimates with their zero cases. Let \(Z\) be a finitely supported centered real random variable satisfying \(\mathbb EZ^4\le K(\mathbb EZ^2)^2\), where \(K\ge3\), and put \(s^2:=\mathbb EZ^2\). If \(s=0\), then \(Z=0\) almost surely, so \(\Pr(Z\ge0)=1\). If \(s>0\), Hölder's inequality with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
s^2&\le(\mathbb E|Z|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Z|^4)^{1/3},\\
\mathbb E|Z|&\ge\frac{s^3}{\sqrt{\mathbb EZ^4}}
\ge\frac{s}{\sqrt K},\\
\mathbb EZ_+&=\frac12\mathbb E|Z|
\ge\frac{s}{2\sqrt K},
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(Z_+:=\max\{Z,0\}\). Cauchy--Schwarz now gives
\(s/(2\sqrt K)\le\mathbb EZ_+\le s\sqrt{\Pr(Z\ge0)}\), hence
\(\Pr(Z\ge0)\ge1/(4K)\). This is the centered scalar closed-cap estimate in the proof of 558e5cac7133593e, with its \(\kappa\) replaced by the scalar regularity parameter \(K\); the variable \(Z\) here is its scalar projection \(T\).

For the positive-threshold estimate, put \(a:=s/(4\sqrt K)>0\). The contribution to \(\mathbb EZ_+\) from \(\{Z<a\}\) is at most \(a\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{s}{4\sqrt K}
&\le\mathbb E[Z_+\mathbf1_{\{Z\ge a\}}]
\le s\sqrt{\Pr(Z\ge a)},\\
\Pr\left(Z\ge\frac{s}{4\sqrt K}\right)
&\ge\frac1{16K},\\
\mathbb Ee^Z
&\ge\frac{e^{s/(4\sqrt K)}}{16K}
\ge\frac{s^2}{512K^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(e^x\ge x^2/2\) for \(x\ge0\). This rederives the regular-center exponential estimate in the proof of 53b9054891b43df0 without using its noise symmetry. The conclusion \(s^2\le512K^2\mathbb Ee^Z\) holds also when \(s=0\).

These Hölder and Cauchy--Schwarz arguments also hold with conditional expectations given any sigma-field. In particular, if a finitely supported variable is conditionally centered and satisfies the fourth-moment inequality with the same constant, then the closed-cap estimate holds almost surely. On the set of positive conditional standard deviation, the positive-threshold estimate holds almost surely with that conditional standard deviation as its threshold parameter. On a set of conditional second moment zero, the variable is zero conditionally almost surely, so the closed-cap probability is one. No finiteness of the latent sigma-field is required.

We now prove the nonsymmetric scalar exponential price. Conditional centering and the measurability of \(W\) give \(\mathbb EN=\mathbb EWN=0\). Consequently \(\operatorname{Var}(W+N)=\mathbb EW^2+\mathbb EN^2\).
Conditional Jensen gives \(\mathbb E[e^N\mid\mathcal G]\ge1\), and ordinary Jensen gives \(\mathbb Ee^W\ge1\). Therefore
\(M_{\exp}=\mathbb E[e^W\mathbb E(e^N\mid\mathcal G)]\ge\mathbb Ee^W\ge1\).
The regular-center estimate just proved yields
\(\mathbb EW^2\le512K_W^2M_{\exp}\).

Put \(\sigma_N:=\sqrt{\mathbb E[N^2\mid\mathcal G]}\), a nonnegative \(\mathcal G\)-measurable variable. Split latent configurations into
\(B:=\{\sigma_N\ge8\sqrt{K_N}|W|\}\) and \(B^c\). On \(B\cap\{\sigma_N>0\}\), the conditional positive-threshold estimate gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\left(N\ge\frac{\sigma_N}{4\sqrt{K_N}}
          \,\middle|\,\mathcal G\right)&\ge\frac1{16K_N},\\
e^W\mathbb E[e^N\mid\mathcal G]
&\ge\frac1{16K_N}
       e^{W+\sigma_N/(4\sqrt{K_N})}\\
&\ge\frac1{16K_N}e^{\sigma_N/(8\sqrt{K_N})}
\ge\frac{\sigma_N^2}{2048K_N^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(W\ge-|W|\) and the definition of \(B\) give the second exponential lower bound. The same final inequality is automatic on \(B\cap\{\sigma_N=0\}\), because its right side is zero. On \(B^c\), \(\sigma_N^2\le64K_NW^2\). Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EN^2
&=\mathbb E\sigma_N^2
\le2048K_N^2M_{\exp}+64K_N\mathbb EW^2,\\
\operatorname{Var}(W+N)
&\le2048K_N^2M_{\exp}+(1+64K_N)\mathbb EW^2\\
&\le\bigl[2048K_N^2+512K_W^2(1+64K_N)\bigr]M_{\exp}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the advertised variance constant. The argument controlled the conditional second moment on latent configurations by a one-sided conditional positive tail. It used neither a conditional symmetric pairing nor a \(\cosh\) identity. If \(\mathbb EW^2=0\), then \(W=0\) almost surely and the same proof still applies. If the total variance is zero, the variance identity forces \(W=N=0\) almost surely, so \(M_{\exp}=1\). All moment quantities used above are finite because \(W,N\) have finite supports.

For the scalar cap, the closed-cap estimate gives \(\Pr(W\ge0)\ge1/(4K_W)\). Its conditional version gives \(\Pr(N\ge0\mid\mathcal G)\ge1/(4K_N)\) almost surely, including latent configurations with zero conditional variance. Since \(\{W\ge0\}\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(W+N\ge0)
&\ge\Pr(W\ge0,N\ge0)\\
&=\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbf1_{\{W\ge0\}}
                  \Pr(N\ge0\mid\mathcal G)\bigr]\\
&\ge\frac1{4K_N}\Pr(W\ge0)
\ge\frac1{16K_WK_N}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the two applications of the scalar positive-cap estimate cost exactly the stated product of constants; no noise symmetry is needed.

Consider next independent block experiments carrying \((W_i,N_i,\mathcal G_i)\), for \(1\le i\le b\). Set \(U:=\sum_iW_i\), \(R:=\sum_iN_i\), and let \(\mathcal G\) be the sigma-field generated by all \(\mathcal G_i\). The centers \(W_i\) are independent and centered. If \(v_i:=\mathbb EW_i^2\), the fourth-power expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EU&=0,&\mathbb EU^2&=\sum_iv_i,\\
\mathbb EU^4
&=\sum_i\mathbb EW_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h\\
&\le K_W\sum_iv_i^2+6\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h
\le K_W\left(\sum_iv_i\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every omitted mixed term has an independent centered factor to the first power. The last inequality uses \(K_W\ge3\). It remains valid when some or all \(v_i\) vanish.

Conditional on \(\mathcal G\), the noises are independent, and their conditional law in block \(i\) is the law given \(\mathcal G_i\). Explicitly, for bounded functions \(f_i\), independence of the full block experiments yields
\(\mathbb E[\prod_i f_i(N_i)\mid\mathcal G]
=\prod_i\mathbb E[f_i(N_i)\mid\mathcal G_i]\).
This identity follows by testing against products of bounded \(\mathcal G_i\)-measurable functions; both sides have the same integrals by independence. Bounded monotone limits then extend the test functions to the generated sigma-field. In particular, \(\mathbb E[R\mid\mathcal G]=0\). Put \(a_i:=\mathbb E[N_i^2\mid\mathcal G_i]\). The same expansion conditionally gives, almost surely,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[R^2\mid\mathcal G]&=\sum_i a_i,\\
\mathbb E[R^4\mid\mathcal G]
&=\sum_i\mathbb E[N_i^4\mid\mathcal G_i]
  +6\sum_{i<h}a_i a_h\\
&\le K_N\sum_i a_i^2+6\sum_{i<h}a_i a_h
\le K_N\left(\sum_i a_i\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(K_N\ge3\), and conditional centering removes every other term, without any assumption on conditional third moments. The sum \(U\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable. Thus \((U,R,\mathcal G)\) satisfies the scalar theorem's hypotheses with unchanged constants. Applying the already proved scalar price and the two cap estimates proves all the assertions for independent scalar block sums.

For the vector consumer, fix the block directions \(\mathbf u_i\). Choose the assumed representation of the corresponding scalar law in each block, and realize these block representations independently. The law of their sum is exactly the law of \(T_{\mathbf u}\) under the original product \(Q\), since each scalar law was represented exactly and the blocks are independent. The scalar sum result therefore gives both displayed directional inequalities. The concatenated mean \(J\) was fixed by the original block laws before the directions were chosen.

A proper closed halfspace containing \(J\) can be written as \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge c\}\), where \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\) and \(c\le\mathbf u\cdot J\). Partitioning \(\mathbf u\) into its block normals, the event \(T_{\mathbf u}\ge0\) is contained in \(H\), because it is the corresponding halfspace with boundary through \(J\). Hence \(Q(H)\ge\delta\), and taking the infimum gives the actual-depth bound in the convention of 558e5cac7133593e. If all chosen block normals vanish, the scalar event has probability one and its variance is zero. None of these arguments requires the means to be support atoms, or replaces the original product law by a center law.

Finally, in the likelihood setting, summing the exact attenuation identity over \(E\) gives \(q=\rho M\). Under \(\nu\), \(T=W+N\) is centered by the scalar hypotheses, so \(M\ge1\). On every positive row atom the attenuation identity also gives \(\log(\nu(\mathbf x)/p_{\mathbf x})=-\log\rho-t(\mathbf x)\). Therefore \(V=\operatorname{Var}_\nu T\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)M\), and \(\rho^2V\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)q^2/M\le c_{\mathrm{reg}}(K_W,K_N)\), using \(0<q\le1\) and \(M\ge1\). The listed aliases identify this calculation with the exact source-face attenuation of 53b9054891b43df0 whenever the additional scalar representation holds. The calculation retains the original face mass \(q\) and does not infer the representation's existence from attenuation.
