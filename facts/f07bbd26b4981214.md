## Statement

For integer $d\ge2$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, set $V=1/d!$, $a=1+\varepsilon^2$, and define
$$
A=\{x:x_i\ge0,\ \varepsilon\le\textstyle\sum_i x_i\le1\},\qquad
P=\{x:x_i\le a,\ \textstyle\sum_i x_i\ge\varepsilon\}.
$$
For every $d$-simplex $R\supseteq A$ and compact convex $L\supseteq R\cup P$, their ordinary $d$-volumes satisfy
$$
(|R|-|A|)+2^{-d}(|L|-|P|)\ge\frac{\varepsilon V}{16d^2}.
$$

## Proof

If $\Delta=|R|-|A|\ge \varepsilon V/(16d^2)$, the assertion follows since $L\supseteq P$. Otherwise set $\eta=\Delta/V$ and $\delta=\eta/(1-\varepsilon^{d-1})$. Then $\delta\le2\eta<\varepsilon/(8d^2)$ and $d\delta<\varepsilon/16$.

For a point $x\in R$ with $x_i<0$, the pyramid from $x$ over the full facet of $A$ in $x_i=0$ lies in $R$ and has interior outside $A$. Its volume is $V(1-\varepsilon^{d-1})(-x_i)$, using the facet area $(1-\varepsilon^{d-1})/(d-1)!$. Thus $x_i\ge-\delta$. The same pyramid argument over the top facet $\sum_i x_i=1$, of area $\sqrt d/(d-1)!$, gives $\sum_i x_i\le1+\eta\le1+\delta$ on $R$.

Since $e_i\in A$, select a vertex $v_i$ of $R$ with $(v_i)_i\ge1$ for each $i$. Two such choices cannot coincide because a vertex with two coordinates at least one would have sum at least $2-(d-2)\delta>1+\delta$. These $d$ vertices have sums at least $1-(d-1)\delta>\varepsilon$. A vertex $v_0$ minimizing the sum has sum at most $\varepsilon$ because $\varepsilon e_1\in A$. It is distinct from all $v_i$, so these are all the vertices of $R$.

For each $i$, express $\varepsilon e_i=\sum_{k=0}^d\alpha_kv_k$ with $\alpha_k\ge0$ and $\sum_k\alpha_k=1$. Its coordinate sum gives
$$
\varepsilon\ge-d\delta\alpha_0+[1-(d-1)\delta](1-\alpha_0)
=(1+\delta)(1-\alpha_0)-d\delta.
$$
Hence $\alpha_0>0$ and $(1-\alpha_0)/\alpha_0\le(\varepsilon+d\delta)/(1-\varepsilon-d\delta)$. Fix $j$ and use the representation with $i\ne j$. The zero $j$th coordinate of $\varepsilon e_i$ and the lower bounds on the other vertices give $(v_0)_j\le\delta(1-\alpha_0)/\alpha_0$. Summing yields $\sum_j(v_0)_j\le d\delta(\varepsilon+d\delta)/(1-\varepsilon-d\delta)<\varepsilon^2/4<\varepsilon/2$.

The facet of $P$ in $\sum_i x_i=\varepsilon$ has area $\sqrt d(da-\varepsilon)^{d-1}/(d-1)!$ by the reflection $x\mapsto a\mathbf1-x$. Its pyramid with apex $v_0$ lies in $L$, has interior outside $P$, and has volume $V(da-\varepsilon)^{d-1}(\varepsilon-\sum_j(v_0)_j)$. Therefore $2^{-d}(|L|-|P|)\ge\varepsilon V(da-\varepsilon)^{d-1}/2^{d+1}\ge\varepsilon V/8$, using $da-\varepsilon>1$ for $d=2$ and $da-\varepsilon>2$ for $d\ge3$. This is stronger than the desired bound in this case.
