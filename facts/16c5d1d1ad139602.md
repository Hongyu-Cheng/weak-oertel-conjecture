## Statement

Use the radial path and notation of the finite radial cap-budget criterion, but allow each cap trace to be proper, strictly full or strictly empty. Write its centered inequality as $u_i\cdot(x-q(z))\ge\tau_{iz}$ and its old volume as $C_{iz}$. Let $S_{iz}$ be the proper parallel section area divided by $\lVert u_i\rVert$, or zero for strict full and empty traces. The derivative of the total cap mass is $\sum_z B_{iz}\sigma_z$, where $B_{iz}=C_{iz}+\tau_{iz}S_{iz}/d$. In particular $w\cdot\sigma=0$, $A\sigma>0$, and $B\sigma<0$ give fixed total mass, strict compatibility, and strict cap-budget decreases for small positive time. The weak first-order system with $B\sigma\le0$ has the same Farkas alternative with $B$ in place of $C$.

If the selected traces cover every whole fiber plane, coverage persists exactly. If instead the cap margins $L_i$ give $\min_{z,x\in K_z}\max_i L_i(z,x)>0$, coverage persists under sufficiently small Hausdorff perturbations.

## Proof

For a fixed trace put $T(\tau)=|(K_z-q(z))\cap\{u_i\cdot x\ge\tau\}|$. Proper parallel section areas vary continuously, for instance by Brunn–Minkowski concavity of their $(d-1)$-volume roots on the open support interval, with the constant section count for $d=1$. Fubini gives $T'(\tau_{iz})=-S_{iz}$. A radial factor $r$ gives trace volume $r^d T(\tau_{iz}/r)$, with derivative $dC_{iz}+\tau_{iz}S_{iz}$ at $r=1$. The derivative of $r_z(t)$ is $\sigma_z/d$. Strict full or empty traces retain their status by continuity, so their formula also follows. Sum over the finitely many fibers. The radial criterion's support and mass calculation is unaffected by the cap status. Negative derivatives give finite cap decrease, and the same finite Farkas calculation applies to the weak first-order system. A zero cap derivative alone gives no finite nonincrease conclusion.

Plane coverage is independent of the fiber shape. For the strict margin condition, each $L_i$ is uniformly Lipschitz in the continuous coordinates, so the maximum retains a positive lower bound when each new fiber lies in a sufficiently small neighborhood of its old fiber.
