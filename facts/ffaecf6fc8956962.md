## Statement

Take bases $0,1$ with target $1/2$. Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a centrally symmetric compact convex body about zero and $L\supseteq K$ a compact convex body, $d\ge1$. The raw depth at continuous mark $0$ for the sum of the two unnormalized source measures is $|K|$, which is also the maximum at this target base over $(K+L)/2$. In particular interpolation of the arrays $(K,L)$ and $(K,L')$ for $K=[-1,1]^d$, $L=[-3,3]^d$, $L'=[-4,4]\times[-3,3]^{d-1}$ gives equality of the fixed-base maximum-depth root throughout, although $L$ grows by proper inclusion. For $d\ge2$ the changed shapes are nonhomothetic; for every $d\ge1$ the two arrays have no common homothety ratio.

## Proof

A pure-base cap through $1/2$ containing only the first source gives depth at most $|K|$ at every mark. Every nonvertical closed cap through $(1/2,0)$ has thresholds $h,-h$ on the two sources in its unit continuous direction. Replace $L$ by its subset $K$ for a lower bound. By reflection of $K$, the second cap of threshold $-h$ has the same volume as the complementary lower cap at $h$ in $K$. Their common hyperplane has zero $d$-volume, so their total is $|K|$, including supporting, empty and full cases. A pure-base through-point cap captures at least one whole source, and any containing cap contains its parallel through-point cap. Hence the lower bound holds at zero, which belongs to the midpoint section.

Both displayed larger boxes contain $K$, and so does each interpolation. The maximum raw depth is always $2^d$, making the root inequality an equality. For $d\ge2$ their side-length ratios are different in the first and remaining directions. In all dimensions the unchanged nondegenerate $K$ would force the common scale factor to be one, inconsistent with the changed volume.
