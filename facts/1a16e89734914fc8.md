## Statement

Under the explicit spatial smallness and midpoint-simplex hypotheses, any finite family of independently oriented simplex partner stars has a full-dimensional convex realization whose integer fibers are exactly the prescribed origin body, negative-unit outer bodies, and nonzero binary-cube simplex fibers of one common volume. The proof computes exact exposed-fiber depths and gives all-direction suppression bounds for higher cube fibers. Moreover, IF a finite strict bad positive fan exists for the stated source-simplex hitting model with |B|<=V, source volumes>=2^(-d)V, weights totaling V-|B|, and all fan scores<Gamma_d V, THEN the proof constructs an actual full-volume mixed-integer body with normalized maximum depth exactly Gamma_d M/[2^n M+(V-M)]<Gamma_d/2^n. No such bad fan is asserted to exist, and no converse reduction from general nonattainment is claimed.

## Proof

# Exact cube completion of independently oriented simplex stars

Research note, 6 November 2026. This is a realization and exclusion theorem,
not a proof of OC. It shows that arbitrary orientations of prescribed
simplex partners do not by themselves force a nonsimplex or larger-volume
high core. The exclusion conclusion is exact for finite positive-volume
sources. The distinction between strict support gaps and contact directions
is retained throughout.

## 1. Data and theorem

Fix n,d>=1, a convex d-body B, and M>0. For i=1,...,n let C_i be a d-simplex
of volume M and let A_i be a convex d-body. Choose p_i in A_i and suppose

    (A_i+C_i)/2 subset B.                                      (1)

Set P_i=(p_i+C_i)/2, a simplex of volume M/2^d contained in B, and let
b_i be its centroid. Let D be the Euclidean closed unit ball. Choose r>0
such that

    b_i+rD subset P_i              for every i.                (2)

Such an r exists because there are finitely many full-dimensional P_i.
Assume the actual spatial smallness condition

    A_i subset p_i+epsilon D,       0<epsilon<r/2.              (3)

Small volume alone is NOT asserted to imply (3).

For every nonempty I subset {1,...,n}, let z_I=sum_(i in I)e_i. For
|I|=1 put K_I=C_i. For |I|=k>=2, choose any index rho(I) in I once and
for all, and put

    K_I=2P_rho+(k-1)b_rho-sum_(i in I)p_i.                      (4)

Here 2P denotes dilation about the coordinate origin. In fact 2P_rho
equals p_rho+C_rho, so each K_I is a TRANSLATE of an original C_rho.
Every K_I is therefore a simplex of volume M, with no newly introduced
shape or orientation. Define the full ambient convex body

    C=conv[({0} times B)
           union bigcup_(I nonempty)({z_I} times K_I)
           union bigcup_i({-e_i} times A_i)].                    (5)

Then its integer base support is exactly

    {0,1}^n union {-e_1,...,-e_n},                               (6)

and all displayed fibers in (5) are exact. In particular C_0=B; every
nonzero core fiber is an ordinary extremal simplex of the SAME volume M,
regardless of the orientations of the original C_i.

For y in B and a unit continuous normal u, write

    h_i=h_(P_i)(u), t=u.y, a_i=|A_i|,
    J_epsilon(y,u)={i:h_i>t-epsilon/2}.

There is an ambient closed halfspace through (0,y) whose mixed volume is
at most

    |B intersect {u.x>=t}|+sum_(i in J_epsilon(y,u))a_i.         (7)

Every higher core K_I, |I|>=2, is excluded completely by that halfspace.
Consequently the actual raw mixed depth at (0,y) is at most the infimum
of (7) over unit u.

There is also the more precise upper bound

    depth_C(0,y)<=|B intersect {u.x>=t}|
      +sum_(i:h_i>t)a_i
      +sum_(i:h_i<=t)|C_i intersect
                       {u.x>=h_(C_i)(u)-epsilon}|.              (8)

No conclusion that the last error is smaller than total outside mass is
claimed. In particular a small-volume endpoint can have a partner whose
boundary cap at height epsilon is much larger than that endpoint.

## 2. Integer census and exposed nonzero bases

The projection of C is

    Q=conv({0,1}^n union {-e_1,...,-e_n}).

Its coordinates lie in [-1,1]. If an integer base has coordinate -1 at i,
all generators in a convex representation must minimize that coordinate.
The unique such generator is -e_i, so the base is -e_i. Otherwise all
integer coordinates are 0 or 1. This proves (6).

Each -e_i is exposed by the functional -z_i. Each nonzero cube vertex
z_I is exposed by a functional having coefficient 1 on I and -a on its
complement, where 0<a<1. Among cube vertices its unique maximizer is z_I,
of value |I|. At a negative generator its value is either -1 or a, both
strictly smaller. Thus every nonzero displayed base is exposed, and its
fiber equals its prescribed generator fiber exactly.

## 3. Exact origin fiber and full dimension

For every |I|=k>=2, equations (2)-(4) give

    K_I+sum_(i in I)A_i
      subset 2P_rho+(k-1)b_rho+k epsilon D
      subset 2P_rho+(k-1)P_rho
      =(k+1)P_rho subset (k+1)B,                               (9)

because k epsilon<(k-1)r. For k=1, the corresponding inclusion is (1).

Consider any convex combination of generators in (5) having integer
coordinate zero. Let lambda_I be its coefficients on positive cube bases,
mu_i those on negative bases, and lambda_0 the coefficient on zero. The
base equations force

    mu_i=sum_(I containing i)lambda_I,
    lambda_0+sum_(I nonempty)(|I|+1)lambda_I=1.                 (10)

Choose the represented points x_I in K_I, a_i' in A_i, and x_0 in B.
Its continuous coordinate is exactly

    lambda_0 x_0
      +sum_I lambda_I (x_I+sum_(i in I)a_i').                   (11)

By (9), or (1) for singletons, each parenthesis belongs to (|I|+1)B.
Equation (10) and convexity imply that (11) belongs to B. Conversely the
whole generator fiber B is present at zero. Hence C_0=B exactly.

The set is compact and convex. It has nonempty (n+d)-dimensional interior:
take x_0 in int B, d independent sufficiently short continuous displacements
inside B at base0, and one generator point at each of e_1,...,e_n. These
n+d+1 points are affinely independent, so their simplex inside C has
positive ambient volume. Thus C is a convex body in the stipulated sense.

## 4. Exact exclusion of the completed high fibers

Consider an ambient halfspace

    H={ (z,x): alpha.z+u.x>=t },                               (12)

which passes through (0,y). For a chosen set J of paid outer indices, set

    alpha_i=p_i.u-t-R             if i in J,
    alpha_i=p_i.u-t+epsilon       if i notin J.                 (13)

For R large enough, each paid A_i lies wholly in H and its unit partner
C_i lies wholly outside H. For every unpaid i, its outer threshold is
p_i.u+epsilon, so the intersection with A_i has zero d-volume: it is
empty or a supporting face, by (3).

First choose J=J_epsilon(y,u). If i is unpaid, h_i<=t-epsilon/2, and its
unit partner threshold is

    t-alpha_i=2t-p_i.u-epsilon
                >=2h_i-p_i.u=h_(C_i)(u).                      (14)

Its cap therefore has zero d-volume. Thus the unit/outer pairs contribute
exactly sum_(i in J)a_i, up to zero-volume supporting faces.

If a higher subset I contains a paid index, its top value in (12) tends
to minus infinity with R, so it is entirely excluded for sufficiently
large R. There are finitely many I, permitting one common finite R.
If I contains no paid index, put k=|I| and rho=rho(I). Then rho is unpaid,
so h_rho<=t. Equation (2) gives u.b_rho<=h_rho-r<=t-r. Hence

    h_(K_I)(u)+sum_(i in I)alpha_i
      =2h_rho+(k-1)u.b_rho-kt+k epsilon
      <=t-(k-1)r+k epsilon<t.                                 (15)

This excludes K_I completely. Equations (14)-(15) prove (7), including
all closed-boundary cases.

For (8), instead choose J={i:h_i>t} and keep (13). Unpaid higher subsets
still satisfy (15). The only additional contributions are unpaid unit
partner caps. Their threshold obeys

    2t-p_i.u-epsilon>=h_(C_i)(u)-epsilon,

which gives exactly the error in (8). The same finite large R excludes
every other generator fiber. Thus (8) is also an actual halfspace bound,
not merely a formal sum of independently optimized pair costs.

## 5. Simultaneous finite-fan consequence

Suppose finitely many continuous test cuts (y_j,u_j), j=1,...,m, are
specified, with |u_j|=1, and every missed source has a strict margin:

    h_(P_i)(u_j)<=u_j.y_j-g
      whenever h_(P_i)(u_j)<=u_j.y_j,                           (16)

for some common g>0. If epsilon<min(r/2,2g), then for every test cut
J_epsilon(y_j,u_j) is exactly its strict-hit set
{i:h_(P_i)(u_j)>u_j.y_j}. Equation (7) therefore realizes the exact local
hitting score at every one of those cuts, while excluding every higher
core. The integer coefficients may depend on j, as halfspace depth allows.
All these cuts apply to the SAME convex body (5); there is no re-selection
of K_I by direction or by test point.

For a positive fan with a common y, the resulting full ambient normals
need not form a positive fan in R^(n+d). Such a balancing assertion is not
needed for the displayed upper bounds and is not claimed.

If a missed source touches its test hyperplane, condition (16) fails.
The present theorem retains that case through (7) or (8), but does not
pretend it contributes zero in an arbitrarily weighted atomic limit.
Replicating many small endpoints may also accumulate the errors in (8);
no uniform-in-n or fixed-total-mass vanishing assertion is made.

## 6. Every exposed fiber has exactly its ordinary raw depth

More generally, in any bounded finite-fiber convex source, let z be an
exposed projection vertex and x belong to its full-dimensional fiber K.
The raw mixed depth of (z,x) equals its ordinary raw K-depth. The lower
bound follows because each containing ambient halfspace includes an
ordinary containing halfspace of K (or all of K). For the reverse bound,
fix any nonzero continuous normal u and an exposing integer-coordinate
functional beta uniquely maximized at z. In the through-(z,x) halfspace
with normal (L beta,u), every other integer fiber is excluded once L is
large: there are finitely many bases with positive exposing gaps, and all
continuous fibers are bounded. The remaining mass is exactly the chosen
ordinary cap of K. Taking the infimum proves equality.

For completeness, the ordinary maximum raw depth of a d-simplex T of
volume M is Gamma_d M. At any x in T, some barycentric coordinate
lambda_j(x) is at least 1/(d+1). The containing vertex cap
{lambda_j>=lambda_j(x)} has volume (1-lambda_j(x))^d M<=Gamma_d M,
because it is a homothetic simplex of linear ratio 1-lambda_j(x). This
proves the upper bound at every point, including boundary points. At
the centroid, the ordinary Gruenbaum theorem gives the lower bound
Gamma_d M for every containing halfspace, proving equality.

Applied to (5), every nonzero core therefore has maximum raw depth
Gamma_d M. Every outer fiber has maximum raw
depth at most a_i/2: the two opposite cuts through any point have total
mass a_i, since their common proper hyperplane has zero d-volume.
Thus these other fibers cannot acquire an ordinary-depth surplus merely
because differently oriented simplex partners coexist in one convex body.
The origin still receives genuine finite pair contributions, including
possible contact-direction contributions; this note makes no assertion
that any constructed body violates OC.

## 7. Scope of the late common-body circuit facts

Accepted 5a1fe0ca819e2d1a constructs exact four-point parallelogram circuits
from transverse same-parity edges and selected-mass metronoid contacts.
Its algebraic circuit identity and convex carriedness do not assert that
independently oriented simplex fibers must enlarge or become nonsimplicial.
Its quantitative aggregate and rank conclusions retain the canonical hard
ledger D<Q/100. Accepted f50134018e214ece also retains that ledger and
excludes a specific deletion-stable rank-five hard instance. Neither
statement supplies a shape-compatibility contradiction for (5).

In fact every multiply occupied parity class of (5) consists only of e_i
and -e_i. The underlying graph of those pairs is a disjoint matching;
there are no two transverse edges incident to a common endpoint from which
the quoted four-point atlas construction could begin. Higher origin
circuits certainly exist, but (9)-(15) explicitly control them without
forcing a larger-volume or nonsimplex high core.

## 8. A strict positive-fan obstruction transfers to an actual OC counterexample

The following implication is unconditional mathematics. Its antecedent is
NOT asserted to occur. It certifies that searching for a strict bad fan in
this simplex-source hitting model is searching for an actual obstruction
to sharp OC, rather than for a harmless local-model counterexample.

Fix d>=1 and V>0. Let B be a convex d-body of volume b<=V. Let P_1,...,P_k
be d-simplices contained in B with |P_i|>=V/2^d. Assign nonnegative weights
E_i with

    sum_i E_i=V-b.                                               (17)

Let y_0 be any point of R^d and let u_1,...,u_m be unit vectors admitting
a positive dependence

    sum_j lambda_j u_j=0,       lambda_j>0.                      (18)

Put t_j=u_j.y_0 and Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d. Suppose every fixed cut has strict
bad score

    F_j:=|B intersect {u_j.x>=t_j}|
            +sum_i E_i 1{h_(P_i)(u_j)>t_j}<Gamma V.             (19)

Then there are a positive integer n and a convex body C in R^n times R^d
with positive d-dimensional mixed volume such that EVERY point of
C intersect (Z^n times R^d) has normalized halfspace depth strictly below
2^(-n)Gamma. Thus (19) would contradict sharp OC.

### Zero weights and the endpoint b=V

Discard every source of weight zero; no score changes. If all weights
vanish, (17) gives b=V. The positive fan covers the ordinary centroid of
B, so one displayed B cap contains that centroid and has volume at least
Gamma V by the ordinary Gruenbaum theorem. This contradicts (19). Thus
the antecedent necessarily has b<V, at least one remaining source, and
all remaining weights positive. Rename their number k. This handles both
zero-weight sources and the zero-total-weight boundary without replication.

### Shrink the source simplices while retaining a strict bad margin

First shrink each P_i about its centroid, if necessary, to a simplex
P_i^0 of exact volume V/2^d. This only decreases support functions and
therefore cannot increase any F_j. Let F=max_j F_j<Gamma V.

Now shrink all P_i^0 by one common ratio 1-tau about their respective
centroids g_i, obtaining P_i'. Choose tau>0 sufficiently small that

    M:=(1-tau)^d V>b,       F<Gamma M.                           (20)

Also choose it small enough that every strict hit h_(P_i^0)(u_j)>t_j
remains a strict hit after shrinking; there are finitely many such strict
inequalities. For a missed source, h_(P_i^0)(u_j)<=t_j, its centroid
satisfies u_j.g_i<h_(P_i^0)(u_j), since P_i^0 is full-dimensional. Hence

    h_(P_i')(u_j)<t_j                                           (21)

for every missed pair (i,j). Thus every new miss is strict, and the new
hit sets are subsets of the original hit sets. All P_i' have volume
M/2^d and lie in int B: the centroid of a full-dimensional subset of B
lies in int B, and a strict contraction toward that point is compactly
contained in int B. Explicitly, if g_i+r_i D subset P_i^0 subset B,
then P_i' + tau r_i D subset (1-tau)P_i^0+tau(g_i+r_i D) subset B.

Let g>0 be a common lower bound for t_j-h_(P_i')(u_j) over all missed
pairs. If there are none, choose any g>0. Let r>0 satisfy

    g_i+rD subset P_i'     for all i.

Choose epsilon>0 smaller than r/2, 2g, and twice the distance from every
P_i' to the complement of int B. All these bounds are strictly positive.

### Realize the fixed weights by arbitrarily small actual balls

Choose a sufficiently large integer N. Replace type i by N distinct
copies, indexed (i,l), 1<=l<=N. At each copy let A_(i,l) be the Euclidean
ball centered at zero of volume E_i/N, and put C_(i,l)=2P_i'. Increase N
until every such ball has radius at most epsilon and volume less than
2 Gamma M. There are n=kN copies. Their total actual outside volume is
exactly sum_i E_i=V-b, independent of N. Moreover

    (A_(i,l)+C_(i,l))/2=P_i'+A_(i,l)/2 subset B.

Apply Sections1-4 with these n pairs, p_(i,l)=0, and the reference volume M.
This produces ONE actual convex body C with exact fiber B at zero,
exact simplex fibers of volume M at all other 2^n-1 cube vertices, and
exact ball fibers A_(i,l) at the negative unit bases. No extra integer
fibers exist. The construction requires arbitrarily many variables, which
is permitted in the all-n conjecture.

### Every fiber is excluded from the conjectured depth target

For each fixed direction u_j, equation (21) and epsilon<2g imply that the
set J_epsilon(y_0,u_j) of Section1 is exactly the new strict-hit set.
Section4 therefore supplies an actual ambient halfspace H_j whose
restriction to the zero fiber is the fixed cut {u_j.x>=t_j}, and whose
TOTAL mixed volume is at most

    |B intersect {u_j.x>=t_j}|
      +sum_i E_i 1{h_(P_i')(u_j)>t_j}
      <=F_j<=F<Gamma M.                                        (22)

Every higher completed core fiber is excluded from H_j. The coefficients
in its integer-coordinate normal can depend on j; the body and all its
fibers have already been fixed.

These H_j together cover the entire zero fiber. Indeed, if a point y
satisfied u_j.(y-y_0)<0 for every j, multiplying by the positive lambda_j
and summing would contradict (18). Thus some j has u_j.y>=t_j, so (0,y)
belongs to H_j. Equation (22) gives its depth strictly below Gamma M.
This argument needs only the positive dependence of the continuous
restrictions; the full ambient normals themselves need not balance.

Every nonzero core base is exposed, so Section6 gives its maximum raw
mixed depth exactly Gamma M. Every negative base is exposed and its ball
has raw depth at most |A_(i,l)|/2<Gamma M. These are all integer fibers.

Finally the exact total mixed volume is

    W=(2^n-1)M+b+(V-b)=2^n M+(V-M),

and V-M>0. Therefore the sharp OC raw target is

    2^(-n)Gamma W=Gamma M+2^(-n)Gamma(V-M)>Gamma M.              (23)

Equations (22)-(23) and the exposed-fiber bounds show that every actual
mixed-integer point fails that target. In fact the global maximum raw
depth is EXACTLY Gamma M: the upper bound holds on every fiber, and a
centroid of any exposed simplex core attains Gamma M by Section6. Its
normalized maximum is therefore Gamma M/[2^n M+(V-M)]<2^(-n)Gamma.
This completes the claimed transfer.

### Logical scope

Consequently, an affirmative sharp OC theorem necessarily implies that
for every data set (17)-(18), at least one score in (19) is >=Gamma V.
This is a precise necessary special-case inequality. No converse proving
full OC from that inequality has been established here, so this note does
not call the two statements logically equivalent. It does show that the
strict positive-fan hitting problem retains the difficulty of a fully
realizable mixed-integer family with arbitrary n. Additional vague
compatibility of simplex orientations or higher cube vertices cannot be
invoked to dismiss its potential counterexamples: Sections1-8 already
satisfy that compatibility exactly.
