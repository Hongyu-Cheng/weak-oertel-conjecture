## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a(z):=|C_z|_d,
\]
where an empty fiber has volume zero and \(|\cdot|_d\) denotes \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Fix \(z_\star\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(a(z_\star)>0\), and let
\[
x_\star:=\frac1{a(z_\star)}\int_{C_{z_\star}}x\,dx
\]
be the centroid of \(C_{z_\star}\).

Let \(H\) be a proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \((z_\star,x_\star)\), written as
\[
H=
\left\{(z,x):
\langle u,z-z_\star\rangle+\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle\geq0
\right\},
\]
where \(u\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\). The condition \(v\neq0\) is precisely what “nonvertical” means here; a vertical halfspace has continuous-coordinate normal \(v=0\) and depends only on \(z\). Define the complementary closed side
\[
H^-=
\left\{(z,x):
\langle u,z-z_\star\rangle+\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle\leq0
\right\}
\]
and, for every \(z\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
b(z):=
\left|
\left\{x\in C_z:
\langle u,z-z_\star\rangle+\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle\leq0
\right\}
\right|_d.
\]
Then there exists a proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z_\star\) such that
\[
b(z)\leq b(z_\star)
\qquad
\text{for every }z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, with
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
\theta_d:=1-\gamma_d,
\]
the nonvertical boundary has zero mixed-integer mass and
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}
\bigl(a(z)-\theta_da(z_\star)\bigr)_+.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

Set \(D:=C\cap H^-\) and \(Q:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D)\). The set \(D\) is compact and convex, so \(Q\) is compact and convex. Its fiber at \(z\) is
\[
D_z=
\left\{x\in C_z:
\langle u,z-z_\star\rangle+\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle\leq0
\right\},
\]
and hence \(b(z)=|D_z|_d\).

We first prove the fiber-root concavity needed below. Take \(z_0,z_1\in Q\), \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), and \(z_\lambda=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1\). Convexity of \(D\) gives
\[
(1-\lambda)D_{z_0}+\lambda D_{z_1}
\subseteq D_{z_\lambda}.
\tag{3}
\]
The endpoint fibers in (3) are nonempty compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\). For \(0<\lambda<1\), the Brunn-Minkowski theorem and monotonicity of Lebesgue measure yield
\[
b(z_\lambda)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)b(z_0)^{1/d}
+\lambda b(z_1)^{1/d}.
\tag{4}
\]
The endpoint cases are identities. Thus
\[
r:Q\longrightarrow[0,\infty),
\qquad
r(z):=b(z)^{1/d},
\]
is concave. Formula (4) remains valid when either fiber has zero \(d\)-volume, including lower-dimensional fibers.

Now form only the finite bad lattice set
\[
B:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:b(z)>b(z_\star)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Every point of \(B\) has positive \(b\)-mass and hence belongs to the compact set \(Q\), so \(B\subseteq Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite. Also \((z_\star,x_\star)\in D\), because it lies on the common boundary, and therefore \(z_\star\in Q\).

Suppose \(B\neq\varnothing\), and let \(K:=\operatorname{conv}(B)\). We claim that \(z_\star\notin K\). Otherwise, after deleting zero coefficients, there would be \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\in B\) and \(\lambda_i>0\) with \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) and \(z_\star=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i\). The finite Jensen inequality following from concavity of \(r\) would give
\[
r(z_\star)
\geq
\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i r(z_i)
>
\sum_{i=1}^m\lambda_i r(z_\star)
=r(z_\star),
\]
a contradiction. Hence the compact convex set \(K\) excludes \(z_\star\).

Choose \(q\in K\) minimizing \(\|z_\star-q\|\), and set \(w:=z_\star-q\neq0\). For every \(k\in K\), convexity puts \(q+t(k-q)\) in \(K\) for \(0\leq t\leq1\). Minimality of \(q\) and expansion of the squared distance give
\[
0
\leq
\|z_\star-q-t(k-q)\|^2-\|z_\star-q\|^2
=-2t\langle w,k-q\rangle+t^2\|k-q\|^2.
\]
Dividing by \(t>0\) and letting \(t\downarrow0\) shows
\[
\langle w,k-q\rangle\leq0
\qquad(k\in K).
\tag{6}
\]
Therefore the proper closed halfspace
\[
J:=
\left\{z\in\mathbb R^n:
\langle w,z-q\rangle\geq\frac12\|w\|^2
\right\}
\tag{7}
\]
contains \(z_\star\), while (6) shows \(J\cap K=\varnothing\). In particular, \(J\cap B=\varnothing\).

If \(B=\varnothing\), instead take
\[
J:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\langle e_1,z-z_\star\rangle\geq0\},
\tag{8}
\]
which is a proper closed halfspace containing \(z_\star\). In either case, a lattice point \(z\in J\) with \(b(z)>b(z_\star)\) would belong to \(B\), contradicting \(J\cap B=\varnothing\). This proves (1). Notice that the argument has strictly separated \(z_\star\) from \(K=\operatorname{conv}(B)\) when \(B\neq\varnothing\). It neither asserts nor needs \(J\) to avoid every nonlattice point of the continuous strict superlevel \(\{z\in Q:b(z)>b(z_\star)\}\).

We next account for the halfspace boundary. For each fixed \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), the slice of \(\partial H\) in \(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d\) is
\[
\left\{x\in\mathbb R^d:
\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle=-\langle u,z-z_\star\rangle
\right\}.
\]
Because \(v\neq0\), this is a genuine affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^d\), so it has \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers of \(C\) are nonempty. Summing the zero boundary masses over those fibers gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap\partial H)=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(H\) and \(H^-\) cover the ambient space and overlap exactly on their common boundary, (9) gives, fiber by fiber,
\[
|C_z\cap H|_d=a(z)-b(z)
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{10}
\]

On the distinguished fiber, \(H\) induces the closed halfspace
\[
L_\star:=
\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle v,x-x_\star\rangle\geq0\},
\]
whose boundary contains the centroid \(x_\star\) of the positive-volume convex body \(C_{z_\star}\). Grünbaum's inequality therefore gives
\[
|C_{z_\star}\cap L_\star|_d
\geq
\gamma_da(z_\star).
\tag{11}
\]
Using (10) at \(z_\star\), we obtain
\[
b(z_\star)
=a(z_\star)-|C_{z_\star}\cap L_\star|_d
\leq
\theta_da(z_\star).
\tag{12}
\]

For every \(z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n\), equations (1), (10), and (12) imply
\[
|C_z\cap H|_d
=a(z)-b(z)
\geq
a(z)-\theta_da(z_\star).
\]
The left side is nonnegative, so in fact
\[
|C_z\cap H|_d
\geq
\bigl(a(z)-\theta_da(z_\star)\bigr)_+.
\tag{13}
\]
Finally, the definition of mixed-integer volume, followed by restriction to the fibers indexed by \(J\cap\mathbb Z^n\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H|_d\\
&\geq
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H|_d\\
&\geq
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}
\bigl(a(z)-\theta_da(z_\star)\bigr)_+,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (2).

If a fiber \(C_z\) is empty or lower-dimensional, then \(a(z)=b(z)=0\); its boundary slice still has zero \(d\)-volume, and its summand in (2) is zero. Lower-dimensional nonempty fibers of \(D\) are also covered by the zero-volume cases of Brunn-Minkowski in (4). Thus no fiber other than the distinguished positive-volume fiber is assumed to be full-dimensional, and the empty bad-set case was handled explicitly in (8).

## External sources

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, “The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, pp. 362-363, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: if \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets, \(0<\lambda<1\), and \((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\) is measurable, then
\[
\bigl|(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\bigr|_d^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)|A|_d^{1/d}
+\lambda|B|_d^{1/d}.
\]
Applicability: in (3), \(D_{z_0}\) and \(D_{z_1}\) are nonempty compact sets, their Minkowski convex combination is compact and measurable, and the theorem permits either input to have zero \(d\)-volume. Inclusion (3) and measure monotonicity then give (4).

Grünbaum inequality: Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c(K)\) satisfies
\[
|K\cap L|_d
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d|K|_d.
\]
Applicability: \(a(z_\star)>0\) makes \(C_{z_\star}\) a full-dimensional compact convex set in \(\mathbb R^d\), \(x_\star\) is its centroid, and \(L_\star\) is a closed halfspace containing \(x_\star\), with nonzero normal \(v\).
