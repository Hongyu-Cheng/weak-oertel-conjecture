## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge1$, $\alpha\in[0,1]$, and $q=1+1/d$. Consider all convex bodies $B\subset\mathbb R^d$, measures $0\le\eta\le\mathbf1_B\,dx$ with $\eta(B)=\alpha|B|$, and closed halfspaces $H$ containing the centroid of $\rho=\mathbf1_B\,dx+2\eta$. The minimum of $\rho(H)/\rho(B)$ equals the minimum of $(s+2r)/(1+2\alpha)$ over the scalar domain
$$
0\le s\le1,\qquad 0\le r\le\min(\alpha,s),\qquad s+\alpha-r\le1,\qquad
q(1+2\alpha)s^{q-1}\ge1+2r^q+2(s+\alpha-r)^q-2s^q.
$$
Every minimizing pair has $0<s<1$ and equality in the final constraint. It is attained by taking $B$ to be a volume-one simplex and $\eta$ to be its volume restricted to the upper-tail rank bands $[0,r]$ and $[s,s+\alpha-r]$ of a vertex barycentric coordinate. At $\alpha=0$ or $1$ the minimum equals $(d/(d+1))^d$.

## Proof

Normalize $|B|=1$. The proof of accepted fact 607dca34de472b94 applies to the same dominating uniform body, the same $0\le\eta\le\mathbf1_B\,dx$ of mass $\alpha$, and the same weighted centroid. It puts the centroid in the interior. For a through-centroid cap with uniform volume $s$ and extra mass $r$ it gives exactly the displayed domination and scalar inequalities by Jensen for the concave cap-volume root and leftmost filling on $[0,s]$ and $[s,1]$. Any containing halfspace includes the parallel through-centroid cap, so the scalar minimum bounds every normalized weighted cap from below.

The scalar domain is compact. It contains $(1,\alpha)$, whose left side minus right side in the final constraint is $(q-1)+2\alpha(q-\alpha^{1/d})>0$. It contains no point with $s=0$, as that forces $r=0$ and gives a positive right side. At a minimizer with strict final inequality and $r>0$, decrease both $s$ and $r$ by the same sufficiently small positive amount. This preserves $r\le s$ and $s+\alpha-r\le1$, and the final constraint persists by continuity. The objective strictly decreases. If $r=0$, decreasing $s$ alone gives the same contradiction. Hence every minimizer satisfies equality, and since $s=1$ forces $r=\alpha$, every minimizing $s$ lies in $(0,1)$.

On a volume-one simplex let $X$ be a vertex barycentric coordinate. Its upper-tail function is $U(t)=(1-t)^d$ on $[0,1]$ by the scaling of parallel simplex caps, and $v=U(X)$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. Set $\eta$ equal to volume restricted to the two displayed rank bands. They overlap at most on a null boundary, have total length $\alpha$, and
$$
\int_B v^{1/d}\,d\eta=\frac{r^q+(s+\alpha-r)^q-s^q}{q}.
$$
Since $X=1-v^{1/d}$, the $X$-coordinate of the $\rho$ centroid is
$$
1-\frac{1+2r^q+2(s+\alpha-r)^q-2s^q}{q(1+2\alpha)}=1-s^{1/d}
$$
by equality at the minimizing pair. The cap through the centroid with this $X$ threshold has ranks in $[0,s]$, giving body volume $s$ and extra mass $r$. This proves attainment. At $\alpha=0$ one has $r=0$ and the final constraint reduces to $qs^{q-1}\ge1$. At $\alpha=1$, domination forces $r=s$ and the final constraint again reduces to $qs^{q-1}\ge1$. In both cases the objective is $s$ and its minimum is $q^{-d}$.
