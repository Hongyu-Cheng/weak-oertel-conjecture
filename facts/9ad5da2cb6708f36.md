## Statement

Fix $d\ge2$ and an integer $N\ge1$. Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, $V=|T|$, $m=d+1$, $h=d/m$, $\Gamma=h^d$, and $g=\mathbf1/m$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/m$ set $E_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(0,g+\varepsilon e_1,\ldots,g+\varepsilon e_d)$ and $e_\varepsilon=|E_\varepsilon|/V=\varepsilon^{d-1}(h+\varepsilon)$. For the ordinary projected measure of density $N1_T+1_{E_\varepsilon}$ the maximum raw and normalized closed-halfspace depths are
$$
N\Gamma V+\frac{|E_\varepsilon|}{m}+o(|E_\varepsilon|),\qquad
\Gamma+\frac{e_\varepsilon}{N}\left(\frac1m-\Gamma\right)+o(e_\varepsilon).
$$
The normalized maximum is strictly below $\Gamma$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Every maximizing point satisfies
$$
y_\varepsilon=g-\frac{e_\varepsilon}{Nmdh^{d-1}}\mathbf1+o(e_\varepsilon),
$$
and lies in $\operatorname{int}E_\varepsilon$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. The measure is the continuous-coordinate projection of the mixed measure on the actual integer fibers of $\operatorname{conv}(([0,N-1]\times T)\cup(\{N\}\times E_\varepsilon))$. All of its projected maximizing points are interior to every real fiber over $[0,N]$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

## Proof

Each generator of $E_\varepsilon$ lies in $T$. The matrix $B=\varepsilon I+g\mathbf1^\top$ of its nonzero vertices has eigenvalues $\varepsilon$ on the sum-zero subspace and $\varepsilon+h$ on the span of $\mathbf1$, giving the volume ratio. In particular the ratio strictly increases with $\varepsilon$ and tends to zero.

Take a uniform barycentric tuple $(L_0,\ldots,L_d)$ and put $R=\sum_{i=1}^d L_i$. The random vector $Rg+\varepsilon(L_1,\ldots,L_d)$ is uniform on $E_\varepsilon$ and tends to $Rg$. The law of $R$ has distribution function $r^d$ on $[0,1]$ by scaling the coordinate simplex, so $R<1$ almost surely. The limiting law on $[0,g]$ assigns no mass to any canonical boundary at $g$, and its canonical cap masses are $(1,0,\ldots,0)$ in the order of vertices $0,e_1,\ldots,e_d$. Apply the weak-addition first-variation theorem to the measure divided by $N$, with $t=|E_\varepsilon|/N$. The strict increase of $t$ permits using $\varepsilon$ as its parameter. Its hypotheses give the displayed values and displacement, since for $i\ge1$ the linear part of $\lambda_i$ is the $i$th coordinate and $\kappa=dVh^{d-1}$. The inequality $m\Gamma>1$ holds because the canonical caps cover $T$ and two of them overlap in positive volume for $d\ge2$.

Write the maximizing point as $(1/m-\theta)\mathbf1+r_\varepsilon$, with $\theta=e_\varepsilon/(Nmdh^{d-1})$ and $\lVert r_\varepsilon\rVert=o(e_\varepsilon)$, uniformly over maximizers by the every-selection conclusion. Its non-apex barycentric tuple in $E_\varepsilon$ is $B^{-1}y_\varepsilon$, with $B^{-1}=\varepsilon^{-1}(I-g\mathbf1^\top/(\varepsilon+h))$. It equals $(1/m-\theta)\mathbf1/(\varepsilon+h)+B^{-1}r_\varepsilon$. The remainder has norm $o(e_\varepsilon/\varepsilon)=o(1)$ because $d\ge2$. Thus each non-apex coordinate tends to $1/d$ and is positive. The apex coordinate is $(\varepsilon+d\theta-\mathbf1^\top r_\varepsilon)/(\varepsilon+h)$ and is positive for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ because $\theta$ is a fixed positive multiple of $e_\varepsilon$. This proves interiority.

The hull has fiber $T$ throughout $[0,N-1]$ by containment and the product generator, and fiber $E_\varepsilon$ at $N$ by exposedness. Above $[N-1,N]$ each fiber contains $E_\varepsilon$ by interpolation, since the endpoint fibers both contain it. The integer fibers therefore give precisely the projected density in the statement, and the interiority conclusion holds for every real fiber.
