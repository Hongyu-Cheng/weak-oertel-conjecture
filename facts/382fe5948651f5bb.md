## Statement

For a finite nonempty support \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) with positive weights \(w_X\), use the notation of accepted fact `e342ff057d5afd95`: \(W=\sum_{X\in D}w_X\), \(p_X=w_X/W\), and
$$
\tau_w(X)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\X\in H}}\sum_{Y\in D\cap H}w_Y,
\qquad
\tau_p(X)=\frac{\tau_w(X)}{W}.
$$
All depths in this packet are computed on the full support \(D\) with the full original weights or probabilities. For a chosen subset \(C\subseteq D\), put \(M=\sum_{X\in C}w_X\) and \(\alpha=\sum_{X\in C}p_X=M/W\). Here \(M\) has the same meaning as the target mass in the per-chain estimate of `e342ff057d5afd95`, with the target now a product cell.

Consider the proposed bridge: there exists \(c>0\), independent of the number \(L\ge1\) of factors, their supports and their log-affine weights, such that the following holds. Each factor \(D_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{N_i}\) has a partition into chains satisfying the full negative cube-completion hypothesis of `e342ff057d5afd95`; choose one target chain \(C_i\) from each partition. On \(D=\prod_{i=1}^L D_i\) use product weights \(w_X=\prod_{i=1}^Lw_i(X_i)\). Then the selected product cell \(C=\prod_{i=1}^L C_i\) satisfies
$$
\sum_{X\in C}w_X\tau_w(X)^3\ge cM^4.
\tag{1}
$$
This proposed bridge is false, even when every factor is the same complete integer interval with a two-chain partition.

Precisely, for every integer \(L\ge1\), take \(D_i=\{-1,0,1\}=[-1,1]\cap\mathbb Z\), \(w_i(x)=3^x\), and the partition \(C_i=\{0,1\}\), \(\{-1\}\). Set \(D=\{-1,0,1\}^L\) and \(C=\{0,1\}^L\). These data satisfy every factor hypothesis above. For every real exponent \(r>0\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^L,
& M&=4^L,
& \alpha&=\left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^L,\\
\frac{\sum_{X\in C}w_X\tau_w(X)^r}{M^{r+1}}
&=\frac{\sum_{X\in C}p_X\tau_p(X)^r}{\alpha^{r+1}}
\le\left(\frac{(13/3)^r+3^{r+1}}{4^{r+1}}\right)^L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
The rates in (2) are \(137/216<1\) at \(r=3\), and \(103/144<1\) at \(r=2\). Consequently no common positive per-cell coefficient exists for either exponent.

For calibration, write \(\mathcal M_r(p)=\sum_{X\in D}p_X\tau_p(X)^r\) for the actual global moment; thus \(\mathcal M_2(p)\) is exactly \(M_2(p)\) of accepted fact `6d7276756d672c31`. On these same laws,
$$
\mathcal M_2(p)\ge\frac{e^{-4}}4\,4^{-L},
\qquad
\mathcal M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-6}}8\,8^{-L}.
\tag{3}
$$
In particular, (2) does not refute a globally scaled second- or cubic-moment bound, the accepted single-chain theorem, or the locked weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

First verify the chain hypotheses exactly. On \(C_i\), use \(q=1\), \(x_0=0\), \(v_1=1\), and \(x_1=1\) in the notation of `e342ff057d5afd95`. The single increment is nonzero and hence linearly independent. At prefix \(s=0\), the possible negative completions are \(x_0=0\) and \(x_0-v_1=-1\), both in \(D_i\). At prefix \(s=1\), the only completion is \(x_1=1\in D_i\). The other chain is the singleton with \(q=0\), \(x_0=-1\); its increment list is empty and its sole completion is itself. The two target chains are nonempty, disjoint, and have union exactly \(D_i\). All factor weights are strictly positive and log-affine, since \(w_i(x)=\exp((\log3)x)\). Thus the hypotheses refer to the full factor support, including its completion point \(-1\), and are satisfied without deleting or reweighting any point.

The product support is the complete integer box \([-1,1]^L\cap\mathbb Z^L\). Its product weights are log-affine: \(w_X=3^{\sum_iX_i}\). The one-factor full mass is \(3^{-1}+1+3=13/3\), and the selected-chain mass is \(1+3=4\). Multiplication gives the three mass identities in (2).

Fix \(X\in C\), and let \(I(X)=\{i:X_i=1\}\) and \(k=|I(X)|\). Suppose first that \(k\ge1\). The set
$$
H_X=\left\{Y\in\mathbb R^L:\sum_{i\in I(X)}Y_i\ge k\right\}
\tag{4}
$$
is a proper closed halfspace with nonzero normal, and \(X\) lies on its boundary. For a supported point \(Y\in D\), each \(Y_i\le1\). Therefore \(Y\in H_X\) if and only if \(Y_i=1\) for every \(i\in I(X)\): the sum of \(k\) terms each at most one is at least \(k\) exactly when all terms equal one. Every other coordinate remains free in the full set \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The full unnormalized mass of (4) is consequently
$$
\sum_{Y\in D\cap H_X}w_Y=3^k\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^{L-k}.
\tag{5}
$$
In particular, all supported boundary points are retained, as required for closed-halfspace depth.

For \(k=0\), the point is \(X=\mathbf0\). The proper closed halfspace \(\{Y\in\mathbb R^L:\sum_{i=1}^LY_i\ge-L\}\) contains every point of \(D\) and contains \(\mathbf0\). Its mass is \(W=(13/3)^L\), which is exactly the expression on the right of (5) with \(k=0\). Thus, in every case,
$$
\tau_w(X)\le3^k\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^{L-k}.
\tag{6}
$$
No claim that these halfspaces minimize depth is needed.

There are \(\binom Lk\) points of \(C\) with \(k\) ones, each of weight \(3^k\). Since \(r>0\), applying (6) and the binomial theorem gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{X\in C}w_X\tau_w(X)^r
&\le\sum_{k=0}^L\binom Lk3^k
\left[3^k\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^{L-k}\right]^r\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^L\binom Lk
\left(3^{r+1}\right)^k
\left(\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^r\right)^{L-k}\\
&=\left[\left(\frac{13}{3}\right)^r+3^{r+1}\right]^L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Dividing by \(M^{r+1}=4^{L(r+1)}\) proves the unnormalized inequality in (2). The normalized identity in (2) follows exactly from
$$
\sum_{X\in C}p_X\tau_p(X)^r
=\frac{\sum_{X\in C}w_X\tau_w(X)^r}{W^{r+1}},
\qquad
\alpha^{r+1}=\frac{M^{r+1}}{W^{r+1}}.
\tag{8}
$$
Thus neither the depth law nor its denominator has been replaced by the law conditional on \(C\).

The two rates are exact rational identities:
$$
\frac{(13/3)^3+3^4}{4^4}
=\frac{2197/27+81}{256}
=\frac{137}{216}<1,
\qquad
\frac{(13/3)^2+3^3}{4^3}
=\frac{169/9+27}{64}
=\frac{103}{144}<1.
\tag{9}
$$
For any proposed \(c>0\), choose a finite integer \(L\) large enough that \((137/216)^L<c\). Equation (2) then contradicts (1) for that finite complete box. The same argument with \(103/144\) refutes a universal coefficient in the corresponding second-moment per-cell inequality. The per-chain estimate of `e342ff057d5afd95` remains untouched: its individual targets are chains with linearly independent increments, whereas the proposed target in (1) is a Cartesian product of chains. For \(L\ge2\), the selected cell has \(2^L>L+1\) points and cannot be such a chain in \(\mathbb R^L\), since a chain with linearly independent increments there has at most \(L+1\) points.

Finally apply `6d7276756d672c31` to the full law \(p\). Its rectangular parameters are dimension \(n=L\), integer endpoints \(a_i=-1\), \(b_i=1\), and tilt coordinates \(\ell_i=\log3\). Its support, weights and ambient closed-halfspace depth are precisely those used here. The count denoted by \(r\) in that predecessor (the number of factors of length at least two) equals \(L\); it is distinct from the moment exponent \(r\) used in (2). The predecessor's absolute rectangular bound gives the first inequality in (3). For \(X\sim p\), apply Jensen's inequality to the convex function \(u\mapsto u^{3/2}\) on \([0,\infty)\), with \(u=\tau_p(X)^2\). It gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_3(p)
&=\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p(X)^2)^{3/2}]
\ge\bigl(\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)^2]\bigr)^{3/2}\\
&\ge\left(\frac{e^{-4}}4\,4^{-L}\right)^{3/2}
=\frac{e^{-6}}8\,8^{-L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
This proves the full-law calibration on the same counterexample family. The cell upper bound controls only its contribution: for example, at exponent three, (2) says \(\sum_{X\in C}p_X\tau_p(X)^3\le(13152/28561)^L\), since \((12/13)^4(137/216)=13152/28561\). It is not an upper bound on the full sum \(\mathcal M_3(p)\). Hence the vanishing per-cell ratio and the global bounds (3) establish distinct, compatible statements.
