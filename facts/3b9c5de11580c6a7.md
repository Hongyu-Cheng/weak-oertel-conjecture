## Statement

Let $K$ be a nondegenerate triangle with centroid $g$, and let $D$ be the union of the three pairwise intersections of its centroid caps $\{\lambda_i\ge1/3\}$, where $\lambda_i$ are barycentric coordinates. Set $J(Q)=|Q|-3|Q\cap D|$, with planar area. Let $Q\subseteq K$ be compact and convex with $g\in\operatorname{int}Q$. For each of the three triangles in $D$, take the two outer intersections $p_j,q_j$ of $Q$ with its two rays from $g$, and let $L_j$ be the closed halfplane containing $g$ bounded by $p_jq_j$. Then $P=K\cap\bigcap_j L_j$ satisfies $J(P)\ge J(Q)$. More precisely, $P$ and $Q\cap\bigcap_jL_j$ have identical intersections with $D$. If $g\in\partial Q$, any uniform upper bound on all such replacements also bounds $J(Q)$.

## Proof

Each negative triangle $D_j$ is $K\cap W_j$ for the closed cone $W_j$ spanned by its two rays; the three cones have disjoint interiors. Fix one cone and use coordinates about $g$ in the basis $(p_j-g,q_j-g)$. In these coordinates the outer ray endpoints are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, and $L_j$ is $a+b\le1$. If $(a,b)\in Q$ with $a<0$ and $a+b>1$, its segment to $(1,0)$ meets the second ray at $(0,b/(1-a))$ beyond $(0,1)$, a contradiction. The case $b<0$ is symmetric. A point on a bounding ray beyond the chord is impossible by the definition of its outer endpoint. Thus $Q\setminus L_j$ is contained in $D_j$, and $Q\cap W_j\cap L_j=\operatorname{conv}(g,p_j,q_j)$: the forward inclusion follows from the cone and halfplane, and the reverse one from convexity.

All six endpoints survive all three cuts, since each lies outside the other cones' interiors; $g$ survives as well. Consequently for $Q'=Q\cap\bigcap_jL_j$ one has
$$
Q'\cap D_j=P\cap D_j=\operatorname{conv}(g,p_j,q_j)
$$
for each $j$. The cuts removed only points with signed density $-2$ from $Q$, whereas enlarging $Q'$ to $P$ adds only points with signed density $+1$. Hence $J(Q)\le J(Q')\le J(P)$.

For $g\in\partial Q$ use $Q_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)Q+\varepsilon K$. It lies in $K$, contains $(1-\varepsilon)g+\varepsilon K$ and hence has $g$ in its interior, and converges in Hausdorff distance to $Q$. If $Q$ has interior, its indicators converge off $\partial Q$: exterior points have positive distance, and an interior point $x$ belongs to $Q_\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$ since $(x-\varepsilon g)/(1-\varepsilon)\in Q$. If $Q$ has zero area, its exterior already has full area in $K$. In both cases dominated convergence gives convergence of the areas of $Q_\varepsilon$ and $Q_\varepsilon\cap D$ to the corresponding areas of $Q$. Thus $J(Q_\varepsilon)\to J(Q)$ and a uniform upper bound on the replacements passes to the boundary case.
