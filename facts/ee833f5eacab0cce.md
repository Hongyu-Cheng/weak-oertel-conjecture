## Statement

Let \(k\ge10\) be an integer, and put \(n=6k-2\), \(Q=2^n-1\), and \(m=Q/3\). Choose a primitive element \(\alpha\) of \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\), put \(\omega=\alpha^m\), and define

\[
\gamma_0=\alpha,\qquad
\gamma_1=\omega/\alpha,\qquad
\gamma_2=1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha.
\]

The element \(\gamma_2\) is nonzero. Let \(c\in\{0,\ldots,Q-1\}\) be determined by \(\alpha^c=\gamma_2\). All label indices below are read modulo \(Q\). Start with labels \(X_{i,t}\), for \(i\in\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\), and midpoint equations

\[
2X_{i,t}=X_{i-d_t,s}+X_{i-d_t,u},
\qquad
(d_0,d_1,d_2)=(1,m-1,c),
\qquad
\{s,u\}=\{0,1,2\}\setminus\{t\}.
\]

Delete the labels \(X_{2h+jm,0}\) and \(X_{2h-1+jm,1}\), for \(0\le h<k\) and \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), and delete every equation with a deleted endpoint. Every deleted label's equation is thereby deleted. The retained labels without equations are precisely the \(6k=n+2\) type-two sinks

\(X_{c+2h+e+jm,2}\), for \(0\le h<k\), \(e\in\{-1,0\}\), and \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\).

Order these sinks by increasing \(h\), then by \(e=-1,0\), then by \(j=0,1,2\). Assign them, in that order, the positions \(0,e_1,\ldots,e_n,\mathbf1\) in \(\mathbb R^n\), where \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) are the standard basis and \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\).

There is a unique rational realization of every retained label satisfying these assignments and all retained midpoint equations. Let \(\mathcal L\) be the additive group generated by its positions, and let

\(P=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_n,\mathbf1\}\), \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\), and \(Z_i=X_{i,2}\).

Then \(\mathcal L\) is a full-rank lattice, the convex hull of the realized labels is exactly \(P\), and the retained midpoint relations force

\[
Z_{c-1}\equiv Z_{c+8}\equiv Z_{c+17}\pmod{3\mathcal L}.
\]

These three assigned positions are respectively \(0,e_{27},e_{54}\). With the oriented, zero-inclusive reflection convention of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7, namely

\(r_B^{\mathcal L}(j)=|\{v\in\mathcal L:j+v,j-v\in B\}|\),

one consequently has

\[
r_B^{\mathcal L}\!\left(\frac{e_{27}+e_{54}}3\right)\ge7.
\]

## Proof

The element \(\omega\) has order three. Since the field has characteristic two, it satisfies \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), so \(\mathbb F_2(\omega)=\mathbb F_4\). The degree of \(\alpha\) over this subfield is exactly \(n/2=3k-1\). Indeed, \(\alpha\) generates \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\) as a field because its powers include every nonzero element. Thus the extension it generates over \(\mathbb F_4\) has size \(2^n=4^{3k-1}\). In particular, no nonzero polynomial over \(\mathbb F_4\) of degree less than \(3k-1\) vanishes at \(\alpha\). If \(\gamma_2=0\), then \(\alpha^2+\alpha+\omega=0\), contradicting this degree statement. This proves that \(c\) is defined.

For use in the connectivity argument, \(2^6\equiv1\pmod9\) and \(n\equiv4\pmod6\) give \(Q\equiv6\pmod9\), hence \(m\equiv2\pmod3\). Therefore

\(\gcd(m-1,Q)=\gcd(m-1,3m)=\gcd(m-1,3)=1\).

Direct each original midpoint equation from its center to its two parents. The type paths \(0\to2\to0\) and \(0\to1\to2\to0\) change an exponent by \(-(1+c)\) and \(-(m+c)\), respectively. In the finite cyclic group of exponents, the semigroup generated by these two increments is their generated subgroup: the inverse of either increment is a nonnegative multiple of it. This subgroup contains their difference \(m-1\), which generates the entire cyclic group by the preceding gcd calculation. Consequently a type-zero label can reach every type-zero label. One further parent step reaches either other type at any desired color, and every label of either other type has a type-zero parent. The original graph is strongly connected.

Write \(U_i=X_{i,0}\) and \(V_i=X_{i-1,1}\). For every original index \(i\), the equations of these two types are

\[
2U_i=V_i+Z_{i-1},\qquad
2V_i=U_{i-m}+Z_{i-m}.
\]

Both \(U_i\) and \(V_i\) are deleted exactly when the residue of \(i\) modulo \(m\) belongs to \(\{0,2,\ldots,2k-2\}\). This set is well-defined with the displayed distinct residues because \(m>2k\). The last inequality follows, for example, from \(2^{6k-2}>6k+1\), which holds at \(k=10\) and is preserved when \(k\) is increased by one.

A deleted \(U_i\) removes the equation centered at \(V_{i+m}\), which is also deleted, and the equation centered at \(Z_{i+c}\). A deleted \(V_i\) removes the equation centered at \(U_i\), which is deleted, and the equation centered at \(Z_{i-1+c}\). The two displayed equations also show that each deleted center has a deleted parent. It follows that the only lost equations at surviving centers are exactly those at the stated type-two sinks. Their residues before adding \(c\) are the distinct consecutive residues \(-1,0,\ldots,2k-2\) modulo \(m\), each with its three translates by \(m\). This proves the asserted deletion and sink classification. In particular, every surviving nonsink retains exactly one equation with two surviving parents.

Every retained label reaches a retained sink along retained equations. To see this, take an original path from that label to a deleted label, using strong connectivity. Stop at the first retained center whose equation was lost. Such a center must occur before the first deleted label, since an edge entering a deleted parent belongs to a lost equation. Every earlier edge lies in a retained equation, and the stopping center is a sink by the classification just proved.

Consider the scalar linear system with zero values prescribed at the sinks. A positive maximum of a solution at a nonsink would force both parents to have that same maximum, since its value is their average. Following a retained parent path would then give that positive value at a zero sink, a contradiction. Apply the same argument to the negative of the solution to exclude a negative minimum. The system has trivial kernel. There is one equation for each nonsink unknown, so its integer coefficient matrix is square and invertible over \(\mathbb Q\). This proves existence and uniqueness of rational solutions for every rational sink assignment, coordinate by coordinate.

For each sink, solve the scalar system with boundary value one there and zero at the other sinks. The same maximum principle makes all these harmonic coefficients nonnegative. Their sum is one, by uniqueness and the constant solution with boundary value one. Thus every realized position is a convex combination of the assigned sinks. Their convex hull is exactly \(P\), because the assigned sinks themselves realize all the displayed generators of \(P\). A common positive integer denominator places their additive group \(\mathcal L\) inside a rational dilation of \(\mathbb Z^n\), while the assigned standard basis gives \(\mathbb Z^n\subseteq\mathcal L\). Hence \(\mathcal L\) is a discrete full-rank lattice, and \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\) is finite and is the complete lattice hull in this lattice.

We now prove the congruences. Throughout the modular argument, read the positions in the vector space \(\mathcal L/3\mathcal L\). Equalities between position arrays in that argument are therefore modulo \(3\mathcal L\). Type-two labels exist at every exponent, so \(Z\) is a cyclic array of length \(Q\). To form an auxiliary cyclic array \(U\), put \(U_i=0\) at deleted type-zero indices; these auxiliary entries are never used as retained labels or as a premise for a deleted equation.

Let \(\mathcal S\) act on cyclic arrays by \((\mathcal S f)_i=f_{i+1}\), and define the commuting operators

\[
\nabla=I-\mathcal S^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal N=I-\mathcal S^{-m},\qquad
\mathcal T=\mathcal S^{c-1}+I+\mathcal S^{-1}.
\]

As the coefficient field has characteristic three and \(Q=3m\), one has \(\mathcal N^3=I-\mathcal S^{-3m}=0\).

Whenever the cycle of \(U_i,V_i\) is retained, reduction of its two integer midpoint equations gives

\[
V_i=-U_i-Z_{i-1},\qquad
U_i-U_{i-m}=Z_{i-m}-Z_{i-1},\qquad
(\nabla Z)_i=(\mathcal N(U+Z))_i.
\]

The type-two equation at \(Z_{i-1+c}\), when retained, is \(2Z_{i-1+c}=U_{i-1}+V_i\). Substituting the first identity gives

\((\nabla U)_i=Z_{i-1+c}-Z_{i-1}\).

Put \(F=\{0,1,\ldots,2k-1\}\subset\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). If the residue of \(i\) is outside \(F\), then both cycles with indices \(i\) and \(i-1\) are retained. Moreover, the equation at \(Z_{i-1+c}\) is retained, since its loss would require \(i-1\in\{-1,0,\ldots,2k-2\}\pmod m\). These availability assertions hold also after translating the index by \(m\). Consequently all the following operations use retained equations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
(\nabla^2 Z)_i
&=(\mathcal N(\nabla U+\nabla Z))_i\\
&=\bigl(\mathcal N(Z_{\cdot+c-1}+Z_{\cdot}-2Z_{\cdot-1})\bigr)_i\\
&=(\mathcal N\mathcal T Z)_i.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality uses \(-2=1\) in characteristic three. Define the defect array \(\mathcal R=(\nabla^2-\mathcal N\mathcal T)Z\). We have proved

\(\mathcal R_i=0\) whenever \(i\bmod m\notin F\).

The commuting-operator identity

\((\nabla^2-\mathcal N\mathcal T)^3=\nabla^6\)

follows from characteristic three and \(\mathcal N^3=0\). Apply this identity to \(Z\), use the definition of \(\mathcal R\), and multiply by \(\nabla^3\). Expanding the remaining square gives

\[
\nabla^9 Z
=\nabla^3\bigl(\nabla^4
 +\mathcal N\mathcal T\nabla^2
 +\mathcal N^2\mathcal T^2\bigr)\mathcal R.
\]

Here the middle coefficient is positive because \(-2=1\). We specify every index needed on the right. The term \(\nabla^7\mathcal R\) uses indices \(i-r\), with \(0\le r\le7\). The term \(\mathcal N\mathcal T\nabla^5\mathcal R\) additionally uses indices \(i+c-r\), with \(1\le r\le6\); its other indices already lie in the first list, up to translation by \(m\). Finally, the shifts in \(\mathcal T^2\) are contained in

\(\{2c-2,c-1,c-2,0,-1,-2\}\).

Since \(\nabla^3=I-\mathcal S^{-3}\), the term \(\mathcal N^2\mathcal T^2\nabla^3\mathcal R\) introduces only the additional indices \(i+2c-2\) and \(i+2c-5\), again up to translations by \(m\). Thus the complete required set of residues is contained in the three lists

\[
\begin{aligned}
&i-r &&(0\le r\le7),\\
&i+c-r &&(1\le r\le6),\\
&i+2c-r &&(r\in\{2,5\}).
\end{aligned}
\]

For either \(i=c+8\) or \(i=c+17\), every exponent in these lists has the form \(rc+a\), with \(r\in\{1,2,3\}\) and \(1\le a\le17\). Translations by \(m\) do not change whether a residue belongs to \(F\).

We claim that no exponent \(rc+a\) with these bounds has residue in \(F\). Suppose otherwise, and write \(rc+a\equiv b\pmod m\), where \(0\le b\le2k-1\). There is an \(\ell\in\{0,1,2\}\) such that \(\alpha^{rc+a}=\omega^\ell\alpha^b\). Using \(\alpha^c=(\alpha^2+\alpha+\omega)/\alpha\) shows that \(\alpha\) is a root of the polynomial over \(\mathbb F_4\)

\[
X^a(X^2+X+\omega)^r-\omega^\ell X^{b+r}.
\]

This polynomial is nonzero. The first term has a nonzero coefficient at its lowest exponent \(a\), coming from the constant term \(\omega^r\), and a nonzero coefficient at its distinct highest exponent \(a+2r\). Subtracting one monomial cannot cancel both of those coefficients. Its degree is at most

\(\max\{a+2r,b+r\}\le\max\{23,2k+2\}<3k-1\),

where the strict inequality holds for every \(k\ge10\). This contradicts the degree of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb F_4\) proved at the start. The claim follows.

All defect entries required at \(i=c+8\) and \(i=c+17\) therefore vanish, so \((\nabla^9 Z)_{c+8}=(\nabla^9 Z)_{c+17}=0\). In characteristic three, \(\nabla^9=I-\mathcal S^{-9}\). Hence

\(Z_{c+8}=Z_{c-1}\) and \(Z_{c+17}=Z_{c+8}\) in \(\mathcal L/3\mathcal L\),

as required. This is a symbolic row-span certificate: the operator identities expand each asserted sink difference into a linear combination, modulo three, of the retained integer midpoint relations used to make the listed defect entries vanish. No rank computation, rational-coordinate solve, or finite parameter check is a premise of this deduction. In particular, the congruences did not use the special linear relation among the assigned sink positions.

To read the three positions, equivalently order the sinks by increasing \(a=-1,0,\ldots,2k-2\), then by \(j=0,1,2\), with exponent \(c+a+jm\). Its zero-based position in the list is \(3(a+1)+j\). The choices \((a,j)=(-1,0),(8,0),(17,0)\) have list positions \(0,27,54\). They all occur because \(2k-2\ge18\), and \(54\le n\). Thus the corresponding assigned sinks are \(0,e_{27},e_{54}\), which are distinct and noncollinear.

Finally put \(a=e_{27}\), \(b=e_{54}\), and \(j=(a+b)/3\). The congruences give \(a/3,b/3\in\mathcal L\), so \(j\in\mathcal L\). Its convex combination of \(0,a,b\) places it in \(P\). The three displacements \(a/3\), \(b/3\), and \((a-b)/3\) lie in \(\mathcal L\) and are nonzero and distinct up to sign, since \(a,b\) are linearly independent. Their six reflected endpoints about \(j\) are

\[
\frac{2a+b}{3},\quad\frac b3,\quad
\frac{a+2b}{3},\quad\frac a3,\quad
\frac{2a}{3},\quad\frac{2b}{3}.
\]

Each endpoint lies in \(\mathcal L\) and in \(\operatorname{conv}\{0,a,b\}\subseteq P\). All six endpoints and the center therefore belong to \(B\). Counting the six nonzero oriented displacements and the zero displacement proves \(r_B^{\mathcal L}(j)\ge7\). A lattice basis identifies \(\mathcal L\) with \(\mathbb Z^n\) and preserves the endpoints, center, and every displacement, so this is exactly the reflection convention of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7, with no lattice-index multiplier. No claim about the other lattice points or other reflection centers is needed for this lower bound.
