## Statement

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact and convex, let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ be nonempty, and put $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$. Fix an actual $Y\in S$ and $0<t<1$. Define $L_t=t(S-Y)\cap[-(1-t)(S-Y)]$, $G_t(Y)=\mathcal H_q(L_t)$, $A_t=Y+L_t/t$, and $B_t=Y-L_t/(1-t)$. For every closed ambient halfspace $H=\{X:a\cdot(X-Y)\ge0\}$ with $a\ne0$,
$$
t^q\mu(A_t\cap H)+(1-t)^q\mu(B_t\cap H)
=G_t(Y)+\mathcal H_q(L_t\cap a^\perp).
$$
Every closed containing halfspace has mass at least $G_t(Y)/(t^q+(1-t)^q)$. At $t=1/2$ this is $\mathcal H_q(S\cap(2Y-S))/2$.

If $S$ consists of one positive intrinsic $q$-dimensional convex fiber with $q\ge1$, or is the product of a binary base cube and a common such fiber, the certificate at any $Y$ is maximized over $0<t<1$ at $t=1/2$. In the trapezoid product of the reflection-selector example, maximizing jointly over $Y$ and $t$ gives $5/8$, attained only at balanced weight and the points $(p,c_*)$.

## Proof

The affine images defining $A_t,B_t$ lie in $S$. For $Z\in L_t$, the two endpoint signs at $H$ are those of $a\cdot Z/t$ and $-a\cdot Z/(1-t)$. Their closed-halfspace indicators add to $1+\mathbf1_{\{a\cdot Z=0\}}$. Integrate over $L_t$ and apply the exact Hausdorff homothety factors $t^q,(1-t)^q$, which are one for $q=0$. The endpoint sets are subsets of $S$, yielding the lower bound. Translating a containing halfspace inward to $Y$ only reduces its mass. At $t=1/2$ the overlap is the translate and half-scale image of $S\cap(2Y-S)$, giving the claimed balanced expression.

For a single fiber translate the center to zero and work in its affine direction. The relation $L_{1-t}=-L_t$ holds, and $(L_t+L_{1-t})/2\subseteq L_{1/2}$. Indeed, if $Z=tA=-(1-t)B$ and $W=(1-t)A'=-tB'$ with all four endpoints in the translated convex fiber, then $(Z+W)/2$ is half of $tA+(1-t)A'$ and minus half of $(1-t)B+tB'$. Thus Brunn–Minkowski in the $q$-space yields $G_t\le G_{1/2}$ whenever $G_t>0$, and the bound is immediate otherwise. Also $t^q+(1-t)^q\ge2^{1-q}$ by convexity. In a binary product an interior weighted average of two distinct binary vectors has a nonintegral coordinate, so at an actual $Y=(p,x)$ the overlap has only the pair $(p,p)$ and the same proof applies. For the trapezoid $q=2$, the denominator inequality is strict at $t\ne1/2$; at $t=1/2$ the reflection-selector example identifies the unique continuous maximizer and the maximum $5/8$.
