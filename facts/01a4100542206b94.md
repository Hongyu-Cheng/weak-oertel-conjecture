## Statement

Let \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\) be a nonempty finite set satisfying
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N,
\]
and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in D}\) be a positive probability law. For a closed halfspace \(H\subset\mathbb R^N\), write
\[
p(H):=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}.
\]
Define the actual closed-halfspace depth and its moments by
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
:=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}p(H),
\qquad
M_q(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q
\quad(q>0).
\]
Put \(t_{\mathbf z}:=\tau_p(\mathbf z)\). For \(s\geq0\), define
\[
r_s(\mathbf z):=\min\{p_{\mathbf z},(t_{\mathbf z}-s)_+\},
\qquad
R_s:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_s(\mathbf z),
\qquad
G(s):=\int_s^\infty R_u\,du.
\]
For any nonnegative table \(r\) on \(D\), use its unnormalized depth
\[
\tau_r(\mathbf z)
:=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}r(\mathbf x).
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. Every \(\mathbf z\in D\) has a depth-minimizing halfspace \(H_{\mathbf z}\) whose boundary contains \(\mathbf z\) and no other point of \(D\). For such a choice,
\[
\mathbf x\in H_{\mathbf z}\cap D,\quad \mathbf x\ne\mathbf z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_{\mathbf x}\leq t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}.
\]
This remains true when \(D\) is lower-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^N\).

2. For every \(s\geq0\) and every closed halfspace \(H\),
\[
r_s(H):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap H}r_s(\mathbf z)
\leq (p(H)-s)_+.
\]
Moreover, \(r_s\) is the componentwise greatest submeasure \(r\leq p\) satisfying these inequalities simultaneously: every such \(r\) obeys \(r(\mathbf z)\leq r_s(\mathbf z)\) for all \(\mathbf z\in D\).

3. The positive support
\[
A_s:=\{\mathbf z\in D:r_s(\mathbf z)>0\}
=\{\mathbf z\in D:t_{\mathbf z}>s\}
\]
is, when nonempty, complete:
\[
A_s=\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
\]
Every partially retained atom is an exposed vertex:
\[
0<r_s(\mathbf z)<p_{\mathbf z}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf z\text{ is an exposed vertex of }\operatorname{conv}(A_s).
\]
If the zero-extended table \(p\) admits a globally log-concave extension to \(\mathbb R^N\), then so does every nonzero zero-extended table \(r_s\), including when its support is lower-dimensional.

4. For every \(s\geq0\),
\[
G(s)
=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_s(\mathbf z)(t_{\mathbf z}-s)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_s(\mathbf z)^2.
\]
For every \(q>0\),
\[
q\int_0^\infty s^{q-1}R_s\,ds
=
\frac1{q+1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}
\left[t_{\mathbf z}^{q+1}-(t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z})^{q+1}\right],
\]
and consequently
\[
\frac{M_q(p)}{q+1}
\leq q\int_0^\infty s^{q-1}R_s\,ds
\leq M_q(p).
\]
For every \(q\geq1\), the sharper atomic comparison is
\[
M_q(p)
\geq
q\int_0^\infty s^{q-1}R_s\,ds
+\frac q{q+1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^{q+1}.
\]

5. Suppose additionally that, for every distinct coordinatewise same-parity pair \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D\),
\[
p_{(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2}
\geq \max\{p_{\mathbf x},p_{\mathbf y}\}.
\]
With \(h:=2^N\), every erosion level satisfies
\[
G(s)\geq\frac{R_s^2}{2h}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
G(s)\geq\frac{(1-hs)_+^2}{2h}.
\]
Hence, for every real \(q\geq1\),
\[
M_q(p)
\geq
\frac{h^{-q}}{q+1}
+\frac q{q+1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^{q+1}
\geq
\frac{2^{-qN}}{q+1}.
\]
In particular this holds for every uniform law on a complete convex lattice support. More generally, the midpoint hypothesis holds if all nonvertices of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) have one common weight and every vertex has weight at most that common weight.

For every fixed \(N\geq1\) and every real \(q\geq1\), the coefficient \(1/(q+1)\) is sharp over the uniform class: for
\[
D_L:=\{0,1\}^{N-1}\times\{0,1,\ldots,L-1\}
\quad(L\geq2)
\]
and the uniform law \(p_L\) on \(D_L\),
\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}2^{qN}M_q(p_L)=\frac1{q+1}.
\]

## Proof

We first prove the assertions about minimizing halfspaces. Since \(D\) is finite, only finitely many subsets \(D\cap H\) can occur. Thus the infimum defining \(t_{\mathbf z}\) is attained. Starting from a minimizing halfspace, translate its boundary toward \(\mathbf z\) until it passes through \(\mathbf z\). This removes no need to contain \(\mathbf z\), cannot add a support point, and therefore preserves minimality.

Write the translated halfspace as
\[
H_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf z)\geq0\},
\qquad \mathbf a\ne\mathbf0.
\]
Suppose another support point lies on its boundary. Because the support is finite, a sufficiently small perturbation of \(\mathbf a\) preserves the signs of all strict inequalities. Choose the perturbation generically so that it is nonorthogonal to every nonzero difference between \(\mathbf z\) and another boundary point, and choose its sign so that at least one such boundary point moves to the excluded side. The perturbed closed halfspace still has \(\mathbf z\) on its boundary, adds no previously excluded support point, and excludes at least one positive atom that the old halfspace contained. Its mass is strictly smaller, a contradiction. Hence a minimizing halfspace with
\[
\partial H_{\mathbf z}\cap D=\{\mathbf z\}
\]
exists. The argument takes place in the ambient space and makes no assumption on \(\operatorname{aff}(D)\).

If \(\mathbf x\in H_{\mathbf z}\cap D\) and \(\mathbf x\ne\mathbf z\), then \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)>0\). Translate the same boundary until it passes through \(\mathbf x\):
\[
H_{\mathbf x}:=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)\geq0\}.
\]
This halfspace contains \(\mathbf x\), excludes \(\mathbf z\), and satisfies
\[
D\cap H_{\mathbf x}
\subseteq (D\cap H_{\mathbf z})\setminus\{\mathbf z\}.
\]
Therefore
\[
t_{\mathbf x}\leq p(H_{\mathbf x})
\leq p(H_{\mathbf z})-p_{\mathbf z}
=t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z},
\]
proving part 1. Notice also that \(t_{\mathbf z}\geq p_{\mathbf z}\), since every containing closed halfspace contains the atom at \(\mathbf z\).

We next prove the cap inequality with all boundary atoms retained. Let
\(H=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf y\geq b\}\), and put \(I=D\cap H\). The assertion is immediate if \(I=\varnothing\). Because \(D\) is finite, the \(\mathbf a\)-scores of \(I\) are strictly separated from those of \(D\setminus I\): every point outside the closed halfspace has score strictly below \(b\). Perturb \(\mathbf a\) slightly so that all scores on \(D\) become distinct while this separation persists. Thus \(I\) remains an initial segment of the decreasing generic score order; in particular every atom that lay on the original boundary is retained.

List this initial segment from largest score to smallest as
\(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_k\), and put
\[
S_i:=\sum_{j=1}^ip_{\mathbf x_j},
\qquad S_0:=0.
\]
The score halfspace through \(\mathbf x_i\) contains \(\mathbf x_i\) and intersects \(D\) in exactly \(\{\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_i\}\). Hence \(t_{\mathbf x_i}\leq S_i\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_s(H)
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^k\min\{p_{\mathbf x_i},(S_i-s)_+\}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl((S_i-s)_+-(S_{i-1}-s)_+\bigr)\\
&=(p(H)-s)_+.
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle equality follows by considering the three possibilities \(s\leq S_{i-1}\), \(S_{i-1}<s<S_i\), and \(s\geq S_i\).

Now let \(r\leq p\) be any submeasure satisfying the same cap inequalities. Apply its inequality to the minimizing halfspace \(H_{\mathbf z}\). Since its boundary contains \(\mathbf z\),
\[
r(\mathbf z)
\leq r(H_{\mathbf z})
\leq(p(H_{\mathbf z})-s)_+
=(t_{\mathbf z}-s)_+.
\]
Together with \(r(\mathbf z)\leq p_{\mathbf z}\), this gives
\(r(\mathbf z)\leq r_s(\mathbf z)\) for every \(\mathbf z\), proving part 2.

For completeness of the surviving support, extend the depth definition to every \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^N\) by the same infimum. Its strict superlevel sets are convex. Indeed, suppose
\(\mathbf y=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf y_i\), where each \(\tau_p(\mathbf y_i)>s\). If a closed halfspace \(H\) contained \(\mathbf y\) and had \(p(H)\leq s\), then it could contain none of the \(\mathbf y_i\), because containment of \(\mathbf y_i\) would give \(\tau_p(\mathbf y_i)\leq p(H)\leq s\). All the \(\mathbf y_i\) would lie in the complementary open halfspace, as would their convex combination \(\mathbf y\), a contradiction.

It follows that every lattice point in \(\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\) has depth greater than \(s\). Such a point belongs to \(D\) by completeness of \(D\), and hence belongs to \(A_s\). Thus
\[
A_s=\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
\]

Suppose \(0<r_s(\mathbf z)<p_{\mathbf z}\). Then
\[
t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}<s<t_{\mathbf z}.
\]
Part 1 implies that every other \(\mathbf x\in H_{\mathbf z}\cap D\) satisfies \(t_{\mathbf x}<s\). Consequently
\[
H_{\mathbf z}\cap A_s=\{\mathbf z\}.
\]
The defining linear functional of \(H_{\mathbf z}\) is therefore uniquely maximized on \(A_s\) at \(\mathbf z\), so \(\mathbf z\) is an exposed vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\).

Assume now that \(p\) has a globally log-concave extension. Then its lattice logarithms satisfy every convex-combination inequality
\[
\log p_{\mathbf z}
\geq \sum_i\lambda_i\log p_{\mathbf x_i}
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
\mathbf z=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf x_i,
\quad \mathbf z,\mathbf x_i\in D.
\]
We verify the corresponding inequalities for \(r_s\) on \(A_s\). If \(\mathbf z\) is a vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\), every representation of \(\mathbf z\) by points of \(A_s\) uses only \(\mathbf z\), so the inequality is tautological. If \(\mathbf z\) is not a vertex, the exposed-partial-atom result gives \(r_s(\mathbf z)=p_{\mathbf z}\). Since \(r_s\leq p\), every representation by points of \(A_s\) satisfies
\[
\sum_i\lambda_i\log r_s(\mathbf x_i)
\leq\sum_i\lambda_i\log p_{\mathbf x_i}
\leq\log p_{\mathbf z}
=\log r_s(\mathbf z).
\]

For completeness, define on \(P_s:=\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\)
\[
g_s(\mathbf y):=
\sup\left\{
\sum_i\lambda_i\log r_s(\mathbf x_i):
\mathbf y=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf x_i,
\ \mathbf x_i\in A_s,
\ \lambda_i\geq0,
\ \sum_i\lambda_i=1
\right\}.
\]
Combining barycentric representations shows that \(g_s\) is concave. The inequalities just proved, together with the one-point representation, give
\(g_s(\mathbf z)=\log r_s(\mathbf z)\) for every \(\mathbf z\in A_s\). Therefore the function equal to \(e^{g_s}\) on \(P_s\) and zero outside \(P_s\) is globally log-concave. Because \(A_s=P_s\cap\mathbb Z^N\), it agrees with the zero-extended table \(r_s\) at every lattice point. This proves part 3, including lower-dimensional \(P_s\).

We turn to the exact integral identities. Fix \(\mathbf z\), abbreviate \(p=p_{\mathbf z}\), \(t=t_{\mathbf z}\), and put \(a=t-p\geq0\). As a function of \(u\), the retained mass is
\[
r_u(\mathbf z)
=
\begin{cases}
p,&0\leq u\leq a,\\
t-u,&a<u<t,\\
0,&u\geq t.
\end{cases}
\]
Direct integration, separately for \(s\leq a\), \(a<s<t\), and \(s\geq t\), gives
\[
\int_s^\infty r_u(\mathbf z)\,du
=r_s(\mathbf z)(t-s)-\frac12r_s(\mathbf z)^2.
\]
Summing proves the formula for \(G(s)\).

Equivalently, \(r_s(\mathbf z)\) is the length of the part of \([t-p,t]\) lying above \(s\). Tonelli's theorem therefore gives, for every \(q>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
q\int_0^\infty s^{q-1}r_s(\mathbf z)\,ds
&=\int_{t-p}^t\left(\int_0^u qs^{q-1}\,ds\right)du\\
&=\int_{t-p}^tu^q\,du\\
&=\frac{t^{q+1}-(t-p)^{q+1}}{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing over \(\mathbf z\) proves the exact layer-cake formula. Since \(0\leq t-p\leq t\),
\[
\frac{p t^q}{q+1}
\leq \int_{t-p}^t u^q\,du
\leq p t^q.
\]
For the lower inequality, after setting \(v=p/t\in(0,1]\), it is enough to use
\(1-(1-v)^{q+1}\geq v\). This proves the two-sided comparison after summation.

For the atomic correction, let \(q\geq1\), write \(x=t/p\geq1\), and define
\[
F_q(x):=x^q-\frac{x^{q+1}-(x-1)^{q+1}}{q+1}.
\]
Then \(F_q(1)=q/(q+1)\), while
\[
F_q'(x)
=qx^{q-1}-\bigl(x^q-(x-1)^q\bigr)
=q\left(x^{q-1}-\int_{x-1}^x u^{q-1}\,du\right)
\geq0.
\]
Thus
\[
p t^q-\int_{t-p}^tu^q\,du
=p^{q+1}F_q(t/p)
\geq\frac q{q+1}p^{q+1}.
\]
Summing gives the asserted correction and finishes part 4.

Assume the midpoint-dominance hypothesis. We first show that every nonzero eroded table inherits it. Let \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in A_s\) be distinct and have the same coordinatewise parity, and put \(\mathbf m=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\). Completeness gives \(\mathbf m\in A_s\). Since \(\mathbf m\) is the midpoint of two distinct points of \(A_s\), it is not a vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\). Part 3 then implies
\[
r_s(\mathbf m)=p_{\mathbf m}
\geq\max\{p_{\mathbf x},p_{\mathbf y}\}
\geq\max\{r_s(\mathbf x),r_s(\mathbf y)\}.
\]

We now prove the needed weighted-depth inequality for any nonnegative table \(r\) of total mass \(R\), positive complete support \(A\), and this same midpoint-dominance property. Fix \(\mathbf m\in A\). Reflection about \(\mathbf m\) partitions those points of \(A\) whose reflected partners also lie in \(A\) into the singleton \(\{\mathbf m\}\) and disjoint antipodal pairs \(\{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\}\) with \(\mathbf x+\mathbf y=2\mathbf m\). Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\) contains at least one endpoint of each such pair. Hence
\[
\tau_r(\mathbf m)
\geq r(\mathbf m)
+\sum_{\substack{\{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\}\subset A,\ \mathbf x\ne\mathbf y\\
\mathbf x+\mathbf y=2\mathbf m}}
\min\{r(\mathbf x),r(\mathbf y)\}.
\]
Multiplying by \(r(\mathbf m)\) and using midpoint dominance gives
\[
r(\mathbf m)\tau_r(\mathbf m)
\geq r(\mathbf m)^2
+\sum_{\substack{\{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\}\subset A,\ \mathbf x\ne\mathbf y\\
\mathbf x+\mathbf y=2\mathbf m}}
r(\mathbf x)r(\mathbf y).
\]
Every unordered distinct same-parity pair in \(A\) has one integer midpoint, which belongs to \(A\) by completeness, so summing over \(\mathbf m\) counts each such pair exactly once. If
\[
R_{\boldsymbol\xi}:=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in A\\
\mathbf z\equiv\boldsymbol\xi\pmod2}}r(\mathbf z)
\qquad
(\boldsymbol\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^N),
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf m\in A}r(\mathbf m)\tau_r(\mathbf m)
&\geq\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}r(\mathbf z)^2
+\frac12\sum_{\boldsymbol\xi}
\left(R_{\boldsymbol\xi}^2-
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in A\\
\mathbf z\equiv\boldsymbol\xi\pmod2}}r(\mathbf z)^2\right)\\
&=\frac12\sum_{\boldsymbol\xi}R_{\boldsymbol\xi}^2
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}r(\mathbf z)^2\\
&\geq\frac{R^2}{2h}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}r(\mathbf z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last line is Cauchy--Schwarz over the at most \(h=2^N\) parity classes. This proof also does not require full-dimensional support.

Apply this inequality to \(r_s\). The cap inequality, applied to a minimizing halfspace for \(t_{\mathbf z}\), gives for every \(\mathbf z\in A_s\)
\[
\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)\leq t_{\mathbf z}-s.
\]
Combining this with the exact identity for \(G\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
G(s)
&=\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)(t_{\mathbf z}-s)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2\\
&\geq\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2\\
&\geq\frac{R_s^2}{2h}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each \(r_s(\mathbf z)\) is continuous and piecewise affine in \(s\). Thus \(R_s\) is continuous, \(G\) is continuously differentiable, and \(G'(s)=-R_s\). Also \(R_0=1\), because \(t_{\mathbf z}\geq p_{\mathbf z}\). The preceding inequality at zero gives \(G(0)\geq1/(2h)\). Wherever \(G(s)>0\),
\[
\frac{d}{ds}\sqrt{G(s)}
=-\frac{R_s}{2\sqrt{G(s)}}
\geq-\sqrt{\frac h2},
\]
because \(R_s\leq\sqrt{2hG(s)}\). Integrating up to any \(s<1/h\), and using \(\sqrt{G(0)}\geq1/\sqrt{2h}\), gives
\[
\sqrt{G(s)}\geq\frac{1-hs}{\sqrt{2h}}.
\]
In particular \(G\) cannot vanish before \(1/h\), so the integration is valid throughout that interval. For \(s\geq1/h\) the claimed lower bound is zero. Therefore
\[
G(s)\geq\frac{(1-hs)_+^2}{2h}
\quad(s\geq0).
\]
This derives the required lower tail from the differential inequality and \(R_0=1\); no additional lower-tail premise is used.

There is also a direct probabilistic form of the layer-cake identity. Sample \(\mathbf Z\) with law \(p\), and conditionally on \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf z\), sample \(Y\) uniformly on
\([t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z},t_{\mathbf z}]\). Then
\[
\mathbb P(Y>s)=R_s,
\qquad 0\leq Y\leq t_{\mathbf Z}.
\]
For \(q=1\),
\[
\mathbb E Y=G(0)\geq\frac1{2h}=\frac{h^{-1}}2.
\]
For \(q>1\), Tonelli's theorem and the tail bound give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E Y^q
&=q(q-1)\int_0^\infty s^{q-2}G(s)\,ds\\
&\geq\frac{q(q-1)}{2h}
\int_0^{1/h}s^{q-2}(1-hs)^2\,ds\\
&=\frac{h^{-q}}{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final equality follows after \(u=hs\) from
\[
\int_0^1u^{q-2}(1-u)^2\,du
=\frac{2}{(q-1)q(q+1)}.
\]
Since \(Y\leq t_{\mathbf Z}\), this already proves
\(M_q(p)\geq h^{-q}/(q+1)\). Combining the same estimate for \(\mathbb E Y^q\) with the atomic comparison from part 4 proves the stronger displayed bound in part 5.

A uniform law satisfies midpoint dominance with equality. If instead every nonvertex has one common weight and all vertex weights are no larger, then the midpoint of two distinct points is a nonvertex and has the common weight, proving the stated more general sufficient condition.

It remains to prove sharpness. Fix \(N\) and let \(p_L\) be uniform on \(D_L\), so every atom has mass \((2^{N-1}L)^{-1}\). For \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}\), the affine functional
\[
\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\mathbf x)
:=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(2\varepsilon_i-1)x_i
\]
is uniquely maximized on \(\{0,1\}^{N-1}\) at \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\), with a score gap of at least one when another Boolean point exists. Fix \(c>L-1\). The two closed halfspaces
\[
\begin{aligned}
c\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\mathbf x)-y
&\geq c\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon)-j,\\
c\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\mathbf x)+y
&\geq c\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon)+j
\end{aligned}
\]
have intersections with \(D_L\) exactly
\[
\{\boldsymbol\varepsilon\}\times\{0,\ldots,j\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{\boldsymbol\varepsilon\}\times\{j,\ldots,L-1\},
\]
respectively: every other Boolean point loses at least \(c\) in the first coordinates, which the last coordinate cannot recover. This also covers \(N=1\), when the Boolean factor is a singleton and \(\phi_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}=0\). Both halfspaces contain \((\boldsymbol\varepsilon,j)\). Hence
\[
\tau_{p_L}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon,j)
\leq
\frac{\min\{j+1,L-j\}}{2^{N-1}L}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\limsup_{L\to\infty}M_q(p_L)
&\leq2^{-q(N-1)}
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1L
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
\left(\frac{\min\{j+1,L-j\}}L\right)^q\\
&=2^{-q(N-1)}\int_0^1\min\{x,1-x\}^q\,dx\\
&=\frac{2^{-qN}}{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The already proved lower bound supplies the reverse liminf. Therefore
\(2^{qN}M_q(p_L)\to1/(q+1)\), completing the proof.
