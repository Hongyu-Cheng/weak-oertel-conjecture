## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a full-dimensional polytope with positive $d$-volume at every integer base $z$ of its projection. Use mixed measure $M$ obtained by summing $d$-volume at those bases. Fix $w_0=(p_0,x_0)\in C$, $v\in\mathbb R^d$, and set $C_\epsilon=\operatorname{conv}(C,w_0+\epsilon(0,v))$ for $\epsilon>0$. For each nonvertical facet $F_i$ write its retained inequality as $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i\le0$, with $\|u_i\|=1$. The visible facets for small $\epsilon$ have $L_i(w_0)=0$ and $c_i=u_i\cdot v>0$. On each visible facet let
$$
T_i(w)=\max\{t\in[0,1]:w=tw_0+(1-t)f,\ f\in F_i\}.
$$
At each integer base $z$, count one representative per class of visible facets having the same $u_i$ and $b_i-a_i\cdot z$. The corresponding pyramids have identical $z$-slices within each class and zero $d$-volume intersection between distinct classes. Define a measure $\nu$ on the old fiber boundaries by summing $c_i T_i\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$ on the representative sections $(F_i)_z$, using counting measure for $d=1$. Then $M(C_\epsilon)-M(C)=\epsilon\nu(\mathrm{total})$. For every fixed closed ambient halfspace $H=\{\ell\ge0\}$, with continuous normal $v_\ell$ of its affine margin $\ell$,
$$
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
\frac{M(C_\epsilon\cap H)-M(C\cap H)}{\epsilon}
=\int\mathbf1_{\{\ell>0\ \mathrm{or}\ (\ell=0,\ v_\ell\cdot v\ge0)\}}\,d\nu .
$$
For a single visible facet $F$ with $u\cdot v=1$, its mass coefficient is equivalently
$$
\nu(\mathrm{total})=\sum_z\int_0^1
 (1-t)^{d-1}\mathcal H^{d-1}\!\left(F_{(z-tp_0)/(1-t)}\right)dt .
$$
If $p_0$ is an integer base and $x_0+\epsilon v$ points outside $C_{p_0}$ for all small positive $\epsilon$, then $\nu(\mathrm{total})>0$.

## Proof

The projection is unchanged because the apex has base $p_0$. No vertical inequality changes at the apex, and each strictly inactive inequality stays strict for small $\epsilon$. Thus exactly the listed facets are violated. The new region outside $C$ is the union, outside $C$, of their pyramids with this common apex. Indeed, every ray from the apex meeting $C$ first enters at a face on which some violated inequality becomes an equality. Conversely the visible pyramids lie in the new hull.

The roof superlevel set at $0\le t<1$ is $tw_0+(1-t)F_i$. This follows from convexity and nesting of these homothetic sets, and also proves concavity of the roof by convex combinations of pyramid parameters. Writing points in a pyramid as convex combinations of its apex and facet gives $w+s\epsilon(0,v)$ for $0\le s\le T_i(w)$, $w\in F_i$. The continuous coordinate map on a sampled fiber has normal Jacobian $\epsilon c_i$ with respect to $ds\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$, including in $d=1$. Indeed $L_i$ at the image is $\epsilon c_i s$, which also fixes $s$ uniquely and then fixes $w$. The bottom boundary has zero $d$-volume in each fiber. In the single-facet case layer cake and the roof superlevel sets give the integral in the statement: a superlevel section at base $z$ comes from base $(z-tp_0)/(1-t)$ with continuous length scale $1-t$. Only the outer sum samples integer bases. The endpoint $t=1$ is immaterial.

If an exterior point lies in two pyramids, the ray from the apex through it has one first entry on both facets. The common part lies on $c_j L_i-c_i L_j=0$, since that affine functional vanishes at the apex and at the common first entry. When $c_j u_i-c_i u_j\ne0$, this set has zero $d$-volume on every fiber. Otherwise positive $c_i,c_j$ and the unit normals give $u_i=u_j$ and $c_i=c_j$. At base $z$ the affine difference can vanish only when the stated supports agree. Conversely if they agree at $z$, the difference $L_i-L_j$ has no continuous component and vanishes also at $p_0$. It then vanishes at every real base $(z-tp_0)/(1-t)$ in the roof superlevel formula. The slices of the two facets there are identical because both are defined inside $C$. The two roofs at $z$ and the pyramid slices coincide. Counting the representatives therefore gives precisely the actual mixed mass, including when an ambient-null seam has positive mixed volume.

For a fixed cap use the same parametrizations and divide its added mass by $\epsilon$. For each representative the inner integral is over $0\le s\le T_i(w)$ of $\mathbf1_{\{\ell(w)+\epsilon s\,v_\ell\cdot v\ge0\}}$, with factor $c_i$ in the measure on the facet section. For $s>0$ the limiting indicator is the one in the statement, including zero pairing because the cap is closed. The bound $T_i(w)$ gives dominated convergence. If the displacement points outside the sampled fiber at $p_0$, some active ordinary fiber facet has strictly positive normal pairing with $v$. Its ordinary pyramid in the new fiber has positive volume proportional to $\epsilon$, so $\nu(\mathrm{total})>0$.

For an example with coincident positive mixed slices take $n=d=1$, $C=\{-1\le z\le1,\ -1\le x,\ x+z\le1,\ x-z\le1\}$, $w_0=(0,1)$ and $v=1$. Both roof facets are visible, both yield the added interval $(1,1+\epsilon]$ at $z=0$, and neither adds length at $z=\pm1$. The actual addition is $\epsilon$ and the separate sum before choosing representatives is $2\epsilon$.
