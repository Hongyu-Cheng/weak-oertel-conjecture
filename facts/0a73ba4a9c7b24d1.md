## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge1$, finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb R^n$, and a ball $R B_d$ centered at zero with $R>0$. Let $H_j=\{(z,x):u_j\cdot x\ge t_j(z)\}$, where $\|u_j\|=1$ and $t_j$ is real affine. Some subsequence has $u_j\to u$ with $\|u\|=1$ and $t_j(z)\to t(z)\in[-\infty,\infty]$ for every $z\in P$. On $P\times R B_d$, the limiting traces $\{x:u\cdot x\ge t(z)\}$ at finite thresholds, the full traces at $t(z)=-\infty$ and the empty traces at $t(z)=+\infty$ are realized by one actual closed halfspace with unit continuous normal $u$ and a finite affine threshold. For summed $d$-volume $\mu$ on any fixed family of measurable fibers inside that ball, $\mu(H_j)$ along the subsequence tends to the mass of that halfspace. Every fixed point of $P\times R B_d$ contained in infinitely many caps of the subsequence belongs to the realizing halfspace.

## Proof

The unit sphere and the extended real line are compact. Finiteness of $P$ gives the simultaneous subsequence. Represent $t_j(z)=w_j\cdot(z,1)$ and let $I=\{z\in P:t(z)\in\mathbb R\}$. The image of the finite-dimensional linear evaluation map $E_I w=(w\cdot(z,1))_{z\in I}$ is closed, so choose $w_0$ with $E_I w_0=(t(z))_{z\in I}$. Take a linear right inverse $J$ on $\operatorname{im}E_I$. The vectors
$$
v_j=w_j-w_0-J(E_I(w_j-w_0))
$$
annihilate the evaluations on $I$. The correction $J(E_I(w_j-w_0))$ tends to zero. Thus $v_j\cdot(z,1)$ tends to $+\infty$ or $-\infty$ with the prescribed sign at each $z\notin I$. If $I\ne P$, fix one $v_j$ with all these strict signs. Then $w=w_0+Tv_j$ for a sufficiently large finite $T>0$ agrees with every finite threshold, exceeds $R$ at every positive divergence and is below $-R$ at every negative divergence. If $I=P$, use $w=w_0$. The same evaluation-map proof works for $I=\varnothing$ with zero target space. The cap $H=\{(z,x):u\cdot x\ge w\cdot(z,1)\}$ realizes the traces.

At a finite threshold, indicators on the fiber converge away from the hyperplane $u\cdot x=t(z)$, which has zero $d$-volume. At an infinite threshold, convergence to the full or empty trace is uniform in $R B_d$. Dominated convergence gives $\mu(H_j)\to\mu(H)$. For fixed $(z,x)$ belonging to infinitely many $H_j$ along this subsequence, at a finite threshold take limits of $u_j\cdot x\ge t_j(z)$ along those memberships. Positive divergence makes membership eventually impossible and negative divergence gives the full trace. Thus the realizing closed cap contains the point.
