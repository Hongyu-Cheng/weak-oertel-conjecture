## Statement

Let $r\ge2$ and $D\ge1$ be integers. Partition the $r(D+1)$ vertices of a simplex $J$ into $r$ groups of $D+1$ vertices, and let $T_i$ be the face spanned by group $i$. Let $\nu$ be the probability giving each $T_i$ mass $1/r$, distributed uniformly with respect to its intrinsic $D$-volume. For $\operatorname{depth}_\nu(y)=\inf_{H\ni y}\nu(H)$ over all proper closed ambient halfspaces,
$$
\max_y\operatorname{depth}_\nu(y)=\frac{\gamma_D}{r},\qquad
\gamma_D=\left(\frac{D}{D+1}\right)^D.
$$
The average of the component centroids is a maximizing mark.

## Proof

Let $g_i$ be the centroid of $T_i$ and $g=r^{-1}\sum_i g_i$. A closed halfspace containing $g$ contains some $g_i$, since an affine margin negative at all $g_i$ is negative at $g$. Its trace on $\operatorname{aff}T_i$ contains $g_i$ and is either the whole affine space or a proper halfspace. Tighten the latter to pass through $g_i$ and identify that affine space isometrically with $\mathbb R^D$. By baee8ddaa588aef0 the closed cap fraction is at least $\gamma_q$ for its number $1\le q\le D$ of negative vertex margins. The monotonicity proved there gives $\gamma_q\ge\gamma_D$. Hence $\nu(H)\ge\gamma_D/r$.

For the upper bound a point outside $J$ has zero depth by strict separation. For $y\in J$ write $\lambda_{ij}$ for its ambient affine barycentric coordinates on $\operatorname{aff}J$ and $\tau_i=\sum_j\lambda_{ij}$. Choose $i$ with $t=\tau_i(y)>0$ and $j$ with $b=\lambda_{ij}(y)/t\ge 1/(D+1)$. For each $\eta>0$ take $\delta=\eta(1-t)/t$. Use the closed halfspace with affine margin
$$
\lambda_{ij}-b\tau_i+\delta\tau_i-\eta(1-\tau_i).
$$
Its margin is zero at $y$, is strictly negative on every other component, and cuts $\lambda_{ij}\ge b-\delta$ on $T_i$. The margin is nonconstant on $T_i$, and its affine extension to the ambient space defines a proper halfspace. For all sufficiently small positive $\eta$, the threshold is in $(0,1]$. This cap is the homothet of $T_i$ about its $j$th vertex of factor $1-b+\delta$, including the zero-volume singleton case. Its probability is $(1-b+\delta)^D/r$. Infimizing over $\eta>0$ gives $\operatorname{depth}_\nu(y)\le(1-b)^D/r\le\gamma_D/r$. The excluded components stay strictly outside each approximating closed cap. At $\eta=0$ they would instead lie in the closed boundary.
