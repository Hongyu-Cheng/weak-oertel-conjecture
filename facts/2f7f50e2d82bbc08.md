## Statement

Let $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ be finitely many distinct points with $N\ge1$, let $e_i\ge0$ and $q\ge1$, and minimize
$$
R=\min_{h,g}\sum_i h_i^q,\qquad h_i\ge e_i,\qquad h_j\ge h_i+g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)\quad(i\ne j),
$$
with the $g_i$ in the direction space of the affine span. At every optimizer define $s_i=q h_i^{q-1}$ for $q>1$, and $s_i=1$ for $q=1$. There are nonnegative obstacle multipliers $\lambda_i$ and nonnegative off-diagonal multipliers $\mu_{ij}$ complementary to the displayed inequalities, with $\mu_{ii}=0$, such that
$$
\sum_j\mu_{ij}(p_j-p_i)=0,\qquad
\lambda_i=s_i+\sum_j\mu_{ij}-\sum_j\mu_{ji},\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_i e_i=\sum_i\lambda_i h_i=qR .
$$
The masses $\lambda_i$ and $s_i$ have equal total mass and equal first moments on the bases. When $q>1$ and $h_i=0$, $\lambda_i$ and all incident $\mu$ vanish.

On the nodes with $s_i>0$, put $a_i=s_i+\sum_j\mu_{ij}$, $T_{ij}=\mu_{ij}/a_i$, and $D=\operatorname{diag}(s_i/a_i)$. The matrix
$$
\pi=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)(I-T)^{-1}D
$$
on these nodes, extended by zero rows and columns elsewhere, is nonnegative. It has row masses $\lambda_i$, column masses $s_j$ and $\sum_j\pi_{ij}(p_j-p_i)=0$ for each $i$. On every positive entry, $h_i=e_i$ and $h_j=h_i+g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)$. In particular $\sum_i\lambda_i\phi(p_i)\le\sum_j s_j\phi(p_j)$ for every convex $\phi$ on the base hull. When no nodes have positive $s_i$, use the zero matrix.

For $q>1$ put $q'=q/(q-1)$ and $c_q=(q-1)q^{-q'}$. Over all nonnegative matrices $\pi$ with $\sum_j\pi_{ij}(p_j-p_i)=0$ in every row, and writing $s_j(\pi)=\sum_i\pi_{ij}$,
$$
R=\max_{\pi}\left(\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij}e_i-c_q\sum_j s_j(\pi)^{q'}\right).
$$
For $q=1$ the dual is $R=\max_{\pi}\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij}e_i$ over the same row conditions with $s_j(\pi)=1$ in every column. Both maxima are attained by the matrix above.

## Proof

A constant height at least $\max_i e_i$ and zero slopes are feasible. The projection of this finite polyhedron onto the height coordinates is a closed polyhedron by elimination. The objective bounds every nonnegative height on each sublevel, so a minimum exists with a feasible slope lift. At an optimizer every direction satisfying the homogeneous active inequalities is feasible for a short positive step. For $q>1$ differentiate the extension $\sum_i(h_i)_+^q$, which has derivative zero at a zero height, and for $q=1$ use the linear objective. Finite Farkas separation puts the negative gradient in the cone generated by the active constraint normals. With constraints written $e_i-h_i\le0$ and $h_i+g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)-h_j\le0$, slope and height stationarity give exactly the two stated identities, with nonnegative complementary multipliers.

For each $i$, complementary support inequalities and the zero outgoing displacement give $\sum_j\mu_{ij}(h_j-h_i)=0$. Dotting height stationarity with $h$ thus gives $\sum_i\lambda_i h_i=\sum_i s_i h_i=qR$. Obstacle complementarity gives the reward identity. Summing height stationarity gives equality of total masses, and multiplying by $p_i$ and summing gives equality of first moments using the row displacements.

For $q>1$, let $Z$ be the zero-height set. The identity $\sum_j\mu_{ij}(h_j-h_i)=0$ and nonnegative heights imply no positive flow from $Z$ to its complement. Summing height stationarity over $Z$, where $s_i=0$, gives $\sum_{i\in Z}\lambda_i$ equal to minus the incoming flow from outside, so both vanish. The internal flow has equal incoming and outgoing totals at each node. By expansion and the row displacement identity,
$$
\sum_{i,j\in Z}\mu_{ij}|p_j-p_i|^2
=\sum_{i,j\in Z}\mu_{ij}(|p_j|^2-|p_i|^2)=0 .
$$
Distinctness of the bases then removes all internal off-diagonal flow. There is no deletion in the case $q=1$.

On the retained nodes all stopping fractions $s_i/a_i$ have a positive finite minimum, so $T$ has maximum row sum strictly below one. Thus $N_0=(I-T)^{-1}=\sum_{m\ge0}T^m$ converges entrywise and is nonnegative. In row-vector notation height stationarity is $a=\lambda+aT$, hence $\lambda N_0=a$. The row sum identity is $(I-T)\mathbf1=D\mathbf1$ and the row displacement identities give $(I-T)p=Dp$, with $p$ the matrix of base coordinates. Multiplication by $N_0$ proves that $\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)N_0D$ has the claimed row masses, column masses and barycenters. All identities still hold if the retained set is empty. Conditional Jensen gives the convex tests. The supporting gaps $h_j-h_i-g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)$ are nonnegative and their $\pi$-weighted sum is $\sum_j s_j h_j-\sum_i\lambda_i h_i=0$. Thus each positive entry is a support contact. Its input is an obstacle contact by complementarity.

For any matrix in the dual feasible set and any primal feasible pair, multiply the support inequalities by $\pi_{ij}$ and sum. The row displacements vanish, giving $\sum_{ij}\pi_{ij} e_i\le\sum_j s_j(\pi)h_j$. For $q=1$ the unit column conditions prove weak duality directly. For $q>1$, maximizing $s t-t^q$ over $t\ge0$ gives $c_qs^{q'}$, including zero at $s=0$. Applying this inequality to the last sum proves weak duality. For the constructed matrix the reward is $qR$ and for $q>1$ the conjugate penalty is $(q-1)R$ because $s_j=q h_j^{q-1}$. For $q=1$ its columns already sum to one. This proves both attained maxima.
