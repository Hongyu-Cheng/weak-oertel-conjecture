## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and $B=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1,\ 1\le i\le d+1\}$ be a full-dimensional simplex of volume $b$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. Let $A,K$ be convex bodies with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$. Assume each cut $v_i\cdot x=-1$ is proper on $A$. Put $U_i=|A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge-1\}|$, $q_i=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge0\}|$, $H_i=1+\max_A v_i\cdot x$, and $P_i=dbH_i/(d+1)$.

Suppose $U_i+q_i=Q$ for all $i$, order $U_1\le\cdots\le U_{d+1}$, and let $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, $\kappa=1/\Gamma-2$, $F=U_1+U_2+\kappa U_3$. Then $k\le Q/\Gamma-F$. If also $Q\le\Gamma(a+b+k)/2$ and $b\le a+k$, then
$$
k\le a+b-2F,\qquad F\le a,\qquad Q\le\Gamma(a+b-F),\qquad
\sum_{i=3}^{d+1}P_i>db .
$$
In dimension three, $16Q>9(U_3+U_4)+3b+\sqrt{3U_3P_3}+\sqrt{3U_4P_4}$.

## Proof

The middle halfspaces cover all of $\mathbb R^d$ because their normals sum to zero. Apply the full profile of 23a563a735c07c25 in ordinary dimension $d$ with $d+1$ proper halfspaces and cutoff $\alpha=Q-U_3$. This is nonnegative since it is a middle cap mass. The cap masses are $q_i=Q-U_i$, so the profile reads $k\le q_1+q_2+(1/\Gamma-2)q_3=Q/\Gamma-F$, also with ties. Substituting the upper bound for $Q$ gives $k\le a+b-2F$ and then $Q\le\Gamma(a+b-F)$. Since $k\ge b-a$, we obtain $F\le a$.

Here $\kappa>0$ because $(1+1/d)^d>2$ for $d\ge2$ by the binomial theorem, and $\kappa<1$ because $(1+1/d)^d<e<3$. Thus the strict complements of the first two $A$ cuts have intersection of volume at least $a-U_1-U_2\ge\kappa U_3>0$. At a common point $x$ in this intersection, $\sum_{i=3}^{d+1}v_i\cdot x>2$ by normal balance. Hence $\sum_{i=3}^{d+1} H_i>d+1$, giving the strict sum of $P_i$. In $d=3$ the two actual local facet bounds of the result “Sharp full-binomial volume reserve for a supporting facet” apply with $q_i=Q-U_i$. Adding them for $i=3,4$ and using $P_3+P_4>3b$ gives the displayed inequality.
