## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$, $V=|T|=1/2$, and $m\ge0$ be an integer. Take $R=r_0\ge r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_m\ge0$ with $R>0$ and nondecreasing drops $r_{h-1}-r_h$. Let $S$ have the two fibers $r_hT$ at integer bases $-h$ and $h+1$ for each $0\le h\le m$, and no other fibers. Write $\mu=\mathcal H_2|_S$, $W=\sum_{h=0}^m r_h^2$, $t=R/3$, and $y=(0,t,t)$. Thus $\mu(S)=2VW$. The set $S$ is realized by a compact convex body with nonempty interior. Every closed ambient halfspace containing $y$ has mass at least $(2/9)\mu(S)$. The fraction $2/9$ is attained within this class when $m=0$. For every $a\in[1/2,2/3]$ and every real $b$, the through-$y$ halfspace $\{bz+ax_1+(1-a)x_2\ge t\}$ has mass at least $V((4/9)W+R^2/162)$.

## Proof

Interpolate the radius linearly between the given integer bases on $[-m,m+1]$. Its slopes are the successive drops in reverse order, zero across $[0,1]$, then the negatives of the drops in forward order. These slopes are nonincreasing. The set defined by $z\in[-m,m+1]$, $x\ge0$ and $x_1+x_2$ at most the interpolated radius is compact and convex, with nonempty interior since the central radius is $R>0$. Its integer fibers are exactly $S$. Null fibers contribute zero to every cap.

**Normal and tilt.** Tighten a containing halfspace to pass through $y$. A halfspace with zero continuous normal then contains at least one whole fiber from each pair and has mass at least $VW$. For a nonzero continuous normal write its margin as $b z+v\cdot x-t\sum_j v_j$. If $v$ has a negative entry, choose $i$ with $v_i=\min_j v_j<0$. On the fiber $rT$, use the area-preserving interchange of barycentric coordinates $x_i$ and $r-x_1-x_2$. In the new coordinates $x'$ the original margin is $bz+w\cdot x'+v_i r-t\sum_jv_j$, with $w_i=-v_i$ and $w_j=v_j-v_i$ for $j\ne i$. Since $\sum_j w_j=\sum_j v_j-3v_i$, the through-$y$ margin $bz+w\cdot x'-t\sum_j w_j$ differs from this by $v_i(R-r)\le0$. It gives one ambient cap of no larger mass on each fiber. If $v\ge0$, take $w=v$ directly. Divide by the positive sum of the coordinates and relabel the continuous coordinates if needed to obtain $u=(a,1-a)$ with $a\in[1/2,1]$.

For this normal write $F_r(s)=|rT\cap\{u\cdot x\ge s\}|/V$. At a fixed $\beta\ge0$, the positive-tilt cap minus the negative-tilt cap, in units of $V$, has paired difference
$$
\big[F_{r_h}(t-\beta(h+1))-F_{r_h}(t-\beta h)\big]
+\big[F_{r_h}(t+\beta h)-F_{r_h}(t+\beta(h+1))\big]\ge0,
$$
by monotonicity of $F_{r_h}$. Hence it suffices to bound the cap with margin $-\beta z+u\cdot x-t$. For $a\ge2/3$, apply accepted fact 5adcdc64a058b8aa with $d=2$, $\tau=1/3$ and maximum coordinate $M=a$. Its hypotheses on the radii, actual fibers, and fixed cap are exactly those here.

**Low-normal sector and affine reduction.** Take $a\in[1/2,2/3]$, put $\gamma=4/9$, and define
$$
G_h(r)=F_r(t-\beta h)+F_r(t+\beta(h+1))-\gamma r^2 .
$$
The cap surplus over $V\gamma W$, divided by $V$, is $\sum_{h=0}^m G_h(r_h)$. Accepted fact 9cef204c64b1bff8 applies with $d=2$, this same nonnegative normalized $u$, this $\beta$, and the given concave paired radii. For $m\ge1$ it gives $\ell_h=R-\alpha h\ge0$ for $0\le h\le m$ with $0\le\alpha\le R/m$ and $G_h(r_h)\ge G_h(\ell_h)$ at every index. It preserves $R$ and compares surpluses relative to the two respective total masses. It suffices to bound the affine profile. The own-cap lemma gives $G_0(R)\ge R^2/18$ and handles $m=0$.

If $\beta\ge\alpha/3$, then $t-\beta h\le\ell_h/3$. For every positive $\ell_h$, the left cap contains that fiber's centroid. Its normalized area is at least $\gamma\ell_h^2$ by accepted fact 700febf4b7a92759 with barycentrics $(1/3,1/3,1/3)$. Thus $G_h(\ell_h)\ge0$ for every outer pair, and the own contribution gives surplus at least $R^2/18$. This includes zero slope and the tilt boundary.

**Full affine leg.** In the remaining case $0\le\beta<\alpha/3$, scale the continuous coordinates by $1/R$, dividing the tilt and all thresholds by $R$ and all planar areas by $R^2$. In the resulting coordinates write $t=1/3$, $k=\alpha/R\in(0,1]$, use $\beta$ for the scaled tilt, and use $r_h=(1-kh)_+$ on the full affine leg. At each positive radius its two thresholds satisfy
$$
q_h=t-\beta h\ge r_h/3>0,\qquad p_h=t+\beta(h+1)\ge r_h/3>0 .
$$
For any $q\in[r/3,r/2)$ with $r>0$, the point $(q,q)$ lies on the cap line and has barycentrics $(1-2q/r,q/r,q/r)$, each at most $1/2$. The first coordinate is the smallest. Accepted fact 700febf4b7a92759 therefore gives $F_r(q)\ge 4q(r-2q)$. For $q\ge r/2$, the positive-part version $F_r(q)\ge 4q(r-2q)_+$ is automatic. The envelope term at the own fiber is $\gamma$, while the own-cap lemma gives $F_1(t)\ge\gamma+1/18$ there.

Use accepted fact 0beb1b53b181c0e7 at $d=2$ and slope $k$. Its deficit subtracts exactly the two envelopes $4q_h(r_h-2q_h)_+$ and $4p_h(r_h-2p_h)_+$ at every positive radius, with $0\le\beta<k/3$. At its first knot, $k=2/3$, the squared-radius sum is $10/9$ and the normalized kernel sum $K_{2,1}$ is $1$. At its second knot, $k=1/2$, both the squared-radius sum and $K_{2,2}$ equal $5/4$. Thus the deficit is at most $\gamma/9=4/81$. Adding the bonus once, at base zero, gives full-leg surplus at least $1/18-4/81=1/162$ in the scaled units.

**Finite prefixes.** On the positive-radius leg, the ratios $(t-\beta h)/(1-kh)$ and $(t+\beta(h+1))/(1-kh)$ increase with $h$. Their derivative numerators over $(1-kh)^2$ are $kt-\beta>0$ and $\beta+k(t+\beta)>0$. Homogeneity gives $F_{r_h}(s)=r_h^2F_1(s/r_h)$, so the sequence of normalized paired surpluses $G_h(r_h)/r_h^2$ is nonincreasing over its positive radii. If the last positive-radius normalized surplus of the original affine prefix is nonnegative, every preceding outer pair has nonnegative surplus and its own contribution is at least $1/18$. If that last normalized surplus is negative, every appended positive-radius term has negative surplus, so the prefix's total surplus is at least the full-leg surplus $1/162$. Zero radii give zero surplus. Scaling back and using the pointwise affine comparison proves the claimed additive $R^2/162$ in units of $V$ on the original low-normal sector, with the positive-tilt sign handled by the paired difference.

The preceding reductions now give the bound for every closed ambient halfspace through or containing $y$. When $m=0$, the cap $-\beta z+x_1\ge R/3$ with $\beta\ge2R/3$ has area $V(4/9)R^2$ at base zero and zero area at base one, giving the stated sharpness.
