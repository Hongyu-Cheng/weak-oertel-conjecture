## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(D_m=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:x+y\le m\}\). For \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2\), use the canonical law \(p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x)=\exp(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x)/Z_{\boldsymbol\theta}\), where \(Z_{\boldsymbol\theta}=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_m}\exp(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x)\). Here \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is the tilt denoted \(\boldsymbol\ell\) in equation (4) of bf1ff94487296374; the present \(m\) is the triangle dilation, and the ambient dimension is two.

For every \(\mathbf j\in D_m\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(D_m\) with mean \(\mathbf j\). Thus \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is precisely \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) of equation (13) of bf1ff94487296374 with \(E=D_m\); for any \(p_{\boldsymbol\theta}\) it is also the entropy minimizer in that fact's equation (2). Its support is the lattice support of the minimal face containing \(\mathbf j\).

The absolute constant \(K=30005053440\) satisfies, for all such \(m,\mathbf j\) and every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^2\),

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\bigl[\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\bigr]^4
\le K\left(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}[\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf X]\right)^2.
\]

## Proof

The entropy and face assertions used below are supplied by bf1ff94487296374. Indeed, for any mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\), log-affinity gives \(D(\nu\|p_{\boldsymbol\theta})=-H(\nu)-\log p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)\), so its entropy minimizer is the maximum-entropy law and is independent of \(\boldsymbol\theta\). That fact proves positivity on the minimal-face support and a finite exponential representation in its intrinsic affine hull. Consequently, a full-face row has probabilities proportional to \(\exp(\lambda_1x+\lambda_2y)\), an edge row is a finite geometric law in a primitive edge coordinate, and a vertex row is deterministic.

First consider a full-face row. Then \(j_1,j_2,m-j_1-j_2\) are positive integers. Introduce the composition coordinates \((N_1,N_2,N_3)=(X_1,X_2,m-X_1-X_2)\). They have sum \(m\) and affine rank two, not rank three. Their exponential coefficients are \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,0)\). Choose an index \(k\) whose coefficient is largest, and let \(i,h\) be the other two indices. The explicit intrinsic chart is \(\mathbf U=(N_i,N_h)\), with inverse \(N_k=m-U_1-U_2\). It identifies the support with \(D_m\). Subtracting the largest coefficient from all three coefficients shows that, in this chart, the row has law

\[
\Pr(\mathbf U=(x,y))=\frac{q_1^xq_2^y}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{(x,y)\in D_m}q_1^xq_2^y,
\qquad 0<q_1,q_2\le1.
\tag{1}
\]

The means of both chart coordinates are at least one. Every original direction has a corresponding chart direction: putting \((c_1,c_2,c_3)=(a_1,a_2,0)\), one has exactly
\(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)=(c_i-c_k)(N_i-\mathbb EN_i)+(c_h-c_k)(N_h-\mathbb EN_h)\).
It therefore suffices to bound all directional moments in (1). No independence of \(U_1,U_2\) is asserted or used.

For \(1\le t\le m\), translating the first coordinate gives

\[
\Pr(U_1\ge t)
=\frac{q_1^t}{Z}\sum_{(v,y)\in D_{m-t}}q_1^vq_2^y
\le q_1^t.
\]

If \(q_1<1\), the tail-sum identity implies \(\mathbb EU_1\le q_1/(1-q_1)\). Since \(\mathbb EU_1\ge1\), this gives \(q_1\ge1/2\). When \(q_1=1\) the same lower bound is immediate. The identical argument applies to \(q_2\). Hence
\(1/2\le q_1,q_2\le1\).

We now prove a variance bound for every law (1) with parameters in this range, whether or not its mean is integral. For a direction \(\mathbf b=(b_1,b_2)\), put \(f(\mathbf z)=\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbb E\mathbf U)\) and \(V=\mathbb Ef(\mathbf U)^2\). Let \(g_s=\sum_{x=0}^s q_1^xq_2^{s-x}\). For \(m\ge1\),
\(g_m=q_1g_{m-1}+q_2^m\le2g_{m-1}\), because \(q_2^m\le q_2g_{m-1}\le g_{m-1}\). It follows that \(\Pr(\mathbf U\in D_{m-1})\ge1/3\).

For either unit vector \(\mathbf e_t\), \(t\in\{1,2\}\), shifting points of \(D_{m-1}\) remains inside \(D_m\), and their probabilities obey \(\Pr(\mathbf U=\mathbf z+\mathbf e_t)=q_t\Pr(\mathbf U=\mathbf z)\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_t^2\Pr(\mathbf U\in D_{m-1})
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_{m-1}}\Pr(\mathbf U=\mathbf z)
       [f(\mathbf z+\mathbf e_t)-f(\mathbf z)]^2\\
&\le2\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_{m-1}}\Pr(\mathbf U=\mathbf z)f(\mathbf z+\mathbf e_t)^2
   +2\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_{m-1}}\Pr(\mathbf U=\mathbf z)f(\mathbf z)^2\\
&\le2q_t^{-1}V+2V\le6V.
\end{aligned}
\]

Applying this to both coordinates gives the useful bound
\(\|\mathbf b\|_2^2\le36V\).

For the continuous comparison, let \(\mathbf W\) have density proportional to \(q_1^{w_1}q_2^{w_2}\) on the convex triangle \(T_{m+1}=\{(w_1,w_2)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2:w_1+w_2\le m+1\}\). Set \(\mathbf B=(\lfloor W_1\rfloor,\lfloor W_2\rfloor)\), whose support, up to null boundary sets, is exactly \(D_m\). A cell based at \(\mathbf z=(x,y)\in D_m\) is a full unit square when \(x+y\le m-1\), and is the unit right triangle with offsets \(t_1,t_2\ge0\), \(t_1+t_2\le1\), when \(x+y=m\). If \(I_{\mathbf z}\) is the integral of \(q_1^{t_1}q_2^{t_2}\) over the appropriate offset cell, then \(1/4\le I_{\mathbf z}\le1\). For a full square the integrand is at least \(1/4\); for a boundary triangle it is at least \(1/2\) and the area is \(1/2\).

Writing \(\widetilde\nu\) for the law of \(\mathbf B\) and \(\nu\) for (1), the cell integrals show

\[
\frac{\widetilde\nu(\mathbf z)}{\nu(\mathbf z)}
=\frac{I_{\mathbf z}}{Z^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_m}q_1^{v_1}q_2^{v_2}I_{\mathbf v}},
\qquad
\frac14\le\frac{\widetilde\nu(\mathbf z)}{\nu(\mathbf z)}\le4.
\tag{2}
\]

We verify directly that every nonzero scalar projection of \(\mathbf W\) has a log-concave density. Choose an invertible linear coordinate system \((s,t)\) with first coordinate the desired projection. The transformed triangle has interval slices \(l(s)\le t\le u(s)\) on the interior of its projection interval. Convexity makes \(l\) convex and \(u\) concave: a convex combination of two lower, respectively upper, slice endpoints belongs to the intervening slice. In particular, \(u-l\) is positive and concave on that interior. The transformed exponential density is proportional to \(e^{\alpha s+\beta t}\), with a constant Jacobian. For \(\beta>0\), its marginal has logarithm
\(\text{constant}+\alpha s+\beta u(s)+\log(1-e^{-\beta(u(s)-l(s))})\).
The function \(v\mapsto\log(1-e^{-\beta v})\) is increasing and concave for \(v>0\), since its first derivative is \(\beta/(e^{\beta v}-1)>0\) and its second derivative is negative. This logarithm of the marginal is consequently concave. For \(\beta<0\), the corresponding expression is
\(\text{constant}+\alpha s+\beta l(s)+\log(1-e^{\beta(u(s)-l(s))})\), which is concave for the same reason, using \(\beta l\) concave. For \(\beta=0\), it is \(\text{constant}+\alpha s+\log(u(s)-l(s))\), also concave. Define the marginal density to be zero outside the open projection interval, including its endpoints; changing those endpoints does not change the law. This extension is log-concave on the whole line. Therefore the scaled fourth-moment conclusion of 8bb84543f325a261 applies to every nonzero projection of \(\mathbf W\).

Continue with the direction \(\mathbf b\) and variance \(V\) above. Put \(\Xi=\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf W-\mathbb E\mathbf U)\) and \(\Delta=\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf W-\mathbf B)\). The coordinate offsets lie in \([0,1]\), so \(\Delta^2\le2\|\mathbf b\|_2^2\) and \(\Delta^4\le4\|\mathbf b\|_2^4\). By (2),

\[
\mathbb E\Xi^2
\le2\mathbb E_{\widetilde\nu}f(\mathbf B)^2+2\mathbb E\Delta^2
\le8V+4\|\mathbf b\|_2^2
\le152V.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), the desired conclusion is immediate. Otherwise the projection result and 8bb84543f325a261 give \(\mathbb E|\Xi-\mathbb E\Xi|^4\le5072(\operatorname{Var}\Xi)^2\). Since both \(\operatorname{Var}\Xi\) and \((\mathbb E\Xi)^2\) are at most \(\mathbb E\Xi^2\),

\[
\mathbb E|\Xi|^4
\le8\mathbb E|\Xi-\mathbb E\Xi|^4+8|\mathbb E\Xi|^4
\le8\cdot5073(\mathbb E\Xi^2)^2
\le8\cdot5073\cdot152^2V^2.
\]

Finally \(f(\mathbf B)=\Xi-\Delta\), and the other direction of (2) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\nu}|f(\mathbf U)|^4
&\le4\mathbb E_{\widetilde\nu}|f(\mathbf B)|^4\\
&\le32\mathbb E|\Xi|^4+32\mathbb E|\Delta|^4\\
&\le\bigl(256\cdot5073\cdot152^2+128\cdot36^2\bigr)V^2\\
&=30005053440V^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

This establishes the full-face assertion, including every chart direction and hence every original direction.

For completeness, consider an edge row and prove its scalar estimate separately. Take the primitive edge coordinate to be \(x\) on a horizontal or diagonal edge and \(y\) on the vertical edge. Its support is \(\{0,1,\ldots,m\}\), and reversing that coordinate if necessary gives probabilities \(\Pr(T=t)=q^t/\sum_{v=0}^m q^v\) with \(0<q\le1\). For a nonvertex integer root its mean in this coordinate is an integer in \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\). The same translated tail sum used above gives \(\mathbb ET\le q/(1-q)\) when \(q<1\), so \(q\ge1/2\).

Let \(v_T=\operatorname{Var}T\). Since \(\Pr(T=m)\le\Pr(T=m-1)\), one has \(\Pr(T<m)\ge1/2\). With \(f_T(t)=t-\mathbb ET\), shifting \(0,\ldots,m-1\) by one, exactly as in the triangle variance calculation, gives
\(\Pr(T<m)\le2q^{-1}v_T+2v_T\le6v_T\).
Consequently \(v_T\ge1/12\).

Let \(J\), independent of \(T\), have density \(q^s/\int_0^1q^u\,du\) on \([0,1]\). The law of \(W=T+J\) has density proportional to \(q^w\) on \([0,m+1]\): on each unit interval the product density is precisely a common normalizing constant times \(q^{t+s}\). This continuous density is log-concave. Also \(\operatorname{Var}J\le1/4\), because \(\operatorname{Var}J\le\mathbb E(J-1/2)^2\le1/4\). Independence gives \(\operatorname{Var}W\le v_T+1/4\le4v_T\). Conditional Jensen and 8bb84543f325a261 now imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(T-\mathbb ET)^4
&=\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbb E(W-\mathbb EW\mid T)\bigr]^4\\
&\le\mathbb E(W-\mathbb EW)^4\\
&\le5072(\operatorname{Var}W)^2
\le81152v_T^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every original directional projection on an edge is a scalar multiple of \(T-\mathbb ET\). Scaling the last estimate therefore proves the claimed bound there, including directions constant on that edge. Finally, vertex rows are deterministic and both sides of the claimed inequality are zero. Since \(81152<K\), the single stated \(K\) works for all rows and all dilations.
