## Statement

Let \(G\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\) be integers. Partition \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\) into nonempty blocks \(I_1,\ldots,I_G\), and write \(k_i:=|I_i|\), so \(\sum_{i=1}^G k_i=k\). Let \(P\subseteq[0,2]^k\) be convex and invariant under every permutation that preserves each block. Suppose \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\ne\varnothing\). No closedness or full-dimensionality assumption is imposed, and \(P\) may couple the blocks arbitrarily.

For \(\mathbf x\in D\), write \(s_i(\mathbf x):=\sum_{\ell\in I_i}x_\ell\) and \(\mathbf s(\mathbf x):=(s_1(\mathbf x),\ldots,s_G(\mathbf x))\). Choose arbitrary positive real numbers \(v_{\mathbf s}\), indexed by \(\mathbf s\in\prod_{i=1}^G\{0,\ldots,2k_i\}\). Set \(A:=D\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\), and define

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}v_{\mathbf s(\mathbf x)},
&p_{(\mathbf x,0)}&:=\frac{v_{\mathbf s(\mathbf x)}}{Z},\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&:=\inf_{\substack{H\ni\mathbf z\\H\text{ a closed affine halfspace of }\mathbb R^{k+1}}}
\sum_{\mathbf w\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf w},
&M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Every halfspace in this definition has the form \(H=\{\mathbf w:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w\ge b\}\), with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Then

$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa_G\,8^{-(k+1)},
\qquad
\kappa_G:=(8\cdot271^3)^{-G}.
\tag{1}
$$

The spatial dimension is \(k\), height is zero, and the auxiliary ambient arithmetic dimension is \(N=k+1\), as in accepted facts \(803f398e39c669b9\) and \(3ddf5150fb1e3435\). Here \(G\) counts blocks; it is distinct from the locked problem's dimension parameters \(n,d\). The theorem does not change those parameters or assert an absolute coefficient as \(G\) grows.

## Proof

We first prove a bound when every block sum is at most its block size. Let \(Q\subseteq[0,2]^k\) be convex, invariant under permutations within each \(I_i\), and satisfy \(\sum_{\ell\in I_i}x_\ell\le k_i\) for every \(\mathbf x\in Q\) and every \(i\). Assume \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^k\ne\varnothing\), and put \(\mathcal A:=(Q\cap\mathbb Z^k)\times\{0\}\). For an integer point of \(Q\), define its deficit tuple by \(j_i:=k_i-\sum_{\ell\in I_i}x_\ell\), and write \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_G)\). Thus \(0\le j_i\le k_i\).

We use the combinatorial meanings of \(j,a,c_j,K_{j,a},K_j,N_j,d_{j,a},T_a\) from accepted fact \(803f398e39c669b9\), as also retained in accepted fact \(3ddf5150fb1e3435\). The present \(j_i,a_i\) are those scalar indices within block \(i\), with that predecessor's spatial dimension replaced by \(k_i\). The symbols \(\mathbf j,\mathbf a\) denote their tuples. The quantities \(c_{\mathbf j},K_{\mathbf j},N_{\mathbf j}\) below are respectively the number of binary roots, the number of balanced endpoint indices at one root, and the number of atoms at tuple rank \(\mathbf j\). They extend the predecessor's \(c_j,K_j,N_j\). Its scalar \(K_{j,a},d_{j,a}\), at dimension \(k_i\), are denoted \(K_i(j_i,a_i),d_i(j_i,a_i)\). Its positive deficit profile is denoted \(w_{\mathbf j}\), to distinguish it from the original sum profile \(v_{\mathbf s}\).

All lattice points are ternary. At deficit \(j_i\), if block \(i\) has \(a_i\) twos, its numbers of zeros and ones must respectively be \(a_i+j_i\) and \(k_i-2a_i-j_i\). Hence \(0\le a_i\le\lfloor(k_i-j_i)/2\rfloor\). Let \(J\) be the set of deficit tuples occurring in \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^k\). For \(\mathbf j\in J\), define \(F_{\mathbf j}\) to be the set of tuples \(\mathbf a\) of numbers of twos that occur at deficit tuple \(\mathbf j\). The support is finite, so \(J\) and every \(F_{\mathbf j}\) are finite and nonempty. Permutations within the blocks act transitively on the points with fixed counts in every block. Consequently the presence of \(\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}\) includes its entire product of block-permutation orbits.

We claim that \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is a downset in the product of the chains \(\{0,\ldots,\lfloor(k_i-j_i)/2\rfloor\}\). Indeed, suppose \(\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}\) and \(a_i>0\). A point with these counts has a two and a zero in block \(i\), since its zero count is \(a_i+j_i\ge a_i>0\). Let \(\pi\) exchange one such two and zero, leaving all other coordinates unchanged. Both the point and its image under \(\pi\) lie in \(Q\). Their midpoint lies in \(Q\) by convexity and is integral: the selected two and zero both become ones. Every block sum is unchanged, and the tuple of two counts becomes \(\mathbf a-\mathbf e_i\). Thus \(\mathbf a-\mathbf e_i\in F_{\mathbf j}\). Repeating this operation establishes the downset claim. In particular \(\mathbf0\in F_{\mathbf j}\). This argument imposes no box or independence condition on \(F_{\mathbf j}\).

Choose arbitrary positive \(w_{\mathbf j}\) for all tuples \(\mathbf j\in\prod_i\{0,\ldots,k_i\}\). Give each atom of \(\mathcal A\) at tuple rank \(\mathbf j\) probability \(w_{\mathbf j}/Z_q\), where \(Z_q\) is the sum of these weights over \(\mathcal A\). Denote this law by \(q\), and define \(\tau_q,M_3(q)\) using the same ambient closed halfspaces as in the statement. For admissible scalar indices define

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i(j_i,a_i)&:=\frac{(k_i-j_i)!}{a_i!^2(k_i-j_i-2a_i)!},
&d_i(j_i,a_i)&:=\binom{j_i+a_i}{a_i},\\
c_{\mathbf j}&:=\prod_{i=1}^G\binom{k_i}{j_i},
&K_{\mathbf j}&:=\sum_{\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}}\prod_{i=1}^G K_i(j_i,a_i),\\
N_{\mathbf j}&:=\sum_{\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}}
\prod_{i=1}^G\frac{k_i!}{a_i!(a_i+j_i)!(k_i-2a_i-j_i)!},
&Z_q&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in J}N_{\mathbf j}w_{\mathbf j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Here \(N_{\mathbf j}\) counts the original atoms of \(\mathcal A\) at rank \(\mathbf j\), by the complete-orbit property proved above. Since \(\mathbf0\in F_{\mathbf j}\), we have \(K_{\mathbf j}\ge1\). Factoring the factorial identity separately within each block gives the exact relation

$$
N_{\mathbf j}
=c_{\mathbf j}\sum_{\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}}
\prod_{i=1}^G\frac{K_i(j_i,a_i)}{d_i(j_i,a_i)}.
\tag{3}
$$

For each \(\mathbf j\in J\), choose \(L_i\subseteq I_i\) with \(|L_i|=k_i-j_i\), and let \(L:=\bigcup_iL_i\). The binary root \(\mathbf y_L:=(\mathbf1_L,0)\) is an atom because \(\mathbf0\in F_{\mathbf j}\). It has probability \(w_{\mathbf j}/Z_q\). For \(\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}\), choose disjoint \(E_i,F_i\subseteq L_i\) of sizes \(|E_i|=|F_i|=a_i\) for every \(i\), and form

$$
\mathbf z_+:=\left(\mathbf1_L+\sum_{i=1}^G(\mathbf1_{E_i}-\mathbf1_{F_i}),0\right),
\qquad
\mathbf z_-:=\left(\mathbf1_L-\sum_{i=1}^G(\mathbf1_{E_i}-\mathbf1_{F_i}),0\right).
\tag{4}
$$

The names \(E_i,F_i\) extend the endpoint-index sets \(E,F\) of fact \(3ddf5150fb1e3435\), which in turn are the \(P,Q\) endpoint-index sets of fact \(803f398e39c669b9\); neither is the convex set used here. Both endpoints in (4) have the fixed deficit tuple \(\mathbf j\) and the two-count tuple \(\mathbf a\). The complete-orbit property puts them in \(\mathcal A\), each with its original probability \(w_{\mathbf j}/Z_q\). Their midpoint is \(\mathbf y_L\).

For fixed \(\mathbf a\), there are exactly \(\prod_iK_i(j_i,a_i)\) ordered choices of all the sets \(E_i,F_i\), so there are \(K_{\mathbf j}\) such indices in total. The map from an index to \(\mathbf z_+\) is injective: within each block its twos recover \(E_i\), and its zeros inside \(L_i\) recover \(F_i\), hence also \(a_i\). Simultaneously exchanging \(E_i\) and \(F_i\) in every block is an involution that exchanges the two endpoints. Its only fixed index has every \(E_i=F_i=\varnothing\), because these sets are disjoint. Thus the indexed points consist of the root and \((K_{\mathbf j}-1)/2\) disjoint pairs of other atoms.

Every closed affine halfspace containing the root contains at least one endpoint from each such pair. Otherwise both affine-functional values would be strictly below the threshold, whereas their midpoint value is at least the threshold. This applies to every nonzero ambient normal, including normals with a height component. Consequently

$$
\tau_q(\mathbf y_L)
\ge\frac{K_{\mathbf j}+1}{2}\frac{w_{\mathbf j}}{Z_q}
\ge\frac{K_{\mathbf j}w_{\mathbf j}}{2Z_q}.
\tag{5}
$$

There are \(c_{\mathbf j}\) binary roots at tuple rank \(\mathbf j\), and roots at different tuple ranks are distinct. Restricting the nonnegative sampling sum defining the original moment of \(q\) to these roots yields

$$
M_3(q)\ge\frac{\mathcal V}{8Z_q^4},
\qquad
\mathcal V:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in J}c_{\mathbf j}K_{\mathbf j}^3w_{\mathbf j}^4.
\tag{6}
$$

Put \(\mathcal S:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in J}N_{\mathbf j}^{4/3}/(c_{\mathbf j}^{1/3}K_{\mathbf j})\). Hölder with exponents \(4\) and \(4/3\), applied to the exact identity

$$
Z_q=\sum_{\mathbf j\in J}
\bigl(c_{\mathbf j}K_{\mathbf j}^3w_{\mathbf j}^4\bigr)^{1/4}
\left(\frac{N_{\mathbf j}^{4/3}}{c_{\mathbf j}^{1/3}K_{\mathbf j}}\right)^{3/4},
$$

gives \(Z_q^4\le\mathcal V\mathcal S^3\).

We now bound \(\mathcal S\) without factoring any coupled feasible set. The finite-list inequality of equation (6) of fact \(3ddf5150fb1e3435\), equivalently equation (10) of fact \(803f398e39c669b9\), states that for positive finite lists \(b_\nu,d_\nu\),

$$
\left(\sum_\nu\frac{b_\nu}{d_\nu}\right)^{4/3}
\le\left(\sum_\nu\frac{b_\nu^{1/3}}{d_\nu^{4/3}}\right)
\left(\sum_\nu b_\nu^3\right)^{1/3}
\le\left(\sum_\nu\frac{b_\nu^{1/3}}{d_\nu^{4/3}}\right)\sum_\nu b_\nu.
\tag{7}
$$

Indeed, Hölder with exponents \(4/3\) and \(4\) applies to the factors \((b_\nu^{1/3}/d_\nu^{4/3})^{3/4}\) and \(b_\nu^{3/4}\), whose product is \(b_\nu/d_\nu\); the second inequality follows from \(\sum_\nu b_\nu^3\le(\sum_\nu b_\nu)^3\). The index \(\nu\) may be an arbitrary finite tuple index. At each fixed \(\mathbf j\in J\), apply (7) over \(\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}\) with \(b_{\mathbf a}:=\prod_iK_i(j_i,a_i)\) and \(d_{\mathbf a}:=\prod_i d_i(j_i,a_i)\). Equations (2)-(3) then imply

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S
&\le\sum_{\mathbf j\in J}\sum_{\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}}
\prod_{i=1}^G
\frac{\binom{k_i}{j_i}K_i(j_i,a_i)^{1/3}}{d_i(j_i,a_i)^{4/3}}\\
&\le\prod_{i=1}^G
\left(\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor k_i/2\rfloor}T_a(k_i)\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

where the full scalar sum is

$$
T_a(m):=\sum_{j=0}^{m-2a}
\frac{\binom m j
\left((m-j)!/(a!^2(m-j-2a)!)\right)^{1/3}}
{\binom{j+a}{a}^{4/3}}
\qquad\left(0\le a\le\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor,\ m\ge1\right).
\tag{9}
$$

To justify the second line of (8), every original term has \(a_i\ge0\), \(j_i\ge0\), and \(j_i+2a_i\le k_i\) for every \(i\). Add the nonnegative terms for all tuples satisfying just these coordinatewise conditions, omitting the restrictions \(\mathbf j\in J\) and \(\mathbf a\in F_{\mathbf j}\). The resulting finite index set is the Cartesian product of the full scalar sets \(\{(j_i,a_i):a_i\ge0,\ j_i\ge0,\ j_i+2a_i\le k_i\}\). The summand is a product of scalar factors, so distributivity factors this enlarged sum into exactly the product in (8). The actual \(F_{\mathbf j}\), \(K_{\mathbf j}\), atom counts, and law have not been replaced by products.

For each \(m\ge1\), (9) is exactly the \(T_a\) of equation (11) of fact \(803f398e39c669b9\), with that fact's spatial dimension \(k=m\), its scalar indices \(j,a\) unchanged, and \(r_{\max}=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\). Its equations (12)-(19) prove the matching full-sum bounds

$$
T_0(m)=2^m,
\qquad
T_a(m)\le2^m\,3a(9/10)^a
\quad\left(1\le a\le\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor\right).
\tag{10}
$$

These are combinatorial inequalities independent of any support cutoff or feasible tuple set. Summing them gives \(\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}T_a(m)\le2^m(1+3\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}a(9/10)^a)=271\,2^m\). In particular when \(m=1\) only \(T_0(1)\) occurs, so blocks of size one are included. Equation (8) therefore yields

$$
\mathcal S\le\prod_{i=1}^G(271\,2^{k_i})=271^G2^k.
\tag{11}
$$

Combining (6), \(Z_q^4\le\mathcal V\mathcal S^3\), and (11) proves the lower-block-sum estimate

$$
M_3(q)\ge\frac1{8\mathcal S^3}
\ge271^{-3G}8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{12}
$$

The argument permits missing tuple ranks, arbitrary coupling of the feasible orbit sets, and proper affine support: it uses only finitely many present ranks, the midpoint operation, and the original ambient halfspace inequalities. Neither closedness nor an interior point of \(Q\) was used.

We apply (12) to disjoint sectors of the original support. For \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_G)\in\{0,1\}^G\), define

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}
&:=\left\{\mathbf x\in P:
s_i(\mathbf x)\le k_i\text{ if }\varepsilon_i=0,
\quad s_i(\mathbf x)>k_i\text{ if }\varepsilon_i=1,
\quad 1\le i\le G\right\},\\
A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}
&:=(P_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\cap\mathbb Z^k)\times\{0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

The sets \(A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) partition \(A\) disjointly. Every \(P_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) is convex and invariant under permutations within the blocks; strict inequalities cause no problem for convexity. Empty sectors will be omitted whenever a conditional law is formed.

Define an affine map \(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}:\mathbb R^{k+1}\to\mathbb R^{k+1}\) by leaving height unchanged and, for \(\ell\in I_i\), replacing \(x_\ell\) by \(x_\ell\) if \(\varepsilon_i=0\), and by \(2-x_\ell\) if \(\varepsilon_i=1\). This is an affine involution preserving the lattice and commuting with every within-block permutation. Let \(Q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) be the spatial image of \(P_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\). It is convex and invariant under those permutations, is contained in \([0,2]^k\), and has every block sum at most \(k_i\). In fact a reflected block has sum strictly less than \(k_i\). Because \(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) preserves integrality in both directions,
\(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})=(Q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\cap\mathbb Z^k)\times\{0\}\).

For a nonempty sector, put \(Z_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}:=\sum_{(\mathbf x,0)\in A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}}v_{\mathbf s(\mathbf x)}\), and let \(q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) be the normalized restriction of \(p\) to that sector. An image point of deficit tuple \(\mathbf j\) has original block sums \(s_i=k_i+(2\varepsilon_i-1)j_i\). Consequently the pushforward law \(\widetilde q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}:=(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})_*q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) has precisely the positive tuple-deficit profile

$$
w^{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}_{\mathbf j}
:=v_{(k_1+(2\varepsilon_1-1)j_1,\ldots,k_G+(2\varepsilon_G-1)j_G)},
\tag{14}
$$

and its normalizer is \(Z_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\). The absent image ranks, including \(j_i=0\) whenever \(\varepsilon_i=1\), are allowed in the lower-block-sum estimate.

We verify that this affine reflection preserves the actual ambient depths. Write \(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(\mathbf w)=M_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\mathbf w+\mathbf a_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\), where \(M_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) is diagonal, with entries \((-1)^{\varepsilon_i}\) in block \(I_i\) and last entry one, and where \(\mathbf a_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) has entries \(2\varepsilon_i\) in block \(I_i\) and last entry zero. For a halfspace \(H=\{\mathbf w:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w\ge b\}\), its inverse image under \(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) is

$$
T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^{-1}(H)
=\left\{\mathbf w:
(M_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf w
\ge b-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf a_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\right\}.
\tag{15}
$$

The new normal is nonzero whenever \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\), since \(M_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) is invertible. Thus inverse image gives a bijection between all admissible closed affine halfspaces containing \(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\mathbf z\) and all such halfspaces containing \(\mathbf z\), with equal corresponding law masses. It follows that \(\tau_{\widetilde q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}}(T_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\mathbf z)=\tau_{q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}}(\mathbf z)\) and \(M_3(\widetilde q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})=M_3(q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})\). Applying (12) to \(Q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\) with profile (14) gives

$$
M_3(q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})\ge271^{-3G}8^{-(k+1)}
\quad\text{for every nonempty sector.}
\tag{16}
$$

Finally put \(\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}:=p(A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})\), including zero for empty sectors. These numbers are nonnegative and sum to one over \(\{0,1\}^G\). For a nonempty sector and \(\mathbf z\in A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\), every admissible halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf z\) satisfies
\(p(A\cap H)\ge\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\cap H)\ge\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\tau_{q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}}(\mathbf z)\).
Taking the infimum yields \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\tau_{q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}}(\mathbf z)\). The sampling weights on this sector are \(p_{\mathbf z}=\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}(q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})_{\mathbf z}\). The sectors are disjoint, so (16) implies

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon:A_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\ne\varnothing}
\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^4 M_3(q_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})\\
&\ge271^{-3G}8^{-(k+1)}
\sum_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^G}\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^4\\
&\ge271^{-3G}8^{-G}8^{-(k+1)}
=(8\cdot271^3)^{-G}8^{-(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$

For the last inequality, convexity of \(t\mapsto t^4\) gives \(2^{-G}\sum_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}^4\ge(2^{-G}\sum_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon}\alpha_{\boldsymbol\varepsilon})^4=2^{-4G}\), hence the sum is at least \(2^{-3G}=8^{-G}\). This also covers any number of empty sectors. Nonemptiness of \(D\) ensures that some sector is nonempty and all normalizations used are positive.

Every probability in (17) belongs to the original law or its explicitly normalized restriction, and every depth uses the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). All boundaries, missing ranks, blocks of size one, and supports of smaller affine dimension have been retained. Equation (17) proves (1).
