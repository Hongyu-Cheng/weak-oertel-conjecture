## Statement

Let \(d\geq1\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d\) be a finite set of distinct vectors with \(\operatorname{aff}(A)=\mathbb R^d\), and put \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\).

If \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\), then \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\) and

\[
|A|\leq4^d k,
\qquad
k:=|\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d|.
\tag{1}
\]

In the canonical type notation of accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45, let \(p=(p_a)_{a\in A}\) be a positive rational probability law with \(\sum_a p_a a=0\). For every integer \(m\geq2\) such that every \(mp_a\) is an integer, let \(\mathcal T_m(p)\) be the words in \(A^m\) with exactly \(mp_a\) occurrences of each \(a\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_m(p),\\
V_m&=\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_m)\in(\mathbb R^d)^m:
                \textstyle\sum_j\mathbf x_j=0\},\\
L_m&=V_m\cap(\mathbb Z^d)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then, with relative interior taken in \(V_m\),

\[
|\mathcal T_m(p)|
\leq4^{d(m-1)}\,|\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m|.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, along the permitted multiples of a denominator of \(p\),

\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\frac{\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|
      -\log|\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m|}{m}
\leq d\log4.
\tag{3}
\]

For a bounded set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) with nonempty lattice intersection, use exactly the unnormalized capacity of accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45:
\(B(E)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^d}
\sum_{a\in E\cap\mathbb Z^d}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}\).
For the finite set \(A\), the sum defining \(B(A)\) runs over \(A\).
If \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), without requiring \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\), then

\[
B(A)\leq4^dB(\operatorname{int}(P)).
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, every full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing zero in its interior satisfies \(B(P)\leq4^dB(\operatorname{int}(P))\). Every lattice count here uses the entire indicated lattice, not the subgroup generated by differences of support points.

## Proof

We first construct a sublattice excluding nonzero interior points. This construction applies to any full-dimensional bounded convex polytope \(P\) with \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), irrespective of a mean condition. Put \(k=|\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d|\), so \(k\geq1\).
If \(k=1\), take \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^d\), of index \(s=1\).
Suppose \(k\geq2\), and choose the power of two \(q=2^{\lceil\log_2 k\rceil}\), so \(k\leq q<2k\).
Let \(X=(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d)\setminus\{0\}\). On every primitive oriented integer ray meeting \(X\), its points form an initial segment \(u,2u,\ldots,Lu\) with \(1\leq L\leq k-1<q\). Indeed, every intermediate point of the segment from zero to an interior point is interior, by convexity and an interior ball at zero. Finiteness follows from boundedness of \(P\). These ray lengths sum to \(|X|=k-1\).

Choose \(c\) uniformly in \((\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^d\). A primitive integer vector \(u\) has at least one odd coordinate, so \(c\cdot u\) is uniform modulo \(q\). Some point of the ray segment \(u,\ldots,Lu\) belongs to the kernel of \(c\) precisely when the additive order of \(c\cdot u\) in \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) is at most \(L\). All such orders are powers of two. For \(r=2^{\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor}\), this event is therefore exactly \(r(c\cdot u)=0\), and it contains exactly \(r\) of the \(q\) possible residues. Its probability is \(r/q\leq L/q\).
The union bound over the primitive oriented rays gives probability at most
\((k-1)/q<1\) that any point of \(X\) belongs to the kernel. Hence some \(c\) has no such point. For that \(c\), let
\(\Gamma=\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:c\cdot x=0\pmod q\}\).
Then \(\mathbb Z^d/\Gamma\) is cyclic of order \(s\) dividing \(q\), and

\[
\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\Gamma=\{0\},
\qquad
s<2k.
\tag{5}
\]

In particular \(s\) is a power of two. This completes the sublattice construction.

We next prove an auxiliary count. Suppose \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d\) is finite, has full affine rank, satisfies \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\), and a full-rank sublattice \(\Gamma\) of cyclic power-of-two index \(s\) satisfies \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\Gamma=\{0\}\), where \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\). Write \(M=|A|\). The arithmetic mean belongs to \(P\), and in fact \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\): a supporting nonzero functional at a boundary point zero would be nonnegative on \(A\), have sum zero on \(A\), and hence vanish on every point of \(A\), contrary to full affine rank. We will prove

\[
M\leq s\binom{2d+1}{d}.
\tag{6}
\]

Fix an odd prime \(\ell>M\), and let \(K\) be an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_\ell\). The probability-law symbol \(p\) from the statement is not used for this prime. Since \(s\) is a power of two, \(\ell\nmid s\). Let \(\Omega\) be the set of characters \(\chi:\mathbb Z^d/\Gamma\to K^\times\). The quotient is cyclic of order \(s\), and \(z^s-1\) has \(s\) distinct roots in \(K\), so \(|\Omega|=s\). Write \(\chi_i=\chi(e_i+\Gamma)\).

Consider the Laurent polynomial ring \(R=K[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_d^{\pm1}]\) and the \(M\)-dimensional vector space \(V=\operatorname{span}_K\{z^a:a\in A\}\), where \(z^a=\prod_i z_i^{a_i}\). Distinct integer exponents remain distinct basis monomials; no quotient has yet been taken.
For \(\chi\in\Omega\), substitute \(z_i=\chi_i+y_i\) to obtain a map \(E_\chi:R\to K[[y_1,\ldots,y_d]]\). It is well defined because \(\chi_i\neq0\), so each \(\chi_i+y_i\) is a unit.
It is injective: after clearing a Laurent monomial, substitution is an invertible translation of a polynomial, and the clearing monomial has a unit image. For nonzero \(f\), define \(\operatorname{ord}_\chi(f)\) to be the least total degree occurring in \(E_\chi(f)\). Nonzero lowest homogeneous parts multiply in a polynomial domain, so
\(\operatorname{ord}_\chi(fg)=\operatorname{ord}_\chi(f)+\operatorname{ord}_\chi(g)\).

For every integer \(N\geq0\), the condition \(\operatorname{ord}_\chi(f)\geq N\) is equivalent to
\(f\in(z_1-\chi_1,\ldots,z_d-\chi_d)^N R\).
To verify this for a Laurent polynomial, multiply by a monomial to obtain a polynomial. Polynomial Taylor expansion gives the asserted ideal membership precisely when the terms of degree below \(N\) vanish. Multiplication or division by that monomial, a unit of \(R\) whose Taylor constant is nonzero, preserves both the ideal membership and the order.

Put \(\nu(f)=\min_{\chi\in\Omega}\operatorname{ord}_\chi(f)\) for nonzero \(f\), and, for \(j\geq0\), put
\(V_j=\{0\}\cup\{f\in V\setminus\{0\}:\nu(f)\geq j\}\).
These are decreasing linear subspaces. Multiplying all of \(V\) by one fixed monomial gives polynomials of bounded total degree; their translated polynomials have the same degree bound. Thus \(V_j=0\) for all sufficiently large \(j\). For \(j\geq1\), membership in \(V_j\) imposes at most \(s\binom{d+j-1}{d}\) linear conditions, one for each Taylor coefficient of total degree below \(j\) at each of the \(s\) points. With \((x)_+=\max\{x,0\}\), it follows that

\[
\dim_K V_j\geq
 \left(M-s\binom{d+j-1}{d}\right)_+,
\qquad
\sum_{j\geq1}\dim_K V_j\geq
\mathcal L_s:=
\sum_{j\geq1}\left(M-s\binom{d+j-1}{d}\right)_+.
\tag{7}
\]

The last sum is finite and depends on \(d,M,s\), not on \(\ell\).

Choose a basis adapted to the filtration, by choosing a complement of \(V_{j+1}\) in each \(V_j\). Thus the basis vectors with \(\nu\geq j\) form a basis of \(V_j\) for every \(j\). We make their supported exponent pivots distinct without changing this property.
Choose a real linear functional \(\xi\) taking distinct values on \(A\), and call the supported exponent of smallest \(\xi\)-value the pivot. If two basis vectors \(f,g\) have the same pivot, arrange their names so that \(\nu(f)\leq\nu(g)\), and replace \(f\) by \(f'=f-cg\), with the scalar \(c\) cancelling their pivot. This elementary basis operation leaves a nonzero vector and gives \(\nu(f')\geq\nu(f)\). Equality must hold. Otherwise, for \(j=\nu(f)+1\), the unchanged basis vectors already belonging to \(V_j\) form a basis of \(V_j\), while the new independent vector \(f'\) would also belong to \(V_j\), a contradiction.
Hence the operation preserves every filtration level of the basis. It strictly increases the rank of the changed pivot in the ordering of \(A\). The sum of all pivot ranks is an integer bounded above by \(M^2\), so repetition terminates at a filtration-adapted basis \(f_1,\ldots,f_M\) with distinct pivots. Those pivots exhaust \(A\), and

\[
\sum_{i=1}^M \nu(f_i)
=\sum_{j\geq1}\dim_K V_j
\geq\mathcal L_s.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(F=\prod_{i=1}^M f_i\). For every \(\chi\in\Omega\), ordinary order additivity at that particular point, together with (8), yields

\[
\operatorname{ord}_\chi(F)
=\sum_{i=1}^M\operatorname{ord}_\chi(f_i)
\geq\sum_{i=1}^M\nu(f_i)
\geq\mathcal L_s.
\tag{9}
\]

No additivity assertion for the minimum \(\nu\) is needed.
The sum of the distinct pivots is \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\). Any selection of a nonpivot exponent in at least one factor increases the sum of the \(\xi\)-values strictly. Therefore \(F\) has a nonzero constant coefficient, contributed uniquely by the product of its pivot terms; every other supported exponent has strictly positive \(\xi\)-value. Also \(\operatorname{supp}(F)\subseteq MP\). For every positive integer \(t\), it follows that \(F^t\) has a nonzero constant coefficient, has all other supported exponents of positive \(\xi\)-value, has support in \(tMP\), and satisfies
\(\operatorname{ord}_\chi(F^t)\geq t\mathcal L_s\) for every \(\chi\in\Omega\).

Set \(t=\lfloor(\ell-1)/M\rfloor\), so \(t\geq1\) and \(tM<\ell\). A dilation of \(P\) by a factor strictly between zero and one lies in \(\operatorname{int}(P)\): an interior ball at zero, combined with any point of \(P\) by convexity, provides an interior ball at every point of the dilation. Consequently

\[
tMP\cap\ell\Gamma=\{0\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Indeed, if \(\ell\gamma\in tMP\) with \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), then
\(\gamma\in(tM/\ell)P\subseteq\operatorname{int}(P)\), and the defining property of \(\Gamma\) forces \(\gamma=0\).

We use the group algebra of \(G=\mathbb Z^d/(\ell\Gamma)\). The quotient

\[
R/(z^{\ell\gamma}-1:\gamma\in\Gamma)
\cong K[G]
\tag{11}
\]

has the classes of the monomials indexed by \(G\) as a \(K\)-basis. One direct verification is to send each Laurent monomial to its exponent class. Monomials with the same class differ by a multiple of a relation in (11), and regrouping a Laurent polynomial by its exponent classes identifies its kernel exactly with the indicated ideal. In this basis the coefficient of the identity class in the image of \(F^t\) is the sum of the coefficients at exponents in \(\ell\Gamma\). By (10) and the support of \(F^t\), this is just its nonzero constant coefficient. Thus the image of \(F^t\) in \(K[G]\) is nonzero.

We now check all local factors of this quotient. An integer basis of the full-rank sublattice \(\Gamma\) identifies
\(E:=\Gamma/(\ell\Gamma)\) with \((\mathbb Z/\ell\mathbb Z)^d\).
It is a subgroup of \(G\), and \(G/E\cong\mathbb Z^d/\Gamma\) is cyclic of order \(s\). The map
\(G/E\to\ell G\), \(g+E\mapsto\ell g\), is well defined because \(\ell E=0\). It is injective because a class killed by \(\ell\) in the group \(G/E\) of order \(s\) must be zero, and it is surjective by the definition of \(\ell G\). Hence \(\ell G\) is cyclic of order \(s\). Its intersection with \(E\) is trivial because their orders are coprime. Their orders multiply to \(|G|=s\ell^d\), so
\(G=E\oplus\ell G\).

The group algebra of \(E\) is
\(K[w_1,\ldots,w_d]/(w_1^\ell-1,\ldots,w_d^\ell-1)\).
In characteristic \(\ell\), changing variables \(u_i=w_i-1\) identifies this with
\(K[u_1,\ldots,u_d]/(u_1^\ell,\ldots,u_d^\ell)\).
The group algebra of the cyclic group \(\ell G\) is \(K[v]/(v^s-1)\). Its \(s\) roots are distinct, so evaluation at those roots identifies it with the product of \(s\) copies of \(K\). Taking group algebras of the direct sum gives

\[
K[G]\cong
\prod_{\chi\in\Omega}
\frac{K[u_1,\ldots,u_d]}{(u_1^\ell,\ldots,u_d^\ell)}.
\tag{12}
\]

The indexing in (12) is precisely by the characters previously used. Every character of \(G\) into \(K^\times\) is trivial on \(E\), since \(K^\times\) has no nontrivial element of order \(\ell\). It therefore factors through \(G/E=\mathbb Z^d/\Gamma\), and conversely every \(\chi\in\Omega\) gives such a character. Thus the residue map of the factor indexed by \(\chi\) sends each original Laurent variable \(z_i\) to \(\chi_i\).

Each factor in (12) has maximal ideal \(J_\chi=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)\), and
\(J_\chi^{d(\ell-1)+1}=0\), since a monomial of degree greater than \(d(\ell-1)\) must have some exponent at least \(\ell\). The image of every \(z_i-\chi_i\) belongs to \(J_\chi\). By the Taylor-order/ideal equivalence proved above and (9), the image of \(F^t\) in this factor belongs to \(J_\chi^{t\mathcal L_s}\).
If \(t\mathcal L_s>d(\ell-1)\), it would vanish in every factor of (12), contradicting its nonzero image in (11). Therefore

\[
t\mathcal L_s\leq d(\ell-1).
\tag{13}
\]

Write \(\ell-1=tM+r\) with \(0\leq r<M\). Dividing (13) by \(t\) gives
\(\mathcal L_s\leq dM+d(M-1)/t\).
There are arbitrarily large odd primes: a finite list of all primes would omit any prime divisor of one plus their product. Letting \(\ell\) tend to infinity makes \(t\) tend to infinity, while \(\mathcal L_s\) is independent of \(\ell\). Hence \(\mathcal L_s\leq dM\).
Since a positive part is at least its argument, retaining the first \(d+1\) summands in (7) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
dM
&\geq\mathcal L_s\\
&\geq(d+1)M
 -s\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\binom{d+j-1}{d}\\
&=(d+1)M-s\binom{2d+1}{d+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last identity follows by telescoping Pascal's identity. Rearranging proves (6).

Return to the mean-zero set \(A\) and \(k=|\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d|\). The sublattice construction, including the case \(k=1\), always has \(s<2k\). Combining (5) and (6) gives
\(M<2k\binom{2d+1}{d}\).
We apply this inequality to the fourth Cartesian power \(A^4\subseteq\mathbb Z^{4d}\). It has \(M^4\) points, full affine rank \(4d\), convex hull \(P^4\), and arithmetic sum zero: in each block the sum is \(M^3\sum_{a\in A}a=0\). Its interior lattice count is \(k^4\), because the interior of a finite Cartesian product of full-dimensional convex bodies is the product of their interiors. Therefore

\[
M^4<2k^4\binom{8d+1}{4d}.
\tag{14}
\]

For every integer \(N\geq4\), one has \(2\binom{2N+1}{N}<4^N\).
At \(N=4\), the two sides are \(252<256\). Moreover, the ratio of the binomial coefficients at \(N+1\) and \(N\) is
\((2N+3)(2N+2)/((N+2)(N+1))=4-2/(N+2)<4\), so induction proves the inequality for all \(N\geq4\).
Use it with \(N=4d\) in (14) to obtain \(M^4<4^{4d}k^4\), and take fourth roots. This proves (1), even with a strict inequality. The sublattice was only an auxiliary algebraic device; the final \(k\) counts the entire original lattice.

We apply (1) to the type-word set. The strict positivity and centeredness of \(p\), together with full affine rank, imply \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\) by the same supporting-functional argument used above. Accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45 proves that the objects \(Q_m,V_m,L_m\) defined in the statement have \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_m)=V_m\), \(\dim V_m=d(m-1)\), and the exact lattice chart

\[
\Phi_m:(\mathbb R^d)^{m-1}\longrightarrow V_m,\qquad
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1})
\longmapsto
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1},
 -\textstyle\sum_{j<m}\mathbf y_j),
\]

which maps \(\mathbb Z^{d(m-1)}\) bijectively onto the full lattice \(L_m\). These are the same objects and normalization as in that fact.
The sum of all distinct words in \(\mathcal T_m(p)\) is fixed by every block permutation, since the set is invariant under such permutations. This sum belongs to \(V_m\), because each word has block sum zero. A block-permutation-fixed vector in \(V_m\) has all blocks equal and hence equals zero.
It follows that \(\Phi_m^{-1}(\mathcal T_m(p))\) is a finite full-affine-rank integer set with arithmetic mean zero. Applying (1) in dimension \(d(m-1)\), and using that a linear isomorphism commutes with relative interior, proves (2). Its interior count is positive because zero is interior in this chart. Taking logarithms of (2), dividing by \(m\), and passing to the indicated limsup proves (3).

Finally, we prove the capacity conclusion and specify where the tilt is used. Suppose \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), and fix any positive rational centered law \(p\) on \(A\). Write \(U=\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^d\). Accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45 establishes that every block of a point in \(\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\) belongs to \(U\). The block sum is zero. Consequently, for every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m|
&\leq
\sum_{\substack{(\mathbf u_1,\ldots,\mathbf u_m)\in U^m\\
                 \sum_j\mathbf u_j=0}}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_j\mathbf u_j}\\
&\leq
\left(\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}\right)^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Taking the infimum gives the bound \(B(\operatorname{int}(P))^m\). This is valid even if the infimum is not attained. The capacity is at least one, since \(0\in U\).
The exact multinomial count
\(|\mathcal T_m(p)|=m!/\prod_a(mp_a)!\) satisfies
\(\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|=mH(p)+O_A(\log(m+1))\), where
\(H(p)=-\sum_a p_a\log p_a\); the elementary factorial estimate and this limit are also proved in accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45. Combining (2) and (15) and letting \(m\) tend to infinity along the permitted multiples gives

\[
H(p)\leq d\log4+\log B(\operatorname{int}(P)).
\tag{16}
\]

For clarity, the final capacity passage uses precisely the same mean-zero entropy normalization as that accepted fact. The function
\(f(\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}\)
is coercive because \(P\) contains a ball of some radius \(\rho>0\) about zero, and therefore
\(f(\boldsymbol\theta)\geq
e^{\max_{a\in A}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot a}
\geq e^{\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}\).
It attains its minimum at \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\), and differentiation gives the positive centered law
\(p_a^*=e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot a}/B(A)\).
Its entropy is \(H(p^*)=\log B(A)\).
The matrix with columns \((1,a)\), \(a\in A\), has rank \(d+1\). Choose \(d+1\) independent columns, approximate the other coordinates of \(p^*\) by rational numbers, and solve for these chosen coordinates using the inverse of the corresponding integer square matrix. The resulting rational laws satisfy normalization and mean zero, converge to \(p^*\), and remain positive for sufficiently close approximations. If there are no remaining coordinates, \(p^*\) itself is rational. Continuity of entropy now lets (16) pass to \(p^*\), giving (4) after exponentiation.
At no point is the arithmetic mean of \(U\) assumed to vanish.
Taking \(A=P\cap\mathbb Z^d\) for a full-dimensional lattice polytope preserves its convex hull and satisfies \(B(A)=B(P)\), proving the stated polytope consequence.
