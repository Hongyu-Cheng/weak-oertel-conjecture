## Statement

Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be nonempty and closed. Define its \(S\)-Helly number \(h(S)\) to be the least positive integer \(h\) with the following property: for every integer \(N\ge h\) and every collection of convex sets \(K_1,\ldots,K_N\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), if
\[
S\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}K_i\ne\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\text{ with }|I|=h,
\]
then
\[
S\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^N K_i\ne\varnothing.
\]
Suppose \(h:=h(S)<\infty\), and let \(\mu\) be a nonzero finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^m\) satisfying \(\mu(\mathbb R^m\setminus S)=0\). Put \(M:=\mu(\mathbb R^m)>0\). Then there exists \(x\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) containing \(x\) satisfies
\[
\mu(G)\ge \frac{M}{h(S)}.
\]

Consequently, for all integers \(n,d\ge0\), every nonzero finite Borel measure \(\nu\) supported on
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d
\]
has a point \(x\in\Lambda\) such that every closed halfspace \(G\ni x\) satisfies
\[
\nu(G)\ge \frac{\nu(\mathbb R^{n+d})}{2^n(d+1)}.
\]

In particular, let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body and define its mixed-volume measure by
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathcal H_d\bigl(\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in A\cap C\}\bigr)
\]
for Borel \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). If \(W:=\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})>0\), then the point can be chosen in \(C\cap\Lambda\), and every closed halfspace \(G\ni x\) satisfies \(\mu_C(G)\ge W/[2^n(d+1)]\).

## Proof

We first prove the assertion for a general closed set \(S\). Since the closed balls centered at the origin increase to \(\mathbb R^m\), continuity from below of the finite measure \(\mu\) gives a closed ball \(B\) such that
\[
\mu(B^c)<\frac{M}{h}.
\]
Consider the family of closed \(S\)-convex sets
\[
\mathcal F:=\{B\cap S\}\mathbin\cup
\{H\cap S:H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mu(H)>M-M/h\}.
\]

The empty subfamily of \(\mathcal F\) has nonempty intersection by convention. Now let \(\mathcal A\) be a nonempty subfamily of \(\mathcal F\) with at most \(h\) members. Write its halfspace members as \(H_1\cap S,\ldots,H_r\cap S\), and let \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) record whether \(B\cap S\) is also present. Then \(1\le r+\varepsilon\le h\). Using \(\mu(S^c)=0\), \(\mu(H_i^c)=M-\mu(H_i)<M/h\), and \(\mu(B^c)<M/h\), we obtain
\[
\mu\!\left(S^c\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^r H_i^c\cup
  \begin{cases}B^c,&\varepsilon=1,\\ \varnothing,&\varepsilon=0,
  \end{cases}\right)
<\frac{(r+\varepsilon)M}{h}\le M.
\]
The set inside the measure is the complement of the intersection of the members of \(\mathcal A\). Hence that intersection has positive \(\mu\)-measure and is nonempty.

It follows that \(\mathcal F\) has the finite-intersection property. Indeed, a finite nonempty subfamily with fewer than \(h\) members intersects by the preceding paragraph. For a finite subfamily with \(N\ge h\) members, every \(h\)-member subfamily intersects by that paragraph, so the defining \(S\)-Helly property gives intersection of all \(N\) members. The empty subfamily was handled separately. Every member of \(\mathcal F\) is closed, and the member \(B\cap S\) is compact. Therefore the finite-intersection property and compactness give
\[
\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal F}F\ne\varnothing.
\]
Fix \(x\) in this intersection. In particular, \(x\in S\), and \(x\) belongs to every closed halfspace whose measure is strictly greater than \(M-M/h\).

Let \(G\) be any closed halfspace containing \(x\). The whole-space case is immediate. Otherwise write
\[
G=\{y\in\mathbb R^m:a\cdot y\le b\}
\]
with \(a\ne0\). Suppose for contradiction that \(\mu(G)<M/h\). For \(j\ge1\), set
\[
H_j:=\{y\in\mathbb R^m:a\cdot y\ge b+1/j\}.
\]
The closed halfspaces \(H_j\) increase to the open complement \(G^c=\{a\cdot y>b\}\). Hence continuity from below gives
\[
\mu(H_j)\uparrow\mu(G^c)=M-\mu(G)>M-M/h.
\]
For some \(j\), therefore, \(\mu(H_j)>M-M/h\). The definition of \(x\) forces \(x\in H_j\), whereas \(x\in G\) and \(G\cap H_j=\varnothing\), a contradiction. Thus \(\mu(G)\ge M/h\). This argument allows atoms and arbitrary mass on hyperplanes: the boundary of \(G\) remains in \(G\), and the increasing union of the \(H_j\) is exactly the open complement of \(G\).

We now specialize. Averkov and Weismantel's mixed-integer Helly theorem gives, for all \(r,q\ge0\),
\[
h(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb Z^q)=(r+1)2^q
\]
with the standard \(N\ge h\) quantifier used in the definition above. The coordinate permutation between \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb Z^n\) and \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is an invertible linear map; it carries convex sets to convex sets and preserves all intersection statements. Therefore
\[
h(\Lambda)=h(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)=2^n(d+1).
\]
Applying the first part with \(S=\Lambda\) proves the asserted bound for every nonzero finite Borel measure supported on \(\Lambda\).

Finally, for a convex body \(C\), compactness of its projection onto \(\mathbb R^n\) implies that only finitely many lattice vectors \(z\) index nonempty fibers. Thus \(\mu_C\) is a finite Borel measure, and by construction it is supported on \(C\cap\Lambda\), hence also on \(\Lambda\). When \(W>0\), the preceding conclusion supplies \(x\in\Lambda\) having the stated positive depth. If \(x\notin C\), strict separation of the point \(x\) from the compact convex set \(C\) gives \(a\ne0\) such that
\[
a\cdot x>\sup_{y\in C}a\cdot y.
\]
Then the closed halfspace \(G:=\{y:a\cdot y\ge a\cdot x\}\) contains \(x\) and is disjoint from \(C\), so \(\mu_C(G)=0\). This contradicts \(\mu_C(G)\ge W/[2^n(d+1)]>0\). Hence \(x\in C\cap\Lambda\), completing the proof.

## External sources

Amitabh Basu and Timm Oertel, "Centerpoints: A Link between Optimization and Convex Geometry," SIAM Journal on Optimization 27(2) (2017), 866--889, DOI 10.1137/16M1092908; author manuscript: https://www.ams.jhu.edu/~abasu9/papers/centerpoints.pdf . With \(F(S,\rho)\) defined as the maximum over \(x\in S\) of the minimum mass of a closed halfspace containing \(x\), their Theorem 15 states that for a probability measure \(\rho\) supported on a nonempty closed \(S\) with finite \(h(S)\), one has \(F(S,\rho)\ge1/h(S)\). Their Corollary 16 specializes this to \(S=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), giving \(1/[2^n(d+1)]\), including the mixed-volume measures of convex bodies. Applying Theorem 15 to \(\rho=\mu/M\) and rescaling gives exactly the finite-measure statement proved above. The proof above is self-contained and does not rely on the corrupted inequality signs in the local Markdown transcription.

Gennadiy Averkov and Robert Weismantel, "Transversal Numbers over Subsets of Linear Spaces," Advances in Geometry 12(1) (2012), 19--28, DOI 10.1515/advgeom.2011.028; primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0948 . Theorem 1.1(3) states exactly \(h(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb Z^q)=(r+1)2^q\) for nonnegative integers \(r,q\), using the standard convention that the finite family has at least \(h\) members. Taking \(r=d\), \(q=n\), and applying the coordinate permutation gives the Helly number used here.
