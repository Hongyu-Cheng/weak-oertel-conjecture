## Statement

Let \(Q\ge1\) be an integer, let \(C_{\mathrm{band}}\ge0\) be finite, and let \(\lambda>\max\{C_{\mathrm{band}},1\}\), \(h\in\mathbb R\), and \(b_1,\ldots,b_Q\in\mathbb R\). For \(u\in\{-1,0,1\}^Q\), define \(s(u)=|\{r:u_r=0\}|\), \(g(u)=\sum_{r=1}^Q3^{r-1}u_r\), and

\[
v(u)=\exp\left(-\lambda(g(u)-h)^2-\sum_{r=1}^Qb_ru_r\right),
\qquad W=\sum_{u\in\{-1,0,1\}^Q}3^{s(u)}2^{Q-s(u)}v(u).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{4^Q}{W}\max_{u\in\{-1,0,1\}^Q}
\frac{v(u)(3^{s(u)}+1)}2>\frac13.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular the infimum in the assigned scalar question is at least \(1/3\), uniformly over all its parameters and over \(Q\).

Here is the complete lattice table to which (1) applies. Set \(N=2Q\), \(\Omega_2=\{0,1\}^2\cup\{0,-1\}^2\), and \(K_2=\operatorname{conv}\Omega_2\). For \(x=(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(Q)})\in\mathbb R^{2Q}\), put \(u_r(x)=x^{(r)}_1-x^{(r)}_2\) and \(g(x)=\sum_r3^{r-1}u_r(x)\). Let \(U\) be the integer linear chart whose first \(N-1\) coordinates are all coordinates of \(x\) except \(x^{(1)}_1\), in a fixed order, and whose last coordinate is \(g(x)\). Put \(P=U(K_2^Q)\) and \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\). The exact original-coordinate map is \(\mathbf I=Ux\), \(x=U^{-1}\mathbf I\), and \(I_N=g(x)\). The table is

\[
\ell(\mathbf I)=\sum_rb_ru_r(U^{-1}\mathbf I),\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=\exp(-\lambda(I_N-h)^2-\ell(\mathbf I))
\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D).
\tag{2}
\]

For this table, \(D=U(\Omega_2^Q)\), its total original mass is the \(W\) above, and its integer dimension is \(n=N=2Q\). The notation \(D,P,W,w_{\max},\phi\), and \(m_{\log}\) has exactly the meaning in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc, with \(w_{\max}=\exp(-\min_D F)\), \(F(\mathbf I)=\lambda(I_N-h)^2+\ell(\mathbf I)\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=F(\mathbf I)-\min_DF\). No weight in (2) is rescaled. The parameter \(h\) here is only the Gaussian center, and \(N\) is only the integer dimension, not a continuous fiber dimension.

For an interval \([a,b]\) and a center \(\mathbf J\in D\) with \(J_N\in[a,b]\), define its original restricted depth by

\[
\tau_{[a,b]}(\mathbf J)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf J\in H}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\cap H\\I_N\in[a,b]}}w_{\mathbf I}.
\]

If \(\lambda(b-a)^2\le C_{\mathrm{band}}\), this interval contains exactly one supported integer height, namely \(J_N\). Write \(\mathbf J=Ux\), let \(u=(u_r(x))_r\), and let \(z(x)\) be the number of blocks of \(x\) equal to \((0,0)\). Then, including every equality atom,

\[
\tau_{[a,b]}(\mathbf J)=\frac{v(u)(3^{z(x)}+1)}2,
\qquad
\max_{\substack{\mathbf J\in D\\J_N=g(u)}}\tau_{\{g(u)\}}(\mathbf J)
=\frac{v(u)(3^{s(u)}+1)}2.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus some center and its singleton band have depth greater than \(W/(3\cdot4^Q)\). At that same center, the canonical integral logarithmic mass also satisfies \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,0;w)>W/(3\cdot4^Q)\).

## Proof

First verify the complete support. Every point of \(K_2\) is a convex combination of points having coordinates in \([-1,1]\) and coordinate difference in \([-1,1]\). It therefore satisfies both properties. An integer point of \(K_2\) has coordinates in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) and cannot be \((1,-1)\) or \((-1,1)\). The other seven possibilities are exactly \(\Omega_2\), and all seven belong to \(K_2\). Hence \(K_2\cap\mathbb Z^2=\Omega_2\). The points \(\pm(1,0)\) and \(\pm(0,1)\) show that \(K_2\) has full dimension. It is compact.

The coefficient of the omitted coordinate \(x^{(1)}_1\) in \(g(x)\) is one. All other coordinates are explicitly retained by \(U\), so solving the last equation for that omitted coordinate gives an integer linear combination of the output coordinates. Thus \(U\) and \(U^{-1}\) both preserve the integer lattice. Taking products proves \(D=U(\Omega_2^Q)\). Also \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), since the convex hull of a Cartesian product of finite sets is the product of their convex hulls. This proves the full lattice census, including boundary sites, and establishes compactness and full dimension of \(P\).

The function \(F\) in the statement is convex: its quadratic part is the nonnegative square of one linear coordinate, and \(\ell\) is linear. Extending \(\exp(-F)\) by zero outside \(P\) is log-concave and agrees with every lattice value in (2), including all zeros outside \(D\). Hence this is a globally extendable finite table in the sense of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc, with exactly the normalization stated above. In particular it uses a genuine rank-one Gaussian potential.

The height map is injective on digit vectors. If \(u\ne u'\), take the largest index \(r\) where they differ. Its contribution to \(g(u)-g(u')\) has absolute value at least \(3^{r-1}\), whereas all smaller contributions have absolute sum at most \(2\sum_{j<r}3^{j-1}=3^{r-1}-1\). Thus the heights differ. In a block, digit zero has exactly the three points \((0,0),(1,1),(-1,-1)\); digit one has exactly \((1,0),(0,-1)\); digit minus one has exactly \((0,1),(-1,0)\). Consequently the full fiber of height \(g(u)\) has exactly \(3^{s(u)}2^{Q-s(u)}\) atoms, each of original weight \(v(u)\). Summing over all digit vectors proves the asserted formula for \(W\).

We now prove the exact closed-halfspace depths (3), rather than use any unverified prior calculation. Every nonzero point of \(\Omega_2\) is an exposed vertex of \(K_2\). Indeed, for a positive point \(\mathbf1_E\), with nonempty \(E\subseteq\{1,2\}\), take an exposing vector having coefficient four on \(E\) and minus one off \(E\). Among positive Boolean points it uniquely maximizes at \(\mathbf1_E\). Every negative Boolean point has value at most \(2-|E|\), which is less than the value \(4|E|\) at \(\mathbf1_E\). Negating the exposing vector exposes \(-\mathbf1_E\). All these functional values are integers, so the gap from the exposed point to any other block atom is at least one.

Fix a center \(x\in\Omega_2^Q\) and its digit vector \(u\). Hold all its nonzero blocks fixed, and let its \(z=z(x)\) zero blocks vary. On the height fiber \(g(u)\), height injectivity forces each varying block to have digit zero. Therefore this fixed-block subset has precisely \(3^z\) points and is centrally symmetric about \(x\). Every point in it has weight \(v(u)\). Every containing closed halfspace contains \(x\) and at least one member of each of its \((3^z-1)/2\) noncentral antipodal pairs. This proves the lower bound \(v(u)(3^z+1)/2\) for every containing closed halfspace, including one whose boundary contains other atoms.

For attainment, sum the exposing functionals of the nonzero center blocks, subtracting their center values, and call the resulting affine functional \(L_{\mathrm{exp}}\). On the entire product support it is zero exactly when all those blocks remain fixed and is at most minus one otherwise. For \(z>0\), list the zero center blocks as \(r_1,\ldots,r_z\), and set \(\gamma(X)=\sum_{j=1}^z3^{j-1}X^{(r_j)}_1\). On their digit-zero product support each block is \(t_j(1,1)\), where \(t_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\). The largest nonzero term in this sum strictly dominates all smaller terms, so \(\gamma\) vanishes only when all varying blocks are zero. Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that \(\varepsilon|\gamma|<1\) at every product-support point; this is possible since the support is finite. The genuine closed halfspace \(L_{\mathrm{exp}}+\varepsilon\gamma\ge0\) contains the center, excludes every point of the restricted height fiber outside the fixed-block subset, and within that subset contains the center and exactly one member of each noncentral antipodal pair. Its restricted weight is the desired lower bound. If \(z=0\), the nonzero exposing functional \(L_{\mathrm{exp}}\) alone selects only the center. If all center blocks are zero, \(L_{\mathrm{exp}}=0\), but \(\gamma\ne0\) still defines the required halfspace. Transforming these halfspaces by \(U\) proves the same assertions in the original coordinates. Finally \(z(x)\le s(u)\), with equality attained by taking the zero vector in every zero-digit block. This proves (3).

Since \(\lambda>C_{\mathrm{band}}\), an allowed interval has length at most \(\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{band}}/\lambda}<1\). All heights are integers. An allowed interval containing a center therefore contains only its integer height, with the entire equality fiber included. This proves the assertion for every allowed band, not just for the selected singleton band.

It remains to prove (1) using the actual Gaussian interaction. Put \(\psi(u)=3^{s(u)}v(u)\) and \(a_* =\max_u\psi(u)>0\). For each \(Z\subseteq\{1,\ldots,Q\}\), let \(\mathcal A_Z\) be the digit vectors whose zero coordinates are exactly \(Z\). These are the parity classes of the digit grid: every coordinate outside \(Z\) is either one or minus one. Distinct heights in one such class differ by a nonzero even integer.

For \(u,u'\in\mathcal A_Z\), their midpoint \(\bar u=(u+u')/2\) belongs to the full digit grid. Let \(j\) be the number of coordinates where these two nonzero digits are opposite, so \(s(\bar u)=|Z|+j\). The affine digit tilt cancels exactly at the midpoint. Expanding the one Gaussian square gives the exact identity

\[
\psi(u)\psi(u')
=3^{-2j}\psi(\bar u)^2
\exp\left(-\frac\lambda2(g(u)-g(u'))^2\right)
\le a_*^2\exp\left(-\frac\lambda2(g(u)-g(u'))^2\right).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus no independent digit penalty or arbitrary profile has been substituted for the Gaussian potential.

Choose \(u_Z\) maximizing \(\psi\) within \(\mathcal A_Z\), and put \(a_Z=\psi(u_Z)>0\). For every \(u\in\mathcal A_Z\), maximality gives \(\psi(u)\le a_Z\), while (4) with \(u'=u_Z\) gives \(\psi(u)\le a_*^2a_Z^{-1}\exp(-\lambda(g(u)-g(u_Z))^2/2)\). Multiplying these two upper bounds and taking a square root yields

\[
\psi(u)\le a_*\exp\left(-\frac\lambda4(g(u)-g(u_Z))^2\right).
\tag{5}
\]

There is at most one class member at each height difference \(2k\) and at most one at \(-2k\), by height injectivity. Therefore

\[
\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_Z}\psi(u)
\le a_*\left(1+2\sum_{k=1}^\infty e^{-\lambda k^2}\right)
=a_*\Theta_\lambda,
\qquad \Theta_\lambda<2.
\tag{6}
\]

For completeness, since \(\lambda>1\) and \(k^2\ge2k-1\), the series is bounded above by \(\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-(2k-1)}=e/(e^2-1)\). The inequality \(2e/(e^2-1)<1\) follows from \(e>5/2\): the function \(t^2-2t-1\) is increasing for \(t>1\) and is \(1/4\) at \(5/2\). This establishes the strict upper bound in (6) without a numerical estimate.

Retaining the original multiplicities and summing (6) over all zero sets gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=\sum_Z2^{Q-|Z|}\sum_{u\in\mathcal A_Z}\psi(u)
\le a_*\Theta_\lambda\sum_Z2^{Q-|Z|}
=a_*\Theta_\lambda3^Q,\\
\max_u\frac{v(u)(3^{s(u)}+1)}2&\ge\frac{a_*}2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The last sum is \((2+1)^Q\). Combining the two inequalities in (7) proves

\[
\frac{4^Q}{W}\max_u\frac{v(u)(3^{s(u)}+1)}2
\ge\frac{(4/3)^Q}{2\Theta_\lambda}
>\frac{(4/3)^Q}{4}\ge\frac13,
\]

because \(Q\ge1\). This is the uniform scalar assertion and supplies the selected center in the depth assertion through (3).

Finally verify the stated canonical logarithmic implication. Apply the exact closed lower-halfspace formula (15) of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc at cutoff zero, with the original normalization above. Its atom scores are \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+t(\phi_{\mathbf J}-\phi_{\mathbf I})\), where \((\mathbf b,t)\ne(0,0)\) and \(t\ge0\). On the entire fiber \(I_N=J_N\), the quadratic terms cancel and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}=\ell(\mathbf I)-\ell(\mathbf J)\). Writing \(\ell(\mathbf I)=\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf I\), these scores restrict to \((\mathbf b-t\mathbf c)\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\). If this base normal is nonzero, its nonnegative atoms in the fiber form an original containing closed halfspace and have weight at least \(\tau_{\{J_N\}}(\mathbf J)\). If it is zero, every fiber atom has score zero and is included, which gives the same lower bound. Additional atoms outside the fiber have nonnegative weights. Taking the minimum over all admissible lifted normals therefore gives \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,0;w)\ge\tau_{\{J_N\}}(\mathbf J)\), with every equality atom retained. The selected center from (1) and (3) proves the final assertion.
