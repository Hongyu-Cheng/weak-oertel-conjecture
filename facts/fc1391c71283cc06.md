## Statement

Use the mixed measure, actual ambient closed-halfspace depth, and maximum-depth notation of accepted fact `2acc8bc803acf1c0`: for a convex body \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), set \(B_z=\{y:(z,y)\in B\}\), \(S_B=B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(\mu_B(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z\cap A_z)\), \(h_B(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_B(H)\), and \(h_{\max}(B)=\max_{p\in S_B}h_B(p)\). The infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces; this \(h_B\) is the locked problem's \(h_{S_B}\). Accepted fact `2acc8bc803acf1c0` supplies attainment of this maximum.

For every integer \(m\ge4\), put \(k=m^2\), \(n=mk=m^3\), \(d=m\), \(\varepsilon=1/(2m)\), and \(R=1+\varepsilon\). Write the base coordinate as \(z=(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)})\in(\mathbb R^k)^m\), and write \(y\in\mathbb R^m\) for the continuous coordinate. Define the rational polytope
\[
C_m=\left\{(z,y):
|z^{(j)}_i|\le y_j\quad(1\le j\le m,\ 1\le i\le k),
\quad\sum_{j=1}^m y_j\le R\right\}.
\]
Let \(C_m^*\) be its fiberwise Schwarz symmetrization: every nonempty continuous fiber at every real base coordinate is replaced by an origin-centered Euclidean ball of the same actual \(m\)-dimensional volume, with a singleton ball for a zero-volume nonempty fiber. Both \(C_m\) and \(C_m^*\) are complete convex bodies.

Put \(q_j(z)=\|z^{(j)}\|_\infty\), \(q(z)=(q_1(z),\ldots,q_m(z))\), and \(\Delta_m=\{u\in\mathbb R^m:u\ge0,\ \sum_j u_j\le1\}\); let \(e_j\) be the \(j\)-th standard basis vector of \(\mathbb R^m\). If \(\kappa_m=\operatorname{vol}_m(\{x:\|x\|_2\le1\})\), then the exact whole-body formula for the symmetrization is
\[
C_m^*=\left\{(z,x):
\sum_{j=1}^m\|z^{(j)}\|_\infty
+(m!\kappa_m)^{1/m}\|x\|_2\le R\right\}.
\]
The original continuous coordinate \(y\) is denoted \(x\) in this formula solely to distinguish a point of the symmetrized fiber.

The complete nonempty lattice-fiber support of either body consists of the base \(0\) and the \(m(3^k-1)\) bases having exactly one nonzero block \(z^{(j)}\in\{-1,0,1\}^k\setminus\{0\}\), with all other blocks zero. Every one of these lattice fibers has positive volume. Their original shapes and volumes are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(C_m)_0&=R\Delta_m,
&w_0&=\frac{R^m}{m!},\\
(C_m)_z&=e_j+\varepsilon\Delta_m
\quad\text{when block }j\text{ is the unique nonzero block},
&w_{\varepsilon}&=\frac{\varepsilon^m}{m!}.
\end{aligned}
\]
There are no other nonempty lattice fibers, including no zero-volume lattice fibers. Writing \(A_m=(3^k-1)w_{\varepsilon}\) for the total original mixed volume in one noncentral block group, the common total mixed mass is \(W_m=w_0+mA_m>0\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C_m)&\le w_0+A_m,
&h_{\max}(C_m^*)&=\frac{W_m}{2},\\
0\le\frac{h_{\max}(C_m)}{h_{\max}(C_m^*)}
&\le\frac2m+2\left(1-\frac1m\right)\frac{w_0}{W_m}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, for every positive real \(\lambda\), some \(m\ge4\) satisfies \(h_{\max}(C_m)<\lambda h_{\max}(C_m^*)\). Thus no absolute positive \(\lambda\) gives \(h_{\max}(C)\ge\lambda h_{\max}(C^*)\) for every positive-mixed-volume convex body and its fiberwise Schwarz symmetrization. The conclusion concerns this stronger bridge, not a counterexample to the locked \(c_0 2^{-n}\) depth bound.

## Proof

First check the whole original body and every real fiber. Resolving each absolute value makes the defining constraints of \(C_m\) finitely many rational linear inequalities. In particular \(y_j\ge|z^{(j)}_i|\ge0\) for every \(j,i\). Thus \(0\le y_j\le R\) and \(|z^{(j)}_i|\le R\), so \(C_m\) is bounded and closed. It is convex. At \(z=0\) and \(y_j=R/(2m)\) for all \(j\), every defining inequality is strict. Hence \(C_m\) has nonempty interior and is a convex body in the complete ambient space \(\mathbb R^{n+m}\).

Fix any real base \(z\). The constraints on \(y\) are precisely \(y_j\ge q_j(z)\) and \(\sum_j y_j\le R\). Therefore, writing \(T(z)=R-\sum_j q_j(z)\), the fiber is empty if \(T(z)<0\), is the singleton \(\{q(z)\}\) if \(T(z)=0\), and is \(q(z)+T(z)\Delta_m\) if \(T(z)>0\). Its volume when \(T(z)\ge0\) is \(T(z)^m/m!\). Translation changes no volume.

A centered Euclidean ball with this volume has radius \(T(z)/(m!\kappa_m)^{1/m}\). Replacing the real fibers by these balls gives exactly the displayed whole-body formula for \(C_m^*\), including the singleton fibers at \(T(z)=0\) and the empty fibers at \(T(z)<0\). The function \(\sum_j\|z^{(j)}\|_\infty+(m!\kappa_m)^{1/m}\|x\|_2\) is a norm on the product space: its summands are nonnegative norms on their coordinate blocks, and it vanishes only at the origin. Its closed sublevel set of positive radius \(R\) is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior. This proves that the symmetrization itself is an actual convex body, rather than merely an abstract fiber table.

Now fix an integer base \(z\in\mathbb Z^{mk}\). Every \(q_j(z)\) is a nonnegative integer. Since \(1<R<2\), nonemptiness requires \(\sum_j q_j(z)\le R\), which is equivalent to \(\sum_j q_j(z)\in\{0,1\}\). Sum zero forces \(z=0\). Sum one means exactly one block has infinity norm one and all other blocks vanish; the active block is exactly a nonzero member of \(\{-1,0,1\}^k\). Conversely all these bases have nonempty fibers, with \(T(0)=R\) and \(T(z)=R-1=\varepsilon>0\) on every noncentral base. This checks the entire lattice support. A zero-volume nonempty real fiber has \(\sum_j q_j(z)=R\), which cannot occur at an integer base because \(R\notin\mathbb Z\). Thus there is no omitted zero-volume lattice boundary case in either body.

There are \(3^k-1\) nonzero ternary vectors in each active block. The different active blocks give disjoint base groups. The exact fiber formulas proved above give \(w_0=R^m/m!\), \(w_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^m/m!\), \(A_m=(3^k-1)\varepsilon^m/m!\), and \(W_m=[R^m+m(3^k-1)\varepsilon^m]/m!\). By its definition the symmetrization has the same volume at every real base, and in particular the same complete lattice table of volumes and the same mixed mass \(W_m\).

We next bound the depth of every mixed point of \(C_m\). Let \(p=(z,y)\in S_{C_m}\) be arbitrary, including any boundary point of any positive fiber, and put \(s=\sum_j y_j\). There are two exhaustive cases.

If \(s<1\), use the proper closed ambient halfspace \(H=\{(u,v):\sum_j v_j\le s\}\), whose boundary contains \(p\). Every point of every noncentral fiber \(e_j+\varepsilon\Delta_m\) has continuous-coordinate sum at least one, so all those fibers are strictly excluded. The only contributing lattice fiber is the central fiber \(R\Delta_m\). Here \(s\ge0\) because \(y\ge0\), and its cap is \(s\Delta_m\), with volume \(s^m/m!\le w_0\). Thus \(h_{C_m}(p)\le w_0\). At \(s=0\) the cap is a singleton and has zero volume, as required by the closed-boundary convention.

If \(s\ge1\), choose a coordinate \(j\) with \(y_j\ge s/m\ge1/m>\varepsilon\). Use the proper closed ambient halfspace \(H=\{(u,v):v_j\ge y_j\}\), again with \(p\) on its boundary. In every noncentral group indexed by an active block \(\ell\ne j\), a fiber is \(e_{\ell}+\varepsilon\Delta_m\), so every point in it has \(v_j\le\varepsilon<y_j\). Hence every such fiber is strictly excluded, including its entire boundary. The central fiber contributes at most \(w_0\), and the \(3^k-1\) fibers in group \(j\) contribute at most their full original total volume \(A_m\). There are no other nonempty lattice fibers. Thus \(h_{C_m}(p)\le w_0+A_m\).

These cases apply to all possible integer bases and all feasible continuous points in their complete fibers. They prove the uniform all-point bound \(h_{C_m}(p)\le w_0+A_m\). Taking the maximum, whose attainment is supplied by accepted fact `2acc8bc803acf1c0`, gives \(h_{\max}(C_m)\le w_0+A_m\). The proof neither prescribes a fiber centroid nor assumes that a locally deep point maximizes actual ambient depth.

It remains to determine the maximum depth of the symmetrization. Its explicit norm formula shows \(C_m^*=-C_m^*\), and \((0,0)\in S_{C_m^*}\). The mixed measure is invariant under \((z,x)\mapsto(-z,-x)\), because this map preserves the lattice base set and Euclidean volume in each continuous fiber. If a closed halfspace \(H\) contains the origin, its parallel boundary-through-origin halfspace \(H_0\subseteq H\) has the form \(\{(z,x):a\cdot z+b\cdot x\ge0\}\). Reflection takes \(H_0\) to \(\{a\cdot z+b\cdot x\le0\}\); these two sets cover the ambient space and have equal mixed mass. Their common boundary is retained in both. Therefore \(\mu_{C_m^*}(H)\ge\mu_{C_m^*}(H_0)\ge W_m/2\), even when \(b=0\) and entire fibers lie on the boundary. The whole ambient space, if admitted as an improper halfspace, has mass \(W_m\) and causes no exception. Consequently \(h_{C_m^*}(0,0)\ge W_m/2\).

For the reverse bound, take any \(p=(z,x)\in S_{C_m^*}\) and consider the two proper closed halfspaces \(H_+=\{(u,v):v_1\ge x_1\}\) and \(H_-=\{(u,v):v_1\le x_1\}\). Both contain \(p\) on their boundary. In each continuous fiber their intersection is contained in the proper hyperplane \(v_1=x_1\), which has \(m\)-dimensional volume zero. The mixed measure is a finite sum over the already audited lattice support, so \(\mu_{C_m^*}(H_+\cap H_-)=0\). Since their union is the ambient space, their masses sum to \(W_m\), and one has mass at most \(W_m/2\). Hence every mixed point has depth at most \(W_m/2\). The origin attains this bound, and \(h_{\max}(C_m^*)=W_m/2\).

Finally define \(\delta_m=w_0/W_m\). Substituting \(R=(2m+1)/(2m)\), \(\varepsilon=1/(2m)\), and \(k=m^2\) gives the exact expression
\[
\delta_m=
\frac{(2m+1)^m}{(2m+1)^m+m(3^{m^2}-1)}
\le\frac{(2m+1)^m}{m(3^{m^2}-1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For example, for \(m\ge4\) one has \(2m+1\le3^{m/2}\): equality holds at \(m=4\), and the ratio of the left side at the next integer to its present value is at most \(11/9<\sqrt3\). Also \(3^{m^2}-1\ge3^{m^2}/2\). Thus the displayed upper bound is at most \(2/[m3^{m^2/2}]\), which explicitly tends to zero. Using \(A_m=(W_m-w_0)/m\), the two depth bounds yield
\[
0\le\frac{h_{\max}(C_m)}{h_{\max}(C_m^*)}
\le\frac{2(w_0+A_m)}{W_m}
=\frac2m+2\left(1-\frac1m\right)\delta_m
\longrightarrow0.
\]
This contradicts every proposed absolute positive comparison factor \(\lambda\). The dimensions here are \(n=m^3\) and \(d=m\); the proof supplies no vanishing upper bound after division by \(2^{-n}W_m\), so its conclusion is precisely the failure of the Schwarz maximum-depth bridge stated above.
