## Statement

Let $X$ maximize $\sum_i f_i(X_i)$ on nonnegative $N\times m$ matrices with each column sum one, with each $f_i$ finite and positively homogeneous of degree one. Choose $X$ with the fewest nonzero rows and let $k$ be that count. For each $i$ in a selected set $S$ of strictly positive rows, assume $f_i$ is $C^2$ near $X_i$ and let $E_i$ be a subspace on which the Hessian $\nabla^2 f_i(X_i)$ is positive definite. Then
$$
k+\sum_{i\in S}\dim E_i\le m.
$$
In particular, this applies to $f_i=\omega_i\phi$ with positive weights on the selected rows and positive-definite subspaces of the Hessian of $\phi$ there.

## Proof

Let $V$ be the active-row span. The least-active row independence lemma gives $\dim V=k$. Define a linear map $\bigoplus_{i\in S}E_i\to\mathbb R^m/V$ by $(v_i)_{i\in S}\mapsto\sum_{i\in S}v_i+V$. If the asserted bound failed, its kernel would contain a nonzero tuple. The exact proportional compensation lemma applies to that tuple, since its sum is in $V$, all selected rows are strictly positive and differentiable, and $X$ is a local maximum. In the objective remainder from that lemma, Taylor expansion and $s_i=t+O(t^2)$ give
$$
L(X(t))-L(X)
=\frac{t^2}{2}\sum_{i\in S}v_i^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2 f_i(X_i)v_i+o(t^2).
$$
Each summand is nonnegative and at least one is positive. This contradicts maximality for small nonzero $t$.
