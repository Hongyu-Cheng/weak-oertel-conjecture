## Statement

There exists a full-dimensional lattice polytope $Q\subset\mathbb R^4$ whose lattice points are exactly its sixteen vertices, one in each parity class, and whose lattice width is two. Here lattice width is the minimum of $\max_{x\in Q}c\cdot x-\min_{x\in Q}c\cdot x$ over nonzero $c\in\mathbb Z^4$. The vertices and strict exposing functionals are given in the proof.

## Proof

Let $Q$ be the convex hull of the listed $p_i$. The gap in the last column is $\min_{j\ne i} n_i\cdot(p_i-p_j)$.

| i | p_i | n_i | minimum gap |
|---|---|---|---:|
|0|(1,0,0,0)|(22,-2,-2,-8)|20|
|1|(0,1,0,0)|(2,36,-19,-4)|23|
|2|(0,0,1,0)|(4,-16,6,-2)|2|
|3|(0,0,0,1)|(-2,-2,-2,-1)|1|
|4|(2,2,3,5)|(16,10,-10,3)|15|
|5|(1,3,5,5)|(-15,17,-3,2)|13|
|6|(0,0,0,2)|(-14,-2,-16,17)|17|
|7|(1,2,4,5)|(2,-14,6,3)|3|
|8|(1,1,2,3)|(7,-24,7,4)|1|
|9|(0,1,1,1)|(-17,6,-2,3)|1|
|10|(0,1,3,2)|(-10,-7,6,2)|5|
|11|(1,2,3,4)|(-8,14,-11,7)|1|
|12|(0,1,2,1)|(3,7,5,-9)|1|
|13|(1,1,2,2)|(7,-5,6,-3)|1|
|14|(1,1,1,2)|(6,6,-11,3)|1|
|15|(1,2,3,3)|(15,15,3,-12)|3|

These positive gaps show that each point is a vertex. The determinant with rows $p_i-p_0$ for $i=1,2,3,4$ is $-11$, so $Q$ is full-dimensional. Coordinate reduction modulo two gives all sixteen distinct parity classes.

Write the coordinates as $(x,y,z,w)$. The listed points lie in $[0,2]\times[0,3]\times[0,5]^2$. Direct substitution shows that all satisfy the seven inequalities
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x+3y+2z-4w&\le3, & x-6y+2z+w&\le2,\\
-3x+6y-5z+3w&\le6, & -10x+3y-z+2w&\le4,\\
2x+y-3z+w&\le2, & 3x-3y+3z-w&\le4,\\
-x-y-z-w&\le-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
For fixed integer $x,y,z$ in the box, the permitted integer fourth coordinates are exactly $L\le w\le U$, where
$$
\begin{aligned}
L&=\max\{0,\lceil(3x+3y+2z-3)/4\rceil,3x-3y+3z-4,1-x-y-z\},\\
U&=\min\{5,2-x+6y-2z,\lfloor(6+3x-6y+5z)/3\rfloor,\lfloor(4+10x-3y+z)/2\rfloor,2-2x-y+3z\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $z=0,\ldots,5$ these bounds give the complete integer census.

| (x,y) | allowed (z,w) |
|---|---|
|(0,0)|(0,1), (0,2), (1,0)|
|(0,1)|(0,0), (1,1), (2,1), (3,2)|
|(0,2)|none|
|(0,3)|none|
|(1,0)|(0,0)|
|(1,1)|(1,2), (2,2), (2,3)|
|(1,2)|(3,3), (3,4), (4,5)|
|(1,3)|(5,5)|
|(2,0)|none|
|(2,1)|none|
|(2,2)|(3,5)|
|(2,3)|none|

The census is exactly the listed vertices. By convexity every lattice point of $Q$ satisfies the box and inequalities, so $Q$ has no additional lattice points.

The first coordinate has width two. If $c\in\mathbb Z^4$ has width at most one, its values at all vertices lie in $\{t,t+1\}$ for some integer $t$. Since all four standard basis vectors are vertices, write $c_i=t+\epsilon_i$ with $\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\}$. Evaluating at $(2,2,3,5)$ gives $11t+2\epsilon_1+2\epsilon_2+3\epsilon_3+5\epsilon_4\in\{0,1\}$. The subset sum is between zero and twelve, forcing $t=0$ or $-1$. The subset sums omit one because each entry is at least two, and omit eleven by complementation. For $t=0$ all $\epsilon_i$ must be zero, and for $t=-1$ all must be one. Both give $c=0$, so no nonzero functional has width at most one.
