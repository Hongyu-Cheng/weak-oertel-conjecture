## Statement

Let \(m,Q\ge1\) be integers. Retain exactly the objects \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,D_m,F_m,i_m\) from accepted fact 032423921efad6c8: choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(B_m\), let \(D_m\) be its nonterminal elements, and write
$$
F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)},\qquad c_{i_m(c)}=0\qquad(c\in D_m).
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector of \(\mathbb R^m\). Define the new block sets
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m,\qquad
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m.
$$
These are block sets, not the tree-coordinate sets of 032423921efad6c8; in particular \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is not its convex body. Put \(\nu=mQ\), identify \(\mathbb R^\nu\) with \(Q\) ordered copies of \(\mathbb R^m\), and write a point as \(x=(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(Q)})\). Define the new serial objects
$$
\Gamma_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{s=0}^Q E_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s},\qquad
P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma_{m,Q}),\qquad
A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^\nu.
$$
Thus \(\nu\), \(P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\), and \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) replace neither the dimension nor the supports of the earlier tree construction. Empty Cartesian products have one element, and empty scalar products below equal one.

The exact block and serial completions are
$$
T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},\qquad
A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=\bigcup_{s=0}^Q T_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s}.
\tag{1}
$$
The polytope \(P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) is compact, full-dimensional, and contained in \([0,2]^\nu\).

Every block of a point in \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) belongs to \(B_m\cup E_m\). Define its last-spike index by
$$
\ell(x)=\max\bigl(\lbrace j:x^{(j)}\in E_m\rbrace\cup\lbrace0\rbrace\bigr).
$$
This index is not the number of spiked blocks. Its exact disjoint strata are
$$
\mathcal L_0=B_m^Q,\qquad
\mathcal L_t=T_m^{t-1}\times E_m\times B_m^{Q-t}\quad(1\le t\le Q).
\tag{2}
$$
Consequently,
$$
|E_m|=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},\qquad
|T_m|=|E_m|+|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|,
$$
$$
|\mathcal L_0|=2^{mQ},\qquad
|\mathcal L_t|=|T_m|^{t-1}|E_m|\,2^{m(Q-t)}\quad(1\le t\le Q),
$$
$$
|A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|
=2^{mQ}+|E_m|\sum_{t=1}^Q|T_m|^{t-1}2^{m(Q-t)}.
\tag{3}
$$

More generally, let \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_Q\in\mathbb R^m\) be arbitrary, and put
$$
Z_X(\gamma)=\sum_{z\in X}e^{\gamma\cdot z}
\quad\bigl(X\in\lbrace B_m,E_m,T_m,C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\rbrace\bigr),\qquad
w_x=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^Q\gamma_j\cdot x^{(j)}\right)
\quad(x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}).
$$
The sum over an empty block set is zero. Then
$$
Z_{T_m}(\gamma)=Z_{E_m}(\gamma)+Z_{C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}}(\gamma),
$$
and the full log-affine normalizer is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
W:=\sum_{x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}}w_x
={}&\prod_{j=1}^Q Z_{B_m}(\gamma_j)\\
&+\sum_{t=1}^Q
\left(\prod_{j<t}Z_{T_m}(\gamma_j)\right)
Z_{E_m}(\gamma_t)
\left(\prod_{j>t}Z_{B_m}(\gamma_j)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
The displayed summands are precisely the weighted masses of the strata in (2), so the fields may differ in every block.

For all chain partitions, the raw serial set is already complete if and only if
$$
\Gamma_{m,Q}=A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q=1\ \text{or}\ C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\varnothing.
\tag{5}
$$
In the standard partitions constructed from dimension zero by the recursion in 032423921efad6c8, \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is empty for \(m=1,2\) and nonempty for every \(m\ge3\). Thus, for these partitions, the raw set is complete exactly when \(Q=1\) or \(m\le2\).

For a finite lattice set \(A\subset\mathbb Z^\nu\), use the oriented reflection convention of 032423921efad6c8,
$$
r_A(j)=|\lbrace v\in\mathbb Z^\nu:j+v\in A,\ j-v\in A\rbrace|.
$$
Every chain partition satisfies
$$
\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^\nu}r_{A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}}(j)\le3^Q.
\tag{6}
$$
For the standard recursive partitions and every \(m\ge4\), define
$$
c_m=(1,1,1,0,0,\ldots,0),\quad
d_m=(1,1,1,1,0,\ldots,0),\quad
a_m=(1,1,1,2,0,\ldots,0).
$$
Then \(c_m,d_m\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), \(c_m\in D_m\), \(i_m(c_m)=4\), \(a_m\in E_m\), and \(d_m=(c_m+a_m)/2\). In particular the within-block segment has all three lattice points \(c_m,d_m,a_m\) in \(T_m\), and
$$
r_{A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}}\bigl((d_m,\ldots,d_m)\bigr)
=\max_jr_{A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}}(j)=3^Q.
\tag{7}
$$

## Proof

Accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 gives injectivity of \(F_m\), the displayed successor relation, and
\(|D_m|=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\). Every point of \(E_m\) has exactly one coordinate equal to two, and all other coordinates are zero or one. Reduction modulo two recovers its source \(c\), so the map from \(D_m\) to \(E_m\) is injective and \(|E_m|=|D_m|\). The set is nonempty: the symmetric chain containing the empty set runs from rank zero to rank \(m\), and its first vertex is nonterminal for \(m\ge1\).

We first verify the block equality in (1). Let \(z\in T_m\), and express it as a convex combination of points of \(E_m\) with positive coefficients. The coordinates of \(z\) are integers between zero and two. If none is two, \(z\in B_m\), hence \(z\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). Otherwise choose \(i\) with \(z_i=2\). Since all generating coordinates are at most two, each positively weighted generator has coordinate \(i\) equal to two. Each such generator has Boolean entries in the other coordinates. Their average in any other coordinate is an integer between zero and one, so every positively weighted generator agrees with that average. They therefore all equal \(z\), proving \(z\in E_m\). Both reverse inclusions are immediate, and the union is disjoint because points of \(E_m\) are not Boolean.

The generator set \(\Gamma_{m,Q}\) is finite, lies in \([0,2]^\nu\), and contains \(B_m^Q\). Its convex hull is therefore compact, contained in that box, and full-dimensional. Let \(x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\), and express \(x\) as a convex combination of generators in \(\Gamma_{m,Q}\), retaining only positive coefficients. If \(x\) has no coordinate equal to two, it belongs to \(B_m^Q\). Otherwise let \(t\) be the last block containing a coordinate equal to two, and choose such a coordinate in that block. Every positively weighted generator has value two at this coordinate. If that generator belongs to \(E_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s}\), this forces \(s\ge t\). Thus in each of the first \(t\) blocks all the generators belong to \(E_m\), so the integer average in that block belongs to \(T_m\). Every block after \(t\) is Boolean by the definition of \(t\). Hence
$$
x\in T_m^t\times B_m^{Q-t}.
$$
This proves one inclusion in the serial equality (1). Conversely, if \(x\in T_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s}\), represent each of its first \(s\) blocks as a convex combination of points of \(E_m\). Taking products of these finite convex coefficients represents \(x\) as a convex combination of points of \(E_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s}\), with the Boolean suffix held fixed. Hence \(x\in P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\); it is an integer point, so it belongs to \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\). The case \(s=0\) is the inclusion of the Boolean cube already noted. This proves (1) for every \(m,Q\ge1\).

The block equality in (1) implies that every serial support block lies in \(B_m\cup E_m\). For \(x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) with last-spike index \(t\ge1\), its first \(t-1\) blocks belong to \(T_m\) by the necessity argument just proved, its \(t\)-th block belongs to \(E_m\), and its later blocks belong to \(B_m\). Conversely every point of the indicated Cartesian product belongs to the support by (1), and has last-spike index exactly \(t\). The no-spike stratum is exactly \(B_m^Q\). This proves the disjoint decomposition (2), whence the counts (3). The weight \(w_x\) factors over its blocks, so summing on each Cartesian product in (2) gives (4). In particular this sum counts each completed support point exactly once, including Boolean points of \(T_m\) occurring before a later spike.

If \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is empty, then \(T_m=E_m\) and (1) gives completeness of \(\Gamma_{m,Q}\). If \(Q=1\), (1) gives
$$
A_{m,1}^{\mathrm{ser}}=B_m\cup T_m=B_m\cup E_m=\Gamma_{m,1},
$$
whether or not \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is empty. For the other direction in (5), suppose \(Q\ge2\) and choose \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) and \(a\in E_m\). Then
$$
(c,a,0,\ldots,0)\in T_m\times E_m\times B_m^{Q-2}
\subseteq A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}},
$$
where for \(Q=2\) the suffix is absent. This point does not belong to \(\Gamma_{m,Q}\). Indeed, a raw generator with its second block in \(E_m\) must have prefix length at least two, forcing its first block to lie in \(E_m\), whereas \(c\) is Boolean. This proves (5).

We now verify all claims concerning the standard recursion. Under that recursion, every old nonterminal successor edge survives after appending a zero coordinate: the first new chain contains the zero-appended old chain in its old order, and consecutive old elements are still consecutive. Consequently, for \(r\le m\), every element of \(E_r\) with \(m-r\) zeros appended belongs to \(E_m\).

In dimension one, \(E_1=\lbrace(2)\rbrace\), so its convex hull has no Boolean point. In dimension two the chains are
$$
00\longrightarrow10\longrightarrow11,\qquad 01,
$$
and therefore \(E_2=\lbrace(2,0),(1,2)\rbrace\). A point on this segment is \((2-u,2u)\), \(0\le u\le1\). For its first coordinate to be Boolean requires \(u=1\), at which its second coordinate is two. Thus \(C_2^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is empty.

In dimension three the recursion gives exactly the chains
$$
000\longrightarrow100\longrightarrow110\longrightarrow111,
\qquad
001\longrightarrow101,
\qquad
010\longrightarrow011.
$$
In particular \((2,0,0),(1,2,0),(1,1,2),(0,1,2)\) belong to \(E_3\), and the exact convex combination
$$
\frac14\bigl((2,0,0)+(1,2,0)+(1,1,2)+(0,1,2)\bigr)=(1,1,1)
\tag{8}
$$
proves \((1,1,1)\in C_3^{\mathrm{bool}}\). The zero-extension property proves that \((1,1,1,0,\ldots,0)\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) for every \(m\ge3\). Together with (5), this proves the standard-partition completeness classification.

In dimension four, the chain containing the empty set is
$$
0000\longrightarrow1000\longrightarrow1100\longrightarrow1110
\longrightarrow1111.
$$
Thus \(c_4=1110\) is nonterminal, \(i_4(c_4)=4\), and its spiked image is \(a_4=1112\in E_4\). After appending zeros, the same edge survives for every \(m\ge4\), so \(c_m\in D_m\), \(i_m(c_m)=4\), and \(a_m\in E_m\). Equation (8) with zeros appended proves \(c_m\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). Since \(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\) contains both \(c_m\) and \(a_m\), it also contains their Boolean midpoint \(d_m\). Hence \(d_m\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), and all three claimed segment points belong to \(T_m\). This derives the segment directly from the specified recursion.

Finally put \(X_m=B_m\cup E_m\). We verify its reflection count directly using the same \(F_m\) as in 032423921efad6c8. Integer endpoints have integer midpoint exactly when they have the same parity. Two Boolean endpoints of the same parity are identical. Two endpoints in \(E_m\) of the same parity have the same recovered source \(c\), so they too are identical. If one endpoint is \(c+2e_{i_m(c)}\in E_m\), the only Boolean endpoint of the same parity is \(c\), and their midpoint is \(F_m(c)\). Injectivity of \(F_m\) means that at each center there is at most one such unordered distinct pair. Conversely every \(F_m(c)\) has precisely this pair. Thus
$$
r_{X_m}(y)=
\begin{cases}
3,&y\in\operatorname{im}F_m,\\
1,&y\in X_m\setminus\operatorname{im}F_m,\\
0,&y\notin X_m.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
$$
Here the value one is the zero displacement, and each unordered distinct pair contributes both orientations. There are no other integer midpoints by the parity analysis.

Since \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\subseteq X_m^Q\), for every integer center \(j=(j^{(1)},\ldots,j^{(Q)})\),
$$
r_{A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}}(j)
\le r_{X_m^Q}(j)
=\prod_{t=1}^Q r_{X_m}(j^{(t)})\le3^Q.
$$
The equality in the middle holds because the admissible displacements for a Cartesian product are exactly the products of its admissible block displacements. This proves (6).

For the standard partitions and \(m\ge4\), (1) contains the entire product \(T_m^Q\). At the center \(j=(d_m,\ldots,d_m)\), every displacement whose block is independently chosen from \(\lbrace-e_4,0,e_4\rbrace\) has both endpoints in \(\lbrace c_m,d_m,a_m\rbrace^Q\subseteq T_m^Q\). These are \(3^Q\) distinct oriented displacements, including zero. Together with (6), this proves (7) and completes the theorem.
