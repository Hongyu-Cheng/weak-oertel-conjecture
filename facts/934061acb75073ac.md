## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$ and $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ for compact convex $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$. Let $\mu$ sum the $d$-volume of its fibers and assume $\mu(S)>0$. Write $D$ for its raw closed ambient halfspace depth. Let $y_e=(p_e,x_e)\in S$ have positive depth, with $p_0\ne p_1$. Suppose an affine base functional $h$ satisfies $h(p_0)=0$, $h(p_1)=1$ and $0\le h(z)\le1$ on every positive-volume base. Then every $y=(1-t)y_0+ty_1\in S$ with $0<t<1$ satisfies $D(y)>\min(D(y_0),D(y_1))$.

## Proof

Any halfspace containing $y$ contains at least one endpoint, so $D(y)\ge\min(D(y_0),D(y_1))$. In case of equality, fact 1ec728c26c11b368 applied to the finitely many compact nonempty fibers gives a minimizing nonvertical through-cap at $y$. The interpolation-equality lemma makes it a common minimizer through both endpoints. The actual fibers are compatible by convexity of $C$, so fact 55a57b1cf55d0c87 puts the positive-depth endpoints in their fiber interiors. Discarding null-volume fibers leaves the cap measure unchanged. Apply the orientation-resolved common-cap lemma to the remaining fibers and to $h$ lifted to the ambient space. The empty-side exterior integral vanishes because $h\in[0,1]$ on every such fiber, whereas the proper section measure is positive. This is a contradiction.
