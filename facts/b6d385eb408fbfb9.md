## Statement

Let $n,d\ge0$ and let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty compact convex. Let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. If $S$ is nonempty, $\operatorname{conv}S$ is compact and has exactly the original mixed set. Suppose now at least one integer fiber has positive $d$-volume, with counting measure for $d=0$. Write $A$ for the affine hull of all occupied integer bases, $r=\dim A$, and $z_*$ for an occupied base. A lattice basis of $(A-z_*)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ gives an affine chart $F(t,x)=(z_*+Bt,x)$ from $\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d$ to $A\times\mathbb R^d$. The set $F^{-1}(\operatorname{conv}S)$ is compact convex, is full dimensional in the reduced space, and has mixed set exactly $F^{-1}(S)$, including every null occupied fiber. The chart preserves summed fiber $d$-volume and every corresponding point's raw and normalized closed-halfspace depth for $r+d>0$. In particular, a reduced bound with coefficient $2^{-r}\gamma_d$ transfers with that coefficient, at least $2^{-n}\gamma_d$, with $\gamma_0=1$. For $r=d=0$ the original mixed set is one point with normalized depth one.

## Proof

More generally whenever $S\ne\varnothing$, boundedness gives finitely many occupied bases, each with a compact convex fiber. Thus $S$ is compact and its hull is compact by Carathéodory's theorem. The inclusions $S\subseteq\operatorname{conv}S\subseteq C$ show $\operatorname{conv}S\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)=S$. This preserves both occupied and empty fibers, including when the hull is lower-dimensional or all $d$-volume vanishes.

Under the positive-volume hypothesis, $A-z_*$ is spanned by integer differences of occupied bases. Its intersection with $\mathbb Z^n$ is a discrete subgroup containing $r$ linearly independent vectors, hence a lattice of rank $r$. Choose a $\mathbb Z$-basis and let $B$ have these basis columns. The construction uses the entire occupied affine span of the canonical hull. In particular $F(\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d)=(A\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d$. A positive-volume fiber supplies all vertical directions, and lifted differences spanning the base affine hull supply $r$ independent quotient directions. Thus the preimage hull has full dimension. The hull identity gives the claimed mixed set, and the chart preserves the mass of every individual fiber because it fixes the continuous coordinate.

For a mark and a containing ambient closed halfspace, its restriction to $A\times\mathbb R^d$ and pullback is a containing closed halfspace or the entire reduced space. In the latter case it has full mass. Conversely every reduced closed halfspace extends to an ambient one by extending its affine defining functional from $A\times\mathbb R^d$. Masses of corresponding cuts are equal because the chart preserves every occupied fiber, including those of zero mass. Hence the infima over cuts agree and so do the maxima and their normalized values. The coefficient comparison uses $r\le n$. When $r=d=0$ all occupied bases agree and each continuous fiber is a point, proving the separate depth assertion.
