## Statement

Let N>=1, let D be a nonempty finite complete support in Z^N, and let p be a positive log-affine probability law on D. For each coordinatewise parity class c, put \(\pi_c=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D,\ \mathbf x\equiv c\pmod 2}p_{\mathbf x}\), and extend p by zero outside D. Use exactly the array, root law, and mutual information of accepted fact 464815b935df9890:
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf x}
&=\frac{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf x}}{\pi_{\mathbf x\bmod 2}},
&q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf x},\\
I(r)&=\sum_{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf x}>0}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf x}\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf x}}{q_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf x}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The actual depth is \(\tau_p(\mathbf J)=\min_{H\ni\mathbf J}p(D\cap H)\), where H ranges over containing closed halfspaces in R^N, and \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}p_{\mathbf J}\tau_p(\mathbf J)^3\). All logarithms are natural.

For each integer k>=32, take the complete support and original weights from accepted fact 001a95dc6616c6b9, with its dimension n renamed N=k^2. Explicitly, put \(t=k^{-1/4}\), \(M=2^k\), \(\theta=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^k\), and let \(\beta\in\mathbb R^N\) be k consecutive copies of theta. Set \(\Omega_k=\{0,1\}^k\cup\{0,-1\}^k\), \(P=\mathbf1_N+(\operatorname{conv}\Omega_k)^k\), and \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N=\mathbf1_N+\Omega_k^k\). On this same D use \(w_{\mathbf J}=e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf J}\), \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf J}\), and \(p_{\mathbf J}=w_{\mathbf J}/W\). Thus the original log-affine parameter is \(\boldsymbol\ell=-\beta\); no probability or weight rescaling other than normalization by W is made.

For these laws, the full canonical array satisfies
\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\log\frac1{\tau_p(\mathbf J)}-I(r)
\ \ge\
\left(1-\frac{16}{k}\right)
\left[\frac14\log k-\log(6\sqrt8)\right]_+-3
\ \longrightarrow\ +\infty.
\]
Consequently no finite absolute C makes the proposed global entropy consumer hold for every N, D, and original log-affine p, even when the array is precisely the complete p-marginal parity construction above.

## Proof

We keep the support, tilt, weights, and depth normalization of 001a95dc6616c6b9. That accepted fact supplies full-dimensionality and exact completeness of the displayed P and D, and verifies genuine depth-minimizing closed caps at every center, including all boundary atoms. We work in centered coordinates \(\mathbf x=\mathbf J-\mathbf1_N\in\Omega_k^k\). Since every copy of theta has coordinate sum zero, \(\beta\cdot\mathbf1_N=0\); hence the original weight is \(e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf x}\). Translation permutes parity classes and sends a reflected endpoint \(2\mathbf J-\mathbf I\) to \(2\mathbf x-\mathbf y\). It therefore preserves the array in 464815b935df9890, as well as the actual containing-halfspace depths. This is only a coordinate translation.

Write \(L=|\Omega_k|=2M-1\) and \(u=\cosh t\). A nonzero block point is \(\mathbf v_E=\mathbf1_E\) or \(-\mathbf v_E\), for a nonempty subset E of [k]. The block parity classes are the singleton {0} and the pairs \(\{\mathbf v_E,-\mathbf v_E\}\). Among the nonempty E, exactly M/2-1 have \(\theta\cdot\mathbf v_E=0\), exactly M/4 have value t, and exactly M/4 have value -t: the first two membership bits agree in the first group and differ in the other two. Thus the block partition sum is \(W_0=M(1+u)-1\), and the full original partition sum is \(W=W_0^k\). The centered block law is \(p^0_{\mathbf x}=e^{-\theta\cdot\mathbf x}/W_0\), and the full p is its product law.

Parity classes of the product support have product probabilities. Substitution into the defining array consequently shows that r, in centered coordinates, is the product of the block arrays. In particular its root law q is a product of the block root laws. This uses all product classes, including singleton classes, rather than conditioning on a parity collision with a changed class distribution. Accepted fact 464815b935df9890 gives the original source marginal p and the row barycenter identities for this same array.

For a nonzero block midpoint \(\mathbf v_E\), the two endpoints must both equal \(\mathbf v_E\). Indeed a coordinate equal to 1 in the midpoint forces both endpoints to have that coordinate equal to 1; both endpoints then lie in the positive Boolean cube, and a midpoint coordinate equal to 0 forces both corresponding coordinates to be 0. The negative case is identical. Thus every nonzero block root has a deterministic conditional source row. At block root zero the row contains the central singleton and every antipodal pair.

Let a be the block root probability at zero, and let rho be the conditional source law at that root. If \(s_E=\theta\cdot\mathbf v_E\), then the zero-root array entries and their total are
\[
\begin{aligned}
r^0_{0,0}&=\frac1{W_0},
&r^0_{0,\pm\mathbf v_E}&=\frac1{2W_0\cosh s_E},\\
a=q^0_0&=\frac{1+\sum_{E\ne\varnothing}(\cosh s_E)^{-1}}{W_0}
=\frac{M(1+u^{-1})}{2W_0}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For k>=2 we have 0<t<=1 and u<2. For example, the power series gives \(e<3\), so \(\cosh1=(e+e^{-1})/2<2\). Since \(W_0<M(1+u)\), it follows that \(a>1/(2u)>1/4\).

Denote the entropy of a finite probability law v by \(S(v)=-\sum v\log v\), as in 464815b935df9890, and put \(H_0=S(\rho)\). If Z is the number of zero blocks of a q-distributed centered root, then \(Z\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,a)\). Conditional source rows are products of the block rows. The deterministic nonzero block rows have entropy zero, while each zero block row has entropy H_0. Therefore the full conditional source entropy is \(\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X\mid\mathbf J)=kaH_0\). Expanding the mutual-information definition of I(r) with source marginal p gives
\(I(r)=S(p)-kaH_0\). This conditional source entropy is not the quantity H(r), which in the accepted predecessor is the opposite conditional assignment entropy.

For an integer s>=0, define exactly the zero-block sum used in the depth proof of 001a95dc6616c6b9 by
\(T_s=\sum_{\mathbf y\in\Omega_k^s}e^{-|\beta_s\cdot\mathbf y|}\), where beta_s consists of s copies of theta and T_0=1. That accepted fact's exact minimum-depth formula, divided by the original W, is
\(\tau_p(\mathbf J)=p_{\mathbf J}(T_Z+1)/2\).
In particular the factor T_Z includes the full zero-block product support; its exponent uses the absolute value only after adding all block energies. It is not a product of separately minimized block caps.

The row barycenter identities of 464815b935df9890 imply \(\mathbb E_q\mathbf J=\mathbb E_p\mathbf X\). Since \(\log p_{\mathbf J}=-\beta\cdot\mathbf J-\log W\) is affine, \(\sum q_{\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}=\sum p_{\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}=-S(p)\). Combining this with the exact depth and information formulas gives the following identity for the global gap:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log\frac1{\tau_p(\mathbf J)}-I(r)
&=S(p)-\mathbb E\log(T_Z+1)+\log2-I(r)\\
&=kaH_0-\mathbb E\log(T_Z+1)+\log2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
This is a sum over the entire root law, not a selected midpoint or a local row estimate.

We next bound H_0 from below. The conditional law rho is proportional to the weights \(v_0=2\) and \(v_{\pm\mathbf v_E}=(\cosh s_E)^{-1}\). Let U be uniform on the L block points. Every v is at least u^{-1}. Using the elementary inequality \(\log x\le x-1\), relative entropy is bounded by the chi-squared divergence. Since the likelihood ratio rho/U equals \(v/(\mathbb E_Uv)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log L-H_0
&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\rho\|U)
\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}_U(v)}{(\mathbb E_Uv)^2}\\
&\le u^2\mathbb E_U(v-1)^2
\le\frac{u^2}{L}+(u-1)^2
\le\frac4L+t^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The central point contributes 1/L to the penultimate expectation, and every other point has v equal to 1 or u^{-1}. For the last inequality, \(\cosh t-1\le t^2\) on [0,1]: integrate \(\sinh x\le x\sinh1\), which follows from convexity of sinh and its zero value at zero, and use \(\sinh1<2\). Also \(L=2^{k+1}-1\ge2k\) for k>=2. Since t^4=1/k and a<=1, (2) yields \(ka(\log L-H_0)\le3\).

Here is a finite-sum estimate for T_s, with no limit theorem. For a block chosen uniformly from Omega_k, its energy divided by t is an integer-valued random variable V with
\(\mathbb P(V=1)=\mathbb P(V=-1)=\lambda=M/(2L)\) and \(\mathbb P(V=0)=1-2\lambda\). The count is M/2 points at each of energies t and -t and M-1 points at energy zero. Consequently, for independent copies V_1,...,V_s,
\(T_s=L^s\mathbb E e^{-t|V_1+\cdots+V_s|}\).

For s>=1 the maximum point probability of this sum is at most \(2/\sqrt{s}\). To verify this uniform bound, note that \(1/4\le\lambda\le1/3\) and its characteristic function is \(\varphi(\xi)=1-2\lambda(1-\cos\xi)\). For \(-\pi\le\xi\le\pi\), putting \(A=1-\cos\xi\in[0,2]\) gives
\(\varphi(\xi)^2=1-4\lambda A(1-\lambda A)\le1-A/3\). Thus
\(|\varphi(\xi)|\le e^{-A/6}\le e^{-\xi^2/(3\pi^2)}\), where \(1-\cos\xi\ge2\xi^2/\pi^2\) follows from the concavity bound \(\sin(|\xi|/2)\ge|\xi|/\pi\). Fourier inversion, obtained by expanding the finite characteristic polynomial and integrating its integer powers, now gives
\[
\mathbb P(V_1+\cdots+V_s=j)
\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\varphi(\xi)|^s\,d\xi
\le\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{2\sqrt{s}}
<\frac2{\sqrt{s}}.
\tag{3}
\]
The middle inequality extends a Gaussian integral to the real line; \(3\pi<16\) gives the last inequality.

Since \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-t|j|}=1+2/(e^t-1)\le3/t\) for 0<t<=1, (3), and the trivial expectation bound by one, prove
\(T_s\le L^s\min\{1,6/(t\sqrt{s})\}\).
Every T_s is at least one because its all-zero summand is one. Hence T_s+1<=2T_s. Define \(f(0)=0\) and \(f(s)=[\log(t\sqrt{s}/6)]_+\) for s>=1. The resulting estimate, including s=0, is
\(\log(T_s+1)\le s\log L+\log2-f(s)\).
Using \(\mathbb EZ=ka\) in (1), followed by (2), therefore gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log\frac1{\tau_p(\mathbf J)}-I(r)
\ge\mathbb E f(Z)-ka(\log L-H_0)
\ge\mathbb E f(Z)-3.
\tag{4}
\]

Finally \(\mathbb EZ=ka\ge k/4\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z)=ka(1-a)\le k/4\). Chebyshev's inequality implies
\(\mathbb P(Z\ge ka/2)\ge1-16/k\). On this event Z>=k/8, so, with the original t=k^{-1/4},
\(f(Z)\ge[\tfrac14\log k-\log(6\sqrt8)]_+\).
Inserting this bound into (4) proves the displayed lower bound in the statement. Its right-hand side tends to positive infinity. Each k gives a finite, complete, full-dimensional support and the original positive log-affine law; all estimates are exact analytic bounds on their finite sums.

The first false step in the proposed entropy route is consequently the asserted global inequality with one absolute C, not the valid variational entropy reduction preceding it. The canonical full parity-midpoint law has an unbounded excess depth-log cost even after subtracting its full mutual information. No separate tilt price, local cap replacement, source-sum invariant, or common-threshold filter is used in this counterexample. The variational lower bound obtained with this particular q can be loose; the calculation does not upper-bound the actual M3 and gives no disproof of the locked weak Oertel statement or of a construction with optimized root and coupling laws.
