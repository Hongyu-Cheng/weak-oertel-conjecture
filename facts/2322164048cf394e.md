## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A\) be a real symmetric positive-definite \(n\times n\) matrix, and let \(1\le\kappa<2\). Let \(V:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) be a real-valued \(C^2\) function satisfying \(2A\preceq\nabla^2V(x)\preceq2\kappa A\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), where \(\preceq\) denotes the order of symmetric matrices. Put \(Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax\), \(\theta_s=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(v)}\) for \(s>0\), and \(\delta=2-\kappa\). All logarithms are natural. Define

\[
q_0=\frac{2}{\delta}\log\!\left(
\frac{2\pi(2+\kappa)}{\delta}\frac{\theta_\kappa}{\theta_2}
\right),
\qquad
c_\kappa=\frac{(2-\kappa)^2}{2\pi(2+\kappa)(2+3\kappa)}.
\tag{1}
\]

The continuous minimum and the minimum of \(V\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\) are attained. Choose any \(j\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}V(z)\). Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) be compact and convex and satisfy the closed retention condition \(\{j+v:v\in\mathbb Z^n,\ Q(v)\le q_0\}\subseteq P\). Define \(w_z=e^{-V(z)}\mathbf1_P(z)\) and \(W=\sum_z w_z\). This is a finite, nonzero table with a finite-valued globally log-concave extension.

For every \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_a>0\), put \(\alpha_a(v)=\min\{w_{a+v},w_{a-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_a=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha_a(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_a(v)=\log(w_a^2/(w_{a+v}w_{a-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_a\). Define \(B_a^\Delta(L)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_a:\Delta_a(v)\le L}\alpha_a(v)\) for \(L\ge0\), \(K_a(w)=\max_{L\ge0}B_a^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\), and \(K(w)=\max_{a:w_a>0}K_a(w)\). Every displacement sum is oriented, counting both \(v\) and \(-v\) when \(v\ne0\), and counting \(v=0\) once. These are exactly the cutoff objects of accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`; \(\alpha_a\) is its \(\alpha\) with the dependence on the fixed center displayed. The reference probability denoted \(Q\) inside that predecessor is not introduced here: throughout this packet \(Q\) denotes only the quadratic form above.

There is a cutoff \(L\in[0,2\kappa q_0]\) such that

\[
K(w)\ge K_j(w)\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}
\ge c_\kappa\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{2}
\]

The cutoff may be chosen as \(L=2\kappa q_*\), where \(q_*\) is one of the finitely many energies \(Q(v)\le q_0\). It can also be decreased to an actual curvature value at \(j\) without decreasing its score. All the maxima defining the scores are attained.

For any \(R\in\mathbb R\), the sublevel truncation \(P=\{x:V(x)\le R\}\) has all the required retention under the following dimension-only sufficient condition:

\[
R-V(j)\ge
\frac{2\kappa n}{2-\kappa}\log\frac2\kappa
+\frac{4\kappa}{2-\kappa}
\log\frac{2\pi(2+\kappa)}{2-\kappa}.
\tag{3}
\]

This condition includes equality. In particular, for \(\kappa=3/2\), condition (3) is \(R-V(j)\ge6n\log(4/3)+12\log(14\pi)\), and the constant in (2) is \(c_{3/2}=1/(182\pi)\).

The following family satisfies the Hessian hypotheses and contains nonlinear examples for \(\kappa>1\):

\[
V(x)=Q(x)+\ell^{\mathsf T}x+
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\rho_r\log\cosh(b_r^{\mathsf T}x+a_r),
\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\rho_r b_rb_r^{\mathsf T}\preceq2(\kappa-1)A,
\tag{4}
\]

where \(m\ge1\) is an integer, \(\rho_r\ge0\), \(b_r,\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), and \(a_r\in\mathbb R\). Any real additive constant may also be included. For \(\kappa=3/2\), the matrix bound in (4) is \(\sum_r\rho_r b_rb_r^{\mathsf T}\preceq A\). The theorem allows arbitrary real tilts, offsets, and coupled positive-definite matrices satisfying these hypotheses.

For an exact realization of the original weights, define \(V_{\min}=\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}V(x)\) and \(R_P=\max_{x\in P}(V(x)-V_{\min})\). If \(P\) is full-dimensional, then for every positive integer \(d\) satisfying \(d\ge2\kappa R_P\), the set

\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
x\in P,\quad |y_i|\le\tfrac12e^{-V(x)/d}
\text{ for }1\le i\le d\right\}
\tag{5}
\]

is a convex body and has actual integer-fiber volumes exactly \(w_z\). In the normalization \(V_{\min}=0\), this is the condition \(d\ge\max\{1,2\kappa\max_P V\}\) for integer \(d\). Formula (5) uses the original \(V\), so it also preserves the original table when \(V_{\min}\ne0\).

Independently of the particular realization (5), let \(d\ge1\) be any integer and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any convex body such that \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=w_z\) for every integer \(z\), where \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), with empty and lower-dimensional fibers assigned volume zero. Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and use the closed-halfspace depth \(h_S\) of the locked problem. There is one \(y^\star\in C_j\), chosen before the halfspace, such that

\[
h_S((j,y^\star))\ge\frac{c_\kappa}{12}\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
W=\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, every closed ambient halfspace containing this point has mixed volume at least the displayed lower bound. This includes halfspaces whose boundary contains whole fibers. For \(\kappa=3/2\), the coefficient in (6) is \(1/(2184\pi)\). For each fixed \(\kappa\in[1,2)\), \(c_\kappa/12\) is a positive constant in \((0,1/e)\), independent of \(n,d,V,A,P\).

## Proof

Positive definiteness gives \(Q(x)\ge\lambda_{\min}(A)\|x\|_2^2\), where \(\lambda_{\min}(A)>0\) is the smallest eigenvalue. Taylor's formula with the global Hessian bounds gives, for all real \(x,v\),

\[
Q(v)\le V(x+v)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}v
\le\kappa Q(v).
\tag{7}
\]

In particular \(V(x)\ge V(0)+\nabla V(0)^{\mathsf T}x+Q(x)\), which tends to infinity as \(\|x\|_2\to\infty\). Thus \(V\) is coercive and continuous, and it attains its continuous minimum. The finite lattice set in the sublevel \(\{V\le V(0)\}\) is nonempty, so it contains a global lattice minimizer. The Hessian lower bound also makes \(V\) convex. Consequently \(e^{-V}\mathbf1_P\) is log-concave: for two points in \(P\) this follows from convexity of \(V\) and \(P\), and if an endpoint is outside \(P\), the required inequality has zero right side for interpolation parameters strictly between zero and one. Endpoint parameters give equality.

The lower bound on \(Q\) proves convergence and positivity of every \(\theta_s\). It also proves local uniform convergence, for \(s>0\), of the sums with any fixed polynomial in \(Q(v)\) inserted. We may therefore differentiate these sums as used below. Since \(\theta_\kappa\ge\theta_2>0\) and \(0<\delta\le1\), the logarithm in (1) is of a number greater than one. Hence \(0<q_0<\infty\), and only finitely many integer vectors satisfy \(Q(v)\le q_0\). Their set is symmetric and contains zero. Retention includes \(j\), so \(w_j>0\). Compactness of \(P\) proves finite support, and in particular \(0<W<\infty\).

Put \(M=e^{-V(j)}\) and \(\mathbf g=\nabla V(j)\). Lattice minimality gives \(M=\max_z w_z\). Apply the upper bound in (7) to both signs of an integer vector \(v\), and use \(V(j\pm v)\ge V(j)\). This gives \(-\kappa Q(v)\le\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v\le\kappa Q(v)\), or \(|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|\le\kappa Q(v)\), for every \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\). Thus, whenever both endpoints are retained,

\[
\alpha_j(v)\ge M e^{-\kappa Q(v)-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|},
\qquad
\Delta_j(v)\le2\kappa Q(v).
\tag{8}
\]

At every positive center \(a\), the two Taylor inequalities in (7) give the more general identity and bounds

\[
\Delta_a(v)=V(a+v)+V(a-v)-2V(a),
\qquad
2Q(v)\le\Delta_a(v)\le2\kappa Q(v)
\quad(v\in\mathcal U_a).
\tag{9}
\]

All these curvatures are finite and nonnegative. Moreover, \(\Delta_a(0)=0\), and each \(\mathcal U_a\) is finite. For any \(L\ge0\), let \(t\) be the largest curvature at \(a\) that is at most \(L\); it exists because zero is available. The cutoff sums at \(t\) and \(L\) agree and \(1+t\le1+L\). This proves the asserted attainment of \(K_a\), and finite positive support proves attainment of \(K\).

For each parity class \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(W_b\) be its total weight. An ordered same-parity pair of positive sites \((x,y)\) corresponds bijectively to \((a,v)=((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)\) with \(a,v\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(a\pm v\in P\). Convexity of \(P\) places its midpoint \(a\) in \(P\), so that \(w_a>0\). Cauchy--Schwarz and (9) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{-n}W^2
&\le\sum_b W_b^2
=\sum_{a:w_a>0}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_a}
w_{a+v}w_{a-v}\\
&\le\sum_{a:w_a>0}w_a^2\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-2Q(v)}
\le MW\theta_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The ordered-pair bijection accounts for both orientations and each diagonal exactly once. Dividing by \(W>0\) proves \(M\theta_2\ge2^{-n}W\).

We give the precise Gaussian Fourier identity needed here, including a proof for arbitrary real linear functionals. For \(s>0\) and \(\xi\in\mathbb R^n\), the angular-frequency transform is

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-sQ(x)}e^{-i\xi^{\mathsf T}x}\,dx
=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{s^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\xi^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\xi}{4s}\right).
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, an orthogonal change of variables diagonalizing \(A\) separates this real integral into one-dimensional integrals. For an eigenvalue \(a_i>0\), let \(I(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-sa_i x^2}e^{-i\eta x}\,dx\). Differentiation and integration by parts, justified by Gaussian decay, give \(I'(\eta)=-\eta I(\eta)/(2sa_i)\). The Gaussian integral gives \(I(0)=\sqrt{\pi/(sa_i)}\), as follows by squaring the integral and using polar coordinates. Solving the differential equation and multiplying the factors proves (11). This diagonalization concerns a real integral only; it makes no change to the integer lattice in any sum.

For any real \(t\), periodize the Schwartz function \(x\mapsto e^{-sQ(x)}e^{it\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}x}\) over \(\mathbb Z^n\). The resulting smooth periodic function has integer Fourier coefficient at \(k\) equal to (11) at \(\xi=2\pi k-t\mathbf g\). The periodization and its derivatives converge uniformly on the unit cube, and these Fourier coefficients decay quadratically exponentially in \(\|k\|_2\). Thus Fourier-series inversion is absolutely convergent. Evaluating it at zero proves

\[
\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(v)}e^{it\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v}
=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{s^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{(2\pi k-t\mathbf g)^{\mathsf T}
A^{-1}(2\pi k-t\mathbf g)}{4s}\right)>0.
\tag{12}
\]

This proof permits every real \(\mathbf g\), every \(s>0\), and every symmetric positive-definite \(A\). It requires no covariance or monotonicity theorem for a shifted Gaussian.

We also need an elementary one-dimensional estimate. Suppose \(X\) is a symmetric real random variable with \(m=\mathbb E|X|<\infty\), and its characteristic function \(\varphi(t)=\mathbb E\cos(tX)\) is nonnegative for every real \(t\). Then

\[
\mathbb E e^{-|X|}\ge\frac1{\pi(1+m)}.
\tag{13}
\]

To prove this, the Fourier kernel identity is \(e^{-|x|}=\pi^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itx}(1+t^2)^{-1}\,dt\). One direct verification for \(x\ge0\) integrates \(e^{izx}/(1+z^2)\) on the upper semicircle of radius \(R>1\). Its only pole is \(z=i\), with residue \(e^{-x}/(2i)\); the arc integral has absolute value at most \(\pi R/(R^2-1)\), which tends to zero. This gives the identity for \(x\ge0\); the integral is even in \(x\), giving the rest. Fubini is valid since \((1+t^2)^{-1}\) is integrable and \(|e^{itX}|=1\). Hence \(\mathbb E e^{-|X|}=\pi^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(t)/(1+t^2)\,dt\). The inequality \(\cos u\ge1-|u|\), obtained by integrating \(|\sin u|\le1\), implies \(\varphi(t)\ge1-m|t|\). Let \(\varepsilon=(1+m)^{-1}\). The latter bound is nonnegative on \([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\). Positivity outside this interval permits discarding that part of the integral, and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E e^{-|X|}
&\ge\frac1{\pi(1+\varepsilon^2)}
\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(1-m|t|)\,dt\\
&=\frac{m+2}{\pi((m+1)^2+1)}
\ge\frac1{\pi(m+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The last inequality is equivalent to \(m\ge0\). No division by \(m\) is used, so the case \(m=0\) is included.

Give \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\) the centered Gaussian probability \(D_s(v)=e^{-sQ(v)}/\theta_s\), and put \(m_s=\mathbb E_s Q(v)\). The distribution of \(X=\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v\) is symmetric, its first absolute moment is finite and at most \(\kappa m_s\), and (12) proves its characteristic function is positive. Applying (13) gives

\[
\mathcal A_s:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}
e^{-sQ(v)-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|}
\ge\frac{\theta_s}{\pi(1+\kappa m_s)}.
\tag{15}
\]

To select the inverse temperature, set \(s_0=\kappa+\delta/2\) and \(g_s=\theta_s/\theta_2\) for \(s\in[s_0,2]\). Then \(g_s\ge1\), and differentiation of the convergent sums gives \(\theta_s'=-m_s\theta_s\), hence \((1/g_s)'=m_s/g_s\). It follows that

\[
\int_{s_0}^2\frac{m_s}{g_s}\,ds
=1-\frac1{g_{s_0}}<1.
\tag{16}
\]

The integrand is continuous and the interval has positive length \(\delta/2\), so some \(s\in[s_0,2]\) satisfies \(m_s/g_s\le2/\delta\). This choice can depend only on \(A,\kappa\). At this temperature, (15) yields

\[
\mathcal A_s
\ge\frac{\theta_2g_s}{\pi(1+2\kappa g_s/\delta)}
=\frac{\delta\theta_2}{\pi(\delta/g_s+2\kappa)}
\ge\frac{\delta\theta_2}{\pi(2+\kappa)}.
\tag{17}
\]

The last step uses \(g_s\ge1\) and \(\delta+2\kappa=2+\kappa\). Put \(\beta=s-\kappa\), so \(\beta\ge\delta/2>0\). The discarded tail satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{Q(v)>q_0}e^{-sQ(v)-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|}
&\le e^{-\delta q_0/2}\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-\kappa Q(v)}\\
&=\frac{\delta\theta_2}{2\pi(2+\kappa)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Here we used \(e^{-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|}\le1\), the strict tail condition \(Q(v)>q_0\), and exactly (1). Subtracting (18) from (17) gives the finite retained sum

\[
\mathcal A_s^{\mathrm{ret}}:=
\sum_{Q(v)\le q_0}e^{-sQ(v)-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|}
\ge\frac{\delta\theta_2}{2\pi(2+\kappa)}.
\tag{19}
\]

No positivity assertion has been made for the characteristic function of a truncated distribution. Positivity was used only for the full centered Gaussian, and (18) accounts for the truncation explicitly.

For \(q\ge0\), let \(D(q)=\sum_{Q(v)\le\min(q,q_0)}e^{-\kappa Q(v)-|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|}\) and let \(k_* =\max_{q\ge0}D(q)/(1+2\kappa q)\). The sum is finite, and \(D(0)=1\). Between consecutive retained energies the numerator is constant and the denominator increases; the same holds after the last retained energy. Thus a maximum occurs at a retained energy \(q_*\le q_0\). Finite summation and \(\beta>0\) give the exact layer-cake identity and estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_s^{\mathrm{ret}}
&=\int_0^\infty\beta e^{-\beta q}D(q)\,dq\\
&\le k_*\int_0^\infty\beta e^{-\beta q}(1+2\kappa q)\,dq\\
&=k_*\left(1+\frac{2\kappa}{\beta}\right)
\le k_*\frac{2+3\kappa}{\delta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

For each summand the integral from \(Q(v)\) to infinity equals \(e^{-\beta Q(v)}\). In particular the central term at energy zero contributes one, so it is fully accounted for. Combining (19)--(20) proves \(k_*\ge c_\kappa\theta_2\).

Every integer \(v\) with \(Q(v)\le q_*\) has both endpoints in \(P\), since retention is symmetric. By (8), its curvature is at most \(2\kappa q_*\), and its endpoint minimum is at least the corresponding summand of \(MD(q_*)\). Therefore, at \(L=2\kappa q_*\),

\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}
\ge M\frac{D(q_*)}{1+2\kappa q_*}
=Mk_*\ge c_\kappa M\theta_2
\ge c_\kappa\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{21}
\]

This proves (2). Once the specified \(q_0\) is fixed, \(q_*\) is selected from its finite list of retained energies. Decreasing \(L\) to the largest actual curvature at most \(L\), as in the earlier attainment argument, preserves the cutoff sum and can only improve its score. All cutoff and retention inequalities are closed, including \(q_* =0\) and equality at \(q_0\).

For the sublevel assertion, put \(t=0\) in (12). This yields

\[
\theta_s=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{s^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}
e^{-\pi^2 k^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}k/s}.
\tag{22}
\]

Since \(\kappa<2\), each term of the dual sum at \(s=\kappa\) is no greater than its term at \(s=2\). Consequently \(\theta_\kappa/\theta_2\le(2/\kappa)^{n/2}\). Substitution into (1) shows that \(2\kappa q_0\) is at most the right side of (3). Also (7) and \(|\mathbf g^{\mathsf T}v|\le\kappa Q(v)\) give \(V(j\pm v)-V(j)\le2\kappa Q(v)\) for every integer \(v\). Condition (3) therefore retains all required points, including equalities. The sublevel is compact by coercivity and convex by convexity of \(V\). The right side of (3) is strictly positive, so \(V(j)<R\); continuity supplies an open neighborhood of \(j\) in this sublevel. Thus these sublevels are also full-dimensional when used in (5).

For (4), every real \(\cosh(b_r^{\mathsf T}x+a_r)\) is positive, so the logarithms define real \(C^\infty\) functions. Direct differentiation gives

\[
\nabla^2V(x)=2A+
\sum_{r=1}^{m}\rho_r\operatorname{sech}^2(b_r^{\mathsf T}x+a_r)
b_rb_r^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{23}
\]

Since \(0<\operatorname{sech}^2 u\le1\) for real \(u\), the Hessian lies between \(2A\) and \(2\kappa A\). Tilts and added constants do not affect it, and all offsets are permitted. To exhibit a nonquadratic coupled member explicitly, for \(n\ge2\) and \(1<\kappa<2\) take \(A=I_n\), \(m=1\), \(\rho_1=\kappa-1\), and \(b_1=e_1+e_2\), with arbitrary \(\ell\) and \(a_1\). The matrix \((\kappa-1)b_1b_1^{\mathsf T}\) has largest eigenvalue \(2(\kappa-1)\), so it satisfies (4). The Hessian has nonconstant mixed entry \((\kappa-1)\operatorname{sech}^2(x_1+x_2+a_1)\) in coordinates one and two. It is therefore nonquadratic and coupled. For \(\kappa=3/2\) this uses \(\rho_1=1/2\); substituting into (1) and (3) gives exactly the constants and sufficient radius stated above.

We verify the realization for the original normalization. For any \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), apply the upper Taylor bound (7) with displacement \(-(2\kappa)^{-1}A^{-1}\nabla V(x)\). The resulting descent estimate is

\[
V_{\min}\le V\!\left(x-\frac1{2\kappa}A^{-1}\nabla V(x)\right)
\le V(x)-\frac1{4\kappa}
\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\nabla V(x).
\tag{24}
\]

Hence \(\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\nabla V(x)\le4\kappa(V(x)-V_{\min})\). Cauchy--Schwarz in the \(A\)-inner product implies the matrix inequality
\(\nabla V(x)\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}\preceq[\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}\nabla V(x)]A\).
For \(r(x)=e^{-V(x)/d}\), direct differentiation, (24), and the lower Hessian bound consequently give, on \(P\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nabla^2r(x)
&=\frac{r(x)}{d^2}
\bigl(\nabla V(x)\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}-d\nabla^2V(x)\bigr)\\
&\preceq\frac{r(x)}{d^2}
\bigl(4\kappa(V(x)-V_{\min})-2d\bigr)A
\preceq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

The last inequality holds for every integer \(d\ge1\) with \(d\ge2\kappa R_P\), including equality. The function \(r\) is globally \(C^2\), and the restriction of its Hessian to every segment contained in \(P\) is nonpositive. The ordinary second-derivative criterion on each such segment shows that \(r\) is concave on \(P\), including its boundary.

Take \((x,y),(x',y')\in C\) as defined in (5), and \(t\in[0,1]\). Convexity of \(P\) gives \((1-t)x+tx'\in P\), and for each coordinate the triangle inequality and concavity of \(r\) give
\(|(1-t)y_i+ty_i'|\le[(1-t)r(x)+tr(x')]/2\le r((1-t)x+tx')/2\).
Thus \(C\) is convex. It is closed by continuity of \(r\) and closedness of \(P\), and bounded by compactness of \(P\). The positive continuous function \(r\) has a positive minimum on \(P\); since \(P\) contains an open ball, \(C\) contains the product of an open base ball and an open box and has nonempty ambient interior. It is a convex body. For every \(x\in P\), its fiber is a box of side length \(r(x)\) in each of \(d\) coordinates and has volume \(r(x)^d=e^{-V(x)}\); for \(x\notin P\) the fiber is empty. This proves the exact table claim, including base-boundary fibers.

Equivalently, subtracting \(V_{\min}\) from the potential leaves all Hessians, lattice minimizers, and curvatures unchanged, and multiplies \(w,W,B_a^\Delta,K_a,K\) by \(e^{V_{\min}}\). In (5) the factor \(e^{-V_{\min}/d}\) in the original radius restores the original fiber volumes. Thus there is no unrecorded normalization change in the realization.

Finally, let any actual convex body with the table in the statement be given, in any positive continuous dimension \(d\). At the specified positive center \(j\) and the cutoff \(L\) from (21), the objects \(w_z,W,\alpha_j,\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j,B_j^\Delta\) are exactly the actual-volume objects of accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`, with only the displayed center subscript added to \(\alpha\). Its per-center, per-cutoff conclusion (3) supplies one \(y^\star\in C_j\), fixed before all ambient halfspaces, with \(h_S((j,y^\star))\ge B_j^\Delta(L)/[12(1+L)]\). Its hypotheses hold: the body is convex and compact, the table is its actual integer-fiber volume table, \(w_j>0\), the support is finite, and the curvature domain includes precisely positive reflected pairs. Applying (21) gives (6). This invocation uses the per-center conclusion, so no replacement of \(j\), the table, or the body occurs. The predecessor includes all closed ambient halfspaces, including whole-fiber boundary mass, as required by the locked definition of depth.

For \(1\le\kappa<2\), positivity of \(\delta\) gives \(c_\kappa>0\), while \(c_\kappa\le1/(30\pi)\). Hence \(0<c_\kappa/12\le1/(360\pi)<1/e\). All arguments allow \(\kappa=1\); none uses division by \(\kappa-1\). The strict endpoint \(\kappa<2\) is used in the positive temperature interval, the tail decay, and (20), and is part of the theorem's hypotheses.
