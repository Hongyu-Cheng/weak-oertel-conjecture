## Statement

Let $S$ be the sampled union of finitely many full-dimensional compact convex $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$, with mixed volume $dm$ of mass $M$, and $C=\operatorname{conv}S$. Let $L=\min_r A_r>0$ on $C$, with a finite list of affine functions $A_r(z,X)=a_r\cdot z+c_r\cdot X+b_r$. Let $Y_i\in C$ with $L_i=L(Y_i)$, and assume positive $\sigma_i$ summing to one and probability laws $\rho_i$ on $S$ satisfy
$$
\int w\,d\rho_i=Y_i,\qquad \int L\,d\rho_i=L_i,\qquad
\sum_i(\sigma_i/L_i)\rho_i=dm/(ML).
$$
For each $i$, every plane active at $Y_i$ has the same continuous gradient $c_i$. The law $\rho_i$ is carried by the label $c(w)=\nabla_X L(w)=c_i$, where $c(w)$ is defined on a mixed-full-volume set. For every bounded function $g$ of the finite labels,
$$
\sum_i(\sigma_i/L_i)g(c_i)(1,Y_i)=\frac1M\int_S g(c(w))\frac{(1,w)}{L(w)}\,dm,\qquad
\sum_i\sigma_i g(c_i)=\frac1M\int_S g(c(w))\,dm.
$$
In particular the occurring positive-volume labels are exactly the positive source labels, and their number is at most the number of source occurrences.

## Proof

For any active plane $A_r$ at $Y_i$, its affine mean is $A_r(Y_i)=L_i$ in $\rho_i$. Since $A_r\ge L$ and the $L$-mean is also $L_i$, the row is carried by $\{A_r=L\}$. Each positive row is absolutely continuous with respect to $dm$. If two active planes at one source had unequal continuous gradients, their common equality set would be a hyperplane or empty on every sampled fiber and hence mixed-null. This contradicts the positive row probability, proving the common slope claim.

The sets in sampled fibers where two affine planes of unequal continuous slopes coincide are mixed-null by finiteness. Elsewhere the active continuous slope on a fiber is unique and is the derivative on the fiber interior, since the inactive planes have strict gaps. In a positive row, the contact with an active source plane therefore puts its targets in the stated label. For the first identity use the row decomposition, masses and barycenters. Multiplying the row decomposition by $L$ and using the row $L$-means gives the second identity. Testing indicator functions of the labels and using $\sigma_i>0$ proves the count and occurrence assertions.
