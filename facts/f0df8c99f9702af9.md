## Statement

For $\rho>0$, put $A_m=\sum_{j=0}^m(\rho+j)$ for integers $m\ge0$. At every actual interior index $m\ge1$, $A_m^{1/2}$ is strictly midpoint concave unless $\rho=1/2$. At $\rho=1/2$ it equals $(m+1)/\sqrt2$ at every index.

Let $a_\ell,\ldots,a_r>0$ be a concave sequence on consecutive integers and set $F_i=\sum_{j=i}^r a_j$. For each $\ell<i<r$,
$$
2\sqrt{F_i}\ge\sqrt{F_{i-1}}+\sqrt{F_{i+1}}.
$$
Equality holds exactly when $a_j=(2(r-j)+1)a_r$ for every $j=i-1,\ldots,r$. The equality indices form an empty set or a terminal block of the actual interior indices. When there are at least three entries, the last interior equality holds exactly for a last-three ratio $5:3:1$, and equality at all interior indices holds exactly for the full odd profile. For example, $(15/2,7,5,3,1)$ has equality at precisely its last two interior indices.

At an interior node with $A=a_{i-1}>b=a_i$, put $F=F_i$, $\delta=A-b$, and $q=b/\delta>1$. Write $q=m+\rho$ with integer $m\ge1$ and $0<\rho\le1$, taking $\rho=1$ if $q$ is an integer. Set
$$
F_{\max}=\delta\sum_{k\ge0}(q-k)_+,qquad
F_*=(A+b)^2/(8\delta).
$$
The exact defect identities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_*-F&=\tfrac\delta2(\rho-\tfrac12)^2+F_{\max}-F,\\
2\sqrt F-\sqrt{F+A}-\sqrt{F-b}
&=\frac{8\delta(F_*-F)}
   {(2\sqrt F+\sqrt{F+A}+\sqrt{F-b})(2F-\delta+2\sqrt{(F+A)(F-b)})}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The two terms in the first identity are nonnegative. If the $a_i$ sum to one, the support is nonsingleton, and the mean $\mu=\sum_i i a_i$ is integral, then the Jensen comparison $\sqrt{F_\mu}>\sum_i a_i\sqrt{F_i}$ is strict.

## Proof

The arithmetic formula
$$
A_m=(m+1)(\rho+m/2)=\frac{(m+\rho+1/2)^2-(\rho-1/2)^2}{2}
$$
is valid for the stated integers and gives a positive continuous extension for real $m\ge0$. Its square root has second derivative
$$
-\frac{(\rho-1/2)^2}
{\sqrt2\big((m+\rho+1/2)^2-(\rho-1/2)^2\big)^{3/2}} .
$$
This proves the arithmetic equality classification.

At a tail node write $F=F_i$, $A=a_{i-1}$, and $b=a_i$. If $A\le b$, the two distinct numbers $F+A,F-b$ have mean at most $F$. Strict concavity and monotonicity of the square root give strictness. If $A>b$, concavity of the masses gives $0<a_{i+k}\le b-k\delta$ for $0\le k\le r-i$. The $k=1$ inequality implies $q>1$. Extend the actual masses by zero past $r$ for the envelope comparison, giving $F\le F_{\max}$. The positive envelope terms read in reverse order give $F_{\max}=\delta A_m$ with the shift in the statement. Since $F_*=(\delta/2)(q+1/2)^2$, the arithmetic formula gives the first defect identity.

Rationalizing the midpoint gap first produces numerator $2F-\delta-2\sqrt{(F+A)(F-b)}$ and first denominator factor $2\sqrt F+\sqrt{F+A}+\sqrt{F-b}$. Multiplying by the conjugate of that numerator gives
$$
(2F-\delta)^2-4(F+A)(F-b)=(A+b)^2-8\delta F=8\delta(F_*-F).
$$
Since $F>b>\delta$, the two denominator factors are positive. Thus equality requires $\rho=1/2$ and $F=F_{\max}$. Each envelope deficit is nonnegative, so the actual positive suffix occupies exactly the same indices $i$ through $i+m$ as the envelope and attains all its terms. Consequently $r=i+m$, $a_r=\delta/2$, and the relation $A=b+\delta$ supplies the same odd formula at $i-1$. Conversely the odd formula gives $F_{i-1},F_i,F_{i+1}$ equal to $a_r$ times $(r-i+2)^2,(r-i+1)^2,(r-i)^2$, respectively, giving equality. A forced suffix also forces every later interior equality. Applying this at the last and first interior indices gives the two stated special cases. The example is concave by its successive differences and has the prescribed odd suffix only from its second entry onward.

For the Jensen comparison there must be at least three entries, since two positive masses on consecutive integers have mean strictly between those integers. The piecewise linear interpolation of $\sqrt{F_i}$ is concave by the midpoint comparisons. If it is affine on the whole interval, the equality classification forces the full odd profile. Writing $N=r-\ell+1$, this profile has weights proportional to $2N-1-2j$ at offsets $0\le j<N$. Their sum is $N^2$, and the sum of their products with $j$ is $N(N-1)(2N-1)/6$. The mean is therefore $\ell+(N-1)(2N-1)/(6N)$, which cannot be integral for $N>1$ because $(N-1)(2N-1)\equiv1\pmod N$. Hence the interpolation has a strict kink. A supporting line to this interpolation at its interior mean must be strictly above it at some index, since otherwise it agrees at every index and the interpolation is affine. All masses are positive at the support indices, so averaging this line comparison gives strict Jensen.
