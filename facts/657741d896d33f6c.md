## Statement

Let \(h\ge6\) be an integer, let \(n=2^{h+1}-1\), and let the tree on vertices \(1,\ldots,n\) have parent \(\lfloor v/2\rfloor\) for each \(v\ge2\). Put \(m=n-1\), \(d=3n-2\), and \(\ell=2n-1\). Let \(k\) be rational with \(1\le k\le n/100\), and set \(R=n/2+k\). In the original ordered continuous coordinates \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^m\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
C=\biggl\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):\;&
t_v^+,t_v^-\ge0,\quad g_v=t_v^+-t_v^-\quad(2\le v\le n),\\
&g_1=0,\qquad q_v=g_v-g_{2v}-g_{2v+1},\\
&y_v\ge\left|z_v-\tfrac12-q_v\right|\quad(1\le v\le n),\\
&\sum_{v=1}^n y_v+\sum_{v=2}^n(t_v^++t_v^-)\le R\biggr\},
\end{aligned}
\]
where a missing child's flow is zero. Use exactly the actual-body conventions of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f: \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{(\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\), and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), over closed ambient halfspaces. In particular, \(W\) includes every integer base. The body is a full-dimensional rational simplex and \(0<W<\infty\).

Let \(\sigma_v=(-1)^{\operatorname{depth}(v)}\) and \(j_v=(1+\sigma_v)/2\). For a real base, define its projection energy
\(E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)=\sum_{v=2}^n|g_v|+\sum_{v=1}^n|z_v-1/2-q_v|\).
For the coloring base, set \(\xi_v=\sigma_vg_v\) on each nonroot edge. Regard \(\boldsymbol\xi\) also as an edge-indexed vector. Its energy is
\[
\mathcal E_h(\boldsymbol\xi)
=\sum_{e}|\xi_e|+
  \sum_{v=1}^n\left|\tfrac12-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\right|.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal V_{\max}\) be the color class at depths congruent to \(h\) modulo two, and let \(\mathcal V_{\min}\) be the other class. Their sizes satisfy
\[
N_{\min}:=|\mathcal V_{\min}|=
\begin{cases}(n-1)/3,&h\text{ even},\\ n/3,&h\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad N_{\max}=n-N_{\min}.
\]
Then \(\min E_{\mathbf j}=E_0=N_{\max}/2\). Its entire minimizing set in the \(\boldsymbol\xi\) coordinates is
\[
\left\{\boldsymbol\xi\ge0:
\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e=\tfrac12\ (v\in\mathcal V_{\min}),
\quad
\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\le\tfrac12\ (v\in\mathcal V_{\max})\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
and this set has affine dimension \(m-N_{\min}\).

The following partition definitions apply to every integer \(r\ge0\), independently of the budget parameters. On the complete tree of height \(r\), write \(N_r=2^{r+1}-1\), use \(N_r-1\) real edge variables, and fix \(\lambda=29/2\). Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\vartheta_\lambda(t)&=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\lambda|a-t|},\\
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(r)}
&=\int_{\mathbb R^{N_r-1}}
 e^{-\lambda\sum_e|\xi_e|}
 \prod_v e^{-\lambda|1/2-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e|}
 \,d\boldsymbol\xi,\\
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}^{(r)}
&=\int_{\mathbb R^{N_r-1}}
 e^{-\lambda\sum_e|\xi_e|}
 \prod_v\vartheta_\lambda\left(\tfrac12-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\right)
 \,d\boldsymbol\xi.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The root has no incoming edge, and missing children supply no variable. At \(r=0\), the empty edge integral means the displayed vertex factor itself. These are positive finite integrals. The second is the Laplace sum over every integer base, not just the Boolean bases. Write \(\mathcal R_r=\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}^{(r)}/\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(r)}\); this symbol denotes a partition ratio, not a probability measure or an actual mixed volume.

Assume the single finite inequality
\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}^{(6)}
\le\left(\frac85\right)^{127}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(6)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the absolute constant \(\mathsf A=(6e^{29/4})^{63}\). Then every original body above satisfies
\[
W\le\frac52\mathsf A\,2^n w_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{5}
\]
In the canonical notation of 03f071403cdfc69f, for any positive center \(\mathbf a\), put
\(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{\mathbf a+v},w_{\mathbf a-v}\}\),
\(\mathcal U_{\mathbf a}=\{v:\alpha(v)>0\}\),
\(\Delta_{\mathbf a}(v)=\log(w_{\mathbf a}^2/(w_{\mathbf a+v}w_{\mathbf a-v}))\),
\(B_{\mathbf a}^{\Delta}(L)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf a},\ \Delta_{\mathbf a}(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\),
\(K_{\mathbf a}(w)=\max_{L\ge0}B_{\mathbf a}^{\Delta}(L)/(1+L)\), and
\(K(w)=\max_{\mathbf a:w_{\mathbf a}>0}K_{\mathbf a}(w)\).
All displacement sums are oriented and include zero. Under (4), \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(0)\ge w_{\mathbf j}\ge2W/(5\mathsf A2^n)\), and hence the same lower bound holds for \(K_{\mathbf j}(w)\) and \(K(w)\). There exists one \(p^\star\in C_{\mathbf j}\), adjoined to its fixed integer base \(\mathbf j\), such that
\(h_S((\mathbf j,p^\star))\ge W/(30\mathsf A2^n)\).
The point is chosen before every closed ambient halfspace. The constant \((30\mathsf A)^{-1}\) is positive, smaller than \(1/e\), and independent of \(h,k\).

For completeness, (4) has the following positive finite sufficient test. Set \(M=1024\), \(q=e^{-29/2048}\), and define, for residues \(a\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\),
\[
\theta_q(a)=\frac{q^{\bar a}+q^{M-\bar a}}{1-q^M},
\qquad 0\le\bar a<M.
\tag{6}
\]
All arguments of \(\theta_q\) below are interpreted modulo \(M\). Define finite arrays recursively by
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_0(a)&=\theta_q(M/2-a),\qquad a\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z,\\
U_{r+1}(a)&=\frac1{M^2}\sum_{b,c\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z}
 \theta_q(b)U_r(b)\,\theta_q(c)U_r(c)\,
 \theta_q(M/2-a-b-c),\\
V_0(a)&=q^{|M/2-a|},\qquad a\in\{-M,\ldots,M\},\\
V_{r+1}(a)&=\frac1{M^2}\sum_{b,c=-M}^{M}
 q^{|b|}V_r(b)\,q^{|c|}V_r(c)\,
 q^{|M/2-a-b-c|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Only six iterations are required. Neither array includes an incoming-edge weight at its own argument; the child weights are included explicitly in the sums. In particular the root values are exactly \(U_6(0)\) and \(V_6(0)\), with no extra root factor. The entirely finite inequality
\[
U_6(0)\le\left(\frac85\right)^{127}q^{378}V_6(0)
\tag{8}

implies (4), and therefore implies all the conditional conclusions above. This packet proves that implication, not inequality (8). All coefficients and terms in (6)--(8) are nonnegative apart from the explicitly positive denominator \(1-q^M\).

## Proof

We first verify the body and its original volume normalization. The nonnegative simplex in coordinates \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\), with total coordinate sum at most \(R\), maps bijectively to \(C\) by \(y_i=u_i+v_i\), \(z_i=1/2+u_i-v_i+q_i\), leaving the two flow blocks unchanged. The inverse recovers \(u_i,v_i\) by adding and subtracting \(z_i-1/2-q_i\) from \(y_i\) and dividing by two. It is an invertible affine map in dimension \(4n-2\). Thus the body is a full-dimensional rational simplex.

For budget calculations only, let \(w_{\mathbf z}(U)\) denote the fiber volume in the same original continuous coordinates when the budget is \(U\ge0\); in particular \(w_{\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}(R)\). For each edge let \(s_e=\min(t_e^+,t_e^-)\). Outside null boundaries,
\(t_e^+=(g_e)_++s_e\), \(t_e^-=(-g_e)_++s_e\)
has absolute Jacobian one. Subtract the residual absolute values from all \(n\) coordinates of \(\mathbf y\). These nonnegative slacks and the \(m\) variables \(2s_e\) form an \(\ell=n+m\) dimensional simplex of remaining budget \(U-E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)\). Scaling the latter \(m\) variables contributes \(2^{-m}\). Consequently
\[
w_{\mathbf z}(U)=\frac{2^{-m}}{\ell!}
 \int_{\mathbb R^m}(U-E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g))_+^\ell\,d\mathbf g.
\tag{9}
\]
Both original flow directions, their common nonnegative freedom, and every slack coordinate have been retained.

The energy is continuous and coercive because it is at least \(\sum_e|g_e|\), so it attains its minimum. The complete nonempty integer support consists exactly of the bases with \(\min E_{\mathbf z}\le R\). Indeed, the corresponding minimum-cost signed flow and minimal residual \(\mathbf y\) give a feasible point, and every feasible point has at least that cost. The positive-volume support consists exactly of the strict inequality: strict inequality gives a neighborhood of flows with positive integrand in (9), whereas at equality the integrand vanishes everywhere. Boundary fibers remain in \(S\).

Each edge contributes to two vertex divergences, so \(\sum_v|q_v|\le2\sum_e|g_e|\). The triangle inequality gives \(\sum_v|z_v-1/2|\le2E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)\). Thus the nonempty integer support is finite. For any Boolean base, take \(t_e^+=t_e^-=\delta\) and \(y_v=1/2+\delta\), with \(0<d\delta<k\). All fiber inequalities and the budget inequality are strict. Hence every Boolean fiber is positive, including \(w_{\mathbf j}\), and \(0<W<\infty\).

The gauge \(g_v=\sigma_v\xi_v\) has absolute Jacobian one. Since a child's sign is the negative of its parent's, \(\sigma_vq_v=\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\). Also \(\sigma_v(j_v-1/2)=1/2\), giving (1). At an arbitrary integer base, \(\sigma_v(z_v-1/2)\) ranges bijectively and independently over \(\mathbb Z+1/2\). Summing the exponential residual factors at that vertex therefore gives \(\vartheta_\lambda(1/2-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e)\). This proves the complete-lattice interpretation of (3), with the same edge measure and no deleted integer bases.

Summing the geometric series on \(0\le t\le1\) gives
\(\vartheta_\lambda(t)=(e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(1-t)})/(1-e^{-\lambda})\).
Its maximum and minimum are respectively \(\coth(\lambda/2)\) and \(1/\sinh(\lambda/2)\). The integrals in (3) are therefore finite by the bound \(\vartheta_\lambda\le\coth(\lambda/2)\) and \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-\lambda|u|}\,du=2/\lambda\), and are strictly positive. All interchanges with the lattice sum use nonnegative integrands and Tonelli's theorem.

We now derive the coloring minimum, including its full minimizing set. The two geometric sums of the level sizes give the stated values of \(N_{\min},N_{\max}\). Each edge meets exactly one vertex of each color. The identity \(|1/2-a|=1/2-a+2(a-1/2)_+\), applied at vertices of \(\mathcal V_{\max}\), yields the exact decomposition
\[
\mathcal E_h(\boldsymbol\xi)=\frac{N_{\max}}2
 +2\sum_e(-\xi_e)_+
 +\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{\min}}\left|\tfrac12-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\right|
 +2\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_{\max}}
       \left(\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e-\tfrac12\right)_+.
\tag{10}
\]
For example, the edge terms simplify because the sum of incident flows over \(\mathcal V_{\max}\) is \(\sum_e\xi_e\). Every term after the constant is nonnegative, and all vanish precisely on (2). That set is nonempty: put \(1/4\) on each edge from a minority parent to a child, and zero on the other edges. Every minority vertex then has incident sum \(1/2\), and every majority vertex has incident sum at most \(1/4\). Thus the minimum is \(E_0=N_{\max}/2\), and (2) is the entire minimizing set.

To check its dimension rather than just a minimizing witness, assign flow \(1/10\) to an edge whose child is in \(\mathcal V_{\min}\), and \(1/5\) to an edge whose child is in \(\mathcal V_{\max}\). If the root is a minority vertex, replace its two outgoing values by \(1/4\). Every nonroot minority vertex has sum \(1/10+1/5+1/5=1/2\); a minority root also has sum \(1/2\). A majority vertex has sum at most \(1/4+1/10+1/10=9/20<1/2\). All edge values are strictly positive. Hence the nonnegativity and majority inequalities are relatively strict at this point. The \(N_{\min}\) minority equalities are linearly independent: their edge supports are disjoint and nonempty. They consequently give the affine hull of (2), whose dimension is \(m-N_{\min}\). In particular, no unproved matching or transport theorem is used to obtain the minimum or the face.

The exact one-variable message recurrences behind (3) are recorded here to fix the boundary convention for a finite certifier. For real incoming flow \(u\), include its edge cost in the message, and write
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf M_0^{\mathrm B}(u)&=e^{-\lambda|u|}e^{-\lambda|1/2-u|},\\
\mathsf M_0^{\mathrm{all}}(u)&=e^{-\lambda|u|}\vartheta_\lambda(1/2-u),\\
\mathsf M_{r+1}^{\mathrm B}(u)
&=e^{-\lambda|u|}\int_{\mathbb R^2}
 \mathsf M_r^{\mathrm B}(v)\mathsf M_r^{\mathrm B}(w)
 e^{-\lambda|1/2-u-v-w|}\,dv\,dw,\\
\mathsf M_{r+1}^{\mathrm{all}}(u)
&=e^{-\lambda|u|}\int_{\mathbb R^2}
 \mathsf M_r^{\mathrm{all}}(v)\mathsf M_r^{\mathrm{all}}(w)
 \vartheta_\lambda(1/2-u-v-w)\,dv\,dw.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Every child incoming edge is integrated once and has its cost in its child message. The artificial root incoming value is fixed to zero and is not integrated. Thus \(\mathsf M_r^{\mathrm B}(0)=\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(r)}\) and \(\mathsf M_r^{\mathrm{all}}(0)=\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}^{(r)}\). Formula (11) follows directly by separating the two child subtrees in the integrals in (3); it is a recursion for the full finite tree, not a replacement of the common actual budget by separate block budgets.

We next compare those integrals with the original budget, keeping both power contributions. Set \(\zeta=R-E_0=N_{\min}/2+k>0\) and \(X=\lambda\zeta\). For \(E_0\le U\le R\), comparison of the nonzero integrands in (9) gives
\(w_{\mathbf j}(U)\le((U-E_0)/\zeta)^\ell w_{\mathbf j}(R)\).
Indeed, \((U-E)/(R-E)\le(U-E_0)/(R-E_0)\) when \(E_0\le E<U\le R\). For \(U\ge R\), take any point of the nonempty minimum-budget fiber and use a homothety about it with ratio \(\zeta/(U-E_0)\). Convexity of all non-budget inequalities and affinity of the budget show that this homothety takes the budget-\(U\) fiber into the budget-\(R\) fiber. It acts in the same \(d\) original continuous coordinates, so
\(w_{\mathbf j}(U)\le((U-E_0)/\zeta)^d w_{\mathbf j}(R)\).
The volume is zero for \(U<E_0\).

The exact Laplace transform of (9) is
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda U}w_{\mathbf j}(U)\,dU
=\frac{2^{-m}}{\lambda^{\ell+1}}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(h)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Using the two preceding powers on their respective ranges, and enlarging each resulting integral to \([E_0,\infty)\), bounds the left side above by
\(w_{\mathbf j}(R)e^{-\lambda E_0}
[\ell!/(\lambda^{\ell+1}\zeta^\ell)+d!/(\lambda^{d+1}\zeta^d)]\).
For every real \(a\), maximizing \(a^\ell e^{-\lambda a}\) on \(a\ge0\) gives
\((a)_+^\ell\le(\ell/(\lambda e))^\ell e^{\lambda a}\).
Apply this in (9) and sum over every integer base. Together with (12) this proves
\[
\frac{W}{w_{\mathbf j}}
\le\mathcal R_h\,e^{F_\ell(X)}
 \left(1+\frac{d!}{\ell!}X^{-(d-\ell)}\right),
\qquad F_a(X):=X-a+a\log(a/X).
\tag{13}
\]

For integers \(d>\ell\ge1\), monotonicity of \(\log\) on each interval \([j-1,j]\) gives
\[
\log\frac{d!}{\ell!}
\le\int_\ell^d\log t\,dt+\log(d/\ell)
=d\log d-\ell\log\ell-(d-\ell)+\log(d/\ell).
\tag{14}
\]
For the first inequality, each summand \(\log j-\int_{j-1}^j\log t\,dt\) is at most \(\log j-\log(j-1)\), and these latter terms telescope. Inserting (14) in (13) gives
\[
\frac{W}{w_{\mathbf j}}
\le\mathcal R_h\left(e^{F_\ell(X)}+
                  \frac d\ell e^{F_d(X)}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
This comparison uses the common actual budget \(R\); neither a block partition nor a separate-budget product has entered it.

Here \(n\ge127\), \(\ell\ge199n/100\), \(d<3n\), \(d/\ell<3/2\), and
\(29n/12\le X\le1537n/600\). The lower bound follows from \(N_{\min}\ge(n-1)/3\) and \(k\ge1\); the upper bound follows from \(N_{\min}\le n/3\) and \(k\le n/100\). Also \(\ell<X<d\) throughout this range. The derivatives \(\partial_X F_a=1-a/X\) and \(\partial_a F_a=\log(a/X)\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\ell(X)/n
&\le\frac{343}{600}+\frac{199}{100}\log\frac{1194}{1537}
\le\frac{117649}{1638600}<\frac3{40},\\
F_d(X)/n
&\le-\frac7{12}+3\log\frac{36}{29}
\le-\frac7{12}+3\left(\frac7{29}-\frac{49}{2\cdot29^2}
                                   +\frac{343}{3\cdot29^3}\right)
<\frac3{40}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
For the first logarithm use \(\log t\le2(t-1)/(t+1)\) for \(0<t\le1\), proved by differentiation and equality at one. For the second use \(\log(1+x)\le x-x^2/2+x^3/3\) for \(x\ge0\); integrating \(1/(1+x)\le1-x+x^2\) proves it. The remaining comparisons in (16) are rational inequalities. Therefore
\[
\frac{W}{w_{\mathbf j}}\le\frac52 e^{3n/40}\mathcal R_h.
\tag{17}
\]

We now prove the block comparison for the zero-incoming-flow root integrals. Cut any selected \(b\) edges of a tree into connected components and call the cut flows \(u_1,\ldots,u_b\). For the full integrand in (3), restoring one cut flow changes one residual argument at each of its two endpoints. The ratio of any two values of \(\vartheta_\lambda\) is at most \(\cosh(\lambda/2)\), by its exact maximum and minimum above. Restoring all cut flows thus multiplies the product of the component integrands by at most \(\cosh^{2b}(\lambda/2)\prod_i e^{-\lambda|u_i|}\). If several cut edges meet one vertex, the bound still holds, either by restoring them successively or by allowing the extra factors, all at least one. Integrating every cut variable gives
\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}(\text{tree})
\le\cosh^{2b}(\lambda/2)(2/\lambda)^b
     \prod_{\text{components}}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}(\text{component}).
\tag{18}
\]
The component factors use zero at every missing boundary edge.

For the Boolean integrand, each endpoint residual absolute value changes by at most \(|u_i|\). Including the restored edge's own cost proves
\(\mathcal E_{\text{tree}}\le\sum\mathcal E_{\text{component}}+3\sum_i|u_i|\).
This inequality is pointwise for all internal and cut flows, without any fixed-coordinate restriction. Integration yields
\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}(\text{tree})
\ge(2/(3\lambda))^b
     \prod_{\text{components}}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}(\text{component}).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus the cost per connecting edge in the partition ratio is exactly bounded by
\(D:=3\cosh^2(\lambda/2)\). At \(\lambda=29/2\), one has \(D<e^{29/2}\): write
\(D=(3/4)e^\lambda(1+e^{-\lambda})^2\), use \(e^{-\lambda}<1/8\), and note \((3/4)(9/8)^2=243/256<1\). For example \(e>2\), obtained from its first three series terms, implies the stated bound on \(e^{-\lambda}\).

Partition a complete height-\(h\) tree into complete height-six blocks by taking the lowest seven levels as disjoint blocks, deleting them, and repeating on the remaining complete tree. What remains at the root is either empty or a complete tree with at most six levels. Write \(B\) for the number of 127-vertex blocks and \(r\) for the number of vertices in this remainder. Then \(n=127B+r\), where \(r=0\) or \(r=2^s-1\) for \(1\le s\le6\), so \(0\le r\le63\). The component graph is a tree, hence there are \(B-1\) cut edges if the remainder is empty and \(B\) otherwise. In particular \(b\le B\). The positive-incidence coloring gauge makes every block integral the same height-six integral, regardless of the original root's color; a complementary block coloring is related by flow reversal.

The partition ratio of a nonempty remainder is at most \((6e^{29/4})^{63}=\mathsf A\). In fact for a remainder of \(r\) vertices, \(\vartheta_\lambda<2\) gives
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}\le2^r(2/\lambda)^{r-1}\).
The energy at zero flow is \(r/2\), and its change under an arbitrary flow is at most three times that flow's \(\ell_1\) norm. Thus
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}\ge e^{-\lambda r/2}(2/(3\lambda))^{r-1}\).
Their ratio is at most \(6^re^{\lambda r/2}\le\mathsf A\). For an empty remainder its factor is one and satisfies the same bound.

Under the finite hypothesis (4), equations (18)--(19) therefore imply
\[
\mathcal R_h\le\mathsf A
  \left[D\left(\frac85\right)^{127}\right]^B
\le\mathsf A\left[\frac85e^{29/254}\right]^n.
\tag{20}
\]
For the last inequality use \(127B\le n\) and that the displayed base exceeds one. Combining (17) and (20) gives
\[
\frac{W}{2^nw_{\mathbf j}}
\le\frac52\mathsf A
 \left[\frac45e^{3/40+29/254}\right]^n
\le\frac52\mathsf A.
\tag{21}
\]
The bracket is strictly below one by exact elementary inequalities. Indeed
\(3/40+29/254=961/5080<7/32\), whereas
\(\log(5/4)\ge1/4-(1/4)^2/2=7/32\).
The latter bound follows by integrating \(1/(1+x)\ge1-x\) for \(x\ge0\). This proves (5).

At the fixed positive coloring center, the canonical zero displacement has \(\alpha(0)=w_{\mathbf j}\) and \(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(0)=0\), so it belongs to \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(0)\). Apply the actual-body conclusion of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f at this center and cutoff zero. It supplies one continuous point in the original fiber, chosen before all ambient halfspaces, with depth at least \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(0)/12\). Equations (5) and (21) give the stated constant \((30\mathsf A)^{-1}\). This accepted conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing entire fibers. The center, cutoff, and point are not chosen in response to the halfspace.

Finally we prove the finite-grid sufficient condition (8), including the complete flow and lattice sums. In this paragraph the tree has exactly 127 vertices and \(m=126\) edges. For arbitrary real flows \(\boldsymbol\xi,\boldsymbol\xi'\) and arbitrary fixed transformed vertex residual targets, the absolute-value triangle inequality gives
\[
|E(\boldsymbol\xi)-E(\boldsymbol\xi')|
\le\sum_e|\xi_e-\xi_e'|+
    \sum_v\left|\sum_{e\ni v}(\xi_e-\xi_e')\right|
\le3\sum_e|\xi_e-\xi_e'|.
\tag{22}
\]
Each edge has two endpoints, which accounts for the factor three. This bound is uniform over all integer residual targets and includes the fixed Boolean target.

Use the disjoint cells with centers \(\mathbf a/M\), \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z^{126}\), and side length \(1/M\). Assign their boundaries in any measurable disjoint way; these boundaries have zero Lebesgue measure. Every point in such a cell differs from its center by at most \(1/(2M)\) in each coordinate. Write \(\eta=3\lambda m/(2M)=5481/2048\). Equation (22) bounds the integral of an exponential energy on that cell above and below by \(e^{\pm\eta}M^{-126}\) times its value at the center. Summing these bounds over every cell and every integer base is legitimate by Tonelli. In particular, if \(\mathsf S_{\mathrm{all}}\) is the infinite grid sum with the factor \(M^{-126}\), and \(\mathsf S_{\mathrm B}^{\mathrm{tr}}\) retains the Boolean grid points with every edge index in \([-M,M]\), then
\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}^{(6)}\le e^\eta\mathsf S_{\mathrm{all}},
\qquad
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}^{(6)}\ge e^{-\eta}\mathsf S_{\mathrm B}^{\mathrm{tr}}.
\tag{23}
\]
The lower bound uses the full disjoint cells around the selected grid points. Their union is contained in the domain of the original edge integral. No full-lattice or flow tail has been omitted from the upper bound.

Since \(q=e^{-\lambda/M}\) and \(M/2\) is an integer, the grid sum at one vertex over every integer base is
\(\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}q^{|Mz-M/2-a|}=\theta_q(M/2-a)\),
where changing the sign of \(a\) gives the same form when needed because \(\theta_q\) is even. The geometric series also gives exactly
\(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}q^{|a+Mj|}=\theta_q(a)\)
for each residue class. Thus every infinite edge sum in \(\mathsf S_{\mathrm{all}}\) can be periodized modulo \(M\), because all its vertex factors already depend only on these residues. The resulting exact finite sum is
\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{all}}=M^{-126}
 \sum_{\mathbf a\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^{126}}
 \prod_e\theta_q(a_e)
 \prod_v\theta_q\left(M/2-\sum_{e\ni v}a_e\right).
\tag{24}
\]
This equality includes every original grid flow index and every integer base. In contrast, the selected Boolean sum is exactly
