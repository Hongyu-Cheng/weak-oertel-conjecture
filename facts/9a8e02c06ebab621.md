## Statement

Use the planar strip family from the exact seven-base reduction, with $a=4LH$, $c=-H/2$, $e=(1,-1)$, $w=(-1/2,-1/2)$, and $\kappa=2/3$. Write $\ell_0(v)=-v_1-v_2$, $\ell_1(v)=v_1$, and $\ell_2(v)=v_2$. Put $j(b)=\min(1,\max(0,1-(b-c)/H))$ and
$$
\mathcal L(v)=\min\{-\kappa\ell_0(v)+a j(\ell_0(v)),\ a-\kappa\ell_1(v),\ a-\kappa\ell_2(v)\}.
$$
For each bounded convergent sequence $v_t$ as $t\downarrow0$, including every fixed $v$,
$$
A_{6,t}(g+tv_t)=\Gamma V+t\mathcal L(v_t)+o(t),\qquad
A_{7,t}(g+tv_t)=\Gamma V+t[a+\mathcal L(v_t)]+o(t).
$$
In particular, the fixed mark $g$ has seven-base coefficient $3a/2$.

## Proof

If a real variable $Z$ has a density bounded by $B$ and is symmetric about $c$, reflection gives for every $b,\eta$
$$
\Pr(Z\ge b+\eta)+\Pr(Z\ge b-\eta)
=\begin{cases}
1-\Pr(\eta-(b-c)<Z-c<\eta+(b-c)),&b\ge c,\\
1+\Pr(\eta-(c-b)<Z-c<\eta+(c-b)),&b\le c.
\end{cases}
$$
Endpoint choices are immaterial by absolute continuity. The sum is thus at least $\max(0,1-2B(b-c))$ for $b\ge c$ and at least one for $b\le c$. For bounded support a sufficiently large opposite shift gives sum one exactly.

The uniform law on the strip has $X=g+Se+tRw$ where $S,R$ are independent uniforms on $[-L,L]$ and $[c-H,c+H]$. For any real $\sigma$ the law of $R+\sigma S$ is symmetric about $c$ with density at most $1/(2H)$, since convolution with a probability preserves that bound. The infimum over opposite shifts about $b$ is at least $j(b)$. At $\sigma=0$, zero shift attains $j(b)$ for $b\ge c$ and a saturated shift attains it for $b\le c$. For $\sigma\ne0$ convolution also gives the density bound $1/(2L|\sigma|)$, so the infimum tends to one as $|\sigma|\to\infty$, uniformly for bounded $b$ by the displayed estimate and saturation.

For any sequence of unit normals $u_t$ tending to the positive canonical direction of $\ell_0$, rescale the normal by the positive factor $1/(u_t\cdot w)$ to a functional $U_t\to\ell_0$ with $U_t\cdot w=1$. The projection of $X-g$ is exactly $t(R+\sigma_t S)$ with $\sigma_t=U_t\cdot e/t$. The center threshold at $g+tv_t$ in units of $t$ is $U_t\cdot v_t$. Thus $J_t(u_t,g+tv_t)\ge a j(U_t\cdot v_t)$ for every sequence of normal deviations, with equality at the exact canonical direction. If $u_t$ tends to the direction of $\ell_1$ or $\ell_2$, then $|u_t\cdot e|$ stays bounded away from zero, and the projection of $X$ has density at most $1/(2L|u_t\cdot e|)$ by convolution in $S$. Its symmetry center is $u_t\cdot(g+tcw)$, at distance $O(t)$ from the center threshold. The same estimate and saturation give $J_t(u_t,g+tv_t)=a+o(1)$.

The exact six-base minimization accounts for all caps. Take minimizing directions along a subsequence and extract a limit. Testing exact canonical normals gives an upper value $\Gamma V+O(t)$; because $J_t\ge0$, the limiting own triangle cap at $g$ has mass at most $\Gamma V$. Fact 53fe12b742a7608d identifies precisely the three canonical positive normals as equality directions. Near such a direction $u_i$, the own cap cuts off the single vertex $x_i$ of the triangle, since its three margins retain their strict signs near $(g,u_i)$. With margins $m_k=u\cdot(x_k-y)$, its area there is $V m_i^2/\prod_{k\ne i}(m_i-m_k)$ by the two edge intercept fractions. This is smooth and has point derivative $-\kappa\ell_i$ at $(g,u_i)$ by the exact canonical area $V(1-\lambda_i(y))^2$. Therefore for $u_t\to u_i$ the own cap expansion is
$$
F_T(g+tv_t,u_t)=F_T(g,u_t)-t\kappa\ell_i(v_t)+o(t)
\ge\Gamma V-t\kappa\ell_i(v_t)+o(t).
$$
The appropriate optimized-tail lower bound then gives at least $\Gamma V+t\mathcal L(v_t)+o(t)$ along that subsequence. The compactness argument applies to any subsequence that would violate the lower expansion. Testing each exact canonical direction gives the reverse inequality by the same own formula and the paired-tail evaluations. Add $ta$ by the exact seven-base reduction; at $v=0$, $j(0)=1/2$ and $\mathcal L(0)=a/2$.
