## Statement

For every continuous dimension $d\ge1$, there is a compact full-dimensional convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ and a closed nonvertical halfspace such that the integer bases where at least half of the positive fiber volume is strictly uncaptured are exactly $\{-1,1\}$. In particular these bases omit their integer midpoint.

## Proof

In dimension $d=1$ take $C=\{(z,x):-1\le z\le1,\ 3|z|-3\le x\le1\}$ and the closed cap $H=\{(z,x):x\le0\}$. The lower graph is convex, the upper bound affine, and the body is compact and has nonempty interior. The only integer fibers are $[0,1],[-3,1],[0,1]$ at bases $-1,0,1$, so their lengths are $1,4,1$. The strict complement of $H$ cuts $(0,1]$ from each, with lengths $1,1,1$. The relative remainders are therefore $1,1/4,1$, giving the claimed superlevel set. In higher continuous dimension take the product with a unit $(d-1)$-cube and use the same cap ignoring the added coordinates. Both numerator and denominator are multiplied by its unit volume, and full dimension and nonverticality are preserved.
