## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), integer \(T\ge1\), and real \(\beta>0\), put
\[
A_T=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z:\|x\|_1\le t\le T\},\qquad
Z_T(\beta)=\sum_{(x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z_T(\beta).
\]
Define the original normalized closed-halfspace depth and its cubic moment by
\[
\tau_p(x,t)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\text{ closed halfspace}\\(x,t)\in H}}p(H\cap A_T),\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{(x,t)\in A_T}p_{(x,t)}\tau_p(x,t)^3.
\]
These are exactly the depth and moment conventions of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, with its arithmetic dimension \(N=n+1\). All logarithms below are natural. Set
\[
K=512,\quad D_*=10K+8,\quad S_*=65536,\quad
\alpha=\frac{729}{4096},\quad B_*=72K+39,\quad
c_*=\frac{e^{-8S_*}}{2048},\quad \delta=\log(8\alpha)>0,
\]
and
\[
N_* =\left\lceil\max\{64,(B_*/\delta)^2\}\right\rceil,\qquad
\kappa=\min\left\{c_*,\frac{1}{32[(N_*+5)\log2]^3}\right\}>0.
\]
Then \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(n+1)}\). More specifically, whenever \(n\ge64\), \(\beta<\log5\), and \(T>n/32\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge c_*e^{-B_*\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\tag{1}
\]
The only predecessor used is the unconditional cubic estimate in accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564` for the finitely many dimensions \(n<N_*\); every cross-sign comparison and finite-cutoff estimate is proved below.

## Proof

First, the infinite-cone normalizer is explicit. For \(s>0\), let \(r=e^{-s}\) and \(c_s=(1+r)/(1-r)\). Writing \(t=\|x\|_1+u\), \(u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), gives
\[
Z_\infty(s)=\sum_{\|x\|_1\le t}e^{-st}
=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-s|j|}\right)^n\sum_{u\ge0}e^{-su}
=\frac{c_s^n}{1-e^{-s}}.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(s\ge\beta\) and \(\lambda=s-\beta\), the original finite normalizer obeys
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le e^{\lambda T}Z_\infty(s),
\tag{3}
\]
because every original height is at most \(T\). This upper bound changes neither the original law nor its normalizer in any depth calculation.

We next establish the cross-sign one-coordinate factor. For the symmetric geometric law \(q_s(j)=r^{|j|}/c_s\), put
\(f_s(j)=q_s(j)(2|j|+c_s)\). Geometric sums give the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{q_s}f_s
&=\frac{1+r^2}{(1+r)^2}+\frac{4r^2}{(1+r)^4}\\
&=\frac34+\frac{(1-r)^4}{4(1+r)^4}\ge\frac34.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
For example the first line follows by summing
\(c_s^{-2}[c_s+2\sum_{j\ge1}r^{2j}(2j+c_s)]\); the second follows after multiplying by \(4(1+r)^4\).

For an auxiliary rate \(s>0\), define
\[
\nu=4s/3,\qquad c=\coth(\nu/2),\qquad
H_s(j)=2|j|+c,\qquad
F(s)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-4s|j|}H_s(j)^3.
\tag{5}
\]
The function \(z\coth z\) increases for \(z>0\), since its derivative has numerator \(\sinh z\cosh z-z\ge0\). Thus \(c\ge(3/4)c_s\), and \(H_s(j)\ge(3/4)(2|j|+c_s)\). Jensen applied to (4) yields
\[
\frac{F(s)}{c_s^4}
=\mathbb E_{q_s}[(q_s(j)H_s(j))^3]
\ge\left(\frac34\right)^3\mathbb E_{q_s}f_s^3
\ge\left(\frac34\right)^6
=\alpha>\frac18.
\tag{6}
\]
All sums in (4)-(6) are finite at each positive rate.

We require elementary uniform second-moment bounds for the new root law
\[
Q_s(j)=\frac{e^{-4s|j|}H_s(j)^3}{F(s)},\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{7}
\]
We prove \(\mathbb E_{Q_s}|j|^2\le512/s^2\). Write \(q=e^{-4s}\), and first use the one-sided law \(P_s(k)\) proportional to \((2k+c)^3q^k\), \(k\ge0\). Since the two-sided law gives multiplicity one at zero and two at positive magnitudes, its second moment is at most twice that of \(P_s\). The Newton expansion is
\[
(2k+c)^3=d_0+d_1\binom{k}{1}+d_2\binom{k}{2}+d_3\binom{k}{3},
\]
where \(d_0=c^3\), \(d_1=6c^2+12c+8\), \(d_2=24c+48\), and \(d_3=48\). Since \(c\ge1\), these are nonnegative and \(d_1\le26c^3\), \(d_2\le72c^3\), \(d_3\le48c^3\).

The identity \(\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{k}{a}q^k=q^a/(1-q)^{a+1}\) represents \(P_s\) as a mixture, for \(0\le a\le3\), of \(a\) plus a sum of \(a+1\) independent geometric variables with probabilities \((1-q)q^v\). If the mixture index is \(A\), this implies
\[
\mathbb E_{P_s}k^2\le2\mathbb E A^2+\frac{40q}{(1-q)^2}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(s\le1\), use \(A\le3\) and
\(q/(1-q)^2=[2\sinh(2s)]^{-2}\le1/(16s^2)\). Thus the two-sided second moment is at most \(41/s^2\). For \(s\ge1\), one has \(q<1/16\) and \((1-q)^{-1}<2\). The mixture normalizer is at least \(c^3\), so
\[
\mathbb E A^2
\le\frac{26q}{(1-q)^2}+\frac{288q^2}{(1-q)^3}+\frac{432q^3}{(1-q)^4}
\le275q.
\]
Equation (8) bounds the two-sided second moment by \(1420q\). Since \(e^{4s}\ge8s^2\), it is at most \(178/s^2\). Both ranges prove
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_s}|j|^2\le512/s^2.
\tag{9}
\]

For a fixed integer root coordinate \(j\), put
\(e_j(v)=(|v|-|j|)_+\), \(v\in\mathbb Z\). The conditional displacement law is
\[
\mathbb P(V=v\mid j)=\frac{e^{-\nu e_j(v)}}{H_s(j)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed the displacement values with \(e_j(v)=0\) are precisely \(-|j|,\ldots,|j|\), and each positive integer excess has two displacement values. Therefore their total weight is
\(2|j|+1+2/(e^\nu-1)=H_s(j)\). If \(m_s(j)=\mathbb E[e_j(V)\mid j]\) and \(a=e^{-\nu}\), summing positive-excess geometric series gives
\[
0\le m_s(j)=\frac{2a}{(1-a)^2H_s(j)}\le\frac1{\sinh\nu}\le\frac1s,
\qquad
\mathbb E[e_j(V)^2\mid j]\le\frac{2a}{(1-a)^2}\le\frac2{s^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(H_s(j)\ge(1+a)/(1-a)\), and \(2\sinh(\nu/2)\ge\nu\).

The signed vertical displacement contributed by this coordinate is the integer
\[
b_j(v)=\frac{|j+v|-|j-v|}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
It is odd in \(v\), and \(|b_j(v)|=\min\{|j|,|v|\}\le|j|\). Under (10) it has mean zero and variance at most \(j^2\). The exact identities
\[
|j+v|=|j|+e_j(v)+b_j(v),\qquad
|j-v|=|j|+e_j(v)-b_j(v)
\tag{13}
\]
hold for every sign and for zero coordinates. These identities are the mechanism coupling different sign cells.

Let \(B_s(j)=|j|+m_s(j)\), and let \(\overline B_s=\mathbb E_{Q_s}B_s(j)\). Equations (9)-(11) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_s}B_s(j)^2\le4096/s^2,
\qquad s\overline B_s\le64.
\tag{14}
\]
The functions \(m_s(j)\), \(F(s)\), and \(\overline B_s\) are continuous at positive \(s\): on a compact interval of positive rates, their series are dominated by a polynomial times a decaying geometric sequence. Also \(\overline B_s\to0\) as \(s\to\infty\), directly from (7) and (11).

We now prove (1). Assume \(n\ge64\), \(\beta<\log5\), and \(T>n/32\). In particular \(T>1\). Define
\[
g(s)=n\overline B_s+D_*\sqrt n/s+1.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(g(\beta)\le T\), choose \(s=\beta\). Otherwise continuity, \(g(\beta)>T\), and \(g(s)\to1<T\) give a choice \(s>\beta\) with \(g(s)=T\). Always \(s\ge\beta\) and \(g(s)\le T\); equality holds when \(\lambda=s-\beta>0\). When \(s=\beta\), one has \(s<\log5<S_*\). In the other case (14)-(15) give
\[
s(T-1)=ns\overline B_s+D_*\sqrt n\le64n+D_*\sqrt n.
\]
Since \(T-1\ge n/64\) for the stated integer \(T\) and \(n\ge64\),
\[
0<s\le4096+64D_*/\sqrt n\le4096+8D_*=45120<S_*.
\tag{16}
\]

Take independent root coordinates \(J_i\) with law (7), and define the good set of roots by
\[
G_s=\left\{j\in\mathbb Z^n:
\left|\sum_iB_s(j_i)-n\overline B_s\right|\le K\sqrt n/s,
\quad \sum_i j_i^2\le K^2 n/s^2\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Chebyshev, (14), Markov, and (9) show
\[
Q_s^{\otimes n}(G_s)
\ge1-\frac{4096+512}{K^2}>\frac12.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(j\in G_s\), write \(m(j)=\sum_i m_s(j_i)\) and \(L=\lfloor1/s\rfloor\). Use the following distinct original integer heights above that root:
\[
t(j,w)=\|j\|_1+\left\lceil m(j)+(K+4)\sqrt n/s\right\rceil+w,
\qquad 0\le w\le L.
\tag{19}
\]
Put \(u=t(j,w)-\|j\|_1\). The ceiling and \(w\le1/s\) give
\[
m(j)+(K+4)\sqrt n/s\le u\le m(j)+(K+5)\sqrt n/s+1.
\tag{20}
\]

For each fixed good root, choose independent \(V_i\) from (10). Put
\(E=\sum_i e_{j_i}(V_i)\) and \(b=\sum_i b_{j_i}(V_i)\). By (11)-(12), Chebyshev gives the symmetric displacement event
\[
\mathcal V_j=\{v:|E-m(j)|\le K\sqrt n/s,\quad |b|\le8K\sqrt n/s\}
\tag{21}
\]
conditional probability at least
\(1-2/K^2-1/64>1/2\). The second failure estimate uses the second condition in (17). The event is invariant under \(v\mapsto-v\).

For every \(v\in\mathcal V_j\) and every integer \(l\) with \(|l|\le L\), set \(h=b+l\). The corresponding reflected pair is
\((j+v,t+h)\), \((j-v,t-h)\), with \(t=t(j,w)\). Equations (13), (20), and (21) prove both lower cone inequalities because
\(u\ge E+|l|\). They also prove the upper cutoff. Indeed \(|h|\le(8K+1)\sqrt n/s\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
t+|h|
&\le\sum_iB_s(j_i)+(K+5)\sqrt n/s+1+(8K+1)\sqrt n/s\\
&\le n\overline B_s+(10K+6)\sqrt n/s+1\\
&=g(s)-2\sqrt n/s\le T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Thus every endpoint belongs to the full original \(A_T\). In particular the roots in (19) belong to \(A_T\), since \(u\ge0\) and \(t\le T\). The map \((v,l)\mapsto(v,h)\) is injective and is negated by \((v,l)\mapsto(-v,-l)\). Consequently these endpoints form a finite symmetric set about the queried root, with no sign restriction.

Each containing closed halfspace captures at least half this symmetric set, including all boundary atoms. Each captured endpoint has its original weight at least
\(p_{(j,t)}e^{-\beta|h|}\ge p_{(j,t)}e^{-(8K+1)\sqrt n}\). From the exact conditional probability in (10), the number of vectors in (21) is at least
\[
\frac12\prod_iH_s(j_i)
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}m(j)-\frac{4K}{3}\sqrt n\right).
\tag{23}
\]
To see this directly, sum
\(\mathbb P(V=v\mid j)\prod_iH_s(j_i)e^{(4s/3)E}\) over (21), using its probability at least one half and its lower excess bound. Hence for every height (19),
\[
\tau_p(j,t)
\ge\frac{2L+1}{4}p_{(j,t)}\prod_iH_s(j_i)
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}m(j)-\left(8K+1+\frac{4K}{3}\right)\sqrt n\right).
\tag{24}
\]

We now sum (24) with the original probabilities. Cubing and multiplying by \(p_{(j,t)}\), with no conditional renormalization, gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{(2L+1)^3e^{-(28K+3)\sqrt n}}{64Z_T(\beta)^4}
\sum_{j\in G_s}\sum_{w=0}^L
e^{-4\beta t(j,w)}\prod_iH_s(j_i)^3e^{4sm(j)}.
\tag{25}
\]
Using the product auxiliary root law from (7), the summand is exactly
\[
F(s)^n Q_s^{\otimes n}(j)
e^{4\lambda t(j,w)}e^{-4s(u-m(j))}.
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (20) bounds the last factor below by
\(e^{-4(K+5)\sqrt n-4s}\). If \(\lambda>0\), (17) and the lower inequality in (20) imply
\[
t(j,w)\ge n\overline B_s+4\sqrt n/s
=T-(D_*-4)\sqrt n/s-1.
\]
Therefore
\[
e^{4\lambda(t(j,w)-T)}
\ge e^{-4(D_*-4)\sqrt n-4s}.
\tag{27}
\]
The same lower bound holds when \(\lambda=0\), since its left side is then one. Combining (2)-(3), (18), and (25)-(27) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac{e^{-(72K+39)\sqrt n-8s}}{128}
(1-e^{-s})^4(2L+1)^3(L+1)
\left(\frac{F(s)}{c_s^4}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The four powers of the original normalizer in (25) have been bounded only by (3). The sum over \(w\) contributes \(L+1\); it is necessary to retain that factor when the auxiliary rate tends to zero.

The vertical lattice factor in (28) is uniformly bounded below by \(1/16\). For \(0<s\le1\), one has \(1-e^{-s}\ge s/2\), \(L+1\ge1/s\), and \(2L+1\ge L+1\). For \(s\ge1\), use \(1-e^{-s}\ge1-e^{-1}>1/2\) and both counting factors at least one. Now (6), (16), and (28) prove (1).

We cover the two elementary regimes by the original atom at \((0,0)\). Every containing closed halfspace retains this atom, so \(M_3(p)\ge p_{(0,0)}^4=Z_T(\beta)^{-4}\). If \(\beta\ge\log5\), (2) gives
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le Z_\infty(\log5)=\frac54\left(\frac32\right)^n,
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac45\right)^4\left(\frac{16}{81}\right)^n.
\tag{29}
\]
If \(T\le n/32\), for every \(\beta>0\) summing the pointwise inequality
\(e^{-\beta t}\le e^{(\log5)(T-t)}\) on the original support gives
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le5^T Z_\infty(\log5),\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac45\right)^4
\left(\frac{16}{81}\,5^{-1/8}\right)^n.
\tag{30}
\]
The base \(\eta=(16/81)5^{-1/8}\) in (30) exceeds \(1/8\):
\((8\eta)^8=(128/81)^8/5>(3/2)^8/5>1\). Thus both (29) and (30) are at least \((4/5)^4 8^{-n}\), in particular at least \(c_*8^{-(n+1)}\).

For \(n\ge N_*\) outside these elementary regimes, (1) applies, and \(B_*\sqrt n\le\delta n\). It follows that \(M_3(p)\ge c_*8^{-n}\ge\kappa8^{-(n+1)}\).

Finally suppose \(1\le n<N_*\). The set
\(Q_T=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\|x\|_1\le t\le T\}\) is compact and convex, and \(A_T=Q_T\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}=\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\). The last equality follows because \(\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\subseteq Q_T\), whereas each lattice point in that convex hull belongs to \(Q_T\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}=A_T\). The original zero-extended law has globally log-concave extension \(e^{-\beta t}\mathbf1_{Q_T}(x,t)/Z_T(\beta)\). Thus the unconditional cubic estimate (8) of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, with \(N=n+1\), gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-(n+1)}}{32[(n+5)\log2]^3}
\ge\frac{8^{-(n+1)}}{32[(N_*+5)\log2]^3}
\ge\kappa8^{-(n+1)}.
\]
This completes all dimensions, rates, and finite cutoffs with the full original normalization, all signs, and closed boundary atoms.
