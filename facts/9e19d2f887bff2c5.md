## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of volume $k>0$, $d\ge1$, and $0\le\eta\le L\mathbf1_K\,dx$. Write $\alpha=\eta(K)/k$, $\delta=L-\alpha$, and let $g$ be the centroid of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$. For any closed through-$g$ cap put $s=|K\cap H|/k$, $r=\eta(H)/k$, and $q=1+1/d$. Then $\max(0,\alpha-L(1-s))\le r\le\min(\alpha,Ls)$, and if $L>0$,
$$
q(1+2\alpha)s^{q-1}\ge1+2L^{1-q}r^q+2L[s+(\alpha-r)/L]^q-2Ls^q.
$$
Let $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Every closed cap containing $g$ has $s\ge b$ and $s+r\ge b+\max(0,Lb-\delta)$, where
$$
b=\Gamma_d\left(\frac{1+2L(\alpha/L)^{1+1/d}}{1+2\alpha}\right)^d
$$
for $L>0$, and $b=\Gamma_d$ when $L=0$. The bound for $s+r$ is attained when $\alpha=L$ by a simplex centroid cap.

If $2\delta\le d(1+2\alpha)$, every such containing cap satisfies
$$
s+r\ge\frac{\Gamma_d}{2}(1+2\alpha)+E_d(L,\delta),\qquad
E_d(L,\delta)=\frac{\Gamma_d}{2}-\delta\left[1-\Gamma_d+
\frac{2\Gamma_d(1+L)}{1+2L-2\delta}\right].
$$
For $\delta\le1/8$ the hypothesis on $2\delta$ is automatic and $E_d\ge(11\Gamma_d-4)/32>0$. If additionally $L\ge1$, then $E_d\ge(39\Gamma_d-11)/88>1/44$.

## Proof

Normalize the measures by $k$, so the uniform volume probability is $\kappa=\mathbf1_K\,dx/k$ and the added measure is $\nu=\eta/k$. The centroid $g$ is interior, since the weighted law has positive mass in the interior and every supporting plane is null. Tighten any containing cap to pass through $g$. For a through cap with nonzero normal $u$, let $V(t)=\kappa\{x:u\cdot x\ge t\}$. On the projection interval $V^{1/d}$ is concave by Brunn–Minkowski on pairs of nested parallel caps. All boundaries are null and every open interior projection interval has positive probability, so $V$ is continuous and strictly decreasing from one to zero. The rank $V(u\cdot x)$ is uniform under $\kappa$ and has density $0\le h\le L$ under $\nu$, with total mass $\alpha$ and mass $r$ in $[0,s]$. Domination gives the bounds on $r$. Jensen applied to $\kappa+2\nu$ after normalization yields
$$
(1+2\alpha)s^{1/d}\ge d/(d+1)+2\int_0^1 v^{1/d}h(v)\,dv.
$$
On any interval a mass of given size under ceiling $L>0$ minimizes its integral against an increasing function by filling the leftmost subinterval at density $L$. Indeed the difference from that filled density integrates to zero, and multiplying by the integrand minus its cutoff value gives a nonnegative product on each side. Apply this to $[0,s]$ and $[s,1]$ with lengths $r/L$ and $(\alpha-r)/L$ and integrate $v^{1/d}$ to obtain the two-band inequality. Applying it to the whole interval with length $\alpha/L$ instead gives $s\ge b$. The complement measure $L\kappa-\nu$ has mass $\delta$, so $r\ge\max(0,Ls-\delta)$ and the combined expression follows by monotonicity. For $L=0$ Jensen gives $s\ge\Gamma_d$ directly. At saturation $\eta=L\mathbf1_K\,dx$, choose the cap from the apex of a simplex cut by the plane through its centroid parallel to its opposite facet. Its volume fraction is $(d/(d+1))^d$ by similarity, so it attains the bound.

For the explicit margin, use $h=L-(L-h)$ and $\int (L-h)=\delta$. Since $v^{1/d}\le1$, Jensen gives
$$
s^{1/d}\ge\frac d{d+1}-\frac{2\delta}{(d+1)(1+2\alpha)}.
$$
The right side is nonnegative by assumption. Taking powers and using Bernoulli gives $s\ge\Gamma_d[1-2\delta/(1+2\alpha)]$. Add $r\ge Ls-\delta$ and substitute $\alpha=L-\delta$ to get the displayed $E_d$. For $\delta\le1/8$ one has $2\delta\le1/4\le d(1+2\alpha)$. The ratio $2(1+L)/(1+2L-2\delta)$ decreases with $L$ and increases with $\delta$; it is at most $9/4$ on $L\ge\delta$ by evaluating at $L=\delta$, and at most $16/11$ on $L\ge1$ by evaluating at $(L,\delta)=(1,1/8)$. Substitution yields the two margins, using $\Gamma_d>4/11>1/3$ because $(1+1/d)^d<e<11/4$. The last strict bound on $e$ follows from the series through degree three plus the geometric tail at most $(1/24)/(1-1/5)$.
