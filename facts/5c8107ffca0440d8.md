## Statement

Let $K$ be a positive $d$-volume actual fiber at integer base $p$ of a compact mixed body, $d\ge1$, and let $\mu$ be the original summed $d$-volume. For finitely many equal-mass pairs of endpoint measures $\gamma_{j,-},\gamma_{j,+}$, with common mass $m_j$ in pair $j$, suppose their total sum is dominated by $\mu$ after removal of the whole own-fiber measure. Suppose each pair has a physical coupling with midpoint law $\eta_j$ on the $p$-fiber, and the sum $\eta=\sum_j\eta_j$, identified there with a measure in $\mathbb R^d$, satisfies $\eta\le\mathbf1_K\,dx$. If $g$ is the centroid of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$, then $g\in\operatorname{int}K$ and the original raw depth at $(p,g)$ is strictly greater than $(3/4)\gamma_d(|K|+\sum_j m_j)$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$.

## Proof

For each closed ambient halfspace $H$, an affine margin nonnegative at the midpoint of two points must be nonnegative at at least one endpoint. Integrating that indicator inequality in each given coupling, summing and using the assumed endpoint domination off the own fiber gives
$$
\mu(H)\ge |K\cap H_p|+\eta(H_p),\qquad H_p=\{x:(p,x)\in H\}.
$$
This inequality holds for the same aggregate coupling budget for every $H$. The aggregate $\eta$ is dominated by the volume on the positive $d$-body $K$, and has mass $\sum_jm_j$. Hence 607dca34de472b94 applies to exactly $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$. It places $g$ in the interior and gives a dimension-only strictly positive surplus times $|K|$ over the asserted bound whenever the continuous normal is nonzero. If a containing halfspace has zero continuous normal, $H_p=\mathbb R^d$ and the payment estimate gives $|K|+\sum_jm_j$. Its surplus over the asserted bound is $(1-3\gamma_d/4)(|K|+\sum_jm_j)>0$. The smaller of these two surpluses is positive and uniform in $H$, so the strict estimate holds for the depth infimum.
