## Statement

For $k,Q_i,\sigma_i>0$, $i=1,2,3$, set $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_i M)$, $P=f_1f_2f_3$ and $\Psi=\sum_i f_i^2+P$. Reduce each $Q_i$ by $E_i\ge0$ and $k$ by $E=\sum_iE_i$, with $\sigma_i$ fixed and all final data positive. If initially $\Psi\le4$, then finally $\Psi\le4$, strictly if $E>0$. At any positive boundary point $\Psi=4$, a unit loss of $k$ and $Q_s$ has derivative $-\alpha_s$ satisfying
$$2k\alpha_s\ge\frac{(8+P)P(4-f_s^2)}{4(2f_s^2+P)}>0.$$

## Proof

Write $D_s=f_s\partial_{f_s}\Psi=2f_s^2+P$ and $C=\sum_iD_i$. Along a unit loss in cap $s$ and total area, logarithmic differentiation gives
$$2k\alpha_s=C+D_sT_s^2-Cf_sT_s,\qquad T_s=\sqrt{k/Q_s}.$$
Here the three common terms in $\log f_i$ contribute $C/(2k)-C/(\sigma_sM)$ to $\alpha_s$, and the term at $s$ contributes $D_s/(2Q_s)$. At $\Psi=4$ we have $C=8+P$, $0<f_s<2$, and $4D_s-Cf_s^2=P(4-f_s^2)$. Completing the square in $T_s$ gives $2k\alpha_s\ge C(4D_s-Cf_s^2)/(4D_s)$, yielding the stated strict bound.

The affine loss path stays in the positive orthant. If $E>0$ its derivative at every point with $\Psi=4$ is $-\sum_s E_s\alpha_s<0$. Initial equality therefore becomes strict for small positive time. A first later equality from below, including at the final endpoint, would have nonnegative left derivative. This is impossible. If $E=0$ the data are unchanged.
