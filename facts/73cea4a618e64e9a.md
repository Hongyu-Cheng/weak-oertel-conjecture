## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), put \(n=3m\) and \(d=16m^2\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
T&=\left\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d:
 y_j\ge0\ (1\le j\le d),\quad \sum_{j=1}^d y_j\le1\right\},\\
s(\mathbf y)&=5m\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^d y_j\right),\\
\ell_i(\mathbf y)&=
\begin{cases}
s(\mathbf y),&1\le i\le2m,\\
m^2s(\mathbf y),&2m<i\le3m,
\end{cases}\\
C&=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
 \mathbf y\in T,\quad -\ell_i(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i(\mathbf y)
 \ (1\le i\le n)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Keep the canonical definitions

\[
\begin{gathered}
\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,\qquad
C_I=\{\mathbf y:(I,\mathbf y)\in C\},\\
W=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_I|,\qquad
N_C(\mathbf y)=\sum_I\mathbf1_{C_I}(\mathbf y),\qquad
R_C(\mathbf y)=\sum_I\mathbf1_{C_I\cap C_{-I}}(\mathbf y),\\
A(\mathbf y)=(\lfloor s(\mathbf y)\rfloor+1)^{2m}
                 (\lfloor m^2s(\mathbf y)\rfloor+1)^m
\quad(\mathbf y\in T).
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(|\cdot|\) denotes \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume, all count densities are zero outside \(T\), and \(\mathbb E_T f=|T|^{-1}\int_T f\). The set \(C\) is a convex body, \(C_{\mathbf0}=T\), and the complete counts, including integer endpoints and continuous boundaries, are

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_C(\mathbf y)&=(2\lfloor s(\mathbf y)\rfloor+1)^{2m}
                   (2\lfloor m^2s(\mathbf y)\rfloor+1)^m
                   &&(\mathbf y\in T),\\
N_C&=2^{3m}A,\qquad
W=2^{3m}|T|\,\mathbb E_T A>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Put \(B_m=(4m^2e^{-16/5})^m\) and \(K=\{\mathbf y\in T:s(\mathbf y)\ge1\}\). The original mass and the original unlifted moment satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-4/5}B_m&\le\mathbb E_T A\le280mB_m,\\
\frac{(\mathbb E_T R_C^{2/3})^{3/2}}{\mathbb E_T A}
&\le3(125m)^{3/2}\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^m
\longrightarrow0\qquad(m\longrightarrow\infty).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently there is no absolute \(c>0\) for the comparison \((\mathbb E_T R_C^{2/3})^{3/2}\ge c\,\mathbb E_T A\) throughout the class of bounded nonnegative concave product roofs, even when every roof is affine.

On the other hand, with the absolute constant \(c_0=3/11200\in(0,1/e)\), for every \(m\ge1\) there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in K\) such that

\[
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))
=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\ \text{a closed halfspace}\\
                  (\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\in H}}
 \mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge \frac{3}{11200}\,\frac{W}{2^{3m}}.
\tag{5}
\]

The point in (5) belongs to the original \(S\) and is selected before all the halfspaces, including halfspaces with zero continuous normal. The denominator uses the full original \(W\) from (2), for every pair \(n=3m,d=16m^2\).

## Proof

The only accepted input used below is the first continuous-density inequality of fact \(\texttt{d4a3be98ad8645b7}\), applied with its convex support \(T\) replaced by the cap \(K\) defined here. Its mixed-integer conclusion requiring support in \([-1,2]^n\) is not applied. We prove all counts, estimates, and the passage to actual depth directly.

The simplex \(T\) is a convex body. Every roof in (1) is affine, continuous, nonnegative, and bounded on \(T\). Its maximum is \(5m\) in the first group and \(5m^3\) in the second group. Thus the defining inequalities make \(C\) closed, bounded, and convex. It contains \([0,1]^n\times T\), which has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), so \(C\) is a convex body. Each map \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\) preserves it, and \(C_{\mathbf0}=T\).

For \(\mathbf y\in T\), let \(M_i=\lfloor\ell_i(\mathbf y)\rfloor\). The complete integer section is \(\prod_i\{-M_i,\ldots,M_i+1\}\). An integer site \(I\) and its negative both occur precisely when \(I\in\prod_i\{-M_i,\ldots,M_i\}\). The first product has \(\prod_i2(M_i+1)\) elements and the second has \(\prod_i(2M_i+1)\) elements. This proves (3) pointwise, including the cases where a roof is an integer, where a roof is zero, and where \(\mathbf y\) lies on any face of \(T\). There are no sites outside the stated products. Integration of these finite counts gives the identity for \(W\). Since \(A\ge1\) on \(T\), its normalized integral and \(W\) are positive.

Under normalized volume on \(T\), the scalar \(s\) has survival function and density

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_T(s\ge u)&=\left(1-\frac{u}{5m}\right)^{16m^2}
                    &&(0\le u\le5m),\\
f_m(u)&=\frac{16m}{5}
       \left(1-\frac{u}{5m}\right)^{16m^2-1}
                    &&(0<u<5m).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, \(\{s\ge u\}=(1-u/(5m))T\). Every level set is contained in an affine hyperplane and has zero \(d\)-volume, including the two endpoint levels, so there are no atoms. Differentiating the survival function gives the density. The inequalities \(1-t\le e^{-t}\) and \(u/(5m)\le1\) imply the common envelope

\[
f_m(u)\le\frac{16m}{5}e^{-16mu/5+u/(5m)}
        \le\frac{16e}{5}m e^{-16mu/5}
        \qquad(0<u<5m).
\tag{7}
\]

We record exact elementary exponential bounds used in the estimates. The series terms for \(e\) through degree three sum to \(8/3\), and its remaining terms sum to strictly less than \((1/24)\sum_{k\ge0}5^{-k}=5/96\). Hence \(e<87/32<11/4<3\). For \(0\le t<1\), termwise comparison of the exponential and geometric series gives \(e^t\le(1-t)^{-1}\). Also \(e^{16/5}>1+16/5+(16/5)^2/2=233/25\). These comparisons use only positive series terms.

The cap is the convex body \(K=(1-1/(5m))T\), because its scale is positive for every \(m\ge1\). Integration of \((1-v)^{-1}\) on \([0,t]\) gives \(\log(1-t)\ge-t/(1-t)\) for \(0\le t<1\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
16m^2\log\left(1-\frac1{5m}\right)
&\ge-\frac{(16/5)m}{1-1/(5m)}\\
&=-\frac{16m}{5}-\frac{16/25}{1-1/(5m)}
 \ge-\frac{16m}{5}-\frac45,\\
\frac{|K|}{|T|}
&\ge e^{-4/5}e^{-16m/5}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

On \(K\), one has \(\lfloor s\rfloor+1\ge2\) and \(\lfloor m^2s\rfloor+1\ge m^2s\ge m^2\). Consequently \(A\ge(4m^2)^m\) there. Integration and (8) prove \(\mathbb E_T A\ge e^{-4/5}B_m\), the lower mass estimate in (4).

We next establish the upper bound for the full original mass. Since \(\lfloor m^2u\rfloor+1\le m^2(u+m^{-2})\), (7) gives

\[
\mathbb E_T A
\le\frac{16e}{5}m\,m^{2m}
 \int_0^\infty
 (u+m^{-2})^m(\lfloor u\rfloor+1)^{2m}e^{-16mu/5}\,du.
\tag{9}
\]

The extension of this nonnegative integrand from \([0,5m]\) to \([0,\infty)\) is solely an upper estimate. On \([0,1)\), the floor factor is one. The function \(v^m e^{-16mv/5}\) has maximum \((5/(16e))^m\) at \(v=5/16\), by differentiating its logarithm. Substituting \(v=u+m^{-2}\) and using the interval length one yields

\[
\int_0^1(u+m^{-2})^m e^{-16mu/5}\,du
\le e^{16/(5m)}\left(\frac5{16e}\right)^m
\le e^{16/5}\left(\frac5{16e}\right)^m.
\tag{10}
\]

For each integer \(j\ge1\), the function \((u+m^{-2})^m e^{-16mu/5}\) is decreasing on \([j,j+1]\), as its logarithmic derivative is \(m/(u+m^{-2})-16m/5<0\). Also
\((j+m^{-2})^m\le j^m e^{1/(mj)}\le e j^m\).
Thus the contribution of \([j,j+1)\) to the integral in (9) is at most \(e[j(j+1)^2e^{-16j/5}]^m\).

For all \(j\ge1\), induction gives \(j\le2^{j-1}\) and \(j+1\le2(3/2)^{j-1}\). The latter follows also from \((j+2)/(j+1)\le3/2\). With \(q=(9/2)e^{-16/5}\), it follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
j(j+1)^2e^{-16j/5}&\le4e^{-16/5}q^{j-1},\\
q&<\frac{225}{466}<\frac12,\\
\sum_{j\ge1} e[j(j+1)^2e^{-16j/5}]^m
&\le\frac{e(4e^{-16/5})^m}{1-q^m}
 \le2e(4e^{-16/5})^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Here the bound on \(q\) uses \(e^{16/5}>233/25\). Combining (9)--(11) and dividing by \(B_m\) gives

\[
\frac{\mathbb E_T A}{B_m}
\le\frac{16e}{5}m
 \left[e^{16/5}\left(\frac5{64}e^{11/5}\right)^m+2e\right].
\tag{12}
\]

The recorded bounds \(e<11/4\) and \(e^{1/5}\le5/4\) imply

\[
\frac5{64}e^{11/5}
<\frac5{64}\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^2\frac54
=\frac{3025}{4096}<1,
\qquad
e^{16/5}<\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^3\frac54
=\frac{6655}{256}<26.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus the right side of (12) is strictly less than
\((44/5)m(26+11/2)=(1386/5)m<280m\).
This proves the upper mass estimate in (4) for every \(m\ge1\), with no part of the original mass omitted.

For the fractional-moment estimate, put \(\varepsilon_m=1/(2m^2)\). The inequality \(2\lfloor m^2u\rfloor+1\le2m^2(u+\varepsilon_m)\), together with (3) and (7), gives

\[
\mathbb E_T R_C^{2/3}
\le\frac{16e}{5}m(2m^2)^{2m/3}
 \int_0^\infty
 (u+\varepsilon_m)^{2m/3}(2\lfloor u\rfloor+1)^{4m/3}
 e^{-16mu/5}\,du.
\tag{14}
\]

As in (9), the nonnegative extension only enlarges the original integral. On \([0,1)\), the floor factor is one, and \(v^{2m/3}e^{-16mv/5}\) has maximum \((5/(24e))^{2m/3}\) at \(v=5/24\). Therefore

\[
\int_0^1(u+\varepsilon_m)^{2m/3}e^{-16mu/5}\,du
\le e^{8/(5m)}\left(\frac5{24e}\right)^{2m/3}
\le e^{8/5}\left(\frac5{24e}\right)^{2m/3}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(j\ge1\), the logarithmic derivative of \((u+\varepsilon_m)^{2m/3}e^{-16mu/5}\) on \([j,j+1]\) is \(m(2/(3(u+\varepsilon_m))-16/5)<0\). Moreover,
\((j+\varepsilon_m)^{2m/3}\le j^{2m/3}e^{1/(3mj)}\le e^{1/3}j^{2m/3}\).
Hence the contribution of this interval to the integral in (14) is at most
\(e^{1/3}[j^{2/3}(2j+1)^{4/3}e^{-16j/5}]^m\).

Induction gives \(2j+1\le3(5/3)^{j-1}\), since \((2j+3)/(2j+1)\le5/3\) for \(j\ge1\). Using also \(j\le2^{j-1}\), and putting \(\beta=(50/9)^{2/3}e^{-16/5}\), we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
j^{2/3}(2j+1)^{4/3}e^{-16j/5}
&\le3^{4/3}e^{-16/5}\beta^{j-1},\\
\beta&<\frac{50/9}{233/25}
 =\frac{1250}{2097}<\frac35,\\
\sum_{j\ge1}e^{1/3}
 [j^{2/3}(2j+1)^{4/3}e^{-16j/5}]^m
&\le\frac{e^{1/3}(3^{4/3}e^{-16/5})^m}{1-\beta^m}
 \le\frac52e^{1/3}(3^{4/3}e^{-16/5})^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The strict bound on \(\beta\) also uses \((50/9)^{2/3}<50/9\). Combining (14)--(16) and dividing by \(B_m^{2/3}\) gives the exact exponential bases

\[
\frac{\mathbb E_T R_C^{2/3}}{B_m^{2/3}}
\le\frac{16e}{5}m
 \left[
 e^{8/5}\left(\frac5{48}e^{11/5}\right)^{2m/3}
 +\frac52e^{1/3}\left(\frac92e^{-8/5}\right)^{2m/3}
 \right].
\tag{17}
\]

Both bases are strictly below \(99/100\). For the first, the same estimates as in (13) give

\[
\frac5{48}e^{11/5}
<\frac5{48}\left(\frac{11}{4}\right)^2\frac54
=\frac{3025}{3072}<\frac{99}{100}.
\tag{18}
\]

For the second, the first four terms of the exponential series give
\(e^{8/5}>1+8/5+(8/5)^2/2+(8/5)^3/6=1711/375\).
Thus \((9/2)e^{-8/5}<3375/3422<99/100\).
Finally, \(e<3\), \(e^{8/5}<e^2<9\), and \(e^{1/3}\le3/2\). The coefficient left in (17) is at most
\((48/5)m(9+15/4)=(612/5)m<125m\).
Consequently

\[
\mathbb E_T R_C^{2/3}
\le125m B_m^{2/3}\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^{2m/3}.
\tag{19}
\]

Raise (19) to the power \(3/2\), divide by the lower mass bound in (4), and use \(e^{4/5}<3\). This proves the moment bound in (4). The ratio of consecutive terms of \(m^{3/2}(99/100)^m\) tends to \(99/100<1\), so the bound tends to zero. This disproves every proposed positive absolute moment constant. For a finite strict violation of the constant one, one can take \(m=4000\): then \(125m=500000<2^{19}\), so \(3(125m)^{3/2}<2^{31}\), while \((99/100)^{4000}\le e^{-40}<2^{-40}\). The displayed ratio is then less than \(2^{-9}\).

It remains to prove (5) for the same bodies. Put \(r_m=3^{2m}(2m^2+1)^m\). The exact counts in (3) imply \(R_C\ge r_m\) everywhere on the closed cap \(K\): on this cap \(\lfloor s\rfloor\ge1\) and \(\lfloor m^2s\rfloor\ge m^2\). By (8) and the upper mass estimate,

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_m|K|
&\ge |T|e^{-4/5}e^{-16m/5}
      3^{2m}(2m^2+1)^m\\
&\ge |T|e^{-4/5}\left(\frac92\right)^m B_m,\\
\frac{r_m|K|}{W/2^{3m}}
&\ge \frac{e^{-4/5}}{280m}\left(\frac92\right)^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

Consider the continuous density \(\rho=R_C\mathbf1_K\) on \(\mathbb R^d\). It is measurable, bounded, nonnegative, and supported on the convex body \(K\). Define
\(\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)=\inf_{L\ni\mathbf y}\int_L\rho\), where \(L\) ranges over proper closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^d\), exactly as in fact \(\texttt{d4a3be98ad8645b7}\). The first density inequality of that accepted fact, with its support replaced by \(K\), supplies a maximizing point \(\mathbf y^\star\in K\) satisfying

\[
\tau_\rho(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{\left(\int_K\sqrt{R_C}\right)^2}
          {(5+2\sqrt6)|K|}
\ge\frac{r_m|K|}{5+2\sqrt6}.
\tag{21}
\]

This application uses the actual dimension \(d=16m^2\), with no dimension change. Since \(K\subseteq T=C_{\mathbf0}\), the point \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\) belongs to the original \(S\).

We verify the ambient halfspace statement at this fixed point. For each continuous coordinate \(\mathbf z\), the finite set
\(\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}=\{I\in\mathbb Z^n:\mathbf z\in C_I\cap C_{-I}\}\)
is centrally symmetric about zero and has cardinality \(R_C(\mathbf z)\). Every site in this set is an actual site of the original \(C\) at \(\mathbf z\).

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star)\) contains a parallel closed halfspace through that point, of the form

\[
H_0=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf z):
 \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0
 \right\},
\qquad (\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0).
\tag{22}
\]

Whenever \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0\), at least half the sites in \(\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}\) satisfy \(\mathbf a\cdot I\le0\), by pairing \(I\) with \(-I\). Each such site lies in \(H_0\). The fixed site \(I=\mathbf0\), when present, is included as well. If \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), integration over the continuous halfspace \(L=\{\mathbf z:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y^\star)\le0\}\) therefore gives

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12\int_L R_C
\ge\frac12\int_L\rho
\ge\frac12\tau_\rho(\mathbf y^\star).
\tag{23}
\]

If \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), the same count holds at every \(\mathbf z\), giving \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)\ge\frac12\int R_C\ge\frac12\int\rho\ge\frac12\tau_\rho(\mathbf y^\star)\). These counts retain every boundary site. If a whole-space halfspace is admitted, its mass is \(W\), which is also at least this bound because \(\rho\le R_C\le N_C\). Thus all containing closed halfspaces satisfy the bound, and taking their infimum yields \(h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))\ge\frac12\tau_\rho(\mathbf y^\star)\).

For every integer \(m\ge1\), the quantity \((9/2)^m/m\) is at least \(9/2\): it equals \(9/2\) at \(m=1\), and its successive ratio is \((9/2)m/(m+1)\ge9/4>1\). Combining this fact with (20), (21), and (23) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))
&\ge\frac{e^{-4/5}}{560(5+2\sqrt6)}
       \frac{(9/2)^m}{m}\,\frac{W}{2^{3m}}\\
&\ge\frac{9e^{-4/5}}{1120(5+2\sqrt6)}\,\frac{W}{2^{3m}}
>\frac3{11200}\,\frac{W}{2^{3m}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The final strict comparison follows from \(e^{4/5}<3\) and \(5+2\sqrt6<10\). It proves (5) with the stated non-strict inequality. Also \(0<3/11200<1/3<1/e\), since \(e<3\). The construction of \(\mathbf y^\star\) in (21) precedes the choice of every ambient halfspace. All mass comparisons in (20)--(24) use the original \(W=2^{3m}|T|\mathbb E_T A\), completing the joint assertion.
