## Statement

Let \(D\) be a finite set satisfying \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^2\), \(D\subseteq\{0,1,2\}\times\mathbb Z\), and \(\dim\operatorname{conv}(D)=2\). Let \(\lambda,\beta\in\mathbb R\), let \(\mathcal Z=\sum_{(x,y)\in D}e^{\lambda x+\beta y}\), and let \(\nu(x,y)=e^{\lambda x+\beta y}/\mathcal Z\). Suppose the full mean of this original law is \(\mathbb E_\nu(X,Y)=(1,j)\), where \(j\in\mathbb Z\) and \((1,j)\in D\). For \(i\in\{0,1,2\}\), write \(D_i=\{y:(i,y)\in D\}\), \(\mathcal Z_i=\sum_{y\in D_i}e^{\beta y}\), \(\alpha_i=e^{\lambda i}\mathcal Z_i/\mathcal Z\), \(m_i=\mathbb E_\nu[Y\mid X=i]\), and let \(H_i\) be the natural-log entropy of the conditional law of \(Y\) given \(X=i\). Then all three columns are nonempty, both original mean equations are

\[
\alpha_0=\alpha_2,
\qquad
\alpha_0m_0+\alpha_1m_1+\alpha_2m_2=j\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad (1,j)\in D,
\]

and \(\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(\log\alpha_i-H_i)<40\). Moreover \(\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\log\nu)<41\). These bounds hold for every real original tilt \(\beta\), including zero, without replacing the full mean by a projected or fractional mean.

## Proof

Positivity on \(D\), two-dimensionality, and \(\mathbb EX=1\) force both outer columns to be occupied. Indeed, if column 0 were absent, positive mass on column 2 would give \(\mathbb EX>1\), while its absence too would make \(D\) one-dimensional; the argument for column 2 is the same. Column 1 is occupied because \((1,j)\in D\). Completeness and convexity imply that each column is an integer interval. Write \(D_i=\{a_i,a_i+1,\ldots,b_i\}\) and \(N_i=b_i-a_i+1\ge1\). The x-mean equation is \(\alpha_1+2\alpha_2=1=\alpha_0+\alpha_1+\alpha_2\), so \(\alpha_0=\alpha_2\). The y-mean equation is exactly \(\sum_i\alpha_i m_i=j\), as displayed in the statement. All subsequent masses and conditional laws remain those of the original law satisfying these two equations.

Reflecting \(y\) to \(-y\), if necessary, replaces \((\beta,j)\) by \((-\beta,-j)\), relabels the probabilities, and preserves both variances and both full-mean equations. Thus assume \(\beta\ge0\).

We first record two consequences of the midpoint interval. The section of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) at \(x=1\) contains

\[
\left[\frac{a_0+a_2}{2},\frac{b_0+b_2}{2}\right].
\]

Its endpoints are half-integers. If they are \(L,U\), the number of integers in this interval is \(\lfloor U\rfloor-\lceil L\rceil+1\ge U-L\); this follows from \(\lfloor U\rfloor\ge U-1/2\) and \(\lceil L\rceil\le L+1/2\). Hence \(N_1\ge(N_0+N_2-2)/2\). Put \(M=\max(N_0,N_2)\). For \(M\ge3\), this gives \(N_1\ge(M-1)/2\ge M/3\). For \(M=1\) or \(2\), the already established \(N_1\ge1\) also gives \(N_1\ge M/3\). Furthermore,

\[
N_1\ge\frac13\max(N_0,N_2),
\qquad
b_1\ge\left\lfloor\frac{b_0+b_2}{2}\right\rfloor
\ge\frac{b_0+b_2}{2}-\frac12.
\]

For the upper-endpoint assertion, the real middle section has upper endpoint at least \((b_0+b_2)/2\); since it contains an integer and \(D\) is complete, its largest integer is the floor of its upper endpoint. This proves the assertion even when the midpoint interval itself contains no integer.

For \(N\ge1\), define \(s_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-\beta k}\) and let \(d_N\) be the mean of \(k\) under probabilities proportional to \(e^{-\beta k}\), \(0\le k<N\). Set \(s_i=s_{N_i}\) and \(d_i=d_{N_i}\). Then \(\mathcal Z_i=e^{\beta b_i}s_i\), \(m_i=b_i-d_i\), and

\[
H_i=\log s_i+\beta d_i.
\]

For \(\beta>0\), the finite geometric sum and its logarithmic derivative give

\[
s_N=\frac{1-e^{-N\beta}}{1-e^{-\beta}},
\qquad
d_N=\frac1{e^\beta-1}-\frac{N}{e^{N\beta}-1}.
\]

In particular \(0\le\beta d_N\le\beta/(e^\beta-1)\le1\), where the final inequality follows from \(e^\beta\ge1+\beta\). At \(\beta=0\), \(s_N=N\) and \(\beta d_N=0\), so the same bound applies.

For this fixed \(\beta\ge0\), the sequence \(s_N/N\) is nonincreasing: it is the average of the first \(N\) terms of a nonincreasing positive sequence. Thus \(N_1\ge M/3\) implies \(s_1\ge s_M/3\). If \(N_1\le M\), use \(s_{N_1}/N_1\ge s_M/M\); if \(N_1\ge M\), use monotonicity of \(s_N\). Consequently \(s_1\ge\max(s_0,s_2)/3\).

Write \(a=\alpha_0=\alpha_2\in(0,1/2)\), so \(\alpha_1=1-2a\). The original x-mean identity gives \(e^{2\lambda}=\mathcal Z_0/\mathcal Z_2\), and therefore

\[
a=\frac1{2+\mathcal Z_1/\sqrt{\mathcal Z_0\mathcal Z_2}}.
\]

Consider first \(0\le\beta\le1\). Define \(u=\log(s_1/\sqrt{s_0s_2})\) and \(v=\tfrac12\log(s_0/s_2)\). The two midpoint consequences yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal Z_1}{\sqrt{\mathcal Z_0\mathcal Z_2}}
&=e^{\beta(b_1-(b_0+b_2)/2)}\frac{s_1}{\sqrt{s_0s_2}}
\ge e^{-\beta/2+u},\\
a&\le e^{1/2-u},
\qquad u\ge |v|-\log3.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(t\ge0\), differentiation shows \(t^2e^{-t}\le4/e^2\). If \(u\ge0\), the last display gives \(au^2\le4/e^{3/2}<1\). If \(u<0\), it gives \(au^2\le(\log3)^2/2<1\). Also,

\[
av^2\le3e^{1/2}|v|^2e^{-|v|}\le\frac{12}{e^{3/2}}<3.
\]

The strict numerical inequalities used here are elementary: \(e>8/3\), so \(e^{3/2}>4\); and \(e^{4/3}>1+4/3+(4/3)^2/2=29/9>3\), so \(\log3<4/3\) and \((\log3)^2/2<8/9<1\). We have proved \(a(u^2+v^2)<4\).

For any real constant \(c\), \(\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(H_i)\le\sum_i\alpha_i(H_i-c)^2\). Take \(c=H_1\). Since each \(\beta d_i\) is in \([0,1]\), the inequality \((r+t)^2\le2r^2+2t^2\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(H_i)
&\le a\{(H_0-H_1)^2+(H_2-H_1)^2\}\\
&\le2a\left\{\left(\log\frac{s_0}{s_1}\right)^2
+\left(\log\frac{s_2}{s_1}\right)^2\right\}+4a\\
&=4a(u^2+v^2)+4a<18.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now consider \(\beta\ge1\). Each conditional entropy is nonnegative, and the preceding geometric estimates give \(H_i\le-\log(1-e^{-1})+1<2\), where the strict inequality follows from \(e>2\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(H_i)\le\sum_i\alpha_i(H_i-1)^2\le1\). In both ranges we have the uniform bound \(\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(H_i)<18\).

For any probability \(p\in(0,1]\), the same maximum of \(t^2e^{-t}\), with \(t=-\log p\), gives \(p(\log p)^2\le4/e^2\). Applying this to the three original column masses yields \(\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(\log\alpha_i)\le\sum_i\alpha_i(\log\alpha_i)^2\le12/e^2\). Centering the two random variables and using \((r-t)^2\le2r^2+2t^2\), we conclude

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(\log\alpha_i-H_i)
\le2\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(\log\alpha_i)
+2\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(H_i)
<\frac{24}{e^2}+36<40.
\]

Here \(e>8/3\) also gives \(e^2>6\), proving the final strict inequality. The entropy estimate uses the supported full mean only to ensure column 1 is present, and uses the exact x-mean to set the two outer masses equal. Thus it in fact proves a stronger statement than needed; it applies, in particular, to every original law satisfying the exact y-mean equation as well.

For completeness, the conditional information variance can also be bounded directly. For \(\beta>0\), differentiating the logarithm of the finite geometric sum twice gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X=i)
&=\beta^2\left\{\frac{e^{-\beta}}{(1-e^{-\beta})^2}
-\frac{N_i^2e^{-N_i\beta}}{(1-e^{-N_i\beta})^2}\right\}\\
&\le\left(\frac{\beta}{2\sinh(\beta/2)}\right)^2\le1,
\end{aligned}
\]

since \(2\sinh(\beta/2)\ge\beta\). At \(\beta=0\), the conditional information is constant and its variance is zero. The conditional expectation of the original log probability is \(\log\alpha_i-H_i\). Hence total variance gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\nu(\log\nu)
=\sum_{i=0}^2\alpha_i\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X=i)
+\operatorname{Var}_\alpha(\log\alpha_i-H_i)<41.
\]

All steps above were derived directly from completeness, convexity, and the original probabilities; no unverified predecessor is used.
