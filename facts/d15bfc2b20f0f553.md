## Statement

For each positive row of the original-volume reweighting, put $B_p=\sum_z\alpha_{pz}D_z\subset D_p$ and $s_p=h_{D_p}-h_{B_p}$. With $S_{D_p}$ the surface-area measure on unit normals,
$$
\int s_p\,dS_{D_p}
=d\left(|D_p|-\sum_z\alpha_{pz}V(D_z,D_p[d-1])\right)\ge0,
$$
where $V(A,B[d-1])$ is the first mixed volume. In $d=1$ use counting measure on the two endpoint normals and $V(A,B[0])=|A|$. When weighted by $\sigma_p$, the mixed-volume term uses the same $\widehat\pi_{pz}$ and a separate source area measure for each $p$. If $S_{D_p}$ has full spherical support and its slack integral vanishes, then $B_p=D_p$.

## Proof

Minkowski addition gives $h_{B_p}=\sum_z\alpha_{pz}h_{D_z}$. Integrate against $S_{D_p}$ and use the first mixed-volume formula $\int h_A\,dS_B=dV(A,B[d-1])$, with $V(B,B[d-1])=|B|$. The one-dimensional formula is the identity $h_A(1)+h_A(-1)=|A|$. Nonnegativity follows from the inclusion, and weighting uses $\sigma_p\alpha_{pz}=\widehat\pi_{pz}$. If the integral vanishes for a measure of full support, continuity and nonnegativity imply $s_p=0$ everywhere. Equality of support functions gives equality of bodies. Without full support, the zero integral alone tests only the normals in the support of the source area measure.
