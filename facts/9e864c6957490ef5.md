## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=W(C):=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,\qquad
 G:=G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\]
Assume
\[
 G<\frac W2.
\tag{1}
\]

Use the free same-parity matching normalization of accepted fact
`f23ff6dd60fbbc37`.  Thus a free matching is a nonnegative family \(p=(p_e)\) on unordered pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z\) with \(z\ne z'\) and \(z\equiv z'\pmod 2\), with
\[
 d_z(p):=\sum_{e\ni z}p_e\leq\frac{a_z}{2},
 \qquad |p|:=\sum_ep_e.
\tag{2}
\]
Call \(p\) maximum when its mass is maximum.  For every maximum \(p\), put
\[
 t_z:=2d_z(p)\quad(z\in Z),\qquad
 E_p:=\{z\in Z:t_z>0\},\qquad
 T(p):=\sum_{z\in Z}t_z=4|p|.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E_p\) be any admissible point, meaning that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_p\cap J}t_z\geq2^{-n}T(p).
\tag{4}
\]
At \(m\), form the degree-saturated core
\[
 \mathcal K_m(t):=
 \bigcap_{\alpha\in\Lambda_m(E_p)}
 \sum_{z\in E_p}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\qquad
 \Lambda_m(E_p):=
 \left\{\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^{E_p}:
 \sum_z\alpha_z=1,\ \sum_z\alpha_zz=m\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Under (1), accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37` ensures that this core is empty for every maximum \(p\) and every admissible \(m\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal Q=(r,(w_j,\theta_j,\ell_j)_{j=1}^r)
\tag{6}
\]
be any support-minimal balanced negative circuit supplied by accepted fact
`07009c5047a9f7bb`, including any valid choice of its exact affine minorants.  In particular, each \(w_j\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), each
\(\ell_j:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) is affine, and
\[
 \ell_j(z)\leq
 h_{\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)}(w_j)
 \qquad(z\in E_p).
\tag{7}
\]
For every actual individual cap in this circuit,
\[
 A_i:=C\cap\{(z,x):w_ix\geq\ell_i(z)\},
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq r,
\tag{8}
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{W(C)-W(A_i)\leq\frac32G(C).}
\tag{9}
\]
The quantifiers in (9) range over every maximum \(p\), every admissible \(m\), every circuit (6), every valid exact affine-minorant choice, and every index \(i\).  Consequently, for the precise descendant collection and capacity defined in accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`,
\[
 \boxed{R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\geq W(C)-\frac32G(C).}
\tag{10}
\]
No attainment of the supremum defining \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\) is assumed.

## Proof

The set \(Z\) is finite because the base projection of the compact body \(C\) is compact.  Also \(G>0\).  Indeed, fix \(z_0\in Z\), take the singleton selected set \(E=\{z_0\}\), selected mass \(a_{z_0}\), and center \(m=z_0\) in accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb`.  Its metronoid core is the nonempty singleton consisting of the centroid of \(C_{z_0}\), while every proper closed base halfspace containing \(z_0\) contains all the selected mass.  The nonempty-core branch of that fact gives \(G\geq a_{z_0}>0\).  Thus the hypotheses of accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37` apply.

We first extract from that accepted fact the unmatched-mass estimate with the correct quantifiers.  It supplies one saturated maximum matching \(p^*\) such that, with
\[
 U(p^*):=\{z\in Z:t_z^*<a_z\},
\]
one has \(t_z^*=a_z\) off \(U(p^*)\) and
\[
 \sum_{z\in U(p^*)}a_z\leq G.
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore
\[
 W-T(p^*)
 =\sum_{z\in Z}(a_z-t_z^*)
 =\sum_{z\in U(p^*)}(a_z-t_z^*)
 \leq G.
\tag{12}
\]
Now let \(p\) be an arbitrary maximum matching.  Since all maximum matchings have the same mass and \(T(p)=4|p|\),
\[
 \sum_{z\in Z}(a_z-t_z)
 =W-T(p)
 =W-T(p^*)
 \leq G.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus (13), not pointwise saturation, is available for every maximum matching.

We record the exact one-dimensional metronoid formula.  If
\(K=[L,U]\) has length \(a=U-L>0\), then for every \(0<s\leq a\),
\[
 \mathsf M_s(K)=
 \left[L+\frac s2,\ U-\frac s2\right].
\tag{14}
\]
For any density \(0\leq f\leq1\) of mass \(s\), the one-dimensional rearrangement inequality places its first moment between the first moments of the leftmost and rightmost subintervals of length \(s\).  These two indicator densities attain the respective bounds.  The admissible densities form a convex set and the barycenter map is affine, so every value between the two extremal barycenters is attained.  This proves (14).

Fix an arbitrary maximum \(p\), admissible \(m\), circuit \(\mathcal Q\), exact-minorant choice, and index \(i\).  Write
\[
 C_z=[L_z,U_z]\quad(z\in Z),
 \qquad
 q_z:=a_z-\operatorname{vol}_1((A_i)_z).
\tag{15}
\]
We treat both possible signs of the nonzero scalar \(w_i\).

Suppose first that \(w_i>0\), and put
\(\lambda:=\ell_i/w_i\).  Then \(\lambda\) is affine and
\[
 (A_i)_z=C_z\cap[\lambda(z),\infty).
\tag{16}
\]
The threshold may lie outside the fiber, and its exact clipped loss is
\[
 q_z=\min\{a_z,\max\{0,\lambda(z)-L_z\}\}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(z\in E_p\), (7) and (14) give
\[
 \lambda(z)\leq U_z-\frac{t_z}{2}<U_z.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus a selected fiber cannot be empty.  If the cap contains the whole selected fiber, then \(\lambda(z)\leq L_z\) and \(q_z=0\).  Otherwise \(q_z>0\), the threshold is inside the fiber, and
\[
 \lambda(z)=L_z+q_z.
\tag{19}
\]

Suppose instead that \(w_i<0\), again put
\(\lambda:=\ell_i/w_i\), and note that division reverses the cap inequality.  Thus
\[
 (A_i)_z=C_z\cap(-\infty,\lambda(z)],
\tag{20}
\]
with exact clipped loss
\[
 q_z=\min\{a_z,\max\{0,U_z-\lambda(z)\}\}.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(z\in E_p\), (7) and (14), with division by the negative scalar \(w_i\), give
\[
 \lambda(z)\geq L_z+\frac{t_z}{2}>L_z.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus a selected fiber again cannot be empty.  If the cap contains the whole selected fiber, then \(\lambda(z)\geq U_z\) and \(q_z=0\).  Otherwise \(q_z>0\), the threshold is inside the fiber, and
\[
 \lambda(z)=U_z-q_z.
\tag{23}
\]

In either orientation define the selected profile
\[
 s_z:=\min\{2q_z,t_z\},\qquad
 E_s:=\{z\in Z:s_z>0\},\qquad
 S:=\sum_{z\in E_s}s_z.
\tag{24}
\]
This is a legal profile: \(0<s_z\leq t_z\leq a_z\) on \(E_s\), while every unselected fiber and every selected fiber wholly contained in the cap has \(s_z=0\).  We claim that
\[
 \lambda(z)\in\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)
 \qquad(z\in E_s).
\tag{25}
\]
In the upper-cap orientation, (19), \(s_z\leq2q_z\), \(s_z\leq t_z\), and (18) give
\[
 L_z+\frac{s_z}{2}
 \leq\lambda(z)
 \leq U_z-\frac{t_z}{2}
 \leq U_z-\frac{s_z}{2}.
\]
In the lower-cap orientation, (22), \(s_z\leq t_z\), (23), and
\(s_z\leq2q_z\) give
\[
 L_z+\frac{s_z}{2}
 \leq L_z+\frac{t_z}{2}
 \leq\lambda(z)
 \leq U_z-\frac{s_z}{2}.
\]
Formula (14) proves (25) in both cases.  The clipped whole-fiber cases are absent from \(E_s\), so (25) does not incorrectly identify an exterior threshold with a metronoid point.

We next prove the affine-selector core certificate
\[
 S\leq G.
\tag{26}
\]
This is immediate if \(S=0\).  If \(S>0\), apply accepted fact
`6d6fcf377e68c79c` with continuous dimension zero to the finite atomic measure
\[
 \nu:=\sum_{z\in E_s}s_z\delta_z.
\]
It gives \(\widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every closed base halfspace containing \(\widehat m\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(2^{-n}S\).  The point \(\widehat m\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}E_s\): otherwise strict separation would give a closed halfspace containing \(\widehat m\) and disjoint from \(E_s\), contradicting the positive depth bound.  For every \(\alpha\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_s)\), affineness of \(\lambda\) and (25) give
\[
 \lambda(\widehat m)
 =\sum_{z\in E_s}\alpha_z\lambda(z)
 \in\sum_{z\in E_s}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z).
\tag{27}
\]
Hence \(\lambda(\widehat m)\) belongs to the complete metronoid core for the profile \((s_z)_{z\in E_s}\).  The centerpoint inequalities make this profile admissible, so the nonempty-core branch of accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb` yields (26).

It remains to compare the actual loss with the selected profile.  We claim, for every \(z\in Z\),
\[
 q_z\leq(a_z-t_z)+\frac{s_z}{2}.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(t_z=0\), then \(s_z=0\) and (28) is the trivial inequality \(q_z\leq a_z\).  Suppose \(t_z>0\).  If \(2q_z\leq t_z\), then \(s_z=2q_z\), and (28) follows from \(a_z-t_z\geq0\).  If \(2q_z>t_z\), then \(s_z=t_z\).  The individual-cap lower bound in accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb`, specialized to
\(\gamma_1=1/2\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_1((A_i)_z)\geq\frac{t_z}{2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 q_z\leq a_z-\frac{t_z}{2}
 =(a_z-t_z)+\frac{s_z}{2},
\]
which proves (28).

Summing (28), then applying (13) and (26), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W-W(A_i)
 &=\sum_{z\in Z}q_z\\
 &\leq\sum_{z\in Z}(a_z-t_z)+\frac12\sum_{z\in E_s}s_z\\
 &\leq G+\frac G2
 =\frac32G.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The matching, admissible point, circuit, minorants, and cap index were arbitrary, so (9) holds with all the stated quantifiers.

Finally, accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37` shows that the descendant collection is nonempty and contains every individual cap just considered.  For any such actual cap,
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\geq W(A_i)\geq W-\frac32G.
\]
This proves (10) directly and does not require the descendant supremum to be attained.
