## Statement

Fix $n\ge0,d\ge1$, finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$, and full-dimensional compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu=\sum_z\delta_z\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_z})$ and $M=\mu(S)$. A cap has margin $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$ with $\|u_i\|=1$, threshold $t_{iz}=b_i-a_i\cdot z$, mass $c_i=\mu\{L_i\ge0\}$, and hinge $\Psi_i=\int_S(L_i)_+\,d\mu$. For each $z$ the traces cover $\mathbb R^d$ if and only if there are simplex weights $\lambda_{zi}$ with $\sum_i\lambda_{zi}u_i=0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_{zi}t_{iz}\le0$. Equivalently the epigraph LP has finite attained value
$$
F_z=\inf_{x\in\mathbb R^d}\max_i L_i(z,x)
=\max_{\lambda\in\Delta,\ \sum_i\lambda_i u_i=0}-\sum_i\lambda_i t_{iz}\ge0.
$$
At any feasible budget $\tau$ with $c_i\le\tau M$, the finite positive minimum of $\Psi=\sum_i\Psi_i$ over such whole-space covers is attained for a fixed cap count. This holds whether all unit normals are variable or some normals are prescribed, in particular with $N$ variable normals and a fixed frame of $2d$ normals $+e_l,-e_l$. Every cap has free offsets and base tilts and contributes to both objective and budgets.

## Proof

The epigraph LP minimizes $v$ over $(x,v)$ with $u_i\cdot x-t_{iz}\le v$ for all $i$. It is always feasible. If the traces cover the whole space then the maximum margin is nonnegative everywhere, so the LP is bounded below. Linear programming duality gives attained primal and dual values, with the stated dual constraints and objective. Conversely any such weights with weighted threshold at most zero give nonnegative weighted margin everywhere, hence a covering cap at every point.

Choose a minimizing sequence with total hinge at most $B$ and a common ball $RB_d$ containing the source fibers. For all $i,z$,
$$
B\ge |K_z|(-R-t_{iz})_+,
$$
so the thresholds are uniformly bounded below by finiteness of $P$ and positive fiber volumes. Apply accepted finite affine clipping 0a73ba4a9c7b24d1 to each cap, along a common subsequence. Unit normals converge to unit normals, fixed ones stay fixed, and each threshold has a finite limit or tends to $+\infty$. The realizing affine thresholds retain all finite evaluations and exceed $R$ at divergent evaluations. For $n=0$ there is one site and the scalar threshold itself has the stated finite limit or can be set above $R$. Masses converge by the clipping lemma (or null boundary at the single site). Hinges converge by uniform convergence of the integrands on each finite-threshold fiber, and by their eventual vanishing on every divergent fiber.

For each $z$ choose balanced certificates on the sequence and take a convergent subsequence of weights. Weights at positive divergent thresholds tend to zero: all terms have a common threshold lower bound, while a weight bounded away from zero there would force the weighted threshold sum to diverge positively. For all sufficiently late indices, the divergent-threshold terms are nonnegative. Dropping them preserves the upper bound of zero on the remaining weighted threshold sum, which therefore passes to the finite limits. The limiting weights on the finite coordinates sum to one, have zero normal resultant, and certify whole-space coverage for the realizing affine thresholds. This proves feasibility and hinge attainment without a bound on any minimizing contact.

For positivity, choose an inball of radius $r_z>0$ in each source fiber. A cap containing its center has hinge on that ball at least the integral of the positive part of a unit linear coordinate through its center, namely $\kappa_{d-1}r_z^{d+1}/(d+1)$ with $\kappa_0=1$. This follows by integrating slices perpendicular to the unit normal. Summing over fibers gives a positive lower bound on the objective.
