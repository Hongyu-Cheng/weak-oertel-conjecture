## Statement

Let $K$ be a planar simplex of area $k>0$ with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ for $i=0,1,2$. Let $N=\sum_{i=0}^2\mathbf1_{\{\lambda_i\ge1/3\}}$ on $K$ and $D=\{N\ge2\}$. Every translated positive homothet $Q=t+\sigma K\subseteq K$ with $\sigma\ge1/2$ satisfies $|Q\cap D|\ge 2|Q|/9$, with equality for a scale-$1/2$ homothet at any vertex.

Suppose $A,K,B$ are the fibers at bases $-1,0,1$ of a mixed set, with planar areas $a,k,b$, $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, $A$ a translated positive homothet of $K$, $B$ a compact convex body, and $a=k+b$ with $b>0$. The maximum of $\int N((x+y)/2)\,d\Pi(x,y)$ over couplings with right marginal $\mathbf1_B\,dy$ and left marginal dominated by $\mathbf1_A\,dx$ is attained and is at least $b+\min(b,2a/9)>4b/3$. Consequently, any three closed ambient halfspaces $H_i$ extending the three prescribed traces $K\cap\{\lambda_i\ge1/3\}$ satisfy
$$
\sum_{i=0}^2\mu(H_i)\ \ge\ 4k/3+b+\min(b,2a/9)\ >\ 4a/3,
$$
where $\mu$ is the sum of the actual fiber areas (any extra nonnegative fiber mass only increases the left side). The three caps therefore cannot all have mass below $(4/9)a$. For a zero-area right fiber and $a=k$, that same impossibility holds by the middle traces alone.

## Proof

Write $\lambda_i(t+\sigma X)=l_i+\sigma\lambda_i(X)$ for $X$ uniform on $K$. Containment gives $l_i\ge0$ by evaluating at a vertex with $\lambda_i=0$. The sum of the $l_i$ equals $1-\sigma$, so $\sigma\le1$. Let $s_i=(1/3-l_i)/\sigma$. These thresholds satisfy $\sum_i s_i=1$ and $-1/3\le s_i\le 2/3$: the upper bound follows from $l_i\ge0$ and the lower bound from $l_i\le1-\sigma$ and $\sigma\ge1/2$. Every point of $K$ has at least one barycentric coordinate at least $1/3$, and only its centroid has all three at least $1/3$. Thus the area fraction of $Q\cap D$ is the sum of the three cap fractions on $Q$ minus one. When all $s_i\ge0$, those fractions are $(1-s_i)^2$ by simplex dilation, and their sum is at least $(\sum_i(1-s_i))^2/3=4/3$. Otherwise exactly one $s_i$, say $s_0$, is negative, since two negative thresholds would force the third above one. The zero-indexed cap contains $Q$, and the other two have fractions $(1-s_1)^2,(1-s_2)^2$. Their sum is at least $(1+s_0)^2/2\ge2/9$. A scale-$1/2$ vertex homothet has thresholds $(-1/3,2/3,2/3)$ up to permutation, attaining the bound.

For each $y\in B$, $(A+y)/2$ is such a homothet, with scale $\sqrt{a/k}/2\ge1/2$. Undoing the dilation gives $|\{x\in A:(x+y)/2\in D\}|\ge2a/9$. Apply the section-capacity matching lemma to the closed relation $\{(x,y)\in A\times B:(x+y)/2\in D\}$ with $c=2a/9$. Complete its good matching of mass at least $\min(b,2a/9)$ to a coupling of the required marginals, since $a\ge b$. All outputs are in $K$ and have $N\ge1$; the good outputs have $N\ge2$. This proves the asserted lower bound even for couplings having an atom at the centroid. The admissible completed couplings are weakly compact by bounded mass on a compact product and closed marginal constraints. The objective is upper semicontinuous under weak convergence because it is a sum of masses of three closed inverse images. The maximum is attained.

Brunn–Minkowski in dimension two gives $2\sqrt{k}\ge\sqrt a+\sqrt b$. With $r=\sqrt{b/a}$ and $k/a=1-r^2$, this gives $5r^2+2r-3\le0$, hence $r\le3/5$. In particular $b/3\le(3/25)a<2a/9$, while $b>b/3$.

For each $H_i$, its affine margin at $(0,(x+y)/2)$ is the average of its values at $(-1,x)$ and $(1,y)$. Thus if the midpoint belongs to the closed cap, one of the two endpoints belongs to it. Integrate the indicator bound against the completed coupling and use its marginal bounds. Summing the three inequalities pays for the midpoint multiplicity from the two disjoint endpoint fibers, counting each cap once. Each actual middle cap has area $(2/3)^2k=4k/9$; adding these three contributions proves the stated bound. When $a=k$ and $b=0$, each middle trace already contributes $4a/9$.
