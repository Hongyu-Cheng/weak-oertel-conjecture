## Statement

Let $\mu$ be normalized summed Lebesgue volume on the integer fibers $K_z$ of a compact mixed body with one integer coordinate, total raw volume $M>0$ and continuous dimension $d\ge1$. At an integer median $z_0$ with $\mu\{z=z_0\}<1/2$, form the usual probability half-marginals by proportional splitting of the central fiber. Consider only their couplings $\sigma$ with zero central-central mass and let $\nu_\sigma$ be their median-segment image on the continuous fiber. For any $h\in\mathbb R^d$ and any such coupling there is another such coupling $\sigma_h$ with
$$
\sup_D|\nu_{\sigma_h}(D)-\nu_\sigma(D-h)|
\le e(h):=\min\left(1,\frac2M\sum_z[|K_z|-|K_z\cap(K_z-h)|]\right),
$$
where $D$ ranges over Borel sets. The two laws share the translated image of the retained pairs as an exact submeasure. Thus both optimized normalized closed-halfspace depth at $y$ and each optimized finite cap-mixture capacity with fixed unit normals and nonnegative weights summing to one change by at most $e(h)$ under $y\mapsto y+h$. In particular they are Lipschitz uniformly over the mixtures.

## Proof

Translate both endpoints by $h$ in the continuous coordinate and retain only pairs whose translated endpoints are still in their own fibers. The translated retained marginals remain dominated by the original half-marginals, since those marginals have constant densities on individual fibers. Write $e$ for the discarded pair mass. The residual marginal masses are both $e$, and the sum of their central masses is at most $e$: translation does not change bases and no discarded pair had two central endpoints. Hence the residual central mass on each side fits in the residual strict mass on the opposite side. Couple each central marginal to an equal-mass submeasure of the opposite strict part and then couple the leftover strict parts, using products normalized by the common block mass and omitting zero-mass blocks. This completes the translated subcoupling without creating a forbidden pair.

On every legal pair the median segment map commutes with common continuous translation, so the new and translated old target laws share the entire translated retained image, including its atoms. The two remainders have equal mass $e$, giving the Borel-set difference bound $e$. The union bound on discarded pairs is the sum of the two endpoint losses. The half-marginals sum to $2\mu$, which gives $e\le e(h)$. Cap membership in the translated old law through $y+h$ agrees with the old membership through $y$. An infimum over through-mark caps, or an average of such indicators with simplex weights, changes by at most $e(h)$. Taking suprema over the couplings gives one inequality and translating by $-h$ gives the reverse, since $e(h)=e(-h)$.

For $h\ne0$, slice each convex $K_z$ along lines parallel to $h$. Each line interval loses at most $\|h\|$ in its intersection with its translate. Fubini bounds the loss by $\|h\|$ times the $(d-1)$-volume of the orthogonal projection, with counting for $d=1$. These finitely many projection volumes are uniformly bounded in direction by enclosing balls.
