## Statement

Let n>=1, let a_i>0 and theta_i<=0 for 1<=i<=n, let u be an integer vector that is nonzero modulo 2, and let r belong to {0,1}. Let R be real and suppose that
\[
D_r=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot z\le R,\ u\cdot z\equiv r\pmod2\}
\]
is nonempty. Define the ORIGINAL probabilities by Z_r=sum_{z in D_r} exp(theta dot z) and p_r(z)=exp(theta dot z)/Z_r. For j in D_r let tau_{p_r}(j) be the infimum of p_r(H intersect D_r) over all closed halfspaces H in R^n containing j, and let M_3(p_r)=sum_{j in D_r}p_r(j)tau_{p_r}(j)^3.

The following constants, with natural logarithms, are absolute:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=(8/27)^{3/2},&\gamma&=8192,&b&=20+\log(48\gamma^4),\\
c&=e^{-(20+b)}/65536,&B&=\gamma b+248,&\delta&=\log(8\alpha)>0,\\
N&=\left\lceil\max\{1,((B+3)/\delta)^2\}\right\rceil,
&\kappa&=\min\{c,1/(64\cdot8^N)\}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then every stated input satisfies
\[
M_3(p_r)\ge c\,n^{-3}e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n,
\qquad M_3(p_r)\ge\kappa\,8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
All depths in (1) use the original probabilities on the complete D_r. This theorem concerns the registered atomic resource model, not the locked mixed-integer theorem.

## Proof

Write w_i for the representative of u_i modulo 2 in {0,1}. Nonemptiness and positive a_i imply R>=0 and finiteness of D_r. We first prove an even-residue estimate allowing w=0 as well. This allowance is needed for the affine charts in the odd-residue argument.

1. Coordinate estimates.

For 0<q<1 put
\[
F_3(q)=\sum_{v\ge0}(2v+1)^3q^v
=\frac{1+23q+23q^2+q^3}{(1-q)^4}.
\]
This identity follows by applying (2q d/dq+1)^3 to (1-q)^-1. For s>0 let Q_s(v)=(2v+1)^3 exp(-4sv)/F_3(exp(-4s)), and write mu(s)=E_{Q_s}v. Put q=exp(-4s). Under Q_s, v has the distribution of K+G, independently, where K has probabilities proportional to (1,23q,23q^2,q^3) on {0,1,2,3}, and G is the sum of four independent geometric variables of mass (1-q)q^k. Indeed their probability generating functions multiply to F_3(qt)/F_3(q). Consequently
\[
E K^2\le124q,\qquad E G^2=\frac{4q+16q^2}{(1-q)^2}.
\]
For t=4s, t^2 exp(-t)<=4/e^2<1 and q/(1-q)^2=[2sinh(t/2)]^-2<=t^-2. The inequality (K+G)^2<=2K^2+2G^2 gives
\[
E_{Q_s}v^2\le18/s^2,\qquad \mu(s)\le5/s.
\tag{2}
\]
Let h=log 3. For 0<s<=h, convexity gives exp(4s)-1<=80s/h, and therefore
\[
\mu(s)\ge4/(e^{4s}-1)\ge1/(20s).
\tag{3}
\]
Here h>1. Differentiation of the convergent exponential series gives mu'(s)=-4 Var_{Q_s}(v)<=0.

We also need the uniform coordinate factor
\[
(1-e^{-s})^4 F_3(e^{-4s})\ge\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
This is proved here without an undeclared input. For x in (0,1), let F_2(q)=(1+6q+q^2)/(1-q)^3=sum_{v>=0}(2v+1)^2q^v. The exact polynomial identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
27(1+6x^3+x^6)-8(1+x+x^2)^3
&=(1-x)^2\{19(1-x^2)^2\\
&\hspace{23mm}+14x(1-x)^2+27x^2\}\ge0
\end{aligned}
\]
shows (1-x)^3 F_2(x^3)>=8/27. Under the geometric probability P_s(v)=(1-e^-s)e^-sv, the nonnegative variable W=(2v+1)P_s(v) has second moment (1-e^-s)^3F_2(e^-3s) and third moment (1-e^-s)^4F_3(e^-4s). The probability-space inequality E W^3>=(E W^2)^{3/2} proves (4).

Finally the coordinate parity bias satisfies
\[
E_{Q_s}(-1)^v\ge-1/2.
\tag{5}
\]
To see this, the bias of K is
\[
\frac{1-23q+23q^2-q^3}{1+23q+23q^2+q^3}.
\]
It is at least -1/2 because 3+69q^2-23q-q^3>=2+69q^2-23q>=2-529/276=23/276>0. The bias of G is ((1-q)/(1+q))^4 in (0,1). Independence proves (5), and every bias is also at most 1. It follows that under any product of Q_s coordinates and deterministic zero coordinates, P(w dot J even)>=1/4: the expectation of (-1)^{w dot J} is a product of factors in [-1/2,1], which is at least -1/2. If w=0 this probability is 1.

2. Resource dual and a root law.

In this section r=0, and the normalizer Z_0 is the actual even-residue normalizer. If R=0 then D_0={0} and M_3(p_0)=1, so the desired even-residue estimate holds. Assume R>0.

Set psi(s)=log F_3(e^-4s)/4, phi(s)=max{0,psi(s)-psi(h)} for s>0, and phi(0)=+infinity. Since psi'=-mu and psi''=4 Var_{Q_s}(v)>=0, phi is convex, vanishes for s>=h, and tends to infinity at zero. Minimize
\[
\lambda R+\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(\lambda a_i-\theta_i)
\quad\text{over }\lambda\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
A positive lambda gives a finite value; finite sublevels are bounded because R>0 and phi>=0. A minimizing subsequence has a limit. Divergence at zero excludes any zero coordinate argument at that limit, so continuity at positive arguments proves attainment. Fix a minimizer and set t_i=lambda a_i, s_i=t_i-theta_i>0, mu_i=mu(s_i), and Lambda=lambda R. Then 0<=t_i<=s_i.

The one-sided derivatives in (6) give a vector x with x_i=mu_i for s_i<h, x_i=0 for s_i>h, and 0<=x_i<=mu_i for s_i=h, such that
\[
a\cdot x\le R,\qquad \lambda(R-a\cdot x)=0,
\qquad \#\{i:0<x_i<\mu_i\}\le1.
\tag{7}
\]
In detail, when lambda>0, those derivatives imply sum_{s_i<h}a_i mu_i<=R<=sum_{s_i<=h}a_i mu_i. Fill the tied coordinates successively, using at most one partial coordinate. When lambda=0, the right derivative gives sum_{s_i<h}a_i mu_i<=R; take all tied coordinates to be zero.

Let I_0={i:x_i=mu_i}. Rates on I_0 are at most h, and all other rates are at least h. We have Lambda=sum_i t_i x_i. If I_0 is nonempty, choose the m=min{|I_0|,ceil(gamma sqrt n)} largest values of a_i mu_i on I_0, denote their index set by I, and put U=sum_{i in I}a_i mu_i>0. If I_0 is empty put I=empty and U=0. For the nonempty case,
\[
|I|\le\gamma\sqrt n+1,\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_0\setminus I}(a_i\mu_i)^2\le U^2/\gamma^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed the sum is zero if all coordinates are selected. Otherwise each remaining value is at most U/m, and their number is at most n, with m>=gamma sqrt n.

Define s_i'=gamma s_i on I and s_i'=s_i on I_0 outside I. Under an auxiliary product law Q, let J_i have law Q_{s_i'} for i in I_0, and let J_i=0 otherwise. By (2), (3), and monotonicity,
\[
E_QJ_i\le\mu_i\ (i\in I_0),\qquad
E_QJ_i\le(100/\gamma)\mu_i\le\mu_i/4\ (i\in I),
\qquad E_QJ_i^2\le18/(s_i')^2.
\tag{9}
\]
For a nonnegative integer root j, put
\[
\sigma_a(j)^2=\sum_i a_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3,
\qquad \sigma_\theta(j)^2=\sum_i\theta_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3.
\tag{10}
\]
These are the variances of a dot V and theta dot V for independent uniform integer V_i in {-j_i,...,j_i}.

For I_0 nonempty, (7), (9) give E_Q(a dot J)<=R-3U/4. Using J_i(J_i+1)<=2J_i^2, (2), (3), and (8), both Var_Q(a dot J) and E_Q sigma_a(J)^2 are at most
\[
7200\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{(a_i\mu_i)^2}{(s_i'/s_i)^2}
\le14400U^2/\gamma^2\le U^2/64^2.
\tag{11}
\]
The selected square sum is at most U^2; the last comparison is 14400*4096<=8192^2. Chebyshev and Markov show, with probability at least 7/8, that a dot J<=E_Q(a dot J)+4 sqrt(Var_Q(a dot J)) and sigma_a(J)<=4 sqrt(E_Q sigma_a(J)^2). On that event,
\[
a\cdot J+4\sigma_a(J)
\le R-3U/4+U/16+U/4\le R.
\]
Zero variances make their conditions automatic. If I_0 is empty, J=0 and this resource condition is automatic.

Because |theta_i|<=s_i<=s_i' on I_0, (9), (10) give E_Q sigma_theta(J)^2<=12n<=18n. Markov gives sigma_theta(J)<=4 sqrt(18n) with probability at least 15/16. Consequently
\[
G=\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot j+4\sigma_a(j)\le R,
\ \sigma_\theta(j)\le4\sqrt{18n}\}
\]
has Q(G)>=13/16. By (5), the set E={j in G:w dot j even} satisfies
\[
Q(E)\ge1/16,
\qquad E\subseteq D_0.
\tag{12}
\]
The probability bound follows by subtracting Q(G complement)<=3/16 from the even-congruence probability >=1/4. This reasoning includes an empty active set and w=0.

Let T=t dot J. Complementary slackness and (2), (9) yield
\[
0\le\Lambda-E_QT\le5(1+|I|).
\tag{13}
\]
Only selected coordinates and the possible single partially filled coordinate contribute, each by at most t_i mu_i<=5. For v>=0 set ell(v)=log E_Q exp(-v(T-E_QT)). Exponential tilting changes each active rate to s_i'+v t_i/4. Thus (2), independence, and t_i<=s_i' give ell''(v)<=18n. Integrating from ell(0)=ell'(0)=0 gives ell(v)<=9nv^2. Restriction to E and conditional Jensen give
\[
\ell(v)\ge\log Q(E)+v(E_QT-E_Q[T\mid E]).
\]
Minimizing (-log Q(E))/v+9nv for v>0, and using log 16<3 and 6sqrt3<11, proves
\[
E_Q[T\mid E]\ge\Lambda-5(1+|I|)-11\sqrt n.
\tag{14}
\]
When Q(E)=1 take v down to zero. All derivatives used exist at the stated positive rates.

3. Congruence-compatible score slabs and original normalization.

Fix j in E. Take the independent uniform displacements from (10), and let L_j=product_i(2j_i+1). The probability that w dot V is even is at least 1/3. Indeed, the coordinate parity bias for V_i is (-1)^{j_i}/(2j_i+1); if some w_i=1 and j_i>=1, the absolute value of their product is at most 1/3. If no such coordinate exists, congruence is automatic. In either case (1+E(-1)^{w dot V})/2>=1/3.

Each of |a dot V|<=4sigma_a(j) and |theta dot V|<=4sigma_theta(j) fails with probability at most 1/16, with zero variance again automatic. Therefore the simultaneous conditions
\[
w\cdot V\equiv0\pmod2,\qquad
|a\cdot V|\le4\sigma_a(j),\qquad
|\theta\cdot V|\le4\sigma_\theta(j)
\tag{15}
\]
hold for at least (1/3-1/8)L_j=(5/24)L_j displacements. This set is symmetric under V->-V. Its endpoints j+V and j-V belong to D_0: the box ensures nonnegativity, the resource margin in G ensures the resource inequality, and the first condition preserves the root's congruence.

Every closed halfspace containing j contains at least one endpoint of each reflected pair. Symmetry thus puts at least half of the displacements satisfying (15) in that halfspace. Every corresponding endpoint has its ORIGINAL probability at least
\[
p_0(j)e^{-4\sigma_\theta(j)}
\ge p_0(j)e^{-16\sqrt{18n}}
\ge p_0(j)e^{-68\sqrt n}.
\]
Hence
\[
\tau_{p_0}(j)\ge(5/48)e^{-68\sqrt n}p_0(j)L_j
\ge(1/16)e^{-68\sqrt n}p_0(j)L_j.
\tag{16}
\]

For every z in D_0, t dot z<=Lambda and theta_i=t_i-s_i. Bounding the ORIGINAL even-residue sum by the entire nonnegative orthant gives
\[
Z_0\le e^\Lambda\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}.
\tag{17}
\]
Let Z_Q=product_{i in I_0}F_3(e^-4s_i'), with empty product 1. At every j in the support of Q,
\[
L_j^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}
=Z_Q Q(J=j)e^{4\sum_{i\in I_0}(s_i'+\theta_i)j_i}
\ge Z_Q Q(J=j)e^{4t\cdot j}.
\tag{18}
\]
Cube (16), multiply by p_0(j), and sum over E, discarding nonnegative terms outside the support of Q. Equations (12), (14), (17), (18) and conditional Jensen give
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p_0)
&\ge\frac{e^{-204\sqrt n}}{4096Z_0^4}
\sum_{j\in E} L_j^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-20}}{65536}
e^{-20|I|-248\sqrt n}Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The fourth-power denominator is Z_0^4, the original normalizer, throughout. E is a subset of finite D_0, so its conditional exponential expectation is finite.

An uninflated active coordinate contributes at least alpha to the final product in (19), by (4). A deterministic coordinate contributes (1-e^-s_i)^4>=(1-e^-h)^4=16/81>=alpha; squaring the last inequality reduces it to 768>=512. For an inflated coordinate, since the numerator polynomial in F_3 lies between 1 and 48 on [0,1], and 1-q^gamma<=gamma(1-q),
\[
F_3(e^{-4\gamma s_i})/F_3(e^{-4s_i})\ge1/(48\gamma^4).
\]
Using (8) in (19) proves, for every even-residue model, including w=0,
\[
M_3(p_0)\ge\frac{e^{-20}}{65536}e^{-b|I|-248\sqrt n}\alpha^n
\ge c e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\tag{20}
\]
The previously separated R=0 case also satisfies this bound since c<1 and alpha<1.

4. Odd residue as a polynomial-size mixture of even-residue charts.

Now take r=1 and enumerate the coordinates with w_i=1 as i_1,...,i_k in any fixed order, where 1<=k<=n. Partition D_1 by the first odd active coordinate: for 1<=ell<=k, the ell-th cell consists of z with z_{i_h} even for h<ell and z_{i_ell} odd. Discard empty cells. Their number m is at most n and their original p_1 masses pi_ell sum to 1.

For the ell-th cell define the invertible affine map z=e_{i_ell}+A_ell y, where A_ell is diagonal with entries 2 on i_1,...,i_ell and entries 1 elsewhere. That cell is exactly the image of
\[
\{y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:
(A_\ell a)\cdot y\le R-a_{i_\ell},\quad
\sum_{h>\ell}y_{i_h}\equiv0\pmod2\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Here diagonal symmetry identifies A_ell a with A_ell^T a. The resource coefficients remain positive; nonemptiness gives R-a_{i_ell}>=0. The conditional law of p_1 on this cell, pulled back through the affine map, has ORIGINAL weights proportional to exp((A_ell theta) dot y), with all scores nonpositive. Thus (20) applies in dimension n, even when ell=k and the remaining congruence is zero. Invertible affine maps preserve atomic halfspace depths.

Let q_ell denote the normalized conditional law on the cell. For any root j in that cell, the original containing-halfspace mass is at least pi_ell times its q_ell mass, so tau_{p_1}(j)>=pi_ell tau_{q_ell}(j). Since p_1(j)=pi_ell q_ell(j),
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p_1)&\ge\sum_\ell\pi_\ell^4M_3(q_\ell)\\
&\ge c e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n\sum_\ell\pi_\ell^4
\ge c n^{-3}e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The last inequality is Jensen's sum pi_ell^4>=m^-3>=n^-3. This remains valid when the whole odd-residue event has arbitrarily small mass under an unconditioned law; only the normalized original p_1 is used. Equation (20) implies the same bound for r=0, proving the first assertion of (1).

5. Intrinsic parity supplies a universal finite-dimension estimate.

For either residue define the full-rank index-two lattice Gamma={v in Z^n:w dot v even}. Choose an index i with w_i=1. A basis is 2e_i together with e_h-w_h e_i for h!=i: it generates precisely the integer vectors satisfying the congruence. Thus Gamma/2Gamma has 2^n elements. The support D_r lies in one affine coset of Gamma. Partition it into the 2^n intrinsic parity classes modulo 2Gamma, and denote their original masses by rho_c.

Whenever x,y in D_r lie in the same intrinsic parity class, j=(x+y)/2 is an integer point in the same coset of Gamma; nonnegativity and the weak resource inequality place j in D_r. Conversely, x,y in D_r with midpoint j in D_r have x-y=2(x-j) in 2Gamma. This is intrinsic parity, not the ordinary coordinate parity of Z^n.

For j in D_r let E_j=D_r intersect (2j-D_r), k_j=#E_j, and mathsf H_j=k_j p_r(j), including zero displacement. Log-affinity gives sqrt(p_r(x)p_r(2j-x))=p_r(j). For any closed halfspace H containing j put A=E_j intersect H. The reflected-pair argument and Cauchy-Schwarz give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf H_j
&\le2\sum_{x\in A}\sqrt{p_r(x)p_r(2j-x)}\\
&\le2\sqrt{p_r(A)p_r(2j-A)}
\le2\sqrt{p_r(H\cap D_r)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore tau_{p_r}(j)>=mathsf H_j^2/4. The exact midpoint enumeration above, with p_r(j)^2=p_r(x)p_r(y), proves
\[
E_{p_r}\mathsf H_j=\sum_c\rho_c^2\ge2^{-n}.
\]
Jensen now yields
\[
M_3(p_r)\ge\frac1{64}E_{p_r}\mathsf H_j^6
\ge\frac1{64}(E_{p_r}\mathsf H_j)^6
\ge\frac1{64}64^{-n}.
\tag{23}
\]

Finally alpha>1/8 because alpha^2=512/19683>1/64, equivalently 32768>19683. Hence delta>0. For n>=1, log n<=sqrt n (for example, the maximum of log x/sqrt x on x>=1 is 2/e<1). The first assertion of (1) consequently gives M_3(p_r)>=c exp(-(B+3)sqrt n)alpha^n. For n>=N the exponent satisfies (B+3)sqrt n<=delta n, so this is at least c8^-n. For 1<=n<N, (23) divided by 8^-n is 8^-n/64>=1/(64*8^N). The definition of kappa proves the second assertion of (1) in every dimension.
