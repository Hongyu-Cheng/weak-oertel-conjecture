## Statement

In one integer and two continuous dimensions take the actual fibers at $-1,0,1,2$ with common second-coordinate interval $[-1,1]$ and centered first-coordinate half-widths $1,3,2,1$, respectively. Take the relations $0=(-1+1)/2=(2/3)(-1)+(1/3)2$ and a common $(X,Y)$ uniform on $[-1,1]^2$, so the shared and exclusive sources are $(X,Y),(2X,Y),(X,Y)$ at $-1,1,2$ and the outputs are $(3X/2,Y),(X,Y)$. For the same gluing, the adaptive credits in caps $x+y\ge0$, $-x+y\ge0$, and $-y\ge0$ are $13/6,13/6,2$, versus the fixed-packing credits $2,2,2$. In each of these caps both relations' all-status anchored residual-root credits are zero. At the origin of the common target fiber, infima over all containing ambient caps of the adaptive and fixed-packing credits both equal $2$.

On an actual product chain $[-1,2]\times K$ with any compact full-dimensional convex fiber $K$, use the same two base relations and the diagonal coupling with one common uniform point of $K$ at every source. The gain is identically zero on every ambient cap, even though both output laws attain the uniform density $1/|K|$ of the Minkowski average fiber.

## Proof

Linear interpolation of the first-coordinate half-widths has slopes $2,-1,-1$, hence is concave. The prescribed centered rectangles are thus the actual sections of a convex ambient body. The indicated sources have the required uniform marginals, and substitution in the two barycentric relations gives the stated outputs. The four fiber volumes are $4,12,8,4$. Thus the shared-source credit uses $a=4,b=8,c=4$ and $c_1=c_2=c_{12}=\rho=4$.

For $x+y\ge0$ both target events have probability $1/2$ by central symmetry. The crossing where the first output hits and the second misses is the strip $-3X/2\le Y<-X$ in the unit-radius square with $0<X\le1$. Its area is
$$
\int_0^{2/3} X/2\,dX+\int_{2/3}^1(1-X)\,dX=1/6.
$$
Reflection gives the reverse crossing and reflection of $X$ gives the same values for $-x+y\ge0$. Each intersection is therefore $11/24$. For $-y\ge0$ the two events coincide and have probability $1/2$. Substitute into pk-w35-trims-shared-source-credit to obtain the three adaptive and fixed values. Every specified cap leaves residual volume $6$ in the middle fiber, by symmetry and nullity of each boundary line. The weighted source-volume roots for the two relations are $1+\sqrt2<\sqrt6$ and $2<\sqrt6$. Thus both positive-part root deficits vanish in 6465fe8e563bb0cb, giving the zero scalar credits.

At the origin in the target, a through-mark cap with nonzero continuous normal has both output probabilities $1/2$, since their symmetric rectangle laws are absolutely continuous. A containing cap has probabilities at least those of the parallel through-mark cap, and a containing pure-base cap has both probabilities one. The fixed packing is consequently at least $2$ for every containing cap, and so is the adaptive credit by the nonnegative gap formula. The cap $-y\ge0$ attains $2$ for both. This proves the asserted infima without converting the two strict directional gains into a uniform one.

For the product chain, convex compatibility is exact and the weighted averages of identical shared-coordinate samples are the same uniform point. Their two events coincide for every cap, so the bracket in the gap formula is identically zero. The Minkowski average of copies of $K$ is $K$ itself, giving the stated density.
