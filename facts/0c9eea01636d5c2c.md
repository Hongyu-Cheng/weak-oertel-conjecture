## Statement

Let $T=\{(x,y):x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\}$ have barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0=1-x-y$, $\lambda_1=x$, $\lambda_2=y$, and put $C=[0,2]\times T$. For $i=0,1,2$ let $H_i=\{(z,x,y):\lambda_i(x,y)-z\ge1/3\}$. The actual normalized mixed-area law $\mu$ gives each of its three fibers probability $1/3$, and $\mu(H_i)=4/27<2/9$ for every $i$. The three caps cover the zero fiber. There is no probability $\nu$ on the same mixed set that keeps its zero-fiber mass $1/3$, has zero-fiber conditional law carried by one affine line, and satisfies all three inequalities $\nu(H_i)\le4/27$. Any such line law of that fiber weight forces at least one cap probability to be at least $1/6$, a gap of $1/54$.

## Proof

The source fibers are all $T$, with area $1/2$ each; $\mathbf1_C$ is jointly log-concave. At $z=1,2$, $\lambda_i-z\le0<1/3$, so the caps strictly miss both foreign fibers. At zero, the $i$th cap is a homothet of $T$ at vertex $i$ with scale $2/3$. Its area is $(2/3)^2(1/2)=2/9$, giving mixed probability $(2/9)/(3/2)=4/27$. Barycentric summation gives coverage at zero because some coordinate is at least $1/3$. The normalized sharp comparison constant for $n=1,d=2$ is $(2/3)^2/2=2/9$, with gap $2/27$ above these masses.

Restrict $\nu$ to base zero and normalize by its mass $1/3$. The resulting probability is supported on the compact interval given by the intersection of its affine line with $T$ (possibly a singleton). Pick a median in that interval; both closed half-lines through a median have conditional mass at least $1/2$, by the defining lower and upper quantile inequalities, including atoms. The median lies in one of the three caps because they cover that entire fiber. Its trace on the supporting line is a closed half-line containing the median or the entire line. It thus takes at least $1/2$ of the conditional mass, giving mixed probability at least $1/6$. Since $1/6-4/27=1/54>0$, all three constraints cannot hold. The argument imposes no condition on the laws in the other fibers.
