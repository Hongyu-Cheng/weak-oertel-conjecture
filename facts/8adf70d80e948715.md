## Statement

For $0<r<1/6$, the auxiliary system $\epsilon(z_i-1/2)+\eta x\le r$ for $i=1,2,3$ and $\epsilon,\eta\in\{-1,1\}$ projects to $[1/2-r,1/2+r]^3$. The inclusion-maximal interior-lattice-free split $1\le z_1+z_2+z_3\le2$ strictly contains this projection. Neither split facet normal is a nonnegative combination of at most two original base normals with vanishing combined continuous normal, even though $d+1=2$.

## Proof

Maximizing over the two signs of each term gives $|z_i-1/2|+|x|\le r$. This implies the projected bounds, and $x=0$ realizes them. The sum of the three coordinates of the cube lies in $[3/2-3r,3/2+3r]\subset(1,2)$. Each original base normal has just one nonzero coordinate, so any combination of at most two has at most two nonzero coordinates. Both split facet normals have three. Larger combinations do give valid bounds: for a fixed sign $\epsilon$, take one row each at coordinates $1,2$ with opposite continuous signs and half of each continuous-sign row at coordinate $3$. The continuous coefficient cancels and the resulting upper bound on $\epsilon\sum_i(z_i-1/2)$ is $3r<1/2$.

The integer unimodular coordinate map $(z_1,z_2,z_3)\mapsto(z_1+z_2+z_3,z_2,z_3)$ sends the split to $[1,2]\times\mathbb R^2$. It has no interior lattice point. If a convex enlargement contains a point $p=(p_1,p')$ with $p_1<1$, choose $t\in(0,1)$ with $t p_1+(1-t)(3/2)=1$ and take $q=(3/2,-t p'/(1-t))$ in the split's interior. The convex combination $tp+(1-t)q$ is the lattice point $(1,0,0)$, and combining $p$ with a small ball about $q$ puts a ball about that point in the enlargement. For $p_1>2$ choose the analogous combination with first coordinate two. Hence the split is inclusion maximal.
