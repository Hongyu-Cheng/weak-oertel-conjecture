## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $v_1,\ldots,v_{d+1}\in\mathbb R^d$ span $\mathbb R^d$ and sum to zero. Put $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$, the centroid-zero simplex defined by this circuit, and $\rho_d=|T\cap(-T)|/|T|$, with ordinary $d$-volume. Write $h_D(u)=\max_{x\in D}u\cdot x$.

For a convex body $B\subseteq T$, the mean opposite support $m=\sum_i h_B(-v_i)/(d+1)$ is positive and $|B|\le |T\cap(-mT)|$. For every $m>0$ one has
$$
 |T\cap(-mT)|\le\left(\frac{1+m}{2}\right)^d|T\cap(-T)|.
$$
Equality holds in the latter inequality at $m=1$.

Let $A,B,K$ be convex bodies with $B\subseteq T$ and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Suppose $K=\{x:v_i\cdot x\ge-t_i\ \forall i\}$ and each hyperplane $v_i\cdot x=-1$ meets $\operatorname{int} A$. Then $|K|>|B|/\rho_d$. The sharp infimum of $|K|/|B|$ under these geometric hypotheses is $1/\rho_d$.

## Proof

The linear evaluation map $x\mapsto (v_i\cdot x)_i$ has image the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane. Hence $\lambda_i(x)=(1-v_i\cdot x)/(d+1)$ is an affine bijection onto the coordinate-sum-one hyperplane and maps $T$ onto the standard barycentric simplex. In particular $0$ is the centroid of $T$, so permutations of its barycentric coordinates induce linear automorphisms of $T$. They preserve volume since their determinants have absolute value one. Average all their images of $B$ by Minkowski addition with equal weights. The average lies in $T$ by convexity and has volume at least $|B|$ by ordinary Brunn–Minkowski. Its supports in the $-v_i$ directions are all $m$ by linearity of support under Minkowski addition and transitivity of the permutations. The average therefore lies in $-mT$. For any interior point $b_0$ of $B$, $h_B(-v_i)>-v_i\cdot b_0$ for all $i$. Summing gives $m>0$.

For $0<s<1$ set $L_s=sT\cap(-(1-s)T)$. The origin lies in the interior of each $L_s$. By convexity $(L_s+L_{1-s})/2\subseteq L_{1/2}$, and $L_{1-s}=-L_s$. Brunn–Minkowski yields $|L_s|\le |L_{1/2}|$. Substitute $s=1/(1+m)$ and scale both volumes to obtain the claimed intersection inequality. At $m=1$ it is an equality.

Put $r=\sum_i t_i/(d+1)$. There is a unique $p$ with $v_i\cdot p=r-t_i$ for every $i$ by the evaluation map. Thus $K=p+r(-T)$ when $r>0$. If $r<0$ the inequalities for $x-p$ contradict their sum, and if $r=0$ they force $x=p$. Full dimension gives $r>0$. All the listed inequalities are facets, so $h_K(-v_i)=t_i$ and $|K|=r^d|T|$. Midpoint containment gives $2t_i\ge h_A(-v_i)+h_B(-v_i)>1+h_B(-v_i)$, using the proper cut of $A$. Averaging gives $r>(1+m)/2$. The two volume bounds now give $|B|\le((1+m)/2)^d|T\cap(-T)|<\rho_d|K|$.

For $\varepsilon>0$ take $B=T\cap(-T)$, $A=(1+\varepsilon)(-T)$ and $K=(1+\varepsilon/2)(-T)$. The minimum of $v_i\cdot x$ on $A$ is $-(1+\varepsilon)$ and the interior point $0$ has value zero. A point in $\operatorname{int} A$ on the line segment from $0$ to that minimum has value $-1$, so all cuts are proper. Since $B\subseteq -T$, convexity gives $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. The ratio is $(1+\varepsilon/2)^d/\rho_d$, converging to the asserted infimum.
