## Statement

All logarithms are natural. In \(\mathbb Z^2\), set \(\mathbf a=(2,-1)\), \(\mathbf b=(-2,1)\), \(\mathbf c=(0,1)\), \(\mathbf u=(1,0)\), and \(\mathbf v=(-1,1)\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c,\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v\},\qquad P=\operatorname{conv}D,\\
p_{\mathbf a}=p_{\mathbf b}&=\frac1{16},\qquad
p_{\mathbf0}=\frac18,\qquad
p_{\mathbf u}=p_{\mathbf v}=p_{\mathbf c}=\frac14,
\end{aligned}
\]

and put \(p_{\mathbf I}=0\) off \(D\). This is a probability table with \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^2\) and a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with every lattice value, including all zeros, in the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The normalization to that fact is \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=1/4\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log((1/4)/p_{\mathbf I})\).

Use exactly the feasible arrays, Euclidean objective, and conditional assignment entropy of accepted fact `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\ (\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\ (\mathbf J\in D)
\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2,\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(0\log0=0\), and \(\mathcal F_{\min}\), \(r^\star\), \(\mathcal T\), \(\mathcal J\), and \(D_{\mathbf J}\) retain precisely their meanings from that fact. The entire variance-minimal face is a singleton, with minimum one. Its unique member, and therefore its entropy maximizer \(r^\star\), has exactly these nonzero entries:

\[
r^\star_{\mathbf0,\mathbf0}=\frac18,\qquad
r^\star_{\mathbf0,\mathbf a}=r^\star_{\mathbf0,\mathbf b}=\frac1{16},\qquad
r^\star_{\mathbf u,\mathbf u}=r^\star_{\mathbf v,\mathbf v}
=r^\star_{\mathbf c,\mathbf c}=\frac14.
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal T\) consists exactly of these six ordered pairs, \(\mathcal J=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}\), and \(q^\star_{\mathbf J}=1/4\) for every \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J\). The exact row supports are \(D_{\mathbf0}=\{\mathbf a,\mathbf0,\mathbf b\}\) and \(D_{\mathbf J}=\{\mathbf J\}\) for \(\mathbf J\in\{\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}\). Every other array entry is forced to vanish throughout \(\mathcal F_{\min}\).

Write \(S(q^\star)=-\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}q^\star_{\mathbf J}\log q^\star_{\mathbf J}\) for ordinary target entropy. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\log2-S(q^\star)+H(r^\star)&=0,\\
\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J}q^\star_{\mathbf J}
\left[\log p_{\mathbf J}
-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}}
\frac{r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q^\star_{\mathbf J}}\log p_{\mathbf I}\right]
&=\frac18\log2>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore no finite absolute constant, or even any finite constant for this one table, can satisfy the proposed inequality.

## Proof

First, the triangle with vertices \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\) is
\(P=\{(x,y):y\le1,\ x+y\le1,\ x+2y\ge0\}\). The inequalities imply \(-1\le y\le1\). At the three integral heights \(y=-1,0,1\), the possible integer \(x\)-coordinates are, respectively, \(2\), \(0,1\), and \(-2,-1,0\). This proves \(P\cap\mathbb Z^2=D\), including its complete boundary. The prescribed masses sum to one.

Define the finite convex function
\(F(x,y)=(\log2)\max\{0,1-x-2y,-2x-2y,-2y\}\) on \(\mathbb R^2\). Its values on \(D\), divided by \(\log2\), are two at \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\), one at \(\mathbf0\), and zero at \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\). Thus the function equal to \(\tfrac14e^{-F(x,y)}\) on \(P\) and zero off \(P\) agrees with the entire table. It is finite-valued and globally log-concave: when both endpoints lie in \(P\), use convexity of \(P\) and \(F\); when an endpoint is outside \(P\), the required right-hand side is zero. This establishes the exact extension hypothesis without assuming only coordinatewise or midpoint log-concavity.

We now characterize every feasible array, before minimizing the objective. If an affine functional is nonnegative on \(P\) and vanishes at a row target, the row moment equation makes the sum of that functional over the row equal to zero. Every positive entry in that row must therefore lie on its zero face. Applying this observation to the three displayed inequalities defining \(P\) proves that the rows at \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\) can receive only their own source. The row at \(\mathbf0\) can receive only \(\mathbf a,\mathbf0,\mathbf b\); the row at \(\mathbf u\) can receive only \(\mathbf a,\mathbf u,\mathbf c\); and the row at \(\mathbf v\) can receive only \(\mathbf b,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\). These are all rows because the complete set \(D\) has already been enumerated.

The three identities \(\mathbf0=(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)/2\), \(\mathbf u=(\mathbf a+\mathbf c)/2\), and \(\mathbf v=(\mathbf b+\mathbf c)/2\) show that the respective endpoint entries of each nonvertex row must be equal. Moreover, each of the sources \(\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) can enter only its corresponding row, so its diagonal entry equals its full prescribed mass. It follows that every feasible array is described uniquely by three parameters \(t_0,t_1,t_2\), as follows:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathbf0,\mathbf0}&=\frac18,&
r_{\mathbf0,\mathbf a}=r_{\mathbf0,\mathbf b}&=t_0,\\
r_{\mathbf u,\mathbf u}&=\frac14,&
r_{\mathbf u,\mathbf a}=r_{\mathbf u,\mathbf c}&=t_1,\\
r_{\mathbf v,\mathbf v}&=\frac14,&
r_{\mathbf v,\mathbf b}=r_{\mathbf v,\mathbf c}&=t_2,\\
r_{\mathbf a,\mathbf a}&=\frac1{16}-t_0-t_1,&
r_{\mathbf b,\mathbf b}&=\frac1{16}-t_0-t_2,\\
r_{\mathbf c,\mathbf c}&=\frac14-t_1-t_2,&
0\le t_0\le\frac1{16},\quad
0\le t_1,t_2&\le\frac1{16}-t_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

All unlisted entries vanish. The stated inequalities also give \(t_1+t_2\le1/8\), so the entry at \((\mathbf c,\mathbf c)\) is positive. Conversely, every parameter triple in the displayed range gives a nonnegative array with the required column sums and row moments. This proves an exact parametrization of the full feasible set, not merely of a trial collection of allocations.

The actual Euclidean squared norms are \(\|\mathbf a\|^2=\|\mathbf b\|^2=5\), \(\|\mathbf c\|^2=\|\mathbf u\|^2=1\), \(\|\mathbf v\|^2=2\), and \(\|\mathbf0\|^2=0\). Substituting the row masses of the parametrized array gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(r)&=\frac{13}{8}-10t_0-4t_1-2t_2,\\
10t_0+4t_1+2t_2
&\le10t_0+6\left(\frac1{16}-t_0\right)
=\frac38+4t_0\le\frac58.
\end{aligned}
\]

Equality in the final inequality forces \(t_0=1/16\), after which feasibility forces \(t_1=t_2=0\). This triple is feasible and gives equality throughout. Hence \(\min_{\mathcal F}V=1\) and \(\mathcal F_{\min}\) consists of exactly the array in the statement. In particular, the entropy optimizer and every forced zero have been proved on the entire face. The computation uses the displayed Euclidean norms directly; no invariance of the objective under a unimodular change of coordinates is used.

The four positive row masses are all \(1/4\). Also, every nonzero entry of \(r^\star\) equals the full mass of its source column. Thus each positive assignment share \(r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I}\) equals one, proving \(H(r^\star)=0\). The share potentials of `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc` are consequently zero on each exact positive row support. Ordinary target entropy is \(S(q^\star)=\log4=2\log2\). For clarity, the distinct ordinary source entropy is \(S(p)=19\log2/8\); it is not the entropy used in the proposed allowance.

The three singleton rows have zero original gap. The normalized row at \(\mathbf0\) assigns probabilities \(1/2,1/4,1/4\) to \(\mathbf0,\mathbf a,\mathbf b\), respectively. Its original logarithmic gap is

\[
\log\frac18-
\left(\frac12\log\frac18+
\frac14\log\frac1{16}+\frac14\log\frac1{16}\right)
=\frac12\log2.
\]

Multiplying by its row mass \(1/4\) proves the positive averaged gap \((\log2)/8\). The allowance is zero by the separately computed target and conditional entropies, so the proposed inequality fails for every finite coefficient.

Finally, \(\mathbf c=\mathbf u+\mathbf v\) and the integer matrix with columns \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v\) has determinant one. Therefore \(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal J)\) is an empty unimodular parallelogram. The four targets have residues \((0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)\) modulo two. This verifies explicitly that the counterexample satisfies the extremal target-cardinality conclusion of `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc`, while retaining all four parity classes.
