## Statement

Let $\mu$ be actual mixed volume on a compact convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $n,d\ge1$, and fix a positive-volume base $j$. For feasible $t>0$ let $M_t(j)$ be the continuous means of dominated measures of mass $t$ with base mean $j$. Suppose $u\ne0$ and the actual cap $H=\{L\ge0\}$, $L=u\cdot x+a\cdot(z-j)-b$, has mass $t$ and centered base moment zero. Assume in a neighborhood of $(u,a,b)$ that every section of a positive-volume fiber is proper, strictly separated, or a supporting section of zero $(d-1)$-measure, and that the coarea moments through order two below are continuous. Use counting measure when $d=1$.

Put $q(z)=(z-j,1)$ and $d\nu=\|u\|^{-1}\sum_z d\mathcal H^{d-1}|_{C_z\cap\{L=0\}}$, with the sum over positive-volume fibers and sections regarded as lifted to their respective bases. Define $G=\int qq^{\mathsf T}\,d\nu$, $B=\int qx^{\mathsf T}\,d\nu$, and $E=\int xx^{\mathsf T}\,d\nu$. If $G\succ0$, then $h=h_{M_t(j)}$ is $C^2$ locally at $u$, with
$$
D^2h(u)=\frac{1}{t}(E-B^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}B),
\qquad
t v^{\mathsf T}D^2h(u)v=\min_c\int(v\cdot x+c\cdot q)^2\,d\nu\quad(v\in\mathbb R^d).
$$
In particular the Hessian is positive semidefinite and annihilates $u$.

## Proof

Set $\theta=(a,-b)$, so $L=u\cdot x+\theta\cdot q$. In a proper fiber, the weighted cap first variation in the proof of 6d4fda353a2353ab applies after translating the section and scaling its normal. Its local normal-column argument is for a continuous bounded weight, and the parameter perturbation in this fiber changes the offset by $\delta\theta\cdot q$. Undoing the volume normalization gives, for the whole finite sum, the derivative of the weighted cap integral of $F(z,x)$ as
$$
\int F(z,x)(\delta u\cdot x+\delta\theta\cdot q)\,d\nu .
$$
For a strictly separated fiber the trace is locally constant; for a supporting zero-measure section, the changing part has volume $o(\|(\delta u,\delta\theta)\|)$ by the slab argument of 1269c554ca9a41b8 §2, and multiplication by any bounded $F$ preserves that estimate. This gives the derivative for $F=q,x$, and the assumed continuity of their derivative matrices gives $C^1$.

The constraint is $\int_H q\,d\mu=(0,t)$, whose derivative is $B\,\delta u+G\,\delta\theta$. By the implicit function theorem there is a $C^1$ choice of $\theta$ locally, with $D\theta=-G^{-1}B$. For each nearby direction its cap remains a support maximizer: integrate $L(\mathbf1_H-f)\ge0$ for any feasible $f$, and cancel $\theta\cdot q$ by the common constraints. Thus the support value is $t^{-1}\int_H u\cdot x\,d\mu$. Its derivative in $u$ is the cap first moment divided by $t$: the extra boundary term equals the negative of the boundary term with weight $\theta\cdot q$, by $L=0$ on the section, and the latter vanishes by the differentiated constraints. Differentiating that first moment gives $t^{-1}(E+B^{\mathsf T}D\theta)$ as claimed. Expanding the square in $c$ minimizes it at $-G^{-1}Bv$. For $v=u$ choose $c=\theta$ to get zero, hence $D^2h(u)u=0$ by positive semidefiniteness.
