## Statement

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty compact convex, for example a nonempty closed-halfspace depth region. Let $D_z$ be its section at base $z$, and let $V$ be the continuous direction space $\operatorname{dir}(\operatorname{aff}D)\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d)$, of dimension $r$. For $z_0,z_1$ in the projection and $0\le\lambda\le1$, one has
$$
D_{(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1}\supseteq (1-\lambda)D_{z_0}+\lambda D_{z_1}.
$$
The root $|D_z|^{1/d}$ is concave on the projection if $d\ge1$. For $r\ge1$ the same is true of $\mathcal H^r(D_z)^{1/r}$, and for $r=0$ each nonempty section is a singleton. Nonempty zero-volume sections are allowed as endpoints.

For any nonempty compact convex $C$ in the same product, the graph of $E_z=C_z+(-D_z)$ is compact convex, with a nonempty section exactly at each common base of $C,D$. For $d\ge1$, $|E_z|^{1/d}$ is concave on that common domain, and there $|E_z|^{1/d}\ge |C_z|^{1/d}+|D_z|^{1/d}$. The section is empty if either operand is empty.

## Proof

Convexity of $D$ gives the asserted inclusion. Apply Brunn–Minkowski in the continuous space $\mathbb R^d$, including nonempty measure-zero operands. Each nonempty section also lies in an affine coset of the common direction space $V$. Choose an affine right inverse of the base projection from $\operatorname{aff}D$ onto its affine base image. Subtracting this affine choice identifies the sections by translation with subsets of $V$, preserves the convex inclusion and their intrinsic measures, and lets Brunn–Minkowski in dimension $r$ prove the intrinsic claim. If $r=0$, the cosets are singletons. The domain of each section assertion is the projection; a zero extension is not used in the convex inclusion.

The set of $(z,x,y)$ with $(z,x)\in C$ and $(z,y)\in D$ is compact convex. Its linear image under $(z,x,y)\mapsto(z,x-y)$ has the asserted sections, with the same base required in the two operands. Apply the first section argument to this graph on its nonempty base domain. At a fixed such base, Brunn–Minkowski for $C_z$ and $-D_z$, whose volume equals that of $D_z$, gives the stated lower bound. This construction compares the two operands at the same base.
