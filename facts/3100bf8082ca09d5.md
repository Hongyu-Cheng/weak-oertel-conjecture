## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66`. In particular, \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
and the canonical hard inequality holds. In particular,
\[
 D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]
The accepted maximum transverse pairing has positive labels
\[
 \sigma=(z;e,f),
 \qquad
 e=\{z,a\},
 \qquad
 f=\{z,b\},
 \qquad
 \theta_\sigma>0,
\tag{3}
\]
and total mass
\[
 P:=\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma>
 \frac{99}{200}Q.
\tag{4}
\]
For every loaded incidence \((e,y)\), let \(f_{e,y}\) be the fixed half-mass representing density of the endpoint contact from that accepted fact. Thus, for every label (3), its two opposite-endpoint densities are
\[
 g_{\sigma,a}:=
 \frac{2\theta_\sigma}{a_a}f_{e,a},
 \qquad
 g_{\sigma,b}:=
 \frac{2\theta_\sigma}{a_b}f_{f,b}.
\tag{5}
\]
They have mass \(\theta_\sigma\) and barycenters \(x_{e,a}\), respectively \(x_{f,b}\). For every subfamily of atlas labels and every physical source fiber, their sum is pointwise at most \(1/2\).

Let \(E_\perp\) be the set of physical bases that occur as an opposite endpoint of at least one positive atlas label. For \(y\in E_\perp\), define
\[
 g_y:=
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma:\ y\text{ is an opposite}\\
                         \text{endpoint of }\sigma}}
 g_{\sigma,y},
 \qquad
 \tau_y:=\int_{C_y}g_y,
 \qquad
 p_y:=\frac1{\tau_y}\int_{C_y}xg_y(x)\,dx.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 0<\tau_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4},
 \qquad
 0\leq g_y\leq\frac12,
 \qquad
 p_y\in\mathsf M_{\tau_y}(C_y).
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover, putting
\[
 T_E:=\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_y,
\tag{8}
\]
one has the exact lossless identity and hard-branch lower bound
\[
 \boxed{T_E=2P>\frac{99}{100}Q.}
\tag{9}
\]

The single positive endpoint measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_E:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_\perp}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}}
\tag{10}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), has mass
\[
 |\nu_E|=\gamma_dT_E,
\tag{11}
\]
and is genuinely halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\):
\[
 \boxed{\nu_E(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{12}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Every endpoint atom satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_y<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n}.}
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, the number of distinct physical endpoint bases satisfies
\[
 \boxed{|E_\perp|>99\gamma_d2^n.}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, some parity class contains at least \(37\) distinct elements of \(E_\perp\).

Define
\[
 A_E:=\operatorname{aff}E_\perp,
 \qquad
 \rho_E:=\dim A_E,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 r_E:=
 \min_{c:A_E\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{p_y-c(y):y\in E_\perp\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_dT_E
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_E}}{r_E+1},}
\tag{17}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 r_E+1>
 99\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_E},}
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{(y,p_y):y\in E_\perp\}
 =\rho_E+r_E\geq n+6.}
\tag{19}
\]

We next state the unconditional endpoint-frame completion conclusion. Let
\[
 y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_n\in E_\perp
\tag{20}
\]
be affinely independent and lie in one parity class. Reorder them so that
\[
 M:=\tau_{y_0}=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\tau_{y_i}.
\tag{21}
\]
Let
\[
 I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,n\},
 \qquad
 I\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 J\ne\varnothing.
\tag{22}
\]
Put \(r:=|I|\) and \(s:=|J|\), so \(r+s=n\). Then there is one integral full-rank two-factor lattice-polysimplex packet \(\Pi\), constructed from actual points in pairwise distinct integral target fibers, such that
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq\mathcal E(\Pi)
 \geq
 \gamma_d
 \frac{
 \left(M+\sum_{i\in I}\tau_{y_i}\right)
 \left(M+\sum_{j\in J}\tau_{y_j}\right)}{M}.}
\tag{23}
\]

For such a basis and partition, define its endpoint-frame score by
\[
 \mathfrak f(y_\bullet;I,J):=
 \frac{
 \left(\tau_{y_0}+\sum_{i\in I}\tau_{y_i}\right)
 \left(\tau_{y_0}+\sum_{j\in J}\tau_{y_j}\right)}
 {\tau_{y_0}}.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak F_E\) be the maximum of (24) over all same-parity affine bases in \(E_\perp\), ordered as in (21), and all nontrivial partitions (22). If no such basis exists, set \(\mathfrak F_E:=0\). Then
\[
 \boxed{D\geq\gamma_d\mathfrak F_E.}
\tag{25}
\]
No conclusion above assumes or proves an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality.

## Proof

We first pool the atlas densities. Take the full atlas subfamily in the simultaneous source-feasibility conclusion of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66`. It gives the second inequality in (7). Each summand in (6) has mass \(\theta_\sigma\), so
\[
 \tau_y=
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma:\ y\text{ is an opposite}\\
                         \text{endpoint of }\sigma}}
 \theta_\sigma.
\tag{26}
\]
For a fixed reservoir edge \(e\ni y\), only pairings of \(e\) at its other endpoint use \(y\) as an opposite source. Pairing feasibility in that accepted fact bounds the total weight of those labels by \(\omega_e\). Therefore
\[
 \tau_y\leq\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_e
 \leq\sum_{e\ni y}r_e.
\tag{26a}
\]
If \(y\) is in a good class \(\xi\), the exact reservoir formula of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66` gives
\[
 \sum_{e\ni y}r_e
 =\frac{a_y}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_y}{A_\xi}\right)
 \leq\frac{a_y}{4};
\tag{26b}
\]
outside the good classes the degree is zero. This proves the first inequality in (7). The density \(g_y\) is therefore admissible for the canonical metronoid of mass \(\tau_y\), and its normalized first moment is \(p_y\) by definition. This proves the membership in (7).

Every label \(\sigma=(z;e,f)\), with \(e=\{z,a\}\) and \(f=\{z,b\}\), has exactly two opposite endpoints \(a\) and \(b\). They are distinct because \(e\) and \(f\) are distinct edges. Thus every \(\theta_\sigma\) occurs exactly twice in the sum of (26). Hence
\[
 T_E=2\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma=2P.
\tag{27}
\]
Equation (4) proves (9). This pooling is over physical bases, so coincident labels at one physical endpoint cause no loss or multiplicity in the support of \(\nu_E\).

By (7) and the metronoid-depth theorem in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`,
\[
 \delta_C(y,p_y)\geq\gamma_d\tau_y
 \qquad(y\in E_\perp).
\tag{28}
\]
We prove (12) for the one sum measure. Let \(H\) be a closed ambient halfspace. For every \(y\) such that \((y,p_y)\in H\), the fiber section
\[
 H_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in H\}
\tag{29}
\]
contains \(p_y\). If \(H_y\) is a proper closed halfspace, (28) gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \geq\gamma_d\tau_y.
\tag{30}
\]
If \(H_y=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from \(a_y\geq\tau_y\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\). An empty section cannot contain \(p_y\). The bases in \(E_\perp\) are pairwise distinct, so summing (30) over their distinct fibers gives
\[
 \nu_E(H)
 =\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in E_\perp:\ (y,p_y)\in H}}
 \tau_y
 \leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{31}
\]
This proves (10)--(12).

Fix \(y\in E_\perp\). Equation (28) says that the mixed-integer point \((y,p_y)\) has ambient halfspace depth at least \(\gamma_d\tau_y\): every ambient halfspace containing it contributes at least the amount in (30) from its own fiber. Therefore
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\tau_y,
 \qquad
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)\geq2^n\gamma_d\tau_y.
\tag{32}
\]
Combining (32) with the strict hard bound (2) proves (13). Since every \(\tau_y\) is strictly below the right side of (13), equations (9) and (13) give
\[
 \frac{99}{100}Q<T_E
 <|E_\perp|\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n},
\tag{33}
\]
which is (14). There are \(2^n\) parity classes. Also
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e,
\tag{34}
\]
for example by \(\log(1+t)<t\) at \(t=1/d\). Hence \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\). Also
\[
 e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac1{5^m}
 =\frac{87}{32}<\frac{11}{4},
\tag{34a}
\]
so \(99/e>36\). The average number of endpoint bases per parity class is therefore strictly greater than \(36\), proving the stated integer lower bound of \(37\) in one parity class.

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to the nonzero measure \(\nu_E\). Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (15)--(16), because there is one atom at each physical base. That theorem gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{|\nu_E|}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}.
\tag{35}
\]
Multiplying (35) by \(2^n\) and using (11) proves (17). Equations (2), (9), and (17) give
\[
 \frac Q{100}>D
 >\frac{99\gamma_dQ}{100}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_E}}{r_E+1},
\tag{36}
\]
which rearranges to (18). The intrinsic-rank identity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{(y,p_y):y\in E_\perp\}
 =\rho_E+r_E.
\tag{37}
\]
For every integer \(q\geq0\), \(q+1\leq2^q\). Moreover,
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 =\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk\frac1{d^k}
 \leq\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3,
\tag{38}
\]
so \(\gamma_d>1/3\). Using (18),
\[
 2^{\rho_E+r_E}
 \geq2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)
 >99\gamma_d2^n
 >33\,2^n
 >2^{n+5}.
\tag{39}
\]
The exponent on the left is an integer. Thus \(\rho_E+r_E\geq n+6\), proving (19).

It remains to prove the endpoint-frame completion theorem. Fix (20)--(22), and put
\[
 I_0:=\{0\}\cup I,
 \qquad
 J_0:=\{0\}\cup J.
\tag{40}
\]
For \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\), define
\[
 h_i:=\frac{y_i-y_0}{2},
 \qquad
 k_j:=\frac{y_j-y_0}{2}.
\tag{41}
\]
The same-parity hypothesis makes all vectors in (41) integral. Affine independence of (20) makes their union linearly independent. Set
\[
 V_1:=\operatorname{span}\{h_i:i\in I\},
 \qquad
 V_2:=\operatorname{span}\{k_j:j\in J\}.
\tag{42}
\]
Then \(\dim V_1=r\), \(\dim V_2=s\), and
\[
 V_1\oplus V_2=\mathbb R^n.
\tag{43}
\]

Choose matrices
\[
 U_1\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r},
 \qquad
 U_2\in\mathbb Z^{n\times s}
\tag{44}
\]
whose columns are lattice bases of \(V_1\cap\mathbb Z^n\), respectively \(V_2\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Since every vector in (41) lies in the corresponding lattice, there are integral intrinsic coordinates
\[
 u_i\in\mathbb Z^r,
 \qquad
 v_j\in\mathbb Z^s
\tag{45}
\]
such that
\[
 h_i=U_1u_i,
 \qquad
 k_j=U_2v_j.
\tag{46}
\]
Put \(u_0:=0\in\mathbb Z^r\) and \(v_0:=0\in\mathbb Z^s\). Since the matrices in (44) are injective on their coordinate spaces and the vectors in (41) are linearly independent, the \(u_i\), \(i\in I\), are linearly independent in \(\mathbb R^r\), and the \(v_j\), \(j\in J\), are linearly independent in \(\mathbb R^s\). Define the resulting full-dimensional lattice simplices and their lattice domains by
\[
 \Delta_1:=
 \operatorname{conv}\{u_i:i\in I_0\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_1:=\Delta_1\cap\mathbb Z^r,
\tag{47}
\]
\[
 \Delta_2:=
 \operatorname{conv}\{v_j:j\in J_0\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^s,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_2:=\Delta_2\cap\mathbb Z^s.
\tag{48}
\]
Let \(\alpha_i\), \(i\in I_0\), be the barycentric-coordinate functions of \(\Delta_1\), and let \(\beta_j\), \(j\in J_0\), be those of \(\Delta_2\).

Define the integral affine map
\[
 \Phi(u,v):=y_0+U_1u+U_2v.
\tag{49}
\]
Equation (43) implies that the concatenated matrix \([U_1\ U_2]\) has rank \(r+s=n\), so \(\Phi\) is injective and has full base rank. Equations (41), (46), and (49) give, for every \(i\in I_0\) and \(j\in J_0\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi(u_i,v_j)=\frac{y_i+y_j}{2}.}
\tag{50}
\]
Here the symbol \(y_0\) is used when either index equals zero. The coordinate pairs \((u_i,v_j)\) are distinct, so injectivity of \(\Phi\) shows that all \((r+1)(s+1)\) target bases in (50) are pairwise distinct.

For \(i\in I_0\) and \(j\in J_0\), put
\[
 t_{ij}:=\min\{\tau_{y_i},\tau_{y_j}\},
 \qquad
 Z_{ij}:=\frac{p_{y_i}+p_{y_j}}2.
\tag{51}
\]
The membership in (7) is represented by the density \(g_{y_i}\) of mass \(\tau_{y_i}\). Multiplying that density by \(t_{ij}/\tau_{y_i}\) gives an admissible density of mass \(t_{ij}\) with the same barycenter \(p_{y_i}\), and similarly at \(y_j\). Therefore
\[
 (y_i,p_{y_i},t_{ij}^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (y_j,p_{y_j},t_{ij}^{1/d})
\tag{52}
\]
belong to the positive-root metronoid generating set in accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. That fact proves this generating set itself convex for positive roots. Taking the midpoint in (52), with the common positive root unchanged, and using (50), gives
\[
 \boxed{
 Z_{ij}\in
 \mathsf M_{t_{ij}}
 \left(C_{\Phi(u_i,v_j)}\right).}
\tag{53}
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` now gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_C\left(\Phi(u_i,v_j),Z_{ij}\right)
 \geq\gamma_dt_{ij}.}
\tag{54}
\]
Thus every product cell required below is an actual point in its displayed integral target fiber. These target fibers are pairwise distinct by (50). The construction does not sum the scaled source densities used in (52); it uses them only to establish the separate geometric memberships (53).

Define positive factor weights by
\[
 \pi_i:=\frac{\tau_{y_i}}{\sqrt M}
 \quad(i\in I_0),
 \qquad
 \varrho_j:=\frac{\tau_{y_j}}{\sqrt M}
 \quad(j\in J_0).
\tag{55}
\]
By maximality of \(M\), every selected endpoint weight is at most \(M\). Hence
\[
 \pi_i\varrho_j
 =\frac{\tau_{y_i}\tau_{y_j}}M
 \leq\min\{\tau_{y_i},\tau_{y_j}\}
 =t_{ij}.
\tag{56}
\]
This is a positive rank-one minorant of the full array of actual cell masses.

Define the two scalar factor sums
\[
 \mathcal B_1:=
 \sum_{u\in\mathcal D_1}
 \left(
  \sum_{i\in I_0}\alpha_i(u)\pi_i^{1/d}
 \right)^d,
\tag{57}
\]
\[
 \mathcal B_2:=
 \sum_{v\in\mathcal D_2}
 \left(
  \sum_{j\in J_0}\beta_j(v)\varrho_j^{1/d}
 \right)^d.
\tag{58}
\]
Use the factor roots
\[
 a_{1,i}:=\gamma_d^{1/d}\pi_i^{1/d},
 \qquad
 a_{2,j}:=\varrho_j^{1/d}.
\tag{59}
\]
Equations (54) and (56) give, at every product vertex,
\[
 \delta_C\left(\Phi(u_i,v_j),Z_{ij}\right)^{1/d}
 \geq a_{1,i}a_{2,j}.
\tag{60}
\]
All hypotheses of the generic factorized lattice-polysimplex theorem in accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5` now hold: the two simplices (47)--(48) are full-dimensional lattice simplices, the chart (49) is integral and has total rank \(n\), and the actual points (53) satisfy the product-root bound (60). Apply that accepted fact directly to these newly constructed cells. It produces one full-rank two-factor construction, which we denote by \(\Pi\), and gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq2^{-n}\gamma_d\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2.
\tag{61}
\]
Define its factor energy by
\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi):=\gamma_d\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2.
\tag{62}
\]
Since \(D=2^nh_{\max}(C)\), equation (61) gives
\[
 D\geq\mathcal E(\Pi).
\tag{63}
\]

Each lattice domain contains every vertex of its simplex. At a vertex, the corresponding barycentric coordinate is one and all others are zero. Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal B_1
 \geq\sum_{i\in I_0}\pi_i
 =\frac{M+\sum_{i\in I}\tau_{y_i}}{\sqrt M},
\tag{64}
\]
\[
 \mathcal B_2
 \geq\sum_{j\in J_0}\varrho_j
 =\frac{M+\sum_{j\in J}\tau_{y_j}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{65}
\]
Equations (62)--(65) prove (23). Because (23) holds for every frame and partition used in the definition of \(\mathfrak F_E\), it gives \(D\geq\gamma_d\mathfrak F_E\) when at least one frame exists. If none exists, \(\mathfrak F_E=0\) and (25) follows from \(D\geq0\). This proves every assertion.
