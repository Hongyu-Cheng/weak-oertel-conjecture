## Statement

Let $n,d\ge2$, $B$ be the closed unit Euclidean $d$-ball of volume $V$, $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ and $r=\varepsilon^{1/d}$. For the convex body
$$
C=\operatorname{conv}\big(([0,1]^n\times B)\cup(\{2e_1\}\times rB)\big),
$$
use summed $d$-volume on its integer fibers and let $D$ be the raw ambient depth region at level $V$ for closed halfspaces. The mixed mass is $(2^n+\varepsilon)V$, every fiber has mass strictly less than $(2^n+\varepsilon)V/2^n$, and the mixed maximum is $(1/2+\varepsilon)V$, attained uniquely at $(e_1,0)$. The region $D$ is full-dimensional and avoids the mixed lattice. Its projection has the facet $F=\{p\ge0:\sum_i p_i=1/2\}$ and $F\times\{0\}\subset D$. For every $p\in\operatorname{relint}F$, every nonvertical mass-$V$ cap containing $(p,0)$ captures an entire unit ball and zero volume in every other fiber. All its fiber boundary sections are empty or supporting singletons, with total $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measure zero.

For any positive minimal spanning circuit of $d+1$ unit vectors $u_j\in\mathbb R^d$, the mass-$V$ caps $u_j\cdot x-2\sum_i z_i\ge-1$ support $D$ along $F\times\{0\}$, and the intersection of these caps projects onto $\sum_i z_i\le1/2$. The projection of $D$ lies in the maximal lattice-free crosspolytope $\sum_i|z_i-1/2|\le n/2$, with a facet in $\sum_i z_i=0$.

## Proof

The only integer bases are the binary vertices and $2e_1$: all other coordinates are in $[0,1]$, and at first coordinate $2$ the projection consists of $2e_1$ alone. The fibers are the prescribed unit balls and $rB$. They lie in $B$ by convexity, and the last fiber is exact by exposedness. Their total mass and the strict individual mass bounds follow.

Every base other than $e_1$ is an exposed vertex of the base hull. Indeed, fixing a binary tuple in the last $n-1$ coordinates exposes that layer; the all-zero layer is the segment $[0,2e_1]$ and every other layer is a unit segment. At an exposed base, an ordinary own-fiber cap is isolated by adding a sufficiently large positive multiple of a base functional exposing that base. Every containing ambient cap already pays at least ordinary own depth. Thus mixed depth there is ordinary ball depth, at most $V/2$. At $(e_1,0)$ the continuous margin at the three bases $0,e_1,2e_1$ of a through-cap is $u\cdot x+a\cdot(z-e_1)$. For $u\ne0$ the own contribution is $V/2$. Each point of $rB$ not captured at $2e_1$ reflects to a point captured at base $0$, giving combined endpoint mass at least $\varepsilon V$. For $u=0$ a closed through-cap contains the middle ball and at least one of the two endpoints. At any mark of the middle ball choose an ordinary minimizing own cap, add a large positive coefficient on $z_1-1$ to take all of $rB$ and none of the base-$0$ ball, then subtract a sufficiently large multiple of $\sum_{j\ge2}z_j$ to exclude the other binary fibers. This realizes own depth plus $\varepsilon V$, with unique maximum at the center, since off-center ball depth is strictly less than $V/2$.

The region $D$ is compact. A cap of mass below $V$ can be enlarged slightly in parallel, preserving mass below $V$ by continuity from above and putting its contained point in its interior. Thus the complement of $D$ is open. A point outside the convex hull of the mixed support has a containing cap of mass zero by strict separation.

Each $(e_i/2,0)$ has depth at least $V$ by central symmetry of the pair of unit fibers at $0,e_i$. Depth superlevels are convex, since any halfspace containing a convex combination contains at least one participating point. Thus $F\times\{0\}\subset D$. For $s=\sum_i p_i<1/2$, the cap through $(p,0)$ with unit $u$ and threshold $u\cdot x\ge(\sum_i z_i-s)/(1-s)$ takes strictly less than $V$ at base $0$ and zero elsewhere. Reflection in $x=0$ preserves the measure, so a point of $D$ above $p$ would also put $(p,0)$ in $D$. Therefore $\sum p_i\ge1/2$ on the projection. Pure base cuts beyond the faces of $[0,1]^n$ capture at most the small fiber, so the projection lies in the cube and intersects this supporting plane exactly in $F$.

For ambient interior, use the $N=n+1$ unit fibers over $0,e_1,\ldots,e_n$ and their base barycenter $g$. Let $T(t)=|B\cap\{u\cdot x\ge t\}|/V$ for unit $u$, and let $q:[0,1]\to[-1,1]$ be its continuous decreasing inverse on $[-1,1]$. Then $q(1-w)=-q(w)$. For nonnegative $w_i$ summing to $1$, the sum of the $q(w_i)$ is positive. If each $w_i\le1/2$, all summands are nonnegative and at least one is positive as $N\ge3$. If one $w_i>1/2$, any other $w_j\le1-w_i$ gives $q(w_j)\ge-q(w_i)$ and all remaining terms are positive. Compactness gives a positive minimum $c_N$. A nonvertical through-cap at $(g,x)$ has these $N$ thresholds summing to $N u\cdot x$. If their tail fractions summed to less than one, no threshold could be at most $-1$. Clip thresholds above $1$ and increase their tail fractions to sum to one. Since $q$ decreases, this proves $N u\cdot x\ge c_N$. A pure base cap through $g$ takes at least one entire unit fiber. Thus $D$ contains the continuous ball $\|x\|<c_N/N$ over $g$. Since $\sum_i g_i=n/(n+1)>1/2$, its convex hull with $F\times\{0\}$ has full ambient dimension. Lattice avoidance follows from the mixed maximum, which is strictly below $V$.

For $p\in\operatorname{relint}F$, the weights of $p$ on $0,e_1,\ldots,e_n$ are $\lambda_0=1/2$ and $\lambda_i=p_i>0$. A through-cap at $(p,0)$ with unit continuous normal has thresholds with weighted average zero. If two thresholds are negative, their tail fractions alone exceed one. If there are no negative thresholds all are zero, again exceeding one. With one negative threshold $-L$, and smallest nonnegative threshold $t$, the balance and $\lambda_{\rm neg}\le1/2$ give $L\ge t$. The negative tail is $1-T(L)$. If $t<1$ and $L>t$, then $T(t)>T(L)$. If $t<1$ and $L=t$, all other thresholds equal $t$, and there are at least two positive copies of $T(t)=T(L)$. In either case the total exceeds one. Hence a mass-$V$ through-cap must have $t\ge1$, $L\ge1$. It captures one whole unit fiber and zero from every other fiber, including those outside the chosen simplex. Tightening any mass-$V$ nonvertical containing cap to its boundary through $(p,0)$ preserves the mass, since this point belongs to $D$; the fiberwise inclusions and unchanged total preserve that same allocation. On a positive-radius ball a whole or zero-volume cap cuts only an empty boundary section or a supporting singleton, which is $\mathcal H^{d-1}$-null for $d\ge2$.

The displayed circuit caps take the whole ball at $0$ and no other mass. Their boundary contains $F\times\{0\}$, and raising each threshold by any sufficiently small positive amount removes positive mass from the base-$0$ ball and adds none elsewhere. Thus each open cap interior is disjoint from $D$. A positive combination canceling the continuous normals gives $\sum z_i\le1/2$ on their intersection, and $x=0$ gives a lift for every base satisfying that inequality.

The cube lies in the stated crosspolytope. Every integer point has $\ell_1$ distance at least $n/2$ from the center, and every facet contains its corresponding binary vertex in its relative interior. A point beyond a facet makes that vertex interior to the enlarged hull: move a small distance from the vertex on the ray away from the added point into the old interior, and take a ball around the resulting point. Convex combinations of this ball with the added point contain a neighborhood of the vertex. This proves maximal lattice-freeness and the parallel facet claim.
