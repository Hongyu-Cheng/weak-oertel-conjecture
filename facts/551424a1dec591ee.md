## Statement

Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For each i let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, closed under integral midpoints, with at most two atoms in every coordinatewise parity class. Choose arbitrary positive original weights \(w_{i,x}\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&
p_{i,x}&=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},&
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,\\
N&=\sum_i n_i,&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,&
\mathcal K&=\prod_iK_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Assume the exact midpoint equalities \(p_{i,u}p_{i,v}=p_{i,(u+v)/2}^{\,2}\) whenever u,v are distinct same-parity atoms of \(A_i\). Extend all laws by zero outside their supports.

Let \(\mathcal J_i\) be the set of all nontrivial integral midpoint hubs in \(A_i\), and let \(R_{i,x}=A_i\cap(2x-A_i)\) for every \(x\in A_i\). For \(j\in\mathcal J_i\), let \(m_{i,j}\ge1\) be the number of unordered nontrivial pairs centered at j, so that \(|R_{i,j}|=2m_{i,j}+1\). Define an undirected graph on all of \(\mathcal J_i\) by placing an edge between distinct hubs j,k whenever \(j\in R_{i,k}\setminus\lbrace k\rbrace\) or \(k\in R_{i,j}\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace\). Thus hub j is joined to hub k if either is an endpoint of a pair centered at the other. Let \(\mathfrak C_i\) be the set of connected components, and for \(C\in\mathfrak C_i\) put
\(B_{i,C}=\bigcup_{j\in C}R_{i,j}\) and \(k_{i,C}=|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in B_{i,C}\rbrace|\).

These component unions are disjoint in both atoms and occupied parity classes. Their union is precisely the atoms that are hubs or endpoints of nontrivial integral-midpoint pairs. Each remaining atom is the sole atom of its parity class in the entire \(A_i\). Moreover, \(B_{i,C}\) is closed under integral midpoints, its hub set is exactly C, and its full reflected row at every \(j\in C\) is exactly \(R_{i,j}\). At every other atom of \(B_{i,C}\), the full reflected row is a singleton.

For each component define its raw conditional normalizer, original mass, and exact conditional law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{i,C}&=\sum_{x\in B_{i,C}}w_{i,x},&
\beta_{i,C}&=\frac{Z_{i,C}}{Z_i},&
\pi_{i,C}(x)&=\frac{p_{i,x}}{\beta_{i,C}}
             =\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_{i,C}}\quad(x\in B_{i,C}),\\
S_{i,C,j}&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace}\pi_{i,C}(x),&
\mu_{i,C,j}&=\frac{S_{i,C,j}/\pi_{i,C}(j)+2}{2(m_{i,j}+1)},\\
M_{i,C}&=\min_{j\in C}\mu_{i,C,j},&
U_{i,C}&=\max_{j\in C}\mu_{i,C,j},\\
A_{0,i,C}&=k_{i,C}^{\,2}\sum_{x\in B_{i,C}}\pi_{i,C}(x)^3
 =\frac{k_{i,C}^{\,2}}{\beta_{i,C}^{\,3}}
   \sum_{x\in B_{i,C}}p_{i,x}^{\,3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
The symbols \(M_{i,C},U_{i,C}\) are the minimum and maximum means of accepted fact 358b4c6d14982ea1; \(m_{i,j}\) is a pair count and \(k_{i,C}\) is an occupied parity count. In particular \(M_{i,C}\) is not the core capacity called M in accepted fact 60336390757d4ce6.

Require \(U_{i,C}\le2M_{i,C}\) only when \(A_{0,i,C}<1\). There is no restriction on ratios of means belonging to different components, and no comparison is required in an inactive conditional component. When \(\mathcal J_i\) is empty there are no component hypotheses.

Use the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and moments of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
  \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace,\\
M_2(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^2,&
M_3(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Set \(C_0=2^{60}\) and \(c=\tfrac18\exp(-72C_0^2)>0\). Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge c\mathcal K^{-2}\ge c4^{-N},\\
M_3(P)&\ge c\mathcal K^{-3}\ge c8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
All original atoms, masses, and normalizers are retained. Arbitrary hub counts, arbitrary outside singleton masses, zero-hub factors, inactive components, deterministic factors, lower-dimensional supports, and zero score variances are included. No support or component union is assumed to be the complete lattice census of a convex set.

## Proof

First fix one factor and omit its index. Every two-atom parity class is a unique unordered pair, whose midpoint is a supported hub by integral-midpoint closure. Conversely, the endpoints of every nontrivial reflected pair have the same parity and exhaust their two-atom class. Therefore each atom can be an endpoint of at most one such pair. In particular the endpoint sets \(R_j\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace\), for distinct hubs j, are disjoint, although full rows may meet at hubs.

If \(x\in R_j\cap R_k\) with distinct hubs j,k, x cannot be in both endpoint sets. Thus x is either j or k, and this intersection exhibits an edge between j and k. Consequently full rows from different graph components are disjoint, proving atomic disjointness of the \(B_C\). Also, if a hub h belongs to \(B_C\), then \(h\in R_j\) for some \(j\in C\). Either h=j, or h is a hub endpoint in that row and is adjacent to j. In both cases \(h\in C\). Thus \(\mathcal J\cap B_C=C\).

We need the stronger fact that a component union contains every parity class it touches. Suppose \(x\in B_C\) has a distinct same-parity partner \(y\in A\), and put \(k=(x+y)/2\in\mathcal J\). Choose \(j\in C\) with \(x\in R_j\). If \(x\ne j\), the unique parity partner of x is \(2j-x\), so y=2j-x and k=j. If x=j, this hub is an endpoint of the pair centered at k; here k differs from j because x and y are distinct. The graph then joins j to k. In either case \(k\in C\) and \(x,y\in R_k\subseteq B_C\). This proves parity-class saturation. Together with atomic disjointness, saturation proves that distinct component unions have disjoint occupied parity classes.

Every hub and every nontrivial-pair endpoint is in some full hub row. Hence the component unions partition exactly those atoms. An atom outside their union cannot have a parity partner: that partner's midpoint would be a hub whose row contained the atom. All remaining atoms therefore have distinct singleton classes, disjoint from every component class.

For \(j\in C\), the inclusions \(R_j\subseteq B_C\subseteq A\) give
\(B_C\cap(2j-B_C)=R_j\). A point of \(B_C\setminus C\) is not a hub of A and so has a singleton full reflected row already in A. Thus all full rows in \(B_C\) are exactly the stated inherited rows, and its hub set is exactly C, because each inherited hub retains at least one pair. If distinct same-parity atoms lie in \(B_C\), the preceding saturation argument places their midpoint in C. This proves integral-midpoint closure of \(B_C\), without a convex-completeness assertion.

Let \(s=|A\setminus\bigcup_C B_C|\). The disjoint parity partition gives the exact capacity identity
\(K=\sum_C k_C+s\). More explicitly,
\(k_C=\sum_{j\in C}m_j+|\lbrace j\in C:j\text{ is alone in its parity class in }A\rbrace|\): every pair class of \(B_C\) is assigned to its unique midpoint in C, while any singleton-class atom of \(B_C\) must itself be a hub, since it cannot occur as an endpoint. This also verifies that all capacities count actual occupied classes, including when several hubs have the same parity. If there are no hubs, the component collection is empty and every atom is a singleton class, so K=s=|A|.

Return to the factor index i. Partition \(A_i\) into its component unions and its individual remaining atoms, and denote this partition by \(\mathcal D_i\). Give \(D=B_{i,C}\) capacity \(k_{i,D}=k_{i,C}\), and give each outside singleton capacity one. The geometry just proved yields
\(\sum_{D\in\mathcal D_i}k_{i,D}=K_i\le2^{n_i}\).
For every piece define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{i,D}&=\sum_{x\in D}w_{i,x},&
\beta_{i,D}&=\sum_{x\in D}p_{i,x}=\frac{Z_{i,D}}{Z_i},&
\pi_{i,D}(x)&=\frac{p_{i,x}}{\beta_{i,D}}
              =\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_{i,D}}\quad(x\in D).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
These are positive masses and exact conditional laws. In a component piece, every distinct same-parity pair u,v and its midpoint j remain in that piece, and conditioning preserves equality exactly:
\(\pi_{i,D}(u)\pi_{i,D}(v)=p_{i,u}p_{i,v}/\beta_{i,D}^{\,2}=p_{i,j}^{\,2}/\beta_{i,D}^{\,2}=\pi_{i,D}(j)^2\).
Also the ratio \(S_{i,C,j}/\pi_{i,C}(j)\) is exactly \(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace}p_{i,x}/p_{i,j}\). Thus conditioning leaves each mean unchanged, while the activity test is precisely the conditional cubic normalization in (2).

We implement the conditional-leaf and exact-capacity transfer mechanism of accepted facts 60336390757d4ce6 and f5955204a537cb64. Their affine-law theorems are not applied to the present arbitrary weights; the necessary transfer and the common mixed-leaf estimate are proved directly below, using the arbitrary-law scalar theorem 358b4c6d14982ea1.

Fix a leaf \(\lambda=(D_1,\ldots,D_L)\in\prod_i\mathcal D_i\). Its support, original mass, capacity, and conditional law are
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_\lambda&=\prod_iD_i,&
\alpha_\lambda&=\prod_i\beta_{i,D_i},&
k_\lambda&=\prod_i k_{i,D_i},\\
P_\lambda(X)&=\prod_i\pi_{i,D_i}(x_i)
 =\frac{\prod_iw_{i,x_i}}{\prod_iZ_{i,D_i}}
 =\frac{P_X}{\alpha_\lambda}\quad(X\in A_\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
For this leaf abbreviate \(\pi_i=\pi_{i,D_i}\) and \(k_i=k_{i,D_i}\); these aliases do not redefine the original \(p_i,P,Z_i,K_i\). Define full reflected quantities \(H_{\pi_i},b(\pi_i),\kappa_{i,j},Y_{i,j},V_{i,j},q_{i,j}\) exactly as in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, now with support \(D_i\), law \(\pi_i\), and full row \(R^{\lambda}_{i,j}=D_i\cap(2j-D_i)\). In particular
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\pi_i}(j)&=\sum_{x\in R^{\lambda}_{i,j}}\sqrt{\pi_i(x)\pi_i(2j-x)},&
b(\pi_i)&=\sum_{j\in D_i}\pi_i(j)H_{\pi_i}(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi_i(x)\pi_i(2j-x)}}{H_{\pi_i}(j)},&
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{\pi_i(x)}{\pi_i(2j-x)},\\
V_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,&
q_{i,j}&=\frac{\pi_i(j)H_{\pi_i}(j)^2}{b(\pi_i)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
All row sums are oriented: nonzero pair orientations occur twice and the central atom once.

Put \(A_{0,i}^{\lambda}=k_i^2\sum_{x\in D_i}\pi_i(x)^3\). A block is active if this is less than one. Every singleton block is inactive, with \(k_i=A_{0,i}^{\lambda}=1\). In every active component block \(D_i=B_{i,C}\), all hypotheses of the scalar theorem 358b4c6d14982ea1 hold: the support is finite, midpoint-closed, of parity multiplicity at most two, and exactly the union of its full rows at its entire hub set C; its law is positive and satisfies every midpoint equality. The exact normalization map to that predecessor is
\((A,p,Z,K,\mathcal J,m_j,M,U)=(B_{i,C},\pi_{i,C},Z_{i,C},k_{i,C},C,m_{i,j},M_{i,C},U_{i,C})\), with raw weights \(w_{i,x}\) restricted to \(B_{i,C}\). In particular its K is this conditional occupied count, and its M is a minimum mean, not a capacity.

The normalized quantities for such a block are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_i^{\lambda}&=4k_i^2\sum_{j\in C}m_{i,j}(m_{i,j}+1)\pi_i(j)^3,&
A_{{\rm full},i}^{\lambda}&=k_i^2b(\pi_i)=A_{0,i}^{\lambda}+\Delta_i^{\lambda},\\
d_i&=-\log A_{0,i}^{\lambda},&
g_i&=\log A_{{\rm full},i}^{\lambda},&
t_i&=\log(2U_{i,C}),&
\nu_i&=\sum_{j\in D_i}q_{i,j}V_{i,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The equality for \(A_{{\rm full},i}^{\lambda}\) follows because full hub rows have \(H_{\pi_i}(j)=(2m_{i,j}+1)\pi_i(j)\), while nonhub rows have \(H_{\pi_i}(j)=\pi_i(j)\). Each \(\mu_{i,C,j}\ge1\) by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality on its pairs. The assumed conditional comparison and 358b4c6d14982ea1 therefore give, with the same \(C_0=2^{60}\),
\(g_i>0\), \(t_i\ge\log2\), \(d_i\le C_0e^{-t_i}g_i\), and \(\nu_i\le C_0t_i^2g_i\).

Perform one binning over all active blocks of this leaf, including all its component types together. For integers \(r\ge0\), put \(I_r=\lbrace i:A_{0,i}^{\lambda}<1,\ r\le t_i<r+1\rbrace\) and \(G_r=\sum_{i\in I_r}g_i\). Retain a bin if \(G_r\ge4C_0(r+1)^2\), and choose the full reflected rows at every supported center in every block of a retained bin. In every other block, including every inactive block, choose singleton auxiliary rows at all supported centers. Only finitely many bins are nonempty.

For a retained bin, let \(T_r=\sum_{i\in I_r}\nu_i\). The scalar price budget gives \(T_r\le C_0(r+1)^2G_r\le G_r^2/4\), hence \(G_r\ge2\sqrt{T_r}\). For a discarded bin, its total lost logarithmic energy is bounded by
\(\sum_{i\in I_r}d_i\le C_0e^{-r}G_r<4C_0^2e^{-r}(r+1)^2\) when the bin is nonempty. Because \(e^{-1}<1/2\) and \(\sum_{r\ge0}(r+1)^2s^r=(1+s)/(1-s)^3\), the total lost energy is at most \(D_0=48C_0^2\).

Write \(\widehat R_{i,j}\) for the chosen symmetric row. Define \(\widehat H_i,\widehat b_i,\widehat\kappa_{i,j},\widehat V_{i,j},\widehat q_{i,j},\widehat\nu_i\) by (7) with the full row replaced by \(\widehat R_{i,j}\), retaining the same score \(Y_{i,j}\), and with \(\widehat\nu_i=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j}\). Thus full selection has \(\log(k_i^2\widehat b_i)=g_i\) and \(\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i\); singleton selection has \(k_i^2\widehat b_i=A_{0,i}^{\lambda}\) and zero price. In particular every inactive block contributes nonnegative logarithmic energy. The raw selected normalization is explicitly
$$
\widehat b_i=\frac1{Z_{i,D_i}^{\,3}}\sum_{j\in D_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{9}
$$
The bin bounds consequently yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\log(k_i^2\widehat b_i)
&\ge\sum_{r\text{ retained}}G_r-D_0\\
&\ge2\sum_{r\text{ retained}}\sqrt{T_r}-D_0
 \ge2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}-D_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
The last inequality follows from \((\sum_r\sqrt{T_r})^2\ge\sum_rT_r\); all statements include empty active or retained collections.

We now verify one source inequality for the entire conditional leaf, following the reflection mechanism of 445a9e668ee1a705. For \(J=(j_i)\in A_\lambda\), set \(\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}\), \(\widehat H_\lambda(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i)\), \(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}\), \(Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i)\), and \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}\). Each chosen row is reflection-invariant; its auxiliary law is invariant under reflection and its score changes sign. Independence and the definition of the score give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J&=0,&
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2&=\sigma_J^2,\\
P_\lambda(X)&=\widehat H_\lambda(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\in\widehat R_J).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The last probability is exactly the conditional law in (6), with denominator \(\prod_iZ_{i,D_i}\).

Every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\) containing J contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflected pair \(X,2J-X\), and contains J itself. Summing over these reflection orbits and taking the infimum over the same ambient halfspaces gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{P_\lambda}(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_\lambda(X),P_\lambda(2J-X)\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_\lambda(J)}2
  \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
 \ge\frac{\widehat H_\lambda(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral pair twice before its factor one half, and uses only half the central mass. Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz prove the final inequality. No coordinate or ambient halfspace is removed when a leaf block is deterministic, and zero variances satisfy the same argument.

Under \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), one has exactly
\(P_\lambda(J)\widehat H_\lambda(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b_i)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}\) and \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\). Squaring (12), averaging against the whole conditional leaf law, and using Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz once more, followed by (10), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P_\lambda)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
    \mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
    \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right)
 \ge\frac{a_0}{k_\lambda^2},
\qquad a_0=\frac14e^{-48C_0^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
Thus a single coefficient is paid for the whole mixed leaf. This includes entirely deterministic leaves and does not multiply coefficients over components or factors.

Finally the leaves partition the original product support, with \(\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda=1\), and their capacities satisfy the exact identity
\(\sum_\lambda k_\lambda=\prod_i\sum_{D\in\mathcal D_i}k_{i,D}=\prod_iK_i=\mathcal K\).
For \(J\in A_\lambda\) and every ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing J, \(P(\mathcal H)\ge\alpha_\lambda P_\lambda(\mathcal H)\). Taking infima gives \(\tau_P(J)\ge\alpha_\lambda\tau_{P_\lambda}(J)\). The original-law transfer, with the exact masses and capacities above, is therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda^3M_2(P_\lambda)
 \ge a_0\sum_\lambda\frac{\alpha_\lambda^3}{k_\lambda^2}
 \ge a_0\mathcal K^{-2},\\
1=\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda
&=\sum_\lambda\frac{\alpha_\lambda}{k_\lambda^{2/3}}k_\lambda^{2/3}
 \le\left(\sum_\lambda\frac{\alpha_\lambda^3}{k_\lambda^2}\right)^{1/3}
       \left(\sum_\lambda k_\lambda\right)^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
The second line is Holder's inequality and proves the last inequality in the first line. This averaging is under the full original P with its denominator \(\prod_iZ_i\). Jensen under that same law gives \(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\ge a_0^{3/2}\mathcal K^{-3}\). Since \(a_0^{3/2}=c\le a_0\) and \(\mathcal K\le2^N\), both bounds in (4) follow with the one stated common c.
