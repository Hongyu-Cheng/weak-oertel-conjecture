## Statement

Let \(n\ge100\) be an integer, let \(k\) be rational with \(1\le k\le n/100\), and put \(R=n/2+k\), \(m=n-1\), and \(d=3n-2\). Write \((a)_+=\max\{a,0\}\). Define the bidirected-star simplex by

\[
\begin{aligned}
C=\biggl\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):\;&
\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-\ge\mathbf0,\quad g_i=t_i^+-t_i^-\quad(1\le i\le m),\\
&y_i\ge|z_i-1/2-g_i|\quad(1\le i\le m),\\
&y_n\ge|z_n-1/2+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^m g_i|,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n y_i+\sum_{i=1}^m(t_i^++t_i^-)\le R\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(C_{\mathbf z}\) is the fiber in the original ordered continuous coordinates \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\). For an integer leaf vector \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z^m\), write \(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}:=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}w_{(\mathbf b,a)}\). This sum retains every integer hub coordinate. The symbol \(\mathsf V_{\mathbf b}\) used in the proof denotes the corresponding real hub section volume, which is distinguished from the actual integer hub sum \(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\).

The body \(C\) is a full-dimensional rational simplex with \(0<W<\infty\). For every positive integer \(\ell\), the admissible parameters \(n=100\cdot3^{601}\ell\) and \(k=n/100\) satisfy

\[
\frac{2^{n-1}\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}}
\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}}{W}
\le1204\cdot150^{-\ell}.
\]

Consequently there is no absolute \(c>0\) such that every member of this family has a Boolean leaf vector \(\mathbf b\) with \(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\ge cW/2^{n-1}\). Every hub integer is included in \(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\), and every integer leaf vector is included in \(W\). This assertion concerns the Boolean-line mass comparison only. It asserts no actual maximum-depth upper bound and is not a counterexample to the family's actual weak-depth claim or to the locked conjecture.

## Proof

The nonnegative simplex in coordinates \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\), with their total sum at most \(R\), maps bijectively onto \(C\) by
\(y_i=u_i+v_i\), \(z_i=1/2+u_i-v_i+g_i\) for \(i\le m\), and \(z_n=1/2+u_n-v_n-\sum_i g_i\), leaving both \(\mathbf t\) blocks unchanged. Its inverse obtains \(u_i,v_i\) by adding and subtracting the corresponding residual from \(y_i\) and dividing by two. Thus \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional rational simplex. In particular its base projection is bounded and its mixed volume is finite. Every Boolean fiber has positive volume: take \(t_i^+=t_i^-=\delta\), \(y_i=1/2+\delta\), and \(0<d\delta<k\). All its defining inequalities are then strict. Consequently \(W>0\).

For completeness, the exact projection cost and complete support can be determined without an approximation. At any real base set \(x_i=z_i-1/2\) for \(i\le m\), \(r=z_n-1/2\), \(P=\sum_i(x_i)_+\), and \(N=\sum_i(-x_i)_+\). Put
\(\Phi_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf g)=\sum_{i=1}^m(|g_i|+|x_i-g_i|)\). The minimum budget required by this base is

\[
\min_{\mathbf g\in\mathbb R^m}
\left(\Phi_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf g)+\left|r+\sum_i g_i\right|\right)
=\max\{P+N,P+r,N-r\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, let \(I_i\) be the interval with endpoints zero and \(x_i\). Clamping \(g_i\) to \(I_i\) decreases \(|g_i|+|x_i-g_i|\) by twice the clamping distance. The hub absolute value increases by at most the sum of those distances. Hence a minimum is attained with every \(g_i\in I_i\). On this box the leaf cost is \(P+N\), and the attainable sums \(\sum_i g_i\) form exactly \([-N,P]\). Minimizing the hub absolute value gives
\(P+N+\max\{0,r-N,-r-P\}\), which is (1). Thus the complete nonempty integer support is the set where the right side of (1) is at most \(R\); the positive-volume support is the strict inequality. At strict inequality, continuity permits positive slack in all the original fiber inequalities. At equality, the volume formula below is zero. No nonempty boundary fiber is removed from \(S\).

We next derive that actual fiber formula, including the freedom common to the two flow directions. Write \(s_i=\min\{t_i^+,t_i^-\}\). Apart from null boundaries the transformation
\(t_i^+=(g_i)_++s_i\), \(t_i^-=(-g_i)_++s_i\) has absolute Jacobian one, with \(\mathbf g\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(\mathbf s\ge\mathbf0\). Subtract the residual absolute values from the \(n\) coordinates of \(\mathbf y\). These \(n\) nonnegative slacks and the \(m\) coordinates \(2s_i\) occupy a simplex, and the latter scaling contributes \(2^{-m}\). With \(p=n+m=2n-1\), this gives the full original fiber volume

\[
w_{(\mathbf b,a)}
=\frac{2^{-m}}{p!}\int_{\mathbb R^m}
\left(R-\Phi_{\mathbf b-(1/2)\mathbf1_m}(\mathbf g)
-\left|a-\frac12+\sum_i g_i\right|\right)_+^p\,d\mathbf g.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular no flow variables have been fixed or deleted. Define
\(\Theta_p(h,u)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}(h-|a-u|)_+^p\) for \(h\ge0\), and define it to be zero for \(h<0\). Summing (2) over every hub integer yields

\[
\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}
=\frac{2^{-m}}{p!}\int_{\mathbb R^m}
\Theta_p\left(R-\Phi_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf g),
\frac12-\sum_i g_i\right)\,d\mathbf g,
\qquad \mathbf x=\mathbf b-\frac12\mathbf1_m.
\tag{3}
\]

All sums and integrals here have nonnegative integrands, so these interchanges are valid. Summing (3) over all \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z^m\) gives the exact complete \(W\).

For comparison only, integrate the hub over the real line and call the result \(\mathsf V_{\mathbf b}\). This is the ordinary \((d+1)\)-volume of the section with the leaf coordinates fixed; it is not substituted for \(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\). Direct integration of (2), using \(\int_{\mathbb R}(h-|r|)_+^p\,dr=2h^{p+1}/(p+1)\), gives

\[
\mathsf V_{\mathbf b}
=\frac{2^{1-m}}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathbb R^m}
\bigl(R-\Phi_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf g)\bigr)_+^{p+1}\,d\mathbf g.
\tag{4}
\]

This quantity depends only on \((|x_i|)_{i=1}^m\), because changing the sign of \(x_i\) and then that of \(\mathbf g\) in the same coordinates preserves the integral. All Boolean leaf bases therefore have the same positive value, denoted \(V_{\mathrm B}\). Their real hub sections are full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\): with the leaf coordinates fixed, choose \(z_n=1/2\), both flow blocks equal to a sufficiently small positive \(\delta\), \(y_i=1/2+\delta\) for \(i\le m\), and \(y_n=\delta\). The budget is \(m/2+d\delta<R\), and every section inequality is strict. The sections therefore satisfy the full-dimensionality requirement of the proposed line method. Their mass relative to the full \(W\) requires a separate comparison.

From now on set \(k=n/100\), and put \(h_0=R-m/2=n/100+1/2\). We prove the uniform upper bound
\(\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\le301V_{\mathrm B}\) for every Boolean \(\mathbf b\). For \(h\ge0\) and the unimodal function \(f(t)=(h-|t-u|)_+^p\), comparison of the lattice sum with the integral separately on the two sides of its maximum gives

\[
\Theta_p(h,u)
\le\int_{\mathbb R}(h-|t-u|)_+^p\,dt+2h^p
=\frac{2h^{p+1}}{p+1}+2h^p.
\tag{5}
\]

For an explicit justification, on either monotone side all lattice terms except the one nearest the maximum are bounded by the integral over their adjacent unit intervals toward the maximum. Those intervals are disjoint and avoid the other monotone side. Each of the two remaining terms is at most \(h^p\). This also covers a maximum at an integer and the case \(h=0\).

For \(x\ne0\), write \(a=|x|\). The pushforward of Lebesgue measure on \(g\in\mathbb R\) under \(|g|+|x-g|-a\) is exactly \(a\delta_0+\mathbf1_{(0,\infty)}(e)\,de\): the flat interval has length \(a\), and each of the two tails has slope two. Consequently, for the Boolean case \(a=1/2\), the integrals
\(I_s(h)=\int_{\mathbb R^m}(h-\sum_i(|g_i|+|x_i-g_i|-1/2))_+^s\,d\mathbf g\), for positive integer \(s\) and \(h\ge0\), satisfy

\[
I_s(h)
=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj2^{-(m-j)}\frac{s!}{(s+j)!}h^{s+j}.
\tag{6}
\]

Each term follows by choosing the \(j\) nonflat coordinates and integrating over their nonnegative excesses. Termwise comparison of (6) for \(s=p\) and \(s=p+1\) gives
\(I_p(h)/I_{p+1}(h)\le(p+m+1)/((p+1)h)\) for \(h>0\). Combining (3)--(6) therefore gives

\[
\frac{\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}}{V_{\mathrm B}}
\le1+(p+1)\frac{I_p(h_0)}{I_{p+1}(h_0)}
\le1+\frac{3n-1}{h_0}
\le301.
\tag{7}
\]

The estimate is uniform in the Boolean sign pattern. In particular it does not infer integer-hub sign symmetry from (4).

An exact periodization calculation supplies the lower estimate needed for the non-Boolean lines. For an integer leaf vector whose first three coordinates are Boolean, restrict \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) to their respective flat intervals \(I_i\), each of length \(1/2\). Let \(A_{\mathbf b}\) be the part of the real hub volume (4) on this restriction. For three independent uniform variables on \([0,1/2]\), the density of their sum is
\(4[t_+^2-3(t-1/2)_+^2+3(t-1)_+^2-(t-3/2)_+^2]\), obtained by integrating the triangular two-variable density. Its periodization on \([0,1]\) is

\[
q_3(t)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac12+8(t-1/4)^2,&0\le t\le1/2,\\
\dfrac32-8(t-3/4)^2,&1/2\le t\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular \(q_3(t)\ge1/2\) everywhere. Changing any of the three flat intervals from \([0,1/2]\) to \([-1/2,0]\) merely translates this periodized density. When the other \(g_i\) are fixed, the remaining budget is independent of these three flat coordinates. Also
\(\int_0^1\Theta_p(h,u-t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb R}(h-|r|)_+^p\,dr\), by summing all the integer terms. Integrating (3) over the three flat coordinates and applying (8), then integrating the other coordinates, proves

\[
\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}\ge\frac12 A_{\mathbf b}.
\tag{9}
\]

This is a comparison with the original complete integer hub sum; it retains every hub coordinate and every phase. Only a nonnegative part of the leaf-flow integral was used for the lower bound.

Write \(A_{\mathrm B}\) for the common value of \(A_{\mathbf b}\) at Boolean leaf bases. We have \(A_{\mathrm B}\ge V_{\mathrm B}/2\). To verify this quantitatively, first fix the remaining \(m-3\) flow coordinates and put \(h=R-\sum_{i=4}^m(|g_i|+|x_i-g_i|)-3/2\). Thus \(0\le h\le h_0\) on the nonzero domain. With \(s=p+1=2n\), integrating the first three flow coordinates in (4) fully gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^3\binom3j2^{-(3-j)}\frac{s!}{(s+j)!}h^{s+j}
\le\frac18 h^s\left(1+\frac{2h_0}{s+1}\right)^3.
\tag{10}
\]

The restricted integral is precisely \(h^s/8\). For \(n\ge100\), one has \(h_0\le3n/200\), and hence \(2h_0/(s+1)<3/200<1/50\). Thus the factor in parentheses cubed is less than \((51/50)^3<2\). Integrating (10) over the remaining flow coordinates proves the claimed comparison.

Now choose an integer \(j\) with \(0\le j\le\min\{m-3,h_0/2\}\). Consider any leaf magnitude pattern with the first three magnitudes equal to \(1/2\), exactly \(j\) of the remaining magnitudes equal to \(3/2\), and all the others equal to \(1/2\). Its excess minimum leaf cost over the Boolean case is exactly \(j\). The energy pushforward used in (6) shows that increasing a flat length from \(1/2\) to \(3/2\) only increases the coefficients in the formula for its restricted real hub volume. If \(A_{\mathrm B}(h)\) denotes the Boolean restricted volume with excess budget \(h\), this proves
\(A_{\mathbf b}\ge A_{\mathrm B}(h_0-j)\).
The expression for \(A_{\mathrm B}(h)\) is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients and with powers from \(p+1=2n\) through \(p+1+m-3=3n-4\), again by the same excess integrations. It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathbf b}
&\ge(1-j/h_0)^{3n-4}A_{\mathrm B}(h_0)\\
&\ge e^{-600j}A_{\mathrm B}
\ge\frac12e^{-600j}V_{\mathrm B},\\
\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}
&\ge\frac14e^{-600j}V_{\mathrm B}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(\log(1-u)\ge-2u\) for \(0\le u\le1/2\), obtained by integrating \(1/(1-u)\le2\), and \((3n-4)/h_0\le300\). The last line uses the exact integer periodization bound (9). The sign choices do not affect (11): each magnitude \(1/2\) corresponds to the two integer coordinates zero and one, while magnitude \(3/2\) corresponds to minus one and two.

There are consequently \(2^m\binom{n-4}{j}\) distinct leaf bases covered by (11). Their complete integer hub lines are disjoint. All other lines make nonnegative contributions to the original \(W\), so

\[
W\ge\frac14\,2^m\binom{n-4}{j}e^{-600j}V_{\mathrm B}.
\tag{12}
\]

This lower bound does not redefine \(W\) or discard any non-Boolean or boundary fiber from the body. Equations (1)--(3) continue to describe its full support and exact normalization.

For the promised explicit sequence, take any positive integer \(\ell\), let \(n=100\cdot3^{601}\ell\) and \(k=3^{601}\ell\), and use \(j=\ell\) in (12). This choice satisfies all the stated hypotheses, and \(\ell\le h_0/2\) and \(\ell\le n-4\). The elementary bound \(\binom Nj\ge(N/j)^j\), valid for \(1\le j\le N\), follows by writing its product factors as \((N-j+i)/i\ge N/j\). Since \(n-4\ge n/2\),

\[
\binom{n-4}{\ell}e^{-600\ell}
\ge\left(50\cdot3^{601}e^{-600}\right)^\ell
\ge150^\ell.
\tag{13}
\]

The last inequality uses \(e<3\), which follows by bounding the exponential series after its first two terms by a geometric series. Finally, (7), (12), and (13) give

\[
\frac{2^{n-1}\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}}
\mathsf L_{\mathbf b}}{W}
\le1204\cdot150^{-\ell}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus even the best complete hub line with Boolean leaves need not carry an absolute multiple of \(W/2^{n-1}\). This proves the stated counterexample to the line-mass comparison. Equations (7)--(14) do not bound the actual depth at any point and therefore do not disprove the family's actual weak-depth claim or the locked conjecture.
