## Statement

Let \(m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m,h,L\) be positive real numbers, let \(\theta_i\le0\) for \(1\le i\le m\), and let \(\lambda\ge0\). The auxiliary dimension \(m\), light coordinates \(\mathbf x\), height coordinate \(y\), and resource parameters below are not identified with or substituted for the locked problem's \(n,d,C,S\), or \(h_S\). Empty sums and the unique vector in \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^0\) have their usual meanings. Define
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
\sum_{i=1}^m a_i x_i+hy\le L\right\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{(\mathbf x,y)\in D}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^m\theta_i x_i+\lambda y\right),
\]
and give each point its full original probability
\(p(\mathbf x,y)=Z^{-1}\exp(\sum_i\theta_i x_i+\lambda y)\).
For every real \(R\), define the light partition function
\[
Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i a_i x_i\le R}}
\exp\left(\sum_i\theta_i x_i\right),
\]
where the sum is zero when \(R<0\). Put \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\). There are integers \(0\le y_0\le y_1\le K\) such that, with \(I=\{y_0,\ldots,y_1\}\) and \(R=L-hy_1\),
\[
\eta:=\frac{|I|Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda y_0}}{Z}
\ge\frac{e^{-6}}{100(m+1)^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, let \(u_I\) be uniform on the complete consecutive interval \(I\), and let \(Q_R\) be the actual decreasing light law at the single common budget \(R\):
\[
Q_R(\mathbf x)=\frac{\exp(\sum_i\theta_i x_i)}{Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)}
\quad\text{for }\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m,\ \sum_i a_i x_i\le R.
\]
Extend \(Q_R\otimes u_I\) by zero outside its product support. Then
\(p\ge\eta(Q_R\otimes u_I)\) pointwise on \(D\). All probabilities and the denominator \(Z\) are the original ones. No integer, rational, commensurability, or strict-boundary assumption on the resource coefficients is required. This includes \(K=0\), score ties, zero light scores, and \(\lambda=0\).

## Proof

Since every resource coefficient is positive, \(D\) is finite and contains the origin, so \(0<Z<\infty\). The feasible heights are exactly the integers \(0,\ldots,K\): at each such height the light vector \(\mathbf0\) is feasible, whereas every integer height larger than \(K\) has negative remaining budget and an empty light support. Thus a nonempty band inside this range has a well-defined positive common-budget light partition function, even when its common budget is zero.

Set \(\ell=L-hK\in[0,h)\), reverse height by \(t=K-y\), and put
\[
b_t=Z_{\mathrm{light}}(\ell+ht)\quad(0\le t\le K),\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K b_t e^{-\lambda t}.
\]
Then \(Z=e^{\lambda K}\mathcal Z\). The numbers \(b_t\) are positive and nondecreasing because their supports are nested and their fixed atom weights are positive. For a consecutive interval \(J=\{u,\ldots,v\}\subseteq\{0,\ldots,K\}\), define
\[
E(J)=\frac{(v-u+1)b_u e^{-\lambda v}}{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{2}
\]
The height interval \(I=\{K-v,\ldots,K-u\}\) has \(y_0=K-v\), \(y_1=K-u\), and common budget \(R=\ell+hu\). Therefore its coefficient \(\eta\) in (1) equals \(E(J)\) exactly. It remains to find such a \(J\) satisfying (1).

We first establish the resource-growth estimate. For integers \(1\le u\le v\le K\), let \(q=\lceil v/u\rceil\). Map every light vector feasible at budget \(\ell+hv\) to the coordinatewise integer quotient \(\mathbf z=(\lfloor x_i/q\rfloor)_{i=1}^m\). The exact weak resource inequalities give
\[
\sum_i a_i z_i\le\frac{\sum_i a_i x_i}{q}
\le\frac{\ell+hv}{q}\le\ell+hu.
\]
The last inequality uses \(v\le qu\) and \(\ell\ge0\). Each \(\mathbf z\) has at most \(q^m\) preimages, since \(qz_i\le x_i\le qz_i+q-1\) for every coordinate. Also \(x_i\ge z_i\) and \(\theta_i\le0\) imply \(\exp(\sum_i\theta_i x_i)\le\exp(\sum_i\theta_i z_i)\). Summing over these preimages proves
\[
b_v\le\lceil v/u\rceil^m b_u.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(m=0\) this argument is the identity \(b_v=b_u=1\). In particular, for every integer \(t\ge2\) in the height range, (3) gives \(b_t\le2^m b_{\lceil t/2\rceil}\). The estimate accommodates all staircase jumps and makes no log-concavity assertion.

We will also use an elementary finite interval partition. For any \(1\le u\le v\le K\), put \(B=\log(b_v/b_u)\) and \(W=\lambda(v-u)\). Assign to every integer \(t\in\{u,\ldots,v\}\) the pair of nonnegative integers
\[
\left(\left\lfloor\log(b_t/b_u)\right\rfloor,
\left\lfloor\lambda(t-u)\right\rfloor\right).
\]
Both entries are nondecreasing in \(t\), so each distinct pair occupies one complete consecutive interval. These intervals partition \(\{u,\ldots,v\}\). The pair starts at \((0,0)\), and at each change the sum of its entries increases by at least one. Consequently the number \(N\) of intervals satisfies
\(N\le1+\lfloor B\rfloor+\lfloor W\rfloor\le1+B+W\).
If one interval is \(J=\{r,\ldots,s\}\), its constant pair implies \(b_s/b_r<e\) and \(\lambda(s-r)<1\). Thus, writing \(w_t=b_t e^{-\lambda t}\),
\[
\sum_{t=r}^s w_t\le e^2(s-r+1)b_r e^{-\lambda s}.
\tag{4}
\]
Suppose the interval being partitioned has \(w\)-mass at least \(\alpha\mathcal Z\). One of its at most \(N\) pieces has mass at least \(\alpha\mathcal Z/N\), and (2)-(4) give \(E(J)\ge e^{-2}\alpha/N\) for that piece. The partition is valid for \(\lambda=0\), when its second entry is always zero.

If \(K=0\), take \(J=\{0\}\). Equation (2) gives \(E(J)=1\), proving (1). We henceforth assume \(K\ge1\).

If \(\lambda=0\), take the interval \(\{u,\ldots,K\}\), where \(u=\lceil K/2\rceil\ge1\). Monotonicity of \(b_t\) and the fact that this upper interval has at least as many indices as its complement imply that its mass is at least \(\mathcal Z/2\). Explicitly, each index \(t\in\{0,\ldots,u-1\}\) is paired with the distinct upper index \(t+u\le K\), and \(b_t\le b_{t+u}\). By (3), \(b_K/b_u\le2^m\), so the partition above has \(N\le1+m\log2\le m+1\) pieces. It yields
\(E(J)\ge e^{-2}/[2(m+1)]\), which is stronger than (1).

For the remaining cases let \(\lambda>0\). Define the even integer
\[
T=2\left\lceil\frac{(m+3)\log2+4}{\lambda}\right\rceil\ge2.
\tag{5}
\]
For every integer \(t\ge T\) within \(0,\ldots,K\), the growth estimate and \(t=\lceil t/2\rceil+\lfloor t/2\rfloor\) imply
\[
w_t\le2^m e^{-\lambda\lfloor t/2\rfloor}w_{\lceil t/2\rceil}
\le2^m e^{-\lambda T/2}w_{\lceil t/2\rceil}.
\]
Each target index \(\lceil t/2\rceil\) has at most two preimages and is itself in \(0,\ldots,K\). Therefore, with an empty tail interpreted as zero,
\[
\sum_{t=T}^K w_t\le2^{m+1}e^{-\lambda T/2}\mathcal Z
\le\frac{e^{-4}}4\mathcal Z.
\tag{6}
\]
This uses only the original normalizer \(\mathcal Z\).

If \(\lambda\ge1\), the interval \(\{0,\ldots,\min(K,T-1)\}\) has mass at least \((1-e^{-4}/4)\mathcal Z\ge3\mathcal Z/4\) by (6). Since \(\log2<1\), (5) gives
\[
T\le\frac{2((m+3)\log2+4)}{\lambda}+2
\le2m+16\le16(m+1).
\]
There are at most \(16(m+1)\) indices in that interval. One singleton \(J=\{t\}\) has \(E(J)=w_t/\mathcal Z\ge3/[64(m+1)]\), again stronger than (1).

It remains to consider \(0<\lambda<1\). Put \(s=\lceil1/\lambda\rceil\), so \(1\le\lambda s\le1+\lambda<2\).
If \(K<2s\), take \(u=\lceil K/2\rceil\) and \(v=K\). The same monotone pairing as in the case \(\lambda=0\) gives \(\sum_{t=u}^K b_t\ge\frac12\sum_{t=0}^K b_t\). Since \(\lambda K<2\lambda s<4\) and \(w_t\le b_t\), it follows that
\[
\sum_{t=u}^K w_t\ge e^{-4}\sum_{t=u}^K b_t
\ge\frac{e^{-4}}2\sum_{t=0}^K b_t
\ge\frac{e^{-4}}2\mathcal Z.
\]
Here (3) gives \(B\le m\log2\le m\), and \(W=\lambda(K-u)<4\). The partition has \(N\le m+5\le5(m+1)\) pieces, hence supplies
\(E(J)\ge e^{-6}/[10(m+1)]\), which implies (1).

Finally suppose \(K\ge2s\). The indices \(s,\ldots,2s-1\) are all feasible. Monotonicity gives
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}w_t\le s b_s,\qquad
\sum_{t=s}^{2s-1}w_t\ge s b_s e^{-\lambda(2s-1)}\ge e^{-4}s b_s.
\]
Thus the total mass at indices \(t\ge s\) is at least \(\mathcal Z/(1+e^4)\ge(e^{-4}/2)\mathcal Z\). Removing the tail in (6) leaves the complete interval
\(\{u,\ldots,v\}=\{s,\ldots,\min(K,T-1)\}\)
with mass at least \((e^{-4}/4)\mathcal Z\). It is nonempty; also (5) gives \(T\ge2s\), since \((m+3)\log2+4>1\).

We bound the number of partition pieces without any parameter depending on the real resource coefficients or on \(K\). From (5), \(\lambda<1\), and \(\log2<1\),
\[
\lambda T\le2((m+3)\log2+4)+2\lambda\le2m+16.
\]
As \(s\ge1/\lambda\), we have \(v/u<T/s\le\lambda T\le2m+16\). Since \(2m+16\) is an integer, \(\lceil v/u\rceil\le2m+16\le16(m+1)\). By (3) and the elementary inequalities \(\log16<3\) and \(\log(m+1)\le m\),
\[
B\le m\log(16(m+1))\le m(m+3),\qquad
W=\lambda(v-u)\le2m+16.
\]
The partition therefore has
\[
N\le1+B+W\le m^2+5m+17\le17(m+1)^2.
\]
Its mass bound and (4) produce a complete piece \(J\) satisfying
\[
E(J)\ge\frac{e^{-2}e^{-4}}{4\cdot17(m+1)^2}
=\frac{e^{-6}}{68(m+1)^2}
\ge\frac{e^{-6}}{100(m+1)^2}.
\]
This completes all cases for (1). Each earlier case has a displayed stronger bound, so the same absolute constant works uniformly.

For the claimed product comparison, use the height interval corresponding to the selected \(J\). At any point of its product support, \(\sum_i a_i x_i\le R=L-hy_1\le L-hy\), so the point is in the original support \(D\). The ratio of the original probability to the product probability there is exactly
\[
\frac{p(\mathbf x,y)}{Q_R(\mathbf x)u_I(y)}
=\frac{|I|Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda y}}{Z}
\ge\frac{|I|Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda y_0}}{Z}
=\eta.
\]
Outside that product support its extended probability is zero. This proves the pointwise comparison everywhere. All resource tests used weak inequalities, and the quotient map used only \(\ell\ge0\). Consequently points at exact real resource boundaries, zero common budget, and the absence of any heights beyond \(K\) are already included in the proof.
