## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $d=m+1$, $a<0<b$, and let $r:[a,b]\to(0,\infty)$ be finite and concave. In the planar layer with density $r(z)^{m-1}/2$ on $a\le z\le b,\ |x|\le r(z)$, let $V=\int_a^b r(z)^m\,dz$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $\sigma_s$ be the pushforward to the $z$-coordinate of the section measure obtained by integrating the layer density against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$ on $z+sx=0$. Set $C(s)=\int d\sigma_s$ and $M(s)=\int z\,d\sigma_s$. On $(a,b)$ set $u=z/r(z)$, let $R(u)=1/r(z(u))$ for the inverse chart, and put $w=(R-uR')R^{-d-1}$ on the transformed open interval and zero outside it. The chart is strictly increasing, $w$ is defined almost everywhere, and $V=\int_{\mathbb R}w$. For $s>0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
 C(s)&=\frac1{2s}\int_{-s}^s w(u)R(u)\,du,&
 M(s)&=\frac1{2s}\int_{-s}^s u w(u)\,du,\\
 P(s)&=\int_{u>s}w(u)\,du+\frac12\int_{-s}^s w(u)\,du+M(s),&
 P(s)-M(s)&=\frac{V+\int_{u>s}w(u)\,du-\int_{u<-s}w(u)\,du}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The product $wR$ is extended by zero outside the chart. On every compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$ the function $P$ is absolutely continuous and $M(s)=-sP'(s)$ almost everywhere.

## Proof

On $(a,b)$, $r$ is locally Lipschitz. At a differentiability point, the tangent majorizes $r(0)$, so $r-zr'\ge r(0)>0$. On each closed subinterval of the interior $u'=(r-zr')/r^2$ is bounded below by a positive constant, and $u$ and its inverse are Lipschitz there. The inverse rule gives $dz/du=r^2/(r-zr')$, $R'=-r'/(r-zr')$, and $R-uR'=1/(r-zr')$. Thus $r^m dz=w(u)du$. Exhaustion by interior subintervals gives the formula throughout the chart. The two original axial endpoints have zero measure in all these integrals. The a.e. identities are enough even for a profile with one-sided endpoint discontinuities.

For $s>0$, parametrizing the section by $z$ gives
$$
d\sigma_s(z)=\frac{r(z)^{m-1}}{2s}\mathbf1_{\{|z|\le s r(z)\}}\,dz.
$$
Changing variables and multiplying by $z=u/R(u)$ for the moment gives $C$ and $M$. At a fixed $z$, the captured fraction of the horizontal interval is $(1+u/s)/2$ for $|u|\le s$, one above this range and zero below it. Integration gives the two tail identities. This clipped affine fraction is uniformly Lipschitz in $s$ on each compact positive slope interval, uniformly in $u$, since its derivative has magnitude at most $1/(2s)$ where it is active. Its derivative off $u=\pm s$ is $-u\mathbf1_{\{|u|<s\}}/(2s^2)$, and each exceptional two-point set is $w\,du$-null. Dominated integration gives the derivative formula.
