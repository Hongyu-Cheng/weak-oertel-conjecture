## Statement

For every integer \(d\geq 1\), put \(m=d+1\), choose \(\delta>0\), and define, for \(x\in[0,2m]\),
\[
g(x)=\delta(x^2,x^3,\ldots,x^{d+1}),
\qquad
L=\max_{x\in[0,2m]}\|g''(x)\|_2,
\]
\[
R_0=dL(2m)^2,
\qquad
R(x)=R_0-\frac L2x^2.
\]
Let
\[
C_d=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d:
0\leq x\leq2m,
\ \|y-g(x)\|_2\leq R(x)\right\}.
\]
For \(0\leq i\leq d+1\), set
\[
v_i=(2i,g(2i)),
\qquad
w_i=\omega_dR(2i)^d,
\]
where \(\omega_d\) is the \(d\)-dimensional volume of the Euclidean unit ball, and define the probability measure
\[
\rho_d=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}w_i}
\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}w_i\delta_{v_i}.
\]
Then \(C_d\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its fiber over every \(x\in[0,2m]\) is the Euclidean ball of radius \(R(x)>0\) centered at \(g(x)\); in particular, \(v_i\) is the centroid of the even integer fiber over \(2i\), and that fiber has volume \(w_i\). The \(d+2\) points \(v_0,\ldots,v_{d+1}\) are affinely independent, and
\[
\frac{\min_i w_i}{\max_i w_i}\geq\frac12.
\]
Moreover, with the infimum taken over closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{d+1}\),
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathbb R^{d+1}}
\inf_{H\ni p}\rho_d(H)
\leq \frac{2}{d+3},
\]
which tends to zero as \(d\to\infty\).

## Proof

The polynomial map \(g\) is twice continuously differentiable on the compact interval \([0,2m]\), so \(L<\infty\). Its first component has second derivative \(2\delta\), hence \(L>0\). Since \(R'(x)=-Lx\), the minimum of \(R\) on this interval occurs at \(2m\), and
\[
R(2m)=dL(2m)^2-\frac L2(2m)^2
=2(2d-1)Lm^2>0.
\]
Thus every displayed fiber radius is positive.

We first prove convexity with the exact interpolation estimate. Fix \(x_0,x_1\in[0,2m]\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), and put
\[
\bar x=(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1,
\qquad
\Delta=x_1-x_0.
\]
Taylor's formula about \(\bar x\), with its vector-valued integral remainder bounded using \(\|g''\|_2\leq L\), gives
\[
g(x_k)=g(\bar x)+g'(\bar x)(x_k-\bar x)+r_k,
\qquad
\|r_k\|_2\leq\frac L2|x_k-\bar x|^2
\quad(k=0,1).
\]
The two linear terms cancel after multiplication by \(1-\lambda\) and \(\lambda\). Since
\(x_0-\bar x=-\lambda\Delta\) and
\(x_1-\bar x=(1-\lambda)\Delta\), it follows that
\[
\left\|(1-\lambda)g(x_0)+\lambda g(x_1)-g(\bar x)\right\|_2
\leq
\frac L2\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta^2.
\tag{1}
\]
On the other hand, the quadratic definition of \(R\) gives the exact concavity gap
\[
R(\bar x)-\bigl((1-\lambda)R(x_0)+\lambda R(x_1)\bigr)
=\frac L2\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Take \((x_k,y_k)\in C_d\), and write \(u_k=y_k-g(x_k)\). Combining the triangle inequality, \(\|u_k\|_2\leq R(x_k)\), (1), and (2), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\|(1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1-g(\bar x)\right\|_2
&\leq (1-\lambda)R(x_0)+\lambda R(x_1)
 +\frac L2\lambda(1-\lambda)\Delta^2\\
&=R(\bar x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Also \(\bar x\in[0,2m]\), so the convex combination belongs to \(C_d\). This proves convexity.

The defining inequalities are closed, and \(x\), \(g(x)\), and \(R(x)\) are bounded on \([0,2m]\), so \(C_d\) is compact. The point \((m,g(m))\) satisfies both endpoint inequalities and the ball inequality strictly: \(0<m<2m\) and \(0<R(m)\). By continuity, a neighborhood of this point lies in \(C_d\). Hence \(C_d\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{d+1}\) and is full-dimensional.

At a fixed \(x\), the fiber is exactly
\[
(C_d)_x=g(x)+R(x)B_2^d.
\]
Its centroid is \(g(x)\) by central symmetry, and its volume is \(\omega_dR(x)^d\). This proves the assertions about the even integer fibers and the weights.

To verify affine independence, form the \((d+2)\times(d+2)\) matrix whose \(i\)-th row is the homogeneous coordinate vector of \(v_i\):
\[
\bigl(1,\ 2i,\ \delta(2i)^2,\ldots,\delta(2i)^{d+1}\bigr),
\qquad 0\leq i\leq d+1.
\]
Factoring \(\delta\) from each of the last \(d\) columns, its determinant is
\[
\delta^d\prod_{0\leq i<j\leq d+1}(2j-2i),
\]
which is nonzero. Therefore \(v_0,\ldots,v_{d+1}\) are affinely independent.

Because \(R\) is decreasing on \([0,2m]\), the exact extremal weight ratio is
\[
\frac{\min_iw_i}{\max_iw_i}
=\left(\frac{R(2m)}{R(0)}\right)^d
=\left(1-\frac1{2d}\right)^d.
\]
Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
\left(1-\frac1{2d}\right)^d
\geq 1-d\frac1{2d}=\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]

It remains to bound graph depth, including points outside the simplex. Let
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_{d+1}\}.
\]
If \(p\notin T\), strict separation from the compact convex set \(T\) supplies a closed halfspace containing \(p\) and no \(v_i\). Hence the Tukey depth of \(p\) under \(\rho_d\) is zero.

Suppose instead that \(p\in T\). Since the vertices are affinely independent, \(p\) has unique barycentric coordinates
\[
p=\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}\alpha_iv_i,
\qquad
\alpha_i\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}\alpha_i=1.
\]
Choose any \(j\) with \(\alpha_j>0\). Let \(\beta_j\) be the affine barycentric-coordinate functional satisfying \(\beta_j(v_j)=1\) and \(\beta_j(v_i)=0\) for \(i\neq j\). The closed halfspace
\[
H_j=\{q\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:\beta_j(q)\geq\alpha_j\}
\]
contains \(p\), and among the support of \(\rho_d\) it contains exactly \(v_j\). Therefore
\[
\inf_{H\ni p}\rho_d(H)
\leq\rho_d(H_j)
=\frac{w_j}{\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}w_i}
\leq\frac{\max_iw_i}{\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}w_i}.
\tag{4}
\]
There are \(d+2\) weights. If \(M=\max_iw_i\), then (3) says that each of the other \(d+1\) weights is at least \(M/2\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{d+1}w_i
\geq M+(d+1)\frac M2
=\frac{d+3}{2}M.
\]
Together with (4), this proves
\[
\inf_{H\ni p}\rho_d(H)\leq\frac{2}{d+3}
\]
for every \(p\in T\), while points outside \(T\) have depth zero. Taking the supremum over all \(p\in\mathbb R^{d+1}\) proves the claimed bound.

The measure \(\rho_d\) retains only one centroid point from each even fiber. The estimate therefore rules out a universal positive centerpoint constant for this graph abstraction even when the integer dimension is fixed at one and the retained fiber volumes are uniformly comparable. It makes no assertion about halfspace mass in the full mixed body \(C_d\), so it is not a counterexample to the locked weak Oertel target or to mechanisms that use more than these centroid atoms. This is a non-final counterexample node, for which target matching is inapplicable.
