## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite nonempty real base with positive-volume compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$. Use their summed volume measure. For a mark $Y=(p,y)$ with $y\in K_p$, let $D_0(Y)$ be ordinary raw closed-halfspace depth and $b_{\rm base}(p)$ be closed atomic base depth for weights $V_z=|K_z|$. Include the whole-base trace, the sole trace in rank zero. Then $D_0(Y)\le b_{\rm base}(p)-V_p/2$.

If $B(y,r)\subseteq K_p$ with $r>0$ and $\Gamma$ is a norm on continuous covectors with $\|u\|\le C$ whenever $\Gamma(u)=1$, let $D_\delta(Y)$ use a displacement $\delta\Gamma(u)$ added to each nonvertical through-mark margin, leaving pure-base caps unchanged, where $\delta>0$. Then
$$
D_\delta(Y)\ \ge\ D_0(Y)+\min\!\left\{\frac{V_p}{2},
\kappa_{d-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt3r}{2}\right)^{d-1}
\min\!\left(\frac r2,\frac{\delta}{C}\right)\right\}>D_0(Y),
$$
where $\kappa_{d-1}$ denotes the unit $(d-1)$-ball volume and $\kappa_0=1$.

## Proof

A minimum-weight base trace exists because there are finitely many traces. Tighten its halfspace to pass through $p$, writing it as $\{a\cdot(z-p)\ge0\}$. Take $a=0$ for the whole-base trace, including the formal rank-zero convention. Fix a Euclidean unit continuous covector $u$. The two actual caps with margins $T a\cdot(z-p)\pm u\cdot(X-y)$ capture every fiber with strictly positive drift and omit every fiber with strictly negative drift when $T$ is sufficiently large. On every zero-drift fiber they capture complementary pieces up to a hyperplane of zero $d$-volume. Their masses therefore sum to $2b_{\rm base}(p)-\sum_{z:a\cdot(z-p)=0}V_z$. The latter sum includes $V_p$, and both caps have nonzero continuous normal even when $a=0$. Taking the smaller mass proves the ordinary bound.

For a nonvertical buffered cap normalize $\Gamma(u)=1$. Relative to its ordinary through-mark cap, it gains the part of the own ball with $-\delta\le u\cdot(X-y)<0$. In the Euclidean unit direction $u/\|u\|$, this includes a strip of thickness $\min(r/2,\delta/C)$ adjacent to the central section. Each of those ball sections has $(d-1)$-volume at least $\kappa_{d-1}(\sqrt3r/2)^{d-1}$. Its gain is therefore at least the second term in the stated minimum, uniformly in the base tilt. Every pure-base cap has mass at least $b_{\rm base}(p)$, hence at least $D_0(Y)+V_p/2$. Infimizing the two bounds gives the claim.
