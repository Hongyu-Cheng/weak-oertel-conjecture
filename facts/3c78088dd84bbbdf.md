## Statement

Let n,d be positive integers, let C be a convex body in R^n x R^d, and let S=C intersect (Z^n x R^d), with mixed-integer volume and halfspace depth exactly as in the locked problem. Assume simultaneous base-negation invariance: (x,y) belongs to C if and only if (-x,y) belongs to C. Coordinate-by-coordinate sign invariance is not required.

Use the actual-fiber notation of accepted facts c6b7777f667984de and fab9dbd4ee2b61d6:

\[
C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\},\quad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\quad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\},\quad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z>0,\quad p_z=w_z/W.
\]

For each positive fiber, use its true volume centroid b_z exactly as in c6b7777f667984de and its true uniform-volume covariance:

\[
b_z=w_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
\Sigma_z=w_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}(y-b_z)(y-b_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dy.
\]

Thus b_z does not rename the symmetric-fiber center c_z of fab9dbd4ee2b61d6; the two agree when that fact's central-symmetry hypothesis holds. The true centroid affine space A and its rank r from c6b7777f667984de are unrestricted in the present theorem. We use different symbols for the approximation space and rank.

Suppose there are numbers sigma_0>0 and 0<=epsilon<=1/(4e), and vectors \(\widetilde b_z\in\mathbb R^d\), for every z in D_+, such that

\[
\|b_z-\widetilde b_z\|_2\le\epsilon\sigma_0,
\qquad \Sigma_z\succeq\sigma_0^2 I_d,
\qquad
\widetilde A=\operatorname{aff}\{\widetilde b_z:z\in D_+\},
\quad \widetilde r=\dim\widetilde A.
\]

Here the covariance inequality means beta^T Sigma_z beta >= sigma_0^2 ||beta||_2^2 for every beta in R^d. Put \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), as in c6b7777f667984de. There exist nonnegative coefficients lambda_z with sum one and points

\[
\widetilde m=\sum_{z\in D_+}\lambda_z\widetilde b_z,
\qquad m=\sum_{z\in D_+}\lambda_z b_z\in C_{0_n},
\qquad \|m-\widetilde m\|_2\le\epsilon\sigma_0,
\]

all chosen before any halfspace, such that the one actual point (0_n,m) in S satisfies

\[
h_S((0_n,m))\ge
\frac{\gamma_d-2\epsilon}{2(\widetilde r+1)}W
\ge \frac{W}{4e(\widetilde r+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, if \(\widetilde r\le n\), then this point satisfies the locked target with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/(4e)\in(0,1/e)\).

For every d>=1 there is a body satisfying these hypotheses with n=1, epsilon=1/(4e), true centroid rank r=d, and approximation rank \(\widetilde r=0\). Thus (1) is an approximate-rank criterion that can apply even when the actual centroids span the entire continuous coordinate space.

## Proof

Compactness of C makes its nonempty integer fibers a finite collection. Every C_z with z in D_+ is a compact convex body in R^d. Zero-volume fibers contribute zero to the locked mixed-volume sum, so W=H_d(S). The true centroids b_z belong to their fibers, as proved in c6b7777f667984de. Base-negation invariance gives C_{-z}=C_z and w_{-z}=w_z. Therefore (z,b_z) and (-z,b_z) belong to C, their midpoint (0_n,b_z) belongs to C, and convexity gives
\(\operatorname{conv}\{b_z:z\in D_+\}\subseteq C_{0_n}\).
This argument uses only simultaneous base negation and does not invoke the stronger coordinate-sign hypothesis in the statements of the two predecessors.

Consider the finite atomic probability \(\widetilde\nu=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z\delta_{\widetilde b_z}\) on the affine space \(\widetilde A\). The atomic centerpoint construction proved in c6b7777f667984de applies to arbitrary finite support in an affine space, with its continuous Helly number \(\widetilde r+1\). Hence there is one \(\widetilde m\in\operatorname{conv}\{\widetilde b_z:z\in D_+\}\) such that every closed intrinsic halfspace containing it has \(\widetilde\nu\)-mass at least \(1/(\widetilde r+1)\). For clarity, this construction intersects the support hull with all closed halfspaces of mass strictly greater than \(1-1/(\widetilde r+1)\). Any at most \(\widetilde r+1\) of these sets have a common support point by the strict union bound. Finite Helly and compactness of the hull give a common point for the full family. If a containing closed halfspace had mass less than \(1/(\widetilde r+1)\), finiteness of the support would allow its open complementary halfspace to be shifted into a closed halfspace of mass strictly greater than \(1-1/(\widetilde r+1)\) excluding that point, a contradiction. If \(\widetilde r=0\), the support is one point and the conclusion holds directly with mass one. Thus atoms, including coincident approximants, are included.

Choose one convex-hull representation \(\widetilde m=\sum_z\lambda_z\widetilde b_z\) with nonnegative coefficients summing to one, and fix those coefficients. Define \(m=\sum_z\lambda_z b_z\). The established true-centroid hull inclusion makes m an actual member of C_{0_n}, and the triangle inequality gives
\(\|m-\widetilde m\|_2\le\sum_z\lambda_z\|b_z-\widetilde b_z\|_2\le\epsilon\sigma_0\).
These choices do not depend on a direction or a later halfspace.

Let Z have exactly the original probability p_z, and conditional on Z=z let Y be uniform on the actual fiber C_z. For every measurable ambient set F,
\(\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in F)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)/W\), by the same actual-volume identity as in c6b7777f667984de and fab9dbd4ee2b61d6. No probability has been changed to an auxiliary weighting.

We first verify the shifted centroid-cap estimate needed below. Fix a positive fiber and a nonzero beta in R^d. Write \(X=\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\) under its conditional uniform law and \(s=(\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta)^{1/2}\). This real variable has mean zero and standard deviation s>0. Its density f is log-concave: the uniform density \(w_z^{-1}\mathbf1_{C_z}\) is log-concave by convexity of C_z, an invertible affine change with first coordinate X preserves this property, and Prékopa's marginal theorem cited below gives log-concavity of the first-coordinate density. In dimension one the projection is simply a scaled uniform interval density, with the same property. The checked one-dimensional density bound below yields \(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}f\le1/s\).

The actual-fiber centroid halfspace inequality used and checked in c6b7777f667984de gives \(\mathbb P(X\ge0\mid Z=z)\ge\gamma_d\). Consequently, for every t>=0,

\[
\mathbb P(X\ge ts\mid Z=z)
=\mathbb P(X\ge0\mid Z=z)-\int_0^{ts}f(u)\,du
\ge\gamma_d-t.
\tag{2}
\]

The conditional projection has a density, so the endpoints do not contribute atoms to this identity. The estimate also remains valid when its right side is negative. In particular, this is a dimension-independent quantitative stability estimate for each actual centroid cap, in units of its own actual directional standard deviation.

Now fix any nonzero beta, leaving the previously chosen m and coefficients fixed. Define
\(I_\beta=\{z\in D_+:\beta\cdot(\widetilde b_z-\widetilde m)\ge0\}\).
The centerpoint property gives \(\sum_{z\in I_\beta}p_z\ge1/(\widetilde r+1)\). If beta's affine functional is constant on \(\widetilde A\), the displayed scalar equals zero on all of its support, so I_beta=D_+ and this claim still holds. For every z in I_beta,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta\cdot(b_z-m)
&=\beta\cdot(\widetilde b_z-\widetilde m)
  +\beta\cdot(b_z-\widetilde b_z)
  -\beta\cdot(m-\widetilde m)\\
&\ge-2\epsilon\sigma_0\|\beta\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The actual conditional standard deviation \(s_z=(\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta)^{1/2}\) is at least \(\sigma_0\|\beta\|_2\). Thus (2), with the nonnegative shift \(2\epsilon\sigma_0\|\beta\|_2\le2\epsilon s_z\), gives
\(\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge2\epsilon\sigma_0\|\beta\|_2\mid Z=z)\ge\gamma_d-2\epsilon\).
By (3), this event implies \(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\). Averaging only over I_beta, with the original probabilities, proves

\[
\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d-2\epsilon}{\widetilde r+1}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\gamma_d-2\epsilon>0\), so discarding the other nonnegative summands is valid. For beta=0 the left side of (4) is one and (4) also holds, since \(\gamma_d\le1\), epsilon>=0, and \(\widetilde r+1\ge1\).

Base negation gives identical actual conditional fibers at z and -z and equal original probabilities. The joint law of (Z,Y) is therefore invariant under (Z,Y) -> (-Z,Y). For every alpha in R^n and beta in R^d, the pointwise inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf1_{\{\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{-\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}\\
&\hspace{12mm}\ge\mathbf1_{\{\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
\end{aligned}
\]

holds: if the scalar on the right is nonnegative, at least one of the two scalars on the left is nonnegative. The two probabilities on the left are equal by the stated joint-law invariance. Taking expectations and using (4) proves
\(\mathbb P(\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0)\ge(\gamma_d-2\epsilon)/[2(\widetilde r+1)]\).

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing (0_n,m) contains its parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through that point. The latter has the displayed form for some nonzero pair (alpha,beta), so the actual-volume identity proves the first inequality in (1) for every containing halfspace. The whole-space case has probability one. This covers beta=0, alpha=0, and functionals constant on the approximation space, and always uses the same actual point.

As in c6b7777f667984de, \(\gamma_d>1/e\). With epsilon<=1/(4e) we have \(\gamma_d-2\epsilon\ge1/(2e)\), proving the second inequality in (1). If \(\widetilde r\le n\), then \(\widetilde r+1\le n+1\le2^n\) for n>=1. Hence (1) implies \(h_S((0_n,m))\ge(1/(4e))2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S)\). The chosen c_0 is independent of n,d,C, and sigma_0, and lies strictly between zero and 1/e.

We finish by verifying the claimed full-true-rank example; this construction is not assumed for general C. Fix d>=1, set epsilon=1/(4e), and let K be the unit Euclidean ball in R^d. For x in R and j=1,...,d, put \(\Phi_j(x)=\max\{|x|-j+1,0\}\), \(\Phi(x)=(\Phi_1(x),\ldots,\Phi_d(x))^{\mathsf T}\), and \(E_\Phi(x)=\sum_j\Phi_j(x)\). These new functions do not rename V or E from the homothetic class in fab9dbd4ee2b61d6. Define

\[
M_d=\left(\sum_{j=1}^dj^2\right)^{1/2},\quad
R_d=d(d+1)/2+1,\quad
\sigma_0=1/\sqrt{d+2},\quad
\delta_d=\epsilon\sigma_0/M_d,
\]

and take n=1 and
\(C=\{(x,y):E_\Phi(x)\le R_d,\ y\in\delta_d\Phi(x)+(R_d-E_\Phi(x))K\}\).
We have 0<delta_d<1. Each Phi_j is a continuous convex function, E_Phi(x)>=|x|, and the base set E_Phi(x)<=R_d is compact. Thus the displayed body is compact, for example by its continuous parameterization from that compact base set times K. It is invariant under x -> -x. At x=0, Phi=0 and the fiber is R_d K; continuity of Phi and E_Phi gives a neighborhood of (0,0) lying in C, so C has full dimension.

To verify convexity, take points (x,y),(x',y') in C and t in [0,1]. Let x_t=tx+(1-t)x', \(q_j=t\Phi_j(x)+(1-t)\Phi_j(x')-\Phi_j(x_t)\ge0\), and \(a_\Phi(x)=R_d-E_\Phi(x)\). Write y=delta_d Phi(x)+u and y'=delta_d Phi(x')+u', with ||u||_2<=a_Phi(x) and ||u'||_2<=a_Phi(x'). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_\Phi(x_t)&=ta_\Phi(x)+(1-t)a_\Phi(x')+\sum_jq_j,\\
\|ty+(1-t)y'-\delta_d\Phi(x_t)\|_2
&=\|tu+(1-t)u'+\delta_d q\|_2\\
&\le ta_\Phi(x)+(1-t)a_\Phi(x')+\delta_d\sum_jq_j
\le a_\Phi(x_t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Also E_Phi(x_t)<=R_d. Therefore the convex combination belongs to C, including when a_Phi(x_t)=0, and C is a convex body.

For integer k with 0<=k<=d, \(\Phi_j(k)=\max\{k-j+1,0\}\), \(E_\Phi(d)=d(d+1)/2\), and \(a_\Phi(k)\ge1\). Furthermore E_Phi(d+1)=E_Phi(d)+d>=R_d, with equality only when d=1, and E_Phi is increasing beyond d. Thus the positive integer fibers have exactly the bases D_+={-d,...,d}. They are the actual balls \(C_k=\delta_d\Phi(k)+a_\Phi(k)K\), with original volumes \(w_k=a_\Phi(k)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), and their true centroids are \(b_k=\delta_d\Phi(k)\). The covariance of the uniform law on K is I_d/(d+2): coordinate symmetry makes it scalar, and radial integration gives mean squared radius d/(d+2). Hence \(\Sigma_k=a_\Phi(k)^2I_d/(d+2)\succeq\sigma_0^2I_d\) for every positive fiber.

Set every approximant \(\widetilde b_k=0\). Since \(\|\Phi(k)\|_2\le M_d\) on D_+, the residual is \(\|b_k\|_2\le\delta_d M_d=\epsilon\sigma_0\), and the approximation rank is zero. Finally b_0=0, and for k=1,...,d,
\(b_k-b_{k-1}=\delta_d\sum_{j=1}^ke_j\), where e_j are the standard coordinate vectors. These d successive differences are linearly independent, as their coordinate matrix is triangular with diagonal delta_d>0. They belong to the span of b_1,...,b_d, so the true centroid affine rank is d. All example hypotheses, original fiber volumes, and rank claims are therefore actual ones.

## External sources

András Prékopa, “On logarithmic concave measures and functions,” Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) 34 (1973), 335–343, [primary journal-repository PDF](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/14411/1/math_034_335-343.pdf), Theorem 8, printed page 343 (PDF page 9). Its statement is that all marginals of a multivariate log-concave probability density are log-concave. Here the actual positive-fiber uniform density is log-concave, and after an invertible affine coordinate change its first marginal is the conditional projection X. For d=1 log-concavity is immediate without marginalization.

Sergey Bobkov and Michel Ledoux, “One-dimensional empirical measures, order statistics, and Kantorovich transport distances,” [authors' primary monograph PDF, December 19, 2016 version](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~bobko001/preprints/2016_BL_Order.statistics_Revised.version.pdf), Proposition B.2, equation (B.7), printed page 114 (PDF page 114). The required upper bound is \(\sup_{u\in\mathbb R} f(u)^2\le1/\operatorname{Var}(X)\) for a real random variable with a log-concave density f. Each conditional projection above has such a density, finite variance by compactness, and positive variance by the stated covariance lower bound. Taking a square root gives \(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}f\le1/s\), which yields (2) by integration. The cap probability at the unshifted true centroid is already supplied by the checked Grünbaum inequality in accepted fact c6b7777f667984de.
