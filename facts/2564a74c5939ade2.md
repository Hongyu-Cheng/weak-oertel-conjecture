## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(Z\) be uniform on \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Let \(U\in\mathbb R^d\) be a random vector independent of \(Z\), whose law is a compactly supported continuous probability, with mean \(m_U\) and positive-definite covariance matrix \(\Gamma\). Assume that, for some finite \(\kappa\ge1\) and every \(b\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
\mathbb E\bigl[b\cdot(U-m_U)\bigr]^4
\le \kappa\bigl(b^{\mathsf T}\Gamma b\bigr)^2.
\]

For any function \(f:\{-1,0,1\}^n\to\mathbb R^d\), set \(X=f(Z)+U\). Choose any \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and \(b_0\in\mathbb R^d\), and define

\[
L:=\max_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}
\left\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\bigl(f(z)-Az-b_0\bigr)\right\|_2,
\qquad
K_4:=128\bigl(\kappa+(1+n)^2L^4\bigr).
\]

Here the matrix square roots are the symmetric positive-definite ones. For every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), the centered scalar \(Y:=a\cdot Z+b\cdot(X-\mathbb EX)\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb EY^4\le K_4\bigl(\mathbb EY^2\bigr)^2.
\]

Its variance is zero exactly when \(a=b=0\). For every nonzero normal \((a,b)\), both \(\mathbb P(Y>0)\) and \(\mathbb P(Y<0)\) are at least \(1/(4K_4)\).

To make the probability/depth normalization explicit, let \(\nu:=\operatorname{Law}(Z,X)\), and for any Borel probability \(\eta\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\) define \(D_\eta(y):=\inf_{H\ni y}\eta(H)\), where the infimum is over closed halfspaces. Then

\[
D_\nu(0,\mathbb EX)
\ge \frac{1}{512\bigl(\kappa+(1+n)^2L^4\bigr)}.
\]

The following is the physical original-source consumer. Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and use the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). For \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\), the original source measure is

\[
\mu_{\mathbf j}(B):=
\lambda_d\bigl(\{x\in B:(\mathbf j,x)\in C\}\bigr),
\qquad B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ Borel},
\]

where \(\lambda_d\) is Lebesgue measure, equal to the locked \(\mathcal H_d\) on a fiber. For a Borel set \(E\subseteq\Lambda\), write \(E_{\mathbf j}:=\{x:(\mathbf j,x)\in E\}\) and define the original mixed-integer measure \(\mu(E):=\sum_{\mathbf j}\mu_{\mathbf j}(E_{\mathbf j})=\mathcal H_d(S\cap E)\). Its total mass is \(V:=\mu(\Lambda)=\mathcal H_d(S)\). For a closed ambient halfspace \(H\), its original source cap capacity is \(c_{\mathbf j}(H):=\mu_{\mathbf j}(H_{\mathbf j})\). Thus the canonical depth is exactly

\[
h_S(y)=\inf_{H\ni y}\sum_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}(H).
\]

Let \(J\in\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(\rho\) be a finite nonnegative measure on \(\mathbb R^d\), represented by a density with respect to \(\lambda_d\), with mass \(v:=\rho(\mathbb R^d)>0\). The assignment's finite density \(\rho\) is here identified with its associated measure; its normalized law \(\rho/v\) is exactly the probability law of \(U\) above. Suppose that for every \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\) the following domination holds as measures, simultaneously:

\[
(T_{f(z)})_\#\rho\le\mu_{J+z},
\qquad T_{f(z)}(u):=f(z)+u.
\]

Equivalently, \(\rho(B-f(z))\le\mu_{J+z}(B)\) for every Borel \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\). Define the retained source measure and its total mass by

\[
\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(E)
:=\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\rho(E_{J+z}-f(z)),
\qquad
M:=3^n v.
\]

The retained probability \(\mathbb P_{\mathrm{stock}}:=\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}/M\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Law}(J+Z,X)\), not an output-renewed source measure. Its barycenter

\[
\mathbf y^\star:=
\left(J,\;m_U+3^{-n}\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}f(z)\right)
=(J,\mathbb EX)
\]

belongs to \(S\). Every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
=\sum_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}(H)
\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(\kappa+(1+n)^2L^4\bigr)},
\qquad
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(\kappa+(1+n)^2L^4\bigr)}.
\]

In particular \(V\ge M>0\), so the normalized original probability \(\mathbb P_{\mathrm{orig}}:=\mu/V\) obeys \(D_{\mathbb P_{\mathrm{orig}}}(\mathbf y^\star)=h_S(\mathbf y^\star)/V\ge (M/V)/(512(\kappa+(1+n)^2L^4))\). No existence or lower bound for \(M/V\), and no upper bound for \(L\), is asserted. The continuous-noise fourth-moment hypothesis remains an explicit assumption.

## Proof

The coordinates of \(Z\) are independent, each uniform on \(\{-1,0,1\}\). In particular \(\mathbb EZ=0\), \(\mathbb EZZ^{\mathsf T}=(2/3)I_n\), and \(\mathbb EZ_i^4=2/3\).

First remove the affine part of the bounded normalized error. Put

\[
g(z):=\Gamma^{-1/2}\bigl(f(z)-Az-b_0\bigr),
\qquad
\bar g:=\mathbb Eg(Z),
\qquad
B:=\frac32\mathbb E\bigl[g(Z)Z^{\mathsf T}\bigr],
\qquad
r(z):=g(z)-\bar g-Bz.
\]

These definitions give \(\mathbb Er(Z)=0\) and

\[
\mathbb E\bigl[r(Z)Z^{\mathsf T}\bigr]
=\mathbb E\bigl[g(Z)Z^{\mathsf T}\bigr]-\frac23 B=0.
\]

Consequently the constant part, the linear part, and the residual are mutually orthogonal for the squared Euclidean \(L^2\) norm:

\[
\mathbb E\|g(Z)\|_2^2
=\|\bar g\|_2^2+\frac23\|B\|_{\mathrm F}^2
  +\mathbb E\|r(Z)\|_2^2
\le L^2,
\]

where \(\|B\|_{\mathrm F}^2\) is the sum of the squared entries of \(B\). For each source point \(z\), weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and \(\|z\|_2^2\le n\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\bar g+Bz\|_2
&\le\|\bar g\|_2+\|B\|_{\mathrm F}\|z\|_2\\
&\le\left(\|\bar g\|_2^2+\frac23\|B\|_{\mathrm F}^2\right)^{1/2}
       \left(1+\frac32\|z\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le L\sqrt{1+\frac32 n}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using \((1+\sqrt{1+(3/2)n})^2\le4+3n\le4(1+n)\), we obtain the uniform residual bound

\[
\max_z\|r(z)\|_2
\le L\left(1+\sqrt{1+\frac32n}\right)
\le2L\sqrt{1+n}.
\]

Now fix a normal \((a,b)\). Define

\[
\ell:=a+A^{\mathsf T}b+B^{\mathsf T}\Gamma^{1/2}b,
\qquad
T:=\ell\cdot Z,
\qquad
R:=b\cdot\Gamma^{1/2}r(Z),
\qquad
W:=b\cdot(U-m_U),
\qquad
q:=b^{\mathsf T}\Gamma b.
\]

Since \(\mathbb EX=b_0+\Gamma^{1/2}\bar g+m_U\), the exact decomposition is \(Y=T+R+W\). All three summands have mean zero. Residual orthogonality gives \(\mathbb ETR=0\); independence and centering of \(W\) give \(\mathbb ETW=\mathbb ERW=0\). Writing \(\tau^2:=\mathbb ET^2\) and \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb EY^2\), we therefore have

\[
\tau^2=\frac23\|\ell\|_2^2,
\qquad
\sigma^2=\tau^2+\mathbb ER^2+q.
\]

The pointwise residual estimate implies

\[
|R|\le2L\sqrt{1+n}\sqrt q,
\qquad
\mathbb ER^4\le16(1+n)^2L^4q^2.
\]

There is no lower bound required for \(\tau^2\): the original affine part can cancel completely in \(\ell\). If \(b\ne0\), positive definiteness gives \(\sigma^2\ge q>0\) even under that cancellation. If \(b=0\), then \(R=W=0\), \(\ell=a\), and \(\sigma^2=(2/3)\|a\|_2^2\). Thus only the zero normal has zero variance.

An explicit expansion using the independent centered ternary coordinates gives

\[
\mathbb ET^4
=3\tau^4-\frac23\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_i^4
\le3\tau^4.
\]

The independence of \(T\) and \(W\), and their zero means, eliminate the cubic cross terms without any symmetry assumption on \(U\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(T+W)^4
&=\mathbb ET^4+6\tau^2q+\mathbb EW^4\\
&\le3\tau^4+6\tau^2q+\kappa q^2\\
&\le3\kappa(\tau^2+q)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The elementary inequality \(|s+t|^4\le8(|s|^4+|t|^4)\), applied to \(s=T+W\) and \(t=R\), now yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY^4
&\le8\mathbb E(T+W)^4+8\mathbb ER^4\\
&\le24\kappa(\tau^2+q)^2
       +128(1+n)^2L^4q^2\\
&\le\bigl(24\kappa+128(1+n)^2L^4\bigr)\sigma^4\\
&\le K_4\sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

All inequalities remain valid for the zero normal, with both sides zero.

For completeness, a centered fourth-moment bound gives the required two-sided sign probabilities as follows. Suppose \(\sigma>0\). Hölder's inequality applied to \(|Y|^{2/3}|Y|^{4/3}\) gives

\[
\sigma^2
\le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Y|^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|Y|\ge\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{K_4}}.
\]

Let \(Y_+:=\max(Y,0)\) and \(Y_-:=\max(-Y,0)\). Since \(\mathbb EY=0\), \(\mathbb EY_+=\mathbb EY_-=(1/2)\mathbb E|Y|\). Cauchy-Schwarz consequently gives

\[
\frac{\sigma^2}{4K_4}
\le(\mathbb EY_+)^2
\le\mathbb P(Y>0)\mathbb EY_+^2
\le\mathbb P(Y>0)\sigma^2.
\]

Applying the same argument to \(Y_-\) gives \(\mathbb P(Y<0)\ge1/(4K_4)\) as well.

Every closed halfspace containing \((0,\mathbb EX)\) can be written with a nonzero normal as \(H=\{(z,x):a\cdot z+b\cdot x\ge t\}\), where \(t\le b\cdot\mathbb EX\). The event \(Y\ge0\) is contained in \(\{(Z,X)\in H\}\), so the positive-sign bound implies \(\nu(H)\ge1/(4K_4)\). If a zero-normal representation is allowed, a containing such set is the whole space and has probability one. Taking the infimum proves the probability-depth assertion.

It remains to verify that all physical charges are to original stocks. Distinct source points \(z\) yield distinct bases \(J+z\). Therefore the assumed domination, for any Borel \(E\subseteq\Lambda\), gives

\[
\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(E)
\le\sum_z\mu_{J+z}(E_{J+z})
\le\mu(E).
\]

Each translated copy has mass \(v\), so \(\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(\Lambda)=3^n v=M\). Normalizing each copy by \(v\) and each source weight by \(3^n\) shows directly that \(\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}/M=\operatorname{Law}(J+Z,f(Z)+U)\). In particular its barycenter is \((J,\mathbb EX)\), with integral base. Domination makes this law carried by the fixed original convex body \(C\).

Here is a direct verification that its barycenter belongs to \(C\). If its expectation \(\mathbf y^\star\) were outside the compact convex set \(C\), choose a nearest point \(p\in C\). For each \(x\in C\), the segment \(p+t(x-p)\), \(0\le t\le1\), lies in \(C\). Comparing squared distances to \(\mathbf y^\star\) and letting \(t\) decrease to zero gives \((\mathbf y^\star-p)\cdot(x-p)\le0\). Taking expectation with respect to the stock law gives \(\|\mathbf y^\star-p\|_2^2\le0\), a contradiction. Thus \(\mathbf y^\star\in C\); its integral base makes it a member of \(S\).

For any closed halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf y^\star\), translate the base by \(-J\) and apply the already proved probability theorem. It gives \(\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(H)/M\ge1/(4K_4)\). In terms of the original source capacities, the entire comparison is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}c_{\mathbf j}(H)
\ge\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\mu_{J+z}(H_{J+z})
\ge\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\rho(H_{J+z}-f(z))
=\mu_{\mathrm{stock}}(H)
\ge\frac{M}{4K_4}.
\]

Here evaluation on an ambient set means evaluation on its intersection with \(\Lambda\). This is simultaneous domination by the fixed original measures, not domination after a renewal or redistribution. Taking the infimum gives the stated bound for the locked \(h_S\). Applying domination to \(\Lambda\) gives \(V\ge M>0\), and division by \(V\) gives the original-probability alias and its depth bound. The constants contain no dependence on \(d\). The proof uses no parity, piecewise-linearity, or retained-mass existence assertion.
