## Statement

Retain the complete canonical hard-branch hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted facts 2fe46f47d19112ea, 86944ca2dce34ab7, bf0d5c250b8f688c, and d01de728d8b5e33c. Fix any \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\), and write

\[
 Y:=\widehat Y(v),
 \qquad
 w_y:=\widehat\tau_y(v),
 \qquad
 W(A):=\sum_{y\in A}w_y
 \quad(\varnothing\ne A\subseteq Y).
\tag{1}
\]

For every nonempty \(A\subseteq Y\), retain the canonical base rank, continuous residual rank, and rank-density score

\[
 \rho_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A),
 \qquad
 r_A^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s(y):y\in A\},
\tag{2}
\]

\[
 \Lambda_v(A)
 :=2^{n-\rho_A}\frac{W(A)}{r_A^c+1}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then there exists a nonempty proper subset \(A_\star\subsetneq Y\) such that

\[
 \boxed{\rho_{A_\star}\geq1,}
\tag{4}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 2^{n-\rho_{A_\star}}W(A_\star)
 >\frac{11Q}{320},}
\tag{5}
\]

and every nonempty lower-rank subset satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 B\subseteq A_\star,
 \quad
 \rho_B<\rho_{A_\star}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 W(B)<\frac12W(A_\star).}
\tag{6}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{r_{A_\star}^c\geq5.}
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, the stronger stopped-set mass still does not itself prove the terminal rank-density inequality. Any rank-one restriction with nonnegative \(\Lambda_v\)-drift must use a decrease in \(r_A^c\) to compensate for the strict mass deficit in (6).

## Proof

Accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c gives

\[
 |Y|\geq n+4,
 \qquad
 W(Y)=2\widehat P(v)>\frac{7Q}{200}.
\tag{8}
\]

Accepted fact bf0d5c250b8f688c gives \(n\geq2\) and the strict dyadic atom cap. Combined with the synchronized endpoint bound in accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c, this yields, for every \(y\in Y\),

\[
 w_y=\widehat\tau_y(v)
 \leq\omega_{e(y)}(v)
 \leq t_{e(y)}
 <\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{9}
\]

Choose any \(y_0\in Y\) and put \(A_0:=Y\setminus\{y_0\}\). By (8), \(A_0\) contains at least \(n+3\geq5\) distinct physical endpoints. Thus \(A_0\) is nonempty and proper and \(\rho_{A_0}\geq1\). Equations (8) and (9) give the strict bound

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W(A_0)
 &=W(Y)-w_{y_0}\\
 &>\frac{7Q}{200}-\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}\\
 &\geq\frac{7Q}{200}-\frac{Q}{1600}
 =\frac{11Q}{320}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The second comparison in (10) may be equality when \(n=2\), but the first comparison is strict, so the final lower bound remains strict. Since \(A_0\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), one has \(\rho_{A_0}\leq n\), and therefore

\[
 2^{n-\rho_{A_0}}W(A_0)
 \geq W(A_0)
 >\frac{11Q}{320}.
\tag{11}
\]

Now run the deterministic rank-one half-mass descent of accepted fact 86944ca2dce34ab7. At a current nonempty set \(A_i\subseteq A_0\) of positive affine rank, if there exists a nonempty subset \(A_{i+1}\subseteq A_i\) satisfying

\[
 \rho_{A_{i+1}}=\rho_{A_i}-1,
 \qquad
 W(A_{i+1})\geq\frac12W(A_i),
\tag{12}
\]

choose one such subset. Otherwise stop. Every successful step preserves the strict lower bound in (11), because

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2^{n-\rho_{A_{i+1}}}W(A_{i+1})
 &=2^{n-\rho_{A_i}+1}W(A_{i+1})\\
 &\geq2^{n-\rho_{A_i}}W(A_i).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The descent cannot reach affine rank zero. Indeed, a nonempty rank-zero subset of the set \(Y\) of distinct physical endpoints is a singleton \(\{y\}\). Equations (11)-(13) would then imply

\[
 2^nw_y>\frac{11Q}{320},
\tag{14}
\]

whereas (9) gives

\[
 2^nw_y<\frac{Q}{400}<\frac{11Q}{320},
\tag{15}
\]

a contradiction. Since the affine rank decreases by one at every successful step, the descent therefore stops after finitely many steps at a set \(A_\star\) of positive affine rank. It is nested in the proper set \(A_0\), so it is a nonempty proper subset of \(Y\), and (11)-(13) prove (5).

At the stopping set there is no rank-\((\rho_{A_\star}-1)\) subset carrying at least half the weight. If a nonempty \(B\subseteq A_\star\) with \(\rho_B<\rho_{A_\star}\) had \(W(B)\geq W(A_\star)/2\), extend an affine basis of \(B\) by points of \(A_\star\) until the resulting subset \(B'\subseteq A_\star\) has rank exactly \(\rho_{A_\star}-1\). Then \(W(B')\geq W(B)\geq W(A_\star)/2\), contradicting the stopping rule. This proves (6).

It remains to strengthen the residual-rank conclusion. The hard selector inequality in accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c and the hard ledger retained in accepted fact bf0d5c250b8f688c give

\[
 \frac{Q}{100}
 >R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq4\gamma_d\Lambda_v(A_\star)
 =4\gamma_d
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{A_\star}}W(A_\star)}
 {r_{A_\star}^c+1}.
\tag{16}
\]

All quantities divided by below are positive. Rearranging (16) and applying (5) gives

\[
 r_{A_\star}^c+1
 >\frac{400\gamma_d}{Q}
 2^{n-\rho_{A_\star}}W(A_\star)
 >\frac{55}{4}\gamma_d.
\tag{17}
\]

Accepted fact 2fe46f47d19112ea gives \(\gamma_d>4/11\), hence

\[
 r_{A_\star}^c+1
 >\frac{55}{4}\gamma_d
 >\frac{55}{4}\cdot\frac4{11}
 =5.
\tag{18}
\]

The residual rank \(r_{A_\star}^c\) is a nonnegative integer. Thus (18) implies \(r_{A_\star}^c+1\geq6\), which is exactly (7). Finally, the exact one-step identity in accepted fact 86944ca2dce34ab7 shows that (6) can be overcome only by a sufficiently smaller denominator \(r_B^c+1\) on rank-lowering subsets, as claimed.
