## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\). For every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), let \(\phi_i,\gamma_i:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty]\) be even convex functions satisfying \(\phi_i(0)=\gamma_i(0)=0\). Set \(\psi_i=\phi_i+\gamma_i\), and assume \(\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\psi_i(k)}<\infty\). For \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n\), set \(\Phi(\mathbf u)=\sum_i\phi_i(u_i)\), \(\Gamma(\mathbf u)=\sum_i\gamma_i(u_i)\), and \(\Psi=\Phi+\Gamma\). Let \(L:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) be affine, and define

\[
g(\mathbf x,t)=
e^{L(\mathbf x)-\Gamma(\mathbf x-j)-t}
\mathbf1_{\{t\ge L(\mathbf x)+\Phi(\mathbf x-j)\}}.
\]

Here \(e^{-\infty}=0\), and no finite \(t\) satisfies \(t\ge+\infty\). Thus a fiber with \(\Phi(\mathbf x-j)=\infty\) or \(\Gamma(\mathbf x-j)=\infty\) is identically zero. This convention does not assign centroids to zero-mass fibers.

On \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\), define \(\mu_g(A)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_A(\mathbf z,t)g(\mathbf z,t)\,dt\), \(W=\mu_g(\Lambda)\), and \(h_g(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mu_g(H)\), where the infimum is over closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). Then \(g\) is log-concave, \(0<W<\infty\), and its fiber masses and positive-fiber centroids are \(a_{\mathbf z}=e^{-\Psi(\mathbf z-j)}\) and \(m_{\mathbf z}=L(\mathbf z)+\Phi(\mathbf z-j)+1\), respectively.

For every finite real \(p\ge2\), define \(\lambda_{\mathbf z}^{(p)}=a_{\mathbf z}^p/\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf v}^p\). These weights have finite absolute first moments, satisfy \(\sum_{\mathbf z}\lambda_{\mathbf z}^{(p)}\mathbf z=j\), and give a finite weighted centroid height. The selected point \(\mathbf y_p=(j,\sum_{a_{\mathbf z}>0}\lambda_{\mathbf z}^{(p)}m_{\mathbf z})\) belongs to \(\Lambda\cap\{g>0\}\) and satisfies

\[
h_g(\mathbf y_p)
\ge \frac{1-e^{-(1-1/p)}}{2e}\,p^{-n}W.
\]

The stronger coefficient above uses the sharp small-deviation theorem specified under EXTERNAL SOURCES. Without that 2026 input, Feige's earlier theorem gives \(h_g(\mathbf y_p)\ge(1-e^{-(1-1/p)})p^{-n}W/26\). In particular, for \(p=2\) the stronger absolute constant is \(c_0=(1-e^{-1/2})/(2e)\in(0,1/e)\), and \(h_g(\mathbf y_2)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W\). Both summands \(\phi_i\) and \(\gamma_i\) are required to be even; evenness of their sum alone is not the stated hypothesis.

## Proof

The positive support of \(g\) is the intersection of the convex domain of \(\Gamma(\mathbf x-j)\) with the epigraph of \(L(\mathbf x)+\Phi(\mathbf x-j)\). On this convex set, \(\log g=L(\mathbf x)-\Gamma(\mathbf x-j)-t\) is concave. Extended-valued convex functions on the real line are Borel measurable, including at their possible domain endpoints, so \(g\) is measurable and log-concave. On a positive fiber, the substitution \(r=t-L(\mathbf z)-\Phi(\mathbf z-j)\) gives \(g(\mathbf z,t)\,dt=e^{-\Psi(\mathbf z-j)}e^{-r}\mathbf1_{\{r\ge0\}}\,dr\). This proves the formulas for \(a_{\mathbf z}\) and \(m_{\mathbf z}\); the conditional excess \(r\) has the exponential law with mean one.

For \(s\ge1\), define \(Z_i(s)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{-s\psi_i(k)}\) and \(Z_s=\prod_iZ_i(s)\). Nonnegativity and Tonelli's theorem give \(Z_s=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-s\Psi(\mathbf u)}\), with \(1\le Z_s\le Z_1<\infty\) and \(W=Z_1\). Define lattice probability laws \(P_1(\mathbf u)=e^{-\Psi(\mathbf u)}/Z_1\) and \(Q_p(\mathbf u)=e^{-p\Psi(\mathbf u)}/Z_p\). Both are products; their coordinate laws are \(P_{1,i}(k)=e^{-\psi_i(k)}/Z_i(1)\) and \(Q_{p,i}(k)=e^{-p\psi_i(k)}/Z_i(p)\). In the original coordinates, \(\lambda_{j+\mathbf u}^{(p)}=Q_p(\mathbf u)\). Expectations of potentials are always taken over their finite-potential support, which is the common positive support of these laws. No expression \(0\cdot\infty\) is needed.

We first check moments. For each \(i\), the integer support of \(e^{-\psi_i}\) is an interval symmetric about zero. If it is bounded, it is finite. If it is unbounded, it is all of \(\mathbb Z\), and summability supplies an integer \(k_0>0\) with \(\psi_i(k_0)>0\). Convexity and \(\psi_i(0)=0\) imply \(\psi_i(k)\ge k\psi_i(k_0)/k_0\) for integers \(k\ge k_0\). Evenness gives the same bound with \(|k|\) on the other tail. Thus \(\sum_k|k|e^{-s\psi_i(k)}<\infty\) for \(s\ge1\). Moreover, for \(r\ge0\), \(r e^{-pr}\le e^{-r}/(e(p-1))\). Consequently \(\mathbb E_{Q_{p,i}}\psi_i<\infty\), and therefore \(\mathbb E_{Q_p}\Phi<\infty\).

Let \(C_p=\mathbb E_{Q_p}\Phi\), the scalar denoted \(C\) in the supplied derivation, distinguished here from the convex body \(C\) in the locked problem. Absolute first moments and evenness imply \(\mathbb E_{Q_p}\mathbf U=\mathbf0\) for \(\mathbf U\sim Q_p\). Since \(L\) is affine, the selected height is \(L(j)+1+C_p\); the sum defining it is absolutely convergent. Because \(\Phi(\mathbf0)=\Gamma(\mathbf0)=0\), the selected point has positive density.

The invertible affine map \(F(\mathbf x,t)=(\mathbf x-j,t-L(\mathbf x))\) maps \(\Lambda\) onto itself, permutes its lattice fibers, and acts as a translation of the continuous coordinate within each fiber. It therefore preserves mixed measure under the corresponding change of density and maps halfspaces bijectively to halfspaces. The transformed density is \(\widetilde g(\mathbf u,r)=e^{-\Gamma(\mathbf u)-r}\mathbf1_{\{r\ge\Phi(\mathbf u)\}}\), and \(F(\mathbf y_p)=(\mathbf0,1+C_p)\). It suffices to work with \(j=\mathbf0\), \(L=0\), and write \(g\) for this transformed density. Put \(t_p=1+C_p\).

The coordinate laws \(P_{1,i}\) and \(Q_{p,i}\) are log-concave on \(\mathbb Z\), with interval support and a mode at zero. Apply Melbourne and Tkocz's entropy comparison, stated under EXTERNAL SOURCES, at order \(p\) to \(P_{1,i}\) and at Shannon order to \(Q_{p,i}\). With natural logarithms, direct substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_p(P_{1,i})-H_\infty(P_{1,i})
&=\frac{\log Z_i(1)-\log Z_i(p)}{p-1}
\le\frac{\log p}{p-1},\\
H(Q_{p,i})-H_\infty(Q_{p,i})
&=p\,\mathbb E_{Q_{p,i}}\psi_i\le1.
\end{aligned}
\]

It follows that \(R:=Z_p/Z_1\ge p^{-n}\). Also \(R\le1\), and, writing \(c_i=\mathbb E_{Q_{p,i}}\phi_i\), we have \(0\le c_i\le1/p\) and \(C_p=\sum_i c_i\). On the common positive support, \(P_1(\mathbf u)/Q_p(\mathbf u)=R e^{(p-1)\Psi(\mathbf u)}\ge R\).

To bound the lower horizontal halfspace, take \(\mathbf U\sim Q_p\), let \(Y_i=\phi_i(U_i)\), and put \(c_*:=\max_i c_i\). These are independent nonnegative variables with finite means. If \(c_*>0\), then \(X_i=Y_i/c_*\) are independent and nonnegative with \(\mathbb E X_i\le1\). The sharp small-deviation theorem at unit slack gives

\[
Q_p\{\Phi(\mathbf U)<C_p+c_*\}
\ge\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n
\ge e^{-1}.
\]

The last inequality follows from \(\log(1+1/n)\le1/n\). On this event, \(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U)>1-c_*\ge1-1/p\). The conditional probability that the exponential fiber excess is at most \(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U)\) is \(1-e^{-(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U))_+}\), where \(r_+=\max\{r,0\}\). Hence its \(Q_p\)-expectation is at least \(e^{-1}(1-e^{-(1-1/p)})\). If \(c_*=0\), every \(Y_i\) vanishes almost surely, so \(C_p=0\) and that expectation is exactly \(1-e^{-1}\), which is also at least the same lower bound. The likelihood-ratio comparison therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mu_g\{t\le t_p\}}{W}
&=\mathbb E_{P_1}\!\left[1-e^{-(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U))_+}\right]\\
&\ge R\,\mathbb E_{Q_p}\!\left[1-e^{-(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U))_+}\right]\\
&\ge \frac{R}{e}\left(1-e^{-(1-1/p)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

For the upper horizontal halfspace, the conditional survival probability is \(e^{-(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U))_+}\). Since \(p\ge2\) and \(0\le\Phi\le\Psi\), we have \((p-1)\Psi\ge\Phi\) and \((t_p-\Phi)_+\le t_p\). The same change of lattice law and Jensen's inequality yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mu_g\{t\ge t_p\}}{W}
&=R\,\mathbb E_{Q_p}
e^{(p-1)\Psi(\mathbf U)-(t_p-\Phi(\mathbf U))_+}\\
&\ge R\,\mathbb E_{Q_p}e^{\Phi(\mathbf U)-t_p}\\
&\ge R e^{C_p-t_p}=R/e.
\end{aligned}
\]

The expectations in the last two lines are legitimate: \(\Phi\) is integrable, and \(e^{\Phi(\mathbf u)}e^{-p\Psi(\mathbf u)}\le e^{-(p-1)\Psi(\mathbf u)}\le e^{-\Psi(\mathbf u)}\). This is the step that uses \(p\ge2\).

We now pass from horizontal to arbitrary halfspaces. Every closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,t_p)\) contains a parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through that point. Consider first such a nonvertical halfspace. For some \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb R^n\), it is either \(H_{\boldsymbol\beta}^+=\{(\mathbf x,t):t\ge t_p+\langle\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf x\rangle\}\) or \(H_{\boldsymbol\beta}^-=\{(\mathbf x,t):t\le t_p+\langle\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf x\rangle\}\).

For each lattice point \(\mathbf z\) and real \(s\), let \(F_{\mathbf z}^+(s)=\int_{t_p+s}^{\infty}g(\mathbf z,t)\,dt\) and \(F_{\mathbf z}^-(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{t_p+s}g(\mathbf z,t)\,dt\). Both evenness assumptions imply \(g(-\mathbf z,t)=g(\mathbf z,t)\), and hence \(F_{-\mathbf z}^{\pm}=F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}\), including zero-mass fibers. The function \(F_{\mathbf z}^+\) is nonincreasing, whereas \(F_{\mathbf z}^-\) is nondecreasing. Since one of \(s,-s\) is nonpositive and the other is nonnegative, each sign satisfies \(F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(s)+F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(-s)\ge F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(0)\). Symmetrizing the lattice sum gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_g(H_{\boldsymbol\beta}^{\pm})
&=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\left[F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(\langle\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf z\rangle)
+F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(-\langle\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf z\rangle)\right]\\
&\ge\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}F_{\mathbf z}^{\pm}(0)
=\frac12\mu_g(H_{\mathbf0}^{\pm}).
\end{aligned}
\]

This symmetrization also handles the fixed fiber \(\mathbf z=\mathbf0\). All fiber-mass series are nonnegative and summable, so infinite support causes no rearrangement problem. Nonvertical boundaries have zero mixed mass because they meet each lattice fiber in at most one point. For a vertical halfspace through \((\mathbf0,t_p)\), its base has the form \(\{\mathbf x:\langle\mathbf b,\mathbf x\rangle\ge0\}\) with \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\). The masses \(a_{\mathbf z}=a_{-\mathbf z}\) imply that this halfspace has mass at least \(W/2\), including all mass on its boundary.

Combining the horizontal bounds with symmetrization, and using \(R\le1\), every closed halfspace containing the selected point has mass at least \(R(1-e^{-(1-1/p)})W/(2e)\). Substituting \(R\ge p^{-n}\) and undoing the affine shear proves the asserted bound.

If Feige's earlier theorem is used instead, the unit-slack probability in the lower-horizontal argument is at least \(1/13\). That argument gives \(R(1-e^{-(1-1/p)})W/13\), while the upper-horizontal and vertical bounds are unchanged. Symmetrization then gives the asserted coefficient \(1/26\). The \(p=2\) conclusion follows by substitution. No nonseparable extension, removal of either evenness assumption, or passage to arbitrary uniform convex-body measures has been used.

## External sources

James Melbourne and Tomasz Tkocz, [Reversals of Rényi Entropy Inequalities under Log-Concavity, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10930), arXiv:2005.10930v1. For an integer-valued log-concave law \(q\), \(H_\alpha(q)-H_\infty(q)<\log\alpha/(\alpha-1)\) for \(0<\alpha<\infty\), with the value at \(\alpha=1\) interpreted as \(1\) for natural logarithms. Here \(H_\alpha(q)=\log(\sum_k q(k)^\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\), \(H(q)=-\sum_kq(k)\log q(k)\), and \(H_\infty(q)=-\log\max_kq(k)\). The source's Definition 2.1 requires log-concavity and interval support, not monotonicity. Both coordinate laws above satisfy these hypotheses. The proof only uses the weaker non-strict comparisons.

Weibo Fu, Yanjun Han, Guanyang Wang, Jun Yan, Peng Zhang, and Zhengqing Zhou, [Sharp small-deviation inequalities for sums of independent nonnegative random variables, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23980v1), arXiv:2607.23980v1, 27 July 2026. The exact specialization used is: for \(N\ge1\), independent nonnegative \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) with \(\mathbb E X_i\le1\) satisfy \(\mathbb P\{\sum_iX_i<\sum_i\mathbb E X_i+1\}\ge(N/(N+1))^N\ge e^{-1}\). There is no identical-distribution, variance, bounded-support, or positive-mean requirement. Apply it with \(N=n\) and \(X_i=\phi_i(U_i)/c_*\) only when \(c_*>0\). This preprint is an external mathematical input; any formalization reported by its authors is not a formal verification of the present epigraph theorem.

Uriel Feige, [On Sums of Independent Random Variables with Unbounded Variance, and Estimating the Average Degree in a Graph, Theorem 1](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~feige/Others/newmarkov.pdf). For independent nonnegative \(X_i\) with \(\mathbb E X_i\le1\) and every \(\delta>0\), it gives \(\mathbb P\{\sum_iX_i<\sum_i\mathbb E X_i+\delta\}\ge\min\{\delta/(1+\delta),1/13\}\). Its specialization \(\delta=1\) supplies the earlier \(1/26\) depth coefficient independently of the 2026 small-deviation input.
