## Statement

There is a compact full-dimensional convex body with $n=1,d=2$, whose three exact positive fibers at $-1,0,1$ have volumes $1,k,1$ for $k=1725/577\in[1,3]$. Its true mixed centroid $Y$ is in the middle fiber and a closed halfspace through $Y$ has original mixed mass divided by total mass $64516/198651<1/3$. Its raw cap mass exceeds the smaller budget $(k+1)/3$ by $11570/39813$.

## Proof

Set $c=91/345$ and $\ell=22/345$. Take the middle triangle $K=\{(x,y):0\le x\le1,\ |y|\le k(1-x)\}$ and let both endpoints be the trapezoid $A=K\cap\{\ell\le x\le c\}$. The lifted hull has projection $[-1,1]$ and the endpoint fibers are exact by extremality. At base zero, the equal-weight endpoint mixture lies in $A\subset K$, so the middle fiber is exact. The triangle spans its plane, and an endpoint point lies off that plane, proving full dimension.

Vertical section length $2k(1-x)$ gives $|K|=k$ and its centroid $(1/3,0)$. Each endpoint has area $k[(1-\ell)^2-(1-c)^2]=1$, with first coordinate of the centroid
$$
k[(c^2-\ell^2)-(2/3)(c^3-\ell^3)]=31807/199065.
$$
Endpoint masses cancel the base moment, and reflection in $y=0$ cancels that continuous component. The remaining centroid component is $(k/3+2(31807/199065))/(k+2)=c$. Thus the actual true mixed centroid is $Y=(0,c,0)$ and is in the middle fiber.

The containing cap $\{x\ge c\}$ meets both endpoint trapezoids only on supporting line segments of area zero. Its middle trace is the similar apex triangle, of raw area $P=k(1-c)^2=64516/39813$. Since $M=k+2=2879/577$, its normalized cost is $P/M=64516/198651$, and $M/3-P=567/13271>0$. Direct subtraction gives $P-(k+1)/3=11570/39813>0$. This supplies an upper certificate at $Y$, with no inference about the exact minimizing orientation.
