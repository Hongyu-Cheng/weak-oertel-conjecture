## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, $d\ge1$, and let $y=(z_0,x_0)\in C$ with $z_0\in\mathbb Z$. Write $K_z$ for its continuous fibers. Set $N_k^\pm=(K_{z_0\pm k}-x_0)/k$ for integers $k\ge1$ and let $h_\pm(a)$ be the extended gauge of $C-y$ evaluated at $(\pm1,a)$. Then $N_k^\pm=\{a:h_\pm(a)\le1/k\}$ are nested decreasing. The strict-side volume pushforwards under $(z_0\pm k,x)\mapsto (x-x_0)/k$ have densities
$$
f_\pm(a)=\sum_{k\ge1}k^d\mathbf1_{N_k^\pm}(a)=F_d(\lfloor1/h_\pm(a)\rfloor),\qquad F_d(m)=\sum_{k=1}^m k^d,
$$
where $1/\infty=0$. They have convex superlevels, total masses $\sum_{k\ge1}|K_{z_0\pm k}|$, and first moments $\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-1}\int_{K_{z_0\pm k}}(x-x_0)\,dx$.

For strict-side endpoint directions $a,b$ at distances $k,\ell$, their intersection with base $z_0$ has continuous coordinate $T=x_0+[k\ell/(k+\ell)](a+b)$. Thus its membership in a closed halfspace through $y$ depends only on the direction sum, and each subcoupling of the directional volume measures lifts to a subcoupling of the strict-side original volumes. In particular the largest subcoupling mass carried by $a+b=0$ is
$$
\int_{\mathbb R^d}\min\{f_-(a),f_+(-a)\}\,da
=\sum_{k\ge1}|K_{z_0-k}\cap(2x_0-K_{z_0+k})|.
$$
Only one copy of each reflected base pair is counted. If the strict-side total volumes are equal and positive, the choice $x_0=\sum_{\pm,k}(w_{\pm k}/k)c_{\pm k}/\sum_{\pm,k}w_{\pm k}/k$, with $w_{\pm k},c_{\pm k}$ the volumes and centroids of the positive-volume fibers, belongs to $K_{z_0}$ and makes the sum of the directional first moments zero.

## Proof

Put $D=C-y$. The gauge $p_D$ is convex by convexity of $D$ and positive homogeneity, allowing the value $+\infty$. For each $t>0$, $p_D(v)\le t$ is equivalent to $v\in tD$: use closedness at a positive limiting scale and $0\in D$ for contractions, while compactness forces $v=0$ if the infimum scale is zero. Therefore $N_k^\pm=\{a:p_D(\pm1,a)\le 1/k\}$. Equivalently, moving a point on a farther fiber along the segment to $y$ gives the same projected direction at every nearer distance. On the $k$th side fiber, the variable change $x=x_0+ka$ has Jacobian $k^d$. This gives the density, total mass and first moment. For every $a$ the admissible indices are an initial segment of the positive integers, and there are only finitely many nonempty layers. This proves the cumulative formula, and any positive superlevel is one of the nested sets or empty.

Solving the base coordinate equation for a pair at $z_0-k,z_0+\ell$ gives the stated $T$. Its continuous displacement is a positive multiple of $a+b$, so an ambient linear margin through $y$ has the same sign as its continuous functional on $a+b$, including zero. To lift a direction subcoupling, for each direction on the corresponding side choose index $k$ with conditional probability $k^d\mathbf1_{N_k^\pm}(a)/f_\pm(a)$ where $f_\pm(a)>0$, and reconstruct the endpoint $(z_0\pm k,x_0+ka)$. Use these choices independently conditional on the two directions. Dominated direction marginals then yield dominated original marginals, with equality for a full direction coupling. The sign calculation itself works for arbitrary endpoint laws. For those laws the conditional index weights are the Radon–Nikodym derivatives of the individual direction-layer measures with respect to their sum, rather than the volume weights $k^d/f_\pm$.

A subcoupling on $a+b=0$ has first marginal dominated both by $f_-\,da$ and by the reflection of $f_+\,da$. The pointwise minimum density is attainable by sending each such $a$ to $-a$. At a fixed $a$ the two nested index sets are initial segments with common cumulative weights $F_d$, so the minimum is $\sum_k k^d\mathbf1_{N_k^-\cap(-N_k^+)}(a)$. Integrating and substituting $x=x_0+ka$ gives the displayed sum of reflected fiber overlaps. The formula concerns strict-cross mass in the raw volume units; any central contribution to an atom is separate.

Finally, under balance of the two strict-side volumes, average the lifted fiber centroids $(z_0\pm k,c_{\pm k})$ with weights $w_{\pm k}/k$. The signed contribution of the bases relative to $z_0$ is the difference of the two total volumes, hence zero. Convexity puts the barycenter $(z_0,x_0)$ in $C$. Substituting its continuous coordinate into the first-moment formula gives zero for the sum.
