## Statement

Use the maximal intrinsic-volume centroid graph setup: $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ is compact and convex with nonempty mixed trace $S$, $n,d\ge0$, $n+d\ge1$, $q=\dim_H S$ is the maximum integer fiber dimension, and $a>0$ is the maximum fiber $\mathcal H_q$-volume. Put $M=\mathcal H_q(S)$ and $M_{\max}=a|P_{\max}|$, where $P_{\max}$ is the set of bases attaining $a$. Write $\gamma_0=1$ and $\gamma_j=(j/(j+1))^j$ for $j\ge1$. There is an actual centroid $y$ in a maximum-volume fiber such that every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing $y$ satisfies $\mathcal H_q(S\cap H)\ge 2^{-n}\gamma_q M_{\max}$. Consequently the normalized depth is at least $2^{-n}\gamma_q M_{\max}/M\ge 2^{-n}\gamma_d M_{\max}/M$. If all positive intrinsic fiber volumes are equal this gives $2^{-n}\gamma_q$. In particular for $q=0$, including $d=0$, it gives normalized counting depth $2^{-n}$.

## Proof

The nonempty integer fibers form a finite disjoint union. Thus $q$ is their maximum affine dimension and $M$ is their summed $\mathcal H_q$-measure, positive and finite. By the maximal intrinsic-volume centroid graph lemma, the midpoint of any distinct same-base-parity centroid marks belongs to the graph. Give all marks weight one and use f4547c4166b39683 with the occupied base parity labels. There are at most $2^n$ labels, so the theorem selects one actual mark $y$ for which every containing closed ambient halfspace retains at least $|P_{\max}|/2^n$ marks. The finite theorem includes the singleton case, hence also $n=0$.

Each retained centroid mark gives at least $\gamma_q a$ of intrinsic mass in its fiber. If $q>0$ and the ambient halfspace is nonconstant in the affine span of that fiber, apply 6769574fc1bd9f8d with continuous dimension $q$ and full mass $a$, identifying the affine span isometrically with $\mathbb R^q$. Its full-mass metronoid is the centroid. A constant trace containing the centroid retains the whole fiber, including when the entire fiber is on the ambient boundary. When $q=0$ the corresponding contribution is exactly one. Sum these lower bounds over retained marks; all other contributions are nonnegative. This proves the raw bound.

Finally $\gamma_j$ decreases with $j\ge1$, since $x\log(1+1/x)$ is increasing for $x>0$ by $\log(1+t)>t/(1+t)$ for $t>0$. Also $\gamma_0=1\ge\gamma_1$. Divide by $M$ and use $q\le d$. For $q=0$ all nonempty fibers are singletons, so $M=M_{\max}$.
