## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $A_1,\ldots,A_N\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies and let $\theta_j>0$ sum to one. Suppose a convex body $K\supseteq\sum_j\theta_j A_j$ satisfies $|K|^{1/d}=\sum_j\theta_j|A_j|^{1/d}$. At $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, take a finite nonempty family of halfspaces $H_i=\{u_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$ with $u_i\ne0$ and positive balance $\sum_i\beta_i u_i=0$, $\beta_i>0$. For each $i$, assume every endpoint cut is proper, the normalized endpoint fractions $|A_j\cap H_i|/|A_j|$ are unequal across the family in the sense of being nonconstant, and
$$
|K\cap H_i|^{1/d}=\sum_j\theta_j|A_j\cap H_i|^{1/d}.
$$
Then $K$ and all the $A_j$ are positively homothetic $d$-simplices, and the $u_i$ span $\mathbb R^d$.

## Proof

Whole-body equality and Brunn–Minkowski give $K=\sum_j\theta_j A_j$ and positive homothety of all $A_j$. For the inclusion, a point of the larger body outside the compact smaller one gives positive volume outside it by taking a sufficiently small convex combination with an interior ball of the larger body. For homothety, any strict pair inequality would stay strict in the full sum. [Brunn–Minkowski inequality for convex bodies, equations (3)–(4)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/jaw/COURSES/520s/523/HO.523.20/Gardner.BullAMS.02.pdf).

Set $a_j=|A_j|^{1/d}$, $s=\sum_j\theta_j a_j$, and $D=(K-y)/s$. Then $|D|=1$ and $0\in\operatorname{int}D$. There are $v_j$ with $A_j=a_jD+v_j$ and $\sum_j\theta_jv_j=y$, by the equality of the Minkowski sum and the boundedness of $D$. For each $i$, the normalized threshold $q_{ij}=u_i\cdot(y-v_j)/a_j$ has weighted mean zero with weights $\theta_j a_j/s$. The normalized fractions are nonconstant, so the thresholds are nonconstant. In particular $\min_j q_{ij}<0$.

Apply the one-direction classification in each actual halfspace. Its lowest truncation of $D$ is a cone, with cut at the strictly negative $\min_j q_{ij}$. The same positive balance now applies to these cones, so the finite-apex theorem gives that $D$ is a $d$-simplex and the normals span all of $\mathbb R^d$. The stated representations give the conclusion for the actual bodies.
