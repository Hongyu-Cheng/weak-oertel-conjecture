## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite, $z\in\mathbb R^n$, and $w=(w_v)_{v\in P}\ge0$. For each nonempty $A\subset P$ with $z\in\operatorname{conv} A$, allow a mass $\lambda_A\ge0$ that charges each $v\in A$ its full value, subject to $\sum_{A\ni v}\lambda_A\le w_v$. The maximum $f_w(z)$ of $\sum_A\lambda_A$ is attained, and restricting to inclusion-minimal qualifying groups does not change it; these have at most $n+1$ points. With those groups, the exact attained dual minimizes $\sum_v w_v t_v$ subject to $0\le t_v\le1$ and $\sum_{v\in A}t_v\ge1$ for every group. If there are no groups both values are zero. At fixed $z$, $f_w(z)$ is nondecreasing, positively homogeneous, concave, piecewise linear and superadditive in $w$, with
$$
|f_w(z)-f_{w'}(z)|\le\sum_v|w_v-w_v'|.
$$
The maximum over any fixed finite nonempty target set is continuous. Thus any universal bound $\max_z f_w(z)\ge c\sum_v w_v$ over that fixed target set for positive weights extends to nonnegative weights.

## Proof

Each qualifying group contains an inclusion-minimal qualifying subgroup. Transferring its mass to the subgroup preserves objective and can only reduce the vertex charges; combine transfers to identical groups. By Carathéodory's theorem the inclusion-minimal groups have size at most $n+1$. The feasible region is nonempty, closed, and bounded since each group is charged to some vertex and $\sum_A\lambda_A\le\sum_v w_v$. Thus the primal optimum is attained. Finite linear programming duality gives equality with the minimum over nonnegative $t_v$ and the group inequalities. Replacing any $t_v>1$ by $1$ preserves the inequalities and does not increase objective because $w_v\ge0$. The resulting dual polytope is nonempty and compact; when no groups exist $t=0$ gives zero.

The dual value is the minimum of linear functions of $w$ with coefficient vectors in this fixed compact polytope in $[0,1]^P$. This gives concavity, coordinatewise monotonicity, positive homogeneity, and the displayed Lipschitz bound by testing a minimizer for each capacity vector in the other objective. Taking the minimum over the finite list of polytope vertices gives piecewise linearity. Adding two feasible packings at the same $z$ gives superadditivity. The fixed finite target maximum is continuous as a maximum of finitely many functions with that Lipschitz bound. Approximating any nonnegative $w$ by strictly positive vectors proves the last assertion.
