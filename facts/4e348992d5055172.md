## Statement

Suppose a cap is strictly empty on all but one $d$-simplex fiber, $d\ge1$, on which its affine margin $L$ is strictly positive at just one vertex and strictly negative at the others. Let $c$ be its mass, $H$ the positive apex margin, $\Psi=\int_{L>0}L\,d\mu$ and $T=\int_{L>0}L^2\,d\mu$. Then for every real $p>-1$,
$$
\int_{L>0}L^p\,d\mu=cH^p\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(d+1)}{\Gamma(d+p+1)}.
$$
Consequently $R=cT/\Psi^2=\kappa_d=2(d+1)/(d+2)$ identically on the open parameter region of these sign conditions. Every smooth cost on the positive moment orthant with source degree $h$ under common scaling and margin degree $k$ under $(c,\Psi,T)\mapsto(c,s\Psi,s^2T)$ has the form $\Phi=c^{h-k}\Psi^k f(R)$. On the stated region,
$$
\Phi=f(\kappa_d)c^h[H/(d+1)]^k,\qquad D\Phi=(h\Phi/c)Dc+(k\Phi/H)DH.
$$
An arbitrary smooth scalar cost of these three moments has its differential in the span of $Dc,DH$ there, and the same holds for smooth costs of any fixed finite collection of positive-order moments.

## Proof

The cap is a simplex. For $0\le s\le H$ the part with $L\ge s$ is its image under dilation of factor $1-s/H$ about the apex. Its mass is $c(1-s/H)^d$. The resulting margin density is $cd(1-s/H)^{d-1}/H$ on $(0,H)$. Substituting $s=Ht$ and using the beta integral gives the formula including the integrable negative powers. In particular $\Psi=cH/(d+1)$ and $T=2cH^2/((d+1)(d+2))$. The signs persist under all small affine parameter changes because the fibers are finite and compact and all inequalities are strict. The derivative of $R$ therefore vanishes on this region. Substituting these two expressions into a smooth cost yields a function of $c,H$ and the claimed span. The moment formula gives the same conclusion for the larger finite lists.

For the two homogeneities first divide all coordinates by $c$ and then scale the margin by $c/\Psi$. The normalized triple is $(1,1,R)$. Reversing the two scaling factors gives $\Phi=c^{h-k}\Psi^k f(R)$, where $f(r)=\Phi(1,1,r)$. Conversely this formula has the two degrees. Substitution on the simplex cap yields the displayed cost and differentiating yields its full affine parameter differential. In particular margin degree zero leaves only the section term, and if both degrees vanish or $f(\kappa_d)=0$ the full differential vanishes.
