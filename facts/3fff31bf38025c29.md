## Statement

Let $K_z$ be the positive $d$-dimensional integer fibers of a compact convex body, $d\ge1$, with volumes $V_z$. Two positive barycentric relations yield the same target base $p$ from the source sets $\{s\}\cup A$ and $\{s\}\cup B$, where $A,B$ are nonempty and disjoint and neither contains $s$ or $p$, and $s\ne p$. Fix any two couplings of the uniform fiber probabilities for the respective relations, and any joint gluing over their common uniform $K_s$ coordinate. Let $Y_1,Y_2$ be the resulting continuous barycentric outputs, both in $K_p$.

For a closed ambient halfspace $H$, let $F_r=\{(p,Y_r)\in H\}$, $f_r=\Pr(F_r)$, $o=\Pr(F_1\cap F_2)$, and let $U_z$ denote the captured volume at source base $z$. Put
$$
a=V_s,\quad b=\min_{z\in A}V_z,\quad c=\min_{z\in B}V_z,\quad
c_1=\min(a,b),\quad c_2=\min(a,c),\quad c_{12}=\min(a,b+c),\quad
\rho=c_1+c_2-c_{12}.
$$
Then $\rho\ge0$ and
$$
U_s+\sum_{z\in A}U_z+\sum_{z\in B}U_z\ \ge\
c_1f_1+c_2f_2-\rho o.
$$
This is the sharp pointwise cost rule based only on the two implications that a target hit forces a hit of a source in its relation. Compared to maximizing $\theta_1 f_1+\theta_2 f_2$ subject to $0\le\theta_1\le b$, $0\le\theta_2\le c$, and $\theta_1+\theta_2\le a$, the increase in the right-hand side is exactly $\rho[\min(f_1,f_2)-o]$. It is strictly positive precisely when $\rho>0$ and both directed crossing events have positive probability.

With $q_r(x)=\Pr(F_r\mid X_s=x)$, the bracket is at least $\min\{\mathbb E(q_1-q_2)_+,\mathbb E(q_2-q_1)_+\}$ for every gluing, and equals $\min\{\mathbb E[q_1(1-q_2)],\mathbb E[q_2(1-q_1)]\}$ under conditional independence. These assertions hold simultaneously on any prescribed finite cap family for one gluing.

## Proof

Existence of a gluing follows by disintegrating the two relation couplings over the shared continuous source and taking the product of the two conditional laws. These laws exist on compact Euclidean Borel spaces. Convexity puts both outputs in $K_p$, and a cap's affine margin at an output is the corresponding positive weighted average of source margins. A nonnegative output margin therefore forces a nonnegative source margin in its relation, including equality on cap boundaries.

Charge each source hit its volume $V_z$. The cheapest Boolean cost on no target hit is zero. On a first-only hit the least forced cost is $c_1$, by choosing a cheapest source in the first relation. The second-only case gives $c_2$. If both targets hit, a shared hit suffices at cost $a$, or one must hit both exclusive sets at cost at least $b+c$. Both abstract Boolean options are achievable, so the sharp cost on this state is $c_{12}$. These cases exhaust the two implications without any extra assumptions on the abstract states. Since $c_1+c_2\ge c_{12}$ by considering whether either minimum equals $a$, their pointwise lower bound is $c_1\mathbf1_{F_1}+c_2\mathbf1_{F_2}-\rho\mathbf1_{F_1\cap F_2}$. Its expectation gives the claimed bound because the individual sources are uniform and each distinct source is charged once.

If $f_1\ge f_2$, the fixed packing objective is $(f_1-f_2)\theta_1+f_2(\theta_1+\theta_2)$, which is at most $(f_1-f_2)c_1+f_2 c_{12}$. It is achieved at $(c_1,c_{12}-c_1)$: both coordinates respect their individual caps and their sum is $c_{12}\le a$. Interchanging the indices gives the other case. Subtracting this optimum yields the stated gap. The bracket equals $\min\{\Pr(F_1\setminus F_2),\Pr(F_2\setminus F_1)\}$.

Under any gluing the conditional overlap is at most $\min(q_1,q_2)$, so the conditional directed differences are at least $(q_1-q_2)_+$ and $(q_2-q_1)_+$, respectively. Integrating and taking the minimum proves the lower bound. Under conditional independence the overlap is $q_1q_2$ conditionally, so the two directed differences have exactly the displayed integrands. The same joint law is used for every cap; no capwise optimization is required.
