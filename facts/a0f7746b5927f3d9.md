## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0.
\]
Let \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
\mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
and, for \(p\in S\), put
\[
h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H),\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p),
\]
where the infimum is over closed halfspaces. For \(k\geq1\), write
\[
\gamma_k:=\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^k.
\]

Fix \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_m>0\). For every \(z\) with \(w_z>0\), let \(\lambda_z\) be normalized Lebesgue measure on \(C_z\), and let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\). Choose one representative from every unordered pair \(\{v,-v\}\), \(v\ne0\), for which \(w_{m+v}w_{m-v}>0\), and call the resulting finite set \(\mathcal R_m\). For \(v\in\mathcal R_m\), define
\[
\alpha_v:=\min\{w_{m+v},w_{m-v}\},\qquad
q_v:=\frac{c_{m+v}+c_{m-v}}2,
\]
and let \(\nu_v\) be the law of \((X^++X^-)/2\), where \(X^+\sim\lambda_{m+v}\) and \(X^-\sim\lambda_{m-v}\) are independent. Its exact Lebesgue density is
\[
f_v(y)=
\frac{2^d}{w_{m+v}w_{m-v}}
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\mathbf 1_{C_{m+v}}(x)
\mathbf 1_{C_{m-v}}(2y-x)\,dx.
\tag{1}
\]
Adjoin an index \(0\), and put
\[
\mathcal I_m:=\{0\}\mathbin\sqcup\mathcal R_m,\qquad
q_0:=c_m,\qquad
\nu_0:=\lambda_m,\qquad
\alpha_0:=w_m.
\]
Thus the reflected midpoint measure from accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041` is
\[
\kappa_m=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_m}\alpha_i\nu_i
=w_m\lambda_m+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v\nu_v.
\tag{2}
\]

For every nonempty \(J\subseteq\mathcal I_m\), define
\[
r_J:=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{q_i:i\in J\}
\]
and
\[
E_J:=
\gamma_d w_m\mathbf 1_{\{0\in J\}}
+\gamma_{2d}\sum_{v\in J\cap\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)
\geq\frac{E_J}{r_J+1}
}
\tag{4}
\]
for every such \(J\), where
\[
\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m):=
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R^d}
\inf_{\substack{G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\y\in G}}
\kappa_m(G).
\]

In particular, with
\[
r_m:=\dim\operatorname{aff}
\left(\{c_m\}\cup\{q_v:v\in\mathcal R_m\}\right),
\qquad
P_m:=w_m+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d w_m+\gamma_{2d}\sum_v\alpha_v}{r_m+1}
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}P_m}{r_m+1}
>
\frac{P_m}{e(r_m+1)}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
The maximum over all nonempty subclouds \(J\) in (4) is also valid, so (4) includes heavy-component and low-dimensional-subcloud bounds that can be stronger than (5).

For \(a,b\geq0\), define
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b):=
\begin{cases}
\left(\dfrac{2a^{1/d}b^{1/d}}{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}\right)^d,&ab>0,\\[2mm]
0,&ab=0,
\end{cases}
\]
and put
\[
H_m:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z}).
\]
Then the full-cloud bound implies
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}}{2^{d+1}(r_m+1)}H_m.
}
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, if \(H_m\geq\eta W\), then the right side of (6) is at least
\[
\frac{\gamma_{2d}\eta}{2^{d+1}(r_m+1)}W.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041` supplies an \(m\) with \(w_m>0\) and \(H_m\geq2^{-n}W\), and for that \(m\),
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}}{2^{d+1}(r_m+1)}\,2^{-n}W
>
\frac{1}{e\,2^{d+1}(r_m+1)}\,2^{-n}W.
}
\tag{8}
\]

There is a stronger bounded-volume-ratio form. Suppose \(K\geq1\) and
\[
K^{-1}\leq\frac{w_{m+v}}{w_{m-v}}\leq K
\qquad(v\in\mathcal R_m),
\]
and define
\[
\theta_d(K):=
\left(\frac{1+K^{-1/d}}2\right)^d.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}\theta_d(K)}{2(r_m+1)}H_m.
}
\tag{9}
\]
For the midpoint furnished by accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, this gives
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}\theta_d(K)}{2(r_m+1)}\,2^{-n}W
\geq
\frac{\gamma_{2d}}{2\sqrt K(r_m+1)}\,2^{-n}W
>
\frac{1}{2e\sqrt K(r_m+1)}\,2^{-n}W.
}
\tag{10}
\]
No equality or concurrence of the component centroids is assumed in (4)-(10).

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty. A nonempty lower-dimensional fiber has \(w_z=0\), contributes zero to every displayed mass sum, and is never normalized. Every fiber used in \(\lambda_z\), \(c_z\), or \(f_v\) has positive \(d\)-volume and hence is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. In particular, (1) is well-defined. Integrating (1), or equivalently applying the change of variables \(y=(x+x')/2\), shows that \(f_v\) has integral one and is exactly the density of \(\nu_v\). Its centroid is \(q_v\), and its support is
\[
\frac12(C_{m+v}+C_{m-v})\subseteq C_m
\tag{11}
\]
by convexity of \(C\). These observations also show that the definitions do not depend on which of \(v,-v\) represents an unordered pair.

We first establish the exact component halfspace constants. For the central component, let \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(q_0=c_m\). The centroid \(c_m\) is the full-mass metronoid point of \(C_m\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied in dimension \(d\) with selected mass \(w_m\), gives
\[
\nu_0(G)=\lambda_m(G)\geq\gamma_d.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix \(v\in\mathcal R_m\), and let \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(q_v\). The inverse image
\[
\widetilde G:=
\left\{(x,x')\in\mathbb R^{2d}:\frac{x+x'}2\in G\right\}
\tag{13}
\]
is a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{2d}\) containing the centroid
\((c_{m+v},c_{m-v})\) of the \(2d\)-dimensional convex body
\(C_{m+v}\times C_{m-v}\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, now applied in dimension \(2d\) with the full selected mass \(w_{m+v}w_{m-v}\), yields
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{2d}
\bigl((C_{m+v}\times C_{m-v})\cap\widetilde G\bigr)
\geq
\gamma_{2d}w_{m+v}w_{m-v}.
\]
After normalization, the left side divided by \(w_{m+v}w_{m-v}\) is exactly \(\nu_v(G)\), so
\[
\nu_v(G)\geq\gamma_{2d}.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (12) and (14) remain true for the whole space if it is admitted as an improper halfspace.

Fix a nonempty \(J\subseteq\mathcal I_m\), and form the effective centroid-cloud measure
\[
\xi_J:=
\gamma_d w_m\mathbf 1_{\{0\in J\}}\delta_{c_m}
+\gamma_{2d}\sum_{v\in J\cap\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v\delta_{q_v}.
\tag{15}
\]
It has mass \(E_J>0\) and is supported on an affine subspace \(A_J\) of dimension \(r_J\). Equations (12) and (14), summed only over those components whose centroids lie in a closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), give the pointwise comparison
\[
\kappa_m(G)
=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_m}\alpha_i\nu_i(G)
\geq\xi_J(G).
\tag{16}
\]

If \(r_J=0\), the only point of \(A_J\) has \(\xi_J\)-depth \(E_J\). If \(r_J>0\), pull \(\xi_J\) back under an affine isomorphism \(\mathbb R^{r_J}\to A_J\). Accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c`, specialized to integer dimension zero and continuous dimension \(r_J\), gives a point \(y_J\in A_J\) such that every closed halfspace of \(A_J\) containing \(y_J\) has \(\xi_J\)-mass at least
\[
\frac{E_J}{r_J+1}.
\tag{17}
\]
For any ambient closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(y_J\), the intersection \(G\cap A_J\) is either all of \(A_J\) or a closed halfspace of \(A_J\) containing \(y_J\). Hence (17) applies, and (16) gives
\[
\kappa_m(G)\geq\frac{E_J}{r_J+1}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\geq E_J/(r_J+1)\). Assertion 2 of accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041` gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m),
\tag{19}
\]
which proves (4), including every lower-dimensional cloud case.

Taking \(J=\mathcal I_m\) proves the first inequality in (5). The elementary sequence
\(\gamma_k=(1+1/k)^{-k}\) is decreasing in \(k>0\): the derivative of
\(-x\log(1+1/x)\) is
\(-\log(1+1/x)+1/(x+1)<0\). Hence \(\gamma_d\geq\gamma_{2d}\), so the second inequality in (5) follows. Finally,
\((1+1/(2d))^{2d}<e\), hence \(\gamma_{2d}>1/e\), proving the strict last inequality.

It remains to prove the harmonic corollaries. Reflection about \(m\) partitions every noncentral positive-positive pair into the two ordered indices represented by a unique \(v\in\mathcal R_m\). Therefore
\[
H_m=w_m+2\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_{m+v},w_{m-v}).
\tag{20}
\]
For one such pair, abbreviate
\[
a:=w_{m+v},\quad b:=w_{m-v},\quad
\alpha:=\min\{a,b\},\quad
s:=\left(\frac{\max\{a,b\}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/d}\geq1.
\]
Directly from the definition,
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b)
=\alpha\left(\frac{2s}{1+s}\right)^d,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha}{\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b)}
=\left(\frac{1+s^{-1}}2\right)^d
\geq2^{-d}.
\tag{21}
\]
It follows from (20)-(21) that
\[
P_m=w_m+\sum_v\alpha_v
\geq
w_m+2^{-d}\sum_v\operatorname{HM}_d(w_{m+v},w_{m-v})
\geq2^{-(d+1)}H_m.
\tag{22}
\]
Combining (22) with (5) proves (6) and (7). Assertion 1 of accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041` supplies a midpoint \(m\) with \(w_m>0\) and \(H_m\geq2^{-n}W\), proving (8).

Under the stated ratio hypothesis, \(s\leq K^{1/d}\), so (21) improves to
\[
\alpha_v\geq\theta_d(K)
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_{m+v},w_{m-v}).
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(0<\theta_d(K)\leq1\), equations (20) and (23) give
\[
P_m\geq\frac{\theta_d(K)}2H_m.
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (24) with (5) proves (9). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
\frac{1+K^{-1/d}}2\geq K^{-1/(2d)},
\qquad
\theta_d(K)\geq K^{-1/2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Using again the selected midpoint from accepted fact `15ec168ab4433041`, together with \(\gamma_{2d}>1/e\), proves (10). Zero-volume reflected partners contribute zero to \(H_m\) and never enter \(\mathcal R_m\), so all zero-mass cases have already been included.
