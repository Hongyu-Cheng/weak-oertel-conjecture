## Statement

Use the notation and normalization of accepted fact 02f7fb5298b097e2. In particular, when the continuous dimension is \(d=1\),
\[
 \alpha=2^{-n},\qquad \gamma _1=\frac12,\qquad \theta _1=\frac12,
\]
\[
 Q_a(t)=\sum_z\left(a_z-\frac t2\right)_+,
 \qquad
 |\eta|=\sum_z M_z,
\]
where \(a_z\) is the length of the fiber and \(M_z\) is its maximum one-dimensional halfspace depth. Put \(D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\). For \(Q_a(t)>0\), let
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}(t):=
 \left\{m\in\mathbb Z^n:
 \sum_{z\in D_+\cap J}
 \left(a_z-\frac t2\right)_+
 \ge 2^{-n}Q_a(t)
 \text{ for every proper closed halfspace }J\subseteq\mathbb R^n
 \text{ with }m\in J
 \right\}.
\]
Accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c implies that this set is nonempty whenever \(Q_a(t)>0\).

Put
\[
 \rho:=\frac{19}{20},\qquad
 \sigma:=\frac{59}{60},\qquad
 R_n:=2^{n^3}\quad(n\ge3),
\]
and, on \(P_n:=R_nB_2^n\), define
\[
 f_n(z):=1-\frac{15}{R_n}
 \bigl(\|z\|_2-\rho R_n\bigr)_+.
\]
Define the \(d=1\) mixed convex body
\[
 C_n:=\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
 \|z\|_2\le R_n,\
 |x|\le\frac12f_n(z)\right\}.
\]
The profile \(f_n\) is even and concave. It is constant on the inner ball and affine as a function of the radius on the outer shell; no claim that it is piecewise affine on \(\mathbb R^n\) is made. Every lattice fiber in the base projection has positive length
\[
 a_z=f_n(z)\in\left[\frac14,1\right].
\]

Let \(Q_n,\eta_n,\operatorname{Cent}_n\) denote the preceding objects for \(C_n\). Set
\[
 s_n:=\frac{\sqrt n}{2},\qquad
 \varepsilon_n:=8\left(\frac{\sqrt n}{R_n-s_n}\right)^n,
 \qquad
 \tau:=\frac{2953}{2997},
\]
and
\[
 c_n:=\max\{\varepsilon_n,4\tau^n\}.
\]
Then \(c_n\to0\), and, for every \(t>0\) with \(Q_n(t)>0\) and every \(m\in\operatorname{Cent}_n(t)\),
\[
 a_m\le t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{Q_n(t)}{|\eta_n|}\le c_n,
\]
and
\[
 a_m>t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{t}{2^{-n}|\eta_n|}\le c_n.
\]
Consequently there is no absolute \(c>0\) for which every mixed convex body admits a level and a centerpoint satisfying either alternative in the proposed centerpoint-selection principle. In fact the scalar variational quantity itself satisfies
\[
 \sup_{t>0}\min\{2^{-n}Q_n(t),t\}
 \le c_n\,2^{-n}|\eta_n|.
\]

Nevertheless, all deepest fiber sections of \(C_n\) are the common singleton \(\{0\}\). With the projected-hit objects of accepted fact 3f83f851f6547bc6,
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C_n)\ge\frac12|\eta_n|.
\]
Moreover, with \(S_n:=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), the actual mixed halfspace depth at the origin is
\[
 h_{S_n}((0,0))
 =\frac12W_n=|\eta_n|,
 \qquad
 W_n:=\sum_{z\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\]
Thus this family refutes only the scalar centerpoint-selection principle. It is neither a projected-hit counterexample nor a weak-Oertel counterexample.

## Proof

First verify the bodies and fibers. The function
\[
 z\longmapsto(\|z\|_2-\rho R_n)_+
\]
is convex, since it is the maximum of the convex function
\(\|z\|_2-\rho R_n\) and zero. Hence \(f_n\) is concave on \(P_n\). If
\((z_i,x_i)\in C_n\) and \(0\le\lambda\le1\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1|
 &\le(1-\lambda)|x_0|+\lambda|x_1|\\
 &\le\frac12\bigl((1-\lambda)f_n(z_0)+\lambda f_n(z_1)\bigr)\\
 &\le\frac12f_n((1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(C_n\) is convex. It is compact and has nonempty interior, hence is a convex body. Its fiber is
\[
 (C_n)_z=\left[-\frac12f_n(z),\frac12f_n(z)\right].
\]
The profile equals \(1\) for \(\|z\|_2\le\rho R_n\), and at
\(\|z\|_2=R_n\) it equals
\[
 1-15(1-\rho)=\frac14.
\]
It is radially decreasing on the intervening shell, proving
\(a_z=f_n(z)\in[1/4,1]\). Since \(d=1\), this is itself the concave root-volume profile. It is even, so \(C_n=-C_n\).

For an interval \([-a/2,a/2]\), the two possible containing rays show directly that
\[
 \delta(z,x)=\min\left\{x+\frac a2,\frac a2-x\right\}
 =\frac a2-|x|.
\]
Consequently, for every lattice base point,
\[
 M_z=\frac{a_z}{2},\qquad \mathcal T_z=\{0\}.
\]
Using the lifted-mass identity in accepted fact 02f7fb5298b097e2,
\[
 |\eta_n|=\sum_zM_z=\frac12W_n.
\]

We next prove the uniform lattice-cap estimate needed for all centerpoints. Write
\[
 \mathcal N_n(r):=\#(rB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n),
 \qquad v_n:=\operatorname{vol}_n(B_2^n),
 \qquad e_n:=\frac{s_n}{R_n}.
\]
For \(n\ge3\), \(e_n<1/1000\). Coordinatewise rounding moves a point by Euclidean distance at most \(s_n\). The unit cubes centered at the lattice points of \(rB_2^n\) therefore cover \((r-s_n)B_2^n\), while their disjoint half-open versions lie in \((r+s_n)B_2^n\). Hence, whenever \(r>s_n\),
\[
 v_n(r-s_n)^n\le\mathcal N_n(r)\le v_n(r+s_n)^n.
 \tag{1}
\]

For a unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), let
\[
 \mathcal K_n(u):=
 \#\{z\in R_nB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n:u^{\mathsf T}z\ge\rho R_n\}.
\]
Every unit cube centered at a point counted by \(\mathcal K_n(u)\) lies in
\[
 (R_n+s_n)B_2^n
 \cap\{x:u^{\mathsf T}x\ge\rho R_n-s_n\}.
\]
Put \(R':=R_n+s_n\) and \(h:=\rho R_n-s_n>0\). Slicing the Euclidean ball perpendicular to \(u\), and using that the slice area decreases for positive slice coordinate, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal K_n(u)
 &\le v_{n-1}\int_h^{R'}(R'^2-y^2)^{(n-1)/2}\,dy\\
 &\le v_{n-1}(R'-h)(R'^2-h^2)^{(n-1)/2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, (1) gives
\[
 \mathcal N_n(\rho R_n)\ge v_n(\rho R_n-s_n)^n.
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(e=e_n\) and \(A_e:=1-\rho+2e\). Since
\[
 R'-h=A_eR_n,
 \qquad
 R'^2-h^2=A_e(1+\rho)R_n^2,
\]
(2) and (3) imply
\[
 \frac{\mathcal K_n(u)}{\mathcal N_n(\rho R_n)}
 \le
 \frac{v_{n-1}}{v_n}
 \frac{A_e}{\rho-e}
 \left(
 \frac{\sqrt{A_e(1+\rho)}}{\rho-e}
 \right)^{n-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The slicing identity for the unit ball also yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_n
 &=v_{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-y^2)^{(n-1)/2}\,dy\\
 &\ge v_{n-1}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}
 \left(\frac34\right)^{(n-1)/2}\,dy,
 \end{aligned}
\]
and hence
\[
 \frac{v_{n-1}}{v_n}\le\left(\frac43\right)^{(n-1)/2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Using \(e\le1/1000\), \(\rho=19/20\), and \(1+\rho=39/20\),
\[
 \frac{A_e}{\rho-e}
 \le\frac{52}{949}<\frac1{18},
 \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \left(\frac{\sqrt{A_e(1+\rho)}}{\rho-e}\right)^2
 \le\frac{600}{5329}<\left(\frac{17}{50}\right)^2.
 \tag{7}
\]
Substitution into (4) gives, uniformly over all unit \(u\),
\[
 \frac{\mathcal K_n(u)}{\mathcal N_n(\rho R_n)}
 <\frac1{18}
 \left(\frac{17}{50}\sqrt{\frac43}\right)^{n-1}
 <\frac1{18}\left(\frac25\right)^{n-1}
 <2^{-n}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The last inequality follows after division by \(2^{-n}\) from
\((1/9)(4/5)^{n-1}<1\).

Now fix \(0<t<1\). Every lattice point with
\(\|z\|_2\le\rho R_n\) has weight
\[
 w_t(z)=1-\frac t2=:w_{\mathrm{pl}}>0.
\]
Suppose \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) has \(\|m\|_2>\rho R_n\), put
\(u=m/\|m\|_2\), and take the proper closed halfspace
\[
 J_m:=\{z:u^{\mathsf T}z\ge\|m\|_2\}.
\]
It contains \(m\), and its lattice points having nonzero fiber lie among those counted by \(\mathcal K_n(u)\). Since \(a_z\le1\), every truncated weight is at most \(w_{\mathrm{pl}}\). By (8),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{z\in D_+\cap J_m}\left(a_z-\frac t2\right)_+
 &\le\mathcal K_n(u)w_{\mathrm{pl}}\\
 &<2^{-n}\mathcal N_n(\rho R_n)w_{\mathrm{pl}}\\
 &\le2^{-n}Q_n(t).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus \(m\) is not a centerpoint. This proves the universal assertion
\[
 0<t<1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Cent}_n(t)
 \subseteq\rho R_nB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n.
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular, every such centerpoint satisfies
\[
 a_m=1>t.
 \tag{11}
\]

For completeness, if \(Q_n(t)>0\), any centerpoint belongs to the convex hull of the positive-weight lattice support. Otherwise strict separation gives a proper closed halfspace containing the centerpoint and disjoint from that support, contradicting the positive lower bound \(2^{-n}Q_n(t)\). The convex hull lies in \(P_n\). Therefore, when \(t\ge1\), every centerpoint satisfies
\[
 a_m\le1\le t.
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (11) and (12) quantify over every centerpoint, not merely over one centerpoint supplied by accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c.

It remains to compare the two possible branches with the relevant total masses. Solving \(f_n(z)=1/2\) in the radial shell gives
\[
 \|z\|_2=\sigma R_n,
 \qquad \sigma=\frac{59}{60}.
\]
Each positive summand of \(Q_n(1)\) is at most \(1/2\), and such a summand can occur only inside \(\sigma R_nB_2^n\). Thus
\[
 Q_n(1)\le\frac12\mathcal N_n(\sigma R_n).
 \tag{13}
\]
Because every lattice fiber has length at least \(1/4\),
\[
 W_n\ge\frac14\mathcal N_n(R_n),
 \qquad
 |\eta_n|=\frac12W_n\ge\frac18\mathcal N_n(R_n).
 \tag{14}
\]
Equations (1), (13), and (14) give
\[
 \frac{Q_n(1)}{|\eta_n|}
 \le4\frac{\mathcal N_n(\sigma R_n)}{\mathcal N_n(R_n)}
 \le4\left(\frac{\sigma+e_n}{1-e_n}\right)^n.
\]
Since \(e_n\le1/1000\),
\[
 \frac{\sigma+e_n}{1-e_n}
 \le\frac{2953}{2997}=\tau<1.
\]
The function \(Q_n(t)\) is nonincreasing, so
\[
 t\ge1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{Q_n(t)}{|\eta_n|}\le4\tau^n.
 \tag{15}
\]

The cube \([-1/\sqrt n,1/\sqrt n]^n\) lies in \(B_2^n\), so
\(v_n\ge(2/\sqrt n)^n\). Using (1) and (14),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1{2^{-n}|\eta_n|}
 &\le\frac{8\cdot2^n}{\mathcal N_n(R_n)}\\
 &\le8\left(\frac{\sqrt n}{R_n-s_n}\right)^n
 =\varepsilon_n.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Because \(R_n=2^{n^3}\), one has \(\varepsilon_n\to0\). Hence, for
\(0<t<1\),
\[
 \frac{t}{2^{-n}|\eta_n|}<\varepsilon_n.
 \tag{17}
\]

Both \(\varepsilon_n\) and \(4\tau^n\) tend to zero, so \(c_n\to0\). Fix \(t>0\) with \(Q_n(t)>0\) and \(m\in\operatorname{Cent}_n(t)\). If \(t<1\), then (11) puts every \(m\) in the high branch and (17) gives the asserted high-branch failure. If \(t\ge1\), then (12) puts every \(m\) in the low branch and (15) gives the asserted low-branch failure. Given any proposed absolute \(c>0\), choosing \(n\) with \(c_n<c\) makes both proposed alternatives fail for every admissible \(t\) and every centerpoint.

The stronger scalar variational estimate follows from the same split. For \(0<t<1\),
\[
 \min\{2^{-n}Q_n(t),t\}\le t
 \le\varepsilon_n2^{-n}|\eta_n|,
\]
while for \(t\ge1\),
\[
 \min\{2^{-n}Q_n(t),t\}
 \le2^{-n}Q_n(t)
 \le4\tau^n2^{-n}|\eta_n|.
\]
Taking the supremum proves the stated bound.

Finally, verify separately why the family does not threaten projected-hit depth or weak Oertel. Let
\[
 I_n:=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad D_z:=\{0\},
 \qquad w_z:=M_z=\frac{a_z}{2}
 \quad(z\in I_n).
\]
At the integral base point \(m=0\), every barycentric Minkowski combination of the \(D_z\) is \(\{0\}\), so the complete core from accepted fact 3f83f851f6547bc6 is
\[
 \mathcal K_0(D)=\{0\}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The weights are even. For any proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni0\), translate its boundary parallel toward \(0\) until it passes through \(0\). The resulting halfspace \(J^0\subseteq J\) and its reflection cover the whole base. Symmetry therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in I_n\cap J}w_z
 \ge\sum_{z\in I_n\cap J^0}w_z
 \ge\frac12\sum_{z\in I_n}w_z
 =\frac12|\eta_n|.
\]
Thus the quantity \(b_0(w)\) of accepted fact 3f83f851f6547bc6 satisfies
\[
 b_0(w)\ge\frac12|\eta_n|.
 \tag{19}
\]
At the central fiber, \(M_0=1/2\) and its entire deepest section is
\[
 L_0(M_0)=\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal K_0(D).
\]
The whole-section conclusion of accepted fact 3f83f851f6547bc6 now gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_0^{\mathrm{top}})
 \ge b_0(w)
 \ge\frac12|\eta_n|,
\]
and hence \(S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C_n)\ge|\eta_n|/2\).

For the actual mixed halfspace depth, the mixed-volume measure of \(C_n\) is invariant under \((z,x)\mapsto(-z,-x)\). The whole-space halfspace has mass \(W_n\). Given any proper closed halfspace containing the origin, translate its boundary toward the origin until it passes through the origin. The translated halfspace is contained in the original one. It and its reflection cover the whole ambient space, and symmetry gives equal mixed mass to the two reflected halfspaces. Each therefore has mass at least \(W_n/2\), even if the common boundary has positive mixed mass. Hence the origin has mixed halfspace depth at least \(W_n/2\). The horizontal halfspace \(\{(z,x):x\ge0\}\) contains exactly half of every interval fiber and has mixed mass \(W_n/2\). Therefore
\[
 h_{S_n}((0,0))=\frac12W_n=|\eta_n|.
\]
This completes both the counterexample verification and the independent deepest-section and actual-depth rescue.
