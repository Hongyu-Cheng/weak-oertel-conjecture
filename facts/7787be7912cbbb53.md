## Statement

Total variation is one half of L1 distance for probability densities or probability mass functions. The symbols \(C,S\) retain their locked-problem meanings.

For every integer \(m\ge2\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat K&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(1,-1),(0,1)\},\\
\Gamma_m&=m^{-1}\mathbb Z\times m^{-4}\mathbb Z,
\qquad \mathbf a_m=(0,1),\\
\Phi_m(j,i)&=(j/m,1-i/m^4),\\
D_m&=\{(j,i)\in\mathbb Z^2:|j|\le m,
\ 2m^3|j|\le i\le2m^4\},\\
\boldsymbol\theta_m&=(0,-m^{-2}),
\qquad Z_m=\sum_{(j,i)\in D_m}e^{-i/m^2},\\
p_{m,(j,i)}&=Z_m^{-1}e^{-i/m^2}\quad((j,i)\in D_m),\\
\widetilde p_m(\Phi_m(j,i))&=p_{m,(j,i)},
\qquad \boldsymbol\lambda_m=(0,m^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Extend the discrete laws by zero outside their supports. The exact alias map to the root law of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` is
\((D,p,\boldsymbol\theta,Z)=(D_m,p_m,\boldsymbol\theta_m,Z_m)\).
The physical law \(\widetilde p_m\) is its pushforward by the invertible affine map \(\Phi_m\). Its weights are proportional to \(e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_m\cdot\mathbf x}\), because
\(e^{\boldsymbol\lambda_m\cdot \Phi_m(j,i)}=e^{m^2}e^{-i/m^2}\).

Use the single half-open cell shape

\[
Q_m=[0,m^{-1})\times[0,m^{-4}),
\qquad v_m=\operatorname{area}(Q_m)=m^{-5},
\]

and cells \(\mathbf x+Q_m\) for every \(\mathbf x\in\mathbf a_m+\Gamma_m\). They partition the plane. For any probability law \(u\) on this affine lattice, define its piecewise constant interpolation by

\[
\mathsf I_m u(\mathbf y)
=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathbf a_m+\Gamma_m}
\frac{u(\mathbf x)}{v_m}\mathbf1_{\mathbf x+Q_m}(\mathbf y).
\]

This is a probability density. The closure of \(Q_m\) has diameter
\(h_m=\sqrt{m^{-2}+m^{-8}}\to0\), and
\(\operatorname{osc}_{Q_m}(\boldsymbol\lambda_m\cdot\mathbf y)=m^{-2}\to0\).
Also

\[
B(\mathbf0,1/(3\sqrt2))\subseteq\widehat K
\subseteq B(\mathbf0,2),
\qquad
\Phi_m(D_m)=\widehat K\cap(\mathbf a_m+\Gamma_m).
\]

In particular, these are complete positive log-affine atomic laws on polygons with lattice vertices and the fixed ball constants supplied by accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`. Only those geometric constants are matched here; the minimum-area quantile property in that predecessor is an additional property, which this example does not claim.

Let

\[
f_m(\mathbf y)=
\frac{e^{m^2y_2}\mathbf1_{\widehat K}(\mathbf y)}
{\displaystyle\int_{\widehat K}e^{m^2z_2}\,d\mathbf z},
\]

and let \(g_m\) be the ordinary continuous midpoint density, namely the law of \((\mathbf X+\mathbf Y)/2\) for independent endpoints with density \(f_m\). Equivalently, in two dimensions,

\[
g_m(\mathbf z)=4\int_{\mathbb R^2}
f_m(\mathbf x)f_m(2\mathbf z-\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x.
\]

For \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^2\), define the original parity masses and the exact canonical row law by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{m,\mathbf b}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\mathbf b\pmod2}p_{m,\mathbf I},\\
q^{\mathrm{can}}_{m,\mathbf J}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I:
\pi_{m,\mathbf I\bmod2}>0}
\frac{p_{m,\mathbf I}p_{m,2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}
{\pi_{m,\mathbf I\bmod2}},\\
\widetilde q^{\mathrm{can}}_m(\Phi_m(\mathbf J))
&=q^{\mathrm{can}}_{m,\mathbf J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the canonical operation selects its parity with probabilities \(\pi_{m,\mathbf b}\), then takes independent conditional endpoints in that parity. It does not condition a pair of independent original endpoints on parity equality. Its transported parity classes are exactly

\[
\Phi_m(\mathbf b)+2\Gamma_m
=\mathbf a_m+(b_1/m,-b_2/m^4)+2\Gamma_m.
\]

Both alleged L1 convergences fail maximally:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathsf I_m\widetilde p_m-f_m\|_1&\longrightarrow2,\\
\|\mathsf I_m\widetilde q^{\mathrm{can}}_m-g_m\|_1
&\longrightarrow2.
\end{aligned}
\]

More strongly, the first conclusion holds with \(\widetilde p_m\) replaced by any probability law on this affine lattice. Consequently no error bound tending to zero as a function of only \(h_m\), the score-cell variation, and the fixed inner and outer ball constants can establish the requested theorem. This counterfamily also satisfies \(p_{\max,m}\to0\) and \(64\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{m,\mathbf I}^4\to0\). If \(\kappa\in(0,1]\) is exactly the one-dimensional constant in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`, then
\(\liminf_m\operatorname{TV}(p_m,\mathcal T(p_m))\ge\kappa\), with \(\mathcal T\) denoting its exact canonical map. In particular, this example is not a near-stationary countersequence.

The original interpolation's weighted boundary-cell mass actually obeys

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2\setminus\widehat K}
\mathsf I_m\widetilde p_m(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y
\longrightarrow1.
\]

For comparison, the following core-conditioning implication remains valid. In the exact notation of accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`, let \(F_k=K_k\cap\mathbb Z^2\), \(\alpha_k=p_k(F_k)\), and let \(u_k\) be the original root law conditioned on \(F_k\): \(u_{k,\mathbf I}=p_{k,\mathbf I}/\alpha_k\) there and zero elsewhere. Its root normalizer is \(Z_k\alpha_k\), with the same score \(\boldsymbol\theta_k\). Write \(\mathcal T\) for exactly the canonical original-mass-parity map in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`. Then

\[
\operatorname{TV}(u_k,\mathcal T(u_k))
\le\delta_k+4(1-\alpha_k)
\le\delta_k+4\eta_k.
\]

Under its affine transport \(A_k(\mathbf x)=G_k^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf u_{k,0})-\mathbf c_\triangle\), the conditional physical law is \(\widetilde u_k(A_k\mathbf I)=u_{k,\mathbf I}\), its support polygon is \(\widehat K_k=A_kK_k\), its group is \(\Gamma_k=G_k^{-1}\mathbb Z^2\), its coset is \(-\mathbf c_\triangle+\Gamma_k\), and its physical score is \(\boldsymbol\lambda_k=G_k^T\boldsymbol\theta_k\). These are exact aliases, not a replacement by a standard integer grid. Common-cell interpolation preserves the displayed discrete total variation. The counterexample says that the asserted geometric and score-cell assumptions alone do not provide the subsequent comparison with continuous midpoint laws.

## Proof

First verify every geometric and normalization assertion. The triangle can be written as

\[
\widehat K=\{(x,y):y\ge-1,
\ 2x+y\le1,
\ -2x+y\le1\}.
\]

For a point of norm at most \(1/(3\sqrt2)\), the absolute value of either functional \(2x+y\) or \(-2x+y\) is at most \(\sqrt5/(3\sqrt2)<1\), and \(y\ge-1\). This proves the inner ball inclusion. Each vertex has norm at most \(\sqrt2\), and convexity of the ball proves the outer inclusion. A site \(\Phi_m(j,i)\) lies in the triangle if and only if \(|j|\le m\) and \(2m^3|j|\le i\le2m^4\), giving the claimed complete support. Its three vertices are respectively the images of \((-m,2m^4)\), \((m,2m^4)\), and \((0,0)\). Therefore \(D_m\) is the complete integer table of its own convex hull and has affine rank two. The root normalizer \(Z_m\) is positive and finite. The relation between \(\boldsymbol\theta_m\) and \(\boldsymbol\lambda_m\) was computed in the statement, so the physical weights have the required log-affine form.

The translates of \(Q_m\) partition the plane up to their half-open boundaries and have the stated common area. Integration over each cell gives its assigned atom mass. The diagonal lengths and the vertical score change give respectively \(h_m\) and \(m^{-2}\), while \(\|\boldsymbol\lambda_m\|=m^2\to\infty\). Thus no bounded-tilt assumption is implicit.

The affine map \(\Phi_m\) is invertible. It sends integer congruence modulo two to the displayed congruence modulo \(2\Gamma_m\). Moreover

\[
\frac{\Phi_m(\mathbf I)+\Phi_m(\mathbf I')}{2}
=\Phi_m\left(\frac{\mathbf I+\mathbf I'}2\right).
\]

Consequently same-parity endpoints have a midpoint in the transported lattice, and convexity and completeness place that midpoint in \(\Phi_m(D_m)\). The canonical sampling representation and formula in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`, with the alias map already specified, give exactly the law \(\widetilde q_m^{\mathrm{can}}\) written above. This verifies the operator, including small parity masses, without replacing its parity weights.

For clarity about interpolation offsets, the sampling representation of \(\mathsf I_m\widetilde q_m^{\mathrm{can}}\) adds one independent uniform \(Q_m\) offset to the exact atomic canonical midpoint. By contrast, if one first adds independent uniform \(Q_m\) offsets \(\mathbf U,\mathbf V\) to its two atomic conditional endpoints and then averages, the offset is \((\mathbf U+\mathbf V)/2\). These are different offset distributions. No equality of these two smoothing operations is used. The following strip estimate applies directly to the stipulated interpolation of the exact canonical law.

Put \(w_m=m^{-3/2}\), and let \(E_m=\{(x,y):|x|\le w_m\}\). For any cell \(\mathbf x+Q_m\), its intersection with this strip has horizontal length at most \(2w_m\). Its full horizontal length is \(1/m\). Therefore for every probability law \(u\) on the transported lattice,

\[
\int_{E_m}\mathsf I_m u
\le2mw_m=2m^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

This does not use the size, balance, or location of its atoms. In particular it holds both for \(\widetilde p_m\) and for \(\widetilde q_m^{\mathrm{can}}\). It also holds after replacing \(Q_m\) by any translate of the same rectangle, so moving the cell anchors does not remedy the example.

To estimate the continuous original law, write \(t=1-y\). The horizontal section of \(\widehat K\) at this height is \([-t/2,t/2]\), of length \(t\), with \(0\le t\le2\). Hence its continuous normalizer is exactly

\[
\int_{\widehat K}e^{m^2y}\,dx\,dy
=e^{m^2}\int_0^2t e^{-m^2t}\,dt
=\frac{e^{m^2}}{m^4}
\left[1-(1+2m^2)e^{-2m^2}\right].
\]

A point outside \(E_m\) must have \(t>2w_m\). Integrating the larger region \(t>2w_m\) up to infinity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^2\setminus E_m}f_m
&\le\varepsilon_m,\\
\varepsilon_m
&:=\frac{(1+2m^2w_m)e^{-2m^2w_m}}
{1-(1+2m^2)e^{-2m^2}}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The denominator is positive because \(e^{2m^2}>1+2m^2\), and the limit uses \(m^2w_m=\sqrt m\to\infty\). If both independent continuous endpoints belong to \(E_m\), their midpoint also belongs to \(E_m\). The union bound consequently gives

\[
\int_{E_m}g_m\ge1-2\varepsilon_m.
\tag{3}
\]

Taking the difference of probabilities of the same measurable strip, (1)--(3) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{TV}(\mathsf I_m\widetilde p_m,f_m)
&\ge1-\varepsilon_m-2m^{-1/2},\\
\operatorname{TV}(\mathsf I_m\widetilde q_m^{\mathrm{can}},g_m)
&\ge1-2\varepsilon_m-2m^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Total variation between probability laws is at most one. Both limits are therefore one, proving the two L1 limits. The first argument, with (1), works for any lattice law in place of \(\widetilde p_m\).

We next verify directly the failure of the weighted boundary estimate and the vanishing atomic moment. Set \(r_m=e^{-1/m^2}\), so the weight of \((j,i)\) before normalization is \(r_m^i\). The central column \(j=0\) has total unnormalized weight

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{2m^4}r_m^i
=\frac{1-r_m^{2m^4+1}}{1-r_m}.
\]

For each \(j\ne0\), its weight is at most
\(r_m^{2m^3|j|}/(1-r_m)=e^{-2m|j|}/(1-r_m)\).
Define

\[
\beta_m=
\frac{2e^{-2m}}
{(1-e^{-2m})(1-e^{-(2m^2+m^{-2})})}.
\]

Summing the geometric series over noncentral columns shows that their total weight divided by the central column's weight is at most \(\beta_m\to0\). If \(c_m\) is the original probability of the central column, then

\[
1-c_m\le\beta_m.
\tag{4}
\]

Conditional on that column, \(i\) has the geometric law with ratio \(r_m\), truncated to \(\{0,\ldots,2m^4\}\). Conditioning an untruncated geometric random variable on this lower interval cannot increase its expectation: every discarded value is larger than every retained value, so the full expectation is at least the conditional retained expectation. The untruncated mean is \(r_m/(1-r_m)\), as follows by differentiating the geometric series. Thus

\[
\mathbb E_{p_m}[i/m^4\mid j=0]
\le\frac1{m^4(e^{1/m^2}-1)}.
\tag{5}
\]

For an atom \(\Phi_m(0,i)\), a point in its chosen cell has horizontal coordinate in \([0,1/m)\), and its coordinate \(t=1-y\) is at most \(i/m^4\). Membership in \(\widehat K\) requires \(0\le x\le t/2\). The area proportion of this cell inside \(\widehat K\) is consequently at most \(mi/(2m^4)\): this upper bound is valid also when it exceeds one or when part of the cell lies above the vertex. By (5), the conditional central-column interpolation mass inside \(\widehat K\) is at most

\[
\frac1{2m^3(e^{1/m^2}-1)}
\le\frac1{2m},
\]

using \(e^s-1\ge s\). The noncentral interpolation contributes at most its entire mass. With (4) this proves

\[
\int_{\widehat K}\mathsf I_m\widetilde p_m
\le\beta_m+\frac1{2m}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

All the interpolation mass outside \(\widehat K\) comes from cells anchored at sites of \(\widehat K\) but crossing its boundary. Thus (6) verifies that this boundary-cell mass tends to one, rather than merely leaving a Riemann-sum error unestimated. The continuous mass is concentrated at the top vertex on the scale \(m^{-2}\) in both spatial coordinates, whereas the horizontal cell size is \(m^{-1}\). The small vertical score change does not resolve that tangential concentration. This is the first false step in the proposed general transfer.

The maximum original atom is the vertex atom \((0,0)\), of probability \(1/Z_m\). The central column lower-bounds \(Z_m\), giving

\[
p_{\max,m}
\le\frac{1-r_m}{1-r_m^{2m^4+1}}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Since \(\sum p_{m,\mathbf I}=1\), it follows that
\(64\sum p_{m,\mathbf I}^4\le64p_{\max,m}^3\to0\).
Also the total original mass of the parity classes with odd first index is at most \(1-c_m\le\beta_m\to0\). Hence even parity equidistribution is not a consequence of the fine-cell score assumption here. Applying the slab estimate of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` to the primitive normal \((1,0)\), offset zero, width \(\omega=0\), and outside mass \(1-c_m\), gives

\[
\operatorname{TV}(p_m,\mathcal T(p_m))
\ge\left[\kappa-\frac{3p_{\max,m}}{c_m}\right]_+
-4(1-c_m).
\]

By (4) and \(p_{\max,m}\to0\), its lower limit is at least \(\kappa\). Thus the example refutes the quadrature hypotheses while explicitly remaining outside the near-stationary setting of accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`.

Finally, prove the stated valid conditioning comparison. Accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`, with its original symbols unchanged, gives \(\alpha_k\ge1-\eta_k\) and the affine transport used in the statement. Conditioning on a set of mass \(\alpha_k\) has exactly
\(\operatorname{TV}(p_k,u_k)=1-\alpha_k\).
Accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` gives the global canonical continuity estimate
\(\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal T(p_k),\mathcal T(u_k))\le3(1-\alpha_k)\)
with the same original-mass parity convention. The triangle inequality yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{TV}(u_k,\mathcal T(u_k))
&\le\operatorname{TV}(u_k,p_k)
+\operatorname{TV}(p_k,\mathcal T(p_k))
+\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal T(p_k),\mathcal T(u_k))\\
&\le\delta_k+4(1-\alpha_k)
\le\delta_k+4\eta_k.
\end{aligned}
\]

The inverse affine formula is
\(\mathbf x=\mathbf u_{k,0}+G_k(\mathbf y+\mathbf c_\triangle)\).
Substituting it into \(\boldsymbol\theta_k\cdot\mathbf x\) gives the constant term plus \((G_k^T\boldsymbol\theta_k)\cdot\mathbf y\), establishing the physical score alias. This affine map transports the parity cosets and commutes with arithmetic midpoints, so the canonical law is pushed forward exactly. For any common partition into cells of area \(v\), integration cell by cell gives

\[
\frac12\|\mathsf I u-\mathsf I\mathcal T(u)\|_1
=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf I}|u_{\mathbf I}-\mathcal T(u)_{\mathbf I}|
=\operatorname{TV}(u,\mathcal T(u)).
\]

Thus core conditioning does retain near-stationarity, and interpolation retains its discrete comparison. Passing from that comparison to the claimed continuous midpoint comparison requires a further valid approximation theorem. Equations (1)--(6) refute such a theorem under only the bounded-core and score-cell hypotheses in this subgoal.
