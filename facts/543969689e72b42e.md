## Statement

Assume the compatible homothetic deformation and boundary-null cone gluing of the shared-source shell-credit lemma, with $M$ the old total mass and $Q=\beta M$ the old global maximum raw mixed depth. Take a finite collection of selected circuits of critical closed caps of mass $Q$, each through a deepest mark and with unit continuous normals $u_j$, weights $\rho_j>0$ satisfying $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=0$, and weighted old margin nonnegative on its assigned base planes. Treat copies of a cap in different circuits separately. Every old fiber trace of each selected cap is proper or strictly full or empty, and $S_j>0$ is the sum of its proper section areas. Let $L_j$ denote its fixed-cap loss derivative and $B_j\le L_j$ the shared-source bound from the lemma.

For a circuit, there are nearby equal-mass caps with the same normals and unchanged weighted threshold budget. Their common raw mass is $Q+\varepsilon q_c+o(\varepsilon)$, where
$$
q_c=-\sum_j\theta_jL_j,\qquad \theta_j=\frac{\rho_j/S_j}{\sum_h\rho_h/S_h}.
$$
If $\sum_j\theta_jB_j>\beta dV_I$ in each selected circuit for the same compatible deformation, and finite covers with strict old cap-mass slack cover every remaining measured base plane, the new global maximum normalized depth is strictly less than $\beta$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.

## Proof

Write the old cap as $\{\ell_j\ge0\}$ with affine $\ell_j$ and unit continuous normal. For bounded threshold speed $s$, the old-body mass at threshold $\varepsilon s$ is $Q-\varepsilon S_js+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly in $s$. Fubini expresses the mass change as the integral of section areas between the thresholds. At a proper threshold the section areas are continuous there: the sections converge into the old section by compactness, and homothetic images of the old section from interior points on either side give the matching lower limit. In dimension one a proper section counts one point. Strict full or empty traces remain constant nearby. The shell loss at the shifted threshold is $\varepsilon L_j+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly for bounded $s$, by the cone representation and nullity from the preceding lemma. Indeed, at any source boundary point whose old margin is nonzero all radius values in $[1-\varepsilon,1]$ and all bounded speeds give the same limiting indicator. Thus the deformed cap mass is $Q-\varepsilon(L_j+S_js)+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly for bounded $s$.

For each $j$, a proper old section persists near its threshold, since the contracted fibers retain interior balls crossing the old threshold at proper traces. The deformed cap-tail function is continuous and strictly decreasing in a fixed neighborhood of that threshold. Its local inverse at mass $Q+\varepsilon q$ has displacement $-\varepsilon(L_j+q)/S_j+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly for bounded $q$, by bracketing with any fixed increments of the speed on either side. The weighted sum of these displacements decreases strictly in the common mass. Substituting fixed $q$ values on either side of the stated $q_c$ brackets zero by the uniform inverse formula. There is therefore a unique nearby common mass with zero weighted threshold displacement and derivative $q_c$.

The weighted margin of the shifted caps remains the old one on the assigned planes. Its nonnegativity implies at least one shifted closed cap covers each point there. In each circuit the sufficient bound gives $q_c\le-\sum_j\theta_j B_j<-\beta dV_I=\beta M'(0+)$. Its common mass is then strictly below $\beta M_\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon>0$. The old slack covers keep their strict inequality by continuity of total mass and each fixed cap mass (indeed the deformed measured fibers are nested). Finiteness gives one positive range of $\varepsilon$ where all these inequalities hold. These caps cover every measured fiber, and the largest of their finitely many masses is still strictly below $\beta M_\varepsilon$. If the compatible deformation is realized with additional nonempty null fibers, the base of any such fiber is outside the convex hull of the positive-volume bases. Otherwise convexity would put a translated positive multiple of one positive-volume fiber in the null fiber. A strictly separating pure-base cap through the null base has zero measured mass. Thus the depth bound holds on the entire new mixed trace.
