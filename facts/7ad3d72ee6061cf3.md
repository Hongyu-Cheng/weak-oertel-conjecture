## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional convex body, $d\ge1$, with volume $V$ and centroid $g$. For $u\ne0$ write $F(s)=|K\cap\{x:u\cdot(x-g)\ge s\}|/V$, and set $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For every $s\in\mathbb R$,
$$
F(0)+F(s)+\frac12F(-s)\ge\gamma_d+\frac12.
$$
The right-hand side is attained. Equivalently, for every Lebesgue-measurable $A\subseteq K$ of volume $a$ and every $t\in\mathbb R$,
$$
|K\cap\{u\cdot(x-g)\ge0\}|+
|A\cap\{u\cdot(x-g)\ge t\}|+
|K\cap\{u\cdot(x-g)\ge-t\}|
\ge \gamma_d V+\frac a2.
$$

## Proof

It suffices to prove the weighted tail inequality for $s>0$: continuity gives $s=0$, and replacing a positive shift by its negative only increases the left-hand side because $F$ is nonincreasing. For $d=1$ a uniform interval has $F(0)=1/2$ and $F(s)+F(-s)=1$, so the claim holds. For $d\ge2$, the centered linear marginal is nonatomic and $1/d$-concave as a measure by Brunn–Minkowski applied to the preimages in $K$ of Borel sets under the linear projection. With its CDF denoted by $G_0$, the desired bound is $G_0(0)+G_0(s)+G_0(-s)/2\le2-\gamma_d$.

If there were a strict violation, continuity would retain it after replacing the first term by $G_0(-\varepsilon)$ for some $0<\varepsilon<s$. On a compact interval containing the support, use Theorem 2 of Fradelizi and Guédon, *The Extreme Points of Subsets of s-Concave Probabilities and a Geometric Localization Theorem*, DCG 31 (2004), 327–335, with probability-concavity parameter $1/d\le1/2$ and constraint $\int x\,d\nu(x)\ge0$. The objective $\nu((-\infty,-\varepsilon])+\nu((-\infty,s])+\nu((-\infty,-s])/2$ is affine and weakly upper semicontinuous because all cuts are closed relative to the interval. Every admissible Dirac lies at $x\ge0$ and contributes at most one, below $2-\gamma_d$. The other possible maximizing measures have mean zero and density proportional to a nonnegative affine function to the power $d-1$ on a nondegenerate interval. Bounding the unshifted objective on all such centered densities bounds the shifted objective on the extremizers, including those satisfying the additional one-sided moment condition of the theorem. This suffices for the original law. [Primary source, Theorem 2](https://perso.math.u-pem.fr/fradelizi.matthieu/pdf/localizationDCG.pdf).

A constant affine density gives a centered uniform interval, for which the left-hand side is at least one, sufficient since $\gamma_d\le1/2$ by the binomial theorem. Any nonconstant affine-power density becomes the increasing law of the endpoint lemma after translation, rescaling, and possibly reflection. Use the lemma's CDF $G$, mean $m$ and endpoint parameter $r$. Write $R=r^d$, $\alpha=1/d$, and $p=G(m)$. The quantile of the increasing law is $q(v)=[R+(1-R)v]^\alpha$ on $0\le v\le1$, so $m=m(R)=\int_0^1 q(v)\,dv$. By concavity of $q$, $m\le q(1/2)$ and $p\le1/2$. Also $M(R)=m(R)^d$ is concave on $[0,1]$. To check this, on $0<R<1$ let $I,A_1,B_1$ be the integrals on $[0,1]$ of $b^\alpha$, $b^{\alpha-1}(1-v)$, $b^{\alpha-2}(1-v)^2$ respectively, with $b=R+(1-R)v$. Differentiation gives
$$
M''(R)=(1-\alpha)I^{1/\alpha-2}(A_1^2-IB_1)\le0
$$
by Cauchy–Schwarz, and continuity gives concavity on the closed interval. Since $M(0)=\gamma_d$ and $M(1)=1$, one gets $p=(M(R)-R)/(1-R)\ge\gamma_d$.

In the increasing orientation, the weighted upper-tail sum at rescaled shift $h\ge0$ is
$$
1-p+1-G(m+h)+\frac12[1-G(m-h)].
$$
On $[0,1-m]$ both thresholds are in the support, because $m\ge(1+r)/2$. The variable terms are nonincreasing there since their derivative in the interior is $-f(m+h)+f(m-h)/2\le0$. After $1-m$ the first variable term vanishes and the other is nondecreasing. At $h=1-m$ the sum is $1-p+[1-G(2m-1)]/2\ge\gamma_d+1/2$, by $p\le1/2$ and the endpoint lemma.

In the reflected orientation, the sum is $p+J(h)$, where $J(h)=G(m-h)+G(m+h)/2$. Put $k=2^{-1/(d-1)}$ and $h_*=m(1-k)/(1+k)$. On $[0,1-m]$, the derivative $-f(m-h)+f(m+h)/2$ is negative before $h_*$ and positive after it, whenever those shifts lie in the interval. On $[1-m,m-r]$ the function is nonincreasing and thereafter is $1/2$. Thus $p+J$ has minimum at the constant branch, giving at least $\gamma_d+1/2$, or at $h_*$ if $h_*\le1-m$. In the latter case $m\le(1+k)/2$. With $D_d=[2/(1+k)]^{d-1}$, direct substitution using $k^{d-1}=1/2$ gives
$$
p+J(h_*)=\frac{(1+D_d)M(R)-(5/2)R}{1-R}.
$$

The quantile expression makes $m(R)$ continuous and strictly increasing from $d/(d+1)$ to $1$. The number $(1+k)/2$ lies between them, since $(1+2/(d-1))^{d-1}\ge3>2$. Let $R_*$ be the unique parameter giving $m(R_*)=(1+k)/2$. The critical branch is $0\le R\le R_*$. The desired inequality there is $H(R)\ge0$ for the concave function
$$
H(R)=(1+D_d)M(R)-(\gamma_d+1/2)-(2-\gamma_d)R.
$$
At $R_*$, $h_*=1-m$, and the weighted sum $p+J(h_*)$ is $p+G(2m-1)+1/2\ge\gamma_d+1/2$. Thus $H(R_*)\ge0$. At $R=0$, $H(0)=D_d\gamma_d-1/2$. For $d=2$, $D_d\gamma_d=16/27$. For $d\ge3$, monotonicity of the power means of $1$ and $1/\sqrt2$ gives $D_d\ge 8/(3+2\sqrt2)>15/11$, by taking the reciprocal of the power mean of order $2/(d-1)$. The last strict inequality follows from $43^2>2\cdot30^2$. Also $\gamma_d>1/e>11/30$, since $d\log(1+1/d)<1$ and $e<65/24+(1/120)\sum_{j\ge0}(1/6)^j=1631/600<30/11$. Thus $H(0)>0$. Concavity gives the critical-branch bound throughout $[0,R_*]$ and completes both affine-power orientations.

Sharpness follows by taking a simplex whose sections scale as $tL$ for $0\le t\le1$, with $L$ a $(d-1)$-simplex. In the negative first-coordinate direction its centroid tail is $(d/(d+1))^d$. A shift beyond both support endpoints gives $F(s)=0$ and $F(-s)=1$. An interval gives the same equality for $d=1$.

For the subset assertion, the $A$-cap has mass at least $\max\{0,a-V+VF(t)\}$, which is at least $(a-V+VF(t))/2$. Add the two $K$-caps and use the weighted inequality at $s=-t$. Conversely, for any fixed $t$, choose $A=K\cap\{u\cdot(x-g)<t\}$, so $a=V[1-F(t)]$ and its $t$-cap is empty. The subset assertion for this $A$ is the weighted inequality at $s=-t$.
