## Statement

Let $E_0,\ldots,E_d$ be independent unit exponentials, $d\ge1$. Set
$$
A_d=\{a\in[-1,1]^{d+1}:a_0=-1,\ \sum_{i=0}^d a_i=0\},\qquad
P(a)=\Pr\{\textstyle\sum_i a_iE_i\ge0\}.
$$
The probability $P$ is continuous on $A_d$ and attains its minimum. At a local minimum with at least one coefficient in $(-1,0)$, at most one coefficient lies in $(0,1)$.

## Proof

Continuity follows by coupling nearby coefficients with the same exponentials. The limiting sum has no atom at zero because $-E_0$ is independent and continuous. Bounded convergence and compactness give attainment.

Suppose a local minimum has two free positive coefficients, meaning coefficients in $(0,1)$, and a free negative coefficient in $(-1,0)$. Write $f$ for the signed sum's density. The signed-exponential derivative lemma 511d89569241b0d6 applies since there are at least two positive coefficients and a negative one. In particular $f$ is positive, differentiable and log-concave on $\mathbb R$. The first derivatives of $P$ on all free coordinates agree, since varying any two by opposite increments is a feasible two-sided variation. Write their common value as $qf(0)$ with $q>0$. For free positive $p$ and free negative $-b$, the two expressions for the normalized derivative are
$$
\phi_+(p)=\frac1p\int_0^\infty e^{-t/p}\frac{f(-t)}{f(0)}\,dt=q,\qquad
\phi_-(b)=\frac1b\int_0^\infty e^{-t/b}\frac{f(t)}{f(0)}\,dt=q.
$$
Here the density is fixed in each transform. If $(\log f)'(0)\ge0$, the tangent inequality for $\log f$ gives $f(-t)\le f(0)$ for $t\ge0$. If $(\log f)'(0)\le0$, it gives $f(t)\le f(0)$ on the other half-line. In the applicable half-line there is an interval of strict inequality, by continuity and integrability. The exponential kernel is positive there, giving $q<1$.

The reciprocal transform lemma 506e246f680da4b5 applies to $h(t)=f(-t)/f(0)$. For $\psi_+=1/\phi_+$, it gives $\psi_+'(p)>0$ when $\phi_+(p)\le1$, hence $\phi_+'(p)<0$. If $p_i,p_j$ are two free positives, vary them by $(s,-s)$. The first derivative is zero. When they are distinct, the positive-pair Hessian formula in 511d89569241b0d6 has zero mixed entry, since the two $\phi_+$ values are equal. Its second derivative in this direction is $2f(0)(\phi_+'(p_i)+\phi_+'(p_j))<0$. When $p_i=p_j=p$, the mixed entry is $f(0)\phi_+'(p)$ and the second derivative is $2f(0)\phi_+'(p)<0$. Either sign of small $s$ is feasible, contradicting local minimality.
