## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
v_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z),\qquad
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:v_z>0\}.
\]
Define the finite Borel measure
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d\bigl(\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\bigr)
\tag{1}
\]
on \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and fix a query point \(y_0=(z_0,x_0)\in
C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). If
\(\mu(\mathbb R^{n+d})=0\), every flag is minimizing and the structural claims are vacuous. Hence suppose \(\mu(\mathbb R^{n+d})>0\), so \(D\) is a nonempty finite set, and put \(B_0:=\operatorname{aff}(D)\).

If \(z_0\notin B_0\), then the halfspace depth of \(y_0\) is zero and there is a minimizing flag of zero \(\mu\)-mass. If \(z_0\in B_0\), put
\[
\Gamma_0:=(B_0-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
q_0:=\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_0=\dim B_0.
\tag{2}
\]
Then there is a minimizing flag at \(y_0\) with a finite support-relevant lexicographic representation having the following properties.

At any support-relevant stage \(j\), the current positive-mass base set is a finite set \(D_j\subseteq D\), its affine hull \(B_j:=\operatorname{aff}(D_j)\) contains \(z_0\), and the current measure is the restriction of \(\mu\) to the full \(d\)-dimensional fibers above \(D_j\). With
\[
\Gamma_j:=(B_j-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
E_j:=B_j\times\mathbb R^d,
\tag{3}
\]
the next relatively open halfspace layer in \(E_j\) can be written
\[
G_j=\{(z,x)\in E_j:
\alpha_j(z-z_0)+\langle\beta_j,x-x_0\rangle>0\},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\alpha_j\in(B_j-z_0)^*\), \(\beta_j\in\mathbb R^d\), and
\((\alpha_j,\beta_j)\ne(0,0)\).

If \(\beta_j\ne0\), then the restriction of the current mixed measure to
\[
Q_j:=\operatorname{relbd}_{E_j}(G_j)
\tag{5}
\]
is the zero measure. The recursive depth term is therefore zero, and this is the last support-relevant layer.

If \(\beta_j=0\), then \(\alpha_j\ne0\), and
\[
L_j:=\Gamma_j\cap\ker\alpha_j,\qquad
A_j:=z_0+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L_j)
\tag{6}
\]
satisfy
\[
\operatorname{rank}L_j<\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_j,\qquad
A_j\cap\mathbb Z^n=z_0+L_j.
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover \(L_j\) is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^n\), and all lattice fibers charged by the boundary restriction lie above \(A_j\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Thus \(A_j\) is a proper primitive affine lattice slice through \(z_0\). If the boundary restriction has positive mass and its positive-mass base points have affine hull containing \(z_0\), the recursion continues after replacing the real boundary by that affine hull times \(\mathbb R^d\). Its affine lattice rank is strictly smaller than \(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_j\). If the boundary restriction is zero, or if \(z_0\) is outside the affine hull of its positive-mass base points, the recursive depth is zero and the flag terminates.

After support-null padding directions in the literal flag definition are suppressed, there are at most \(q_0\) pure-base layers. In particular there are at most \(q_0\) pure-base layers of positive mixed mass and at most \(q_0\) pure-base transitions having positive boundary mass. The minimizing depth decomposes as the sum of the masses of the displayed relatively open layers; the final singleton has zero \(\mu\)-mass because \(d\ge1\).

No estimate of the form
\[
\mu\bigl((A_j\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d\bigr)
\ge 2^{-(n-\operatorname{rank}L_j)}
\mu(\mathbb R^{n+d})
\tag{8}
\]
is asserted, nor is the current-layer analogue with exponent
\(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_j-\operatorname{rank}L_j\). Consequently the primitive-slice conclusion alone does not meet the heaviness hypothesis of accepted fact `314b32bda8a0b01e`.

## Proof

Because \(C\) is compact, its projection onto \(\mathbb R^n\) meets only finitely many points of \(\mathbb Z^n\). Hence (1) is a finite Borel measure and \(D\) is finite. Fibers with \(v_z=0\) are \(\mu\)-null and may be discarded throughout.

If \(\mu(\mathbb R^{n+d})=0\), the zero measure assigns mass zero to every flag, so the theorem is immediate. We henceforth work in the positive-mass case.

We first record two elementary affine-support reductions. Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional affine space, let \(\nu\) be a finite Borel measure on \(V\) supported on an affine subspace \(E\subseteq V\), and define \(D_V(y;\nu)\) using relatively closed halfspaces of \(V\) whose relative boundary contains \(y\).

If \(y\in E\), then
\[
D_V(y;\nu)=D_E(y;\nu).
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, the restriction to \(E\) of a relative halfspace of \(V\) through \(y\) is either a relative halfspace of \(E\) through \(y\), or all of \(E\). This gives \(D_V(y;\nu)\ge D_E(y;\nu)\). Conversely, a defining linear functional for any relative halfspace of \(E\) extends linearly to the direction space of \(V\), giving the reverse inequality. A flag in \(E\) can also be lifted to a flag in \(V\) without changing its \(\nu\)-mass: choose a codimension-one affine flag from \(V\) down to \(E\), put the support in the boundary at every added layer, and then append the given flag in \(E\). All added relatively open layers are \(\nu\)-null.

If \(y\notin E\), then
\[
D_V(y;\nu)=0.
\tag{10}
\]
To see this, write \(E=a+W\). Since \(y-a\notin W\), there is a linear functional \(\ell\) on the direction space of \(V\) that vanishes on \(W\) and does not vanish at \(y-a\). The value of \(p\mapsto\ell(p-y)\) is a fixed nonzero constant on \(E\). Choosing the opposite orientation gives a closed halfspace through \(y\) disjoint from \(E\). The same construction starts a zero-mass minimizing flag. When \(V\) and \(E\) both contain the full \(\mathbb R^d\) continuous factor and only their base affine spaces differ, \(\ell\) may be chosen with zero continuous component.

Moving the boundary of a closed halfspace containing \(y\) parallel toward \(y\) only shrinks that halfspace. Thus the depth defined using all closed halfspaces containing \(y\) equals the boundary-through-\(y\) depth used above and in the cited flag-halfspace theorem.

Suppose first that \(z_0\notin B_0\). Apply (10) with
\[
V=\mathbb R^{n+d},\qquad E=B_0\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad y=y_0.
\]
The separating functional can be taken in the base coordinates, and the resulting halfspace has zero \(\mu\)-mass. This proves the first alternative in the statement.

Now assume \(z_0\in B_0\). The differences of points of \(D\) span \(B_0-z_0\) and are integer vectors. Therefore \(\Gamma_0\) spans \(B_0-z_0\) over \(\mathbb R\), proving (2). More generally, whenever a finite set \(D_j\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) has \(z_0\in B_j:=\operatorname{aff}(D_j)\), the group
\[
\Gamma_j=(B_j-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n
\tag{11}
\]
has rank \(\dim B_j\), spans \(B_j-z_0\), and is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^n\). Primitivity follows directly: if \(k u\in\Gamma_j\) for \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) and nonzero \(k\in\mathbb Z\), then \(u\in B_j-z_0\), hence \(u\in\Gamma_j\).

Apply Theorem 1 and recursive formula (12) of the cited paper to the current finite Borel measure \(\nu_j\) in the affine space \(E_j=B_j\times\mathbb R^d\). They give a relatively open halfspace \(G_j\) through \(y_0\), of the form (4), such that
\[
D_{E_j}(y_0;\nu_j)
=\nu_j(G_j)+
D_{Q_j}\bigl(y_0;\nu_j|_{Q_j}\bigr),
\qquad
Q_j=\operatorname{relbd}_{E_j}(G_j).
\tag{12}
\]
The theorem also says that a minimizing flag for the restricted term may be appended inside \(Q_j\). Formula (9) permits that tail flag to be constructed in the affine hull of its positive-mass fibers and then lifted back to \(Q_j\) by \(\nu_j\)-null padding layers.

We now prove the dichotomy for (4). Suppose first that \(\beta_j\ne0\). For each \(z\in D_j\), the section of \(Q_j\) in the fiber \(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d\) is
\[
\{z\}\times
\{x\in\mathbb R^d:
\langle\beta_j,x-x_0\rangle=-\alpha_j(z-z_0)\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The second factor in (13) is an affine hyperplane of \(\mathbb R^d\), so it has zero \(\mathcal H_d\)-measure. There are only finitely many charged fibers. Summing their zero measures gives
\[
\nu_j(Q_j)=0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the second term in (12) is zero. No later layer can contribute mixed mass, so this continuous-normal layer is terminal.

Suppose instead that \(\beta_j=0\). Then \(\alpha_j\ne0\) on \(B_j-z_0\). Since \(\Gamma_j\) spans that vector space, the homomorphism
\[
\phi_j:\Gamma_j\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\qquad
\phi_j(\gamma)=\alpha_j(\gamma)
\tag{15}
\]
is nonzero. Its kernel is \(L_j\). The quotient
\(\Gamma_j/L_j\) is isomorphic to the additive subgroup
\(\phi_j(\Gamma_j)\subseteq\mathbb R\), which is torsion-free. Hence \(L_j\) is primitive in \(\Gamma_j\). It has strictly smaller rank because \(\phi_j\ne0\).

The primitivity of \(\Gamma_j\) in \(\mathbb Z^n\) and of \(L_j\) in \(\Gamma_j\) implies that \(L_j\) is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^n\): if \(k u\in L_j\) for \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) and nonzero \(k\in\mathbb Z\), then first \(u\in\Gamma_j\), and then \(u\in L_j\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L_j)\cap\mathbb Z^n=L_j,
\tag{16}
\]
which proves (7).

This argument does not require \(\alpha_j\) to be rational. Indeed, even for an irrational \(\alpha_j\), the lattice points of the relative boundary are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{z\in B_j\cap\mathbb Z^n:\alpha_j(z-z_0)=0\}
&=z_0+L_j\\
&=A_j\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The real hyperplane \(\ker\alpha_j\) may be larger than
\(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L_j)\), and the rank drop may exceed one, but (17) shows that the mixed measure on the boundary is supported on the primitive affine slice \(A_j\).

Because a pure base boundary does not cut the continuous coordinate, its positive-mass base set is
\[
D_{j+1}:=D_j\cap(z_0+L_j).
\tag{18}
\]
If this set is empty, the boundary restriction in (12) is zero. If it is nonempty but \(z_0\notin\operatorname{aff}(D_{j+1})\), apply (10) inside \(Q_j\); the recursive depth is zero, with a pure-base zero-mass terminal halfspace. If
\[
z_0\in B_{j+1}:=\operatorname{aff}(D_{j+1}),
\tag{19}
\]
then (9) identifies the recursive depth in \(Q_j\) with the depth of the same restricted measure in
\[
E_{j+1}:=B_{j+1}\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{20}
\]
Moreover
\[
\Gamma_{j+1}:=(B_{j+1}-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n
\subseteq L_j,
\tag{21}
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_{j+1}
\le\operatorname{rank}L_j
<\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_j.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves the strict rank recursion. It also covers an empty strict side of \(G_j\): equation (12) remains valid with \(\nu_j(G_j)=0\), while the boundary classification and rank drop are unchanged.

Starting with rank \(q_0\), equation (22) can hold at most \(q_0\) times. If the recursion reaches rank zero while retaining positive mass and the query in the support affine hull, then \(B_j=\{z_0\}\). A nonconstant functional on \(E_j=\{z_0\}\times\mathbb R^d\) necessarily has \(\beta_j\ne0\), so (14) terminates the recursion. Termination may occur earlier because of (14), an empty boundary support, or (10). This proves all termination claims and the stated layer bound, including lower-rank initial support.

For completeness, the layer count also includes a terminal pure-base layer whose boundary has zero mass. Before the \(k\)-th support-relevant pure-base layer, the current rank is at most \(q_0-k+1\); that layer requires positive current rank because its nonzero \(\alpha_j\) acts on the span of \(\Gamma_j\). Hence \(k\le q_0\). Thus even after including such a terminal layer, there are at most \(q_0\) support-relevant pure-base layers.

Iterating (12) gives
\[
D_{E_0}(y_0;\mu)
=\sum_j \nu_j(G_j),
\tag{23}
\]
where the sum is over the finite support-relevant sequence and the final recursive term is zero. The layers are disjoint because each later one lies in the relative boundary of all earlier ones. By Definition (3) in the cited paper, adjoining the lifted tail flags and, where necessary, support-null codimension-one padding layers produces a literal flag halfspace. The padding layers have zero mixed mass. Finally,
\[
\mu(\{y_0\})=0
\tag{24}
\]
because \(d\ge1\) and a singleton has zero \(\mathcal H_d\)-measure in its fiber. Equations (23) and (24) show that the constructed flag has mass exactly the halfspace depth, so it is minimizing.

If \(B_0\ne\mathbb R^n\), apply the lifting part of (9) once more from
\(E_0=B_0\times\mathbb R^d\) to \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). This inserts only support-null pure-base padding layers and gives a literal minimizing flag in the original ambient space.

The only conclusion about a pure base boundary is the structural containment (17). Formula (12) is additive and gives no lower bound for the boundary term. Accepted fact `314b32bda8a0b01e` requires, separately, a dyadic lower bound on the total mass of the primitive slice before its rank-drop conclusion can be used. Since no such estimate is proved here, (8) and the corresponding induction step remain unavailable.

Definition (3), pp. 3–4: in a \(p\)-dimensional affine space a flag halfspace at \(y\) is \(\{y\}\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^p G_k\), where \(G_p\) is an open halfspace whose boundary contains \(y\), and each \(G_k\) is a relatively open halfspace at \(y\) in the relative boundary of \(G_{k+1}\). Theorem 1, equation (5), pp. 4–6: for every finite Borel measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R^p\) and every \(y\), \(D(y;\nu)=\min\{\nu(F):F\in\mathcal F(y)\}\), so a minimizing flag always exists. Recursive formula (12), p. 6: the proof constructs the first open layer \(G_p\) so that \(D(y;\nu)=\nu(G_p)+D(y;\nu|_{\operatorname{relbd}(G_p)})\), and then repeats the same identity in that relative boundary. Applicability: (1) is a finite Borel measure, every space \(E_j\) is finite-dimensional and may be affinely identified with a Euclidean space, and restriction to a relative boundary is again a finite Borel measure. The source theorem therefore applies at every stage used above.
