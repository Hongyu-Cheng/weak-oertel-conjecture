## Statement

Take $A=[-1/2,1/2]^2$, $B=[-1,1]\times[-1/4,1/4]$, $M=(A+B)/2$, and $K=\operatorname{conv}(M\cup\{(-17/3,0)\})$. The lifted convex hull of $A,K,B$ at $-1,0,1$ has exactly those integer fibers, with $k=|K|=95/32$. The closed ambient cap $s+z/2\ge0$ through the averaged endpoint-centroid point $(0,0,0)$ has raw mixed area $21/16=(k+1)/3-1/96$. The generic ordinary-centroid lower bound $(4/9)k$ is $(k+1)/3-1/288$.

## Proof

The extreme levels force their own rectangles. At level zero any mixture with nonzero total endpoint weights uses equal weights on both endpoints and is a convex combination of a point of $M$ and a point of $K$. This lies in $K$ because $M\subseteq K$; lifting all of $K$ gives the reverse inclusion. The added triangle attaches to the side $s=-3/4$ of length $3/4$, has altitude $17/3-3/4=59/12$, and has area $59/32$. Thus $k=9/8+59/32$. The triangle lies in $s\le-3/4$, so the middle cap area at $s\ge0$ remains $9/16$. The endpoints contribute $0$ and $3/4$ as follows by clipping at $s=1/2$ and $s=-1/2$, respectively. Their centroid average is the origin. The target is $127/96$, the cap area is $126/96$, and $(4/9)k=95/72$.
