## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge6\), set
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 a&=1,& b&=k2^k,& t&=2^{-k}\log2,\\
 m&=2^{2k+8}b^2,& n&=m+1,& K&=2^n,& r&=\frac{m}{8b}.
 \end{aligned}
$$
All of \(m\), \(m/2\), and \(r\) are integers. In coordinates \((x,z)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R\), put
$$
 \mathcal U=
 \left\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m:
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^m u_i-\frac m2\right|\le r\right\rbrace,
 \qquad N=|\mathcal U|,\qquad j_\star=(0,b),
$$
and define the lattice polytope
$$
 Q=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
       (\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
       \cup(\mathcal U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
       \cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2b\rbrace)\bigr).
$$
Then \(Q\) is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^n\), and its entire lattice support is
$$
 A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n
  =(\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
    \cup(\mathcal U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
    \cup\lbrace j_\star\rbrace
    \cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2b\rbrace).
 \tag{1}
$$
The primitive integer functional \(H(x,z)=z\) has exactly the occupied heights
\(\lbrace0,a,b,2b\rbrace\), with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(0<a<b/4\). Every ambient parity class contains at most two points of \(A\). Both heavy levels have affine dimension \(n-1\). Moreover, in the notation of accepted fact 2fb7953b22f4b674,
$$
 C_{\mathrm H}=2N,\qquad C_b=1,\qquad J=N.
 \tag{2}
$$

Use the unchanged raw law \(w_v=e^{-tH(v)}\), normalizer \(Z=\sum_{v\in A}w_v\), and probabilities \(p_v=w_v/Z\), and put \(\lambda=e^{-t}=2^{-1/2^k}\) and \(\beta=e^{-tb}=2^{-k}\). Thus \(tb=k\log2\ge\log64\), \(ta<\log64\), and
$$
 Z=N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+\beta.
 \tag{3}
$$
With the same-weight selected rows
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat R_j
   &=\lbrace v\in A:2j-v\in A,\ H(v)=H(j)\rbrace,\\
 \widehat H_p(j)
   &=\sum_{v\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_vp_{2j-v}},\\
 b_{\mathrm{equal}}
   &=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2
 \end{aligned}
$$
and the atomic quantity \(A_0=K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3\), exactly as in accepted fact 2fb7953b22f4b674, one has \(\widehat R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\) for every \(j\in A\), and
$$
 K^2b_{\mathrm{equal}}
 =A_0
 =\frac{K^2\bigl(N(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6)+\beta^3\bigr)}
        {\bigl(N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+\beta\bigr)^3}
 <1.
 \tag{4}
$$
Every noncentral full reflection pair is
\((u,0),(-u,2b)\), centered at \(j_\star\). Its absolute score is
$$
 \frac12\left|\log\frac{p_{(u,0)}}{p_{(-u,2b)}}\right|
 =tb=k\log2,
 \tag{5}
$$
which is unbounded along this family.

## Proof

Write \(L=m/2-r\) and \(U=m/2+r\), and let \(P=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal U)\). The chosen parameters give
\(1\le L<U\le m-1\), with \(L\) and \(U\) integers. In particular the Boolean vectors of weights \(L\) and \(L+1\) belong to \(\mathcal U\). The affine hull of the vectors of weight \(L\) has difference space
\(\lbrace x:\sum_i x_i=0\rbrace\): starting with any vector of weight \(L\), exchanges of a selected and an unselected coordinate give the vectors \(e_j-e_i\), whose span is that space. Adding a vector of weight \(L+1\) enlarges the affine hull to \(\mathbb R^m\). Hence \(P\) has dimension \(m\). The polytope \(Q\) contains \(P\times[0,1]\), so it has dimension \(m+1=n\).

The set \(P\) is contained in
$$
 [0,1]^m\cap\lbrace x:L\le\sum_i x_i\le U\rbrace.
 \tag{6}
$$
Every integer point in (6) belongs to \(\mathcal U\), and every point of \(\mathcal U\) belongs to \(P\). Thus \(P\cap\mathbb Z^m=\mathcal U\).

We now check all lattice points of \(Q\), including boundary points and all intermediate integer heights. Every point of \(Q\) can be written
$$
 \begin{gathered}
 (x,z)=(\alpha u+\gamma v-\theta w,\ \gamma+2b\theta),\\
 u,v,w\in P,\qquad
 \alpha,\gamma,\theta\ge0,\qquad
 \alpha+\gamma+\theta=1.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{7}
$$
If \(\theta=0\), then \(x\in P\) and \(0\le z\le1\). Its integer points are exactly the two lower layers in (1). If \(\theta=1\), the integer points are exactly \(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2b\rbrace\).

Suppose \(0<\theta<1\) and (7) is integral. Coordinatewise, \(-\theta\le x_i\le1-\theta\). Both endpoints lie strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\), so every \(x_i=0\). Taking the coordinate sum in (7), and using (6), gives \((1-\theta)L\le\theta U\) and \(\theta L\le(1-\theta)U\). Consequently \(L/(L+U)\le\theta\le U/(L+U)\). Since \(0\le\gamma\le1-\theta\), it follows that
$$
 \frac{2bL}{L+U}
 \le z\le
 1+\frac{(2b-1)U}{L+U}.
 \tag{8}
$$
Here \(L+U=m\) and \(r/m=1/(8b)\), so (8) becomes
$$
 b-\frac14\le z\le b+\frac34-\frac1{8b}.
 \tag{9}
$$
The only integer in this closed interval is \(b\). Conversely,
\(j_\star=(0,b)\) is the midpoint of \((u,0)\) and \((-u,2b)\) for every \(u\in\mathcal U\), so it belongs to \(Q\). This proves the complete census (1). It also directly verifies the hub symmetry: the affine reflection
\((x,z)\mapsto(-x,2b-z)\) interchanges the two generating layers of
$$
 \operatorname{conv}\bigl(
  (\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)\cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2b\rbrace)\bigr)
 \subseteq Q
$$
and fixes \(j_\star\).

The functional \(H\) is primitive because \(H(0,1)=1\). Every listed level is nonempty, and the inequalities involving \(a\), \(b\), and \(t\) in the statement follow from \(k\ge6\). The convex hulls of the two heavy lattice levels are copies of \(P\), so they both have dimension \(m\).

Distinct Boolean vectors have distinct coordinate parities. The layers at heights \(0\) and \(2b\) are even in the last coordinate, and their points of the same parity are exactly
$$
 \lbrace(u,0),(-u,2b)\rbrace,\qquad u\in\mathcal U.
 \tag{10}
$$
The height-one layer gives \(N\) different parity classes with odd last coordinate. Since \(b\) is even, the hub has even last coordinate and horizontal parity zero. The zero vector does not belong to \(\mathcal U\), because \(L>0\). Its parity class is therefore different from every class in (10), and from every heavy height-one class. This lists all occupied ambient classes, proves occupancy at most two, and proves (2).

Each individual height level is injective modulo \(2\mathbb Z^n\). If two points in one such level reflect about an integer point, they have the same parity and must therefore coincide. Thus every same-weight selected row is the singleton consisting of its own center. All central atoms are retained, and \(\widehat H_p(j)=p_j\). Also (10) lists all pairs of distinct points with an integer midpoint, and their midpoint is always \(j_\star\). This proves all asserted row identities and (5).

It remains to prove strict activity in the original ambient normalization. We first obtain an exact lower bound for \(N\). Under the uniform distribution on \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), the coordinate sum has mean \(m/2\) and variance \(m/4\). These follow directly by expanding the square and using that the distinct coordinate cross terms have zero centered expectation. Each excluded Boolean vector has squared deviation greater than \(r^2\). Therefore
$$
 1-\frac{N}{2^m}
 \le\frac{m}{4r^2}
 =\frac{16b^2}{m}
 =2^{-2k-4}
 =\frac{\beta^2}{16}.
 \tag{11}
$$
Set \(\varepsilon=\beta^2/16\) and \(\vartheta=N/2^m\). Thus \(N\ge1\),
\(1-\varepsilon\le\vartheta\le1\), and
\(1-\vartheta^2\le2\varepsilon\).

The inequality
$$
 \left(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\right)^{2^k}
 =\left(1+\frac1{2^k-1}\right)^{2^k}
 >1+\frac{2^k}{2^k-1}>2
$$
follows from the binomial expansion. Since \(\lambda^{2^k}=1/2\), it proves
$$
 1-\beta<\lambda<1,\qquad
 \lambda>\frac{63}{64},\qquad
 0<\beta\le\frac1{64}.
 \tag{12}
$$
Define, only for the following scalar comparison, \(\Delta=(1+\lambda+\beta^2)^3-4(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6)\). Expansion and factorization give the exact identity
$$
 \Delta
 =3(1+\lambda)
       \bigl(\beta^2(1+\lambda)-(1-\lambda)^2\bigr)
   +3\beta^4(1+\lambda-\beta^2).
 \tag{13}
$$
By (12), \((1-\lambda)^2<\beta^2\). The first term in (13) is therefore greater than
\(3\beta^2\lambda(1+\lambda)\), and the second is positive. Since
\(\lambda(1+\lambda)>1\), we have
$$
 \Delta>3\beta^2.
 \tag{14}
$$
Also
$$
 (1+\lambda+\beta^2)^3
 <\left(2+\frac1{4096}\right)^3<9.
 \tag{15}
$$
Using (11), (14), and (15), and retaining the hub's cubic contribution, yields
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 &\vartheta^2(1+\lambda+\beta^2)^3
       -4\left(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6+\frac{\beta^3}{N}\right)\\
 &\quad=
 \Delta-(1-\vartheta^2)(1+\lambda+\beta^2)^3
          -\frac{4\beta^3}{N}\\
 &\quad>3\beta^2-18\varepsilon-4\beta^3\\
 &\quad\ge
 \left(3-\frac{18}{16}-\frac1{16}\right)\beta^2
 =\frac{29}{16}\beta^2>0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
$$
The last inequality uses \(N\ge1\), \(\varepsilon=\beta^2/16\), and \(\beta\le1/64\). Since \(K=2^{m+1}\), one has \(K^2/N^2=4/\vartheta^2\). The exact expression in (4) consequently satisfies
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 K^2b_{\mathrm{equal}}=A_0
 &=\frac{4}{\vartheta^2}
   \frac{1+\lambda^3+\beta^6+\beta^3/N}
        {(1+\lambda+\beta^2+\beta/N)^3}\\
 &\le
 \frac{4(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6+\beta^3/N)}
      {\vartheta^2(1+\lambda+\beta^2)^3}
 <1,
 \end{aligned}
$$
where the final strict inequality is (16). This is computed with the full normalizer (3) and the original ambient capacity \(K\); neither a conditional law nor an occupied-class replacement is used. Equations (1)-(16) prove every claimed property and the actual selected-energy deficit.
