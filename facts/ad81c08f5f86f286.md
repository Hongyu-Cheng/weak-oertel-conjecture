## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a smooth strictly convex body and $c\in\operatorname{int}K$. There is a smooth strictly positive outward normal speed $\psi$ on $\partial K$ with $\int_{\partial K}\psi\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}=1$ and $\int_{\partial K\cap H}\psi\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}=1/2$ for every closed halfspace $H$ bounded by a hyperplane through $c$.

## Proof

For $d\ge2$ write $X(\omega)=c+R(\omega)\omega$ for the radial parametrization, and let $n$ be the outward unit normal at $X(\omega)$. Since $c$ lies strictly on the interior side of the supporting plane, $(X(\omega)-c)\cdot n>0$. Thus the radial map and its inverse are smooth by transversality. The derivative of $x\mapsto(x-c)/\lVert x-c\rVert$ on the tangent hyperplane is orthogonal projection to $\omega^\perp$ divided by $R(\omega)$. Its tangential Jacobian is $(n\cdot\omega)/R(\omega)^{d-1}$. Therefore $\psi(X(\omega))=(n\cdot\omega)/(|\mathbb S^{d-1}|R(\omega)^{d-1})$ pushes the normalized spherical measure forward to $\psi\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$. A halfspace through $c$ corresponds to a hemisphere, whose boundary has zero spherical measure. In $d=1$ put speed $1/2$ at each endpoint and use counting measure.
