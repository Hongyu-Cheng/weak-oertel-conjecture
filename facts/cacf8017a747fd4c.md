## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,
\]
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\tag{1}
\]
For a positive-volume bounded measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<m\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), put
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{\frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\quad 0\leq f\leq1\ {\rm a.e.},\quad
 \int_Kf(x)\,dx=m\right\}.
\]
For \(z\in Z\), define
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
 \qquad
 D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\]
Here \(G(C)\) is the global complete metronoid-core capacity used in accepted facts `c81f02314cb34f8b` and `8aac56c49321466c`. For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}a_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Define the strong canonical product matching by
\[
 q^\times_{\{u,v\}}
 :=\frac{\gamma_d a_ua_v}{A_\xi}
 \quad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2),\ A_\xi>0),
\tag{3}
\]
and set all other entries to zero. In the notation of accepted fact `c81f02314cb34f8b`, this is exactly \(q^\times=2\gamma_dp^\times\).

Use lower-case \(a_z\) here for the quantity denoted \(A_z\) in accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884`. If \(q^\times=0\), put \(\Omega^\times=0\), \(F_+=\varnothing\), and leave the stress family empty. If \(q^\times\ne0\), choose any load-minimal optimal stress and its contact data as in that fact. For \(e=\{z,z'\}\), put \(D_e:=(D_z+D_{z'})/2\), and write
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),\qquad
 \Omega^\times:=\sum_e\omega_e,\qquad
 F_+:=\{e:\omega_e>0\},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(F_+\) is the set denoted \(F_\omega\) there. In the positive-load case \(\Omega^\times>0\), retain the canonical contact quantities \(\lambda_{e,z},u_{e,z},x_{e,z}\) from `3f9592d2fb367884`. At a loaded endpoint \(z\in e\), put
\[
 t_z:=\sum_{e\in F_+: e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z},\qquad
 \widetilde p_z:=\frac1{t_z}\sum_{e\in F_+: e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}x_{e,z},
\]
\[
 U_z:=\sum_{e\in F_+: e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}u_{e,z},\qquad
 \mathcal I_z^\times
 :=t_z-\langle U_z,\widetilde p_z-c_z\rangle,\qquad
 \mathcal I^\times:=\sum_{z:t_z>0}\mathcal I_z^\times.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal I^\times\) is exactly the same-body interaction \(\mathcal I\) of `3f9592d2fb367884`, specialized to \(q^\times\).

For \(e=\{z,a\}\), define its half-direction at the endpoint \(z\) by
\[
 h_{e,z}:=\frac{a-z}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Let \(\mathcal A_2\) be the family of unordered pairs \(\{e,f\}\subseteq F_+\) of distinct edges for which \(e\cap f=\{z\}\) and \(h_{e,z},h_{f,z}\) are linearly independent. Define \(P_{\mathrm{rk2}}\) as the optimum of
\[
 \max\left\{
 \sum_{\{e,f\}\in\mathcal A_2}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}:
 \vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\geq0,\quad
 \sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal A_2}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
 \leq\omega_e\quad(e\in F_+)
 \right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
For any optimizer in (7), set
\[
 c_e:=\sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal A_2}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}},
 \qquad
 r_e:=\omega_e-c_e,
 \qquad
 R:=\sum_{e\in F_+}r_e.
\tag{8}
\]
Then every connected component of the graph with edge set \(\{e\in F_+:r_e>0\}\) is contained in an affine lattice line, and
\[
 \boxed{R=\Omega^\times-2P_{\mathrm{rk2}}\leq2G(C).}
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{P_{\mathrm{rk2}}+G(C)\geq\frac14\mathcal I^\times.}
\tag{10}
\]
If \(q^\times=0\) or \(\Omega^\times=0\), interpret \(P_{\mathrm{rk2}}=\mathcal I^\times=0\); then (9)-(10) remain true.

In particular, with the canonical nonnegative capacities \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C),M_{\mathrm{off}}(C),R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) of accepted fact `c1e3b8400d5b5b10` and \(h_{\max}(C)\) of accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884`,
\[
 P_{\mathrm{rk2}}+G(C)+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq\frac14\mathcal I^\times.
\tag{11}
\]

## Proof

First check the product normalization rather than importing it across scales. For \(u\equiv\xi\) with \(A_\xi>0\), direct summation in (3) gives
\[
 d_u(q^\times)
 =\sum_{\substack{v\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)\\v\ne u}}
 \frac{\gamma_da_ua_v}{A_\xi}
 =\gamma_da_u\left(1-\frac{a_u}{A_\xi}\right)
 \leq\gamma_da_u.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(q^\times\) satisfies the strong degree hypothesis of accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884`. The zero cases in the statement are immediate, so assume \(q^\times\ne0\) and \(\Omega^\times>0\). That fact then gives
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq q_e^\times,
 \qquad
 \sum_z t_z=\Omega^\times,
 \qquad
 h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})=1.
\tag{13}
\]

The feasible region of (7) is a nonempty compact polytope, so an optimizer exists. Each pairing variable is charged once to each of its two source edges. Hence
\[
 \sum_ec_e=2P_{\mathrm{rk2}},
 \qquad
 R=\sum_e(\omega_e-c_e)=\Omega^\times-2P_{\mathrm{rk2}}.
\tag{14}
\]
Suppose \(e,f\) share \(z\), both \(r_e,r_f\) are positive, and \(h_{e,z},h_{f,z}\) are linearly independent. Increasing \(\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\) by any positive number at most \(\min\{r_e,r_f\}\) preserves every constraint in (7) and strictly increases its objective. This contradicts optimality. Therefore, at each base vertex, all incident positive-residual edge directions are pairwise linearly dependent.

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) be an edge path in one residual component. At each common path vertex, the directions of two consecutive edges are parallel. The direction of one edge at its other endpoint is its negative, so induction along the path shows that all edge differences in the component belong to one fixed one-dimensional linear subspace. Each component contains an edge and hence two distinct integer points. It is therefore contained in the affine lattice line through the endpoints of any one of its edges. This proves the component assertion in the statement. Every component also lies in one parity class because all edges of \(q^\times\) are same-parity.

We next derive the physical-mass bound for one such line from accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c`. Let \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be an affine line containing a residual component and put
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in Z\cap L}a_z.
\tag{15}
\]
The component contains two distinct positive-fiber bases, so \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(Z\cap L)=1\). If \(n>1\), equation (15) of `8aac56c49321466c` with \(q=1\) and \(\alpha_1=1/4\) gives
\[
 G(C)\geq
 \min\left\{\frac{W_L}{4}+\frac{W-W_L}{2},\ 2^{n-3}W_L\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(n=1\), then \(L=\mathbb R\), \(W_L=W\), and equation (11) of `8aac56c49321466c` gives \(G(C)\geq W/4\), which is again (16). Since
\[
 \frac{W_L}{4}+\frac{W-W_L}{2}
 =\frac W2-\frac{W_L}{4}\geq\frac W4,
\tag{17}
\]
the regime \(G(C)<W/4\) forces
\[
 W_L\leq2^{3-n}G(C)
\tag{18}
\]
for every line carrying a residual component.

Fix a parity class \(\xi\), enumerate the positive-residual components in it by \(j\), let \(V_{\xi,j}\) be their pairwise disjoint vertex sets, and put
\[
 B_{\xi,j}:=\sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z,
 \qquad
 R_\xi:=\sum_{\substack{e:r_e>0\\e\text{ lies in parity }\xi}}r_e.
\tag{19}
\]
The disjointness gives \(\sum_jB_{\xi,j}\leq A_\xi\). Using \(r_e\leq\omega_e\leq q_e^\times\) and then summing over all possible unordered pairs inside each component gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_\xi
 &\leq\sum_j\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V_{\xi,j}}q^\times_{\{u,v\}}\\
 &=\frac{\gamma_d}{2A_\xi}
 \sum_j\left(B_{\xi,j}^2-\sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z^2\right)\\
 &\leq\frac{\gamma_d}{2A_\xi}\sum_jB_{\xi,j}^2\\
 &\leq\frac{\gamma_d}{2A_\xi}
 \left(\max_jB_{\xi,j}\right)\sum_jB_{\xi,j}
 \leq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\max_jB_{\xi,j}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The inequality is vacuous when the class has no residual edge.

Assume first that \(G(C)<W/4\). Every \(V_{\xi,j}\) lies on a line \(L_{\xi,j}\), and therefore
\[
 B_{\xi,j}\leq W_{L_{\xi,j}}\leq2^{3-n}G(C).
\]
Together with (20), this implies
\[
 R_\xi\leq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{3-n}G(C)
 =4\gamma_d2^{-n}G(C).
\tag{21}
\]
There are \(2^n\) parity classes. Also,
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 =\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk d^{-k}\geq2,
\]
so \(\gamma_d\leq1/2\). Summing (21) therefore yields
\[
 R=\sum_\xi R_\xi\leq4\gamma_dG(C)\leq2G(C).
\tag{22}
\]
This estimate is independent of the number of residual components and of the directions of their supporting lines.

Assume instead that \(G(C)\geq W/4\). Equations (12)-(13) give
\[
 R\leq\Omega^\times\leq|q^\times|
 =\frac12\sum_zd_z(q^\times)
 \leq\frac{\gamma_dW}{2}
 \leq\frac W4
 \leq G(C).
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (22)-(23), together with (14), prove (9) in every case.

It remains to convert load to interaction. Accepted fact `3f9592d2fb367884` gives at each loaded endpoint
\[
 \mathcal I_z^\times\leq t_z+\tau_z,
 \qquad
 \tau_z:=h_{D_z}(U_z).
\tag{24}
\]
By subadditivity and positive homogeneity of the support function, (5) and (13) give
\[
 \tau_z
 =h_{D_z}\left(\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}u_{e,z}\right)
 \leq\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})
 =t_z.
\tag{25}
\]
Summing (24)-(25) and using (13) gives
\[
 \mathcal I^\times\leq2\sum_zt_z=2\Omega^\times.
\tag{26}
\]
Finally, (9), (14), and (26) imply
\[
 \mathcal I^\times
 \leq2(2P_{\mathrm{rk2}}+R)
 \leq4P_{\mathrm{rk2}}+4G(C)
 =4\bigl(P_{\mathrm{rk2}}+G(C)\bigr),
\tag{27}
\]
which is (10). The capacities \(R_{\mathrm{sel}},M_{\mathrm{off}},R_{\mathrm{cloud}}\) in accepted fact `c1e3b8400d5b5b10` are suprema of nonnegative scores, and \(h_{\max}(C)\geq0\) by its definition in `3f9592d2fb367884`. Adding these terms to (10) proves (11). No step compares a nonzero angle to a metric threshold, and the proof uses no compatibility between distinct local rank-two germs.
