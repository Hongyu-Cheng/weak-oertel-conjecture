## Statement

For a convex body
\[
B\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^d
\]
of positive mixed mass, write
\[
b_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z),
\qquad
W_B:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^k}b_z,
\]
and let
\[
h_{\max}(B):=
\max_{p\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^d)}h_B(p).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \({\rm WO}(c)\) denote the assertion that
\[
h_{\max}(B)\geq c\,2^{-k}W_B
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(k,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and every such \(B\).

Consider now a genuine cardinality-two empty-core datum from accepted fact
`ebc478d91566cb69`.  Thus
\[
C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^D
\]
is a convex body of positive mixed mass, the datum has a weighted lattice
centerpoint \(m\), and, after the standard positive rescaling of its two
normal-threshold pairs, its inclusion-minimal balanced circuit has
\[
v_+=u,
\qquad
v_-=-u,
\qquad
\theta_+=\theta_-=\frac12,
\qquad
u\neq0.
\tag{3}
\]
Retain the affine minorants \(\ell_+,\ell_-\), the caps \(G_+,G_-\), and the
proper covering base halfspace \(Q\ni m\) supplied by that accepted fact.  For
\[
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_D(C_z),
\qquad
W_C:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\qquad
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}.
\]
For \(z\in E\), put
\[
f_\pm(z):=
\operatorname{vol}_D\bigl(C_z\cap(G_\pm)_z\bigr),
\qquad
\Psi(z):=\sqrt{f_+(z)f_-(z)},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
S_Q:=\sum_{z\in E\cap Q}\Psi(z),
\qquad
S_{\rm all}:=\sum_{z\in E}\Psi(z),
\qquad
T:=\min\{S_Q,2^{-n}S_{\rm all}\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \({\rm TC2}(\beta)\) denote the assertion that every such genuine datum
satisfies
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\beta T.
\tag{6}
\]

Then, for every \(c,\beta>0\),
\[
{\rm WO}(c)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\rm TC2}(c),
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
{\rm TC2}(\beta)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\rm WO}\!\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}{56}\,\beta\right).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, the existence of an absolute positive constant in (6) is
equivalent to the existence of an absolute
\(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) in the weak Oertel statement.  If the constant on the
right of (8) is not below \(1/e\), one may replace it by any smaller positive
constant below \(1/e\).

The implication (8) is witnessed by the following explicit gadget.  Let
\(B\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^d\) be arbitrary, with \(W_B>0\),
and choose a weighted lattice centerpoint
\(m_B\in\mathbb Z^k\) of the masses \((b_z)\) as in accepted fact
`6d6fcf377e68c79c`.  With one new real base coordinate \(\tau\) and one new
continuous coordinate \(s\), define
\[
q(\tau):=4(1-|\tau-1|)
\qquad(0\leq\tau\leq2)
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
C:=
\left\{
(z,\tau;x,s):
(z,x)\in B,
\ 0\leq\tau\leq2,
\ -1\leq s-z_1\leq q(\tau)
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Put
\[
N:=k+1,
\qquad
D:=d+1,
\qquad
m:=(m_B,1),
\qquad
u:=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb R^D.
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(C\) is a convex body in
\(\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^D\), its positive-volume lattice support and
fiber volumes are
\[
E_C=E_B\times\{0,1,2\},
\qquad
a_{(z,0)}=b_z,
\quad
a_{(z,1)}=5b_z,
\quad
a_{(z,2)}=b_z,
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
W_C=7W_B.
\tag{13}
\]
The point \(m\) satisfies the exact base-centerpoint hypothesis in integer
dimension \(N\): every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{(z,j)\in E_C\cap J}a_{(z,j)}
\geq2^{-N}W_C.
\tag{14}
\]

For the half-mass metronoids of these fibers, let
\(h_{(z,j)}\) and \(\phi\) have the meanings in accepted fact
`ebc478d91566cb69`.  Their exact support values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
j&h_{(z,j)}(u)-z_1&h_{(z,j)}(-u)+z_1\\ \hline
0,2&-\frac14&\frac34\\[1mm]
1&\frac{11}{4}&-\frac14.
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\phi(u)=(m_B)_1-\frac14,
\qquad
\phi(-u)=-(m_B)_1-\frac14,
\qquad
\frac{\phi(u)+\phi(-u)}2=-\frac14<0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the half-mass core at \(m\) is empty, and
\(\{u,-u\}\), with weights \(1/2,1/2\), is an inclusion-minimal balanced
certificate.

The globally affine functions
\[
\ell_+(z,\tau):=z_1-\frac14,
\qquad
\ell_-(z,\tau):=-z_1+\tau-\frac54
\tag{17}
\]
are valid optimal linear-programming minorants, exact at \(m\).  Their caps
are
\[
G_+:=\{(z,\tau;x,s):s\geq z_1-\tfrac14\},
\tag{18}
\]
\[
G_-:=\{(z,\tau;x,s):s\leq z_1+\tfrac54-\tau\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Both caps are proper and genuinely oblique.  Their target-fiber boundaries
are the distinct parallel hyperplanes
\[
s=(m_B)_1-\frac14,
\qquad
s=(m_B)_1+\frac14.
\tag{20}
\]
The averaged threshold and its canonical base halfspace are
\[
L(z,\tau):=\frac{\ell_+(z,\tau)+\ell_-(z,\tau)}2
=\frac\tau2-\frac34,
\qquad
Q:=\{(z,\tau):\tau\leq\tfrac32\}.
\tag{21}
\]
They satisfy
\[
Q\times\mathbb R^D\subseteq G_+\cup G_-.
\tag{22}
\]
All cap boundaries have zero \(D\)-dimensional fiber volume, and the exact
profiles are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
j&f_+(z,j)/b_z&f_-(z,j)/b_z&\Psi(z,j)/b_z\\ \hline
0&\frac14&1&\frac12\\[1mm]
1&\frac{17}{4}&\frac54&\frac{\sqrt{85}}4\\[1mm]
2&\frac14&\frac14&\frac14.
\end{array}
\tag{23}
\]
In particular,
\[
S_Q=\frac{2+\sqrt{85}}4W_B,
\qquad
S_{\rm all}=\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}4W_B,
\tag{24}
\]
and the normalized score of this gadget is exactly
\[
T_C
=2^{-N}S_{\rm all}
=\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}4\,2^{-(k+1)}W_B.
\tag{25}
\]
The true depths obey
\[
h_{\max}(C)\leq7h_{\max}(B).
\tag{26}
\]

Corollary, unnormalized aggregate-energy no-go.  There is no absolute
\(\eta>0\) for which every genuine two-normal datum satisfies either
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\eta S_Q
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
h_{\max}(C)\geq\eta S_{\rm all}.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, for the gadgets obtained from
\[
B_k:=[0,1]^k\times[0,1]
\qquad(k\geq1),
\tag{28}
\]
one has
\[
h_{\max}(C_k)\leq7,
\tag{29}
\]
whereas
\[
S_{Q,k}=\frac{2+\sqrt{85}}4\,2^k,
\qquad
S_{{\rm all},k}=\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}4\,2^k.
\tag{30}
\]
Hence both ratios in (27) tend to zero.  On the other hand,
\[
T_{C_k}=\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}8,
\tag{31}
\]
and accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` gives
\[
h_{\max}(C_k)\geq\frac76.
\tag{32}
\]
Thus this corollary concerns only unnormalized aggregation and supplies no
counterexample to (6) or (2).

## Proof

We first note that the maxima in (1) and (6) are well-defined.  The mixed
measure of a compact convex body is a finite Borel measure supported on the
compact set obtained by intersecting the body with the closed mixed lattice.
Its halfspace-depth function is upper semicontinuous.  Indeed, if
\(p_j\to p\), choose, within any prescribed error, a proper closed halfspace
\(H\) containing \(p\), and translate its boundary parallel toward \(p\) so
that the boundary passes through \(p\), without increasing its measure.
Write the resulting halfspace as
\(H=\{x:\langle a,x\rangle\geq\langle a,p\rangle\}\), and put
\[
H_j:=\{x:\langle a,x\rangle\geq\langle a,p_j\rangle\}.
\]
For every \(\delta>0\), all sufficiently large \(j\) satisfy
\[
H_j\subseteq
\{x:\langle a,x\rangle\geq\langle a,p\rangle-\delta\}.
\]
The halfspaces on the right decrease to \(H\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).
Continuity from above of the finite measure therefore gives
\[
\limsup_j\mu(H_j)\leq\mu(H).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves upper semicontinuity.  Compactness then
gives a maximizer.

We prove (7).  In every genuine two-cap datum, each cap section is contained
in the full fiber, so
\[
f_+(z)\leq a_z,
\qquad
f_-(z)\leq a_z,
\qquad
\Psi(z)\leq a_z.
\tag{33}
\]
Therefore
\[
T\leq2^{-n}S_{\rm all}\leq2^{-n}W_C.
\tag{34}
\]
If \({\rm WO}(c)\) holds, applying it to \(C\) and using (34) gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq c2^{-n}W_C
\geq cT.
\]
This is \({\rm TC2}(c)\).

We next construct and verify the gadget.  The function \(q\) in (9) is
nonnegative and concave on \([0,2]\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal D:=
\{(\tau,t):0\leq\tau\leq2,\ -1\leq t\leq q(\tau)\}
\tag{35}
\]
is a compact full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^2\).  The map
\[
(z,x;\tau,t)\longmapsto(z,\tau;x,s=t+z_1)
\tag{36}
\]
is an invertible, base-preserving affine shear.  It maps
\(B\times\mathcal D\) onto (10).  Thus \(C\) is compact, convex, and
full-dimensional.  The only integer values of \(\tau\) in \([0,2]\) are
\(0,1,2\), and the corresponding \(t\)-interval lengths are \(1,5,1\).
The shear is a translation on every continuous fiber.  This proves
(12)-(13), including the assertion that there are no other positive-volume
lattice fibers.

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite measure
\[
\nu_B:=\sum_{z\in E_B}b_z\delta_z
\tag{37}
\]
on \(\mathbb Z^k\).  It supplies \(m_B\in\mathbb Z^k\) such that every
proper closed halfspace \(J_B\ni m_B\) has
\[
\nu_B(J_B)\geq2^{-k}W_B.
\tag{38}
\]
Moreover, \(m_B\in\operatorname{conv}E_B\): otherwise strict separation
would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(m_B\) and disjoint from
the support, contradicting (38).  In particular,
\(m=(m_B,1)\in\operatorname{conv}E_C\), as required for the barycentric
core.

Let \(J\subsetneq\mathbb R^{k+1}\) be a proper closed halfspace containing
\(m=(m_B,1)\).  Its section
\[
J_1:=\{z:(z,1)\in J\}
\]
is either all of \(\mathbb R^k\) or a proper closed halfspace containing
\(m_B\).  In both cases (38), with the all-space case immediate, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{(z,j)\in E_C\cap J}a_{(z,j)}
&\geq5\sum_{z\in E_B\cap J_1}b_z\\
&\geq5\,2^{-k}W_B\\
&\geq7\,2^{-(k+1)}W_B
=2^{-N}W_C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
This proves (14).  It also shows directly that the chosen target base point
has the precise centerpoint property required in the accepted balanced-cap
theorem.

We compute the half-mass metronoid supports.  Over a positive base point
\((z,j)\), the fiber is
\[
C_{(z,j)}=B_z\times[z_1-1,z_1+q(j)].
\tag{40}
\]
Its cross-sectional \(d\)-volume at each \(s\)-level is the constant \(b_z\).
For an admissible density \(g\) of half the fiber mass, put
\[
\rho(s):=\int_{B_z}g(x,s)\,dx.
\]
Then \(0\leq\rho(s)\leq b_z\), and its integral is half the mass of the
interval product.  Moving any available density from a lower \(s\)-level to
a higher one does not decrease its \(u\)-moment.  Thus the maximum
\(u\)-moment is attained by filling the upper half of the interval, and the
maximum \((-u)\)-moment is attained by filling the lower half.  Equivalently,
for an interval \([A,A+L]\), the two support values are
\[
A+\frac{3L}{4}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-A-\frac{L}{4}.
\tag{41}
\]
For \(j=0,2\), one has \(A=z_1-1\) and \(L=1\); for \(j=1\), one has the
same \(A\) and \(L=5\).  Formula (41) proves the table (15).

Let \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_C)\), and let \(\lambda_j\) be its total
coefficient on layer \(j\).  Its two base-barycenter conditions give
\[
\lambda_0+\lambda_1+\lambda_2=1,
\qquad
\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=1,
\qquad
\lambda_0=\lambda_2,
\tag{42}
\]
and its \(z\)-barycenter condition gives
\[
\sum_{(z,j)}\lambda_{(z,j)}z_1=(m_B)_1.
\tag{43}
\]
Using (15),
\[
\sum_{(z,j)}\lambda_{(z,j)}h_{(z,j)}(u)
=(m_B)_1-\frac14+3\lambda_1,
\tag{44}
\]
\[
\sum_{(z,j)}\lambda_{(z,j)}h_{(z,j)}(-u)
=-(m_B)_1+\frac34-\lambda_1.
\tag{45}
\]
Both relevant endpoints of \(\lambda_1\) are feasible.  Choose any convex
representation of \(m_B\) by \(E_B\).  Placing that representation entirely
on layer \(1\) gives \(\lambda_1=1\), while splitting it equally between
layers \(0\) and \(2\) gives \(\lambda_1=0\).  Minimizing (44)-(45) proves
(16).

The support-envelope identity in accepted fact `ebc478d91566cb69` says that
the half-mass core is contained simultaneously in
\[
\{(x,s):s\leq(m_B)_1-\tfrac14\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{(x,s):s\geq(m_B)_1+\tfrac14\}.
\tag{46}
\]
These halfspaces are disjoint, so the core is empty.  The two normals balance
with the positive weights \(1/2,1/2\), and (16) gives the strict Farkas
inequality.  No singleton containing a nonzero normal can balance positively
to zero, and each individual support halfspace in (46) is nonempty.  Hence
the circuit is inclusion-minimal and has cardinality exactly two.  This
verifies that the construction is genuinely in the required empty-core branch
rather than merely carrying two arbitrarily chosen caps.

We now check the affine minorants.  The table (15) gives, for every
\((z,j)\in E_C\),
\[
\ell_+(z,j)\leq h_{(z,j)}(u),
\qquad
\ell_-(z,j)\leq h_{(z,j)}(-u).
\tag{47}
\]
At \(m=(m_B,1)\), equations (16)-(17) give
\[
\ell_+(m)=\phi(u),
\qquad
\ell_-(m)=\phi(-u).
\tag{48}
\]
Thus these globally affine functions are feasible dual minorants and are
optimal because they attain the primal values at \(m\).  Substituting them
into the cap definition of the accepted fact gives exactly (18)-(19).
The full normal of each cap has a nonzero base component and a nonzero
continuous component, so both caps are proper and genuinely oblique.
At the target base point, their boundaries are (20), proving
nonconcurrence directly.

Averaging the two minorants proves the formula for \(L\) in (21).  It is
nonconstant, \(L(m)=-1/4<0\), and hence the canonical choice
\(Q=\{L\leq0\}\) is the proper halfspace displayed there.  If
\(\tau\leq3/2\), then
\[
z_1-\frac14\leq z_1+\frac54-\tau.
\tag{49}
\]
Every real \(s\) is therefore at least the left threshold or at most the
right threshold.  This proves the full ambient cover (22), not just a cover
inside \(C\).

It remains to compute the profiles.  Write \(t=s-z_1\).  The two caps become
\[
G_+:t\geq-\frac14,
\qquad
G_-:t\leq\frac54-\tau.
\tag{50}
\]
Intersecting these two half-lines with \([-1,0]\) on layers \(0,2\), and
with \([-1,4]\) on layer \(1\), gives the first two columns of (23).  The
cap boundaries are single \(s\)-levels and hence have zero \(D\)-dimensional
fiber volume.  Taking geometric means gives the last column of (23).

For completeness, these caps also meet the pointwise quantitative conclusion
of the accepted branch.  Their smallest relative cap volume is \(1/4\).
Since
\[
\gamma_D:=\left(\frac D{D+1}\right)^D\leq\frac12,
\tag{51}
\]
one has \(f_\pm(z,j)\geq(\gamma_D/2)a_{(z,j)}\) on every positive fiber.
The inequality in (51) follows from
\((1+1/D)^D\geq2\), with equality only when \(D=1\).

The halfspace \(Q\) contains exactly the positive integer layers \(0,1\).
Summing the last column of (23) proves (24).  Since \(N=k+1\geq2\),
\[
2^{-N}S_{\rm all}<S_Q,
\tag{52}
\]
and (25) follows.

We next prove the true-depth comparison (26).  Undo the shear (36), and take
any mixed point
\[
p=(z,j;x,t)\in(B\times\mathcal D)
\cap(\mathbb Z^{k+1}\times\mathbb R^{d+1}).
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a closed halfspace \(H_B\) containing
\((z,x)\) whose \(B\)-mixed mass is at most
\[
h_B(z,x)+\varepsilon.
\]
Lift \(H_B\) to the product by ignoring \(\tau,t\).  On the three integer
layers its section masses are respectively \(1,5,1\) times the corresponding
section mass in \(B\).  Hence its mixed mass in \(B\times\mathcal D\) is
\[
7\mu_B(H_B).
\tag{53}
\]
The shear maps this lifted halfspace to a halfspace containing the
corresponding point of \(C\), and it preserves every continuous fiber volume.
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives, pointwise,
\[
h_C(\text{shear}(p))\leq7h_B(z,x).
\]
Taking maxima proves (26).

Assume now \({\rm TC2}(\beta)\).  The verified gadget is a genuine datum, so
(6), (25), and (26) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
7h_{\max}(B)
&\geq h_{\max}(C)\\
&\geq\beta\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}4
2^{-(k+1)}W_B.
\end{aligned}
\]
Dividing by \(7\) yields
\[
h_{\max}(B)
\geq
\beta\frac{3+\sqrt{85}}{56}
2^{-k}W_B.
\tag{54}
\]
Because \(B\) was arbitrary, this proves (8).  Equations (7)-(8) prove the
claimed quantitative and existential equivalence.  They do not prove either
assertion independently.

Finally, take \(B_k\) from (28).  It has \(2^k\) unit-volume positive fibers,
so \(W_{B_k}=2^k\).  For every mixed point over a cube vertex \(z\), the
proper vertical halfspace whose base inequality exposes that vertex contains
only the fiber over \(z\).  Explicitly, one may use
\[
\left\{
y\in\mathbb R^k:
\sum_{i=1}^k(2z_i-1)y_i
\geq
\sum_{i=1}^k(2z_i-1)z_i
\right\}.
\tag{55}
\]
It captures mixed mass one.  Therefore
\[
h_{\max}(B_k)\leq1.
\tag{56}
\]
Applying (24), (26), and (56) proves (29)-(30) and hence the two vanishing
unnormalized ratios.

For these gadgets the continuous dimension is \(D=2\), the integer dimension
is \(N=k+1\), and their total mixed mass is \(7\cdot2^k\).  Applying accepted
fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to their mixed-volume measures gives
\[
h_{\max}(C_k)
\geq
\frac{7\cdot2^k}{2^{k+1}(2+1)}
=\frac76.
\]
Together with (25), this proves (31)-(32).  Thus the cube family disproves
only a transfer from unnormalized aggregate energy.  Its normalized score
does not collapse, and neither \({\rm TC2}\) nor weak Oertel is refuted.
