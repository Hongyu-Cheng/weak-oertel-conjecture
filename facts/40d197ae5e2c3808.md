## Statement

Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer, let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\ge0\), let \(\beta>0\), and let \(0<T<\infty\). Put \(n=m+1\) and

\[
P:=\left\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m,t)\in\mathbb R^{m+1}:
0\le t\le T,\quad -a_it\le x_i\le1+a_it\ (1\le i\le m)\right\},
\qquad A:=P\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\]

For \(z=(x,t)\in\mathbb Z^n\), define the auxiliary scalar quantities

\[
\widetilde w_z:=
\begin{cases}
e^{-\beta t},&z\in A,\\
0,&z\notin A,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widetilde W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\widetilde w_z,
\qquad
\widetilde S_j:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\min\{\widetilde w_{j+v},\widetilde w_{j-v}\}.
\]

These auxiliary objects have exactly the meanings of \(\widetilde w_z,\widetilde W,\widetilde S_j,P,A\) in accepted fact `89138dbe4e583e2b` under the substitution \(\boldsymbol\lambda=(0,\ldots,0,-\beta)\in\mathbb R^n\).

Then there is an integer \(s\in\{0,\ldots,\lfloor T\rfloor\}\) such that every \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m\) gives a center \(j=(\varepsilon,s)\in A\) satisfying

\[
\widetilde S_j\ge
\frac{9}{544e}\,2^{-n}\widetilde W.
\]

In particular, this coefficient is independent of \(m,\beta,T\) and all the slopes.

There is also the following conditional statement for actual convex bodies. Let \(d\ge1\) be an integer, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\}\) and \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), taking zero volume for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Assume that the positive-volume integer-fiber support is exactly \(A\), and that there are numbers \(\rho>0\), \(M\ge1\) satisfying \((\rho/M)\widetilde w_z\le w_z\le\rho M\widetilde w_z\) for every \(z\in A\). Define \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). With \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\), there exists one point \(p^\star\in S\) such that

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge
\frac{9\ell}{8704e\,M^2(1+4\log M)}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

The actual-volume objects \(C_z,w_z,W,S,h_S\), the comparison parameters \(\rho,M\), and the constant \(\ell\) have exactly their meanings in accepted fact `89138dbe4e583e2b`. The tilded weights remain the auxiliary model and are not substituted for actual fiber volumes. The point \(p^\star\) is selected before the ambient halfspace: the same point satisfies the displayed bound for every closed ambient halfspace containing it. For every fixed \(M\), this positive coefficient is independent of \(m,d,\beta,T,a_1,\ldots,a_m,\rho\), and \(C\). This actual-body theorem assumes the stated support and comparison; it makes no assertion that such a convex body exists for arbitrary choices of the parameters or of \(d\). All logarithms are natural.

## Proof

We first prove the scalar assertion without using the accepted predecessor. The defining inequalities make \(P\) compact and convex, and \(A\) is finite and nonempty. Put \(N:=\lfloor T\rfloor\), \(q:=e^{-\beta}\in(0,1)\), and \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}:=\max_{j\in A}\widetilde S_j\). A positive reflected pair has its integer midpoint in \(A\) by convexity, so \(\widetilde S_j=0\) for \(j\notin A\). In particular, the maximum over all integer centers equals \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\).

For \(0\le t\le N\), with \(t\) integer, define \(B_i(t):=\lfloor a_it\rfloor+1\), \(B_t:=\prod_{i=1}^m B_i(t)\), and \(k(t):=\#\{i:B_i(t)\ge2\}\). The sequence \(k(t)\) is nondecreasing. The exact horizontal interval in coordinate \(i\) is
\(I_i(t):=\{-B_i(t)+1,\ldots,B_i(t)\}\), which has center \(1/2\) and cardinality \(2B_i(t)\). Thus the full slice has cardinality \(2^mB_t\). With
\(Z:=\sum_{t=0}^Nq^tB_t\), the normalization is \(\widetilde W=2^mZ\), and consequently \(2^{-n}\widetilde W=Z/2\).

We first give the horizontal counting facts used below. Fix an integer center height \(s\) and an integer \(h\ge0\) for which both heights \(s-h,s+h\) are allowed. For one coordinate, write \(b_-:=B_i(s-h)\) and \(b_+:=B_i(s+h)\), so \(b_-\le b_+\). At an integer horizontal center \(u_i\), the admissible displacements are
\((I_i(s+h)-u_i)\cap(u_i-I_i(s-h))\). These intervals have respective cardinalities \(2b_+,2b_-\), and their centers have distance \(|2u_i-1|\ge1\).

For two integer-endpoint intervals of these lengths and center distance \(\delta\), their intersection has cardinality
\(\max\{0,\min\{2b_-,b_-+b_+-\delta\}\}\). Indeed, in the containment case it has \(2b_-\) points, and in the partial-overlap case its length is \(b_-+b_+-1-\delta\), with one more integer point than this length when nonnegative. The intersection count is therefore maximized at \(u_i=0\) or \(u_i=1\). Its maximum is

\[
R_i(s,h):=\min\{2b_-,b_-+b_+-1\}
=
\begin{cases}
2b_--1,&b_-=b_+,\\
2b_-,&b_-<b_+.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]

For negative \(h\), use \(R_i(s,h):=R_i(s,-h)\). The horizontal coordinates are independent counting choices, and the minimum endpoint weight is \(q^{s+|h|}\). It follows that the same choices \(u_i\in\{0,1\}\) maximize every vertical-displacement contribution simultaneously. Consequently \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\) is attained at \((\mathbf0,s)\) for some \(0\le s\le N\), and at that height all the centers \((\varepsilon,s)\), \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m\), have the same mass.

Here is a local estimate on a height interval. Let \(J=\{l,l+1,\ldots,u\}\) be any nonempty integer interval in \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\), put \(A_J:=\{(x,t)\in A:t\in J\}\), and write \(Z_J:=\sum_{t\in J}q^tB_t\). For any integer center \(j\), let \(K_{J,j}\) be its unweighted reflected count in \(A_J\), namely \(\#\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v,j-v\in A_J\}\). Put \(K_J(s):=K_{J,(\mathbf0,s)}\) and \(K_J^{\max}:=\max_{s\in J}K_J(s)\). The horizontal maximization just proved applies also to this restricted support.

The map sending a reflected pair \((j,v)\) to its ordered endpoints \((j+v,j-v)\) is a bijection onto ordered endpoint pairs of equal coordinatewise parity. All their integer midpoints belong to \(A_J\), since \(A_J\) is the lattice intersection of the convex set \(P\cap\{l\le t\le u\}\). Therefore

\[
\sum_j K_{J,j}
=\sum_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^n}
\left|\{z\in A_J:z\equiv\eta\pmod2\}\right|^2
\ge 2^{-n}|A_J|^2.
\tag{2}
\]

The inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz on the \(2^n\) parity-class cardinalities. Since there are \(|A_J|\) possible centers with positive count and all counts are at most \(K_J^{\max}\), it follows that \(K_J^{\max}\ge2^{-n}|A_J|\).

We strengthen this when \(1\le l\le u\le2l\). Set \(r:=k(l)\) and \(\gamma:=3/4\). We claim

\[
K_J^{\max}\ge\gamma^{-r}2^{-n}|A_J|.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix \(s,h\) with \(s\pm h\in J\), and again take \(h\ge0\) by symmetry. For coordinate \(i\), put \(b:=B_i(s)\) and retain \(b_-,b_+\) above. Summing the admissible horizontal-displacement count over all integer horizontal centers gives exactly \(2b_-b_+\): each endpoint interval has \(b_-\), respectively \(b_+\), points of each parity, and equal-parity ordered endpoints correspond bijectively to an integer center and displacement.

By (1), the ratio of this sum to \((2b)R_i(s,h)\) is

\[
\frac{2b_-b_+}{(2b)R_i(s,h)}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{b}{2b-1},&b_-=b_+,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{b_+}{2b},&b_-<b_+.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

In the first case, monotonicity forces \(b_-=b=b_+\). In general the ratio is at most one. For the second case, this follows from \(s+h\le2s\) and \(a_is<b\), which give \(a_i(s+h)<2b\) and hence \(b_+\le2b\).

If \(B_i(l)\ge2\), then \(b\ge2\), and the ratio in (4) is at most \(\gamma=3/4\). To check the second case precisely, the endpoint bounds imply \(s+h\le2(s-h)\), hence \(h\le s/3\) and \(a_i(s+h)<4b/3\). Consequently
\(b_+\le\lceil4b/3\rceil\le3b/2\) for every integer \(b\ge2\). The last elementary inequality can be checked by parity. If \(b\) is even, \(3b/2\) is an integer at least \(4b/3\). If \(b\ge3\) is odd, \((3b-1)/2\) is an integer at least \(4b/3\). In the first case of (4), \(b/(2b-1)\le2/3\le3/4\).

There are \(r\) such expanded coordinates throughout \(J\). Multiplying the coordinate inequalities, then summing over oriented vertical displacements, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^m}K_{J,(\mathbf u,s)}
&=\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathbb Z\\s-h,s+h\in J}}
\prod_{i=1}^m 2B_i(s-h)B_i(s+h)\\
&\le\gamma^r(2^mB_s)
\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathbb Z\\s-h,s+h\in J}}
\prod_{i=1}^mR_i(s,h)\\
&=\gamma^r(2^mB_s)K_J(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Summing (5) over \(s\in J\) yields
\(\sum_jK_{J,j}\le\gamma^r|A_J|K_J^{\max}\). Combining this with (2) proves (3).

Every reflected pair counted in \(A_J\) has minimum global weight at least \(q^u\). Also \(|A_J|=2^m\sum_{t\in J}B_t\), and \(Z_J\le q^l\sum_{t\in J}B_t\). Thus the basic parity estimate and (3), respectively, give the following two local weighted bounds:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_J&\le 2e\,\widetilde{\mathcal M}
&&\text{if }\beta(u-l)\le1,\\
Z_J&\le 2e\,\gamma^{k(l)}\widetilde{\mathcal M}
&&\text{if }\beta(u-l)\le1
\text{ and }1\le l\le u\le2l.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For example, the strengthened bound follows from
\(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\ge q^uK_J^{\max}
\ge(\gamma^{-k(l)}/2)q^{u-l}Z_J
\ge(\gamma^{-k(l)}/(2e))Z_J\).
This proof of (6) includes single-height intervals and intervals ending at the actual top height \(N\).

Now take the positive integer \(L:=\lceil1/\beta\rceil\), so \(\beta L\ge1\) and \(\beta(L-1)<1\). Partition the integer heights into the possibly empty blocks
\(J_b:=\{t\in\{0,\ldots,N\}:bL\le t<(b+1)L\}\), \(b=0,1,\ldots\). Every nonempty block has weight variation satisfying the first condition of (6). The first 24 blocks therefore contribute at most \(48e\,\widetilde{\mathcal M}\) to \(Z\).

For \(b\ge24\), set \(r_b:=\lfloor b/12\rfloor\), and split the block mass into \(Z_b^{\mathrm{hi}}\), from heights with \(k(t)\ge r_b\), and \(Z_b^{\mathrm{lo}}\), from the remaining heights. Since \(k(t)\) is nondecreasing, the high part, if nonempty, is an integer interval with endpoints \(l,u\) satisfying
\(l\ge bL\ge1\) and \(u\le(b+1)L-1\le2bL\le2l\). Also \(k(l)\ge r_b\). The second bound in (6) gives

\[
Z_b^{\mathrm{hi}}\le2e\,\gamma^{r_b}\widetilde{\mathcal M}.
\tag{7}
\]

We bound the low part by exact height halving. For an integer \(t\ge2\), put \(t':=\lceil t/2\rceil\). Then \(0\le t'\le t\le N\) whenever \(t\) is a height in our support. For each coordinate,
\(B_i(t)\le2B_i(t')\), since \(t\le2t'\) and
\(\lfloor2a_it'\rfloor+1\le2\lfloor a_it'\rfloor+2\).
If \(B_i(t)=1\), monotonicity also gives \(B_i(t')=1\), so this coordinate requires no factor of two. Multiplication gives the exact bound

\[
B_t\le2^{k(t)}B_{t'}.
\tag{8}
\]

For a low height \(t\in J_b\), \(b\ge24\), we have \(k(t)<r_b\), \(t\ge bL\), and
\(t-t'=\lfloor t/2\rfloor\ge t/3\). Therefore \(\beta(t-t')\ge b/3\), while \(2^{r_b}\le e^{b/12}\). Using (8) gives

\[
q^tB_t
\le2^{r_b}q^{t-t'}q^{t'}B_{t'}
\le e^{-b/4}q^{t'}B_{t'}.
\tag{9}
\]

Each integer \(t'\) has at most two preimages under \(t\mapsto\lceil t/2\rceil\). Summing (9) over the low heights of this block, and then allowing every height \(0\le t'\le N\) in the resulting sum, yields
\(Z_b^{\mathrm{lo}}\le2e^{-b/4}Z\).

The two tails are summable with absolute constants. For the high part,
\(\sum_{b=24}^\infty\gamma^{\lfloor b/12\rfloor}
=12\sum_{r=2}^\infty\gamma^r=27\), so (7) gives
\(\sum_{b\ge24}Z_b^{\mathrm{hi}}\le54e\,\widetilde{\mathcal M}\).
For the low part,

\[
\sum_{b\ge24}Z_b^{\mathrm{lo}}
\le\frac{2e^{-6}}{1-e^{-1/4}}Z
\le10e^{-6}Z
\le\frac5{32}Z.
\tag{10}
\]

Here \(e^{1/4}\ge1+1/4\) gives \(1-e^{-1/4}\ge1/5\), and \(e\ge2\) gives \(e^{-6}\le1/64\). Empty blocks or missing heights in the last block only reduce these sums.

Adding the first 24 blocks and the two tail estimates proves
\(Z\le102e\,\widetilde{\mathcal M}+(5/32)Z\). Rearranging and using the original normalization gives

\[
\widetilde{\mathcal M}\ge
\frac{9}{1088e}Z
=\frac{9}{544e}\,2^{-n}\widetilde W.
\]

The horizontal maximization established at the start supplies one height \(s\) where every center \((\varepsilon,s)\), \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m\), attains \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\). This proves the scalar statement. When \(N=0\), the first-block argument already covers the support. Zero slopes remain exact two-point horizontal coordinates throughout, contribute no expanded-coordinate gain, and are included in every count and normalization above.


We finally apply the general actual-body comparison in accepted fact `89138dbe4e583e2b`. Use its compact convex support \(P\), lattice support \(A\), and auxiliary table with the exact substitution \(\boldsymbol\lambda=(0,\ldots,0,-\beta)\); then \(e^{\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot z}=e^{-\beta t}\) for \(z=(x,t)\). Its quantities \(\widetilde w_z,\widetilde W,\widetilde S_j\) are therefore identical to ours. Its actual-volume objects and its parameters \(C_z,w_z,W,S,h_S,\rho,M,\ell\) are also exactly those in our conditional statement.

All hypotheses of that accepted comparison hold. The scalar support was proved compact, convex, and nonempty; \(n=m+1\ge1\), \(d\ge1\), and \(C\) is a convex body by assumption. The positive-volume support and both comparison inequalities are the explicit hypotheses of our statement. In particular, the integer origin belongs to \(A\), and \(w_{\mathbf0}\ge\rho/M>0\), so \(W>0\). Compactness of \(C\) makes the actual integer-fiber sum finite. The accepted theorem consequently supplies one point \(p^\star\in S\) for which

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(p^\star)
&\ge
\frac{\ell}{16M^2(1+4\log M)}
\frac{\max_{j\in A}\widetilde S_j}{\widetilde W}\,W\\
&\ge
\frac{\ell}{16M^2(1+4\log M)}
\frac{9}{544e}\,2^{-n}W\\
&=
\frac{9\ell}{8704e\,M^2(1+4\log M)}\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second line uses the scalar result proved in this packet. This substitution only decreases the lower bound at the point already supplied by the predecessor, which selects that point before every ambient halfspace. Hence the stated simultaneous halfspace quantifier is preserved. The profile scale \(\rho\) has canceled in the accepted comparison, and no existence assertion about realizing the model as actual fiber volumes has been used.
