## Statement

For arbitrarily large integers \(m\), put \(K=2^m\) and \(N=m+1\). There are full-dimensional complete supports \(D_m\subset\mathbb Z^N\) and finite parameters \(\bm{\theta}_m\) such that the actual cubic depth objective
\[
F_D(\bm{\theta})
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)
\tau_{p_{\bm{\theta}}}(\mathbf z)^3,
\qquad
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=\frac{e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf z}}{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf x}},
\]
is analytic near \(\bm{\theta}_m\), has zero gradient there, and has positive-definite Hessian in all \(N\) tilt directions. Here
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
=\min_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}p(H).
\]

Writing \(p_m=p_{\bm{\theta}_m}\), \(T_m=\max_{\mathbf z}\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf z)\), and \(a=(7/8)^{1/5}\), one has
\[
M_3(p_m)\sim K^{-3},\qquad
T_m\sim a^{-2}K^{-4/5},
\]
\[
\frac{M_3(p_m)}{T_m^3}\sim a^6K^{-3/5}\to0,
\qquad
8^NM_3(p_m)\to8.
\]

## Proof

Define
\[
P_m=\left\{(t,\mathbf x):
-1\le t\le1,\quad
\frac{t-1}{2}\le x_i\le\frac{t+1}{2}\right\},
\]
\[
D_m=(\{1\}\times\{0,1\}^m)\cup\{\mathbf0\}
\cup(\{-1\}\times\{0,-1\}^m).
\]
The only possible integer values of \(t\) are \(-1,0,1\), and their integer sections are exactly those displayed. The invertible real transformation \((t,\mathbf x)\mapsto(t,2\mathbf x-t\mathbf1)\) maps \(P_m\) to a cube. Thus \(P_m=\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\), and \(D_m\) is complete and full-dimensional.

For weights \(B^t\), \(B>1\), put \(Z=1+K(B+B^{-1})\). Every nonzero supported point is a vertex, so its depth is its own probability. At the origin, each containing halfspace contains at least one endpoint of every antipodal pair. The unique minimizing trace consists of the origin and the entire bottom layer. Therefore
\[
f_K(B)
=\frac{K(B^4+B^{-4})+(1+K/B)^3}
{[1+K(B+B^{-1})]^4}.
\]

To include every tilt direction, use parameters \((s,\bm{\eta})\) and weights
\[
e^{st+\bm{\eta}\cdot(2\mathbf x-t\mathbf1)}.
\]
The original-coordinate parameter is
\((s-\sum_i\eta_i,2\eta_1,\ldots,2\eta_m)\), an invertible linear change. Put
\[
C(\bm{\eta})=\prod_i2\cosh\eta_i,\qquad
C_4(\bm{\eta})=\prod_i2\cosh4\eta_i.
\]
In the open region \(s>\sum_i|\eta_i|\), the origin's minimizing trace remains the bottom layer and the origin, and all vertex traces remain singletons. Hence
\[
F(s,\bm{\eta})
=\frac{(e^{4s}+e^{-4s})C_4(\bm{\eta})
+(1+e^{-s}C(\bm{\eta}))^3}
{[1+(e^s+e^{-s})C(\bm{\eta})]^4}.
\]

Uniformly through two derivatives on \(x\in[19/20,1]\),
\[
K^3f_K(xK^{2/5})
=1+K^{-4/5}(x^{-7}-4x^{-2})+O(K^{-7/5}).
\]
This follows by factoring \(KB\) from the denominator and \(KB^4\) from the numerator; the remaining denominators stay uniformly away from zero on this interval.

For \(L(x)=x^{-7}-4x^{-2}\),
\[
L'(x)=x^{-8}(8x^5-7),\qquad L''(x)>0
\quad(19/20\le x\le1).
\]
Its unique critical point is \(a=(7/8)^{1/5}\). Thus, for all sufficiently large \(K\), there is a unique critical point \(B_K\) in
\[
(19/20)K^{2/5}<B_K<K^{2/5},
\]
with \(B_K\sim aK^{2/5}\) and \(f_K''(B_K)>0\).

The full objective is even in each \(\eta_i\). At \((s,\bm{\eta})=(\log B_K,\mathbf0)\), every mixed Hessian entry vanishes. With
\[
V=K(B_K^4+B_K^{-4}),\quad A=1+K/B_K,\quad
Z=1+K(B_K+B_K^{-1}),
\]
direct differentiation gives
\[
F_{\eta_i\eta_i}
=Z^{-4}\left[(12+4/Z)V+(-1+4/Z)A^3-3A^2\right].
\]
Since \(A\ge1\), this is at least \(Z^{-4}(12V-4A^3)\). Also
\[
A^3/V\le8K^2/B_K^7=O(K^{-4/5})\to0.
\]
Thus every transverse entry is positive. The remaining entry is
\[
F_{ss}=B_K^2f_K''(B_K)>0.
\]
This proves full positive definiteness and strict local minimality.

Finally,
\[
Z\sim aK^{7/5},\quad V\sim a^4K^{13/5},\quad
A\sim a^{-1}K^{3/5}.
\]
Therefore \(M_3=(V+A^3)/Z^4\sim K^{-3}\). The origin is uniquely deepest because
\[
\frac{A/Z}{B_K/Z}=B_K^{-1}+K/B_K^2>1,
\]
and its depth is \(A/Z\sim a^{-2}K^{-4/5}\). These formulas prove all limits.

The support contains only \(2^N+1\) points. For every law on it,
\[
8^NM_3(p)\ge8^N\sum p_{\mathbf z}^4
\ge\left(\frac{2^N}{2^N+1}\right)^3.
\]
Thus this support family cannot yield a vanishing normalized cubic moment by another choice of tilt.
