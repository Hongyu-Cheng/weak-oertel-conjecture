## Statement

There exists a full-dimensional rational polytope C in R^52 x R^d, with d=274784+2^61=2305843009213968736, whose nonempty mixed-integer intersection S has every nonempty integer fiber of dimension d and sup_{y in S} depth_S(y)<1/(e 2^52). Depth uses all containing proper closed ambient halfspaces and normalized d-dimensional Euclidean Hausdorff measure, equivalently the sum of fiber Lebesgue volumes. This also violates Gamma_d/2^52. It improves the previous n=76 counterexample but remains above a billion continuous variables. It refutes the specific constant 1/e, not the locked assertion that some smaller positive absolute constant exists. This result is not Lean-formalized.

## Proof

# Complete geometric lemma


This section supplies the exact integer census and origin fiber rather than
assuming that adding negative Boolean bases preserves the old geometry.

Partition `n=4N` coordinate indices into four disjoint blocks `I_a`, each
of size `N`. Let `B0` be the centered standard tetrahedron, with barycentric
coordinates `lambda_0,...,lambda_3`; put `f_j=lambda_j-1/4` and

    T_a={x in B0: lambda_j(x)<=1/4 for j!=a},
    theta=|T_a|/|B0|=3/32,       beta=27/64.

In the three coordinates lambda_j with j!=a, T_a is the cube [0,1/4]^3 and B0 is the simplex of volume 1/6. Their common affine Jacobian cancels, so theta=3/32. The origin cap lambda_j>=1/4 is a homothetic tetrahedron of linear scale 3/4, hence volume fraction beta=27/64.

Choose `c_a` with barycentric coordinate `1/4+3 epsilon` at `a` and
`1/4-epsilon` elsewhere. Take parameters satisfying

    0<epsilon<1/4,       1/2<s<1,
    0<t<eta<2(1-s),      1/2<q<1,
    delta_s=2s-1,        delta_t=1-t,       delta_q=2q-1,
    N delta_t<1,        N delta_q<1,
    N[delta_s+4 epsilon(3t+1-delta_s)] <=1.              (6)

Let `F=[-1,1]^h`, with `h>=1`, and set

    B=B0 x F,
    P_a^0=c_a+s(T_a-c_a),
    K(a,k)=[2P_a^0+(k-1)c_a] x (2q F).

For each nonempty Boolean set `I`, choose one of its indices and call its
block type `a(I)`. Put `K_I=K(a(I),|I|)` at the base `1_I`.
For each nonempty `J subset I_a`, with `r=|J|`, put at base `-1_J`
the fiber

    t_r=1-r delta_t,       u_r=1-r delta_q,
    G_(a,r)=[t_r(T_a-c_a)-(r-1)c_a] x (u_r F).          (7)

Finally let `C0` be the convex hull of these fibers and `{0} x B`.
All component fibers are compact full-dimensional convex bodies. If the
parameters are rational, they and `C0` are rational polytopes.

### Exact integer census and exposed nonzero bases

The integer projection points are exactly

    {0,1}^n union { -1_J : empty!=J subset I_a,
                              a=0,1,2,3 }.             (8)

Indeed, all projected coordinates belong to `[-1,1]`. If an integer
projection has coordinate `-1` at `i in I_a`, every generator used in any
convex representation has that coordinate `-1`. All such generators are
negative Boolean vectors supported in the same block `I_a`, so the entire
projection is a negative Boolean vector in that block. If no coordinate
is negative, it belongs to `{0,1}^n`. Every point listed in (8) is a
generator, proving equality.

Every nonzero base in (8) is uniquely exposed. Put `rho=1/(n+1)`.
For `1_I`, use coefficient `1` on `I` and `-rho` elsewhere. Its value
`|I|` beats every other Boolean vertex, and every negative-block base has
value at most `rho N<1`. For `-1_J`, use coefficient `-1` on `J` and
`rho` elsewhere. Its value is `|J|`; all other negative vectors in its
block have smaller value, negative vectors in other blocks have negative
value, and every positive Boolean vertex has value at most
`rho(n-|J|)<1`. Thus the actual nonzero fibers are exactly the displayed
`K_I` and `G_(a,r)`.

### An affine-facet proof that the origin fiber is exactly B

Put

    v=delta_s/4+(1-delta_s)epsilon.

Every generator `(z,x,w)` satisfies the following affine inequalities:

    lambda_j(x)+v sum_i z_i >=0                 (j=0,1,2,3),
    +/-w_l <=1+delta_q sum_i z_i               (l=1,...,h).  (9)

For a positive Boolean base of size `k`, the free-coordinate claim is
`2q=1+delta_q<=1+k delta_q`. If the selected type is not `j`,

    min_(K_I) lambda_j
      =-delta_s/4-(k-delta_s)epsilon
      >=-k v,

because the difference is `(k-1)delta_s(1/4-epsilon)>=0`.
For the selected type itself, the barycentric minimum is
`1/4+3(k-delta_s)epsilon>0`, so its inequality follows too.

For a negative base of size `r`, the free-coordinate inequality is an
equality at the appropriate free-box face. For `j!=a`, (7) gives

    min lambda_j
      =(1-t_r)/4+(t_r+r-1)epsilon
      =r(delta_t/4+t epsilon)
      >=r v,

since the difference is `r(delta_t-delta_s)(1/4-epsilon)>0`.
For `j=a`, the fact that `lambda_a>=1/4` on `T_a` gives

    min lambda_a =1/4-3r t epsilon >=r v

by the last condition in (6). The middle generators plainly satisfy (9).

Affine inequalities are preserved under convex combinations. At the
origin integer base, (9) says exactly that `x in B0` and `w in F`.
The origin fiber is therefore contained in `B`; the reverse inclusion
holds because `B` was explicitly included among the generators. This
proves equality for arbitrary overlapping negative subsets, without a
regrouping assumption.

The body has full ambient affine span: its origin fiber spans the vertical
directions, and the positive unit bases span all integer directions.
A full-dimensional convex polytope has nonempty interior. All actual
fibers in (8) have dimension `3+h`.

### Exact volumes and origin cuts

Write `b=|B|`, and define

    m=|K_I|/b=theta (2s)^3 (2q)^h,
    alpha_r=|G_(a,r)|/b=theta t_r^3 u_r^h,
    E=4 sum_(r=1)^N binom(N,r) alpha_r.

The total mixed-integer volume is

    W=b[(2^n-1)m+1+E].                                  (10)

For row `j`, use integer coefficients:
`-R` on block `I_j`, `eta epsilon` elsewhere, with `R=3(n+1)`.
The cut is `f_j(x)+sum_i beta_i z_i>=0` and ignores `w`.

Every positive Boolean fiber is strictly excluded. If it meets `I_j`,
the continuous score is at most `k+1` and the integer score at most
`-R+k-1`, so the total is negative. Otherwise its selected type differs
from `j`, and its maximum score is
`[k eta-(k-delta_s)]epsilon<0`, because `eta<1-delta_s`.

On `G_(a,r)` with `a!=j`, its maximum continuous score is
`(t_r+r-1)epsilon=r t epsilon`, and its integer score is
`-r eta epsilon`, so it is strictly excluded. On `G_(j,r)`, the
continuous minimum is `-3r t epsilon`, while its integer score is
`r R`, so it is entirely included. There is exactly one paid block
per cut. The four cuts cover the middle fiber because `sum_j f_j=0`,
and each has exact mixed-integer mass

    b(beta+E/4).                                        (11)

The normals are nonzero, all excluded fibers have strict score gaps,
and the middle cap boundaries have zero volume. Thus (11) is valid for
the stated closed halfspaces.

# An intermediate 52-integer-variable counterexample to the 1/e bound

Status: complete geometric deduction and exact rational scalar certificate;
two independent full mathematical and exact-certificate reviews passed. This
is not Lean-formalized. Its continuous dimension is about `2.31*10^18`, so
it does **not** meet the current billion-scale dimension target.

## Statement and dimensions

There is a rational convex polytope with nonempty ambient interior in

    R^52 x R^(274784+2^61)

such that, for its induced mixed-integer set, every point belongs to a
proper closed ambient halfspace containing strictly less than
`1/(e*2^52)` of the mixed-integer volume. All its nonempty integer
fibers have full continuous dimension. In particular, the same statement
holds for the intrinsic Hausdorff measure specified in the task.

The continuous dimension is exactly

    d=2305843009213968736.

This reduces the common-simplex dimension from `2^93` in the formally
verified 76-variable example to `2^61`. It is an intermediate improvement,
not completion of the requested dimension reduction.

## Exact parameters and unpadded body

    N=13,                  n=4N=52,

    s=500000001/10^9,       t=999999997/10^9,
    eta=399999999/400000000,
    epsilon=1/10^12,        q=100001/200000,
    theta=3/32,             beta=27/64.

For clarity, the integer support consists of all Boolean bases together
with all nonempty negative Boolean subsets contained in any one of four

    Z={0,1}^52 union { -1_J : empty!=J subset I_a, a=0,1,2,3 }.

Let `B0` be the centered standard tetrahedron, with barycentric

    T_a={x in B0: lambda_j(x)<=1/4 for j!=a},
    lambda_a(c_a)=1/4+3 epsilon,
    lambda_j(c_a)=1/4-epsilon       (j!=a),

At a positive Boolean base `1_I`, put

    K_I=[2(c_a+s(T_a-c_a))+(|I|-1)c_a] x (2q F),

where `a` is the block of any fixed selected member of `I`. At a

    G_(a,r)=[t_r(T_a-c_a)-(r-1)c_a] x (u_r F),

Include `B` at zero and take the convex hull of these finitely many

1. Its integer projection points are exactly `Z`.
2. Every nonzero integer base is exposed, so its actual fiber is exactly

3. The origin fiber is exactly `B`. Specifically every generator obeys
   `lambda_j(x)+v sum_i z_i>=0` and
   `+/-w_l<=1+(2q-1)sum_i z_i`, where

4. `C0` is a full-dimensional rational convex polytope, and every actual
   nonempty integer fiber is a convex body in `R^(3+h)`.
5. Four proper closed halfspaces cover the origin fiber, strictly avoid
   every positive Boolean fiber, and each capture precisely one negative
   block and a tetrahedral `beta`-fraction of `B`.

All the hypotheses used for these conclusions are certified below.
In particular the argument controls convex combinations involving
overlapping negative subsets; no unproved independence of those subsets
is assumed.

### Why the power certificate is rigorous

The fourteen powers requiring enclosure are `(2q)^h` and
`(1-r(2q-1))^h` for `1<=r<=13`. All bases are positive rational
numbers. Put `D=10^40`. The initial integer bounds on a base `a/b`
are `floor(Da/b)` and `ceil(Da/b)`, interpreted after division by
`D`. For two nonnegative enclosed quantities, their product is
enclosed by flooring the product of the lower integer bounds divided
by `D`, and ceiling the product of the upper bounds divided by `D`.
This follows from monotonicity of multiplication on nonnegative reals.

Apply this rule at each multiplication and squaring in binary
exponentiation. Induction over the binary digits of `h` proves that
the accumulator intervals enclose the required exact power. Every
pre-update exponent, power interval, and accumulator interval is
recorded in the JSON certificate; all rounded operations are ordinary
integer floor or ceiling divisions. Multiplying by the exact positive
rational factors `theta(2s)^3` and `theta t_r^3`, and summing with
the exact nonnegative binomial coefficients, preserves enclosure.
The program then compares the resulting rational endpoints with (1).
Thus (1) is an exact interval certificate, not floating-point evidence.

## Padding with the sharper finite-dimensional estimate

For every positive integer `ell`, write

    Gamma_ell=(ell/(ell+1))^ell.

Then

    log(e Gamma_ell)
      = integral_0^1 t/(ell+t) dt < 1/(2ell).

Since `exp(x)<1/(1-x)` for `0<x<1`—equivalently
`-log(1-x)>x`, by integrating `1/(1-t)>1`—we obtain

    e Gamma_ell < 1+1/(2ell-1).                       (5)

This improves the earlier convenient estimate `1+1/ell` by enough
to use `ell=2^61` here. From (3)--(4),

    (2ell-1) Delta > 2^52 m.

Consequently

    e Gamma_ell * 2^52 m
      < 2^52 m + Delta = W/b.                        (6)

## Every point has a strictly small proper closed halfspace

Let `J` be any full-dimensional rational `ell`-simplex, and set
`C=C0 x J`. This is a rational convex body with the integer and
continuous dimensions stated above. Its total mixed-integer volume
is `W vol(J)`. All its nonempty integer fibers are full dimensional,
so its intrinsic dimension is `d=3+h+ell`.

For a point in the origin fiber, choose one of the four origin cuts
containing its `B0` coordinate; these cuts cover because the four
linear functions `lambda_j-1/4` sum to zero. Extend the chosen cut
to ignore the simplex coordinates. Its volume is exactly
`b(beta+E/4)vol(J)`, which is less than `mb vol(J)/e` by (2).
Since `W>2^52 mb` by (3), its normalized mass is strictly less than
`1/(e*2^52)`.

For a point above a nonzero integer base, take a simplex cap through
its `J` coordinate of relative volume at most `Gamma_ell`. Such a
cap exists at every point: choose a largest barycentric coordinate
`lambda`, which is at least `1/(ell+1)`, and use the cap where that
coordinate is at least `lambda`. Its relative volume is
`(1-lambda)^ell<=Gamma_ell`. Since the chosen integer base is
exposed and there are only finitely many other fibers, adding a
sufficiently large positive multiple of its integer exposing
functional to the simplex cap's functional produces a proper closed
ambient halfspace that agrees with the cap on this fiber and misses
every other fiber. Compactness bounds the cap functional uniformly,
and the exposing gaps are strictly positive, so a finite multiplier
suffices. The halfspace's mass is at most
`Gamma_ell mb vol(J)`, strictly below the target by (6).

All cuts are ambient halfspaces with nonzero normals. The integer
exclusion gaps are strict; the continuous cap boundaries have zero
volume. Thus their stated masses are valid for closed halfspaces,
including points on the boundary of a fiber. The uniform bound

    max{ b(beta+E/4), Gamma_ell mb } / W < 1/(e*2^52)

applies to every point of the actual mixed-integer set. This proves
the claimed intermediate counterexample.

## Measure normalization and comparison with the sharp constant

The integer support is finite. Each nonempty fiber is a full-dimensional compact convex body in its Euclidean continuous affine subspace and has positive finite d-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Standard-normalized Euclidean Hausdorff measure H_d restricts to that Lebesgue measure on each such affine subspace. Finite additivity on the disjoint Borel fibers gives H_d(S)=sum_z vol_d(S_z), and the same identity after intersection with every closed halfspace. Their union has Hausdorff dimension d, so this is also the intrinsic measure. Finally log(1+1/d)<1/d gives Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d>1/e. The strict bound already proved therefore also refutes the sharp Gamma_d/2^52 assertion.
