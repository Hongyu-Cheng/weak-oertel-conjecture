## Statement

The proposed midpoint concavity at all integers $m\ge1$ of $[\mathbb E\binom{m+k-V}{k}]^{1/k}$ for Poisson-binomial $V$ with at most $k-1$ Bernoulli trials is false. The binomial coefficient is zero when its integer upper argument is below $k$. For $k=3$ and $V\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(2,1/2)$, the expectation is $F_m=[(m+1)^3+(m+1)/2]/6$ for every integer $m\ge0$. The sequence $F_m^{1/3}$ is strictly convex at every integer midpoint $m\ge1$, and $\sum_{m\ge0}F_m z^m=(1+z)^2/[4(1-z)^4]$ has a numerator with only negative real roots.

## Proof

The mixture equals $\frac14\binom{m+3}{3}+\frac12\binom{m+2}{3}+\frac14\binom{m+1}{3}$. The cubic formulas agree with truncation even at $m=0$, where the terms with tops $2$ and $1$ vanish. Setting $x=m+1$ and expanding gives $6F_m=x^3+x/2$.

For $P(x)=x^3+x/2$, the second derivative of $P^{1/3}$ has the sign of $PP''-\frac23(P')^2=x^2-1/6>0$ for $x\ge1$. Hence the displayed real extension of $F_m^{1/3}$ is strictly convex on $m\ge0$. In particular the first three expectations are $1/4,3/2,19/4$ and all the integer midpoints violate the asserted concavity. Finally the binomial generating series $\sum_{m\ge0}\binom{m+3-r}{3}z^m=z^r/(1-z)^4$ for $r=0,1,2$ gives the generating function on taking the same mixture. Its numerator has a double root at $-1$.
