## Statement

Let $P$ be finite and lattice-complete and let the positive convex bodies $K_z$ satisfy the sampled anchored Minkowski inclusions. Let $\Phi(u,z,X)$ be jointly continuous for $u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ and all physical coordinates $(z,X)$, finite and convex in $(z,X)$ for each $u$. For $t\ge0$ define
$$
K_z(t)=\{X:u\cdot X+t\Phi(u,z,X)\le h_{K_z}(u)\text{ for every unit }u\}.
$$
For small $t$ these are positive compact convex bodies $O(t)$ Hausdorff-close to the old ones, satisfying the same sampled compatibility, hence they are their canonical hull's actual sampled fibers. At a regular old boundary point $X$ with unique outer unit normal $n$, let the endpoint on its ray from an old inball center $o$ be $X_t=o+(1-s_t(X))(X-o)$. Then $|s_t(X)|=O(t)$ uniformly and
$$
\frac{s_t(X)}t\longrightarrow\frac{\Phi(n,z,X)}{n\cdot(X-o)}.
$$
This gives a bounded measurable signed inward speed almost everywhere for surface area, with counting measure on the endpoints if $d=1$. Finite nonnegative combinations of the fields realize the corresponding sums of speeds. Both signs of normal-dependent affine physical fields are allowed. Independent finite cap-normal velocities and, when the scale is interior, either sign of $s=\dot{\log\varepsilon}$ can be appended.

## Proof

At each sampled $z$, convexity along rays from the continuous origin and the common bound on $\Phi$ for unit $u$ and $|X|\le1$ give $\Phi(u,z,X)\ge-C(1+|X|)$ uniformly. Taking $u=X/|X|$ in the constraints bounds all new points in a fixed ball for small $t$. There $\Phi$ has a uniform bound $L$. The support inequalities put $K_z(t)$ within the parallel body of radius $tL$ around $K_z$, and an inward homothet of ratio $1-tL/r$ about an old inball center with radius $r$ lies inside $K_z(t)$. Thus there are two-sided $O(t)$ homothetic bounds about the same center and the radial displacements satisfy the uniform bound.

For any convex representation $p=\sum_z\lambda_z z$, and $X_z\in K_z(t)$, convexity of the field gives
$$
u\cdot\sum_z\lambda_z X_z+t\Phi\left(u,p,\sum_z\lambda_z X_z\right)
\le\sum_z\lambda_z h_{K_z}(u)\le h_{K_p}(u).
$$
This is precisely compatibility. The completeness of $P$ means the new canonical hull has the same integer bases.

Fix a regular point and test the old normal $n$ at $X_t$. Its constraint implies $s_t n\cdot(X-o)\ge t\Phi(n,z,X_t)$. There is an active new constraint $u_t$ at $X_t$ by compactness of the unit sphere and the absence of uniform strict slack at a boundary point. Its equality gives $s_t u_t\cdot(X-o)\le t\Phi(u_t,z,X_t)$ by $u_t\cdot X\le h_{K_z}(u_t)$. Every limiting $u_t$ supports the old fiber at $X$, so $u_t\to n$ and $u_t\cdot(X-o)$ is eventually positive, bounded away from zero by the old inball. The bounds squeeze the stated derivative. Regularity holds surface-almost everywhere, including both endpoints in dimension one. The formula is linear in the test field and the class is closed under finite nonnegative sums. Finite normal perturbations $N_{ij}+t\nu_{ij}$ retain nonzero continuous parts for small $t$, and $\varepsilon e^{st}$ realizes the stated scale velocities.
