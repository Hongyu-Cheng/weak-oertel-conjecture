## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies with $d\ge1$, and set $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$. For every convex body $K$ and $y\in K$, let $T_K(y)$ be the largest mass of a measurable density $0\le h\le1$ on $K$ with $\int_K(X-y)h(X)\,dX=0$. Then $f(z,y)=T_{C_z}(y)^{1/d}$ is continuous and concave on $C$, positive in each own-fiber interior and zero on each own-fiber boundary. It is inclusion-monotone in the marked fiber and has degree one under common marked homotheties.

## Proof

At an interior mark, translate $y$ to zero and minimize $G(v)=\int_K[1+v\cdot X]_+\,dX$. The centered inball bounds $G(v)$ below by a positive constant times $\|v\|$ by integrating $(v\cdot X)_+$, so a minimum exists. Differentiation under the integral is valid by boundedness and the null zero set of the hinge for $v\ne0$, with positivity near $v=0$. Its gradient is the moment of $D=K\cap\{1+v\cdot X\ge0\}$. At a minimizer $D$ has centroid zero and contains an inball about zero. For every feasible density $h$, $\int_K h=\int_K(1+v\cdot X)h\le G(v)$. At the minimizing $v$, $\mathbf1_D$ attains the bound and $T_K(y)=|D|$. At a boundary mark any feasible density is supported up to null sets on a supporting plane by the moment condition and hence has mass zero.

For joint concavity at interior marks, apply the probability-density interpolation in f6641f09c19819a3 to the full-dimensional optimal retained caps of the input marks. For weights $\lambda_i$ the resulting probability law is supported in their Minkowski combination, with mean the combined mark and density at most $(\sum_i\lambda_i T_{K_i}(y_i)^{1/d})^{-d}$. Multiplying by the reciprocal of this bound gives an admissible retained density on any containing body. The corresponding canonical target fiber contains this Minkowski combination.

By 7227bbfcd12ad3d2 the centered fibers vary continuously in Hausdorff distance with the base and mark. At an interior limiting mark, centered contractions and expansions of ratio $1\pm\delta/r$ sandwich a centered fiber at distance $\delta$ if the limiting centered fiber contains a ball of radius $r>\delta$. The support-function inequality gives the sandwich, and inclusion monotonicity and degree-$d$ scaling of $T$ follow directly by extending or transporting feasible densities. Thus the sandwich proves continuity there. At a boundary limiting mark $y$, let $v$ be a unit outward support normal. For fixed $R>0$, the test hinge $[1+Rv\cdot(X-y_j)]_+$ bounds $T_{C_{z_j}}(y_j)$ above even for boundary marks by the moment inequality. Its integral tends to the corresponding integral on the limiting body, by Hausdorff convergence, which gives convergence of indicators off its null boundary. On that body the limiting hinge lies in $[0,1]$ and tends to zero off the supporting plane as $R\to\infty$. This proves the zero boundary limit. Approximating marks inside their own fibers now extends the joint concavity to all of $C$.
