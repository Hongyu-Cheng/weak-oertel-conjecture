## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite, with compact convex positive $d$-volume fibers, and let $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$, with unit $u_i\in V\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, define finitely many closed caps covering the whole continuous space over each base in $P$. In the attained-span chart, allow free offsets and base tilts, independent normal variations on the unit sphere in $V$, and one common ambient rotation. Suppose there is a consistent Fritz–John stress with objective multiplier zero, cap-budget multipliers $r_i\ge0$, coverage multipliers $\eta_z\ge0$, and selected effective coarea measures $\tau_i$ on the actual sections $\{L_i=0\}$, with moments $(S_i,Z_i,X_i)=\int(1,z,x)\,d\tau_i$. At each $\eta_z>0$ let the coverage feature be represented by a probability law $\nu_z$ on active saddle pairs $(x,\lambda)$ with $x\in V$, $\lambda$ in the cap-index simplex, $\sum_i\lambda_i u_i=0$, $L_k(z,x)\le0$ for every $k$, and $\lambda_iL_i(z,x)=0$. Write $p_{zi}=\int\lambda_i\,d\nu_z$ and $q_{zi}=\int\lambda_ix\,d\nu_z$. Then
$$
r_i(S_i,Z_i,P_VX_i)=\sum_z\eta_z(p_{zi},zp_{zi},q_{zi}),
\qquad
\sum_i r_i S_i u_i=0,\quad
\sum_i r_i u_i Z_i^\top=0,\quad
\sum_i r_i X_i u_i^\top=0.
$$
For every $i,k$ one has $r_i\int L_k\,d\tau_i=\sum_z\eta_z\int\lambda_iL_k(z,x)\,d\nu_z\le0$, with equality for $k=i$. When $r_iS_i>0$, the section barycenter in $\mathbb R^n\times V$ is an average of its assigned contacts and lies in all closed cap complements. Its base coordinate need not be integer. The individual continuous-moment identity is asserted only after projection to $V$.

## Proof

Use local sphere coordinates for $v_i\in V$ and ambient rotations $R$ with $u_i=Rv_i$, taking $R=I$ at the reference parameters. Rotating all normals does not change whole-space coverage: the change of variable $x=Ry$ preserves its minimum of the maximum margin over the whole continuous space. The assumed stress uses the same physical cap-mass covector $(Z_i,X_i,-S_i)$ in every chart direction, and each coverage covector is the average of $(\lambda_i z,\lambda_i x,-\lambda_i)_i$ in the base, in-span normal and offset coordinates. Coverage has zero common-rotation covector. With objective coefficient zero, offsets and base tilts therefore give the asserted equations for $S_i$ and $Z_i$. Stationarity on the individual sphere in $V$ gives
$$
r_iP_VX_i-\sum_z\eta_zq_{zi}=\sigma_i u_i
$$
for some scalar $\sigma_i$. Pairing this equation with $u_i$ makes its left-hand side zero: the section identity is $u_i\cdot X_i=b_iS_i-a_i\cdot Z_i$, and contact complementarity gives $u_i\cdot q_{zi}=(b_i-a_i\cdot z)p_{zi}$. The offset and tilt equations cancel these terms exactly. Thus $\sigma_i=0$.

Sum the mass and base equations with factors $u_i$ to obtain the two force identities by $\sum_i\lambda_i u_i=0$. For $T=\sum_i r_iX_i u_i^\top$, the projected equations give $P_VT=0$ by the same saddle balance, and $T=TP_V$ because $u_i\in V$. Common rotation stationarity gives $\sum_i r_iX_i\cdot A u_i=0$ for every skew-symmetric $A$. This says $T$ is orthogonal to the skew-symmetric matrices and hence symmetric. It follows that $T=P_VT=0$. This sum gives no separate identity for each perpendicular moment.

For the cross-margin equation expand $L_k$ and substitute the three individual balances. Its continuous term uses only $P_VX_i$ because $u_k\in V$. The signs and the equality for $k=i$ follow from the saddle contact assumptions. If $r_iS_i>0$, the nonnegative measures $\eta_z\lambda_i\,d\nu_z$ have total mass $r_iS_i$ and their normalized first moments equal the projected section barycenter, proving the assertion about the complements.
