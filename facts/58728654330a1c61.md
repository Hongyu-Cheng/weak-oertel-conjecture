## Statement

Fix finitely many bases $P$ and positive compact convex $d$-fibers $K_z^j\subset RB_d$, $d\ge1$, converging in Hausdorff distance to positive $K_z$. At each $j$ let a probability law of actual nonvertical caps $H=\{a\cdot z+u\cdot X\ge c\}$ have common mixed $d$-volume $Q_j\to Q$, with $0<u_{\min}\le |u|\le u_{\max}$. Finitely many fixed group labels may be included. Retain the original boundary selector $s_H$ and the section coarea $\lambda_{H,z}$. The selector is the strict-side bit away from a supporting facet. The section coarea is full $\mathcal H^{d-1}/|u|$ on a proper section, $(1-s_H)dS/|u|$ at inside support, $s_HdS/|u|$ at outside support, and zero for strict full or empty traces. Use endpoint counting for $d=1$.

Fix $B>u_{\max}R$. There are uniformly bounded Borel cap records preserving every threshold $c-a\cdot z$ in $[-B,B]$ and moving the others only within their respective exterior rays. They preserve all the given fiber traces and allocations. Along one subsequence the law of these records, its joint selected boundary measure recording the outward unit normal, and its joint allocated section measure converge weakly. The disintegrated limits use the same allocation rules for almost every record, and every support record has mixed $d$-volume $Q$. For each fiber, almost every record satisfies, for every $C^1$ spatial test $\psi$,
$$
\int_{\partial K_z}s_H\psi n\,dS
=\int_{K_z\cap H}\nabla_X\psi\,dX+u\int\psi\,d\lambda_{H,z}.
$$
In particular the allocated coarea masses and first moments converge jointly as measures of the record. There is no extra singular section measure at a supporting edge.

## Proof

The threshold vector on $P$ is the evaluation of $(c,a)$ against the fixed rows $(1,-z)$. Keep each evaluation in $[-B,B]$ fixed and constrain each other one to its corresponding closed outer ray. The original coefficients are feasible. Choose the unique minimum Euclidean norm feasible vector. It is perpendicular to the kernel of the active rows because small displacements in both signs of that kernel are feasible. An independent basis $D$ of these rows gives the vector $D^{\mathsf T}(DD^{\mathsf T})^{-1}b$, where all entries of $b$ have absolute value at most $B$. If no rows are active the vector is zero. There are finitely many possible bases. The resulting bound includes deficient base rank and eliminates every invisible direction. This choice is Borel. On each Borel threshold pattern there are finitely many candidate vectors from these formulas, and the true minimizer is the feasible candidate of least norm. Since $|u\cdot X|\le u_{\max}R<B$ on the fibers, the record preserves all traces, all proper or supporting thresholds, and every assigned allocation. It also preserves the exact margin at any marked point whose cut is proper in its own fiber.

Let $\Lambda_j$ be the cap-record law, with any retained label included in the record. Let $\eta_j$ be the sum of actual fiber boundary areas marked by $(z,X,n)$, and form the joint boundary and section measures using $\Lambda_j$ and the original allocations. If different original caps give one record, the joint measure is the pushforward of the original weighted measures. The selected boundary measure is dominated by $\Lambda_j\otimes\eta_j$. The section measure has record marginal at most $S_{\max}\Lambda_j$ for $S_{\max}=|P|\omega_{d-1}R^{d-1}/u_{\min}$ with $\omega_0=1$. All spaces can be taken compact.

For each fiber choose a common center in the limiting interior. A smaller ball at this center is contained in the limiting and all sufficiently late current fibers by Hausdorff convergence. The convex gauges about that center converge uniformly on compact sets by the inclusions between small centered dilations of the limiting fiber. At directions where all gauges are differentiable, their gradients converge by the subgradient inequalities and differentiability of the limiting gauge. The exceptional directions have zero sphere area by homogeneity and almost-everywhere differentiability. In the radial graph parametrization with radius $\rho_j(v)$ and exterior normal $n_j(v)$, the area Jacobian is $\rho_j(v)^{d-1}/(n_j(v)\cdot v)$. The denominator is bounded below by the common inradius divided by the outer radius about the center. The Jacobian and the recorded normal converge almost everywhere. Dominated convergence proves $\eta_j\rightharpoonup\eta$, the actual normal-marked area of the limiting fibers. For intervals this is direct convergence of the two endpoints and their normals.

Extract the three joint weak limits on one subsequence. Product convergence preserves the two dominations, which give the common-law disintegrations by a density $0\le s_H\le1$ and finite measures $\lambda_{H,z}$. The strict-side selector values follow by testing with functions supported where the limiting cap margin is strictly positive or strictly negative. For a converging sequence of records, the integral of any fixed continuous spatial test over $K_z^j\cap H_j$ converges to that over $K_z\cap H$. Indeed the indicators converge outside the boundary of $K_z$ and the cut plane, both $d$-null, and the integrands are uniformly bounded. This sequential continuity, on the compact record range, also gives uniform convergence of the current captured integrals to the limiting ones as functions of the record. Taking the test one and using the common mass $Q_j$ shows that the continuous limiting mass function equals $Q$ on the entire support of the record law.

At each index the asserted identity is Gauss–Green on a proper cap, whose cut-plane outward normal is $-u/|u|$. At inside support the portion deleted from the full-body boundary flux is $(1-s_H)\psi(-u/|u|)dS$, and at outside support the only surviving boundary flux is $s_H\psi(u/|u|)dS$. Thus the same identity holds at both kinds of support and in strict full or empty fibers. Multiply by a continuous record test and pass to the joint limit using the continuity of the captured integrals just proved. Disintegrate and use a countable dense family of spatial $C^1$ tests on a fixed box to obtain the identity for every $\psi$ on one full-law set of records. The strict-side values and this identity determine the section measure. Subtract the ordinary captured-part formula and use $u\ne0$. The result is full proper coarea at a proper cut, the inside complement or outside equal fraction at support, and zero elsewhere. In particular no additional singular edge term can satisfy the same identity. Testing with $1$ and $X_\ell$ gives the zeroth and first coarea moments, which also pass directly from joint weak convergence on the compact range.

For the interval $[-1,1]$, the proper caps $X\ge-1+1/j$ limit to inside support, with section counting measure one and boundary selector zero at $-1$. The proper caps $X\ge1-1/j$ limit to outside support with section counting measure and boundary selector both one at $1$. Each example has its own common-volume sequence. The original zero inside-section convention therefore need not persist in the limit. For identical intervals at bases $-1,0,1$, replacing $X+mz\ge0$, $m>2$, by $X+2z\ge0$ keeps all traces and changes the base coefficient. Accordingly the bounded record preserves the allocated measures and may change a projected normal resultant.
