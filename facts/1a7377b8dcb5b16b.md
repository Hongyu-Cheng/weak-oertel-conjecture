## Statement

Let $d\ge 2$ and let $K\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body of volume $V$ and centroid zero. The only positive-volume integer fibers of a convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ are $A=a+rK$, $B=b+RK$, and $C=c+sK$ at bases $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $r,R,s>0$ and $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$. Write $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and $M=V(r^d+R^d+s^d)$. Raw mass means the sum of the $d$-dimensional volumes of the traces on the actual integer fibers. If any fiber has volume at least $M/2$, the ordinary centroid of any such fiber has raw ambient halfspace depth at least $\gamma_d M/2$. If every fiber has volume less than $M/2$, the ordinary centroid $(0,b)$ of the actual middle fiber has raw ambient halfspace depth at least $\gamma_d M/2$. Depth is taken over all closed ambient halfspaces containing the stated point.

## Proof

First, if a fiber has volume at least $M/2$, the full-volume case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives ordinary depth at least $\gamma_d M/2$ at its centroid. Every containing closed ambient halfspace has a trace on that fiber which is either a containing ordinary halfspace or the whole fiber, so the same lower bound holds for the actual mixed cap.

Suppose now all three fiber volumes are less than $M/2$. Reverse the base if necessary so that $r\le s$. If $R>s$, then $R^d<r^d+s^d$ by nonheaviness of the middle. The quantitative middle-centroid theorem applies and proves the claim at $(0,b)$.

If $R\le s$, shear as $(z,x)\mapsto(z,x-b-z(c-b))$ to make $B=RK$, $C=sK$, and $A=a'+rK$ with $a'=a+c-2b$. The shear preserves the actual fiber masses, and the middle centroid goes to $(0,0)$. Midpoint inclusion gives $a'+(r+s)K\subseteq2RK$. Widths give $2R\ge r+s$, and support inequalities give $a'\in(2R-r-s)K$. Thus $A\subseteq(2R-s)K\subseteq B\subseteq C$, using $0\in K$. Apply the common-core assertion of 8812feca6c8a1e97 with core $B=RK$, volume $VR^d$, smaller endpoint volume $Vr^d$, and extra mass $V(s^d-R^d)$. The latter is less than $Vr^d$ by nonheaviness of $C$ and therefore at most $(1/\gamma_d-1)Vr^d$, since $\gamma_d\le1/2$. The resulting bound is at least $\gamma_dM/2$ in raw units, at the core's centroid $(0,0)$. This is the actual middle centroid after undoing the shear. The nested fact can be applied just to the three positive fibers, since any additional null fibers have zero mass in every cap.
