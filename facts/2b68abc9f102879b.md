## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let
\[
\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+:T\longrightarrow[0,\infty),\qquad 1\le i\le n,
\]
be bounded upper-semicontinuous concave functions.  Define
\[
C:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times T:
-\ell_i^-(y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(y)\quad(1\le i\le n)\right\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For \(y\in T\), put
\[
a_i(y):=\lfloor\ell_i^-(y)\rfloor,
\qquad b_i(y):=\lfloor\ell_i^+(y)\rfloor,
\qquad N_i(y):=a_i(y)+b_i(y)+2,
\]
and use the canonical full-count and cell-count densities
\[
A_{\mathrm{asym}}(y):=\prod_{i=1}^nN_i(y),
\qquad
B_{\mathrm{asym}}(y):=\prod_{i=1}^n(N_i(y)-1).
\]
Then the full original mixed mass is
\[
W:=\mathcal H_d(S)=\int_TA_{\mathrm{asym}}(y)\,dy.
\]
For a nonnegative integrable density \(\rho\) on a convex body \(K\), define its continuous halfspace depth by
\[
\tau_\rho(z):=
\inf_{\substack{L\subset\mathbb R^{\dim K}\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\z\in L}}
\int_L\rho.
\]
For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H).
\]
For \(0<\alpha<1\), write
\[
\kappa_\alpha:=\left(3^{1-\alpha}-2^{1-\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}.
\]
Then:

1. There is \(y_B\in T\) such that every proper closed halfspace \(L\subset\mathbb R^d\) containing \(y_B\) satisfies
\[
\int_LB_{\mathrm{asym}}(y)\,dy
\ge \kappa_{3/4}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{A}
\]
This is only a continuous-depth conclusion for the cell density \(B_{\mathrm{asym}}\); no actual mixed-integer depth conclusion is asserted in the asymmetric case.

2. Suppose \(\ell_i^- =\ell_i^+=:\ell_i\) for every \(i\).  Put
\[
m_i:=\lfloor\ell_i\rfloor,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\prod_i(m_i+1),
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\prod_i(2m_i+1).
\]
These are exact aliases for the canonical densities:
\[
A_{\mathrm{sym}}=2^{-n}A_{\mathrm{asym}},
\qquad R_{\mathrm{sym}}=B_{\mathrm{asym}},
\qquad W=2^n\int_TA_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\tag{B}
\]
There is \(y_0\in T\), chosen before any ambient halfspace, such that \(p_0:=(0,y_0)\in S\) and every proper closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni p_0\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge \frac{\kappa_{3/4}}2\,2^{-n}W
>\frac1{32}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{C}
\]

3. Suppose \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is partitioned into possibly empty sets \(\mathcal S,\mathcal B,\mathcal G\) such that
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
i\in\mathcal S:&\ell_i^- =\ell_i^+;\\
i\in\mathcal B:&0\le\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+\le1;\\
i\in\mathcal G:&L_i:=1+\ell_i^-+\ell_i^+\ge7\quad\text{on }T.
\end{array}
\tag{D}
\]
There is one point \(p^\star\in S\), chosen before all halfspaces, such that every proper closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni p^\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge \frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{E}
\]
No independence or uncoupling of the roof functions is assumed in any assertion.

## Proof

We first compose the accepted entropy and reference-depth theorems.  Let \(V:=|T|\) and let \(\mu:=V^{-1}\mathbf1_Tdy\).  Accepted fact `d279c7ab11895287`, with exactly the canonical \(A_{\mathrm{asym}},B_{\mathrm{asym}}\) and \(W\) above, gives
\[
\left(\mathbb E_\mu B_{\mathrm{asym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}
\ge2^{-n}\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{asym}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Apply accepted fact `9ee12600178ce4dd` with its convex body \(K=T\), reference density \(g=V^{-1}\mathbf1_T\), density \(\rho=B_{\mathrm{asym}}\), and exponent \(\alpha=3/4\).  It supplies a maximizer \(y_B\in T\) and, using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{B_{\mathrm{asym}}}(y_B)
&\ge \kappa_{3/4}
\frac{\left(\int_TB_{\mathrm{asym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}}{V^{1/3}}\\
&=\kappa_{3/4}V
\left(\mathbb E_\mu B_{\mathrm{asym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}\\
&\ge\kappa_{3/4}2^{-n}V\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{asym}}
=\kappa_{3/4}2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
This proves (A).  Nothing in this argument selects a fixed integer center for the asymmetric fibers, so it yields no additional assertion about \(h_S\).

Now assume all roofs are symmetric.  The exact aliases (B), including the equality of the probability tilts by \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\) and by \(A_{\mathrm{sym}}\), are part of accepted fact `d279c7ab11895287`.  Its symmetric fractional-moment conclusion is
\[
\left(\mathbb E_\mu R_{\mathrm{sym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}
\ge\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `9ee12600178ce4dd`, now with \(\rho=R_{\mathrm{sym}}\), chooses \(y_0\in T\), before any ambient halfspace, such that
\[
\tau_{R_{\mathrm{sym}}}(y_0)
\ge\kappa_{3/4}\int_TA_{\mathrm{sym}}
=\kappa_{3/4}2^{-n}W.
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(y\), the original integer section is
\[
I(y)=\prod_i\{-m_i(y),\ldots,m_i(y)+1\},
\]
and it contains the zero-centered reflection box
\[
P_y:=\prod_i\{-m_i(y),\ldots,m_i(y)\},
\qquad |P_y|=R_{\mathrm{sym}}(y).
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \((0,y_0)\in S\).

Fix a proper closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((0,y_0)\).  It contains the parallel halfspace through that point, which after choosing the sign of its nonzero normal can be written
\[
H_0=\{(x,y):a\cdot x+b\cdot(y-y_0)\le0\}\subseteq H.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(b\ne0\), put \(L:=\{y:b\cdot(y-y_0)\le0\}\).  For each \(y\in L\), the involution \(x\mapsto-x\) pairs the points of \(P_y\), and at least half of them satisfy \(a\cdot x\le0\).  Those points all lie in \(H_0\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\frac12\int_LR_{\mathrm{sym}}
\ge\frac12\tau_{R_{\mathrm{sym}}}(y_0).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(b=0\), the same pairing over all \(T\) gives the same lower bound.  Closed inequalities include fixed points and boundary pairs.  Combining (4) and (7) proves the first inequality in (C).

For the numerical consequence, convexity of \(t\mapsto t^{-3/4}\) gives
\[
3^{1/4}-2^{1/4}
=\frac14\int_2^3t^{-3/4}\,dt
\ge\frac1{4(5/2)^{3/4}}>\frac18,
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \((5/2)^3<16\).  Hence \(\kappa_{3/4}>1/16\), proving the strict \(1/32\) bound in (C).

It remains to prove the hybrid assertion.  Put
\[
q:=|\mathcal S|+|\mathcal B|,
\qquad r:=|\mathcal G|,
\qquad q+r=n.
\]
Reorder the integer coordinates by a permutation so that the \(q\) retained coordinates \(\mathcal S\cup\mathcal B\) precede the \(r\) wide coordinates \(\mathcal G\).  This permutation preserves the lattice, the full mixed measure, and ambient halfspaces, so it does not change depth.
We first prove a retained-coordinate lemma whenever \(q>0\).  Let \(U\subset\mathbb R^D\) be any convex body carrying \(|\mathcal S|\) symmetric roofs and \(|\mathcal B|\) thin asymmetric roofs, with the same hypotheses as in (D).  For \(z\in U\), set
\[
k_i(z):=\lfloor\ell_i(z)\rfloor\quad(i\in\mathcal S),
\qquad
a_i(z):=\lfloor\ell_i^-(z)\rfloor,\quad
b_i(z):=\lfloor\ell_i^+(z)\rfloor,\quad
u_i(z):=a_i(z)+b_i(z)\in\{0,1,2\}\quad(i\in\mathcal B),
\]
and define the normalized retained census
\[
A_U(z):=
\prod_{i\in\mathcal S}(k_i(z)+1)
\prod_{i\in\mathcal B}\frac{u_i(z)+2}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
The exact full retained integer count is
\[
A_{\mathrm{full},U}(z)=2^qA_U(z).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus, when accepted fact `d279c7ab11895287` is applied to this \(q\)-integer roof body over \(U\), its canonical \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\) is exactly \(A_{\mathrm{full},U}\); multiplying a density by the constant \(2^q\) does not change its normalized tilt.  Let \(\mu_U\) be uniform probability on \(U\), set \(Z_U:=\mathbb E_{\mu_U}A_U\), let \(d\nu_U/d\mu_U=A_U/Z_U\), and write \(D_U:=D(\nu_U\Vert\mu_U)\).

Use the scale-three inequality (6) of accepted fact `d279c7ab11895287`.  In a symmetric coordinate its \(\Delta_3(a,b)\) is \(\delta_3(k)\).  In a thin coordinate, \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\), and its exact formula gives
\[
\Delta_3(a,b)
=\frac{2(a+b)}{a+b+2}\log2
=\frac{2u}{u+2}\log2.
\]
Consequently
\[
D_U\le\mathbb E_{\nu_U}\left[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal S}\frac32\delta_3(k_i)
+\sum_{i\in\mathcal B}\frac{3u_i}{u_i+2}\log2
\right].
\tag{10}
\]
This derives the thin-coordinate cost from the accepted general two-core formula and remains valid when either coordinate group is empty.

Fix \(\alpha:=4/5\), and for \(u\in\{0,1,2\}\) define
\[
F(u):=
\begin{cases}
1,&u=0,\\
(1+3^\alpha)/2,&u=1,\\
3^\alpha,&u=2.
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Phi(z):=
\prod_{i\in\mathcal S}(2k_i(z)+1)
\prod_{i\in\mathcal B}F(u_i(z))^{1/\alpha}.
\tag{11}
\]
We need two elementary scalar estimates:
\[
\frac32\delta_3(k)\le4\log\frac{2k+1}{k+1}
\quad(k\ge0),
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\frac{3u}{u+2}\log2
\le4\log\frac{F(u)^{1/\alpha}}{(u+2)/2}
\quad(u=0,1,2).
\tag{13}
\]
For (12), \(k=0\) is equality.  At \(k=1\), the estimate is equivalent to \(2^{11}\le3^8\).  For \(k\ge2\), use \(\delta_3(k)\le\log3\), \((2k+1)/(k+1)\ge5/3\), and \(3^{11}\le5^8\).  For (13), \(u=0\) is equality.  At \(u=1\), the equivalent inequality
\[
\frac{1+3^{4/5}}2
\ge\left(\frac32\right)^{4/5}2^{1/5}
\]
follows from \(1+3^{-4/5}\ge4/3\ge2^{2/5}\), the last comparison being \(2^8\ge3^5\).  At \(u=2\), the equivalent comparison \(3^{4/5}\ge2^{11/10}\) follows from \(3^8\ge2^{11}\).  Therefore (10)-(13) give
\[
D_U\le4\,\mathbb E_{\nu_U}\log\frac{\Phi}{A_U}.
\tag{14}
\]

Jensen's inequality and \(d\nu_U/d\mu_U=A_U/Z_U\) now yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\mathbb E_{\mu_U}\Phi^\alpha
&\ge\alpha\log Z_U
+\alpha\mathbb E_{\nu_U}\log(\Phi/A_U)
-(1-\alpha)D_U\\
&\ge\alpha\log Z_U,
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
because \(\alpha=4(1-\alpha)\).  Hence
\[
\left(\mathbb E_{\mu_U}\Phi^{4/5}\right)^{5/4}
\ge\mathbb E_{\mu_U}A_U.
\tag{16}
\]

We next select one fixed binary corner.  For \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal B|}\), let \(J_\varepsilon\in\mathbb Z^q\) use center zero in each symmetric coordinate and center \(\varepsilon_i\) in each thin coordinate.  Let
\[
I_U(z):=
\prod_{i\in\mathcal S}\{-k_i(z),\ldots,k_i(z)+1\}
\times
\prod_{i\in\mathcal B}\{-a_i(z),\ldots,b_i(z)+1\},
\]
and let
\[
R_\varepsilon(z):=
\left|I_U(z)\cap(2J_\varepsilon-I_U(z))\right|
\tag{17}
\]
be the number of original retained sites whose reflection through this fixed center remains in the retained integer section \(I_U(z)\).  A symmetric coordinate contributes \(2k_i+1\).  A thin coordinate contributes \(1+2a_i\) at center zero and \(1+2b_i\) at center one.  Therefore, with the empty-corner average interpreted as its single term,
\[
2^{-|\mathcal B|}\sum_\varepsilon R_\varepsilon(z)^\alpha
=\Phi(z)^\alpha.
\tag{18}
\]
Average (18) over \(U\) and use (16).  Some single \(\varepsilon^\star\), chosen independently of all halfspaces, satisfies
\[
\left(\mathbb E_{\mu_U}R_{\varepsilon^\star}^{4/5}\right)^{5/4}
\ge\mathbb E_{\mu_U}A_U.
\tag{19}
\]
Apply accepted fact `9ee12600178ce4dd` with uniform reference on \(U\), density \(R_{\varepsilon^\star}\), and exponent \(4/5\).  It chooses \(z^\star\in U\), still before any ambient halfspace, such that
\[
\tau_{R_{\varepsilon^\star}}(z^\star)
\ge\kappa_{4/5}\int_UA_U.
\tag{20}
\]

Let \(E_U\) be the retained roof body over \(U\), let \(\mu_q\) denote its mixed measure with precisely these \(q\) integer coordinates, and set
\[
h_{E_U}(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_q(E_U\cap H),
\]
where the infimum is over proper closed ambient halfspaces.  The point \(\widetilde p:=(J_{\varepsilon^\star},z^\star)\) lies in its mixed set.  For any proper closed halfspace containing \(\widetilde p\), pass to its parallel boundary halfspace and write its functional relative to \(\widetilde p\) as
\[
c\cdot(x-J_{\varepsilon^\star})+b\cdot(z-z^\star)\le0.
\]
If \(b\ne0\), restrict to the base halfspace \(b\cdot(z-z^\star)\le0\).  At each such \(z\), reflection about \(J_{\varepsilon^\star}\) pairs the set counted by \(R_{\varepsilon^\star}(z)\), and at least half of its points have \(c\cdot(x-J_{\varepsilon^\star})\le0\).  If \(b=0\), use the same pairing over all of \(U\).  Thus the same single point satisfies
\[
h_{E_U}(\widetilde p)
\ge\frac12\tau_{R_{\varepsilon^\star}}(z^\star)
\ge\frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\int_UA_U
=\frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\,2^{-q}\mu_q(E_U).
\tag{21}
\]
This proof includes \(\mathcal S=\varnothing\) or \(\mathcal B=\varnothing\); it only assumes \(q>0\).

Return to the body in (D).  Erode only the wide coordinates and define the convex body
\[
U:=\left\{(x_{\mathcal G},y):y\in T,\quad
-\ell_i^-(y)+\frac32\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(y)-\frac32
\quad(i\in\mathcal G)\right\}\subset\mathbb R^{r+d}.
\tag{22}
\]
Every displayed interval has length \(L_i-3\ge4\), so \(U\) is indeed a convex body.  The retained roofs, pulled back from \(T\) to \(U\), remain bounded nonnegative upper-semicontinuous concave functions.  Let \(E\subset\mathbb R^{q+r+d}\) be their \(q\)-integer roof body over \(U\).  Thus the normalized census \(A_U\) in (8) is independent of \(x_{\mathcal G}\), its full retained count is \(2^qA_U\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde W:=\mu_q(E)
&=2^q\int_UA_U\\
&=\int_T
\prod_{i\in\mathcal S}2(k_i+1)
\prod_{i\in\mathcal B}(u_i+2)
\prod_{i\in\mathcal G}(L_i-3)\,dy.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
This explicitly distinguishes the normalized census \(A_U\) from the canonical full count \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\).  For every \(i\in\mathcal G\),
\[
N_i=a_i+b_i+2\le L_i+1,
\qquad
L_i-3\ge\frac{L_i+1}{2}\ge\frac{N_i}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
Multiplying (24) pointwise and comparing (23) with the original formula for \(W\) gives the eroded-mass estimate
\[
\widetilde W\ge2^{-r}W.
\tag{25}
\]
This also holds for \(r=0\), with empty products equal to one and \(\widetilde W=W\).

Assume first that \(q>0\) and \(r>0\).  Apply (21) to this \(E\) and obtain a prescribed point \(\widetilde p\in E\cap(\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb R^{r+d})\) satisfying
\[
h_{q,E}(\widetilde p)
\ge\frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\,2^{-q}\widetilde W.
\tag{26}
\]
Here \(h_{q,E}\) is the depth defined exactly as \(h_{E_U}\) above, now for the concrete body \(E\).
Define the compact convex buffer
\[
P_0:=\{0_q\}\times[-1,1]^r\times\{0_d\}.
\]
Its wide-coordinate projection contains \(0+1[-1,1]^r\), and (22) gives the exact identity
\[
E+\frac32P_0=C.
\tag{27}
\]
Apply accepted fact `8444e75361af26b0` with its \(q,r,d\) equal to the present positive integers, \(P=P_0\), \(R=1\), \(\alpha=3/2\), and prescribed point \(\mathbf y=\widetilde p\).  That fact rounds the \(r\) wide continuous coordinates by a corner-interpolated translate \(w\in P_0\), chosen before any halfspace, and produces
\[
p^\star:=\widetilde p+\frac32w\in S,
\qquad
h_S(p^\star)\ge h_{q,E}(\widetilde p).
\tag{28}
\]
Its halfspace-dependent rounding map affects only the mass comparison, not \(w\) or \(p^\star\).  Equations (25), (26), and (28) prove
\[
h_S(p^\star)
\ge\frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\,2^{-q}\widetilde W
\ge\frac{\kappa_{4/5}}2\,2^{-(q+r)}W.
\tag{29}
\]

If \(q>0\) and \(r=0\), then \(U=T\), \(E=C\), and \(\widetilde W=W\).  The retained point from (21) itself is \(p^\star\), and (29) follows directly without invoking `8444e75361af26b0` outside its hypothesis \(r>0\).

Finally suppose \(q=0\).  Then necessarily \(r=n>0\), all coordinates are uniformly wide, and \(E=U\) is a full \((n+d)\)-dimensional convex body with
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E)=\widetilde W\ge2^{-n}W.
\tag{30}
\]
Use the same \(P_0=[-1,1]^n\times\{0_d\}\), for which \(E+(3/2)P_0=C\).  Accepted fact `a4e3dbc02619d36f`, with its integer dimension \(n\), continuous dimension \(d\), \(P=P_0\), \(R=1\), and this \(E\), supplies a point \(p^\star\in S\), chosen before every halfspace, such that
\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\gamma_{n+d}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(E),
\qquad
\gamma_M:=\left(\frac M{M+1}\right)^M.
\tag{31}
\]
For completeness,
\[
\left(1+\frac1M\right)^M
\le\sum_{j=0}^M\frac1{j!}<3,
\]
so \(\gamma_M>1/3\).  Also \(3^{1/5}<3/2\) and \(2^{1/5}>1\), whence \(0<\kappa_{4/5}<(1/2)^{5/4}<1/2\).  Therefore \(\gamma_M>\kappa_{4/5}/2\).  Combining this with (30)-(31) proves (E) in the case \(q=0\).  The three cases \((q,r)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^2\), \(q>0,r=0\), and \(q=0,r>0\) exhaust all possibly empty coordinate groups and establish (E) with one point quantified before all halfspaces.
