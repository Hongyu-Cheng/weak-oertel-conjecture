## Statement

Let $T$ be a $4$-simplex and $D_\delta=T\cap\lbrace \lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_4\le\delta\rbrace $, $0<\delta<1$. At a mark with barycentrics $y=(x,x,z,z,z)$, $x\ge z>0$, $2x+3z=1$, the own-section-centroid low cap for $i=2,3,4$ has margin $f_i=1-\sum_{k\ne i}\lambda_k/(4y_k)$. Its maximum on $D_\delta$ is $1-(1-\delta)/(4x)$. At zero width a pair of such margins with opposite base shifts can have both endpoint captured volumes zero exactly when $\delta\le1-4x$; the strict inequality gives a positive miss reserve and equality gives support contact. Along the formal branch
$$
1/5\le x\le1/4,\qquad 256xz^2(x-z)=4\delta^3-3\delta^4
$$
the geometric miss inequality switches at the unique root in $(0,1/5)$ of $101\delta^4-92\delta^3-24\delta^2-16\delta+4=0$.

For $1/5\le x<1/4$ write
$$
B_0=1-4(2x^2+3z^2),\quad
\beta=\frac{256x^2z^3}{B_0},\quad
\theta=\frac{2x(1-4x)}{B_0},\quad
R=\frac{\beta+\theta\delta^3}{1+2\delta^3}.
$$
Whenever $\delta\le1-4x$, one has $R\ge256/635>3/8>1/e$. This scalar bound does not require the common-mass equation.

## Proof

In the margin sum, each high coefficient is $1/(4x)$, the other low coefficients are nonnegative, and the total high mass is at least $1-\delta$. Equality in this lower bound on the sum is attained by putting the entire allowed low mass at vertex coordinate $i$, and distributing the remainder over the two highs. If the maximum of $f_i$ is positive, at least one of the maxima after opposite shifts is positive; since $D_\delta$ is full dimensional, positivity somewhere gives positivity on an open interior set of positive volume. If the maximum is nonpositive, zero tilt makes both endpoint interiors miss, and any supporting hyperplane has zero $4$-volume. At equality, the contact set for the low margin has $\lambda_i=\delta$, other low coordinates zero, and high sum $1-\delta$. Strict inequality leaves a reserve for sufficiently small outward smoothing at the fixed mark and direction.

Put $s=x/z$; the branch is $256s(s-1)/(2s+3)^4=4\delta^3-3\delta^4$, $s\in[1,3/2]$. The derivative on that interval has the sign of $-4s^2+12s-3>0$. Its left side runs from zero to $4/27$, and the right side increases strictly for $0<\delta<1$ and is smaller than $4/27$ at $\delta=1/5$. Thus a unique branch exists throughout $0\le\delta\le1/5$, with $x=s/(2s+3)$ strictly increasing. At contact $x=(1-\delta)/4$, $z=(1+\delta)/6$, and multiplying the common-mass equation by $27$ gives $27(4\delta^3-3\delta^4)=4(1-\delta)(1+\delta)^2(1-5\delta)$, the displayed polynomial. Its endpoint values are $4$ and $-459/625$; its derivative is at most $404/125-16<0$ on $[0,1/5]$. The contact candidate at its root lies in the proper branch interval, hence it is the unique crossing, also by the monotonicity of $\delta+4x$. Any $\delta>1/5$ on the formal branch already violates the miss inequality by $x\ge1/5$.

For the scalar bound, $s\in[1,3/2)$ and
$$
B_0=\frac{-4s^2+12s-3}{(2s+3)^2}>0,\qquad
\beta=\frac{256s^2}{(2s+3)^3(-4s^2+12s-3)}.
$$
Expansion gives
$$
625s^2-(2s+3)^3(-4s^2+12s-3)=(s-1)^2(32s^3+112s^2+81)\ge0.
$$
Thus $\beta\ge256/625$ and $\theta\ge0$. Since $\delta\le1/5$, $R\ge (256/625)/(1+2/125)=256/635$. The inequality $8\cdot256>3\cdot635$ gives the next strict comparison and $e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3$ gives the last.
