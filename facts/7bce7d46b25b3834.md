## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a convex body and let a finite hyperplane arrangement partition the complement of its hyperplanes into open full-dimensional cells. Let circumscribed polytopes $P_j$ converge to $K$ in Hausdorff distance and already match the volume of $K$ in each cell except possibly a fixed list of cells $R_i$. Suppose every $R_i$ contains an exposed point of $K$. For all sufficiently large $j$, at most one truncation per listed cell gives a polytope matching all cell volumes. The corrected polytopes can be chosen to converge to $K$ in Hausdorff distance.

## Proof

Take a unit normal $n_i$ exposing a unique point $p_i\in R_i$ and put $b_i=h_K(n_i)$. The closed caps $K\cap\{n_i\cdot x\ge b_i-\delta\}$ shrink to $p_i$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, by compactness and unique exposure. For any sufficiently small fixed $\delta_i>0$ the corresponding $K$ cap is contained in $R_i$ and has positive volume, since its threshold is below the support and $K$ has interior. For all large $j$ the corresponding closed $P_j$ cap lies in $R_i$ as well. Otherwise a sequence of its points outside $R_i$ has a limit in the closed $K$ cap outside $R_i$, by Hausdorff convergence and openness of $R_i$.

The nonnegative excess in $R_i$ is at most $|P_j|-|K|\to0$, using $K\subseteq P_j\subseteq K+\operatorname{dist}_H(P_j,K) B_d$ and continuity of measure on the decreasing outer parallel sets. Moving the cut threshold from $b_i-\delta_i$ to $h_{P_j}(n_i)$ continuously decreases the removed volume from at least the fixed positive $K$ cap volume to zero, by nullity of hyperplanes. Choose a threshold removing exactly the excess. The removed sets belong to distinct open cells, so the cuts commute and preserve the previously exact cells.

The exact thresholds converge to $b_i$: at every fixed positive shallow depth the removed volume is at least the corresponding positive $K$ cap volume, while the excess tends to zero and the outer supports tend to $b_i$. Fix $o\in\operatorname{int}K$ and $0<\rho<1$. The support of $o+\rho(K-o)$ in each $n_i$ is strictly less than $b_i$, so this inner copy survives all the exact cuts for sufficiently large $j$. The corrected polytope is contained in $P_j$, and letting $\rho$ tend to one gives Hausdorff convergence.
