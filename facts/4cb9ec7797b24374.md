## Statement

Let $p$ range in an open finite-dimensional chart, $X\subset\mathbb R^d$ and $A$ be compact, and $\phi(p,x,a)$ be continuous. Assume it is $C^1$ in $(p,x)$ uniformly over $a$ near $(p_0,x_0)$ and uniformly Lipschitz in $p$ on the relevant compact set. Suppose $x_0$ is the unique maximizer of $\min_{a\in A}\phi(p_0,x,a)$ over $X$ and $x_0$ is interior to $X$. Let $A_0$ be the active minimizing indices at $(p_0,x_0)$, $q_a=\nabla_x\phi(p_0,x_0,a)$, and $B_a=D_p\phi(p_0,x_0,a)$. Suppose $0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{q_a:a\in A_0\}$ in $\mathbb R^d$. Then the one-sided derivative of $V(p)=\max_{x\in X}\min_{a\in A}\phi(p,x,a)$ in any direction $\delta$ is
$$
V'(p_0;\delta)
=\max_{h\in\mathbb R^d}\min_{a\in A_0}\bigl(B_a(\delta)+q_a\cdot h\bigr)
=\min_{\theta}\int B_a(\delta)\,d\theta(a),
$$
where $\theta$ ranges over probability measures on $A_0$ with $\int q_a\,d\theta=0$. The minimum can be attained on a support-minimal positive dependence of at most $d+1$ active gradients.

For actual mixed depth, assume a fixed finite collection of positive sampled fibers and a smooth parameter chart. At each old deepest base $z$, assume the unique restricted maximum has an interior mark and satisfies these sharpness and uniform cap representation hypotheses, with through-mark functions $\phi_{zj}(p,x)=\mu_p\{v_j\cdot(w-z)+u_j\cdot(\xi-x)\ge0\}$ for a finite or compact uniformly smooth menu of nonvertical caps with unit continuous normals $u_j$. Use fixed compact mark domains containing the relevant fibers locally and assume uniform control of the $p$-variation so that other bases with strict depth gaps remain slack. At active cuts assume ordinary transverse section differentiation, let $S_{zj}>0$ be total section area, and let $B_{zj}$ be the body derivative of the cap mass at its old mark. Write $M$ for the old total mass, $M'$ for its linear derivative and $\beta$ for the old normalized maximum. If $\rho$ is a positive balanced circuit of active continuous normals, put
$$
L_{z,\rho}(\delta)=\frac{\sum_j\rho_j B_{zj}(\delta)/S_{zj}}{\sum_j\rho_j/S_{zj}}.
$$
The normalized maximum has one-sided derivative
$$
\beta'(\delta)=M^{-1}\max_{z\ {\rm deepest}}\min_{\rho\ {\rm active\ at}\ z}
\bigl[L_{z,\rho}(\delta)-\beta M'(\delta)\bigr].
$$
Let $\Gamma_z$ be the compact convex set of functionals from all balanced probabilities at $z$ after subtracting $\beta M'$. At a local minimum in an open chart, every selection $g_z\in\Gamma_z$ satisfies $0\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_z\}$. In particular with one deepest fiber every balanced circuit gives $L_{z,\rho}=\beta M'$ on the whole signed parameter space. For a cone of admissible directions, the necessary condition is absence of an admissible direction strictly negative on every member of any one selection.

## Proof

The interior gradient condition gives $c>0$ such that $\min_{a\in A_0}q_a\cdot v\le-2c\|v\|$ for all $v$. Testing only active indices and using the uniform expansion in $x$ gives $f(p_0,x)\le V(p_0)-c\|x-x_0\|$ near $x_0$, where $f=\min_a\phi$. Uniqueness and compactness give a strict gap outside that neighborhood. The uniform Lipschitz bound in $p$ shows that every maximizer $x_t$ at $p_0+t\delta$ lies near $x_0$, and comparison with $f(p_0+t\delta,x_0)$ gives $\|x_t-x_0\|=O(t)$.

For bounded $h$,
$$
f(p_0+t\delta,x_0+th)
=V(p_0)+t\min_{a\in A_0}(B_a(\delta)+q_a\cdot h)+o(t)
$$
uniformly. The upper bound tests active indices. For the lower bound, any limit of minimizing indices along $t\downarrow0$ and bounded $h$ is active by continuity and compactness. The old minimum gap is nonnegative for every index, and uniform continuity of the derivative coefficients on the compact index set gives the lower bound, by taking convergent subsequences of the bounded $h$ and minimizing indices. Test fixed $h$ for the lower derivative of $V$ and use the bounded $(x_t-x_0)/t$ for the upper derivative. The function being maximized is continuous and tends to $-\infty$ as $\|h\|\to\infty$ by the sharpness bound, so it attains its maximum.

For the dual identity let $C$ be the compact convex hull of $(q_a,B_a(\delta))$, and let $(0,b)$ be its lowest point with zero first coordinate. The epigraph obtained by adding the upward vertical ray is closed and convex. A supporting hyperplane at $(0,b)$ has a strictly positive last coefficient when oriented as a lower bound. Indeed the upward ray makes it nonnegative, and zero last coefficient would support the $q$ projection at its interior point zero. Normalize the coefficient to one to find $h$ with $q_a\cdot h+B_a(\delta)\ge b$ for every active $a$. Averaging any balanced probability gives the reverse bound. A convex representation of $(0,b)$ in the exposed face has at most $d+1$ points, since that face lies in the graph of an affine function of $q$. A support-minimal positive dependence within that finite support has the same cost because all its points are on this graph. This proves attainment by a circuit, including a singleton if a zero gradient is active in the abstract setting.

In the cap application the center displacement $h$ raises a cap threshold by $u_j\cdot h$. Ordinary transverse differentiation with the continuous normal normalized gives $q_{zj}=-S_{zj}u_j$, and interiority gives a positive proper section on the own fiber. Thus positive balanced weights for the gradients correspond to $\theta_j=(\rho_j/S_{zj})/\sum_l\rho_l/S_{zl}$. The weighted shape derivative is precisely $L_{z,\rho}$. The cap representation need only hold near the old maximizers when uniform control and the old strict gaps localize all new maximizers there. For example for locally Hausdorff-Lipschitz compatible positive bodies the symmetric-difference estimate of the usual common-inball comparison is uniform for every closed cap: nearby bodies contain a common ball of radius $r$ and each lies in the other dilated by $1+O(\operatorname{dist}_{\rm H}/r)$ about its center. Their volume symmetric difference is then $O(\operatorname{dist}_{\rm H})$. Taking infima over containing caps gives uniform $p$-control. Old depth is upper semicontinuous on a fixed compact mark set, since each through-mark closed cap mass is upper semicontinuous by bounded convergence of the upper indicator limit, and an infimum preserves upper semicontinuity. Hence strict gaps away from the old maximizers suffice. A fixed finite maximum over the old deepest bases differentiates as the maximum of their one-sided derivatives, and the quotient by the positive common mass gives the stated normalized formula.

The balanced probabilities form a compact set in the weak topology and the functional coefficients are continuous in the index, so the induced sets $\Gamma_z$ are compact and convex. Failure of a selection's convex hull to contain zero is equivalent by strict separation to a direction on which all its selected functionals are negative. Conversely, a negative derivative yields such a selection by minimizing separately at every deepest base. For one deepest base every selected functional must consequently be zero in an open chart. The same negative-direction argument restricted to the admissible cone gives the constrained version.
