## Statement

Let \(D\geq 1\), and let
\[
 P_0=P(h^0):=\left\{y\in\mathbb R^D:
 \langle \nu_j,y\rangle\leq h_j^0,\ 1\leq j\leq m\right\}
\]
be a compact full-dimensional polytope. Assume that the vectors
\(\nu_j\in\mathbb S^{D-1}\) are fixed unit outward normals and that the
description is irredundant. Thus
\[
 F_j:=\{y\in P_0:\langle\nu_j,y\rangle=h_j^0\}
\]
is a facet for every \(j\). Put
\[
 A_j:=\mathcal H_{D-1}(F_j)>0,\qquad
 \bar y_j:=\frac1{A_j}\int_{F_j}y\,d\mathcal H_{D-1}(y),
\]
and let \(V_0>0\) and \(c_0\) denote the volume and centroid of \(P_0\).

Let \(B\) be a nonempty convex set. For every sufficiently small
\(\varepsilon>0\) and every \(x\in B\), suppose that
\[
 P_\varepsilon(x):=
 \left\{y\in\mathbb R^D:
 \langle\nu_j,y\rangle\leq h_j(x,\varepsilon),
 \ 1\leq j\leq m\right\}
\]
is a compact full-dimensional polytope and every displayed inequality is
facet-defining. Suppose also that
\[
 \mathcal C_\varepsilon
 :=\{(x,y)\in B\times\mathbb R^D:y\in P_\varepsilon(x)\}
\]
is convex. The required small-parameter hypothesis is the one-sided,
pointwise expansion
\[
 h_j(x,\varepsilon)
 =h_j^0+\varepsilon f_j(x)+\varepsilon\rho_j(x,\varepsilon),
 \qquad
 \lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\rho_j(x,\varepsilon)=0,
 \tag{1}
\]
for every \(j\) and \(x\). In particular, to pass a concavity inequality
at fixed \(x_0,x_1\), and
\(x_\theta=\theta x_0+(1-\theta)x_1\), it is enough that (1) hold at
those three points; no remainder uniformity over \(B\) is needed.

Then each \(f_j\) is concave on \(B\). Pointwise in \(x\), the fiber
volume \(V_\varepsilon(x)\), first moment \(M_\varepsilon(x)\), and
centroid \(c_\varepsilon(x)\) have the expansions
\[
 V_\varepsilon(x)=V_0+\varepsilon v_1(x)+o_x(\varepsilon),
 \qquad
 v_1(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m A_j f_j(x),
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 M_\varepsilon(x):=\int_{P_\varepsilon(x)}y\,dy
 =V_0c_0+\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^m
 A_j\bar y_j f_j(x)+o_x(\varepsilon),
 \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 c_\varepsilon(x)=c_0+\varepsilon c_1(x)+o_x(\varepsilon),
 \qquad
 c_1(x)=\frac1{V_0}\sum_{j=1}^m
 A_j f_j(x)(\bar y_j-c_0).
 \tag{4}
\]
If \(v_1\) is affine on \(B\), meaning that it preserves every convex
combination of two points of \(B\), then every \(f_j\) and \(c_1\) is
affine on \(B\).

For the fiberwise mode count, let
\[
 L:\mathbb R^m\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\qquad
 L(a)=\sum_{j=1}^m A_j a_j,
\]
and let
\[
 T:\mathbb R^D\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m,\qquad
 T(b)=(\langle\nu_1,b\rangle,\ldots,
       \langle\nu_m,b\rangle).
\]
Every vector in \(\mathbb R^m\) is a support velocity of a sufficiently
small path with the same active normal list. The map \(T\) is injective,
its image is contained in \(\ker L\), and
\[
 \dim\bigl(\ker L/T(\mathbb R^D)\bigr)=m-D-1.
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular, if \(P_0\) is a simplex, then \(m=D+1\), the quotient in
(5) is zero, and every volume-neutral support velocity is the velocity
of a unique translation. Hence, for a convexly compatible simplex
family with \(v_1\equiv0\), there is an affine map \(b:B\to\mathbb R^D\)
such that
\[
 f_j(x)=\langle\nu_j,b(x)\rangle
 \quad(1\leq j\leq D+1),
 \qquad c_1(x)=b(x).
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus such a family cannot have nonaffine first-order centroid motion.

Equations (1) through (6) concern only the coefficient of
\(\varepsilon\). They do not assert that \(h_j(\cdot,\varepsilon)\) is
affine for any fixed positive \(\varepsilon\), that a deformation is an
exact translation, or that first-order volume cancellation is
finite-amplitude volume cancellation. No such conclusion is made without
separate higher-order or uniform remainder information.

## Proof

First we prove the transport formulas rather than invoking a prism
heuristic. For \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_m)\) near \(h^0\), write
\[
 P(q):=\{y:\langle\nu_k,y\rangle\leq q_k,\ 1\leq k\leq m\}.
\]
There is a neighborhood \(U\) of \(h^0\) in which \(P(q)\) is compact,
full-dimensional, and has every one of the \(m\) active facets. Indeed,
an interior point of \(P_0\) remains strictly feasible after a small
change of \(q\). The recession cone
\(\{d:\langle\nu_k,d\rangle\leq0\text{ for all }k\}\) is
\(\{0\}\), because \(P_0\) is bounded, and it is independent of \(q\),
so nearby nonempty polytopes remain bounded. Finally, for each \(j\),
choose \(y_j\in\operatorname{relint}F_j\). If \(q\) is close to
\(h^0\), then
\[
 y_j(q):=y_j+(q_j-h_j^0)\nu_j
\]
satisfies \(\langle\nu_j,y_j(q)\rangle=q_j\), while all other
inequalities remain strict. Hence the \(j\)-th facet remains active.

Fix \(j\). For \(q_{-j}\) near \(h^0_{-j}\) and \(s\) near \(h_j^0\),
define the slice
\[
 S_j(q_{-j},s):=
 \{y:\langle\nu_j,y\rangle=s,
       \ \langle\nu_k,y\rangle\leq q_k\ (k\ne j)\}.
\]
Its area and vector first moment converge as
\((q_{-j},s)\to(h^0_{-j},h_j^0)\):
\[
 \mathcal H_{D-1}(S_j(q_{-j},s))\longrightarrow A_j,
 \qquad
 \int_{S_j(q_{-j},s)}y\,d\mathcal H_{D-1}(y)
 \longrightarrow A_j\bar y_j.
 \tag{7}
\]
Here is a direct proof. Write \(y=z+s\nu_j\), with
\(z\in\nu_j^\perp\). The slice is identified isometrically with
\[
 Q_j(q_{-j},s)=
 \left\{z\in\nu_j^\perp:
 \left\langle
 \nu_k-\langle\nu_k,\nu_j\rangle\nu_j,z
 \right\rangle
 \leq q_k-s\langle\nu_k,\nu_j\rangle,\ k\ne j
 \right\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The base set in (8) is an isometric copy of the bounded facet \(F_j\).
Therefore the recession cone determined by the fixed left-hand sides in
(8) is \(\{0\}\). Compactness of the unit sphere in \(\nu_j^\perp\)
then gives \(\gamma>0\) such that, for every unit
\(u\in\nu_j^\perp\), at least one left-hand-side normal \(w_k\) in
(8) satisfies \(\langle w_k,u\rangle\geq\gamma\). Since the right-hand
sides in (8) stay bounded near the base parameters, every such
\(Q_j(q_{-j},s)\) lies in one fixed ball. Away from the finitely many
base boundary hyperplanes, its indicator converges pointwise to the
indicator of the base set. A projected zero normal can only give an
inequality that is strict on \(F_j\), and it remains strict nearby, so
it causes no exceptional set. Dominated convergence on
\(\nu_j^\perp\) proves both limits in (7), the second because
\(z+s\nu_j\) is uniformly bounded on that fixed ball. The same argument
in dimension zero uses counting measure and is immediate.

Suppose \(q,q'\in U\) differ only in their \(j\)-th coordinates. Slicing
Lebesgue measure by \(s=\langle\nu_j,y\rangle\), whose Jacobian is one
because \(\nu_j\) is a unit vector, gives the exact identities
\[
 \operatorname{vol}P(q')-\operatorname{vol}P(q)
 =\int_{q_j}^{q'_j}
 \mathcal H_{D-1}(S_j(q_{-j},s))\,ds,
 \tag{9}
\]
and
\[
 \int_{P(q')}y\,dy-\int_{P(q)}y\,dy
 =\int_{q_j}^{q'_j}
 \left(\int_{S_j(q_{-j},s)}y\,d\mathcal H_{D-1}(y)\right)ds.
 \tag{10}
\]
The oriented integrals also cover \(q'_j<q_j\).

For \(\delta\to0\), telescope from \(P(h^0)\) to
\(P(h^0+\delta)\) by changing the support coordinates one at a time.
At every step the other support coordinates and the interval of
integration in (9) and (10) tend to their base values. Thus (7) yields
\[
 \operatorname{vol}P(h^0+\delta)
 =V_0+\sum_{j=1}^m A_j\delta_j+o(\|\delta\|),
 \tag{11}
\]
\[
 \int_{P(h^0+\delta)}y\,dy
 =V_0c_0+\sum_{j=1}^m A_j\bar y_j\delta_j
 +o(\|\delta\|).
 \tag{12}
\]
More explicitly, continuity in (7) bounds the total error by
\(\eta\sum_j|\delta_j|\) once \(\delta\) is sufficiently small, and
\(\eta\downarrow0\), which proves the displayed remainders for any
fixed norm on \(\mathbb R^m\).

At a fixed \(x\in B\), (1) says that
\(\delta_j=\varepsilon f_j(x)+o_x(\varepsilon)\). Substitution into
(11) and (12) proves (2) and (3). Since
\(c_\varepsilon=M_\varepsilon/V_\varepsilon\), the quotient rule gives
\[
 c_1(x)
 =\frac{1}{V_0}
 \left(\sum_jA_j\bar y_jf_j(x)-c_0\sum_jA_jf_j(x)\right),
\]
which is (4).

We next use convex compatibility. Fix \(x_0,x_1\in B\),
\(0\leq\theta\leq1\), and
\(x_\theta=\theta x_0+(1-\theta)x_1\). Convexity of
\(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) gives the fiber inclusion
\[
 \theta P_\varepsilon(x_0)+(1-\theta)P_\varepsilon(x_1)
 \subseteq P_\varepsilon(x_\theta).
 \tag{13}
\]
Because every \(j\)-th inequality is facet-defining, its right-hand
side is the actual support value in direction \(\nu_j\):
\[
 h_j(x,\varepsilon)=
 \max_{y\in P_\varepsilon(x)}\langle\nu_j,y\rangle.
\]
Taking support values in (13), and using additivity of a support value
under Minkowski addition, gives
\[
 h_j(x_\theta,\varepsilon)
 \geq\theta h_j(x_0,\varepsilon)
 +(1-\theta)h_j(x_1,\varepsilon).
 \tag{14}
\]
Insert (1), cancel \(h_j^0\), divide by the positive number
\(\varepsilon\), and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). The three
pointwise remainder limits give
\[
 f_j(x_\theta)\geq
 \theta f_j(x_0)+(1-\theta)f_j(x_1),
\]
so \(f_j\) is concave. This also explains exactly why activity and the
one-sided \(o(\varepsilon)\) expansion are the needed hypotheses.

For the rigidity step, define the concavity gap
\[
 g_j:=f_j(x_\theta)-
 \theta f_j(x_0)-(1-\theta)f_j(x_1)\geq0.
\]
Equation (2) gives the exact gap identity
\[
 v_1(x_\theta)-\theta v_1(x_0)-(1-\theta)v_1(x_1)
 =\sum_{j=1}^m A_jg_j.
 \tag{15}
\]
If \(v_1\) is affine, the left-hand side is zero. Since every
\(A_j>0\), (15) forces \(g_j=0\) for every \(j\). This holds for every
pair and every \(\theta\), hence every \(f_j\) is affine on \(B\).
Formula (4) then makes \(c_1\) affine as well. This is the
positive-weight concavity-gap rigidity; it uses no cancellation between
different facets.

It remains to prove the equilibrium identity and the mode count. For
an arbitrary \(b\in\mathbb R^D\), apply the divergence theorem to the
constant vector field \(b\). The outward normal equals \(\nu_j\) on
the relative interior of \(F_j\), while lower-dimensional facet
intersections have surface measure zero. Therefore
\[
 0=\int_{P_0}\operatorname{div}b\,dy
 =\int_{\partial P_0}\langle b,n(y)\rangle
 \,d\mathcal H_{D-1}(y)
 =\left\langle b,\sum_{j=1}^m A_j\nu_j\right\rangle.
\]
Since this holds for every \(b\),
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m A_j\nu_j=0.
 \tag{16}
\]
A translation \(P_0+t b\) changes its \(j\)-th support at velocity
\(\langle\nu_j,b\rangle\), so \(T\) is precisely the translation
velocity map. Equation (16) gives
\[
 L(Tb)=\sum_jA_j\langle\nu_j,b\rangle=0,
\]
and hence \(T(\mathbb R^D)\subseteq\ker L\).

The map \(T\) is injective. Indeed, if \(T(b)=0\), then for any
\(y\in P_0\) and every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \langle\nu_j,y+tb\rangle=\langle\nu_j,y\rangle\leq h_j^0
 \quad\text{for all }j.
\]
Thus \(y+\mathbb Rb\subseteq P_0\), contradicting boundedness unless
\(b=0\). Consequently \(\dim T(\mathbb R^D)=D\). The functional \(L\)
is nonzero because \(A_j>0\), so \(\dim\ker L=m-1\). This proves (5).
The earlier neighborhood argument also shows that every
\(a\in\mathbb R^m\) is realized by \(P(h^0+t a)\) for all sufficiently
small \(|t|\), so this count is the actual fixed-active-normal tangent
count, not merely a formal upper bound.

If \(P_0\) is a simplex, \(m=D+1\), so (5) and the inclusion already
proved imply \(\ker L=T(\mathbb R^D)\). For a family with
\(v_1\equiv0\), the vector \(f(x)=(f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x))\) lies in
\(\ker L\) at every \(x\), and there is a unique \(b(x)\) with
\(f(x)=T(b(x))\). Every component of \(f\) is affine by (15), and the
linear inverse of \(T:\mathbb R^D\to T(\mathbb R^D)\) shows that \(b\)
is affine. Finally, apply the already proved centroid transport formula
to the exact translated path \(P_0+t b\). Its support velocity is
\(T(b)\) and its centroid is \(c_0+t b\), so (4) gives
\[
 \frac1{V_0}\sum_jA_j\langle\nu_j,b\rangle
 (\bar y_j-c_0)=b.
\]
Substituting \(b=b(x)\) proves \(c_1(x)=b(x)\) and completes (6).
