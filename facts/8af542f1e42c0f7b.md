## Statement

Assume the complete triangular-homothetic saturated three-circuit setting and notation of accepted facts 05e841287760f20f, 6ce65197bbcae680, d33ef001609a8cc8, and f67005b3d1b63d77.  Thus
\[
 C_z=c_z+\kappa_z\mathcal T,
 \qquad
 a_z=A\kappa_z^2,
 \qquad
 A:=\operatorname{area}(\mathcal T),
\tag{1}
\]
\[
 U_0:=\sum_{u\in U}a_u,
 \qquad
 L_z:=\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}
 \quad(z\in B_0),
 \qquad
 L_0:=\sum_{z\in B_0}L_z.
\tag{2}
\]
For every \(z\in B_0\), the normalized thresholds satisfy
\[
 \xi_{i,z}\leq\frac13,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,z}
 =a_z\bigl(2\xi_{i,z}^+-(\xi_{i,z}^+)^2\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\rho\in\Delta_3\), retain the common globally affine slack map
\[
 \langle u_i,q^\rho(z)\rangle
 =\ell_i(z)-\rho_i\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_j(z)
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(\mathcal Z_+:=\{z\in B_0:L_z>0\}\), let \(J_z\) be the interval assigned to \(z\) in accepted fact 05e841287760f20f, and join \(z,z'\) when \(J_z\cap J_{z'}=\varnothing\).  Let \(\pi\) be any maximum fractional matching in this graph with
\[
 d_z(\pi):=\sum_{e\ni z}\pi_e\leq L_z,
 \qquad
 \Pi:=\sum_e\pi_e.
\tag{5}
\]
Define the total loss overflow
\[
 \varepsilon_z:=(L_z-a_z)^+,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E:=\sum_{z\in B_0}\varepsilon_z.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the following conclusions hold.

1. The overflow has the one-profile bound
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E\leq\frac G3.}
\tag{7}
\]
More precisely, there is one index \(k_*\in\{1,2,3\}\) such that the single affine profile on \(q^{e_{k_*}}\), where \(e_{k_*}\) is a vertex of \(\Delta_3\), has total metronoid mass at least \(3\mathcal E\).

2. There is a submatching \(\mu\leq\pi\) with physical vertex capacities
\[
 h_z:=d_z(\mu)\leq a_z,
 \qquad
 P:=\sum_e\mu_e,
\tag{8}
\]
such that
\[
 \boxed{\Pi-P\leq\mathcal E\leq\frac G3.}
\tag{9}
\]

3. For every parity class \(\omega\) meeting \(\operatorname{supp}h\), put
\[
 Z_\omega:=\{z\in Z:z\equiv\omega\pmod2\}.
\]
There is a loopless fractional matching
\(\nu^\omega=(\nu^\omega_{\{x,y\}})\) on \(Z_\omega\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{y\in Z_\omega\setminus\{x\}}\nu^\omega_{\{x,y\}}=a_x
 \qquad(x\in Z_\omega),
\tag{10}
\]
whose positive edges have pairwise distinct midpoints within the fixed color \(\omega\).  For the union \(\nu\) of these colored matchings, extend \(h_z\) by zero to the unused vertices of the relevant parity classes and define
\[
 r_z:=\frac{h_z}{a_z}\in[0,1],
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 \chi_e:=\frac{\nu_e}{2}(r_x+r_y),
 \qquad
 w_e:=\frac{x+y}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 p_e:=\frac{c_x+c_y}{2}
 \quad(e=\{x,y\}).
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
 0\leq\chi_e\leq\nu_e,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\sum_e\chi_e=P,}
\tag{13}
\]
and every positive transported mass is physically feasible:
\[
 \boxed{p_e\in\mathsf M_{\chi_e}(C_{w_e})\quad(\chi_e>0).}
\tag{14}
\]
No source fiber is used beyond its area in (10).  Every \(w_e\) is integral.  Repeated midpoints can occur only between edges of different parity colors; within one color they do not occur.

4. In view of (9), the narrower physical charge
\[
 \boxed{P\leq\frac23G+U_0}
\tag{15}
\]
is sufficient for the missing matching charge:
\[
 \Pi\leq G+U_0.
\tag{16}
\]
A sufficient geometric statement for (15) is the following rainbow-midpoint affine-amplification property.  After charging an amount
\[
 0\leq U_{\mathrm{ch}}\leq U_0
\tag{17}
\]
to distinct physical fibers in \(U\), there exist a finite lattice support \(S\), masses \(0<\tau_x\leq a_x\), and one globally affine map \(Q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^2\) such that every physical fiber is used once,
\[
 Q(x)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_x}(C_x)
 \qquad(x\in S),
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{x\in S}\tau_x
 \geq\frac32(P-U_{\mathrm{ch}}).}
\tag{19}
\]
It is enough in (18) that \(Q=q^\rho\) for one common \(\rho\), but the statement permits any one affine complete-core profile.

Conclusions (7), (9), and (10)-(14), and the sufficiency implications
\[
 (19)\Longrightarrow(15)\Longrightarrow(16),
\tag{20}
\]
are asserted and proved below.  The amplification property (17)-(19) itself is not proved.  Consequently neither (15), nor (16), nor the unconditional registered dispersion-charge inequality is asserted by this lemma.

## Proof

We first prove the overflow bound.  Fix \(z\in B_0\) with \(\varepsilon_z>0\).  Since \(z\) is clipped, at least one normalized threshold is negative.  Equation (3) shows that a nonpositive threshold contributes zero loss and that one positive threshold contributes at most \(5a_z/9<a_z\).  Therefore exactly two thresholds are positive.  Write
\[
 x:=\xi_{i,z},
 \qquad
 y:=\xi_{j,z},
 \qquad
 0<x,y\leq\frac13,
\tag{21}
\]
and let \(k\) be the unique index with \(\xi_{k,z}<0\).

Put
\[
 s_z:=1-\sum_{m=1}^3\xi_{m,z}.
\]
The barycentric-coordinate identity in accepted facts 05e841287760f20f and 6ce65197bbcae680 is
\[
 \beta_{m,z}(q^\rho(z))
 =\xi_{m,z}+s_z\rho_m.
\tag{22}
\]
At the vertex \(\rho=e_k\), equation (22) gives, in the order
\((i,j,k)\),
\[
 \bigl(\beta_{i,z},\beta_{j,z},\beta_{k,z}\bigr)
 =\bigl(x,y,1-x-y\bigr).
\tag{23}
\]
The third coordinate is at least \(1/3\).  Apply the simplex transportation theorem in accepted fact 75e9cb08300c59fb.  Its relative-area product is exactly
\[
 (3x)(3y)=9xy,
\tag{24}
\]
where the same formula remains literal if \(x=1/3\) or \(y=1/3\), because the corresponding factor then equals one.  Hence \(C_z\) contains a subtriangle centered at \(q^{e_k}(z)\) and of area \(9a_zxy\).

The loss formula (3) gives
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon_z}{a_z}
 =2x-x^2+2y-y^2-1.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(0\leq x,y\leq1/3\),
\[
 xy-\bigl(2x-x^2+2y-y^2-1\bigr)
 =x^2+y^2+xy-2x-2y+1
 \geq0.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, both partial derivatives of the last expression are negative on
\([0,1/3]^2\), so its minimum is its value zero at
\((1/3,1/3)\).  Equations (24)-(26) give
\[
 9\varepsilon_z\leq9a_zxy\leq a_z.
\]
Shrinking the transported subtriangle about its centroid proves
\[
 q^{e_k}(z)\in\mathsf M_{9\varepsilon_z}(C_z).
\tag{27}
\]

Partition the positive overflows by their unique negative index:
\[
 \mathcal E_k
 :=\sum_{\substack{z\in B_0:\ \varepsilon_z>0\\
                    \xi_{k,z}<0}}\varepsilon_z,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E=\mathcal E_1+\mathcal E_2+\mathcal E_3.
\tag{28}
\]
Choose \(k_*\) with
\(\mathcal E_{k_*}\geq\mathcal E/3\).  If
\(\mathcal E=0\), conclusion (7) is immediate, so suppose otherwise.
On the finite support in the \(k_*\)-th sum, assign masses
\[
 \tau_z:=9\varepsilon_z.
\]
Equation (27) puts these masses on the one globally affine graph
\(q^{e_{k_*}}\).

Apply the \(d=0\) case of the weighted lattice centerpoint theorem in accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to
\(\sum_z\tau_z\delta_z\).  It gives an integral center
\(\widehat m\) of positive base depth, hence
\(\widehat m\in\operatorname{conv}\{z:\tau_z>0\}\).  For every
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(\{z:\tau_z>0\})\), affinity gives
\[
 q^{e_{k_*}}(\widehat m)
 =\sum_z\lambda_zq^{e_{k_*}}(z)
 \in\sum_z\lambda_z\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z).
\tag{29}
\]
Thus this selected profile has nonempty complete metronoid core.  The nonempty-core conclusion of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb gives
\[
 G\geq\sum_z\tau_z
 =9\mathcal E_{k_*}
 \geq3\mathcal E.
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (7).  Notice that (30) uses only the maximizing color
\(k_*\); it does not add three separate bounds on \(G\).

We next trim the matching.  Start with \(\mu:=\pi\).  Whenever a vertex
\(z\) has current degree greater than \(a_z\), decrease weights on its
incident positive edges by total amount exactly
\(d_z(\mu)-a_z\).  This is possible because their current total is
\(d_z(\mu)\).  A decrease cannot create an overload at another vertex,
so after processing the finite vertex set one obtains
\[
 \mu\leq\pi,
 \qquad
 d_z(\mu)\leq a_z.
\tag{31}
\]
The amount removed while processing \(z\) is no larger than its initial
overload, and
\[
 (d_z(\pi)-a_z)^+
 \leq(L_z-a_z)^+
 =\varepsilon_z.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Pi-P\leq\sum_z\varepsilon_z=\mathcal E.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (7) and (33) prove (9).

We now construct the colored parity transport.  Fix a parity class
\(\omega\) meeting \(\operatorname{supp}h\).  Since
\(\operatorname{supp}h\subseteq B_0\), the definition of \(B_0\) says
that \(Z_\omega\cap U=\varnothing\).  The actual maximum same-parity
matching in accepted fact 6ce65197bbcae680 therefore saturates every
vertex of \(Z_\omega\): its degree at \(z\) is \(a_z/2\).  Doubling its
edge weights proves that the polytope of loopless fractional matchings
satisfying (10) is nonempty.  It is compact.

Choose from this polytope a minimizer of
\[
 \sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Z_\omega}
 \nu^\omega_{\{x,y\}}\lVert x-y\rVert^2.
\tag{34}
\]
We prove midpoint uniqueness within \(\omega\).  Suppose two distinct
positive edges satisfy
\[
 x+y=x'+y'=2w.
\tag{35}
\]
Edges with one common endpoint and the same sum are equal, so the four
endpoints in (35) are distinct.  Write
\[
 x=w+u,\quad y=w-u,
 \qquad
 x'=w+v,\quad y'=w-v.
\]
Let
\[
 \delta:=\min\{\nu^\omega_{\{x,y\}},
                 \nu^\omega_{\{x',y'\}}\}>0.
\]
Remove \(\delta\) from the two old edges.  One may add \(\delta\) either
to the pair of edges
\[
 \{x,x'\},\{y,y'\}
\]
or to the pair
\[
 \{x,y'\},\{y,x'\}.
\]
Both switches are loopless, preserve every degree, and remain inside
\(Z_\omega\).  The two possible new squared-length sums are
\[
 2\lVert u-v\rVert^2,
 \qquad
 2\lVert u+v\rVert^2.
\tag{36}
\]
Their sum is
\[
 4(\lVert u\rVert^2+\lVert v\rVert^2),
\]
which is also the old squared-length sum
\[
 \lVert2u\rVert^2+\lVert2v\rVert^2.
\tag{37}
\]
Because the old sum is positive, at least one quantity in (36) is at
most half of (37).  Using that switch strictly decreases (34), a
contradiction.  Hence positive edges have distinct midpoints within the
fixed parity color.  Since \(x\equiv y\pmod2\), every such midpoint is
integral.  Repeating this construction independently in each relevant
parity class gives \(\nu\).

Extend \(h_z\) by zero as in the statement.  Equations (8) and (10) give
\(0\leq r_z\leq1\).  Consequently (12) gives
\[
 0\leq\chi_e\leq\nu_e.
\tag{38}
\]
Summing by endpoints and using (10),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_e\chi_e
 &=\frac12\sum_e\nu_e(r_x+r_y)\\
 &=\frac12\sum_zr_zd_z(\nu)\\
 &=\frac12\sum_zh_z\\
 &=P,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
because \(\sum_zh_z=2\sum_e\mu_e=2P\).  This proves (13).

The source allocation is literal at the density level.  For every
incidence \(x\in e\), use on \(C_x\) the constant density
\[
 f_{x,e}:=\frac{\nu_e}{a_x}\mathbf 1_{C_x}.
\]
It has mass \(\nu_e\) and barycenter \(c_x\), and (10) gives
\[
 \sum_{e\ni x}f_{x,e}=\mathbf 1_{C_x}
 \quad\text{almost everywhere on }C_x.
\]
Thus the edge allocations neither exceed nor reuse any physical source
area.

For \(e=\{x,y\}\), convexity of the actual body and (1) give
\[
 \frac12C_x+\frac12C_y
 =
 p_e+\frac{\kappa_x+\kappa_y}{2}\mathcal T
 \subseteq C_{w_e}.
\tag{40}
\]
The inner triangle in (40) has area
\[
 A\left(\frac{\kappa_x+\kappa_y}{2}\right)^2
 \geq A\kappa_x\kappa_y
 =\sqrt{a_xa_y}
 \geq\min\{a_x,a_y\}.
\tag{41}
\]
Since a single edge weight is no larger than either endpoint degree in
(10),
\[
 \nu_e\leq\min\{a_x,a_y\}.
\tag{42}
\]
Equations (38), (40)-(42) show that the inner triangle can be shrunk
about \(p_e\) to area \(\chi_e\) whenever \(\chi_e>0\).  Its indicator
is an admissible metronoid density, proving (14); zero-charge edges are
ignored.  Source ownership is exact because
\(\sum_{e\ni z}\nu_e=a_z\).  Midpoint uniqueness was proved only within
one parity color; edges from different colors can still have the same
integral midpoint.  This proves every assertion in conclusion 3.

It remains to verify the conditional arithmetic and to delimit the
unproved step.  If (15) is available, (9) gives
\[
 \Pi
 \leq P+\frac G3
 \leq\frac23G+U_0+\frac G3
 =G+U_0,
\]
which is (16).  Accepted fact 05e841287760f20f then combines (16) with
\[
 L_0\leq3M+2\Pi
\]
to give \(L_0\leq3M+2G+2U_0\); its conditional conclusion, together
with accepted fact f67005b3d1b63d77, gives the registered
dispersion-charge inequality under the stated hard hypotheses.

Finally suppose the affine-amplification property (17)-(19) is
available.  By affinity of \(Q\), the same argument as in (29), using a
weighted lattice centerpoint for the profile \((\tau_x)_{x\in S}\),
shows that it has nonempty complete core.  Accepted fact
07009c5047a9f7bb therefore gives
\[
 G\geq\sum_{x\in S}\tau_x
 \geq\frac32(P-U_{\mathrm{ch}}).
\tag{43}
\]
Thus
\[
 P\leq\frac23G+U_{\mathrm{ch}}
 \leq\frac23G+U_0,
\]
which proves the first implication in (20); the second was just proved.

No construction of \(U_{\mathrm{ch}},S,(\tau_x)\), or \(Q\) satisfying
(17)-(19) has been supplied.  Equations (12)-(14) alone do not provide
one affine graph: different parity colors may collide at the same
midpoint, and chord centers at distinct midpoints need not be affine.
The exact remaining theorem must resolve all rainbow midpoint stacks
without reusing a midpoint fiber and aggregate the non-\(U\) mass into
one common-\(\rho\) graph or one affine complete-core profile with the
factor \(3/2\) in (19).  Pairwise collision bounds or separate
applications of the capacity \(G\) do not prove that amplification.
Therefore (15), (16), the amplification property, and the full
registered conclusion remain outside the assertions of this lemma.
