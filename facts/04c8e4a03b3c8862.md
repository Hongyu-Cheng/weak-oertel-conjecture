## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \([n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and let \(b:2^{[n]}\to[0,\infty)\) satisfy \(M:=b(\varnothing)>0\) and \(b(A\cap B)^2\ge b(A)b(B)\) for all \(A,B\subseteq[n]\).

For \(z\in\{0,1,2\}^n\), set \(w_z:=b(\{i:z_i=2\})\), and set \(w_z:=0\) at all other points of \(\mathbb Z^n\). Put \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\) and \(T:=2^{-n}W\). For every fixed integer center \(j\) with \(w_j>0\), put \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). For \(L\ge0\), put \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\). These are exactly the scalar definitions and oriented-displacement normalization of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; the suppressed dependence of \(\alpha(v)\) on the fixed center is unchanged, and zero is included in the sum. All logarithms are natural and are taken only at positive weights.

All defined curvatures are nonnegative. For every positive center define \(K_j:=\max_{L\ge0}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\), and put \(K:=\max_{j:w_j>0}K_j\). These maxima are attained. Then

\[
K\ge\max_{\substack{j\in\{1,2\}^n\\w_j>0}}K_j
\ge\max\left\{M,\frac{T^2}{20M}\right\}
\ge\frac{T}{\sqrt{20}}.
\]

The intersection hypothesis implies that every positive superlevel family of \(b\) is closed under intersection. It does not require the positive support of \(b\) to be a downset, nor require \(b(A)\ge b(B)\) for every \(A\subseteq B\) when \(b(A)=0\). For the general hypothesis the center used to establish a threshold estimate is allowed to depend on that threshold; the displayed maximum nevertheless yields one final center and cutoff.

If, in addition, \(b\) is inclusion-nonincreasing, meaning \(b(A)\ge b(B)\) whenever \(A\subseteq B\), the single prescribed center \(\mathbf 1:=(1,\ldots,1)\) satisfies the stronger conclusion

\[
K_{\mathbf 1}\ge\max\left\{M,\frac{T^2}{20M}\right\}
\ge\frac{T}{\sqrt{20}}.
\]

Inclusion monotonicity alone implies the intersection hypothesis. In particular, this fixed-center conclusion holds for \(b(A)=g(\sum_{i\in A}c_i)\) with arbitrary \(c_i>0\) and any nonnegative nonincreasing function \(g:[0,\sum_i c_i]\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying \(g(0)>0\); log-concavity of \(g\) is not required.

The underlying counting conclusion holds for every nonempty family \(\mathcal D\subseteq2^{[n]}\), without a closure assumption. Define \(Z_{\mathcal D}:=\sum_{A\in\mathcal D}2^{-|A|}\) and \(r_{\mathcal D}(J):=|\{(A,B)\in\mathcal D^2:A\cap B=J\}|\), where pairs are ordered. Then \(\max_{J\subseteq[n]}r_{\mathcal D}(J)\ge Z_{\mathcal D}^2\). If \(\mathcal D\) is a downset, then \(r_{\mathcal D}(\varnothing)\ge r_{\mathcal D}(J)\) for every \(J\), so the same lower bound holds at the empty intersection.

Every inclusion-nonincreasing \(b\) with \(M>0\) has the following exact realization for each integer \(d\ge1\). On \([0,1]^n\) define

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(u)&:=\min_{A\subseteq[n]}
\left\{b(A)^{1/d}+M^{1/d}\sum_{i\in A}(1-u_i)\right\},\\
\rho(x)&:=F\bigl((x_1-1)_+,\ldots,(x_n-1)_+\bigr)
\quad(x\in[0,2]^n),
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(s_+:=\max\{s,0\}\). The set

\[
C^{(b,d)}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
x\in[0,2]^n,\quad 0\le y_k\le\rho(x)\ (1\le k\le d)\}
\]

is a convex body, and its integer fiber volumes are exactly \(w_z\) at every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), including all zero weights. Thus the monotone conclusion has actual common-body instances in every continuous dimension, without assuming any previously specified continuous extension has a concave \(d\)-th root.

For the depth consequence, let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body. Define the actual fibers \(C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\}\), actual volumes \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero. Suppose these actual volumes have the stated ternary plateau form for a set function \(b\) with \(M:=b(\varnothing)>0\), and again put \(T:=2^{-n}W\). In this actual-body situation the intersection hypothesis follows from convexity and requires no additional assumption. All actual-body and cutoff objects retain their meanings in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`.

With \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\), there is one point \(p^\star\in S\), whose integer coordinate belongs to \(\{1,2\}^n\), such that

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{\ell}{16}
\max\left\{M,\frac{(2^{-n}W)^2}{20M}\right\}
\ge\frac{\ell}{16\sqrt{20}}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

If \(b\) is inclusion-nonincreasing, the point can be chosen in \(\{\mathbf 1\}\times C_{\mathbf 1}\). In either case this same point satisfies the bound for every containing closed ambient halfspace. The coefficient \(\ell/(16\sqrt{20})\) is absolute and belongs to \((0,1/e)\).

## Proof

First we prove the counting assertion. Fix a nonempty \(\mathcal D\subseteq2^{[n]}\). Let \(X,Y\) be independent random sets with the same distribution \(\mathbb P(X=A)=\mathbb P(Y=A)=2^{-|A|}/Z_{\mathcal D}\) for \(A\in\mathcal D\). Write \(I:=X\cap Y\) and \(q_J:=\mathbb P(I=J)\). We use finite Shannon entropy with natural logarithms, \(\mathsf H(U):=-\sum_u\mathbb P(U=u)\log\mathbb P(U=u)\), and conditional entropy as its average over the conditioning variable, with \(0\log0=0\).

The entropy identity needed here is

\[
\mathsf H(I\mid X)+\mathsf H(I\mid Y)-\mathsf H(I)
=\mathsf H(X\mid I)+\mathsf H(Y\mid I)-\mathsf H(X,Y\mid I)
\ge0.
\]

To verify the equality, expand conditional entropies by the chain rule, use \(\mathsf H(X,Y)=\mathsf H(X)+\mathsf H(Y)\) from independence, and use \(\mathsf H(I,X,Y)=\mathsf H(X,Y)\) because \(I\) is a function of \((X,Y)\). The expression on the right is the average, over values of \(I\), of the relative entropy of the conditional joint distribution of \((X,Y)\) with respect to the product of its two conditional marginals. This relative entropy is nonnegative: for probability vectors \(p,q\) with \(q_a>0\) whenever \(p_a>0\), the elementary inequality \(-\log u\ge1-u\) gives \(\sum_{a:p_a>0}p_a\log(p_a/q_a)\ge1-\sum_{a:p_a>0}q_a\ge0\). Every positive entry of a joint distribution has positive marginals, so this applies to each conditioning value. The same argument against the uniform probability law shows that a random variable with at most \(N\) possible values has entropy at most \(\log N\).

Given \(X=A\), the intersection \(I\) is a subset of \(A\) and therefore has at most \(2^{|A|}\) values. Consequently \(\mathsf H(I\mid X)\le(\log2)\mathbb E|X|\). The analogous bound given \(Y\), the identical distributions of \(X,Y\), and the preceding identity imply \(\mathsf H(I)\le2(\log2)\mathbb E|X|\).

For every \(J\) with \(q_J>0\), the sampling distribution gives the exact identity

\[
r_{\mathcal D}(J)
=Z_{\mathcal D}^2q_J\,
\mathbb E\bigl[2^{|X|+|Y|}\mid I=J\bigr].
\]

Indeed, multiplying each conditional pair probability by \(Z_{\mathcal D}^2q_J2^{|X|+|Y|}\) gives one. Jensen's inequality for the exponential then gives

\[
\log\frac{r_{\mathcal D}(J)}{Z_{\mathcal D}^2}
\ge\log q_J+(\log2)\mathbb E[|X|+|Y|\mid I=J].
\]

Average over \(q_J\). The result is

\[
\sum_{J:q_J>0}q_J
\log\frac{r_{\mathcal D}(J)}{Z_{\mathcal D}^2}
\ge-\mathsf H(I)+2(\log2)\mathbb E|X|
\ge0.
\]

At least one logarithm in this finite weighted average is nonnegative, proving \(\max_Jr_{\mathcal D}(J)\ge Z_{\mathcal D}^2\). All probabilities and entropies used above are finite, and values with \(q_J=0\) were omitted before logarithms were taken.

If \(\mathcal D\) is a downset, fix \(J\). The map \((A,B)\mapsto(A,B\setminus J)\), restricted to pairs in \(\mathcal D^2\) with \(A\cap B=J\), has its image in disjoint ordered pairs of \(\mathcal D\): the second component stays in \(\mathcal D\) by downward closure. It is injective for this fixed \(J\), because the original second component is recovered by adjoining \(J\). Thus \(r_{\mathcal D}(J)\le r_{\mathcal D}(\varnothing)\). This proves the downset refinement directly from the intersection count.

We now turn to the plateau profile. Applying its hypothesis with \(A=\varnothing\) gives \(M^2\ge M b(B)\), hence \(0\le b(B)\le M\) for every \(B\). If \(b(A),b(B)\ge u>0\), then \(b(A\cap B)^2\ge u^2\), so \(b(A\cap B)\ge u\). This proves the asserted intersection closure of every positive superlevel family. For example, when \(n=2\), the values \(b(\varnothing)=b(\{1,2\})=1\) and \(b(\{1\})=b(\{2\})=0\) satisfy the intersection hypothesis: a pair of positive entries intersects in either \(\varnothing\) or \(\{1,2\}\), with value one, and every other right side is zero. This example is not inclusion-nonincreasing and its positive support is not a downset.

To check all defined curvatures, fix \(j\) with \(w_j>0\) and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). Both endpoints and the center lie in the ternary cube. If \(j_i\) is zero or two, the two endpoints can lie in \([0,2]\) only when \(v_i=0\). If \(j_i=1\), the endpoint pair is \((0,2)\), \((1,1)\), or \((2,0)\). Thus, writing \(A:=\{i:(j+v)_i=2\}\) and \(B:=\{i:(j-v)_i=2\}\), one has \(A\cap B=\{i:j_i=2\}\). The intersection hypothesis gives \(w_j^2\ge w_{j+v}w_{j-v}>0\), proving that \(\Delta_j(v)\) is finite and nonnegative. It is even, and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\).

There are finitely many positive centers and finitely many positive reflected pairs at each center. Each \(\mathcal U_j\) contains zero, with \(\alpha(0)=w_j>0\). At a fixed center the cutoff numerator changes only at the finite nonempty set \(\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\), which contains zero. Replacing any \(L\ge0\) by the largest such value at most \(L\) preserves its numerator and cannot increase its denominator. This proves attainment of every \(K_j\), of \(K\), and of the maximum restricted to centers in \(\{1,2\}^n\).

Put \(f(A):=b(A)/M\). For \(t\ge0\) define \(\mathcal D_t:=\{A\subseteq[n]:f(A)\ge e^{-t}\}\) and \(Z(t):=\sum_{A\in\mathcal D_t}2^{-|A|}\). Since \(f(\varnothing)=1\) and \(0\le f\le1\), each \(\mathcal D_t\) is nonempty and is a positive superlevel family closed under intersection. The counting assertion supplies \(J\) with \(r_{\mathcal D_t}(J)\ge Z(t)^2>0\). In particular, some pair of members intersects in \(J\), so \(J\in\mathcal D_t\).

Let \(\mathbf 1_J\in\{0,1\}^n\) be the indicator vector of \(J\), and choose \(j(J):=\mathbf 1+\mathbf 1_J\). It has weight \(b(J)>0\). For each ordered pair \((A,B)\in\mathcal D_t^2\) with \(A\cap B=J\), set \(v:=\mathbf 1_{A\setminus J}-\mathbf 1_{B\setminus J}\). At coordinates in \(J\) both endpoints equal two. At coordinates in \(A\setminus J\) their values are two and zero; at coordinates in \(B\setminus J\) their values are zero and two; at all remaining coordinates both equal one. Therefore the labels of the two endpoints are exactly \(A\) and \(B\), so

\[
\alpha(v)=\min\{b(A),b(B)\}\ge Me^{-t},
\qquad
0\le\Delta_{j(J)}(v)
=\log\frac{b(J)^2}{b(A)b(B)}\le2t.
\]

The upper bound uses \(b(J)\le M\), and the lower curvature bound uses the intersection hypothesis. For this fixed \(J\), the positive and negative coordinates of \(v\), together with \(J\), recover \(A\) and \(B\), so the map is injective. Reversing the ordered pair reverses \(v\); the pair \((J,J)\) gives zero. Consequently this is exactly oriented counting, including zero, and it proves

\[
B_{j(J)}^\Delta(2t)
\ge Me^{-t}r_{\mathcal D_t}(J)
\ge Me^{-t}Z(t)^2.
\]

Denote the restricted maximum by \(K_{12}:=\max_{j\in\{1,2\}^n:w_j>0}K_j\). It is positive and attained, and \(K_{12}\le K\). The last inequality at each threshold gives \(Z(t)\le\sqrt{(K_{12}/M)e^t(1+2t)}\) for every \(t\ge0\). Although \(J\) may depend on \(t\), this estimate uses the same scalar \(K_{12}\) for all thresholds; it does not sum masses from different centers or interchange a center maximum with an integral.

For a fixed label \(A\), exactly \(2^{n-|A|}\) ternary sites have their coordinates equal to two precisely on \(A\), by choosing zero or one independently elsewhere. It follows that \(T/M=\sum_A2^{-|A|}f(A)\). For every \(a\in[0,1]\), including \(a=0\), one has \(a=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\mathbf 1_{\{a\ge e^{-t}\}}\,dt\). Summing this identity over the finitely many labels gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T}{M}
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}Z(t)\,dt\\
&\le\sqrt{\frac{K_{12}}{M}}
\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}\sqrt{1+2t}\,dt\\
&\le\sqrt{\frac{20K_{12}}{M}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last step, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the square of the integral by \((\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}\,dt)(\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}(1+2t)\,dt)=2(2+8)=20\); the second integral uses \(\int_0^\infty t e^{-t/2}\,dt=4\), obtained by integration by parts. Thus \(K_{12}\ge T^2/(20M)\). The center \(\mathbf 1\) has weight \(M\), and its zero displacement contributes \(M\) at cutoff zero, so \(K_{12}\ge K_{\mathbf 1}\ge M\). Finally the maximum of two nonnegative numbers is at least their geometric mean, which gives \(\max\{M,T^2/(20M)\}\ge T/\sqrt{20}\). This proves the general scalar conclusion.

Suppose now that \(b\) is inclusion-nonincreasing. Since \(A\cap B\subseteq A,B\), one has \(b(A\cap B)\ge\max\{b(A),b(B)\}\), proving the intersection hypothesis. Each \(\mathcal D_t\) is a downset. The downset refinement of the counting assertion gives \(r_{\mathcal D_t}(\varnothing)\ge Z(t)^2\), so the entire preceding displacement construction can use \(J=\varnothing\) and the same center \(j(\varnothing)=\mathbf 1\) at every threshold. In particular \(B_{\mathbf 1}^\Delta(2t)\ge Me^{-t}Z(t)^2\) for all \(t\). Repeating the displayed integration with \(K_{\mathbf 1}\) in place of \(K_{12}\) gives \(K_{\mathbf 1}\ge T^2/(20M)\). Its zero displacement gives \(K_{\mathbf 1}\ge M\), proving the fixed-center conclusion. For the stated resource profile, \(A\subseteq B\) implies \(\sum_{i\in A}c_i\le\sum_{i\in B}c_i\), so the monotonicity of \(g\) gives precisely the required inclusion monotonicity of \(b\), including zero values.

We verify the exact realization. Fix an inclusion-nonincreasing \(b\) and an integer \(d\ge1\). Every affine expression in the definition of \(F\) is nonnegative on \([0,1]^n\) and coordinatewise nonincreasing. Their finite minimum is continuous, nonnegative, and coordinatewise nonincreasing. It is concave: each affine expression at a convex combination equals that combination of its endpoint expressions, which is at least the corresponding combination of the two minima; taking the minimum preserves this lower bound.

At a Boolean vertex \(\mathbf 1_S\), the expression indexed by \(A=S\) equals \(b(S)^{1/d}\). Every other expression is at least this value. Indeed, if \(A\subseteq S\), its penalty term is zero and \(b(A)^{1/d}\ge b(S)^{1/d}\). If \(A\not\subseteq S\), at least one penalty summand is one, so its value is at least \(M^{1/d}\ge b(S)^{1/d}\). Hence \(F(\mathbf 1_S)=b(S)^{1/d}\), also when \(b(S)=0\). In particular, \(F(0)=M^{1/d}\).

For \(x,x'\in[0,2]^n\) and \(0\le\lambda\le1\), convexity of each function \((x_i-1)_+\) gives the coordinatewise inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl((\lambda x_i+(1-\lambda)x_i'-1)_+\bigr)_{i=1}^n
&\le\lambda\bigl((x_i-1)_+\bigr)_{i=1}^n\\
&\qquad +(1-\lambda)\bigl((x_i'-1)_+\bigr)_{i=1}^n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Applying the nonincreasing property of \(F\), followed by its concavity, proves \(\rho(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)x')\ge\lambda\rho(x)+(1-\lambda)\rho(x')\). Thus \(\rho\) is continuous, nonnegative, and concave on the box. Moreover \(\rho(x)=M^{1/d}\) on \([0,1]^n\).

These facts make \(C^{(b,d)}\) compact and convex: a convex combination preserves the base box and each lower bound on \(y_k\), and its upper bound on \(y_k\) follows from concavity of \(\rho\). The empty-label term in \(F\) bounds \(\rho\le M^{1/d}\), ensuring boundedness. The set contains \([0,1]^n\times[0,M^{1/d}]^d\), whose interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\) is nonempty, so it is a convex body. At any ternary integer point \(z\), the vector \(((z_i-1)_+)_{i=1}^n\) is the Boolean indicator of \(\{i:z_i=2\}\). Its fiber is the box \([0,\rho(z)]^d\), of volume \(\rho(z)^d=b(\{i:z_i=2\})\). This includes zero-volume singleton fibers when \(\rho(z)=0\); outside the base box the fiber is empty. The required equality therefore holds at every integer site.

Finally let \(C\) be an actual convex body as in the depth assertion. We first justify the intersection hypothesis rather than assuming it. Given labels \(A,B\), use the integer base points \(2\mathbf 1_A\) and \(2\mathbf 1_B\), whose weights are \(b(A),b(B)\). If either is zero, the intersection inequality is automatic. Otherwise both fibers have positive volume, and their integer midpoint is \(m:=\mathbf 1_A+\mathbf 1_B\). Convexity gives \((C_{2\mathbf 1_A}+C_{2\mathbf 1_B})/2\subseteq C_m\). In particular \(w_m>0\): fix a point of the second fiber and translate a half-scale copy of the first fiber into \(C_m\). We may thus apply the midpoint fiber-volume inequality used in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` at the positive center \(m\). It gives

\[
b(A\cap B)^{1/d}=w_m^{1/d}
\ge\frac{b(A)^{1/d}+b(B)^{1/d}}2
\ge\bigl(b(A)b(B)\bigr)^{1/(2d)}.
\]

The label of \(m\) is \(A\cap B\), and the last inequality is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. Raising to the power \(2d\) proves the intersection hypothesis. Also \(W\ge M>0\), so all hypotheses of the actual-body predecessor hold.

By the proved scalar assertion and attainment, select one positive \(j\in\{1,2\}^n\) and one finite \(L\ge0\) satisfying \(B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\ge\max\{M,T^2/(20M)\}\). Under inclusion monotonicity take \(j=\mathbf 1\). For this fixed pair set \(q_v:=\alpha(v)\) when \(v\in\mathcal U_j\) and \(\Delta_j(v)\le L\), and set \(q_v:=0\) otherwise. This is the even thinning of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`, with its exact notation \(M_q=\sum_vq_v=B_j^\Delta(L)>0\) and \(\overline\Delta_{j,q}=M_q^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}q_v\Delta_j(v)\le L\). The scalar \(M=b(\varnothing)\) is not this selected mass \(M_q\); no fiber volumes or cutoff masses have been rescaled in this application.

The fixed-center assertion of that accepted fact supplies \(x_{j,q}^\star\in C_j\) for which, with \(p^\star:=(j,x_{j,q}^\star)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(p^\star)
&\ge\frac{\ell M_q}{16(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2)}\\
&\ge\frac{\ell}{16}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}\\
&\ge\frac{\ell}{16}\max\left\{M,\frac{T^2}{20M}\right\}
\ge\frac{\ell}{16\sqrt{20}}\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2\le L+1\). The same body \(C\), fibers \(C_z\), actual volumes \(w_z\), mass \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\), reflected weights \(\alpha(v)\), positive displacement set \(\mathcal U_j\), curvatures \(\Delta_j(v)\), and oriented cutoff sums \(B_j^\Delta\) are used throughout, exactly as in the accepted fact. Its point is chosen after \(j,q\) and before any halfspace, and its conclusion concerns all closed ambient halfspaces, including those with purely integer-coordinate normal. The definition of \(h_S\) therefore gives the claimed bound for every containing closed halfspace at this single point. Finally \(0<\ell<1\) because \(e>2\), and \(e<3<16\sqrt{20}\) gives \(\ell/(16\sqrt{20})<1/e\).
