## Statement

Let \(q\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(1\le i\le q\), let \(m_i,h_i,L_i\) be positive integers with \(3h_i\le L_i<4h_i\), let \(\beta_i\ge0\) and \(\theta_i\in\mathbb R\) be finite, and let the original row \((\mathbf X_i,Y_i)\) have law
\[
 p_i(\mathbf x,y)
 =\frac{\exp(\beta_i\sum_{a=1}^{m_i}x_a+\theta_i y)}{\mathcal Z_i},
 \qquad
 E_{L_i,h_i,m_i}
 =\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m_i+1}:
                       \sum_{a=1}^{m_i}x_a+h_i y\le L_i\right\},
\]
where \(\mathcal Z_i\) sums the numerator over this entire original support. Assume that the rows are independent and that their full means are \((j_i\mathbf1_{m_i},2)\), with \(j_i\in\mathbb Z\). Then these mean points belong to their original supports. For every \(\mathbf a_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i}\) and \(c_i\in\mathbb R\), set
\[
 T=\sum_{i=1}^q
       \left[\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-j_i\mathbf1_{m_i})
                              +c_i(Y_i-2)\right].
\]
With the absolute constant
\[
 c_\star=\frac1{9\cdot2^{128}\cdot236844^4}>2^{-204},
 \qquad
 \min\{\Pr(T\ge0),\Pr(T\le0)\}\ge c_\star.
\]
Consequently every closed halfspace containing the full original product mean has original product probability at least \(c_\star\). All parameters may vary independently across rows. The statement includes \(m_i=1\), \(\beta_i=0\), \(L_i=3h_i\), zero directions, and zero noise variance.

## Proof

We first work with one row and suppress its index. Write \(X_\Sigma=\sum_{a=1}^mX_a\), and reflect only the height by \(Y'=3-Y\). This is a bijective relabeling of the original four heights, with \(\mathbb EY'=1\). Its slice budgets and probabilities are
\[
 B_y'=L-3h+hy\quad(0\le y\le3),
 \qquad
 p_y=\Pr(Y'=y)
 =\frac{e^{-\theta y}Z_m(B_y',\beta)}
        {\sum_{z=0}^3e^{-\theta z}Z_m(B_z',\beta)},
\]
where \(D_m(s)=\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}\) and \(Z_m(B,u)=\sum_{s=0}^BD_m(s)e^{us}\). These partition functions and composition counts have exactly the meanings used in accepted facts `43cb56218b18ec82` and `fc05e99e54e6cdad`. The reflected height-one kernel is the original height-two kernel; no transverse law is changed. In particular \(\mathbb E\mathbf X=j\mathbf1_m\) remains the original transverse mean.

Let \(A=B_{Y'}'-X_\Sigma=L-X_\Sigma-hY\) be the original slack, and put \(b=B_1'=L-2h\). Positivity of the original law at the origin implies \(\mathbb EA>0\). The exact full mean gives \(\mathbb EA=b-mj\in\mathbb Z\), so \(\mathbb EA\ge1\). Positivity at a point with \(x_1=1\) gives \(j>0\), hence \(j\ge1\). Thus
\[
 b\ge m+1,\qquad
 0\le B_y'\le L\le3b,\qquad
 mj+2h=L-\mathbb EA\le L-1.
\tag{1}
\]
The inequality \(L\le3b\) follows from \(L\ge3h\). The last inequality proves legality of the original mean.

The conditional total at budget \(B\) has positive weights \(D_m(s)e^{us}\), \(0\le s\le B\), whose successive ratios \(e^u(s+m)/(s+1)\) decrease. For every real \(u\), the budget partition sequence is log-concave. Indeed, writing \(t_s=D_m(s)e^{us}\), the ratio property gives \(t_{B+1}t_{s-1}\le t_Bt_s\) for \(1\le s\le B\). Summing proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,u)&\le t_B\sum_{s=1}^Bt_s<t_BZ_m(B,u),\\
 Z_m(B,u)^2-Z_m(B-1,u)Z_m(B+1,u)
     &=t_BZ_m(B,u)-t_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,u)>0
                      \quad(B\ge1).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Therefore the successive differences of \(\log Z_m(B,u)\) decrease. Summing over successive intervals of length \(h\) shows that \(y\mapsto\log Z_m(B_y',u)\) is concave, although the reflected budgets increase. Consequently \((p_y)_{y=0}^3\) is a positive log-concave height law with mean one.

We need its mass at height one. The mean identity is \(p_0=p_2+2p_3\), so \(p_0\ge p_2\). Log-concavity gives \(p_1^2\ge p_0p_2\ge p_2^2\), hence \(p_2\le p_1\). Put \(\rho=p_1/p_0\). If \(\rho\ge1\), then \(p_0\le p_1\). If \(\rho<1\), log-concavity gives \(p_{y+1}\le\rho p_y\); shifting the finite tail sums then gives \(\Pr(Y'\ge k)\le\rho^k\). Hence \(1=\mathbb EY'\le\rho/(1-\rho)\), so \(\rho\ge1/2\). In either case \(p_0\le2p_1\). Finally \(p_3\le1/3\) by the mean, and therefore
\[
 \frac23\le p_0+p_1+p_2\le4p_1,
 \qquad p_1\ge\frac16.
\tag{3}
\]

We next prove the original averaged-noise bound with the budget factor three. Set \(r=e^{-\beta}\in(0,1]\), and let \(A_B\) denote the slack in a transverse slice of budget \(B\). Its law is
\[
 \Pr(A_B=a)=\frac{r^aD_m(B-a)}{H_m(B,r)},
 \qquad H_m(B,r)=\sum_{a=0}^Br^aD_m(B-a)=r^BZ_m(B,\beta).
\tag{4}
\]
Its successive mass ratios are \(r(B-a)/(B-a+m-1)\), so its masses decrease and are log-concave. If \(r<1\), shifting the masses gives \(\Pr(A_B\ge k)\le r^k\), and finite support gives \(\mathbb EA_B<r/(1-r)\), including budget zero. Averaging over the original row and using \(\mathbb EA\ge1\) forces \(r>1/2\). Thus always \(r\ge1/2\).

Here are the elementary scalar slack estimates used in the proof of accepted fact `fc05e99e54e6cdad`, re-established independently of budget orientation. For any decreasing log-concave law on \(\{0,\ldots,B\}\) with masses \(u_a\), mean \(\mu\), and variance \(v\), put \(u_{B+1}=0\). The weights \((a+1)(u_a-u_{a+1})\) express the law as a mixture of uniforms on \(\{0,\ldots,a\}\). If \(M\) is its mixture index, the uniform first and second moments give
\[
 \mu=\frac{\mathbb EM}{2},\qquad
 v=\frac{\mathbb EM^2}{3}+\frac{\mathbb EM}{6}
                       -\frac{(\mathbb EM)^2}{4}
   \ge\frac{\mu^2+\mu}{3}.
\tag{5}
\]
The mixture weights are nonnegative and sum to one by telescoping. For the survival masses \(F(k)\), the sum \(F(k)/u_k=\sum_{a\ge k}u_a/u_k\) decreases with \(k\), by decreasing successive mass ratios. Hence \(F(k+1)/F(k)\) decreases, and \(F(k+\ell)\le F(k)F(\ell)\), with zero tails handled directly. Take \(t=\lceil2(\mu+1)\rceil\le3(\mu+1)\). Markov's inequality gives \(F(t)\le1/2\), so \(F(nt)\le2^{-n}\). Dividing the nonnegative support into intervals \([nt,(n+1)t)\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(A_B+1)^4
 \le t^4\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)^4\cdot2^{-n}
 =300t^4\le24300(\mu+1)^4.
\tag{6}
\]
The geometric-series identity in this display follows by four differentiations of \(\sum_{n\ge0}z^n=(1-z)^{-1}\).

Write \(\mu_b^A=\mathbb EA_b\) and \(v_1=\operatorname{Var}A_b=\operatorname{Var}(X_\Sigma\mid Y'=1)\). The first unnormalized slack ratio at budget \(b\) is \(rb/(b+m-1)\ge1/4\), by (1). Its zero atom is therefore at most \(4/5\), and \(\mu_b^A\ge1/5\). Equations (5) and (6) imply
\[
 v_1\ge\frac{2}{25}>\frac1{20},\qquad
 (\mu_b^A+1)^2\le6((\mu_b^A)^2+\mu_b^A)\le18v_1,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(A_b+1)^4\le24300\cdot324\cdot v_1^2.
\tag{7}
\]
This middle-slice variance bound is proved on the reflected increasing budgets; no decreasing-budget source theorem is applied to them.

Equation (2) implies that the positive successive ratios of \(H_m(B,r)\) decrease. The exact tail identity \(\Pr(A_B\ge k)=r^kH_m(B-k,r)/H_m(B,r)\), for \(k\le B\), then shows that each slack tail increases with \(B\). In particular \(\mathbb EA_{B_y'}^4\le\mathbb EA_L^4\). To compare the latter to budget \(b\), set
\[
 w_B(a)=r^a\frac{D_m(B-a)}{D_m(B)},\qquad
 W_B=\sum_{a=0}^Bw_B(a),\qquad
 \frac{D_m(B-a)}{D_m(B)}
   =\prod_{s=1}^{m-1}\left(1-\frac{a}{B+s}\right).
\tag{8}
\]
The empty product for \(m=1\) equals one. For each fixed \(a\), the product increases with \(B\), so \(W_L\ge W_b\). If \(3k\le L\), then \(k\le b\), and \(L+s\le3(b+s)\). Equation (8) and \(r^{3k}\le r^k\) give \(w_L(3k)\le w_b(k)\), hence \(\Pr(A_L=3k)\le\Pr(A_b=k)\). Decreasing slack masses give the same upper bound at \(3k+1\) and \(3k+2\) whenever these atoms are present. Grouping these three atoms, and extending absent endpoint atoms by zero, gives
\[
 \mathbb EA_L^4\le243\mathbb E(A_b+1)^4.
\tag{9}
\]
For a nonnegative variable, the fourth-power sum inequality and Jensen give \(\mathbb E(U-\mathbb EU)^4\le16\mathbb EU^4\). Let \(V_A=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(A\mid Y')\). Equations (3), (7), and (9) yield \(V_A\ge v_1/6\) and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(A-\mathbb E[A\mid Y'])^4
 &\le16\cdot243\cdot24300\cdot324\cdot v_1^2\\
 &\le1{,}101{,}996{,}057{,}600\cdot V_A^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

For \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^m\) with \(\sum_ab_a=0\), put \(Q=\sum_ab_a^2\) and \(T_C=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf X\). Given \((Y',X_\Sigma)=(y,s)\), \(\mathbf X\) is uniform on the complete compositions of \(s\) into \(m\) parts. The composition moment calculation proved in accepted fact `fc05e99e54e6cdad` applies to exactly this unchanged kernel: its total \(s\), dimension \(m\), and coefficients \(\mathbf b\) have the same meanings. It gives conditional mean zero, conditional second moment \(s(s+m)Q/[m(m+1)]\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[T_C^4\mid Y',X_\Sigma=s]
 \le Q^2\left(9(s/m)^4+18(s/m)^3+10(s/m)^2+s/m\right).
\tag{11}
\]
These are composition identities independent of the source height mean or budget orientation. Since \(s\le L\le3b\) and \(L/m\ge1\), (11) gives \(\mathbb ET_C^4\le38\cdot81(b/m)^4Q^2\). At zero transverse tilt, appending the slack produces a uniform complete composition of \(b\) into \(m+1\) parts, so its total mean is \(mb/(m+1)\). Differentiating the finite partition shows that the total mean increases with the transverse tilt, with derivative its variance. Therefore \(\beta\ge0\) and (3) give, for \(V_C=\mathbb ET_C^2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_C&=\frac{Q}{m(m+1)}\mathbb E[X_\Sigma(X_\Sigma+m)]\\
 &\ge\frac{Q}{6m(m+1)}
                (\mathbb E[X_\Sigma\mid Y'=1])^2
 \ge\frac{Qm b^2}{6(m+1)^3}
 \ge\frac{Q}{48}(b/m)^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(\mathbb ET_C^4\le7{,}091{,}712\cdot V_C^2\). For \(m=1\), \(\mathbf b=0\), and the same conclusion holds directly.

For an arbitrary row normal \(\mathbf a\), define \(\bar a=m^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^m a_a\), \(b_a=a_a-\bar a\), and the original noise \(N=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbb E[\mathbf X\mid Y'])\). Conditional permutation symmetry gives
\[
 N=\bar a\bigl(X_\Sigma-\mathbb E[X_\Sigma\mid Y']\bigr)+T_C,
 \qquad
 \sigma^2:=\mathbb EN^2=\bar a^2V_A+V_C.
\tag{13}
\]
The cross term vanishes by the conditional mean-zero composition identity. Equations (10)--(13) and \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\) prove
\[
 \mathbb EN^4
 \le8{,}815{,}968{,}460{,}800\,\sigma^4
 \le2^{44}\sigma^4,
 \qquad \sigma^2\ge\bar a^2v_1/6.
\tag{14}
\]
This argument used the original height probabilities throughout.

We now bound the nonlinear transverse drift. Write \(\mu_y=\mathbb E[X_\Sigma\mid Y'=y]\), \(s=\mu_1-\mu_0\), and
\[
 R_y=\mu_y+(y-1)\mu_0-y\mu_1,
 \qquad
 V_X=\mu_{Y'}-mj-s(Y'-1).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(y=2,3\), define \(D_y(u)=y\log Z_m(b,u)-(y-1)\log Z_m(B_0',u)-\log Z_m(B_y',u)\). Concavity established in (2) implies \(D_y(u)\ge0\) for every real \(u\). Differentiation of the finite partitions gives
\[
 D_y'(\beta)=-R_y,
 \qquad
 D_y''(u)=y v_b(u)-(y-1)v_{B_0'}(u)-v_{B_y'}(u)
          \le y v_b(u),
\tag{16}
\]
where \(v_B(u)\) is the total variance in budget \(B\) at tilt \(u\). We use only the scalar exponential-tilt estimate proved in accepted fact `43cb56218b18ec82`: a finite log-concave integer law with contiguous positive support and variance \(\sigma_0^2\ge1/20\) has tilted variance at most \(16384\sigma_0^2\) for \(|t|\sigma_0\le1/32\). This estimate is established there before the source application and has no height or budget-orientation hypothesis. It applies to the present budget-\(b\) total by (7), so \(v_b(\beta+t)\le16384v_1\) for \(|t|\le1/(32\sqrt{v_1})\).

Taylor's formula with integral remainder in (16), using \(t=-\operatorname{sgn}(D_y'(\beta))/(32\sqrt{v_1})\) when the derivative is nonzero, gives
\[
 0\le D_y(\beta+t)
    \le D_y(\beta)-\frac{|R_y|}{32\sqrt{v_1}}+8y,
 \qquad
 |R_y|\le32\sqrt{v_1}(D_y(\beta)+8y).
\tag{17}
\]
The last inequality also holds when the derivative vanishes. The exact height partition ratio and mean identity give
\[
 \frac{p_0^{y-1}p_y}{p_1^y}=e^{-D_y(\beta)},
 \qquad p_0\ge(y-1)p_y,
 \qquad p_y\le e^{-D_y(\beta)/y}.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(d\ge0\), \((d+8)^4e^{-d/2}\le8^4\), since its logarithmic derivative is \(4/(d+8)-1/2\le0\). Using (17), and applying (18) to one square-root factor of \(p_y\), gives
\[
 p_yR_y^4
 \le2^{32}y^4\sqrt{p_y}\,v_1^2.
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(R_0=R_1=0\) and \(2^4+3^4=97<2^7\), we obtain \(\mathbb ER_{Y'}^4\le2^{39}v_1^2\). The two original mean identities \(\mathbb E\mu_{Y'}=mj\) and \(\mathbb EY'=1\) imply \(V_X=R_{Y'}-\mathbb ER_{Y'}\). Centering by Jensen and the fourth-power sum inequality therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb EV_X=0,\qquad
 \mathbb EV_X^4\le2^{43}v_1^2.
\tag{20}
\]
At \(\beta=0\), the complete-composition symmetry additionally gives \(\mu_y=mB_y'/(m+1)\), so \(V_X=0\). The preceding proof already covers that value of the tilt.

Restore the row indices. Apply accepted fact `3a60a638a17036a8` to the reflected height marginals \(p_{i,y}=\Pr(Y_i'=y)\). Their supports are the entire \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), they are log-concave by (2), and their means are exactly one. Attach its labels \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}\) to the original reflected pairs, using its Bayes construction with the conditional kernel of \(\mathbf X_i\) given \(Y_i'=y\). Its scalar height \(Y_i\) is here \(Y_i'\), and its label \(\Lambda_i\) is here \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}\). Deleting the labels recovers exactly the original joint product after reversing the heights; every transverse kernel remains unchanged. In particular the labels need not preserve transverse means component by component.

Take the augmented latent element \(\mathcal Y_i=(Y_i',\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i})\), and define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_i&=(\bar a_i s_i-c_i)(Y_i'-1),\\
 V_i&=\bar a_i V_{X,i},\\
 N_i&=\mathbf a_i\cdot
              (\mathbf X_i-\mathbb E[\mathbf X_i\mid Y_i']),
 \qquad \sigma_i^2=\mathbb EN_i^2.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
The scalar variables \(W_i,V_i,N_i\) and label here have exactly the roles of the same variables in accepted fact `828c61552093316c`; its latent element called \(Y_i\) is our augmented \(\mathcal Y_i\). Its label sigma-algebra is contained in \(\sigma(\mathcal Y_i)\). Fact `3a60a638a17036a8` gives
\[
 \mathbb E[W_i\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[W_i^4\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]
 \le236844
       \bigl(\mathbb E[W_i^2\mid\Lambda_{\mathrm{lab},i}]\bigr)^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[N_i\mid\mathcal Y_i]=0.
\tag{22}
\]
All variables are bounded for each finite row, so integrability and finiteness of all required moments hold. By (14) and (20), \(\mathbb EV_i=0\) and
\[
 \mathbb EN_i^4\le2^{49}\sigma_i^4,
 \qquad
 \mathbb EV_i^4
 \le2^{43}\bar a_i^4v_{1,i}^2
 \le36\cdot2^{43}\sigma_i^4
 \le2^{49}\sigma_i^4.
\tag{23}
\]
These inequalities include \(\sigma_i=0\): (14) then forces \(\bar a_i=0\), and \(N_i=V_i=0\) almost surely. Independence of the original rows and the conditionally independent Bayes label draws gives independence of the full augmented experiments \((\mathcal Y_i,N_i)\).

Equations (21)--(23) satisfy every hypothesis of the independent-sum assertion of accepted fact `828c61552093316c`, with \(K=2^{49}\) and \(K_0=236844\). Moreover reflection and (15) give the exact original directional identity
\[
 W_i+V_i+N_i
   =\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-j_i\mathbf1_{m_i})
                               +c_i(Y_i-2).
\tag{24}
\]
Its sum bound is precisely \(1/(9\cdot2^{30}(2^{49})^2\cdot236844^4)=c_\star\). If \(\sum_i\sigma_i^2=0\), its stronger bound \(1/(4\cdot236844)\) applies as well. Since \(9<2^4\) and \(236844<2^{18}\), the denominator defining \(c_\star\) is less than \(2^{204}\), proving the strict displayed comparison.

Finally, a closed halfspace containing the full mean contains the parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through that mean. Its centered normal defines a scalar \(T\) of the displayed form, so the corresponding closed-sign probability is at least \(c_\star\). This conclusion uses the original product probabilities and the original full mean, by (24) and exact preservation of the kernels and joint law.
