## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite with weights $w_v\ge0$ of positive total $M$. At each step record the convex hull of the current positive support, its number $r_j$ of vertices and the smallest positive vertex weight $\delta_j$, then subtract $\delta_j$ at each of those vertices. Every lattice point in the last nonempty recorded hull supports a full-occurrence fractional convex-hull packing of value at least $\sum_j\delta_j$. Moreover $M=\sum_j r_j\delta_j$, so this certifies the bound $M/2^n$ whenever $\sum_j r_j\delta_j\le2^n\sum_j\delta_j$, in particular if all recorded hulls have at most $2^n$ vertices.

If a counterexample to $\max_{z\in P}f_w(z)\ge M/2^n$ exists among such weighted lattice-complete sets, any counterexample with a minimum number of positive sites has more than $2^n$ vertices in its positive-support hull. Consequently that hull has two distinct vertices of the same lattice parity.

## Proof

At each step at least one positive site loses its entire remaining capacity, so the iteration stops after finitely many steps. The positive-support hulls are nested. The last one has a lattice point, for example a vertex, and any of its lattice points belongs to $P$ by completeness. At such a mark every recorded vertex set is a qualifying group. Giving each its recorded mass $\delta_j$, and adding the masses if groups recur, respects every source capacity because those charges are exactly the amounts subtracted. The mass removed at step $j$ is $r_j\delta_j$, and the procedure exhausts all capacities. This proves the stated sufficient bound.

Now take a hypothetical counterexample with as few positive weights as possible. If its first hull has $r\le2^n$ vertices, perform one subtraction of $\delta$, the smallest vertex weight. If the residual mass $M'=M-r\delta$ is positive, minimality applied on the same lattice-complete set with the new weights gives a packing of at least $M'/2^n$ at some $z\in P$. Its positive value implies $z$ belongs to the residual positive-support hull, since every positive group uses only positive-capacity sites. If $M'=0$, choose any vertex of the original positive hull and the zero residual packing. In either case $z$ lies in the original positive hull, so its full vertex group can carry mass $\delta$ in addition to the residual packing using the exact removed capacities. The total is at least $\delta+(M-r\delta)/2^n\ge M/2^n$, a contradiction. Thus there are more than $2^n$ vertices, and the $2^n$ parity classes give a repeated parity.
