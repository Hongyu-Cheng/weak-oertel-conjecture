## Statement

Let $C_0$ be the lifted convex hull of the intervals $[0,1],[0,5],[0,1]$ at $-1,0,1$, and $L=[-1,1]$. For $t\ge0$ let $C_t=C_0+(\{0\}\times tL)$. For $-1/2\le t<0$ let $C_t=\{w:w+(\{0\}\times(-t)L)\subset C_0\}$. The maximum normalized mixed length depth is
$$
\begin{cases}
(3+2t)/(7+6t),&-1/2\le t\le1/2,\\
(5+6t)/[2(7+6t)],&t\ge1/2.
\end{cases}
$$
The unique deepest points on the respective branches are $(0,2-t)$ and $(0,3/2)$. In particular the actual optimized depth on $[0,3]$ is neither quasiconcave nor monotone in either direction, despite fixed sampled sites, fixed individual fiber centers and positive fiber lengths on this interval.

## Proof

The real fiber over $z\in[-1,1]$ is $[0,5-4|z|]$. The zero-base Minkowski addend changes each interval to $[-t,5-4|z|+t]$. For negative $t$ in the stated range the erosion gives the same formula, since containing the vertical segment is equivalent to both endpoint inequalities in that same real fiber. The projection remains $[-1,1]$ and there are exactly three nonempty sampled sites. Translating height by $t$ gives endpoint intervals $[0,a]$ of equal length $a=1+2t$ and middle interval $[0,b]$ with $b=5+2t$. For $t>-1/2$, subtract $a/2$ from the heights to apply the accepted three-interval formula with endpoint lengths $a,a$, middle width $b$ and shift $(b-a)/2$. Its compatibility condition is $b\ge a$, which holds here. Its thresholds are $0$ and $a$, so the nontrivial seam occurs at $(b-a)/2=a$, or $t=1/2$. Undoing the translations gives the claimed centers and masses divided by $b+2a=7+6t$. At $t=-1/2$ the extreme fibers are exposed zero-length points of depth zero and the middle interval has length $4$ and normalized maximum $1/2$ at its midpoint. The ambient body has positive area. The values at $0,1/2,3$ are $3/7,2/5,23/50$, with the middle strictly below both others.
