## Statement

Let $T\subset\mathbb Z^k$ be a finite nonempty set and let $K\subset\mathbb R^s$ be a convex body with $s\ge1$. Suppose the actual mixed source is $\operatorname{conv}(T)\times K$ with positive integer base set exactly $T$. There is an actual mixed mark with normalized closed-halfspace depth at least $\Gamma_s/h(T)$, where $\Gamma_s=(s/(s+1))^s$. For an integral Jensen filter with occupied slices $S_a$, this is at least $\Gamma_s/\sum_a h(S_a)$. For a complete original base in $\mathbb Z^m$ with $r$ binary height coordinates it is at least $\Gamma_s/2^{m+r}$.

## Proof

Apply 6d6fcf377e68c79c with the finite closed location set $T$, its relative Helly number, and the uniform atomic probability on $T$. It gives a base $t_*\in T$ with at least $1/h(T)$ of the atoms in every containing closed base halfspace. Put the centroid $g_K$ of $K$ over $t_*$. For a closed ambient halfspace through $(t_*,g_K)$, the set of bases of contained centroids $(t,g_K)$ is a closed halfspace through $t_*$ in the base coordinates, or all the base space. Each of those fibers contributes at least $\Gamma_s|K|$ by the zero-shift case of 6a7d56e450b3126f, or its full mass if the continuous restriction is constant. All original fibers have equal volume. Parallel shrinking to a through-mark cap thus gives the stated normalized depth for every containing cap.

For the filter, use the preceding height-slice bound. A complete $P\subset\mathbb Z^m$ has $h(P)\le h(\mathbb Z^m)=2^m$, by intersecting convex traces with $\operatorname{conv}P$ in the lattice Helly property. The lattice value is given in 6d6fcf377e68c79c. For the binary filter the preceding theorem gives $h(T)\le2^rh(P)$ and exact fine lattice sampling of its hull. This yields the final bound with the actual common-fiber centroid mark.
