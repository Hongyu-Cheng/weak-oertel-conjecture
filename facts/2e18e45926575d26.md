## Statement

Let $L\ge6$ be divisible by three, $h=1/L$ and $\varepsilon=h^2$. Place the triangles
$$
Q_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,0),(1,-\varepsilon),(1,\varepsilon)\},\qquad
Q_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(-h,-1),(h,1),(0,2h)\}
$$
at coarse levels $z=0,1$ in $(z,u,x)$ and let $C_h$ be their convex hull. Use the fine trace on $\mathbb Z\times h\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R$, weighted by $h$ per fine length, and the coarse law of area on the two triangles. Their masses are $h^2(4+h)$ and $4h^2$, respectively, with fine masses $h^2(2+h)$ and $2h^2$ by coarse level. The body equals the convex hull of its fine trace. The map $(z,u,x)\mapsto(4z-2,4u,4x)$ places it between $B_2^3$ and $6B_2^3$, and gives fine covering radius $2h$ and coarse mass $64h^2$. The resulting fine-radius-to-coarse-mass ratio $1/(32h)$ diverges and remains divergent under any further uniformly round affine normalization preserving the two-base/one-continuous block. For a further map with linear part $(b,x)\mapsto(Bb,c^\mathsf Tb+dx)$, this ratio is multiplied by $1/|d|$.

Using all ambient closed halfspaces, the maxima of normalized depth over the respective actual fine and coarse traces are $1/(4+h)$ and $2/9$. A deepest fine point is $(1,0,h)$. On the fixed cap $x+2(z-1)\ge0$, the fine and coarse normalized masses are $2/(4+h)$ and $(1+4h)/(4(1+2h))$, with respective limits $1/2$ and $1/4$ despite the total-mass ratio tending to one. Componentwise block normalizations of absolute determinant $1/(4h^2)$ can give fixed area-$1/2$ triangle images with fine spacings $h$ and $1/2$. For every block normalization of $Q_1$ whose image has inball radius at least $b>0$, its fine spacing is at least $b$ and its covering radius at least $b/2$.

## Proof

Because the coarse base interval has endpoints zero and one, the only coarse fibers are exactly the two placed triangles. All vertices are on the fine grid, so the hull of the fine trace is precisely $C_h$. The determinants of the triangles' two edge vectors have absolute value $4h^2$ each, giving area $2h^2$ each. At $z=0$ and $-1\le u\le1$, the fine section has endpoints $\pm\varepsilon(1+u)/2$. At $z=1$, write $t=x-u/h$. For $|u|\le h$ the section is $0\le t\le2h(1-|u|/h)$. Thus the only sampled fine levels on $Q_1$ are $-h,0,h$, with singleton endpoints and interval $[0,2h]$ at zero. Summing the affine lengths at $u=i/L$, $-L\le i\le L$, gives $h^2(2+h)$ on $Q_0$ and the second interval contributes $2h^2$.

The two triangles contain the segments $(z,u,x)=(0,t,0)$ and $(1,ht,t)$, $-1\le t\le1$. Their hull is the image of $\{0\le z\le1,\ |u|\le1-z,\ |x|\le z\}$ under $(z,u,x)\mapsto(z,u+hx,x)$. The four facet distances from $(1/2,0,0)$ to the original tetrahedron are $1/(2\sqrt2)$. The inverse shear has norm at most $1+h$, giving an inball of radius at least $1/[2\sqrt2(1+h)]\ge1/4$ at the same center in $C_h$. The box $[0,1]\times[-1,1]^2$ contains $C_h$ and lies in the radius-$3/2$ ball about this center. The claimed fixed scaling follows. Its coarse area multiplier is $16$ and its fine spacing is $4h$, so the radius-to-mass ratio is $1/(32h)$, compared with $1/(8h)$ in the original coordinates.

For the depth bound on $Q_0$, its barycentric coordinates are
$$
\lambda_A=(1-u)/2,\qquad
\lambda_B=(1+u)/4-x/(2\varepsilon),\qquad
\lambda_C=(1+u)/4+x/(2\varepsilon).
$$
The three caps $\lambda_i\ge1/3$ cover the triangle. The first cap retains the full section for $u\le1/3$. The second has section length $\varepsilon(u+1/3)$ for $u\ge-1/3$ and zero otherwise. The third is its reflection. Both grid endpoints $\pm1/3$ are sampled. Summing the arithmetic progressions gives the same weighted mass $\varepsilon(8/9+2h/3)\le\varepsilon$ in each cap. They are closed ambient caps excluding $Q_1$ after replacing their margins by $\lambda_i-1/3-(2/\varepsilon)z$. Indeed on $Q_1$, $|u|\le h$, $|x|\le1$ give $\lambda_A\le(1+h)/2$ and $\lambda_B,\lambda_C\le(1+h)/4+1/(2\varepsilon)$, strictly below $1/3+2/\varepsilon$.

On $Q_1$ the two margins $t-h$ and $h-t$ give a cap cover and each cap retains length $h$ from the sole positive fine interval. On $Q_0$, $|t|\le1/h+\varepsilon$. Subtracting $(2/h+2)(1-z)$ from each margin therefore strictly excludes $Q_0$. These five closed ambient caps cover all sampled points, including the zero-length fine fibers, and each has fine mass at most $\varepsilon$. Conversely every containing halfspace at $(1,0,h)$ retains at least half of the centered interval $[0,2h]$, including the whole interval if its $x$ coefficient vanishes. The point's raw depth is $\varepsilon$, giving the stated maximum. For the coarse law the centroid of either triangle has raw depth at least $(4/9)2h^2$ by 6769574fc1bd9f8d in dimension two at full mass, applied to each containing trace on its plane, with the whole plane allowed. Three barycentric caps on each triangle cover it, each of area fraction $(2/3)^2=4/9$ by homothety at a vertex. They extend to isolating ambient halfspaces by subtracting a sufficiently large multiple of the other coarse level's indicator. Thus the coarse maximum is exactly $2/9$.

The fixed cap excludes $Q_0$ and restricts to $x\ge0$ on $Q_1$. It contains all of the positive fine interval. Its complement on $Q_1$ cuts off the corner at $(-h,-1)$ with fractions $1/2$ and $1/(1+2h)$ along the two outgoing edges. The corresponding area fraction is their product. Normalizing by both equal coarse areas gives $(1+4h)/(4(1+2h))$ on the closed cap.

For further round normalizations, if $B_2^3\subseteq K\subseteq6B_2^3$ and $B_2^3\subseteq UK+t_0\subseteq RB_2^3$, the images of the inner balls and outer bounds, compared by widths, give $\lVert U\rVert\le R$ and $\lVert U^{-1}\rVert\le6$. In the indicated block form, a fine base generator in direction $e_u$ has its length multiplied by $\lVert Be_u\rVert$. The coarse fibers in the $(u,x)$ planes have area multiplier $\lVert Be_u\rVert|d|$, by wedging the image of $e_u$ with the vertical image $(0,d)$. The transformed fine covering radius is measured in its one-dimensional base line. Hence the ratio changes by $1/|d|$ exactly. The vertical vector and the two norm bounds give $1/6\le|d|\le R$.

The component block maps are $(u,x)\mapsto(u,x/(4h^2))$ and $(u,x)\mapsto(u/(2h),(x-u/h)/(2h))$. Both have determinant $1/(4h^2)$. Their image vertices are respectively $(-1,0),(1,-1/4),(1,1/4)$ and $(-1/2,0),(1/2,0),(0,1)$. Their fine spacings are $h$ and $1/2$. In any other block frame for $Q_1$ with fine scalar $A$, the projected width is $2|A|h$ and the spacing is $|A|h$. An inball of radius $b$ implies $2|A|h\ge2b$, proving the lower bounds.
