## Statement

Fix a real number \(B\ge0\). Let \(L\ge1\) be an integer. For every \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(n_\ell\ge1\) be an integer and let \(A_\ell\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty. Assume separately in every factor that every coordinatewise parity class has at most three points, that the midpoint of every distinct same-parity pair belongs to \(A_\ell\), and that distinct unordered pairs of distinct same-parity points have distinct midpoints.

For any one such support \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), let \(\mathcal J\) be the set of its distinct-pair midpoints and write \(\lbrace u_j,v_j\rbrace\) for the unique endpoint pair with midpoint \(j\in\mathcal J\). Form the directed midpoint graph with the two endpoint incidences \(j\to u_j\) and \(j\to v_j\). For every nonempty \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal J\), let \(L_A(\mathcal U)\) be the number of these incidences whose source lies in \(\mathcal U\) and whose target lies outside \(\mathcal U\). Define exactly the intrinsic coefficient of accepted fact e974512801a94ae5 by
$$
\mathfrak L(A)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\max_{\varnothing\ne\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal J}
\frac{2|\mathcal U|}{L_A(\mathcal U)},&\mathcal J\ne\varnothing,\\
0,&\mathcal J=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
$$
Here \(L_A(\mathcal U)\) is the quantity denoted \(L(\mathcal U)\) in that accepted fact; its denominators are positive by that fact. Suppose \(\mathfrak L(A_\ell)\le B\) for all factors. This graph coefficient is not the mixed lattice \(\Lambda\) of the locked problem.

Choose arbitrary positive weights \(w_{\ell,x}\) on every point of \(A_\ell\), subject only to midpoint log concavity. Retain the complete original laws, normalizers, product, and total ambient dimension:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_\ell&=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},&
p_{\ell,x}&=\frac{w_{\ell,x}}{Z_\ell},&
P&=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,&
N&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell,\\
p_{\ell,j}&\ge\sqrt{p_{\ell,u_j}p_{\ell,v_j}}
&&\left(j\in\mathcal J_\ell\right),\\
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}&=
\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^L w_{\ell,x_\ell}}
     {\prod_{\ell=1}^L Z_\ell}
&&\left((x_1,\ldots,x_L)\in\prod_{\ell=1}^L A_\ell\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Every law is zero outside its stated support. The midpoint inequality in (1) is equivalent to the same inequality for the weights.

Use the unchanged canonical normalizations of accepted facts ee640ebdfbcb632d and fe74c97317412b6c. For a finite law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\), they are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\left\lbrace p(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j
\right\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},&
b(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,&
\chi(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,&
M_3(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
The reflected sum includes both nonzero orientations and includes zero once. The halfspaces in (2) are always in the original ambient space, including when the support has lower affine dimension. In particular, for \(P\) this space is \(\mathbb R^N\); \(N\) is not the cardinality of an individual factor support.

With natural logarithms, define explicit constants depending only on \(B\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_B&=11+2^{18}B,&
G_B&=26C_B,&
D_B&=864G_B^2,\\
c_B&=\frac14\exp\left[
-101088-36D_B\bigl(2+12(\log2)^2\bigr)
\right]>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Then every product in (1) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_B4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_B^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{4}
$$
The constant is independent of all factor dimensions, supports, weights, normalizers, and the number of factors. Singleton supports and empty sets of nontrivial rows are allowed. No endpoint is required to have a singleton reflected row.

## Proof

The identity map on \(n,A,w,Z,p,\tau,H,b,\chi,M_2,M_3\) identifies our objects with those of ee640ebdfbcb632d and fe74c97317412b6c. We use the global dyadic selection mechanism of those accepted facts, but prove its estimates and actual-depth conversion below for the present support class. Neither predecessor's parity-two product conclusion is applied. The only energy charge used is the accepted three-parity graph charge of e974512801a94ae5, with its identical graph, selected-row energies, and intensities. All hatted objects below are auxiliary restricted reflected rows or auxiliary laws; the original probabilities in (1) never change. The atom sum \(A_0\) below is not the canonical ratio \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\).

Work first in one factor and suppress \(\ell\), writing \(n,A,p\) for its objects and putting \(K=2^n\). A distinct reflected pair about an integer point has same-parity endpoints. Midpoint closure and uniqueness therefore give the exact full supported rows
$$
R_x:=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace
=\begin{cases}
\lbrace u_x,x,v_x\rbrace,&x\in\mathcal J,\\
\lbrace x\rbrace,&x\in A\setminus\mathcal J.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
$$
Every atom is a center of at most one nontrivial row. If its parity class has \(m\le3\) members, it is an endpoint of exactly \(m-1\le2\) distinct same-parity pairs. Consequently each atom can belong, through its center and endpoint roles together, to at most three nontrivial rows. Those roles may overlap. Also \(|A|\le3K\).

Choose either ordering of the endpoints at each \(j\in\mathcal J\), and set
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_j&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{u_j}p_{v_j}}}{p_j}\in(0,1],&
z_j&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{v_j}}{p_{u_j}},\\
\rho_j&=\log(1/s_j)\ge0,&
\beta_j&=Ks_jp_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
In particular, \(H_p(j)=(1+2s_j)p_j\) on \(\mathcal J\), and \(H_p(x)=p_x\) otherwise.

Fix any selection \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal J\). Define \(\widehat R_x=R_x\) when \(x\in\mathcal S\), and \(\widehat R_x=\lbrace x\rbrace\) at every other supported center. Write \(h_x=1+2s_x\) for \(x\in\mathcal S\), and \(h_x=1\) otherwise. Define the auxiliary quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H(x)&=\sum_{y\in\widehat R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}=h_xp_x,&
\widehat b&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x\widehat H(x)^2,&
\widehat\chi&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x\widehat H(x),\\
\widehat\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{\widehat H(x)}
&&\left(y\in\widehat R_x\right),\\
\widehat Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},&
\widehat V_x&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_x}\widehat Y_x^2,\\
\widehat q_x&=\frac{p_x\widehat H(x)^2}{\widehat b},&
\widehat\nu&=\sum_{x\in A}\widehat q_x\widehat V_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Every probability on the right remains \(p_x=w_x/Z\) on the entire original support. Each row contains its center, so its denominator is positive, as is \(\widehat b\). Reflection preserves \(\widehat\kappa_x\) and reverses \(\widehat Y_x\), giving \(\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_x}\widehat Y_x=0\).

Put \(A_0=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3\) and \(A_{\mathcal S}=K^2\widehat b\). Since \(1\le h_x\le3\), and Jensen under the uniform law on \(A\) gives \(\sum_xp_x^3\ge|A|^{-2}\), we have
$$
\frac19\le A_0\le A_{\mathcal S}\le K^2b(p)\le9A_0.
\tag{8}
$$

For all \(K\) ambient parity classes \(\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), including empty classes, put \(r_\eta=p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=K\sum_\eta r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_\eta(Kr_\eta-1)^2\ge0,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x(K\widehat H(x)-1)^2,&
\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}&=4K\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}s_j(1-s_j)p_j^2,\\
\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}&=\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}+2\mathcal D+\mathcal T_{\mathcal S},&
\beta_{\mathcal S}&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}\beta_j,\\
\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}&=K\sum_{j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal S}s_j^2p_j^2
=K\sum_{j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal S}p_{u_j}p_{v_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The deviation energy \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\) is distinct from each score variance \(\widehat V_x\). Every unordered pair within a parity class contributes one cross term to that class's squared mass, and midpoint injectivity identifies those pairs with \(\mathcal J\). Hence, even for three-point parity classes,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2
&=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}s_j^2p_j^2,\\
\widehat\chi&=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}s_jp_j^2,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}&=A_{\mathcal S}-2K\widehat\chi+1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting these three identities into (9) gives
$$
A_{\mathcal S}=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}-4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
\tag{10}
$$
The selected \(\widehat H\), \(r_\eta\), \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\), \(\mathcal D\), \(\mathcal T_{\mathcal S}\), \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\), and \(\beta_{\mathcal S}\) here are exactly those of e974512801a94ae5, with \(K=2^n\) and \(\mathcal S\) unchanged. Its graph-charge inequality therefore applies to every selection and gives
$$
\beta_{\mathcal S}
\le\bigl(11+2^{18}\mathfrak L(A)\bigr)\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}
\le C_B\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}.
\tag{11}
$$

We next prove the score estimate with the present incidence and atom constants:
$$
\widehat\nu\le216\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}(1+\rho_j^2)\beta_j
+3888\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}.
\tag{12}
$$
At a selected center the endpoint masses are \(p_{u_j}=s_jp_je^{-z_j}\) and \(p_{v_j}=s_jp_je^{z_j}\). Thus
$$
\widehat V_j=\frac{2s_jz_j^2}{1+2s_j},\qquad
p_j\widehat H(j)^2\widehat V_j
=2s_j(1+2s_j)p_j^3z_j^2.
\tag{13}
$$
At all other centers the variance is zero. For one selected row abbreviate \(s=s_j\), \(z=z_j\), \(\rho=\rho_j\), and put \(\xi=Kp_j\), \(\upsilon=Kp_{u_j}\), \(\omega=Kp_{v_j}\), and \(G_j=2s(1+2s)\xi^3z^2\). Equation (13) gives the exact expression
$$
\widehat\nu=
\frac{K^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}G_j}{A_{\mathcal S}}.
\tag{14}
$$

If \(\max(\xi,\upsilon,\omega)\le2\), the larger endpoint equals \(s\xi e^{|z|}\le2\), so \(|z|\le\rho+\log(2/\xi)\). For \(t\ge0\), differentiation shows \(t^2e^{-t}\le4/e^2\). Substituting \(t=\log(2/\xi)\) yields \(\xi\log^2(2/\xi)\le8/e^2<2\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_j&\le12s\xi^3\bigl(\rho^2+\log^2(2/\xi)\bigr)\\
&\le24s\xi^2(1+\rho^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

For every \(s\in(0,1]\) and \(z\in\mathbb R\), with \(\rho=\log(1/s)\), one has
$$
z^2\le2\rho^2+4s^3\cosh(3z).
\tag{16}
$$
If \(|z|\le\rho\), this is immediate. Otherwise write \(|z|=\rho+t\), where \(t>0\). Then \(z^2\le2\rho^2+2t^2\), while \(s^3\cosh(3z)\ge e^{3t}/2\), and \(e^{3t}\ge t^2\) follows from the quadratic term of the exponential series. These inequalities prove (16). Also \(s\rho^2\le4/e^2<1\), by the same one-variable maximum, and \(\upsilon^3+\omega^3=2s^3\xi^3\cosh(3z)\). Thus if \(M=\max(\xi,\upsilon,\omega)>2\), (16) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_j&\le12s\rho^2\xi^3+12s(\upsilon^3+\omega^3)\\
&\le12(\xi^3+\upsilon^3+\omega^3)\le36M^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
Choose one atom \(x\) in that row with \(Kp_x=M\). Its contribution to \(K\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\) is
$$
M(h_xM-1)^2\ge M(M-1)^2\ge\frac{M^3}{4},
$$
since \(h_x\ge1\) and \(M>2\). Therefore (17) costs at most 144 times that atom's deviation contribution. Each atom can be chosen by at most three rows, once as a center and at most twice as an endpoint, including all overlaps. Summing (17) and dividing by \(K\) therefore costs at most \(432\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\). Summing (15) and dividing by \(K\) costs at most \(24\sum_{j\in\mathcal S}(1+\rho_j^2)\beta_j\). Finally divide by \(A_{\mathcal S}\ge1/9\) in (14). The resulting coefficients are \(9\cdot24=216\) and \(9\cdot432=3888\), proving (12).

Assume now \(A_0<1\). Cauchy--Schwarz under the original law \(p\) gives \(K\sum_xp_x^2\le\sqrt{A_0}<1\). The endpoint indegree bound two and \(2p_up_v\le p_u^2+p_v^2\) give the required omitted-pair estimate
$$
\begin{aligned}
\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}
&\le\frac K2\sum_{j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal S}
\bigl(p_{u_j}^2+p_{v_j}^2\bigr)\\
&\le K\sum_{x\in A}p_x^2<1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Equations (8) and (10) imply \(0\le\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}=A_{\mathcal S}-1+4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}<12\). The identity (10), \(A_{\mathcal S}\ge1/9\), and the elementary logarithmic inequalities \(\log(1+t)\ge t/(1+t)\) and \(\log(1+t)\le t\) for \(t\ge0\), now show
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log A_{\mathcal S}
&=\log(1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal S})
-\log\left(1+\frac{4\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}}{A_{\mathcal S}}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}}{13}-36\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}\\
&\ge\frac{\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}}{26}
+\frac{\beta_{\mathcal S}}{G_B}-36\zeta_{\mathcal S^c}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
For the last line, nonnegativity gives \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\ge\mathcal V_{\mathcal S}\), and (11) gives \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\ge\beta_{\mathcal S}/C_B\); half their sum is consequently a lower bound for \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}\). This explains both the unchanged positive denominator 26 and the new loss coefficient 36.

We select rows globally across the heterogeneous product. For every factor define \(K_\ell=2^{n_\ell}\) and \(A_{0,\ell}=K_\ell^2\sum_{x\in A_\ell}p_{\ell,x}^3\). If \(A_{0,\ell}\ge1\), retain only singleton auxiliary rows in that factor. Then \(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell=A_{0,\ell}\ge1\) and \(\widehat\nu_\ell=0\).

Group the rows in all remaining factors into global dyadic bands
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_k&=\left\lbrace(\ell,j):A_{0,\ell}<1,\ j\in\mathcal J_\ell,
\ 2^{-k-1}<s_{\ell,j}\le2^{-k}\right\rbrace,\\
\beta_k&=\sum_{(\ell,j)\in\mathcal B_k}
K_\ell s_{\ell,j}p_{\ell,j}^2,&
h_k&=1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2
\qquad(k\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Only finitely many bands contain rows. Select band \(k\) if and only if \(\beta_k\ge D_Bh_k\), and retain a full row precisely when its band is selected. This determines \(\mathcal S_\ell\subseteq\mathcal J_\ell\) in every remaining factor. Put \(U=\sum_{\ell:A_{0,\ell}<1}\mathcal V_{\mathcal S_\ell}\). In band \(k\) one has \(\rho_{\ell,j}<(k+1)\log2\) and \(s_{\ell,j}\le2^{-k}\). Summing (12) and (19), and including the nonnegative logarithms and zero variances of the other factors, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\ell=1}^L\log(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell)
&\ge\frac U{26}+\frac1{G_B}\sum_{k\text{ selected}}\beta_k
-36\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k,\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat\nu_\ell
&\le3888U+216\sum_{k\text{ selected}}h_k\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
For the loss term, the exact identity is \(\zeta_{\mathcal S_\ell^c}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell\setminus\mathcal S_\ell}s_{\ell,j}\beta_{\ell,j}\). Selection changes auxiliary rows only; all original atoms and every \(Z_\ell\) remain present.

We prove the conversion to actual ambient halfspace depth explicitly. Fix any supported target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat R_J&=\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat R_{\ell,j_\ell},&
\widehat\kappa_J&=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L\widehat\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\\
\widehat Y_J(X)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),&
\sigma_J^2&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every \(\widehat R_J\) is symmetric about \(J\). Product factorization and the definitions (7) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_P(J)&:=\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}
=\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat H_\ell(j_\ell),\\
P_X&=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{\widehat Y_J(X)}
\qquad\left(X\in\widehat R_J\right),\\
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J&=0,&
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J^2&=\sigma_J^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
The first reflected sum is restricted and need not equal canonical \(H_P(J)\). The likelihood identity uses exactly the original product masses \(P_X\) in (1); independence and the zero score means in (7) give the second-moment identity.

Every closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) contains at least one point of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\) in \(\widehat R_J\), and contains the central atom \(J\). Counting each noncentral orbit once and using half the available central mass, then using (22), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|\widehat Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The last step follows from Jensen, \(\mathbb E e^{-|Y|}\ge e^{-\mathbb E|Y|}\), and Cauchy--Schwarz, \(\mathbb E|Y|\le\sqrt{\mathbb EY^2}\). This holds for every original ambient containing halfspace and therefore bounds \(\tau_P(J)\), without any condition on affine dimension.

Let \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L\widehat q_\ell\), solely as an auxiliary center law. Square (23) and average over the entire original law \(P\). The exact weight identity
$$
P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2
=\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat b_\ell\right)
\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat q_{\ell,j_\ell}
$$
and Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat b_\ell\right)
\mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L\widehat b_\ell\right)
\exp\left[-2\sqrt{\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat\nu_\ell}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
Here \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell\), directly from (7) and (22). Thus no auxiliary law replaces the original averaging law defining \(M_2\).

Since \(\prod_\ell K_\ell^2=4^N\), combine (21) and (24) and use the subadditivity of the square root on nonnegative sums. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(4^NM_2(P))
&\ge-\log4+\frac U{26}-2\sqrt{3888U}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k\text{ selected}}
\left[\frac{\beta_k}{G_B}-2\sqrt{216h_k\beta_k}\right]
-36\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
Completing the square gives \(U/26-2\sqrt{3888U}\ge-26\cdot3888=-101088\). For a selected band, \(\beta_k\ge D_Bh_k=864G_B^2h_k\), which implies \(\beta_k/G_B\ge2\sqrt{216h_k\beta_k}\) by squaring nonnegative sides. Every selected bracket in (25) is therefore nonnegative. For an unselected band, \(\beta_k<D_Bh_k\). The geometric series and its first two derivatives give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^\infty2^{-k}&=2,&
\sum_{k=0}^\infty(k+1)^22^{-k}&=12,\\
\sum_{k=0}^\infty2^{-k}h_k&=2+12(\log2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
For example, the second identity follows by applying \(t\frac{d}{dt}+1\) twice to \((1-t)^{-1}\), obtaining \((1+t)/(1-t)^3\), and evaluating at \(t=1/2\). Thus the last term in (25) is bounded below by \(-36D_B\bigl(2+12(\log2)^2\bigr)\), uniformly over the number of factors and their rows. Equation (25) is at least \(\log c_B\) as defined in (3), proving the second-moment assertion in (4).

Finally Jensen under the entire original product law gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(P)&=\mathbb E_P\bigl[(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\bigr]\\
&\ge\bigl[\mathbb E_P\tau_P(J)^2\bigr]^{3/2}
=M_2(P)^{3/2}\ge c_B^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All constructions allow empty selections, empty sets of nontrivial rows, singleton factors, and zero score variances. No step divides by a score variance or assumes that an endpoint's own row is trivial. This proves the theorem under the stated fixed graph bound.
