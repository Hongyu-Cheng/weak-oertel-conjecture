## Statement

Let \(d,R\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) with \(R\ge2\), and put
\[
K_R:=[-R,R]\times[-1,1],
\qquad
z_0:=(0,0),
\qquad
F_R(x,y):=1-\frac{|x|}{R}.
\]
Then \(K_R\) is a compact full-dimensional convex set, and
\(F_R:K_R\to[0,\infty)\) is continuous and concave. Define
\[
D_R:=\{z\in K_R\cap\mathbb Z^2:F_R(z)>0\},
\qquad
M_{d,R}(z):=F_R(z)^d.
\]
Thus
\[
D_R=\{-R+1,-R+2,\ldots,R-2,R-1\}\times\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
Define the weighted planar halfspace depth
\[
\Delta_{d,R}
:=
\inf_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
z_0\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in D_R\cap Q}M_{d,R}(z).
\]

For \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), let \(v_0,\ldots,v_q\in D_R\) be affinely independent, put
\[
T:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\},
\]
and suppose \(z_0\in T\). If \(q=0\), put
\(\kappa(T,z_0):=1\). If \(q>0\), put
\[
\kappa(T,z_0)
:=
\min_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(T)\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
z_0\in Q}}
\#(T\cap Q\cap\mathbb Z^2).
\]
Let
\[
\mathscr S_{d,R}
:=
\max_T
\left[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M_{d,R}(v)\,
\kappa(T,z_0)
\right],
\]
where the maximum is over all such eligible simplices of ranks zero, one, and two. Then the following exact formulas hold:
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{d,R}
=
2+\frac3{R^d}\sum_{s=1}^{R-1}s^d
}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr S_{d,R}
=
\max\left\{
2,\
\max_{1\le r\le R-1}
(r+1)\left(\frac{R-r}{R}\right)^d
\right\}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(d\ge1\),
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\frac{\mathscr S_{d,R}}{\Delta_{d,R}}
=
\frac13\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{3}
\]
and therefore
\[
\lim_{d\to\infty}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\frac{\mathscr S_{d,R}}{\Delta_{d,R}}
=\frac1{3e}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, for every \(c>1/(3e)\), some pair of integers \(d\ge1\), \(R\ge2\) gives
\[
\mathscr S_{d,R}<c\,\Delta_{d,R}.
\tag{5}
\]

For the exact pair
\[
d=10,\qquad R=44,
\]
one has
\[
\mathscr S_{10,44}=2,
\qquad
\Delta_{10,44}
=
\frac{170\,755\,679\,495\,096\,437}
{13\,598\,680\,469\,209\,088},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\frac{\mathscr S_{10,44}}{\Delta_{10,44}}
=
\frac{27\,197\,360\,938\,418\,176}
{170\,755\,679\,495\,096\,437}
<\frac{11}{64}.
\tag{7}
\]
Every profile \(M_{d,R}\) in this family is the maximal absolute fiber-depth profile of an explicit compact full-dimensional convex body in
\(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\).

## Proof

The function \((x,y)\mapsto-|x|/R\) is concave. Hence \(F_R\) is continuous, concave, and nonnegative on \(K_R\). It vanishes exactly on the two sides \(x=\pm R\), so its positive lattice set is exactly the displayed set \(D_R\). Thus the two zero boundary columns are treated literally and are not inserted into \(D_R\).

We first prove the depth formula (1), including all properness and closed-boundary issues. Apart from \(z_0\), the finite set \(D_R\) is a disjoint union of antipodal pairs \(\{z,-z\}\), and
\[
M_{d,R}(z)=M_{d,R}(-z).
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(Q\) be any proper closed halfspace containing \(z_0\). Write
\[
Q=\{z:a^\top z\ge b\},
\qquad
a\ne0,\quad b\le0.
\]
The boundary-through-\(z_0\) halfspace
\[
Q_0:=\{z:a^\top z\ge0\}
\]
is contained in \(Q\). For each antipodal pair, \(Q_0\) contains at least one member, and it contains both members if they lie on its closed boundary. It also contains \(z_0\). Therefore, if
\[
W_{d,R}:=\sum_{z\in D_R}M_{d,R}(z),
\]
then
\[
\sum_{z\in D_R\cap Q}M_{d,R}(z)
\ge
1+\frac12\sum_{z\in D_R\setminus\{z_0\}}M_{d,R}(z)
=\frac{W_{d,R}+1}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Conversely, the proper closed halfspace
\[
Q_*:=\{(x,y):x+\sqrt2\,y\ge0\}
\]
has no nonzero lattice point on its boundary. It therefore contains exactly one member of every nonzero antipodal pair, together with \(z_0\), and equality holds in (9). There are three lattice points above each first coordinate, so
\[
W_{d,R}
=
3+\frac6{R^d}\sum_{s=1}^{R-1}s^d.
\tag{10}
\]
Taking \((W_{d,R}+1)/2\) proves (1). This also proves attainment of the infimum and keeps every positive-mass point on a closed boundary.

We next prove the exact optimized-score formula (2), exhausting all three ranks and all minimal-face positions of \(z_0\). Let \(T\) be eligible and set
\[
r:=\max_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}|v_1|.
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(0\le r\le R-1\), and monotonicity in \(|x|\) gives
\[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M_{d,R}(v)
=
\left(\frac{R-r}{R}\right)^d.
\tag{12}
\]

If \(r=0\), all vertices lie on the vertical line. Rank two is impossible. Rank zero has score \(1\). In rank one, \(T\) is contained in the three lattice points
\[
(0,-1),\quad(0,0),\quad(0,1).
\]
Every proper closed relative halfspace containing \(z_0\) contains at most two of these points after choosing the smaller boundary-through-\(z_0\) closed ray, so
\[
\kappa(T,z_0)\le2.
\tag{13}
\]
The vertical segment
\[
T_0:=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,-1),(0,1)\}
\]
has \(\kappa(T_0,z_0)=2\), because each of its two boundary-through-\(z_0\) closed rays contains exactly two of its three lattice points. Both endpoint weights are \(1\), so score \(2\) is attained.

Now suppose \(1\le r\le R-1\). Every vertex of \(T\) belongs to
\[
[-r,r]\times[-1,1]\cap\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Accepted fact `663b0775a8892a6a`, applied with its strip parameter equal to \(r\), proves
\[
\kappa(T,z_0)\le r+1
\tag{14}
\]
for every eligible rank-zero, rank-one, or rank-two simplex. That accepted proof uses proper closed relative halfspaces and reduces to the minimal face of \(T\) containing \(z_0\). Hence (14) includes the cases in which \(z_0\) lies on an edge or is a vertex, and it does not discard boundary lattice points. Equations (12) and (14) give
\[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M_{d,R}(v)\,
\kappa(T,z_0)
\le
(r+1)\left(\frac{R-r}{R}\right)^d.
\tag{15}
\]

This upper bound is attained for every \(1\le r\le R-1\). Indeed, take
\[
T_r:=\operatorname{conv}\{(-r,0),(r,0)\}.
\]
Its lattice points are
\[
(-r,0),(-r+1,0),\ldots,(r,0),
\]
and its two boundary-through-\(z_0\) closed rays each contain exactly
\(r+1\) points. Any proper closed relative halfspace containing \(z_0\) contains one of those two rays, so
\[
\kappa(T_r,z_0)=r+1.
\tag{16}
\]
Both endpoint weights equal \(((R-r)/R)^d\). Combining the attained score \(2\) at \(r=0\), the upper bound (15), and the attaining segments (16) proves (2) exactly.

We now derive the limits without exchanging unproved optimizations. Fix \(d\). Dividing (2) by \(R\) gives
\[
\frac{\mathscr S_{d,R}}R
=
\max\left\{
\frac2R,\
\max_{1\le r\le R-1}
\left(\frac rR+\frac1R\right)
\left(1-\frac rR\right)^d
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Let
\[
f_d(u):=u(1-u)^d\qquad(0\le u\le1).
\]
For every grid point \(u=r/R\), the second expression in (17) differs from \(f_d(u)\) by at most \(1/R\). Conversely, choosing
\[
r_R:=\left\lfloor\frac{R}{d+1}\right\rfloor
\]
for all sufficiently large \(R\) gives \(1\le r_R\le R-1\) and
\(r_R/R\to1/(d+1)\). Thus the discrete maxima in (17) converge to
\(\max_{0\le u\le1}f_d(u)\). Since
\[
f_d'(u)
=(1-u)^{d-1}\bigl(1-(d+1)u\bigr),
\]
the unique maximum occurs at \(u=1/(d+1)\), and
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr S_{d,R}}R
=
\frac1{d+1}\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{18}
\]

On the other hand, formula (1) gives
\[
\frac{\Delta_{d,R}}R
=
\frac2R
+\frac3R\sum_{s=1}^{R-1}\left(\frac sR\right)^d.
\tag{19}
\]
The sum in (19) is the left Riemann sum for
\(\int_0^1u^d\,du=1/(d+1)\). Therefore
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{d,R}}R
=\frac3{d+1}.
\tag{20}
\]
The limit in (20) is positive. Dividing (18) by (20) proves (3).
Finally,
\[
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
=
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}
\longrightarrow e^{-1},
\]
which proves (4).

To prove the coefficient consequence (5), fix \(c>1/(3e)\). By (4), choose a fixed integer \(d\) for which
\[
\frac13\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d<c.
\]
Then (3) supplies an integer \(R\) large enough that
\(\mathscr S_{d,R}/\Delta_{d,R}<c\). This proves that no coefficient strictly greater than \(1/(3e)\) can hold uniformly over the registered class.

It remains to verify the advertised fully exact disproof of \(11/64\). Set
\[
d=10,\qquad R=44.
\]
For real \(x\in[1,43]\), define
\[
g(x):=(x+1)(44-x)^{10}.
\]
Then
\[
g'(x)=(44-x)^9(34-11x).
\]
Hence the integer maximum of \(g(r)\), \(1\le r\le43\), occurs at
\(r=3\) or \(r=4\). Exact arithmetic gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\cdot44^{10}-4\cdot41^{10}
&=704\,084\,636\,226\,748>0,\\
2\cdot44^{10}-5\cdot40^{10}
&=1\,965\,921\,876\,836\,352>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Formula (2) and the attained vertical score therefore give
\[
\mathscr S_{10,44}=2.
\tag{22}
\]

The elementary identity
\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N}s^{10}
=
\frac{N^{11}}{11}
+\frac{N^{10}}2
+\frac{5N^9}{6}
-N^7+N^5-\frac{N^3}{2}+\frac{5N}{66}
\]
is checked by taking its difference at \(N\) and \(N-1\), which is
\(N^{10}\), and by checking its value \(0\) at \(N=0\). At \(N=43\),
\[
\sum_{s=1}^{43}s^{10}
=95\,705\,545\,704\,452\,174,
\qquad
44^{10}=27\,197\,360\,938\,418\,176.
\tag{23}
\]
Formula (1) now gives
\[
\Delta_{10,44}
=
\frac{341\,511\,358\,990\,192\,874}
{27\,197\,360\,938\,418\,176}
=
\frac{170\,755\,679\,495\,096\,437}
{13\,598\,680\,469\,209\,088}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus
\[
\frac{\mathscr S_{10,44}}{\Delta_{10,44}}
=
\frac{27\,197\,360\,938\,418\,176}
{170\,755\,679\,495\,096\,437}.
\]
The literal strict comparison is certified by
\[
11\cdot170\,755\,679\,495\,096\,437
-64\cdot27\,197\,360\,938\,418\,176
=137\,681\,374\,387\,297\,543>0.
\tag{25}
\]
This is exactly
\(\mathscr S_{10,44}/\Delta_{10,44}<11/64\), so the registered statement is false.

For completeness, every member of the family has a genuine convex-body realization. Define the centrally symmetric \(d\)-dimensional cube
\[
B_d
:=
\left[
-2^{-(d-1)/d},\,
2^{-(d-1)/d}
\right]^d.
\]
Its side length is \(2^{1/d}\), so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)=2.
\]
Let \(p_{B_d}\) be its Minkowski functional and define
\[
C_{d,R}
:=
\left\{
(x,y,u)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d:
|y|\le1,\quad
p_{B_d}(u)+\frac{|x|}{R}\le1
\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
This is a compact convex set because both defining functions are convex, and it has nonempty interior. Hence it is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its base projection is \(K_R\), and its fiber over \(z=(x,y)\in K_R\) is
\[
(C_{d,R})_z=F_R(z)B_d.
\tag{27}
\]

Every boundary-through-zero halfspace bisects \(B_d\), because \(B_d=-B_d\) and every hyperplane has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume. Moving the boundary of any containing halfspace toward zero shows that the absolute Tukey depth of \(0\) in \(B_d\) is \(1\). At an arbitrary point of \(B_d\), the two closed sides of any hyperplane through that point have total volume \(2\), so one side has volume at most \(1\). Thus no point has greater absolute depth, and the maximal absolute depth of \(B_d\) is exactly \(1\). Homothetic scaling in (27) multiplies all \(d\)-dimensional cap volumes by \(F_R(z)^d\). Therefore the maximal absolute fiber depth of \((C_{d,R})_z\) is exactly
\[
F_R(z)^d=M_{d,R}(z),
\]
including the zero fibers at \(x=\pm R\). This proves the claimed genuine full-dimensional realization.
