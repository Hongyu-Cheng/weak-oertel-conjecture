## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the exact canonical objects and normalizations of accepted facts 4b2c4fa5918f50d8 and 6b09aae189415c70. In particular, put

\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]

\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
\]

and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-dominant inequalities

\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\ge O.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(r=(r_e)\) be the half-strength canonical reservoir, let
\((v_e)\) be its load-minimal dual optimizer, and put

\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad \Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
 \qquad \beta_z:=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(\beta_z>0\), use the canonical source point
\(p_z^\flat\), common body \(D_*\), half-metronoid
\(\mathcal K_z=c_z+D_z\), and inclusion

\[
 p_z^\flat+\frac12D_z\subseteq\mathcal K_z,
 \qquad D_*\subseteq D_z.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained product matching. Let
\(\mathcal E_I\) be the canonical \(I\)-bad edge family, let
\(\mathcal B\) be the canonical exceptional family, and put

\[
 J:=\{e\in\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B:s_e>0\},
 \qquad S:=\sum_{e\in J}s_e.
\tag{4}
\]

Accepted fact 4b2c4fa5918f50d8 gives

\[
 S>\frac{6Q}{25}.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(z\) incident with \(J\), define

\[
 d_z^J:=\sum_{\substack{e\in J\\e\ni z}}s_e,
 \qquad E_J^{\mathrm{src}}:=\{z:d_z^J>0\},
 \qquad
 \rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}E_J^{\mathrm{src}},
\tag{6}
\]

\[
 r_J^{\mathrm{src}}:=
 \min_{b:\operatorname{aff}E_J^{\mathrm{src}}\to\mathbb R^d
 \ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{p_z^\flat-b(z):z\in E_J^{\mathrm{src}}\}.
\tag{7}
\]

For such an affine map \(b\), put

\[
 \operatorname{Tub}_J(b):=
 \sum_{z\in E_J^{\mathrm{src}}}d_z^J
 \mathbf 1_{\{\|p_z^\flat-b(z)\|_{D_*}\le1/2\}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tub}_J^*:=\sup_b\operatorname{Tub}_J(b).
\tag{8}
\]

Then the two distinct canonical capacities satisfy

\[
 \boxed{G\ge\operatorname{Tub}_J^*,}
\tag{9}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\ge
 2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{S}{r_J^{\mathrm{src}}+1}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently, the hard inequality forces the strict joint source screen

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Tub}_J^*
 +2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{S}{r_J^{\mathrm{src}}+1}
 <\frac Q{100},}
\tag{11}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 r_J^{\mathrm{src}}+1>
 48\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}}.}
\tag{12}
\]

If affine maps \(b_1,\ldots,b_K\) satisfy

\[
 z\in E_J^{\mathrm{src}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \min_{1\le j\le K}
 \|p_z^\flat-b_j(z)\|_{D_*}\le\frac12,
\tag{13}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{K\ge49.}
\tag{14}
\]

There is also a macroscopic actual-contact overlap. Define

\[
 \chi_e:=
 \begin{cases}
  \min\{\omega_e,s_e/16\},&e\in J,\\
  0,&e\notin J,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad X:=\sum_e\chi_e.
\tag{15}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{X>\frac Q{200}.}
\tag{16}
\]

For every edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(\chi_e>0\), let

\[
 \ell_e=\frac{x_{e,u}+x_{e,v}}2
\tag{17}
\]

be the actual support-contact midpoint furnished by accepted fact
6496cb3b0fd44f9d. Thus \(x_{e,u}\in\mathcal K_u\),
\(x_{e,v}\in\mathcal K_v\), and \(\ell_e\) supports
\(L_e=(\mathcal K_u+\mathcal K_v)/2\) in direction \(v_e\ne0\). Put

\[
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]

Every such loaded incidence and midpoint obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \|x_{e,u}-p_u^\flat\|_{D_u}\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 \|x_{e,v}-p_v^\flat\|_{D_v}\ge\frac12,}
\tag{19}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_e}\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 \|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_*}\ge\frac12.}
\tag{20}
\]

Finally, the positive contact measure

\[
 \sigma_\chi:=
 \frac12\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}
\tag{21}
\]

is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), is
halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\), and has mass

\[
 \boxed{|\sigma_\chi|=\frac X2>\frac Q{400}.}
\tag{22}
\]

No sum of the contact certificate (21) with the separate canonical
edge-point certificate is asserted.

## Proof

We first prove the source conclusions. Accepted fact
6b09aae189415c70 gives

\[
 0<d_z^J\le d_z(s)\le\beta_z
 \qquad(z\in E_J^{\mathrm{src}}).
\tag{23}
\]

Fix an affine map \(b\), and let \(E_b\) be the set of sources counted
in (8). If \(E_b=\varnothing\), then
\(\operatorname{Tub}_J(b)=0\le G\). Otherwise, origin symmetry of
\(D_*\), equations (3), (8), and (23) give

\[
 b(z)\in p_z^\flat+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z),
 \qquad
 b(z)\in\mathsf M_{d_z^J}(C_z)
 \quad(z\in E_b),
\tag{24}
\]

where the second membership is obtained by scaling one representing
density by \(d_z^J/\beta_z\le1\).

Apply the lattice centerpoint theorem in accepted fact
6d6fcf377e68c79c to the finite atomic base measure

\[
 \sum_{z\in E_b}d_z^J\delta_z.
\]

It supplies an integral base point \(m\) in
\(\operatorname{conv}E_b\) with the required base-halfspace depth.
For every convex representation of \(m\) by \(E_b\), affinity of \(b\)
and (24) place \(b(m)\) in the corresponding weighted Minkowski sum of
the selected metronoids. The exact complete-core definition in
accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12 therefore makes the masses
\((d_z^J)_{z\in E_b}\) admissible and gives

\[
 G\ge\sum_{z\in E_b}d_z^J
 =\operatorname{Tub}_J(b).
\tag{25}
\]

Taking the supremum over \(b\) proves (9). This uses one selected
density in each distinct source fiber and no target-fiber pooling.

For every \(z\in E_J^{\mathrm{src}}\), equation (3) gives
\(p_z^\flat\in\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z)\). The metronoid depth theorem
6769574fc1bd9f8d and (23) give the legal selector weight

\[
 \delta_C(z,p_z^\flat)\ge\gamma_d\beta_z
 \ge\gamma_dd_z^J.
\tag{26}
\]

The total of these weights is

\[
 \gamma_d\sum_zd_z^J=2\gamma_dS.
\tag{27}
\]

Apply the residual-selector definition and bound of accepted fact
be359f8a6c628483 with the ranks (6)-(7). Equations (26)-(27) prove
(10). The quantities \(G\) and \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) are distinct
summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\). Thus (1), (9), and (10) prove (11).
Dropping the first nonnegative term in (11) and using (5) gives

\[
 r_J^{\mathrm{src}}+1
 >200\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}}\frac SQ
 >48\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_J^{\mathrm{src}}},
\]

which is (12).

Suppose (13) holds. Every source degree \(d_z^J\) is counted by at
least one of the \(K\) tubes, so

\[
 2S=\sum_zd_z^J
 \le\sum_{j=1}^K\operatorname{Tub}_J(b_j)
 \le K\operatorname{Tub}_J^*
 \le KG
 <\frac{KQ}{100}.
\tag{28}
\]

Equation (5) gives \(2S>48Q/100\), hence \(K>48\). Since \(K\) is an
integer, (14) follows.

We next prove the contact overlap. Accepted fact
6b09aae189415c70 gives, coordinatewise,

\[
 \frac1{16}s\le r,
 \qquad 0\le\omega\le r,
 \qquad \Omega\ge Q-R,
 \qquad \sum_er_e=Q.
\tag{29}
\]

If \(0\le x\le r_e\), then

\[
 \min\{\omega_e,x\}\ge x-(r_e-\omega_e).
\tag{30}
\]

Apply (30) with \(x=s_e/16\) and sum over \(J\). Equations (1), (5),
and (29) give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X
 &\ge\frac S{16}-\sum_e(r_e-\omega_e)
 =\frac S{16}-(Q-\Omega)\\
 &\ge\frac S{16}-R
 >\frac{3Q}{200}-\frac Q{100}
 =\frac Q{200}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]

This proves (16). In particular, every \(\chi\)-positive edge has
\(\omega_e>0\), so its actual support contacts in (17) are defined.

Fix such an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\). At endpoint \(u\), the contact
\(x_{e,u}\) supports \(\mathcal K_u\) in direction \(v_e\ne0\). Indeed,
the support function of the Minkowski average and (17) give

\[
 h_{L_e}(v_e)
 =\frac12\bigl(h_{\mathcal K_u}(v_e)
                 +h_{\mathcal K_v}(v_e)\bigr)
 =\frac12\bigl(\langle v_e,x_{e,u}\rangle
                 +\langle v_e,x_{e,v}\rangle\bigr).
\]

Each contact pairing on the right is at most its corresponding support
value, so equality of the sums forces equality separately. Choose
\(h_u\in D_u\) with

\[
 \langle v_e,h_u\rangle=h_{D_u}(v_e).
\]

Equation (3) puts \(p_u^\flat+h_u/2\) in \(\mathcal K_u\). Support at
\(x_{e,u}\) therefore gives

\[
 \langle v_e,x_{e,u}-p_u^\flat\rangle
 \ge\frac12h_{D_u}(v_e).
\tag{32}
\]

Duality of the Minkowski norm and support function yields the first
inequality in (19). The proof at \(v\) is identical.

Averaging the two inclusions in (3) gives

\[
 F_e^\flat+\frac12D_e\subseteq L_e,
 \qquad D_e:=\frac12(D_u+D_v).
\tag{33}
\]

Choose \(h_e\in D_e\) supporting \(v_e\). Since \(\ell_e\) supports
\(L_e\) in the same direction, (33) gives

\[
 \langle v_e,\ell_e-F_e^\flat\rangle
 \ge\frac12h_{D_e}(v_e)
 =\frac12\omega_e.
\tag{34}
\]

Duality proves the \(D_e\)-norm inequality in (20). Moreover,
\(D_*\subseteq D_u\cap D_v\) implies

\[
 D_*\subseteq\frac12(D_u+D_v)=D_e.
\]

Hence \(\|y\|_{D_*}\ge\|y\|_{D_e}\) for every \(y\), proving the second
inequality in (20). The same inclusion also strengthens (19) to the
corresponding \(D_*\)-norm inequalities, although those are not needed
in the statement.

Finally, the reservoir \(r\) satisfies the strong endpoint bounds in
accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d. That fact proves that

\[
 \frac12\sum_e\omega_e\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}
\tag{35}
\]

is carried by the mixed-integer body and is halfspace-dominated by its
mixed-volume measure. Since \(0\le\chi_e\le\omega_e\), the measure
\(\sigma_\chi\) in (21) is a positive submeasure of (35), and is
therefore halfspace-dominated as well. Equations (16) and (21) give
(22). This contact certificate is used only once and is not added to a
second lower bound for the same numerical capacity.
