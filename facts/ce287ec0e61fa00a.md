## Statement

Let \(m_1,m_2,h,L\) be positive integers with \(L\ge h\), and let \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\theta\in\mathbb R\), with \(\beta_1\ne\beta_2\). Define the original support, normalization, and probability by
\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf u,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m_1}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m_2}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
          \textstyle\sum_i x_i+\sum_i u_i+hy\le L\},\\
\mathcal Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf u,y)\in D}
                 e^{\beta_1\sum_i x_i+\beta_2\sum_i u_i+\theta y},\\
p(\mathbf x,\mathbf u,y)&=\mathcal Z^{-1}
                 e^{\beta_1\sum_i x_i+\beta_2\sum_i u_i+\theta y}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Its actual closed-halfspace depth is \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H)\), where the infimum is over all closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{m_1+m_2+1}\), and its third moment is \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\).

For integers \(k\ge1\) and \(a\ge0\), put \(F_k(a)=\binom{a+k-1}{k-1}\). Let \(q(a,b,v)\) be the original probability that the two group totals and heavy coordinate are \(a,b,v\). Thus its support is the integer tuples \(a,b,v\ge0\), \(a+b+hv\le L\), and
\[
q(a,b,v)=\frac{F_{m_1}(a)F_{m_2}(b)e^{\beta_1a+\beta_2b+\theta v}}{\mathcal Z}.
\]
Let \(g>1\) be the absolute constant in accepted fact 609407d6db81b18b, and set \(g_0=\min\{2,g^{3/2}\}>1\), \(N=m_1+m_2+1\), and \(R=m_1+m_2-2=N-3\). Then the exact block reduction is
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{g_0^R}{64}\,8^{-R}
       \sum_{\substack{a,b,v\ge0\\a+b+hv\le L}}q(a,b,v)^4.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, for arbitrary stipulated scores,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8g_0^R}{(L+1)^9}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{2}
\]
If the three score separations satisfy
\[
\beta_1\ge\log2,\qquad
\beta_1-\beta_2\ge\log2,\qquad
h\beta_1-\theta\ge\log2,
\tag{3}
\]
then one absolute constant works for every \(m_1,m_2,h,L\) and every score vector in this chamber:
\[
M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-N},
\qquad
\kappa=2^{-25}g_0^{-2}\left(\frac{\log g_0}{3}\right)^3>0.
\tag{4}
\]
Every root probability and every depth in (1)--(4) belongs to the displayed original law.

## Proof

We first record the uniform-simplex rank surplus that will be used on the two composition factors. For integers \(T,r\ge0\), let \(u_{T,r}\) be uniform on \(\Delta_{T,r}=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r:\sum_i z_i\le T\}\). For \(r=0\), interpret this as the singleton law, whose depth and third moment are one. We claim
\[
M_3(u_{T,r})\ge\frac18g_0^r8^{-r}
\quad(T,r\ge0).
\tag{5}
\]
For \(r\ge1,T\ge2\), apply accepted fact 609407d6db81b18b with its rank equal to \(r\), its dilation symbol \(m\) equal to the present \(T\), and its tilt vector equal to zero. This is an exact identification of its uniform law with \(u_{T,r}\). A target \(\mathbf j\in\Delta_{T,r}\) is identified with the composition \(\mathbf j^+=(T-\sum_i j_i,j_1,\ldots,j_r)\). The predecessor's \(k_{\mathbf j^+}\) is precisely the number of points \(\mathbf z\in\Delta_{T,r}\) whose reflections \(2\mathbf j-\mathbf z\) also lie in this simplex. Write \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=k_{\mathbf j^+}u_{T,r}(\mathbf j)\). The predecessor's reflection-moment conclusion, rather than its capacity conclusion, gives
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,r}(\mathbf j)\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^{\,2}
\ge g^r4^{-r}.
\tag{6}
\]
Reflection partitions the counted endpoints into the singleton \(\mathbf j\) and antipodal pairs. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of every pair. Uniformity therefore gives \(\tau_{u_{T,r}}(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}/2\). Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(s\mapsto s^{3/2}\), applied to \(s=\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^{\,2}\), now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(u_{T,r})
&\ge\frac18\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,r}(\mathbf j)\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^{\,3}\\
&\ge\frac18\left(\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,r}(\mathbf j)
                                    \mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^{\,2}\right)^{3/2}
\ge\frac18g^{3r/2}8^{-r}
\ge\frac18g_0^r8^{-r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(T=1,r\ge1\), the support consists of \(r+1\) affinely independent vertices. Each has depth exactly \(1/(r+1)\), since an exposing halfspace isolates that vertex and every containing halfspace contains its atom. Thus \(M_3=(r+1)^{-3}\). The inequality \((r+1)^3\le2\cdot4^r\) holds at \(r=1\) and persists by induction, because \(((r+2)/(r+1))^3\le27/8<4\). Since \(g_0\le2\), it implies (5). For \(T=0\), and also for \(r=0\), the singleton law satisfies (5) directly. This proves (5) in all cases without a capacity-to-depth inference.

Fix a feasible total tuple \((a,b,v)\). Conditional on this tuple under the original law, the two compositions are independent and uniform, because the exponential weight is constant on the whole tuple. Denote this conditional law, embedded in the original ambient space, by \(U_{a,b,v}\). Its support is the affine image of \(\Delta_{a,m_1-1}\times\Delta_{b,m_2-1}\) under
\[
\Phi_{a,b,v}(\mathbf z,\mathbf w)
=\left(\mathbf z,a-\sum_i z_i,\mathbf w,b-\sum_i w_i,v\right).
\tag{7}
\]
The empty coordinate blocks when \(m_i=1\) have their singleton interpretation. The map (7) is injective. Restricting an ambient halfspace to its affine image gives an intrinsic halfspace, or the whole affine image; every intrinsic affine functional also extends to the ambient space. Hence the conditional law has exactly its intrinsic actual depth.

For any two independent probability laws on Euclidean spaces, actual depth at a product root is at least the product of the two factor depths. To check this here, translate any containing halfspace inward so that its boundary passes through the root. In intrinsic product coordinates its centered inequality is \(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot(\mathbf Z-\mathbf z)+\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot(\mathbf W-\mathbf w)\ge0\). The two events \(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot(\mathbf Z-\mathbf z)\ge0\) and \(\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot(\mathbf W-\mathbf w)\ge0\) together imply this inequality. Their probabilities are at least the respective factor depths and multiply by independence. A zero coefficient block gives an event of probability one; the same argument includes singleton factors. Integration and (5) give
\[
M_3(U_{a,b,v})\ge
M_3(u_{a,m_1-1})M_3(u_{b,m_2-1})
\ge\frac1{64}g_0^R8^{-R}.
\tag{8}
\]

The block coefficient is exact in the original normalization: at every point of this block, \(p=q(a,b,v)U_{a,b,v}\), and for every closed halfspace \(H\), \(p(H)\ge q(a,b,v)U_{a,b,v}(H)\). Therefore each root in the block has \(\tau_p\ge q(a,b,v)\tau_{U_{a,b,v}}\). The block's contribution to the original moment is consequently at least \(q(a,b,v)^4M_3(U_{a,b,v})\). The four factors consist of the one original root-probability factor and the three original depth factors. Summing these contributions over the disjoint total blocks and using (8) proves (1).

There are at most \((L+1)^3\) feasible total tuples. Jensen's inequality for the fourth power gives \(\sum q^4\ge(L+1)^{-9}\). Since \(8^{-R}=8^3 8^{-N}\), equation (1) then proves (2).

It remains to bound the total-law loss under (3). Let \(A=\sum_i X_i\), \(B=\sum_i U_i\), \(Y=y\), and introduce the original integral slack \(\Omega=L-A-B-hY\ge0\). The map from \((A,B,Y)\) to \((B,Y,\Omega)\) is a bijection on feasible total tuples, with \(A=L-B-hY-\Omega\). Define
\[
\rho_B=e^{\beta_2-\beta_1},\qquad
\rho_Y=e^{\theta-h\beta_1},\qquad
\rho_\Omega=e^{-\beta_1}.
\]
All three ratios lie in \((0,1/2]\) by (3). After the common factor \(e^{\beta_1L}\) cancels, the original total law in these affine coordinates has weights
\[
F_{m_1}(L-b-hv-c)F_{m_2}(b)
       \rho_B^b\rho_Y^v\rho_\Omega^c
\quad(b,v,c\ge0,\ b+hv+c\le L).
\tag{9}
\]

Let \(P\) be an auxiliary independent product law as follows. Its \(B\) variable is the sum of \(m_2\) independent geometric variables with masses \((1-\rho_B)\rho_B^j\), \(j\ge0\); its \(Y\) and \(\Omega\) variables are geometric with ratios \(\rho_Y\) and \(\rho_\Omega\). The \(B\) probability at \(b\) is \((1-\rho_B)^{m_2}F_{m_2}(b)\rho_B^b\), by counting the nonnegative compositions of \(b\). Consequently (9), including its original normalization, is exactly \(P\) multiplied and renormalized by
\[
w(b,v,c)=
\begin{cases}
F_{m_1}(L-b-hv-c),&b+hv+c\le L,\\
0,&b+hv+c>L.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]
This weighting function is nonnegative, bounded, and nonincreasing in each coordinate. Its expectation is positive, since it is positive at \((0,0,0)\).

For completeness, multiplying an independent product law by such a weighting function cannot increase any individual coordinate mean. For one coordinate \(V\), average \(w\) over the other independent coordinates to obtain a nonincreasing function \(W(V)\). If \(V'\) is an independent copy of \(V\), then
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}_P(V,W(V))
=\mathbb E_P[(V-V')(W(V)-W(V'))]\le0.
\]
The weighted mean is \(\mathbb E_P[VW(V)]/\mathbb E_P[W(V)]\), so this covariance inequality gives the assertion. The variables have finite means and \(W\) is bounded, justifying the calculation. Applying it to the three coordinates in (10) proves, for the original total law,
\[
\mathbb E_q B\le\frac{m_2\rho_B}{1-\rho_B}\le m_2,
\qquad
\mathbb E_q Y\le1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_q\Omega\le1.
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(E\) be the box of integer triples satisfying \(0\le B<4(m_2+1)\), \(0\le Y<8\), and \(0\le\Omega<8\). Markov's inequality, (11), and the union bound give
\[
q(E)\ge1-\frac{m_2}{4(m_2+1)}-\frac18-\frac18\ge\frac12.
\]
The box contains exactly \(256(m_2+1)\) integer triples; infeasible triples have original probability zero. Jensen's inequality applied to its probabilities therefore gives
\[
\sum_{a,b,v}q(a,b,v)^4
=\sum_{b,v,c}q(B=b,Y=v,\Omega=c)^4
\ge\frac{q(E)^4}{[256(m_2+1)]^3}
\ge\frac{2^{-28}}{(m_2+1)^3}.
\tag{12}
\]
The equality uses the bijection of original total tuples, without a change of probability law.

Combining (1) and (12), and again using \(N=R+3\), yields
\[
M_3(p)\ge2^{-25}\frac{g_0^R}{(m_2+1)^3}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(m_1\ge1\), one has \(R\ge m_2-1\). Write \(\alpha=\log g_0>0\). The elementary inequality \(e^s\ge s\), used with \(s=\alpha(m_2+1)/3\) and cubed, gives
\[
\frac{g_0^R}{(m_2+1)^3}
\ge\frac{g_0^{m_2-1}}{(m_2+1)^3}
=g_0^{-2}\frac{e^{\alpha(m_2+1)}}{(m_2+1)^3}
\ge g_0^{-2}\left(\frac\alpha3\right)^3.
\]
Substitution in (13) proves (4). All affine total and slack coordinates above are integral coordinates of the original roots. The argument uses the original source law in its last summation and never substitutes an auxiliary mean as a root.
