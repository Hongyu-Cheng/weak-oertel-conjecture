## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a $d$-simplex of volume $v$, and put $S=\{0,1\}^n\times K$ with summed fiber volume $\mu$ and mass $M=2^n v$. Write $H_j$ for the altitude from vertex $v_j$, $\lambda_j$ for its barycentric coordinate, $g$ for the centroid, $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, and $N_0=2^n(d+1)$. Consider finite full covers of $S$ by $N$ closed ambient caps with margins $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$, $\lVert u_i\rVert_2=1$, each of mass at most $\gamma_d v$. Each feasible cover contains, for every binary $p$ and each $j$, a distinct cap with own-fiber margin $H_j(\lambda_j(x)-1/(d+1))$ and zero cap volume on all other fibers. Thus $N\ge N_0$, and at $N=N_0$ these are exactly all the caps. For every $N\ge N_0$ the exact attained minimum is
$$
\min\sum_i\int_S(L_i)_+\,d\mu
=\frac{M d\gamma_d}{(d+1)^2}\sum_{j=0}^d H_j.
$$
Every feasible cover at $N=N_0$ has this value. For a regular simplex of inradius $r$ it is $M d r\gamma_d$.

## Proof

Set $c=1/(d+1)$. A cap containing $(p,g)$ has own-fiber mass at least $\gamma_d v$. Indeed, shift its cutting plane to pass through $g$ and apply baee8ddaa588aef0. The bound there gives $\gamma_d$ when there are $d$ negative vertex margins and a strictly larger fraction when there are at least two included vertices and $d\ge2$. For $d=1$ the centered cuts are half-intervals. If the margin was strictly positive at $g$, the shift removed an interior slab of positive volume. Thus equality requires the plane through $g$ and, by the equality case of that fact, one positive vertex margin and $d$ equal negative ones. The centered cap is therefore $\{\lambda_j\ge c\}$ for some $j$. Its Euclidean unit-normal margin is $H_j(\lambda_j-c)$, since $\lVert\nabla\lambda_j\rVert_2=1/H_j$. The individual budget forces zero mass on all other fibers. In particular, a cap cannot serve two centroids.

For each $p$, the finitely many caps strictly missing $(p,g)$ miss an open neighborhood of $g$ in that fiber. If one canonical type $j$ is absent, add $d\varepsilon$ to its barycentric coordinate at $g$ and subtract $\varepsilon$ from every other coordinate. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ the resulting point is in that neighborhood and misses all the caps containing $(p,g)$. This contradicts full coverage. The required distinct indices therefore number at least $N_0$, and exhaust the count at equality.

The volume of $\{\lambda_j\ge t\}$ is $v(1-t)^d$ for $0\le t\le1$ by homothety about $v_j$. The hinge cost of each forced cap is $H_jv\int_c^1(1-t)^d\,dt=vH_jd\gamma_d/(d+1)^2$. All other fibers give zero contribution for that cap, and all extra indices give nonnegative contributions. This proves the bound. To attain it, use the margins $H_j(\lambda_j-c-Aq_p(z))$ for all $(p,j)$, with $A>1$ and
$$
q_p(z)=\sum_{k=1}^n[(1-p_k)z_k+p_k(1-z_k)].
$$
This affine function vanishes at $p$ and is at least one at every other binary base. The caps therefore exclude other fibers, attain the individual budget, and cover their own fibers since $\max_j\lambda_j\ge c$. If $N>N_0$, append caps strictly disjoint from the compact source. In the regular case the centroid is the incenter and its distance to the opposite facet of $v_j$ is $H_j/(d+1)=r$.
