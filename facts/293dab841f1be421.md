## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). Use the notation \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(a_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(D_+(C)=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\), and \(W(C)=\sum_z a_z>0\) from accepted fact 3316a7b28eca6a19. Let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\) for \(z\in D_+(C)\). Write \(\mu_C\) and \(h_S\) for the mixed-volume measure and halfspace depth in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483, and use the absolute fiber depth \(\delta_C(z,y)\) of accepted fact 4e868f774de77608. Its continuous variable \(x\) is denoted by \(y\) here.

For every nonempty \(V\subseteq D_+(C)\), set \(Q_V=\operatorname{conv}V\), \(E_V=\operatorname{conv}\{(z,c_z):z\in V\}\), \(r_V=\dim\operatorname{aff}Q_V\), and \(q_V=\dim\operatorname{aff}E_V-r_V\). Define
\[
\mathscr L_C(V)
=\frac{\bigl(\min_{z\in V}\delta_C(z,c_z)\bigr)
\,|Q_V\cap\mathbb Z^n|}{2^{r_V}(q_V+1)},
\qquad
\mathscr B(C)=\max_{\varnothing\ne V\subseteq D_+(C)}\mathscr L_C(V).
\]
This certificate is valid: there is one \(p\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\ni p\) satisfies \(\mu_C(H)\ge\mathscr B(C)\).

The geometric estimate used below is the following. For every \(n\ge1\), \(R>0\), and nonempty finite \(V\subseteq RB_2^n\), writing \(m=|V|\) and \(\kappa_n=\operatorname{vol}_n(B_2^n)\), one has
\[
\operatorname{conv}V
\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in V}\left(\frac v2+\frac R2B_2^n\right),
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}V)
\le m\,2^{-n}\kappa_nR^n.
\]
If \(V\subseteq RB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and \(R\ge4n^2\), then
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{conv}V\cap\mathbb Z^n|}
{m\,|RB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n|}
\le\left(\frac{R+\sqrt n}{2R-\sqrt n}\right)^n
\le2^{1-n}.
\]
If \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}V<n\), one also has \(|\operatorname{conv}V\cap\mathbb Z^n|\le(2\lfloor R\rfloor+1)^r\), including \(r=0\).

For each integer \(n\ge2\), take exactly the family \(C_n\) constructed in accepted fact 3316a7b28eca6a19. Explicitly, keep
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_n&=2^{n^3},& B_n&=R_nB_2^n,& D_n&=B_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
N_n&=|D_n|,& d_n&=N_n,& \delta_n&=\frac1{4n},\\
b_n&=2R_n+1,&
\ell_n(x)&=\sum_{j=1}^n b_n^{j-1}x_j,&
H_n&=2R_n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_n^j,
\end{aligned}
\]
and set \(\varepsilon_n=\delta_n/(N_nH_n)\) and \(\psi_w(x)=1-\varepsilon_n|\ell_n(x)-\ell_n(w)|\) for \(w\in D_n\). The continuous coordinates are indexed by \(D_n\), and
\[
C_n=\left\{(x,y):x\in B_n,\quad
y_w\ge-\psi_w(x)\ (w\in D_n),\quad
\sum_{w\in D_n}y_w\le1\right\}.
\]
Write \(W_n=W(C_n)\) and \(S^{(n)}=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{N_n})\).

Define the common inner simplex
\[
K_n=\left\{y\in\mathbb R^{N_n}:
y_w\ge-\left(1-\frac{\delta_n}{N_n}\right)\ (w\in D_n),
\quad\sum_wy_w\le1\right\},
\]
and fix the explicit rational mixed-integer point
\(p_n=(0,c_{K_n})\), where
\(c_{K_n}=\delta_n\mathbf1/[N_n(N_n+1)]\) is its centroid. Then
\[
\mathscr B(C_n)\le2(n+1)4^{-n}W_n,
\qquad
h_{S^{(n)}}(p_n)\ge\frac7{16e}W_n.
\]
Every nonempty \(V\subseteq D_n\) has the exact rank identity \(q_V+1=|V|-r_V\). Each fiber centroid is its fiber's unique deepest point, so the same obstruction holds if the certificate is allowed to choose any deepest point in each selected fiber.

There is also an explicit full-dimensional rational polytope with the same assertions and exactly the same mixed support: put \(P_n=\operatorname{conv}D_n\) and \(\widehat C_n=C_n\cap(P_n\times\mathbb R^{N_n})\). In particular, no absolute \(c>0\) can guarantee \(\mathscr B(C)\ge c\,2^{-n}W(C)\) for every mixed-integer convex body, or even for every such rational polytope, because \(\mathscr B(C_n)/(2^{-n}W_n)\le2(n+1)2^{-n}\to0\).

## Proof

We first verify the certificate and its rank normalization. Fix nonempty \(V\subseteq D_+(C)\), and put \(t_V=\min_{z\in V}\delta_C(z,c_z)>0\). Positivity follows, for example, from the centroid depth inequality in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483: \(\delta_C(z,c_z)\ge\gamma_da_z\), where \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). Every point of \(E_V\) lies in \(C\) and has absolute fiber depth at least \(t_V\), by the joint concavity of \(\delta_C^{1/d}\) in accepted fact 4e868f774de77608.

For every \(z\in Q_V\cap\mathbb Z^n\), choose \(y_z\) with \((z,y_z)\in E_V\). This is possible because the base projection of \(E_V\) is \(Q_V\). At each \(z\in V\), choose \(y_z=c_z\). Define the finite atomic measure
\(\sigma=t_V\sum_{z\in Q_V\cap\mathbb Z^n}\delta_{(z,y_z)}\), where the last \(\delta\) denotes a Dirac measure. Its mass is \(|\sigma|=t_V|Q_V\cap\mathbb Z^n|\), and its support is carried by \(S\).

For any proper ambient closed halfspace \(H=\{(z,y):\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot y\ge u\}\), each atom \((z,y_z)\in H\) yields a fiber contribution at least \(t_V\). If \(\beta\ne0\), this follows from \(\delta_C(z,y_z)\ge t_V\); if \(\beta=0\), that fiber section of \(H\) is the whole continuous space, whose fiber mass is at least \(t_V\). Distinct atoms have distinct base coordinates, so summing gives \(\sigma(H)\le\mu_C(H)\). The whole-space case is immediate.

The selected support is contained in \(E_V\) and includes all its generating centroid points. Thus its convex hull is exactly \(E_V\), and its base affine hull is \(\operatorname{aff}Q_V\). In the notation of the dominated-measure residual theorem in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483, the exact map is therefore \(\rho_\sigma=r_V\) and \(r_\sigma=q_V\). The latter uses its identity \(r_\sigma=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\sigma)-\rho_\sigma\). That theorem gives a point \(p_V\in S\) with \(h_S(p_V)\ge\mathscr L_C(V)\), including both zero-rank cases. Since \(D_+(C)\) is finite, a maximizing subset exists, and its point proves the assertion for \(\mathscr B(C)\).

Next we prove the geometric estimates. Write \(V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_m\}\). For \(x=\sum_i\lambda_iv_i\in\operatorname{conv}V\), with \(\lambda_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\), the equality \(\|x\|_2^2=\sum_i\lambda_i\langle x,v_i\rangle\) gives an index \(i\) with \(\langle x,v_i\rangle\ge\|x\|_2^2\). Consequently
\[
\left\|x-\frac{v_i}{2}\right\|_2^2
=\|x\|_2^2-\langle x,v_i\rangle+\frac{\|v_i\|_2^2}{4}
\le\frac{R^2}{4}.
\]
This proves the covering, and subadditivity of volume gives the asserted volume bound, regardless of the affine rank of \(V\).

A Euclidean ball of radius \(R/2\), at any center, contains at most \(\kappa_n((R+\sqrt n)/2)^n\) lattice points: the unit cubes centered at those points have disjoint interiors and lie in the concentric ball with radius enlarged by \(\sqrt n/2\). The covering therefore gives
\[
|\operatorname{conv}V\cap\mathbb Z^n|
\le m\kappa_n\left(\frac{R+\sqrt n}{2}\right)^n.
\]
Conversely, rounding each coordinate of a point of \((R-\sqrt n/2)B_2^n\) to a nearest integer produces a point of \(RB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The unit cubes centered at these latter points cover the smaller ball, so
\(|RB_2^n\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge\kappa_n(R-\sqrt n/2)^n\). Dividing proves the first ratio bound. If \(R\ge4n^2\), then \(R\ge2n\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
n\log\left(\frac{2R+2\sqrt n}{2R-\sqrt n}\right)
&\le n\log\left(1+\frac{3n}{2R-n}\right)\\
&\le\frac{3n^2}{2R-n}
\le\frac{2n^2}{R}
\le\frac12<\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying the desired ratio by \(2^n\) now proves its bound by \(2^{1-n}\).

For the proper-rank estimate, take a basis matrix for the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}V\), of rank \(r\). Some \(r\) rows form a nonsingular minor. Projection onto those \(r\) coordinates is injective on the direction space and hence on the affine space. Its image of the lattice points of \(\operatorname{conv}V\) has integer coordinates between \(-\lfloor R\rfloor\) and \(\lfloor R\rfloor\), proving the count. When \(r=0\), the affine space is a singleton and the bound is one.

We now use the exact accepted family. All parameters, fibers, and centroid vectors of \(C_n\) are those in accepted fact 3316a7b28eca6a19; no radius or perturbation parameter is changed. For the following calculations abbreviate \(R=R_n\), \(N=N_n\), and \(\delta=\delta_n\). That fact proves that \(C_n\) is a full-dimensional convex body, that \(D_+(C_n)=D_n\), and that
\[
(C_n)_x=-\boldsymbol\psi(x)+S_n(x)\Delta_N,
\qquad
N+1-\delta\le S_n(x)\le N+1
\quad(x\in B_n),
\]
where \(\Delta_N=\{u\in\mathbb R^N:u_w\ge0,\ \sum_wu_w\le1\}\). It also proves that the \(N\) vectors \(c_z\), \(z\in D_n\), are linearly independent.

Put \(M_0=(N+1)^N/N!\). Since \(0<\delta=1/(4n)\le1/8\), Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
\left(1-\frac{\delta}{N+1}\right)^N
\ge1-\frac{N\delta}{N+1}
>1-\delta\ge\frac78.
\]
The exact simplex volumes therefore satisfy \(\frac78M_0<a_z\le M_0\) for every \(z\in D_n\), and \(\frac78M_0N<W_n\le M_0N\).

We record the simplex depth calculation, including the equality case from the vertex-cap argument in accepted fact 01bace1a2c4ef916. For any full-dimensional \(N\)-simplex of volume \(a\), let \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_N\) be its affine barycentric coordinate functions. If \(y\) is a point of the simplex and \(\lambda_i(y)\) is its largest barycentric coordinate, the containing halfspace \(\{v:\lambda_i(v)\ge\lambda_i(y)\}\) cuts a vertex cap of volume \((1-\lambda_i(y))^Na\). At the centroid this volume is \(\gamma_Na\), where \(\gamma_N=(N/(N+1))^N\). The centroid halfspace inequality in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 gives the matching lower bound. At any other point, the largest barycentric coordinate is strictly larger than \(1/(N+1)\), so the displayed cap is strictly smaller. Hence the centroid is the unique deepest point, of depth exactly \(\gamma_Na\). In particular, \(\delta_{C_n}(z,c_z)=\gamma_Na_z\). Also \(e^{-1}<\gamma_N\le1/2\): the lower bound follows from \(\log(1+1/N)<1/N\), and the upper bound from \((1+1/N)^N\ge2\).

Fix an arbitrary nonempty \(V\subseteq D_n\), and write \(m=|V|\), \(r=r_V\), and \(Q=Q_V\). An affine dependence among the \(m\) points \((z,c_z)\) gives a linear dependence among the corresponding \(c_z\), which are independent. Thus these points are affinely independent, so \(\dim\operatorname{aff}E_V=m-1\), \(q_V=m-1-r\), and \(q_V+1=m-r\ge1\). The certificate consequently satisfies
\[
\frac{\mathscr L_{C_n}(V)}{2^{-n}W_n}
\le\frac{8\gamma_N}{7}
\frac{2^{n-r}|Q\cap\mathbb Z^n|}{(m-r)N}.
\]

For \(r=n\), the radius \(R_n=2^{n^3}\) is at least \(4n^2\). The geometric ratio bound and \(m\ge n+1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathscr L_{C_n}(V)}{2^{-n}W_n}
&\le\frac{8\gamma_N}{7}\frac{m}{m-n}\,2^{1-n}\\
&\le\frac87(n+1)2^{-n},
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(m/(m-n)=1+n/(m-n)\le n+1\) and \(\gamma_N\le1/2\).

For \(r<n\), the injective coordinate projection bound and \(m-r\ge1\) give
\[
\frac{\mathscr L_{C_n}(V)}{2^{-n}W_n}
\le\frac47\frac{2^n(R+1)^{n-1}}{N}.
\]
Here \((2R+1)^r/2^r=(R+1/2)^r\le(R+1)^{n-1}\). No divisibility of \(R\) by \(n\) is needed. Indeed, the lattice cube \([-\lfloor R/n\rfloor,\lfloor R/n\rfloor]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n\) lies in \(D_n\), and \(2\lfloor R/n\rfloor+1\ge R/n\) because \(R/n\ge1\). Thus \(N\ge(R/n)^n\). Also \((1+1/R)^{n-1}\le\exp((n-1)/R)<2\), since \(R\ge4n^2\). It follows that
\[
\frac{\mathscr L_{C_n}(V)}{2^{-n}W_n}
\le\frac87\frac{2^n n^n}{R}.
\]
For every integer \(n\ge2\), the elementary inequality \(n\le2^{n-1}\) and the identity \(n^3-n^2-n-2=(n-2)(n^2+n+1)\ge0\) show
\[
4\cdot4^n n^n\le2^{n^2+n+2}\le2^{n^3}=R_n.
\]
This also justifies \(R_n\ge4n^2\) used above. Hence, uniformly over all proper ranks including zero,
\(\mathscr L_{C_n}(V)/(2^{-n}W_n)\le(2/7)2^{-n}\). Combining the two rank cases and maximizing over every nonempty subset proves \(\mathscr B(C_n)\le2(n+1)4^{-n}W_n\).

It remains to prove the lower bound at the point fixed in the statement. Accepted fact 3316a7b28eca6a19 gives \(\psi_w(x)\ge1-\delta/N\) throughout \(B_n\). Consequently \(B_n\times K_n\subseteq C_n\), and
\[
K_n=-\left(1-\frac\delta N\right)\mathbf1+(N+1-\delta)\Delta_N,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_N(K_n)=\frac{(N+1-\delta)^N}{N!}>\frac78M_0.
\]
Its centroid is exactly \(c_{K_n}=\delta\mathbf1/[N(N+1)]\), so \(p_n=(0,c_{K_n})\in S^{(n)}\). This point is chosen independently of the halfspace.

Consider any proper closed halfspace through \(p_n\), written as
\(H=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot(y-c_{K_n})\ge0\}\), with \((\alpha,\beta)\ne0\). Since \(D_n=-D_n\), at least \(N/2\) of its lattice bases satisfy \(\alpha\cdot z\ge0\). For each such base, the section \(H_z\) contains \(c_{K_n}\). If \(\beta\ne0\), the centroid halfspace inequality gives \(\operatorname{vol}_N(K_n\cap H_z)\ge\gamma_N\operatorname{vol}_N(K_n)\); if \(\beta=0\), the section is the whole space and the same bound holds. Summing only these fiber contributions yields
\[
\mu_{C_n}(H)
\ge\frac N2\gamma_N\operatorname{vol}_N(K_n)
>\frac{7\gamma_N}{16}NM_0
\ge\frac{7\gamma_N}{16}W_n
>\frac7{16e}W_n.
\]
Every proper closed halfspace containing \(p_n\) contains its parallel sub-halfspace whose boundary passes through \(p_n\). The same lower bound therefore holds for every containing halfspace; the whole-space case is immediate. Taking the infimum proves \(h_{S^{(n)}}(p_n)\ge7W_n/(16e)\).

Finally, \(D_n\subseteq P_n\subseteq B_n\) implies \(P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_n\), so restricting the base to \(P_n\) changes none of the lattice fibers. Thus \(\widehat C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{N_n})=S^{(n)}\), and all masses, fiber centroids, certificates, and mixed depths coincide. The polytope \(P_n\) has integer vertices, and each absolute-value constraint is equivalent to two affine inequalities with rational coefficients. Hence \(\widehat C_n\) is a rational polytope. It is bounded as a subset of \(C_n\); it has nonempty interior because \(P_n\) contains the full-dimensional crosspolytope with vertices \(\pm R_ne_i\), while \((0,0)\) satisfies all continuous-fiber inequalities strictly. The common inner simplex and the point \(p_n\) remain present after restriction. This proves the rational-polytope assertion and completes the counterexample to the centroid-certificate strengthening.
