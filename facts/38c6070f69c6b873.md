## Statement

Let $E\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $d\ge1$, and let $r:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)$ be finite and convex. Then $E[-r]=\{(z,x):x+r(z)B_d\subset E_z\}$ is compact and convex. If $E$ is described by finitely many inequalities $a_f\cdot z+u_f\cdot x\le b_f$, the erosion replaces each by $a_f\cdot z+u_f\cdot x+\|u_f\|_2 r(z)\le b_f$. In particular $r(z)=s\exp(v\cdot(z-j))$, $s>0$, gives convex erosion, with polytopal integer fibers when $E$ is a polytope. The convex hull of the positive integer fibers of $E[-r]$ preserves its entire $d$-dimensional mixed measure.

## Proof

If two points belong to the erosion, convexity of $E$ puts the ball at the interpolated point with radius the convex combination of the two radii inside the interpolated fiber. Convexity of $r$ makes the required radius no larger. Finiteness and convexity of $r$ on $\mathbb R^n$ give continuity, so the erosion is closed by passing to the limit for each point of $B_d$. It is a subset of $E$ and hence compact. Maximizing each defining linear functional over the ball adds $\|u_f\|_2r(z)$, including zero when $u_f=0$. At a fixed base the inequalities remain affine in the fiber coordinates. There are only finitely many integer bases, and the hull of the positive integer fibers lies inside the eroded set by convexity and contains every positive fiber. Thus the other integer fibers have zero $d$-volume both before and after taking the hull, which proves preservation of the mixed measure.
