## Statement

Take $C=[0,1]^2\times[0,1]$ with probability $\mu$ given by length on its four integer fibers divided by $4$. The maximum normalized ambient halfspace depth on the sampled fibers is $1/8$. For $y_t=(0,t,1/2)$ with $0\le t\le1$, its full ambient depth is $1/[8\max(t,1-t)]$.

At $\tau=1/5$, let $D_\tau$ be the ambient region of depth at least $\tau$ and let $P_\tau$ be its base projection. Then $P_\tau$ has full dimension, contains no integer point and lies in the maximal lattice-free split $[0,1]\times\mathbb R$. Its supporting line $z_1=0$ exposes an actual facet containing the segment $3/8\le z_2\le5/8$. No nonzero finite nonnegative combination of complementary inequalities of closed ambient caps of mass at most $\tau$ gives $-z_1\le0$. Every finite intersection of such complementary closed halfspaces contains an ambient neighborhood of $y_{1/2}$, whereas $D_\tau$ does not.

## Proof

At $L=0$ in a4bfc45c35ac42b8 the hull is precisely this box, with four unit intervals at the four binary bases and probability normalization by $4$. Its depth calculation gives the sampled maximum $1/8$, attained at every fiber midpoint.

For $y_t$, it suffices to consider halfspaces through the point: shifting a containing halfspace inward until its boundary reaches the point can only decrease its mass. Restrict to $z_1=0$. A nonzero continuous coefficient can be made positive by reflecting $x$ in $1/2$, which preserves the measure and the mark. After rescaling, the inequality there is $x\ge1/2-r(z_2-t)$. Its two retained lengths sum to
$$
\operatorname{clip}(1/2-rt,0,1)+\operatorname{clip}(1/2+r(1-t),0,1).
$$
When the continuous coefficient is zero the restricted inequality retains at least one full edge fiber (possibly both). Reflection of $z_2$ reduces the clipped calculation to $0\le t\le1/2$. For $r\ge0$, the second term reaches $1$ before the first reaches $0$. Prior to that saturation the sum is $1+r(1-2t)\ge1$, and afterwards it is at least $1$. For $r=-s\le0$, the second term reaches zero at $s=1/[2(1-t)]$ before the first reaches $1$. The sum is $1-s(1-2t)$ up to this first saturation and is nondecreasing thereafter. Its minimum is $1/[2(1-t)]$, also valid when $t=0$ or $t=1/2$. Any such restricted cap extends to the full ambient space by subtracting a sufficiently large positive multiple of $z_1$ from its margin to exclude both other fibers. Dividing its minimum retained length by $4$ proves the edge-depth formula.

The region of depth at least $\tau$ is closed and convex. Indeed a point of depth below $\tau$ is in a cap of mass below $\tau$, and slightly decreasing that cap's threshold puts the point in the open cap while retaining mass below $\tau$ by continuity from above. The region is therefore an intersection of complementary closed halfspaces. Strict separation also gives depth zero off the convex hull of the mixed support. Thus $D_\tau$ is compact and lies in the box. Every integer base in its projection would be a sampled base with its lift in the corresponding unit interval, contradicting the sampled maximum. The edge-depth formula puts the entire stated segment in $P_\tau$, including $y_{1/2}$ as a lift of its midpoint with depth $1/4$. The four symmetric edge-midpoint projections belong to $P_\tau$, so it has full dimension. Its face at $z_1=0$ is therefore a facet. The split contains the projected square and has lattice-free interior. It is maximal because a proper convex enlargement containing a point strictly to either side would make an integer point on the corresponding boundary line interior, by convexity with an open ball on the other side of that line inside the split.

Since the depth of $y_{1/2}$ is $1/4>\tau$, every closed cap of mass at most $\tau$ excludes $y_{1/2}$ strictly. Every corresponding complementary closed inequality and every nonzero finite positive-weight sum is therefore strict there, whereas $-z_1\le0$ is tight. A finite intersection likewise contains a neighborhood of $y_{1/2}$, which cannot lie in $D_\tau\subseteq\{z_1\ge0\}$. For an explicit limiting sequence take the caps $x-Lz_1\ge2/3$ with $L\ge1$. They have mass $1/6<\tau$ and their complementary inequalities divided by $L$ converge to $-z_1\le0$. The limiting closed cap $-z_1\ge0$ has mass $1/2$.
