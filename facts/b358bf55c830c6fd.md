## Statement

There is a full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2$ with exactly four integer fibers, the disks $c_j+r_jB_2$ for $j=0,1,2,3$, where $B_2$ is the unit disk,
$$
(r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3)=(1,2,2,1),\qquad
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=(0,(1/10,0),(0,1/10),0).
$$
No common mark $t\in\mathbb R^2$ makes $j\mapsto c_j+r_jt$ preserve all anchored convex representations. Nevertheless each actual point $(1,0,0)$ and $(2,0,0)$ has normalized mixed depth at least $461/2000=2/9+149/18000$ for the original summed area.

## Proof

Interpolate the radii and centers piecewise affinely on $[0,3]$. At each interior integer the radius surplus over the adjacent midpoint is $1/2$, and the center displacement from that midpoint has Euclidean norm $\sqrt5/20$. Thus at every continuous normal $u$ the support $u\cdot c(z)+r(z)\|u\|$ has discrete midpoint surplus at least $(1/2-\sqrt5/20)\|u\|\ge0$. Its piecewise-affine interpolant is concave. For every real-base convex combination this implies the support inequality for inclusion of the Minkowski average of the input disks in the disk at the combined base. Hence their union over $[0,3]$ is convex. It is compact by continuity of centers and radii, and full-dimensional because radii are positive and the base contains an interval. Its only integer bases are $0,1,2,3$.

The midpoint marking identity at $1$ forces $t=(-1/5,1/10)$ because the center surplus there is $(1/10,-1/20)$ and the radius surplus is $1/2$. The identity at $2$ forces $t=(1/10,-1/5)$ by the other surplus. These marks differ.

The origin-centered inner disks of radii $(1,19/10,19/10,1)$ are contained in their respective fibers by the triangle inequality. Their squared radii sum to $461/50$, while the original squared radii sum to $10$. Each of $1,2$ is a median of the inner squared radii on the base interval: the two inner weights are equal, as are the two outer weights, so each inclusive side of either site has at least half the total weight. The base sites where an ambient closed cap contains the origin of its disk form a closed one-dimensional halfspace or the whole line. For a cap through or containing one of the specified marks this set thus carries at least half the inner weight. Each counted disk contributes at least half its area by symmetry, with a constant restriction retaining the entire disk. Consequently every such cap captures at least $\frac14(461/50)\pi$ from original mass $10\pi$. This gives the asserted depth, with the stated excess by subtraction.
