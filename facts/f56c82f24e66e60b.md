## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body, $y$ an interior point, and $n_1,n_2,n_3$ fixed spanning normals with $\sum_j n_j=0$. For their three caps $n_j\cdot(x-y)\ge0$ let $q_j$ be the areas, let $k=|K|$, and let $\ell_j$ be the length of the central chord $n_j\cdot(x-y)=0$ divided by $\lVert n_j\rVert$. Write $M=\sum_j q_j/\ell_j$, $f_j=2\sqrt{kq_j}/(\ell_j M)$ and $\Phi_i=f_i+(f_j+f_l)^2/4$ for $\{i,j,l\}=\{1,2,3\}$.

For each selected index $i$, there is a triangle or quadrilateral containing $y$ in its interior, with the same areas in all six sectors of the three fixed lines, whose $\Phi_i$ is at least that of $K$. The polygon can realize an attained global maximum among bodies with these fixed sector areas, preserving all six ray endpoints of the maximizing datum. Different selected rows may use different polygons.

## Proof

Translate $y$ to $0$ and use the linear map $T$ with rows $n_1,n_2$. These rows are independent because the normals span and $n_3=-n_1-n_2$. The cap inequalities become $x_1\ge0$, $x_2\ge0$, $x_1+x_2\le0$. The sectors and caps correspond bijectively and areas scale by $|\det T|$. The coarea lengths scale by the same factor. For each $j$, take a unit tangent $v$ to the original cutting line and let $u=n_j/\lVert n_j\rVert$. The image normal is $n_j'=T^{-\top}n_j$. The vector $Tv$ is tangent to the image line, and the normal component of $Tu$ has magnitude $\lVert n_j\rVert/\lVert n_j'\rVert$ since $n_j'\cdot Tu=n_j\cdot u$. Thus $|\det T|=|\det(Tv,Tu)|=\lVert Tv\rVert\lVert n_j\rVert/\lVert n_j'\rVert$. Multiplying the ordinary chord length by $\lVert Tv\rVert$ and dividing by $\lVert n_j'\rVert$ gives the claimed scaling. Therefore $M$ and all $f_j$ are invariant. In the canonical coordinates the coarea length on the diagonal $x_1+x_2=0$ is its chord length divided by $\sqrt2$, giving exactly the radius sum on the rays generated by $(-1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$.

The six sector areas in these coordinates are strictly positive since the origin is interior and the rays are distinct. They are jointly feasible, witnessed by the image of $K$. Apply the fixed-sector singular-block theorem to the selected row at those six fixed areas. It yields an attained maximizing radius vector and at least two core sectors and two singular blocks for its feasible matrix $L$. The exact sparse sector reconstruction applies because the six endpoints are nonzero with consecutive gaps less than $\pi$, their data are feasible, and at least one core is present. It gives a polygon with $\operatorname{rank}L\le6-2=4$ vertices. It is full dimensional with interior origin, so it has at least three vertices. The reconstruction retains all six maximizing endpoints and all six sector areas. These determine the row, so the polygon has the maximum value. Mapping back preserves the row and restores the six original sector areas.
