## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,-4),(4,0),(0,4)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2$ and let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a nondegenerate triangle with barycentric functions $\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$. For $\ell(z)=(-z_1-z_2,2z_1-z_2,2z_2-z_1)$, define $H_j=\{(z,x):\lambda_j(x)-1/3+\ell_j(z)\ge0\}$ and $W=(1/3)\sum_{j=0}^2\mathbf1_{H_j}$. Let $\eta$ be the sum of arclength measures on $\{z\}\times\partial K$ for $z\in P$, marked by the outer unit normal $n$ of the continuous edge away from its endpoints. Then
$$
\sup_{\Phi}
\frac{\int \Phi(n,z,x)W(z,x)\,d\eta}{\int \Phi(n,z,x)\,d\eta}
=\frac{40}{93}.
$$
The supremum ranges over finite nonnegative potentials convex in the physical variables $(z,x)$ for every fixed $n$, with finite positive denominator. A constant potential attains it. The efficiency falls short of $4/9$ by $4/279$.

## Proof

It suffices to bound the numerator by $40/93$ times the denominator on each continuous edge. Its normal is fixed. A permutation of the three continuous vertices, together with the corresponding permutation of the three base facet forms, maps any continuous edge to any other and preserves the profile under relabeling. The base map permutes $e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2$ and is an integer linear bijection because it and its inverse send the integer basis vertices to integer vectors. The continuous map is affine and changes arclength on the edge by a constant factor. Precomposing a potential with these affine maps preserves physical convexity at the fixed normals. Thus it suffices to use the edge $\lambda_0=0$, parametrized by $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(t,1-t)$ for $t\in[0,1]$.

For $t=(r+s)/3$ with $0<r<1$ and $s\in\{0,1,2\}$, the integer $3W$ equals
$$
w(z,s)=|\{j:\ell_j(z)>0\}|
+\mathbf1_{\{\ell_1(z)=0,\ s\ge1\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{\ell_2(z)=0,\ s\le1\}}.
$$
Indeed positive and negative offsets give full and empty traces, and zero offsets require the respective barycentric coordinate at least $1/3$. Here $\lambda_0=0$. The excluded endpoints of the thirds have zero arclength. Enumerate $x,y=-4,\ldots,4$ in order, retain pairs with $\min(-x-y,2x-y,2y-x)\ge-4$, and for each enumerate $s=0,1,2$ in order. This gives 31 actual bases, with $8,11,8$ triples of value two for $s=0,1,2$ respectively, and $23,20,23$ of value one. The totals are 27 and 66. Let $H,L$ be those two ordered lists of triples.

For fixed $r$, mapping a triple $(z,s)$ to the physical point $(z,x((r+s)/3))$ on the chosen edge is affine. Thus $\pi_{hl}/22$ is a probability distribution on each row whose physical barycenter is the corresponding physical source. Jensen's inequality at this fixed normal, followed by the column-sum identity, gives
$$
22\sum_{h\in H}\Phi\bigl(n,z_h,x((r+s_h)/3)\bigr)
\le 9\sum_{l\in L}\Phi\bigl(n,z_l,x((r+s_l)/3)\bigr).
$$
On the high and low triples, $W-40/93$ is $22/93$ and $-9/93$ respectively. Integrate in $r$, using the common arclength factor of the edge times $dt=dr/3$. The inequality becomes $\int\Phi(W-40/93)\,d\eta\le0$ on this edge. Each row kept the same normal throughout, and the argument applies separately to all three normals. It therefore bounds the entire stated cone. For a constant potential the profile average on each edge is $(2\cdot27+66)/(9\cdot31)=40/93$, giving equality and the gap $4/9-40/93=4/279$.
