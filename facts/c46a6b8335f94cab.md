## Statement

Let $f_0,\ldots,f_{N-1}$ be a nonnegative, nonzero concave sequence with $N\ge1$ and $m=\max_i f_i$. Fix a real $d\ge1$, put $w_i=f_i^d$ and $W=\sum_i w_i$, and assume that $j=\sum_i iw_i/W$ is an integer. Then
$$
f_j\ge\frac{d+1}{d+2}m+\frac{m^{d+1}}{(d+2)NW},
\qquad
w_j>\left(\frac{d+1}{d+2}\right)^d\max_i w_i.
$$
The last coefficient is the infimum of $w_j/\max_i w_i$ for each fixed $d$ among these sequences with integer mean.

For positive integer $d$, let $C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of positive mixed $d$-volume. If its true mixed-volume centroid has integer base $j$, these bounds apply with $w_i$ the positive integer-fiber volumes, $f_i=w_i^{1/d}$, $W$ the total mixed volume, and $N$ the number of positive-volume integer fibers. The same homogeneous coefficient is sharp for actual convex bodies.

## Proof

The piecewise affine interpolant of the sequence is concave because its successive slopes are nonincreasing. Its domain contains the mean since the weights $w_i/W$ are nonnegative and sum to one. Jensen's inequality at the integer mean gives $f_j\ge\sum_i f_i^{d+1}/W$. The singleton case is immediate and fits the same inequality. Apply the finite power-moment theorem for nonnegative concave sequences with $p=d$, then divide by $(d+2)W$. This gives the finite correction. It is positive, so raising the resulting strict inequality $f_j>((d+1)/(d+2))m$ to $d$ gives the bound for $w_j$.

For sharpness, use $f_i(L)=L-i$ on the consecutive indices $0\le i<L$ for real $L>1$. These entries are positive and affine. The exact integer-centering construction in the proof of accepted fact 4d24a4fbd205befd uses precisely the weights $(L-i)_+^d$ on nonnegative integers, with any extra zero entries omitted. It shows that for each positive integer $k$ there is $L_k>k$ for which the weighted mean is exactly $k$. It also shows $L_k\to\infty$ and $k/L_k\to1/(d+2)$, using continuity and strict increase of the weighted mean in $L$ and its Riemann-sum limit after division by $L$. Thus at these exact means the weight ratio equals $(1-k/L_k)^d$, with limit $((d+1)/(d+2))^d$.

For the geometric consequence, positive integer-fiber volumes have consecutive support and concave $d$th roots, as established by the convexity and Brunn–Minkowski argument in the geometric proof of 4d24a4fbd205befd. The volume-weighted mean of their bases is the base coordinate of the true mixed centroid. Translate the index interval to start at zero if needed and apply the scalar result. The cones from the same accepted proof, given by $0\le z\le L_k$ and $\lVert x\rVert_2\le L_k-z$, have volumes proportional to $(L_k-i)_+^d$ at integer bases $i$. Their centering and weight ratios are unchanged by that constant. A possible zero-volume apex contributes nothing, so these bodies attain the sharp limit.
