## Statement

Use the canonical four-fiber family and outward covariance-width model in the triangle-source theorem. For the center $c=(-2\sigma,-2\sigma)$ of $R$ and small $h>0$, the unique maximizing mark in fiber $1$ is $(1,c)$, of depth $Q_R(h,L)=q_R(h,L)+L^2$, where $q_R(h,L)$ is its own smoothed square depth at $c$ with the same norm. Every active cap there captures $E_L$ fully and misses $T,D$, with zero outside transition coarea. At zero width,
$$
8\sigma-8\sigma^2\le Q_T(0,L)-|R|/2\le8\sigma-(7/2)\sigma^2.
$$
For every sufficiently small positive $h$ there exists $L_h\in[2\sqrt\sigma,4\sqrt\sigma]$ such that $Q_T(h,L_h)=Q_R(h,L_h)$. Then the two exhibited marks are the only global deepest marks and occupy both parities. Sending each outside base to its midpoint with the deepest base of the same parity assigns $-1$ to $0$ and $2$ to $1$.

## Proof

Fix the data of the triangle-source theorem. A cap through $(1,c)$ has margin $a(z-1)+u\cdot(X-c)$. If $E_L$ is not fully captured, then for some $X_E\in E_L$ one has $a+u\cdot(X_E-c)<0$. Therefore at base $0$ the margin is greater than $u\cdot(X+X_E-2c)$. The point $2c-X_E$ has both coordinates in $[4\sigma,4\sigma+L]\subseteq[0,0.041]$, so the square of side $1/2$ centered there is contained in $T$. Half of this square has positive margin, giving capture at least $1/8>L^2$. Consequently outside mass is always at least $L^2$ at this center, and equality requires full $E_L$ and zero $T,D$. A sufficiently large positive $a$ realizes equality for each continuous direction, at every mark of $R$, because the outside bases are fixed at distinct signed distances from base $1$. Pure-base caps at $1$ include $R$ and at least the endpoint on their selected side, so have mass strictly above $Q_R$ for small $h$. Saturation gives zero outside coarea as in the triangle-source theorem.

Here is a direct proof that $c$ uniquely maximizes own smoothed square depth. Normalize continuous normals to the ellipsoid $u^\top\Sigma u=1$ so the kernel width is $h$. At $c$ choose a minimizing normal $u$. By symmetry $-u$ is also minimizing. If $w$ is a nonzero displacement with $u\cdot w\ne0$, one of the two cuts strictly loses own mass at $c+w$ by positive strip volume near the center and monotonicity. If $u\cdot w=0$, the cap with direction $u$ has the same mass at both marks. If the displaced mark could attain at least the own depth at $c$, then $u$ would minimize in the ellipsoid at both marks. For each tangent vector $v$ at $u$, differentiation of the ramp integral yields the own normal derivative
$$
\frac1h\int_{\{-h<u\cdot(X-x)<0\}\cap R}v\cdot(X-x)\,dX
$$
at a mark $x$. This uses bounded convergence of difference quotients since the two kink lines have zero area. The strips coincide for $x=c,c+w$ and have positive area. Subtracting their two vanishing derivatives shows $v\cdot w=0$ for every tangent $v$, so $w$ is parallel to $\Sigma u$. Since $u\cdot w=0$ and $u^\top\Sigma u=1$, this implies $w=0$. This proves uniqueness. The large-tilt construction bounds actual depth at all square marks by their own depth plus $L^2$. The outside lower bound gives equality at $c$.

At $h=0$ central symmetry gives $q_R(0,L)=|R|/2=(2-4\sigma)^2/2$. The triangle-source bounds give the displayed gap. It is strictly between $4\sigma$ and $16\sigma$ for sufficiently small positive $\sigma$. The signs of $Q_R-Q_T$ at the two endpoints of the $L$ interval are thus opposite. Own smoothed masses are continuous in all parameters, uniformly on the compact continuous-direction circle. For the limit $h=0$ this follows by bounded convergence of the threshold integrands off null cut lines, also for varying normals and marks. Their minima are continuous by compactness, and the implicit triangle source is continuous by its construction. Hence both endpoint signs persist for sufficiently small positive $h$ and the intermediate value theorem gives $L_h$. Pure-base exposing caps at $-1,2$ have depths at most $v,L_h^2$ there, strictly below the common value near two. The two unique fiber maxima therefore exhaust the global maximum. The same-parity pairs $(-1,1)$ and $(2,0)$ have midpoints $0$ and $1$, respectively, giving the stated assignments.
