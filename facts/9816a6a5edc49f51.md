## Proof

First identify actual halfspace depth. A closed halfspace
\(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge t\}\) containing \(\mathbf j\) has \(t\le\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf j\), so it contains the centered cap
\(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)\ge0\}\).
Every centered cap with nonzero normal is itself a containing proper closed halfspace. The zero normal gives the whole support, a trace also realizable by a proper closed halfspace on any finite support in positive ambient dimension. Thus the centered definition in the statement is exactly actual closed-halfspace depth. On a singleton the sole containing trace on its support is full, including with the dimension-zero convention. This verifies the depth normalization used in c5abe3c72b8b89eb and f1b85039a5de43a6.

We record the exact count geometry. For a capped factor, 55b5ec7cf9276f99 and 76266dd5f06a2b13 identify its convex hull with
\[
\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2k}:
 \sum_hx_h=2k,\quad
 k-1\le\sum_{h\le k}x_h\le k+1\right\}.
\]
Its integer points are exactly \(J_k\), and its affine hull is
\(H_k=\{\sum_hx_h=2k\}\). Deleting \(x_{2k}\), with inverse
\(x_{2k}=2k-\sum_{h<2k}x_h\), bijects \(H_k\cap\mathbb Z^{2k}\) with \(\mathbb Z^{2k-1}\). Its linear part appends minus the sum of the retained coordinates. The resulting difference lattice is the saturated kernel of the integer sum map. To check that differences of actual support points generate this entire lattice, let \(\mathbf j_0=(1,\ldots,1)\). Both \(\mathbf j_0\) and
\(\mathbf j_0+\mathbf e_h-\mathbf e_{2k}\), for every \(h<2k\), belong to \(J_k\): the first-block total is either \(k\) or \(k+1\), and all counts remain nonnegative. Their chart differences are every standard unit vector. Consequently the affine dimension and the difference-lattice rank are exactly \(2k-1\), and the chart support is complete in \(\mathbb Z^{2k-1}\).

For each \(s\in\{k-1,k,k+1\}\), the slice with first-block total \(s\) has the exact chart
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf x&=(N_1,\ldots,N_{k-1}),
&N_k&=s-\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}x_h,\\
\mathbf y&=(N_{k+1},\ldots,N_{2k-1}),
&N_{2k}&=2k-s-\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}y_h.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus its support is \(D_{s,k-1}\times D_{2k-s,k-1}\), of rank \(2k-2\), and the chart bijects its full affine lattice with \(\mathbb Z^{2k-2}\). Both totals are positive. Each composition block has a support point consisting of its total in the deleted coordinate and the points obtained by transferring one unit to each retained coordinate. These differences generate the full slice difference lattice. It is saturated, being the kernel of the two block-sum equations. Equivalently, the first-block sum is primitive on the full difference lattice because it takes value one at \(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_{2k}\). This includes the dilation-one slices when \(k=2\), with no extra moment certificate asserted for those individual slices.

All root faces also have exact saturated count charts. By the minimal-face description in 55b5ec7cf9276f99, the face at \(\mathbf j\in J_k\) sets every count with \(j_h=0\) to zero, and additionally fixes the block total precisely when \(\sum_{h\le k}j_h\) is \(k-1\) or \(k+1\). Each block has at least one active coordinate. At an interior block-total root, the integer block total is \(k\); delete one active coordinate of the second block. Its total-sum reconstruction and the inactive zero coordinates biject the full face affine lattice with \(\mathbb Z^{|I|-1}\), where \(I=\{h:j_h>0\}\). Differences
\(\mathbf e_h-\mathbf e_t\), with \(t\) the deleted active coordinate and \(h\in I\setminus\{t\}\), are realized by \(\mathbf j+\mathbf e_h-\mathbf e_t\) and \(\mathbf j\). These points remain in the face support since \(j_t\ge1\) and the block total becomes at most \(k+1\). They generate its entire saturated difference lattice. At an endpoint root, delete one active coordinate in each block and reconstruct using the two fixed integer block totals. The resulting chart is the product of the two complete composition charts, of rank \(|I|-2\). Transfers from each deleted active coordinate to the other active coordinates in its block realize every basis difference, again because positive root counts are at least one. The face support is complete in these charts: its real constraints are exactly the full-support constraints together with the inactive zero coordinates and any forced endpoint equality. These arguments include zero-dimensional faces.

We now identify the rows used by all three capped certificates. For any mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\) on a capped factor, log-affinity of the original source gives
\[
D(\nu\|p_i)=-H(\nu)-\log p_{i,\mathbf j},
\qquad
H(\nu):=-\sum_{\mathbf n:\nu(\mathbf n)>0}
                \nu(\mathbf n)\log\nu(\mathbf n).
\]
The boundary-inclusive entropy-capacity identity and row description in bf1ff94487296374 therefore show that the unique relative-entropy minimizing row is precisely
\(\nu_{i,\mathbf j}=r_{J_{k_i},\mathbf j}\), with
\[
\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j)
 =e^{-D(\nu_{i,\mathbf j}\|p_i)}
 =p_{i,\mathbf j}e^{H(\nu_{i,\mathbf j})}.
\]
It is positive on the entire minimal face support and zero elsewhere. The face-relative exponential representation in bf1ff94487296374 says that it is the original log-affine source multiplied by an exponential of a linear functional on that face, with a normalizing constant. Hence it has positive count weights on its active coordinates, exactly as required by 55b5ec7cf9276f99. There is no assumption that a boundary row has a finite ambient capacity minimizer.

In the deletion chart the original source tilt is
\((\ell_{i,h}-\ell_{i,2k_i})_{h<2k_i}\), exactly the tilt \(\log(q_h/q_{2k})\) in d52d1dcf56679b30 when its \(q_h=w_{i,h}\). The pushforward row is its stated maximum-entropy row. Restricting an ambient affine functional to the chart, or extending an intrinsic one by setting its deleted normal component to zero, preserves centered exponential sums, so capacities agree in the chart and the full count space. Thus there is no change of normalization in applying d52d1dcf56679b30 or 76266dd5f06a2b13.

Every capacity is at least its original root atom \(p_{i,\mathbf j}>0\); hence \(\mathcal B_2(p_i)>0\) and \(\omega_i\) is a probability law. The likelihood variance is finite on the finite positive row support. With all these aliases fixed, the three accepted certificates give, for every capped factor,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}}
 [(\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf N_i-\mathbf j))^4]
&\le\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}
 \left(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}}
       [\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf N_i]\right)^2
 &&(\mathbf j\in J_{k_i},\ \mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^{2k_i}),\\
\mathbb E_{\omega_i}V_{i,\mathbf j}
&\le C_{\mathrm{cap}}r_i,
&\mathcal B_2(p_i)&\ge g_{\mathrm{cap}}^{r_i}4^{-r_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first is 55b5ec7cf9276f99, the second is d52d1dcf56679b30, and the third is 76266dd5f06a2b13. Substituting
\(\log D_{\mathrm{num}}=\log256+2\log3+\frac83(\log7+\log2)\)
from d52d1dcf56679b30 gives exactly the displayed \(C_{\mathrm{cap}}\). In particular its price is proportional to rank, not bounded by a constant per factor.

Assume first that the capped group is nonempty. Fix any root tuple \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf j_i)_i\in D_{\mathrm{cap}}\) and let
\(Q_{\mathbf j}:=\bigotimes_i\nu_{i,\mathbf j_i}\). This is also \(r_{D_{\mathrm{cap}},\mathbf j}\): any law with this mean has marginals of means \(\mathbf j_i\), and its entropy is at most the sum of their entropies, which are at most the row entropies. Entropy subadditivity follows from nonnegativity of relative entropy to the product of its marginals. The product rows attain both bounds, and uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of the mean-constrained entropy maximizer. Only independence of these product rows is used below.

For any product direction let
\(Y_i=\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf N_i-\mathbf j_i)\),
\(s_i^2=\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y_i^2\), and \(Y=\sum_iY_i\).
All \(Y_i\) are independent and centered. Expansion of the fourth power eliminates every term with a singleton centered factor, leaving
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y^4
&=\sum_i\mathbb E Y_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}s_i^2s_h^2\\
&\le\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}\sum_i s_i^4+
          6\sum_{i<h}s_i^2s_h^2
 \le\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}\left(\sum_i s_i^2\right)^2
 =\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}(\mathbb E Y^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}\ge3\), so the same constant survives every finite number of factors, including zero-variance directions.

Put \(\sigma^2=\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y^2\). If \(\sigma>0\), Hölder and the preceding fourth-moment bound give
\[
\mathbb E Y^2\le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}
                      (\mathbb E Y^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|Y|\ge\sigma/\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\]
Since \(Y\) is centered, \(\mathbb E Y_+=\mathbb E|Y|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\((\mathbb E Y_+)^2\le\sigma^2Q_{\mathbf j}(Y\ge0)\).
Therefore \(Q_{\mathbf j}(Y\ge0)\ge1/(4\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}})\).
If \(\sigma=0\), \(Y=0\) almost surely and this probability is one. Every containing closed halfspace contains a parallel centered cap, so all such halfspaces have \(Q_{\mathbf j}\)-mass at least \(1/(4\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}})\).

We use the pointwise likelihood mechanism of c5abe3c72b8b89eb, with the rank-charged averaging proved here rather than invoking its per-block-price theorem. On the positive support of \(Q_{\mathbf j}\), put
\[
L_{\mathbf j}:=\log\frac{Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)}
                             {P_{\mathrm{cap}}(\mathbf N)},
\qquad
v(\mathbf j):=\sum_i V_{i,\mathbf j_i}.
\]
The row identity and independence imply
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}L_{\mathbf j}
 =-\sum_i\log\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{\mathbf j}}L_{\mathbf j}=v(\mathbf j).
\]
If \(v(\mathbf j)>0\), Chebyshev bounds
\[
Q_{\mathbf j}\left(
L_{\mathbf j}>\mathbb E L_{\mathbf j}
          +\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}v(\mathbf j)}\right)
 \le\frac1{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\]
If \(v(\mathbf j)=0\), the exceptional event with threshold zero is empty. Intersect the complementary event with any halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\). Its \(Q_{\mathbf j}\)-mass is at least \(\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}}\). At each point of this intersection,
\[
P_{\mathrm{cap}}(\mathbf n)
 =Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf n)e^{-L_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf n)}
 \ge Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf n)
       e^{-\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}v(\mathbf j)}}
       \prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\]
Summing there and taking the halfspace infimum proves the pointwise actual-depth bound
\[
\tau_{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}(\mathbf j)
 \ge\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}}
      e^{-\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}v(\mathbf j)}}
      \prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\]
Original mass outside the row support is nonnegative and is not needed. This treats all face roots without conditioning the original source law on those faces.

Square and average this bound with the original root probabilities
\(P_{\mathrm{cap}}(\mathbf j)=\prod_i p_{i,\mathbf j_i}\). With
\(\Omega:=\bigotimes_i\omega_i\), direct factorization gives
\[
\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{cap}})
 \ge\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}}^2
        \left(\prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i)\right)
        \mathbb E_\Omega
          e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}}\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}}.
\]
The law \(\Omega\) occurs only as an algebraic rewriting of this squared-capacity sum. It changes neither the root law in \(\mathcal M_2\) nor the law measuring halfspace masses. The rank-linear prices give
\[
\mathbb E_\Omega v(\mathbf j)
 =\sum_i\mathbb E_{\omega_i}V_{i,\mathbf j_i}
 \le C_{\mathrm{cap}}S_{\mathrm{rank}},
\qquad
\mathbb E_\Omega\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}
 \le\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{cap}}S_{\mathrm{rank}}}.
\]
The second inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz. Jensen for the convex decreasing function
\(z\mapsto e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}}z}\) now yields
\[
\mathbb E_\Omega
 e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}}\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}}
 \ge e^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}}.
\]
Capacity factorization is exact: the centered exponential sum under a product law is the product of its block sums, and the normal vectors vary independently. Each sum is at least its positive infimum; approaching every block infimum gives the reverse product inequality without requiring attainment. Raising the resulting identity to the second power and summing with the original product root weights gives
\[
\mathsf C_{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}(\mathbf j)
 =\prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i),
\qquad
\mathcal B_2(P_{\mathrm{cap}})
 =\prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i)
 \ge g_{\mathrm{cap}}^{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}4^{-S_{\mathrm{rank}}}.
\]
This agrees with the certificate-independent factorization in c5abe3c72b8b89eb. Combining the last three displays proves the refined capped second-moment bound. Completing the square once for the entire capped group gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{rank}}\log g_{\mathrm{cap}}
       -2\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}
&=\log g_{\mathrm{cap}}
 \left(\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}
        -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}}{\log g_{\mathrm{cap}}}\right)^2
 -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}^2}{\log g_{\mathrm{cap}}}\\
&\ge-\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{cap}}^2}{\log g_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\(\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{cap}})\ge a_{\mathrm{cap}}^2 4^{-R_{\mathrm{cap}}}\).
There is no factor-dependent power of \(a_{\mathrm{cap}}\). If the capped group is empty, its depth and moments are one, \(S_{\mathrm{rank}}=0\), and both the refined bound and this uniform bound hold because \(\alpha_{\mathrm{cap}},a_{\mathrm{cap}}\le1\).

The hypotheses and normalizations of f1b85039a5de43a6 apply to the entire mixed group, with its ranks \(r_i\), tilts \(\boldsymbol\ell_i\), support \(D=D_{\mathrm{mix}}\), and law \(P=P_{\mathrm{mix}}\) in that predecessor. Thus
\(\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{mix}})\ge a_{\mathrm{mix}}^2 4^{-R_{\mathrm{mix}}}\),
including the empty group. We compose this group with the entire capped group once. For two independent laws \(p,q\), a centered halfspace at a product root \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) has event
\(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf x)+\mathbf w\cdot(\mathbf Y-\mathbf y)\ge0\).
It contains the product of the two events where the individual summands are nonnegative. Each event has probability at least its block depth; a zero normal gives probability one. Consequently
\[
\tau_{p\otimes q}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
 \ge\tau_p(\mathbf x)\tau_q(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\mathcal M_2(p\otimes q)\ge\mathcal M_2(p)\mathcal M_2(q),
\]
where the second inequality follows by squaring and averaging with \(p_{\mathbf x}q_{\mathbf y}\). This is actual-depth supermultiplicativity from c5abe3c72b8b89eb, re-established without row hypotheses. It gives
\(\mathcal M_2(P)\ge(a_{\mathrm{cap}}a_{\mathrm{mix}})^2 4^{-R}\).

For any probability law in this proof, Jensen under its original root weights gives

 =\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p(\mathbf j)^2)^{3/2}]
 \ge(\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf j)^2)^{3/2}
 =\mathcal M_2(p)^{3/2}.

Apply this to \(P_{\mathrm{cap}}\) and \(P\). Since
\((4^{-r})^{3/2}=8^{-r}\), it proves all asserted third-moment bounds. The coefficient \(a\) is positive and less than one because the three capped certificate constants are absolute, \(g_{\mathrm{cap}}>1\), and \(a_{\mathrm{mix}}\in(0,1)\). Neither group contributes a coefficient per individual factor. If \(R=0\), no capped factor is present and all mixed factors are singletons, so every normalized depth, capacity, and moment is exactly one.

We finish the lattice and weight transport. By f1b85039a5de43a6, the mixed reduced support \(D_{\mathrm{mix,red}}\) is complete in \(\mathbb Z^{R_{\mathrm{mix}}}\), has full affine dimension, and has difference lattice \(\mathbb Z^{R_{\mathrm{mix}}}\); its inverse chart inserts only zero coordinates. Together with the capped charts proved above, this gives full affine dimension \(R\) and difference lattice \(\mathbb Z^R\) for \(E\). Indeed, holding all other factors at fixed support points realizes every difference generator in each coordinate group. Also the convex hull of a finite Cartesian product is the product of the convex hulls: multiply the coordinate convex-combination coefficients to express every point of the latter as a convex combination of product support points. Completeness of each chart factor therefore gives
\(E=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^R\).

Write \(\psi(\mathbf y)=\mathbf v_0+B_0\mathbf y\). The vector \(\mathbf v_0\) is integral, with value \(2k_i\) in each restored last-count coordinate and zero elsewhere. The matrix \(B_0\) retains the intrinsic coordinates, appends their negative sums in capped blocks, and inserts zero rows in deleted mixed coordinates. It has column rank \(R\). If \(B_0\mathbf y\) is integral for a real vector \(\mathbf y\), all its retained coordinates are integral, so \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb Z^R\). Hence
\(B_0\mathbb Z^R=(B_0\mathbb R^R)\cap\mathbb Z^{n_0}\).
It follows that the full affine lattice of \(D\) is
\(\mathbf v_0+B_0\mathbb Z^R\), its difference lattice is \(B_0\mathbb Z^R\), and both ranks are \(R\). If an integer point lies in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=\psi(\operatorname{conv}(E))\), its retained coordinates are an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\), hence belong to \(E\). Thus it belongs to \(D\). This proves ambient completeness of \(D\), as well as saturation of its difference lattice.

For any injective real affine chart \(\mathbf y\mapsto\mathbf v+B\mathbf y\) of column rank \(R\), an ambient affine inequality

\((B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf y\ge t-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\).
The map \(B^{\mathsf T}:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^R\) is surjective, so every nonconstant intrinsic affine inequality extends to an ambient one. Constant inequalities give full or empty traces, also realizable by proper halfspaces on finite supports in positive ambient dimension. For a zero-dimensional singleton the sole containing trace is full. Thus the chart preserves all closed cap traces and their containing-root traces. Centered exponential sums pull back with normal \(B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u\); surjectivity likewise proves equality of capacities. The same argument applies to \(\psi\) through \(B_0\). Since the individual weights are pushed forward unchanged, trace masses, original root probabilities, actual depths, capacities, and their moments are preserved exactly.

Since \(E\) has affine hull \(\mathbb R^R\), the integer saturation condition on \(B\) in the statement gives

Here affine fullness and the already proved difference lattice of \(E\) justify both rank assertions. If \(\mathbf z\in\operatorname{conv}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^n\), write

It does not assert completeness for an unsaturated embedding.
