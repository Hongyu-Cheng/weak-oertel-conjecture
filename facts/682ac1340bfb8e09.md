## Statement

Fix $d\ge1$ and a nonempty finite lattice-complete set $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ with positive actual compact convex fibers $K_z$ in their canonical hull $C$. Write $h_z$ for their support functions. Fix $p\in P$ and a continuous slope $A(u)$ on $\mathbb S^{d-1}$ with $\Delta_z(u)=h_p(u)+A(u)\cdot(z-p)-h_z(u)\ge0$. Let $\Theta$ be a nonempty compact metric space. Let $V_\theta(u,z,x)$ be jointly continuous and nonnegative on $\Theta\times\mathbb S^{d-1}\times C$, convex in $(z,x)$, and independent of $\theta$ on the source fiber $K_p$. For a Borel probability $\nu$ on $\Theta$ set $V_\nu=\int V_\theta\,d\nu$ and
$$
C_\varepsilon[\nu]
=C\cap\bigcap_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}
\{(z,x):u\cdot x+\varepsilon V_\nu(u,z,x)\le h_p(u)+A(u)\cdot(z-p)\}.
$$

At a regular $x\in\partial K_z$ with normal $n_z(x)$ set $v_{\nu,z}(x)=\mathbf1_{\{\Delta_z(n_z(x))=0\}}V_\nu(n_z(x),z,x)$. Write $v_{\theta,z}=v_{\delta_\theta,z}$ for the pure field speed. For a fixed closed ambient cap $H$, let $\kappa_{z,H}$ be one for a wholly captured $K_z$, zero when $H_z$ misses its interior, and $\mathbf1_{\operatorname{int} H_z}$ for a proper trace on $K_z$. Use $dS=d\mathcal H^{d-1}$ on each fiber boundary, counting the two endpoints when $d=1$. For each regular $x$ the fractional radial loss about any fixed $o_z\in\operatorname{int}K_z$, divided by $\varepsilon$, converges to $v_{\nu,z}(x)/(n_z(x)\cdot(x-o_z))$. The speed equals $\int v_{\theta,z}(x)\,d\nu(\theta)$. If $\mu_\varepsilon(H)$ denotes the actual mixed volume in the fixed cap for $C_\varepsilon[\nu]$, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
|K_z|-|(C_\varepsilon[\nu])_z|&=\varepsilon\int_{\partial K_z}v_{\nu,z}\,dS+o(\varepsilon),\\
\mu_0(H)-\mu_\varepsilon(H)&=\varepsilon\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}\kappa_{z,H}v_{\nu,z}\,dS+o(\varepsilon).
\end{aligned}
$$
The integrated errors are uniform over all Borel probabilities $\nu$ and over each fixed finite menu of caps.

## Proof

Use 84cd8a85417b0d7a with the single ceiling $h_p(u)+A(u)\cdot(z-p)$. Its hypotheses hold for each average by integration, and every field is bounded on the old hull by the same number $M_0=\max_{(\theta,u,w)\in\Theta\times\mathbb S^{d-1}\times C}V_\theta(u,w)$. Its radial bound is therefore $0\le s_\varepsilon(x;\nu)\le\varepsilon M_0/r_z$, with $r_z>0$ a radius of an inball about $o_z$. The single fixed contact indicator commutes with averaging. The first variation for a fixed field and the fixed inward selectors are precisely those of the predecessor.

For uniformity, if a claimed integrated remainder were nonuniform, take $\varepsilon_l\downarrow0$ and $\nu_l$ witnessing this. Probabilities on compact metric $\Theta$ are weakly sequentially compact. On a subsequence $V_{\nu_l}\to V_\nu$ uniformly on $\mathbb S^{d-1}\times C$: weak convergence gives pointwise convergence there, and the joint uniform modulus of the original fields yields uniform convergence by a finite net. Fix a regular old radial point $x$, let $n=n_z(x)$, and put $x_l=o_z+(1-s_l)(x-o_z)$ with $s_l=s_{\varepsilon_l}(x;\nu_l)$. At a contact $n$ the cut gives
$$
s_l\,n\cdot(x-o_z)\ge\varepsilon_l V_{\nu_l}(n,z,x_l).
$$
For the upper bound along indices with $s_l>0$, $x_l$ is interior to $K_z$ and some cut is tight, by compactness of the sphere. For a tight normal $u_l$ the old gap $h_z(u_l)-u_l\cdot x+\Delta_z(u_l)$ is nonnegative and equals $\varepsilon_lV_{\nu_l}(u_l,z,x_l)-s_lu_l\cdot(x-o_z)=O(\varepsilon_l)$. Every subsequential limit of tight normals is both supporting at $x$ and a contact normal, hence equals $n$. If $n$ is not a contact there is no such positive-loss subsequence. Otherwise discard the nonnegative gap and divide by $u_l\cdot(x-o_z)$, which tends to $n\cdot(x-o_z)\ge r_z$. Uniform field convergence gives the upper limit, including when the limiting speed is zero. Together these inequalities show $s_l/\varepsilon_l-v_{\nu_l,z}(x)/(n\cdot(x-o_z))\to0$.

Now use the predecessor's radial shell Jacobian $(1-s)^{d-1}n\cdot(x-o_z)$ with the uniform homothet bound. The cap status for the thin radial shell is exactly full or empty in the first two selector cases, and eventually agrees with $\kappa_{z,H}$ outside the surface-null proper cutting plane in the third. Bounded dominated convergence on each fiber and finiteness of bases and caps show the integrated normalized errors tend to zero on this subsequence. This contradicts the choice of the sequence and proves uniformity.
