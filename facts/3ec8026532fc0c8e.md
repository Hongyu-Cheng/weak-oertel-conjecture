## Statement

For integers $m\ge8$ put $a=1/m$, $\eta=2^{-m}$, $b=a-\eta$, $U=1-b$, $K_m=\{x,y\ge0:a\le x+y\le1\}$, $V_m=|K_m|=(1-a^2)/2$, and $\ell_m=x+y-b$. Let $c_m=\exp[-V_m^{-1}\int_{K_m}\log\ell_m]$ and $L_m=c_m\ell_m$. With $Z_m=\int_{K_m}\ell_m^{-1}$ define the probability $\rho_m=dX/(Z_m\ell_m)$ on $K_m$, its mean $Y_m$, and $L_{i,m}=L_m(Y_m)$. Then $\mathbb E_{\rho_m}L_m=L_{i,m}$, $\rho_m/L_{i,m}=dX|_{K_m}/(V_m L_m)$, and $(L_m/L_{i,m})\rho_m=dX|_{K_m}/V_m$. Among positive concave $F$ on $K_m$ with mean logarithm zero, $F(Y_m)\ge L_{i,m}$, with equality only if $F=L_m$ almost everywhere. Moreover $B(Y_m,1/32)\subset K_m$, $L_{i,m}\ge63/256$, $\max L_m\le e^{3/2}$ and $\int dX/(V_m L_m)\le256/63$.

Let $K=\{x,y\ge0:x+y\le1\}$. Then $L_m\to e^{1/2}(x+y)$ coefficientwise, while
$$
\frac{dX|_{K_m}}{V_m L_m}\rightharpoonup 2e^{-1/2}\left[\frac{dX|_K}{x+y}+(\log 2)\delta_0\right],\qquad
\rho_m\rightharpoonup \frac{dX|_K/(x+y)+(\log 2)\delta_0}{1+\log 2}.
$$
In particular the inverse densities are not uniformly integrable. The tilted barycenters tend to $(1,1)/[4(1+\log 2)]$, and the original-volume laws tend to $dX|_K/|K|$ with centroid $(1/3,1/3)$. For sufficiently large $m$, $Y_m$ is not an ordinary deepest mark.

## Proof

Set $t=x+y$ and write $(x,y)=(ts,t(1-s))$ for $0\le s\le1$; the area element is $t\,dt\,ds$. Direct division yields
$$
Z_m=1-a+b\log(U/\eta),\qquad
I_m=\int_a^1 \frac{t^2}{t-b}\,dt=V_m+b(1-a)+b^2\log(U/\eta).
$$
Thus $I_m-bZ_m=V_m$, $Y_m=(I_m,I_m)/(2Z_m)$ and $L_{i,m}=c_mV_m/Z_m$. The contact identities follow, using affinity for the $L_m$-mean. Logarithmic Jensen under $\bar\rho_m=(L_m/L_{i,m})\rho_m$ gives $\int\log(F/L_m)\,d\bar\rho_m\le\log(\int F\,d\rho_m/L_{i,m})\le\log(F(Y_m)/L_{i,m})$ by concavity of $F$. Its left side is zero. Equality forces $F/L_m$ constant almost everywhere, and normalization fixes that constant.

For $m\ge8$, $0<b<a\le1/8$ and $\log(U/\eta)<m$, so $7/8\le Z_m<2$ and $63/128\le V_m\le I_m<1/2+2a\le3/4$. The coordinates of $Y_m$ lie between $63/512$ and $3/7$. Its distances to the two diagonal boundaries are at least $(31/256)/\sqrt 2$ and $(1/7)/\sqrt 2$. These bounds give the claimed ball. Since $\ell_m<1$, $c_m\ge1$ and $L_{i,m}\ge63/256$. For the upper bound, a concave function of maximum $B$ on a two-dimensional convex body has $\mathbb P(L_m\ge sB)\ge(1-s)^2$ under normalized area, by contraction about its maximizer. Hence $\int\log(B/L_m)\,dX/V_m\le\int_0^\infty[1-(1-e^{-u})^2]\,du=3/2$ and normalization gives $B\le e^{3/2}$. The bound on total inverse mass is $1/L_{i,m}$.

For the log integral, substitute $s=t-b$. A primitive is $s^2\log s/2-s^2/4+b(s\log s-s)$. Its upper evaluation tends to $-1/4$ and its lower evaluation tends to zero since $\eta^2|\log\eta|$ and $b\eta|\log\eta|$ do. Thus $c_m\to e^{1/2}$. Also $b\log(U/\eta)\to\log 2$, so $Z_m\to1+\log 2$, $I_m\to1/2$ and $V_m\to1/2$. For every continuous $\phi$ on the common triangle, split the integral against $dX/\ell_m$ using $t/(t-b)=1+b/(t-b)$. The first term tends by bounded convergence to $\int_K\phi/(x+y)\,dX$. The second has total mass $b\log(U/\eta)\to\log 2$, and for any fixed $\delta>0$ its mass at $t\ge\delta$ is at most $b\log((1-b)/(\delta-b))\to0$. Uniform continuity then gives its limit $(\log 2)\phi(0)$. This proves both limits and the mean limits. The positive atom rules out uniform integrability on the common triangle. The original-volume mixture has exactly normalized area at every $m$.

At the limiting tilted barycenter the diagonal lower halfspace has normalized area $1/[4(1+\log 2)^2]<1/4$. The standard triangle centroid has ordinary depth $4/9$. To see this directly, for a line through the centroid isolating one vertex, let $a_1,a_2\in(0,1]$ be its incident edge intercept fractions. The centroid condition is $1/a_1+1/a_2=3$, so $a_1a_2\ge4/9$ by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. Also $1/a_1,1/a_2\ge1$ and their sum is three, so $a_1a_2\le1/2$ and the complementary cap has mass fraction at least $1/2$. This covers the case of a line through a vertex by allowing an intercept fraction of one. A side-parallel cap attains $4/9$. This strict inequality persists on $K_m$: symmetric-difference volume controls every fixed cap mass, and varying the through-mark threshold by the mark displacement costs at most a uniform slab constant times that displacement. Hence the limiting comparison excludes ordinary deepestness for all sufficiently large $m$.
