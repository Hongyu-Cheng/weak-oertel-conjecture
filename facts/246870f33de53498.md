## Statement

For integers \(k,d\ge1\) and a measurable set \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^d\), its mixed-integer volume is

\[
\mathcal H_d(A)
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^k}
\mathcal L^d\bigl(\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in A\}\bigr).
\]

For a nonempty compact such set \(A\) and \(p\in A\), define its closed-halfspace depth and maximal depth by

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_A(p)&=\inf\{\mathcal H_d(A\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }p\},\\
h_{\max}(A)&=\max_{p\in A}h_A(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here a closed halfspace has the form \(\{X:\mathbf a\cdot X\ge\beta\}\) with \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\). The maxima in this definition exist, as shown below. For a convex body \(Q\subset\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^d\), its integer lattice width means width in the integer variables:

\[
\operatorname{lw}_{\mathbb Z^k}(Q)
=\inf_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z^k\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\left(\max_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in Q}\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x
-\min_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in Q}\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x\right).
\]

For the positive-volume construction, let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Set \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf z})\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Assume \(W>0\), which implies \(S\ne\varnothing\). Put \(D=\{\mathbf z:w_{\mathbf z}>0\}\) and \(S_+=\bigcup_{\mathbf z\in D}(\{\mathbf z\}\times C_{\mathbf z})\).

There are an affine map \(T(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(U(\mathbf x-\mathbf z_0),\mathbf y)\), with \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\) and \(\mathbf z_0\in D\), and a convex body \(B\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\), such that \(S_B:=B\cap\Lambda=T(S_+)\), \(\mathcal H_d(S_B)=W\), and \(\operatorname{lw}_{\mathbb Z^n}(B)\ge1\). Every positive fiber is preserved exactly under its base relabeling, and

\[
h_{S_B}(T p)=h_S(p)\quad(p\in S_+),
\qquad h_S(p)=0\quad(p\in S\setminus S_+).
\]

Define \(\Phi_0(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(\mathbf x,0,\mathbf y)\), \(\Phi_1(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(-\mathbf x,1,-\mathbf y)\), and

\[
\widehat C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\Phi_0(B)\cup\Phi_1(B)\bigr),
\qquad
\widehat S=\widehat C\cap(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\times\mathbb R^d).
\]

Then \(\widehat C\) is a full-dimensional centrally symmetric convex body with center \((\mathbf0,1/2,\mathbf0)\), integer lattice width exactly one, and complete mixed-integer set \(\widehat S=\Phi_0(S_B)\cup\Phi_1(S_B)\). For every \(p\in S_+\) and \(i\in\{0,1\}\), its pointwise depth satisfies \(h_{\widehat S}(\Phi_i(Tp))=h_S(p)\). In particular,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat W:=\mathcal H_d(\widehat S)&=2W,
&h_{\max}(\widehat S)&=h_{\max}(S),\\
\frac{2^{n+1}h_{\max}(\widehat S)}{\widehat W}
&=\frac{2^n h_{\max}(S)}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The constructed symmetry center is outside \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\times\mathbb R^d\).

For each fixed \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), the following two universally quantified assertions are equivalent.

(i) For every pair of integers \(n,d\ge1\) and every convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) for which \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\), there exists \(p\in S\) such that \(h_S(p)\ge c_0 2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S)\). Equivalently, the same \(p\), chosen before the halfspace, satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c_0 2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S)\) for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(p\).

(ii) For every pair of integers \(m,d\ge1\) and every centrally symmetric convex body \(Q\subset\mathbb R^{m+d}\) for which \(\operatorname{lw}_{\mathbb Z^m}(Q)=1\) and \(S_Q:=Q\cap(\mathbb Z^m\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\), there exists \(q\in S_Q\) such that \(h_{S_Q}(q)\ge c_0 2^{-m}\mathcal H_d(S_Q)\). Equivalently, the same \(q\), chosen before the halfspace, satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S_Q\cap H)\ge c_0 2^{-m}\mathcal H_d(S_Q)\) for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+d}\) containing \(q\).

Both assertions include zero-volume instances subject to their stated nonemptiness hypotheses, and each uses one constant independent of the dimensions and body. Consequently existence of an absolute \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) satisfying (i) is equivalent to existence of such a constant satisfying (ii).

## Proof

For the positive-volume construction, compactness of \(C\) makes \(D\) finite, and \(W>0\) makes it nonempty. Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). If \(\mathbf z\in P\), express it as a convex combination of sites in \(D\). Choose one positive coefficient and fix one point in each of the other fibers. Convexity then puts a translate of a positive scalar multiple of the chosen positive-volume fiber inside \(C_{\mathbf z}\). Thus \(C_{\mathbf z}\) has positive \(d\)-volume, and in particular \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\).

Let \(C_+=\operatorname{conv}S_+\). It is compact and convex, is contained in \(C\), has projection \(P\), and contains every complete fiber \(\{\mathbf z\}\times C_{\mathbf z}\) for \(\mathbf z\in D\). Consequently its fiber at every such base equals the original fiber, and \(C_+\cap\Lambda=S_+\). The finite union \(S\setminus S_+\) of zero-volume fibers has mixed volume zero. If \(p=(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\) lies in that union, then \(\mathbf z\notin P\). Strict separation from the compact convex set \(P\) supplies a vertical closed halfspace containing \(p\) and no fiber with base in \(D\). Its mixed volume is zero, so \(h_S(p)=0\).

We next obtain integer coordinates suited to \(P\). Fix \(\mathbf z_0\in D\), let \(E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D-\mathbf z_0)\), and put \(r=\dim E\). The subgroup \(\Gamma=E\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is saturated: if a nonzero integer multiple of an integer vector belongs to \(\Gamma\), that vector itself belongs to \(E\), hence to \(\Gamma\). It spans \(E\).

A saturated subgroup can be sent to \(\mathbb Z^r\times\{\mathbf0\}\) by a unimodular matrix. Here is a direct justification. If its rank is positive, divide any nonzero member by the greatest common divisor of its coordinates. Saturation keeps the resulting primitive vector in the subgroup. The Euclidean algorithm, implemented by integer coordinate swaps, sign changes, and addition of integer multiples of coordinates, sends this vector to the first standard basis vector by a unimodular map. The transformed subgroup then splits as that first coordinate group plus its intersection with the remaining coordinate space: subtract the first coordinate times that basis vector from each member. The latter subgroup is again saturated and has rank one less. Induction proves the assertion, including rank zero. Therefore choose \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\) with \(UE=\mathbb R^r\times\{\mathbf0\}\), and use the displayed affine map \(T\).

Write \(T(C_+)=\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf0,\mathbf y):(\mathbf u,\mathbf y)\in K\}\), defining a compact convex set \(K\subset\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\), and let \(P_r\) be its projection onto \(\mathbb R^r\). The set \(P_r\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope in that space, and its lattice points correspond bijectively to \(D\). Moreover \(K\) has dimension \(r+d\): its projection has dimension \(r\), and its affine span contains all \(d\) vertical directions because one of its fibers has positive \(d\)-volume. Thus define the full-dimensional convex body

\[
B=\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf y):
(\mathbf u,\mathbf y)\in K,\quad
\mathbf v\in[-1/2,1/2]^{n-r}\}.
\]

The only integer vector in the transverse cube is \(\mathbf0\). Hence every integer fiber of \(B\) is either empty or exactly the original fiber \(C_{\mathbf z}\) at the relabeled base \(U(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)\), for \(\mathbf z\in D\). This proves \(S_B=T(S_+)\) and equality of total mixed volumes. Rank zero is covered by taking \(\mathbb R^0\) to be a singleton; rank \(n\) is covered by an empty transverse block.

The map \(T\) bijects the ambient lattice fibers, acts identically on their continuous coordinates, and bijects ambient closed halfspaces. It therefore preserves the mixed measure of every measurable subset of \(S_+\) and its depth at every point. Since deleting \(S\setminus S_+\) changes no halfspace measure, this proves \(h_{S_B}(Tp)=h_S(p)\) for \(p\in S_+\).

The projection of \(B\) is \(P_r\times[-1/2,1/2]^{n-r}\). For an integer functional with blocks \((\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\), its range on this product has length equal to its range length on \(P_r\) plus \(\sum_j|b_j|\). If \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\), the first length is a positive integer: the extrema occur at lattice vertices, and positivity follows from full dimension of \(P_r\). If \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), the second length is at least one. These cases exhaust nonzero integer functionals, proving \(\operatorname{lw}_{\mathbb Z^n}(B)\ge1\).

The hull \(\widehat C\) is compact and convex. Its section at new integer coordinate zero is exactly \(\Phi_0(B)\), its section at one is exactly \(\Phi_1(B)\), and all its new coordinates lie in \([0,1]\). Indeed, every point of the hull is a convex combination of one point from each of these two convex sets, so an endpoint coordinate forces membership in the corresponding endpoint set. This proves the stated complete lattice census. The bottom section has dimension \(n+d\), and any top point is outside its affine hull, proving full dimension \(n+d+1\). The affine reflection \((\mathbf x,s,\mathbf y)\mapsto(-\mathbf x,1-s,-\mathbf y)\) exchanges the two sections, so it preserves their hull and gives the stated symmetry center. Each layer has mixed volume \(W\), since its vertical map is an isometry. The layers are disjoint, giving \(\widehat W=2W\).

Fix \(q\in S_B\). Any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\Phi_0(q)\) can be written as \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+b s+\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y\ge\beta\), with combined normal nonzero. If \((\mathbf a,\mathbf c)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\), its bottom restriction is an old closed halfspace containing \(q\); the bottom layer alone therefore has mass at least \(h_{S_B}(q)\). If \((\mathbf a,\mathbf c)=(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\), containment of \(\Phi_0(q)\) implies \(\beta\le0\), and the whole bottom layer belongs to the halfspace. Its mass is then \(W\ge h_{S_B}(q)\). Nonnegative mass on the other layer proves \(h_{\widehat S}(\Phi_0(q))\ge h_{S_B}(q)\).

Conversely, take any old proper closed halfspace \(H_0=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y\ge\beta\}\) containing \(q\). Choose a finite positive number \(M\) with

\[
M>\max_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in B}
(-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x-\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y)-\beta.
\]

This is possible by compactness. The new halfspace \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y-Ms\ge\beta\) contains \(\Phi_0(q)\), restricts exactly to \(H_0\) on the bottom, and strictly excludes the entire top section. By the complete lattice census, its mixed volume is exactly \(\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap H_0)\). Infimizing over \(H_0\) gives the reverse depth inequality. Reflection preserves the mixed measure and bijects closed halfspaces, so \(h_{\widehat S}(\Phi_1(q))=h_{\widehat S}(\Phi_0(q))=h_{S_B}(q)\). No boundary mass was discarded in either direction.

For completeness, the maxima used in the statement exist for every nonempty compact \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^d\). Its projection contains only finitely many integer bases, and its continuous fibers are bounded, so its mixed measure is finite. The depth function is upper semicontinuous: given a closed halfspace \(\mathbf a\cdot X\ge\beta\) containing a point \(p\), the relaxed halfspace \(\mathbf a\cdot X\ge\beta-\varepsilon\) contains all sufficiently nearby points. Its measure decreases to that of the original closed halfspace as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), by continuity from above of a finite measure. Taking the infimum over the original containing halfspaces proves upper semicontinuity. Compactness and nonemptiness of \(A\) therefore give a maximum. For our sets, the discarded original points have depth zero; the retained points are nonempty and have nonnegative depths. The pointwise identities and the complete lattice census give \(h_{\max}(\widehat S)=h_{\max}(S_B)=h_{\max}(S)\).

Finally consider an integer functional \((\mathbf a,b)\ne(\mathbf0,0)\) on the integer variables of \(\widehat C\). If \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\), its range on the bottom section already has length at least one, by the width property of \(B\). If \(\mathbf a=\mathbf0\), its range length is \(|b|\ge1\). The new coordinate itself has range length exactly one, proving \(\operatorname{lw}_{\mathbb Z^{n+1}}(\widehat C)=1\). The displayed scaled-depth identity follows from \(\widehat W=2W\).

Fix \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\). Assertion (i) implies (ii) because the bodies admitted in (ii) form a subclass of those admitted in (i). Conversely, assume (ii) and take any instance \(n,d,C\) admitted in (i). In particular, its set \(S\) is nonempty. If \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)=0\), choose any \(p\in S\). Every containing halfspace has nonnegative mixed volume, giving \(h_S(p)\ge0=c_0 2^{-n}W\), as required in (i).

If \(W>0\), apply the construction and then (ii) to \(Q=\widehat C\) in integer dimension \(m=n+1\) and continuous dimension \(d\). All its hypotheses have been proved, including \(\widehat S\ne\varnothing\), central symmetry, and integer lattice width one. The selected point equals \(\Phi_i(Tp)\) for some \(p\in S_+\) and \(i\in\{0,1\}\), by the complete lattice census. Its depth is at least \(c_0 2^{-(n+1)}\widehat W\); the pointwise identity and \(\widehat W=2W\) give \(h_S(p)\ge c_0 2^{-(n+1)}(2W)=c_0 2^{-n}W\). This proves (i), including its quantifier order and all its zero-volume instances, with the same constant. Since this holds for every fixed \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), it also proves the stated equivalence concerning existence of an absolute constant.
