## Statement

Let $m,N\ge1$. For each row $i$ let $f_i:\mathbb R_+^m\to\mathbb R$ satisfy $f_i(\alpha x)=\alpha f_i(x)$ for $\alpha\ge0$. Suppose that $\sum_{i=1}^N f_i(X_i)$ attains its maximum over the nonnegative $N\times m$ matrices with each column sum equal to one. At every maximizer with the fewest nonzero rows, the nonzero rows are linearly independent. In particular there are at most $m$ such rows.

## Proof

Write $I=\{i:X_i\ne0\}$ for the active rows of a least-active maximizer $X$. Suppose $\sum_{i\in I} a_iX_i=0$ for coefficients not all zero. The coefficients have both signs, since each $X_i$ is nonzero and nonnegative. For all $t$ with $1+t a_i\ge0$ for every $i\in I$, let $X_i(t)=(1+t a_i)X_i$ for $i\in I$ and keep all other rows zero. This matrix is feasible, since its column-sum vector is $\sum_iX_i+t\sum_{i\in I}a_iX_i=\mathbf 1$. By homogeneity the objective on this interval is affine, with slope $\sum_{i\in I}a_i f_i(X_i)$. Both signs of $t$ are feasible near zero, so maximality makes the slope zero. At a finite endpoint one of the active rows vanishes, and the objective is still maximal. This contradicts the choice of $X$.
