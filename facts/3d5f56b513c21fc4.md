## Statement

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite subset of a real affine space. Its dependence components $P_i$ are the components of the hypergraph whose edges consist of a target and all positive-coefficient sources of any nontrivial anchored convex representation on $P$. Equivalently, for each nonvertex $z$ of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ use the single hyperedge $P\cap F_z$, where $F_z$ is its minimal face. Then for every $A\subseteq P$,
$$
P\cap\operatorname{conv}(A)=\bigcup_i\bigl(P_i\cap\operatorname{conv}(A\cap P_i)\bigr),
\qquad h(P)=\sum_i h(P_i),
$$
where $h$ is the relative Helly number for finite families of convex sets. Each $P_i$ is a closed minor. If $P$ is a complete subset of an affine lattice, its components are complete and their occupied parity classes in that lattice are disjoint. In particular $k(P)=\sum_i k(P_i)$ for the occupied class counts $k$. For a complete base in $\mathbb Z^n$, $n\ge1$, one also has $\sum_i h(P_i)\le2^n$.

## Proof

Every positive-coefficient source representing $z$ lies in the minimal face $F_z$ by the supporting equalities of that face. For a nonvertex $z$, the other sites in $F_z$ include all its vertices, and $z$ is in the relative interior of their hull. Give each other site a small positive weight. After subtracting those weighted sites and rescaling the remaining mass, the resulting point stays in their hull if the initial weights are small enough. Represent it there and add its coefficients to the initial ones. This gives one representation using every other site in $F_z$, proving the equivalence of hypergraphs for connectivity. For a point in $P\cap\operatorname{conv}(A)$, its representation uses only its own component unless it is the trivial representation, in which case the point already belongs to $A$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. Applying the equality to a component makes it closed.

For any finite family of convex sets missing $P$ in common, choose a certificate of size at most $h(P_i)$ on each component. Their union certifies emptiness on $P$. Conversely, choose on each $P_i$ a minimal infeasible family of $h(P_i)$ closed traces. Such a family exists from the definition of the relative Helly number and the realization of closed traces by their convex hulls. Extend each trace by including all other components. The split trace formula makes each extension closed. All these traces together have empty intersection, and deleting any one leaves a witness in its own component, proving the reverse bound.

For a complete lattice base, each component is complete by its closedness. If sites in two different components have the same parity, their lattice midpoint belongs to $P$, and its anchored representation joins those components. Thus distinct components use disjoint occupied classes. For the Helly cap in $\mathbb Z^n$, apply accepted fact bee3cc8e50a0dcea to $P$ with one zero mark in $\mathbb R$ per site. Every same-parity midpoint is present by completeness, so the fact's midpoint hypothesis holds. The convex sets may be taken as products with $\mathbb R$, giving $h(P)\le2^n$ on the base.
