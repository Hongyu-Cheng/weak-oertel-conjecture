## Statement

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a triangle of area $V$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$, and use the mixed source of $[0,1]\times T$. For $1-1/\sqrt2<t<1/3$ take closed cap groups
$$
H_{0,i}=\{\lambda_i-t-z\ge0\},\qquad H_{1,i}=\{\lambda_i-t-(1-z)\ge0\},\qquad i=0,1,2.
$$
All cap masses are $(1-t)^2V$, and each group covers its own fiber alone. The polytope cut out by the six reversed weak inequalities is bounded and full-dimensional with exact base projection $[1/3-t,2/3+t]$. For every $(1-t)^2V<\alpha<V/2$, the real depth-$\alpha$ region is nonempty and lies in that polytope, so has no mixed-integer point. Yet no two choices of probability weights $\pi_{s,i}$ make $\sum_{s,i}\pi_{s,i}\mathbf1_{H_{s,i}}-(4/9)\mathbf1_S$ nonnegative pointwise on the mixed source $S$. At $t=3/10$ one may take $\alpha=99V/200$.

## Proof

At the own base each cap is a homothetic corner triangle of area $(1-t)^2V$. The threshold at the other base is $1+t>1$, so the cap is empty there. The three own caps cover the fiber because $\max_i\lambda_i\ge1/3>t$. Summing each group's three reversed inequalities and using $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$ yields the stated base interval. The triangle centroid $g$ is feasible throughout this interval, and strictly feasible for interior bases, proving exact projection and full dimension. The base interval is bounded and all three barycentric coordinates have uniform upper bounds on it, so their sum one gives uniform lower bounds and bounds $x$ as well. At the left and right endpoint paired with $g$, the corresponding group's three margins are zero and the other group's margins are all $-1/3-2t$. At $t=3/10$ this is $-14/15$. Either projected restriction is needed to exclude the corresponding integer base, as is seen by deleting its group and using $g$ at that base.

A point in a listed closed cap has raw depth below $\alpha$. Thus the real depth region lies in the reversed polytope. Rado's centerpoint theorem for a finite Borel measure in $\mathbb R^3$ gives a real point of closed-halfspace depth at least $(2V)/4=V/2>\alpha$. The projected interval is strictly inside $(0,1)$, so it has no integer base. The parameter bound $t>1-1/\sqrt2$ is exactly what allows $\alpha<V/2$ above the cap mass. At $t=3/10$, $(1-t)^2=49/100<99/200<1/2$.

An open relative region near each triangle vertex lies in exactly its own vertex cap among all six. Nonnegativity there would require the three probabilities for each group to be at least $4/9$ each. Their sum is one, a contradiction.
