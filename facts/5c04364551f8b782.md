## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), use precisely the standard recursive chain partition and completed block objects in accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409. In particular, \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), \(D_m\) is the nonterminal set, \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) flips the first unmatched zero under the left-to-right last-zero stack matching, and
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\qquad
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}.
$$
Here \(e_h\) is a coordinate unit vector; coordinate counts below use different symbols.

Choose any integer \(L\ge1\). Independently for each \(1\le\ell\le L\), choose a width \(m_\ell\ge1\) and finite real penalties \(\alpha_{\ell,h}\ge0\), \(1\le h\le m_\ell\). On the entire block support define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\psi_\ell(x)&=\sum_{h=1}^{m_\ell}\alpha_{\ell,h}\max(0,x_h-1),\qquad
w_{\ell,x}=e^{-\psi_\ell(x)},\\
Z_\ell&=\sum_{x\in T_{m_\ell}}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=w_{\ell,x}/Z_\ell\quad(x\in T_{m_\ell}),\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Each law is zero outside its displayed support. Put \(N=\sum_\ell m_\ell\). The original probabilities are exactly
\(P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}=\exp(-\sum_\ell\psi_\ell(x_\ell))/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\).

Retain the actual depths and moments of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
All original ambient containing closed halfspaces are included, even for lower-dimensional supports. With natural logarithms, define
$$
\delta=\log(1024/841)>0,\qquad
c_{\mathrm{coord}}=\frac14\exp\left[-\frac9\delta
-147456\left(2+12(\log2)^2\right)\right]>0.
\tag{3}
$$
Then every choice in (1) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{coord}}4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{coord}}^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{4}
$$
These bounds include zero penalties and are uniform as any finite penalties increase without bound. Each original block support is complete in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_{m_\ell})\), and their product is complete in the product of these convex sets.

## Proof

We first give the one-block calculations with all original normalizations. Suppress \(\ell\), put \(K=2^m\), \(t_h=e^{-\alpha_h/2}\in(0,1]\), and define the integer counts
$$
v_m=|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|,\qquad
e_{m,h}=|\lbrace c\in D_m:i_m(c)=h\rbrace|,\qquad
h_{m,h}=|\lbrace c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m:i_m(c)=h\rbrace|.
\tag{5}
$$
The subscripted count \(e_{m,h}\) is unrelated to the unit vector \(e_h\). Every Boolean atom has weight one, and the spike with source \(c\) has weight \(t_{i_m(c)}^2\). The spike map is injective, since its coordinate equal to two identifies the changed coordinate and replacing that entry by zero recovers \(c\). Consequently, with \(A\) denoting an unnormalized cubic atom sum,
$$
Z=v_m+\sum_h e_{m,h}t_h^2,\qquad
A=\sum_{x\in T_m}w_x^3=v_m+\sum_h e_{m,h}t_h^6,\qquad
\sum_xp_x^3=A/Z^3.
\tag{6}
$$
In particular \(v_m\le K\), \(\sum_h e_{m,h}=|D_m|\le K\), and \(\sum_h h_{m,h}\le K\).

The block application of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 has exactly the aliases \(n_1=m\), \(A_1=T_m\), \(w_{1,x}=w_x\), \(Z_1=Z\), and \(p_1=p\). It proves that every distinct same-parity pair is
\(\lbrace c,c+2e_{i_m(c)}\rbrace\), with \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\), that its center is \(F_m(c)\), and that these centers are distinct. Every supported reflected row is this pair together with its center, or a singleton. The endpoint sets of distinct pairs are disjoint. At the pair indexed by a source with \(i_m(c)=h\), its Boolean center and Boolean endpoint have probability \(1/Z\), while its spike endpoint has probability \(t_h^2/Z\).

For reference retain the full canonical quantities
\(H_p(j)=\sum_x\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\), \(b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2\), \(\kappa_j(x)=\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}/H_p(j)\), \(Y_j(x)=\frac12\log(p_x/p_{2j-x})\), \(V_j=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2\), and \(q_j=p_jH_p(j)^2/b(p)\). The reflected sum includes both nonzero orientations and zero once, exactly as in that accepted fact. A nontrivial row of coordinate \(h\) has
$$
H_p(j)=\frac{1+2t_h}{Z},\qquad
V_j=\frac{2t_h}{1+2t_h}\log^2\frac1{t_h}.
$$
Its three scores are \(\log(1/t_h),0,-\log(1/t_h)\), and its row masses are \(t_h/(1+2t_h),1/(1+2t_h),t_h/(1+2t_h)\). Singleton rows have zero variance. Therefore the exact full-row formulas are
$$
\begin{aligned}
b(p)&=\frac{A+\sum_hh_{m,h}(4t_h+4t_h^2)}{Z^3},\\
\sum_jq_jV_j
&=\frac{\sum_h2h_{m,h}t_h(1+2t_h)\log^2(1/t_h)}
{A+\sum_hh_{m,h}(4t_h+4t_h^2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Distinctness of centers justifies adding each row enhancement once, including when a center is itself another pair's endpoint.

We will also use restricted symmetric rows. Choose any subset \(\mathcal S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\). Keep the full three-point row at centers whose source coordinate is in \(\mathcal S\), and use only the singleton \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) at every other center. Call these symmetric sets \(\widehat R_j\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\qquad
\widehat b=\sum_jp_j\widehat H(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{\widehat H(j)}
\quad(x\in\widehat R_j),\qquad
\widehat Y_j(x)=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
\widehat V_j&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_j}\widehat Y_j^2,\qquad
\widehat q_j=\frac{p_j\widehat H(j)^2}{\widehat b},\qquad
\widehat\nu=\sum_j\widehat q_j\widehat V_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Hats designate restricted auxiliary quantities; the canonical full \(H_p,b(p),\kappa_j,Y_j,V_j,q_j\) remain unchanged. All hats are normalized using the original \(p=w/Z\). In particular, if
\(B_{\mathcal S}=A+\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}h_{m,h}(4t_h+4t_h^2)\), then
$$
\widehat b=B_{\mathcal S}/Z^3,\qquad
\widehat\nu=
\frac{\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}2h_{m,h}t_h(1+2t_h)\log^2(1/t_h)}{B_{\mathcal S}}.
\tag{9}
$$
These follow from exactly the row calculation proving (7).

Suppose now that \(m\ge7\). The walk characterization of accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409 gives \(v_7=37\). Appending either bit preserves a prefix that has hit height three. Hence \(v_{m+1}\ge2v_m\), and
\(v_m/K\ge37/128\ge1/4\) for every \(m\ge7\).

Group the unnormalized weights by their \(K\) parity classes, let \(r_\eta\) be the sum in class \(\eta\in B_m\), and put \(\mathcal Q=\sum_{\eta\in B_m}r_\eta^3\). A nonsingleton class is exactly one of the pairs above, of weights \(1,t_h^2\). Expansion of that class cube gives the exact identity
$$
\mathcal Q=A+3\sum_hh_{m,h}(t_h^2+t_h^4).
\tag{10}
$$
Because \(\sum_\eta r_\eta=Z\), Jensen gives \(\mathcal Q\ge Z^3/K^2\). Also \(r_\eta\le2\) and \(Z\le2K\), so \(\mathcal Q\le4Z\le8K\).

Set
$$
G=\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}h_{m,h}(4t_h+t_h^2-3t_h^4),\qquad
R=3\sum_{h\notin\mathcal S}h_{m,h}(t_h^2+t_h^4).
$$
Then \(B_{\mathcal S}=\mathcal Q+G-R\). For \(0<t\le1\),
\(4t+t^2-3t^4\ge2t\), since \(2+t-3t^3=(1-t)(3t^2+3t+2)\ge0\). Thus
$$
2\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}h_{m,h}t_h\le G\le5K,\qquad
R\le6\sum_{h\notin\mathcal S}h_{m,h}t_h^2,\qquad
B_{\mathcal S}\ge A\ge v_m\ge K/4.
$$
For \(G,R\ge0\) with \(\mathcal Q+G-R>0\), integration of \(1/u\) gives
\(\log((\mathcal Q+G)/\mathcal Q)\ge G/(\mathcal Q+G)\) and
\(\log((\mathcal Q+G-R)/(\mathcal Q+G))\ge-R/(\mathcal Q+G-R)\).
Using also \(\mathcal Q+G\le13K\le16K\) proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(K^2\widehat b)
&\ge\log(B_{\mathcal S}/\mathcal Q)\\
&\ge\frac18\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}\frac{h_{m,h}}K t_h
-24\sum_{h\notin\mathcal S}\frac{h_{m,h}}K t_h^2,\\
\widehat\nu
&\le24\sum_{h\in\mathcal S}\frac{h_{m,h}}K t_h\log^2\frac1{t_h}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The last inequality is (9), since \(2t(1+2t)\le6t\) and \(B_{\mathcal S}\ge K/4\). This proves both estimates for every coordinate selection independently, while retaining all weights in \(Z\).

For the small widths \(1\le m\le6\), keep every full row. The exact counts in a2c2d2b38e2fa409 give
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
m&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
|T_m|&1&2&6&12&29&58\\
4^m/|T_m|^2&4&4&16/9&16/9&1024/841&1024/841.
\end{array}
$$
Jensen for the full original atom probabilities and the central term of \(H_p\) give \(\widehat b=b(p)\ge\sum_xp_x^3\ge|T_m|^{-2}\). The uniform row-price conclusion of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 applies to the same original weights. Therefore
$$
\log(4^m\widehat b)\ge\delta,\qquad \widehat\nu\le9
\quad(1\le m\le6).
\tag{12}
$$

We next establish the actual product estimate for any choices of the restricted rows (8) in the factors. For a supported target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), use \(\widehat R_J=\prod_\ell\widehat R_{\ell,j_\ell}\), the product row law \(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_\ell\widehat\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell}\), and the score \(\widehat Y_J(X)=\sum_\ell\widehat Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(X_\ell)\). These rows are symmetric about their centers, so each score has mean zero. Direct factorization and independence yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_P(J)&:=\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}
=\prod_\ell\widehat H_\ell(j_\ell),\\
P_X&=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{\widehat Y_J(X)},\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J^2
=\sigma_J^2:=\sum_\ell\widehat V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
The first quantity in (13) is restricted and is not asserted to equal the full canonical \(H_P(J)\). The likelihood identity uses exactly the original probabilities in (1).

Every original ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each distinct reflected pair in \(\widehat R_J\), as well as the central atom. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|\widehat Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
The factor one half counts each distinct orbit once and uses only half the available central mass. The last inequality is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking the infimum over the containing closed halfspaces, squaring, and averaging under the original \(P\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
\mathbb E_{\bigotimes_\ell\widehat q_\ell}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
Indeed \(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell)\prod_\ell\widehat q_{\ell,j_\ell}\), and under this auxiliary product center law, \(\mathbb E\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell\). These are exact identities, and another Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz give the second line. This proves (15) without changing the original averaging law or any original atom.

It remains to choose the restricted rows. Let \(s\) be the number of small-width factors \(m_\ell\le6\). In those factors keep every row, as in (12). For all remaining factors put \(K_\ell=2^{m_\ell}\), retain their independent coordinate parameters \(t_{\ell,h}\), and partition their coordinates into the bands
$$
\mathcal B_k=\lbrace(\ell,h):m_\ell\ge7,
\ 2^{-k-1}<t_{\ell,h}\le2^{-k}\rbrace\qquad(k\ge0).
$$
Define
$$
\beta_k=\sum_{(\ell,h)\in\mathcal B_k}
\frac{h_{m_\ell,h}}{K_\ell}t_{\ell,h},\qquad
A_k=1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2,\qquad D=6144.
\tag{16}
$$
Only finitely many bands are occupied. Select a band precisely when \(\beta_k\ge D A_k\), and in each large-width factor keep the full reflected row precisely when its source coordinate belongs to a selected band. This specifies the coordinate selections \(\mathcal S_\ell\) used in (11). A band is an index set of auxiliary rows across factors; it is not assumed to be a product factor of the original support.

In a band, \(t_{\ell,h}\le2^{-k}\) and \(\log^2(1/t_{\ell,h})\le A_k\). Summing (11)--(12) therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_\ell\log(4^{m_\ell}\widehat b_\ell)
&\ge\delta s+\frac18\sum_{k\text{ selected}}\beta_k
-24\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k,\\
\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell
&\le9s+24\sum_{k\text{ selected}}A_k\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
For nonnegative numbers the square root of their sum is at most the sum of their square roots. Applying this in (15) and (17) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(4^NM_2(P))
\ge{}&-\log4+\delta s-6\sqrt s\\
&+\sum_{k\text{ selected}}
\left(\frac{\beta_k}8-2\sqrt{24A_k\beta_k}\right)
-24\sum_{k\text{ unselected}}2^{-k}\beta_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
If \(k\) is selected, \(\beta_k\ge6144A_k\), which is exactly sufficient for \(\beta_k/8\ge2\sqrt{24A_k\beta_k}\). Every selected summand in (18) is consequently nonnegative. If it is unselected, \(\beta_k<6144A_k\). Also
\(\delta s-6\sqrt s=\delta(\sqrt s-3/\delta)^2-9/\delta\ge-9/\delta\). Hence
$$
\log(4^NM_2(P))
\ge-\log4-\frac9\delta
-147456\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}
\left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right).
\tag{19}
$$
The geometric-series identities \(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=2\), \(\sum_{k\ge0}k2^{-k}=2\), and \(\sum_{k\ge0}k^22^{-k}=6\), obtained by differentiating \(\sum_{k\ge0}z^k=(1-z)^{-1}\) for \(|z|<1\), give \(\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^22^{-k}=12\). Thus (19) is the second-moment assertion in (4) with exactly (3). Only finitely many bands enter the actual row construction; the infinite series bounds their total possible loss.

Finally Jensen under the original law \(P\) gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the third-moment assertion. The halfspace argument includes singleton restricted rows, zero score variances, and all ambient dimensions without a spanning assumption. The value \(t_{\ell,h}=1\), corresponding to \(\alpha_{\ell,h}=0\), lies in the zeroth band and has zero score, so it is covered directly. Every finite \(\alpha_{\ell,h}\) gives \(t_{\ell,h}>0\), with no lower bound used anywhere. Completeness of the block supports is their defining equality for \(T_m\), and Cartesian products preserve that equality. This establishes all assertions.
