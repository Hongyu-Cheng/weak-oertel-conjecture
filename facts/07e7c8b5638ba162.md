## Statement

Assume there exists one absolute constant \(a>0\) with the following property. For every integer \(N\ge1\), every compact convex \(Q\subset\mathbb R^N\) with nonempty \(\mathcal D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\), every \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), and every \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), put \(v_{\mathbf z}=\exp(\beta+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z)\) for \(\mathbf z\in\mathcal D\), \(V=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal D}v_{\mathbf z}\), and

\[
\tau_v(\mathbf z)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal D\cap H}v_{\mathbf x}.
\]

The assumed property is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathcal D}v_{\mathbf z}\tau_v(\mathbf z)
\ge \frac{a}{2^N}V^2.
\tag{1}
\]

No full-dimensionality condition is imposed on \(Q\) or \(\mathcal D\).

For an integer \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \((\operatorname{conv}D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), and let \(w_{\mathbf I}>0\) on \(D\), with zero table entries off \(D\). Retain the notation of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc:
\(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\),
\(w_{\max}=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\),
\(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\),
\(B=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\phi_{\mathbf I}\), and
\(P=\operatorname{conv}D\).
Assume full convex-admissibility: for every \(\mathbf J\in D\) and every coefficient vector \((\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) with nonnegative entries, sum one, and \(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\), one has \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\).

For each \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) be the nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and let \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}>0\) be their unique barycentric coefficients. As in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf J},&
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)&=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:G_E\le L\},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\min_{\substack{F\subseteq D\\
F\cap E\ne\varnothing\ \text{for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I},&
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Then (1) implies

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\ge\frac{a}{2^{n+2}}W.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, for every \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and every convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) with \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\), there is one \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge \frac{c_0}{2^n}\mathcal H_d(S),
\qquad
c_0=\min\left\{\frac a{24},\frac1{2e}\right\}\in(0,1/e).
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\). The table in this last assertion is the actual complete fiber table \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Write \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), with the infimum over closed ambient halfspaces. The constant is independent of \(n,d,C\). The implication from (3) to (4) uses the same-table root comparison in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc and the actual-body consumer in accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893.

## Proof

We first work with the positive table in (2). All weights throughout the epigraph construction remain in the original normalization. In particular we do not replace \(W\) by a probability total or by the weight of a subtable. The normalized potential has \(\min_D\phi=0\), but the density below includes \(w_{\max}\).

By the convex-roof conclusion of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc, the function

\[
\widehat\phi(x)=
\min_{\substack{\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\ \sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=x}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
\qquad(x\in P)
\tag{5}
\]

is attained, convex, and continuous on all of \(P\) in its relative topology. It satisfies \(0\le\widehat\phi\le B\) and \(\widehat\phi(\mathbf I)=\phi_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D\). The ambient integer dimension stays \(n\), even if \(P\) is a point or has intermediate rank.

Define the finite measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\), supported on the integer-base epigraph, by

\[
\mu(A)=
w_{\max}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
\int_{\phi_{\mathbf I}}^\infty
\mathbf 1_A(\mathbf I,t)e^{-t}\,dt.
\tag{6}
\]

Here \(A\) is a Borel set, and \(\mu(\mathbb R^{n+1})=W\). For any finite measure \(\nu\) used below, write
\(\tau_\nu(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\nu(H)\), where the infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces. Every such halfspace contains the parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(p\). Thus this depth is also the infimum over through-point halfspaces with nonzero normals. Adding the improper whole-space halfspace, if that convention is used, does not change the infimum.

We prove the consequence of (1) for (6) by discretizing only its last coordinate. Fix \(R>B\). Let \(\mu_R\) be the restriction of (6) to heights at most \(R\), and set
\(W_R=W-|D|w_{\max}e^{-R}>0\).
For every positive integer \(m\), consider the compact convex set

\[
Q_{R,m}=
\{(x,s)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
x\in P,\ m\widehat\phi(x)\le s\le mR\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Convexity follows from convexity of \(\widehat\phi\), and compactness follows from its continuity on compact \(P\). Its complete lattice support is exactly

\[
\mathcal D_{R,m}
=Q_{R,m}\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}
=\{(\mathbf I,k):
\mathbf I\in D,\ k\in\mathbb Z,\ \phi_{\mathbf I}\le k/m\le R\}.
\tag{8}
\]

The equality uses \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), so it does not omit any forced integer site. This support is nonempty: a site with potential zero gives \((\mathbf I,0)\in\mathcal D_{R,m}\). On it assign the positive affine exponential weights
\(v_{\mathbf I,k}=(w_{\max}/m)e^{-k/m}\).
Their logarithm is the affine function \(\log w_{\max}-\log m-k/m\).

The invertible linear map \((x,s)\mapsto(x,s/m)\) sends their atomic measure to

\[
\mu_{R,m}
=\frac{w_{\max}}m
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\ \phi_{\mathbf I}\le k/m\le R}}
e^{-k/m}\,\delta_{(\mathbf I,k/m)}.
\tag{9}
\]

An invertible linear map carries containing closed halfspaces bijectively to containing closed halfspaces and preserves each atomic weight. Applying (1) in the integer dimension \(N=n+1\) therefore gives, with \(W_{R,m}=\mu_{R,m}(\mathbb R^{n+1})\),

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}\,d\mu_{R,m}
\ge \frac{a}{2^{n+1}}W_{R,m}^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This use of (1) is permitted even when (7) has affine dimension less than \(n+1\). No affine lattice chart, lattice-index factor, or transformation of the original base lattice is involved.

We next justify a uniform approximation over all closed halfspaces, including those whose boundary contains whole vertical fibers. For every closed interval \([u,v]\subset[0,R]\),

\[
\left|
\frac1m\sum_{k/m\in[u,v]}e^{-k/m}
-\int_u^v e^{-t}\,dt
\right|\le\frac3m.
\tag{11}
\]

To prove this, set \(\delta=1/m\). If the interval contains no mesh point, then \(v-u<\delta\) and its integral is at most \(\delta\). Otherwise write \(r=\lceil mu\rceil\), \(s=\lfloor mv\rfloor\), and compare the sum with the integral on \([r\delta,(s+1)\delta]\). On every mesh cell, monotonicity gives

\[
0\le
\delta e^{-k\delta}
-\int_{k\delta}^{(k+1)\delta}e^{-t}\,dt
\le
\delta\bigl(e^{-k\delta}-e^{-(k+1)\delta}\bigr).
\]

The sum of these errors is at most \(\delta\) by telescoping. The union of the cells differs from \([u,v]\) by endpoint pieces of total length at most \(2\delta\); their integrals have absolute difference at most \(2\delta\), because \(e^{-t}\le1\) for \(t\ge0\). This proves (11), also for a degenerate interval and for mesh points exactly on its endpoints.

The intersection of a closed halfspace with \(\{\mathbf I\}\times[\phi_{\mathbf I},R]\) is empty or a closed interval in that fiber. When the normal has last coordinate zero, the intersection is the entire interval or is empty, with the entire interval included on equality. Thus (11), summed over the original finite set \(D\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{H\text{ closed halfspace}}
|\mu_{R,m}(H)-\mu_R(H)|
&\le\varepsilon_m,
&\varepsilon_m&=\frac{3|D|w_{\max}}m,\\
|W_{R,m}-W_R|&\le\varepsilon_m,
&\sup_{p\in\mathbb R^{n+1}}
|\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}(p)-\tau_{\mu_R}(p)|
&\le\varepsilon_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The total-mass estimate follows by applying (11) to every full fiber interval. The depth estimate follows by taking infima over the same family of halfspaces containing each fixed \(p\). In particular no continuity in the normal, or deletion of boundary mass, is assumed.

To pass the depth averages to the limit, fix \(\mathbf J\in D\). For each fixed nonzero normal \((\mathbf b,c)\), the through-point halfspace at height \(t\) has the form
\(\{(x,s):\mathbf b\cdot(x-\mathbf J)+c(s-t)\ge0\}\).
If \(c=0\), its \(\mu_R\)-mass is independent of \(t\). If \(c\ne0\), its cutoff on the fiber at \(\mathbf I\) is \(t-\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)/c\), with the inequality direction determined by the sign of \(c\). Replacing \(t\) by \(t'\) shifts every cutoff by \(t'-t\). Its symmetric difference inside each fiber interval has length at most \(|t'-t|\), and its density is at most \(w_{\max}\). Hence the mass function for every normal is Lipschitz with the same constant \(|D|w_{\max}\). Taking the infimum over all these normals proves

\[
|\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t)-\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t')|
\le |D|w_{\max}|t-t'|.
\tag{13}
\]

In particular this depth is measurable on every supporting fiber. It is bounded between zero and \(W_R\le W\). Consequently
\(t\mapsto e^{-t}\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t)\)
is Lipschitz on \([\phi_{\mathbf J},R]\), with Lipschitz constant at most \(W+|D|w_{\max}\). Its mesh sums with both endpoints included converge to its integral: on complete mesh cells the error is bounded by the Lipschitz constant times the cell length squared, and the at most two endpoint pieces contribute a bound tending to zero. Since \(D\) is finite, (9) therefore gives

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_R}\,d\mu_{R,m}
\longrightarrow
\int\tau_{\mu_R}\,d\mu_R.
\tag{14}
\]

By (12), the difference between the left side here and
\(\int\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}\,d\mu_{R,m}\)
is at most \(\varepsilon_m W_{R,m}\), which tends to zero. Also \(W_{R,m}\to W_R\). Letting \(m\to\infty\) in (10) proves

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_R}\,d\mu_R
\ge\frac{a}{2^{n+1}}W_R^2.
\tag{15}
\]

The removal of the top cutoff is uniform over halfspaces as well. Set
\(\eta_R=|D|w_{\max}e^{-R}\).
The nonnegative measure \(\mu-\mu_R\) has total mass exactly \(\eta_R\), so for every halfspace \(H\) and every point \(p\),

\[
0\le\mu(H)-\mu_R(H)\le\eta_R,
\qquad
0\le\tau_\mu(p)-\tau_{\mu_R}(p)\le\eta_R.
\tag{16}
\]

In particular \(\tau_\mu\) is measurable on each supporting fiber as a uniform limit of the measurable functions in (13). It is bounded by \(W\). Using both inequalities in (16) yields

\[
0\le
\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu-\int\tau_{\mu_R}\,d\mu_R
\le\eta_RW_R+W\eta_R
\le2W\eta_R.
\tag{17}
\]

Letting \(R\to\infty\) in (15), using \(W_R\to W\) and (17), proves

\[
\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu
\ge\frac{a}{2^{n+1}}W^2.
\tag{18}
\]

We now compare the epigraph depth to the logarithmic integral hitting mass. We prove the precise one-sided comparison

\[
\tau_\mu(\mathbf J,\phi_{\mathbf J}+L)
\le m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
\qquad(\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0).
\tag{19}
\]

This argument verifies directly that the closed lower cuts associated with the definition in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc control the full epigraph fibers.

Fix \(\mathbf J,L\). There is a minimum hitting set \(F\) in (2), since the collection of subsets of \(D\) is finite and \(D\) is feasible. Put \(U=D\setminus F\) and \(p=(\mathbf J,\phi_{\mathbf J}+L)\). If \(U\) is empty, then \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)=W\), whereas every halfspace has \(\mu\)-mass at most \(W\), proving (19).

Suppose \(U\ne\varnothing\). No representation of \(\mathbf J\) by a convex combination of sites in \(U\) can have mean potential at most \(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L\). Indeed, if such a representation existed, minimize its linear mean-potential objective over all coefficient vectors on \(U\) with barycenter \(\mathbf J\). This is a nonempty compact coefficient polytope. Choose an optimizer with the smallest positive support \(E\). If \(E\) were affinely dependent, a nonzero dependence vector on \(E\) would preserve both the coefficient sum and the barycenter under sufficiently small perturbations of either sign. Optimality of the linear objective forces its change in that direction to be zero. Moving in one direction until a coefficient first vanishes then preserves the objective and reduces the positive support, a contradiction. Hence \(E\) is affinely independent. Its positive coefficients show \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and its mean potential gives \(G_E\le L\). This produces a member of \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\) contained in \(U\), contradicting that \(F\) hits every such member. This argument includes representations on the boundary, because zero coefficients are omitted from the positive support.

Consider the nonempty convex set

\[
K_F=
\operatorname{conv}\{(\mathbf I,\phi_{\mathbf I}):\mathbf I\in U\}
+\{(\mathbf0,s):s\ge0\}.
\tag{20}
\]

It is closed: in a convergent sequence from this sum, the compact convex-hull summands have a convergent subsequence, and the corresponding ray summands then converge in the closed ray. Membership of \(p\) in \(K_F\) would be exactly a retained barycentric representation with mean potential at most \(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L\), which was just excluded. Thus \(p\notin K_F\).

For completeness, let \(p_0\in K_F\) minimize distance to \(p\). Such a point exists because a minimizing sequence is bounded and \(K_F\) is closed. The nonzero vector \(u=p-p_0\) satisfies \(u\cdot(q-p_0)\le0\) for every \(q\in K_F\): differentiate the squared distance along the segment from \(p_0\) to \(q\). It follows that
\(u\cdot(q-p)\le-\|u\|^2<0\).
Since \(K_F\) contains every upward ray from each of its points, the last coordinate of \(u\) is nonpositive. Write \(u=(\mathbf b,-c)\) with \(c\ge0\). The proper closed halfspace

\[
H=\{(x,t):
\mathbf b\cdot(x-\mathbf J)+c(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-t)\ge0\}
\tag{21}
\]

passes through \(p\) and strictly excludes every point of \(K_F\). In particular it excludes every entire retained epigraph fiber
\(\{(\mathbf I,t):t\ge\phi_{\mathbf I}\}\), \(\mathbf I\in U\).
Equivalently, its score is strictly negative at each retained graph site and weakly decreases as height increases. Each deleted fiber has total \(\mu\)-mass \(w_{\mathbf I}\), so
\(\mu(H)\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}=m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\).
This proves (19). If \(c=0\), the separating halfspace is vertical and its selected entire fibers are still all deleted. Any equality fibers in this case, and any equality graph sites when \(c>0\), are on the deleted side; no retained boundary mass has been ignored. Equality in \(G_E\le L\) was included throughout the support-reduction argument.

The logarithmic cutoff families are finite, so \(L\mapsto m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) is a measurable step function. The maxima in (2) are attained as in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc: a cutoff may be moved down to the largest support cost it includes, with unchanged family and no larger denominator. The singleton at \(\mathbf J\) has cost zero, so there is always such a support cost. In particular (2) gives the pointwise bound
\(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\le(1+L)\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\).
Changing variables \(t=\phi_{\mathbf J}+L\) in (6), so that \(w_{\max}e^{-t}=w_{\mathbf J}e^{-L}\), and applying (19), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu
&=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}
\int_0^\infty e^{-L}
\tau_\mu(\mathbf J,\phi_{\mathbf J}+L)\,dL\\
&\le\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}
\int_0^\infty e^{-L}m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\,dL\\
&\le W\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)\,dL
=2W\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

The last integral is two by direct integration, including one integration by parts for its \(L\) term. Combining (18) and (22), and dividing by \(2W>0\), proves (3).

Finally let \(C\) be a body in the locked problem. If \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)=0\), any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\) satisfies (4), since every halfspace mass is nonnegative. Suppose \(W>0\), and take its actual fiber table, with \(D\) consisting precisely of the positive-volume fibers. Accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc gives global extendability of this complete table, including its zero-volume and boundary fibers, and consequently the lattice-convex positive support and fully convex-admissible potential used above. Thus (3) applies with this unchanged original \(W\) and integer dimension \(n\).

The same accepted fact supplies the pointwise root comparison
\(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\)
at every center and cutoff, with identical capacities, and hence
\(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\).
For clarity, \(m_d\) has exactly the hitting definition (2) with the root cost
\(L_E=\log\!\bigl(w_{\mathbf J}/(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d})^d\bigr)\)
in place of \(G_E\). The symbol \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}\) is the optimized integral hitting score of accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893, not its fractional score. The global conclusion of that fact, including attainment of the maximum over actual mixed points, gives

\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)
\ge\frac16\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\ge\frac16\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\ge\frac{a}{24}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{23}
\]

Choose one attaining point. This choice precedes every containing closed halfspace in the definition of its depth. Decreasing the coefficient to \(c_0\) in (4) proves the locked quantifier order and the required strict range for the absolute constant. Neither zero-volume fibers nor affine rank has introduced a new normalization, an index factor, or any dependence on \(d\). All conclusions above are conditional on (1).
