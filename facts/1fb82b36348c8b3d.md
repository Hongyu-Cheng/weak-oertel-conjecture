## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, nonempty, and complete, meaning \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(p_{\mathbf I}>0\) on \(D\) have total mass one and zero values off \(D\). Assume the finite-valued globally log-concave extension, including agreement at every lattice zero, of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Retain the canonical normalization \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=p_{\max}\), \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\), \(B=\max_D\phi\), and \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\).

Use exactly the original arrays and entropies of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(p)&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\ (\mathbf I\in D),\
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\
(\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\\
H(r)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\qquad 0\log0=0,\\
S(q)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose there is a nonempty set \(\mathcal J_0\subseteq D\), with \(K_0=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal J_0\), satisfying \(K_0\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J_0\), and every member of \(\mathcal J_0\) is a vertex of \(K_0\). Suppose also that \(\phi\) is affine on \(\mathcal J_0\): there are \(\boldsymbol u\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R\) with \(\phi_{\mathbf J}=\beta+\boldsymbol u\cdot\mathbf J\) for all \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_0\).

Let \(\mathscr A\) be a set of distinct ordered pairs \((\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\) of different members of \(\mathcal J_0\), such that the reflected site \(\mathbf I_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}=2\mathbf J-\mathbf K\) belongs to \(D\setminus\mathcal J_0\). Suppose positive numbers \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\), indexed by \(\mathscr A\), satisfy the following original-capacity conditions:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A\\
2\mathbf J-\mathbf K=\mathbf I}}a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
&=p_{\mathbf I}
&&(\mathbf I\in D\setminus\mathcal J_0),\\
t_{\mathbf K}:=\sum_{\mathbf J:(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
&=\sum_{\mathbf L:(\mathbf L,\mathbf K)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf L\mathbf K}
\le p_{\mathbf K}
&&(\mathbf K\in\mathcal J_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Empty sums are zero. The first equation accounts for every external source with its full original column mass. The second is a balanced directed circulation, together with the original target-source capacities. Distinct pairs can have the same external site.

Then there is a finite globally strongly convex function \(F\) on \(\mathbb R^n\) for which \(F(\mathbf J)=0\) on \(\mathcal J_0\), \(F(\mathbf I)>0\) on \(D\setminus\mathcal J_0\), and the entire minimizing face of \(V_F(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}F(\mathbf J)\) is exactly
\(\{r\in\mathcal F(p):q_{\mathbf I}=0\text{ for }\mathbf I\notin\mathcal J_0\}\).
If \(r_F\) is the unique maximizer of \(H\) on this entire face and \(q_F\) its target law, then

\[
0\le G_F:=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{F,\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J})
\le H(r_F)
\le n\log2-S(q_F)+H(r_F).
\]

Thus this class satisfies the registered selector inequality with the absolute coefficient one.

The class includes complete globally extendable tables with \(G_F\) unbounded as \(n\) grows and with shared original target-source columns. Explicitly, for \(n\ge2\), put \(b_n=2^{-1-1/n}\), \(a_n=1/2-b_n\), \(D=\{-1,0,1,2\}^n\), \(\mathcal J_0=\{0,1\}^n\), and
\(p_{\mathbf I}=a_n^{h(\mathbf I)}b_n^{n-h(\mathbf I)}\), where \(h(\mathbf I)\) counts coordinates equal to \(-1\) or \(2\). For the strictly convex cost \(F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-1/2)^2-n/4\), the full minimizing face is the face above. Its entropy-selected array has \(S(q_F)=n\log2\) and
\(G_F=2na_n\log(b_n/a_n)\longrightarrow\infty\), while \(H(r_F)\ge G_F\).

## Proof

First construct a feasible array \(\overline r\) using all the specified original capacities. Set \(\overline r_{\mathbf K,\mathbf K}=p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K}\) for \(\mathbf K\in\mathcal J_0\). For every \((\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A\), set
\(\overline r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf K}=a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\) and add \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\) to \(\overline r_{\mathbf J,\,2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}\). All other entries are zero.

These instructions do not collide within the target-source part because the ordered pairs are distinct and have different endpoints. Within the external-source part, a given source and destination determine the predecessor uniquely: \(\mathbf K=2\mathbf J-\mathbf I\). Thus each positive external entry also corresponds to exactly one arrow, although its source column may occur in several rows.

The source column at \(\mathbf K\in\mathcal J_0\) has sum \(p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K}+t_{\mathbf K}=p_{\mathbf K}\). Each external column has sum \(p_{\mathbf I}\) by its prescribed equality. In row \(\mathbf J\), the arrow from \(\mathbf K\) contributes two equal entries whose total moment is
\(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}[(\mathbf K-\mathbf J)+(2\mathbf J-\mathbf K-\mathbf J)]=\mathbf0\). The diagonal entry contributes zero moment. Hence every row moment equation holds. Nonnegativity follows from \(t_{\mathbf K}\le p_{\mathbf K}\). This proves \(\overline r\in\mathcal F(p)\), supported on target rows \(\mathcal J_0\), without treating shared columns as independent capacities. Its row masses are \(\overline q_{\mathbf J}=p_{\mathbf J}-t_{\mathbf J}+2t_{\mathbf J}=p_{\mathbf J}+t_{\mathbf J}\).

We next expose the whole face of arrays supported on those target rows. Apply the finite strict Jensen criterion of accepted fact c300e9d112cefeb0 to the complete lattice set \(\mathcal J_0\). Since each site is a vertex of its convex hull, no site has a nontrivial positive barycentric representation by sites of \(\mathcal J_0\). Consequently the vector assigning zero to every member of \(\mathcal J_0\) lies in that fact's strict Jensen cone, with no nonsingleton tests. Its exact extension theorem supplies a finite globally strongly convex function \(g\) on \(\mathbb R^n\) with \(g(\mathbf J)=0\) for every \(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_0\). This assertion includes all affine ranks.

Let \(d_0(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,K_0)\). It is a finite convex function: for nearest points \(z,z'\in K_0\) to \(x,x'\), convexity of \(K_0\) and the triangle inequality give
\(d_0((1-s)x+sx')\le(1-s)d_0(x)+sd_0(x')\) for \(0\le s\le1\). At sites of \(\mathcal J_0\), \(d_0=0\); at every site of \(D\setminus\mathcal J_0\), \(d_0>0\), by the complete lattice intersection of \(K_0\).

If \(D\setminus\mathcal J_0\) is empty, set \(F=g\). Otherwise choose a positive \(\varepsilon\) sufficiently small that \(d_0(\mathbf I)+\varepsilon g(\mathbf I)>0\) at each of the finitely many external sites; this is possible because all their distances are positive and all their \(g\)-values are finite. Set \(F=d_0+\varepsilon g\). A convex function plus a positive multiple of a globally strongly convex function is globally strongly convex. This is an exact cost extension with values zero on \(\mathcal J_0\) and positive on every other site of \(D\).

Every feasible array therefore has \(V_F(r)\ge0\), and equality holds precisely when all its rows outside \(\mathcal J_0\) vanish. The feasible array \(\overline r\) attains equality. This proves the asserted characterization of the entire minimizing face. This construction uses the accepted strict cone and its exact extension, rather than an assumed extension of arbitrary finite costs.

The face is nonempty, compact, and convex. Its entropy has a unique maximizer by the strict concavity argument for the canonical conditional entropy in accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc, or directly because each summand \(-s\log(s/p_{\mathbf I})\) is continuous and strictly concave on \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). In particular \(H(r_F)\ge H(\overline r)\).

The original energy is constant on this entire face. Indeed, summing the canonical row moment equations gives \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J=\boldsymbol\mu\), where \(\boldsymbol\mu=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\). For every member of the face the target law is supported on \(\mathcal J_0\), so the affine target-potential hypothesis yields
\(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\phi_{\mathbf J}=\beta+\boldsymbol u\cdot\boldsymbol\mu\). The source expectation \(\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\) is fixed. Hence \(G_F\) equals the energy of \(\overline r\), including all members of the tied face with other target masses.

For \(\overline r\), each arrow contributes
\(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}(\phi_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}+\phi_{\mathbf K}-2\phi_{\mathbf J})\) to the energy, and diagonal entries contribute zero. The normalized potential of 488f0a583886b5fc makes this contribution exactly
\(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log[p_{\mathbf J}^2/(p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}p_{\mathbf K})]\). Convex-admissibility of that potential makes every such contribution nonnegative.

The circulation balances every function of the target sites, in particular their logarithmic original masses:
\(\sum_{\mathscr A}a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}
=\sum_{\mathscr A}a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf K}\).
Consequently the original energy has the exact expression

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_F
&=\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
\log\frac{p_{\mathbf J}^2}
{p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}p_{\mathbf K}}\\
&=\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
\log\frac{p_{\mathbf K}}{p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}}\\
&\le\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
\log\frac{p_{\mathbf K}}{a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last inequality uses \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\le p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}\), which follows from the complete external-column equality and positivity of its summands. Each summand on the last line is a conditional assignment entropy contribution in an original source column \(\mathbf K\in\mathcal J_0\). The remaining diagonal contribution is
\((p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K})\log[p_{\mathbf K}/(p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K})]\ge0\), interpreted as zero if \(p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K}=0\). Every external column also has nonnegative conditional entropy. Thus the last line is at most \(H(\overline r)\), and \(G_F\le H(\overline r)\le H(r_F)\).

It remains to include the target entropy rather than silently identify it with assignment entropy. Distinct members of \(\mathcal J_0\) cannot have the same parity: their midpoint would be an integer point of \(K_0\), hence a member of \(\mathcal J_0\), but would not be a vertex. Therefore \(|\mathcal J_0|\le2^n\). Since \(q_F\) is supported there, \(S(q_F)\le\log|\mathcal J_0|\le n\log2\). This proves the last inequality and the coefficient-one conclusion. If there are no external sources, the circulation is empty and \(G_F=0\), so the same argument covers that case.

For the scalable example, \(a_n>0\), \(b_n>a_n\), and \(2a_n+2b_n=1\). The displayed table is a product probability law on the complete box \(D=[-1,2]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Let \(L_n=\log(b_n/a_n)>0\). The finite convex function
\(\Phi(x)=L_n\sum_{i=1}^n\max\{-x_i,0,x_i-1\}\) equals \(h(\mathbf I)L_n\) at every site of \(D\). Thus \(b_n^n e^{-\Phi}\) on \([-1,2]^n\), extended by zero outside this box, is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with the entire original table and every lattice zero, exactly in the convention of 488f0a583886b5fc. The canonical potential is \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=h(\mathbf I)L_n\), and is zero, hence affine, on \(\mathcal J_0\).

Every external site has a unique representation \(2\mathbf J-\mathbf K\) with \(\mathbf K,\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\) distinct. Coordinatewise, values \(-1,0,1,2\) correspond respectively to pairs \((K_i,J_i)=(1,0),(0,0),(1,1),(0,1)\). Use all ordered pairs of different binary vectors as arrows and set \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}=p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}\). For a fixed \(\mathbf K\), its total outgoing mass is \((a_n+b_n)^n-b_n^n=2^{-n}-b_n^n=b_n^n\), since \(b_n^n=2^{-n-1}\). The same coordinatewise sum gives the incoming mass \(b_n^n\). This is exactly its original capacity \(p_{\mathbf K}\). All external columns are accounted for once, and every core source is shared across all \(2^n-1\) other target rows in the explicit array.

For the stated quadratic \(F\), the value at every binary site is zero and the value at an external site is \(2h(\mathbf I)>0\). The explicit feasible array therefore proves that its entire minimizing face is exactly all feasible arrays supported on \(\mathcal J_0\), as before. The source law, feasible constraints, face, and entropy are invariant under independently reflecting any coordinate by \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\). Uniqueness of the entropy maximizer implies invariance of \(r_F\) and its target law. These reflections act transitively on \(\{0,1\}^n\), so \(q_F\) is uniform there and \(S(q_F)=n\log2\).

Since the potential vanishes on every allowed target, \(G_F=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\). In each source coordinate the probability of an exterior value is \(2a_n\). Therefore \(G_F=2na_n L_n\). The general circulation argument gives \(H(r_F)\ge G_F\). In fact its explicit comparison array has \(H(\overline r)=G_F\): every external column is deterministic, every core diagonal residual is zero, and its arrow entropy is
\(p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}\log[b_n^n/p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}]\).

Finally, put \(\delta_n=(\log2)/n\). Then \(2na_n=n(1-e^{-\delta_n})\ge(\log2)/2\), because \(0<\delta_n<1\) and \(1-e^{-s}\ge s-s^2/2\ge s/2\) on \([0,1]\). Also \(b_n/a_n=e^{-\delta_n}/(1-e^{-\delta_n})\ge n/(2\log2)\), using \(e^{-s}\ge1-s\), \(1-e^{-s}\le s\), and \(\delta_n<1/2\). Hence
\(G_F\ge(\log2/2)\log[n/(2\log2)]\to\infty\). This verifies that the entropy payment is not merely a bounded-energy calibration.
