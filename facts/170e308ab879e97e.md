## Statement

For positive data $k,Q_i,\sigma_i$, $1\le i\le3$, let $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_i M)$, $P=f_1f_2f_3$, and $\Psi=\sum_i f_i^2+P$. At a positive boundary point $\Psi=4$, write $D_i=2f_i^2+P$, $C=8+P$ and $T_i=\sqrt{k/Q_i}$. For a unit gain in $k$ and in the two $Q_i$ with $i\ne s$, holding $Q_s$ and all $\sigma_i$ fixed, its derivative $\beta_s$ satisfies
$$
2k\beta_s=C+\sum_{i\ne s}(D_iT_i^2-Cf_iT_i)\le-\frac{317}{882}
$$
whenever all three $Q_i/k\le128/279$.

Start with any such positive data with $\Psi\le4$ and let nonnegative $E_i,G_i$ denote unique-sector losses and double-sector gains, the gain $G_i$ being omitted by cap $i$. Put $E=\sum_i E_i$, $G=\sum_i G_i$, and change the data with $\sigma_i$ fixed along
$$
k(t)=k+t(G-E),\qquad Q_i(t)=Q_i+t(G-G_i-E_i),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
$$
If the final data are positive and all three cap fractions at both endpoints are at most $128/279$, then the final $\Psi\le4$, strictly if any $E_i$ or $G_i$ is positive.

## Proof

Logarithmic differentiation gives $d\log f_i=dk/(2k)+dQ_i/(2Q_i)-dM/M$ with fixed $\sigma_i$, and $f_i\partial_{f_i}\Psi=D_i$. At $\Psi=4$ their sum is $C=8+P$. A unit double gain omitted by cap $s$ has $dk=1$ and $dQ_i=\mathbf1_{\{i\ne s\}}$. Using $1/(\sigma_i M)=f_iT_i/(2k)$ gives the stated derivative formula.

We bound that expression at a boundary point with all cap fractions in the band. All $f_i$ are in $(0,2)$. Since $(2+f_i) f_j f_l\le f_j^2+f_l^2+P=4-f_i^2$ for the other indices $j,l$, one has $f_jf_l\le2-f_i$. Then $(f_j+f_l)^2=4-f_i^2+(2-f_i)f_jf_l\le4(2-f_i)$. Thus $F=\sum_i f_i\le3$ by $f_i+2\sqrt{2-f_i}\le3$. The same three inequalities are $(f_i-(F-2))^2\le4(3-F)$. The identity $\sum_i f_i\sqrt{Q_i/k}=2$ gives $F\ge2/\sqrt{128/279}>59/20$. Writing $3-F=\delta\in[0,1/20)$ gives
$$
1/2<19/20-1/\sqrt5<f_i<1+1/\sqrt5<3/2.
$$
Let $h=\sqrt{128/279}$, so $2/3<h<17/25<7/10$. The same moment identity gives $2\le f_i\sqrt{Q_i/k}+(3-f_i)h$, so $\sqrt{Q_i/k}\ge h-(3h-2)/f_i>4-5h>3/5$. Therefore $10/7<T_i<5/3$. Finally $3P^{2/3}+P\le4$ gives $P\le1$ and $8<C\le9$.

Fix $s$ and put $x=f_s$, $U=f_j+f_l=F-x$ and $D=f_j-f_l$ for the two captured indices. Then $29/20<U<5/2$, $|D|<1$, $D_j+D_l=2(4-x^2)$ and $D_j-D_l=2UD$. The derivative expression is separately convex in $T_j,T_l$, so it is bounded by the largest value at the four corners of their closed bounding rectangle. At equal endpoints $a$ it is $C(1-aU)+2a^2(4-x^2)$. Since $1-aU<0$, use $C\ge8$ and $U\ge59/20-x$. Completing the square in $x$ gives the upper bound $8[2-(59/20)a+a^2]$, equal to $-68/49$ at $a=10/7$ and $-10/9$ at $a=5/3$. For the mixed corners put $a=65/42$ and $b=5/42$. Expanding at $T_j=a\pm b$, $T_l=a\mp b$ gives
$$
C(1-aU)+2(a^2+b^2)(4-x^2)\ \pm b D(4aU-C).
$$
The bounds on $U,C$ give $-1/42<4aU-C<10a-8$ and $10a-8>1/42$. The same square bound on the terms with $b=0$, and the upper bound on the remaining terms, give $8[2-(59/20)a+a^2]+8b^2+b(10a-8)=-317/882$. All four upper bounds give the claimed derivative inequality.

For the finite change every intermediate data entry is positive by affine interpolation. Each cap fraction $Q_i(t)/k(t)$ is fractional linear with derivative of constant sign, so it stays in the band. At any $\Psi=4$ on this path, 49a3b81814664012 applies to these same positive raw variables and gives strictly negative derivative for a unit unique loss. The unit double gains just proved also give strictly negative derivative at that boundary. By linearity the total derivative there is strictly negative if at least one coefficient is positive. An initial equality becomes strict for small positive time. A subsequent first contact from below would have nonnegative left derivative, including at the final time, a contradiction. With all coefficients zero the data are fixed.
