## Statement

Under the hypotheses of the symmetric-base pyramid centroidal section classification, choose finitely many inward unit normals $u_i$ to through-zero sections whose Euclidean area centroid is zero. Let $S_i$ be the section areas. If $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\theta_i=1$, and $\sum_i\theta_iS_i u_i=0$, then
$$
\sum_i\theta_i\,|K\cap\{x:u_i\cdot x\ge0\}|=\frac{|K|}{2}.
$$
The apex-facing and opposite horizontal cap fractions are $[H/(H+1)]^d$ and its complement. At $H=d-1$ each vertical cap has fraction $1/2$, and the vector balance splits into the line of horizontal-section normals and the subspace of vertical-section normals. In particular the mean fraction strictly exceeds $(d/(d+1))^d$.

## Proof

By symmetric-base-pyramid-centroidal-section-classification, every selected section is horizontal or, only if $H=d-1$, vertical. The apex-facing horizontal cap is a homothetic copy of the whole pyramid from its apex with scale $H/(H+1)$; the opposite cap is its volume complement. The two horizontal orientations have the same section area. Every vertical cap has half the volume, since $(X,z)\mapsto(-X,z)$ preserves $K$ and exchanges its sides. The last component of the vector balance forces equal total weights on the two horizontal orientations, as the vertical-section normals have zero last component. The horizontal part therefore averages to half its total weight times $|K|$, and every vertical term is already $|K|/2$. The remaining components of the balance lie in the vertical-section normal subspace, giving the split. The strict comparison follows from $(1+1/d)^d>2$ for $d\ge2$ by the binomial theorem.
