## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge4\), put \(h=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), \(k=m+1\), \(N=k+1=m+2\), and \(\rho=32\). For \(x\in\mathbb R^m\), write \(|x|=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i\). Define the following exact polytopes and finite lattice supports:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_m&=\left\lbrace x\in[0,2]^m:
\sum_{i=1}^m\max(x_i-1,0)\le1,\quad h\le|x|\le h+1\right\rbrace,
&E_m&=R_m\cap\mathbb Z^m,\\
P_m&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((R_m\times\lbrace2\rbrace)\cup\lbrace(0,1)\rbrace\bigr),
&D&=P_m\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The full support is denoted \(D\), the canonical notation of both accepted predecessors; \(E_m\) denotes only its base after deleting the fixed last coordinate. The origin in the apex \((0,1)\) is the zero vector of \(\mathbb R^m\).

Then \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\), \(\operatorname{aff}(D)=\mathbb R^k\), and \(D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\). Every point has at most two coordinates equal to two, and \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\) is not contained in \(D\).

Use the original field and law, with exactly the definitions of accepted facts 4b70729b0d8aef8f and f27ee487069e27e7:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta&=(-\log\rho,\ldots,-\log\rho,0)\in\mathbb R^k,
&w_{(x,t)}&=\exp(-\beta\cdot(x,t))=\rho^{|x|},\\
Z&=\sum_{(x,t)\in D}w_{(x,t)},
&A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,
&p_{((x,t),0)}&=w_{(x,t)}/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
For \(u\in D\), abbreviate \(p_u=p_{(u,0)}\). The original depth and cubics are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{v\in A\cap H}p_v,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{u,v\in D\\u+v=2c}}\min(p_u,p_v),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum is over ordered pairs, with the diagonal once. Halfspaces can be required to have nonzero normal; allowing the whole space does not change these infima, since a halfspace defined by the extra zero coordinate contains the entire support.

The good block is the single coordinate \(t\), of activity one, and the bad block is \(F=\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\), all of whose activities are \(\rho>16\). Every double point has its two-indices in one coordinate of each block. The actual core \(D_1=\lbrace u\in D:\#\lbrace i:u_i=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace\) has original mass \(\alpha=p(D_1)\).

The actual bad one-two labels are precisely
$$
\mathcal Q_m=\left\lbrace q=(b,i):
b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\ b_i=0,
|b|\in\lbrace h-2,h-1\rbrace,\ 1\le i\le m\right\rbrace,
\qquad y_q=b+2e_i.
$$
For each such label define its actual good section and the original quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_q&=\lbrace t:(y_q,t)\in D\rbrace,
&Z_q&=\sum_{t\in S_q}1^t,
&B_0&=1+1=2,\\
\zeta_q&=Z_q/B_0,
&\lambda_q&=p(\lbrace(y_q,t):t\in S_q\rbrace),
&\xi_q&=\lambda_q\max\lbrace1,\sqrt{\zeta_q}\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
They satisfy \(S_q=\lbrace2\rbrace\), \(Z_q=1\), \(\zeta_q=1/2\), and \(\xi_q=\lambda_q=\rho^{|y_q|}/Z\). For all \(m\ge4\),
$$
\alpha\le\frac8{h+8},
\qquad
2^m\max_{q\in\mathcal Q_m}\xi_q\le\frac8{\sqrt m},
\qquad
\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_m}\lambda_q=1-\alpha.
$$
Consequently, for every fixed \(\delta>0\), all sufficiently large integers \(m\) have \(\alpha\le1/4\) and
$$
\sum_{\substack{q\in\mathcal Q_m\\\xi_q\le\delta2^{-|F|}}}\lambda_q
=1-\alpha\longrightarrow1.
$$
In particular there is no absolute \(\delta>0\) forcing this sum to be at most \(1/2\) under these hypotheses without Boolean containment.

This failure cannot be removed by an orthogonal field gauge: if two fields on this same \(D\) induce the same normalized law, the fields are equal.

For calibration on the same original law, let \(q_{E_m}\) be the number of occupied parity classes of \(E_m\), and let \(\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}=p(E_m\times\lbrace2\rbrace)\). Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{E_m}&=\sum_{j=h-2}^{h+1}\binom mj\le2^m,
&\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}&=(Z-1)/Z\longrightarrow1,\\
M_3(p),T(p)&\ge
\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}^4}{512q_{E_m}^3}
\ge\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}^4}{8}\,8^{-N}
\ge2^{-7}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first lower bound is the actual one-step theorem f27ee487069e27e7 transferred with the original slice mass to the fourth power. A direct original within-parity calculation gives the stronger bounds
$$
M_3(p)\ge8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge8\,8^{-N}.
$$

## Proof

First, \(R_m\) is an exact compact convex polytope. Indeed,
\(\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)=\max_{I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace}\sum_{i\in I}(x_i-1)\), so its defining condition is a finite family of linear inequalities with integer coefficients. The other constraints are also closed linear inequalities, and the box makes the set bounded.

For an integer \(x\in[0,2]^m\), the sum \(\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\) is exactly its number of twos. Therefore \(E_m\) consists precisely of the following disjoint families:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\qquad |x|\in\lbrace h,h+1\rbrace;\\
&x=b+2e_i,\qquad b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\quad b_i=0,\quad
|b|\in\lbrace h-2,h-1\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both families are nonempty for \(m\ge4\). Completeness of \(E_m\) follows without an integrality assertion about \(R_m\): the inclusion \(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\subseteq R_m\) gives
\(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m\subseteq R_m\cap\mathbb Z^m=E_m\), and the reverse inclusion is immediate.

Every point of \(P_m\) has a representation \((\theta x,1+\theta)\), where \(x\in R_m\) and \(0\le\theta\le1\). This follows by aggregating all convex coefficients of the convex base \(R_m\times\lbrace2\rbrace\). Its only possible integral last coordinates are one and two. At last coordinate one it is the apex, and at last coordinate two it lies in the base. Thus the exact complete lattice support is
$$
D=\lbrace(0,1)\rbrace\ \mathbin{\dot\cup}\ (E_m\times\lbrace2\rbrace).
$$
It is finite. Since \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq P_m\), the definition \(D=P_m\cap\mathbb Z^k\) implies \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\). Its coordinate and two-budget assertions now follow from the displayed description of \(E_m\). Its actual core is exactly the apex together with the Boolean members of \(E_m\) at last coordinate two. Every other point is a double point with one bad two and the good two \(t=2\). The origin of \(\mathbb R^k\) is absent, so \(B_k\not\subseteq D\).

For full affine rank, fix any \(i\le m\) and choose a set \(U_i\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace\) of size \(h\). Such a set exists since \(h\le m-1\). The indicator vectors of \(U_i\) and \(U_i\cup\lbrace i\rbrace\) are both in \(E_m\), and their difference is \(e_i\). Hence differences of base points span \(\mathbb R^m\times\lbrace0\rbrace\). A difference from the apex to any base point has last coordinate one. Together these differences span \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\), proving \(\operatorname{aff}(D)=\mathbb R^k\). This also proves that \(R_m\) and \(P_m\) have their asserted full dimensions.

If fields \(\beta,\beta'\) give the same normalized probabilities on \(D\), then dividing their positive weights gives that \((\beta'-\beta)\cdot u\) is constant for \(u\in D\). The difference vector is therefore orthogonal to the span of all support differences. Full affine rank makes it zero, proving the claimed absence of field-gauge freedom.

At bad total rank \(r\in\lbrace h,h+1\rbrace\), there are exactly \(\binom mr\) Boolean points and \(m\binom{m-1}{r-2}\) one-two points. For the latter, choose the unique two-index and then the \(r-2\) ones among the other \(m-1\) coordinates. Each point at that rank has original weight \(\rho^r\); the apex has weight one. Define the total Boolean-base and double-point weights by
$$
W_0=\sum_{r=h}^{h+1}\binom mr\rho^r,
\qquad
W_2=\sum_{r=h}^{h+1}m\binom{m-1}{r-2}\rho^r.
$$
Thus \(Z=1+W_0+W_2\), \(\alpha=(1+W_0)/Z\), and the double-point mass is \(W_2/Z=1-\alpha\). This is the original normalization, with no conditioning.

The good/bad split is exactly the one in the statement, because the activities are \(32\) and \(1\). Each actual bad one-two vector occurs only at \(t=2\), and its unique label is determined by its two-index and its remaining bits. Consequently its actual section is the complete singleton \(S_q=\lbrace2\rbrace\). The formulas for \(B_0,Z_q,\zeta_q,\lambda_q,\xi_q\) follow directly. These singleton sections partition all double points, so their original masses sum to \(1-\alpha\).

For the core estimate, the exact ratio of the two rank counts is
$$
\frac{m\binom{m-1}{r-2}}{\binom mr}
=\frac{r(r-1)}{m-r+1}
\ge\frac{h(h-1)}{h+2}
\ge\frac h4
\qquad(r\in\lbrace h,h+1\rbrace).
$$
Here \(m\in\lbrace2h,2h+1\rbrace\), and \((h-1)/(h+2)\ge1/4\) for \(h\ge2\). Hence \(W_2\ge(h/4)W_0\). Since \(W_0\ge1\),
$$
\alpha
=\frac1{1+W_2/(1+W_0)}
\le\frac1{1+(h/4)W_0/(1+W_0)}
\le\frac1{1+h/8}
=\frac8{h+8}.
$$
In particular \(\alpha\to0\), and \(\alpha\le1/4\) whenever \(m\ge48\).

We give a hand binomial estimate for the individual-cell bound. For every integer \(j\ge1\),
$$
\binom{2j}{j}\ge\frac{4^j}{2\sqrt j}.
$$
The base case \(j=1\) is equality. The ratio of consecutive central coefficients is \(4(2j+1)/(2j+2)\), and
$$
\left(\frac{2j+1}{2j+2}\right)^2-\frac j{j+1}
=\frac1{4(j+1)^2}>0.
$$
Multiplying the induction hypothesis by this ratio proves the next case. If \(m=2h\), it follows that
\(\binom{m-1}{h-1}=\frac12\binom{2h}{h}\ge2^{m-2}/\sqrt h\).
If \(m=2h+1\), then
\(\binom{m-1}{h-1}=\frac h{h+1}\binom{2h}{h}\ge2^{m-3}/\sqrt h\).
Both cases give the convenient uniform bound
$$
\binom{m-1}{h-1}\ge\frac{2^{m-3}}{\sqrt m}.
$$
Because \(\rho>1\), the maximum label mass occurs at rank \(h+1\). Retaining just the rank-\(h+1\) double points in \(Z\) gives
$$
\max_q\xi_q
=\frac{\rho^{h+1}}Z
\le\frac1{m\binom{m-1}{h-1}}
\le\frac8{\sqrt m}\,2^{-m}.
$$
For a fixed \(\delta>0\), every integer \(m\ge\max\lbrace48,64/\delta^2\rbrace\) therefore has \(\alpha\le1/4\) and \(\xi_q\le\delta2^{-m}\) for every label. The displayed low-adjusted-mass sum is then exactly \(1-\alpha\ge3/4\), and it tends to one. This proves the counterexample with its full quantifier order.

We next calibrate the actual original cubics using the accepted one-step theorem f27ee487069e27e7. The support \(E_m\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^m\) is finite, nonempty, complete, and has at most one two, as proved above. Its occupied parity vectors are precisely the Boolean vectors of ranks \(h-2,h-1,h,h+1\). For ranks \(h,h+1\) the vectors themselves occur. For ranks \(h-2,h-1\), a zero coordinate is available, and placing a two there gives an actual member of \(E_m\). No other parities occur. Thus
\(q_{E_m}=\sum_{j=h-2}^{h+1}\binom mj\le2^m\).

On \(E_m\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\), use the unchanged bad-coordinate field \((-\log\rho,\ldots,-\log\rho)\), partition \(Z_E=\sum_{x\in E_m}\rho^{|x|}=Z-1\), and conditional probabilities \(\nu_{(x,0)}=\rho^{|x|}/Z_E\). The normalization map to the accepted theorem is \(k_{\mathrm{there}}=m\), \(D_{\mathrm{there}}=E_m\), \(N_{\mathrm{there}}=m+1\), \(Z_{\mathrm{there}}=Z_E\), and \(p_{\mathrm{there}}=\nu\). Its actual parity count is \(q_{E_m}\), and no Boolean containment is invoked. That theorem yields
$$
M_3(\nu),T(\nu)\ge\frac1{512q_{E_m}^3}.
$$
The affine injection of this actual support into \(D\) is \(x\mapsto(x,2)\), and the original mass of its image is
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}=Z_E/Z\). Pointwise,
\(p_{(x,2)}=\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}\nu_x\).
To check the closed-halfspace transfer explicitly, pull any original containing halfspace back under \((x,u)\mapsto(x,2,u)\), where \(u\) is the extra zero coordinate. If its restricted normal is nonzero, the pullback is a conditional containing closed affine halfspace. If its restricted normal is zero, the pullback is the whole conditional space, since it contains the center; its conditional mass one is at least conditional depth. Retaining only actual base points therefore gives
\(\tau_p(((c,2),0))\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}\tau_\nu((c,0))\).
The affine injection also sends each ordered reflected pair to an original ordered reflected pair, preserving its midpoint and diagonal convention, so
\(s_p((c,2))\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}s_\nu(c)\).
Multiply the cubes by the original center mass
\(p_{(c,2)}=\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}\nu_c\), and sum over actual base centers. This proves
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge
\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}^4\min\lbrace M_3(\nu),T(\nu)\rbrace
\ge\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}^4}{512q_{E_m}^3}.
$$
Since \(Z_E\ge W_0\ge1\), we have \(\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}\ge1/2\); also \(Z_E\ge\rho^h\to\infty\), so \(\lambda_{\mathrm{slice}}\to1\). Finally \(N=m+2\) and \(q_{E_m}\le2^m\) give
\(1/(512q_{E_m}^3)\ge1/(512\,8^m)=(1/8)8^{-N}\).
This proves every stated slice-transfer calibration, with exactly the fourth power of the original mass.

For a stronger calibration, all remaining calculations use the full original law directly. Let \(\mathcal P_D\) and \(q_D\) be the occupied parity set and its cardinality in the notation of f27ee487069e27e7, and put
\(P_b=\sum_{u\in D:\,u\equiv b\pmod2}p_u\).
The apex has odd \(t\)-parity and is its own parity class. Every base point has even \(t\)-parity. In the base, a Boolean point of rank \(h\) or \(h+1\) is alone in its parity class. A parity vector of rank \(h-2\) supports exactly the points \(b+2e_i\) of total rank \(h\), and a parity vector of rank \(h-1\) supports exactly those of total rank \(h+1\), with \(i\) ranging over the zero coordinates of \(b\). Thus probabilities are equal within each occupied parity class of the full original \(D\), and \(q_D=q_{E_m}+1\).

Completeness puts the midpoint of each ordered same-parity pair \(u,v\in D\) in \(D\). Conversely, every reflected pair about an integral center has the same parity. The original affine law gives
\(p_{(u+v)/2}^2=p_up_v\).
Equal probabilities within each such pair now yield the exact identity, including all diagonal pairs,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)
&=\sum_{\substack{u,v\in D\\u\equiv v\pmod2}}
p_{(u+v)/2}\min(p_u,p_v)\\
&=\sum_{\substack{u,v\in D\\u\equiv v\pmod2}}p_up_v
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal P_D}P_b^2
\ge\frac1{q_D}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step is Cauchy's inequality and \(\sum_bP_b=1\). Jensen's inequality for the original center probabilities gives
\(T(p)\ge(\sum_cp_cs_p(c))^3\ge q_D^{-3}\).

For completeness, the depth comparison does not require Boolean containment. At a fixed center \(c\), distinct unordered reflected pairs have disjoint endpoints, none equal to \(c\). Each containing closed halfspace contains at least one endpoint of each pair, since otherwise their midpoint is strictly outside its defining closed inequality. The center atom and these endpoint masses give
\(\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\frac12(s_p(c)-p_c)\ge\frac12s_p(c)\).
This handles boundary containment as well. Hence \(M_3(p)\ge T(p)/8\). Since
\(q_D=q_{E_m}+1\le2^m+1\le2^{m+1}\), the exact direct bounds imply
$$
T(p)\ge\frac1{q_D^3}\ge8^{-(m+1)}=8\,8^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{8q_D^3}\ge8^{-(m+2)}=8^{-N}.
$$
Every support, parity mass, pair sum, and depth in this last calculation is that of the original law on the full \(D\). This proves that the failed auxiliary cap coexists with the stated original cubic lower bounds.
