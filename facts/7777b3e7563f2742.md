## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers, let \(\beta\ge0\) be real, and let \(B_i,C_{4,i}\) be integers satisfying \(b\le B_i\le2b\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) for every \(i\in[k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\). Give every spatial vector multiplicity one. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),
&f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(E\) is a nonnegative integer. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm full}&=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
&Z_4=Z_{\rm full}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\rm full}}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},\\
D_{\rm sel}&=\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
&Z_3=Z_{\rm sel}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\rm sel}}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},
&R&=\lfloor T/b\rfloor.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the aliases \(Z_{\rm full}=Z_4\) and \(Z_{\rm sel}=Z_3\) refer to these exact full and all-ternary domains. For \(0\le\ell\le k\), let \(Z^{(\ell)}\) be the sum of the same original weights \(f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\) over spatial vectors having exactly \(\ell\) coordinates equal to three. In particular, \(Z^{(0)}=Z_3\) and \(Z^{(1)}=Z_{\rm one3}\); the superscripted count masses are distinct from \(Z_3,Z_4\).

Then the following bounds hold, with \(0^{3/2}=0\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm one3}&\le \frac{20R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}Z_3,\\
\ell Z^{(\ell)}&\le
\frac{20R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k-\ell+1}}Z^{(\ell-1)}
\qquad(1\le\ell\le k),\\
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}&\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{21R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

Retain every integer height in the original supports
$$
\mathcal A_a=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in D_a,\ t\in\mathbb Z,
\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,
\qquad
p^a_{(\mathbf x,t)}=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{Z_a}
\quad(a\in\lbrace{\rm full},{\rm sel}\rbrace).
$$
For either law, define \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) as the infimum of \(p(H)\) over closed affine halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero normal containing \(\mathbf z\), and let \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\). Then
$$
M_3(p^{\rm full})\ge
\frac1{2939328}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{84R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$
For every real \(A\ge0\), it follows that
$$
R\le A k^{1/3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{21A^{3/2}},
\qquad
M_3(p^{\rm full})\ge
\frac{e^{-84A^{3/2}}}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$
The only accepted input is `cd9532b1d59c5977`, used for the final all-ternary moment estimate: its \(k,T,\beta,B_i,g_i,\Gamma,A_T,Z,p\) are exactly the present \(k,T,\beta,B_i,g_i|_{\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace},\Gamma|_{D_{\rm sel}},\mathcal A_{\rm sel},Z_3,p^{\rm sel}\). Its ambient space and halfspace family are unchanged. The locked mixed-integer dimensions and convex body retain their locked meanings.

## Proof

The zero spatial vector at height zero is present, so both normalizers are positive and finite. Write \(w(\mathbf x)=f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\). A spatial vector is called feasible if its energy is at most \(T\). The complete original tails satisfy \(f_E\le f_{E'}\) whenever \(0\le E'\le E\). Therefore every energy-decreasing transformation below compares the entire original height tail, including its boundary height, for every \(\beta\ge0\).

We repeatedly use elementary weighted edge counting. If edges \(\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y\) decrease energy, then
$$
\sum_{\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y}w(\mathbf x)
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y}w(\mathbf y).
\tag{4}
$$
The same comparison holds after multiplying each edge by any nonnegative kernel entry. In particular, a kernel with row sums one and column sums at most \(L\) shows that its total source mass is at most \(L\) times the total allowed target mass. These assertions follow by summing the individual tail inequalities; they require no equal-energy assumption between distinct source or target classes.

For a feasible spatial vector put \(Q(\mathbf x)=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|\). Every coordinate counted by \(Q\) costs at least \(b\), so \(Q(\mathbf x)\le R\). Clipping every three to two decreases energy. A ternary target with \(Q\) twos has at most \(2^Q\) full preimages and at most \(Q\) preimages containing exactly one three. Thus
$$
Z_4\le2^R Z_3,
\qquad
Z_{\rm one3}\le RZ_3.
\tag{5}
$$
If \(R=0\), no feasible vector contains a two or a three, and every conclusion about the masses follows directly. We first prove the single-third estimate when \(R\ge1\).

Suppose \(R>k/16\). Then \(R\le4R^{3/2}/\sqrt{k}\), and (5) proves the first inequality in (1), with room in its constant. It remains to treat
$$
1\le R\le k/16.
\tag{6}
$$
In particular \(k\ge16\). Order the coordinate labels by nondecreasing \(B_i\), breaking ties in any fixed manner, and put \(m=\lceil\sqrt{kR}\rceil\). Let \(I_{\rm low}\) be the first \(m\) labels and \(I_{\rm high}\) the others. Then \(B_j\le B_i\) for \(j\in I_{\rm low}\), \(i\in I_{\rm high}\). The bounds in (6) give
$$
\sqrt{kR}\le m\le\frac54\sqrt{kR},
\qquad m\ge4R,
\qquad m-R\ge\frac34m,
\qquad m<k.
\tag{7}
$$
Indeed \(\sqrt{kR}\ge4\), so the ceiling adds at most one quarter of this quantity; also \(\sqrt{kR}\ge4R\), while \(m\le k/4+1\le5k/16<k\).

For a single-third source let \(a\) be its total number of ones. First consider sources with \(a\ge k/4\) whose unique three lies at \(i\in I_{\rm low}\). For every source one-coordinate \(j\), replace \(x_i=3\) by zero and \(x_j=1\) by two. The exact energy change is \(B_j-1-C_{4,i}\), and
$$
B_j-1-C_{4,i}\le2b-1-(2B_i-1)\le0.
\tag{8}
$$
Thus every such source has exactly \(a\) distinct feasible ternary targets. Occupancy is preserved: \(Q(\mathbf y)=Q(\mathbf x)\). For any target, an inverse source is determined by its former three-position \(i\in I_{\rm low}\), now zero, and its promoted position \(j\), now two. Its indegree is therefore at most \(mQ(\mathbf y)\le mR\). Applying (4), with the source outdegree at least \(k/4\), bounds this source mass by
$$
\frac{4mR}{k}Z_3.
\tag{9}
$$

Next consider sources with \(a\ge k/4\) whose unique three lies at \(i\in I_{\rm high}\). For every one-coordinate \(j\in I_{\rm low}\), clip the three at \(i\) to two and promote the one at \(j\) to two. The energy change is at most
$$
(B_j-1)-(C_{4,i}-B_i)
\le (B_j-1)-(B_i-1)\le0.
\tag{10}
$$
Here \(Q(\mathbf y)=Q(\mathbf x)+1\). The outdegree need not be bounded below at each individual source. Its weighted average has the required lower bound, for the following exact reason.

Fix \(i\in I_{\rm high}\), the set \(S\) of source two-coordinates, and the integer \(a\ge k/4\). Write \(q=|S|+1\le R\). The ones may occupy any \(a\)-subset of \([k]\setminus(S\cup\lbrace i\rbrace)\), which has size \(k-q>0\). Every one-placement in this class has the identical energy \(C_{4,i}+\sum_{j\in S}B_j+a\), so every placement has the identical original weight and is feasible whenever one is. The average number of ones in \(I_{\rm low}\) is exactly
$$
\frac{a\bigl(m-|S\cap I_{\rm low}|\bigr)}{k-q}
\ge\frac{m-q+1}{4}\ge\frac{m-R}{4}.
\tag{11}
$$
To see the equality, each available coordinate belongs to exactly the fraction \(a/(k-q)\) of the \(a\)-subsets. This proves exchangeability of the one-positions directly in the original weighted class. Summing (11) over these disjoint classes shows that the total source-weighted outdegree is at least \((m-R)/4\) times the mass now under consideration.

A ternary target has at most \(Q(\mathbf y)^2\le R^2\) inverse sources for these edges: choose its two-position in \(I_{\rm high}\) to restore to three and its two-position in \(I_{\rm low}\) to restore to one. The choices determine the source uniquely and require no pairing factor. Thus (4) and (11) bound this second source mass by
$$
\frac{4R^2}{m-R}Z_3.
\tag{12}
$$
More precisely, partitioning this calculation by \(Q(\mathbf x)=q\) retains the intermediate coefficient \(4Q(\mathbf y)^2/(m-Q(\mathbf y)+1)\) on its targets, since \(Q(\mathbf y)=q+1\) and (11) is at least \((m-q)/4\). We use only the uniform bound in (12).

It remains to bound all single-third sources with \(a<k/4\), regardless of the position of the three. Use the common integer
$$
h=\min\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac{k}{8R}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace\ge1.
\tag{13}
$$
If \(z\) is a source's number of zeros and \(q=Q(\mathbf x)\), then \(z=k-a-q\). Consequently (6), (13), and \(a<k/4\) give
$$
z>\frac{11k}{16},\qquad
h\le\frac{k}{8},\qquad
z-h>\frac{9k}{16},\qquad
a+h<\frac{3k}{8}.
\tag{14}
$$
Clip the unique three, at \(i\), to two and change any \(h\)-subset of the source zeros to ones. Its energy change is \(h-(C_{4,i}-B_i)\le (b-1)-(B_i-1)\le0\). There are exactly \(\binom zh\) distinct targets; give each the kernel entry \(1/\binom zh\), so the row sums to one. Occupancy is preserved, and the target one-count and zero-count are \(a+h\) and \(z-h\).

For a target with an inverse source, these two target counts determine \(a\) and \(z\) uniquely, because \(h\) is common to all sources. An inverse source is specified by a target two-coordinate to restore to three and \(h\) target one-coordinates to restore to zero. Its column sum is at most
$$
Q(\mathbf y)\frac{\binom{a+h}{h}}{\binom zh}
\le Q(\mathbf y)\theta^h,
\qquad
\theta=\frac{a+h}{z-h+1}.
\tag{15}
$$
The product formula proves the inequality, since every numerator factor is at most \(a+h\) and every denominator factor is at least \(z-h+1\). By (14), \(0\le\theta\le2/3\). Feasibility also gives \(a\le T<b(R+1)\), and \(h\le b-1\); hence \(a+h\le3bR\) for \(R\ge1\). Using (14) again gives
$$
\theta\le\frac{6bR}{k}.
\tag{16}
$$
This is the step that uses the full cap through the number of binary ones, rather than a bound involving occupancy alone.

We claim that \(\theta^h\le32R/k\). If \(h=b-1\), put \(u=R/k>0\). For every integer \(b\ge2\),
$$
\min\lbrace2/3,6bu\rbrace^{b-1}\le12u.
\tag{17}
$$
Indeed the two entries agree at \(u=1/(9b)\). For \(u\) above this value, \((2/3)^{b-1}/u\) is at most \(9b(2/3)^{b-1}\); below it, \((6bu)^{b-1}/u\) is nondecreasing and has that same endpoint value. The latter value is at most twelve: it equals twelve at \(b=2\), and its successive ratio is \(2(b+1)/(3b)\le1\). Equations (16)-(17) prove the claim in this case. If \(h=\lfloor k/(8R)\rfloor\), then \(k/(8R)\ge2\), so \(h\ge k/(16R)\). Bernoulli's inequality gives \((3/2)^h\ge1+h/2\ge h/2\), and thus
$$
\theta^h\le(2/3)^h\le\frac2h\le\frac{32R}{k}.
\tag{18}
$$
At least one case applies in the minimum (13), including ties. The claim follows.

Equation (15) is the actual target column estimate of this kernel, with \(Q(\mathbf y)=Q(\mathbf x)\); after the claim it is at most \(32Q(\mathbf y)R/k\le32R^2/k\). The kernel version of (4) therefore bounds the entire low-one source mass by
$$
\frac{32R^2}{k}Z_3.
\tag{19}
$$
No extra sum over one-count or zero-count classes is present: the target already recovers both source counts. The three source cases in (9), (12), and (19) are disjoint and exhaustive. Their target sets can overlap, and adding their three bounds pays for all such overlaps.

Writing \(u_*=R^{3/2}/\sqrt{k}\), equations (6)-(7) give
$$
\frac{4mR}{k}\le5u_*,\qquad
\frac{4R^2}{m-R}\le\frac{16}{3}u_*,\qquad
\frac{32R^2}{k}\le8u_*.
$$
The sum is \((55/3)u_*\le20u_*\). This proves the single-third estimate under (6), and (5) already proved it for \(R>k/16\). Together with the \(R=0\) case, it holds for all of the stated parameters.

We now prove the source-count recurrence in the original normalization. Fix \(1\le\ell\le k\). For every \(F\subseteq[k]\) of size \(\ell-1\), fix exactly the coordinates in \(F\) at site three and put \(E_F=\sum_{i\in F}C_{4,i}\), \(T_F=T-E_F\), and \(k_F=k-\ell+1\). Let \(U_0(F)\) be the original mass with these fixed coordinates and all other coordinates ternary, and let \(U_1(F)\) be the original mass with these fixed coordinates and exactly one further three. If \(T_F<0\), both masses vanish. Otherwise the identity
$$
f_{E_F+E}=e^{-\beta E_F}
\sum_{t'=E}^{T_F}e^{-\beta t'}
\tag{20}
$$
holds for every nonnegative integer \(E\), with an empty sum interpreted as zero. It follows by the exact height translation \(t'=t-E_F\), retaining every integer height. The remaining \(k_F\) coordinates satisfy the same window hypothesis, with the same \(b\), and residual rank \(R_F=\lfloor T_F/b\rfloor\le R\). The single-third theorem just proved applies to their actual complete height tails; the common factor \(e^{-\beta E_F}\) cancels. Therefore
$$
U_1(F)\le\frac{20R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k_F}}U_0(F).
\tag{21}
$$
This remains true for \(T_F<0\), because then both sides are zero.

Summing (21) over these \(F\) gives exactly
$$
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_0(F)=Z^{(\ell-1)},
\qquad
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_1(F)=\ell Z^{(\ell)}.
\tag{22}
$$
For the first equality a source has exactly one possible \(F\), its entire set of three-positions. For the second, a source with \(\ell\) threes has exactly \(\ell\) possible \(F\), obtained by omitting one three-position. Every spatial vector has multiplicity one, and the same full original height tail is counted each time. Equations (21)-(22) prove the recurrence in (1), including odd \(\ell\).

To sum all sources, again first suppose (6) holds. Feasible sources have at most \(R\) threes, and for \(1\le\ell\le R\) one has \(k_F\ge k-R+1\ge15k/16\). The recurrence consequently gives
$$
\ell Z^{(\ell)}\le D Z^{(\ell-1)},
\qquad
D=\frac{21R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}},
\tag{23}
$$
since \(80/\sqrt{15}\le21\). Induction yields \(Z^{(\ell)}\le D^\ell Z^{(0)}/\ell!\). Summing these nonnegative masses, and using \(Z^{(0)}=Z_3\), proves \(Z_4\le e^D Z_3\). If \(R>k/16\), (5) instead gives
$$
Z_4\le2^RZ_3\le e^RZ_3
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{4R^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)Z_3
\le e^D Z_3.
\tag{24}
$$
The \(R=0\) equality was already established. This proves every mass assertion in (1).

Finally, the selected law is literally the all-ternary original law in accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977`, with the object, normalizer, and ambient-halfspace map given in the statement. That fact supplies
\(M_3(p^{\rm sel})\ge2939328^{-1}8^{-(k+1)}\). Put \(\alpha=Z_3/Z_4\). The complete supports satisfy \(\mathcal A_{\rm sel}\subseteq\mathcal A_{\rm full}\), and every selected atom has original full probability \(p^{\rm full}_{\mathbf z}=\alpha p^{\rm sel}_{\mathbf z}\). Hence for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\),
\(p^{\rm full}(H)\ge\alpha p^{\rm sel}(H)\). Taking infima over the identical family of halfspaces containing a selected atom gives \(\tau_{p^{\rm full}}(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\tau_{p^{\rm sel}}(\mathbf z)\). Restricting the full moment sum to these original atoms yields
$$
M_3(p^{\rm full})\ge\alpha^4M_3(p^{\rm sel})
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{25}
$$
There are exactly four original normalizer factors, one from the sampling probability and three from the depth. The last inequality of (1) gives \(\alpha^4\ge\exp(-84R^{3/2}/\sqrt{k})\), proving (2). Under \(R\le A k^{1/3}\), one has \(R^{3/2}/\sqrt{k}\le A^{3/2}\), which proves (3).

All exchanges use weak energy inequalities and compare the complete original height tails. In particular (8) uses a one-coordinate at the receiving site, whose promotion costs \(B_j-1\le2b-1\); it does not replace this cost by \(2b-2\).
