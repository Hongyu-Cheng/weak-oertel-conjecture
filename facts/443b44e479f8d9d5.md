## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$, at respective integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and volumes $0<a=|A|\le b=|B|$ and $k=|K|$. Their lifted convex hull has these exact integer fibers. Write $g_A,g_B,g_K$ for their ordinary centroids and set $Y=(0,[k g_K+a(g_A+g_B)]/(k+2a))$. Raw mixed depth at $Y$ is the infimum of original mixed volume over all closed ambient halfspaces containing $Y$. Put $v_*=((a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2)^d$, $\theta=a/v_*$, $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, $A_d=1/\Gamma_d$, and $L_d=4A_d/3-1$. Define
$$
\varepsilon_d=1+A_d/3-\frac{2d}{d+1}\left[(1+1/(2d))^{d+1}+(1-L_d/(2d))_+^{d+1}-1\right]>0.
$$
For every integer $d\ge1$, the raw mixed depth at $Y$ is at least
$$
\frac34\Gamma_d(k+a)+
\frac{3\Gamma_d^2 k}{4(1+2a/k)^d}
\left[\frac{1-\theta}{3\Gamma_d}+\theta\varepsilon_d\right].
$$
For fixed $b,k$, the displayed bound tends to $\Gamma_d k$ as $a$ tends to zero through compatible triples.

## Proof

The lifted hull projects onto $[-1,1]$; its extreme sections are the lifted endpoints, and its middle section equals $\operatorname{conv}(K\cup (A+B)/2)=K$. Apply the triangular transport construction in accepted fact 486fe778f838d99d to $A,B$, with interpolation weight $1/2$ and containing body $(A+B)/2$. It gives a single coupling of the uniform probabilities whose midpoint law is supported in $(A+B)/2$ with density at most $1/v_*$, where $v_*=((a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2)^d$. Multiply the coupling and its midpoint law by $a$; call the latter $\eta$. The coupling marginals are the volume on $A$ and $a/b$ times the volume on $B$. The measure $\eta$ has mass $a$ and mean $(g_A+g_B)/2$ and satisfies $\eta\le(a/v_*)\mathbf1_K\,dx\le\mathbf1_K\,dx$. The continuous coordinate $y$ of $Y$ is therefore the centroid of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+2\eta$ and belongs to $\operatorname{int} K$: a supporting linear functional is strictly below its support value throughout the positive-volume interior.

For each ambient closed halfspace $H$, let $H_z$ denote its trace at base $z$. If a coupled pair has midpoint in $H_0$, at least one of its lifted endpoints is in $H$ by the affine defining inequality. Integrate with the mass-$a$ coupling and use $a/b\le1$ to see that the original captured mixed volume is at least $|K\cap H_0|+\eta(H_0)$. It suffices to consider halfspaces through $Y$ because moving the boundary to $Y$ from a containing position only reduces the captured mass. A pure-base through-halfspace captures at least $k+a$.

For a nonvertical through-halfspace put $\kappa=\mathbf1_K\,dx/k$, $\nu=\eta/(\theta k)\le\kappa$, and $\alpha=\nu(K)=v_*/k\le1$ by Brunn–Minkowski. The same point $y$ is the centroid of $\kappa+2\theta\nu$, and $\theta\alpha=a/k$. Set $s=\kappa(H_0)$ and $r=\nu(H_0)$. Both sides of this through-$y$ trace have positive volume, so $0<s<1$. For its nonzero continuous normal $u$ put $V(t)=\kappa\lbrace x:u\cdot x\ge t\rbrace $. By Brunn–Minkowski on the parallel caps $V^{1/d}$ is concave on the projection interval, and $V$ decreases continuously and strictly from one to zero there, since projected interior intervals have positive volume and hyperplanes have zero volume. Thus $V(u\cdot x)$ is uniform on $[0,1]$ under $\kappa$. Under $\nu$ it has a density $f$ in $[0,1]$, of mass $\alpha$ and mass $r$ on $[0,s]$. Jensen gives
$$
(1+2\theta\alpha)s^{1/d}\ge \frac{d}{d+1}+2\theta\int_0^1 v^{1/d}f(v)\,dv .
$$
For an increasing integrand the minimum on each of $[0,s]$ and $[s,1]$ is attained by filling its leftmost part of the prescribed mass. Indeed subtract that indicator from the density and the integrand value at its right endpoint from the integrand; the product is nonnegative and the density difference integrates to zero. Use lengths $r$ and $\alpha-r$, which fit by domination. Write $q=1+1/d$, $\phi(x)=x-x^q/q$, $U=1/s$, $E=r/s$, $Q=(s+\alpha-r)/s$. Integrating the two filled intervals, dividing the Jensen inequality by $s^q$ and using $\alpha=s(Q+E-1)$ gives
$$
\phi(U)+2\theta\phi(Q)+2\theta\phi(E)\ge 2\theta/(d+1).
$$

For $x\ge0$ maximize $\phi(x)+jx$ by differentiation to get the bound $(1+j)_+^{d+1}/(d+1)$. Apply it with slopes $1/d,1/(2d),-L_d/(2d)$ and multiplicities $1,2\theta,2\theta$. After subtracting the preceding lower bound and multiplying by $d$, this gives
$$
U+\theta Q-\theta L_d E\le A_d+\theta(1+A_d/3-\varepsilon_d).
$$
The formula for $\varepsilon_d$ is exactly the positive scalar margin in accepted fact 607dca34de472b94, by expanding its $\frac{d}{d+1}\Delta_d$. Using $(1+\theta\alpha)/s=U+\theta Q+\theta E-\theta$ in the last upper bound yields
$$
s+\theta r\ge\frac{3\Gamma_d}{4}
\left[1+\theta\alpha+\left((1-\theta)A_d/3+\theta\varepsilon_d\right)s\right].
$$
The first Jensen inequality gives $s\ge\Gamma_d/(1+2\theta\alpha)^d$. The coefficient of $s$ in the bracket is positive, so substitution and multiplication by $k$ give the claimed bound for original captured mass.

The binomial inequality $(1+1/(2d))^{d+1}\ge1+(d+1)/(2d)$ shows $\varepsilon_d\le A_d/3$. The whole claimed bound is therefore at most $\Gamma_d k+(3/4)\Gamma_d a<k+a$, handling the pure-base directions. As $a\downarrow0$ with $b,k$ fixed, $\theta$ tends to zero and the two displayed terms tend respectively to $3\Gamma_d k/4$ and $\Gamma_d k/4$.
