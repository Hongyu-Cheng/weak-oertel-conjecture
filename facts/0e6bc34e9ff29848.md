## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\ge1\) be integers, let \(0\le a\le b\le\lfloor L/h\rfloor\) be integers, and let \(Y\) have masses proportional to \(e^{sy}\), for some finite \(s\in\mathbb R\), on \(\{a,\ldots,b\}\). The singleton case \(a=b=k\) is allowed. Suppose \(\mathbb EY=k\in\mathbb Z\) and \(j=(L-hk)/(m+1)\) is a positive integer. Conditional on \(Y=y\), let \(\mathbf X\) be uniform on
\[
 \left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}:
                 \sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i\le L-hy\right\}.
\]
Then the full mean is \((j\mathbf1,k)\), and, for every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(c\in\mathbb R\), the centered projection \(T=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-j\mathbf1)+c(Y-k)\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb ET^4\le60516\,\bigl(\mathbb ET^2\bigr)^2.
\]
Zero-variance directions and singleton height laws are included.

More generally, take any finite log-concave lattice height law \(p\) with contiguous positive support inside \(\{0,\ldots,\lfloor L/h\rfloor\}\), integral mean \(k\), and positive integral \(j=(L-hk)/(m+1)\). Attach the same uniform transverse kernels to obtain a law \(R\) on \((\mathbf X,Y)\). Then \(R\) is an exact finite mixture of the preceding lifted geometric laws, all with full mean \((j\mathbf1,k)\). Every proper closed halfspace containing this mean has \(R\)-mass at least \(1/242064\). For every finite product of independently specified laws of this form, with arbitrary choices of ranks, budgets, and heavy coefficients, every proper closed halfspace containing its concatenated original mean has product mass at least \(1/242064\). Each original mean is a lattice support point. The same product assertion allows deterministic blocks arising from \(j=0\). No unconditional fourth-moment bound is asserted for an arbitrary log-concave-height mixture.

In particular, this applies to the original zero transverse tilt entropy rows on
\[
 E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:
                    \sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i+hy\le L\right\}
\]
with integral full means: these are the laws proportional to \(e^{\gamma y}\) on \(E_{L,h,m}\), with finite \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), and the uniform height-face endpoint laws when such a mean is on a height endpoint. Here zero transverse tilt means \(\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf0\) in the exponent \(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x+\gamma y\). Their height masses are log-concave, as proved below.

The alias map to accepted fact `889f821f12f55878` is as follows. Its scalar variable \(X\), scalar integral mean \(j\), and component parameter \(s_\ell\) become the present height variable \(Y\), height mean \(k\), and geometric parameter \(s\), respectively. In the mixture application its component count, denoted \(L\) there, is denoted \(R_0\) here; the present \(L\) always means the weighted-simplex budget. Its reusable mixture principle is applied to blocks of dimension \(m+1\), with their full means \((j\mathbf1,k)\), and with \(\kappa=60516\). For the scalar moment bound, accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191` is used at rank \(r=1\), with its dilation parameter \(m=b-a\), support \(D_{b-a,1}=\{0,\ldots,b-a\}\), and mean vector \(\mathbf j=(k-a)\). Its random scalar coordinate is the present \(Y-a\), and its constant 45648 is retained. Thus that predecessor's \(m\) means height interval length, whereas the present \(m\) means transverse rank.

## Proof

Write \(q=m+1\) and \(B=L-hY\). Augment \(\mathbf X\) by the slack coordinate \(N_q=B-\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_i\), and put \(N_i=X_i\) for \(i\le m\). Conditional on the nonnegative integer budget \(B\), \(\mathbf N\) is uniform on all complete compositions of \(B\) into \(q\) nonnegative parts. Exchangeability and \(\sum_iN_i=B\) give \(\mathbb E[N_i\mid B]=B/q\). Consequently \(\mathbb E X_i=(L-hk)/q=j\). Moreover \(mj+hk=L-j\le L\), so \((j\mathbf1,k)\) is in the original lattice simplex. Its height is in the positive height support because that support is contiguous and its mean is integral.

We first derive the exact uniform-composition moments, allowing every integer \(B\ge0\). For a real vector \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^q\) with \(\sum_i b_i=0\), define \(U=\sum_i b_iN_i\), \(S_2=\sum_i b_i^2\), and \(S_4=\sum_i b_i^4\). Use falling factorials \((B)_{\underline r}=B(B-1)\cdots(B-r+1)\), rising factorials \((q)^{\overline r}=q(q+1)\cdots(q+r-1)\), and \(F_r=(B)_{\underline r}/(q)^{\overline r}\). The empty products equal one. For nonnegative integers \(r_i\) with \(r=\sum_i r_i\),
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\prod_i(N_i)_{\underline{r_i}}\mid B\right]
       =F_r\prod_i r_i!.
\]
Indeed, \(\sum_{n\ge0}(n)_{\underline t}z^n=t!z^t/(1-z)^{t+1}\). Multiplying these generating functions and extracting the coefficient of \(z^B\) gives \(\prod_i r_i!\binom{B+q-1}{q+r-1}\); division by the number \(\binom{B+q-1}{q-1}\) of compositions gives the displayed expression. Both expressions vanish when \(r>B\).

In particular, the second moment is \(\mathbb E[U^2\mid B]=(F_1+F_2)S_2\). For the fourth moment, the preceding factorial identity and the elementary expansions of ordinary powers in falling factorials give, for pairwise distinct indicated indices,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[N_i^4\mid B]&=24F_4+36F_3+14F_2+F_1,\\
 \mathbb E[N_i^3N_j\mid B]&=6F_4+6F_3+F_2,\\
 \mathbb E[N_i^2N_j^2\mid B]&=4F_4+4F_3+F_2,\\
 \mathbb E[N_i^2N_jN_t\mid B]&=2F_4+F_3,\\
 \mathbb E[N_iN_jN_tN_v\mid B]&=F_4.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The coefficient sums in the fourth-power expansion reduce using
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i\ne j}b_i^3b_j&=-S_4,\\
 \sum_{i<j}b_i^2b_j^2&=(S_2^2-S_4)/2,\\
 \sum_i b_i^2\sum_{\substack{j<t\\j,t\ne i}}b_jb_t
       &=S_4-S_2^2/2,\\
 \sum_{i<j<t<v}b_ib_jb_tb_v&=S_2^2/8-S_4/4.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first three identities follow by expanding \(\sum_i b_i=0\) and \(S_2^2\). The last is the degree-four Newton identity with the first power sum zero. Empty index sums are zero, so these identities also cover \(q=2,3\). The multinomial coefficients for the five index patterns are \(1,4,6,12,24\). Substitution therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U^4\mid B]
 = (F_1+7F_2+12F_3+6F_4)S_4
   +(3F_2+6F_3+3F_4)S_2^2.
\]
Factoring the scalar coefficients proves the following exact formulas:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[U^2\mid B]
   &=\frac{B(B+q)}{q(q+1)}S_2,\\
 \mathbb E[U^4\mid B]
   &=\frac{B(B+q)}{q(q+1)(q+2)(q+3)}
       \left(3(B-1)(B+q+1)S_2^2
              +[6B(B+q)+q(q-1)]S_4\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(B=0\) both sides vanish, and for \(B=1\) the fourth moment is \(S_4/q\), while the variance is \(S_2/q\). Thus these formulas do not assert a conditional kurtosis bound at sparse budgets. For example, for the projection of the first count at \(B=1\), the standardized fourth moment is \(q-2+1/(q-1)\), which diverges with \(q\).

We next control the whole geometric budget law. Set \(\mu=\mathbb EB=qj\ge q\). For a singleton height, the bounds \(\mathbb EB^2\le3\mu^2\) and \(\mathbb EB^4\le75\mu^4\) are immediate. Otherwise positivity of the height endpoint masses gives \(a<k<b\). Put \(G=b-Y\), \(n=b-a\), and \(t=\mathbb EG=b-k\). Then \(t\) is an integer at least one, \(G\) has masses proportional to \(r^g\) on \(\{0,\ldots,n\}\), where \(r=e^{-s}>0\), and \(B=B_{\min}+hG\) with \(B_{\min}=L-hb\ge0\).

Let \(A_d=\sum_{g=0}^{d}r^g\) and \(T_i=\Pr(G\ge i)\), with \(T_0=1\) and \(T_i=0\) for \(i>n\). For \(0\le i\le n\), \(T_i=r^iA_{n-i}/A_n\). The positive successive ratios are
\(T_{i+1}/T_i=rA_{n-i-1}/A_{n-i}\). They decrease with \(i\): indeed \(A_{d-1}/A_d=1-(\sum_{u=0}^{d}r^{-u})^{-1}\) increases with \(d\). Multiplying successive ratios now gives \(T_{i+v}\le T_iT_v\) for all nonnegative integers \(i,v\), including the cases in which a tail is zero.

For any positive integer \(r_0\), the hockey-stick identity yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E(G)_{\underline{r_0}}
  &=r_0!\sum_{i\ge r_0}\binom{i-1}{r_0-1}T_i\\
  &=r_0!\sum_{u_1,\ldots,u_{r_0}\ge1}
                 T_{u_1+\cdots+u_{r_0}}\\
  &\le r_0!\left(\sum_{u\ge1}T_u\right)^{r_0}
   =r_0!t^{r_0}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All sums are finite after the zero tails are included. Expanding ordinary second and fourth powers therefore gives \(\mathbb EG^2\le2t^2+t\le3t^2\) and \(\mathbb EG^4\le24t^4+36t^3+14t^2+t\le75t^4\), using \(t\ge1\). Minkowski's inequality in \(L^2\) and \(L^4\), applied to the nonnegative constant \(B_{\min}\) and \(hG\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\mathbb EB^2)^{1/2}
    &\le B_{\min}+h\sqrt3\,t\le\sqrt3\,\mu,\\
 (\mathbb EB^4)^{1/4}
    &\le B_{\min}+h75^{1/4}t\le75^{1/4}\mu.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the same two budget moment bounds hold in every allowed case. Cauchy-Schwarz also gives \(\mathbb EB^3\le\sqrt{\mathbb EB^2\,\mathbb EB^4}\le15\mu^3\).

Let \(w=B(B+q)\). Since \(q\le\mu\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb Ew^2
   &=\mathbb EB^4+2q\mathbb EB^3+q^2\mathbb EB^2
     \le108\mu^4,\\
 \mathbb Ew&=\mathbb EB^2+q\mu\ge\mu^2+q\mu\ge\mu^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Also \(q(q-1)/\mathbb Ew\le1\). Since \(S_4\le S_2^2\) and \((B-1)(B+q+1)=B(B+q)-(q+1)\le w\), the exact conditional fourth moment, for \(B\ge1\), is at most
\(S_2^2[9w^2+q(q-1)w]/[q(q+1)(q+2)(q+3)]\). At \(B=0\) this inequality is also true because both sides vanish. Averaging and using \(\mathbb E[U\mid B]=0\) shows, whenever \(S_2>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\mathbb EU^4}{(\mathbb EU^2)^2}
 &\le\frac{q(q+1)}{(q+2)(q+3)}
   \left(9\frac{\mathbb Ew^2}{(\mathbb Ew)^2}
                   +\frac{q(q-1)}{\mathbb Ew}\right)\\
 &\le9\cdot108+1=973.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If \(S_2=0\), all \(b_i\) are zero, so the conclusion \(\mathbb EU^4\le973(\mathbb EU^2)^2\) is again true. The crucial budget lower bound here is on the mean \(\mu=qj\), not on individual conditional budgets.

For the requested full direction, extend \(a_q=0\), put \(\alpha=q^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{q}a_i\), \(b_i=a_i-\alpha\), and \(d=c-h\alpha\). Then \(\sum_i b_i=0\), and direct use of \(B/q-j=-h(Y-k)/q\) gives
\[
 T=U+d(Y-k),\qquad
 \mathbb E[U\mid Y]=0,\qquad
 \mathbb ET^2=\mathbb EU^2+d^2\operatorname{Var}(Y).
\]
For a nonsingleton height, its translated law on \(\{0,\ldots,b-a\}\) is proportional to \(e^{sz}\) and has integral mean \(k-a\). It is the unique maximum-entropy law with that mean: for any law \(Q\) with the same mean, relative entropy to this strictly positive geometric law equals the geometric law's entropy minus \(H(Q)\), and is nonnegative, with equality only for identical laws. This follows from \(\log u\le u-1\). Therefore the translated law is exactly the rank-one row in accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`, under the alias map in the statement. Its fourth-moment inequality gives \(\mathbb E(Y-k)^4\le45648(\operatorname{Var}Y)^2\). For the singleton both sides are zero. Write \(v_U=\mathbb EU^2\) and \(v_D=d^2\operatorname{Var}Y\). Minkowski followed by Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\mathbb ET^4)^{1/4}
    &\le973^{1/4}\sqrt{v_U}+45648^{1/4}\sqrt{v_D},\\
 \mathbb ET^4
    &\le(\sqrt{973}+\sqrt{45648})^2(v_U+v_D)^2\\
    &\le(32+214)^2(v_U+v_D)^2
     =60516(\mathbb ET^2)^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The numerical inequalities use \(973<32^2\) and \(45648<214^2\). They do not require independence of \(U\) and \(Y\). If the total variance is zero, both summands are zero almost surely and the desired inequality remains true.

Now let the original height law be the arbitrary log-concave \(p\) in the statement. By the exact scalar decomposition of accepted fact `889f821f12f55878`, write \(p=\sum_{\ell=1}^{R_0}\pi_\ell\nu^{(\ell)}\), where every \(\nu^{(\ell)}\) is a geometric interval law or \(\delta_k\), has height mean \(k\), and is supported inside the original height interval. Attach the original uniform kernel at every height to each component. If \(K_y(\mathbf x)\) denotes this kernel, the identity
\[
 p_yK_y(\mathbf x)
     =\sum_{\ell=1}^{R_0}\pi_\ell\nu_y^{(\ell)}K_y(\mathbf x)
\]
is an equality of the original joint masses, including zeros. Each resulting component has full mean \((j\mathbf1,k)\), because the conditional transverse mean is the affine function \((L-hy)\mathbf1/q\). Every component satisfies the directional bound just proved, with the same constant 60516. Applying the reusable mixture principle of accepted fact `889f821f12f55878` gives the original-product cap assertion with \(1/(4\cdot60516)=1/242064\), for any number of blocks and any ranks. Conditional on independent labels, centered block projections are independent and expansion gives \(\sum_i\mathbb ET_i^4+6\sum_{i<t}\mathbb ET_i^2\mathbb ET_t^2\le60516(\sum_i\mathbb ET_i^2)^2\). Consequently the product constant is used once; it is not raised to the number of factors. Zero conditional variances give closed-side probability one, as in that accepted principle. The preceding support check applies to each original mean and hence to the concatenated mean.

If \(j=0\) is allowed for an original block, then \(\mathbb EB=qj=0\) and \(B\ge0\) force \(B=0\) almost surely. Thus \(\mathbf X=\mathbf0\) and \(Y=L/h=k\) almost surely; this is a deterministic legal lattice point and satisfies the component hypothesis with zero moments. Such a block can therefore be included without changing the product constant.

Finally, a finite zero transverse tilt law on \(E_{L,h,m}\) has uniform conditional kernels and height masses
\[
 p_y=Z^{-1}e^{\gamma y}\binom{L-hy+m}{m},
       \qquad 0\le y\le\lfloor L/h\rfloor.
\]
There are \(\binom{L-hy+m}{m}\) lattice points in its height slice by the composition count used above. Its height support is contiguous and positive. On the real interval \([0,\lfloor L/h\rfloor]\), the logarithm of the unnormalized height weight equals \(\gamma y+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\log(L-hy+i)-\log(m!)\), whose second derivative is \(-h^2\sum_{i=1}^{m}(L-hy+i)^{-2}\le0\). The midpoint concavity inequality at integer heights proves \(p_y^2\ge p_{y-1}p_{y+1}\) at every interior height. Outside the support the required inequalities hold because the neighboring external masses are zero. An endpoint height row has height law \(\delta_k\), which also has contiguous log-concave support and is already covered.

For completeness, the finite tilted law is the unique maximum-entropy law at its full mean: for any law \(Q\) on the same simplex with that mean, \(D(Q\Vert R)=H(R)-H(Q)\ge0\), because \(\log R(\mathbf x,y)=\gamma y-\log Z\) and \(\mathbb E_QY=\mathbb E_RY\). The nonnegativity and equality case follow from \(\log u\le u-1\), with equality only for identical laws. On an endpoint height face the same argument uses the uniform law on that slice; the height is forced to the endpoint by its mean. Thus all the stated original zero transverse tilt entropy rows fall under the proved common-mean mixture consumer.
