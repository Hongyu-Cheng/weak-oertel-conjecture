## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put

\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\]

where \(G\) is the global complete metronoid-core capacity of accepted
fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`. Assume

\[
 G<\frac W2.
\tag{1}
\]

For every parity class
\(\omega\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), define

\[
 A_\omega:=\sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z,\qquad
 M_\omega:=\max_{z\equiv\omega}a_z,
\tag{2}
\]

with \(M_\omega=0\) when \(A_\omega=0\). Then every positive fiber obeys

\[
 \boxed{a_z\leq 2^{-n}G\qquad(z\in Z),}
\tag{3}
\]

and consequently

\[
 \sum_\omega M_\omega\leq G.
\tag{4}
\]

Use the free matching normalization of accepted fact
`8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`: a free same-parity matching is a nonnegative family
\(p=(p_{uv})\) on unordered pairs of distinct points \(u,v\in Z\) with
\(u\equiv v\pmod 2\), with degrees and mass

\[
 d_u(p):=\sum_{v\ne u}p_{uv}\leq\frac{a_u}{2},\qquad
 |p|:=\sum_{\{u,v\}}p_{uv}.
\tag{5}
\]

On every nonempty parity class define the canonical product matching by

\[
 p^\times_{uv}:=\frac{a_ua_v}{2A_\omega}
 \qquad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\omega),
\tag{6}
\]

and put it equal to zero on all other pairs. It is a legal free matching,
with

\[
 d_u(p^\times)
 =\frac{a_u}{2}\left(1-\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right),
 \qquad
 t_u^\times:=2d_u(p^\times)
 =a_u\left(1-\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right),
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 4|p^\times|
 =W-\sum_{\omega:A_\omega>0}
 \frac{\sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z^2}{A_\omega}
 \geq W-G.}
\tag{8}
\]

Call a nonempty parity class good when

\[
 M_\omega\leq\frac{A_\omega}{4},
\tag{9}
\]

and bad otherwise. Define the half-strength product reservoir

\[
 r_{uv}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\omega},
 &u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\omega,\ \omega\text{ good},\\[5pt]
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]

There is an actual maximum free matching \(p\) containing \(r\), in the
coordinatewise sense that \(p_{uv}\geq r_{uv}\) on every eligible pair,
and

\[
 \boxed{|r|\geq\frac{3}{32}(W-4G).}
\tag{11}
\]

More precisely, the total physical mass of the good parity classes is at
least \(W-4G\), and each good class contributes at least
\(3A_\omega/32\) to \(|r|\).

Finally, if

\[
 G<\frac W4,
\tag{12}
\]

then there is a good parity class \(\omega_*\) such that, writing
\(A_*:=A_{\omega_*}\),

\[
 \boxed{A_*\geq\frac{W-4G}{2^n}.}
\tag{13}
\]

For the probability weights

\[
 \mu_z:=\frac{a_z}{A_*}
 \qquad(z\equiv\omega_*),
\tag{14}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{
 \max_{z\equiv\omega_*}\mu_z
 \leq\frac{G}{W-4G}.}
\tag{15}
\]

The denominator in (15) is positive by (12). In addition, goodness gives
\(\max_z\mu_z\leq1/4\).

## Proof

The set \(Z\) is finite because the base projection of the compact body
\(C\) is compact. We first derive (3) without using a conditionally scoped
fiber statement. Fix \(z\in Z\), take the rank-zero affine lattice
subspace \(A:=\{z\}\), and let \(G_A^{\mathrm{rel}}\) be the relative
complete metronoid-core capacity from part (21) of accepted fact
`3d1f63abf09b2abb`. The only possible nonempty support in \(A\) is
\(\{z\}\), so \(G_A^{\mathrm{rel}}\leq a_z\). Conversely, choose that
support, mass \(a_z\), and center \(z\). The full-mass metronoid of \(C_z\)
is its centroid singleton, hence its complete core is nonempty. A
zero-dimensional affine space has no proper relative closed halfspaces,
so the relative balance condition is vacuous. Thus

\[
 G_A^{\mathrm{rel}}=a_z.
\tag{16}
\]

For this \(A\), its dimension is zero and its physical mass is \(a_z\).
Part (22) of `3d1f63abf09b2abb` therefore gives

\[
 G\geq
 \min\left\{
 a_z+\frac{W-a_z}{2},\ 2^na_z
 \right\}
 =\min\left\{\frac{W+a_z}{2},\ 2^na_z\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

The first entry in (17) is at least \(W/2>G\) by (1). Hence the second
entry must be at most \(G\), proving (3). There are \(2^n\) parity
classes, so (3) gives \(M_\omega\leq2^{-n}G\) for every \(\omega\).
Summing proves (4).

Fix a nonempty parity class \(\omega\). Directly from (6),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_u(p^\times)
 &=\sum_{\substack{v\equiv\omega\\v\ne u}}
 \frac{a_ua_v}{2A_\omega}\\
 &=\frac{a_u}{2A_\omega}(A_\omega-a_u)
 =\frac{a_u}{2}\left(1-\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right)
 \leq\frac{a_u}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

This proves legality and (7). Since every unordered edge contributes
twice to the sum of degrees,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 4|p^\times|
 &=2\sum_ud_u(p^\times)
 =\sum_ut_u^\times\\
 &=\sum_{\omega:A_\omega>0}
 \left(A_\omega-
 \frac{\sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z^2}{A_\omega}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

For every nonempty class,

\[
 \sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z^2
 \leq M_\omega\sum_{z\equiv\omega}a_z
 =M_\omega A_\omega.
\tag{20}
\]

Equations (4), (19), and (20) prove (8).

We next construct a maximum matching containing \(r\). Fix a good class.
Its reservoir degree at \(u\) is

\[
 d_u(r)
 =\frac{a_u}{4}\left(1-\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right).
\tag{21}
\]

The residual degree required to saturate the free capacity \(a_u/2\) is

\[
 \rho_u:=\frac{a_u}{2}-d_u(r)
 =\frac{a_u}{4}\left(1+\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right).
\tag{22}
\]

Put \(R_\omega:=\sum_{u\equiv\omega}\rho_u\). Then

\[
 R_\omega
 =\frac{A_\omega}{4}
 +\frac{\sum_{u\equiv\omega}a_u^2}{4A_\omega}
 \geq\frac{A_\omega}{4}.
\tag{23}
\]

Goodness gives \(a_u\leq A_\omega/4\), and therefore

\[
 2\rho_u
 =\frac{a_u}{2}\left(1+\frac{a_u}{A_\omega}\right)
 \leq\frac{5A_\omega}{32}
 <\frac{A_\omega}{4}
 \leq R_\omega.
\tag{24}
\]

The circle construction in the proof of accepted fact
`8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` gives the following exact degree-sequence criterion:
nonnegative numbers \(s_u\) are the degrees of an off-diagonal fractional
matching on the complete graph precisely when
\(2\max_us_u\leq\sum_us_u\). Applying its sufficiency direction to
\((\rho_u)_u\) using (24) gives an off-diagonal residual matching
\(q^\omega\) whose degree at every \(u\) is exactly \(\rho_u\). Hence
\(r+q^\omega\) has degree \(a_u/2\) at every vertex in the good class.
Its classwise mass is therefore

\[
 \frac12\sum_{u\equiv\omega}\frac{a_u}{2}
 =\frac{A_\omega}{4}.
\tag{25}
\]

Every matching in that class has mass at most \(A_\omega/4\), by summing
its degree capacities, so (25) is maximum. On each bad class choose any
maximum matching supplied by the exact free-capacity result in
`8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, and on each good class use \(r+q^\omega\). Free
matching constraints and objectives decompose over parity classes. Their
union is therefore an actual global maximum \(p\), and it contains \(r\).

It remains to prove the reservoir mass and diffuse-class bounds. For one
good class,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |r|_\omega
 &=\sum_{\substack{u<v\\u,v\equiv\omega}}
 \frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\omega}\\
 &=\frac{A_\omega^2-
 \sum_{u\equiv\omega}a_u^2}{8A_\omega}\\
 &\geq\frac{A_\omega^2-M_\omega A_\omega}{8A_\omega}
 \geq\frac{3A_\omega}{32}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

For each bad class, \(M_\omega>A_\omega/4\), so
\(A_\omega<4M_\omega\). Including the possibility that there are no bad
classes and then using (4),

\[
 \sum_{\omega\text{ bad}}A_\omega
 \leq4\sum_{\omega\text{ bad}}M_\omega
 \leq4G.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus

\[
 \sum_{\omega\text{ good}}A_\omega\geq W-4G.
\tag{28}
\]

Summing (26) and using (28) proves (11).

Under (12), the right side of (28) is positive, so at least one good
class is nonempty. Choose a good class of largest mass \(A_*\). There are
at most \(2^n\) good classes, and therefore (28) gives (13). Finally, (3)
and (13) imply

\[
 \max_{z\equiv\omega_*}\mu_z
 =\frac{M_{\omega_*}}{A_*}
 \leq
 \frac{2^{-n}G}{2^{-n}(W-4G)}
 =\frac{G}{W-4G},
\tag{29}
\]

which proves (15). Goodness also gives
\(M_{\omega_*}/A_*\leq1/4\). This completes the proof.
