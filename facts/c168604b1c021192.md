## Statement

For every integer \(j\geq2\), put

\[
 d_j:=j,\qquad \varepsilon_j:=j^{-3},\qquad
 \psi_i(t):=1-\varepsilon_j|t-i|\quad(1\leq i\leq j),
\]

and define, in base coordinates \((t,u,v)\in\mathbb R^3\) and continuous
coordinates \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_j)\in\mathbb R^j\),

\[
 C_j:=\left\{(t,u,v,x):
 \begin{array}{l}
 1\leq t\leq j,\quad -j\leq u,v\leq j,\\
 x_i\geq-\psi_i(t)\quad(1\leq i\leq j),\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^j x_i\leq1
 \end{array}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Then \(C_j\subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^j\) is a full-dimensional
convex body.  For \(1\leq t\leq j\), set

\[
 q_t:=\sum_{i=1}^j|t-i|,\qquad
 S_t:=j+1-j^{-3}q_t,\qquad
 a_t:=\frac{S_t^j}{j!},
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
 c_t:=j^{-3}\left((|t-i|)_{i=1}^j
 -\frac{q_t}{j+1}\mathbf{1}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^3\), write
\(a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_j((C_j)_z)\), and, when \(a_z>0\), let \(c_z\)
denote the fiber centroid.  The positive-volume lattice support, fiber
volumes, and fiber centroids are

\[
 D_{+,j}=\{1,\ldots,j\}\times\{-j,\ldots,j\}^2,\qquad
 a_{(t,u,v)}=a_t,\qquad c_{(t,u,v)}=c_t.
\tag{4}
\]

Write

\[
 M_j:=(2j+1)^2,\qquad N_j:=jM_j,\qquad
 W_j:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^3}a_z=M_j\sum_{t=1}^ja_t,
\tag{5}
\]

\[
 S_-:=S_1=S_j,\qquad S_+:=\max_{1\leq t\leq j}S_t,\qquad
 a_-:=\frac{S_-^j}{j!},\qquad a_+:=\frac{S_+^j}{j!},
\tag{6}
\]

and

\[
 b_j:=\frac{(j-1)^2}{4j^3},\qquad
 \chi_j:=e^{b_j},\qquad
 \gamma_j:=\left(\frac j{j+1}\right)^j,\qquad
 \theta_j:=1-\gamma_j,
\tag{7}
\]

\[
 \rho_j:=j!\left(\frac2{j+1}\right)^j.
\tag{8}
\]

The scores below are exactly the accepted scores specialized to \(n=3\).
Accepted fact 2146b3093a062470 supplies the truncated-excess score and its
\(n=3\) normalization.  For \(\tau>0\), define

\[
 Q_j(\tau):=\sum_{z\in D_{+,j}}(a_z-\theta_j\tau)_+,\qquad
 T(C_j):=\sup_{\tau>0}\min\{Q_j(\tau),8\gamma_j\tau\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Accepted fact c2ed1a4e6f4a7c4d supplies the pooled parity-reflection score.
For an affine map \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^j\) and
\(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3\), put

\[
 F_{r,s}(y):=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^3\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}
 \mathbf{1}_{(C_j)_z-s(z)}(y),
\]

\[
 \mathfrak R_j(s):=\frac12\sum_r\int_{\mathbb R^j}
 \min\{F_{r,s}(y),F_{r,s}(-y)\}\,dy,\qquad
 R(C_j):=\sup_s\mathfrak R_j(s).
\tag{10}
\]

Accepted fact 428d46805148687c supplies the residual-rank score.  For
nonempty \(I\subseteq D_{+,j}\), write

\[
 W_I:=\sum_{z\in I}a_z,\qquad
 r_{\rm aff}(I):=\min_{s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^j\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_z-s(z):z\in I\},
\]

\[
 L(C_j):=\sup_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq D_{+,j}}
 \frac{\gamma_jW_I}{r_{\rm aff}(I)+1}.
\tag{11}
\]

For every proper primitive affine lattice subspace
\(A\subsetneq\mathbb R^3\), let

\[
 W_A:=\sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^3}a_z,\qquad
 P(C_j):=\sup_A2^{\operatorname{codim}A}W_A.
\tag{12}
\]

Finally, for an admissible factorized lattice-polysimplex packet \(\Pi\) of
total rank \(q\), with factor energies \(A_i\), let
\(\mathcal E(\Pi):=\prod_iA_i\) be its raw factor energy and use the ambient
normalization from accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12,

\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_3(\Pi):=2^{3-q}\mathcal E(\Pi),\qquad
 2^{-q}\mathcal E(\Pi)=2^{-3}\operatorname{Sc}_3(\Pi).
\tag{13}
\]

Put

\[
 \mathcal P_{<3}(C_j):=\sup_{\Pi:\,1\leq q(\Pi)<3}
 \operatorname{Sc}_3(\Pi),
\tag{14}
\]

with the supremum of an empty packet family equal to zero.  Then the
following explicit estimates hold:

\[
 \boxed{\frac{T(C_j)}{W_j}<\frac{8\chi_j}{j(2j+1)^2}},
\tag{15}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\frac{R(C_j)}{W_j}
 <\frac12\chi_j\,j(2j+1)^2\rho_j
 \leq\frac12\chi_j\,j(2j+1)^2
 \left(\frac34\right)^{\lfloor(j+1)/4\rfloor}},
\tag{16}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\frac{L(C_j)}{W_j}<\frac{4\gamma_j\chi_j}{j}
 \leq\frac{2\chi_j}{j}},
\tag{17}
\]

\[
 \boxed{\frac{\mathcal P_{<3}(C_j)}{W_j}
 \leq\frac{P(C_j)}{W_j}<\frac{2\chi_j}{j}}.
\tag{18}
\]

In particular, every ratio in (15)-(18) tends to zero as \(j\to\infty\).
The last estimate is uniform over every proper-rank packet, every rank
partition, every integral embedding, and every nonsaturated image lattice.

Nevertheless, \(C_j\) has the following explicit full-rank packet.  Take
three one-dimensional lattice simplices

\[
 \Delta_1=[1,j],\qquad \Delta_2=\Delta_3=[-j,j],
\tag{19}
\]

embed their product by

\[
 \Phi(t,u,v)=(t,u,v),
\tag{20}
\]

and at every product vertex
\((t,u,v)\in\{1,j\}\times\{-j,j\}^2\) choose the continuous corner point
\(x_{t,u,v}:=c_t\).  Put

\[
 s_j:=\gamma_ja_-,
\tag{21}
\]

take both vertex values of the first factor to be \(s_j^{1/j}\), and take
both vertex values of each of the other two factors to be \(1\).  The factor
functions and energies are

\[
 g_1\equiv s_j^{1/j},\qquad g_2\equiv g_3\equiv1,
\tag{22}
\]

\[
 A_1=js_j,\qquad A_2=A_3=2j+1.
\tag{23}
\]

This is an admissible \(1+1+1\) packet of total rank three, with exact raw
energy

\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi_j)=j(2j+1)^2\gamma_ja_-=N_j\gamma_ja_-.
\tag{24}
\]

It satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\frac{\mathcal E(\Pi_j)}{W_j}
 \geq\gamma_j\frac{a_-}{a_+}>\frac1e}.
\tag{25}
\]

Consequently accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 supplies a point
\(p_j\in C_j\cap(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R^j)\) with

\[
 \boxed{h_{C_j\cap(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R^j)}(p_j)
 \geq\frac18\mathcal E(\Pi_j)>\frac1{8e}W_j}.
\tag{26}
\]

Thus this family refutes any compensation theorem asserting that the five
scores in (15)-(18) have a dimension-free positive maximum relative to
\(W\).  It does not refute the locked weak Oertel target, because (26) is a
target-scale positive certificate on the same family.

## Proof

We first verify that (1) defines one full-dimensional common convex body.
Each \(\psi_i\) is concave, so
\((t,x)\mapsto x_i+\psi_i(t)\) is concave and its nonnegative superlevel set
is convex.  The base-box constraints and \(\sum_i x_i\leq1\) are affine.
Their intersection \(C_j\) is therefore convex and closed.  On
\(1\leq t\leq j\), one has \(0<\psi_i(t)\leq1\).  Hence every point of
\(C_j\) satisfies \(x_i\geq-1\), while

\[
 x_i\leq1-\sum_{k\ne i}x_k
 \leq1+\sum_{k\ne i}\psi_k(t)\leq j.
\]

Together with the bounded base box, this proves compactness.  Choose any
\(t_0\in(1,j)\), set \(u=v=0\), and set \(x=0\).  All base inequalities and
all inequalities \(x_i>-\psi_i(t_0)\), \(\sum_i x_i<1\), are strict.  Thus
\(C_j\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{3+j}\), proving full
dimensionality.

For a base point \((t,u,v)\) in the box, make the translation

\[
 y_i:=x_i+\psi_i(t).
\]

The fiber constraints become

\[
 y_i\geq0,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^jy_i\leq
 1+\sum_{i=1}^j\psi_i(t)=S_t.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
 \Delta_j:=\{y\in\mathbb R^j:y_i\geq0,\ \sum_i y_i\leq1\},
\]

the exact fiber is

\[
 (C_j)_{(t,u,v)}=-\bigl(\psi_1(t),\ldots,\psi_j(t)\bigr)+S_t\Delta_j.
\tag{27}
\]

It has volume \(S_t^j/j!=a_t\), and its centroid is

\[
 -\bigl(\psi_1(t),\ldots,\psi_j(t)\bigr)
 +\frac{S_t}{j+1}\mathbf{1}
 =c_t.
\tag{28}
\]

This proves (2)-(5) directly from the common body, rather than positing
arbitrary fiber data.

We next compare all fiber volumes.  For an integer \(1\leq t\leq j\),

\[
 q_t=\frac{t(t-1)}2+\frac{(j-t)(j-t+1)}2,\qquad
 q_1-q_t=(t-1)(j-t).
\tag{29}
\]

Thus \(S_t\geq S_1=S_j\), and

\[
 S_- =j+1-\frac{j-1}{2j^2}>j,\qquad
 0\leq S_t-S_-=\frac{(t-1)(j-t)}{j^3}
 \leq\frac{(j-1)^2}{4j^3}.
\tag{30}
\]

It follows from \(\log(1+x)\leq x\) that

\[
 \log\frac{a_+}{a_-}
 =j\log\frac{S_+}{S_-}
 \leq j\frac{S_+-S_-}{S_-}
 <\frac{(j-1)^2}{4j^3}=b_j.
\tag{31}
\]

Hence

\[
 \frac{a_+}{a_-}<\chi_j,\qquad
 N_ja_-\leq W_j\leq N_ja_+.
\tag{32}
\]

We also record the exact centroid-rank property needed for (17).  Let

\[
 D^{(j)}:=(|t-i|)_{t,i=1}^j,\qquad
 M^{(j)}:=I-\frac1{j+1}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}.
\]

The matrix whose \(t\)-th row is \(c_t^{\mathsf T}\) is

\[
 j^{-3}D^{(j)}M^{(j)}.
\tag{33}
\]

Both factors are invertible.  Indeed, if \(D^{(j)}b=0\) and
\(y_t:=\sum_i|t-i|b_i\), then

\[
 y_{t+1}-2y_t+y_{t-1}=2b_t\qquad(2\leq t\leq j-1),
\]

so every interior coordinate \(b_t\) vanishes.  The endpoint equations
\(y_1=(j-1)b_j=0\) and \(y_j=(j-1)b_1=0\) then give \(b=0\), including the
case \(j=2\), where there are no interior coordinates.  The matrix
\(M^{(j)}\) is the identity on \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\) and has eigenvalue
\(1/(j+1)\) on \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1}\}\).  Hence the \(j\) vectors
\(c_1,\ldots,c_j\) are linearly independent, and every subset of them is
linearly independent.

We prove (15).  The binomial theorem gives

\[
 \gamma_j^{-1}=\left(1+\frac1j\right)^j\geq2,
\]

so \(\gamma_j\leq1/2\), \(\theta_j\geq1/2\), and
\(\gamma_j/\theta_j\leq1\).  Let \(\tau>0\).  If
\(\tau\geq a_+/\theta_j\), then every summand in \(Q_j(\tau)\) vanishes.  If
\(0<\tau<a_+/\theta_j\), then

\[
 \min\{Q_j(\tau),8\gamma_j\tau\}
 \leq8\gamma_j\tau
 <8\frac{\gamma_j}{\theta_j}a_+\leq8a_+.
\]

This is uniform over every threshold, so \(T(C_j)\leq8a_+\).  Divide by
\(W_j\geq jM_ja_-\) and use (32) to obtain (15).

We next prove the primitive-slice and proper-rank packet estimates.  A
primitive affine lattice plane has an equation

\[
 \alpha_1t+\alpha_2u+\alpha_3v=m
\]

with a nonzero primitive integer normal.  After fixing the two coordinates
complementary to any nonzero coefficient, there is at most one possible
value of the remaining coordinate.  Thus such a plane contains at most
\(M_j=(2j+1)^2\) points of \(D_{+,j}\).  A primitive affine lattice line has
integer points \(z_0+kg\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\), for a nonzero primitive
\(g\in\mathbb Z^3\).  Along a coordinate on which \(g\) is nonzero,
successive points differ by at least one, so the line contains at most
\(2j+1\) supported points.  A rank-zero primitive affine slice contains at
most one supported point.  Consequently

\[
 P(C_j)\leq
 \max\{2M_j,\ 4(2j+1),\ 8\}a_+=2M_ja_+.
\tag{34}
\]

Equations (32) and (34) give the strict bound for \(P(C_j)/W_j\) in (18).
Now let \(\Pi\) be any admissible packet of total rank \(1\leq q<3\).
Conclusion 3 of accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12, applied with \(n=3\),
passes to the primitive closure of the packet's affine image and proves

\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_3(\Pi)\leq P(C_j).
\tag{35}
\]

That accepted conclusion already includes arbitrary rank partitions,
nonsaturated integral embeddings, injectivity, and every zero factor value.
Taking the supremum in (35) proves the first inequality in (18), without
reproving its rank-one and rank-two special cases.

We prove (17).  Fix a nonempty \(I\subseteq D_{+,j}\), let
\(J_I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j\}\) be the set of represented first coordinates,
and put \(k:=|J_I|\).  Each \(t\)-level contains \(M_j\) supported points, so

\[
 W_I\leq kM_ja_+.
\tag{36}
\]

Choose one \(z_t=(t,u_t,v_t)\in I\) for every \(t\in J_I\).  For an arbitrary
affine map \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^j\), write

\[
 s(t,u,v)=b+t\alpha+u\beta+v\zeta.
\]

Let \(G\) be the \(k\times j\) matrix with rows \(c_t^{\mathsf T}\), and let
\(E\) be the \(k\times j\) matrix with rows
\((c_t-s(z_t))^{\mathsf T}\), for \(t\in J_I\).  Then

\[
 G=E+XB,
\]

where \(X\) is the \(k\times4\) matrix with rows
\((1,t,u_t,v_t)\) and \(B\) is a \(4\times j\) coefficient matrix.  The row
independence proved after (33) gives \(\operatorname{rank}G=k\), while
\(\operatorname{rank}(XB)\leq4\).  Hence

\[
 \operatorname{rank}E\geq k-4.
\]

The span of all residuals from \(I\) contains the selected residual rows.
Since the preceding estimate holds for every affine \(s\),

\[
 r_{\rm aff}(I)\geq\max\{0,k-4\}.
\tag{37}
\]

For \(1\leq k\leq4\), one has \(k/(r_{\rm aff}(I)+1)\leq k\leq4\).  For
\(k\geq5\), (37) gives
\(k/(r_{\rm aff}(I)+1)\leq k/(k-3)\leq5/2<4\).  Thus, using (36),

\[
 \frac{\gamma_jW_I}{r_{\rm aff}(I)+1}
 \leq4\gamma_jM_ja_+.
\]

Take the supremum over every nonempty \(I\), divide by
\(W_j\geq jM_ja_-\), and use (32) and \(\gamma_j\leq1/2\).  This proves
(17), uniformly over all subsets and all affine residual fits.

It remains to prove the pooled-reflection estimate (16).  We first establish
the required reflected-simplex bound.  For every \(d\geq1\), every
\(p,q\in\mathbb R^d\), and every \(0<\alpha,\beta\leq S\),

\[
 |(p+\alpha\Delta_d)\cap(q-\beta\Delta_d)|_d
 \leq \rho_d\frac{S^d}{d!},\qquad
 \rho_d:=d!\left(\frac2{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{38}
\]

By enlargement, translation, and scaling, it is enough to bound
\(K:=\Delta_d\cap(w-\Delta_d)\).  If \(K\) is empty, there is nothing to
prove.  Otherwise \(K\) is centrally symmetric about \(c=w/2\), and
\(c\in\Delta_d\).  Let

\[
 \lambda_0(x):=1-\sum_{i=1}^dx_i,\qquad
 \lambda_i(x):=x_i\quad(1\leq i\leq d)
\]

be the barycentric coordinates of \(\Delta_d\), and put
\(a_i:=\lambda_i(c)\).  Then \(a_i\geq0\), \(\sum_{i=0}^da_i=1\), and for
every \(x\in K\),

\[
 0\leq\lambda_i(x)\leq2a_i\qquad(0\leq i\leq d),
\tag{39}
\]

because both \(x\) and \(2c-x\) lie in \(\Delta_d\).  Choose \(i_0\) with
\(a_{i_0}\geq1/(d+1)\).  In the barycentric coordinate chart obtained by
deleting \(\lambda_{i_0}\), which has absolute Jacobian one, (39) places
\(K\) inside a box of volume \(2^d\prod_{i\ne i_0}a_i\).  The
arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives

\[
 |K|_d\leq2^d\prod_{i\ne i_0}a_i
 \leq2^d\left(\frac{1-a_{i_0}}d\right)^d
 \leq\left(\frac2{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

Since \(|\Delta_d|_d=1/d!\), this proves (38).

Fix now an arbitrary affine \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^j\), and write
\(D_z:=(C_j)_z-s(z)\).  The profiles \(F_{r,s}\) are nonnegative
integer-valued functions, so pointwise

\[
 \min\{F_{r,s}(y),F_{r,s}(-y)\}
 \leq F_{r,s}(y)F_{r,s}(-y).
\tag{40}
\]

Integrating and expanding the finite sums gives

\[
 \int F_{r,s}(y)F_{r,s}(-y)\,dy
 =\sum_{\substack{z,z'\in D_{+,j}\\z\equiv z'\equiv r\pmod2}}
 |D_z\cap(-D_{z'})|_j.
\tag{41}
\]

By (27), \(D_z\) is a translate of \(S_t\Delta_j\), while
\(-D_{z'}\) is a translate of \(-S_{t'}\Delta_j\).  Since
\(S_t,S_{t'}\leq S_+\), equation (38) gives, independently of \(s,z,z'\),

\[
 |D_z\cap(-D_{z'})|_j\leq\rho_j\frac{S_+^j}{j!}=\rho_ja_+.
\tag{42}
\]

If \(N_r\) is the number of supported base points in parity class \(r\),
then (40)-(42) imply

\[
 \mathfrak R_j(s)\leq\frac12\rho_ja_+\sum_rN_r^2
 \leq\frac12\rho_ja_+N_j^2.
\tag{43}
\]

The bound is uniform over \(s\), so it also bounds \(R(C_j)\).  Dividing by
\(W_j\geq N_ja_-\) and using (32) gives the first inequality in (16).
Finally,

\[
 \rho_j=\prod_{\ell=1}^j\frac{2\ell}{j+1}.
\]

Pair the factors with indices \(\ell\) and \(j+1-\ell\).  Each paired
product is

\[
 \frac{4\ell(j+1-\ell)}{(j+1)^2}\leq1,
\]

and it is at most \(3/4\) whenever
\(1\leq\ell\leq\lfloor(j+1)/4\rfloor\).  If \(j\) is odd, the unpaired
middle factor is one.  Therefore

\[
 \rho_j\leq\left(\frac34\right)^{\lfloor(j+1)/4\rfloor},
\]

which completes (16).  Since \(\chi_j\to1\), the right sides of
(15)-(18) all tend to zero.

We finish by verifying the full-rank rescue packet and its exact constant.
The product in (19) consists of three full-dimensional lattice intervals,
and the matrix of (20) is the \(3\times3\) identity, so the packet has total
rank \(1+1+1=3\) and an injective integral embedding.  At every product
vertex the first coordinate is \(1\) or \(j\), so (27) is a translate of
\(S_-\Delta_j\), has volume \(a_-\), and has centroid \(c_t\).  The density
\(f\equiv1\) represents \(c_t\) in its full-mass metronoid.  Accepted fact
6769574fc1bd9f8d, applied with selected mass \(m=a_-\), therefore gives

\[
 \delta_{C_j}((t,u,v),c_t)\geq\gamma_ja_-=s_j.
\tag{44}
\]

In fact equality holds.  In the translated \(y\)-coordinates, the closed
halfspace

\[
 \left\{y:\sum_{i=1}^jy_i\leq\frac{jS_-}{j+1}\right\}
\]

contains the centroid on its boundary and cuts out
\((j/(j+1))S_-\Delta_j\), of volume exactly \(\gamma_ja_-\).  Thus the
corner factor values stated after (21) satisfy the packet corner condition
with equality.  Their barycentric interpolants are the constant functions
in (22), and summing their \(j\)-th powers over the three lattice intervals
gives (23) and the exact energy (24).

Since \(W_j\leq N_ja_+\),

\[
 \frac{\mathcal E(\Pi_j)}{W_j}
 \geq\gamma_j\frac{a_-}{a_+}
 >\gamma_je^{-b_j}.
\tag{45}
\]

For \(j\geq2\), the alternating-series upper bound gives

\[
 \log\left(1+\frac1j\right)\leq
 \frac1j-\frac1{2j^2}+\frac1{3j^3}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 -\log(\gamma_je^{-b_j})
 &=j\log\left(1+\frac1j\right)+b_j\\
 &\leq1-\frac1{2j}+\frac1{3j^2}
 +\frac{(j-1)^2}{4j^3}\\
 &=1+\frac{-3j^2-2j+3}{12j^3}\leq1.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]

Thus \(\gamma_je^{-b_j}\geq e^{-1}\), and the strict inequality in (45)
proves (25).  Accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 now gives (26), including
every closed ambient halfspace, vertical and nonvertical halfspaces, and
halfspace-boundary mass.

The packet has \(q=n=3\) and three factors.  It is therefore exactly in the
surviving class \(q=n,\ k\geq2\) isolated by accepted fact
fbeb0a501b268d12; neither its proper-rank slice domination nor its
one-factor complete-core domination applies.  Equation (26) proves the
stated non-counterexample limitation.
