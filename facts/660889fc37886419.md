## Statement

Let $r\ge1$, $\beta\ge r+1$, and let $f:\mathbb R^r\to[0,\infty]$ be a measurable density of mass $M\in(0,\infty)$ with full-dimensional positive support. Assume $g=f^{-1/\beta}$ with value $+\infty$ where $f=0$ is convex, interpreting $g=0$ where $f=+\infty$. For every $y\in\operatorname{int}\{f>0\}$ there exist $C,c,\delta>0$ such that
$$
f(x)\le C(1+\|x-y\|)^{-\beta}\quad(x\in\mathbb R^r),\qquad
f(x)\ge c\quad(\|x-y\|<\delta).
$$
In particular $f$ has no infinite point values, and the integrals $\int_0^\infty t^{r-1}f(y+tu)\,dt$ have a positive lower bound and a finite upper bound uniform over unit directions $u$.

## Proof

Translate $y$ to $0$. The nonnegative convex $g$ is finite and continuous on the interior of its effective domain. Choose $\delta,A>0$ so that $g\le A$ on the ball of radius $\delta$ about $0$. For any $x$ with $0<\epsilon=g(x)\le A$, take $a=\epsilon/A$. Convexity bounds $g$ by $2\epsilon$ on $(1-a)x+a B(0,\delta)$, so, writing $\omega_r$ for unit-ball volume,
$$
M\ge\omega_r\delta^r 2^{-\beta}A^{-r}\epsilon^{r-\beta}.
$$
Since $\beta>r$, this gives a uniform positive lower bound on such $\epsilon$. Values exceeding $A$ already have a lower bound. If $g(x)=0$, use any $a\in(0,1)$ and the bound $g\le aA$ on the same image ball. The resulting lower bound $M\ge\omega_r\delta^r A^{-\beta}a^{r-\beta}$ contradicts finite mass as $a\downarrow0$. Thus $g$ is uniformly bounded away from zero on its effective domain.

The convex sublevel $\{g\le2g(0)\}$ contains a ball centered at zero by continuity. If unbounded, it contains cones formed from arbitrarily distant points and the fixed-radius central disk perpendicular to each corresponding ray. Their volumes grow linearly in the distance of the apex and $f\ge(2g(0))^{-\beta}$ on them, contradicting integrability. For $r=1$ the cones are intervals. Choose $R>0$ with that sublevel contained in the open radius-$R$ ball. For an effective-domain point $x$ of norm $s>R$, convexity gives
$$
2g(0)<g(Rx/s)\le (1-R/s)g(0)+(R/s)g(x).
$$
Hence $g(x)>g(0)(1+s/R)$. Combine this with the uniform positive lower bound on the radius-$R$ ball, and with $f=0$ off the effective domain, to obtain the claimed $C$. One can take $c=A^{-\beta}$. Integrating the bounds against $t^{r-1}dt$ yields the asserted uniform radial bounds since $\beta>r$.
