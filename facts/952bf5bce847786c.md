## Statement

For every $n\ge3$ and $d\ge1$ there are a full-dimensional compact lattice-complete convex body with equal $d$-volume fibers, its actual marked boundary surface measure $\eta$, and a finite positive source $\nu$ admitting a same-normal physical martingale to $\eta$, such that every fiber is strictly below the total mixed mass divided by the number of occupied parity classes. There is a convex base potential $\phi$ whose every integral midpoint defect
$$
\frac{\phi(p)+\phi(q)}2-\phi((p+q)/2)
$$
is zero for distinct integer bases with integral midpoint, while $\int\phi\,d(\eta-\nu)=8d\,2^{n-3}>0$. Thus no finite nonnegative combination of such midpoint defects can always bound that transport gap. The witness can use three equally weighted actual closed cap indicators of common mixed volume fraction $1/2$, with source equal to twice their average boundary incidence times $\eta$.

## Proof

Let $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$ and $Q=\operatorname{conv}\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}$. The triangle is cut out by $x+y\le1$, $-2x+y\le1$, $x-2y\le1$. Testing the nine integer points of $[-1,1]^2$ gives exactly $B=\{0,v_1,v_2,v_3\}$, of four distinct parities. Use $C=Q\times[0,2]\times[0,1]^{n-3}\times[0,1]^d$, with the final cube as the continuous fiber. The base set is $P=B\times\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}^{n-3}$. All fibers have volume one, and there are $12\,2^{n-3}$ fibers and $2^n$ occupied parities, giving the strict comparison $1<3/2$. If the midpoint of two bases is integer, both their $B$ and binary coordinates agree. For distinct bases their third coordinates must then be $0$ and $2$. In particular these midpoint edges are nonempty and completely enumerated.

Each cube has marked surface mass $\tau=2d$, including the two endpoint atoms when $d=1$. Relative to the actual summed surface law set source density $2$ on $B$-coordinate zero and $2/3$ on the other $B$-coordinates. For each fixed third coordinate, binary coordinate, continuous point and normal, send origin density $1$ to itself and $1/3$ to each vertex. Keep vertex source density $2/3$ fixed. This fills every target density one and preserves the full physical mean because $v_1+v_2+v_3=0$. It also preserves the mark. The three-vertex probability template of weights $1/3$ is extreme on its affinely independent support, and it is active since all fibers coincide.

Take $\phi(b,j,k)=|b|^2$. It is constant on each column and all integral midpoint defects vanish. At a fixed third and binary coordinate the gap is $\tau(1+1+2)/3=4\tau/3$ by the vertex densities and the zero potential at the origin. Summing gives $4\tau\,2^{n-3}=8d\,2^{n-3}$. The column masses themselves change, whereas every integral midpoint term has zero column total.

The three closed caps are $2z_1-z_2\ge0$, $-z_1+2z_2\ge0$ and $-z_1-z_2\ge0$. Their margins sum to zero, so they cover the ambient space. At the origin all three include the boundary fibers; at $v_i$ only the $i$-th holds, strictly. Thus the source is $(2/3)$ times their sum of closed indicators against $\eta$, and each cap contains precisely half the original unit-volume fibers. For a direct depth check, take the point at $b=0$, third coordinate $1$, any one binary coordinate and the continuous cube center. Its three-fiber column is centrally symmetric and contributes at least $3/2$ raw mass to every closed containing halfspace, so its normalized depth is at least $2^{-n}$.
