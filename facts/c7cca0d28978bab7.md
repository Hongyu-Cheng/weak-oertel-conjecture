## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$, and let $T=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:a_i\cdot x\le1,\ 0\le i\le d\}$ be a full-dimensional bounded simplex of volume $V$, where the normals span $\mathbb R^d$ with $\sum_i a_i=0$. Fix $e$ with $a_0\cdot e=1$, and use $x=y+te$ with $t=a_0\cdot x$, $y\in\ker a_0$. Let $\pi_e(x)=y$, let $\nu$ be $\mathcal H_{d-1}/\|a_0\|$ on $\ker a_0$, and let $\ell(y)$ be the length of the $t$ interval on that line in $T$, zero for an empty section. Set $\sigma=\sum_i(a_i\cdot e)_+$, $L=m/\sigma$, and $P=Vd/L$.

For $0\le s\le L$, $T\cap(T-se)$ is a translate of $(1-s/L)T$, interpreted as a point at $s=L$, and the intersection is empty for $s>L$. The transverse projection $\pi_eT$ has measure $P$, and
$$
\nu\{\ell>s\}=P(1-s/L)^{d-1}\quad(0\le s<L).
$$
The pushforward of $\nu|_{\pi_eT}$ under $\ell$ has density $\frac{P(d-1)}{L}(1-v/L)^{d-2}$ on $(0,L)$ and no atoms.

## Proof

The determinant for the coordinates $x=y+te$ is the absolute normal component of $e$ relative to $\ker a_0$, namely $1/\|a_0\|$. In particular $|T|=\int\ell\,d\nu$. Since $a_0\cdot e=1$, $\sigma\ge1$. The overlap inequalities are
$$
a_i\cdot x\le 1-s(a_i\cdot e)_+ \quad(0\le i\le d).
$$
Their right sides are the sum of $1-s\sigma/m$ and the entries of a vector of coordinate sum zero. The map $x\mapsto(a_i\cdot x)_i$ is an isomorphism from $\mathbb R^d$ onto the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane, so the latter vector specifies a unique translation. When $s<L$ this gives the translated homothety. At $s=L$ all translated margins must be zero since they sum to zero, yielding one point. For $s>L$ summing the inequalities gives $0\le m-s\sigma<0$.

On each parallel line the intersection has length $(\ell-s)_+$. Thus $\int(\ell-s)_+\,d\nu=V(1-s/L)^d$ for $0\le s\le L$. The right derivative equals $-\nu\{\ell>s\}$ by dominated convergence on the bounded projection, proving the tail. The projection is a $(d-1)$-dimensional convex body. Each interior projected point is the projection of some interior point of $T$: extend a line segment from the projection of an interior point of $T$ past the desired projected point inside $\pi_eT$, then interpolate an interior representative with a representative of the farther point. Hence $\ell>0$ almost everywhere on $\pi_eT$, since its boundary is null. At $s=0$ the tail gives its measure. The continuous tail and its limit zero at $L$ for $d\ge2$ prove absence of positive atoms, and differentiation gives the density.
