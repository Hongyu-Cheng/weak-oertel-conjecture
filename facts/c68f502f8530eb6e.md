## Statement

For the uniform outward cap model in the finite-width Hessian theorem, take a sequence of locally minimizing nonvertical normals on the same finite base set, with compact positive convex fibers $K_{z,l}\to K_z$ in Hausdorff distance and all limiting fibers positive. Suppose $h_l\downarrow0$, $b_l\to b\ge0$, $Y_l\to Y$, $\Sigma_l\to\Sigma$ positive definite, and $N_l=(a_l,u_l)\to N=(a,u)$ with $\Gamma_l(u_l)=1$. Set $c_l=N_l\cdot Y_l-b_l$, $c=N\cdot Y-b$ and $\bar Y=Y-b(0,\Sigma u)$. Use actual balanced endpoint coareas at $c_l-h_l,c_l$. On a common subsequence they converge weakly. Their lower-minus-upper difference vanishes on each proper or null/empty limiting cut and is $\chi_z d\mathcal H^{d-1}/\|u\|$ on each positive-area outside-supporting facet $F_z$, and $-\chi_z d\mathcal H^{d-1}/\|u\|$ on each inside-supporting facet, with $0\le\chi_z\le1$. For every full normal direction $D$,
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{out}}\int_{F_z}\chi_z[D\cdot(w-\bar Y)]^2\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}
\ \ge\
\sum_{\mathrm{in}}\int_{F_z}\chi_z[D\cdot(w-\bar Y)]^2\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}.
$$
For pure-base $D=(a',0)$ the incidence is $a'\cdot(z-p)$. If the finite-width strip coareas satisfy $S_l>0$, their actual centroid tends to $\bar Y$.

On the same subsequence, the actual outside boundary capture and inside boundary omission selectors at the moving facet-side roofs converge strongly in $L^1$ on each compact inner facet patch to $\tau_z$, and $\mathbf 1_{0<\tau_z<1}\le\chi_z\le1$ almost everywhere. For the joint weak graph record $\nu_w$ of actual scaled roof arguments $q_l^F$ on the compactified real line, the conditional transition value equals one on $-1<q<0$, zero outside $[-1,0]$, and may take values in $[0,1]$ at $-1,0$. Consequently $\chi_z(w)\le\nu_w([-1,0])\le\nu_w(\mathbb R)$ a.e. The record is taken on the same facets and subsequence as the balanced endpoint limits.

## Proof

All endpoints have uniformly bounded coarea by the common outer radius and the nonzero limiting $\|u\|$, so take weak limits on the common compact ambient support. On each proper limiting cut, the sections at both endpoints converge to the ordinary section, after isometrically identifying their planes, by the Hausdorff convergence argument in d4e455a28be6cde4. The same argument gives weak convergence of section measures by dominated convergence off the relative boundary of the proper limiting section. For an empty or lower-dimensional limiting contact the weak section limits vanish: project along the nearby unit normal and rotate to the limiting tangential plane. The projected endpoint sets are contained in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the limiting trace by compactness, and their area densities there are at most one. This also proves on each limiting facet that the endpoint limits are dominated by full coarea and have no mass on its relative boundary. For $d=1$ the tangential plane is a point with counting measure; empty traces still vanish.

Fix a positive-area supporting facet and a compact convex inner patch in its relative interior. Use moving orthonormal normal coordinates with tangential coordinates identified by rotations and translations tending to the identity. In these coordinates a normal column through the patch contains an interval extending a uniform positive distance inward from the limiting facet plane for all large $l$. Indeed, for each inner patch take a small convex interpolation of the facet with an interior ball of the limiting fiber. The translated and shrunk tangential projection of that interpolated set covers a neighborhood of the patch when the interpolation weight is small. A smaller compact neighborhood of points so obtained is interior and eventually belongs to the approximating fibers by Hausdorff convergence of support functions. Hence in each column both moving endpoint planes are separated from the far end of the interval. At outside support let $q_l^F$ be the actual normalized strip argument at the near upper roof; at inside support use the near lower roof. For an outside patch the lower and upper endpoint footprints are the tests $q_l^F\ge-1$ and $q_l^F\ge0$; for an inside patch they are $q_l^F\le-1$ and $q_l^F\le0$. At each endpoint equality use weight $1/2$. This agrees a.e. with the balanced coarea convention: on a proper endpoint any equality with the near roof lies in the relative boundary of the section and is null; on a supporting endpoint the facet trace has exactly half weight. The signed difference of these footprints has magnitude
$$
\chi_l=\mathbf1_{-1<q_l^F<0}+\tfrac12\mathbf1_{q_l^F\in\{-1,0\}}.
$$
The coarea factor tends to $1/\|u\|$. This proves the ordering of the two limiting section measures and their claimed densities on inner patches. Exhaust the facet by nested compact convex inner patches, using the absence of limit mass at the facet boundary.

The actual selectors on the patch are $\tau_l=\Psi(q_l^F)$ outside and $\tau_l=1-\Psi(q_l^F)$ inside. The upper roof is concave and the lower roof convex, so these are clipped heights $\min(1,(g_l)_+)$ of a concave function $g_l=q_l^F+1$ outside or $g_l=-q_l^F$ inside. Their subgraphs at positive heights are the convex sets in the patch times $[0,1]$ cut out by $t\le g_l$. On a subsequence these bounded compact convex sets converge in Hausdorff distance by Blaschke selection, or are empty along the selected subsequence. Their indicators converge in $L^1$: for a positive-volume limit the indicators converge off its null boundary, and for a lower-dimensional limit the volumes tend to zero. By integration in height this gives strong $L^1$ convergence of $\tau_l$. The $d=1$ case is compactness for a single clipped scalar height. The subgraphs are closed because each actual upper roof is upper semicontinuous and each lower roof is lower semicontinuous by compactness of its fiber. A diagonal subsequence on nested patches and the finitely many labels gives common $\tau_z$ and a further a.e.-convergent subsequence. At each point with $0<\tau_z<1$, eventually $-1<q_l^F<0$, hence $\chi_l=1$. Bounded convergence on this set and the bounded weak limits of the $\chi_l$ give $\chi_z=1$ there. Saturated selectors do not determine $\chi_z$: $q_l^F=-1+1/l$ and $q_l^F=-1-1/l$ both give $\tau_z=0$ outside, and their transition weights are $1$ and $0$.

Extract also the weak graph measures of $(q_l^F,\chi_l)$ on the compactified real line times $[0,1]$, with facet area as the tangential marginal on each patch. Equivalently, exhaust the facet and take one joint extraction, since the discarded facet boundary neighborhoods have vanishing area. Disintegration on these compact metric spaces gives the conditional graph records. The closure of the graph of the footprint rule restricts the second component to one on $(-1,0)$, zero outside $[-1,0]$, and $[0,1]$ at its corners. The conditional mean of the second component is $\chi_z$ by testing the already identified footprint limit; integrating against the first-component marginal $\nu_w$ proves the stated bound. The same strong selector convergence fixes $\Psi(q)=\tau_z$ outside and $\Psi(q)=1-\tau_z$ inside almost surely in the record: test the absolute difference against continuous tangential approximants to the limiting selector in $L^1$, then use the fixed area marginal and continuity of $\Psi$ on the compactification. This construction retains actual one-roof records and needs no bounded scaled roof assumption.

Apply the finite-width balanced-trace inequality and multiply by $h_l$. The correction tends to zero: $S_l$ is bounded by the uniform coarea bound averaged over the width, $|A_l|\le h_lS_l$, $b_l$ stays bounded, and $\eta_l$ is bounded for fixed $D$. The tests $k_{D,l}$ converge uniformly on the common compact support. Their restrictions to the limiting cut have $m=-b$, and hence $k_D=D\cdot(w-\bar Y)$ there. Pass the lower-minus-upper moments to the weak limit and cancel the common $\|u\|^{-1}$, obtaining the displayed PSD form for every $D$ on this same subsequence. Finally, when $S_l>0$, stationarity in every direction gives the actual kernel centroid as $Y_l-(b_l-A_l/S_l)(0,\Sigma_l u_l)$, and $|A_l/S_l|\le h_l$ gives its stated limit.
