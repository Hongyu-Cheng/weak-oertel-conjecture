## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
For \(x\in C_z\), define
\[
\delta_z(x):=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J\}.
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\), an affine map \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\) feasible at the vertices, and an anchor \(z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\), define
\[
\alpha_{z_0}(E,s):=
\inf_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(E)\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
z_0\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}\delta_z(s(z)),
\]
with the usual single-fiber definition in rank zero.

Prove the following two statements.

1. Let \(a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
P:=z_0+[-a,a]^2,
\]
assume its four vertex fibers have positive \(d\)-volume, and let \(s:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^d\) be affine and feasible at those four vertices. Put
\[
D_0:=\delta_{z_0}(s(z_0)).
\]
For \(E=\operatorname{vert}P\),
\[
\alpha_{z_0}(E,s)
\ge
D_0\left[
1+4\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}r\left(1-\frac ra\right)^d
\right].
\]

2. For \(a,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(R=4a\) and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{R,d}:=\{(x,y,u_1,\ldots,u_d):\;&0\le x,y\le2R,\\
&\max\{0,x+y-2R\}\le u_j\le\min\{x,y\}
\quad(1\le j\le d)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(z_0=(R,R)\), write
\[
W_{R,d}:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}\operatorname{vol}_d((C_{R,d})_z).
\]
Using the centered square \(z_0+[-a,a]^2\) and the constant affine selector \(s(z)=(R/2,\ldots,R/2)\), prove an exact certificate \(L_{a,d}\) satisfying
\[
L_{a,d}>\frac{W_{R,d}}{64}
\qquad(a,d\ge1),
\]
and prove the sharp calibration
\[
\lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{L_{a,d}}{W_{4a,d}}=\frac1{64}
\qquad\text{for every fixed }d.
\]

## Proof

We first prove the square estimate, including feasibility away from the four
vertices.  Every \(z\in P\) is a convex combination
\[
z=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{vert}P}\lambda_vv,
\qquad
\lambda_v\geq0,\qquad
\sum_v\lambda_v=1.
\]
Affineness gives
\[
(z,s(z))
=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{vert}P}\lambda_v(v,s(v)).
\]
Every point on the right belongs to \(C\) by vertex feasibility, so convexity
of \(C\) gives \((z,s(z))\in C\).  Thus the selector is feasible throughout
the closed square, including all four edges.

Fix \(z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^2\), and put
\[
r:=\lVert z-z_0\rVert_\infty.
\]
If \(0<r\leq a\), define
\[
v_z:=z_0+\frac a r(z-z_0)\in\partial P.
\]
Then
\[
(z,s(z))
=\left(1-\frac ra\right)(z_0,s(z_0))
+\frac ra(v_z,s(v_z)).
\]
Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` says that
\((z,x)\mapsto\delta_z(x)^{1/d}\) is jointly concave on \(C\).  Since
depth is nonnegative, it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_z(s(z))^{1/d}
&\geq
\left(1-\frac ra\right)D_0^{1/d}
+\frac ra\,\delta_{v_z}(s(v_z))^{1/d}\\
&\geq
\left(1-\frac ra\right)D_0^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\delta_z(s(z))
\geq
D_0\left(1-\frac ra\right)^d
\qquad(0\leq r\leq a),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(r=0\) is the definition of \(D_0\).  At \(r=a\), (1) retains every
boundary lattice point and assigns it the valid zero minorant.

Let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be any proper closed halfspace containing
\(z_0\).  Moving its boundary parallel toward \(z_0\) gives a proper closed
halfspace \(Q_0\subseteq Q\) whose boundary contains \(z_0\).  For
\(1\leq r\leq a\), let
\[
\Sigma_r:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:\lVert z-z_0\rVert_\infty=r\}.
\]
This shell has
\[
\lvert\Sigma_r\rvert=(2r+1)^2-(2r-1)^2=8r
\]
points, partitioned into \(4r\) antipodal pairs about \(z_0\).  Write
\[
Q_0=\{z_0+w:\ell(w)\geq0\}
\]
for a nonzero linear functional \(\ell\).  In each pair
\(\{z_0+w,z_0-w\}\), at least one point belongs to \(Q_0\), and if
\(\ell(w)=0\), both closed-boundary points belong to it.  Hence
\[
\lvert Q_0\cap\Sigma_r\rvert\geq4r.
\tag{2}
\]
The anchor itself belongs to the closed boundary of \(Q_0\) and contributes
the full quantity \(D_0\), not half of it.  Combining (1) and (2), using
\(Q_0\subseteq Q\), and omitting only the zero minorants on \(\Sigma_a\),
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in P\cap Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}\delta_z(s(z))
&\geq
\sum_{z\in P\cap Q_0\cap\mathbb Z^2}\delta_z(s(z))\\
&\geq
D_0+
4D_0\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}
r\left(1-\frac ra\right)^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Taking the infimum over all proper closed \(Q\) proves statement 1.  Notice
that (2) explicitly allows a base boundary to contain lattice points; its
closedness can only increase the shell count.

We now analyze \(C_{R,d}\).  Its displayed inequalities are linear after
writing the maximum and minimum constraints separately, so it is a compact
convex set.  It has nonempty interior for \(R>0\), hence it is a convex
body.  At an integer base point \((i,j)\) with \(0\leq i,j\leq2R\), the
fiber is the \(d\)-cube
\[
\left[
\max\{0,i+j-2R\},\min\{i,j\}
\right]^d.
\]
Its side length is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_R(i,j)
&:=\min\{i,j\}-\max\{0,i+j-2R\}\\
&=\min\{i,j,2R-i,2R-j\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The identity follows by separating the cases \(i+j\leq2R\) and
\(i+j\geq2R\).  Outside the base square the fiber is empty.  Formula (4)
shows that the positive-volume lattice fibers are exactly those with
\[
1\leq i,j\leq2R-1.
\]
For \(1\leq k\leq R\), the number of lattice points with \(m_R(i,j)\geq k\)
is
\[
(2R-2k+1)^2.
\]
Therefore the number with side length exactly \(k\) is
\[
\begin{cases}
8(R-k),&1\leq k<R,\\
1,&k=R.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
This is the full positive lattice-fiber profile, including the unique
central fiber.  It gives the exact total
\[
\boxed{
W_{R,d}=R^d+8\sum_{k=1}^{R-1}(R-k)k^d.}
\tag{6}
\]
The base-boundary fibers have side length zero and therefore contribute
zero \(d\)-volume.

Set \(R=4a\) and
\[
P:=(R,R)+[-a,a]^2=[3a,5a]^2.
\]
For every \((x,y)\in P\),
\[
\min\{x,y\}\geq3a\geq2a=\frac R2
\]
and
\[
\max\{0,x+y-2R\}\leq2a=\frac R2,
\]
because \(x+y\leq10a\) and \(2R=8a\).  Thus the constant selector
\[
s(x,y)=(R/2,\ldots,R/2)
\tag{7}
\]
is feasible throughout the closed square.  At each of its four vertices,
(4) gives side length \(3a>0\), as required by statement 1.

The anchor fiber is \([0,R]^d\), and \(s(R,R)\) is its center.  Its absolute
halfspace depth is
\[
D_0=\frac{R^d}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, for any proper closed fiber halfspace containing the center, move
the boundary parallel to the center to obtain a contained
boundary-through-center halfspace.  Central reflection preserves the cube
and exchanges the two sides.  Their common boundary is a hyperplane of
zero \(d\)-dimensional volume, so each closed side has cube volume \(R^d/2\).
The coordinate halfspace \(u_1\geq R/2\) attains this value.  This also
checks the closed fiber-boundary convention in the definition of
\(\delta\).

Apply (3) with (8).  Define the literal square certificate
\[
\boxed{
L_{a,d}:=
\frac{(4a)^d}{2}
\left[
1+4\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}
r\left(1-\frac ra\right)^d
\right].}
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_{(R,R)}(\operatorname{vert}P,s)\geq L_{a,d}.
\tag{10}
\]
Accepted fact `decc826ae843d87c` also gives
\[
h_{C_{R,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)}
\bigl((R,R),s(R,R)\bigr)
\geq
\alpha_{(R,R)}(\operatorname{vert}P,s),
\tag{11}
\]
so (9) is an actual mixed-depth certificate as well.

For the exact comparison, put
\[
S_{N,d}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(N-k)k^d.
\tag{12}
\]
Reindexing \(k=a-r\) in (9) gives
\[
\boxed{
L_{a,d}
=\frac{4^da^d}{2}+2\cdot4^d S_{a,d}
=4^{d-1}\bigl(W_{a,d}+a^d\bigr).}
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (6) and (13) yield the exact gap identity
\[
\boxed{
64L_{a,d}-W_{4a,d}
=31\cdot4^da^d
+8\left(4^{d+2}S_{a,d}-S_{4a,d}\right).}
\tag{14}
\]

We bound the bracket in (14) without floating point.  For \(N\geq1\), put
\[
g_d(t):=(1-t)t^d,\qquad
A_{N,d}:=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}g_d(k/N)
=\frac{S_{N,d}}{N^{d+2}},
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
I_d:=\int_0^1g_d(t)\,dt
=\frac1{(d+1)(d+2)}.
\tag{16}
\]
We record an exact composite-trapezoid error bound.  For a \(C^2\) function
\(f\) on an interval \([x,x+h]\), two integrations by parts give
\[
\frac h2\bigl(f(x)+f(x+h)\bigr)
-\int_x^{x+h}f(t)\,dt
=\frac12\int_x^{x+h}
(t-x)(x+h-t)f''(t)\,dt.
\tag{17}
\]
Since
\[
0\leq\frac12(t-x)(x+h-t)\leq\frac{h^2}{8},
\]
summing (17) on the \(N\) intervals of length \(1/N\), and using
\(g_d(0)=g_d(1)=0\), gives
\[
\lvert A_{N,d}-I_d\rvert
\leq
\frac1{8N^2}\int_0^1\lvert g_d''(t)\rvert\,dt.
\tag{18}
\]

Suppose \(d\geq2\).  Direct differentiation gives
\[
g_d'(t)=t^{d-1}\bigl(d-(d+1)t\bigr)
\]
and
\[
g_d''(t)=dt^{d-2}\bigl((d-1)-(d+1)t\bigr).
\]
The derivative \(g_d'\) increases from \(0\) to
\[
q_d:=\left(\frac{d-1}{d+1}\right)^{d-1}
\]
at \(t=(d-1)/(d+1)\), and then decreases to \(-1\).  Hence
\[
\int_0^1\lvert g_d''(t)\rvert\,dt=1+2q_d.
\tag{19}
\]
Moreover,
\[
q_d
=\left(1+\frac2{d-1}\right)^{-(d-1)}
\leq\frac13,
\tag{20}
\]
because the binomial theorem gives
\((1+2/(d-1))^{d-1}\geq1+2=3\).  Equations (18)-(20) imply the uniform,
exact estimate
\[
\lvert A_{N,d}-I_d\rvert\leq\frac5{24N^2}
\qquad(d\geq2).
\tag{21}
\]
Using (15) at the nested meshes \(a\) and \(4a\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
4^{d+2}S_{a,d}-S_{4a,d}
&=(4a)^{d+2}(A_{a,d}-A_{4a,d})\\
&\geq
-\frac{85}{384a^2}(4a)^{d+2}\\
&=
-\frac{85}{24}\,4^da^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Substitution into (14) gives
\[
64L_{a,d}-W_{4a,d}
\geq
\left(31-\frac{85}{3}\right)4^da^d
=\frac83\,4^da^d>0
\qquad(d\geq2).
\tag{23}
\]

For \(d=1\), direct summation gives
\[
S_{N,1}=\frac{N^3-N}{6}.
\tag{24}
\]
Therefore
\[
4^3S_{a,1}-S_{4a,1}=-10a,
\]
and (14) gives the exact positive gap
\[
64L_{a,1}-W_{4a,1}=44a>0.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (23) and (25) prove
\[
L_{a,d}>\frac{W_{4a,d}}{64}
\qquad(a,d\geq1).
\tag{26}
\]

It remains to prove the calibration.  The exact estimate (21) gives
\(A_{N,d}\to I_d\) for every fixed \(d\geq2\).  For \(d=1\), (24) gives the
same conclusion, with \(I_1=1/6\).  Dividing (6) and (13) by
\((4a)^{d+2}\) gives
\[
\frac{L_{a,d}}{(4a)^{d+2}}
=\frac1{32a^2}+\frac18A_{a,d}
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
\frac{W_{4a,d}}{(4a)^{d+2}}
=\frac1{16a^2}+8A_{4a,d}.
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(I_d>0\), equations (16), (27), and (28) imply
\[
\lim_{a\to\infty}
\frac{L_{a,d}}{W_{4a,d}}
=
\frac{I_d/8}{8I_d}
=\frac1{64}
\]
for every fixed \(d\).  Together with the strict finite-\(a\) inequality
(26), this proves the asserted sharp calibration.
