## Statement

Let $\alpha\preceq_{\rm cx}\beta$ be compactly supported finite nonnegative measures of equal mass. For affine $\ell$, if $\int(\ell-t)_+\,d\alpha=\int(\ell-t)_+\,d\beta$, then
$$
\beta\{\ell\ge t\}\ge\alpha\{\ell\ge t\},\qquad
\beta\{\ell>t\}\le\alpha\{\ell>t\}.
$$
If $\beta\{\ell=t\}=0$, the corresponding cap masses agree and $\alpha\{\ell=t\}=0$. In any martingale coupling, a row starting strictly below or above threshold stays in the corresponding closed side almost surely, and a row starting on the threshold stays on it.

## Proof

For $\sigma=\alpha,\beta$ put $C_\sigma(s)=\int(\ell-s)_+\,d\sigma$. Bounded convergence for one-sided threshold quotients gives $C_\sigma'(t-)=-\sigma\{\ell\ge t\}$ and $C_\sigma'(t+)=-\sigma\{\ell>t\}$. The nonnegative difference $C_\beta-C_\alpha$ has minimum zero at $t$, so its left derivative is nonpositive and its right derivative nonnegative. This gives the stated inequalities. If the target boundary is null, the bounds $\alpha\{\ell\ge t\}\le\beta\{\ell\ge t\}=\beta\{\ell>t\}\le\alpha\{\ell>t\}$ force equality.

In a martingale coupling $(X,Y)$, the conditional Jensen defect of the hinge is nonnegative and has integral zero. Where $\ell(X)<t$, its vanishing means $\mathbb E[(\ell(Y)-t)_+\mid X]=0$, so $\ell(Y)\le t$ almost surely. Where $\ell(X)>t$, subtract the row mean $\ell(X)-t$ from the hinge expectation and use $(a)_+-a=(-a)_+$ to get $\ell(Y)\ge t$. On $\ell(X)=t$ the first argument gives $\ell(Y)\le t$ and the mean forces equality.
