## Statement

Let $\eta$ be a finite nonzero capacity measure on a compact subset of a Euclidean physical space, let $L_i$ be a finite list of affine margins, and set $W_\theta=\sum_i\theta_i\mathbf1_{\{L_i\ge0\}}$ for $\theta_i\ge0$. Fix $\beta>0$ and suppose $\mu_\theta=(W_\theta/\beta)\eta$ has the same total mass as $\eta$. Suppose neither measure charges the boundaries $\{L_i=0\}$ of the active margins ($\theta_i>0$). Assume there is a physical martingale from $\mu_\theta$ to $\eta$ and write $J_i=\int[L_i]_+\,d(\eta-\mu_\theta)$. The following conditions are equivalent.
- $\sum_i\theta_iJ_i=0$.
- $J_i=0$ for every active $i$.
- $\mu_\theta=\eta$.
- Every physical martingale between the two measures is concentrated on the identity.

The equivalence also holds for martingales required to preserve a given measurable label when at least one such martingale exists and the two measures are understood on the labelled space with $\mu_\theta=(W_\theta/\beta)\eta$. Identity here refers to the full physical coordinate.

## Proof

For a physical martingale, Jensen gives $J_i\ge0$, so the weighted sum vanishes exactly when all active deficits vanish. At a source where $L_i>0$ the affine support of $[L_i]_+$ with the same value there is $L_i$, and at $L_i<0$ it is zero. The integrated difference between the hinge at the target and this affine support is exactly $J_i$ by the martingale property. It is nonnegative and vanishes only when the target stays on the same closed side. Because boundary masses vanish for active margins, zero active deficits preserve the whole vector of active signs almost everywhere.

On each cell with a fixed active sign vector, $W_\theta/\beta$ is constant. The transport stays in the cell, so the source and target cell masses coincide. A cell of positive capacity must have factor one. It follows that $\mu_\theta=\eta$, including on a labelled space since the same density is constant throughout each cell.

For a martingale with equal full physical marginals, expansion of the squared displacement and the conditional mean identity give
$$
\int\|X'-X\|^2\,d\pi=\int\|X\|^2\,d(\eta-\mu_\theta)=0.
$$
This applies to every admissible coupling. Conversely if every physical martingale is an identity in the physical coordinates, the assumed existence gives equal physical marginals. For a preserved label the same coupling fixes both coordinates, giving equal labelled marginals. Equal marginals immediately give zero hinge deficits.
