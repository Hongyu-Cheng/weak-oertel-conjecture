## Statement

There are admissible one-sided roof bodies with roofs bounded by \(1\), anchor measures \(\nu_n\) of mass \(M_n\), and cell-depth optima \(\Delta_{B_n}\), such that
\[
\frac{\sup_{\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{2n}}D_{\nu_n}(\mathbf q)}
{\Delta_{B_n}}
\le\frac{192\log n}{n}\to0,
\]
while their actual mixed-depth optima satisfy
\[
\max_{\mathbf p\in S_n}h_{S_n}(\mathbf p)\ge\Delta_{B_n}/2.
\]
Here an admissible roof body is
\[
C=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in T,\ -\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)\},
\]
with compact convex \(T\) and nonnegative bounded upper-semicontinuous concave roofs. The anchor measure uses bounds
\(-\ell_i^-\le x_i\le\ell_i^+\). The cell density is
\(B=\prod_i(\lfloor\ell_i^-\rfloor+\lfloor\ell_i^+\rfloor+1)\).
All depths use containing closed halfspaces and unnormalized mass.

## Proof

For \(n\ge16\), set
\[
T=[0,1]^n,\quad \varepsilon=2\log n/n,\quad
\ell_i^-=0,\quad
\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)=\min\{1,y_i/(1-\varepsilon)\}.
\]
Let \(E_i=\{y_i\ge1-\varepsilon\}\). Then
\[
B=\prod_i(1+\mathbf1_{E_i}),\qquad M=(1+\varepsilon)^n.
\]
Under the normalized anchor measure, the integer coordinates are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(p=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)\).

A real center outside \([0,1]^n\) in its integer-coordinate component has depth zero. If some component \(u_i>0\), the halfspace \(x_i\ge u_i\) has anchor mass at most \(pM\). At \(\mathbf u=0\), use \(\sum x_i\le0\), with mass \((1-p)^nM\). Therefore
\[
\sup_{\mathbf q}D_\nu(\mathbf q)
\le M\max\{p,(1-p)^n\}\le\varepsilon M.
\]
The last step uses \((1+\varepsilon)^{-n}\le e^{-n\varepsilon/2}=1/n\).

The normalized base coordinates are independent with density
\[
g_\varepsilon(t)=
\frac{1+\mathbf1_{[1-\varepsilon,1]}(t)}{1+\varepsilon}.
\]
This density is at least \(1/2\). Each coordinate variance is at least \(1/24\), and its fourth centered moment is at most its variance, hence at most twenty-four times its variance squared. Independence gives, for every nonzero centered linear combination \(Z\),
\[
\mathbb EZ^4\le24(\mathbb EZ^2)^2.
\]
Interpolation yields \(\mathbb E|Z|\ge(\mathbb EZ^2)^{1/2}/\sqrt{24}\). Since \(Z\) is centered, Cauchy-Schwarz applied to \(Z_+\) gives
\(\mathbb P(Z>0)\ge1/96\), and the same holds for \(-Z\).
Thus the base barycenter has \(B\)-depth at least \(M/96\). This proves the ratio estimate.

For actual mixed depth, fix the integer vector \(\mathbf1\). A section is \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{0,1,2\}\) in each coordinate. Its same-fiber reflection count about \(1\) is respectively \(1\) or \(3\), at least the corresponding cell count \(1\) or \(2\). Hence the full reflected density \(R_{\mathbf1}\) satisfies \(R_{\mathbf1}\ge B\).

For every ambient halfspace through \((\mathbf1,\mathbf y_0)\), restrict to the favorable base halfspace. At least half the reflected section points lie in the ambient halfspace. Therefore
\[
h_S((\mathbf1,\mathbf y_0))
\ge\tfrac12\tau_{R_{\mathbf1}}(\mathbf y_0)
\ge\tfrac12\tau_B(\mathbf y_0).
\]
Choose a maximizer of \(\tau_B\).
