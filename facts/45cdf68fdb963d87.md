## Statement

Let \(M\le N\) be integers, let \(F:\{M,\ldots,N\}\to\mathbb R\) be any function, and let \(p\) be a probability mass function whose positive support is a contiguous subinterval of \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\), with \(p_y^2\ge p_{y-1}p_{y+1}\) at every integer after extending the masses by zero. Set \(k=\sum_y yp_y\) and \(j=\sum_y F(y)p_y\). These two means need not be integral.

There are an integer \(1\le A\le N-M+1\), positive weights \(\pi_\ell\) summing to one, and probability laws \(\nu^{(\ell)}\) such that
\[
 p_y=\sum_{\ell=1}^A\pi_\ell\nu_y^{(\ell)},\qquad
 \sum_y y\nu_y^{(\ell)}=k,\qquad
 \sum_y F(y)\nu_y^{(\ell)}=j
 \quad(1\le\ell\le A).
\]
Each component is supported on an integer interval \(\{a_\ell,\ldots,b_\ell\}\subseteq\{M,\ldots,N\}\), has strictly positive masses there, and is of one of three forms.

A singleton component is \(\delta_{a_\ell}\), necessarily with \(a_\ell=k\) and \(F(a_\ell)=j\). A geometric interval component has masses \(C_\ell e^{s_\ell y}\), for finite \(s_\ell\in\mathbb R\). The remaining form has masses
\[
 \nu_y^{(\ell)}=C_\ell
  \exp\!\left(s_\ell y-t_\ell(y-c_\ell)_+\right)
  \quad(a_\ell\le y\le b_\ell),\qquad
 a_\ell<c_\ell<b_\ell,\quad c_\ell\in\mathbb Z,\quad t_\ell>0,
\]
where \(s_\ell\) is finite, \(C_\ell>0\) normalizes the masses, and \((y-c)_+=\max\{y-c,0\}\). All masses are zero outside the stated interval. In the last form \(c_\ell\) is the sole interior index with strict log-concavity. Every nonsingleton component has \(a_\ell<k<b_\ell\). These are necessary forms of the extreme components used in the decomposition; no converse assertion that every law of these forms is extreme is made.

In particular, let \(m,h,L\ge1\) be integers, \(\beta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\), and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\). Use the original weighted-simplex notation
\[
 E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:
                  \sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\},\qquad
 R(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{\exp(\beta\sum_i x_i+\theta y)}
                        {\sum_{(\mathbf z,v)\in E_{L,h,m}}
                           \exp(\beta\sum_i z_i+\theta v)}.
\]
Suppose its full mean is \((j\mathbf1,k)\), with \(j,k\in\mathbb Z\). For nonnegative integers \(B\), define
\[
 Z_m(B,\beta)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\\
                              \sum_i x_i\le B}}
                          e^{\beta\sum_i x_i},\qquad
 K_y(\mathbf x)=\frac{e^{\beta\sum_i x_i}}{Z_m(L-hy,\beta)}
       \mathbf1_{\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m,\ \sum_i x_i\le L-hy\}}.
\]
Here \(K_y\) is defined for \(0\le y\le\lfloor L/h\rfloor\). The height marginal and the original conditional transverse mean are
\[
 p_y=\frac{e^{\theta y}Z_m(L-hy,\beta)}
                {\sum_{v=0}^{\lfloor L/h\rfloor}
                    e^{\theta v}Z_m(L-hv,\beta)},\qquad
 f_\beta(y)=\sum_{\mathbf x}x_1K_y(\mathbf x)
 \quad(0\le y\le\lfloor L/h\rfloor).
\]
This \(p\) is log-concave with contiguous positive support, and the general theorem applies with \(M=0\), \(N=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\), and \(F=f_\beta\). Attach the unchanged original kernel \(K_y\) to each resulting height component, defining \(R_\ell(\mathbf x,y)=\nu_y^{(\ell)}K_y(\mathbf x)\) on these heights and zero elsewhere. Then \(R=\sum_\ell\pi_\ell R_\ell\) exactly at every joint mass, and every \(R_\ell\) has the original full mean \((j\mathbf1,k)\). This mean belongs to \(E_{L,h,m}\). The assertion supplies neither a fourth-moment bound nor a cap bound for these components.

## Proof

First prove the general scalar decomposition. Regard laws on \(\{M,\ldots,N\}\) as vectors with \(N-M+1\) coordinates. Let \(\mathcal S\) be the class of log-concave probability laws with contiguous positive support inside this interval satisfying both equalities \(\sum_y yr_y=k\) and \(\sum_y F(y)r_y=j\). The original \(p\) belongs to \(\mathcal S\).

The class \(\mathcal S\) is compact. All its requirements except contiguity pass directly to coordinatewise limits. To check that requirement, suppose \(r^{(v)}\to r\) with each \(r^{(v)}\in\mathcal S\), and \(r_a,r_b>0\), where \(a<b\). For all large \(v\), both endpoint masses are positive, so contiguity makes every mass between them positive. The local log-concavity inequalities make the successive differences of \(\log r_y^{(v)}\) nonincreasing. Consequently, for \(a<y<b\),
\[
 r_y^{(v)}\ge
   (r_a^{(v)})^{(b-y)/(b-a)}
   (r_b^{(v)})^{(y-a)/(b-a)}.
\]
For completeness, the inequality follows by comparing the average of the first \(y-a\) successive log differences with the average of all \(b-a\) differences. Taking limits gives \(r_y>0\). Thus \(r\) has no internal zero, and \(\mathcal S\) is closed in the compact probability simplex.

Its convex hull \(\mathcal T=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal S)\) is also compact. Indeed, any finite convex combination with more than \(N-M+1\) terms has an affine dependence among its mass vectors, since they lie in the hyperplane of total mass one. Write that dependence as \(\sum_i d_i r^{(i)}=0\), \(\sum_i d_i=0\), with some \(d_i>0\). After deleting zero weights, replace weights \(w_i\) by \(w_i-\varepsilon d_i\), where \(\varepsilon=\min_{d_i>0}w_i/d_i\). This preserves the represented point and the total weight, keeps weights nonnegative, and deletes at least one term. Repeating leaves at most \(N-M+1\) terms. Padding shorter combinations with zero weights shows that \(\mathcal T\) is the continuous image of the compact product of a weight simplex and \(N-M+1\) copies of \(\mathcal S\).

Every point of a nonempty compact convex set in finite dimensions is a finite convex combination of its extreme points. Here is a proof of the precise finite statement used. Induct on the affine dimension. Dimension zero is immediate. At a relative boundary point \(z\) of a positive-dimensional compact convex set \(T\), choose points \(u_v\notin T\) in its affine hull tending to \(z\), and nearest points \(z_v\in T\) to \(u_v\). Then \(z_v\to z\). Minimizing squared distance along the segment from \(z_v\) to an arbitrary \(x\in T\) gives
\[
 \frac{u_v-z_v}{\|u_v-z_v\|}\cdot(x-z_v)\le0.
\]
A subsequence of these unit vectors converges to a unit vector \(w\) in the direction space of the affine hull. Hence \(w\cdot(x-z)\le0\) for every \(x\in T\). Its equality set is a nonempty compact exposed face of smaller affine dimension: if it had the full affine dimension, \(w\) would be orthogonal to the entire direction space, contradicting that \(w\) is a unit vector in that space. By induction, \(z\) is a finite convex combination of extreme points of this face. Each is extreme in \(T\), because equality in the displayed supporting inequality for a positive convex combination forces equality for both constituent points. A relative interior point lies strictly between the endpoints of the compact segment cut out by any line through it in a nonzero affine-hull direction. Those endpoints are relative boundary points, so combining their finite representations proves the assertion for that point too.

Apply this assertion to \(\mathcal T\) and its point \(p\), then use the preceding affine-dependence reduction to retain at most \(N-M+1\) extreme points and delete zero weights. Every extreme point \(\nu\) of \(\mathcal T\) belongs to \(\mathcal S\): by the definition of convex hull it is a finite convex combination of members of \(\mathcal S\), and extremality forces all positive-weight members to equal \(\nu\). Thus every retained component already satisfies both mean equalities. It remains to prove the claimed component forms.

Fix such an extreme point \(\nu\), with positive support \(\{a,\ldots,b\}\). Singleton support has the asserted form and means. For nonsingleton support let \(J\subseteq\{a+1,\ldots,b-1\}\) consist of the strict log-concavity indices, namely those \(y\) with \(\nu_y^2>\nu_{y-1}\nu_{y+1}\). Let \(V\) be the real vector space of functions \(u\) on this interval satisfying
\[
 2u_y=u_{y-1}+u_{y+1}
 \quad\text{whenever }y\in\{a+1,\ldots,b-1\}\setminus J.
\]
Its dimension is \(2+|J|\). One can choose \(u_a,u_{a+1}\) freely, and then compute each successive value from its second difference; that second difference is forced to zero at the stated indices and is free at each index in \(J\). This also gives dimension two when \(b=a+1\).

If \(\nu\) had at least two strict indices, this dimension would be at least four. Therefore the following three homogeneous linear equations would have a nonzero solution \(u\in V\):
\[
 \sum_{y=a}^b\nu_yu_y=0,\qquad
 \sum_{y=a}^b y\nu_yu_y=0,\qquad
 \sum_{y=a}^b F(y)\nu_yu_y=0.
\]
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), define \(\nu_y^\pm=\nu_y(1\pm\varepsilon u_y)\) on the support and zero elsewhere. Choose \(\varepsilon\) first small enough that both laws are positive on the interval. The displayed three equations give total mass one and the same two means for both laws. At an interior index with equality in the original log-concavity inequality, membership in \(V\) gives \(2u_y=u_{y-1}+u_{y+1}\), and an exact calculation yields
\[
 (\nu_y^\pm)^2-\nu_{y-1}^\pm\nu_{y+1}^\pm
 =\frac{\varepsilon^2\nu_y^2}{4}
                 (u_{y-1}-u_{y+1})^2\ge0.
\]
At every strict index, the same difference is positive for both signs when \(\varepsilon\) is small enough, by continuity. There are only finitely many indices, so one choice works for all of them. At support endpoints and outside the interval the inequalities follow from the zero external masses. Thus \(\nu^+,\nu^-\in\mathcal S\), they are distinct because \(u\ne0\), and \(\nu=(\nu^++\nu^-)/2\). This contradicts extremality in \(\mathcal T\). Hence \(\nu\) has at most one strict index.

If there is no strict index, all successive differences of \(\log\nu_y\) are equal, giving the geometric form. If there is exactly one strict index \(c\), these differences are constant on \(\{a+1,\ldots,c\}\) and on \(\{c+1,\ldots,b\}\), with a strictly smaller constant on the latter set. Denote the first constant by \(s\) and their positive difference by \(t\). Summing the differences gives precisely \(\nu_y=C\exp(sy-t(y-c)_+)\). Finally, positive mass at both endpoints of any nonsingleton interval gives \(\mathbb E_\nu(Y-a)>0\) and \(\mathbb E_\nu(b-Y)>0\), so \(a<k<b\). This completes the scalar theorem, including both constraints and the component count.

Now verify its applicability to the original tilted weighted-simplex law. The number of nonnegative \(m\)-vectors with coordinate sum exactly \(s\) is \(\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}\): insert \(m-1\) separators into a string of \(s\) units, allowing empty parts. Write \(w_s=\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}e^{\beta s}>0\), so \(Z_m(B,\beta)=\sum_{s=0}^B w_s\). The successive ratios satisfy \(w_{s+1}/w_s=e^\beta(s+m)/(s+1)\), and are nonincreasing in \(s\ge0\), including \(m=1\). For \(B\ge1\) this yields \(w_{B+1}w_{i-1}\le w_Bw_i\) for \(1\le i\le B\). Summing gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,\beta)
   &\le w_B\sum_{i=1}^B w_i
    < w_BZ_m(B,\beta),\\
 Z_m(B,\beta)^2-Z_m(B-1,\beta)Z_m(B+1,\beta)
   &=w_BZ_m(B,\beta)-w_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,\beta)>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the logarithm of \(Z_m(B,\beta)\) is a concave sequence on nonnegative integers. For any \(B\ge h\), its nonincreasing successive differences imply
\[
 \log Z_m(B,\beta)-\log Z_m(B-h,\beta)
 \ge \log Z_m(B+h,\beta)-\log Z_m(B,\beta),
\]
by comparing the \(h\) preceding differences with the \(h\) following ones. Taking \(B=L-hy\) at an interior height proves log-concavity of the subsequence \(Z_m(L-hy,\beta)\). Multiplication by \(e^{\theta y}\) and normalization preserve the local log-concavity inequalities. All its masses on \(\{0,\ldots,\lfloor L/h\rfloor\}\) are positive, so the contiguity hypothesis holds. If this interval is a singleton the conclusion is immediate.

Permutation symmetry of \(K_y\) gives \(\mathbb E[X_i\mid Y=y]=f_\beta(y)\) for every \(1\le i\le m\). Equivalently,
\[
 f_\beta(y)=\frac{\sum_{s=0}^{L-hy}s\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}e^{\beta s}}
                        {mZ_m(L-hy,\beta)}.
\]
The original full-mean assumption is therefore exactly \(\sum_y yp_y=k\) and \(\sum_y f_\beta(y)p_y=j\). Apply the scalar theorem with this specific function, without imposing any unproved shape property on it. At every allowed height the component lift retains the same \(K_y\), so the exact mixture identity follows directly from
\[
 R(\mathbf x,y)=p_yK_y(\mathbf x)
   =\sum_\ell\pi_\ell\nu_y^{(\ell)}K_y(\mathbf x).
\]
For each component its height mean is \(k\), and for every transverse coordinate its mean is \(\sum_y f_\beta(y)\nu_y^{(\ell)}=j\). This proves preservation of the entire original mean rather than only its height coordinate. Lastly, expectation of the defining inequality of \(E_{L,h,m}\) gives \(mj+hk\le L\); nonnegativity and the assumed integrality of \(j,k\) imply \((j\mathbf1,k)\in E_{L,h,m}\). All steps use the original tilt and original conditional kernels. No estimate on lifted component moments has been used.
