## Statement

For every positive integer \(m\), put \(\varepsilon=3^{-m}\) and \(n=d=3^{2m}\), and define
\[
C=C_m=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n:
\begin{array}{ll}
0\le x_i\le2,&1\le i\le n,\\
2|y_i|\le1,&1\le i\le n,\\
2|y_i|+(1-\varepsilon)x_i\le2-\varepsilon,&1\le i\le n
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Use the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^n\) and \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\) and \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_n(C_z)\). Here \(w_z\) always means an actual, unnormalized fiber volume. Define the actual mixed-volume measure on Borel sets \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}\) by
\[
\mu_C(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_n\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in A\},
\qquad W=\mu_C(\mathbb R^{2n}).
\]
Thus \(\mu_C(A)=\mathcal H_n(S\cap A)\) in the locked normalization, and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H)\), over closed ambient halfspaces.

The set \(C\) is a full-dimensional compact rational convex polytope. Its nonempty lattice fibers all have positive volume, their base support is \(D=\{0,1,2\}^n\), and, with \(k(z)=|\{i:z_i=2\}|\), one has \(w_z=\varepsilon^{k(z)}\) for \(z\in D\) and \(w_z=0\) otherwise. In particular, \(\max_z w_z=1\), \(W=(2+\varepsilon)^n\), and
\[
2^{-n}W=\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^n
\ge \exp\left(\frac{3^m}{4}\right)\longrightarrow\infty
\quad\text{as }m\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{1}
\]

The symbol \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\) below denotes a fourth-moment constant, with no relation to a curvature score. Let \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\ge3\) be real, let \(M>0\), and let \(\nu\) be any Borel probability measure on \(\mathbb R^{2n}\) such that \(M\nu(A)\le\mu_C(A)\) for every Borel set \(A\). Write \((Z,Y)\sim\nu\) and \(p_\nu=\mathbb E_\nu(Z,Y)\). Suppose \(\mathbb E_\nu Z\in\mathbb Z^n\) and, for every \(u\in\mathbb R^{2n}\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu\langle u,(Z,Y)-p_\nu\rangle^4
\le K_{\mathrm{mom}}
\left(\mathbb E_\nu\langle u,(Z,Y)-p_\nu\rangle^2\right)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(r\) be the number of nonconstant base coordinates under \(\nu\), and let \(t\) be the number of base coordinates equal to two almost surely. Then, with natural logarithms,
\[
\log M\le\log3\left[
r\left(1-\frac{m}{2K_{\mathrm{mom}}}\right)-mt
\right].
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, if \(m\ge2K_{\mathrm{mom}}\), the maximum possible admissible mass \(M\) is exactly one. It is attained by uniform probability on the central cube \(\{\mathbf1_n\}\times[-1/2,1/2]^n\). Without imposing \(m\ge2K_{\mathrm{mom}}\), the quantitative implication is \(M>1\Longrightarrow K_{\mathrm{mom}}>m/2=\log n/(4\log3)\).

Fix \(p=(\mathbf1_n,\mathbf0_n)\in S\) and let \(B=C\cap(2p-C)\). For Borel \(A\), define \(\mu_B(A)=\mu_C(A\cap B)\), the actual mixed volume restricted to \(B\). The set \(B\) is a full-dimensional compact rational convex polytope centrally symmetric about \(p\), and \(\mu_B(\mathbb R^{2n})=(1+2\varepsilon)^n\). For every proper closed halfspace \(H_0\) with \(p\in\partial H_0\),
\[
2\mu_B(H_0)=(1+2\varepsilon)^n+\mu_B(\partial H_0).
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, for every closed halfspace \(H\) containing the same fixed point \(p\),
\[
\mu_C(H)\ge\frac12(1+2\varepsilon)^n
\ge\frac12\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
h_S(p)\ge\frac12(1+2\varepsilon)^n.
\tag{5}
\]
The normalized probability \(\widehat\nu_B=\mu_B/(1+2\varepsilon)^n\) has mean \(p\) and exactly half-depth there: \(\inf_{H\ni p}\widehat\nu_B(H)=1/2\). Nevertheless, for every base coordinate its kurtosis is
\[
\frac{\mathbb E_{\widehat\nu_B}(Z_i-1)^4}
{\bigl(\mathbb E_{\widehat\nu_B}(Z_i-1)^2\bigr)^2}
=\frac{1+2\varepsilon}{2\varepsilon}
=1+\frac{3^m}{2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{6}
\]
Therefore no absolute constants \(\eta>0\) and finite positive \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\) guarantee, even for every member of this family, a probability measure \(\nu\) with integer base mean, the directional bound (2), and domination \(\eta 2^{-n}W\nu\le\mu_C\). In particular, the corresponding assertion for all positive-mass convex bodies in the locked problem is false.

## Proof

Each inequality containing an absolute value in the definition of \(C\) is equivalent to two rational linear inequalities, since \(\varepsilon\) is rational. Thus \(C\) is a closed rational polyhedron. It is bounded by \(0\le x_i\le2\) and \(|y_i|\le1/2\), so it is compact. All defining inequalities hold strictly throughout \((0,1)^n\times(-1/2,1/2)^n\): in particular, there \(2|y_i|+(1-\varepsilon)x_i<1+(1-\varepsilon)=2-\varepsilon\). Hence \(C\) is full-dimensional and is a rational convex polytope.

For an integer base point outside \(D\), some coordinate violates \(0\le z_i\le2\), and its fiber is empty. At \(z\in D\), the conditions on \(y_i\) are \(2|y_i|\le\min\{1,2-\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)z_i\}\). This minimum is one for \(z_i=0\) or \(z_i=1\), and is \(\varepsilon\) for \(z_i=2\). The fiber is therefore a centered box with exactly these side lengths. Every such box has positive volume, so the stated support and formula for \(w_z\) follow. Summing the product of side lengths over all \(z\in D\) yields \(W=(2+\varepsilon)^n\); a fiber with no coordinate equal to two has volume one, and no fiber is larger.

For \(0\le s\le1\), one has \(\log(1+s)=\int_0^s(1+v)^{-1}\,dv\ge s/2\). Apply this with \(s=\varepsilon/2\), which lies in \((0,1)\), to get
\[
\log(2^{-n}W)
=n\log(1+\varepsilon/2)
\ge\frac{n\varepsilon}{4}
=\frac{3^m}{4}.
\]
Exponentiation proves (1).

Now fix arbitrary \(M,K_{\mathrm{mom}},\nu\) as in the statement. The complement of \(S\) has \(\mu_C\)-measure zero. Domination and \(M>0\) therefore imply \(\nu(S)=1\), so \(Z\in D\) almost surely. All moments used below are finite because \(S\) is bounded. In fact, \(p_\nu\in S\): each of the finitely many defining linear inequalities of \(C\) passes to expectations, and its base is integer by hypothesis.

Let \(q_z=\nu(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^n)\) for \(z\in D\), and let \(Q=\{z\in D:q_z>0\}\). The \(q_z\) sum to one. Evaluating domination on a whole fiber gives the exact capacity inequality \(Mq_z\le w_z=\varepsilon^{k(z)}\).

Put \(j=\mathbb E_\nu Z\). Since \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(0\le Z_i\le2\), each \(j_i\) is zero, one, or two. If \(j_i=0\), the nonnegative variable \(Z_i\) has expectation zero and is zero almost surely. If \(j_i=2\), the same argument applies to \(2-Z_i\). Let \(I\) be the set of nonconstant base coordinates, so \(|I|=r\). Thus \(j_i=1\) for every \(i\in I\), and the other coordinates are fixed at zero, one, or two.

For \(i\in I\), the equations \(\mathbb EZ_i=1\) and \(\sum_{a=0}^2\mathbb P(Z_i=a)=1\) imply \(\mathbb P(Z_i=0)=\mathbb P(Z_i=2)\). Set \(\tau_i=\mathbb P(Z_i\ne1)>0\). Because \(Z_i-1\in\{-1,0,1\}\), one has \(\mathbb E(Z_i-1)^2=\mathbb E(Z_i-1)^4=\tau_i\) and \(\mathbb P(Z_i=2)=\tau_i/2\). Applying (2) to the direction selecting the \(i\)-th base coordinate yields \(\tau_i\le K_{\mathrm{mom}}\tau_i^2\). Division by the positive \(\tau_i\) shows \(\tau_i\ge1/K_{\mathrm{mom}}\). Linearity of expectation now gives
\[
\mathbb E_\nu k(Z)
=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb P(Z_i=2)
=t+\frac12\sum_{i\in I}\tau_i
\ge t+\frac{r}{2K_{\mathrm{mom}}}.
\tag{7}
\]
This uses only coordinate consequences of (2); it requires no independence.

All coordinates outside \(I\) are fixed, so \(|Q|\le3^r\). Define the finite entropy \(H(q)=-\sum_{z\in Q}q_z\log q_z\). Concavity of the logarithm gives
\[
H(q)=\sum_{z\in Q}q_z\log\frac1{q_z}
\le\log\left(\sum_{z\in Q}q_z\frac1{q_z}\right)
=\log|Q|\le r\log3.
\tag{8}
\]
For each \(z\in Q\), taking logarithms in \(Mq_z\le\varepsilon^{k(z)}\) gives \(\log M+\log q_z\le k(z)\log\varepsilon\). Multiply by \(q_z\), sum, and use (7)-(8) and \(\log\varepsilon=-m\log3<0\). The result is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log M
&\le H(q)+(\log\varepsilon)\mathbb E_\nu k(Z)\\
&\le r\log3-m\log3\left(t+\frac{r}{2K_{\mathrm{mom}}}\right)\\
&=\log3\left[r\left(1-\frac{m}{2K_{\mathrm{mom}}}\right)-mt\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (3), including \(r=0\), when \(Q\) is a singleton and its entropy is zero. If \(m\ge2K_{\mathrm{mom}}\), both terms in the bracket are nonpositive, so \(M\le1\). If \(M>1\), the bracket must be positive. Since \(t\ge0\), this forces \(r>0\) and \(1-m/(2K_{\mathrm{mom}})>0\). Thus \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}>m/2\). The identity \(\log n=2m\log3\) gives the stated dimensional form.

For attainment, take \(Z=\mathbf1_n\) deterministically and let the \(Y_i\) be independent uniform variables on \([-1/2,1/2]\). This is uniform probability \(\nu\) on the central fiber, whose volume is one. For every Borel \(A\), \(\nu(A)\) equals that fiber's contribution to \(\mu_C(A)\), so \(\nu\le\mu_C\). Its mean is \(p=(\mathbf1_n,\mathbf0_n)\), and its base mean is integer. Direct integration gives \(\mathbb EY_i=0\), \(\mathbb EY_i^2=1/12\), and \(\mathbb EY_i^4=1/80\). An arbitrary centered ambient linear form is \(T=\sum_i b_iY_i\), because the centered base coordinates vanish. Independence and zero means eliminate every term in the fourth-power expansion having a coordinate with odd exponent. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb ET^2&=\frac1{12}\sum_i b_i^2,\\
\mathbb ET^4
&=\frac1{80}\sum_i b_i^4
+\frac6{144}\sum_{i<j}b_i^2b_j^2\\
&=3(\mathbb ET^2)^2-\frac1{120}\sum_i b_i^4
\le3(\mathbb ET^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This also covers \(b=0\), when both moments vanish. Thus (2) holds for this witness with constant three and with any larger \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\). It is admissible for \(M=1\), proving the exact optimum whenever \(m\ge2K_{\mathrm{mom}}\). Independence was used only to verify this particular witness.

We next verify the symmetric depth certificate. The point \(p\) satisfies every defining linear inequality of \(C\) strictly, so it lies in the interior of \(C\), and its base is integer. Let \(R(x,y)=(2\mathbf1_n-x,-y)\), reflection through \(p\). Then \(R^2\) is the identity and \(B=C\cap R(C)\) satisfies \(R(B)=B\). Since \(R(p)=p\) and \(p\) is interior to both bodies, \(B\) is full-dimensional. It is also compact, convex, and rational, as an intersection of two such polytopes.

For \(z\in D\), the fiber of \(B\) is \(B_z=C_z\cap(-C_{2\mathbf1_n-z})\). Each fiber of \(C\) is centered, so the minus sign leaves it unchanged. Intersecting the two boxes gives side length one when \(z_i=1\), and side length \(\varepsilon\) when \(z_i=0\) or two. Fibers outside \(D\) are empty. Therefore \(\operatorname{vol}_n(B_z)=\varepsilon^{|\{i:z_i\ne1\}|}\) on \(D\), and summing these product weights yields \(\mu_B(\mathbb R^{2n})=(1+2\varepsilon)^n\).

Reflection maps the mixed lattice to itself: the integer base \(z\) maps to \(2\mathbf1_n-z\). On each continuous fiber, \(y\mapsto-y\) preserves \(n\)-dimensional volume. Reindexing the finite fiber sum and using \(R(B)=B\) thus proves \(\mu_B(R(A))=\mu_B(A)\) for every Borel \(A\). Boundedness and this symmetry imply that the mean of \(\widehat\nu_B\) is \(p\): the mean is fixed by the affine map \(R\), whose only fixed point is \(p\).

Write a proper closed halfspace through \(p\) as \(H_0=\{v:\langle u,v-p\rangle\ge0\}\) with \(u\ne0\). Its reflection is the opposite closed halfspace \(\{v:\langle u,v-p\rangle\le0\}\). Their union is the whole space and their intersection is \(\partial H_0\). Inclusion-exclusion and reflection invariance give (4). In particular, the boundary term is nonnegative. It has not been discarded and may be positive when the hyperplane contains entire lattice fibers.

Every proper closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(p\) can be written as \(\{v:\langle u,v\rangle\ge a\}\) with \(u\ne0\) and \(a\le\langle u,p\rangle\). It contains the parallel halfspace \(H_0=\{v:\langle u,v\rangle\ge\langle u,p\rangle\}\) through \(p\). Since \(\mu_B\le\mu_C\), monotonicity and (4) give \(\mu_C(H)\ge\mu_B(H_0)\ge(1+2\varepsilon)^n/2\). If the whole space is included as an improper halfspace, the same bound holds because its mass is \(W\ge\mu_B(\mathbb R^{2n})\). Finally, \(1+2\varepsilon\ge1+\varepsilon/2\), so (1) gives the comparison with \(2^{-n}W/2\) in (5). The point \(p\) was fixed before choosing any halfspace.

Dividing (4) by \((1+2\varepsilon)^n\), together with the same parallel-halfspace argument, gives \(\widehat\nu_B(H)\ge1/2\) for all closed \(H\ni p\). The particular halfspace \(\{(x,y):y_1\ge0\}\) has boundary of \(\mu_B\)-measure zero: in each of the finitely many fibers, \(y_1=0\) has \(n\)-dimensional volume zero, with \(n\ge1\). Equation (4) therefore gives probability exactly \(1/2\) for this halfspace. This proves the asserted exact half-depth.

Under \(\widehat\nu_B\), summing the other coordinates' product weights shows that each base coordinate has probabilities \(\varepsilon/(1+2\varepsilon),1/(1+2\varepsilon),\varepsilon/(1+2\varepsilon)\) at zero, one, and two, respectively. Thus its mean is one and both \(\mathbb E(Z_i-1)^2\) and \(\mathbb E(Z_i-1)^4\) equal \(2\varepsilon/(1+2\varepsilon)>0\). Dividing the fourth moment by the squared second moment proves (6). In particular, any all-direction fourth-moment constant for this normalized symmetric measure must be at least \(1+3^m/2\), although its depth at its mean is exactly one half.

Finally, suppose absolute constants \(\eta>0\) and finite positive \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\) guaranteed the universal core assertion in the statement. Enlarging its moment constant to \(\max\{3,K_{\mathrm{mom}}\}\) preserves that assertion, so assume \(K_{\mathrm{mom}}\ge3\). Choose an integer \(m\) with \(m\ge2K_{\mathrm{mom}}\) and \(\eta\exp(3^m/4)>1\). The asserted core for \(C_m\) would satisfy the hypotheses above with \(M=\eta2^{-n}W>1\), by (1), contradicting (3). This proves the universal obstruction. The independently established (5) supplies a valid depth point for every body in this counterexample family.
