## Statement

Let \(n\ge100\) be an integer, let \(1\le k\le n/100\) and \(2/5<\beta\le1\) be rational, and put \(R=n/2+k\). Indices on \(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^n\) are cyclic, so \(t_0=t_n\). Define

\[
C_\beta=\left\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\in\mathbb R^{3n}:
\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0,\quad
y_i\ge\left|z_i-\frac12-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})\right|\ (1\le i\le n),\quad
\sum_i y_i+\sum_i t_i\le R\right\}.
\]

Retain the exact meanings of \(S=C_\beta\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{2n})\), the actual fibers \(C_{\mathbf z}\), their volumes \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_{2n}(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\), and the actual halfspace depth \(h_S\) from accepted fact \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\). Its continuous variable is the ordered pair \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t)\), and its continuous dimension is \(d=2n\).

Put \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and let \(\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^n\) have \(j_{2r-1}=1\) for \(1\le r\le m\) and every other coordinate zero. Then

\[
0<W\le e^{1700}\,2^n w_{\mathbf j}
\le e^{1700}\,2^n\max_{\mathbf a\in\{0,1\}^n}w_{\mathbf a}.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, there exists one \(\mathbf p^\star\in S\) in the fiber over \(\mathbf j\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf p^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_{2n}(S\cap H)\ge e^{-1700}W/(12\,2^n)\).

## Proof

The body is convex and closed. Its coordinates \(y_i,t_i\) are nonnegative and have bounded total sum, and the inequalities then bound every \(z_i\), so it is compact. Taking \(z_i=1/2\), \(t_i=y_i=\delta\), with \(0<2n\delta<R\), gives a point satisfying all defining inequalities strictly. Thus \(C_\beta\) is a full-dimensional convex body. Its base projection is bounded, so only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty.

For each integer base put \(F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)=\sum_i t_i+\sum_i|z_i-1/2-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})|\). Subtracting the absolute values from the coordinates \(y_i\) leaves a nonnegative \(n\)-simplex of radius \(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\). Fubini therefore gives the original full fiber volume

\[
w_{\mathbf z}=\frac1{n!}\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}
\bigl(R-F_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf t)\bigr)_+^n\,d\mathbf t.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, every Boolean fiber has positive volume: at \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\) the value of \(F_{\mathbf z}\) is \(n/2<R\), and continuity permits strictly positive coordinates \(\mathbf t\) and strictly positive residual \(\mathbf y\)-slacks while keeping strict total budget. Hence \(w_{\mathbf j}>0\) and \(W>0\).

We first obtain an upper bound summing every integer base. Let \(\tau>0\) be an exponential rate, to be chosen later, and write \(q=e^{-\tau}\). Define the one-periodic function

\[
\theta_\tau(x)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\tau|a-x|}.
\]

For \(0\le x\le1\), summing the two geometric series gives \(\theta_\tau(x)=(e^{-\tau x}+e^{-\tau(1-x)})/(1-q)\). In particular \(\theta_\tau(1/2+u)=\theta_\tau(1/2-u)\). Define the symmetric nonnegative kernel on \(\mathbb R_+^2\)
\(K_\tau(t,u)=e^{-\tau(t+u)/2}\theta_\tau(1/2+\beta(t-u))\), and put

\[
J(\tau)=\tau\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau u}
\theta_\tau(1/2+\beta u)^2\,du.
\tag{3}
\]

These functions are finite because \(\theta_\tau\) is bounded. On the original domain \(y_i\ge|z_i-1/2-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})|\), bound the indicator of the total-budget inequality by \(\exp(\tau(R-\sum_i y_i-\sum_i t_i))\). Integrating first over the residual \(\mathbf y\)-slacks then bounds the full mixed volume by

\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&\le e^{\tau R}\tau^{-n}
\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}e^{-\tau\sum_i t_i}
\prod_{i=1}^n\theta_\tau(1/2+\beta(t_i-t_{i-1}))\,d\mathbf t\\
&=e^{\tau R}\tau^{-n}
\int_{\mathbb R_+^n}\prod_{i=1}^nK_\tau(t_i,t_{i-1})\,d\mathbf t\\
&\le e^{\tau R}\tau^{-2n}J(\tau)^{n/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Here is a direct justification of the last inequality, including odd \(n\). For square-integrable kernels, pointwise Cauchy--Schwarz followed by integration gives \(\|A*B\|_2\le\|A\|_2\|B\|_2\), where \((A*B)(x,y)=\int_0^\infty A(x,u)B(u,y)\,du\), and the norm is in \(L^2(\mathbb R_+^2)\). Iteration and one final \(L^2\) Cauchy--Schwarz inequality bound any \(n\)-cycle integral by \(\|K_\tau\|_2^n\), for every integer \(n\ge2\). Splitting the integral of \(K_\tau^2\) into \(t\ge u\) and \(u\ge t\), using its symmetry, and integrating the smaller variable gives
\(\|K_\tau\|_2^2=\tau^{-1}\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau u}\theta_\tau(1/2+\beta u)^2\,du=J(\tau)/\tau^2\).
This proves (4). All interchanges use nonnegative integrands. In particular, the summation leading to (4) includes every integer base, irrespective of its support or distance from the Boolean cube.

We next construct a product region inside the specified Boolean fiber. Put \(b=2\beta\), \(c=1+b\), \(\epsilon=n-2m\in\{0,1\}\), and \(h(a)=a+|1-ba|\) for \(a\ge0\). The coefficient \(b\) is a scalar parameter; it is not a base point or parity class. The triangle inequality on the two coordinates adjacent to each \(t_{2r-1}\) gives

\[
F_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf t)
\le \frac{\epsilon}{2}+\sum_{i:j_i=1}h(t_i)
+c\sum_{i:j_i=0}t_i.
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, at coordinates \(2r-1\) and \(2r\), the two residual absolute values are bounded by \(|1/2-\beta t_{2r-1}|+\beta t_{2r-2}\) and \(|1/2-\beta t_{2r-1}|+\beta t_{2r}\), respectively. If \(n\) is odd, the remaining coordinate has absolute value at most \(1/2+\beta(t_n+t_{n-1})\). Thus every \(t_i\) with \(j_i=0\) receives total absolute-value coefficient \(2\beta=b\), and its own cost adds one. This proves (5) also at the two consecutive zero coordinates when \(n\) is odd.

Let \(\mathbf r\ge\mathbf0\) denote continuous residual \(\mathbf y\)-slacks, and set
\(E(\mathbf t,\mathbf r)=\epsilon/2+\sum_{i:j_i=1}h(t_i)+c\sum_{i:j_i=0}t_i+\sum_i r_i\).
The region \(E\le R\) maps into \(C_{\mathbf j}\) by
\(y_i=|j_i-1/2-\beta(t_i-t_{i-1})|+r_i\).
For each fixed \(\mathbf t\), this is a translation in \(\mathbf r\), so it preserves the \(2n\)-dimensional volume by Fubini. This is a lower region obtained from the full absolute values; no restriction on their signs is imposed.

Define \(I(\tau)=\tau\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau h(a)}\,da>0\). The full exponential integral of this product region is

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_n(\tau)
&:=\int_{\mathbb R_+^{2n}}e^{-\tau E(\mathbf t,\mathbf r)}\,d\mathbf t\,d\mathbf r\\
&=\tau^{-2n}I(\tau)^m c^{-(n-m)}q^{\epsilon/2}\\
&=\tau^{-2n}\left(\frac{I(\tau)}c\right)^{n/2}
\left(\frac{q}{cI(\tau)}\right)^{\epsilon/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

We choose \(\tau\) in a way that returns (6) to the fixed budget with a controlled loss. Put \(h_{\min}=\min\{1,1/b\}\) and \(E_0=mh_{\min}+\epsilon/2\), the minimum of \(E\). Under the probability density \(e^{-\tau E}/L_n(\tau)\), the \(m\) variables \(h(t_i)-h_{\min}\) for \(j_i=1\) are independent copies of a variable \(Q_\tau\); the remaining \(2n-m\) energy contributions \(ct_i\) and \(r_i\) are independent exponentials of rate \(\tau\).

For every \(\tau>0\), the variable \(Q_\tau\) satisfies
\(\mathbb E Q_\tau\le1/\tau\) and \(\mathbb E Q_\tau^2\le2/\tau^2\).
To verify this, push Lebesgue measure on \(a\ge0\) forward by \(h(a)-h_{\min}\). If \(b<1\), its density is \(1/(1-b)\) on \(0<v<(1-b)/b\) and \(1/(1+b)\) thereafter. If \(b>1\), its density is \(1/(b-1)+1/(b+1)\) on \(0<v<(b-1)/b\) and \(1/(b+1)\) thereafter. These densities are nonincreasing. At \(b=1\), the pushforward instead has an atom of mass one at zero and density \(1/2\) on \(v>0\). For any of these nonincreasing densities \(g\), and any \(v>0\), substitution and \(g(v+u)\le g(u)\) give
\(\int_v^\infty e^{-\tau x}g(x)\,dx\le e^{-\tau v}\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau u}g(u)\,du\).
An atom at zero only increases the normalizing denominator. Therefore \(\mathbb P(Q_\tau>v)\le e^{-\tau v}\); integrating this tail and twice its product with \(v\) gives the stated moments.

Let \(\mu(\tau)\) be the expectation of \(E\) under this probability law. The preceding bounds and independence give

\[
\frac{2n-m}{\tau}\le\mu(\tau)-E_0\le\frac{2n}{\tau},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(E)\le\frac{2n+m}{\tau^2}
\le\frac{5n}{2\tau^2}.
\tag{7}
\]

The function \(\mu(\tau)\) is continuous for positive \(\tau\), as follows directly by dominated convergence on compact intervals of rates in these exponentially integrable product integrals. Also \(E_0\le n/2<R\). The bounds in (7) imply that \(\mu(\tau)+4\sqrt n/\tau\) tends to infinity as \(\tau\downarrow0\) and tends to \(E_0\) as \(\tau\to\infty\). Fix a positive rate satisfying
\(\mu(\tau)+4\sqrt n/\tau=R\).
At this rate, Chebyshev's inequality and (7) give probability at least \(27/32\) to the event
\(R-8\sqrt n/\tau\le E\le R\).
Multiplying back by \(e^{\tau E}\) on this event yields the fixed-budget lower bound

\[
w_{\mathbf j}\ge\frac{27}{32}\,e^{\tau R-8\sqrt n}L_n(\tau).
\tag{8}
\]

The chosen rate also satisfies

\[
\frac{2n-m+4\sqrt n}{R-E_0}\le\tau
\le\frac{2n+4\sqrt n}{R-E_0}.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(b\le1\), one has \(E_0=n/2\), so \(\tau\ge150\). For \(1\le b\le2\), one has \(E_0\ge n/(2b)\), \(2n-m\ge3n/2\), and \(R\le51n/100\), so
\(\tau\ge3/(51/50-1/b)\ge75/13>5\).
These are uniform over all allowed \(n,k,\beta\).

We now compare \(J(\tau)\) and \(I(\tau)\) at this rate. The geometric-series formula for \(\theta_\tau\) gives the exact identity

\[
\theta_\tau(x)^2
=\frac{1+q}{1-q}\theta_{2\tau}(x)
+\frac{2q}{(1-q)^2}
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
\tag{10}
\]

For example, on \(0\le x\le1\), square its two-term numerator, whose cross product is \(q\), and use \(1-q^2=(1-q)(1+q)\). Periodicity then proves (10) everywhere.

Put \(\xi=e^{-\tau/\max\{1,b\}}\). To estimate the integral of \(\theta_{2\tau}\) in (10), first consider its summand at a positive integer \(a=\ell+1\), where \(\ell\ge0\). Its contribution is
\[
\tau\int_0^\infty e^{-\tau[u+b|u-(2\ell+1)/b|]}\,du
\le(2\ell+1)\xi^{2\ell}I(\tau).
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, substitute \(u=(2\ell+1)v\). The cost in the exponent becomes \((2\ell+1)h(v)\), and \(h(v)\ge h_{\min}=1/\max\{1,b\}\), proving the inequality. The summand at the nonpositive integer \(a=-\ell\) contributes exactly \(q^{2\ell+1}/c\). Summing all these nonnegative contributions, using
\(\sum_{\ell\ge0}(2\ell+1)\xi^{2\ell}=(1+\xi^2)/(1-\xi^2)^2\),
and applying (10) in (3), gives

\[
\frac{cJ(\tau)}{I(\tau)}
\le
c\,\frac{1+q}{1-q}\frac{1+\xi^2}{(1-\xi^2)^2}
+\frac{q}{I(\tau)}
\left(\frac{1+q}{(1-q)(1-q^2)}
+\frac{2c}{(1-q)^2}\right).
\tag{12}
\]

This estimate keeps all the sign crossings and every integer summand.

The bounds on the chosen rate imply \(q<1/100\) and \(\xi^2<1/100\). Indeed, \(\tau>5\), \(\max\{1,b\}\le2\), \(e^5>100\), and \(e^{5/2}>10\). These two elementary exponential bounds follow already from finite initial sums of the exponential series.

For \(4/5<b<1\), integration on \(0\le a\le1/b\) gives
\[
\frac q{I(\tau)}
\le\frac{1-b}{1-e^{-\tau(1-b)/b}}
\le1-b+\frac b\tau<\frac14.
\tag{13}
\]

The second inequality follows from \(x/(1-e^{-x})\le1+x\), which follows from \(e^x\ge1+x\). Here \(\tau\ge150\). At \(b=1\), the same interval instead gives \(I(\tau)/q\ge\tau\), so \(q/I(\tau)<1/4\) there too.

For \(1<b\le2\), put \(x=b-1\in(0,1]\). Integration on the same interval gives \(q/I(\tau)\le x/(e^{\tau x/b}-1)\). If \(x\le1/4\), the rate bound in (9) and \(e^u-1\ge u\) give
\[
\frac q{I(\tau)}\le\frac b\tau
\le\frac{1+51x}{150}\le\frac{11}{120}<\frac1{10}.
\]

If \(x\ge1/4\), then \(\tau x/b\ge150x/(1+51x)\ge30/11>5/2\). The first six terms of the exponential series show \(e^{5/2}>11\), so \(q/I(\tau)\le x/(e^{5/2}-1)<1/10\). At \(b=1\), the stronger bound \(q/I(\tau)\le1/\tau\le1/150<1/10\) also holds.

Substitute these bounds into (12). To record the numerical margin with rational bounds only, its right side is at most

\[
c\,\frac{101}{99}\frac{10100}{9801}
+\frac q{I(\tau)}
\left(\frac{101}{99}\frac{10000}{9999}
+2c\,\frac{10000}{9801}\right).
\tag{14}
\]

For \(b\le1\), substitute \(c\le2\) and \(q/I(\tau)\le1/4\) in (14); the resulting rational number is less than \(17/5<39/10\). For \(b\ge1\), substitute \(c\le3\) and \(q/I(\tau)\le1/10\); the resulting rational number is less than \(39/10\). Thus in every case
\[
\frac{cJ(\tau)}{I(\tau)}\le\frac{39}{10}<4.
\tag{15}
\]

It remains to bound the single unmatched-coordinate factor in (6). Put \(D=(1-1/b)_+\). Since \(R-E_0=k+mD\ge1\), (9) and \(n\ge100\) give \(\tau\le2n+4\sqrt n\le12n/5\). Moreover \(\tau D\le(2n+4\sqrt n)/m<5\); for this last inequality use \(m\ge(n-1)/2\ge99n/200\). Integration of the two linear pieces of \(h\), with \(h(a)\ge1-D\) on \(0\le a\le1/b\), gives
\[
\frac{I(\tau)}q\le e^{\tau D}
\left(\frac{\tau}{b}+\frac1c\right).
\]

Consequently \(cI(\tau)/q\le e^5(15\tau/4+1)\le10e^5n\), using \(c\le3\) and \(b>4/5\). This bounds the factor whether \(\epsilon=0\) or \(\epsilon=1\), since \(10e^5n>1\).

Combining (4), (6), (8), and (15), including this unmatched-coordinate bound, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W}{2^n w_{\mathbf j}}
&\le\frac{32}{27}e^{8\sqrt n}
\left(\frac{\sqrt{cJ(\tau)/I(\tau)}}2\right)^n
\left(\frac{cI(\tau)}q\right)^{\epsilon/2}\\
&\le\frac{32}{27}\sqrt{10e^5n}\,
\exp\left(8\sqrt n-\frac n{80}\right)
<e^{1700}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

For the second line, use \(\tfrac12\log(40/39)\ge1/80\), which follows from \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\). For the last inequality, use \(\sqrt n\le e^{\sqrt n}\) and
\(9\sqrt n-n/80=1620-(\sqrt n-360)^2/80\le1620\).
The remaining constant \((32/27)\sqrt{10e^5}\) is less than \(e^6\): it is less than \(8e^{5/2}<e^{11/2}\), since \(e>2\). Thus the stronger upper bound \(e^{1626}\) would already suffice in (16). This proves (1).

Finally apply equation (3) of accepted fact \(\texttt{03f071403cdfc69f}\) to this actual convex body, the fixed positive center \(\mathbf j\), and cutoff zero. Its oriented cutoff sum contains the zero integer displacement, whose weight is \(w_{\mathbf j}\) and whose curvature is zero. It therefore supplies one continuous point in \(C_{\mathbf j}\) with actual depth at least \(w_{\mathbf j}/12\). By (1), this is at least \(e^{-1700}W/(12\,2^n)\). All hypotheses of that accepted result hold for the compact full-dimensional body and its original finite positive-volume table established above. Its point is selected before every closed ambient halfspace, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing entire fibers. This proves the stated depth consequence.
