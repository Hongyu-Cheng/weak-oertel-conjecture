## Statement

For the actual body $[-1,1]\times[0,1]$ with integer base $z$, continuous coordinate $x$, and original mixed length, the base $j=0$ has raw marginal depth $D_0=2$. At balanced mass $t=2$ and direction $u=1$, the support of the continuous mean interval is $h_{M_2(0)}(1)=2/3$, while the full mean of the optimized directional capacity law is $J_2(1)=5/8$. Thus the support gap is $1/24$ despite identical endpoint fibers and a midpoint coupling of their unit measures with density exactly one on the middle fiber.

## Proof

The three fibers over $-1,0,1$ all have length one; the two containing marginal half-lines through zero each capture two. Take the top interval $[1/3,1]$ in each fiber. This selection has total mass two and centered base moment zero. For any other dominated source of mass two, integrating $(x-1/3)(\mathbf1_{\{x\ge1/3\}}-f)\ge0$ proves maximality of its first moment, even without balance. Its continuous mean is $2/3$.

Write $F(s)=\min(1,(1-s)_+)$. For a tilt $a$, the endpoint cost is $F(s+a)+F(s-a)$. If either trace is full, the sum is at least one. Otherwise the positive-part convexity inequality makes the sum at least $2(1-s)_+$. When $2(1-s)_+\le1$, zero tilt attains that value; when $2(1-s)_+\ge1$, a sufficiently large finite tilt attains one by making exactly one endpoint trace full. Thus the endpoint infimum is $\min(1,2(1-s)_+)$. Adding the middle trace, $V_1$ equals $2$ for $s\le0$, $2-s$ for $0\le s\le1/2$, $3(1-s)$ for $1/2\le s\le1$, and zero for $s\ge1$. Its law is carried by $[0,1]$ and has mass two. Its mean is
$$
\tfrac12\int_0^1 V_1(s)\,ds=\tfrac12(7/8+3/8)=5/8.
$$
For the uniform diagonal coupling of the endpoints, the continuous midpoint is the same uniform variable on $[0,1]$, proving the asserted domination with no density loss.
