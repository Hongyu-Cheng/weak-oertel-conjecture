## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and for \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n\) write
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
a(\mathbf z):=\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z}),
\qquad
g(\mathbf z):=a(\mathbf z)^{1/d}.
\]
Fix \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(\mathbf x_0\in C_{\mathbf z_0}\). Define the absolute fiber depth
\[
\delta_0:=
\inf\{\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z_0}\cap L):
L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf x_0\in L\},
\]
and suppose \(\delta_0>0\). Put
\[
\beta:=\frac{\delta_0}{a(\mathbf z_0)},
\qquad
q:=(1-\beta)^{1/d}.
\]
Let \(\mathbf w\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}\), and suppose
\[
2g(\mathbf z_0)-g(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w)-g(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w)
\le (1-q)g(\mathbf z_0).
\]
Then every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) whose boundary contains \((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf x_0)\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w}\cap H_{\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w})
+\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w}\cap H_{\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w})
\ge
\beta\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w)\},
\]
where \(H_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in H\}\).

Consequently, let \(\mathcal P\) be any family of pairwise disjoint two-point sets
\[
P=\{\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w_P,\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w_P\}
\subseteq\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
\mathbf w_P\ne\mathbf 0,
\]
such that the displayed flatness condition holds for every \(P\in\mathcal P\). Set
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda,\qquad
\mathbf y_0:=(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf x_0).
\]
Interpret mixed-integer volume by
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)
:=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap A_{\mathbf z}),
\]
where \(A_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in A\}\), and define
\[
h_S(\mathbf y):=
\inf\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf y\in H\}.
\]
Then
\[
h_S(\mathbf y_0)
\ge
\delta_0+
\beta\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}
\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w_P),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w_P)\}
=
\beta\left(
a(\mathbf z_0)+
\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}
\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w_P),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w_P)\}
\right).
\]

## Proof

First, \(a(\mathbf z_0)>0\) because \(\delta_0>0\). Choose any nonzero \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^d\). The two closed halfspaces
\[
L^\pm:=\{\mathbf x:\pm\mathbf v^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)\ge0\}
\]
both contain \(\mathbf x_0\), and their common boundary has zero \(\mathcal H_d\)-measure in \(\mathbb R^d\). Hence
\[
\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z_0}\cap L^+)
+\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z_0}\cap L^-)
=a(\mathbf z_0).
\]
At least one summand is at most \(a(\mathbf z_0)/2\), so
\[
0<\beta\le\frac12,\qquad 0<q<1,\qquad 1-q^d=\beta.
\]

Fix a proper closed halfspace \(H\) whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y_0\). It can be written as
\[
H=\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf x):
\mathbf u^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)
+\mathbf v^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)\ge0\},
\]
where \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\ne(\mathbf 0,\mathbf 0)\).

Suppose first that \(\mathbf v=\mathbf 0\). Then \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf 0\), and the two endpoint values of the defining affine function are \(\mathbf u^\top\mathbf w\) and \(-\mathbf u^\top\mathbf w\). If they are nonzero, one of the two endpoint fibers is contained in \(H\); if they are zero, both endpoint fibers are contained in \(H\). Thus the two fibers contribute at least
\[
\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w)\},
\]
which is at least the claimed quantity because \(\beta\le1/2\). This also treats all vertical halfspaces. If the convention for closed halfspaces includes the whole space, its contribution is trivially no smaller.

Now suppose \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf 0\), and introduce the opposite closed cut
\[
H^-:=\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf x):
\mathbf u^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)
+\mathbf v^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0)\le0\},
\qquad
B:=C\cap H^-.
\]
For every \(\mathbf z\), the common boundary of \(H_{\mathbf z}\) and \(H^-_{\mathbf z}\) is an affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^d\), hence has zero \(\mathcal H_d\)-measure. Therefore, with
\[
b(\mathbf z):=\mathcal H_d(B_{\mathbf z}),
\qquad
r(\mathbf z):=b(\mathbf z)^{1/d},
\]
the retained contribution of fiber \(\mathbf z\) is exactly
\[
\mathcal H_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap H_{\mathbf z})=a(\mathbf z)-b(\mathbf z).
\]
At \(\mathbf z_0\), both \(H_{\mathbf z_0}\) and \(H^-_{\mathbf z_0}\) are closed halfspaces through \(\mathbf x_0\). In particular, the retained side has volume at least \(\delta_0\), so
\[
b(\mathbf z_0)\le a(\mathbf z_0)-\delta_0
=(1-\beta)a(\mathbf z_0)
=q^d a(\mathbf z_0),
\qquad
r(\mathbf z_0)\le qg(\mathbf z_0).
\]

Root concavity is used only on the positive support
\[
D:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n:b(\mathbf z)>0\}.
\]
Indeed, if \(\mathbf z,\mathbf z'\in D\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), convexity of \(B\) gives
\[
(1-\lambda)B_{\mathbf z}+\lambda B_{\mathbf z'}
\subseteq
B_{(1-\lambda)\mathbf z+\lambda\mathbf z'}.
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality then yields
\[
r((1-\lambda)\mathbf z+\lambda\mathbf z')
\ge
(1-\lambda)r(\mathbf z)+\lambda r(\mathbf z').
\]
In particular, the intermediate profile is positive, so \(D\) is convex and \(r\) is concave on \(D\). No zero extension of \(r\) outside \(D\) is asserted or used.

Write
\[
\mathbf z_1:=\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w,\quad
\mathbf z_2:=\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w,\quad
A_i:=g(\mathbf z_i),\quad
r_i:=r(\mathbf z_i)
\quad(i=1,2),
\qquad
A_0:=g(\mathbf z_0).
\]
Relabel the endpoints if necessary so that \(A_1\ge A_2\). If \(A_2=0\), the desired lower bound is zero and is immediate.

If either endpoint is outside \(D\), then its removed profile has volume zero, so that endpoint's full fiber volume is retained. Its contribution is at least \(A_2^d\), and hence at least \(\beta A_2^d\). This is the required support-loss case.

It remains to treat \(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2\in D\). Concavity on \(D\), applied only to these two positive-profile endpoints, gives
\[
r_1+r_2\le2r(\mathbf z_0)\le2qA_0.
\]
Also \(0\le r_i\le A_i\). Set \(R:=2qA_0\). The flatness assumption is equivalent to
\[
A_1+A_2\ge(1+q)A_0.
\]
Since \(q<1\), this implies
\[
R=2qA_0<(1+q)A_0\le A_1+A_2.
\]

We now solve the needed capped two-variable optimization. For \(A_1\ge A_2\ge0\) and \(0\le R<A_1+A_2\),
\[
\max\{s_1^d+s_2^d:
0\le s_i\le A_i,\ s_1+s_2\le R\}
=
\begin{cases}
R^d,&R\le A_1,\\
A_1^d+(R-A_1)^d,&R>A_1.
\end{cases}
\]
To verify this, monotonicity lets a maximizer be chosen with \(s_1+s_2=R\). The objective \(s_1^d+(R-s_1)^d\) is convex on its feasible interval, so its maximum is at an endpoint. If \(R\le A_1\), concentrating all mass in the first coordinate is feasible and \(s_1^d+s_2^d\le(s_1+s_2)^d=R^d\). If \(R>A_1\), then also \(R>A_2\) and \(R-A_1<A_2\). Among the two capped endpoints, \((A_1,R-A_1)\) is at least as unequal as \((R-A_2,A_2)\): its larger coordinate \(A_1\) is at least both \(A_2\) and \(R-A_2\), the latter because \(R<A_1+A_2\). Convexity of \(t\mapsto t^d\) gives the second formula. The argument also covers \(d=1\), when the objective equals \(R\).

The pair's retained volume is
\[
T=A_1^d+A_2^d-r_1^d-r_2^d.
\]
If \(R\le A_1\), the optimization formula gives
\[
T\ge A_1^d+A_2^d-R^d\ge A_2^d\ge\beta A_2^d.
\]
If \(R>A_1\), flatness and \(A_1\ge A_2\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_1+qA_2-2qA_0
&\ge
A_1+qA_2-\frac{2q}{1+q}(A_1+A_2)\\
&=
\frac{1-q}{1+q}(A_1-qA_2)
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(R-A_1\le qA_2\), and the optimization formula yields
\[
T
\ge A_2^d-(R-A_1)^d
\ge(1-q^d)A_2^d
=\beta A_2^d.
\]
Because \(A_2^d=\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w)\}\), this proves the local pair bound in every case.

For the family statement, fix any boundary-through-\(\mathbf y_0\) halfspace \(H\). If \(H\) is nonvertical, its anchor-fiber section is a closed halfspace through \(\mathbf x_0\), so the anchor fiber contributes at least \(\delta_0\). If \(H\) is vertical, its entire anchor fiber lies on its boundary and contributes \(a(\mathbf z_0)\ge\delta_0\). Apply the local bound to every \(P\in\mathcal P\). The anchor coordinate is distinct from every pair endpoint because \(\mathbf w_P\ne\mathbf0\), and pairwise disjointness of the sets in \(\mathcal P\) ensures that no endpoint fiber appears twice. Hence these contributions are over disjoint integer fibers and there is no double counting. Ignoring all remaining nonnegative fiber contributions gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge
\delta_0+
\beta\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}
\min\{a(\mathbf z_0+\mathbf w_P),a(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf w_P)\}.
\]
Accepted fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\) states that the defining infimum for \(h_S(\mathbf y_0)\) may be restricted to closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y_0\). Taking the infimum over those halfspaces proves the asserted depth bound. Finally, \(\delta_0=\beta a(\mathbf z_0)\), which gives its second displayed form.
