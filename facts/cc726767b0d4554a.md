## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full simplex of volume $V$ and centroid $g$. Let $\lambda_i$ be its barycentric coordinates and $\ell_i$ their linear parts for $0\le i\le d$. Set $b=1/(d+1)$, $\Gamma=(1-b)^d$ and $\kappa=dV(1-b)^{d-1}$. At each of the four bases in $\{0,1\}^2$ place $T$. At $r=(-1,0)$, $p=(-1,1)$ and $p'=(1,-1)$ place convex bodies $R_t,A_t,C_t\subseteq T$ of respective volumes $t w_t,t a_t,t c_t$, where $(w_t,a_t,c_t)\to(w,a,c)\in(0,\infty)^3$ as $t\downarrow0$. Take their lifted convex hull and use the sum of $d$-volumes on the actual integer fibers and all closed ambient halfspaces. The auxiliary six-base hull deletes the generator at $r$. Let $q=(0,0)$, let $D_{6,t}^0$ and $D_{7,t}^0$ be the maximum raw depths on the $q$ fibers, and let $D_{7,t}$ be the global maximum raw depth in the seven-base hull.

Assume either of these two regimes.

- The normalized uniform laws of $A_t,C_t$ converge weakly to $\nu,\omega$, respectively, and both limits give zero mass to every level of every $\lambda_i$. Set
  $J_i=\min_{s\in\mathbb R}[a\,\nu\{\lambda_i\ge b+s\}+c\,\omega\{\lambda_i\ge b-s\}]$.
- With $\epsilon_t=t^{1/d}$, write $A_t=g+\epsilon_t B_t$ and $C_t=g+\epsilon_t E_t$ where $B_t,E_t$ converge in Hausdorff distance to full-dimensional convex bodies $B,E$. Then $a=|B|,c=|E|$. Set
  $J_i=\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb R}[|B\cap\{\ell_i\ge\sigma\}|+|E\cap\{\ell_i\ge-\sigma\}|]$.

The displayed minima are attained and can be restricted to compact shift intervals. Write $\bar J=(d+1)^{-1}\sum_iJ_i$ and $L=\max\{a,c,w+\bar J\}$. Then $D_{6,t}^0=\Gamma V+t\bar J+o(t)$, and
$$
D_{7,t}=\Gamma V+tL+o(t),\qquad
D_{7,t}/M_t=\Gamma/4+t[4L-\Gamma(w+a+c)]/(16V)+o(t).
$$
Every maximizing mark on the seven-base $q$ fiber satisfies $y_t=g+t v_*+o(t)$, where the unique $v_*$ is given by $\ell_i(v_*)=(J_i-\bar J)/\kappa$ for every $i$. If $w+\bar J>\max(a,c)$, every global maximizer lies at $q$ for sufficiently small $t$. If $a>\max(c,w+\bar J)$ the unique global maximizer is $((0,1),g)$, and if $c>\max(a,w+\bar J)$ it is $((1,0),g)$.

## Proof

For fixed $t$, write the six-base $q$ depth as $\min_{\|u\|=1}[F_T(y,u)+tJ_t(u,y)]$ by e6ed7f0a4aa574cd, with $F_T(y,u)=|T\cap\{u\cdot x\ge u\cdot y\}|$. Here $J_t$ is the minimum over opposite unit-normal shifts of the sum of the actual two endpoint volumes divided by $t$. It is nonnegative and at most $\min(a_t,c_t)$ by saturation. The unit canonical normal is $u_i=\nabla\lambda_i/\|\nabla\lambda_i\|$. We show that $J_t(u_t,y_t)\to J_i$ whenever $u_t\to u_i$ and $y_t=g+O(t)$.

In the first regime the six-base result restricts unit-normal shifts to $[-D-1,D+1]$ where $D=\operatorname{diam}T$. Along any converging sequence of shifts, the affine halfspace tests converge uniformly on compact $T$. The moving cap is sandwiched between the limiting open and closed caps with thresholds offset by an arbitrary small positive margin. Portmanteau and atomlessness at the limiting threshold give convergence of its probabilities as the margin tends to zero. This holds for each of the two caps. The coefficients $a_t,c_t$ converge and the probabilities are bounded by one. The limiting cap costs are continuous in the shift by atomlessness. Compactness of the shift interval now gives uniform convergence in shift and convergence of the minima. Converting the unit-normal shift to a $\lambda_i$ shift multiplies it by $\|\nabla\lambda_i\|$. The resulting interval already includes shifts saturating both tails in either direction since $\|x-g\|\le D$ on $T$. This identifies the minimum with $J_i$.

In the second regime rescale the opposite unit-normal shifts as $s=\epsilon_t\sigma$. The divided sum of the two volumes becomes
$$
|B_t\cap\{u\cdot x\ge u\cdot(y-g)/\epsilon_t+\sigma\}|
+|E_t\cap\{u\cdot x\ge u\cdot(y-g)/\epsilon_t-\sigma\}|.
$$
At $y=g+O(t)$, the offset is $O(t^{1-1/d})=o(1)$. All prelimit and limit tangent bodies lie in a fixed ball of radius $R$. For small $t$, shifts beyond $[-R-1,R+1]$ give one of the two saturated costs, also obtained at its endpoints. Hausdorff convergence gives almost-everywhere convergence of the body indicators: an interior point satisfies every supporting inequality with a uniform positive margin and is eventually in the approximants, an exterior point is eventually out, and the limiting convex boundary is null. Dominated convergence gives convergence of volumes, so $a=|B|$ and $c=|E|$. Along any converging sequence of shifts with $u_t\to u_i$, the cap indicators also converge off a null hyperplane, and dominated convergence gives convergence of the divided volumes. Compactness again gives uniform convergence in shift and convergence of minima. Rescaling from $u_i$ to $\ell_i$ preserves the minimum over all shifts. This proves the claimed stability in both regimes without a rate hypothesis on the body or law convergence.

By 53fe12b742a7608d, $F_T(g,u)\ge\Gamma V$ with equality exactly at the canonical $u_i$. Near $(g,u_i)$ the own cap isolates vertex $v_i$ from the other simplex vertices $v_j$. Its volume is
$$
F_T(y,u)=V\prod_{j\ne i}\frac{u\cdot(v_i-y)}{u\cdot(v_i-v_j)},
$$
by the edge intercept ratios. This is smooth and its derivative in $y$ at $(g,u_i)$ is $-\kappa\ell_i$, as follows from $F_T(y,u_i)=V(1-\lambda_i(y))^d$. The barycentric linear map identifies $\mathbb R^d$ with the zero-sum $(d+1)$-tuples. Hence there is a unique $v_*$ solving the specified equations.

Test the six-base mark $g+t v_*$, which lies in $T$ for small $t$. Its depth is at most $\Gamma V+O(t)$ by a canonical cap with bounded endpoint cost. Along any sequence $t\downarrow0$, a subsequence of minimizing normals converges to a canonical $u_i$, since endpoint costs are nonnegative and the own cost is continuous. The smooth expansion along the subsequence gives $F_T(g+t v_*,u_t)=F_T(g,u_t)-t\kappa\ell_i(v_*)+o(t)$. The nonnegative own excess $F_T(g,u_t)-\Gamma V$ may be retained in this lower bound. The pair cost is $tJ_i+o(t)$ by the proven stability, and $J_i-\kappa\ell_i(v_*)=\bar J$. Applying this to a putative violating sequence gives the lower bound $\Gamma V+t\bar J-o(t)$ for $D_{6,t}^0$.

By seven-base-general-exact-depth-reduction all maximizing marks at $q$ in either hull coincide for small $t$ and are $g+O(t)$. At any six-base maximizer $y_t$, each fixed canonical normal bounds the depth above by
$$
\Gamma V-\kappa\ell_i(y_t-g)+tJ_i+o(t).
$$
Average over $i$ to cancel the displacements and obtain the matching upper bound. Each quantity $J_i-\kappa\ell_i((y_t-g)/t)-\bar J$ is consequently bounded below by $-o(1)$, and their sum is zero. Each tends to zero, so every maximizing selection has the stated displacement. Applying the exact global reduction gives the two expansions and all strict base selections.
