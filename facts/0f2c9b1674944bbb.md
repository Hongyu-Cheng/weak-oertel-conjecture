## Statement

Let $f_i=U_i/M$, $1\le i\le k$, be $C^2$ normalized upper profiles on an open admissible body parameter domain, equal to the actual maximum $\beta_0$ at $r_0$ and jointly covering all the current maximizing source neighborhoods, with a persistent strict gap for other candidates. Suppose the $k-1$ rows $D(f_i-f_k)(r_0)$ for $i<k$ are independent and $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, $\sum_i\lambda_iDf_i(r_0)=0$. This probability vector is unique. The common tie set is a local $C^2$ submanifold. Along any $C^2$ tie curve with tangent $v$ in the kernel of the tie rows, the common first derivative vanishes and the common second derivative is $\sum_i\lambda_i D^2f_i(r_0)[v,v]$. If the latter is negative for some $v$ in the kernel then the actual normalized maximum strictly decreases along a short nonconstant tie curve. Thus at a local minimum the quadratic form is nonnegative on that kernel.

## Proof

Independence of the differences makes the gradients affinely independent, giving uniqueness of any affine combination with coefficient sum one and value zero. The implicit function theorem gives the tie submanifold and a $C^2$ curve through $r_0$ for every tangent vector. On a tie curve all first derivatives coincide, and their weighted sum is zero by the stationarity relation. Their second derivatives also coincide, and weighting the chain-rule second derivatives cancels the acceleration term $\sum_i\lambda_iDf_i(r_0)r''(0)$. The remaining sum is the displayed quadratic form. A negative value makes the common profile less than $\beta_0$ on a sufficiently short punctured segment by Taylor expansion, and the strict gap and upper bounds then give actual descent. A zero quadratic value yields no exact neutral curve claim.
