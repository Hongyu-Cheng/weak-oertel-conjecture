## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies, $d\ge1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and volumes $0<a=|A|\le b=|B|$, $k=|K|$. Place them at respective integer bases $-1,0,1$ in their lifted convex hull, and suppose $K,B$ are centrally symmetric about $c_K,c_B$. At least one of the actual points $(0,c_K),(1,c_B)$ has raw mixed depth strictly above $2(a+k+b)/9$. More precisely their respective certified lower bounds are $k/2+a\max(0,1-k/(2v_*))$ and $b/2$, where $v_*=((a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2)^d$.

## Proof

Compatibility ensures the lifted hull has precisely these integer fibers, since at base zero the endpoint weights in every convex representation agree. The middle bound follows from the single reflected pair in the accepted symmetry-center coupling bound. Every closed cap containing $(1,c_B)$ captures at least half of $B$ by symmetry (and the entire fiber for a pure-base cap). Thus the better of the two fixed marks certifies at least the maximum of the two displayed lower bounds.

Normalize volumes by $b$, put $u=\sqrt{a/b}$ and write $w=k/b$. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives $v_*/b\ge u$, strictly when $a<b$. Twice the better normalized raw lower bound is at least $F(u,w)=\max\{1,w,(1-u)w+2u^2\}$. We show $F(u,w)/(1+u^2+w)\ge4/9$ with equality only at $(u,w)=(1/2,1)$. For $u\le1/2$, the third term is at most $\max(1,w)$: for $w\le1$ it is at most $1-u+2u^2\le1$, and for $w\ge1$ it is at most $w$ because $2u\le w$. The resulting ratio is minimized at $w=1$ and is then $1/(2+u^2)\ge4/9$, with the indicated equality. For $1/2<u<1$, the breakpoints of the upper envelope are $w_0=(1-2u^2)/(1-u)$ when nonnegative, and $2u$. On each affine branch the ratio is monotone or constant. At $2u$ it exceeds $4/9$ by $2(2u-1)(2-u)/[9(1+u)^2]$. At a nonnegative $w_0$ it exceeds $4/9$ because
$$
9/4-(1+u^2+w_0)=\frac{(2u-1)(2u^2+3u-1)}{4(1-u)}>0.
$$
When $w_0\ge0$ the ratio on $[0,w_0]$ decreases to its right endpoint. When $w_0<0$, the ratio at zero is $2u^2/(1+u^2)>2/3$. Its limit at infinity is one. At $u=1$ it is $\max(2,w)/(2+w)\ge1/2$. This proves the relaxed scalar inequality and its equality classification. At the relaxed equality point, $a=b/4$ and $k=b$, so strict arithmetic-geometric mean gives $v_*>b/2$ and the middle lower bound is strictly above $b/2$. The original bound is therefore strict in every case. For $d\ge2$, $\Gamma_d/2\le2/9$ by monotonicity of $(1+1/d)^d$.
