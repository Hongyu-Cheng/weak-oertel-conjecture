## Statement

Fix real numbers \(a>0\) and \(D\ge0\). There is a coefficient \(\kappa(a,D)>0\), defined explicitly below and depending only on \(a,D\), with the following property. Let \(k\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers, let \(\beta\ge0\) be real, and let \(B_i,C_{4,i}\) be integers satisfying

$$
b\ge a\sqrt{k},\qquad b\le B_i\le2b,\qquad C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1
\quad(i\in[k]),\qquad
T\le\frac{k}{2}+Db\bigl[1+\log^2(k+1)\bigr].
\tag{1}
$$

Here \([k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), and every logarithm is natural. Let \(\Phi:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be any nondecreasing function with \(\Phi(0)=0\). Give every spatial vector multiplicity one, and retain all integer heights in each indicated interval. Use exactly the energy and normalizers of accepted fact `330a02ac07b88af9`:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
r(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k\max(x_i-1,0),&
\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i)+\Phi(r(\mathbf x)),\\
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}&
Q(\mathbf x)&=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,\\
Z_{4,\Phi}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},&
Z_{3,\Phi}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},\\
Z_{\rm bin}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

The tail \(f_E\) is defined for nonnegative integer \(E\). Define the full and selected supports and their normalized laws by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{T,\Phi}^{[4]}&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}&=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{Z_{4,\Phi}}
\quad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[4]}),&
p^{[3]}_{(\mathbf x,t)}&=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{Z_{3,\Phi}}
\quad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

For either law \(\mu\), supported on its indicated finite support \(A\), its ambient depth and moment are

$$
\tau_\mu(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf v\in A\cap H}\mu_{\mathbf v},
\qquad
M_3(\mu)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}\mu_{\mathbf z}\tau_\mu(\mathbf z)^3,
\tag{4}
$$

where \(H=\lbrace\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\ge\gamma\rbrace\) ranges over all closed affine halfspaces with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Both laws use this same family of halfspaces in the same ambient space. Then

$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa(a,D)\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{5}
$$

Here is a finite definition of the coefficient. As in `330a02ac07b88af9`, set \(K_a=4(1+a^{-2})\) and \(K_{a,D}=K_a(D+2)+1\). For every integer \(k\ge1\), put \(\overline H_k=2K_{a,D}(1+\log k)^2\). Define \(K(a,D)\) to be the least integer \(K\ge2\) such that every integer \(k\ge K\) satisfies all three inequalities

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline H_k&\le\frac{k}{128},\\
\min\left\lbrace a\sqrt{k}-1,
\left\lfloor\frac{k-\overline H_k}{16\overline H_k}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace
&\ge\frac{a\sqrt{k}}2,\\
\frac{100\overline H_k^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}
+2\overline H_k\left(\frac23\right)^{a\sqrt{k}/2}&\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

This integer exists, as proved below. The coefficient in (5) is

$$
\kappa(a,D)=\min\left\lbrace
\frac1{2939328(e+1/2)^4},\,
\frac{2^{-5(K(a,D)-1)}}{448}
\right\rbrace>0.
\tag{7}
$$

If \(\Phi\) also has nondecreasing successive differences, the full support in (3) is the lattice-point support of a convex epigraph with cap \(T\). In particular \(\Phi(j)=\lambda j^2\), for any integer \(\lambda\ge0\), is permitted; its spatial energy is nonseparable when \(\lambda>0\) and \(k\ge2\). The locked problem's \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S,c_0\) and any locked count \(N\) retain their original meanings. All supports and normalizers in (2)-(4) are auxiliary lattice tables.

## Proof

The energy is a nonnegative integer and \(\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf0)=0\). Thus the atom \((\mathbf0,0)\) belongs to both supports, all supported heights lie between zero and \(T\), and \(0<Z_{\rm bin}\le Z_{3,\Phi}\le Z_{4,\Phi}<\infty\).

First apply accepted fact `11d156df58653939` to the restriction of \(\Gamma_\Phi\) to \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\). Each restricted \(g_i\) is nondecreasing because \(0<1<B_i\), the function \(r\) is coordinatewise nondecreasing, and \(\Phi\) is nondecreasing. Hence the restricted energy is coordinatewise nondecreasing and zero at \(\mathbf0\), exactly as required there. Its \(\Gamma,A_T,Z,p\) map respectively to this restriction of \(\Gamma_\Phi\), \(A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]},Z_{3,\Phi},p^{[3]}\). Its ambient halfspaces are exactly those in (4). Consequently

$$
M_3(p^{[3]})\ge\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{8}
$$

We next prove the transfer with all normalizer factors retained. Put \(\alpha=Z_{3,\Phi}/Z_{4,\Phi}\). For every selected atom \(\mathbf z\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}\), (3) gives \(p_{\mathbf z}=\alpha p^{[3]}_{\mathbf z}\). For every ambient halfspace containing that atom,

$$
\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[4]}\cap H}p_{\mathbf v}
\ge\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}\cap H}p_{\mathbf v}
=\alpha\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}\cap H}p^{[3]}_{\mathbf v}.
\tag{9}
$$

Taking the infimum over this identical family of containing halfspaces gives \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\tau_{p^{[3]}}(\mathbf z)\). All other original moment contributions are nonnegative, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_{T,\Phi}^{[3]}}
\alpha p^{[3]}_{\mathbf z}
\bigl(\alpha\tau_{p^{[3]}}(\mathbf z)\bigr)^3\\
&=\left(\frac{Z_{3,\Phi}}{Z_{4,\Phi}}\right)^4M_3(p^{[3]}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

The fourth power consists of one sampling factor and three depth factors. Every atom in the selected support retains its original spatial location and every feasible height; (9) uses the full original halfspace mass.

We verify the cutoff and its consequence using precisely the finite bounds of accepted fact `330a02ac07b88af9`. Since \(\overline H_k\) is a constant depending only on \(a,D\) times \((1+\log k)^2\), one has \(\overline H_k/k\to0\), \(\overline H_k^{3/2}/\sqrt{k}\to0\), and \(\sqrt{k}/\overline H_k\to\infty\). In particular,

$$
\frac1{\sqrt{k}}\left\lfloor\frac{k-\overline H_k}{16\overline H_k}\right\rfloor\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\overline H_k\left(\frac23\right)^{a\sqrt{k}/2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
$$

Also \(a\sqrt{k}-1\ge a\sqrt{k}/2\) eventually. These limits imply all three inequalities in (6) for every sufficiently large integer \(k\), with the threshold depending only on \(a,D\). The defining set for \(K(a,D)\) is therefore nonempty and its least member is finite.

For any model satisfying (1), choose the accepted cap parameter to be \(L_{\rm cap}=D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\). Thus its cap hypothesis is exactly \(T\le k/2+L_{\rm cap}b\). The accepted quantities \(g_i,\Gamma_\Phi,r,Q,f_E,Z_{4,\Phi},Z_{3,\Phi},Z_{\rm bin},k,b,T,\beta,B_i,C_{4,i}\) are identical to (1)-(3), including the original complete-height normalizers. Set, exactly as in that fact,

$$
H_0=\left\lceil K_a\bigl[L_{\rm cap}+\log(k+1)+1\bigr]\right\rceil.
\tag{12}
$$

Its tail conclusion and cutoff estimate give

$$
Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H_0}\le\frac12Z_{\rm bin},
\qquad
1\le H_0\le K_{a,D}[1+\log^2(k+1)]\le\overline H_k.
\tag{13}
$$

The occupation restrictions here are exactly restrictions of the sum in (2), as in the accepted fact. The last inequality uses \(\log(k+1)\le1+\log k\) and \(1\le(1+\log k)^2\).

Now let \(k\ge K(a,D)\). Equations (6) and (13) imply \(1\le H_0\le k/128\), so its accepted low-occupation estimate applies with

$$
h_{H_0}=\min\left\lbrace b-1,
\left\lfloor\frac{k-H_0}{16H_0}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace,
\qquad
D_{H_0}=\frac{100H_0^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}
+2H_0\left(\frac23\right)^{h_{H_0}}.
\tag{14}
$$

These are the same \(h_H,D_H\) as in the accepted statement. Since \(b-1\ge a\sqrt{k}-1\), \(H_0\le\overline H_k\), and the function \(u\mapsto(k-u)/(16u)\) is decreasing for \(u>0\), the second inequality in (6) yields \(h_{H_0}\ge a\sqrt{k}/2\). The third then gives \(D_{H_0}\le1\). The accepted low-occupation bound is consequently \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H_0}\le eZ_{3,\Phi}\). Partitioning the full original mass at this integer cutoff, and using (13), proves

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{4,\Phi}
&=Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H_0}+Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H_0+1}\\
&\le eZ_{3,\Phi}+\frac12Z_{\rm bin}
\le(e+1/2)Z_{3,\Phi}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

Together (8), (10), and (15) prove (5) for \(k\ge K(a,D)\) with the first coefficient in (7). The comparison applies separately to each permitted \(\Phi\); no comparison between two different penalties is used.

For the finite remaining dimensions we give the height consumer directly on original atoms. We use and rederive the scalar reflection estimate from accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977` with the following exact map. Its auxiliary height length \(M\) is the present \(T\), its scalar ratio \(r\) is denoted here by \(\rho=e^{-\beta}\) to avoid confusion with the spatial excess \(r(\mathbf x)\), its \(Z_{\rm aux}\) is \(f_0\), and its height weights are

$$
q_t=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{f_0}\quad(0\le t\le T),\qquad
q_t=0\quad(t\notin\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace),\qquad
F_q(t)=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min\lbrace q_{t+h},q_{t-h}\rbrace.
\tag{16}
$$

Here \(f_0=\sum_{j=0}^T\rho^j>0\); it is the original zero-vector height mass, not the full table normalizer. Put \(U_t=\sum_{j=0}^t q_j\), \(V_t=\sum_{j=t}^Tq_j\), and \(\theta_t=\min\lbrace U_t,V_t\rbrace\). The intervals \([U_t-q_t,U_t]\) partition \([0,1]\) up to endpoints. If \(v\) belongs to the interval indexed by \(t\), then \(v\le U_t\) and \(1-v\le V_t\). Thus

$$
\sum_{t=0}^Tq_t\theta_t^6
\ge\int_0^1\min\lbrace v,1-v\rbrace^6\,dv
=2\int_0^{1/2}v^6\,dv=\frac1{448}.
\tag{17}
$$

Since \(0<\rho\le1\), the exact reflection formula is

$$
F_q(t)=q_t\left(1+2\sum_{h=1}^{\min\lbrace t,T-t\rbrace}\rho^h\right).
\tag{18}
$$

If \(t\le T/2\), this is at least \(q_t\sum_{h=0}^t\rho^h=\rho^tU_t\), while \(V_t=\rho^t(\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\rho^j)/f_0\le\rho^t\). Hence \(F_q(t)\ge U_tV_t\ge\theta_t^2\). If \(t\ge T/2\), (18) is at least \(q_t\sum_{h=0}^{T-t}\rho^h=V_t\ge\theta_t^2\). This includes equality at the midpoint, \(T=0\), and \(\rho=1\). Equation (17) therefore proves the scalar estimate in the mapped normalization:

$$
\sum_{t=0}^Tq_tF_q(t)^3\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{19}
$$

Every \(\mathbf z_t=(\mathbf0,t)\), for \(0\le t\le T\), is an original atom because \(\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf0)=0\). At a fixed such root, for every positive integer \(h\le\min\lbrace t,T-t\rbrace\), the two distinct original atoms \(\mathbf z_{t+h},\mathbf z_{t-h}\) have midpoint \(\mathbf z_t\). Different positive \(h\) give disjoint pairs, all avoiding the root. Every ambient closed halfspace containing the root contains at least one atom from each pair: otherwise its defining affine functional would be strictly below its threshold at both endpoints and at their midpoint. The smaller original endpoint mass is \(e^{-\beta(t+h)}/Z_{4,\Phi}\). Charge this mass for each pair and half of the root's original mass. This gives, uniformly for all containing halfspaces,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z_t)
&\ge\frac1{2Z_{4,\Phi}}
\sum_{|h|\le\min\lbrace t,T-t\rbrace}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}\\
&=\frac{f_0}{2Z_{4,\Phi}}F_q(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

The corresponding original sampling mass is \(p_{\mathbf z_t}=(f_0/Z_{4,\Phi})q_t\). Cubing (20), multiplying by this sampling mass, summing only these distinct original roots, and using (19), we obtain

$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{t=0}^Tp_{\mathbf z_t}\tau_p(\mathbf z_t)^3
\ge\frac{f_0^4}{8Z_{4,\Phi}^4}\sum_{t=0}^Tq_tF_q(t)^3
\ge\frac{f_0^4}{3584Z_{4,\Phi}^4}.
\tag{21}
$$

All four denominator factors in (21) are the full original normalizer. The auxiliary scalar law only expresses the root-height sum; the halfspaces in (20) remain those of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\), with all original atoms available.

Because every energy in (2) is nonnegative, each spatial vector contributes at most \(f_0\), including vectors whose energy exceeds \(T\). There are \(4^k\) spatial vectors. Thus \(Z_{4,\Phi}\le4^kf_0\), and (21) implies the bound, valid for every \(k\),

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{3584\,4^{4k}}
=\frac{2^{-5k}}{448}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{22}
$$

For \(1\le k<K(a,D)\), one has \(2^{-5k}\ge2^{-5(K(a,D)-1)}\). Hence (22) proves (5) with the second coefficient in (7). Combining this finite range with the range already proved from (15) establishes (5) for every positive integer \(k\).

For the epigraph assertion, linearly interpolate each \(g_i\) on \([0,3]\). Its successive slopes are \(1,B_i-1,C_{4,i}-B_i\), which are nondecreasing by (1), so the interpolant \(\overline g_i\) is convex. When \(\Phi\) has nondecreasing successive differences, its linear interpolation \(\overline\Phi\) on \([0,\infty)\) is convex and nondecreasing. The real-variable excess \(\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\) is convex. Therefore

$$
\overline\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)
=\sum_i\overline g_i(x_i)
+\overline\Phi\left(\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\right),\qquad
\mathcal P_{T,\Phi}
=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in[0,3]^k,
\ \overline\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace
\tag{23}
$$

define a convex energy and a compact convex epigraph with cap. At integer spatial vectors the excess is integer, so \(\overline\Gamma_\Phi=\Gamma_\Phi\); hence \(\mathcal P_{T,\Phi}\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}=A_{T,\Phi}^{[4]}\). This includes supports of lower affine dimension. For \(\Phi(j)=\lambda j^2\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), the interpolation has the required properties. If \(\lambda>0\) and \(k\ge2\), vary two coordinates independently between one and two while holding the others at one. The mixed difference of the four values of \(\Gamma_\Phi\) is \(4\lambda-\lambda-\lambda+0=2\lambda>0\); a sum of functions of individual coordinates has mixed difference zero. Thus these energies are nonseparable.

All energy sublevels, height intervals, and halfspaces above use weak inequalities, so all boundary atoms are retained. Tied costs and equality in the cost window or cap cause no change. When \(\beta=0\), the scalar ratio is \(\rho=1\), already covered. When \(T=0\), every nonzero spatial vector has positive additive cost and \(\Phi\ge0\), so the original support is the singleton \(\lbrace(\mathbf0,0)\rbrace\) and \(M_3(p)=1\); the displayed estimates also remain valid. The zero atom guarantees every denominator is positive throughout, and \(k=1\) belongs to the finite range if it is below the cutoff. No further comparison or moment premise is needed.
