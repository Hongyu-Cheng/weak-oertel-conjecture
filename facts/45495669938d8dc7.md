## Statement

Fix any integer \(k\ge8\), and use exactly the \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) specialization of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, with the tree construction of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, set
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor
\quad(8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}b_j,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\qquad N=n+1.
$$
Put \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) whenever \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). Choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\). Its nonterminal set is \(D_m\), and its injective successor is \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), where \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\). Spatial coordinates are indexed by
$$
\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace^r.
$$
For a node \(v\) of depth less than \(Q\), including the empty root, its child block is \(z[v]=(z_{v1},\ldots,z_{vm})\). For a Boolean source \(b\), the canonical path starts at the root, proceeds from \(v\) to \(vi_m(b[v])\) whenever \(b[v]\in D_m\), and stops at the first failure or at depth \(Q\). Write its length as \(h(b)\) and its selected coordinates as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\). Retain the complete original supports
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{m,Q}
&=\left\lbrace z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}:
b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},\quad
0\le s\le h(b)\right\rbrace,\\
\widehat A
&=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(\operatorname{level}(z)\) is the number of coordinates equal to two.

Fix any full rooted spine \(v_0=\varnothing,v_1,\ldots,v_Q\), and put
$$
S=\bigcup_{r=0}^{Q-1}\lbrace v_ri:1\le i\le m\rbrace,
\qquad |S|=mQ,\qquad
R_S=\lbrace z|_S:z\in A_{m,Q}\rbrace.
$$
For every positive function \(f:R_S\to(0,\infty)\) and every vector \((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_Q)\in\mathbb R^Q\), define, at every original index,
$$
w_{(z,t)}=
f(z|_S)\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{Q}\xi_r\sum_{|u|=r}z_u\right)2^{-t},
\qquad W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},
\qquad p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W.
$$
All other lattice weights are zero. Define
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=\inf\left\lbrace
\sum_{\mathbf J\in H\cap\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I
\right\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-110}8^{-N}.
$$
The coefficient is independent of the height cutoff, tree depth, chain partition, spine, function \(f\), and all real depth coefficients. All original spatial points, all original heights, the full original normalizer, and all containing closed ambient halfspaces occur in this conclusion.

## Proof

The tree support, unique Boolean sources, and inverse reconstruction have exactly the meanings of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. The height support and all entrances have exactly the meanings of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df. Put
$$
\eta=|D_m|2^{-m}=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2},\qquad
\delta=1-\eta,\qquad
T_s=\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}.
$$
These accepted facts give
$$
Q\ge4,\quad m\ge1250,\quad
0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2},\quad \eta^Q>\frac34,
\qquad T_s\le2\,2^{-a_s}.
\tag{1}
$$
At every atom, every containing halfspace includes that atom. Thus for any of the finite probability laws used below, with depths defined by all closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^N\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}^4.
\tag{2}
$$
We repeatedly use the elementary inequality
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{h}x_i^4\ge h^{-3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}x_i\right)^4
\quad(x_i>0),
\tag{3}
$$
which is Hölder's inequality for the finite sum.

We first prove the required entropy estimate with the arbitrary multiplier present. For each fixed spatial level \(s\), let
$$
\mathcal L_s^S=\lbrace z|_S:z\in A_{m,Q},\ \operatorname{level}(z)=s\rbrace.
$$
Reduction modulo two recovers the restriction of the Boolean source to \(S\). Conversely, that source restriction determines the entire label at level \(s\), whenever this level occurs. Indeed, follow its tests along the fixed spine until reaching level \(s\), failing, or first selecting a child different from the spine. Up to that first divergence all the tested blocks lie in \(S\). The divergent selected coordinate also lies in \(S\), because its parent is on the spine. Every later selected coordinate lies outside \(S\). Consequently the source restriction determines exactly which of its zero coordinates in \(S\) become two. This proves
$$
0<|\mathcal L_s^S|\le2^{mQ}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular this estimate is valid for the union of all first-divergence classes at that level, not only within one such class.

For \(\omega\in\mathcal L_s^S\), let \(d(\omega)\) be its first divergent selection depth if a divergence occurs within the selected prefix, and otherwise let \(d(\omega)=s\). For \(s=0\), this convention gives \(d(\omega)=0\). The label itself determines the divergence when present: its twos consist, within \(S\), of a spine prefix followed by the one divergent coordinate. Set
$$
\mathcal D(x)=\sum_{c\in D_m}e^{x\sum_i c_i},\qquad
C_r=m^r-m.
$$
Let \(H_{s,\omega}(\xi)\) be the weighted sum over the outside-\(S\) coordinates at this level and label:
$$
H_{s,\omega}(\xi)=
\sum_{\substack{z\in A_{m,Q},\ \operatorname{level}(z)=s\\z|_S=\omega}}
\exp\left(\sum_{u\notin S}\xi_{|u|}z_u\right).
$$
There is the exact factorization
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{s,\omega}(\xi)
={}&\prod_{d(\omega)<r\le s}
\left[e^{2\xi_r}\mathcal D(\xi_r)
(1+e^{\xi_r})^{C_r-m}\right]\\
&\mathrel{}\cdot
\prod_{\substack{1\le r\le Q\\r\le d(\omega)\text{ or }r>s}}
(1+e^{\xi_r})^{C_r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
To verify it, the fixed label fixes every source bit in \(S\), all tests on the spine in the selected prefix, and its divergent direction if any. At depth \(r\) there are exactly \(C_r\) coordinates outside \(S\). If \(d(\omega)<r\le s\), precisely one child block of \(m\) of them is visited and must have its source pattern in \(D_m\); its selected zero becomes two. All other outside coordinates at that depth are free. For every such depth \(r\ge2\), the exponent \(C_r-m=m^r-2m\) is nonnegative. The location of the next visited block is determined by the preceding patterns. All coefficients at its coordinate depth are identical, so summing its possible patterns gives the same \(\mathcal D(\xi_r)\), independently of its location. This successive summation proves (5). At every other depth all outside bits are free. The unique-source property proves both that all choices counted are distinct and that all points with the specified label are counted. The same formula therefore holds with \(4\xi\) in place of \(\xi\).

Define
$$
\mathcal R(x)=\frac{8(1+e^{4x})}{(1+e^x)^4},\qquad
V'=\sum_{r=2}^{Q}(m^r-2m)\log\mathcal R(\xi_r).
$$
Inequality (3) and \(|D_m|\le2^m\) give
$$
\frac{\mathcal D(4x)}{\mathcal D(x)^4}\ge8^{-m},
\qquad \mathcal R(x)\ge1.
$$
Taking the fourth-power ratio in (5), the spike factors cancel. A constrained outside block costs at most its \(m\) Boolean coordinates in (3); at every depth \(r\ge2\) at least \(m^r-2m\) outside coordinates are free. The total number of outside coordinates is \(n-mQ\). It follows that
$$
\frac{H_{s,\omega}(4\xi)}{H_{s,\omega}(\xi)^4}
\ge8^{-(n-mQ)}e^{V'}.
\tag{6}
$$
For clarity, this also covers a depth with no outside test: its \(C_r\) free coordinates contribute \(8^{-C_r}\mathcal R(\xi_r)^{C_r}\), which is at least the corresponding bound using only \(m^r-2m\) free coordinates.

Let \(v_z=f(z|_S)\exp(\sum_r\xi_r\sum_{|u|=r}z_u)\), and put
$$
\mathcal Z_s^{(1)}=\sum_{\operatorname{level}(z)=s}v_z,
\qquad
\mathcal Z_s^{(4)}=\sum_{\operatorname{level}(z)=s}v_z^4.
$$
For a label \(\omega\), its common inside factor is
$$
g_\omega=f(\omega)
\exp\left(\sum_{u\in S}\xi_{|u|}\omega_u\right).
$$
Consequently \(\mathcal Z_s^{(1)}=\sum_\omega g_\omega H_{s,\omega}(\xi)\) and \(\mathcal Z_s^{(4)}=\sum_\omega g_\omega^4H_{s,\omega}(4\xi)\). Applying (6), then (3) to the label sum, and finally (4), gives
$$
\frac{\mathcal Z_s^{(4)}}{(\mathcal Z_s^{(1)})^4}
\ge8^{-(n-mQ)}e^{V'}|\mathcal L_s^S|^{-3}
\ge8^{-n}e^{V'}.
\tag{7}
$$
Thus the unbounded and correlated multiplier does not remove the surplus.

Choose a spatial level \(s\) maximizing \(\mathcal Z_s^{(1)}T_s\). The full original normalizer satisfies
$$
W=\sum_{j=0}^{Q}\mathcal Z_j^{(1)}T_j
\le(Q+1)\mathcal Z_s^{(1)}T_s.
$$
Use only the actual atoms at this level's entrance height. By (1), (2), and (7),
$$
8^N M_3(p)
\ge8^N\frac{2^{-4a_s}\mathcal Z_s^{(4)}}{W^4}
\ge\frac{e^{V'}}{2(Q+1)^4}.
\tag{8}
$$
If \(V'\ge\log m\), this proves the theorem with coefficient one, because \(m=2(Q+1)^4\).

Suppose henceforth that \(V'<\log m<5Q\). The scalar estimate of accepted fact d63d54549cda4df4 states that \(\mathcal R\) is even, nondecreasing in \(|x|\), and

\tag{9}

Here \(2/3<\log2<1\), by integrating \(1/x\) on \([1,2]\). Also \(m>128Q\) for \(Q\ge4\). If some \(r\ge2\) had \(|\xi_r|\ge\log2\), monotonicity and (9) would give

a contradiction. The final inequality follows already from \(m-2>30Q\) and \(m\ge1250\). Thus (9) applies to every \(\xi_r\) with \(r\ge2\). Since \(m^r-2m\ge m^r/2\) for \(r\ge2\),

$$

\qquad n_U=|U|=m^2+m(Q-1).

Define \(\lambda_u=0\) on \(U\) and \(\lambda_u=\xi_{|u|}\) outside \(U\). For a field \(\theta\), use the path variation of accepted fact c6487efe457e5084, namely

\qquad u_0=\varnothing,\quad u_{r+1}\text{ a child of }u_r.

&\le m\sum_{r=3}^{Q}|\xi_r|\\
&\le m\left(\sum_{r=3}^{Q}m^r\xi_r^2\right)^{1/2}

\tag{11}
$$
For an occurring \(U\)-label \(\omega\), set

\exp\left(\sum_{u\in U}\xi_{|u|}\omega_u\right)>0.
$$

This is an equality of all original weights; it makes no comparison of entire probability densities.

Partition the spatial support into the following classes. The two early classes consist of all points of level zero and of level one, respectively. Starting at its second selected node, require \(s-2\) successive forward \(D_m\) tests, followed by \(Q-s\) inverse \(\operatorname{im}F_m\) tests.
