## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite set of sampled bases with compact convex fibers $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ and mixed measure $\mu=\sum_{p\in P}\delta_p\otimes(\mathbf 1_{K_p}\,dx)$. Let $f$ be concave on a convex set containing the sampled marks, let $\varepsilon\ge0$, and let $\Gamma$ be a norm on continuous normals. The buffered depth at a mark $Y$ is the infimum of closed cap masses using threshold $N\cdot Y-\varepsilon\Gamma(u)f(Y)$ for $N=(a,u)$ with $u\ne0$, and the usual closed pure-base caps. Take a nonempty family of marks $Y_p=(p,y_p)$ of equal maximal buffered raw depth $Q$, including $Y_q$. At $q$ let each selected cap have $\Gamma(u_i)=1$, threshold $c_i=N_i\cdot Y_q-\varepsilon f(Y_q)$, actual mass $Q$, and a proper cut of $K_q$ (its boundary meets the fiber interior). Then
$$
N_i\cdot Y_p-\varepsilon f(Y_p)\le c_i
\qquad\text{at every marked }p.
$$
Suppose $\rho_i>0$, $\sum_i\rho_i=1$, $\sum_i\rho_i u_i=\varepsilon g$, and $(\alpha,g)$ is a full supergradient of $f$ at $Y_q$ when $\varepsilon>0$. Put $B=\operatorname{conv}\{p:Y_p\text{ is marked}\}$ and $a=\sum_i\rho_i a_i-\varepsilon\alpha$. Then $a\cdot(p-q)\le0$ for all $p\in B$. At every mark over the minimal face $F$ of $B$ containing $q$, each displayed quantile inequality is an equality, and the upper tangent for $f$ is an equality if $\varepsilon>0$.

When $\varepsilon=0$, no supergradient is needed. Each cap plane contains every marked point over $F$. If $q\in\operatorname{relint}B$, then $a$ annihilates $\operatorname{lin}(B-B)$ and is zero if $B$ is full-dimensional. If the marks over $F$ satisfy $y_p=Ap+b$, the affine fiber shear $x'=x-Az-b$ makes each new base coefficient annihilate $\operatorname{lin}(F-F)$. If $F$ is full-dimensional in the base, the caps are exactly $u_i\cdot x'\ge0$ and their ambient normal rank equals their continuous normal rank.

## Proof

For each cap, increasing $c_i$ by any strictly positive amount removes a positive $d$-volume slab from the own fiber at $q$. Indeed an interior ball centered on its section meets the open slab between $c_i$ and the higher threshold in positive volume, even if the latter extends beyond the ball. All other fiber contributions can only decrease. Thus the selected threshold is the right $Q$-quantile for that normal. At any mark of depth $Q$, its own buffered threshold for the same normalized normal cannot exceed the right quantile, proving the individual inequalities.

Write $s_i=N_i\cdot(Y_p-Y_q)-\varepsilon(f(Y_p)-f(Y_q))\le0$. For positive $\varepsilon$, put $D_p=\alpha\cdot(p-q)+g\cdot(y_p-y_q)-[f(Y_p)-f(Y_q)]\ge0$ by the full supergradient inequality. Then
$$
\sum_i\rho_i s_i=a\cdot(p-q)+\varepsilon D_p.
$$
At zero buffer the same identity holds with the second term zero by continuous balance. This gives the outward supporting inequality on the marks and hence on their base hull. The support face of this functional at $q$ contains the minimal face $F$; the functional also vanishes identically relative to $q$ if it is constant on $B$. On $F$ the right side is nonnegative and the left side nonpositive. All the positively weighted slacks must therefore vanish, as must $D_p$ for positive buffer.

At zero buffer these are exactly the original plane equations. If $q\in\operatorname{relint}B$ then $F=B$, giving annihilation of its direction span. When the marked graph over $F$ is affine, substituting $y_p=Ap+b$ into each plane equation and subtracting the equation at $q$ gives $(a_i+A^{\mathsf T}u_i)\cdot(p-q)=0$. The marked bases in a face of a finite hull span that face because they include all its vertices. Under the shear the new base coefficients are $a_i+A^{\mathsf T}u_i$, so they annihilate the face direction span. In full base dimension these coefficients vanish, and each constant is zero because the sheared cap plane passes through $(q,0)$. An invertible affine shear preserves ambient normal rank, while in the new coordinates the ambient normals are $(0,u_i)$. This argument uses actual zero-buffer caps throughout that case.
