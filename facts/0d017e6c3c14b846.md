## Statement

Let $k,N\ge1$. Let $\gamma_i$ be compactly supported finite nonnegative Borel measures on $\mathbb R^N$ of common mass $M>0$, and let $\lambda_i>0$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Set $b(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i x_i$. Let $\phi\in C^2(\mathbb R^N)$ with $D^2\phi\ge\varepsilon I_N$ everywhere for some $\varepsilon>0$. There are finite convex $C^1$ functions $V_i$ on $\mathbb R^N$ with globally Lipschitz gradients such that, for $F_i(z)=\nabla V_i(\lambda_i\nabla\phi(z))$, every coupling $\pi$ maximizing $\int \phi(b(x))\,d\pi(x)$ satisfies $x_i=F_i(b(x))$ for $\pi$-almost every tuple. In particular $F_i$ is locally Lipschitz, $(F_i)_\#(b_\#\pi)=\gamma_i$, and $\sum_i\lambda_i F_i(z)=z$ for $b_\#\pi$-almost every $z$.

## Proof

Use compact Lipschitz marginal duality on $K_i=\operatorname{supp}\gamma_i$ with cost $\phi\circ b$. The cost is separately Lipschitz there by boundedness of the gradient on a compact convex set containing all tuple averages and the segments between them. Let $u_i$ be optimal dual potentials. With all the other coordinates held in their compact supports, define
$$
U_i(v)=\max_{x_j\in K_j,\ j\ne i}
\left[\phi\left(\lambda_i v+\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j x_j\right)
-\sum_{j\ne i}u_j(x_j)\right].
$$
For $k=1$ the expression is $\phi(v)$. This finite continuous function is $\varepsilon\lambda_i^2$-strongly convex, since every term of the maximum has that common strong convexity. Dual feasibility gives $U_i\le u_i$ on $K_i$. At any dual equality tuple, its other coordinates attain the maximum at $v=x_i$. Their smooth convex term lies below $U_i$ everywhere and agrees there, so its tangent plane gives $\lambda_i\nabla\phi(b(x))\in\partial U_i(x_i)$.

For a finite $\kappa$-strongly convex function $U$, the minimizer $T(p)$ of $U(v)-p\cdot v$ exists by coercivity and is unique. The subgradient condition is $p\in\partial U(T(p))$. Strong monotonicity gives $\kappa|T(p)-T(q)|^2\le(p-q)\cdot(T(p)-T(q))$, so $T$ is $1/\kappa$-Lipschitz. The defining maximum for $U^*$ bounds $U^*(p+h)-U^*(p)$ between $h\cdot T(p)$ and $h\cdot T(p+h)$. Consequently $U^*$ is finite and $C^1$ with gradient $T$. Take $V_i=U_i^*$. The subgradient inclusion at equality tuples gives $x_i=F_i(b(x))$. The dual equality holds almost everywhere for every optimizer. The asserted pushforward identities and local Lipschitzness follow.
