## Statement

Use the canonical convex-potential data and fractional logarithmic quantities of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\ge0\), \(\min_D\phi=0\), satisfy convex-admissibility: every positive barycentric representation of a lattice target \(\mathbf J\in D\) satisfies \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\). Set \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\), zero elsewhere, and \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\).

At each \(\mathbf J\in D\), components have a nonempty set \(E\subseteq D\), positive coefficients with sum one and barycenter \(\mathbf J\), and a nonnegative mass \(x_E\). Distinct components may use the same support and different coefficients. The logarithmic cost is \(G_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). For \(L\ge0\), \(P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) is their maximal total mass subject to \(G_E\le L\) for positive-mass components and the original full-occurrence capacities \(\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E\le w_{\mathbf I}\). A positive coefficient charges the full component mass, including when the target itself is a source. Central mass is the singleton. Define

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}(w)
=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\,L\ge0}
\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\]

The unrestricted component formulation here equals the accepted finite LP in `488f0a583886b5fc`; its occurrence convention is also exactly that of accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`. No barycentric coefficient multiplies a source charge. The convex hull is the canonical \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\); it is not a probability law. The relation to the universal actual-mixture entropy lower bound is only the equivalence up to absolute factors in accepted fact `7c0ab5ae2756b54d`.

There are two assertions.

First, on every finite complete support \(\widetilde D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) of intrinsic rank \(\dim\operatorname{conv}(\widetilde D)\le1\), every affine potential \(\widetilde\phi\) has \(\mathcal B_{\log}(\widetilde w)/\widetilde W\ge1/2\). Here tildes mean a separate table with precisely the same definitions, with \(\widetilde\phi\) the restriction of an affine function to its affine hull and \(\widetilde w=e^{-\widetilde\phi}\) after normalization. Rank zero has score exactly one. In rank one, order the sites along their line and, at each site \(\mathbf J\), define \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-\) and \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\) as the total original weights strictly before and strictly after it. Then for every target and every cutoff,

\[
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;\widetilde w)
=\widetilde w_{\mathbf J}
+\min\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}
\qquad(L\ge0).
\tag{1}
\]

Second, in integer dimension \(n=1\), write the canonical indices \(\mathbf I\) as the scalars \(I\). Set \(D=\{-1,0,1\}\), \(\tau=\log(9/5)\), and \(\phi_I=\tau|I|\). Thus \(w_{-1}=w_1=5/9\), \(w_0=1\), and \(W=19/9\). Its complete unrestricted packing values are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\log}(-1,L;w)&=P_{\log}(1,L;w)=5/9 &&(L\ge0),\\
P_{\log}(0,L;w)&=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le L<\tau,\\
14/9,&L\ge\tau.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal B_{\log}(w)=1\) and \(\mathcal B_{\log}(w)/W=9/19<1/2\), with a gap of \(1/38\).

In particular, it is false that every complete admissible table admits an affine-potential complete replacement in its original integer dimension with normalized score no larger than its own, even when the replacement support is arbitrary. More generally, the same claim is false when arbitrary replacement supports in lattice spaces are permitted but their intrinsic rank must not exceed the original rank. It remains false if a replacement is a sequence of such affine tables whose normalized scores are required to have limit superior at most the original normalized score.

## Proof

All estimates below use the original exponential weights and the full occurrence capacities. The proof directly treats arbitrary positive barycentric supports, so it does not presume that pair constructions optimize a restricted surrogate.

First establish (1). An affine potential has \(G_E=0\) for every barycentric component at every target. Thus the feasible support family is identical at all \(L\ge0\), including zero. Fix a target \(\mathbf J\). A component containing \(\mathbf J\) is charged its entire mass at that source, so the sum of the masses of all such components is at most \(\widetilde w_{\mathbf J}\). Any component not containing \(\mathbf J\) must contain at least one site strictly on each side: a positive convex combination of sites all strictly on one side cannot equal \(\mathbf J\). Every such component therefore incurs at least its full mass on the left and at least its full mass on the right. Summing the source capacities separately over the two sides bounds the total mass of components not containing \(\mathbf J\) by both \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-\) and \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\). This proves the upper bound in (1), at every cutoff and even when the two categories also share side sources.

For a matching lower bound, take the target singleton with its full original mass \(\widetilde w_{\mathbf J}\). If one side has zero total weight, this already attains the upper bound. Otherwise choose a real affine coordinate \(t\) increasing along the support line. This is just an ordering chart; no lattice, weight, index, or volume is changed. For each left site \(\mathbf I\) and right site \(\mathbf K\), the pair \(\{\mathbf I,\mathbf K\}\) has positive coefficients

\[
\lambda_{\mathbf I}
=\frac{t(\mathbf K)-t(\mathbf J)}{t(\mathbf K)-t(\mathbf I)},
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathbf K}
=\frac{t(\mathbf J)-t(\mathbf I)}{t(\mathbf K)-t(\mathbf I)}.
\]

Their sum is one and their barycenter is \(\mathbf J\). Give this component the full mass
\(x_{\mathbf I,\mathbf K}=\widetilde w_{\mathbf I}\widetilde w_{\mathbf K}/\max\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}\). Every left source is used with total mass
\(\widetilde w_{\mathbf I}\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+/\max\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}\le\widetilde w_{\mathbf I}\), and every right source with total mass
\(\widetilde w_{\mathbf K}\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-/\max\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}\le\widetilde w_{\mathbf K}\). These are full charges \(x_{\mathbf I,\mathbf K}\), not coefficient-weighted charges. The pairs use no target source. Their total mass is exactly \(\min\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}\). Every pair has zero cost, proving the lower bound in (1) for all cutoffs.

One may also check the upper bound as an exact source-incidence LP dual: assign price one to the target and every site on a side having the smaller total weight, and zero to all other sites. Every component either contains the target or meets both sides, so its sum of source prices is at least one. The dual objective is the right side of (1). Endpoint targets and a side of zero weight are included in this argument.

Since (1) is independent of \(L\), the full cutoff maximization at every target uses \(L=0\). Choose the first site \(\mathbf J\) whose cumulative weight, including its own weight, is at least \(\widetilde W/2\). This site exists in the finite ordered support. Then \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-<\widetilde W/2\) and \(\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\le\widetilde W/2\). It follows from (1) that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\log}(\widetilde w)
&\ge\widetilde w_{\mathbf J}
+\min\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}\\
&=\widetilde W
-\max\{\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^-,\widetilde W_{\mathbf J}^+\}
\ge\widetilde W/2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Rank zero means a singleton support. Only that singleton component is possible; its mass is its original weight, every cost is zero, and its normalized score is exactly one. This proves the entire affine-class assertion without a full-dimensionality or lattice-index assumption. Completeness of the replacement support is allowed throughout; indeed the lower bound is valid even without needing that extra property.

The constant \(1/2\) is exact in this class. For the complete interval \(\{-1,0,1\}\), let \(s=(\sqrt5-1)/2\) and take the affine weights \((1,s,s^2)\) in that order. They are exponentials of an affine potential, \(s+s^2=1\), and their total weight is two. Formula (1) gives packing masses one at \(-1\), one at zero, and \(s^2\) at one. Hence their full normalized score is \(1/2\). A complete two-point interval with equal weights also attains \(1/2\).

Now verify the nonaffine witness. Its support is exactly \([-1,1]\cap\mathbb Z\), so it is complete and has rank one in the original integer dimension. The function \(\widehat\phi(x)=\tau|x|\) on \([-1,1]\) is convex, has the prescribed values at all three sites, and is the canonical convex roof: on each of the intervals \([-1,0]\) and \([0,1]\), the convex lower bound \(\tau|x|\) is attained by the two endpoint values of that interval. In particular all barycentric convex-admissibility inequalities hold. Alternatively, the globally defined function equal to \(e^{-\tau|x|}\) on \([-1,1]\) and zero off that interval is a finite-valued log-concave extension agreeing with every table zero. Normalization is exactly \(w_{\max}=1\), \(\min_D\phi=0\), as in `488f0a583886b5fc`.

At the two endpoint targets a positive barycentric representation cannot use any other site, since each endpoint is an extreme point of the complete support. Thus only its singleton is possible, giving the endpoint values in (2) for every cutoff.

At target zero, every component not containing zero must have support \(\{-1,1\}\), coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), and cost \(\tau\). Every other component contains zero and is charged its full mass at zero. In particular a three-source component has equal positive endpoint coefficients \(\theta\), with \(0<\theta<1/2\), and cost \(2\theta\tau\); allowing all these coefficients does not avoid the charge at zero.

For \(0\le L<\tau\), the endpoint pair is excluded. Every allowable component is charged at zero, so its entire feasible total mass is at most one. The singleton of mass one attains this, proving the first center case in (2). For \(L\ge\tau\), the total mass of components containing zero is at most one, and the total mass of components not containing zero is at most \(5/9\), since all of the latter use each endpoint with its full mass. Thus their total is at most \(14/9\). The singleton of mass one together with the endpoint pair of mass \(5/9\) simultaneously saturates the three original capacities and attains this upper bound. The equality cutoff \(L=\tau\) includes the pair, proving the second center case in (2).

The corresponding full-incidence dual certificates are also explicit at every cutoff. At either endpoint assign price one to that endpoint and zero elsewhere. At the center for \(L<\tau\), assign price one to zero and zero to both endpoints. At the center for \(L\ge\tau\), assign price one to zero and to \(-1\), and zero to one. Their costs are respectively \(5/9\), one, and \(14/9\); every allowable component has sum of source prices at least one in its applicable program. The primal constructions just given match these certificates. Thus no omitted target, cutoff, support, or LP can raise (2).

To optimize the full objective, it remains to compare its cutoff values exactly. For \(x>1\), differentiation gives
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\log x-2(x-1)/(x+1)]=(x-1)^2/[x(x+1)^2]>0\), and the expression in brackets is zero at one. Therefore
\(\tau=\log(9/5)>4/7>5/9\). At the endpoint targets the best ratio is \(5/9\) at zero cutoff. At the center below \(\tau\), the best ratio is one at zero cutoff. At and above \(\tau\), the best ratio is \((14/9)/(1+\tau)<1\) at \(\tau\). Consequently the FULL maximum is exactly one. Dividing by the complete original mass \(19/9\) proves its normalized value \(9/19\).

Apply (3) to any proposed affine replacement in the same integer dimension one. Its complete support can be any finite interval of integer sites or a singleton, and its score is at least \(1/2\). Thus it cannot have score at most \(9/19\). The same argument applies in any ambient lattice space if the replacement rank is at most one. It requires no containment of the new support in the old support and no cardinality bound. In particular it includes support deletion, arbitrary support perturbation along a line, complete convex faces, and a rank-zero terminal support. An incomplete deletion such as keeping \(\{-1,1\}\) is not needed or permitted as a substitute for completeness.

Limits cannot evade the obstruction. Every finite nonzero affine table in the proposed terminal class has normalized score at least \(1/2\), even if its weights, number of sites, line, or common scale vary. Hence the limit inferior of any sequence of these scores is at least \(1/2\). This excludes a terminal score of at most \(9/19\) without requiring compactness, attainment of the class infimum, or stability of a particular LP branch. If a proposed limiting table itself is nonzero and complete of rank at most one with a finite affine potential on its positive support, (3) applies directly; a singleton face has score one. The zero table is excluded because its normalized score is undefined. Scaling all weights by one common positive factor does not change gaps or normalized scores and cannot affect any of these conclusions.

This witness refutes exactly the universal affine no-increase reduction described in the statement. Its score remains positive, and it supplies no sequence with \(2^n\mathcal B_{\log}/W\) tending to zero. Accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647` supplies the depth consumer for a positive scalar lower bound; accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` supplies the complete-body realization of logarithmic data; and accepted fact `7c0ab5ae2756b54d` identifies the universal actual-mixture entropy bridge up to absolute factors. None of these facts asserts the no-increase affine reduction refuted here. A bounded-factor compression, a rank-increasing construction with a separately controlled change in the integer-dimension factor, or a different solved terminal class is not ruled out by this counterexample. No supplied READY obstacle is used as a premise.
