## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(B\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\), \(\varepsilon\ge0\), and \(R>0\). The norm \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\) is its Euclidean operator norm. Define \(f(z)=\varepsilon(Bz)_+\) for every \(z\in\mathbb R^n\), where the positive part is coordinatewise, and define the full-dimensional standard simplex \(\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:u_j\ge0,\ \sum_{j=1}^d u_j\le1\}\). Let \(Z\) be uniform on \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\), let \(U\) be independent and uniform with respect to Lebesgue measure on \(\Delta_d\), and let \(X=f(Z)+RU\). Set

\[
\eta=\frac{n^2\varepsilon^4\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4(d+1)^4(d+2)^2}{R^4},
\qquad K=18(1+\eta).
\]

For every \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), including zero normals, put \(Y=\alpha\cdot Z+b\cdot(X-\mathbb EX)\), \(\alpha'=\alpha+(\varepsilon/2)B^Tb\), and \(V=(\varepsilon/2)b\cdot(|BZ|-\mathbb E|BZ|)\), where absolute value is coordinatewise. Then \(\mathbb EY=0\), \(\operatorname{Cov}(\alpha'\cdot Z,V)=0\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2:=\mathbb EY^2
&=\frac23\|\alpha'\|_2^2+\mathbb EV^2
 +\frac{R^2\bigl((d+1)\|b\|_2^2-(\sum_{j=1}^d b_j)^2\bigr)}{(d+1)^2(d+2)}\\
&\ge\frac23\|\alpha'\|_2^2
 +\frac{R^2\|b\|_2^2}{(d+1)^2(d+2)},\\
\mathbb EY^4&\le K(\mathbb EY^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both actual central closed cap probabilities satisfy \(\mathbb P(Y\ge0)\ge1/(4K)\) and \(\mathbb P(Y\le0)\ge1/(4K)\). If \(\sigma^2>0\), the same bounds hold with strict inequalities inside the probabilities. If \(\sigma^2=0\), both closed cap probabilities equal one. In particular, every ambient closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbb EX)\) has probability at least \(1/(4K)\) under the law of \((Z,X)\).

For the original-stock conclusion, use the canonical objects

\[
\begin{aligned}
C&=\left\{(z,y)\in\mathbb R^{n+d}:z\in[-1,1]^n,\ y_j\ge f_j(z)\ (1\le j\le d),\ \sum_{j=1}^d y_j\le d\right\},\\
\Lambda&=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,\\
C_z&=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad W=\mathcal H_d(S),\\
F_{\max}&=\max_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\sum_{j=1}^d f_j(z).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(C_z\) denotes the unembedded continuous fiber; its embedded original fiber is \(\{z\}\times C_z\). The symbol \(W\) is exactly the original mixed-integer volume \(\mathcal H_d(S)\), and \(h_S\) is the original halfspace depth from the locked problem. Suppose \(F_{\max}<d\) and specialize \(R=d-F_{\max}\) in the preceding probability model and in \(K\). Then \(C\) is a convex body and, for every integral base \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\), the same-radius simplex \(P_z=f(z)+R\Delta_d\) is a subset of \(C_z\).

For measurable \(A\subseteq\Lambda\), define the original source measures \(\mu_z(A)=\mathcal H_d(A\cap(\{z\}\times C_z))\) and their restricted submeasures \(\nu_z(A)=\mathcal H_d(A\cap(\{z\}\times P_z))\). The measure \(\nu=\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\nu_z\) has mass \(3^nR^d/d!\), is dominated by \(\mathcal H_d|_S\), and its normalization is exactly the law of \((Z,X)\). Its barycenter is

\[
\mathbf y^\star=(\mathbf0,\mathbb EX)
 =\left(\mathbf0,\mathbb Ef(Z)+\frac{R}{d+1}\mathbf1\right)\in\{\mathbf0\}\times C_{\mathbf0}\subseteq S.
\]

Consequently the original measures satisfy

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{3^nR^d}{4K\,d!},
\qquad
W=\frac1{d!}\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}\left(d-\sum_{j=1}^d f_j(z)\right)^d
 \le\frac{3^nd^d}{d!}.
\]

Thus also \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge(R/d)^dW/(4K)\). No identification of \(\mathbf y^\star\) with the barycenter of the full original measure or with a uniform target-fiber centroid is assumed.

## Proof

All inputs are derived here; there are no accepted predecessor facts.

First, each coordinate of \(Z\) has mean zero, second moment \(2/3\), and fourth moment \(2/3\), and its coordinates are independent. The coordinatewise identity \(t_+=(t+|t|)/2\) gives

\[
f(Z)=\frac\varepsilon2BZ+\frac\varepsilon2|BZ|,
\qquad
Y=L+V+N,
\quad L=\alpha'\cdot Z,
\quad N=R\,b\cdot(U-\mathbb EU).
\]

All three summands have mean zero, and \(N\) is independent of \((L,V)\). The distribution of \(Z\) is invariant under \(Z\mapsto-Z\). Under this map \(L\) changes sign and \(V\) is unchanged, since \(|B(-Z)|=|BZ|\). Hence \(\mathbb ELV=0\), proving the claimed exact covariance identity. The same symmetry gives \(\mathbb EL^3V=\mathbb ELV^3=0\). Expanding the fourth moment of a sum of independent centered coordinates yields

\[
\mathbb EL^2=\frac23\|\alpha'\|_2^2,
\qquad
\mathbb EL^4=\frac43\|\alpha'\|_2^4-\frac23\sum_{i=1}^n(\alpha'_i)^4
 \le3(\mathbb EL^2)^2.
\]

Indeed, the fourth-moment expansion has terms \((2/3)\sum_i(\alpha'_i)^4\) and \(6(2/3)^2\sum_{i<j}(\alpha'_i)^2(\alpha'_j)^2\), which give the displayed formula.

We next derive the simplex moments exactly. Put \(m=d+1\), \(T_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^dU_j\), and \(T_j=U_j\) for \(1\le j\le d\). For nonnegative integers \(k_0,\ldots,k_d\), direct integration gives

\[
\mathbb E\prod_{j=0}^dT_j^{k_j}
 =\frac{d!\prod_{j=0}^dk_j!}{(d+\sum_{j=0}^dk_j)!}.
\]

For completeness, the integral before multiplication by the uniform density is \(\prod_jk_j!/(d+\sum_jk_j)!\). It follows by induction on \(d\), integrating the last coordinate after substituting \(u_d=(1-\sum_{j<d}u_j)t\). The last-coordinate integral contributes \(k_0!k_d!/(k_0+k_d+1)!\) and replaces the residual exponent \(k_0\) by \(k_0+k_d+1\) in dimension \(d-1\). The induction then gives the asserted factorial ratio. The base case is \(\int_0^1t^r(1-t)^s\,dt=r!s!/(r+s+1)!\), obtained by integration by parts for nonnegative integers \(r,s\). Taking all exponents zero gives \(\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)=1/d!\), so the uniform density is \(d!\). In particular \(\mathbb EU_j=1/m\).

Let \(s=\sum_{j=1}^db_j\), \(\gamma_0=-s/m\), and \(\gamma_j=b_j-s/m\) for \(1\le j\le d\). Thus \(\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j=0\), and \(b\cdot(U-\mathbb EU)=\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_jT_j\). Define \(p_2=\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j^2\) and \(p_4=\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j^4\). The monomial formula and the multinomial expansion imply that the \(k\)-th moment of this linear form equals

\[
\frac{k!\,d!}{(d+k)!}[t^k]\prod_{j=0}^d(1-\gamma_jt)^{-1}.
\]

Here \([t^k]\) denotes the coefficient in the formal power series. Since \(\sum_j\gamma_j=0\), the identity

\[
\log\prod_{j=0}^d(1-\gamma_jt)^{-1}
 =\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^d\gamma_j^r}{r}t^r
\]

gives the coefficients \([t^2]=p_2/2\) and \([t^4]=p_4/4+p_2^2/8\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(b\cdot U)
 &=\frac{p_2}{m(m+1)}
 =\frac{m\|b\|_2^2-s^2}{m^2(m+1)},\\
\mathbb E\bigl(b\cdot(U-\mathbb EU)\bigr)^4
 &=\frac{3p_2^2+6p_4}{m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)}
 \le9\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(b\cdot U)\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last inequality, \(p_4\le p_2^2\) and \(m(m+1)\le(m+2)(m+3)\); these also prove it without division when \(p_2=0\). Cauchy gives \(s^2\le d\|b\|_2^2\), so \(m\|b\|_2^2-s^2\ge\|b\|_2^2\). Multiplication by the appropriate powers of \(R\) therefore gives

\[
\mathbb EN^2\ge\frac{R^2\|b\|_2^2}{(d+1)^2(d+2)},
\qquad \mathbb EN^4\le9(\mathbb EN^2)^2.
\]

The covariance calculation and independence now prove the exact formula \(\sigma^2=\mathbb EL^2+\mathbb EV^2+\mathbb EN^2\) and both variance assertions in the statement.

We bound the fourth moment of \(V\) using a finite bounded-difference martingale. Write \(g(z)=(\varepsilon/2)b\cdot|Bz|\), so \(V=g(Z)-\mathbb Eg(Z)\). If \(z,z'\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\) differ only in coordinate \(i\), the Euclidean contraction property of coordinatewise absolute value gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|g(z)-g(z')|
 &\le\frac\varepsilon2\|b\|_2\,\bigl\||Bz|-|Bz'|\bigr\|_2\\
 &\le\frac\varepsilon2\|b\|_2\,\|B(z-z')\|_2
 \le\varepsilon\|b\|_2\|Be_i\|_2=:\delta_i.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(M_i=\mathbb E[g(Z)\mid Z_1,\ldots,Z_i]\), for \(0\le i\le n\), and let \(D_i=M_i-M_{i-1}\). Conditional on \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1}\), the possible values of \(M_i\) lie in an interval of length at most \(\delta_i\): differences between two choices of \(Z_i\) are expectations of differences of \(g\) with the same remaining coordinates. Thus \(D_i\) is conditionally centered and lies in an interval of length at most \(\delta_i\).

Here is the needed exponential estimate with its constant. For a centered bounded random variable \(D\) supported in an interval of length \(\delta\), put \(\psi(t)=\log\mathbb E e^{tD}\). Under exponential tilting, \(\psi''(t)\) is the variance of a distribution supported in that same interval. Its variance is at most \(\delta^2/4\), since its squared distance to the interval midpoint is at most \(\delta^2/4\), and variance is no larger than this expected squared distance. As \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), integrating the second-derivative bound on the interval between zero and \(t\) gives \(\psi(t)\le t^2\delta^2/8\) for every real \(t\). This argument applies separately to each conditional distribution of \(D_i\).

Because \(V=\sum_iD_i\), iterated conditional expectation gives

\[
\mathbb E e^{tV}\le e^{t^2Q/8},
\qquad
Q:=\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_i^2
 =\varepsilon^2\|b\|_2^2\sum_{i=1}^n\|Be_i\|_2^2
 \le n\varepsilon^2\|b\|_2^2\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2.
\]

If \(Q=0\), all the martingale increments vanish and \(V=0\). If \(Q>0\), Markov's inequality applied with \(t=4a/Q\) gives \(\mathbb P(V\ge a)\le e^{-2a^2/Q}\) for \(a>0\), and applying the same argument to \(-V\) gives \(\mathbb P(|V|\ge a)\le2e^{-2a^2/Q}\). Integration of this tail gives

\[
\mathbb EV^4
 =4\int_0^\infty a^3\mathbb P(|V|>a)\,da
 \le8\int_0^\infty a^3e^{-2a^2/Q}\,da
 =Q^2.
\]

The final equality follows by the substitution \(t=2a^2/Q\), reducing the integral to \(Q^2\int_0^\infty te^{-t}\,dt=Q^2\). Thus \(\mathbb EV^4\le Q^2\) also when \(Q=0\). The simplex variance lower bound shows, without dividing by \(\sigma\), that

\[
Q\le\frac{n\varepsilon^2\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2(d+1)^2(d+2)}{R^2}\,\sigma^2
 =\sqrt\eta\,\sigma^2.
\]

For the final fourth-moment combination, put \(s_L=\mathbb EL^2\), \(s_V=\mathbb EV^2\), and \(s_N=\mathbb EN^2\), so \(s_L+s_V+s_N=\sigma^2\). Independence of \(N\), centering, and the two odd-even fourth-order cancellations proved above give the exact expansion

\[
\mathbb EY^4
 =\mathbb EL^4+\mathbb EV^4+\mathbb EN^4
 +6\mathbb EL^2V^2+6(s_L+s_V)s_N.
\]

Cauchy yields \(\mathbb EL^2V^2\le\sqrt{\mathbb EL^4\,\mathbb EV^4}\le\sqrt3\,s_LQ\). Also

\[
3s_L^2+9s_N^2+6(s_L+s_V)s_N
 \le9(s_L+s_V+s_N)^2=9\sigma^4,
\]

because \(3s_L^2\le9(s_L+s_V)^2\) and \(6(s_L+s_V)s_N\le18(s_L+s_V)s_N\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY^4
 &\le9\sigma^4+6\sqrt3\,s_LQ+Q^2\\
 &\le\bigl(9+6\sqrt{3\eta}+\eta\bigr)\sigma^4\\
 &\le(18+4\eta)\sigma^4
 \le18(1+\eta)\sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The penultimate inequality is exactly \(6\sqrt{3\eta}\le9+3\eta\), equivalent to \((\sqrt{3\eta}-3)^2\ge0\). This proves the requested absolute constant, including zero variance. Only the finite product structure, sign symmetry, and explicit independent simplex noise were used; no log-concavity of the mixed discrete-continuous law was assumed.

We now prove the cap inference for the actual functional \(Y\). When \(\sigma^2>0\), Hölder with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\) gives

\[
\sigma^2=\mathbb E\bigl(|Y|^{2/3}|Y|^{4/3}\bigr)
 \le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Y|^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|Y|\ge\frac{\sigma^3}{\sqrt{\mathbb EY^4}}
 \ge\frac\sigma{\sqrt K}.
\]

Put \(Y_+=\max(Y,0)\) and \(Y_-=\max(-Y,0)\). Since \(\mathbb EY=0\), \(\mathbb EY_+=\mathbb EY_-=(1/2)\mathbb E|Y|\). Cauchy gives \(\mathbb EY_+\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Y>0)}\) and \(\mathbb EY_-\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Y<0)}\). Combining these inequalities proves \(\mathbb P(Y>0),\mathbb P(Y<0)\ge1/(4K)\), and hence the closed cap assertions. When \(\sigma^2=0\), the centered variable \(Y\) equals zero almost surely, so both closed cap probabilities are one. In this model the variance lower bounds further show that zero variance forces \(b=0\), then \(\alpha'=\alpha=0\), but the preceding argument already covers it.

Any closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbb EX)\), written as \(\alpha\cdot z+b\cdot y\ge t\), has \(t\le b\cdot\mathbb EX\). It therefore contains the central cap \(\alpha\cdot z+b\cdot(y-\mathbb EX)\ge0\), to which the bound just proved applies. This proves the ambient halfspace assertion.

Finally, specialize to the original set \(C\) in the statement with \(R=d-F_{\max}>0\). The constraints \(y_j\ge f_j(z)\) are equivalent to the two affine inequalities \(y_j\ge0\) and \(y_j\ge\varepsilon(Bz)_j\). Thus \(C\) is convex and closed. It is bounded because \(z\in[-1,1]^n\) and \(0\le y_j\le d\). Since \(0<R\le d\), the point \((\mathbf0,(R/(2d))\mathbf1)\) satisfies all defining inequalities strictly, including the cube constraints, so \(C\) has nonempty interior and is a convex body.

Its only integral bases are \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\). At such a base, let \(r_z=d-\sum_j f_j(z)\). By the definition of \(F_{\max}\), \(R\le r_z\le d\), and the original fiber is exactly \(C_z=f(z)+r_z\Delta_d\): subtracting \(f(z)\) leaves nonnegative coordinates with sum at most \(r_z\). It follows that \(P_z=f(z)+R\Delta_d\subseteq C_z\), \(\operatorname{vol}_d(P_z)=R^d/d!\), and \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=r_z^d/d!\). The ordinary Lebesgue volume here is exactly the \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure on the embedded fiber, since \(y\mapsto(z,y)\) is an isometry.

Thus each \(\nu_z\) is literally a restriction of its original source \(\mu_z\), not a newly weighted or renewed source. The embedded fibers for different bases are disjoint. Summing each source's restriction once gives \(\nu(A)\le\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)\) for every measurable \(A\subseteq\Lambda\), and \(\nu(\Lambda)=3^nR^d/d!\). Because the restricted fibers all have this same volume \(R^d/d!\), normalizing \(\nu\) chooses each ternary base with probability \(3^{-n}\) and then chooses uniformly from \(P_z\). The latter conditional law is exactly \(f(z)+RU\). This proves the claimed identification with \((Z,X)\) using the original submeasures.

The restricted measure's barycenter has integral base \(\mathbb EZ=\mathbf0\). Its continuous coordinate is \(\mathbb EX=\mathbb Ef(Z)+(R/(d+1))\mathbf1\), which is coordinatewise positive. Furthermore

\[
\sum_{j=1}^d\mathbb EX_j
 =\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^df_j(Z)+\frac{Rd}{d+1}
 \le F_{\max}+\frac{Rd}{d+1}
 =d-\frac R{d+1}<d.
\]

Since \(f(\mathbf0)=\mathbf0\), the actual integral fiber at the origin is \(C_{\mathbf0}=d\Delta_d\). The displayed inequalities prove \(\mathbf y^\star\in\{\mathbf0\}\times C_{\mathbf0}\subseteq S\) directly. In particular the membership calculation uses the actual original fiber, not an assumed centroid of a target simplex.

For any closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), the actual-law cap bound and domination of the restricted original measure give

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\nu(H\cap\Lambda)
 =\frac{3^nR^d}{d!}\mathbb P((Z,X)\in H)
 \ge\frac{3^nR^d}{4K\,d!}.
\]

Taking the infimum over these halfspaces is exactly the claimed lower bound for the original \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\). Finally, summing the original fiber volumes gives \(W=\sum_zr_z^d/d!\le3^nd^d/d!\). Therefore \(3^nR^d/d!\ge(R/d)^dW\), which gives the normalized bound as well. Every estimate concerns the original sources or their single restrictions, with no output renewal.
