## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $t>1$, $1/2<b\le1$, $p=d-1$, $R=2t-1$, and $x=2d(1-b)/(d+1)$. Write $B=b^d$, $S=1-B$, $A_{\max}=(2b-1)^d$, $Z=A_{\max}-S/t$, and $H=[c-x]_+^d$. For $0\le A\le A_{\max}$ and $0\le c\le b$, put
$$G(A,c)=\max\{B+tA,1+tH\}-(A+B+1+c^d)/2.$$
Its minimum over $A$ is attained at $\widehat A=\min(A_{\max},H+S/t)$ and satisfies
$$2G(\widehat A,c)=-c^d+\max\{S-A_{\max}+2tH,(1-1/t)S+RH\}.$$
If $x\ge b$, take $c=b$ to minimize over $c$. If $0<x<b$, write $H_\alpha=x^d/(\alpha^{1/p}-1)^d$ for $\alpha>1$. The minimum over $c$ is attained at
$$H=\min\{(b-x)^d,\max\{H_{2t},\min\{Z,H_R\}\}\},\qquad c=x+H^{1/d}.$$
If $b=1$, the overall minimum is zero.

For every $x\ge0,\alpha>1$, the sharp loss over $v\ge0$ is
$$\max_{v\ge0}\{v^d-\alpha[v-x]_+^d\}=x^d(1-\alpha^{-1/p})^{-p}.$$
Over $0\le v\le b$, use the same value if $x\le b(1-\alpha^{-1/p})$ and use $b^d-\alpha[b-x]_+^d$ otherwise. If $L$ is this clipped loss with $\alpha=R$, then
$$G(A,c)\ge\frac{(t-1)S-tL}{2t}.$$
In particular $G(A,c)\ge0$ uniformly in both endpoints whenever
$$\frac{1-b}{b}\le(1-R^{-1/p})\left[\frac{d(t-1)}{t(2d/(d+1))^d}\right]^{1/p}.$$

## Proof

The expression $v^d-\alpha[v-x]_+^d$ increases up to $x$ when $x>0$. Above $x$ its derivative has the sign of $v^p-\alpha(v-x)^p$, positive before $v=x/(1-\alpha^{-1/p})$ and negative after it. Substitution at this point proves the loss formula, and clipping the maximizing point proves the restricted formula. For $x=0$ the expression is $(1-\alpha)v^d$ and has maximum zero at $v=0$.

Weight the two terms of the maximum defining $G$ by $1/(2t)$ and $1-1/(2t)$. After subtracting the half-total, the coefficient of $A$ cancels and the resulting lower bound is $[(t-1)S-t(c^d-RH)]/(2t)$. This gives the clipped estimate. With the unrestricted loss in place of $L$, the displayed sufficient condition follows from $S=(1-b)(1+b+\cdots+b^{d-1})\ge d(1-b)b^{d-1}$ when $b<1$, and at $b=1$ the estimate is zero.

After subtracting the half-total, the two affine pieces in $A$ have slopes $t-1/2>0$ and $-1/2<0$, crossing at $H+S/t\ge0$. Clipping yields $\widehat A$, and substitution gives the two branches in the displayed minimum formula. Their first-minus-second difference is $H-Z$. For $x\ge b$ the remaining dependence on $c$ is just $-c^d$. For $0<x<b$, the expression decreases up to $c=x$, so minimize on $c\ge x$. The derivative of $(x+H^{1/d})^d$ for $H>0$ is $(1+x/H^{1/d})^p$, strictly decreasing from infinity to one. Thus the minimum formula is convex in $H$. Its derivative has constant term $R$ below $Z$ and $2t$ above $Z$, minus this decreasing derivative on both sides. Since $H_{2t}<H_R$, its minimizer is the median of $H_{2t},Z,H_R$ clipped to $[0,(b-x)^d]$. Finally at $b=1$ the weighted lower bound is zero and $A=c=0$ attains it.
