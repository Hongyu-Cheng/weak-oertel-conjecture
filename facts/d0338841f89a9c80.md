## Statement

Retain the canonical dyadic endpoint-disjoint matching \(\mathcal M_2\), its weights
\[
 t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}\qquad(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal M_2),
\]
and the fiber notation \(C_y,a_y,c_y,D_y,D_e\) of accepted facts `3016926516cc39df`, `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, and `9c47a4888e8a2947`. Thus
\[
 t_e\leq \frac{a_y}{4}\qquad(y\in e).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(v=(v_e)\) be any dual-feasible stress in the normalization of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`, with scalar matching weights there renamed \(t_e\). Put
\[
 U_y:=\frac12v_{e(y)},\qquad
 \sigma_y:=h_{D_y}(U_y),\qquad
 Y_+:=\{y:\sigma_y>0\},
\]
\[
 N:=|Y_+|,\qquad
 T_*:=\sum_y\sigma_y.
\tag{2}
\]
For every same-parity affine basis \(B=\{y_0,\ldots,y_n\}\subseteq Y_+\), order it so that \(\sigma_{y_0}\) is maximal. For every nontrivial partition \(I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), put
\[
 F_*(B;I,J):=
 \frac{
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}\right)
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}\right)}
 {\sigma_{y_0}}.
\]
Let \(\mathfrak F_*\) be the maximum of these scores, or zero if there is no such basis. This is the basis, ordering, and partition convention of accepted facts `3100bf8082ca09d5` and `5246ad66b0032812`, with the stress loads \(\sigma_y\) in place of the unrelated pooled weights \(\tau_y\). With the convention \(\max\varnothing=0\), one has
\[
 \boxed{R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq2^n\max_{y\in Y_+}\sigma_y},
 \qquad
 \boxed{T_*\leq \frac{N}{2^n}R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)}.
\tag{3}
\]
The zero stress satisfies (3) trivially. In particular,
\[
 N\leq4\cdot2^n
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T_*\leq4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+8\mathfrak F_*.
\tag{4}
\]
If the stress has exactly \(k\) nonzero edges, then \(N=2k\). Hence for \(n=2\), (4) holds whenever \(k\leq8\).

Now specialize to \(n=2,d=1\), and suppose the endpoint fibers of every nonzero stressed edge are nondegenerate intervals. For an edge \(e=\{u,w\}\), let \(m_e=(u+w)/2\). Then
\[
 \boxed{R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq8\omega_e},
 \qquad
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
\tag{5}
\]
and therefore every stress supported on \(k\) edges satisfies
\[
 \boxed{T_*\leq\frac{k}{8}R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus the proposed inequality holds for all such interval-fiber stresses with \(k\leq32\).

For the exact two-edge preflight, let
\[
 e_1=\{m-p,m+p\},\qquad
 e_2=\{m-q,m+q\},
\tag{7}
\]
where \(m,p,q\in\mathbb Z^2\), \(p,q\) are linearly independent, and all four endpoints are distinct. Assume the load-minimal dual optimizer of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` is nonzero and is supported on these two edges. Then
\[
 \boxed{T_*<\frac16R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)}.
\tag{8}
\]
If the endpoint parity of \(e_1\) differs from that of \(e_2\), each positive parity class has only two endpoints, so
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak F_*=0}.
\tag{9}
\]
If both edges have the same endpoint parity, all four endpoints have that parity and every three form an affine basis. In this case
\[
 \boxed{T_*\leq\frac98\mathfrak F_*}.
\tag{10}
\]
The canonical two-edge conclusions are (8)-(10). Their proofs use only the broader hypotheses that \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\) is a genuine convex body, the two edges form an arbitrary legal endpoint-disjoint off-diagonal same-parity matching, their endpoint fibers are nondegenerate intervals, and
\[
 t_e\leq\frac{a_y}{4}\qquad(y\in e).
\]
Thus (8)-(10) also hold in this broader class. Only in this broader class, the coefficient \(9/8\) is attained by a genuine rational interval-fiber body and a certified load-minimal stress, and the coefficient \(1/6\) is the best possible limiting coefficient: for every rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) there is such a two-edge body with different endpoint parities and
\[
 T_*=1,\qquad
 \mathfrak F_*=0,\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)=6+2\varepsilon.
\tag{11}
\]
No matching in these sharpness examples is asserted to arise from the retained canonical pooled weights, dyadic bins, or hard-branch construction.

Still only in this broader arbitrary-matching class, there is a genuine smallest three-edge affine-circuit calibration with three different endpoint parities. Namely, put
\[
 z_1^\pm=(-1,\pm1),\qquad
 z_2^\pm=(0,\pm2),\qquad
 z_3^\pm=(1,\pm2),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
 I_0=[-1,1],\qquad I_2=[1,3],
\]
and
\[
 C^{(3)}:=\operatorname{conv}\Bigl(
   (\{z_1^-,z_1^+,z_3^-,z_3^+\}\times I_0)
   \cup(\{z_2^-,z_2^+\}\times I_2)
 \Bigr)\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(e_i:=\{z_i^-,z_i^+\}\) and \(t_{e_i}=1/2\), this is an arbitrary legal endpoint-disjoint off-diagonal same-parity matching satisfying the numerical capacity bound \(t_{e_i}\leq a_y/4\) at each endpoint. No pooled weights, dyadic-bin provenance, or canonical hard-branch realization is asserted. Its unique load-minimal optimal dual stress is
\[
 (v_{e_1},v_{e_2},v_{e_3})=\left(\frac12,-1,\frac12\right).
\tag{14}
\]
It has
\[
 |t|=\frac32,\qquad
 R_{C^{(3)}}(t)=1,\qquad
 \Delta_{C^{(3)}}(t)=1,\qquad
 T_*=1,\qquad
 \mathfrak F_*=0,\qquad
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C^{(3)})\geq8.
\tag{15}
\]
In particular, the three-edge circuit satisfies the proposed inequality by a factor much larger than required.

## Proof

Accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947` gives, edge by edge,
\[
 \sigma_u+\sigma_w=\omega_e\leq t_e
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}),
\tag{16}
\]
and \(T_*=\sum_e\omega_e\). Hence (1) and nonnegativity give
\[
 0<\sigma_y\leq t_{e(y)}\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in Y_+).
\tag{17}
\]
The centroid depth incorporated in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` is
\[
 \delta_C(y,c_y)\geq\gamma_da_y,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d>\frac1e>\frac14.
\tag{18}
\]
The last rational comparison is also recorded in accepted fact `5246ad66b0032812`. Thus the point \(c_y\) with weight \(\sigma_y\) is admissible in the exact definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) from `be359f8a6c628483`. For the singleton \(E=\{y\}\), take the affine map with value \(c_y\). Then \(\rho_E=r_s=0\), so
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq2^n\sigma_y.
\tag{19}
\]
Maximizing (19), then summing at most \(N\) positive loads, proves (3). Equation (4) follows because \(\mathfrak F_*\geq0\). Every nonzero edge has two positive endpoint loads: accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` gives that each \(D_y\) is a convex body with the origin in its interior, and the matching is endpoint-disjoint. Thus \(N=2k\).

We next compute the exact interval geometry. If
\[
 C_y=[c_y-a_y/2,c_y+a_y/2],
\]
then the half-mass metronoid definition of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` gives
\[
 \mathcal K_y=[c_y-a_y/4,c_y+a_y/4],
 \qquad
 D_y=[-a_y/4,a_y/4].
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, the minimum and maximum means of a density of mass \(a_y/2\), bounded between zero and one, are obtained by the left and right half-intervals, and convexity supplies all intermediate means.

For one edge \(e=\{u,w\}\), convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
 \frac12(C_u+C_w)\subseteq C_{m_e}.
\tag{21}
\]
The interval on the left has length \((a_u+a_w)/2\), so, writing \(a_{m_e}\) for the length of \(C_{m_e}\),
\[
 a_{m_e}\geq\frac{a_u+a_w}{2}
 \geq\min\{a_u,a_w\}
 \geq4t_e
 \geq4\omega_e.
\tag{22}
\]
The midpoint of the interval \(C_{m_e}\) has exact fiber depth \(a_{m_e}/2\). Using this point and weight as a singleton in \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), with \(n=2\), gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq4\frac{a_{m_e}}2
 =2a_{m_e}\geq8\omega_e.
\tag{23}
\]
This proves (5). Taking the maximum edge load and using \(T_*=\sum_e\omega_e\) proves (6). Notice that no capacity values from different supports were added.

Consider the two common-midpoint edges (7). Write their endpoint intervals as
\[
 C_{m-p_i}=[c_{i,-}-a_{i,-}/2,c_{i,-}+a_{i,-}/2],
 \quad
 C_{m+p_i}=[c_{i,+}-a_{i,+}/2,c_{i,+}+a_{i,+}/2]
\]
for \(p_1=p,p_2=q\), and put
\[
 \bar c_i:=\frac{c_{i,-}+c_{i,+}}2,
 \qquad
 b_i:=\frac{a_{i,-}+a_{i,+}}2.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation (20) gives
\[
 L_i=[\bar c_i-b_i/4,\bar c_i+b_i/4],
 \qquad
 D_i=[-b_i/4,b_i/4].
\tag{25}
\]
Both edge midpoints equal \(m\), so the two dual moment equations reduce to
\[
 v_{e_2}=-v_{e_1}.
\tag{26}
\]
Write \(v_{e_1}=s\ne0\). A nonzero load-minimal optimizer has positive dual objective: if the optimum were zero, the zero stress would be an optimizer of strictly smaller load. Equivalently, the two intervals \(L_1,L_2\) are disjoint. Reverse the labels and the sign if necessary so that \(s>0\) and
\[
 \bar c_1+\frac{b_1}{4}
 <\bar c_2-\frac{b_2}{4}.
\tag{27}
\]
Put \(g:=\bar c_2-\bar c_1\). Then
\[
 g>\frac{b_1+b_2}{4}.
\tag{28}
\]
The exact stress load is
\[
 T_*=\omega_1+\omega_2
 =\frac{|s|(b_1+b_2)}4.
\tag{29}
\]
Dual feasibility and the dyadic endpoint bound give, for each \(i\),
\[
 \frac{|s|b_i}{4}=\omega_i
 \leq t_{e_i}
 \leq\frac{\min\{a_{i,-},a_{i,+}\}}4
 \leq\frac{b_i}{4},
\]
so \(|s|\leq1\). The midpoint fiber \(C_m\) contains both intervals
\[
 A_i:=\frac12(C_{m-p_i}+C_{m+p_i})
 =[\bar c_i-b_i/2,\bar c_i+b_i/2].
\tag{30}
\]
It therefore contains their convex hull. Its length \(a_m\) satisfies
\[
 a_m\geq
 \left(\bar c_2+\frac{b_2}{2}\right)
 -\left(\bar c_1-\frac{b_1}{2}\right)
 =g+\frac{b_1+b_2}{2}
 >\frac{3(b_1+b_2)}4
 \geq3T_*.
\tag{31}
\]
The singleton selector at the midpoint of \(C_m\) now gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq2a_m>6T_*,
\tag{32}
\]
which proves (8). This is a genuine single-fiber capacity witness, not a sum of incompatible support suprema.

The parity assertions are exact. Within each edge the endpoints have one parity. If the two edge parities differ, each positive parity class contains only two points, proving (9). If they agree, all four endpoints have one parity. Since \(p,q\) are linearly independent, every three of \(m\pm p,m\pm q\) are affinely independent.

Order their four positive stress loads as
\[
 s_1\geq s_2\geq s_3\geq s_4>0.
\]
The triple carrying \(s_1,s_2,s_3\) is a same-parity affine basis. In base dimension two the only nontrivial partition has one nonpivot point in each part, so the score convention of accepted facts `3100bf8082ca09d5` and `5246ad66b0032812` gives
\[
 \mathfrak F_*
 \geq
 f_{123}:=\frac{(s_1+s_2)(s_1+s_3)}{s_1}.
\tag{33}
\]
Set \(x=s_2/s_1\), \(y=s_3/s_1\), and \(z=s_4/s_1\). Then
\[
 1\geq x\geq y\geq z>0
\]
and
\[
 \frac{T_*}{f_{123}}
 =\frac{1+x+y+z}{1+x+y+xy}
 \leq\frac{1+x+2y}{1+x+y+xy}
 \leq\frac98.
\tag{34}
\]
For the last inequality, after clearing denominators the difference between the right and left sides is
\[
 9(1+x+y+xy)-8(1+x+2y)
 =1+x+(9x-7)y.
\]
If \(x\geq7/9\), this is positive. If \(x<7/9\), its minimum over \(0\leq y\leq x\) is
\[
 1+x+(9x-7)x=(1-3x)^2\geq0.
\]
Equations (33)-(34) prove (10).

The canonical proof is complete through (10). We now work only in the broader arbitrary legal endpoint-disjoint interval-fiber matching class stated after (10), and verify sharpness of both two-edge constants with genuine bodies. First take a rational \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), put
\[
 a:=\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2},
 \quad I_-=[-a-1,-a+1],
 \quad I_+=[a-1,a+1],
\]
and use the genuine body from the sharpness construction of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`,
\[
 C_\varepsilon:=\operatorname{conv}\Bigl(
 (\{\pm(1,0)\}\times I_-)
 \cup(\{\pm(0,1)\}\times I_+)
 \Bigr).
\tag{35}
\]
Its four endpoint fibers have length two. Put arbitrary legal matching weight \(t_e=1/2\) on each reflected edge. This matching is endpoint-disjoint, each edge is off-diagonal and same-parity, and \(t_e=a_y/4\) at every endpoint. No canonical pooled or dyadic provenance is claimed. By (20), both edge bodies are \([-1/2,1/2]\), while
\[
 L_-=[-1-\varepsilon/2,-\varepsilon/2],
 \qquad
 L_+=[\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2].
\tag{36}
\]
The dual equations force \((v_-,v_+)=(s,-s)\), and feasibility gives \(|s|\leq1\). Equation (36) makes the unique load-minimal optimum \((1,-1)\). Hence all four endpoint loads are \(1/4\), so \(T_*=1\), and the two edge parities differ, so \(\mathfrak F_*=0\).

The base diamond has exactly five integral points: its four vertices and the origin. The origin fiber is
\[
 C_{\varepsilon,0}=[-a-1,a+1]=[-L,L],
 \qquad L:=a+1=\frac{3+\varepsilon}{2}.
\tag{37}
\]
The four vertex fibers have maximum depth one, and the origin fiber has maximum depth \(L\). We claim
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_\varepsilon)=4L=6+2\varepsilon.
\tag{38}
\]
The origin singleton proves the lower bound. For the upper bound, consider any selector in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}\). If its base affine rank is zero, its value is at most \(4L\). If the rank is two, then its total selected weight is at most \(L+4\); residual rank zero gives value at most \(L+4\leq4L\), and residual rank one only divides this by two.

Suppose the base affine rank is one. If the residual rank is one, the selector value equals its total weight, at most \(L+2<4L\). If the residual rank is zero and at most two bases are selected, its value is at most \(2(L+1)\leq4L\). The only collinear triples among the five base points are the two coordinate-axis triples. On the axis whose endpoint-fiber center is \(-a\), write the selected endpoint points as \(-a+r_-\) and \(-a+r_+\), with \(|r_\pm|\leq1\). Affine consistency forces the selected origin point to be \(-a+(r_-+r_+)/2\). The sum of the three exact interval depths is at most
\[
 2-|r_-|-|r_+|
 +L-\left|-a+\frac{r_-+r_+}{2}\right|
 \leq3,
\tag{39}
\]
because, with \(r=(r_-+r_+)/2\),
\[
 |r_-|+|r_+|\geq2|r|,
 \qquad
 2|r|+|r-a|\geq a.
\]
The other axis is identical with \(a\) in place of \(-a\). Thus a rank-one, residual-rank-zero selector has value at most \(2\cdot3=6<4L\). This proves (38), hence (11). Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves limiting sharpness of the coefficient \(1/6\) in the broader arbitrary-matching class; at \(\varepsilon=0\) the two edge sets meet and the load-minimal stress becomes zero, explaining the strict inequality in (8).

Still in the broader arbitrary-matching class, for equality in the frame estimate, let
\[
 y_1=(1,1),\quad y_2=(-1,-1),
 \quad y_3=(1,-1),\quad y_4=(-1,1),
\]
and define the full-dimensional body
\[
 C^\square:=\operatorname{conv}\Bigl(
 (\{y_1\}\times[-5,1])
 \cup(\{y_2\}\times[-3,-1])
 \cup(\{y_3,y_4\}\times[1,3])
 \Bigr).
\tag{40}
\]
All four displayed bases are exposed vertices, so these are their actual fibers. Use the diagonal edges \(e_1=\{y_1,y_2\}\), \(e_2=\{y_3,y_4\}\), each of weight \(1/2\). This is an arbitrary legal endpoint-disjoint matching satisfying \(t_e\leq a_y/4\) at every endpoint; no canonical pooled or dyadic origin is claimed. Their edge sets and centered bodies are
\[
 L_1=[-3,-1],\quad D_1=[-1,1],
 \qquad
 L_2=[3/2,5/2],\quad D_2=[-1/2,1/2].
\tag{41}
\]
The two moment equations force \((v_1,v_2)=(s,-s)\); feasibility gives \(|s|\leq1/2\), and (41) makes \((1/2,-1/2)\) the unique load-minimal optimum. The four endpoint loads are
\[
 \left(\frac38,\frac18,\frac18,\frac18\right).
\tag{42}
\]
All four bases have parity \((1,1)\). A triple containing the first load has score
\[
 \frac{(3/8+1/8)^2}{3/8}=\frac23,
\]
while the all-small triple has score \(1/2\). Thus
\[
 T_*=\frac34=\frac98\cdot\frac23
 =\frac98\mathfrak F_*,
\]
proving genuine sharpness of the broader arbitrary-matching extension of (10).

It remains, still in the broader arbitrary-matching class, to audit the three-edge circuit (12)-(15). The six displayed base points are exposed vertices of a two-dimensional hexagon, so (13) is a full-dimensional convex body and their fibers are exactly the displayed intervals. Every endpoint volume is two, so \(t_{e_i}=1/2=a_y/4\); the three endpoint parity classes are respectively
\[
 (1,1),\qquad(0,0),\qquad(1,0).
\tag{43}
\]
Thus the matching is legal in the sense of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, satisfies the numerical bound \(t_e\leq a_y/4\), is globally endpoint-disjoint, and has no same-parity affine basis. No canonical pooled weights, dyadic bins, or hard-branch provenance are asserted. Hence \(\mathfrak F_*=0\).

The edge midpoints are \((-1,0),(0,0),(1,0)\). Scalar affine moment cancellation forces every stress to have the form
\[
 (v_{e_1},v_{e_2},v_{e_3})=\lambda(1,-2,1).
\tag{44}
\]
Here
\[
 L_1=L_3=[-1/2,1/2],
 \qquad L_2=[3/2,5/2],
 \qquad D_1=D_2=D_3=[-1/2,1/2].
\tag{45}
\]
Dual feasibility gives \(|\lambda|\leq1/2\). For \(\lambda\geq0\), the sum of the three support values in (45) is \(-2\lambda\), while for \(\lambda<0\) it is positive. Therefore the unique dual optimum is \(\lambda=1/2\), with dual value one and load
\[
 \Omega=\frac14+\frac12+\frac14=1.
\]
This certifies both optimality and load-minimality of (14). Directly assigning \(U_y=v_{e_i}/2\) at the two endpoints of each edge and using (45) gives \(T_*=\Omega=1\). An affine scalar function can hit any two of the three intervals in (45), but if it hits both outer intervals, its value at the middle midpoint lies in \([-1/2,1/2]\) and misses \(L_2\). Thus \(R_{C^{(3)}}(t)=1\). The dual value and direct primal choice with outer values \(1/2\) give \(\Delta_{C^{(3)}}(t)=1\).

Finally, the origin fiber of \(C^{(3)}\) is exactly \([-1,3]\): it contains \(I_2\) from the middle reflected edge and \(I_0\) from convex combinations of the two outer reflected edges, and no generating fiber leaves \([-1,3]\). Its midpoint has exact depth two. The origin singleton in \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}\) therefore has value \(4\cdot2=8\). This proves every assertion in (15) and completes the proof.
