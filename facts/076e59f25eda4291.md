## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body with $d\ge3$, and let $U$ be a nonempty relatively open subset of $\partial K$ that is a $C^1$ hypersurface and contains no nontrivial line segment. Let $H_j$ be finitely many closed halfspaces with nonzero normals and weights $\omega_j\ge0$. Suppose $\sum_j\omega_j\mathbf1_{H_j}(x)=b$ at every $x\in U$ off all the boundary hyperplanes of the $H_j$. For each listed unoriented plane $L$ meeting $U$ and intersecting $\operatorname{int}K$, the sum of the weights of the $H_j$ with boundary $L$ in one orientation equals the sum in the other orientation.

## Proof

Fix such an $L$. At a point of $U\cap L$, the tangent plane of $U$ is the supporting plane of $K$. It cannot equal $L$ because $L$ intersects $\operatorname{int}K$. Hence the affine linear equation for $L$ restricts to a function with nonzero differential on $U$ at every such point. The implicit function theorem shows that $T=U\cap L$ is a $C^1$ submanifold of $L$ of dimension $d-2\ge1$.

For any different listed plane $L'$, the set $T\cap L'$ is relatively closed in $T$. If $L'$ is parallel to $L$, it is empty. Otherwise $L\cap L'$ has dimension $d-2$. If it contained a relatively open patch of $T$, at any point of that patch the tangent space of $T$ would equal the direction space of $L\cap L'$. The inverse function theorem in local coordinates, applied to the orthogonal projection onto $L\cap L'$, would then give a relatively open subset of $L\cap L'$ contained in that patch. Its positive dimension would give a nontrivial segment in $U$, contrary to hypothesis. Thus $T\cap L'$ has empty interior in $T$.

The complements of these finitely many closed sets are open dense in $T$. Their intersection contains a point $v\in T$. Every plane other than $L$ has a strict margin at $v$, and the restriction of the equation for $L$ to $U$ has nonzero differential there. Thus there are nearby points of $U$ strictly on opposite sides of $L$ at which the incidences for all planes other than $L$ agree. Subtracting their two score equations gives the difference between the two orientation weights on $L$, which must be zero.

For sharpness in $d=2$, take the unit circle and the three caps $u_i\cdot x\ge1/2$ with $u_1=(1,0)$, $u_2=(-1/2,\sqrt3/2)$, and $u_3=(-1/2,-\sqrt3/2)$. The interiors of their $2\pi/3$ arcs partition the circle off their shared endpoints. Equal weights $1/3$ give constant score $1/3$ there, while each of the three distinct lines has only one orientation present. For $d=1$, a proper cutting plane is a point in the interior of an interval, so it cannot meet its boundary.
