## Statement

Assume the complete triangular-homothetic setting and notation of accepted fact `d33ef001609a8cc8`.  Thus \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) is a convex body, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\) its fiber is
\[
 C_x:=\{y\in\mathbb R^2:(x,y)\in C\},
\]
and
\[
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 C_z=c_z+\kappa_z\mathcal T,
 \qquad
 a_z=A\kappa_z^2,
 \qquad
 A:=\operatorname{area}(\mathcal T),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathcal T\) is a full-dimensional triangle with centroid zero and barycentric coordinates
\[
 \beta_i(y)=\frac13+\langle u_i,y\rangle,
 \qquad
 u_1+u_2+u_3=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Write
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
 \qquad
 U_0:=\sum_{u\in U}a_u.
\tag{3}
\]

The following conclusions hold.

1. Let
\[
 \sigma:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_r\}
\tag{4}
\]
be any lattice simplex with affinely independent vertices
\(v_0,\ldots,v_r\in Z\), where \(0\leq r\leq n\).  For
\(x\in\sigma\), let \(\lambda_0(x),\ldots,\lambda_r(x)\) be its barycentric coordinates and put
\[
 \bar c_\sigma(x):=
 \sum_{j=0}^r\lambda_j(x)c_{v_j},
 \qquad
 \bar\kappa_\sigma(x):=
 \sum_{j=0}^r\lambda_j(x)\kappa_{v_j}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then every lattice point of \(\sigma\) belongs to \(Z\), and
\[
 \bar c_\sigma(x)
 \in
 \mathsf M_{A\bar\kappa_\sigma(x)^2}(C_x)
 \qquad(x\in\sigma\cap\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, the points in (6) are the restriction of one globally affine map, and they form one complete-core profile.  In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 G\geq
 \sum_{x\in\sigma\cap\mathbb Z^n}
 A\bar\kappa_\sigma(x)^2.}
\tag{7}
\]
No lattice fiber is reused in this profile.

2. For distinct \(z,z'\in Z\), define their lattice content by
\[
 g(z,z'):=
 \gcd\bigl(|z_1-z'_1|,\ldots,|z_n-z'_n|\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]
Writing \(g:=g(z,z')\), the exact segment consequence of (7) is
\[
 \boxed{
 G\geq
 A\sum_{k=0}^{g}
 \left(
 \left(1-\frac{k}{g}\right)\kappa_z
 +\frac{k}{g}\kappa_{z'}
 \right)^2}
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 =A(g+1)
 \left[
 \kappa_z\kappa_{z'}
 +\frac{2g+1}{6g}(\kappa_z-\kappa_{z'})^2
 \right].
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{G\geq(g+1)\min\{a_z,a_{z'}\}.}
\tag{11}
\]

3. Let \(z_0^-,z_0^+,z_1^-,z_1^+\in Z\) satisfy
\[
 z_0^-+z_0^+=z_1^-+z_1^+=2w
 \qquad(w\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{12}
\]
Put
\[
 p_r:=\frac{c_{z_r^-}+c_{z_r^+}}2,
 \qquad
 \bar\kappa_r:=
 \frac{\kappa_{z_r^-}+\kappa_{z_r^+}}2
 \qquad(r=0,1),
\tag{13}
\]
and define the triangular translation distance
\[
 D_{\mathcal T}(p_0,p_1)
 :=-\sum_{i=1}^3
 \min\{\langle u_i,p_0\rangle,
          \langle u_i,p_1\rangle\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 D_{\mathcal T}(p_0,p_1)
 =\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3
 |\langle u_i,p_0-p_1\rangle|
 \geq0,
\tag{15}
\]
the midpoint fiber is positive, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \kappa_w\geq
 \min\{\bar\kappa_0,\bar\kappa_1\}
 +D_{\mathcal T}(p_0,p_1),}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 a_w\geq
 A\left(
 \min\{\bar\kappa_0,\bar\kappa_1\}
 +D_{\mathcal T}(p_0,p_1)
 \right)^2.}
\tag{17}
\]

As an exact consequence, suppose \(Q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(K:=2Q-1\), all four endpoint scales in (12) equal one, and
\[
 \bigl(\langle u_1,p_0\rangle,
       \langle u_2,p_0\rangle,
       \langle u_3,p_0\rangle\bigr)
 =\left(-\frac K3,\frac{2K}{3},-\frac K3\right),
\tag{18}
\]
\[
 \bigl(\langle u_1,p_1\rangle,
       \langle u_2,p_1\rangle,
       \langle u_3,p_1\rangle\bigr)
 =\left(\frac{2K}{3},-\frac K3,-\frac K3\right).
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \kappa_w\geq2Q,
 \qquad
 a_w\geq4AQ^2,
 \qquad
 G\geq4AQ^2.}
\tag{20}
\]
If, in addition, \(w\) is evaluated in the saturated circuit of
`d33ef001609a8cc8` and its actual affine minorants satisfy
\[
 \ell_i(w)=-\frac K3
 \qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{21}
\]
then
\[
 \xi_{i,w}\geq\frac1{3\kappa_w}>0
 \qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{22}
\]
so \(w\in V\).  If instead \(w\in U\), then
\[
 U_0\geq a_w\geq4AQ^2.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus a genuine body containing the common-midpoint calibration (18)-(21) cannot retain simultaneously a small \(U_0\) and a saturated clipped midpoint \(w\): the forced fiber is charged by (20), (22), or (23).

4. Retain also the notation of accepted fact `6ce65197bbcae680`.  In particular,
\[
 L_B=L_0+L_U,
\tag{24}
\]
and define the reduced common-slack objective
\[
 M:=
 \sup_{\rho\in\Delta_3}
 \sup_{\substack{B'\subseteq B_0,\ 0<\tau_z\leq a_z\\
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z)}}
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z.
\tag{25}
\]
Also put
\[
 a_*:=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&B_0=\varnothing,\\
 \max_{z\in B_0}a_z,&B_0\ne\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\tag{25a}
\]
For some \(\eta\in(0,1]\), assume the hard descendant inequalities of `d33ef001609a8cc8`,
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\leq\frac{\eta}{6}W,
\tag{26}
\]
and suppose that the following global gluing estimate is available:
\[
 L_0\leq3M+2G+2U_0.
\tag{27}
\]
Then the registered dispersion-charge inequality follows:
\[
 \sup_{\rho\in\Delta_3}
 \sup_{\substack{B'\subseteq B,\ 0<\tau_z\leq a_z\\
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z)}}
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z
 \geq\frac14L_B-U_0.
\tag{28}
\]

No assertion that (27) always holds is part of this lemma.  Conclusions
(7), (9)-(11), and (16)-(23) do not prove (27).  Therefore the unconditional registered inequality (28) remains open.

## Proof

We first prove the lattice-simplex profile.  Fix \(x\in\sigma\), and use its barycentric coordinates from (5).  Convexity of \(C\) gives the fiberwise Minkowski inclusion
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^r\lambda_j(x)C_{v_j}
 \subseteq C_x.
\tag{29}
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one.  Since every vertex fiber is a positive homothet of the same triangle,
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^r\lambda_j(x)C_{v_j}
 =\bar c_\sigma(x)+
   \bar\kappa_\sigma(x)\mathcal T.
\tag{30}
\]
The interpolated scale is strictly positive.  Thus (29)-(30) show that every lattice point of \(\sigma\) has a positive fiber, proving
\(\sigma\cap\mathbb Z^n\subseteq Z\).

The inner triangle in (30) has area
\(A\bar\kappa_\sigma(x)^2\) and centroid
\(\bar c_\sigma(x)\).  Its indicator is an admissible metronoid density in \(C_x\), which proves (6).  Because the vertices of \(\sigma\) are affinely independent, \(\bar c_\sigma\) is affine on their affine hull and has a globally affine extension
\[
 q_\sigma:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^2.
\tag{31}
\]

Put
\[
 E_\sigma:=\sigma\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 \tau_x:=A\bar\kappa_\sigma(x)^2,
 \qquad
 T_\sigma:=\sum_{x\in E_\sigma}\tau_x.
\tag{32}
\]
Apply the \(d=0\) specialization of accepted fact
`6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite atomic measure
\(\sum_{x\in E_\sigma}\tau_x\delta_x\).  It gives
\(\widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni\widehat m\) has
\[
 \sum_{x\in E_\sigma\cap J}\tau_x
 \geq2^{-n}T_\sigma.
\tag{33}
\]
Positive depth also forces
\(\widehat m\in\operatorname{conv}E_\sigma\): otherwise strict separation from the finite polytope
\(\operatorname{conv}E_\sigma\) would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(\widehat m\) and no profile mass, contradicting (33).

For every
\(\alpha\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_\sigma)\), affinity of
\(q_\sigma\) and (6) give
\[
 q_\sigma(\widehat m)
 =\sum_{x\in E_\sigma}\alpha_xq_\sigma(x)
 \in
 \sum_{x\in E_\sigma}
 \alpha_x\mathsf M_{\tau_x}(C_x).
\tag{34}
\]
Hence the complete metronoid core of this one selected profile is nonempty.  The arbitrary-selected-mass nonempty-core conclusion of accepted fact
`07009c5047a9f7bb`, applied with (33), yields
\[
 G\geq T_\sigma.
\tag{35}
\]
This is (7).  The profile is indexed by the set
\(E_\sigma\), so coincident representations do not duplicate any physical fiber.

For the segment conclusion, the lattice points of
\([z,z']\) are exactly
\[
 z+\frac{k}{g}(z'-z)
 \qquad(0\leq k\leq g).
\tag{36}
\]
Apply (7) to this one-dimensional lattice simplex.  Its interpolated scales are the quantities in (9), which proves that inequality.  With
\(d:=\kappa_{z'}-\kappa_z\), direct summation gives
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{g}
 \left(\kappa_z+\frac{k}{g}d\right)^2
 =(g+1)\left[
 \kappa_z\kappa_{z'}+
 \frac{2g+1}{6g}d^2
 \right],
\tag{37}
\]
proving (10).  Every interpolated scale is at least
\(\min\{\kappa_z,\kappa_{z'}\}\); using (1) proves (11).

We next prove the common-midpoint collision estimate.  Convexity gives, for \(r=0,1\),
\[
 p_r+\bar\kappa_r\mathcal T
 =\frac12C_{z_r^-}+\frac12C_{z_r^+}
 \subseteq C_w.
\tag{38}
\]
Put
\[
 \bar\kappa:=\min\{\bar\kappa_0,\bar\kappa_1\}.
\]
Shrinking the two triangles in (38) about their centroids gives
\[
 p_r+\bar\kappa\mathcal T\subseteq C_w
 \qquad(r=0,1).
\tag{39}
\]
The lower \(u_i\)-facet value of \(C_w\) is
\[
 F_i(w):=\langle u_i,c_w\rangle-\frac{\kappa_w}{3}.
\tag{40}
\]
Inclusion (39) implies
\[
 F_i(w)\leq
 \min\{\langle u_i,p_0\rangle,
          \langle u_i,p_1\rangle\}
 -\frac{\bar\kappa}{3}
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{41}
\]
Because \(u_1+u_2+u_3=0\), summing (40) gives
\(\sum_iF_i(w)=-\kappa_w\).  Summing (41) therefore yields
\[
 \kappa_w\geq
 \bar\kappa-
 \sum_{i=1}^3
 \min\{\langle u_i,p_0\rangle,
          \langle u_i,p_1\rangle\},
\tag{42}
\]
which is (16).  To verify (15), set
\(a_i:=\langle u_i,p_0\rangle\) and
\(b_i:=\langle u_i,p_1\rangle\).  Both triples sum to zero, so
\[
 \sum_i\min\{a_i,b_i\}
 =\frac12\sum_i(a_i+b_i-|a_i-b_i|)
 =-\frac12\sum_i|a_i-b_i|.
\tag{43}
\]
This proves (15), and (17) follows from
\(a_w=A\kappa_w^2\).

Under (18)-(19), equation (15) gives
\[
 D_{\mathcal T}(p_0,p_1)=K.
\tag{44}
\]
The common scale in (16) is one, so
\[
 \kappa_w\geq1+K=2Q,
 \qquad
 a_w\geq4AQ^2.
\tag{45}
\]
The case \(r=0\) of the lattice-simplex profile, or directly the singleton selected profile, gives \(G\geq a_w\), proving (20).

For the threshold consequence, (38) and (18)-(19) give, separately for every \(i\),
\[
 F_i(w)\leq-\frac{K+1}{3}
 =\ell_i(w)-\frac13.
\tag{46}
\]
The normalized threshold definition in `d33ef001609a8cc8` is equivalently
\[
 \kappa_w\xi_{i,w}=\ell_i(w)-F_i(w).
\tag{47}
\]
Equations (46)-(47) prove (22), and saturation then puts \(w\) in \(V\).  If \(w\in U\), its area is one of the nonnegative summands defining \(U_0\), which proves (23).  This establishes every assertion in part 3.

It remains to prove the conditional reduction.  Accepted fact
`d33ef001609a8cc8` and the hard inequalities (26) give
\[
 L_B\geq3\eta W-8G\geq10G.
\tag{48}
\]
Accepted fact `6ce65197bbcae680` gives
\[
 L_U\leq\frac{10}{9}U_0.
\tag{49}
\]
The accepted statement is strict in its nonempty dominant-star case; the weak form (49) is all that is needed here.  Equations (24), (48), and (49) imply
\[
 G\leq\frac{L_B}{10}
 =\frac{L_0+L_U}{10}
 \leq\frac{L_0}{10}+\frac{U_0}{9}.
\tag{50}
\]

If
\[
 L_0\leq4a_*+\frac{26}{9}U_0,
\tag{51}
\]
then conclusion 3 of `6ce65197bbcae680` already gives the registered dispersion-charge inequality.  It remains to consider the strict high-dispersion alternative
\[
 L_0>4a_*+\frac{26}{9}U_0,
\tag{52}
\]
which in particular gives
\[
 L_0>\frac{26}{9}U_0>\frac{10}{9}U_0.
\tag{53}
\]
Assuming (27), equation (50) gives
\[
 3M\geq L_0-2G-2U_0
 \geq\frac45L_0-\frac{20}{9}U_0,
\tag{54}
\]
and hence
\[
 M\geq\frac4{15}L_0-\frac{20}{27}U_0.
\tag{55}
\]
The difference between the last expression and the reduced target in
`6ce65197bbcae680` is
\[
 \left(\frac4{15}L_0-\frac{20}{27}U_0\right)
 -\left(\frac14L_0-\frac{13}{18}U_0\right)
 =\frac{L_0}{60}-\frac{U_0}{54}>0,
\tag{56}
\]
where (53) was used in the final inequality.  Thus
\[
 M\geq\frac14L_0-\frac{13}{18}U_0.
\tag{57}
\]
Conclusion 2 of `6ce65197bbcae680` says that (57) suffices for the full objective over \(B\) to be at least
\(L_B/4-U_0\), which is (28).  This proves the conditional reduction.  Since no step above proves hypothesis (27), the gluing inequality and the unconditional registered conclusion remain open.
