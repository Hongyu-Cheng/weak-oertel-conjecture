## Statement

All logarithms except \(\log_2\) are natural. Fix a positive integer \(M\) and a real number \(R\ge1\). Write \(C_{\mathrm{occ}}(M,U)\) for the finite nonnegative constant called \(C(M,T)\) in accepted fact 31c9b0b9219d18d9, with its score-bound parameter \(T\) set to \(U\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_R&=\lceil\log_2(2R)\rceil,&
\beta_R&=2^{m_R+1}-1,&
T_*&=\beta_R\log(2048M^3),\\
U&=RT_*,&
C_{\mathrm{lab}}&=\max\lbrace512M^3,\,2R^2\beta_R^2\rbrace,&
C_0&=C_{\mathrm{occ}}(M,U),\\
c_{M,R}&=\frac14\exp(-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2-C_0)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(m_R\ge1\) and \(\beta_R\ge3\); all constants in (1) depend only on \(M,R\).

Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For every \(i\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, with positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) at every \(x\in A_i\). Assume every coordinatewise parity class contains at most \(M\) atoms of \(A_i\). For every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\), require
$$
j=\frac{x+y}{2}\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{2}
$$
For every factor having an unequal-weight same-parity pair, assume there is a number \(T_i>0\) such that all such pairs satisfy
$$
T_i\le\left|\frac12\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|\le RT_i.
\tag{3}
$$
Factors with no unequal-weight same-parity pair are allowed. There are no bounds on the number of weight values, midpoint centers, or pairs sharing a midpoint, and no common-endpoint-weight hypothesis.

Retain the full original normalizers, probability laws, and occupied parity counts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&
p_{i,x}&=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&
N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i,&
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}&=\frac{\prod_iw_{i,x_i}}{\prod_iZ_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
All laws are extended by zero off their supports. With the actual ambient closed-halfspace convention of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, for every supported \(\mathbf j\) define
$$
\tau_P(\mathbf j)=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,
\qquad
M_r(P)=\sum_{\mathbf j}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\quad(r\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace).
\tag{5}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_{M,R}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-2},
\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_{M,R}^{3/2}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-3}.
\tag{6}
$$
The coefficient is paid once for the entire product. Apart from its stated dependence on \(M,R\), it is independent of all dimensions, weights, occupied counts, band parameters, and the number of factors. Singleton and lower-dimensional supports are included.

For clarity, the scalar quantities used in (6) retain the definitions of 8a51b83b22717cf1. Suppress the factor index and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},&
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},&
\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j,\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace x\in R_j:w_x=w_j\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b_{\mathrm{equal}}&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2,\\
\widehat\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j),&
E&=K^2b_{\mathrm{equal}},&
F&=K^2b(p),\qquad G=F-E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Here \(j\in A\), and \(\kappa_j,Y_j\) are defined on \(R_j\). The full reflected quantities agree with 445a9e668ee1a705, and the hats and \(E,F,G\) agree with 98948b8e373afa7e, always using the occupied count \(K\). Every full row includes both orientations of each noncentral pair and its central atom once.

One always has \(E\ge M^{-2}\) and \(F\ge1\). If \(E<1\), an unequal-weight pair exists, so a band parameter \(T>0\) from (3) is available. If also \(T\ge T_*\), then \(F>1\), and with \(d=-\log E\), \(g=\log F\), and \(\theta=T/\beta_R\),
$$
d\le512M^3e^{-\theta}g,\qquad
\nu\le\frac54R^2\beta_R^2\theta^2g.
\tag{8}
$$
Consequently \(d\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}e^{-\theta}g\) and
\(\nu\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}\theta^2g\). Selecting every same-weight row of the factor has logarithmic scaled energy \(-d\) and score price zero; selecting every full row has logarithmic scaled energy \(g\) and score price \(\nu\). All centers remain in either selection.

## Proof

Fix one factor. For \(x\in R_j\), the endpoints \(x,2j-x\) have the same coordinatewise parity. Equation (2), including the central case directly, gives \(p_xp_{2j-x}=p_j^2\). Thus every geometric row summand is \(p_j\), and \(\kappa_j\) is uniform on its full row. The same equality shows that \(w_x=w_j\) if and only if both endpoints have weight \(w_j\), and equal endpoint weights necessarily equal the positive midpoint weight. Hence \(\widehat R_j\) consists exactly of the equal-weight reflection orbits. It is symmetric about \(j\), contains \(j\), and has score zero throughout. All row masses and \(b(p),b_{\mathrm{equal}}\) are positive.

The occupancy hypothesis gives \(|A|\le MK\). Hölder's inequality and the central summands yield
$$
E\ge K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3
\ge\frac{K^2}{|A|^2}\ge\frac1{M^2}.
\tag{9}
$$
For each occupied parity class \(\eta\), write
\(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\), let
\(\mu_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x\), and put \(R_\eta=K\mu_\eta\).
The map \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) bijects full oriented row entries with ordered same-parity pairs. Its inverse midpoint lies in \(A\) by (2). The exact midpoint product makes each corresponding contribution \(p_j\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\) equal to \(p_xp_{2j-x}\). Thus, with \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\),
$$
\chi(p)=\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2\ge\frac1K,
\qquad b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2,\qquad F\ge1.
\tag{10}
$$
Diagonal pairs are counted once. If there is no unequal-weight same-parity pair, the full rows coincide with their same-weight rows, so \(E=F\ge1\) and every score vanishes. In particular this handles \(M=1\).

Assume now \(E<1\). Let \(\mathscr Q_{\ne}\) be the set of all unordered pairs of distinct same-parity atoms with unequal weights. For \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\), let \(\eta(e)\) be its source parity class, let \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\), and put
\(s_e=\frac12|\log(p_{u_e}/p_{v_e})|\in[T,RT]\).
Set \(\rho_j=Kp_j\) for every \(j\in A\) and \(\rho_e=\rho_{j_e}\).
The \(\rho_j\) retain their meaning from 8a51b83b22717cf1 and equal the scaled atoms \(t_j\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9. The pair index is necessary here because one class can contain multiple mixed pairs. Exact midpoint equality gives
\(\rho_e^2=\rho_{u_e}\rho_{v_e}\).

Use
$$
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}
=\sum_{\eta\text{ occupied}}(R_\eta-1)^2
=K^2\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2-K,
\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}
=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}}\rho_e^2.
\tag{11}
$$
The defect is exactly the unnormalized occupied defect of 8a51b83b22717cf1 and 98948b8e373afa7e; the normalized \(\mathcal D\) in 31c9b0b9219d18d9 is \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\). The loss is twice the sum over all mixed unordered pairs. Under occupancy two each mixed class has exactly one pair, so (11) is exactly the class-indexed loss of 8a51b83b22717cf1. No identity equating a pair's endpoint mass with its whole class mass is assumed for general \(M\).

Deleting the two orientations of every mixed pair from (10) gives
$$
K\widehat\chi(p)
=1+\frac{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}}{K},
\qquad
E\ge\bigl(K\widehat\chi(p)\bigr)^2.
\tag{12}
$$
Since \(E<1\) and \(\widehat\chi(p)>0\),
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\).

For the rest of the scalar proof assume \(T\ge T_*\). Because
\(\beta_R\ge1\) and \(M\ge1\), this implies
$$
T\ge\log(2048M^3)>2\log(2M),\qquad
T>\log(4M),\qquad T>2\log2,
\qquad
4M^2e^{-2T}\le\frac12,\qquad 16e^{-T}\le\frac14.
\tag{13}
$$
For example \(2048M^3>(2M)^2\) and \(2048M^3>4M\), while
\(4M^2/(2048M^3)^2\le1/2\); these verify the asserted thresholds.

Call a source class balanced when \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\), and define
$$
\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}
=\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}:R_{\eta(e)}\in[1/2,2]\rbrace,
\qquad
\mathcal B_2=\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}}\rho_e^2.
\tag{14}
$$
Thus \(\mathcal B_2\) sums over source pairs, allowing multiple contributions from one source class and from one midpoint. For \(M=2\), it agrees exactly with \(\mathcal B_2\) in 8a51b83b22717cf1.

Order the endpoints of each mixed pair so that \(\rho_{u_e}<\rho_{v_e}\). Then \(\rho_{u_e}/\rho_{v_e}=e^{-2s_e}\le e^{-2T}\), so
\(\rho_e^2=\rho_{u_e}\rho_{v_e}\le e^{-2T}\rho_{v_e}^2\le e^{-2T}R_{\eta(e)}^2\).
There are at most \(M(M-1)/2\) unordered pairs in a class. For an unbalanced class, \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\). Summing first within each such class, and then over its distinct class defect, gives
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}
\le2\mathcal B_2+
M^2e^{-2T}\sum_{\eta\text{ unbalanced}}R_\eta^2
\le2\mathcal B_2+4M^2e^{-2T}\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{15}
$$
Here \(2\binom{M}{2}\le M^2\) includes all mixed pairs, not only one chosen pair. Equations (12), (13), and (15) imply
$$
\mathcal B_2>0,\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<4\mathcal B_2,\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\le2\mathcal B_2.
\tag{16}
$$
If \(\mathcal B_2=0\), they would imply
\((1-4M^2e^{-2T})\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<0\), which is impossible.

Write \(a_{\mathrm{eq}}=K\widehat\chi(p)\) for the next calculation. Then \(0<a_{\mathrm{eq}}<1\), and (9), (12), and (16) yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-E&\le1-a_{\mathrm{eq}}^2
\le2(1-a_{\mathrm{eq}})
=\frac{2(\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})}K
\le\frac{4\mathcal B_2}{K},\\
d=-\log E&\le\frac{1-E}{E}
\le M^2(1-E)\le\frac{4M^2\mathcal B_2}{K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
For every \(e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}\), the larger endpoint has scaled mass at most two, so the same endpoint-ratio estimate gives
\(\rho_e\le2e^{-T}\).
There are at most \(K M(M-1)/2\) mixed pairs in total. Consequently
$$
\mathcal B_2\le2M^2Ke^{-2T},
\qquad d\le8M^4e^{-2T}.
\tag{18}
$$

We also need a lower bound on the midpoint mass of every pair in a balanced source class. Its largest scaled atom is at least the class mean \(R_\eta/|\mathcal C_\eta|\ge1/(2M)\). Every other atom is at least \(e^{-2RT}\) times that largest mass: distinct unequal atoms satisfy the upper bound in (3), and equal atoms satisfy the same ratio bound. Thus every atom in that class is at least \(e^{-2RT}/(2M)\). Applying the exact midpoint product to any of its pairs gives
$$
\frac{e^{-2RT}}{2M}\le\rho_e\le2e^{-T}
\qquad(e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}).
\tag{19}
$$
The lower bound uses the whole class's largest atom; it does not replace the source class by a two-atom class.

Define a finite sequence of thresholds by
$$
\mathsf T_0=\frac32T,\qquad
\mathsf T_\ell=2\mathsf T_{\ell-1}-\log(2M)
\quad(1\le\ell\le m_R).
\tag{20}
$$
These are cutoffs for minus the logarithm of scaled midpoint mass, not cutoffs for the pair scores \(s_e\).
The explicit formula is
\(\mathsf T_\ell=2^\ell(3T/2-\log(2M))+\log(2M)\).
By (13), these thresholds strictly increase and
\(\mathsf T_\ell\ge2^\ell T+\log(2M)\).
As \(2^{m_R}\ge2R\), one has
\(\mathsf T_{m_R}\ge2RT+\log(2M)\).
For \(0\le\ell\le m_R\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell)}
&=\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}:\rho_e\ge e^{-\mathsf T_\ell}\rbrace,\\
\mathcal B_2^{(\ell)}
&=\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell)}}\rho_e^2,
\qquad
\mathcal J_\ell
=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell)}\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
Put \(\mathcal B_2^{(-1)}=0\).
Equation (19) implies
\(\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(m_R)}=\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}\) and
\(\mathcal B_2^{(m_R)}=\mathcal B_2\).
Each \(\mathcal J_\ell\) consists of distinct centers, however many source pairs share one midpoint.

Every \(j\in\mathcal J_\ell\) satisfies
$$
e^{-\mathsf T_\ell}\le\rho_j\le2e^{-T}<\frac1{2M}.
\tag{22}
$$
For those centers whose own parity class is unbalanced, each distinct target class contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\), and contains at most \(M\) centers. Thus (16) and (22) give
$$
\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal J_\ell\\
                 \text{own class unbalanced}}}\rho_j
\le(2e^{-T})M(4\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})
\le32Me^{-T}\mathcal B_2.
\tag{23}
$$
This counts distinct target classes and distinct centers. A target class may also supply source pairs; no separate source charge is being added to (23). Its different instances for different \(\ell\) are separate inequalities and are not summed.

Fix once and for all a largest scaled atom \(z_\eta\) in each balanced class \(\eta\), breaking ties by lexicographic order on \(A\). Then
$$
\frac1{2M}\le\frac{R_\eta}{|\mathcal C_\eta|}
\le\rho_{z_\eta}\le R_\eta\le2.
\tag{24}
$$
If \(j\in\mathcal J_\ell\) has balanced own class \(\eta(j)\), (22)--(24) imply
\(\rho_j<\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}\), so \(j\ne z_{\eta(j)}\). Define the unordered pair
\(e(j)=\lbrace j,z_{\eta(j)}\rbrace\).
It is a mixed source pair in a balanced source class, and its midpoint belongs to \(A\). Exact midpoint equality and (24) give
$$
\rho_{e(j)}^2=\rho_j\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}},
\qquad
\rho_j\le2M\rho_{e(j)}^2.
\tag{25}
$$
Its midpoint mass is strictly larger than \(\rho_j\), since
\(\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}>\rho_j\).

For \(1\le\ell\le m_R\), (20), (22), and (24) further give
$$
\rho_{e(j)}
\ge\sqrt{\frac{e^{-\mathsf T_\ell}}{2M}}
=\exp\left(-\frac{\mathsf T_\ell+\log(2M)}2\right)
=e^{-\mathsf T_{\ell-1}}.
\tag{26}
$$
Hence \(e(j)\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell-1)}\).
The map \(j\mapsto e(j)\) is injective on distinct balanced-target centers. Pairs from different own parity classes cannot coincide. Within one class the atom \(z_\eta\) was fixed once, and every target center is different from \(z_\eta\), so equality
\(\lbrace j,z_\eta\rbrace=\lbrace j',z_\eta\rbrace\) implies \(j=j'\).
Consequently, for \(1\le\ell\le m_R\),
$$
\sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal J_\ell\\
                 \text{own class balanced}}}\rho_j
\le2M\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell-1)}}\rho_e^2
=2M\mathcal B_2^{(\ell-1)}.
\tag{27}
$$
Several image pairs may lie in one source class or share their new midpoint. Both are permitted: the right side sums over pairs, with exactly the required multiplicities.

At level zero no target center has a balanced own class. Otherwise its pair \(e(j)\) just constructed would have score at least \(T\), so its smaller endpoint \(j\) would satisfy
\(\rho_j\le\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}e^{-2T}\le2e^{-2T}\).
But \(j\in\mathcal J_0\) implies \(\rho_j\ge e^{-3T/2}\), and
\(e^{-3T/2}>2e^{-2T}\) because \(T>2\log2\). This is a contradiction.
Together with (23) and (27), this proves for every \(0\le\ell\le m_R\) that
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}\rho_j
\le32Me^{-T}\mathcal B_2+2M\mathcal B_2^{(\ell-1)}.
\tag{28}
$$
Balanced class endpoints \(R_\eta=1/2,2\) and midpoint cutoff equalities
\(\rho_e=e^{-\mathsf T_\ell}\) have been included throughout. All strict exclusions used above follow from (13), so no equality boundary has been removed.

For \(j\in A\), put \(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}=|\widehat R_j|\), let
\(r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\) count all mixed unordered source pairs with midpoint \(j\), and let \(r_{\ell,j}\) count those in
\(\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(\ell)}\) with midpoint \(j\).
Distinct reflection orbits at one center are disjoint, even if a source class has many atoms. Thus
\(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}\ge1\),
\(|R_j|=h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\), and
\(r_{\ell,j}\le r_{\mathrm{mix},j}\).
Using the exact geometric row summands gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
G&=\frac1K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
\bigl((h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j})^2-h_{\mathrm{eq},j}^2\bigr)
\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}r_{\ell,j}^2\rho_j^3,\\
\mathcal B_2^{(\ell)}
&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}r_{\ell,j}\rho_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz on distinct centers, using the vectors
\((r_{\ell,j}\rho_j^{3/2})_j\) and \((\rho_j^{1/2})_j\), and then (28)--(29), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\mathcal B_2^{(\ell)}\bigr)^2
&\le
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}r_{\ell,j}^2\rho_j^3\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_\ell}\rho_j\right)\\
&\le\frac{KG}{4}
\bigl(32Me^{-T}\mathcal B_2+2M\mathcal B_2^{(\ell-1)}\bigr)\\
&=\frac{MKG}{2}
\bigl(16e^{-T}\mathcal B_2+\mathcal B_2^{(\ell-1)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
$$
If the source subset is empty, its two sums and \(\mathcal B_2^{(\ell)}\) are zero, so (30) remains valid without division by a zero center sum. At level \(m_R\), its positive left side already implies \(G>0\).

Normalize the recurrence by
$$
\xi_\ell=\frac{\mathcal B_2^{(\ell)}}{\mathcal B_2},
\qquad
\xi_{-1}=0,\qquad
\zeta=\frac{MKG}{2\mathcal B_2},
\qquad
\epsilon=16e^{-T}.
\tag{31}
$$
Then \(\xi_{m_R}=1\), \(0<\epsilon\le1/4\), and
\(\xi_\ell^2\le\zeta(\epsilon+\xi_{\ell-1})\) for \(0\le\ell\le m_R\).
First, \(\zeta\ge\epsilon\). Indeed, if \(0\le\zeta<\epsilon\), then
\(\xi_0^2<\epsilon^2\). Inductively, \(\xi_{\ell-1}\le2\epsilon\) would imply
\(\xi_\ell^2<3\epsilon^2\), hence \(\xi_\ell<2\epsilon\).
This would give \(\xi_{m_R}<2\epsilon\le1/2\), a contradiction.

A second induction yields
$$
\xi_\ell\le
2\epsilon^{1/2^{\ell+1}}\zeta^{\,1-1/2^{\ell+1}}
\qquad(0\le\ell\le m_R).
\tag{32}
$$
The base case has the stronger bound \(\xi_0\le\sqrt{\epsilon\zeta}\).
For the induction step, \(\zeta\ge\epsilon\) implies
\(\epsilon\le\epsilon^{1/2^\ell}\zeta^{1-1/2^\ell}\).
Substituting (32) at level \(\ell-1\) in the recurrence therefore gives
$$
\xi_\ell
\le\sqrt3\,\epsilon^{1/2^{\ell+1}}\zeta^{\,1-1/2^{\ell+1}}
\le2\epsilon^{1/2^{\ell+1}}\zeta^{\,1-1/2^{\ell+1}}.
$$
At \(\ell=m_R\), use \(\xi_{m_R}=1\) and raise the rearranged inequality to the positive power \(2^{m_R+1}/\beta_R\). This gives
$$
\zeta
\ge2^{-2^{m_R+1}/\beta_R}\epsilon^{-1/\beta_R}
=2^{-1-5/\beta_R}e^{T/\beta_R}
\ge\frac1{64}e^{T/\beta_R},
\tag{33}
$$
where the last inequality uses \(\beta_R\ge1\).
Combining (17), (31), and (33), we obtain
$$
G\ge\frac{e^{T/\beta_R}\mathcal B_2}{32MK}>0,
\qquad
d\le128M^3e^{-T/\beta_R}G.
\tag{34}
$$

Put \(\theta=T/\beta_R\). At the chosen threshold,
\(\theta\ge\log(2048M^3)\), so (34) gives \(d\le G/16\).
If \(0<G\le1\), then \(1-E\le-\log E=d\), whence
\(F=E+G\ge1+G/2>1\).
Integrating \(1/(1+u)\ge1/2\) for \(0\le u\le G/2\) gives
\(g=\log F\ge G/4\). Thus \(d\le512M^3e^{-\theta}g\).
If \(G\ge1\), then \(E\ge M^{-2}\) implies
\(F\ge1+M^{-2}>1\) and
\(g\ge\log(1+M^{-2})\ge1/(M^2+1)\ge1/(2M^2)\).
Equation (18) gives
$$
d\le8M^4e^{-2T}
\le16M^6e^{-2T}g
\le\frac{M^3}{128}e^{-\theta}g
\le512M^3e^{-\theta}g.
\tag{35}
$$
For the third inequality, \(2T-\theta\ge\theta\) since \(\beta_R\ge1\), and
\(e^{-\theta}\le(2048M^3)^{-1}\).
Thus \(F>1\) and the first inequality in (8) hold in every case, including \(G=1\). In particular
\(d\le g/4\), using the specific constant \(512M^3\) before enlarging to \(C_{\mathrm{lab}}\).

Uniformity of the full row law gives score zero at the center and the two scores \(\pm s_e\) for every mixed pair with that midpoint. Thus the full score numerator is exactly
$$
F\nu=\frac2K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{mix},j})
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\\j_e=j}}s_e^2.
\tag{36}
$$
Since \(s_e\le RT\) and
\(2r(h+2r)\le(h+2r)^2-h^2\) for \(r\ge0\), \(h\ge1\),
the exact expression for \(G\) in (29) proves \(F\nu\le(RT)^2G\).
Also \(G/F=1-E/F\le\log(F/E)=g+d\). Consequently
$$
\nu\le(RT)^2(g+d)
\le\frac54R^2T^2g
=\frac54R^2\beta_R^2\theta^2g.
\tag{37}
$$
This proves (8), and both label bounds with \(C_{\mathrm{lab}}\).

For factors with \(E<1\) and \(T<T_*\), apply accepted fact 31c9b0b9219d18d9 with its occupancy parameter equal to the present \(M\), and its fixed score bound set to \(U=RT_*\). Every row score is zero or has magnitude at most \(RT<U\).
Its midpoint and closure assumptions are (2); normalization by the full \(Z\) changes neither equality nor scores. Its occupied count is this \(K\), its full energy \(A_{\mathcal J}\) is this \(F\), and its \(H_p,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu\) agree with (7). It gives

F\ge1,\qquad \nu\le C_0\log F.
\tag{38}
$$
Only its full-row scalar assertion is used here.

We now perform one selection for the entire product, using the binning mechanism of 98948b8e373afa7e and 8a51b83b22717cf1 with the estimates just proved. To specify the notation map, every proof capacity \(k_i\) in 98948b8e373afa7e is set to the present occupied \(K_i\). Its label scale \(T_i\) is replaced by \(\theta_i=T_i/\beta_R\), its label constant by \(C_{\mathrm{lab}}\) in (1), and its bounded-score price constant by \(C_0\). No parity-two, common-score, or common-endpoint scalar assertion from that fact is invoked.

If \(E_i\ge1\), choose every same-weight row of factor \(i\). This includes every factor without unequal-weight pairs. If \(E_i<1\) and \(T_i<T_*\), choose every full row and use (38). The remaining factors have \(E_i<1\), \(T_i\ge T_*\), and labels \((d_i,g_i,\theta_i)\) satisfying (8). Retaining a label means choosing every full row of that factor; discarding it means choosing every same-weight row.

For every integer \(m\ge0\), let \(I_m\) contain the remaining labels with
\(\theta_i\in[m,m+1)\), and set \(G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i\).
Retain the whole bin exactly when \(G_m\ge4C_{\mathrm{lab}}(m+1)^2\).
There are finitely many nonempty bins. For a retained bin put
\(W_m=C_{\mathrm{lab}}(m+1)^2G_m\).
Its total price is at most \(W_m\), and its retention inequality gives
\(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\).
For a discarded bin its total logarithmic deficit is at most
$$
\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i
\le C_{\mathrm{lab}}e^{-m}G_m
<4C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2.
\tag{39}
$$
An empty bin contributes zero. The geometric-series identity
\(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\), obtained by differentiating the geometric series for \(0\le r<1\), and \(e^{-1}<1/2\) show that the total discarded deficit is at most \(48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2\).

Use stars for the chosen rows and leave hats for the same-weight rows of (7). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&

V_{i,j}^*&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&

\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
$$
Here \(Y_{i,j}\) is the score in (7), restricted to the selected row. These stars have the same meaning as the selected-row quantities in 98948b8e373afa7e and 8a51b83b22717cf1, with all original probabilities unchanged. A full selection gives the full quantities. A same-weight selection gives \(b_i^*=b_{\mathrm{equal},i}\) and \(\nu_i^*=0\). Every selected row contains its positive-mass central atom, so all auxiliary laws are defined and every original center remains.

Let \(B_{\log}\) be the sum of \(\log F_i\) over the factors using (38), with empty sum zero. It is nonnegative, and those factors have total price at most \(C_0B_{\log}\). The initially chosen same-weight factors have nonnegative logarithmic scaled energy and price zero. Therefore, with \(S_g=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*)\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g&\ge B_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2,\\
\sum_i\nu_i^*&\le C_0B_{\log}+\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
$$
Subadditivity of the square root and \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}
&\ge B_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_0B_{\log}}-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2\\
&\ge-C_0-48C_{\mathrm{lab}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
$$
The last inequality is
\((\sqrt{B_{\log}}-\sqrt{C_0})^2\ge0\).
This calculation includes an empty bounded-score group, no labels, and no retained bins.

We finally convert the selected quantities to actual halfspaces under the full original product. We prove this directly, following the reflection convention of 445a9e668ee1a705. Each selected row is symmetric about \(j\) and contains \(j\). Reflection preserves \(\kappa_{i,j}^*\) and negates \(Y_{i,j}\), so the score has mean zero. Direct substitution gives, for every \(x\in R_{i,j}^*\),
$$
p_{i,x}=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}
=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},
\qquad
b_i^*=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{43}
$$
The denominator is the full original \(Z_i\), and the center sum includes every atom of \(A_i\).

For a supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
$$
The geometric row mass on the selected product row factors as \(H_P^*\).
Independence and the zero score means give
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}=0\) and
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\).
Multiplying (43) yields the exact likelihood identity
\(P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}\),
where \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,x_i}/\prod_iZ_i\) remains the original law.

Every closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflected pair \(X,2\mathbf j-X\): an affine inequality that strictly excluded both endpoints would strictly exclude their midpoint. It also contains the central atom. Simultaneous reflection preserves the selected product row and its geometric law, and negates its score. Summing the smaller original mass in every noncentral orbit and half the central mass, then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}
\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral pair twice before its factor \(1/2\), and counts the central atom once before that factor. Thus it uses only half the available central mass and includes singleton rows, lower-dimensional supports, zero variance, and all containing closed halfspaces with boundary atoms.

Let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\). Equations (40) and (44) give
$$
P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2
=\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\prod_iq_{i,j_i}^*,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*.
$$
Squaring (45), summing under the original \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under this auxiliary center law yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)
\ge c_{M,R}\prod_iK_i^{-2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
$$
where the last inequality is (1) and (42).
The selected rows and auxiliary laws have neither conditioned nor renormalized an original probability. Finally, Jensen under that same original probability gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge c_{M,R}^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}.
$$
This proves (6). Every capacity throughout is an occupied count \(K_i\). The dependence on the fixed \(M,R\) in (1) is retained, so the theorem does not supply a coefficient uniform in unbounded occupancy or score width and does not prove the locked universal conjecture.
