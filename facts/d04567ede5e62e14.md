## Statement

Use the exact body and original continuous coordinates of accepted fact 4f09fb34a7d37fc1. Namely, let \(n\ge100\) be an integer, let \(k\) be rational with \(1\le k\le n/100\), and put \(m=n-1\), \(d=3n-2\), and \(R=n/2+k\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
C=\biggl\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):\;&
\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-\ge\mathbf0,\qquad
g_i=t_i^+-t_i^-\quad(1\le i\le m),\\
&y_i\ge|z_i-1/2-g_i|\quad(1\le i\le m),\\
&y_n\ge\left|z_n-1/2+\sum_{i=1}^m g_i\right|,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n y_i+\sum_{i=1}^m(t_i^++t_i^-)\le R
\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), let \(C_{\mathbf z}\) be its fiber in the original ordered coordinates \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\), and let \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\) and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\). In particular, \(W\) includes every integer leaf vector and every integer hub coordinate. The body is a full-dimensional rational simplex and \(0<W<\infty\), as proved in 4f09fb34a7d37fc1.

For a center \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_{\mathbf j}>0\), use exactly the normalization of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{w_{\mathbf j+v},w_{\mathbf j-v}\},&
\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\},\\
\Delta_{\mathbf j}(v)&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf j}^2}
 {w_{\mathbf j+v}w_{\mathbf j-v}}\quad(v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}),&
B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)&=
 \sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}\\
                  \Delta_{\mathbf j}(v)\le L}}\alpha(v),\\
K_{\mathbf j}(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)}{1+L},&
K(w)&=\max_{\mathbf j:w_{\mathbf j}>0}K_{\mathbf j}(w).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(L\ge0\), and all logarithms are natural. All displacement sums are oriented and include zero. The bold center \(\mathbf j\) is the center \(j\) of 03f071403cdfc69f; these definitions make no change of weights or normalization.

Choose any \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^m\) having exactly \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) ones and set \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf b,1)\). Put \(h_0=k+1/2\), \(\rho=2n/h_0\), \(m_0=m-\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and
\(q=\lfloor m_0e^{-9\rho/10}\rfloor\). Here \(q\) is an integer shell size. For the explicit finite cutoff \(L=28+(16/5)\rho q\), one has

\[
\frac{B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)}{1+L}
\ge\frac{e^{-15}}{1218}\,\frac{W}{2^n}.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently \(K(w)\ge K_{\mathbf j}(w)\ge e^{-15}W/(1218\,2^n)\). There exists one point \(p^\star=(\mathbf j,\mathbf y^\star,\mathbf t^{+\star},\mathbf t^{-\star})\in S\), selected before every ambient halfspace, such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies
\(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge e^{-15}W/(14616\,2^n)\).

## Proof

The only accepted premises are 03f071403cdfc69f and 4f09fb34a7d37fc1. We first put the exact original-coordinate fiber formula of 4f09fb34a7d37fc1 into centered flow coordinates. Its exponent is \(p=2n-1\), so \(p+m=d\). For an arbitrary integer leaf vector \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb Z^m\), put
\(x_i=\beta_i-1/2\), \(a_i=|x_i|\), and \(T=\sum_i a_i\). Thus \(a_i\ge1/2\). Write \(h=R-T\) and
\(c_{\boldsymbol\beta}=1/2-\frac12\sum_i x_i\).
The symbol \(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}\) below is exactly the real hub section volume in 4f09fb34a7d37fc1, while \(\mathsf L_{\boldsymbol\beta}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}w_{(\boldsymbol\beta,a)}\) remains its complete integer hub sum.

Let \(\xi_i=g_i-x_i/2\). Directly,
\(|g_i|+|x_i-g_i|=a_i+2(|\xi_i|-a_i/2)_+\).
Define \(\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\boldsymbol\xi)=2\sum_i(|\xi_i|-a_i/2)_+\). The centered real hub profile is therefore

\[
\varphi_{\mathbf a}(r)
=\frac{2^{-m}}{p!}
 \int_{\mathbb R^m}
 \left(h-\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\boldsymbol\xi)
             -\left|r+\sum_i\xi_i\right|\right)_+^p
 \,d\boldsymbol\xi,
\qquad
w_{(\boldsymbol\beta,a)}
=\varphi_{\mathbf a}(a-c_{\boldsymbol\beta}).
\tag{2}
\]

The last identity holds for a real hub coordinate \(a\) as well. It is obtained from the full fiber formula of 4f09fb34a7d37fc1 by a translation in the \(m\) flow integration variables. Its factor \(2^{-m}/p!\), and thus its original \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\) volume normalization, are unchanged. In particular, no continuous coordinate has been removed. We also have
\(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi_{\mathbf a}(r)\,dr\).

If \(h\le0\), the profile and both volumes are zero. If \(h>0\), its value at zero is positive, it is continuous and compactly supported, and it is even. Positivity follows from a neighborhood of \(\boldsymbol\xi=0\) in (2). Continuity follows by dominated convergence: on a nonzero integrand every coordinate satisfies \(|\xi_i|\le a_i/2+h/2\), so one fixed compact box dominates the integration domain. Compact support follows from the same bound. Evenness follows by \(\boldsymbol\xi\mapsto-\boldsymbol\xi\). Thus the actual profile is symmetric about \(c_{\boldsymbol\beta}\); equivalently, the original substitution is \(g_i\mapsto x_i-g_i\).

The profile is log-concave. Indeed, apply the Brunn–Minkowski inequality in the precise form recorded in 03f071403cdfc69f to two positive-volume fibers with these fixed leaves and arbitrary real hub coordinates. Convexity of the same body gives the required Minkowski-average containment. It follows that \(\varphi_{\mathbf a}^{1/d}\) is concave between positive fibers, and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives log-concavity of \(\varphi_{\mathbf a}\), with zero outside its support. This application concerns real hub fibers and does not assume a log-concave extension of an arbitrary table. Evenness and log-concavity imply that the profile is nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\): any \(0\le r\le s\) with \(s>0\) is a convex combination of \(s\) and \(-s\), whose profile values coincide.

There is a useful uniform width estimate which does not replace a hub sum by an integral. For \(0<r<T/2\), put \(\lambda=1-2r/T\) and \(\tau_i=-ra_i/T\). Each \(\tau_i/(1-\lambda)\) equals \(-a_i/2\), where the \(i\)-th summand of \(\Psi_{\mathbf a}\) is zero. Convexity consequently gives
\(\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\lambda\boldsymbol\xi+\boldsymbol\tau)\le\lambda\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\boldsymbol\xi)\). Also \(\sum_i \tau_i=-r\). The change of variables
\(\boldsymbol\xi'=\lambda\boldsymbol\xi+\boldsymbol\tau\) in (2) therefore proves

\[
\varphi_{\mathbf a}(r)\ge\lambda^{p+m}\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)
=\left(1-\frac{2r}{T}\right)^d\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0).
\tag{3}
\]

Equation (3) also holds at \(r=0\) by identity. Here the available budget \(h\) is nonnegative, so
\(h-\lambda u\ge\lambda(h-u)\); the change of variables contributes \(\lambda^m\), and the power in (2) contributes \(\lambda^p\). Integrating (3) on both sides of zero yields
\(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}\ge T\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)/(d+1)\).

For any nonnegative unimodal integrable function on the line, its sum on a translated integer lattice is at most its integral plus twice its maximum. To see this, separate the two monotone sides of a mode. Except for the lattice term nearest the mode on each side, each term is bounded by the integral over the adjacent unit interval toward the mode. These intervals are disjoint on their respective sides, and the two leftover terms are at most the maximum. Applying this to the actual hub profile and then using \(T\ge(n-1)/2\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf L_{\boldsymbol\beta}
&\le\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}+2\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)\\
&\le\left(1+\frac{2(d+1)}{T}\right)\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}
\le14\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The final coefficient follows from
\(1+4(3n-1)/(n-1)=13+8/(n-1)<14\). For \(h\le0\) both sides of (4) are zero, so (4) holds for every integer leaf vector and every hub phase.

We now bound the full \(W\), including all non-Boolean patterns. Let \(s=p+1=2n\). Every leaf magnitude has a unique representation \(a_i=1/2+q_i\) with \(q_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\); put \(q_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_iq_i\). There are exactly two integer leaf coordinates for each magnitude, namely \(-q_i\) and \(1+q_i\). The available leaf excess budget is \(h=h_0-q_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

The real hub integral in 4f09fb34a7d37fc1 gives
\(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}=2^{1-m}(s!)^{-1}
\int_{\mathbb R^m}(h-\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\boldsymbol\xi))_+^s\,d\boldsymbol\xi\).
For one coordinate, the pushforward of Lebesgue measure under
\(\xi\mapsto2(|\xi|-a/2)_+\) is \(a\delta_0+\mathbf1_{(0,\infty)}(e)\,de\). Choosing the coordinates with positive excess therefore gives the exact finite expansion, for \(h\ge0\),

\[
\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}
=\frac{2^{1-m}}{s!}
 \sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}
 \left(\prod_{i\notin J}a_i\right)
 \frac{s!}{(s+|J|)!}\,h^{s+|J|}.
\tag{5}
\]

The simplex integral used for a set \(J\) of size \(j\) is
\(\int_{\mathbb R_{\ge0}^j}(h-\sum e_i)_+^s\,d\mathbf e
=s!h^{s+j}/(s+j)!\), obtained by \(j\) successive one-dimensional integrations. Thus (5) also directly checks the normalization.

All Boolean patterns have the same real hub volume \(V_{\mathrm B}>0\), as in 4f09fb34a7d37fc1. If \(q_{\mathrm{tot}}<h_0\), compare each term of (5) with the corresponding Boolean term. Its coefficient increases by at most \(\prod_i(1+2q_i)\), and its power of \(h\), which is at least \(s\), decreases by a factor at most
\((1-q_{\mathrm{tot}}/h_0)^s\le e^{-\rho q_{\mathrm{tot}}}\). If \(q_{\mathrm{tot}}\ge h_0\), the real hub volume is zero. Consequently the bound
\(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}\le V_{\mathrm B}e^{-\rho q_{\mathrm{tot}}}\prod_i(1+2q_i)\)
holds for every integer leaf vector.

Since \(h_0\le3n/200\), we have \(\rho\ge400/3>100\). Set \(\varepsilon=e^{-\rho}\), so \(\varepsilon<1/3\). Summing the last bound over all magnitudes and all their signs, and using (4), proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=\sum_{\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb Z^m}\mathsf L_{\boldsymbol\beta}\\
&\le14\cdot2^m V_{\mathrm B}
 \left(\sum_{q_i=0}^{\infty}(1+2q_i)\varepsilon^{q_i}\right)^m\\
&=14\cdot2^m V_{\mathrm B}
 \left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon)^2}\right)^m
\le14\cdot2^m V_{\mathrm B}e^{4m\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For the last inequality, use
\(\log(1+\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon\) and
\(-\log(1-\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)\le3\varepsilon/2\).
All sums in the first line are the actual complete integer sums. The infinite geometric expansion only enlarges an upper bound. In particular, no non-Boolean line or boundary fiber has been omitted from \(W\).

We next prove an integer overlap estimate for each centered profile. Fix \(\eta=e^{-14}\). From (3), the elementary inequality
\(\log(1-u)\ge-u/(1-u)\) for \(0\le u<1\), and \(T\ge(n-1)/2\), we obtain

\[
\varphi_{\mathbf a}(1)
\ge \exp\left(-\frac{2d}{T-2}\right)\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)
\ge e^{-13}\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0).
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, \(2d/(T-2)\le4(3n-2)/(n-5)<13\) for \(n\ge100\). Let
\(a_\eta=\max\{r\ge0:\varphi_{\mathbf a}(r)\ge\eta\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)\}\).
For every positive profile this is finite, at least one, and satisfies
\(\varphi_{\mathbf a}(a_\eta)=\eta\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)\).
Log-concavity gives
\(\varphi_{\mathbf a}(r)\le\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)e^{-14r/a_\eta}\)
for \(r\ge a_\eta\): apply log-concavity at \(a_\eta\) as a convex combination of zero and \(r\). It follows that
\(\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}\le
2a_\eta\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)(1+\eta/14)
\le3a_\eta\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)\).

For any \(0\le\delta\le1/2\), the number of integers \(u\) satisfying
\(\delta+|u|\le a_\eta\) is at least \(a_\eta/2\). For \(1\le a_\eta<2\), zero alone suffices for this count. For \(a_\eta\ge2\), the count is
\(1+2\lfloor a_\eta-\delta\rfloor\ge2a_\eta-2\ge a_\eta\).
Evenness and monotonicity now give the exact sampled overlap estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z\\\delta+|u|\le a_\eta}}
\min\{\varphi_{\mathbf a}(\delta+u),
      \varphi_{\mathbf a}(\delta-u)\}
&=\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z\\\delta+|u|\le a_\eta}}
 \varphi_{\mathbf a}(\delta+|u|)\\
&\ge\frac{\eta}{6}\mathsf V_{\boldsymbol\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Both endpoint values in every retained term are at least
\(\eta\varphi_{\mathbf a}(0)\). This is a lower bound for a specified set of original integer hub displacements, proved through sampling; it is not a substitution of a real hub integral for either endpoint sum.

Return to the prescribed center \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf b,1)\), and let \(I_0=\{i:b_i=0\}\), whose size is \(m_0\). Its real hub profile is denoted by \(\varphi_{\mathrm B}\). The common center of that profile is
\(c_{\mathbf b}=1/2\) if \(m\) is even and \(c_{\mathbf b}=3/4\) if \(m\) is odd. Thus
\(\delta=1-c_{\mathbf b}\in\{1/2,1/4\}\).
Its actual central fiber has weight
\(w_{\mathbf j}=\varphi_{\mathrm B}(\delta)>0\).

For an integer \(q\ge0\) with \(2q\le m_0\), choose disjoint sets
\(I_+,I_-\subseteq I_0\), each of size \(q\). Set a leaf displacement equal to \(+1\) on \(I_+\), \(-1\) on \(I_-\), and zero elsewhere. There are
\(N_q=\binom{m_0}{q}\binom{m_0-q}{q}\)
such oriented leaf displacements. Both endpoint leaf sums equal the central leaf sum. At either endpoint exactly \(q\) magnitudes are \(3/2\) and all other magnitudes are \(1/2\).

Formula (2) is invariant under permutation of its magnitude coordinates. Hence every such endpoint has the same centered profile, denoted by \(\varphi_q\), and both endpoints have the same profile center \(c_{\mathbf b}\). This proves the required equality of the entire endpoint profiles, not merely equality of their integrals or of their minimum leaf costs. Write \(V_q=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi_q(r)\,dr\); in particular \(\varphi_0=\varphi_{\mathrm B}\) and \(V_0=V_{\mathrm B}\).

Suppose also \(q<h_0\), and put \(\lambda=1-q/h_0\). Write \(\Psi_{\mathrm B}=\Psi_{(1/2,\ldots,1/2)}\). At the enlarged magnitudes the centered leaf excess function is pointwise at most this Boolean excess function. Convexity and its zero value at the origin give
\(\Psi_{\mathbf a}(\lambda\boldsymbol\xi)
\le\Psi_{\mathrm B}(\lambda\boldsymbol\xi)
\le\lambda\Psi_{\mathrm B}(\boldsymbol\xi)\).
The available budget is now exactly \(\lambda h_0\). Scaling the variables in (2) therefore gives

\[
\varphi_q(\lambda r)\ge\lambda^d\varphi_{\mathrm B}(r),
\qquad
\varphi_q(0)\ge\lambda^d\varphi_{\mathrm B}(0),
\qquad
V_q\ge\lambda^{d+1}V_{\mathrm B}.
\tag{9}
\]

The last inequality follows by integrating the first with \(dr'=\lambda\,dr\). This also proves it at the original real hub volume normalization.

Use the level radius \(a_\eta\) for \(\varphi_q\) in (8). For each of the \(N_q\) leaf displacements, retain all integer hub displacements \(u\) with \(\delta+|u|\le a_\eta\). The corresponding two actual endpoint weights are \(\varphi_q(\delta+u)\) and \(\varphi_q(\delta-u)\). Each is at least
\(\eta\lambda^d\varphi_{\mathrm B}(0)\ge\eta\lambda^dw_{\mathbf j}\).
Their actual curvature consequently satisfies
\(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(v)\le28+2d\log(1/\lambda)\).
Summing their actual minima by (8) proves

\[
B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}\bigl(28+2d\log(1/\lambda)\bigr)
\ge\frac{e^{-14}}6\,N_q V_q.
\tag{10}
\]

Every retained endpoint is positive. All the counted displacement vectors are distinct; exchanging \(I_+\) and \(I_-\) and negating \(u\) supplies the opposite orientation. When \(q=0\), there is one zero leaf displacement, and the hub displacement zero is counted once. Thus (10) has exactly the oriented convention of 03f071403cdfc69f, including zero. The full \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}\) can only be larger than this selected sum.

We now choose the integer \(q=\lfloor m_0e^{-9\rho/10}\rfloor\) from the statement. It satisfies \(2q\le m_0\) and \(q\le h_0/16\). For the latter assertion, \(m_0\le n/2\), and the exponential series gives
\(e^{9\rho/10}\ge(9\rho/10)^2/2\ge4\rho\) for \(\rho\ge100\). Hence
\(q\le n/(8\rho)=h_0/16\).
The former assertion follows from \(e^{-9\rho/10}<1/2\). Thus \(\lambda\ge15/16\), and
\(\log(1/\lambda)\le16q/(15h_0)\).
Since \(d\le3n\) and \(d+1\le3n\), (9) and (10) yield

\[
\frac{B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)}{1+L}
\ge\frac{e^{-14}}6
 \frac{N_q V_{\mathrm B}e^{-(8/5)\rho q}}
      {29+(16/5)\rho q},
\qquad
L=28+(16/5)\rho q.
\tag{11}
\]

This includes \(q=0\), for which \(N_0=1\) and \(\lambda=1\).

If \(q=0\), then \(m_0e^{-9\rho/10}<1\). Since \(m/m_0\le2\),
\(4m\varepsilon<8e^{-\rho/10}\le8e^{-10}<1\).
The last inequality follows, for example, from \(e>2\).
Equation (6) gives \(W/2^n\le7eV_{\mathrm B}\), while (11) gives
\(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)/(1+L)\ge e^{-14}V_{\mathrm B}/174\).
Together these prove (1) in this case.

Suppose \(q\ge1\), and put \(t=\rho q\). The elementary product bound
\(\binom{M}{q}\ge(M/q)^q\) for \(1\le q\le M\) gives
\(N_q\ge(m_0/(2q))^{2q}\), because \(m_0-q\ge m_0/2\).
For completeness, in the product for \(\binom Mq\), each factor
\((M-q+i)/i\), \(1\le i\le q\), is at least \(M/q\).
The chosen \(q\) satisfies \(m_0/q\ge e^{9\rho/10}\). Using \(\log2<1\) and \(\rho\ge100\), we obtain

\[
N_qe^{-(8/5)\rho q}
\ge e^{(1/5)\rho q-2q\log2}
\ge e^{(9/50)t}.
\tag{12}
\]

Also \(m_0e^{-9\rho/10}<q+1\le2q\), so
\(4m\varepsilon<16q e^{-\rho/10}\le16qe^{-10}<q\le t/100\).
Finally, for \(t\ge0\),

\[
\frac{e^{(9/50)t}}{29+(16/5)t}
\ge\frac{e^{t/100}}{29}.
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed, (13) is equivalent to
\(e^{17t/100}\ge1+16t/145\), which follows from
\(e^u\ge1+u\) and \(17/100>16/145\).
Combining (11)--(13) and then (6) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)}{1+L}
&\ge\frac{e^{-14}}{174}V_{\mathrm B}e^{t/100}
\ge\frac{e^{-14}}{174}V_{\mathrm B}e^{4m\varepsilon}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-14}}{1218}\frac{W}{2^n}
\ge\frac{e^{-15}}{1218}\frac{W}{2^n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

This proves (1) for every admissible \(n,k\).

Apply the actual-body depth conclusion of 03f071403cdfc69f at this fixed positive center \(\mathbf j\) and the fixed finite cutoff \(L\). It supplies one continuous point in the original fiber \(C_{\mathbf j}\), chosen before any closed ambient halfspace, whose ambient mixed-integer depth is at least
\(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)/(12(1+L))\).
Adjoining the base \(\mathbf j\) gives the claimed point of \(S\), and (1) gives its stated constant \(e^{-15}/14616\). That accepted conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing entire fibers. The constant is positive and smaller than \(1/e\), and is independent of \(n,k\).
