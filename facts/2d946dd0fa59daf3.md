## Statement

Let the prescribed cyclic ray endpoints for an ordinary planar body with interior mark zero be
$$
(P_0,\ldots,P_5)=((A,0),(0,B),(-t,t),(-a,0),(0,-b),(r,-r)),
\qquad (R_0,\ldots,R_5)=(A,B,t,a,b,r)>0.
$$
Write $W_i$ for the sector area from $P_i$ to $P_{i+1}$, with cyclic indices, and assume $W_i\ge R_iR_{i+1}/2$. Put
$$
h_i=\frac1{R_iR_{i+1}}-\frac1{2W_i},\qquad
\ell_i=\frac1{2W_{i-1}}+\frac1{2W_i}-\frac1{R_i^2}.
$$
Let $L$ have diagonal $\ell_i$, entries $-h_i$ on cyclic adjacent positions, and zero elsewhere off diagonal. Write $\Delta_{23}=\ell_2\ell_3-h_2^2$ and $\Delta_{05}=\ell_0\ell_5-h_5^2$. When the two path blocks $\{2,3\}$ and $\{0,5\}$ are positive definite, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\ell_1-h_1^2\ell_3/\Delta_{23}-h_0^2\ell_5/\Delta_{05},\\
\delta&=\ell_4-h_3^2\ell_2/\Delta_{23}-h_4^2\ell_0/\Delta_{05},\\
\gamma&=h_1h_2h_3/\Delta_{23}+h_0h_5h_4/\Delta_{05}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With $x_1=z,x_4=w$ fixed, the minimum of $x^{\mathsf T}Lx$ is $\alpha z^2-2\gamma zw+\delta w^2$. The sector datum is feasible exactly when $\alpha,\delta\ge0$ and $\alpha\delta\ge\gamma^2$. If these inequalities fail there is a nonnegative witness vector with negative quadratic form.

In every actual feasible datum, including when one of the determinants vanishes, put
$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\Delta_{23}\Delta_{05},\\
F&=\ell_1P-h_1^2\ell_3\Delta_{05}-h_0^2\ell_5\Delta_{23},\\
G&=\ell_4P-h_3^2\ell_2\Delta_{05}-h_4^2\ell_0\Delta_{23},\\
J&=h_1h_2h_3\Delta_{05}+h_0h_5h_4\Delta_{23}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then $\Delta_{23},\Delta_{05},F,G\ge0$, $FG\ge J^2$, and $FB^2-2JBb+Gb^2\ge0$. At a zero determinant these are necessary conditions.

## Proof

The consecutive and next-neighbor determinants of the listed unit-scale canonical directions are one. Thus the determinants of consecutive endpoints are $R_iR_{i+1}$ and the determinants of $P_{i-1},P_{i+1}$ are $R_{i-1}R_{i+1}$. The consecutive angular gaps are strictly less than $\pi$, so the exact prescribed-sector criterion 7d1ee06fa4119930 applies. Its core conditions are exactly the given lower area bounds, and its diagonal coefficient $b_i-h_{i-1}-h_i$ simplifies to $\ell_i$ by substituting these determinants. Therefore feasibility of these exact endpoints and areas is equivalent to $L\succeq0$.

With $x_1=z,x_4=w$ fixed, the variables split into the two paths. The terms involving $(x_2,x_3)$ have positive-definite quadratic block with diagonal $(\ell_2,\ell_3)$ and off-diagonal $-h_2$, and their linear term is $-2(h_1 z,h_3 w)\cdot(x_2,x_3)$. The terms involving $(x_0,x_5)$ have the block with diagonal $(\ell_0,\ell_5)$ and off-diagonal $-h_5$, and their linear term is $-2(h_0z,h_4w)\cdot(x_0,x_5)$. Inverting the two blocks gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_2,x_3)&=\frac{(\ell_3 h_1z+h_2 h_3w,\ h_2h_1z+\ell_2h_3w)}{\Delta_{23}},\\
(x_0,x_5)&=\frac{(\ell_5 h_0z+h_5 h_4w,\ h_5h_0z+\ell_0h_4w)}{\Delta_{05}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Completing the two block squares leaves the asserted endpoint quadratic form with the stated coefficients. The two-by-two PSD test gives the exact feasibility inequalities. Since $\gamma\ge0$, replacing $z,w$ by their absolute values cannot increase the endpoint form. Each minimizing path vector is then nonnegative by the displayed expressions, proving the witness claim. In particular if all $h_i>0$ and the whole $L$ is PSD, the two path blocks are positive definite. Otherwise a nonzero null vector of a singular path block, extended by zero, would have zero full quadratic form and therefore lie in the kernel of $L$. The two outside neighbors each have a strictly positive incident edge coefficient to one of its entries and no other edge into that path, forcing both entries to vanish.

For a feasible datum the two path determinants are nonnegative by positive semidefiniteness. For positive-definite paths, multiplying the Schur matrix by $P$ gives the entries $F,-J,G$. Its diagonal and determinant inequalities give the polynomial test, and its evaluation at $(z,w)=(B,b)$ gives the last inequality. In a feasible datum with singular paths, apply the algebraic calculation to the fixed matrix $L+\eta I$, which is positive definite for $\eta>0$ with the same $h_i$. The displayed polynomials are continuous in its entries, so all inequalities persist as $\eta\downarrow0$. This limit concerns matrices with the given data fixed and requires no realizability of a perturbed body. A zero determinant does not justify inverting a singular path, and the exact PSD criterion remains the feasibility test there.
