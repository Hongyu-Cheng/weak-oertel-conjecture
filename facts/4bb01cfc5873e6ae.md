## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let positive $d$-volume convex bodies $A,K,B$ satisfy $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use the actual fibers at $-1,0,1$ of their layered convex hull, measured by the sum of their $d$-volumes. Put $b=|B|\ge|A|>0$, $a=|A|/b$, $k=|K|/b$, $M=b(1+a+k)$, $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, and $\rho=((d-1)/(d+1))^d$. For $h\ge0$ define $t(h)=(2h^{1/d}-1)_+^d$.

Suppose an actual $c\in K$ has ordinary normalized depth at least $\sigma\in[\Gamma,1/2]$. Set $r=\sigma/(1-\sigma)$, $h=(1-\sigma)k$, $H=\Gamma/r$ and $T=(2H^{1/d}-1)^d$. Choose $(0,c)$ if $rh+(a-t(h))_+\ge\Gamma$, and otherwise $(1,g_B)$, where $g_B$ is the centroid of $B$. The selected point has normalized mixed closed-halfspace depth at least
$$
\beta_d(\sigma)=\frac{\Gamma}{1+\Gamma+H+T}.
$$
This is the exact minimum of $\max\{\Gamma,rh+(a-t(h))_+\}/[1+a+(1+r)h]$ over $0\le a\le1,\ h\ge0$, attained at $a=T,\ h=H$. An ordinary deepest point exists in $K$ and is admissible with its attained normalized depth. The certificate reaches $\Gamma/2$ exactly when $H+(2H^{1/d}-1)^d\le1-\Gamma$. For $d=2$ this requires $\sigma=1/2$. For $d\ge3$ it is equivalent to $\sigma\ge\Gamma/(\Gamma+H_c)$, where $H_c$ is the unique solution of $H_c+(2H_c^{1/d}-1)^d=1-\Gamma$ in $(\Gamma,1-\Gamma)$. A centrally symmetric core of volume at least $2\Gamma|K|/(\Gamma+H_c)$ gives this sufficient depth at its center.

If $K$ is centrally symmetric about $c$, take $\sigma=1/2$ and $h=k/2$. The selected normalized depth is at least $\beta_d(1/2)=\Gamma/(1+2\Gamma+\rho)\ge\Gamma/2$, strictly larger than $\Gamma/2$ for $d\ge3$. The relaxed scalar minimum is attained at $a=\rho,h=\Gamma$. The constant is sharp among choices of the better of the two prescribed points $(0,c),(1,g_B)$ for actual three-fiber examples in every $d$.

For $d\ge2$, take a symmetric $(d-1)$-dimensional convex body $D$ of volume $d$ in the height-zero plane, centered at zero. Let $B$ be the cone on $D$ with apex at height one directly above zero, $A=((d-1)/(d+1))B$, and $K=\operatorname{conv}((d/(d+1))B,-(d/(d+1))B)$. The exact raw depths of both prescribed marks are $\Gamma$ and the total mass is $1+2\Gamma+\rho$. For $d=2$ one may take $D=[-1,1]$ giving the diamond of area $8/9$. For $d=1$ take $B=[0,1]$, $A=[0,a]$, and $K=[-(1+a)/2,(1+a)/2]$ with $0<a\le1$. The two raw depths are $(1+a)/2$ and $1/2$, and their best normalized depth is $1/4$.

## Proof

The hull has projection $[-1,1]$. The endpoints are exposed, and every convex combination at base zero uses equal endpoint weights, so the midpoint inclusion and convexity of $K$ give the prescribed middle fiber exactly. A positive fiber has Hausdorff dimension $d$, and all mixed cap masses here are sums of the original fiber volumes captured by the cap.

Apply f0399a8f0bae48c6 §2 to the one pair $(-1,1)$, with ordinary depth in $K$ at least $b\sigma k=brh$ and missed budget at most $b(1-\sigma)k=bh$. Monotonicity and homogeneity of the residual function give raw depth at least $b[rh+(a-t(h))_+]$ at $(0,c)$. The fact handles pure-base cuts by retaining the middle and an endpoint. For $(1,g_B)$ apply 6769574fc1bd9f8d with density one and selected mass $b$ in $B$, giving $b\Gamma$ in every ordinary containing trace and also in the full-fiber case. Dividing the larger payment by $M$ gives the displayed scalar ratio.

First, $\Gamma\le1/2$ by $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$. Hence $2^{-d}\le\Gamma\le H\le1-\Gamma<1$ and $0<r\le1$. For fixed $0\le h\le H$, the numerator is constant $\Gamma$ until $a_0=\Gamma-rh+t(h)$ and is $rh+a-t(h)$ thereafter. The breakpoint is nonnegative and at most $\Gamma+(1-r)H=H$, because $t(h)\le h$ on $[0,1]$. The constant branch decreases in $a$ after division by the denominator, and the affine branch increases since the derivative has numerator $1+h+t(h)$. Its exact minimum at $a_0$ is $\Gamma/(1+\Gamma+h+t(h))\ge\beta_d(\sigma)$.

For $H\le h\le1$ the same monotonicity puts the minimum at $a=t(h)$, yielding $rh/[1+t(h)+(1+r)h]$. On this interval the inverse relation is $h=v(t)=2^{-d}(1+t^{1/d})^d$, including the possible left endpoint $t=0,\ h=2^{-d}$. This function is concave on $[0,1]$ by its binomial expansion into nonnegative multiples of concave powers. The affine function $H(1+t)/(1+T)$ agrees with it at $t=T$ and is at most it at $t=1$, since convexity of $x^d$ gives $H=v(T)\le(1+T)/2$. Thus $h/(1+t)\ge H/(1+T)$, and the quotient is at least $rH/[1+T+(1+r)H]=\beta_d(\sigma)$. Finally $h\ge1$ gives at least $rh/[2+(1+r)h]\ge r/(3+r)\ge\beta_d(\sigma)$, the last inequality again using $2H\le1+T$. The point $(a,h)=(T,H)$ attains the value, with $a=0$ allowed in the scalar minimization.

For ordinary depth, minimizing over closed through-point caps is equivalent to minimizing over all containing caps by parallel tightening. For every fixed nonzero normal the through-point cap volume is continuous in the point, since hyperplanes are null. The infimum is upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum on compact $K$. It is at least $\Gamma|K|$ by 6769574fc1bd9f8d at the ordinary centroid, and at most $|K|/2$ by complementary through-point cuts. Comparing $\beta_d(\sigma)$ with $\Gamma/2$ gives the stated criterion. The function $H+(2H^{1/d}-1)^d$ is strictly increasing on $[\Gamma,1-\Gamma]$. At $H=\Gamma$ it equals $\Gamma+\rho$. The odd terms in $(d+1)^d-(d-1)^d$ give at least $2d^d$, strictly for $d\ge3$, so $2\Gamma+\rho\le1$ with precisely that strict range. For $d=2$ equality therefore occurs at the smallest allowed $H$ only. For $d\ge3$ the left side at $H=1-\Gamma$ is strictly greater than $1-\Gamma$, since $1-\Gamma>2^{-d}$. This proves the claimed unique threshold. Every containing ordinary halfspace at the center of a symmetric core pays at least half its volume.

If $K$ is symmetric about $c$, its own ordinary depth at $c$ is exactly $|K|/2$, by reflection and the null hyperplane boundary. Substituting $\sigma=1/2$ gives $H=\Gamma,T=\rho$ and the claimed symmetric constant and improvement. For the sharpness construction with $d\ge2$, put $p=d/(d+1)$ and $q=(d-1)/(d+1)$. The section of $B$ at height $x_d=t\in[0,1]$ is $(1-t)D$. Integration gives $|B|=1$, centroid height $1/(d+1)$, and apex cap volume $\Gamma$ at that height. Since $(1+q)/2=p$, the endpoint midpoint body is $pB$. The union $pB\cup(-pB)$ is convex: it has sections $(p-|t|)D$ for $|t|\le p$, and the section factor is nonnegative and concave. Thus it is exactly $K$, with volume $2\Gamma$, while $|A|=\rho$. At $(0,0)$ the downward through-point cap $x_d\le0$ captures only null endpoint faces and half of $K$. At $(1,g_B)$ the cap $x_d+(z-1)\ge1/(d+1)$ captures the apex cap and misses the other two fibers, as their maximal heights are $p$ and $q$. These upper bounds match the own-middle symmetry and endpoint centroid lower bounds, respectively. In $d=1$ the endpoint midpoint interval is $[0,(1+a)/2]\subset K$. A downward cap at the middle center leaves just half $K$ in length; a finite outward tilt isolates the upper half of $B$ at its centroid. The own intervals supply the matching lower bounds. The total length is $2(1+a)$, so the better normalized depth is $1/4$.
