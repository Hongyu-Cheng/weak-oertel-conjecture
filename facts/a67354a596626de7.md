## Statement

Let $K$ be a planar convex body containing zero in its interior. On six rays its boundary endpoints in cyclic order are
$$
(A,0),\ (0,B),\ (-t,t),\ (-a,0),\ (0,-b),\ (r,-r)
$$
with all radii positive. Index the rays from zero and let $W_i$ be the area of the sector between rays $i$ and $i+1$ modulo six. Suppose sectors $0,2,3,5$ equal their core triangles spanned by zero and their two ray endpoints, and the triples of endpoints on rays $(5,0,1)$ and $(2,3,4)$ are collinear. Let $q_1,q_2,q_3$ be the areas in the three closed halfplanes $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$ and $x+y\le0$. Then
$$
\max_i q_i\ge \frac{8}{17}|K|.
$$
In particular these data cannot occur when all three fractions are at most $128/279$.

## Proof

The collinearities give $A=Br/(B+r)$ and $a=bt/(b+t)$. Put $x=W_1$, $y=W_4$ and
$$
P=Bt/2,\quad Q=br/2,\quad U=Br/2,\quad V=bt/2,\quad Z=(AB+at)/2.
$$
The core inclusions give $x\ge P$, $y\ge Q$, and $UV=PQ$. For instance $W_0+W_5=A(B+r)/2=U$ and $W_2+W_3=a(t+b)/2=V$. Adding the appropriate sectors gives
$$
|K|=x+y+U+V,\qquad q_1=y+U,\quad q_2=x+Z,\quad q_3=y+V.
$$
With $\xi=B/r$ and $p=P/Q$, the formulas for $A,a$ give
$$
Z=U\frac{\xi}{1+\xi}+V\frac{p}{\xi+p}.
$$

If a cap fraction is at least $1/2$ the assertion follows. Otherwise, set $T=\max(U,V)$, $R=\min(U,V)$ and $S=U+V$. The inequalities $2(y+T)<x+y+S$ and $2(x+Z)<x+y+S$ yield $T-R<x-y<S-2Z$, so $Z<R$. Order $U\ge V$ in the scalar calculation by interchanging the two terms and replacing $\xi$ by $p/\xi$ when needed. From $Z<V$, division by $\xi>0$ gives $U/(1+\xi)<V/(\xi+p)$, hence $(U-V)\xi<V-Up$. Thus $V>Up$, and $UV=PQ$ gives $U<Q$. We have $0<P<R\le T<Q$.

Write $Z_0=P(2Q-S)/(Q-P)>0$, since $(Q-U)(Q-V)>0$ and $UV=PQ$ imply $S<P+Q<2Q$. Substitution into the expression for $Z$ gives the exact identity
$$
Z-Z_0=\frac{[PQ(1+\xi)-PU-QU\xi]^2}{U(Q-P)(P+Q\xi)(1+\xi)}\ge0.
$$
At fixed $y,T,R,Z$, the largest cap fraction is $\max(y+T,x+Z)/(x+y+S)$. As a function of $x\ge0$ this decreases up to $x=y+T-Z$ and increases thereafter, since the latter derivative has positive numerator $y+S-Z$. Consequently it is at least
$$
\frac{y+T}{2y+T-Z+S}\ \ge\
\frac{Q+T}{2Q+T-Z+S}\ \ge\
\frac{Q+T}{2Q+T-Z_0+S}.
$$
The first comparison within this display uses the positive derivative numerator $R-Z$ and $y\ge Q$. All denominators are positive.

Rescale the area variables by $Q$ so that $Q=1$. Write $P=s^2$, $0<s<1$, and note $s\le T<1$ and $R=P/T$. The last quotient is
$$
F_P(T)=\frac{(1-P)T(1+T)}{(2-P)T^2+(2-4P)T+P}.
$$
Its derivative is $P(1-P)(1-T)(3T+1)$ divided by the squared denominator, so $F_P(T)\ge F_P(s)$. Finally,
$$
F_{s^2}(s)-\frac8{17}
=\frac{(1+s)^2}{s^2+5s+2}-\frac8{17}
=\frac{(3s-1)^2}{17(s^2+5s+2)}\ge0.
$$
The strict comparison $8/17-128/279=56/4743>0$ gives the last clause.
