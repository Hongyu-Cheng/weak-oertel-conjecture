## Statement

For every integer $s\ge2$ and full-dimensional $s$-simplex $K$, there is a full-dimensional body in coordinates $(z,u,x)\in\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^s$, equal to the convex hull of its fine mixed source with $(z,u)\in\mathbb Z^4$. The coarse source takes only $z\in\mathbb Z^3$, and both sources use the sum of their intrinsic $\mathcal H_s$ fiber measures. The fine source has nine equal positive fibers, and the largest fine mass fraction on a hyperplane in the $u$ coordinate alone is exactly $1/9$. The respective maximum normalized fine and coarse closed-halfspace depths are $\Gamma_s/9$ and $\Gamma_s/2$, where $\Gamma_s=(s/(s+1))^s$. Thus the fine maximum is strictly below $\Gamma_{s+1}/8$ and strictly above $\Gamma_s/16$.

## Proof

Start with 358113ce6a951053 and apply the invertible integral shear $(z,u,x)\mapsto(z,u+100z_1+10z_2+z_3,x)$. It preserves both sampling rules, since its translation in the fine coordinate is integral at every coarse base. It preserves every conditional intrinsic $\mathcal H_s$ measure because the action in each coarse fiber is a translation, and it bijects ambient closed halfspaces. Hence it preserves the two normalized all-halfspace depths, full dimension, and equality with the hull of the fine source.

In the index order $0,1,3,4,5,6,7,8,10$ of 358113ce6a951053, the fine heights become $2,101,102,110,111,201,210,211,220$. The only extra coarse fiber has $z=(1,0,1)$ and height $203/2$, still nonintegral. In the one-dimensional fine coordinate a hyperplane fixes one height, so its maximal mass fraction is precisely one out of the nine equal fibers. In contrast the combined-base hyperplane $u-100z_1-10z_2-z_3=0$ contains seven of them. The strict comparison with $\Gamma_{s+1}/8$ is the one proved in the accepted predecessor, and $\Gamma_s/9>\Gamma_s/16$ follows from $\Gamma_s>0$.
