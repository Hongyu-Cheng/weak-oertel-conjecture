## Statement

Fix a real number \(q>2\). For an integer \(N\ge1\) and a number \(\kappa_N>0\), let
\((\mathrm H_{q,N}(\kappa_N))\) denote the following dimension-local arithmetic hypothesis. For every compact convex set \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) with nonempty complete lattice support
\(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\), and every
\(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), define

\[
p_{\mathbf z}
=
\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z)}
{\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x)}
\qquad(\mathbf z\in A)
\]

and its normalized actual closed-halfspace depth

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}.
\]

The hypothesis is

\[
M_q(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q
\ge \kappa_N\,2^{-qN}.
\tag{HqN}
\]

No full-dimensionality assumption is imposed on \(Q\) or \(A\). Hypothesis
\((\mathrm H_{q,N}(\kappa_N))\) is not asserted unconditionally in this theorem.

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, put
\(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), and assume \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). Let
\(w_{\mathbf I}>0\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\), with table value zero at every integer site outside \(D\). In the canonical notation of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, set

\[
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
w_{\max}=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\mathbf I}},\qquad
B=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\phi_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here and throughout, \(B\) is the maximum potential. It is never a hitting score. Assume full convex-admissibility: whenever nonnegative coefficients
\((\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) have sum one and
\(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\in D\),

\[
\phi_{\mathbf J}\le
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{2}
\]

For each \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) be the nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) for which
\(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and write
\(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}>0\) for their unique barycentric coefficients. Define, exactly as in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf J},\\
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)
&=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:G_E\le L\},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\min_{\substack{F\subseteq D\\
F\cap E\ne\varnothing\ \text{for every }E\in
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Every equality \(G_E=L\) is included. The singleton at \(\mathbf J\) is included, every selected source is charged its full original weight, and \(W\) is the full original mass. The equivalent convention using all finite barycentric supports has the same hitting value by accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`.

If the single dimension-local hypothesis
\((\mathrm H_{q,n+1}(\kappa_{n+1}))\) holds, then

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\ge
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/q}}{2C_q^{1/q}}\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
C_q:=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)^q\,dL.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, let \(d\ge1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, put
\(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S=C\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\), and use the locked closed-halfspace depth \(h_S\). If
\((\mathrm H_{q,n+1}(\kappa_{n+1}))\) holds, then there is one point
\(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), selected before the arbitrary containing halfspace, such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/q}}{12C_q^{1/q}}\,2^{-n}
\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{5}
\]

In (5), the weights are the actual fiber volumes

\[
w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I}),
\qquad
C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\},
\qquad
W=\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{6}
\]

Thus \(w\) always denotes the actual weights, \(W\) the original full mass,
\(n\) the original integer dimension, \(d\) the continuous dimension, and
\(N\) the arithmetic ambient dimension in (HqN). In the application
\(N=n+1\). The potential \(\phi\), the maximum potential \(B\), and the score
\(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\) have exactly the meanings in (1) and (3).

For the two requested integer exponents,

\[
C_3=16,
\qquad
\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)
\ge
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/3}}{12\,16^{1/3}}\,2^{-n}W,
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
C_4=65,
\qquad
\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)
\ge
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/4}}{12\,65^{1/4}}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{8}
\]

If (HqN) holds in every \(N\ge2\) and
\(\kappa_*:=\inf_{N\ge2}\kappa_N>0\), then (5) proves the locked target with

\[
c_0=
\min\left\{
\frac{\kappa_*^{1/q}}{12C_q^{1/q}},\frac1{2e}
\right\}\in(0,1/e).
\tag{9}
\]

No positivity of \(\kappa_*\), and no instance of (HqN) in any arithmetic dimension \(N\ge2\), is proved here.

Independently of the preceding transfer exponent \(q>2\), fix any real exponent \(s>0\). Two calibrations hold unconditionally for this \(s\). For every Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R\), define

\[
\tau_\nu(x)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R\text{ a closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}
\nu(H).
\]

Then

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\tau_\nu(x)^s\,d\nu(x)
\ge\frac1{2^s(s+1)}.
\tag{Cal1}
\]

The constant is sharp, with uniform laws on increasingly long consecutive lattice intervals approaching equality. Thus, for exponent \(s\), the best possible coefficient in the dimension-one analogue of (HqN) is
\(\kappa_1=1/(s+1)\); in particular any dimension-uniform cubic coefficient \(\kappa\) must satisfy \(\kappa\le1/4\).

There is also an exact decomposition budget for every \(s>0\). Let a finite probability law \(p\) be partitioned into disjoint nonempty pieces \(A_i\) of masses \(\alpha_i>0\), let \(p_i\) be its conditional law on \(A_i\), and let \(\tau_p,\tau_{p_i}\) be actual closed-halfspace depths in the common ambient space. Suppose nonnegative integers \(r_i\) and a number \(\kappa>0\) satisfy

\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_i}p_i(\mathbf z)\tau_{p_i}(\mathbf z)^s
\ge\kappa\,2^{-sr_i}
\qquad\text{for every }i.
\]

Writing

\[
M_s(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^s,
\]

one has

\[
M_s(p)\ge
\kappa\sum_i\alpha_i^{s+1}2^{-sr_i}
\ge
\frac{\kappa}{\left(\sum_i2^{r_i}\right)^s}.
\tag{Cal2}
\]

The second inequality is sharp for the prescribed \(r_i\). Hence a decomposition satisfying
\(\sum_i2^{r_i}\le2^N\) preserves the desired coefficient at the scale
\(\kappa2^{-sN}\). Three arbitrary pieces with \(r_i=N-1\) yield only
\((2/3)^s\kappa2^{-sN}\).

## Proof

We first prove (4). The exact alias map to the epigraph constructions in accepted facts `07e7c8b5638ba162` and `2f0204d9d167faa7` is as follows. Their base set \(D\), polytope \(P\), weights \(w_{\mathbf I}\), total \(W\), maximum weight \(w_{\max}\), potentials \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\), maximum potential \(B\), and convex roof \(\widehat\phi\) are precisely the objects in (1)-(2). Their last epigraph coordinate \(t\), top cutoff \(R\), and mesh parameter \(m\) are retained. Their unnormalized depth \(\tau_\nu\) is the infimum of \(\nu(H)\) over closed halfspaces containing the queried point. The complete discretized epigraph has arithmetic ambient dimension \(N=n+1\). The exponent \(q\) here replaces only the exponent \(2\) in the moment passage of accepted fact `2f0204d9d167faa7`; it does not denote a continuous dimension, a root index, or a probability weight.

Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` supplies the convex roof

\[
\widehat\phi(x)=
\min_{\substack{\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,
\ \sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=x}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
\qquad(x\in P).
\tag{10}
\]

It is convex and continuous on \(P\) in its relative topology, satisfies
\(0\le\widehat\phi\le B\), and has
\(\widehat\phi(\mathbf I)=\phi_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D\), including when \(P\) has lower affine rank.

Use the exponential epigraph measure from the unconditional construction in accepted fact `07e7c8b5638ba162`:

\[
\mu(A')=
w_{\max}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
\int_{\phi_{\mathbf I}}^\infty
\mathbf 1_{A'}(\mathbf I,t)e^{-t}\,dt.
\tag{11}
\]

Its total mass is exactly

\[
\mu(\mathbb R^{n+1})
=w_{\max}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}=W.
\tag{12}
\]

On the fiber over \(\mathbf I\), writing
\(t=\phi_{\mathbf I}+L\) gives the exact original-weight identity

\[
w_{\max}e^{-t}\,dt=w_{\mathbf I}e^{-L}\,dL.
\tag{13}
\]

Thus no normalization or selected submass replaces the original capacities or \(W\).

Fix \(R>B\), let \(\mu_R\) be the restriction of \(\mu\) to heights at most \(R\), and put

\[
W_R=\mu_R(\mathbb R^{n+1})
=W-|D|w_{\max}e^{-R}>0.
\tag{14}
\]

For each integer \(m\ge1\), the complete log-affine discretization from accepted fact `07e7c8b5638ba162` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{R,m}
&=\{(x,s):x\in P,\ m\widehat\phi(x)\le s\le mR\},\\
Q_{R,m}\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}
&=\{(\mathbf I,k):\mathbf I\in D,\ k\in\mathbb Z,
\ \phi_{\mathbf I}\le k/m\le R\},\\
\mu_{R,m}
&=\frac{w_{\max}}m
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\
\phi_{\mathbf I}\le k/m\le R}}
e^{-k/m}\delta_{(\mathbf I,k/m)},\\
W_{R,m}&=\mu_{R,m}(\mathbb R^{n+1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

The first set is compact and convex. Its displayed lattice support is complete because \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) and
\(\widehat\phi(\mathbf I)=\phi_{\mathbf I}\). A minimum-potential site gives a lattice point at height zero. Before the invertible scaling
\((x,s)\mapsto(x,s/m)\), the weight at \((\mathbf I,k)\) is
\((w_{\max}/m)e^{-k/m}\), whose logarithm is affine in the integer coordinates. Invertible scaling bijects containing closed halfspaces and preserves their atomic masses. Hence the sole hypothesis
\((\mathrm H_{q,n+1}(\kappa_{n+1}))\) applies to (15), even if the support has lower affine rank.

If an unnormalized positive table \(v\) has total mass \(V\), and
\(p=v/V\), then \(\tau_p=\tau_v/V\), so

\[
\sum p\tau_p^q
=V^{-(q+1)}\sum v\tau_v^q.
\]

Applying (HqN) with \(N=n+1\) therefore gives

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}^q\,d\mu_{R,m}
\ge
\kappa_{n+1}2^{-q(n+1)}W_{R,m}^{q+1}.
\tag{16}
\]

This is the only use of the unproved arithmetic hypothesis. We now prove the required \(q\)-th-moment convergence. The unconditional halfspace estimates in accepted fact `07e7c8b5638ba162`, with the alias map above, give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{H\text{ closed halfspace}}
|\mu_{R,m}(H)-\mu_R(H)|
&\le\varepsilon_m,
&\varepsilon_m&=\frac{3|D|w_{\max}}m,\\
|W_{R,m}-W_R|&\le\varepsilon_m,
&
\sup_{p\in\mathbb R^{n+1}}
|\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}(p)-\tau_{\mu_R}(p)|
&\le\varepsilon_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

These uniform estimates use closed fiber intervals and include both mesh endpoints, every halfspace-boundary atom, and the case in which a boundary contains an entire vertical fiber. The same accepted fact gives the vertical Lipschitz bound

\[
|\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t)-\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t')|
\le |D|w_{\max}|t-t'|
\qquad(\mathbf J\in D).
\tag{18}
\]

In particular the fiberwise depths are measurable and lie in \([0,W]\). For \(u,v\in[0,W]\), the mean value theorem gives

\[
|u^q-v^q|\le qW^{q-1}|u-v|.
\tag{19}
\]

Combining (18)-(19), the function

\[
f_{\mathbf J}(t)=e^{-t}\tau_{\mu_R}(\mathbf J,t)^q
\]

on \([\phi_{\mathbf J},R]\) is bounded by \(W^q\) and is Lipschitz with constant at most

\[
W^q+qW^{q-1}|D|w_{\max}.
\tag{20}
\]

Indeed, \(e^{-t}\) has Lipschitz constant one on \([0,\infty)\), and (19) applies to the depth factor.

The endpoint-inclusive Riemann estimate proved in accepted fact `2f0204d9d167faa7` states that every bounded Lipschitz function \(f\) on \([u,v]\) obeys

\[
\left|
\frac1m\sum_{\substack{k\in\mathbb Z\\k/m\in[u,v]}}f(k/m)
-\int_u^v f(t)\,dt
\right|
\le
\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(f)(v-u)}{2m}
+\frac{3\|f\|_\infty}{m}.
\tag{21}
\]

This includes the cases of no mesh point, one mesh point, and exact equality at either endpoint. Applying (21) to (20), summing over the finite set \(D\), and retaining the factor \(w_{\max}\), yields

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_R}^q\,d\mu_{R,m}
\longrightarrow
\int\tau_{\mu_R}^q\,d\mu_R.
\tag{22}
\]

It remains to replace the depth inside the mesh integral. For all sufficiently large \(m\), (17) gives
\(W_{R,m}\le W+1\). Both depths in question lie in \([0,W+1]\). Hence \(x\mapsto x^q\) is Lipschitz there with constant
\(q(W+1)^{q-1}\), and (17) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\int\tau_{\mu_{R,m}}^q\,d\mu_{R,m}
-\int\tau_{\mu_R}^q\,d\mu_{R,m}
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
q(W+1)^{q-1}\varepsilon_m W_{R,m}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Also \(W_{R,m}\to W_R\). Letting \(m\to\infty\) in (16) by (22)-(23) proves

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_R}^q\,d\mu_R
\ge
\kappa_{n+1}2^{-q(n+1)}W_R^{q+1}.
\tag{24}
\]

We next remove the top cutoff. Put
\(\eta_R=|D|w_{\max}e^{-R}\). The unconditional truncation estimate in accepted fact `07e7c8b5638ba162` says

\[
0\le\tau_\mu(p)-\tau_{\mu_R}(p)\le\eta_R
\qquad(p\in\mathbb R^{n+1}).
\tag{25}
\]

The measure \(\mu-\mu_R\) has total mass \(\eta_R\). The functions in (25) take values in \([0,W]\), so (19) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le
\int\tau_\mu^q\,d\mu
-\int\tau_{\mu_R}^q\,d\mu_R\\
&=
\int(\tau_\mu^q-\tau_{\mu_R}^q)\,d\mu_R
+\int\tau_\mu^q\,d(\mu-\mu_R)\\
&\le qW^{q-1}\eta_RW_R+W^q\eta_R
\le(q+1)W^q\eta_R
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

This also proves measurability of the full fiberwise depth as the uniform limit of the truncated measurable depths. Since \(W_R\to W\), (24)-(26) give

\[
\int\tau_\mu^q\,d\mu
\ge
\kappa_{n+1}2^{-q(n+1)}W^{q+1}.
\tag{27}
\]

For the upper bound, use the unconditional separation estimate proved in accepted fact `07e7c8b5638ba162` and reused in accepted fact `2f0204d9d167faa7`, with exactly the objects in (1), (3), and (11):

\[
\tau_\mu(\mathbf J,\phi_{\mathbf J}+L)
\le m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
\le(1+L)\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\tag{28}
\]

for every \(\mathbf J\in D\) and \(L\ge0\). Its strict separating halfspace excludes every full retained epigraph fiber. Consequently it charges at most the full original weights of a minimum hitting set. The construction includes vertical separators, equality graph sites, every equality \(G_E=L\), and an empty retained set. Thus (28) neither deletes boundary mass nor discounts any source occurrence.

Using (13), raising the nonnegative inequality (28) to the real power \(q\), and summing over every original fiber gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int\tau_\mu^q\,d\mu
&=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}
\int_0^\infty
\tau_\mu(\mathbf J,\phi_{\mathbf J}+L)^q e^{-L}\,dL\\
&\le
W\bigl(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\bigr)^q
\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)^q\,dL\\
&=C_qW\bigl(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\bigr)^q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

The constant \(C_q\) is finite and positive. On \(L\ge1\),
\((1+L)^q\le2^qL^q\), and for all sufficiently large \(L\) one has
\(q\log L\le L/2\), so \(L^qe^{-L}\le e^{-L/2}\). The integrand is continuous on every bounded interval. Combining (27) and (29), dividing by \(C_qW>0\), and taking the nonnegative \(q\)-th root gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&\ge
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/q}}{C_q^{1/q}}
2^{-(n+1)}W\\
&=
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}^{1/q}}{2C_q^{1/q}}
2^{-n}W,
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (4).

Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives its global log-concave extension, including all empty, lower-dimensional, and boundary fibers. In particular its positive support is exactly lattice-complete and its potential satisfies (2), so (4) applies without changing \(n\), \(w\), or \(W\).

For the exact notation map to the root comparison and actual-depth consumer, \(D,w,W,\mathbf J,L\) are unchanged; the continuous dimension in accepted facts `488f0a583886b5fc` and `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` is the present \(d\). Their root cost is

L_E=

{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}
\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}.
\tag{30}

Their \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\) and

\qquad

\tag{31}
\]

The attained actual-body conclusion of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` then gives

Choose an attaining \(\mathbf y^\star\) before quantifying over the arbitrary containing closed halfspace. Equation (32) then gives (5), including vertical halfspaces and all boundary mass.

For an integer \(r\ge0\), repeated integration by parts gives

\[
\int_0^\infty L^r e^{-L}\,dL=r!.
\tag{33}
\]

Expanding the requested cases and using (33),

\[
C_3=1+3(1)+3(2)+6=16
\]

and

This proves (7)-(8). If the dimension-local hypotheses hold for all \(N\ge2\) with \(\kappa_*>0\), substitute

Finally, the relation to the accepted second-moment route is exact. Let
\(X(\mathbf z)=\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) on the probability space with atom weights \(p_{\mathbf z}\). For \(q>2\), monotonicity of probability \(L^r\) norms gives

\[
\left(\sum p_{\mathbf z}X(\mathbf z)^q\right)^{1/q}
\ge
\left(\sum p_{\mathbf z}X(\mathbf z)^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{34}
\]

For completeness, put \(r=q/2>1\). Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(u\mapsto u^r\), applied to \(X^2\), gives
\(\sum pX^q\ge(\sum pX^2)^{q/2}\), which is (34). Therefore the dimension-uniform second-moment hypothesis (H2) stated in accepted fact `2f0204d9d167faa7`, with
\(\sum p\tau_p^2\ge a^2 2^{-2N}\), implies (HqN) with
\(\kappa_N=a^q\) for every \(N\). The fixed-\(q\) arithmetic hypothesis used here is consequently weaker as a sufficient premise. Conversely, the elementary implication from (HqN) gives only
\(\sum p\tau_p^2\ge\kappa_N2^{-qN}\), which is not the scale required by (H2). No converse, strict separation, or unconditional estimate in any arithmetic dimension \(N\ge2\) is claimed.

We finish with the two unconditional calibrations. Fix an arbitrary real \(s>0\). For (Cal1), let
\(F(x)=\nu((-\infty,x])\) and
\(F_-(x)=\nu((-\infty,x))\). The closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R\) are closed rays, so

\[
\tau_\nu(x)=\min\{F(x),1-F_-(x)\}.
\tag{35}
\]

Let \(U\) be uniform on \((0,1)\), and let
\(X=\inf\{x:F(x)\ge U\}\) be the generalized quantile. The limits of \(F\) at the two infinities make \(X\) finite. For every real \(x\), monotonicity and right-continuity give
\(X\le x\) if and only if \(U\le F(x)\), so
\(\mathbb P(X\le x)=F(x)\) and \(X\) has law \(\nu\). The definition of the infimum, again with right-continuity, also gives

\[
F_-(X)\le U\le F(X).
\tag{36}
\]

Equation (35) therefore gives
\(\tau_\nu(X)\ge\min\{U,1-U\}\), including every atom at \(X\) in both candidate closed rays. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}\tau_\nu(x)^s\,d\nu(x)
&\ge\int_0^1\min\{u,1-u\}^s\,du\\
&=2\int_0^{1/2}u^s\,du
=\frac1{2^s(s+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]

For the uniform law on \(\{1,\ldots,K\}\),

\[
\tau_p(j)=\min\left\{\frac jK,\frac{K-j+1}{K}\right\}.
\]

Its \(s\)-th moment is the Riemann sum

\[
\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^K
\min\left\{\frac jK,\frac{K-j+1}{K}\right\}^s,
\]

which converges to the integral in (37). This proves sharpness and the asserted value of the best dimension-one coefficient for every \(s>0\).

For (Cal2), every closed halfspace \(H\) containing
\(\mathbf z\in A_i\) has

\[
p(H)\ge\alpha_i p_i(H).
\]

Taking the infimum over the same containing closed halfspaces gives
\(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha_i\tau_{p_i}(\mathbf z)\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_s(p)
&=\sum_i\alpha_i
\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_i}p_i(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^s\\
&\ge\sum_i\alpha_i^{s+1}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_i}p_i(\mathbf z)\tau_{p_i}(\mathbf z)^s\\
&\ge\kappa\sum_i\alpha_i^{s+1}2^{-sr_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]

To minimize the last sum subject to \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\), keep \(s>0\) fixed. The objective is strictly convex on the probability simplex. A minimizer cannot have \(\alpha_i=0\): moving mass \(\varepsilon\) from any positive coordinate to coordinate \(i\) decreases the former term by a positive multiple of \(\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\), while the new term is only a positive multiple of \(\varepsilon^{s+1}=o(\varepsilon)\). Thus the minimizer is interior, and the Lagrange equations give

\[
(s+1)2^{-sr_i}\alpha_i^s=\lambda
\qquad\text{for every }i,
\]

so the unique minimizer satisfies

\[
\alpha_i=\frac{2^{r_i}}{\sum_j2^{r_j}}.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
\min_{\substack{\alpha_i\ge0\\\sum_i\alpha_i=1}}
\sum_i\alpha_i^{s+1}2^{-sr_i}
=\frac1{\left(\sum_i2^{r_i}\right)^s}.
\tag{39}
\]

This proves (Cal2), including its equality claim for every \(s>0\). If
\(\sum_i2^{r_i}\le2^N\), (39) is at least \(2^{-sN}\). For three pieces with
\(r_i=N-1\), the denominator is
\((3\cdot2^{N-1})^s=(3/2)^s2^{sN}\), giving exactly the factor
\((2/3)^s\) stated above.
