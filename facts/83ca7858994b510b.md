## Statement

Let $A,T,\eta>0$. For $h\le T$ and $\eta+h+k>0$, put $I(h,k)=A\int_h^T\log(\eta+x+k)\,dx$ and $F(y)=y\log y-y$ for $y>0$. With $h=\eta s$, $k=\eta t$ and $J(v)=(1+v)\log(1+v)-v$, its remainder after the full linear term at $(0,0)$ is
$$
A B_F(T+\eta+\eta t,T+\eta)-A\eta J(s+t),\qquad
B_F(y,x)=F(y)-F(x)-F'(x)(y-x).
$$
For bounded $s,t$ as $\eta\downarrow0$ at fixed $T$, the first term is $O(A\eta^2/T)$. The singular remainder in $-\lambda I$ for $\lambda>0$ is nonnegative, with Hessian at $(h,k)=(0,0)$ equal to $(\lambda A/\eta)\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ and null direction $h+k=0$.

## Proof

The exact primitive gives $I=A[F(T+\eta+k)-F(\eta+h+k)]$. At the origin $I_h=-A\log\eta$ and $I_k=A\log((T+\eta)/\eta)$. Subtracting the linear terms leaves the upper Bregman remainder minus $A B_F(\eta+h+k,\eta)$. The latter is $A\eta J((h+k)/\eta)$, yielding the identity. Since $F''(y)=1/y$, the upper remainder is nonnegative and at most a constant times $A k^2/T$ for bounded $t$ and small $\eta$. The singular part in $-\lambda I$ is $\lambda A\eta J((h+k)/\eta)$. Here $J(0)=J'(0)=0$ and $J''(v)=1/(1+v)>0$, giving the sign, Hessian and null line. At any fixed nonzero $s+t$ the singular scale is $\lambda A\eta$ and the upper term is smaller by order $\eta/T$. The computation holds with the upper endpoint and transverse area frozen.
