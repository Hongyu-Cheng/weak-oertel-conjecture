## Statement

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$ and let $A$ have the vertical edge $x=-101/100$ with ordinates $[-899/200,1101/200]$ and right vertex $(-899/900,899/1800)$. Use the three canonical normals $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$, the proper cuts at level zero on $K$ and at level $-1$ on $A$, and normalized chord lengths divided by the normal length. The middle data are $k=9/2$, chord lengths $k_i=2$, centers $c_i=0$, and cap areas $q_i=2$. The $A$ chord lengths are $(1,e,e)$ with $e=699/89900$ and $a=|A|=1/18$. The actual $A,K$ satisfy the centered chord, six-ray, core, envelope and two-chord-area screen of the formal six-ray example, except that the area ratio is now $a/k=1/81<1/31$. One may scale both bodies to make $\Lambda=\sum_i 1/(k_i+2a_i)=1$. The full normalized reciprocal weights are $p_1=(1+e)/(5+e)<2/9$ and $p_2=p_3=2/(5+e)$. The actual $A$ cap areas are $U_1=1/1800$ and $U_2=U_3=13582591/323640000$, with
$$
U_{\min}+U_{\rm mid}+U_{\max}/4
=68632155/1294560000<71920000/1294560000=a.
$$
The actual sums $q_i+U_i$ are not equal; no compatible third fiber is imposed.

## Proof

The full horizontal width of $A$ is $1/90$, its vertical edge length is $10$, and the cut $x=-1$ lies $1/900$ from the apex. Thus the area is $1/18$, the first section has length $10(1/900)/(1/90)=1$, and the first upper cap is a triangle of area $1/1800$. The line $y=-1$ lies $699/200$ above the lower endpoint and below the other two vertices. The rise to the apex from that endpoint is $899/180$, giving horizontal width $(1/90)(699/200)/(899/180)=e$. The missed triangle there has area $(699/200)e/2=488601/35960000$. Reflection $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-x-y)$ preserves the triangle and exchanges this cut with $x+y=1$, preserving area and the horizontal coordinate. It therefore gives the same normalized section and missed area on the third cut. All three cuts are proper. Subtracting the missed area from $a$ gives the stated $U_2=U_3$; the displayed profile follows by addition.

The formal six-ray counterexample computed the middle quantities, the core and the envelopes at unit radial parameter; these depend only on the same actual $K$ and not on the endpoint sections. Here $0<a_i<2$ for all $i$, so the centered bounds are strict. The largest two-chord lower bound is $e/2<a$, and $a/k=1/81$. The two bodies can be scaled together by the original $\Lambda>0$ to normalize its value; all chord comparisons and reciprocal-weight ratios are unchanged, and all area comparisons are multiplied by the square of that factor. Substituting the three endpoint sections gives the stated weights. The strict inequality is equivalent to $7e<1$, which holds directly. Since the three middle cap areas equal $2$ and the $A$ cap areas are unequal, the corresponding raw sums are unequal.
