## Statement

Let \(P\) be either a nonempty finite chain, or a Cartesian product \(P=A\times B\) of finite lattice posets with \(|A|\ge2\) and \(|B|\ge2\). In the product branch, \((a,b)\le_P(a',b')\) means \(a\le_Aa'\) and \(b\le_Bb'\). In either branch, \(s<_Pt\) means \(s\le_Pt\) and \(s\ne t\). Put \(N=|P|\), and define
$$
D_P=\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{P}:
       x_s=2,\ x_t=0\Longrightarrow s<_P t\}.
$$
Then \(D_P\) is the entire lattice census of a full-dimensional compact convex body in \(\mathbb R^P\), and \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^P=D_P\).

For every field \(\ell\in\mathbb R^P\), retain the original law on all of \(D_P\):
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in D_P}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z},\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^P\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}p(H),
\qquad
M_r(p)=\sum_{x\in D_P}p_x\tau_p(x)^r,
$$
where \(p(H)=\sum_{y\in D_P\cap H}p_y\), and the infimum allows arbitrary nonzero halfspace normals.

Set \(\kappa=4^{-1/3}\) and
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{rect}}
  &=\frac1{(1-\kappa)^2}+\frac{1+\kappa}{1-\kappa},\\
c_{\mathrm{prod}}
  &=\frac14\exp\!\left(
       -\frac{9\bigl((\log3)^2+2\bigr)}
                    {2\log(1+18^{-8}/36)}\right),\\
c_{\mathrm{ch}}
  &=\frac{\bigl[\log(1+3\tanh^2(1/32))\bigr]^3}{3456},\qquad
c=\min\left\{c_{\mathrm{ch}},\frac{c_{\mathrm{prod}}}{C_{\mathrm{rect}}^3}\right\}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(c_{\mathrm{prod}}\) is exactly \(c_M\) from accepted fact ad475d6d41d953b3 at cardinality cap \(M=3\), and \(c_{\mathrm{ch}}\) is exactly the constant called \(c_2\) in accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf. The cardinality parameter \(M\) in that predecessor is unrelated to the moments \(M_r\). Uniformly in every allowed \(P\) and every field,
$$
M_2(p)\ge c\,4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c^{3/2}\,8^{-N}.
\tag{1}
$$
In particular, (1) holds for every rectangular product \([w]\times[m]\), with \(w,m\ge1\) and \(N=wm\): when both side lengths are at least 2 its chain factors are finite lattices of cardinality at least 2, and otherwise the rectangle itself is a chain.

## Proof

For either allowed poset, define the compact convex polytope
$$
K_P=\{x\in[0,2]^P:
       x_s-x_t\le1\text{ whenever }s,t\in P,\ s\ne t,
                                      \ s\not<_P t\}.
\tag{2}
$$
For an integer point in \([0,2]^P\), a displayed difference inequality fails exactly when \(x_s=2,x_t=0\). Thus \(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^P=D_P\), including every boundary point. The point \(\mathbf1\) is strictly inside the box and satisfies every difference inequality strictly, so \(K_P\) is full-dimensional. Since \(D_P\subset\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\subset K_P\), intersecting these inclusions with the lattice proves the asserted convex-hull completeness.

First consider the product branch \(P=A\times B\). A finite lattice has a bottom element, a top element, and joins and meets of every nonempty finite subset. The product is a finite lattice whose joins, meets, bottom, and top are taken coordinatewise. For a pivot \(t\in P\), put
\(L_t=\{s:s<_P t\}\), \(U_t=\{s:t<_P s\}\), and
\(F_t=P\setminus(L_t\cup\{t\}\cup U_t)\).
Thus \(F_t\) consists exactly of the elements incomparable with \(t\).
Define the independent-coordinate product support
$$
E_t=
\{1,2\}^{L_t}\times\{0,1,2\}^{\{t\}}
\times\{0,1\}^{U_t}\times\{1\}^{F_t},
\tag{3}
$$
with the factors placed in their original labelled coordinates. In a member of \(E_t\), any coordinate carrying 2 lies at or below \(t\), and any coordinate carrying 0 lies at or above \(t\). Those two coordinates cannot be the same. Transitivity of \(\le_P\) therefore makes the former strictly precede the latter, proving \(E_t\subset D_P\).

Conversely, let \(x\in D_P\), \(T=\{s:x_s=2\}\), and \(O=\{s:x_s=0\}\). Let \(a_*\) be the join of \(T\), with default bottom when \(T\) is empty, and let \(b_*\) be the meet of \(O\), with default top when \(O\) is empty. If both sets are nonempty, each member of \(O\) is an upper bound for \(T\), because every member of \(T\) strictly precedes every member of \(O\). Hence \(a_*\le_Po\) for every \(o\in O\), so \(a_*\le_Pb_*\). This last inequality also holds when either set is empty, by the bottom and top defaults. Choose any pivot \(t\) with \(a_*\le_Pt\le_Pb_*\), for example \(t=a_*\).

Every member of \(T\) is at or below \(t\), and every member of \(O\) is at or above \(t\). Thus coordinates in \(L_t\) cannot carry 0, coordinates in \(U_t\) cannot carry 2, and coordinates in \(F_t\) can carry neither 2 nor 0. At the pivot, the actual value can be any of 0, 1, or 2 and is allowed by (3). Therefore \(x\in E_t\). This proves the exact cover \(D_P=\bigcup_{t\in P}E_t\), including the absence of 2s, the absence of 0s, and their simultaneous absence.

Put \(f_t=|F_t|\) and \(d_t=N-f_t\). The pivot ensures \(d_t\ge1\). Use the original component mass and conditional law
\(\alpha_t=p(E_t)=Z_t/Z\) and \(q_t(x)=p_x/\alpha_t=e^{\ell\cdot x}/Z_t\) on \(E_t\). Their exact normalizer is
$$
Z_t=
e^{\sum_{s\in F_t}\ell_s}
\left(\prod_{s\in L_t}(e^{\ell_s}+e^{2\ell_s})\right)
(1+e^{\ell_t}+e^{2\ell_t})
\left(\prod_{s\in U_t}(1+e^{\ell_s})\right).
\tag{4}
$$
Empty products equal 1. Delete precisely the coordinates in \(F_t\), and let \(\iota_t:\mathbb R^{d_t}\to\mathbb R^P\) insert 1 at these coordinates while preserving the other labels. The reduced law \(\widehat q_t(y)=q_t(\iota_ty)\) has normalizer \(\widehat Z_t=e^{-\sum_{s\in F_t}\ell_s}Z_t\). By (4), it is exactly the product of the normalized original-field affine laws on \(\{1,2\}\) for coordinates in \(L_t\), on \(\{0,1\}\) for coordinates in \(U_t\), and on \(\{0,1,2\}\) at the pivot. There are exactly \(d_t\) one-dimensional factors; each is the full integer census of its real interval and has cardinality 2 or 3.

Write \(\tau_{q_t}\) for actual depth in the original ambient \(\mathbb R^P\), and \(\tau_{\widehat q_t}\) for actual depth in \(\mathbb R^{d_t}\). The trace under \(\iota_t\) of any original containing halfspace is a reduced closed containing halfspace when its restricted normal is nonzero. If that normal vanishes, its trace is all of \(\mathbb R^{d_t}\), since it contains the target. Conversely, every reduced halfspace lifts to an original halfspace by assigning zero normal coefficients on \(F_t\). It follows in both directions that \(\tau_{q_t}(\iota_ty)=\tau_{\widehat q_t}(y)\), so their depth moments agree exactly. Apply accepted fact ad475d6d41d953b3 once to this whole product, with cardinality cap \(M=3\), every block dimension equal to 1, and total dimension \(d_t\). It yields
\(M_2(q_t)=M_2(\widehat q_t)\ge c_{\mathrm{prod}}4^{-d_t}
=c_{\mathrm{prod}}4^{-(N-f_t)}\).

We now implement directly the original-mass weighted-cover mechanism of accepted fact 06be599574a55f19. For \(x\in E_t\) and every original ambient containing halfspace \(H\),
$$
p(H)\ge p(H\cap E_t)=\alpha_t q_t(H)
                 \ge\alpha_t\tau_{q_t}(x).
$$
Taking the infimum over these \(H\) gives \(\tau_p(x)\ge\alpha_t\tau_{q_t}(x)\). For each pivot separately, retain its targets in the original moment to obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(p)
&\ge\sum_{x\in E_t}p_x\tau_p(x)^2
 \ge\alpha_t^3M_2(q_t)\\
&\ge c_{\mathrm{prod}}\alpha_t^3\,4^{-(N-f_t)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Each \(\alpha_t\) retains the original full denominator \(Z\) through (4). Equation (5) is a separate bound for each pivot; overlapping target contributions are not summed in a lower bound, and component moments have not been multiplied.

It remains to bound the full profile \(\sum_t4^{-f_t/3}\). Let \(w=|A|\ge2\) and \(m=|B|\ge2\), so \(N=wm\). Choose arbitrary linear extensions of \(A\) and \(B\). For \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\), write \(i(a)\in[w]\) and \(j(b)\in[m]\) for their respective positions. Here these are positions in a total ordering consistent with the partial order; no graded-lattice assumption is used. Such an ordering exists for every finite poset, by repeatedly removing a minimal element.

Use the inclusive principal sets \(\downarrow_Aa=\{a':a'\le_Aa\}\) and \(\uparrow_Aa=\{a':a\le_Aa'\}\), and similarly in \(B\) and \(P\). If \(t=(a,b)\), then
\(\downarrow_Pt=(\downarrow_Aa)\times(\downarrow_Bb)\) and
\(\uparrow_Pt=(\uparrow_Aa)\times(\uparrow_Bb)\).
Their intersection is exactly \(\{t\}\), by antisymmetry. Consequently
$$
f_t=wm-|\downarrow_Aa|\,|\downarrow_Bb|
          -|\uparrow_Aa|\,|\uparrow_Bb|+1.
\tag{6}
$$
Every member of \(\downarrow_Aa\) occurs at or before position \(i=i(a)\), and every member of \(\uparrow_Aa\) occurs at or after it. Thus their cardinalities are at most \(i\) and \(w-i+1\). The analogous bounds in \(B\), at position \(j=j(b)\), are \(j\) and \(m-j+1\). Inserting these upper bounds into (6) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_t
&\ge wm-ij-(w-i+1)(m-j+1)+1\\
&=(i-1)(m-j)+(w-i)(j-1)=:h(i,j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The position map \((a,b)\mapsto(i(a),j(b))\) is a bijection from \(P\) to \([w]\times[m]\). Since \(0<\kappa<1\), (7) therefore gives
\(\sum_{t\in P}\kappa^{f_t}\le\sum_{i=1}^w\sum_{j=1}^m\kappa^{h(i,j)}\).

Partition this rectangular index range into the disjoint sets
\(\{i<w,j<m\}\), \(\{i=w,1\le j\le m\}\), and \(\{j=m,1\le i<w\}\).
On the first set, both \(m-j\) and \(w-i\) are at least 1, so \(h(i,j)\ge(i-1)+(j-1)\). On the second set, \(h(w,j)=(w-1)(m-j)\ge m-j\). On the third set, \(h(i,m)=(m-1)(w-i)\ge w-i\). It follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t\in P}4^{-f_t/3}
&\le\sum_{i=1}^{w-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\kappa^{i+j-2}
   +\sum_{j=1}^{m}\kappa^{m-j}
   +\sum_{i=1}^{w-1}\kappa^{w-i}\\
&\le\frac1{(1-\kappa)^2}
   +\frac1{1-\kappa}+\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa}
 =C_{\mathrm{rect}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The corner \((w,m)\) occurs only in the second set. The third sum has exponents from 1 through \(w-1\), so no boundary pivot is counted twice.

Let \(U=4^NM_2(p)\). For each pivot, (5) implies
\(\alpha_t\le(U/c_{\mathrm{prod}})^{1/3}4^{-f_t/3}\).
The exact cover implies \(\sum_{t\in P}\alpha_t\ge1\). Summing the preceding upper bounds on the original masses, and using (8), gives
$$
1\le\sum_{t\in P}\alpha_t
 \le (U/c_{\mathrm{prod}})^{1/3}
             \sum_{t\in P}4^{-f_t/3}
 \le (U/c_{\mathrm{prod}})^{1/3}C_{\mathrm{rect}}.
$$
Hence \(M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{prod}}C_{\mathrm{rect}}^{-3}4^{-N}\) in the product branch.

In the chain branch, list the \(N\ge1\) coordinates in chain order. This identifies \(D_P\) exactly with \(D_N\) of accepted fact 3db95e3d61a4eedf. The coordinate permutation preserves the full normalizer, the arbitrary field, and all ambient containing halfspaces. That fact therefore gives \(M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{ch}}4^{-N}\). The definition of \(c\) proves the first bound of (1) in both branches. Finally Jensen's inequality under the entire original law gives
\(M_3(p)=\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p^2)^{3/2}]\ge M_2(p)^{3/2}\), which proves the second bound of (1).
