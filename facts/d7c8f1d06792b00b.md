## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A\) be a real symmetric positive-definite \(n\times n\) matrix, let \(1\le\kappa<\infty\), and let \(V:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) be \(C^2\), with \(2A\preceq\nabla^2V(x)\preceq2\kappa A\) for every real \(x\). Put \(Q(v)=v^{\mathsf T}Av\), and write \(V_{\min}=\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}V(x)\) and \(r=\min_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}V(z)\). Both minima exist. Fix any finite real \(R\ge r\), and define the complete support \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:V(z)\le R\}\), the weights \(w_z=e^{-V(z)}\mathbf1_D(z)\), and \(W=\sum_z w_z\). Then \(D\) is finite and nonempty and \(0<W<\infty\).

For each supported center \(j\in D\), retain the canonical definitions

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\},
&\mathcal U_j&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\},\\
\Delta_j(v)&=\log\frac{w_j^2}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}
\quad(v\in\mathcal U_j),
&B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
\quad(L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\]

The dependence of \(\alpha\) on the fixed center is suppressed, as in accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`. All these objects have exactly their meanings in that fact. Every displacement sum is oriented, includes both signs of a nonzero displacement, and includes zero once. Throughout this packet \(Q\) is the displayed quadratic form; the reference probability denoted \(Q\) inside that predecessor is not introduced here. All logarithms are natural.

Set \(\beta=1/(8\kappa)\), and define

\[
\gamma(\beta)
=3^{-\beta}(2\beta)^{1+\beta}
(1+2\beta)^{-(1+\beta)(1+1/(2\beta))}.
\tag{1}
\]

The maxima defining \(K_j(w)\) and \(K(w)\) are attained, with a maximizing cutoff at an actual curvature value. Moreover,

\[
\gamma(\beta)>\frac1{20\kappa},
\qquad
K(w)\ge\frac{\gamma(\beta)}5\,2^{-n}W
\ge\frac1{100\kappa}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{2}
\]

At the boundary level \(R=r\), the stronger conclusion \(K(w)=e^{-r}\ge2^{-n}W\) holds.

The auxiliary kernel assertion has independent affine-lattice scope. For the same \(V,A,\kappa\), let \(\Gamma=b+\mathcal L\subset\mathbb R^n\) be any affine lattice, where \(\mathcal L\) may have any rank, and let \(R_\Gamma\) be any finite real number such that \(D_\Gamma=\{z\in\Gamma:V(z)\le R_\Gamma\}\) is nonempty. Choose a minimizer \(z_0\) of \(V\) on \(\Gamma\), and put \(q(z)=V(z)-V(z_0)\), \(Z=\sum_{z\in D_\Gamma}e^{-q(z)}\), and \(\mu(z)=e^{-q(z)}/Z\) on \(D_\Gamma\). Define
\(\delta_V(x,y)=V(x)+V(y)-2V((x+y)/2)\).
For independent \(X,Y\) with law \(\mu\),

\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(
\frac{\delta_V(X,Y)}4
-\frac{|V(X)-V(Y)|}{2}\right)
\ge\gamma(\beta)>\frac1{20\kappa}.
\tag{3}
\]

The real midpoint \((X+Y)/2\) in this assertion need not belong to \(\Gamma\).

For \(\kappa=1\), the potential necessarily has the form \(V(x)=V_{\min}+Q(x-a)\) for some \(a\in\mathbb R^n\). In particular, all finite Gaussian sublevels, with arbitrary shifts and positive-definite coupling, satisfy the sharper bound

\[
K(w)\ge
\left(\frac{2^{72}}{3\,5^{53}}\right)^{1/8}2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\left(\frac{2^{72}}{3\,5^{53}}\right)^{1/8}>\frac1{100}.
\tag{4}
\]

Every table in this theorem has an exact convex-body realization. When \(R>V_{\min}\), put \(P_R=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:V(x)\le R\}\). For every positive integer \(d\) satisfying \(d\ge2\kappa(R-V_{\min})\), the set

\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
x\in P_R,\quad
|y_i|\le\frac12e^{-V(x)/d}\quad(1\le i\le d)\right\}
\tag{5}
\]

is a compact, full-dimensional convex body with \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=w_z\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), where \(C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\}\). This includes the positive volumes on \(V(z)=R\). Formula (5) uses the original potential without subtracting its minimum. If \(R=V_{\min}\), the assumptions force the unique continuous minimizer \(a\) to be an integer point and \(D=\{a\}\). In this case, for every positive integer \(d\), an exact full-dimensional realization is

\[
\bigl(a+[-1/4,1/4]^n\bigr)
\times
\left[-\frac12e^{-V_{\min}/d},\frac12e^{-V_{\min}/d}\right]^d.
\tag{6}
\]

Finally, let \(d\ge1\) be any integer, and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any convex body whose actual integer-fiber volumes are exactly \(w_z\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero. Define \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), so that \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\), and put \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. There is one point \(p^\star\in S\), chosen before the halfspace, such that

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{K(w)}{12}
\ge\frac{\gamma(\beta)}{60}\,2^{-n}W
\ge\frac1{1200\kappa}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{7}
\]

The maximum \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)\) is attained and is at least the quantities in (7). This conclusion applies to every realization, in every positive continuous dimension for which it exists. In the Gaussian case its coefficient can be replaced by one twelfth of the coefficient in (4). Every closed ambient halfspace containing the selected point is covered, including vertical halfspaces and those whose boundary contains whole fibers.

## Proof

Taylor's formula with integral remainder and the Hessian bounds gives, for all \(x,v\in\mathbb R^n\),

\[
Q(v)\le V(x+v)-V(x)-\nabla V(x)^{\mathsf T}v
\le\kappa Q(v).
\tag{8}
\]

Indeed, the remainder is \(\int_0^1(1-t)v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2V(x+tv)v\,dt\), and \(\int_0^1 2(1-t)\,dt=1\). Since \(A\) is positive definite, (8) at \(x=0\) implies that \(V\) tends to infinity as \(\|v\|_2\) tends to infinity. It is therefore coercive. Continuity gives an attained continuous minimum, and strict convexity, also following from (8), makes its minimizer unique. Every affine lattice has finitely many points in each compact set. Taking the sublevel through any one of its points proves that the minimum of \(V\) on that lattice is attained. This argument includes a rank-zero lattice. All the nonempty finite-level supports in the statement are consequently finite.

Convexity of \(V\) shows that if two supported integer points have the same parity, their integer midpoint is supported. At any supported center \(j\), summing (8) for the two signs of \(v\in\mathcal U_j\) gives

\[
\Delta_j(v)=V(j+v)+V(j-v)-2V(j),
\qquad
2Q(v)\le\Delta_j(v)\le2\kappa Q(v).
\tag{9}
\]

Thus every curvature in its prescribed domain is finite and nonnegative, \(\Delta_j(0)=0\), and \(\alpha(0)=w_j>0\). For any \(L\ge0\), the largest actual curvature \(t\le L\) exists because zero occurs. The cutoff sums at \(t\) and \(L\) agree, while \(1+t\le1+L\). Hence the maximum over cutoffs is attained at one of finitely many actual curvature values. The finite positive support then gives attainment of \(K(w)\).

We prove the affine-lattice kernel (3). Retain its notation, and put \(T=R_\Gamma-V(z_0)\ge0\). The minimum property of \(z_0\) gives \(q(z)\ge0\) for every \(z\in\Gamma\). For \(0\le s\le T\), define the finite sum

\[
\Theta_\kappa(s)
=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal L\\\kappa Q(v)\le s}}
e^{-\kappa Q(v)}.
\tag{10}
\]

The upper bound in (8), summed for \(z_0+v\) and \(z_0-v\), gives \(q(z_0+v)+q(z_0-v)\le2\kappa Q(v)\). At least one of these endpoints therefore has excess energy at most \(\kappa Q(v)\). If \(\kappa Q(v)\le s\le T\), that endpoint belongs to the complete support \(D_\Gamma\), and its contribution to \(Z\) is at least \(e^{-\kappa Q(v)}\).

Choose such an endpoint once for each unordered nonzero pair \(\{v,-v\}\) in (10). Distinct unordered pairs give distinct chosen points: equality of two points \(z_0\pm v=z_0\pm v'\) would give \(v'=v\) or \(v'=-v\). None is the point \(z_0\), whose contribution to \(Z\) is one. The two orientations have the same summand in (10). Consequently,

\[
Z\ge1+\frac{\Theta_\kappa(s)-1}{2},
\qquad
\Theta_\kappa(s)\le2Z-1\le2Z.
\tag{11}
\]

Only the selected endpoint is required to survive the cutoff. In particular, (11) does not require a symmetric supported neighborhood of \(z_0\).

For an arbitrary \(h>0\), partition \(D_\Gamma\) into the bands \(D_k=\{z\in D_\Gamma:kh\le q(z)<(k+1)h\}\), for integers \(k\ge0\). Write \(p_k=\mu(D_k)\). Their probabilities sum to one, and only finitely many are positive. When \(p_k>0\), let \(\mu_k\) be the conditional law on \(D_k\). Every atom of this law is at most \(e^{-kh}/(Zp_k)\), and \(kh\le T\).

Fix \(x\in D_k\), and split the sum over \(y\in D_k\) according to \(\kappa Q(x-y)\le kh\) or \(\kappa Q(x-y)>kh\). In the first part the map \(y\mapsto x-y\) is injective into \(\mathcal L\), so (11) bounds its unweighted exponential sum. In the second part the exponential is at most \(e^{-kh}\), and the total conditional probability is at most one. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y\in D_k}\mu_k(y)e^{-\kappa Q(x-y)}
&\le\frac{e^{-kh}}{Zp_k}\Theta_\kappa(kh)+e^{-kh}\\
&\le e^{-kh}\left(\frac2{p_k}+1\right)
\le\frac{3e^{-kh}}{p_k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(\mathbb E_k\) denote expectation for two independent samples with law \(\mu_k\). Averaging (12) over \(x\) gives \(\mathbb E_k e^{-\kappa Q(X-Y)}\le3e^{-kh}/p_k\). Applying the lower bound in (8) at the real midpoint, with displacement \((x-y)/2\) and its negative, gives \(\delta_V(x,y)\ge Q(x-y)/2\). This use of (8) requires no lattice membership of that midpoint. Since \(\beta\kappa=1/8\), Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(u\mapsto u^{-\beta}\) on \((0,\infty)\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_k e^{\delta_V(X,Y)/4}
&\ge\mathbb E_k e^{\beta\kappa Q(X-Y)}\\
&\ge\left(\mathbb E_k e^{-\kappa Q(X-Y)}\right)^{-\beta}
\ge3^{-\beta}e^{\beta kh}p_k^\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The two energies in a common band differ by less than \(h\). Keeping only same-band pairs in the nonnegative kernel expectation gives

\[
\mathbb E e^{\delta_V(X,Y)/4-|V(X)-V(Y)|/2}
\ge e^{-h/2}3^{-\beta}
\sum_{k\ge0}e^{\beta kh}p_k^{2+\beta}.
\tag{14}
\]

Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(2+\beta\) and \((2+\beta)/(1+\beta)\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
1=\sum_{k\ge0}p_k
&=\sum_{k\ge0}
\left(e^{\beta kh}p_k^{2+\beta}\right)^{1/(2+\beta)}
e^{-\beta kh/(2+\beta)}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{k\ge0}e^{\beta kh}p_k^{2+\beta}\right)^{1/(2+\beta)}
\left(\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-\beta kh/(1+\beta)}\right)^{(1+\beta)/(2+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

The geometric series converges because \(\beta,h>0\); the first sum is finite. Rearranging (15) bounds that first sum below by \((1-e^{-\beta h/(1+\beta)})^{1+\beta}\). Choose
\(h=(1+\beta)\log(1+2\beta)/\beta>0\).
Then \(e^{-\beta h/(1+\beta)}=(1+2\beta)^{-1}\), and (14) becomes

\[
\mathbb E e^{\delta_V(X,Y)/4-|V(X)-V(Y)|/2}
\ge3^{-\beta}
(1+2\beta)^{-(1+\beta)/(2\beta)}
\left(\frac{2\beta}{1+2\beta}\right)^{1+\beta}
=\gamma(\beta).
\tag{16}
\]

All band endpoints are covered by their weak lower and strict upper inequalities. The argument is valid for \(T=0\), when only \(D_0\) is nonempty.

We establish the stated analytic bound on \(\gamma\). Direct differentiation of (1) gives, for every \(\beta>0\),

\[
\frac{d}{d\beta}\log\frac{\gamma(\beta)}\beta
=\log\frac{2\beta}{3(1+2\beta)}
+\frac{\log(1+2\beta)-2\beta}{2\beta^2}<0.
\tag{17}
\]

The first term is negative because its logarithm argument is less than one, and the second is negative by \(\log(1+t)<t\) for \(t>0\). Since \(0<\beta\le1/8\), it follows that \(\gamma(\beta)/\beta\ge8\gamma(1/8)\). Substitution in (1) gives

\[
\gamma(1/8)=\left(\frac{2^{72}}{3\,5^{45}}\right)^{1/8},
\qquad
(20\gamma(1/8))^8=\frac{2^{88}}{3\,5^{37}}>1.
\tag{18}
\]

The strict inequality follows exactly from
\(2^{88}=16(2^7)^{12}>16(5^3)^{12}>15\,5^{36}=3\,5^{37}\), since \(2^7=128>125=5^3\). Hence \(\gamma(1/8)>1/20\), and \(\gamma(\beta)>(2/5)\beta=1/(20\kappa)\). This proves (3) without a numerical approximation.

Return to the original integer table. At each supported center define the auxiliary discounted sum \(G_j=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\alpha(v)e^{-\Delta_j(v)/4}\). The exponent in this auxiliary sum is \(-\Delta_j(v)/4\); the canonical cutoff score still has its original denominator \(1+L\). Finite summation and the exact Laplace identity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_j
&=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\alpha(v)
\int_{\Delta_j(v)}^\infty\frac14e^{-L/4}\,dL\\
&=\int_0^\infty\frac14e^{-L/4}B_j^\Delta(L)\,dL\\
&\le K_j(w)\int_0^\infty\frac14e^{-L/4}(1+L)\,dL
=5K_j(w)\le5K(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

The two integrals of the constant and linear terms are one and four. A displacement of curvature zero contributes its full weight, since its integral starts at zero. Multiplying (19) by \(w_j\) and summing over \(j\in D\) yields
\(\sum_{j\in D}w_jG_j\le5K(w)W\).

For every ordered supported same-parity pair \((x,y)\), put \(j=(x+y)/2\) and \(v=(x-y)/2\). These vectors are integral, convexity gives \(V(j)\le R\), and the exact endpoints \(j\pm v\) are supported. Conversely, every \(j\in D\) and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\) gives exactly this ordered pair. This is a bijection, including both orientations and each diagonal once. For its corresponding arguments \(\Delta_j(v)=\delta_V(x,y)\). Moreover, \(\alpha(v)=\sqrt{w_xw_y}\,e^{-|V(x)-V(y)|/2}\) and \(w_j/\sqrt{w_xw_y}=e^{\delta_V(x,y)/2}\), so

\[
w_j\alpha(v)e^{-\Delta_j(v)/4}
=w_xw_y\exp\!\left(
\frac{\delta_V(x,y)}4-\frac{|V(x)-V(y)|}{2}\right).
\tag{20}
\]

For each occupied parity class \(c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put \(D_c=D\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^n)\) and \(W_c=\sum_{z\in D_c}w_z\). This support is precisely the complete sublevel at level \(R\) on the affine lattice \(c+2\mathbb Z^n\). Its probability law \(w_z/W_c\) agrees with the law in (3), because subtracting the class minimum multiplies all its weights by the same positive constant. The energy difference and \(\delta_V\) are unaffected by that subtraction. Applying (3) separately in each occupied class and summing (20) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in D}w_jG_j
&=\sum_c\sum_{x,y\in D_c}w_xw_y
e^{\delta_V(x,y)/4-|V(x)-V(y)|/2}\\
&\ge\gamma(\beta)\sum_cW_c^2
\ge\gamma(\beta)\,2^{-n}W^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

The last inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz over all \(2^n\) classes, with zero masses in the empty classes. Combining (19)--(21) and dividing by \(5W>0\) proves (2). The support arguments use weak inequalities at the cutoff throughout.

For an explicit check at the minimum lattice level, suppose \(R=r\). All supported weights equal \(e^{-r}\). If distinct supported \(x,y\) had the same parity, (8) at their midpoint would imply
\(V((x+y)/2)\le r-Q(x-y)/4<r\), contradicting lattice minimality. Hence \(|D|\le2^n\). At any supported center a nonzero displacement with both endpoints supported is also impossible: its two endpoints and center would all have energy \(r\), contradicting \(\Delta_j(v)\ge2Q(v)>0\). Thus \(\mathcal U_j=\{0\}\), \(K_j(w)=e^{-r}\), and \(K(w)=e^{-r}=W/|D|\ge2^{-n}W\), as claimed.

When \(\kappa=1\), (8) is an equality. Let \(a\) be the unique continuous minimizer, so \(\nabla V(a)=0\). Applying that equality at \(a\) proves \(V(x)=V_{\min}+Q(x-a)\). In this case \(\delta_V(x,y)=Q(x-y)/2\), so (3) is precisely the Gaussian kernel with exponent
\(Q(X-Y)/8-|Q(X-a)-Q(Y-a)|/2\).
By (18), the coefficient \(\gamma(1/8)/5\) in (2) equals \((2^{72}/(3\,5^{53}))^{1/8}>1/100\). This proves (4). No change to the original weight normalization is made: the additive constant \(V_{\min}\) multiplies \(w,W,B_j^\Delta,K_j,K\) by the same factor \(e^{-V_{\min}}\) relative to the potential \(Q(x-a)\), and leaves all curvatures unchanged.

We now prove the exact realization. Suppose first that \(R>V_{\min}\), and take an integer \(d\ge1\) with \(d\ge2\kappa(R-V_{\min})\). For any real \(x\), put \(g=\nabla V(x)\) and apply the upper bound in (8) with displacement \(-A^{-1}g/(2\kappa)\). It gives

\[
V_{\min}\le V\!\left(x-\frac{A^{-1}g}{2\kappa}\right)
\le V(x)-\frac{g^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}g}{4\kappa},
\qquad
g^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}g\le4\kappa(V(x)-V_{\min}).
\tag{22}
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz gives \((u^{\mathsf T}g)^2\le(u^{\mathsf T}Au)(g^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}g)\) for every real \(u\). Thus \(gg^{\mathsf T}\preceq4\kappa(V(x)-V_{\min})A\). For the positive \(C^2\) function \(f(x)=e^{-V(x)/d}\), differentiation and the lower Hessian bound imply, on \(P_R\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nabla^2 f(x)
&=\frac{f(x)}{d^2}
\bigl(gg^{\mathsf T}-d\nabla^2V(x)\bigr)\\
&\preceq\frac{f(x)}{d^2}
\bigl(4\kappa(V(x)-V_{\min})-2d\bigr)A
\preceq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

The last inequality includes equality in the condition on \(d\). The convexity of \(P_R\) and the ordinary second-derivative criterion on every segment inside it show that \(f\) is concave on \(P_R\), including its boundary.

For two points \((x,y),(x',y')\) in (5) and \(t\in[0,1]\), their base interpolation lies in \(P_R\). For every coordinate, the triangle inequality and concavity give
\(|(1-t)y_i+ty_i'|\le((1-t)f(x)+tf(x'))/2\le f((1-t)x+tx')/2\).
This proves convexity of (5). Coercivity makes \(P_R\) compact. Continuity makes (5) closed, and compactness of \(P_R\) and boundedness of \(f\) on it make (5) bounded. Since \(R>V_{\min}\), continuity supplies an open base ball around the continuous minimizer contained in \(P_R\). On all of \(P_R\), \(f(x)\ge e^{-R/d}>0\), so (5) contains the product of that ball with an open box of positive side length. Hence it has nonempty ambient interior and is a convex body.

At every \(x\in P_R\), the fiber in (5) is a box of side length \(f(x)\) in all \(d\) coordinates and has volume \(f(x)^d=e^{-V(x)}\). Outside \(P_R\) the fiber is empty. This proves the exact integer table, with the original potential and with all base-boundary fibers retained. In particular, when \(R=r>V_{\min}\), this construction still applies without any positive margin above the lattice minimum.

If \(R=V_{\min}\), then \(V_{\min}\le r\le R\) forces \(r=V_{\min}\). The unique continuous minimizer \(a\) is therefore integral, and \(D=\{a\}\). The base cube in (6) contains precisely one integer point, namely \(a\). Formula (6) is a product of full-dimensional compact boxes, and its only integer fiber has volume \((e^{-V_{\min}/d})^d=e^{-V_{\min}}\). It is the asserted exact convex body in every positive integer dimension \(d\). This construction handles the degenerate level separately; the singleton base \(P_R\) itself is not used to assert full-dimensionality in (5).

Finally let any actual realization \(C\) in the statement be given. Its actual volume table, the supported centers, and all the quantities \(\alpha,\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j,B_j^\Delta,K_j,K\) coincide with those of accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`. Choose a supported center \(j^\star\) and actual curvature cutoff \(L^\star\) attaining \(K(w)\), whose existence was proved above. The per-center, per-cutoff conclusion (3) of that accepted fact gives one \(y^\star\in C_{j^\star}\), fixed before every ambient halfspace, with
\(h_S((j^\star,y^\star))\ge B_{j^\star}^\Delta(L^\star)/(12(1+L^\star))=K(w)/12\).
The predecessor's hypotheses hold: \(C\) is a convex body, the weights are its exact finite integer-fiber volume table, \(W>0\), the center is positive, and the curvature domain contains exactly the positive reflected pairs. Its conclusion uses every closed ambient halfspace, including those carrying positive boundary fiber mass, and it also supplies attainment of \(h_{\max}(C)\). Inserting (2) proves (7); inserting (4) proves the sharper Gaussian consequence. The center, cutoff, and continuous point are all selected before the halfspace.
