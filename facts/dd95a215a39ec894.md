## Statement

Let $C=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(4,0),(2,61/20),(0,2)\}$ and let $\mu$ be its normalized mixed length on $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R$. No log-concave probability $\nu$ carried by a single integer fiber of $C$ satisfies $\nu(H)\le 2\mu(H)$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$.

## Proof

The two upper edges give the lengths $80/40,101/40,122/40,61/40,0$ at $z=0,1,2,3,4$, and all fibers start at $x=0$. The raw mass is $M=91/10$. The base cuts $z\le1$ and $z\ge3$ have respective raw masses $181/40$ and $61/40$, each strictly below $M/2=182/40$. Thus a dominated probability must be carried at $z=2$.

For $H_R=\{x\ge -z/2+121/40\}$ the positive-length fibers contribute $0,0,41/40,0$ in base order. For $H_L=\{x\le4z-437/80\}$ they contribute $0,0,203/80,61/40$. The zero-length fiber contributes nothing. Their traces at $2$ give
$$
\nu([81/40,\infty))\le q:=41/182,\qquad
\nu((203/80,\infty))\ge B:=39/364.
$$

Partition $(-1/40,61/20]$ into six consecutive intervals $I_j$ of length $41/80$, open on the left and closed on the right. Their masses $m_j=\nu(I_j)$ sum to one. For a log-concave measure the $m_j$ form a log-concave sequence with consecutive positive indices: the midpoint of $I_j$ and $I_{j+2}$ is $I_{j+1}$, and any intermediate bin is a convex combination of two others. Apply set log-concavity to compact subsets approximating the masses from within the relevant bins. This also applies to Dirac probabilities. If log-concavity is given in density form, the same inequality follows directly by integrating $f(x+h)f(y)\ge f(x)f(y+h)$ for $x\in I_j,y\in I_{j+1}$ and $h=41/80$: the points $x+h,y$ are complementary convex combinations of $x,y+h$. The support interval also gives consecutiveness in this case.

The last two bins lie above $81/40$, and the last is $(203/80,61/20]$. Hence $m_4+m_5\le q$, $m_5\ge B$, and $m_4\le A:=43/364$. Some earlier bin is positive because $q<1$, so $m_4>0$ by consecutiveness. Log-concavity implies each preceding positive adjacent ratio $m_j/m_{j+1}$ is at most $m_4/m_5\le A/B$. This gives $m_{4-k}\le A(A/B)^k$ for $1\le k\le4$, including zeros. Summing gives
$$
1\le q+A\sum_{k=1}^4(A/B)^k=\frac{50046773}{60149466}<1.
$$
This excludes the required probability.
