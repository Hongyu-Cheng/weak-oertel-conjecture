## Statement

Let $K$ be a nondegenerate triangle inside an outer triangle with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ satisfying $\min_K\lambda_i=0$. Let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, $u_i=\lambda_i(y)$, and suppose each cap $C_i=K\cap\{\lambda_i\ge u_i\}$ strictly captures vertex $P_i$ and strictly excludes the other two, with the $P_i$ distinct. Put $S_{ij}=\lambda_i(P_j)$, $R=S^{-1}$, $a=Ru$, $q_i=|C_i|/|K|$, and $p_i=|C_j\cap C_l|/|K|$ for $\{i,j,l\}=\{1,2,3\}$. Set $\alpha_i=2u_i/S_{ii}$ and $\beta_i=2-2a_i/R_{ii}$. Then $R_{ii}>0$ and $0<\alpha_i,\beta_i<2$. For $0\le\rho\le2$ let $E_\rho=\{\xi\ge0:\sum_i\xi_i=\rho,\ S\xi\le2u\}$. Define its area by the vertex map $\xi\mapsto\sum_i\xi_iP_i$, so the full $\rho$-simplex has area $\rho^2|K|$. Then
$$
\frac{|E_\rho|}{|K|}
=\rho^2-4\sum_i q_i\left(\frac{(\rho-\alpha_i)_+}{2-\alpha_i}\right)^2
+4\sum_i p_i\left(\frac{(\rho-\beta_i)_+}{2-\beta_i}\right)^2.
$$
For fixed $K,y$ its maximum over $\rho\in[0,2]$ is among the endpoints, breakpoints, and interior stationary points of nonconstant quadratic pieces.

## Proof

The columns of $S$ are the outer barycentric vectors of three affinely independent points. Hence $S$ is invertible, nonnegative, and column-stochastic. The positive vector $a=Ru$ is the inner barycentric vector of $y$. Strict incidence gives $0<u_i<S_{ii}$ and $S_{ij}<u_i$ for $j\ne i$, so $\alpha_i\in(0,2)$.

In the full $\rho$-simplex the set removed by row $i$ is the portion $S_i\xi>2u_i$. For $0<\rho\le2$ it can meet only its own vertex and the two edges incident with it, because on the opposite edge its maximum is less than $\rho u_i\le2u_i$. It is empty for $\rho\le\alpha_i$, and above $\alpha_i$ the two edge lengths from its vertex are fixed positive multiples of $\rho-\alpha_i$ by linear interpolation along the edges. At $\rho=2$ the area is $4q_i|K|$ by scale two. This gives the individual area terms.

For rows $j,l$ consider their joint removed area. Their intersection cannot meet either edge opposite one of the respective captured vertices. At $\rho=2$ their equality lines meet at $2a$ in the interior, and they are independent on the affine plane because the stochastic invertible map $S$ is an isomorphism on the zero-sum tangent plane. There are consequently interior points satisfying both strict inequalities, so the area is positive there and for nearby scales. At any scale with positive area, the two cutting lines and the edge $\xi_i=0$ are the only possible sides of this bounded full-dimensional intersection. All three must therefore be present, and its closure is a triangle. The sides have fixed pairwise nonparallel directions: independence gives the two cuts, and strict inequalities $S_{jj}>S_{jl}$ and $S_{ll}>S_{lj}$ show neither cut is parallel to the edge $\xi_i=0$.

After dividing by $\rho$ the two removed caps are nested as $\rho$ increases since $u_j,u_l>0$. The pair area is continuous for $\rho>0$ by dominated convergence on the fixed unit simplex, because the moving lines are area null there. The scales with positive pair area are therefore $(b,2]$ for a threshold $0<b<2$, since below $\max(\alpha_j,\alpha_l)$ at least one cap is empty. On this interval the triangle's three vertices are affine in $\rho$, each given by intersecting two fixed-direction lines with affine right-hand sides. They coincide at $b$: continuity gives zero area, and three limiting vertices with pairwise nonparallel side directions cannot be collinear unless all coincide. In particular the tip on both cuts meets the base $\xi_i=0$ there. Its coordinate is $2a_i+(\rho-2)R_{ii}$, since the tip satisfies $S\xi=2u+(\rho-2)e_i$ by column stochasticity. Thus $R_{ii}=2a_i/(2-b)>0$ and $b=\beta_i$. The affine vertices and their common value at $\beta_i$ make the triangle area proportional to $(\rho-\beta_i)^2$. At $\rho=2$ the area is $4p_i|K|$, giving the pair term.

All three strict removals cannot occur simultaneously because $\sum_i(S\xi)_i=\rho\le2\sum_i u_i$. Boundary lines have zero area, and at $\rho=0$ all areas vanish. Inclusion–exclusion proves the formula. Each summand is a quadratic on the intervals between the finitely many listed breakpoints. Endpoints suffice on constant intervals, giving the asserted finite optimization rule.
