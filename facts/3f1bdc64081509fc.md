## Statement

Let $C$ be the region between $x=0$ and the piecewise affine lower boundary through the points $(i,-w_i)$ for $i=0,\ldots,4$, with $w=(9,75,140,200,25)$. Use its original summed integer-fiber length, of mass $M=449$, and set $t=M/2=449/2$. At a base of closed marginal depth at least $t$, take the interval of continuous barycenters of dominated sources of mass $t$ with that base barycenter. Choose the eligible base with largest interval length and return the interval midpoint. For $C$, base $3$ is uniquely eligible, its interval is $[-185399/1796,-126801/1796]$ with positive length $29299/898$, and its chosen midpoint is $y=-39025/449$. The point $(3,y)$ has normalized depth exactly $50250/201601<1/4$. Another point of the same fiber, $(3,-175/2)$, has normalized depth exactly $225/898>1/4$.

## Proof

The width slopes are $66,65,60,-175$, hence the width is concave and $C$ is compact convex with interior and exactly the five stated integer interval fibers $[-w_i,0]$. The prefix through $2$ has mass $224<t$, and the suffix from $4$ has mass $25<t$. At $3$ the two closed base-side masses are $424,225$, so this is the unique eligible base with depth $225$. Also $\max_i w_i=200<t$. For a base-balanced dominated source at $3$, the right mass is at most $25$. Its right torque is that same mass, so the left mass, at distances at least one, is at most $25$. Adding the middle capacity $200$ gives maximum mass $250$, attained by $(0,0,25,200,25)$. In particular $t$ is strictly feasible.

Apply f96beff4bebe6a19 to these actual convex interval fibers and the fixed feasible mass $t$. If $m_i$ are the retained lengths, they satisfy $0\le m_i\le w_i$, $\sum m_i=t$, and $\sum(i-3)m_i=0$. The upper raw continuous moment objective is $-\sum_i m_i^2/2$, and the negative lower raw moment objective is $\sum_i(w_i m_i-m_i^2/2)$. Both are maximized at $m^*=(0,0,25,349/2,25)$. This row is feasible. For the first objective subtract the affine multiplier $-349/2-(299/2)(i-3)$ from its gradient at $m^*$ to obtain $(-274,-249/2,0,0,299)$. For the second subtract $51/2-(179/2)(i-3)$ to obtain $(-285,-259/2,0,0,64)$. Negative entries are at zero masses, positive entries are at full masses, and free coordinates have zero residual. For any feasible displacement the subtracted affine term vanishes by the two equalities and the residual dot product is nonpositive. The objective change has the additional term $-\lVert m-m^*\rVert^2/2$, proving uniqueness and both optimal values. Substituting in the two moments and dividing by $t$ gives the stated endpoints. The interval is filled by convex combinations of the two attaining sources. Its midpoint is $-39025/449$ and is in the actual fiber.

For either $v=-39025/449$ or $v=-175/2$, a downward nonvertical through-cap at $(3,v)$ contains at least the own length $200+v$. An upward through-cap has own length $-v$ and thresholds $s_2,s_4$ with $s_2+s_4=2v$ at bases $2,4$. If either of those traces is full, their combined length is at least $25$ since their widths are $140,25$. If neither is full, their lengths are $(-s_2)_+$ and $(-s_4)_+$, with sum at least $-2v>25$. Thus the upward mass is at least $25-v$. The pure-base through-caps contain the whole middle fiber and each has mass at least $225$. For both chosen $v$ one has $200+v\ge25-v$, and all through-caps thus have mass at least $25-v$. Any containing cap can be tightened through the mark without increasing mass. The cap $x+100(z-3)\ge v$ excludes bases $0,1,2$, captures the upper own length $-v$, and includes all of base $4$. It attains $25-v$. At the selected midpoint this is $50250/449$, below $M/4$ by $601/1796$. At the separate mark it is $225/2$. Dividing by $449$ gives the stated normalized depths.
