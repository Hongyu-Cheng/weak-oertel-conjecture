## Statement

Let $N\ge1$ bounded intervals of positive widths $w_i$ be equipped with the sum of their separate length measures. Suppose the combined continuous centroid is zero. If $x\ge0$ wholly captures none of the intervals, then its length under the sum measure is at least $\sum_iw_i^2/(2\max_iw_i)$, which is at least $M/(1+\sqrt N)$ for $M=\sum_iw_i$. The intervals need not be the fibers of a convex body.

## Proof

Write $r_i$ for the upper endpoints, and set $H=\max_iw_i$, $Q=\sum_i w_i^2$. The hypothesis on whole intervals makes every lower endpoint strictly negative. Thus the captured mass is $\sum_i(r_i)_+$. The zero first moment gives $Q/2=\sum_iw_i r_i\le H\sum_i(r_i)_+$. For $N>1$, Cauchy on all widths other than one attaining $H$ yields $Q\ge H^2+(M-H)^2/(N-1)$. Hence
$$
\frac{Q}{HM}\ge \frac{N(H/M)-2+M/H}{N-1}\ge \frac{2}{1+\sqrt N},
$$
where the last step uses AM–GM. For $N=1$ the interval is centered at zero and the two inequalities are equalities.
