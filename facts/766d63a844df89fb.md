## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$, $c>\rho>0$ and $h=(h_1,h_2)\in\mathbb R^2$. Take the hull of the three lifted planar triangles $\rho T,\varepsilon h+cT,\rho T$ at integer bases $-1,0,1$. For all sufficiently small $|\varepsilon|$ these are exactly the positive integer fibers, of total area $(9/2)R$, where
$$
R=c^2+2\rho^2,\qquad W=c+2\rho,\qquad \lambda=c/W,\qquad D=2R/W^2.
$$
For closed ambient halfspaces the globally deepest mixed mark is unique, of the form $(0,y(\varepsilon))$, and its full raw depth $Q(\varepsilon)$ and mark satisfy
$$
y(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon\lambda h+\varepsilon^2 y_2+O(\varepsilon^3),
\qquad
Q(\varepsilon)=2R+\varepsilon^2 q_2+O(\varepsilon^3),
$$
where
$$
q_2=\frac D3(h_1^2+h_1h_2+h_2^2),
\qquad
(y_2)_j=\frac{D h_j^2/2-q_2}{2W}\quad (j=1,2).
$$
The complete minimizing menu at that mark has three continuous normal rays with zero first angular derivative. They form a positive circuit and all three integer tilts are exactly zero. The branches and maximizing mark depend smoothly on $(\varepsilon,c)$ locally at each $c>\rho$, with a locally uniform Taylor remainder in $c$. For $h\ne0$ both signs of every sufficiently small nonzero translation strictly increase the true optimized depth from its value at zero, at the same mixed mass.

## Proof

At base zero any lifted convex combination uses equal total weights at the endpoints, whose Minkowski midpoint is $\rho T$. Its inclusion in $\varepsilon h+cT$ is equivalent to $-\varepsilon h\in(c-\rho)T$. This is strict in a neighborhood of the stated parameters since $0\in\operatorname{int}T$ and $c>\rho$. The endpoint bases are exposed and the base projection is $[-1,1]$, proving the census. At $\varepsilon=0$, apply c4ebd085f1b2ab31 to the compatible centered radii $(\rho,c,\rho)$. The middle centroid has raw depth $2R$ and every minimizing continuous direction is one of $u_1=(1,0)$, $u_2=(0,1)$, $u_0=(-1,-1)$. In each canonical direction, at $|\alpha|<\rho$ the endpoint cap sum is $4\rho^2+\alpha^2$ by the cap-area formula, and at $|\alpha|\ge\rho$ one endpoint contributes its entire area $(9/2)\rho^2>4\rho^2$. Thus the only minimizing tilt is zero. Pure-base caps do worse. At either endpoint the ordinary maximum $2\rho^2$ is the exact mixed maximum by outward tilt isolation, and it remains unchanged. At the middle baseline any $y\ne0$ in the fiber has $u_i\cdot y>0$ for at least one $i$ since these vectors span and sum to zero. The corresponding cap of zero integer tilt through $y$ strictly loses middle area relative to the cap at the centroid and gains no endpoint area.

For all middle marks in a bounded neighborhood of zero, write a through-mark cap using a unit continuous normal and real base tilt. Beyond a common absolute tilt bound both endpoints are strictly saturated, one full and one empty. Clamping to a bound chosen beyond all saturation thresholds leaves their traces and the middle trace unchanged. A pure-base cap contains the middle plane and one entire endpoint; a finite nonvertical cap with the same endpoint statuses and a proper middle trace strictly improves it for interior marks near zero. Thus the compact cylinder of unit directions and bounded tilts gives the complete minimum near zero. Joint cap-mass continuity there follows by dominated convergence: after pulling each translated and dilated triangle back to $T$, each cutting line has area zero. Uniform continuity on that compact cylinder preserves a positive gap off neighborhoods of the three baseline directions, including when $c$ varies in a sufficiently small neighborhood of its fixed value.

For the first direction, write the unnormalized continuous normal as $(1,\vartheta)$ and the integer tilt as $\alpha$. For $z=-1,0,1$ let $r_z=\rho,c,\rho$ and let the centers $c_z$ be $0,\varepsilon h,0$. In this open proper chamber, the sole retained vertex of each triangle is $c_z+r_z(2,-1)$. If its margin is $m_z=(2-\vartheta)r_z+(1,\vartheta)\cdot(c_z-y)+\alpha z$, the two edge ratios are $m_z/(3r_z)$ and $m_z/[3r_z(1-\vartheta)]$. Therefore the exact cap mass is
$$
F_1(y,\varepsilon;\alpha,\vartheta)=\sum_{z=-1}^1
\frac{\big((2-\vartheta)r_z+(1,\vartheta)\cdot(c_z-y)+\alpha z\big)^2}{2(1-\vartheta)}.
$$
The endpoint squares have the same center term and opposite tilt, giving exact minimizing $\alpha=0$ in the chart. At the baseline the gradient in the normal variables vanishes and their Hessian is $\operatorname{diag}(2,R)$, obtained by expanding $(2-\vartheta)^2R/[2(1-\vartheta)]+\alpha^2/(1-\vartheta)$. The other two charts follow by simultaneous linear vertex permutations of $T$, which have absolute determinant one and fix the base coordinates. The compact-parameter normal-envelope result 269d96bcbbe3370f applies to each chart, with smoothness by the same implicit equation since the cap mass is smooth. The full local depth is the minimum of these three smooth optimized branch values $q_i(y,\varepsilon,c)$.

Their baseline $y$-gradients are $-2Wu_i$ by the cap formula and stationarity in the normal variables. The gradients of the two tie equations $q_1-q_0=q_2-q_0=0$ are independent. The implicit-function theorem gives a unique smooth local common meeting mark $y(\varepsilon,c)$. The three gradients continue to have a spanning positive dependence, since the baseline kernel coefficients are all positive and any two are independent. Thus for each unit displacement direction at this meeting mark some branch gradient has strictly negative projection, uniformly over the circle and over nearby parameters by compactness. Taylor expansion in the mark gives a strict local maximum of the minimum of the three branches. Middle-plane depth is quasiconcave: any closed halfspace containing a convex combination of two marks contains at least one of them. Any second middle mark with at least this depth would contradict the strict local maximum along their segment. The meeting mark belongs to the middle fiber since its baseline is interior. Its raw value stays above both endpoint maxima by continuity, proving global uniqueness. The actual three minimizing caps are exactly the three local optimized branches from the compact complement gap. At each attained normal, the source gradient of the optimized branch is minus its positive total scalar coarea times its continuous normal. The positive dependence of the three source gradients therefore gives one among their actual continuous normals.

Write $y=\varepsilon y_1+\varepsilon^2 y_2+O(\varepsilon^3)$. At first order, using stationarity of each normal, branch $i$ has coefficient $2u_i\cdot(ch-Wy_1)$. They are equal and the $u_i$ sum to zero, so the coefficient is zero and $y_1=\lambda h$. The first center-to-mark displacements are $\delta_0=(1-\lambda)h$ and $\delta_{-1}=\delta_1=-\lambda h$, with $\sum_z r_z\delta_z=0$ and $\sum_z\delta_z\delta_z^{\mathsf T}=Dhh^{\mathsf T}$. To see the cancellation in normal optimization, put $d_z=c_z-y$. At $\alpha=\vartheta=0$, differentiating the exact formula gives
$$
\partial_\vartheta F_1
=\sum_z r_z(d_{z,1}+2d_{z,2})
 +\frac12\sum_z d_{z,1}(d_{z,1}+2d_{z,2}).
$$
The first coefficient in $\varepsilon$ vanishes by the weighted vector balance. Since the angular Hessian is $R>0$, the optimal angular derivative vanishes. In the other charts the same calculation uses linearly transported displacements, which still have zero weighted sum. Thus normal motion is $O(\varepsilon^2)$ and gives no second-order correction to the branch value.

At canonical direction $i$ and zero tilt the exact branch mass before optimizing the angle is $\tfrac12\sum_z[2r_z+u_i\cdot(c_z-y)]^2$. Its second Taylor coefficient is $-2Wu_i\cdot y_2+(D/2)(u_i\cdot h)^2$. Summing the three equal coefficients and using $\sum_i u_i=0$ gives $3q_2=(D/2)[h_1^2+h_2^2+(h_1+h_2)^2]$. The equations with $u_1,u_2$ give the stated coordinates of $y_2$. The angular and source systems are smooth jointly in $c$; positivity of the old chamber margins, complement gap, and endpoint separation persist locally in $c$. Taylor's theorem on a smaller compact parameter neighborhood gives the uniform remainders. Finally the quadratic form $h_1^2+h_1h_2+h_2^2$ is positive for $h\ne0$.
