## Proof

The base has ten points, four occupied parities and each coordinate sum equal to ten. The fibers are centered, so $Y$ is the full original centroid. Its within-fiber covariance is $1/3$. For the interval at $y$, changing variables by the projective map $X-y\mapsto(X-y)/(1+a(X-y))$ gives the interval length
$$
\frac{1-y}{1+a(1-y)}+\frac{1+y}{1-a(1+y)}.
$$
This length is exactly the energy objective since the Jacobian is $(1+a(X-y))^{-2}$. At an interior $y$ it is strictly convex in $a$ and its derivative vanishes at $a=-y/(1-y^2)$, where both denominators are positive and the value is $2(1-y^2)$. The energy is zero at the boundary by convention. Thus $E/M$ and the covariance norm give the stated shift at $L=1$; a general parameter multiplies the shift by $\sqrt{L(p,y)}$.

At $p=(1,1)$, the base $p$ and the three symmetric pairs $(1,0),(1,2)$, $(2,0),(0,2)$ and $(0,1),(2,1)$ give a centrally symmetric lifted subfamily of total mass 14 about $Y$. $$
(-1,-4),(1,-4),(-4,-1),(-4,1),(3,4),(4,3).
$$
Each cap contains its indicated base and at most one other, by direct substitution into $a\cdot(z-p')\ge0$ at that base $p'$.

For a closed pure-base cap through $p$, at least one point from each symmetric pair and at least one of the three remaining bases are included. The latter offsets sum to zero.
