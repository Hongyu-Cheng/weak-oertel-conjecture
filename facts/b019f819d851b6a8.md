## Statement

Under the hypotheses of the support-anchored cap domination lemma, suppose $K$ is a polytope whose affine automorphism group is transitive on its vertices (in particular, a simplex). Fix a vertex $v_0$ and put $T=K-v_0$. The fixed compatible comparison family with fibers $r_pT$ satisfies, for every $j\in P$,
$$
 \inf_{H\ni(j,c_j)}\sum_{p\in P}|K_p\cap H_p|
 \ \ge\
 \inf_{G\ni(j,-r_jv_0)}\sum_{p\in P}|r_pT\cap G_p|,
$$
where both infima are over closed ambient halfspaces and subscripts denote traces at base $p$. Thus one common-vertex arrangement simultaneously minimizes the depth at each corresponding own-fiber centroid among all compatible translation arrangements with these radii.

## Proof

Translate any original containing cap inward in parallel until its boundary passes through $(j,c_j)$; this cannot increase its mass. For a nonzero continuous normal $u$, a minimizer of $u$ on $K$ may be chosen to be a vertex $v$. Apply the support-anchored cap domination lemma with this $u,v$ and the cap's base tilt. It gives a no-larger cap through $(j,0)$ in the compatible family $L_p=r_jv+r_p(K-v)$. Choose an affine automorphism of $K$ sending $v_0$ to $v$. It fixes the centroid zero, so it is linear, say $A$, and has $|\det A|=1$ because it preserves $K$. The ambient affine map fixing the base and sending $x$ to $A^{-1}(x-r_jv)$ takes $L_p$ to $r_pT$ for every $p$ and the comparison mark to $(j,-r_jv_0)$. It maps the comparison cap to one genuine closed cap and preserves all its fiber volumes. Thus the original cap pays at least the right-hand infimum. A pure-base through-cap has exactly the same mass in both families, so it also pays the right-hand infimum.

The $r_pT$ are compatible because the radius inequality proved in the lemma implies $\sum_p\lambda_p r_pT\subset r_kT$ when $0\in T$ and $k=\sum_p\lambda_p p$. Their fiber centroids are $-r_pv_0$. The reference family is therefore among the admissible arrangements and is independent of the chosen anchor and direction, proving simultaneous minimization at the prescribed centroids.
