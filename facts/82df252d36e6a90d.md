## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\) be a convex body, and set
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\neq\varnothing.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), write
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{t\in\mathbb R:(\mathbf z,t)\in C\}
\]
and let \(|C_{\mathbf z}|_1\) denote its one-dimensional Lebesgue measure, with value zero for an empty fiber. Then, for every \(s>0\), there exists \(\mathbf y_s\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\) containing \(\mathbf y_s\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_1(S\cap H)\geq
2^{-n}s\,\#\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:|C_{\mathbf z}|_1\geq2s\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)\geq
2^{-n}s\,\#\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:|C_{\mathbf z}|_1\geq2s\}
\qquad\text{for every }s>0.
\]

## Proof

Fix \(s>0\), and put
\[
E_s:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:|C_{\mathbf z}|_1\geq2s\},
\qquad N_s:=\#E_s.
\]
The projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is compact, so it contains only finitely many lattice points. Hence \(E_s\) is finite. If \(N_s=0\), choose any \(\mathbf y_s\in S\). Every mixed-integer halfspace measure is nonnegative, so the claimed bound, whose right-hand side is zero, is immediate.

Assume henceforth that \(N_s>0\). For every \(\mathbf z\in E_s\), the fiber is a compact interval
\[
C_{\mathbf z}=[a_{\mathbf z},b_{\mathbf z}]
\quad\text{with}\quad
b_{\mathbf z}-a_{\mathbf z}\geq2s.
\]
Choose the closed subinterval
\[
J_{\mathbf z}:=[a_{\mathbf z},a_{\mathbf z}+2s]\subseteq C_{\mathbf z}.
\]
In particular, if \(|C_{\mathbf z}|_1=2s\), then \(J_{\mathbf z}=C_{\mathbf z}\), so equality-level fibers are retained with their full mass.

For every Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\), define
\[
\mu_C(A):=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\lambda_1\bigl(\{t:(\mathbf z,t)\in C\cap A\}\bigr)
=\mathcal H_1(S\cap A)
\]
and
\[
\nu(A):=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E_s}
\lambda_1\bigl(J_{\mathbf z}\cap\{t:(\mathbf z,t)\in A\}\bigr),
\]
where \(\lambda_1\) is Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R\). These are finite Borel measures, and the fiberwise inclusions \(J_{\mathbf z}\subseteq C_{\mathbf z}\) give
\[
\nu(A)\leq\mu_C(A)
\quad\text{for every Borel }A.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\nu(\mathbb R^{n+1})=2sN_s.
\]
Its support is the compact set
\[
U:=\operatorname{supp}\nu
=\bigcup_{\mathbf z\in E_s}(\{\mathbf z\}\times J_{\mathbf z})
\subseteq C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R).
\]

Let \(T:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\). This is a nonempty closed subset of \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). The mixed-integer Helly theorem gives
\[
h(T)=h(\mathbb R\times\mathbb Z^n)=2^{n+1},
\]
where the first equality uses a permutation of coordinates. Apply the centerpoint theorem recorded in accepted fact \(bb2b5f9519ae23e8\) to \(T\) and the probability measure
\[
\widehat\nu:=\frac{\nu}{2sN_s},
\]
which is supported on \(T\). It yields a point \(\mathbf y_s\in T\) such that every closed halfspace \(G\) whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y_s\) satisfies
\[
\nu(G)\geq\frac{2sN_s}{2^{n+1}}
=2^{-n}sN_s. \tag{1}
\]

We next prove that the centerpoint supplied by the theorem lies in \(C\). Let
\[
K:=\operatorname{conv}(U).
\]
Because \(U\) is compact, \(K\) is compact. If \(\mathbf y_s\notin K\), strict separation of the point \(\mathbf y_s\) from the compact convex set \(K\) gives a vector \(\mathbf v\neq\mathbf0\) such that
\[
\sup_{\mathbf u\in K}\mathbf v^\top\mathbf u
<\mathbf v^\top\mathbf y_s.
\]
The closed halfspace
\[
G:=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf v^\top\mathbf x
\geq\mathbf v^\top\mathbf y_s\}
\]
has boundary through \(\mathbf y_s\) and is disjoint from \(U=\operatorname{supp}\nu\). Thus \(\nu(G)=0\), contradicting the positive lower bound in (1). Hence \(\mathbf y_s\in K\). Since \(U\subseteq C\) and \(C\) is convex, \(K\subseteq C\). Together with \(\mathbf y_s\in T\), this proves
\[
\mathbf y_s\in C\cap T=S.
\]

Finally, let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y_s\). If \(H\) is proper, write
\[
H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf v^\top\mathbf x\geq c\},
\qquad
\mathbf v^\top\mathbf y_s\geq c,
\]
and translate its boundary to \(\mathbf y_s\):
\[
G_{\mathbf y_s}:=
\{\mathbf x:\mathbf v^\top\mathbf x
\geq\mathbf v^\top\mathbf y_s\}\subseteq H.
\]
Then (1) and monotonicity give \(\nu(H)\geq2^{-n}sN_s\). The same inequality is immediate if \(H=\mathbb R^{n+1}\). This inclusion argument neither discards nor divides any mass on the boundary. Since \(\nu\leq\mu_C\),
\[
\mathcal H_1(S\cap H)
=\mu_C(H)
\geq\nu(H)
\geq2^{-n}sN_s.
\]
Taking the infimum over all such \(H\), and then the supremum over \(\mathbf y\in S\), proves the statement.

## External sources

Amitabh Basu and Timm Oertel, "Centerpoints: A Link between Optimization and Convex Geometry," SIAM Journal on Optimization 27(2) (2017), 866-889, Theorem 15, https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1092908 (author manuscript: https://www.ams.jhu.edu/~abasu9/papers/centerpoints.pdf). Exact statement used: if \(T\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) is nonempty and closed, a probability measure is supported on \(T\), and \(h(T)<\infty\), then there is a point of \(T\) for which every closed boundary-through-the-point halfspace has measure at least \(1/h(T)\). Applicability check: \(T=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\) is nonempty and closed, \(\widehat\nu\) is a probability measure supported on \(T\), and the next source gives \(h(T)=2^{n+1}\). This is the external theorem already recorded in accepted fact \(bb2b5f9519ae23e8\).

Gennadiy Averkov and Robert Weismantel, "Transversal Numbers over Subsets of Linear Spaces," Advances in Geometry 12(1) (2012), 19-28, Theorem 1.1(3), https://doi.org/10.1515/advgeom.2011.028 (preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1002.0948). Exact statement used: for all integers \(r,q\geq0\),
\[
h(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb Z^q)=(r+1)2^q.
\]
Applicability check: taking \(r=1\) and \(q=n\), then permuting coordinates, gives \(h(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)=2^{n+1}\).
