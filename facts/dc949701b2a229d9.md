## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, with planar convex bodies of nonempty interior and support functions $h_z$ satisfying every Jensen support inequality
$$
h_z\ge\sum_{w\in P}p_wh_w,\qquad p_w\ge0,\quad\sum_wp_w=1,\quad\sum_wp_ww=z.
$$
Let $I=[a,b]$ be a closed normal interval with $0<b-a<\pi$. Fix a finite list of prescribed caps for each fiber. For each such cap, given by a fixed halfplane, suppose the entire exposed boundary arc over $I$ is on one strict side of its cap line, at a uniformly positive distance. Let $B_z$ be the homogeneous solution of $B_z''+B_z=0$ matching $h_z$ at $a,b$, and put $f_z=h_z-B_z$.

Suppose the curvature measures $R_z=h_z''+h_z$ are nonatomic on $I$ and $f_z=c_z f$ with $c_z>0$ for one $f\in H_0^1(I)$ with finite, nonzero, positive curvature source $\nu=f''+f$ on $\operatorname{int} I$. Then there are arbitrarily close simultaneous replacements by actual convex bodies, piecewise homogeneous on a common finite mesh on $I$, with supports unchanged outside $\operatorname{int} I$. All Jensen inequalities, individual areas and prescribed cap areas are preserved exactly. Endpoint facets are allowed.

In particular, the same conclusion holds for at most three distinct base points if instead $c\nu\le R_z\le C\nu$ on $I$ for a nonzero finite nonatomic positive measure $\nu$ and $0<c\le C<\infty$, and both endpoints are inward accumulation points of $\operatorname{supp}\nu$. This includes $C^2$ supports on a neighborhood of $I$ with strictly positive curvature on $I$.

## Proof

**Sine interpolation and area.** The quadratic form $Q(f,g)=\int_I(f'g'-fg)\,d\theta$ is an inner product equivalent to the $H_0^1(I)$ norm by Poincaré's inequality since $b-a<\pi$. On a cell $[u,v]$ of a mesh, write $\delta=v-u$. The nodal sine interpolant of a function $k$ is
$$
(\mathcal P k)(\theta)=\frac{\sin(v-\theta)k(u)+\sin(\theta-u)k(v)}{\sin\delta}.
$$
It fixes homogeneous solutions of $k''+k=0$. Restricted to $H_0^1(I)$, $\mathcal P$ is the $Q$-orthogonal projection onto the piecewise homogeneous functions with zero endpoint values. The error vanishes at the nodes and cellwise integration by parts against a homogeneous test gives zero. These projections converge strongly to the identity as the maximum cell length tends to zero. For a smooth function this follows from the cell Green formula below and its first derivative. For a general $H_0^1$ function, use smooth approximation and the norm bound of orthogonal projection.

For a globally periodic support $h$, area is $\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}(h^2-h'^2)\,d\theta$. Thus if a new support agrees with $h$ outside $\operatorname{int} I$ and has residual $\widetilde f=\widetilde h-B$ on $I$, its area minus the old area is
$$
-\frac12\bigl[Q(\widetilde f,\widetilde f)-Q(f,f)\bigr].
$$
Indeed, the cross term with $B$ vanishes by $B''+B=0$ and the zero endpoint values of both residuals. In particular the uncorrected interpolation increases area by $\frac12 Q(f-\mathcal P f,f-\mathcal P f)$. The global area formula accounts for any facets introduced at $a,b$.

**Cell estimates and actual support functions.** If $k''+k=R$ on a cell with nonatomic finite signed measure $R$, then $k-\mathcal P k=-\int G_{uv}(\,\cdot\,,t)\,dR(t)$, where
$$
G_{uv}(s,t)=\frac{\sin(\min(s,t)-u)\sin(v-\max(s,t))}{\sin\delta}.
$$
This is the Dirichlet Green kernel of $-D^2-1$: it vanishes at the endpoints, is homogeneous off the diagonal and has a derivative jump of $-1$ there. It is strictly positive for both arguments interior. Also $\|G_{uv}\|_\infty=O(\delta)$ and its one-sided derivatives in the first argument are uniformly bounded as $\delta\to0$. The support and both one-sided derivatives of the interpolant therefore converge uniformly on the cells for any fixed nonatomic $R$ on $I$, since the maximum $|R|$-mass of a cell tends to zero. For bounded curvature density these two errors are $O(\delta^2)$ and $O(\delta)$, respectively. Differentiation at the left endpoint gives
$$
(\mathcal P k)'(u^+)-k'(u)=\int_{[u,v]}\frac{\sin(v-t)}{\sin\delta}\,dR(t).
$$
At the right endpoint, $k'(v)-(\mathcal P k)'(v^-)$ is the same integral with $\sin(t-u)$ in the numerator. Thus an interior derivative jump of $\mathcal P k$ is the sum of these weighted integrals from its two adjacent cells. At a global endpoint $a,b$, just the one inner cell contributes the derivative jump beyond the unchanged outer derivative of an originally differentiable support. The same formulas apply to a signed source in the interior, with absolute jumps bounded by the integrals for its total variation.

For an original support $h$, testing a maximizing point at $\theta$ in the two adjacent directions gives $h(\theta-t)+h(\theta+t)\ge2\cos(t)h(\theta)$. Dividing by $t^2$ and passing to distributions gives $h''+h\ge0$. Conversely, a continuous periodic candidate support with nonnegative distribution $h''+h$ is a support function. For a smooth function with positive $h''+h$, the curve $x(\theta)=h(\theta)u(\theta)+h'(\theta)u'(\theta)$, $u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, has $x'=(h''+h)u'$. For each $\alpha$, the derivative of $u(\alpha)\cdot x(\theta)$ has sign $\sin(\alpha-\theta)$, so the maximum is attained at $\alpha$ and is $h(\alpha)$. This proves the support criterion in the smooth case and gives the area formula by integrating $\frac12\det(x,x')$ and then integrating by parts. For the nonsmooth case, convolve periodically with nonnegative smooth approximate identities and add positive constants tending to zero. The smooth supports converge uniformly and their $H^1$ convergence gives the area formula by continuity of area for convex bodies under Hausdorff convergence. The uniform support limit is a support function by the closedness of sublinearity of its homogeneous extension. A support uniformly close to the original contains a smaller concentric copy of an original interior ball.

**Find a guarded source for an area correction.** For each $z$, the nonnegative weights with sum one and barycenter $z$ form a compact polytope. Hence it suffices to preserve its finitely many vertex inequalities, by linearity in the weights. Each corresponding gap on $I$ is $g=D+\kappa f\ge0$, where $D$ is homogeneous. Write $\nu$ also for its extension to $I$ assigning no mass to the endpoints. If $g$ is identically zero, applying $\frac{d^2}{d\theta^2}+1$ shows $\kappa=0$ because $\nu\ne0$, and then $D=0$. Such a gap is preserved by every replacement of the common residual $f$.

Every other gap has a $\nu$-null zero set. If $\kappa<0$, $g''=\kappa\nu-g\,d\theta\le0$ as a measure. The concave nonnegative $g$ cannot have an interior zero without being identically zero, by comparing to any two values bracketing that zero. If $\kappa=0$, an interior zero of a nonnegative homogeneous solution has zero derivative and forces the solution to vanish. If $\kappa>0$ and the gap has two or more zeros on $I$, let $p<q$ be its first and last zeros. The zero-boundary Green formula on $[p,q]$ gives $g=-\kappa\int G_{pq}(\,\cdot\,,t)\,d\nu(t)$. Positive mass in $(p,q)$ would make $g$ strictly negative inside, so there is no such mass. Endpoints and singleton zero sets are null by nonatomicity. The finitely many nonidentity gaps are consequently all positive on an open subset of $\operatorname{int} I$ carrying full $\nu$-measure. Choose a compact interval $J\subset\operatorname{int} I$ in that open subset and a nonzero measure $\nu'\le\nu$ supported compactly in $\operatorname{int} J$. This is possible by covering the open set with a countable collection of compact subintervals lying strictly inside it and restricting to one of positive measure, then enlarging that subinterval slightly. Each nonidentity gap has positive minimum on $J$.

Choose a bounded signed density $\psi$ on $\nu'$ with $\int\psi\sin t\,d\nu'(t)=\int\psi\cos t\,d\nu'(t)=0$ and $\int f\psi\,d\nu'<0$ as follows. Project $f$ in $L^2(\nu')$ onto the span of $\sin,\cos$, obtaining the homogeneous function $D_0$ on $I$ as a representative. The projection error has strictly positive norm. Otherwise continuity would give $f=D_0$ on $\operatorname{supp}\nu'$. Let $p<q$ be the extreme points of this support. Since $\nu'$ is nonzero and nonatomic, it has positive mass in $(p,q)$, where $\nu\ge\nu'$. The zero-boundary Green formula for $f-D_0$ on $[p,q]$ would make it strictly negative at every point of $(p,q)$, contradicting its zero value at an interior point of $\operatorname{supp}\nu'$. Now $\psi=D_0-f$ on the support is bounded and has the two required moments by projection. Moreover $\int f\psi\,d\nu'=-\|f-D_0\|_{L^2(\nu')}^2<0$.

Put $\phi(\theta)=\int_{[a,\theta]}\sin(\theta-t)\psi(t)\,d\nu'(t)$. Variation of constants gives $\phi''+\phi=\psi\nu'$. Before the first point of $\operatorname{supp}\nu'$ it vanishes by construction, and after the last point it vanishes by the two moment conditions. Thus it is compactly supported in $\operatorname{int} J$, belongs to $H_0^1(I)$ and satisfies $Q(f,\phi)=-\int f\psi\,d\nu'>0$ by weak integration by parts.

**Restore the common energy.** Use a sequence of common meshes of maximum cell length tending to zero. Set $F=\mathcal P f$ and $\Phi=\mathcal P\phi$. For sufficiently fine meshes $\Phi$ is supported in $J$, since its cell values are determined by its nodal values. Put $A=Q(F,\Phi)$, $B=Q(\Phi,\Phi)$ and $E=Q(f-F,f-F)$. By projection convergence $A\to Q(f,\phi)>0$ and $B\to Q(\phi,\phi)>0$, while $E\to0$. For sufficiently fine meshes
$$
\xi=\frac{E}{A+\sqrt{A^2+BE}}
$$
is defined, tends to zero, and satisfies $2A\xi+B\xi^2=E$. Thus $F+\xi\Phi$ has exactly the $Q$-norm of $f$ by orthogonality. Replacing $h_z$ on $I$ by $B_z+c_z(F+\xi\Phi)$ restores each area. The common sine interpolants $B_z+c_z F$ preserve every Jensen gap by nonnegative sine coefficients at the nodes. The scalar repair preserves identity gaps, leaves the gaps unchanged outside $J$, and keeps nonidentity gaps positive on $J$ for fine meshes, since the interpolated gaps converge uniformly and $\xi\Phi\to0$ uniformly.

At an interior node, the jump of $\Phi$ is bounded in absolute value by $\|\psi\|_\infty$ times the nonnegative jump obtained from interpolating $f$, using the cell formulas and $|\psi|\nu'\le\|\psi\|_\infty\nu$. Both jumps vanish if the latter is zero. Hence a sufficiently small $\xi$ keeps the corrected jump nonnegative. At $a,b$ there is no correction because $\Phi$ vanishes in their neighborhoods, so the nonnegative endpoint jumps from interpolation are retained. The new supports have nonnegative global curvature. The cell estimates for $\nu$ and $\psi\nu'$ and the bound $\xi\to0$ give uniform convergence of values and one-sided derivatives, and so actual convex bodies with polygonal arcs.

**Caps and fixed endpoints.** At angle $\theta$ the exposed face consists of the points $h(\theta)u(\theta)+s u'(\theta)$ with $h'_-(\theta)\le s\le h'_+(\theta)$. The derivative extremes follow by taking the directional derivatives of the maximum over the body. Thus the uniform convergence of support values and one-sided derivatives on $I$ keeps the whole new arc, including any endpoint facets, on the same strict side of every prescribed cap line as the old arc. Faces at normals outside $I$ coincide, and the original arc endpoints are unchanged on the exterior sides of the endpoint faces, since the exterior derivatives and support values at $a,b$ are unchanged. Traverse the two boundaries counterclockwise. After cancelling their common exterior path, the difference is the closed path made of the new arc and the reversed old arc. For any point off the boundaries the difference of the two interior indicators is its winding number around this path. The path lies in one open halfplane of each cap line and can be contracted within it, so the indicator difference vanishes almost everywhere in the opposite halfplane. The cap area change is consequently zero if the arcs are outside the cap, and is the full area change if the arcs are inside. Both vanish.

**Placed fibers.** Support inequalities $h_z\ge\sum_w p_wh_w$ for every $p_w\ge0$ with $\sum_w p_w=1$ and $\sum_w p_w w=z$ are precisely the anchored Minkowski inclusions. With these inequalities and actual compact convex fibers established, the exact-hull-sections assertion of fact ba0f3db1bf4f62c6 applies to the finite real base $P$ with $d=2$. The hull of the placed new fibers has those exact sections at $P$. Its projection is $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, so if $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, it has exactly the prescribed integer fibers. The hull is full-dimensional in $\operatorname{aff}(P)\times\mathbb R^2$: a simplex of affinely independent bases gives each of its base sections a ball of a common positive radius, with its center depending affinely on the base, by combining interior balls from its vertices.

**At most three fibers.** Under common domination, the Green representation gives $f_z<0$ throughout $\operatorname{int} I$ since each $R_z$ has nonzero mass there. Apply the three-index tensor rank criterion to the nonzero $f_z$. In the independent case the assumptions of the nonatomic tensor-affine polygonization theorem are exactly the stated cap guards, inward accumulation and domination. In the dependent case the criterion gives positive proportionality, so the rank-one proof applies with any one residual as $f$ and its nonatomic positive source as $\nu$. For the smooth specialization the continuous positive radii of the finite family are uniformly comparable to Lebesgue measure on the compact interval, which also has inward accumulation at both endpoints.
