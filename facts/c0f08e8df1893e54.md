## Statement

The following two assertions hold.

1. Let \(E\) be a finite set, let \(U,V\) be finite-dimensional real vector spaces, and let

\[
A:\mathbb R^E\longrightarrow U,
\qquad
P:\mathbb R^E\longrightarrow V
\]

be linear maps. Then

\[
\min_{L:\operatorname{im}A\to V}
\dim\operatorname{im}(P-L\circ A)
=
\dim P(\ker A).
\tag{1}
\]

More explicitly, choose \(B\subseteq E\) so that the columns \(A\mathbf e_b\), \(b\in B\), form a basis of \(\operatorname{im}A\). For every \(e\in E\setminus B\), write uniquely

\[
A\mathbf e_e=\sum_{b\in B}\xi_{be}A\mathbf e_b
\]

and define the fundamental-circuit vector

\[
\mathbf c^{\,e}:=\mathbf e_e-\sum_{b\in B}\xi_{be}\mathbf e_b.
\tag{2}
\]

Then \(\{\mathbf c^{\,e}:e\in E\setminus B\}\) is a basis of \(\ker A\), and the common value in (1) is

\[
\dim\operatorname{span}\{P\mathbf c^{\,e}:e\in E\setminus B\}.
\tag{3}
\]

These formulas include the cases \(\ker A=\{0\}\), \(\operatorname{im}A=\{0\}\), and \(E=\varnothing\), with the span of an empty family understood to be \(\{0\}\).

2. Let \(d\geq1\), let \(\mu\) be a finite nonnegative Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^d\) with finite first moment, and, for \(0<s\leq\mu(\mathbb R^d)\), define

\[
\mathsf M_s(\mu):=
\left\{
\frac1s\int_{\mathbb R^d}\mathbf x h(\mathbf x)\,d\mu(\mathbf x):
h\text{ measurable},\ 0\leq h\leq1\ \mu\text{-a.e.},\
\int h\,d\mu=s
\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(I,J\) be finite sets and let \(f_i,g_j:\mathbb R^d\to[0,\infty)\) be measurable functions such that

\[
F:=\sum_{i\in I}f_i\leq1,
\qquad
G:=\sum_{j\in J}g_j\leq1
\quad \mu\text{-a.e.}
\tag{5}
\]

Put

\[
a_i:=\int f_i\,d\mu,
\qquad
b_j:=\int g_j\,d\mu,
\qquad
A:=\sum_{i\in I}a_i,
\qquad
B:=\sum_{j\in J}b_j.
\tag{6}
\]

Every index with \(a_i=0\) or \(b_j=0\) may be discarded: its nonnegative density is zero \(\mu\)-almost everywhere and it changes none of \(F,G,A,B\) or the constructions below. Assume after this deletion that both families are nonempty, so \(A,B>0\), and let \(\beta>0\) satisfy

\[
A\leq\beta,
\qquad
B\leq\beta.
\tag{7}
\]

Define

\[
\mathbf p_i:=\frac1{a_i}\int\mathbf x f_i(\mathbf x)\,d\mu(\mathbf x),
\qquad
\mathbf q_j:=\frac1{b_j}\int\mathbf x g_j(\mathbf x)\,d\mu(\mathbf x),
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
T:=\frac{AB}{2\beta},
\qquad
\alpha_{ij}:=\frac{a_i b_j}{AB},
\qquad
\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}:=\mathbf q_j-\mathbf p_i,
\tag{9}
\]

\[
\mathbf c:=\frac12\left(
\frac1A\sum_{i\in I}a_i\mathbf p_i
+
\frac1B\sum_{j\in J}b_j\mathbf q_j
\right).
\tag{10}
\]

The bounds above imply \(0<T\leq A/2\leq\mu(\mathbb R^d)/2\) and \(0<2T\leq A\leq\mu(\mathbb R^d)\), so both metronoid mass parameters used below are admissible.

Then every independent choice \(\theta_{ij}\in[-1/2,1/2]\) is realized at the same selected mass \(T\):

\[
\mathbf c+
\sum_{i\in I,\,j\in J}
\alpha_{ij}\theta_{ij}\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}
\in\mathsf M_T(\mu).
\tag{11}
\]

Equivalently, the actual product-box zonotope satisfies

\[
\mathbf c+
\sum_{i\in I,\,j\in J}
\left[-\frac{\alpha_{ij}}2\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij},
\frac{\alpha_{ij}}2\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\right]
\subseteq\mathsf M_T(\mu).
\tag{12}
\]

The same center has a simultaneous realization at twice that mass:

\[
\mathbf c\in\mathsf M_{2T}(\mu)
=\mathsf M_{AB/\beta}(\mu).
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, with

\[
\Sigma:=\sum_{i\in I,\,j\in J}
\alpha_{ij}^2\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}^{\mathsf T},
\tag{14}
\]

and \(B_2^d\) the Euclidean unit ball, one has the possibly lower-dimensional ellipsoid inclusion

\[
\mathbf c+\frac12\Sigma^{1/2}B_2^d
\subseteq\mathsf M_T(\mu).
\tag{15}
\]

If \(A=0\) or \(B=0\) before deletion, the corresponding entire family vanishes \(\mu\)-almost everywhere, there are no positive-weight pair switches, and \(T=0\); since (4) is a positive-mass normalization, (11)-(15) make no assertion in that degenerate case.

## Proof

We first prove (1). Fix any linear map \(L:\operatorname{im}A\to V\). If \(\mathbf z\in\ker A\), then

\[
(P-L\circ A)\mathbf z=P\mathbf z.
\]

Therefore

\[
P(\ker A)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(P-L\circ A),
\]

so every admissible \(L\) satisfies

\[
\dim\operatorname{im}(P-L\circ A)\geq\dim P(\ker A).
\tag{16}
\]

Because the spaces are finite-dimensional, the surjection \(A:\mathbb R^E\to\operatorname{im}A\) has a linear right inverse \(S:\operatorname{im}A\to\mathbb R^E\), so \(A\circ S\) is the identity on \(\operatorname{im}A\). Take \(L:=P\circ S\). Then

\[
P-L\circ A=P\circ(I-S\circ A).
\tag{17}
\]

The image of \(I-S\circ A\) is exactly \(\ker A\). Indeed,

\[
A\circ(I-S\circ A)=A-(A\circ S)\circ A=0,
\]

so its image is contained in \(\ker A\), while for every \(\mathbf z\in\ker A\),

\[
(I-S\circ A)\mathbf z=\mathbf z.
\]

Thus (17) gives

\[
\operatorname{im}(P-L\circ A)=P(\ker A),
\]

which attains the lower bound (16) and proves (1). If \(\ker A=\{0\}\), then \(A\) is injective and its inverse on \(\operatorname{im}A\) is a right inverse \(S\); hence \(S\circ A=I\), the attaining residual is zero, and both sides of (1) are zero. If \(\operatorname{im}A=\{0\}\), the unique right inverse from the zero space is the zero map, \(I-S\circ A=I\), and both residual space and \(P(\ker A)\) equal \(P(\mathbb R^E)\). This also covers \(E=\varnothing\).

For completeness, each vector in (2) lies in \(\ker A\). The coefficient of \(\mathbf e_e\) in \(\mathbf c^{\,e}\) is one, while its coefficient at every other index of \(E\setminus B\) is zero. Hence the family in (2) is linearly independent. If \(\mathbf z\in\ker A\), subtract

\[
\sum_{e\in E\setminus B}z_e\mathbf c^{\,e}
\]

from \(\mathbf z\). The result lies in \(\ker A\) and is supported on \(B\). Since the columns indexed by \(B\) are linearly independent, that result is zero. Thus the vectors (2) form a basis of \(\ker A\), and applying \(P\) proves (3).

We now prove the product-box assertions. The deletion of zero-mass indices is valid because a nonnegative integrable function with integral zero vanishes almost everywhere. After deletion, all barycenters in (8) are defined, \(T>0\), all \(\alpha_{ij}>0\), and

\[
\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}=1.
\tag{18}
\]

Fix arbitrary \(\theta_{ij}\in[-1/2,1/2]\), put \(\lambda_{ij}:=1/2+\theta_{ij}\in[0,1]\), and define the single density

\[
h_{\boldsymbol\lambda}:=
\sum_{i,j}\left[
(1-\lambda_{ij})\frac{b_j}{2\beta}f_i
+
\lambda_{ij}\frac{a_i}{2\beta}g_j
\right].
\tag{19}
\]

It is nonnegative. Pointwise almost everywhere,

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\boldsymbol\lambda}
&\leq
\sum_{i,j}\left(
\frac{b_j}{2\beta}f_i+
\frac{a_i}{2\beta}g_j
\right)\\
&=\frac{B}{2\beta}F+
\frac{A}{2\beta}G\\
&\leq\frac12F+\frac12G
\leq1,
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

where (7) and (5) give the last two inequalities. Each pair has selected mass independent of its switch parameter:

\[
\int\left[
(1-\lambda_{ij})\frac{b_j}{2\beta}f_i
+
\lambda_{ij}\frac{a_i}{2\beta}g_j
\right]d\mu
=\frac{a_i b_j}{2\beta}.
\tag{21}
\]

Summing (21) yields

\[
\int h_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\,d\mu
=\frac{AB}{2\beta}=T.
\tag{22}
\]

Using (8), (9), and (18), the normalized first moment of (19) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1T\int\mathbf x h_{\boldsymbol\lambda}(\mathbf x)\,d\mu(\mathbf x)
&=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}
\bigl((1-\lambda_{ij})\mathbf p_i+
\lambda_{ij}\mathbf q_j\bigr)\\
&=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}(\mathbf p_i+\mathbf q_j)
+\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}\theta_{ij}(\mathbf q_j-\mathbf p_i)\\
&=\mathbf c+\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}\theta_{ij}\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Equations (20), (22), and (23) prove (11), and allowing all independent switch parameters proves the equivalent Minkowski-sum inclusion (12). In particular, all switch choices are represented simultaneously within one density and at the same mass \(T\); no separate-choice or formal-product interpretation is used.

For the double-mass center, define

\[
h_{\mathbf c}:=\frac{B}{2\beta}F+\frac{A}{2\beta}G.
\tag{24}
\]

The final two inequalities in (20) show \(0\leq h_{\mathbf c}\leq1\). Its mass is

\[
\int h_{\mathbf c}\,d\mu
=\frac{BA}{2\beta}+\frac{AB}{2\beta}
=\frac{AB}{\beta}=2T,
\tag{25}
\]

and its normalized first moment is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{2T}\int\mathbf x h_{\mathbf c}(\mathbf x)\,d\mu(\mathbf x)
&=\frac12\left(
\frac1A\sum_i a_i\mathbf p_i+
\frac1B\sum_j b_j\mathbf q_j
\right)\\
&=\mathbf c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

This proves (13), including its exact factor of two.

It remains to prove (15). Let

\[
Z_0:=\sum_{i,j}
\left[-\frac{\alpha_{ij}}2\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij},
\frac{\alpha_{ij}}2\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\right].
\]

For every \(\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^d\), its support function is

\[
h_{Z_0}(\mathbf w)
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}
\left|\left\langle\mathbf w,\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\right\rangle\right|.
\tag{27}
\]

The support function of \(\frac12\Sigma^{1/2}B_2^d\) is

\[
\frac12\|\Sigma^{1/2}\mathbf w\|_2
=\frac12\left(
\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}^2
\left\langle\mathbf w,\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\right\rangle^2
\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{28}
\]

The \(\ell_1\)-norm of the finite scalar family

\[
\left(
\alpha_{ij}\left|\left\langle\mathbf w,\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\right\rangle\right|
\right)_{i,j}
\]

dominates its \(\ell_2\)-norm, so (27) is at least (28). Both sets are compact and convex, including when \(\Sigma\) is singular; support-function domination is therefore equivalent to

\[
\frac12\Sigma^{1/2}B_2^d\subseteq Z_0.
\]

Combining this with (12) proves (15).

Finally, (3) says that affine residual rank counts exactly the span of projected fundamental-circuit defects. Assertions (11)-(12) say that if such defect directions are actually represented by selector-barycenter differences \(\boldsymbol\Delta_{ij}\) satisfying (5)-(7), then their weighted independent switches are genuine metronoid points. The linear-algebra identity alone neither constructs those densities nor proves their simultaneous capacity bounds, so no global circuit completion or absorption conclusion follows from this packet.
