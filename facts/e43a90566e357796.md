## Statement

For $d\ge2$, let $K=B$ be a volume-one $d$-simplex with apex $P$, and let $t$ be its affine height, one at $P$ and zero at the opposite facet. Take $A=P+\varepsilon(K-P)$, $a=\varepsilon^d$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/(10d)$, at the left endpoint of the actual three-fiber hull $A,K,B$ at $-1,0,1$. Its prescribed weight-two midpoint source is $y=[g_K+a(g_A+g_B)]/(1+2a)$ on the middle fiber. The height of $y$ is $g=[1+a(d+2-d\varepsilon)]/[(d+1)(1+2a)]$. The closed cap $t+(g-1)z\ge g$ through this source has raw mass
$$
Q=\gamma_d[(1+a(1+\varepsilon))/(1+2a)]^d+a,\qquad
\gamma_d(1+a/2)-Q\ge3a/50>0.
$$
For $K=\operatorname{conv}((0,0),(2,0),(0,1))$ and $\varepsilon=1/100$ this gives $g=166733/500100$, $Q=11115856669/25010001000$, and target gap $159443/15631250625$.

## Proof

The convex hull of the lifts has projection $[-1,1]$. Its extreme slices are the stated endpoints and all its continuous coordinates lie in $K$, which gives its exact middle slice. The middle slice and a nonzero base direction give ambient full dimension. The homothety from $A$ to $B$ sends their uniform probabilities onto one another and preserves increasing rank in every continuous projection. The midpoint law of mass $a$ is constant density $a/[(1+\varepsilon)/2]^d$ on the simplex $P+[(1+\varepsilon)/2](K-P)$, and attains the sharp midpoint density ceiling. In particular all directional section-root and cap-root properties hold for this law by section and cap Brunn–Minkowski.

The centroid heights are $1/(d+1)$ on $K,B$ and $1-d\varepsilon/(d+1)$ on $A$, by the apex homothety. Thus the displayed formula for $g$ follows. Since $a\le\varepsilon^2\le1/(100d^2)$, $g\le1/(d+1)+2a<1/2$. The closed cap at base $-1$ has threshold $2g-1<0$ and captures all of $A$; at base $1$ its threshold is one, so it captures only the null apex. Its middle trace has volume $(1-g)^d$ by simplex dilation. Thus its mass is the stated $Q$, and the mixed target at $n=1$ is $\gamma_d(2+a)/2$.

Let $s=a(1-\varepsilon)/(1+2a)$. Since $1-(1-s)^d\ge ds-d(d-1)s^2/2$ for $0\le s\le1$ by integrating its second derivative twice, the deficit divided by $a$ is at least
$$
\gamma_d[1/2+d(1-\varepsilon)/(1+2a)-d(d-1)a(1-\varepsilon)^2/(2(1+2a)^2)]-1
\ \ge\ \gamma_d[d+1/2-d\varepsilon-2da-d^2a/2]-1
\ \ge\ \gamma_d[d+77/200]-1 .
$$
The last estimate uses $d\varepsilon\le1/10$, $2da\le1/100$, and $d^2a/2\le1/200$. At $d=2$ the last expression equals $3/50$. At $d\ge3$ it exceeds $77/600>3/50$ because $(1+1/d)^d<\sum_{j\ge0}1/j!<3$. The latter bound follows by comparing the terms starting at $j=2$ with the geometric series of terms $2^{-(j-1)}$, strictly at $j\ge3$. The rational planar calibration follows from $a=1/10000$ in the mass and height formulas.
