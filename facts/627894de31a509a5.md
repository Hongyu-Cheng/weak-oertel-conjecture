## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $y$ be an interior point of a $d$-simplex $T$ with barycentric coordinates $0<\lambda_i\le1/d$. Write $g$ for the ordinary centroid of $T$. For each vertex $i$, use the section through $y$ with section centroid $y$ that separates this vertex from all others, allowing other vertices on the section. Orient its unit normal $u_i$ toward vertex $i$. Let $p_i$ be the volume of this cap divided by $|T|$, $S_i$ its section area, and $s_i=S_i u_i\cdot(g-y)/|T|$. Put $z_i=d\lambda_i$, omit two distinct indices attaining the minimum and maximum of $z_i$, and call the remaining index set $I$. Set $D=d+1-\sum_{j\in I}1/z_j$ and $c=(d+1)/(dD)$. Then $D\ge1$ and, with $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$,
$$\Gamma\le p_i-cs_i\le5/9.$$
The upper bound is attained in every dimension $d\ge2$ at the coordinate vector $(z_0,\ldots,z_d)=(1/3,1,2/3,1,\ldots,1)$ by choosing $z_i=2/3$.

The same correction gives $p-cs\ge\Gamma$ for the complementary orientations. Consequently, for any finite family of these oriented sections with weights $\theta_i\ge0$ summing to one, scalar balance $\sum_i\theta_i S_i u_i\cdot(g-y)=0$ implies $\sum_i\theta_i p_i\ge\Gamma$.

## Proof

Write $\xi_j$ for barycentric coordinates on $T$. A section isolating $i$ is the simplex of its edge intercepts from $i$ toward $j\ne i$. The centroid condition fixes the intercept fractions as $d\lambda_j$, all in $(0,1]$. Thus the unique section in this class is given by raw margin $f_i=d-\sum_{j\ne i}\xi_j/\lambda_j=0$, oriented toward $i$, and $p_i=d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j$. For a small nonnegative shift $h$, the cap $f_i\ge h$ has fraction $p_i(1-h/d)^d$ by homothety at $i$. Its right derivative at zero equals $-S_i/(|T|\lVert\nabla f_i\rVert)$ by coarea and continuity of the section area at this interior cut, including the cases of a vertex on the section. Since $f_i(y)=0$, this gives $s_i=p_i f_i(g)$, where $f_i(g)=d-(d+1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}1/\lambda_j$.

Write $a=\min z_i$, $b=\max z_i$, and $P=\prod_{j\in I}z_j$. In terms of the deficits $1-z_j$ of sum one, each denominator $z_j$ is at least $a$, and the remaining deficits have total $a+b-1\le a$. Thus $\sum_{j\in I}(1-z_j)/z_j\le1$ and $D=2-\sum_{j\in I}(1-z_j)/z_j\ge1$. Substituting the cap and moment formulas yields
$$Q_i=p_i-cs_i=\frac{P}{D}\left(\frac{a+b}{z_i}-\frac{ab}{z_i^2}\right).$$
The factor in parentheses is a concave quadratic in $1/z_i$ on $[1/b,1/a]$ and equals one at both endpoints. This remains true when all coordinates coincide. Since $D+\sum_{j\in I}1/z_j=d+1$, AM–GM on these $d$ positive numbers gives $P/D\ge\Gamma$. This proves the lower bound. For the upper bound it suffices to check $i\in I$, since omitted indices attain the minimum.

Keep the $z_j$, $j\in I$, fixed. Then $a+b=d-\sum_{j\in I}z_j\ge1$, and increasing their spread decreases $ab$ and increases $Q_i$. The extreme choice has $b=1$ and $a=d-1-\sum_{j\in I}z_j$, preserving all order constraints. If $a=0$ at that choice then all remaining $z_j=1$ and the expression is $1/2$. Otherwise write $t=1-z_i$ and denote the other deficits in $I$ by $y_1,\ldots,y_{d-2}$. Put $Y=\sum_j y_j$, $m=1-t-Y$, $A=1-t-t^2$ and $B=2-3t$. Then $a=t+Y$, $0\le t,y_j\le m$, $0\le t\le1/2$, and
$$Q_i=\frac{\prod_j(1-y_j)(A-tY)}{B-(1-t)\sum_jy_j/(1-y_j)}.$$
Here the denominator is $(1-t)D>0$ and $A-tY\ge(1-t)^2$.

Fix $t,Y$. Vary two deficits of sum $z$ and product $p$ inside $[0,m]$. Up to a positive factor, their contribution to the expression is
$$\frac{(1-z+p)^2}{B_0(1-z+p)-(1-t)(z-2p)},\qquad B_0\le B.$$
Its derivative in $p$ has the sign of $(B_0-2(1-t))(1-z)+(B_0+2(1-t))p-2(1-t)z$. Since $p\le z^2/4$, $0\le z\le1$, and $0\le t\le1/2$, this is at most $-t(1-z)+(-1+3t/4)z\le0$. Moving the pair toward maximum spread does not decrease the expression and makes one coordinate hit a bound. After finitely many such moves, there are $k$ entries $m$, at most one residual $r\in[0,m]$, and zeros. A dummy zero residual has no effect. They satisfy $(k+1)m+r=1-t$ and $m\ge t$.

For $k\ge1$, fix $t,r$ and allow real $k$ down to one, which preserves $t,r\le m$. Put $z=1-t-r$, $w=z/(k+1)$, $u=1-w$, $h=1-t$, $v=1-r$. The expression becomes $u^k v(h-tu)/(hD_k)$, with $D_k=k+4-1/h-k/u-1/v\ge1$ by the same deficit argument with multiplicity $k$. The factor $v(h-tu)/h$ decreases weakly with $k$. Here $z>0$, and
$$
-(\log u^k)'=\frac{w^2}{z(1-w)}-L(w),\quad
-D_k'=\frac{w^2(1-z)}{z(1-w)^2},\quad
L(w)=\log(1-w)+\frac{w}{1-w}\le\frac{w^2}{2(1-w)^2}.
$$
The last inequality follows by integrating $L'(w)=w/(1-w)^2$. Thus $-(\log u^k)'+D_k'/D_k\ge w^2(z-w)/[z(1-w)^2]-L(w)\ge0$, since $w\le z/2$. It suffices to bound $k=1$.

In that case the domain is $t,r\ge0$, $3t+r\le1$, $t+3r\le1$. Set $u=(1+t+r)/2$, $h=1-t$, $v=1-r$, $N=uv(h-tu)/h$, and $D_1=5-1/h-1/u-1/v$. The identity $D_1-2N=P_1(t,r)/[2(1-r)(1-t)(1+t+r)]$ holds with
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1={}&t^4(r-1)^2+t^3(3r^3-r^2-7r+5)+t^2(3r^4+4r^3-12r^2+10r-3)\\
&+t(r^5+3r^4-6r^3+8r^2-5r+1)-2r^4-4r^2+2r.
\end{aligned}
$$
The domain splits into two triangles with common vertices $(0,0),(1/4,1/4)$ and third vertex $(1/3,0)$ or $(0,1/3)$. For $U,V,W\ge0$ and $H=U+V+W>0$, the table gives the coefficients of $U^i V^j W^k$ in $41472 H^6 P_1((U/3+V/4)/H,(V/4)/H)$ and $124416 H^6 P_1((V/4)/H,(U/3+V/4)/H)$ respectively. The only unlisted monomial $W^6$ has coefficient zero.

Both polynomials have nonnegative coefficients, so $D_1\ge2N$ and the ratio for $k\ge1$ is at most $1/2$.

For $k=0$ the sole possibly nonzero deficit besides $t$ is $y$, with $0\le y\le\min\{(1-t)/2,1-2t\}$, and the expression is $(1-y)^2(A-ty)/(B-Cy)$ with $C=3-4t>0$. Polynomial division gives a quadratic quotient of leading coefficient $t/C$ and remainder $(1-t)^2(2t-1)(2t^2+3t-3)/C^3\ge0$ on $0\le t\le1/2$. Its second derivative in $y$ is nonnegative because $B-Cy>0$. At $y=0$ the gap from $5/9$ is $(3t-1)^2/[9(2-3t)]$. For $t\le1/3$ the other endpoint is $(1-t)/2$, the already bounded $k=1,r=0$ case. For $t\ge1/3$ the other endpoint is $1-2t$, where the expression minus $1/2$ equals
$$-\frac{(2t-1)(4t^3-6t^2+5t-1)}{2(8t^2-7t+1)}\le0.$$
The denominator is negative on this interval, and the cubic is positive at $1/3$ and has derivative $12(t-1/2)^2+2>0$. This proves $Q_i\le5/9$. Equality occurs at $t=1/3,y=0$ with the coordinate vector stated.

Complementing sends $(p_i,s_i)$ to $(1-p_i,-s_i)$ because the cutting plane has zero $d$-volume. Thus the complementary corrected fraction is at least $4/9\ge\Gamma$, using the decrease of $(x/(x+1))^x$ for $x>0$ from the derivative of its logarithm. Multiplying the corrected inequalities by the weights and summing cancels the common correction by scalar balance.
