## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ have between one and three distinct points, and let $f_z$ be nonzero vectors in a real vector space. If $P$ is affinely independent, then the vectors $(1,z)\otimes f_z$ are independent. If $P$ consists of three collinear points, then these tensors are dependent exactly when the three $f_z$ are proportional. When the $f_z$ are real functions strictly negative at a common argument, the proportionality factors relative to any one $f_z$ are positive.

## Proof

For affinely independent $P$, the vectors $(1,z)$ have dual linear functionals that isolate one index at a time. Applying them on the first tensor factor proves independence since $f_z\ne0$.

For three collinear points write $z_i=z_*+t_i v$ with $v\ne0$ and $t_i$ distinct. The vectors $(1,z_*)$ and $(0,v)$ are independent. A dependence with coefficients $\lambda_i$ is thus exactly $\sum_i\lambda_i f_i=0$ and $\sum_i t_i\lambda_i f_i=0$. If one coefficient is zero, the two remaining equations with distinct $t_i$ force both remaining coefficients to be zero. In a nonzero dependence, subtract $t_1$ times the first equation from the second. The resulting relation gives proportionality of $f_2,f_3$, and then the first equation gives proportionality of $f_1$ with them. Conversely, if $f_i=c_i f$ with each $c_i\ne0$, divide the coefficients of a nonzero relation among the three vectors $(1,z_i)$ by $c_i$. The resulting coefficients give a tensor dependence. Strict negativity at the common argument makes each ratio relative to one $f_i$ positive.
