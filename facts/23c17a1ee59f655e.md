## Statement

Use the mixed-integer measure from the locked problem. For a convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(W(C)=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). For \(\mathbf y\in S\), define \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces, and put \(\mathcal D(C)=W(C)^{-1}\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)\). This is precisely the normalization of accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`: \(\mathcal D\) is normalized, whereas \(h\) and \(W\) are not.

For every integer \(r\ge1\), put \(n=r+1\), \(d=1\), \(q=2^r\), \(\varepsilon=1/(16q)\), and \(\eta=(1+\varepsilon)/2\). With integer-base coordinates \(\mathbf z=(t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r\) and continuous coordinate \(y\in\mathbb R\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_r&=\left\{(t,\mathbf x):-1\le t\le1,\quad
 \frac{t-1}{2}\le x_i\le\frac{t+1}{2}\quad(1\le i\le r)\right\},\\
a(t)&=\frac{1+t}{2}+\varepsilon\frac{1-t}{2},\\
\widehat C_r&=\left\{(t,\mathbf x,y):(t,\mathbf x)\in P_r,\quad
 -\frac{a(t)}2\le y\le\frac{a(t)}2\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(P_r\) uses exactly the base inequalities of accepted fact `81d02df725cefd4a`, now for every \(r\ge1\). The new profile \(a(t)\) and interval lengths \(\varepsilon,\eta\) are the displayed ones; the padding body's profile \(a_r(t)\), its weights \(\epsilon_r,\eta_r\), and its continuous dimension \(D=r^3\) from that fact are not substituted here. The body \(\widehat C_r\) is the body called \(C_r\) in the first archived response, and is distinct from the simplex-product family \(C_n\) of accepted fact `02a48d28569660b9`.

The set \(\widehat C_r\) is a full-dimensional rational compact convex polytope. Its entire integer-base support, denoted \(D_r\), is

\[
D_r=\{(1,\mathbf b):\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^r\}
 \cup\{(0,\mathbf0)\}
 \cup\{(-1,-\mathbf b):\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^r\}.
\]

Every base in \(D_r\) has the actual interval fiber \([-w_{\mathbf z}/2,w_{\mathbf z}/2]\), where

\[
w_{(1,\mathbf b)}=1,\qquad
w_{(0,\mathbf0)}=\eta,\qquad
w_{(-1,-\mathbf b)}=\varepsilon.
\]

Every other integer-base fiber is empty. Thus no additional nonempty zero-length fibers are omitted. Put \(S_r=\widehat C_r\cap(\mathbb Z^{r+1}\times\mathbb R)\), \(W_r=W(\widehat C_r)\), and \(p_{\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}/W_r\), with both tables extended by zero outside \(D_r\). For measurable \(B\subseteq S_r\), its mixed measure is \(\mathcal H_1(B)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_r}\operatorname{length}\{y:(\mathbf z,y)\in B\}\).

The support \(D_r\) is the source's \(A_r\) and is the positive support called \(D\) in accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`; the source's table \(p_r\) is \(p\) here for the chosen \(r\). Use that fact's scores with precisely its normalization:

\[
\begin{aligned}
E(p)&=\max_{\mathbf j\in D_r}\inf_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^{r+1}}
 \sum_{\mathbf z\in D_r}p_{\mathbf z}
       e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf j)},\\
H_{\mathbf j}(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^{r+1}}
 \sqrt{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}},
 \qquad H_*(p)=\max_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^{r+1}}H_{\mathbf j}(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

The displayed formula for \(E(p)\) is exactly \(e^{-I_*}\) from that fact, since its \(I(\mathbf j)\) is the negative natural logarithm of the displayed infimum and \(I_*\) is its minimum over the positive support. The Hellinger sum includes zero displacement once and both orientations of each nonzero displacement. Its unnormalized version satisfies \(H_{\mathbf j}(w)=W_rH_{\mathbf j}(p)\), so \(H_*(w)=W_rH_*(p)\).

The exact mass, maximum actual depth, and information score are

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_r&=q(1+\varepsilon)+\eta
     =q+\frac9{16}+\frac1{32q},\\
\sup_{\mathbf y\in S_r}h_{S_r}(\mathbf y)&=\frac12,
 &\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)&=\frac1{2W_r},\\
E(p)=H_*(p)&=\frac{2q\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\eta}{W_r}
 =\frac{\frac{\sqrt q}{2}+\frac12+\frac1{32q}}{W_r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^r\), the fixed point \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}=(1,\mathbf b,0)\in S_r\) attains this supremum. Thus the source's maximum-depth convention and the canonical supremum convention agree on this family; \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}\) is the point called \(\mathbf p_{\mathbf b}\) in the source.

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)}{E(p)}
 &=\frac1{2^{r/2}+1+2^{-r-4}},\\
2^{r+1}\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)
 &=\frac{2^r}{2^r+\frac9{16}+2^{-r-5}}
 \longrightarrow1,\\
(r+1)^\alpha\frac{\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)}{E(p)}
 &\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{for every fixed real }\alpha\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

There are therefore no constants \(c>0\) and \(\alpha\ge0\), independent of the body and integer dimension, such that \(\mathcal D(C)\ge cE(p)/n^\alpha\) for every positive-mass actual convex body and its entire normalized fiber-volume table. Here \(\alpha\) is the exponent called \(a\) in the source's polynomial-loss comparison, separated from the interval profile \(a(t)\). The failure already holds for full-dimensional rational bodies with \(d=1\) and \(E(p)=H_*(p)\); it also rules out the same comparison with \(H_*(p)\) in place of \(E(p)\). Nevertheless every \(\widehat C_r\) satisfies the locked conclusion for every \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), using any one fixed \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}\) before the closed halfspace is chosen.

## Proof

Fix \(r\ge1\). We verify the new body directly, rather than infer its fiber lengths or depths from the different padding profile in accepted fact `81d02df725cefd4a`. On \([-1,1]\), the affine function \(a(t)\) belongs to \([\varepsilon,1]\), with \(a(-1)=\varepsilon\), \(a(0)=\eta\), and \(a(1)=1\). Thus \(\widehat C_r\) is bounded, closed, and defined by finitely many rational affine inequalities. It is convex, and all defining inequalities are strict at \((t,\mathbf x,y)=(0,\mathbf0,0)\). Hence it is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^{r+2}\) and is a rational compact convex polytope. Its projection onto the base coordinates is exactly \(P_r\), since \(y=0\) is allowed above every point of \(P_r\).

At an integer base, the first coordinate is \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}\). At \(t=1\), each remaining integer coordinate lies in \([0,1]\), so it is Boolean. At \(t=0\), each lies in \([-1/2,1/2]\), so all are zero. At \(t=-1\), each lies in \([-1,0]\), giving exactly the negatives of the Boolean vectors. Conversely each listed base belongs to \(P_r\). This proves \(P_r\cap\mathbb Z^{r+1}=D_r\) and exhausts every mixed-integer point. The defining inequalities for \(y\) give the asserted full intervals and their strictly positive lengths. There are \(q\) bases in each outer layer and one in the middle, so

\[
W_r=q(1+\varepsilon)+\eta
 =q+\frac1{16}+\frac12+\frac1{32q}
 =q+\frac9{16}+\frac1{32q}.
\]

We first establish the lower depth bound at a point fixed before the halfspace. Fix \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^r\) and \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}=(1,\mathbf b,0)\). Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}\). Its intersection with the continuous line above \((1,\mathbf b)\) is either the whole line or a closed ray containing \(y=0\). The empty case is impossible because the midpoint is contained. A ray containing zero retains length at least \(1/2\) from the actual interval \([-1/2,1/2]\), while the whole line retains length one. In particular, if the whole fiber lies on the boundary of \(H\), its entire length is counted. All other fiber contributions are nonnegative. Therefore \(\mathcal H_1(S_r\cap H)\ge1/2\) for every such \(H\), and \(h_{S_r}(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b})\ge1/2\).

For the all-point upper bound, expose each outer base by a concrete linear functional. At \(\mathbf v=(1,\mathbf b)\), use

\[
\ell_{\mathbf b}(t,\mathbf x)
 =(r+1)t+\sum_{i=1}^r(2b_i-1)x_i.
\]

For a fixed \(t\in[-1,1]\), each coefficient \(2b_i-1\) is nonzero, so this functional is maximized uniquely by taking \(x_i=(t+1)/2\) when \(b_i=1\) and \(x_i=(t-1)/2\) when \(b_i=0\). Substitution gives the maximum over that section as \((r/2+1+\sum_i b_i)t+r/2\). Its coefficient of \(t\) is positive. Thus \(\ell_{\mathbf b}\) has its unique maximum on \(P_r\) at \((1,\mathbf b)\). Since \(P_r=-P_r\), the functional \(\mathbf z\mapsto\ell_{\mathbf b}(-\mathbf z)\) uniquely exposes \((-1,-\mathbf b)\). Each of these functionals has integer coefficients. Accordingly, for either outer base \(\mathbf v\), its exposing functional \(\ell\) satisfies \(\ell(\mathbf z)-\ell(\mathbf v)\le-1\) at every \(\mathbf z\in D_r\setminus\{\mathbf v\}\).

Fix any mixed point \((\mathbf v,y_0)\) above an outer base. Choose \(\beta=1\) if \(y_0\ge0\), and \(\beta=-1\) otherwise. In the selected fiber, the inequality \(\beta(y-y_0)\ge0\) retains exactly length \(w_{\mathbf v}/2-|y_0|\le w_{\mathbf v}/2\). Every continuous coordinate of \(S_r\), including \(y_0\), belongs to \([-1/2,1/2]\), so \(|\beta(y-y_0)|\le1\). The closed ambient halfspace

\[
H_{\mathbf v,y_0}
 =\left\{(\mathbf z,y):
 2\bigl(\ell(\mathbf z)-\ell(\mathbf v)\bigr)
       +\beta(y-y_0)\ge0\right\}
\]

contains \((\mathbf v,y_0)\) on its boundary. On every other actual fiber its left side is at most \(-2+1=-1\), so those fibers are entirely excluded, including their endpoints. Its retained mixed measure is exactly \(w_{\mathbf v}/2-|y_0|\). Thus every top-fiber point has depth at most \(1/2\), and every bottom-fiber point has depth at most \(\varepsilon/2\). This also supplies the upper bound \(h_{S_r}(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b})\le1/2\). When a selected point is an endpoint, the retained singleton has length zero, as required by the same formula.

Now fix an arbitrary point \(((0,\mathbf0),y_0)\) in the middle fiber and choose \(\beta\) by the same rule. Consider the closed halfspace

\[
H_{0,y_0}=\{(t,\mathbf x,y):-2t+\beta(y-y_0)\ge0\}.
\]

It contains the selected point. The bound \(|\beta(y-y_0)|\le1\) shows that its defining expression is at least one on every bottom fiber and at most minus one on every top fiber. Thus it retains every bottom interval in full, excludes every top interval, and retains exactly length \(\eta/2-|y_0|\) in the middle interval. Its mixed measure is

\[
q\varepsilon+\frac\eta2-|y_0|
 \le\frac5{16}+\frac1{64q}
 \le\frac{41}{128}<\frac12,
\]

where \(q\ge2\). The support classification covers all possible mixed points, so these explicit halfspaces prove \(h_{S_r}(\mathbf y)\le1/2\) for every \(\mathbf y\in S_r\). Together with the lower bound at any fixed \(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b}\), this proves the claimed maximum and \(\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)=1/(2W_r)\). In particular, the lower-bound center was never chosen after seeing the halfspace, and all upper bounds used actual fibers and closed ambient halfspaces with nonzero normals.

We next compute the information score in the normalization of accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`. At an outer base \(\mathbf v\), the exponential sum is at least \(p_{\mathbf v}\), from its term with \(\mathbf z=\mathbf v\). Let \(\mathbf u\) be the coefficient vector of its exposing functional \(\ell\). Taking \(\mathbf a=T\mathbf u\) with \(T\to+\infty\) makes every other term tend to zero because \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf v)\le-1\). Finiteness of the support gives an infimum of exactly \(p_{\mathbf v}\). Therefore the top and bottom scores are respectively \(1/W_r\) and \(\varepsilon/W_r\). This limiting argument concerns only the exponential infimum; the actual-depth halfspaces above already have finite coefficients.

At the middle base \(\mathbf j=(0,\mathbf0)\), write \(\mathbf a=(s,\mathbf c)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^r\). Summing independently over the Boolean coordinates gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_r}p_{\mathbf z}e^{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf z}
 =\frac{e^s\prod_{i=1}^r(1+e^{c_i})
       +\varepsilon e^{-s}\prod_{i=1}^r(1+e^{-c_i})
       +\eta}{W_r}.
\]

The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality applied to the first two numerator terms, followed by \(e^u+e^{-u}\ge2\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^s\prod_{i=1}^r(1+e^{c_i})
 +\varepsilon e^{-s}\prod_{i=1}^r(1+e^{-c_i})
 &\ge2\sqrt{\varepsilon
        \prod_{i=1}^r(2+e^{c_i}+e^{-c_i})}\\
 &\ge2\sqrt{\varepsilon\,4^r}
  =2q\sqrt{\varepsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both inequalities are equalities at \(\mathbf c=\mathbf0\) and \(s=\tfrac12\log\varepsilon\). Hence the middle-base infimum is exactly \((2q\sqrt\varepsilon+\eta)/W_r\). Its numerator equals \(\sqrt q/2+1/2+1/(32q)>1\), since \(q\ge2\). As \(0<\varepsilon<1\), this score is strictly larger than both types of outer score. The support is exhausted, so it is exactly \(E(p)\).

For the Hellinger maximum, we must also check every integer center outside the positive support. If \(p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}>0\), both endpoints belong to \(P_r\), so their midpoint \(\mathbf j\) belongs to \(P_r\). If \(\mathbf j\) is integral, the support calculation gives \(\mathbf j\in D_r\). Consequently \(H_{\mathbf j}(p)=0\) for every \(\mathbf j\notin D_r\).

At a top center \(\mathbf j=(1,\mathbf b)\), the first coordinates of two endpoints averaging to \(\mathbf j\) must both be one. Their remaining coordinates are Boolean; an average equal to \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\) forces both endpoint coordinates to equal \(b_i\). Thus the only positive reflected displacement is zero, and \(H_{(1,\mathbf b)}(p)=1/W_r\). The same reasoning for the negative Boolean layer gives \(H_{(-1,-\mathbf b)}(p)=\varepsilon/W_r\). At the middle center, either both endpoints are the middle base, or they are \((1,\mathbf b)\) and \((-1,-\mathbf b)\). There are exactly \(q\) such outer pairs, each counted in both orientations, and the middle atom is counted once. Therefore

\[
H_{(0,\mathbf0)}(p)
 =\frac{\eta+2q\sqrt\varepsilon}{W_r}
 =E(p).
\]

The already proved strict comparison with the outer scores, together with zero outside the support, proves \(H_*(p)=E(p)\) in the full canonical maximization over integer centers.

The exact ratios now follow by division and substitution:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)}{E(p)}
 &=\frac1{2(2q\sqrt\varepsilon+\eta)}
  =\frac1{\sqrt q+1+1/(16q)}
  =\frac1{2^{r/2}+1+2^{-r-4}},\\
2^{r+1}\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)
 &=\frac q{W_r}
  =\frac1{1+9/(16q)+1/(32q^2)}
  \longrightarrow1.
\end{aligned}
\]

For fixed \(\alpha\ge0\), the nonnegative quantity \((r+1)^\alpha\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)/E(p)\) is at most \((r+1)^\alpha2^{-r/2}\). The logarithm of this upper bound is \(\alpha\log(r+1)-(r/2)\log2\), which tends to minus infinity. Hence the stated polynomial-weighted ratio tends to zero. Given any proposed constants \(c>0\) and \(\alpha\ge0\), choose \(r\) for which this ratio is less than \(c\); with \(n=r+1\), the resulting actual body violates \(\mathcal D(C)\ge cE(p)/n^\alpha\). Equality of the two scores gives the identical obstruction for \(H_*(p)\).

Accepted fact `02a48d28569660b9` gives \(\mathcal D(C_n)=3/[2(n+2)]\) for its different simplex-product body and gives information score one for that body's normalized table, proving failure of constant-loss information-score lifting. The exponential ratio proved here strengthens that conclusion to every fixed polynomial loss. Since the present \(p\) is the entire normalized table of \(\widehat C_r\), it also meets the realization hypothesis of accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`, which identifies \(E(p)\) as the limiting product-depth rate. The separation proved here is between that canonical score and the actual depth of one body, with no alteration of the actual fibers or center quantifiers.

Finally, \(q\ge2\) gives \(9/16+1/(32q)\le37/64<q\), so \(W_r<2q\). Thus \(2^{r+1}\mathcal D(\widehat C_r)=q/W_r>1/2>1/e\). Every fixed top midpoint therefore satisfies \(h_{S_r}(\mathbf m_{\mathbf b})>c_0 2^{-(r+1)}W_r\) for every \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), and the lower-depth proof applies to every closed halfspace containing that same point. This establishes the weak-Oertel-scale calibration and shows that this family is not a disproof of the locked conjecture.
