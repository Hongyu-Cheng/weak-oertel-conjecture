## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer and put \(n=N-1\). Let \(A<B\) be real, and let \(R,T:[A,B]\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous concave functions that are positive on \((A,B)\). For \(n\ge1\), fix real coefficients \(a_i\ge0\) and \(b_i,c_i>0\), for \(1\le i\le n\), and define

\[
P:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
A\le t\le B,\quad x_i\ge0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^n b_i x_i\le R(t),\quad
\sum_{i=1}^n c_i x_i\le T(t)\right\}.
\]

For \(n=0\), define \(P=[A,B]\), using the empty-coordinate convention below. Assume that the complete support \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\) is nonempty. For any \(h\in\mathbb R\), put \(w_{(x,s)}:=\exp(-(s-h)^2-a\cdot x)\) on \(D\), with \(a\cdot x=0\) when \(n=0\), and put \(W:=\sum_{(x,s)\in D}w_{(x,s)}\).

There are one integer \(s_\star\in[A,B]\) and one original lattice point \(J=(j,s_\star)\in D\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{(x,s)\in D\cap H\\s=s_\star}}
w_{(x,s)}
\ge e^{-310}\,2^{-N}W.
\]

Thus the true weighted containing-closed-halfspace depth of \(J\), computed using only its original row, is at least \(e^{-310}W/2^N\). All original lattice boundary points are included; neither integral endpoints nor rational resource coefficients are assumed. The scalar quantitative estimate used from accepted fact `c847c935ecb28857` is mapped explicitly in the proof.

## Proof

Let \(\mathcal I:=[A,B]\cap\mathbb Z\). For \(s\in\mathcal I\), let \(D_s:=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot x\le R(s),\ c\cdot x\le T(s)\}\); when \(n=0\), let \(D_s\) consist of the unique empty vector. Nonnegativity of both budgets means that each such row contains zero. Hence \(\mathcal I\) is nonempty, and these are exactly the occupied integer heights. Continuity of \(R\) on the compact interval bounds every horizontal coordinate by \(\max_{[A,B]}R/b_i\) when \(n\ge1\). Thus \(D\) is finite and \(0<W<\infty\).

For \(\rho,\tau\ge0\), define

\[
F(\rho,\tau):=
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\\b\cdot z\le\rho,\ c\cdot z\le\tau}}
e^{-a\cdot z}.
\]

For \(n=0\), set \(F(\rho,\tau)=1\). This function is positive and nondecreasing in both arguments. It obeys the simultaneous weighted doubling bound
\(F(2\rho,2\tau)\le2^nF(\rho,\tau)\), including zero budgets. Indeed, every vector in the sum on the left has the unique coordinatewise decomposition \(z=2y+\varepsilon\), where \(y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\). Positivity of both resource vectors gives \(b\cdot y\le\rho\) and \(c\cdot y\le\tau\). Nonnegativity of the tilts gives \(e^{-a\cdot z}=e^{-2a\cdot y-a\cdot\varepsilon}\le e^{-a\cdot y}\). At most \(2^n\) vectors \(z\) map to any fixed \(y\), proving the bound. For \(n=0\) it is an equality.

Write \(g_s:=e^{-(s-h)^2}\) and \(M_s:=\sum_{x\in D_s}w_{(x,s)}=g_sF(R(s),T(s))\). Suppose that \(s,t\in\mathcal I\) have the same parity, and put \(m=(s+t)/2\). Then \(m\in\mathcal I\). For each \(\nu\in\{s,t\}\), concavity and nonnegativity give \(R(m)\ge R(\nu)/2\) and \(T(m)\ge T(\nu)/2\). Applying monotonicity followed by simultaneous doubling yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(R(m),T(m))
&\ge F(R(\nu)/2,T(\nu)/2)\\
&\ge 2^{-n}F(R(\nu),T(\nu)).
\end{aligned}
\]

This holds separately for both endpoints, so its right-hand side can be replaced by \(2^{-n}\sqrt{F(R(s),T(s))F(R(t),T(t))}\). Since \(g_m=e^{(s-t)^2/4}\sqrt{g_sg_t}\), we obtain the whole-row midpoint inequality

\[
M_m\ge 2^{-n}e^{(s-t)^2/4}\sqrt{M_sM_t}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(M_\star:=\max_{s\in\mathcal I}M_s\), choose a maximizing height \(s_\star\), and define \(V_s:=\log(M_\star/M_s)\ge0\). If two heights of the same parity satisfy \(V_s,V_t\le\eta\), where \(\eta\ge0\), then (1) and \(M_m\le M_\star\) imply \(1\ge2^{-n}e^{(s-t)^2/4-\eta}\). Therefore \(|s-t|\le2\sqrt{\eta+n\log2}\). In either height parity class, consecutive distinct integers are at least two apart. Consequently that parity class contributes at most \(\sqrt{\eta+n\log2}+1\) heights to the sublevel set, and
\(\#\{s\in\mathcal I:V_s\le\eta\}\le2(\sqrt{\eta+n\log2}+1)\).

Using \(e^{-V_s}=\int_{V_s}^{\infty}e^{-\eta}\,d\eta\) and interchanging the finite sum with the integral gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W}{M_\star}
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta}
\#\{s\in\mathcal I:V_s\le\eta\}\,d\eta\\
&\le2\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta}
\bigl(\sqrt\eta+\sqrt{n\log2}+1\bigr)\,d\eta\\
&\le2\sqrt{n\log2}+4
\le6e^{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(\sqrt{u+v}\le\sqrt u+\sqrt v\) and \(\sqrt\eta\le(\eta+1)/2\) give the first two bounds, using \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta}\,d\eta=\int_0^\infty\eta e^{-\eta}\,d\eta=1\). For the last bound, \(\log2\le1\) and \(e^r\ge1+r\), for \(r\ge0\), give \(2\sqrt{n\log2}+4\le2\sqrt n+4\le6e^{\sqrt n}\). Both parity classes and every endpoint height were retained in this argument.

Assume for now that \(n\ge1\). Extend \(w\) by zero to \(\mathbb Z^N\setminus D\), and define the original oriented row reflection sum, including the zero displacement, by

\[
S_{j,s}:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\min\{w_{(j+v,s)},w_{(j-v,s)}\}.
\]

Suppose first that \(R(s_\star),T(s_\star)>0\). Apply the scalar construction in accepted fact `c847c935ecb28857` to this row. Its support denoted \(A\) is our \(D_{s_\star}\), and its fixed budgets denoted \(R,T\) are the numbers \(R(s_\star),T(s_\star)\). Its \(n,a,b,c\) are unchanged. Its scalar weights are precisely \(\widetilde w_z=g_{s_\star}^{-1}w_{(z,s_\star)}\), including their zero extension, so \(\widetilde W=g_{s_\star}^{-1}M_\star\) and \(\widetilde S_j=g_{s_\star}^{-1}S_{j,s_\star}\).

Equation (10) of that accepted proof, before the coarser dimension-free estimate in its statement, supplies one \(j\in D_{s_\star}\) such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{S_{j,s_\star}}{2^{-n}M_\star}
&\ge\frac14\exp\left(
n\log\frac43-2\sqrt{\frac{8n}{3}}-2\log3
-(6\sqrt n+2)\log7\right)\\
&\ge\exp\left(\frac n4-16\sqrt n-10\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For the second inequality, \(\log(4/3)=\int_1^{4/3}t^{-1}\,dt\ge1/4\), \(2\sqrt{8/3}\le4\), and \(\log3,\log4,\log7<2\). In the logarithm of the first expression, the square-root terms therefore cost at most \(16\sqrt n\), and the remaining negative constants cost at most \(2+4+4=10\). The bound \(\log7<2\) follows, for example, from the exponential series \(e^2>1+2+2+4/3+2/3=7\); it also implies the other stated logarithm bounds. This use of the quantitative accepted proof preserves its dimension-dependent surplus.

If instead either selected budget is zero, strict positivity of the corresponding resource vector gives \(D_{s_\star}=\{0\}\). Choose \(j=0\). Then \(S_{j,s_\star}=M_\star\), and (3) still holds, because its left-hand side is \(2^n\) and \(\log2\ge1/2\ge1/4\). Thus in every case an original center \(J=(j,s_\star)\in D\) satisfies (3).

To compare (3) with true same-row depth, fix any closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\). For every \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), at least one of \(J+(v,0)\) and \(J-(v,0)\) lies in \(H\): otherwise both would satisfy the strict opposite linear inequality, and their midpoint \(J\) would satisfy it as well. Put \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{(j+v,s_\star)},w_{(j-v,s_\star)}\}\), so \(\alpha(v)=\alpha(-v)\). Changing \(v\) to \(-v\) in a finite supported sum gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\sum_{\substack{x\in D_{s_\star}\\(x,s_\star)\in H}}
w_{(x,s_\star)}
&\ge\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\alpha(v)
\bigl(\mathbf1_H(J+(v,0))+\mathbf1_H(J-(v,0))\bigr)\\
&\ge\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\alpha(v)
=S_{j,s_\star}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The zero displacement is included throughout. All weights in (4) are the original weights on the complete original support.

Combining (2), (3), and (4), and recalling \(N=n+1\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{x\in D_{s_\star}\\(x,s_\star)\in H}}
w_{(x,s_\star)}
&\ge\frac{M_\star}{W}
\exp\left(\frac n4-16\sqrt n-10\right)\frac{W}{2^N}\\
&\ge\frac16\exp\left(\frac n4-17\sqrt n-10\right)\frac{W}{2^N}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-299}}6\frac{W}{2^N}
\ge e^{-310}\frac{W}{2^N}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, \(n/4-17\sqrt n=(\sqrt n/2-17)^2-289\ge-289\), and \(\log6<2\) gives \(e^{-299}/6>e^{-301}>e^{-310}\). The selected row and center precede the choice of \(H\), so this proves the simultaneous halfspace conclusion for \(n\ge1\).

When \(n=0\), every row is one point and (2) gives \(W/M_\star\le4\). Choose \(J=s_\star\). Every closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains that entire singleton row, whose mass is \(M_\star\ge W/4\ge e^{-310}W/2\). Since \(N=1\), this is the required bound.

Finally, taking \(c_i=b_i\) and \(T=R\) leaves the one-resource support and every original weight unchanged, so the stated constant also applies to the entire one-resource class. No step imposed rationality on a resource coefficient or integrality on an interval endpoint.
