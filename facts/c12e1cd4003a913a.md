## Statement

Let $\mu$ be uniform probability on $\Delta_3=\{\xi\in\mathbb R^4:\xi\ge0,\ \sum_j\xi_j=1\}$. Take
$$
c_1=(7,12,-28,-28),\qquad c_2=(12,7,-28,-28),\qquad c_3=(-19,-19,56,56).
$$
The proper quadrilateral sections $c_i\cdot\xi=0$ have the same section centroid $b=(56,56,19,19)/150$, and $\sum_i c_i=0$. Set $H_i=\{\xi:c_i\cdot\xi\ge0\}$, $P_i=\mu(H_i)$, and let $f_i$ be the density at zero of $\sum_j c_{ij}E_j$ for independent unit exponentials. Then
$$
(P_1,P_2,P_3)=\left(\frac4{25},\frac4{25},\frac{354368}{421875}\right),\quad
(f_1,f_2,f_3)=\left(\frac1{100},\frac1{100},\frac{2128}{421875}\right),\quad
T=\sum_iP_i/f_i=\frac{3772}{19}.
$$
The probability density $w=T^{-1}\sum_i \mathbf1_{H_i}/f_i$ relative to $\mu$ has the same centroid as $\mu$, but the measures $w\mu,\mu$ are incomparable in convex order. The integrals against $(w-1)\mu$ of $(\xi_0-\xi_1)^2$ and $(\xi_0+\xi_1-4/5)_+$ are respectively $-24871/106087500$ and $63/1178750$.

This law is realized by strictly positive column-stochastic rows $x_i=((P_i/f_i)\mathbf1-c_i)/T$ for $i=1,2,3$. For $F_x(t)=\Pr_\mu(x\cdot\xi\le t)$ and $\phi_3(x)=\inf_{t>0,\ F_x(t)>0} t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$, row $x_i$ has the unique proper minimizing threshold $P_i/(Tf_i)$ and $P_i^{1/3}\nabla\phi_3(x_i)=b$. Thus the weighted capacity sum $\sum_iP_i^{1/3}\phi_3(x_i)$ is stationary under column-sum-preserving row motions.

## Proof

The raw signs give a strict two-versus-two split for each cap. In the diagonal-coordinate formula of 1462fa9ce6ced54e the three diagonal intersections are $(12,7,3,3)/25$, $(7,12,3,3)/25$, and $(56,56,19,19)/150$. The latter is obtained by reversing the orientation for the split used in that formula. The section centroid formula gives $b$ in each case. If $L_i$ denotes the normalized section coarea, that fact's raw cap and coarea formulas, applied with the actual orientation, give the displayed $P_i$ and $L_i=3f_i$. In this last identity, if $G=\sum_jE_j$, then $E/G$ is uniform on $\Delta_3$ and independent of $G$. The density of $c_i\cdot(E/G)$ at zero is $L_i$ by coarea, is bounded, and is continuous at zero. Conditioning on $G$ multiplies it at zero by $\mathbb E G^{-1}=1/3$, since $G$ has gamma shape four. This also verifies the normalization for the actual raw scale of each $c_i$. Substitution gives $T$.

For the common first moment, the tangent field $\xi_j(e_j-\xi)$ has divergence $1-4\xi_j$ in the simplex affine hull. Its flux on the original simplex facets vanishes. On the cap's cutting plane its outward normal flux is proportional to $-c_{ij}\xi_j$, because $c_i\cdot\xi=0$ there. Dividing the divergence theorem by the simplex volume gives
$$
\int_{H_i}\xi_j\,d\mu=\frac{P_i+L_i c_{ij}b_j}{4}.
$$
The definition of $T$ gives total mass one for $w\mu$. Summing the displayed identity with weights $1/(Tf_i)$ gives each centroid coordinate $1/4$, by $L_i/f_i=3$ and $\sum_i c_i=0$.

For the quadratic, write $Q=(\xi_0-\xi_1)^2$. On a cap with positive vertices $e_i,e_j$ and negative vertices $e_k,e_\ell$, put $A,B,C,D$ for the edge intersections on $ik,i\ell,j\ell,jk$. The cap splits into the tetrahedra with vertices $(e_i,A,B,C)$, $(e_i,A,C,D)$ and $(e_i,e_j,C,D)$. For a positive index $i$ and a negative index $k$, the intersection is $(-c_k e_i+c_i e_k)/(c_i-c_k)$, with $c$ the raw normal of this cap. For each tetrahedron with barycentric vertices $v_a$, its normalized mass is $|\det(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)|$, and its conditional expectation of $Q$ is
$$
\frac{\sum_a \ell(v_a)^2+(\sum_a \ell(v_a))^2}{20},\qquad
\ell(v)=v_0-v_1.
$$
Indeed, the uniform barycentric coordinates on a tetrahedron have second moments $\mathbb E[\xi_a\xi_b]=(1+\mathbf1_{a=b})/20$ by elementary simplex integration. The three determinant sums for $\int_{H_i}Q\,d\mu$ are $37/1000,\ 37/1000,\ 742504448/11865234375$ in order. The weighted sum gives $10583879/106087500$ for $\int Qw\,d\mu$, while the same second moments give $\int Q\,d\mu=1/10$. This proves the negative difference.

Write $y=\xi_0+\xi_1$. Where $(y-4/5)_+>0$, the first two normal margins are at least $7y-28(1-y)>0$ and the third is $56-75y<0$. The weight there is $200/T=950/943$. The density of $y$ is $6y(1-y)$ on $[0,1]$, obtained by splitting the simplex coordinates into the two pairs. Thus $\int (y-4/5)_+\,d\mu=9/1250$, and multiplying by $7/943$ gives the positive difference. The two continuous convex tests therefore obstruct the two directions of convex order.

Finally put $t_i=P_i/f_i=(16,16,3164/19)_i$. The unscaled rows $Tx_i$ are $(9,4,44,44)$, $(4,9,44,44)$ and $(3525,3525,2100,2100)/19$. They are positive and nonconstant, and their column sums equal $T$ by raw balance. At $s_i=t_i/T$ the cap is exactly $H_i$, so $F_{x_i}(s_i)=P_i$ and $F'_{x_i}(s_i)=TL_i$. Thus $s_iF'_{x_i}(s_i)=3P_i$. Brunn–Minkowski for the nested simplex caps makes $F_{x_i}^{1/3}$ concave on the range of $x_i\cdot\xi$. Its tangent at $s_i$ has zero intercept and positive slope, so the full graph there lies below the tangent. It also lies below that line above the largest row entry because the graph is then constant. There is no positive cap below the smallest entry. This proves that the proper threshold minimizes the ratio. By d2ba4e3cbd6eb730 it is unique and $P_i^{1/3}\nabla\phi_3(x_i)=b$. The sum of weighted first variations is therefore $b\cdot\sum_i dx_i=0$ under the column constraints.
