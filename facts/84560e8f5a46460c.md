## Statement

Retain all notation and conclusions of accepted fact `39fdc9bb7abafc70`. In
particular,
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{cap}}
 =\frac12\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr A}
   \sum_{z:\beta_z^\alpha>0}\beta_z^\alpha
   \sum_i\pi_i^\alpha\delta_{(z,p_z(u_i))},
 \qquad
 4\gamma_d\nu_{\mathrm{cap}}\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C,
 \qquad
 |\nu_{\mathrm{cap}}|=\Omega_b.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(\alpha,z,i,k\) with \(\beta_z^\alpha>0\), put
\[
 t_{\alpha,z,i,k}
 :=\frac{\beta_z^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha\pi_k^\alpha}{4},
 \qquad
 r_{\alpha,z,i,k}
 :=\left(z,\frac{p_z(u_i)+p_z(u_k)}2\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Then the exact endpoint-incidence ledger is
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_k t_{\alpha,z,i,k}+\sum_k t_{\alpha,z,k,i}
 =\frac{\beta_z^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha}{2}.}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the midpoint measure
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{mid}}
 :=\sum_{\alpha,z,i,k}
 t_{\alpha,z,i,k}\delta_{r_{\alpha,z,i,k}}
\tag{4}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\mathrm{mid}}\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\nu_{\mathrm{cap}},
 \qquad
 4\gamma_d\nu_{\mathrm{mid}}\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C.}
\tag{5}
\]

More generally, for each such ordered pair let
\(\lambda_{\alpha,z,i,k}\) be an arbitrary Borel probability measure
supported on the closed segment
\[
 [(z,p_z(u_i)),(z,p_z(u_k))],
\]
and define
\[
 \Lambda
 :=\sum_{\alpha,z,i,k}
 t_{\alpha,z,i,k}\lambda_{\alpha,z,i,k}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then \(\Lambda\) is carried by the same mixed-integer body and
\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\nu_{\mathrm{cap}},
 \qquad
 4\gamma_d\Lambda\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C.}
\tag{7}
\]
Both clouds have the exact mass
\[
 \boxed{
 |\nu_{\mathrm{mid}}|=|\Lambda|
 =\frac{\Omega_b}{2}
 =\frac12|\nu_{\mathrm{cap}}|
 >\frac{97}{200}Q.}
\tag{8}
\]
Their labeled ordered segments carry the exact canonical energy
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\alpha,z,i,k}t_{\alpha,z,i,k}
 \left\langle
 u_i-u_k,p_z(u_i)-p_z(u_k)
 \right\rangle
 =\sum_\alpha\Psi_\alpha.}
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, replacing the canonical prefactor \(1/4\) by \(1/2\) in the
same \(\beta_z^\alpha\)-sum defines \(2\sum_\alpha\Psi_\alpha\). Equation
(9), not that doubled normalization, is the canonical identity.

The conclusions include diagonal ordered pairs and geometrically coincident
cap points. They do not assert that the canonical normal differences
\(u_i-u_k\) align with the normal of an arbitrary affine separator, and hence
do not yield an arbitrary-separator selector or terminal absorption.

## Proof

Fix \(\alpha,z,i\). Since \(\sum_k\pi_k^\alpha=1\), direct summation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_k t_{\alpha,z,i,k}+\sum_k t_{\alpha,z,k,i}
 &=\frac{\beta_z^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha}{4}
   \sum_k\pi_k^\alpha
   +\frac{\beta_z^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha}{4}
   \sum_k\pi_k^\alpha\\
 &=\frac{\beta_z^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is exactly the coefficient of the corresponding labeled atom in
\(\nu_{\mathrm{cap}}\), proving (3), including zero \(\pi_i^\alpha\).

Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace. If both endpoints of one ordered
pair lie outside \(H\), they lie in its convex open complementary halfspace,
so their entire closed segment is outside \(H\). If at least one endpoint lies
in \(H\), a probability measure assigns at most mass one to \(H\). Thus,
including the case of an endpoint on the boundary,
\[
 \lambda_{\alpha,z,i,k}(H)
 \leq
 \mathbf1_H(z,p_z(u_i))+\mathbf1_H(z,p_z(u_k)).
\tag{10}
\]
Multiplying by the weights, summing, and using (3) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Lambda(H)
 &\leq\sum_{\alpha,z,i}
 \left(\sum_k t_{\alpha,z,i,k}
       +\sum_k t_{\alpha,z,k,i}\right)
 \mathbf1_H(z,p_z(u_i))\\
 &=\frac12\sum_{\alpha,z}\beta_z^\alpha
   \sum_i\pi_i^\alpha\mathbf1_H(z,p_z(u_i))
 =\nu_{\mathrm{cap}}(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Choosing every probability measure to be the Dirac mass at the corresponding
midpoint proves the first assertion in (5). The remaining domination
assertions follow from (1). Each segment lies in the convex fiber \(C_z\), so
both clouds are carried by the mixed-integer body.

Because every \(\lambda_{\alpha,z,i,k}\) is a probability measure, equation
(29) of accepted fact `39fdc9bb7abafc70` gives
\[
 |\Lambda|=|\nu_{\mathrm{mid}}|
 =\frac14\sum_{\alpha,z}\beta_z^\alpha
 =\frac14\sum_\alpha 2\Omega_\alpha
 =\frac{\Omega_b}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Together with \(\Omega_b>97Q/100\), this proves (8).

For every ordered pair, support at the two cap points gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\langle u_i-u_k,p_z(u_i)-p_z(u_k)\right\rangle\\
 &\quad=
 \left\langle u_i,p_z(u_i)-p_z(u_k)\right\rangle
 +\left\langle u_k,p_z(u_k)-p_z(u_i)\right\rangle
 \geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The second line of equation (31) in accepted fact `39fdc9bb7abafc70` is
precisely
\[
 \Psi_\alpha
 =\frac14\sum_{z:\beta_z^\alpha>0}\beta_z^\alpha
   \sum_{i,k}\pi_i^\alpha\pi_k^\alpha
   \left\langle u_i-u_k,p_z(u_i)-p_z(u_k)\right\rangle.
\tag{14}
\]
Substitution of (2) into (14), followed by summation over \(\alpha\), proves
(9). Thus a prefactor \(1/2\) in place of the accepted prefactor \(1/4\)
makes the resulting auxiliary energy twice the canonical one; it does not
produce an additional loss in the segment packing.

If \(i=k\), the segment is a singleton, its energy in (13) is zero, and its
weight is charged twice in the endpoint-incidence calculation, exactly as in
(10). If distinct indices have coincident cap points, their segment is again a
singleton and their energy is zero. Coincident atoms from any labels simply
aggregate in (11). Hence diagonal pairs, coincident points, zero weights, and
closed-halfspace boundary points require no separate capacity or mass charge.
Finally, (13) uses the canonical pair-dependent normal difference
\(u_i-u_k\). Nothing above compares it with the normal of an arbitrary affine
separator, which proves the stated limitation.
