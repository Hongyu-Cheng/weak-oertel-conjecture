## Statement

There do not exist constants \(M<\infty\) and \(\rho>0\) such that the following property holds for every \(n,d,R,\psi,K,B\) in the assigned homothetic symmetric-fiber class: with \(V_i(x)=\psi(|x_i|)\), \(V(x)=(V_1(x),\ldots,V_n(x))^{\mathsf T}\), \(a(x)=R-\sum_iV_i(x)\), \(D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a(z)>0\}\), \(a_z=a(z)\), \(p_z=a_z^d/\sum_{u\in D_+}a_u^d\), \(\mu=\mathbb E_pV(Z)\), and \(U\) uniform on \(K\), one has, for every nonzero \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\mathbb P_p\!\left(
 \frac{|\beta\cdot(B\mu-BV(Z))|}
 {a_Z\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\beta\cdot U)}}\le M
\right)\ge\rho.
\]

Here \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(R>0\), \(K\) is a centrally symmetric convex body about \(0_d\) with nonempty interior, each column of \(B\) belongs to \(K\), and \(\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) is finite, convex and nondecreasing, with \(\psi(0)=0\) and \(\psi(1)>0\). The actual body is
\(C=\{(x,y):\sum_iV_i(x)\le R,\ y\in BV(x)+a(x)K\}\).
The probabilities above use its actual fiber-volume weights, not a root-weight law.

More precisely, for each integer \(n\ge1\), set \(d=4^n\) and \(R=d\). Define \(\psi(t)=t\) for \(0\le t\le1\) and \(\psi(t)=1+2d(t-1)\) for \(t\ge1\). Let \(K=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{j=1}^d|u_j|\le1\}\), and let every column \(b_i\) of \(B\) equal the first coordinate unit vector \(e_1\). Let \(L=\sum_iV_i(Z)\), let \(\ell=\mathbb E_pL=\sum_i\mu_i\), and choose \(\beta=e_1\). Then \(D_+=\{-1,0,1\}^n\), the proposed point is \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,\ell e_1)\) in the origin fiber, and \(\operatorname{Var}(\beta\cdot U)=2/[(d+1)(d+2)]\).

Put \(r=2/(e+2)\) and \(\sigma_d=\sqrt{2/[(d+1)(d+2)]}\). The exact original normalized displacement satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_p\!\left[\frac{(L-\ell)^2}{a_Z^2\sigma_d^2}\right]
\ge\frac{e^{-1/2}}2\,nr(1-r).
\]
Moreover, for every real \(M\ge0\),
\[
\mathbb P_p\!\left(\frac{|L-\ell|}{a_Z\sigma_d}\le M\right)
\le
\frac{e^{1/2}\bigl(\lfloor2\sqrt2\,M\rfloor+1\bigr)\sqrt\pi}
 {2\sqrt{2nr(1-r)}}.
\]
Consequently this probability tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\) for each fixed \(M\).

The notation \(D_+,a_z,p_z,V(Z),L,\mathcal Z_k,q^{(k)}\) agrees with accepted fact 284ed29f0cbbaaa9, with
\(\mathcal Z_k=\sum_{z\in D_+}a_z^k\) and \(q_z^{(k)}=a_z^k/\mathcal Z_k\).
Its displayed cube fiber-volume factor \(2^d\) is replaced here by
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)=2^d/d!\); that factor is independent of \(z\) and cancels, so the original \(p\) remains exactly \(q^{(d)}\). For this family, and without changing the center \(\ell\), the precise reweighting is
\[
\mathbb E_p\!\left[\frac{(L-\ell)^2}{a_Z^2}\right]
=
\frac{\mathcal Z_{d-2}}{\mathcal Z_d}
\left(
 \operatorname{Var}_{q^{(d-2)}}(L)
 +\bigl(\mathbb E_{q^{(d-2)}}L-\ell\bigr)^2
\right).
\]
Thus the growing original normalized second moment includes the mean-shift term under the reweighted law.

## Proof

We first check all geometric and probability hypotheses. The specified \(\psi\) is continuous, finite, nonnegative, nondecreasing and convex: its slopes are \(1\) and \(2d\), in that order. It has \(\psi(0)=0\) and \(\psi(1)=1\). The set \(K\) is compact and convex, is centrally symmetric about \(0_d\), and has nonempty interior. Every \(b_i=e_1\) lies in \(K\).

Write \(L(x)=\sum_i\psi(|x_i|)\), so \(L(Z)=L\). The displayed actual body can be written as \(L(x)\le d\) together with the finitely many inequalities
\(\varepsilon\cdot y+(1-\varepsilon_1)L(x)\le d\) for
\(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^d\).
Indeed, the maximum over these \(\varepsilon\) of
\(\varepsilon\cdot(y-L(x)e_1)\) is
\(\sum_j|y_j-L(x)(e_1)_j|\).
Each coefficient \(1-\varepsilon_1\) is either \(0\) or \(2\), so each left side is convex in \((x,y)\). The base constraint is also convex. Hence \(C\) is convex and closed. Coercivity of \(\psi\) bounds \(x\) in the base. For a point of \(C\), write \(y=L(x)e_1+(d-L(x))u\) with \(u\in K\). Since \(0\le L(x)\le d\), one has
\(\sum_j|y_j|\le L(x)+(d-L(x))=d\).
This bounds \(y\), proving compactness. At \((0_n,0_d)\), all displayed inequalities and the base constraint are strict. Their continuity gives an ambient open neighborhood in \(C\), so \(C\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

If any integer coordinate has absolute value at least \(2\), its energy is at least \(\psi(2)=1+2d>d\), so that integer vector is outside \(D_+\). Conversely, for \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\), each \(V_i(z)\) is either \(0\) or \(1\), and \(L(z)\le n<d\). Thus \(D_+\) is exactly \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Its actual fiber is \(L(z)e_1+(d-L(z))K\) and its \(d\)-volume is
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)(d-L(z))^d\).
The crosspolytope \(K\) consists of \(2^d\) congruent orthant simplices of volume \(1/d!\), hence \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)=2^d/d!\).
Therefore the actual-volume law is exactly
\(p_z=(d-L(z))^d/\mathcal Z_d\), where
\(\mathcal Z_k=\sum_{z\in D_+}(d-L(z))^k\).
This is precisely the normalization \(p=q^{(d)}\) of accepted fact 284ed29f0cbbaaa9 after canceling the common fiber-volume factor stated above.

The law is invariant under every integer-coordinate sign change. Therefore \(\mathbb E_pZ=0_n\). Conditional on \(Z=z\), the actual uniform fiber law is \(Y=L(z)e_1+a_zU\), with \(U\) independently uniform on \(K\) and \(\mathbb EU=0_d\). Hence the original full mean is \((0_n,\ell e_1)\). Since \(0\le\ell\le n<d\), the vector \(\ell e_1\) belongs to \(dK\), the origin fiber, and this is a legal mixed-integer point fixed before the normal is chosen. The normalized directional offset at that mean is \((\ell-L)/(a_Z\sigma_d)\), as asserted.

For \(t\in[-1,1]\), the cross section of \(K\) at \(u_1=t\) is a \((d-1)\)-dimensional crosspolytope of radius \(1-|t|\). Dividing its volume by \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) gives the density
\(f_d(t)=(d/2)(1-|t|)^{d-1}\).
Symmetry gives \(\mathbb EU_1=0\). Two integrations by parts give
\(d\int_0^1t^2(1-t)^{d-1}\,dt=2/[(d+1)(d+2)]\).
This is exactly \(\operatorname{Var}(U_1)\), establishing \(\sigma_d\) with the original uniform-noise normalization.

There are \(\binom nj2^j\) integer vectors with \(L=j\). Let \(\nu\) be the binomial law with \(n\) trials and success probability \(r=2/(e+2)\). Its probabilities are
\(\nu_j=\binom nj(2/e)^j/(1+2/e)^n\) for \(j=0,\ldots,n\).
Define \(h_j=e^j(1-j/d)^d\). Factoring \(d^d\) out of every actual weight gives the exact identity
\(\mathbb P_p(L=j)=\nu_jh_j/\sum_{k=0}^n\nu_kh_k\).
This comparison does not replace \(p\) in the theorem; it estimates the explicitly displayed actual \(p\).

For \(0\le x<1\), the power series for \(-\log(1-x)\) gives
\(0\le-\log(1-x)-x=\sum_{k\ge2}x^k/k\le x^2/[2(1-x)]\).
With \(x=j/d\) this yields
\(e^{-n^2/[2(d-n)]}\le h_j\le1\).
For \(d=4^n\), the inequality \(d\ge n^2+n\) follows by induction: it holds at \(n=1\), and
\(4(n^2+n)\ge(n+1)^2+(n+1)\) for every \(n\ge1\).
Consequently \(n^2/[2(d-n)]\le1/2\). The denominator
\(\sum_k\nu_kh_k\) is also in \([e^{-1/2},1]\). We obtain, for every \(j\),
\(e^{-1/2}\nu_j\le\mathbb P_p(L=j)\le e^{1/2}\nu_j\).

For any real \(m\), this lower comparison implies
\(\mathbb E_p(L-m)^2\ge e^{-1/2}\mathbb E_\nu(L-m)^2\).
Take \(m=\ell\). The right side is at least
\(e^{-1/2}\operatorname{Var}_\nu(L)=e^{-1/2}nr(1-r)\),
because the variance is the minimum of the mean-square distance to a constant. Also \(a_Z=d-L\le d\). It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\!\left[\frac{(L-\ell)^2}{a_Z^2\sigma_d^2}\right]
&\ge\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2d^2}\operatorname{Var}_p(L)\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-1/2}}2\,nr(1-r).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the claimed divergent second moment directly in the original measure.

For completeness, we also prove the original positive-mass obstruction, which does not follow from divergent second moments alone. Fourier inversion for the binomial polynomial gives, for each integer \(j\),
\[
\nu_j=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 (1-r+re^{it})^ne^{-ijt}\,dt.
\]
This identity follows by expanding the polynomial and integrating its finitely many exponential monomials. The squared modulus of its base is
\(1-4r(1-r)\sin^2(t/2)\).
Thus its modulus to the \(n\)-th power is at most
\(e^{-2nr(1-r)\sin^2(t/2)}\), using \(1-s\le e^{-s}\).
Concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/2]\) gives
\(\sin(|t|/2)\ge|t|/\pi\) for \(|t|\le\pi\).
Taking absolute values in the inversion formula, extending the resulting nonnegative integral to the real line, and evaluating the Gaussian integral proves
\[
\max_j\nu_j\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-2nr(1-r)t^2/\pi^2}\,dt
=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt{2nr(1-r)}}.
\]
The Gaussian evaluation follows by squaring the integral and changing to polar coordinates, so no probabilistic approximation or asymptotic theorem is used.

If \(|L-\ell|/(a_Z\sigma_d)\le M\), then
\(|L-\ell|\le Ma_Z\sigma_d\le Md\sigma_d\le\sqrt2\,M\).
An interval of length \(2\sqrt2\,M\) contains at most
\(\lfloor2\sqrt2\,M\rfloor+1\) integers.
The upper comparison of \(p\) with \(\nu\) and the preceding maximum-atom bound therefore give exactly the stated probability bound. For any fixed \(M<\infty\) and \(\rho>0\), its right side is less than \(\rho\) for all sufficiently large integers \(n\). This contradicts the proposed absolute constants. The same maximum-atom argument would apply with any other real scalar center in place of \(\ell\).

Finally, the reweighting is an exact finite-sum identity. Cancelling \(a_z^2\) from the original \(a_z^d\) weight gives
\[
\mathbb E_p\!\left[\frac{(L-\ell)^2}{a_Z^2}\right]
=\frac{\mathcal Z_{d-2}}{\mathcal Z_d}
 \mathbb E_{q^{(d-2)}}(L-\ell)^2.
\]
Expanding around the actual \(q^{(d-2)}\) mean gives the variance plus squared mean difference in the statement; the cross term vanishes under that law. No assertion that \(\ell\) equals the \(q^{(d-2)}\) mean is made. Also
\(1/d^2\le\mathcal Z_{d-2}/\mathcal Z_d\le1/(d-n)^2\),
since this ratio is \(\mathbb E_p(1/a_Z^2)\).
Here \(d-2\ge0\), so the accepted nonnegative-power covariance conclusion is applicable, but it cannot contradict the explicit original lower bound already proved. The original weights, center, conditional noise variance, and reweighted mean are all retained with their exact normalizations. This proves the counterexample to the bounded absolute directional displacement certificate.
