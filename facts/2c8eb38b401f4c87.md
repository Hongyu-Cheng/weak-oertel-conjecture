## Statement

Let $K$ be the actual depth-region projection in the unbounded-container example. For $|s|<1$ put
$$
L_s=\{z:(1+s)|z_1-1/2|+(1-s)|z_2-1/2|\le1\}.
$$
Each $L_s$ is interior-lattice-free and contains $K$ strictly in its interior. The four facet centroids of $L_0$ are its binary lattice contacts and all facet lengths equal $\sqrt2$, so $L_0$ satisfies the facet-centroid and lattice-contact equations for free lattice-free stationarity. It is a strict local volume maximum with normals fixed. Nevertheless $|L_s|=2/(1-s^2)$, with first derivative zero and second derivative $4$ at zero.

## Proof

At any integer point both absolute coordinate deviations are at least $1/2$. The sum in the inequality is therefore at least one. On the unit square both deviations are at most $1/2$ and equality occurs only at its four corners. The depth-region projection is contained in $P_0$ from the unbounded-container example, which omits those corners. Thus $K\subset\operatorname{int}L_s$.

The axis intercepts relative to the center $(1/2,1/2)$ are $\pm(1/(1+s),0)$ and $\pm(0,1/(1-s))$. The area follows from the perpendicular diagonals. At $s=0$ the four facet midpoints are exactly the binary corners and the facets have length $\sqrt2$. Assigning that length to each sole relative-interior lattice point gives the centroid conditions. The outward unit normal at the binary contact $p$ is $\sqrt2(p-(1/2,1/2))$, so summing these weights gives zero vector and matrix $2I_2$, and its trace is four.

Each contact strictly satisfies the other three inequalities. Thus for all sufficiently small fixed-normal offset perturbations, any positive offset change brings its contact into the interior, regardless of the other offsets. The feasible perturbations have all offsets nonincreasing. A nonzero such change cuts positive area because the original facets are irredundant. This proves strict fixed-normal local maximality. The exact area formula gives the positive second derivative under the free-normal family.
