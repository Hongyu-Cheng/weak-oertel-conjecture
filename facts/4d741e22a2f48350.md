## Statement

For every even integer \(k\ge4\), let \(m=k^3\), \(n=km\),
\[
T=\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^2:\|\mathbf y\|_2\le1\},\qquad
\mathbf u_j=(\cos(2\pi j/k),\sin(2\pi j/k)),\qquad
\delta=\frac1{10k^2},
\]
and
\[
K_j=\{\mathbf y\in T:\mathbf u_j\cdot\mathbf y\ge1-\delta\}
\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
For \(1\le j\le k\) and \(1\le s\le m\), define
\[
\ell_{j,s}^-(\mathbf y)=0,
\qquad
\ell_{j,s}^+(\mathbf y)
=\min\{1,(1+\mathbf u_j\cdot\mathbf y)/(2-\delta)\},
\]
and define the one-sided concave-roof body with unit core
\[
C=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^n\times T:
0\le x_{j,s}\le1+\ell_{j,s}^+(\mathbf y)
\text{ for every }j,s\right\}.
\]
Its actual mixed set is
\[
S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2).
\]
For \(\mathbf y\in T\), write
\(C_{\mathbf y}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in C\}\),
and define the set of anchors of unit cells contained in this fiber by
\[
A_{\mathbf y}
=\prod_{j=1}^k\prod_{s=1}^m
\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\ell_{j,s}^+(\mathbf y)\rfloor\}
\subseteq\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
B(\mathbf y)=|A_{\mathbf y}|.
\]
Define the filled-cell section and its union by
\[
E_{\mathbf y}
=\bigcup_{\mathbf a\in A_{\mathbf y}}(\mathbf a+[0,1]^n)
=\prod_{j=1}^k\prod_{s=1}^m
[0,1+\mathbf1_{K_j}(\mathbf y)],
\]
where \(\mathbf1_{K_j}\) is the indicator of \(K_j\), and
\[
E=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^n\times T:
\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf y}\}.
\]
Thus
\[
M=\operatorname{vol}_{n+2}(E)=\int_TB(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y.
\]
For \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{n+2}\), set
\[
D_E(\mathbf q)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+2}\text{ closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf q\in H}}
\operatorname{vol}_{n+2}(E\cap H),
\]
and set
\[
\Delta_B=
\max_{\mathbf y\in T}
\inf_{\substack{L\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf y\in L}}
\int_{T\cap L}B(\mathbf y')\,d\mathbf y'.
\]
For \(\mathbf p\in S\), let
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+2}\text{ closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf p\in H}}
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H).
\]
Then the continuous base dimension is \(2\), every added roof \(\ell_{j,s}^+\) is bounded by \(1\), and, as \(k\to\infty\) through even integers,
\[
\Delta_B=M/2,\qquad
\sup_{\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{n+2}}D_E(\mathbf q)/\Delta_B\to0,
\qquad
\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)/\Delta_B\to\infty.
\]

## Proof

Fix an even \(k\ge4\), and use the objects in the statement. The caps \(K_j\) are pairwise disjoint because
\(\delta<1-\cos(\pi/k)\). They occur in opposite pairs and have common area
\[
v=\int_{1-\delta}^12\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt
\ge\tfrac43\delta^{3/2}.
\]
The functions \(\ell_{j,s}^+\) are nonnegative continuous concave roofs, and
\[
\lfloor\ell_{j,s}^+(\mathbf y)\rfloor
=\mathbf1_{K_j}(\mathbf y).
\]
Consequently, the anchor count is
\[
B=2^m\text{ on }\bigcup_jK_j,
\qquad
B=1\text{ elsewhere},
\]
and hence
\[
M=\pi+(2^m-1)kv.
\]
The measure with density \(B\) on \(T\) is centrally symmetric and absolutely continuous. Its halfspace depth at the origin is \(M/2\), while the depth at any point is at most \(M/2\), because a hyperplane through that point has two complementary closed halfspaces and its boundary has measure zero. Therefore
\[
\Delta_B=M/2.
\]

Consider normalized Lebesgue measure on each filled-cell section. Conditional on a cap \(K_i\), the \(i\)-th coordinate group is uniform on \([0,2]^m\), while every other group is uniform on \([0,1]^m\). For any \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{n+2}\), let \(q_{j,s}\) denote its first \(n\) coordinates and set
\[
s_j(\mathbf q)=m^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^m q_{j,s}-1/2.
\]
If some \(s_j(\mathbf q)\ge1/(4k)\), use the closed upper halfspace through \(\mathbf q\) determined by the average of the \(j\)-th coordinate group. Since the caps are disjoint, \(kv\le\pi\), and hence the mass from \(K_j\) is \(2^mv\le M/k\). Outside \(K_j\), that group is uniform on \([0,1]^m\). Hoeffding's bound gives
\[
D_E(\mathbf q)
\le M(1/k+e^{-m/(8k^2)}).
\]
If all \(s_j(\mathbf q)<1/(4k)\), then \(\sum_js_j(\mathbf q)<1/4\). Use the closed lower halfspace through \(\mathbf q\) determined by the sum of all \(k\) group averages. On every cap that sum has mean \(k/2+1/2\), so the threshold lies at least \(1/4\) below the mean. The sum of the squared ranges of its independent summands is
\[
\frac{4}{m}+\frac{k-1}{m}=\frac{k+3}{m}.
\]
The part of \(E\) outside all caps has mass at most \(\pi\), and another application of Hoeffding's bound gives
\[
D_E(\mathbf q)
\le\pi+Me^{-m/(8(k+3))}.
\]
Here the form of Hoeffding's bound used in both cases follows from
\[
\log\mathbb E e^{t(X-\mathbb EX)}
\le t^2(b-a)^2/8
\quad\text{for }X\in[a,b]:
\]
the second derivative of the left-hand side is a tilted variance bounded by \((b-a)^2/4\); integrating twice and optimizing proves the stated tails.

Since \(m=k^3\), \(k+3\le k^2\), and \(v\ge(4/3)\delta^{3/2}\), one has \(\pi/M=o(k^{-1})\). The two cases therefore give
\[
\sup_{\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^{n+2}}
\frac{D_E(\mathbf q)}{\Delta_B}
\le\frac2k+o(k^{-1}).
\]

It remains to compare with the actual mixed depth. Let \(\mathbf1\in\mathbb Z^n\) be the all-ones vector. Since \(0<\ell_{j,s}^+(0)<1\), the point \((\mathbf1,0)\) belongs to \(S\). For \(\mathbf y\in T\), define the same-fiber reflected subset
\[
R_{\mathbf y}
=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:
(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in S
\text{ and }(2\mathbf1-\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in S\}.
\]
Each \(R_{\mathbf y}\) is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf1\), and its cardinality is
\[
r(\mathbf y):=|R_{\mathbf y}|
=3^m\text{ on }\bigcup_jK_j,
\qquad
r(\mathbf y)=1\text{ elsewhere}.
\]
The density \(r\) is centrally symmetric on \(T\). Let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing \((\mathbf1,0)\). Shrinking \(H\) toward that point, it is enough to treat a halfspace whose boundary passes through \((\mathbf1,0)\), written as
\[
H=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf1)+\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\}.
\]
For each \(\mathbf y\) with \(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\), pairing \(\mathbf z\in R_{\mathbf y}\) with \(2\mathbf1-\mathbf z\) shows that at least half of \(R_{\mathbf y}\) lies in \(H\). Central symmetry of \(r\) gives at least half of its total mass to the base halfspace \(\{\mathbf y:\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\}\) (and gives all of it when \(\mathbf b=0\)). Since the reflected subset is contained in \(S\),
\[
h_S((\mathbf1,0))
\ge\frac14\int_T r(\mathbf y)\,d\mathbf y
=\tfrac14[\pi+(3^m-1)kv].
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)}{\Delta_B}
\ge
\frac{\pi+(3^m-1)kv}
{2[\pi+(2^m-1)kv]}
\sim\tfrac12(3/2)^m\to\infty.
\]
