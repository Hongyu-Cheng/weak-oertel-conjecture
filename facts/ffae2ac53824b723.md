## Statement

For each integer \(n\ge5\), put \(H=n\). There is a unique \(q_H\in(0,1)\) such that the probability law
\[
p_i^{(H)}=\frac{q_H^{i+1}}{Z_H}\quad(-1\le i\le H),
\qquad Z_H=\sum_{i=-1}^{H}q_H^{i+1},
\qquad \sum_{i=-1}^{H}i p_i^{(H)}=\frac12
\]
has the displayed mean. In fact \(3/5<q_H<2/3\). Define the complete lattice support \(D=\{-1,0,\ldots,H\}^n\), the table \(w_{\mathbf I}=q_H^{\sum_{t=1}^n(I_t+1)}\) on \(D\), and zero weights at every other lattice point. Put \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}=Z_H^n\), \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W=\prod_t p_{I_t}^{(H)}\), and \(T=W/2^n\). These tables have finite-valued globally log-concave extensions agreeing with all their lattice values, including zeros.

Use the arrays, row masses, and second-moment objective of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\ (\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\ (\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},
\qquad V(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
To avoid confusing the truncation height \(H\) with that predecessor's entropy functional \(H(r)\), denote the latter here by \(\mathsf H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\log(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\), with \(0\log0=0\). Let \(r^\star\) be its unique maximizer among the minimizers of \(V\).

There are unique real parameters \(A_H,B_H\), with \(B_H>0\), for which
\[
\theta_i=\frac1{1+\exp(A_H+B_H i)},\qquad
\sum_{i=-1}^{H}p_i^{(H)}\theta_i=\frac12,\qquad
\sum_{i=-1}^{H}i p_i^{(H)}\theta_i=0 .
\tag{1}
\]
They satisfy \(1/9\le e^{A_H}\le e^{B_H}\le81\). The exact entropy-selected array is
\[
r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle p_{\mathbf I}\prod_{t=1}^{n}
\theta_{I_t}^{\,1-J_t}(1-\theta_{I_t})^{J_t},
&\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n,\ \mathbf I\in D,\\
0,&\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{0,1\}^n,\ \mathbf I\in D .
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The minimum of \(V\) is \(n/2\). Every minimizing array has its positive row targets in \(\{0,1\}^n\), and every such target occurs in (2). For (2), \(q^\star_{\mathbf J}=2^{-n}\) at every Boolean target, and the complete positive source support of each such row is \(D\).

Define the two scalar normalized row laws by \(\nu_0(i)=2p_i^{(H)}\theta_i\) and \(\nu_1(i)=2p_i^{(H)}(1-\theta_i)\) on \(\{-1,\ldots,H\}\). Their means are \(0\) and \(1\), respectively. The normalized row at \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\) is exactly \(\nu_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=\prod_t\nu_{J_t}(I_t)\), so \(Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=T\nu_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)\). Both scalar laws have full support and satisfy the uniform bounds
\[
\nu_b(i)\ge\frac13\,135^{-(i+1)}
\quad(b\in\{0,1\},\ 0\le i\le H),\qquad
\nu_b(i)\le(2/3)^{i+1}
\quad(b\in\{0,1\},\ -1\le i\le H).
\tag{3}
\]

Use exactly the full occurrence charges and original logarithmic costs in accepted fact 6ca311257acdfbcb. Thus a positive barycentric component at \(\mathbf J\) consists of a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) and coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}>0\) satisfying \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). Its cost is \(G=\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\), where \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\) and \(w_{\max}=\max_Dw_{\mathbf I}\). Let \(P_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)\) be the supremum of \(\sum_k x_k\) over finite collections of such components of cost at most \(L\), with \(x_k\ge0\) and
\[
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D).
\tag{4}
\]
No occurrence is discounted by a barycentric coefficient. The fractional score is \(\mathcal R_{\log}(r^\star;w)=\sup_{\mathbf J:q^\star_{\mathbf J}>0,\ L\ge0}P_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)/(1+L)\).

For the integral counterpart, let \(m_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)\) be the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) over sets \(F\subseteq D\) hitting every support of a component at \(\mathbf J\) with cost at most \(L\). Put \(\mathcal R_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(r^\star;w)=\sup_{\mathbf J:q^\star_{\mathbf J}>0,\ L\ge0}m_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)/(1+L)\). These are the hitting conventions of accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893, with source weights replaced by the fixed assigned capacities \(Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) and with the original logarithmic costs retained. In this zero-cost setting, using all barycentric supports or only affinely independent barycentric supports gives the same hitting mass.

Set
\[
k_n=\left\lfloor\frac{\log n}{2\log135}\right\rfloor,\qquad
\varepsilon_n=\frac2{k_n+1}+\exp\!\left(-\frac{\sqrt n}{405}\right),\qquad
c=\frac1{4\cdot2206\cdot25^2}>0 .
\tag{5}
\]
All original barycentric costs are zero. For every \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\) and every \(L\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)
&=P_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,0)\le\varepsilon_nT,\\
m_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,L)
&=m_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,0)
=\min_{\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0}}
Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\ge cT .
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The minimum in the second line is over \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\), and includes every equality atom in its closed halfspace. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf J\) has the same lower bound \(cT\) for this row measure. Consequently
\[
\frac{\mathcal R_{\log}(r^\star;w)}T
\le\varepsilon_n\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\mathcal R_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(r^\star;w)\ge cT .
\tag{7}
\]
The fractional upper bound is uniform over all positive target rows, all cutoffs, and all component arities. The integral score uses cutoff zero at every row. These claims concern the specified globally extendable tables and assigned row capacities; they do not assert any failure of actual mixed-integer depth.

## Proof

We first determine the scalar geometric law. For \(q\in(0,1)\), its mean on \(\{-1,\ldots,H\}\) is
\[
\mu_H(q)=\frac q{1-q}-1-
\frac{(H+2)q^{H+2}}{1-q^{H+2}} .
\tag{8}
\]
This follows by differentiating the finite geometric sum. Differentiating the normalized finite sum also gives \(\mu_H'(q)=\operatorname{Var}_q(i)/q>0\), because its support contains distinct sites with positive probabilities. At \(q=3/5\), (8) is strictly less than \(1/2\). At fixed \(q\), increasing \(H\) strictly increases the mean: the newly added site exceeds every old site and has positive weight. At \(q=2/3\) and \(H=5\), (8) is \(1-7(2/3)^7/(1-(2/3)^7)>1/2\), since \(15(2/3)^7=1920/2187<1\). Continuity and strict monotonicity prove the unique root and \(3/5<q_H<2/3\) for every \(H\ge5\).

Temporarily suppress the superscript on \(p_i^{(H)}\). The scalar normalization gives
\[
\frac13<p_{-1}<\frac12,\qquad
p_{-1}+p_0>\frac59 .
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, \(p_{-1}=(1-q_H)/(1-q_H^{H+2})>1-q_H>1/3\). Also \(Z_H>\sum_{k=0}^{3}(3/5)^k=272/125>2\), proving the upper bound. Finally \(p_{-1}+p_0=(1+q_H)p_{-1}>1-q_H^2>5/9\).

For each real \(B\), the sum \(\sum_i p_i/(1+e^{A+Bi})\) is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(A\), with limits one and zero at its two infinite endpoints. There is therefore a unique \(A=A(B)\) making this sum \(1/2\). Write \(\theta_i(B)=(1+e^{A(B)+Bi})^{-1}\) and \(M(B)=\sum_i i p_i\theta_i(B)\). The finite sums are smooth and their \(A\)-derivative is strictly negative, so implicit differentiation is valid. With \(\tau_i=p_i\theta_i(B)(1-\theta_i(B))>0\), it gives
\[
A'(B)=-\frac{\sum_i i\tau_i}{\sum_i\tau_i},\qquad
M'(B)=-\left(\sum_i i^2\tau_i-
\frac{(\sum_i i\tau_i)^2}{\sum_i\tau_i}\right)<0 .
\tag{10}
\]
The final strict inequality is the positive weighted variance of the distinct scalar sites. At \(B=0\), \(A(0)=0\), so \(M(0)=1/4\). In particular \(M(B)>0\) for \(B\le0\).

For completeness, the large-\(B\) limit is an exact finite-support argument. Along any sequence \(B\to\infty\), a subsequence of the share vectors converges to some \((\ell_i)_{i=-1}^{H}\in[0,1]^{H+2}\). If \(i<j\) and \(\ell_i<1\), the odds identity
\[
\frac{\theta_j(B)}{1-\theta_j(B)}
=e^{-B(j-i)}\frac{\theta_i(B)}{1-\theta_i(B)}
\]
forces \(\ell_j=0\). The mass identity and \(p_{-1}<1/2\) then force \(\ell_{-1}=1\): otherwise every higher limit is zero and the total mass is below \(1/2\). The inequality \(p_{-1}+p_0>1/2\) forces \(\ell_0<1\), and hence all \(\ell_i\) with \(i\ge1\) are zero. The remaining mass determines \(\ell_0=(1/2-p_{-1})/p_0\in(0,1)\). Every subsequential limit is the same, so \(M(B)\to-p_{-1}<0\). Together with (10), this proves existence and uniqueness of \(B_H>0\), and then of \(A_H=A(B_H)\), in (1).

Since \(B_H>0\), the shares decrease with \(i\). Their average is \(1/2\), so \(\theta_{-1}\ge1/2\). Also \(1/2=\sum_i p_i\theta_i\ge(p_{-1}+p_0)\theta_0\), which gives \(\theta_0\le9/10\) by (9). Thus \(e^{A_H-B_H}\le1\) and \(e^{-A_H}\le9\). The zero moment in (1) now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac16
&\le p_{-1}\theta_{-1}
=\sum_{i=1}^{H}i p_i\theta_i\\
&\le9e^{-B_H}\sum_{i=1}^{H}i p_i
=9e^{-B_H}\left(\frac12+p_{-1}\right)
\le\frac{27}{2}e^{-B_H}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(\theta_i\le e^{-A_H-B_H i}\le9e^{-B_H i}\), \(i\ge1\), and \(e^{-B_H i}\le e^{-B_H}\). Consequently \(e^{B_H}\le81\). The two earlier share inequalities give \(1/9\le e^{A_H}\le e^{B_H}\), as asserted.

For \(i\ge0\), these parameter bounds imply
\[
\theta_i\ge\frac1{1+81^{i+1}}\ge\frac12\,81^{-(i+1)},\qquad
1-\theta_i\ge1-\theta_0\ge\frac1{10}
\ge\frac12\,81^{-(i+1)} .
\tag{12}
\]
By (9) and \(q_H>3/5\), \(p_i\ge(1/3)(3/5)^{i+1}\). Multiplying by twice the appropriate share in (12) proves the lower bound (3), since \(81\cdot5/3=135\). For all \(-1\le i\le H\), \(2p_i=2p_{-1}q_H^{i+1}\le(2/3)^{i+1}\) by (9), so both normalized row probabilities satisfy the upper bound (3). Their strict positivity follows from the finite logistic parameters. The two identities in (1), together with the original mean \(1/2\), show that \(\nu_0,\nu_1\) have total mass one and respective means zero and one.

The box \([-1,H]^n\) has complete lattice intersection \(D\). The function equal to \(q_H^{\sum_t(x_t+1)}\) on this box and zero off it is finite-valued and globally log-concave: inside the convex support its logarithm is affine, and an endpoint outside the support makes the defining log-concavity lower bound zero. It agrees with \(w\) at every lattice point. Dividing by \(W\) gives the required extension of \(p\), so the table hypotheses of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc hold with exactly the stated \(D,p\).

Let \(\widehat r\) denote the displayed array (2) for the moment. Summing its conditional factors over all Boolean \(\mathbf J\) gives one in each column, so its column sums are \(p_{\mathbf I}\). The scalar mass and moment identities prove that each row has mass \(2^{-n}\) and barycenter \(\mathbf J\). Hence \(\widehat r\in\mathcal F\), with all entries positive on \(\{0,1\}^n\times D\).

Every \(r\in\mathcal F\) has \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)\), by the column sums and row identities. For any integer vector \(\mathbf J\), \(\|\mathbf J\|^2-\sum_tJ_t=\sum_tJ_t(J_t-1)\ge0\), with equality exactly for Boolean \(\mathbf J\). Therefore \(V(r)\ge n/2\), and equality holds precisely when all its positive row targets are Boolean. The feasible array \(\widehat r\) attains equality. This identifies the entire minimum face, rather than just proving that a particular allocation minimizes \(V\).

It remains to prove that \(\widehat r\) is exactly the entropy selection on this whole face. For Boolean \(\mathbf J\), its log conditional share is
\[
\log\frac{\widehat r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
=-\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log(1+e^{A_H+B_H I_t})
+\sum_{t=1}^{n}J_t(A_H+B_H I_t).
\tag{13}
\]
For any \(r\) on the minimum face and each \(t\), its row identities give
\[
\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}J_t I_t
=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}J_t^2
=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}J_t=\frac12 .
\tag{14}
\]
The second equality uses the Boolean target support already established. The expectation of the first sum on the right of (13) is fixed by the column sums; the expectation of its second sum is \(n(A_H+B_H)/2\) by (14). Consequently the expectation of (13) is the same under every \(r\) on this face, including \(\widehat r\). It follows that
\[
\mathsf H(\widehat r)-\mathsf H(r)
=\sum_{\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n,\ \mathbf I\in D}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\log
\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{\widehat r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}
\ge0 .
\tag{15}
\]
To justify both the inequality and equality case without an entropy optimization premise, apply \(x\log x-x+1\ge0\) for \(x\ge0\), with equality only at \(x=1\), at each ratio \(r/\widehat r\), and multiply by the strictly positive denominator. Both arrays have total mass one, so the linear terms cancel. Equality holds only when \(r=\widehat r\). This proves (2) for the unique \(r^\star\) of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc. It also proves all its asserted row masses and supports. In particular the normalized source law at every Boolean row is the product \(\nu_{\mathbf J}\), with no independence assertion made for an arbitrary minimizing allocation.

The maximum original weight is one, attained at \((-1,\ldots,-1)\). Hence \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=-(\log q_H)\sum_t(I_t+1)\), an affine function on the entire support. Every barycentric cost \(G\) defined in the statement is therefore zero, regardless of its coefficients or arity. All cutoff families for \(L\ge0\) coincide with their cutoff-zero families.

We now bound the fractional program with the precise full occurrence convention of accepted fact 6ca311257acdfbcb. Define a nonnegative function on the original complete source support by
\[
u(\mathbf I)=\min\left\{1,\frac2{1+\max\{0,I_1,\ldots,I_n\}}\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
For any positive barycentric component at a Boolean target \(\mathbf J\), fix one source \(\mathbf I\) with coefficient \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\). If \(m=\max\{0,I_1,\ldots,I_n\}\) is positive, choose a coordinate \(t\) with \(I_t=m\). Every other source has coordinate at least \(-1\), so
\[
J_t\ge\lambda_{\mathbf I}m-(1-\lambda_{\mathbf I})
=\lambda_{\mathbf I}(m+1)-1 .
\]
Since \(J_t\le1\), this gives \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\le2/(m+1)\); also \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\le1\). If \(m=0\), \(u(\mathbf I)=1\) and the latter inequality suffices. Thus \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\le u(\mathbf I)\) at every participating source, and summing proves \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}u(\mathbf I)\ge1\) for every component. This includes every singleton and requires no affine independence.

For any finite packing satisfying (4), multiply these cover inequalities by the nonnegative component masses and then use the capacities:
\[
\sum_kx_k
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}u(\mathbf I)
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k
\le W\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}u(\mathbf I)
=T\,\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf J}}u(\mathbf X).
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\mathbf X\) is the original source vector drawn from the normalized row law. This directly proves weak duality for the needed cover without assuming an optimization theorem.

Under that row law the coordinates \(X_t\) are independent, with marginal \(\nu_{J_t}\). Put \(M=\max\{0,X_1,\ldots,X_n\}\). For each integer \(0\le k\le H\), the event \(M<k\) is contained in \(\bigcap_t\{X_t<k\}\), and the mass at the retained site \(k\) in (3) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_{\nu_{\mathbf J}}(M<k)
&\le\prod_{t=1}^{n}\left(1-\nu_{J_t}(k)\right)
\le\left(1-\frac1{3\cdot135^{k+1}}\right)^n\\
&\le\exp\!\left(-\frac n{3\cdot135^{k+1}}\right),\\
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf J}}u(\mathbf X)
&\le\frac2{k+1}+\mathbb P_{\nu_{\mathbf J}}(M<k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The last line uses \(u\le2/(k+1)\) on \(M\ge k\) and \(u\le1\) otherwise. These inequalities also hold at \(k=0\), when the first event is empty. Since \(H=n\), the choice \(k=k_n\) in (5) satisfies \(0\le k_n\le H\) for every \(n\ge5\), and \(135^{k_n}\le\sqrt n\). Formula (18) therefore bounds the expectation in (17) by \(\varepsilon_n\), independently of the target. This proves the first line of (6), and then the fractional conclusion (7). Both terms in \(\varepsilon_n\) tend to zero.

For the integral estimate we first give uniform centered moments for the exact scalar row laws. If \(X\) has law \(\nu_0\), then \(\mathbb E X=0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E X^2\ge\nu_0(-1)=2p_{-1}\theta_{-1}\ge1/3\). If it has law \(\nu_1\), then \(\mathbb E X=1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\ge\nu_1(0)\ge p_0/5\ge1/25\), using \(1-\theta_0\ge1/10\) and \(p_0=p_{-1}q_H\ge1/5\). Thus for both \(b=0,1\), a random variable with law \(\nu_b\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E(X-b)=0,\qquad
\mathbb E(X-b)^2\ge\frac1{25},\qquad
\mathbb E(X-b)^4\le2206 .
\tag{19}
\]
For the fourth moment, use the upper bound (3), the inequality \(|-1-b|^4\le16\), and \(|i-b|\le i+1\) for \(i\ge0\). Extending the resulting nonnegative upper bound to an infinite geometric series gives
\[
\mathbb E(X-b)^4
\le16+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^4(2/3)^k
=16+2190=2206.
\tag{20}
\]
The exact series value follows from
\(\sum_{k\ge1}k^4t^k=t(1+11t+11t^2+t^3)/(1-t)^5\), obtained by applying \(t\,d/dt\) four times to \((1-t)^{-1}\), and substituting \(t=2/3\). In particular this bounds the whole original finite law, including the site \(H\).

Fix any Boolean target \(\mathbf J\), retain its product source law, and let \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0\) be an arbitrary real normal. The independent variables \(X_t-J_t\) are centered. Put \(Y=\sum_t v_t(X_t-J_t)\), write \(\sigma_t^2=\mathbb E(X_t-J_t)^2\), and put \(\sigma^2=\mathbb EY^2=\sum_t v_t^2\sigma_t^2>0\). Let \(\kappa=2206\cdot25^2\), so (19) implies \(\mathbb E(X_t-J_t)^4\le\kappa\sigma_t^4\) and \(\kappa\ge3\). Independence and centering give the complete fourth-moment expansion
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY^4
&=\sum_t v_t^4\mathbb E(X_t-J_t)^4
+6\sum_{s<t}v_s^2v_t^2\sigma_s^2\sigma_t^2\\
&\le\kappa\left(\sum_t v_t^2\sigma_t^2\right)^2
=\kappa\sigma^4 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
All expansion terms with an index occurring exactly once vanish. The coefficient comparison in the inequality is \(6\le2\kappa\).

Hölder's inequality, followed by (21), yields
\[
\sigma^2
=\mathbb E\bigl(|Y|^{2/3}|Y|^{4/3}\bigr)
\le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb EY^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|Y|\ge\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt\kappa}.
\tag{22}
\]
Because \(\mathbb EY=0\), the positive and negative parts have equal expectations, each \(\mathbb E|Y|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(\mathbb EY_+\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Y>0)}\) and \(\mathbb EY_-\le\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Y<0)}\). Consequently both strict probabilities are at least \(1/(4\kappa)=c\). Every closed halfspace through \(\mathbf J\) contains its corresponding strict event and hence has row weight at least \(cT\). All equality atoms are retained. A closed halfspace merely containing \(\mathbf J\) contains the parallel through-target halfspace, so the same bound holds for every containing closed halfspace.

We finish by checking the exact integral hitting interpretation, rather than substituting a different row certificate. Since every original cost is zero, all cutoffs have the same family. A finite barycentric support contains an affinely independent barycentric sub-support: choose a representation with smallest positive support; an affine dependence would permit a signed perturbation preserving the sum and barycenter until a positive coefficient first vanishes. Thus hitting the affinely independent supports in the convention of accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893 is equivalent here to hitting all the supports used in the statement.

Every closed halfspace through \(\mathbf J\) hits every barycentric support, because the barycentric average of its defining scores is zero, so the scores cannot all be strictly negative. Hence its assigned row weight is at least \(m_{\log}^{\mathrm{row}}(\mathbf J,0)\).

Conversely, an optimal hitting set \(F\subseteq D\) exists, since \(D\) is finite. If \(D\setminus F\) is nonempty, its convex hull does not contain \(\mathbf J\), or a barycentric representation from its sites would avoid \(F\). Let \(\mathbf z_0\) be a closest point of that compact convex hull to \(\mathbf J\). The nonzero vector \(\mathbf v=\mathbf J-\mathbf z_0\) satisfies \(\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)\le0\) throughout the hull, by differentiating squared distance along its segments. Thus \(\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\le-\|\mathbf v\|^2<0\) for every retained site \(\mathbf I\in D\setminus F\). The associated closed halfspace through \(\mathbf J\) contains only sites in \(F\), so its row weight is at most the optimal hitting weight. If \(D\setminus F\) is empty, any nonzero normal gives a halfspace of row weight at most \(T=\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}Wr^\star_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), which suffices for the same reverse inequality. This proves the exact minimum characterization in (6), with an attaining normal and the original fixed capacities. Combining it with (21)-(22) proves its lower bound.

Finally all cutoff families are unchanged as \(L\) increases from zero, while \(1+L\) increases. Both row score optimizations therefore use \(L=0\). The estimates already hold at each of the complete set of positive rows in the uniquely selected array, proving (7). No numerical parameter estimate, entropy computation, solver output, or unaccepted mathematical assertion is a premise.
