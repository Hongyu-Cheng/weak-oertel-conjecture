## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty and $d\ge1$. On this fixed base let compact convex bodies $K_{j,z}$ with nonempty interiors converge in Hausdorff distance, for each $z$, to a compact convex body $K_z$ with nonempty interior. Write $S_j=\bigcup_z\{z\}\times K_{j,z}$ and $C_j=\operatorname{conv}S_j$, with $S,C$ for the limits. Assume compatibility $C_j\cap(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d)=\{z\}\times K_{j,z}$ and the same for $C$. Use the sampled volume $dm_j=\sum_z\delta_z\otimes dX|_{K_{j,z}}$, mass $M_j$, fiber centroids $g_{j,z}$ and pooled covariance
$$
\Sigma_j=M_j^{-1}\sum_z\int_{K_{j,z}}(X-g_{j,z})(X-g_{j,z})^{\mathsf T}\,dX,
$$
and their limiting versions $dm,M,\Sigma$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $N=d+1$. For any positive continuous concave $U$ on $C_j$ with $\int_{S_j}\log U\,dm_j/M_j=0$, define the raw buffered depth at a mark $(p,y)\in S_j$ as the infimum of the masses of closed pure-base caps containing $p$ and of the closed caps
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon\bigl(U(p,y)E_{K_{j,p}}(y)/M_j\bigr)^{1/N}
\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_j u}\ge0,\qquad u\ne0 .
$$
Here $E_K(y)$ is marked projective energy with its zero boundary value, the infimum of $\int_K[1+b\cdot(X-y)]^{-N}\,dX$ over denominators strictly positive on $K$ at interior marks. Allow the whole-base trace, in particular in base rank zero. Let $Q_j(U)$ be the maximum raw depth on $S_j$, and define $Q(L)$ on the limit family in the same way.

Assume $L>0$ is continuous and concave on $C$, with $\int_S\log L\,dm/M=0$. Suppose there are finitely many marks $Y_i=(p_i,y_i)\in S$, positive weights $\sigma_i$ summing to one, and probabilities $\rho_i$ on $S$ satisfying
$$
\int w\,d\rho_i=Y_i,\qquad \int L\,d\rho_i=L_i=L(Y_i),\qquad
\sum_i(\sigma_i/L_i)\rho_i=dm/(ML).
$$
Every $Y_i$ is assumed to have maximal raw depth $Q=Q(L)$, strictly below its own pure-base depth $b_{\rm base}(p_i)$. If $U_j$ are admissible parameters with $Q_j(U_j)\le\inf_U Q_j(U)+o(1)$, then $U_j(z,\cdot)\to L(z,\cdot)$ uniformly on every compact subset of $\operatorname{int}K_z$ at the same coordinates. The zero extensions of $U_j$ and, separately, of $\log U_j$ on the sampled fibers converge in mixed $L^1$ to the corresponding zero extensions of $L$ and $\log L$. The energy-weighted factors $(U_j(z,y)E_{K_{j,z}}(y)/M_j)^{1/N}$ converge uniformly on the sampled coordinate spaces after zero extension, and so do the actual physical shifts uniformly in Euclidean unit continuous normals. Finally $Q_j(U_j)\to Q$ and $Q_j(U_j)/M_j\to Q/M$.

## Proof

**Continuity of cap comparison and a reference competitor.** Hausdorff convergence to positive convex bodies gives convergence of the fiber indicators almost everywhere, because every point off the boundary eventually has its limiting membership by support-function comparison. All bodies lie in a common ball. Thus the total symmetric difference of the sampled volumes tends to zero and $M_j\to M$, $\Sigma_j\to\Sigma$ by convergence of the moments. The covariances are positive definite since every fiber has nonempty interior. We use the comparison proved in f2ead43a963c7026. At two marks in corresponding fibers of the same base, take $\|u\|=1$ by rescaling the normal. The thresholds of two corresponding nonvertical caps differ by at most the distance of the marks plus the supremum of the difference of the physical shifts over these unit normals. The arbitrary base tilt cancels. The change in mass is bounded by the total sampled symmetric difference plus this threshold difference times a common upper bound for the sum of all perpendicular fiber section volumes. A common ball gives that bound, with zero-dimensional section measure one for $d=1$. For a pure-base cap only the symmetric difference contributes. Taking infima preserves the bound, and its fixed-family version gives continuity in marks and attainment of the maxima for every continuous parameter. The bound applies to any paired marks of distance tending to zero if their physical shifts converge uniformly in the unit normal.

The hulls $C_j$ converge to $C$ in their common relative ambient space, since replacing the sampled points in a convex combination moves that combination by at most the maximum fiber Hausdorff error. They are full-dimensional relative to $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$, since the base spans $\operatorname{aff}P$ and the sampled fibers contain balls. Fix a relative inball $B(O,r)\subset C$. Put $e_j=d_H(C_j,C)$ and $\lambda_j=r/(r+e_j)$. The map $H_j(w)=O+\lambda_j(w-O)$ sends $C_j$ into $C$. Indeed, in each relative unit direction $u$, the support of $C-O$ is at least $r$, and $\lambda_j(h_{C-O}(u)+e_j)\le h_{C-O}(u)$. Geometrically normalize $L\circ H_j$ on $S_j$ to get a legal reference $L_j$. Its scalar normalization tends to one by uniform continuity, strict positivity of $L$ on $C$, and the convergence of the sampled indicators. Hence $L_j$ converges to $L$ on corresponding sampled marks whose distance tends to zero. The accepted marked-energy continuity b3c0088cf09b2894 gives the same convergence for the energy, including boundary limits. By compactness on the fixed finite base these convergences are uniform on the correspondence of distance at most the maximum fiber Hausdorff error. The cap comparison yields $Q_j(L_j)\to Q$. Near-minimality therefore gives $Q_j(U_j)\le Q+o(1)$.

**Source marks and their pushed rows.** The old maximum is positive because every buffered cap at any sampled own-interior point contains at least a half of a centered own ball. At a boundary mark the energy is zero. The supporting slope of 155691fc3768c06f applies by anchored compatibility and gives an outward cap meeting every sampled fiber in at most a Lebesgue-null slice. Thus every old source is own-interior. Its energy is positive because the energy objective on a centered own ball is bounded below by that ball’s volume by Jensen for the convex inverse power. For a scalar $s>L_i$ inserted at the old fixed mark $Y_i$, its depth is strictly larger than $Q$. To see this, write $\delta=\varepsilon(sE_{K_{p_i}}(y_i)/M)^{1/N}$ and normalize nonvertical normals by $\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}=1$. At this scalar the depth is at most $b_{\rm base}(p_i)$. If equality holds, the old strict gap gives the claim. If the depth is strictly below that value, 20210aa9d661295a gives an attained nonvertical cap with a proper own section, since the mark is interior, the scalar is positive and the actual covariance is positive definite. Reducing the scalar from $s$ to $L_i$ raises its threshold, removing a positive own strip near the proper section and weakly reducing every other fiber mass. Its resulting mass is at least the old depth $Q$, so the original minimizing cap at $s$ has mass strictly greater than $Q$. This argument needs the strict gap only at the particular old source.

Choose fixed interior points $g_z\in K_z$ with a common inball radius $r_0>0$. For sufficiently large $j$ put $t_j=\max_z d_H(K_{j,z},K_z)/r_0<1$. Support functions give
$$
I_{j,z}=g_z+(1-t_j)(K_z-g_z)\subset K_{j,z}\subset g_z+(1+t_j)(K_z-g_z).
$$
On the sampled points put $T_j(z,X)=(z,g_z+(1-t_j)(X-g_z))$. The push of each $\rho_i$ has barycenter $Y_i^j=(p_i,(1-t_j)y_i+t_j\int g_Z\,d\rho_i)$, which belongs to $C_j$ and hence to the actual $p_i$ fiber by compatibility. It tends to $Y_i$. The depth at $Y_i^j$ using the fixed scalar $s>L_i$ tends to its limiting scalar depth at $Y_i$ by marked-energy continuity and the cap comparison. Monotonicity in the scalar and $Q_j(U_j)\le Q+o(1)$ consequently give
$$
\eta_j=\max_i[\log(U_j(Y_i^j)/L_i)]_+\longrightarrow0.
$$
Here a failure at any one of the finitely many marks would give a fixed $s>L_i$ and a subsequence with $U_j(Y_i^j)\ge s$.

Set $dP_0=dm/M$ and $R_j(w)=U_j(T_jw)/L(w)$. Jensen for $U_j$ applied to the pushed rows, which need not arise from one affine map, gives
$$
b_j=\int R_j\,dP_0
=\sum_i(\sigma_i/L_i)\int U_j(T_jw)\,d\rho_i
\le \sum_i\sigma_i U_j(Y_i^j)/L_i\le e^{\eta_j}.
$$

**Sampled bounds and the logarithmic collars.** Put $q_j=(1-t_j)/(1+t_j)\to1$. The inner set $I_{j,z}$ contains $g_z+q_j(K_{j,z}-g_z)$. Positivity and concavity give $\max_{I_{j,z}} U_j\ge q_j\max_{K_{j,z}}U_j$. The homothetic superlevel argument gives $\max_{I_{j,z}}U_j\le(d+1)\int_{I_{j,z}}U_j/|I_{j,z}|$. Changing variables through the common factor $1-t_j$ and using the bound on $b_j$ gives
$$
\max_{K_{j,z}}U_j
\le\frac{(d+1)M\max_S L}{q_j |K_z|}e^{\eta_j}.
$$
Fix a common upper bound $B\ge1$. With $V_{j,z}=|K_{j,z}|$ and $m_{j,z}=\max_{K_{j,z}}U_j$, normalization gives
$$
0\le (V_{j,z}/M_j)\log m_{j,z}+(1-V_{j,z}/M_j)\log B.
$$
The positive limiting volume proportions bound the $m_{j,z}$ away from zero uniformly. On each fiber the homothet toward a maximizer yields
$$
|\{U_j<s m_{j,z}\}\cap K_{j,z}|\le [1-(1-s)^d]V_{j,z}\le d s V_{j,z}\qquad(0<s<1).
$$
It follows that $m_j\{U_j<r\}\le A r$ for small $r>0$ and a common constant $A$. Increasing $A$ bounds the same quantity for $0<r\le1$. For any measurable subset of the sampled fibers of volume $h$, the integral of the negative part of $\log U_j$ is at most $\int_0^\infty\min(h,Ae^{-s})\,ds$. Together with the upper bound $B$, this bounds the absolute logarithmic integral by $O(h(1+|\log h|))$ as $h\downarrow0$, uniformly in $j$.

The contraction Jacobian is $J_j=(1-t_j)^d$ on every fiber. Using the exact normalizations on their own bodies,
$$
\xi_j=\int\log R_j\,dP_0
=-\frac{1}{M J_j}\sum_z\int_{K_{j,z}\setminus I_{j,z}}\log U_j(z,X)\,dX\longrightarrow0.
$$
The convergence follows from the collar estimate since the total collar volume tends to zero. By logarithmic Jensen, $e^{\xi_j}\le b_j\le e^{\eta_j}$. The probability-law estimate in quantitative-log-contact-certificate-rigidity applies to the positive integrable $R_j$, without requiring any full-space concavity of the pullback. Its reverse entropy is $\log b_j-\xi_j\to0$ and $b_j\to1$, so $R_j\to1$ in $L^1(P_0)$. Thus $U_j\circ T_j\to L$ in mixed $L^1$ on $S$.

**Convergence on the moving sampled domains.** On each fixed old fiber, $U_j\circ T_j$ is concave with uniformly bounded maximum. The core estimate of quantitative-log-contact-certificate-rigidity therefore gives locally uniform convergence to $L$ in that fiber's interior. For any fixed interior compact set, support-function comparison places both it and its $T_j$ image in a common positive-radius core of $K_{j,z}$ for all large $j$. Bounded nonnegative concave functions on those cores have uniformly bounded slopes by the same secant argument. Since $T_j\to\operatorname{id}$ uniformly on $S$, the locally uniform convergence holds at the same continuous coordinates. Using an exhaustion by compact fiber interiors and the small volumes of the remaining collars now gives mixed $L^1$ convergence of the zero-extended parameters. On every fixed compact fiber interior the limit is bounded away from zero, so the logarithms converge uniformly there. The uniform logarithmic integral bound treats the small-volume complements, yielding the mixed $L^1$ convergence of the zero-extended logarithms.

On the interiors the energy-weighted factors converge by this local uniform convergence and marked energy continuity. At a sequence of points tending to an old own-boundary point, the marked energies tend to zero and the parameters are uniformly bounded. The limiting factors also tend to zero there. For a limit outside the old fiber, the factors are eventually zero by Hausdorff convergence. This sequential argument in a common compact ball and on the finite base proves uniform convergence with the zero extensions, and $\Sigma_j\to\Sigma$ gives the same for the physical shifts over unit continuous normals. The cap comparison now applies uniformly on the nearest-fiber correspondences, even though the parameters themselves need not converge there uniformly. The two maximum comparisons and $M_j\to M$ prove the raw and normalized convergence.
