## Statement

For $L,\epsilon>0$, let $P=\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}$ and
$$
C=\operatorname{conv}\{(z,Lz_1z_2):z\in P\}+(\{0\}\times[0,\epsilon])
\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R.
$$
Its actual integer fibers are the six intervals $[Lz_1z_2,Lz_1z_2+\epsilon]$. For $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)$ one has $C=\operatorname{conv}S$, total mixed length $M=6\epsilon$, and each fiber length $\epsilon<M/4$.

After any continuous affine shear $x'=x-a z_1-b z_2-c$, and for every nonzero primitive integer direction $v=(p,q)$, let $A_v$ be the sum of the integer-base section lengths of the actual projected body in $(w,x')$, $w=qz_1-pz_2$. Then
$$
A_v\ge L\max(2|q|,|p|)\ge L.
$$
The quotient by both integer directions is the continuous shadow, of length $A_{12}\ge L+\epsilon$ after every shear; its minimum over shears is exactly $L+\epsilon$, independently of the unimodular base used to describe the quotient. Thus the best one-direction ratio $A_v/M$ is unbounded over this family even with simultaneous optimization over shears and primitive directions, and the optimized full-quotient ratio is $(L+\epsilon)/(6\epsilon)$, also unbounded.

## Proof

The four lifted lower corners are affinely independent: their alternating height sum is $2L\ne0$ while the same alternating base sum vanishes. The projection rectangle has exactly the six listed integer bases, all on its two horizontal edges. A convex combination over such an edge uses only that edge, where the lower height is affine in $z_1$. Adding the vertical segment gives the asserted fibers. Conversely each lower and upper lifted generator belongs to an actual fiber, and the hull of these generators is exactly $C$ by distributing the addition of the vertical segment over convex combinations. This proves hull exactness, full ambient dimension and the strict weight statement.

Drop the translation $c$, which only translates the projected continuous coordinate. The map $z\mapsto qz_1-pz_2$ sends $\mathbb Z^2$ onto $\mathbb Z$ by primitivity, with kernel $\mathbb Zv$. The quotient coordinate therefore carries ordinary lattice counting, with no Jacobian in the continuous coordinate. In the quotient plane the lower corners are $0,U,V,U+V+W$ where
$$
U=(2q,-2a),\qquad V=(-p,-b),\qquad W=(0,2L).
$$
For any such four points, the hull area is at least $|\det(U,W)|/2$. Indeed the two triangles sharing $[0,U]$ have signed doubled areas $\det(U,V)$ and $\det(U,V)+\det(U,W)$. If these have opposite signs the interiors are disjoint and their areas add; if the signs agree the larger area is at least half the absolute difference of the signed doubled areas. The same argument interchanging $U,V$ also bounds area below by $|\det(V,W)|/2$. Thus the quotient polygon has area at least $L\max(2|q|,|p|)$.

The entire quotient polygon is generated by the projected lower and upper lifted corners, all having integer $w$. Therefore it equals the hull of its exact grid trace, and the trapezoid identity in e61be865fa5bee2c applies at $t=0,h=1$ with its actual section lengths. The resulting area is $A_v$ minus half the sum of the two nonnegative endpoint lengths, so $A_v$ dominates the area. Every primitive vector occurs as a column of a unimodular integer basis by Bézout; the bound holds for each such quotient and every shear.

Freeing both integer directions leaves the interval spanned by the four sheared lower heights, then enlarged by $[0,\epsilon]$. The alternating sum $h_{00}+h_{21}-h_{20}-h_{01}=2L$ is shear invariant. Its magnitude is at most twice the range of the four heights, so the full quotient has length at least $L+\epsilon$. At $a=L/2,b=L,c=0$ the four heights in the order $00,20,01,21$ are $0,-L,-L,0$. This gives equality. Full quotient does not depend on the choice of integer basis.

For the depth distinction, restrict to either horizontal base edge. Its three intervals have affine centers, so their measure of mass $3\epsilon$ is centrally symmetric about the center over its middle lattice site. Every ambient closed halfspace containing that site-center takes at least half of the edge measure, giving at least $M/4$ in the full mixed set, above the sharp target $M/8$. The coefficient obstruction concerns the quotient shadow, not a depth deficit.
