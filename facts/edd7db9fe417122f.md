## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}),
\]
where an empty or lower-dimensional fiber has volume zero, and assume
\[
W:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}>0.
\]
For \(\mathbf y\in S\), write
\[
h_S(\mathbf y)
:=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf y\in H}}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H),
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y).
\]
Set
\[
\delta_n:=2^{-n},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
\theta_d:=1-\gamma_d,
\]
and
\[
\chi_d
:=
1-\theta_d-\theta_d\log\frac1{\theta_d}
=
\gamma_d-\theta_d\log\frac1{\theta_d}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\gamma_d-\chi_d
=
\theta_d\log\frac1{\theta_d}
>0.
\]

There exists \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(\mathbf m\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}
\geq
\delta_nW.
\tag{2}
\]
Every \(\mathbf m\) satisfying (2) has \(a_{\mathbf m}>0\). Let \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf m}\) be the centroid of \(C_{\mathbf m}\). Since a convex body contains its centroid, \((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\in S\).

Let \(\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\) be the finite set of directed primitive vectors
\[
\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}
:=
\left\{
\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n:
\gcd(|v_1|,\ldots,|v_n|)=1,
\ a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}>0
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\) and \(k\geq0\), define
\[
u_{\mathbf v,k}:=
\frac{a_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v}}{a_{\mathbf m}},
\qquad
T_{\mathbf v}:=\sum_{k\geq1}u_{\mathbf v,k},
\qquad
X_{\mathbf v}:=
\sum_{k\geq1}(u_{\mathbf v,k}-\theta_d)_+,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(x_+:=\max\{x,0\}\), and put
\[
\Delta_{\mathbf v}
:=
\bigl(\chi_dT_{\mathbf v}-X_{\mathbf v}\bigr)_+.
\tag{5}
\]
Then every ray sequence in (4) has finite interval support and is log-concave, and
\[
0\leq\Delta_{\mathbf v}\leq\gamma_d-\chi_d.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}\geq a_{\mathbf m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta_{\mathbf v}=0.
\tag{7}
\]

Define the angular deficit
\[
\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
:=
\sup_{\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\mathbf q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
\Delta_{\mathbf v}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then the exact primitive-ray deficit certificate is
\[
h_S\bigl((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\bigr)
\geq
\max\left\{
\gamma_da_{\mathbf m},
\ \chi_d\delta_nW
+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
-a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

For the bounded-descending-ray corollary, define
\[
R_-(\mathbf m)
:=
\sup_{\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\#\left\{
\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}:
\mathbf q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0,
\ a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}<a_{\mathbf m}
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
This is a finite nonnegative integer. If \(R_-(\mathbf m)=0\), then
\[
h_S\bigl((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\bigr)
\geq
\chi_d\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(R:=R_-(\mathbf m)\geq1\), then
\[
h_S\bigl((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\bigr)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d\chi_d}
{\gamma_d+(R-1)(\gamma_d-\chi_d)}
\,2^{-n}W
\geq
\frac{\chi_d}{R}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, the exact coefficient in the first inequality of (12) equals \(\chi_d\) when \(R=1\). Finally, with
\[
\chi_*
:=
\frac1e-\left(1-\frac1e\right)
\log\frac e{e-1}
>0,
\tag{13}
\]
one has the uniform dimension-independent consequence
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
h_S\bigl((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\bigr)
\geq
\frac{\chi_*}{\max\{1,R_-(\mathbf m)\}}
\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{14}
\]

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty. Thus all sums below are finite, even when written as ray series.

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c`, with continuous dimension zero, to the nonzero finite atomic measure
\[
\nu:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}\delta_{\mathbf z}
\]
supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\). It supplies \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfying (2). We first prove the asserted positivity of its fiber. Let
\[
D_+:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_{\mathbf z}>0\}.
\]
If \(\mathbf m\notin\operatorname{conv}D_+\), strict separation from the convex hull of this finite set gives a proper closed base halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\) and no point of \(D_+\). Its \(\nu\)-mass is zero, contradicting (2). Hence there are \(\mathbf z_1,\ldots,\mathbf z_s\in D_+\) and positive \(\lambda_i\) with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i=1,
\qquad
\mathbf m=\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i\mathbf z_i.
\tag{15}
\]
If \(s=1\), then \(a_{\mathbf m}>0\) directly. If \(s\geq2\), fix \(\mathbf x_i\in C_{\mathbf z_i}\) for \(i\geq2\). Convexity of \(C\) and (15) give
\[
\lambda_1C_{\mathbf z_1}
+\sum_{i=2}^s\lambda_i\mathbf x_i
\subseteq
C_{\mathbf m}.
\]
The set on the left is a translate of \(\lambda_1C_{\mathbf z_1}\), so it has volume \(\lambda_1^da_{\mathbf z_1}>0\). Therefore \(a_{\mathbf m}>0\), as required.

We next prove all ray assertions, including finite support. Fix \(\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\). Since the positive-volume lattice support is finite, there is a largest \(N\geq1\) with \(a_{\mathbf m+N\mathbf v}>0\). If \(1\leq j<N\), then
\[
\mathbf m+j\mathbf v
=
\left(1-\frac jN\right)\mathbf m
+\frac jN(\mathbf m+N\mathbf v).
\]
Fixing one point in \(C_{\mathbf m+N\mathbf v}\) and varying the point in the positive-volume fiber \(C_{\mathbf m}\), exactly as above, shows that \(a_{\mathbf m+j\mathbf v}>0\). Thus the positive support of \((u_{\mathbf v,k})_{k\geq0}\) is exactly \(\{0,1,\ldots,N\}\). This also proves that if any positive-volume lattice point lies at \(\mathbf m+k\mathbf v\) for a primitive directed \(\mathbf v\) and \(k\geq1\), then \(a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}>0\); hence no occupied lattice point will be missed by (3).

For \(0\leq k\leq N\), put
\[
r_k:=a_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v}^{1/d}.
\]
Suppose \(1\leq k<N\). For each of the two positive endpoint fibers at \(\mathbf m+(k-1)\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf m+(k+1)\mathbf v\), the density equal to one realizes its full-mass centroid in the positive-root set used in accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. The verified proof of that fact establishes convexity of this positive-root set before closure. Its midpoint therefore belongs to the same set at base point \(\mathbf m+k\mathbf v\), with root coordinate \((r_{k-1}+r_{k+1})/2\). Membership supplies a density bounded by one on \(C_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v}\) of mass \(((r_{k-1}+r_{k+1})/2)^d\), so
\[
r_k\geq\frac{r_{k-1}+r_{k+1}}2
\geq\sqrt{r_{k-1}r_{k+1}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
a_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v}^2
\geq
a_{\mathbf m+(k-1)\mathbf v}
a_{\mathbf m+(k+1)\mathbf v}.
\tag{16}
\]
At the last positive index and throughout the zero tail, (16) is automatic because its right side contains a zero whenever needed. Division by \(a_{\mathbf m}^2>0\) proves that \((u_{\mathbf v,k})_{k\geq0}\) is log-concave. No monotonicity of this sequence is asserted or used.

Apply accepted fact `772688f4504b1b5b` to this finite-support log-concave sequence with threshold \(\theta_d\). That accepted fact explicitly includes finite support and the one-term support case. Since \(u_{\mathbf v,0}=1\), its conclusion reads
\[
\gamma_d+X_{\mathbf v}
=
\sum_{k\geq0}(u_{\mathbf v,k}-\theta_d)_+
\geq
\chi_d\sum_{k\geq0}u_{\mathbf v,k}
=
\chi_d(1+T_{\mathbf v}).
\]
Thus
\[
X_{\mathbf v}
\geq
\chi_dT_{\mathbf v}-(\gamma_d-\chi_d),
\]
which proves (6) from the definition (5).

For the zero-deficit assertion, suppose
\[
u_{\mathbf v,1}
=
\frac{a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}}{a_{\mathbf m}}
\geq1.
\]
Define the shifted normalized sequence
\[
w_j:=\frac{u_{\mathbf v,j+1}}{u_{\mathbf v,1}}
\qquad(j\geq0),
\]
and the shifted threshold
\[
\alpha:=\frac{\theta_d}{u_{\mathbf v,1}}.
\]
Then \(w_0=1\), \(0<\alpha<1\), and \((w_j)_{j\geq0}\) is again a summable log-concave sequence with finite interval support. If the original ray has only its first positive step after the anchor, this shifted sequence has support \(\{0\}\), which is among the finite-support cases of `772688f4504b1b5b`. Applying that fact and multiplying by \(u_{\mathbf v,1}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
X_{\mathbf v}
&=
u_{\mathbf v,1}
\sum_{j\geq0}(w_j-\alpha)_+\\
&\geq
q(\alpha)
\sum_{j\geq0}u_{\mathbf v,j+1}
=q(\alpha)T_{\mathbf v},
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
where
\[
q(t):=1-t-t\log\frac1t.
\]
Since \(q'(t)=\log t<0\) on \((0,1)\) and \(\alpha\leq\theta_d\),
\[
q(\alpha)\geq q(\theta_d)=\chi_d.
\]
Equation (17) gives \(X_{\mathbf v}\geq\chi_dT_{\mathbf v}\), so \(\Delta_{\mathbf v}=0\). This proves (7), including equality in the first-step comparison and every finite-support endpoint case.

We now prove (9). Put \(p:=(\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\), and let \(H\) be a closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\). If the whole ambient space is admitted as an improper halfspace, it has mixed volume \(W\). It dominates \(\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}\), and it also dominates the second expression in (9), because \(\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)\geq0\), \(a_{\mathbf m}\leq W\), \(\delta_n\leq1\), and
\[
\chi_d\delta_nW+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
\leq\gamma_dW\leq W.
\]
Thus assume \(H\) is proper. Translate its boundary parallel toward \(p\) to obtain a proper closed halfspace \(H^0\subseteq H\) whose boundary contains \(p\).

First suppose \(H^0\) is vertical. Its base side is a proper closed halfspace through \(\mathbf m\). Therefore (2) gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H^0)
\geq
\delta_nW.
\tag{18}
\]
The whole anchor fiber lies in \(H^0\), so also
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq a_{\mathbf m}.
\tag{19}
\]
Let
\[
B:=\chi_d\delta_nW+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}.
\]
If \(a_{\mathbf m}\leq\delta_nW\), then
\[
B\leq\gamma_d\delta_nW\leq\delta_nW;
\]
if \(a_{\mathbf m}\geq\delta_nW\), then
\[
B\leq\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}\leq a_{\mathbf m}.
\]
Equations (18)-(19), together with \(\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)\geq0\), show that every vertical halfspace satisfies both lower bounds in (9).

Suppose instead that \(H^0\) is nonvertical. Accepted fact `c13409b4041cc35d`, applied with distinguished fiber \(\mathbf m\) and its centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf m}\), gives a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni\mathbf m\) such that
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H^0)
\geq
\sum_{\mathbf z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}
(a_{\mathbf z}-\theta_da_{\mathbf m})_+.
\tag{20}
\]
Write \(J=\{\mathbf z:\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z\geq b\}\) with \(\boldsymbol\ell\neq\mathbf0\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf m\geq b\), and shrink it to the boundary-through-anchor halfspace
\[
J^0:=
\{\mathbf z:
\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf z-\mathbf m)\geq0\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then \(J^0\subseteq J\), it is proper and contains \(\mathbf m\), and every summand in (20) is nonnegative.

Every positive-volume lattice point \(\mathbf z\neq\mathbf m\) has a unique representation
\[
\mathbf z=\mathbf m+k\mathbf v,
\qquad
k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1},
\qquad
\gcd(|v_1|,\ldots,|v_n|)=1,
\tag{22}
\]
where the sign of the directed primitive vector \(\mathbf v\) is fixed by \(\mathbf z-\mathbf m\). The interval-support argument above shows that this \(\mathbf v\) belongs to \(\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\). Moreover, (21) contains \(\mathbf m+k\mathbf v\) exactly when \(\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0\). Hence the positive-volume lattice points of \(J^0\), other than \(\mathbf m\), partition disjointly into precisely the included directed rays, including both directed rays on a line when that line lies in the boundary of \(J^0\). Therefore (20) gives the exact ray decomposition
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&\geq
\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}
+a_{\mathbf m}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
X_{\mathbf v}\\
&\geq
\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}
+\chi_da_{\mathbf m}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
T_{\mathbf v}
-a_{\mathbf m}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
\Delta_{\mathbf v}\\
&=
\chi_d
\sum_{\mathbf z\in J^0\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}
+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
-a_{\mathbf m}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
\Delta_{\mathbf v}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(J^0\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\), equations (2), (8), and (23) yield
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\chi_d\delta_nW
+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
-a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m).
\tag{24}
\]

It remains to establish the first lower bound in (9) for every orientation. The density equal to one shows that \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf m}\in\mathsf M_{a_{\mathbf m}}(C_{\mathbf m})\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore says that every closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^d\) containing \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf m}\) captures at least \(\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}\) from \(C_{\mathbf m}\). If \(H^0\) is nonvertical, its restriction to the anchor fiber is such a closed halfspace with boundary through \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf m}\). If \(H^0\) is vertical, it contains the anchor fiber in full. Thus every \(H\ni p\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq\gamma_da_{\mathbf m}.
\tag{25}
\]
Combining (18)-(25) and taking the infimum over \(H\) proves (9), with vertical, nonvertical, boundary, and improper-halfspace cases all included.

We finish with the bounded-descending-ray consequences. By (6)-(7), for every \(\mathbf q\neq\mathbf0\), only rays satisfying \(a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}<a_{\mathbf m}\) can contribute to the sum in (8), and each contributes at most \(\gamma_d-\chi_d\). Hence
\[
\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
\leq
(\gamma_d-\chi_d)R_-(\mathbf m).
\tag{26}
\]
Substitution into (9) gives, with \(R:=R_-(\mathbf m)\),
\[
h_S\bigl((\mathbf m,\mathbf c_{\mathbf m})\bigr)
\geq
\max\left\{
\gamma_da_{\mathbf m},
\ \chi_d\delta_nW
-(R-1)(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
\right\}.
\tag{27}
\]
When \(R=0\), the second expression in (27) is
\[
\chi_d\delta_nW
+(\gamma_d-\chi_d)a_{\mathbf m}
\geq\chi_d\delta_nW,
\]
which proves (11). This case is separate: substituting \(R=0\) into the balancing formula for \(R\geq1\) would be invalid because both terms in (27) then increase with \(a_{\mathbf m}\).

Now let \(R\geq1\). The maximum in (27), viewed as a function of a nonnegative value of \(a_{\mathbf m}\), is bounded below by the value where its two affine entries meet. That value is
\[
\frac{\gamma_d\chi_d}
{\gamma_d+(R-1)(\gamma_d-\chi_d)}
\,\delta_nW.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(R=1\), the second entry of (27) is already the constant \(\chi_d\delta_nW\), and (28) equals that same value. Thus no division by \(R-1\) or omitted constant case occurs. Since \(\chi_d>0\),
\[
\gamma_d+(R-1)(\gamma_d-\chi_d)
\leq R\gamma_d,
\]
so (28) is at least \((\chi_d/R)\delta_nW\). This proves (12).

Finally,
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\gamma_d>\frac1e.
\]
The function
\[
F(x):=x-(1-x)\log\frac1{1-x}
\]
satisfies \(F'(x)=-\log(1-x)>0\) for \(0<x<1\), and \(F(x)>0\) there because \(\log(1/(1-x))<x/(1-x)\). Consequently,
\[
\chi_d=F(\gamma_d)>F(1/e)=\chi_*>0.
\]
Combining this with (11)-(12) gives (14).
