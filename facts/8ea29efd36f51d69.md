## Statement

For a measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R\) and \(0<t\leq |K|_1\), write
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq 1\ \text{a.e. on }K,
\quad \int_K f(x)\,dx=t
\right\}.
\]
For \(K=[0,1]\) and every \(0<t\leq1\),
\[
\mathsf M_t([0,1])=\left[\frac t2,1-\frac t2\right].
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, take \(t_1=t_2=1/4\) and \(q_1=q_2=1/8\). There are two separate admissible selectors of masses \(t_1,t_2\) having barycenters \(q_1,q_2\), and
\[
t_1+t_2=\frac12\leq |K|_1=1.
\]
Nevertheless their mass-weighted barycenter satisfies
\[
\frac{t_1q_1+t_2q_2}{t_1+t_2}=\frac18
\notin \mathsf M_{1/2}([0,1]).
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, even under the scalar capacity condition \(t_1+t_2\leq |K|_1\), the separate conditions \(q_i\in\mathsf M_{t_i}(K)\) do not imply that their mass-weighted barycenter belongs to \(\mathsf M_{t_1+t_2}(K)\).

The terminal example in accepted fact fc828b039898bc41 has requested mass \(3/4\) in a fiber of volume \(1/2\), so it detects capacity overflow. The present example is distinct: the total requested mass is within the fiber volume, and failure is caused by incompatibility of the separate barycenter demands rather than by scalar overflow.

## Proof

Fix \(0<t\leq1\), and let \(f:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) be measurable with \(\int_0^1 f=t\). Put
\[
I(f):=\int_0^1 x f(x)\,dx.
\]
Set
\[
a:=\int_0^t(1-f(x))\,dx.
\]
The mass identity gives
\[
a=t-\int_0^t f(x)\,dx=\int_t^1 f(x)\,dx.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
I(f)-\frac{t^2}{2}
&=\int_t^1x f(x)\,dx-\int_0^t x(1-f(x))\,dx\\
&\geq t\int_t^1 f(x)\,dx-t\int_0^t(1-f(x))\,dx
=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(I(f)\geq t^2/2\). Applying this lower bound to the reflected selector \(\widetilde f(x):=f(1-x)\), whose mass is \(t\) and whose first moment is \(t-I(f)\), yields
\[
t-I(f)\geq\frac{t^2}{2},
\qquad
I(f)\leq t-\frac{t^2}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
After division by \(t\), (3) and (4) show
\[
\mathsf M_t([0,1])\subseteq\left[\frac t2,1-\frac t2\right].
\tag{5}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, the selectors \(\mathbf1_{[0,t]}\) and \(\mathbf1_{[1-t,1]}\) both have mass \(t\), with normalized first moments \(t/2\) and \(1-t/2\), respectively. For every \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\),
\[
f_\lambda:=(1-\lambda)\mathbf1_{[0,t]}+\lambda\mathbf1_{[1-t,1]}
\]
still satisfies \(0\leq f_\lambda\leq1\) a.e., including on a possible overlap of the two intervals, and has mass \(t\). Its normalized first moment is the corresponding convex combination of the two endpoints. Hence every point of the interval on the right-hand side of (5) is attained, proving (1).

At \(t=1/4\), formula (1) gives
\[
\mathsf M_{1/4}([0,1])=\left[\frac18,\frac78\right].
\]
More explicitly, each of the two separate selectors
\[
f_1=f_2=\mathbf1_{[0,1/4]}
\]
has mass \(1/4\) and normalized first moment \(1/8\). Their scalar masses sum to \(1/2\leq1\), but the profiles are not simultaneously feasible under the unit density cap because \(f_1+f_2=2\) on \([0,1/4]\). Formula (1) at \(t=1/2\) gives
\[
\mathsf M_{1/2}([0,1])=\left[\frac14,\frac34\right],
\]
which proves (2). Thus the scalar inequality on total mass does not encode simultaneous density compatibility, as claimed.
