## Statement

Fix $d\ge1$, $R,v>0$ and a finite lattice-complete base $P$ with $k$ occupied base parities and $|P|>k$. Consider actual compatible fibers $K_p\subseteq R B_d$ with $|K_p|\ge v$, where $B_d$ is the Euclidean unit ball. Write $g_p$ for their centroids and $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. For distinct equal-parity pairs $p,q$ and $m=(p+q)/2$, let
$$
E=\max_{p,q}\bigl[|K_m|^{1/d}-(|K_p|^{1/d}+|K_q|^{1/d})/2\bigr],
\qquad
E_{\rm cent}=\max_{p,q}\|g_m-(g_p+g_q)/2\|.
$$
On the class where maximum normalized mixed closed-halfspace depth is at most $\Gamma/k$, each of these two functions has a strictly positive attained minimum whenever that class is nonempty. In particular there exist positive thresholds depending only on $P,d,R,v$ such that making either respective defect no greater than its threshold forces maximum normalized depth strictly above $\Gamma/k$.

## Proof

The bounded compact convex subsets of $R B_d$ form a Hausdorff-compact class by boundedness and equicontinuity of their support functions. The finite product remains compact. The volume lower bounds are closed by bounded convex Hausdorff continuity of volume, and all anchored inclusions persist by approximating each source point in a fixed representation and passing its weighted combination to the limit. Thus the class of compatible families is compact and its limiting fibers have positive volume.

For maximum normalized depth, use accepted marked-depth stability 43ed87d91b76249c with the same base, ordinary $d$-volume on every positive fiber, and marks converging in their fixed fiber. Each maximum is attained, since within-fiber depth is Lipschitz by the slab estimate of that fact and there are finitely many compact fibers. To obtain the lower limit of the maxima, approximate a maximizing point of the limit by points of the same fiber of the sequence. For the upper limit, take a maximizing point of each family on a subsequence realizing the limsup, and pass to a further subsequence with fixed base and convergent continuous coordinate. Hausdorff convergence makes the limit feasible, so marked stability bounds the limsup by the limiting maximum. Total mass stays at least $|P|v$, permitting normalization. Consequently the stated depth sublevel is compact.

If $E_{\rm cent}=0$, the centroid graph is midpoint closed for pairs with equal base parity. Give its points weights $|K_p|$ of total $M$. Accepted fact f4547c4166b39683 yields a graph point of raw atomic depth at least $M/k$ whose minimal graph face weighs strictly more than $M/k$, because $|P|>k$. The actual centroid transfer c2180d0c4d7ea592 then gives raw mixed depth strictly greater than $\Gamma M/k$ there, excluding this family from the sublevel.

Compatibility and Brunn–Minkowski give nonnegativity of every root deficit. At $E=0$, equality in each midpoint volume root and fact 9e6b5c9eeaab615d with weights $1/2,1/2$ give positive homothetic endpoints and the exact Minkowski midpoint fiber. Write the endpoints as $aK+t,bK+s$ with $a,b>0$. Since $K$ is convex, the midpoint is $((a+b)/2)K+(t+s)/2$, whose volume centroid is the average of the two endpoint centroids. Thus $E=0$ implies $E_{\rm cent}=0$ and also excludes the family.

The pair list is finite and nonempty. Volumes and first moments are continuous under bounded convex Hausdorff convergence by indicator convergence in $L^1$, and centroid denominators are at least $v$. Therefore both defects are continuous. Each attains its minimum on a nonempty compact sublevel, and neither minimum can be zero by the preceding implications. Half of each minimum gives its threshold. If the sublevel is empty, either threshold may be any positive number.
