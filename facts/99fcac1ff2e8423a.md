## Statement

Let $r\ge1,s\ge0$. Let $Q\subset\mathbb R^r_u\times\mathbb R^s_x$ be compact and convex, with projection $P\subset[-R,R]^r$, $R>0$. Sample on a full-rank coset $a+L$ with covolume $v$ and a measurable fundamental cell of $L$ in $[-\delta,\delta]^r$. Write $F=\max_u|Q_u|_s$, $M=|Q|_{r+s}$, $\mu(H)=v\sum_{\lambda\in a+L}|(Q\cap H)_\lambda|_s$, and $\nu(H)=|Q\cap H|_{r+s}$. Use counting in $\mathbb R^0$ and take $F=0$ when $Q=\varnothing$. For every closed ambient halfspace or the whole space $H$,
$$
|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|\le 2r\delta(2R+2\delta)^{r-1}F.
$$
No positive-volume hypothesis is required. If $M>0$, then
$$
F|P|_r\le\binom{r+s}{r}M,\qquad
\frac{|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|}{M}
 \le\binom{r+s}{r}\frac{2r\delta(2R+2\delta)^{r-1}}{|P|_r}.
$$
The peak comparison is equality for $s=0$.

For $M>0$, normalize only $u$ by $u'=Au+b$ so $B_2^r\subset AP+b\subset R_r B_2^r$ with $R_r=r(r+2)$. The sampled and coarse measures both acquire $|\det A|$, so normalized masses are unchanged. If the transformed lattice has a fundamental cell in $[-\eta,\eta]^r$, then
$$
\sup_H\frac{|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|}{M}\le
e(r,s,\eta):=\frac{2r\binom{r+s}{r}}{\kappa_r}\,\eta(2R_r+2\eta)^{r-1},
\qquad \kappa_r=|B_2^r|_r.
$$
Covering radius at most $\eta$ suffices. If $e<1$, the sampled total is positive and the difference between separately normalized halfspace masses is at most $2e/(1-e)$.

For a finite family $Q_z$ in the same $(u,x)$ dimensions attached to coarse labels $z$, with positive coarse volumes $M_z$ and the same original fine coset, each projection may use its own such normalization and cell bound $\eta_z$. For every original ambient closed halfspace or the whole space,
$$
\frac{|\sum_z\mu_z(H)-\sum_z\nu_z(H)|}{\sum_z M_z}
 \le \frac{\sum_z e(r,s,\eta_z)M_z}{\sum_z M_z}.
$$
For a fixed finite family of labels this implies uniform convergence of the separately normalized cap laws when all $\eta_z\to0$, even when the positive $M_z$ vary. The same estimate and convergence hold for countably many labels with $0<\sum_z M_z<\infty$ and a uniform error tending to zero. A zero-coarse-volume fiber is governed only by the absolute bound and may have positive sampled mass.

## Proof

For $s\ge1$, the section volume of $Q\cap H$ is bounded and upper semicontinuous: when $u_j\to u$, all subsequential limits of points in the sections lie in the limiting compact section. If that section is empty then nearby sections are eventually empty; otherwise nearby sections lie in any prescribed outer neighborhood of it. Volume continuity from above on these neighborhoods gives upper semicontinuity. The same applies to $Q$, so $F$ is attained. Brunn–Minkowski on the $s$-dimensional sections makes every positive closed superlevel set of $u\mapsto |(Q\cap H)_u|_s$ convex. They are compact subsets of $[-R,R]^r$; empty superlevels cause no error. When $s=0$ the function is the indicator of the compact convex projection of $Q\cap H$, with the same superlevel property.

Apply the per-set boxed discrepancy in the proof of e4ef1ac154c5d280 with its continuous dimension set to zero. For each superlevel set $D_t$, this compares $v\#(D_t\cap(a+L))$ to $|D_t|_r$ with error $2r\delta(2R+2\delta)^{r-1}$. It applies also to degenerate or empty $D_t$, before the positive-total condition in the aggregate normalization of that fact. Integrating from $0$ to $F$ and using Tonelli proves the absolute bound: the two integrals give $\mu(H)$ and $\nu(H)$ respectively. The closed superlevel convention counts every sampled boundary value.

For the peak comparison when $M>0$ and $s\ge1$, take $u_0$ with $|Q_{u_0}|_s=F$. For any $u\in P$ choose a point in the fiber there. Convexity gives a translate of $(1-\theta)Q_{u_0}$ in the section at $(1-\theta)u_0+\theta u$. Hence the $t$-superlevel set of the original profile contains the projection dilated about $u_0$ by $1-(t/F)^{1/s}$. Integrating over $0<t<F$ gives
$$
M\ge F|P|_r\int_0^1 (1-a^{1/s})^r\,da
=F|P|_r\,\frac{s!\,r!}{(r+s)!}.
$$
The last equality follows by substituting $b=a^{1/s}$ and integrating $s b^{s-1}(1-b)^r$ on $[0,1]$. For $s=0$ use $F=1$ and $M=|P|_r$. Dividing the absolute bound by $M$ now yields the projection-volume denominator.

Since $M>0$, $P$ is full dimensional. The maximal-simplex sandwich in 03bc771bddd39a8a maps it between the standard simplex and $[-1,1]^r$. The simplex contains the ball of radius $1/((r+1)\sqrt r)$ about its barycenter: the distances to coordinate facets are $1/(r+1)$ and to the opposite facet $1/((r+1)\sqrt r)$. The cube lies within radius $\sqrt r(1+1/(r+1))$ of that center. Recenter and scale by $(r+1)\sqrt r$ to obtain $R_r=r(r+2)$. The map leaves section volumes unchanged, multiplies the sampling covolume by $|\det A|$ and multiplies full volume by the same determinant. Arbitrary original halfspaces are taken to halfspaces. Apply the relative bound in the new coordinates using $\kappa_r$ as a lower bound on projection volume. A Voronoi fundamental cell of a lattice of covering radius at most $\eta$ is contained in the ball of that radius, hence in the cube. For $T=\mu(\mathrm{total})$, the discrepancy of totals implies $T\ge(1-e)M$; then
$$
\left|\frac{\mu(H)}{T}-\frac{\nu(H)}{M}\right|
\le \frac{|\mu(H)-\nu(H)|}{T}+\frac{\nu(H)|T-M|}{TM}
\le \frac{2e}{1-e}.
$$

At any fixed coarse label, the original ambient cut restricts to a halfspace, the whole fiber space, or the empty set. Undo the determinant for that label to obtain $|\mu_z(H)-\nu_z(H)|\le e(r,s,\eta_z)M_z$ in the original units. Summation gives the weighted error; the maps need not fit into a common affine map of the body. For a countable family with bounded errors and finite total coarse mass, the total-mass inequalities imply absolute summability of sampled mass as well. In either case the same normalization algebra applies with the weighted average in place of $e$. This average is bounded by the maximum or supremum of the errors, proving the stated convergence even when the coarse masses vary. A null coarse fiber is not covered by the division by $M_z$: for example, $\{0\}\times[0,1]^s$ has zero coarse volume and positive sampled mass when the coset contains zero. Retain such fibers in the absolute estimate. Uniform mass discrepancy by itself supplies no feasible sampled center.
