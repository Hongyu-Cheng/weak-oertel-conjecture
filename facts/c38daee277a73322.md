## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge1\), used throughout as the dilation parameter, and put \(\beta_k=(k+1)^{k+1}/k^k\). For every integer \(d\ge0\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d\) be a nonempty finite set of distinct vectors satisfying

\[
\operatorname{aff}(A)=\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\sum_{a\in A}a=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{int}\!\left(\frac1k\operatorname{conv}(A)\right)
\cap\mathbb Z^d=\{0\}.
\]

Then \(|A|\le\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d}\). For every \(d\ge1\), equality holds for the complete lattice set of \(k((d+1)\Delta_d-\mathbf1)\), where \(\Delta_d=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_d\}\) and \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\). The theorem does not require \(A\) to be the complete lattice set of its hull or to contain zero, and it imposes no continuous-centroid or lattice-index hypothesis.

For every integer \(r\ge0\), let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope such that \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). Retain the complete support \(D=kP\cap\mathbb Z^r\), including every boundary lattice point. Its unnormalized capacity satisfies

\[
B(kP):=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
\le\beta_k^r.
\]

The infimum is attained and equals the maximum entropy of a mean-zero probability vector on \(D\) after taking logarithms. In particular, for every probability vector \(p\) on \(D\) with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\),
\(H(p):=-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}\le r\log\beta_k\), with natural logarithms and \(0\log0=0\).

For every real potential \(\phi:D\to\mathbb R\), put \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\). If these original weights satisfy \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\), then their original normalized law \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) satisfies

\[
g:=\frac1W\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf0}
=H(p)+\log p_{\mathbf0}
\le r\log\beta_k.
\]

In particular, this energy inequality holds when \(\phi\) is the restriction to \(D\) of a real-valued convex function on \(kP\) and its original weights satisfy the separate condition \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). For \(k=2\), every real potential \(\phi:2P\cap\mathbb Z^r\to\mathbb R\) whose original weights satisfy \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in 2P\cap\mathbb Z^r}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\) obeys \(g\le r\log(27/4)\), with \(w,W,g\) defined above for this complete support.

The capacity base is sharp for each fixed \(k\): for \(r\ge1\), the polytopes \(P_\Delta=(r+1)\Delta_r-\mathbf1\) satisfy

\[
B(kP_\Delta)=\binom{(k+1)r+k}{r},
\qquad
\lim_{r\to\infty}B(kP_\Delta)^{1/r}=\beta_k.
\]

Consequently no smaller positive base works uniformly over all dimensions. In dimension zero the finite count and capacity are one, and the entropy and energy gap are zero.

## Proof

We first prove the finite-set assertion for \(d\ge1\). Write \(M=|A|\). The hypothesis gives \(0\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{conv}(A)/k)\). For every \(0\le u<1\),
\(u\operatorname{conv}(A)/k\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{conv}(A)/k)\): if a ball about zero of radius \(\varepsilon>0\) is contained in \(\operatorname{conv}(A)/k\), convexity puts a ball of radius \((1-u)\varepsilon\) about each point of \(u\operatorname{conv}(A)/k\) in the same body. Therefore, if positive integers \(q,t\) satisfy \(q>ktM\), then

\[
tM\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap q\mathbb Z^d=\{0\}.
\]

Indeed, if \(qv\in tM\operatorname{conv}(A)\) for \(v\in\mathbb Z^d\), then \(v\in(ktM/q)(\operatorname{conv}(A)/k)\), which is contained in the interior of \(\operatorname{conv}(A)/k\). Hence \(v=0\).

We use the Taylor-filtration and pivot-product construction proved in accepted fact ac927038e59fbb6d. Its finite set \(A\), dimension \(d\), and cardinality \(M\) retain exactly their meanings here. Its prime denoted \(p\) is denoted \(q\) below, reserving \(p\) for probability laws. For clarity, the algebraic output we use is the following. In
\(R_q=\mathbb F_q[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_d^{\pm1}]\), substitution \(z_i=1+y_i\) is injective into the formal power-series ring. Let \(\operatorname{ord}\) be its least nonzero total Taylor degree, and set

\[
\mathcal L=\sum_{j\ge1}
\left(M-\binom{d+j-1}{d}\right)_+,
\qquad (x)_+=\max\{x,0\}.
\]

The construction gives a nonzero \(F\in R_q\), depending on \(q\), and a real linear functional \(\ell\) such that

\[
\operatorname{ord}(F)\ge\mathcal L,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(F)\subseteq M\operatorname{conv}(A),
\qquad
[z^0]F\ne0,
\qquad
\ell(v)>0\quad
\bigl(v\in\operatorname{supp}(F)\setminus\{0\}\bigr).
\]

These algebraic conclusions require only the finite distinct integer exponents and \(\sum_{a\in A}a=0\), so they apply under the present hypotheses. Specifically, in the \(M\)-dimensional span of the distinct Laurent monomials \(z^a\), the kernel of Taylor coefficients of degree less than \(j\) has dimension at least \((M-\binom{d+j-1}{d})_+\). The simultaneous filtration-adapted basis and distinct-pivot elimination proved in ac927038e59fbb6d give a basis whose pivot exponents exhaust \(A\). Its product is \(F\). Additivity of Taylor order gives the stated lower bound, while the sum of its pivot exponents is zero and every other product choice has strictly larger \(\ell\)-value. This gives the nonzero constant coefficient and the strict positivity for all other supported exponents. None of those construction steps uses an interior-lattice-point condition. The construction takes place before any quotient of the Laurent ring, so no reduction of exponent vectors or lattice-index assumption enters it. The integer \(\mathcal L\) is finite and depends only on \(d,M\), not on \(q\).

For every positive integer \(t\), additivity of order and the strict \(\ell\)-positivity give \(\operatorname{ord}(F^t)\ge t\mathcal L\), \(\operatorname{supp}(F^t)\subseteq tM\operatorname{conv}(A)\), and \([z^0]F^t=([z^0]F)^t\ne0\). Choose any prime \(q>kM\) and put \(t=\lfloor(q-1)/(kM)\rfloor\). Then \(t\ge1\) and \(ktM<q\), so the geometric exclusion just proved applies.

As proved in ac927038e59fbb6d, the quotient

\[
R_q/(z_1^q-1,\ldots,z_d^q-1)
\cong\mathbb F_q[y_1,\ldots,y_d]/(y_1^q,\ldots,y_d^q)
\]

has a Laurent-monomial basis indexed by exponent residues modulo \(q\), and its map from \(R_q\) agrees with Taylor expansion followed by the displayed truncation. The residue-zero coefficient of the image of \(F^t\) is the sum of its coefficients at exponents in \(q\mathbb Z^d\). Only exponent zero can occur there, by the geometric exclusion. Hence this image is nonzero. Every surviving Taylor monomial has degree at most \(d(q-1)\), so

\[
t\mathcal L\le\operatorname{ord}(F^t)\le d(q-1).
\]

Write \(q-1=kMt+s\) with \(0\le s<kM\). Dividing by \(t\) gives \(\mathcal L\le kdM+d(kM-1)/t\). There are arbitrarily large primes, as established by the elementary argument in ac927038e59fbb6d, and \(t\to\infty\) along them. Since \(\mathcal L\) is independent of \(q\), it follows that \(\mathcal L\le kdM\).

Keep precisely the first \(k(d+1)\) terms in \(\mathcal L\). Positive parts dominate their arguments, and Pascal's identity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
kdM
&\ge\mathcal L\\
&\ge\sum_{j=1}^{k(d+1)}
\left(M-\binom{d+j-1}{d}\right)\\
&=k(d+1)M-\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the binomial sum telescopes to \(\binom{d+k(d+1)}{d+1}\). Thus \(kM\le\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d+1}\). Finally,

\[
\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d+1}
=\frac{kd+k}{d+1}\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d}
=k\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d}.
\]

Dividing by \(k>0\) proves the finite bound. For \(d=0\), a nonempty set of distinct points in \(\mathbb Z^0\) is \(\{0\}\), giving the asserted bound directly.

For the finite equality example, take \(P_\Delta=(d+1)\Delta_d-\mathbf1\), with the same meaning as in ac927038e59fbb6d. That accepted fact proves that \(P_\Delta\) is full dimensional and has sole interior lattice point zero. Its inequalities are \(x_i\ge-1\) and \(\sum_i x_i\le1\). For \(A=kP_\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^d\), the map
\(a\mapsto(b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_d)\), given by \(b_i=a_i+k\) for \(i\ge1\) and \(b_0=k-\sum_i a_i\), is a bijection to the nonnegative integer vectors whose coordinate sum is \(k(d+1)\). The stars-and-bars count is \(\binom{k(d+1)+d}{d}\). Permuting these \(d+1\) coordinates preserves this finite set, so every coordinate has average \(k\); hence the average of every \(a_i=b_i-k\) is zero. The vertices of \(kP_\Delta\) are integral and belong to \(A\), giving \(\operatorname{conv}(A)=kP_\Delta\). Thus all finite-set hypotheses hold, and the bound is attained.

We record the asymptotic size of this binomial coefficient. For fixed \(k\), the elementary estimate \(\log(h!)=h\log h-h+O(\log(h+1))\), proved by integral comparison in a2e6106c48c8e525, gives, as \(d\to\infty\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\binom{(k+1)d+k}{d}
&=((k+1)d+k)\log((k+1)d+k)
-d\log d\\
&\quad -(kd+k)\log(kd+k)+O_k(\log(d+1))\\
&=d\bigl((k+1)\log(k+1)-k\log k\bigr)
+O_k(\log(d+1))\\
&=d\log\beta_k+O_k(\log(d+1)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, for each fixed positive \(c\), expanding \(\log(cd+k)=\log d+\log c+\log(1+k/(cd))\) shows that \((cd+k)\log(cd+k)=cd\log d+cd\log c+k\log d+O_{c,k}(1)\). Substituting \(c=k+1\) and \(c=k\) cancels the \(d\log d\) and \(k\log d\) terms as displayed.

We now prove the capacity assertion for \(r\ge1\). Here \(P\) and the complete support \(D=kP\cap\mathbb Z^r\) have exactly the meanings in the statement. Since \(kP\) is a lattice polytope, \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=kP\); in particular \(D\) is finite, contains zero, and has full affine rank. The entropy and rational-approximation arguments in a2e6106c48c8e525 use only these properties and \(0\in\operatorname{int}(kP)\). We give their applicability explicitly, since no unique-interior-point condition is imposed on \(kP\).

Choose \(\rho>0\) such that the radius-\(\rho\) ball about zero is contained in \(kP\). The defining exponential sum is at least \(\exp(\max_{\mathbf x\in kP}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x)\ge e^{\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}\). It is therefore continuous and coercive and has a minimizer \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\). The derivative equation shows that
\(\lambda^*_{\mathbf I}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}/B(kP)\) is a positive probability vector with exactly zero mean. Its entropy is \(\log B(kP)\). For any mean-zero probability vector \(p\) and any \(\boldsymbol\theta\), concavity of the logarithm gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(p)
&=\sum_{\mathbf I:p_{\mathbf I}>0}
p_{\mathbf I}\log\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}\\
&\le\log\!\left(\sum_{\mathbf I:p_{\mathbf I}>0}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\right)
\le\log\!\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first equality uses exactly the original zero-mean equation. Taking the infimum proves \(H(p)\le\log B(kP)\), and the equality at \(\lambda^*\) proves the entropy maximum identity.

The integer matrix with columns \((1,\mathbf I)\), \(\mathbf I\in D\), has rank \(r+1\). Choose \(r+1\) independent columns, approximate all other coordinates of \(\lambda^*\) by rational numbers, and solve the probability and zero-mean equations for the selected coordinates. The selected square matrix has rational inverse. The resulting coordinates are rational and converge to those of \(\lambda^*\), so all remain positive when the approximation is sufficiently close. This is the rational-approximation construction of a2e6106c48c8e525 with its support polytope replaced by \(kP\), and all its required rank and interior conditions have just been checked. Entropy continuity therefore reduces the capacity bound to \(H(\lambda)\le r\log\beta_k\) for each rational full-support mean-zero vector \(\lambda\) on \(D\).

Fix such a \(\lambda\). We use the word construction of a2e6106c48c8e525 and the distinct-word arithmetic average of 4df6862a0a6c787d, with their word-length symbol \(k\) renamed \(m\). Our dilation \(k\) remains fixed. Let \(m\ge2\) run through positive multiples of a common denominator of the coordinates of \(\lambda\). Let \(\mathcal T_m(\lambda)\) consist of all distinct words in \(D^m\) containing exactly \(m\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) copies of each label \(\mathbf I\). Every prescribed multiplicity is positive, and every word has block sum zero. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_m(\lambda),\\
V_m&=\left\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_m)\in(\mathbb R^r)^m:
\sum_{a=1}^m\mathbf x_a=\mathbf0\right\},\\
L_m&=V_m\cap(\mathbb Z^r)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Certainly \(Q_m\subseteq V_m\cap(kP)^m\). For distinct block positions \(a,b\) and distinct labels \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\), positive multiplicities allow a word with these labels at those positions. Subtracting its swap gives a difference of words with blocks \(\mathbf I-\mathbf J\) at \(a\), \(\mathbf J-\mathbf I\) at \(b\), and zero elsewhere. Because \(D-D\) spans \(\mathbb R^r\), the real span of these differences contains every vector with blocks \(\mathbf u,-\mathbf u\) at \(a,b\). Taking \(b=m\) and all \(a<m\) spans \(V_m\). Thus \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_m)=V_m\) and its dimension is \(r(m-1)\), exactly as in the accepted construction.

Every block permutation acts bijectively on the set of distinct words. As proved for that set in 4df6862a0a6c787d, its uniform average is therefore fixed by every block permutation and has all blocks equal to a vector \(\mathbf u\). The average lies in \(V_m\), so \(m\mathbf u=\mathbf0\). Thus the arithmetic mean of the distinct words is exactly zero. It also follows that \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\): if a nonzero supporting functional on \(V_m\) were nonnegative on \(Q_m\) and zero at the average, its values at all the words would be zero, contradicting their full affine span. Scaling gives \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_m/k)\).

We verify the changed interior assertion in the entire lattice \(L_m\). For each block \(a\), the projection \(\pi_a:V_m\to\mathbb R^r\) is surjective and open. Explicitly, choose another block \(b\ne a\); any perturbation \(\mathbf h\) of block \(a\) lifts to the vector in \(V_m\) with blocks \(\mathbf h,-\mathbf h\) at \(a,b\), of norm \(\sqrt2\|\mathbf h\|\). If \(\mathbf x\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_m/k)\), there is a relative ball in \(V_m\) about \(\mathbf x\) of some radius \(\varepsilon>0\) contained in \(Q_m/k\). Its block projection contains the radius-\(\varepsilon/\sqrt2\) ball about \(\mathbf x_a\). Since \(Q_m/k\subseteq P^m\), this projected ball is contained in \(P\). Therefore \(\mathbf x_a\in\operatorname{int}(P)\) for every block, including those that are zero. Consequently every \(\mathbf x\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_m/k)\cap L_m\) has all blocks in \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). The reverse inclusion was proved above, so

\[
\operatorname{relint}(Q_m/k)\cap L_m=\{\mathbf0\}.
\]

This argument uses relative neighborhoods and the full block projection, so no boundary lattice point of \(D\) is discarded and no boundary point is incorrectly treated as interior. The lattice in this equality is \(L_m\), without dilation.

Use the exact chart from a2e6106c48c8e525,

\[
\Phi_m:(\mathbb R^r)^{m-1}\longrightarrow V_m,
\qquad
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1})\longmapsto
\left(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1},-
\sum_{a=1}^{m-1}\mathbf y_a\right).
\]

This is a real linear isomorphism whose inverse retains the first \(m-1\) blocks. It maps \(\mathbb Z^{r(m-1)}\) bijectively onto all of \(L_m\); hence the lattice rank is exactly \(r(m-1)\). Put \(d_m=r(m-1)\) and \(A_m=\Phi_m^{-1}(\mathcal T_m(\lambda))\). The set \(A_m\) consists of distinct integer vectors, has full affine hull \(\mathbb R^{d_m}\), and has arithmetic sum zero by linearity of the chart. Also
\(\operatorname{conv}(A_m)/k=\Phi_m^{-1}(Q_m/k)\). The preceding interior equality and the chart's bijection on the full lattices therefore give
\(\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{conv}(A_m)/k)\cap\mathbb Z^{d_m}=\{0\}\).

The finite-set theorem proved above applies directly to \(A_m\), and distinct words remain distinct under the chart. The exact count is thus

\[
\frac{m!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(m\lambda_{\mathbf I})!}
=|\mathcal T_m(\lambda)|
=|A_m|
\le\binom{(k+1)d_m+k}{d_m}.
\]

The multinomial equality divides permutations of the \(m\) positions by the permutations among equal labels. Only this distinct-word set is counted. No claim about the arithmetic mean of all lattice points of \(Q_m\), or about a continuous centroid, is used.

The same elementary factorial estimate from a2e6106c48c8e525 gives
\(\log|\mathcal T_m(\lambda)|=mH(\lambda)+O(|D|\log(m+1))\). The binomial asymptotic proved above, with \(d=d_m=r(m-1)\), gives

\[
\frac1m\log\binom{(k+1)d_m+k}{d_m}
=\frac{r(m-1)}m\log\beta_k
+O_{k,r}\!\left(\frac{\log(m+1)}m\right).
\]

Letting \(m\to\infty\) along the permitted multiples proves \(H(\lambda)\le r\log\beta_k\). The bound is independent of \(\lambda\); rational full-support zero-mean approximations to \(\lambda^*\) now yield \(\log B(kP)\le r\log\beta_k\). This proves the capacity bound, and the already established entropy inequality proves it for every original mean-zero law on \(D\).

For sharpness, apply the finite equality construction above with \(d=r\). Its polytope \(P_\Delta\) has sole interior lattice point zero, and the complete set \(D=kP_\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^r\) has cardinality \(\binom{(k+1)r+k}{r}\) and arithmetic sum zero. For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\), the inequality \(e^x\ge1+x\) gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\ge|D|+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\mathbf I=|D|\). Equality holds at \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\). Thus \(B(kP_\Delta)=|D|\), and the binomial asymptotic gives \(\log B(kP_\Delta)/r\to\log\beta_k\). Every fixed smaller positive base is exceeded by these capacity roots in all sufficiently large dimensions.

Finally, for the original weights in the statement, \(-\log p_{\mathbf I}=\phi_{\mathbf I}+\log W\) and \(\log p_{\mathbf0}=-\phi_{\mathbf0}-\log W\). Therefore
\(g=H(p)+\log p_{\mathbf0}\le H(p)\le r\log\beta_k\), since \(0<p_{\mathbf0}\le1\). The entropy inequality applies because the original weights satisfy \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). This proves the assertion for every real potential with that original zero-mean condition, including restrictions of real-valued convex functions whose original weights satisfy the same condition. For \(k=2\), under \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in 2P\cap\mathbb Z^r}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\), the value \(\beta_2=3^3/2^2=27/4\) gives \(g\le r\log(27/4)\). When \(r=0\), \(P=D=\{\mathbf0\}\), the capacity sum is one, \(p_{\mathbf0}=1\), and both entropy and energy gap are zero, as asserted.
