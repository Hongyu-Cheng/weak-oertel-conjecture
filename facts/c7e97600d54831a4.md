## Statement

For $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,(1,0),(0,1))$, $a=1+\varepsilon^2$, and take
$$
A=\{(x,y):x,y\ge0,\ \varepsilon\le x+y\le1\},\quad
B=\varepsilon^2T,\quad
P=\{(x,y):x,y\le a,\ x+y\ge\varepsilon\}.
$$
The hull of the lifts of $A,P/2,B$ at bases $0,1,2$ has exactly those three integer fibers, all of positive area. For $n\ge1$, pad the source with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ in additional integer coordinates. It has exactly $3\cdot 2^{n-1}$ positive fibers, each layer repeating the same three.

Write $\delta(K)=|K|(D(K)-4/9)$, where $D$ is maximum normalized ordinary planar closed-halfspace depth. The sum of all source deficits satisfies $0<\sum\delta(K)\le 2^{n-1}\cdot2\varepsilon^2/9$. For any triangle containing each actual fiber, the hull of the lifts of the chosen triangles at the same bases adds at least $2^{n-1}\varepsilon/16$ in actual mixed area. Thus no finite constant times the summed ordinary deficits can bound that cost even if all enclosing triangles are jointly chosen or required to be compatible.

## Proof

The triangle $P$ has vertices $(a,a),(a,\varepsilon-a),(\varepsilon-a,a)$. The coordinate maxima of $A+B$ are at most $a$, and its coordinate sum is at least $\varepsilon$, so $A+B\subseteq P$. The source projection is $[0,2]$. At bases zero and two only the corresponding lifts contribute. At base one the endpoint weights agree, and the endpoint average lies in $P/2$ by that inclusion. This proves the complete integer-fiber census. Each fiber has interior and the endpoint bases differ, so the hull is a convex body. Its areas are
$$
|A|=(1-\varepsilon^2)/2,\qquad |P/2|=(2a-\varepsilon)^2/8,\qquad |B|=\varepsilon^4/2.
$$
The affine inequality $x+y\ge\varepsilon(1-j/2)$, with base coordinate $j$, holds on all three source lifts and hence on the whole source. At $j=1$ its equality line cuts out the lower edge of $P/2$.

Fact 03bc771bddd39a8a in dimension two gives nonnegative ordinary deficits for all three planar convex bodies and zero exactly for triangles. Thus only $A$ contributes and its deficit is positive, since $A$ has four distinct edges of positive length. For any point of $A\subseteq T$, at least one of its three barycentric coordinates in $T$ is at least $1/3$. The closed cap toward the corresponding vertex, through that point and parallel to the opposite edge, meets $T$ in a similar triangle of area at most $(4/9)|T|$. Its trace on $A$ has no greater area. Taking the maximum over all candidates of $A$ gives $|A|D(A)\le(4/9)|T|$. Subtracting $(4/9)|A|$ gives $\delta(A)\le2\varepsilon^2/9$.

Let $R_j$ be any of the enclosing triangles, and let $K'_j$ be the actual fibers after taking their lifted hull. The new projection is still $[0,2]$, with $K'_0=R_0$ and $K'_2=R_2$. At base one it contains both $P/2$ and $(R_0+R_2)/2$. Since $0\in B\subseteq R_2$, the compact convex set $L=2K'_1$ contains $P$ and $R_0$. The planar nested-triangle lemma applies to these exact sets. In its small-$\Delta$ case the vertex below the affine cut at base zero yields its midpoint with $(2,\mathbf0)$ below the same cut at base one; the lemma measures the exterior triangle over the original lower edge there. The scaled excess in the lemma is precisely $|K'_1|-|P/2|$. The remaining endpoint has nonnegative excess. Thus the total added mixed area is at least $\varepsilon/16$.

The only sampled extra coordinates in the padded source are the binary vertices. At any such vertex all contributing points of the new hull must come from the same layer, by extremality in each padded coordinate. Each layer therefore obeys the same estimate even with different choices. The deficits and the lower bound on the cost multiply by $2^{n-1}$. Their ratio is at least $9/(32\varepsilon)$, which diverges. If the chosen containers are already compatible, their own fibers coincide with those of their hull and the bound still applies.
