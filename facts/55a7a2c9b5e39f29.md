## Statement

## Proof

All sums are finite because \(C\) is compact. The first-label identities, original target packing, total mass in (1), and source occurrence identity
\[
\sum_\tau w_\tau^{(1)}N_z(\tau)=\frac{\beta_z}{2},\qquad
\beta_z\le\frac{a_z}{4}
\tag{13}
\]
are supplied by accepted facts dcca4b6d66d80dd4 and 4ebcf4455419a224 with the alias map stated above. In particular, every positive current target lies in the actual \(Z\).

The original reservoir formula gives \(Q\le W/8\). The hard ledger of 637b5f7874d8c875 gives \(G<Q/100\), so \(G<W/2\). Accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b therefore applies in its original normalization. Its parity index \(\omega\) is denoted by \(\xi\) here, while its original \(a_z,W,G,r\) are unchanged. With \(M_\xi^a=\max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\), zero for an empty original class, it gives \(\sum_\xi M_\xi^a\le G\).

For every current bad class, \(T_\xi<4M_\xi^t\le2M_\xi^a\), since \(t_u\le a_u/2\). Summing proves that the omitted current target mass is at most \(2G\), hence \(T_g\ge T_1-2G>0\). This estimate concerns current bad classes and does not replace them by original bad classes.

Direct summation of (2) gives the degree identity in (3). The elementary binomial bounds \(2\le(1+1/d)^d<3\) give \(1/3<\gamma_d\le1/2\). For completeness, the upper bound follows from
\(\binom dk d^{-k}\le1/k!\) and \(\sum_{k=0}^d1/k!<3\), the latter following from \(1/k!\le2^{-(k-1)}\) for \(k\ge2\), with strictness in the finite sum. The lower binomial bound keeps just its constant and linear terms. Thus the degree bound is both the original free-matching bound \(a_u/2\) and the stronger \(\gamma_da_u\) bound.

Expand \(T_\xi^2\) and count each distinct physical pair once. Its mass is \(\gamma_d(T_\xi-\sum_ut_u^2/T_\xi)\). The full ordered product mass is \(\gamma_dT_\xi\), and the removed physical diagonal mass is exactly \(\gamma_d\sum_ut_u^2/T_\xi\). Before pooling labels, this diagonal sums \(w_\tau^{(1)}w_\sigma^{(1)}\) over every pair with \(\zeta_\tau=\zeta_\sigma=u\), whose sum is \(t_u^2\). Thus no collision between distinct first labels has been omitted.

Current goodness gives \(\sum_ut_u^2/T_\xi\le M_\xi^t\le T_\xi/4\), yielding \(B_{\mathrm{new}}\ge3\gamma_dT_g/4\). Independently, \(M_\xi^t\le M_\xi^a/2\) gives \(F_{\mathrm{phys}}^{\mathrm{new}}\le\gamma_dG/2\). Combining this with \(T_g\ge T_1-2G\) proves the second linear mass bound in (3). Its right side is positive because \(T_1>97Q/200\) and \(G<Q/100\). All denominators are therefore restricted to positive current class mass.

Every affinely realizable submatching of \(q^{\mathrm{new}}\) is a legal original same-parity matching by the established degree bounds. The characterization of \(R_C\) in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d therefore bounds it by the global \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\). The affine matching certificate of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 says \(D\ge\gamma_d\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\). This proves (4), without any comparison of the new edge weights to \(r\).

Applying 6496cb3b0fd44f9d with the stated fresh aliases gives \(\Omega_{\mathrm{new}}\ge B_{\mathrm{new}}-R_C(q^{\mathrm{new}})\), and hence (5) except for its explicit rational bound. Since \(\gamma_d\le1/2\), one has \(5\gamma_d/2\le5/4<2\le1/\gamma_d\). Thus the hard ledger gives
\[
\begin{aligned}

&>\frac{97\gamma_dQ}{200}
-\frac{5\gamma_dG}{2}-\frac D{\gamma_d}\\
&>\left(\frac{97\gamma_d}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q

\end{aligned}

The stronger endpoint bounds \(d_u(q^{\mathrm{new}})\le\gamma_da_u\) allow the \(1/2\)-domination conclusion of that same accepted fact. It proves all claims about (6), including support in \(S\), using its actual new contacts.

For the ancestry, summing (7) over first labels at a fixed distinct pair gives exactly \(q_{\{u,v\}}^{\mathrm{new}}\); hence their total mass is \(B_{\mathrm{new}}\). Their full positions are averages of the four original full positions \((z_\tau^\pm,x_\tau^\pm)\) and \((z_\sigma^\pm,x_\sigma^\pm)\). Convexity puts these averages in \(C\). Their physical base is \((u+v)/2\), which is integral by same parity. For a first label \(\tau\) at \(u\), its total new incidence weight is exactly
\[

\tag{15}
\]
in a retained class and zero otherwise. This is an incidence sum irrespective of which physical endpoint precedes the other. Summing (15) against each full original occurrence \(N_z(\tau)\), and then using (13), proves exactly (8).

Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace. Every ancestral atom in \(H\) has at least one of its four original leaf occurrences in \(H\), because its full position is their average. Assign its full \(\alpha_{\tau\sigma}^{\mathrm{new}}\)-weight to one such occurrence. The total assigned charge \(O_z\) at source \(z\) is bounded by \(L_z^{\mathrm{new}}\). If it is positive, the fiber trace \(H_z\) contains a point of \(\mathcal K_z\). Apply accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with its body \(K\) equal to \(C_z\) and its mass \(m\) equal to \(a_z/2\). Repeated occurrences have already been counted in (8). The non-strict membership test retains all boundary atoms. This proof uses the four actual ancestral leaves and does not replace their atoms by pooled means or fresh contacts.

To prove (10), add the physical diagonal back to the unordered product. The full ordered expression is
\(\sum_{u,v}\gamma_dt_ut_vT_\xi^{-1}\|(u+v)/2-p\|^2\). If two independent variables have distribution \(t_u/T_\xi\), their midpoint has mean \(\mu_\xi\) and centered squared norm expectation \(V_\xi/(2T_\xi)\). This follows by expansion: the mixed centered inner product has zero expectation. Thus the full expression is \(\gamma_d(T_\xi\|\mu_\xi-p\|^2+V_\xi/2)\). Subtracting the diagonal \(\gamma_dT_\xi^{-1}\sum_ut_u^2\|u-p\|^2\) proves (10). Subtracting the actual removed loads gives the first two terms on the right of (11). For each new parity class, the identity

Here is an exact calibration of the regrouping term. This is an arithmetic test of the local mass hypotheses and the proposed spread, not a construction of the canonical hard system or an assertion about a particular dual optimizer. Take \(n=d=1\), so \(\gamma_1=1/2\), and let \(L\) be a positive multiple of four. Give mass one to each base in
\(\{0,2,4,6\}\cup\{L+1,L+3,L+5,L+7\}\), and zero elsewhere. These masses obey \(t_u\le a_u/2\) in the genuine convex rectangle \([0,L+7]\times[-1,1]\), whose actual integer fibers all have volume two. Both current parity classes are good. Every one of their six distinct pairs has weight \(q_e^{\mathrm{new}}=1/4\), so \(B_{\mathrm{new}}=3\). The old parity spread is \(20+20=40\).

For the formal retained array \(\omega=q^{\mathrm{new}}\), the pooled masses \(t'_m=\frac12\sum_{e:m_e=m}q_e^{\mathrm{new}}\) have new parity spread
\[
\Phi(t')=\frac52+\frac{(L+1)^2}{3}.
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, in the new even class the left cluster has mass \(1/4\), mean three, and centered spread \(1/4\), while the right cluster has mass \(1/2\), mean \(L+4\), and centered spread one. In the new odd class these two cluster masses and internal spreads are reversed. Mixing the two means contributes \((1/6)(L+1)^2\) in each new parity class. This proves (16) by the two-component variance identity.

The increase persists for every formal retention array \(0\le\omega\le q^{\mathrm{new}}\) with total removed mass \(\delta\le1/4\). In each new parity, its two cluster raw \(\omega\)-masses are at least \(1/2-\delta\) and \(1-\delta\); their pooled masses are half those values. Their total pooled mass is at most \(3/4\), and their means remain separated by at least \(L-2\). The between-cluster variance in each parity is therefore at least \((1/3)(1/2-\delta)(1-\delta)(L-2)^2\). Summing the two classes gives
\[

\ge\frac18(L-2)^2.
\tag{17}
\]
At \(L=24\), this is \(121/2>40\). Thus even a small total removal bound does not yield contraction of this parity-conditioned spread from the local coefficient identities. The rectangle itself has zero fresh load because its pair bodies are affinely realizable, so (16)-(17) are not presented as an actual hard-branch counterexample. They identify exactly the additional old-class-mean information that a proof for the actual hard system would have to supply.

The same arithmetic test also quantifies why removed-load location cannot be discarded. Remove only weight \(\varepsilon\in(0,1/4]\) from one edge whose midpoint is \(L+4\). The old raw output mean is \(\mu_q=(L+7)/2\), whereas the retained raw output mean is
\[

=-\frac{\varepsilon(L+1)}{2(3-\varepsilon)}.
\tag{18}
\]
This follows by subtracting \(\varepsilon(L+4)\) from the first moment and \(\varepsilon\) from its mass three. Its absolute value is unbounded as \(L\) grows for any fixed arbitrarily small positive \(\varepsilon\). The removed second moment about zero is \(\varepsilon(L+4)^2\). These are exact coefficient computations. They do not assert that this removal is a dual optimum; they show that a mass-only error estimate cannot replace the location-dependent terms in (11) without further information about the actual optimizer.

For the integer support potential, let \(P=\operatorname{conv}(A)\). A midpoint of two distinct points of \(A\) cannot be an extreme vertex of \(P\). A convex combination of such midpoints cannot equal an extreme vertex either, because extremality would force every midpoint in that combination to equal that vertex. Thus the next convex hull is contained in \(P\) and omits every old extreme vertex. At least one exists and is an integer point, so the finite count \(N\) decreases strictly. A restriction to actual positive fresh loads preserves this containment and vertex omission. This proves the support assertion even when the affine hull has dimension less than \(n\).

Finally, \(\Omega_{\mathrm{new}}\le B_{\mathrm{new}}\le\gamma_dT_g\le\gamma_dT_1\) proves (12). The density packing used at the start was the single original family furnished by dcca4b6d66d80dd4. The fresh theorem 6496cb3b0fd44f9d supplies the domination of (6), not a joint density packing at its physical targets with the same pointwise bound. The present ancestral proof also supplies halfspace domination, not such a packing. A support decrease can therefore be iterated only after establishing the missing fresh admissibility; and even a hypothetical iteration of the stated fresh masses has the upper mass decay (12). The finite number of lattice points in the starting hull has no absolute bound in this problem. Consequently finite support descent plus these per-step estimates gives no absolute retained-mass floor, and no final depth conclusion follows. This is the unresolved descent, rather than an identification of fresh and ancestral points.
