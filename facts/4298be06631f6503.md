## Statement

Retain one common planar instance to which accepted facts `21e1ba1c6a54236b` and `c838ea042e7a92dc` both apply.  Thus \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) is a convex body of positive mixed volume,

\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{area}(C_z)>0,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]

and one has a saturated maximum-matching selected profile, an admissible integral center \(m\), an empty degree-saturated core, and a support-minimal balanced negative circuit of cardinality three.  Assume that every positive lattice fiber is a translate of a positive homothet of one triangle and that the three circuit normals are its side normals, so this is the complete triangular-homothetic setting of `21e1ba1c6a54236b`.  Let \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\) be the same descendant-retention capacity in both accepted facts.  Fix \(\eta\in(0,1]\) and assume the contrary hard-descendant inequalities

\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\leq\frac{\eta}{6}W.
\tag{2}
\]

Assume additionally that the same body and the same quantities \(W,G,D\) lie in the canonical hard branch of accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`.  Thus its half-strength canonical product reservoir has mass \(Q>0\), its other canonical capacities are nonnegative, and

\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 :=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
   +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D
 <\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 Q\leq\frac W8.
\tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{148}{1053}\eta+\frac GW<\frac1{800},}
\tag{4}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac1{2^n+1}\leq\frac GW<\frac{351}{517600},}
\tag{5}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac6{2^n+1}\leq\eta
 <\frac{1053}{148}
 \left(\frac1{800}-\frac1{2^n+1}\right)
 <\frac{1053}{118400}.}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{n\geq11.}
\tag{7}
\]

Equivalently, under the additional accepted canonical hard hypothesis (3), the registered implication

\[
 \left[
 G\leq\frac{\eta}{6}W,
 \quad \mathcal K_m(t)=\varnothing,
 \quad r=3
 \right]
 \Longrightarrow
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)>(1-\eta)W
\tag{8}
\]

holds for every triangular-homothetic instance with \(n\leq10\).  In dimensions \(n\geq11\), accepted facts `21e1ba1c6a54236b`, `7ec63df101c30343`, and `c838ea042e7a92dc` alone give the survivor window (5)-(6), not the full implication (8).

## Proof

All six summands in the hard ledger (3) are nonnegative.  Retaining only the two distinct summands \(D\) and \(G\), and then using the reservoir comparison in (3), gives

\[
 D+G<\frac Q{100}\leq\frac W{800}.
\tag{9}
\]

No two lower bounds for the same capacity are added here: \(D\) and \(G\) are separate literal summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\).

Under (2), the direct physical-source estimate of accepted fact `21e1ba1c6a54236b` is

\[
 D\geq\frac{148}{1053}\eta W.
\tag{10}
\]

Substitution of (10) into (9), followed by division by \(W>0\), proves (4).

Accepted fact `c838ea042e7a92dc` applies to the same saturated empty-core support-minimal three-circuit and gives

\[
 \frac GW\geq\frac1{2^n+1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Combining (11) with the second inequality in (2) yields

\[
 \eta\geq6\frac GW\geq\frac6{2^n+1}.
\tag{12}
\]

Equations (10) and (12) also give a useful lower bound for \(D\) in terms of the distinct core summand:

\[
 D
 \geq\frac{148}{1053}\eta W
 \geq\frac{888}{1053}G
 =\frac{296}{351}G.
\tag{13}
\]

Therefore (9) and (13) imply

\[
 \frac{647}{351}G
 =G+\frac{296}{351}G
 \leq G+D
 <\frac W{800}.
\tag{14}
\]

Rearranging (14) proves the strict upper bound in (5), since

\[
 \frac{351}{647\cdot800}=\frac{351}{517600}.
\tag{15}
\]

The lower bound in (5) is (11).

For the sharper gap window, combine (4) with (11):

\[
 \frac{148}{1053}\eta+\frac1{2^n+1}
 \leq
 \frac{148}{1053}\eta+\frac GW
 <\frac1{800}.
\tag{16}
\]

Rearranging (16) gives the middle strict inequality in (6), while (12) gives its lower inequality.  Since \(1/(2^n+1)>0\), dropping this positive term from the right side of the middle bound gives

\[
 \eta<\frac{1053}{148}\frac1{800}
 =\frac{1053}{118400},
\tag{17}
\]

which is the last inequality in (6).

It remains to prove the integral dimension consequence.  Combining (11) with the strict upper bound in (5) gives

\[
 \frac1{2^n+1}<\frac{351}{517600},
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
 351(2^n+1)>517600.
\tag{18}
\]

If \(n\leq10\), then \(2^n+1\leq1025\), whereas

\[
 351\cdot1025=359775<517600,
\tag{19}
\]

contradicting (18).  Thus \(n\geq11\), proving (7).  Consequently the contrary assumptions (2) cannot hold in the canonical hard branch when \(n\leq10\), which is exactly (8) on that range.  For \(n\geq11\), the intervals in (5)-(6) are nonempty for some parameters, so this arithmetic composition does not silently claim the remaining implication.
