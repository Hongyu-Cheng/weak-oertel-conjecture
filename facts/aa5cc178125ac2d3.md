## Statement

All logarithms are natural unless the base is displayed. For every integer \(d\geq0\), let \(n=d+1\), so that \(n\) is the number of coordinates of the ordinary simplex \(\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:u_i\geq0,\ \sum_{i=0}^d u_i=1\}\). Let \(\nu_d\) be its normalized \(d\)-dimensional volume measure; for \(d=0\), use the point mass. Set \(H(u)=-\sum_{i=0}^d u_i\log u_i\), with \(0\log0=0\). For every real \(\alpha\geq0\), define the probability measure \(\mu_{d,\alpha}\) by

\[
\frac{d\mu_{d,\alpha}}{d\nu_d}(u)
=\frac{\exp(-\alpha H(u))}{\int_{\Delta_d}\exp(-\alpha H(v))\,d\nu_d(v)}.
\]

Its centroid is \(\bar u=(1/n,\ldots,1/n)\). For every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), the closed cap \(A_a=\{u\in\Delta_d:a\cdot(u-\bar u)\geq0\}\) satisfies

\[
\mu_{d,\alpha}(A_a)\ \geq\ \frac{e^{-8}}{36}\,2^{-\alpha}.
\]

Consequently the same bound holds for the intersection with \(\Delta_d\) of any closed halfspace containing \(\bar u\). For \(d\geq1\), the positive continuous density \(f=d\mu_{d,\alpha}/d\nu_d\), evaluated also on the boundary by the displayed formula, satisfies \(f((u+v)/2)\geq2^{-\alpha}\sqrt{f(u)f(v)}\) for every \(u,v\in\Delta_d\); the constant \(2^{-\alpha}\) in that midpoint inequality is optimal.

## Proof

For \(n=1\), every cap in the statement contains the sole point and has mass one. Assume throughout the remainder that \(n\geq2\). The density and reference volume are invariant under every permutation of coordinates. Thus every coordinate of the centroid of \(\mu_{d,\alpha}\) is equal, and their sum is one, proving the stated centroid.

First we establish an elementary uniform-simplex cap bound. Let \(E_0,\ldots,E_d\) be independent exponential random variables of mean one, put \(T=\sum_i E_i\), and let \(U_i=E_i/T\). The change of variables \(E_i=t u_i\), using \(u_0,\ldots,u_{d-1}\) as simplex coordinates, has Jacobian \(t^{n-1}\). The joint exponential density becomes \(e^{-t}t^{n-1}\), independently of \(u\). Therefore \(U\) has law \(\nu_d\).

For the given normal \(a\), put \(b_i=a_i-n^{-1}\sum_j a_j\), so that \(\sum_i b_i=0\) and \(A_a=\{u:b\cdot u\geq0\}\). If every \(b_i=0\), this is the entire simplex. Otherwise let \(Y=\sum_i b_i(E_i-1)\) and \(\sigma^2=\sum_i b_i^2>0\). Since \(T>0\), the event \(U\in A_a\) is exactly \(Y\geq0\). Integration of the exponential density gives \(\mathbb E(E_i-1)=0\), \(\mathbb E(E_i-1)^2=1\), and \(\mathbb E(E_i-1)^4=9\). Independence consequently gives

\[
\mathbb EY=0,\qquad \mathbb EY^2=\sigma^2,\qquad
\mathbb EY^4=3\sigma^4+6\sum_i b_i^4\leq9\sigma^4.
\]

Hölder's inequality, applied to \(|Y|^{2/3}|Y|^{4/3}\), gives \(\mathbb EY^2\leq(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Y|^4)^{1/3}\), and hence \(\mathbb E|Y|\geq\sigma/3\). With \(Y_+=\max(Y,0)\), the mean-zero identity gives \(\mathbb EY_+=\mathbb E|Y|/2\geq\sigma/6\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(\mathbb EY_+\leq\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(Y\geq0)}\). It follows that

\[
\nu_d(A_a)\geq\frac1{36}.
\tag{1}
\]

We next bound the normalizing effect of the entropy tilt when \(0\leq\alpha\leq\log_2 n\). Define \(D(u)=\log n-H(u)\) and \(X(u)=n\sum_i u_i^2\). On the interior of the simplex, concavity of the logarithm with weights \(u_i\) gives

\[
D(u)=\sum_i u_i\log(nu_i)
\leq\log\!\left(\sum_i u_i(nu_i)\right)=\log X(u).
\tag{2}
\]

Continuity extends (2) to the closed simplex. Also \(X(u)\geq1\) by Cauchy-Schwarz.

The exact uniform-simplex monomial integral, for nonnegative integers \(m_0,\ldots,m_d\), is

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_d}\prod_i U_i^{m_i}
=\frac{(n-1)!\prod_i m_i!}{(n-1+\sum_i m_i)!}.
\tag{3}
\]

For completeness, integrate one simplex coordinate at a time and rescale the remaining coordinates by its complement. The resulting one-dimensional integrals have the form \(\int_0^1 t^p(1-t)^q\,dt=p!q!/(p+q+1)!\), which follows by repeated integration by parts. These factors telescope to (3); multiplication by \((n-1)!\), the normalizing density of uniform simplex coordinates, gives the stated expectation.

For an integer \(k\geq0\), let

\[
F_k=\sum_{(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\in\{0,\ldots,d\}^k}
\prod_{i=0}^d (2r_i)!,
\]

where \(r_i\) is the number of occurrences of \(i\) in the tuple, and set \(F_0=1\). Expanding the power of \(\sum_i U_i^2\) and applying (3) gives \(\mathbb E_{\nu_d}X^k=n^kF_k/(n)_{2k}\), where \((n)_{2k}=n(n+1)\cdots(n+2k-1)\) and \((n)_0=1\). Appending the coordinate \(i\) to a tuple multiplies its summand in \(F_k\) by \((2r_i+1)(2r_i+2)\). Since \(\sum_i r_i=k\) and \(\sum_i r_i^2\leq k^2\), the sum of these multipliers is

\[
\sum_i(2r_i+1)(2r_i+2)
=2n+6k+4\sum_i r_i^2\leq2n+6k+4k^2.
\]

Thus \(F_{k+1}\leq(2n+6k+4k^2)F_k\). Using \((n)_{2k}\geq n^{2k}\) and \(1+t\leq e^t\), for every integer \(k\geq1\) we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\nu_d}X^k
&\leq2^k\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{2j^2+3j}{n}\right)\\
&\leq2^k\exp\!\left(\frac{k(k-1)(4k+7)}{6n}\right)
\leq2^k\exp(k^3/n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The last inequality holds because \(6k^3-k(k-1)(4k+7)=k(2k^2-3k+7)>0\).

For \(0<\alpha\leq\log_2 n\), choose \(k=\lceil\alpha\rceil\). Then \(n\geq2^{k-1}\), and \(k^3/n\leq k^3/2^{k-1}\leq8\). To verify the latter bound, the values for \(k=1,2,3,4\) are \(1,4,27/4,8\), while for \(k\geq4\) the ratio of the next value to the current value is \((1+1/k)^3/2\leq125/128<1\). Jensen's inequality for the concave power \(t^{\alpha/k}\), followed by (2) and (4), now gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_d}e^{\alpha D}
\leq\mathbb E_{\nu_d}X^\alpha
\leq(\mathbb E_{\nu_d}X^k)^{\alpha/k}
\leq2^\alpha e^{8\alpha/k}\leq e^8 2^\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(\alpha=0\), (5) also holds directly. The entropy bound \(H(u)\leq\log n\) follows from concavity of \(-t\log t\) applied to the \(n\) coordinates; therefore \(D(u)\geq0\). Since \(e^{-\alpha H}=n^{-\alpha}e^{\alpha D}\), equations (1) and (5) yield

\[
\mu_{d,\alpha}(A_a)
=\frac{\mathbb E_{\nu_d}[\mathbf1_{A_a}e^{\alpha D}]}{\mathbb E_{\nu_d}e^{\alpha D}}
\geq\frac{\nu_d(A_a)}{e^8 2^\alpha}
\geq\frac{e^{-8}}{36}\,2^{-\alpha}
\]

throughout \(0\leq\alpha\leq\log_2 n\).

It remains to handle \(\alpha\geq\log_2 n\). Let \(R\) cyclically permute the \(n\) coordinates. For each \(u\in\Delta_d\), one has \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b\cdot R^j u=\sum_i b_i=0\). At least one of these evaluations is nonnegative, including the possible case in which all are zero. Hence \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf1_{A_a}(R^j u)\geq1\). Integrating against the permutation-invariant measure \(\mu_{d,\alpha}\) gives \(n\mu_{d,\alpha}(A_a)\geq1\). Since \(1/n\geq2^{-\alpha}\) in the present regime, this proves the required cap bound for all remaining \(\alpha\). A closed halfspace containing \(\bar u\) contains the parallel closed halfspace with boundary through \(\bar u\), so the asserted extension follows.

Finally, concavity of entropy gives \(H((u+v)/2)-(H(u)+H(v))/2\geq0\). Expanding that difference gives

\[
H((u+v)/2)-\frac{H(u)+H(v)}2
=\frac12\sum_i u_i\log\frac{2u_i}{u_i+v_i}
+\frac12\sum_i v_i\log\frac{2v_i}{u_i+v_i}
\leq\log2.
\]

Terms with zero prefactors are defined by continuity. The upper bound follows because each ratio with a positive prefactor is at most two and \(\sum_i u_i=\sum_i v_i=1\). Exponentiating after multiplication by \(-\alpha\), and canceling the common density normalization, proves the midpoint inequality. Two distinct simplex vertices have entropy zero and their midpoint has entropy \(\log2\), giving equality in the density ratio. Thus its defect constant is optimal. Every auxiliary fact used in the proof has been established here.
