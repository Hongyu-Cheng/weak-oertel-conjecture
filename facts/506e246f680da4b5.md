## Statement

For a positive log-concave probability density on $[0,\infty)$, the associated variable $X$ satisfies $\mathbb E X^2\le 2(\mathbb E X)^2$, with equality exactly for an exponential law. Let $h:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous, integrable and log-concave with $h(0)=1$. For $a>0$ put
$$
M_r(a)=\int_0^\infty t^r e^{-t/a}h(t)\,dt,\qquad \psi(a)=a/M_0(a).
$$
Then $\psi(0+)=1$ and $\psi''(a)=[2M_1(a)^2-M_0(a)M_2(a)]/[a^3M_0(a)^3]\ge0$. Moreover $\psi(a)\ge1$ implies $\psi'(a)>0$.

## Proof

For the density $k$ of $X$, write $S(t)=\int_t^\infty k(x)\,dx$. The ratio $k(t+s)/k(t)$ is nonincreasing in $t>0$ for each $s\ge0$ by concavity of $\log k$. Integration in $s$ shows that $S(t)/k(t)$ is nonincreasing. Thus the hazard $k/S$ is nondecreasing and integrating $-(\log S)'=k/S$, with $S(0)=1$, gives $S(s+t)\le S(s)S(t)$. An integrable positive log-concave density has an exponential upper bound in the tail by the secant inequality for its logarithm, so both moments are finite. By Tonelli,
$$
\frac{\mathbb E X^2}{2}=\int_0^\infty t S(t)\,dt
=\int_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty S(s+t)\,ds\,dt
\le\left(\int_0^\infty S(t)\,dt\right)^2=(\mathbb E X)^2.
$$
Equality forces the continuous nonnegative difference $S(s)S(t)-S(s+t)$ to vanish everywhere, and the continuous positive solutions with $S(0)=1$ are $S(t)=e^{-\beta t}$. Integrability forces $\beta>0$, and these laws attain equality.

Differentiation under the integral is justified by exponential domination for $a$ on compact positive intervals. Using $M_r'(a)=M_{r+1}(a)/a^2$ gives the displayed formula for $\psi''$, whose sign follows from the moment inequality applied to $e^{-t/a}h(t)/M_0(a)$. The function $h$ is bounded by continuity and its exponential tail bound. Thus $M_0(a)/a=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}h(as)\,ds\to1$. If $\psi''$ vanishes anywhere, the equality case of the moment inequality makes $h(t)=e^{-\alpha t}$, with $\alpha>0$ by integrability, and then $\psi(a)=1+\alpha a$. Otherwise $\psi''>0$ everywhere and the strict secant inequality gives $\psi'(a)>(\psi(a)-1)/a$.
