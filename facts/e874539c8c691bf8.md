## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$ and let a compact convex set in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ have positive-volume fibers $A_1,\ldots,A_k$ at integer bases $z_1,\ldots,z_k$, with $k\ge2$ and $m=k^{-1}\sum_j z_j\in\mathbb Z^n$. Let $K$ be its actual $m$-fiber, with volume $b>0$ and volume centroid $g_K$. Consider couplings whose marginals $\sigma_j\le \mathbf1_{A_j}\,dx$ all have mass $t$, and whose arithmetic-mean pushforward satisfies $\eta\le\mathbf1_K\,dx$. Let $Y$ be the set of centroids of $\mathbf1_K\,dx+k\eta$ over all such couplings, including the zero coupling. For $t>0$ put $Z_t(A)=\{t^{-1}\int x\,d\sigma:0\le\sigma\le\mathbf1_A\,dx,\ \sigma(A)=t\}$.

Then $Y$ is compact, convex, and contained in $K$. Its part obtainable at fixed mass $t>0$ is exactly
$$
 Y_t=\frac{b g_K+tZ_t(A_1)+\cdots+tZ_t(A_k)}{b+kt},
 \qquad 0<t\le\min_j|A_j|,
$$
and at $t=0$ it is $\{g_K\}$. At fixed marginals the center does not depend on the coupling. For every $u\ne0$, $h_Y(u)=\max_{y\in Y}u\cdot y$ is the unique real root in $h$ of
$$
 D_u(h)=b(u\cdot g_K-h)+
 \min_{\sum_j v_j=0}\sum_{j=1}^k \int_{A_j}(u\cdot x-h+v_j)_+\,dx.
$$
The inner minimum is attained at finite multipliers. At any minimizer the $k$ selected strict positive-part caps have equal mass, and their volume restrictions maximize the corresponding primal equal-mass objective. The outer function is continuous and $D_u(h_2)\le D_u(h_1)-b(h_2-h_1)$ for $h_2>h_1$. For $u=0$ the support is zero. In particular for $k=2$ the multiplier vector is $(v,-v)$ and the same formulas give the exact two-source center set and its one-scalar inner minimum.

For every closed ambient halfspace $H$, write $H_z=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}$. Every feasible coupling gives $\eta(H_m)\le\sum_j \sigma_j(H_{z_j})$. When the source bases are pairwise distinct and different from $m$, $|K\cap H_m|+\eta(H_m)$ is therefore a lower bound for the original mixed cap mass.

## Proof

First fix any dominated marginals with common mass $t>0$. Normalize them to compactly supported probability densities bounded by $1/t$. The proof of dfd25e72c1f45a4e uses an actual displacement map for the specified two densities, with their exact marginals; its density estimate shows that any displacement interpolation between two such densities also has bound $1/t$. Starting with the first normalized source variable, suppose a joint law of the first $j$ sources has average $\bar X_j$ with density bounded by $1/t$. Apply that displacement construction from its law to the next source law and set $X_{j+1}$ equal to the transport map evaluated at $\bar X_j$. All preceding marginals persist, the next is exact, and $\bar X_{j+1}$ is the displacement interpolant at weight $1/(j+1)$. This gives the joint probability coupling by induction. Its average belongs to $k^{-1}\sum_j A_j\subseteq K$ by actual convexity. Multiplying the joint law by $t$ gives the required dominated midpoint measure with the prescribed marginals.

Linearity of the midpoint map gives $k\int x\,d\eta=\sum_j\int x\,d\sigma_j$ for every coupling. This and the existence just proved give the fixed-mass formula in both directions. The feasible couplings form a convex and weakly compact set: their joint support is compact, masses are uniformly bounded, and each marginal or pushforward domination is closed when tested with nonnegative continuous functions. The center map is continuous because its denominator $b+kt$ is bounded below by $b$. The centers lie in $K$ because the measures giving them are supported there. If two feasible couplings give $y_1,y_2$ and denominators $M_i=b+kt_i$, a mixture with weight $\lambda M_2/[(1-\lambda)M_1+\lambda M_2]$ on the first coupling has center $\lambda y_1+(1-\lambda)y_2$. This proves convexity with variable denominators.

Fix $u\ne0$ and $h\in\mathbb R$. The inner hinge sum is convex and continuously differentiable in the multiplier coordinates: the $j$th derivative is the volume of $A_j\cap\{u\cdot x>h-v_j\}$, by dominated convergence and nullity of each boundary hyperplane. On the zero-sum hyperplane, an unbounded sequence of multipliers has unbounded maximum positive entry, along any subsequence whose norm tends to infinity. Compact source supports and positive source volumes then force the sum to infinity, so a minimizer exists. Its gradient is constant across the coordinates by stationarity on the zero-sum hyperplane. Thus the selected strict positive-part caps have equal masses, including the zero and full cap possibilities. For any other equal-mass dominated marginals, the multipliers cancel from the sum of their integrals, and the integral in coordinate $j$ is at most the hinge integral on $A_j$. The selected restrictions attain all these bounds simultaneously and admit the required actual coupling. Therefore
$$
 D_u(h)=\max_{\text{feasible couplings}}(b+kt)(u\cdot y-h).
$$
The maximum is continuous in $h$ (the slopes are uniformly bounded) and decreases at least at rate $b$. At the maximum of $u\cdot y$ it is exactly zero, proving the root claim and uniqueness.

Finally an affine margin at the arithmetic mean of the lifted endpoints is the mean of their margins. If it is nonnegative at least one source margin is nonnegative. Integrating this indicator implication yields the one-copy budget for $\eta$, also on closed boundaries. Summing it with the middle volume does not reuse any physical fiber under the stated distinctness condition. The same calculation applies to a two-source midpoint in arbitrary integer ambient dimension.
