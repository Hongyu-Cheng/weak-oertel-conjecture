## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, and suppose it contains the midpoint of every pair of its points of equal coordinate parity. If $I\subseteq P$ meets every parity occupied by $P$, then $\operatorname{aff}I=\operatorname{aff}P$. In particular, every affine function vanishing on $I$ vanishes on $P$.

## Proof

For each $z\in P$ fix a point $p(z)\in I$ of the same parity. Then $T(z)=(z+p(z))/2$ belongs to $P$. Since $p(z)\in\operatorname{aff}I$, orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of the direction space of $\operatorname{aff}I$ gives $\operatorname{dist}(T(z),\operatorname{aff}I)=\operatorname{dist}(z,\operatorname{aff}I)/2$. A positive initial distance would give infinitely many distinct positive distances along the iterates, impossible for finite $P$. Thus $P\subseteq\operatorname{aff}I$, and the reverse inclusion of affine hulls follows from $I\subseteq P$. The zero set of an affine function containing $I$ contains its affine hull.
