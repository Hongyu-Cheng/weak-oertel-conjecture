## Statement

Let \(m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let \(a,b,Q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) satisfy \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(Q\ge2ab\), and let \(c_i>0\), \(\theta_i\le0\) for \(1\le i\le m\) and \(\lambda\ge0\). Keep the original support, original normalizer, and original law of accepted fact `08d22fb76700c3e2`:
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf z,x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:\sum_{i=1}^m c_i z_i+ax+by\le Q\right\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{(\mathbf z,x,y)\in D}e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i+\lambda(ax+by)},\qquad
p(\mathbf z,x,y)=Z^{-1}e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i+\lambda(ax+by)}.
\]
For an original atom \(j\in D\), define \(\tau_p(j)\) as the infimum of \(p(D\cap H)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+2}\) containing \(j\), and define \(M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3\). Boundary atoms are included in full. Empty light sums and products have their usual meanings.

Use \(\alpha,c_{\mathrm L},B_{\mathrm L}\) for the constants called \(\alpha,c,B\) in accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`. Explicitly, with natural logarithms, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=(8/27)^{3/2},&
\beta&=20+\log(48\cdot2048^4),&
c_{\mathrm L}&=e^{-(20+\beta)}/128,&
B_{\mathrm L}&=2048\beta+144,\\
\delta&=\log(8\alpha),&
T&=\left\lceil\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{B_{\mathrm L}+8}{\delta}\right)^2\right\}\right\rceil,&
K&=\frac{e^{-16}c_{\mathrm L}}{256\cdot300^4},&
\kappa&=\min\left\{64K,\frac1{64\cdot8^{T+1}}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(\delta>0\), \(\kappa>0\), and for every stated input
\(M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(m+2)}\).
More precisely, when \(m\ge1\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge K(m+1)^{-8}e^{-B_{\mathrm L}\sqrt m}\alpha^m.
\tag{1}
\]
The support, normalizer, and averaging law in these conclusions remain the full original \(D,Z,p\). No original atom is removed or assigned a new probability. The complete uniform heavy-support estimate is supplied by accepted fact `01a4100542206b94`; the product-cap mechanism of accepted fact `e3fd8ac258382c95` is proved below for a two-dimensional second factor, rather than inferred from that fact's scalar statement. None of the auxiliary parameters here is identified with the locked problem's \(n,d,C,S,h_S\).

## Proof

All costs are positive, so \(D\) is finite, contains the origin, and has \(Z>0\). It is complete: if \(P\) is the real nonnegative resource simplex defining \(D\), then \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^{m+2}\) and \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq P\), whence \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^{m+2}\).

First select the band given by accepted fact `08d22fb76700c3e2`. It supplies represented endpoint heights \(0\le q_0\le q_1\le Q\) and
\[
E=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:q_0\le ax+by\le q_1\},\qquad
N=|E|>0,\qquad R=Q-q_1\ge0.
\]
Here \(N\) is exactly the heavy-band cardinality in that predecessor, not an ambient dimension. Write \(u_E\) for its uniform heavy law and retain the predecessor's light law
\[
Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\\\sum_i c_i z_i\le R}}e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i},\qquad
Q_R(\mathbf z)=\frac{e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i}}{Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)}
\quad\left(\sum_i c_i z_i\le R\right).
\]
Define the comparison product law \(q=Q_R\otimes u_E\), extended by zero outside its product support. This support lies in the original \(D\), since its resource sum is at most \(R+q_1=Q\). The accepted comparison is
\[
p_j\ge\eta q_j\quad(j\in D),\qquad
\eta=\frac{N Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda q_0}}Z
\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{300(m+1)^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus its denominator remains the full original \(Z\); no restriction or renormalization by heavy height is made.

The heavy support is complete in the ambient lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\). Indeed, let \(P_E=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2:q_0\le ax+by\le q_1\}\). Positivity of \(a,b\) and finiteness of \(q_1\) make \(P_E\) compact; \(E=P_E\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Hence \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\subseteq P_E\) implies \(E=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\). The uniform case of accepted fact `01a4100542206b94`, with its ambient dimension equal to two and its moment exponent equal to three, gives \(M_3(u_E)\ge2^{-6}/4=1/256\). That theorem explicitly allows lower-dimensional supports, so singleton bands and bands of rank one require no additional assumption.

We next prove the required product-cap assertion in arbitrary factor dimensions. This extends the cap containment used in the scalar-factor accepted fact `e3fd8ac258382c95`; its scalar moment constant is not used for \(u_E\). Let \(\mu,\nu\) be finite atomic probability laws on \(\mathbb R^s,\mathbb R^t\), respectively, with the same normalized closed-halfspace depth convention. Every proper closed halfspace containing \((v,w)\) contains a parallel halfspace through \((v,w)\), obtained by moving its boundary to that point. A through-point halfspace has the form
\[
H=\{(v',w'):A\cdot(v'-v)+B\cdot(w'-w)\ge0\},\qquad (A,B)\ne0.
\]
Set \(L_A=\{v':A\cdot(v'-v)\ge0\}\) and \(L_B=\{w':B\cdot(w'-w)\ge0\}\). When one normal is zero, its set is the entire corresponding space and has probability one. Otherwise it is a closed halfspace through the corresponding root. In either case \(\mu(L_A)\ge\tau_\mu(v)\) and \(\nu(L_B)\ge\tau_\nu(w)\). Each summand defining \(H\) is nonnegative on \(L_A\times L_B\), so that product is contained in \(H\), including all equality cases. Independence therefore gives \((\mu\otimes\nu)(H)\ge\tau_\mu(v)\tau_\nu(w)\). Taking the infimum over through-point halfspaces, cubing, and summing with the product probabilities proves
\[
\tau_{\mu\otimes\nu}(v,w)\ge\tau_\mu(v)\tau_\nu(w),\qquad
M_3(\mu\otimes\nu)\ge M_3(\mu)M_3(\nu).
\tag{3}
\]
For a zero-dimensional factor its unique point has depth one, and the identical argument uses the whole singleton as its cap. Thus (3) also covers \(m=0\) when applied to \(Q_R,u_E\).

For each root in the product support, every containing closed halfspace satisfies \(p(H)\ge\eta q(H)\) by (2). Infima give \(\tau_p(j)\ge\eta\tau_q(j)\). Discarding only nonnegative moment summands outside the product support and using the pointwise root-probability comparison again yields
\[
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}q}p_j\tau_p(j)^3
\ge\eta^4 M_3(q)
\ge\frac{\eta^4}{256}M_3(Q_R).
\tag{4}
\]
The first \(\eta\) factor comes from the original root weight; the other three come from original halfspace depth. The laws \(Q_R,u_E,q\) are comparison laws only and never replace \(p\) in the claimed moment.

If \(m\ge1\), accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816` applies to \(Q_R\) by the exact aliases: its dimension is \(m\), its positive real costs are \(c_i\), its capacity is \(R\), its scores are \(\theta_i\le0\), and its original support and probabilities are precisely those defining \(Q_R\) above. It includes \(R=0\) and zero scores. Its strong bound is \(M_3(Q_R)\ge c_{\mathrm L}e^{-B_{\mathrm L}\sqrt m}\alpha^m\). Substituting this and (2) into (4) proves (1). When \(m=0\), the light law is the singleton law with moment one; (2)-(4) instead give \(M_3(p)\ge e^{-16}/(256\cdot300^4)\). In particular, no uniform-law claim is made for the positively tilted original heavy law.

We prove an intrinsic parity bound for the finite range directly, allowing arbitrary affine scores. In the original \(D\), a light coordinate with \(c_i>Q\) vanishes at every atom. Conversely \(c_i\le Q\) makes its unit coordinate vector feasible. Also the two heavy unit vectors are feasible because \(Q\ge2ab\ge\max\{a,b\}\). Thus the affine hull is the coordinate subspace of dimension \(r=2+\#\{i:c_i\le Q\}\le m+2\). In this paragraph identify each original atom with its \(r\) remaining coordinates. This is a bijection retaining each original \(p_j\) and the same original \(Z\), without any conditioning. The resulting support is complete in \(\mathbb Z^r\). Its halfspace depths equal the original depths: restricting an ambient halfspace gives a containing halfspace in this coordinate subspace, or the entire support if its restricted normal is zero; every halfspace with nonzero restricted normal lifts by putting zeros in the inactive coordinates. The entire-support mass is one and cannot lower the infimum.

For an original root \(j\), let \(F_j=D\cap(2j-D)\), \(k_j=|F_j|\), and \(\mathsf H_j=k_jp_j\), including zero displacement. Log-affinity of the original probabilities gives \(\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=p_j\) for every \(x\in F_j\), regardless of the signs of the scores. For a closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(j\), at least one endpoint of each reflected pair lies in \(H\). With \(A=F_j\cap H\), reflection and Cauchy-Schwarz imply
\[
\mathsf H_j
=\sum_{x\in F_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}
\le2\sum_{x\in A}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}
\le2\sqrt{p(A)p(2j-A)}
\le2\sqrt{p(H)}.
\]
The singleton \(j\) also satisfies the first inequality. Taking the infimum gives \(\tau_p(j)\ge\mathsf H_j^2/4\).

Partition the original atoms by their parity in the \(r\) active coordinates, with masses \(\pi_\xi\), \(\xi\in\{0,1\}^r\). Every oriented same-parity pair \((x,y)\) has an integer midpoint \(j=(x+y)/2\) in the original support, by convexity and completeness, and determines one summand in \(k_j\); the converse holds as well. Since \(p_j^2=p_xp_y\), this exact enumeration gives
\[
\sum_{j\in D}p_j\mathsf H_j
=\sum_{j\in D}k_jp_j^2
=\sum_{\xi\in\{0,1\}^r}\pi_\xi^2
\ge2^{-r}.
\]
The final inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz and uses \(\sum_\xi\pi_\xi=1\). Jensen for the sixth power now proves, always with the original root law,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}\sum_{j\in D}p_j\mathsf H_j^6
\ge\frac1{64}\left(\sum_{j\in D}p_j\mathsf H_j\right)^6
\ge\frac1{64}64^{-r}
\ge\frac1{64}64^{-(m+2)}.
\tag{5}
\]
This includes \(m=0\), inactive light coordinates, and arbitrary real affine scores in the displayed original law; the decreasing-light hypothesis was not used in (5).

It remains to absorb the losses with explicit absolute constants. One has \(\alpha^2=512/19683>1/64\), because \(32768>19683\), so \(\delta=\log(8\alpha)>0\). For every \(m\ge0\),
\(\log(m+1)\le\sqrt m\): integrate \(2t/(1+t^2)\le1\) from zero to \(\sqrt m\), using \(2t\le1+t^2\). For \(m\ge T\), the definition of \(T\) implies \(\delta m\ge(B_{\mathrm L}+8)\sqrt m\). Therefore (1) and \(\alpha^m=8^{-m}e^{\delta m}\) give
\[
M_3(p)\ge K8^{-m}
e^{\delta m-B_{\mathrm L}\sqrt m-8\log(m+1)}
\ge K8^{-m}
=64K8^{-(m+2)}.
\]
For the remaining integers \(0\le m<T\), one has \(m+2\le T+1\), so (5) gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-(m+2)}}{64\cdot8^{m+2}}
\ge\frac{8^{-(m+2)}}{64\cdot8^{T+1}}.
\]
Both ranges imply the claimed bound with the displayed \(\kappa>0\). All constants are independent of \(m,a,b,Q,c_i,\theta_i,\lambda\). The band selection, product comparison, intrinsic parity estimate, and final moment all retain the original \(D,Z,p\), completing this auxiliary-class theorem.
