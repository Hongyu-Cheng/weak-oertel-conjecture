## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\tag{1}
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), let
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in H\},
\tag{2}
\]
and put
\[
 G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\tag{3}
\]
with the exact complete metronoid-core normalization used in accepted facts `fbeb0a501b268d12` and `e9d321d26b2e51c2`. For every \(z\in\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C\), define the attained maximum depth root
\[
 \rho_C(z):=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}.
\tag{4}
\]
The maximum in (4) exists by accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, including when \(C_z\) has zero \(d\)-volume, in which case \(\rho_C(z)=0\).

Fix \(k\geq1\). For \(1\leq i\leq k\), let \(r_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
 \Delta_i=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{i,0},\ldots,v_{i,r_i}\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^{r_i}
\tag{5}
\]
be a full-dimensional lattice simplex, let \(D_i:=\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i}\), and let \(\lambda_{i,j}\) be the barycentric coordinate of \(v_{i,j}\) on \(\Delta_i\). Put \(q:=\sum_i r_i\leq n\), and fix an integral affine injection
\[
 \Phi(t_1,\ldots,t_k)
 :=b+\sum_{i=1}^kU_it_i,
 \qquad b\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad U_i\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r_i},
\tag{6}
\]
whose concatenated matrix \([U_1\ \cdots\ U_k]\) has rank \(q\). Assume that every product vertex base
\[
 z_{j_1,\ldots,j_k}
 :=\Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k})
\tag{7}
\]
belongs to \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C\).

For nonnegative factor-vertex values \(\alpha_{i,j}\), define
\[
 g_i(t_i):=\sum_{j=0}^{r_i}\lambda_{i,j}(t_i)\alpha_{i,j},
 \qquad
 A_i:=\sum_{u_i\in D_i}g_i(u_i)^d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(\alpha):=\prod_{i=1}^kA_i.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(\alpha_{i,j}\) is the same object denoted \(a_{i,j}\) in accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`; the new letter avoids collision with the fiber volume \(a_z\) in (1). Define the fixed-chart native scalar capacity by
\[
 \mathscr R_C(\Phi):=
 \max\left\{
 \mathcal E(\alpha):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \alpha_{i,j}\geq0,\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle
 \prod_{i=1}^k\alpha_{i,j_i}
 \leq\rho_C(z_{j_1,\ldots,j_k})
 \text{ for every product vertex }(j_1,\ldots,j_k)
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

The following conclusions hold.

1. Exact fixed-chart scalarization and attainment. The maximum in (9) is attained, satisfies
\[
 0\leq\mathscr R_C(\Phi)\leq W,
\tag{10}
\]
and is exactly the maximum energy of all admissible factorized lattice-polysimplex packets, in the sense of accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`, that use the fixed simplices and chart (5)-(7):
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr R_C(\Phi)
 =\max\{\mathcal E(\Pi):\Pi\text{ is an admissible packet using }\Phi\}.
 }
\tag{11}
\]
Both maxima in (11) are actual maxima, not only suprema. If \(\mathscr R_C(\Phi)>G(C)\), every packet attaining the right side of (11) has a canonical selector that fails to agree with any affine map on all product lattice points of positive packet weight. This last assertion is the contrapositive of accepted fact `e9d321d26b2e51c2`.

2. Pointwise slice and support bounds. Let \(\Pi\) be any admissible packet using (5)-(7), use (8) for its factor data, and suppose \(k\geq2\) and
\[
 E:=\mathcal E(\Pi)=\prod_iA_i>0.
\tag{12}
\]
Put
\[
 \omega_i(u_i):=g_i(u_i)^d,
 \qquad
 p_i(u_i):=\frac{\omega_i(u_i)}{A_i},
 \qquad
 S_i:=\{u_i\in D_i:\omega_i(u_i)>0\},
 \qquad
 s_i:=|S_i|.
\tag{13}
\]
Then, for every factor \(j\) and every fixed tuple \(u_{-j}\in\prod_{i\ne j}D_i\),
\[
 \boxed{
 E\prod_{i\ne j}p_i(u_i)\leq G(C).
 }
\tag{14}
\]
In particular \(G(C)>0\). If \(m_i:=\max_{u_i\in D_i}p_i(u_i)\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 \prod_{i\ne j}m_i\leq\frac{G(C)}E
 \quad(1\leq j\leq k),
 \qquad
 \prod_{i=1}^km_i
 \leq\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{k/(k-1)}.
 }
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \prod_{i\ne j}s_i\geq\frac E{G(C)}
 \quad(1\leq j\leq k),
 \qquad
 \prod_{i=1}^ks_i
 \geq\left(\frac E{G(C)}\right)^{k/(k-1)}.
 }
\tag{16}
\]
These conclusions include zero values of individual \(g_i(u_i)\); only the nonzero total-energy assumption (12) is needed to define the probabilities.

3. Source-level residual-selector improvement. Assume now that \(q=n\), \(k\geq2\), \(W>0\), and that \(C\) satisfies the strict no-rank-drop condition
\[
 W_L<2^{\dim L-n}W
 \quad\text{for every proper primitive affine lattice subspace }
 L\subsetneq\mathbb R^n.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) be the intrinsic residual-selector capacity with the exact definition (including the factor \(2^{n-\rho_E}\)) in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Suppose \(G(C)<W/4\), and let \(\Pi\) be a full-rank packet with \(E>G(C)\). For each factor, let \(\mathsf U_i\) be the minimum unmatched mass obtained by fractional matching of the weights \(\omega_i\) separately within each parity class, and set
\[
 \theta_i:=\frac{\mathsf U_i}{A_i},
 \qquad
 \chi(\Pi):=
 \prod_{i=1}^k(r_i+1)-1-n.
\tag{18}
\]
Then there are distinct factors \(i,j\) such that
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{\chi(\Pi)+1}(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{\chi(\Pi)+1}
 \left[1-\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}\right]^2.
 }
\tag{19}
\]
The estimate (19) uses the source mass before midpoint averaging. It therefore has no factor \(1/2\), unlike the midpoint-capacity estimate in accepted fact `674b50aa42f420d8`.

For integers \(K\geq2\) and \(\Chi\geq0\), define
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^{K,\Chi}(C)
 :=\sup\{\mathcal E(\Pi):
 \Pi\text{ is full-rank},\ 2\leq k(\Pi)\leq K,\
 \chi(\Pi)\leq\Chi\},
\tag{20}
\]
where the supremum of the empty family is zero. Under (17), without assuming \(G(C)<W/4\), one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \max\{G(C),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\}
 \geq c_{K,\Chi}\,\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^{K,\Chi}(C),
 \qquad
 c_{K,\Chi}:=
 \min\left\{2^{-(K-1)},\frac1{4(\Chi+1)}\right\}.
 }
\tag{21}
\]
The constant \(1/[4(\Chi+1)]\) in (21) comes from squaring a bracket strictly larger than \(1/2\).

4. Hard-branch exponential grid obstruction. In addition to (17), assume \(\operatorname{aff}(D_+)=\mathbb R^n\) and, for some \(0<\varepsilon\leq1/64\), the exact hard-branch hypotheses of accepted fact `c1e3b8400d5b5b10`:
\[
 G(C)<\varepsilon W,
 \quad R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\varepsilon W,
 \quad M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)<\varepsilon W,
 \quad R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)<\varepsilon W.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(\Pi\) be any full-rank packet with \(k\geq2\), \(E>0\), and positive supports \(S_i\) as in (13). Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \prod_{i=1}^ks_i
 >\frac{\gamma_dE}{\varepsilon W}\,2^n,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
 }
\tag{23}
\]
In particular, if \(E\geq\lambda W\) for some \(\lambda>0\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 \prod_{i=1}^ks_i
 >\frac{\gamma_d\lambda}{\varepsilon}\,2^n,
 \qquad
 \prod_{i\ne j}s_i>\frac\lambda\varepsilon
 \quad(1\leq j\leq k).
 }
\tag{24}
\]
Thus a macroscopic hard-branch packet cannot be a bounded rectangle or any subexponential positive grid. Moreover, for every \(K\geq2\) and \(\Chi\geq0\), (21)-(22) give
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^{K,\Chi}(C)
 <\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{K,\Chi}}W.
 }
\tag{25}
\]

If a packet has \(E=0\), (10), (11), (14), (19), and (23) reduce to their stated nonnegative or conditional cases; no probability or division by \(E\) is invoked. No conclusion above asserts extraction of a chart satisfying \(E=\Omega(W)\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` proves that \((z,x)\mapsto\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}\) is upper semicontinuous on \(C\), that its maximum over every compact fiber is attained, and that the value is zero on every zero-volume fiber. This proves that (4) is well-defined with the stated boundary convention.

We first prove (10)-(11). Every admissible packet using \(\Phi\) has, at a product vertex \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\),
\[
 \prod_i\alpha_{i,j_i}
 \leq\delta_C(z_{\mathbf j},x_{\mathbf j})^{1/d}
 \leq\rho_C(z_{\mathbf j}).
\tag{26}
\]
Its scalar factor data are therefore feasible in (9), and its energy is at most \(\mathscr R_C(\Phi)\).

Conversely, take any scalar data feasible in (9). At each product vertex choose, using `4e868f774de77608`, a maximizer
\[
 x_{\mathbf j}\in C_{z_{\mathbf j}},
 \qquad
 \delta_C(z_{\mathbf j},x_{\mathbf j})^{1/d}
 =\rho_C(z_{\mathbf j}).
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (9) and (27) are exactly the vertex hypotheses of an admissible packet in accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`, with its factor value \(a_{i,j}\) identified with \(\alpha_{i,j}\). That fact constructs the canonical multiaffine selector \(x(u)\) and proves, for every \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i\),
\[
 x(u)\in C_{\Phi(u)},
 \qquad
 \delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \geq\prod_i g_i(u_i)^d.
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(\Phi\) is injective and \(\delta_C(z,x)\leq a_z\), finite distributivity gives
\[
 \mathcal E(\alpha)
 =\sum_{u\in\prod_iD_i}\prod_i g_i(u_i)^d
 \leq\sum_{u\in\prod_iD_i}a_{\Phi(u)}
 \leq W.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus every feasible scalar energy is realized by a packet and is at most \(W\), while (26) gives the reverse comparison of the two optimization problems.

It remains to show that the scalar supremum is attained. If its supremum is zero, the identically zero factor data attain it. For \(k=1\), feasibility directly gives
\[
 0\leq\alpha_{1,j}\leq\rho_C(z_j)
\tag{30}
\]
for every vertex, so the feasible set is compact and the continuous objective attains its maximum.

Suppose \(k\geq2\) and the supremum is positive. Choose a feasible maximizing sequence with positive energy. For every member of the sequence and every factor put
\[
 M_i:=\max_j\alpha_{i,j}>0.
\]
Rescale its factors by
\[
 \widetilde\alpha_{i,j}:=M_i^{-1}\alpha_{i,j}\quad(i<k),
 \qquad
 \widetilde\alpha_{k,j}:=\left(\prod_{i<k}M_i\right)\alpha_{k,j}.
\tag{31}
\]
The product of the \(k\) scaling factors is one. Hence (31) preserves every corner product and preserves the energy, because scaling the \(i\)-th factor values by \(t_i\) scales \(A_i\) by \(t_i^d\). For \(i<k\), all normalized values lie in \([0,1]\) and their maximum is one. After passage to a subsequence, choose fixed indices \(j_i^*\) at which these maxima are attained. For every last-factor vertex \(j\), feasibility gives
\[
 0\leq\widetilde\alpha_{k,j}
 \leq
 \rho_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1^*},\ldots,v_{k-1,j_{k-1}^*},v_{k,j})
 \right).
\tag{32}
\]
The finitely many right sides are finite. Thus the normalized maximizing sequence lies in a compact box. The corner constraints are closed and the energy is continuous, so a convergent subsequence attains the scalar supremum. Choosing the corner maximizers (27) produces a packet attaining the same value. This proves (10)-(11), including all zero cases. If this value is greater than \(G(C)\), accepted fact `e9d321d26b2e51c2` rules out an affine canonical selector on all positive-weight product lattice points, proving the last assertion of part 1.

We next prove the pointwise slice bound. Let \(\Pi\) satisfy (12), fix \(j\), and fix \(u_{-j}\in\prod_{i\ne j}D_i\). Restrict the canonical selector in (28) to the \(j\)-th factor. This is a one-factor packet on \(\Delta_j\), with integral rank-\(r_j\) chart
\[
 t_j\longmapsto b+U_jt_j+\sum_{i\ne j}U_iu_i
\tag{33}
\]
and factor-vertex values
\[
 \alpha'_{j,\ell}
 :=\alpha_{j,\ell}\prod_{i\ne j}g_i(u_i).
\tag{34}
\]
Equation (28) at the vertices of this slice verifies its admissibility, including when the multiplier in (34) is zero. Its one-factor energy is
\[
 \sum_{u_j\in D_j}
 \left(g_j(u_j)\prod_{i\ne j}g_i(u_i)\right)^d
 =A_j\prod_{i\ne j}\omega_i(u_i)
 =E\prod_{i\ne j}p_i(u_i).
\tag{35}
\]
Accepted fact `fbeb0a501b268d12` says that every one-factor packet is dominated by the complete core. Applying it to (33)-(35) proves (14).

Every \(p_i\) is a probability mass function with positive finite support, so \(m_i>0\) and \(m_i\geq1/s_i\). Maximizing (14) over the independently chosen coordinates gives
\[
 \prod_{i\ne j}m_i\leq G(C)/E.
\tag{36}
\]
The left side is positive, hence \(G(C)>0\). Multiplication of (36) over all \(j\) gives
\[
 \left(\prod_i m_i\right)^{k-1}
 \leq\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^k.
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (36)-(37) prove (15), and \(m_i\geq1/s_i\) proves (16).

We now prove (19). Under (17), \(G(C)<W/4\), full rank, and \(E>G(C)\), accepted fact `674b50aa42f420d8` supplies distinct factors \(i,j\) satisfying
\[
 (1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)
 \geq
 \left[1-\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}\right]^2.
\tag{38}
\]
For these factors it constructs source weights
\[
 b_u:=d_i(u_i)d_j(u_j)
 \prod_{\ell\ne i,j}\omega_\ell(u_\ell),
\tag{39}
\]
where \(d_i,d_j\) are the matched degrees, and proves
\[
 0\leq b_u\leq\delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u)),
 \qquad
 B:=\sum_ub_u=E(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j).
\tag{40}
\]
It also expands the canonical selector into its constant, one-factor, and higher mixed terms. The constant and one-factor terms define an affine map \(s\) on the base image, and every source residual lies in a space of dimension at most
\[
 \chi(\Pi)=\prod_i(r_i+1)-1-n.
\tag{41}
\]
Because (38) is positive, \(B>0\). Omit the zero weights in (39), use the distinct integral bases \(\Phi(u)\), the points \(x(u)\), the weights \(b_u\), and the restriction of \(s\) in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) from accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. If \(\rho_0\) is the affine dimension of these bases and \(r_0\) the resulting residual rank, then \(\rho_0\leq n\), \(r_0\leq\chi(\Pi)\), and therefore
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq2^{n-\rho_0}\frac B{r_0+1}
 \geq\frac{E(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)}{\chi(\Pi)+1}.
\tag{42}
\]
Combining (38) and (42) proves (19). The source mass in (40) is \(B\); it is the subsequent all-pairs midpoint construction in `674b50aa42f420d8` that has mass \(B/2\). This proves the claimed factor-two improvement without changing any matching normalization.

To prove (21), fix a packet counted in (20), and write its energy as \(E\). Part 1 gives \(E\leq W\). Also \(c_{K,\Chi}\leq1/4\). If \(G(C)\geq W/4\), then
\[
 G(C)\geq E/4\geq c_{K,\Chi}E.
\tag{43}
\]
Suppose \(G(C)<W/4\). If \(E\leq G(C)\), the desired bound is immediate. If \(E>G(C)\), put \(x:=G(C)/E\). When
\[
 x\geq2^{-(k-1)},
\]
one has
\[
 G(C)\geq2^{-(k-1)}E\geq2^{-(K-1)}E.
\tag{44}
\]
Otherwise \(x^{1/(k-1)}<1/2\), so the bracket in (19) is strictly larger than \(1/2\) and its square is strictly larger than \(1/4\). Thus
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 >\frac{E}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \geq\frac{E}{4(\Chi+1)}.
\tag{45}
\]
Equations (43)-(45) show that \(\max\{G,R_{\mathrm{sel}}\}\geq c_{K,\Chi}E\) for every packet in (20). Taking the supremum, without requiring its attainment, proves (21).

Finally assume (22). Accepted fact `c1e3b8400d5b5b10` applies with exactly these hypotheses and proves the strict uniform fiber bound
\[
 a_z<\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma_d}2^{-n}W
 \qquad(z\in D_+).
\tag{46}
\]
For every tuple \(u\in\prod_iS_i\), (28) and (46) give
\[
 0<\prod_i\omega_i(u_i)
 \leq\delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \leq a_{\Phi(u)}
 <\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma_d}2^{-n}W.
\tag{47}
\]
Finite distributivity, with all zero-weight tuples omitted, yields
\[
 E
 =\sum_{u\in\prod_iS_i}\prod_i\omega_i(u_i)
 <\left(\prod_i s_i\right)
 \frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma_d}2^{-n}W.
\tag{48}
\]
Rearranging proves (23). If \(E\geq\lambda W\), (23) gives the first inequality in (24), while (16) and \(G(C)<\varepsilon W\) give
\[
 \prod_{i\ne j}s_i
 \geq\frac E{G(C)}
 >\frac\lambda\varepsilon,
\tag{49}
\]
which proves the second. Finally, (21) and the first two strict inequalities in (22) give
\[
 c_{K,\Chi}\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^{K,\Chi}(C)
 \leq\max\{G(C),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\}
 <\varepsilon W,
\]
proving (25).
