## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex and let $L$ be a finite minimum of affine functions on $C$. For a continuous covector $c$ and $z$ in the real base projection, put $F_c(z)=\max_{X\in C_z}(L(z,X)-c\cdot X)$ and write $B_z^c$ for the maximizer set. Then $F_c$ is concave and each $B_z^c$ is compact convex and nonempty. At any fiber-interior differentiability point of $L(z,\cdot)$, membership in $B_z^c$ is equivalent to $\nabla_X L(z,X)=c$. For $X_i\in B_{z_i}^c$ and positive weights $\lambda_i$ of sum one, set $z=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i$. If $F_c(z)=\sum_i\lambda_iF_c(z_i)$, then $\sum_i\lambda_iX_i\in B_z^c$.

Let a probability row on $C$ have full barycenter $Y$, $L$-mean $L(Y)$, and an active plane $A(z,X)=c\cdot X+b(z)$ at $Y$, with $b$ affine in the base. Then the row is carried by $\{(z,X):F_c(z)=b(z),\ X\in B_z^c\}$. In particular the sufficient affine-profile condition holds for combinations of bases on a common such contact set.

## Proof

Compactness and continuity ensure the maxima exist, and concavity of $L(z,\cdot)-c\cdot X$ makes each maximizer set convex. At an interior differentiability point, a concave function on the fiber attains its maximum exactly when its derivative is zero, by the differentiable supporting-plane inequality and the two interior directions. For points $X_i$ realizing the maxima, ambient convexity ensures their combination belongs to $C_z$. Concavity of $L-c\cdot X$ gives its value there at least $\sum_i\lambda_iF_c(z_i)$, and the definition of $F_c$ bounds that value above by $F_c(z)$. This proves concavity and the stated membership condition.

For the row, the nonnegative gap $A-L$ has mean $A(Y)-L(Y)=0$, so it vanishes almost surely. Since $A$ majorizes $L$, on every fiber $F_c\le b$. A point of contact has value $L(z,X)-c\cdot X=b(z)$ and therefore realizes equality and is a maximizer. On an affine contact set $\{F_c=b\}$, the same majorization and concavity imply $F_c(\sum_i\lambda_i z_i)=\sum_i\lambda_iF_c(z_i)$, so the sufficient condition applies. Different rows can use different affine base majorants. This argument provides the equality test on a common contact set and does not supply the cross-row equality or cross-row maximizer inclusion for general higher rank.
