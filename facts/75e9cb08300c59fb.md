## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}
\]
be a full-dimensional \(d\)-simplex, and let
\[
x=\sum_{i=0}^d\lambda_i v_i,\qquad
\lambda_i\ge0,\qquad\sum_{i=0}^d\lambda_i=1.
\]
Put
\[
L(x):=\{i:(d+1)\lambda_i<1\}.
\]
There is a possibly degenerate simplex
\[
Q_x=\operatorname{conv}\{w_0,\ldots,w_d\}\subseteq T
\]
whose vertex centroid is \(x\) and whose relative \(d\)-volume is
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_x)}{\operatorname{vol}_d(T)}
=
\prod_{i\in L(x)}(d+1)\lambda_i.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for normalized halfspace depth defined using proper closed
halfspaces,
\[
\operatorname{depth}_T(x)
\ge
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
\prod_{i\in L(x)}(d+1)\lambda_i.
\tag{2}
\]
The construction is affine invariant and includes all boundary cases.

For the application, put
\[
p(x,y):=|x|+|y|+|x+y|,
\qquad
\overline H:=\{(x,y):p(x,y)\le1\},
\]
\[
\Delta_{30}:=\left\{u\in\mathbb R^{30}:
u_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{30}u_i\le1\right\},
\]
\[
K_{x,y}:=
\bigl(|x|,|y|,|x+y|,0,\ldots,0\bigr)
+(1-p(x,y))\Delta_{30}.
\]
Let \(\delta_{x,y}(v)\) be the absolute proper-closed-halfspace depth of
\(v\) in \(K_{x,y}\), and, for affine \(s\) and \(\tau\ge0\), put
\[
D(s,\tau):=\{(x,y)\in\overline H:
s(x,y)\in K_{x,y},\ \delta_{x,y}(s(x,y))\ge\tau\}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathfrak U_\infty:=
\sup_{s,\tau}\frac{\tau}{4}\operatorname{area}D(s,\tau),
\qquad
W_\infty:=\frac3{1984\cdot30!}.
\]
Then the constant affine map
\[
s_1=s_2=s_3=\frac{22}{465},
\qquad
s_i=\frac{19}{620}\quad(4\le i\le30)
\tag{3}
\]
and the threshold
\[
\tau_*:=\frac{\eta_*}{30!},
\qquad
\eta_*:=
\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30}
\left(\frac{19}{20}\right)^{29}
\frac{83}{120}
\tag{4}
\]
satisfy
\[
\eta_*\operatorname{area}D(s,\tau_*)>\frac3{31744}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathfrak U_\infty>\frac{W_\infty}{64}.
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

Put \(m:=d+1\) and
\[
r_i:=m\lambda_i\qquad(0\le i\le d).
\]
Thus \(r_i\ge0\) and
\[
\sum_{i=0}^d r_i=m.
\tag{7}
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal L:=\{i:r_i<1\},
\qquad
\mathcal H:=\{j:r_j\ge1\}.
\]

If \(\mathcal L=\varnothing\), (7) forces \(r_i=1\) for every \(i\).
Take the identity matrix \(A=I_m\), so \(Q_x=T\).  Notice also that
\(\mathcal H\) cannot be empty: if every \(r_i<1\), their sum would be
strictly less than \(m\).

Suppose now that \(\mathcal L\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
\mathcal D:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal L}(1-r_i).
\]
Then \(\mathcal D>0\), and (7) gives the exact deficit-surplus identity
\[
\mathcal D
=\sum_{j\in\mathcal H}(r_j-1).
\tag{8}
\]
For \(i\in\mathcal L\) and \(j\in\mathcal H\), define
\[
t_{ji}:=
\frac{(r_j-1)(1-r_i)}{\mathcal D}.
\tag{9}
\]
All these coefficients are nonnegative.  Equation (8) gives both sets of
transportation identities:
\[
\sum_{j\in\mathcal H}t_{ji}=1-r_i
\quad(i\in\mathcal L),
\qquad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal L}t_{ji}=r_j-1
\quad(j\in\mathcal H).
\tag{10}
\]

Define an \(m\times m\) matrix \(A=(A_{ik})\), with both rows and columns
indexed by \(\{0,\ldots,d\}\), by
\[
A_{ii}=r_i\quad(i\in\mathcal L),\qquad
A_{ji}=t_{ji}\quad(j\in\mathcal H,\ i\in\mathcal L),
\qquad
A_{jj}=1\quad(j\in\mathcal H),
\tag{11}
\]
and set every other entry equal to zero.  For a column \(i\in\mathcal L\),
(10) says that its sum is \(r_i+(1-r_i)=1\).  A column indexed by
\(\mathcal H\) has only its diagonal entry \(1\).  Hence \(A\) is
nonnegative and column-stochastic.  Its row sums are, again by (10),
\[
\sum_k A_{ik}=r_i\qquad(0\le i\le d).
\tag{12}
\]

For each column \(k\), define
\[
w_k:=\sum_{i=0}^d A_{ik}v_i.
\tag{13}
\]
Nonnegativity and the column sums show that \(w_k\in T\), and hence
\(Q_x:=\operatorname{conv}\{w_0,\ldots,w_d\}\subseteq T\).  Its vertex
centroid is
\[
\frac1m\sum_{k=0}^d w_k
=
\sum_{i=0}^d\frac{\sum_kA_{ik}}m v_i
=
\sum_{i=0}^d\lambda_i v_i
=x,
\tag{14}
\]
where (12) was used in the second equality.

Order the indices with \(\mathcal L\) first and \(\mathcal H\) second.
Then (11) has block form
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
\operatorname{diag}(r_i:i\in\mathcal L)&0\\
(t_{ji})_{j\in\mathcal H,\ i\in\mathcal L}&I_{\mathcal H}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
so
\[
\det A=\prod_{i\in\mathcal L}r_i.
\tag{15}
\]
For completeness, this determinant is exactly the oriented affine-volume
ratio.  Column-stochasticity says
\(\mathbf 1^\top A=\mathbf 1^\top\), so the difference space
\[
E:=\{c\in\mathbb R^m:\mathbf 1^\top c=0\}
\]
is invariant under \(A\), and the induced map on the one-dimensional
quotient \(\mathbb R^m/E\) is the identity.  Therefore
\(\det A=\det(A|_E)\).  Affine independence of the \(v_i\)'s identifies
\(E\) with the translation space of \(T\), and under this identification
\(A|_E\) is precisely the linear part of the affine map \(v_k\mapsto w_k\).
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_x)}{\operatorname{vol}_d(T)}
=|\det A|
=\prod_{i\in\mathcal L}r_i,
\]
which is (1).  This argument also covers \(r_i=0\), when both the
determinant and the \(d\)-volume vanish.

If the product in (1) is zero, (2) is the trivial nonnegativity of depth.
If it is positive, \(Q_x\) is full-dimensional.  Its centroid is \(x\),
so the exact simplex-centroid proper-closed-halfspace bound in accepted
fact `3d19a8ec7063f156` gives, for every proper closed halfspace \(J\)
containing \(x\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(T\cap J)
\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_x\cap J)
\ge
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_x).
\]
Divide by \(\operatorname{vol}_d(T)\) and take the infimum over \(J\) to
obtain (2).

This includes \(d=1\) without a separate construction.  Indices with
\(r_i=1\) belong to \(\mathcal H\) and have zero surplus in (9), so the
equality case is literal.  Zero barycentric coordinates belong to
\(\mathcal L\) and give a degenerate simplex of the asserted zero volume.
If \(\mathcal L\) is empty, the identity construction above applies; an
empty \(\mathcal H\) is impossible by (7).  Finally, (9), (11), and (13)
use only barycentric coordinates.  For every invertible affine map
\(\Phi\),
\[
\Phi(w_k)=\sum_iA_{ik}\Phi(v_i),
\]
so the construction commutes with affine changes of simplex coordinates.
This proves the registered lemma and all its boundary and invariance
claims.

We now give the exact six-cone certificate.  The choice in (3) is the
member \(a=1/60\) of the symmetric constant-graph family
\[
r=\frac{1-3a}{31},\qquad
s_1=s_2=s_3=r+a,\qquad s_i=r\ (4\le i\le30).
\]
Indeed, at \(a=1/60\), one has
\[
r=\frac{19}{620},
\qquad r+a=\frac{22}{465},
\]
and
\[
1-\sum_{i=1}^{30}s_i=r.
\tag{16}
\]

Let \(P\) be the convex hull of the following vertices, written in
counterclockwise order:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\frac1{60},-\frac1{40}\right),
\left(\frac1{40},-\frac1{60}\right),
\left(\frac1{40},-\frac1{120}\right),
\left(\frac1{60},\frac1{120}\right),\\
&\left(\frac1{120},\frac1{60}\right),
\left(-\frac1{120},\frac1{40}\right),
\left(-\frac1{60},\frac1{40}\right),
\left(-\frac1{40},\frac1{60}\right),\\
&\left(-\frac1{40},\frac1{120}\right),
\left(-\frac1{60},-\frac1{120}\right),
\left(-\frac1{120},-\frac1{60}\right),
\left(\frac1{120},-\frac1{40}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
At every vertex of \(P\), the three numbers
\[
|x|,\quad |y|,\quad |x+y|
\]
are a permutation of
\[
\frac1{40},\quad\frac1{60},\quad\frac1{120}.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, \(p=1/20\) at every vertex.  Convexity of \(p\) gives
\(p\le1/20\) throughout \(P\), so \(P\subseteq\overline H\).

The thirty-one absolute barycentric slacks of the point \(s(x,y)\) in
\(K_{x,y}\) are
\[
\lambda_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^{30}s_i,\qquad
\lambda_1=s_1-|x|,\quad
\lambda_2=s_2-|y|,\quad
\lambda_3=s_3-|x+y|,
\qquad
\lambda_i=s_i\ (4\le i\le30).
\tag{19}
\]
By (16) and (18), their multiset at each vertex of \(P\) is
\[
\left\{
\frac{83}{3720},\
\underbrace{\frac{114}{3720},\ldots,\frac{114}{3720}}_{29\ {\rm times}},\
\frac{145}{3720}
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Their sum, the fiber scale, is
\[
R=1-p=\frac{19}{20},
\qquad
\frac R{31}=\frac{19}{620}=\frac{114}{3720}.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus there is exactly one normalized barycentric coordinate strictly
below \(1/31\), and its factor in (1) is
\[
\frac{31(83/3720)}{19/20}=\frac{83}{114}.
\tag{22}
\]
Applying the proved lemma in dimension \(30\) to \(K_{x,y}\) at each
vertex of \(P\) gives
\[
\delta_{x,y}(s(x,y))
\ge
\frac1{30!}
\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30}
\left(\frac{19}{20}\right)^{30}\frac{83}{114}
=\tau_*,
\tag{23}
\]
where the last equality is the simplification
\[
\left(\frac{19}{20}\right)^{30}\frac{83}{114}
=
\left(\frac{19}{20}\right)^{29}\frac{83}{120}.
\]

We include the convex-hull step rather than assume it.  The total
six-cone bundle
\[
\mathcal K:=\{(x,y,u):(x,y)\in\overline H,\ u\in K_{x,y}\}
\]
is convex, since it is described by
\[
u_1\ge|x|,\quad u_2\ge|y|,\quad u_3\ge|x+y|,\quad
u_i\ge0\ (4\le i\le30),\quad \sum_i u_i\le1.
\tag{24}
\]
Fix any affine graph \(s\) and any nonzero \(b\in\mathbb R^{30}\), and
write
\[
A_b(x,y):=
K_{x,y}\cap\{u:b^\top(u-s(x,y))\ge0\}.
\]
Convexity of (24) and affinity of \(s\) imply the Minkowski inclusion
\[
(1-\theta)A_b(z_0)+\theta A_b(z_1)
\subseteq A_b((1-\theta)z_0+\theta z_1).
\]
Brunn-Minkowski therefore makes
\[
z\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}_{30}(A_b(z))^{1/30}
\]
concave, with the zero-volume convention.  Every proper closed halfspace
containing \(s(z)\) can be moved parallel toward \(s(z)\) until its
boundary contains \(s(z)\), without increasing its intersection with the
fiber.  Hence
\[
\delta_z(s(z))^{1/30}
=
\inf_{b\ne0}\operatorname{vol}_{30}(A_b(z))^{1/30}.
\tag{25}
\]
The pointwise infimum in (25) is concave: for \(z_\theta=(1-\theta)z_0+
\theta z_1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\inf_b\operatorname{vol}(A_b(z_\theta))^{1/30}
&\ge
\inf_b\left((1-\theta)\operatorname{vol}(A_b(z_0))^{1/30}
+\theta\operatorname{vol}(A_b(z_1))^{1/30}\right)\\
&\ge
(1-\theta)\inf_b\operatorname{vol}(A_b(z_0))^{1/30}
+\theta\inf_b\operatorname{vol}(A_b(z_1))^{1/30}.
\end{aligned}
\]
It follows that every \(D(s,\tau)\) is convex.  Equations (17) and (23)
therefore give
\[
P\subseteq D(s,\tau_*).
\tag{26}
\]

A shoelace calculation on (17) gives
\[
\operatorname{area}P
=3\left(\frac1{60}\right)
\left(2\left(\frac1{40}\right)-\frac1{60}\right)
=\frac1{600}.
\tag{27}
\]
The parameters in (3) and (17) also arise from an exact threshold
optimization.  In the same constant-graph family, take the orbit of
\((u,-a)\) and impose \(a=2u/3\).  The orbit polygon has area
\[
\frac{8u^2}{3},
\]
and the transportation certificate at every orbit vertex is
\[
\eta(u)=
\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30}
(1-2u)^{29}\left(1-\frac{37u}{3}\right).
\]
Thus the normalized common-threshold score in this one-parameter family is
\[
\frac83\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30}
u^2(1-2u)^{29}\left(1-\frac{37u}{3}\right).
\tag{28}
\]
Its only critical point in \(0<u<3/37\) is the smaller root of
\[
2368u^2-297u+6=0,
\]
namely
\[
u=\frac{297-\sqrt{31377}}{4736}.
\]
The rational choice \(u=1/40\), and hence \(a=1/60\), is the exact nearby
choice used above.

Finally, (4), (26), and (27) give
\[
\eta_*\operatorname{area}D(s,\tau_*)
\ge\frac{\eta_*}{600}.
\]
The required comparison is strict by the following complete integer
certificate:
\[
\frac{\eta_*/600}{3/31744}
=
\frac{
7677262627908203979626835008219879756641311146561859
}{
7449875685035274867236675806384557764676113819238400
}
\]
\[
=1+
\frac{
227386942872929112390159201835321991965197327323459
}{
7449875685035274867236675806384557764676113819238400
}
>1.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (5).  Since \(\tau_*=\eta_*/30!\),
\[
\mathfrak U_\infty
\ge
\frac{\tau_*}{4}\operatorname{area}D(s,\tau_*)
>
\frac1{4\cdot30!}\frac3{31744}
=
\frac3{126976\cdot30!}
=
\frac{W_\infty}{64}.
\]
This is (6) and completes the exact six-cone application.
