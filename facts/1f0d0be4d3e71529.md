## Statement

Let $\pi$ be a finite martingale coupling of $\mu$ to $\nu'\le\nu$ in physical coordinates. Let $z$ be a linear projection of the source point, $z'$ the same projection of the target point, and assume $\mu,\nu$ have finite projected second moments. Set $\delta=\nu-\nu'$. For every projected vector $a$,
$$
\int (z-a)(z-a)^{\mathsf T}\,d(\nu-\mu)
=\int (z'-z)(z'-z)^{\mathsf T}\,d\pi
+\int (z-a)(z-a)^{\mathsf T}\,d\delta .
$$
Its trace is the corresponding scalar quadratic identity. Equality of the projected marginals of $\mu$ and $\nu$, or just equality of their total masses and of one such scalar quadratic moment, forces $\delta=0$ and $z'=z$ $\pi$-almost everywhere. The identities add for any finite nonnegative labeled sum, retaining every capacity remainder.

## Proof

Put $D=z'-z$. The projected martingale property is $\mathbb E[D\mid\text{source}]=0$. The second-moment assumptions and Cauchy–Schwarz give integrability of $(z-a)D^{\mathsf T}$ and its transpose. These terms integrate to zero by conditioning. Expand $(z'-a)(z'-a)^{\mathsf T}$ using $z'-a=(z-a)+D$. After integrating, the cross terms vanish and the marginal terms yield the identity with $\nu'$ in place of $\nu$ and no remainder. Adding the $\delta$ term gives the displayed identity. Taking traces gives the sum of two nonnegative integrals. Equal source and full target masses force $\delta=0$, since $\pi$ already transports all of $\mu$ and $\delta$ is nonnegative. Equality of the scalar quadratic moments then forces $\int|D|^2\,d\pi=0$, hence $D=0$ almost everywhere. Equality of projected marginals implies both needed equalities. Linearity proves the labeled sum assertion.
