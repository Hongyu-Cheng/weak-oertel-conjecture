## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), and let \(D\) be finite with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). Suppose \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\), and every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two. For each \(b\in B_k\), form the graph with edges \(\lbrace i,j\rbrace\) whenever \(b_i=b_j=0\) and \(b+2e_i+2e_j\in D\). Assume each nonempty such graph is a star: all its edges contain one index, which may depend on \(b\). One-step extensions may be absent.

For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), set \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\), \(w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x)\), \(Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x\), and \(p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z\). Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\). Let \(\tau_p(z)\) be the infimum of the original \(p\)-mass over all closed affine halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) containing \(z\), and put \(M_3(p)=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3\). Halfspaces can be required to have nonzero normal; including the whole space leaves the infimum unchanged. For \(c\in D\), define \(s_p(c)=\sum_{x,y\in D:x+y=2c}\min(p_x,p_y)\) and \(T(p)=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3\). These reflection sums use ordered pairs, including the diagonal once.

Let \(D_1\) be the actual points of \(D\) having at most one two, and let \(D_1^{\mathrm{full}}\) be the entire ternary one-step set, consisting of all points of \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) having at most one two. Define
$$
W_1=\sum_{x\in D_1}w_x,\qquad
W_1^{\mathrm{full}}=\sum_{x\in D_1^{\mathrm{full}}}w_x,\qquad
\delta=\frac{W_1}{W_1^{\mathrm{full}}}\in(0,1],\qquad
\alpha=\frac{W_1}Z.
$$
For a nonempty coordinate block \(I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\) and an actual pattern \(u\in\pi_I(D)\) containing a two, put \(F_u=\lbrace x\in D:x_I=u\rbrace\), \(p_I(u)=p(F_u)\), and \(D_u=\lbrace x_{I^c}:x\in F_u\rbrace\). Let \(q_u\) be the number of actually occupied parity classes of \(D_u\) in \(\mathbb Z^{I^c}\). When \(I\) is the entire coordinate set, \(q_u=1\). Define
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}=
\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace}
8^k\sum_{\substack{u\in\pi_I(D)\\u\text{ contains a two}}}
\frac{p_I(u)^4}{q_u^3}.
$$
Let \(r_i=\exp(-\beta_i)\), \(H=\lbrace i:r_i>16\rbrace\), and \(h=|H|\). Write \(p_H\) for the original marginal on \(H\), and put \(P_H=8^h\sum_{x\in\pi_H(D):x\text{ contains a two}}p_H(x)^4\), with \(P_H=0\) if \(H=\varnothing\). Then
$$
1\le4353\frac{\alpha}{\delta}+512P_H^{1/4},\qquad
P_H\le P_{\mathrm{par}},\qquad
\frac{\alpha}{\delta}+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge2^{-40},
$$
and
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)},\qquad
T(p)\ge2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Thus a positive lower bound on \(\delta\) supplies a uniform coefficient on this class. No such lower bound on \(\delta\) is assumed or proved.

In particular, when the entire one-step core is present, \(\delta=1\). Put \(S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4\), and define the ordered Boolean-midpoint sum
$$
L_B=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2\\(x+y)/2\in B_k}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}.
$$
The full-core corollary is
$$
\alpha+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge2^{-40},\qquad
\alpha+2^{k/3}L_B+8^kS_4+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge2^{-40},\qquad
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-62}8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
The only predecessor used is accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\), specifically its original affine one-step bounds \(M_3,T\ge1/(512q^3)\) for a complete nonempty ternary support with at most one two and \(q\) occupied parity classes. The original normalizations and ambient embeddings for its uses are specified below.

## Proof

Put
$$
B=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+r_i),\qquad
a_i=\frac{r_i^2}{1+r_i},\qquad
a=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i.
$$
The hypothetical full one-step partition has the exact identity \(W_1^{\mathrm{full}}=B(1+a)\): the Boolean contribution is \(B\), and fixing the unique two at coordinate \(i\) contributes \(r_i^2\prod_{j\ne i}(1+r_j)=Ba_i\). In particular
$$
\frac{W_1^{\mathrm{full}}}Z=\frac{\alpha}{\delta}.
$$
This hypothetical full core uses the same field \(\beta\). Its normalized weights would be \(w_x/W_1^{\mathrm{full}}\), and multiplying these by \(\alpha/\delta\) gives \(w_x/Z\). We use the full core only as a superset for upper bounds on completion weights. We do not treat missing full-core points as atoms of the original distribution.

Let \(J=H^c\). Consider the level-two points whose two coordinates both belong to \(J\). In a fixed Boolean parity class \(b\), their total weight divided by \(w_b\) is the sum of \(r_i^2r_j^2\) over the corresponding edges. If the star center lies in \(H\), there are no such edges. Otherwise its activity is at most sixteen, and the edge sum is at most \(256\sum_{j:b_j=0}r_j^2\). The bound holds for an empty edge set too. Summing over Boolean sources gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{x\in D\setminus D_1\\x\text{ has no two in }H}}w_x
&\le256\sum_{b\in B_k}w_b\sum_{j:b_j=0}r_j^2\\
&=256B\sum_j\frac{r_j^2}{1+r_j}
\le256W_1^{\mathrm{full}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
If \(m_H=p(\exists i\in H:x_i=2)\), the event complementary to this one consists of core points without a high two and level-two points without a high two. Its core part has mass at most \(\alpha\). Since \(\delta\le1\), it follows that
$$
m_H\ge1-\alpha-256\frac{\alpha}{\delta}
\ge1-257\frac{\alpha}{\delta}. \tag{1}
$$
When \(H=\varnothing\), this gives \(\alpha/\delta\ge1/257\), so the first claimed inequality holds with \(P_H=0\).

We now prove a finite weight estimate for nonempty \(H\). Let \(h\ge1\) and \(r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_h>16\) be arbitrary, put \(B_H=\prod_{i=1}^h(1+r_i)\), \(a_H=\sum_{i=1}^h r_i^2/(1+r_i)\), and let \(X\) be all ternary vectors in \(h\) coordinates with exactly one or two twos. Define
$$
g_x=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\prod_i r_i^{x_i}}{B_H},&x\text{ has exactly one two},\\[4pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\prod_i r_i^{x_i}}{B_H(1+a_H)},&x\text{ has exactly two twos}.
\end{cases}
$$
We claim
$$
\sum_{x\in X}\min(g_x,2^{-h})\le4096. \tag{2}
$$
The activity order only relabels coordinates. Put \(t=2^{-h}\) and \(\rho=5/(4\sqrt2)<9/10\).

For a point with its unique two at \(i\), let \(S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,h\rbrace\setminus\lbrace i\rbrace\) be its zero coordinates. The factors for its binary coordinates and \(r_i^2/(1+r_i)\le r_i\) give \(g_x\le r_i/\prod_{j\in S}r_j\). If \(S\) contains no index less than \(i\), there are \(2^{h-i}\) choices of the other binary coordinates. Their total truncated contribution, summed over \(i\), is at most \(\sum_i2^{h-i}t=\sum_i2^{-i}\le1\). Otherwise choose \(j\in S\) with \(j<i\). Since \(r_j\ge r_i\), we have \(g_x\le16^{1-|S|}\). Using \(\min(v,t)\le\sqrt{vt}\) and enlarging this second set to all zero sets for each \(i\), its contribution is at most
$$
\begin{aligned}
h\sum_{S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,h-1\rbrace}
\sqrt{t\,16^{1-|S|}}
&=4h2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-1}\\
&=2\sqrt2\,h\rho^{h-1}<300.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed \(h\rho^{h-1}\le\sum_{n\ge1}n(9/10)^{n-1}=100\), and \(2\sqrt2<3\). Thus the one-two contribution to (2) is at most \(301\).

For a point with twos at \(i<j\), let \(S\) be its zero coordinates, disjoint from \(\lbrace i,j\rbrace\). Since \(1+a_H\ge r_i^2/(1+r_i)\ge r_i/2\), we have \(g_x\le2r_j/\prod_{\ell\in S}r_\ell\). If there is no index in \(S\) less than \(j\) other than the already excluded index \(i\), there are \(2^{h-j}\) such patterns for this pair. Their total truncated contribution is at most \(\sum_{j=2}^h(j-1)2^{-j}\le2\). Otherwise choose \(\ell\in S\) with \(\ell<j\) and \(\ell\ne i\). Since \(r_\ell\ge r_j\), we obtain \(g_x\le2\,16^{1-|S|}\). For \(h\ge2\), enlarging again to every zero set for each pair bounds the remaining contribution by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\binom h2\sum_{S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,h-2\rbrace}
\sqrt{t\,2\,16^{1-|S|}}
&=\binom h2\sqrt{32}\,2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-2}\\
&=\sqrt2\,h(h-1)\rho^{h-2}<3000.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(h(h-1)\rho^{h-2}\le\sum_{n\ge2}n(n-1)(9/10)^{n-2}=2000\), and \(\sqrt2<3/2\). The two-two contribution is absent for \(h=1\) and is at most \(3002\) otherwise. Since \(301+3002<4096\), this proves (2).

Return to \(H\ne\varnothing\) in the original support. Relabel its coordinates in decreasing activity order only while applying (2). Put \(B_H=\prod_{i\in H}(1+r_i)\), \(B_J=\prod_{i\in J}(1+r_i)\), \(a_H=\sum_{i\in H}a_i\), and \(a_J=\sum_{i\in J}a_i\). If a high-block pattern \(x\) has exactly one two, its low-block completions have at most one two. Their weight sum is at most the hypothetical full low-core partition \(B_J(1+a_J)\), regardless of which completion points are actually present. Hence
$$
p_H(x)\le\frac{\bigl(\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{x_i}\bigr)B_J(1+a_J)}Z
=\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\frac{1+a_J}{1+a_H+a_J}\frac{\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{x_i}}{B_H}
\le\frac{\alpha}{\delta}g_x.
$$
If \(x\) has exactly two twos, its low-block completions are binary, so
$$
p_H(x)\le\frac{\bigl(\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{x_i}\bigr)B_J}Z
=\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\frac{\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{x_i}}{B_H(1+a_H+a_J)}
\le\frac{\alpha}{\delta}g_x.
$$
Assign marginal mass zero to nonactual high patterns, so these bounds hold on all of \(X\). Put \(C=4096\). The part with \(g_x\le t\) has total original marginal mass at most \((\alpha/\delta)\sum_x\min(g_x,t)\le C\alpha/\delta\). The number of patterns with \(g_x>t\) is at most \(C/t=C2^h\). Hölder on that latter part yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_H=\sum_{x\in X}p_H(x)
&\le C\frac{\alpha}{\delta}+(C2^h)^{3/4}
\left(\sum_{x\in X}p_H(x)^4\right)^{1/4}\\
&=C\frac{\alpha}{\delta}+512P_H^{1/4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Combining this with (1) proves \(1\le4353\alpha/\delta+512P_H^{1/4}\). For every actual pattern on \(H\), its residual support occupies at most \(2^{k-h}\) parity classes. The block \(I=H\) in \(P_{\mathrm{par}}\) therefore gives \(P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge P_H\). For empty \(H\) this comparison follows from \(P_H=0\), and the finite inequality was already proved.

If \(\alpha/\delta\ge1/8706\), then \(\alpha/\delta+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge1/8706>2^{-40}\). Otherwise the finite inequality gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge P_H>\frac1{16\cdot4096^3}=2^{-40}.
$$
The case \(H=\varnothing\) lies in the former branch. This proves the quantitative weighted-coverage bridge.

Next establish the original cubic consumers with actual occupied parity counts. Fix a nonempty block \(I\) and an actual pattern \(u\) containing a two. Put \(J=I^c\), \(s=|J|\), and \(\lambda=p_I(u)>0\). Let \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(D)\), and let \(Q_u=\lbrace y\in\mathbb R^J:(u,y)\in Q\rbrace\), with coordinates placed in their original order. It is compact and convex. Completeness of \(D\) gives \(Q_u\cap\mathbb Z^J=D_u\). In particular \(D_u\) is complete, since its convex hull lies in \(Q_u\). It is nonempty and ternary with at most one two, because the fixed pattern already contains at least one of the at most two twos. No residual Boolean point or parity source is presumed present.

On \(D_u\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{s+1}\), define
$$
Z_u=\sum_{y\in D_u}\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y),\qquad
\sigma_{(y,0)}=\frac{\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y)}{Z_u},\qquad
\iota(y,v)=((u,y),v).
$$
Factoring the original weights gives \(\lambda=\exp(-\beta_I\cdot u)Z_u/Z\) and \(p_{\iota(y,0)}=\lambda\sigma_{(y,0)}\). Thus \(\sigma\) is exactly the original conditional affine law with residual field \(\beta_J\). Write \(\sigma_y=\sigma_{(y,0)}\), and define its depth and ordered-reflection cubics by the same formulas in this residual ambient space and residual support.

The pullback under \(\iota\) of a containing ambient halfspace \(\lbrace(x,v):\ell\cdot x+cv\ge b\rbrace\) is \(\lbrace(y,v):\ell_J\cdot y+cv\ge b-\ell_I\cdot u\rbrace\). If \((\ell_J,c)\ne0\), this is a containing closed halfspace in the residual space. If \((\ell_J,c)=0\), containment implies \(b-\ell_I\cdot u\le0\), so the pullback is the whole residual space and has conditional mass one. Conversely every residual halfspace with nonzero normal extends to an ambient one by putting zero coefficients on \(I\). These observations handle all zero pullback normals and prove equality of the conditional halfspace infima. The full original mass of any containing halfspace is at least its mass on the section. Therefore \(\tau_p(\iota(z))\ge\lambda\tau_\sigma(z)\), and
$$
\sum_{x\in F_u}p_x\tau_p((x,0))^3\ge\lambda^4M_3(\sigma).
$$
For a center \(c=(u,y)\in F_u\), the ordered pairs of original points in this same section reflected about \(c\) are exactly \(((u,v),(u,v'))\) with \(v,v'\in D_u\) and \(v+v'=2y\). Their sum of original minimum probabilities is \(\lambda s_\sigma(y)\), because \(p_{(u,v)}=\lambda\sigma_v\). The full original reflection sum includes these pairs with nonnegative summands. Thus \(s_p(c)\ge\lambda s_\sigma(y)\), and \(\sum_{c\in F_u}p_c s_p(c)^3\ge\lambda^4T(\sigma)\).

When \(s\ge1\), apply the one-step occupied-parity assertion of accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\) with support \(D_u\), dimension \(s\), original residual field \(\beta_J\), probabilities \(\sigma\), and actual parity count \(q_u\). Its depths and full ordered-reflection definitions coincide with those just used, giving \(M_3(\sigma),T(\sigma)\ge1/(512q_u^3)\). If \(D_u\) is a singleton, its conditional probability and every containing-halfspace mass are one; its sole reflected pair is its diagonal, whose minimum probability is one. Its depth, reflection sum, and both cubics are therefore exactly one. This includes \(s=0\), when \(D_u\) consists of the unique vector in \(\mathbb R^0\) and \(q_u=1\). Thus both lower bounds hold in all cases. Distinct pattern sections for a fixed block are disjoint. Sum their original cubic contributions, and then maximize over blocks, to obtain
$$
\min(M_3(p),T(p))\ge\frac1{512}
\max_{I\ne\varnothing}\sum_{u\text{ containing a two}}
\frac{p_I(u)^4}{q_u^3}
=\frac{P_{\mathrm{par}}}{512\cdot8^k}. \tag{3}
$$

There is also an actual-core consumer. The set \(D_1\) is complete even when it misses one-step extensions. Indeed, an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) is ternary. If two of its coordinates equaled two, every generator with positive coefficient would have both coordinates equal to two, which no generator in \(D_1\) does. Hence it has at most one two. It also belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\), so it belongs to \(D_1\). Since \(B_k\subseteq D_1\), its occupied parity count is exactly \(2^k\).

Condition the original distribution on this actual core, obtaining probabilities \(p_x/\alpha=w_x/W_1\), with the same field \(\beta\) and original ambient embedding. Every ambient halfspace has original mass at least \(\alpha\) times its core conditional mass. At a core center, restricting the full original reflection sum to pairs of actual core endpoints gives exactly \(\alpha\) times its core conditional reflection sum, because taking a minimum preserves the common positive factor \(\alpha\). Thus the actual core terms of each original cubic are at least \(\alpha^4\) times the corresponding core conditional cubic. Applying accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\) to this complete actual one-step core, with field \(\beta\), normalization \(W_1\), and parity count \(2^k\), proves
$$
\min(M_3(p),T(p))\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\cdot8^k}. \tag{4}
$$
The finite bridge proof gave either \(\alpha/\delta\ge1/8706>2^{-14}\), or \(P_{\mathrm{par}}>2^{-40}\). In the first case \(\alpha>2^{-14}\delta\), and (4) gives both cubics at least \(2^{-65}\delta^4 8^{-k}=2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)}\). In the second case (3) gives both cubics at least \(2^{-49}8^{-k}\), which is stronger since \(\delta\le1\). This proves the quantitative original cubic bounds.

If the full one-step core is present, \(W_1=W_1^{\mathrm{full}}\), so \(\delta=1\). Conversely \(\delta=1\) forces the full core to be present, because every omitted point would have strictly positive weight. Substituting \(\delta=1\) proves the full-core bridge and both absolute cubic bounds. Adding the nonnegative terms \(2^{k/3}L_B\) and \(8^kS_4\) proves the displayed full-core four-term corollary.

Only in this full-core case, the actual-parity score also has the following comparison with the dimension-only block score \(8^{|I|}\sum_{u\text{ containing a two}}p_I(u)^4\). A section whose pattern has exactly one two contains every binary residual completion, so \(q_u=2^{k-|I|}\). A section whose pattern has two twos has only binary residual points, so \(q_u=|D_u|\), and Hölder gives \(p_I(u)^4/q_u^3\le\sum_{x\in F_u}p_x^4\). It follows block by block that the actual-parity score is at least the dimension-only score and at most the dimension-only score plus \(8^kS_4\). Maximizing over blocks preserves these bounds. This comparison is not asserted for missing-core supports.

Finally \(\delta\) can tend to zero within the hypotheses: take \(k=1\), \(D=\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), and activity \(r_1\to\infty\). The support is complete and its level-two graph is empty, while \(\delta=(1+r_1)/(1+r_1+r_1^2)\to0\). Thus the weighted-coverage theorem does not assume or produce an absolute positive coverage bound on the entire star class.
