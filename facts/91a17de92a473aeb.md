## Statement

In the setting and subsequential limit of the sampled concave roof compactness lemma, let $h_j>0$ on $C_j$, put $S_j=\bigcup_z(\{z\}\times K_{j,z})$, and use summed volume $dm_j$ of total $M_j$. Fix a nonempty $J\subseteq P$ and marks $Y_{j,p}=(p,y_{j,p})\to Y_p=(p,y_p)$ with $y_p\in\operatorname{int}K_p$. Suppose that for each $j$ there are nonnegative weights $\sigma_{j,p}$ summing to one and probability measures $\rho_{j,p}$ on $S_j$ for positive weights, with
$$
\int w\,d\rho_{j,p}=Y_{j,p},\qquad
\int h_j\,d\rho_{j,p}=h_j(Y_{j,p}),\qquad
\sum_{p:\,\sigma_{j,p}>0}\frac{\sigma_{j,p}}{h_j(Y_{j,p})}\rho_{j,p}
=\frac{dm_j}{M_jh_j}.
$$
For positive weights set $d\eta_{j,p}=(\sigma_{j,p}h_j/h_j(Y_{j,p}))\,d\rho_{j,p}$, and otherwise set $\eta_{j,p}=0$. These measures have masses $\sigma_{j,p}$ and sum to $dm_j/M_j$. Along a further subsequence $\eta_{j,p}\rightharpoonup\eta_p$ and $\sigma_{j,p}\to\sigma_p$, with $\eta_p$ of mass $\sigma_p$ and $\sum_p\eta_p=dm/M$, where $dm$ is the limiting summed volume of total $M$.

Writing $F_p$ for the smallest face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ containing $p$, rows with $p\in Z$ are supported over $F_p\cap P\subseteq Z$ already at finite $j$, and rows with $p\notin Z$ allocate zero limiting original volume over $Z$. The two limiting sums are exactly the restrictions of $dm/M$ over $Z$ and its complement. In particular, with $V(T)=\sum_{z\in T}|K_z|$,
$$
\sum_{p\in J\cap Z}\sigma_p=\frac{V(Z)}M,\qquad
\sum_{p\in J\setminus Z}\sigma_p=\frac{V(P\setminus Z)}M,\qquad
Z=P\cap\bigcup_{\substack{p\in J\cap Z\\ \sigma_p>0}}F_p .
$$
The same cover uses any faces $G_p$ for the surviving zero-class sources with $F_p\subseteq G_p$ and $P\cap G_p\subseteq Z$, in particular the faces from the exact finite-plane convergence conclusion when that conclusion applies and $h$ is nonzero.

## Proof

Integrating the definition of $\eta_{j,p}$ gives its mass by the second row identity. Multiplying the last row identity by $h_j$ gives the sum formula and therefore domination by $dm_j/M_j$. The measures are supported in a fixed compact set and have bounded masses. Choose a common weakly convergent subsequence. Convergence of the volumes in symmetric difference on each base gives $\sum_p\eta_p=dm/M$. Testing against the constant one gives the limiting masses. Testing against a continuous base function equal to the indicator of any specified subset on $P$ gives convergence of the masses on that subset of bases.

For $p\notin Z$, the denominator $h_j(Y_{j,p})$ converges to $h_p(y_p)>0$ by local uniform convergence, while $\max_{z\in Z,\ X\in K_{j,z}}h_j(z,X)\to0$ if $Z$ is nonempty. Thus the mass of $\eta_{j,p}$ over $Z$ is at most that maximum times $\sigma_{j,p}/h_j(Y_{j,p})$ and tends to zero. The assertion is immediate when $Z$ is empty. For $p\in Z$ the base mean of $\rho_{j,p}$ is $p$, and a base-affine exposing function for $F_p$ is nonnegative on $\operatorname{conv}P$ and has expectation zero. Thus $\rho_{j,p}$ and its reweighting are supported over $F_p$; if $F_p$ is the whole base the assertion is automatic. The sampled concave roof compactness lemma gives $P\cap F_p\subseteq Z$. Hence the two class sums are exactly the respective restrictions of the total limiting original volume. Each fiber over $Z$ has positive volume, so some surviving zero-class row allocates mass to it; that row's supporting face contains its base. This proves the face cover. Replacing each $F_p$ by such a $G_p$ can enlarge the sampled union only within $Z$, so the cover remains exact.
