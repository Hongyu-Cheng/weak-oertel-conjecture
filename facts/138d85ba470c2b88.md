## Statement

Let $\mu$ be uniform probability on $\Delta_d$, $d\ge1$. Consider a nonempty finite family of proper caps $H_i=\{\xi:c_i\cdot\xi\ge0\}$ with the same actual section centroid $b$. Put $P_i=\mu(H_i)$ and let $f_i>0$ be the density at zero of $\sum_j c_{ij}E_j$ for independent unit-rate exponentials. If $\alpha_i>0$ and $\sum_i\alpha_i c_i=0$, then
$$
w=\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i}{f_i}\mathbf1_{H_i},\qquad T=\sum_i\frac{\alpha_iP_i}{f_i}
$$
satisfy $\int w\,d\mu=T>0$ and $\int \xi_j w\,d\mu=T/(d+1)$ for all $j$. In particular $w/T$ is a probability density with the ordinary simplex centroid.

## Proof

The mass equality follows by integrating the indicators. The proper-cap first-moment identity applies to each cap with $L_i=df_i$. Multiply by $\alpha_i/f_i$ and sum. The additional term in coordinate $j$ has numerator $d b_j\sum_i\alpha_i c_{ij}=0$ by raw balance, giving the claimed moment.

For the full-row specialization, take a finite family $x_i>0$ with componentwise sum $\mathbf1$. Write $F_i(t)=\mu\{x_i\cdot\xi\le t\}$ and suppose $t_i$ is a proper differentiable minimizing threshold of $t/F_i(t)^{1/d}$ for $t>0$ with $F_i(t)>0$. Assume the actual section centroids are common. Use $c_i=t_i\mathbf1-x_i$. The proper threshold is interior to the range of the row heights, so differentiating at $t_i$ gives $t_iF_i'(t_i)=d F_i(t_i)$. Here $F_i'(t_i)=L_i=df_i$, hence $t_i=P_i/f_i$. Since $x_i\cdot b=t_i$ and $\sum_i x_i=\mathbf1$, one has $\sum_i t_i=1$ and $\sum_i c_i=0$. Thus $\alpha_i=1$ gives $T=1$ in this specialization.
