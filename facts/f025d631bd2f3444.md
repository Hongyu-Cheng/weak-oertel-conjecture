## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and, for \(y\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
 C_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_y:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y),
 \qquad
 Z_+:=\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}.
\]
For \(y\in Z_+\), put
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 L_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y),
 \qquad
 D_y:=L_y-c_y,
\]
where, for a positive-volume convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
  f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\
  \int_Kf(x)\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\]
Thus, by accepted fact `b69e3ccf4199c079`, every \(D_y\) is a full-dimensional compact convex origin-symmetric body.

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite nonempty label set. For every \(\sigma\in\Sigma\), let \(\eta_\sigma>0\), let \(a_\sigma,b_\sigma\in Z_+\), and assume
\[
 a_\sigma\equiv b_\sigma\pmod 2.
\]
Define the endpoint-occurrence multiplicity and load by
\[
 N_{\sigma,y}:=
 \mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma=y\}}+
 \mathbf1_{\{b_\sigma=y\}},
 \qquad
 \ell_y:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\eta_\sigma N_{\sigma,y},
\]
and assume
\[
 \ell_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in Z_+).
\]
In particular, a loop \(a_\sigma=b_\sigma=y\) has \(N_{\sigma,y}=2\) and contributes \(2\eta_\sigma\) to \(\ell_y\). Put
\[
 m_\sigma:=\frac{a_\sigma+b_\sigma}{2},
 \qquad
 q_\sigma:=\frac{c_{a_\sigma}+c_{b_\sigma}}2,
 \qquad
 B_\sigma:=\frac{D_{a_\sigma}+D_{b_\sigma}}2.
\]

Fix a linear subspace \(W\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), put \(V:=\mathbb R^d/W\), and let \(\pi:\mathbb R^d\to V\) be the quotient map. Write \(\bar q_\sigma:=\pi(q_\sigma)\) and \(\bar B_\sigma:=\pi(B_\sigma)\). For a compact convex set \(Q\) write
\[
 h_Q(v):=\max_{x\in Q}\langle v,x\rangle,
\]
and use the gauge
\[
 \|z\|_{\bar B_\sigma}:=
 \inf\{t\geq0:z\in t\bar B_\sigma\}.
\]
Define
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}:=
 \min_{\bar s:\operatorname{aff}\{m_\sigma:\sigma\in\Sigma\}\to V\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\eta_\sigma
 \left(
  \|\bar q_\sigma-\bar s(m_\sigma)\|_{\bar B_\sigma}
  -\frac12
 \right)_+.
\]
Identify \(V^*\) with \(W^\perp\). Accepted fact `b69e3ccf4199c079` proves dual attainment. Fix any maximizing force family \((\theta_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\). Thus
\[
 \theta_\sigma\in W^\perp,
 \qquad
 h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)\leq\eta_\sigma,
\]
\[
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\theta_\sigma=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\theta_\sigma m_\sigma^{\mathsf T}=0,
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
 =\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \left[
  \langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle
  -\frac12h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right].
\]

For \(y\in Z_+\), define
\[
 U_y:=\frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}N_{\sigma,y}\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \pi_y:=\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma),
 \qquad
 \tau_y:=h_{D_y}(U_y).
\]
Then the reflected objective has the exact endpoint decomposition
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
 =\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \bigl(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\pi_y\bigr).
 }
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_y=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_yy^{\mathsf T}=0,
 }
\]
and, for every \(y\in Z_+\),
\[
 \boxed{
 0\leq\tau_y\leq2\pi_y\leq\ell_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}.
 }
\]
Equivalently, the two bounds requested separately are
\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_y=h_{D_y}(U_y)\leq2\pi_y,
 \qquad
 \pi_y\leq\frac{\ell_y}{2}\leq\frac{a_y}{8}.
 }
\]
All these formulas retain both occurrences of every loop.

For every \(y\in Z_+\) with \(\ell_y>0\), define an endpoint contact as follows. If \(\tau_y>0\), choose \(d_y\in D_y\) such that
\[
 \langle U_y,d_y\rangle=h_{D_y}(U_y)=\tau_y
\]
and set \(x_y:=c_y-d_y/2\). If \(\tau_y=0\), set
\[
 x_y:=c_y.
\]
Then
\[
 x_y\in\mathsf M_{\ell_y}(C_y)\subseteq C_y.
\]
With
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
\]
and
\[
 \mu_C(H):=
 \sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_y:(y,x)\in H\},
\]
the genuine mixed-integer contact cloud satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_d\sum_{\substack{y\in Z_+\\\ell_y>0}}
 \ell_y\delta_{(y,x_y)}
 \preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C,
 }
\]
meaning that its mass in every closed halfspace is at most the corresponding \(\mu_C\)-mass. The same contacts obey
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{y\in Z_+\\\ell_y>0}}
 \langle U_y,x_y\rangle
 \geq\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}.
 }
\]

Let
\[
 I:=\{y\in Z_+:\tau_y>0\}.
\]
For \(y\in I\), define
\[
 \alpha_y:=\min\left\{1,\frac{\pi_y}{\tau_y}\right\},
 \qquad
 K'_y:=c_y+\alpha_yD_y.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac12\leq\alpha_y\leq1,
 \qquad
 0<\pi_y\leq\frac{a_y}{8},
 \qquad
 \boxed{K'_y\subseteq\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y)}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
 \|z\|_{\alpha_yD_y}:=
 \inf\{t\geq0:z\in t\alpha_yD_y\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}(z,K'_y):=
 \inf_{x\in K'_y}\|z-x\|_{\alpha_yD_y}.
\]
If \(I\neq\varnothing\), define the diagonal affine defect by
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}I\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{y\in I}\pi_y
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}\bigl(s(y),K'_y\bigr).
\]
Both this minimum and the following maximum are attained, and the exact finite-product duality is
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}
 =\max\quad&-\sum_{y\in I}h_{K'_y}(V_y)\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&V_y\in\mathbb R^d,\\
 &h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V_y)\leq\pi_y\qquad(y\in I),\\
 &\sum_{y\in I}V_y=0,\\
 &\sum_{y\in I}V_yy^{\mathsf T}=0.
 \end{aligned}
 }
\]
If \(I=\varnothing\), define \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}:=0\); the dual uses the unique empty force family, has empty objective sum, and also has value zero. In every case,
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\geq\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}.
 }
\]

No assertion is made that this diagonal defect is absorbed by any diagonal-metronoid capacity or terminal quantity. Neither contact cloud is asserted to be admissible for any off-diagonal matching object, and no conclusion about the locked weak Oertel statement is made.

## Proof

Because \(C\) is compact, its projection onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is compact, so \(Z_+\) is finite. Hence all endpoint regroupings below are finite. Accepted fact `b69e3ccf4199c079` supplies the stated structure of \(D_y\), the reflected dual formula, and the maximizing force family.

First expand one label's contribution. Support functions commute with positive dilations and add under Minkowski sums, so
\[
 h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 =\frac12\left(
  h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
  +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle
 -\frac12h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)\\
 &\quad=
 \frac12\left(
  \langle\theta_\sigma,c_{a_\sigma}\rangle
  +\langle\theta_\sigma,c_{b_\sigma}\rangle
 \right)
 -\frac14\left(
  h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
  +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Regrouping the two endpoint occurrences by \(y\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
 &=\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \left[
  \left\langle
   \frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}N_{\sigma,y}\theta_\sigma,
   c_y
  \right\rangle
  -\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
   N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right]\\
 &=\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \bigl(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\pi_y\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For a loop \(a_\sigma=b_\sigma=y\), this regrouping contributes
\[
 \frac12N_{\sigma,y}\theta_\sigma=\theta_\sigma
 \quad\text{to }U_y,
 \qquad
 \frac14N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 =\frac12h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 \quad\text{to }\pi_y,
\]
which is exactly that loop label's contribution
\(\langle\theta_\sigma,c_y\rangle-h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)/2\). Thus no loop occurrence has been discarded or divided twice.

Subadditivity and positive homogeneity of \(h_{D_y}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau_y
 &=h_{D_y}\left(
   \frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}N_{\sigma,y}\theta_\sigma
  \right)\\
 &\leq\frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 =2\pi_y.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every \(D_y\) is origin-symmetric, so its support function is nonnegative. If \(N_{\sigma,y}>0\), then \(y\) is one of the two endpoint occurrences of \(\sigma\), and
\[
 h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 \leq
 h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 =2h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 \leq2\eta_\sigma.
\]
Applying this bound once for each occurrence, including twice when the label is a loop, yields
\[
 \pi_y
 \leq\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 N_{\sigma,y}(2\eta_\sigma)
 =\frac{\ell_y}{2}
 \leq\frac{a_y}{8}.
\]
This proves all support-load bounds.

For each label, \(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}=2\), also for a loop. Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_y
 =\frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \left(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}\right)\theta_\sigma
 =\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\theta_\sigma
 =0.
\]
Also \(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}y=a_\sigma+b_\sigma=2m_\sigma\), with the identity \(2y=2m_\sigma\) for a loop. Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_yy^{\mathsf T}
 &=\frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \theta_\sigma
 \left(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}y\right)^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \theta_\sigma m_\sigma^{\mathsf T}
 =0.
 \end{aligned}
\]

We next record the metronoid monotonicity needed twice below. If \(0<m\leq t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) and \(p\in\mathsf M_t(K)\) is represented by a density \(f\), then \(g:=(m/t)f\) satisfies \(0\leq g\leq1\), \(\int_Kg=m\), and
\[
 \frac1m\int_Kxg(x)\,dx
 =\frac1t\int_Kxf(x)\,dx
 =p.
\]
Thus
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_m(K)
 \qquad(0<m\leq t).
\]

Fix \(y\) with \(\ell_y>0\). If \(\tau_y>0\), compactness of \(D_y\) gives the required support maximizer \(d_y\). Origin symmetry gives \(-d_y/2\in D_y/2\). If \(\tau_y=0\), the prescribed point is \(c_y\). In both cases,
\[
 x_y\in c_y+\frac12D_y
 \subseteq c_y+D_y
 =L_y
 =\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y).
\]
Since \(0<\ell_y\leq a_y/4<a_y/2\), metronoid monotonicity gives
\[
 x_y\in\mathsf M_{\ell_y}(C_y).
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives both \(x_y\in C_y\) and the fiber-depth estimate
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap J)\geq\gamma_d\ell_y
\]
for every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(x_y\), including the improper whole-space case.

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed halfspace and define its vertical slice
\[
 H_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in H\}.
\]
This is a closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\), possibly empty or the whole space. Whenever \((y,x_y)\in H\), the slice \(H_y\) contains \(x_y\), so the preceding fiber-depth estimate applies. Summing over those contacts gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in Z_+:\ell_y>0\\(y,x_y)\in H}}\ell_y
 &\leq
 \sum_{\substack{y\in Z_+:\ell_y>0\\(y,x_y)\in H}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)\\
 &\leq
 \sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 =\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the global halfspace domination.

Because \(D_y\) contains a neighborhood of the origin, \(h_{D_y}(U_y)=0\) implies \(U_y=0\). Therefore the two cases in the definition of \(x_y\) satisfy the single identity
\[
 \langle U_y,x_y\rangle
 =\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\frac12\tau_y
 \qquad(\ell_y>0).
\]
If \(\ell_y=0\), then \(N_{\sigma,y}=0\) for every \(\sigma\), so \(U_y=0\), \(\pi_y=0\), and \(\tau_y=0\). It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{y\in Z_+\\\ell_y>0}}\langle U_y,x_y\rangle
 &=\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \left(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\frac12\tau_y\right)\\
 &\geq\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \bigl(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\pi_y\bigr)
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}},
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the inequality is exactly \(\tau_y\leq2\pi_y\).

Now let \(y\in I\). Then \(\tau_y>0\) and \(\tau_y\leq2\pi_y\), so \(\pi_y>0\) and
\[
 \frac12\leq\frac{\pi_y}{\tau_y}.
\]
Thus \(1/2\leq\alpha_y\leq1\). Since \(D_y\) is convex and contains the origin,
\[
 K'_y=c_y+\alpha_yD_y
 \subseteq c_y+D_y
 =\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y).
\]
The already proved bound \(0<\pi_y\leq a_y/8<a_y/2\), followed by metronoid monotonicity, proves
\[
 K'_y\subseteq\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y).
\]

We now prove the finite-product diagonal gauge-distance duality directly. Assume first that \(I\neq\varnothing\), and let
\[
 E:=(\mathbb R^d)^I,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L:=
 \{(s(y))_{y\in I}:s:\operatorname{aff}I\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{affine}\}
 \subseteq E.
\]
Every \(\alpha_yD_y\) is a full-dimensional compact convex origin-symmetric body, so its gauge is a norm. For \(z\in\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
 G_y(z):=\pi_y\operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}(z,K'_y),
 \qquad
 F((z_y)_{y\in I}):=\sum_{y\in I}G_y(z_y).
\]
Each \(G_y\), and hence \(F\), is finite, continuous, and convex.

We compute the conjugate of every summand without invoking any off-diagonal result. For \(V\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_y^*(V)
 &=\sup_{z\in\mathbb R^d}
 \left[
  \langle V,z\rangle
  -\pi_y\inf_{x\in K'_y}\|z-x\|_{\alpha_yD_y}
 \right]\\
 &=\sup_{\substack{x\in K'_y\\u\in\mathbb R^d}}
 \left[
  \langle V,x+u\rangle
  -\pi_y\|u\|_{\alpha_yD_y}
 \right]\\
 &=h_{K'_y}(V)
 +\sup_{u\in\mathbb R^d}
 \left[
  \langle V,u\rangle
  -\pi_y\|u\|_{\alpha_yD_y}
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Origin symmetry gives the exact dual-norm identity
\[
 h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V)
 =\max_{\|u\|_{\alpha_yD_y}\leq1}\langle V,u\rangle.
\]
If \(h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V)\leq\pi_y\), homogeneity makes the last supremum zero, attained at \(u=0\). If \(h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V)>\pi_y\), scaling a support maximizer in \(\alpha_yD_y\) makes that supremum \(+\infty\). Hence
\[
 G_y^*(V)=
 \begin{cases}
 h_{K'_y}(V),&h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V)\leq\pi_y,\\
 +\infty,&h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V)>\pi_y.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since the product variables separate,
\[
 F^*((V_y)_{y\in I})
 =\sum_{y\in I}G_y^*(V_y).
\]

Under the product pairing, the orthogonal complement of \(\mathcal L\) is exactly
\[
 \mathcal L^\perp=
 \left\{
  (V_y)_{y\in I}:
  \sum_{y\in I}V_y=0,
  \quad
  \sum_{y\in I}V_yy^{\mathsf T}=0
 \right\}.
\]
Indeed, testing the restrictions of all ambient affine maps \(s(y)=b+Ay\) proves necessity. Conversely, every affine map on \(\operatorname{aff}I\) extends to an affine map on \(\mathbb R^n\), and the two displayed moment identities annihilate the evaluation of every such extension.

For completeness, we prove that there is no duality gap. The function \(F\) is coercive on \(E\). To see this, for every \(y\) choose \(R_y<\infty\) with \(\alpha_yD_y\subseteq R_y\mathbb B_2^d\) and \(M_y<\infty\) with \(K'_y\subseteq M_y\mathbb B_2^d\). Then
\[
 G_y(z)\geq\frac{\pi_y}{R_y}(\|z\|_2-M_y).
\]
Because \(I\) is finite and every \(\pi_y>0\), \(F(z)\to+\infty\) as the product norm of \(z\) tends to infinity. The closed subspace \(\mathcal L\) therefore contains a minimizer \(z^*\) of \(F\).

Let \(p(u):=F'(z^*;u)\) be the one-sided directional derivative. It is a finite sublinear function. Since \(z^*\) minimizes \(F\) on \(\mathcal L\), one has \(p(u)\geq0\) for every \(u\in\mathcal L\). The zero linear functional on \(\mathcal L\) is therefore dominated by \(p\). The finite-dimensional dominated-extension theorem extends it to a linear functional \(V\in E^*\) satisfying
\[
 V|_{\mathcal L}=0,
 \qquad
 \langle V,u\rangle\leq p(u)
 \quad(u\in E).
\]
For a finite convex function,
\[
 p(u)\leq F(z^*+u)-F(z^*),
\]
so \(V\in\partial F(z^*)\). Thus
\[
 F^*(V)=\langle V,z^*\rangle-F(z^*)=-F(z^*),
\]
where \(\langle V,z^*\rangle=0\) because \(V\in\mathcal L^\perp\) and \(z^*\in\mathcal L\). The computed formula for \(F^*\) shows that this \(V\) satisfies all support and moment constraints and that its dual objective equals the primal minimum. Conversely, for every feasible \(V\in\mathcal L^\perp\) and every \(z\in\mathcal L\), the conjugate inequality gives
\[
 F(z)+F^*(V)\geq\langle V,z\rangle=0,
\]
so no dual feasible value can exceed the primal value. This proves the displayed dual equality.

The primal minimum is attained as just proved. The dual maximum is also attained directly: each \(\alpha_yD_y\) contains some Euclidean ball \(r_y\mathbb B_2^d\), so
\[
 h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V_y)\geq r_y\|V_y\|_2.
\]
Consequently the product of the support-constraint sets is compact. Its intersection with the two closed moment constraints is compact and nonempty, and the dual objective is continuous there.

It remains to compare the defects. If \(y\notin I\), then \(\tau_y=0\), and full dimensionality of \(D_y\) gives \(U_y=0\). Hence the global moment identities remain valid after restricting their sums to \(I\). Define
\[
 V_y:=-U_y
 \qquad(y\in I).
\]
For \(y\in I\), origin symmetry of \(D_y\) and the definition of \(\alpha_y\) give
\[
 h_{\alpha_yD_y}(V_y)
 =\alpha_yh_{D_y}(-U_y)
 =\alpha_y\tau_y
 =\min\{\tau_y,\pi_y\}
 \leq\pi_y.
\]
Thus \((V_y)_{y\in I}\) is dual feasible. Its objective equals
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 -\sum_{y\in I}h_{K'_y}(-U_y)
 &=\sum_{y\in I}
 \left(
  \langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\alpha_y\tau_y
 \right)\\
 &=\sum_{y\in I}
 \left(
  \langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\min\{\tau_y,\pi_y\}
 \right)\\
 &\geq\sum_{y\in I}
 \bigl(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\pi_y\bigr)\\
 &\geq\sum_{y\in Z_+}
 \bigl(\langle U_y,c_y\rangle-\pi_y\bigr)
 =\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate inequality holds because \(U_y=0\) and \(\pi_y\geq0\) for \(y\notin I\). Maximizing over all dual feasible families proves
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\geq\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) when \(I\neq\varnothing\).

Finally suppose \(I=\varnothing\). Then \(U_y=0\) for every \(y\), and the exact endpoint identity gives
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}=-\sum_{y\in Z_+}\pi_y\leq0.
\]
The defining minimum for \(\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is nonnegative, so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}=0\). By the stated empty-product convention \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}=0\), and the desired comparison holds in this case as well. This also verifies the empty dual objective and constraints.

The constructions prove only the stated halfspace domination and diagonal defect comparison. They do not supply any diagonal or terminal absorption theorem, and they do not establish off-diagonal matching admissibility or the locked target.
