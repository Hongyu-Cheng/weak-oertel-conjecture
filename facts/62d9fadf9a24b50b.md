## Statement

Take the canonical hull of the intervals $[-1/2,1/2],[-1,1],[-1/2,1/2]$ at integer bases $-1,0,1$, with summed length $M=4$. For every $\tau\in(1/2,1)$ the two caps of margins $x$ and $-x$ minimize total hinge among full two-cap covers with unit continuous normals and each cap mass at most $\tau M$. Their cap masses are $2$, hinges $3/4$, section masses $3$, and cap and section base means zero. They have objective-one stress with contact $(z,0)$ at each base, two equal cap weights at each contact, coverage multipliers $(1,2,1)$, zero budget multipliers and unit section density. The origin is an actual deepest mixed point of normalized depth $1/2$, and these two caps attain its depth. For either cap the section-weighted slopes have mean $2/3$, zero covariance with the base, and variance $1/18$. The resulting hinge $3/4$ exceeds the variance-free bound $2/3$.

## Proof

The displayed fibers are actual. The two endpoint intervals average to an interval contained in the middle interval, and all other anchored representations are trivial or combine that inclusion with the middle fiber. The displayed caps cover all fibers, and the ordinary interval integrals give cap masses $1/2+1+1/2=2$ and hinge for each $(1^2+2^2+1^2)/8=3/4$. There is one proper section at $x=0$ in each interval.

For any unit-normal two-cap cover and any interval of width $w$, if one cap contains the interval then its hinge is at least $w^2/2$. If neither cap contains the interval, coverage requires one of each sign and thresholds $p\le q$ for $x\ge p$ and $x\le q$. The two hinges pointwise dominate $|x-(p+q)/2|$ because $q-p\ge0$. On an interval the integral of distance from any point is at least $w^2/4$, attained by its midpoint. Therefore the sum over the three actual fibers is at least $(1^2+2^2+1^2)/4=3/2$. The displayed cover attains the bound and each mass is strictly below $\tau M$.

The two margins have maximum $|x|$ with unique minimum at $0$ on each fiber, and the weighted average with two equal weights is zero for all $x$. Thus the proposed contact and weights are saddle data with balanced normals, and their positive weights occur at zero margins. Each cap's contact mass is $(1+2+1)/2=2$ and its contact base moment is zero by symmetry. Its continuous contact and section moments are both zero. Its continuous cap moment is $\pm3/4=\Psi_i u_i$. With budget multipliers zero this gives the objective-one mass, base, and continuous Euler balances and both complementarity conditions.

Reflection through the origin preserves the measured fibers, so every closed cap through the origin has at least half the total mass. The displayed caps have exactly half. At any mark, opposite nonvertical through-mark caps have total mass $M$ because their plane is volume-null, so one has mass at most $M/2$. This proves maximality. The section probabilities are $1/3$ on the three bases. The slopes are $(1/2,1,1/2)$ with mean $2/3$, zero covariance with the symmetric base, and variance $[(1/2-2/3)^2+(1-2/3)^2+(1/2-2/3)^2]/3=1/18$. The variance-free expression is $c^2/(2S)=2/3<3/4$.
