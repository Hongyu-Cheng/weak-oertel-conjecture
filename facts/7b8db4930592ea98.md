## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be planar convex bodies at bases $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. For three spanning normals $v_i$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$ and $y_0\in\operatorname{int}K$, suppose the ambient closed caps with respective fiber levels $2v_i\cdot y_0-h_i$, $v_i\cdot y_0$, $h_i=h_B(v_i)$ have a common mass $Q$ and proper cuts in $A,K$. Write $b_i=2v_i\cdot y_0-h_i$, let $s_i=2\sigma_{A,i}+\sigma_{K,i}$ with the old section lengths divided by $\|v_i\|$, and normalize $p_i=(1/s_i)/\sum_j(1/s_j)$. On a whole exposed face $F=[\alpha,\beta]\times\{0\}$ of $A$ with $A$ below it, let $W(s)=\sum_i p_i\mathbf1_{\{v_i\cdot(s,0)\ge b_i\}}$. Let $T_t$ be the piecewise-affine tent on $[\alpha,\beta]$ with endpoint heights zero and height one at $t$. Choose a relative-interior apex base $(t,0)$ in $\operatorname{int}E$ for $E=\{x:x+B\subseteq2K\}$, and let $A_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(A\cup\{(t,\varepsilon)\})$. Then $A_\varepsilon,K,B$ are the actual integer fibers of their lifted hull. At some $y_\varepsilon=y_0+O(\varepsilon)$ in $K$ the same normals and $B$ support thresholds give equal actual cap mass
$$
q_\varepsilon=Q+\varepsilon\int_\alpha^\beta T_t(s) W(s)\,ds+o(\varepsilon).
$$
If $Q$ was the global raw maximum and the ordinary $A$ maximum was strictly below $Q$, the new global raw maximum is at most $q_\varepsilon$, including when the unchanged $B$ maximum equals $Q$. With $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$, $r=Q/M$ and $C(t)=\int_\alpha^\beta T_t(s)[W(s)-r],ds$, the normalized upper certificate changes by $\varepsilon C(t)/M+o(\varepsilon)$. If $C(t)<0$ this gives strict normalized descent.

## Proof

The apex stays in the closed convex set $E$ for small $\varepsilon$, so midpoint inclusion holds for $A_\varepsilon,K,B$. Any combination of the three lifted bodies at base zero has equal outer weights and is in $K$ by that inclusion. The extreme sections are exactly the extreme bodies, and there are only three integer bases.

For a moving mark $y$, define the original cap rows $F_i(y)=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge v_i\cdot y\}|+|A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge2v_i\cdot y-h_i\}|$. The support traces in $B$ have area zero. At proper cuts, the chord lengths are continuous by concavity on the interior of the projection interval, hence $F_i$ is $C^1$ near $y_0$ and its derivative is $-s_i v_i$. The map $G(y,q)=(F_i(y)-q)_i$ is locally invertible near $(y_0,Q)$: multiply a Jacobian kernel relation by $1/s_i$ and sum to obtain zero $q$-component, and then use spanning. Write $\Delta_i^\varepsilon(y)$ for the added area in the moving $A$ cap. The tent lemma gives $\Delta_i^\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon)$ and, uniformly for $|y-y_0|=O(\varepsilon)$,
$$
\Delta_i^\varepsilon(y)=\varepsilon\int_\alpha^\beta T_t(s)\mathbf1_{\{v_i\cdot(s,0)\ge b_i\}}\,ds+o(\varepsilon).
$$
In a fixed small neighborhood of $y_0$ these functions have Lipschitz constants tending to zero: each transverse cutting line has length $O(\varepsilon)$ in the thin added band, and integrating those lengths bounds the moving-cap area change. Each parallel cutting line stays at a fixed positive distance from the band, so the added area in its cap is locally constant. The map $(y,q)\mapsto G^{-1}(-\Delta^\varepsilon(y))$ consequently has Lipschitz constant less than one on a sufficiently large ball of radius $O(\varepsilon)$ about $(y_0,Q)$. It maps that ball into itself by the bounded derivative of the inverse and the uniform added-area bound. Its fixed point gives the actual equal budgets. Expand the rows there and sum with the $p_i$ to cancel the mark derivatives, since $p_i s_i$ is constant. This gives the stated budget.

At the new mark the middle cap margins sum to zero, so they cover the whole middle plane. The original $A$ cap thresholds satisfy $\sum_i b_i=-\sum_i h_i<0$: evaluate the supports at one point in $\operatorname{int}B$, where every support inequality is strict. The original $A$ caps thus cover the plane, so $W\ge\min_i p_i>0$. The positive tent integral gives $q_\varepsilon>Q$ for small positive $\varepsilon$. The mixed depth at an outer base equals ordinary depth on its own fiber: one can isolate either extreme by a sufficiently large finite tilt for any own cut, and every ambient cut through the mark includes its own trace. At any continuous point the old ordinary $A$ depth rises by at most the total added area, since every halfplane does. The old depth is zero outside $A$, by strict separation, so this also bounds new marks. The strict $A$ gap persists and the unchanged $B$ maximum is at most $Q$. This proves the global upper bound. Dividing by the actual mass $M+\varepsilon\int T_t+o(\varepsilon)$ gives the coefficient and strict descent.
