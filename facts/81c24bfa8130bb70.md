## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(a_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(L\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\), and let \(\theta_i\in\mathbb R\) for \(1\le i\le n\). Define the complete original support and law by
\[
D=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:\textstyle\sum_i a_i z_i\le L\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{z\in D}e^{\theta\cdot z},\qquad
p_z=e^{\theta\cdot z}/Z.
\]
For \(j\in D\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of \(p(H\cap D)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j\), and let \(M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3\). These are the original-law conventions of accepted facts `5cebfce93303cec1` and `ac02a0ffd389f816`. The resource dimension \(n\) here is not identified with the locked problem's integer dimension in an application to a mixed-integer body.

Set \(L_0=\lfloor L\rfloor\). Suppose at least one \(\theta_i>0\), and choose any \(h\) attaining \(\max_i\theta_i/a_i\). Then \(\theta_h>0\). Put \(q=a_h\), and order \(I=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{h\}\) increasingly. Use transformed coordinates \(u=((y_i)_{i\in I},s)\), with the slack coordinate last, and define on the entire ambient space
\[
T_h(z)=((z_i)_{i\in I},L_0-\textstyle\sum_i a_i z_i),\qquad
T_h^{-1}(u)_i=y_i\ (i\in I),\qquad
T_h^{-1}(u)_h=\frac{L_0-\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i-s}{q}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the unscaled-slack version of the chart in accepted fact `5cebfce93303cec1`: when \(q\) divides \(L_0\) and every \(a_i\), the present slack \(s\) equals \(q\) times that fact's slack, and every other transformed coordinate is identical.

The map \(T_h\) is an invertible affine map of \(\mathbb R^n\). Its linear part has determinant of absolute value \(q\), and its integer image is exactly
\[
\mathcal L_h=\left\{((y_i)_{i\in I},s)\in\mathbb Z^n:
s+\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i\equiv L_0\pmod q\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
This is an affine coset of an index-\(q\) subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\). In particular, \(T_h\) is affine unimodular on the full coordinate lattice if and only if \(q=1\).

Define the complete transformed support, resource vector, score vector, and law by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde D
&=\left\{((y_i)_{i\in I},s)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:
\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i+s\le L_0,\quad
\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i+s\equiv L_0\pmod q\right\},\\
\widetilde a&=((a_i)_{i\in I},1),\qquad
\widetilde\theta=((\theta_i-\theta_h a_i/q)_{i\in I},-\theta_h/q),\\
\widetilde Z&=\sum_{u\in\widetilde D}e^{\widetilde\theta\cdot u},\qquad
\widetilde p_u=e^{\widetilde\theta\cdot u}/\widetilde Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Every coefficient of \(\widetilde a\) is a positive integer and every coordinate of \(\widetilde\theta\) is nonpositive. The support is the entire intersection of the closed nonnegative resource simplex with the affine coset (2). The following identities hold exactly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_h(D)&=\widetilde D,\qquad
\theta\cdot z=\theta_hL_0/q+\widetilde\theta\cdot T_h(z),\\
Z&=e^{\theta_hL_0/q}\widetilde Z,\qquad
p_z=\widetilde p_{T_h(z)}\quad(z\in D),\\
\tau_p(j)&=\tau_{\widetilde p}(T_h(j))\quad(j\in D),\qquad
M_3(p)=M_3(\widetilde p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
All boundary atoms are retained. These assertions include tied maximizing ratios, coordinates forced to zero on the lattice support, \(n=1\), \(L_0=0\), and every singleton support.

Here is a precise conditional consequence. Suppose there exists an absolute \(\kappa>0\) such that for every integer \(m\ge1\), every \(b\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^m\), every \(R\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), every \(q'\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), every \(r\in\mathbb Z\) with \(\gcd(q',b_1,\ldots,b_m)=1\), and every \(\varphi\in\mathbb R_{\le0}^m\), the nonempty support
\[
E=\{v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:b\cdot v\le R,
\ b\cdot v\equiv r\pmod {q'}\}
\]
with its full law \(\rho_v=e^{\varphi\cdot v}/\sum_{w\in E}e^{\varphi\cdot w}\) satisfies \(M_3(\rho)\ge\kappa8^{-m}\), using ambient \(\mathbb R^m\) closed-halfspace depth. The constant is required to be independent of the index \(q'\) and all the other inputs. Then every original \(D,p\) stated above, for every finite real score vector \(\theta\), satisfies \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-n}\). This is an implication with an explicit unproved antecedent, not a claimed extension of the decreasing full-lattice theorem in accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`.

## Proof

Because every \(a_i z_i\) is an integer on \(\mathbb Z^n\), the inequalities \(\sum_i a_i z_i\le L\) and \(\sum_i a_i z_i\le L_0\) are equivalent there. Hence replacing the real capacity by \(L_0\) changes neither \(D\), \(Z\), nor any probability. The support is finite, since \(0\le z_i\le L_0/a_i\), and contains zero. Thus \(Z\) is finite and strictly positive.

Assume some score is positive. The maximal ratio is positive, so the chosen \(h\) has \(\theta_h>0\) and \(q=a_h\ge1\). In input order \(((z_i)_{i\in I},z_h)\), the linear part of \(T_h\) is
\[
A_h=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&0\\-(a_i)_{i\in I}^{\mathsf T}&-q\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
Its determinant is \(-q\); returning to the original input order changes only the sign. Formula (1) is its inverse on all real vectors. Therefore the affine map is invertible regardless of the affine rank of its finite support.

Let \(t_h=((0)_{i\in I},L_0)\) be the translation. An integer input gives integer output, and direct substitution gives \(s+\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i=L_0-qz_h\), hence the congruence (2). Conversely, any integer \(u\) satisfying (2) makes the numerator for \(T_h^{-1}(u)_h\) divisible by \(q\), so (1) gives an integer inverse. Thus \(T_h(\mathbb Z^n)=\mathcal L_h\).

The subgroup parallel to this coset is
\(\Gamma_q=\{((y_i),s)\in\mathbb Z^n:s+\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i\equiv0\pmod q\}\). The homomorphism \(((y_i),s)\mapsto s+\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i\pmod q\) onto \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) is surjective because its slack coefficient is one. Its kernel is \(\Gamma_q\), which consequently has index \(q\). Moreover \(\mathcal L_h=t_h+\Gamma_q\). If \(q>1\), this is a proper coset, so \(T_h\) is not a bijection of the full integer coordinate lattice. If \(q=1\), the translation and the linear part are integral and the inverse (1) is integral, proving affine unimodularity.

For \(z\in D\), the slack \(s=L_0-\sum_i a_i z_i\) is a nonnegative integer, all \(y_i=z_i\) are nonnegative integers, and
\(\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i+s=L_0-qz_h\le L_0\). The congruence has already been checked. Hence \(T_h(z)\in\widetilde D\). Conversely, for \(u\in\widetilde D\), its congruence makes \(z_h\) in (1) integral and its weak resource inequality makes \(z_h\ge0\). The other inverse coordinates are nonnegative integers, and
\(\sum_i a_i z_i=L_0-s\le L_0\). Thus the inverse lies in \(D\). Together with global invertibility, these facts prove the one-to-one correspondence of the entire supports in (4).

The same substitution identifies the real simplex \(\{z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n:\sum_i a_i z_i\le L_0\}\) with \(\{u\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n:\widetilde a\cdot u\le L_0\}\). Its saturated resource face maps to \(s=0\), its anchor face \(z_h=0\) maps to \(\widetilde a\cdot u=L_0\), and each other coordinate face maps to \(y_i=0\). Every intersection of faces is retained because all inequalities are weak. For nonintegral \(L\), there is no lattice point on \(\sum_i a_i z_i=L\); replacing that capacity by \(L_0\) still retains every original lattice atom. This proves completeness and the boundary assertion without assuming the simplex has positive dimension on its lattice support.

Maximality of \(\theta_h/a_h=\theta_h/q\) gives \(\theta_i\le\theta_h a_i/q\) for every \(i\in I\). Thus the corresponding transformed scores are nonpositive, including ratio ties, and the slack score \(-\theta_h/q\) is strictly negative. Substituting the inverse gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta\cdot z
&=\sum_{i\in I}\theta_i y_i
 +\frac{\theta_h}{q}\left(L_0-\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i-s\right)\\
&=\frac{\theta_h L_0}{q}
 +\sum_{i\in I}\left(\theta_i-\frac{\theta_h a_i}{q}\right)y_i
 -\frac{\theta_h}{q}s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Summing this score identity over the support bijection proves the normalizer identity in (4). Cancelling its common factor proves \(p_z=\widetilde p_{T_h(z)}\). In particular, \(\widetilde p\) is exactly the pushforward of the original law. The determinant \(q\) creates no multiplicity or Jacobian factor in this finite sum: there is precisely one transformed atom for each original atom.

For a proper closed halfspace \(H=\{z:\eta\cdot z\ge v\}\) with \(\eta\ne0\), the image under \(T_h(z)=A_hz+t_h\) is
\[
T_h(H)=\{u:(A_h^{-\mathsf T}\eta)\cdot u
\ge v+(A_h^{-\mathsf T}\eta)\cdot t_h\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Its normal is nonzero by invertibility. The inverse affine map gives the inverse correspondence of proper closed halfspaces. Therefore these maps biject all closed halfspaces containing \(j\) with all closed halfspaces containing \(T_h(j)\), retaining equality points. The whole space, if admitted by the convention, also maps to itself. For each corresponding pair, the support and probability identities give
\(p(H\cap D)=\widetilde p(T_h(H)\cap\widetilde D)\). Taking the infimum proves the depth identity in (4). Summing its cube with the preserved root probabilities proves \(M_3(p)=M_3(\widetilde p)\). This argument uses only ambient affine invertibility and exact atomic pushforward, not unimodularity or a full-rank support.

For \(n=1\), the empty-coordinate convention reduces the chart to \(s=L_0-qz_h\), with inverse \(z_h=(L_0-s)/q\). Its full image support is \(\{s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:s\le L_0,\ s\equiv L_0\pmod q\}\), and every preceding equality remains valid. For \(L_0=0\), both supports are the singleton zero. More generally, if \(D\) is a singleton, it is \(\{0\}\), its image is \(\{t_h\}\), and each law assigns mass one to its unique atom. Every containing halfspace then has mass one, so each depth and third moment equals one. Coordinates forced to zero impose no restriction on the ambient halfspace bijection.

To identify the exact relation to the accepted divisible chart, suppose \(q\mid L_0\) and \(q\mid a_i\) for every \(i\in I\). Alias the capacity \(L\) of accepted fact `5cebfce93303cec1` to \(L_0\), its \(Q\) to \(L_0/q\), and its \(r_i\) to \(a_i/q\), leaving its original \(n,D,Z,p,\theta,h\) identical to the present ones. Its transformed coordinate \(y\) is \(((y_i)_{i\in I},s/q)\). In this case the congruence in (3) is precisely \(q\mid s\), and dividing the slack by \(q\) transforms (3) into that fact's complete full-lattice support \(\sum_{i\in I}r_i y_i+s/q\le Q\). Its transformed score is \(((\theta_i-\theta_h r_i)_{i\in I},-\theta_h)\), which evaluates to the same expression as the present \(\widetilde\theta\cdot u\). Thus the composition of (1) with this slack rescaling is exactly its unimodular chart.

In general that rescaling does not produce its full-lattice simplex. For instance, take \(n=1\), \(a_1=2\), \(L=3\), and \(\theta_1=1\). Then \(D=\{0,1\}\), while (1) maps it to \(\widetilde D=\{3,1\}\) with decreasing slack score \(-1/2\). The full integer resource interval \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) has two additional atoms. Accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816` requires the entire full-coordinate-lattice resource support, so it cannot be directly instantiated with this \(\widetilde D\). The example verifies the distinction between an exact decreasing coset law and an already covered decreasing full-lattice law; it asserts no failure of a coset moment inequality.

Finally assume the conditional consumer in the statement. If some original score is positive, use it with \(m=n\), \(b=\widetilde a\), \(R=L_0\), \(q'=q\), \(r=L_0\), \(\varphi=\widetilde\theta\), \(E=\widetilde D\), and \(\rho=\widetilde p\). Its coprimality condition holds because the last resource coefficient is one, its support is nonempty by the exact bijection, and its scores are nonpositive. It gives \(M_3(\widetilde p)\ge\kappa8^{-n}\), which transfers to the original law by (4). If all original scores are nonpositive, apply the same hypothetical consumer with \(m=n\), \(b=a\), \(R=L_0\), \(q'=1\), \(r=0\), \(\varphi=\theta\), and \(E=D\); the congruence is automatic. These inputs are also directly in the already accepted full-lattice class of `ac02a0ffd389f816`, with its capacity \(R\) aliased to \(L_0\) and all its original-law objects unchanged. Thus every score vector is covered by the conditional implication, always with the original law and normalizer.
