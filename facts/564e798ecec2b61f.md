## Statement

Let $E_1,\ldots,E_p,F_1,\ldots,F_q$ be independent exponential random variables of mean one. Let $a_i,b_j$ be positive with $\sum_i a_i=\sum_j b_j>0$ and put
$$
P(a,b)=\Pr\left(\sum_i a_iE_i\ge\sum_j b_jF_j\right).
$$
Lists of nonnegative coefficients are allowed after zero entries are discarded. If $p\ge2$, $a_i=\max_k a_k$, and $j\ne i$ is another positive index, then replacing $(a_i,a_j)$ by $(a_i+t,a_j-t)$ strictly decreases $P(a,b)$ for every $0<t\le a_j$. At $t=0$ it leaves $P(a,b)$ unchanged. Any tied largest index can be chosen. A zero donor allows only the unchanged transfer.

For all $p,q\ge1$,
$$
P(a,b)\ge\prod_{j=1}^q\left(1+\frac{b_j}{\sum_i a_i}\right)^{-1}
\ge\Gamma_q,\qquad \Gamma_q=\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)^q.
$$
Equality in the first bound holds exactly when $p=1$. Equality at $\Gamma_q$ holds exactly when $p=1$ and the $b_j$ are equal. For each fixed positive phase count $p$ and negative phase count $q$, $\Gamma_q$ is the infimum.

## Proof

**Conditional means.** Consider a sum $S=\sum_k Z_k$ of independent $Z_k=c_k E_k$ with nonzero coefficients of both signs. Set $\mu=\sum_k c_k$ and $c_+=\max_k c_k$. Write $r_k$ for the signed-exponential density of $Z_k$, $g_k$ for the density of $S-Z_k$, and $f$ for the density of $S$. A positive and a negative phase have a convolution positive everywhere. Convolving their sum with the other phases gives $f(s)>0$ on $\mathbb R$.

The kernel $r_k(x-y)$ has nonnegative two-by-two determinants for increasing row and column arguments. Indeed, if the off-diagonal product is positive, the two diagonal arguments lie between the off-diagonal arguments in the same halfline, and the two products coincide by the exponential formula. If the off-diagonal product vanishes, the inequality is automatic. This determinant property persists under convolution. For density kernels $K(x,z)=k(x-z)$ and $L(z,y)=l(z-y)$, the determinant of their composed kernel at $(x_1,x_2)$ and $(y_1,y_2)$ is
$$
\int_{u<v}
\det\begin{pmatrix}K(x_1,u)&K(x_1,v)\\K(x_2,u)&K(x_2,v)\end{pmatrix}
\det\begin{pmatrix}L(u,y_1)&L(u,y_2)\\L(v,y_1)&L(v,y_2)\end{pmatrix}
\,du\,dv.
$$
This follows by expanding the products and exchanging $u,v$ on the other ordered half of the double integral. All terms are integrable since the convolution densities are bounded and integrable. Thus $g_k(s-z)$ has this property, including when $g_k$ has one-sided support.

Define the conditional density $p_s(z)=r_k(z)g_k(s-z)/f(s)$ and the conditional mean $m_k(s)=\int z p_s(z)\,dz$. For $s_1<s_2$ and $u<v$, the determinant property gives $p_{s_1}(u)p_{s_2}(v)\ge p_{s_1}(v)p_{s_2}(u)$. Integrating the difference against $v-u$ on $u<v$ gives $m_k(s_2)-m_k(s_1)\ge0$. The numerator and denominator of $m_k$ are continuous by $L^1$ translation continuity of $g_k$, since both $r_k(z)$ and $z r_k(z)$ are bounded. In particular $f$ is continuous. The identity $\sum_k m_k(s)=s$ holds for almost every $s$ by conditioning and extends everywhere by continuity and $f>0$. The nonnegative increments of the $m_k$ sum to the increment of $s$, so each $m_k$ is 1-Lipschitz.

**Density derivative.** Set $\tau(s)=\sum_k c_k m_k(s)$. The conditional densities assign probability one to $c_k Z_k>0$, so $\tau>0$. It is locally Lipschitz, with $\tau'\le c_+$ almost everywhere because $m_k'\ge0$ and $\sum_k m_k'=1$. Exponential integration by parts gives $\mathbb E[(E_k-1)h(E_k)]=\mathbb E[E_k h'(E_k)]$ for a bounded smooth test $h$ with bounded derivative. Applying this identity to a smooth compactly supported $\phi(S)$ with other phases held fixed gives
$$
\mathbb E[(Z_k-c_k)\phi(S)]=\mathbb E[c_kZ_k\phi'(S)].
$$
The coefficient on the right after differentiating is $c_k^2 E_k$, valid for both signs. Summing gives $(\tau f)'=(\mu-s)f$ in distributions. The right side is continuous, so $\tau f$ is continuously differentiable and $f$ is locally absolutely continuous. Almost everywhere,
$$
f'(s)\ge\frac{\mu-s-c_+}{\tau(s)}f(s).
$$
This gives the density monotonicity on $(-\infty,\mu-c_+]$. On an interval where $f$ is continuously differentiable, the inequality holds everywhere by continuity.

**Transfer.** Use the current coefficients and write $V=\sum_k a_kE_k-\sum_\ell b_\ell F_\ell$. The negative sum has bounded density $h$ continuous on $(0,\infty)$, including when it has just one phase. Conditioning on the positive sum and differentiating its argument in the negative-sum distribution function is justified by the bound $\lVert h\rVert_\infty E_i$. For an independent mean-one exponential $E_i'$, size bias replaces $E_i$ by $E_i+E_i'$, since the latter has density $t e^{-t}$ on $t>0$. Therefore
$$
\partial_{a_i}P=\mathbb E\left[E_i h\left(\sum_k a_kE_k\right)\right]
=f_{V+a_iE_i'}(0).
$$
These partial derivatives are continuous on the positive coefficient domain by dominated convergence and continuity of $h$ there. The density evaluations use the continuous convolution versions. Put $W=V+a_iE_i'+a_jE_j'$ with independent added phases. If $W=X+cE'$ for one of these phases, the convolution formula
$$
f_W(x)=\frac1c\int_{-\infty}^x e^{-(x-u)/c} f_X(u)\,du
$$
gives $f_W+c f_W'=f_X$. Here $f_X$ is continuous because it is a convolution of at least two bounded integrable phase densities. Thus $f_W'$ is continuous. Removing the two added phases in turn and subtracting gives
$$
\partial_{a_i}P-\partial_{a_j}P=(a_j-a_i)f_W'(0).
$$
The balanced totals give $\mathbb EW=a_i+a_j$. If $a_i$ is largest, the largest positive coefficient of $W$ is $a_i$. The density derivative estimate at zero gives $f_W'(0)\ge a_j f_W(0)/\tau_W(0)>0$, with $\tau_W$ defined as above for the phases of $W$. Along a transfer into $a_i$, the receiver remains largest and is strictly greater than the donor for every $0<t<a_j$. Hence the derivative along the path is negative there. At the final endpoint, couple the variables before and after deleting the vanishing coefficient. The signed sum converges almost surely and its limiting law has no atom at zero, since a positive and a negative phase remain. The probabilities converge by bounded convergence. This gives strict decrease for every positive transfer including the endpoint, whether or not there was a tie initially.

**Sharp bound.** Let $A=\sum_i a_i$. If $p>1$, finitely many transfers into a largest positive coefficient strictly decrease $P$ to the single-phase probability
$$
\Pr\left(AE_1\ge\sum_j b_jF_j\right)=\prod_j(1+b_j/A)^{-1}.
$$
Since $\sum_j b_j/A=1$, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives the bound $\Gamma_q$ with equality in the product bound exactly when all the $b_j$ are equal. For a fixed $p>1$, take the common total to be one, choose all $b_j=1/q$, and let $p-1$ positive coefficients be $\varepsilon$ with the remaining one $1-(p-1)\varepsilon$. The same no-atom convergence as above makes the probability tend to $\Gamma_q$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. For $p=1$ the bound is attained.
