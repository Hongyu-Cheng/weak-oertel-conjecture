## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, at the three integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Fix $y_0\in\operatorname{int}K$ and $d+1$ spanning normals $v_i$ with sole dependence $\sum_i v_i=0$. Put $s_{i0}=v_i\cdot y_0$, $h_i=h_B(v_i)$, and $\beta_i=2s_{i0}-h_i$. Assume the caps with respective continuous thresholds $\beta_i,s_{i0},h_i$ on the three bases have common raw mass $Q$. Write $U_i=|A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge\beta_i\}|$.

Fix $p\in A$, $q\in B$ and contraction rates $u,v\ge0$. For small $t\ge0$, use $A_t=p+(1-ut)(A-p)$, $B_t=q+(1-vt)(B-q)$ and unchanged $K$. Put $c_i=\beta_i-v_i\cdot p$ and $e_i=h_i-v_i\cdot q$. At each $t$ there is a unique nearby meeting point $y(t)$ with equal budget $Q(t)$ for the retained normals and the actual B support thresholds $h_i-tv e_i$. Their middle traces cover $\mathbb R^d$. Let $k_i$ denote the K section area at $s_{i0}$ divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, and let $\sigma_i$ denote the analogously normalized A section at $\beta_i$, using the support-face limit at an extreme threshold and zero for strict exterior thresholds. Put $\psi_i(x)=x$ for proper A cuts, $x_+$ for a full cut at minimum support, $\min(x,0)$ for an empty cut at maximum support, and $0$ for strict exterior cuts. The actual menu loss $L=-Q'(0+)$ is the unique zero of $\Phi(L;u,v)=\sum_i Z_i(L;u,v)$, where
$$
Z_i(L;u,v)=\frac{L-duU_i}{k_i}
 -\frac{\sigma_i}{k_i(k_i+2\sigma_i)}
 \psi_i\!\left(2L-2duU_i+k_i(uc_i+ve_i)\right).
$$
The meeting-point right velocity is given by $v_i\cdot y'(0+)=Z_i(L;u,v)$ for every $i$.

## Proof

The endpoint contractions are nested, so compatibility persists. The three lifted fibers are exact in their convex hull because any convex combination at base $0$ uses equal total weights at the endpoints, and at either extreme base it uses only that fiber. The exact new B support is $h_i-tv e_i$ with $e_i\ge0$, so the middle level $s$ gives the raw cap mass
$$
f_i(s,t)=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge s\}|+
|A_t\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge2s-h_i+tv e_i\}|.
$$
The B trace is supporting and has zero $d$-volume. The middle cut is proper near $s_{i0}$. All normalized parallel section areas are bounded on these compact bodies, and the middle area is continuous and bounded away from zero near its proper cut. Fubini therefore bounds the absolute slope in $s$ of $f_i$ above and away from zero uniformly near $s_{i0}$ for small $t$. The upper bound may use enclosing balls and also holds at supporting A levels. Changing $t$ changes A volume by at most the volume removed in the nested contraction plus the volume of a strip of width $O(t)$, uniformly in $s$. Thus $f_i(s,t)=f_i(s,0)+O(t)$. On a fixed small common interval about $Q$, each $f_i$ has a unique nearby continuous local inverse in $s$, strictly decreasing in its mass argument. Its value at $Q$ changes by $O(t)$. The sum of the inverses has opposite signs at the endpoints of this interval for small $t$, because it is strictly decreasing with old zero at $Q$. Its slope in absolute value is bounded below by the upper slope bound for each $f_i$, so it has a unique zero $Q(t)=Q+O(t)$. The inverse levels there are $s_{i0}+O(t)$ and have sum zero. The map $y\mapsto (v_i\cdot y)_i$ is an isomorphism onto that sum-zero space. This gives $y(t)=y_0+O(t)$, in the interior for small $t$, and the resulting middle traces cover the plane because their margins sum to zero.

For bounded $z_i$ and $s=s_{i0}+t z_i$, pulling the A cut back through its homothety gives threshold $\beta_i+t(2z_i+uc_i+ve_i)+O(t^2)$ and volume factor $(1-ut)^d$. Proper section areas are continuous. Sections tending to a support plane converge to its face after parallel translation: any limit point lies in the face, and interpolation of the whole face with a fixed interior point approximates the face uniformly from inside. Convex section volumes then converge, including to zero for a lower-dimensional face. When $d=1$, sections inside the support interval and the extreme singleton have counting measure one. These one-sided limits and Fubini give, uniformly for bounded $z_i$,
$$
f_i(s_{i0}+tz_i,t)=Q+t[-k_i z_i-duU_i-\sigma_i\psi_i(2z_i+uc_i+ve_i)]+o(t).
$$
In particular the empty supporting cut has a negative-part term, and the full supporting cut a positive-part term. Solving the bracketed response equal to $-L$ gives the stated inverse: on an active branch its value is $(L-duU_i-\sigma_i(uc_i+ve_i))/(k_i+2\sigma_i)$, and on an inactive branch it is $(L-duU_i)/k_i$. The sign of the hinge argument on either branch is the sign of $2L-2duU_i+k_i(uc_i+ve_i)$, since their respective denominators are positive. This proves the inverse including ties. Each $Z_i$ is continuous, strictly increasing and onto with slopes between $1/(k_i+2\sigma_i)$ and $1/k_i$. Their sum thus has exactly one zero. The already established $O(t)$ bounds make the difference quotients bounded, and any convergent subsequence must solve the response equations and the sum-zero relation. The solution is unique, so the actual right derivatives exist.
