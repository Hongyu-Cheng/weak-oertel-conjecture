## Statement

Let $\mu$ be a finite positive mixed source with total mass $m>0$, positive full-dimensional convex fibers on finitely many integer bases, and true centroid $(j,c)$. Let positive mixed measures $\nu,\rho$ have masses $\delta,\kappa\delta$, where $\delta>0$ and $0\le\kappa\le1$, and equal first base moments about $j$. Assume $\mu'=\mu+\nu-\rho$ is a positive source, with mass $m'=m+(1-\kappa)\delta$ and centroid $(j,c')$. For $L(z,x)=a\cdot(z-j)+u\cdot(x-c)$ with $\|u\|=1$, let $H_s=\{L\ge s\}$ and $s_H=u\cdot(c'-c)$. Then
$$
 c'-c=\frac{\int(x-c)\,d(\nu-\rho)}{m'},\qquad s_H=\frac{\int L\,d(\nu-\rho)}{m'},
$$
and for any $\beta$,
$$
 \mu'(H_{s_H})-\beta m'
 =\mu(H_0)-\beta m+\nu(H_{s_H})-\rho(H_{s_H})
 -\beta(1-\kappa)\delta+\mu(H_{s_H})-\mu(H_0).
$$
For pure-base caps use $s_H=0$. Let $R$ bound $\|x-c\|$ on the additions and removals, and $L_0$ bound the sum of old fiber section areas uniformly over levels and unit fiber normals, with counting measure on sections when $d=1$. If every old closed through-centroid cap has mass at least $\beta m+\sigma$, then every closed cap containing the new centroid has mass at least $\beta m'$ provided
$$
 [\beta(1-\kappa)+\kappa]\delta+
 \frac{L_0R(1+\kappa)\delta}{m+(1-\kappa)\delta}\le\sigma .
$$

## Proof

Subtract the old centered first moments from the new ones. The vanishing signed base moment proves the common base and the two displacement identities. The translated cap passes through $(j,c')$. Expanding $\mu'=\mu+\nu-\rho$ on this very closed cap and adding and subtracting $\mu(H_0)$ proves the deficit identity, also for pure-base caps with their unchanged levels. No boundary mass is altered by the algebra.

The displacement is at most $R(1+\kappa)\delta/m'$. In nonvertical directions the old source loses at most $L_0|s_H|$ by coarea of each fiber along the unit normal, since these section boundaries have zero fiber volume. For $d=1$ one uses at most one point per fiber at each level, so $L_0=|P|$ suffices. In pure-base directions the old cap does not move. The added measure is nonnegative and the removed mass is at most $\kappa\delta$. Substituting these estimates yields the sufficient inequality for every through-centroid cap. Any closed cap containing the centroid contains a parallel through-centroid cap, giving the final conclusion.
