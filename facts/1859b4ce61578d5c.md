## Statement

Put $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$ and $V=|T|=1/2$. Take the convex hull of the three fibers $r_zT$ at $z=-1,0,1$ with radii $(1/2,3/2,3/4)$. For the actual middle centroid $y=(0,1/2,1/2)$, let $\mu$ sum the areas of the integer fibers, and write
$$
H_{a,\beta}=\{(z,x):a x_1+(1-a)x_2+\beta z\ge1/2\},\qquad 1/2\le a\le1,\quad\beta\in\mathbb R.
$$
Then the infima of $\mu(H_{a,\beta})/V$ over $\beta$ for $a=1$ and $a=1/2$ are $33/32$ and $5/4$, respectively. They are attained at $\beta=-1/8$ and at every $\beta\in[-1/4,1/8]$, respectively. Interchanging the coordinates gives the same minimum for the other coordinate normal. At $(a,\beta)=(6/7,-3/28)$ the mass in units of $V$ is $81/80$, with strict gap $3/160$ below the coordinate minimum. Thus restricting normalized nonnegative continuous normals to the two coordinate normals and the equal-coefficient normal can increase the infimum even with arbitrary shared tilt.

## Proof

The endpoint fibers are unchanged by taking the convex hull. At zero the midpoint radius of the endpoints is $5/8$, so every convex combination with base zero belongs to $(3/2)T$ and this fiber is also unchanged. There are no other integer bases. The body has interior because there are positive-area fibers at distinct bases.

For $1/2<a<1$, the edge-intercept computation in accepted fact b026600b8d8e369b gives the cap area in $rT$, divided by $V$, as $r^2-t^2/[a(1-a)]$ for $0\le t\le(1-a)r$, and $(ar-t)^2/[a(2a-1)]$ for $(1-a)r\le t\le ar$. The same two edge fractions in its proof stay in $[0,1]$ through the entire latter interval. A threshold $t\le0$ takes the whole fiber and $t\ge ar$ has zero area. At $a=1$ the proper cap is a simplex of scale $r-t$ for $0\le t\le r$. At $a=1/2$ its complement has scale $2t$ for $0\le t\le r/2$. The thresholds on the three fibers here are $1/2+\beta,1/2,1/2-\beta$.

At $a=1$ the middle cap contributes one in units of $V$. For $-1/4\le\beta\le0$, the two other contributions are $\beta^2$ and $(1/4+\beta)^2$. The total is $33/32+2(\beta+1/8)^2$. For $\beta\ge0$ the right cap is at least its value $1/16$ at zero tilt, by monotonicity in the threshold. For $\beta\le-1/4$ the left cap is at least its value $1/16$ at $\beta=-1/4$. Thus neither exterior interval attains a smaller value. The source and mark are unchanged by swapping the two continuous coordinates.

At $a=1/2$ the middle cap always gives $(3/2)^2-4(1/2)^2=5/4$. The two endpoint caps have zero area precisely when $1/2+\beta\ge1/4$ and $1/2-\beta\ge3/8$, which holds on the stated interval.

At $(6/7,-3/28)$ the thresholds are $11/28,1/2,17/28$. Each lies between the second-largest height $(1-a)r$ and the largest $ar$. The respective positive margins $ar-t$ are $1/28,11/14,1/28$. Their squared sum divided by the common denominator $a(2a-1)=30/49$ is $81/80$. Subtracting from $33/32$ gives $3/160$, and $33/32<5/4$. All proper cut lines are area-null, so the formulas give the mass of the closed caps.
