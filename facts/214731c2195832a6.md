## Statement

For $m\ge1$ let $P_m=\{0,\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_m\}\subset\mathbb Z^m$ and $g_m(p)=(1+\sum_i p_i)/2$. The relative Helly number of $P_m$ is $m+1$. The integral graph filter $T_m$ consists of the $2m$ lifted nonzero vertices, has $h(T_m)=2m$, and its two height slices have Helly number $m$ each. Its saturated affine integer graph lattice has rank $m$ and projects bijectively to the odd-coordinate-sum coset of an index-two sublattice of $\mathbb Z^m$. In particular $h(T_m)/h(P_m)=2m/(m+1)\to2$ despite equal original and fine affine ranks.

## Proof

The source $P_m$ is the full lattice set in the unit $\ell_1$ ball. For a finite source use the upper bound in the empty-vertex-set lemma of bee3cc8e50a0dcea. Its reverse bound follows by taking the deletion hulls of an empty vertex set of size at least two. Each deletion hull omits its deleted vertex and all their common source points would have to lie in the empty hull, so the whole family misses the source while every proper subfamily meets it. For a singleton witness the lower bound one is automatic. Thus $h(P_m)$ is the largest size of a set of vertices whose hull has no other source points.

The simplex on $0,e_1,\ldots,e_m$ gives an empty vertex set of size $m+1$. Any set of at least $m+2$ sites containing zero also contains two opposite vertices, so zero fails to be a vertex of its hull. Such a set omitting zero contains an opposite pair as well, so its hull contains the omitted source point zero. This proves the upper bound. The heights are one at the positive vertices, zero at the negative vertices, and $1/2$ at zero. Since projection preserves the coordinate exposures, the retained graph is in convex position. Its whole vertex set, and separately each entire height slice in its own source, is an empty vertex set. This gives the indicated three Helly numbers.

The nonzero vertices span $\mathbb R^m$ affinely, so their lifted affine hull is the graph plane $2u-\sum_i p_i=1$. Its integer points are precisely the graph of $u=(1+\sum_i p_i)/2$ over integer $p$ of odd coordinate sum. The even-sum lattice has rank $m$ and index two by the surjective sum-modulo-two map. This proves the rank and index assertions and the limiting ratio.
