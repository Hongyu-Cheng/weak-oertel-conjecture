## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\ge1\) be integers with \(2h\le L<3h\), and let \(\beta\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) be finite. On the original support
\[
 E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:
                        \sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\},
 \qquad
 R(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{\exp(\beta\sum_i x_i+\theta y)}
    {\sum_{(\mathbf z,v)\in E_{L,h,m}}
                           \exp(\beta\sum_i z_i+\theta v)},
\]
suppose the full mean is \((j\mathbf1,1)\), where \(j\ge1\) is an integer. Write \(X_\Sigma=\sum_{i=1}^m X_i\), \(\mu_y=\mathbb E_R[X_\Sigma\mid Y=y]\), and \(v_1=\operatorname{Var}_R(X_\Sigma\mid Y=1)\). For nonnegative integers \(B\), define the original transverse partition function
\[
 Z_m(B,u)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\\
                                      \sum_i x_i\le B}}
                                      \exp(u\sum_i x_i).
\]
Set \(Z_y=Z_m(L-hy,\beta)\) for \(y=0,1,2\). The original endpoint probability is exactly
\[
 \varepsilon=\Pr_R(Y=0)=\Pr_R(Y=2)
       =\frac{\sqrt{Z_0Z_2}}{Z_1+2\sqrt{Z_0Z_2}}.
\]
Then \(v_1\ge1/20\), and the uniform bound
\[
 \varepsilon(\mu_0+\mu_2-2\mu_1)^4
                         \le 2^{36}v_1^2
\]
holds for every such original law. No product-cap conclusion is asserted from this inequality alone.

## Proof

All ingredients are proved below without mathematical predecessors. Put \(r=e^\beta\), \(a=L-2h\), \(b=L-h=a+h\), and \(c=L=a+2h\). The three slice budgets, in increasing order, are \(a,b,c\), and \(0\le a<h\), \(c\le2b\). The number of nonnegative \(m\)-vectors of total \(s\) is \(\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}\), by inserting \(m-1\) separators into a string of \(s\) units. Thus the conditional total at budget \(B\) has probabilities proportional to
\[
 w_s=\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}r^s\quad(0\le s\le B),
 \qquad
 Z_m(B,\beta)=\sum_{s=0}^B w_s.
\]
Its successive weight ratios satisfy \(w_{s+1}/w_s=r(s+m)/(s+1)\), and are nonincreasing in \(s\). In particular each conditional total law is log-concave with positive integer-interval support.

The original height probabilities are proportional to \(Z_0,e^\theta Z_1,e^{2\theta}Z_2\). Since the height support is precisely \(\{0,1,2\}\), the condition \(\mathbb E_RY=1\) gives equal endpoint probabilities, hence \(e^{2\theta}Z_2=Z_0\). This proves the stated exact formula for \(\varepsilon\).

First establish the variance lower bound using the full mean. The slack \(L-X_\Sigma-hY\) is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive at the positive-mass atom \((\mathbf0,0)\). Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_R[L-X_\Sigma-hY]=b-mj>0.
\]
It is an integer, so \(b-mj\ge1\) and \(b\ge m+1\). Write \(p_s=w_s/Z_m(b,\beta)\) for the middle-slice probabilities and \(p_*:=\max_{0\le s\le b}p_s\).

Suppose first that \(r<1\). On all nonnegative transverse vectors the untruncated weights \(r^{\sum_i x_i}\), normalized by \((1-r)^m\), give independent geometric coordinates with mean \(r/(1-r)\). Its total mean is \(mr/(1-r)\). Conditioning this total on being at most any finite budget lowers its mean: the conditional mean on that event is at most the conditional mean on its complement, so is at most the unconditional mean. Every original slice is such a conditioning. The full mean consequently gives \(mj\le mr/(1-r)\), and thus \(r\ge j/(j+1)\ge1/2\).

Every possible location of a maximum middle-slice atom has an adjacent atom of at least half its mass. At \(s=0\), the next-atom ratio is \(mr\ge1/2\). At \(s=b\), the preceding-atom ratio is
\[
 \frac{p_{b-1}}{p_b}=
       \frac{b}{r(b+m-1)}\ge\frac{b}{b+m-1}\ge\frac12.
\]
At \(1\le s\le b-1\), the two adjacent ratios have product
\[
 \frac{p_{s-1}p_{s+1}}{p_s^2}
    =\frac{s(s+m)}{(s+1)(s+m-1)}\ge\frac12,
\]
because twice the numerator minus the denominator is \(s^2+m(s-1)+1\ge0\). At least one adjacent ratio is therefore at least \(1/\sqrt2\), hence at least \(1/2\). It follows that \(p_*\le2/3\) in this case.

Suppose next that \(r\ge1\). The weights are nondecreasing, so \(p_*=p_b\). If \(p_b>9/10\), the middle-slice first deficit ratio would satisfy
\[
 \eta:=\frac{b}{r(b+m-1)}
       =\frac{p_{b-1}}{p_b}
       \le\frac{1-p_b}{p_b}<\frac19.
\]
For every positive \(B\le c\le2b\), monotonicity of \(B/(B+m-1)\) and the inequality
\(2b/(2b+m-1)\le2b/(b+m-1)\) give
\(B/[r(B+m-1)]\le2\eta<2/9\). In a slice of budget \(B\), write the deficit as \(D_B=B-X_\Sigma\). Its unnormalized deficit weight relative to the top atom is
\[
 H_d:=\frac{w_{B-d}}{w_B},\qquad H_0=1,
 \qquad
 \frac{H_d}{H_{d-1}}
      =\frac{B-d+1}{r(B-d+m)}
      \le\frac{B}{r(B+m-1)}<\frac29
      \quad(1\le d\le B).
\]
Consequently \(H_d\le(2/9)^d\), and division by \(\sum_{d=0}^B H_d\ge1\) shows
\[
 \mathbb E D_B\le\sum_{d\ge0}d(2/9)^d
                  =\frac{2/9}{(1-2/9)^2}
                  =\frac{18}{49}<1.
\]
At budget zero the deficit is zero. Thus every original slice would have expected slack less than one, contradicting their average \(b-mj\ge1\). This proves \(p_*\le9/10\) also when \(r\ge1\).

For an independent copy \(X_\Sigma'\) of the middle-slice total, integrality gives
\[
 2v_1=\mathbb E(X_\Sigma-X_\Sigma')^2
     \ge\Pr(X_\Sigma\ne X_\Sigma')
     =1-\sum_s p_s^2\ge1-p_*\ge\frac1{10}.
\]
Hence \(v_1\ge1/20\), as asserted.

We next prove a scalar estimate for any finitely supported log-concave integer law with contiguous positive support. Let its random variable be \(V\), with mean \(\mu\), variance \(\sigma^2\), and \(\sigma^2\ge1/20\). Its survival sequence \(Q(k)=\Pr(V\ge k)\) is log-concave. To check this directly, write its masses as \(q_i\), extended by zero. At an index in the positive support, the nonincreasing successive mass ratios imply \(q_kq_i\ge q_{k-1}q_{i+1}\) for \(i\ge k\), with the assertion trivial if \(q_{k-1}=0\). Summing gives
\[
 Q(k)^2-Q(k-1)Q(k+1)
       =q_kQ(k)-q_{k-1}Q(k+1)\ge0.
\]
Before or after the positive support, the same survival inequality is immediate from the constant-one or constant-zero values. The reflected law has the same property.

Set \(k_0=\lfloor\mu-2\sigma\rfloor\) and \(k_1=\lceil\mu+2\sigma\rceil\). Chebyshev's inequality gives \(Q(k_0)\ge3/4\) and \(Q(k_1)\le1/4\). Their integer difference \(\ell=k_1-k_0\) satisfies
\[
 1\le\ell\le4\sigma+2\le13\sigma,
\]
using \(\sigma\ge1/\sqrt{20}\) and \(4+2\sqrt{20}<13\). If \(Q(k_1)=0\), every upper tail at distance at least \(2\sigma\) vanishes. Otherwise, concavity of \(\log Q\) gives, for \(k\ge k_1\) whenever \(Q(k)>0\),
\[
 \log Q(k)-\log Q(k_1)
    \le\frac{k-k_1}{\ell}
                        \log\frac{Q(k_1)}{Q(k_0)}
    \le-\frac{k-k_1}{\ell}\log3.
\]
The inequality also holds as an upper bound on \(Q(k)\) when that value is zero. For \(u\ge7\) and \(k=\lceil\mu+u\sigma\rceil\),
\(k-k_1\ge(u-2)\sigma-1\ge(u-7)\sigma\), since \(1/\sigma\le\sqrt{20}<5\). Therefore
\[
 \Pr(V-\mu\ge u\sigma)\le\frac14\cdot3^{-(u-7)/13}.
\]
Apply the same argument to \(-V\) and sum the two bounds. Since \(\log3>1\) and \(3^{7/13}<3\), for \(u\ge7\) this sum is at most \(4e^{-u/16}\). For \(0\le u\le7\), the same upper bound exceeds one, since \(4e^{-7/16}>4/e>1\). We have proved the uniform tail estimate
\[
 \Pr(|V-\mu|\ge u\sigma)\le4e^{-u/16}
                         \quad(u\ge0).
\]

Let \(U=|V-\mu|/\sigma\). The identity
\(f(U)=\int_0^U f'(u)\,du\), with \(f(u)=u^2e^{u/32}\), and the preceding tail bound yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[U^2e^{U/32}]
 &\le4\int_0^\infty (2u+u^2/32)e^{-u/32}\,du\\
 &=4\left(2\cdot32^2+\frac{2\cdot32^3}{32}\right)
   =16384.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All expectations are finite; the displayed integral identity can be averaged directly because its integrand is nonnegative. Under an exponential tilt of this law by any real \(t\) with \(|t|\sigma\le1/32\), the normalizing factor \(\mathbb E e^{t(V-\mu)}\) is at least one, by \(e^x\ge1+x\). The variance of the tilted law is at most its second moment about the original \(\mu\). Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{tilt} t}(V)
 \le\frac{\mathbb E[(V-\mu)^2e^{t(V-\mu)}]}
             {\mathbb E e^{t(V-\mu)}}
 \le16384\sigma^2.
\]

It remains to connect this scalar estimate to the original partitions. For every real \(u\), the sequence \(B\mapsto Z_m(B,u)\) is log-concave on the nonnegative integers. Indeed, put \(\widetilde w_s=\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}e^{us}\). Its successive ratios are nonincreasing. For \(B\ge1\), this implies
\(\widetilde w_{B+1}\widetilde w_{i-1}\le\widetilde w_B\widetilde w_i\) for \(1\le i\le B\). Summing these inequalities gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde w_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,u)
   &\le\widetilde w_B\sum_{i=1}^B\widetilde w_i
    <\widetilde w_BZ_m(B,u),\\
 Z_m(B,u)^2-Z_m(B-1,u)Z_m(B+1,u)
   &=\widetilde w_BZ_m(B,u)
           -\widetilde w_{B+1}Z_m(B-1,u)>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the successive differences of \(\log Z_m(B,u)\) are nonincreasing. Summing the \(h\) differences from \(a\) to \(b\) and comparing with those from \(b\) to \(c\) proves
\[
 D(u):=2\log Z_m(b,u)-\log Z_m(a,u)-\log Z_m(c,u)\ge0
                          \quad(u\in\mathbb R).
\]
This definition uses a scalar log-partition gap, distinct from each slice deficit \(D_B\) used earlier.

For a budget \(B\), denote the conditional total mean and variance at tilt \(u\) by \(\mu_B(u)\) and \(v_B(u)\). Differentiating the positive finite partition sums gives
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\log Z_m(B,u)=\mu_B(u),\qquad
 \frac{d^2}{du^2}\log Z_m(B,u)=v_B(u).
\]
Hence, with \(\Omega:=\mu_0+\mu_2-2\mu_1\), the exact original-budget correspondence gives
\[
 D'(\beta)=-\Omega,\qquad
 D''(u)=2v_b(u)-v_a(u)-v_c(u)\le2v_b(u),\qquad
 \varepsilon=\frac{e^{-D(\beta)/2}}{1+2e^{-D(\beta)/2}}
                                      \le e^{-D(\beta)/2}.
\]
Put \(\sigma=\sqrt{v_1}=\sqrt{v_b(\beta)}\). The middle-slice law is log-concave and has \(\sigma^2\ge1/20\), so the scalar tilt bound just proved applies to it. Changing \(\beta\) to \(\beta+t\) is exactly that exponential tilt. Thus throughout \(|t|\le1/(32\sigma)\),
\[
 v_b(\beta+t)\le16384\sigma^2,
 \qquad D''(\beta+t)\le32768\sigma^2.
\]
If \(D'(\beta)=0\), the desired inequality is immediate. Otherwise choose
\(t=-\operatorname{sgn}(D'(\beta))/(32\sigma)\). Taylor's theorem with the preceding second-derivative upper bound, together with \(D(\beta+t)\ge0\), gives
\[
 0\le D(\beta+t)
      \le D(\beta)-\frac{|D'(\beta)|}{32\sigma}+16.
\]
It follows that \(|\Omega|\le32\sigma(D(\beta)+16)\). Finally, for \(d\ge0\), the function \((d+16)^4e^{-d/2}\) is decreasing, because its logarithmic derivative is \(4/(d+16)-1/2<0\). Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\varepsilon\Omega^4}{v_1^2}
 &\le32^4(D(\beta)+16)^4e^{-D(\beta)/2}\\
 &\le32^4\,16^4=2^{36}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the claimed original-source absorption, uniformly in every original parameter and legal integer full mean. The proof uses no replacement height law and establishes no additional representation needed for product caps.
