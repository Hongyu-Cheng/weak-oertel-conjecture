## Statement

Let $F\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a full-dimensional compact convex polytope, $m\ge1$, and let $A\ge H>0$ be convex finite piecewise-affine functions on $F$. Write $t=x+y$ and take
$$
K_j=\{(s,x,y):s\in F,\ x,y\ge0,\ A(s)/j\le t\le1\},\qquad
L_j=c_j(t-H(s)/j+e^{-j}),
$$
where $c_j$ gives volume-mean logarithm zero. The positive parameters are concave finite piecewise affine. The bodies converge to $K=F\times\{x,y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\}$, $M_j=|K_j|\to M=|F|/2$, and $L_j\to ct$ uniformly in affine presentation, where $c=e^{1/2}$. Put $E=\{A=H\}$ and $n_0=(0,\ldots,0,-1,-1)/\sqrt2$. The weak limit of $dX|_{K_j}/(M_jL_j)$ is its ordinary density $dX|_K/(Mct)$ plus
$\zeta=(2/(|F|c))H\mathbf1_E\,ds\,\delta_{(x,y)=0}$. The weak limit of $(-\log L_j)_+$ times the actual boundary normal-position measure is its ordinary limiting trace plus
$b=\sqrt2 H\mathbf1_E\,ds\,\delta_{(x,y)=0}\delta_{n_0}$. These satisfy $-n_0 b=M(0,\ldots,0,c,c)\zeta$ after identifying their physical positions. The construction has a fixed finite set of exact affine contact rows with uniformly interior source balls, positive lower bounds on marked values, and positive limiting source weights. The original-volume rows are uniform on the corresponding actual active cells.

For $F=[0,1]$, $H=\max\{2-s,1+s\}$ and $A=\max\{2-s,17/10,1+s\}$, both defects have tangential support $[0,3/10]\cup[7/10,1]$ in one exposed zero edge. They are neither log-concave measures nor uniform measures on convex supports. The two source marks converge to $(206/1055,25/211,25/211)$ and its reflection in $s=1/2$, with limiting values $50c/211$ and weights $1/2$. For all sufficiently large $j$ these marks are shallower than the ordinary centroid. More generally any nonempty finite union of positive-length closed subintervals of $[0,1]$ occurs as the tangential support.

## Proof

The lower roof is the epigraph of $A/j$, and $L_j$ is a minimum of finitely many affine functions with floor at least $c_je^{-j}$. The bounded roof height tends uniformly to zero, which gives Hausdorff convergence. Use $x=tu,\ y=t(1-u)$ for $0\le u\le1$, with volume element $t\,dt\,du\,ds$. Put $a=A(s)/j$ and $b_j(s)=H(s)/j-e^{-j}$. The primitive of $t\log(t-b_j)$ is $v^2\log v/2-v^2/4+b_j(v\log v-v)$, with $v=t-b_j$. The upper endpoint tends uniformly to $-1/4$ and the lower endpoint to zero, since $v=O(j^{-1})$ there and $v\log v\to0$. Hence the raw logarithm has volume-mean limit $-1/2$, which gives $c_j\to c$.

The integral of the raw reciprocal at fixed $s$ is
$$
1-a+b_j\log\frac{1-b_j}{a-b_j}.
$$
It follows from $t/(t-b_j)=1+b_j/(t-b_j)$. The first term is ordinary reciprocal density in the limit. The excess term is bounded uniformly in $s$ since $0<a-b_j$ is at least $e^{-j}$ and $b_j=O(1/j)$. Its mass tends pointwise to $H(s)\mathbf1_E(s)$, and its mass on $t\ge\epsilon>0$ tends uniformly to zero. Dominated convergence in $s$ and uniform continuity in the collapsing $(x,y)$ coordinates give the weak limit. Dividing by $M_jc_j$ gives $\zeta$.

On the lower roof, graph coordinates $(s,x)$ with $0\le x\le A(s)/j$ give the area element $(A/j)\sqrt{2+|\nabla A|^2/j^2}\,ds\,du$ and outward normal $(\nabla A/j,-1,-1)/\sqrt{2+|\nabla A|^2/j^2}$. Finite affine seams have zero surface measure. The negative log there divided by $j$ is uniformly bounded and tends pointwise to $\mathbf1_E$; on $E$, $A=H$. This gives the displayed roof defect by dominated convergence. For the remaining side pieces $x=0$ or $y=0$, the log is integrated in $dt$, and the raw negative-log integral over $t\le\epsilon$ is bounded uniformly for large $j$ by the integral of $-\log v$ on $[0,2\epsilon]$ plus $O(\epsilon)$, using $v=t-b_j$ and bounded $\log c_j$. Side pieces above facets of $F$ have the additional factor $t\le1$ and satisfy the same bound. Away from $t=0$ the weights converge uniformly and the side domains converge almost everywhere in these coordinates. The top has no positive negative-log weight eventually. Thus all other pieces give exactly the ordinary trace. The vector identity now follows by substitution of the two explicit defects.

Partition $F$ modulo null sets into the positive-volume active cells of the distinct affine pieces of $H$, and let $D_{ij}$ be the portions of $K_j$ above them. Write $V_{ij}=|D_{ij}|$, $\ell_j=L_j/c_j$ and $Z_{ij}=\int_{D_{ij}}dX/\ell_j$. Let $\rho_{ij}=\mathbf1_{D_{ij}}dX/(Z_{ij}\ell_j)$, $Y_{ij}=\int X\,d\rho_{ij}$ and $\sigma_{ij}=V_{ij}/M_j$. Since $L_j$ is affine on the convex cell $D_{ij}$, the value $L_{ij}=L_j(Y_{ij})=\int L_j\,d\rho_{ij}=c_jV_{ij}/Z_{ij}$ satisfies
$$
\sum_i(\sigma_{ij}/L_{ij})\rho_{ij}=\frac{\mathbf1_{K_j}dX}{M_jL_j},\qquad
(L_j/L_{ij})\rho_{ij}=\frac{\mathbf1_{D_{ij}}dX}{V_{ij}}.
$$
The previous dominated calculation applies on each fixed base cell. Each limiting probability has a positive ordinary component on the interior of its product cell and all additional mass on the closure. Its mean is strictly inside that cell and thus inside $K$, giving a fixed interior ball in all sufficiently late bodies around the current source. Also $Z_{ij}$ tends to a positive finite limit and $V_{ij}$ to half the positive base cell volume. These give the marked-value and weight claims.

For the displayed two-plane example the equality set is exactly the two stated intervals. On the left active cell $[0,1/2]$ the ordinary raw reciprocal mass is $1/2$, and the excess mass is $\int_0^{3/10}(2-s)\,ds=111/200$. The ordinary raw $s,x,y$ moments are each $1/8$. The excess first $s$ moment is $81/1000$ and the excess normal-coordinate moments vanish. Division by $211/200$ gives the left mark; reflection gives the right. Affineness gives $L_{ij}\to50c/211$ and the limiting volume weights are $1/2$. The caps $x+y\le x(Y_{ij})+y(Y_{ij})$ have limiting volume fraction $(50/211)^2<27/64$, by the Jacobian $t$ on the prism. Fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with $d=3$ and retained mass $|K_j|$ gives the ordinary centroid fraction at least $27/64$ at every $j$. The limiting gap therefore persists. The nonzero defects have disconnected support, whereas neighborhoods of any two support points of a log-concave measure force their strict combinations into its support.

For a finite union $E$ of positive-length intervals, take $g(s)=\operatorname{dist}(s,E)$, a nonnegative finite piecewise-affine function. At each breakpoint with slope jump $J$, give a piecewise-affine $H$ a slope jump $\max(0,-J)$, and add a constant large enough that $H>0$. Then $A=H+g$ is convex, $H$ is convex, and $A=H$ exactly on $E$. The positive-length condition makes the support of $H\mathbf1_E\,ds$ equal to $E$.
