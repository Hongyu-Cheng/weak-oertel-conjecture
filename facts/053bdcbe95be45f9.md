## Statement

Use exactly the rate-normalized cubic law of accepted facts `336979d75ca7a348` and `4862d5efd356636d`:
\[
F_3(s)=\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj},\qquad
Q_s(j)=\frac{(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}}{F_3(s)},\qquad
P_s(j)=(1-e^{-s})e^{-sj}.
\]
Let \(n,q\ge1\) be integers, let \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(r\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\), let \(a_i\ge0\) and \(s_i>0\), and suppose an index \(h\) satisfies
\[
\gcd(u_h,q)=1,\qquad qs_h\le\frac1{64},\qquad
R\ge64\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{a_i}{s_i}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(R\) is real. The residue \(r\) is the residue denoted by \(a\) in accepted fact `4862d5efd356636d`; the vector \(a\) here retains its resource meaning from accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`.

Let \(Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^nQ_{s_i}\), and let \(J\) have law \(Q\). For every \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), define
\[
\sigma_a(j)^2=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^n a_i^2j_i(j_i+1),\qquad
\sigma_s(j)^2=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^n s_i^2j_i(j_i+1),\qquad
N(j)=\prod_{i=1}^n(2j_i+1).
\tag{2}
\]
These are exactly the independent uniform-displacement variances used by accepted fact `a5639440d05f3bfe`, with its two matrix rows chosen as \(a\) and \(s\). Put
\[
E=\left\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:
u\cdot j\equiv r\pmod q,\quad j_h\ge q,\quad
a\cdot j+3\sigma_a(j)\le R,\quad
\sigma_s(j)\le6\sqrt n\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then, with the absolute constant \(b_*=(2/3)e^{-8}>0\),
\[
Q(E)\ge\frac{b_*}{q}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular (4) also holds if the score cutoff in (3) is enlarged to \(64\sqrt n\), giving the same constant 64 in all three registered hypotheses and cutoffs.

There is also an original-law consequence. Define the complete original support and its original law by
\[
D=\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot j\le R,\ u\cdot j\equiv r\pmod q\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{j\in D}e^{-s\cdot j},\qquad
p_j=\frac{e^{-s\cdot j}}Z\quad(j\in D).
\tag{5}
\]
The hypotheses imply \(0<Z<\infty\), even when some resource coefficients are zero. Extend \(p\) by zero outside \(D\). Following the original atomic depth-moment notation of accepted fact `ac02a0ffd389f816`, let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of \(p(H\cap D)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j\), and let \(M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3\). The exact score alias is \(\theta=-s\). The support (5) is the specified coset model in this theorem, and no support or probability is subsequently replaced. In the rational-function notation of `ac02a0ffd389f816`, its generating-function argument would be \(e^{-4s_i}\); \(F_3(s_i)\) here is the rate-normalized function of `336979d75ca7a348`.

Use the accepted constants
\[
C_{\mathrm{slab}}=\frac{2\log64}{1-\sqrt{7/8}},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{mod}}=8e^4(1+e^4),\qquad
\alpha=\left(\frac8{27}\right)^{3/2}>\frac18,
\]
and define
\[
\beta_*=\frac{b_*e^{-6C_{\mathrm{slab}}}}{64C_{\mathrm{mod}}^4},\qquad
\delta=\log(8\alpha)>0,\qquad
\kappa_*=\beta_*e^{-729/\delta}>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the original quantity in (5) satisfies
\[
M_3(p)\ge\beta_*e^{-54\sqrt n}\alpha^n
\ge\kappa_*8^{-n}.
\tag{7}
\]
All constants in (4) and (7) are independent of \(n,q,u,r,a,s,R\). This is a bounded-regime atomic input, not a final for the locked Weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

Accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`, with exactly the law stated above, supplies
\[
F_3(s)=\frac{1+23e^{-4s}+23e^{-8s}+e^{-12s}}{(1-e^{-4s})^4},\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_s}J\le\frac5{2s},\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2\le\frac8{s^2},\qquad
(1-e^{-s})^4F_3(s)\ge\alpha.
\tag{8}
\]
The weak second-moment version in (8) follows from its stricter bound \(23/(3s^2)<8/s^2\). All moments used below are therefore finite.

First prove a residue estimate restricted to a broad interval. Let \(0<s\le1/(64q)\), and set \(I_s=[1/s,2/s]\cap\mathbb Z\). Each residue class modulo \(q\) contains at least \(\lfloor1/(qs)\rfloor\) points in \(I_s\). Indeed, its least point at least \(1/s\) is less than \(1/s+q\), and that point together with its next \(\lfloor1/(qs)\rfloor-1\) successors of spacing \(q\) is at most \(2/s\). Since \(1/(qs)\ge64\), this number is at least \(1/(2qs)\).

For every \(j\in I_s\), one has \((2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}\ge8e^{-8}/s^3\). Also \(4s\le1\), and the inequality \(e^{4s}\ge1+4s\) gives
\(1-e^{-4s}\ge4s/(1+4s)\ge2s\). The numerator polynomial in (8) is at most 48. Consequently \(F_3(s)\le48/(2s)^4=3/s^4\). Combining these estimates proves, for every residue \(t\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\),
\[
Q_s\{J\in I_s, J\equiv t\pmod q\}
\ge\frac1{2qs}\frac{8e^{-8}/s^3}{3/s^4}
=\frac{4e^{-8}}{3q}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is an estimate within \(I_s\), rather than a whole-coset estimate.

Now control the other coordinates. Write \(S=\sum_i a_i/s_i\) and \(S_{-h}=\sum_{i\ne h}a_i/s_i\). Define an event depending only on \((J_i)_{i\ne h}\) by
\[
\mathcal E_{-h}=\left\{
\sum_{i\ne h}a_iJ_i\le16S_{-h},\quad
\frac13\sum_{i\ne h}s_i^2J_i(J_i+1)\le32n
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(S_{-h}>0\), the mean bound in (8) and Markov's inequality give probability at most \(5/32\) for failure of the first inequality. If \(S_{-h}=0\), nonnegativity of the \(a_i\) and positivity of the \(s_i\) force \(a_i=0\) for every \(i\ne h\), and the first inequality holds surely. For every nonnegative integer \(j\), \(j(j+1)\le2j^2\). Thus (8) gives
\[
\mathbb E_Q\left[\frac13\sum_{i\ne h}s_i^2J_i(J_i+1)\right]
\le\frac{16}3(n-1)\le\frac{16}3n.
\]
Markov's inequality bounds the failure probability of the second inequality in (10) by \(1/6\). A union bound therefore proves
\[
Q(\mathcal E_{-h})\ge1-\frac5{32}-\frac16=\frac{65}{96}>\frac12.
\tag{11}
\]
This includes \(n=1\), when both sums in (10) are empty and the event holds surely.

Fix other coordinates in \(\mathcal E_{-h}\), and take \(j_h\in I_{s_h}\). By (1), \(j_h\ge1/s_h\ge64q\ge q\), and \(j_h\le2/s_h\). The resource value satisfies
\(a\cdot j\le16S_{-h}+2a_h/s_h\le16S\). Since the resource coefficients are nonnegative, (2) and \(j(j+1)\le2j^2\) imply
\[
\sigma_a(j)^2
\le\frac23\sum_i(a_ij_i)^2
\le\frac23(a\cdot j)^2.
\]
It follows that
\[
a\cdot j+3\sigma_a(j)
\le(1+\sqrt6)a\cdot j
\le4a\cdot j\le64S\le R.
\tag{12}
\]
There is no division by \(S\) or \(a\cdot j\), so zero resource coefficients and \(S=0\) are included. Moreover \(s_h\le1\), so
\[
\sigma_s(j)^2
\le32n+\frac{s_h^2j_h(j_h+1)}3
\le32n+\frac{4+2s_h}3
\le32n+2\le34n<36n.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus such a root satisfies every inequality in (3).

For each fixed choice of the other coordinates, coprimality gives a unique residue \(t\) of \(j_h\) such that \(u\cdot j\equiv r\pmod q\). Independence permits application of (9) at rate \(s_h\) to that residue. Integrating over the event (10), all the roots counted this way lie in \(E\) by (12)-(13), and hence
\[
Q(E)\ge Q(\mathcal E_{-h})\frac{4e^{-8}}{3q}
\ge\frac12\frac{4e^{-8}}{3q}
=\frac{b_*}{q}.
\]
This proves (4) for the prescribed residue, including \(q=1\).

It remains to establish the original-law consumer. The event \(E\) has positive probability and is a subset of \(D\), so \(D\) is nonempty. Since all rates are positive,
\(Z\le\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}<\infty\), proving that (5) defines a probability law. The definitions of \(\tau_p\) and \(M_3\) are the original-law definitions in `ac02a0ffd389f816`, with score \(\theta=-s\); its theorem for the unrestricted resource support is not being asserted for this new coset support.

Fix \(j\in E\), and let \(B_j=\prod_i\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Apply accepted fact `a5639440d05f3bfe` with the same \(j,u,q,h\), and with its two matrix rows equal to \(a\) and \(s\). Its variance formula is exactly (2). Since \(j_h\ge q\), it gives a symmetric set
\[
\mathcal T(j)=\{v\in B_j:u\cdot v\equiv0\pmod q,\quad
|a\cdot v|\le3\sigma_a(j),\quad
|s\cdot v|\le3\sigma_s(j)\}
\]
with \(|\mathcal T(j)|\ge e^{-2C_{\mathrm{slab}}}N(j)/(2q)\).
For every \(v\in\mathcal T(j)\), both \(j+v\) and \(j-v\) have nonnegative coordinates, the prescribed residue, and resource at most \(a\cdot j+3\sigma_a(j)\le R\). Thus both endpoints belong to the actual support \(D\). Their original probabilities obey
\[
p_{j+v}=p_j e^{-s\cdot v}
\ge p_j e^{-3\sigma_s(j)}
\ge p_j e^{-18\sqrt n}.
\tag{14}
\]

If a closed halfspace contains \(j\), at least one endpoint in every pair \(j+v,j-v\) belongs to the halfspace. Because \(\mathcal T(j)=-\mathcal T(j)\), at least half of the distinct points \(j+v\), \(v\in\mathcal T(j)\), belong to it; zero displacement also satisfies this assertion. Summing their original probabilities using (14), and then taking the infimum over containing halfspaces, proves
\[
\tau_p(j)\ge\frac{e^{-2C_{\mathrm{slab}}}}{4q}
e^{-18\sqrt n}p_jN(j)\qquad(j\in E).
\tag{15}
\]

Write \(P=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}\), \(G=\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}\), and \(\mathcal F_3=\prod_iF_3(s_i)\), using exactly the original-support normalizer notation of accepted fact `4862d5efd356636d`. Its identities give \(Z=GP(D)\) and
\(Q(j)=N(j)^3e^{-4s\cdot j}/\mathcal F_3\). The support \(D\) is contained in the prescribed whole coset, and \(qs_h\le1/64\le1\), so its modular estimate gives \(P(D)\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\). This upper bound is used only for the original normalizer. The resource-conditioned lower bound for \(Q(E)\) is the separately proved (4).

Cube (15), multiply by the original \(p_j\), and sum over \(E\subseteq D\). The resulting exact product normalization, (4), and the preceding bound on \(P(D)\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac{e^{-6C_{\mathrm{slab}}-54\sqrt n}}{64q^3Z^4}
\sum_{j\in E}N(j)^3e^{-4s\cdot j}\\
&=\frac{e^{-6C_{\mathrm{slab}}-54\sqrt n}}{64q^3}
G^{-4}\mathcal F_3 P(D)^{-4}Q(E)\\
&\ge\frac{b_*e^{-6C_{\mathrm{slab}}}}{64C_{\mathrm{mod}}^4}
e^{-54\sqrt n}G^{-4}\mathcal F_3\\
&\ge\beta_*e^{-54\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The last step is the coordinate-factor inequality in (8). The powers of the modulus are exactly \(q^{-3}\) from the cubed displacement count, \(q^4\) from the original denominator, and \(q^{-1}\) from the resource-conditioned root probability. All series here are finite because \(\sum_jN(j)^3e^{-4s\cdot j}=\mathcal F_3<\infty\), and the original normalizer remains \(Z\) throughout.

Finally, since \(\delta=\log(8\alpha)>0\), completion of the square gives, for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\delta n-54\sqrt n
=\left(\sqrt\delta\sqrt n-\frac{27}{\sqrt\delta}\right)^2
-\frac{729}{\delta}
\ge-\frac{729}{\delta}.
\]
Thus \(e^{-54\sqrt n}\alpha^n\ge e^{-729/\delta}8^{-n}\). Applying this to (16) proves (7), with all boundary cases already included.
