## Statement

Fix an integer \(M\ge2\). Let \(k\ge1\). For every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let \(n_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(Q_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_i}\) be compact and convex, and suppose that
\[
A_i:=Q_i\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i}\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
m_i:=|A_i|\le M.
\]
No full-dimensionality assumption is made on \(Q_i\) or on \(\operatorname{conv}A_i\). Choose an arbitrary tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\), and define the log-affine probability law
\[
p_i(\mathbf z)
:=\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf z)}{Z_i},
\qquad
Z_i:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A_i}
\exp(\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf x)
\quad(\mathbf z\in A_i).
\]
Put
\[
N:=\sum_{i=1}^k n_i,
\qquad
A:=\prod_{i=1}^k A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^N,
\qquad
p:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k p_i,
\]
and let \(\mathbf X\) have law \(p\). For \(\mathbf j\in A\), define its normalized ambient halfspace depth by
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
:=
\inf\left\{
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A\cap H}p(\mathbf x):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf j\in H
\right\}.
\]
Thus the infimum includes halfspaces whose normals mix different factors.

Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_M&:=(2M^2)^{-2^M},&
\varepsilon_M&:=\frac{a_M}{4M^2},&
\delta_M&:=\log(1+\varepsilon_M),\\
v_M&:=\frac{M^2}{2}\bigl((\log M)^2+2\bigr),&
c_M&:=\frac14\exp\!\left(-\frac{v_M}{\delta_M}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(c_M>0\), and
\[
\mathbb E_p\!\left[\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2\right]
\ge c_M\,4^{-N}.
\tag{1}
\]

More precisely, let
\[
b:=\left|\left\{i:|A_i|>2^{n_i}\right\}\right|.
\]
Then
\[
4^N\mathbb E_p\!\left[\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2\right]
\ge
\frac14\exp\!\left(b\delta_M-2\sqrt{b v_M}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(b\ge1\), the inequality in (2) is strict. Consequently, for fixed \(M\), along any sequence of such products for which \(b\to\infty\), one has
\[
4^N\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2]\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
even when all supports, dimensions, affine ranks, and tilts vary along the sequence.

The quantifiers are important: after \(M\) is fixed, \(k\) and every triple \((n_i,Q_i,\boldsymbol\ell_i)\) are arbitrary. No finite family containing the possible \(Q_i\), \(A_i\), or \(p_i\) is fixed beforehand. The constant depends only on \(M\). The omitted case \(M=1\) is immediate: every factor is a singleton, \(\tau_p\equiv1\), and one may take \(c_1=1\).

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. We first prove two one-block estimates, then a product comparison that incurs one factor \(1/4\) for the entire product.

The product body \(Q:=\prod_{i=1}^k Q_i\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) is compact and convex, and
\[
Q\cap\mathbb Z^N=\prod_{i=1}^k(Q_i\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i})=A.
\]
Thus the product support itself is complete in the ambient lattice.

Consider one complete support
\[
A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\ne\varnothing,
\qquad
p_{\mathbf x}
=\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x)}
{\sum_{\mathbf y\in A}\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf y)},
\qquad
B:=2^n.
\]
For \(\mathbf j\in A\), define the full reflected displacement set
\[
D_{\mathbf j}
:=
\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n:
\mathbf j+\mathbf v\in A,\ \mathbf j-\mathbf v\in A\},
\qquad
r_{\mathbf j}:=|D_{\mathbf j}|,
\qquad
H_{\mathbf j}:=p_{\mathbf j}r_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{3}
\]
The set \(D_{\mathbf j}\) is oriented, is invariant under
\(\mathbf v\mapsto-\mathbf v\), and contains \(\mathbf0\).

For every \(\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}\), log-affinity gives
\[
p_{\mathbf j}^2
=p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}.
\tag{4}
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mathbf j}
&=\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
\sqrt{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}\\
&\le
\left(\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\right)^{1/2}
\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The first inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz. Each of the two maps
\(\mathbf v\mapsto\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v\) is injective into \(A\), which proves the last inequality. This argument is unaffected if \(A\) has lower affine rank.

For \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\{0,1\}^n\), let
\[
\pi_{\boldsymbol\beta}
:=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in A\\
\mathbf x\equiv\boldsymbol\beta\pmod2}}p_{\mathbf x}.
\]
Every ordered pair \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in A^2\) of the same coordinatewise parity has
\[
\mathbf j=\frac{\mathbf x+\mathbf y}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
\mathbf v=\frac{\mathbf x-\mathbf y}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]
Convexity gives \(\mathbf j\in Q\), and completeness
\(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) therefore gives \(\mathbf j\in A\). Thus
\((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\leftrightarrow(\mathbf j,\mathbf v)\), with
\(\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}\), is a bijection. Using (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
q
&:=\sum_{\boldsymbol\beta}\pi_{\boldsymbol\beta}^2
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in A\\
\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod2}}p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}^2r_{\mathbf j}
=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}H_{\mathbf j}
=\mathbb E_p H_{\mathbf X}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
There are \(B=2^n\) parity classes, including classes of zero mass, so
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(q\ge B^{-1}\). Define
\[
\mathcal R(p)
:=\mathbb E_p H_{\mathbf X}^2
=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}^3r_{\mathbf j}^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Jensen's inequality and (6) give
\[
\mathcal R(p)\ge q^2\ge B^{-2}.
\tag{8}
\]

We next prove the uniform strict gap
\[
B^2\mathcal R(p)>1+\varepsilon_M
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
B<|A|\le M.
\tag{9}
\]
Assume for contradiction that
\(B^2\mathcal R(p)\le1+\varepsilon_M\). Since
\(q^2\le\mathcal R(p)\), (6) and (8) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\boldsymbol\beta}
\left(\pi_{\boldsymbol\beta}-\frac1B\right)^2
&=q-\frac1B\\
&\le\frac{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_M}-1}{B}
\le\frac{\varepsilon_M}{2B}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(\sqrt{1+t}\le1+t/2\) for \(t\ge0\). Since
\(a_M\le1\), \(B<M\), and \(M\ge2\), one has
\(\varepsilon_M\le1/(2B)\). Hence (10) gives, for every parity class,
\[
\left|\pi_{\boldsymbol\beta}-\frac1B\right|
\le\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_M}{2B}}
\le\frac1{2B},
\qquad
\pi_{\boldsymbol\beta}\ge\frac1{2B}.
\tag{11}
\]
Each parity class has at most \(|A|\le M\) points. It therefore contains a point whose probability is at least
\[
\frac1{2BM}\ge\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha:=\frac1{2M^2},
\tag{12}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(B<M\le M\).

We now establish the midpoint-ascent bound
\[
p_{\mathbf x}\ge a_M=\alpha^{2^M}
\quad\text{for every }\mathbf x\in A.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(p_{\mathbf x}\ge\alpha\), this is immediate. Otherwise, choose
\(\mathbf y\) in the parity class of \(\mathbf x\) with
\(p_{\mathbf y}\ge\alpha\), as guaranteed by (12), and put
\(\mathbf x'=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\). The same completeness argument used above gives \(\mathbf x'\in A\), and log-affinity gives
\[
p_{\mathbf x'}=\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}}>p_{\mathbf x}.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(p_{\mathbf x'}<\alpha\), repeat the construction from
\(\mathbf x'\), using a high-probability representative in its own parity class. The probabilities strictly increase, so all visited sites are distinct. As \(|A|\le M\), after at most \(M\) steps the procedure reaches a site \(\mathbf x_t\) with
\(p_{\mathbf x_t}\ge\alpha\).

At a single ascent step, (4) and \(p_{\mathbf y}\le1\) give
\[
p_{\mathbf x}
=\frac{p_{\mathbf x'}^2}{p_{\mathbf y}}
\ge p_{\mathbf x'}^2.
\]
Iterating backwards through \(t\le M\) steps yields
\[
p_{\mathbf x}
\ge p_{\mathbf x_t}^{\,2^t}
\ge\alpha^{2^t}
\ge\alpha^{2^M}=a_M,
\]
which proves (13).

Because \(\log p_{\mathbf x}\) is affine in \(\mathbf x\), its minimum on the polytope \(\operatorname{conv}A\) is attained at a vertex
\(\mathbf j_0\in A\). Thus \(p_{\mathbf j_0}=p_{\min}:=\min_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}\). A vertex cannot be the midpoint of two distinct points of \(A\); consequently
\[
D_{\mathbf j_0}=\{\mathbf0\},
\qquad
r_{\mathbf j_0}=1,
\qquad
H_{\mathbf j_0}=p_{\min}.
\tag{15}
\]
This vertex argument is relative to \(\operatorname{aff}A\), so it also covers every lower-rank support.

The contradictory assumption, together with \(q\ge B^{-1}\), gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_p(H_{\mathbf X})
=\mathcal R(p)-q^2
\le\frac{\varepsilon_M}{B^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
The \(\mathbf j_0\)-summand in this variance and (13)--(15) imply
\[
|p_{\min}-q|
\le\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_M}}{B\sqrt{p_{\min}}}
\le\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_M}}{B\sqrt{a_M}}
=\frac1{2MB}.
\tag{17}
\]
Therefore
\[
p_{\min}
\ge q-\frac1{2MB}
\ge\frac1B\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right).
\tag{18}
\]
But \(|A|\ge B+1\), so (18) gives
\[
1=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}
\ge
\frac{B+1}{B}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)>1.
\tag{19}
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to \(2M>B+1\), which follows from
\(M\ge|A|\ge B+1\). This contradiction proves (9).

We now prove the product comparison. For every factor \(i\) and center
\(\mathbf j_i\in A_i\), choose a nonempty symmetric displacement set
\(E_{i,\mathbf j_i}\) equal either to its full set
\[
D_{i,\mathbf j_i}
:=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^{n_i}:
\mathbf j_i+\mathbf v,\mathbf j_i-\mathbf v\in A_i\}
\]
or to the atomic set \(\{\mathbf0\}\). Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{i,\mathbf j_i}&:=|E_{i,\mathbf j_i}|,&
H_{i,\mathbf j_i}&:=p_i(\mathbf j_i)r_{i,\mathbf j_i},\\
\sigma_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2
&:=\frac1{r_{i,\mathbf j_i}}
\sum_{\mathbf v\in E_{i,\mathbf j_i}}
(\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf v)^2,\\
\mathcal R_i
&:=\sum_{\mathbf j_i\in A_i}
p_i(\mathbf j_i)H_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2,\\
\mathcal V_i
&:=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf j_i\in A_i}
p_i(\mathbf j_i)H_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2
\sigma_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2}{\mathcal R_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The denominator \(\mathcal R_i\) is positive because every chosen set contains \(\mathbf0\).

Fix a product center
\(\mathbf j=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_k)\), and let
\(\mathbf V_i\) be independent and uniform on
\(E_{i,\mathbf j_i}\). Write
\(\mathbf V=(\mathbf V_1,\ldots,\mathbf V_k)\). The product displacement set is centrally symmetric, and all points
\(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf V\) belong to \(A\).

Let \(H\) be any closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\) containing
\(\mathbf j\). For every nonzero antipodal displacement pair
\(\{\mathbf v,-\mathbf v\}\), at least one of
\(\mathbf j+\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf j-\mathbf v\) belongs to \(H\). This remains true if the boundary of \(H\) does not pass through \(\mathbf j\): writing \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge c\}\) with
\(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf j\ge c\), choose the sign of \(\mathbf v\) having nonnegative scalar product with \(\mathbf u\). The zero displacement contributes the center itself, which belongs to \(H\) because the halfspace is closed.

Summing the smaller endpoint mass for every nonzero antipodal pair, and half the center mass for the zero pair, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(A\cap H)
&\ge
\frac12\sum_{\mathbf v\in\prod_iE_{i,\mathbf j_i}}
\min\{p(\mathbf j+\mathbf v),p(\mathbf j-\mathbf v)\}\\
&=
\frac12\,p(\mathbf j)
\sum_{\mathbf v\in\prod_iE_{i,\mathbf j_i}}
\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf v_i\right|
\right)\\
&=
\frac12\left(\prod_iH_{i,\mathbf j_i}\right)
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\right|
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
In the first line, the factor \(1/2\) removes the double count from the two orientations of each nonzero pair; it undercounts the center by a factor \(1/2\). In the second line, log-affinity was used in the form
\[
\min\{p(\mathbf j+\mathbf v),p(\mathbf j-\mathbf v)\}
=p(\mathbf j)\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf v_i\right|
\right).
\]
Since (21) holds for every such halfspace,
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
\ge
\frac12\left(\prod_iH_{i,\mathbf j_i}\right)
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\right|
\right).
\tag{22}
\]
This is where the one-time product comparison occurs. It pairs the whole product set at once, so the loss is \(1/2\) before squaring, rather than one \(1/2\) for each factor. It also treats halfspaces with cross-factor normals.

Each \(E_{i,\mathbf j_i}\) is symmetric, so
\(\mathbb E(\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i)=0\). Independence therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\right)^2
=\sum_i\sigma_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2.
\]
By Jensen's inequality for \(t\mapsto e^{-t}\), followed by
Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\right|
\right)
&\ge
\exp\!\left(
-\mathbb E\left|\sum_i\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\right|
\right)\\
&\ge
\exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\sum_i\sigma_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Squaring (22), applying (23), and averaging over
\(\mathbf j\sim p\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2
\ge\frac14
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}
\left(\prod_i p_i(\mathbf j_i)H_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2\right)
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i
\sigma_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Define probability laws
\[
\widehat p_i(\mathbf j_i)
:=
\frac{p_i(\mathbf j_i)H_{i,\mathbf j_i}^2}{\mathcal R_i}.
\tag{25}
\]
With independent \(\mathbf J_i\sim\widehat p_i\), equation (24) becomes
\[
\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2
\ge
\frac14\left(\prod_i\mathcal R_i\right)
\mathbb E_{\otimes_i\widehat p_i}
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i
\sigma_{i,\mathbf J_i}^2}\right).
\tag{26}
\]
For \(U:=\sum_i\sigma_{i,\mathbf J_i}^2\ge0\), Jensen and
\(\mathbb E\sqrt U\le\sqrt{\mathbb E U}\) imply
\[
\mathbb E e^{-2\sqrt U}
\ge e^{-2\mathbb E\sqrt U}
\ge e^{-2\sqrt{\mathbb E U}}.
\]
By (20) and (25),
\(\mathbb E U=\sum_i\mathcal V_i\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2
\ge
\frac14\left(\prod_i\mathcal R_i\right)
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\mathcal V_i}\right).
\tag{27}
\]

It remains to bound \(\mathcal V_i\) without any restriction on the tilt. Return to one block and use the full reflected sets in (3). Put
\[
T_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v):=\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v.
\]
Equations (5), (7), and (20), followed by the ordered-pair midpoint bijection used in (6), give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R(p)\mathcal V
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}
p_{\mathbf j}^3r_{\mathbf j}
\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
T_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)^2\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}
p_{\mathbf j}^2H_{\mathbf j}
\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
T_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)^2\\
&\le
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}^2
\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_{\mathbf j}}
T_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)^2\\
&=
\frac14
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in A\\
\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod2}}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
(\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y})^2\\
&\le
\frac14\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in A}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
(\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y})^2\\
&=
\frac12\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p_{\mathbf X}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Indeed, under
\(\mathbf x=\mathbf j+\mathbf v\) and
\(\mathbf y=\mathbf j-\mathbf v\), equation (4) gives
\(p_{\mathbf j}^2=p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}\), while
\[
T_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)
=\frac12(\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}).
\]
The final equality in (28) is the usual independent-copy identity
\(\mathbb E(Y-Y')^2=2\operatorname{Var}(Y)\).

For completeness, we now prove a bound for the last variance that is uniform over all probability laws on at most \(M\) points. Let
\[
Z:=-\log p_{\mathbf X}\ge0,
\qquad
L:=\log M.
\]
For every \(u\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(Z>L+u)
&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in A\\
p_{\mathbf x}<e^{-(L+u)}}}p_{\mathbf x}\\
&\le |A|e^{-(L+u)}
\le Me^{-(L+u)}
=e^{-u}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Tail integration gives
\[
\mathbb E(Z-L)_+^2
=\int_0^\infty 2u\,\mathbb P(Z-L>u)\,du
\le\int_0^\infty2u e^{-u}\,du=2.
\]
Since \(Z\ge0\), also \((Z-L)_-^2\le L^2\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p_{\mathbf X})
=\operatorname{Var}_p(Z)
\le\mathbb E(Z-L)^2
\le(\log M)^2+2.
\tag{30}
\]

If a block satisfies \(|A|>B=2^n\), choose its full reflected sets. From
(8), (28), and (30),
\[
\mathcal V
\le\frac1{2\mathcal R(p)}
\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p_{\mathbf X})
\le
\frac{B^2}{2}\bigl((\log M)^2+2\bigr)
\le v_M,
\tag{31}
\]
and the strict gap (9) gives
\[
B^2\mathcal R(p)>1+\varepsilon_M=e^{\delta_M}.
\tag{32}
\]

If a block satisfies \(|A|\le B\), choose
\(E_{\mathbf j}=\{\mathbf0\}\) at every center. Then
\[
H_{\mathbf j}=p_{\mathbf j},
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=0,
\qquad
\mathcal V=0,
\qquad
\mathcal R=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}^3.
\]
Convexity of \(t\mapsto t^3\), or Hölder's inequality, gives
\[
\mathcal R\ge |A|^{-2}\ge B^{-2}.
\tag{33}
\]
This atomic choice covers in particular singleton supports, supports lying in a proper affine subspace, and every factor for which the nominal ambient parity count already dominates its cardinality.

Apply (27) with full reflected sets in precisely the \(b\) factors satisfying
\(|A_i|>B_i:=2^{n_i}\), and atomic sets in all other factors. Since
\(4^N=\prod_iB_i^2\), equations (31)--(33) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
4^N\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2
&\ge
\frac14\left(\prod_iB_i^2\mathcal R_i\right)
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\mathcal V_i}\right)\\
&\ge
\frac14
\exp\!\left(b\delta_M-2\sqrt{b v_M}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
When \(b\ge1\), the first inequality supplied by the product of the
\(b\) instances of (32) is strict, so (34) is strict. This proves (2).

For every real \(b\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
b\delta_M-2\sqrt{b v_M}
&=
\left(\sqrt{b\delta_M}
-\sqrt{\frac{v_M}{\delta_M}}\right)^2
-\frac{v_M}{\delta_M}\\
&\ge-\frac{v_M}{\delta_M}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Combining (34) and (35) gives (1) with the stated \(c_M\).

If \(b=0\), one can also see the boundary case directly. Every closed halfspace counted in \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\) contains the atom
\(\mathbf j\), so \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\ge p(\mathbf j)\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p\tau_p(\mathbf X)^2
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p(\mathbf j)^3
=\prod_i\sum_{\mathbf x\in A_i}p_i(\mathbf x)^3\\
&\ge\prod_i|A_i|^{-2}
\ge\prod_i4^{-n_i}
=4^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The same observation confirms that atoms on halfspace boundaries cause no loss; closedness forces their full mass to be counted. Together with the relative-vertex argument in (15), this completes all lower-rank and boundary cases.

Finally, because \(\delta_M>0\) and \(v_M<\infty\) for fixed \(M\),
\[
b\delta_M-2\sqrt{b v_M}\longrightarrow+\infty
\quad\text{as }b\to\infty.
\]
Equation (2) therefore proves the asserted divergence. The proof used no restriction on the sequence of support geometries, ambient dimensions, affine ranks, or tilts beyond the common bound \(|A_i|\le M\). Its constant nevertheless deteriorates with \(M\), so it is a bounded-cardinality product H2 theorem and does not assert the support-independent H2 estimate for an arbitrary single complete lattice support.
