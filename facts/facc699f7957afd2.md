## Statement

Let $f_0,\ldots,f_{L-1}$ be a nonnegative, nonzero concave sequence on consecutive indices, with $L\ge1$. Put $R=\max_i f_i$ and choose any $j$ with $f_j=R$. For real $p\ge2$ and $0\le c\le R$, set $Q_p(c)=\sum_i(f_i-c)_+^p$, where $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For $0\le t<R$ let $[a(t),b(t)]$ be the strict superlevel interval of indices with $f_i>t$, and set
$$
\kappa_j(t)=\frac12\left(\frac1{2(j-a(t))+1}+\frac1{2(b(t)-j)+1}\right).
$$
The function $\kappa_j$ is nondecreasing and $1/L\le\kappa_j(t)\le1$. For $0\le c<R$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_p(c)
&\ge (1-c/R)^{p+1}Q_p(0)
  +(R-c)^{p+1}\int_0^c\frac{\kappa_j(t)}{(R-t)^2}\,dt\\
&\ge (1-c/R)^{p+1}Q_p(0)+\kappa_j(0)\frac{c(R-c)^p}{R}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0<c<R$, partition $[0,c]$ at the distinct sequence values in $(0,c)$, writing the endpoints and knots as $0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=c$. The integral is exactly
$$
\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\kappa_j(s_\ell)
  \left(\frac1{R-t_{\ell+1}}-\frac1{R-t_\ell}\right),
\qquad s_\ell\in(t_\ell,t_{\ell+1}),
$$
and it is zero at $c=0$. At $c=R$ the scaled integral remainder has continuous extension zero. Both inequalities are equalities at $c=0$ and $c=R$, and are identities for every $c$ if $L=1$. The bound with the remainder dropped is strict for $0<c<R$.

## Proof

By concavity, if two entries exceed $t$, all intervening entries exceed $t$ as well. The peak $j$ belongs to every strict superlevel block for $t<R$. As $t$ increases, $a(t)$ is nondecreasing and $b(t)$ is nonincreasing, so $\kappa_j(t)$ is nondecreasing. The two positive denominators in its definition sum to $2(b(t)-a(t)+1)$. The reciprocal-mean inequality gives $\kappa_j(t)\ge1/(b(t)-a(t)+1)\ge1/L$, and each reciprocal is at most one.

For $0\le c<R$, apply the chosen-peak power moment inequality to the concave sequence $f_i-c$ restricted to its strict superlevel block. Its maximum is $R-c$, and the peak index relative to the block is $j-a(c)$. Since both powers are positive, all omitted entries contribute zero to the corresponding positive-part sums. Thus
$$
(p+1)Q_p(c)-p(R-c)Q_{p-1}(c)\ge\kappa_j(c)(R-c)^p,
$$
where $Q_{p-1}$ is the sum defined by the same formula with exponent $p-1$.

Each positive-part power of exponent $p\ge2$ is continuously differentiable as a function of $c$, with derivative zero when its argument is zero. Therefore $Q_p'(c)=-pQ_{p-1}(c)$ even at knots. Differentiating the normalized sum gives
$$
\frac{d}{dc}\frac{Q_p(c)}{(R-c)^{p+1}}
=\frac{(p+1)Q_p(c)-p(R-c)Q_{p-1}(c)}{(R-c)^{p+2}}
\ge\frac{\kappa_j(c)}{(R-c)^2}.
$$
Integrate from $0$ to $c<R$ and multiply by $(R-c)^{p+1}$ to obtain the first bound. Replacing $\kappa_j(t)$ in the integral by its lower bound $\kappa_j(0)$ and integrating $(R-t)^{-2}$ gives the second bound. The exact knot sum follows by integrating the constant value of $\kappa_j$ on each open interval of the partition. Values at individual knots do not affect the integral.

At $c=0$ the integral vanishes. Since $\kappa_j\le1$, the scaled integral remainder for $c<R$ is at most $c(R-c)^p/R$, which tends to zero at $R$, where all other terms also vanish. For $L=1$, $Q_p(c)=(R-c)^p$ and $\kappa_j(t)=1$, and the two terms of either bound add to $(R-c)^p$. For $0<c<R$, the simple remainder is strictly positive, giving strictness of the bound without the remainder.
