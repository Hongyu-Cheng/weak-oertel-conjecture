## Source

- [c324e86ba3893a31](../candidates/c324e86ba3893a31.json), c324e86ba3893a31:03 §4, two exact phase-merging witnesses.

## Statement

For independent unit exponentials $Y_i$ and any coefficient vector $c$ with both signs, write $V=\sum_i c_iY_i$, $F=\Pr(V\ge0)$, and
$$
\mathcal R(c)=\frac{\prod_i\mathbb E[Y_i\mid V=0]}{F(1-F)}
$$
using coarea conditioning. Replacing the two negative unit coefficients by $-2$ and $0$ changes $\mathcal R$ from $81/64$ to $6/5$ for $c=(1,1,-1,-1)$, and from $161728/129375$ to $14/11$ for $c=(1,3,-1,-1)$. The changes have opposite signs.

## Proof

For $c=(a,b,-1,-1)$, let $T=aY_0+bY_1$. The sum of the negative phases has $\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)$ density $t e^{-t}$ on $t>0$. Thus at $V=0$ the conditional density of the two positive-phase exponentials $x,y$ is proportional to $(ax+by)e^{-(1+a)x-(1+b)y}$. Given $x,y$, the two negative-phase exponentials have fixed sum $ax+by$ and uniform splitting, by their constant joint density along that segment. Their means are both $(a\mathbb E x+b\mathbb E y)/2$. Also the gamma distribution gives $F=1-\mathbb E[(1+T)e^{-T}]$.

For $(a,b)=(1,1)$, the normalizing integral is $1/4$, each positive first-moment integral is $3/16$, and symmetry gives $F=1/2$. All four means equal $3/4$. For $(a,b)=(1,3)$ the normalizing integral is $5/32$ and the positive first-moment integrals are $7/64$ and $1/16$, giving means $7/10,2/5$ and the two negative means $19/20$. Here $\mathbb E e^{-T}=1/8$ and $\mathbb E[Te^{-T}]=5/32$, so $F=23/32$.

After merging, $V=aY_0+bY_1-2Y_2$. Conditioning at zero gives independent positive-phase exponentials of rates $1+a/2,1+b/2$ by eliminating $Y_2$. The means are $2/(2+a),2/(2+b),a/(2+a)+b/(2+b),1$, with the last coordinate independent. The same negative exponential gives $F=1-4/[(2+a)(2+b)]$. The two pairs give $(F,\mathcal R)=(5/9,6/5)$ and $(11/15,14/11)$. The initial ratios from the previous paragraph are $81/64$ and $161728/129375$. The second change is $32242/1423125>0$, and the first is negative.
