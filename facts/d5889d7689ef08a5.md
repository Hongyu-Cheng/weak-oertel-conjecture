## Statement

Let an affine coordinate on a full-dimensional triangle have vertex values $0,l,m$ with $0\le l\le m$ and $m>0$. Put $t=l/m$ and $c=128/279$. If the cap at threshold $z$ has area at most $c$ times the triangle area, then $z/m\ge\phi_c(t)$, where
$$
\phi_c(t)=
\begin{cases}
1-\sqrt{c(1-t)}, & t\le1-c,\\
\sqrt{(1-c)t}, & t\ge1-c.
\end{cases}
$$
For $b_0=\sqrt{151/279}$ one has $\phi_c(t)\ge (3b_0-1)/4+(1+b_0)t/4$. In particular $z\ge(241/800)m+(347/800)l$.

## Proof

Rescale the affine coordinate by $m$ and write $x=z/m$. On $t\le x\le1$ with $t<1$, the cap is the tip at the top vertex with relative area $(1-x)^2/(1-t)$. On $0\le x\le t$ with $t>0$, its complement is the tip at the low vertex with relative area $x^2/t$. These formulas are products of two edge ratios and agree for the cap area at $x=t$, where that area fraction is $1-t$. They apply at $t=0,1$ by using the available interval, and a threshold outside $[0,1]$ either violates the cap hypothesis or satisfies the bound trivially. Inversion gives $\phi_c$ including its transition.

For the first branch, set $u=1-t$. The minorant is equivalent to $(1-b_0)+(1+b_0)u/4\ge\sqrt{(1-b_0^2)u}$, which is AM–GM. For the second let $w=\sqrt t\ge b_0$, $A=(3b_0-1)/4$, and $B=(1+b_0)/4$. Then $b_0=A+B$ and $b_0w-A-Bw^2=(1-w)(Bw-A)\ge0$, since $Bb_0-A=(1-b_0)^2/4$. Squaring proves $b_0>147/200$, and substitution in both minorant coefficients gives the rational bound.
