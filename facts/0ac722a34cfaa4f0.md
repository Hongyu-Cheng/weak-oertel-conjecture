## Statement

Let $\mu_0$ be normalized mixed length on $[0,3]\times[0,1]$ with integer first coordinate. From $\mu_k$, take two independent samples, condition on equal parity of their first coordinates, and let $\mu_{k+1}$ be their midpoint law. At every stage the retained probability is $1/2$, the support is $\{0,1,2,3\}\times[0,1]$, and the law is a product with symmetric base weights whose inner-to-outer ratio is $2^{2^k}-1$. The continuous factor is the average of $2^k$ independent uniform variables on $[0,1]$. The exact maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth at step $k\ge0$ is $1/4+2^{-2^k-2}$, strictly decreasing to $1/4$. In particular, $\mu_1$ has base weights $(1,3,3,1)/8$ and continuous density $4\min(x,1-x)$ on $[0,1]$, and the depth drops from $3/8$ to $5/16$.

## Proof

The even and odd classes of symmetric weights $(a,b,b,a)$ both have mass $a+b$ before normalization, giving retention probability $1/2$. The retained ordered pairs send $(0,0)$ and $(3,3)$ to the two outer bases, of raw weights $a^2$ each. At base one the pairs $(0,2),(2,0),(1,1)$ give $2ab+b^2$. At base two base reflection gives the same weight. Therefore the ratio $R_k$ of the inner weight to the outer weight satisfies $R_0=1$ and $R_{k+1}+1=(R_k+1)^2$, so $R_k=2^{2^k}-1$. Every weight stays positive.

The continuous coordinates are independent of the two bases, so conditioning leaves their independent product unchanged. Averaging two independent averages of $2^k$ independent uniforms produces the average of $2^{k+1}$ independent uniforms. These laws are symmetric, nonatomic and have support $[0,1]$. For two uniforms, the convolution density of the sum is $\min(s,2-s)$ on $[0,2]$, giving $4\min(x,1-x)$ after dividing the sum by two.

The four-fiber product depth lemma now applies with $b/a=R_k\ge1$ and gives $(2+R_k)/(4(1+R_k))=1/4+2^{-2^k-2}$. It applies at $k=0$ as well as to every transformed law, since the measure at each stage is a product on the same candidate set.
