## Statement

In the exact planar seven-base strip family, every global maximizer of raw mixed closed-halfspace depth for sufficiently small $t>0$ has base $q=(0,0)$. Every maximizing selection has continuous mark $y_t=g+t v_*+o(t)$, where
$$
\ell_0(v_*)=-\frac{2LH}{1+4L},\qquad \ell_1(v_*)=\ell_2(v_*)=\frac{LH}{1+4L}.
$$
Put $L_*=a[1-1/(6(1+4L))]$. The global raw maximum $D_t$ and the normalized maximum satisfy
$$
D_t=\Gamma V+t(a+L_*)+o(t),\qquad
\frac{D_t}{4V+3ta}=\frac{\Gamma}{4}+t\,\frac{4(a+L_*)-3\Gamma a}{16V}+o(t).
$$
For $L=1/6,H=1$, one has $v_*=(1/10,1/10)$, $L_*=3/5$, $D_t=2/9+(19/15)t+o(t)$, and $D_t/(4V+3ta)=1/9+(47/90)t+o(t)$. At the trial mark $g+tv_*$ the three indicated canonical finite-tilt caps have exact masses $2/9+(19/15)t+t^2/50$ for $\ell_0$ and $2/9+(19/15)t+t^2/200$ for $\ell_1,\ell_2$ for all sufficiently small $t>0$.

## Proof

Apply the local strip response. At fixed $b=\ell_0(v)$, the minimum of the last two scores of $\mathcal L(v)$ is at most $a+\kappa b/2$, with equality exactly when $\ell_1(v)=\ell_2(v)=-b/2$ since their sum is $-b$. The first score $-\kappa b+aj(b)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, whereas $a+\kappa b/2$ is strictly increasing. At $b=c=-H/2$, the first score is strictly above the second; at $b=0$, it is $a/2<a$. They therefore cross uniquely in $(c,0)$, where $j(b)=1-(b-c)/H$. The minimum of the two scores has its unique maximum there. Solving gives $b=ac/(3\kappa H/2+a)=-2LH/(1+4L)$ and the common value $L_*=a+\kappa b/2=a[1-1/(6(1+4L))]$. At that crossing both remaining scores must attain their mean to reach $L_*$, so the unique maximizing vector is $v_*$.

The exact seven-base reduction gives attainment on $q$, and bounds every maximizing displacement $(y_t-g)/t$. Apply the local response to the fixed trial displacement $v_*$ for the lower bound. Along any convergent subsequence of maximizing displacements, the local response then forces its limit to maximize $\mathcal L$, hence to equal $v_*$. Compactness gives the asserted convergence for every maximizing selection and the maximum on $q$ equals $\Gamma V+t(a+L_*)+o(t)$. The two other anchors have exact maximum $\Gamma V+ta$, the fourth core has maximum $\Gamma V$, and the three additions are bounded by $ta$, all by the exact reduction. As $L_*>0$, only $q$ can maximize globally for small $t$. Divide by the exact total to obtain the normalized coefficient.

For $L=1/6,H=1$, substitution gives $a=2/3$ and the stated displacement, scores and derivatives. At the trial mark the $r_0$ area is $ta$ for each bounded-tilt realizing cap. At $\ell_0$ choose zero opposite pair shift. The uniform $R$ on $[-3/2,1/2]$ has tail at $\ell_0(v_*)=-1/5$ equal to $7/20$, so the pair contributes $2ta(7/20)$. For $\ell_1,\ell_2$ choose a saturated pair shift, giving $ta$. The own canonical area is $V(2/3-t\ell_i(v_*))^2$; its quadratic coefficients are $1/50$ and $1/200$ respectively. Adding the two types of endpoint mass gives the asserted exact values.
