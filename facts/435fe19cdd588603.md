## Statement

Retain the full reflected quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. Explicitly, for a positive law \(p\) on a finite lattice support \(A\), extended by zero off \(A\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=A\cap(2j-A),
&H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},
&b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},
&Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},
&V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
Every reflected row includes both orientations of each nonzero displacement and its central atom once. The symbols \(p,Z,b,q,\nu\) have precisely these original-law and full-row meanings throughout.

There is a sequence of full-dimensional convex lattice polytopes \(Q\) in dimensions \(n\) tending to infinity such that \(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is the union of its two complete one-center cores \(R_0,R_1\), with exactly two nontrivial integral midpoint centers \(c_0,c_1\). For core indices \(a\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), the exact alias is \(R_a:=R_{c_a}\) in the generic reflected-row notation above. These cores satisfy \(|R_0\cap R_1|=1\). In the core-capacity notation of accepted fact 9e1805a461c7be2c, let \(M_a=(|R_a|+1)/2\). Define \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=|R_0\cap R_1|\); this is the overlap cardinality called \(q\) in the preceding research handoff, and is unrelated to the canonical auxiliary probability law \(q_j\). Here and below the capacity is exactly
$$
K:=|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^n:x\in A\rbrace|
=M_0+M_1-q_{\mathrm{ov}},
\qquad q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1.
$$
In particular, \(K\) does not denote \(2^n\).

For each member of this sequence there is one global affine law \(w_x=e^{\ell\cdot x}\), with its original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\) and \(p_x=w_x/Z\), such that eventually \(A_0:=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3<1\) and, with \(d=-\log A_0\) and \(g=\log(K^2b(p))\),
$$
d>0,\qquad g>0,\qquad
\frac{g}{d}\longrightarrow13,\qquad
\frac{\nu}{g}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{1}
$$
More precisely, there are integers \(m\) tending to infinity, \(K=m+3\), and \(\theta=(2/3)\log m\) such that
$$
md\longrightarrow2,\qquad
mg\longrightarrow26,\qquad
\frac{m\nu}{\theta^2}\longrightarrow4.
\tag{2}
$$
Consequently, writing \(\log_+(u)=\max(0,\log u)\) for \(u>0\), there is no absolute \(C\) for which all active two-core unions obey
$$
\nu\le Cg\left[1+\log_+(g/d)\right]^2.
\tag{3}
$$
There is also no absolute \(C\) for which each such union admits some \(s\ge1\) satisfying both \(d\le Ce^{-s}g\) and \(\nu\le Cs^2g\).

## Proof

For each positive integer \(h\) put \(r=3h\) and \(m=2^r\), and use coordinates \((x,z,y)\in\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\), so \(n=r+3\). Let \(e_1,e_2\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^2\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_+&=\lbrace(b,1,0):b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^r\rbrace,
&A_-&=-A_+,\\
E&=\lbrace(0,1,e_1),(0,1,-e_1),(0,1,e_2),(0,1,-e_2)\rbrace,
&A&=\lbrace0\rbrace\cup A_+\cup A_-\cup E,
\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}(A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Here \(0\) always denotes the zero vector in the coordinates in which it occurs. We first prove the entire lattice census.

All points of \(Q\) have \(-1\le z\le1\). The top slice at \(z=1\) is the convex hull of \([0,1]^r\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) and \(\lbrace0\rbrace\times\operatorname{conv}\lbrace e_1,-e_1,e_2,-e_2\rbrace\), in the \((x,y)\) coordinates. Its \(y\) projection is the crosspolytope \(|y_1|+|y_2|\le1\), whose integral points are \(0\) and the four indicated vertices. If \(y=0\), every integral \(x\) belongs to \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^r\) and is already represented in \(A_+\). If \(y\) is one of the four nonzero vertices, its extremality forces the point to be the corresponding member of \(E\), with \(x=0\). Thus the top slice has exactly the lattice census \(A_+\cup E\). The bottom slice at \(z=-1\) is \([-1,0]^r\times\lbrace0\rbrace\), whose lattice census is \(A_-\).

The origin is the midpoint of \((0,1,0)\in A_+\) and \((0,-1,0)\in A_-\). Therefore \(Q\) is already the convex hull of its top and bottom slices. Every point of its slice at \(z=0\) is a midpoint of a point in each of those slices. Each \(x\) coordinate and each \(y\) coordinate is consequently in \([-1/2,1/2]\). The only integral possibility is \(x=y=0\), which occurs. The only possible integral heights are \(-1,0,1\), so \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\) exactly. The top slice has affine dimension \(r+2\) and the bottom slice adds a height direction, so \(Q\) is full-dimensional.

The parity classes can also be listed exactly. Within \(A_+\), distinct Boolean \(b\) have different parities, and the same holds within \(A_-\). A point \((b,1,0)\) and a point \((-b',-1,0)\) have the same parity exactly when \(b=b'\), so these classes are precisely the \(m\) antipodal pairs with midpoint \(0\). The four points of \(E\) have nonzero \(y\) parity. They occupy exactly two further parity classes, the two pairs \((0,1,e_i),(0,1,-e_i)\), with common midpoint \(c_1=(0,1,0)\). Their \(y\) parities distinguish them from \(A_+\) and \(A_-\). Finally, the origin has even \(z\) parity, whereas every other supported point has odd \(z\) parity. Thus it is a singleton class. These are all the parity classes. A distinct integral pair has an integral midpoint exactly when its points have the same parity, so the complete nontrivial midpoint-center set is exactly \(\lbrace c_0,c_1\rbrace\), where \(c_0=0\).

Reflection about the two centers now gives the exact cores
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_0&=\lbrace0\rbrace\cup A_+\cup A_-,
&|R_0|&=2m+1,
&M_0&=m+1,\\
R_1&=\lbrace c_1\rbrace\cup E,
&|R_1|&=5,
&M_1&=3,\\
R_0\cap R_1&=\lbrace c_1\rbrace,
&A&=R_0\cup R_1,
&K&=m+3=M_0+M_1-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
For example, reflection of a top point \((b,1,0)\) about \(c_1\) gives \((-b,1,0)\), which is supported only when \(b=0\). Reflection of the origin about \(c_1\) gives height \(2\), and reflection of a bottom point gives height \(3\). Reflection about \(c_0\) sends every member of \(E\) to an unsupported point of height \(-1\) with nonzero \(y\). This proves the row assertions in (5) directly. Each core is complete: the bottom, middle, and top slice argument above applies to \(R_0\) alone, and \(R_1\) is an integral translate of the two-dimensional crosspolytope. The parity list proves that each has only its own center. This also verifies the core and capacity conventions of accepted fact 9e1805a461c7be2c without needing a structural classification.

Choose \(\theta=2h\log2=(2/3)\log m\), \(\varepsilon=e^{-\theta}=m^{-2/3}\), and take the single global linear functional \(\ell\cdot(x,z,y)=\theta z\). The original weights and original normalizer are
$$
w_x=\exp(\theta z),\qquad
Z=1+(m+4)\varepsilon^{-1}+m\varepsilon.
\tag{6}
$$
Introduce only the algebraic alias \(S:=\varepsilon Z=m+4+\varepsilon+m\varepsilon^2\). This does not replace the original normalizer or probability law. Directly from (6), the original probabilities are
$$
p_{c_0}=\frac{\varepsilon}{S},\qquad
p_x=\frac1S\quad(x\in A_+\cup E),\qquad
p_x=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{S}\quad(x\in A_-).
\tag{7}
$$
The shared center \(c_1\) is in the top layer, so \(p_{c_1}=1/S\). Its reflection through \(c_0\) is \(-c_1\in A_-\), and the required cross-center affine relation is exactly \(p_{-c_1}=p_{c_0}^2/p_{c_1}=\varepsilon^2/S\). The other cross-membership is absent. Thus the shared center and the affine coupling are both retained.

Using the entire support in (7), the atomic energy is exactly
$$
A_0=\frac{K^2}{S^3}
\left[m+4+\varepsilon^3+m\varepsilon^6\right].
\tag{8}
$$
There are no nontrivial reflected rows outside the two centers. Affinity makes every geometric-mean contribution in a row about \(j\) equal to \(p_j\). Hence \(H_p(c_0)=(2m+1)p_{c_0}\), \(H_p(c_1)=5p_{c_1}\), and \(H_p(j)=p_j\) otherwise. Define the auxiliary abbreviation \(B_{\mathrm{full}}:=K^2b(p)\). The full-row energy is exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\mathrm{full}}&=A_0+\Delta_0+\Delta_1,\\
\Delta_0&=\frac{4m(m+1)K^2\varepsilon^3}{S^3},
&\Delta_1&=\frac{24K^2}{S^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The shared atom \(c_1\) contributes to the cubic sum (8) once, as required for the original support. The two increments in (9) are the changes from singleton rows to full rows at \(c_0\) and \(c_1\), respectively.

For the canonical score quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, the row law at \(c_0\) is uniform on its \(2m+1\) points. Its scores are \(\theta\) on \(A_+\), \(-\theta\) on \(A_-\), and zero at \(c_0\). The five points of \(R_1\) all have the same original probability \(1/S\), so every score at \(c_1\) is zero. All other rows are singletons. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{c_0}&=\frac{2m}{2m+1}\theta^2,
&V_j&=0\quad(j\ne c_0),\\
q_{c_0}&=\frac{K^2(2m+1)^2\varepsilon^3}{S^3B_{\mathrm{full}}},
&\nu&=\frac{2m(2m+1)K^2\varepsilon^3}{S^3B_{\mathrm{full}}}\theta^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
These are the full canonical \(q\) and \(\nu\), computed from the original \(p\) and \(b\).

It remains to verify the asymptotics used to refute the universal bound. Because \(\varepsilon=m^{-2/3}\), the exact expressions give
$$
S=m+4+o(1),\qquad
m+4+\varepsilon^3+m\varepsilon^6=m+4+o(1),
\qquad \varepsilon^3=m^{-2}.
$$
Substituting into (8), expanding after division of numerator and denominator by \(m^3\), gives
$$
A_0=\frac{(m+3)^2[m+4+o(1)]}{[m+4+o(1)]^3}
=1-\frac2m+o(m^{-1}).
\tag{11}
$$
To make the coefficient explicit, the numerator after division by \(m^3\) is \(1+10/m+o(1/m)\), and the denominator is \(1+12/m+o(1/m)\). The two occurrences of \(o(1)\) in (11) need not agree; their stated size suffices for both coefficients. Equation (9) gives \(m\Delta_0\to4\) and \(m\Delta_1\to24\). Therefore
$$
B_{\mathrm{full}}=1+\frac{26}{m}+o(m^{-1}).
\tag{12}
$$
In particular \(A_0<1\) and \(B_{\mathrm{full}}>1\) for every sufficiently large \(h\). The elementary limit \(\log(1+u)/u\to1\) at zero gives \(md\to2\) and \(mg\to26\). From (10), using \(\varepsilon^3=m^{-2}\), \(S/m\to1\), \(K/m\to1\), and \(B_{\mathrm{full}}\to1\), one obtains \(m\nu/\theta^2\to4\). This proves (2) entirely from the exact finite formulas.

It follows that \(g/d\to13\) and
$$
\frac{\nu}{g[1+\log_+(g/d)]^2}
\sim\frac{2\theta^2}{13(1+\log13)^2}
\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{13}
$$
Thus (3) fails for every fixed \(C\). The separate inequality \(d\le Cg\) is consistent with this example, since \(d/g\to1/13\); it cannot repair the failure of the score bound.

The same formulas explain why selecting the two hub rows separately remains possible in this family. If only the small row at \(c_1\) is retained in full, while every other auxiliary row is a singleton, its scaled energy is \(A_0+\Delta_1=1+22/m+o(1/m)>1\) eventually. Its entire selected score price is zero, since the retained nontrivial row has constant original probability on all five atoms. Thus the obstruction comes specifically from requiring the full high-score row at \(c_0\) to accompany the row that supplies this positive energy.

The more general proposed whole-factor scale also fails. If one fixed \(C\ge1\) allowed \(s\ge1\) with \(d\le Ce^{-s}g\), then, for all sufficiently large \(h\), \(g/d\le14\) would force \(s\le\log(14C)\). The additional estimate \(\nu\le Cs^2g\) would then bound \(\nu/g\) by \(C[\log(14C)]^2\), contradicting (10)-(12). Taking \(C\ge1\) loses no possibility because increasing an admissible positive constant preserves both estimates.

Finally, this example uses exactly the occupied capacity \(K=m+3\). Its ambient parity capacity is \(2^n=8m\). With that different scaling, the atomic energy would tend to \(64\) and would eventually be inactive. Hence the counterexample proves exactly the occupied-capacity scalar obstruction asserted here, with one shared center; it makes no claim against an ambient-scaled active-case statement or against the locked depth conjecture.
