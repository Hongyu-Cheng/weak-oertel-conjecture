## Statement

Use \(C_z,a_z,W,A_*,\mathcal K_z,c_z,D_z\) with the normalization of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`: \(C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(a_z=\mathcal H_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_z a_z\), \(A_*=\max_{a_z>0}a_z\), \(c_z\) is the centroid of a positive-mass fiber, \(\mathcal K_z=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\), and \(D_z=\mathcal K_z-c_z\). Thus the assignment's \(K_z\) is \(\mathcal K_z\), and its \(a_{\max}\) is \(A_*\). Write \(c_{\mathbf0}\) for the zero-fiber centroid, not for the conjectural absolute constant. For a symmetric convex body \(D_z\), define its gauge by \(\|v\|_{D_z}=\inf\{\lambda>0:v\in\lambda D_z\}\).

For integers \(3\leq k\leq n\) and a real \(\epsilon>0\), put \(d=2^k\binom nk\), index the continuous coordinates by \((A,\sigma)\) with \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(|A|=k\), and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^A\), and define
\[
C=\left\{(u,x):u\in[-1,1]^n,\quad
x_{(A,\sigma)}\geq\epsilon\max\left\{\sum_{i\in A}\sigma_i u_i-(k-1),0\right\},\quad
\sum_{(A,\sigma)}x_{(A,\sigma)}\leq d\right\}.
\]
This is a convex body. For a ternary root \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\), let \(m=|\{i:z_i\ne0\}|\) and \(N_m=\binom mk\), with \(N_m=0\) for \(m<k\). Its fiber is positive-mass exactly when \(\epsilon N_m<d\), is a singleton exactly when \(\epsilon N_m=d\), and is empty when \(\epsilon N_m>d\). All integer roots outside \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\) have empty fibers. Moreover,
\[
a_z=\frac{(d-\epsilon N_m)_+^d}{d!},\qquad
A_*=\frac{d^d}{d!},\qquad
W=A_*\sum_{m=0}^n\binom nm2^m
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon N_m}{d}\right)_+^d.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus (1) includes every positive and zero fiber, with no omitted completed support.

There is also an exact actual gauge formula. For every positive-mass ternary root with \(m\geq k\), put \(r=2N_m\). Let \(q_{r,d}\in(0,1)\) be the unique median of the probability density
\[
p_{r,d}(y)=\frac{d!}{(r-1)!(d-r)!}\,y^{r-1}(1-y)^{d-r},\qquad 0<y<1,
\]
and put \(\kappa_{r,d}=2q_{r,d}(1-q_{r,d})p_{r,d}(q_{r,d})/(d+1)\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|c_z+c_{-z}-2c_{\mathbf0}\|_{D_z}
&=\frac{\epsilon r(d+1-r)}{(d+1)(d-\epsilon N_m)\kappa_{r,d}}\\
&\geq\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{r(d+1-r)(d+2)}}{d-\epsilon N_m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
For positive roots with \(m<k\), that gauge is zero.

In particular, for every integer \(k\geq3\), choose \(n=2k\) and \(\epsilon=1\), and let \(R_k=\{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{2k}:|\{i:z_i\ne0\}|=k\}\). All \(3^{2k}\) ternary fibers are positive-mass, and
\[
\frac{2^{2k}A_*}{W}\leq\frac{4(2k+1)}{2^k}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\frac{\sum_{z\in R_k}a_z}{W}\geq\frac1{12}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every \(z\in R_k\) satisfies
\[
\|c_z+c_{-z}-2c_{\mathbf0}\|_{D_z}
\geq\sqrt{\frac{2(d+2)}{d-1}}>\sqrt2.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently the two screens hold with the fixed choices \(\eta=1\) and original-mass fraction \(1/12\), along \(k\to\infty\).

## Proof

First audit the body and all its integer fibers. Each coordinate constraint is the intersection of \(x_{(A,\sigma)}\geq0\) and \(x_{(A,\sigma)}\geq\epsilon(\sum_{i\in A}\sigma_i u_i-(k-1))\). Hence \(C\) is closed and convex. All its continuous coordinates lie in \([0,d]\), so it is bounded. At \(u=\mathbf0\) and \(x_{(A,\sigma)}=1/2\), every defining inequality is strict, including the base bounds and the sum bound. A sufficiently small open neighborhood of this point therefore lies in \(C\). Thus \(C\) is a convex body for every finite \(\epsilon>0\).

An integral base point in the base box is ternary. At such a point, every summand \(\sigma_i z_i\) belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The activation expression is positive exactly when all \(k\) summands equal 1, and its value is then 1. This occurs precisely when \(A\) is a \(k\)-subset of the occupied entries and \(\sigma_i=z_i\) on \(A\). Let \(b_z\in\{0,1\}^d\) be this activation indicator; it has exactly \(N_m\) entries equal to 1. Thus
\[
C_z=\left\{\epsilon b_z+y:y_i\geq0,\quad\sum_i y_i\leq d-\epsilon N_m\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
A negative right-hand side makes (5) empty, a zero right-hand side makes it a singleton, and a positive one makes it a full-dimensional simplex. Its volume is the first formula in (1). Since \(N_m\geq0\), the maximum fiber mass is \(d^d/d!\), attained in particular at the zero root. There are \(\binom nm2^m\) ternary roots with \(m\) occupied entries, giving the complete sum in (1).

We next compute the actual half-metronoid gauge, rather than using a centroid displacement alone. Put \(\Delta_d=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:y_i\geq0,\ \sum_i y_i\leq1\}\), and let \(X\) be uniform on \(\Delta_d\). Append the slack coordinate \(X_0=1-\sum_{i=1}^dX_i\). The \(d+1\) barycentric coordinates are exchangeable: permuting them induces an affine automorphism of \(\Delta_d\) that preserves its uniform probability measure. In particular, \(\mathbb E X_i=1/(d+1)\) for each barycentric coordinate.

For any specified group of \(r\) barycentric coordinates, where \(1\leq r\leq d\), their sum \(Y\) has density \(p_{r,d}\). To verify this directly, use exchangeability to put the group among the first \(r\) nonslack coordinates. Integrating their sum at \(y\) contributes \(y^{r-1}/(r-1)!\), and integrating the remaining \(d-r\) nonslack coordinates contributes \((1-y)^{d-r}/(d-r)!\). Uniform measure on \(\Delta_d\) has density \(d!\). Multiplying gives the asserted density; its integral is 1 by successive integration by parts. The same beta integrals give
\[
\mathbb EY=\frac r{d+1},\qquad
\mathbb EY^2=\frac{r(r+1)}{(d+1)(d+2)},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\frac{r(d+1-r)}{(d+1)^2(d+2)}.
\tag{6}
\]
The density is positive on \((0,1)\), so the median \(q_{r,d}\) exists uniquely and has upper-tail probability \(1/2\).

Define the centered half-metronoid of \(\Delta_d\) by
\[
B_d=\left\{2\mathbb E[Xf(X)]-\mathbb EX:
0\leq f\leq1,\quad\mathbb Ef=\frac12\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Complementing \(f\) changes the sign of the represented centered point. Averaging admissible densities shows convexity. This is the same half-mass normalization as \(D_z\) in accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`. Its gauge is a genuine finite norm: if \(\theta\) is sufficiently small, \(f(X)=(1+\langle\theta,X-\mathbb EX\rangle)/2\) is admissible and represents \(\operatorname{Cov}(X)\theta\). The covariance is positive definite because a nonzero linear functional cannot be constant on the full-dimensional simplex. Hence (7) contains a neighborhood of zero. The half-metronoid is compact, as also supplied by the normalization in that accepted fact.

Let the group functional be the sum of the specified \(r\) barycentric coordinates. Its centered support on \(B_d\) is maximized by \(f=\mathbf1_{\{Y\geq q_{r,d}\}}\). Indeed, for every admissible \(f\), the pointwise product
\((Y-q_{r,d})(f-\mathbf1_{\{Y\geq q_{r,d}\}})\) is nonpositive, and the two densities have equal expectation. Therefore the centered support equals
\[
2\mathbb E\left[\left(Y-\frac r{d+1}\right)
\mathbf1_{\{Y\geq q_{r,d}\}}\right]=\kappa_{r,d}.
\tag{8}
\]
For the equality with the formula in the statement, differentiate the density:
\[
\frac{d}{dy}\bigl(y(1-y)p_{r,d}(y)\bigr)
=\bigl(r-(d+1)y\bigr)p_{r,d}(y).
\]
Integrating this identity from \(q_{r,d}\) to 1 proves (8) with \(\kappa_{r,d}=2q_{r,d}(1-q_{r,d})p_{r,d}(q_{r,d})/(d+1)>0\). Also, since \(2\mathbf1_{\{Y\geq q_{r,d}\}}-1\) has absolute value 1, Cauchy-Schwarz and (6) give
\[
\kappa_{r,d}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}
=\frac{\sqrt{r(d+1-r)}}{(d+1)\sqrt{d+2}}.
\tag{9}
\]

Return to a positive fiber and put \(t_m=d-\epsilon N_m>0\). Changing variables in the defining half-metronoid integral in (5) gives
\[
c_z=\epsilon b_z+\frac{t_m}{d+1}\mathbf1_d,
\qquad D_z=t_mB_d.
\tag{10}
\]
At the zero root, \(c_{\mathbf0}=d\mathbf1_d/(d+1)\). If \(m<k\), then \(b_z=b_{-z}=0\) and the curvature vector is zero. Otherwise the supports of \(b_z\) and \(b_{-z}\) are disjoint, because the activating sign patterns on each occupied \(k\)-subset are opposite. Their union is a group \(G_z\) of \(r=2N_m\) continuous coordinates. Notice that \(N_m\leq\binom nk=d/2^k\), so \(r\leq d/4<d+1\). Equation (10) gives
\[
v_z:=c_z+c_{-z}-2c_{\mathbf0}
=\epsilon\left(b_z+b_{-z}-\frac r{d+1}\mathbf1_d\right).
\tag{11}
\]
Lift a centered continuous vector \(v\) to \((v,-\sum_i v_i)\) by appending its centered slack coordinate. Under this linear isomorphism, (11) is exactly
\(\epsilon(\mathbf1_{G_z}-r\mathbf1_{d+1}/(d+1))\), and the lifted \(B_d\) is invariant under permutations of all barycentric coordinates.

The group functional evaluates the lifted vector in (11) at \(\epsilon r(d+1-r)/(d+1)\), whereas its support on \(D_z\) is \(t_m\kappa_{r,d}\). This gives the lower bound for its gauge by their ratio. The ratio is also attained. Take the point of \(B_d\) represented by the maximizing density in (8), and average its barycentric lift over permutations within \(G_z\) and within its complement. The averaged point remains in the lifted \(B_d\), has unchanged group functional \(\kappa_{r,d}\), has coordinate sum zero, and is constant on each of the two groups. It is consequently
\[
\frac{(d+1)\kappa_{r,d}}{r(d+1-r)}
\left(\mathbf1_{G_z}-\frac r{d+1}\mathbf1_{d+1}\right).
\]
Scaling this point by \(t_m\) proves the equality in (2). Substituting (9) proves its stated lower bound. Thus (2) is an estimate for the actual half-metronoid gauge, with no replacement by a surrogate body.

Finally choose \(n=2k\) and \(\epsilon=1\). For every ternary root,
\(N_m\leq\binom{2k}{k}=d/2^k<d\). Hence every ternary fiber is positive-mass and there are no nonempty zero-mass integer fibers. All other integer fibers are empty. Write \(w_m=\binom{2k}{m}2^m\) and \(g_m=(1-N_m/d)^d\). For \(m<k\), \(g_m=1\); for \(m=k\), \(N_m=1\) and \(g_k=(1-1/d)^d\). Since \(d\geq2\),
\[
\frac14\leq g_k\leq1.
\tag{12}
\]
For completeness, the lower bound follows because \(x\log(1-1/x)\) is increasing for \(x\geq2\): its derivative is \(\log(1-t)+t/(1-t)\geq0\), with \(t=1/x\), as follows by integrating \(1/(1-s)\) on \([0,t]\). Its value at \(x=2\) is \(\log(1/4)\).

The central binomial coefficient is largest in its row. Therefore
\[
\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}w_mg_m
\leq w_k\sum_{i=1}^k2^{-i}<w_k.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(m=k+j\), \(1\leq j\leq k\), one has \(N_m=\binom{k+j}{k}\geq k+1\), \(g_m\leq e^{-N_m}\), and \(w_m/w_k\leq2^j\). Thus all the higher-occupancy positive fibers, including their complete mass, satisfy
\[
\sum_{m=k+1}^{2k}w_mg_m
\leq w_ke^{-(k+1)}\sum_{j=1}^k2^j
<w_k\left(\frac2e\right)^{k+1}<w_k.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (1), (12), (13), and (14) imply
\(A_*w_k/4\leq W<3A_*w_k\). The roots in \(R_k\) have total original mass \(A_*w_kg_k\), so their mass fraction is at least \(1/12\).

Since the \(2k+1\) coefficients in the \(2k\)-th binomial row sum to \(4^k\), \(\binom{2k}{k}\geq4^k/(2k+1)\). The lower bound for \(W\) therefore gives
\[
\frac{2^{2k}A_*}{W}
\leq\frac{4\cdot2^k}{\binom{2k}{k}}
\leq\frac{4(2k+1)}{2^k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For every root in \(R_k\), equation (2) has \(N_m=1\), \(r=2\), and \(t_m=d-1\), giving (4). This proves both required screens with the stated fixed original-mass fraction and completes the claim.
