## Statement

For every even integer \(n\ge4\), put \(k:=n-1\) and use exactly the widening-box polytope, support, weights, and total mass of accepted fact 373e992eb02b82d6:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n:=\{(t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^k:\;&
0\le t\le k,\quad -1\le x_i\le2,\\
&-t\le i x_i\le i+t\quad(1\le i\le k)\},\\
A_n&:=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
w_{n,(t,\mathbf x)}&:=2^{-t}\quad((t,\mathbf x)\in A_n),\\
W_n&:=\sum_{j\in A_n}w_{n,j}=n2^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set \(w_{n,j}:=0\) outside \(A_n\). For \(j\in A_n\), define the ordinary depth against this entire table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{w_n}(j)
&:=\inf\left\{
\sum_{z\in A_n\cap H}w_{n,z}:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace},\ j\in H
\right\},\\
\mathcal A_n&:=\sum_{j\in A_n}w_{n,j}\tau_{w_n}(j).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal A_n\) is the scalar called \(A\) in the registered subgoal; \(A_n\) retains its accepted support meaning. Let \(\rho\in(1/2,1)\) be the unique positive solution of \(2\rho^3+\rho^2=1\), and put \(\mathcal Q:=\rho^{-1}+1+\rho+\rho^2\). Then \(3<\mathcal Q<4\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_n
&\ge
\frac{2^{-(n-1)}}{128}\,
\rho^{32\sqrt{n-1}}(1+\mathcal Q)^{n-1},\\
\frac{2^n\mathcal A_n}{W_n^2}
&\ge
\frac{\rho^{32\sqrt{n-1}}}{64n^2}
\left(\frac{1+\mathcal Q}{4}\right)^{n-1}
\longrightarrow+\infty
\qquad(n\to\infty,\ n\text{ even}).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, the normalized full average has an absolute positive lower bound for all even \(n\ge4\).

## Proof

The function \(r\mapsto2r^3+r^2\) is strictly increasing for \(r>0\), takes the value \(1/2\) at \(r=1/2\), and takes the value \(3\) at \(r=1\). This proves the stated existence, uniqueness, and range of \(\rho\). The derivative of \(r^{-1}+1+r+r^2\) has the sign of \(2r^3+r^2-1\), so this function is minimized uniquely at \(\rho\). Its value there is strictly less than its value \(4\) at \(r=1\). Also \(\rho+\rho^{-1}\ge2\) and \(1+\rho^2>1\), giving \(\mathcal Q>3\).

We first prove a counting estimate for all halfspaces through the origin of the four-point product. For \(m\ge1\), let \(\mathcal B_m:=\{-1,0,1,2\}^m\). We claim that every closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) containing \(\mathbf0\), and also \(G=\mathbb R^m\), satisfies

\[
|\mathcal B_m\cap G|
\ge
\frac{\mathcal Q^m}{128}\rho^{32\sqrt m}.
\tag{1}
\]

Define a random variable \(Y\) by \(\mathbb P(Y=r)=\rho^r/\mathcal Q\) for \(r\in\{-1,0,1,2\}\). The defining equation of \(\rho\) gives
\(\mathbb E Y=(-\rho^{-1}+\rho+2\rho^2)/\mathcal Q=0\).
Writing \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb E Y^2\), the atom at \(-1\), the inequalities \(\rho<1\) and \(\mathcal Q<4\), and the support bound \(|Y|\le2\) give \(1/4<\sigma^2\le4\). Pointwise \(Y^4\le4Y^2\), so \(\mathbb E Y^4\le4\sigma^2\le16\sigma^4\).

Let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\) be independent copies of \(Y\). For a nonzero coefficient vector \(\mathbf c\in\mathbb R^m\), put \(U:=\sum_i c_iY_i\) and \(V:=\mathbb E U^2=\sigma^2\sum_i c_i^2>0\). Independence and zero means yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E U^4
&=(\mathbb E Y^4)\sum_i c_i^4
  +6\sigma^4\sum_{i<j}c_i^2c_j^2\\
&\le16\sigma^4\left(\sum_i c_i^2\right)^2
=16V^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Hölder's inequality applied to
\(|U|^2=|U|^{2/3}|U|^{4/3}\) gives
\(\mathbb E|U|\ge V^{3/2}/(\mathbb E U^4)^{1/2}\ge\sqrt V/4\).
Since \(\mathbb E U=0\), its positive part \(U_+:=\max(U,0)\) satisfies
\(\mathbb E U_+=\mathbb E|U|/2\ge\sqrt V/8\).
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\(\mathbb E U_+\le\sqrt V\,\mathbb P(U>0)^{1/2}\).
Consequently \(\mathbb P(U>0)\ge1/64\), and in particular
\(\mathbb P(U\ge0)\ge1/64\). For \(\mathbf c=\mathbf0\), the latter probability is \(1\).

Every closed halfspace \(G\) containing \(\mathbf0\) contains a halfspace of the form \(\{\mathbf y:\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\}\). Indeed, write its inequality as \(\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y\ge b\); containment of \(\mathbf0\) gives \(b\le0\). Thus the product law just constructed assigns mass at least \(1/64\) to \(G\). This is also true when \(G=\mathbb R^m\).

Put \(T:=\sum_iY_i\). Its mean is zero and its variance is \(m\sigma^2\le4m\). Chebyshev's inequality shows
\(\mathbb P(T<-32\sqrt m)\le1/256\). Hence the set
\(\{\mathbf Y\in G,\ T\ge-32\sqrt m\}\) has probability at least
\(1/64-1/256=3/256\ge1/128\).
For each \(\mathbf y\in\mathcal B_m\), the product probability is
\(\mathbb P(\mathbf Y=\mathbf y)=\rho^{\sum_i y_i}/\mathcal Q^m\).
Reversing this density on the preceding set gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal B_m\cap G|
&\ge
\mathcal Q^m
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf y\in\mathcal B_m\cap G\\
                 \sum_i y_i\ge-32\sqrt m}}
\mathbb P(\mathbf Y=\mathbf y)\rho^{-\sum_i y_i}\\
&\ge
\mathcal Q^m\rho^{32\sqrt m}\,
\mathbb P(\mathbf Y\in G,\ T\ge-32\sqrt m)\\
&\ge
\frac{\mathcal Q^m}{128}\rho^{32\sqrt m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second inequality uses \(0<\rho<1\), so
\(\rho^{-\sum_i y_i}\ge\rho^{32\sqrt m}\) whenever
\(\sum_i y_i\ge-32\sqrt m\). This proves (1) without discarding any boundary atom. For \(m=0\), the corresponding set is a singleton, and the bound with right side \(1/128\) is immediate.

We now use (1) at the top height of the original support. The defining inequalities of \(P_n\), or the exact support formula in accepted fact 373e992eb02b82d6, give
\(\{(t,\mathbf x)\in A_n:t=k\}=\{k\}\times\{-1,0,1,2\}^k\).
Every site there has weight \(2^{-k}\). More generally, the same accepted support formula gives \(2^{k+t}\) sites at height \(t\), for each integer \(0\le t\le k\), which also confirms \(W_n=(k+1)2^k=n2^k\).

Fix a top-height site \(j=(k,\mathbf a)\), and let \(m\) be the number of its coordinates in \(\{0,1\}\). Restrict attention to the subset of the top height obtained by fixing \(x_i=a_i\) at every remaining coordinate, where \(a_i\in\{-1,2\}\). All its \(m\) free coordinates range independently over \(\{-1,0,1,2\}\), and all its sites remain in \(A_n\). For each free coordinate with \(a_i=1\), make the affine change \(y_i=1-x_i\); for each free coordinate with \(a_i=0\), take \(y_i=x_i\). These changes preserve the four-point coordinate set and send the selected center to \(\mathbf0\).

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be any closed halfspace containing \(j\). Restrict its defining affine inequality to the affine space where \(t=k\) and the outer coordinates are fixed as above. After the preceding changes in the \(m\) free coordinates, this inequality defines a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf0\), or the whole space if its linear part vanishes. It cannot define the empty set because it holds at the selected center. Applying (1), including the singleton case \(m=0\), shows that the mass of the full original table in \(H\) is at least
\(2^{-k}\mathcal Q^m\rho^{32\sqrt m}/128\).
All other support sites have nonnegative mass. Taking the infimum over the original ambient halfspaces therefore gives

\[
\tau_{w_n}(j)\ge
\frac{2^{-k}}{128}\mathcal Q^m\rho^{32\sqrt m}.
\tag{3}
\]

This is a lower bound on the full depth. Restricting to a coordinate subset was used only to lower bound each halfspace's mass, so no upper bound from a restricted face or from an individual height is being asserted.

There are exactly \(\binom{k}{m}2^k\) top-height centers with \(m\) coordinates in \(\{0,1\}\): select those coordinates, then each coordinate has two choices of its specified type. Multiplying (3) by each center's original weight \(2^{-k}\), summing over all these types, and retaining their contribution to the full nonnegative average gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_n
&\ge
\frac{2^{-k}}{128}
\sum_{m=0}^k\binom{k}{m}\mathcal Q^m\rho^{32\sqrt m}\\
&\ge
\frac{2^{-k}}{128}\rho^{32\sqrt k}(1+\mathcal Q)^k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\rho^{32\sqrt m}\ge\rho^{32\sqrt k}\) for \(0\le m\le k\). Using \(n=k+1\) and \(W_n=n2^k\) in (4) gives the normalized inequality in the statement. Since \((1+\mathcal Q)/4>1\), the logarithm of its right side is
\(k\log((1+\mathcal Q)/4)+32\sqrt k\log\rho-2\log(k+1)-\log64\), which tends to \(+\infty\). Thus the full normalized average tends to \(+\infty\). The displayed lower bound is positive for every \(k\ge3\) and tends to \(+\infty\), so its infimum over these integer \(k\) is positive. This also proves the claimed absolute positive lower bound.
