## Statement

Use the six endpoints $P=((A,0),(0,B),(-t,t),(-a,0),(0,-b),(r,-r))$ with positive radii $R=(A,B,t,a,b,r)$. The indices are cyclic modulo six and $W_i$ is the area in the sector from $P_i$ to $P_{i+1}$. A core sector is one with $W_i=R_iR_{i+1}/2$.

Fix six positive sector areas $W_i$ realized by at least one planar convex body containing $0$ in its interior, with some positive radii. The set of feasible radius vectors is nonempty and compact, and each feasible radius satisfies
$$
\ell_i\le R_i\le\frac{2W_i}{\ell_{i+1}},
\qquad
\ell_i=\left(\frac1{2W_{i-1}}+\frac1{2W_i}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$
The target normalized inverse chord weight is
$$
p(R)=\left[1+\frac{B+b}{A+a}+\frac{B+b}{r+t}\right]^{-1}.
$$

It attains a minimum at a datum realized by a convex polygon. Every minimizing datum has a core sector in each of $\{0,1\}$ and $\{3,4\}$.

## Proof

Put $c_i=1/(2W_i)$. The symmetric cyclic matrix $L$ has diagonal $L_{ii}=c_{i-1}+c_i-1/R_i^2$ and entries $L_{i,i+1}=L_{i+1,i}=c_i-1/(R_iR_{i+1})$, with other off-diagonal entries zero.

The consecutive and next-neighbor determinants of the canonical ray vectors are one. Thus the exact criterion in `7d1ee06fa4119930` identifies the feasible radius vectors with the positive vectors satisfying $R_iR_{i+1}\le2W_i$ for every $i$ and $L\succeq0$. Its ray-gap hypothesis holds for these six rays. Nonnegativity of $L_{ii}$ gives $R_i\ge\ell_i>0$, and the inequality on the following sector gives $R_i\le2W_i/\ell_{i+1}$. The core inequalities and the PSD condition are closed within this positive compact box because all entries of $L$ are continuous there. Hence the exact feasible set is compact. It is nonempty by hypothesis.

The function $p$ is continuous on the positive box, so it has a minimizer on the feasible set. The same exact criterion gives a polygon realizing this datum. If either of the target endpoints lacked an incident core, `ordinary-central-radius-saturation` would increase that radius at fixed $W$ and strictly decrease $p$. This contradicts minimality.
