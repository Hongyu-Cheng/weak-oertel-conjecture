## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and assume that \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), where \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). Let \((\phi_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) be arbitrary real convex-admissible values: whenever \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0\), \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\), and \(\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\), require
\(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\).
Define the original weights, their total mass, and their mean by

\[
w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}},\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
\mathbf J_0=\frac1W\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I.
\]

Here \(D,P,W\) retain their support, convex hull, and total-weight meanings from accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. For this table, that fact's normalized potential is \(\phi-\min_D\phi\), and its normalization \(w_{\max}\) is \(e^{-\min_D\phi}\).

Assume that this original mean satisfies \(\mathbf J_0\in\mathbb Z^n\), and put \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}D\). Then \(\mathbf J_0\in D\cap\operatorname{relint}P\). Set \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D-\mathbf J_0)\) and use the whole intrinsic lattice \(\Gamma=E_{\mathrm{aff}}\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Choose a linear isomorphism \(\iota:\mathbb R^r\to E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) with \(\iota(\mathbb Z^r)=\Gamma\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_0&=\iota^{-1}(D-\mathbf J_0),&
P_0&=\operatorname{conv}D_0,\\
\psi_{\mathbf u}
&=\phi_{\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf u)}-\phi_{\mathbf J_0}
&&(\mathbf u\in D_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(D_0=P_0\cap\mathbb Z^r\), \(0\in\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^r}P_0\), and \(\psi_0=0\). For every \(\beta>0\), define

\[
C_\psi(\beta)
=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}
e^{-\beta\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}.
\]

The infimum is taken separately at each \(\beta\). For every \(0<t<1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_\psi(t)&\le \left(2+\frac4t\right)^r C_\psi(1),\\
0\le
g:=\frac1W\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf J_0}
&\le \frac{\log(2+4/t)}{1-t}\,r.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(g\le(4\log18/3)r\), by taking \(t=1/4\).

More generally, the same capacity comparison holds for every \(r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), every finite nonempty \(D_0=P_0\cap\mathbb Z^r\) with \(P_0=\operatorname{conv}D_0\) and \(\operatorname{aff}D_0=\mathbb R^r\), and every real convex-admissible potential \(\psi\) with \(0\in\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb R^r}P_0\) and \(\psi_0=0\). This general comparison requires no mean-zero hypothesis on the un-tilted weights \(e^{-\psi_{\mathbf u}}\). In intrinsic language its origin hypothesis is relative interior, including when the original support has smaller affine rank than \(n\). For \(r=0\), the support is the singleton \(\{0\}\), the capacity is one, and the original energy gap is zero.

All lattice sites of the original \(D\), including its relative boundary sites, occur in the chart and in the original law used to define \(g\). There is no sign restriction on \(\phi\) or on \(\psi\) away from zero. For every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^r\) and \(b\in\mathbb R\), set \(\widetilde\psi_{\mathbf u}=\psi_{\mathbf u}+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf u+b\). The same capacity definition gives
\(C_{\widetilde\psi}(\beta)=e^{-\beta b}C_\psi(\beta)\);
the minimizing tilts change by \(\boldsymbol\theta_\beta\mapsto\boldsymbol\theta_\beta+\beta\mathbf a\).
Consequently the centered optimizing law is unchanged, and subtracting the new value at zero leaves the normalized capacity comparison unchanged. In the original-mean application above, zero tilt is a minimizer at \(\beta=1\), so the law used for the energy conclusion is exactly \(w/W\).

## Proof

First, a positive convex combination of every point of a finite set belongs to the relative interior of its convex hull. Indeed, if that combination were on the relative boundary, a supporting affine functional, nonconstant on the affine hull, could be chosen nonnegative on the hull and zero at the combination. Positivity of every coefficient would force it to vanish at every point of the finite set, contradicting its being nonconstant on their affine hull. Apply this to the strictly positive original probabilities \(w_{\mathbf I}/W\). It gives \(\mathbf J_0\in\operatorname{relint}P\), and integrality and completeness give \(\mathbf J_0\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\).

The space \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is spanned by integer vectors. Its intersection \(\Gamma\) with \(\mathbb Z^n\) is therefore a lattice of rank \(r\): it is discrete and contains \(r\) real-linearly independent integer vectors spanning \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\). To justify the basis assertion directly, every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\) has an integer basis by induction on \(n\). Project to one coordinate; if the image is nonzero, choose an element at its positive generator and split off the kernel of that projection, to which the induction applies; if the image is zero, apply induction immediately. The integer rank here equals the dimension of the real span, since a real dependence among integer vectors is detected by the rank of a rational matrix and hence gives a rational, and then integer, dependence. Thus an integer basis of the whole \(\Gamma\) defines the asserted \(\iota\).

The map \(\mathbf u\mapsto\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf u)\) bijects \(\mathbb Z^r\) with the whole affine lattice \(\mathbf J_0+\Gamma\), and it bijects \(D_0\) with \(D\). If \(\mathbf u\in P_0\cap\mathbb Z^r\), then its image is in \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), proving \(D_0=P_0\cap\mathbb Z^r\). The converse inclusion follows from the definition. This chart preserves all relative boundary points, affine dimension, and relative interior. It changes no weights or probabilities and introduces no lattice-index factor. Convex-admissibility is preserved by the chart and the subtraction of \(\phi_{\mathbf J_0}\).

If \(r=0\), then \(D=\{\mathbf J_0\}\), \(D_0=\{0\}\), and \(\psi_0=0\). All the claimed capacity and energy conclusions follow immediately. The general rank-zero case is identical. Henceforth assume \(r\ge1\), and prove the capacity comparison under the stated general hypotheses on \(D_0\) and \(\psi\).

We use the full convex roof from accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. In that fact the positive support \(D\) and its hull \(P\) are our \(D_0\) and \(P_0\); its normalized potential is our \(\psi-\min_{D_0}\psi\). After adding this minimum back, its roof is the finite convex function

\[
\widehat\psi(x)=
\min\left\{
\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}\lambda_{\mathbf u}\psi_{\mathbf u}:
\lambda_{\mathbf u}\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}\lambda_{\mathbf u}=1,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}\lambda_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=x
\right\}
\qquad(x\in P_0).
\]

The accepted roof is defined on all of the closed \(P_0\), including its boundary, and agrees with the prescribed values at every site of \(D_0\). The essential properties can also be read directly from this formula: its feasible sets are nonempty compact subsets of a finite probability simplex, so the minimum is attained and finite; combining minimizing coefficients proves convexity; convex-admissibility and the singleton representation give \(\widehat\psi(\mathbf u)=\psi_{\mathbf u}\) for every \(\mathbf u\in D_0\). In particular \(\widehat\psi(0)=0\). The subtraction of the minimum is used only to map the normalization of the accepted predecessor, and imposes no nonnegativity assumption on our \(\psi\).

For \(\beta>0\), write
\(Z_\psi(\beta,\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}e^{-\beta\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}\).
Because \(0\in\operatorname{int}P_0\), choose \(\rho>0\) such that the closed radius-\(\rho\) ball is contained in \(P_0\). The maximum of a linear functional over \(P_0\) is its maximum over \(D_0\), and therefore

\[
Z_\psi(\beta,\boldsymbol\theta)
\ge
e^{-\beta\max_{D_0}\psi+
\max_{\mathbf u\in D_0}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}
\ge e^{-\beta\max_{D_0}\psi+\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}.
\]

Thus this continuous function is coercive and attains its infimum at some \(\boldsymbol\theta_\beta\). Differentiation at that minimizer shows that the strictly positive probability law
\(p^{(\beta)}_{\mathbf u}=e^{-\beta\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta_\beta\cdot\mathbf u}/C_\psi(\beta)\)
has mean zero. Also \(1\le C_\psi(\beta)<\infty\), since the term at zero is always one and the sum at zero tilt is finite.

For a probability law \(p=(p_{\mathbf u})_{\mathbf u\in D_0}\), write
\(H(p)=-\sum_{\mathbf u}p_{\mathbf u}\log p_{\mathbf u}\), with \(0\log0=0\), and
\(\mathbb E_p\psi=\sum_{\mathbf u}p_{\mathbf u}\psi_{\mathbf u}\).
For every mean-zero \(p\) and every tilt, Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(p)-\beta\mathbb E_p\psi
&=
\sum_{\mathbf u:p_{\mathbf u}>0}
p_{\mathbf u}
\log\frac{e^{-\beta\psi_{\mathbf u}+
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}}{p_{\mathbf u}}\\
&\le
\log\left(
\sum_{\mathbf u:p_{\mathbf u}>0}
e^{-\beta\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u}
\right)
\le\log Z_\psi(\beta,\boldsymbol\theta).
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking the infimum over tilts bounds the left side by \(\log C_\psi(\beta)\). At \(p=p^{(\beta)}\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta=\boldsymbol\theta_\beta\), equality follows by substituting the defining Gibbs formula and using its mean zero. Hence the finite variational identity is

\[
\log C_\psi(\beta)=
\max_{\substack{p_{\mathbf u}\ge0,\ \sum_{\mathbf u}p_{\mathbf u}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf u}p_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=0}}
\bigl(H(p)-\beta\mathbb E_p\psi\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

Fix \(0<t<1\) and a rational probability law \(p\) of full support on \(D_0\) with \(\sum_{\mathbf u}p_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=0\). Let \(m\ge2\) run through multiples of a common denominator of its coordinates. Retain the type-word notation from the proof of accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45, with that fact's \(A,P,d,\lambda\) equal to our \(D_0,P_0,r,t\). Thus \(\mathcal T_m(p)\) consists of the distinct words in \(D_0^m\) containing exactly \(mp_{\mathbf u}\) copies of each \(\mathbf u\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_m(p),\\
V_m&=\left\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in(\mathbb R^r)^m:
\sum_{j=1}^m x_j=0\right\},\\
L_m&=V_m\cap(\mathbb Z^r)^m.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are the same objects as in that accepted proof. It establishes that \(Q_m\subseteq V_m\cap P_0^m\), that \(Q_m\) has affine hull \(V_m\) and dimension \(r(m-1)\), and that its continuous centroid is zero. The full-support hypothesis makes all label multiplicities positive, so the swaps of any two labels in any two positions used to establish that affine hull are available. Block-permutation invariance then establishes the centroid assertion. Its lattice chart, which retains the first \(m-1\) blocks and recovers the last by the zero-sum constraint, identifies the whole \(L_m\) with \(\mathbb Z^{r(m-1)}\). In particular, equation (2) in the proof of 9c50e23fa3b45d45 gives the following estimate before that proof bounds the contracted interior count:

\[
|\mathcal T_m(p)|
\le\left(2+\frac4t\right)^{r(m-1)}
\left|t\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\right|.
\tag{2}
\]

Here relative interior is taken in \(V_m\). No lattice generated by a proper subgroup of support differences is substituted for \(L_m\).

For every type word, the sum of its prescribed potential values is exactly \(m\mathbb E_p\psi\). The function \((x_1,\ldots,x_m)\mapsto\sum_j\widehat\psi(x_j)\) is convex on \(P_0^m\). Consequently
\(\sum_j\widehat\psi(x_j)\le m\mathbb E_p\psi\) for every \(x\in Q_m\).
Since \(\widehat\psi(0)=0\), contraction toward zero gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^m\widehat\psi(t x_j)
\le t\sum_{j=1}^m\widehat\psi(x_j)
\le t m\mathbb E_p\psi
\qquad(x\in Q_m).
\tag{3}
\]

Every \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\in t\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\) has \(z_j\in tP_0\subseteq P_0\) and \(z_j\in\mathbb Z^r\). Completeness therefore gives \(z_j\in D_0\), so (3) bounds its original site cost \(\sum_j\psi_{z_j}\). It also has \(\sum_j z_j=0\). For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r\), these two properties imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-m\mathbb E_p\psi}
\left|t\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\right|
&\le
\sum_{z\in t\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m}
e^{-(1/t)\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_{z_j}
+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_{j=1}^m z_j}\\
&\le
\sum_{(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\in D_0^m}
e^{-(1/t)\sum_{j=1}^m\psi_{z_j}
+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_{j=1}^m z_j}\\
&=Z_\psi(1/t,\boldsymbol\theta)^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The first inequality follows because each summand there is at least \(e^{-m\mathbb E_p\psi}\), with the tilt term exactly zero. The second includes every tuple from the contracted hull in the complete original product support, and all additional summands are positive. Taking the infimum over \(\boldsymbol\theta\) yields

\[
\left|t\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m\right|
\le e^{m\mathbb E_p\psi}C_\psi(1/t)^m.
\tag{5}
\]

This passage uses an inequality valid for every tilt and the identity \(\inf_\theta Z(\theta)^m=(\inf_\theta Z(\theta))^m\) for nonnegative \(Z\). It makes no assumption about attainment of a capacity on a smaller interior support.

There are exactly \(m!/\prod_{\mathbf u\in D_0}(mp_{\mathbf u})!\) type words. The elementary factorial estimate \(\log(k!)=k\log k-k+O(\log(k+1))\), obtained by comparing the sum of \(\log j\) with its integral, gives
\(m^{-1}\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|\to H(p)\).
Combining (2) and (5), taking logarithms, dividing by \(m\), and passing to the limit along the permitted multiples proves

\[
H(p)-\mathbb E_p\psi
\le r\log(2+4/t)+\log C_\psi(1/t).
\tag{6}
\]

We now approximate the strictly positive centered law \(p^{(1)}\) by rational laws satisfying the same full-support and mean conditions. The integer matrix with columns \((1,\mathbf u)\), \(\mathbf u\in D_0\), has rank \(r+1\). Select \(r+1\) independent columns. Approximate all other coordinates of \(p^{(1)}\) by rationals and solve the normalization and mean-zero equations for the selected coordinates. The selected square integer matrix has rational inverse, so the resulting vector is rational. It converges to \(p^{(1)}\) and therefore has every coordinate positive for sufficiently close approximations. If there are no other coordinates, the same inverse shows that \(p^{(1)}\) is already rational. This also establishes the existence of the rational laws used above.

Both \(H(p)\) and \(\mathbb E_p\psi\) are continuous on the finite probability simplex. Applying (6) to these approximations and using (1) at \(\beta=1\) gives
\(\log C_\psi(1)\le r\log(2+4/t)+\log C_\psi(1/t)\).
The potential \(t\psi\) has the same complete support, is convex-admissible, and has value zero at the origin. Applying this last inequality to \(t\psi\), and using
\(C_{t\psi}(1)=C_\psi(t)\) and \(C_{t\psi}(1/t)=C_\psi(1)\), proves the asserted general comparison
\(C_\psi(t)\le(2+4/t)^r C_\psi(1)\).
The minimizing tilts at these parameters were free to differ throughout.

Return to the original-mean application. The exact original law in the chart is

\[
p^{\mathrm{orig}}_{\mathbf u}
=\frac{w_{\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf u)}}W
=\frac{e^{-\psi_{\mathbf u}}}
{\sum_{\mathbf v\in D_0}e^{-\psi_{\mathbf v}}},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf u\in D_0}p^{\mathrm{orig}}_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{orig}}}\psi=g.
\]

For every tilt, \(e^x\ge1+x\) gives
\(Z_\psi(1,\boldsymbol\theta)\ge Z_\psi(1,0)+
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\sum_{\mathbf u}e^{-\psi_{\mathbf u}}\mathbf u
=Z_\psi(1,0)\).
Thus zero tilt minimizes at \(\beta=1\),
\(C_\psi(1)=e^{\phi_{\mathbf J_0}}W\), and we may take \(p^{(1)}=p^{\mathrm{orig}}\).
Evaluating (1) at this original law for the parameter \(t\), and then using the capacity comparison, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log C_\psi(1)+(1-t)g
&=H(p^{\mathrm{orig}})-t\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{orig}}}\psi\\
&\le \log C_\psi(t)\\
&\le \log C_\psi(1)+r\log(2+4/t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \(1-t>0\), subtraction and division prove the claimed upper bound for \(g\). Convexity of \(\widehat\psi\) at the mean-zero original law gives \(g=\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{orig}}}\psi\ge\widehat\psi(0)=0\). At \(t=1/4\), \(2+4/t=18\) and \(1/(1-t)=4/3\), giving the stated absolute coefficient.

Finally, for \(\widetilde\psi_{\mathbf u}=\psi_{\mathbf u}+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf u+b\), direct substitution yields
\(Z_{\widetilde\psi}(\beta,\boldsymbol\theta)
=e^{-\beta b}Z_\psi(\beta,\boldsymbol\theta-\beta\mathbf a)\).
Taking infima proves the capacity transformation, and substitution at a minimizing tilt proves the asserted tilt shift and invariance of its centered probability law. For that law, the mean-zero identity also gives
\(\mathbb E_{p^{(\beta)}}\widetilde\psi-\widetilde\psi_0
=\mathbb E_{p^{(\beta)}}\psi\).
After subtracting \(\widetilde\psi_0=b\), the capacity is exactly \(C_\psi\) at every parameter. This is an affine normalization of centered capacities and their optimizing laws; the energy inequality already proved was evaluated at the original law \(w/W\), whose centering followed from the stated original integer-mean hypothesis.
