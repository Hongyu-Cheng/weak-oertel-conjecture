## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$, and let $v_0,\ldots,v_d$ span $\mathbb R^d$ with sole dependence generated by $\sum_jv_j=0$. Write $T=\{x:v_j\cdot x\le1\ \forall j\}$. Let $B\subseteq T$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body. Let $\lambda$ be a probability vector on all indices other than one fixed index $i$, extend it by $\lambda_i=0$, and put $J=\{j:\lambda_j>0\}$, $k=|J|$, and $u=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j$. For every $\varepsilon\ge0$,
$$
\bigl|B\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge1-\varepsilon\}\bigr|
\le
\min\left\{|B|,\frac{|T|\binom{d}{k}\varepsilon^k}{m^k\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j}\right\}.
$$
The face of $T$ at $u\cdot x=1$ lies in the intersection of the $k$ corresponding facets and has dimension at most $d-k$. The same dimension bound holds for the nonempty equality section of $B$.

## Proof

The affine map $x\mapsto q$ with $q_j=(1-v_j\cdot x)/m$ maps $\mathbb R^d$ bijectively to $\sum_jq_j=1$ by the rank and zero-sum hypotheses. It identifies $T$ with the standard simplex $q_j\ge0$. Use $i$ as the deleted coordinate. Uniform probability volume on $T$ has constant density $d!$ in the remaining $d$ coordinates because the standard coordinate simplex has volume $1/d!$. The cap inequality is $\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_j q_j\le\varepsilon/m$. At fixed $(q_j)_{j\in J}$ with nonnegative entries summing to at most one, the remaining $d-k$ coordinates form a coordinate simplex of volume $(1-\sum_{j\in J}q_j)^{d-k}/(d-k)!$. For $k=d$ this volume is one in dimension zero. The resulting $k$-coordinate marginal density is at most $d!/(d-k)!$.

Enlarge the cap domain to all nonnegative $k$ coordinates with the same weighted sum constraint. The substitution $y_j=\lambda_j q_j$ shows that its $k$-volume is $(\varepsilon/m)^k/(k!\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j)$. Multiplying by the density bound and $|T|$ gives the stated estimate for the enclosing simplex, and $B$ is also bounded by its own volume. At $\varepsilon=0$ every positively weighted nonnegative coordinate vanishes, so the equality set is in the indicated facet intersection of dimension $d-k$ and has zero $d$-volume.
