## Statement

In the body-tied profile setting at a fixed $\varepsilon>0$, let $Q>0$ be the maximum raw buffered depth. Assume every deepest mark is own-interior, every nonvertical buffered cut through one has a proper own section, and its closed pure-base depth exceeds $Q$. Write $I$ for the deepest bases. Then $I=\operatorname{conv}(I)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ and each $p\in I$ has a unique deepest mark $Y_p$. There is a collection of at most $k(I)\le k(P)$ groups covering $I$, with parity taken in the inherited affine lattice. Each group has a finite set of assigned bases $J_i$ in one occupied face of $\operatorname{conv}(I)$. Its at most $d+1$ actual mass-$Q$ nonvertical caps have normals $N_{ij}=(a_{ij},u_{ij})$ with $\Gamma(u_{ij})=1$, thresholds $c_{ij}$, and weights $\rho_{ij}>0$ with $\sum_j\rho_{ij}=1$. For all $p\in J_i$ and all $w=(p,X)$ in the whole fiber,
$$
\psi_i(w):=\sum_j\rho_{ij}(N_{ij}\cdot w-c_{ij})-\varepsilon f(w)\ge0.
$$
Its zero set there is precisely $\{Y_p\}$, where every $N_{ij}\cdot Y_p-\varepsilon f(Y_p)=c_{ij}$ and $\sum_j\rho_{ij}u_{ij}=\varepsilon\nabla_y f(Y_p)$. In particular the group covers each assigned whole fiber by buffered caps of mass at most $Q$.

## Proof

For $u\ne0$, mixed volume above the threshold is continuous and nonincreasing, and the largest threshold $c_Q(a,u)$ with mass at least $Q$ exists and has mass $Q$. For pure-base normals the finite closed tail also has a largest threshold with mass at least $Q$. Thus the deepest superlevel on the canonical hull is the intersection of the convex sublevels $a\cdot z+u\cdot X-\varepsilon f(z,X)\Gamma(u)\le c_Q(a,u)$ and the pure-base halfspaces. Its projection contains $\operatorname{conv}(I)$, and at all integer bases the depth condition is exactly the sampled one. This proves lattice completeness of $I$.

Fix a deepest mark $Y=(p,y)$ and normalize $\Gamma(u)=1$. A bounded tilt representative can be found for every through-mark cap near $Y$. Choose $L$ larger than all absolute continuous evaluations $|u\cdot(X-y')+\varepsilon f(p,y')|$ there. For a given tilt keep the evaluations $a\cdot(z-p)$ in $[-L,L]$ fixed, and preserve the corresponding $\ge L$ or $\le-L$ inequality for all others. The resulting polyhedron is nonempty. Its minimum-norm point lies in the span of its active rows, as a small displacement in either direction orthogonal to all active rows is feasible. A basis of the active rows gives that point as a fixed pseudoinverse applied to entries bounded by $L$. There are finitely many such row bases, so the tilt is uniformly bounded. The through-mark cap masses are preserved. Compactness and continuity of the tail then give attainment of the nonvertical infimum. The pure-base reserve and the proper own-section condition persist locally in $y'$ since the base is fixed and normalized $u$ ranges over a compact sphere. At $Y$ a mass-$Q$ nonvertical cap is active. Its own trace is proper, so its tail is strictly decreasing at the threshold, which must be $c_Q(a,u)$.

For the subgradient calculation also clip near-active right quantiles. The own evaluation $c_Q(a,u)-a\cdot p$ of any such threshold is within a fixed sufficiently small distance of $u\cdot y-\varepsilon f(Y)$. Apply the same tilt clipping with $L$ enlarged to cover those evaluations. It preserves the cap mass and all own thresholds, which stay proper. Strict decrease there makes the preserved threshold the right quantile of the representative. The representatives can be taken in the compact set of triples $(a,u,c)$ with the resulting bound on $a$, $\Gamma(u)=1$, cap mass $Q$, and $c-a\cdot p$ in a fixed closed small window around $u\cdot y-\varepsilon f(Y)$. Quantile terms outside that window are irrelevant near $y$: they have a positive slack at $y$, their $u$ are bounded, and an active term exists. Locally, then, the convex maximum of $a\cdot p+u\cdot X-\varepsilon f(p,X)-c$ over this compact set has value zero at $y$ and is nonnegative. Indeed at any nearby mark, either a cap has mass below $Q$ and its margin exceeds the right quantile, or its buffered depth is $Q$ and an attained proper cap yields equality. The compact-maximum subgradient rule and Carathéodory in $\mathbb R^d$ give at most $d+1$ active normals with strictly positive weights after removing zero weights, satisfying $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=\varepsilon\nabla_y f(Y)$. An active proper cap at a midpoint of two distinct deepest marks at one base would give a strict inequality there by strict own concavity and the two quantile inequalities at the endpoints. This contradicts its active equality and proves uniqueness.

Let $B=\operatorname{conv}(I)$. At a source $p$ choose the finite full supergradient $(\alpha,g)$ of $f$ from the affine chart result, with $g=\nabla_y f(Y_p)$. The weighted cap contact function obeys $\psi(z,X)\ge b\cdot(z-p)$ on $C$ for $b=\sum_j\rho_j a_j-\varepsilon\alpha$. At every deepest mark all selected quantile inequalities give $\psi(Y_z)\le0$. Consequently $b\cdot(z-p)\le0$ throughout $B$ and is zero on the smallest face containing $p$. On this face $\psi\ge0$ throughout every fiber and $\psi(Y_z)=0$ at every deepest mark, so every individual weighted quantile inequality there is equality. At a fixed base no further zero can occur, because an interior zero and any other zero would contradict strict concavity of $f$ on the interior of their segment. Differentiation at these own-interior minima of $\psi$ gives the asserted continuous normal identity at every assigned mark.

Call a face occupied if its relative interior contains an integer point. The inclusion-maximal occupied faces cover $I$, since every point of $I$ lies in the relative interior of its smallest face. Choose one integer relative-interior witness per maximal occupied face and apply the preceding menu construction at each witness. The midpoint of two such witnesses in the same parity class is an integer point in the relative interior of their join face, contradicting maximality for distinct faces. This bounds the group count. For any assigned whole-fiber point the nonnegative weighted contact function gives at least one buffered threshold at or above its selected threshold, so that cap has mass at most $Q$.
