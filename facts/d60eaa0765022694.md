## Statement

Let $X$ be any nonnegative $3\times3$ matrix whose columns sum to one. For a row $x$ write
$$
Q_j(x)=x_j(x_k+x_l)-x_kx_l,\qquad F(x)=\max_j Q_j(x),\qquad \phi(x)=\sqrt{F(x)},
$$
where $\{j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3\}$. Put $F_i=F(X_i)$. Then
$$
F_1+F_2+F_3+2\sqrt{F_1F_2F_3}\le1.
$$
The bound is attained when the diagonal is zero and each off-diagonal entry is $1/2$. When all $F_i>0$, equality occurs exactly when a permutation of columns gives a zero diagonal and, in the resulting representation $F_i=u_i(1-u_{i+1})$ with cyclic indices, $\prod_i u_i=\prod_i(1-u_i)$. The proof gives equality tests including zero capacities.

## Proof

Since $Q_j-Q_k=2x_l(x_j-x_k)$, a largest entry always gives a maximizing index. If $a$ is such an entry and $b,c$ are the other two, then $F=a(b+c)-bc=a^2-(a-b)(a-c)\in[0,a^2]$. Let $B$ be symmetric with diagonal zero and cyclic off-diagonal entries $\phi_i=\sqrt{F_i}$. The cubic is equivalent to $I-B$ being positive semidefinite. Indeed the cubic deficit is its determinant, and its other principal minors are $1$ and $1-F_i\ge0$. Thus the goal is that the largest eigenvalue $J(X)$ of $B$ is at most one.

First, fix $c_i>0$. A local maximum of $\sum_i c_i\phi(X_i)$ has at most one fully positive row. Suppose rows $r,s$ are both fully positive. Small signed transfers $X_r+tv,X_s-tv$ are feasible. Each smooth branch $\sqrt{Q_j}$ corresponding to a maximizing index in a positive row is positive there and lies below $\phi$ locally. Replacing each row function by any such branch therefore preserves the local maximum at the point and forces equality of the two weighted gradients under every transfer. A tied pair of maximizing entries $j,k$ in one row gives branch-gradient difference $(x_l/\phi(x))(e_j-e_k)\ne0$, a contradiction. For the unique indices $j,k$ of the two rows, their common weighted gradient $\lambda$ is positive entrywise: the derivatives of a maximizing $Q_j$ are $x_k+x_l$, $x_j-x_l$, and $x_j-x_k$ in the respective positions. When $j=k$, take $v_j=0$ and the other coordinates $v_a=\lambda_b$, $v_b=-\lambda_a$, so $Q_j(v)>0$. When $j\ne k$, set $v_j=v_k=1$ and $v_l=-(\lambda_j+\lambda_k)/\lambda_l$, so $Q_j(v)=Q_k(v)=1$. In either case $\lambda\cdot v=0$, and the second derivative of each branch along its transfer is $Q(v)/\sqrt{Q(x)}>0$, by differentiating its homogeneous quadratic. The sum has strictly positive second derivative, contrary to local maximality.

Next, suppose two rows each have a zero. If two chosen zeros are in the same column, write the columns as $(0,0,1),(a,b,c),(p,q,r)$. Then $F_1=ap$, $F_2=bq$, $F_3=c+r-cr$, and $\sum_iF_i=1-aq-bp$. As $F_3\le1$, AM–GM gives $aq+bp\ge2\sqrt{abpq}\ge2\sqrt{F_1F_2F_3}$. Equality is equivalent to $aq=bp=0$: positive equal products would give $a,b,p,q>0$, hence $c,r<1$ and $F_3<1$.

If the chosen zeros are in different columns, permute labels to write
$$
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&b\\c&0&e\\r&s&t\end{pmatrix},\qquad
a=1-s,\quad c=1-r,\quad L=b+e=1-t .
$$
Now $F_1=ab$, $F_2=ce$. Put $Z=(1-aL)(1-cL)$ and $z=F_3$. The differences $Z-Q_j(r,s,t)$ for $j=1,2,3$ are respectively
$$
t(1-r)[t(1-s)+2s],\quad
t(1-s)[t(1-r)+2r],\quad
t^2(1-r)(1-s)+2rs(1-t).
$$
Hence $0\le z\le Z\le1$. If $L=0$ the cubic reduces to $z\le1$, with equality exactly when $z=1$. For $L>0$ the matrix
$$
N=(1-z)I-L\begin{pmatrix}a&\sqrt{acz}\\\sqrt{acz}&c\end{pmatrix}
$$
is positive semidefinite: $1-z\ge1-Z=L(a+c-acL)\ge L\max(a,c)$ and $\det N=(1-z)(Z-z)\ge0$. Its quadratic form at $(\sqrt b,\sqrt e)$ is $L[1-z-ab-ce-2\sqrt{ac z b e}]$. This proves the cubic here, and equality is exactly $N(\sqrt b,\sqrt e)^{\mathsf T}=0$.

Finally $J$ attains a maximum by continuity and compactness. If a maximizing $X$ has $J(X)>1$, at least two of the three edges of $B$ are positive, since an isolated single edge has weight at most one. The graph is then connected and $B$ has a positive unit top eigenvector $w$: a Rayleigh maximizer can be made nonnegative by absolute values, and any zero entry would propagate to its neighbors by the eigenvector equation. Holding $w$ fixed, the Rayleigh quotient $w^{\mathsf T}B(X')w$ is at most $J(X')\le J(X)$ and equals $J(X)$ at $X'=X$. It is a positive weighted sum of the three row values $\phi_i$, so the first step forces two rows with zeros and the boundary step gives a contradiction. This proves $J\le1$.

The cubic deficit vanishes exactly when $J=1$. If at most one $F_i>0$, equality requires that value to be one, equivalent to its row having two entries equal to one by the formula $a^2-(a-b)(a-c)$. At any equality point with at least two positive capacities, the same fixed-eigenvector argument applies, so some two rows have zeros and the boundary equality tests are exhaustive. If all three capacities are positive, the same-column case cannot give equality. In the different-column case $a,b,c,e>0$, $L>0$, and $z<1$ by $1-z\ge L\max(a,c)>0$. Equality requires $\det N=0$, so $z=Z$. For $t>0$ all three displayed differences are strictly positive since $a,c>0$. Thus $t=0$, which puts zeros in three distinct columns in the three rows. With diagonal zero after permutation, write the three rows as $(0,u_1,1-u_2)$, $(1-u_3,0,u_2)$, $(u_3,1-u_1,0)$. Here all $u_i\in(0,1)$. Expanding the cubic deficit gives
$$
\left(\sqrt{\textstyle\prod_i u_i}-\sqrt{\textstyle\prod_i(1-u_i)}\right)^2.
$$
This proves the exact equality condition and gives equality at $u_1=u_2=u_3=1/2$.
