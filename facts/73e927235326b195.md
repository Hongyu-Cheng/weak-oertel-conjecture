## Statement

There is a compact convex polygon in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ with exactly ten nonempty integer interval fibers and an inclusion-minimal cover by two open halfspaces. Each closed cap has mixed length $500$ from total $1000$, and the largest fiber has fraction $257/2000$. There is no partition of the ten sites into at most two subsets with an affine center on each subset, belonging to every assigned fiber and preserving the fixed open cover under all continuous dilation factors $s\ge1$. A single affine map with exterior centers does preserve the cover, and the only normalized positive continuous circuit of the cap normals has constant strictly positive complementary term.

## Proof

Put $L=257/2$ and $C=\{(z,x):0\le z\le9,\ g(z)\le x\le L\}$, where $g$ linearly interpolates $j^2$ at the integers $0,\ldots,9$. Its slopes are $2j+1$ on $[j,j+1]$ and increase, so $g$ is convex and $C$ is a full-dimensional polygon. These ten sites give all sampled fibers, with $K_j=[j^2,L]$. Use $p(z)=130z-382$ and the open caps $x-p(z)>1/2$ and $x-p(z)<-1/2$. The complement at $j$ is $[p(j)-1/2,p(j)+1/2]$. For $j\le3$, it lies strictly below $K_j$: the difference $j^2-p(j)-1/2$ decreases over those sites and at $j=3$ equals $1/2$. For $j\ge4$, its lower endpoint is at least $275/2>L$. Thus one cap strictly contains each entire interval and the other misses it. Both groups are nonempty, proving minimality. The two closed cap masses are $4L-(0+1+4+9)=500$ and $6L-(16+25+36+49+64+81)=500$. The maximum fiber length is $L$.

For $c\in[a,b]$ and a complementary interval strictly below $[a,b]$, indefinite avoidance requires $c=a$: if $c>a$ the lower endpoint $c+s(a-c)$ reaches the complementary interval at finite $s$, while $c=a$ keeps the dilate above it. The corresponding upper-side condition is $c=b$. Thus the centers at $0,1,2,3$ must be $j^2$ and the other six centers must be $L$. One affine map agrees with $j^2$ at at most two distinct sites. If two maps serve all four left sites, each is a secant through $(a,a^2),(b,b^2)$ for integers $0\le a<b\le3$. Its value at $j\ge4$ is $(a+b)j-ab\le5j-6\le39<L$: the expression increases in each of $a,b$ there and is maximal at $(2,3)$. Neither map can serve any of the right sites.

The map $p$ is below each left interval and above each right interval, so every dilation about it moves the respective endpoints away from the complementary strip. The continuous normals are $1,-1$. Their normalized balancing weights are uniquely $(1/2,1/2)$ and their weighted complementary term is $1/2$ because the strip width is one.
