## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, and use the saturated maximum-matching notation and descendant-retention capacity of accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`:
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{area}(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),\qquad
 0<G<\frac W2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(p\) be a saturated maximum free same-parity matching furnished by that fact, put \(t_z:=2d_z(p)\), and define
\[
 U:=\{z\in Z:t_z<a_z\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
 t_z=a_z\quad(z\in Z\setminus U),
 \qquad
 \sum_{z\in U}a_z\le G.
\tag{3}
\]

Fix an admissible integral center for this selected profile and an actual support-minimal balanced negative circuit of cardinality three, with exact globally affine minorants, from the empty-core branch of accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb`. In the individual-cap notation used in accepted facts `6974f69acff32c8c` and `b7646e88958a76a4`, write
\[
 (w_i,\ell_i,A_i)_{i=1}^3,
 \qquad
 A_i=C\cap\{(z,x):\langle w_i,x\rangle\ge\ell_i(z)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Assume this circuit lies in the aligned triangular-homothetic subclass described as follows. There is a full-dimensional triangle \(\mathcal T\subseteq\mathbb R^2\), translated so that its centroid is the origin, whose barycentric coordinates are
\[
 \beta_i(y)=\frac13+\langle u_i,y\rangle
 \quad(i=1,2,3),
 \qquad
 u_1+u_2+u_3=0,\qquad
 \mathcal T=\{y:\beta_i(y)\ge0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\},
\tag{5}
\]
and for every \(z\in Z\) there are a centroid \(c_z\in\mathbb R^2\) and a scale \(\kappa_z>0\) such that
\[
 C_z=c_z+\kappa_z\mathcal T.
\tag{6}
\]
After relabeling the three circuit members and positively rescaling each pair \((w_i,\ell_i)\), which leaves the cap in (4) unchanged and is legal by `07009c5047a9f7bb`, assume
\[
 w_i=u_i\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{7}
\]

For \(z\in Z\setminus U\), define the normalized circuit thresholds and the cap losses by
\[
 \xi_{i,z}:=
 \frac{\kappa_z/3-\langle u_i,c_z\rangle+\ell_i(z)}{\kappa_z},
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,z}:=a_z-\operatorname{area}((A_i)_z).
\tag{8}
\]
Define the unclipped and clipped fully selected fibers by
\[
 V:=\{z\in Z\setminus U:\xi_{i,z}\ge0\text{ for }i=1,2,3\},
 \qquad
 B:=(Z\setminus U)\setminus V.
\tag{9}
\]
For a planar convex body \(K\) and \(0<b\le\operatorname{area}(K)\), write
\[
 \mathsf M_b(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1b\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\le f\le1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf(x)\,dx=b
 \right\}.
\tag{10}
\]

Then the following conclusions hold.

1. For every \(z\in Z\setminus U\) and every \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
 \xi_{i,z}\le\frac13,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,z}
 =a_z\bigl(2\xi_{i,z}^{+}-(\xi_{i,z}^{+})^2\bigr),
 \qquad
 0\le\Delta_{i,z}\le\frac59a_z,
\tag{11}
\]
where \(x^+:=\max\{x,0\}\).

2. There is one affine map \(q:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^2\), independent of \(z\), such that
\[
 q(z)\in\mathsf M_{a_z/3}(C_z)
 \qquad(z\in V).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{z\in V}a_z\le3G.}
\tag{13}
\]

3. Let \(0<\eta\le1\), let \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\) be exactly the capacity defined in `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`, and assume the hard descendant inequalities
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\le(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\le\frac{\eta}{6}W.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{z\in B}\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}
 \ge3\eta W-8G
 \ge\frac53\eta W,}
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{\sum_{z\in B}a_z\ge\frac32\eta W.}
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, (14) is impossible when \(B=\varnothing\).

The conclusions include all threshold boundary cases. A threshold \(\xi_{i,z}=0\) has zero loss and belongs to the unclipped case; \(\xi_{i,z}=1/3\) is allowed. If \(V=\varnothing\), (12) is vacuous and (13) reads \(0\le3G\). Under (14), \(B\) cannot be empty by (15).

## Proof

Because \(z\notin U\) implies \(t_z=a_z>0\), every \(z\in Z\setminus U\) belongs to the selected circuit support. Full selected mass makes its metronoid the centroid singleton,
\[
 \mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)=\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)=\{c_z\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The exact-minorant inequality in accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb` therefore gives
\[
 \ell_i(z)\le\langle u_i,c_z\rangle
 \qquad(z\in Z\setminus U),
\tag{18}
\]
which proves \(\xi_{i,z}\le1/3\).

The barycentric coordinates on the fiber \(C_z=c_z+\kappa_z\mathcal T\) are the nonnegative affine functions
\[
 b_{i,z}(x):=\frac{\kappa_z}{3}+\langle u_i,x-c_z\rangle,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^3b_{i,z}(x)=\kappa_z.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (4), (7), and (8) give the exact cap description
\[
 (A_i)_z
 =\{x\in C_z:b_{i,z}(x)\ge\kappa_z\xi_{i,z}\}.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\xi_{i,z}\le0\), this is the whole triangle. If \(0\le\xi_{i,z}\le1/3\), it is the translate of a positive homothet of \(C_z\) with linear ratio \(1-\xi_{i,z}\), and therefore has area \(a_z(1-\xi_{i,z})^2\). This proves the loss identity in (11). The function \(2x-x^2\) is increasing on \([0,1/3]\) and takes the value \(5/9\) at \(1/3\), proving the remaining bounds in (11), including both endpoint cases.

We construct the common affine selector. Since the three vectors \(u_i\) span \(\mathbb R^2\) and sum to zero, for each \(z\in\mathbb R^n\) there is a unique \(q(z)\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfying
\[
 \langle u_i,q(z)\rangle
 =\ell_i(z)-\frac13\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_j(z)
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{21}
\]
The right sides sum to zero and are affine in \(z\), so \(q\) is a globally affine map.

Fix \(z\in V\), and, to avoid collision with the support-radius notation of `6974f69acff32c8c`, write the local triangle slack as
\[
 \varrho_z:=1-\sum_{i=1}^3\xi_{i,z}
 =-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_i(z)}{\kappa_z}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equation (18), summed over \(i\), gives \(\varrho_z\ge0\), while the nonnegativity of all three thresholds on \(V\) gives \(\varrho_z\le1\). The barycentric coordinates of \(q(z)\) in \(C_z\) are
\[
 \lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}
 :=\frac{b_{i,z}(q(z))}{\kappa_z}
 =\xi_{i,z}+\frac{\varrho_z}{3}.
\tag{23}
\]
They are nonnegative and sum to one. Moreover, (11), (22), and (23) give
\[
 \frac{\varrho_z}{3}
 \le\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}
 \le\frac{1+\varrho_z}{3},
 \qquad
 3\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}\ge1-2\varrho_z.
\tag{24}
\]
For the last inequality, use
\[
 \xi_{i,z}
 =1-\varrho_z-\xi_{j,z}-\xi_{k,z}
 \ge\frac13-\varrho_z,
\tag{25}
\]
because each of the other two thresholds is at most \(1/3\).

Apply the simplex transportation theorem in accepted fact `75e9cb08300c59fb` in dimension two to \(C_z\) and \(q(z)\). It supplies a subtriangle centered at \(q(z)\) with relative area
\[
 P_z:=
 \prod_{\{i:3\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}<1\}}
 3\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}.
\tag{26}
\]
We claim that
\[
 P_z\ge\frac13.
\tag{27}
\]
There are at most two factors in (26), since the three numbers \(3\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}\) sum to three. If there is no factor, \(P_z=1\). If there is one factor \(a=3\lambda_{i,z}^{\triangle}\), then (24) gives
\[
 a\ge\max\{\varrho_z,1-2\varrho_z\}\ge\frac13.
\tag{28}
\]
If there are two factors, call them \(a,b<1\). Equation (24), applied also to the remaining coordinate, gives
\[
 a,b\ge\varrho_z,
 \qquad
 a+b\ge2-\varrho_z.
\tag{29}
\]
Minimizing \(ab\) over the closed relaxation of (29) with \(a,b\le1\) gives
\[
 ab\ge
 \begin{cases}
 1-\varrho_z,&0\le\varrho_z\le1/2,\\
 2\varrho_z(1-\varrho_z),&1/2\le\varrho_z\le2/3,\\
 \varrho_z^2,&2/3\le\varrho_z\le1.
 \end{cases}
\tag{30}
\]
Indeed, in the first range an extremal point uses one upper bound \(1\), in the second it uses one lower bound \(\varrho_z\) and the active sum \(a+b=2-\varrho_z\), and in the third the two lower bounds already satisfy the sum constraint. Every expression in (30) is at least \(4/9\) on its stated range. This proves (27).

The subtriangle from (26) has area at least \(a_z/3\) and centroid \(q(z)\). Shrink it homothetically about \(q(z)\) to area exactly \(a_z/3\). Convexity keeps the shrunken triangle inside \(C_z\), and its centroid remains \(q(z)\). Its indicator is an admissible density in (10), proving (12).

It remains to prove the complete-core bound (13). If \(V=\varnothing\), it is immediate. Suppose \(V\ne\varnothing\), and consider the finite lattice measure
\[
 \nu_V:=\sum_{z\in V}\frac{a_z}{3}\,\delta_z,
 \qquad
 S_V:=\nu_V(\mathbb R^n)=\sum_{z\in V}\frac{a_z}{3}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
The \(d=0\) specialization of accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` gives an integral point \(\widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni\widehat m\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in V\cap J}\frac{a_z}{3}\ge2^{-n}S_V.
\tag{32}
\]
Positive depth forces \(\widehat m\in\operatorname{conv}V\): otherwise strict separation from the finite polytope \(\operatorname{conv}V\) would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(\widehat m\) and no point of \(V\), contrary to (32).

For every \(\alpha\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(V)\), affineness of \(q\) and (12) give
\[
 q(\widehat m)
 =\sum_{z\in V}\alpha_zq(z)
 \in
 \sum_{z\in V}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{a_z/3}(C_z).
\tag{33}
\]
Thus the complete metronoid core of the selected profile \((a_z/3)_{z\in V}\) at \(\widehat m\) is nonempty. Apply the arbitrary-selected-mass nonempty-core branch of accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb` using (32). It yields
\[
 G\ge S_V=\frac13\sum_{z\in V}a_z,
\tag{34}
\]
which is exactly (13). This argument also shows why a separate selector on each fiber would not suffice: the same globally affine \(q\) supplies the point in every Minkowski combination in (33).

Assume now (14). Each \(A_i\) in the actual circuit (4) is an individual descendant in the collection defining \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\), as recorded in accepted facts `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`, `6974f69acff32c8c`, and the planar setup of `b7646e88958a76a4`. Therefore
\[
 W(A_i)\le R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\le(1-\eta)W
 \qquad(i=1,2,3),
\tag{35}
\]
and hence
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(W-W(A_i)\bigr)\ge3\eta W.
\tag{36}
\]
The sets \(U,V,B\) partition \(Z\). On the nonsaturated fibers, the trivial loss bound and (3) give
\[
 \sum_{z\in U}\sum_{i=1}^3
 \bigl(a_z-\operatorname{area}((A_i)_z)\bigr)
 \le3\sum_{z\in U}a_z\le3G.
\tag{37}
\]
On \(V\), equations (11) and (13) give
\[
 \sum_{z\in V}\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}
 \le\frac53\sum_{z\in V}a_z
 \le5G.
\tag{38}
\]
Subtracting (37) and (38) from the total loss (36) proves
\[
 \sum_{z\in B}\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}
 \ge3\eta W-8G.
\tag{39}
\]
The small-core hypothesis in (14) gives
\[
 3\eta W-8G
 \ge3\eta W-\frac{8\eta W}{6}
 =\frac53\eta W,
\tag{40}
\]
so (15) follows.

Finally, every \(z\in B\) has at least one threshold \(\xi_{i,z}<0\). By (20), that cap contains the whole fiber and has zero loss. The other two losses are each at most \(5a_z/9\) by (11), so
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}\le\frac{10}{9}a_z
 \qquad(z\in B).
\tag{41}
\]
Summing (41) and using (15) gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in B}a_z
 \ge\frac9{10}\cdot\frac53\eta W
 =\frac32\eta W,
\tag{42}
\]
which proves (16). Since \(\eta W>0\), equation (15) rules out \(B=\varnothing\) under (14). All conclusions follow.
