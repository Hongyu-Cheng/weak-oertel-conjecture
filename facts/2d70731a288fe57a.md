## Statement

Use the actual integer fibers and simplex-vertex neighborhoods from the parity bridge theorem, with $d\ge2$ and $0<\delta<\min(1/2,1/d)$. In barycentric coordinates of $T$ let $H_i=\{\lambda_i\ge1/(d+1)\}$ for all $d+1$ vertices and $B_i=\sum_z|K_z\cap H_i|$. For each selected label,
$$
B_i\ge M_i+\sum_{j\ne i}L_{ij},\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^d B_i\ge M+\sum_{i<j}L_{ij},
$$
where only selected labels occur in sums involving $L_{ij}$. For $n=1,d=5$ and all six cluster masses equal to $w>0$, one has $E\ge3w$, $\max_iB_i\ge2w$, and $\max_i B_i/M\ge(1+3w/M)/6$. If $E\le8w$ this gives $\max_i B_i/M\ge17/84=\Gamma_5/2+157/108864$ with $\Gamma_5=(5/6)^5$. If all six canonical caps have mass strictly less than $\Gamma_5 M/2$, necessarily $E>(4578/533)w$.

## Proof

The canonical caps cover all of $\mathbb R^d$ since the barycentric coordinates sum to one. In $Q_i$, $\lambda_i\ge1-\delta/(d+1)$ and all other coordinates are at most $d\delta/(d+1)<1/(d+1)$. Thus each $Q_i$ belongs only to its own cap. In $R_{ij}$ both indicated coordinates are at least $1/2-\delta/(d+1)>1/(d+1)$ under the hypotheses on $d,\delta$. This follows from $(d-1)/2>\delta$, which holds for $d\ge2$ and $\delta<1/2$. Thus the pair region belongs to both caps. The parity bridge theorem applies to the same actual fibers and masses, and its regions are disjoint outside the clusters. Each selected cap pays its own cluster and every incident pair region. Summing all canonical cap masses pays the whole source once and every pair region at least one additional time. This proves both inequalities.

For six equal labels in one integer dimension, each label has a parity carrying at least $w/2$. Assign one such parity to each. If the group sizes are $s$ and $6-s$, they give $\binom s2+\binom{6-s}2\ge6$ within-group pairs. Each such pair has $L_{ij}\ge\Phi_5(w/2,w/2)=w/2$, since the defining root expression is monotone in both masses. Thus the sum of bridges is at least $3w$, giving $E\ge3w$ and $\sum_iB_i\ge M+3w$. At least one group has at least three labels, so its individual cap budgets are at least $w+2(w/2)=2w$. If $E\le8w$, then $M\le14w$ and the average cap ratio is at least $(1+3/14)/6=17/84$. If all caps were strictly below $\Gamma_5 M/2$, the sum bound would give $3w/M<3\Gamma_5-1=533/2592$. Since $M=6w+E$, this rearranges to the stated strict lower bound on $E$.
