## Statement

At integer bases $0,1,2$ take $A=[-1/2,1/2]^2$, $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(-1/2,2/3),(-1/2,-2/3)\}$, and $B=[-1/20,1/20]^2$ in their canonical hull. They are the exact integer fibers, of areas $1,1,1/100$, with $M=201/100$. Every proper closed minor $T$ has mass $V(T)<k(T)M/2$, where $k(T)$ counts occupied parities. The unique ordinary deepest mark is the center at base zero, of raw depth $1/2$, while depths at base one are at most $409/900$ and at base two at most $1/200$. The within-fiber covariance is $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(25001/241200,170009/2170800)$.

Fix $0<\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$ and allow all positive affine multipliers $\ell$ on $[0,2]$ with $\ell_0^{100}\ell_1^{100}\ell_2=1$. At $(p,y)$ the outward displacement in a nonvertical normal $u$ is $\varepsilon(\ell_p E_{K_p}(y)/M)^{1/3}\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}$ for Euclidean-unit $u$, with ordinary pure-base caps also allowed. There is an attained global minimizer of the actual raw buffered maximum over these parameters, with maximal value $1/2<q_\varepsilon<1/2+\varepsilon$ attained at the square center and at a middle mark $y_\varepsilon$. The latter's displacement is at least $41/3600$ in every Euclidean-unit continuous normal. At the stated minimizer,
$$
M(41/900)^3\varepsilon^{-3}\le \ell_1<64M\varepsilon^{-3},\qquad
1<\ell_2/\ell_1<2,\qquad
\ell_1,\ell_2=\Theta(\varepsilon^{-3}),\quad
\ell_0=\Theta(\varepsilon^{303/100}),\quad
q_\varepsilon-1/2=\Theta(\varepsilon^{201/100}),
$$
with constants uniform in the stated $\varepsilon$ range.

## Proof

The midpoint $(A+B)/2$ is $[-11/40,11/40]^2$, strictly inside $K$, since $x_1\ge-1/2$ and $|x_2|\le(4/9)(1-x_1)$ describe $K$ and at the rightmost square corners $11/40<29/90$. An anchored convex combination at base one is a mixture of this midpoint and $K$, so the three sampled fibers are exact. The proper nonempty closed minors are the singletons and two consecutive pairs, giving the strict quotas since each singleton has area at most one and both pairs have both parities and area below $M$. All centroids are zero. The three covariance matrices are respectively $\operatorname{diag}(1/12,1/12)$, $\operatorname{diag}(1/8,2/27)$ (sum of the three vertex outer products divided by $12$), and $\operatorname{diag}(1/1200,1/1200)$. The area-weighted average gives the displayed covariance and $I/16<\Sigma<I/4$.

At either endpoint base any own cap can be isolated with a finite steep base tilt. The own square depths are uniquely maximized at zero with value $1/2$, by central symmetry and a cut normal to any nonzero mark; the tiny square depth is at most $1/200$. A largest barycentric coordinate at any middle mark is at least $1/3$; its vertex-isolating triangle cap has area at most $(1-1/3)^2=4/9$. A finite tilt leaves this trace while making the large endpoint empty and the small endpoint full, giving $409/900$ as claimed.

Put $r=\ell_1/\ell_0>1/2$. Affineness and normalization give $\ell_2=(2r-1)\ell_0$ and $\ell_0=[r^{100}(2r-1)]^{-1/201}$. On $r\ge1$, $\ell_0$ decreases strictly from one to zero and $\ell_1$ increases strictly from one to infinity, by differentiating their logarithms. On $1/2<r\le1$, $\ell_0\ge1$. Put $\tau_p=\varepsilon(\ell_p/M)^{1/3}$ and let $A_\varepsilon(r)$ and $C_\varepsilon(r)$ be the raw maxima at bases zero and one. Base two is always bounded by its isolating pure-base cap of mass $1/100$. At base zero finite isolation works for buffered caps as well. The superlevel sets of its own buffered depth are convex since the through-mark threshold for each normal is convex in the mark by concavity of $E^{1/3}$ (from 5aeb5916857f97a9); they are also centrally symmetric. Each nonempty one contains zero, so $A_\varepsilon$ is its centered value. At the centroid $E_A(0)=1$ by efa4558174ce60cf. The square contains the radius-$1/2$ disk, and any Euclidean-unit section at distance at most $1/4$ from zero has length at least $1/2$, while every section has length at most $\sqrt 2<2$. When $\tau_0\le1/2$, the physical strip has width between $\tau_0/4$ and $\tau_0/2$ in every direction, so
$$
1/2+\tau_0/8\le A_\varepsilon(r)\le1/2+\tau_0.
$$
For two shifts in this range the added strip has length at least $1/2$ throughout and gains at least their difference in $\tau_0$ divided by eight. Taking infima over all unit normals preserves this strict increase in $\tau_0$ and continuity follows from the upper strip bound. Thus on $r\ge1$ this maximum is strictly decreasing to $1/2$. On $r\le1$ it is at least its value at $1$ by monotonicity, including if the small strip bound no longer applies.

At a middle mark use the ordinary vertex-isolating direction, with steep tilt as before. Since $\operatorname{diam}(K)<2$ and $\Sigma<I/4$, the added strip has area at most $\tau_1 E_K(y)^{1/3}$, so $b_\varepsilon(1,y)\le409/900+\tau_1 E_K(y)^{1/3}\le409/900+\tau_1$. The last bound uses $E_K\le1$ by testing the constant denominator. Varying $\tau_1$ changes every nonvertical cap mass at a fixed mark by at most $|\Delta\tau_1|/2$ times the sum of fiber diameters, uniformly in the normals and tilts, and pure-base masses are fixed. Thus $C_\varepsilon$ is continuous and nondecreasing in $\tau_1$, and $C_\varepsilon(1)<1/2$. The maximum at base one is attained: each fixed-normal cap mass is continuous in $y$ by energy continuity (including the boundary, as shown in 32418f8d50e792af), so the depth infimum is upper semicontinuous on compact $K$. If $\tau_1\ge4$, the centroid shift in every continuous direction is at least one, since $E_K(0)=1$ and $\Sigma>I/16$. The whole unit-disk-contained own triangle is captured, and the pure-base caps there cost at least $101/100$. Hence $C_\varepsilon\ge1$ then.

There is consequently a first finite crossing $r_\varepsilon>1$ with $A_\varepsilon=C_\varepsilon=q_\varepsilon$. Before it, the square value exceeds the crossing value; after it, the middle value is at least the crossing value. Values $r\le1$ are excluded by the square value. This gives a global affine-parameter minimizer with $1/2<q_\varepsilon<1/2+\varepsilon$. At its middle maximizer the middle strip bound and the exclusion of $\tau_1\ge4$ give $41/900\le\tau_1 E_K(y_\varepsilon)^{1/3}\le\tau_1<4$, and the mark is own-interior by positivity of its energy. Its unit-normal displacement is at least $(41/900)/4$. Cubing the $\tau_1$ bounds gives the stated bounds on $\ell_1$. Now $\ell_2/\ell_1=2-1/r_\varepsilon\in(1,2)$ and $\ell_0=\ell_1^{-1}\ell_2^{-1/100}$ give the two powers of $\ell$. The square strip bounds give $q_\varepsilon-1/2=\Theta(\tau_0)=\Theta(\varepsilon^{201/100})$.
