## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), use exactly the standard symmetric chain partition in accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409: scan a Boolean word from left to right, matching each one to the last previously unmatched zero whenever possible. Let \(D_m\) be the nonterminal words, let \(i_m(c)\) be the first unmatched-zero position of \(c\in D_m\), and retain the block notation of accepted facts 6fee4107100a027d and 4d36c889dd0a9ba2:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_m&=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\qquad F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)},\\
E_m&=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\\
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\\
T_m&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
       =E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},\qquad
J_m=F_m(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(e_h\) is the coordinate unit vector. The nontrivial reflected row centered at \(j=F_m(c)\in J_m\) has endpoints \(u_j=c\) and \(v_j=c+2e_{i_m(c)}\).

Fix \(\eta\in(0,1]\). Let \(L\ge1\), and choose independently for each \(1\le i\le L\) an integer \(m_i\ge1\), a finite real potential table \(\phi_i:T_{m_i}\to\mathbb R\), and a scale \(t_i\in(0,1]\). Require full convex-admissibility: whenever \(x\in T_{m_i}\), \(\lambda_y\ge0\), \(\sum_{y\in T_{m_i}}\lambda_y=1\), and \(x=\sum_y\lambda_y y\), one has \(\phi_i(x)\le\sum_y\lambda_y\phi_i(y)\). Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in T_{m_i}}e^{-\phi_i(x)},\qquad
p_{i,x}=\frac{e^{-\phi_i(x)}}{Z_i}\quad(x\in T_{m_i}),\\
s_{i,j}&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,u_j}p_{i,v_j}}}{p_{i,j}},
\qquad \eta t_i\le s_{i,j}\le t_i\quad(j\in J_{m_i}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Extend every \(p_i\) by zero outside its entire stated support. Empty row families are allowed, in which case the ratio condition is vacuous. All affine spatial terms are allowed as part of \(\phi_i\).

Put \(N=\sum_i m_i\) and \(P=\bigotimes_i p_i\). Its original atom probabilities and support are exactly
$$
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}
=\frac{\exp(-\sum_i\phi_i(x_i))}{\prod_i Z_i},
\qquad (x_1,\ldots,x_L)\in\prod_iT_{m_i}.
\tag{3}
$$
For any finite law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\), use the actual closed-halfspace depth and oriented reflected quantities of accepted facts ce8cccfdbee6f1cc and 4d36c889dd0a9ba2:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
  \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},\qquad
b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in \(H_p\), and zero occurs once. Every ambient containing closed halfspace is included even for lower-dimensional supports.

Using natural logarithms, define
$$
\kappa_\eta=\frac{\eta}{58320},\qquad
D_\eta=\frac{96}{\kappa_\eta^2}+\frac{2\log4}{\kappa_\eta},\qquad
c_\eta=\exp\!\left[-4D_\eta\left(2+12(\log2)^2\right)\right]>0.
\tag{5}
$$
Then every choice in (1)--(3) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_\eta4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_\eta^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$
More precisely, each block in (2), with its original law, satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{m_i}b(p_i)&\ge1+\frac{\eta t_i}{29160},\\
4^{m_i}\sum_xp_{i,x}^3&\ge e^{-4t_i^2},\\
\sum_{j\in T_{m_i}}q_{i,j}V_{i,j}
&\le24t_i\left(1+\log^2\frac1{t_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
In the last line, the auxiliary laws and scores have exactly the normalization of 4d36c889dd0a9ba2: with \(R_{i,j}=\lbrace x\in T_{m_i}:2j-x\in T_{m_i}\rbrace\),
$$
\kappa_{i,j}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_{p_i}(j)},\quad
Y_{i,j}(x)=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},\quad
V_{i,j}=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\quad
q_{i,j}=\frac{p_{i,j}H_{p_i}(j)^2}{b(p_i)}.
\tag{8}
$$
These laws are auxiliary; all actual moments in (6) use (3).

## Proof

First fix one block and suppress its index, writing \(m,t,p,Z,\phi\) for \(m_i,t_i,p_i,Z_i,\phi_i\), and put \(K=2^m\). The normalization map to 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 is \(n_\ell=m\), \(A_\ell=T_m\), \(w_{\ell,x}=e^{-\phi(x)}\), \(Z_\ell=Z\), and \(p_\ell=p\). For the atom notation in d8907f5eb4e7e5a2, its original cubic sum \(a_3\) will be written \(A_3=\sum_xp_x^3\) here; this is not the distinct canonical quantity \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\).

The support and parity description in 6fee4107100a027d, also used in the block application of 4d36c889dd0a9ba2, show that every parity class contains at most two supported points. Its distinct pairs are precisely \(\lbrace c,c+2e_{i_m(c)}\rbrace\) for \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\). Their supported midpoints \(F_m(c)\) are distinct. Different pairs have disjoint endpoint sets, since they belong to different parities. Consequently each supported reflected row is either \(\lbrace j\rbrace\), or \(\lbrace u_j,j,v_j\rbrace\) for \(j\in J_m\). A center may be an endpoint of another pair. These facts give
$$
H_p(j)=
\begin{cases}(1+2s_j)p_j,&j\in J_m,\\p_j,&j\notin J_m.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
$$
In particular \(H_p(j)\ge p_j>0\), \(b(p)\ge A_3>0\), and all laws in (8) are well-defined. Reflection preserves each row law and negates its score, so the scores have mean zero and the stated \(V_j\) are their variances. Convex-admissibility at each midpoint also gives \(s_j\le1\), consistently with (2).

We first extend the atom and sparse-price arguments of d8907f5eb4e7e5a2 to the present variable ratios, proving all required modifications. Let \(r_\alpha=p(\alpha+2\mathbb Z^m)\) for \(\alpha\in B_m\), and put \(\mathcal Q=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^3\). The \(K\) parity masses sum to one, so \(\mathcal Q\ge K^{-2}\). Exact expansion of each pair mass gives
$$
\mathcal Q
=A_3+3\sum_{j\in J_m}s_j^2p_j^2(p_{u_j}+p_{v_j})
\le A_3+3t^2\sum_{j\in J_m}p_j^2(p_{u_j}+p_{v_j}).
\tag{10}
$$
For \(a,r\ge0\), \(3a^2r\le2a^3+r^3\), since the difference equals \((r-a)^2(r+2a)\). Apply this to each summand. Distinctness of centers and disjointness of endpoint parity classes, used separately, yield
$$
\mathcal Q\le(1+2t^2)A_3+t^2\mathcal Q.
\tag{11}
$$
If \(\delta=t^2\le1/2\), then
\(K^2A_3\ge(1-\delta)/(1+2\delta)\ge e^{-4\delta}\). Indeed \(1+2\delta\le e^{2\delta}\) and \(\log(1-\delta)+2\delta\ge0\), the latter having derivative \((1-2\delta)/(1-\delta)\ge0\) on this interval. If \(\delta\ge1/2\), then \(|T_m|\le2K\), so Jensen gives \(K^2A_3\ge1/4\ge e^{-2}\ge e^{-4\delta}\). Here \(e^2>4\), for example from its exponential series. This proves the atom assertion of (7). If there are no pairs, (10) simply says \(\mathcal Q=A_3\).

For the sparse price put \(z_j=\tfrac12\log(p_{v_j}/p_{u_j})\) and \(\rho_j=\log(1/s_j)\ge0\). The exact row formulas in (8) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{u_j}&=s_jp_je^{-z_j},\qquad p_{v_j}=s_jp_je^{z_j},\\
p_{u_j}^3+p_{v_j}^3&=2s_j^3p_j^3\cosh(3z_j),\\
p_jH_p(j)^2V_j&=2s_j(1+2s_j)p_j^3z_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
Every singleton row has zero variance. For \(s\in(0,1]\), \(\rho=\log(1/s)\), and real \(z\), one has
\(z^2\le2\rho^2+4s^3\cosh(3z)\). If \(|z|\le\rho\) this is immediate. Otherwise write \(|z|=\rho+a\), \(a>0\). Then \(e^{3a}\ge a^2\), \(s^3\cosh(3z)\ge e^{3a}/2\), and \((\rho+a)^2\le2\rho^2+2a^2\), proving the claim, including \(s=1\).

Combining this inequality with (12) and \(1+2s_j\le3\) gives
$$
p_jH_p(j)^2V_j
\le12s_j\left[\rho_j^2p_j^3+p_{u_j}^3+p_{v_j}^3\right].
\tag{13}
$$
The function \(f(s)=s(1+\log^2(1/s))\) is nondecreasing on \((0,1]\), because \(f'(s)=(\log(1/s)-1)^2\ge0\). Thus \(s_j\rho_j^2\le f(t)\) and \(s_j\le t\). Sum (13), using distinct centers and disjoint endpoints separately, to obtain
$$
\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2V_j
\le12\bigl(f(t)+t\bigr)A_3
\le24f(t)b(p).
\tag{14}
$$
A center that is also an endpoint is charged once in each of the two separate sums, exactly as allowed by \(f(t)+t\). Division by \(b(p)\) proves the final assertion of (7), with no change of the original law.

We next prove the reflected gain. Define the nonnegative parity deviation and reflected deviation by
$$
\mathcal D=\frac1K\sum_{\alpha\in B_m}(Kr_\alpha-1)^2
          =K\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2-1,\qquad
\mathcal V=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2.
\tag{15}
$$
These retain the quantities of 2117de3255444894 evaluated at the present law; they do not assert that this law is log-affine. Pair expansion and (9) instead give the precise replacement for the log-affine parity identity:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(p)-\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2
&=2\sum_{j\in J_m}s_j(1-s_j)p_j^2,\\
K^2b(p)-1
&=\mathcal V+2\mathcal D
  +4K\sum_{j\in J_m}s_j(1-s_j)p_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
All terms on the last line are nonnegative.

Suppose first \(m\ge7\). Use only the geometric packing in 2117de3255444894. It provides \(\mathcal G_m\subseteq J_m\) with \(|\mathcal G_m|=K/128\) and four injective maps \(z^{(a)}:\mathcal G_m\to E_m\), \(1\le a\le4\), whose witness spikes all have Boolean sources in \(J_m\), and for which
\(y=\tfrac14\sum_{a=1}^4z^{(a)}(y)\). These statements are purely geometric. Full convex-admissibility and the original normalizer imply
$$
\phi(y)\le\frac14\sum_{a=1}^4\phi(z^{(a)}(y)),\qquad
p_y\ge\left(\prod_{a=1}^4p_{z^{(a)}(y)}\right)^{1/4}.
\tag{17}
$$
No stronger penalized probability identity is used.

Partition the centers into \(J^{\mathrm{lo}}=\lbrace j\in J_m:s_j\le7/8\rbrace\) and \(J^{\mathrm{hi}}=J_m\setminus J^{\mathrm{lo}}\). Put
\(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lo}}=K\sum_{j\in J^{\mathrm{lo}}}p_j^2\) and \(\mathcal U=\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lo}}+9\mathcal V\). We claim that every \(x\in J_m\) with \(Kp_x\ge4/9\) contributes at least \(16/(81K)\) to \(\mathcal U\). For a low-ratio center its contribution to \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lo}}\) is \(Kp_x^2\ge16/(81K)\). For a high-ratio center, (9) gives \(KH_p(x)\ge(11/4)Kp_x\ge11/9\). Consequently its contribution to \(9\mathcal V\) is at least
\(9\cdot(4/(9K))(2/9)^2=16/(81K)\). The strict high-ratio inequality only strengthens this bound.

Let \(E^{<}\) be the spikes \(z\) whose Boolean source \(c\) lies in \(J_m\) and for which \(Kp_z<4/9\). If \(Kr_c<8/9\), its source parity contributes more than \(1/(81K)\) to \(\mathcal D\). Distinct spikes have distinct source parities. Every remaining spike in \(E^{<}\) has \(r_c=p_c+p_z\), hence \(Kp_c>4/9\); the preceding center bound applies. Distinct spikes have distinct sources. These two charges yield
$$
\frac{|E^{<}|}{K}\le81\mathcal D+\frac{81}{16}\mathcal U.
\tag{18}
$$
By injectivity of each witness map, at least \(|\mathcal G_m|-4|E^{<}|\) targets have all four witness probabilities at least \(4/(9K)\). For every such target, (17) gives \(Kp_y\ge4/9\), and therefore its contribution to \(\mathcal U\) is at least \(16/(81K)\). Distinct targets can be charged separately. It follows that
$$
\mathcal U
\ge\frac{16}{81}\left(\frac1{128}-\frac{4|E^{<}|}{K}\right)
\ge\frac1{648}-64\mathcal D-4\mathcal U,
\qquad
5\mathcal U+64\mathcal D\ge\frac1{648}.
\tag{19}
$$
When the count lower bound is negative, its first inequality still follows from \(\mathcal U\ge0\). Sources and targets may overlap: (18) and (19) are separate inequalities for the same nonnegative sum, so no disjointness between these two families is asserted or needed.

On \(J^{\mathrm{lo}}\), the lower ratio hypothesis gives \(4s_j(1-s_j)\ge s_j/2\ge\eta t/2\). Write \(\Delta=K^2b(p)-1\). From (16),
$$
\Delta\ge\mathcal V+2\mathcal D+\frac{\eta t}{2}\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lo}}.
\tag{20}
$$
Since \(0<\eta t\le1\), multiplication by \(45/(\eta t)\) shows
$$
\frac{45}{\eta t}\Delta
\ge5\mathcal S_{\mathrm{lo}}+45\mathcal V+64\mathcal D
=5\mathcal U+64\mathcal D
\ge\frac1{648}.
\tag{21}
$$
For the first inequality, the three respective coefficients on its left are \(45/2\ge5\), \(45/(\eta t)\ge45\), and \(90/(\eta t)\ge64\). Thus \(K^2b(p)\ge1+\eta t/29160\) for \(m\ge7\).

For \(1\le m\le6\), the exact counts of a2c2d2b38e2fa409 give \(|T_m|=1,2,6,12,29,58\), respectively. Jensen and \(b(p)\ge A_3\) imply
\(4^mb(p)\ge4^m/|T_m|^2\ge1024/841\ge1+\eta t/29160\), since \(\eta t\le1\). This covers all small widths and empty row families independently of the chosen potential. We have proved all of (7). Finally \(\log(1+u)\ge u/2\) for \(0\le u\le1\), so
$$
\log(4^mb(p))\ge\kappa_\eta t.
\tag{22}
$$

It remains to compose actual moments without a loss depending on the number of factors. Let \(I\) be any nonempty subset of the factor indices and set \(P_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}p_i\), \(N_I=\sum_{i\in I}m_i\), and \(\nu_i=\sum_jq_{i,j}V_{i,j}\). We claim
$$
M_2(P_I)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b(p_i)\right)
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_{i\in I}\nu_i}\right).
\tag{23}
$$
Here is the variance-sensitive reflection argument, retaining the definitions (8). At a supported target \(J=(j_i)_{i\in I}\), its row and row law factor as \(R_J=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}\) and \(\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}\). The oriented reflection factorization in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc gives \(H_{P_I}(J)=\prod_iH_{p_i}(j_i)\). Set \(Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i)\) and \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}\). Independence and the mean-zero scores give \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2\), while the exact probability identity is
\((P_I)_X=H_{P_I}(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}\) on \(R_J\).

Every ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing \(J\) contains the center and at least one point of each distinct pair \(X,2J-X\). Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_I(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace(P_I)_X,(P_I)_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
The factor \(1/2\) counts each distinct pair twice and uses only half the central atom, so the first line is valid also for singleton rows. Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz give the last two inequalities. Taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves (24) for \(\tau_{P_I}(J)\). Squaring and averaging under the original law \(P_I\), and using the auxiliary product center law \(q_I=\bigotimes_iq_i\), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P_I)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b(p_i)\right)\mathbb E_{q_I}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b(p_i)\right)e^{-2\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J}
\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b(p_i)\right)
    e^{-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J^2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
The identity used in its first line is \((P_I)_JH_{P_I}(J)^2=(\prod_i b(p_i))q_I(J)\), with the same original \(Z_i\). Since \(\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\nu_i\), this proves (23), including zero variances and lower-dimensional supports.

Write \(\alpha_I=\sum_{i\in I}t_i\). Combining (7), (22), and (23), and separately using \(\tau_{P_I}(J)\ge(P_I)_J\), yields two bounds for the same original law:
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{N_I}M_2(P_I)
&\ge\frac14\exp\!\left[\kappa_\eta\alpha_I
  -2\sqrt{24\sum_{i\in I}t_i\left(1+\log^2\frac1{t_i}\right)}\right],\\
4^{N_I}M_2(P_I)
&\ge\prod_{i\in I}\left(4^{m_i}\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\right)
\ge\exp\!\left(-4\sum_{i\in I}t_i^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
Partition indices into \(I_k=\lbrace i:2^{-k-1}<t_i\le2^{-k}\rbrace\) for integers \(k\ge0\). There are finitely many nonempty bands and all indices belong to one. For a nonempty band put \(\alpha_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i\), \(N_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}m_i\), and \(A_k=1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\). Its defining inequalities imply
\(\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i(1+\log^2(1/t_i))\le A_k\alpha_k\) and \(\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i^2\le2^{-k}\alpha_k\).

If \(\alpha_k\ge D_\eta A_k\), then Young's inequality
\(2\sqrt{24A_k\alpha_k}\le\kappa_\eta\alpha_k/2+48A_k/\kappa_\eta\), followed by (26), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log\left(4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\right)
&\ge-\log4+\frac{\kappa_\eta}2\alpha_k-\frac{48}{\kappa_\eta}A_k\\
&\ge-\log4+
  \left(\frac{\kappa_\eta}2D_\eta-\frac{48}{\kappa_\eta}\right)A_k
=\log4\,(A_k-1)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
If \(\alpha_k<D_\eta A_k\), the second bound of (26) gives
\(4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\ge\exp(-4D_\eta2^{-k}A_k)\). The first case has a bound at least one, hence also at least this expression. Therefore every nonempty band satisfies
$$
4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})
\ge\exp\!\left[-4D_\eta2^{-k}
  \left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right)\right].
\tag{28}
$$

Accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc proves actual moment supermultiplicativity for arbitrary finite laws. Apply it to the finitely many band laws. Reordering coordinate blocks preserves depths because it sends ambient containing closed halfspaces bijectively to such halfspaces. Since \(\sum_kN_k=N\), (28) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^NM_2(P)
&\ge\prod_{k:I_k\ne\varnothing}4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\\
&\ge\exp\!\left[-4D_\eta\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}
  \left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right)\right]
=c_\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
The numerical sums are \(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=2\) and \(\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^22^{-k}=12\). For example, differentiate \((1-z)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}z^k\) twice on \(|z|<1\) and evaluate at \(z=1/2\). No infinite collection of probability factors is introduced.

Finally Jensen under the original \(P\) gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\ge c_\eta^{3/2}8^{-N}\). This proves (6).

All factors with \(t_i=1\) lie in the first band and satisfy the same estimates. As scales approach zero, the total losses remain bounded by the convergent numerical series in (29), with no lower bound on any scale. Empty row families and all small widths were included in (10)--(14) and the separate cardinality argument. Every average defining an actual moment retains all original atoms and the normalizers in (2)--(3). The theorem concerns the support family (1) and makes no reduction claim for other complete convex lattice supports.
