## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body with
\[
v:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)>0,
\]
and set \(C=[0,1]^n\times K\). For every Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define the mixed measure
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in A\cap C\}\right).
\]
Its total mass is \(W=2^n v\). For \(0<a\leq W\), define
\[
\mathsf M_a(C):=
\left\{
\frac1a\int_C p\,g(p)\,d\mu_C(p):
g\text{ is }\mu_C\text{-measurable},\quad
0\leq g\leq1\ \mu_C\text{-a.e.},\quad
\int_Cg\,d\mu_C=a
\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathsf M_a(C)\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0<a\leq v=\frac{W}{2^n}.
\]
Consequently, accepted fact 725defc391f9d55d, which universally supplies an integral-base barycenter for a dominated submeasure of mass \(W/2^n\), has the largest possible universal mass factor at this interface: for every \(a\in(W/2^n,W]\), the product body above has no such metronoid point.

## Proof

Write \(V:=\{0,1\}^n\). The only nonempty integer-base fibers of \(C\) are
\[
C_z=K\qquad(z\in V),
\]
and hence \(W=|V|v=2^n v\).

First suppose that
\[
y=(q,r)\in\mathsf M_a(C)\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
Choose an arbitrary representing density \(g\) from the definition of \(\mathsf M_a(C)\), and for each \(z\in V\) put
\[
m_z:=\int_K g(z,x)\,dx.
\]
These masses satisfy
\[
0\leq m_z\leq v,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in V}m_z=a.
\tag{1}
\]
Taking the first \(n\) coordinates of the barycenter identity gives
\[
q=\frac1a\sum_{z\in V}m_z z.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(q\in[0,1]^n\). Since \(q\in\mathbb Z^n\), one has \(q\in V\).

Fix a coordinate \(j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). If \(q_j=0\), then (2) and nonnegativity give
\[
0=a q_j=\sum_{\substack{z\in V\\z_j=1}}m_z,
\]
so \(m_z=0\) for every \(z\in V\) with \(z_j=1\). If \(q_j=1\), then (1) and (2) give
\[
0=a-aq_j
=\sum_{z\in V}m_z(1-z_j)
=\sum_{\substack{z\in V\\z_j=0}}m_z,
\]
so \(m_z=0\) for every \(z\in V\) with \(z_j=0\). Every \(z\in V\setminus\{q\}\) differs from \(q\) in at least one coordinate, and the corresponding conclusion shows that \(m_z=0\). Therefore all selected mass lies on the single fiber over \(q\), and
\[
a=m_q\leq v.
\tag{3}
\]
This proves the necessary inequality for an arbitrary measurable density \(g\); in particular, no fiberwise constancy or regularity of \(g\) was used.

Conversely, let \(0<a\leq v\) and fix any \(q\in V\). Define a density, up to a \(\mu_C\)-null set, by
\[
g(z,x):=
\begin{cases}
a/v,&z=q,\ x\in K,\\
0,&z\in V\setminus\{q\},\ x\in K.
\end{cases}
\]
It is measurable, satisfies \(0\leq g\leq1\), and has total mass \(a\), including at the endpoint \(a=v\). If
\[
c_K:=\frac1v\int_Kx\,dx
\]
is the centroid of \(K\), then
\[
\frac1a\int_Cp\,g(p)\,d\mu_C(p)=(q,c_K).
\]
The centroid belongs to \(K\), since it is the barycenter of a probability measure supported on the compact convex set \(K\). Hence
\[
(q,c_K)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
which proves sufficiency.

Finally, accepted fact 725defc391f9d55d gives, for every positive-mass mixed-integer convex body, a dominated submeasure of mass \(W/2^n\) whose barycenter has integral first coordinate; its fiberwise density is a valid density in the definition of \(\mathsf M_{W/2^n}(C)\). For the present product body, \(W/2^n=v\), while (3) rules out every larger mass even in the broader class of arbitrary measurable densities. Thus no universal improvement of the factor \(2^{-n}\) is possible for metronoid membership with integral base.
