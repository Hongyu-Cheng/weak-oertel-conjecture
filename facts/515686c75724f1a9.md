## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Put \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let
\[
\mu_C(B):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\}
\tag{2}
\]
for Borel \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\tag{3}
\]

Retain the canonical hard stopped-set system shared by accepted facts
d06d919de3083960 and 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b. The exact canonical alias map used here is
\[
\begin{aligned}
C&=C_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
  =C_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}},\\
Q&=Q_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
  =Q_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}},\\
A_*&=(A_\star)_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
    =(A_\star)_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}},\\
w_y&=(w_y)_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
    =(w_y)_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}},\\
W_*&=W(A_*)_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
    =(W_\star)_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}},\\
\rho&=(\rho_{A_*})_{\mathtt{d06d919de3083960}}
     =q_{\mathtt{6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The fiber centroid \(c_y:=a_y^{-1}\int_{C_y}x\,dx\) is likewise the same canonical centroid in both predecessors. Thus \(Q>0\), \(A_*\subseteq D_+\) is finite and nonempty, and
\[
w_y>0,
\qquad
W_*:=\sum_{y\in A_*}w_y,
\qquad
\rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_*)\geq1.
\tag{5}
\]
The stopped-set estimates are
\[
4w_y\leq a_y,
\qquad
w_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}
\quad(y\in A_*),
\qquad
2^{n-\rho}W_*>\frac{11}{320}Q.
\tag{6}
\]
Finally, with
\[
D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{7}
\]
the canonical hard inequality of accepted fact 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b implies
\[
D<\frac{Q}{100}.
\tag{8}
\]

For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
A_\xi:=\{y\in A_*:y\equiv\xi\pmod2\},
\qquad
B_\xi:=\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y,
\tag{9}
\]
and define
\[
\beta_\xi:=
\begin{cases}
0,&A_\xi=\varnothing,\\
\max_{y\in A_\xi}w_y,&A_\xi\ne\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\qquad
U_\xi:=(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+.
\tag{10}
\]
Then there are nonnegative symmetric weights
\[
s_{\{y,z\}}=s_{\{z,y\}}\geq0
\qquad
(y,z\in A_*,\ y\ne z,\ y\equiv z\pmod2)
\tag{11}
\]
and nonnegative unmatched weights \(\upsilon_y\) such that
\[
w_y=\upsilon_y+
\sum_{\substack{z\in A_*\\z\ne y,\ z\equiv y\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
s_{\{y,z\}}
\qquad(y\in A_*),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\sum_{y\in A_\xi}\upsilon_y=U_\xi
\qquad
(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n).
\tag{13}
\]
For an empty parity class, (9), (10), and (13) are the identity \(0=0\), and there are no vertices or edges. For a singleton class \(A_\xi=\{y\}\), one has \(B_\xi=\beta_\xi=U_\xi=w_y\), \(\upsilon_y=w_y\), and there is no loop. Thus (11) is genuinely loopless in every boundary case.

Define
\[
d_y(s):=
\sum_{\substack{z\in A_*\\z\ne y,\ z\equiv y\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
s_{\{y,z\}},
\qquad
M:=
\sum_{\substack{\{y,z\}\subseteq A_*\\y\ne z,\ y\equiv z\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
s_{\{y,z\}}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
d_y(s)\leq w_y
\qquad(y\in A_*),
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
M=\frac12\left(W_*-\sum_\xi U_\xi\right),
\qquad
\boxed{2^{n-\rho}M>\frac{51}{3200}Q.}
\tag{16}
\]
In particular \(M>0\). Let
\[
\mathcal E_s:=
\{e=\{u,v\}\subseteq A_*:u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\pmod2,\ s_e>0\}.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal E_s\), define
\[
m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
q_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}\in C_{m_e},
\qquad
T_e(x,x'):=\left(m_e,\frac{x+x'}2\right),
\tag{18}
\]
\[
\nu_e:=(T_e)_\#\left(
\frac{s_e}{a_ua_v}
\mathcal H_d|_{C_u}\otimes\mathcal H_d|_{C_v}
\right),
\qquad
\nu:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_s}\nu_e.
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
\gamma_{2d}:=\left(\frac{2d}{2d+1}\right)^{2d},
\qquad
\sigma_s:=
2\gamma_{2d}
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_s}s_e\delta_{(m_e,q_e)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Then \(|\nu|=M\), \(|\sigma_s|=2\gamma_{2d}M\), both measures are carried by \(S\), and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), including boundary, empty, and whole-space cases, satisfies
\[
2\nu(H)\leq\mu_C(H),
\qquad
\boxed{\sigma_s(H)\leq\mu_C(H).}
\tag{21}
\]

Define the midpoint base rank and midpoint-centroid residual rank by
\[
\rho_s:=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal E_s\},
\tag{22}
\]
\[
r_s:=
\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal E_s\}
\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}
\{q_e-a(m_e):e\in\mathcal E_s\}.
\tag{23}
\]
The minimum is attained, \(0\leq\rho_s\leq\rho\), and
\[
\boxed{
D
\geq
\frac{2\gamma_{2d}\,2^{n-\rho_s}M}{r_s+1}
>
\frac{51\gamma_{2d}}{1600(r_s+1)}Q.}
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently the canonical hard branch forces
\[
\boxed{r_s\geq1.}
\tag{25}
\]
Equivalently, there is no affine map \(a\) on the midpoint affine hull satisfying \(q_e=a(m_e)\) for every positive matching edge. No assertion is made about positive \(r_s\), about a further canonical matching state, or about final closure.

## Proof

Equations (5), the strict atom cap and stopped mass estimate in accepted fact d06d919de3083960, and the stopped-fiber normalization and hard inequality in accepted fact 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b give exactly (6) and (8) under the alias map (4). We first count the parity classes that can meet \(A_*\). Fix \(y_0\in A_*\), and set
\[
L:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(A_*-y_0),
\qquad
\Gamma:=L\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{26}
\]
The differences of an affine basis of \(A_*\) are \(\rho\) real-linearly independent integer vectors in \(\Gamma\), while a discrete subgroup of the \(\rho\)-dimensional space \(L\) has rank at most \(\rho\). Hence \(\Gamma\) is a free abelian group of rank exactly \(\rho\). Every \(y\in A_*\) satisfies \(y-y_0\in\Gamma\). Reduction modulo \(2\mathbb Z^n\) maps \(\Gamma\) to a quotient of \(\Gamma/2\Gamma\), so its image has cardinality at most
\[
|\Gamma/2\Gamma|=2^\rho.
\tag{27}
\]
Therefore at most \(2^\rho\) of the classes \(A_\xi\) are nonempty.

We now invoke, without reproving it, the exact finite minimum-unmatched matching assertion proved in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483, equations (23)-(26) and (63)-(66) there. It is the finite combinatorial statement for arbitrary nonnegative vertex weights. Its exact alias map in the present application is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(D_+,z,b_z,B_\xi,\beta_\xi,U_\xi,
\lambda_{z,z'},u_z)_{\mathtt{be359f8a6c628483}}
\longmapsto
(A_*,y,w_y,B_\xi,\beta_\xi,U_\xi,
s_{\{y,z\}},\upsilon_y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The construction is applied separately in every nonempty parity class. The empty and singleton interpretations stated after (13) are the exact zero-vertex and one-vertex cases of the same formula. This proves (11)-(13), and (12) immediately gives (15).

Summing (12) over all vertices counts every loopless edge twice, so
\[
W_*=\sum_\xi U_\xi+2M,
\tag{29}
\]
which is the identity in (16). For every nonempty parity class,
\[
0\leq U_\xi=(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+\leq\beta_\xi
<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n};
\tag{30}
\]
for an empty class all three terms are zero. Since there is at least one nonempty class and at most \(2^\rho\) of them, (27) and (30) give the strict bound
\[
2^{n-\rho}\sum_\xi U_\xi<\frac{Q}{400}.
\tag{31}
\]
Using (6), (29), and (31),
\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{n-\rho}M
&=\frac12\left(
2^{n-\rho}W_*
-2^{n-\rho}\sum_\xi U_\xi
\right)\\
&>\frac12\left(\frac{11}{320}-\frac1{400}\right)Q
=\frac{51}{3200}Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
This proves every assertion in (16), including \(M>0\), so the positive edge set \(\mathcal E_s\) in (17) is finite and nonempty.

We next apply accepted fact 68a56094b7617ea9. Its exact alias map keeps \(C,n,d,D_+\) unchanged and sends its symbols
\[
(\mathcal E,s_e,\deg_s(z),Q,m_e,q_e,T_e,\nu_e,\nu)_{
\mathtt{68a56094b7617ea9}}
\longmapsto
(\mathcal E_s,s_e,d_z(s),M,m_e,q_e,T_e,\nu_e,\nu).
\tag{33}
\]
Here \(d_z(s):=0\) for \(z\in D_+\setminus A_*\). In particular, the matching-mass symbol \(Q\) of that predecessor is \(M\) here and is not the canonical stopped-system mass \(Q\). For \(y\in A_*\), equations (6) and (15) give
\[
d_y(s)\leq w_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}<\frac{a_y}{2},
\tag{34}
\]
while the degree is zero at every other positive-volume fiber. Thus the source-capacity hypothesis of 68a56094b7617ea9 holds. That accepted theorem gives (18)-(19), including integrality of every \(m_e\), membership \(q_e\in C_{m_e}\), support in \(S\), \(|\nu|=M\), and
\[
2\nu(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{35}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\).

It remains to prove that the atomically supported measure in (20) is also globally dominated. Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\) and an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal E_s\) for which \((m_e,q_e)\in H\). Define
\[
\widetilde H_e:=
\left\{(x,x')\in\mathbb R^{2d}:
\left(m_e,\frac{x+x'}2\right)\in H
\right\}.
\tag{36}
\]
This is a proper closed halfspace or the whole space, and it contains the centroid \((c_u,c_v)\) of the \(2d\)-dimensional convex body \(C_u\times C_v\). Apply accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d under the exact alias map
\[
(d,K,a,m,q,\gamma_d)_{\mathtt{6769574fc1bd9f8d}}
\longmapsto
(2d,C_u\times C_v,a_ua_v,a_ua_v,(c_u,c_v),\gamma_{2d}).
\tag{37}
\]
For \(m=a_ua_v\), the admissible density is the constant function \(1\), so the displayed point is the product centroid in the full-mass metronoid. The accepted halfspace conclusion, including its whole-space case, yields
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{2d}\bigl((C_u\times C_v)\cap\widetilde H_e\bigr)
\geq\gamma_{2d}a_ua_v.
\tag{38}
\]
Using the exact pushforward normalization in (19),
\[
\nu_e(H)
=\frac{s_e}{a_ua_v}
\operatorname{vol}_{2d}\bigl((C_u\times C_v)\cap\widetilde H_e\bigr)
\geq\gamma_{2d}s_e.
\tag{39}
\]
This includes halfspaces whose boundary contains \((m_e,q_e)\). If \(H\) is empty, there is no counted edge; if it is the whole space, (38)-(39) remain valid.

Sum (39) over exactly the edges whose atom belongs to \(H\), retain the remaining nonnegative convolution components, and use (35). This gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_s(H)
&=2\gamma_{2d}
\sum_{e:(m_e,q_e)\in H}s_e\\
&\leq2\sum_{e:(m_e,q_e)\in H}\nu_e(H)
\leq2\nu(H)
\leq\mu_C(H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
Thus (21) holds for every closed ambient halfspace, with no target-fiber depth assumption and no loss from repeated midpoint atoms.

Finally apply the dominated-measure residual theorem in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 to \(\sigma_s\). The exact alias map for this second use of that predecessor is
\[
(\sigma,P_\sigma,A_\sigma,\rho_\sigma,r_\sigma)_{
\mathtt{be359f8a6c628483}}
\longmapsto
(\sigma_s,
\{(m_e,q_e):e\in\mathcal E_s\},
\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal E_s\},
\rho_s,r_s).
\tag{41}
\]
All atom weights are positive, so the finite set in (41), with repetitions removed, is exactly the support of \(\sigma_s\). The accepted theorem proves attainment in (23), including the cases \(\rho_s=0\) and \(r_s=0\), and gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{|\sigma_s|}{2^{\rho_s}(r_s+1)}
=\frac{2\gamma_{2d}M}{2^{\rho_s}(r_s+1)}.
\tag{42}
\]
Every \(m_e\) is a midpoint of two points in \(A_*\), hence belongs to \(\operatorname{aff}(A_*)\). Therefore \(0\leq\rho_s\leq\rho\). Multiplying (42) by \(2^n\), then using (32), proves the strict screen
\[
\begin{aligned}
D
&\geq
\frac{2\gamma_{2d}\,2^{n-\rho_s}M}{r_s+1}\\
&\geq
\frac{2\gamma_{2d}\,2^{n-\rho}M}{r_s+1}
>
\frac{51\gamma_{2d}}{1600(r_s+1)}Q,
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
which is (24).

Suppose \(r_s=0\). Accepted fact be359f8a6c628483, applied with continuous dimension \(2d\) in its elementary estimate for the Grünbaum constant, gives
\[
\gamma_{2d}>\frac1e.
\tag{44}
\]
Also
\[
e=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac1{k!}
<2+\sum_{k=2}^\infty\frac1{2^{k-1}}
=3,
\tag{45}
\]
where \(k!\geq2^{k-1}\) for \(k\geq2\) and at least one comparison after \(k=2\) is strict. Equations (43)-(45) imply
\[
D>
\frac{51}{1600e}Q
>
\frac{51}{4800}Q
=\frac{17}{1600}Q
>
\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{46}
\]
contradicting (8). Hence \(r_s\geq1\). Since the minimum in (23) is attained, \(r_s=0\) is equivalent to the existence of one affine map taking every \(m_e\) to \(q_e\), so the final interpretation follows.
