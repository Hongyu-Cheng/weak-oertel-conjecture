## Statement

Use the raw cubic variables of the full-cap-band barrier, with $k$ the area and $Q_i,\sigma_i$ the cap areas and cutting chord lengths divided by their continuous normal norms. The triangle
$$
T=\operatorname{conv}\{(87/100,-49/100),\ (-1/200,1/100),\ (-1/200,-23/200)\}
$$
and the through-zero caps with normals $e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2$ have $f=(7/4,1/2,1/2)$, $\Psi=4$, and cap fractions $((174/175)^2,(1/25)^2,(12/25)^2)$. The two fractions for caps 2 and 3 are below $128/279$, but the formal fixed-section unit gain in $k,Q_2,Q_3$ holding $Q_1$ fixed has $2k\beta_1=371855/768>0$. No positive pure gain of that double-sector area keeping the other sector areas and the six actual ray endpoints fixed is realizable by a planar convex body.

## Proof

The barycentrics of zero are $(1,164,10)/175$, so the mark is interior. Evaluating the three margins at the vertices gives positive own heights $a_i=(87/100,1/100,3/25)$ and the off-diagonal negative heights $-b_{ij}$ with
$$
b_{12}=b_{13}=1/200,\qquad
b_{21}=49/100,\quad b_{23}=23/200,\qquad
b_{31}=19/50,\quad b_{32}=1/200 .
$$
Thus every cap strictly isolates its own vertex. At threshold $h$ close to zero the area fraction is $(a_i-h)^2/[(a_i+b_{ij})(a_i+b_{il})]$ for the other two vertices $j,l$, by the two incident-edge intercept fractions. At zero these are the stated cap fractions. Differentiating the polynomial in $h$ gives $\sigma_i=2Q_i/a_i$, hence $\sum_i Q_i/\sigma_i=\sum_i a_i/2=1/2$ and $f_i=2a_i/\sqrt{Q_i/k}$. This yields the stated $f$ and cubic equality. The two captured caps have fractions less than the ceiling by direct comparison. Using the derivative identity of the full-cap-band barrier, which requires no ceiling to identify the derivative itself, here $P=7/16$, $C=135/16$, $D_2=D_3=15/16$, and $T_2=25$, $T_3=25/12$. Substitution gives
$$
2k\beta_1=\frac{135}{16}+
\sum_{a\in\{25,25/12\}}\left(\frac{15}{16}a^2-\frac{135}{32}a\right)
=\frac{371855}{768}>0 .
$$

The normals form a balanced spanning circuit, the mark is interior, and the vertex captures are strictly distinct. Apply 212f957189de9062 at this actual triangle and its six fixed ray endpoints. All three unique-sector reciprocal losses are zero for the pure gain, which forces every double gain to vanish. Thus the positive algebraic derivative is an obstruction to replacing the three ceilings by the two captured-cap ceilings in the raw-data derivative bound. The fixed-ray feasibility condition requires losses in the two adjacent unique corners for an actual positive double-sector gain.
