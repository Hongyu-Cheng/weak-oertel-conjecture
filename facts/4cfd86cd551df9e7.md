## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(D\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}=\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(K:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D)\). For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n\), let
\[
D_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in D\}.
\]
Assume that for some \(\mathbf z_D\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(k>1/2\),
\[
\mathbf z_D+k\mathbb B_\infty^n\subseteq K,
\qquad
\mathbb B_\infty^n:=\{\mathbf u:\|\mathbf u\|_\infty\leq 1\}.
\]
Let \(S_D:=D\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), with mixed-integer volume
\[
\mathcal H_d(S_D):=\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathcal H_d(D_{\mathbf m}).
\]
Then
\[
\left(1-\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}\mathcal H_{n+d}(D)
\leq
\mathcal H_d(S_D)
\leq
\left(1+\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}\mathcal H_{n+d}(D).
\]

## Proof

Write \(|A|_r:=\mathcal H_r(A)\) for Lebesgue volume in \(\mathbb R^r\), and set \(Q:=[-1/2,1/2]^n\). Thus \(|Q|_n=1\) and \(2kQ=k\mathbb B_\infty^n\).

First establish the regularity and concavity of the slice radii. For \(\mathbf z\in K\), define
\[
f(\mathbf z):=|D_{\mathbf z}|_d,
\qquad
g(\mathbf z):=f(\mathbf z)^{1/d}.
\]
The function \(f\) is upper semicontinuous on \(K\). Indeed, suppose \(\mathbf z_j\to\mathbf z\) in \(K\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\), compactness of \(D\) implies
\[
D_{\mathbf z_j}\subseteq D_{\mathbf z}+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j.
\]
Otherwise, after taking \(\mathbf x_j\in D_{\mathbf z_j}\setminus(D_{\mathbf z}+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d)\), compactness would give a subsequence \((\mathbf z_j,\mathbf x_j)\to(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in D\), hence \(\mathbf x\in D_{\mathbf z}\), contradicting the displayed exclusion for large \(j\). Therefore
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}f(\mathbf z_j)
\leq |D_{\mathbf z}+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d|_d.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the compact parallel sets on the right decrease to \(D_{\mathbf z}\); continuity from above of Lebesgue measure applies because the first such parallel set is bounded. Hence their volumes decrease to \(|D_{\mathbf z}|_d\), proving upper semicontinuity. Since \(t\mapsto t^{1/d}\) is continuous and increasing, \(g\) is also upper semicontinuous.

For \(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf z_1\in K\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), convexity of \(D\) gives
\[
(1-\lambda)D_{\mathbf z_0}+\lambda D_{\mathbf z_1}
\subseteq
D_{(1-\lambda)\mathbf z_0+\lambda\mathbf z_1}.
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality in \(\mathbb R^d\), including the case of compact sections of zero \(d\)-volume, now yields
\[
g((1-\lambda)\mathbf z_0+\lambda\mathbf z_1)
\geq
(1-\lambda)g(\mathbf z_0)+\lambda g(\mathbf z_1).
\]
Thus \(g\) is concave on \(K\).

Extend \(g\) by zero:
\[
\widetilde g(\mathbf z):=
\begin{cases}
g(\mathbf z),&\mathbf z\in K,\\
0,&\mathbf z\notin K.
\end{cases}
\]
This extension is upper semicontinuous. For \(s>0\), its superlevel set is the closed set \(\{\mathbf z\in K:g(\mathbf z)\geq s\}\), while for \(s\leq0\) its superlevel set is all of \(\mathbb R^n\). In particular, \(\widetilde g\) is Borel measurable. For \(s>0\), put
\[
L_s:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n:\widetilde g(\mathbf z)\geq s\}.
\]
Each \(L_s\) is compact and contained in \(K\).

For every \(a\geq0\),
\[
a^d=d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}\mathbf 1_{\{a\geq s\}}\,ds.
\]
Tonelli's theorem and the section formula for the indicator of the compact set \(D\) therefore give the continuous layer-cake identity
\[
|D|_{n+d}
=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\widetilde g(\mathbf z)^d\,d\mathbf z
=d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s|_n\,ds. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(K\) is bounded, it contains only finitely many lattice points. The same identity, now integrated against counting measure, gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S_D)
=\sum_{\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n}\widetilde g(\mathbf m)^d
=d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}\#(L_s\cap\mathbb Z^n)\,ds. \tag{2}
\]
Writing the integrals over \((0,\infty)\) is important: since \(\widetilde g=0\) off \(K\), the weak zero superlevel set would be \(L_0=\mathbb R^n\), with infinite volume and infinitely many lattice points. Excluding the single parameter value \(s=0\) changes neither (1) nor (2), including when \(d=1\). Fibers with \(g=0\) likewise contribute zero to both formulas.

We next prove the lattice sandwich used at every positive level. If \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is closed, define
\[
A+Q:=\{a+\mathbf q:a\in A,\ \mathbf q\in Q\},
\qquad
A\ominus Q:=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf u+Q\subseteq A\}.
\]
Then
\[
|A\ominus Q|_n\leq\#(A\cap\mathbb Z^n)\leq|A+Q|_n. \tag{3}
\]
For the first inequality, if \(\mathbf u\in A\ominus Q\), coordinatewise rounding gives \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap(\mathbf u+Q)\). Then \(\mathbf m\in A\), and symmetry of \(Q\) gives \(\mathbf u\in\mathbf m+Q\). Hence
\[
A\ominus Q\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathbf m\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}(\mathbf m+Q),
\]
and subadditivity with \(|Q|_n=1\) proves the lower bound in (3). For the other inequality, the sets \(\mathbf m+\operatorname{int}Q\), \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^n\), are pairwise disjoint, have volume one, and lie in \(A+Q\) whenever \(\mathbf m\in A\). This proves (3). Applying it to the compact set \(L_s\) for every \(s>0\), then using (2), gives
\[
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s\ominus Q|_n\,ds
\leq\mathcal H_d(S_D)
\leq
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s+Q|_n\,ds. \tag{4}
\]

It remains to control the two integrals in (4). Define the fiberwise centered body
\[
E:=\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\mathbf z\in K,\ \|\mathbf x\|_2\leq g(\mathbf z)\}.
\]
This is a compact convex set. Compactness follows from compactness of \(K\), boundedness and upper semicontinuity of \(g\); convexity follows from concavity of \(g\) and the triangle inequality. If \(v_d:=|\mathbb B_2^d|_d\), Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
|E|_{n+d}
=v_d\int_{\mathbb R^n}\widetilde g(\mathbf z)^d\,d\mathbf z
=v_dd\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s|_n\,ds. \tag{5}
\]

Let \(E^+:=E+(Q\times\{\mathbf0\})\) and define
\[
g_+(\mathbf z):=\max_{\mathbf q\in Q}\widetilde g(\mathbf z-\mathbf q).
\]
The maximum exists because \(\widetilde g\) is upper semicontinuous and \(Q\) is compact. For \(s>0\),
\[
\{\mathbf z:g_+(\mathbf z)\geq s\}=L_s+Q. \tag{6}
\]
The \(\mathbf z\)-fiber of \(E^+\) is a union of concentric closed balls with radii \(g(\mathbf z-\mathbf q)\) over the admissible \(\mathbf q\). If the maximum is positive, that union is the ball of radius \(g_+(\mathbf z)\). If there is no admissible \(\mathbf q\), or all admissible radii are zero, the actual fiber is respectively empty or a singleton, and in either case its \(d\)-volume is \(v_dg_+(\mathbf z)^d=0\), since \(d\geq1\). Thus (6), the layer-cake identity, and Tonelli give
\[
|E^+|_{n+d}
=v_dd\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s+Q|_n\,ds. \tag{7}
\]

Likewise, let \(E^-:=E\ominus(Q\times\{\mathbf0\})\) and define
\[
g_-(\mathbf z):=\inf_{\mathbf q\in Q}\widetilde g(\mathbf z+\mathbf q).
\]
For \(s>0\),
\[
\{\mathbf z:g_-(\mathbf z)\geq s\}=L_s\ominus Q. \tag{8}
\]
If \(\mathbf z+Q\subseteq K\), the \(\mathbf z\)-fiber of \(E^-\) is the intersection of concentric balls and hence is the ball of radius \(g_-(\mathbf z)\). If \(\mathbf z+Q\nsubseteq K\), the actual fiber is empty, whereas \(g_-(\mathbf z)=0\); again the empty fiber and the formal radius-zero ball have the same \(d\)-volume zero. Consequently,
\[
|E^-|_{n+d}
=v_dd\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s\ominus Q|_n\,ds. \tag{9}
\]
The right sides of (6) and (8) are closed for every \(s>0\), so \(g_+\) and \(g_-\) are measurable; thus all uses of layer cake above are legitimate.

Now use the projection thickness. Put \(\mathbf p:=(\mathbf z_D,\mathbf0)\) and \(\varepsilon:=1/(2k)\). Since \(\mathbf z_D+2kQ\subseteq K\), for every \(\mathbf q\in Q\) the point
\[
\mathbf a_{\mathbf q}:=(\mathbf z_D+2k\mathbf q,\mathbf0)
\]
lies in \(E\). For \(\mathbf y\in E\) and \(\mathbf q\in Q\), the point
\[
\mathbf w:=\frac1{1+\varepsilon}\mathbf y+
\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\mathbf a_{\mathbf q}
\]
belongs to \(E\), and direct calculation gives
\[
\mathbf y+(\mathbf q,\mathbf0)
=\mathbf p+(1+\varepsilon)(\mathbf w-\mathbf p).
\]
Therefore
\[
E^+\subseteq \mathbf p+(1+\varepsilon)(E-\mathbf p),
\qquad
|E^+|_{n+d}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{n+d}|E|_{n+d}. \tag{10}
\]

For erosion, \(k>1/2\) is exactly what gives \(0<\varepsilon<1\). For every \(\mathbf y\in E\) and every \(\mathbf q\in Q\),
\[
\bigl[\mathbf p+(1-\varepsilon)(\mathbf y-\mathbf p)\bigr]+(\mathbf q,\mathbf0)
=(1-\varepsilon)\mathbf y+\varepsilon\mathbf a_{\mathbf q}\in E
\]
by convexity. Hence
\[
\mathbf p+(1-\varepsilon)(E-\mathbf p)\subseteq E^-,
\qquad
|E^-|_{n+d}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{n+d}|E|_{n+d}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the restriction \(k>1/2\) is not cosmetic: it makes the erosion homothety a convex interpolation with a positive scaling factor.

Substituting (5), (7), and (9) into (10) and (11), and cancelling \(v_d>0\), yields
\[
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s+Q|_n\,ds
\leq
\left(1+\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s|_n\,ds, \tag{12}
\]
\[
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s\ominus Q|_n\,ds
\geq
\left(1-\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}
d\int_{(0,\infty)}s^{d-1}|L_s|_n\,ds. \tag{13}
\]
Finally, combine (4), (12), and (13), and use (1). This gives
\[
\left(1-\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}|D|_{n+d}
\leq\mathcal H_d(S_D)
\leq
\left(1+\frac1{2k}\right)^{n+d}|D|_{n+d},
\]
as claimed.

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, p. 362, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: if \(A,B\subset\mathbb R^d\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets, \(0<\lambda<1\), and \((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\) is measurable, then
\[
|(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B|_d^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)|A|_d^{1/d}+\lambda|B|_d^{1/d}.
\]
Here \(A=D_{\mathbf z_0}\) and \(B=D_{\mathbf z_1}\) are nonempty compact sets because \(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf z_1\in K\); their Minkowski convex combination is compact, so every hypothesis holds. The endpoint cases \(\lambda=0,1\) are identities, and the theorem permits zero-volume sections.

Tonelli theorem: T. Tao, "245B, notes 0: A quick review of measure and integration theory," Theorem 4(1), https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2009/01/01/245b-notes-0-a-quick-review-of-measure-and-integration-theory/. Exact form used: for sigma-finite measure spaces \((X,\mu)\), \((Y,\nu)\) and measurable \(h:X\times Y\to[0,\infty]\),
\[
\int_{X\times Y}h\,d(\mu\times\nu)
=\int_X\left(\int_Yh(x,y)\,d\nu(y)\right)d\mu(x)
=\int_Y\left(\int_Xh(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\right)d\nu(y).
\]
All applications above use nonnegative Borel functions on Euclidean Lebesgue spaces, or counting measure on the countable set \(\mathbb Z^n\); these spaces are sigma-finite. Compactness supplies finiteness where it is needed.
