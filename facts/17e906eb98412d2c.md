## Statement

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a compact convex body invariant under all three coordinate sign flips and under the cyclic permutation \((x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(x_2,x_3,x_1)\). Set \(C=K\times[-1,1]\), with the first three coordinates integer-base coordinates and the last coordinate continuous. Write \(N=\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^3)\) and \(W=2N\), the original mixed-integer volume of \(C\).

For \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\) and \(t\in\mathbb R\), let \(T_i(t)\) be the sum of the original continuous-fiber volumes over all integer choices of the other two base coordinates, with base coordinate \(i\) fixed to \(t\). Every allowed continuous fiber has volume \(2\), including fibers over boundary base points. Then, for every \(i\),

\[
\max\left\{
\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}(2k+1)T_i(k),
\quad 2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}T_i(t)\,dt
\right\}\ge \frac W2.
\]

The two quantities are precisely the remaining mixed masses of the proposed replacements \(P_{i,k}=(2k+1)\mathbf1_C|_{x_i=k}\) and \(Q_i=2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\mathbf1_C\,dx_i\). In particular, no permutation-symmetric or cyclically permutation-invariant rational unconditional \(K\) with these constant fibers can make all coordinate alternatives retain strictly less than half the original mixed mass.

## Proof

Use \(S_i(t)=T_i(t)/2\), the complete count of allowed integer pairs in the remaining two base coordinates at real coordinate \(t\). Cyclic invariance identifies these three functions; write their common value as \(S(t)\), and write \(S_k=S(k)\) at nonnegative integers. All counts are unnormalized counts of original base points. In particular \(N=S_0+2\sum_{k\ge1}S_k\).

First there is an exact lower bound for the integrated alternative that requires neither symmetry between coordinates nor log-concavity of a discrete marginal. For each integer pair in the coordinate-zero section, its allowed coordinate interval is \([-r,r]\) for some \(r\ge0\). This follows from convexity and the relevant sign flip. Put \(m=\lfloor r\rfloor\). This column contributes \(2m+1\) to \(N\), one to \(S_0\), one to \(S_1\) exactly when \(m\ge1\), and \(2(r-1/2)_+\) to \(2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}S(t)\,dt\). If \(m=0\) the latter contribution is nonnegative; if \(m\ge1\), it is at least \(2m-1\). Summing the finitely many columns proves

\[
2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}S(t)\,dt\ge N-S_0-S_1.
\tag{1}
\]

This calculation includes radius-zero columns and all integer boundary points. It uses the exact column lengths of the original body, rather than a discrete-marginal regularity assumption.

Let \(r\) be the maximum of the first coordinate on \(K\), and set \(M=\lfloor r\rfloor\). The sign flips allow the other two coordinates of any point attaining this maximum to be averaged to zero. Cyclic invariance therefore puts all six axis points \(\pm r e_i\) in \(K\). Consequently \(K\) contains the real cross-polytope \(\{x:\sum_i|x_i|\le M\}\), and all its integer points have coordinates in \([-M,M]\).

Suppose first that \(M\ge4\). The integer cross-polytope of radius \(M\) has total count \(N_M=1+6M+12\binom M2+8\binom M3\), and its coordinate-zero section has count \(S_{0,M}=1+4M+4\binom M2=2M^2+2M+1\). These formulas count points by the number of nonzero coordinates: choose their coordinates and signs, then positive integer absolute values with sum at most \(M\).

They give \(N_4-3S_{0,4}=6\), and
\[
(N_{M+1}-3S_{0,M+1})-(N_M-3S_{0,M})
=4M^2-4M-6>0\qquad(M\ge4).
\]
Thus \(N_M\ge3S_{0,M}\).

Now partition the integer points of \(K\) outside that cross-polytope into their cyclic-permutation and sign-flip orbits. Every such orbit has at most one zero coordinate in each absolute-value vector: every origin or axis point of \(K\cap\mathbb Z^3\) already belongs to the cross-polytope. If an orbit has total size \(w\) and its absolute-value vectors have \(a\le1\) zero entries, cyclic symmetry makes its contribution to \(S_0\) exactly \(wa/3\). Its contribution to \(N-3S_0\) is therefore \(w(1-a)\ge0\). Adding the orbits proves \(N\ge3S_0\) for the whole body.

If \(3S_1\ge N/2\), the \(k=1\) discrete alternative succeeds. Otherwise \(S_1<N/6\), and (1) gives
\[
2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}S(t)\,dt
\ge N-S_0-S_1
>N-\frac N3-\frac N6=\frac N2.
\]
This proves the assertion for \(M\ge4\).

For completeness, the remaining radii \(0\le M\le3\) admit a small exhaustive count with exact integer arithmetic. The nonnegative integer support \(D=K\cap\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3\) is a coordinatewise downset. Indeed, convexity and a coordinate sign flip allow any one nonnegative coordinate to be decreased while the others are fixed, and this can be repeated. It is cyclically invariant, lies in \(\{0,\ldots,M\}^3\), and contains all nonnegative triples of sum at most \(M\).

Represent each cyclic orbit of nonnegative triples by its lexicographically smallest member. For a representative \(p\), let \(w_p\) be the total number of signed integer points represented by that orbit. Let \(s_{p,k}\) be the number of those signed points with first coordinate \(k\ge0\). If \(a_k\) is the number of entries of \(p\) equal to \(k\), then \(w_p=|\operatorname{orb}(p)|2^{3-a_0}\), \(s_{p,0}=w_pa_0/3\), and \(s_{p,k}=w_pa_k/6\) for \(k\ge1\). These are integers, including the diagonal orbits.

Enumerating every union of the optional orbits, retaining exactly the downsets containing the mandatory cross-polytope orbits, gives the following complete table. An exceptional downset means one satisfying both \(2(2k+1)S_k<N\) for every \(0\le k\le M\), and \(2(N-S_0-S_1)<N\). By (1), every actual body on which all the alternatives fail must have such a downset.

| \(M\) | Cyclic orbit representatives | Optional representatives | Downsets checked | Exceptional downsets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| 1 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 0 |
| 2 | 11 | 7 | 14 | 0 |
| 3 | 24 | 16 | 87 | 2 |

In the radius-zero row, use \(S_1=0\). The two radius-three exceptions both have \(N=111\) and \((S_0,S_1,S_2,S_3)=(41,17,11,7)\). Their maximal nonnegative triples, listed up to cyclic permutation, are respectively
\[
\{(0,2,3),(0,3,1),(1,1,1)\},
\qquad
\{(0,1,3),(0,3,2),(1,1,1)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Here is the complete enumeration certificate. It uses only Python's standard library, examines every optional subset, calculates all original sign multiplicities with integer arithmetic, and checks the entire table and both exceptional supports.

```python
from itertools import product

def orbit(p):
    return {p, p[1:] + p[:1], p[2:] + p[:2]}

def key(p):
    return min(orbit(p))

expected = [
    (1, 0, 1, 0), (4, 2, 3, 0),
    (11, 7, 14, 0), (24, 16, 87, 2)
]
expected_maxima = {
    frozenset({(0, 2, 3), (0, 3, 1), (1, 1, 1)}),
    frozenset({(0, 1, 3), (0, 3, 2), (1, 1, 1)})
}
for M in range(4):
    grid = list(product(range(M + 1), repeat=3))
    reps = sorted({key(p) for p in grid})
    mandatory = {p for p in reps if sum(p) <= M}
    optional = [p for p in reps if p not in mandatory]
    data = {}
    for p in reps:
        w = len(orbit(p)) * 2 ** sum(a > 0 for a in p)
        s = [w * p.count(0) // 3]
        s += [w * p.count(k) // 6 for k in range(1, M + 1)]
        assert 3 * s[0] == w * p.count(0)
        assert all(6 * s[k] == w * p.count(k)
                   for k in range(1, M + 1))
        below = {key(q) for q in grid
                 if any(all(a <= b for a, b in zip(q, r))
                        for r in orbit(p))}
        data[p] = w, s, below
    checked = 0
    bad = []
    for mask in range(1 << len(optional)):
        chosen = mandatory | {
            p for j, p in enumerate(optional) if mask >> j & 1
        }
        if any(not data[p][2] <= chosen for p in chosen):
            continue
        checked += 1
        N = sum(data[p][0] for p in chosen)
        S = [sum(data[p][1][k] for p in chosen)
             for k in range(M + 1)]
        S1 = S[1] if M else 0
        if (any(2 * (2 * k + 1) * S[k] >= N
                for k in range(M + 1))
                or 2 * (N - S[0] - S1) >= N):
            continue
        full = {q for p in chosen for q in orbit(p)}
        maxima = frozenset(
            key(p) for p in full
            if not any(p != q and all(a <= b for a, b in zip(p, q))
                       for q in full)
        )
        bad.append((N, S, chosen, maxima))
    result = len(reps), len(optional), checked, len(bad)
    assert result == expected[M]
    if M == 3:
        assert {b[3] for b in bad} == expected_maxima
        for N, S, chosen, maxima in bad:
            assert N == 111 and S == [41, 17, 11, 7]
            assert key((1, 1, 2)) not in chosen
    print(M, result)
```

The certificate was executed to completion and printed the four rows in the table: \(0:(1,0,1,0)\), \(1:(4,2,3,0)\), \(2:(11,7,14,0)\), and \(3:(24,16,87,2)\). There is no floating-point calculation or solver status in this finite certificate.

Neither exception in (2) can be the support of a convex \(K\). For the first, the mandatory radius-three cross-polytope supplies \((2,0,1)\), while \((0,2,3)\) is present, so their midpoint \((1,1,2)\) must be present. Its cyclic orbit is absent. For the second, \((2,1,0)\) is mandatory and \((0,3,2)\) is present, so their midpoint \((1,2,1)\), in the same absent cyclic orbit, must be present. This excludes both exceptions exactly.

It follows that for every \(M\le3\) either some \((2k+1)S_k\ge N/2\), or \(N-S_0-S_1\ge N/2\), in which case (1) makes the integrated alternative succeed. The cases \(M\le3\) and \(M\ge4\) exhaust all compact bodies.

Finally multiply the count masses by the original continuous-fiber volume \(2\). The discrete alternative becomes \((2k+1)T_i(k)\), the integrated alternative becomes \(2\int_{1/2}^{\infty}T_i(t)\,dt\), and \(N/2\) becomes \(N=W/2\). Cyclic symmetry gives this conclusion for every coordinate. This proves the statement.
