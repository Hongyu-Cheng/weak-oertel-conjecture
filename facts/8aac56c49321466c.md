## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in I_+}a_z.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be an arbitrary affine subspace such that
\[
 D_A:=I_+\cap A\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 B:=\operatorname{aff}(D_A),
 \qquad
 q:=\dim B\le2,
 \qquad
 W_A:=\sum_{z\in D_A}a_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(B\subseteq A\), \(B\) is spanned by integer points, and
\[
 I_+\cap B=D_A.
\tag{3}
\]

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t\le\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), define
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\le f\le1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf=t
 \right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), put
\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\quad
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m
 \right\},
\tag{5}
\]
and, for positive masses \(t_z\le a_z\), put
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\tag{6}
\]

Define the intrinsic relative complete-core capacity of the slice \(A\) by
\[
 G_A^{\mathrm{intr}}:=
 \sup\left\{
 \sum_{z\in E}t_z:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \varnothing\ne E\subseteq D_A,\quad 0<t_z\le a_z,\\
 \text{there is }m\in B\cap\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E
 \text{ with }\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing,\\
 \displaystyle
 \sum_{z\in E\cap J_B}t_z\ge2^{-q}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
 \text{ for every proper relative closed halfspace }J_B\subseteq B
 \text{ containing }m
 \end{array}
 \right\},
\tag{7}
\]
where the relative-halfspace condition is vacuous when \(q=0\), and the supremum of the empty family is zero. Set
\[
 \alpha_0:=1,
 \qquad
 \alpha_1:=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \alpha_2:=\frac1{64}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{G_A^{\mathrm{intr}}\ge\alpha_qW_A.}
\tag{9}
\]
More precisely, there is one admissible tuple in (7), of total mass \(\tau\), such that
\[
 \tau\ge\alpha_qW_A.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(q=0\), one has the exact equality \(G_A^{\mathrm{intr}}=W_A\). For \(q=1,2\), (9) and (10) do not assert that the supremum in (7) is attained; they assert only that the displayed lower bound is attained by a particular admissible tuple.

Let \(G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) be the global complete metronoid-core capacity, namely the same supremum as in (7), but with \(E\subseteq I_+\), \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), and ambient balance \(2^{-n}\) against every proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\). If \(B=\mathbb R^n\), necessarily \(q=n\le2\), \(W_A=W\), and
\[
 \boxed{G\ge\tau\ge\alpha_qW.}
\tag{11}
\]
Suppose \(B\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\). There is an affine functional \(\varphi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 \varphi=0\text{ on }B,
 \qquad
 \varphi(z)\ne0\quad(z\in I_+\setminus B).
\tag{12}
\]
For \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), define
\[
 I_\sigma:=\{z\in I_+:\sigma\varphi(z)>0\},
 \qquad
 H_\sigma:=\sum_{z\in I_\sigma}a_z,
 \qquad
 H_*:=\max\{H_+,H_-\}.
\tag{13}
\]
For the actual tuple of mass \(\tau\) in (10), the exact per-side augmentation bounds are
\[
 \boxed{
 G\ge\min\{\tau+H_\sigma,\ 2^{n-q}\tau\}
 \ge
 \min\{\alpha_qW_A+H_\sigma,\ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\}
 }
 \qquad(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}).
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 G\ge
 \min\{\alpha_qW_A+H_*,\ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\}
 \ge
 \min\left\{\alpha_qW_A+\frac{W-W_A}{2},\ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\right\}.
 }
\tag{15}
\]
If \(B\) is the affine hull of an exposed face of \(\operatorname{conv}I_+\), the functional may be oriented so that \(H_*=W-W_A\), and (15) improves to
\[
 \boxed{
 G\ge
 \min\{\alpha_qW_A+W-W_A,\ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\}.
 }
\tag{16}
\]
In all cases, including \(B=\mathbb R^n\), one has the support-free uniform consequence
\[
 \boxed{
 G\ge\frac1{64}\min\{W,\ 2^{n-q}W_A\}.
 }
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, for \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), let \(h_C(p)\) be the infimum of \(\sum_z\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(p\), where \(H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}\), and put \(h_{\max}(C):=\sup_p h_C(p)\). The master implication in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}G,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{18}
\]
so every right-hand side in (11), (14)-(17) also gives a mixed-depth lower bound after multiplication by \(\gamma_d2^{-n}\). The new content is the relative and global complete-core capacity, not a replacement for stronger low-rank depth estimates already available by other mechanisms.

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes \(I_+\) finite. In particular, \(D_A\) is finite, \(W_A>0\), and all sums below are finite.

We first dispose of rank zero. If \(q=0\), then \(D_A=\{b\}\) for one \(b\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(W_A=a_b\). Take \(E=\{b\}\), \(m=b\), and \(t_b=a_b\). A density \(f\) with \(0\le f\le1\) and integral \(a_b=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_b)\) equals one almost everywhere, so \(\mathsf M_{a_b}(C_b)\) is the singleton consisting of the centroid of \(C_b\). Hence the complete core is nonempty, the relative balance condition is vacuous, and the tuple has mass \(W_A\). Conversely every tuple in (7) is supported on \(\{b\}\) and has total mass at most \(a_b\). Thus \(G_A^{\mathrm{intr}}=W_A\), proving (9)-(10) for \(q=0\).

Assume henceforth that \(q\in\{1,2\}\). Choose \(b\in D_A\) and put
\[
 \Gamma:=(B-b)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(B\) is the affine hull of integer points in \(D_A\), the group \(\Gamma\) contains \(q\) real-linearly independent vectors and has rank \(q\). It is the complete intersection lattice, not merely the possibly nonsaturated subgroup generated by a chosen set of differences. In particular,
\[
 B\cap\mathbb Z^n=b+\Gamma.
\tag{20}
\]
It is also saturated in \(\mathbb Z^n\): if \(k u\in\Gamma\) for \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) and nonzero \(k\in\mathbb Z\), then \(u\in B-b\), hence \(u\in\Gamma\). Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\) of \(\Gamma\), set
\[
 Uu:=\sum_{i=1}^qu_i g_i,
 \qquad
 \Phi(u,x):=(b+Uu,x),
\tag{21}
\]
and define
\[
 K:=\Phi^{-1}\bigl(C\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^q\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{22}
\]
The map \(u\mapsto b+Uu\) is an affine bijection from \(\mathbb R^q\) to \(B\), and (20) gives the exact lattice identity
\[
 \Phi(\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb R^d)
 =(B\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus neither a lattice-index factor nor a determinant appears, even if the original presentation of the slice used nonsaturated generators.

The set \(K\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{q+d}\). Compactness and convexity are immediate. For full dimensionality, choose affinely independent \(b_0,\ldots,b_q\in D_A\), choose an interior point in each positive-volume fiber \(C_{b_i}\), and choose \(d\) small independent vertical perturbations in the fiber above \(b_0\). Relative to the first chosen point, the \(q\) differences to the other fibers have independent base components, and the \(d\) vertical differences are independent. Hence \(C\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)\), and therefore \(K\), has dimension \(q+d\).

Let \(P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^q}K\), write \(K_u:=\{x:(u,x)\in K\}\), and put
\[
 a(u):=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_u),
 \qquad
 g(u):=a(u)^{1/d}
 \qquad(u\in P).
\tag{24}
\]
We verify the concavity needed below directly from accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. In its proof, the set
\[
 \mathcal G_K:=\{(u,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(K_u)\}
\tag{25}
\]
is proved convex before its closure is taken. Its projection onto the \(u,s\) coordinates is exactly
\[
 \{(u,s):u\in P,\ 0<s\le g(u)\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, if \(0<s^d\le a(u)\), the constant density \(f=s^d/a(u)\) on \(K_u\) produces a member of \(\mathsf M_{s^d}(K_u)\), and the reverse implication follows from \(0\le f\le1\). Convexity of (25) therefore gives
\[
 g((1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1)
 \ge(1-\lambda)g(u_0)+\lambda g(u_1)
\tag{27}
\]
when both endpoint values are positive. If, say, \(g(u_0)=0\), choose any \(x_0\in K_{u_0}\). Convexity of \(K\) gives
\[
 (1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda K_{u_1}
 \subseteq K_{(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1},
\]
whose volume is \(\lambda^d a(u_1)\); this proves (27) in the remaining case. Thus \(g:P\to[0,\infty)\) is concave.

By (23), and because \(I_+\cap B=D_A\), the fiber-volume sum is exact:
\[
 \sum_{u\in P\cap\mathbb Z^q}g(u)^d
 =\sum_{z\in D_A}a_z
 =W_A.
\tag{28}
\]
Apply accepted fact `62f2ec156b347dfe` with integer dimension \(q\), exponent \(d\), compact convex domain \(P\), and concave function \(g\). There is \(s>0\) such that, with
\[
 D_s:=\{u\in P\cap\mathbb Z^q:g(u)\ge s\},
 \qquad
 N:=|D_s|,
\tag{29}
\]
one has
\[
 s^dN\ge2^{-q-1}W_A.
\tag{30}
\]
The set \(D_s\) is nonempty. Concavity implies
\[
 \operatorname{conv}(D_s)\cap\mathbb Z^q=D_s,
\tag{31}
\]
because every point of the convex hull still has \(g\ge s\).

If \(q=1\), let \(T:=\operatorname{conv}(D_s)\) and \(E:=T\cap\mathbb Z=D_s\), so
\[
 |E|=N.
\tag{32}
\]
If \(q=2\), apply the planar lattice-simplex extraction proved in accepted fact `daef2580cdfec07b` to the compact convex lattice hull \(\operatorname{conv}(D_s)\), using (31). It gives a lattice simplex \(T\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D_s)\), of some affine dimension \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), whose vertices lie in \(D_s\), such that, with \(E:=T\cap\mathbb Z^2\),
\[
 E\subseteq D_s,
 \qquad
 |E|\ge\frac N8.
\tag{33}
\]
That accepted extraction includes the lower-rank cases \(k=0,1\), so no full-dimensionality of the selected level is assumed.

Put \(t:=s^d\). For every \(u\in E\), one has \(0<t\le a(u)\), and hence
\[
 Q_u:=\mathsf M_t(K_u)
\tag{34}
\]
is nonempty and convex. Write the vertices of \(T\) as \(v_0,\ldots,v_k\), and let \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_k\) be the barycentric coordinates on \(T\). Convexity of the set (25), now with the same \(s\) in every vertex, gives the set-valued interpolation
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^k\lambda_i(u)Q_{v_i}\subseteq Q_u
 \qquad(u\in E).
\tag{35}
\]

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the uniform counting measure on the finite set \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^q\). There is \(m\in\mathbb Z^q\) such that every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^q\) containing \(m\) contains at least \(2^{-q}|E|\) points of \(E\). Necessarily
\[
 m\in\operatorname{conv}E,
\tag{36}
\]
because otherwise strict separation would give a closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E\), contradicting the positive lower bound. After affinely identifying \(\operatorname{aff}T\) with \(\mathbb R^k\), apply the set-valued simplex-core principle in accepted fact `fbeb0a501b268d12` to (35), the finite set \(E\), and \(m\). The affine identification preserves every barycentric representation, so it yields
\[
 \varnothing\ne
 \sum_{i=0}^k\lambda_i(m)Q_{v_i}
 \subseteq
 \bigcap_{\eta\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{u\in E}\eta_uQ_u.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus the complete core is nonempty, for every barycentric representation of \(m\), not merely for a selected representation.

Map this tuple back by (21). Put
\[
 E':=b+UE\subseteq D_A,
 \qquad
 m':=b+Um\in B\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 t_z:=t\quad(z\in E').
\tag{38}
\]
The affine bijection \(u\mapsto b+Uu\) identifies \(\Lambda_m(E)\) with \(\Lambda_{m'}(E')\), and \(K_u=C_{b+Uu}\), so (37) is exactly
\[
 \mathcal K_{m'}(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing.
\tag{39}
\]
Every proper relative closed halfspace of \(B\) containing \(m'\) pulls back to a proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^q\) containing \(m\). Since all selected masses equal \(t\), the counting-depth property gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in E'\cap J_B}t_z
 \ge2^{-q}t|E'|
 =2^{-q}\sum_{z\in E'}t_z.
\tag{40}
\]
Hence (38) is an admissible tuple in (7), of actual total mass
\[
 \tau=t|E|.
\tag{41}
\]
For \(q=1\), equations (30) and (32) give
\[
 \tau=s^dN\ge\frac14W_A.
\tag{42}
\]
For \(q=2\), equations (30) and (33) give
\[
 \tau\ge\frac18s^dN\ge\frac1{64}W_A.
\tag{43}
\]
Together with the rank-zero construction, this proves (9)-(10). Zero-volume fibers never enter the selected support because \(s,t>0\). The argument uses the complete lattice (19)-(23), so nonsaturated descriptions of \(A\), \(B\), or the selected simplex create no missing lattice points or index loss.

It remains to prove the ambient assertions. If \(B=\mathbb R^n\), the balance condition (40) is already the ambient \(2^{-n}\) condition because \(q=n\). The tuple is admissible for \(G\), proving (11).

Suppose \(B\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\). Apply the complete-capacity form of the one-sided augmentation theorem in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` to the actual tuple (38), with relative balance
\[
 \beta=2^{-q}\ge2^{-n}.
\tag{44}
\]
That fact supplies (12), and for each sign \(\sigma\) gives
\[
 G\ge\min\{\tau+H_\sigma,\ 2^n\beta\tau\}
 =\min\{\tau+H_\sigma,\ 2^{n-q}\tau\}.
\tag{45}
\]
The function \(r\mapsto\min\{r+H_\sigma,2^{n-q}r\}\) is nondecreasing. Using (10) in (45) proves (14). Equation (3) and the choice (12) give the disjoint partition
\[
 H_++H_-=W-W_A,
 \qquad
 H_*\ge\frac{W-W_A}{2},
\tag{46}
\]
which proves (15). If \(B\) is the affine hull of an exposed face of \(\operatorname{conv}I_+\), the exposed-face clause of accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` puts every point of \(I_+\setminus B\) on one strict side and gives \(H_*=W-W_A\), proving (16).

For (17), note that \(\alpha_q\ge1/64\) for \(q=0,1,2\). In the proper-\(B\) case, the two entries in the last minimum of (15) satisfy
\[
 \alpha_qW_A+\frac{W-W_A}{2}\ge\frac W{64},
 \qquad
 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A\ge\frac1{64}2^{n-q}W_A.
\tag{47}
\]
Taking their minimum proves (17). If \(B=\mathbb R^n\), equation (11) gives the same conclusion. Finally, (18) is exactly the master capacity-to-depth implication included in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`. No limiting argument is used in (14): it augments the explicit tuple (38). This separately audits the possible nonattainment of either supremum in (7) or in the definition of \(G\).
