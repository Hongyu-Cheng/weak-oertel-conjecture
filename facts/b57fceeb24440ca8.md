## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) and \(R\ge0\) be integers, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the locked definitions \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(h_S\). For every \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in C\}\) and \(w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf z})\), including volume zero for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Suppose
\[
D=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf z}>0\}
=\left\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:\sum_{i=1}^n z_i\le R\right\},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf z}=g_{\sum_i z_i}\quad(\mathbf z\in D),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(g_0,\ldots,g_R\) are positive and nonincreasing, and \(g_t^2\ge g_{t-1}g_{t+1}\) for every integer \(1\le t<R\). Write \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}w_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\) and \(T=2^{-n}W\). The source index \(\mathbf z\) is the index \(\mathbf I\) of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647; \(C_{\mathbf z},D,w_{\mathbf z},W,S,h_S\) have exactly that predecessor's meanings.

Use its packing score with full occurrence capacities. Explicitly, for \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) consist of the nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) such that \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\). Write \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf z}>0\) for the unique barycentric coefficients of \(\mathbf J\) on \(E\), and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_E&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d}\right)^d},\\
P_d(\mathbf J,L)&=\max\left\{
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\\L_E\le L}}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\\L_E\le L,\ \mathbf z\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf z}\ \ (\mathbf z\in D)
\right\},\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(L\) is the nonnegative cost cutoff, and all logarithms are natural. The family includes the singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\), with cost zero. Every occurrence of a source in a positive-mass component is charged its full component mass, independently of the barycentric coefficient.

There is an integer target \(\mathbf J\in D\) for which
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_d(\mathbf J,n)&\ge\frac{W}{128(n+1)^3(7/4)^n},\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&\ge\frac{W}{128(n+1)^4(7/4)^n}
\ge 2^{-27}T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently there is one point \(\mathbf p^\star\in S\), chosen before the containing ambient halfspace, such that \(h_S(\mathbf p^\star)\ge\mathcal B_d(w)/6\ge T/(6\cdot2^{27})\). Thus the same lower bound holds for \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)\). The target and scalar scheme can be chosen from the table alone; the depth point may depend on the actual body.

If additionally \(g_t=(a+b(R-t))^d\) for some \(a>0\) and \(b\ge0\), then every barycentric root-volume cost is zero and
\[
\max_{\mathbf J\in D}P_d(\mathbf J,0)
\ge\frac{W}{128(n+1)^3(7/4)^n}
\ge 2^{-22}T.
\tag{4}
\]
No common fiber shape, affine placement of fiber centroids, or description of the real base projection is assumed.

## Proof

The support in (1) is finite and contains the origin, with \(w_{\mathbf0}=g_0>0\). Hence \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(W>0\). Each fiber used as a source is a compact full-dimensional convex set in \(\mathbb R^d\). A fiber outside \(D\) can be nonempty, but has volume zero and contributes zero to every mixed-volume halfspace sum. In particular, the support hypothesis does not require every such fiber to be empty. All outer-layer fibers in \(D\), including those with coordinate sum \(R\), have the positive volume \(g_R\).

We use two conclusions of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647, with the actual fiber objects just identified. For any positive barycentric representation of an integer target from sources in \(D\), its root mean \(V=(\sum_{\mathbf z}\lambda_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d})^d\) satisfies \(0<V\le w_{\mathbf J}\). This uses the actual convex body. Thus its cost \(\log(w_{\mathbf J}/V)\) is finite and nonnegative. Also, any finite component can be replaced at the same mass by one supported on an affinely independent subset of its own support, without increasing its cost or any source usage. Therefore a scheme with arbitrary finite positive barycentric supports, one target, and component costs at most \(L\) supplies its full total mass as a lower bound for the program \(P_d(\mathbf J,L)\) in (2).

If \(R=0\), then \(D=\{\mathbf0\}\) and \(W=g_0\). Assigning mass \(W\) to the singleton at \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\) gives \(P_d(\mathbf0,0)=\mathcal B_d(w)=W\). All packing bounds claimed for this case follow immediately. We henceforth assume \(R\ge1\).

For integers \(k,t\ge0\), put \(N_k(t)=\binom{k+t}{k}\). This counts the nonnegative integer \(k\)-tuples with coordinate sum at most \(t\): append their slack to obtain a composition of \(t\) into \(k+1\) nonnegative parts and place the \(k\) separators. In particular \(N_0(t)=1\), and the number of sources at layer \(t\) of (1) is \(\binom{n+t-1}{n-1}\). Therefore
\[
W=\sum_{t=0}^R\binom{n+t-1}{n-1}g_t.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose \(m\in\{0,\ldots,R\}\) maximizing \(N_n(m)g_m\). We prove both the threshold estimate and the weight-ratio estimate needed for its packing:
\[
W\le8(n+1)N_n(m)g_m,
\qquad
\log\frac{g_0}{g_m}\le n\quad\hbox{if }m\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]

The ratios \(g_t/g_{t-1}\), for \(1\le t\le R\), are positive and nonincreasing by the stipulated log-concavity. If \(m=0\), maximality at index \(1\) gives \(g_1/g_0\le1/(n+1)\). Ratio monotonicity then gives \(g_t\le g_0(n+1)^{-t}\) for all \(0\le t\le R\). Multiplying \(n\) geometric series yields
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+t-1}{n-1}q^t=(1-q)^{-n}
\quad(0<q<1).
\tag{7}
\]
Use (7) with \(q=1/(n+1)\) and extend the positive sum in (5). It follows that \(W\le g_0(1+1/n)^n<3g_0\), which proves the first part of (6) for \(m=0\). The inequality \((1+1/n)^n<3\) follows by bounding its binomial expansion by \(\sum_{j\ge0}1/j!<3\); for the latter bound use \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\), with strict inequality for \(j\ge3\).

Now suppose \(m\ge1\), and put \(q_-=m/(m+n)\) and \(q_+=(m+1)/(m+n+1)\), both in \((0,1)\). Maximality at \(m-1\) gives \(g_m/g_{m-1}\ge q_-\). Earlier ratios are at least this large. If \(m<R\), maximality at \(m+1\) gives \(g_{m+1}/g_m\le q_+\), and later ratios are at most this large. Consequently
\[
g_t\le
\begin{cases}
g_m q_-^{\,t-m},&0\le t\le m,\\
g_m q_+^{\,t-m},&m\le t\le R.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
When \(m=R\), the second line only asserts equality at \(t=m\), and needs no following ratio. The first line at \(t=0\) gives
\[
\log\frac{g_0}{g_m}
\le m\log\left(1+\frac nm\right)\le n,
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x\ge0\) follows by integrating \(1/(1+x)\le1\).

For the remaining part of (6), sum the first line of (8) over \(0\le t\le m\) and the second over \(m\le t\le R\). Counting the layer \(m\) twice only enlarges the sum. Extend both positive geometric sums to all \(t\ge0\) and use (7). This gives
\[
\frac{W}{N_n(m)g_m}
\le
\frac1{N_n(m)q_-^m(1-q_-)^n}
+\frac1{N_n(m)q_+^m(1-q_+)^n}.
\tag{10}
\]
Each denominator is the probability of exactly \(n\) successes in a binomial law with \(n+m\) trials. The respective success probabilities are \(p=n/(n+m)\) and \(p=n/(n+m+1)\). In both cases the mean is at most \(n\), and \(n\) is a mode. Indeed, the ratio of the probabilities at counts \(j+1\) and \(j\) equals \(((n+m-j)/(j+1))p/(1-p)\) and decreases with \(j\). For the first \(p\) this ratio is greater than one at \(j=n-1\) and less than one at \(j=n\). For the second \(p\) it equals one at \(j=n-1\) and is less than one at \(j=n\).

For either of these binomial variables \(X\), Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(X\ge2n+1)\le n/(2n+1)<1/2\). Thus the probabilities of counts \(0,\ldots,2n\) sum to at least \(1/2\). There are at most \(2n+1\) such counts, and each probability is at most the modal probability \(\Pr(X=n)\). It follows that \(\Pr(X=n)\ge1/(4n+2)\). Counts exceeding the total number of trials simply have probability zero, so this argument also covers \(m<n\). Substitution in (10) proves \(W\le(8n+4)N_n(m)g_m\le8(n+1)N_n(m)g_m\), completing (6).

We next construct one feasible scheme for a chosen truncation. Let \(0\le k\le n\) and let \(1\le s\le m\) be divisible by \(k+1\). Use the source set
\[
A_{k,s}=
\left\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:
z_{k+1}=\cdots=z_n=0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^k z_i\le s\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The empty coordinate sum is zero, so \(A_{0,s}=\{\mathbf0\}\). Every source in (11) lies in \(D\), has original layer at most \(s\le m\), and has actual capacity \(w_{\mathbf z}\ge g_m\).

Append the coordinate \(s-\sum_{i=1}^kz_i\). This bijects \(A_{k,s}\) with the nonnegative integer \((k+1)\)-tuples summing to \(s\). The appended coordinate records slack relative to this chosen \(s\), not the original radius \(R\), and introduces no extra physical lattice coordinate. Partition these tuples into orbits under cyclic permutation of their \(k+1\) entries. Put \(q=s/(k+1)\) and \(\mathbf J=(q,\ldots,q,0,\ldots,0)\), with \(k\) entries equal to \(q\). This target is an integer point in \(D\), since \(kq\le s\le R\).

For any appended tuple, averaging all \(k+1\) cyclic shifts gives the tuple with every entry equal to \(q\). Every distinct element of its orbit occurs equally often among these shifts: for the cyclic group action, the shifts producing any one orbit element form a coset of the stabilizer and thus have the same cardinality. The average of the distinct original sources of the orbit is consequently exactly \(\mathbf J\). For each orbit \(O\), use the positive coefficients \(1/|O|\) on these distinct sources and assign component mass \(x_O=g_m\).

The orbits partition the one set \(A_{k,s}\). Thus each physical source in it is charged exactly \(g_m\) in exactly one component, and every other source is charged zero. These are simultaneous full occurrence charges; no factor \(1/|O|\) discounts a source's charge. A singleton orbit is necessarily \(\{\mathbf J\}\) and uses its one allowed singleton capacity. No additional central mass is added.

For each orbit, let \(V_O=(|O|^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in O}w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d})^d\). All its weights are at least \(g_m\), so \(V_O\ge g_m\), while monotonicity of the actual profile gives \(w_{\mathbf J}\le g_0\). Because these are actual fibers, accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 also gives \(V_O\le w_{\mathbf J}\). Together with (9), this proves
\[
0\le \log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}{V_O}
\le \log\frac{g_0}{g_m}\le n.
\tag{12}
\]
The first inequality in (12) is supplied by actual convex-body interpolation; it is not inferred from realizability of an arbitrary log-concave sequence.

Each orbit has at most \(k+1\) elements, and \(|A_{k,s}|=N_k(s)\). There are therefore at least \(N_k(s)/(k+1)\) components. If an orbit support is affinely dependent, apply the support reduction from accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 to that component. The reduced support is a subset of the same orbit and has at most \(n+1\) elements, its cost is still at most \(n\), its mass is unchanged, and its full source charges only decrease. In particular, reducing all components does not introduce overlaps between different orbits. We have proved
\[
P_d(\mathbf J,n)\ge \frac{N_k(s)}{k+1}g_m.
\tag{13}
\]

It remains to choose one pair \((k,s)\) for which this count is large enough. If \(m=0\), take only the singleton at \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\), with mass \(g_0=N_n(0)g_0\) and cost zero. This case requires no \(s\) and no estimate (9).

If \(1\le m\le n\), choose \(k=m-1\) and \(s=m\). Then \(k+1=m\) divides \(s\), and (13) gives mass at least \(\binom{2m-1}{m-1}g_m/m\). Since the central coefficient is the largest of the \(2m-1\) binomial coefficients of order \(2m-2\),
\(\binom{2m-1}{m-1}\ge\binom{2m-2}{m-1}\ge4^{m-1}/(2m-1)\).
On the other hand, applying (7) with dimension \(n+1\) gives \(\sum_{t\ge0}N_n(t)4^{-t}=(4/3)^{n+1}\), whence \(N_n(m)\le4^m(4/3)^{n+1}\). Therefore
\[
\frac{N_n(m)}{\binom{2m-1}{m-1}/m}
\le4m(2m-1)(4/3)^{n+1}
\le16(n+1)^2(7/4)^n.
\tag{14}
\]
For the last inequality, use \(4m(2m-1)\le8(n+1)^2\) and \((4/3)^{n+1}\le2(7/4)^n\). These inequalities include \(m=1\): then \(k=0\), the source set is the single origin, and the binomial estimate of order zero is \(1\ge1\).

If \(m\ge n+1\), choose \(k=n\) and \(s=(n+1)\lfloor m/(n+1)\rfloor\). Then \(n+1\le s\le m\le s+n\). The product formula for binomial coefficients and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N_n(m)}{N_n(s)}
&=\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{m+i}{s+i}
\le\prod_{i=1}^n\left(1+\frac n{s+i}\right)\\
&\le\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^n\frac1{s+i}\right)^n
\le(7/4)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, since \(s\ge n+1\), monotonicity of \(1/x\) gives
\(\sum_{i=1}^n1/(s+i)\le\sum_{i=1}^n1/(n+1+i)\le\int_{n+1}^{2n+1}dx/x<\log2<3/4\).
The last inequality follows by integrating the strict upper chord bound for the convex function \(1/x\) between \(1\) and \(2\): its integral is less than \((1+1/2)/2=3/4\). Thus (13) gives mass at least \(N_n(m)g_m/((n+1)(7/4)^n)\).

These three choices, together with (14)-(15), show that the total mass \(M\) of one constructed scheme satisfies
\[
M\ge\frac{N_n(m)g_m}{16(n+1)^2(7/4)^n}
\ge\frac{W}{128(n+1)^3(7/4)^n}.
\tag{16}
\]
The second inequality is (6). In all cases every component has cost at most \(n\). Taking the cutoff \(n\) in (2) therefore gives the first inequality of (3), and division by \(n+1\) gives its first bound for \(\mathcal B_d(w)\). The argument selects one truncation and one orbit partition; it never sums schemes built at different truncations. In particular, (16) retains the already checked global capacities. For \(n=1\) its cases are exactly \(m=0\), \(m=1\) with \(k=0\), and \(m\ge2\) with \(k=1\), so no positive dimension case is omitted.

We verify the absolute constants. Repeated differentiation of the geometric series gives, for \(0<r<1\),
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^4r^j
=\frac{1+11r+11r^2+r^3}{(1-r)^5},
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^3r^j
=\frac{1+4r+r^2}{(1-r)^4}.
\tag{17}
\]
At \(r=7/8\), their values are respectively \(646080\) and \(21568\). Thus
\(128(n+1)^4(7/8)^n\le128\cdot646080=82698240<2^{27}\).
Since \((7/4)^n=2^n(7/8)^n\), the bound for \(\mathcal B_d(w)\) in (3) is at least \(2^{-27}T\), as claimed.

For the affine-root subcase, the stated formula for \(g_t\) gives \(w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d}=a+bR-b\sum_i z_i\) on \(D\). At every positive barycentric representation with target \(\mathbf J\), one has \(\sum_{\mathbf z}\lambda_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}^{1/d}=a+bR-b\sum_i J_i=w_{\mathbf J}^{1/d}\). Hence every such cost is zero, including every cost after support reduction. The same threshold and orbit proof applies because this positive nonincreasing sequence is log-concave: its positive roots are affine, and their consecutive ratios are nonincreasing (constant if \(b=0\)). Therefore (16) now bounds \(P_d(\mathbf J,0)\). The second identity in (17) gives
\(128(n+1)^3(7/8)^n\le128\cdot21568=2760704<2^{22}\),
which proves (4), including the already settled \(R=0\) case.

Finally, the all-shape depth conclusion is exactly the consumer of accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 applied to the actual body, its unchanged table, and its score \(\mathcal B_d(w)\) from (2). It supplies one point of \(S\) with depth at least \(\mathcal B_d(w)/6\), simultaneously for every containing closed ambient halfspace, including vertical halfspaces and boundary equality. Combining this with (3) gives the stated absolute constant \(1/(6\cdot2^{27})\), which is in \((0,1/e)\) because \(e<3\). No claim that a general log-concave sequence is realizable has entered this deduction. Positive \(g_0\) excludes \(W=0\) from the hypotheses of (1); for a locked instance with \(W=0\), the predecessor's convention \(\mathcal B_d(w)=0\) and any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\) give the automatic zero lower bound without any positive-fiber normalization.
