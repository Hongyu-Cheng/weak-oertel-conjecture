## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge7\), put
$$
K=2^n,\qquad m=K-1,\qquad t=m^{-1/3},\qquad
a_+=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,\qquad
B=\frac{a_+}{t},\qquad M=K^2,\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\log B}{M}.
\tag{1}
$$
The numerical parameters \(K,m,t,a_+\) agree with those of accepted fact 5a434b8dc317dfaf, and \(B=a_+/t\). All probabilities and moments here use the complete support and full normalizer defined below. Write \(e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C&=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace,
&u_c&=(M+c_1,c_2,\ldots,c_n),\\
A_{\mathrm{thin}}&=\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\cup
\lbrace u_c,-u_c:c\in\mathcal C\rbrace,
&Q&=\operatorname{conv}(A_{\mathrm{thin}}),\qquad
D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Then \(Q\) is a full-dimensional convex body and
$$
Q=\operatorname{conv}(P\cup(-P)),\qquad
P=[M,M+1]\times[0,1]^{n-1}.
\tag{3}
$$
Its exact complete lattice census is the following disjoint union:
$$
\begin{aligned}
D={}&\bigl(\lbrace M,M+1\rbrace\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n-1}\bigr)\\
&\mathbin{\dot\cup}
-\bigl(\lbrace M,M+1\rbrace\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n-1}\bigr)\\
&\mathbin{\dot\cup}
\lbrace j e_1:j\in\mathbb Z,\ -M<j<M\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Here the second line means the negative of the entire set in the first line. In particular,
$$
|D|=2M+2K-1,
\qquad
D\cap\mathbb R e_1=\lbrace j e_1:-M-1\le j\le M+1,\ j\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
\tag{5}
$$
Give every point of this complete census its original affine Gibbs weight, and use its entire original normalizer:
$$
w_x=e^{\lambda x_1},\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}e^{\lambda x_1},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\lambda x_1}}{Z}\quad(x\in D).
\tag{6}
$$
Extend \(p\) by zero off \(D\). Define actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its cubic moment by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(x)&=\inf\lbrace p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }x\rbrace,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{x\in D}p_x\tau_p(x)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The exact partition function is
$$
Z=\sum_{j=-M+1}^{M-1}e^{\lambda j}
 +\frac K2\bigl(e^{\lambda M}+e^{\lambda(M+1)}
                  +e^{-\lambda M}+e^{-\lambda(M+1)}\bigr).
\tag{8}
$$
The original probability of the integer axis satisfies
$$
\alpha:=p(D\cap\mathbb R e_1)>\frac14,
\tag{9}
$$
and the original actual depth moment obeys
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{32}>2^{-13}.
\tag{10}
$$
There is also a point \(j e_1\in D\) with \(\tau_p(j e_1)>1/8\). Consequently \(2^{3n}M_3(p)>2^{3n-13}\) for this completed family.

## Proof

Since \(m\ge127>8\), one has \(0<t<1/2\), so the square root in (1) is real and \(1/2<a_+<1\). Thus \(B>1\) and \(\lambda>0\). These are exactly the numerical parameter choices reused from accepted fact 5a434b8dc317dfaf; the support and normalization in (2) and (6) are used throughout this proof.

Every vertex of \(P\) other than \(M e_1\) is some \(u_c\) with \(c\in\mathcal C\). The remaining vertex also belongs to \(Q\), because \(u_{e_1}=(M+1)e_1\) and
$$
M e_1=\frac M{M+1}u_{e_1}+\frac1{M+1}\mathbf0.
$$
Hence \(P\subseteq Q\). Central symmetry of \(A_{\mathrm{thin}}\) gives \(-P\subseteq Q\), proving one inclusion in (3). Conversely all nonzero points of \(A_{\mathrm{thin}}\) belong to \(P\cup(-P)\), and \(\mathbf0\) belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(P\cup(-P))\) by symmetry. This proves (3). The set on its right is compact and convex and contains the full-dimensional box \(P\), so \(Q\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

For the census, (3) first gives
$$
|x_1|\le M+1,\qquad -1\le x_i\le1\quad(2\le i\le n)
\quad(x\in Q).
\tag{11}
$$
If \(x_i=1\) for some \(i\ge2\), any convex representation of \(x\) by points of \(P\cup(-P)\) must use only points with that coordinate equal to one. Such points lie in \(P\), because the corresponding coordinate of every point of \(-P\) is nonpositive. Therefore
$$
x_i=1\Longrightarrow M\le x_1\le M+1.
\tag{12}
$$
The same argument with signs reversed gives
$$
x_i=-1\Longrightarrow -M-1\le x_1\le-M.
\tag{13}
$$
For an integer point with \(-M<x_1<M\), equations (11)--(13) force all coordinates other than the first to vanish. At first coordinate \(M\) or \(M+1\), they force those other coordinates to be in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\); at first coordinate \(-M\) or \(-M-1\), they force them to be in \(\lbrace-1,0\rbrace\). These are all possible integer first coordinates by (11). Conversely every point in the first two lines of (4) belongs to \(P\cup(-P)\), and the entire segment
\([-(M+1)e_1,(M+1)e_1]\) belongs to \(Q\). This proves (4) and (5), including the four boundary axis points without duplicating them in the interior-axis set. Each of the four caps has \(2^{n-1}=K/2\) points and the interior axis has \(2M-1\), proving the cardinality and (8).

We now bound the mass of the axis while retaining every off-axis term of the original normalizer. Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{axis}}&=\sum_{j=-M-1}^{M+1}e^{\lambda j},\\
Z_{\mathrm{off}}&=\left(\frac K2-1\right)
\bigl(e^{\lambda M}+e^{\lambda(M+1)}
      +e^{-\lambda M}+e^{-\lambda(M+1)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
Equation (4) gives the exact identities
\(Z=Z_{\mathrm{axis}}+Z_{\mathrm{off}}\) and
\(\alpha=Z_{\mathrm{axis}}/Z\). There are \(2K-4\) off-axis points. Writing \(E=e^{\lambda(M+1)}\), all their weights are at most \(E\), whence
$$
Z_{\mathrm{off}}\le(2K-4)E<2KE.
\tag{15}
$$
The choice \(M=K^2\) and \(a_+<1\) gives
$$
\lambda K=\frac{\log B}{K}
<\frac{\log m}{3K}<\frac13.
\tag{16}
$$
For the final inequality, \(\log m=\int_1^m s^{-1}\,ds<m-1<K\). Also \(e^{1/3}<3/2\), for instance because
\(\log(3/2)=\int_1^{3/2}s^{-1}\,ds>1/3\).
The last \(K+1\) positive-axis points all lie in (5), since \(M+1-K>0\), and their total weight is at least
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{axis}}
&\ge\sum_{r=0}^K e^{\lambda(M+1-r)}\\
&\ge(K+1)E e^{-\lambda K}
>\frac23(K+1)E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
Combining (15) and (17) yields
$$
\frac{Z_{\mathrm{off}}}{Z_{\mathrm{axis}}}
<\frac{3K}{K+1}<3,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac1{1+Z_{\mathrm{off}}/Z_{\mathrm{axis}}}>\frac14.
\tag{18}
$$
Thus (9) concerns the full law (6), not a renormalized replacement for it.

We next prove the required inclusive one-dimensional moment inequality directly. Let \(r_1,\ldots,r_L>0\) be any finite probability law on increasing points of a line. Put
$$
F_0=0,\qquad F_j=\sum_{h=1}^j r_h,\qquad
\delta_j=\min\lbrace F_j,1-F_{j-1}\rbrace.
$$
The quantity \(\delta_j\) is its closed-halfspace depth on that line: the two rays with endpoint at the point have masses \(F_j\) and \(1-F_{j-1}\), and every ray containing it contains one of these inclusive tails. For every \(u\in[F_{j-1},F_j]\),
$$
F_j\ge u,\qquad 1-F_{j-1}\ge1-u,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\delta_j\ge\min\lbrace u,1-u\rbrace.
$$
Integrating over these intervals, which partition \([0,1]\) up to endpoints, proves
$$
\sum_{j=1}^L r_j\delta_j^3
\ge\int_0^1\min\lbrace u,1-u\rbrace^3\,du
=2\int_0^{1/2}u^3\,du=\frac1{32}.
\tag{19}
$$

Apply (19) to the conditional axis law, ordered by its first coordinate:
$$
r_j=\frac{e^{\lambda j}}{Z_{\mathrm{axis}}}
\quad(-M-1\le j\le M+1).
\tag{20}
$$
Use \(\delta_j\) for its inclusive line depth. For any ambient closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j e_1\), its intersection with the axis is a closed ray containing \(j e_1\), or the entire axis. Indeed, restricting a defining affine inequality for \(H\) to the axis gives a nonconstant linear inequality in the first coordinate, or a constant inequality; in the constant case containment of \(j e_1\) makes the whole axis belong to \(H\). It follows that the conditional axis mass in \(H\) is at least \(\delta_j\). Its original mass in \(H\) is therefore at least \(\alpha\delta_j\), and consequently
$$
\tau_p(j e_1)\ge\alpha\delta_j.
\tag{21}
$$
Retaining the original target-point probabilities \(p_{j e_1}=\alpha r_j\) as well, equations (19)--(21) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{j=-M-1}^{M+1}p_{j e_1}\tau_p(j e_1)^3\\
&\ge\alpha^4\sum_{j=-M-1}^{M+1}r_j\delta_j^3
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{32}>\frac{(1/4)^4}{32}=2^{-13}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
The fourth power of \(\alpha\) accounts for the target-point probability and all three depth factors.

Finally, choose the first axis point whose cumulative conditional probability is at least \(1/2\). Its inclusive left tail is at least \(1/2\), and its inclusive right tail is greater than \(1/2\). Hence \(\delta_j\ge1/2\), and (21) gives \(\tau_p(j e_1)\ge\alpha/2>1/8\). Equations (18) and (22) quantify the recovery caused by convex completion using the original global affine field and its full partition function. In particular \(2^{3n}M_3(p)>2^{3n-13}\); these conclusions concern precisely the family (1)--(6).
