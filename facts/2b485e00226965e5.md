## Statement

For integers $D\ge m\ge1$, let $\mu$ be a weak probability limit in $\mathbb R^D$ of normalized intrinsic $m$-volume laws of compact $m$-dimensional convex bodies, with possibly varying affine hulls. Write $q=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$. The limit is $1/m$-concave and $q\le m$. For $q=0$ it is a Dirac mass; for $q=m$ it is the uniform probability on a compact convex $m$-body. If $0<q<m$, its intrinsic $q$-density $f$ has compact convex support and $f^{1/(m-q)}$ has a bounded upper-semicontinuous concave representative on that support. Conversely all laws in these three classes occur. For $0<q<m$ the centered $(m-q)$-ball lift projects ordinary $m$-volume exactly to $f\,d\mathcal H^q$, even for finite positive mass, and the exponent $1/(m-q)$ is sharp. For $q=m$ the identity lift uses normalized volume; a volume-one lift with ordinary-volume pushforward is possible by adding a prism direction.

## Proof

For compact positive-mass input sets $A,B$, ordinary Brunn–Minkowski in the affine $m$-plane of each prelimit body gives $1/m$-concavity of its probability: intersect each input with the body before forming their Minkowski average. The common normalizing volume cancels. For $0<t<1$ put $S=(1-t)A+tB$. Apply the inequality to open $\varepsilon$-neighborhoods of the inputs, using compact inner approximation. Their average is in the closed $\varepsilon$-neighborhood $S_\varepsilon$ of $S$. Lower Portmanteau on the two inputs and upper Portmanteau on $S_\varepsilon$ give
$$
\mu(S_\varepsilon)^{1/m}\ge(1-t)\mu(A)^{1/m}+t\mu(B)^{1/m}.
$$
The input masses are positive eventually. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the assertion for $S$. This argument does not require fixed prelimit affine hulls. Positive-mass compact neighborhoods of support points also show convexity of the support.

Use Borell's affine-support characterization, as stated in [Fradelizi and Guédon, §2, p. 329, theorem citing [3]](https://perso.math.u-pem.fr/fradelizi.matthieu/pdf/localizationDCG.pdf): for affine support dimension $q>0$ the parameter $s$ of an $s$-concave probability cannot exceed $1/q$, and its density has concavity exponent $s/(1-qs)$, with the endpoint $s=1/q$ giving constant positive density on the convex support. Taking $s=1/m$ gives the claimed exponent or constant case. To see boundedness of the support and of the root $h=f^{1/(m-q)}$ when $q<m$, take a fixed ball in the relative interior where $h$ has a positive lower bound. Its midpoint balls with a sequence of escaping positive points would have a disjoint subsequence with uniformly positive mass. A sequence of points with unbounded $h$ would give midpoint balls of the same fixed radius and arbitrarily large mass, since $h$ there is at least half the point value. Both contradict integrability. The upper-semicontinuous closure of the hypograph gives the desired bounded concave boundary extension, without changing the density almost everywhere. For a constant positive density the same disjoint-ball argument gives boundedness of the support.

Put $r=m-q$ and let $\kappa_r$ be the volume of the unit $r$-ball. The set of $(x,y)$ over the support with $\lVert y\rVert\le(f(x)/\kappa_r)^{1/r}$ is a full-dimensional compact convex body in the $m$-plane: the radius is concave, upper semicontinuous, and positive in the support interior. Fubini gives its exact projected volume and total volume $\int f$. For probability $f$, contract the last $r$ coordinates by $\varepsilon$; the resulting volume is $\varepsilon^r$, and normalization leaves the projected probability unchanged. Uniform continuity of test functions on the common compact container gives weak convergence to the prescribed law as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Embed the hidden space in any $r$-plane orthogonal to the affine support. A shrinking intrinsic $m$-ball realizes the Dirac case, and a constant sequence of the given body realizes the uniform $q=m$ case. When $q=m$, the ordinary-volume pushforward by identity is already a probability precisely if the body's volume is one; the prism of height reciprocal to that volume gives a volume-one lift in dimension $m+1$.

On the standard $q$-simplex, take $f$ proportional to $(1-\sum_i x_i)^r$. Its $1/r$ power is affine, but each higher power of $f$ is strictly convex along an interior line on which the sum varies. This example occurs by the same thin lift and gives sharpness.
