## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), with halfspace depth and mixed-integer volume exactly as in the locked problem. For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define

\[
C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
D_+=\{z:w_z>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z.
\]

Assume \(W>0\). For each \(z\in D_+\), use its true volume centroid and its original probability:

\[
b_z=\frac1{w_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
p_z=\frac{w_z}{W},\qquad
A=\operatorname{aff}\{b_z:z\in D_+\},\qquad
r=\dim A,\qquad
\gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

For every such \(C\), there is one
\(\mathbf y^\star=(z^\star,m^\star)\in\operatorname{conv}\{(z,b_z):z\in D_+\}\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times A)\subseteq S\)
such that

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge
\frac{\gamma_d}{2^n(r+1)}W.
\tag{1}
\]

If additionally \(C\) is invariant under every coordinate sign flip in its first \(n\) coordinates, there is one
\(m\in\operatorname{conv}\{b_z:z\in D_+\}\subseteq C_{0_n}\)
such that the actual point \((0_n,m)\in S\) satisfies

\[
h_S((0_n,m))\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{2(r+1)}W.
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\), and each selected point is fixed independently of all subsequently tested halfspaces. If the extra symmetry holds and \(r\le n\), (2) implies the locked bound with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/(2e)\). More generally, for any fixed nonnegative integer \(R_0\), (1) implies the locked bound with \(c_0=1/[2e(R_0+1)]\) on the class of bodies whose actual centroid rank satisfies \(r\le R_0\).

The symbols \(C_z,w_z,D_+,W,p_z\) retain their meanings from accepted fact \(\texttt{fab9dbd4ee2b61d6}\). The new symbol \(b_z\) is the centroid just defined; it does not rename that fact's center \(c_z\) for asymmetric fibers. When all positive fibers are centrally symmetric about \(c_z\), one has \(b_z=c_z\), the two definitions of \(r\) agree, and the sharper bound \(W/[4(r+1)]\) from \(\texttt{fab9dbd4ee2b61d6}\) remains available under its base symmetry hypothesis.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes the set of integer bases with nonempty fibers finite. Each positive fiber is compact and convex with positive \(d\)-dimensional volume, so it is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\). Thus \(D_+\) is finite and nonempty, the integrals defining \(b_z\) exist, and all probabilities \(p_z\) are well-defined. Fibers of volume zero contribute zero to the locked mixed-volume sum, so \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\).

Every \(b_z\) belongs to \(C_z\). To check this directly, if \(b_z\notin C_z\), choose a nearest point \(k\in C_z\). Minimizing distance along the segment from \(k\) to any \(y\in C_z\) gives \((b_z-k)\cdot(y-k)\le0\). Integrating this inequality with the uniform probability on \(C_z\) gives \(\|b_z-k\|_2^2\le0\), a contradiction. In particular, every \((z,b_z)\) belongs to \(C\).

Let \(Z\) have exactly the law \(\mathbb P(Z=z)=p_z\), and, conditional on \(Z=z\), let \(Y\) be uniform on the actual fiber \(C_z\). For any measurable ambient set \(F\),

\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in F)
=\sum_{z\in D_+}\frac{w_z}{W}
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in F\})}{w_z}
=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)}W.
\tag{3}
\]

Grünbaum's centroid halfspace inequality, in the exact form checked below, gives for every \(z\in D_+\) and every nonzero \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\)
\(\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge0\mid Z=z)\ge\gamma_d\).
For \(\beta=0\), the conditional probability is one, so the same inequality still holds. Hence, for all \(\beta\),

\[
\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge0\mid Z=z)\ge\gamma_d.
\tag{4}
\]

We give the atomic centerpoint construction with its point-location constraint. Let \(\mathcal E\) be a finite-dimensional affine space, let \(T\subseteq\mathcal E\) be closed, and suppose that intersections with \(T\) of convex subsets of \(\mathcal E\) have finite Helly number at most the positive integer \(h\). Let \(\eta\) be a probability with finite nonempty support in \(T\), and put \(P_\eta=\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\eta)\). Consider \(P_\eta\) together with all closed affine halfspaces \(J\subseteq\mathcal E\) satisfying \(\eta(J)>1-1/h\).

Any subfamily of at most \(h\) of these sets has a common support point. Indeed, \(\eta(P_\eta)=1\), and the union of the complements of its halfspace members has mass strictly less than one, because each complement has mass strictly less than \(1/h\). The uncovered positive mass contains a support point, which belongs to \(T\). The Helly hypothesis now shows that every finite subfamily has a common point in \(T\). Since \(P_\eta\cap T\) is nonempty and compact, the finite intersection property for closed subsets of that compact set yields a point \(q\in P_\eta\cap T\) belonging to every halfspace in the family.

This point has \(\eta(J)\ge1/h\) for every closed affine halfspace \(J\) containing it. Otherwise write \(J=\{v:\ell(v)\ge t\}\) and suppose \(\eta(J)<1/h\). Its complement has mass greater than \(1-1/h\). Because the support is finite, there is \(\delta>0\) such that all support points with \(\ell<t\) have \(\ell\le t-\delta\). The halfspace \(\{\ell\le t-\delta\}\) then belongs to the defining family and excludes \(q\), contradicting its construction. A functional constant on \(\mathcal E\) cannot cause a counterexample: a containing halfspace of this type contains all of \(\mathcal E\), and has mass one. This construction therefore covers atoms and affine restrictions without an absolute-continuity assumption.

We first prove (1). Define the joint centroid probability \(\nu_{zb}=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z\delta_{(z,b_z)}\), regarded as a probability on \(\mathcal E=\mathbb R^n\times A\), and set \(T=\mathbb Z^n\times A\). Choose an affine isomorphism \(\iota:\mathbb R^r\to A\). The affine bijection \((x,u)\mapsto(x,\iota(u))\) identifies \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^r\) with \(T\), preserves convex sets and their intersections, and leaves all integer coordinates unchanged. The mixed-integer Helly theorem checked below consequently gives Helly number \(h=2^n(r+1)\) for \(T\) in \(\mathcal E\), including \(r=0\).

Applying the construction gives
\(\mathbf y^\star=(z^\star,m^\star)\in Q\cap T\), where
\(Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(z,b_z):z\in D_+\}\),
and every containing closed halfspace in \(\mathcal E\) has \(\nu_{zb}\)-mass at least \(1/[2^n(r+1)]\). This is an actual affine convex-hull lift: all vertices of \(Q\) belong to \(C\), so convexity gives \(Q\subseteq C\), while \(z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\) gives \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\). No positivity assertion about the chosen fiber \(C_{z^\star}\) is needed for this membership.

Fix this point. For arbitrary \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), its joint centroid halfspace has probability

\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\
\alpha\cdot(z-z^\star)+\beta\cdot(b_z-m^\star)\ge0}}p_z
\ge\frac1{2^n(r+1)}.
\tag{5}
\]

The ambient affine functional restricts either to a nonconstant functional on \(\mathcal E\), giving a containing intrinsic halfspace, or to a constant functional there. In the latter case the displayed condition holds at every support point, so its probability is one. Thus (5) includes all restrictions.

For each summand selected in (5), the event \(\beta\cdot(Y-b_z)\ge0\) implies
\(\alpha\cdot(Z-z^\star)+\beta\cdot(Y-m^\star)\ge0\).
Averaging (4) over exactly those bases, with the original probabilities, proves

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\alpha\cdot(Z-z^\star)+\beta\cdot(Y-m^\star)\ge0\bigr)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{2^n(r+1)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) contains its parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf y^\star\). The latter has the form in (6), for a nonzero pair \((\alpha,\beta)\). Equation (3) therefore proves (1) for every containing halfspace; the whole-space case has probability one.

Now assume base coordinate sign invariance and prove (2). It gives \(C_{-z}=C_z\), \(w_{-z}=w_z\), and \(b_{-z}=b_z\). Since \((z,b_z)\) and \((-z,b_z)\) belong to \(C\), their midpoint \((0_n,b_z)\) belongs to \(C\). Hence
\(P=\operatorname{conv}\{b_z:z\in D_+\}\subseteq C_{0_n}\).

Apply the atomic construction to \(\nu_b=\sum_{z\in D_+}p_z\delta_{b_z}\) in \(\mathcal E=A\), with \(T=A\) and \(h=r+1\). For \(r\ge1\), this Helly number is the continuous special case of the theorem checked below after an affine identification with \(\mathbb R^r\). For \(r=0\), \(A\) is a singleton, and taking that point directly has depth one. We obtain one \(m\in P\) for which all containing intrinsic halfspaces have \(\nu_b\)-mass at least \(1/(r+1)\). The inclusion \(P\subseteq C_{0_n}\) makes \((0_n,m)\) legal before any halfspace is chosen.

For every \(\beta\), the condition \(\beta\cdot(b_z-m)\ge0\) has total probability at least \(1/(r+1)\). If the restricted functional is constant on \(A\), this condition holds at every centroid, so the same claim holds with probability one. On each selected fiber, (4) implies a conditional probability at least \(\gamma_d\) of \(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\). Consequently,

\[
\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{r+1}.
\tag{7}
\]

The original joint law of \((Z,Y)\) is invariant under \((Z,Y)\mapsto(-Z,Y)\), because both the base probabilities and the actual conditional fibers agree. Pointwise, for any \(\alpha,\beta\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf 1_{\{\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{-\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}\\
&\hspace{15mm}\ge\mathbf 1_{\{\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking expectations, invariance makes the two terms on the left equal. Equation (7) thus gives
\(\mathbb P(\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0)\ge\gamma_d/[2(r+1)]\).
The same parallel-halfspace argument as for (6), together with (3), proves (2). This includes \(\alpha=0\), \(\beta=0\), and normals orthogonal to the centroid affine space.

Finally, \(\log(1+t)<t\) for \(t>0\) implies
\(\log\gamma_d=-d\log(1+1/d)>-1\), so \(\gamma_d>1/e\).
For \(n\ge1\), induction gives \(n+1\le2^n\). Therefore, under the additional condition \(r\le n\),

\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{2(r+1)}
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{2(n+1)}
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{2\,2^n}
>\frac1{2e}\,2^{-n}.
\]

The constant \(1/(2e)\) lies in \((0,1/e)\). Likewise, if \(r\le R_0\), then
\(\gamma_d/[2^n(r+1)]>2^{-n}/[e(R_0+1)]\), which implies the stated bound with \(c_0=1/[2e(R_0+1)]\). Both implications retain an explicit rank hypothesis; no rank reduction for unrestricted \(C\) is used.

For the comparison with \(\texttt{fab9dbd4ee2b61d6}\), uniform volume in a centrally symmetric fiber about \(c_z\) is invariant under \(y\mapsto2c_z-y\). Its expectation therefore satisfies \(b_z=2c_z-b_z\), and hence \(b_z=c_z\). All actual-volume and affine-rank normalizations then agree exactly with that accepted theorem, which supplies its stated stronger symmetric-fiber bound.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257–1261, [primary publisher PDF](https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf). Section 3, printed page 1259, proves the stronger centroid form of Theorem 2: for a convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\), its volume centroid \(g_K\) satisfies \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap H)\ge(d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) for every closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(g_K\). Each actual \(C_z\) with \(z\in D_+\) is such a body, and \(b_z=g_{C_z}\). The cap through \(b_z\) with normal \(\beta\ne0\) is such a halfspace, giving (4).

Gennadiy Averkov and Robert Weismantel, “Transversal Numbers over Subsets of Linear Spaces,” [authors' primary arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0948), Theorem 1.1, equation (3), together with condition (H) in the introduction: for nonnegative integers \(a,b\), \(h(\mathbb R^a\times\mathbb Z^b)=(a+1)2^b\). Here \(h\) is the Helly number for intersections of that space with convex subsets of the ambient Euclidean space. Take \((a,b)=(r,n)\), permute the coordinate blocks, and apply the explicitly specified affine isomorphism in the continuous block to obtain the theorem on \(\mathbb Z^n\times A\). For the centroid-only construction with \(r\ge1\), take \((a,b)=(r,0)\). The compact-hull argument above supplies the passage from finite Helly to the infinite defining family.
