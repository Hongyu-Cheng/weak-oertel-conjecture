## Statement

For a finite nonempty support \(B\subset\mathbb Z^n\), let \(p_B\) be its original uniform probability law, extended by zero off \(B\). For an integer vector \(v\), write \(\operatorname{supp}(v)=\lbrace\ell:v_\ell\ne0\rbrace\). For \(\lambda>0\), define the new coordinate-damped quantities by
$$
T_{B,j}(\lambda)=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathbb Z^n\\j+v,j-v\in B}}
\lambda^{|\operatorname{supp}(v)|},\qquad
b_\lambda(p_B)=\sum_{j\in B}p_{B,j}^3T_{B,j}(\lambda)^2.
$$
The sum is oriented: \(v=0\) occurs once and both nonzero orientations occur. These are new damped quantities. In particular \(b_\lambda\) is not the canonical undamped \(b\) in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705.

Let \(\mathbf e=(1,1,1,1)\), \(A=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^4\cup\lbrace2\mathbf e\rbrace\), \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(A)\), and \(p=p_A\). Thus \(|A|=17\). For each integer \(k\ge1\), put \(A^{(k)}=A^k\), \(Q^{(k)}=Q^k\), and \(P_k=p^{\otimes k}=p_{A^{(k)}}\), in ambient dimension \(n=4k\). These are full-dimensional complete convex supports: \(A^{(k)}=Q^{(k)}\cap\mathbb Z^{4k}\). Each law is affine with weights identically one and is midpoint-log-concave.

At \(\lambda=3/4\), the exact values are
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{A,\mathbf e}(3/4)&=\frac{209}{128},\qquad
T_{A,j}(3/4)=1\quad(j\in A\setminus\lbrace\mathbf e\rbrace),\\
4^4b_{3/4}(p)&=\frac{305825}{314432}<1,\\
4^{4k}b_{3/4}(P_k)&=\left(\frac{305825}{314432}\right)^k\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently there is no positive absolute constant \(c\) such that \(b_{3/4}(p_B)\ge c4^{-n}\) for every such complete convex uniform affine law. There are also no constants \(c>0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) giving the stronger positive exponential surplus \(4^nb_{3/4}(p_B)\ge c\exp(\varepsilon n)\) on that class.

For the actual moment notation use exactly accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705: \(\tau_{P_k}(J)\) is the infimum of \(P_k(\mathcal H)\) over all ambient closed halfspaces \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^{4k}\) containing \(J\), and \(M_a(P_k)=\sum_J(P_k)_J\tau_{P_k}(J)^a\) for \(a=2,3\). Its canonical undamped mass is \(H_p(j)=\sum_v\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}\), with \(b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2\). The exact predecessor map is \(L=k\), \(n_i=4\), \(A_i=A\), \(w_{i,x}=1\), \(Z_i=17\), \(p_i=p\), \(P=P_k\), and \(N=4k\); the number \(17\) counts atoms, not dimensions. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
b(p)&=\frac{25}{4913},\\
4^{4k}M_2(P_k)&\ge\frac14\left(\frac{6400}{4913}\right)^k\ge\frac14,\\
8^{4k}M_3(P_k)&\ge\frac18\left(\frac{6400}{4913}\right)^{3k/2}\ge\frac18.
\end{aligned}
$$
Finally, define the unmasked directed-difference graph census associated with a loopless directed edge set \(E\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace^2\) by
\(A_E=\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^n:x_u-x_v\le1\text{ for all }(u,v)\in E\rbrace\).
None of the sets \(A^{(k)}\) is such an \(A_E\) in its displayed coordinates, regardless of degrees. Thus this counterexample does not establish a failure within that graph family.

## Proof

Every coordinate of every point of \(Q\) lies in \([0,2]\). If an integer point \(x\in Q\) has a coordinate equal to \(2\), write \(x\) as a convex combination of points of \(A\). Every atom with positive coefficient must have that coordinate equal to \(2\). The only such atom is \(2\mathbf e\), so \(x=2\mathbf e\). If no coordinate equals \(2\), integrality implies \(x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^4\). This proves \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^4=A\). The included unit cube proves full dimensionality. Cartesian products now give \(Q^k\cap\mathbb Z^{4k}=A^k\), and \(Q^k\) is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope. The original weights equal \(\exp(0)\), so they are affine exponential weights. Every supported integer midpoint has the same probability as its endpoints; completeness ensures that midpoint belongs to the support. This proves midpoint-log-concavity, also on products.

There is exactly one pair of distinct atoms in \(A\) with the same coordinatewise parity: \(\mathbf0\) and \(2\mathbf e\). Indeed all sixteen Boolean atoms have distinct parity vectors, and \(2\mathbf e\) has the parity vector of \(\mathbf0\). Any nontrivial reflected row at an integer center must have endpoints of the same parity. The midpoint of the only eligible distinct pair is \(\mathbf e\). Therefore the entire reflected row \(R_j=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace\) equals \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) when \(j\ne\mathbf e\), while \(R_{\mathbf e}=\lbrace\mathbf0,\mathbf e,2\mathbf e\rbrace\). Equivalently the latter center has displacement vectors \(0,\mathbf e,-\mathbf e\), and both nonzero vectors have support size four. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{A,\mathbf e}(3/4)&=1+2(3/4)^4=\frac{209}{128},\\
b_{3/4}(p)&=\frac{16+(209/128)^2}{17^3}
=\frac{305825}{80494592},\\
4^4b_{3/4}(p)&=\frac{305825}{314432}
=1-\frac{8607}{314432}<1.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are exact rational identities.

A displacement in \(\mathbb Z^{4k}\) is a concatenation of its \(k\) four-coordinate blocks. Its support size is the sum of the block support sizes. Membership of both reflected endpoints in \(A^k\) holds precisely block by block. Thus for \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\in A^k\), the finite oriented sums factor as \(T_{A^k,J}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^kT_{A,j_i}(\lambda)\). The original probability is \((P_k)_J=\prod_i p_{j_i}\), so summing its cube times the squared factored row sum yields \(b_\lambda(P_k)=b_\lambda(p)^k\). This proves the asserted product formula and its limit. In particular a proposed coefficient \(c>0\) would force \((305825/314432)^k\ge c\) for every \(k\), which is impossible. Writing \(\eta=\frac14\log(314432/305825)>0\), the scaled damped value is exactly \(\exp(-\eta n)\) on \(n=4k\). It therefore also rules out the stated positive exponential surplus for any \(c,\varepsilon>0\).

For calibration, retain the undamped objects of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 with the alias map in the statement. Since \(p_j=1/17\), one has \(H_p(j)=|R_j|/17\) and every geometric-row score \(Y_j(x)=\frac12\log(p_x/p_{2j-x})\) is zero. The exact row census gives \(b(p)=(16+3^2)/17^3=25/4913\). In particular \(4^4b(p)=6400/4913>1\).

For clarity, the zero-score specialization of the source pairing argument in equations (25)--(26) of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 is verified directly here. For any supported \(J\in A^k\), its full reflected row is \(R_J=\prod_iR_{j_i}\) and its undamped reflected mass is \(H_{P_k}(J)=\prod_iH_p(j_i)=|R_J|/17^k\). Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of every pair \(X,2J-X\) in that row. Otherwise a linear defining inequality excluding both endpoints would exclude their midpoint \(J\). It also contains the central atom \(J\). Since every atom has mass \(17^{-k}\), this gives \(P_k(\mathcal H)\ge|R_J|/(2\cdot17^k)=H_{P_k}(J)/2\). Taking the infimum preserves this bound for \(\tau_{P_k}(J)\), including halfspaces whose boundary passes through atoms.

Squaring and averaging against the original law gives
$$
M_2(P_k)\ge\frac14\sum_{J\in A^k}(P_k)_JH_{P_k}(J)^2
=\frac14b(p)^k
=\frac14\left(\frac{25}{4913}\right)^k.
$$
Multiplying by \(4^{4k}\) proves the displayed second-moment calibration. Jensen's inequality under the same original law yields \(M_3(P_k)=\mathbb E_{P_k}(\tau_{P_k}(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P_k)^{3/2}\). Since \(8^{4k}=(4^{4k})^{3/2}\), this proves the third-moment calibration. The strict inequality \(6400>4913\) supplies their positive exponential surplus. Thus the damped loss is not a failure of these original depth-moment bounds.

For every edge set \(E\) in the statement, every vector of \(\lbrace1,2\rbrace^n\) satisfies \(x_u-x_v\le1\) for every edge. Hence \(\lbrace1,2\rbrace^n\subseteq A_E\). But \((2,1,1,1)\) belongs to this upper Boolean cube in dimension four and does not belong to \(A\). For every \(k\), concatenate that missing block with \(k-1\) copies of \(\mathbf e\). The result belongs to \(\lbrace1,2\rbrace^{4k}\) and does not belong to \(A^k\). This proves exclusion from the displayed graph-census family. It leaves that restricted family and the locked mixed-integer maximum-depth conjecture unresolved.
