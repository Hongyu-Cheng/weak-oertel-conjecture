## Statement

For integers $L\ge6$ divisible by three let $h=L^{-2}$, $\varepsilon=h^2$ and $a=L^{-1}$. At coarse levels zero and one place
$$
Q_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,0),(1,-\varepsilon),(1,\varepsilon)\},\qquad
Q_1=\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,-1),(a,1),(0,2\varepsilon/a)\}.
$$
Use the fine trace on $\mathbb Z\times h\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R$, weighted by $h$ per fine length. The convex hull equals the hull of its fine trace and the fixed map $(z,u,x)\mapsto(4z-2,4u,4x)$ puts it between $B_2^3$ and $6B_2^3$. Both coarse areas are $2\varepsilon$. The original fine-radius-to-coarse-mass ratio is $L^2/8\to\infty$. The two dependence components admit uniformly round block maps of determinant $1/(4\varepsilon)$ with fine spacings $h$ and $1/(2L)$, so both transformed covering radii vanish. Their weighted fine masses are $\varepsilon(2+h)$ and $2\varepsilon$, and the maximum normalized fine depth tends to $2/9$. In the crossed-triangle sequence with $h=1/L$ and $a=h$, the second transformed mesh instead stays positive and the fine depth tends to $1/4$.

## Proof

The only coarse sites are the exposed endpoints $0,1$ and these are distinct dependence components. All vertices lie on the fine grid because $1/h=L^2$ and $a/h=L$ are integers. Thus the hull is exact. The two edge determinants have magnitude $4\varepsilon$ each. The geometric argument of the crossed-triangles predecessor applies with shear parameter $a$ because $Q_1$ contains the segment $(u,x)=(at,t)$, $-1\le t\le1$, all vertices stay in $[-1,1]^2$, and $a\le1/6$. The same fixed inball and outball scaling follows. The original covering radius $h/2$ divided by $4\varepsilon$ is $L^2/8$. The fixed scaling multiplies the ratio by $1/4$, so it diverges there too.

Use the maps
$$
(u,x)\mapsto(u,x/(4\varepsilon)),\qquad
(u,x)\mapsto(u/(2a),a(x-u/a)/(2\varepsilon)).
$$
Each has determinant $1/(4\varepsilon)$. The fixed images have vertices $(-1,0),(1,-1/4),(1,1/4)$ and $(-1/2,0),(1/2,0),(0,1)$, and spacings $h$ and $h/(2a)=1/(2L)$. The coarse weight of each component is exactly $1/2$. The fine law of the first triangle is $\varepsilon(2+h)$ by its sections of length $\varepsilon(1+u)$ on $[-1,1]\cap h\mathbb Z$. On the second triangle, $t=x-u/a$ ranges over $[0,(2\varepsilon/a)(1-|u|/a)]$ when $|u|\le a$. Hence its entire fine census is the levels $u=ih$ for $-L\le i\le L$ with those section lengths, including singleton endpoint levels. The weighted sum is $h(2\varepsilon/a)\sum_{i=-L}^L(1-|i|/L)=2\varepsilon$, since the tent sum is $L$.

Each fixed triangle image has normalized coarse maximum $4/9$. The centroid lower bound follows from 6769574fc1bd9f8d at dimension two and full mass, and the three homothetic vertex caps through the centroid cover the triangle with area fraction $4/9$ each. Both image centroids are fine-feasible: their first coordinates are $1/3$ in $h\mathbb Z$ and $0$ in $(h/(2a))\mathbb Z$. The normalized halfspace error of e4ef1ac154c5d280 applies to each fixed triangle separately with its own fine spacing and tends to zero. Block affine covariance of the normalized sampled measures follows from the common continuous section Jacobian and the common fine covolume multiplier in each triangle. The fine maximum on each component therefore tends to $4/9$, by evaluating the lower bound at its feasible centroid and using the coarse maximum for the upper bound.

Any cut in a component plane extends to a cut excluding the other component by adding a sufficiently large affine coarse tilt, and any containing ambient halfspace restricts to a containing cut or the whole plane on the component. Thus normalized depth at any sampled mark equals its within-component normalized depth multiplied by that component's fine mass fraction. Both fractions tend to $1/2$, giving the limit $2/9$. The comparison case is the crossed-triangles predecessor with $a=h=1/L$, which has the fixed positive second covering radius and exact fine depth $1/(4+h)$.
