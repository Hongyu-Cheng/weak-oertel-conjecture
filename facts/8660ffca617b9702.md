## Statement

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\) with \(a\le b\), let \(D=\{a,\ldots,b\}\), and let \(w=(w_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) satisfy \(w_j>0\) on \(D\), \(w_j=0\) off \(D\), and \(w_j^2\ge w_{j-1}w_{j+1}\) for every \(j\in\mathbb Z\). Use the canonical notation \(W=\sum_{j\in D}w_j\), \(w_{\max}=\max_{j\in D}w_j\), and \(\phi_j=\log(w_{\max}/w_j)\). The scalar indices \(j,J\) are the \(n=1\) aliases of \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\) in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`; \(D,W,w_{\max},\phi\) have exactly that fact's meanings. The potential \(\phi\) is convex-admissible, so this complete table belongs to that fact's globally extendable class.

Define the centered Hellinger sum and its maximum by

\[
\mathcal R_J(w)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}\sqrt{w_{J+v}w_{J-v}},
\qquad
R_{1/2}(w)=\max_{J\in D}\mathcal R_J(w).
\]

The displacement sum is oriented and includes \(v=0\). These sums are not the canonical capacity sums: at center \(J\), the suppressed center \(j\) in the accepted fact's notation \(\alpha(v)\) is \(J\), and \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{J+v},w_{J-v}\}\).

For every such table,

\[
R_{1/2}(w)\ge \frac W2,
\qquad
\max\left\{0,\frac12-\frac{w_{\max}}W\right\}
\le 364500000\left(\frac{R_{1/2}(w)}W-\frac12\right)^2.
\]

The half-mass coefficient is sharp. Equality \(R_{1/2}(w)=W/2\) holds if and only if \(D\) consists of two adjacent sites of equal weight.

More precisely, define the normalized aliases \(p_j=w_j/W\), \(p_{\max}=w_{\max}/W\), and \(\delta=R_{1/2}(w)/W-1/2\). If \(p_{\max}<1/2\) and \(\delta<1/27000\), there exist \(\ell,\ell+1\in D\) such that

\[
p_\ell>\frac25,
\qquad p_{\ell+1}>\frac25,
\qquad
\sum_{j\notin\{\ell,\ell+1\}}p_j\le100\delta^2,
\qquad
\frac12-p_{\max}\le50\delta^2.
\]

## Proof

Taking logarithms of the discrete log-concavity inequalities at interior sites gives \(\phi_{j+1}-\phi_j\ge\phi_j-\phi_{j-1}\). Thus the piecewise-affine interpolation of \(\phi\) on \([a,b]\) is convex. Jensen's inequality proves convex-admissibility for every finite barycentric representation with center in \(D\). This is precisely the canonical admissibility condition in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, whose stated equivalence therefore places the complete table in the globally extendable class. The singleton case is immediate. In particular, for \(i,k\in D\) with \(i+k\) even, completeness and convexity give

\[
\frac{i+k}{2}\in D,
\qquad
w_{(i+k)/2}\ge\sqrt{w_iw_k}.
\tag{1}
\]

Normalization preserves all hypotheses and leaves the potential unchanged: \(\log(p_{\max}/p_j)=\phi_j\). The centered sums satisfy \(\mathcal R_J(p)=\mathcal R_J(w)/W\), and \(R_{1/2}(p)=R_{1/2}(w)/W\). We work with \(p\), whose total mass is one.

The map \((J,v)\mapsto(i,k)=(J+v,J-v)\) bijects the positive terms with the ordered pairs \(i,k\in D\) of equal parity. By (1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\mathcal R_J(p)
&=\sum_{\substack{i,k\in D\\i+k\ \mathrm{even}}}
p_{(i+k)/2}\sqrt{p_ip_k}\\
&\ge\sum_{\substack{i,k\in D\\i+k\ \mathrm{even}}}p_ip_k\\
&=\left(\sum_{i\ \mathrm{even}}p_i\right)^2
+\left(\sum_{i\ \mathrm{odd}}p_i\right)^2
\ge\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The maximum of the centered sums is at least their \(p\)-weighted average, proving \(R_{1/2}(p)\ge1/2\). Consequently \(\delta\ge0\), and (2) also gives the deficit bound

\[
0\le\sum_{j\in D}p_j\bigl(R_{1/2}(p)-\mathcal R_j(p)\bigr)
\le\delta.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(R_{1/2}(p)=1/2\), (2) forces \(\mathcal R_j(p)=1/2\) at every positive site. A singleton has centered sum one and cannot give equality. Otherwise the endpoint centered sums are \(\mathcal R_a(p)=p_a\) and \(\mathcal R_b(p)=p_b\), so \(p_a=p_b=1/2\). There can be no other positive site; completeness then gives two adjacent sites. Conversely, two equal adjacent weights have normalized centered sums \(1/2\), proving the equality characterization and sharpness.

We next prove the quantitative localization assertion. For any site \(j\), Cauchy-Schwarz, applied to the positive displacements, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_j(p)
&=p_j+2\sum_{v\ge1}\sqrt{p_{j-v}p_{j+v}}\\
&\le p_j+2\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i<j}p_i\right)
\left(\sum_{i>j}p_i\right)}
\le p_j+2\sqrt{\sum_{i<j}p_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The same bound holds with the right-tail sum in place of the left-tail sum.

Assume \(p_{\max}<1/2\) and \(\delta<1/27000\), and set \(h=1/900\). Let \(\ell\) be the least index with \(\sum_{i\le\ell}p_i\ge h\), and let \(u\) be the greatest index with \(\sum_{i\ge u}p_i\ge h\). These indices exist in \(D\), and

\[
\sum_{i<\ell}p_i<h,
\qquad
\sum_{i>u}p_i<h.
\tag{5}
\]

Suppose \(p_\ell\le2/5\). All earlier atoms are smaller than \(h<2/5\), so every \(j\le\ell\) has \(p_j\le2/5\) and left-tail mass below \(h\). Equation (4) gives \(\mathcal R_j(p)\le2/5+2\sqrt h=7/15\) on that entire prefix. Equations (3) and \(R_{1/2}(p)\ge1/2\) imply

\[
\delta
\ge\sum_{j\le\ell}p_j\bigl(R_{1/2}(p)-\mathcal R_j(p)\bigr)
\ge h\left(\frac12-\frac7{15}\right)
=\frac1{27000},
\]

a contradiction. Hence \(p_\ell>2/5\). Applying the right-tail version of (4) gives \(p_u>2/5\) by the same argument.

If \(u<\ell\), the left-tail sum in (5) contains \(p_u>2/5>h\), a contradiction. If \(u=\ell\), (5) gives \(1<p_{\max}+2h<1/2+2/900<1\), again a contradiction. Thus \(u>\ell\). Log-concavity gives, for every integer \(j\) between them,

\[
p_j\ge p_\ell^{(u-j)/(u-\ell)}p_u^{(j-\ell)/(u-\ell)}>\frac25.
\]

If \(u\ge\ell+2\), at least three atoms exceed \(2/5\), contradicting total mass one. Therefore \(u=\ell+1\).

Let \(\theta=1-p_\ell-p_{\ell+1}\) denote the mass outside these two sites, and write \(x=p_{\ell-1}\), \(y=p_{\ell+2}\), with zero values off \(D\). Then \(0\le\theta<1/5\), and \(x,y\le\theta\). The outward successive ratios on the left tail are at most \(x/p_\ell\), by log-concavity; the outward successive ratios on the right tail are at most \(y/p_{\ell+1}\). Both ratios are below \(1/2\). Summing their geometric majorants gives

\[
\sum_{j<\ell}p_j\le\frac{x}{1-x/p_\ell}\le2x,
\qquad
\sum_{j>\ell+1}p_j\le\frac{y}{1-y/p_{\ell+1}}\le2y.
\tag{6}
\]

For clarity, the first majorant follows by iterating
\(p_{\ell-k}\le x(x/p_\ell)^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge1\) while the sites are in \(D\); the remaining terms are zero. If \(x=0\), completeness makes the entire left tail empty, so (6) still holds. The right tail is identical in this respect.

It follows from (6) that \(\theta\le2(x+y)\), hence \(\max\{x,y\}\ge\theta/4\). Also, because each atom is at most \(p_{\max}<1/2\), both central atoms are at least \(1/2-\theta\). If \(x\ge\theta/4\), the displacement-one terms at center \(\ell\) yield

\[
R_{1/2}(p)\ge\mathcal R_\ell(p)
\ge p_\ell+2\sqrt{xp_{\ell+1}}
\ge\frac12-\theta+\sqrt{\frac{2\theta}{5}}.
\]

If instead \(y\ge\theta/4\), the same lower bound follows at center \(\ell+1\), using the pair \(\ell,\ell+2\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta
&\ge\sqrt{\frac{2\theta}{5}}-\theta
=\sqrt\theta\left(\sqrt{\frac25}-\sqrt\theta\right)\\
&\ge\sqrt\theta\left(\frac35-\frac12\right)
=\frac{\sqrt\theta}{10}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The penultimate inequality uses \(2/5\ge(3/5)^2\) and \(\theta<1/5<1/4\). Thus \(\theta\le100\delta^2\). Since \(p_{\max}\ge(p_\ell+p_{\ell+1})/2=(1-\theta)/2\), we also have \(1/2-p_{\max}\le\theta/2\le50\delta^2\). This proves all the stated localization claims.

Finally, if \(p_{\max}\ge1/2\), the proposed stability inequality has left side zero. If \(p_{\max}<1/2\) and \(\delta<1/27000\), the stronger bound just proved applies. In the remaining case \(\delta\ge1/27000\),

\[
\frac12-p_{\max}\le\frac12
\le\frac{27000^2}{2}\,\delta^2
=364500000\,\delta^2.
\]

Rescaling back to \(w\) proves the universal stability inequality.

The supplied three-site family gives an exact calibration. For \(0<t\le1\), the complete table \((w_0,w_1,w_2)=(1,1,t^2)\), zero elsewhere, is log-concave: its only nontrivial interior inequality is \(1\ge t^2\). Its centered sums are \(1\), \(1+2t\), and \(t^2\), so \(W=2+t^2\), \(w_{\max}=1\), and \(R_{1/2}(w)=1+2t\). Direct substitution gives

\[
\frac12-\frac{w_{\max}}W=\frac{t^2}{2(2+t^2)},
\qquad
\delta=\frac{t(4-t)}{2(2+t^2)},
\qquad
\frac{\frac12-w_{\max}/W}{\delta^2}
=\frac{2(2+t^2)}{(4-t)^2}\longrightarrow\frac14
\quad(t\downarrow0).
\]

This calibration concerns the Hellinger sum alone, with the actual pair capacities still equal to the endpoint minima specified in the statement.
