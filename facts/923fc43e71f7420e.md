## Statement

Fix $R\ge2$ and let $L\to\infty$, $H=L^{-2}$. Let $r_L(x)=-x^2$ on $|x|\le1$ and $r_L(x)=-1-L(|x|-1)$ on $1\le|x|\le R$. At integer bases $-1,0,1$ use the actual fibers
$$
K_- =\{(x,t):|x|\le R,\ -1\le t\le H(1/2+r_L(x))\},\quad
K_+=-K_-,\quad K_0=[-R,R]\times[H/2-R,H/2+R].
$$
Let $\Sigma_L$ be their actual pooled within-fiber covariance, $h_L=H/\sqrt{(\Sigma_L)_{tt}}$, and $\Psi(q)=(q+1)_+-q_+$. For a nonvertical normal $(a,u)$ at a sampled mark $Y=(p,y)$ use the smoothed capture density $\Psi((a(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y))/(h_L\sqrt{u^\mathsf T\Sigma_Lu}))$, with ordinary closed pure-base caps also allowed. For sufficiently large $L$, the unique deepest mark is $(0,0,H/2)$, with raw depth $|K_-|+2R^2+RH$. The untilted positive vertical cap is an actual minimizer, and a finitely tilted negative vertical cap gives an opposite minimizing continuous direction. On the limiting outer supporting facets for the positive vertical cap the outside and inside selectors are both $(1-x^2)_+$, and the reflected roof-offset records have equal costs for every common normal Bregman test and every transition quadratic. Each facet's original complementary mass is $2R-4/3$, of which $2(R-1)$ is at infinite offsets invisible to those records. The proper load test $x\ge0$ sees original, selected and complementary lengths $R,2/3,R-2/3$. With the common coarea factor canceled and fixed physical displacements, the finite-record costs are independent of $R$.

## Proof

For $L\ge2$ the slopes of $r_L$ jump downward at the joins, so it is concave. At fixed $R$, its scaled height perturbation is uniformly $O(1/L)$. The outer fibers are therefore positive for large $L$ and their midpoint set lies in $[-R,R]\times[-1,1]\subset K_0$. The lifted hull has the stated exact integer sections: any combination at zero uses equal endpoint weights, and each extreme base is exposed.

Write $V_L=|K_-|$ and $c_L=\sqrt{(\Sigma_L)_{xx}/(\Sigma_L)_{tt}}$. Reflection in $x$ gives zero off-diagonal covariance. The outer fibers tend to $[-R,R]\times[-1,0]$ and its reflection, and the middle tends to the centered square. Their limiting total outer mass is $4R$, middle mass $4R^2$, horizontal variance $R^2/3$ in each, and vertical variances $1/12$ and $R^2/3$ respectively. Consequently
$$
c_L^2\longrightarrow\frac{4R^2(R+1)}{4R^3+1}>1.
$$
Fix bounds $1<c_-\le c_L\le c_+$ for large $L$. At $Y=(0,0,H/2)$ pair $(z,x,t)$ with $(-z,-x,-t)$ on the outer fibers. Their ramp arguments sum to $-1/\sqrt{1+c_L^2b^2}$ for continuous normal $(b,1)$, the opposite value for $(b,-1)$, and zero for a horizontal normal, independently of the base tilt. Since $\Psi(q)+\Psi(-1-q)=1$ and $\Psi$ is nondecreasing, every such cap captures at least $V_L$ on the outer pair. Equality holds for the positive vertical direction for every tilt. It holds for the negative vertical direction for a sufficiently large finite tilt, saturating one outer fiber and excluding the other.

For the middle square, the centered projection density $f_b$ of $bx+t-H/2$ is the convolution of the two interval section densities. For $b\ne0$ it is proportional to the overlap length of the intervals of half-widths $|b|R$ and $R$ as their centers separate. Thus $f_b(0)=2R/\max(1,|b|)$, it has that value on $|s|\le R|1-|b||$, and it decreases linearly to zero at $|s|=R(1+|b|)$, with the constant-density interval interpretation for $b=0$. In particular $f_b(s)\ge f_b(0)(1-s/m)$ for $0\le s\le m=R\max(1,|b|)$. With width $w=H\sqrt{1+c_L^2b^2}$ the middle gain over half-volume is $\int_0^w (1-s/w)f_b(s)\,ds$. For $|b|\le1/2$ and sufficiently small $H$ the strip lies in the plateau, so the gain is $Rw\ge RH$. For $|b|\ge1/2$, write $G=\sqrt{1+c_L^2b^2}/\max(1,|b|)$. The ratio $w/m=HG/R$ is uniformly small, and the displayed bound gives gain at least $RH G(1-HG/(3R))$. Uniformly in these $b$, $\min(\sqrt{1+c_-^2/4},c_-)\le G\le\sqrt{1+c_+^2}$. The lower bound is strictly larger than one, so the gain exceeds $RH$ for sufficiently small $H$. Horizontal directions give gain $RHc_L$, and the opposite vertical component signs use the same middle symmetry. Pure-base caps through zero have mass at least $4R^2+V_L$. The least mass at $Y$ is therefore exactly $V_L+2R^2+RH$, attained by the stated positive and negative vertical choices.

For a mark at base zero with nonzero vertical displacement from $H/2$, choose the vertical orientation facing that displacement, and a finite tilt that isolates one full outer fiber. The middle capture strictly decreases from the centered value. It is monotone with outward displacement and strictly decreases for small displacement because the initial transition interval lies in the interior of the vertical range and has positive width. This proves strictness for every nonzero displacement, including near the ends of the square. At height $H/2$ and horizontal mark coordinate $x_0\ne0$, the untilted positive vertical cap has the same mass as at $Y$. Its derivative under changing $(0,1)$ to $(b,1)$ at $b=0$ is $H^{-1}\int_{-H<t-H/2<0}(x-x_0)\,d\mu_L$ for the actual summed volume $\mu_L$. The width derivative is zero, the kink lines are volume-null, and differentiation uses the bounded ramp derivative. Reflection makes the $x$ integral zero and the strip has positive middle mass, so the derivative is nonzero. A suitable small signed change strictly reduces the mass. At each outer base an exposing pure-base cap has mass $V_L$. This proves uniqueness and the actual rank-one menu.

On the limiting support coordinates for the positive vertical cap, the old upper and lower roof arguments are $r_L(x)$ and $-1-r_L(-x)$. They are $-x^2$ and $x^2-1$ on the open core, and tend to $-\infty,+\infty$ on the tails. Consequently the outside capture and inside omission both have value $\tau=(1-x^2)_+$; the transition footprint equals one almost everywhere on the core and zero on the tails. The reflected facet points are $(-1,x,0)$ and $(1,-x,0)$ through the limiting source. Every common normal displacement is of opposite sign on the two. The primitive $J$ has derivative $\Psi$, so differentiating in $s$ and using the complement identity gives $B(-1-q,-s)=B(q,s)$ from equality at $s=0$. At either infinite offset the primitive is affine for finite displacements in the limiting tail, so $B$ is zero. Thus the paired Bregman and quadratic records agree for all full physical displacements, and proper middle columns have zero Bregman residual because the normal-column integral over the full transition is zero. On each facet the selected length is $\int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)\,dx=4/3$ and the complement is $2R-4/3$. Exactly $2(R-1)$ lies outside the core. The proper vertical load test $x\ge0$ halves all these lengths. The visible costs after canceling the common coarea factor use only the fixed core at the fixed reflected physical coordinates. Varying $R$ therefore cannot be detected by these records alone although it changes the original complementary mass. The selector also has a genuine convex lift over $[-1,1]$ as the region under the concave roof $1-x^2$ and above zero. By contrast, the unit-prism complement over the original face contains $(-1,1/2)$ and $(1,1/2)$ but omits their midpoint $(0,1/2)$. The lift's vertical lengths are extended by zero outside its own projection only for integration of the loads.
