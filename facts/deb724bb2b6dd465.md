## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers. Let \(a>0\), \(L\ge0\), and \(\beta\ge0\) be real numbers such that
$$
b\ge a\sqrt{k},\qquad T\le k/2+Lb.
$$
For every \(i\in[k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), let \(B_i,C_{4,i}\) be integers satisfying
$$
B_i\ge b,\qquad C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1.
$$
Give every spatial vector multiplicity one and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),&
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}\\
Q(\mathbf x)&=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,&
Z_4&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here every argument \(E\) of \(f_E\) is a nonnegative integer, and every integer height in its indicated interval, including both endpoints, is retained. Put
$$
Z_{\rm bin}=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},
\qquad
Z_4^{Q\ge H}=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k\\Q(\mathbf x)\ge H}}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}
$$
for every integer \(H\ge1\), with an empty sum equal to zero. Thus \(Z_{\rm bin}\) is the all-binary restriction in the same original ambient space and with the same height coordinate as \(Z_4\).

Whenever the integer \(H\) satisfies
$$
H\ge\max\left\lbrace1,4L,\frac4{a^2}\log(2k)\right\rbrace,
$$
one has the explicit finite inequality
$$
\frac{Z_4^{Q\ge H}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le
\frac{2(k+1)\exp(-a^2H^2/4)}{1-\exp(-a^2H/4)}.
\tag{1}
$$
In particular, define
$$
K_a=4(1+a^{-2}),\qquad
H_0=\left\lceil K_a\bigl[L+\log(k+1)+1\bigr]\right\rceil.
\tag{2}
$$
Then
$$
Z_4^{Q\ge H_0}\le\frac12Z_{\rm bin}.
\tag{3}
$$
For every fixed real \(D\ge0\), if \(L\le D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\), this same integer threshold satisfies
$$
H_0\le K_{a,D}[1+\log^2(k+1)],
\qquad K_{a,D}=K_a(D+2)+1.
\tag{4}
$$
All logarithms are natural. The theorem includes \(\beta=0\), \(T=0\), and every cap below \(k/2\). No accepted fact is used as a premise; the model and every counting estimate needed here are defined and proved below. The locked mixed-integer dimensions, convex body, and mixed-integer set retain their locked meanings.

## Proof

The original atom consisting of the zero spatial vector at height zero shows that \(Z_{\rm bin}>0\).

For an auxiliary number \(m\ge0\) of binary coordinates and any integer \(U\), define the exact binary-height normalizer
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\beta)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}
\sum_{t=\sum_i\epsilon_i}^{U}e^{-\beta t},&U\ge0,\\
0,&U<0,
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
$$
where an inner sum is zero when its lower endpoint exceeds its upper endpoint. The notation for \(m=0\) uses the single empty binary vector, whose sum is zero. In particular, the original \(Z_{\rm bin}\) in the statement is exactly \(Z_{\rm bin}(k,T;\beta)\). Define
$$
A_m(t)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,t)}\binom mj,&t\ge0,\\
0,&t<0.
\end{cases}
$$
Counting the binary vectors at each original height gives, for \(U\ge0\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\beta)&=\sum_{t=0}^{U}e^{-\beta t}A_m(t),\\
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;0)&=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,U)}\binom mj(U-j+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
The factor \(U-j+1\) is the exact number of allowed integer heights, not an approximation.

We first prove the tilt comparison
$$
\frac{e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\beta)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\beta)}
\le
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}
\tag{7}
$$
for every integer \(m\ge0\), integer \(\delta\ge0\), integer \(T\ge0\), and real \(\beta\ge0\). No assumption about the position of \(T\) relative to \(m/2\) is imposed.

The sequence \(A_m(t)\), on nonnegative integer indices, is positive and log-concave. For \(m=0\) it is constant one. For \(m\ge1\), write \(v_j=\binom mj\). The positive ratios
$$
\frac{v_{j+1}}{v_j}=\frac{m-j}{j+1}\qquad(0\le j<m)
$$
decrease with \(j\). Consequently, for \(1\le t<m\),
$$
v_{t+1}A_m(t-1)
=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}v_{t+1}v_j
\le v_t\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}v_{j+1}
\le v_tA_m(t).
$$
Using \(A_m(t)=A_m(t-1)+v_t\) and \(A_m(t+1)=A_m(t)+v_{t+1}\), this is precisely
\(A_m(t)^2\ge A_m(t-1)A_m(t+1)\). At \(t=0\) the same inequality follows from \(A_m(-1)=0\); at \(t=m\) it follows from \(A_m(m+1)=A_m(m)\ge A_m(m-1)\); beyond \(m\) it is an equality. Thus
$$
\rho_t=\frac{A_m(t-1)}{A_m(t)}\qquad(t\ge0)
$$
is nondecreasing. For fixed \(\delta\ge1\), the sequence
$$
r_\delta(t)=\frac{A_m(t-\delta)}{A_m(t)}
$$
is zero for \(0\le t<\delta\), while for \(t\ge\delta\) it equals
\(\prod_{j=0}^{\delta-1}\rho_{t-j}\). It is therefore nondecreasing. For \(\delta=0\), it is identically one.

Set \(w_t=A_m(t)\) and \(\alpha_t=e^{-\beta t}\) for \(0\le t\le T\). Since \(\alpha_t\) is nonincreasing and \(r_\delta(t)\) is nondecreasing,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left(\sum_{t=0}^T w_t\alpha_t r_\delta(t)\right)
 \left(\sum_{t=0}^T w_t\right)
-\left(\sum_{t=0}^T w_t r_\delta(t)\right)
 \left(\sum_{t=0}^T w_t\alpha_t\right)\\
&\qquad=\frac12\sum_{s,t=0}^T
w_sw_t(\alpha_s-\alpha_t)(r_\delta(s)-r_\delta(t))\le0.
\end{aligned}
$$
All denominators are positive. In view of (6) and the exact change of height index,
$$
e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\beta)
=\sum_{t=0}^T e^{-\beta t}A_m(t-\delta),
$$
this proves (7), including \(T<\delta\), when both its numerators vanish.

We next record two uniform-count bounds. Suppose \(m\ge1\) and \(\delta\ge1\) are integers. If \(0\le T\le m/2\), then
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}
\le\exp\left(-\frac{2\delta^2}{m+1}\right).
\tag{8}
$$
If \(T<\delta\), the numerator is zero. Otherwise, reindex the second formula in (6) to obtain
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)
=\sum_{h=\delta}^{T}\binom m{h-\delta}(T-h+1).
$$
For \(\delta\le h\le T\le m/2\), the exact binomial ratio is
$$
\frac{\binom m{h-\delta}}{\binom mh}
=\prod_{j=0}^{\delta-1}\frac{h-j}{m-h+j+1}
\le\exp\left(-\frac{2\delta^2}{m+1}\right).
$$
Indeed, put \(x_j=(m-2h+2j+1)/(m+1)\). Then \(0<x_j<1\), the corresponding factor is \((1-x_j)/(1+x_j)\), and
$$
\log\frac{1-x_j}{1+x_j}\le-2x_j
\le-\frac{2(2j+1)}{m+1}.
$$
The first inequality follows by differentiation, since the derivative of its left side plus \(2x_j\) is \(-2x_j^2/(1-x_j^2)\le0\), and its value at zero is zero. Summing uses \(\sum_{j=0}^{\delta-1}(2j+1)=\delta^2\). Multiplying by the nonnegative factors \(T-h+1\) and summing proves (8).

For the second bound, for every real \(s\ge0\) the elementary finite binomial estimate is
$$
2^{-m}\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le m\\j\le m/2-s}}\binom mj
\le\exp(-2s^2/m).
\tag{9}
$$
To prove it, for \(\lambda\ge0\) bound its left side by
$$
e^{-\lambda s}\,2^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom mj e^{-\lambda(j-m/2)}
=e^{-\lambda s}\cosh(\lambda/2)^m
\le\exp(-\lambda s+m\lambda^2/8).
$$
Here \(\log\cosh u\le u^2/2\) for \(u\ge0\): its derivative is \(\tanh u\le u\), which follows by integrating \((\tanh u)'=1/\cosh^2u\le1\) from zero. Taking \(\lambda=4s/m\) proves (9), also when the sum is empty.

Suppose now that \(T>m/2\) and
$$
U:=T-\delta\le m/2-\delta/2.
\tag{10}
$$
If \(U<0\), the shifted normalizer is zero. Otherwise, binomial symmetry gives
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)
\ge\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\binom mj
\ge2^{m-1}.
$$
Every term used has its original height factor \(T-j+1\ge1\). Also \(0\le U\le m/2\), so (6) and (9), with \(s=m/2-U\ge\delta/2\), give
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;0)
\le(m+1)\sum_{j=0}^{U}\binom mj
\le(m+1)2^m\exp(-\delta^2/(2m)).
$$
Thus, in the regime (10),
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}
\le2(m+1)\exp(-\delta^2/(2m)).
\tag{11}
$$

We apply these estimates directly to the original four-site sectors. Let \(Z_4^{Q=q}\) denote the original mass with exactly \(q\) high coordinates. Fix \(1\le q\le k\), a high-coordinate set \(I\) of size \(q\), and one choice of site two or three at each coordinate in \(I\). Write
$$
m=k-q,\qquad \delta=bq,\qquad E=\sum_{i\in I}g_i(x_i).
$$
Both high sites cost at least \(b\): this is assumed for site two and follows from \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge b\) for site three. Hence \(E\ge\delta\). The exact mass with this fixed choice is
$$
e^{-\beta E}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-E;\beta)
\le e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\beta).
\tag{12}
$$
The equality is the original height translation \(t'=t-E\); the inequality also follows term by term from \(f_{E+j}\le f_{\delta+j}\) for each remaining binary one-count \(j\). Setting all coordinates of \(I\) to zero embeds the complete binary-height support with \(m\) coordinates into the original all-binary support. Therefore
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(k,T;\beta)\ge Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\beta).
$$
There are exactly \(\binom kq\) choices of \(I\) and \(2^q\) choices of its high labels, so (7) and (12) yield
$$
\frac{Z_4^{Q=q}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le\binom kq2^q
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}.
\tag{13}
$$

Assume in addition that \(q\ge4L\). Then \(Lb\le\delta/4\), and \(q/2\le\delta/4\) because \(b\ge2\). Consequently the original cap hypothesis implies
$$
T-\delta\le m/2+q/2+Lb-\delta
\le m/2-\delta/2.
\tag{14}
$$
If \(m=0\), (14) is negative, so the sector is empty. If \(m\ge1\), apply (8) when \(T\le m/2\) and (11) when \(T>m/2\). Since \(m\le k\), \(m+1\le k\) for \(q\ge1\), and \(\delta=bq\), both cases imply
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}
\le2(k+1)\exp\left(-\frac{b^2q^2}{2k}\right).
\tag{15}
$$
This upper bound also holds for the empty sector when \(m=0\). Combining (13)-(15), \(b^2/k\ge a^2\), and \(\binom kq2^q\le(2k)^q\), gives
$$
\frac{Z_4^{Q=q}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le2(k+1)\exp\left(q\log(2k)-\frac{a^2q^2}{2}\right)
\quad(q\ge\max\lbrace1,4L\rbrace).
\tag{16}
$$
If \(H\) satisfies the hypothesis of (1), every \(q\ge H\) also satisfies
\(q\log(2k)\le a^2q^2/4\). Hence summing (16) over the disjoint original sectors gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_4^{Q\ge H}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
&\le2(k+1)\sum_{q=H}^{k}\exp(-a^2q^2/4)\\
&\le2(k+1)\exp(-a^2H^2/4)
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\exp(-a^2Hj/4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality uses \((H+j)^2\ge H^2+Hj\). The geometric series converges because \(a>0\) and \(H\ge1\), and its sum proves (1). If \(H>k\), the original tail is empty and the same inequality still holds.

It remains to check the stated threshold and its finite constants. Put \(x=\log(k+1)\ge0\). Since \(\log(2k)\le x+1\), \(K_a\ge4\), and \(K_a\ge4/a^2\), the integer \(H_0\) in (2) satisfies every hypothesis of (1). In particular,
$$
e^{-a^2H_0/4}\le e^{-\log(2k)}\le\frac12.
$$
Moreover, \(aK_a=4(a+a^{-1})\ge8\), and therefore
$$
\frac{a^2H_0^2}{4}\ge16(x+1)^2
\ge x+\log8.
$$
For the last inequality, \(16(x+1)^2-(x+3)=16x^2+31x+13\ge0\) and \(\log8<3\). Equation (1) consequently yields
$$
\frac{Z_4^{Q\ge H_0}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le4(k+1)e^{-a^2H_0^2/4}
\le\frac12,
$$
which proves (3). Finally, \(x+1\le2(1+x^2)\) for \(x\ge0\). If \(L\le D(1+x^2)\), the ceiling in (2) gives
$$
H_0\le K_a\bigl[D(1+x^2)+x+1\bigr]+1
\le\bigl[K_a(D+2)+1\bigr](1+x^2),
$$
proving (4).

Every sector comparison used complete original height tails. All low-site costs stayed exactly zero and one, all spatial vectors had multiplicity one, and (6) retained the exact endpoint factor \(U-j+1\). The comparison in (7) was proved on the original binary-height sums for every cap, so no independent Gibbs law was identified with an original law. In particular, \(T=0\) leaves only the original zero vector at height zero, and every high-occupation tail in the theorem is zero in that case.
