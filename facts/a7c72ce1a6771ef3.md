## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex with nonempty mixed trace $S$, and write $K_z=C_z$ at its finitely many integer sites. Put $q=\dim_H S$ and $M=\mathcal H_q(S)>0$. For an affine $c:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d$ and $s\ge1$, let $S_s$ be the mixed trace of the affine image under $(z,x)\mapsto(z,c(z)+s(x-c(z)))$. Then $\mathcal H_q(S_s)=s^q M$. For a fixed closed halfspace $H=\{(z,x):u\cdot z+v\cdot x\ge b\}$, put $r_z(x)=v\cdot(x-c(z))$ and $\delta_z=b-u\cdot z-v\cdot c(z)$. Its normalized mass is
$$
p(s)=M^{-1}\sum_z\mathcal H_q\{x\in K_z:r_z(x)\ge\delta_z/s\},
$$
and
$$
\lim_{s\to\infty}p(s)
=M^{-1}\sum_z\left(\mathcal H_q\{x\in K_z:r_z(x)>0\}
+\mathbf1_{\{\delta_z\le0\}}\mathcal H_q\{x\in K_z:r_z(x)=0\}\right).
$$
This includes $q=0$ counting measure and pure-base caps. For $q=d\ge1$ and $v\ne0$ the boundary term vanishes and one can use the closed inequality $r_z\ge0$ in the limit.

## Proof

The affine map preserves the integer projection and is a similarity of factor $s$ at each fiber. Summing the similarity identity for $\mathcal H_q$ over the finitely many disjoint fibers gives $\mathcal H_q(S_s)=s^q M$. Pulling back the fixed closed cap gives precisely $r_z\ge\delta_z/s$. Its indicator tends to one if $r_z>0$, to zero if $r_z<0$, and equals $\mathbf1_{\{\delta_z\le0\}}$ at $r_z=0$ for every $s>0$. Each convex fiber has finite $\mathcal H_q$ because its affine dimension is at most $q$ and it is compact. Dominated convergence gives the displayed limit. If $v=0$, the signed threshold condition is exactly the original pure-base inequality at each site. If $q=d\ge1$ and $v\ne0$, each boundary trace lies in a proper affine hyperplane of zero $d$-volume.
