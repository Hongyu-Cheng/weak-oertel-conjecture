## Statement

In $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R$, let $\gamma_1$ be unit Lebesgue measure on each interval $\{0\}\times[0,1]$ and $\{1\}\times[0,1]$, and let $\gamma_2$ be unit Lebesgue measure on each interval $\{2\}\times[0,1]$ and $\{1\}\times[0,1]$. Couplings with same-parity endpoint bases exist, but every such coupling has midpoint mass two in $\{1\}\times[0,1]$. No such midpoint law is dominated by unit mixed-integer volume.

## Proof

The only allowed pairs of bases are $(0,2)$ and $(1,1)$, each with midpoint base $1$. A valid coupling uses unit Lebesgue measure on the common continuous coordinate in each pair and copies that coordinate to both endpoints. Every admissible coupling has total mass two by the marginals and every tuple midpoint is in the displayed interval. Unit mixed-integer volume assigns that interval mass one, proving the claimed obstruction.
