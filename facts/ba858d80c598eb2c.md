## Statement

For $K=\{(s,h):0\le h\le1,\ |s|\le(1+h)/2\}$, the function $R_K(c)=|K\cap(2c-K)|$ on $K$ has the unique maximizer $c_*=(0,1/2)$ with value $5/4$. Its ordinary raw depth is $5/8$, or $5/12$ after normalization, below $\Gamma_2=4/9$. For every integer $n\ge0$, let $C=[0,1]^n\times K$ and $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2)$. The maximizers of $\mathcal H_2(S\cap(2Y-S))$ over $Y\in S$ are exactly $(p,c_*)$ with $p\in\{0,1\}^n$, and every such point has normalized actual mixed depth $5/(12\cdot 2^n)<\Gamma_2/2^n$.

## Proof

At $c=(\xi,\eta)$, the horizontal slices of $K$ and $2c-K$ at height $h$ have lengths $1+h$ and $1+2\eta-h$ and centers $0$ and $2\xi$. The common height interval is $[\max(0,2\eta-1),\min(1,2\eta)]$. Aligning the interval centers maximizes their overlap for fixed heights. Integrating the smaller length $1+\eta-|h-\eta|$ on that interval gives
$$
R_K(0,\eta)=
\begin{cases}
2\eta+\eta^2,&0\le\eta\le1/2,\\
1+2\eta-3\eta^2,&1/2\le\eta\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
The first branch increases strictly and the second decreases strictly. At $\eta=1/2$, any fixed nonzero $\xi$ strictly decreases the slice overlap on an interval about $h=1/2$, where the difference in the slice lengths tends to zero and their center separation is $2|\xi|$. This proves the unique maximizer and its value.

The overlap is centrally symmetric about $c_*$. Each containing closed halfplane takes at least half its area, so the raw depth is at least $5/8$. The original $K$ has area $3/2$, and the halfplane $h\le1/2$ takes exactly $\int_0^{1/2}(1+h)\,dh=5/8$, attaining the bound.

For binary bases, $z+w=2p$ forces $z=w=p$ coordinatewise, giving the ordinary overlap and exactly the stated maximizing set. Every containing ambient halfspace takes at least $5/8$ in the own fiber, whose trace is either a containing halfplane or the whole fiber. For the reverse inequality, put $\ell_p(z)=\sum_j(2p_j-1)(z_j-p_j)$. At $p$ it is zero, and at any other binary base it is at most $-1$. Thus the actual closed ambient halfspace $\ell_p(z)+1/2-h\ge0$ takes only the own downward cap. Dividing $5/8$ by the total mass $2^n(3/2)$ proves the result, including $n=0$.
