## Statement

For each integer \(G\geq1\), let \(k=2G\) be the spatial dimension and \(N=k+1\) the auxiliary ambient dimension. Put \(\epsilon=6^{-4G}\), and define

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\lbrace(s,t)\in[0,2]^2:|s-t|\leq1\rbrace, & P&=Q^G,\\
V&=\lbrace(0,0),(0,1),(1,2),(2,2),(2,1),(1,0)\rbrace, & D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^k,\\
A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subset\mathbb R^N.
\end{aligned}
$$

For \(s\in\lbrace0,1,2,3,4\rbrace\), set \(\gamma_2=\epsilon\) and \(\gamma_0=\gamma_1=\gamma_3=\gamma_4=1\). These \(\gamma_s\) are scalar block-profile values. Their associated tuple profile is precisely \((s_1,\ldots,s_G)\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^G\gamma_{s_i}\), which is strictly positive on \(\lbrace0,1,2,3,4\rbrace^G\). For \(z=(x,0)\in A\), evaluate that tuple profile on the original block sums and define the original atom weights, normalization, and atom law by

$$
w_z=\prod_{i=1}^{G}\gamma_{x_{2i-1}+x_{2i}},\qquad
Z=\sum_{z\in A}w_z,\qquad p_z=\frac{w_z}{Z}.
$$

For this original law define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf\left\lbrace\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u:
 H=\lbrace u\in\mathbb R^N:a\cdot u\geq b\rbrace,
\ a\in\mathbb R^N\setminus\lbrace0\rbrace,
\ b\in\mathbb R,\ z\in H\right\rbrace,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then \(P\) is compact, convex, and full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^k\), and \(D=(V\cup\lbrace(1,1)\rbrace)^G\) is its complete lattice support. The spatial affine rank of \(A\) is \(k\). Both its support and its law are invariant under every swap of the two coordinates in any one block. Moreover,

$$
Z=(6+\epsilon)^G,\qquad
M_3(p)\leq2\,6^{-3G},\qquad
8^{k+1}M_3(p)\leq16\left(\frac8{27}\right)^G\longrightarrow0.
$$

Consequently no absolute \(\kappa>0\), independent of \(G\), gives \(M_3(p)\geq\kappa 8^{-(k+1)}\) throughout this class of arbitrary positive block-sum profiles. Neither the original weights nor their normalized law admit a log-affine or globally log-concave extension to \(P\times\lbrace0\rbrace\).

## Proof

The defining inequalities of \(Q\) are affine, and \(Q\subset[0,2]^2\) is closed, so \(Q\), and hence \(P\), is compact and convex. The open square \((1/2,3/2)^2\) lies in \(Q\); thus \(P\) is full-dimensional. Its integer points have both coordinates in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\). The condition \(|s-t|\leq1\) removes exactly \((0,2)\) and \((2,0)\) from these nine points. Therefore \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^2=V\cup\lbrace(1,1)\rbrace\), and taking products gives the stated formula for \(D\).

For completeness, \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(V)\). If \(0\leq s\leq1\), the allowed vertical interval is \(0\leq t\leq s+1\). Its lower endpoint \((s,0)\) lies on the segment joining \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\), and its upper endpoint \((s,s+1)\) lies on the segment joining \((0,1)\) and \((1,2)\). If \(1\leq s\leq2\), the allowed interval is \(s-1\leq t\leq2\). Its endpoints lie on the segments from \((1,0)\) to \((2,1)\) and from \((1,2)\) to \((2,2)\), respectively. Each vertical interval is therefore contained in \(\operatorname{conv}(V)\), and the reverse containment follows from convexity of \(Q\). Products of convex combinations show \(P=\operatorname{conv}(V^G)\). The spatial origin and every spatial standard basis vector belong to \(D\), so the spatial affine rank is exactly \(k\). All atoms have auxiliary height zero, as required.

The inequalities defining \(Q\) are unchanged by exchanging its coordinates. Each weight depends only on the sum of the two coordinates in each block. This proves the asserted support and law invariance. Among the seven points of \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^2\), the point \((1,1)\) is the only one with coordinate sum two. It has one-block weight \(\epsilon\), and the six points of \(V\) each have one-block weight one. All weights are strictly positive, and the product sum gives \(Z=(6+\epsilon)^G\). In particular, each of the \(6^G\) atoms in \(V^G\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) has original probability \(Z^{-1}\leq6^{-G}\).

We next construct an admissible closed halfspace selecting each such atom. The following table gives an integer exposing vector \(a_v\) for every \(v\in V\). The last column writes the exposing inequality as the sum of two defining inequalities of \(Q\).

| \(v\) | \(a_v\) | Inequality for \((s,t)\in Q\) |
|---|---|---|
| \((0,0)\) | \((-1,-1)\) | \((-s)+(-t)\leq0+0\) |
| \((0,1)\) | \((-2,1)\) | \((-s)+(t-s)\leq0+1\) |
| \((1,2)\) | \((-1,2)\) | \(t+(t-s)\leq2+1\) |
| \((2,2)\) | \((1,1)\) | \(s+t\leq2+2\) |
| \((2,1)\) | \((2,-1)\) | \(s+(s-t)\leq2+1\) |
| \((1,0)\) | \((1,-2)\) | \((-t)+(s-t)\leq0+1\) |

In every row, equality requires equality in both component inequalities, which forces \((s,t)=v\). Hence \(a_v\cdot(y-v)\leq0\) for every \(y\in Q\), with equality exactly at \(v\). For an integer point \(y\in(Q\cap\mathbb Z^2)\setminus\lbrace v\rbrace\), integrality strengthens this to \(a_v\cdot(y-v)\leq-1\).

Fix \(z=(x,0)\in V^G\times\lbrace0\rbrace\), and write \(x^{(i)}=(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})\). In the original ambient space, set

$$
H_z=\left\lbrace(u,h)\in\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R:
\sum_{i=1}^{G}a_{x^{(i)}}\cdot\bigl(u^{(i)}-x^{(i)}\bigr)\geq0\right\rbrace.
$$

Its normal is the concatenation of the \(G\) nonzero exposing vectors followed by a zero height coordinate. Since \(G\geq1\), that ambient normal is nonzero. This is a closed affine halfspace, and \(z\) lies on its boundary. On \(P\), every summand is nonpositive. Their sum can vanish only when every block is the prescribed block of \(x\). For every other original support atom, at least one summand is an integer at most \(-1\), and all others remain nonpositive. Thus \(A\cap H_z=\lbrace z\rbrace\), including all boundary atoms. In particular every point of \(V^G\) is an exposed vertex of \(P\).

Every halfspace containing \(z\) contains its atom, so \(\tau_p(z)\geq p_z\). The halfspace just constructed gives the reverse inequality. Consequently \(\tau_p(z)=p_z=Z^{-1}\) on these \(6^G\) atoms. For any other root, \(0\leq\tau_p(z)\leq1\); the upper bound also follows directly from the admissible nonzero-normal halfspace \(\lbrace(u,h):h\geq0\rbrace\), which contains all original atoms.

The total original mass outside \(V^G\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) is

$$
1-\frac{6^G}{Z}
=1-\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{6+\epsilon}\right)^G
\leq\frac{G\epsilon}{6+\epsilon}
\leq\frac{G\epsilon}{6}
\leq6^{-3G}.
$$

Here \(1-(1-r)^G\leq Gr\) for \(0\leq r\leq1\) follows by expanding \(1-(1-r)^G=r\sum_{j=0}^{G-1}(1-r)^j\). For the last inequality, \(6^G\geq G\) for every \(G\geq1\), for example by the binomial bound \((1+5)^G\geq1+5G\), and \(\epsilon=6^{-4G}\).

Partitioning the defining expectation under its original law, with no conditioning or replacement of the root law, now gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&=\sum_{z\in V^G\times\lbrace0\rbrace}p_z\tau_p(z)^3
 +\sum_{z\in A\setminus(V^G\times\lbrace0\rbrace)}p_z\tau_p(z)^3\\
&\leq\frac{6^G}{Z^4}+1-\frac{6^G}{Z}
\leq6^{-3G}+6^{-3G}
=2\,6^{-3G}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since \(k=2G\), multiplication by \(8^{k+1}\) yields \(8^{k+1}M_3(p)\leq16(8/27)^G\to0\). Given any \(\kappa>0\), a sufficiently large integer \(G\) makes this quantity strictly less than \(\kappa\), disproving the proposed uniform coefficient for arbitrary positive profiles.

Finally, keep every block except the first fixed at \((0,0)\). Let \(u\in D\) have first block \((0,0)\), let \(v\in D\) have first block \((2,2)\), and let \(m=(u+v)/2\in D\), whose first block is \((1,1)\). The original weights at \((u,0),(v,0),(m,0)\) are \(1,1,\epsilon\), respectively, and \(0<\epsilon<1\). Any nonnegative log-concave extension \(F\) of the weights to the convex set \(P\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) would require \(F((m,0))\geq\sqrt{F((u,0))F((v,0))}=1\), contradicting \(F((m,0))=\epsilon\). An extension of the normalized probabilities would instead require \(\epsilon/Z\geq1/Z\), the same contradiction. A positive log-affine extension requires equality in this midpoint relation and is therefore also impossible. The contradiction persists for any convex larger domain containing these three points. Thus the family is outside the log-affine and globally log-concave classes even though it uses the complete lattice support of a convex polytope; it refutes only the asserted extension to arbitrary positive block-sum profiles.
