## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be positive-volume $d$-dimensional convex bodies at $-1,0,1$ with volumes $a,c,b$ and total mixed mass $M=a+c+b$. Suppose two closed through-$(0,y)$ caps of common raw depth $Q$ have nonzero opposite continuous normals after positive rescaling. Write their margins as $t+\alpha z$ and $-t+\beta z$, where $t=u\cdot(x-y)$ and $u\ne0$. If $\alpha+\beta>0$, let $J_B=|B\cap\{-\alpha\le t\le\beta\}|$ and $G_A=|A\cap\{-\beta<t<\alpha\}|$. Then $2Q=M+J_B-G_A$. If $\alpha+\beta<0$, the analogous identity has overlap $J_A=|A\cap\{\alpha\le t\le-\beta\}|$ and gap $G_B=|B\cap\{\beta<t<-\alpha\}|$. If $\alpha+\beta=0$, then $2Q=M$. Always $Q\ge(M-\max(a,b))/2$. If all three masses are at most $M/2$, then $Q\ge M/4\ge\gamma_d M/2$ for $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, with $Q>\gamma_d M/2$ for $d\ge2$.

## Proof

The middle traces are $t\ge0,t\le0$, partitioning $K$ up to a null hyperplane. At $z=1$ the traces are $t\ge-\alpha,t\le\beta$; at $z=-1$ they are $t\ge\alpha,t\le-\beta$. Thus a positive slope sum gives a full cover with overlap at $B$ and disjoint traces with a gap at $A$; a negative slope sum exchanges the roles, and a zero sum gives two more partitions modulo null hyperplanes. Inclusion-exclusion, summed over all three distinct fiber planes, gives the identities, and the gap is at most the full volume of its endpoint. The nonheavy condition gives the bound $M/4$. The binomial expansion of $(1+1/d)^d$ gives $1/\gamma_d\ge2$, strictly for $d\ge2$.
