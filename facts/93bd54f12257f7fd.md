## Statement

Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer, let \(\mathcal I=\{u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_m,v_m\}\), and write \(\lambda(u_i)=\lambda(v_i)=i\). Let \(P\) be the strict partial order defined by \(a<_P b\) if and only if \(\lambda(a)<\lambda(b)\). Thus each pair \(\{u_i,v_i\}\) is incomparable, and every label in an earlier pair is below every label in a later pair. Define its entire ternary census by
$$
D_P=\bigl\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{\mathcal I}:
 x_a=2,\ x_b=0\ \Longrightarrow\ a<_P b\bigr\}.
$$
For any strict total order \(T\) on \(\mathcal I\), put
$$
D_T=\bigl\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^{\mathcal I}:
 x_a=2,\ x_b=0\ \Longrightarrow\ a<_T b\bigr\}.
$$
An allowed component is a set \(E=D_T\cap\prod_{a\in\mathcal I}A_a\) contained in \(D_P\), where every mask \(A_a\) belongs to \(\{\{1\},\{0,1\},\{1,2\},\{0,1,2\}\}\). Its cost is \(4^{-f(E)/3}\), where \(f(E)=\#\{a:A_a=\{1\}\}\). In this class the coordinate projection of \(E\) onto coordinate \(a\) is exactly \(A_a\), so this definition is independent of any alternative representation of the same component.

Allow different total orders for different components and allow all overlaps. The exact minimum weighted cost over finite covers is
$$
\inf_{\substack{N\ge1,\ E_1,\ldots,E_N\text{ allowed}\\
                  D_P=\bigcup_{j=1}^N E_j}}
 \sum_{j=1}^N4^{-f(E_j)/3}=m+1,
$$
and this infimum is attained.

The poset \(P\) has the following interval representation with all \(4m\) endpoints pairwise distinct:
\(J_{u_i}=[4i,4i+2]\) and \(J_{v_i}=[4i+1,4i+3]\), with \(a<_P b\) exactly when the right endpoint of \(J_a\) is strictly smaller than the left endpoint of \(J_b\).
Moreover, the full-dimensional bounded convex polytope
$$
K_P=\bigl\{x\in[0,2]^{\mathcal I}:
 x_a-x_b\le1\text{ for every distinct }a,b
                         \text{ with }\lambda(a)\ge\lambda(b)\bigr\}
$$
has \(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathcal I}=D_P\), and consequently \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathcal I}=D_P\).

For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\), retain the original affine law on this entire support and its single global normalizer:
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in D_P}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z},\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}
          \sum_{y\in D_P\cap H}p_y,\qquad
M_r(p)=\sum_{x\in D_P}p_x\tau_p(x)^r.
$$
All nonzero halfspace normals are permitted, and targets on halfspace boundaries are included. With the exact constant from accepted fact cd2c8174ab54fd1a,
$$
c_{\mathrm{blk}}=
 \frac{\bigl[\log\bigl(1+3\tanh^2(1/32)\bigr)\bigr]^3}{11664}>0,
\qquad
M_2(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{blk}}4^{-2m},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_{\mathrm{blk}}^{3/2}8^{-2m}.
$$
These are the actual ambient depths in \(\mathbb R^{2m}\), with no restriction on the signs, magnitudes, or order of the fields.

## Proof

First, every mask contains 1. For any coordinate \(a\) and any \(t\in A_a\), take the vector having value \(t\) at \(a\) and value 1 at every other coordinate. It belongs to every \(D_T\), because it cannot contain both a 2 and a 0. It also respects all masks. This proves that the coordinate projection of a component is exactly \(A_a\), as claimed. In particular every component is nonempty.

For \(0\le r\le m\), define the cut witness \(x^{(r)}\) by assigning value 2 to both labels in each block of index at most \(r\), and value 0 to both labels in every later block. Every 2-label of \(x^{(r)}\) is strictly below every 0-label in \(P\), so \(x^{(r)}\in D_P\). This includes \(x^{(0)}\), which has no 2, and \(x^{(m)}\), which has no 0. None of these witnesses has a coordinate equal to 1.

Suppose an allowed component \(E=D_T\cap\prod_a A_a\) contains two witnesses \(x^{(r)},x^{(s)}\) with \(r<s\). Choose the block \(k=r+1\), which satisfies \(r<k\le s\). At each of its two coordinates, the first witness has value 0 and the second has value 2. Thus both masks in this block contain 0 and 2, so both are \(\{0,1,2\}\). Let \(a,b\) be its two labels, named so that \(a<_T b\). Define \(z_a=2\), \(z_b=0\), and \(z_c=1\) for every other label \(c\). The masks are respected: the two selected masks are full ternary and every other mask contains 1. The only 2-label of \(z\) is \(a\), and the only 0-label is \(b\), so \(a<_T b\) implies \(z\in D_T\). But \(a,b\) belong to the same incomparable block, hence \(a\not<_P b\) and \(z\notin D_P\). This contradicts \(E\subseteq D_P\). Therefore every allowed component contains at most one cut witness, regardless of its total order.

If a component contains any cut witness, none of its masks can be \(\{1\}\), since that witness has no coordinate equal to 1. Its cost is therefore exactly 1. In any finite cover, choose one covering component for each of the \(m+1\) witnesses. The preceding paragraph makes these chosen components distinct. Their costs sum to \(m+1\), and all other costs are positive. Thus every allowed finite cover has cost at least \(m+1\). This argument permits arbitrary overlaps and arbitrary independent choices of total order.

For the matching upper bound, fix the blockwise total order \(T_0\) given by \(u_1<_{{T_0}}v_1<_{{T_0}}u_2<_{{T_0}}v_2<_{{T_0}}\cdots<_{{T_0}}u_m<_{{T_0}}v_m\). For \(0\le r\le m\), let
$$
B_r=\prod_{\lambda(a)\le r}\{1,2\}
       \times\prod_{\lambda(a)>r}\{0,1\},
$$
where every factor occupies its original labeled coordinate. In a point of \(B_r\), any 2 occurs in a block of index at most \(r\), and any 0 occurs in a block of index greater than \(r\). Thus all such points belong to both \(D_P\) and \(D_{T_0}\). Consequently \(B_r=D_{T_0}\cap B_r\) is an allowed masked-chain component, and its cost is 1.

Take \(x\in D_P\). If it has no 2, then \(x\in B_0\). If it has a 2 but no 0, then \(x\in B_m\). Otherwise let \(r\) be the largest block index containing a 2. The defining condition of \(D_P\) makes every block containing a 0 strictly later than \(r\). Thus all coordinates in blocks of index at most \(r\) belong to \(\{1,2\}\), and all coordinates in later blocks belong to \(\{0,1\}\). Hence \(x\in B_r\). These cases include the all-ones point and both possible empty sets of extreme labels. They prove \(D_P=\bigcup_{r=0}^m B_r\). The total cost is \(m+1\), establishing attainment and the exact minimum. When \(m=1\), this is the cover by \(\{0,1\}^2\) and \(\{1,2\}^2\); the same two-witness lower bound gives its optimum 2.

For the interval representation, the two intervals in block \(i\) overlap on \([4i+1,4i+2]\), so neither lies strictly before the other. Every right endpoint in block \(i\) is at most \(4i+3\), which is strictly below every left endpoint in a later block \(j>i\), since \(4i+3<4j\). No interval in a later block lies strictly before an earlier one. Thus the represented order is exactly \(P\). The endpoints of block \(i\) are the four distinct integers \(4i,4i+1,4i+2,4i+3\); these sets are disjoint across blocks. All endpoints are therefore pairwise distinct.

An integer point in the coordinate box defining \(K_P\) has all values in \(\{0,1,2\}\). At such a point, the inequality \(x_a-x_b\le1\) fails exactly for \(x_a=2,x_b=0\). The inequalities indexed by \(\lambda(a)\ge\lambda(b)\) therefore exclude exactly the 2-to-0 pairs forbidden by \(P\). This proves \(K_P\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathcal I}=D_P\), including boundary points. The point \(\mathbf1\) satisfies all coordinate bounds and difference inequalities strictly, so \(K_P\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{\mathcal I}\); it is bounded and convex by its definition. Finally \(D_P\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\subseteq K_P\), so intersecting with the integer lattice proves \(\operatorname{conv}(D_P)\cap\mathbb Z^{\mathcal I}=D_P\).

For the positive moment calibration, apply accepted fact cd2c8174ab54fd1a with its dimension \(n=2m\), block count \(L=m\), and ordered partition \(I_i=\{2i-1,2i\}\). Identify its coordinate \(2i-1\) with \(u_i\) and coordinate \(2i\) with \(v_i\). Under this coordinate identification, its block map \(\lambda\), support \(D\), and defining polytope \(P\) are exactly the present \(\lambda\), \(D_P\), and \(K_P\), respectively. Its field \(\ell\) is the present arbitrary field, and its entire-support normalizer \(Z\) and probabilities \(p_x=e^{\ell\cdot x}/Z\) are exactly those displayed here. Its \(\tau_p\) takes the infimum over all original ambient containing closed halfspaces, and its \(M_r\) is the same moment; the coordinate identification is a permutation and preserves this entire halfspace class. The constant in that accepted fact is \(c_{\mathrm{blk}}=d_*^3/11664\) with \(d_*=\log(1+3\tanh^2(1/32))\), exactly as displayed. Substituting \(n=2m\) in its two conclusions gives the asserted bounds on the actual \(M_2(p)\) and \(M_3(p)\). No conditioning or replacement of the global normalizer is involved.

Since the exact cover cost is \(m+1\) for every \(m\), no absolute upper bound on that cost exists for this cover class, even on the represented interval-order family. The positive moment calibration simultaneously holds on the same entire supports for every affine field. Thus the unboundedness concerns this cover interface alone and supplies no counterexample to those moment bounds or to the locked weak Oertel conjecture.
