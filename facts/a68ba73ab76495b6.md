## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body. Suppose finitely many proper closed halfspaces with boundaries through one point $y\in K$ cover $\mathbb R^d$ and each intersects $K$ in volume at most $\gamma_d |K|$, where $\gamma_j=(j/(j+1))^j$. Then $y$ is the centroid of $K$, its ordinary normalized Tukey depth is $\gamma_d$, and the largest cap fraction is $\gamma_d$.

## Proof

Write the cover as $H_i=\{x:u_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$ with nonzero $u_i$. The convex hull of its normals contains zero, since strict separation would give a vector strictly negative against every $u_i$ and thus an uncovered point. Choose a minimal subset whose convex hull contains zero. It has a positive convex dependence and its halfspaces still cover all space. If its normals failed to span, a common boundary line through $y$ would contain a point outside $K$, since $K$ is bounded. Applying 2eb9fc1dbf88b7c8 at that point to the same subcover gives a cap fraction at least $\gamma_{d-1}>\gamma_d$, a contradiction.

Let $g$ be the centroid. It lies in the interior: the average of a supporting functional is strictly smaller than its maximum because the strict inequality holds on the interior. The full-volume metronoid consists of $g$, so 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives volume at least $\gamma_d|K|$ in each through-$g$ cap. If $u_i\cdot(g-y)>0$ for a selected normal, moving the cap boundary to $g$ would discard a positive-volume open slab meeting an interior ball about $g$. This contradicts the budget. Thus all selected margins $u_i\cdot(g-y)$ are nonpositive. Their strictly positively weighted sum is zero, hence each is zero, and spanning gives $g=y$. The centroid lower bound makes the ordinary depth at least $\gamma_d$, while the cover contains through-$y$ caps with fraction at most $\gamma_d$, forcing equality of the depth and the largest cap fraction.
