## Statement

Let $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$ and $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\text{ for all }i\}$. For a planar convex body $B\subseteq T$ set $b=|B|$, $S=\sum_i h_B(-v_i)$ and $m=S/3$, where $h$ denotes the support function and $|\cdot|$ ordinary area. Then $0<m\le2$ and $b/|T|\le f(m)$, where $|T|=9/2$ and
$$
 f(m)=\begin{cases}
 m^2&0<m\le1/2,\\
 1-(2-m)^2/3&1/2\le m\le2,\\
 1&m\ge2 .
 \end{cases}
$$
For each feasible $m$ the bound is attained by $T\cap(-mT)$. Moreover $6\sqrt{b/|T|}-S\le3\sqrt7-6<3$, with equality in the first inequality attained at $m=2-3/\sqrt7$.

## Proof

The vertices of $T$ are $(1,1),(-2,1),(1,-2)$, so $|T|=9/2$ and $h_T(-v_i)=2$. For any interior point $p$ of $B$, each $h_B(-v_i)>-v_i\cdot p$. Summing and using $\sum_i v_i=0$ shows $m>0$, and $B\subseteq T$ gives $m\le2$.

The map $R(x,y)=(-x-y,x)$ preserves area, has order three and permutes the three normals by precomposition. The Minkowski average $B_*=(B+RB+R^2B)/3$ lies in $T$ by convexity and invariance. Ordinary planar Brunn–Minkowski on the three compact full-dimensional summands gives $|B_*|^{1/2}\ge b^{1/2}$. Additivity of support functions gives $h_{B_*}(-v_i)=m$ for each $i$, hence $B_*\subseteq T\cap(-mT)$. For $m\le1/2$, $-mT$ is contained in $T$ and has relative area $m^2$. For $1/2\le m\le2$, the constraints $v_i\cdot x\ge-m$ remove three vertex triangles with linear ratio $(2-m)/3$. Their interiors are disjoint since two strict violations would force the third coordinate $v_j\cdot x$ above $2m\ge1$. For $m\ge2$ there is no removal. This proves the formula for the relative area. For $0<m\le2$, the evaluations $(-m,m/2,m/2)$ and their cyclic permutations belong to that intersection, so its three opposite supports are exactly $m$ and it gives equality.

For $m\le1/2$ the excess upper bound $6\sqrt{f(m)}-3m$ is $3m\le3/2$. On the middle interval set $z=2-m$. By Cauchy–Schwarz on $(\sqrt{1-z^2/3},z/\sqrt3)$,
$$6\sqrt{1-z^2/3}+3z-6\le\sqrt{36+27}-6=3\sqrt7-6.$$
Equality is possible at $z=3/\sqrt7\in(0,3/2)$, with $B=T\cap(-mT)$. This bound exceeds $3/2$ and is less than three, by squaring the corresponding positive comparisons.
