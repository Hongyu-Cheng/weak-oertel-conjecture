## Statement

For every integer \(j\ge1\), let \(B_j=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^j\) and let
\(D^{(1)}_j=\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^j:\#\lbrace h:x_h=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace\).
Thus \(D^{(1)}_j\) is the entire one-step ternary support, as in accepted fact \(fc09e20d237c5387\), rather than the chain-domain set denoted \(D_m\) in accepted fact \(032423921efad6c8\).

For every integer \(r\ge2\), set \(m=T=4^r\), \(k=r+m\), \(N=k+1\), and \(D_{\mathrm{old}}=D^{(1)}_r\times D^{(1)}_m\). Write its coordinates as \((u,v)\), where \(u\) is the high-activity block. Let \(E_r\) consist exactly of those points \((u,v)\in D_{\mathrm{old}}\) having one coordinate \(u_i=2\) and one coordinate \(v_j=2\), and satisfying \(u_h=1\) for every \(h<i\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{new}}&=D^{(1)}_k\cup E_r,
&A&=D_{\mathrm{new}}\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subset\mathbb R^N,\\
\beta&=(\underbrace{-\log T,\ldots,-\log T}_{r},
\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{m}),
&w_{(u,v)}&=\exp(-\beta\cdot(u,v))=T^{\sum_{h=1}^r u_h},\\
Z_{\mathrm{old}}&=\sum_{x\in D_{\mathrm{old}}}w_x,
&p^{\mathrm{old}}_x&=w_x/Z_{\mathrm{old}},\\
Z_{\mathrm{new}}&=\sum_{x\in D_{\mathrm{new}}}w_x,
&p_{(x,0)}&=w_x/Z_{\mathrm{new}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Use \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) only as an abbreviation. All these probabilities use the displayed original affine weights, with no change of field.

Then \(B_k\subseteq D_{\mathrm{new}}\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\), every point has at most two coordinates equal to two, and
\(\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathrm{new}})\cap\mathbb Z^k=D_{\mathrm{new}}\).
The points of \(D_{\mathrm{new}}\) having at most one two are exactly \(D^{(1)}_k\). For each Boolean parity source \(b\in B_k\), form the graph whose vertices are the zero coordinates of \(b\), and whose edges are the sets \(\lbrace a,c\rbrace\) for which \(b+2e_a+2e_c\in D_{\mathrm{new}}\). All edges of this graph share one vertex whenever the graph is nonempty. More precisely, that vertex is the first zero of the high block of \(b\), and its neighbors are exactly the zero coordinates of the neutral block. This verifies the paritywise-star hypothesis, including empty edge families and isolated vertices.

Define the original scores by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\sum_{x\in D^{(1)}_k}p_x,
&S_4&=\sum_{x\in D_{\mathrm{new}}}p_x^4,\\
L_B&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D_{\mathrm{new}},\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2\\(x+y)/2\in B_k}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sum defining \(L_B\) is ordered. The counterfamily satisfies
\(\alpha+\max_{1\le h\le k}p(x_h=2)+2^{k/3}L_B+8^kS_4\longrightarrow0\)
as \(r\to\infty\). Consequently no positive absolute constant lower-bounds this sum on complete paritywise-star max-two supports with the full one-step core.

For \(z\in A\), let \(\tau_p(z)\) be the infimum of the original \(p\)-mass of a closed affine halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\) containing \(z\), and put \(M_3(p)=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3\). For \(c\in D_{\mathrm{new}}\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D_{\mathrm{new}}\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),\\
T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D_{\mathrm{new}}}p_c\,s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus \(s_p\) counts ordered reflected pairs, with the diagonal once. The notation \(T(p)\) denotes this functional, whereas the scalar parameter above is \(T=m\). On this same original law, for every \(r\ge2\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{128}\,8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge\frac1{1024}\,8^{-k}.
$$
Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal. Allowing the whole space as well leaves the depths and all conclusions unchanged. The moment and reflection normalizations agree exactly with accepted fact \(f27ee487069e27e7\).

## Proof

First observe that the points of \(D_{\mathrm{old}}\) with at most one two form exactly \(D^{(1)}_k\). Indeed, every ternary point with at most one two has at most one two in each block and hence belongs to the product. The remaining product points have exactly one two in each block. Therefore \(D^{(1)}_k\subseteq D_{\mathrm{new}}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{old}}\), and the displayed description of the full core and the max-two property follow.

In fact any choice of a subset of the double points, together with the full one-step core, is complete. To prove this directly for the present support, take \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathrm{new}})\cap\mathbb Z^k\), and express it as a convex combination of generators with positive coefficients. The hull lies in \([0,2]^k\), so \(x\) is ternary. Whenever \(x_h=2\), every positively weighted generator has coordinate \(h\) equal to two, because two is the largest possible generating coordinate. Consequently \(x\) cannot have three coordinates equal to two: all generators would then have three such coordinates, contrary to their max-two property. If \(x\) has zero or one coordinate equal to two, it belongs to the retained full core. If it has exactly two, all positively weighted generators have those same two coordinates equal to two, and their remaining coordinates are binary. Each remaining coordinate of \(x\) is an integer in \([0,1]\). An average of binary entries can equal such an integer only when every positive summand equals that integer. Hence all positively weighted generators equal \(x\), and \(x\in D_{\mathrm{new}}\). The opposite inclusion is immediate. This proves completeness. The hull contains \([0,1]^k\), so it is also full-dimensional in its base space.

For the star condition, write a parity source as \(b=(a,c)\in B_r\times B_m\). Its retained one-step points are \(b\) and \(b+2e_h\) for every zero coordinate \(h\) of \(b\). A possible double point of the old product in this parity class is exactly \(b+2e_i+2e_{r+j}\), where \(a_i=0\) and \(c_j=0\). Its retained-point condition is \(a_h=1\) for all \(h<i\), so \(i\) must be the smallest high index with \(a_i=0\). If the high block has no zero, there are no double edges. Otherwise call its first zero \(i_0\). The double-edge family is precisely
\(\lbrace\lbrace i_0,r+j\rbrace:c_j=0\rbrace\).
It is empty if the neutral block has no zero, and otherwise all its edges share \(i_0\). This describes every parity class and proves the required star property exactly.

We next check the original partition functions and the effect of pruning. Set
\(Z_H=(1+T)^r+rT^2(1+T)^{r-1}\) and
\(Z_L=2^m+m2^{m-1}=2^m(1+m/2)\).
The high-block Boolean points contribute \((1+T)^r\); for each choice of its unique two-coordinate, the high block contributes \(T^2(1+T)^{r-1}\). The neutral block has \(2^m\) Boolean points and \(m2^{m-1}\) one-two points, each of weight one. Since the original weight depends only on the high block, direct summation gives \(Z_{\mathrm{old}}=Z_HZ_L\). This is exactly the old normalization in accepted fact \(fc09e20d237c5387\), with its \(D=D_{\mathrm{old}}\), \(Z=Z_{\mathrm{old}}\), and \(p_x=p^{\mathrm{old}}_x\), and with identical \(r,m,T,k,N,\beta\).

For \(1\le i\le r\), let \(h^{(i)}\in D^{(1)}_r\) have coordinate two at \(i\) and coordinate one at every other high index, and put \(G_i=\lbrace h^{(i)}\rbrace\times D^{(1)}_m\). These are pairwise disjoint. Their points with Boolean neutral block lie in the full core; their points with a neutral two satisfy every required preceding-high-coordinate condition. Thus each entire \(G_i\) is retained. Every point of \(G_i\) has original weight \(T^{r+1}\).

Let \(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}\) be the old mass of the union of these sections. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}
&=\frac{rT^{r+1}Z_L}{Z_{\mathrm{old}}}
=\frac{rT^{r+1}}{Z_H},\\
\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}^{-1}
&=(1+T^{-1})^{r-1}
\left(1+\frac{1+T}{rT^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular \(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}\to1\): the logarithm of the first factor lies between zero and \((r-1)/T\to0\), and the second factor tends to one.

Here is also a uniform bound valid from \(r=2\). The inequality \(4^r\ge8r\) holds at \(r=2\) and is preserved when \(r\) increases by one, since \(4r\ge r+1\). Thus \((r-1)/T\le1/8\). For an integer \(n\ge1\) with \(n/T<1\), the binomial theorem gives
\((1+T^{-1})^n\le\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(n/T)^\ell\le(1-n/T)^{-1}\).
Taking \(n=r-1\) bounds the first factor of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}^{-1}\) by \(8/7\). Since \(r\ge2\), \(T\ge16\), and \(T+1\le rT\), its second factor is at most \(1+1/T\le17/16\). Therefore
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}^{-1}\le17/14\), so
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}\ge14/17>1/2\).

Put \(\rho=Z_{\mathrm{new}}/Z_{\mathrm{old}}\). The inclusion \(D_{\mathrm{new}}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{old}}\) and retention of all the \(G_i\), using the same positive weights, imply
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}\le\rho\le1\).
Hence \(\rho\to1\), and for every retained point the exact normalization identity is \(p_x=p^{\mathrm{old}}_x/\rho\).

Define \(\alpha_{\mathrm{old}},L_{B,\mathrm{old}},S_{4,\mathrm{old}}\) by the displayed score definitions with old support and old probabilities. Their core is the same entire \(D^{(1)}_k\). Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\rho^{-1}\alpha_{\mathrm{old}},\\
\max_{1\le h\le k}p(x_h=2)
&\le\rho^{-1}\max_{1\le h\le k}p^{\mathrm{old}}(x_h=2),\\
L_B&\le\rho^{-4/3}L_{B,\mathrm{old}},\\
S_4&\le\rho^{-4}S_{4,\mathrm{old}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first equality, sum the exact normalization identity over the core, all of which is retained. For each coordinate face, its new summation set is a subset of its old summation set; summing the same identity and then taking the maximum proves the second inequality. The ordered-pair set defining the new \(L_B\) is a subset of the old one, because the parity and Boolean-midpoint conditions are unchanged. On each retained pair both endpoint probabilities have the same positive multiplier \(\rho^{-1}\). Its summand therefore has multiplier \(\rho^{-(7/6+1/6)}=\rho^{-4/3}\), which proves the third inequality without changing which endpoint is smaller. The last inequality follows by summing \(p_x^4=\rho^{-4}(p^{\mathrm{old}}_x)^4\) over a subset of old support. Every summand used in these comparisons is nonnegative.

Under the precise old normalization identified above, accepted fact \(fc09e20d237c5387\) proves that each of
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{old}}\),
\(\max_h p^{\mathrm{old}}(x_h=2)\),
\(2^{k/3}L_{B,\mathrm{old}}\), and
\(8^kS_{4,\mathrm{old}}\)
tends to zero. Since \(\rho\to1\), the four checked comparisons prove the claimed vanishing of all four new scores.

It remains to bound the actual moment and full ordered-reflection cubic on this same new law directly. Let \(\nu\) be the uniform original law on \(D^{(1)}_m\), with \(\nu_v=1/Z_L\), embedded on \(D^{(1)}_m\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subset\mathbb R^{m+1}\). Let \(\tau_\nu,M_3(\nu),s_\nu,T(\nu)\) use precisely the depth and ordered-reflection definitions of the statement in this residual space. The initial one-step realization assertion in accepted fact \(032423921efad6c8\), with every one-step extension included, proves completeness of \(D^{(1)}_m\). It is finite and nonempty, has at most one two per point, and occupies all \(2^m\) parity classes. The final one-step assertion of accepted fact \(f27ee487069e27e7\), applied with its dimension \(k=m\), its field \(\beta=0\), its partition function \(Z=Z_L\), and its original probability \(p=\nu\), therefore yields
$$
M_3(\nu)\ge\frac1{512}\,8^{-m},
\qquad
T(\nu)\ge\frac1{512}\,8^{-m}.
$$
No theorem for a different conditional normalization is being used here.

Let \(\lambda_i=p(G_i)\) and \(\lambda_G=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\). Direct summation of the unchanged weights on the retained sections gives
$$
\lambda_i=\frac{T^{r+1}Z_L}{Z_{\mathrm{new}}}
=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}}{r\rho},
\qquad
\lambda_G=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}}{\rho}\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{old}}\ge\frac12.
$$
In particular all section masses are equal to \(\lambda_G/r\). For the embedding
\(\iota_i:\mathbb R^{m+1}\to\mathbb R^{k+1}\),
\(\iota_i(y,t)=(h^{(i)},y,t)\), the exact original conditional identity is
\(p_{\iota_i(v,0)}=\lambda_i\nu_v\).

To verify the depth comparison in the original ambient space, take any containing closed affine halfspace with nonzero normal, written
\(H=\lbrace(x,y,t):a\cdot x+b\cdot y+ct\ge s\rbrace\),
where \(\iota_i(v,0)\in H\). Its pullback is
\(\lbrace(y,t):b\cdot y+ct\ge s-a\cdot h^{(i)}\rbrace\).
If \((b,c)\ne0\), this is a residual closed affine halfspace containing \((v,0)\), so its \(\nu\)-mass is at least \(\tau_\nu((v,0))\). If \((b,c)=0\), containment forces \(s-a\cdot h^{(i)}\le0\); the pullback is the entire residual space and has mass one, again at least the residual depth. Thus no containing ambient halfspace has empty pullback, including when all its nonzero coefficients occur in the fixed high block. Conversely every residual halfspace with nonzero normal extends to an ambient halfspace by putting zero on the high coefficients, so its normal remains nonzero and the conditional depth is exactly the residual depth. Adding the whole space to either halfspace convention has no effect because its probability is one. Using the conditional identity and retaining only section mass in each ambient halfspace proves
\(\tau_p(\iota_i(v,0))\ge\lambda_i\tau_\nu((v,0))\).
Averaging only over that section then gives
$$
\sum_{v\in D^{(1)}_m}p_{\iota_i(v,0)}
\tau_p(\iota_i(v,0))^3
\ge\lambda_i^4M_3(\nu).
$$
The fourth power accounts for the original sampling probability as well as the three depth factors.

For the reflection comparison, fix \(v\in D^{(1)}_m\). Each ordered residual reflected pair \((v_1,v_2)\), with \(v_1+v_2=2v\), maps injectively to the ordered full-support pair \(((h^{(i)},v_1),(h^{(i)},v_2))\) centered at \((h^{(i)},v)\). All these endpoints are retained. Their minimum original probability is exactly \(\lambda_i\min(\nu_{v_1},\nu_{v_2})\). This includes the diagonal once and preserves both orientations of every distinct pair. Keeping just these terms in the full nonnegative reflection sum proves
\(s_p((h^{(i)},v))\ge\lambda_i s_\nu(v)\), and hence
$$
\sum_{v\in D^{(1)}_m}p_{(h^{(i)},v)}
s_p((h^{(i)},v))^3
\ge\lambda_i^4T(\nu).
$$
This uses the full original ordered-reflection sum and makes no claim that other reflected pairs are absent.

The sections \(G_i\) are disjoint, so their sampling contributions can be added in both cubics. The residual bounds and equal section masses therefore imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)&\ge\frac1{512}\,8^{-m}\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i^4
=\frac{\lambda_G^4}{512r^3}\,8^{-m}
\ge\frac1{8192r^3}\,8^{-m},\\
T(p)&\ge\frac1{512}\,8^{-m}\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i^4
=\frac{\lambda_G^4}{512r^3}\,8^{-m}
\ge\frac1{8192r^3}\,8^{-m}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For integers \(r\ge2\), one has \(8^r/r^3\ge8\): equality holds at \(r=2\), and the ratio of successive values is \(8(r/(r+1))^3\ge64/27>1\). Since \(k=m+r\), the common bound is consequently at least
\((1/1024)8^{-k}\). For the actual moment this equals \((1/128)8^{-(k+1)}=(1/128)8^{-N}\); for the reflection functional it is the asserted \((1/1024)8^{-k}\). Both calibrations were proved on the original pruned distribution, without a continuity or total-variation inference.
