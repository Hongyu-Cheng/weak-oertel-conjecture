## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted facts `3100bf8082ca09d5` and `d41b002f3091f5c4`. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z:a_z>0}a_z>0,
 \qquad
 \gamma_k:=\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^k,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
and \(G:=\mathfrak C_{\rm core}(C)\) and the half-strength canonical product-reservoir mass \(Q>0\) satisfy the canonical hard inequality. In particular,
\[
 G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(y\in E_\perp\), let \(\tau_y>0\) and \(p_y\in\mathsf M_{\tau_y}(C_y)\) be the pooled endpoint weight and pooled endpoint point of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`. Then
\[
 \tau_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4},
\tag{3}
\]
and the single measure
\[
 \nu_E:=\gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_\perp}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}
\tag{4}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\).

For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), retain the reflected endpoint-pair family
\[
 \mathcal P_m:=
 \left\{
  \{u,v\}\subseteq E_\perp:
  u\ne v,\ u+v=2m
 \right\}
\tag{5}
\]
from accepted fact `d41b002f3091f5c4`. Fix any \(m\) with \(\mathcal P_m\ne\varnothing\), and fix a nonempty subfamily \(J\subseteq\mathcal P_m\). For \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\), put
\[
 t_e:=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\},
 \qquad
 u_e:=\sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v},
 \qquad
 q_e^{\mathrm p}:=\frac{p_u+p_v}{2},
\tag{6}
\]
and define
\[
 r_{\mathrm p}:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{q_e^{\mathrm p}:e\in J\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Then the positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_J^{\mathrm p}:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in J}t_e
 \delta_{(m,q_e^{\mathrm p})}}
\tag{8}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_J^{\mathrm p}(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{9}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Consequently, the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_d2^n
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}t_e}{r_{\mathrm p}+1}.}
\tag{10}
\]

For every positive-volume fiber, let
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx
\tag{11}
\]
be its centroid. For \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\), put
\[
 q_e^{\mathrm c}:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},
 \qquad
 r_{\mathrm c}:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{q_e^{\mathrm c}:e\in J\}.
\tag{12}
\]
In the exact accepted normalization of \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, one has
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)
 \geq
 4\gamma_{2d}2^n
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}t_e}{r_{\mathrm c}+1}.}
\tag{13}
\]

Let \(K\geq1\), and suppose that every \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\) satisfies
\[
 \max\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}
 \leq K\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{t_e\geq\frac{u_e}{\sqrt K}}
 \qquad(e\in J),
\tag{15}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \frac{\gamma_d2^n}{\sqrt K}
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}u_e}{r_{\mathrm p}+1},}
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{4\gamma_{2d}2^n}{\sqrt K}
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}u_e}{r_{\mathrm c}+1}.}
\tag{17}
\]
For the midpoint supplied by accepted fact `d41b002f3091f5c4`, if (14) holds on \(J=\mathcal P_m\), then \(\sum_{e\in J}u_e=S_m>73M_m\), so (16)--(17) also give
\[
 D>
 \frac{73\gamma_d2^nM_m}
 {\sqrt K\,(r_{\mathrm p}+1)},
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)>
 \frac{292\gamma_{2d}2^nM_m}
 {\sqrt K\,(r_{\mathrm c}+1)}.
\tag{18}
\]

Finally, let
\[
 V(J):=\bigcup_{e\in J}e,
 \qquad
 B_J:=\operatorname{aff}V(J),
 \qquad
 q:=\dim B_J,
 \qquad
 U_J:=\sum_{e\in J}u_e.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(q\leq2\), define
\[
 \alpha_0:=1,
 \qquad
 \alpha_1:=\frac14,
 \qquad
 \alpha_2:=\frac1{64}.
\tag{20}
\]
Then the rank-two complete-core theorem of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c`, combined with the canonical hard branch, gives the affine endpoint-span absorption bound
\[
 \boxed{
 U_J\leq
 \frac{2^{q-n}G}{8\alpha_q}.}
\tag{21}
\]

All assertions (8)--(18) concern one fixed midpoint \(m\). They do not authorize summing the corresponding measures or capacity scores over different midpoints, because endpoint-disjointness need not persist across different reflected families. Neither \(q\leq2\) nor endpoint-disjointness bounds \(r_{\mathrm p}\) or \(r_{\mathrm c}\) when \(d\) is unbounded. In particular, the theorem proves neither an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality nor the locked final theorem.

## Proof

Every pair in \(\mathcal P_m\) is endpoint-disjoint from every other pair in \(\mathcal P_m\). Indeed, reflection about \(m\) sends an endpoint \(u\) to its unique possible partner \(2m-u\). This is also the endpoint-disjointness conclusion of accepted fact `d41b002f3091f5c4`.

We first prove (8)--(10). For \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\), convexity of \(C\) and \(u+v=2m\) give
\[
 \left(m,q_e^{\mathrm p}\right)
 =\frac12(u,p_u)+\frac12(v,p_v)
 \in C.
\tag{22}
\]
The base \(m\) is integral, so every atom of \(\sigma_J^{\mathrm p}\) lies in the mixed-integer part of \(C\).

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed halfspace. If the midpoint atom \((m,q_e^{\mathrm p})\) lies in \(H\), at least one of \((u,p_u)\) and \((v,p_v)\) lies in \(H\). This follows by evaluating an affine functional defining \(H\) at the identity (22). For each edge whose midpoint atom lies in \(H\), orient that edge toward one such endpoint. Endpoint-disjointness makes all chosen endpoints distinct. Since \(t_e\leq\tau_y\) at either endpoint \(y\) of \(e\), equation (4) and its accepted halfspace domination give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_J^{\mathrm p}(H)
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e\in J:\
        (m,q_e^{\mathrm p})\in H}}t_e\\
 &\leq
 \gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\text{ chosen}:\\(y,p_y)\in H}}
 \tau_y
 \leq\nu_E(H)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
This proves (9), including coincident midpoint atoms and halfspaces whose boundary contains one or more atoms.

The measure in (8) is nonzero and has mass
\[
 |\sigma_J^{\mathrm p}|=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in J}t_e.
\tag{24}
\]
Its base support is the singleton \(\{m\}\), so its intrinsic base rank is zero. By the coordinate-free residual-rank identity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`, its continuous residual rank is exactly
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{q_e^{\mathrm p}:e\in J\}=r_{\mathrm p}.
\tag{25}
\]
Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem in that accepted fact to (9). Equations (24)--(25) give
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d\frac{\sum_{e\in J}t_e}{r_{\mathrm p}+1}.
\tag{26}
\]
Multiplication by \(2^n\) proves (10).

We next prove the centroid-cloud screen (13). Equation (3) and positivity of every \(\tau_y\) show that every endpoint fiber used by \(J\) has positive \(d\)-volume, so its centroid (11) is defined. Fix one \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\). Each of the convex bodies \(C_u,C_v\) contains a nonempty open ball. The average of two such balls is a nonempty open ball contained in
\[
 \frac12(C_u+C_v)\subseteq C_m.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus \(a_m>0\), as required in the definition of the reflected-cloud capacity.

Distinct unordered pairs in \(J\) correspond to distinct reflected directions \(\{v,-v\}\) about \(m\). For such a pair, the reflected component in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) has centroid \(q_e^{\mathrm c}\) and coefficient
\[
 \min\{a_u,a_v\}
 \geq4\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}
 =4t_e,
\tag{28}
\]
where (3) gives the inequality. In the supremum defining \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, use the fixed midpoint \(m\), take precisely the reflected subcloud indexed by \(J\), and omit the central component. Its affine centroid rank is \(r_{\mathrm c}\). Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)
 &\geq
 \gamma_{2d}2^n
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}\min\{a_u,a_v\}}
 {r_{\mathrm c}+1}\\
 &\geq
 4\gamma_{2d}2^n
 \frac{\sum_{e\in J}t_e}
 {r_{\mathrm c}+1},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
which is (13). This uses exactly the factor \(2^n\) in the accepted capacity normalization and the reflected-component constant \(\gamma_{2d}\).

Suppose (14) holds. For one edge, abbreviate
\[
 a:=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}=t_e,
 \qquad
 b:=\max\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}\leq Ka.
\]
Then
\[
 u_e=\sqrt{ab}\leq\sqrt K\,a=\sqrt K\,t_e,
\tag{30}
\]
which proves (15). Summing (15) and substituting it into (10) and (13) proves (16)--(17). For the distinguished midpoint of accepted fact `d41b002f3091f5c4`, its definitions give
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal P_m}u_e=S_m>73M_m.
\tag{31}
\]
Use (31) in (16)--(17) to obtain (18).

It remains to prove (21). We first record the exact reservoir upper bound. In a good parity class \(\xi\), accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` defines
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi},
 \qquad
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z.
\tag{32}
\]
Hence the reservoir mass in that class is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_\xi
 &=\frac1{4A_\xi}
 \sum_{u<v}a_ua_v\\
 &=\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}
 {8A_\xi}
 \leq\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
Bad classes contribute zero. Summing (33) over the good classes gives
\[
 Q\leq\frac W8.
\tag{34}
\]
Together with the strict hard bound (2), this yields
\[
 G<\frac W{800}.
\tag{35}
\]

Assume now that \(q=\dim B_J\leq2\), and put
\[
 D_J:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\cap B_J,
 \qquad
 W_J:=\sum_{z\in D_J}a_z.
\tag{36}
\]
Every endpoint in \(V(J)\) belongs to \(D_J\), by (3). Since those endpoints affinely span \(B_J\),
\[
 \operatorname{aff}D_J=B_J,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{aff}D_J=q.
\tag{37}
\]
Apply accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` to the affine slice \(A=B_J\). If \(B_J=\mathbb R^n\), then \(q=n\leq2\), and its ambient conclusion gives
\[
 G\geq\alpha_qW_J
 =2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_J.
\tag{38}
\]
If \(B_J\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\), its per-side augmentation conclusion gives
\[
 G\geq
 \min\left\{
  \alpha_qW_J+\frac{W-W_J}{2},
  \ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_J
 \right\}.
\tag{39}
\]
For \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), one has \(1/64\leq\alpha_q\leq1\). In the proper-subspace case here, \(q<n\), while a nonempty reflected pair in fact gives \(q\geq1\); in particular \(\alpha_q\leq1/2\). Therefore the first entry of the minimum in (39) obeys
\[
 \alpha_qW_J+\frac{W-W_J}{2}
 \geq\alpha_qW
 \geq\frac W{64}
 >G,
\tag{40}
\]
where the strict last inequality is (35). If the second entry in (39) were also greater than \(G\), equation (39) would be impossible. Hence
\[
 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_J\leq G.
\tag{41}
\]
Equation (41) also follows from (38) in the full-space case. Thus, in all cases,
\[
 W_J\leq\frac{2^{q-n}G}{\alpha_q}.
\tag{42}
\]

For each \(e=\{u,v\}\in J\), equation (3) and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
 u_e=\sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}
 \leq\frac14\sqrt{a_ua_v}
 \leq\frac{a_u+a_v}{8}.
\tag{43}
\]
The pairs in \(J\) are endpoint-disjoint. Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_J
 &=\sum_{e\in J}u_e
 \leq\frac18\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in J}(a_u+a_v)\\
 &=\frac18\sum_{y\in V(J)}a_y
 \leq\frac{W_J}{8}
 \leq\frac{2^{q-n}G}{8\alpha_q}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
This is (21). The proof never combines endpoint orientations from different midpoints, and none of (25), (29), or (37) bounds either continuous affine rank. This proves every claimed screen and all stated limitations.
