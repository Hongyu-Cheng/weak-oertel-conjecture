## Statement

Use the compact-rate finite geometric cell hypotheses of the positive-rate inverse slice theorem, and fix $o\in\operatorname{int}K$. Let $\widehat b$ be the restriction to $R>0$ of any simultaneous joint weak limit of
$(-\log L_j)_+\,dS_j\,\delta_{r_j}$ on boundary normal, physical position and rate, where $dS_j$ records the actual outward unit normal $n$. Let $\widehat\zeta_i$ be the positive-rate part of the geometric cell inverse limit. Put $h(n,X)=n\cdot(X-o)$ and $b_i=A_i(o)>0$. Then on the positive-rate range
$$
M\sum_i b_i\widehat\zeta_i(dX,dr)
=\left[\int_{R\ge r}\frac{h(n,X)}{R}\,\widehat b(dn,dX,dR)\right]\,dr.
$$
Every cell has no atom at any fixed positive rate. Extend its post-threshold conditional $\gamma_i$ by zero below $R_i$. The weighted total $M\sum_i b_i\gamma_i(r)$ has a decreasing version in measure order, and at every $R>0$ its one-sided limits give
$$
\sum_{R_i=R}b_i\gamma_i(R+)
\le\sum_{R_i<R}b_i\bigl(\gamma_i(R-)-\gamma_i(R+)\bigr)
$$
as spatial measures. For a single affine plane, the surface tail in brackets is decreasing and is $1/(d-1)$-concave on positive-mass compact sets for almost every $r>0$.

## Proof

Use the common compact rate interval $[0,R_*]$. For large $j$, $K_j$ contains a fixed ball around $o$, so $n\cdot(X-o)$ is uniformly bounded below on its actual boundary. Write $\ell_j=(-\log L_j)_+$. The reciprocal bound yields $\int_{K_j}\ell_j\le \int_{K_j}L_j^{-1}\le M_jC_{\rm inv}$. Bounded affine slopes also give a uniform $\int_{K_j}|\nabla\ell_j|\le \max_i|c_{ij}|\int_{K_j}L_j^{-1}$, where $c_{ij}$ is the gradient of $A_{ij}$. Gauss–Green applied to $\ell_j(X-o)$ therefore gives a uniform boundary $\ell_j$ mass bound. Every boundary limit normal is a genuine support normal at its limiting point, by convergence of support functions, so $h$ remains positive. At any positive-rate limiting position, $L=0$ by uniform coefficient convergence. For the cell inverse measures one also has $A_i=L=0$ on this range.

Take any continuous $f$ on the compact rate interval, vanishing on a neighborhood of zero, and put $g(R)=R^{-1}\int_0^R f(r)\,dr$ for $R>0$ and $g(0)=0$. This is $C^1$, vanishes near zero and satisfies $g+Rg'=f$. For a $C^1$ physical scalar test $\phi$, use the vector field $\phi(X)(X-o)\ell_j g(r_j)$ on $K_j$. It is Lipschitz for fixed $j$. Its derivative of the last factor is $-f(r_j)c_{ij}/A_{ij}$ almost everywhere on the geometric cell $C_{ij}$, including zero on $r_j=0$. Because the original field is continuous through cell interfaces, the full-body application of Gauss–Green has no cell-interface term. The bulk term containing $\operatorname{div}(\phi(X)(X-o))$ tends to zero. Indeed $g$ vanishes for $r_j<\epsilon$ for some $\epsilon>0$, and $\ell_j\mathbf1_{\{\ell_j\ge\epsilon/\lambda_j\}}\le\sup_{t\ge\epsilon/\lambda_j}(t e^{-t})/L_j$. Thus passing to the joint limits and using $-c_i\cdot(X-o)=A_i(o)$ on the positive-rate support of each cell gives
$$
\int \phi(X)h(n,X)g(R)\,d\widehat b
=M\sum_i b_i\int \phi(X)f(r)\,d\widehat\zeta_i.
$$
Substitute the definition of $g$ and use Fubini. Density of smooth physical tests and continuous rate tests supported away from zero proves the stated identity on $r>0$. The radial weight is applied to the full surface limit; no plane- or source-labeled boundary allocation is used.

Positivity of each $b_i$ and of the component measures gives the absence of positive-rate atoms for every cell. The identity gives the weighted total by the slice theorem, and its tail version is decreasing. At any positive threshold the total is locally bounded (the tail has mass at most $\int h\,d\widehat b/r$). Since all weights are positive, each component has finite one-sided weak limits, including its right limit at its own threshold, by its post-threshold monotonicity and the total bound. The difference of the two total limits is a positive measure. Taking the finite sum of component differences gives the displayed compensation inequality. In the one-plane case the identity makes the tail a positive constant times the affine conditional, so the asserted concavity follows.
