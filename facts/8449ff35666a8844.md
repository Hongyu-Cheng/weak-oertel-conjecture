## Statement

Fix any real number \(0\le\sigma<\log 2\), where \(\log\) is the natural logarithm. For every \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(J\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(\epsilon\ge0\) satisfying \(\epsilon d\le\sigma n\), put \(Q:=\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and use the locked notation \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\). Write \(C_{\mathbf j}:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf j,x)\in C\}\). Assume \(\lambda_d(C_{\mathbf j})=0\) for every \(\mathbf j\notin J+Q\); these other fibers may be nonempty.

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) be any compact full-dimensional convex body, and let \(f:Q\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfy

\[
f(z)+K\subseteq C_{J+z}\subseteq f(z)+(1+\epsilon)K
\qquad(z\in Q).
\]

No centering, smoothness, symmetry, kurtosis bound, or covariance-radius bound is assumed for \(K\), and no affinity, parity, or regularity is assumed for \(f\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
v&:=\lambda_d(K),&
m_K&:=\frac1v\int_K u\,du,&
\Gamma&:=\frac1v\int_K(u-m_K)(u-m_K)^{\mathsf T}\,du,\\
K_0&:=K-m_K,&
f_0(z)&:=f(z)+m_K,&
R_K&:=\max_{u\in K}\|\Gamma^{-1/2}(u-m_K)\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The matrix \(\Gamma\) is positive definite. The notation \(R_K\) here means the centered covariance radius, equivalently the radius of \(K_0\). In the application of accepted fact 8d52b9903d517dab, its stock \(K\) and its function \(f\) are respectively \(K_0\) and \(f_0\) here. Its \(\Gamma,R_K,J,Q\) have the same meanings after this map.

Let \(e_i\) be the standard basis vectors, put \(b_0:=f_0(0)\), and define \(Ae_i:=(f_0(e_i)-f_0(-e_i))/2=(f(e_i)-f(-e_i))/2\). Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
L&:=\max_{z\in Q}\|\Gamma^{-1/2}(f_0(z)-Az-b_0)\|_2,\\
M&:=3^n v,\qquad V:=\mathcal H_d(S),\qquad W:=V,\\
\mathbf y^\star&:=\left(J,m_K+3^{-n}\sum_{z\in Q}f(z)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(V\) is the canonical original total mass, \(W\) is its assignment alias, and \(M\) is the retained-stock mass. Define \(\beta_\sigma:=\log 2-\sigma>0\), which is the scalar called \(\beta\) in the proposed exponential calibration, not a halfspace-normal component. Then the exact coefficient

\[
c(\sigma):=\min\left\{\frac1{2e},
\frac{(e\beta_\sigma/10)^{10}}
{512\bigl[5072+4(3e\sigma)^4\bigr]}\right\}
\]

belongs to \((0,1/e)\), depends only on \(\sigma\), and satisfies

\[
\mathbf y^\star\in S,\qquad
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c(\sigma)2^{-n}W.
\]

More precisely, with \(\kappa:=5072\), the following estimates hold:

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<M&\le W\le(1+\epsilon)^dM,&
\frac MW&\ge e^{-\sigma n},\\
L&\le2n\epsilon R_K\le3e\sigma n^2,&
512\bigl(\kappa+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)
&\le512\bigl[5072+4(3e\sigma)^4\bigr]n^{10},\\
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
&\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(5072+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These assertions include \(\sigma=0\), \(\epsilon=0\), \(d=1\), and \(n=1\).

## Proof

Apply accepted fact f1d59e746de9db52 to the original convex body \(K\). Its centroid \(m\) is our \(m_K\), and its covariance is our \(\Gamma\). Its dummy direction \(b\) can be denoted \(\xi\) here and is not the vector \(b_0\). That fact gives positive definiteness and, for every \(\xi\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
\frac1v\int_K\bigl(\xi\cdot(u-m_K)\bigr)^4\,du
\le5072(\xi^{\mathsf T}\Gamma\xi)^2,
\qquad R_K\le\frac{e(d+2)}2.
\]

These are conclusions for every such \(K\), including all its boundary points and \(d=1\), not additional assumptions. Translating the uniform law by \(-m_K\) gives uniform probability on \(K_0\), with centroid zero, the same covariance, and the same fourth-moment and radius bounds. The budget \(\sigma\) in this theorem is unrelated to the directional standard deviation used locally in the proof of f1d59e746de9db52.

Centering preserves each retained stock exactly: \(f_0(z)+K_0=f(z)+K\). It does not preserve the upper sandwich with the same parameter. The actual centered upper inclusion is

\[
C_{J+z}\subseteq f_0(z)+(1+\epsilon)K_0+\epsilon m_K.
\]

We retain this shift rather than silently dropping it. We first control the nonlinear translation error directly using the original, uncentered sandwich.

For \(\epsilon>0\), the sandwich implies \(K\subseteq(1+\epsilon)K\). Consequently, if \(u\in K\), every \(u/(1+\epsilon)^k\) belongs to \(K\), and closedness gives \(0\in K\). By the definition of \(R_K\), this implies \(\|\Gamma^{-1/2}m_K\|_2\le R_K\). For every \(u,u'\in K\), therefore,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Gamma^{-1/2}u\|_2&\le2R_K,\\
\|\Gamma^{-1/2}(u-u')\|_2&\le2R_K.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second inequality does not require \(0\in K\), since the centroid cancels. No conclusion \(0\in K\) is needed when \(\epsilon=0\).

For every legal reflected triple \(z,z+v',z-v'\in Q\), define
\(t_{z,v'}:=(f(z+v')+f(z-v'))/2-f(z)\). The same expression using \(f_0\) is equal to \(t_{z,v'}\). For each \(u\in K\), convexity of \(C\), applied to the two retained points at bases \(J+z\pm v'\), shows that
\((f(z+v')+f(z-v'))/2+u\in C_{J+z}\). Hence \(t_{z,v'}+K\subseteq(1+\epsilon)K\). Taking support functions gives
\(p\cdot t_{z,v'}\le\epsilon\max_{u\in K}p\cdot u\) for every \(p\in\mathbb R^d\). For \(\epsilon>0\), the support characterization of a closed convex body yields \(t_{z,v'}\in\epsilon K\). For \(\epsilon=0\), taking \(p=t_{z,v'}\) yields \(t_{z,v'}=0\).

Use \(\|x\|_\Gamma:=\|\Gamma^{-1/2}x\|_2\). The preceding bounds imply, for any two legal defects,

\[
\|t_{z,v'}\|_\Gamma\le2\epsilon R_K,
\qquad
\|t_{z,v'}-t_{z',v''}\|_\Gamma\le2\epsilon R_K.
\]

For positive \(\epsilon\), the second bound follows because the difference belongs to \(\epsilon(K-K)\); for zero \(\epsilon\), both defects vanish. Using the difference bound rather than adding the two individual bounds is essential to the stated constant.

Define the odd part \(o(z):=(f_0(z)-f_0(-z))/2\) and the even residual \(E(z):=(f_0(z)+f_0(-z))/2-f_0(0)=t_{0,z}\). Thus \(f_0(z)-b_0=o(z)+E(z)\), \(o(-z)=-o(z)\), \(o(e_i)=Ae_i\), and \(\|E(z)\|_\Gamma\le2\epsilon R_K\).

Suppose \(z_1,z_2\in Q\) have disjoint coordinate supports. All the points \(\pm z_1\pm z_2\) belong to \(Q\), so the four defects in the following exact identity are legal:

\[
o(z_1+z_2)-o(z_1)-o(z_2)
=\frac12\bigl(t_{z_1,z_2}-t_{-z_1,z_2}+t_{z_2,z_1}-t_{-z_2,z_1}\bigr).
\]

The identity follows by expanding the four midpoint defects. Applying the difference bound to each of the two differences gives
\(\|o(z_1+z_2)-o(z_1)-o(z_2)\|_\Gamma\le2\epsilon R_K\).
For any nonzero \(z\in Q\), let \(k\) be the number of its nonzero coordinates. Add its \(k\) signed coordinate vectors successively. At every addition the partial sum and the next vector have disjoint coordinate supports. Since \(o(\pm e_i)=\pm Ae_i\), telescoping gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|o(z)-Az\|_\Gamma&\le2(k-1)\epsilon R_K,\\
\|f_0(z)-Az-b_0\|_\Gamma
&\le2(k-1)\epsilon R_K+2\epsilon R_K
\le2n\epsilon R_K.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(z=0\), the residual is zero. This proves \(L\le2n\epsilon R_K\) for all \(n\ge1\), including the single-coordinate case, in which the telescoping sum is empty. It also proves \(L=0\) directly when \(\epsilon=0\). The convex-stock radius bound and the budget now give

\[
L\le en\epsilon(d+2)
\le e\sigma n^2\frac{d+2}{d}
\le3e\sigma n^2,
\]

where \((d+2)/d\le3\) includes \(d=1\).

For each \(z\in Q\), the original fiber has volume between \(v\) and \((1+\epsilon)^dv\). All other integral fibers have zero volume by hypothesis. Compactness of \(C\) implies that only finitely many integral fibers are nonempty, and hence

\[
V=W=\sum_{z\in Q}\lambda_d(C_{J+z}),\qquad
M=3^nv\le W\le(1+\epsilon)^dM,
\qquad
\frac MW\ge(1+\epsilon)^{-d}
\ge e^{-\epsilon d}\ge e^{-\sigma n}.
\]

Here \(v>0\), so \(M\) and \(W\) are positive. Lower-dimensional nonempty fibers outside \(J+Q\) contribute zero to both the original total mass and every cap mass.

We next apply the original-depth clause of accepted fact 8d52b9903d517dab with the centered stock \(K_0\). To justify all its hypotheses without pretending that the displayed shifted upper inclusion is a centered sandwich, choose an auxiliary finite dilation \(\delta\ge0\) as follows. The centroid of a compact full-dimensional convex body belongs to its interior: its supporting inequalities hold upon integration, and equality in a nonconstant supporting functional is impossible because the functional is strictly smaller on the interior, which has positive volume. Thus \(0\in\operatorname{int}(K_0)\). Since \(Q\) is finite and \(C\) is compact, all the translated fibers \(C_{J+z}-f_0(z)\) lie in a common bounded set. A sufficiently large dilation of \(K_0\), which contains a ball about zero, therefore contains this set. In particular there exists finite \(\delta\ge0\) such that

\[
f_0(z)+K_0\subseteq C_{J+z}\subseteq f_0(z)+(1+\delta)K_0
\qquad(z\in Q).
\]

This auxiliary \(\delta\) is only an applicability parameter. Neither the mass ratio nor the bound for \(L\) is estimated using \(\delta\).

The complete map into 8d52b9903d517dab is: its \(K,f,\epsilon\) are our \(K_0,f_0,\delta\); its \(J,Q,\Gamma,R_K,A,b_0,L,M,V\) are exactly those defined here; its \(\kappa\) is \(5072\). In particular its residual radius \(L\) is the actual maximum defined above, not its upper estimate involving its dilation parameter. Its original-depth inequality uses this actual \(L\), so there is no loss involving \(\delta\). Its assignment alias \(W\) is our original total mass \(W=V\), not a centered scalar noise variable. The mean in that fact is

\[
\left(J,3^{-n}\sum_{z\in Q}f_0(z)\right)
=\left(J,m_K+3^{-n}\sum_{z\in Q}f(z)\right)
=\mathbf y^\star.
\]

Thus the accepted fact gives \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) and
\(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge M/[512(5072+(n+1)^2L^4)]\). This is the corrected stock mean, not a substitution of \(f(0)\) for the mean of a nonlinear \(f\).

For clarity, this conclusion concerns the original cap measures, including all boundary cases. Let \(\bar f_0:=3^{-n}\sum_{z\in Q}f_0(z)\), let \(Z\) be uniform on \(Q\), and let \(U_0\) be independent uniform probability on \(K_0\). These are the retained law in the accepted depth clause. Rename that fact's normal components \((\alpha,\beta)\) to \((\alpha,\xi)\) here. Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) has the form

\[
H=\{(\mathbf j,x):
\alpha\cdot(\mathbf j-J)+\xi\cdot(x-\bar f_0)\ge s\},
\qquad s\le0,
\]

with \((\alpha,\xi)\ne0\). The strict-positive-score conclusion in the proof of 8d52b9903d517dab and the restriction of original volume to each retained stock give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&\ge\sum_{z\in Q}\lambda_d\bigl(H_{J+z}\cap(f_0(z)+K_0)\bigr)\\
&=M\,\Pr\bigl((J+Z,f_0(Z)+U_0)\in H\bigr)\\
&\ge M\,\Pr\bigl(\alpha\cdot Z+
\xi\cdot(f_0(Z)+U_0-\bar f_0)>0\bigr)\\
&\ge\frac{M}{512\bigl(5072+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The slice notation \(H_{\mathbf j}:=\{x:(\mathbf j,x)\in H\}\) is used in the first line. Every retained summand is a restriction at its own original base, not a relocated measure. The strict positive event lies in the cap for every \(s\le0\), even when \(s=0\). Consequently purely discrete normals \(\xi=0\), purely continuous normals \(\alpha=0\), and positive mass on a discrete boundary require no alteration. A containing zero-normal halfspace, if admitted, is the whole space and has mass \(W\). This also explains why nonempty zero-volume fibers outside \(J+Q\) cannot change the certificate.

It remains to calibrate the bound uniformly in \(n,d\). Since \(n\ge1\), we have \((n+1)^2\le4n^2\) and \(5072\le5072n^{10}\). Combining these with the proved bound on \(L\) yields

\[
512\bigl(5072+(n+1)^2L^4\bigr)
\le512\bigl[5072+4(3e\sigma)^4\bigr]n^{10}.
\]

Together with the retained fraction, this gives

\[
\frac{h_S(\mathbf y^\star)}{W}
\ge\frac{e^{-\sigma n}}
{512\bigl[5072+4(3e\sigma)^4\bigr]n^{10}}
=2^{-n}\frac{e^{\beta_\sigma n}/n^{10}}
{512\bigl[5072+4(3e\sigma)^4\bigr]}.
\]

For any positive real \(x\), the logarithmic derivative of \(e^{\beta_\sigma x}/x^{10}\) is \(\beta_\sigma-10/x\). It is negative before \(x=10/\beta_\sigma\) and positive afterward, so its global minimum is \((e\beta_\sigma/10)^{10}\). In particular
\(e^{\beta_\sigma n}/n^{10}\ge(e\beta_\sigma/10)^{10}\) for every integer \(n\ge1\). This proves the depth bound with the unclipped coefficient, and hence with the stated \(c(\sigma)\). Its denominator is finite and positive, \(\beta_\sigma>0\), and clipping gives \(0<c(\sigma)\le1/(2e)<1/e\).

If \(\sigma=0\), the budget forces \(\epsilon=0\). More generally, whenever \(\epsilon=0\), all midpoint defects vanish, \(L=0\), and the original sandwich is equality on every ternary fiber, so \(M=W\) and the depth clause gives \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge W/(512\cdot5072)\) directly. The stated coefficient remains strictly positive at \(\sigma=0\), with \(\beta_0=\log2\), and follows from the same exact calibration. No limiting argument or division by \(\sigma\) or \(\epsilon\) occurs. The dimension-one covariance estimates are already included in f1d59e746de9db52, and all bounds above explicitly include \(d=1\) and \(n=1\). This completes the class theorem.
