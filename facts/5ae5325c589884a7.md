## Statement

Retain one common canonical hard instance of accepted facts `b5739a77433de383` and `c0efb33112a166d5`. Thus \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is the canonical convex body, \(Q>0\),
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and
\[
 H=\{(z,y):\Phi(z,y)\geq0\}
\]
is the actual proper common-normal halfspace. Its continuous normal is nonzero, and
\[
 \mu_C(H)<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
 \qquad \gamma_d>\frac4{11},
 \qquad 2^n\geq2.
\tag{1}
\]
For every positive-volume integer fiber, write
\[
 C_r:=\{y:(r,y)\in C\},
 \qquad a_r:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_r),
 \qquad c_r:=\frac1{a_r}\int_{C_r}y\,dy.
\tag{2}
\]

Retain from `c0efb33112a166d5` its finite off-diagonal same-parity physical matching \(q=(q_e)\), its simultaneous endpoint densities \(f_{e,r}\) and strictly outside endpoint barycenters \(y_{e,r}\), its host-retained submatching \(q'\leq q\), and its exact minimum-unmatched canonical centroid matching \(\lambda^{\mathrm{cen}}\). In particular, for every incidence \(r\in e\),
\[
 d_r(q):=\sum_{e\ni r}q_e\leq\frac{a_r}{4},
 \qquad
 0\leq f_{e,r},
 \qquad
 \sum_{e\ni r}f_{e,r}\leq1\quad\text{a.e. on }C_r,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
 \int_{C_r}f_{e,r}=q_e,
 \qquad
 \frac1{q_e}\int_{C_r}y f_{e,r}(y)\,dy=y_{e,r},
 \qquad
 y_{e,r}\in C_r,
 \qquad
 \Phi(r,y_{e,r})<0,
\tag{4}
\]
where (4) is used only for positive edges, and
\[
 q'_e\leq q_e,
 \qquad
 q'_e\leq\lambda^{\mathrm{cen}}_e,
 \qquad
 |q'|>\frac{597}{12800}Q.
\tag{5}
\]
For an edge \(e=\{r,s\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{r+s}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde c_e:=\frac{y_{e,r}+y_{e,s}}2,
 \qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_r+c_s}2.
\tag{6}
\]

Then all of the following hold.

First, the nonzero positive measure
\[
 \widetilde\sigma
 :=\gamma_d\sum_e q'_e\delta_{(m_e,\widetilde c_e)}
\tag{7}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), every atom in its support satisfies
\[
 \Phi(m_e,\widetilde c_e)<0,
\tag{8}
\]
and, for every closed ambient halfspace \(G\), including boundary and improper cases,
\[
 \boxed{\widetilde\sigma(G)\leq\mu_C(G).}
\tag{9}
\]
Coincident atoms in (7) are counted with their full summed weights. If
\[
 \widetilde\rho
 :=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:q'_e>0\},
\tag{10}
\]
\[
 \widetilde r
 :=\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:q'_e>0\}\to\mathbb R^d\ {\mathrm{affine}}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{\widetilde c_e-a(m_e):q'_e>0\},
\tag{11}
\]
then the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_d|q'|}{2^{\widetilde\rho}(\widetilde r+1)}
 >
 \frac{597\gamma_d}{12800}
 \frac{Q}{2^{\widetilde\rho}(\widetilde r+1)}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Second, define the bad used-source set
\[
 I:=\{r:d_r(q')>0\text{ and }\Phi(r,c_r)\geq0\}
\tag{13}
\]
and delete every edge incident to \(I\):
\[
 q_e^{\mathrm{out}}
 :=
 \begin{cases}
 q'_e,&e\cap I=\varnothing,\\
 0,&e\cap I\ne\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{|q^{\mathrm{out}}|>\frac{553}{12800}Q.}
\tag{15}
\]
Every positive retained edge has two strictly outside source centroids, so
\[
 \Phi(r,c_r)<0,
 \qquad
 \Phi(s,c_s)<0,
 \qquad
 \Phi(m_e,\bar c_e)<0
 \quad(q_e^{\mathrm{out}}>0).
\tag{16}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sigma^{\mathrm{out}}
 :=\sum_e q_e^{\mathrm{out}}\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}
\tag{17}
\]
is a nonzero positive submeasure of the exact canonical centroid-midpoint host
\[
 \nu^{\mathrm{cen}}
 :=\sum_e\lambda_e^{\mathrm{cen}}\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}.
\tag{18}
\]
This submeasure assertion remains literal when several different edges produce the same atom.

Third, put
\[
 u_r:=d_r(q^{\mathrm{out}}),
 \qquad
 E:=\{r:u_r>0\},
 \qquad
 w_r:=4\gamma_du_r\quad(r\in E).
\tag{19}
\]
Every source in \(E\) is strictly outside \(H\), and the weights form one legal source-centroid selector:
\[
 0<w_r\leq\delta_C(r,c_r),
\tag{20}
\]
where \(\delta_C\) is the absolute fiber depth in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. In particular, the atomic source measure
\[
 \tau^{\mathrm{src}}
 :=\sum_{r\in E}w_r\delta_{(r,c_r)}
\tag{21}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\), is strictly outside \(H\), and has the exact single-use normalization
\[
 \boxed{|\tau^{\mathrm{src}}|
 =\sum_{r\in E}w_r
 =8\gamma_d|q^{\mathrm{out}}|.}
\tag{22}
\]
Define
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{src}}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\tag{23}
\]
\[
 r_{\mathrm{src}}
 :=\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\mathrm{affine}}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_r-a(r):r\in E\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 8\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{|q^{\mathrm{out}}|}{r_{\mathrm{src}}+1}
 >
 \frac{553\gamma_d}{1600}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{Q}{r_{\mathrm{src}}+1}.}
\tag{25}
\]

## Proof

For every incidence \(r\in e\), define a retained endpoint density by
\[
 \widehat f_{e,r}:=
 \begin{cases}
 (q'_e/q_e)f_{e,r},&q'_e>0,\\
 0,&q'_e=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]
The first case has \(q_e>0\) by (5), so (26) is well-defined. Equations (3)-(5) give
\[
 0\leq\widehat f_{e,r}\leq f_{e,r},
 \qquad
 \int_{C_r}\widehat f_{e,r}=q'_e,
 \qquad
 \frac1{q'_e}\int_{C_r}y\widehat f_{e,r}(y)\,dy=y_{e,r},
\tag{27}
\]
for \(q'_e>0\), and
\[
 \sum_{e\ni r}\widehat f_{e,r}\leq1
 \quad\text{a.e. on }C_r.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus scaling to \(q'\) neither changes an endpoint barycenter nor violates the simultaneous source ledger.

Since every edge of \(q'\) joins two same-parity sources, \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\). Equations (4), (6), and convexity of \(C\) give
\[
 (m_e,\widetilde c_e)
 =\frac{(r,y_{e,r})+(s,y_{e,s})}{2}
 \in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{29}
\]
Affinity of \(\Phi\) and the two strict endpoint inequalities in (4) give (8). This proves the support and sign assertions for (7).

We prove (9) without separating coincident atoms. Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(G\). For every positive edge \(e=\{r,s\}\) whose atom \((m_e,\widetilde c_e)\) lies in \(G\), orient that occurrence of the edge toward one endpoint point among \((r,y_{e,r})\) and \((s,y_{e,s})\) that lies in \(G\). Such an endpoint always exists: an affine defining functional for \(G\) takes at the midpoint the average of its two endpoint values. If both endpoints lie in \(G\), choose either one. The choice is made edge by edge, so different edges producing the same atom retain their separate weights.

For each source \(r\), sum the densities in (26) over the edges oriented toward \(r\):
\[
 F_r^G:=\sum_{\substack{e\text{ oriented toward }r}}\widehat f_{e,r},
 \qquad
 v_r:=\int_{C_r}F_r^G
 =\sum_{\substack{e\text{ oriented toward }r}}q'_e.
\tag{30}
\]
By (28), \(0\leq F_r^G\leq1\) almost everywhere. If \(v_r>0\), its normalized barycenter is
\[
 b_r^G
 =\frac1{v_r}
 \sum_{\substack{e\text{ oriented toward }r}}q'_e y_{e,r}.
\tag{31}
\]
Every point in this convex combination lies in the closed fiber section \(G_r\), so \(b_r^G\in G_r\). Equations (30)-(31) also show that \(b_r^G\in\mathsf M_{v_r}(C_r)\). If \(G_r\) is a proper closed halfspace, the metronoid depth inclusion in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` yields
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_r\cap G_r)\geq\gamma_dv_r.
\tag{32}
\]
If \(G_r=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from \(v_r\leq a_r\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\). If \(G_r=\varnothing\), no endpoint can have been oriented to \(r\), so \(v_r=0\) and (32) again holds. These alternatives also cover improper ambient halfspaces.

Every relevant edge was oriented exactly once. Hence, with multiplicity preserved,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widetilde\sigma(G)
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e:(m_e,\widetilde c_e)\in G}}q'_e
 =\gamma_d\sum_rv_r\\
 &\leq\sum_r\operatorname{vol}_d(C_r\cap G_r)
 =\mu_C(G).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
This proves (9). Its mass is \(\gamma_d|q'|>0\), so accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`, applied with the ranks (10)-(11), gives (12), including the zero-rank cases.

We next prove the pruning estimate. If \(r\in I\), then \(d_r(q')>0\) and (3), (5) imply \(a_r>0\). The point \(c_r\) is the centroid of \(C_r\), and \(\Phi(r,c_r)\geq0\). Because the continuous normal of \(H\) is nonzero, the fiber section \(H_r\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(c_r\). The centroid case of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_r\cap H_r)\geq\gamma_da_r.
\tag{34}
\]
The sources in \(I\) are distinct fiber indices. Summing (34) and using (1) gives
\[
 \gamma_d\sum_{r\in I}a_r
 \leq\mu_C(H)
 <\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
\]
hence
\[
 \sum_{r\in I}a_r
 <\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n}.
\tag{35}
\]

Let \(L_{\mathrm{del}}\) be the total weight of the edges deleted in (14). Every deleted edge has at least one endpoint in \(I\), while an edge with both endpoints in \(I\) may harmlessly be counted twice. Therefore (3), (5), and (35) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_{\mathrm{del}}
 &\leq\sum_{r\in I}d_r(q')
 \leq\sum_{r\in I}d_r(q)
 \leq\frac14\sum_{r\in I}a_r\\
 &<\frac{Q}{400\gamma_d2^n}
 <\frac{11}{3200}Q
 =\frac{44}{12800}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The penultimate strict inequality uses both strict bounds in (1). Combining (5) and (36) proves
\[
 |q^{\mathrm{out}}|
 =|q'|-L_{\mathrm{del}}
 >\frac{597-44}{12800}Q,
\]
which is (15).

If \(q_e^{\mathrm{out}}>0\) for \(e=\{r,s\}\), then both \(r\) and \(s\) have positive \(q'\)-degree and neither belongs to \(I\). Definition (13) therefore gives the first two strict inequalities in (16), and affinity gives the third. Also, (5) and (14) give
\[
 0\leq q_e^{\mathrm{out}}\leq\lambda_e^{\mathrm{cen}}
 \qquad(e).
\tag{37}
\]
If several edges have the same point \((m_e,\bar c_e)\), summing (37) over exactly those edges shows that the atom weight of \(\sigma^{\mathrm{out}}\) is at most the corresponding atom weight of \(\nu^{\mathrm{cen}}\). Thus (17) is literally a positive submeasure of (18), not merely an edgewise formal submatching. Its mass is positive by (15), and all its atoms are strictly outside by (16).

Finally, let \(r\in E\). The off-diagonal matching \(q^{\mathrm{out}}\) is a submatching of \(q\), so (3) gives
\[
 0<u_r\leq d_r(q)\leq\frac{a_r}{4}.
\tag{38}
\]
The centroid-depth assertion in accepted facts `6769574fc1bd9f8d` and `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 \delta_C(r,c_r)\geq\gamma_da_r.
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (19), (38), and (39) prove (20). Moreover, \(r\) is incident to a retained edge, so the argument after (36) gives \(\Phi(r,c_r)<0\). Thus every atom of (21) is strictly outside \(H\).

For completeness, (20) also proves directly that (21) is halfspace-dominated. For any closed ambient halfspace \(G\), every atom \((r,c_r)\in G\) has a fiber section containing \(c_r\); its \(C_r\)-volume is at least \(w_r\) if the section is proper by the definition of \(\delta_C\), and at least \(w_r\) if it is the whole fiber by (38). Summing once over the distinct sources proves \(\tau^{\mathrm{src}}(G)\leq\mu_C(G)\); empty sections contribute neither an atom nor mass.

Because \(q^{\mathrm{out}}\) is loopless, every edge weight occurs once in the degree at each of its two distinct endpoints. Consequently
\[
 \sum_{r\in E}u_r=2|q^{\mathrm{out}}|.
\tag{40}
\]
Multiplying (40) by \(4\gamma_d\) proves (22). In particular, no source weight is duplicated for each incident edge: all incidences at \(r\) have first been aggregated into the single number \(u_r\).

The residual-rank identity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`, applied to the finite support \(\{(r,c_r):r\in E\}\), shows that the minimum in (24) is attained. Choose an attaining affine map in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\). The legal data (19)-(20), followed by (22), then give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{\sum_{r\in E}w_r}{r_{\mathrm{src}}+1}
 =
 8\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{src}}}
 \frac{|q^{\mathrm{out}}|}{r_{\mathrm{src}}+1}.
\tag{41}
\]
The strict second inequality in (25) follows from (15), since \(8\cdot553/12800=553/1600\). This completes all three constructions, including zero-weight edges, coincident target atoms, boundary conventions, pruning loss, canonical-host inclusion, and selector normalization.
