## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and retain the convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), the nonempty finite canonical stopped set \(A_\star\), its affine hull \(L_\star:=\operatorname{aff}(A_\star)\), and its affine rank

\[
\rho_\star:=\dim L_\star,
\]

the positive weights \(w_y\), the subset-weight notation \(W(S):=\sum_{y\in S}w_y\) for \(S\subseteq A_\star\), and the total weight

\[
W_\star:=\sum_{y\in A_\star}w_y,
\]

the fibers \(C_y\), their volumes \(a_y\), their centroids \(c_y\), and the hard-branch scale \(Q>0\) from accepted facts `d06d919de3083960` and `efdbf9e9886a0e94`. Thus

\[
2^{\,n-\rho_\star}W_\star>\frac{11}{320}Q,
\qquad
0<w_y\le\frac{a_y}{4},
\qquad
0<w_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}
\quad(y\in A_\star).
\tag{1}
\]

Retain the applicable relative or global complete-core charge from accepted fact `efdbf9e9886a0e94`, and assume its canonical hard-branch threshold failure: if \(\rho_\star<n\), then

\[
2^{\,n-\rho_\star}G_{L_\star}^{\mathrm{rel}}<\frac Q{100},
\tag{1a}
\]

whereas if \(\rho_\star=n\), then

\[
G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1b}
\]

For \(y\in A_\star\), put

\[
D_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)-c_y,
\qquad
K:=\bigcap_{y\in A_\star}D_y.
\tag{2}
\]

Accepted fact `35ca05c7683b7d12` shows that \(K\) is an origin-symmetric convex body. Write \(\|\cdot\|_K\) for its Minkowski functional. It also supplies the following full-product labels. For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), set

\[
A_\xi:=\{y\in A_\star:y\equiv\xi\pmod 2\},
\qquad
M_\xi:=\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(M_\xi>0\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), set

\[
B_{\xi,m}:=
\frac1{M_\xi}
\sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
w_uw_v.
\tag{4}
\]

The ordered sum includes every diagonal pair. Whenever \(B_{\xi,m}>0\), set

\[
q_{\xi,m}:=
\frac1{M_\xi B_{\xi,m}}
\sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
w_uw_v\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Labels of zero mass are omitted. In particular,

\[
\sum_m B_{\xi,m}=M_\xi,
\qquad
\sum_{\xi,m}B_{\xi,m}=W_\star.
\tag{6}
\]

For every affine map \(s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d\), define

\[
r_y(s):=c_y-s(y),
\qquad
\mathcal B_s:=\left\{y\in A_\star:
\|r_y(s)\|_K>\frac65\right\},
\tag{7}
\]

and its full-product defect

\[
\mathfrak P(s):=
\sum_{\xi:M_\xi>0}\frac1{M_\xi}
\sum_{u,v\in A_\xi}w_uw_v
\mathbf 1_{\left\{
\left\|\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}
-s\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)\right\|_K>\frac25
\right\}}.
\tag{8}
\]

Then the pointwise triangle-free bound is

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak P(s)\ge
\frac{W(\mathcal B_s)^2}{2W_\star}.}
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently, if a number \(\mathcal G\ge0\) satisfies

\[
W(A_\star\setminus\mathcal B_s)\le\mathcal G
\quad\text{for every affine }s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d,
\tag{10}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
\ge
\frac{(W_\star-\mathcal G)_+^2}{2W_\star}.}
\tag{11}
\]

In the canonical hard branch, accepted fact `efdbf9e9886a0e94` implies the sharper uniform estimate

\[
W(\mathcal B_s)>\frac{39}{55}W_\star
\quad\text{for every affine }s.
\tag{12}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
\ge\frac{1521}{6050}W_\star,}
\qquad
\boxed{
2^{\,n-\rho_\star}\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
>\frac{16731}{1936000}Q.}
\tag{13}
\]

The total full-product mass of all diagonal pairs is

\[
L_{\mathrm{loop}}:=
\sum_{\xi:M_\xi>0}
\frac{\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y^2}{M_\xi}.
\tag{14}
\]

It satisfies

\[
\boxed{
2^{\,n-\rho_\star}L_{\mathrm{loop}}<\frac Q{400}.}
\tag{15}
\]

If \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{off}}(s)\) is defined by (8) with all pairs \(u=v\) omitted, then

\[
\boxed{
2^{\,n-\rho_\star}\inf_s\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{off}}(s)
>\frac{11891}{1936000}Q.}
\tag{16}
\]

Finally, for an ordered pair \((u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\), write

\[
p_{u,v}:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},
\qquad
m_{u,v}:=\frac{u+v}{2}.
\tag{17}
\]

Define the within-label variation

\[
I_\star:=
\sum_{\xi:M_\xi>0}\frac1{M_\xi}
\sum_{u,v\in A_\xi}w_uw_v
\mathbf 1_{\left\{
\|p_{u,v}-q_{\xi,m_{u,v}}\|_K>\frac15
\right\}},
\tag{18}
\]

and the labelled-output affine defect

\[
O_\star:=
\inf_s
\sum_{\substack{\xi,m\\B_{\xi,m}>0}}
B_{\xi,m}
\mathbf 1_{\left\{
\|q_{\xi,m}-s(m)\|_K>\frac15
\right\}}.
\tag{19}
\]

For every \(m\) carrying at least one label, put

\[
\alpha_m:=\sum_\xi B_{\xi,m},
\qquad
M_m:=\max_\xi B_{\xi,m},
\tag{20}
\]

choose any \(\xi(m)\) attaining the maximum, and define

\[
C_{\mathrm{col}}:=\sum_m(\alpha_m-M_m),
\tag{21}
\]

\[
O_{\mathrm{dom}}:=
\inf_s\sum_{m:\alpha_m>0}
M_m
\mathbf 1_{\left\{
\|q_{\xi(m),m}-s(m)\|_K>\frac15
\right\}}.
\tag{22}
\]

Here \(C_{\mathrm{col}}\) is exactly the total mass of all nondominant labels after one label is retained at each physical midpoint. These quantities obey

\[
\boxed{I_\star+O_\star\ge\inf_s\mathfrak P(s),}
\qquad
\boxed{O_\star\le C_{\mathrm{col}}+O_{\mathrm{dom}},}
\tag{23}
\]

and therefore the exact interface ledger is

\[
\boxed{
I_\star+C_{\mathrm{col}}+O_{\mathrm{dom}}
\ge\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
\ge\frac{1521}{6050}W_\star.}
\tag{24}
\]

In particular,

\[
\max\{I_\star,C_{\mathrm{col}},O_{\mathrm{dom}}\}
\ge\frac{507}{6050}W_\star.
\tag{25}
\]

## Proof

Fix an affine map \(s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d\). Since \(s\) is affine, every \(u,v\in A_\star\) satisfy

\[
\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}
-s\!\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)
=\frac{r_u(s)+r_v(s)}2.
\tag{26}
\]

Fix a parity class \(\xi\) with \(M_\xi>0\). On the vertex set \(\mathcal B_s\cap A_\xi\), form a simple graph by joining two distinct vertices \(u,v\) exactly when

\[
\left\|\frac{r_u(s)+r_v(s)}2\right\|_K\le\frac25.
\tag{27}
\]

This graph is triangle-free. Indeed, suppose that distinct vertices \(u,v,z\) formed a triangle. Each of the three pair sums would then have \(K\)-norm at most \(4/5\). The identity

\[
2r_u(s)
=(r_u(s)+r_v(s))+(r_u(s)+r_z(s))-(r_v(s)+r_z(s))
\]

and the symmetry and triangle inequality of \(\|\cdot\|_K\) would give

\[
2\|r_u(s)\|_K\le\frac45+\frac45+\frac45=\frac{12}{5}.
\]

Thus \(\|r_u(s)\|_K\le6/5\), contrary to \(u\in\mathcal B_s\).

We next prove the weighted triangle-free estimate used below. Let a finite triangle-free simple graph carry nonnegative vertex weights of total mass \(T\). Its edge energy is

\[
E:=\sum_{\{u,v\}\text{ an edge}}w_uw_v.
\]

If two positive-weight vertices \(u,v\) are nonadjacent, let \(S_u\) and \(S_v\) be their respective weighted neighbor sums. Because \(u,v\) are nonadjacent, the part of \(E\) involving either vertex is \(w_uS_u+w_vS_v\), with both \(S_u,S_v\) unchanged when weight is transferred between \(u\) and \(v\). Move the combined weight \(w_u+w_v\) to a vertex whose neighbor sum is at least the other's. This does not decrease \(E\) and strictly reduces the positive support. Repeating finitely many times leaves a clique as the positive support. Triangle-freeness makes that clique have at most two vertices, and its edge energy is at most the product of two nonnegative numbers summing to \(T\), hence at most \(T^2/4\). Since the compression never decreased the energy, the original graph also satisfies

\[
E\le\frac{T^2}{4}.
\tag{28}
\]

Put

\[
T_\xi:=W(\mathcal B_s\cap A_\xi).
\]

The total ordered bad-bad pair mass in this parity class is \(T_\xi^2\). By (28), the ordered mass of distinct bad-bad pairs satisfying (27) is twice an unordered edge energy and is therefore at most \(T_\xi^2/2\). A bad diagonal pair is never counted by (27), because

\[
\left\|\frac{r_y(s)+r_y(s)}2\right\|_K
=\|r_y(s)\|_K>\frac65>\frac25.
\]

Thus at least \(T_\xi^2/2\) of the ordered bad-bad mass is charged by the defect indicator in (8). After applying the normalization \(1/M_\xi\), summing over parity classes, and using Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak P(s)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{\xi:M_\xi>0}\frac{T_\xi^2}{M_\xi}\\
&\ge\frac12
\frac{\left(\sum_\xi T_\xi\right)^2}{\sum_\xi M_\xi}
=\frac{W(\mathcal B_s)^2}{2W_\star}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (9), with both the ordered-pair factor \(2\) and the diagonal convention explicit. Under (10), one has

\[
W(\mathcal B_s)
=W_\star-W(A_\star\setminus\mathcal B_s)
\ge(W_\star-\mathcal G)_+.
\]

Substitution into (9), followed by the infimum over \(s\), proves (11).

We now invoke the canonical hard-branch obstruction. Accepted fact `efdbf9e9886a0e94` says that every affine \(s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d\) misses \(c_y+(6/5)D_y\) on more than \(39W_\star/55\) of the stopped weight. Because \(K\subseteq D_y\) for every \(y\), each such missed point also satisfies

\[
s(y)\notin c_y+\frac65K,
\]

which is equivalent to \(y\in\mathcal B_s\). This proves (12). Equations (9) and (12) give

\[
\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
\ge\frac12\left(\frac{39}{55}\right)^2W_\star
=\frac{1521}{6050}W_\star.
\]

Multiplying by \(2^{n-\rho_\star}\) and using the strict stopped-mass lower bound in (1), supplied by accepted fact `d06d919de3083960`, yields

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}\inf_s\mathfrak P(s)
>\frac{1521}{6050}\frac{11}{320}Q
=\frac{16731}{1936000}Q.
\]

This proves (13).

We next bound the diagonal mass. Choose \(y_0\in A_\star\), and let \(\Gamma\) be the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\) generated by the differences \(y-y_0\), \(y\in A_\star\). Its rank is \(\rho_\star\), because its real span is the direction space of \(L_\star\). The reduction of \(\Gamma\) modulo \(2\mathbb Z^n\) is generated over \(\mathbb F_2\) by the reductions of a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(\Gamma\), so it has at most \(2^{\rho_\star}\) elements. The parity classes represented by \(A_\star\) form a translate of a subset of this image. Hence at most \(2^{\rho_\star}\) parity classes have positive \(M_\xi\).

For each such class,

\[
\frac{\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y^2}{M_\xi}
\le\max_{y\in A_\xi}w_y.
\]

The strict atom cap in (1), again from accepted fact `d06d919de3083960`, therefore gives

\[
L_{\mathrm{loop}}
<2^{\rho_\star}\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\]

This is (15). For every \(s\), the diagonal contribution to \(\mathfrak P(s)\) is nonnegative and at most \(L_{\mathrm{loop}}\), so

\[
\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{off}}(s)
\ge\mathfrak P(s)-L_{\mathrm{loop}}.
\]

Taking infima, multiplying by \(2^{n-\rho_\star}\), and applying (13) and (15) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{n-\rho_\star}\inf_s\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{off}}(s)
&>\left(\frac{16731}{1936000}-\frac1{400}\right)Q\\
&=\frac{11891}{1936000}Q,
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (16).

It remains to prove the interface ledger. Every ordered pair in (18) belongs to a positive-mass label, so its \(q_{\xi,m_{u,v}}\) is defined by (5). For any affine \(s\), the triangle inequality shows that

\[
\left\|p_{u,v}-s(m_{u,v})\right\|_K>\frac25
\]

implies at least one of

\[
\left\|p_{u,v}-q_{\xi,m_{u,v}}\right\|_K>\frac15,
\qquad
\left\|q_{\xi,m_{u,v}}-s(m_{u,v})\right\|_K>\frac15.
\tag{29}
\]

After multiplying by \(w_uw_v/M_\xi\) and summing, the first alternative contributes at most \(I_\star\). For a fixed positive label \((\xi,m)\), the total normalized ordered-pair mass over \(u+v=2m\) is exactly \(B_{\xi,m}\) by (4), so the second alternative contributes its corresponding labelled-output term. Thus, for every \(s\),

\[
\mathfrak P(s)\le I_\star+
\sum_{\substack{\xi,m\\B_{\xi,m}>0}}
B_{\xi,m}
\mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,m}-s(m)\|_K>1/5\}}.
\]

Taking the infimum over \(s\) proves the first inequality in (23).

At a fixed physical midpoint \(m\), the total mass of all labels other than the chosen dominant one is exactly \(\alpha_m-M_m\). Therefore, for every affine \(s\), the full labelled-output defect at \(m\) is at most \(\alpha_m-M_m\) plus the defect indicator of the dominant label multiplied by \(M_m\). Summing over \(m\) and then taking a sequence of affine maps approaching the infimum in (22) gives

\[
O_\star\le C_{\mathrm{col}}+O_{\mathrm{dom}}.
\]

This proves the second inequality in (23). Combining the two inequalities in (23) with (13) proves (24). The three terms in (24) are nonnegative, so at least one is at least one third of \(1521W_\star/6050\), which is \(507W_\star/6050\). This proves (25) and completes the proof.
