## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite nonempty complete base in an affine lattice, with positive compact convex $d$-fibers $K_z$ ($d\ge1$) satisfying the anchored Minkowski inclusions $\sum_z\lambda_z K_z\subseteq K_p$ whenever $\lambda_z\ge0$, $\sum_z\lambda_z=1$, and $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$. Put $S=\bigcup_z\{z\}\times K_z$, $C=\operatorname{conv}S$, $dm=\sum_z\delta_z\otimes dX|_{K_z}$ and $M=m(S)$. All affine functions are relative to $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$. Let $\varepsilon>0$, $N=d+1$, and $G(u)=(M^{-1}\sum_z\int_{K_z}[u\cdot(X-g_z)]^2\,dX)^{1/2}$, where $g_z$ are the fiber centroids. Use marked energy $E_K(y)=\inf_a\int_K[1+a\cdot(X-y)]^{-d-1}\,dX$ over denominators strictly positive throughout $K$, extended by zero on $\partial K$. For a parameter $L$ put $f(z,y)=[L(z,y)E_{K_z}(y)/M]^{1/N}$ and use the infimum $b_L(p,y)$ of mixed masses of the caps
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\varepsilon G(u)f(p,y),\qquad u\ne0,
$$
and of closed pure-base caps containing $p$ (including the whole-base trace). Write $b_{\rm base}(p)$ for the latter minimum alone. Let $Q=\max_S b_L$, $I$ be the bases of maximizing marks, $J=\{p\in I:b_{\rm base}(p)>Q\}$, $A=I\setminus J$, and $k(T)$ count inherited lattice parities occupied by $T$, with $k(\varnothing)=0$.

Assume $L$ is finite nonnegative upper-semicontinuous concave on $C$, positive on every sampled own interior, with $M^{-1}\int_S\log L\,dm=0$. The pair is an attained global minimizer of $Q/M$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ in this enlarged class of compatible positive families and log-normalized parameters, with $0<Q/M<1/k(P)$. At each proper deepest base assume one own-interior maximizing mark. Assume the atomic deepest bases admit a cover by $m$ old closed pure-base caps of mass $Q$, with $(Q/M)m<1$; take $m=0$ when $A=\varnothing$.

Then $J$ is nonempty. For each proper deepest mark take an affine sampled touching majorant $A_p$ of $L$. Their minimum $T$ agrees with $L$ throughout every sampled fiber interior and is an equivalent normalized global minimizer with at most $|J|$ planes. Its sampled depths agree with the original ones, including at boundary marks.

## Proof

The sampled touching construction of 155691fc3768c06f applies by using the hypograph lifts $\{(X,s):X\in K_z,\ 0\le s\le L(z,X)\}$. These are compact convex bodies of positive volume, satisfy anchored inclusion by concavity, and at a proper mark a finite own-coordinate supergradient of $L$ gives the support normal of the lifted fiber. The accepted base-slope inequality then gives a full affine majorant touching the old value and dominating $L$ on $S$. Each plane is nonnegative on $C$ and positive on every sampled interior. Their finite minimum has the same properties.

At a sampled point $L E$ is continuous through the own boundary: $L$ is bounded on compact $C$, is continuous in each own interior, and the marked energy tends to zero at the boundary by 0b2fbdaf8d3134a0. Fixed-family depth is continuous within each fiber by the uniform threshold slab estimate in that fact, which also permits a change of source position. Boundary depth is zero by the supporting cap in that fact. If all maximizing bases are atomic, apply 2fe966187abc0788 to the assumed caps. Each has normalized mass strictly below $Q/M$ on the eroded compatible family. Renormalize the old raw $L$ on the eroded family by the geometric mean. Since $\log L$ is integrable and the removals tend to zero in volume, this scalar tends to one. By energy monotonicity under inclusion (d90ba05577427854) the product of the new mass, energy and covariance factors can increase the physical shift at retained points by at most a uniform $o(1)$, using convergence of the total mass and of the pooled covariance. The upper slab estimate preserves the normalized gap on all old nondeepest bases, so the atomic cover produces a strict descent. This is impossible. The same cleanup applies to a fixed-family parameter change if its new raw maximum is $Q$ and all new maximizing bases are contained in the old atomic cover.

If $T>L$ on positive sampled volume, interpolate the concave raw parameter $L+t(T-L)$ and multiply it by $\exp[-D(t)]$, with
$$
D(t)=M^{-1}\int_S\log\bigl(1+t(T-L)/L\bigr)\,dm .
$$
The integrand is nonnegative on the sampled interiors and dominated for $0<t<1$ by $\log(T/L)$, integrable since $T$ is bounded above, $T\ge L$ on samples, and $\log L$ is integrable. Hence $D(t)\to0$. Truncate $(T-L)/L$ at a finite positive level with positive mean. For small $t$, $\log(1+tq)\ge tq/2$ for this bounded nonnegative truncation $q$. Thus $D(t)\ge2ct$ for some fixed $c>0$. On fixed small interior neighborhoods of all the proper marks, $(T-L)/L\le c$ by equality at the marks and continuity in the own fibers. The normalized parameter is then at most $e^{-ct}L$ there. The old positive-buffer proper-gap cap at each old proper mark is attained by 20210aa9d661295a. Raising its threshold strictly decreases its captured own mass. Every other point of these neighborhoods was already below $Q$ and can only decrease weakly. Outside the neighborhoods at proper bases and at all nondeepest bases, the old strict compact gap persists by uniform profile convergence and the all-normal slab estimate. Maxima are attained on the closed fibers by continuity. The old atomic caps still bound those bases by $Q$. Thus the parameter move either strictly lowers the maximum or permits the all-atomic cleanup above, both contradictions.

It follows that $L=T$ almost everywhere on the samples and hence everywhere in each sampled interior by continuity there. The new parameter has exactly the old normalization and buffered depths, since boundary energy is zero. It therefore achieves the same global minimum.
