## Statement

Let $J$ be an open bounded interval in $(0,\infty)$, let $A$ be a finite concave function on $J$, and let $w:J\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous. Suppose $H\in C^2(J)$ has one fixed sign and
$$
H''(v)=w(v)[av+b+cA(v)],\qquad \operatorname{supp}(-A'')\subseteq\{H=0\}.
$$
If $c\ne0$, or if $c=0$ and $av+b$ is a nonzero affine function, then the curvature measure $-A''$ is supported on at most two points in $J$.

## Proof

Replace $H$ and its three coefficients by their negatives if needed so that $H\ge0$. The load $B(v)=av+b+cA(v)$ is convex, concave or affine. Because $w>0$, its signs are the signs of $H''$.

If $x<y$ are zeros of $H$ with a positive value between them, the mean-value theorem gives an earlier positive and a later negative derivative between $x,y$. Since $H'(x)=H'(y)=0$, a second use of the theorem gives the ordered signs $+,-,+$ of $H''$ between $x$ and $y$. If $B$ is concave it cannot have these signs, so the zero set has at most one connected component. If $B$ is convex and the zero set has three components, take ordered zeros from the three components. There is a positive value between the first two and between the last two. The two sign triples give the forbidden subsequence $-,+,-$ of a convex function. Thus the zero set has at most two components. An affine load also satisfies this argument.

If the zero set contains an interval with nonempty interior, $B$ vanishes there. This is impossible for a nonzero affine load. For $c\ne0$, it makes $A$ affine there. A convex function that vanishes on an interval is everywhere nonnegative, by the secant-slope inequalities. Similarly a concave such function is everywhere nonpositive. In the convex case this makes $H$ convex, so its nonnegative zero set is an interval. In the concave case $H$ is concave, and its nonnegative interior zero forces it to vanish everywhere by taking a chord across the zero. In either case, any interior of the zero interval carries no curvature, and at most its two endpoints in $J$ can carry curvature. When $H\equiv0$, the allowed coefficients force $A$ affine on $J$. If the zero set contains no nontrivial interval it consists of at most two singleton components. The support hypothesis now proves the bound.
