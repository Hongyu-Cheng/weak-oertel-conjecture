## Statement

Let \(m,h_1,h_2,L\) be positive integers with \(L\ge\max\{h_1,h_2\}\), and let \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). Define the complete support and the original law by
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:
       \textstyle\sum_i x_i+h_1y_1+h_2y_2\le L\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)=\frac{e^{\lambda(h_1y_1+h_2y_2)}}{\mathcal Z},
\]
where \(\mathcal Z\) sums the numerator over all of \(D\). The actual closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\) and moment \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\) have exactly the meanings in 01a4100542206b94, with its \(N=m+2\) and no renormalization of the original roots. Let \(g>1\) be the absolute constant of 609407d6db81b18b and put \(g_0=\min\{2,g^{3/2}\}>1\).

Put \(g_{\rm ar}=\gcd(h_1,h_2)\), \(a=h_1/g_{\rm ar}\), \(b=h_2/g_{\rm ar}\), and \(Q=\lfloor L/g_{\rm ar}\rfloor\). Choose any integers \(u,v\) with \(au+bv=1\), and define
\[
q=ay_1+by_2,\qquad k=vy_1-uy_2,
\qquad
I_q=\left[\left\lceil-\frac{uq}{b}\right\rceil,
              \left\lfloor\frac{vq}{a}\right\rfloor\right]\cap\mathbb Z,
\qquad n(q)=|I_q|.
\]
The symbol \(g_{\rm ar}\) is the arithmetic gcd and is unrelated to the rank-gain constant \(g\). For integers \(T\ge0\), write
\[
\Delta_{T,m}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\textstyle\sum_i x_i\le T\},
\qquad F_m(T)=|\Delta_{T,m}|=\binom{T+m}{m}.
\]
Then the following conclusions hold.

1. The displayed integer change of coordinates is unimodular, with inverse
\[
y_1=uq+bk,\qquad y_2=vq-ak.
\]
It sends the entire original support bijectively to the complete support
\[
D'=\{(\mathbf x,q,k)\in\mathbb Z^{m+2}:
 0\le q\le Q,\ \mathbf x\in\Delta_{L-g_{\rm ar}q,m},\ k\in I_q\}.
\]
All normalizations are exact:
\[
\mathcal Z=\sum_{q=0}^Q n(q)F_m(L-g_{\rm ar}q)e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q},
\qquad
p'(\mathbf x,q,k)=\frac{e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q}}{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every actual original closed halfspace is carried to an actual closed halfspace and has the same mass. In particular, for every original root, its depth and its original probability are unchanged by the bijection.

2. The residues are exactly indexed by
\(0\le c<b\), \(0\le d<a\). Put \(q_{c,d}=ac+bd\). Their disjoint parametrizations are
\[
y_1=c+bj_1,\quad y_2=d+aj_2,\quad j_1,j_2\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
\qquad
q=q_{c,d}+ab(j_1+j_2).
\tag{2}
\]
The original support in this residue consists of precisely
\[
\sum_i x_i+g_{\rm ar}ab(j_1+j_2)\le L-g_{\rm ar}q_{c,d},
\tag{3}
\]
and every one of its original atom weights is
\[
\frac{e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q_{c,d}}
           e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}ab(j_1+j_2)}}{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{4}
\]
The map \((c,d)\mapsto q_{c,d}\pmod{ab}\) is a bijection. If \(q=ab t+r\), with \(0\le r<ab\), let \((c,d)\) be its unique residue and write \(q_{c,d}=r+ab\delta_r\), where \(\delta_r\in\{0,1\}\). Then
\[
n(q)=\max\{0,t-\delta_r+1\},\qquad n(q+ab)=n(q)+1.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (1) and (2)--(5) count all original atoms, including every residue and every lattice point on a normal-height edge.

3. For any integers \(q_0\le q_1\) in \(\{0,\ldots,Q\}\) with \(n(q_0),n(q_1)>0\), put
\[
T=L-g_{\rm ar}q_1,\qquad
E_{q_0,q_1}=\{(q,k)\in\mathbb Z^2:q_0\le q\le q_1,\ k\in I_q\},
\qquad N_{q_0,q_1}=\sum_{q=q_0}^{q_1}n(q),
\]
and define its exact original band coefficient by
\[
\eta_{q_0,q_1}
=\frac{F_m(T)N_{q_0,q_1}}{\mathcal Z}
 \begin{cases}
 e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q_0},&\lambda\ge0,\\
 e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q_1},&\lambda<0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The heavy support \(E_{q_0,q_1}\) is complete in its ambient lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\), even if it is lower-dimensional. With the original root probabilities and all actual closed caps, one has
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{\eta_{q_0,q_1}^{4}g_0^m}{32}\,8^{-(m+2)}.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular this comparison takes all residues in the selected normal strip together; no residue probability or conditional root probability has replaced an original root probability in (7).

There is also the exact slice certificate. Set
\[
w_q=\frac{n(q)F_m(L-g_{\rm ar}q)e^{\lambda g_{\rm ar}q}}{\mathcal Z}
\quad(n(q)>0).
\]
Then
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{g_0^m}{4}\,8^{-(m+2)}
                   \sum_{q:n(q)>0}w_q^4.
\tag{8}
\]
For the fixed support, let \(q_*\) be its largest occupied normal height. As \(\lambda\to+\infty\), the actual original law and its third moment converge to the uniform law and moment on the full exposed support
\(\Delta_{L-g_{\rm ar}q_*,m}\times\{q_*\}\times I_{q_*}\), and
\[
\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}M_3(p)
\ge \frac{g_0^m}{4}\,8^{-(m+2)}.
\tag{9}
\]

4. There is no absolute lower bound on the probability of a best single residue class. Explicitly, take \(m=1\), \(g_{\rm ar}=1\), \(\lambda=0\), coprime unequal \(a,b\ge2\), and \(L=4ab\). Every class in (2) has original probability strictly less than \(5/(ab)\). Consequently the sum of the fourth powers of all those class probabilities is at most \(125/(ab)^3\). Along \(a=A,b=A+1\), these quantities vanish, whereas the actual original uniform law satisfies
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac14\,8^{-3}
\tag{10}
\]
by 01a4100542206b94. Moreover, the primitive projected height law need not have interval support or be log-concave: for \(m=1,a=2,b=3,g_{\rm ar}=1,L=6\), its unnormalized masses are
\[
7,\ 0,\ 5e^{2\lambda},\ 4e^{3\lambda},\ 3e^{4\lambda},\ 2e^{5\lambda},\ 2e^{6\lambda}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus neither a single-residue reduction nor an unproved one-dimensional log-concavity assertion supplies the missing normal-band estimate.

For precision, define \(\eta_*\) to be the maximum of (6) over its specified nonempty bands. The first missing assertion in this route is:
there exist absolute constants \(C,A>0\) such that
\[
\eta_*\ge\frac{1}{C(m+1)^A}
\tag{12}
\]
for all of the parameters in this statement. If (12) is established, (7) immediately proves the requested original threshold, with the explicit absolute choice
\[
\kappa=\frac{1}{32C^4g_0}
                \left(\frac{\log g_0}{4A}\right)^{4A}.
\tag{13}
\]
Only this implication is asserted concerning (12); its uniform validity remains open in this packet.

## Proof

The matrix of the heavy-coordinate map is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\v&-u\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whose determinant is \(-au-bv=-1\). Its inverse is the displayed integer inverse. The inequalities \(y_1,y_2\ge0\) become \(uq+bk\ge0\) and \(vq-ak\ge0\), which give exactly \(I_q\). The budget becomes \(\sum_i x_i+g_{\rm ar}q\le L\). Thus the map gives precisely \(D'\). The original \(D\) is the integer-point set of a bounded convex polyhedron, and therefore \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^{m+2}\): its convex hull stays within that polyhedron, whose every integer point belongs to \(D\). The same argument applies to \(D'\), or follows from unimodularity. Adjoining the nonnegative slack \(T-\sum_i x_i\) gives the composition count \(F_m(T)=\binom{T+m}{m}\). Summing the unchanged atom weights over its \(n(q)F_m(L-g_{\rm ar}q)\) atoms at each \(q\) proves (1).

For completeness, an original halfspace
\[
\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf x+\beta_1y_1+\beta_2y_2\ge\beta_0
\]
becomes
\[
\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf x+(u\beta_1+v\beta_2)q
                                  +(b\beta_1-a\beta_2)k\ge\beta_0.
\]
The real linear map is invertible, so nonzero normals remain nonzero and every new halfspace is obtained in this way. Containment of the root, closed boundary atoms, and each atom's probability are all preserved. This verifies the depth convention of 01a4100542206b94 for the entire original support, rather than just selected caps.

Each nonnegative integer \(y_1\) has the unique remainder \(c\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}\) and quotient \(j_1\ge0\); the analogous division of \(y_2\) gives \(d,j_2\). This proves (2)--(4) and disjointness. If two residue pairs have the same \(q_{c,d}\) modulo \(ab\), reduction modulo \(b\) and coprimality imply \(c=c'\); reduction modulo \(a\) implies \(d=d'\). There are \(ab\) pairs, proving the bijection. Since \(0\le q_{c,d}\le2ab-a-b<2ab\), its carry \(\delta_r\) is zero or one. At \(q=ab t+r\), equation (2) is exactly \(j_1+j_2=t-\delta_r\). Its number of nonnegative solutions is (5), including the empty case. Since \(t\ge0\) and \(\delta_r\le1\), increasing \(t\) by one always increases this number by one. No putative lattice point in a missing projected height has been inserted.

We record the uniform transverse estimate supplied by 609407d6db81b18b. For every \(T\ge0\), if \(u_{T,m}\) is uniform on \(\Delta_{T,m}\), then
\[
M_3(u_{T,m})\ge\frac18g_0^m8^{-m}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(T\ge2\), apply that accepted fact with its rank \(r=m\), its dilation symbol \(m\) equal to the present \(T\), and its vector tilt zero. This is the exact alias map. Its composition of a root \(\mathbf j\) is \((T-\sum_i j_i,j_1,\ldots,j_m)\). Its reflected count is exactly
\(K_{\mathbf j}=|\{\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,m}:2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,m}\}|\). Write \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=K_{\mathbf j}/F_m(T)\). The accepted reflection-moment bound is
\(\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,m}(\mathbf j)\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^{\,2}\ge g^m4^{-m}\). Every closed cap containing \(\mathbf j\) contains the root and at least one endpoint of every distinct reflected pair. Uniform endpoint weights therefore give \(\tau_{u_{T,m}}(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}/2\), including boundary roots. Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(s^{3/2}\) gives
\[
M_3(u_{T,m})\ge\tfrac18\mathbb E\mathsf H^3
\ge\tfrac18(\mathbb E\mathsf H^2)^{3/2}
\ge\tfrac18g^{3m/2}8^{-m}\ge\tfrac18g_0^m8^{-m}.
\]
When \(T=1\), the support consists of the \(m+1\) simplex vertices, each of actual depth \(1/(m+1)\), so its third moment is \((m+1)^{-3}\). The inequality \((m+1)^3\le2\cdot4^m\) holds at \(m=1\), and persists by induction because \(((m+2)/(m+1))^3\le27/8<4\). Since \(g_0\le2\), this implies (14). For \(T=0\), the support is a singleton of depth one, again proving (14). This derivation uses the reflection conclusion of the accepted fact; it does not infer a depth threshold from a capacity bound.

The band \(E_{q_0,q_1}\) is the full integer-point set of the bounded convex region
\[
q_0\le q\le q_1,\qquad uq+bk\ge0,\qquad vq-ak\ge0.
\]
Consequently it is complete by the same convex-hull argument as above. The uniform law \(u_E\) on it meets the midpoint-dominance condition of 01a4100542206b94. Apply that fact with its \(N=2,q=3\); the support may be lower-dimensional, as explicitly allowed there. Thus
\[
M_3(u_E)\ge\tfrac14\,8^{-2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Let \(U=u_{T,m}\otimes u_E\) on the product support \(P=\Delta_{T,m}\times E_{q_0,q_1}\). This support is contained in \(D'\), since its largest \(q\) is \(q_1\). An atom of \(U\) has mass \(1/[F_m(T)N_{q_0,q_1}]\). Formula (1), and monotonicity of the exponential on the band, prove the pointwise original-normalization comparison
\[
p'\ge\eta_{q_0,q_1}U
\tag{16}
\]
on the entire lattice, with \(U\) extended by zero off \(P\).

Actual depth is supermultiplicative for this product. Any closed halfspace containing a product root can be translated inward to have its boundary pass through that root, without increasing its mass. For such a centered cap, write its inequality as
\[
\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)
                  +\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(\mathbf V-\mathbf v)\ge0.
\]
The two events that the individual displayed terms are nonnegative have intersection contained in this cap. Their probabilities are at least the actual component depths, and they are independent. A zero coefficient block has event probability one. Therefore
\(\tau_U(\mathbf j,\mathbf v)\ge\tau_{u_{T,m}}(\mathbf j)\tau_{u_E}(\mathbf v)\). Summing with the product root probabilities, (14) and (15) yield
\[
M_3(U)\ge\frac{g_0^m}{32}\,8^{-(m+2)}.
\tag{17}
\]
For every actual original cap, (16) gives \(p'(H)\ge\eta U(H)\), and hence \(\tau_{p'}(\mathbf z)\ge\eta\tau_U(\mathbf z)\) at each product root. Keep its original root weight \(p'(\mathbf z)\ge\eta U(\mathbf z)\), and keep the remaining original roots' nonnegative contributions. This proves
\[
M_3(p)=M_3(p')
\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in P}p'(\mathbf z)(\eta\tau_U(\mathbf z))^3
\ge\eta^4M_3(U),
\]
which is (7). The four powers have exactly one factor from an original root weight and three from its original actual depth.

At a fixed occupied \(q\), the conditional law is exactly the uniform product of \(\Delta_{L-g_{\rm ar}q,m}\) and the whole consecutive interval \(I_q\), at the constant affine coordinate \(q\). Any ambient cap restricts to a cap of that affine slice or the whole slice; conversely every slice cap extends to an ambient cap. Its actual depth therefore has the ordinary product meaning. By 01a4100542206b94 with \(N=1,q=3\), the uniform interval has third depth moment at least \(1/32\), including when it is a singleton. With (14), the conditional product's moment is at least \(g_0^m8^{-m}/256=(g_0^m/4)8^{-(m+2)}\). The original measure pointwise dominates \(w_q\) times this conditional product. Summing the resulting original-root estimates over the disjoint slices proves (8).

At fixed finite \(D'\), (1) shows that as \(\lambda\to+\infty\) the original law tends to the uniform law on the entire occupied top slice \(q=q_*\); every smaller \(q\) is exponentially suppressed. For a fixed root, actual depth is the minimum of finitely many linear mass expressions, since only finitely many intersections of \(D'\) with caps exist. It is continuous in the atom table, including tables with zero atoms. Thus the finite sum defining the third moment also converges. The preceding slice-product estimate proves (9). In particular, no endpoint of the primitive edge was substituted for that edge.

For the claimed residue obstruction, \(m=1,g_{\rm ar}=1,\lambda=0,L=4ab\). The number of atoms in a class is exactly
\[
Z_{c,d}=\sum_{t\ge0}(t+1)\bigl(4ab-q_{c,d}-ab t+1\bigr)
\]
where terms are included only when \(4ab-q_{c,d}-ab t\ge0\). Its maximum is \(Z_{0,0}=20ab+15\). Since \(q_{c,d}<2ab\), the terms at \(t=0,1,2\) are present and give
\[
Z_{c,d}\ge\sum_{t=0}^{2}(t+1)\bigl(ab(2-t)+1\bigr)=4ab+6.
\]
There are \(ab\) disjoint classes. Therefore each original class probability is at most
\[
\frac{20ab+15}{ab(4ab+6)}<\frac5{ab}.
\]
For their probabilities \(W_{c,d}\),
\(\sum W_{c,d}^4\le(\max W_{c,d})^3\sum W_{c,d}<125/(ab)^3\). This obstruction concerns only the residue bridge; the original support is complete and uniform, so the actual original moment satisfies (10) by the accepted erosion theorem with \(N=3,q=3\). It is not a counterexample to the requested threshold.

For (11), the representations of \(q=0,\ldots,6\) by \(2y_1+3y_2\) have counts \(1,0,1,1,1,1,2\), respectively. The transverse scalar count is \(7-q\). Multiplication by the original exponential gives exactly (11). Its zero at \(q=1\) lies between two positive masses. Even its positive consecutive tail fails log-concavity: the square of its mass at \(q=5\) is \(4e^{10\lambda}\), whereas the product of the masses at \(q=4,6\) is \(6e^{10\lambda}\). Thus the exact primitive-height marginal does not justify a one-dimensional log-concavity argument.

Finally, if the presently missing bound (12) is established with absolute \(C,A>0\), choose a maximizing band in (7). With \(r=4A>0\), the elementary inequality \(e^z\ge z\) at \(z=(m+1)\log g_0/r\), raised to \(r\), gives
\[
\frac{g_0^m}{(m+1)^{4A}}
\ge\frac1{g_0}\left(\frac{\log g_0}{4A}\right)^{4A}.
\]
Substitution into (7) proves the conditional conclusion (13). This leaves exactly (12) as the first unresolved arithmetic concentration step in this certificate.
