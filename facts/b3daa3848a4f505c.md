## Statement

Let $\mu$ be the sum of Lebesgue $d$-volume on finitely many compact full-dimensional convex fibers, $d\ge1$. For $L=a\cdot z+u\cdot x-b$ with $u\ne0$, put $H=\{L\ge0\}$, $c=\mu(H)$, $\Psi=\int L_+\,d\mu$, and $T=\int L_+^2\,d\mu$. The last two functions are continuously differentiable in the affine margin parameters, with $D\Psi[\dot L]=\int_H\dot L\,d\mu$ and $DT[\dot L]=2\int_H L\dot L\,d\mu$. Call a cap regular if on every fiber the cut plane meets its interior, is strictly separated from it, or meets it in a supporting section of zero $(d-1)$-measure. At a regular cap let $\tau=\sum_z\mathcal H^{d-1}|_{K_z\cap\{L=0\}}/\lVert u\rVert$, with the base label attached, so $Dc[\dot L]=\int\dot L\,d\tau$. For a $C^1$ cost $\Phi(c,\Psi,T)$ its derivative is the affine moment of the signed measure
$$
\Phi_c\tau+\Phi_\Psi\mu|_H+2\Phi_T L\mu|_H.
$$
An invertible continuous affine change with common absolute fiber determinant $\Delta$ and a unimodular change of integer bases sends the moments of the same margin to $(\Delta c,\Delta\Psi,\Delta T)$. Multiplication of the margin by $s>0$ sends them to $(c,s\Psi,s^2T)$.

## Proof

Each fiber plane $L=0$ has $d$-volume zero. The pointwise affine parameter derivatives of the two integrands off these planes are $\mathbf1_H\dot L$ and $2L_+\dot L$. On a bounded parameter neighborhood and compact source, the absolute values of the integrand difference quotients in unit parameter directions have a common bound. Dominated convergence gives the derivatives, with uniformity in the direction following from the Lipschitz bounds and compactness of the parameter unit sphere. Their coordinate functions are continuous by dominated convergence as the parameters tend to any margin with $u\ne0$. For the cap-mass derivative, slice each fiber along lines parallel to the old unit normal $e=u/\lVert u\rVert$. In the coordinates $x=y+t e$, $y\in e^\perp$, the perturbed cutting height is $(b-a\cdot z-u\cdot y)/(u\cdot e)$ with the perturbed parameters on the right. On each nonempty line interval the retained length is the original upper endpoint minus the larger of the cutting height and the original lower endpoint, truncated below at zero. Away from a cut at a line endpoint, its derivative is minus the height derivative on a proper line cut and zero on a saturated line. For a proper fiber cut the exceptional line labels lie in the relative boundary of the old section, since the relative interior of a section through the body interior consists of body-interior points. For a zero-area supporting section they form a null set by its assumed measure. A strictly separated cut has no exceptional labels. The height variation is bounded uniformly by a constant times the parameter increment on the compact projection. Thus dominated convergence of the line difference quotients gives the derivative of the full fiber volume. At an old section point the negative height derivative is $\dot L/\lVert u\rVert$, giving exactly $Dc[\dot L]=\int\dot L\,d\tau$. The ordinary chain rule now gives the signed measure. In particular positivity of the cost alone imposes no sign on its partial derivatives. The change of variables within each fiber gives the factor $\Delta$ for all three integrals, and the same support and pointwise powers give the second scaling law.
