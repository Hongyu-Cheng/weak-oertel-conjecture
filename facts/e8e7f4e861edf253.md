## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge8\), use precisely the log-affine specialization of accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`: \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) for \(0\le t\le k\), \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), \(b_\ell=\lfloor2^\ell/(8\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^t b_\ell\), \(Q=Q_k\), and \(m=2(Q+1)^4\). Set \(n_{\mathrm{tree}}=n_{m,Q}=\sum_{s=1}^Qm^s\) and \(N=n_{\mathrm{tree}}+1\). These are exactly that predecessor's tree dimension \(n\) and integer dimension \(N\), respectively. Use its entrance heights \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\), and its support
\[
\widehat A=\{(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The rooted-tree objects \(A_{m,Q}\), \(z_s(b)\), and the Boolean-source path length \(h(b)\) have their exact meanings in accepted fact `032423921efad6c8`. Write \(\eta=\eta_m\). Define the complete table by \(w_{(z,t)}=2^{-t}\) on \(\widehat A\), zero elsewhere, and set \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}\), \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). The support \(D\) and integer dimension \(n\) of accepted fact `464815b935df9890` are here exactly \(D=\widehat A\) and \(n=N\). Its canonical array and root law are unchanged:
\[
\pi_{\boldsymbol\epsilon}=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\boldsymbol\epsilon\pmod2}p_{\mathbf I},
\qquad
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=\frac{p_{\mathbf I}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}{\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}},
\qquad
q_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D).
\]
Here \(p\) is zero off \(D\), and \(q_{\mathrm{can}}\) is the row law \(q\) of that accepted fact, not a globally conditioned independent-pair law. All logarithms below are natural.

For every fixed \(\beta\in(0,1)\), let \(E_k(\beta)=\{(z,t)\in\widehat A:t\le\lfloor k^\beta\rfloor\}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(E_k(\beta))&\longrightarrow\beta,
&q_{\mathrm{can}}(E_k(\beta))&\longrightarrow\beta^2,\\
\liminf_{k\to\infty}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\mathrm{can}}\|p)
&\ge\frac34\log3-\log2>0,
&8^N\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}^4
&\le\left(\frac{2^N}{W}\right)^3\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same separation and fourth-moment limit hold after any lattice translation of the table and any common positive rescaling of its weights. The definitions of \(C\), \(S\), and halfspace depth in the locked problem are not changed or identified with this atomic calculation.

## Proof

Accepted fact `604889294a17d7df` gives the exact support, its full-dimensional convex polytope \(\widehat P\), and the globally log-concave zero extension of \(2^{-t}\) from that polytope. Thus the canonical construction of accepted fact `464815b935df9890` applies with the dimension and support substitutions above. In particular it is a probability array with source marginal \(p\), midpoint root law \(q_{\mathrm{can}}\), and positive root mass at every site of \(D\).

The construction can equivalently be sampled by choosing a full integer parity class with probability \(\pi\), then choosing two endpoints independently from \(p\) conditional on that class, and taking their midpoint. This equivalence is part of `464815b935df9890`; directly, an ordered pair of same-parity endpoints \(\mathbf I,\mathbf K\) has probability \(p_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf K}/\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}\). For its midpoint \(\mathbf J\), this is exactly \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\). The two original endpoint marginals therefore remain \(p\), and the class sampling weights are \(\pi\), not weights proportional to \(\pi^2\).

We first prove a uniform estimate for the height counts. Put \(\omega_t=(1+Q_t)2^{-t}\) for \(0\le t\le k\). For every integer \(8\le t\le k\), we claim
\[
\left|\omega_t-\frac1{4t}\right|\le\frac4{t^2}.
\]
Since each floor error \(2^\ell/(8\ell)-b_\ell\) lies in \([0,1)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_t
&=\frac18\sum_{\ell=8}^t\frac{2^{\ell-t}}{\ell}
 +2^{-t}\left(1-\sum_{\ell=8}^t\left(\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}-b_\ell\right)\right),\\
\left|2^{-t}\left(1-\sum_{\ell=8}^t\left(\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}-b_\ell\right)\right)\right|
&\le t2^{-t}\le\frac2{t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality follows because the sequence \(t^3 2^{-t}\) is decreasing for integers \(t\ge8\): its consecutive-term ratio is at most \((9/8)^3/2<1\), and its value at eight is two. For the first sum, change variables \(\iota=t-\ell\). Since \(\sum_{\iota=0}^{\infty}2^{-\iota}=2\),
\[
\sum_{\ell=8}^t\frac{2^{\ell-t}}{\ell}-\frac2t
 =\sum_{\iota=0}^{t-8}\frac{\iota2^{-\iota}}{t(t-\iota)}-\frac{2^{8-t}}t.
\]
For \(\iota\le t/2\), the sum of these nonnegative terms is at most \(2t^{-2}\sum_{\iota=0}^{\infty}\iota2^{-\iota}=4/t^2\). The identity \(\sum\iota2^{-\iota}=2\) follows by writing \(\iota=\sum_{j=1}^{\iota}1\) and summing the resulting geometric tails. For \(\iota>t/2\) in this finite sum, \(t-\iota\ge8\) and \(\iota/t\le1\). Their sum is therefore at most
\[
\frac18\sum_{\iota>t/2}2^{-\iota}\le\frac14\,2^{-t/2}\le\frac1{t^2}.
\]
Here \(t^2 2^{-t/2}\le4\) for integers \(t\ge8\): its consecutive-term ratio is at most \((9/8)^2/\sqrt2<1\), and its value at eight is four. Similarly \(t2^{8-t}\le8\), since its consecutive-term ratio is at most \(9/16<1\). Thus the remaining tail has size at most \(8/t^2\). The absolute difference of the first sum from \(2/t\) is at most \(13/t^2\). Combining the two estimates gives \(13/(8t^2)+2/t^2<4/t^2\), proving the claim with a constant independent of \(t\), \(k\), and the Boolean-chain partition.

For \(e\in\{0,1\}\) and integers \(0\le T\le k\), define
\[
Z_e(T)=\sum_{\substack{0\le t\le T\\t\equiv e\pmod2}}\omega_t,
\qquad Z(T)=Z_0(T)+Z_1(T).
\]
For \(t<8\), \(\omega_t=2^{-t}\). The uniform estimate just proved and convergence of \(\sum_{t=8}^{\infty}t^{-2}\) show that its error contributes a bounded amount to each \(Z_e(T)\); convergence follows, for example, from comparison with the integral of \(x^{-2}\) on \([7,\infty)\). Integral comparison also gives \(\sum_{j=1}^u1/j=\log u+O(1)\) with an absolute bound on the error. The sum over positive even \(t\le T\) is one half of this harmonic sum at \(\lfloor T/2\rfloor\); the odd sum is the full harmonic sum minus the even sum. Removing the finitely many \(t<8\) terms does not change the bounded error. Hence, uniformly for integers \(8\le T\le k\),
\[
Z_e(T)=\frac18\log T+O(1)\quad(e=0,1),
\qquad Z(T)=\frac14\log T+O(1).
\]

We next calculate the source height distribution and control the mass of every short Boolean-source class. Accepted fact `032423921efad6c8` gives exactly \(2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\eta^s\) tree points at level \(s\), exactly one Boolean source \(b\) for each tree parity class, and exactly \(2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\eta^Q\) Boolean sources with \(h(b)=Q\). A level \(s\) point occurs at height \(t\) precisely when \(s\le Q_t\): for \(s>0\) this is the defining entrance-height condition, and level zero occurs at every nonnegative height. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z:(z,t)\in\widehat A}w_{(z,t)}
 &=2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}2^{-t}\sum_{s=0}^{Q_t}\eta^s,\\
\eta^Q\omega_t
 &\le2^{-t}\sum_{s=0}^{Q_t}\eta^s\le\omega_t,\\
2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\eta^Q Z(k)
 &\le W\le2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}Z(k).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the chosen \(m\), the central-binomial estimate in `032423921efad6c8` yields \(0<1-\eta<(Q+1)^{-2}\). Bernoulli's inequality, which here follows by induction on the exponent, gives
\[
0\le1-\eta^Q\le Q(1-\eta)<\frac{Q}{(Q+1)^2}\longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(Q\ge b_k\ge2^k/(8k)-1\longrightarrow\infty\). Summing the height bounds through \(T\) and dividing by the bounds on \(W\) gives
\[
\eta^Q\frac{Z(T)}{Z(k)}\le p\{t\le T\}\le\eta^{-Q}\frac{Z(T)}{Z(k)}.
\]
For \(T=\lfloor k^\beta\rfloor\), the logarithm satisfies \(\log T/\log k\longrightarrow\beta\), so the preceding estimates prove \(p(E_k(\beta))\longrightarrow\beta\).

A full integer parity class is specified by a tree Boolean source \(b\in\{0,1\}^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\) and a height parity \(e\in\{0,1\}\). Its sites are exactly
\[
\{(z_s(b),t):0\le s\le h(b),\ a_s\le t\le k,\ t\equiv e\pmod2\}.
\]
Every one of these classes has positive mass, since level zero permits height zero and height one. At a fixed height \(t\equiv e\pmod2\), the number of sites in this class is \(1+\min\{h(b),Q_t\}\). If \(h(b)=Q\), its total unnormalized mass is exactly \(Z_e(k)\), its canonical class probability is \(\pi_{(b,e)}=Z_e(k)/W\), and its conditional endpoint height probability at \(t\) is \(\omega_t/Z_e(k)\). This conditional height law is the same for every full-length \(b\), but retains its even or odd height restriction. Any Boolean source of shorter length has total mass across the two height parities at most \(Z(k)\).

The total \(p\)-mass of the endpoint classes with \(h(b)=Q\) is exactly
\[
\frac{2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\eta^Q Z(k)}{W}\ge\eta^Q.
\]
Thus the total sampling probability of all short endpoint classes, with both height parities included, is at most \(1-\eta^Q\longrightarrow0\). This is a bound on their mass, not merely on their number. The full-length or short classification refers to the sampled endpoint class; the midpoint need not have the same parity as either endpoint.

Condition now on an endpoint class \((b,e)\) with \(h(b)=Q\). The two endpoint heights \(t_1,t_2\) are independent with the height law just obtained. Their midpoint is an integer point of \(\widehat P\), hence lies in \(\widehat A\), and its actual last coordinate is exactly \((t_1+t_2)/2\). This coordinate is not the tree level. The same height parity ensures it is an integer, for either \(e=0\) or \(e=1\). Since all heights are nonnegative, the events satisfy
\[
\{t_1\le T,\ t_2\le T\}
 \subseteq\{(t_1+t_2)/2\le T\}
 \subseteq\{t_1\le2T,\ t_2\le2T\}.
\]
For \(T=\lfloor k^\beta\rfloor\), eventually \(2T\le k\). Independence therefore bounds the conditional probability of the middle event between \((Z_e(T)/Z_e(k))^2\) and \((Z_e(2T)/Z_e(k))^2\). For each of the two height parities, both ratios tend to \(\beta\), by the uniform logarithmic estimates. Consequently the conditional midpoint probability tends to \(\beta^2\), uniformly over all full-length Boolean sources and both height parities. The remaining classes have total sampling probability at most \(1-\eta^Q\); their conditional midpoint probabilities lie between zero and one. Averaging with the canonical weights \(\pi_{(b,e)}\) proves \(q_{\mathrm{can}}(E_k(\beta))\longrightarrow\beta^2\). No class-count approximation or independence across different parity classes is used.

For completeness, binary coarse-graining gives a relative-entropy lower bound without an additional theorem. For a nonempty proper subset \(E\subset D\), both \(q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)\) and \(p(E)\) lie in \((0,1)\), because both laws have full support. Normalizing each law within \(E\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf J\in E}q_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf J}\log\frac{q_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf J}}{p_{\mathbf J}}
&=q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)\log\frac{q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)}{p(E)}\\
&\quad+q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q_{\mathrm{can}}(\cdot\mid E)\|p(\cdot\mid E)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
For any probability laws \(\xi,\zeta\) with \(\zeta>0\) on the support of \(\xi\), the inequality \(\log x\le x-1\) gives \(\sum \xi\log(\xi/\zeta)\ge1-\sum_{\xi>0}\zeta\ge0\). Thus the conditional divergence is nonnegative. The same expansion on \(D\setminus E\) proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\mathrm{can}}\|p)
&\ge q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)\log\frac{q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)}{p(E)}\\
&\quad+(1-q_{\mathrm{can}}(E))\log\frac{1-q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)}{1-p(E)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Take \(E=E_k(1/2)\), which is nonempty and proper for \(k\ge8\). The established limits are \(q_{\mathrm{can}}(E)\longrightarrow1/4\) and \(p(E)\longrightarrow1/2\). Continuity of the binary expression yields
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\mathrm{can}}\|p)
 \ge\frac14\log\frac12+\frac34\log\frac32
 =\frac34\log3-\log2>0.
\]
The final strict inequality follows from \(27>16\), since four times this constant is \(\log(27/16)\).

The fourth-moment bound uses the original table normalization \(0\le w_{\mathbf I}\le1\). It gives \(\sum w_{\mathbf I}^4\le\sum w_{\mathbf I}=W\), and therefore
\[
8^N\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}^4
 =\frac{8^N}{W^4}\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^4
 \le\frac{8^N}{W^3}
 =\left(\frac{2^N}{W}\right)^3\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed the bounds above imply \(W/2^N\sim(\log k)/8\longrightarrow\infty\), also consistent with the exact log-affine asymptotic of `604889294a17d7df`. Here the factor two is essential: \(N=n_{\mathrm{tree}}+1\), so \(2^N=2\cdot2^{n_{\mathrm{tree}}}\).

Finally, for a lattice translation \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z^N\) and a common rescaling \(\gamma>0\), define \(w'_{\mathbf I+\mathbf a}=\gamma w_{\mathbf I}\). Then \(W'=\gamma W\), \(p'_{\mathbf I+\mathbf a}=p_{\mathbf I}\), and \(\pi'_{(\mathbf I+\mathbf a)\bmod2}=\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}\). Since \(2(\mathbf J+\mathbf a)-(\mathbf I+\mathbf a)=(2\mathbf J-\mathbf I)+\mathbf a\), the canonical formula gives \(r'_{\mathbf J+\mathbf a,\mathbf I+\mathbf a}=r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\) and \(q'_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf J+\mathbf a}=q_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf J}\). Relative entropy and \(8^N\sum p_{\mathbf I}^4\) are consequently unchanged. The translated event \(E_k(\beta)+\mathbf a\) has the same two probabilities; its height cutoff is shifted by the last coordinate of \(\mathbf a\). This verifies the translation and probability-normalization assertions without substituting a different midpoint law or asserting a conclusion about actual mixed-integer depth.
