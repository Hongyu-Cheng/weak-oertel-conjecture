## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, with mixed set measured by $\mathcal H_q$ for its maximal integer fiber dimension $q\ge1$. At distinct integer bases $z_j$ with positive $q$-volume put $V_j=\mathcal H_q(C_{z_j})$ and $v_j=V_j^{1/q}$. Fix $\theta_j>0$ summing to one, with $p=\sum_j\theta_jz_j$ an integer base whose section has dimension $q$. Let $H$ be one original closed ambient halfspace of total measured mixed mass at most $B$, where $0<B<\min_j V_j$, and let $u=\mathcal H_q(C_p\cap H^c)^{1/q}$. Then
$$
\sum_j\theta_jv_j-u\le\max_j\theta_j\big[v_j-(V_j-B)^{1/q}\big].
$$
If $\delta=\sum_j\theta_jv_j-u>0$, there is an index $j$ with $\delta<\theta_jv_j$ and $B\ge V_j-(v_j-\delta/\theta_j)^q$. For equal destination volume $V$ and equal weights $1/N$, this gives $V^{1/q}-u\le [V^{1/q}-(V-B)^{1/q}]/N$.

## Proof

Write $A_j=\mathcal H_q(C_{z_j}\cap H)$. Since the destinations are distinct, their captures sum to at most $B$, even if the source is also a destination. Every strict complement section at a destination has positive $q$-volume $V_j-A_j$. Express $H$ by an affine inequality $L\ge0$. For $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently small, the compact destination subcuts with $L\le-\varepsilon$ have positive intrinsic $q$-volume. Their weighted Minkowski sum lies in the source strict complement by convexity and affinity of $L$. The direction space of the sum contains each destination direction space, by fixing all other summands. Since the source has dimension $q$ and the subcuts each span their own $q$-dimensional fiber, all these direction spaces agree. Translate to this common Euclidean space and apply Brunn–Minkowski. The subcuts increase to the strict complements as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, so
$$
u\ge\sum_j\theta_j(V_j-A_j)^{1/q}.
$$

For $0\le s\le B$ let $F_j(s)=\theta_j[v_j-(V_j-s)^{1/q}]$. It is increasing and convex with $F_j(0)=0$, so $F_j(A_j)\le(A_j/B)F_j(B)$. Summing gives $\sum_j F_j(A_j)\le\max_j F_j(B)$, and subtracting the Brunn–Minkowski inequality from the whole-root average gives the claim. The maximum over the relaxed allocation simplex is attained by spending all $B$ at a maximizing index. A given actual cap can impose additional constraints on its allocations.

For $\delta>0$ choose a maximizing index in the scalar bound. As $B<V_j$, the remaining root is positive and hence $\delta<\theta_jv_j$. Rearrange and raise to the positive power $q$ to obtain the inverted budget test. Conversely a qualifying index with this budget inequality makes its scalar term at least $\delta$, so the two necessary scalar tests agree under the same budget hypothesis. Substituting equal weights and volumes gives the stated equal-row bound.
