## Statement

For every integer \(r\geq 13\), set \(n=d=r\).  Let \(\mathbf 1\in\mathbb R^r\) be the all-one vector, put
\[
 T_r:=\operatorname{conv}\{-\mathbf 1,e_1,\ldots,e_r\},
 \qquad
 t_r:=\left(\frac{r!}{r+1}\right)^{1/r},
 \qquad
 K_r:=t_rT_r,
\]
and let \(w:[0,3]\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) be the piecewise-linear function with
\[
 w(0)=\frac15,\qquad w(1)=\frac35,\qquad
 w(2)=1,\qquad w(3)=\frac25.
\]
Define
\[
 g_r(x):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^r w(x_i)\right)^{1/r}
 \quad (x\in[0,3]^r)
\]
and
\[
 C_r:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^r:
 x\in[0,3]^r,\ y\in g_r(x)K_r\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(C_r\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body.  The simplex \(K_r\) has volume one and centroid zero.  The positive lattice fibers of \(C_r\) are indexed exactly by
\(z\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r\), and their volumes are
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_r((C_r)_z)
 =\prod_{i=1}^r w(z_i).
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if
\(S_r=C_r\cap(\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^r)\), then its mixed mass is
\[
 W_r:=\mathcal H_r(S_r)=\left(\frac{11}{5}\right)^r,
 \qquad
 2^{-r}W_r=\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r.
\tag{3}
\]

Put
\[
 \gamma_r:=\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^r,
 \qquad
 q_r:=(1-\gamma_r)^{1/r}.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(A,B,\sigma\geq0\), define
\[
 \Phi_{r,\sigma}(A,B)
 :=A^r+B^r-
 \max\{s^r+t^r:0\leq s\leq A,\ 0\leq t\leq B,\ s+t\leq\sigma\},
\]
\[
 \Psi_{r,\sigma}(A,B):=
 \min\{\Phi_{r,\sigma}(A,B),A^r,B^r\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For a positive-fiber base point \(m\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r\), let
\[
 \mathcal P_r(m):=
 \bigl\{\{z,z'\}:z,z'\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r,
 z\ne z',\ z+z'=2m\bigr\}
\tag{6}
\]
and define the complete centroid-anchor support-safe pair certificate
\[
 \mathcal E_r(m):=
 \gamma_ra_m+
 \sum_{\{z,z'\}\in\mathcal P_r(m)}
 \Psi_{r,\,2q_ra_m^{1/r}}
 \bigl(a_z^{1/r},a_{z'}^{1/r}\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
The point \((m,0)\) is the centroid of its fiber and has exact absolute fiber depth \(\gamma_ra_m\).  The pairs in (6) are pairwise disjoint reflection orbits, so accepted fact `aa56458a4606be95` gives
\[
 h_{S_r}(m,0)\geq\mathcal E_r(m).
\tag{8}
\]
Nevertheless, uniformly for every such centroid anchor,
\[
 \mathcal E_r(m)
 \leq 1+3P_r,
 \qquad
 P_r:=\sum_{j=0}^{12}\binom rj3^j.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \frac{\max_{m\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r}\mathcal E_r(m)}
 {2^{-r}W_r}
 \leq
 \frac{1+3P_r}{(11/10)^r}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus no absolute \(c>0\) can make the complete expression (7), maximized over all fiber-centroid anchors, at least \(c2^{-r}W_r\) for every mixed-integer convex body.

This family is not a counterexample to the locked weak Oertel statement.  The constant affine map \(s:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R^r\), \(s(z)=0\), satisfies
\[
 Q_{C_r}(s)=\gamma_rW_r.
\tag{11}
\]
Accepted fact `287974843b6abd69` therefore yields
\[
 \max_{p\in S_r}h_{S_r}(p)
 \geq\gamma_r2^{-r}W_r
 >e^{-1}2^{-r}W_r.
\tag{12}
\]
The quantitative no-go assertion (9)-(10) concerns only anchors \((m,0)\) at the fiber centroids.  It makes no assertion about the pair-energy expression at an arbitrary non-centroid anchor \((m,x_0)\), and in particular does not rule out such anchors.

## Proof

The vertices of \(T_r\) have sum zero, so the centroid of this simplex is zero.  Taking \(-\mathbf1\) as the reference vertex, its edge matrix is \(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), whose determinant is \(r+1\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_r(T_r)=\frac{r+1}{r!},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{vol}_r(K_r)=t_r^r\operatorname{vol}_r(T_r)=1.
\tag{13}
\]
Equal positive barycentric coordinates also show that zero is in the interior of \(K_r\).

The slopes of \(w\) on the three unit intervals are \(2/5,2/5,-3/5\).  They are nonincreasing, so \(w\) is concave, and its four displayed values show that it is positive.  The geometric mean
\[
 G(u_1,\ldots,u_r):=(u_1\cdots u_r)^{1/r}
\]
is coordinatewise increasing and concave on the positive orthant.  Indeed, for \(u_i>0\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^r\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2G(u)v
 =\frac{G(u)}{r^2}
 \left[
 \left(\sum_i\frac{v_i}{u_i}\right)^2
 -r\sum_i\left(\frac{v_i}{u_i}\right)^2
 \right]\leq0
\tag{14}
\]
by Cauchy-Schwarz.  Coordinatewise concavity of \(w\), monotonicity of \(G\), and then concavity of \(G\) show that \(g_r\) is concave on \([0,3]^r\).

Let \(\rho_{K_r}\) be the Minkowski gauge of \(K_r\).  Since zero is interior to \(K_r\), this is a finite continuous convex function and
\[
 C_r=\{(x,y):x\in[0,3]^r,\ \rho_{K_r}(y)-g_r(x)\leq0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The function in the last inequality is convex, so (15) proves convexity.  Continuity and boundedness of the box, of \(g_r\), and of \(K_r\) prove compactness.  At \(x=(3/2,\ldots,3/2)\) the box inequalities are strict and
\(\rho_{K_r}(0)-g_r(x)<0\); hence \((x,0)\) is an interior point of \(C_r\).  Thus \(C_r\) is full dimensional in \(\mathbb R^{2r}\).

For every real \(x\in[0,3]^r\), the fiber in (1) is exactly \(g_r(x)K_r\).  The only integer base points in the box are \(\{0,1,2,3\}^r\).  Since \(g_r\) is positive and \(K_r\) has volume one, scaling gives (2).  Summing the product weights and using
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^3w(j)=\frac15+\frac35+1+\frac25=\frac{11}{5}
\]
gives (3).

We next compute the exact fiber depth at zero.  Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace inequality, stated precisely under EXTERNAL SOURCES, says that every closed halfspace containing the centroid of an \(r\)-dimensional convex body contains at least a \(\gamma_r\) fraction of its volume.  It remains to verify equality for \(K_r\).  Let \(\lambda_0\) be the barycentric coordinate corresponding to the vertex \(-t_r\mathbf1\).  At the centroid zero, \(\lambda_0=1/(r+1)\), and
\[
 K_r\cap\left\{\lambda_0\geq\frac1{r+1}\right\}
 =-t_r\mathbf1+\frac r{r+1}
 \bigl(K_r+t_r\mathbf1\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]
The halfspace in (16) has zero on its boundary, and the displayed homothet has volume \(\gamma_r\operatorname{vol}_r(K_r)=\gamma_r\).  The lower inequality and (16) prove that the absolute halfspace depth of zero in \(K_r\) is exactly \(\gamma_r\).  Homothetic scaling therefore gives
\[
 \delta_{C_r}(z,0)=\gamma_rg_r(z)^r=\gamma_ra_z
\tag{17}
\]
at every positive lattice fiber.  Its centroid is zero because the centroid of \(K_r\) is zero.

Fix \(m\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r\).  In the notation of accepted fact `aa56458a4606be95`, equations (17) and (4) give
\[
 \beta=\gamma_r,\qquad q=q_r,\qquad
 R=a_m^{1/r},\qquad S_0=2q_ra_m^{1/r}.
\tag{18}
\]
Reflection about \(m\) is an involution.  Its nonfixed support orbits are exactly the unordered pairs in (6), so these pairs are pairwise disjoint.  Any reflected pair with an endpoint outside the positive support has one zero endpoint volume and hence zero \(\Psi\)-term.  Thus (7) is the complete nonzero support-safe reflected-pair right-hand side from that accepted fact, and (8) follows.

It remains to count the pairs that contribute positively to (7).  Choose an ordering \((z,z')\) of one pair, where \(z'=2m-z\), and write
\[
 \alpha_i:=w(z_i),\qquad \beta_i:=w(z'_i),\qquad
 c_i:=w(m_i),
\]
\[
 A:=\left(\prod_i\alpha_i\right)^{1/r},\qquad
 B:=\left(\prod_i\beta_i\right)^{1/r},\qquad
 R:=\left(\prod_i c_i\right)^{1/r}.
\tag{19}
\]
All these numbers are positive.  The endpoint formula for \(\Phi\) in accepted fact `aa56458a4606be95` shows that
\[
 \Psi_{r,2q_rR}(A,B)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A+B>2q_rR.
\tag{20}
\]
Call such an ordered endpoint, or its reflected pair, productive.  Put
\(\lambda=A/(A+B)\in(0,1)\).  Since
\[
 A+B=\frac{A^\lambda B^{1-\lambda}}
 {\lambda^\lambda(1-\lambda)^{1-\lambda}},
\]
raising to the \(r\)-th power gives the exact factorization
\[
 \left(\frac{A+B}{2R}\right)^r
 =\prod_{i=1}^r
 \frac{\alpha_i^\lambda\beta_i^{1-\lambda}}
 {2c_i\lambda^\lambda(1-\lambda)^{1-\lambda}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Weighted AM-GM, applied to \(\alpha_i/\lambda\) and
\(\beta_i/(1-\lambda)\), gives
\[
 \frac{\alpha_i^\lambda\beta_i^{1-\lambda}}
 {\lambda^\lambda(1-\lambda)^{1-\lambda}}
 \leq\alpha_i+\beta_i.
\tag{22}
\]
The equality \((z_i+z'_i)/2=m_i\), together with concavity of \(w\), gives
\(\alpha_i+\beta_i\leq2c_i\).  Hence every local factor in (21) is at most one.

The only possible nonself local reflection about \(m_i=2\) has endpoints \(1,3\) in one of the two orders.  Its weights are \(3/5,2/5\), whereas \(c_i=1\).  By (22), its factor in (21) is at most \(1/2\).  On the other hand,
\[
 \theta_r:=q_r^r=1-\gamma_r\geq\frac12,
\tag{23}
\]
because the binomial theorem gives
\((1+1/r)^r\geq2\), hence \(\gamma_r\leq1/2\).  Productivity and (21) require the product of the local factors to be strictly larger than \(\theta_r\).  Since all other factors are at most one, (23) rules out every productive pair having a nonself \(m_i=2\) coordinate.

For all remaining local states one has \(\alpha_i+\beta_i=2c_i\).  Define
\[
 p_i:=\frac{\alpha_i}{\alpha_i+\beta_i}.
\]
Direct enumeration of the possible reflections gives
\[
 p_i\in\left\{\frac16,\frac12,\frac56\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
More explicitly, reflection about \(m_i=1\) permits the ordered endpoints
\((0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\), giving the three values in (24), respectively.  Reflection about \(m_i=0\) or \(3\) is necessarily self, and after the preceding exclusion the only productive state about \(m_i=2\) is also self; these states all give \(p_i=1/2\).

Using \(\alpha_i=2c_ip_i\) and
\(\beta_i=2c_i(1-p_i)\), the local factor in (21) is exactly
\[
 \exp\bigl(-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\lambda\Vert p_i)\bigr),
\]
where
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\Vert p)
 :=u\log\frac up+(1-u)\log\frac{1-u}{1-p}.
\]
Thus (20), (21), and (23) imply
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^rD_{\mathrm{KL}}(\lambda\Vert p_i)
 <-\log\theta_r\leq\log2.
\tag{25}
\]
For completeness, Bernoulli Pinsker in the needed normalization is
\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\Vert p)\geq2(u-p)^2.
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed, as a function of \(u\), the difference between the two sides has second derivative
\(1/[u(1-u)]-4\geq0\), and its value and first derivative vanish at \(u=p\).

For \(r\geq13\), there is a unique
\[
 p_*\in\left\{\frac16,\frac12,\frac56\right\}
 \quad\text{such that}\quad |\lambda-p_*|<\frac16.
\tag{27}
\]
The three open intervals in (27) are pairwise disjoint, proving uniqueness.  If none existed, (24) and (26) would make the left side of (25) at least \(r/18\geq13/18>\log2\), a contradiction.  The last strict inequality can be checked without decimals: the exponential series gives
\[
 e^{7/10}>1+\frac7{10}+\frac{(7/10)^2}{2}
 +\frac{(7/10)^3}{6}
 =\frac{12013}{6000}>2,
\]
so \(\log2<7/10<13/18\).

If \(p_i\ne p_*\), the spacing of the three values in (24) and (27) gives
\(|\lambda-p_i|>1/6\).  By (26), each such coordinate contributes more than \(1/18\) to (25).  There can therefore be at most twelve exceptional coordinates:
\[
 \#\{i:p_i\ne p_*\}\leq12.
\tag{28}
\]

Now fix the anchor \(m\) and one of the three possible values of \(p_*\).  Outside the exceptional set, the ordered coordinate \(z_i\) is uniquely determined by \((m_i,p_*)\), if such a state exists at all.  At an exceptional coordinate there are at most three choices for \(z_i\).  Choosing the exceptional set and then its coordinates shows that the number of productive ordered endpoints for this fixed \(p_*\) is at most
\[
 P_r=\sum_{j=0}^{12}\binom rj3^j.
\]
Allowing the three choices of \(p_*\) gives at most \(3P_r\) productive ordered endpoints, uniformly in \(m\).  The number of productive unordered pairs is no larger.

Every fiber mass is at most one because all four values of \(w\) are at most one.  By definition,
\[
 \Psi_{r,\sigma}(A,B)\leq\min\{A^r,B^r\},
\]
so every productive pair contributes at most one.  The anchor term
\(\gamma_ra_m\) is also at most one.  Nonproductive pairs contribute zero.  This proves (9).

For \(r\geq13\),
\[
 P_r\leq13\cdot3^{12}r^{12}.
\tag{29}
\]
The sequence \(r^{12}(10/11)^r\) tends to zero, for example because the ratio of consecutive terms tends to \(10/11<1\).  Equations (3), (9), and (29) prove (10).  Equivalently, for every \(c>0\), all sufficiently large \(r\) in this family satisfy
\[
 \max_m\mathcal E_r(m)<c2^{-r}W_r.
\]

Finally, let \(s(z)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^r\).  It is an affine map, and it meets every positive lattice fiber at its centroid.  Equations (2) and (17) therefore give
\[
 Q_{C_r}(s)=\sum_{z\in\{0,1,2,3\}^r}\gamma_ra_z
 =\gamma_rW_r,
\]
which is (11).  Applying accepted fact `287974843b6abd69` gives the first inequality in (12).  Moreover,
\[
 \gamma_r=\left(1+\frac1r\right)^{-r}>e^{-1},
\]
because \(\log(1+1/r)<1/r\).  This proves the strict inequality in (12) and confirms that the construction separates the two accepted mechanisms rather than disproving the locked target.  Nothing in the productive-pair analysis applies to a non-centroid point \(x_0\ne0\) in a fiber, whose depth ratio and support-safe threshold can differ.  Therefore the claimed obstruction is precisely restricted to all centroid anchors.
