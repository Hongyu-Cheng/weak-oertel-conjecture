## Statement

Let $P$ be a fixed finite base set and let $K_{z,l}\subset\mathbb R^m$ be compact convex sets, all in one bounded region, satisfying the same specified anchored inclusions for each $l$. Fix a common orthogonal projection $\pi$ to $\mathbb R^q$ with $0\le q<m$. Suppose $M_l=\sum_{z\in P}|K_{z,l}|>0$ and the projected volume measures divided by $M_l$ converge weakly to finite measures $\nu_z$ of positive mass $w_z$ and full affine support dimension $q$ in the projection space. Then the simultaneous ball replacements at scale $1/M_l$ converge in Hausdorff distance along the entire sequence to the unique centered-ball lifts of $\nu_z$. The limits have full $m$-dimension and volumes $w_z$, project exactly to $\nu_z$, and preserve the anchored inclusions. If the original limiting laws are carried by a common affine $q$-plane on which $\pi$ is an isometry, the projected laws identify those full laws.

## Proof

Use the common-scale ball replacement at $c=1/M_l$ and call the bodies $L_{z,l}$. Each has volume at most one and the prescribed projected measure by the replacement identity. For $q>0$, choose $q+1$ affinely independent points in the support of $\nu_z$. Small neighborhoods of these points have positive mass and every choice of one point from each spans a simplex containing a ball of some fixed positive radius $\rho_z$: this follows, for example, by continuity of the simplex's barycentric coordinates in its nondegenerate vertices around the centroid of the reference simplex. By lower Portmanteau each projected body meets all neighborhoods for large $l$. The base projections therefore contain such a ball, and are bounded above by the old container. If $H$ is the largest hidden radius, convexity gives radius at least $H/2$ on the midpoint of that ball with a point attaining $H$. It follows that
$$
1\ge\kappa_q(\rho_z/2)^q\,\kappa_{m-q}(H/2)^{m-q}.
$$
Thus the replacements lie in a common compact region for each $z$. For $q=0$ they are centered $m$-balls of volumes converging to $w_z$, so the assertion follows directly.

For $q>0$, Blaschke selection and finiteness of $P$ give simultaneous Hausdorff subsequential limits. Volume continuity and $w_z>0$ make each limit full dimensional with volume $w_z$. The volumes converge as measures, because indicators of Hausdorff-converging convex bodies converge off the null boundary of the full-dimensional limit. Projection and weak convergence then identify each limit's projected volume as $\nu_z$. Every $L_{z,l}$ is invariant under the orthogonal group on the hidden factor, including reflection if $m-q=1$. The invariance survives Hausdorff limits, so all nonempty hidden sections of the limit are centered balls. The projected density determines the radius almost everywhere, and the radius is continuous on the projection interior by concavity. The projection itself is the support of the projected measure: each projection of an interior point has a positive-radius neighborhood of positive mass, and their closure is the entire projection. Finally the full-dimensional convex body is the closure of its interior, so the density also determines the closed lift at the boundary. This proves uniqueness of the subsequential limit. Relative compactness now implies full-sequence convergence, and continuity of finite Minkowski sums passes each original inclusion to the limit. The common-plane assertion follows by the affine inverse of $\pi$ on that plane.
