## Statement

All logarithms except \(\log_2\) are natural. Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For every \(i\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, with positive weights \(w_{i,x}\). Assume that every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\) satisfies
$$
j=(x+y)/2\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^2=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{1}
$$
Retain the original normalizers, laws, and occupied parity counts
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&
p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i,&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&
N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Extend the laws by zero off their supports. For every supported product center \(\mathbf j\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
  \mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,\\
M_r(P)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\qquad(r=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

First fix an integer \(M\ge1\) and a real \(R\ge1\), and put
$$
m_R=\lceil\log_2(2R)\rceil,\qquad
\beta_R=2^{m_R+1}-1,\qquad
C=2048M^6R^2\beta_R^2,
$$
$$
T_0=2\beta_R\log(4C),\qquad
c_{M,R}=\frac14\exp(-48C^2).
\tag{4}
$$
Assume each parity class of each \(A_i\) contains at most \(M\) atoms. For each factor suppose there is a finite list of bases \(T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,\ell_i}\), possibly empty, such that
$$
T_{i,1}\ge T_0,\qquad T_{i,b+1}\ge4R\,T_{i,b}
\tag{5}
$$
whenever the indices exist, and every distinct unequal-weight same-parity pair satisfies
$$
\left|\frac12\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\in\bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},RT_{i,b}].
\tag{6}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_{M,R}\prod_iK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_{M,R}^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}.
\tag{7}
$$

There is also a heterogeneous version with an absolute coefficient. For every factor separately, let \(M_i\ge1\) be an integer and \(R_i\ge1\) a real number, and set
$$
\beta_i=2^{\lceil\log_2(2R_i)\rceil+1}-1,\qquad
C_i=2048M_i^6R_i^2\beta_i^2.
\tag{8}
$$
Assume each parity class of \(A_i\) contains at most \(M_i\) atoms and that its unequal-weight same-parity scores are contained in a finite union
\(\bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},R_iT_{i,b}]\), with
$$
T_{i,1}\ge8\beta_i(\log C_i+1),\qquad
T_{i,b+1}\ge4R_iT_{i,b}.
\tag{9}
$$
Then, with the single absolute number
$$
c_{\mathrm{abs}}=\frac14\exp(-3600),
$$
one has
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{abs}}\prod_iK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{abs}}^{3/2}\prod_iK_i^{-3}.
\tag{10}
$$
There is no common upper bound on \(M_i\) or \(R_i\) in this second assertion.

Both assertions allow bands containing no scores, equal-weight pairs, factors without unequal-weight pairs, singleton supports, and lower-dimensional supports. An empty band list requires the absence of unequal-weight same-parity pairs. There is no bound on the number of factors, bands, dimensions, occupied counts, or total spectral span. The stronger threshold (9) is a substantive hypothesis; neither assertion covers arbitrary score spectra or proves the universal mixed-integer-volume conjecture.

## Proof

The full reflected quantities below have the normalization of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. The same-weight energy \(E\), full energy \(F\), scaled atom masses, unnormalized defect, and all-mixed-pair loss retain their definitions from accepted facts 0539ec4ffa895fed and 5e2dee70e6a96862. The quantity \(B_*\) below is the largest-atom-star sum of 0539ec4ffa895fed; it is not the all-balanced-pair sum \(\mathcal B_2\) of 5e2dee70e6a96862. The parameters \(m_R,\beta_R\) keep their exact meanings from 5e2dee70e6a96862. We rederive the transfer, recurrence, selection, and halfspace conversion, without applying either predecessor outside its hypotheses.

Fix one factor satisfying the common scalar hypotheses with parameters \(M,R\) and first base at least \(T_0\), and suppress its index. For every original center \(j\in A\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,&
\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j),\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace x\in R_j:w_x=w_j\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\\
\widehat\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j),&
E&=K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2,&
F&=K^2b(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The endpoints \(x,2j-x\) have the same parity. By (1), with the central case immediate, \(p_xp_{2j-x}=p_j^2\). Hence every geometric row summand equals \(p_j\). Equal endpoint weights are equivalent to both endpoint weights equaling \(w_j\). Thus the hatted rows are precisely the zero-score orbits. Each row used below includes the central atom once and both orientations of every retained noncentral orbit.

For an occupied parity class \(\mathcal C_\eta\), put
$$
\mu_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x,\qquad
R_\eta=K\mu_\eta,\qquad
\rho_x=Kp_x,\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}=\sum_{\eta\text{ occupied}}(R_\eta-1)^2.
\tag{12}
$$
There are \(K\) occupied classes and \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), so
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}=K^2\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2-K\).
The map \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) is a bijection between full oriented row entries and ordered same-parity pairs, including the diagonal. Its inverse exists by (1). Its contribution to \(\chi(p)\) equals the product of its endpoint probabilities. Consequently
$$
\chi(p)=\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2\ge K^{-1},\qquad
F\ge K^2\chi(p)^2\ge1.
\tag{13}
$$
Jensen is taken under the original law \(p\). The central row entries and \(|A|\le MK\) also give
$$
E\ge K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3
\ge K^2|A|^{-2}\ge M^{-2}.
\tag{14}
$$
The middle inequality follows from Hölder and \(\sum_jp_j=1\).

Let \(\mathscr Q_{\ne}\) be the set of unordered distinct same-parity pairs with unequal weights. For \(e=\lbrace x,y\rbrace\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\), write
$$
j_e=(x+y)/2,\qquad
s_e=\frac12|\log(p_x/p_y)|,\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}}\rho_{j_e}^2.
\tag{15}
$$
In particular, \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\) counts pairs with their multiplicities even when midpoints coincide. Deleting both orientations of every mixed pair in (13) gives
$$
K\widehat\chi(p)=
1+\frac{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}}{K},
\qquad
E\ge\bigl(K\widehat\chi(p)\bigr)^2.
\tag{16}
$$
If no mixed pair exists, then \(E=F\ge1\). This includes every \(M=1\) factor. Such a factor will need no scalar label. Henceforth suppose \(E<1\). Then \(0<K\widehat\chi(p)<1\), so
\(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\), and the band list is nonempty.

For later strict comparisons, \(\beta_R\ge3\) and \(C\ge2048M^6\). Therefore
$$
T_1\ge2\beta_R\log(4C)\ge2\log(4C)>2\log(4M),
\qquad
4M^2e^{-2T_1}\le\frac{4M^2}{(4C)^4}\le\frac1{64M^2}<\frac12.
\tag{17}
$$
These inequalities also imply \(T_1>2\log(2M)\) and \(T_1>2\log2\).

Call a class balanced if \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). Fix one largest atom \(z_\eta\) in each balanced class, breaking ties lexicographically, and let \(\mathscr S\) consist of the pairs
\(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) with \(\rho_x<\rho_{z_\eta}\). Define
$$
B_*=\sum_{e\in\mathscr S}\rho_{j_e}^2,\qquad
B_b=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr S\\s_e\in[T_b,RT_b]}}\rho_{j_e}^2.
\tag{18}
$$
The bands are disjoint, so \(B_*=\sum_bB_b\).

Orient any mixed pair in a balanced class by \(\rho_x<\rho_y\). Its smaller endpoint determines the star pair \(\lbrace x,z_\eta\rbrace\), and midpoint equality gives
$$
\rho_{(x+y)/2}^2=\rho_x\rho_y
\le\rho_x\rho_{z_\eta}=\rho_{(x+z_\eta)/2}^2.
$$
For fixed \(x\) there are at most \(M-1\) larger endpoints. Thus the sum of squared midpoint masses over all mixed pairs in balanced source classes is at most \(M B_*\). Ties among largest atoms cause no omission: a strictly smaller endpoint still maps to the fixed maximum, while a maximum cannot be the smaller endpoint of a mixed pair with another maximum.

For a mixed pair in an unbalanced source class, the smaller-to-larger endpoint ratio is \(e^{-2s_e}\le e^{-2T_1}\). Its squared midpoint mass is therefore at most \(e^{-2T_1}R_\eta^2\). There are at most \(M(M-1)/2\) unordered pairs in a class. For unbalanced \(R_\eta\), one has \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\). Hence
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}
\le2M B_*+4M^2e^{-2T_1}\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{19}
$$
Combining (16)--(19) yields
$$
B_*>0,\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<4M B_*,
\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\le2M B_*.
\tag{20}
$$
Indeed, \(B_*=0\) would force a nonnegative defect to be strictly smaller than half itself. Put \(d=-\log E>0\). With \(a_{\mathrm{eq}}=K\widehat\chi(p)\in(0,1)\), (14), (16), and (20) show
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-E&\le1-a_{\mathrm{eq}}^2
\le2(1-a_{\mathrm{eq}})
=\frac{2(\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})}K
\le\frac{4M B_*}{K},\\
d&\le(1-E)/E\le M^2(1-E)\le\frac{4M^3B_*}{K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$

We now prove the same-band transfer. Every chosen balanced-class maximum satisfies
$$
\frac1{2M}\le\rho_{z_\eta}\le2.
\tag{22}
$$
For a star \(e=\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) in band \(b\), abbreviate \(T=T_b\) and \(j=j_e\). Exact midpoint equality gives
$$
\rho_j=\rho_{z_\eta}e^{-s_e},\qquad
\frac{e^{-RT}}{2M}\le\rho_j\le2e^{-T}<\frac1{2M}.
\tag{23}
$$
If the own class of \(j\) is balanced, its fixed maximum \(z_{\eta(j)}\) is strictly larger than \(j\). Thus
\(e(j)=\lbrace j,z_{\eta(j)}\rbrace\) is a star. Its score satisfies
$$
s_{e(j)}
=\frac{s_e}{2}
+\frac12\log\frac{\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}}{\rho_{z_\eta}}.
\tag{24}
$$
Put \(\gamma=\frac12\log(4M)\). The bounds (22) give
$$
s_{e(j)}\in[T/2-\gamma,RT/2+\gamma].
\tag{25}
$$
By (17), \(T>4\gamma\), so the lower endpoint exceeds \(T/4\), while the upper endpoint is strictly below
\(RT/2+T/4\le3RT/4<4RT\).
Every earlier band ends at or below \(T/4\), because
\(RT_{b-1}\le T_b/4\), and every later band starts at or above \(4RT\).
The positive score in (25) must belong to an allowed band. It therefore belongs to band \(b\) itself. This argument does not exclude balanced targets within the current band.

The map \(j\mapsto e(j)\) is injective on distinct such target atoms. Image pairs from different own classes cannot coincide. Within one class their maximum is fixed and every target atom is strictly smaller than that maximum; equality of the unordered pairs then implies equality of the target atoms. No injectivity of their new midpoints is asserted.

For every band \(b\), retain the row system
$$
R_{b,j}=\widehat R_j\cup
\lbrace x\in R_j:|Y_j(x)|\in[T_b,RT_b]\rbrace
\qquad(j\in A).
\tag{26}
$$
It retains all same-weight orbits and all mixed orbits in the band, including those with unbalanced source classes. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{b,j}}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
F_b&=K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_b(j)^2,&
G_b&=F_b-E,\\
\kappa_{b,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_b(j)},&
q_{b,j}&=\frac{K^2p_jH_b(j)^2}{F_b},\\
V_{b,j}&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{b,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_b&=\sum_{j\in A}q_{b,j}V_{b,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
Every central atom is retained, so these auxiliary laws are defined. The original \(p,Z,A\) are unchanged. The full energy \(F\) in (11) is not redefined by \(F_b\).

Fix a band with \(B=B_b>0\), and keep \(T=T_b\). Define midpoint-mass cutoffs, not score cutoffs, by
$$
u_0=3T/2,\qquad u_\ell=2u_{\ell-1}-\log(2M)
\quad(1\le\ell\le m_R).
$$
Their exact formula and consequences of (17) are
$$
u_\ell=2^\ell(3T/2-\log(2M))+\log(2M)
\ge2^\ell T+\log(2M),\qquad
u_{m_R}\ge2RT+\log(2M).
\tag{28}
$$
They strictly increase, since \(u_0>\log(2M)\) and
\(u_\ell-u_{\ell-1}=u_{\ell-1}-\log(2M)>0\).
Let
$$
\mathscr S_\ell=
\lbrace e\in\mathscr S:s_e\in[T,RT],\ \rho_{j_e}\ge e^{-u_\ell}\rbrace,
\quad
B_\ell=\sum_{e\in\mathscr S_\ell}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\quad
J_\ell=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr S_\ell\rbrace,
\tag{29}
$$
and set \(B_{-1}=0\). The sets \(J_\ell\) contain distinct centers; the sums \(B_\ell\) count source pairs. By (23), every star in this band has midpoint mass at least \(e^{-RT}/(2M)\), which is at least \(e^{-u_{m_R}}\) by (28). Hence \(B_{m_R}=B\).

An unbalanced own class meeting \(J_\ell\) contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\), and contains at most \(M\) atoms. Thus (20) and (23) give
$$
\sum_{\substack{j\in J_\ell\\\text{own class unbalanced}}}\rho_j
\le (2e^{-T})(4M\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})
\le32M^2e^{-T}B_*.
\tag{30}
$$
This counts distinct target classes and distinct target atoms. It does not charge a class once per source pair.

For \(\ell\ge1\), every balanced target \(j\in J_\ell\) maps by (24)--(25) to a star \(e(j)\) in the same band. If \(k=(j+z_{\eta(j)})/2\), then
$$
\rho_k^2=\rho_j\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}\ge\frac{\rho_j}{2M},
\qquad
\rho_k\ge
\exp\left(-\frac{u_\ell+\log(2M)}2\right)
=e^{-u_{\ell-1}}.
\tag{31}
$$
Thus \(e(j)\in\mathscr S_{\ell-1}\) and \(\rho_j\le2M\rho_k^2\).
Injectivity into source pairs, proved above, now gives
$$
\sum_{\substack{j\in J_\ell\\\text{own class balanced}}}\rho_j
\le2M B_{\ell-1}.
\tag{32}
$$
Several image pairs can share a midpoint; each still has its own summand in \(B_{\ell-1}\), which is exactly what (32) needs.

There are no balanced targets in \(J_0\). Such a target would map to a same-band star of score at least \(T\), making
\(\rho_j\le2e^{-2T}<e^{-3T/2}\), contrary to its defining cutoff. The strict inequality follows from \(T>2\log2\).
Consequently, for every \(0\le\ell\le m_R\),
$$
\sum_{j\in J_\ell}\rho_j
\le32M^2e^{-T}B_*+2M B_{\ell-1}.
\tag{33}
$$
The endpoint values \(R_\eta=1/2,2\) and equality at any mass cutoff have all been included.

Let \(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}=|\widehat R_j|\), let \(r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) count all mixed unordered pairs of band \(b\) centered at \(j\), and let \(r_{\ell,j}\) count the pairs in \(\mathscr S_\ell\) centered at \(j\). At one center, distinct unordered pairs are disjoint reflection orbits, because each endpoint has a unique reflected mate. Therefore
$$
|R_{b,j}|=h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j},
\qquad h_{\mathrm{eq},j}\ge1,\qquad r_{\ell,j}\le r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}.
$$
Every geometric row entry equals \(p_j\). Thus the exact gain and the truncated star sum satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_b&=\frac1K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
\left((h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})^2-h_{\mathrm{eq},j}^2\right)
\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in J_\ell}r_{\ell,j}^2\rho_j^3,\\
B_\ell&=\sum_{j\in J_\ell}r_{\ell,j}\rho_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz on distinct centers, with entries
\(r_{\ell,j}\rho_j^{3/2}\) and \(\rho_j^{1/2}\), and then (33)--(34), gives
$$
B_\ell^2\le
\frac{KG_b}{4}\left(32M^2e^{-T}B_*+2M B_{\ell-1}\right).
\tag{35}
$$
If \(J_\ell\) is empty, this remains true with zero left side. At level \(m_R\) the left side is positive, so \(G_b>0\).

We next select one band with sufficient weight relative to \(B_*\).
Since \(R\ge1\), successive bases are at least four times their predecessors. Hence
\(T_b\ge T_1+3T_1(b-1)\).
Put \(\omega=e^{-T_1/(4\beta_R)}\). The threshold gives
\(\omega\le(4C)^{-1/2}<1/2\), and therefore
$$
\sum_b e^{-T_b/(4\beta_R)}
\le\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}\omega^{1+3(b-1)}
=\frac{\omega}{1-\omega^3}<\frac47<1.
\tag{36}
$$
Because \(B_*=\sum_bB_b>0\), some band satisfies
$$
B_b\ge e^{-T_b/(4\beta_R)}B_*>0.
\tag{37}
$$
Otherwise summing the strict reverse inequalities would contradict (36).
Empty bands cannot be chosen.

Use this band for the remainder of the scalar argument. With \(B=B_b\) and \(T=T_b\), normalize (35) by
$$
\xi_\ell=B_\ell/B,\qquad \xi_{-1}=0,\qquad
\zeta=\frac{MKG_b}{2B},\qquad
\epsilon=16M e^{-T}\frac{B_*}{B}.
\tag{38}
$$
Thus \(\xi_{m_R}=1\) and
$$
\xi_\ell^2\le\zeta(\epsilon+\xi_{\ell-1}).
\tag{39}
$$
These are the recurrence variables of 5e2dee70e6a96862 with the star sum replacing its all-balanced-pair sum; the new \(\epsilon\) includes the exact band-to-total ratio in (38).
By (37),
$$
0<\epsilon\le16M\exp\left(-T\left(1-\frac1{4\beta_R}\right)\right)\le\frac14.
\tag{40}
$$
To verify the last threshold explicitly,
$$
T\left(1-\frac1{4\beta_R}\right)
\ge(2\beta_R-\tfrac12)\log(4C)\ge\log(4C),
$$
and \(16M/(4C)\le1/4\) since \(C\ge16M\).

Here is the scalar recurrence argument in full. If \(\zeta<\epsilon\), then \(\xi_0<\epsilon\), and induction using (39) shows
\(\xi_\ell<2\epsilon\): from \(\xi_{\ell-1}<2\epsilon\), one gets
\(\xi_\ell^2<3\epsilon^2\).
This contradicts \(\xi_{m_R}=1\) because \(2\epsilon\le1/2\).
Therefore \(\zeta\ge\epsilon>0\).
A second induction gives
$$
\xi_\ell\le
2\epsilon^{1/2^{\ell+1}}\zeta^{1-1/2^{\ell+1}}
\qquad(0\le\ell\le m_R).
\tag{41}
$$
The base case has the stronger estimate \(\xi_0\le\sqrt{\epsilon\zeta}\).
For the induction step,
\(\epsilon\le\epsilon^{1/2^\ell}\zeta^{1-1/2^\ell}\) follows from \(\zeta\ge\epsilon\).
Substitution of the previous bound into (39) gives the right side of (41) with coefficient \(\sqrt3\), which is at most two.
At the final level, (41) and \(\beta_R=2^{m_R+1}-1\) imply
$$
\zeta\ge2^{-1-1/\beta_R}\epsilon^{-1/\beta_R}
\ge\frac14\epsilon^{-1/\beta_R}.
\tag{42}
$$
Since \((16M)^{-1/\beta_R}\ge(16M)^{-1}\), (40)--(42) yield
$$
\zeta\ge\frac1{64M}
\exp\left(\frac{T(1-1/(4\beta_R))}{\beta_R}\right),\qquad
G_b\ge\frac{B_b}{32M^2K}
\exp\left(\frac{T(1-1/(4\beta_R))}{\beta_R}\right).
\tag{43}
$$

Set
$$
\theta=\frac{T}{2\beta_R},\qquad F_*=F_b,\qquad G_*=G_b,\qquad \nu_*=\nu_b.
\tag{44}
$$
The scale \(\theta\) here is half the scale \(T/\beta_R\) in the single-band theorem; (44) is its explicit replacement. The full \(F\) remains the quantity in (11).
Combining (21), (37), and (43), with the signs of both ratio exponents retained, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
d&\le128M^5
\exp\left[-T\left(\frac{3}{4\beta_R}-\frac1{4\beta_R^2}\right)\right]G_*\\
&\le128M^5e^{-T/(2\beta_R)}G_*
=128M^5e^{-\theta}G_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
$$
The last comparison is valid because
\(3/(4\beta_R)-1/(4\beta_R^2)-1/(2\beta_R)
=(\beta_R-1)/(4\beta_R^2)\ge0\).

There are at most \(K(M-1)\le MK\) star pairs, and every star in the selected band has squared midpoint mass at most \(4e^{-2T}\). Consequently
\(B_b\le4MK e^{-2T}\). Equations (21) and (37) give the separate absolute deficit bound
$$
\begin{aligned}
d&\le16M^4\exp\left[-\left(2-\frac1{4\beta_R}\right)T\right]\\
&\le16M^4e^{-7T/4}\le16M^4e^{-7\theta/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{46}
$$
Here \(\beta_R\ge1\) and \(T=2\beta_R\theta\ge2\theta\).

Now \(\theta\ge\log(4C)\). If \(0<G_*\le1\), (45) gives
$$
d\le\frac{32M^5}{C}G_*\le G_*/64,
$$
since \(C\ge2048M^5\). Also \(1-E\le-\log E=d\), so
\(F_*=E+G_*\ge1+63G_*/64\ge1+G_*/2>1\).
It follows that \(g=\log F_*\ge G_*/4\), by integrating \(1/(1+u)\ge1/2\) for \(0\le u\le G_*/2\). Thus
$$
d\le512M^5e^{-\theta}g\le Ce^{-\theta}g.
\tag{47}
$$
If \(G_*\ge1\), (14) gives \(F_*\ge1+M^{-2}>1\) and
$$
g\ge\log(1+M^{-2})\ge\frac1{M^2+1}\ge\frac1{2M^2}.
$$
Using (46), we obtain
$$
d\le32M^6e^{-7\theta/2}g\le Ce^{-\theta}g,
\tag{48}
$$
because \(\theta\ge0\) and \(C\ge32M^6\). Both arguments include their shared boundary \(G_*=1\). Therefore
$$
g>0,\qquad d\le Ce^{-\theta}g\le g/4.
\tag{49}
$$

We compute the selected-row variance directly. Its row law is uniform, and its nonzero scores occur in pairs \(\pm s_e\). From (27),
$$
F_*\nu_*=\frac2K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\\j_e=j,\ s_e\in[T,RT]}}s_e^2.
\tag{50}
$$
Every displayed \(s_e\) is at most \(RT\). For \(h\ge1,r\ge0\),
$$
2r(h+2r)\le(h+2r)^2-h^2,
$$
since the difference is \(2hr\). Comparing (50) with the exact gain in (34) yields
\(F_*\nu_*\le(RT)^2G_*\).
Moreover,
$$
\frac{G_*}{F_*}=1-\frac E{F_*}
\le\log(F_*/E)=g+d.
$$
By (44) and (49),
$$
\nu_*\le(RT)^2(g+d)
\le5R^2\beta_R^2\theta^2g\le C\theta^2g.
\tag{51}
$$
This calculation uses the actual selected rows, without assuming that they form a midpoint-closed support or applying a full-row score theorem to them.

We have proved the following genuine alternatives for each factor. If \(E_i\ge1\), its same-weight row system has nonnegative scaled logarithmic energy and variance price zero. If \(E_i<1\), the same-weight system has scaled energy \(e^{-d_i}\) and price zero, and one chosen-band system has scaled energy \(e^{g_i}\) and price at most \(C_i\theta_i^2g_i\), with
$$
d_i\le C_i e^{-\theta_i}g_i,\qquad g_i>0,\qquad
\theta_i\ge\log(4C_i).
\tag{52}
$$
For the fixed-parameter assertion \(C_i=C\). Every choice is a whole-factor row system at all original centers.

For any final choices, denote the chosen row at \(j\in A_i\) by \(R_{i,j}^*\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&
q_{i,j}^*&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2}{b_i^*},\\
V_{i,j}^*&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&
\nu_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}^*V_{i,j}^*,&
S_g&=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*).
\end{aligned}
\tag{53}
$$
All these denominators are positive because the original central atom is retained. The score is always the original score restricted to the selected row.

For the fixed-\(M,R\) assertion, choose the same-weight system for every \(E_i\ge1\). Bin all remaining labels by the integer \(m\ge0\) with \(\theta_i\in[m,m+1)\), and put \(G_m=\sum_{i\text{ in bin }m}g_i\). Retain the chosen-band system for the entire bin if
$$
G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2;
$$
otherwise choose the same-weight system for the entire bin. There are finitely many nonempty bins. For a retained bin, its total price is at most
\(W_m=C(m+1)^2G_m\), and its retention condition implies \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\).
For a discarded bin, (52) gives total deficit at most
$$
Ce^{-m}G_m<4C^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2.
$$
The identity
$$
\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=\frac{1+r}{(1-r)^3}\qquad(0\le r<1)
$$
follows by differentiating the geometric series. Since \(e^{-1}<1/2\),
$$
\sum_{m\ge0}e^{-m}(m+1)^2
\le\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m}(m+1)^2=12.
$$
Thus the total discarded deficit is at most \(48C^2\), and
$$
S_g\ge\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-48C^2,\qquad
\sum_i\nu_i^*\le\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m.
$$
Subadditivity of the square root for finitely many nonnegative numbers now gives
$$
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\ge-48C^2.
\tag{54}
$$

For the heterogeneous assertion, the threshold (9) is stronger than the scalar threshold used above. Indeed \(C_i\ge1\) and
$$
4(\log C_i+1)-\log(4C_i)=3\log C_i+4-\log4>0.
$$
The scalar argument therefore applies separately to every factor with its own \(M_i,R_i,\beta_i,C_i\). Its chosen band also satisfies
$$
\theta_i=\frac{T_{i,b_i}}{2\beta_i}\ge4(\log C_i+1).
\tag{55}
$$
Again use same-weight rows for all \(E_i\ge1\). For the remaining labels, put
$$
h_i=\lfloor\log C_i\rfloor,\qquad m_i=\lfloor\theta_i\rfloor.
$$
Then \(h_i,m_i\) are nonnegative integers,
\(C_i<e^{h_i+1}\), \(\theta_i<m_i+1\), and (55) implies
\(m_i\ge4h_i+4\), because \(4h_i+4\) is an integer.
For each nonempty bin \((h,m)\), let \(G_{h,m}\) be its sum of \(g_i\).
Retain that whole bin precisely when
$$
G_{h,m}\ge4e^{h+1}(m+1)^2.
\tag{56}
$$
For a retained bin the total price is at most
\(W_{h,m}=e^{h+1}(m+1)^2G_{h,m}\), and (56) yields
\(G_{h,m}\ge2\sqrt{W_{h,m}}\).
For a discarded bin, (52) gives total deficit at most
$$
e^{h+1-m}G_{h,m}<4e^{2h+2-m}(m+1)^2.
\tag{57}
$$

It remains to bound the sum of (57) without a bound on \(h\). All bins obey \(m\ge4h+4\). Writing \(j=m-4h-4\ge0\), the sum of their upper bounds is at most
$$
4e^{-2}\sum_{h,j\ge0}e^{-2h-j}(4h+5+j)^2.
\tag{58}
$$
For \(h,j\ge0\),
$$
4h+5+j\le5(h+1)(j+1),
$$
since the difference is \(5hj+h+4j\ge0\). Hence (58) is at most
$$
100e^{-2}
\left(\sum_{h\ge0}e^{-2h}(h+1)^2\right)
\left(\sum_{j\ge0}e^{-j}(j+1)^2\right).
\tag{59}
$$
Both series are at most 12 by the geometric-series identity above and \(e^{-1}<1/2\). Also \(100e^{-2}<25\). Thus (59) is strictly below \(25\cdot12^2=3600\).
The total discarded deficit is consequently at most 3600. Retained bins are finite in number because there are only \(L\) factors. Their gains and prices yield, as in (54),
$$
S_g\ge\sum_{(h,m)\text{ retained}}G_{h,m}-3600,\qquad
\sum_i\nu_i^*\le\sum_{(h,m)\text{ retained}}W_{h,m},
$$
and therefore
$$
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\ge-3600.
\tag{60}
$$
Both selection arguments include empty bins, no deficient factors, and no retained bins. The parameters \(C_i\) in (60) need no common upper bound.

We finish both assertions by converting the selected quantities to the actual original product law. Every chosen row is symmetric about its original center, so reflection preserves \(\kappa_{i,j}^*\) and negates \(Y_{i,j}\). In particular the score has mean zero. Direct substitution gives
$$
p_{i,x}=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}
=w_{i,x}/Z_i
\qquad(x\in R_{i,j}^*).
\tag{61}
$$
The selected energy retains its original normalizer and every original center:
$$
b_i^*=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{62}
$$

For every supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{63}
$$
Finite geometric-mean sums show that \(H_P^*(\mathbf j)\) is the geometric mass of this selected product row. Independence and the zero score means give
\(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\).
Multiplying (61) gives the exact likelihood identity
$$
P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}
=\frac{\prod_iw_{i,X_i}}{\prod_iZ_i}.
\tag{64}
$$
No original probability has been conditioned or renormalized.

Every closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflected pair
\(\lbrace X,2\mathbf j-X\rbrace\): strict exclusion of both endpoints by an affine inequality would strictly exclude their midpoint. It also contains the central atom. Simultaneous reflection preserves the selected product row and its geometric law, and reverses the score. Thus, by summing the smaller original mass in each noncentral orbit and half the available central mass,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}
\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2
e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{65}
$$
The last two inequalities are Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz. The oriented sum counts every noncentral orbit twice before its factor \(1/2\) and the center once. This proves the bound for all containing closed halfspaces, including boundary atoms, singleton rows, lower-dimensional supports, and zero variance.

Let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\), an auxiliary center law only. From (53) and (63),
$$
P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2
=\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\prod_iq_{i,j_i}^*,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*.
\tag{66}
$$
Squaring (65), summing under the original \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q^*\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{67}
$$
Substituting (54) proves the first assertion of (7), and substituting (60) proves the first assertion of (10). Finally, Jensen under the same original law gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2},
$$
which proves both third-moment assertions.

Only selected reflection masses and auxiliary laws factor in this proof. There is no assertion that actual halfspace depths or their moments tensorize. All capacities are the original occupied counts \(K_i\), all original atoms remain possible centers, and every original normalizer in (2) is preserved.
