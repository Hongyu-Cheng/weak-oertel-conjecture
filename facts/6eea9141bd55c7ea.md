## Statement

Fix an integer $q\ge1$, a volume-one convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^q$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}K$, and $0<a\le b$. The body over $[-1,1]$ with fiber $(b-(b-a)|z|)K$ is full-dimensional and has integer fibers of masses $(a^q,b^q,a^q)$. Write $M=b^q+2a^q$, $\gamma=(q/(q+1))^q$, $c=1-\gamma$, and $J_*$ for the maximum of $\sum_z[w_z-c\ell(z)^q]_+$ over finite convex majorants of the volume roots on the base interval. Then
$$
J_*=\gamma b^q+\max\left\{2[a^q-cb^q]_+,\ \gamma a^q+[a^q-c(2b-a)^q]_+\right\}.
$$
The constant majorant $b$ or the affine majorant with sampled values $(a,b,2b-a)$ attains this value. If $a^q\le cb^q$, then $J_*=\gamma(b^q+a^q)<\gamma M$. Products with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ in additional integer coordinates multiply both $J_*$ and $M$ by $2^{n-1}$ for every integer $n\ge1$.

When $q=1$,
$$
J_*=(a+b)/2+(3a-2b)_+/2,\qquad J_*/(\gamma M)\ge5/7,
$$
with equality in the ratio exactly when $a=2b/3$. For $a=2,b=3,K=[-1/2,1/2]$ the actual body has $M=7$, $J_*=5/2$, and maximum normalized ambient depth $1/2$. The formal budget $B=3/2$ satisfies $B\ge\gamma\max_z w_z$, $2B\ge J(\ell)$ for every convex root majorant, and $\sum_z[w_z-B]_+=5/2\le 2B$, even though $B<\gamma M/2=7/4$. In this example $J_*/2=5/4$.

## Proof

The radius is positive and concave. The weighted sum of two homothets with the given fixed shape has the linearly averaged radius. Since the origin is in the shape, it is contained in the fiber at the concave target radius. This proves convexity. Compactness and full dimension follow from the compact base interval and positive radii.

Write the three majorant values as $L,C,R$. Their exact constraints are $L,R\ge a$, $C\ge b$, $L+R\ge2C$ by Jensen and piecewise-linear interpolation. Decrease $C$ to $b$ and then decrease endpoints within their floors until $L+R=2b$. Neither operation decreases the objective, which is coordinatewise nonincreasing. By symmetry take $L\in[a,b]$, $R=2b-L$. The middle contribution is $\gamma b^q$. Let $t=ac^{-1/q}>a$. If $R\le t$, the two endpoint terms sum to $2a^q-c[L^q+(2b-L)^q]$. It is nondecreasing as $L$ increases to $b$, so the balanced majorant maximizes this branch. If $L<t<R$, only the first endpoint term is positive and it decreases with $L$. If this branch occurs, it contains $L=a$, since then $L$ is smaller and $R$ larger. If $L\ge t$, both endpoint contributions are zero. Continuity handles the boundary values of the branches. Evaluating the two actual affine majorants gives the formula, and when $a^q\le cb^q$ both terms involving radii at least $b$ have zero excess.

For the product, every slice indexed by a binary vector in the added integer coordinates restricts any admissible majorant to the same three-point program, giving an upper bound of $2^{n-1}J_*$. An optimizing affine majorant independent of the additional coordinates gives equality. Each copy has mass $M$.

For $q=1$, the formula simplifies to $(a+b)/2+(3a-2b)_+/2$. In terms of $s=a/b$, the ratio is $(1+s)/(1+2s)$ for $s\le2/3$ and $(4s-1)/(1+2s)$ for $s\ge2/3$. Their derivative numerators are $-1$ and $6$, respectively, so the unique minimum is $5/7$. At $(a,b)=(2,3)$ the formula gives $J_*=5/2$. The actual interval body is centrally symmetric in $(z,x)$ about $(0,0)$. Every closed halfspace through the origin has at least half the mass, and $x\ge0$ has exactly half. For any other candidate, the continuous-coordinate halfspace through the candidate facing away from zero has at most half the mass, with either orientation valid at coordinate zero. Thus the maximum depth is exactly half. Direct substitution gives $\gamma\max w=3/2$ and $\sum_z[w_z-3/2]_+=1/2+3/2+1/2=5/2$. The formal budget passes the separate scalar inequalities. Any actual cover must include a cap containing the origin, so its maximum cap mass is at least $7/2$.

Finally, for any nonnegative weights of positive total on several majorant inequalities $2^nB\ge J(\ell_i)$, the normalized right-hand average is at most $J_*$. The computation applies to these scalar bounds and their positive combinations.
