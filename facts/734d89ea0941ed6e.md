## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), use exactly the standard recursive symmetric saturated chain partition in accepted fact a2c2d2b38e2fa409. Explicitly, start with the one-element chain consisting of the empty set in dimension zero and replace a chain \(S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k}\) in the next dimension by the chains
$$
S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k},S_{m-k}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,
\qquad
S_k\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,\ldots,S_{m-k-1}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,
$$
omitting an empty second chain. Retain precisely the block objects of accepted facts 6fee4107100a027d and a2c2d2b38e2fa409:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_m&=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\qquad
D_m=\lbrace c\in B_m:c\text{ is nonterminal in its chain}\rbrace,\\
F_m(c)&=c+e_{i_m(c)},\qquad c_{i_m(c)}=0,\\
E_m&=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\\
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\\
T_m&=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
     =E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(F_m(c)\) is the chain successor and \(e_i\) is a coordinate unit vector. Thus these are the completed block supports, with the same standard coordinate order as in those accepted facts.

Let \(L\ge1\) be any integer. Independently for every \(1\le i\le L\), choose an integer \(m_i\ge1\), a spatial field \(\gamma_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i}\), and a spike penalty \(0<\epsilon_i\le1\). On the entire support \(T_{m_i}\) define
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{i,x}&=\exp(\gamma_i\cdot x)\epsilon_i^{\mathbf1_{\lbrace x\in E_{m_i}\rbrace}},\\
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in T_{m_i}}w_{i,x}
    =\sum_{x\in C_{m_i}^{\mathrm{bool}}}e^{\gamma_i\cdot x}
     +\epsilon_i\sum_{x\in E_{m_i}}e^{\gamma_i\cdot x},\\
p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i\qquad(x\in T_{m_i}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
and extend \(p_i\) by zero off \(T_{m_i}\). The parameter \(\epsilon_i\) denotes the spike penalty, not a height deviation. The block normalizers \(Z_i\) do not replace the serial normalizer \(W\) in 6fee4107100a027d.

Put \(N=\sum_{i=1}^L m_i\) and \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i\). In particular, every original product atom has exactly the probability
$$
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}
=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^L\left(e^{\gamma_i\cdot x_i}
  \epsilon_i^{\mathbf1_{\lbrace x_i\in E_{m_i}\rbrace}}\right)}
 {\prod_{i=1}^L Z_i}
\qquad(x_i\in T_{m_i}).
\tag{3}
$$
For any finitely supported probability law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\), use the actual depth and moments
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
  \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}p}p_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}p}p_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
These are exactly the conventions of accepted facts ce8cccfdbee6f1cc and 4d36c889dd0a9ba2. Every closed halfspace in the original ambient space is included, also when a support is lower-dimensional.

Define the absolute constants, using natural logarithms, by
$$
\kappa=\frac1{51840},\qquad
D_0=\frac{48}{\kappa^2}+\frac{2\log4}{\kappa},\qquad
c_{\mathrm{pen}}=
\exp\!\left[-4D_0\left(2+12(\log2)^2\right)\right]>0.
\tag{5}
$$
Then, for every independent choice above,
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{pen}}4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_{\mathrm{pen}}^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$
The support in (3) is complete in the convex set \(\prod_i\operatorname{conv}(E_{m_i})\). This is a convex-potential family: on \(T_m\), the convex function \(\psi_m(x)=\max(0,\max_{1\le h\le m}(x_h-1))\) equals \(\mathbf1_{\lbrace x\in E_m\rbrace}\), so the full product weights are the restrictions of
\(\exp(\sum_i\gamma_i\cdot x_i-\sum_i(-\log\epsilon_i)\psi_{m_i}(x_i))\).

## Proof

All laws and normalizers in this proof are those in (2)--(3). The only additional probability laws below are explicitly designated auxiliary reflected-row and center laws.

Write \(t_i=\sqrt{\epsilon_i}\in(0,1]\). First record the exact normalization map for the accepted estimates. In the block application of d8907f5eb4e7e5a2, take \(m=m_i\), \(\mathcal A=T_{m_i}\), \(w_x=w_{i,x}\), \(Z=Z_i\), \(p=p_i\), and \(t=t_i\). In 4d36c889dd0a9ba2, the corresponding aliases are \(n_\ell=m_i\), \(A_\ell=T_{m_i}\), \(w_{\ell,x}=w_{i,x}\), \(Z_\ell=Z_i\), and \(p_\ell=p_i\) when the index \(\ell\) labels factor \(i\). These retain the full original support and law.

For clarity, define their reflected quantities at each supported center \(j\in T_{m_i}\) by
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{i,j}&=\lbrace x\in T_{m_i}:2j-x\in T_{m_i}\rbrace,\\
H_i(j)&=H_{p_i}(j)=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},\\
b_i&=b(p_i)=\sum_{j\in T_{m_i}}p_{i,j}H_i(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i(j)}
\quad(x\in R_{i,j}),\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},\qquad
V_{i,j}=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i(j)^2}{b_i},\qquad
\nu_i=\sum_{j\in T_{m_i}}q_{i,j}V_{i,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
The symbol \(\kappa\) without subscripts is only the absolute constant in (5); \(\kappa_{i,j}\) retains the accepted row-law notation. The symbol \(\nu_i\) in (7) is a row price, not the serial ambient dimension \(\nu\) of 6fee4107100a027d. The sum in \(H_i\) is oriented, since the points \(x\) and \(2j-x\) both occur when distinct. The central term gives \(H_i(j)\ge p_{i,j}>0\), hence \(b_i>0\), and (7) defines probability laws \(\kappa_{i,j}\) and \(q_i\).

We check the geometric and penalty hypotheses explicitly. The block results of 6fee4107100a027d, as used in the block application of 4d36c889dd0a9ba2, state that a parity class contains at most two points of \(T_m\). Its distinct same-parity pairs are precisely
\(\lbrace c,c+2e_{i_m(c)}\rbrace\) for \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\). Their midpoints \(F_m(c)\) are supported Boolean points and are distinct by injectivity of \(F_m\). The endpoint sets of different such pairs are disjoint. Thus every supported reflected row is a singleton or one such pair together with its midpoint, exactly as required in 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 and d8907f5eb4e7e5a2. In factor \(i\), the original probabilities of these endpoints and their center satisfy
$$
\sqrt{p_{i,c}p_{i,c+2e_{i_{m_i}(c)}}}
=\frac{t_i e^{\gamma_i\cdot(c+e_{i_{m_i}(c)})}}{Z_i}
=t_i p_{i,F_{m_i}(c)}.
\tag{8}
$$
This identity holds for every field, with the same \(t_i\) for every pair in that factor. It does not identify the fields or penalties in different factors. The block theorem of d8907f5eb4e7e5a2 therefore gives
$$
4^{m_i}\sum_{x\in T_{m_i}}p_{i,x}^3\ge e^{-4t_i^2},\qquad
\nu_i\le12t_i\left(1+\log^2\frac1{t_i}\right).
\tag{9}
$$
In particular, (9) applies to singleton rows and to centers that are also endpoints of another row.

We next prove a reflected gain valid at every width:
$$
4^{m_i}b_i\ge1+\frac{t_i}{25920},\qquad
\log(4^{m_i}b_i)\ge\kappa t_i.
\tag{10}
$$
For \(m_i\ge7\), the first inequality is the theorem of 34a8aa3368c8e3f7, with exactly the standard partition, spatial field \(\gamma_i\), penalty \(\epsilon_i\), original normalizer \(Z_i\), and oriented \(H_{p_i}\) in (1), (2), and (7). For the remaining six widths, the exact count formulas of a2c2d2b38e2fa409 give
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
m&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
|E_m|&1&2&5&10&22&44\\
|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|&0&0&1&2&7&14\\
|T_m|&1&2&6&12&29&58.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
$$
Jensen's inequality for the cubes of the full original atom probabilities gives
\(\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\ge|T_{m_i}|^{-2}\). Since \(b_i\ge\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\), (11) implies, for every law on each of these supports,
$$
4^{m_i}b_i
\ge\frac{4^{m_i}}{|T_{m_i}|^2}
\ge\frac{1024}{841}
=1+\frac{183}{841}
\ge1+\frac{t_i}{25920}.
\tag{12}
$$
Here the six ratios are respectively \(4,4,16/9,16/9,1024/841,1024/841\), and \(t_i\le1\). This checks every small width independently of the field and penalty. Finally, for \(0\le u\le1\), \(\log(1+u)=\int_0^u(1+s)^{-1}\,ds\ge u/2\). Apply this with \(u=t_i/25920\) to obtain the second inequality in (10), because \(\kappa=1/51840\).

We now establish the variance-sensitive actual product estimate needed for composition. For any nonempty index set \(I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), put \(P_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}p_i\), \(N_I=\sum_{i\in I}m_i\), and \(\alpha_I=\sum_{i\in I}t_i\). We claim
$$
M_2(P_I)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b_i\right)
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_{i\in I}\nu_i}\right).
\tag{13}
$$
This is the paired-minimum argument of 4d36c889dd0a9ba2 with its actual row prices retained, rather than replaced by its coarser bound \(9|I|\). Here is the full argument with these quantities.

Fix a supported target \(J=(j_i)_{i\in I}\). Its reflected row and auxiliary row law factor as \(R_J=\prod_{i\in I}R_{i,j_i}\) and \(\kappa_J=\bigotimes_{i\in I}\kappa_{i,j_i}\). The reflected factorization in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc, with the identical oriented convention, gives \(H_{P_I}(J)=\prod_{i\in I}H_i(j_i)\). The score \(Y_J(X)=\sum_{i\in I}Y_{i,j_i}(X_i)\) has mean zero under \(\kappa_J\), because reflection preserves every factor row law and negates its score. Independence therefore gives
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,
\qquad
\sigma_J=\left(\sum_{i\in I}V_{i,j_i}\right)^{1/2},
\qquad
(P_I)_X=H_{P_I}(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\in R_J).
\tag{14}
$$
The last identity follows by multiplying the factor identities from (7); in particular its left side has exactly the product of the original normalizers \(Z_i\).

Let \(\mathcal H\) be any original ambient closed halfspace containing \(J\). It contains at least one endpoint of every distinct reflected pair \(X,2J-X\) and contains the central atom. For a distinct pair, excluding both endpoints strictly would exclude their midpoint. Summing over reflection orbits and using (14) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_I(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace(P_I)_X,(P_I)_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_{P_I}(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
The sum in the first line counts a distinct pair twice and the central atom once, so its factor \(1/2\) uses no more than the available mass. Reflection sends \(Y_J\) to \(-Y_J\), proving the equality from (14). The last line is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking the infimum over all such halfspaces proves the same bound for \(\tau_{P_I}(J)\).

Let \(q_I=\bigotimes_{i\in I}q_i\), solely as an auxiliary center law. Square (15) and average under the original \(P_I\). The exact identity
\((P_I)_JH_{P_I}(J)^2=(\prod_{i\in I}b_i)\prod_{i\in I}q_{i,j_i}\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P_I)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b_i\right)
  \mathbb E_{q_I}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b_i\right)
  e^{-2\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b_i\right)
  \exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
By (7) and (14), \(\mathbb E_{q_I}\sigma_J^2=\sum_{i\in I}\nu_i\), so (16) is (13). All arguments allow zero variances and lower-dimensional supports. Combining (9), (10), and (13) gives the first of the following two bounds for every nonempty \(I\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{N_I}M_2(P_I)
&\ge\frac14\exp\!\left[
\kappa\alpha_I
-2\sqrt{12\sum_{i\in I}t_i\left(1+\log^2\frac1{t_i}\right)}
\right],\\
4^{N_I}M_2(P_I)
&\ge\exp\!\left(-4\sum_{i\in I}t_i^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
To check the second bound directly, every containing closed halfspace contains the target atom, so \(\tau_{P_I}(J)\ge(P_I)_J\). Consequently
\(M_2(P_I)\ge\sum_J(P_I)_J^3=\prod_{i\in I}\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\), and the first inequality of (9) proves the asserted bound. Thus both lines of (17) concern the same full original law.

Partition the factor indices by their actual independent parameters:
\(I_k=\lbrace i:2^{-k-1}<t_i\le2^{-k}\rbrace\), for integers \(k\ge0\). These sets are pairwise disjoint and cover every index, including \(t_i=1\) in \(I_0\). There are only finitely many nonempty sets. For such a band, write \(\alpha_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i\), \(r_k=(k+1)\log2\), \(A_k=1+r_k^2\), and \(N_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}m_i\). The band definitions imply
$$
\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i\left(1+\log^2\frac1{t_i}\right)
\le A_k\alpha_k,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i^2\le2^{-k}\alpha_k.
\tag{18}
$$
Indeed \(0\le\log(1/t_i)<r_k\), and \(t_i\le2^{-k}\), for each index of this band. No comparison between different fields or between parameters in different bands is made.

If \(\alpha_k\ge D_0A_k\), use the first line of (17). The nonnegative square
\((\sqrt{\kappa\alpha_k/2}-\sqrt{24A_k/\kappa})^2\) proves the exact Young inequality
\(2\sqrt{12A_k\alpha_k}\le\kappa\alpha_k/2+24A_k/\kappa\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log\bigl(4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\bigr)
&\ge-\log4+\kappa\alpha_k-2\sqrt{12A_k\alpha_k}\\
&\ge-\log4+\frac\kappa2\alpha_k-\frac{24}{\kappa}A_k\\
&\ge-\log4+
\left(\frac\kappa2D_0-\frac{24}{\kappa}\right)A_k\\
&=\log4\,(A_k-1)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
If instead \(\alpha_k<D_0A_k\), the second line of (17) and (18) give
\(4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\ge\exp(-4D_0 2^{-k}A_k)\). In the first case, (19) gives a normalized moment at least one, which is also at least this last expression. Thus every nonempty band satisfies the single bound
$$
4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})
\ge\exp\!\left[-4D_0 2^{-k}\left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right)\right].
\tag{20}
$$

It remains to compose actual moments of these bands. The pointwise and moment supermultiplicativity in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc applies to arbitrary finite laws, so it applies to the band laws with their original atoms. Grouping the original factors into bands changes only the ordering of coordinates. Coordinate permutation maps ambient closed halfspaces bijectively to ambient closed halfspaces and transports the same probabilities, so it preserves the depths and moments in (4). Iterating the accepted supermultiplicativity and using \(\sum_kN_k=N\) therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^NM_2(P)
&\ge\prod_{k:I_k\ne\varnothing}4^{N_k}M_2(P_{I_k})\\
&\ge\exp\!\left[-4D_0
\sum_{k:I_k\ne\varnothing}
2^{-k}\left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right)\right]\\
&\ge\exp\!\left[-4D_0
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
2^{-k}\left(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\right)\right]
=c_{\mathrm{pen}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
For completeness, the geometric series is \(\sum_{k\ge0}z^k=(1-z)^{-1}\) for \(|z|<1\). Differentiation there gives \(\sum_{k\ge0}kz^k=z/(1-z)^2\) and \(\sum_{k\ge0}k(k-1)z^k=2z^2/(1-z)^3\). Evaluating at \(z=1/2\) gives \(\sum2^{-k}=2\), \(\sum k2^{-k}=2\), and \(\sum k^22^{-k}=6\), hence \(\sum(k+1)^22^{-k}=12\). This verifies the last equality in (21) and the constant (5). Only finitely many actual band moments are multiplied; the infinite series is a convergent numerical upper bound on their total loss.

Finally, Jensen's inequality for \(s\mapsto s^{3/2}\) under the original product law yields
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P\bigl(\tau_P(J)^2\bigr)^{3/2}
\ge\bigl(\mathbb E_P\tau_P(J)^2\bigr)^{3/2}
=M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge c_{\mathrm{pen}}^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{22}
$$
Together (21)--(22) prove (6).

The argument covers \(t_i=1\) directly: such indices occupy the first band, their logarithmic prices in (9) have no singularity, and (19)--(20) require no strict penalty. It is uniform as any positive \(t_i\) tends to zero: the possible losses in successive bands are bounded by the summable terms in (21), with no minimum penalty imposed. Arbitrarily many different occupied bands and arbitrary independent fields are already included in that sum. No atom was removed when using either estimate in (17), and no original normalizer was replaced. The small widths were checked in (11)--(12), including the singleton support at width one.

The support and potential assertions follow directly from (1): taking a Cartesian product commutes with intersecting each coordinate block with its integer lattice, and \(\psi_m\) is zero on the Boolean points and one on each spike, since each spike has exactly one coordinate equal to two and all its other coordinates are Boolean. The maximum of these affine functions and zero is convex, and the coefficients \(-\log\epsilon_i\) are nonnegative. This proves the stated convex-potential interpretation without extending the theorem to other support families.
