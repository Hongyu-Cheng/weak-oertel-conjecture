## Statement

Let \(n\ge100\) be an integer and let \(k\) be rational with \(1\le k\le n/100\). Put \(m=n-1\), \(d=3n-2\), and \(R=n/2+k\). In the original ordered continuous coordinates \((\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^m\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
C=\biggl\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):\;&
\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-\ge\mathbf0,\qquad
g_i=t_i^+-t_i^-\quad(1\le i\le m),\qquad g_0=g_n=0,\\
&y_i\ge\left|z_i-\frac12-g_i+g_{i-1}\right|
       \quad(1\le i\le n),\\
&\sum_{i=1}^n y_i+\sum_{i=1}^m(t_i^++t_i^-)\le R
\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Use the exact actual-body meanings of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f: \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{(\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}\), and \(h_S\) is depth measured against closed ambient halfspaces. Thus \(W\) includes every integer base.

The body is a full-dimensional rational simplex and \(0<W<\infty\). Let \(\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^n\) have \(j_i=1\) for odd \(i\) and \(j_i=0\) for even \(i\). Then
\[
W\le16e^{24}\,2^n w_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently there exists one point \(p^\star\in C_{\mathbf j}\), adjoined to its fixed base \(\mathbf j\), such that every closed ambient halfspace containing \((\mathbf j,p^\star)\) has mixed-integer volume at least \(e^{-24}W/(192\,2^n)\). Equivalently, \(h_S((\mathbf j,p^\star))\ge e^{-24}W/(192\,2^n)\). The point is selected before the halfspace. The constant \(e^{-24}/192\) is positive, smaller than \(1/e\), and independent of \(n\) and \(k\).

## Proof

We first derive the original-coordinate formula and account for the complete support. The nonnegative simplex with coordinates \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\) and total coordinate sum at most \(R\) maps bijectively onto \(C\) by \(y_i=u_i+v_i\) and \(z_i=1/2+u_i-v_i+g_i-g_{i-1}\), leaving the two flow blocks unchanged. Adding and subtracting the residual from \(y_i\), and dividing by two, gives the inverse. This is an invertible affine map in dimension \(n+d=4n-2\), so \(C\) is a full-dimensional rational simplex.

For a real base \(\mathbf z\), define
\[
E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)
=\sum_{i=1}^m|g_i|
 +\sum_{i=1}^n\left|z_i-\frac12-g_i+g_{i-1}\right|,
\qquad \mathbf g\in\mathbb R^m,\quad g_0=g_n=0.
\tag{2}
\]
For the budget calculations only, \(w_{\mathbf z}(U)\) denotes the original-coordinate fiber volume with \(R\) replaced by \(U\ge0\); in particular \(w_{\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}(R)\). Put \(\ell=n+m=2n-1\), so \(d=\ell+m\).

Write \(s_i=\min(t_i^+,t_i^-)\). Outside null boundaries the substitution \(t_i^+=(g_i)_++s_i\), \(t_i^-=(-g_i)_++s_i\) has absolute Jacobian one. Subtract the residual absolute values from the \(n\) coordinates \(y_i\). The resulting \(n\) nonnegative slacks and the \(m\) nonnegative variables \(2s_i\) occupy a simplex; the latter scaling has Jacobian factor \(2^{-m}\). It follows that
\[
w_{\mathbf z}(U)
=\frac{2^{-m}}{\ell!}
 \int_{\mathbb R^m}
       \bigl(U-E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)\bigr)_+^\ell
 \,d\mathbf g.
\tag{3}
\]
This formula retains both flow directions, their common nonnegative freedom, and all \(n\) slack coordinates. Its volume is in the original continuous coordinates.

The function in (2) is continuous and coercive, since it is at least \(\sum_i|g_i|\), so it attains its minimum. The nonempty integer support is exactly the set of \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\) for which \(\min_{\mathbf g}E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)\le R\), and the positive-volume support is exactly the strict inequality. The first assertion follows by taking the minimal residual values for \(y_i\), choosing the indicated signed flow, and setting \(s_i=0\). For strict inequality, a neighborhood of a minimizing flow has positive remaining budget in (3), so the integral is positive. At equality the integrand in (3) vanishes everywhere, although the boundary fiber remains part of \(S\).

Also \(\sum_i|z_i-1/2|\le2E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)\), by the triangle inequality and \(\sum_i|g_i-g_{i-1}|\le2\sum_i|g_i|\). Hence the nonempty integer support is finite. Every Boolean fiber has positive volume: choose \(t_i^+=t_i^-=\delta\), \(y_i=1/2+\delta\), and \(0<d\delta<k\). These choices satisfy all fiber inequalities strictly. Thus \(0<W<\infty\). These observations and (3) include every non-Boolean and boundary fiber.

We next introduce two exact, finite Laplace integrals, always at parameter \(12\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}
 &=\int_{\mathbb R^m}e^{-12E_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf g)}\,d\mathbf g,\\
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}
 &=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
       \int_{\mathbb R^m}e^{-12E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)}\,d\mathbf g.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The second expression is an auxiliary sum over every integer base, rather than a change in the definition of \(W\). Its finiteness will also follow directly from the kernel bound below.

Let \(\sigma_i=(-1)^{i+1}\) and substitute \(g_i=\sigma_i\xi_i\), with \(\xi_0=\xi_n=0\). Then
\[
\left|z_i-\frac12-g_i+g_{i-1}\right|
=\left|\sigma_i(z_i-1/2)-\xi_i-\xi_{i-1}\right|.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(\mathbf z=\mathbf j\), every first term inside this absolute value is \(1/2\). As \(\mathbf z\) ranges over all integer bases, the numbers \(\sigma_i(z_i-1/2)\) range independently and bijectively over \(\mathbb Z+1/2\).

Define the continuous periodic function
\(\vartheta(t)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}e^{-12|a-t|}\). For \(0\le t\le1\), it is exactly
\(\vartheta(t)=(e^{-12t}+e^{-12(1-t)})/(1-e^{-12})\).
Define symmetric positive kernels on \(\mathbb R\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}(u,v)
 &=e^{-6(|u|+|v|)}e^{-12|1/2-u-v|},\\
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{all}}(u,v)
 &=e^{-6(|u|+|v|)}\vartheta(1/2-u-v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The integrals in (4) are exactly the length-\(n\) kernel path integrals from zero to zero, with \(n-1\) integrated intermediate coordinates, for the respective kernels in (6). The endpoint half-weights in (6) are one at zero, and every internal coordinate receives two half-weights. Equation (5) therefore proves the representation with no Jacobian or dimension change. All sum-integral interchanges here are Tonelli interchanges of nonnegative terms.

We prove an explicit bound for the complete kernel. Set
\(\beta=e^{-3}/4\) and
\(\psi(u)=e^{-6|u|}\cosh(6\operatorname{dist}(u,\mathbb Z))\).
Then \(\psi>0\), \(\psi(0)=1\), and
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\mathcal T_{\mathrm{all}}(u,v)\psi(v)\,dv
\le\beta\psi(u)
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R).
\tag{7}
\]
Here is an elementary verification including the constants. Put
\(G(v)=\vartheta(v)\cosh(6\operatorname{dist}(v,\mathbb Z))\), a continuous, even, one-periodic function, and
\(F(u)=\int_0^1\vartheta(1/2-u-v)G(v)\,dv\).
Periodizing \(e^{-12|v|}\) shows that the left side of (7) is \(e^{-6|u|}F(u)\). The function \(F\) is even and one-periodic, so it suffices to prove \(F(u)\le\beta\cosh(6u)\) on \(0\le u\le1/2\).

For completeness, this one-variable convolution can be computed exactly. Away from its integer cusps, \(\vartheta''=144\vartheta\), and its first derivative has jump \(-24\) at each integer. Differentiating the convolution by splitting its integral at these cusps gives
\(F''(u)-144F(u)=-24G(1/2-u)\).
On \(0\le u\le1/2\), this is
\[
F''(u)-144F(u)
=-\frac{12}{\sinh6}
 \bigl(\cosh(3+6u)+\cosh(3-18u)\bigr),
\qquad F'(0)=F'(1/2)=0.
\tag{8}
\]
The derivative boundary conditions follow from the evenness and periodicity. A particular solution is
\(\cosh(3+6u)/(9\sinh6)-\cosh(3-18u)/(15\sinh6)\);
adding a linear combination of \(\cosh(12u)\) and \(\sinh(12u)\) and imposing the two derivative conditions gives the following expression.

Put \(\tau=e^3\), \(x=e^{6u}\), and let
\[
\begin{aligned}
N={}&x\left(5+\frac{2x}{\tau}-\frac{3x^2}{\tau^2}\right)
 +\left(\frac{7\tau^2}{\tau^2+1}+\frac2\tau\right)x^{-2}
 +\frac5{\tau^2}x^{-1}
 +\frac7{\tau^2(\tau^2+1)}x^2-3x^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Solving (8) gives \(F(u)=N/[45\tau(1-\tau^{-4})]\). This expression can also be checked by substitution in (8) and the two boundary conditions; those conditions determine the solution uniquely, since the homogeneous equation has no nonzero solution with both derivatives zero.

The exponential series gives \(163/60<e<11/4\), and consequently \(20<\tau<21\). One upper-series check is
\(e<8/3+(1/24)/(1-1/5)=87/32<11/4\); the lower bound is the sum through \(1/5!\). Thus \(1\le x\le\tau\) and \(\tau>20\). Put
\(N_0=x(5+2x/\tau-3x^2/\tau^2)+7x^{-2}-3x^{-3}\).
Directly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
45(x+x^{-1})-8N_0
&=x\left(5-\frac{16x}{\tau}+\frac{24x^2}{\tau^2}\right)
  +45x^{-1}-56x^{-2}+24x^{-3}\\
&\ge\frac73x+13x^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, \(5-16y+24y^2=24(y-1/3)^2+7/3\), while
\(45x^2-56x+24-13x^2=8(4x-3)(x-1)\ge0\).

The positive error terms are bounded without approximation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
8(N-N_0)+45\tau^{-4}(x+x^{-1})
&\le \frac{16}{\tau}x^{-2}
 +\frac{40}{\tau^2}x^{-1}
 +\frac{56}{\tau^2(\tau^2+1)}x^2
 +45\tau^{-4}(x+x^{-1})\\
&\le2x+3x^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
For the last line, use respectively \(16/\tau\le1\), \(x^{-2}\le x^{-1}\), \(40/\tau^2\le1\), \(x^2\le\tau x\), \(56/[\tau(\tau^2+1)]\le1\), and \(45/\tau^4\le1\). Combining (9) and (10) proves \(8N\le45(1-\tau^{-4})(x+x^{-1})\). Hence
\(F(u)\le(x+x^{-1})/(8\tau)=\beta\cosh(6u)\), as required for (7).

Let \(f(u)=\mathcal T_{\mathrm{all}}(0,u)\). Since
\(\vartheta(t)\le\vartheta(0)=\coth6<2\) and \(\cosh(6\operatorname{dist}(u,\mathbb Z))\ge1\), we have \(f\le2\psi\). Iterating the positive-kernel inequality (7), and using symmetry at the two endpoints, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}
 &=\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)
       (\mathcal T_{\mathrm{all}}^{\,n-2}f)(u)\,du\\
 &\le2\beta^{n-2}\int_{\mathbb R}f(u)\psi(u)\,du
 \le2\beta^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The final integral is \((\mathcal T_{\mathrm{all}}\psi)(0)\le\beta\). All these integrals are finite already from the boundedness of \(\vartheta\) and the exponential weights. Thus (11) applies to the full sum in (4).

We now obtain a lower bound for the alternating kernel by retaining only intermediate coordinates in \(I=[0,1/2]\). Regard the restriction of \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}\) to \(I\times I\) as an integral operator. It is bounded and symmetric, and its kernel is strictly positive. The length of \(I\) is \(a_I=1/2\). Its constant-function average is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_0
 &=\frac1{a_I}\int_I\int_I\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}(u,v)\,du\,dv\\
 &=2e^{-6}\int_0^{1/2}t e^{6t}\,dt
   +2e^6\int_{1/2}^1(1-t)e^{-18t}\,dt\\
 &=\frac{13e^{-3}}{81}+\frac{e^{-6}}{18}
   +\frac{e^{-12}}{162}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The middle line integrates according to \(t=u+v\), whose unnormalized section lengths in the square are \(t\) and \(1-t\). Since \(e^3<21\),
\(\lambda_0\ge(52/27+2/63)e^{-3}/12>
(39/20)e^{-3}/12\).
Define \(\gamma=13e^{-3}/80=(39/20)e^{-3}/12\), so \(\lambda_0\ge\gamma>0\). Also \(\lambda_0\le a_I<1\), because the kernel on this square is at most one.

We record the even-path inequality used here, rather than relying on an unproved operator estimate. With inner products and norms in \(L^2(I)\), put
\(u_r=\langle1,\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}^{\,2r}1\rangle
=\|\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}^{\,r}1\|_2^2\) for integers \(r\ge0\).
Symmetry and Cauchy-Schwarz give
\(u_r^2\le u_{r-1}u_{r+1}\) for \(r\ge1\).
All these numbers are positive, so their successive ratios are nondecreasing. Another application of Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\(u_1\ge a_I\lambda_0^2\), while \(u_0=a_I\).
Consequently \(u_r\ge a_I\lambda_0^{2r}\) for every \(r\ge0\).

For \(u\in I\), the endpoint factor
\(\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}(0,u)=e^{-6+6u}\) is at least \(e^{-6}\). If \(n\) is even, the \(n-2\) internal kernel steps have even length, so restriction to \(I\) gives
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}\ge(e^{-12}/2)\lambda_0^{n-2}\).
If \(n\) is odd, bound one internal step below by \(e^{-12}\), which is the minimum of \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm B}\) on \(I\times I\), and integrate its now isolated endpoint coordinate over \(I\). The remaining \(n-3\) internal steps have even length, giving
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}\ge(e^{-24}/4)\lambda_0^{n-3}\).
Since \(\lambda_0<1\), both estimates imply the uniform bound
\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}
 \ge\frac{e^{-24}}4\,\gamma^{n-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (11) and (13), with \(\beta/\gamma=20/13\), yield
\(\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}/\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}
\le8e^{24}(20/13)^{n-1}\).

It remains to pass from these Laplace integrals to the common actual budget \(R\). We give this passage explicitly. The minimum energy of the alternating base is
\[
E_0:=\min_{\mathbf g}E_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf g)
=\begin{cases}n/4,&n\ \text{even},\\(n+1)/4,&n\ \text{odd}.\end{cases}
\tag{14}
\]
To prove the lower bounds, for any real \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) satisfying \(|a_i|\le1\) and \(|a_i-a_{i+1}|\le1\), summation by parts in (2) gives
\(\sum_i a_i(j_i-1/2)\le E_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf g)\).
For even \(n\), take \(a_i=\sigma_i/2\), obtaining \(n/4\).
For odd \(n\), take \(a_i=1\) on odd indices and \(a_i=0\) on even indices, obtaining \((n+1)/4\).
For the matching upper bounds, set \(g_i=1/2\) on odd edges and \(g_i=0\) on even edges. All residuals vanish for even \(n\). For odd \(n\), only the last residual remains, with value \(1/2\). This proves (14).

Put \(\zeta=R-E_0\). The parameter assumptions imply
\(n/4\le\zeta\le13n/50\), and \(12\zeta\ge3n>d\).
For \(E_0\le U\le R\), formula (3) and \(E_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf g)\ge E_0\) give
\(w_{\mathbf j}(U)\le((U-E_0)/\zeta)^\ell w_{\mathbf j}(R)\).
Indeed, the ratio of the two nonzero residual budgets in the integrand is at most \((U-E_0)/\zeta\).

For \(U\ge R\), choose any point in the nonempty minimum-budget fiber at \(E_0\). A homothety about this point with ratio \(\zeta/(U-E_0)\) maps the entire budget-\(U\) fiber into the budget-\(R\) fiber: all non-budget inequalities are convex, and the budget is affine. This takes place in the same original \(d\) continuous coordinates. Its volume factor proves
\(w_{\mathbf j}(U)\le((U-E_0)/\zeta)^d w_{\mathbf j}(R)\).
For \(U<E_0\) the fiber is empty.

Tonelli's theorem in (3) gives the exact Laplace transform
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-12U}w_{\mathbf j}(U)\,dU
=\frac{2^{-m}}{12^{\ell+1}}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}.
\tag{15}
\]
Use the low-budget power on \(E_0\le U\le R\) and the high-budget power on \(U\ge R\), and enlarge each of the two resulting nonnegative integrals to all \(U\ge E_0\). Equation (15) is therefore at most
\[
w_{\mathbf j}(R)e^{-12E_0}
\left(\frac{\ell!}{12^{\ell+1}\zeta^\ell}
      +\frac{d!}{12^{d+1}\zeta^d}\right).
\]
Because \(d-\ell=m\) and every factor in \(d!/\ell!\) is at most \(d<12\zeta\), the second summand is at most the first. We conclude that
\[
w_{\mathbf j}
\ge\frac{2^{-m}}{2\ell!}
       e^{12E_0}\zeta^\ell\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}.
\tag{16}
\]

On the other hand, maximizing \(u^\ell e^{-12u}\) over \(u\ge0\) gives
\(u_+^\ell\le(\ell/(12e))^\ell e^{12u}\) for every real \(u\). Apply this pointwise in (3) at budget \(R\) and sum over every integer base. This proves
\[
W\le\frac{2^{-m}}{\ell!}
       \left(\frac{\ell}{12e}\right)^\ell
       e^{12R}\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}.
\tag{17}
\]
This upper bound has used the full sum in (4), including the non-Boolean tail.

For clarity, the remaining numerical estimates are exact finite inequalities. Set \(X=12\zeta\). Then \(X\le(78/25)n\), \(\ell\ge(199/100)n\), and \(X>\ell\), since \(n\ge100\). The function \(X-\ell+\ell\log(\ell/X)\) is increasing in \(X\) and decreasing in \(\ell\) on this range. For \(0<t\le1\), the inequality \(\log t\le2(t-1)/(t+1)\) follows by differentiation and equality at one. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
X-\ell+\ell\log(\ell/X)
&\le n\left(\frac{113}{100}
       +\frac{199}{100}\log\frac{199}{312}\right)\\
&\le n\left(\frac{113}{100}
       -\frac{199}{100}\frac{226}{511}\right)
=\frac{12769}{51100}n
<\frac n4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (11), (13), (16), (17), and (18) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W}{2^n w_{\mathbf j}}
&\le2^{1-n}
   \exp\!\bigl(X-\ell+\ell\log(\ell/X)\bigr)
   \frac{\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}}{\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}}\\
&\le16e^{24}\,2^{-n}e^{n/4}
       \left(\frac{20}{13}\right)^{n-1}\\
&=16e^{24}\frac{13}{20}
       \left(\frac{10e^{1/4}}{13}\right)^n
\le16e^{24}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The last factor is less than one: \(e<11/4<(13/10)^4\), so \(e^{1/4}<13/10\). This proves (1) for every stated \(n,k\).
