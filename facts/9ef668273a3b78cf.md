## Proof

We first compare the Hall edge load with the original transverse ancestry. Put

\[
 c_e:=\alpha\omega_e^b,
 \qquad
 \pi_e:=\frac12
 \sum_{\sigma:\,e\in\{e_\sigma,f_\sigma\}}\eta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \delta_y:=\frac12d_y^\eta.
\tag{23}
\]

Accepted fact d389b79093f65ed0 and the definitions give

\[
 \sum_ec_e=\alpha\Omega_b=P_\cap,
 \qquad
 \sum_e\pi_e=P_\cap,
 \qquad
 \sum_y\delta_y=P_\cap.
\tag{24}
\]

For one original atlas label (3) and one vertex set \(S\), its common-anchor ancestry gives the pointwise indicator inequality

\[
 \mathbf1_{e_\sigma\subseteq S}
 +\mathbf1_{f_\sigma\subseteq S}
 \leq
 \mathbf1_{a(\sigma)\in S}
 +\mathbf1_{b(\sigma)\in S}.
\tag{25}
\]

Indeed, if \(z\notin S\), the left side is zero; if \(z\in S\), the two sides are equal. Multiplying (25) by \(\eta_\sigma/2\) and summing once over the original labels proves

\[
 \boxed{\pi(E(S))\leq\delta(S)}
 \qquad\text{for every }S.
\tag{26}
\]

This uses the actual transverse parent edges from accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66, not an unrelated graph on the two opposite endpoints.

Equations (18), (23), and (26), followed by positive-part subadditivity, give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H
 &\leq\sum_e(c_e-\pi_e)_+\\
 &\leq
 \alpha\sum_e(\omega_e^b-\kappa_e)_+
 +\sum_e(\alpha\kappa_e-\pi_e)_+.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

Because \(0\leq\omega_e^b\leq r_e\) by accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b and \(0\leq\kappa_e\leq r_e\) by (5), every original reservoir edge is counted once in

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_e(\omega_e^b-\kappa_e)_+
 &\leq\sum_e(r_e-\kappa_e)\\
 &=Q-\Omega^{\mathrm{orig}}+|\lambda^\perp|\\
 &\leq R+|\lambda^\perp|.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

On the second term in (27), expand \(\kappa_e\) and \(\pi_e\), use positive-part subadditivity on each parent edge, and then combine the exactly two parent-edge occurrences of each label:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_e(\alpha\kappa_e-\pi_e)_+
 &\leq
 \sum_e\sum_{\sigma:\,e\in\{e_\sigma,f_\sigma\}}
 \left(\alpha\theta_\sigma-\frac{\eta_\sigma}{2}\right)_+\\
 &=\sum_\sigma(2\alpha\theta_\sigma-\eta_\sigma)_+.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

Thus no parent edge or label is reused.

Define the synchronization and completion losses

\[
 J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha):=
 \sum_\sigma
 (2\alpha\theta_\sigma-\widehat\theta_\sigma)_+,
 \qquad
 L_{\mathrm{comp}}:=
 \sum_\sigma(\widehat\theta_\sigma-\eta_\sigma).
\tag{30}
\]

Since \(0\leq\eta_\sigma\leq\widehat\theta_\sigma\), labelwise positive-part subadditivity gives

\[
 (2\alpha\theta_\sigma-\eta_\sigma)_+
 \leq
 (2\alpha\theta_\sigma-\widehat\theta_\sigma)_+
 +\widehat\theta_\sigma-\eta_\sigma.
\tag{31}
\]

The completion loss is controlled after every physical source collision is aggregated. For every label,

\[
 1-\min\{q_{a(\sigma)},q_{b(\sigma)}\}
 \leq
 (1-q_{a(\sigma)})+(1-q_{b(\sigma)}).
\tag{31a}
\]

Thus (15)--(16), followed by summation over the two distinct source occurrences of each label, give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_{\mathrm{comp}}
 &\leq
 \sum_yw_y(1-q_y)\\
 &=\sum_y(w_y-\beta_y^b)_+.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]

Accepted fact 27024aed67e77532 gives \(w_y\leq d_y^r\) and \(\beta_y^b\leq d_y^r\). Hence every physical source is counted once in

\[
 L_{\mathrm{comp}}
 \leq\sum_y(d_y^r-\beta_y^b)
 =2(Q-\Omega_b).
\tag{33}
\]

With \(B_{\mathrm{cong}}\) denoting the canonical original congestion loss in accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b, that fact gives

\[
 |b|\geq Q-R-B_{\mathrm{cong}}-G,
 \qquad
 B_{\mathrm{cong}}\leq R,
 \qquad
 R_C(b)\leq R.
\tag{34}
\]

Applying accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d to the fresh load-minimal optimizer for \(b\) gives

\[
 \Omega_b\geq|b|-R_C(b).
\tag{35}
\]

Therefore

\[
 Q-\Omega_b\leq3R+G,
 \qquad
 \boxed{L_{\mathrm{comp}}\leq6R+2G.}
\tag{36}
\]

Combining (5), (27)--(31), and (36), and using \(\alpha\leq1\), yields the ancestry-and-completion estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_H\leq7R+3G+J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha).}
\tag{37}
\]

It remains to bound the one synchronization loss without omitting the outer-core occurrences. By (12),

\[
 J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha)
 =\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma
 \left(
 2\alpha-\min\{\lambda_{a(\sigma)}^{\mathrm{syn}},
                  \lambda_{b(\sigma)}^{\mathrm{syn}}\}
 \right)_+.
\tag{38}
\]

For \(x,z\geq0\),

\[
 (2\alpha-\min\{x,z\})_+
 \leq(2\alpha-x)_++(2\alpha-z)_+.
\tag{39}
\]

Using the exact occurrence identity (6), accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c and (39) give, with every original source occurrence counted once,

\[
 J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha)
 \leq
 \sum_{y\in E_\perp}
 \tau_y(2\alpha-\lambda_y^{\mathrm{syn}})_+.
\tag{40}
\]

Assume (19). For one dyadic pair \(h=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal M_2\), equations (10)--(13) imply

\[
 2\alpha\tau_u<t_h,
 \qquad
 2\alpha\tau_v<t_h,
 \qquad
 0\leq\omega_h^{\mathrm{syn}}\leq t_h.
\tag{41}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\tau_u(2\alpha-\lambda_u^{\mathrm{syn}})_+
 +\tau_v(2\alpha-\lambda_v^{\mathrm{syn}})_+\\
 &\quad=
 (2\alpha\tau_u-\omega_h^{\mathrm{syn}})_+
 +(2\alpha\tau_v-\omega_h^{\mathrm{syn}})_+\\
 &\quad\leq2(t_h-\omega_h^{\mathrm{syn}}).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]

The dyadic pairs are globally endpoint-disjoint, while \(\lambda_y^{\mathrm{syn}}=0\) off \(Y_2\). Summing (42) once over the matched bins therefore gives

\[
 J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha)
 \leq
 2\alpha\sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus Y_2}\tau_y
 +2(S_2-T_*).
\tag{43}
\]

Apply accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d to the dyadic matching \(t=(t_h)\) and the load-minimal optimizer retained in (11). It gives

\[
 S_2-R_C(t)\leq T_*,
 \qquad
 S_2-T_*\leq R_C(t).
\tag{44}
\]

The same accepted fact identifies \(R_C(t)\) as the maximum mass of an affinely half-metronoid-realizable submatching of \(t\). By (10), the dyadic matching is off-diagonal and same-parity and has endpoint degree at most \(a_y/4\leq a_y/2\), so every such submatching is admissible for the global affine-matching capacity of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5. Its affine-matching certificate therefore gives

\[
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq\gamma_dR_C(t).
\tag{45}
\]

Thus the matched-bin loss in (43) is at most \(2D/\gamma_d\).

We now retain both disjoint parts of the unmatched mass. By (7)--(8),

\[
 E_\perp\setminus Y_2
 =U_2\mathbin{\dot\cup}
 (E_\perp\setminus E_{1/20}).
\tag{46}
\]

The within-core singleton estimate is (8). For the outer-core part, the definition of \(E_{1/20}\) and (2) give

\[
 \sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus E_{1/20}}\tau_y
 <\frac1{20}
 \sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus E_{1/20}}d_y^r
 \leq\frac Q{10}.
\tag{47}
\]

Every unmatched source occurs in exactly one term of (46). Equations (8), (46), and (47) therefore give

\[
 2\alpha\sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus Y_2}\tau_y
 <2\alpha\left(\frac Q{200}+\frac Q{10}\right)
 =\frac{21\alpha Q}{100}.
\tag{48}
\]

Combining (43)--(45) and (48),

\[
 \boxed{
 J_{\mathrm{syn}}^-(\alpha)
 <\frac{21\alpha Q}{100}
 +\frac{2D}{\gamma_d}.}
\tag{49}
\]

Equations (37) and (49) prove (20). The proof has used every original parent edge once in (28), every label once in (29)--(31), every physical completion source once in (32)--(33), every matched dyadic pair once in (42)--(44), and every unmatched source once in (46)--(48).

Finally assume (21), and put

\[
 M:=\max\{R,G,D\}.
\tag{50}
\]

Then \(21\alpha Q/100\leq\Delta_H/2\), so (20) gives

\[
 \frac{\Delta_H}{2}
 <7R+3G+\frac{2D}{\gamma_d}.
\tag{51}
\]

For every \(d\geq1\), the binomial expansion gives

\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 \leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}<3,
\tag{52}
\]

where the final strict inequality follows from \(k!\geq2^{k-1}\) for \(k\geq2\), with strict inequality for some \(k\). Hence \(\gamma_d>1/3\), and therefore

\[
 7R+3G+\frac{2D}{\gamma_d}<16M.
\tag{53}
\]

Equations (51)--(53) imply \(M>\Delta_H/32\), which is (22). No step proves or uses a conclusion for \(\alpha>1/4\) or for \(\Delta_H<21\alpha Q/50\).
