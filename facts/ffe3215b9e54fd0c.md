## Statement

For every odd integer r>=3, set A=2, B=r^2-1, and
\(D_{A,B}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(A,0),(0,B)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2\).
Put \(\mathbf j_r=(1,B/4)\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Z_r&=2r^2+\frac{r(r^2+1)}2,\\
\nu_r(x,y)&=\frac{r^x}{\mathcal Z_r}
\qquad ((x,y)\in D_{A,B}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\mathcal Z_r\) is the original exponential normalizer, not a random variable. The point \(\mathbf j_r\) is an integer support point strictly interior to the triangle. The law \(\nu_r\) is positive on the entire original support and is its unique maximum-entropy law with full vector mean \(\mathbf j_r\). Its original exponential parameter is \((\lambda_x,\beta_{\mathrm v})=(\log r,0)\), so \(q_{\mathrm v}=\exp(\beta_{\mathrm v})=1\in[1/2,1]\).

For the original random vector \(\mathbf X=(X,Y)\) with law \(\nu_r\), the original centered direction \(\mathbf u=(1,0)\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\nu_r}[(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j_r))^2]
&=\mathbb E_{\nu_r}(X-1)^2
=\frac{2r^2}{\mathcal Z_r}>0,\\
\mathbb E_{\nu_r}[(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j_r))^4]
&=\mathbb E_{\nu_r}(X-1)^4
=\frac{2r^2}{\mathcal Z_r},\\
\frac{\mathbb E_{\nu_r}(X-1)^4}
     {[\mathbb E_{\nu_r}(X-1)^2]^2}
&=1+\frac r4+\frac1{4r}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(r\to\infty\text{ through odd integers}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently there is no finite absolute K for which every complete right lattice triangle, every positive maximum-entropy row at a supported interior integer full mean with q_v in [1/2,1], and every real original direction \(\mathbf u\) satisfy \(\mathbb E[(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j))^4]\le K(\mathbb E[(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j))^2])^2\).

## Proof

We compute the entire original support first. Since A=2 and B is even, its exact integer columns are
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{A,B}
={}&\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le y\le B\}\\
&\cup\{(1,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le y\le B/2\}
\cup\{(2,0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are obtained from the original inequalities x>=0, y>=0, and x/2+y/B<=1; no intermediate lattice points have been removed. Because r is odd, r^2-1 is divisible by 8, so B/4 is an integer. At x=1 the upper ordinate is B/2, and 0<B/4<B/2. Moreover j_r has both coordinates positive and its noncoordinate facet value is 1/2+(B/4)/B=3/4<1. Thus j_r is a supported integer point strictly inside the original triangle.

The unnormalized exponential weight at (x,y) is exactly exp((log r)x+0y)=r^x. Summing this weight over each complete column gives, respectively,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y=0}^{B}r^0&=B+1=r^2,\\
\sum_{y=0}^{B/2}r^1&=r(B/2+1)=\frac{r(r^2+1)}2,\\
r^2&=r^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their sum is precisely \(\mathcal Z_r\). Hence the displayed law is normalized and positive at every original support point; all its probabilities are rational. In particular,
\(\Pr(X=0)=\Pr(X=2)=r^2/\mathcal Z_r\), while
\(\Pr(X=1)=r(r^2+1)/(2\mathcal Z_r)\).
The two equal outer column probabilities give the first full mean equation \(\mathbb EX=1\) exactly.

Conditional on X=x, the original Y-coordinate is uniform on its whole integer column, so
\(\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x]=\tfrac12 B(1-x/2)\) for all x=0,1,2, including the singleton apex column. Therefore the second full mean equation is
\(\mathbb EY=B/2-(B/4)\mathbb EX=B/4\).
Thus \(\mathbb E\mathbf X=\mathbf j_r\) in the original coordinates, and the displayed original parameter has q_v=1 as claimed.

We verify the maximum-entropy identification without assuming a row-existence theorem. Let \(\eta\) be any probability law on the full set \(D_{A,B}\) with full vector mean \(\mathbf j_r\), and let H denote its Shannon entropy, using natural logarithms and 0 log 0=0. Direct substitution of \(\log\nu_r(x,y)=x\log r-\log\mathcal Z_r\) and \(\mathbb E_\eta X=1\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\nu_r)&=\log\mathcal Z_r-\log r,\\
D(\eta\Vert\nu_r)
&=\sum_{\eta(x,y)>0}\eta(x,y)
       \log\frac{\eta(x,y)}{\nu_r(x,y)}
=H(\nu_r)-H(\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, applying -log t>=1-t to t=nu_r(x,y)/eta(x,y) at each positive eta-mass shows
\(D(\eta\Vert\nu_r)\ge1-\sum_{\eta(x,y)>0}\nu_r(x,y)\ge0\).
If eta omits any original point, the last inequality is strict because nu_r is positive everywhere. If eta has full support, equality requires equality in -log t>=1-t at every point, hence eta=nu_r. This proves that nu_r is exactly the unique maximum-entropy law subject to the original full vector mean. Its displayed exponential parameter is also unique: equality of two positive exponential laws, evaluated at (0,0),(1,0),(0,1), forces their two parameters to agree.

Finally the original centered projection X-1 takes values -1,0,1. Its second and fourth powers are both one on the outer columns and zero on the middle column. Hence both moments equal \(2r^2/\mathcal Z_r\), and their ratio is
\(\mathcal Z_r/(2r^2)=1+(r^2+1)/(4r)=1+r/4+1/(4r)\).
This tends to infinity. Given any proposed finite absolute K, choose an odd r>=3 with r>4K; this original row and direction have positive variance and ratio greater than K. They violate the proposed fourth-moment inequality while meeting every original support, positivity, integer-mean, and vertical-parameter hypothesis. No latent branch law, auxiliary mean, or rare upper-facet event is used. The conclusion concerns that unconditional moment inequality; no product cap probability is estimated in this argument.
