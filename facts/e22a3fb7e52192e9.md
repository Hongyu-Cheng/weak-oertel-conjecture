## Statement

Let \(k,G\ge1\) be integers, and partition \(\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\) into nonempty blocks \(I_1,\ldots,I_G\), with \(k_i:=|I_i|\). For each \(i\), fix an integer \(s_i\in\lbrace0,\ldots,2k_i\rbrace\). Let \(P\subseteq[0,2]^k\) be convex and invariant under every permutation preserving each block. Suppose that every \(\mathbf x\in P\) satisfies \(\sum_{\ell\in I_i}x_\ell=s_i\) for every \(i\), and that \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\ne\varnothing\). The set \(P\) need not be closed or have full affine dimension, and need not be a Cartesian product.

For arbitrary \(\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_k)\in\mathbb R^k\), set \(A:=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\), \(w_{\mathbf x}:=e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf x}\), \(Z:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x}\), and \(p_{(\mathbf x,0)}:=w_{\mathbf x}/Z\). Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&:=\inf_{\substack{H\ni\mathbf z\\
 H=\lbrace\mathbf w:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w\ge b\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1},\ \mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}}
 \sum_{\mathbf w\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf w},\\
M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\qquad c:=\frac{e^{-7/24}}{12\sqrt{6\pi}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{(\mathbf x,0)}\tau_p((\mathbf x,0))&\ge c\,2^{-k},\\
M_3(p)&\ge c^3\,8^{-k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

All sampling probabilities and halfspace masses are those of the original affine law. Here \(G,k_i,I_i,s_i,D,A,Z,\tau_p,M_3\) use the block and ambient conventions of accepted fact \(\texttt{ae556dca21175ef7}\), except that its tuple-constant weight table is replaced by the explicitly displayed individual-coordinate affine table. The spatial dimension is \(k\), height is zero, and the auxiliary ambient dimension is \(N=k+1\). These parameters do not replace the locked problem's \(n,d\), and \(c\) does not denote its reserved constant \(c_0\).

## Proof

Write \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\) and \(h_i:=s_i-k_i\). The centered vector \(\mathbf x-\mathbf1\) is ternary with values in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\), and its sum in block \(i\) is the fixed integer \(h_i\). Its number of nonzero coordinates in that block is \(|h_i|+2a_i\), where \(a_i\) is the smaller of its positive and negative counts. Its positive, negative, and zero counts are therefore respectively

$$
a_i+\max\lbrace h_i,0\rbrace,\qquad
a_i+\max\lbrace-h_i,0\rbrace,\qquad
k_i-|h_i|-2a_i,
\quad 0\le a_i\le\left\lfloor\frac{k_i-|h_i|}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{1}
$$

For clarity about the predecessor count notation, if \(h_i\le0\), this \(a_i\) is precisely the two count in the lower-block-sum construction of \(\texttt{ae556dca21175ef7}\), with its deficit \(j_i=-h_i\). If \(h_i>0\), it is that construction's two count after reflecting this block by \(x_\ell\mapsto2-x_\ell\), with deficit \(j_i=h_i\). These are identifications of the counts only; we continue throughout with the original points and original weights.

Let \(F\) be the nonempty finite set of tuples \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_G)\) occurring in \(D\). The within-block permutations act transitively on all points with the counts (1) in every block. Their independent actions in the different blocks consequently supply the entire product of those orbits whenever \(\mathbf a\in F\).

Moreover, \(F\) is a downset in the product of the ranges in (1). Indeed, if \(a_i>0\), a point with those counts has a zero and a two in block \(i\). Exchange those two coordinates. The exchanged point belongs to \(P\), and its midpoint with the original point belongs to \(P\) by convexity. That midpoint is integral and replaces the selected zero and two by two ones. It thus belongs to \(D\) and has count tuple \(\mathbf a-\mathbf e_i\). Repetition proves the downset claim. This is the midpoint mechanism of \(\texttt{ae556dca21175ef7}\), proved here also for positive \(h_i\); it asserts no product structure for \(F\).

For \(\mathbf x\in D\), let \(E_i:=\lbrace\ell\in I_i:x_\ell\ne1\rbrace\) and \(\mathbf E:=(E_1,\ldots,E_G)\). The tuple \(\mathbf E\) specifies exactly its coordinatewise parity class. Put \(L_i:=|E_i|\). A tuple of subsets \(\mathbf E\) is present if and only if

$$
L_i\ge|h_i|,\qquad L_i\equiv h_i\pmod2\quad(1\le i\le G),
\qquad \left(\frac{L_i-|h_i|}{2}\right)_{i=1}^G\in F.
\tag{2}
$$

For the converse in (2), the full-orbit property just proved supplies every sign assignment on the prescribed sets \(E_i\) with block sign sums \(h_i\). Thus each present parity class consists exactly of all the points
\(\mathbf x=\mathbf1+\xi\) with \(\xi_\ell=0\) off \(\bigcup_iE_i\), \(\xi_\ell\in\lbrace-1,1\rbrace\) on \(E_i\), and \(\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\xi_\ell=h_i\) for every \(i\).

Denote the family of present tuples by \(\mathscr E\), and their original masses by

$$
V_{\mathbf E}:=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in D\\
 \lbrace\ell\in I_i:x_\ell\ne1\rbrace=E_i\ (1\le i\le G)}}w_{\mathbf x},
\qquad Q_{\mathrm{par}}:=\sum_{\mathbf E\in\mathscr E}V_{\mathbf E}^2,
\qquad Z=\sum_{\mathbf E\in\mathscr E}V_{\mathbf E}.
\tag{3}
$$

These are the analogues of \(V_E,Q_{\mathrm{par}},Z\) in \(\texttt{6ebad92de963eae2}\), with its single parity set \(E\) replaced by the union of the present tuple's disjoint sets. No weights have been rescaled: \(w_{\mathbf1+\xi}=e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf1}e^{-\beta\cdot\xi}\).

We first obtain a lower bound for \(Q_{\mathrm{par}}\). Relabel the blocks so that \(m:=k_1=\max_i k_i\), hence \(m\ge k/G\). Accepted fact \(\texttt{98175b11f4e453f3}\) proves that the Boolean lattice on \(m\) coordinates can be partitioned into \(M_m:=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) saturated symmetric chains, and proves \(M_m\le2^m/\sqrt{m+1}\). This applies to subsets of \(I_1\), with that fact's Boolean dimension \(n=m\). We use only those combinatorial statements here, not its support theorem or its product formula for parity masses.

Fix subsets \(E_i\subseteq I_i\), \(2\le i\le G\), with \(|E_i|\equiv h_i\pmod2\). If there is no completion to a present tuple, retain nothing for this choice. Otherwise the corresponding counts \(a_2,\ldots,a_G\) are fixed, and the downset property of \(F\) says that the feasible first counts are exactly \(0,1,\ldots,r\) for some integer \(r\). By (2), the permissible cardinalities of \(E_1\) are therefore exactly \(|h_1|,|h_1|+2,\ldots,|h_1|+2r\). From every Boolean chain retain just its sets with those cardinalities. The result is a possibly empty chain in which successive sets differ by the addition of two coordinates. Together with the fixed \(E_2,\ldots,E_G\), these retained chains partition all present parity tuples with that fixed choice. The empty tuple is the single choice when \(G=1\).

Suppose successive tuples in one such chain differ by adding \(\alpha,\gamma\) to \(E_1\). Extend each original point in the smaller class by replacing its entries \(x_\alpha=x_\gamma=1\) with \((2,0)\) or \((0,2)\). Both extended points belong to the larger class by its full-orbit description, and all these extensions are distinct. The ratios of their original weights to the old point's weight are \(e^{-\beta_\alpha+\beta_\gamma}\) and \(e^{\beta_\alpha-\beta_\gamma}\). Hence the successive masses satisfy

$$
V_{\mathbf E'}\ge
2\cosh(\beta_\alpha-\beta_\gamma)V_{\mathbf E}\ge2V_{\mathbf E}.
\tag{4}
$$

For a nonempty retained chain with masses \(u_1,\ldots,u_l\) in increasing order, (4) gives \(\sum_{i<j}u_i\le u_j\sum_{a\ge1}2^{-a}=u_j\). Thus \((\sum_j u_j)^2\le3\sum_j u_j^2\), also valid for an empty chain.

Since each \(k_i\ge1\), exactly \(2^{k_i-1}\) subsets of \(I_i\) have either prescribed cardinality parity: toggling a fixed coordinate is a bijection between the two parity families. Therefore our partition has at most \(M_m\prod_{i=2}^G2^{k_i-1}=M_m2^{k-m-(G-1)}\) chains. Cauchy-Schwarz over these chains, including empty ones if necessary, yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z^2&\le3M_m2^{k-m-(G-1)}Q_{\mathrm{par}},\\
Q_{\mathrm{par}}&\ge
\frac{2^{G-1}\sqrt{m+1}}{3\,2^k}Z^2
\ge\frac{\sqrt k}{3\,2^k}Z^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

For the last inequality, \(k\le Gm\) and \(4^{G-1}\ge G\) for all integers \(G\ge1\). The latter follows by induction, since \(4G\ge G+1\). Consequently \(2^{G-1}\sqrt{m+1}\ge\sqrt{Gm}\ge\sqrt k\). This chain allocation is the only step that couples the block count and the analytic dimension estimate below.

We next bound the conditional energy variance within each parity class. Accepted fact \(\texttt{6ebad92de963eae2}\), proof equations (1)-(3), establishes the following estimate for every integer \(q\) with \(1\le q\le L-1\): if independent Bernoulli variables have parameters \(\rho_\ell\in(0,1)\) with \(\sum_\ell\rho_\ell=q\), and \(\widehat B\) is their conditional law given \(\sum_\ell B_\ell=q\), then, for all real coefficients \(\theta_\ell\),

$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_\ell\theta_\ell\widehat B_\ell\right)
\le12\sum_\ell\theta_\ell^2\rho_\ell(1-\rho_\ell).
\tag{6}
$$

Here the predecessor's length \(L\), integer \(q\), parameters \(\rho_\ell\), and conditional variables \(\widehat B_\ell\) retain their meanings; its coefficient \(a_\ell\) in equation (3) is our \(\theta_\ell\), and is unrelated to the orbit counts \(a_i\). The estimate (6) was proved there before specializing to \(L=2q\), so it does not require half balance.

Fix \(\mathbf E\in\mathscr E\). In block \(i\), the signs on \(E_i\) have exactly \(q_i:=(L_i+h_i)/2\) positive entries, with probabilities proportional to \(e^{-\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\beta_\ell\xi_\ell}\). If \(q_i=0\) or \(q_i=L_i\), this sign vector is deterministic and its energy variance is zero. This includes \(L_i=0\) and all cases of \(L_i=1\).

In the remaining case \(1\le q_i\le L_i-1\), choose the unique real \(\lambda\) satisfying \(\sum_{\ell\in E_i}(1+e^{2(\beta_\ell-\lambda)})^{-1}=q_i\). Existence and uniqueness follow from continuity and strict increase from zero to \(L_i\). Set \(\theta_\ell:=2(\beta_\ell-\lambda)\) and \(\rho_\ell:=(1+e^{\theta_\ell})^{-1}\). The signs \(\xi_\ell=2\widehat B_\ell-1\) under the conditional Bernoulli law in (6) have exactly the required law: conditioning fixes \(\sum_\ell\widehat B_\ell=q_i\), so the factor involving \(\lambda\) is constant. Also

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\beta_\ell\xi_\ell
&=\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\theta_\ell\widehat B_\ell
 +2\lambda q_i-\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\beta_\ell,\\
\theta_\ell^2\rho_\ell(1-\rho_\ell)
&=\frac{\theta_\ell^2}{4\cosh^2(\theta_\ell/2)}\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

The last inequality uses \(\cosh u\ge1+u^2/2\ge|u|\). Equations (6)-(7) give variance at most \(12L_i\) for the block energy. This explicitly extends the energy calculation in equation (4) of \(\texttt{6ebad92de963eae2}\) from zero sign sum to the present fixed sum \(h_i\).

The full-orbit description gives a product of these fixed-sign-sum spaces inside this one parity class. Since its original weight is \(e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf1}\prod_i e^{-\sum_{\ell\in E_i}\beta_\ell\xi_\ell}\), its conditional original law factors across the blocks. Thus, if \(X\) has probabilities \(w_{\mathbf x}/V_{\mathbf E}\) on this class, the block energies are independent and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\beta\cdot X)
\le12\sum_{i=1}^G L_i\le12k.
\tag{8}
$$

This factorization concerns only the law conditional on a present parity tuple; the distribution of tuples and the original support remain coupled.

Take independent \(X,Y\) with this conditional original law and put \(T_{\mathbf E}:=\beta\cdot(X-Y)/2\). Then \(\mathbb ET_{\mathbf E}=0\) and \(\mathbb ET_{\mathbf E}^2\le6k\) by (8). Its characteristic function is nonnegative on the entire real line, since
\(\mathbb E e^{itT_{\mathbf E}}=|\mathbb E e^{it\beta\cdot X/2}|^2\ge0\).
This does not require independence of coordinates. As it is real, \(\cos u\ge1-u^2/2\) gives \(\mathbb E\cos(tT_{\mathbf E})\ge1-3kt^2\).

We use equation (7) of \(\texttt{98175b11f4e453f3}\), the Gaussian identity \(e^{-z^2}=\pi^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-s^2}\cos(2sz)\,ds\). Applying it to the finite law of \(T_{\mathbf E}\), nonnegativity permits restriction to \(|s|\le1/\sqrt{24k}\), where the characteristic function at \(2s\) is at least \(1/2\). Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E e^{-T_{\mathbf E}^2}
&\ge\frac1{\sqrt\pi}
\int_{-1/\sqrt{24k}}^{1/\sqrt{24k}}e^{-s^2}\frac12\,ds
\ge\frac{e^{-1/24}}{2\sqrt{6\pi}\sqrt k},\\
\mathbb E e^{-|T_{\mathbf E}|}
&\ge e^{-1/4}\mathbb E e^{-T_{\mathbf E}^2}
\ge\frac{e^{-7/24}}{2\sqrt{6\pi}\sqrt k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

The second line uses \((|z|-1/2)^2\ge0\). The first uses \(k\ge1\), so \(e^{-s^2}\ge e^{-1/24}\) on the integration interval. It also covers a deterministic conditional energy, including the all-empty parity tuple when present. Thus (9) is the analytic bound of equation (8) of \(\texttt{6ebad92de963eae2}\) with its parity set replaced by \(\mathbf E\), justified by (8) rather than any independent-coordinate law.

It remains to connect these estimates to the original ambient depths. Put \(\tau_w(\mathbf J):=Z\tau_p((\mathbf J,0))\), and define the unnormalized scalar average \(\mathcal A_1:=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}\tau_w(\mathbf J)\). The scalar depth average called \(A\) in \(\texttt{98175b11f4e453f3}\) is denoted \(\mathcal A_1\) here; our \(A\) continues to denote \(D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\). Its normalizer \(W\) is replaced in this role by our original \(Z\). We prove the ambient version of its reflection identity directly.

For \(\mathbf J\in D\), set

$$
S_{\mathbf J}:=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^k\\
\mathbf J+\mathbf v\in D,\ \mathbf J-\mathbf v\in D}}
\min\lbrace w_{\mathbf J+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf J-\mathbf v}\rbrace.
\tag{10}
$$

Every closed ambient halfspace containing \((\mathbf J,0)\) contains at least one of the two reflected endpoints \((\mathbf J+\mathbf v,0),(\mathbf J-\mathbf v,0)\), since their midpoint is \((\mathbf J,0)\). Hence

$$
w_{\mathbf J+\mathbf v}\mathbf1_H((\mathbf J+\mathbf v,0))
+w_{\mathbf J-\mathbf v}\mathbf1_H((\mathbf J-\mathbf v,0))
\ge\min\lbrace w_{\mathbf J+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf J-\mathbf v}\rbrace.
$$

Summed over the oriented, zero-inclusive index \(\mathbf v\) in (10), the left side is exactly twice the original weight in \(H\) of the reflected subset \((D\cap(2\mathbf J-D))\times\lbrace0\rbrace\), and is at most twice the original weight of \(A\cap H\). The term \(\mathbf v=0\) contributes \(2w_{\mathbf J}\) on that left side. Taking the infimum over the original containing halfspaces proves \(\tau_w(\mathbf J)\ge S_{\mathbf J}/2\), and therefore

$$
\mathcal A_1\ge\frac12\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}.
\tag{11}
$$

All ambient normals are included in this argument, including pure height normals.

The assignment \((\mathbf J,\mathbf v)\mapsto(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(\mathbf J+\mathbf v,\mathbf J-\mathbf v)\) is a bijection from the summation indices in (11) onto ordered pairs in \(D^2\) with equal coordinatewise parity. Indeed, the forward endpoints differ by \(2\mathbf v\). Conversely, equal parity makes \(\mathbf J=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) and \(\mathbf v=(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)/2\) integral. Convexity puts \(\mathbf J\) in \(P\), and completeness \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^k\) puts it in \(D\). These assignments are inverse, including diagonal pairs. Closedness of \(P\) is not needed. Equal parity means exactly that the endpoint tuples \(\mathbf E\) coincide.

Log-affinity gives \(w_{\mathbf x}w_{\mathbf y}=w_{\mathbf J}^2\) and
\(w_{\mathbf J}\min\lbrace w_{\mathbf x},w_{\mathbf y}\rbrace=w_{\mathbf x}w_{\mathbf y}e^{-|\beta\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)/2|}\).
Consequently the preceding bijection gives the exact original-weight identity

$$
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}S_{\mathbf J}
=\sum_{\mathbf E\in\mathscr E}V_{\mathbf E}^2\,
\mathbb E e^{-|T_{\mathbf E}|}.
\tag{12}
$$

This is equation (7) of \(\texttt{6ebad92de963eae2}\) for the current complete convex support: the endpoint laws, normalizers, and ordered-pair multiplicities are exactly those defined in (3), (8), and (10).

Since \(\mathcal A_1/Z^2\) is the original first-depth average, equations (5), (9), (11), and (12) yield

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}p_{(\mathbf J,0)}\tau_p((\mathbf J,0))
&\ge\frac{e^{-7/24}}{4\sqrt{6\pi}\sqrt k}
\frac{Q_{\mathrm{par}}}{Z^2}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-7/24}}{12\sqrt{6\pi}}\,2^{-k}
=c\,2^{-k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, Jensen's inequality for the original probability law \(p\) and the convex function \(t\mapsto t^3\) on \([0,\infty)\) proves \(M_3(p)\ge c^3 8^{-k}\). Nonemptiness of \(D\) ensures \(Z>0\). The proof permits every boundary sum \(s_i=0\) or \(2k_i\), blocks of size one, a single original atom, and lower-dimensional supports, because deterministic conditional sign laws and empty retained chains were included explicitly.
