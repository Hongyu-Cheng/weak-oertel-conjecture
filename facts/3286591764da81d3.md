## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $d=m+1$, $a<0<b$, and let $r:[a,b]\to(0,\infty)$ be finite and concave. In the planar layer with density $r(z)^{m-1}/2$ on $a\le z\le b,\ |x|\le r(z)$, let $V=\int_a^b r(z)^m\,dz$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $\sigma_s$ be the pushforward to the $z$-coordinate of the section measure obtained by integrating the layer density against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$ on $z+sx=0$. Set $C(s)=\int d\sigma_s$ and $M(s)=\int z\,d\sigma_s$. Put $r_0=r(0)$ and take any affine majorant $r_0(1+b_0z)\ge r(z)$, which exists by concavity at the interior point $0$. For every $s\ge0$,
$$
 \int (1+b_0z)^{-d}\,d\sigma_s(z)\le C(0)=r_0^m,\qquad
 C(s)-d b_0M(s)\le C(0).
$$
For any probability law on finite slopes with average $M$ zero, the average of $C$ is at most $C(0)$. If $b_0\ne0$, equality of the averaged bound requires and is attained by the law concentrated at $s=0$.

## Proof

At slope zero the coarea measure is $r_0^m\delta_0$. At $s>0$, on $E_s=\{z\in[a,b]:|z|\le sr(z)\}$ the $z$-parametrization of the section gives $d\sigma_s=r(z)^{m-1}dz/(2s)$. Outside $E_s$ the measure is zero. The majorant is positive on the compact axial interval. The increasing substitution $q=z/[s(1+b_0z)]$ has $dq=dz/[s(1+b_0z)^2]$, and $|q|\le r_0$ on $E_s$. Also $r(z)^{m-1}\le r_0^{m-1}(1+b_0z)^{m-1}$ there, including $m=1$. Since $d=m+1$, these give
$$
 \int (1+b_0z)^{-d}\,d\sigma_s(z)\le \tfrac12 r_0^{m-1}|q(E_s)|\le r_0^m.
$$
The function $(1+b_0z)^{-d}$ is above its tangent $1-d b_0z$, strictly away from zero if $b_0\ne0$. Integrating proves the second bound; averaging cancels $M$. The measures and moments are bounded uniformly because the denominator in the first bound is bounded above and below on the axial interval. For each $s>0$, $\sigma_s$ has positive density on an interval around zero, so the tangent step is strict for every such $s$ when $b_0\ne0$. A probability law assigning positive mass to $(0,\infty)$ therefore gives strict average inequality.
