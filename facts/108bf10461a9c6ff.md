## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be real, let $\ell\le r$ be integers, and let $a_\ell,\ldots,a_r>0$ have concave $d$th roots, meaning the successive differences of $a_i^{1/d}$ are nonincreasing. Set $F_i=\sum_{j=i}^r a_j$. Then $(F_i^{1/(d+1)})_{i=\ell}^r$ is concave on its actual indices. The exponent is the largest positive exponent that works for every such sequence at fixed $d$.

## Proof

Write $p=1/(d+1)$. At an interior index $\ell<i<r$, put $F=F_i$, $A=a_{i-1}$ and $b=a_i$. Then $F_{i-1}=F+A$ and $F_{i+1}=F-b>0$. If $A\le b$, their average is at most $F$, and monotonicity and concavity of the $p$th power give $(F+A)^p+(F-b)^p\le 2F^p$.

If $A>b$, put $\delta=A^{1/d}-b^{1/d}$ and $q=b^{1/d}/\delta$. For each $0\le k\le r-i$, successive differences of the roots give $0<a_{i+k}^{1/d}\le b^{1/d}-k\delta$. Taking $k=1$ gives $q>1$. Thus $b\le F\le F_{\max}=\delta^d S_d(q)$ by termwise comparison with the positive-part sequence. Terms past $r$ are zero for this comparison. The identities
$$
F_{\max}+A=\delta^d S_d(q+1),\qquad
F_{\max}-b=\delta^d S_d(q-1)
$$
hold because $A=\delta^d(q+1)^d$ and $b=\delta^d q^d$. Applying arithmetic-power-sum-concavity with this $d$ and $q$ gives $(F_{\max}+A)^p+(F_{\max}-b)^p\le2F_{\max}^p$.

The function $H(s)=(1+As)^p+(1-bs)^p-2$ is concave on $[0,1/b]$ with $H(0)=0$. Its secant slope $H(s)/s$ is nonincreasing for $s>0$. The inequality at $F_{\max}$ says $H(1/F_{\max})\le0$, so $1/F\in[1/F_{\max},1/b]$ gives $H(1/F)\le0$. Multiplication by $F^p$ proves the desired midpoint inequality at $i$. This exhausts the actual interior indices. For one or two indices there is no midpoint condition. The sequence ends at $F_r=a_r$.

For exponent optimality, take $a_i=(r+1-i)^d$ on $i=0,\ldots,r$. Its roots are affine and $F_i=A_{r-i}$ for the arithmetic sequence with $\rho=1$. For any larger exponent choose an interior violation at $m$ in that sequence, whose existence was proved in arithmetic-power-sum-concavity, and take $r\ge m+1$ and $i=r-m$. This places the violation at an actual interior tail index.
