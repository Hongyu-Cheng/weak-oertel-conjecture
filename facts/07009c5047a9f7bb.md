## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n),
\]
\[
W(C):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), write
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\
\int_Kf(x)\,dx=t
\right\}.
\]

The following arbitrary-selected-mass construction holds.

Let \(E\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\) be finite and nonempty, choose masses
\[
0<t_z\leq a_z\qquad(z\in E),
\qquad
T:=\sum_{z\in E}t_z,
\tag{1}
\]
and let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\).  Define
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{
\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,
\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m
\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\qquad
\mathcal K_m(t):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zD_z.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(\mathcal K_m(t)\neq\varnothing\), then
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq
\gamma_d
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, if
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z\geq2^{-n}T
\tag{5}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), then
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq T,
\qquad
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}T.
\tag{6}
\]

Suppose instead that \(\mathcal K_m(t)=\varnothing\).  For \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
h_z(v):=h_{D_z}(v),
\qquad
\phi_t(v):=
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zh_z(v).
\tag{7}
\]
Then there are an integer \(r\) with \(2\leq r\leq d+1\), nonzero directions \(w_1,\ldots,w_r\in\mathbb R^d\), positive weights \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r\), and globally affine functions \(\ell_i:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_iw_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i\phi_t(w_i)<0,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\ell_i(z)\leq h_z(w_i)\quad(z\in E),
\qquad
\ell_i(m)=\phi_t(w_i).
\tag{9}
\]
The family may be chosen with minimum possible support cardinality among all families satisfying (8).  Define the proper closed ambient halfspaces and their cap bodies by
\[
G_i:=\{(z,x):\langle w_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\},
\qquad
A_i:=C\cap G_i,
\tag{10}
\]
and put
\[
f_i(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d((A_i)_z).
\tag{11}
\]
Every \(A_i\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body contained in \(C\), and
\[
f_i(z)\geq\gamma_dt_z
\qquad(z\in E,\ 1\leq i\leq r).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently,
\[
W(A_i)\geq\gamma_dT
\qquad(1\leq i\leq r).
\tag{13}
\]

For every strictly positive probability vector
\(
\boldsymbol\vartheta=(\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_r)
\), define the same-base reweighting \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) by
\[
\widehat B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}:=
\left\{
(z,x_1,\ldots,x_r):(z,x_i)\in A_i\text{ for every }i
\right\},
\tag{14}
\]
\[
B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}:=
\left\{
\left(z,\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_ix_i\right):
(z,x_1,\ldots,x_r)\in\widehat B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}
\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body contained in \(C\), with exact fibers
\[
(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})_z
=\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i(A_i)_z,
\tag{16}
\]
where a Minkowski sum with an empty summand is empty.  If
\(
b_z^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}:=
\operatorname{vol}_d((B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})_z)
\), then
\[
b_z^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\geq\gamma_dt_z
\qquad(z\in E),
\tag{17}
\]
and therefore
\[
W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})\geq\gamma_dT.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover, (15) is genuinely a positive reweighting of the same selected-mass cap circuit: defining
\[
w_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}:=
\frac{\theta_i}{\vartheta_i}w_i,
\qquad
\ell_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}:=
\frac{\theta_i}{\vartheta_i}\ell_i
\tag{19}
\]
preserves the cap halfspaces \(G_i\), preserves exactness of the affine minorants, and gives a support-minimal balanced negative family with weights \(\vartheta_i\).

The cap circuit has exact selected-mass contacts.  Put
\[
\rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}E.
\tag{20}
\]
For every \(i\), there is a nonempty affinely independent set \(T_i\subseteq E\) such that
\[
1\leq|T_i|\leq\rho+1\leq n+1,
\qquad
\ell_i(z)=h_z(w_i)\quad(z\in T_i),
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
\gamma_dt_z\leq f_i(z)\leq t_z
\qquad(z\in T_i).
\tag{22}
\]
Hence
\[
W(C)-W(A_i)
\geq
\sum_{z\in T_i}(a_z-t_z).
\tag{23}
\]

Now let \(p=(p_e)\) be any nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching on the positive-volume lattice fibers of \(C\), in the normalization of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`.  Thus \(p_e\geq0\), its mass is
\[
P:=|p|:=\sum_ep_e>0,
\tag{24}
\]
and its endpoint degrees satisfy
\[
d_z(p):=\sum_{e\ni z}p_e\leq\frac{a_z}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Put
\[
E_p:=\{z:d_z(p)>0\},
\qquad
t_z:=2d_z(p)\quad(z\in E_p).
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
0<t_z\leq a_z,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in E_p}t_z=4P.
\tag{27}
\]
There is \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E_p\) satisfying (5) for this selected profile.  At that \(m\), exactly one of the following two alternatives holds.

1. The degree-saturated core \(\mathcal K_m(t)\) is nonempty.  Then
   \[
   \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq4P,
   \qquad
   h_{\max}(C)\geq4\gamma_d2^{-n}P.
   \tag{28}
   \]

2. The degree-saturated core is empty.  Then the cap circuit above satisfies
   \[
   \operatorname{vol}_d((A_i)_z)
   \geq2\gamma_dd_z(p)
   \qquad(z\in E_p),
   \tag{29}
   \]
   and every positive reweighting satisfies
   \[
   \operatorname{vol}_d((B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})_z)
   \geq2\gamma_dd_z(p)
   \qquad(z\in E_p).
   \tag{30}
   \]
   Therefore the edge family
   \[
   p_e^+:=\gamma_dp_e
   \tag{31}
   \]
   is a free same-parity matching in every individual cap body \(A_i\) and in every positive reweighting \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\).  Its mass is exactly
   \[
   |p^+|=\gamma_dP>\frac Pe.
   \tag{32}
   \]
   Every one of these bodies has mixed mass at least
   \[
   4\gamma_dP.
   \tag{33}
   \]
   At every contact fiber in (21), the sharper bounds and pruning identity are
   \[
   2\gamma_dd_z(p)
   \leq
   \operatorname{vol}_d((A_i)_z)
   \leq
   2d_z(p),
   \tag{34}
   \]
   \[
   W(C)-W(A_i)
   \geq
   \sum_{z\in T_i}\bigl(a_z-2d_z(p)\bigr).
   \tag{35}
   \]

The objects in alternative 2 have three distinct statuses.  Each \(A_i=C\cap G_i\) is an individual cap body.  Each \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) with all \(\vartheta_i>0\) is an arbitrary-selected-mass same-base cap reweighting.  If, in a special application, the selected masses and the circuit satisfy the half-mass hypotheses of accepted facts `51ff186e1aaeaa85` and `e3679dc542b23efe`, then (15) is also a legal balanced-cap descendant in their narrower sense.  For general selected masses, including the degree-saturated masses (26), only the first two statuses are asserted.  In particular, no half-mass-descendant monotonicity, bounded stopping, inherited-stress localization, primitive rank reduction, or global stress-absorption conclusion is part of this theorem.

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty, so every sum in the statement is finite.  If \(a_z>0\), then \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` says that, for every mass in (1), \(D_z=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(C_z\), and every \(y\in D_z\) has absolute fiber halfspace depth at least
\[
\gamma_dt_z.
\tag{36}
\]

If the core (3) is nonempty, apply accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` with \(D_z=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\) and depth weights \(\gamma_dt_z\).  It gives (4).  Under (5), the tuple \((E,(t_z),m)\) is admissible in the definition of \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\), so \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq T\).  Equation (6) then follows either directly from (4) or from the master bound in the same accepted fact.

Suppose the core is empty.  Apply the abstract support-envelope and empty-core theorem in accepted fact `ebc478d91566cb69` to the sets \(D_z\).  It supplies a balanced negative family satisfying (8), with \(2\leq r\leq d+1\), and exact affine minorants satisfying (9).  Among all balanced negative families choose one with the smallest support cardinality.  The accepted theorem already supplies one with at most \(d+1\) members, while a positive dependence among nonzero vectors requires at least two, so this minimum exists and remains in the displayed range.

Fix \(i\) and \(z\in E\).  Compactness of \(D_z\) supplies \(q_{i,z}\in D_z\) such that
\[
\langle w_i,q_{i,z}\rangle=h_z(w_i)\geq\ell_i(z).
\tag{37}
\]
Thus \(q_{i,z}\in(G_i)_z\).  The fiber section \((G_i)_z\) is a proper closed halfspace because \(w_i\neq0\).  Applying (36) at \(q_{i,z}\) gives
\[
f_i(z)=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap(G_i)_z)
\geq\gamma_dt_z,
\tag{38}
\]
which proves (12).  Summing over \(E\) gives (13).

Each \(A_i\) is compact and convex.  It is full-dimensional.  Indeed, fix \(z_0\in E\).  Equation (38) gives positive \(d\)-volume to \((A_i)_{z_0}\).  Since the boundary hyperplane of \((G_i)_{z_0}\) has zero \(d\)-volume, there is \(x_i\in C_{z_0}\) with
\[
\langle w_i,x_i\rangle>\ell_i(z_0).
\tag{39}
\]
Choose \(q=(z_*,x_*)\in\operatorname{int}C\).  For \(0<s<1\), the point
\[
(1-s)(z_0,x_i)+sq
\tag{40}
\]
lies in \(\operatorname{int}C\).  Its cap slack is affine in \(s\) and is positive at \(s=0\), so it remains positive for all sufficiently small \(s>0\).  Hence \(\operatorname{int}C\cap\operatorname{int}G_i\neq\varnothing\), and \(A_i=C\cap G_i\) has nonempty ambient interior.

We prove the reweighting assertions.  The set in (14) is a closed convex subset of the compact product \(A_1\times\cdots\times A_r\), cut out by equality of the base coordinates.  Its image (15) is therefore compact and convex, and direct inspection gives the exact fiber formula (16).  Every point of every \((A_i)_z\) belongs to the convex set \(C_z\), so their \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\)-weighted average also belongs to \(C_z\).  Thus \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\subseteq C\).

For \(z\in E\), all summands in (16) are nonempty \(d\)-dimensional compact convex sets.  Brunn-Minkowski and (38) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
(b_z^{\boldsymbol\vartheta})^{1/d}
&\geq
\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i f_i(z)^{1/d}\\
&\geq
\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i(\gamma_dt_z)^{1/d}
=(\gamma_dt_z)^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
This proves (17), and summing proves (18).

The body \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) is full-dimensional.  Use the same \(z_0\in E\) as above and, for each \(i\), choose a strict cap point \((z_0,x_i)\) as in (39).  Perturb all of them toward the same point \(q\in\operatorname{int}C\).  For one sufficiently small common \(s>0\), the resulting points
\[
p_i=(z_s,y_i):=(1-s)(z_0,x_i)+sq
\tag{42}
\]
have the same base \(z_s\) and lie in \(\operatorname{int}A_i\) for every \(i\).  Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) such that the ambient \(\varepsilon\)-ball about each \(p_i\) is contained in \(A_i\).  Then, for every \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) with \(\|(u,v)\|<\varepsilon\), all points \((z_s+u,y_i+v)\) belong to \(A_i\), and their weighted image is
\[
\left(z_s+u,\sum_i\vartheta_iy_i+v\right).
\]
Thus \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) contains an ambient ball and is full-dimensional.

Positive homogeneity gives
\[
h_z(cw)=c h_z(w),
\qquad
\phi_t(cw)=c\phi_t(w)
\quad(c>0).
\tag{43}
\]
For (19), equations (8), (9), and (43) imply
\[
\sum_i\vartheta_iw_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}
=\sum_i\theta_iw_i=0,
\tag{44}
\]
\[
\sum_i\vartheta_i\phi_t(w_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
=\sum_i\theta_i\phi_t(w_i)<0,
\tag{45}
\]
and
\[
\ell_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}(z)
\leq h_z(w_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}),
\qquad
\ell_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}(m)
=\phi_t(w_i^{\boldsymbol\vartheta}).
\tag{46}
\]
Multiplication by the positive scalar \(\theta_i/\vartheta_i\) leaves the halfspace \(G_i\) unchanged.  The rescaled family has the same cardinality as a globally support-minimal family, so it is support-minimal as well.  This proves the assertion following (19).

We prove the contact statement.  For fixed \(i\), the finite linear program defining \(\phi_t(w_i)\) has an optimal vertex \(\lambda^i\) of the compact polytope \(\Lambda_m(E)\).  Let
\[
T_i:=\{z\in E:\lambda_z^i>0\}.
\tag{47}
\]
The set \(T_i\) is nonempty and affinely independent.  Otherwise there would be a nonzero vector \(\delta\) supported on \(T_i\) satisfying
\[
\sum_z\delta_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_z\delta_zz=0,
\]
and sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations of \(\lambda^i\) along \(\delta\) would express it as the midpoint of two distinct points of \(\Lambda_m(E)\), contradicting vertexhood.  Hence \(|T_i|\leq\rho+1\).

Affineness of \(\ell_i\), the barycenter constraint, optimality of \(\lambda^i\), and (9) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=\phi_t(w_i)-\ell_i(m)\\
&=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z^i
\bigl(h_z(w_i)-\ell_i(z)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Every summand is nonnegative.  Thus each summand on \(T_i\) vanishes, proving (21).

Fix \(z\in T_i\), and abbreviate \(h=h_z(w_i)=\ell_i(z)\).  The cap fiber is
\[
(A_i)_z=C_z\cap\{x:\langle w_i,x\rangle\geq h\}.
\tag{49}
\]
Its lower volume bound is (38).  Suppose its volume were \(s>t_z\).  The boundary hyperplane is \(d\)-null, so the strict superlevel set
\[
U:=\{x\in C_z:\langle w_i,x\rangle>h\}
\]
also has volume \(s>t_z\).  The density
\[
f:=\frac{t_z}{s}\mathbf1_U
\tag{50}
\]
is admissible in the definition of \(D_z=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\), but its normalized barycenter \(y\) satisfies
\[
\langle w_i,y\rangle
=\frac1s\int_U\langle w_i,x\rangle\,dx
>h=h_{D_z}(w_i),
\tag{51}
\]
a contradiction.  This proves the upper bound in (22).  Since \(A_i\subseteq C\), every fiberwise mass loss is nonnegative.  Summing the losses on \(T_i\) proves (23).

We now specialize to a free matching.  Freeness gives the first assertion in (27), while double-counting edge endpoints gives
\[
\sum_{z\in E_p}t_z
=2\sum_zd_z(p)
=4\sum_ep_e
=4P,
\tag{52}
\]
which proves the second.

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the nonzero finite lattice measure
\[
\nu:=\sum_{z\in E_p}t_z\delta_z.
\tag{53}
\]
It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfying (5).  This \(m\) belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}E_p\): otherwise strict separation from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}E_p\) would give a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\) and no point of \(E_p\), contradicting (5).  The arbitrary-selected-mass alternative therefore applies at this \(m\).  Its nonempty-core branch and (27) give (28).

In the empty-core branch, equations (12), (17), and \(t_z=2d_z(p)\) give (29) and (30).  For an individual cap,
\[
d_z(p^+)=\gamma_dd_z(p)
\leq\frac12\operatorname{vol}_d((A_i)_z),
\tag{54}
\]
and the identical inequality with \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) in place of \(A_i\) follows from (30).  At lattice fibers outside \(E_p\), the matching degree is zero.  Thus \(p^+\) is a legal free same-parity matching in every asserted body.  Its mass is \(\gamma_dP\).  Since
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e,
\]
one has \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\), proving (32).  Equations (13), (18), and \(T=4P\) prove (33).  Equations (22), (23), and (26) give (34) and (35).

The final status paragraph is a statement about the scope of the construction.  The individual cap \(A_i\) is not a positive same-base reweighting because the formal weight vector \(e_i\) lies on the boundary of the probability simplex.  The bodies \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) use strictly positive weights and are valid for every selected profile (1).  Accepted facts `51ff186e1aaeaa85` and `e3679dc542b23efe` designate the corresponding construction as a legal balanced-cap descendant under their half-mass hypotheses.  Nothing in the arbitrary-selected-mass proof supplies those additional hypotheses in general, so no theorem specific to that narrower class is invoked beyond its stated domain.  This also explains why no stopping or localization conclusion follows from the one-step recycling result.
