## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the canonical hypotheses and notation of accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 7ec63df101c30343. In particular, let \(Q>0\) be the half-strength canonical product-reservoir mass, let \(R\) be its realizable-submatching capacity, put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
and use the canonical nonnegative quantities \(G\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\). Put
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
\]
and assume the hard inequality
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(I\) and \(O\) be the canonical within-label and labelled-output defects of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, and assume
\[
 I\ge O.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(\mathcal E_I\) and \(t_e\) be the actual \(I\)-bad opposite-edge family and actual opposite-edge triangle masses of accepted fact 7ec63df101c30343. For \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 T_c:=\sum_\xi
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
 \qquad
 E:=\{c\in\mathbb Z^n:T_c>0\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_I:=\sum_{c\in E}T_c.
\tag{3}
\]
At every \(c\in E\), choose a retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing its contribution
\[
 \Theta_{\xi,c}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
\]
and put \(q_c:=q_{\xi(c),c}^{\circ}\). Define
\[
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\qquad
 r_E:=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-a(c):c\in E\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then no configuration satisfying (1)--(4) can have
\[
 \rho_E=2,
 \qquad r_E\le2.
\tag{5}
\]
More precisely, (1)--(5) would imply
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge
 \frac{88}{33075}
 \left(1+\frac{32\gamma_d}{3}\right)Q
 >\frac{3608}{297675}Q
 >\frac Q{100},}
\tag{6}
\]
which is impossible.

## Proof

Under (1), the hard alternative in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives
\[
 \max\{I,O\}>\frac Q4.
\]
Assumption (2) therefore gives \(I>Q/4\), so the actual \(I\)-triangle construction of accepted fact 7ec63df101c30343 applies.

Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 proves the exact defect inequality
\[
 I+O\ge
 \frac{11}{16}Q-\frac{67}{4}R-\frac{51}{128}G.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(I\ge O\), equation (7) gives
\[
 I\ge\frac{I+O}{2}
 \ge
 \frac{11}{32}Q-\frac{67}{8}R-\frac{51}{256}G.
\tag{8}
\]
The exceptional-edge estimate in the proof of accepted fact 7ec63df101c30343, equation (40) there, is the stronger pre-simplification
\[
 \Theta_I\ge\frac18(I-G).
\tag{9}
\]
Substituting (8) into (9) yields
\[
 \Theta_I+\frac{67}{64}R+\frac{307}{2048}G
 \ge\frac{11}{256}Q.
\tag{10}
\]
All constants in (10) retain the non-strict directions from (7)--(9); no use has been made of the weaker simplified estimate \(\Theta_I>3Q/100\).

Under the target-rank hypothesis in (5), accepted fact 07889c9b4686d8b4, applied to the complete positive support \(E\), gives
\[
 \Theta_I\le16G.
\tag{11}
\]
Under both rank hypotheses in (5), accepted fact e2263a787ace07a4, again applied to the complete support \(E\), gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \ge
 \frac{4\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\Theta_I
 \ge\frac{\gamma_d}{3}\Theta_I.
\tag{12}
\]
The same accepted fact gives \(D\ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\). Since these are distinct nonnegative summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\),
\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+D
 \ge R+G+k\Theta_I,
 \qquad
 k:=\frac{2\gamma_d}{3}.
\tag{13}
\]

It remains to combine (10)--(11) without assuming that the residual support has a bounded cell cover. Put
\[
 B:=\frac{67}{64},
 \qquad C_0:=\frac{307}{2048},
 \qquad A_0:=\frac{11}{256}Q,
\]
and define
\[
 \lambda:=\frac{1+16k}{16+C_0},
 \qquad
 \mu:=\lambda-k=\frac{1-kC_0}{16+C_0}.
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(0<\gamma_d<1\), one has \(0<k<2/3\). Hence \(\mu>0\), since \(C_0<1\). Moreover,
\[
 \lambda B<1.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, \(k<2/3\) gives \(1+16k<35/3\), while the exact comparison
\[
 (16+C_0)-\frac{35}{3}B
 =\frac{24185}{6144}>0
\]
proves (15).

Direct expansion of (14) gives the identity
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R+G+k\Theta_I
 &={}
 \lambda(\Theta_I+BR+C_0G)
 +\mu(16G-\Theta_I)
 +(1-\lambda B)R.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) and the nonnegativity established above therefore imply
\[
 R+G+k\Theta_I
 \ge\lambda A_0
 =\frac{88}{33075}
 \left(1+\frac{32\gamma_d}{3}\right)Q,
\tag{17}
\]
because \(16+C_0=33075/2048\).

Finally,
\[
 \gamma_d=\frac1{(1+1/d)^d}>\frac13.
\tag{18}
\]
For completeness, the binomial theorem gives
\[
 (1+1/d)^d
 =\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj d^{-j}
 \le\sum_{j=0}^d\frac1{j!}<3;
\]
the last strict inequality follows from \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\), with strictness in the finite sum or in one term. Combining (13), (17), and (18) gives
\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 >\frac{88}{33075}\left(1+\frac{32}{9}\right)Q
 =\frac{3608}{297675}Q.
\]
The exact integer comparison
\[
 100\cdot3608-297675=63125>0
\]
proves the last inequality in (6), contradicting (1). Thus (5) is impossible in every \(d\).

This proof uses only one selected parity label in each physical target fiber through accepted fact e2263a787ace07a4. It neither pools target-fiber densities across labels nor assumes that the residual support is coverable by finitely many translates of \(D_*\cap V\) with a uniform bound on their number.
