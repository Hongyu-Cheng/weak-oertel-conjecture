## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $n=d+1$, and let $T$ be a $d$-simplex with vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_d$, centroid $g$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$. Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$ and put $P=\{0,\pm e_0,\ldots,\pm e_d\}\subset\mathbb Z^n$. The canonical hull of $K_0=T$, $K_{\pm e_i}=\varepsilon v_i+(1-\varepsilon)T$ has exactly the specified integer fibers. Their centroid graph is nonaffine, while joint axis and vertex permutations and independent base sign changes preserve the hull and fix $(0,g)$.

For a convex representation of base $0$ by $P$, call the $i$th offset tight when the corresponding weighted sum of $\min_{K_z}\lambda_i$ equals $\min_T\lambda_i=0$. For every vertex type $j$ there is a nontrivial representation at $0$ making all offsets $i\ne j$ tight. No nontrivial representation makes all $d+1$ offsets tight. Here nontrivial means positive weight on at least one base other than $0$.

## Proof

The base projection is the unit $\ell_1$ ball, with exactly the indicated integer points. Every nonzero indicated base is an extreme point, so its actual hull fiber is the specified body. All specified bodies lie in $T$, and the central body equals $T$, which identifies the actual fiber at $0$. The two centroid values at $\pm e_i$ coincide at $\varepsilon v_i+(1-\varepsilon)g$ and differ from $g$. An affine graph would make their midpoint equal to $g$, giving a contradiction. A permutation acts linearly on the base axes and by the affine simplex automorphism that makes the same permutation of vertices. These maps preserve all generating fibers as a family and fix $(0,g)$. Base sign changes alone exchange equal fibers and fix the same point.

For $x\in T$, $\lambda_i(\varepsilon v_j+(1-\varepsilon)x)=\varepsilon\mathbf1_{i=j}+(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_i(x)$. The minimum over $K_{\pm e_j}$ is consequently $\varepsilon\mathbf1_{i=j}$. The midpoint representation using $\pm e_j$ makes every offset incident to vertex $v_j$, namely $i\ne j$, tight. For an arbitrary representation at $0$, the sum over all $i$ of the weighted offset minima is $\varepsilon$ times the total weight on the endpoint bases. This is strictly positive for a nontrivial representation, proving the obstruction.
