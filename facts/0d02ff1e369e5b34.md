## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be a compact convex body with positive mixed length $M$. Write $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$, $\mu=\mathcal H^1|_S$, and let $K_z$ be its integer interval fibers. Fix any actual $y=(j,c)\in S$, including an endpoint or a point fiber. Define
$$
F(a)=\mu\{(z,x):x-c-a(z-j)\ge0\},\qquad R(y)=\mu(S\cap(2y-S)).
$$
For $k\ge1$ set $I_k^+=(K_{j+k}-c)/k$ and $I_k^-=(c-K_{j-k})/k$, with a missing fiber giving the empty interval. Then almost everywhere in $a$,
$$
F'(a)=\sum_{k\ge1}k\bigl(\mathbf1_{I_k^-}(a)-\mathbf1_{I_k^+}(a)\bigr),
$$
and
$$
M-R(y)=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb R}(F)+|F(+\infty)+F(-\infty)-M|.
$$
Here $F$ is continuous and piecewise affine with constant tails, and $\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb R}(F)=\int_{\mathbb R}|F'(a)|\,da$. If the anchor lies on a positive-length fiber, $N$ is the number of positive-length fibers, $A_C(y)$ is the raw closed-halfspace depth, and $\delta=M/2-A_C(y)$, then $M-R(y)\le4(N-1)\delta$ for $N\ge2$, and $M-R(y)=2\delta$ for $N=1$.

## Proof

Write $m_+=\sum_{k\ge1}|K_{j+k}|$ and $m_-=\sum_{k\ge1}|K_{j-k}|$. Let $w_0=|K_j|$ and $U_0=|K_j\cap[c,\infty)|$. Length at a missing or point fiber is zero. By the clipped affine profile in dcb05945476b6c35, $F$ is continuous and piecewise affine. The positive tail is $m_-+U_0$ and the negative tail is $m_++U_0$, by saturation of every noncentral interval. Each opposite finite-tilt closed cap has mass $M-F(a)$ since the line is $\mu$-null. The two pure-base through-$y$ cap masses are $m_-+w_0$ and $m_++w_0$, at least the corresponding two tails of $F$. Moving any containing cap parallel to $y$ therefore gives
$$
A_C(y)=\min\{\inf_aF(a),\,M-\sup_aF(a)\}
      =M/2-\sup_a|F(a)-M/2|.
$$

Convexity and the actual point $(j,c)$ give $c+(k/\ell)(K_{j\pm\ell}-c)\subset K_{j\pm k}$ for $1\le k<\ell$. Thus each of the two slope interval families is nested, even when some fibers are points or absent. Away from interval endpoints the indices active in either family form a prefix. On these same slopes the derivative contribution from the positive base $j+k$ is $-k\mathbf1_{I_k^+}$, and that from the negative base $j-k$ is $k\mathbf1_{I_k^-}$ by differentiating the clipped affine length. The two prefixes are comparable, so the nonzero indicator differences all have the same sign. It follows that
$$
|F'(a)|=\sum_{k\ge1}k|\mathbf1_{I_k^-}(a)-\mathbf1_{I_k^+}(a)|
$$
almost everywhere. At the pair of bases $j\pm k$ the loss of retained length is
$$
|K_{j+k}|+|K_{j-k}|-2|K_{j+k}\cap(2c-K_{j-k})|
=k\,|I_k^+\mathbin{\triangle}I_k^-|.
$$
Integrating the derivative formula thus gives the full loss from the noncentral fibers. At the central fiber the retained interval has length $2\min(U_0,w_0-U_0)$, so its loss is $|2U_0-w_0|=|F(+\infty)+F(-\infty)-M|$. This proves the identity.

For the bound let the anchor fiber have positive length. Every value of $F$ lies in $[M/2-\delta,M/2+\delta]$. If $N\ge2$, the $N-1$ noncentral positive intervals contribute at most $2(N-1)$ breakpoints, and the at most $2(N-1)-1$ intervening affine segments each change by at most $2\delta$. The own term has absolute value at most $2\delta$, giving $4(N-1)\delta$. If $N=1$, the profile is constant at $U_0$ and the variation is zero. Then $|2U_0-M|=2\delta$.
