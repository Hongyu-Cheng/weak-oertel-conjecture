## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0,
\]
\[
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed measure
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}.
\]
For \(y\in S\), define its mixed halfspace depth by
\[
h_S(y):=\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]
Set
\[
a:=2^{-n}W,
\]
and suppose that \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\geq a
\tag{1}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\).

For \(0<t\leq W\), define the global mixed metronoid
\[
\mathsf M_t^{\mathrm{mix}}(C):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int p\,d\nu(p):
0\leq\nu\leq\mu_C,\ |\nu|=t
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Define the fixed-base selected-state class and its continuous barycenter slice by
\[
\mathcal D_m(a):=
\left\{
\nu:0\leq\nu\leq\mu_C,\ |\nu|=a,\
\int z\,d\nu=a m
\right\},
\tag{3}
\]
\[
Y_m:=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(m,x)\in
\mathsf M_a^{\mathrm{mix}}(C)\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Finally, put
\[
\mathcal A_m(a):=
\left\{
\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z=1,\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z z=m,\quad
a\alpha_z\leq a_z\ (z\in E)
\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Then \(\mathcal D_m(a)\), \(Y_m\), and \(\mathcal A_m(a)\) are nonempty, \(Y_m\) is compact and convex, and the following exact slice identity holds:
\[
Y_m=
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_m(a)}
\sum_{\substack{z\in E\\\alpha_z>0}}
\alpha_z\,\mathsf M_{a\alpha_z}(C_z).
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\mathsf M_s(C_z)\) is the one-fiber metronoid used in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`.

For every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_m(a)\), let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\) and define
\[
x_\alpha:=\sum_{z\in E}\alpha_zc_z,
\qquad y_\alpha:=(m,x_\alpha).
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
y_\alpha\in \mathsf M_a^{\mathrm{mix}}(C)\cap S
\tag{8}
\]
and its mixed halfspace depth satisfies
\[
h_S(y_\alpha)\geq
\gamma_d a
\left(\sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\right)^d.
\tag{9}
\]

There is also the following general target-mass augmentation statement.  Suppose that \(B\subseteq C\) is a compact convex set whose lattice-fiber volumes
\[
b_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z)
\]
satisfy
\[
b_z\geq\kappa a_z\qquad(z\in E)
\tag{10}
\]
for some \(0<\kappa<1\), and let \(\mu_B\) denote its mixed measure.  For every \(0<\beta\leq\kappa\), there exist positive measures \(\nu_B,\nu_R\) such that
\[
0\leq\nu_B\leq\mu_B,qquad
0\leq\nu_R\leq\mu_C-\nu_B,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
|\nu_B|=\beta a,qquad
|\nu_R|=(1-\beta)a,
\tag{12}
\]
and
\[
\int z\,d\nu_B=\beta a m,qquad
\int z\,d\nu_R=(1-\beta)a m.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently \(\nu:=\nu_B+\nu_R\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_m(a)\).  If
\[
\overline\nu_B=(m,x_B),\qquad
\overline\nu_R=(m,x_R),
\]
where bars denote normalized barycenters, then
\[
(m,x_B)\in B,\qquad (m,x_R)\in C,
\tag{14}
\]
and the barycenter \(y=\overline\nu\) satisfies
\[
y=\beta(m,x_B)+(1-\beta)(m,x_R)
\in\mathsf M_a^{\mathrm{mix}}(C)\cap S.
\tag{15}
\]

In the empty half-mass-core branch, accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85` supplies a compact full-dimensional convex body \(B\subseteq C\) satisfying (10) with
\[
\kappa=\frac{\gamma_d}{2}>\frac1{2e}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus (11)--(15) hold there with the dimension-free choice
\[
\beta=\frac1{2e}.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, an exact target-mass selected state can be chosen so that at least the fraction \(1/(2e)\) of its selected mass is supplied by one common convex descendant \(B\), and both the descendant part and the residual part have base barycenter exactly \(m\).

For completeness, let
\[
v_1,\ldots,v_r\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\},\qquad
\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r>0,
\]
and affine functions \(\ell_i:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) be the support-minimal cap circuit supplied in the same empty-core branch by accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85`.  Thus
\[
2\leq r\leq d+1,\qquad
\sum_i\theta_i=1,\qquad
\sum_i\theta_iv_i=0,
\tag{18}
\]
\[
\ker[v_1\ \cdots\ v_r]
=\operatorname{span}\{(\theta_i)_i\},
\qquad
\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(m)<0.
\tag{19}
\]
Define
\[
s_i(z,x):=\langle v_i,x\rangle-\ell_i(z)
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
\Delta:=
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)}
\min_{1\leq i\leq r}
\frac1a\int s_i\,d\nu.
\tag{21}
\]
Then the exact minimax identities are
\[
\Delta=
\min_{\rho\in\Delta_r}
\max_{\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)}
\frac1a\int\sum_i\rho_i s_i\,d\nu
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\Delta=
\min_{\rho\in\Delta_r}
\left[
h_{Y_m}\left(\sum_i\rho_iv_i\right)
-\sum_i\rho_i\ell_i(m)
\right],
\tag{23}
\]
where \(\Delta_r:=\{\rho\in\mathbb R_+^r:\sum_i\rho_i=1\}\) and
\(h_{Y_m}(u):=\max_{x\in Y_m}\langle u,x\rangle\).  Moreover, every \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\) satisfies
\[
\frac1a\int\sum_i\theta_i s_i\,d\nu
=-\sum_i\theta_i\ell_i(m)>0.
\tag{24}
\]

If \(\Delta\geq0\), some \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\), with barycenter \((m,x)\), satisfies
\[
\langle v_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(m)
\qquad(1\leq i\leq r).
\tag{25}
\]
If \(\Delta<0\), there is a minimizer \(\rho^*\in\Delta_r\) such that, with
\[
w:=\sum_i\rho_i^*v_i\neq0,
\qquad
\ell_{\rho^*}:=\sum_i\rho_i^*\ell_i,
\tag{26}
\]
one has
\[
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)}
\frac1a\int
\bigl(\langle w,x\rangle-\ell_{\rho^*}(z)\bigr)\,d\nu
=\Delta<0.
\tag{27}
\]
In this second alternative, accepted fact `a8cfba4caf85ff82` supplies an exact halfspace cap \(A\subseteq C\) of mixed mass \(a\), with mixed centroid \(y_A=(m,x_A)\in A\cap S\), such that
\[
x_A\in\operatorname*{argmax}_{x\in Y_m}\langle w,x\rangle
\tag{28}
\]
and
\[
\langle w,x_A\rangle-\ell_{\rho^*}(m)=\Delta<0.
\]
Thus the centroid of this cap realizes the negative score in (27).  No assertion that \(y_A\), the point in (15), or either component barycenter in (14) has mixed halfspace depth proportional to \(a\) is part of the theorem.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes \(E\) finite.  Dividing (1) by \(W\), accepted fact `725defc391f9d55d` applies with \(\tau=2^{-n}\) and supplies a measure in \(\mathcal D_m(a)\).  Hence \(\mathcal D_m(a)\), \(Y_m\), and \(\mathcal A_m(a)\) are nonempty; for the last class, take the selected mass in each fiber divided by \(a\).

We prove (6).  Given \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\), put
\[
s_z:=\nu(\{z\}\times C_z),\qquad
\alpha_z:=\frac{s_z}{a}.
\]
Then \(0\leq s_z\leq a_z\), \(\sum_zs_z=a\), and \(\sum_zs_zz=am\), so \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_m(a)\).  If \(s_z>0\), the normalized continuous barycenter \(q_z\) of the restriction of \(\nu\) to the fiber \(z\) belongs to \(\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)\), and the continuous coordinate of \(\overline\nu\) is
\[
\frac1a\int x\,d\nu=\sum_{s_z>0}\alpha_zq_z.
\]
This proves one inclusion in (6).  Conversely, if \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_m(a)\) and \(q_z\in\mathsf M_{a\alpha_z}(C_z)\) for every positive coefficient, choose a representing density in each such fiber and combine them.  The resulting measure is dominated by \(\mu_C\), has mass \(a\), base first moment \(am\), and continuous barycenter \(\sum_z\alpha_zq_z\).  This proves the reverse inclusion.

For compactness, identify a selected measure with its Radon--Nikodym density \(g\in L^\infty(\mu_C)\).  The order interval \(0\leq g\leq1\) is weak-star compact, the mass and base-moment constraints are weak-star closed, and all coordinate functions are in \(L^1(\mu_C)\) because \(C\) is compact.  Thus \(\mathcal D_m(a)\) is weak-star compact and its continuous-barycenter image \(Y_m\) is compact.  Convexity of the order interval and of the constraints proves convexity of both \(\mathcal D_m(a)\) and \(Y_m\).

Fix \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_m(a)\).  In fiber \(z\), the constant density \(a\alpha_z/a_z\) has mass \(a\alpha_z\) and barycenter \(c_z\).  Combining these densities proves (8).  For every \(z\) with \(\alpha_z>0\),
\[
c_z\in\mathsf M_{a\alpha_z}(C_z).
\tag{29}
\]
The proof of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` establishes convexity already for the positive-root triples before taking their closure.  Apply that positive-root convexity to
\[
\left(z,c_z,(a\alpha_z)^{1/d}\right)
\]
with convex coefficients \(\alpha_z\).  Since \(\sum_z\alpha_zz=m\), it gives
\[
x_\alpha\in\mathsf M_t(C_m),
\qquad
t=a\left(\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\right)^d.
\tag{30}
\]
Because \(\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\leq\sum_z\alpha_z=1\), one has \(t\leq a\).

Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace containing \(y_\alpha\).  The whole-space convention, if allowed, gives \(\mu_C(H)=W\geq a\).  Otherwise, if its continuous normal is zero, its base projection is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\), and (1) gives \(\mu_C(H)\geq a\).  If its continuous normal is nonzero, its section in the anchor fiber \(m\) is a closed fiber halfspace containing \(x_\alpha\).  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied to (30), gives
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_m\cap H_m)
\geq\gamma_dt.
\]
Since \(\gamma_dt\leq a\), the two cases prove (9).

We next prove the augmentation statement.  We use the following finite-capacity selection observation.  Let \(q_z\geq0\) have finite support, let \(t>0\), and suppose
\[
\sum_{z:\,u^\top(z-m)\geq0}q_z\geq t
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).
\tag{31}
\]
Then there are \(0\leq s_z\leq q_z\) with
\[
\sum_zs_z=t,\qquad \sum_zs_zz=tm.
\tag{32}
\]
Indeed, the capacity in (31) permits selecting exactly mass \(t\) from the indicated side for each \(u\).  Therefore the compact metronoid
\[
K_t:=\left\{
\frac1t\sum_zs_zz:
0\leq s_z\leq q_z,\ \sum_zs_z=t
\right\}
\]
has support function at least \(u^\top m\) in every direction \(u\).  If \(m\notin K_t\), strict separation of a point from a compact convex set would give the opposite strict inequality in some direction.  Hence \(m\in K_t\), proving (32).

From (1) and (10), every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in J}b_z\geq\kappa\sum_{z\in J}a_z
\geq\kappa a\geq\beta a.
\tag{33}
\]
Apply (31)--(32) to \(q_z=b_z\) and \(t=\beta a\).  It gives numbers
\[
0\leq s_z^B\leq b_z,\qquad
\sum_zs_z^B=\beta a,
\qquad
\sum_zs_z^Bz=\beta am.
\tag{34}
\]
In each fiber with \(b_z>0\), take the constant density \(s_z^B/b_z\) on \(B_z\); zero fibers carry no selected mass.  This defines \(\nu_B\) with the first parts of (11)--(13).

Let
\[
r_z:=a_z-s_z^B.
\]
This is the total mass in fiber \(z\) of the residual measure \(\mu_C-\nu_B\).  For every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\),
\[
\sum_{z\in J}r_z
=\sum_{z\in J}a_z-\sum_{z\in J}s_z^B
\geq a-\sum_zs_z^B
=(1-\beta)a.
\tag{35}
\]
Apply (31)--(32) again, now to \(q_z=r_z\) and \(t=(1-\beta)a>0\).  It gives
\[
0\leq s_z^R\leq r_z,\qquad
\sum_zs_z^R=(1-\beta)a,
\qquad
\sum_zs_z^Rz=(1-\beta)am.
\tag{36}
\]
In each positive residual fiber, multiply the residual measure \((\mu_C-\nu_B)_z\) by the constant \(s_z^R/r_z\).  This defines \(\nu_R\) and proves all of (11)--(13).

The measure \(\nu_B\) is supported on the compact convex set \(B\), and \(\nu_R\) is supported on the compact convex set \(C\).  Their normalized barycenters therefore belong to \(B\) and \(C\), respectively.  Their base coordinates equal \(m\) by (13), proving (14).  Adding the two selected measures proves that \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\), and its barycenter decomposition is exactly (15).

Accepted fact `51ff186e1aaeaa85` gives \(B\subseteq C\) with
\[
b_z\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z
\qquad(z\in E),
\]
so the preceding result applies with \(\kappa=\gamma_d/2\).  Since
\[
\gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1},
\]
where the strict inequality follows from \(\log(1+t)<t\) for \(t>0\), one has (16), and the universal choice (17) is admissible.

It remains to prove the minimax assertions.  For \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\), put
\[
q_i(\nu):=\frac1a\int s_i\,d\nu,
\qquad
Q:=\{(q_i(\nu))_{i=1}^r:\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\}.
\]
The already proved compactness and convexity of \(\mathcal D_m(a)\), together with continuity and affinity of the score map, show that \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^r\) is compact and convex.  For any \(q\in\mathbb R^r\),
\[
\min_iq_i=\min_{\rho\in\Delta_r}\rho^\top q.
\tag{37}
\]
We prove the resulting minimax equality directly.  The inequality
\[
\max_{q\in Q}\min_iq_i
\leq\min_{\rho\in\Delta_r}\max_{q\in Q}\rho^\top q
\tag{38}
\]
follows because \(\min_iq_i\leq\rho^\top q\) for every \(q,\rho\).  Let
\[
\delta:=\max_{q\in Q}\min_iq_i
\]
and fix \(t>\delta\).  The compact convex set \(Q\) and the closed convex upper orthant \(t\mathbf1+\mathbb R_+^r\) are disjoint, so strong separation gives a nonzero vector \(\rho\) satisfying
\[
\sup_{q\in Q}\rho^\top q
<\inf_{u\in t\mathbf1+\mathbb R_+^r}\rho^\top u.
\tag{39}
\]
The infimum on the right can be finite only if \(\rho\geq0\).  Normalize \(\rho\) to have coordinate sum one.  Then the right side of (39) is \(t\), so
\[
\min_{\rho\in\Delta_r}\max_{q\in Q}\rho^\top q<t.
\]
Letting \(t\downarrow\delta\) proves the reverse inequality in (38), hence (22).

For \(\rho\in\Delta_r\), put \(w_\rho:=\sum_i\rho_iv_i\) and \(\ell_\rho:=\sum_i\rho_i\ell_i\).  Since each \(\ell_i\) is affine and every \(\nu\in\mathcal D_m(a)\) has base barycenter \(m\),
\[
\frac1a\int\sum_i\rho_i s_i\,d\nu
=\left\langle w_\rho,\frac1a\int x\,d\nu\right\rangle
-\ell_\rho(m).
\tag{40}
\]
Maximizing (40) over \(\mathcal D_m(a)\) proves (23).  Taking \(\rho=\theta\), using (18), (19), and the same affine-barycenter identity proves (24).

All maxima and minima in (21)--(23) are attained by compactness.  If \(\Delta\geq0\), choose a maximizing \(\nu\) in (21).  Every one of its scores is nonnegative, and (40) with a simplex vertex gives (25).  If \(\Delta<0\), choose a minimizing \(\rho^*\) in (22).  Equations (23) and (40) give (27).  If \(w=0\), then (19) implies \(\rho^*\in\operatorname{span}\{\theta\}\); since both are probability vectors, \(\rho^*=\theta\).  But (24) says that the value at \(\theta\) is positive, contradicting \(\Delta<0\).  Thus \(w\neq0\), proving (26).

Write \(\ell_{\rho^*}(z)=c+g^\top z\), and choose \(x^*\in Y_m\) maximizing \(\langle w,x\rangle\).  Put \(y^*:=(m,x^*)\) and use the linear functional
\[
F(z,x):=\langle w,x\rangle-g^\top z.
\]
Its continuous normal is \(w\neq0\).  Apply accepted fact `a8cfba4caf85ff82` to \(y^*\) and to the halfspace through \(y^*\) defined by \(-F(p)\leq-F(y^*)\).  It supplies an adjusted halfspace cap \(A\subseteq C\) of mixed mass exactly \(a\), base centroid \(m\), and mixed centroid \(y_A=(m,x_A)\) in that halfspace.  Hence
\[
F(y_A)\geq F(y^*).
\]
Because \(x_A\in Y_m\) and \(x^*\) maximizes \(\langle w,x\rangle\) on \(Y_m\), the reverse inequality also holds.  Thus equality holds, (28) follows, and (23), (26), and (27) show that the cap centroid realizes score \(\Delta<0\).  The purification theorem supplies no depth estimate, so this final statement does not go beyond the asserted minimax obstruction.
