## Statement

Let \(r,s,h_x,h_y\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(n=r+s\), and let \(a\ge0\) and \(B\in\mathbb R^{s\times r}\). Retain the geometric notation of accepted fact `cd6a1446e7ce3997`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,h_x]^r\cup\{-\mathbf1_r\}\bigr),
&Q&=[-h_y,h_y]^s,\\
P&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((R\times\{\mathbf0\})\cup(\{\mathbf0\}\times Q)\bigr),
&D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Write \(\mathbf I=(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb Z^s\). Let \(V:R\to\mathbb R\) be finite and convex, with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\), and define the original potential and complete table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(x,y)&=V(x)+a\|y-Bx\|_\infty\quad((x,y)\in P),
&\phi_{\mathbf I}&=\phi(x,y),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
&w_{\mathbf I}&=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),
&W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Assume the original weighted mean is \(\mathbf J_0=W^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). The support, indices, and weights in this hypothesis are exactly (1)-(2). In particular, \(B\) may have arbitrary irrational entries.

For \(L\ge0\), use the original full-capacity logarithmic hitting quantity of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, a barycentric support at \(\mathbf0\) consists of a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) and positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) satisfying \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). Its gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_E\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}\). Define \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)\) to be the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over subsets \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every such support with \(G_\lambda\le L\). Each selected source is charged its full original weight. The accepted fact identifies this definition with its finite family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) of affinely independent supports, equipped with their unique positive barycentric coefficients. All cutoff inequalities include equality.

The objects \(n,P,D,w,W\) have their canonical meanings in `488f0a583886b5fc`. Our normalization is \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), so that fact's normalized potential is \(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\min_D\phi\), and its \(w_{\max}\) is \(e^{-\min_D\phi}\). These substitutions preserve every weight and every gap. Its scalar amplitude called \(B\) would be \(\max_D\phi-\min_D\phi\); it is distinct from the coupling matrix in (2). The geometric objects and the first-block meaning of \(V\) agree with `cd6a1446e7ce3997`, while the table (2) uses the present real matrix.

The exact complete support, including boundary sites, is

\[

\frac{\|x\|_\infty}{h_x}+\frac{\|y\|_\infty}{h_y}\le1
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The table is globally extendable in the sense of `488f0a583886b5fc`, \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) has dimension \(n\), \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Put \(\gamma=2/\sqrt3\) and \(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}w_{(\mathbf0,y)}\). Then

&\ge\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}

For an explicit stationary irrational family, take \(r=2\), \(s=1\), \(B=(\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)\), any integers \(h_x,h_y\ge2\), and any \(a>0\). ## Proof

The support geometry and central-pair calculations from `cd6a1446e7ce3997` concern the unchanged objects (1) and the restriction \(\phi(\mathbf0,y)=a\|y\|_\infty\). We verify those subclaims for the present real matrix below. The mass comparison uses the real-shift statement of `f55232eea7862d8c`, without applying the integer-matrix mass theorem of `cd6a1446e7ce3997` to new hypotheses.

Every point of \(R\) can be written as \(t v-(1-t)\mathbf1_r\), with \(0\le t\le1\) and \(v\in[0,h_x]^r\). Its coordinates are at least \(t-1\ge-1\) and at most \((h_x+1)t-1\le h_x\). A negative integer coordinate must equal \(-1\), and the lower bound then forces \(t=0\). Thus the integer points of \(R\) are exactly \(-\mathbf1_r\) and \(\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r\).

Every point of \(P\) has the form \((x,y)=(\lambda v,(1-\lambda)v')\), where \(v\in R\), \(v'\in Q\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Its first projection belongs to \(R\), since \(\mathbf0\in R\). If an integer first projection is \(-\mathbf1_r\), then \(x_i\ge-\lambda\) forces \(\lambda=1\) and \(y=\mathbf0\). Otherwise its first projection is nonnegative, and the bounds \(x_i\le\lambda h_x\) and \(\|y\|_\infty\le(1-\lambda)h_y\) imply the displayed inequality in (3). Conversely, for such a nonnegative \(x\), set \(\lambda=\|x\|_\infty/h_x\). For \(0<\lambda<1\), the points \(x/\lambda\in[0,h_x]^r\) and \(y/(1-\lambda)\in Q\) provide the required representation in \(P\). For \(\lambda=0\), \(x=\mathbf0\) and \(y\in Q\); for \(\lambda=1\), \(y=\mathbf0\) and \(x\in[0,h_x]^r\). This proves (3).

The finite integer vertices generating \(P\) all belong to \(D\), so \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). The origin is the strictly positive barycenter, with coefficients \(1/(r+1)\), of the affinely independent points \(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\) in \(R\). Therefore \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}R\). Since also \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}Q\) and \((R/2)\times(Q/2)\subseteq P\), the origin is interior to \(P\). This also proves \(\dim P=n\).

The function \(\phi\) is convex on \(P\): the first term is a convex function under projection, and the second is a nonnegative multiple of a norm composed with a real linear map. This argument imposes no arithmetic condition on \(B\). Define \(f=e^{-\phi}\) on \(P\) and \(f=0\) outside \(P\). It is finite-valued. Convexity of \(P\) and \(\phi\) proves log-concavity when both endpoints lie in \(P\); when either endpoint is outside \(P\), the right side of the log-concavity inequality is zero. By (3), \(f\) agrees with every lattice value in (2), including every zero. Thus the table has the required global extension. Convexity gives \(G_\lambda\ge0\) for every barycentric support, and \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) gives \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\).

Apply `f55232eea7862d8c` with its \(s=s\), \(h=h_y\), \(Q=Q\), and \(a=a\). Its shift \(v\) is any real vector here, and its central partition value \(Z_Q(a;0)\) is exactly \(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\). Its conclusion gives, for every \(v\in\mathbb R^s\),

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y-v\|_\infty}
\le2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\tag{7}
\]

This includes \(a=0\) and shifts outside the cube.

On \(\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\), retain the auxiliary first-block law \(\rho_x=2^{-(r+1)}2^{-(x_1+\cdots+x_r)}\) from `cd6a1446e7ce3997`. Its mass at the negative apex is \(1/2\), and its total mass on the nonnegative orthant is \(2^{-(r+1)}(\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j})^r=1/2\). Hence \(\sum_x\rho_x=1\). Define, solely for comparison on the original \(D\),

\[
q_{(x,y)}=
\frac{\rho_x e^{-a\|y-Bx\|_\infty}}
{2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}.
\tag{8}
\]

These \(q_{\mathbf I}\) are auxiliary numbers, distinct from the continuous dimension \(q\) in the later approximation construction. Every original first projection is in the domain of \(\rho\), and every original second projection belongs to \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\). Equation (7) applies with \(v=Bx\) for each such \(x\), including \(x=-\mathbf1_r\). Extending the sum only in this auxiliary comparison gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<\sum_{(x,y)\in D}q_{(x,y)}
&\le\sum_{x\in\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}
\rho_x
\frac{\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|y-Bx\|_\infty}}
{2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}\\
&\le\sum_x\rho_x=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Positivity follows, for example, from the original origin site. Thus \((q_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) is a positive subprobability on \(D\).

Let \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) be the original probability law. Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives \(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(q_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\le\log\sum_Dq_{\mathbf I}\le0\). Substitution of (2) and (8) cancels the entire coupled norm cost and gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\log\frac{p_{(x,y)}}{q_{(x,y)}}\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
 +(r+1)\log2+\log(2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}})
 +(\log2)\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
 +(r+1)\log2+\log(2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last equality uses precisely the first-block coordinates of the stipulated original mean. At the same original law, convexity of \(V\) gives \(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)\ge V(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x)=V(\mathbf0)=0\). Equation (10) proves \(W\le2^{r+1}\,2\gamma^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\). No comparison-law weights have entered either the mean assumption or the hitting capacities.

At \(x=\mathbf0\), the table is exactly \(w_{(\mathbf0,y)}=e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}\) because \(B\mathbf0=\mathbf0\). The singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) has gap zero. Each unordered pair \(\{(\mathbf0,y),(\mathbf0,-y)\}\) with \(y\ne\mathbf0\) is an affinely independent barycentric support with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), gap \(a\|y\|_\infty\), and equal endpoint weights. These unordered pairs are mutually disjoint and avoid the singleton. Every hitting set must therefore pay for the singleton and one full endpoint from every pair with gap at most \(L\). Hence

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
\ge1+\frac12
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s,\ y\ne\mathbf0\\a\|y\|_\infty\le L}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}.
\tag{11}
\]

We verify the central-tail estimate used in `cd6a1446e7ce3997` on this identical central cube. Define \(\eta(y)_j=\operatorname{sgn}(y_j)\lfloor|y_j|/2\rfloor\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\). Its image stays in \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), and \(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty\le\|y\|_\infty/2\). A zero coordinate of the image has at most the preimages \(-1,0,1\), and a nonzero coordinate has at most two preimages. Thus each image vector has at most \(3^s\) preimages, including after restriction to the finite cube, and

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}
\le\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-a\|\eta(y)\|_\infty}
\le3^s Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(a>0\), the strict tail beyond the weak cutoff \(4s\) consequently satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\\a\|y\|_\infty>4s}}
e^{-a\|y\|_\infty}
&\le e^{-2s}\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-(a/2)\|y\|_\infty}\\
&\le(3e^{-2})^sZ_{\mathrm{cube}}
<\frac12Z_{\mathrm{cube}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Indeed \(e^2>1+2+2+8/6>6\), so \(3e^{-2}<1/2\), and \(s\ge1\). For \(a=0\) the tail is empty. In all cases the eligible central-cube mass is at least \(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}/2\). The right side of (11) is half that eligible mass plus \(1/2\), which proves \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)\ge Z_{\mathrm{cube}}/4\).

With the proved total-mass bound and \(n=r+s\), this yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,4s;w)}{1+4s}
&\ge\frac{Z_{\mathrm{cube}}}{4(1+4s)}\\
&\ge\frac{W}{2^n}
\frac{2^{s-3}}{2\gamma^s(1+4s)}
\ge\frac{W}{48\,2^n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The last step is exactly the absorption inequality of `f55232eea7862d8c`, with the same integer \(s\) and the same \(\gamma\). Explicitly, its middle factor equals \(3^{s/2}/[16(1+4s)]\) and is at least \(1/48\) for every \(s\ge1\). The finite-cutoff attainment statement of `488f0a583886b5fc` applies to our globally extendable table and shows that the cutoff maximum exists; it is at least its value at \(4s\). This proves (4).

For the stationary irrational family, fix \(r=2\), \(s=1\), \(B=(\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)\), \(h_x,h_y\ge2\), and \(a>0\). Use the same finite-moment construction parameters \(u,c_-,\delta\) as in `cd6a1446e7ce3997`, now defined with the actual present costs:
