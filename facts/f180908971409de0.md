## Statement

Let \(n,d,q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), set
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\bigl|\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\bigr|_d,
\]
where \(|\cdot|_d\) denotes \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. For \(p\in S\), set
\[
h_S(p):=\inf\{\mu(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(x\in C_z\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
\delta_C(z,x):=\inf\{|C_z\cap K|_d:
K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in K\}.
\]

Take linearly independent \(u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(L_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and define
\[
B:=\left\{m+\sum_{i=1}^q k_i u_i:
-L_i\le k_i\le L_i, k_i\in\mathbb Z\right\},
\qquad
v_\varepsilon:=m+\sum_{i=1}^q\varepsilon_iL_i u_i
\]
for \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^q\). Suppose that \(s\ge0\) and that for every \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^q\) there is \(x_\varepsilon\in C_{v_\varepsilon}\) such that
\[
\delta_C(v_\varepsilon,x_\varepsilon)\ge s.
\]
Then
\[
p:=\left(m,2^{-q}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^q}x_\varepsilon\right)\in S
\]
and
\[
h_S(p)\ge s\prod_{i=1}^q(L_i+1)
\ge 2^{-q}s|B|.
\]

For calibration, let \(0<s<1/2\), let \(M>2-4s\), and in \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\) let
\[
C_M:=\operatorname{conv}\Bigl(
(\{(0,0)\}\times[0,1])\cup
(\{(2,0)\}\times[0,1])\cup
(\{(0,2)\}\times[0,1])\cup
(\{(2,2)\}\times[M,M+1])
\Bigr).
\]
There is no affine \(g:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) whose value at each of the four displayed corners belongs to that corner fiber's depth-\(s\) core. Nevertheless, applying the certificate to the symmetric box \(B=\{0,1,2\}^2\) gives a point \(p\in C_M\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)\) with
\[
h_{C_M\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)}(p)\ge4s,
\]
and therefore, in particular, mixed depth at least \(9s/4\). No minimality claim is made about this calibration.

## Proof

For
\[
T:=\prod_{i=1}^q[-L_i,L_i]
\]
and \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_q)\in T\), define
\[
\lambda_\varepsilon(t):=
\prod_{i=1}^q\frac{1+\varepsilon_i t_i/L_i}{2},
\qquad
x(t):=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^q}
\lambda_\varepsilon(t)x_\varepsilon,
\qquad
z(t):=m+\sum_{i=1}^q t_i u_i.
\]
Each weight is nonnegative on \(T\), and expansion of the product gives
\[
\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon(t)=1,
\qquad
\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon(t)\varepsilon_i=t_i/L_i
\quad(1\le i\le q).
\]
Consequently,
\[
(z(t),x(t))=
\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon(t)(v_\varepsilon,x_\varepsilon)\in C.
\tag{1}
\]
The map \(x(t)\) is multi-affine, meaning affine in each coordinate \(t_i\) with all other coordinates fixed.

Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` says that
\[
(z,x)\longmapsto\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}
\]
is jointly concave on \(C\). Applying finite-point concavity to (1) yields, for every real \(t\in T\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_C(z(t),x(t))^{1/d}
&\ge\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon(t)
\delta_C(v_\varepsilon,x_\varepsilon)^{1/d}\\
&\ge\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon(t)s^{1/d}
=s^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\delta_C(z(t),x(t))\ge s
\quad\text{for every }t\in T.
\tag{2}
\]
This propagation includes \(s=0\). At \(t=0\), all weights equal \(2^{-q}\), so (1) gives
\[
(z(0),x(0))=p\in C.
\]
Since \(z(0)=m\in\mathbb Z^n\), this proves \(p\in S\). If \(s=0\), nonnegativity of \(h_S\) already proves both asserted lower bounds. We continue with the counting argument, which also remains valid when \(s=0\).

By the boundary reduction in accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42`, it suffices to bound every closed ambient halfspace whose boundary contains \(p\). Let such a halfspace be
\[
H=\{(z,x):\ell(z,x)\ge0\},
\qquad
\ell(z,x):=a\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+b\mathbin{\cdot}(x-x(0)),
\tag{3}
\]
where \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\). Define
\[
F(t):=\ell(z(t),x(t)).
\]
Because \(z(t)\) is affine and \(x(t)\) is multi-affine, \(F\) is multi-affine, and \(F(0)=0\). Hence \(F\) has an expansion
\[
F(t)=\sum_{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}}c_I\prod_{i\in I}t_i,
\qquad c_\varnothing=F(0)=0.
\tag{4}
\]

For each absolute-coordinate vector
\[
r=(r_1,\ldots,r_q)\in\prod_{i=1}^q\{0,1,\ldots,L_i\},
\]
average (4) over all sign labels \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^q\). Every nonconstant monomial has zero sign average, so
\[
2^{-q}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^q}
F(\sigma_1r_1,\ldots,\sigma_qr_q)=F(0)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Formula (5) remains valid when some \(r_i=0\). In that case different sign labels can represent the same point, but the repeated labels merely give that point its correct multiplicity in the displayed average. Thus at least one distinct point \(t(r)\) in the sign orbit of \(r\) satisfies
\[
F(t(r))\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
This selects only one point from each orbit, so zero coordinates create no double-counting.

There are exactly
\[
\prod_{i=1}^q(L_i+1)
\tag{7}
\]
such sign orbits, because an integer vector \(k\in\prod_i[-L_i,L_i]\) has the unique absolute-coordinate vector \((|k_1|,\ldots,|k_q|)\). If \(r\ne r'\), then \(t(r)\ne t(r')\), since their absolute-coordinate vectors differ. Linear independence of \(u_1,\ldots,u_q\) now gives
\[
z(t(r))\ne z(t(r')).
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover, every \(t(r)\) has integer coordinates, and \(m,u_i\in\mathbb Z^n\), so all \(z(t(r))\) are distinct integer fibers.

We next show that each selected fiber contributes at least \(s\) to \(\mu(H)\). If \(b\ne0\), the section of (3) in the fiber \(z=z(t(r))\) is the closed halfspace
\[
H_{z(t(r))}:=
\{x\in\mathbb R^d:
b\mathbin{\cdot}(x-x(0))\ge-a\mathbin{\cdot}(z(t(r))-m)\}.
\]
By (6), this halfspace contains \(x(t(r))\), and therefore (2) and the definition of absolute fiber depth give
\[
|C_{z(t(r))}\cap H_{z(t(r))}|_d\ge s.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(b=0\), the ambient halfspace is vertical, in the sense that membership depends only on \(z\). For a selected fiber, (6) makes its section all of \(\mathbb R^d\), including the equality case. Also
\[
|C_{z(t(r))}|_d\ge\delta_C(z(t(r)),x(t(r)))\ge s,
\tag{10}
\]
so (9) holds in this case as well. This explicitly covers ambient halfspaces with zero continuous normal.

Summing (9) over the distinct fibers in (8) and using (7) gives
\[
\mu(H)\ge s\prod_{i=1}^q(L_i+1).
\]
For an arbitrary closed halfspace containing \(p\), accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` supplies a boundary-through-\(p\) halfspace contained in it, so the same lower bound follows by monotonicity. Taking the infimum proves
\[
h_S(p)\ge s\prod_{i=1}^q(L_i+1).
\tag{11}
\]

The linear independence of the \(u_i\) also makes the parameterization of \(B\) injective, hence
\[
|B|=\prod_{i=1}^q(2L_i+1).
\]
Since \(L_i+1\ge(2L_i+1)/2\) for each \(i\), (11) implies
\[
h_S(p)\ge s\prod_{i=1}^q(L_i+1)
\ge2^{-q}s\prod_{i=1}^q(2L_i+1)
=2^{-q}s|B|.
\]

It remains to check the calibration. The four corner fibers of \(C_M\) are exactly the three intervals \([0,1]\) at \((0,0),(2,0),(0,2)\) and the interval \([M,M+1]\) at \((2,2)\). Indeed, a convex representation projecting to a vertex of the square can use only the vertical segment over that vertex. For an interval \([a,a+1]\subseteq\mathbb R\), the absolute depth of \(x\in[a,a+1]\) is
\[
\min\{x-a,a+1-x\},
\]
so its depth-\(s\) core is \([a+s,a+1-s]\). Thus the three low cores are \([s,1-s]\), while the shifted core is \([M+s,M+1-s]\).

Every affine \(g:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) satisfies
\[
g(0,0)+g(2,2)=g(2,0)+g(0,2).
\tag{12}
\]
If its four corner values lay in the corresponding depth-\(s\) cores, then
\[
g(0,0)+g(2,2)\ge M+2s,
\qquad
g(2,0)+g(0,2)\le2-2s.
\]
The assumption \(M>2-4s\) makes the first bound strictly larger than the second, contradicting (12). This proves only the stated failure of a four-corner affine selector and makes no claim that the example is minimal in any sense.

Finally, take \(q=2\), \(m=(1,1)\), \(u_1=(1,0)\), \(u_2=(0,1)\), and \(L_1=L_2=1\). Then \(B=\{0,1,2\}^2\), and every corner fiber has a point of absolute depth at least \(s\). The proved certificate gives
\[
h_{C_M\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R)}(p)
\ge s(1+1)(1+1)=4s\ge\frac94s.
\]
This is the requested \(9s/4\) calibration, with the stronger orbit-count bound retained. The argument ends with this specified-box certificate. It does not prove, and does not use, any dyadic affine-box decomposition or global captured-mass statement.
