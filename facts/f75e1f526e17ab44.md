## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
\Delta_d=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}
\]
be a full-dimensional simplex, let \(x\in\operatorname{relint}\Delta_d\), and write \(a_j=\lambda_j(x)>0\). For every \(i\), there is a unique \(\mu_i(x)>0\) such that
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{a_j}{\min\{1,\mu_i(x)a_j\}}=1.
\]
The optimization
\[
V_i(x)=
\min\left\{
\prod_{j\ne i}t_j:
0<t_j\leq1,\quad
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{a_j}{t_j}\leq1
\right\}
\]
has the unique minimizer
\[
t_j=\min\{1,\mu_i(x)a_j\}\qquad(j\ne i),
\]
and hence
\[
V_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}\min\{1,\mu_i(x)a_j\}.
\]
For fixed \(i\), both \(x\mapsto\mu_i(x)\) and \(x\mapsto V_i(x)\) are continuous on \(\operatorname{relint}\Delta_d\). These statements and the normalized volumes of the corresponding one-vertex caps are invariant under nonsingular affine changes of coordinates. At a boundary point the normalized halfspace depth is zero, and the finite minimum \(\min_iV_i\) has the continuous boundary extension zero.

However, the proposed identity
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_d}(x)=\min_iV_i(x)
\]
is false. In the standard tetrahedron
\[
\Delta_3=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}\subseteq\mathbb R^3,
\]
take the point \(x\) with barycentric coordinates
\[
(\lambda_0(x),\lambda_1(x),\lambda_2(x),\lambda_3(x))
=\left(\frac38,\frac38,\frac18,\frac18\right).
\]
Then
\[
V_0(x)=V_1(x)=\frac4{25},
\qquad
V_2(x)=V_3(x)=\frac12,
\]
while the proper closed halfspace
\[
H=\left\{y\in\mathbb R^3:
\lambda_2(y)+\lambda_3(y)\leq\frac14
\right\}
\]
contains \(x\) and satisfies
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_3(\Delta_3\cap H)}
{\operatorname{vol}_3(\Delta_3)}
=\frac5{32}<\frac4{25}=\min_iV_i(x).
\]

## Proof

Fix \(i\), put \(J=\{0,\ldots,d\}\setminus\{i\}\), and retain the notation \(a_j=\lambda_j(x)>0\). Since
\[
\sum_{j\in J}a_j=1-\lambda_i(x)<1,
\]
the point \(t_j=1\) is feasible. Moreover, every feasible point satisfies \(t_j\geq a_j\), because every nonnegative summand \(a_j/t_j\) is at most their sum, which is at most one. Thus the original feasible set equals a closed subset of the compact box \(\prod_{j\in J}[a_j,1]\), and the minimum exists.

Set \(u_j=1/t_j\). Minimizing \(\prod_jt_j\) is equivalent to maximizing
\[
\Phi(u)=\sum_{j\in J}\log u_j
\]
over the compact convex set
\[
u_j\geq1,\qquad \sum_{j\in J}a_ju_j\leq1.
\]
The budget constraint is active at a maximizer: if it were strict, one coordinate could be increased slightly, strictly increasing \(\Phi\). The strict concavity of \(\Phi\) also implies that the maximizer is unique.

Define
\[
f_a(\mu)=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{a_j}{\min\{1,\mu a_j\}}
=\sum_{j\in J}\max\left\{a_j,\frac1\mu\right\}
\qquad(\mu>0).
\]
This function is continuous and nonincreasing, and
\[
\lim_{\mu\downarrow0}f_a(\mu)=+\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{\mu\uparrow\infty}f_a(\mu)=\sum_{j\in J}a_j<1,
\]
so it has a solution of \(f_a(\mu)=1\). That solution is unique. Indeed, at any solution at least one index \(j\) satisfies \(\mu a_j<1\), since otherwise \(f_a(\mu)=\sum_ja_j<1\). If \(0<\mu_1<\mu_2\) were two solutions, choose an index unsaturated at \(\mu_2\). Its contribution is \(1/\mu\) at both \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\), so it strictly decreases, while every other contribution is nonincreasing. Hence \(f_a(\mu_1)>f_a(\mu_2)\), a contradiction.

Let \(\mu_i(x)\) be this unique solution and set
\[
u_j^*=\max\left\{1,\frac1{\mu_i(x)a_j}\right\}.
\]
Then \(\sum_ja_ju_j^*=1\). For every feasible \(u\), the tangent inequality for the logarithm gives
\[
\sum_j\log u_j-\sum_j\log u_j^*
\leq
\sum_j\frac{u_j-u_j^*}{u_j^*}.
\]
If \(u_j^*>1\), then \(1/u_j^*=\mu_i(x)a_j\). If \(u_j^*=1\), then \(\mu_i(x)a_j\geq1\) and \(u_j-u_j^*\geq0\), so
\[
\frac{u_j-u_j^*}{u_j^*}\leq
\mu_i(x)a_j(u_j-u_j^*).
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_j\log u_j-\sum_j\log u_j^*
\leq
\mu_i(x)\sum_ja_j(u_j-u_j^*)\leq0.
\]
Thus \(u^*\) is the unique maximizer, including when some values \(\mu_i(x)a_j\) equal one or several \(a_j\)'s coincide. Returning to \(t_j=1/u_j\) gives
\[
t_j^*=\min\{1,\mu_i(x)a_j\},
\qquad
V_i(x)=\prod_{j\ne i}\min\{1,\mu_i(x)a_j\}.
\]

We next prove the asserted interior continuity, including at changes of the saturated set. Regard \(a=(a_j)_{j\in J}\) as ranging over
\[
\mathcal D_i=\left\{a_j>0:\sum_{j\in J}a_j<1\right\}.
\]
The function
\[
F(a,\mu)=\sum_{j\in J}\max\left\{a_j,\frac1\mu\right\}
\]
is jointly continuous on \(\mathcal D_i\times(0,\infty)\). Fix \(a\in\mathcal D_i\) and choose \(0<\mu_-<\mu_+\) such that
\[
F(a,\mu_-)>1>F(a,\mu_+);
\]
such a pair exists by the two limits above. The strict inequalities persist for all \(a'\) sufficiently close to \(a\). Since \(\mu\mapsto F(a',\mu)\) is nonincreasing and has its unique level-one solution, that solution \(\mu(a')\) lies in \((\mu_-,\mu_+)\).

Now let \(a^{(m)}\to a\). The preceding local bound makes \(\mu(a^{(m)})\) bounded above and bounded away from zero. Every subsequence has a further convergent subsequence; if its limit is \(\bar\mu\), joint continuity along that further subsequence gives
\[
F(a,\bar\mu)
=\lim F(a^{(m)},\mu(a^{(m)}))=1.
\]
Uniqueness forces \(\bar\mu=\mu(a)\). Hence every subsequence has the same possible accumulation point and \(\mu(a^{(m)})\to\mu(a)\). Thus \(\mu_i(x)\) is continuous throughout the relative interior, even when a coordinate enters or leaves the saturated set. Since barycentric coordinates, the minimum operation, and finite products are continuous, the displayed formula then proves continuity of every \(V_i\) on the relative interior.

For completeness, the cap
\[
\Delta_d\cap
\left\{y:\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\lambda_j(y)}{t_j}\leq1\right\}
\]
is exactly the simplex with vertices
\[
v_i,\qquad (1-t_j)v_i+t_jv_j\quad(j\ne i).
\]
Indeed, putting \(\alpha_j=\lambda_j(y)/t_j\) expresses every point of the displayed cap as
\[
\left(1-\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_j\right)v_i
+\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_j\bigl((1-t_j)v_i+t_jv_j\bigr),
\]
and the converse follows from the same formula. Scaling the \(d\) edge vectors from \(v_i\) by \(t_j\) shows that its relative volume is \(\prod_{j\ne i}t_j\).

Now specialize to the stated tetrahedron and point. For \(i=0\), the three coefficients in the water-filling equation are \(3/8,1/8,1/8\). Taking \(\mu_0=16/5\) gives
\[
\min\left\{1,\mu_0\frac38\right\}=1,
\qquad
\min\left\{1,\mu_0\frac18\right\}=\frac25,
\]
and
\[
\frac38+\frac{1/8}{2/5}+\frac{1/8}{2/5}=1.
\]
Uniqueness of the water level therefore gives
\[
V_0(x)=1\cdot\frac25\cdot\frac25=\frac4{25}.
\]
By symmetry \(V_1(x)=4/25\). For \(i=2\), the three coefficients are \(3/8,3/8,1/8\). Taking \(\mu_2=4\) gives the saturated values \(1,1\) and the unsaturated value \(1/2\), and
\[
\frac38+\frac38+\frac{1/8}{1/2}=1.
\]
Hence \(V_2(x)=1/2\), and by symmetry \(V_3(x)=1/2\).

The halfspace \(H\) is proper and closed, and \(x\) lies on its cutting hyperplane because \(\lambda_2(x)+\lambda_3(x)=1/4\). It contains the two vertices \(v_0,v_1\), excludes \(v_2,v_3\), and its boundary contains no simplex vertex. Thus the failure is a nondegenerate two-versus-two cut, with no ambiguity from a vertex lying on the cutting hyperplane.

Write \(s=\lambda_2+\lambda_3\). In the standard coordinates \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\), for fixed \(s\in[0,1/4]\), the coordinate \(\lambda_2\) ranges over an interval of length \(s\), and \(\lambda_1\) ranges over an interval of length \(1-s\). The change from \((\lambda_2,\lambda_3)\) to \((\lambda_2,s)\) has Jacobian one. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(\Delta_3\cap H)
=\int_0^{1/4}s(1-s)\,ds
=\frac5{192}.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{vol}_3(\Delta_3)=1/6\), the relative cap volume is \(5/32\). Finally,
\[
\frac5{32}<\frac4{25}
\]
because \(125<128\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_3}(x)
\leq\frac5{32}
<\min_iV_i(x),
\]
which disproves the proposed equality.

If \(T\) is a nonsingular affine map, it maps proper closed halfspaces bijectively to proper closed halfspaces and multiplies every \(d\)-volume by the same positive determinant factor. It also preserves barycentric coordinates. Thus normalized depth, every \(V_i\), the water-filling formula, its continuity, and the counterexample are affine invariant.

Finally, every boundary point of a simplex lies on a facet. The closed supporting halfspace on the exterior side of that facet contains the point and meets the simplex only in the facet, so its \(d\)-volume is zero and the boundary depth is zero. To prove the claimed continuous extension of \(\min_iV_i\), let arbitrary interior points tend to a boundary point. Choose \(k\) whose limiting barycentric coordinate is zero and \(i\ne k\) whose limiting barycentric coordinate is positive. For all sufficiently late points, set \(t_j=1\) for \(j\ne i,k\), and set
\[
t_k=\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_i+\lambda_k}.
\]
These values are positive, at most one, and satisfy
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\lambda_j}{t_j}
=\sum_{j\ne i,k}\lambda_j+\lambda_i+\lambda_k
=1.
\]
Therefore
\[
0\leq\min_\ell V_\ell\leq V_i
\leq\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_i+\lambda_k}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Together with the already proved interior continuity of each \(V_i\), this shows that their finite minimum extends continuously to every boundary point by the value zero. The corresponding one-vertex caps also give
\[
0\leq\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_d}(x)\leq\min_iV_i(x),
\]
so the interior normalized depth tends to its boundary value zero along every such limit.
