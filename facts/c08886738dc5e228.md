## Statement

Use the distal quantile path of an actual mixed critical cover and suppose $C$ is a polytope. Write $G(T)=\mu(C\cap\bigcap_i\{\ell_i\le T_i\})$. Then $G$ is continuous and piecewise polynomial of degree at most $d$ on a finite polyhedral subdivision. Each original tail $F_i$ has the corresponding piecewise polynomial property in its threshold. Off finitely many levels, the selected inverse branches $t_i(a)$ are analytic and algebraic with
$$
S_i=-F_i'(t_i)>0,\qquad t_i'=1/S_i,\qquad t_i''=-S_i'/S_i^3,
$$
where $S_i'$ is the threshold derivative and $S_i$ sums original proper section areas. After finitely subdividing $0<a<B$, the path is on a single chamber or persistent face on each open piece, and a chamber polynomial $P$ evaluates $G$ along it. On that piece,
$$
M'=\sum_i P_i/S_i,\qquad
M''=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}/(S_iS_j)-\sum_i P_i S_i'/S_i^3 .
$$
On open chambers without moving-facet coincidences, $P_i$ is the sum of exposed clipped areas for that cut, between $0$ and $S_i$.

Let $q_0$ be the largest affine dimension of a nonempty core fiber $(C_0)_z$ over an originally positive fiber. Then $0\le q_0\le d-1$ and
$$
M(a)=c\,a^{d-q_0}+O(a^{d-q_0+1})\qquad(a\downarrow0)
$$
for some $c>0$. In particular $M(a)/a\to0$ if $q_0\le d-2$ and $M(a)/a\to c$ if $q_0=d-1$.

## Proof

In each originally positive sampled polytope, all possible vertices after truncation are solutions of $d$ linearly independent tight fixed normals, and are affine functions of $T$. Their linear feasibility slacks split threshold space into finitely many polyhedral chambers with constant feasible vertex labels and active incidence data. When a trace is full-dimensional, pull its vertices in a fixed order to triangulate its fixed face lattice, so each determinant has a fixed orientation and is polynomial of degree at most $d$. Lower-dimensional or empty traces contribute zero. Since changes in $T$ only change measure on finitely many moving slabs of vanishing thickness, the volume is continuous and a neighboring chamber polynomial can be restricted to a chamber face. This argument also applies to a single original tail.

On each nonconstant original branch the section-area sum is strictly positive off the finitely many levels of projected fiber vertices: the interval is then strictly between a fiber's extrema for at least one positive fiber. Outside such intervals a constant branch has a single tail value and can be omitted along with endpoint levels. Coarea gives $S_i$ because the continuous normal is unit. The inverse function theorem gives the analytic inverse and the two derivative formulas, while the polynomial equation gives algebraicity. Each affine chamber wall evaluated on an inverse branch is analytic algebraic. If it is not identically zero, it has finitely many zeros: its polynomial relation may be taken with nonzero constant term in the value variable by canceling all powers of that variable, and any zero then annuls a fixed nonzero polynomial in $a$. Identically vanishing walls give persistent faces. Thus the finite refinement exists. Differentiating $P(t(a))$ gives the displayed derivatives. For a unique moving facet and no coincident facet, moving just its threshold changes the corresponding fiber by a slab whose first volume coefficient is its exposed area. This area cannot exceed the original section area. On persistent faces any adjacent polynomial that agrees on the face gives the same derivatives of the composition; no counting of repeated facets is needed.

Each original cap has a proper own section at its old threshold. The tail polynomial on its right has finite strictly negative right slope, since that section has positive area. Hence $t_i(a)=b_i+\kappa_i a+O(a^2)$ with $\kappa_i>0$ and an analytic extension to $a=0$ from the right. The finite chamber refinement just proved makes $M(a)$ right analytic there. At least one originally positive core fiber is nonempty because it contains an old deepest point. All core fibers have zero $d$-volume by the mass law, so the maximal dimension is in the asserted range.

In a nonempty $k$-dimensional core fiber, all candidate vertices feasible for arbitrarily small positive $a$ converge to points in the core, at distance $O(a)$, since the vertex functions are affine in $t(a)$. The truncated fiber lies in an $O(a)$ parallel neighborhood of the core and has volume $O(a^{d-k})$. Conversely let $B_*$ be a full-dimensional ball inside the original fiber and $L$ its core. For some fixed $c_0>0$ and every sufficiently small $a>0$, $(1-c_0 a)L+c_0 a B_*$ is in the truncated fiber. Convexity puts it in the original fiber, and the finite upper bounds on the cap functionals on $B_*$ together with the positive increments $\kappa_i a+O(a^2)$ ensure every clipping inequality. A relative $k$-ball in $L$ combined with $B_*$ gives volume bounded below by a positive multiple of $a^{d-k}$. Empty core fibers remain empty for small $a$ by compactness and convergence of all thresholds. The resulting upper and lower total bounds by positive multiples of $a^{d-q_0}$ identify the leading power and its strictly positive coefficient in the right analytic expansion.
