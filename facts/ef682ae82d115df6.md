## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, meaning a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Assume that, for every \((x,y)\in C\) and every \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\), the point \(((\varepsilon_i x_i)_{i=1}^n,y)\) belongs to \(C\). Define \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and use the locked original mixed-integer volume \(\mathcal H_d\) and halfspace depth \(h_S\).

The actual integer fibers are \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\) for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), their volumes are \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), and the original total volume is \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Put \(D=\{y:\exists x\ (x,y)\in C\}\), \(C_0=\{y:(0_n,y)\in C\}\), and \(K_y=\{x:(x,y)\in C\}\). The horizontal lattice count and smoothed horizontal volume are, for every \(y\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
N(y)=\#(K_y\cap\mathbb Z^n),\qquad
V(y)=\operatorname{vol}_n(K_y+[-1/2,1/2]^n),
\]

where an empty horizontal section gives \(N(y)=V(y)=0\). These use the original actual body, without changing the integer-fiber weights. In one base coordinate, \(V(y)=2\max\{|x|:x\in K_y\}+1\) on \(D\), so \(V\) is the scalar smoothing marginal also denoted \(v\) in one-base notation. In this packet \(m\) is explicitly the original \(N/W\) mean, not the mean of that smoothed marginal.

Then \(D=C_0\) is a compact convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\), \(N,V\) are measurable, and

\[
N(y)\le V(y)\le3^nN(y)\quad(y\in\mathbb R^d),\qquad
W=\int_{\mathbb R^d}N(y)\,dy>0.
\tag{1}
\]

The actual convex body \(\widetilde C=C+([-1/2,1/2]^n\times\{0_d\})\) has horizontal-volume marginal \(V\). Its ordinary volume \(\widetilde W=\int V\) satisfies \(W\le\widetilde W\le3^nW\), and \(V\), extended by zero outside \(D\), is log-concave. Thus \(V/\widetilde W\) is a log-concave probability density.

Let \(P\) denote the original continuous marginal probability law with density \(N/W\), and put \(m=W^{-1}\int yN(y)\,dy\). Then \(m\in C_0\), and \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,m)\) is an actual point of \(S\). Define

\[
p_* = \inf_{\beta\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0_d\}}
P\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}.
\]

For every \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), including either zero normal component,

\[
\mathcal H_d\{(z,y)\in S:\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}
\ge \frac12\int_{\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}N(y)\,dy.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge(W/2)p_*\). All these conclusions concern closed halfspaces and the original mixed-integer volume. Every nonzero \(\beta\) gives zero \(P\)-mass to its boundary hyperplane, so the definition of \(p_*\) is unchanged if the inequality is strict. Under the stated full-dimensional unconditional hypotheses \(W=0\) cannot occur; in the locked problem's separate zero-volume branch the required depth inequality is automatic at any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\).

## Proof

First, \(D\) is compact and convex because it is a linear projection of the compact convex set \(C\). It has nonempty interior: an open ball contained in \(C\) projects to an open \(d\)-dimensional ball. For \(y\in D\), choose \(x\in K_y\). The assumed reflections include simultaneous base negation, so \(-x\in K_y\). Their midpoint gives \(0_n\in K_y\). This proves \(D\subseteq C_0\); the reverse inclusion is part of the definition of \(D\). Each nonempty \(K_y\) is compact, convex, coordinate-unconditional, and contains \(0_n\), including its lower-dimensional boundary sections.

We prove the pointwise comparison for any nonempty compact convex coordinate-unconditional set \(K\subset\mathbb R^n\). Such a set is closed under coordinate contractions: if \(u\in K\) and \(|v_i|\le|u_i|\) for every \(i\), then \(v\in K\). Indeed, change the coordinates one at a time. If the current \(i\)-th coordinate is nonzero, its replacement by \(v_i\) is achieved by the convex combination of the current point and its reflection in that coordinate, with coefficient \((1+v_i/u_i)/2\in[0,1]\) on the current point. The other coordinates remain fixed. If \(u_i=0\), the desired coordinate is also zero and no change is needed. Repeating this in all coordinates proves the assertion, without an interior assumption on \(K\).

Put \(Q=[-1/2,1/2]^n\). The lattice set \(K\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite. For each of its points \(z\), the cube \(z+Q\) lies in \(K+Q\). Distinct integer-centered unit cubes have disjoint interiors, and any intersection of two such cubes has \(n\)-dimensional volume zero: some differing integer coordinate gives either disjoint intervals or a common endpoint. Each cube has volume one. Their finite union therefore proves \(\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^n)\le\operatorname{vol}_n(K+Q)\).

For the reverse comparison define the coordinatewise contraction \(\rho:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb Z^n\) by

\[
\rho_i(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x_i)
\left\lfloor\max\{|x_i|-1/2,0\}\right\rfloor,
\qquad \operatorname{sgn}(0)=0.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(x=u+q\in K+Q\), with \(u\in K\) and \(q\in Q\), then \(|x_i|-1/2\le|u_i|\). Hence \(|\rho_i(x)|\le\max\{|x_i|-1/2,0\}\le|u_i|\). The contraction property just proved shows \(\rho(x)\in K\cap\mathbb Z^n\).

The exact one-dimensional cells of (3) are

\[
\{t:\rho_i(t)=k\}=
\begin{cases}
(-3/2,3/2),&k=0,\\
[k+1/2,k+3/2),&k>0,\\
(k-3/2,k-1/2],&k<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the zero cell has length three and every nonzero cell has length one. In particular, endpoints \(\pm3/2\) belong to the appropriate nonzero cells, and all cells are measurable and pairwise disjoint. For a lattice vector \(z\), the preimage \(\rho^{-1}(z)\) is the product of these cells, with volume \(3^{\#\{i:z_i=0\}}\). Since every point of \(K+Q\) is sent to a point of \(K\cap\mathbb Z^n\), finite subadditivity gives

\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(K+Q)
\le\sum_{z\in K\cap\mathbb Z^n}3^{\#\{i:z_i=0\}}
\le3^n\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{5}
\]

This argument includes \(K=\{0_n\}\) and every other lower-dimensional nonempty section. The empty section gives both sides zero. Applying the two comparisons to every actual \(K_y\) proves the first part of (1) everywhere, not merely almost everywhere.

By boundedness of \(C\), only finitely many \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) have \(C_z\ne\varnothing\). Each \(C_z\) is compact and therefore Borel measurable, and \(N(y)=\sum_z\mathbf1_{C_z}(y)\) is a finite sum of such indicators. For every measurable \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), finite summation and integration give the exact original-volume identity

\[
\mathcal H_d\bigl(S\cap(\mathbb R^n\times E)\bigr)
=\sum_z\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap E)
=\int_E N(y)\,dy.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, \(W=\int N<\infty\). Zero-volume integer fibers contribute zero to (6) and are not replaced or discarded in the count. Since \(0_n\in K_y\) for all \(y\in D\), one has \(N\ge1\) on \(D\). Consequently \(W\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(D)>0\), and \(S\) is nonempty.

The Minkowski sum \(\widetilde C\) is compact, since it is the image of \(C\times Q\) under the continuous map \(((x,y),q)\mapsto(x+q,y)\), and convex, since convex combinations of sums are sums of convex combinations. It contains \(C\), so it has nonempty interior. Adding \(Q\) in the base coordinates preserves the \(y\)-coordinate. Thus its horizontal section at every \(y\) is exactly \(K_y+Q\). Its indicator is Borel measurable, and Tonelli's theorem gives the measurable marginal \(V(y)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\mathbf1_{\widetilde C}(x,y)\,dx\) and \(\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(\widetilde C)=\int V\). Compact support and boundedness make this integral finite. Integrating the pointwise comparison proves \(W\le\widetilde W\le3^nW\).

The indicator \(\mathbf1_{\widetilde C}\) is log-concave on all of \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\): for a convex-combination parameter in \((0,1)\), two points where the indicator equals one have their convex combination in \(\widetilde C\), and if either value is zero the required right-hand side is zero. Apply Prékopa's marginal theorem, stated and checked under EXTERNAL SOURCES, to this actual indicator, integrating over the entire convex set \(\mathbb R^n\) of base coordinates. The resulting marginal is exactly \(V\), so for all \(y_0,y_1\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t<1\),

\[
V(ty_0+(1-t)y_1)\ge V(y_0)^t V(y_1)^{1-t}.
\tag{7}
\]

This includes support boundary points, with the zero extension already built into the indicator integral. The endpoint parameters are immediate. Dividing by the positive \(\widetilde W\) preserves log-concavity and yields the claimed probability density.

The original mean \(m\) is well-defined since \(N/W\) is a probability density supported on compact \(D\). We show \(m\in D\). If not, compactness supplies a point \(k\in D\) minimizing distance to \(m\). Put \(\delta=m-k\ne0_d\). For any \(y\in D\), the segment \(k+t(y-k)\) lies in \(D\), and the right derivative at \(t=0\) of its squared distance to \(m\) is nonnegative by the minimizing property. Therefore \(\delta\cdot(y-k)\le0\). Integrating against \(N/W\) gives \(\delta\cdot(m-k)\le0\), contradicting \(\|\delta\|_2^2>0\). Thus \(m\in D=C_0\), and \((0_n,m)\in S\). Simultaneous base negation also makes the base-coordinate mean of the original mixed-integer measure zero, by the finite reindexing \(z\mapsto-z\); hence this is its actual centroid, as well as a legal selected point. No claim that it equals the ordinary centroid of \(\widetilde C\) is used.

Now fix arbitrary \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\), \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), and write \(E_\beta=\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\) and \(H_{\alpha,\beta}=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\). For all lattice \(z\) and all \(y\),

\[
\mathbf1_{\{\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{-\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}
\ge\mathbf1_{E_\beta}(y).
\tag{8}
\]

Indeed, if \(\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\), the two scalars on the left sum to a nonnegative number, so they cannot both be negative. If it is negative, the right side is zero. This includes every equality case.

Base negation gives \(C_{-z}=C_z\). Reindexing the finite original integer-fiber sum therefore shows \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_{\alpha,\beta})=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_{-\alpha,\beta})\). Integrating (8) on each original \(C_z\), summing, and then using (6), yields

\[
2\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_{\alpha,\beta})
\ge\sum_z\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap E_\beta)
=\int_{E_\beta}N(y)\,dy.
\tag{9}
\]

This is (2) and uses no marginal from the smoothed body. If \(\beta=0_d\), then \(E_\beta=\mathbb R^d\) and (9) gives \(W/2\), including pure base normals. If \(\alpha=0_n\), the original volume is exactly \(\int_{E_\beta}N\) by (6). When \(\beta\ne0_d\), the boundary \(\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)=0\}\) is a \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue-null hyperplane, so it has zero mass under the density \(N/W\). Closed and strict original marginal mean-halfspaces consequently have the same probability. Boundary mass on integer fibers for pure base normals is retained by the closed inequalities in (8) and (9).

Every proper closed halfspace containing \((0_n,m)\) has a description \(H=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\ge a\}\), where \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0_n,0_d)\) and \(a\le\beta\cdot m\). Therefore \(H_{\alpha,\beta}\subseteq H\). If \(\beta\ne0_d\), (9) gives \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge(W/2)P(E_\beta)\ge(W/2)p_*\). If \(\beta=0_d\), it gives \(W/2\ge(W/2)p_*\), since \(p_*\le1\). The whole space, if included among halfspaces, has original volume \(W\) and obeys the same bound. Taking the infimum in the locked depth definition proves the claimed inequality at the one point selected before any normal. Finally, if the locked problem is considered separately with \(W=0\) and \(S\ne\varnothing\), every depth is nonnegative, so its required inequality has zero right-hand side and needs none of these normalized laws.

## External sources

András Prékopa, “On logarithmic concave measures and functions,” Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) 34 (1973), 335–343, [primary journal repository PDF](https://acta.bibl.u-szeged.hu/14411/1/math_034_335-343.pdf). Theorem 6 on printed page 342 states the following marginal property: for a log-concave function \(f(u,v)\) on \(\mathbb R^a\times\mathbb R^b\) and a convex integration set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^b\), the function \(u\mapsto\int_A f(u,v)\,dv\) is log-concave. Here \(a=d\), \(b=n\), \(u=y\), \(v=x\), \(A=\mathbb R^n\), and \(f(y,x)=\mathbf1_{\widetilde C}(x,y)\). This function is nonnegative and Borel measurable, is log-concave by the proved convexity of the actual \(\widetilde C\), and has compact support and finite marginal integrals. Thus every hypothesis holds and its marginal is exactly \(V\). This theorem is the sole non-elementary external input.
