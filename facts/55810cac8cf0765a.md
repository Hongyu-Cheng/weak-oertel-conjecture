## Statement

For a planar convex body $K$ with ordinary centroid $g_K$, let $F_K(s)$ denote its normalized upper cap area at threshold $e_1\cdot g_K+s$. For equal-area planar bodies $A,D$ put $\delta=|(A+D)/2|/|A|-1$. For every $\alpha>3/4$ and every finite $C$ there are full-dimensional rational trapezoids $A,D$ with the same four edge directions and a shift $s>0$ such that
$$
F_A(s)+F_D(-s)+C\delta^\alpha<\frac89.
$$
The same cuts are obtained with opposite thresholds relative to $(g_A+g_D)/2$. More precisely, the family below gives $\delta=\varepsilon^4/(uv)$ for $u=1+\varepsilon+2\varepsilon^2$, $v=1+\varepsilon$, and rational $0<\varepsilon\le1/100$. Its shortfall $S=8/9-F_A(s)-F_D(-s)$ satisfies $S>\varepsilon^3/4$ and $S=(4/9)\varepsilon^3+O(\varepsilon^4)$.

## Proof

For $w>1$ put $h(w)=(w+w^{-1})/2$, $\ell(w)=(w-w^{-1})/2$ and
$$
K_w=\{(x,y):\ell(w)\le x\le h(w),\ |y|\le x/2\}.
$$
The section width is $x$, so $|K_w|=1/2$ by $h(w)^2-\ell(w)^2=1$. Symmetry gives the centroid $(m(w),0)$, where integration gives $m(w)=2(h(w)^3-\ell(w)^3)/3=w/2+1/(6w^3)$. All vertices are rational when $w$ is rational. Take $A=K_u$, $D=K_v$ with the displayed parameters. The Minkowski midpoint has the same width $x$ between the averaged endpoints: for every feasible first coordinate the vertical section intervals add to the centered interval of the required width. Therefore its relative area excess is
$$
\delta=\frac14(u/v+v/u-2)=\frac{(u-v)^2}{4uv}=\frac{\varepsilon^4}{uv}\le\varepsilon^4.
$$

Let $L(w)=h(w)-m(w)=1/(2w)-1/(6w^3)$ and take $s=L(u)>0$. The $A$ cap is its supporting edge with zero area. The $D$ threshold is $t=m(v)-s=t_0+\Delta$, where $t_0=2m(v)-h(v)$ and $\Delta=L(v)-L(u)>0$. In fact
$$
\frac{d}{dw}(2m(w)-h(w))=\frac{(w^2-1)(w^2+2)}{2w^4},\qquad
-L'(w)=\frac{w^2-1}{2w^4}.
$$
Hence $t_0\ge1/3>\ell(v)$ since $\ell(v)\le\varepsilon$, while $t<m(v)<1\le h(v)$. The upper bound on $m(v)$ follows from $v\le101/100$ and $v^{-3}\le1$. Thus the normalized paired cost is $h(v)^2-t^2$. The average centroid has first coordinate $g=(m(u)+m(v))/2<1$ since $u<51/50$, and the common shift $h(u)-g>0$ gives the identical two thresholds $h(u)$ and $m(u)+m(v)-h(u)=t$.

Apply the explicit $d=2$ computation of accepted fact 75a1d57b1e81fdab to the normalized projection $x/h(v)$ of area on $K_v$. It has density proportional to the coordinate on $[r,1]$ for $r=\ell(v)/h(v)$, and its mean is $m(v)/h(v)$. The threshold $t_0/h(v)$ is twice that mean minus one. Complementing the CDF, which has no atoms, gives
$$
h(v)^2-t_0^2=\frac89+\frac{8r^3}{9(1+r)^3}
=\frac89+\frac{(v^2-1)^3}{9v^6}.
$$
Consequently $S=2t_0\Delta+\Delta^2-(v^2-1)^3/(9v^6)$. The derivative formula gives
$$
\Delta\ge\frac{(v^2-1)(u-v)}{2u^4}=\frac{\varepsilon^3(2+\varepsilon)}{u^4}.
$$
Using $t_0\ge1/3$, $u\le51/50$, $v\ge1$ and $2+\varepsilon\le201/100$, we obtain
$$
S\ge\left(\frac{4}{3(51/50)^4}-\frac{(201/100)^3}{9}\right)\varepsilon^3>\frac{\varepsilon^3}{4}.
$$
The difference between the bracket and $1/4$ is $1613599914733/20295603000000>0$. Also $-L'(w)=\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)$ uniformly on $[v,u]$, so $\Delta=2\varepsilon^3+O(\varepsilon^4)$. Substitution with $t_0=1/3+O(\varepsilon)$ gives the stated leading shortfall. For $\alpha>3/4$, $S/\delta^\alpha>\varepsilon^{3-4\alpha}/4$ tends to infinity along positive rational $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, proving the assertion for every finite $C$.
