## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be positive-volume convex bodies at the integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use their summed $d$-dimensional volume and closed ambient halfspaces. For an ordinary body $F$, let $D_F$ be ordinary raw halfspace depth and $m_F$ its maximum. Suppose $g$ maximizes $D_K$ and $2g-A\subseteq B$. The raw depth at $(0,g)$ is $m_K+|A|$ and the exact global raw maximum is
$$
\max\{m_A,m_B,m_K+|A|\}.
$$

In dimension two take $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\}$ and $(A,K,B)=(aT,h+cT,bT)$, with $a,b,c>0$. Suppose $c\ge(a+b)/2$, $b\ge2a$, $-h\in[c-(a+b)/2]T$ and $h\in(b/2-a)T$, interpreting zero scale as $\{0\}$. The raw maximum is $\max\{2a^2,2b^2,2c^2+(9/2)a^2\}$ and the mixed mass is $(9/2)(a^2+b^2+c^2)$. With strict support slack in both containments, both quantities are constant over an open neighborhood of translations $h$. In particular, $b=c=1$, $h=(1/10,0)$ and $0<a<1/4$ give a nonaffine centroid graph and normalized maximum
$$
\frac{2+(9/2)a^2}{(9/2)(2+a^2)}>\frac29
$$
tending to $2/9$ as $a\downarrow0$.

Under the initial arbitrary-dimensional assumptions, let $A_t=p+t(A-p)$ for $p\in A$ and $0<t\le1$, leaving $K,B$ unchanged. Write $x=t^d|A|$ and $k=|K|$. The exact normalized maximum is
$$
\frac{\max\{m_B,m_K+x\}}{k+|B|+x}.
$$
Contraction strictly lowers it while the middle branch is active, and strictly raises it on the strictly endpoint-active branch after a possible switch at $m_K+x=m_B$. In the planar family with $b=c=1$, contraction about the origin stays on the middle branch and strictly decreases the maximum toward $2/9$ while preserving the nonzero centroid defect.

## Proof

The prescribed convex hull has precisely these integer fibers. At an endpoint its continuous section uses only that endpoint. At base zero, the total endpoint weights agree, so the endpoint part of any convex combination is in their Minkowski midpoint and the remaining part is already in $K$. The projection is $[-1,1]$.

At $(0,g)$ apply the one-pair overlap certificate of a93e9443922f47dc. Its reflection overlap is $|A\cap(2g-B)|=|A|$ by containment, so the lower bound is $m_K+|A|$. Equivalently, for each $x\in A$, any containing closed halfspace captures at least one of $(-1,x),(1,2g-x)$. At an arbitrary middle mark $(0,y)$, extend an ordinary containing cap $u\cdot(x-y)\ge0$ to $u\cdot(x-y)-Lz\ge0$. For sufficiently large finite $L$ the endpoint traces are all of $A$ and none of $B$, by compactness. Taking the infimum over the ordinary caps gives the upper bound $D_K(y)+|A|$. At each endpoint every containing halfspace pays at least its ordinary depth and any prescribed own cap through that mark can be isolated by adding a sufficiently large signed multiple of the base displacement from that endpoint. Hence endpoint depths equal their ordinary depths pointwise. Ordinary maximum depth is attained since through-mark cap mass is jointly continuous in the mark and unit normal, using nullity of hyperplane boundaries, and the unit sphere and body are compact. It is positive at an interior inball center.

For the triangular case use the facet normals $(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,1)$, with support values $1$ on $T$ and $2$ on $-T$. The Minkowski midpoint is $((a+b)/2)T$, so its inclusion in $h+cT$ is equivalent to $-h\in[c-(a+b)/2]T$ for the stated scale. The three support comparisons for $2h-aT\subseteq bT$ are exactly $h\in(b/2-a)T$. The ordinary depth maximum in $rT$ is $2r^2$ at the origin by the ordinary triangle calculation in c4ebd085f1b2ab31, and translations transport that mark and value. The general formula now gives the stated raw maximum, and the area of $rT$ is $(9/2)r^2$. Strict support inequalities persist in an open set of $h$. In the displayed family, the available scales $(1-a)/2>3/8$ and $1/2-a>1/4$ make the respective inclusions strict. The endpoint centroids are zero and the middle centroid is $h\ne0$. Its raw value $2+(9/2)a^2$ exceeds both endpoint values. The normalized fraction exceeds $2/9$ for $a>0$ by subtraction and converges to $2/9$ as $a\downarrow0$.

For contraction, $A_t\subseteq A$ preserves midpoint inclusion and $2g-A_t\subseteq B$. The actual raw formula applies with $|A_t|=x$ and $m_{A_t}=t^dm_A$. The latter is at most $x<m_K+x$, yielding the claimed normalized formula. The derivatives with respect to $x$ on the middle and endpoint branches are respectively $(k+|B|-m_K)/(k+|B|+x)^2>0$ and $-m_B/(k+|B|+x)^2<0$. Here $m_K\le k$ and $m_B>0$. Since contraction decreases $x$, the two signs are as stated. When $b=c=1$, $m_B=m_K=2$ so the middle branch is strictly active for every positive $x$. Contracting about the origin keeps both endpoint centroids at zero and keeps $h$ fixed.
