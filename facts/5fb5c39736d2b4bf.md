## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0.
\tag{1}
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\(H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}\), and define
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\tag{2}
\]
Retain the canonical normalizations
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\tag{3}
\]
where \(G\) is exactly the global complete metronoid-core capacity of accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`.

When \(G<W/4\), form the half-strength canonical product reservoir \(r=(r_e)\) of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. Retain exactly its mass \(Q:=\sum_er_e>0\), its affinely half-metronoid-realizable submatching capacity \(R:=R_C(r)\), and its three canonical capacities \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\). Thus
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
 Q\geq\frac3{32}(W-4G).
\tag{5}
\]

Assume the following conditional split-retention premise for \(C\). If \(G<W/4\) and the canonical hard ledger
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}
\tag{6}
\]
holds, form all split-ledger objects with exactly the definitions, alias maps, ranks, and zero-support conventions of accepted fact `637b5f7874d8c875`. In particular, let \(\omega_e\) be the original load-minimal optimizer, let \(\Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e\), and retain its pooled, ancestral, congestion-retention, and fresh scores
\[
 P_{\mathrm{pool}},\qquad P_{\mathrm{anc}},\qquad C_\beta,\qquad F_{\mathrm{fr}}.
\]
Put, exactly as in that accepted fact,
\[
 A:=\max\{P_{\mathrm{pool}},P_{\mathrm{anc}}\},
\tag{7}
\]
and define the split score
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\mathrm{split}}
 :=A+\max\{Q-\Omega,C_\beta\}+\max\{A,F_{\mathrm{fr}}\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here the scalar \(A\) in (7) is distinct from the calligraphic aggregate \(\mathcal A(C)\) in (4). The conditional premise is
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak S_{\mathrm{split}}\geq\frac{\Omega}{99}.}
\tag{SR}
\]

Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \frac{3\gamma_d}{12800\gamma_d+6412}\,W
 >\frac3{12800+6412e}\,W.}
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
 \boxed{c_*:=\frac3{12800+6412e},}
\tag{10}
\]
one has
\[
 h_{\max}(C)>c_*2^{-n}W.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, if `(SR)` holds for every canonical hard branch, then (11) is the full weak Oertel conclusion for all positive-mass mixed convex bodies. Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{0<c_*<\frac1e,
 \qquad
 c_*>\frac3{64000}.}
\tag{12}
\]
The second inequality is a strict numerical improvement over the coarse closure constant in accepted fact `41c6cbb26797981c`.

## Proof

We first exclude the canonical hard ledger under `(SR)`. Suppose \(G<W/4\) and, toward a contradiction, (6) holds. Accepted fact `637b5f7874d8c875`, with exactly the objects retained in (7)-(8), gives both
\[
 \Omega>\frac{99}{100}Q
\tag{13}
\]
and the strict hard-branch screen
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\mathrm{split}}<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{14}
\]
On the other hand, `(SR)` and (13) give
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\mathrm{split}}
 \geq\frac{\Omega}{99}
 >\frac Q{100},
\tag{15}
\]
contradicting (14). Therefore
\[
 \boxed{G<W/4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal A(C)\geq Q/100.}
\tag{16}
\]
This uses `(SR)` only to rule out the hard branch; no split-retention assertion has been proved here.

We next establish the exact comparison between the aggregate capacity and \(D\). Accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` gives
\[
 D\geq\gamma_dG.
\tag{17}
\]
The reservoir capacity \(R\) is bounded above by the global affinely half-metronoid-realizable matching capacity of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5`, because every realizable submatching of \(r\) is one of the matchings allowed in that global capacity. The affine matching certificate in the same accepted fact therefore gives
\[
 D\geq\gamma_dR.
\tag{18}
\]
Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, with the exact normalizations used in (4), gives the three separate bounds
\[
 D\geq R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C),
 \qquad
 D\geq M_{\mathrm{off}}(C),
 \qquad
 D\geq R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C).
\tag{19}
\]
Using (17)-(19) term by term in the literal six-term sum (4) yields
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(C)
 \leq
 \left(4+\frac2{\gamma_d}\right)D.}
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, \(R\) and \(G\) contribute at most \(D/\gamma_d\) each, the three capacities in (19) contribute at most \(D\) each, and the final summand of \(\mathcal A(C)\) is \(D\) itself. This is a scalar upper bound on the aggregate and does not add different depth witnesses.

We now treat both core-mass branches. If \(G\geq W/4\), then (17) gives
\[
 D\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{4}W
 >\frac{3\gamma_d}{12800\gamma_d+6412}W,
\tag{21}
\]
because \(12800\gamma_d+6412>12\).

Suppose instead that \(G<W/4\). Combining (5), (16), and (20) gives
\[
 D
 \geq\frac{Q}{100(4+2/\gamma_d)}
 \geq
 \frac{3(W-4G)}{3200(4+2/\gamma_d)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Independently, (17) gives \(D\geq\gamma_dG\). Put \(g:=G/W\in[0,1/4)\). Dividing these two bounds by \(W\) gives
\[
 \frac DW\geq
 \max\left\{
 \gamma_dg,
 \frac{3(1-4g)}{3200(4+2/\gamma_d)}
 \right\}.
\tag{23}
\]

For the exact optimization, the first function in (23) is increasing in \(g\) and the second is decreasing. Their unique intersection is
\[
 g_*:=\frac3{12800\gamma_d+6412}.
\tag{24}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \frac{3}{3200(4+2/\gamma_d)}
 =\frac{3\gamma_d}{6400(2\gamma_d+1)},
\tag{25}
\]
and substitution of (24) gives
\[
 \gamma_dg_*
 =\frac{3\gamma_d}{12800\gamma_d+6412}
 =\frac{3(1-4g_*)}{3200(4+2/\gamma_d)}.
\tag{26}
\]
Also \(0<g_*<1/4\), because its denominator is greater than \(12\). If \(g\leq g_*\), the decreasing term in (23) is at least its value at \(g_*\); if \(g\geq g_*\), the increasing term is at least its value at \(g_*\). Hence (23) proves
\[
 D\geq\frac{3\gamma_d}{12800\gamma_d+6412}W.
\tag{27}
\]
Together with (21), this proves the first inequality in (9) in both core-mass branches.

It remains to make the dimension-free constant and its comparison exact. Since
\[
 d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1,
\]
one has \((1+1/d)^d<e\), and therefore
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>\frac1e.
\tag{28}
\]
The function
\[
 \phi(t):=\frac{3t}{12800t+6412}
\]
is strictly increasing for \(t>0\), because
\[
 \phi'(t)=\frac{19236}{(12800t+6412)^2}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (27)-(29) give
\[
 \frac{3\gamma_d}{12800\gamma_d+6412}
 >\phi(1/e)
 =\frac3{12800+6412e}=c_*,
\tag{30}
\]
which proves the strict inequality in (9) and hence (11).

Positivity of \(c_*\) is immediate, and
\(12800+6412e>3e\) gives \(c_*<1/e\). Finally,
\[
 e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}<3,
\tag{31}
\]
because \(1/k!\leq2^{-(k-1)}\) for \(k\geq2\), with strict inequality for \(k\geq3\). Therefore
\[
 12800+6412e<12800+3\cdot6412=32036<64000,
\]
and consequently
\[
 c_*=\frac3{12800+6412e}>\frac3{64000}.
\tag{32}
\]
Accepted fact `41c6cbb26797981c` identifies \(3/64000\) as the previously accepted coarse closure constant, so (32) proves the claimed strict improvement. Every conclusion remains conditional on `(SR)`.
