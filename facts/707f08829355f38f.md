## Statement

Let \(n\geq1\) be an integer, let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be compact and convex, and suppose that \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is a nonempty subset of \(\{0,1,2\}^n\). Assume that \(D\) is coordinatewise downward closed: if \(I\in D\) and \(K\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfy \(0\leq K_i\leq I_i\) for every \(i\), then \(K\in D\). No full-dimensionality hypothesis is imposed on \(P\).

For real numbers \(\beta_i\geq0\), define \(w_I=\exp(-\sum_{i=1}^n\beta_i I_i)\) for \(I\in D\), extend \(w_I=0\) to \(\mathbb Z^n\setminus D\), and put \(W=\sum_{I\in D}w_I\). For \(J\in D\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_w(J)&=\inf\left\{\sum_{I\in D\cap H}w_I:
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\right\},\\
S_J&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min(w_{J+v},w_{J-v}),
\\
A&=\sum_{J\in D}w_J\tau_w(J),
&R&=\sum_{J\in D}w_JS_J.
\end{aligned}
\]

The sum defining \(S_J\) is oriented and includes \(v=0\). Then

\[
R\geq\frac{W^2}{2^n},
\qquad
A\geq\frac12R\geq\frac{W^2}{2^{n+1}}.
\tag{1}
\]

## Proof

Put \(r_i=e^{-\beta_i}\in(0,1]\). We first prove a functional inequality on the entire ternary cube. For \(r\in(0,1]\), let \(\mathsf K_r\) be the symmetric matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by \(0,1,2\):

\[
\mathsf K_r=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&r^3\\
0&r^2&0\\
r^3&0&r^4
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]

For real-valued functions \(f,g\) on \(\{0,1,2\}^n\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_n(f,g)
&=\sum_{x,y\in\{0,1,2\}^n}
 f(x)g(y)\prod_{i=1}^n\mathsf K_{r_i}(x_i,y_i),\\
\mathcal Q_n(f)&=\mathcal B_n(f,f),
&\mathcal L_n(f)&=\sum_{x\in\{0,1,2\}^n}f(x)\prod_{i=1}^n r_i^{x_i}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Every matrix entry in (2) is nonnegative. Thus \(\mathcal B_n(f,g)\geq0\) whenever both functions are nonnegative. Also \(\mathcal Q_n(f)\geq0\) for every real-valued \(f\), without any monotonicity assumption. To verify this, let \(e_0,e_1,e_2\) be the coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb R^3\). The exact decomposition

\[
\mathsf K_r
=(e_0+r^3e_2)(e_0+r^3e_2)^{\mathsf T}
 +r^2e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}
 +r^4(1-r^2)e_2e_2^{\mathsf T}
\tag{4}
\]

has nonnegative coefficients. Multiplying these decompositions over the coordinates expands \(\mathcal Q_n(f)\) into a finite sum of nonnegative coefficients times squares of real linear expressions in the values of \(f\). This proves the asserted positivity, including \(r_i=1\).

We claim that every nonnegative coordinatewise nonincreasing function \(f\) on \(\{0,1,2\}^n\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal Q_n(f)\geq 2^{-n}\mathcal L_n(f)^2.
\tag{5}
\]

We prove (5) by induction, permitting dimension zero as the base case. In dimension zero the domain consists of one empty tuple, the products are empty products, and (5) is equality. For the induction step, set \(r=r_n\) and write \(f_a(x)=f(x,a)\) for \(a\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(x\in\{0,1,2\}^{n-1}\). Each section is nonnegative and coordinatewise nonincreasing, and \(f_0\geq f_1\geq f_2\) pointwise. Define
\(g=f_0+rf_1+r^2f_2\) and \(h=f_0-rf_1-r^2f_2\). Here \(\mathcal B_{n-1},\mathcal Q_{n-1},\mathcal L_{n-1}\) use the first \(n-1\) parameters in (3).

Expanding (2) and then these two linear combinations gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_n(f)
&=\mathcal Q_{n-1}(f_0)+r^2\mathcal Q_{n-1}(f_1)
 +r^4\mathcal Q_{n-1}(f_2)
 +2r^3\mathcal B_{n-1}(f_0,f_2)\\
&=\tfrac12\mathcal Q_{n-1}(g)
 +\tfrac12\mathcal Q_{n-1}(h)
 +2r^3\mathcal B_{n-1}(f_0-f_1,f_2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The second term in the last line is nonnegative by (4), even though \(h\) need not be nonnegative. The third term is nonnegative because \(f_0-f_1\geq0\), \(f_2\geq0\), and every kernel entry is nonnegative. The function \(g\) is nonnegative and coordinatewise nonincreasing, so the induction hypothesis applies to it. Finally \(\mathcal L_{n-1}(g)=\mathcal L_n(f)\). Consequently

\[
\mathcal Q_n(f)
\geq\tfrac12\mathcal Q_{n-1}(g)
\geq2^{-n}\mathcal L_{n-1}(g)^2
=2^{-n}\mathcal L_n(f)^2.
\tag{7}
\]

This completes the proof of (5). The sections in this induction were arbitrary ordered functions, so no product assumption about their supports was used.

We next relate (5) to the full reflected average. The assignments \(I=J+v\), \(K=J-v\) give a bijection between pairs \((J,v)\) with \(J\in D\) and \(J+v,J-v\in D\), and ordered endpoint pairs \((I,K)\in D^2\) having the same coordinatewise parity. Indeed, one direction is immediate. Conversely, equal parity makes \(J=(I+K)/2\) and \(v=(I-K)/2\) integral. Convexity gives \(J\in P\), and completeness \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) then gives \(J\in D\). These formulas are inverse to the first assignments and include the diagonal pairs.

For any such endpoint pair, log-affinity yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_J\min(w_I,w_K)
&=w_Iw_K\exp\left(-\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\beta_i\frac{I_i-K_i}{2}\right|\right)\\
&\geq w_Iw_K\prod_{i=1}^n
 \exp\left(-\beta_i\frac{|I_i-K_i|}{2}\right)
 =\prod_{i=1}^n\mathsf K_{r_i}(I_i,K_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The inequality is the triangle inequality together with \(\beta_i\geq0\). For the last equality, a coordinate of an equal-parity pair is one of \((0,0),(1,1),(2,2),(0,2),(2,0)\); their respective factors are \(1,r_i^2,r_i^4,r_i^3,r_i^3\), exactly the entries of (2). For a pair of different coordinatewise parity, the product of kernel entries is zero.

Let \(f=\mathbf1_D\), viewed as a function on the entire ternary cube. It is nonnegative and coordinatewise nonincreasing, and \(\mathcal L_n(f)=W\). Reindexing the full reflected sum as just proved, then summing (8), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
R
&=\sum_{\substack{I,K\in D\\I\equiv K\pmod2}}
 w_{(I+K)/2}\min(w_I,w_K)\\
&\geq\sum_{I,K\in D}\prod_{i=1}^n\mathsf K_{r_i}(I_i,K_i)
 =\mathcal Q_n(\mathbf1_D)
 \geq2^{-n}W^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

It remains to compare with depth against the entire original table. Fix \(J\in D\) and a closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(J\). If \(J+v,J-v\in D\), at least one of these two endpoints lies in \(H\), since their midpoint is \(J\). Therefore
\(w_{J+v}\mathbf1_H(J+v)+w_{J-v}\mathbf1_H(J-v)\geq\min(w_{J+v},w_{J-v})\).
Summing over the finite set of such oriented displacements, the left side is twice the original weight in \(H\cap D\cap(2J-D)\), which is at most \(2\sum_{I\in H\cap D}w_I\). At \(v=0\) it contributes \(2w_J\), so this count also includes that displacement correctly. Thus every original containing halfspace has mass at least \(S_J/2\), and \(\tau_w(J)\geq S_J/2\). Multiplication by \(w_J\) and summation over all \(J\in D\), followed by (9), proves (1). Finiteness and the midpoint argument are unaffected when \(P\) has lower rank.
