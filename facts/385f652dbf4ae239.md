## Statement

Let $T$ be a full-dimensional $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$, of volume $V$ and centroid $g$. Put $m=d+1$, $h=d/m$, $\Gamma=h^d$, and $\kappa=dVh^{d-1}$. Let $\lambda_i$ and $\ell_i$ denote its barycentric coordinates and their linear parts. For $t>0$ let $\nu_t$ be probability measures with weak limit $\nu$ as $t\downarrow0$, where $\nu$ has compact support and $\nu\{\lambda_i=1/m\}=0$ for every $i$. Put $p_i=\nu\{\lambda_i\ge1/m\}$ and $\bar p=m^{-1}\sum_i p_i$. The ordinary closed-halfspace depth for $\mu_t=1_T\,dx+t\nu_t$ attains its maximum for all sufficiently small $t$. Its maximal raw and normalized values are
$$
D_t=\Gamma V+t\bar p+o(t),\qquad
\frac{D_t}{V+t}=\Gamma+\frac{t}{V}(\bar p-\Gamma)+o(t).
$$
Every maximizing point satisfies $y_t=g+tv_*+o(t)$, with $\ell_i(v_*)=(p_i-\bar p)/\kappa$. Equivalently $v_*=\kappa^{-1}\sum_i(p_i-\bar p)v_i$ for the simplex vertices $v_i$. The coefficient $\bar p$ ranges between $1/m$ and $d/m$, with both endpoints attained by laws avoiding the canonical planes.

## Proof

For a point $y$ and unit vector $u$ let $F(y,u)=|T\cap\{u\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}|$. Every containing halfspace contains a parallel through-point halfspace. The raw depth, as the infimum over $u$ of upper semicontinuous functions of $y$, is upper semicontinuous: each fixed closed cap has this property by continuity from above of the finite measure. At $g$ the depth is at least $\Gamma V$ by fact 53fe12b742a7608d. Outside $\operatorname{int}T$ a separating or supporting cap has no $T$-volume and hence total mass at most $t$. For $t<\Gamma V$ the compact maximum on $T$ is global and lies in its interior.

For a maximizer $y_t$, each canonical cap has $T$-mass $V(1-\lambda_i(y_t))^d$ and added mass at most $t$. Comparing to $\Gamma V$ yields $\lambda_i(y_t)\le1/m+O(t)$ uniformly in maximizers, by taking $d$th roots. Since $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, the coordinates also satisfy the reverse bound $\lambda_i(y_t)\ge1/m-O(t)$, and $y_t=g+O(t)$.

If $u_t\to u$ and $z_t\to g$ with $\nu\{u\cdot(x-g)=0\}=0$, the $\nu_t$ masses of the moving through-$z_t$ caps tend to $\nu\{u\cdot(x-g)\ge0\}$. Indeed, choose an open ball containing the support of $\nu$, so its complement has $\nu_t$ mass tending to zero by Portmanteau. On the ball the defining affine forms converge uniformly. Sandwich the moving caps by fixed open and closed caps at offsets $\pm\delta$, allowing the complement error, apply Portmanteau, and let $\delta\downarrow0$. Nullity of the limiting plane gives the claim.

The function $F$ is continuous by dominated convergence because its hyperplane boundaries are volume-null. The fact cited above gives its minimum at $g$ and the exact equality normals $u_i=\nabla\lambda_i/\lVert\nabla\lambda_i\rVert$. Near $(g,u_i)$ the margin $a_i=u\cdot(v_i-y)$ is positive and all other vertex margins $a_j$ are negative. The cap consists of the simplex at $v_i$ with edge intercept ratios $a_i/(a_i-a_j)$, so $F(y,u)=Va_i^d/\prod_{j\ne i}(a_i-a_j)$ there. This is smooth. Its derivative in $y$ at $(g,u_i)$ is $-\kappa\ell_i$, by the canonical volume formula.

The barycentric linear map is a bijection onto the subspace of zero-sum tuples, since the barycentric coordinates are affine coordinates on $T$. Thus the stated equations define $v_*$, with the vertex formula following by the barycentric reconstruction of points. Take $z_t=g+tv_*$. If its depth were below $\Gamma V+t\bar p$ by a fixed positive multiple of $t$ along a sequence, choose approximately minimizing normals along that sequence and pass to a limit $u$. Nonnegativity of the addition and continuity force $F(g,u)\le\Gamma V$, hence $u=u_i$ for some $i$. The smooth formula gives
$$
F(z_t,u_t)=F(g,u_t)-t\kappa\ell_i(v_*)+o(t)
\ge\Gamma V-t\kappa\ell_i(v_*)+o(t).
$$
The moving-cap limit gives added mass $tp_i+o(t)$. The sum is at least $\Gamma V+t\bar p+o(t)$, a contradiction.

Conversely, for each $i$ the canonical cap through a maximizer has total mass $\Gamma V-\kappa\ell_i(y_t-g)+tp_i+o(t)$, by localization, the canonical volume formula and the moving-cap limit. Averaging these upper bounds cancels the displacement and proves the raw expansion. Each upper bound is at least the known maximum. The $m$ quantities $p_i-\kappa\ell_i((y_t-g)/t)-\bar p$ therefore have lower bounds $-o(1)$ and sum to zero, so they all tend to zero. This gives the displacement for every maximizing selection and division by the exact mass $V+t$ gives the normalized expansion.

The barycentric margins relative to $1/m$ sum to zero. Where no margin vanishes, the number of positive margins is between one and $d$. Its expectation divided by $m$ is $\bar p$. In the interior near a vertex one finds a point with just one positive margin, and near the relative interior of the opposite facet one finds a point with $d$ positive margins. Each condition persists in an open region, so laws supported there attain the bounds.
