## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}\{(-3,1),(2,-1),(3,0),(2,3)\}$ and let $\mu$ be normalized mixed length on $(P\cap\mathbb Z^2)\times[-1,1]$. For $z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^2$ put $g(z)=\sup_x\operatorname{depth}_\mu(z,x)$ for closed ambient halfspaces. Then
$$
\bigl(g(-2,1),g(-1,1),g(0,1)\bigr)=\tfrac1{30}(3,5,9).
$$
In particular $g$ violates log-concavity, and every positive-power concavity inequality, at the displayed integer midpoint.

## Proof

The polygon inequalities are $2z_1+5z_2\ge-1$, $z_2\ge z_1-3$, $3z_1+z_2\le9$ and $5z_2-2z_1\le11$. At the successive integer first coordinates $-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3$ they give second-coordinate sets $\{1\},\{1\},\{1\},\{0,1,2\},\{0,1,2\},\{-1,0,1,2,3\},\{0\}$. Thus the actual product has fifteen fibers of length two. At any base $z$, reflection in $x$ and convexity of depth superlevels give $g(z)=\operatorname{depth}_\mu(z,0)$. The latter convexity follows because a containing halfspace at a convex combination contains at least one of the combined points of positive weight.

For the lower bounds, a group of $k\ge2$ distinct indices with mean $z$ supplies at least two units of length to every ambient cap through $(z,0)$. A pure-base cap includes at least one whole group fiber since the base margins average zero. For a nonzero continuous normal, after reflection and scaling write the group cap lengths as $\min(2,\max(0,1-r_j))$, where the thresholds $r_j$ are linear in the displacements from $z$ and sum to zero. If any $1-r_j\ge2$ the claim is immediate. Otherwise clipping negative entries upward gives a sum at least $\sum_j(1-r_j)=k\ge2$. This also proves the claim for containing caps by tightening through the mark.

At each displayed base the own fiber contributes at least one unit. At $(-2,1)$ the separate group is $\{(-3,1),(-1,1)\}$. At $(-1,1)$ use $\{(-3,1),(1,1)\}$ and $\{(-2,1),(0,1)\}$. At $(0,1)$ use $\{(-2,1),(2,1)\}$, $\{(-1,1),(1,1)\}$, $\{(0,0),(0,2)\}$, and $\{(-3,1),(1,0),(2,2)\}$. The groups at each mark are disjoint from each other and from the own fiber, and each group has mean equal to that mark's base. This gives lower bounds of $3,5,9$.

The matching closed caps are $x\ge z_1+2$, $x\ge z_1+1$, and $x\ge2z_1+z_2-1$, respectively. In the first case $(-3,1)$ contributes two and $(-2,1)$ one. In the second case $(-3,1),(-2,1)$ contribute two each and $(-1,1)$ one. In the third case $(-3,1),(-2,1),(-1,1),(0,0)$ contribute two each and $(0,1)$ one. Every unlisted trace has threshold at least one, giving zero length. Dividing the exact lengths by thirty proves the table. Its midpoint inequality fails because $5^2<3\cdot9$, and every positive-power mean of the endpoint depths is at least their geometric mean.
