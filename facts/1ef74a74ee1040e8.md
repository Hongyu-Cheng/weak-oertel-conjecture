## Statement

Let a finite nonempty base set $P$ carry compact convex bodies $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, all full-dimensional, with summed fiber volume. Fix a globally deepest measured point $Y=(p,y)$. Suppose $T_r(z,x)=(z,A_rx+B_rz+c_r)$ are finitely many ambient affine maps preserving each fiber bijectively and fixing $Y$. Suppose
$H=\{(z,x):a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$, $u\ne0$, attains depth at $Y$ and is regular, meaning its mixed mass has the usual coarea derivative in all affine hyperplane parameters. If some positive weights $w_r$ summing to one satisfy $\sum_r w_rA_r^\top u=0$, there is a minimal positive circuit of at most $d+1$ regular critical preimage caps $H_j$ with positive total coareas.

Let $\eta$ be the sum of the actual fiber boundary measures, marked by their almost-everywhere outward unit normals $n$. There is one nonnegative continuous potential $\Phi(n,z,x)$, convex in $(z,x)$ for fixed $n$, such that $\int\Phi\,d\eta>0$ and
$$
\frac{\int\Phi\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf1_{H_j}\,d\eta}{\int\Phi\,d\eta}
   \ge\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
$$
for every probability vector $\theta$ on this circuit. In particular the bound holds for its normalized coarea response weights.

## Proof

Every $A_r$ is invertible with $|\det A_r|=1$, by volume preservation on any full fiber. Thus $H_r=T_r^{-1}H$ attains the same mass through $Y$. Its base normal is $a+B_r^\top u$ and its continuous normal is $u_r=A_r^\top u\ne0$. The affine hyperplane parameters of the preimage are obtained by an invertible linear change on the full coefficient and threshold space. The equality of cap mass functions under this change transfers full differentiability. It also transfers the coarea formula: on a preimage cut plane with continuous unit normal $u_r/\lVert u_r\rVert$, the tangential area multiplier of $A_r$ is
$|\det A_r|\lVert A_r^{-\top}u_r\rVert/\lVert u_r\rVert=\lVert u\rVert/\lVert u_r\rVert$.
Dividing image area by $\lVert u\rVert$ thus gives preimage area divided by $\lVert u_r\rVert$. The differentiated affine margin pulls back to the differentiated preimage margin.

The positive weighted own-plane margins sum to zero. The $H_r$ cover the own plane, so their common own-fiber cap mass is positive. It is strictly less than $|K_p|$: otherwise all margins would be nonnegative on the interior of $K_p$ and their positive weighted sum would vanish there, forcing each nonzero continuous normal to vanish. Thus every own cut is proper, giving positive total coarea. Select an inclusion-minimal sublist with zero in the convex hull of the continuous normals. Carathéodory bounds its size by $d+1$, and minimality makes all balancing coefficients positive and excludes positive dependence on a proper sublist.

Let $g_z$ be the centroid of $K_z$. The identities $A_rg_z+B_rz+c_r=g_z$ and $A_ry+B_rp+c_r=y$ give, on multiplying their difference by $u$ and averaging,
$$
u\cdot(g_z-y)=u\cdot\left(\sum_r w_rB_r\right)(z-p).
$$
The margin of every $H_r$ at $(z,g_z)$ equals the margin of $H$ there, since $T_r$ fixes that centroid. By the displayed identity this common margin is affine in $z$ and vanishes at $p$. Choose a maximizing exposed vertex $e$ of $\operatorname{conv}P$, so the common margin at $e$ is nonnegative. Every selected cap on $K_e$ therefore contains its parallel through-centroid cap.

Choose an affine exposer $\ell$ with $\ell(e)=0>\ell(z)$ for $z\in P\setminus\{e\}$. By finiteness and boundedness, for some finite $B>0$ the potential $\Phi(n,z,x)=[n\cdot(x-g_e)+B\ell(z)]_+$ vanishes on every other fiber boundary, uniformly in $n$. At $e$ it equals the radial flux $n\cdot(x-g_e)$, nonnegative by convexity, and the divergence theorem gives total $d|K_e|$. The radial flux on the flat part of a through-centroid cut is zero, so the integral over the original boundary part of that central cap equals $d$ times its volume. These flux identities in $d=1$ are the endpoint identities with outward signs and counting boundary measure. The through-centroid cap has volume at least $(d/(d+1))^d|K_e|$ by 6769574fc1bd9f8d at full retained mass. Nonnegativity and inclusion give the same lower bound times $d$ for the contribution of each selected cap. The ratio follows for every mixture. Positive total coareas $S_j$ ensure the response weights proportional to the positive circuit coefficients divided by $S_j$ define such a probability vector.
