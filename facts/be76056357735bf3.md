## Statement

For a finite probability law \(v\) on \(\mathbb Z^2\), define its original-mass canonical operator by selecting a full parity class \(\mathbf B\) with probability \(\pi^v_{\mathbf b}=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\mathbf b\pmod2}v_{\mathbf I}\), then selecting two independent endpoints with law \(v\) conditional on \(\mathbf B\), and averaging them. Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal T(v)_{\mathbf J}
=\sum_{\mathbf I:\,\pi^v_{\mathbf I\bmod2}>0}
\frac{v_{\mathbf I}v_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}{\pi^v_{\mathbf I\bmod2}}.
\]
This is precisely the original-mass operator of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`. It does not globally condition an independent pair with original law on a parity match. Write \(\operatorname{TV}(v,w)=\frac12\sum|v-w|\).

First, let \(D=K\cap\mathbb Z^2\) be the lattice points of a bounded convex polygon, with a nonempty integer interval on each occupied vertical line, and let the original root law be \(p_{(i,j)}=Z^{-1}e^{s i+t j}\), \(t>0\). Let \(b_i\) be the largest occupied integer ordinate, \(G=\{(i,b_i)\}\), \(u=p(\,\cdot\mid G)\), \(\eta=p(D\setminus G)\), and \(\delta=\operatorname{TV}(p,\mathcal T(p))\). Let \(\varepsilon=\mathcal T(u)(\mathbb Z^2\setminus G)\), using the original parity masses of the auxiliary graph law \(u\). Then
\[
\eta\le e^{-t},\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(u,\mathcal T(u))\le\delta+4\eta,
\qquad \varepsilon\le\delta+4e^{-t}.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(f\) is the upper boundary of \(K\), then for graph endpoints with the same full parity \((i,b_i),(k,b_k)\), the integer graph defect satisfies
\[
b_{(i+k)/2}-\frac{b_i+b_k}{2}\ge0
\tag{2}
\]
Graph leakage is exactly the probability that this integer is at least one under the canonical graph-endpoint sampling.

Second, put \(\alpha=\sqrt2\). For every integer \(t\ge100\), set \(M=t^6\) and define the two-facet concave roof
\[
f_t(x)=\alpha x-\frac{|x|}{tM},\qquad
K_t=\{(x,y):-M\le x\le M,\ -3M\le y\le f_t(x)\},
\qquad D_t=K_t\cap\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Define the original root law and its top graph by
\[
p_{t,(i,j)}=\frac{e^{t(j-\alpha i)}}{Z_t}\quad((i,j)\in D_t),
\qquad b_{t,i}=\lfloor f_t(i)\rfloor,
\qquad G_t=\{(i,b_{t,i}):-M\le i\le M\},
\tag{4}
\]
with \(p_t\) zero elsewhere. Write \(u_t=p_t(\,\cdot\mid G_t)\), \(m_t=\max p_t\), \(\delta_t=\operatorname{TV}(p_t,\mathcal T(p_t))\), and \(\varepsilon_t=\mathcal T(u_t)(\mathbb Z^2\setminus G_t)\). The exact aliases to the root law of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` are \((D,p,\boldsymbol\theta,Z)=(D_t,p_t,(-t\alpha,t),Z_t)\). The auxiliary \(u_t\) never replaces the original root in an atomic moment or slab assertion.

The support \(D_t\) is finite, complete, and of affine rank two. The score in the primitive vertical coordinate is \(t\to\infty\). No assertion is made that this vertical coordinate is a shortest-vector coordinate of a normalized mass core. With \(c=e^{-2}/1792\), all the following bounds hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{e^{-2}M}{2t}&\le Z_t\le\frac{56M}{t},
&m_t&=Z_t^{-1}\le\frac{2e^2t}{M},\\
\varepsilon_t&\le12e^2t e^{-t/4},
&\delta_t&\ge\frac{c^2}{2},\\
\operatorname{TV}(u_t,\mathcal T(u_t))
&\ge\frac{c^2}{2}-4e^{-t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
For every nonnegative integer \(\omega\), every primitive \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^2\), and every integer \(A\), the original law satisfies
\[
p_t\{\mathbf I:A\le\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf I\le A+\omega\}
\le(\omega+1)m_t\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{8M}{t}}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, \(m_t\to0\), \(64\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{t,\mathbf I}^{\,4}\to0\), and \(\varepsilon_t\to0\), while for every fixed \(\omega\) the supremum of the mass of all primitive width \(\omega\) slabs tends to zero, also after conditioning on the top graph. Nevertheless the canonical defect of the original law, and eventually also that of its graph conditioning, stays bounded away from zero. The full near-stationarity hypothesis is therefore essential; this family does not satisfy it.

## Proof

For the general reduction, the conditional law on a vertical line, counted downward from its top site, has weights proportional to \(1,e^{-t},\ldots,e^{-tL}\). Its top-site conditional mass is at least \(1-e^{-t}\). Averaging over the original line masses proves \(\eta\le e^{-t}\). Conditioning on \(G\) gives \(\operatorname{TV}(p,u)=\eta\). The canonical continuity bound of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` applies to arbitrary finite laws and gives \(\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal T(p),\mathcal T(u))\le3\eta\). The triangle inequality proves the second bound of (1). Since \(u(G)=1\), the event \(G^c\) gives \(\varepsilon\le\operatorname{TV}(u,\mathcal T(u))\), proving the last bound.

For (2), the full parity match makes both midpoint coordinates integers. Concavity gives \(f((i+k)/2)\ge(b_i+b_k)/2\). Since the right side is an integer, its floor is no larger than \(b_{(i+k)/2}\). The midpoint is in \(G\) exactly when equality holds. Completeness ensures that every such midpoint is in \(D\). This argument retains the full original-mass parity selection throughout.

Now consider (3)-(4). The roof has slopes \(\alpha+1/(tM)\) and \(\alpha-1/(tM)\), so it is concave and has two distinct facets. Its value on \([-M,M]\) is greater than \(-3M\), hence \(K_t\) is a bounded full-dimensional convex polygon. The definition \(D_t=K_t\cap\mathbb Z^2\) implies completeness: \(\operatorname{conv}(D_t)\subseteq K_t\), while every point of \(D_t\) belongs to its convex hull, so \(\operatorname{conv}(D_t)\cap\mathbb Z^2=D_t\). The three noncollinear lattice points \((0,-3M),(0,0),(1,0)\) belong to \(D_t\). Each integer abscissa in \([-M,M]\) has a consecutive nonempty vertical interval with top \(b_{t,i}\).

Suppress \(t\) in the next estimates and put \(d_i=f_t(i)-b_{t,i}\in[0,1)\). The unnormalized top weight is
\[
w_i=e^{t(b_{t,i}-\alpha i)}=e^{-|i|/M}e^{-td_i}.
\tag{7}
\]
Every unnormalized weight is at most one, and the origin has weight one. Thus \(m_t=1/Z_t\). Write \(Z_G=\sum_{i=-M}^{M}w_i\). The geometric vertical sums give \(Z_G\le Z_t\le Z_G/(1-e^{-t})\).

We use an elementary rational approximation only to count sufficiently many top sites. Expand \((1+\sqrt2)^n=P_n+Q_n\sqrt2\). The positive integers \(P_n,Q_n\) satisfy \(P_n^2-2Q_n^2=(-1)^n\), so they are coprime and \( |\alpha-P_n/Q_n|<Q_n^{-2}\). Also \(Q_{n+1}=P_n+Q_n<3Q_n\). Choose the first \(q=Q_n\ge t^2\), and put \(r=P_n\). Then
\[
t^2\le q<3t^2,\qquad (r,q)=1,\qquad
|\alpha-r/q|<q^{-2}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, \(q/t\ge100\) and \(M\ge16q\).

For any consecutive block \(i=B+\ell\), \(0\le\ell<q\), the difference between \(f_t(B+\ell)\) and \(f_t(B)+\ell r/q\) has absolute value at most
\[
q|\alpha-r/q|+\frac{q}{tM}\le\frac2q.
\tag{9}
\]
The fractional parts \(g_\ell=\{f_t(B)+\ell r/q\}\) form a translated grid of \(q\) points with spacing \(1/q\). An interval of length \(L\) contains between \(qL-1\) and \(qL+1\) such points.

At least \(q/t-5\ge q/(2t)\) of these grid points lie in \([2/q,1/t-2/q]\). At those indices (9) gives \(0\le d_i\le1/t\), without wraparound. If the block lies in \([-M,M]\), (7) gives \(w_i\ge e^{-2}\) at each selected site. The \(2M+1\) indices contain at least \(M/q\) full blocks. Therefore
\[
Z_G\ge\frac{e^{-2}M}{2t}.
\tag{10}
\]

For an upper bound in a block, all but at most three grid points have \(g_\ell<1-2/q\). At each such point, (9) implies \(e^{-td_i}\le e^{2t/q}e^{-tg_\ell}\); the same inequality holds if \(g_\ell<2/q\), since its right side is at least one. Sorting the translated grid and bounding its least fractional part by zero gives
\[
\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}e^{-td_{B+\ell}}
\le e^{2t/q}\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}e^{-t\ell/q}+3
\le2(1+q/t)+3\le7q/t.
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(e^{2t/q}\le2\), and \(1/(1-e^{-z})\le1+1/z\) for \(z>0\); the latter follows from \(e^z\ge1+z\). A partial block is bounded by extending it to a full block, using the same globally defined \(f_t\). Since \(w_i\le e^{-td_i}\), covering all indices by at most \((2M+1)/q+1\) blocks gives \(Z_G\le7(2M+1+q)/t\le28M/t\). Finally \(1/(1-e^{-t})\le2\), so
\[
\frac{e^{-2}M}{2t}\le Z_t\le\frac{56M}{t}.
\tag{12}
\]
This proves the normalization and largest-atom bounds in (5).

For graph leakage, (7) and (10) give
\[
u_t\{d_i>1/4\}
\le\frac{(2M+1)e^{-t/4}}{Z_G}
\le6e^2t e^{-t/4}.
\tag{13}
\]
Take two graph endpoints with the same full parity. The concavity gap of the roof at their midpoint is exactly
\[
f_t((i+k)/2)-\frac{f_t(i)+f_t(k)}2
=\frac{(|i|+|k|)/2-|(i+k)/2|}{tM}
\in[0,1/t].
\tag{14}
\]
If both endpoint deficits are at most \(1/4\), the roof deficit of their integer midpoint is at most \(1/4+1/t<1\). It is nonnegative by concavity. Thus that midpoint is precisely the top lattice site of its line. The canonical sampling of \(u_t\) has both unconditional endpoint marginals equal to \(u_t\). The union bound applied to (13) proves \(\varepsilon_t\le12e^2t e^{-t/4}\). Neither (13) nor this union bound replaces the class-selection weights by squared parity masses.

We next prove (6) directly for the original law. Let \(\mathbf v=(a,b)\) be primitive. Each integer level of \(ai+bj\) has primitive lattice direction \(\mathbf w=(b,-a)\). Convexity makes its intersection with \(D_t\) a consecutive finite sequence in this direction. Along this sequence the probabilities form a geometric progression, whose score increment is \(-t(a+\alpha b)\). For any finite geometric progression with largest term at most \(m_t\), its total mass is at most \(m_t/(1-e^{-t|a+\alpha b|})\).

If \(b\ne0\), there are at most \(1+2M/|b|\) sites on a level. Moreover,
\[
|a+\sqrt2 b|\ge\frac1{4|b|}.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, when the left side is at least one the bound is immediate. Otherwise \( |a-\sqrt2 b|\le1+2\sqrt2|b|<4|b|\), and the nonzero integer \(a^2-2b^2\) has absolute value at least one. Multiplying the two factors proves (15). The geometric and cardinality bounds together therefore bound each level mass by
\[
m_t\min\{1+2M/|b|,\ 1+4|b|/t\}
\le m_t\left(1+\sqrt{8M/t}\right).
\tag{16}
\]
If \(b=0\), primitivity gives \(a=\pm1\); the same bound follows from the vertical score \(t\), since its level mass is at most \(m_t(1+1/t)\). A width \(\omega\) slab contains at most \(\omega+1\) integer levels. Summing (16) proves (6), uniformly in its primitive normal and offset. Since \(M=t^6\), its upper bound for each fixed \(\omega\) tends to zero. The same slab conclusion for \(u_t\) follows by dividing the original slab mass by \(p_t(G_t)\ge1-e^{-t}\). The atomic moment assertion follows from \(\sum p_t^4\le m_t^3\).

Finally we verify that the missing on-graph redistribution has a fixed defect. It suffices to use the original full parity class \(\mathbf b=(0,0)\). On a block of \(q\) consecutive even indices \(i=B+2\ell\), with \(B\) even, divide \(f_t(i)\) by two. Its difference from \(f_t(B)/2+\ell r/q\) is again at most \(2/q\), by (8) and the Lipschitz bound on \(|x|\). At least \(q/(2t)-5\ge q/(4t)\) grid points lie in \([2/q,1/(2t)-2/q]\). For each such index the top ordinate \(b_{t,i}\) is even and \(d_i\le1/t\). Its unnormalized weight is at least \(e^{-2}\).

Each of the intervals \([-M,-M/2]\) and \([M/2,M]\) contains at least \(M/4-1\) even indices, hence at least \(M/(8q)\) full blocks of \(q\) even indices, because \(M\ge16q\). Thus the original mass, in parity \((0,0)\), of top sites in each interval is at least
\[
\frac{e^{-2}M/(32t)}{Z_t}\ge\frac{e^{-2}}{1792}=c.
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(\pi=\pi^{p_t}_{(0,0)}\). Conditional on that parity, the endpoint's first coordinate has left-interval probability at least \(c/\pi\) and right-interval probability at least \(c/\pi\). The two intervals are separated by at least \(M\). The pairwise variance formula gives
\[
\pi\operatorname{Var}(X_1\mid\mathbf B=(0,0))
\ge\frac{c^2M^2}{\pi}\ge c^2M^2.
\tag{18}
\]
The canonical covariance decrement in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`, under the exact aliases above, therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E_{p_t}X_1^2-
\mathbb E_{\mathcal T(p_t)}J_1^2
=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf b}\pi^{p_t}_{\mathbf b}
\operatorname{Var}(X_1\mid\mathbf B=\mathbf b)
\ge\frac{c^2M^2}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]
Both the original sites and canonical midpoints have first coordinate in \([-M,M]\). The bounded test \(i^2/M^2\in[0,1]\) thus proves \(\delta_t\ge c^2/2\). This is a bounded-coordinate variance comparison, with no unbounded spatial fourth-moment bound or uniform-integrability assertion. Finally the triangle inequality and the same canonical continuity bound give \(\delta_t\le\operatorname{TV}(u_t,\mathcal T(u_t))+4p_t(D_t\setminus G_t)\), hence \(\operatorname{TV}(u_t,\mathcal T(u_t))\ge\delta_t-4e^{-t}\). This proves the remaining claims of (5) and the stated limitation.
