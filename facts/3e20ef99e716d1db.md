## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(B\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers, and let \(\beta\ge0\). Define
$$
g(0)=0,\qquad g(1)=1,\qquad g(2)=B,\qquad g(3)=2B-1,
\qquad \Gamma(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^k g(x_i).
$$
For every integer \(E\ge0\), let
$$
f_E=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}
$$
when \(E\le T\), and let \(f_E=0\) when \(E>T\). Put
$$
Z_4=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},
\qquad
Z_3=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},
\qquad R=\lfloor T/B\rfloor.
$$
Then, with the absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{mass}}=256\),
$$
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}
\le \exp\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{mass}}R^2}{k-R}\right)
\qquad\text{whenever }R<k.
\tag{1}
$$
Moreover, for every real \(A\ge0\),
$$
R\le A\sqrt{k}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{256A^2}.
\tag{2}
$$
The latter conclusion has no restriction \(R<k\).

For the full original probability law on
$$
A_T^{(4)}=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):
\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z_4,
$$
define its ambient closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\) and moment
\(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T^{(4)}}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\)
as in accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977`. A consequence of (2) and that fact is
$$
R\le A\sqrt{k}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-1024A^2}}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$
All three conclusions retain the entire height tails, unit multiplicities of spatial vectors, and the complete original normalizers. The locked symbols \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) retain their locked meanings.

These conclusions also hold for every real-valued potential \(\widetilde g\) on the same four sites with
$$
\widetilde g(0)=0,\qquad \widetilde g(1)=1,\qquad
\widetilde g(2)=B,\qquad \widetilde g(3)\ge2B-1.
$$
For this extension put \(\widetilde\Gamma(\mathbf x)=\sum_i\widetilde g(x_i)\), and define \(\widetilde A_T^{(4)}\), \(\widetilde Z_4\), and \(\widetilde p\) by the same original integer-height epigraph construction, with \(\widetilde\Gamma\) in place of \(\Gamma\). Then (1)-(2) hold for \(\widetilde Z_4/Z_3\), and (3) holds for \(M_3(\widetilde p)\). In particular the result applies to every homogeneous convex four-site potential with the first three displayed costs: convexity of its linear interpolation is equivalent to the displayed fourth-site condition when \(B\ge2\).

## Proof

The quantities \(Z_3\) and \(Z_4\) are positive because their zero spatial vector has positive mass \(f_0\). The only property of the complete height tails used in the mass comparison is
$$
E'\le E\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_E\le f_{E'}.
\tag{4}
$$
This follows directly from their displayed definitions, including at the cap and when \(\beta=0\).

First, coordinatewise clipping of 3 to 2 weakly lowers energy. A vector in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) has at most \(2^k\) preimages under this clipping. Summing (4) over all preimages gives the general bound
$$
Z_4\le 2^k Z_3.
\tag{5}
$$
If \(R=0\), then \(T<B\le2B-1\), so every vector containing a 3 has zero mass and \(Z_4=Z_3\). We henceforth analyze
$$
1\le R\le k/16.
\tag{6}
$$
For \(m\ge0\), let \(Z_m\) be the part of \(Z_4\) from vectors containing exactly \(m\) coordinates equal to 3. Thus \(Z_0=Z_3\). A positive-mass vector in this part has occupation counts \(m_2,m_1,m_0\) at 2, 1, and 0, respectively, with
$$
m+m_2+m_1+m_0=k,\qquad
E=B m_2+(2B-1)m+m_1\le T.
\tag{7}
$$
Since \(2B-1\ge B\), it follows that
$$
m_2+m\le R.
\tag{8}
$$
In particular only \(m\le R\) can contribute. For a fixed occupation class in (7), all its vectors have the same energy \(E\), and its full original mass is
$$
\frac{k!}{m!m_2!m_1!m_0!}\,f_E.
\tag{9}
$$
We compare such classes with three-site classes. Within either comparison below, for fixed \(m\), the target occupation counts determine the source occupation counts uniquely. Consequently summing target class masses uses each three-site class at most once in that comparison.

Consider first the part \(Z_m^{\mathrm{hi}}\) having \(m_1\ge k/4\), with \(m\ge1\). By (6) and (8), \(m_1\ge m\). Replace all \(m\) coordinates equal to 3 and \(m\) of the coordinates equal to 1 by coordinates equal to 2. This gives the three-site occupation counts
$$
q=m_2+2m\text{ at 2},\qquad m_1-m\text{ at 1},\qquad m_0\text{ at 0}.
$$
Its energy is exactly \(E\), since \((2B-1)+1=2B\). Also
\(E=Bq+(m_1-m)\), so \(q\le R\). The ratio of the source multinomial multiplicity in (9) to this target multinomial multiplicity is exactly
$$
\frac{q!(m_1-m)!}{m!m_2!m_1!}
=\frac{\binom qm\binom{q-m}m}{\binom{m_1}m}.
\tag{10}
$$
Here
$$
\binom qm\binom{q-m}m\le\frac{R^{2m}}{(m!)^2},
\qquad
\binom{m_1}m\ge\frac{(m_1-m+1)^m}{m!}
\ge\frac{(3k/16)^m}{m!},
$$
because \(m_1\ge k/4\) and \(m\le R\le k/16\). The target class has exactly the same \(f_E\). The uniqueness of source counts from target counts now gives
$$
Z_m^{\mathrm{hi}}
\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{6R^2}{k}\right)^m Z_3.
\tag{11}
$$
The slightly larger constant 6 replaces \(16/3\).

For the remaining part \(Z_m^{\mathrm{lo}}\), where \(m_1<k/4\), set
$$
h=\min\left\lbrace B-1,\left\lfloor\frac{k}{8R}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace.
\tag{12}
$$
This integer depends on \(k,B,R\) and is fixed for every class in this part. It is at least 1. By (6), (8), and (12),
$$
hm\le k/8,\qquad
m_0=k-(m_2+m)-m_1>11k/16.
\tag{13}
$$
Thus there are at least \(hm\) zero coordinates. Replace every 3 by 2 and turn \(hm\) zero coordinates into 1. The target counts are
$$
m_2+m\text{ at 2},\qquad m_1+hm\text{ at 1},\qquad m_0-hm\text{ at 0}.
$$
Their energy is
$$
E'=B(m_2+m)+m_1+hm
=E-(B-1-h)m\le E.
\tag{14}
$$
Hence their full height tail is at least the original one by (4). The ratio of multinomial multiplicities is exactly
$$
\binom{m_2+m}m
\frac{\binom{m_1+hm}{hm}}{\binom{m_0}{hm}}.
\tag{15}
$$
The second factor is bounded by
$$
\frac{\binom{m_1+hm}{hm}}{\binom{m_0}{hm}}
\le
\left(\frac{m_1+hm}{m_0-hm+1}\right)^{hm}.
\tag{16}
$$
Indeed, compare the corresponding \(hm\) numerator and denominator factors; every numerator factor is at most \(m_1+hm\), and every denominator factor is at least \(m_0-hm+1\).

Write the fraction in parentheses in (16) as \(\vartheta\). Equations (13) and \(m_1<k/4\) show
$$
\vartheta\le\frac{3k/8}{9k/16}=\frac23.
\tag{17}
$$
There is also an energy-sensitive estimate. From (7),
\(m_1\le T<B(R+1)\), and \(h\le B-1\), \(m\le R\), so
$$
m_1+hm\le B(R+1)+(B-1)R\le3BR.
$$
Using the denominator bound from (13) yields
$$
\vartheta\le\frac{6BR}{k}.
\tag{18}
$$
We claim that (17)-(18), together with (12), imply
$$
\vartheta^h\le\frac{32R}{k}.
\tag{19}
$$
If \(h=B-1\), then
$$
\vartheta^h\le
\min\left\lbrace\frac23,\frac{6BR}{k}\right\rbrace^{B-1}
\le\frac{12R}{k}.
\tag{20}
$$
For the last inequality, consider the two sides of \(R/k=1/(9B)\). Above that value use \((2/3)^{B-1}\); below it use \((6BR/k)^{B-1}\). After division by \(R/k\), either estimate is at most \(9B(2/3)^{B-1}\le12\). The final bound follows because its value is 12 at \(B=2\), and the ratio of consecutive values is \(2(B+1)/(3B)\le1\) for \(B\ge2\).

If \(h=\lfloor k/(8R)\rfloor\), condition (6) gives \(k/(8R)\ge2\), and therefore \(h\ge k/(16R)\). Bernoulli's inequality gives \((3/2)^h\ge1+h/2\ge h/2\). Thus
$$
\vartheta^h\le(2/3)^h\le\frac2h\le\frac{32R}{k}.
\tag{21}
$$
At least one of these two alternatives holds, including their tie, proving (19).

By (8), \(\binom{m_2+m}m\le R^m/m!\). Combining (15)-(16) and (19) bounds the multiplicity ratio by
$$
\frac{R^m}{m!}(\vartheta^h)^m
\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{k}\right)^m.
$$
Equation (14) compares the complete height tails in the correct direction. Since the target occupation counts determine \(m_2,m_1,m_0\) uniquely for the fixed \(m,h\), summing over the low-one source classes proves
$$
Z_m^{\mathrm{lo}}
\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{k}\right)^m Z_3.
\tag{22}
$$
In particular this argument uses the binary entropy created by the released energy; it does not require a positive proportion of coordinates to be 1 in the source.

Summing (11) and (22) over \(m\ge1\), with \(Z_0=Z_3\), gives under (6)
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}
&\le 1+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{(6R^2/k)^m+(32R^2/k)^m}{m!}\\
&=e^{6R^2/k}+e^{32R^2/k}-1
\le e^{38R^2/k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The last inequality is equivalent to the nonnegativity of
\((e^{6R^2/k}-1)(e^{32R^2/k}-1)\).

We can now prove (1). The case \(R=0\) was settled above. If \(1\le R\le k/16\), (23) is stronger than (1), since \(38/k\le256/(k-R)\). If \(k/16<R<k\), then
$$
\frac{R^2}{k-R}\ge\frac{R^2}{k}>\frac{k}{256}.
$$
Together with (5) and \(\log2<1\), this gives
\(Z_4/Z_3\le2^k\le\exp(256R^2/(k-R))\), as required.

For (2), if \(R\le k/16\), use equality at \(R=0\) or (23) to get \(Z_4/Z_3\le e^{38A^2}\). If \(R>k/16\), then \(R\le A\sqrt{k}\) implies \(k<256A^2\), and (5) gives \(Z_4/Z_3\le e^{256A^2}\). This includes all values \(R\ge k\).

For the extension, \(\widetilde g\ge g\) pointwise, so every original integer-height atom in \(\widetilde A_T^{(4)}\) also belongs to \(A_T^{(4)}\) with the same unnormalized weight. Thus \(\widetilde Z_4\le Z_4\). The entire three-site epigraph is unchanged, with the same mass \(Z_3\). Equations (1)-(2) therefore hold for \(\widetilde Z_4/Z_3\) as well. This inclusion argument also covers a noninteger value of \(\widetilde g(3)\).

Finally apply accepted `cd9532b1d59c5977` with its \(B_i=B\), its \(k,T,\beta\) identical to the present ones, its original spatial coordinates and ambient halfspaces unchanged, and its normalizer equal to the present \(Z_3\). Its support is exactly
\(A_T^{(3)}=A_T^{(4)}\cap(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\times\mathbb Z)\).
Let \(p^{(3)}\) be that accepted law and put \(\alpha=Z_3/Z_4\). For every atom \(\mathbf z\in A_T^{(3)}\),
\(p_{\mathbf z}=\alpha p^{(3)}_{\mathbf z}\). Every ambient halfspace has original mass at least \(\alpha\) times its mass under \(p^{(3)}\), so
\(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\tau_{p^{(3)}}(\mathbf z)\).
Summing only these original sampled atoms and using the accepted theorem gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge\alpha^4 M_3(p^{(3)})
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Substitution of (2) proves (3). The same original-depth comparison applies to \(\widetilde p\), with \(\alpha=Z_3/\widetilde Z_4\) and the unchanged three-site law, and proves its asserted version of (3). The normalizer in each original moment remains its full \(Z_4\) or \(\widetilde Z_4\); \(Z_3\) is its explicitly compared mass portion. All comparisons used weak energy feasibility and the complete positive height sums, so cap atoms, \(\beta=0\), singleton height intervals, and equality atoms are included. When \(B=2\), (12) gives \(h=1\), and the low-one conversion (14) preserves energy exactly. If \(k<16\), every positive integer \(R\) lies in the branch using (5), while \(R=0\) was treated separately; thus no small dimension is excluded.
