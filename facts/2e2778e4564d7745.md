## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z>0,
\]
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
Let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed measure
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\},
\]
and, for \(y\in S\), let
\[
h_S(y):=\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }y\}.
\]

Fix \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(a\in(0,W]\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z\geq a
\tag{1}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\). Let \(B\subseteq C\) be a nonempty compact convex set, put
\[
B_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in B\},\qquad
b_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z),
\]
and let \(\mu_B\) be its mixed measure. For \(t\in(0,a)\), define
\[
\mathcal D_{m,B}(t):=
\left\{\nu:0\leq\nu\leq\mu_B,\ |\nu|=t,\
\int z\,d\nu=tm\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
\[
\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t):=
\left\{\frac1t\int (z,x)\,d\nu(z,x):
\nu\in\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\right\},
\tag{3}
\]
and the descendant halfspace surplus
\[
\Delta_{m,B}(t):=
\inf_{\substack{J\text{ proper closed base halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\left(\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}b_z-t\right).
\tag{4}
\]

If \(\Delta_{m,B}(t)>0\), then \(\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\) is nonempty, \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of
\[
B\cap(\{m\}\times\mathbb R^d)\subseteq S,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\max_{y\in\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)}h_S(y)
\geq
\min\left\{\frac{a}{d+1},\frac{\Delta_{m,B}(t)}d\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Moreover, every \(\nu_B\in\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\), not merely a specially chosen one, has a residual completion: there is a positive measure \(\nu_R\) such that
\[
0\leq\nu_R\leq\mu_C-\nu_B,\qquad
|\nu_R|=a-t,\qquad
\int z\,d\nu_R=(a-t)m.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(\nu_B+\nu_R\) is a target-mass selected state of mass \(a\) and base barycenter \(m\), in the notation of accepted fact ca3ef8792d78322a. The normalized barycenters of \(\nu_B\) and \(\nu_R\) belong respectively to
\[
B\cap(\{m\}\times\mathbb R^d)
\quad\text{and}\quad
C\cap(\{m\}\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{8}
\]

For the legal balanced-cap specialization, set
\[
a=2^{-n}W,\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
\beta:=\frac1{2e}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(m\) and \(B\subseteq C\) be as in the empty half-mass-core branch of accepted fact 51ff186e1aaeaa85; in particular, (1) holds and
\[
b_z\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a_z
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{10}
\]
Then, for \(t=\beta a\),
\[
\Delta_{m,B}(\beta a)
\geq\left(\frac{\gamma_d}{2}-\beta\right)a
=\frac{\gamma_d-e^{-1}}2\,a>0,
\tag{11}
\]
so some \(\nu_B\in\mathcal D_{m,B}(\beta a)\) has a residual completion (7) and its normalized barycenter \(y_B\) satisfies
\[
h_S(y_B)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d-e^{-1}}{2d}\,2^{-n}W
\geq\frac{1}{6ed^2}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{12}
\]

## Proof

We first record the finite-capacity selection principle used below. Let \((q_z)\) be nonnegative capacities with finite support, let \(r>0\), and suppose
\[
\sum_{\{z:u^\top(z-m)\geq0\}}q_z\geq r
\qquad(u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).
\tag{13}
\]
Then there are \(s_z\in[0,q_z]\) such that
\[
\sum_zs_z=r,\qquad \sum_zs_zz=rm.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, consider the nonempty compact convex metronoid
\[
K_r:=\left\{\frac1r\sum_zs_zz:
0\leq s_z\leq q_z,\ \sum_zs_z=r\right\}.
\]
For every nonzero \(u\), hypothesis (13) permits selecting total capacity exactly \(r\) using only points with \(u^\top(z-m)\geq0\). Hence \(h_{K_r}(u)\geq u^\top m\). The same is automatic for \(u=0\). If \(m\notin K_r\), strict separation of \(m\) from \(K_r\) would give a direction \(u\) with \(u^\top m>h_{K_r}(u)\), a contradiction. Thus \(m\in K_r\), which is (14). This is the finite-capacity observation used in the proof of accepted fact ca3ef8792d78322a; the argument above records all hypotheses needed here.

Compactness of \(C\) and \(B\) implies that their positive-volume lattice-fiber supports are finite. If \(\Delta_{m,B}(t)>0\), then every directional base halfspace through \(m\) has \(B\)-capacity at least \(t+\Delta_{m,B}(t)>t\). Apply (13)-(14) with \(q_z=b_z\) and \(r=t\). For every \(z\) with \(b_z>0\), put the constant density \(s_z/b_z\) on \(B_z\), and put zero density on zero-volume fibers. The resulting measure belongs to \(\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\), proving nonemptiness.

Identify a measure dominated by \(\mu_B\) with its Radon-Nikodym density \(g\), \(0\leq g\leq1\), in \(L^\infty(\mu_B)\). The order interval \(0\leq g\leq1\) is weak-star compact; the mass and base-moment constraints in (2) are weak-star closed; and all coordinates are in \(L^1(\mu_B)\) because \(B\) is compact. Thus \(\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\) is weak-star compact, and the barycenter map is weak-star continuous. It follows that \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\) is compact. Both sets are convex. A probability measure supported on the compact convex set \(B\) has its barycenter in \(B\), while the base-moment constraint makes the base coordinate equal to \(m\). This proves (5). This is the same compactness mechanism as for the fixed-base slice in accepted fact ca3ef8792d78322a.

We next prove residual completion for an arbitrary \(\nu_B\in\mathcal D_{m,B}(t)\). Let
\[
s_z:=\nu_B(\{z\}\times B_z),\qquad r_z:=a_z-s_z.
\]
Since \(B\subseteq C\) and \(\nu_B\leq\mu_B\), the fiber restriction of the positive measure \(\mu_C-\nu_B\) has mass \(r_z\). For every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\),
\[
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}r_z
=\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
-\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}s_z
\geq a-t.
\tag{15}
\]
Apply (13)-(14) to the capacities \(r_z\) and target \(a-t>0\). It gives \(s_z^R\in[0,r_z]\) of total mass \(a-t\) and base first moment \((a-t)m\). In a fiber with \(r_z>0\), multiply the restriction of \(\mu_C-\nu_B\) by the constant \(s_z^R/r_z\); use zero measure when \(r_z=0\). The resulting measure is \(\nu_R\) and satisfies (7). Its normalized barycenter lies in \(C\), and the normalized barycenter of \(\nu_B\) lies in \(B\); their base coordinates are \(m\). This proves (8) and the asserted target-state completion.

It remains to prove (6). By accepted fact 978e8555ec5bac42, \(h_S\) is upper semicontinuous. Hence its restriction to the compact set \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\) has a maximum. Put
\[
c:=\min\left\{\frac{a}{d+1},\frac{\Delta_{m,B}(t)}d\right\}.
\]
Suppose for contradiction that this maximum is smaller than \(c\). Choose
\[
\max_{y\in\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)}h_S(y)<\delta<c.
\tag{16}
\]

For every \(y=(m,x)\in\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\), accepted fact 978e8555ec5bac42 permits the infimum defining \(h_S(y)\) to be restricted to halfspaces whose boundary contains \(y\). Choose such a proper closed halfspace \(H_y^0\) with
\[
\mu_C(H_y^0)<\delta.
\tag{17}
\]
The continuous component of its normal is nonzero. Otherwise \(H_y^0\) would be the inverse image of a proper closed base halfspace through \(m\), and (1) would give \(\mu_C(H_y^0)\geq a>\delta\).

Because the continuous normal is nonzero, the boundary of \(H_y^0\) meets every lattice fiber in a subset of a \((d-1)\)-dimensional affine hyperplane. Therefore
\[
\mu_C(\partial H_y^0)=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Enlarge \(H_y^0\) by moving its boundary a sufficiently small positive distance away from \(y\). Continuity from above and (18) show that the enlarged closed halfspace \(H_y\) still satisfies \(\mu_C(H_y)<\delta\), while \(y\) is in the interior of its slice over \(m\). These slice interiors form an open cover of the compact set \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\). Choose a finite subcover \(H_1,\ldots,H_N\), and write
\[
H_i=\left\{(z,x):
\langle v_i,x\rangle+\langle u_i,z-m\rangle\geq c_i\right\},
\qquad v_i\neq0,
\tag{19}
\]
with \(\mu_C(H_i)<\delta\). Let
\[
K_i:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle v_i,x\rangle\leq c_i\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Here and below the affine slice \(\{m\}\times\mathbb R^d\) is identified with \(\mathbb R^d\) by \((m,x)\leftrightarrow x\) whenever it is intersected with the \(K_i\)'s. Under this identification, since the slice interiors of the \(H_i\)'s cover the reachable set,
\[
\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^N K_i=\varnothing.
\tag{21}
\]

There are two cases.

First suppose \(\bigcap_{i=1}^N K_i=\varnothing\). Helly's theorem in \(\mathbb R^d\) gives indices, relabeled \(1,\ldots,k\), with \(k\leq d+1\) and \(\bigcap_{i=1}^kK_i=\varnothing\). The exact Farkas alternative for the infeasible system
\[
\langle v_i,x\rangle\leq c_i\qquad(1\leq i\leq k)
\]
gives coefficients \(\theta_i\geq0\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_iv_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_ic_i<0.
\tag{22}
\]
Define the affine function
\[
L(z):=\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i
\bigl(c_i-\langle u_i,z-m\rangle\bigr)
\tag{23}
\]
and \(J:=\{z:L(z)\leq0\}\). One has \(L(m)<0\), so if \(L\) is nonconstant, \(J\) is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\); if \(L\) is constant, then it is a negative constant and \(J=\mathbb R^n\).

For every \(z\in J\), the halfspaces \(H_1,\ldots,H_k\) cover the entire continuous fiber space. Indeed, if \(x\) were outside every \(H_i\), then all corresponding inequalities would be strict, and (22) would give
\[
0=\left\langle\sum_i\theta_iv_i,x\right\rangle
<\sum_i\theta_i
\bigl(c_i-\langle u_i,z-m\rangle\bigr)
=L(z)\leq0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence
\[
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
\leq\mu_C\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^kH_i\right)
\leq\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_C(H_i)
<k\delta\leq(d+1)\delta<a.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(J\) is proper, this contradicts (1). If \(J=\mathbb R^n\), the left side is \(W\geq a\), again a contradiction. Notice that no assertion that \(k=d+1\), or that the \(v_i\)'s span all of \(\mathbb R^d\), is used or implied.

Now suppose \(Q:=\bigcap_{i=1}^NK_i\neq\varnothing\). Apply Helly's theorem to the finite convex family
\[
\{\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t),K_1,\ldots,K_N\}.
\]
In view of (21), it has an empty subfamily of at most \(d+1\) members. Since every subfamily consisting only of the \(K_i\)'s has nonempty intersection, this Helly subfamily contains \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\). After relabeling, for some \(1\leq k\leq d\),
\[
\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\cap K_1\cap\cdots\cap K_k=\varnothing.
\tag{25}
\]
Put \(U:=H_1\cup\cdots\cup H_k\). Equation (25) implies \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\subseteq U\), while
\[
\mu_B(U)\leq\mu_C(U)
\leq\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_C(H_i)
<k\delta\leq d\delta<\Delta_{m,B}(t).
\tag{26}
\]
For each lattice fiber put
\[
U_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in U\},
\qquad
q_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_z\setminus U_z).
\]
For every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), (4) and (26) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}q_z
&=\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}b_z
-\mu_B\bigl(U\cap(J\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr)\\
&\geq t+\Delta_{m,B}(t)-\mu_B(U)>t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Apply (13)-(14) to these capacities with target \(t\). Choosing in each fiber the corresponding constant fraction of the restricted measure \(\mu_B\!\restriction(B\setminus U)\) gives a measure
\[
0\leq\widetilde\nu_B\leq\mu_B\!\restriction(B\setminus U),
\qquad
|\widetilde\nu_B|=t,
\qquad
\int z\,d\widetilde\nu_B=tm.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus its normalized barycenter \(\widetilde y\) belongs to \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\). For each \(i\leq k\), let
\[
F_i(z,x):=\langle v_i,x\rangle
+\langle u_i,z-m\rangle-c_i,
\]
so that \(H_i=\{F_i\geq0\}\). The measure \(\widetilde\nu_B\) is carried by the set where \(F_i<0\). Since it has positive mass,
\[
F_i(\widetilde y)
=\frac1t\int F_i\,d\widetilde\nu_B<0
\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).
\]
Hence \(\widetilde y\notin U\), contradicting both (25) and \(\mathcal Y_{m,B}(t)\subseteq U\). The two cases prove (6).

Finally consider the legal descendant. Accepted fact 51ff186e1aaeaa85 gives (1), (10), and \(B\subseteq C\). Since
\[
\gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1},
\]
one has \(0<\beta<\gamma_d/2\). For every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\),
\[
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}b_z
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
\sum_{z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}a.
\]
This proves (11). The second term in (6) is the smaller one because
\[
\frac{\gamma_d-e^{-1}}{2d}<\frac1{2d}\leq\frac1{d+1}.
\]
Thus (6), together with residual completion, proves the first inequality in (12).

For the explicit estimate, if \(d\geq2\), the alternating-series bound gives
\[
\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)
\leq\frac1d-\frac1{2d^2}+\frac1{3d^3}
\leq\frac1d-\frac1{3d^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\gamma_d\geq e^{-1+1/(3d)}
\geq e^{-1}\left(1+\frac1{3d}\right),
\]
so \(\gamma_d-e^{-1}\geq(3ed)^{-1}\). For \(d=1\), the same inequality follows from
\[
\frac12-\frac1e\geq\frac1{3e},
\]
which is equivalent to \(e\geq8/3\). Hence the second inequality in (12) holds for every \(d\geq1\).
