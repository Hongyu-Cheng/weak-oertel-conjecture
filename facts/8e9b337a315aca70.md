## Statement

For $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, set $a=1+\varepsilon^2$ and
$$
A=\{(x,y):x,y\ge0,\ \varepsilon\le x+y\le1\},\qquad
P=\{(x,y):x,y\le a,\ x+y\ge\varepsilon\}.
$$
For every triangle $R\supseteq A$ and compact convex set $L\supseteq R\cup P$, their planar areas satisfy
$$
(|R|-|A|)+\tfrac14(|L|-|P|)\ge\varepsilon/16.
$$

## Proof

Write $\Delta=|R|-|A|$. The conclusion holds if $\Delta\ge\varepsilon/16$, so suppose $\Delta<\varepsilon/16$. Set $\eta=2\Delta/(1-\varepsilon)<\varepsilon/[8(1-\varepsilon)]\le2\varepsilon/15<\varepsilon/4$.

For a point $(x,y)\in R$ with $x<0$, the triangle joining it to the edge from $(0,\varepsilon)$ to $(0,1)$ is in $R$ and its interior is disjoint from $A$. Its area is $(1-\varepsilon)(-x)/2\le\Delta$. The horizontal edge gives the corresponding bound when $y<0$. For $x+y>1$, the exterior triangle over the edge from $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$ has area $(x+y-1)/2\le\Delta$. Thus every point of $R$ satisfies
$$
x,y\ge-\eta,\qquad x+y\le1+2\Delta.
$$

Let $q_0$ be a vertex minimizing $x+y$. Since $(\varepsilon,0)\in R$, its coordinate sum is at most $\varepsilon$ and each coordinate lies in $[-\eta,\varepsilon+\eta]$. The points $(1,0),(0,1)\in R$ give vertices $q_1,q_2$ with $q_{1x}\ge1$ and $q_{2y}\ge1$. They are different because $1+2\Delta<2$, and both differ from $q_0$ because their coordinate sums are at least $1-\eta>\varepsilon$. The preceding bounds give $|q_{1y}|,|q_{2x}|\le\eta$, since $2\Delta\le\eta$. These are all three vertices. The determinant of columns $q_1-q_0,q_2-q_0$ is at least
$$
(1-\varepsilon-\eta)^2-(\varepsilon+2\eta)^2=(1-2\varepsilon-3\eta)(1+\eta)>0 .
$$
The edge from $q_0$ to $q_1$ therefore keeps $(\varepsilon,0)$ to its left, and the edge from $q_2$ to $q_0$ keeps $(0,\varepsilon)$ to its left. Expanding the two determinants gives
$$
q_{0y}\le\frac{q_{1y}(q_{0x}-\varepsilon)}{q_{1x}-\varepsilon},
\qquad
q_{0x}\le\frac{q_{2x}(q_{0y}-\varepsilon)}{q_{2y}-\varepsilon}.
$$
The denominators are at least $1-\varepsilon>0$. The absolute values of the numerators are bounded by $\eta(\varepsilon+\eta)$, so
$$
q_{0x}+q_{0y}\le \frac{2\eta(\varepsilon+\eta)}{1-\varepsilon}
\le\tfrac54\varepsilon^2<\varepsilon/2.
$$
Here $\eta<\varepsilon/4$ and $1/(1-\varepsilon)\le2$.

The lower edge $F=[(a,\varepsilon-a),(\varepsilon-a,a)]$ of $P$ lies on $x+y=\varepsilon$. Since $q_0\in R\subseteq L$, the triangle $\operatorname{conv}(F,q_0)$ lies in $L$. Its interior lies strictly below that line and is disjoint from $P$. Its area is $(2a-\varepsilon)(\varepsilon-q_{0x}-q_{0y})/2$. Consequently
$$
(|L|-|P|)/4>(2a-\varepsilon)\varepsilon/16\ge31\varepsilon/256>\varepsilon/16.
$$
This proves the bound for the second case and completes the proof.
