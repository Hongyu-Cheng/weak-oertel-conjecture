## Statement

Let $B=\{x\in\mathbb R^5:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le1\}$, $V=|B|=1/120$, and $g=(1/6,\ldots,1/6)$. Set $R=9/2$, $r=497/3000$, $\ell=11/12-Rr$, and $T=1-r-\ell=3979/6000$. Let $D$ be the simplex with vertices $p=(0,11/12,0,0,0)$, $b_0=(r,\ell,0,0,0)$, and $b_0+T e_i$ for $i=2,3,4,5$. Put

$$
m=|D|/V=rT^4=\frac{124581095445185057}{3888000000000000000},
\qquad q=m^{-1/5},\qquad A=(2-q)D,\qquad C=qD,
\qquad a=(2m^{1/5}-1)^5.
$$

The convex body $L=\operatorname{conv}((\{-1\}\times A)\cup(\{0\}\times B)\cup(\{1\}\times C))$ has exactly the three integer fibers $A,B,C$ at $-1,0,1$, with volumes $aV,V,V$ and $0<a<10^{-5}$. Each fiber has strictly less than half the total mixed volume. Write $\mu(H)$ for the summed five-dimensional volumes of the integer fibers of $L$ in an ambient halfspace $H$, and $\Gamma_5=(5/6)^5$.

Set $h_1=1/6-(2/25)a$ and $h_2=1/6+(17/50)a$. The two proper closed ambient halfspaces

$$
H_1=\{(z,x):x_1+(1-q)rz\ge h_1\},\qquad
H_2=\{(z,x):x_2-2z\ge h_2\}
$$

cover $\{0\}\times\operatorname{conv}(\{g\}\cup D)$. Their respective mixed masses in units of $V$ are

$$
\frac{\mu(H_1)}{V}=\Gamma_5[1+(12/125)a]^5,\qquad
\frac{\mu(H_2)}{V}=\Gamma_5[1-(51/125)a]^5+a.
$$

Both are strictly below $\Gamma_5(1+a/2)$. Consequently the maximum original mixed depth normalized by $(2+a)V$ on $\{0\}\times\operatorname{conv}(\{g\}\cup D)$ is strictly below $\Gamma_5/2$.

## Proof

**Simplex and integer fibers.** The value $\ell=1027/6000$ is positive, the sums of the coordinates of $p$ and $b_0$ are $11/12$ and $1-T$, and each $b_0+T e_i$ has coordinate sum one. Thus every vertex is in $B$. Every vertex satisfies $x_1\le r<1/6$ and $R x_1+x_2\ge11/12=(R+1)/6$, so the whole simplex does. In the edge matrix based at $p$, subtract the $b_0-p$ column from each of the other four columns. The resulting columns $b_0-p,T e_2,T e_3,T e_4,T e_5$ have determinant $rT^4>0$. Dividing by $5!$ gives the volume of $D$ and the stated ratio $m$.

The exact positive differences are

$$
m-\frac{1}{32}=\frac{3081095445185057}{3888000000000000000},\qquad
\left(\frac{11}{20}\right)^5-m=\frac{71095869554814943}{3888000000000000000}.
$$

Thus $1/2<m^{1/5}<11/20$, so $20/11<q<2$ and $0<a<10^{-5}$. Homothety gives $|C|/V=q^5m=1$ and $|A|/V=(2-q)^5m=a$. Since the two positive homothety coefficients sum to two and $D$ is convex, $(A+C)/2=D\subseteq B$. The lifted hull is compact and full-dimensional because it contains $\{0\}\times B$ and a point at a nonzero level. Its projection is $[-1,1]$ and the two extreme levels give exactly the prescribed endpoints. At level zero the weights on the two endpoints in a convex combination agree. If positive, their normalized contribution lies in $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$; the other contribution belongs to $B$ as well. Hence the middle fiber is exactly $B$. Since $0<a<1$, both $1$ and $a$ are strictly less than $(2+a)/2$.

**Closed halfspace traces.** For $H_1$, the maximum of $x_1+(1-q)rz$ on $A$ at $z=-1$ is $(2-q)r-(1-q)r=r$, and on $C$ at $z=1$ it is $qr+(1-q)r=r$. Both are strictly less than $h_1$, since $(2/25)a<1/1000=1/6-r$. Thus both endpoint traces are empty. For $H_2$ on $A$, $x_2-2z=x_2+2\ge2>h_2$. On $C\subseteq qB$ it is $x_2-2\le q-2<0<h_2$. Thus $H_2$ takes the whole of $A$ and none of $C$, with strict margins.

At level zero take any $y=(1-t)g+t x$ with $x\in D$ and $0\le t\le1$; these exhaust the larger hull by convexity of $D$. If $y_1\ge h_1$, the point lies in $H_1$, including at equality. Otherwise $t(1/6-x_1)>(2/25)a$, and the wedge inequality gives

$$
y_2-1/6=t(x_2-1/6)\ge R t(1/6-x_1)>(9/25)a>(17/50)a.
$$

This places the point strictly in $H_2$.

**Masses and strict comparison.** Both thresholds lie in $(0,1)$. On $B$ the traces have orientation $x_i\ge h_i$, with $i=1,2$. Apply the singleton barycentric upper-cap formula of accepted fact `290ddd8516d76865` with $d=5$, $I=\{i\}$ and point $h_i e_i\in B$: the relative mass is $1-F_{5,1}(h_i)=(1-h_i)^5$. Together with the endpoint traces and $|A|/V=a$, these give the two displayed masses.

For the first cap, use $a<1/100$ and the mean value theorem:

$$
\frac{[1+(12/125)a]^5-1}{a}
\le \frac{12}{25}\left(1+\frac{12}{12500}\right)^4
=\frac{1148810043113472}{2384185791015625}<\frac12.
$$

For the second cap, put $s=(51/125)a\in(0,1)$. The binomial identity $(1-s)^5=1-5s+10s^2-s^3(10-5s+s^2)$ gives an upper bound of $1-5s+10s^2$. Dividing the resulting lower bound on the second cap's shortfall by $a>0$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Gamma_5(1+a/2)-\mu(H_2)/V}{a}
&\ge \Gamma_5(1/2+51/25)-1-10\Gamma_5(51/125)^2a\\
&\ge \frac{323}{15552}-\frac{289}{43200}
=\frac{2737}{194400}>0,
\end{aligned}
$$

where the quadratic term uses $a\le1/100$ and $\Gamma_5=3125/7776$. All comparisons use rational bounds on the algebraic parameter.

Every point of the larger hull therefore has depth at most $\max(\mu(H_1),\mu(H_2))$, a fixed strict bound. A maximum exists there: depth is the infimum over unit normals $u$ of the cap mass $\mu(\{w:u\cdot w\ge u\cdot v\})$ through the ambient query point $v$, since moving a cap's boundary to $v$ can only decrease its mass. For fixed $u$ this mass is upper semicontinuous in $v$ by finiteness of the mixed measure and the closed threshold, so the infimum is upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum on the compact larger hull.
