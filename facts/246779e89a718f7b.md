## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$ be integers and let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex. Assume the nonempty set $P$ of positive-$d$-volume integer fibers is finite, and that these fibers are $K_p=a_p+r_pK$, with $r_p>0$ and one full-dimensional convex body $K$ of centroid zero. At each occupied parity choose a designated root $v_c$ in that class. Suppose the forest given by $f(p)=(p+v_c)/2$ for a nonroot $p$ in class $c$ terminates at the roots and that every edge satisfies $K_{f(p)}=(K_p+K_{v_c})/2$.

Write $G_p=(p,r_p)$ and let $F_p$ be the minimal face of the lifted polytope $\widehat Q=\operatorname{conv}\lbrace G_p:p\in P\rbrace$ containing $G_p$, allowing the whole polytope as a face. Every $G_p$ lies in a face supported from above by the graph of an affine majorant of the sampled radii. For each edge, $F_p$ and $F_{v_c}$ are contained in $F_{f(p)}$. Thus the sampled lifts of the nodes and of all partner roots on any path terminating at $v$ lie in $F_v$, and the inclusion-maximal terminal-root faces cover all sampled lifts.

For each terminal-root face $F$, base projection $\pi$ is an affine isomorphism from $F$ to $\pi(F)$. The sampled bases on $F$ are exactly $\pi(F)\cap\mathbb Z^n$. On those bases the radius is affine, and there is a single affine map on $\operatorname{aff}\pi(F)$ giving all the actual centroids $a_p$.

## Proof

If $z=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i$ is an integer base, with $\lambda_i\ge0$ summing to one and $p_i\in P$, convexity gives
$$
\left(\sum_i\lambda_i a_{p_i}\right)+\left(\sum_i\lambda_i r_{p_i}\right)K
=\sum_i\lambda_i K_{p_i}\subseteq K_z.
$$
The left side has positive volume, so $z\in P$. Comparing volumes and taking positive $d$th roots gives $r_z\ge\sum_i\lambda_i r_{p_i}$. This proves the anchored inequality directly by homothety.

For a sampled base $p$, maximize $\sum_{q\in P}\lambda_q r_q$ under $\lambda_q\ge0$, $\sum_q\lambda_q=1$, and $\sum_q\lambda_q q=p$. The optimum is attained and equals $r_p$ by the anchored inequality and the point mass at $p$. The dual is minimization of $\ell(p)$ over affine functions on the base span satisfying $\ell(q)\ge r_q$ for all $q\in P$; it is feasible, for instance by a constant majorant. Finite linear programming duality and attainment give an affine majorant with $\ell(p)=r_p$, also for boundary bases. Its graph intersects $\widehat Q$ in a face containing $G_p$ and hence containing $F_p$. The base projection on this supporting graph is affine and injective.

Each forest edge gives $G_{f(p)}=(G_p+G_{v_c})/2$: the exact set equality implies the radius equality by volumes, and the base identity holds by definition. A face containing this midpoint contains both endpoints. Minimality gives the two claimed inclusions, and iteration along terminating paths proves the covering assertion.

Fix a root face $F=F_v$ and the above supporting majorant $\ell$ at $v$. If $z\in\pi(F)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, lift it into $F$ and express that lift as a convex combination of sampled lifts on $F$. The radii of these lifts lie on $\ell$. The anchored inequality gives $z\in P$ and $r_z\ge\ell(z)$; the majorant gives the reverse inequality. The lift $(z,r_z)$ is therefore the same point of $F$. The reverse inclusion is by definition. Put $P_F=\pi(F)\cap\mathbb Z^n$.

For any convex representation $z=\sum_{p\in P_F}\lambda_p p$ with $z\in P_F$, the Minkowski inclusion above has equal radii on both sides because $\ell$ is affine. The two convex bodies coincide: if a point of the larger body were outside the smaller closed body, a nearby interior point of the larger one would have a ball disjoint from the smaller one, contradicting equal volume. Their centroids give $a_z=\sum_p\lambda_p a_p$.

The root $v$ is in the relative interior of $\pi(F)$, since its lift is in the relative interior of its minimal face. It has a convex representation by $P_F$ with all coefficients positive: for each $p\in P_F$ put a sufficiently small positive coefficient on $p$, leaving the residual base in $\pi(F)$, then average these representations. Any affine dependence of $P_F$ can be added to and subtracted from this coefficient vector with sufficiently small multiplier. The two perturbed vectors still represent $v$; subtracting the anchored centroid identities gives the same affine dependence among the $a_p$. This is precisely the criterion that the assignment $p\mapsto a_p$ extends to an affine map on the base span of $P_F$, which is $\operatorname{aff}\pi(F)$.
