## Statement

Take the compatible simplex fibers and notation of the simultaneous facet-contact lemma. Let $n_i$ be the outward unit normal of $F_i(z)$, independent of $z$, and let $\eta$ be the sum of the facet $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measures on the actual placed fibers with their normal marks $n_i$. For $d=1$ this counts the corresponding endpoints. Let $H_l$ be a finite list of closed ambient halfspaces, $\theta_l\ge0$, $\sum_l\theta_l=1$, and
$$
W=\sum_l\theta_l\mathbf1_{H_l},\qquad
W_{\rm strict}(X)=\sum_{\{l:X\in\operatorname{int}H_l\}}\theta_l.
$$
An admissible potential is a continuous nonnegative function $\Phi(n,z,x)$ convex in $(z,x)$ for each fixed $n$, with $\int\Phi\,d\eta>0$. If a lifted vertex $X=(p,v_j(p))$ has no simultaneous representation by other bases as in the contact lemma, there is an admissible potential with efficiency $\int\Phi W\,d\eta/\int\Phi\,d\eta\ge W_{\rm strict}(X)$; the constructed efficiency equals $W(X)$ when no cap boundary contains $X$. Hence if the supremum of efficiencies is at most $c$, every such vertex with $W_{\rm strict}(X)>c$ has the simultaneous anchored representation for all its active offsets.

## Proof

By the contact lemma $X$ is a vertex of an incident $B_i$. Choose an affine functional $A$ whose unique maximum on $B_i$ is $h=A(X)$. Pick a continuous nonnegative selector $\psi_i$ on the normal sphere, one at $n_i$ and zero at all other $n_k$ (the product of the squared distances from those normals, normalized at $n_i$, suffices). Then

$\Phi(n,z,x)=\psi_i(n)[A(z,x)-h+\delta]_+$

is continuous, nonnegative and convex in $(z,x)$ at each fixed $n$. For any neighborhood of $X$, the compact complement of that neighborhood in $B_i$, if nonempty, has maximum strictly below $h$. Take $\delta>0$ smaller than the corresponding gap. Its positive set on the actual marked bundle lies in that neighborhood, and may be restricted to the $i$-facet at base $p$ since the base list is finite. It has positive facet measure: it includes a relative neighborhood of the vertex on that simplex facet, or the counted vertex for $d=1$. All integrals are finite by compactness.

Every strictly containing cap stays containing on a sufficiently small neighborhood of $X$, so the integral ratio is at least $W_{\rm strict}(X)$. If no boundary contains $X$, every cap's indicator is constant there, giving equality with $W(X)$. Taking the contrapositive gives the efficiency rigidity statement.
