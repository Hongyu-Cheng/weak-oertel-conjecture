## Statement

In $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2$ use planar area on integer-base fibers, with coordinates $(z,x,y)$. Consider the two convex bodies
$$
C_A=\{-1\le z\le1,\ |x|+|y|\le 2-|z|\},\qquad
C_B=\{-1\le z\le1,\ |x|+|y-z/2|\le1\}.
$$
Each has mixed centroid zero and attains maximal normalized closed-halfspace depth $1/2$ there. The two opposite horizontal caps are active there, every integer trace is proper, and their full coarea centroids are zero with opposite normals balancing at equal weights. The actual inverse square-root-tail lift for $y$ is jointly concave. At $z=-1,0,1$, the volume and horizontal coarea weight vectors are $(2,8,2)$ and $(2,4,2)$ for $C_A$, and $(2,2,2)$ and $(1,2,1)$ for $C_B$. For the normalized base laws the volume law is strictly below the section law in convex order for $C_A$ and strictly above for $C_B$. Their volume variances are $1/3$ and $2/3$, respectively, and both section variances are $1/2$.

## Proof

Both sets are compact, convex and full dimensional by the displayed convex inequalities. A diamond of radius $R$ shifted vertically by $h$ has area $2R^2$ and horizontal section at zero of length $2(R-|h|)$ when $|h|<R$. Its inverse square-root-tail lift is $h+R U_0(t/R)$ on $0\le t\le\sqrt2R$, where
$$
U_0(t)=\begin{cases}
1-t&0\le t\le1,\\
-1+\sqrt{2-t^2}&1\le t\le\sqrt2.
\end{cases}
$$
Indeed the unit diamond's width at level $s$ is $2(1-|s|)$ on $[-1,1]$. Integrating gives upper-tail area $(1-s)^2$ for $s\ge0$ and $2-(1+s)^2$ for $s\le0$ on that interval. The joint concavity for the real-base bodies follows from b170894d1a8b4d11 with $L=y$ and their actual fibers. The radii and shifts are $(1,2,1)$ and zero for $C_A$, and unit radii and shifts $z/2$ for $C_B$. They give the listed area and coarea weights because the vertical coefficient is one. At the cut the roots $c_z$ and inverse slope magnitudes $-\partial_t U(z,c_z)$ are $(1,2,1)$ and $(1,1,1)$ for $C_A$, and $(1/2,1,\sqrt7/2)$ and $(1,1,\sqrt7)$ for $C_B$, from the two branches of $U_0$.

Reflection at zero preserves both mixed volume measures and both full section measures, giving their centroids. Any closed halfspace through zero and its reflection have equal mass and together cover the support, even when a boundary fiber has positive mass. Thus each has at least half the mass. The horizontal plane has zero planar area on all fibers, so its opposite caps attain exactly half the mass and have identical coareas and opposite normals. At any other point one of the two horizontal halfspaces through it lies on the outward side of the zero level and has at most half the total mass. This proves global maximality at zero and exact active-cap and section stationarity.

On $\{-1,0,1\}$, a centered probability with total outer mass $p$ gives a convex test expectation $f(0)+p[(f(-1)+f(1))/2-f(0)]$, increasing in $p$ by convexity and strictly increasing for $f(z)=z^2$. That mass is the variance. Normalize the listed weights to obtain the asserted comparisons.
