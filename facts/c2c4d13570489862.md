## Statement

For every integer $d\ge2$ there is a compact convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ with exactly three positive integer fibers and three closed ambient caps of mixed mass strictly less than $\gamma_d M/2$, where $M$ is the total mixed $d$-volume and $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Each integer fiber has a cap capturing exactly fraction $\gamma_d$ of its volume, and the ordinary $d$-volume roots of the three strict remainders are concave over the entire real base interval. Yet the caps do not cover the mixed source.

## Proof

Write $\gamma=\gamma_d$, $a=1-\gamma$, $c=a^{1/d}$, $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, and $v=|T|=1/d!$. Then $0<c<1$ and $\gamma<1/2$ since $(1+1/d)^d\ge 2+(d-1)/(2d)>2$. For $z\in[-1,1]$ put $r(z)=1-(1-c)|z|$. The body $C=\{(z,x):|z|\le1,\ x\in r(z)T\}$ is compact and convex by the inequalities $x_i\ge0$ and $\sum_i x_i+(1-c)|z|\le1$, and it has nonempty interior. Its integer fibers are $cT,T,cT$, of respective volumes $av,v,av$, and $M=(3-2\gamma)v$.

Put $s(x)=\sum_i x_i$ and use the caps
$$
s(x)\ge c,\qquad s(x)\ge 1+(1-c^2)z,\qquad s(x)\ge 1-(1-c^2)z .
$$
For a fiber $RT$ and $0\le h\le R$, its part with $s<h$ has volume $h^dv$, since it is a simplex of radius $h$ up to a volume-null plane. The first cap has no positive endpoint mass and its middle mass is $(1-c^d)v=\gamma v$. The second cap has thresholds $c^2,1,2-c^2$ at $-1,0,1$. The first is strictly below $c$, the middle equals the support height, and $2-c^2>c$. Its only positive mass is therefore $(c^d-c^{2d})v=\gamma av$ on the left. Reflection gives the same mass for the third cap, on the right. The designated fractions are $\gamma$ on the left, middle and right. The largest cap mass $\gamma v$ is strictly less than $\gamma M/2$ because $\gamma<1/2$.

The strict remainder on a real fiber is the simplex of the smaller of its original radius and the cap threshold, up to its null boundary. For the first cap, the root divided by $v^{1/d}$ is $c$. For the second it equals $1+(1-c^2)z$ on $[-1,0]$ and $1-(1-c)z$ on $[0,1]$: on the left the threshold minus the radius is $c(1-c)z\le0$, while on the right the threshold is at least one. These two branches join and their slopes decrease, hence the root is concave. The third is the reflected profile. The sampled root rows are $(c,c,c)$, $(c^2,1,c)$ and $(c,1,c^2)$. All three thresholds are positive on the whole real base interval, so each point $(z,0)$ there misses all three caps, including at the three integer bases.

The coefficient $1-c^2$ gives the exact left threshold $c^2$; replacing it by $\gamma$ would instead give $c^d$ and endpoint captured mass $(a-a^d)v$, equal to $\gamma av$ only for $d=2$. At $d=4$, $a=369/625$ and $a-a^4-256/625=9048072704/152587890625>0$, so that substitution would even exceed the middle cap mass.
