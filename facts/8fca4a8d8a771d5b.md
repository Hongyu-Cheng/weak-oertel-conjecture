## Statement

Let $A,B$ be planar convex bodies of areas $0<a\le b$, let $M=(A+B)/2$ have centroid $g_M$, and put $r=|M|/a$. For $u\ne0$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, let $P$ be the sum of the areas of the endpoint caps with thresholds $u\cdot g_M+t$ in $A$ and $u\cdot g_M-t$ in $B$, both with upper orientation. If $w_A,w_B$ are the widths in direction $u$, then
$$
\frac Pa\ \ge\ \frac{(w_A+w_B)^2}{9(w_A^2+w_B^2)}
\ \ge\ \frac{2r}{9(2r-1)}.
$$

## Proof

Normalize $u$ to have norm one, also rescaling $t$. A homothety of a planar body from a minimum support point, of ratio $s\in[0,1]$, places fraction $s^2$ of its area below relative projection level $s$. Integrating the complementary distribution bounds its normalized centroid projection by $\int_0^1(1-s^2)\,ds=2/3$. In particular, the centroid of $M$ is at least one third of its width below the upper support.

The two upper cap heights in $A,B$ are $\Delta_A=h_A(u)-u\cdot g_M-t$ and $\Delta_B=h_B(u)-u\cdot g_M+t$, where $h_A,h_B$ are the support functions. Their sum is at least $(w_A+w_B)/3$ by the support identities for $M$. An upper-support homothety gives cap area at least the body's area times the squared height-to-width ratio clipped to $[0,1]$. If either endpoint cap is full, its area alone is at least $a$, while $(w_A+w_B)^2\le2(w_A^2+w_B^2)$. Otherwise put $x_D=(\Delta_D/w_D)_+$ for $D=A,B$. Then $P\ge a(x_A^2+x_B^2)$ and $w_Ax_A+w_Bx_B\ge(w_A+w_B)/3$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the first bound.

Let $\ell_A,\ell_B$ be the minimum projections and $f_A,f_B$ the lengths of the sections perpendicular to $u$. For every relative level $s\in[0,1]$, the section of $A+B$ at $\ell_A+\ell_B+(w_A+w_B)s$ contains the sum of the sections at $\ell_A+w_As$ and $\ell_B+w_Bs$. Their lengths add because the sections are intervals. Integrating gives
$$
4|M|=|A+B|\ \ge\ (w_A+w_B)(a/w_A+b/w_B)
\ \ge\ a(2+w_A/w_B+w_B/w_A).
$$
It follows that $r\ge1$ and $w_A/w_B+w_B/w_A\le4r-2$. In the first cap estimate, the width factor before division by nine equals $1+2/(w_A/w_B+w_B/w_A)$, which is at least $2r/(2r-1)$.
