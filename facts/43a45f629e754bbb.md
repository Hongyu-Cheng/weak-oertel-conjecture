## Statement

Let \(n,d\) be integers with \(d\ge n\ge1\), let \(R>0\), and let \(B\) be any real \(d\)-by-\(n\) matrix satisfying \(|B_{li}|\le1\) for every entry. For \(x\in\mathbb R^n\), define \(|x|=(|x_1|,\ldots,|x_n|)\), \(a(x)=R-\sum_i|x_i|\), and \(c_B(x)=B|x|\). Use exactly the body and original fiber normalizations of accepted fact `38622219f22cea58`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_B&=\left\{(x,y):\sum_i|x_i|\le R,\quad
c_B(x)-a(x)\mathbf1_d\le y\le c_B(x)+a(x)\mathbf1_d\right\},\\
S_B&=C_B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
D_+=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i|z_i|<R\right\},\\
a_z&=R-\sum_i|z_i|,\qquad
C_{B,z}=c_B(z)+[-a_z,a_z]^d\quad(z\in D_+),\\
w_z&=(2a_z)^d,\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z,\qquad p_z=w_z/W,\qquad
T_z=2a_z I_d\quad(z\in D_+).
\end{aligned}
\]

The vector inequalities are coordinatewise. The depth \(h_{S_B}\) is the locked depth, namely the infimum of \(\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap H)\) over all ambient closed halfspaces \(H\) containing the queried point. In the notation of accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3`, our \(C_{B,z}\) is its \(C_{\mathbf z}\) for \(C=C_B\), our \(w_z,W,D_+\) are its actual fiber volumes, total volume, and positive integer support, and its \(h_C\) is exactly \(h_{S_B}\).

Set \(N_0=64\cdot10^{10}\), and retain the cube-cap constant of accepted fact `abd45e8b46c96d55` without changing it:

\[
\delta=\delta(5/2,1/4)
=\frac{e^{-4}}{96}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{225}{2}e^8-60\sqrt6\,e^6\right),
\qquad
c_0=\min\!\left\{
\frac{\delta}{4},\,
\frac{1}{24\sqrt{15}(\log2)(N_0+5)},\,
\frac{1}{2e}
\right\}.
\]

Then \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), and for every \(n,d,R,B\) as above there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S_B\), fixed before any containing closed halfspace is chosen, such that
\(h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c_0 2^{-n}W\). Here \(W=\mathcal H_d(S_B)>0\), and \(c_0\) is independent of \(n,d,R,B\).

More precisely, when \(n\ge N_0\), define exactly as in accepted fact `38622219f22cea58`
\(\mathcal Z_{d-2}=\sum_{z\in D_+}a_z^{d-2}\),
\(q_z^{(d-2)}=a_z^{d-2}/\mathcal Z_{d-2}\), and
\(\mu=\mathbb E_{q^{(d-2)}}|Z|\). Then the fixed point \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,B\mu)\) satisfies the stronger conclusion
\(h_{S_B}((0_n,B\mu))\ge(5\delta/16)W\).

## Proof

First check the body and volume hypotheses for every \(d\ge n\ge1\), including \(n<5\). For row \(l\), the upper and lower constraints defining \(C_B\) can be written as
\(y_l\le R-\sum_i(1-B_{li})|x_i|\) and
\(y_l\ge-R+\sum_i(1+B_{li})|x_i|\).
Every coefficient in these two sums is nonnegative. The first right side is concave and the second is convex, so their hypographs and epigraphs are convex. Together with the convex constraint \(\sum_i|x_i|\le R\), this proves convexity of \(C_B\). The constraints are closed and imply \(|x_i|\le R\) and \(-R\le y_l\le R\), so \(C_B\) is compact. All inequalities are strict at \((0_n,0_d)\); continuity therefore supplies an ambient open neighborhood of that point in \(C_B\). Thus \(C_B\) is a full-dimensional convex body.

Its integer fiber at \(z\) is empty when \(\sum_i|z_i|>R\), is the cube \(C_{B,z}\) of volume \((2a_z)^d\) when \(\sum_i|z_i|<R\), and is the singleton \(\{c_B(z)\}\) when \(\sum_i|z_i|=R\). The last fibers have zero \(d\)-dimensional volume because \(d\ge1\). There are finitely many nonempty integer fibers, and \(0_n\in D_+\) has volume \((2R)^d>0\). Consequently \(S_B\ne\varnothing\) and \(W>0\). The original-fiber summation and depth normalization of accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3`, applied with \(C=C_B\) and the exact aliases stated above, give for every ambient closed halfspace \(H\)

\[
\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap H)
=\sum_{z\in D_+}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{B,z}\cap H_z),
\qquad
\mathcal H_d(S_B)=W,
\]

where \(H_z=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\). Boundary fibers contribute zero to both identities. All weights used below remain these actual volumes.

Now assume \(n\ge N_0\). In particular \(d\ge n\ge5\), so all conclusions of accepted fact `38622219f22cea58` apply. Its partition function \(\mathcal Z_{d-2}\) is positive, and its \(\mu\) has exactly the definition in the statement. Use its unchanged fit events

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_B&=\left\{z\in D_+:\|B(|z|-\mu)\|_\infty\le a_z/2\right\},\\
G_B&=\left\{z\in D_+:\|B(|z|-\mu)\|_2^2\le25a_z^2\right\},
\qquad E=F_B\cap G_B.
\end{aligned}
\]

That accepted fact gives
\(p(E)\ge[1-n/(8d)-16\cdot10^{10}n^2/d^3]_+\). Since \(d\ge n\) and \(n\ge64\cdot10^{10}\), the estimates
\(n/(8d)\le1/8\) and
\(16\cdot10^{10}n^2/d^3\le16\cdot10^{10}/n\le1/4\)
prove \(p(E)\ge5/8\). This is a fraction of the original \(p_z=w_z/W\) mass, so \(\sum_{z\in E}w_z\ge(5/8)W\).

The same accepted fact proves that \((0_n,B\mu)\in S_B\). To check its legality explicitly, \(|B_{li}|\le1\) and \(\mu_i\ge0\) imply
\(|(B\mu)_l|\le\sum_i\mu_i=\mathbb E_{q^{(d-2)}}\sum_i|Z_i|<R\),
since every point of the finite support \(D_+\) has \(\sum_i|z_i|<R\). Hence \(B\mu\in[-R,R]^d=C_{B,0_n}\). Fix this point now, independently of every halfspace.

For \(z\in E\), put \(v_z=T_z^{-1}(B\mu-c_B(z))\). This is the normalized displacement denoted \(v\) in the statement of accepted fact `abd45e8b46c96d55`, not a halfspace normal. Indeed, \(T_z=2a_z I_d\) and the definitions of \(F_B,G_B\) give
\(\|v_z\|_\infty\le1/4\) and \(\|v_z\|_2\le5/2\).
Applying that fact with \(L=5/2\) and \(\epsilon=1/4\) proves that every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\) containing \(v_z\) has uniform \([-1/2,1/2]^d\) probability at least the stated \(\delta\). This applies to equality in either norm bound. In particular \(v_z\) lies in that cube, so \(B\mu\in C_{B,z}\) on all these fibers.

The sets \(D_+,F_B,G_B,E\), and the actual weights \(w_z\), are invariant under \(z\mapsto-z\), because all their defining expressions in \(z\) depend only on \(|z|\). For any \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\), reflection maps \(\{z\in E:\alpha\cdot z\ge0\}\) bijectively onto \(\{z\in E:\alpha\cdot z\le0\}\) and preserves every weight. Their union is \(E\), and their overlap has nonnegative weight. Therefore

\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in E\\\alpha\cdot z\ge0}}w_z
\ge\frac12\sum_{z\in E}w_z
\ge\frac5{16}W.
\]

This also holds when \(\alpha=0\), when the left side is the entire \(E\) weight. The nonstrict sign includes every point on the base boundary.

Let an arbitrary proper closed ambient halfspace containing the fixed point be
\(H=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\ge t\}\), with \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\). Its containment condition gives \(t\le\beta\cdot B\mu\). Thus it contains the parallel closed halfspace through that point,
\(H^0=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot(y-B\mu)\ge0\}\).

Fix \(z\in E\) with \(\alpha\cdot z\ge0\). If \(\beta\ne0\), the fiber section \(H^0_z\) contains \(B\mu\). Under the affine map \(u\mapsto c_B(z)+T_z u\), its preimage is the proper closed halfspace
\(\{u:(T_z^{\mathsf T}\beta)\cdot(u-v_z)\ge-\alpha\cdot z\}\),
which contains \(v_z\) and has nonzero normal because \(a_z>0\). The just-applied cube theorem gives probability at least \(\delta\) under the original uniform cube law. The Jacobian of this map is \(\det T_z=(2a_z)^d=w_z\); the unit cube has volume one. Consequently
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{B,z}\cap H^0_z)\ge\delta w_z\).
If \(\beta=0\), the condition \(\alpha\cdot z\ge0\) makes \(H^0_z=\mathbb R^d\), so its actual fiber volume is \(w_z\ge\delta w_z\). Here \(0<\delta<1\) follows directly from its displayed formula. No mass is discarded because of a halfspace boundary: all sections and base signs are closed.

Summing these actual fiber estimates and using \(H^0\subseteq H\) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap H)
&\ge\sum_{\substack{z\in E\\\alpha\cdot z\ge0}}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{B,z}\cap H^0_z)\\
&\ge\delta\sum_{\substack{z\in E\\\alpha\cdot z\ge0}}w_z
\ge\frac{5\delta}{16}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The same inequality holds for the full ambient space, if it is included in the halfspace convention, since its mixed volume is \(W\). Taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves
\(h_{S_B}((0_n,B\mu))\ge(5\delta/16)W\).
Neither the reflection argument nor the summation assumes independence of the centroid coordinates. The only continuous law used in each normalized fiber is its actual uniform cube law, exactly as required by accepted fact `abd45e8b46c96d55`.

It remains to treat \(1\le n<N_0\), with arbitrary \(d\ge n\). Apply equation (9), the actual-body conclusion of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, to this same original convex body \(C=C_B\), with \(S=S_B\) and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S_B)\). Its hypotheses were checked in the first two paragraphs, with no restriction \(n\ge5\). That conclusion selects a point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S_B\) before the arbitrary containing halfspace, satisfying

\[
h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{1}{24\sqrt{15}(\log2)(n+5)}\,2^{-n}W
\ge\frac{1}{24\sqrt{15}(\log2)(N_0+5)}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

This application uses only its stated actual-body conclusion; its internal potential notation is not used, and \(B\) throughout this packet remains the given matrix.

Every member of the minimum defining \(c_0\) is positive, and \(c_0\le1/(2e)<1/e\). For \(n<N_0\), the preceding bound is at least \(c_0 2^{-n}W\). For \(n\ge N_0\), we have
\(c_0 2^{-n}\le c_0\le\delta/4\le5\delta/16\),
so the stronger large-dimension bound also gives the required inequality. The point in each branch is fixed before all containing closed halfspaces. All arguments use only strict positive-support sums and the exact radius \(R\); integer boundary fibers have already been accounted for by their zero volume. Thus the conclusion holds for every \(R>0\), including integral radii and arbitrarily small radii, with one absolute \(c_0\) for the stated class.
