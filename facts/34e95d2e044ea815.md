## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain exactly the actual-body notation of accepted facts 03f071403cdfc69f and d887d95075dfcc06: \(C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\), \(T=2^{-n}W\), and \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero.

Suppose \(M_1,\ldots,M_n\) are arbitrary nonnegative integers, \(b,c\in\mathbb R_{>0}^n\), and \(g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) is finite-valued, nonincreasing, and globally log-concave, with \(g(0)>0\). Here log-concavity means \(g((1-\theta)a+\theta a')\ge g(a)^{1-\theta}g(a')^\theta\) for every \(a,a'\ge0\) and \(0<\theta<1\), with a positive power of zero equal to zero. Assume these data specify the entire actual integer table by
\[
w_z=
\begin{cases}
g(\max\{b\cdot z,c\cdot z\}),&z\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad 0\le z_i\le M_i\text{ for every }i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
No continuity of \(g\), rationality of the rows, or full-dimensionality of a coordinate slice is assumed.

Put \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). For \(j\in D\) and finite \(t\ge0\), retain the original threshold family
\(F_j(t)=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ w_{j+u}\ge e^{-t}w_j\}\).
This is the set-valued \(F_j(t)\) of accepted fact 102f49bbb2cedb49 and the fixed-center \(F_t\) of d887d95075dfcc06; it is distinct from the unparameterized scalar all-pair score \(F_j\) discussed in 03f071403cdfc69f. All coordinate inequalities are componentwise.

At each fixed positive center retain \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), with the original weights and the center dependence of \(\alpha\) suppressed exactly as in those accepted facts. Retain also
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
&& (L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Every displacement sum is oriented and includes zero. Every difference set counts each distinct integer difference once.

Then the corner maximum is attained and
\[
\max_{\substack{j\in D\\t\ge0}}
\frac{w_je^{-t}|F_j(t)-F_j(t)|}{1+2t}
\ge e^{-76}T,
\qquad K(w)\ge e^{-76}T.
\tag{3}
\]
There exist a positive center \(j\), a finite \(t\ge0\), and one \(x^\star\in C_j\), all selected before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
h_S((j,x^\star))
\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(2t)}{12(1+2t)}
\ge\frac{e^{-76}}{12}T
=\frac{e^{-76}}{12\,2^n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{4}
\]
This holds simultaneously for every closed ambient halfspace containing that point, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing whole fibers.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. The table in (1) is coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative integer orthant, including zeros. Indeed, if \(0\le u\le v\) and \(v\) is inside the capped box, then \(u\) is also inside and both row values at \(u\) are no greater. If \(v\) is outside the box, its weight is zero. Also \(w_0=g(0)>0\).

We first establish a finite-coordinate estimate. For an integer \(m\ge2\), put \(h=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\ge1\), and define
\[
\mathcal M_m(s)=\max_{0\le k\le h,\ k\in\mathbb Z}(2k+1)e^{-sk},
\qquad
Z_m(v)=\sum_{a=0}^{m}e^{-va}.
\tag{5}
\]
We claim that \(2\mathcal M_m(s)/Z_m(3s/4)>11/10\) for every \(s\ge0\). The unbounded function \(\mathcal M(s)=\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}(2k+1)e^{-sk}\), defined for \(s>0\), has exactly the meaning used in accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790. Its equation (9) proves
\(2(1-e^{-3s/4})\mathcal M(s)>11/10\) for every \(s>0\).

Set \(s_0=\log((2h+3)/(2h+1))>0\). The successive ratios of the terms defining the unbounded maximum are \(e^{-s}(2k+3)/(2k+1)\), strictly decreasing with \(k\). For \(s\ge s_0\), the ratio at \(k=h\) is at most one, so an unbounded maximizer is attained at some \(k\le h\). At \(s=s_0\), the terms at \(h\) and \(h+1\) tie, and the term at \(h\) still attains the maximum. Thus \(\mathcal M_m(s)=\mathcal M(s)\) for \(s\ge s_0\). Since \(Z_m(3s/4)\le(1-e^{-3s/4})^{-1}\), the claimed estimate follows on this range.

For \(0\le s\le s_0\), all successive ratios before \(h\) are greater than one, so \(\mathcal M_m(s)=(2h+1)e^{-sh}\). The logarithm of the desired ratio on this interval is
\(\log(2(2h+1))-sh-\log Z_m(3s/4)\).
It is concave: twice differentiating the last logarithm gives \(9/16\) times the variance of the integer \(a\) under probabilities proportional to \(e^{-3sa/4}\), hence a nonnegative number. A concave function on a closed interval is bounded below by the smaller endpoint value. At \(s=s_0\) the ratio is greater than \(11/10\) by the preceding paragraph. At \(s=0\), it is \(2(2h+1)/(m+1)\), which equals two for \(m=2h\) and equals \(2-1/(h+1)\ge3/2\) for \(m=2h+1\). This proves, including zero and the tie,
\[
\frac{2\mathcal M_m(s)}{Z_m(3s/4)}>\frac{11}{10}
\qquad(m\ge2,\ s\ge0).
\tag{6}
\]

We next prove a scalar capped counting lemma in dimension \(r\ge1\). In this lemma alone, \(M_1,\ldots,M_r\ge2\) and \(b,c\in\mathbb R_{>0}^r\) are arbitrary, and \(R\ge0\) is finite. Define the capped analogue of the auxiliary set in accepted fact 102f49bbb2cedb49 by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D&=\{z\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le z_i\le M_i,\ b\cdot z\le R,\ c\cdot z\le R\},\\
A_j&=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le u\le j,\ j+u\in\mathcal D\}
\qquad(j\in\mathcal D),\\
Q_r^{(2)}&=\frac1{2\cdot3^6}\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r
\exp\left(-\frac83(\sqrt r+1)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The coefficient \(Q_r^{(2)}\) is exactly the coefficient of 102f49bbb2cedb49, with dimension \(r\). We will find \(j\in\mathcal D\) satisfying \(j_i\le h_i:=\lfloor M_i/2\rfloor\) and
\[
|A_j-A_j|\ge Q_r^{(2)}\,2^{-r}|\mathcal D|.
\tag{8}
\]
The caps make \(\mathcal D\) finite, and zero belongs to it even when \(R=0\).

Use the same function \(\ell\) as in the proof of 102f49bbb2cedb49: it is the continuous piecewise-linear interpolation of \(\ell(k)=\log(2k+1)\) at nonnegative integers. Its slopes \(\log((2k+3)/(2k+1))\) are positive and decreasing. Hence \(\ell\) is increasing and concave, with all slopes at most \(\log3\). Equation (12) of accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790 gives \(k\log((2k+1)/(2k-1))\le\log3<4/3\) for every integer \(k\ge1\). On each interval \((k-1,k)\), this implies \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\).

For any nonempty \(E\) in the capped integer box, let \(\bar z=|E|^{-1}\sum_{z\in E}z\) and \(x_i=\min\{3\bar z_i/4,h_i\}\). If \(x_i<h_i\), put \(k_i'=\lfloor x_i\rfloor\) and \(s_i=\log((2k_i'+3)/(2k_i'+1))>0\). Here \(k_i',k_i'+1\le h_i\). The decreasing slopes imply that these two integers maximize \((2k+1)e^{-s_i k}\), including over the capped range. Affinity on their interval gives \(e^{\ell(x_i)-s_ix_i}=\mathcal M_{M_i}(s_i)\). If \(x_i=h_i\), set \(s_i=0\); then the same equality holds because \(\mathcal M_{M_i}(0)=2h_i+1\). In either case \(s_ix_i=(3/4)s_i\bar z_i\), also when \(x_i=0\), when \(x_i\) is an integer, or when the clipped mean equals \(h_i\) exactly.

The formula
\(\pi(z)=\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{e^{-(3s_i/4)z_i}}{Z_{M_i}(3s_i/4)}\)
is a strictly positive probability mass function on the entire capped integer box. A coordinate with \(s_i=0\) is uniform on its finite chain. Since its mass on \(E\) is at most one, finite arithmetic-geometric mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{|E|}
&\ge\frac1{|E|}\sum_{z\in E}\pi(z)\\
&\ge\left(\prod_{z\in E}\pi(z)\right)^{1/|E|}
=\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{e^{-s_ix_i}}{Z_{M_i}(3s_i/4)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Multiplying by \(\exp(\sum_i\ell(x_i))\) and using (6) yields
\[
\exp\left(\sum_i\ell(x_i)\right)
\ge\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r2^{-r}|E|.
\tag{10}
\]
The clipping decreases each coordinate, so for \(E=\mathcal D\) both rows satisfy \(a\cdot x\le3R/4\), where \(a\in\{b,c\}\).

We use the rounding observation proved as equation (10) of 102f49bbb2cedb49, with its unchanged \(\ell\). Given a nonnegative real vector \(x\) and \(m'\) linear forms with nonnegative coefficients, there is an integer \(p\) in \(\prod_i[\lfloor x_i\rfloor,\lceil x_i\rceil]\) such that all the specified forms at \(p\) are at most their values at \(x\) and \(\sum_i\ell(p_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-m'\log3\). For completeness, maximize the affine function \(\sum_i\ell(y_i)\) on this rectangle subject to the form bounds. The rectangle contains the feasible point \(x\). If a maximizing point has more than \(m'\) noninteger coordinates, a nonzero direction supported on them annihilates all the forms. Small moves of either sign stay feasible, so optimality forces zero objective slope. Move until a coordinate reaches an integer endpoint and repeat. This produces a maximizing point with at most \(m'\) noninteger coordinates. Rounding those down preserves all form bounds and loses at most \(\log3\) per coordinate. In particular, if \(x_i\le h_i\) with integer \(h_i\), the entire rounding rectangle stays below \(h_i\); no cap needs to be listed among the forms.

Apply this observation to the clipped vector for \(E=\mathcal D\), using the two row forms. It gives an integer \(k\) with \(0\le k_i\le h_i\),
\[
a\cdot k\le\frac{3R}{4}\quad(a\in\{b,c\}),
\qquad
\sum_i\ell(k_i)\ge\sum_i\ell(x_i)-2\log3.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(k=0\), choose \(j=0\). The product lower bound (17) below then follows directly from (10)--(11). Otherwise both \(\beta_a:=a\cdot k\) are positive. Define
\[
\theta_i=\max\left\{\frac{b_ik_i}{\beta_b},\frac{c_ik_i}{\beta_c}\right\},
\qquad
\widetilde k_i=\frac{k_i}{1+(\sqrt r+1)\theta_i}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then \(0\le\theta_i\le1\), \(\sum_i\theta_i\le2\), and \(0\le\widetilde k_i\le k_i\le h_i\). For each row \(a\), put
\(\delta_a=\beta_a-a\cdot\widetilde k\) and
\(\zeta_a^2=\sum_i a_i^2k_i\widetilde k_i\), with \(\zeta_a\ge0\). These have exactly the meanings of the corresponding quantities in the two-row shrink argument of 102f49bbb2cedb49. Directly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_a
&=\sum_i\frac{a_ik_i(\sqrt r+1)\theta_i}{1+(\sqrt r+1)\theta_i}
\ge\frac{\sqrt r+1}{\beta_a}\zeta_a^2,\\
\beta_a-\delta_a
&=a\cdot\widetilde k
\le\sqrt r\left(\sum_i a_i^2\widetilde k_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\sqrt r\,\zeta_a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The first inequality uses \(\theta_i\ge a_ik_i/\beta_a\); the last uses \(\widetilde k_i\le k_i\). They imply \(\delta_a\ge\zeta_a\). This is immediate for \(\zeta_a=0\); if instead \(\delta_a<\zeta_a\), the second line gives \(\beta_a<(\sqrt r+1)\zeta_a\), and the first then gives \(\delta_a>\zeta_a\), a contradiction.

Round \(\widetilde k\) using the four nonnegative forms \(b\cdot y\), \(c\cdot y\), \(\sum_i b_i^2k_i y_i\), and \(\sum_i c_i^2k_i y_i\), holding \(k\) fixed. The resulting integer \(j\) has \(0\le j_i\le k_i\le h_i\), because every upper endpoint \(\lceil\widetilde k_i\rceil\) is at most the integer \(k_i\). It satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
a\cdot j&\le a\cdot\widetilde k,\\
\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2&\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i j_i
\le\sum_i a_i^2k_i\widetilde k_i=\zeta_a^2,\\
\sum_i\ell(j_i)&\ge\sum_i\ell(\widetilde k_i)-4\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (11), (13), and (14) gives, for both rows,
\[
R\ge\frac43\beta_a
\ge\frac43\left(a\cdot j+
\left(\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\right)^{1/2}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
This also holds when \(k=0\) and \(j=0\), and it puts \(j\) in \(\mathcal D\).

For each positive \(k_i\), \(\widetilde k_i>0\). Integrating \(u\ell'(u)\le\log3\) over the intervening finitely many affine intervals yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\bigl(\ell(k_i)-\ell(\widetilde k_i)\bigr)
&\le\log3\sum_{i:k_i>0}\log\frac{k_i}{\widetilde k_i}\\
&=\log3\sum_i\log(1+(\sqrt r+1)\theta_i)\\
&\le2(\sqrt r+1)\log3
<\frac83(\sqrt r+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Zero coordinates contribute zero. By (10), (11), (14), and (16),
\[
\prod_i(2j_i+1)
\ge\frac1{3^6}\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^r
\exp\left(-\frac83(\sqrt r+1)\right)2^{-r}|\mathcal D|.
\tag{17}
\]
For \(k=0\), (10)--(11) give the stronger estimate
\(1\ge3^{-2}(11/10)^r2^{-r}|\mathcal D|\), which proves (17) for that case too.

At the selected center, \(2j_i\le M_i\). Therefore for every \(u\) with \(0\le u\le j\), the point \(j+u\) automatically satisfies all the coordinate caps. Write \(v_i^+=\max\{v_i,0\}\) and \(v_i^-=\max\{-v_i,0\}\). The exact difference-set identity at this center is
\[
A_j-A_j=
\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^r:
|v_i|\le j_i\text{ for every }i,\quad
a\cdot v^+\le R-a\cdot j,\quad
a\cdot v^-\le R-a\cdot j
\text{ for }a\in\{b,c\}\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, from \(v=u-u'\) with \(u,u'\in A_j\), one has \(v^+\le u\), \(v^-\le u'\), and all the displayed inequalities follow. Conversely, the displayed inequalities give the row constraints for \(j+v^+\) and \(j+v^-\), and these two points satisfy the caps since \(j_i+v_i^\pm\le2j_i\le M_i\) for every \(i\). Thus \(v^\pm\in A_j\) and \(v=v^+-v^-\). The identity concerns distinct differences, not their representations.

Let the \(V_i\) be independent and uniform on the integers \(-j_i,\ldots,j_i\). Equations (13)--(14) in accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790 give, for exactly this law,
\(\mathbb E V_i^+\le j_i/3\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(V_i^+)\le2j_i^2/9\), including \(j_i=0\). Reflection gives the same bounds for \(V_i^-\). Thus for each row \(a\), the two sums \(a\cdot V^\pm\) have common mean \(\mu_a\le a\cdot j/3\) and common variance \(\sigma_a^2\le(2/9)\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\). They need not be independent of each other or of the sums for the other row. Equation (15) gives
\[
R-a\cdot j
\ge\frac13a\cdot j+\frac43\left(\sum_i a_i^2j_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\ge\mu_a+\sqrt8\,\sigma_a.
\tag{19}
\]
For each of the four sums having positive variance, its probability of exceeding \(\mu_a+\sqrt8\,\sigma_a\) is at most \(1/8\) by the second-moment inequality. A sum of variance zero equals its mean almost surely and cannot violate (19). The union bound therefore gives probability at least \(1/2\) that all four row-and-sign inequalities in (18) hold. The vector \(V\) is uniform on a box of \(\prod_i(2j_i+1)\) distinct integer vectors, so \(|A_j-A_j|\ge\frac12\prod_i(2j_i+1)\). With (17) this proves (8).

This argument used only the two row constraints and then four nonnegative forms. Its rounding rectangles remained within the integer half-caps throughout. It applies when the rows coincide, when resource bounds or interpolation slopes tie, when some selected coordinates vanish, and when \(R=0\); the latter case has \(\mathcal D=\{0\}\) and is already covered by \(k=0\).

We now turn this scalar count into the weighted bound without assuming any convex body for a lower-dimensional coordinate restriction. Put \(I=\{i:M_i\ge2\}\), \(r=|I|\), and \(q=n-r\). Let \(\iota:\mathbb Z^I\to\mathbb Z^n\) insert zeros in all coordinates outside \(I\). Define the scalar restricted table \(\bar w_y=w_{\iota(y)}\) and its total \(\bar W=\sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^I}\bar w_y\). This is an explicit restriction of the original table, not a replacement meaning for \(w\) or \(W\). For each fixed \(y\), every allowed assignment in the other coordinates has weight at most \(w_{\iota(y)}\) by monotonicity. There are \(\prod_{i\notin I}(M_i+1)\le2^q\) such assignments. Consequently
\[
W\le2^q\bar W,
\qquad 2^{-r}\bar W\ge2^{-n}W.
\tag{20}
\]
All original fibers, including those with a nonzero removed coordinate, remain in \(W\).

If \(r=0\), \(\bar W=w_0\), and (20) gives \(w_0\ge2^{-n}W\). At the original center \(j=0\), \(F_0(0)=\{0\}\), so its original corner value is \(w_0\). This already proves the corner lower bound, with \(t=0\). We defer its original-body depth consequence to the final common step below.

Suppose henceforth \(r\ge1\), and write \(I=\{i_1<\cdots<i_r\}\) to identify \(\mathbb Z^I\) with \(\mathbb Z^r\). Define \(\bar M_a=M_{i_a}\), \((b_I)_a=b_{i_a}\), and \((c_I)_a=c_{i_a}\) for \(1\le a\le r\). The restricted rows are positive, and the scalar table is exactly
\(\bar w_y=g(\max\{b_I\cdot y,c_I\cdot y\})\) for \(0\le y_i\le\bar M_i\), with zero elsewhere. There is no offset, and all its caps are at least two. We use only this scalar formula. Put \(\bar D=\{y:\bar w_y>0\}\), a finite set containing zero.

For \(y\in\bar D\), coordinatewise floor-halving stays in the capped box, and both row values at \(\lfloor y/2\rfloor\) are at most half their values at \(y\). The monotonicity and log-concavity of \(g\) therefore give
\[
\bar w_{\lfloor y/2\rfloor}
\ge g\left(\frac12\max\{b_I\cdot y,c_I\cdot y\}\right)
\ge\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}.
\tag{21}
\]
Every image has at most \(2^r\) preimages, of the form \(2u+\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r\). Summing (21) gives \(\sum_{y\in\bar D}\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\le2^r\bar W\).

Set \(J=\lceil2(r+1)\log2\rceil\), so \(1\le J\le2(r+1)\). At positive sites with \(\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-J}\), the inequality \(\bar w_y\le e^{-J/2}\sqrt{w_0\bar w_y}\) shows that their total weight is at most \(e^{-J/2}2^r\bar W\le\bar W/2\). The \(J\) bands
\(\{y\in\bar D:w_0e^{-(k+1)}<\bar w_y\le w_0e^{-k}\}\), for \(k=0,\ldots,J-1\), thus contain a band of mass at least \(\bar W/(2J)\). The strict lower and weak upper endpoints include every boundary weight in exactly one band. Fix such a \(k\), put \(t_0=k+1\), and define \(\bar D_{t_0}=\{y:\bar w_y\ge w_0e^{-t_0}\}\). The selected band lies in this prefix and its weights are at most \(ew_0e^{-t_0}\). Hence
\[
w_0e^{-t_0}|\bar D_{t_0}|\ge\frac{\bar W}{2eJ},
\qquad 1\le t_0\le J.
\tag{22}
\]

Define \(R=\max_{y\in\bar D_{t_0}}\max\{b_I\cdot y,c_I\cdot y\}\), which is finite and attained. Then
\[
\bar D_{t_0}
=\{y\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le y_i\le\bar M_i,\ b_I\cdot y\le R,\ c_I\cdot y\le R\}.
\tag{23}
\]
The forward inclusion follows from the definition of \(R\). For the reverse one, choose \(y_0\) in the prefix attaining \(R\). Any point of the capped box satisfying the two row bounds has weight at least \(g(R)=\bar w_{y_0}\ge w_0e^{-t_0}\). This uses a maximum of a finite lattice set, so no continuity or real superlevel-endpoint assumption is needed.

Apply (8) to (23) with caps \(\bar M_i\) and the restricted rows. It supplies a center \(p\in\bar D_{t_0}\), with \(p_i\le\lfloor\bar M_i/2\rfloor\), such that
\(|A_p-A_p|\ge Q_r^{(2)}2^{-r}|\bar D_{t_0}|\), where
\(A_p=\{u\in\mathbb Z^r:0\le u\le p,\ p+u\in\bar D_{t_0}\}\).
Put \(\tau=\log(\bar w_p/(w_0e^{-t_0}))\). Since \(w_0e^{-t_0}\le\bar w_p\le w_0\), one has \(0\le\tau\le t_0\le J\) and the exact threshold identity \(\bar w_pe^{-\tau}=w_0e^{-t_0}\).

Now choose the original center \(j=\iota(p)\). Every original displacement \(0\le u\le j\) has zero coordinates outside \(I\). Consequently the original family satisfies the exact identity \(F_j(\tau)=\iota(A_p)\), including all threshold ties, and the injective linear map \(\iota\) preserves difference-set cardinalities. Also \(w_j=\bar w_p\). Thus (22) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_je^{-\tau}|F_j(\tau)-F_j(\tau)|}{1+2\tau}
&\ge\frac{Q_r^{(2)}2^{-r}w_0e^{-t_0}|\bar D_{t_0}|}{1+2J}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_r^{(2)}}{2eJ(1+2J)}\,2^{-r}\bar W\\
&\ge\frac{Q_r^{(2)}}{20e(r+1)^2}\,2^{-r}\bar W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Here \(J\le2(r+1)\) and \(1+2J\le5(r+1)\).

The constant calculation is the same numerical one as in equation (28) of accepted fact 102f49bbb2cedb49, now at the retained dimension \(r\). Explicitly, \(\log(11/10)>1/11\) by integration. For real \(x\ge1\), the derivative of \(\sqrt x-\log(x+1)\) is \((\sqrt x-1)^2/[2\sqrt x(x+1)]\ge0\), and its value at one is positive; hence \(\log(r+1)\le\sqrt r\). The elementary estimates \(\log3<4/3\) and \(\log40<4\) are also proved in accepted fact 3a39f48aba7a2790. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{Q_r^{(2)}}{20e(r+1)^2}
&=r\log\frac{11}{10}-\frac83\sqrt r-2\log(r+1)
-\frac{11}{3}-6\log3-\log40\\
&>\frac r{11}-\frac{14}{3}\sqrt r-\frac{47}{3}\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\frac r{11}}-\frac{7\sqrt{11}}3\right)^2-\frac{680}{9}
>-76.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(680/9<76\), (20), (24), and (25) prove the original corner lower bound in (3). In particular, the dimension reduction preserved the required original normalization \(2^{-n}W\).
