## Statement

Retain the complete canonical hard-branch hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted facts 2fe46f47d19112ea, bf0d5c250b8f688c, and d01de728d8b5e33c. Fix any \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\), and abbreviate

\[
 Y:=\widehat Y(v),
 \qquad
 w_y:=\widehat\tau_y(v),
 \qquad
 W(A):=\sum_{y\in A}w_y
 \quad(\varnothing\ne A\subseteq Y).
\tag{1}
\]

For every such \(A\), retain the canonical ranks

\[
 \rho_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A),
 \qquad
 r_A^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s(y):y\in A\},
\tag{2}
\]

and the exact score

\[
 \Lambda_v(A)
 =2^{n-\rho_A}\frac{W(A)}{r_A^c+1}.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(\varnothing\ne B\subseteq A\), then

\[
 r_B^c\leq r_A^c.
\tag{4}
\]

If in addition \(\rho_A\geq1\) and \(\rho_B=\rho_A-1\), then the exact deterministic one-step identity is

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\Lambda_v(B)}{\Lambda_v(A)}
 =2\frac{W(B)}{W(A)}
 \frac{r_A^c+1}{r_B^c+1}.}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently, for any probability distribution \(K_A\) supported on the nonempty subsets \(B\subseteq A\) with \(\rho_B=\rho_A-1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}[\Lambda_v(B)]
 \geq\Lambda_v(A)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}
 \left[\frac{W(B)}{r_B^c+1}\right]
 \geq\frac{W(A)}{2(r_A^c+1)}.}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, the scalar condition

\[
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}[W(B)]\geq\frac12W(A)
\tag{7}
\]

is sufficient for nonnegative \(\Lambda_v\)-drift, but it is not available throughout the canonical descent. More precisely, there exists a nonempty proper subset

\[
 \boxed{A_\star\subsetneq Y}
\tag{8}
\]

such that

\[
 \boxed{\rho_{A_\star}\geq1,}
\tag{9}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 2^{n-\rho_{A_\star}}W(A_\star)
 >\frac{7Q}{240},}
\tag{10}
\]

and every nonempty rank-lowering subset \(B\subseteq A_\star\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_B<\rho_{A_\star}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 W(B)<\frac12W(A_\star).}
\tag{11}
\]

The stopped subset necessarily has

\[
 \boxed{r_{A_\star}^c\geq4.}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus every kernel \(K_{A_\star}\) supported on rank-one restrictions has expected retained mass strictly below \(W(A_\star)/2\). Equation (6) remains the exact criterion: a kernel can have nonnegative \(\Lambda_v\)-drift at \(A_\star\) only if the smaller denominators \(r_B^c+1\) compensate for this strict mass deficit. No existence of such a residual-rank-compensated kernel, and no terminal rank-density subset, is asserted.

## Proof

For (4), let \(s_A\) attain the minimum in (2) for \(A\). Its restriction to \(\operatorname{aff}(B)\) is affine, and

\[
 \operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-s_A(y):y\in B\}
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-s_A(y):y\in A\}.
\tag{13}
\]

The restricted map is an admissible competitor in the definition of \(r_B^c\), proving (4).

If \(\rho_B=\rho_A-1\), divide (3) for \(B\) by (3) for \(A\). Since all weights in \(Y\) are positive, both scores are positive, and direct cancellation gives (5). Averaging the undistributed form of (5),

\[
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}[\Lambda_v(B)]
 =2^{n-\rho_A+1}
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}
 \left[\frac{W(B)}{r_B^c+1}\right],
\tag{14}
\]

and comparing with (3) proves the equivalence (6). By (4), one has \(r_B^c+1\leq r_A^c+1\) on the support of \(K_A\). Hence (7) implies

\[
 \mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}
 \left[\frac{W(B)}{r_B^c+1}\right]
 \geq
 \frac{\mathbb E_{B\sim K_A}[W(B)]}{r_A^c+1}
 \geq\frac{W(A)}{2(r_A^c+1)},
\tag{15}
\]

which proves the stated sufficient condition.

We now construct the stopped proper subset. Accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c gives

\[
 |Y|\geq n+4\geq6,
 \qquad
 W(Y)=2\widehat P(v)>\frac{7Q}{200}.
\tag{16}
\]

Choose \(y_0\in Y\) of minimum weight and put

\[
 A_0:=Y\setminus\{y_0\}.
\tag{17}
\]

Then \(A_0\) is a nonempty proper subset of \(Y\), it contains at least five distinct points, and therefore \(\rho_{A_0}\geq1\). Minimality of \(w_{y_0}\) and (16) give

\[
 W(A_0)
 \geq\left(1-\frac1{|Y|}\right)W(Y)
 \geq\frac56W(Y)
 >\frac{7Q}{240}.
\tag{18}
\]

In particular,

\[
 2^{n-\rho_{A_0}}W(A_0)
 \geq W(A_0)
 >\frac{7Q}{240}.
\tag{19}
\]

Starting from \(A_0\), perform the following finite deterministic descent. At a current set \(A_i\) of positive affine rank, if there is a nonempty subset \(A_{i+1}\subseteq A_i\) with

\[
 \rho_{A_{i+1}}=\rho_{A_i}-1,
 \qquad
 W(A_{i+1})\geq\frac12W(A_i),
\tag{20}
\]

choose one such subset. Otherwise stop and set \(A_\star:=A_i\). Each successful step preserves the lower bound in (19), because (20) gives

\[
 2^{n-\rho_{A_{i+1}}}W(A_{i+1})
 =2^{n-\rho_{A_i}+1}W(A_{i+1})
 \geq2^{n-\rho_{A_i}}W(A_i).
\tag{21}
\]

The descent cannot reach affine rank zero. Indeed, a nonempty rank-zero subset of the set \(Y\) of distinct physical endpoints is a singleton \(\{y\}\). Equations (18)-(21) would then give

\[
 2^nw_y>\frac{7Q}{240}.
\tag{22}
\]

On the other hand, equations (18) of accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c and (4) of accepted fact bf0d5c250b8f688c give the exact canonical endpoint cap

\[
 w_y=\widehat\tau_y(v)
 \leq t_{e(y)}
 <\frac{Q}{400\,2^n},
\tag{23}
\]

so \(2^nw_y<Q/400<7Q/240\), contradicting (22). Therefore the descent stops with positive affine rank. It began inside the proper set \(A_0\), so its terminal set satisfies (8)-(10).

At the stopping set there is no rank-\((\rho_{A_\star}-1)\) subset carrying half the weight. If a nonempty \(B\subseteq A_\star\) with smaller rank carried at least half, extend an affine basis of \(B\) by points of \(A_\star\) until its rank is exactly \(\rho_{A_\star}-1\). The resulting subset \(B'\subseteq A_\star\) has \(W(B')\geq W(B)\geq W(A_\star)/2\), contradicting the stopping rule. This proves (11). Since the family is finite, (11) also implies strict failure of (7) for every probability distribution on rank-one restrictions of \(A_\star\).

Finally, equation (38) of accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c and the hard selector inequality give, for the nonempty subset \(A_\star\subseteq Y\),

\[
 \frac{Q}{100}
 >R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq4\gamma_d\Lambda_v(A_\star).
\tag{24}
\]

Using (3) and (10) in (24) yields

\[
 r_{A_\star}^c+1
 >\frac{400\gamma_d}{Q}
 2^{n-\rho_{A_\star}}W(A_\star)
 >\frac{35}{3}\gamma_d.
\tag{25}
\]

Accepted fact 2fe46f47d19112ea gives \(\gamma_d>4/11\), and hence

\[
 r_{A_\star}^c+1
 >\frac{140}{33}>4.
\tag{26}
\]

The left side is an integer, so it is at least five. This proves (12). Equations (6), (11), and (12) show precisely what a further random or deterministic restriction must supply: its mass deficit can be repaired only through a coupled reduction of \(r_B^c\). The affine-lattice rank factor and the synchronized weight ledger alone have already been exhausted by the descent above.
