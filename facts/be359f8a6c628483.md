## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), write
\[
C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\tag{1}
\]
and let \(\mu_C\) be the mixed-volume measure
\[
\mu_C(A):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in A\}
\tag{2}
\]
for Borel \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\tag{3}
\]

### Dominated-measure residual theorem

Let \(\sigma\) be a nonzero positive finite Borel measure carried by \(S\),
and suppose that
\[
\sigma(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{4}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Let
\[
P_\sigma:=\operatorname{supp}\sigma,
\qquad
A_\sigma:=
\operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(P_\sigma),
\qquad
\rho_\sigma:=\dim A_\sigma.
\tag{5}
\]
Define the continuous residual rank by
\[
r_\sigma:=
\min_{s:A_\sigma\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}
\{y-s(z):(z,y)\in P_\sigma\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The minimum is attained, including when \(\rho_\sigma=0\) or
\(r_\sigma=0\), and it has the coordinate-free identity
\[
\boxed{
r_\sigma
=
\dim\operatorname{aff}(P_\sigma)-\rho_\sigma.
}
\tag{7}
\]
There is a point \(p_\sigma\in S\) such that every closed halfspace
\(H\ni p_\sigma\) satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H)
\geq
\sigma(H)
\geq
\frac{|\sigma|}{2^{\rho_\sigma}(r_\sigma+1)}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{|\sigma|}{2^{\rho_\sigma}(r_\sigma+1)}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

### Same-parity midpoint specialization

For \((z,q)\in C\), define the absolute depth of \(q\) in its fiber by
\[
\delta_C(z,q):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L):
L\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }q\in L
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Let \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, and for every \(z\in I\) choose
\(q_z\in C_z\) and \(b_z>0\) satisfying
\[
b_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z).
\tag{11}
\]
Put
\[
\rho_0:=\sum_{z\in I}b_z\delta_{(z,q_z)},
\qquad
B:=|\rho_0|=\sum_{z\in I}b_z>0.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(\rho_{0,\xi}\) be the
restriction of \(\rho_0\) to base parity \(\xi\), put
\(B_\xi:=|\rho_{0,\xi}|\), and, for
\(T(p,p')=(p+p')/2\), define
\[
\nu:=
\sum_{\xi:B_\xi>0}
\frac1{2B_\xi}
T_\#(\rho_{0,\xi}\otimes\rho_{0,\xi}).
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
|\nu|=\frac B2,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\nu\subseteq S,
\qquad
\nu(H)\leq\rho_0(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{14}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\). Let \(\rho_\nu\) and \(r_\nu\) be the
intrinsic base rank and residual rank in (5)-(6), with \(\sigma=\nu\).
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{B}{2^{\rho_\nu+1}(r_\nu+1)}
\geq
\frac{B}{2^{n+1}(r_\nu+1)}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Assume now that \(W>0\), put
\[
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{16}
\]
and apply (12)-(13) with
\[
I=D_+,
\qquad
q_z=c_z,
\qquad
b_z=\gamma_da_z.
\tag{17}
\]
Denote the resulting measure by \(\nu_{\rm mid}\), and denote its ranks by
\(\rho_{\rm mid}\) and \(r_{\rm mid}\). Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_dW}
{2^{\rho_{\rm mid}+1}(r_{\rm mid}+1)}
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2(r_{\rm mid}+1)}\,2^{-n}W.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\), for every integer \(R\geq0\),
\[
r_{\rm mid}\leq R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h_{\max}(C)
>
\frac1{2e(R+1)}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{19}
\]

### Exact off-diagonal fractional matching

Define the intrinsic residual-selector capacity
\[
R_{\rm sel}(C):=
\sup
2^{n-\rho_E}
\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{r_s+1},
\tag{20}
\]
where the supremum ranges over nonempty \(E\subseteq D_+\), points
\(q_z\in C_z\), weights
\(0<w_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\), and affine maps
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\), with
\[
\rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\qquad
r_s:=
\dim\operatorname{span}\{q_z-s(z):z\in E\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Accepted fact `ae8bb1ec375ace78` gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}R_{\rm sel}(C).
\tag{22}
\]

Retain the centroid points and weights in (17). For every parity class
\(\xi\), put
\[
B_\xi:=\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}b_z,
\qquad
\beta_\xi:=
\max_{\substack{z\in D_+\\z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}b_z,
\tag{23}
\]
with \(B_\xi=\beta_\xi=0\) for an empty class, and define
\[
U_\xi:=\max\{2\beta_\xi-B_\xi,0\}.
\tag{24}
\]
There are symmetric edge weights
\[
\lambda_{z,z'}=\lambda_{z',z}\geq0
\qquad
(z\ne z',\ z\equiv z'\pmod2)
\tag{25}
\]
and unmatched weights \(u_z\geq0\) such that
\[
b_z
=
u_z+
\sum_{\substack{z'\in D_+\\z'\ne z,\ z'\equiv z\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
\lambda_{z,z'},
\qquad
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}u_z
=U_\xi.
\tag{26}
\]
Moreover, \(U_\xi\) is the least possible total unmatched mass in that
parity class.

Define the unordered, genuinely off-diagonal midpoint measure
\[
\nu_{\rm off}:=
\sum_{\substack{\{z,z'\}\subseteq D_+\\
z\ne z',\ z\equiv z'\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
\lambda_{z,z'}
\delta_{\left(
(z+z')/2,(c_z+c_{z'})/2
\right)}.
\tag{27}
\]
Every summand in (27) comes from two distinct base fibers, even when several
summands have the same midpoint. This measure satisfies
\[
\operatorname{supp}\nu_{\rm off}\subseteq S,
\qquad
\nu_{\rm off}(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{28}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\), and its mass is exactly
\[
|\nu_{\rm off}|
=
\frac12\left(\gamma_dW-
\sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}U_\xi\right).
\tag{29}
\]
The unmatched mass obeys
\[
\sum_\xi U_\xi
\leq R_{\rm sel}(C),
\tag{30}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
|\nu_{\rm off}|
\geq
\frac{\gamma_dW-R_{\rm sel}(C)}2.
}
\tag{31}
\]

If \(\nu_{\rm off}\ne0\), let \(\rho_{\rm off}\) and \(r_{\rm off}\)
be its ranks from (5)-(6). Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_dW-\sum_\xi U_\xi}
{2^{\rho_{\rm off}+1}(r_{\rm off}+1)}
\geq
\frac{(\gamma_dW-R_{\rm sel}(C))_+}
{2^{\rho_{\rm off}+1}(r_{\rm off}+1)}.
}
\tag{32}
\]
In particular, if \(R_{\rm sel}(C)<\gamma_dW\), then
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_dW-R_{\rm sel}(C)}
{2^{n+1}(r_{\rm off}+1)}.
\tag{33}
\]
No statement above requires or implies that
\((c_z+c_{z'})/2\) has positive absolute depth in the target fiber
\(C_{(z+z')/2}\).

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that only finitely many integer base fibers
meet \(C\). Thus \(S\) is compact, all base supports below are finite, and
all measures have compact support.

We first prove the dominated-measure theorem. Put
\[
P:=P_\sigma,
\qquad
A:=A_\sigma,
\qquad
\rho:=\rho_\sigma,
\qquad
K:=\operatorname{aff}(P).
\tag{34}
\]
Choose \(p_0=(z_0,y_0)\in P\), and let
\[
L_K:=\operatorname{lin}(K-p_0),
\qquad
L_0:=A-z_0.
\tag{35}
\]
The base projection maps \(L_K\) surjectively onto \(L_0\). Its kernel is
of the form
\[
\{0\}\times V_0,
\qquad
\dim V_0=\dim K-\rho.
\tag{36}
\]
Choose a linear right inverse \(R:L_0\to L_K\) of this projection. Write
\[
R(u)=(u,L(u))
\tag{37}
\]
for a linear map \(L:L_0\to\mathbb R^d\), and define
\[
s_0(z_0+u):=y_0+L(u).
\tag{38}
\]
The graph of \(s_0\) lies in \(K\). For every \((z,y)\in P\),
\[
(z,y)-(z,s_0(z))=(0,y-s_0(z))\in\{0\}\times V_0.
\tag{39}
\]
These residual vectors span \(V_0\). Indeed, given \(v\in V_0\), write
\[
(0,v)=\sum_i\alpha_i\bigl((z_i,y_i)-p_0\bigr)
\qquad((z_i,y_i)\in P).
\]
Projecting to \(L_0\) gives
\(\sum_i\alpha_i(z_i-z_0)=0\). By linearity of \(R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(0,v)
&=\sum_i\alpha_i
\left((z_i,y_i)-p_0-R(z_i-z_0)\right)\\
&=\sum_i\alpha_i(0,y_i-s_0(z_i)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every element of \(V_0\) is in the span of the residuals, while (39)
gives the reverse inclusion. Hence the map \(s_0\) gives residual rank
\[
\dim V_0=\dim K-\rho.
\tag{40}
\]

Conversely, let \(s:A\to\mathbb R^d\) be any affine map and let \(V_s\)
be the span of its residual vectors on \(P\). The affine injective map
\[
(z,y)\longmapsto(z,y-s(z))
\tag{41}
\]
sends \(P\) into \(A\times V_s\). Therefore
\[
\dim K
=
\dim\operatorname{aff}(P)
\leq
\rho+\dim V_s.
\tag{42}
\]
Together, (40) and (42) prove attainment and the identity (7).

Fix an attaining affine map \(s\), and put
\[
V:=\operatorname{span}
\{y-s(z):(z,y)\in P\},
\qquad
r:=\dim V=r_\sigma.
\tag{43}
\]
The space \(L_0\) in (35) is spanned by differences of integer points, so
it is rational. Hence
\[
\Gamma:=L_0\cap\mathbb Z^n
\tag{44}
\]
is a lattice of rank \(\rho\) spanning \(L_0\). Choose a
\(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_\rho\) of \(\Gamma\), with the empty
basis when \(\rho=0\), and define
\[
F:\mathbb R^\rho\to A,
\qquad
F(t):=z_0+\sum_{i=1}^\rho t_ig_i.
\tag{45}
\]
Then \(F\) is an affine isomorphism and, crucially,
\[
F(\mathbb Z^\rho)=A\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{46}
\]
Choose a linear isomorphism \(\vartheta:V\to\mathbb R^r\), with the
unique map between zero spaces when \(r=0\). On \(P\), define
\[
\Phi(z,y):=
\bigl(F^{-1}(z),\vartheta(y-s(z))\bigr),
\tag{47}
\]
and put \(\widetilde\sigma:=\Phi_\#\sigma\). By (46), this is a nonzero
finite Borel measure carried by
\[
\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^r,
\qquad
|\widetilde\sigma|=|\sigma|.
\tag{48}
\]

Accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c`, including its zero-dimensional cases,
gives a point
\[
\bar p\in\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^r
\tag{49}
\]
such that every closed halfspace containing \(\bar p\) has
\(\widetilde\sigma\)-mass at least
\[
\Delta:=
\frac{|\sigma|}{2^\rho(r+1)}>0.
\tag{50}
\]
The point \(\bar p\) belongs to
\(\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\widetilde\sigma)\). Otherwise,
strict separation from this compact convex hull would give a proper closed
halfspace containing \(\bar p\) and disjoint from the support, contradicting
(50).

Define the affine embedding
\[
\Psi:\mathbb R^\rho\times\mathbb R^r
\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\Psi(u,v):=
\bigl(F(u),s(F(u))+\vartheta^{-1}(v)\bigr).
\tag{51}
\]
It is inverse to \(\Phi\) on \(P\). Hence
\[
p_\sigma:=\Psi(\bar p)
\in\operatorname{conv}(P)
\subseteq C.
\tag{52}
\]
Its base coordinate belongs to \(F(\mathbb Z^\rho)\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\),
so \(p_\sigma\in S\).

Let \(H\) be a closed ambient halfspace containing \(p_\sigma\). Its
pullback \(\Psi^{-1}(H)\) contains \(\bar p\) and is either a proper closed
halfspace or the whole space. In the former case (50) applies. In the latter
case its mass is \(|\sigma|\geq\Delta\). Since
\(\Psi\circ\Phi\) is the identity on \(P\),
\[
\sigma(H)
=
\widetilde\sigma(\Psi^{-1}(H))
\geq\Delta.
\tag{53}
\]
The domination hypothesis (4) now gives
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\sigma(H)\geq\Delta.
\tag{54}
\]
This proves (8)-(9), including whole-space halfspaces and arbitrary boundary
mass.

We next prove the same-parity midpoint specialization. First, the atomic
source measure in (12) is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\). To see this,
let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace and write
\[
H=\{(z,y):\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot y\geq t\},
\qquad
(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0).
\tag{55}
\]
If \((z,q_z)\in H\) and \(\beta\ne0\), then the fiber section \(H_z\) is
a proper closed halfspace containing \(q_z\), and (11) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\geq b_z.
\tag{56}
\]
If \((z,q_z)\in H\) and \(\beta=0\), then \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), and (56)
again holds because
\[
b_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\leq a_z.
\tag{57}
\]
Summing (56) over the atoms in \(H\), and discarding every other
nonnegative fiber contribution, gives
\[
\rho_0(H)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{58}
\]
The same inequality is immediate for the whole-space halfspace.

Accepted fact `3ac39fef94fcc0da`, applied to \(\rho_0\), gives
\[
|\nu|=\frac B2,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\nu\subseteq S,
\qquad
\nu(H)\leq\rho_0(H)
\tag{59}
\]
for every closed halfspace, including boundary and zero-parity cases.
Combining (58) and (59) proves (14). Applying (9) to \(\nu\) gives the
first inequality in (15), and \(\rho_\nu\leq n\) gives the second.

For every \(z\in D_+\), the positive-volume compact convex fiber \(C_z\)
is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Grünbaum's centroid halfspace theorem
therefore gives
\[
\delta_C(z,c_z)\geq\gamma_da_z.
\tag{60}
\]
Thus the choices (17) satisfy (11), their total mass is
\[
B=\sum_{z\in D_+}\gamma_da_z=\gamma_dW,
\tag{61}
\]
and (18) follows from (15). The elementary strict inequality
\(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\) gives
\[
\gamma_d=(1+1/d)^{-d}>e^{-1},
\tag{62}
\]
which proves (19).

It remains to prove the exact off-diagonal refinement. We first prove the
finite fractional matching assertion in one parity class. Let the vertex
weights in that class have total mass \(B\) and maximum \(\beta\). If
\(B=0\), there is nothing to prove. If \(\beta>B/2\), the maximizing vertex
is unique. Pair every other vertex completely with it. The heavy vertex
then has unmatched mass
\[
\beta-(B-\beta)=2\beta-B,
\tag{63}
\]
and every other vertex has unmatched mass zero.

Suppose instead that \(\beta\leq B/2\). Partition the circle
\(\mathbb R/B\mathbb Z\) into consecutive half-open arcs \(I_z\) of
length \(b_z\), and let \(\tau(t)=t+B/2\) be the antipodal translation.
Define
\[
\lambda_{z,z'}:=
\operatorname{len}(I_z\cap\tau(I_{z'})).
\tag{64}
\]
Translation invariance and \(\tau^2={\rm id}\) give
\(\lambda_{z,z'}=\lambda_{z',z}\). Since every arc has length at most
\(B/2\),
\[
\lambda_{z,z}
=
\operatorname{len}(I_z\cap\tau(I_z))
=0.
\tag{65}
\]
The translated arcs \(\tau(I_{z'})\) partition the circle up to their null
boundaries, so
\[
\sum_{z'}\lambda_{z,z'}
=
\operatorname{len}(I_z)
=b_z.
\tag{66}
\]
Thus all mass is matched in this case.

For any off-diagonal fractional matching, a vertex of weight \(\beta\) can
be matched to at most the total mass \(B-\beta\) of the other vertices.
Therefore every such matching leaves total unmatched mass at least
\(\max\{2\beta-B,0\}\). Constructions (63)-(66) attain this lower bound.
Applying them independently in every parity class proves (24)-(26),
including exact minimality.

For the centroid weights \(b_z=\gamma_da_z\), the singleton choice in
(20) with
\[
E=\{z\},
\qquad
q_z=c_z,
\qquad
w_z=b_z,
\qquad
s(z)=c_z
\tag{67}
\]
has \(\rho_E=r_s=0\). Hence
\[
R_{\rm sel}(C)\geq2^nb_z
\qquad(z\in D_+).
\tag{68}
\]
Also \(U_\xi\leq\beta_\xi\). Since there are exactly \(2^n\) parity
classes, with empty ones included,
\[
\sum_\xi U_\xi
\leq
2^n\max_{z\in D_+}b_z
\leq
R_{\rm sel}(C).
\tag{69}
\]
This proves (30).

Every edge in (27) joins two distinct base points of the same parity, so its
base midpoint is integral. Convexity of \(C\) puts the full midpoint in
\(C\), proving the support assertion in (28). Summing the vertex degrees in
(26) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\nu_{\rm off}|
&=
\sum_{\{z,z'\}}\lambda_{z,z'}\\
&=
\frac12\sum_{z\in D_+}(b_z-u_z)\\
&=
\frac12\left(
\gamma_dW-\sum_\xi U_\xi
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{70}
\]
Equations (69)-(70) prove (29)-(31).

Finally, let \(H\) be any closed halfspace. The endpoint indicator
inequality from accepted fact `3ac39fef94fcc0da` gives
\[
\mathbf1_H\left(\frac{p+p'}2\right)
\leq
\mathbf1_H(p)+\mathbf1_H(p')
\tag{71}
\]
for all \(p,p'\), including the boundary case. Apply this with
\(p=(z,c_z)\), \(p'=(z',c_{z'})\), multiply by \(\lambda_{z,z'}\), and
sum over the unordered edges. Equation (26) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{\rm off}(H)
&\leq
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\(z,c_z)\in H}}
\sum_{z'}\lambda_{z,z'}\\
&=
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\(z,c_z)\in H}}(b_z-u_z)\\
&\leq
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\(z,c_z)\in H}}b_z\\
&\leq\mu_C(H),
\end{aligned}
\tag{72}
\]
where the last inequality is the atomic halfspace domination (58). This
proves (28) without any target-fiber depth premise. If
\(\nu_{\rm off}\ne0\), applying the dominated-measure theorem (9) and then
(69)-(70) proves (32). If \(R_{\rm sel}(C)<\gamma_dW\), equation (31)
makes \(\nu_{\rm off}\) nonzero, and \(\rho_{\rm off}\leq n\) proves
(33). This completes the proof.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by
Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261,
Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pp. 1259-1260,
https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF
https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. The exact form used is:
if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body with
centroid \(c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\)
containing \(c(K)\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap L)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K).
\]
For every \(z\in D_+\), the fiber \(C_z\) is compact and convex and has
positive \(d\)-volume, so it is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body and the
theorem gives exactly (60).
