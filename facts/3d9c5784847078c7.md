## Statement

Assume the canonical hard branch and retain the objects and normalizations of accepted facts `1f80aa23bb960de6`, `2fe46f47d19112ea`, `3016926516cc39df`, `8aac56c49321466c`, and `e482e1c83a1dc247`. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in I_+}a_z,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
\(Q>0\) is the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir, and \(G\) is the canonical global complete metronoid-core capacity. The hard inequality and the reservoir-volume comparison give
\[
 G<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 W\geq8Q.
\tag{1}
\]

Retain the finite bad-centroid label family \(\mathscr D_c\), its mass
\[
 M_c:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr D_c}\eta_\sigma,
\]
and the accepted choice of exactly one bad physical endpoint \(y_{\mathrm{def}}(\sigma)\) for every \(\sigma\in\mathscr D_c\). Put
\[
 Y_{\mathrm{def}}
 :=\{y_{\mathrm{def}}(\sigma):\sigma\in\mathscr D_c\},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{def}}
 :=\dim\operatorname{aff}(Y_{\mathrm{def}}),
\]
and, for each \(y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}\), define the aggregate selected label mass
\[
 \alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}
 :=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr D_c\\
 y_{\mathrm{def}}(\sigma)=y}}\eta_\sigma.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(Y_{\mathrm{def}}\ne\varnothing\), every \(\alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}>0\), and
\[
 \sum_{y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}}\alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}=M_c.
\tag{3}
\]
In every such hard instance,
\[
 \boxed{d\geq10,}
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 |Y_{\mathrm{def}}|
 >2^n\left(18-\frac5{\gamma_d}\right)
 >\frac{141}{32}\,2^n,}
\tag{5}
\]
\[
 \boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{def}}\geq2,}
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_{\mathrm{def}}=2
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 3\leq n\leq5.}
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover, at the minimal dimensions in (4) and (7),
\[
 \boxed{n=3,\ d=10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |Y_{\mathrm{def}}|\geq41.}
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `e482e1c83a1dc247` gives a nonempty full internal centroid support and its intrinsic continuous residual rank \(r_{\mathrm{int}}^c\geq10\). The zero map on its affine base hull is an admissible affine map in the definition of \(r_{\mathrm{int}}^c\), and its residual vectors lie in \(\mathbb R^d\). Hence
\[
 10\leq r_{\mathrm{int}}^c\leq d,
\]
which proves (4). This also checks that no empty-support convention is being used in the dimension argument.

We next prove the exact endpoint count. Accepted fact `e482e1c83a1dc247` identifies the quantity in (2) with its aggregate defect incidence and proves, for every \(y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}\),
\[
 0<\alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}
 \leq t_{e_2(y)}
 \leq\frac{a_y}{4},
\tag{9}
\]
as well as
\[
 M_c>
 \frac{18\gamma_d-5}{400\gamma_d}Q
 =\frac1{400}\left(18-\frac5{\gamma_d}\right)Q
 >\frac{141}{12800}Q.
\tag{10}
\]
Accepted fact `2fe46f47d19112ea` identifies \(e_2(y)\) as an edge of the endpoint-disjoint dyadic matching constructed in accepted fact `3016926516cc39df`. If \(e_2(y)=\{u,w\}\), that construction gives
\[
 t_{e_2(y)}=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\},
 \qquad
 \tau_u,\tau_w<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\]
Consequently (9) sharpens to the strict physical-atom bound
\[
 0<\alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}
 <\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}
 \qquad(y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}).
\tag{11}
\]
The family is finite and nonempty by (3) and (10). Summing (11), and then comparing with (10), gives
\[
 \frac1{400}\left(18-\frac5{\gamma_d}\right)Q
 <M_c
 <|Y_{\mathrm{def}}|\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\]
Cancellation of the positive number \(Q/400\) proves the first strict inequality in (5).

The same accepted defect fact proves the strict elementary estimate
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac{32}{87}.
\]
Therefore \(1/\gamma_d<87/32\), and
\[
 18-\frac5{\gamma_d}
 >18-\frac{435}{32}
 =\frac{141}{32}.
\]
This proves the second strict inequality in (5), with no rounding of either constant.

For the two base-rank screens, set
\[
 A_{\mathrm{def}}:=\operatorname{aff}(Y_{\mathrm{def}}),
 \qquad
 D_{\mathrm{def}}:=I_+\cap A_{\mathrm{def}},
 \qquad
 B_{\mathrm{def}}:=\operatorname{aff}(D_{\mathrm{def}}),
\]
\[
 q:=\dim B_{\mathrm{def}},
 \qquad
 W_{\mathrm{def}}:=\sum_{z\in D_{\mathrm{def}}}a_z.
\tag{12}
\]
This is the exact alias map from the slice notation \(A,D_A,B,q,W_A\) in accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` to the present physical defect slice. Equation (9) implies \(a_y>0\) for every \(y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}\), so \(Y_{\mathrm{def}}\subseteq D_{\mathrm{def}}\). Since \(D_{\mathrm{def}}\subseteq A_{\mathrm{def}}\), the two inclusions yield
\[
 B_{\mathrm{def}}=A_{\mathrm{def}},
 \qquad
 q=\rho_{\mathrm{def}},
\tag{13}
\]
and (3), (9) give the actual physical slice-mass bound
\[
 W_{\mathrm{def}}
 \geq\sum_{y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}}a_y
 \geq4\sum_{y\in Y_{\mathrm{def}}}\alpha_y^{\mathrm{def}}
 =4M_c.
\tag{14}
\]
In particular \(D_{\mathrm{def}}\ne\varnothing\) and \(W_{\mathrm{def}}>0\), so all hypotheses of the accepted rank-at-most-two physical-slice theorem are genuine rather than vacuous. Also \(W_{\mathrm{def}}\leq W\).

Accepted fact `1f80aa23bb960de6` proves \(Q\leq W/8\) for this same reservoir, which is the second inequality in (1). The strict numerical part of (10) also gives
\[
 M_c>\frac{141}{12800}Q>\frac Q{100}.
\tag{15}
\]

Suppose first that \(q\leq1\). If \(B_{\mathrm{def}}=\mathbb R^n\), positivity of \(n\) forces \(q=n=1\). The full-slice clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c`, with \(\alpha_1=1/4\), gives
\[
 G\geq\frac W4\geq2Q>\frac Q{100},
\]
contrary to (1). If \(B_{\mathrm{def}}\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\), the same accepted theorem, with \(\alpha_0=1\) and \(\alpha_1=1/4\), gives
\[
 G\geq
 \min\left\{
 \alpha_qW_{\mathrm{def}}+\frac{W-W_{\mathrm{def}}}{2},
 \ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_{\mathrm{def}}
 \right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(q\in\{0,1\}\), equations (14) and (15) imply
\[
 \alpha_qW_{\mathrm{def}}\geq M_c>\frac Q{100}.
\]
Moreover \(W-W_{\mathrm{def}}\geq0\) and \(2^{n-q}\geq1\). Thus both entries of the minimum in (16) are strictly greater than \(Q/100\), again contradicting (1). These alternatives include the rank-zero case, for which the relative balance condition in the accepted theorem is vacuous. Hence \(q\geq2\), and (13) proves (6).

It remains to assume \(q=\rho_{\mathrm{def}}=2\). Since \(q\leq n\), one has \(n\geq2\). If \(n=2\), then \(B_{\mathrm{def}}=\mathbb R^2\), and the full-slice clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c`, now with \(\alpha_2=1/64\), gives
\[
 G\geq\frac W{64}
 \geq\frac Q8
 >\frac Q{100},
\]
contrary to (1). Therefore \(n\geq3\).

If instead \(n\geq6\), then \(B_{\mathrm{def}}\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\). The proper-slice clause of the same accepted theorem gives
\[
 G\geq
 \min\left\{
 \frac{W_{\mathrm{def}}}{64}
 +\frac{W-W_{\mathrm{def}}}{2},
 \ 2^{n-2}\frac{W_{\mathrm{def}}}{64}
 \right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The first entry satisfies
\[
 \frac{W_{\mathrm{def}}}{64}
 +\frac{W-W_{\mathrm{def}}}{2}
 =\frac W{64}+\frac{31}{64}(W-W_{\mathrm{def}})
 \geq\frac W{64}
 \geq\frac Q8
 >\frac Q{100},
\tag{18}
\]
while (14), (15), and \(n\geq6\) give for the second entry
\[
 2^{n-2}\frac{W_{\mathrm{def}}}{64}
 \geq2^{n-6}M_c
 \geq M_c
 >\frac Q{100}.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (17)-(19) contradict (1). Thus \(n\leq5\), and (7) follows.

Finally assume \(n=3\) and \(d=10\). Then \(\gamma_{10}=(10/11)^{10}\), and the exact integer calculation
\[
 13\cdot10^{10}-5\cdot11^{10}
 =312876995>0
\]
shows \(13>5(11/10)^{10}=5/\gamma_{10}\). Hence
\[
 2^3\left(18-\frac5{\gamma_{10}}\right)>8\cdot5=40.
\]
Equation (5) gives \(|Y_{\mathrm{def}}|>40\). Since this is the cardinality of a finite set, it is an integer, so \(|Y_{\mathrm{def}}|\geq41\), proving (8).
