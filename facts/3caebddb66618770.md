## Statement

Let $T$ be a full-dimensional $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$, of volume $V$, centroid $g$ and barycentrics $\lambda_i$, and set $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. Write $L_\tau=\{x\in T:f_T(x)\ge\tau V\}$ for its ordinary raw depth region. The unique maximum is $\Gamma V$ at $g$. On each vertex-to-centroid ray $f_T(v_i+t(g-v_i))=\Gamma Vt^d$ for $0\le t\le1$. For $\tau\le\Gamma$ sufficiently close to $\Gamma$ the exact level region is given by the simultaneous inequalities $d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(x)\ge\tau$ on $T$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
$$
(\Gamma/\varepsilon)(L_{\Gamma-\varepsilon}-g)\longrightarrow -(T-g)
\quad\text{in Hausdorff distance},\qquad
|L_{\Gamma-\varepsilon}|/V=(\varepsilon/\Gamma)^d(1+O_d(\varepsilon)).
$$

## Proof

By 53fe12b742a7608d the exact centroid depth is $\Gamma V$. Away from $g$ some barycentric coordinate exceeds $1/(d+1)$, and the corresponding canonical cap has volume $V(1-\lambda_i)^d<\Gamma V$. At $v_i+t(g-v_i)$, for $t>0$, the homothetic simplex $v_i+t(T-v_i)$ has that mark as centroid and volume $t^dV$. Its depth at the mark is $\Gamma t^dV$ by the same accepted fact. Inclusion gives this lower bound in $T$, and the canonical vertex-$i$ cap, with ratio $dt/(d+1)$, gives equality. At $t=0$ the supporting cap has zero volume.

The exact local depth formula in d2f95e21650e61cb with every truncation zero gives $f_T(x)/V=\min_i d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(x)$ near $g$. In dimension one the same formula follows directly from the two endpoint distances. Membership in $L_\tau$ implies $\lambda_i\le1-\tau^{1/d}$ for each $i$ by the canonical caps. The product constraints imply the same bounds by AM–GM. As $\tau\uparrow\Gamma$ these bounds and the sum constraint force both regions uniformly to $g$, proving equality of the two descriptions.

Write $m=d+1$ and $\lambda_i=1/m+\delta_i$. The coordinate upper bounds and zero sum give $\max_i|\delta_i|=O_d(\varepsilon)$ on the region at $\tau=\Gamma-\varepsilon$. In these coordinates the $i$th product times $d^d$ is $\Gamma-m\Gamma\delta_i+O_d(\varepsilon^2)$. Thus the region lies between the zero-sum coordinate simplexes with all $\delta_i\le\varepsilon/(m\Gamma)-C_d\varepsilon^2$ and all $\delta_i\le\varepsilon/(m\Gamma)+C_d\varepsilon^2$, for a fixed sufficiently large $C_d$. For the inner inclusion, the same zero-sum bound gives $\max_i|\delta_i|=O_d(\varepsilon)$ on the smaller simplex itself, so the Taylor estimate applies there and the barycentrics are positive. The centered simplex $T-g$ in these coordinates is given by $\delta_i\ge-1/m$. The two bounding simplexes therefore correspond to homothetic copies of $-(T-g)$ with positive factors $\varepsilon/\Gamma+O_d(\varepsilon^2)$. The sandwich gives the stated Hausdorff and volume limits.
