## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(T\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let
\[
\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+:T\longrightarrow[0,\infty),\qquad 1\le i\le n,
\]
be bounded upper-semicontinuous concave functions.  Define
\[
C:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times T:
-\ell_i^-(y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(y)\quad(1\le i\le n)\right\}
\]
and \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).  For \(y\in T\), put
\[
a_i(y):=\lfloor\ell_i^-(y)\rfloor,
\qquad b_i(y):=\lfloor\ell_i^+(y)\rfloor,
\qquad N_i(y):=a_i(y)+b_i(y)+2,
\]
and
\[
A_{\mathrm{asym}}(y):=\prod_{i=1}^nN_i(y),
\qquad
B_{\mathrm{asym}}(y):=\prod_{i=1}^n\bigl(N_i(y)-1\bigr).
\]
Then the original mixed-integer mass is
\[
W:=\mathcal H_d(S)=\int_TA_{\mathrm{asym}}(y)\,dy.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(V:=\operatorname{vol}_d(T)\), let \(\mu:=V^{-1}\mathbf1_Tdy\), set
\[
Z:=\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{asym}}=W/V,
\qquad
\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}:=\frac{A_{\mathrm{asym}}}{Z},
\qquad
D:=D(\nu\Vert\mu).
\tag{2}
\]
All logarithms below are natural.  If \(U_m\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\), define, for integers \(q\ge2\) and \(m\ge0\),
\[
\delta_q(m):=\mathsf H\bigl(U_m\mid\lfloor U_m/q\rfloor\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(m+1=qs+t\) with \(s\ge0\) and \(0\le t<q\), one has the exact formula
\[
\delta_q(m)=\frac{qs\log q+t\log t}{m+1},
\qquad 0\log0:=0.
\tag{4}
\]
For nonnegative integers \(a,b\), define
\[
\Delta_q(a,b):=
\frac{a+1}{a+b+2}\,\delta_q(a)
+\frac{b+1}{a+b+2}\,\delta_q(b).
\tag{5}
\]
Then, for every integer \(q\ge2\),
\[
D\le\frac q{q-1}\,
\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{i=1}^n\Delta_q(a_i,b_i).
\tag{6}
\]
The exact scalar inequalities used at scales two and three are, for every \(m\ge0\),
\[
\delta_2(m)+\frac34\delta_3(m)
\le3\log\frac{2m+1}{m+1},
\tag{7}
\]
and, for all nonnegative integers \(a,b\), with \(N=a+b+2\),
\[
\Delta_2(a,b)+\frac34\Delta_3(a,b)
\le3\log\frac{2(N-1)}N.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently,
\[
D(\nu\Vert\mu)
\le3\,\mathbb E_\nu
\log\frac{2^nB_{\mathrm{asym}}}{A_{\mathrm{asym}}},
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\left(\mathbb E_\mu B_{\mathrm{asym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}
\ge2^{-n}\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{asym}}.
\tag{10}
\]
The coefficient one on the right side of (10) is sharp.

If the roofs are symmetric, \(\ell_i^- =\ell_i^+=:\ell_i\), and
\[
m_i:=\lfloor\ell_i\rfloor,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\prod_i(m_i+1),
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{sym}}:=\prod_i(2m_i+1),
\tag{11}
\]
then the pointwise aliases are exactly
\[
A_{\mathrm{asym}}=2^nA_{\mathrm{sym}},
\qquad B_{\mathrm{asym}}=R_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, \(Z=2^n\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{sym}}\), the probability measure \(\nu\) in (2) is exactly the tilt with density \(A_{\mathrm{sym}}/\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{sym}}\), and (9)-(10) become
\[
D(\nu\Vert\mu)\le3\mathbb E_\nu\log\frac{R_{\mathrm{sym}}}{A_{\mathrm{sym}}},
\qquad
\left(\mathbb E_\mu R_{\mathrm{sym}}^{3/4}\right)^{4/3}
\ge\mathbb E_\mu A_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\tag{13}
\]

## Proof

The integer section of \(C\) over \(y\) is the Cartesian product
\[
I(y):=\prod_{i=1}^n\{-a_i(y),-a_i(y)+1,\ldots,b_i(y)+1\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, \(\lceil-\ell_i^-(y)\rceil=-a_i(y)\) and \(\lfloor1+\ell_i^+(y)\rfloor=b_i(y)+1\).  Thus \(|I(y)|=A_{\mathrm{asym}}(y)\).  Boundedness of all roofs leaves only finitely many possible integer labels, so Tonelli's theorem applied to their fiber indicators gives (1).  It also makes \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{asym}}\) bounded, while \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\ge2^n\) and \(B_{\mathrm{asym}}\ge1\).

Formula (4) follows by counting the fibers of \(u\mapsto\lfloor u/q\rfloor\) on \(\{0,\ldots,m\}\): there are \(s\) blocks of size \(q\), and, if \(t>0\), one block of size \(t\).  A block of size \(r\) has probability \(r/(m+1)\) and conditional entropy \(\log r\), which proves (4).

Fix \(q\ge2\).  Sample \(Y\sim\nu\), and, conditionally on \(Y=y\), sample \(K=(K_1,\ldots,K_n)\) uniformly on \(I(y)\).  Because the roofs are bounded, \(K\) takes values in a fixed finite subset of \(\mathbb Z^n\), so every Shannon entropy below is finite.  Conditional uniformity and (2) give
\[
\mathsf H(K\mid Y)
=\mathbb E_\nu\log A_{\mathrm{asym}}
=\log Z+D.
\tag{15}
\]

The Brenier displacement framework supplied by accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` gives a convex potential \(\phi\) such that \(F:=\nabla\phi\) pushes \(\nu\) forward to \(\mu\).  Put
\[
t:=1-\frac1q,
\qquad
Y_q:=(1-t)Y+tF(Y)=\frac1qY+\left(1-\frac1q\right)F(Y),
\qquad
\nu_q:=\mathcal L(Y_q).
\tag{16}
\]
The endpoints \(\nu\) and \(\mu\), viewed as measures on \(\mathbb R^d\), have the bounded compactly supported Lebesgue densities
\[
\rho_0=A_{\mathrm{asym}}\mathbf1_T/W,
\qquad
\rho_1=\mathbf1_T/V.
\tag{17}
\]
McCann's Theorem 2.2 applies to the internal-energy integrand \(r\mapsto r\log r\), with \(0\log0=0\), and proves
\[
\int\rho_t\log\rho_t
\le(1-t)\int\rho_0\log\rho_0+t\int\rho_1\log\rho_1,
\tag{18}
\]
where \(\rho_t\) is the density of \(\nu_q\).  Since \(Y,F(Y)\in T\) almost surely and \(T\) is convex, \(Y_q\in T\) almost surely.  Therefore relative entropy with respect to the uniform law on \(T\) is internal energy plus \(\log V\), and (18) yields
\[
D(\nu_q\Vert\mu)
\le(1-t)D(\nu\Vert\mu)+tD(\mu\Vert\mu)
=\frac Dq.
\tag{19}
\]

Define the two-core coarsening map \(c_q:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\) by
\[
c_q(k):=
\begin{cases}
\lceil k/q\rceil,&k\le0,\\
1+\lfloor(k-1)/q\rfloor,&k\ge1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
K_q:=\bigl(c_q(K_1),\ldots,c_q(K_n)\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]
This map contracts the two sides toward the distinct core sites \(0\) and \(1\), and it preserves feasibility in the original section over \(Y_q\).  To see this, concavity and nonnegativity give
\[
\ell_i^\pm(Y_q)
\ge q^{-1}\ell_i^\pm(Y)+(1-q^{-1})\ell_i^\pm(F(Y))
\ge q^{-1}\ell_i^\pm(Y).
\tag{21}
\]
If \(K_i\le0\), then
\[
c_q(K_i)\ge K_i/q\ge-\ell_i^-(Y)/q\ge-\ell_i^-(Y_q),
\qquad
c_q(K_i)\le0\le1+\ell_i^+(Y_q).
\tag{22}
\]
If \(K_i\ge1\), then
\[
c_q(K_i)\le1+(K_i-1)/q
\le1+\ell_i^+(Y)/q
\le1+\ell_i^+(Y_q),
\qquad
c_q(K_i)\ge1\ge-\ell_i^-(Y_q).
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(K_q\in I(Y_q)\) almost surely.

For every probability measure \(\sigma\ll\mu\), bounded positivity of \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}\) and (2) give the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E_\sigma\log A_{\mathrm{asym}}-D(\sigma\Vert\mu)
=\log Z-D(\sigma\Vert\nu)
\le\log Z.
\tag{24}
\]
The entropy in (19) is finite, so (24) applies to \(\sigma=\nu_q\).  Given \(Y_q=y\), the conditional law of \(K_q\) is supported on the \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}(y)\) points of \(I(y)\).  Hence
\[
\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y_q)\le\mathbb E_{\nu_q}\log A_{\mathrm{asym}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Moreover, \(Y_q\) is a function of \(Y\), so conditioning monotonicity gives
\[
\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y_q)\ge\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y).
\tag{26}
\]

Conditional on \(Y=y\), the coordinates of \(K\) are independent and uniform on the intervals in (14).  The outputs of \(c_q\) on \(\{-a_i,\ldots,0\}\) are nonpositive, while its outputs on \(\{1,\ldots,b_i+1\}\) are positive, so the two sides never merge.  On the first side, \(-K_i\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,a_i\}\) and \(c_q(K_i)=-\lfloor(-K_i)/q\rfloor\); on the second side, \(K_i-1\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,b_i\}\) and \(c_q(K_i)-1=\lfloor(K_i-1)/q\rfloor\).  Therefore (3)-(5) and conditional independence give exactly
\[
\mathsf H(K\mid K_q,Y)
=\mathbb E_\nu\sum_{i=1}^n\Delta_q(a_i,b_i).
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(K_q\) is a function of \(K\), the conditional chain rule, followed by (15), gives
\[
\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y)
=\log Z+D-
\mathbb E_\nu\sum_i\Delta_q(a_i,b_i).
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (19), (24), (25), (26), and (28),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log Z
&\ge\mathbb E_{\nu_q}\log A_{\mathrm{asym}}-D(\nu_q\Vert\mu)\\
&\ge\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y_q)-D/q\\
&\ge\mathsf H(K_q\mid Y)-D/q\\
&=\log Z+(1-1/q)D-
\mathbb E_\nu\sum_i\Delta_q(a_i,b_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Rearrangement proves (6).

It remains to verify and combine the exact scalar bounds.  From (3), \(\delta_q(0)=0\), \(\delta_2(1)=\delta_3(1)=\log2\), and \(\delta_q(m)\le\log q\).  For \(m=0\), (7) is equality.  For \(m=1\), (7) is equivalent, after multiplying logarithms by four and exponentiating, to
\[
2^{19}\le3^{12},
\tag{30}
\]
which holds because \(524288\le531441\).  For \(m\ge2\),
\[
\delta_2(m)+\frac34\delta_3(m)
\le\log2+\frac34\log3
\le3\log\frac53
\le3\log\frac{2m+1}{m+1}.
\tag{31}
\]
The middle inequality is exactly \(16\cdot3^{15}\le5^{12}\), namely \(229582512\le244140625\), and the last follows because \((2m+1)/(m+1)\) is increasing and equals \(5/3\) at \(m=2\).  This proves (7) without decimal approximation.

Let \(N=a+b+2\).  Applying (7) separately to \(a\) and \(b\), then using concavity of the logarithm with weights \((a+1)/N\) and \((b+1)/N\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_2(a,b)+\frac34\Delta_3(a,b)
&\le3\left[
\frac{a+1}{N}\log\frac{2a+1}{a+1}
+\frac{b+1}{N}\log\frac{2b+1}{b+1}
\right]\\
&\le3\log\left[
\frac{2a+1}{N}+\frac{2b+1}{N}
\right]
=3\log\frac{2(N-1)}N,
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
which is (8).

Taking half the sum of (6) at \(q=2\) and \(q=3\) yields the exact combination
\[
D\le\mathbb E_\nu\sum_i
\left(\Delta_2(a_i,b_i)+\frac34\Delta_3(a_i,b_i)\right).
\tag{33}
\]
By (8), the right side is at most
\[
3\mathbb E_\nu\sum_i\log\frac{2(N_i-1)}{N_i}
=3\mathbb E_\nu\log\frac{2^nB_{\mathrm{asym}}}{A_{\mathrm{asym}}},
\tag{34}
\]
which proves (9).

Finally, the change of measure in (2), Jensen's inequality for the logarithm, and (9) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\mathbb E_\mu(2^nB_{\mathrm{asym}})^{3/4}
&=\log\mathbb E_\nu\left[
\frac{Z(2^nB_{\mathrm{asym}})^{3/4}}{A_{\mathrm{asym}}}
\right]\\
&\ge\frac34\log Z
+\frac34\mathbb E_\nu\log\frac{2^nB_{\mathrm{asym}}}{A_{\mathrm{asym}}}
-\frac14D\\
&\ge\frac34\log Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Exponentiating, raising to the power \(4/3\), and cancelling the factor \(2^n\) proves (10).  If every roof is identically zero, then \(a_i=b_i=0\), \(A_{\mathrm{asym}}=2^n\), and \(B_{\mathrm{asym}}=1\), so equality holds in (10).  Hence its coefficient one cannot be increased.

In the symmetric case, \(a_i=b_i=m_i\), and direct substitution gives
\[
N_i=2(m_i+1),
\qquad
N_i-1=2m_i+1.
\tag{36}
\]
Multiplication proves (12).  The normalization constants differ by the same constant factor \(2^n\), so the two definitions of \(\nu\) agree exactly.  Substitution into (9) and (10) proves (13), completing the symmetric specialization without a second proof.

## External sources

Robert J. McCann, “A Convexity Principle for Interacting Gases,” Advances in Mathematics 128 (1997), 153-179, DOI https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1997.1634, author-hosted primary-source PDF https://www.math.toronto.edu/~mccann/papers/advances.pdf.

Exact result used: Definition 1.1 and equation (7) define, for absolutely continuous probability measures \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), the displacement interpolant \(\lambda_t=[(1-t)\operatorname{id}+t\nabla\phi]_\#\lambda_0\), where \((\nabla\phi)_\#\lambda_0=\lambda_1\) and \(\phi\) is convex.  Proposition 1.3(ii) states that \(\lambda_t\) is absolutely continuous.  Theorem 2.2 states that if \(\mathcal A:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}\) satisfies \(\mathcal A(0)=0\) and \(s\mapsto s^d\mathcal A(s^{-d})\) is convex and nonincreasing on \((0,\infty)\), then \(t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb R^d}\mathcal A(\rho_t(x))\,dx\) is convex along this displacement interpolation, where \(\rho_t\) is the density of \(\lambda_t\).

Applicability: take \(\mathcal A(r)=r\log r\) and \(\mathcal A(0)=0\).  For \(s>0\),
\[
s^d\mathcal A(s^{-d})=-d\log s,
\]
which is convex and nonincreasing, so Theorem 2.2 gives (18) with the displayed parameter \(t=1-1/q\).  The endpoint measures in (17) are absolutely continuous probability measures with bounded compactly supported densities, so their internal entropies are finite; no smoothness or strict positivity of the roofs or densities is required.  The interpolant is absolutely continuous by Proposition 1.3(ii), and its support lies in \(T\) because both endpoints are supported in the convex set \(T\).  Thus adding \(\log V\) to internal energy is exactly relative entropy with respect to \(\mu\), proving (19) with no unproved entropy-displacement premise.
