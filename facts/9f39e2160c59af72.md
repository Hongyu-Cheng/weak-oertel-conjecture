## Statement

There is no finite positive constant \(C\) such that, for every integer \(s\ge1\), every origin-symmetric compact convex body \(Q\subset\mathbb R^s\), every finite even convex \(F:\mathbb R^s\to[0,\infty)\) with \(F(\mathbf0)=0\), and every \(v\in\mathbb R^s\), one has

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-F(y-v)}
\le C\,2^s\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-2F(y)}.
\]

Specifically, take \(s=3\), \(v=\mathbf0\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q&=[-9,9]\times[-54,54]\times[-54,54],\\
F(y)&=\log(5/4)\max\{6|y_1|,|y_2|,|y_3|\}
\qquad(y\in\mathbb R^3),\\
Z_1&=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^3}e^{-F(y)},&
Z_2&=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^3}e^{-2F(y)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These complete finite sums satisfy \(Z_1>2881/4\) and \(Z_2<90\). In particular, \(Z_1/(8Z_2)>2881/2880>1\). For every integer \(k\ge1\), their Cartesian-product construction in dimension \(3k\) has normalized ratio greater than \((2881/2880)^k\).

## Proof

The displayed box \(Q\) is origin-symmetric, compact, convex, and has nonempty interior. The expression defining \(F/\log(5/4)\) is the maximum of the six linear functions \(6y_1,-6y_1,y_2,-y_2,y_3,-y_3\). Since \(\log(5/4)>0\), \(F\) is finite, convex, even, nonnegative on all of \(\mathbb R^3\), and vanishes at the origin.

For an integer point \(y\), write \(m(y)=\max\{6|y_1|,|y_2|,|y_3|\}\). The entire lattice support is
\(Q\cap\mathbb Z^3=\{-9,\ldots,9\}\times\{-54,\ldots,54\}^2\), of cardinality \(19\cdot109^2=225739\). At every one of these points \(m(y)\) is an integer between \(0\) and \(54\), and its exact two weights are \((4/5)^{m(y)}\) and \((16/25)^{m(y)}\).

For each integer \(0\le j\le54\), the condition \(m(y)\le j\) is exactly
\(|y_1|\le\lfloor j/6\rfloor\), \(|y_2|\le j\), and \(|y_3|\le j\). These bounds imply \(y\in Q\), including at \(j=54\). Consequently the complete cumulative and shell counts are

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_j&=(2\lfloor j/6\rfloor+1)(2j+1)^2
\qquad(0\le j\le54),\\
c_0&=1,&
c_j&=N_j-N_{j-1}\qquad(1\le j\le54),\\
Z_1&=\sum_{j=0}^{54}c_j(4/5)^j,&
Z_2&=\sum_{j=0}^{54}c_j(16/25)^j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus all sites on every shell and on the boundary of \(Q\) are included. For an alternative direct evaluation of the same coefficients, subtracting the two squares in (1) gives
\(c_{6a}=384a^2+2\) for \(1\le a\le9\), and
\(c_{6a+r}=8(6a+r)(2a+1)\) for \(0\le a\le8\) and \(1\le r\le5\), together with \(c_0=1\).

Here are exact integer certificates for the needed strict inequalities. Substitution of either displayed formula for \(c_j\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
4\sum_{j=0}^{54}c_j4^j5^{54-j}-2881\cdot5^{54}
&=17774801494955064467736902999977976859>0,\\
90\cdot5^{108}-\sum_{j=0}^{54}c_j16^j25^{54-j}
&=6938264773648001915237683573203503262633545083165524435581609083121261609>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

The first identity divided by \(4\cdot5^{54}\) is \(Z_1-2881/4>0\); the second divided by \(5^{108}\) is \(90-Z_2>0\). Both denominators are positive. Moreover \(Z_2\ge1\), since its origin term is one. It follows that
\(Z_1/(8Z_2)>(2881/4)/(8\cdot90)=2881/2880\).
This is an exact rational inequality; no approximation to the logarithm or exponential is used.

For completeness, (2) is reproducible by the following finite integer calculation of the coefficients in (1):

~~~python
N = [(2 * (j // 6) + 1) * (2 * j + 1)**2 for j in range(55)]
c = [1] + [N[j] - N[j - 1] for j in range(1, 55)]
A = sum(c[j] * 4**j * 5**(54 - j) for j in range(55))
B = sum(c[j] * 16**j * 25**(54 - j) for j in range(55))
assert 4*A - 2881*5**54 == 17774801494955064467736902999977976859
assert 90*5**108 - B == 6938264773648001915237683573203503262633545083165524435581609083121261609
~~~

It remains to verify that a seed in fixed dimension rules out every absolute \(C\). For \(k\ge1\), define \(Q^{(k)}=Q^k\subset\mathbb R^{3k}\), take \(v^{(k)}=\mathbf0\), and, for \(y=(y^{(1)},\ldots,y^{(k)})\) with each block \(y^{(i)}\in\mathbb R^3\), put \(F^{(k)}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^k F(y^{(i)})\). The product box remains an origin-symmetric compact convex body with nonempty interior. The sum defining \(F^{(k)}\) is finite, convex, even, nonnegative, and zero at the origin.

The entire product lattice support is exactly \((Q\cap\mathbb Z^3)^k\). Each of its \(225739^k\) sites has weight \((4/5)^{\sum_i m(y^{(i)})}\), and its squared-potential weight is \((16/25)^{\sum_i m(y^{(i)})}\). Distributivity of the finite sums therefore gives

\[
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{y\in Q^{(k)}\cap\mathbb Z^{3k}}
e^{-F^{(k)}(y-v^{(k)})}}
{\displaystyle2^{3k}\sum_{y\in Q^{(k)}\cap\mathbb Z^{3k}}
e^{-2F^{(k)}(y)}}
=\left(\frac{Z_1}{8Z_2}\right)^k
>\left(1+\frac1{2880}\right)^k
\ge1+\frac{k}{2880}.
\]

The last inequality is the binomial theorem. Given any finite \(C>0\), choose an integer \(k\ge1\) with \(1+k/2880>C\). The displayed complete finite sums violate the proposed bound for that \(C\). All shifts in this construction are zero, so allowing arbitrary real shifts cannot restore a universal constant.
