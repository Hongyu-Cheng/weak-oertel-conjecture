## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(A\) be a real symmetric positive-definite \(n\times n\) matrix, and put \(Q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax\). For \(s>0\), define \(\theta_s=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-sQ(v)}\), the probability distribution \(D_s(v)=e^{-sQ(v)}/\theta_s\), and \(m_s=\mathbb E_s Q(v)\), where \(\mathbb E_s\) denotes expectation under \(D_s\). These are the centered theta sums and quadratic-form normalization used in accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15`.

Choose \(s_*\) to be the smallest minimizer on \([1,2]\) of \(s\mapsto(1+m_s)\sqrt{\theta_2/\theta_s}\), and put \(L_*(A)=4m_{s_*}\). This choice exists, depends only on \(A\), and satisfies \(0<L_*(A)\le2n\).

For every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), let \(w_z=e^{-Q(z-a)}\) for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(W=\sum_z w_z\). Choose any integer minimizer \(j\) of \(Q(z-a)\), and put \(u=j-a\) and \(r=Q(u)\). Define the actual reflected quantities by the formulas of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`: \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), and \(B_j^\Delta(L)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\) for \(L\ge0\). Logarithms are natural. Every sum over displacements is oriented, counting both \(v\) and \(-v\) when \(v\ne0\), and including \(v=0\). In this untruncated table, \(\mathcal U_j=\mathbb Z^n\), \(0<W<\infty\), \(\Delta_j(v)=2Q(v)\), and every cutoff sum at finite \(L\) is finite. Then

\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L_*(A))}{1+L_*(A)}
\ge\frac{w_j\theta_2}{144\pi}
\ge\frac{1}{144\pi}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{1}
\]

For finite \(R\ge r+L_*(A)\), define \(w_z^{(R)}=e^{-Q(z-a)}\mathbf1_{\{Q(z-a)\le R\}}\) and \(W_R=\sum_z w_z^{(R)}\). Define \(\alpha_R(v)\), \(\mathcal U_{j,R}\), \(\Delta_{j,R}(v)\), and \(B_{j,R}^\Delta(L)\) by the same formulas with \(w^{(R)}\) replacing \(w\); in particular, \(\Delta_{j,R}\) is defined only on \(\mathcal U_{j,R}\). These are exactly the finite-table objects \(w_z,W,\alpha,\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j,B_j^\Delta\) of accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15`, with the displayed subscripts distinguishing the two tables here. Then \(w_j^{(R)}=w_j>0\), \(B_{j,R}^\Delta(L_*(A))=B_j^\Delta(L_*(A))\), and

\[
\frac{B_{j,R}^\Delta(L_*(A))}{1+L_*(A)}
\ge\frac{1}{144\pi}\,2^{-n}W
\ge\frac{1}{144\pi}\,2^{-n}W_R.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, \(R\ge r+2n\) is sufficient. Equalities in the endpoint condition \(Q(z-a)\le R\) are included.

For any positive integer \(d\) and any convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define \(C_z=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\) and suppose \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=w_z^{(R)}\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), with empty and lower-dimensional fibers assigned volume zero. Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and \(\ell=-\log(1-e^{-1})\). There is a point \(p^\star=(j,x^\star)\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge [\ell/(2304\pi)]2^{-n}W_R\). Equivalently, \(h_S(p^\star)\ge [\ell/(2304\pi)]2^{-n}W_R\), with \(h_S\) as in the problem statement and accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. The point is selected before the halfspace. The coefficient \(\ell/(2304\pi)\) is an absolute number in \((0,1/e)\).

The realization in accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15` applies for every positive integer \(d\ge2R\), since the present radius condition implies \(R>0\). Explicitly, if \(B_d\) is the Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb R^d\), that realization is

\[
C=\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
Q(z-a)\le R,\quad
\|x\|_2\le\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)^{-1/d}e^{-Q(z-a)/d}\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

No claim that the untruncated table is the fiber-volume table of a compact convex body is needed. The depth conclusion concerns the finite tables specified above.

## Proof

Positive definiteness bounds \(Q(x)\) below by a positive multiple of \(\|x\|_2^2\). Consequently every bounded ellipsoid has finitely many integer points, the minimum defining \(r\) is attained, and all Gaussian sums above are finite and positive. On every compact interval \(s\in[s_0,s_1]\subset(0,\infty)\), Gaussian decay bounds the sums of \(Q(v)^k e^{-sQ(v)}\), for every fixed nonnegative integer \(k\), uniformly by summable sequences. This justifies all derivatives of centered Gaussian sums used below. In particular, \(m_s\) is continuous and strictly positive: at least one nonzero integer vector has positive \(D_s\)-probability and positive energy.

Put \(T(v)=-2u^{\mathsf T}Av\); this linear functional is distinct from the auxiliary scalar cutoff called \(T\) in accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15`. The quadratic identities and nearestness give \(Q(u+v)=r+Q(v)-T(v)\ge r\) and \(Q(u-v)=r+Q(v)+T(v)\ge r\) for every integer \(v\). Therefore \(|T(v)|\le Q(v)\). They also give the exact canonical identities \(\Delta_j(v)=2Q(v)\) and \(\alpha(v)=w_j e^{-Q(v)-|T(v)|}\). Thus the original curvature is \(2Q\), even when an auxiliary inverse temperature \(s\ne1\) is used. Also \(\alpha(0)=w_j\), and \(w_j\) is a largest weight.

We first prove the parity bound \(W\le2^n w_j\theta_2\). For a parity class \(c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(W_c\) be its total untruncated weight. The map from an ordered same-parity pair \((x,y)\) to \((z,v)=((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)\) is a bijection onto \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z^n\). All summands are nonnegative, so Tonelli's theorem permits the rearrangement even for this infinite table. Cauchy--Schwarz over the \(2^n\) parity classes and the quadratic identity give

\[
2^{-n}W^2
\le\sum_c W_c^2
=\sum_{z,v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-2Q(z-a)}e^{-2Q(v)}
=\theta_2\sum_z w_z^2
\le\theta_2 w_j W.
\tag{4}
\]

Division by \(W>0\) proves the bound.

We next establish the Fourier and monotonicity estimates with their normalizations. Put \(Q^*(y)=y^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}y\). For functions on \(\mathbb R^n\), use \(\widehat f(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\eta^{\mathsf T}x}\,dx\). The Gaussian transform is

\[
\widehat{e^{-sQ}}(\eta)
=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{s^{n/2}\sqrt{\det A}}
e^{-\pi^2Q^*(\eta)/s}.
\tag{5}
\]

For completeness, orthogonally diagonalizing \(A\) reduces the integral to one-dimensional Gaussian transforms. For an eigenvalue \(\lambda>0\), differentiation in the frequency and integration by parts give \(I'(\eta)=-2\pi^2\eta I(\eta)/(s\lambda)\), while \(I(0)=\sqrt{\pi/(s\lambda)}\). Solving this equation and multiplying the factors proves (5). The operations are justified by Gaussian decay.

For a fixed \(\xi\in\mathbb R\), set \(b_\xi=(\xi/\pi)Au\). Then \(e^{i\xi T(v)}=e^{-2\pi i b_\xi^{\mathsf T}v}\). Multiplication by this character shifts the transform in (5) from \(\eta\) to \(\eta+b_\xi\). Poisson summation, applied to the Schwartz function \(e^{-sQ(x)}e^{-2\pi i b_\xi^{\mathsf T}x}\), therefore gives the characteristic function

\[
\varphi_s(\xi):=\mathbb E_s e^{i\xi T(v)}
=\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-\pi^2Q^*(k+b_\xi)/s}}
{\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-\pi^2Q^*(k)/s}}.
\tag{6}
\]

This instance of Poisson summation can also be obtained by periodizing the Schwartz function: its integer Fourier coefficients are (5) evaluated at \(k+b_\xi\), the Fourier series is absolutely convergent, and evaluation of that series at zero gives (6). The prefactor in (5) cancels. All numerator and denominator terms in (6) are positive, so \(\varphi_s(\xi)>0\). Symmetry of \(D_s\) and oddness of \(T\) also show directly that \(\varphi_s(\xi)=\mathbb E_s\cos(\xi T)\), and the characteristic-function bound gives \(\varphi_s(\xi)\le1\).

For fixed \(b\), let \(\mathbb E^*_{b,s}\) denote expectation of \(Y\) on \(\mathbb Z^n+b\) with probability proportional to \(e^{-\pi^2Q^*(Y)/s}\). The dual Gaussian series and their derivatives converge locally uniformly in \(s>0\) for fixed \(b\). Differentiating (6), with \(b_\xi\) fixed as \(s\) varies, yields

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\log\varphi_s(\xi)
=\frac{\pi^2}{s^2}
\left(\mathbb E^*_{b_\xi,s}Q^*(Y)-\mathbb E^*_{0,s}Q^*(Y)\right).
\tag{7}
\]

Here the sign of the derivative follows from the shifted-covariance theorem, with an explicit Euclidean normalization. Let \(A^{-1/2}\) be the symmetric positive-definite inverse square root, let \(\mathcal L=A^{-1/2}\mathbb Z^n\), let \(x_\xi=A^{-1/2}b_\xi\), and let \(t_s=\sqrt{s/\pi}\). These give a full-rank Euclidean lattice, an arbitrary real shift, and a positive Gaussian width. If \(Y\) has the shifted dual law just defined, then \(Z=A^{-1/2}Y\) has the law \(D_{\mathcal L+x_\xi,t_s}\) with weights \(e^{-\pi\|Z\|_2^2/t_s^2}\). Corollary 3.2 of Regev and Stephens-Davidowitz, stated precisely in EXTERNAL SOURCES, applies at unit width to \(t_s^{-1}\mathcal L\) and shift \(t_s^{-1}x_\xi\). Scaling its covariance inequality by \(t_s^2\) gives the same inequality at width \(t_s\). The centered mean is zero. Taking the Euclidean trace thus proves

\[
\mathbb E^*_{b_\xi,s}Q^*(Y)-\mathbb E^*_{0,s}Q^*(Y)
\ge\left\|A^{-1/2}\mathbb E^*_{b_\xi,s}Y\right\|_2^2
\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (7)--(8) prove \(\partial_s\varphi_s(\xi)\ge0\) for every real \(\xi\) and every \(s>0\). In the source's notation, (6) is exactly \(f_{\mathcal L,t_s}(x_\xi)\); its Proposition 4.1 has the same direction because \(t_s\) increases with \(s\). In particular, the source's width parameter is \(\sqrt{s/\pi}\), not the primal inverse temperature \(s\) itself. No diagonal-lattice hypothesis enters this application.

The zero-frequency Poisson identity also bounds the centered energy. Setting \(b=0\) before differentiating the prefactor in (5) gives

\[
m_s=-\frac{d}{ds}\log\theta_s
=\frac{n}{2s}-\frac{\pi^2}{s^2}\mathbb E^*_{0,s}Q^*(Y)
\le\frac{n}{2s}.
\tag{9}
\]

We now use the Laplace kernel without any variance bound on \(T\). Put \(M_s=\mathbb E_s e^{-|T|}\). With the one-dimensional angular-frequency convention, direct integration over the positive and negative half-lines gives \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-|t|}e^{-i\xi t}\,dt=2/(1+\xi^2)\). The function and its transform are integrable, so Fourier inversion gives \(e^{-|t|}=\pi^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{i\xi t}(1+\xi^2)^{-1}\,d\xi\). Hence

\[
M_s=\frac1\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{\varphi_s(\xi)}{1+\xi^2}\,d\xi.
\tag{10}
\]

Fubini is valid because the integral of \((1+\xi^2)^{-1}\mathbb E_s|e^{i\xi T}|\) is \(\pi\). For clarity, differentiating this integral is valid as well. Differentiation of the primal characteristic-function sum gives \(\partial_s\varphi_s(\xi)=m_s\varphi_s(\xi)-\mathbb E_s[Qe^{i\xi T}]\), whose absolute value is at most \(2m_s\). By (9) this is at most \(n/s_0\), uniformly in \(\xi\) and \(s\in[s_0,s_1]\). Multiplication by \((1+\xi^2)^{-1}\) supplies an integrable dominating function for differentiation of (10). Thus (7)--(8) imply \(M_s'\ge0\). Differentiating instead its defining lattice sum, again using Gaussian domination and \(e^{-|T|}\le1\), yields \(M_s'=m_sM_s-\mathbb E_s[Qe^{-|T|}]\). We have proved

\[
\mathbb E_s[Qe^{-|T|}]\le m_sM_s.
\tag{11}
\]

To lower-bound \(M_s\), the inequality \(\cos t\ge1-|t|\) and \(|T(v)|\le Q(v)\) give \(\varphi_s(\xi)\ge1-m_s|\xi|\). Set \(\varepsilon_s=(1+m_s)^{-1}\le1\). On \([-\varepsilon_s,\varepsilon_s]\) this lower bound is nonnegative, and the denominator \(1+\xi^2\) is at most two. Outside the interval, the integrand in (10) remains nonnegative by (6). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_s
&\ge\frac1\pi\int_{-\varepsilon_s}^{\varepsilon_s}
\frac{1-m_s|\xi|}{1+\xi^2}\,d\xi
\ge\frac1\pi\int_0^{\varepsilon_s}(1-m_s\xi)\,d\xi\\
&=\frac{m_s+2}{2\pi(1+m_s)^2}
\ge\frac1{2\pi(1+m_s)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Since \(M_s>0\), the masses \(e^{-sQ(v)-|T(v)|}/(\theta_sM_s)\) define a probability law on \(\mathbb Z^n\). Equation (11) states that its mean energy is at most \(m_s\). Markov's inequality, applied to the event \(Q(v)>2m_s\), consequently gives \(\mathbb E_s[e^{-|T|}\mathbf1_{\{Q\le2m_s\}}]\ge M_s/2\). Division by \(m_s\) is legitimate by its strict positivity proved above. The closed cutoff includes every equality case.

For every \(s\in[1,2]\), use the actual identity for \(\alpha(v)\), the inequality \(e^{-Q(v)}\ge e^{-sQ(v)}\), and (12). The resulting bound is

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(4m_s)
&=w_j\sum_{Q(v)\le2m_s}e^{-Q(v)-|T(v)|}\\
&\ge w_j\sum_{Q(v)\le2m_s}e^{-sQ(v)-|T(v)|}
\ge\frac{w_j\theta_sM_s}{2}
\ge\frac{w_j\theta_s}{4\pi(1+m_s)},\\
\frac{B_j^\Delta(4m_s)}{1+4m_s}
&\ge\frac{w_j}{16\pi}\frac{\theta_s}{(1+m_s)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The last step uses \(1+4m_s\le4(1+m_s)\). Every sum in this cutoff is finite, and none of the operations changes the oriented counting or the central term.

It remains to choose \(s\) using only \(A\). Let \(f(s)=\sqrt{\theta_2/\theta_s}\) on \([1,2]\). Because \(\theta_s\) is nonincreasing, \(0<f(s)\le1\) and \(f(2)=1\); differentiation gives \(f'(s)=m_sf(s)/2\). It follows that

\[
\int_1^2(1+m_s)f(s)\,ds
=\int_1^2f(s)\,ds+2(f(2)-f(1))
\le3.
\tag{14}
\]

The integrand is continuous on the compact interval, so it has a nonempty compact set of minimizers and a smallest minimizer \(s_*\). Its minimum is at most its integral over this length-one interval. Thus \((1+m_{s_*})\sqrt{\theta_2/\theta_{s_*}}\le3\), or \(\theta_{s_*}/(1+m_{s_*})^2\ge\theta_2/9\). Combining this with (13) and then (4) proves (1). Equation (9) gives \(L_*(A)=4m_{s_*}\le2n/s_*\le2n\); strict positivity was already established. The minimizing function contains no shift or choice of nearest point, so the same \(s_*\) and \(L_*(A)\) work for every \(a\) and every nearest \(j\).

Now let \(R\) satisfy the finite-radius hypothesis. For a displacement in the selected untruncated cutoff, the exact endpoint condition is

\[
\max_{\pm}Q(j\pm v-a)
=r+Q(v)+|T(v)|
\le r+2Q(v)
\le r+L_*(A)
\le R.
\tag{15}
\]

Both endpoint weights and the central weight are therefore retained, with their original values. Conversely, any displacement counted by \(B_{j,R}^\Delta(L_*(A))\) has two positive endpoints, and the same quadratic identity gives \(\Delta_{j,R}(v)=2Q(v)\le L_*(A)\). Its weight agrees with \(\alpha(v)\). This proves the exact equality of the two selected cutoff sums. Also \(0<W_R\le W\), because \(j\) is retained. Equation (2) follows from (1). This argument uses no Fourier claim for a hard-truncated distribution; it retains the specific finite set of terms certified in the untruncated proof.

For the geometric conclusion, use the actual integer-fiber volumes of the stipulated convex body \(C\). Its objects \(w_z,W,\alpha,\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j,B_j^\Delta\) in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` are precisely \(w_z^{(R)},W_R,\alpha_R,\mathcal U_{j,R},\Delta_{j,R},B_{j,R}^\Delta\) here. Its fixed-center cutoff conclusion, applied at \(j,L_*(A)\), provides \(x^\star\in C_j\) of depth at least \((\ell/16)B_{j,R}^\Delta(L_*(A))/(1+L_*(A))\). The hypotheses hold: the table is finite, the central weight is positive, the cutoff includes \(v=0\), and every curvature used belongs to a positive-volume reflected pair. Substitution of (2) gives \([\ell/(2304\pi)]2^{-n}W_R\). That accepted fact selects one point before all containing ambient halfspaces and includes vertical halfspaces and positive mass on their boundaries. It therefore supplies precisely the asserted quantifier order and closed-halfspace depth. Positivity of \(\ell\) is immediate, and \(e>2\) gives \(\ell<1\); hence \(0<\ell/(2304\pi)<1/e\).

Finally, the radius condition and \(L_*(A)>0\) ensure \(R>0\). The realization paragraph and proof of accepted fact `a7090e5d5aa26b15` give exactly (3) whenever the positive integer \(d\) satisfies \(d\ge2R\). This use of its realization does not require a uniform bound on \(r\): its construction uses only \(R>0\) and \(d\ge2R\) (equivalently, for each individual table its stated parameter \(r_0\) can be set equal to the finite value \(r\)). To make the applicability explicit, on \(Q(z-a)\le R\) its radius is a positive continuous multiple of \(e^{-Q(z-a)/d}\), and its second directional derivative in direction \(h\) is bounded above by that positive multiple times \((4R/d^2-2/d)h^{\mathsf T}Ah\le0\). This proves concavity of the radius. The norm triangle inequality proves convexity of (3); compactness follows from the compact base ellipsoid, and \(R>0\) gives nonempty interior. Its fiber volumes are exactly \(e^{-Q(z-a)}\) on the closed ellipsoid, including its boundary, and zero outside. Thus the existing realization supplies actual convex bodies for every stated \(d\), while the depth implication applies in any \(d\) for which such a body is given. This completes the claimed finite-body applicability without asserting the remaining radius range or the unrestricted weak Oertel conjecture.

## External sources

Oded Regev and Noah Stephens-Davidowitz, “An Inequality for Gaussians on Lattices,” arXiv:1502.04796v3 (24 December 2016), [primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04796v3). The following are its exact mathematical statements with the width parameter renamed \(t\) to distinguish it from our inverse temperature \(s\).

For a full-rank Euclidean lattice \(\mathcal L\subset\mathbb R^n\), define \(\rho_t(y)=e^{-\pi\|y\|_2^2/t^2}\) and \(\rho_t(E)=\sum_{y\in E}\rho_t(y)\), let \(D_{\mathcal L+x,t}\) assign masses proportional to \(\rho_t\), and set \(f_{\mathcal L,t}(x)=\rho_t(\mathcal L+x)/\rho_t(\mathcal L)\).

[Corollary 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04796v3#S3) at \(t=1\) asserts \(\operatorname{Cov}(D_{\mathcal L+x,1})\succeq\mathbb E_{Z\sim D_{\mathcal L,1}}[ZZ^{\mathsf T}]\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\). Scaling gives the corresponding statement at every \(t>0\).

[Proposition 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04796v3#S4) asserts \(\partial_t\log f_{\mathcal L,t}(x)\ge [t/(2\pi)]\|\nabla_x\log f_{\mathcal L,t}(x)\|_2^2\).

Applicability is checked in (7)--(8): \(\mathcal L=A^{-1/2}\mathbb Z^n\), \(x=A^{-1/2}b_\xi\), \(t=\sqrt{s/\pi}\). Covariance comparison is taken after this Euclidean transformation. All other estimates are proved above or obtained from the two declared accepted facts.
