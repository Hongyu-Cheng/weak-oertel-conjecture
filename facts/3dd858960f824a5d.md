## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, $n\ge0$, and let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be compact convex bodies of positive volume, with $\sum_z\lambda_z K_z\subset K_p$ whenever $\lambda_z\ge0$, $\sum_z\lambda_z=1$ and $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$. Let $\mu$ sum $d$-volume on the lifted fibers and let $b_{\rm base}(p)$ be the minimum weight of a closed base halfspace containing $p$, using weights $|K_z|$. Include the whole-base trace, which is the sole trace in rank zero. Every closed ambient halfspace that contains $\{p\}\times K_p$ has mass at least $b_{\rm base}(p)$.

Fix $y\in\operatorname{int}K_p$, a positive definite matrix $\Sigma$ and $\delta>0$. Write $\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}$ and let $b_\delta(p,y)$ be the infimum of masses of the caps
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\delta\Gamma(u)\ge0,\qquad u\ne0,
$$
and the pure-base caps. For $R(p,y)=\max_{X\in K_p}\sqrt{(X-y)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}(X-y)}$,
$$
b_\delta(p,y)<b_{\rm base}(p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta<R(p,y),\qquad
b_\delta(p,y)=b_{\rm base}(p)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\delta\ge R(p,y).
$$
Under the strict gap, every minimizing cap is nonvertical and its boundary cuts the interior of $K_p$.

## Proof

Let $H$ contain $\{p\}\times K_p$. If every sampled fiber is contained in $H$, its mass is the total weight, at least $b_{\rm base}(p)$. Otherwise let $A$ be the set of bases whose fibers are not wholly contained in $H$, and choose a strictly excluded point in each such fiber. If $p\in\operatorname{conv}(A)$, compatibility puts a convex combination of these points in the $p$-fiber. The combination is strictly excluded by the defining affine inequality of $H$, which contradicts whole-fiber capture. Hence $p$ is outside $\operatorname{conv}(A)$. Strict separation gives a closed base halfspace containing $p$ and avoiding $A$. All its selected fibers are wholly contained in $H$, proving the lower bound.

If $\delta<R(p,y)$, covariance-norm duality provides $u$ with $\Gamma(u)=1$ and $\min_{X\in K_p}u\cdot(X-y)<-\delta$. The strict inequality holds on a subset of positive $d$-volume, by convex interpolation with an interior ball. The base infimum is attained since there are finitely many traces. Move its boundary through $p$, which only decreases its weight, and represent it by $a_0\cdot(z-p)\ge0$. Use $a_0=0$ for the whole-base trace. In the buffered cap with $a=L a_0$, sufficiently large finite $L$ captures every positive-drift fiber and excludes every negative-drift fiber. Each zero-drift fiber contributes at most its full mass, and the zero-drift fiber at $p$ loses positive mass. The resulting cap is strictly lighter than $b_{\rm base}(p)$.

If $\delta\ge R(p,y)$, duality gives $u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\delta\Gamma(u)$ for every $X\in K_p$. All buffered caps contain the whole own fiber, so the first part bounds their mass below by $b_{\rm base}(p)$. A pure-base minimizer attains this value. Under the strict gap a minimizing cap is nonvertical and cannot contain the whole own fiber. It strictly contains $y$ and hence a neighborhood of $y$ in the own fiber. Its boundary therefore cuts the own interior.
