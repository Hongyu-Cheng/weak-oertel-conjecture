## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $U$ be uniform on $\Delta_d$, and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)>0}t/\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)^{1/d}$. Let $x=(a,m,\ldots,m)$ with $0<a<m$, and write $\beta=(m-a)/m$. In a direction with zero low-coordinate component and high-coordinate components $w$, the directional derivative from positive steps is
$$
\min_{s\in\mathbb R}\left[s+\frac{\mathbb E(W-s)_+}{\beta}\right],
$$
where $W$ is the linear form with coefficients $w$ on a uniform $\Delta_{d-1}$ point. In particular, for the scalar parameter increasing one high coordinate, the right and left derivatives are
$$
D_+=1-\frac{d-1}{d}\beta^{1/(d-1)},\qquad
D_-=1-\frac{d-1}{d\beta}\big[1-(1-\beta)^{d/(d-1)}\big].
$$
They satisfy $D_+>D_-$. At a constant strictly positive row the corresponding derivatives are $1$ and $0$. For every nonnegative row and each of its strictly positive coordinates the two coordinate derivatives exist and $D_+-D_-\ge0$.

## Proof

For the first assertion write $L$ for the low barycentric coordinate. It has density $d(1-L)^{d-1}$ on $[0,1]$, and the high barycentric coordinates divided by $1-L$ are independent of it and uniform on $\Delta_{d-1}$, by simplex slicing. For $\epsilon>0$ the perturbed affine height is $m-(m-a)L+\epsilon(1-L)W$. For bounded $s$, its upper tail at $m+\epsilon s$ is therefore the probability of
$$
L<\frac{\epsilon(W-s)_+}{m-a+\epsilon W}.
$$
For sufficiently small $\epsilon$ the right-hand side lies in $[0,1)$. Integrating the density of $L$ and expanding the ratio gives, uniformly on bounded $s$,
$$
\frac{m+\epsilon s}{F_{x+\epsilon(0,w)}(m+\epsilon s)^{1/d}}
=m+\epsilon\left[s+\beta^{-1}\mathbb E(W-s)_+\right]+O(\epsilon^2).
$$

To localize minimizing thresholds, put $M=\max_j|w_j|$. The full threshold gives capacity at most $m+\epsilon M$. Any minimizing threshold $t$ can be chosen at most this value. If $t+\epsilon M<m$, cap inclusion gives $F_{x+\epsilon(0,w)}(t)\le F_x(t+\epsilon M)$, so the unperturbed vertex-simplex formula and the upper bound give
$$
\frac{t(m-a)}{t+\epsilon M-a}\le m+\epsilon M.
$$
The denominator is positive because the perturbed cap has positive measure. Rearranging gives $t\ge(m+\epsilon M)(a-\epsilon M)/(a+\epsilon M)=m-O(\epsilon)$. If $t+\epsilon M\ge m$ the same localization follows directly. Thus the scaled minimizing thresholds $s=(t-m)/\epsilon$ are bounded. The expansion at those thresholds and comparison with each fixed $s$ prove the directional formula. The convex expression in $s$ has a minimum in $[\min W,\max W]$, with the minimum equal to the mean of the upper $\beta$ fraction of $W$, splitting any atom at its boundary. This follows from its left and right slopes $1-\Pr(W\ge s)/\beta$ and $1-\Pr(W>s)/\beta$ and evaluation at a minimizing quantile.

For the increasing direction of a single high coordinate, $W=Z$ has density $(d-1)(1-z)^{d-2}$ on $[0,1]$. Its upper $\beta$ quantile starts at $1-\beta^{1/(d-1)}$ and the mean of that tail is $D_+$ as displayed. With $W=-Z$ the value is minus the mean of the lowest $\beta$ fraction of $Z$, so the left derivative equals that lower-tail mean $D_-$. Indeed the lower $\beta$ fraction ends at $1-(1-\beta)^{1/(d-1)}$, and integration of $z(d-1)(1-z)^{d-2}$ gives the displayed $D_-$. For $p=d/(d-1)>1$ the difference is $(d-1)[1-\beta^p-(1-\beta)^p]/(d\beta)>0$. Varying the low entry alone keeps the capacity equal to $m$ locally by the full-cut classification.

For a constant row $m\mathbf 1$, lowering one coordinate keeps capacity $m$ by that classification. When one coordinate is increased by $\epsilon>0$, a minimizing threshold is $m+\epsilon s_\epsilon$ with $0<s_\epsilon\le1$. The ratio is at least $m+\epsilon s_\epsilon$ and is at most $m+\epsilon$. If $s_\epsilon\le 1-\delta$ along a subsequence for some $\delta>0$, then the captured probability, $\Pr(U_j\le s_\epsilon)$, would be bounded above by a constant strictly less than one along it, contradicting the upper bound as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Thus $s_\epsilon\to1$ and $D_+=1$.

For strictly positive rows with a proper minimum, the unique-threshold lemma gives a derivative. It remains to check rows with zeros while varying a positive coordinate. With exactly one zero, use the $d$ positive-coordinate barycentric variables $u_j$ as simplex coordinates. The cap is $\{u\ge0:\sum_j u_j\le1,\ \sum_jx_ju_j\le t\}$, a subset of the orthant simplex $\{u\ge0:\sum_jx_ju_j\le t\}$ and equal to it for $0<t\le\min_{x_j>0}x_j$. The cap fraction is at most $t^d/\prod_{x_j>0}x_j$, with equality there, so the capacity is the geometric mean of the positive entries. This is smooth when the zero is retained. For $k$ positive entries with $1\le k<d$, the event that their total barycentric weight is at most $t/\max_j x_j$ has probability bounded below by a positive multiple of $t^k$ near zero, by the marginal density proportional to $(1-\sum u_j)^{d-k}$ on the supported coordinates. It is included in the cap, so the capacity is zero. This remains true on varying a positive coordinate with the support fixed. The all-zero row also has capacity zero and has no positive coordinate.
