## Statement

Let $A,K,C$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, with volumes $a,b,c>0$ and $(A+C)/2\subseteq K$. For $0<t<\min(a,c)$, homothetically contract $A,C$ about any points in them to volumes $a-t,c-t$ and expand $K$ about any point in it to volume $b+2t$. The resulting $A_t,K_t,C_t$ are the exact integer fibers of their lifted hull at bases $-1,0,1$, and have the same total volume $M=a+b+c$. For every $1<p\le1+1/d$ the sum of the $p$th powers of fiber volumes strictly increases, so in particular $[(a^{1+1/d}+b^{1+1/d}+c^{1+1/d})/M]^d$ increases under this transfer. For every $p>1+1/d$ and positively homothetic endpoint bodies of unequal volumes with $K=(A+C)/2$, that sum instead decreases for all sufficiently small positive $t$.

## Proof

The contracted bodies are subsets of their old endpoints by convexity. The expanded middle contains $K$, by applying the same argument to the inverse contraction. Thus $(A_t+C_t)/2\subseteq K_t$. Any convex combination of the three lifts giving base zero has equal coefficients at the two endpoints. Grouping those terms, its continuous coordinate belongs to the convex hull of $K_t$ and $(A_t+C_t)/2$, which is $K_t$. The endpoint bases are extreme and there are no other integer bases, so the census is exact. The specified volumes sum to $M$.

Brunn–Minkowski gives $b^{1/d}\ge(a^{1/d}+c^{1/d})/2$. For $q=p-1\in(0,1/d]$, raise to $dq$ and use concavity of the power $dq$ to obtain $2b^q\ge a^q+c^q$. The derivative of the new power sum is
$$
p[2(b+2t)^q-(a-t)^q-(c-t)^q],
$$
which is nonnegative at zero and strictly positive for positive admissible $t$. Integration proves the strict increase, and the normalization by $M$ is unchanged. For positively homothetic endpoints of unequal volumes, their exact midpoint volume satisfies the Brunn–Minkowski bound with equality. If $q>1/d$, strict convexity of the power $dq$ gives $2b^q<a^q+c^q$. Thus the derivative at zero is strictly negative and the claimed decrease follows by continuity. For example in dimension two the squares of sides $1,2,3$ at bases $-1,0,1$ give volumes $1,4,9$, and at $p=2$ the change is $-4t+6t^2$, equal to $-5/8$ at $t=1/4$.
