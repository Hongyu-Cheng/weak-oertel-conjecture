## Statement

For \(D_N=\{-1,0,1\}^N\), let
\[
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=\frac{e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf z}}{Z_{D_N}(\bm{\theta})},
\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),
\]
where all halfspaces are ambient closed halfspaces, and define
\[
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_N}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\qquad
F_{D_N}(\bm{\theta})=M_3(p_{\bm{\theta}}).
\]
Put \(v_N=\inf_{\bm{\theta}}F_{D_N}(\bm{\theta})\) and \(v_0=1\).

Let \(x_*\in(2,3)\) be the unique root of
\[
x^3+2x^2-2x-29=0,
\]
and define
\[
\theta_*=\operatorname{arcosh}(x_*/2),\quad
a_*=\frac{x_*^4-4x_*^2+29}{(x_*+1)^4},\quad
c_*=\frac{x_*^4+1}{(x_*+1)^4}.
\]
There are absolute \(C_1,C_2>0\) such that
\[
C_1(N+1)^{-3/2}a_*^N
\le v_N\le C_2(N+1)^{-3/2}a_*^N.
\]
Furthermore, \(v_N<v_{N-1}\), every global minimizer is finite, and for each \(\delta>0\) there is \(C_\delta<\infty\), independent of \(N\), such that every global minimizer satisfies
\[
\#\{j:\ ||\theta_j|-\theta_*|\ge\delta\}\le C_\delta.
\]

For any sequence of global-minimizer laws \(p_N\), let
\(\alpha_{\mathbf c}=p_N(D_N\cap(\mathbf c+2\mathbb Z^N))\). Then
\[
\left(\frac{M_3(p_N)}{\sum_{\mathbf c}\alpha_{\mathbf c}^4}\right)^{1/N}
\to\frac{a_*}{c_*}<1,
\qquad
8^NM_3(p_N)\to\infty.
\]

## Proof

We first apply the exact depth formula in accepted fact 089a4139d9314589 with its normalization made explicit. Equations (3)--(5) of that fact concern the box \(Q_N=\{0,1,2\}^N\), unnormalized weights \(w_{\mathbf x}=\prod_i t_i^{x_i}\), total weight \(W=\sum_{\mathbf x}w_{\mathbf x}\), normalized law \(p_{\mathbf x}=w_{\mathbf x}/W\), and normalized depth \(\tau_p=\tau_w/W\). Its equation (5) states
\[
\tau_w(\mathbf x)=w_{\mathbf x}\frac{\widetilde T_{I(\mathbf x)}+1}{2},
\qquad
\widetilde T_I=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\{-1,0,1\}^I}
e^{-|\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_iv_i|},
\quad \alpha_i=-\log t_i>0.
\]

Suppose first that every \(\theta_i\ne0\). The affine isometry from \(D_N\) to \(Q_N\) defined coordinatewise by
\[
x_i=1-\operatorname{sgn}(\theta_i)z_i
\]
is translation by \(1\) for negative tilts and translation followed by coordinate reflection for positive tilts. It transports \(p_{\bm\theta}\) to the normalization of the weights
\[
w_{\mathbf x}=\prod_i t_i^{x_i},
\qquad t_i=e^{-|\theta_i|},
\]
because \(w_{\mathbf x}=e^{-\sum_i|\theta_i|}e^{\bm\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\). Ambient halfspace depth is invariant under this affine isometry, and \(x_i=1\) exactly when \(z_i=0\). Moreover, the coordinatewise bijection \(v_i\mapsto\operatorname{sgn}(\theta_i)v_i\) on \(\{-1,0,1\}^I\) shows that \(\widetilde T_I\) equals
\[
T_I=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{I}}
e^{-|\sum_{j\in I}\theta_ju_j|}.
\]
Thus accepted equation (5), divided by \(W\) as prescribed in its equation (4), gives
\[
\tau_{p_{\bm\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=p_{\bm\theta}(\mathbf z)\frac{T_{I(\mathbf z)}+1}{2},
\qquad I(\mathbf z)=\{j:z_j=0\}. \tag{1}
\]
This remains valid when some \(\theta_i=0\). Indeed, on a finite support \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) is the minimum of the finitely many linear mass functions corresponding to the distinct subsets \(D_N\cap H\) with \(H\ni\mathbf z\), so it is continuous in \(p\). Both sides of (1) are therefore continuous in \(\bm\theta\), and nonzero tilts are dense.

Write
\[
Z_j=1+2\cosh\theta_j,
\qquad A_j=2\cosh4\theta_j.
\]
Substituting (1) into the definition of \(F_{D_N}\) and grouping points by their zero-coordinate set gives
\[
F_{D_N}(\bm{\theta})
=\frac1{8\prod_jZ_j^4}
\sum_{I\subseteq[N]}(T_I+1)^3\prod_{j\notin I}A_j. \tag{2}
\]

We next prove finite attainment and strict decrease by induction on \(N\). Compactify each parameter by allowing \(\theta_j=\pm\infty\). The corresponding product laws form a compact set, and \(M_3\) is continuous there by the same finite-minimum observation used above. A boundary law fixes at least one coordinate at an endpoint; after deleting all fixed coordinates, its moment is the moment of a finite ternary tilt in some dimension \(k<N\), because ambient halfspaces restricted to that supporting face give all halfspaces of the face. By the induction hypothesis its value is at least \(v_k\ge v_{N-1}\).

Conversely, take a finite minimizing law in dimension \(N-1\), and write \(t_{\mathbf z}=\tau_p(\mathbf z)\). Add a coordinate whose unnormalized layer weights are \(B,1,B^{-1}\). At a point in either outer layer, an ambient halfspace can isolate that layer and realize a minimizing halfspace for \(t_{\mathbf z}\) inside it. At a middle-layer point, extend such a minimizing halfspace with a sufficiently large coefficient in the new coordinate; it excludes the heavy layer, has the chosen trace on the middle layer, and includes the light layer. Therefore
\[
F_{D_N}\le
\frac{(B^4+B^{-4})v_{N-1}
+\mathbb E_p(t_{\mathbf Z}+B^{-1})^3}
{(B+1+B^{-1})^4}
=v_{N-1}(1-4/B+O(B^{-2}))+O(B^{-4}).
\]
For all sufficiently large \(B\), this is below \(v_{N-1}\). Thus the minimum on the compactification cannot lie on its boundary, so it is attained at a finite tilt, and \(v_N<v_{N-1}\). The base case is \(v_0=1\).

We use the following small-ball estimate. For every fixed \(B>0\), there is \(b_B>0\) such that
\[
\sum_{\mathbf u\in\{-1,0,1\}^k}
e^{-|\sum_i\lambda_i u_i|}
\ge b_B3^k/\sqrt{k+1}
\quad\text{whenever }|\lambda_i|\le B. \tag{3}
\]
Let \(U_i\) be independent uniform ternary variables and \(Y=\sum_i\lambda_iU_i\). Then
\[
\sigma^2=\tfrac23\sum_i\lambda_i^2,
\qquad
\sum_i\mathbb E|\lambda_iU_i|^3\le B\sigma^2.
\]
The nonidentically distributed Berry--Esseen theorem gives Kolmogorov error at most \(C_{\rm BE}B/\sigma\) when \(\sigma>0\).

Choose a sufficiently large absolute \(L\). If \(\sigma\le LB\), Chebyshev gives \(\mathbb P(|Y|\le2LB)\ge3/4\). If \(\sigma>LB\), the standard normal probability of this interval is at least \(cLB/\sigma\); subtracting the two Berry--Esseen endpoint errors and taking \(L\) sufficiently large gives probability at least \(c'B/\sigma\ge c''/\sqrt{k}\). Multiplication by \(e^{-2LB}\) proves (3). The case \(\sigma=0\) is immediate.

Define
\[
a(t)=\frac{2\cosh4t+27}{(1+2\cosh t)^4},
\qquad
b(t)=\frac{2\cosh4t}{(1+2\cosh t)^4}.
\]
With \(x=2\cosh t\), the derivative of \(a\) with respect to \(x\) is
\[
\frac{4(x^3+2x^2-2x-29)}{(x+1)^5}.
\]
The cubic is strictly increasing for \(x\ge2\), is negative at \(2\), and is positive at \(3\). Hence \(a\) has its unique minimum in \(|t|\) at \(\theta_*\), with value \(a_*\). Since \(a_*\le a(0)=29/81<1\) and \(b(t)\to1\) as \(|t|\to\infty\), fix \(B>\theta_*\) and \(b_0>a_*\) such that \(b(t)\ge b_0\) for \(|t|>B\).

For an arbitrary tilt, let \(J=\{j:|\theta_j|\le B\}\). In (2), retain only \(I\subseteq J\) and apply (3). Expanding the resulting product gives
\[
F_{D_N}(\bm{\theta})
\ge\frac{b_B^3}{8(N+1)^{3/2}}
\prod_{j\in J}a(\theta_j)\prod_{j\notin J}b(\theta_j)
\ge C_1(N+1)^{-3/2}a_*^N. \tag{4}
\]

For the upper bound, use the trial parameter \((\theta_*,\ldots,\theta_*)\). If \(U_1,\ldots,U_k\) are independent uniform ternary variables, Fourier inversion gives
\[
\sup_r\mathbb P(U_1+\cdots+U_k=r)
\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\left|\frac{1+2\cos t}{3}\right|^kdt
\le C/\sqrt{k+1}.
\]
The last estimate follows from a quadratic bound near zero and a strict bound below one on its complement. Since \(\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\theta_*|r|}<\infty\),
\[
T_I+1\le C'3^{|I|}/\sqrt{|I|+1}.
\]
Substituting into (2), with \(A_*=2\cosh4\theta_*\), yields
\[
F_{D_N}(\theta_*,\ldots,\theta_*)
\le Ca_*^N\mathbb E(K+1)^{-3/2},
\]
where \(K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,27/(A_*+27))\). Splitting at half the mean and using the exponential lower-tail bound proves
\[
v_N\le C_2(N+1)^{-3/2}a_*^N. \tag{5}
\]

At a global minimizer, compare (4) with (5). After cancelling the common polynomial factor and taking logarithms,
\[
\sum_{j\in J}\log\frac{a(\theta_j)}{a_*}
+|J^c|\log\frac{b_0}{a_*}\le C.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative. The second term bounds \(|J^c|\), and compactness of \([-B,B]\) together with uniqueness of the minimum of \(a\) bounds, for every fixed \(\delta>0\), the number of remaining coordinates satisfying \(||\theta_j|-\theta_*|\ge\delta\). This proves the clustering assertion.

The two parity masses in coordinate \(j\) are \(1/Z_j\) and \(2\cosh\theta_j/Z_j\). Thus
\[
\sum_{\mathbf c}\alpha_{\mathbf c}^4
=\prod_j
\frac{(2\cosh\theta_j)^4+1}{(1+2\cosh\theta_j)^4}. \tag{6}
\]
Each factor lies in \([17/81,1]\). The clustering assertion applied for every \(\delta>0\) therefore makes the \(N\)-th root of (6) converge to \(c_*\), while (4)--(5) give \(M_3(p_N)^{1/N}=v_N^{1/N}\to a_*\).

Finally, the defining cubic is negative at \(\sqrt7\), so \(x_*>\sqrt7\). Hence
\[
c_*-a_*=\frac{4(x_*^2-7)}{(x_*+1)^4}>0,
\qquad
a_*>\frac{50}{256}>\frac18.
\]
The first inequality proves the ratio limit, and the lower bound in (4) with \(a_*>1/8\) proves \(8^NM_3(p_N)\to\infty\).

## External sources

Ilya Tyurin, "New Estimates of the Convergence Rate in the Lyapunov Theorem," arXiv:0912.0726v1, equation (1):
https://arxiv.org/html/0912.0726v1

The theorem used is the existence of an absolute \(C_{\rm BE}\) such that independent centered real variables \(X_i\), with total variance \(\sigma^2>0\) and finite third absolute moments, satisfy
\[
\sup_t\left|
\mathbb P\left(\sum_iX_i/\sigma\le t\right)-\Phi(t)
\right|
\le C_{\rm BE}\sigma^{-3}\sum_i\mathbb E|X_i|^3.
\]
Here \(X_i=\lambda_iU_i\); independence, centering, finite moments, and the required Lyapunov-ratio bound were checked above.
