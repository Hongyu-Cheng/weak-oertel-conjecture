## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^r$ be a convex body, $r\ge2$, and let $\mu$ be an absolutely continuous probability supported on $C$. Let $L=c+\mathbb Rv$, $\|v\|=1$, intersect $C$. If every closed halfspace containing $L$ has $\mu$-mass at least $k>0$, there is a nonatomic measure $\nu$ of mass $k$ on $L\cap C$ with $\nu(H)\le\mu(H)$ for all closed ambient halfspaces $H$. In the line coordinate $t$, put $p_t=c+tv$ and let $G(t)$ be the minimum through-$p_t$ cap mass over unit normals $b$ with $b\cdot v\ge0$. One such measure has cumulative mass $\max(0,k-G(t))$. Necessarily $k\le1/2$, and the maximum halfspace depth of $\nu$ is exactly $k/2\le1/4$.

## Proof

Let $F(t)$ be the minimum through-$p_t$ cap mass for normals $b\cdot v\le0$, where a cap with normal $b$ is $\{x:b\cdot(x-p_t)\ge0\}$. The two minima exist and are continuous in $t$ by compactness of their normal hemispheres and joint cap continuity. The latter follows by dominated convergence since hyperplanes are $\mu$-null. The signs of $b\cdot v$ make $G$ nonincreasing and $F$ nondecreasing.

Take one minimizing normal from each hemisphere. If either is perpendicular to $v$, its cap alone has mass at least $k$. Otherwise a positive linear combination of the two normals cancels the component along $v$. If the combination is nonzero, its cap contains $L$ and lies in the union of the two caps, since a point strictly outside both has strictly negative product with their combination. If the combination is zero the two normals are opposite and their caps cover space. This proves $F(t)+G(t)\ge k$ for every $t$.

Write $L\cap C=\{p_t:t_-\le t\le t_+\}$. For $t<t_-$, strict separation gives a through-$p_t$ cap disjoint from $C$. Its normal has negative product with $v$ by testing any point of $L\cap C$, hence $F(t)=0$. For $t>t_+$ the same argument gives $G(t)=0$. It follows that $B(t)=\max(0,k-G(t))$ is continuous and nondecreasing, with the tails zero and $k$ outside $[t_-,t_+]$. Its Stieltjes measure on the line is nonatomic and supported on the claimed interval. When a closed cap traces the right ray starting at $t$, its $\nu$-mass is $k-B(t)\le G(t)\le\mu(H)$. For a left ray it is $B(t)\le F(t)\le\mu(H)$. The continuity of $B$ makes both formulas valid for closed rays. A parallel cap either misses the line or contains it, where the premise applies.

Two opposite caps through the line with perpendicular normals have combined probability one, so $2k\le1$. At each line point the two opposite line rays have total mass $k$, hence their smaller mass is at most $k/2$. A median attains $k/2$ by continuity of the cumulative mass, because a cap containing the median contains a ray through it or all of $L$. Points off the line have depth zero by separation.
