## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) constructed support of 604889294a17d7df and the rooted-tree objects of 032423921efad6c8. More explicitly, put
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\quad(8\le\ell\le k),
\qquad Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\qquad N=n+1,
\qquad a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\ \text{if }Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell.
$$
Thus \(k\) is the height cutoff and \(Q\) is the tree depth. Choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), with nonterminal set \(D_m\) and injective successor \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\). Coordinates are indexed by \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\), and the child block at a node \(v\) is \(z[v]=(z_{v1},\ldots,z_{vm})\). For a Boolean source \(b\), start at the empty root and select the child \(vi_m(b[v])\) whenever \(b[v]\in D_m\), stopping at the first failure or at depth \(Q\). Write its selected path as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and retain
$$
A_{m,Q}=\left\lbrace z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}:
b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},\ 0\le s\le h(b)\right\rbrace,
\qquad
\widehat A=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
$$
Here level is the number of coordinates equal to two. None of these objects is rebuilt when a height coefficient changes.

Let a nonempty family of \(B\) full rooted paths of length \(Q\) satisfy \(B\le64(Q+1)^{11}\). Let \(P_j\) be its depth-\(j\) nodes, with \(P_0=\lbrace\varnothing\rbrace\), and define
$$
S=\bigcup_{j=0}^{Q-1}\ \bigcup_{v\in P_j}\lbrace vi:1\le i\le m\rbrace,
\qquad
U=S\cup\lbrace u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}:|u|\le\min(7,Q)\rbrace,
\qquad n_U=|U|.
$$
In particular, \(S\) contains complete child blocks, including an exit child whose parent is in the forest.

Let \(G:R_U\to(0,\infty)\) be arbitrary, where \(R_U=\lbrace z|_U:z\in A_{m,Q}\rbrace\). Let \(\lambda\in\mathbb R^n\) vanish on \(U\), and suppose that its child-block path variation satisfies
$$
P_\lambda:=\Pi(\lambda)
=\max_{v_0,\ldots,v_Q}\sum_{j=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{v_ji}|\le1,
$$
where the maximum ranges over full rooted paths with \(v_0=\varnothing\). This is the normalization of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3 and cdf4b723cb4dc8ec.

For every real \(\zeta>-\log2\), define, on the full original support,
$$
w_{(z,t)}=G(z|_U)e^{\lambda\cdot z+\zeta t},\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W,
$$
and put zero weights off \(\widehat A\). For this probability law set
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=\inf\lbrace p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Then \(M_3(p)\ge2^{-400}8^{-N}\). The coefficient is independent of \(k,Q,B\), the chain partition, the forest, \(G,\lambda\), and \(\zeta\). Every original height in every original column, the full original normalizer, the actual sampling law, and all original ambient closed halfspaces are retained.

## Proof

Write \(v_z=G(z|_U)e^{\lambda\cdot z}\), \(\eta=2^{-m}|D_m|\), and \(\delta=1-\eta\). The symbol \(\delta\) always denotes this tree failure probability. The positive height deviation will be denoted by \(\epsilon_h=\zeta+\log2\), a different quantity. The exact support, source reconstruction, and parameter estimates from 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df give
$$
|\lbrace z:\operatorname{level}(z)=s\rbrace|=2^n\eta^s\le2^n,
\qquad 0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2},\qquad \eta^Q>\frac34.
\tag{1}
$$
Directly \(b_8=4\) and \(b_9=7\). Consequently \(k=8\) gives \(Q=4\), and \(k\ge9\) gives \(Q\ge11\).

For any nonempty spatial class \(E\), write \(\widehat E\) for its lift retaining every original column height, \(\nu_{\widehat E}\) for the restriction of the actual unnormalized measure, \(W_{\widehat E}\) for its mass, and \(p^{\widehat E}=\nu_{\widehat E}/W_{\widehat E}\). Every depth used for this conditional law is taken over all closed halfspaces of the original \(\mathbb R^N\). If \(h_{\nu_{\widehat E}}=W_{\widehat E}\tau_{p^{\widehat E}}\), then
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat E})
=W_{\widehat E}^{-4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat E}w_{\mathbf I}h_{\nu_{\widehat E}}(\mathbf I)^3
\ge W_{\widehat E}^{-4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat E}w_{\mathbf I}^4.
\tag{2}
$$
For any finite spatial partition, omit empty classes and put \(\alpha_{\widehat E}=W_{\widehat E}/W\). The mass comparison for each original halfspace gives \(\tau_p\ge\alpha_{\widehat E}\tau_{p^{\widehat E}}\) on \(\widehat E\). Therefore
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{\widehat E}\alpha_{\widehat E}^4M_3(p^{\widehat E}),
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge8^{-n}\left(\sum_{\widehat E}\kappa_{\widehat E}^{-1/3}\right)^{-3}
\quad\text{if }M_3(p^{\widehat E})\ge8^{-n}\kappa_{\widehat E}.
\tag{3}
$$
The second assertion is Hölder applied to
\(1=\sum\alpha_{\widehat E}\le(\sum\kappa_{\widehat E}\alpha_{\widehat E}^4)^{1/4}(\sum\kappa_{\widehat E}^{-1/3})^{3/4}\). This uses actual weighted class masses only.

We first recall and verify the vertical-column estimate used in cdf4b723cb4dc8ec. For any probability law \(q\) on finitely many ordered heights, set \(d_q(t)=\min(\sum_{r\le t}q_r,\sum_{r\ge t}q_r)\). Heights where the first closed tail is less than \(1/4\) have total mass at most \(1/4\), and the analogous assertion holds for the second tail. Thus a set of mass at least \(1/2\) has \(d_q(t)\ge1/4\), giving \(\sum_tq_td_q(t)^3\ge1/128\). If \(E\) lies at one spatial level, its conditional law factors into its arbitrary spatial probabilities \(r_z\) and a common height law. A containing ambient halfspace contains, within the same column, a closed tail containing the sample height, or the whole column. Hence \(\tau_{p^{\widehat E}}(z,t)\ge r_zd_q(t)\), and finite Hölder yields
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat E})\ge\frac1{128}\sum_{z\in E}r_z^4
\ge\frac1{128|E|^3}.
\tag{4}
$$
This holds for every real height coefficient, including a one-atom height law. If \(Q<7\), partition into its at most seven spatial levels. By (1), (3), and (4),
\(M_3(p)\ge8^{-n}/(128\cdot7^3)>2^{-16}8^{-N}\).
This covers \(k=8\). Henceforth \(Q\ge11\).

Use the forest partition of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3, with exactly the same objects and normalization. The seven early classes are the levels \(0,\ldots,6\). GOOD consists of level-\(s\) points with \(s\ge7\) and \(u_7\notin P_7\). For \(7\le j\le Q\), STOP\(_j\) consists of level-\(j\) points with \(u_j\in P_j\). For \(7\le j<Q\), BRANCH\(_j\) consists of points of level at least \(j+1\), with \(u_j\in P_j\) and \(u_{j+1}\notin P_{j+1}\). The STOP condition specifies the point's level, without a requirement that its source next fail. Ancestor closure proves that these classes are disjoint and exhaustive. All are lifted with all original heights.

Here are the spatial consequences of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3 that we use. If \(\mathcal B_j\) is the set of level-\((j+1)\) anchors of BRANCH\(_j\), then
$$
|\mathrm{STOP}_j|\le2^nB\delta^j,
\qquad |\mathcal B_j|\le2^nB\delta^j.
\tag{5}
$$
Indeed, a prescribed next direction has fair block fraction at most \(\delta\): each symmetric chain adds a given coordinate at most once, and there are \(2^m\delta\) chains. Prescribing \(j\) directions fixes disjoint blocks and gives at most \(2^n\delta^j\) sources. Sum over at most \(B\) forest paths to depth \(j\); a permitted exit test has fraction at most one. These are source cardinalities, without a claim about their weighted masses. At each fixed later level, mapping a BRANCH point to the anchor with the same source is injective by parity. All spikes after the anchor are outside \(U\), so its label and its anchor's label agree exactly, and its spatial weight is at most \(e^{2P_\lambda}\) times the anchor weight. The first exit spike is retained in that anchor even if it belongs to \(S\).

For GOOD put \(\mathcal L=\lbrace z|_U:z\in\mathrm{GOOD},\ \operatorname{level}(z)=7\rbrace\). All subsequent GOOD formulas are used only when this class is nonempty, in which case \(\mathcal L\) is nonempty. Every GOOD point has exactly its level-seven anchor's label. Every \(\omega\in\mathcal L\) occurs at level seven with every Boolean assignment outside \(U\), and parity on \(\mathcal L\) is injective. In particular \(|\mathcal L|\le2^{n_U}\). All selected coordinates after depth seven descend from \(u_7\notin P_7\), and therefore lie outside \(S\) and outside \(U\). This is why the full \(G\)-label stays immutable.

For a field \(\theta\) vanishing on \(U\), define
$$
Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}=\prod_{u\notin U}(1+e^{\theta_u}),\qquad
\mathfrak g_1=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal L}G(\omega),\qquad
\mathfrak g_4=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal L}G(\omega)^4.
$$
An empty product is one. These are the outside normalizers of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3; its full Boolean normalizer is \(2^{n_U}Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}\). Finite Hölder and \((1+x)^4\le8(1+x^4)\) give
$$
\frac{\mathfrak g_4}{\mathfrak g_1^4}\ge8^{-n_U},
\qquad
\frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}\ge8^{-(n-n_U)}.
\tag{6}
$$
Let \(\mathcal C_s(\omega)\) be the GOOD level-\(s\) points of label \(\omega\) whose canonical inverse reconstruction reaches the original depth \(Q=Q_k\). The height-independent weighted census of 6edf2d6eb3514aa3 gives
$$
\sum_{z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega)}e^{\theta\cdot z}
\ge\eta^{Q-7}e^{-3\Pi(\theta)}Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}
\qquad(7\le s\le Q).
\tag{7}
$$
Its tests are \(s-7\) forward tests and \(Q-s\) inverse tests in fresh disjoint outside child blocks. Their fair success probability is \(\eta^{Q-7}\). For a revealed bit the tilted-to-fair likelihood ratio is \(2e^{\theta_ub_u}/(1+e^{\theta_u})\ge e^{-|\theta_u|}\). Summing the disjoint successful transcript cylinders costs at most \(e^{-\Pi(\theta)}\); turning the selected forward zeros into twos costs at most \(e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}\). This proves (7), and also shows explicitly that its depth is the original \(Q_k\), regardless of any height window used below.

At fixed \(s\ge7\) and label, parity injects the points into all Boolean outside assignments, and the forward spikes cost at most \(e^{2P_\lambda}\). Consequently, with
\(T_s(\zeta)=\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}e^{\zeta t}\),
$$
W_{\widehat{\mathrm{GOOD}}}
\le e^{2P_\lambda}\mathfrak g_1Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}
\sum_{s=7}^{Q}T_s(\zeta).
\tag{8}
$$
All the heights of the conditional law are present in this bound.

We treat three exhaustive ranges of the positive deviation \(\epsilon_h=\zeta+\log2\). First suppose \(\epsilon_h\ge2^{-16}\), and set \(C=2^{40}\). We claim that
$$
R_{\mathrm{pos}}(\zeta):=\sum_{s=1}^{Q}T_s(\zeta)
\le CQe^{\zeta k}.
\tag{9}
$$
The scalar entrance estimates proved in cdf4b723cb4dc8ec, or their direct induction, are \(Q_t\le2^{t-1}/t\) for \(t\ge8\) and \(Q\ge b_k\ge2^k/(16k)\). Swapping the finite height sums therefore gives
$$
\frac{R_{\mathrm{pos}}(\zeta)}{Qe^{\zeta k}}
\le8\sum_{r=0}^{k-8}\frac{k}{k-r}e^{-\epsilon_h r}.
\tag{10}
$$
Put \(h_0=2^{-16}\). Terms with \(r\le k/2\) sum to at most \(16/(1-e^{-h_0})\). In the remaining terms \(k-r\ge8\); their sum is at most \(ke^{-h_0k/2}/(1-e^{-h_0})\). The function \(x e^{-h_0x/2}\) is at most \(2/(eh_0)\). Since \(1-e^{-h_0}\ge h_0/2\), the sum in (10) is at most \(32/h_0+4/h_0^2<2^{40}\). Empty parts of the split contribute zero. This proves (9) for the entire stated range, with no upper bound on \(\zeta\).

For a STOP class with spatial sum \(L_j=\sum_zv_z\), its full mass is at most \(CQe^{\zeta k}L_j\). Its actual top atoms have fourth-weight sum \(e^{4\zeta k}\sum_zv_z^4\). By (2), finite Hölder, and (5),
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat{\mathrm{STOP}}_j})
\ge8^{-n}\frac1{(CQ)^4(B\delta^j)^3}.
\tag{11}
$$
For a BRANCH class let \(L_j^{\mathrm b}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal B_j}v_z\). The injective anchor maps, exact preservation of \(G\), and (9) bound its full mass by \(CQe^{\zeta k}e^{2P_\lambda}L_j^{\mathrm b}\). Retain the actual top anchor atoms in (2) and apply weighted finite Hölder. This yields
$$
M_3(p^{\widehat{\mathrm{BRANCH}}_j})
\ge8^{-n}\frac{e^{-8}}{(CQ)^4(B\delta^j)^3}.
\tag{12}
$$
Thus rare-class capacities use the sums of actual anchor weights and their fourth powers.

For GOOD, (8) and (9) bound its full mass by \(CQe^{\zeta k}e^{2P_\lambda}\mathfrak g_1Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}\). For \(z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega)\), \(7\le s<Q\), let \(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_Q\) be its inverse continuation and \(V_r=\sum_{q=s+1}^{r}e_{u_q}\). All these endpoints are distinct, remain GOOD, and retain label \(\omega\). Also \(|\lambda\cdot V_r|\le P_\lambda\). It follows directly that
\(h_{\nu_{\widehat{\mathrm{GOOD}}}}(z,k)\ge(Q-s)e^{\zeta k}G(\omega)e^{\lambda\cdot z-P_\lambda}\).
Use the first \(h_*:=\lfloor(Q-6)/2\rfloor\) levels \(s=7,\ldots,6+h_*\). Writing \(T_{\mathrm{tail}}=Q-6\), we have \(h_*\ge T_{\mathrm{tail}}/3\), \(Q-s\ge T_{\mathrm{tail}}/2\), and \(T_{\mathrm{tail}}/Q\ge1/3\). Cube this actual depth bound and sum against the actual top sampling weights, then use (6)--(7) with \(\theta=4\lambda\). The fourth full conditional normalizer gives

\ge8^{-n}\frac{e^{-23}}{32\cdot81\cdot C^4}
>2^{-220}8^{-n}.
\tag{13}
$$
Here the costs are three from the cubed depth, twelve from the census, and eight from the fourth normalizer. More explicitly, the factor preceding (6) is at least \(\eta^{Q-7}e^{-23P_\lambda}h_*(T_{\mathrm{tail}}/2)^3/(CQ)^4\), which gives (13) by (1). The last inequality follows from \(e^{23}<2^{37}\), \(32\cdot81<2^{12}\), and \(C^4=2^{160}\).

The early coefficients in (3) are \(1/128\), by (4). For the coefficients (11)--(13), use

\sum_{s=0}^{Q}T_s(\zeta)
\le4+\frac12\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}\frac{e^{\epsilon_h\ell}}\ell
\le8(L_K+D).

$$
Here is a proof of the last bound, keeping both terms. The portion \(\ell\le K\) is at most \(eH_K\le6L_K\). For \(\ell\ge K+1>1/\epsilon_h\), the function \(e^{\epsilon_h x}/x\) is increasing, so the remaining sum is at most

For \(x\in[1,2]\), this last integral up to \(x\) is at most \((x-1)e^x\le2e^x/x\). For \(x\ge2\), the derivative of \(2e^x/x\) is at least \(e^x/x\), so the same bound persists. It follows that the remaining sum is at most

For clarity we prove the localized pointwise assertion, retaining the original \(m=m(Q_k)\) and the census (7). \qquad
T_{\mathrm{end}}:=\sum_{\ell=e_-}^{k}b_\ell\rho_\ell

\tag{23}
$$

Fix a center \(z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega)\), put \(a=a_s\), and consider a proper halfspace through \((z,a+1)\) as above. \ge2^{k-K}\frac Kk\ge1.

$$

$$
