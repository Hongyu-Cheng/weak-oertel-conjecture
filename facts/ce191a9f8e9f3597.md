## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R$ be a convex body, $k\ge1$, with $0\in\operatorname{int}K$, and write $(z,t)$ for its coordinates. Suppose $\partial K$ is $C^2$ near $t=0$ and the horizontal component $N_z$ of its outer unit normal $(N_z,N_t)$ is nonzero along $\partial K\cap\{t=0\}$. Let $V=|K|$, $Q_t=\{z:(z,t)\in K\}$, and let $S(t),M_1(t),M_2(t)$ be the integrals of $1,z,zz^\mathsf T$ on $Q_t$. For $F(h,b)=|K\cap\{t+h\cdot z\ge b\}|/V$, the gradient and Hessian at $(h,b)=(0,0)$ are
$$
\nabla F(0,0)=\frac1V\binom{M_1(0)}{-S(0)},\qquad
\nabla^2F(0,0)=\frac1V
\begin{pmatrix}
-M_2'(0)&M_1'(0)\\
M_1'(0)^\mathsf T&-S'(0)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The direction $(0,1)$ is stationary for the spherical through-origin cap fraction $p$ exactly when $Q_0$ has centroid $0$. At such a direction, the spherical Hessian in horizontal tangent coordinates is
$$
-\frac1V M_2'(0)
=\frac1V\int_{\partial Q_0}zz^\mathsf T\,\frac{N_t}{\lVert N_z\rVert}\,d\mathcal H^{k-1}(z).
$$

## Proof

For small $(h,b)$ put $Q_{h,b}=\{z:(z,b-h\cdot z)\in K\}$. Its boundary is a compact transverse intersection, since the graph is near $t=0$ over the bounded projection of $K$, and its horizontal defining-function gradient remains nonzero by compactness and the hypothesis on $N_z$. The intersection is proper because $0$ is interior and $(h,b)$ is small.

First vary $(h,b)$ along $(a,\beta)$. At each fixed $z$ the vertical threshold changes by the parameter times $\beta-a\cdot z$. If $z$ is interior to $Q_{h,b}$ the threshold lies inside the vertical fiber, and if $z$ is outside $Q_{h,b}$ the signed derivative is zero. The excluded boundary of $Q_{h,b}$ is Lebesgue null. The change of captured fiber length is bounded in magnitude by the threshold change and the projection of $K$ is bounded. Differentiating by dominated convergence gives $\nabla_h F=V^{-1}\int_{Q_{h,b}} z\,dz$ and $\partial_b F=-V^{-1}|Q_{h,b}|$.

For the derivatives of these integrals, use local defining functions $G(z,t)$ for $\partial K$ with $G\le0$ inside, so $\nabla G/\lVert\nabla G\rVert=N$ at the boundary. A boundary point of $Q_{\varepsilon a,\varepsilon\beta}$ satisfies $G(z(\varepsilon),\varepsilon(\beta-a\cdot z(\varepsilon)))=0$. Differentiating at zero, its outward normal speed in $z$ is $(\beta-a\cdot z)(-N_t/\lVert N_z\rVert)$. Each chart can be written as a graph along a horizontal direction where $\nabla_zG$ is nonzero. The derivative of a domain integral of a smooth function of $z$ is its boundary integral against this normal speed. This follows by integrating first along the graph coordinate, differentiating the endpoint, and summing with a partition of unity. The same charts give continuous first derivatives of these domain integrals for small $(h,b)$, so $F$ is $C^2$ there. For the horizontal slice family the speed is $-N_t/\lVert N_z\rVert$ by taking $a=0,\beta=1$. Thus differentiating the first derivatives of $F$ gives the three Hessian blocks $-M_2'(0)/V$, $M_1'(0)/V$, and $-S'(0)/V$.

The chart $h\mapsto(h,1)/\sqrt{1+\lVert h\rVert^2}$ has orthonormal tangent derivative at zero, and $F(h,0)$ is the spherical cap fraction in this chart. The gradient formula gives stationarity exactly when $M_1(0)=0$. At a stationary direction the ordinary chart Hessian agrees with the spherical Hessian in those coordinates. The transport formula for $M_2'(0)$ gives the displayed boundary integral.
