## Statement

Let $\mu$ be a finite positive measure of mass $M>0$. For measurable $A,B$ of the same mass $Q$, $\mu(A\setminus B)=\mu(B\setminus A)=\mu(A\triangle B)/2$. Let $\theta$ be a probability law on measurable caps of common mass $Q=\beta M$, with jointly measurable incidence, and put $W(w)=\int\mathbf1_H(w)\,d\theta(H)$. Then
$$
\frac1M\int(W-\beta)^2\,d\mu
=\beta(1-\beta)-\frac1{2M}\iint\mu(H\triangle H')\,d\theta(H)\,d\theta(H').
$$
In particular two nested caps differing by a strip of positive mass cannot both have mass $Q$.

## Proof

Subtract $\mu(A\cap B)$ from the two equal masses to get the first equality, and add the two differences to get the symmetric difference. By Fubini $\int W\,d\mu=Q$. For independent $H,H'$ under $\theta$, their incidence indicators disagree at $w$ with probability $2W(w)(1-W(w))$. Integrating and using $\int(W-\beta)^2\,d\mu=M\beta(1-\beta)-\int W(1-W)\,d\mu$ gives the identity. The strict strip claim follows from the first equality.
