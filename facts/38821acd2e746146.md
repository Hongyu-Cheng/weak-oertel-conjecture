## Statement

Let $E_0,\ldots,E_d$ be independent unit exponentials, $d\ge1$. Set
$$
A_d=\{a\in[-1,1]^{d+1}:a_0=-1,\ \sum_{i=0}^d a_i=0\},\qquad
P(a)=\Pr\{\textstyle\sum_i a_iE_i\ge0\}.
$$
Every minimizing vector has at least one of the following forms.

- All nonzero negative coefficients are $-1$. In this case $P(a)\ge1/2$.
- All positive coefficients are $1$.
- The positive coefficients are $k\ge1$ copies of $1$ and one $p\in(0,1)$. The negative coefficients are the fixed $-1$ and $m\ge k$ copies of $-b$, where $b=(k+p-1)/m\in(0,1)$. There are no zero coefficients in this case, so $k+m=d-1$.

## Proof

Use the free-positive-pair theorem. If all nonzero negatives are units, the unit-negative half-mass theorem applies. Otherwise some negative coefficient is free, and there is at most one free positive. If none occurs, all positives are units. If a free positive $p$ occurs, the sum of positives is at least one because of the fixed negative unit, so there is also at least one positive unit. Thus the signed-exponential derivative lemma 511d89569241b0d6 applies.

As in the free-positive-pair proof, varying two free coordinates in opposite directions gives the same positive first derivative on every free coordinate. With $f$ the current signed density, write it as $qf(0)$. The two half-line transforms at the free positive and any free negative have value $q$. The tangent bound for $\log f$ and integrability on the applicable half-line give $q<1$ exactly as in that proof. For each free negative magnitude $b$, the reciprocal transform $\psi_-(b)=f(0)/f_{V-bE'}(0)$ therefore equals $1/q>1$. Apply 506e246f680da4b5 to $h(t)=f(t)/f(0)$. The transform is convex and has positive derivative at each such $b$. The level $1/q$ can occur at only one positive argument, by the tangent inequality at the smaller of any two such arguments. Hence all free negative magnitudes equal $b$.

If there were an additional saturated negative coordinate $-1$, convexity would give $\psi_-(1)>\psi_-(b)$, so its first derivative as a signed coefficient is smaller than $qf(0)$. Increasing that coefficient and decreasing $p$ by the same small amount is feasible and has negative first derivative, a contradiction. A zero coefficient has derivative $f(0)>qf(0)$ by 511d89569241b0d6. Increasing $p$ and decreasing that zero coefficient by the same small amount is another feasible descent. Thus the only saturated negative is the fixed one and no zero remains. With $k$ positive units and $m$ negative magnitudes $b$, balance gives $mb=k+p-1$ and $b<1$ gives the integer inequality $m\ge k$. Counting coordinates yields $k+m=d-1$.
