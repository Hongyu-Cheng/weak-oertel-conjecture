## Statement

Let \(N,d\ge1\) be integers and put \(n=N-1\). Let \(A<B\) be real, and let \(R,T:[A,B]\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous concave functions positive on \((A,B)\). For \(1\le i\le n\), fix \(a_i\ge0\) and \(b_i,c_i>0\), and define

\[
P:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
A\le t\le B,\quad x_i\ge0,\quad
b\cdot x\le R(t),\quad c\cdot x\le T(t)\right\}.
\]

When \(n=0\), let \(P=[A,B]\), with the unique empty horizontal vector and empty dot products equal to zero. Assume that the complete support \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\) is nonempty. Let \(V:[A,B]\to\mathbb R\) be any finite continuous convex function. Define \(\widetilde w_{(x,s)}:=\exp(-V(s)-a\cdot x)\) on \(D\), extend \(\widetilde w\) by zero to \(\mathbb Z^N\setminus D\), and put \(\widetilde W:=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^N}\widetilde w_I\).

For \(J\in D\), let \(\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J:=\{\xi\in\mathbb Z^N:J+\xi,J-\xi\in D\}\). For \(\xi\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi)&:=\min\{\widetilde w_{J+\xi},\widetilde w_{J-\xi}\},\\
\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)&:=
\log\frac{\widetilde w_J^2}{\widetilde w_{J+\xi}\widetilde w_{J-\xi}},\\
\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)&:=
\sum_{\substack{\xi\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\\
\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)\le L}}
\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi)\qquad(L\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\]

These sums are oriented and include zero. The curvatures are nonnegative. Set \(\Theta_N:=2+(2N-2+2\log3)/(4\sqrt N)\). There is one original center \(J\in D\) such that

\[
\frac{\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(\Theta_N)}{1+\Theta_N}
\ge e^{-500}\,2^{-N}\widetilde W,
\qquad \Theta_N\le2+\sqrt N.
\tag{1}
\]

For the actual mixed-volume consequence, let \(C\subset\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body. For \(z\in\mathbb R^N\), put \(C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\}\) and \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), using zero for empty or lower-dimensional fibers. Suppose that \(\{I\in\mathbb Z^N:w_I>0\}=D\), and that for some \(K>0\) and \(M\ge1\),

\[
\frac K M\widetilde w_I\le w_I\le KM\widetilde w_I
\qquad(I\in D).
\tag{2}
\]

Write \(W:=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^N}w_I\), \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where the infimum ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. These are the actual geometric objects, with exactly the meanings in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; that fact's integer base dimension \(n\) is the present \(N\), and its continuous dimension \(d\) is unchanged. The scalar quantities \(\widetilde w,\widetilde W\) above are distinct from \(w,W\).

The center \(J\) in (1) has a point \(y^\star\in C_J\) such that \(p^\star:=(J,y^\star)\in S\) satisfies

\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge c(M)\,2^{-N}W,
\qquad
c(M):=\frac{-\log(1-e^{-1})}{16}
\frac{e^{-500}}{M^2(1+4\log M)}>0.
\tag{3}
\]

The constant depends only on \(M\), and is independent of \(N,d,V,R,T,a,b,c,K\) and the shapes of the fibers. The same point \(p^\star\) works for every closed halfspace containing it. All support, curvature-cutoff, and halfspace boundary equalities are retained.

## Proof

Let \(\mathcal I:=[A,B]\cap\mathbb Z\). Every height in \(\mathcal I\) contains the horizontal zero vector, and these are exactly the occupied heights. Thus \(\mathcal I\ne\varnothing\). For \(n\ge1\), each horizontal coordinate is bounded by \(\max_{[A,B]}R/b_i\). Hence \(D\) is finite, and \(0<\widetilde W<\infty\).

We first specify precisely the quantitative input extracted from the proof of accepted fact `647ec0c586d3f2f8`. Its \(N,n,P,D,A,B,R,T,a,b,c\) have the present meanings. Its fixed-budget support \(K_{\rho,\tau}\) will be written \(\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\) here, to distinguish it from the scale \(K\) in (2). Its fixed-budget scalar weights \(\widetilde w_z\) will be written \(\omega_z^{\rho,\tau}\), and its row reflection sum \(S_j^{\rho,\tau}\) will be written \(\mathscr S_j^{\rho,\tau}\), to distinguish them from the full scalar profile and the mixed set in the present statement. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}
&:=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le\rho,\ c\cdot z\le\tau\},\\
\omega_z^{\rho,\tau}
&:=e^{-a\cdot z}\mathbf 1_{\{z\in\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\}},\\
F(\rho,\tau)&:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\omega_z^{\rho,\tau},\\
\mathscr S_j^{\rho,\tau}
&:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\min\{\omega_{j+v}^{\rho,\tau},\omega_{j-v}^{\rho,\tau}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, on a complete original row, \(\widetilde w_{(z,s)}=e^{-V(s)}\omega_z^{R(s),T(s)}\). The predecessor's full scalar height weights \(w_{(x,s)}\) and scalar total \(W\) correspond to the present \(\widetilde w_{(x,s)},\widetilde W\), with its Gaussian potential replaced here by \(V\). Only the fixed-budget assertions below are reused as accepted inputs; the height argument for this replacement is proved below. The predecessor's \(F\) is the present \(F\). Its resource multipliers \(\beta,\gamma\), its auxiliary function \(f\), and its auxiliary profile \(\Psi\) retain their meanings: \(f(t):=\max_{m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}\{\log(2m+1)-tm\}\) for \(t>0\), \(f(0):=+\infty\), and, for \(n\ge1\),

\[
\Psi(\rho,\tau):=\inf_{\beta,\gamma\ge0}
\left\{\beta\rho+\gamma\tau+
\sum_{i=1}^n f(a_i+\beta b_i+\gamma c_i)\right\}.
\]

For \(n=0\), \(\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\) consists of the empty vector, \(F=1\), and \(\Psi=0\). Put \(\kappa_n:=e^{-16\sqrt n-10}\) and \(\eta_n:=2n+2\log3\), exactly as in that predecessor's proof. Equation (2), its following concavity assertion, and Equation (7) of that accepted proof give, for every \(\rho,\tau\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\rho,\tau)&\le(3/2)^n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)},\\
\text{some }j\in\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\text{ satisfies}\quad
\mathscr S_j^{\rho,\tau}&\ge\kappa_n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)},\\
\Psi(t\rho,t\tau)&\le\Psi(\rho,\tau)+(t-1)\eta_n
\qquad(t\ge1),\\
\Psi((1-\varepsilon)\rho,(1-\varepsilon)\tau)
&\ge\Psi(\rho,\tau)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\eta_n
\qquad(0<\varepsilon<1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The function \(\Psi\) is finite, nonnegative, concave, and nondecreasing in each budget. These particular accepted assertions concern only fixed horizontal budgets; they have no hypothesis on a height weight. Their hypotheses are exactly \(a_i\ge0\), \(b_i,c_i>0\), and nonnegative budgets as imposed here. Their accepted proof includes the case that either budget is zero: then \(\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}=\{0\}\), \(F=1\), \(\Psi=0\), and \(\mathscr S_0^{\rho,\tau}=1\) for \(n\ge1\). It also includes the empty-coordinate case. Thus (4) applies at every original endpoint as well as at positive interior budgets.

Define \(\psi(t):=\Psi(R(t),T(t))\) on \([A,B]\). For \(0\le\theta\le1\), concavity of the budgets gives coordinatewise lower bounds by the corresponding convex combinations. Monotonicity of \(\Psi\), followed by its concavity, therefore shows that \(\psi\) is concave. For \(s\in\mathcal I\), put \(g_s:=e^{-V(s)}\) and \(U_s:=g_s e^{\psi(s)}\), and let \(Z:=\sum_{s\in\mathcal I}U_s\), \(\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}:=\max_{s\in\mathcal I}U_s\). The symbol \(\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\) corresponds to the peak called \(M\) in the proof of `647ec0c586d3f2f8`; it is distinct from the profile-comparison factor \(M\) in (2). The present height factor \(g_s\) replaces the Gaussian height factor of that proof. The first inequality in (4) gives

\[
\widetilde W
=\sum_{s\in\mathcal I}g_sF(R(s),T(s))
\le(3/2)^n Z.
\tag{5}
\]

Because \(-V+\psi\) is concave, \(U_s\) is a positive log-concave sequence on the finite integer interval \(\mathcal I\). Let \(\mathcal E:=\{s\in\mathcal I:U_s\ge\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}/e\}\). This is a nonempty consecutive interval of integers. Denote its endpoints by \(s_-,s_+\) and let \(L:=s_+-s_-+1\). We claim that

\[
Z\le3L\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}.
\tag{6}
\]

The mass in \(\mathcal E\) is at most \(L\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\). Choose a maximizing height \(s_\star\in\mathcal E\). If a right tail exists, \(s_++1\) is at distance at most \(L\) from \(s_\star\), and its log weight is strictly below \(\log\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}-1\). The secant slope from \(s_\star\) to that height is therefore at most \(-1/L\); discrete concavity bounds every subsequent increment by the same number. For each right-tail height \(s_++r\), \(r\ge1\), this implies \(U_{s_++r}\le\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}e^{-r/L}\). Their total is at most \(\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}/(e^{1/L}-1)\le L\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\). The left tail has the same bound by reversing the index, and a missing tail contributes zero. This proves (6) using ordinary log-concavity alone.

For \(J=(j,k)\in D\) and \(\xi=(v,u)\in\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\), direct substitution into the original weights gives

\[
\widetilde\Delta_J(v,u)
=V(k+u)+V(k-u)-2V(k)\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]

The horizontal affine terms cancel, and the inequality is convexity of \(V\) at the original midpoint \(k\).

Set \(\delta:=1/(8\sqrt N)\), so \(0<\delta\le1/8\), and note that the cutoff in the statement is \(\Theta_N=2+2\delta\eta_n\). Suppose first that \(L\le64/\delta\). Choose the maximizing height \(s_\star\) and apply the second inequality of (4) to \(R(s_\star),T(s_\star)\). It supplies \(j\) such that, for \(J=(j,s_\star)\), the oriented original row reflection sum is at least \(g_{s_\star}\kappa_n e^{\psi(s_\star)}=\kappa_n\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\). All its pairs have height displacement zero, hence curvature zero by (7). Equations (5) and (6) therefore give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^N}{\widetilde W}
\frac{\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(\Theta_N)}{1+\Theta_N}
&\ge\frac{2\kappa_n}{3L(1+\Theta_N)}
\left(\frac43\right)^n\\
&\ge\frac{\delta\kappa_n}{96(1+\Theta_N)}
\left(\frac43\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

This case includes a singleton occupied height and any maximizing row at a zero-budget endpoint.

Now suppose that \(L>64/\delta\). Let \(k:=\lfloor(s_-+s_+)/2\rfloor\) and \(r:=\lfloor\delta L/16\rfloor\). Since \(\delta L/16>4\), we have \(r\ge\delta L/32\), and consequently \(2r+1\ge\delta L/16\). Each distance from \(k\) to \(s_-,s_+\) is at least \((L-2)/2\), whereas \(r\le\delta L/16\). Thus \([k-r,k+r]\subseteq[s_-,s_+]\). The distances from \(k\) to the real endpoints \(A,B\) are also at least \((L-2)/2\), so \(k\in(A,B)\) and \(R(k),T(k)>0\).

For every real \(t\in[k-r,k+r]\), nonnegative concavity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-\delta)R(k)&\le R(t)\le(1+\delta)R(k),\\
(1-\delta)T(k)&\le T(t)\le(1+\delta)T(k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, for \(0\le u\le r\), the chord from \(k\) to \(B\) gives \(R(k+u)\ge(1-u/(B-k))R(k)\), while writing \(k\) as a convex combination of \(A\) and \(k+u\) gives \(R(k+u)\le(1+u/(k-A))R(k)\). Negative displacements exchange the endpoints, and the same argument applies to \(T\). Every ratio is at most \(2r/(L-2)\le\delta L/(8(L-2))\le\delta\), since here \(L>512\). This proves (9) for the original real endpoints without changing or rounding the budgets.

Let \(\rho:=(1-\delta)R(k)\) and \(\tau:=(1-\delta)T(k)\). The entire support \(\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\) lies in every original row of this band. Choose \(j\in\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\) by (4). Its reflection sum satisfies

\[
\mathscr S_j^{\rho,\tau}
\ge\kappa_n
\exp\left(\psi(k)-\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\eta_n\right).
\tag{10}
\]

For any integer \(s\in[k-r,k+r]\), monotonicity, (9), and the scaling inequality of (4) imply \(\psi(s)\le\psi(k)+\delta\eta_n\). Also \(s,k\in\mathcal E\), so \(U_s\ge\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}/e\) and \(U_k\le\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(s)-V(k)
&=\psi(s)-\psi(k)+\log U_k-\log U_s
\le1+\delta\eta_n,\\
g_s=U_se^{-\psi(s)}
&\ge\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}
\exp(-1-\psi(k)-\delta\eta_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Apply the first inequality to \(s=k+u\) and \(s=k-u\). Convexity of \(V\) and (7) yield, for every original pair with both heights in the band,

\[
0\le\widetilde\Delta_J(v,u)
\le2+2\delta\eta_n=\Theta_N,
\qquad J:=(j,k),\quad |u|\le r.
\tag{12}
\]

This bound is on the second difference of the original potential, not on the auxiliary profile.

For each integer \(-r\le u\le r\) and horizontal displacement \(v\) with \(j\pm v\in\mathcal K_{\rho,\tau}\), both original endpoints \((j+v,k+u),(j-v,k-u)\) belong to \(D\). Their minimum original weight is at least the lower bound on \(g_s\) in (11), times \(\min\{e^{-a\cdot(j+v)},e^{-a\cdot(j-v)}\}\). By (12), every such pair occurs in the original cutoff sum. Summing over these distinct oriented displacements, with the zero displacement retained, and using (10), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(\Theta_N)
&\ge(2r+1)\kappa_n\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}
\exp\left(-1-\left(\delta+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\eta_n\right)\\
&\ge\frac{\delta L}{16}\kappa_n\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}
e^{-1-3\delta\eta_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The last inequality uses \(\delta\le1/8\). No weight of an original endpoint has been replaced in the cutoff definition; the common support supplies only a lower bound on that full sum. Combining (13), (5), and (6) yields

\[
\frac{2^N}{\widetilde W}
\frac{\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(\Theta_N)}{1+\Theta_N}
\ge\frac{\delta\kappa_n}{24(1+\Theta_N)}
\left(\frac43\right)^n e^{-1-3\delta\eta_n}.
\tag{14}
\]

Both (8) and (14) are at least

\[
\frac{\delta\kappa_n}{96(3+2\delta\eta_n)}
\left(\frac43\right)^n e^{-1-3\delta\eta_n}.
\tag{15}
\]

We give an elementary bound uniform in every dimension. Since \(\log3<2\) and \(n=N-1\), we have \(\eta_n\le4N\). Thus \(3\delta\eta_n\le(3/2)\sqrt N\), \(\Theta_N\le2+\sqrt N\), and \(3+2\delta\eta_n\le4\sqrt N\). Using \(\sqrt n\le\sqrt N\) and \(\log(4/3)\ge1/4\), the logarithm of (15) is at least

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N-1}{4}-16\sqrt N-11
-\log(768\sqrt N)-\frac32\sqrt N-\log(4\sqrt N)
&=\frac N4-\frac{35}{2}\sqrt N-\log N-\log3072-\frac{45}{4}\\
&\ge\frac N4-20\sqrt N-21\\
&=\left(\frac{\sqrt N}{2}-20\right)^2-421
\ge-421.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\log N=2\log\sqrt N\le2\sqrt N\), and \(\log3072<9\): the elementary exponential series gives \(e^2>7\) and \(e>2\), so \(e^9>2\cdot7^4>3072\). The inequality \(\log(4/3)\ge1/4\) follows by integrating \(1/t\ge3/4\) from \(1\) to \(4/3\). Therefore (15) is at least \(e^{-421}\), in particular at least \(e^{-500}\), proving (1).

All preceding estimates include \(n=0\): the horizontal support has its single empty vector, the horizontal reflection sum is one, \(F=1\), \(\Psi=0\), and the same inequalities (4) hold. The height argument and the dimension estimate use \(N\ge1\), so their formulas remain valid. Zero budgets were included in the short case, and the long case has a strictly interior center. This completes the scalar assertion for the stated full class of potentials and supports.

We now transfer this center to the actual body. Define \(\alpha_J(\xi):=\min\{w_{J+\xi},w_{J-\xi}\}\) for every \(\xi\in\mathbb Z^N\), and let \(\mathcal U_J:=\{\xi\in\mathbb Z^N:\alpha_J(\xi)>0\}\). For \(\xi\in\mathcal U_J\), put \(\Delta_J(\xi):=\log(w_J^2/(w_{J+\xi}w_{J-\xi}))\), and let \(B_J^\Delta(L):=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal U_J:\Delta_J(\xi)\le L}\alpha_J(\xi)\). In accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`, its \(j,v,\alpha(v),\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j(v),B_j^\Delta(L)\) are respectively the present \(J,\xi,\alpha_J(\xi),\mathcal U_J,\Delta_J(\xi),B_J^\Delta(L)\). Its integer base dimension is \(N\), not the horizontal dimension \(n=N-1\). Its \(C,C_z,w_z,W,S,h_S\) are exactly the actual objects already defined in the statement. In particular, its nonnegativity of curvature holds for these actual volumes by convexity of \(C\).

The common positive support gives \(\mathcal U_J=\widetilde{\mathcal U}_J\). Write \(w_I=K\widetilde w_Ie^{\varepsilon_I}\) for \(I\in D\). The comparison (2) says \(|\varepsilon_I|\le\log M\). For every \(\xi\) in the common pair support, it follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_J(\xi)&\ge\frac K M\widetilde\alpha_J(\xi),\\
0\le\Delta_J(\xi)
&=\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)
+2\varepsilon_J-\varepsilon_{J+\xi}-\varepsilon_{J-\xi}
\le\widetilde\Delta_J(\xi)+4\log M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The scalar \(K\) cancels in the second difference. Since \(W\le KM\widetilde W\) and \(1+\Theta_N+4\log M\le(1+\Theta_N)(1+4\log M)\), (16) and (1) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{B_J^\Delta(\Theta_N+4\log M)}{1+\Theta_N+4\log M}
&\ge\frac{K}{M(1+4\log M)}
\frac{\widetilde B_J^{\widetilde\Delta}(\Theta_N)}{1+\Theta_N}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-500}}{M^2(1+4\log M)}\,2^{-N}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

For completeness, the lifting theorem is used at this fixed original center. In the even-thinning conclusion of `280b299bc1e87c14`, take \(q_\xi:=\alpha_J(\xi)\) for \(\xi\in\mathcal U_J\) with \(\Delta_J(\xi)\le\Theta_N+4\log M\), and take \(q_\xi:=0\) otherwise. The family is even, bounded by the actual pair minima, and has positive mass because \(q_0=w_J>0\). Its mass \(M_q\), which is distinct from the comparison factor \(M\) and the peak \(\mathsf M_{\mathrm{peak}}\), equals \(B_J^\Delta(\Theta_N+4\log M)\). Its mean actual curvature is at most \(\Theta_N+4\log M\). All hypotheses of the accepted lifting theorem hold: \(N,d\ge1\), \(C\) is a convex body, \(w_J>0\), and \(0<W<\infty\). It supplies one \(y^\star\in C_J\), after this center and thinning have been fixed, whose depth is at least

\[
\frac{\ell M_q}{16(\Theta_N+4\log M+\log2)}
\ge\frac{\ell}{16}
\frac{B_J^\Delta(\Theta_N+4\log M)}{1+\Theta_N+4\log M},
\qquad \ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1}).
\]

Together with (17), this proves (3). The accepted conclusion selects the point before every closed ambient halfspace and includes vertical halfspaces whose boundary contains the central fiber. All zero-volume integer fibers outside \(D\) remain part of \(S\) and contribute zero to \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). The selected base is an original lattice point, and every reflected-pair sum throughout retains both orientations, zero, and equality atoms. Thus the actual point and all halfspace quantifiers have the required meanings. Finally, \(\ell>0\) and \(M\ge1\), so the displayed \(c(M)\) is positive; it is also less than \(1/e\), since \(\ell<1\) follows from \(e>2\).
