## Statement

Fix \(\lambda=12\), write \(\kappa(u)=e^{-12|u|}\), and put \(\vartheta(a)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\kappa(a+z)\). Thus \(\vartheta\) is exactly the function denoted \(\vartheta_\lambda\) in the registered local test at \(\lambda=12\). For \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B^{(r)}(f_1,\ldots,f_r)(u)
 &=\kappa(u)\int_{\mathbb R^r}
       \prod_{i=1}^r f_i(v_i)\,
       \kappa\!\left(\tfrac12-u-\sum_i v_i\right)\,d\mathbf v,\\
\mathcal A^{(r)}(P_1,\ldots,P_r)(a)
 &=\vartheta(a)\int_{[0,1]^r}
       \prod_{i=1}^rP_i(b_i)\,
       \vartheta\!\left(\tfrac12-a-\sum_i b_i\right)\,d\mathbf b,\\
(\mathcal F f)(a)
 &=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\bigl(f(a+z)+f(-a+z)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
An empty integral has its usual value, so the leaf Boolean message is \(f_{T_0}(u)=\kappa(u)\kappa(1/2-u)\), and \(\mathcal A^{(0)}(a)=\vartheta(a)\vartheta(1/2-a)\). For a finite rooted tree with at most two children at each vertex, its actual Boolean incoming message is recursively \(f_T=\mathcal B^{(r)}(f_{T_1},\ldots,f_{T_r})\). This definition includes the full factor \(\kappa(u)\) for the incoming edge.

The matching type is recursively \(\tau(T)=\#\{i:\tau(T_i)=0\}\), with \(\tau(T_0)=0\). These are the three types in the registered test. For the trees used here, this agrees directly with counting the children whose root can be left unmatched in a maximum matching.

There are no positive continuous even one-periodic functions \(\phi_0,\phi_1,\phi_2\) and no \(0<\rho\le63/40\) such that, for every \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), every tuple of actual child messages, and every real \(a\),
\[
\mathcal A^{(r)}
 \bigl(\phi_{\tau(T_1)}\mathcal Ff_{T_1},\ldots,
       \phi_{\tau(T_r)}\mathcal Ff_{T_r}\bigr)(a)
 \le \rho\,\phi_{\tau(T)}(a)\,\mathcal Ff_T(a).
\tag{2}
\]
In fact it suffices to require the leaf instance and the five transitions in (5) below. Every tree in these transitions is a descendant subtree of an admissible original subcubic-tree simplex with \(n=100\), \(k=1\), \(d=3n-2\), both signed-flow blocks, and common budget \(R=n/2+k\).

## Proof

All analytic and finite arithmetic ingredients are proved here; no accepted predecessor is used as a premise. Since increasing the positive factor on the right of (2) preserves that inequality, it suffices to disprove (2) at \(\rho=63/40\).

We first record the message bounds that justify the operators, sums, and continuous-phase estimates. If \(T\) has \(m_T\) internal edges, its defining integral gives
\[
0<f_T(u)\le \kappa(u)(1/6)^{m_T},
\qquad
e^{-24|u-v|}f_T(v)\le f_T(u)\le e^{24|u-v|}f_T(v).
\tag{3}
\]
For the first bound, discard all vertex factors and integrate each internal edge factor, whose integral is \(1/6\). For the second, the incoming variable appears only in its own edge factor and the root residual. Changing it from \(v\) to \(u\) changes the sum of these two absolute values by at most \(2|u-v|\), pointwise in all internal variables. These arguments also show finiteness and positivity. Dominated convergence gives continuity. In particular, the series defining \(\mathcal Ff_T\) converges uniformly on compact sets. Write \(D_T=\mathcal Ff_T\). It is positive, continuous, even, and one-periodic, and satisfies the same exponential ratio bound with parameter \(24\). The corresponding ratio bound for \(\vartheta\) has parameter \(12\). All unfolding steps below are Tonelli rearrangements of nonnegative terms.

For every nonnegative integrable tuple for which the displayed folds converge,
\[
\mathcal A^{(r)}(\mathcal Ff_1,\ldots,\mathcal Ff_r)
 \ge \mathcal F\mathcal B^{(r)}(f_1,\ldots,f_r),
\qquad r=0,1,2.
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a direct proof, including multiplicities. In the leaf case, expand \(\vartheta(a)\vartheta(1/2-a)\) as the sum of \(\kappa(a+k)\kappa(1/2-a+l)\) over \((k,l)\in\mathbb Z^2\). The two parts of the folded leaf message are exactly the disjoint diagonals \(l=-k\) and \(l=-1-k\). For the second diagonal, reindex its incoming integer and use \(\kappa(1/2+a+k)=\kappa(1/2-a-1-k)\). This proves (4) for \(r=0\).

For \(r\ge1\), unfold each child fold in the left side of (4). The resulting integrand, apart from \(\prod_i f_i(v_i)\), is
\(\vartheta(a)\sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^r}
\vartheta(1/2-a-\sum_i\epsilon_i v_i)\).
The all-positive sign choice contains every term
\(\kappa(a+k)\kappa(1/2-a-k-\sum_i v_i)\)
of the first incoming fold, by selecting the indicated integer from the second periodic sum. The all-negative sign choice contains every term of the second incoming fold: after reindexing that fold, select the residual integer \(-1-k\) and use evenness of \(\kappa\). These two sign choices are distinct for \(r\ge1\), so no summand has been used twice. This proves (4).

Let \(T_h\) be the complete binary tree of height \(h\), for \(0\le h\le3\). Let \(U\) have one child, namely \(T_1\), and let \(V\) have children \(T_0,T_2\). The actual child types are \(\tau(T_0)=0\), \(\tau(T_1)=2\), and \(\tau(T_2)=0\). A leaf can be free in its maximum matching; the root of \(T_1\) cannot be free; and the root of \(T_2\) can be free in a maximum matching of size two. These facts follow by inspecting the two leaves of \(T_1\) and the two disjoint \(T_1\) branches of \(T_2\). The five required transitions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{children}&\text{parent}&\text{parent type}\\ \hline
T_1&U&0\\
T_1,T_1&T_2&0\\
T_0,T_0&T_1&2\\
T_0,T_2&V&2\\
T_2,T_2&T_3&2 .
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, \(\phi_1\) cannot repair a failure of these instances.

Suppose these instances and the leaf instance hold. The leaf instance and (4) give \(\phi_0(a)\ge1/\rho\) for all \(a\). On the other hand, let
\(\mu=\min\{\phi_0(a),\phi_2(a):a\in[0,1]\}\).
Continuity, periodicity, and strict positivity give \(0<\mu<\infty\). Apply (4) to the transitions with children \(T_1,T_1\) and \(T_0,T_0\). In each case both input envelopes are at least \(\mu\), so (2), positivity of the parent fold, and (4) imply \(\phi_0(a)\ge\mu^2/\rho\) and \(\phi_2(a)\ge\mu^2/\rho\) for every \(a\). Consequently
\[
\mu\le\rho=63/40.
\tag{6}
\]
The certificate will derive \(\phi_0(a)\ge2\) and \(\phi_2(a)\ge2\) at every real phase from the same instances, contradicting (6).

We next give a uniform relative quadrature estimate. Let \(g(x)=c\exp(-12\sum_{i=1}^s|x-a_i|)\), with \(c>0\), \(1\le s\le3\), and arbitrary real \(a_i\). On each interval between its cusps, \(g''\) is nonnegative and at most \(9\cdot12^2g\). At a cusp the first derivative has a negative jump. Since \(g'\) tends to zero at both infinities, the signed measure \(g''\) has total mass zero. Its total variation is therefore twice the mass of its nonnegative ordinary part, and is at most \(18\cdot12^2\int_{\mathbb R}g\).

For mesh size \(1/M\), the integral of the piecewise linear interpolant through \(g(k/M)\) is \(M^{-1}\sum_k g(k/M)\). On a single mesh interval, integrating the interpolation error gives the integral of \(g''\) against a function of absolute value at most \(1/(8M^2)\). To verify this last bound directly, on an interval \([a,b]\) of length \(1/M\) the integrated Green kernel at \(t\in[a,b]\) is \((t-a)(b-t)/2\), which is at most \(1/(8M^2)\). This remains valid for the derivative jumps by the measure form of the same integration identity. Summing the intervals and using the total-variation bound yields
\[
\left|\frac1M\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}g(k/M)
       -\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx\right|
 \le\delta\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx,
\qquad \delta=\frac{324}{M^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The decaying tails justify passage from finite intervals to the whole line.

At a fixed incoming value, each internal edge variable in the Boolean subtree energy occurs in exactly three absolute values: its edge cost and the residuals at its two endpoints. Thus (7) applies to that variable for every choice of the other variables. Replacing one integration at a time by its infinite grid sum, and preserving inequalities under every remaining nonnegative integration or summation, proves the following. Let \(J_T(k)\) be the full infinite grid version of \(f_T(k/M)\), including the factor \(M^{-m_T}\) for its \(m_T\) internal edge variables, but no grid factor for its prescribed incoming variable. Then
\[
(1-\delta)^{m_T}f_T(k/M)
 \le J_T(k)\le
(1+\delta)^{m_T}f_T(k/M).
\tag{8}
\]
This argument permits arbitrary residual cusps between grid points; it assumes no alignment of those cusps with the mesh.

For the certificate set \(M=8192\), \(L=8\), \(J=LM\), and \(q=e^{-12/M}\). Let \(\widetilde J_T(k)\) be the grid sum in (8) with every internal edge index restricted to \([-J,J]\). For the incoming indices in that same interval, it obeys the exact finite recursion
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde J_{T_0}(k)
 &=q^{|k|+|M/2-k|},\\
\widetilde J_T(k)
 &=\frac{q^{|k|}}{M^r}
   \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r=-J}^{J}
   \left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^r\widetilde J_{T_\alpha}(i_\alpha)\right)
   q^{|M/2-k-\sum_\alpha i_\alpha|},
 \qquad r=1,2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
These are direct finite sums, not continuous message identities.

Here is an explicit bound on everything omitted when (9) is used to compute a fold. Put \(S_M=M^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}q^{|i|}\). Since \(0<12/M<1\) and \(e^{-x}\le1-x/2\) for \(0\le x\le1\), we have \(S_M=M^{-1}(1+q)/(1-q)\le1/3\). The elementary inequality for the exponential follows from \(e^{-x}\le1-x+x^2/2\). Also \(q^M=e^{-12}<1/3\), since \(e>2\), and hence \(\vartheta(a)<2\).

The fraction of the one-edge grid mass with \(|i|>J\) is \(2q^{J+1}/(1+q)\). Discarding the vertex factors and taking a union bound over \(m_T\) internal edges gives, for a fixed incoming index \(k\),
\[
0\le J_T(k)-\widetilde J_T(k)
 \le q^{|k|}\,m_T S_M^{m_T}
       \frac{2q^{J+1}}{1+q}.
\tag{10}
\]
When the prescribed incoming indices are folded in the two residue classes \(j\) and \(-j\) modulo \(M\), their full edge-factor sum is \(2\vartheta(j/M)\). The sum of their omitted incoming edge factors with \(|k|>J\) is at most \(4q^{J+1}/(1-q^M)\). Applying the trivial bound \(J_T(k)\le q^{|k|}S_M^{m_T}\) to these latter terms, and using (10) for the internal-edge truncation, gives the single uniform bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le&
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\bigl(J_T(j+Mz)+J_T(-j+Mz)\bigr)\\
&-\!\!\sum_{\substack{-J\le k\le J\\k\equiv j\pmod M}}
          \widetilde J_T(k)
 -\!\!\sum_{\substack{-J\le k\le J\\k\equiv-j\pmod M}}
          \widetilde J_T(k)
 \le \frac{8(m_T+1)}{3^{m_T}}q^{J+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Coincident residue classes, at phase zero or one-half, are counted twice on both sides as the definition of \(\mathcal F\) requires. To check the constant, the internal-error coefficient is \(4m_T\vartheta(j/M)/(1+q)<8m_T\), and the incoming-tail coefficient is \(4/(1-q^M)<6<8\).

Combining (8) and (11) produces rigorous sample bounds \(d_T^-(j)\le D_T(j/M)\le d_T^+(j)\). If \(\widetilde d_T^-(j)\) and \(\widetilde d_T^+(j)\) enclose the two truncated sums in (11), valid choices are
\[
d_T^-(j)=\frac{\widetilde d_T^-(j)}{(1+\delta)^{m_T}},
\qquad
d_T^+(j)=
 \frac{\widetilde d_T^+(j)+8(m_T+1)3^{-m_T}q^{J+1}}
      {(1-\delta)^{m_T}}.
\tag{12}
\]
The program rounds the first quantity downward and the second upward.

It remains to turn these message samples into necessary inequalities for entire phase cells. Let \(C_j\) be the centered interval
\([(j-1/2)/M,(j+1/2)/M)\), interpreted modulo one. Suppose numbers \(x_t(j)\ge0\) are already known lower bounds for \(\phi_t\) throughout \(C_j\), for \(t=0,2\). For one of the transitions in (5), with \(r\) children, use these input lower bounds in the left side of (2) and split its integration domain into the products of cells \(C_{i_1}\times\cdots\times C_{i_r}\). The ratio bounds following (3) give, at every \(a\in C_j\),
\[
\phi_{\tau(T)}(a)\ge
\frac{M-60}{M}\,
\frac{\vartheta(j/M)}
     {\rho M^r d_T^+(j)}
 \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r=0}^{M-1}
 \left(\prod_{\alpha=1}^r
       x_{\tau(T_\alpha)}(i_\alpha)d_{T_\alpha}^-(i_\alpha)\right)
 \vartheta\!\left(\frac12-\frac{j+\sum_\alpha i_\alpha}{M}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
For clarity, before the rational simplification the exponential factor here is
\(e^{-12(2+3r/2)/M}\).
The outside \(\vartheta\) loses at most \(e^{-6/M}\), each child fold at most \(e^{-12/M}\), the residual kernel at most \(e^{-6(r+1)/M}\), and dividing by the upper bound for the parent fold loses at most \(e^{-12/M}\). For \(r=1,2\), the total exponent is at most \(60/M\); \(e^{-t}\ge1-t\) then gives \((M-60)/M\). Integer changes in representatives of a phase cell do not affect the periodic kernels. Each product cell has volume \(M^{-r}\). Thus (13) is a bound on every real phase, with no regularity assumption on the unknown envelopes beyond that in the statement.

The finite program below implements (9), (12), and (13). Its names L, B1, B2, U, V, and B3 mean \(T_0,T_1,T_2,U,V,T_3\), respectively. The arrays for messages and kernels have scale \(Q=2^{192}\); the arrays for envelope lower bounds have scale \(R_{\mathrm{ar}}=2^{64}\), called R only inside the code. This integer scaling variable is unrelated to the original body's common budget \(R=n/2+k\).

We specify every arithmetic enclosure rule. The alternating exponential series at \(x=12/M\) has decreasing term magnitudes. Its sums through degrees 15 and 16 enclose \(q\). The integers qn and qu are the respective outward roundings at scale \(D_{\mathrm{ar}}=2^{128}\). Repeated multiplication with downward or upward rounding consequently gives arrays pl and pu satisfying \({\rm pl}[j]/Q\le q^j\le{\rm pu}[j]/Q\). The array Tl is a lower bound for \(Q\vartheta(j/M)\), by the geometric identity
\(\vartheta(j/M)=(q^j+q^{M-j})/(1-q^M)\).
Its numerator is rounded down and its positive denominator rounded up.

The routine conv computes an exact integer convolution. If neither input is zero, every coefficient is at most \(\min(\operatorname{len}(a),\operatorname{len}(b))\max(a)\max(b)\). The byte width chooses a radix strictly larger than this bound, so packing the arrays as digits, multiplying the two Python integers, and unpacking the product causes no carry between coefficients. A zero input is handled by its exact zero convolution. The routine circular adds the two possible ordinary-convolution coefficients in each residue class. It therefore implements exactly the indicated circular convolution, without an FFT or floating arithmetic.

For the residual convolution in (9), if c is the ordinary convolution of the child arrays, the two recurrences used in lift enclose
\(\sum_i c_i q^{|j-i|}\).
The left recurrence is \(l_j=c_j+q l_{j-1}\), and the right recurrence is \(r_j=q(c_{j+1}+r_{j+1})\), with the stated endpoint values. Every multiplication is rounded in the direction of the desired enclosure. For the only possible exterior target, \(j\) beyond the last coefficient, the sum is \(q^{j-(\operatorname{len}(c)-1)}l_{\operatorname{len}(c)-1}\), with the same directed rounding. Multiplication by the incoming factor and division by \(Q^rM^r\) give the required message scale \(Q\). Thus lift preserves a lower or upper bound for (9) by induction on the tree. The routine folds uses (11), then the exact rational factors in (12). Its tail is rounded upward using pu.

Initially the envelope arrays are the downward rounding of \(1/\rho\) in state zero and zero in state two, which are valid by the leaf inequality and positivity. Each subsequent array update is the downward rounding of (13), using lower bounds for its numerator and the upper bound for its parent denominator. Taking the maximum with previous lower bounds preserves validity. Capping a new lower bound at four also preserves validity. The kernels and sums are positive, so every division by a message upper bound is legitimate; the program additionally checks positivity of every message lower array. By induction, every envelope array is a lower bound throughout its corresponding real phase cell.

Here is the complete executable certificate, using only Python standard-library integers and rational numbers.

```python
from fractions import Fraction

M, L, P = 8192, 8, 192
Q, D, R = 1 << P, 1 << 128, 1 << 64
rho_n, rho_d = 63, 40
J = L * M
def down(a,b): return a // b
def up(a,b): return (a+b-1)//b
x = Fraction(12,M)
term = total = Fraction(1)
for j in range(1,17):
    term *= -x/j
    total += term
    if j == 15: elo = total
ehi = total
qn = down(elo.numerator*D,elo.denominator)
qu = up(ehi.numerator*D,ehi.denominator)
assert 0 < qn < qu < D
pl,pu = [Q],[Q]
for j in range(2*J+M//2):
    pl.append(qn*pl[-1]//D)
    pu.append(up(qu*pu[-1],D))
assert pu[M] < Q//3
Tl = [Q*(pl[j]+pl[M-j])//(Q-pl[M]) for j in range(M)]

def conv(a,b):
    ma,mb=max(a),max(b)
    if not ma or not mb: return [0]*(len(a)+len(b)-1)
    width = (min(len(a),len(b))*ma*mb).bit_length()
    width = (width+7)//8
    aa = int.from_bytes(b''.join(v.to_bytes(width,'little') for v in a),'little')
    bb = aa if a is b else int.from_bytes(b''.join(v.to_bytes(width,'little') for v in b),'little')
    n = len(a)+len(b)-1
    data = (aa*bb).to_bytes(width*n,'little')
    return [int.from_bytes(data[j*width:(j+1)*width],'little') for j in range(n)]

def circular(a,b):
    c=conv(a,b)
    return [c[j]+(c[j+M] if j+M<len(c) else 0) for j in range(M)]

def lift(arrays,upper):
    r=len(arrays)
    c=arrays[0] if r==1 else conv(arrays[0],arrays[1])
    q=qu if upper else qn
    rnd=up if upper else down
    left=c.copy()
    right=[0]*len(c)
    for j in range(1,len(c)):
        left[j]+=rnd(q*left[j-1],D)
    for j in range(len(c)-2,-1,-1):
        right[j]=rnd(q*(c[j+1]+right[j+1]),D)
    powers=pu if upper else pl
    out=[]
    den=Q**r*M**r
    for k in range(-J,J+1):
        j=M//2-k+r*J
        if j>=len(c):
            value=rnd(powers[j-len(c)+1]*left[-1],Q)
        else:
            value=left[j]+right[j]
        out.append(rnd(powers[abs(k)]*value,den))
    return out

def folds(lo,hi,m):
    a,b=[0]*M,[0]*M
    for j,k in enumerate(range(-J,J+1)):
        a[k%M]+=lo[j]
        b[k%M]+=hi[j]
    a=[a[j]+a[-j%M] for j in range(M)]
    b=[b[j]+b[-j%M] for j in range(M)]
    tail=up(8*(m+1)*pu[J+1],3**m)
    n=(M*M)**m
    dl,du=(M*M+324)**m,(M*M-324)**m
    a=[v*n//dl for v in a]
    b=[up((v+tail)*n,du) for v in b]
    assert min(a)>0
    return a,b

data={}
lo=[pl[abs(k)+abs(M//2-k)] for k in range(-J,J+1)]
hi=[pu[abs(k)+abs(M//2-k)] for k in range(-J,J+1)]
data['L']=(lo,hi,0,folds(lo,hi,0))
def build(name,children):
    lo=lift([data[c][0] for c in children],False)
    hi=lift([data[c][1] for c in children],True)
    m=sum(data[c][2] for c in children)+len(children)
    data[name]=(lo,hi,m,folds(lo,hi,m))
build('B1',['L','L'])
build('B2',['B1','B1'])
build('U',['B1'])
build('V',['L','B2'])
build('B3',['B2','B2'])

rules=[
    (0,['B1'],'U'),
    (0,['B1','B1'],'B2'),
    (2,['L','L'],'B1'),
    (2,['L','B2'],'V'),
    (2,['B2','B2'],'B3'),
]
types={'L':0,'B1':2,'B2':0}
phi={0:[R*rho_d//rho_n]*M,2:[0]*M}
denbase=M*rho_n
for iteration in range(1,101):
    inputs={name:[v*p//R for v,p in zip(data[name][3][0],phi[types[name]])]
            for name in types}
    convol={name:circular(value,Tl) for name,value in inputs.items()}
    out={t:phi[t].copy() for t in phi}
    for t,children,parent in rules:
        r=len(children)
        raw=convol[children[0]] if r==1 else circular(convol[children[0]],inputs[children[1]])
        upper=data[parent][3][1]
        den=denbase*M**r*Q**(r+1)
        for j in range(M):
            value=(M-60)*rho_d*R*Tl[j]*raw[(M//2-j)%M]//(den*upper[j])
            out[t][j]=max(out[t][j],min(4*R,value))
    phi=out
    if min(phi[0])>=2*R and min(phi[2])>=2*R:
        break
assert iteration<100
assert min(phi[0])>=2*R and min(phi[2])>=2*R
print('PASS')
print('mesh:',M,'cutoff:',L,'precision:',P)
print('q enclosure:',qn,qu)
print('iterations:',iteration)
print('minimum envelopes:',min(phi[0]),min(phi[2]),'scale:',R)
print('folded message brackets at phase zero:')
for name in data:
    print(name,data[name][3][0][0],data[name][3][1][0])
```

\[
C=\left\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-):
\begin{array}{l}
t_v^+,t_v^-\ge0\quad(v\ne1),\\
y_v\ge|z_v-1/2-q_v|\quad(v\in V(G)),\\
\displaystyle\sum_v y_v+\sum_{v\ne1}(t_v^++t_v^-)\le R
\end{array}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Both nonnegative flow blocks and the one common budget remain present. The affine map from the nonnegative simplex in variables \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf t^+,\mathbf t^-)\) with total sum at most \(R\), given by \(y_v=u_v+v_v\) and \(z_v=1/2+u_v-v_v+q_v\), is invertible. Its inverse adds and subtracts the residual from \(y_v\) and divides by two. Thus (14) is a full-dimensional rational simplex in the required original dimension. Every Boolean fiber is positive: take \(t_v^+=t_v^-=\varepsilon\), \(y_v=1/2+\varepsilon\), with \(0<d\varepsilon<1\).

For a real base \(\mathbf z\), its original energy is
\(E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g)=\sum_{v\ne1}|g_v|+\sum_v|z_v-1/2-q_v|\).
With \(\sigma_v=(-1)^{\operatorname{depth}(v)}\), \(j_v=(1+\sigma_v)/2\), and the edge alias \(\xi_{\{p(v),v\}}=\sigma_vg_v\), the change of edge coordinates has absolute Jacobian one and gives \(\sigma_vq_v=\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\). The Boolean coloring energy is exactly
\[
E_{\mathbf j}(\boldsymbol\xi)
 =\sum_e|\xi_e|+\sum_v\left|\frac12-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\right|.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently its full descendant integrals at parameter \(12\), with the incoming edge charged to the descendant, are precisely the messages in (1). This is not a minimum-energy approximation.

To verify the original-volume normalization directly, write \(s_v=\min(t_v^+,t_v^-)\). The substitution \(t_v^+=(g_v)_++s_v\), \(t_v^-=(-g_v)_++s_v\) has absolute Jacobian one off null boundaries. Subtract the residuals from the \(n\) variables \(y_v\); these slacks and the \(m\) variables \(2s_v\) fill a nonnegative \(\ell\)-simplex. Thus the original fiber volume is
\[
w_{\mathbf z}
 =\frac{2^{-m}}{\ell!}\int_{\mathbb R^m}
          (R-E_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf g))_+^\ell\,d\mathbf g.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, the auxiliary messages have not replaced either flow block or changed the budget of the original body.

As each original integer coordinate \(z_v\) ranges through \(\mathbb Z\), the quantity \(1/2-\sigma_v(z_v-1/2)\) ranges bijectively through \(\mathbb Z\). Summing the Laplace integrals over every original integer base therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}(G,12)
 &=\int_{\mathbb R^m}
       e^{-12\sum_e|\xi_e|}
       \prod_v \kappa\!\left(\tfrac12-\sum_{e\ni v}\xi_e\right)

 &=\int_{\mathbb R^m}
       e^{-12\sum_e|\xi_e|}

       \,d\boldsymbol\xi .
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Every integer base is included in the second integral. Its finiteness follows from boundedness of \(\vartheta\) and integrability of the edge factors.

For completeness, let \(M_T^{\mathrm{all}}\) be the incoming message obtained by using \(\vartheta\) at the vertices in place of \(\kappa\), while retaining every edge factor. Define \(A_T(a)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}M_T^{\mathrm{all}}(a+z)\). Since \(M_T^{\mathrm{all}}(u)=\kappa(u)\) times a one-periodic function, periodizing the child variables gives exactly

\[
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm B}(G,12)=f_G(0),
\qquad
\mathsf Z_{\mathrm{all}}(G,12)=A_G(0)/\vartheta(0).
\tag{18}
\]
The root division removes the periodized artificial incoming edge and leaves every true edge intact. Thus the failed local test has precisely the normalization required for the original tree partition comparison.
