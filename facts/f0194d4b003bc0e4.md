## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\) be positive integers with \(L\ge h\), and let \(\theta_1,\theta_2\in\mathbb R\). Put
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:
       \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+h(y_1+y_2)\le L\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)=
\frac{e^{\theta_1y_1+\theta_2y_2}}
 {\sum_{(\mathbf u,v_1,v_2)\in D}e^{\theta_1v_1+\theta_2v_2}}.
\]
Use the actual closed-halfspace depth and third moment of 01a4100542206b94 with its \(N=m+2\), its support equal to this \(D\), and its probability equal to this original \(p\):
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{\substack{H\subset\mathbb R^{m+2}
                         \text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}p(H),
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]
There is an absolute constant \(\kappa>0\) such that
\(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-(m+2)}\) for every stipulated parameter choice. Explicitly, let \(g>1\) be the absolute constant in 609407d6db81b18b, put \(g_0=\min\{2,g^{3/2}\}>1\), and put \(C_*=200000000\). One may take
\[
\kappa=\frac{1}{128C_*^4g_0}
             \left(\frac{\log g_0}{8}\right)^8.
\]

## Proof

The displayed support is complete: it is the intersection of the integer lattice with a convex bounded polytope, and consequently every lattice point of its own convex hull belongs to it. Thus the definitions in 01a4100542206b94 apply without a change of support or normalization.

1. The exact shear and a geometric interval comparison.

Set \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\ge1\) and \(\ell=L-hK\), so \(0\le\ell<h\). The map \(U=y_1+y_2\), \(J=y_1\) is unimodular, with inverse \((y_1,y_2)=(J,U-J)\). Its support condition is \(0\le J\le U\), and the exponent is \(\theta_2U+(\theta_1-\theta_2)J\). In particular, the conditional law of \(J\) at fixed \(U\) is exactly the truncated geometric law with these weights.

For the proof let \(\alpha=\max\{\theta_1,\theta_2\}\), \(\beta=|\theta_1-\theta_2|\), and let \(V\) be the coordinate with the smaller tilt: take \(V=J\) when \(\theta_1\le\theta_2\), and \(V=U-J\) otherwise. This additional map is unimodular and gives exponent \(\alpha U-\beta V\). Finally set \(t=K-U\). These invertible affine maps preserve every closed halfspace and its probability, and the original probability in the new coordinates is exactly
\[
0\le t\le K,\quad 0\le V\le K-t,\quad
\mathbf x\in\Delta_{\ell+ht,m},\qquad
p(\mathbf x,t,V)=\frac{e^{-\alpha t-\beta V}}{\mathcal Z},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\Delta_{T,m}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i x_i\le T\}\) and
\[
F(t)=|\Delta_{\ell+ht,m}|=\binom{\ell+ht+m}{m}
 =\frac{h^m}{m!}\prod_{i=1}^m(t+a_i),\quad a_i=\frac{\ell+i}{h}>0,
\qquad
G_\beta(U)=\sum_{j=0}^{U}e^{-\beta j},
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\alpha t}F(t)G_\beta(K-t).
\tag{2}
\]
The factor \(e^{\alpha K}\) has canceled from the original normalizing sum. The count for \(F\) follows by adjoining the nonnegative slack and counting compositions. Write
\(q(t)=e^{-\alpha t}F(t)G_\beta(K-t)/\mathcal Z\) for the original marginal law of \(t\), and put \(r=m+1\).

We use the following elementary monotone weighting observation. Multiplication of a line probability by a nonincreasing nonnegative function, followed by normalization, decreases probabilities of upper intervals and decreases the mean of a nonnegative coordinate. For an increasing function \(A\) and decreasing weighting function \(B\), this follows from
\(2\operatorname{Cov}(A(W),B(W))=\mathbb E[(A(W)-A(W'))(B(W)-B(W'))]\le0\), where \(W,W'\) are independent. The reversed statement holds for increasing weights.

If integers \(0\le U_-\le U_+\) satisfy \(U_++1\le2(U_-+1)\), there exists an integer \(n_V\), with \(1\le n_V\le U_-+1\), such that
\[
\frac{n_Ve^{-\beta(n_V-1)}}{G_\beta(U_+)}\ge\frac1{12}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\beta=0\), take \(n_V=U_-+1\), giving at least \(1/2\). For \(\beta>1\), take \(n_V=1\); the infinite geometric sum gives at least \(1-e^{-\beta}>1/2\). For \(0<\beta\le1\), put \(s_V=\min\{U_-+1,1/\beta\}\ge1\) and \(n_V=\max\{1,\lfloor s_V\rfloor\}\). Then \(n_V\ge s_V/2\), \(\beta(n_V-1)\le1\), and
\(G_\beta(U_+)\le\min\{U_++1,2/\beta\}\le2s_V\). Here \(1-e^{-\beta}\ge\beta/(1+\beta)\ge\beta/2\) follows from \(e^\beta\ge1+\beta\). Thus the quotient is at least \(1/(4e)>1/12\).

We will construct a nonempty consecutive interval \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq\{0,\ldots,K\}\) such that, with \(U_-=K-b\), \(U_+=K-a\), and \(w=b-a\),
\[
\frac{F(b)}{F(a)}\le e^{1/8},\qquad
|\alpha|w\le\frac1{16},\qquad U_++1\le2(U_-+1),
\qquad
q(I)\ge\frac{1}{25\cdot205825\,r^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Singleton intervals satisfy the first three conditions automatically. Assuming (4), choose \(n_V\) from (3) and let
\(Q=\operatorname{Unif}(\Delta_{\ell+ha,m}\times I\times\{0,\ldots,n_V-1\})\), extended by zero elsewhere. This support lies inside (1). The coefficient
\[
\lambda=\frac{|I|F(a)n_V
              (\min_{t\in I}e^{-\alpha t})e^{-\beta(n_V-1)}}{\mathcal Z}
\tag{5}
\]
satisfies \(p\ge\lambda Q\) pointwise. Moreover,
\[
q(I)\le\frac{|I|(\max_{t\in I}e^{-\alpha t})F(b)G_\beta(U_+)}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\lambda\ge e^{-3/16}\frac{q(I)}{12}\ge\frac{q(I)}{24}
\ge\frac1{C_*r^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality uses \(24\cdot25\cdot205825=123495000<C_*\). Thus it remains to prove (4).

2. Singleton cases.

If \(K<128r\), some height atom has \(q(t_0)\ge1/(K+1)\ge1/(128r)\). Its singleton interval satisfies (4).

Suppose \(\alpha\ge1/4\). For every \(t\ge8m\) with \(t+1\le K\),
\[
\frac{q(t+1)}{q(t)}
\le e^{-\alpha}\left(1+\frac1t\right)^m\le e^{-1/8},
\]
because \(G_\beta(K-t)\) is nonincreasing in \(t\). Shifting the tail by 32 gives \(q(t\ge8m+32)\le e^{-4}<1/16\), with empty tails allowed. Hence the at most \(33r\) heights \(0\le t\le\min\{K,8m+32\}\) have mass at least \(15/16\); one has \(q(t_0)\ge1/(36r)\). Its singleton interval satisfies (4).

Suppose \(\alpha=-v\) with \(v\ge1/4\). The original \(U\)-marginal is proportional to
\(e^{-vU}F(K-U)G_\beta(U)\), \(0\le U\le K\). The average \(G_\beta(U)/(U+1)\) of the first \(U+1\) decreasing geometric weights is nonincreasing. Consequently this marginal is stochastically dominated by the truncated law proportional to \((U+1)e^{-vU}\). The untruncated law has mean \(2/(e^v-1)\le2/v\le8\); truncation only decreases it. Markov's inequality gives original mass at least \(3/4\) on \(U\le32\). Some such height has mass at least \(1/44\). Again its singleton interval in \(t=K-U\) satisfies (4).

3. The case \(0<\alpha<1/4\) and \(K\ge128r\).

Put \(s=\min\{K,1/\alpha\}>4\) and \(A=\lceil s/4\rceil\), so \(s/4\le A\le s/2\). For each \(0\le t<A\), shifting to \(t+A\) multiplies its unnormalized height weight by at least \(1/4\). Indeed \(F(t+A)\ge F(t)\), \(\alpha A\le1/2\), and
\[
\frac{G_\beta(K-t-A)}{G_\beta(K-t)}
\ge\frac{K-t-A+1}{K-t+1}\ge\frac12.
\]
The first inequality holds because the average of initial geometric weights is nonincreasing; the second follows from \(3A\le K+2\). The shifted heights are in the support. The shift is injective into \(t\ge A\), so \(q(t\ge A)\ge1/5\).

We also have \(\mathbb E_qt\le2r/\alpha\). To verify this directly, remove the decreasing factor \(G_\beta(K-t)\), which can only increase the mean. The positive polynomial \(F(t)\) has an expansion \(\sum_{k=0}^m c_kt^k\) with all \(c_k>0\). The remaining height law is a convex mixture of laws proportional to \(e^{-\alpha t}t^k\) on \(0\le t\le K\), using \(0^0=1\). For \(k=0\), the untruncated geometric mean is at most \(1/\alpha\). For \(k\ge1\), put \(S_k=\sum_{t\ge1}t^ke^{-\alpha t}\). Integral comparison on consecutive unit intervals gives
\[
S_k\ge e^{-\alpha}\frac{k!}{\alpha^{k+1}},\qquad
S_{k+1}\le e^{\alpha}\frac{(k+1)!}{\alpha^{k+2}},\qquad
\frac{S_{k+1}}{S_k}\le e^{2\alpha}\frac{k+1}{\alpha}<\frac{2r}{\alpha}.
\]
For the first comparison use \(x^ke^{-\alpha x}\le e^\alpha t^ke^{-\alpha t}\) on \([t-1,t]\); for the second use \(x^{k+1}e^{-\alpha x}\ge e^{-\alpha}t^{k+1}e^{-\alpha t}\) on \([t,t+1]\). The integrals over the positive half-line equal the factorial expressions by integration by parts. Truncation lowers these means. This proves the claimed original mean bound.

Set \(B=\min\{K,\lceil200r/\alpha\rceil\}\). Then \(q(t>B)\le1/100\) and \(B\le201rs\). Put \(r_0=\lfloor K/(128r)\rfloor\ge1\). The marginal \(q\) is stochastically dominated by the reference law proportional to \(F(t)\), since \(e^{-\alpha t}G_\beta(K-t)\) is decreasing. Let \(n_0=\lfloor K/(4r)\rfloor\). The exact product formula and Bernoulli's inequality give
\[
\frac{F(K-n_0)}{F(K)}\ge(1-n_0/K)^m\ge\frac34.
\]
The normalizing sum of the reference law is at least \((n_0+1)F(K-n_0)\), while its last \(r_0\) heights have total weight at most \(r_0F(K)\). Therefore
\[
q(t>K-r_0)\le\frac43\frac{r_0}{n_0+1}\le\frac1{24}.
\]
It follows that the consecutive interval \([A,\min\{B,K-r_0\}]\cap\mathbb Z\) has original mass at least
\(1/5-1/100-1/24=89/600>1/8\).

Partition this interval into consecutive blocks of at most
\(n=\max\{1,\lfloor s/(512r)\rfloor\}\) heights. Since \(n\ge s/(1024r)\), there are at most
\(1+B/n\le205825r^2\) blocks. One block \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\) has mass at least \(1/(8\cdot205825r^2)\), which exceeds the mass required in (4). Its width satisfies \(w\le n-1\le s/(512r)\), and \(a\ge s/4\), so
\[
\frac{F(b)}{F(a)}\le(1+w/a)^m\le e^{m/(128r)}\le e^{1/8},
\qquad \alpha w\le\frac1{512r}\le\frac1{16}.
\]
Furthermore \(U_-=K-b\ge r_0\ge K/(256r)\), whereas \(w\le K/(512r)\le U_-/2\). Thus \(U_++1\le2(U_-+1)\). This proves (4) in this case.

4. The case \(\alpha=-v\), \(0\le v<1/4\), and \(K\ge128r\).

Work with the original \(U\)-marginal, proportional to
\(W(U)=e^{-vU}F(K-U)G_\beta(U)\). Since \(F(K-U)\), \(e^{-vU}\), and \(G_\beta(U)/(U+1)\) are all nonincreasing, the monotone weighting observation compares this law to the reference weights \(U+1\). Hence
\[
\mathbb P_q(U\le\lfloor K/2\rfloor)
\ge\frac{(\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1)(\lfloor K/2\rfloor+2)}{(K+1)(K+2)}\ge\frac14.
\tag{7}
\]
When \(v>0\), comparison instead to weights \((U+1)e^{-vU}\), as in part 2, also gives \(\mathbb E_qU\le2/v\).

Put \(s=\min\{K/(16r),1/v\}\), with \(1/v=+\infty\) when \(v=0\), and put \(A=\lceil s/4\rceil\). Here \(s>4\), \(A\le s/2\), and \(2A-1\le s\le K/(16r)\). For \(0\le U<A\),
\[
\frac{F(K-U-A)}{F(K-U)}
\ge\left(1-\frac{A}{K-s}\right)^m
\ge1-\frac{m}{16r}\ge\frac12.
\]
Indeed \(A\le K/(32r)\) and \(K-s\ge K/2\). Also \(vA\le1/2\), and \(G_\beta(U+A)\ge G_\beta(U)\). Thus \(W(U+A)/W(U)\ge1/4\). The shifted heights lie between \(A\) and \(\lfloor K/2\rfloor\). If \(L_0\) is the mass on \(U<A\) and \(H_0\) the mass on \(A\le U\le\lfloor K/2\rfloor\), this injection gives \(H_0\ge L_0/4\). By (7), \(L_0+H_0\ge1/4\), and therefore \(H_0\ge1/20\).

For \(v>0\), let \(B=\min\{\lfloor K/2\rfloor,\lceil200/v\rceil\}\); for \(v=0\), let \(B=\lfloor K/2\rfloor\). The mean bound and Markov's inequality give original mass at least \(1/20-1/100=1/25\) on \(A\le U\le B\). When \(v=0\), the stronger bound \(1/20\) holds. In either case \(B\le201rs\): if \(s=K/(16r)\), use \(B\le K/2=8rs\), and if \(s=1/v\), use \(B\le200s+1\le201s\).

Partition \([A,B]\cap\mathbb Z\) into blocks of at most
\(n=\max\{1,\lfloor s/(512r)\rfloor\}\) heights. There are at most \(205825r^2\) blocks, by the same count as in part 3. One block \([u,z]\cap\mathbb Z\) has mass at least \(1/(25\cdot205825r^2)\). Take \(I=[K-z,K-u]\cap\mathbb Z\) in the \(t\)-coordinate. Its width is \(w=z-u\le s/(512r)\), its minimum total height is \(U_-=u\ge s/4\), and its minimum \(t\)-coordinate is \(K-z\ge K/2\). Consequently
\[

\frac{F(K-u)}{F(K-z)}\le\exp(2mw/K)\le e^{1/8},
\qquad vw\le\frac1{512r}\le\frac1{16}.

For the count bound use \(s\le K/(16r)\). These are precisely (4), proving it in the final case. All possibilities for \(\alpha\), \(K\), and \(\beta\) have now been covered, so (6) supplies the pointwise original comparison \(p\ge[C_*r^2]^{-1}Q\) for every stipulated input.

5. Moments and the original-root comparison.

For every integer \(T\ge0\), let \(u_{T,m}\) be uniform on \(\Delta_{T,m}\). We first establish
\[
M_3(u_{T,m})\ge\frac18g_0^m8^{-m}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(T\ge2\), apply 609407d6db81b18b with its rank \(r=m\), its dilation parameter (called \(m\) there) equal to the present \(T\), and its tilt vector equal to zero. This is the exact normalization map. For a root \(\mathbf j\), its composition is \(\mathbf j^+=(T-\sum_i j_i,j_1,\ldots,j_m)\), and that fact's count \(k_{\mathbf j^+}\) is exactly the number of endpoints \(\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,m}\) with \(2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\in\Delta_{T,m}\). Put \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=k_{\mathbf j^+}/|\Delta_{T,m}|\). The accepted reflection-moment bound states
\(\sum_{\mathbf j}u_{T,m}(\mathbf j)\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2\ge g^m4^{-m}\).
Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains the target and at least one endpoint of each antipodal pair. Uniform weights therefore give \(\tau_{u_{T,m}}(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}/2\), including boundary roots. Probability \(L^p\)-norm monotonicity now gives
\[
M_3(u_{T,m})\ge\frac18\mathbb E\mathsf H^3
\ge\frac18(\mathbb E\mathsf H^2)^{3/2}
\ge\frac18g^{3m/2}8^{-m}\ge\frac18g_0^m8^{-m}.
\]
For \(T=1\), the \(m+1\) vertices have actual depth \(1/(m+1)\), so the third moment is \((m+1)^{-3}\). The inequality \((m+1)^3\le2\cdot4^m\) follows at \(m=1\) and then by induction from \(((m+2)/(m+1))^3\le27/8<4\); together with \(g_0\le2\) this proves (8). For \(T=0\), the law is a singleton of depth one, again proving (8).

Each uniform interval factor of \(Q\) has \(M_3\ge1/32\), by 01a4100542206b94 with \(N=1\), exponent \(q=3\), and its support equal to that consecutive interval. This includes singleton intervals. Actual depth is supermultiplicative for a product law: a halfspace through a product root has centered projection
\(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)+b_0(T_0-t)+c_0(V_0-v)\ge0\). The intersection of the three events in which the separate centered projections are nonnegative lies inside the cap. Independence gives its probability at least the product of the three factor depths. A zero coefficient has event probability one, which preserves this inequality. For a containing halfspace whose boundary does not pass through the root, first translate its boundary to the root to obtain a contained halfspace. Thus
\[
M_3(Q)\ge M_3(u_{\ell+ha,m})
 M_3(\operatorname{Unif}(I))
 M_3(\operatorname{Unif}\{0,\ldots,n_V-1\})
\ge\frac1{8192}g_0^m8^{-m}.
\tag{9}
\]

The comparison \(p\ge\lambda Q\) is in the original normalization of (1). Every original closed halfspace has \(p(H)\ge\lambda Q(H)\), so every original root in the product support has \(\tau_p\ge\lambda\tau_Q\). \[
\begin{aligned}

&\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in\operatorname{supp}Q}
 p(\mathbf z)(\lambda\tau_Q(\mathbf z))^3
\ge\lambda^4M_3(Q)\\
&\ge\frac{1}{8192C_*^4}
       \frac{g_0^m}{(m+1)^8}\,8^{-m}.

\tag{10}

Exactly one factor of \(\lambda\) comes from the original root probability and three from its original depth. No conditional law has replaced the original root weighting in (10).

Finally, \(e^u\ge u\) with \(u=(m+1)\log g_0/8\), raised to the eighth power, yields
\(g_0^m/(m+1)^8\ge g_0^{-1}(\log g_0/8)^8\). Insert this in (10) and use \(8^{-m}=64\cdot8^{-(m+2)}\) to obtain the stated \(\kappa\). Returning through the invertible affine shear proves the bound for all original roots and all original closed halfspaces.
