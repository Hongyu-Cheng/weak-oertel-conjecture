## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf x})_{\mathbf x\in A}\) be a strictly positive probability law. For \(\mathbf j\in A\), define its actual closed-halfspace depth, entropy capacity, capacity second moment, and depth second moment by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^m\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
                         \mathbf j\in H}}p(H),\\
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^m}
       \sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}
       e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},\\
\mathcal B_2(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}
                         \mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2,\\
\mathcal M_2(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}
                         \tau_p(\mathbf j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Writing \(D(\nu\|p)=\sum_{\mathbf x}\nu_{\mathbf x}
\log(\nu_{\mathbf x}/p_{\mathbf x})\), with zero summands omitted, one has for every \(\mathbf j\in A\)

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
=\exp\!\left[-\min_{\substack{\nu\text{ a probability on }A\\
                              \sum_{\mathbf x}\nu_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x=\mathbf j}}
                       D(\nu\|p)\right],
\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf j)\le \mathsf C_p(\mathbf j).
}
\tag{2}
\]

The entropy formula includes every boundary center. If \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is the minimal face of \(\operatorname{conv}A\) containing \(\mathbf j\), the unique entropy minimizer is supported on \(A\cap F_{\mathbf j}\) and is positive at every point of that face support. The exponential infimum in (1) need not be attained in the ambient space when \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is proper.

For every fixed finite positive law \(p\), with no lattice or log-affinity hypothesis,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathcal M_2(p^{\otimes q})^{1/q}
=\mathcal B_2(p).
}
\tag{3}
\]

Now let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be complete,
\(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let
\(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\). Interpret parity in the intrinsic affine lattice
\(\operatorname{aff}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), which has \(2^r\) parity classes. If

\[
p_{\mathbf x}=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}
 {\sum_{\mathbf y\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf y}}
\qquad(\mathbf x\in A)
\tag{4}
\]

for an arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_2(p)\ge4^{-r}.}
\tag{5}
\]

Equality is completely characterized:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_2(p)=4^{-r}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p\text{ is uniform on exactly }2^r\text{ points and every point of }A
\text{ is a vertex of }\operatorname{conv}A.
}
\tag{6}
\]

If \(|A|>2^r\) and \(\min_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}\ge\eta>0\), the strict improvement has the quantitative form

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_2(p)\ge4^{-r}+\frac{\eta^3}{4}.}
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently, in the equality case of (6),
\(\mathcal M_2(p^{\otimes q})=4^{-rq}\) for every \(q\ge1\); outside that case,

\[
4^{rq}\mathcal M_2(p^{\otimes q})\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{8}
\]

Finally, fix a finite family
\(\mathscr A=\{A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(s)}\}\) of nonempty finite complete supports, where every \(n_t\ge1\),
\(A^{(t)}\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_t}\), and put
\(r_t=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A^{(t)})\). Choose any \(k\ge1\), indices
\(t(1),\ldots,t(k)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\), with repetitions allowed, set
\(A_i=A^{(t(i))}\) and \(r_i=r_{t(i)}\), and choose arbitrary, independently chosen tilts
\(\boldsymbol\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_{t(i)}}\). Let \(p_i\) be the law (4) on \(A_i\), let

\[
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k p_i,
\qquad
R=\sum_{i=1}^k r_i.
\tag{9}
\]

There is an explicitly defined constant \(a_{\mathscr A}>0\), depending only on the fixed supports and not on \(k\), their repetitions, the tilts, or the ambient dimensions retained around their affine hulls, such that

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_2(P)\ge a_{\mathscr A}^{\,2}4^{-R}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Here is one admissible explicit choice. For \(A\in\mathscr A\), put
\(M_A=|A|\) and \(r_A=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\). For every nonempty \(B\subseteq A\), let \(F_A(B)\) be the minimal face of \(\operatorname{conv}A\) containing \(B\), let
\(\mathbf b_B=|B|^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf x\in B}\mathbf x\), and fix one representation

\[
\mathbf b_B=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)}
              \lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf v,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)}
              \lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}=1,
\qquad
\lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}>0
\quad(\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)).
\tag{11}
\]

Define

\[
\lambda_A:=\min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne B\subseteq A\\
                \mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)}}
                \lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}>0,
\qquad
\eta:=\min_{A\in\mathscr A}
       \frac{\exp[-\log(4M_A)/\lambda_A]}{M_A}>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(\mathscr F\) be the finite collection of all nonempty face supports
\(E=A\cap F\), where \(A\in\mathscr A\) and \(F\) is a face of \(\operatorname{conv}A\). For \(E\in\mathscr F\) and \(\mathbf j\in E\), let \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(E\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), and set

\[
\rho:=\min_{\substack{E\in\mathscr F,\ \mathbf j\in E,\ \mathbf x\in E\\
                       r_{E,\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)>0}}
                 r_{E,\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)>0,
\quad
\kappa:=\max\{3,\rho^{-1}\},
\quad
D_0:=\log\frac1{\eta\rho},
\quad
\alpha:=\frac1{8\kappa},
\quad
\beta:=\sqrt{8\kappa}\,D_0.
\tag{13}
\]

If every \(A\in\mathscr A\) satisfies \(|A|\le2^{r_A}\), set \(g=1\) and one may take
\(a_{\mathscr A}=1\). Otherwise define

\[
g:=\min\!\left\{\frac{27}{16},
       \min_{\substack{A\in\mathscr A\\|A|>2^{r_A}}}
       \left(1+\frac{4^{r_A}\eta^3}{4}\right)\right\}>1
\tag{14}
\]

and one may take

\[
\boxed{
a_{\mathscr A}
=\alpha\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta^2}{2\log g}\right)>0.
}
\tag{15}
\]

More precisely, if \(b\) of the selected factors satisfy
\(|A_i|>2^{\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_i)}\), then

\[
\boxed{
4^R\mathcal M_2(P)
\ge\alpha^2\exp\!\left(b\log g-2\beta\sqrt b\right).
}
\tag{16}
\]

For supports presented in ambient dimensions \(n_{t(i)}\), (10) implies the weaker ambient H2 normalization
\(\mathcal M_2(P)\ge a_{\mathscr A}^{\,2}4^{-\sum_i n_{t(i)}}\). The dependence of (12)--(15) on \(\mathscr A\) is essential to the scope of this theorem; no support-independent lower bound for \(a_{\mathscr A}\) is asserted.

## Proof

All assertions are invariant under an invertible affine change of coordinates on the affine hull of a support. If \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) has intrinsic rank \(r\), its affine lattice
\(L_A=\operatorname{aff}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is an affine translate of a rank-\(r\) lattice. Choosing a lattice basis identifies it with \(\mathbb Z^r\). Ambient closed halfspaces restrict to intrinsic closed halfspaces or to the whole affine hull, and every intrinsic halfspace extends to an ambient one. Thus depth is unchanged. Completeness becomes
\(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^r\). We may therefore work intrinsically whenever parity or dimension is used. When \(r=0\), the original positive-dimensional ambient space is retained for the definition of proper halfspaces; the support is a singleton and its depth is one.

We first prove (2), including its boundary assertion. Fix \(\mathbf j\in A\), let \(F=F_{\mathbf j}\) be its minimal face, and write
\(V_F=\operatorname{span}(F-F)\). An exposing vector \(\mathbf b\) may be chosen so that

\[
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)=0\quad(\mathbf x\in A\cap F),
\qquad
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)<0\quad(\mathbf x\in A\setminus F).
\tag{17}
\]

If \(F=\operatorname{conv}A\), the second condition is empty and we take \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\). Every probability law on \(A\) with mean \(\mathbf j\) is supported on \(A\cap F\): taking the expectation in (17) leaves a sum of nonpositive terms equal to zero. Since \(\mathbf j\in\operatorname{relint}F\), there is a feasible mean-\(\mathbf j\) law positive on all of \(A\cap F\). Indeed, give a small common positive mass to all those points; the remaining required mean is still in \(F\) when that mass is small, and can be represented by its vertices. Relative entropy has a unique minimizer on the compact feasible polytope. That minimizer is positive throughout \(A\cap F\), because mixing it with the preceding positive feasible law would otherwise create a negative \(t\log t\) first-order term at a zero coordinate and strictly decrease the entropy objective for small \(t>0\).

The Lagrange multiplier equations on the relative interior of this feasible polytope give a vector \(\mathbf a\in V_F\) and a normalizing scalar such that the minimizer \(r_{\mathbf j}\) satisfies

\[
r_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
=\frac{p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}}
       {Z_F(\mathbf a)},
\qquad
Z_F(\mathbf a):=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A\cap F}
       p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}.
\tag{18}
\]

Its mean equation says that \(\mathbf a\) minimizes \(Z_F\) on \(V_F\), and

\[
D(r_{\mathbf j}\|p)=-\log Z_F(\mathbf a).
\tag{19}
\]

For every ambient \(\mathbf u\), the sum defining \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\) is at least its part on \(F\), and orthogonal projection of \(\mathbf u\) onto \(V_F\) does not change that part. Hence it is at least \(Z_F(\mathbf a)\). Conversely, substituting \(\mathbf u=\mathbf a+t\mathbf b\) and letting \(t\to\infty\) leaves the terms on \(F\) unchanged and sends every term outside \(F\) to zero. Thus the exponential infimum equals \(Z_F(\mathbf a)=e^{-D(r_{\mathbf j}\|p)}\). This proves the entropy identity even when its ambient infimum is attained only in the limit.

For every nonzero \(\mathbf u\), the closed halfspace
\(H_{\mathbf u}=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)\ge0\}\) contains \(\mathbf j\), and pointwise

\[
\mathbf1_{H_{\mathbf u}}(\mathbf x)
\le e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}.
\tag{20}
\]

Therefore \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\) is at most every nonzero-\(\mathbf u\) exponential sum. For \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\), the same numerical bound is \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\le1\). Taking the infimum proves the direction \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\le\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\).

We next prove a product conversion used both for (3) and for the family theorem. Let \(p_1,\ldots,p_q\) be arbitrary finite positive laws. For each target \(\mathbf j_i\), let \(r_{i,\mathbf j_i}\) be its entropy minimizer from (2). Suppose one constant \(\kappa\ge3\) satisfies

\[
r_{i,\mathbf j_i}(\mathbf x)>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_{i,\mathbf j_i}(\mathbf x)\ge\kappa^{-1},
\tag{21}
\]

and suppose, for every target tuple, that

\[
\sum_{i=1}^q
\operatorname{Var}_{r_{i,\mathbf j_i}}
 \left[\log\frac{r_{i,\mathbf j_i}(\mathbf X_i)}
                   {p_i(\mathbf X_i)}\right]
\le V.
\tag{22}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa V}}}{(8\kappa)^2}
\prod_{i=1}^q\mathcal B_2(p_i)
\le
\mathcal M_2\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^q p_i\right)
\le
\prod_{i=1}^q\mathcal B_2(p_i).
}
\tag{23}
\]

To prove the lower bound, fix a target tuple
\(\mathbf j=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_q)\) and put
\(Q=\bigotimes_i r_{i,\mathbf j_i}\). Its mean is \(\mathbf j\). A closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains its parallel translate whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf j\). Under \(Q\), membership in the latter halfspace is the event
\(Y=\sum_iY_i\ge0\), where the scalar block projections \(Y_i\) are independent and centered. Put \(V_i=\mathbb E_QY_i^2\). At every positive atom of the \(i\)-th entropy law, (21) gives
\(Y_i^2\le\kappa V_i\), and hence

\[
\mathbb E_QY_i^4\le\kappa V_i^2.
\tag{24}
\]

Independence and centering, followed by \(\kappa\ge3\), give

\[
\mathbb E_QY^4
=\sum_i\mathbb E_QY_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}V_iV_h
\le\kappa\left(\sum_iV_i\right)^2
=\kappa(\mathbb E_QY^2)^2.
\tag{25}
\]

If \(\mathbb E_QY^2>0\), Hölder's inequality and then Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
\mathbb E_Q|Y|\ge
\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E_QY^2}{\kappa}},
\qquad
Q(Y\ge0)\ge
\frac{(\mathbb E_QY_+)^2}{\mathbb E_QY^2}
\ge\frac1{4\kappa},
\tag{26}
\]

where \(\mathbb E_QY_+=\mathbb E_Q|Y|/2\) because \(Y\) is centered. If the variance is zero, \(Y=0\) almost surely and the same probability bound holds. This also covers normals that vanish on the affine support.

On the support of \(Q\), define

\[
L=\log\frac{dQ}{d(\bigotimes_i p_i)}.
\tag{27}
\]

By (2), its mean is
\(\sum_i[-\log\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i)]\), and (22) bounds its variance by \(V\). If \(V>0\), Chebyshev gives

\[
Q\!\left(L>\sum_i[-\log\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i)]
                 +\sqrt{8\kappa V}\right)
\le\frac1{8\kappa}.
\tag{28}
\]

For \(V=0\), use threshold zero and the exceptional event is empty. Intersecting the complementary event with (26) leaves \(Q\)-mass at least \(1/(8\kappa)\). Changing measure only on this intersection, which is contained in the original halfspace, yields

\[
\tau_{\otimes_i p_i}(\mathbf j)
\ge\frac1{8\kappa}e^{-\sqrt{8\kappa V}}
       \prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\tag{29}
\]

Squaring (29) and summing with the product target probabilities proves the lower bound in (23). For the upper bound, the exponential infimum factorizes exactly:

\[
\mathsf C_{\otimes_i p_i}(\mathbf j)
=\prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\tag{30}
\]

Indeed, every product of exponential sums is at least the product of their positive infima, while choosing each block vector arbitrarily close to its own infimum gives the reverse inequality. Equations (2) and (30), followed by summation, prove the upper bound in (23).

For a fixed \(p\), only finitely many entropy minimizers occur. Set
\(\kappa_p=\max\{3,r_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)^{-1}:
\mathbf j,\mathbf x\in A,\ r_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)>0\}<\infty\), and

\[
\sigma_p^2:=\max_{\mathbf j\in A}
\operatorname{Var}_{r_{\mathbf j}}
 \left[\log\frac{r_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)}{p(\mathbf X)}\right]<\infty.
\tag{31}
\]

Using \(V=q\sigma_p^2\) in (23) gives

\[
\frac{e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa_p\sigma_p^2q}}}{(8\kappa_p)^2}
\mathcal B_2(p)^q
\le\mathcal M_2(p^{\otimes q})
\le\mathcal B_2(p)^q.
\tag{32}
\]

Taking \(q\)-th roots proves (3).

We now prove (5)--(7). Work in intrinsic coordinates, so \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\) is full-dimensional, and let \(K=2^r\). For \(\mathbf j\in A\), define the oriented reflected Hellinger mass

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}
:=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in A\\2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\in A}}
       \sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}}.
\tag{33}
\]

For every \(\mathbf u\), pair the terms indexed by
\(\mathbf x\) and \(2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\) in the exponential sum. Arithmetic--geometric mean gives

\[
p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}
+p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}e^{-\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}
\ge2\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}}.
\tag{34}
\]

The diagonal term is \(p_{\mathbf j}\), and unpaired terms are nonnegative, so
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\ge\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}\).

Let \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\) be the \(p\)-mass of parity class
\(\mathbf c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r\). Completeness places the integral midpoint of every same-parity pair back in \(A\). Log-affinity gives

\[
p_{\mathbf j}\sqrt{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}
=p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}.
\tag{35}
\]

The same-parity midpoint bijection therefore gives

\[
S:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in A}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}
=\sum_{\mathbf c}\pi_{\mathbf c}^2
\ge K^{-1}.
\tag{36}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mathcal B_2(p)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2
\ge S^2\ge K^{-2}=4^{-r},
\tag{37}
\]

which proves (5).

Suppose equality holds in (37). Then all \(K\) parity masses equal \(K^{-1}\), equality holds in the weighted Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, and every nonnegative difference
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2-\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}^2\) vanishes. Hence

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=K^{-1}
\qquad(\mathbf j\in A).
\tag{38}
\]

At a vertex \(\mathbf v\) of \(\operatorname{conv}A\), no distinct reflected endpoints about \(\mathbf v\) lie in \(A\), so
\(\mathsf H_{\mathbf v}=p_{\mathbf v}=K^{-1}\). Because \(\log p_{\mathbf x}\) is affine on \(A\), every \(\mathbf j\in A\), as a convex combination of vertices, also has \(p_{\mathbf j}=K^{-1}\). Thus \(|A|=K\). If some \(\mathbf j\in A\) were not a vertex, it would be the mean of a non-Dirac law \(\nu\) on \(A\). Uniformity would give

\[
D(\nu\|p)=\log K-H(\nu)<\log K,
\tag{39}
\]

so (2) would give \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)>K^{-1}\), contrary to (38). Hence every support point is a vertex. Conversely, if \(p\) is uniform on exactly \(K\) vertices, the only mean-\(\mathbf j\) law at each support point is the Dirac law, so
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=p_{\mathbf j}=K^{-1}\), and (6) follows.

For the quantitative strictness, assume \(|A|>K\) and
\(p_{\mathbf x}\ge\eta\). Let \(R_0\subseteq A\) be the set of support points whose parity class contains at least two support points, and choose a vertex \(\mathbf j\) of \(\operatorname{conv}R_0\). The set \(R_0\) is nonempty. There are no distinct reflected endpoints about \(\mathbf j\): such endpoints would both lie in \(R_0\) and would express \(\mathbf j\) as their nontrivial midpoint, contradicting extremality. Hence
\(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=p_{\mathbf j}\). If \(\mathbf c\) is its parity class, then
\(\pi_{\mathbf c}\ge p_{\mathbf j}+\eta\).

If \(\pi_{\mathbf c}\ge K^{-1}+\eta/2\), then

\[
S-K^{-1}
=\sum_{\mathbf d}(\pi_{\mathbf d}-K^{-1})^2
\ge\eta^2/4.
\tag{40}
\]

Since \(\eta\le|A|^{-1}<K^{-1}\), (37) improves to

\[
\mathcal B_2(p)\ge S^2
\ge K^{-2}+\frac{\eta^2}{2K}
\ge K^{-2}+\frac{\eta^3}{4}.
\tag{41}
\]

Otherwise,

\[
\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}=p_{\mathbf j}
\le\pi_{\mathbf c}-\eta
<K^{-1}-\eta/2
\le S-\eta/2.
\tag{42}
\]

Using \(S=\sum p_{\mathbf x}\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}\),

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}^2
=S^2+\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}
                 (\mathsf H_{\mathbf x}-S)^2
\ge K^{-2}+p_{\mathbf j}(\eta/2)^2
\ge K^{-2}+\eta^3/4.
\tag{43}
\]

Equations (41) and (43) prove (7).

In the equality case of (6), each support point is a vertex and has mass \(K^{-1}\), so its actual depth equals its atom mass. Every point of the product support is again a vertex, giving
\(\mathcal M_2(p^{\otimes q})=K^{-2q}=4^{-rq}\). Outside that case, (6) gives
\(4^r\mathcal B_2(p)>1\); applying (3), or directly (32), proves (8).

It remains to prove the fixed-family estimate and its constants. We first establish the face-conditioning bound encoded in (11)--(12). Fix \(A\in\mathscr A\), a tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell\), and the resulting law \(p\). Put
\(m=\max_{\mathbf x\in A}\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x\),
\(L=\log(4M_A)\), and

\[
B=\{\mathbf x\in A:
       \boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x\ge m-L\}.
\tag{44}
\]

The partition function is at least \(e^m\), while the total numerator outside \(B\) is at most \(M_Ae^{m-L}\). Therefore \(p(B)\ge3/4\). Let
\(F=F_A(B)\). The arithmetic mean \(\mathbf b_B\) lies in \(\operatorname{relint}F\): if a supporting functional is tight at the average, it is tight at every point of \(B\), and the resulting face contains \(B\); minimality then gives the assertion. This justifies the strictly positive vertex representation (11).

Every vertex \(\mathbf v\) of \(F\) belongs to \(A\), and

\[
\sum_{\mathbf v}\lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}
  \bigl(m-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v\bigr)
=m-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf b_B\le L.
\tag{45}
\]

All summands are nonnegative. Hence every vertex of \(F\), and then every point of \(A\cap F\), has tilt score at least
\(m-L/\lambda_A\). With \(q=p(F)\) and
\(\pi=p(\,\cdot\mid F)\), this proves

\[
q\ge\frac34,
\qquad
\pi_{\mathbf x}\ge
\frac{e^{-L/\lambda_A}}{M_A}\ge\eta
\quad(\mathbf x\in A\cap F).
\tag{46}
\]

The conditional law is log-affine on the complete face support
\(A\cap F\): completeness of \(A\) gives \(A\cap F=F\cap\mathbb Z^r\) in intrinsic coordinates.

For such a log-affine law \(\pi\) on a face support \(E\), the entropy minimizer at \(\mathbf j\) is \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) from (13), independent of the tilt. Indeed, for every mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\),

\[
D(\nu\|\pi)=-H(\nu)-\log\pi_{\mathbf j}.
\tag{47}
\]

Thus minimizing relative entropy is exactly maximizing entropy. On the positive support of \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\), (13) and (46) give

\[
\log\rho
\le\log\frac{r_{E,\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)}{\pi_{\mathbf x}}
\le-\log\eta.
\tag{48}
\]

The interval in (48) has length \(D_0\), so every likelihood variance is at most \(D_0^2\). Conditions (21)--(22) therefore hold for a product of \(b\) such conditional laws with the constants in (13) and \(V=bD_0^2\). Equation (23) becomes

\[
\mathcal M_2\!\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^b\pi_i\right)
\ge\alpha^2e^{-2\beta\sqrt b}
       \prod_{i=1}^b\mathcal B_2(\pi_i).
\tag{49}
\]

We apply this only to the factors with
\(M_{A_i}>2^{r_i}\), where \(r_i=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_i)\). Select \(F_i,q_i,\pi_i\) using (44)--(46), and let
\(s_i=\dim F_i\). If \(F_i=\operatorname{conv}A_i\), then \(q_i=1\), every atom is at least \(\eta\), and (7) gives

\[
4^{r_i}q_i^3\mathcal B_2(\pi_i)
\ge1+\frac{4^{r_i}\eta^3}{4}\ge g.
\tag{50}
\]

If \(F_i\) is proper, then \(s_i\le r_i-1\), and (5), (46) give

\[
4^{r_i}q_i^3\mathcal B_2(\pi_i)
\ge4^{r_i-s_i}\left(\frac34\right)^3
\ge\frac{27}{16}\ge g.
\tag{51}
\]

Put \(P_{\mathrm{big}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^b p_i\),
\(\Pi=\bigotimes_{i=1}^b\pi_i\), and
\(q_{\mathrm{tot}}=\prod_iq_i\). At every target in the product of the selected faces, its \(P_{\mathrm{big}}\)-probability is \(q_{\mathrm{tot}}\) times its \(\Pi\)-probability. Every containing closed halfspace has \(P_{\mathrm{big}}\)-mass at least \(q_{\mathrm{tot}}\) times its \(\Pi\)-mass on that face product. Therefore

\[
\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{big}})
\ge q_{\mathrm{tot}}^3\mathcal M_2(\Pi).
\tag{52}
\]

The third power has exactly one factor from the target probability and two from the squared depth; no face-complement mass is discarded inside a depth estimate. Combining (49)--(52) gives

\[
4^{\sum_{i=1}^b r_i}\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{big}})
\ge\alpha^2e^{-2\beta\sqrt b}g^b.
\tag{53}
\]

The omitted factors satisfy \(M_{A_i}\le2^{r_i}\). For any probability law on \(M\) atoms,

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf x)\ge p_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
\mathcal M_2(p)\ge\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}^3\ge M^{-2}.
\tag{54}
\]

Actual depth is supermultiplicative: for laws \(p,q\) and a product target, a halfspace through that target contains the product of the two events on which the separate block projections are nonnegative. Each event has probability at least the corresponding depth, with probability one if its block normal is zero. Hence

\[
\tau_{p\otimes q}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
\ge\tau_p(\mathbf x)\tau_q(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\mathcal M_2(p\otimes q)\ge\mathcal M_2(p)\mathcal M_2(q).
\tag{55}
\]

By (54), every omitted factor contributes at least \(4^{-r_i}\) and hence costs no normalized loss. Restoring all of them in (53) proves (16). If \(b=0\), (54)--(55) directly give
\(\mathcal M_2(P)\ge4^{-R}\), which also proves (16) because then \(g^b=1\) and \(\alpha\le1\).

For \(b\ge1\), completing the square gives

\[
b\log g-2\beta\sqrt b
=\log g\left(\sqrt b-\frac{\beta}{\log g}\right)^2
-\frac{\beta^2}{\log g}
\ge-\frac{\beta^2}{\log g}.
\tag{56}
\]

Equations (15), (16), and (56) prove (10). The entire argument used intrinsic ranks; since each intrinsic rank is at most its displayed ambient integer dimension, the final ambient-normalized H2 consequence follows as stated. All constants in (12)--(15) are selected from finitely many subsets, faces, and mean-constrained entropy laws of the fixed family. They are independent of the factor count and every tilt, but the proof supplies no positive lower bound for them as the support geometry varies. Thus the theorem does not establish unrestricted H2.
