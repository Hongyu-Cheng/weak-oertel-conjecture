## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write

\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\tag{1}
\]

For \(0<t\leq a_z\), put

\[
 \mathsf M_t(C_z):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_{C_z}x f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \int_{C_z}f=t
 \right\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z).
\tag{2}
\]

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard system of accepted facts 6bfe50fed9a5540b, cb96640c07ffd5fa, and e55f3e45bc9f1c34. Thus the complete half-strength canonical product reservoir has weights

\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \quad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi,\ \xi\text{ good}),
\tag{3}
\]

where

\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z,
 \qquad
 \max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\leq\frac{A_\xi}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]

Put

\[
 Q:=\sum_fr_f>0,
 \qquad
 R:=R_C(r).
\tag{5}
\]

For every reservoir edge \(f=\{z,z'\}\), write

\[
 m_f:=\frac{z+z'}2,
 \qquad
 L_f:=\frac12(\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'}).
\tag{6}
\]

Retain from accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b the original reservoir primal optimizer \(s_0\), the missed family and scaled reservoir

\[
 \mathscr M_*:=\{f:s_0(m_f)\notin L_f\},
 \qquad
 \alpha_f:=\chi_{m_f}r_f\mathbf1_{\mathscr M_*}(f),
\tag{7}
\]

the target quantities

\[
 t_m:=\sum_{f:m_f=m}\alpha_f,
 \qquad
 \lambda_m\in[0,t_m],
\tag{8}
\]

and the canonical subcapacity

\[
 b_f:=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{\lambda_{m_f}}{t_{m_f}}\alpha_f,&t_{m_f}>0,\\[2mm]
 0,&t_{m_f}=0.
 \end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]

Retain the single global gauge-load-minimal dual optimizer \(v^b\) for \(b\), and choose one paired global primal optimizer \(s_b\). Extend its self-saturated load by zero to all reservoir edges:

\[
 q_f:=q_f^{\mathrm{sat}}:=h_{D_f}(v_f^b),
 \qquad
 0\leq q_f\leq b_f\leq r_f.
\tag{10}
\]

Then

\[
 R_C(b)\leq R,
\tag{11}
\]

and accepted fact cb96640c07ffd5fa gives the global budgets

\[
 \sum_{f\ni z}q_f\leq\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 \sum_{f:m_f=m}\frac{q_f}{\vartheta_f}\leq1.
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(E\) be any family of positive \(q\)-load edges \(e=\{u_e,v_e\}\) such that

\[
 v_e-u_e\in(8\mathbb Z)^n.
\tag{13}
\]

Define the one-step dyadic child

\[
 x_e:=\frac{3u_e+v_e}{4},
 \qquad
 y_e:=\frac{u_e+3v_e}{4},
 \qquad
 T(e):=\{x_e,y_e\}.
\tag{14}
\]

Then \(T\) is injective, every child is an actual positive reservoir edge in the same good parity class, and

\[
 m_{T(e)}=m_e,
 \qquad
 r_{T(e)}\geq r_e.
\tag{15}
\]

The same global self-saturated load satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}
 \geq\sum_{e\in E}q_e-2R.}
\tag{16}
\]

More precisely, there are two nonnegative losses \(H_E,D_E\), each bounded by the same accepted capacity \(R\), such that

\[
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}
 \geq\sum_{e\in E}q_e-H_E-D_E,
 \qquad
 H_E\leq R,
 \qquad
 D_E\leq R.
\tag{17}
\]

The loss \(H_E\) is realized on child edges hit by the fixed original global map \(s_0\), while \(D_E\) is realized on child edges hit by the fixed global map \(s_b\). In particular, if either loss is at least \(Q/100\), then the already legal terminal \(R\geq Q/100\) holds.

Put

\[
 \mu:=\sum_{e\in E}q_e.
\tag{18}
\]

If

\[
 R<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 \mu>\frac{17Q}{800},
\tag{19}
\]

then the positive-load child family

\[
 F:=\{T(e):e\in E,\ q_{T(e)}>0\}
\tag{20}
\]

is edge-no-reuse and obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{f\in F}q_f>\frac{\mu}{17},
 \qquad
 \sum_{f\in F}q_f>\frac Q{800}.}
\tag{21}
\]

It is a restriction of the same global optimizer and hence satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{f\in F\\f\ni z}}q_f\leq\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 \sum_{\substack{f\in F\\m_f=m}}
 \frac{q_f}{\vartheta_f}\leq1.}
\tag{22}
\]

If, in addition, all edges in \(E\) have one common minimum nonzero coordinatewise \(2\)-adic valuation \(s\geq3\), then every edge in \(T(E)\) has valuation \(s-1\). Thus

\[
 T(E)\cap E=\varnothing,
\tag{23}
\]

so (20) is also no-reuse across the current and child levels and has strictly smaller dyadic complexity.

There is an exact comparison of the endpoint bodies. For \(e=\{u,v\}\in E\), define

\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,x}
 :=\frac34\mathcal K_u+\frac14\mathcal K_v,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,y}
 :=\frac14\mathcal K_u+\frac34\mathcal K_v.
\tag{24}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,x}\cup\mathcal K_{x_e}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{x_e}),
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,y}\cup\mathcal K_{y_e}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{y_e}).}
\tag{25}
\]

The contracted pair body is still the parent pair body,

\[
 \frac12\left(
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,x}
 +\widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,y}
 \right)=L_e,
\tag{26}
\]

whereas the actual child pair body is

\[
 L_{T(e)}=\frac12\left(
 \mathcal K_{x_e}+\mathcal K_{y_e}
 \right).
\tag{27}
\]

No inclusion between the two bodies in (26) and (27) is claimed.

Finally, let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be any positive-volume convex body of volume \(a\) and centroid \(c\). For every \(0<t\leq a/2\),

\[
 \boxed{
 c+\frac{t}{a-t}\bigl(\mathsf M_t(K)-c\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{a/2}(K).}
\tag{28}
\]

Applied with \(K=C_{x_e}\) and \(t=2q_e\), equation (28) maps every contracted endpoint point in (25) to an actual child half-metronoid point. Its coefficient and center depend on the child endpoint. The theorem makes no claim that this map preserves exposed KKT contacts, pair contacts, synchronous target barycenters, or affine residual rank.

## Proof

Fix \(e=\{u,v\}\in E\). Since \(v-u\in(8\mathbb Z)^n\), both points in (14) are integral and have the same parity as \(u,v\). Direct inversion gives

\[
 u=\frac{3x_e-y_e}{2},
 \qquad
 v=\frac{3y_e-x_e}{2},
\tag{29}
\]

so \(T\) is injective. Direct averaging gives

\[
 \frac{x_e+y_e}{2}=\frac{u+v}{2},
\tag{30}
\]

which is the target identity in (15).

Brunn-Minkowski gives

\[
 a_{x_e}^{1/d}
 \geq\frac34a_u^{1/d}+\frac14a_v^{1/d}
 \geq a_u^{3/(4d)}a_v^{1/(4d)},
\tag{31}
\]

\[
 a_{y_e}^{1/d}
 \geq\frac14a_u^{1/d}+\frac34a_v^{1/d}
 \geq a_u^{1/(4d)}a_v^{3/(4d)}.
\tag{32}
\]

Multiplication gives

\[
 a_{x_e}a_{y_e}\geq a_ua_v.
\tag{33}
\]

The parent and child lie in the same good parity class and therefore use the same denominator \(4A_\xi\) in (3). Equation (33) proves \(r_{T(e)}\geq r_e>0\), completing (15).

We prove the support-load estimate. Split

\[
 E_0:=\{e\in E:s_0(m_e)\in L_{T(e)}\},
 \qquad
 E_1:=E\setminus E_0,
\tag{34}
\]

and put

\[
 H_E:=\sum_{e\in E_0}q_e.
\tag{35}
\]

Assign comparison weight \(q_e\) to \(T(e)\) for \(e\in E_0\), and zero elsewhere. Injectivity prevents repeated child coordinates, while (10) and (15) give

\[
 q_e\leq r_e\leq r_{T(e)}.
\tag{36}
\]

The one global affine map \(s_0\) hits every actual child pair body in this comparison family. The exact affine-realizability identity of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d therefore gives

\[
 H_E\leq R_C(r)=R.
\tag{37}
\]

Now fix \(e\in E_1\). Positivity of \(q_e\) and (10) imply \(b_e>0\). Equations (7)-(9) then imply

\[
 \alpha_e>0,
 \qquad
 t_{m_e}>0,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{m_e}>0.
\tag{38}
\]

In particular \(e\in\mathscr M_*\). The definition of \(E_1\) says that \(T(e)\in\mathscr M_*\) as well. Since \(m_{T(e)}=m_e\), the multiplier \(\lambda_m/t_m\) in (9) is identical for the parent and child. Equations (7), (9), and (15) give

\[
 \frac{b_{T(e)}}{b_e}
 =\frac{\alpha_{T(e)}}{\alpha_e}
 =\frac{r_{T(e)}}{r_e}\geq1.
\tag{39}
\]

Thus

\[
 b_{T(e)}\geq b_e\geq q_e.
\tag{40}
\]

Define a child subcapacity by

\[
 d_{T(e)}:=(q_e-q_{T(e)})_+
 \quad(e\in E_1),
 \qquad
 d_f:=0
 \quad(f\notin T(E_1)),
\tag{41}
\]

and put

\[
 D_E:=\sum_fd_f.
\tag{42}
\]

Whenever \(d_{T(e)}>0\), equations (40)-(41) give

\[
 q_{T(e)}<q_e\leq b_{T(e)}.
\tag{43}
\]

Apply the complementary-saturation implication of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d to the fixed primal-dual pair \(s_b,v^b\) for capacity \(b\):

\[
 s_b(m_f)\notin L_f
 \Longrightarrow h_{D_f}(v_f^b)=b_f.
\tag{44}
\]

By (10), the right side of (44) is \(q_f=b_f\). The contrapositive of (44), together with (43), yields

\[
 s_b(m_{T(e)})\in L_{T(e)}
 \qquad(d_{T(e)}>0).
\tag{45}
\]

Also \(d_{T(e)}\leq b_{T(e)}\). Hence \(d\) is one subcapacity of \(b\), all of whose positive coordinates are hit by the one fixed global affine map \(s_b\). No coordinate is reoptimized or transported from its parent. The same exact affine-realizability identity gives

\[
 D_E\leq R_C(b)\leq R.
\tag{46}
\]

For \(e\in E_1\), definition (41) gives

\[
 q_{T(e)}\geq q_e-d_{T(e)}.
\tag{47}
\]

Discard the nonnegative child load on \(T(E_0)\), sum (47), and use (35), (42), (37), and (46):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}
 &\geq\sum_{e\in E_1}q_{T(e)}\\
 &\geq\sum_{e\in E_1}q_e-D_E\\
 &=\sum_{e\in E}q_e-H_E-D_E\\
 &\geq\sum_{e\in E}q_e-2R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]

This proves (16)-(17). Equations (37) and (46) also prove the stated \(Q/100\) terminal reading for either individual loss.

Assume (19). Since \(R<Q/100\), equation (48) gives

\[
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}
 >\mu-\frac{2Q}{100}.
\tag{49}
\]

The current-load hypothesis gives

\[
 \frac{2Q}{100}
 =\frac{16Q}{800}
 <\frac{16}{17}\mu.
\tag{50}
\]

Substitution in (49) proves

\[
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}>\frac{\mu}{17}.
\tag{51}
\]

The same equations also give

\[
 \sum_{e\in E}q_{T(e)}
 >\frac{17Q}{800}-\frac{16Q}{800}
 =\frac Q{800}.
\tag{52}
\]

Because \(T\) is injective, deleting its zero-load children does not change either sum. This proves (20)-(21). The family \(F\) is literally a restriction of the one global load \(q\), so restricting the two inequalities in (12) proves (22), including all endpoint and target collisions.

If every parent edge has minimum nonzero coordinatewise valuation \(s\), then

\[
 y_e-x_e=\frac{v_e-u_e}{2}
\tag{53}
\]

has valuation \(s-1\). When \(s\geq3\), both child endpoints still have the same parity, and no child edge can equal a parent edge of valuation \(s\). This proves (23) and the strict complexity decrease.

We prove the endpoint-body comparison. Accepted fact e55f3e45bc9f1c34 gives the positive-root masses

\[
 \theta_{e,x}:=
 \left(
 \frac34(a_u/2)^{1/d}
 +\frac14(a_v/2)^{1/d}
 \right)^d,
\tag{54}
\]

\[
 \theta_{e,y}:=
 \left(
 \frac14(a_u/2)^{1/d}
 +\frac34(a_v/2)^{1/d}
 \right)^d,
\tag{55}
\]

and the inclusions

\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,x}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\theta_{e,x}}(C_{x_e})
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{x_e}),
\tag{56}
\]

\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,y}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\theta_{e,y}}(C_{y_e})
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{y_e}).
\tag{57}
\]

For the actual child half-metronoids, goodness of the parent class gives

\[
 A_\xi\geq4\max\{a_u,a_v\},
\tag{58}
\]

and hence

\[
 r_e=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \leq\frac1{16}\min\{a_u,a_v\}.
\tag{59}
\]

Equations (31)-(32) also give

\[
 a_{x_e}\geq\min\{a_u,a_v\},
 \qquad
 a_{y_e}\geq\min\{a_u,a_v\}.
\tag{60}
\]

Therefore

\[
 2q_e\leq2r_e
 \leq\frac18\min\{a_u,a_v\}
 \leq\frac18\min\{a_{x_e},a_{y_e}\}.
\tag{61}
\]

In particular \(2q_e\leq a_{x_e}/2\) and \(2q_e\leq a_{y_e}/2\). If a point of \(\mathcal K_{x_e}\) is represented by a density of mass \(a_{x_e}/2\), multiply that density by \(4q_e/a_{x_e}\leq1\). This preserves its barycenter and gives a density of mass \(2q_e\). Thus

\[
 \mathcal K_{x_e}\subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{x_e}).
\tag{62}
\]

The same argument gives

\[
 \mathcal K_{y_e}\subseteq\mathsf M_{2q_e}(C_{y_e}).
\tag{63}
\]

Equations (56)-(57) and (62)-(63) prove (25). Minkowski linearity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac12\left(
 \widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,x}
 +\widetilde{\mathcal K}_{e,y}
 \right)
 &=\frac12\mathcal K_u+\frac12\mathcal K_v\\
 &=L_e,
\end{aligned}
\tag{64}
\]

which proves (26). Equation (27) is the definition of the actual child pair body.

It remains to prove (28). Take \(p\in\mathsf M_t(K)\) and choose a representing density \(f\), so

\[
 0\leq f\leq\mathbf1_K,
 \qquad
 \int_Kf=t,
 \qquad
 \int_Kxf(x)\,dx=tp.
\tag{65}
\]

Set

\[
 \lambda:=\frac{a/2-t}{a-t},
 \qquad
 g:=f+\lambda(\mathbf1_K-f).
\tag{66}
\]

Because \(0<t\leq a/2\), one has \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), and hence

\[
 0\leq g\leq\mathbf1_K.
\tag{67}
\]

Its mass is

\[
 \int_Kg
 =t+\lambda(a-t)
 =\frac a2.
\tag{68}
\]

Using \(\int_Kx\,dx=ac\), its normalized first moment is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \frac2a\int_Kxg(x)\,dx
 &=\frac2a\left(
 tp+\lambda(ac-tp)
 \right)\\
 &=c+\frac{t}{a-t}(p-c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{69}
\]

Thus the point in (69) belongs to \(\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)\). Since \(p\) was arbitrary, this proves (28).

Equation (28) is a set-membership completion only. At a child endpoint its coefficient is

\[
 \frac{2q_e}{a_{x_e}-2q_e}
\tag{70}
\]

and its center is the endpoint centroid. These quantities vary with \(e\) and with the endpoint. The argument does not preserve an exposed face, the pair contact in (26), a collision-pooled target barycenter, or the affine span of the current residual vectors. Therefore no packed residual-rank conclusion follows from (25) or (28).

The remaining interface can be stated exactly. Fix an affine map and a residual subspace \(V\) attaining the current packed residual rank. At each physical target \(m\), pool the actual child pair bodies \(L_f\) with the restricted global weights \(q_f\) from (20). To continue the descent without rank increase, the pooled body must meet the prescribed affine residual flat \(a(m)+V\), simultaneously at all retained targets and with one shared density ledger. If an intersection fails, quotient separation produces targetwise functionals, but no accepted fact yet assembles those functionals into one of \(G\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\), or \(D\) with mass at least \(Q/100\). Proving that pooled intersection-or-terminal separator is the remaining bridge. It must use the actual child bodies and the same global optimizer; a new local optimizer would not prove the required descent.
