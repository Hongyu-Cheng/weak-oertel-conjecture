## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, let $q\ge1$, and let $w_z>0$, $r_z=w_z^{1/q}$ for $z\in P$. Put $\gamma=(q/(q+1))^q$ and $c=1-\gamma$. For each $z$, form the polytope of representations $\lambda\ge0$ with $\sum_p\lambda_p=1$ and $\sum_p\lambda_pp=z$. Let $D$ have a row for $l_z-\sum_p\lambda_p l_p$ for every vertex of each such polytope, dropping any zero rows. The sample vectors of finite convex functions $\ell$ on $\operatorname{conv}P$ with $\ell(z)\ge r_z$ are exactly $\mathcal L=\{l:l\ge r,\ Dl\le0\}$. Every vector in $\mathcal L$ has a positive convex piecewise-affine interpolant on the relative domain. For $A\subseteq P$, put $V_A=\min_{l\in\mathcal L}\sum_{z\in A}l_z^q$, with $V_{\varnothing}=0$. Every minimum is attained, and so is the maximum
$$
J_*=\max_\ell\sum_{z\in P}[w_z-c\ell(z)^q]_+
=\max_{A\subseteq P}\left(\sum_{z\in A}w_z-cV_A\right).
$$

For the same $V_A$ and $D$, the following dual maxima are attained. For $q=1$,
$$
V_A=\max\{\eta\cdot r:\ \eta-D^\mathsf{T}\lambda=\mathbf1_A,\ \eta,\lambda\ge0\}.
$$
For $q>1$, put $p=q/(q-1)$ and $\kappa=(q-1)q^{-p}$. Then
$$
V_A=\max\left\{\eta\cdot r-\kappa\sum_{z\in A}a_z^p:
\eta-D^\mathsf{T}\lambda=a,\ \eta,\lambda,a\ge0,\ a_z=0\ (z\notin A)\right\}.
$$
No strict feasibility assumption is needed.

## Proof

Jensen's inequality gives the necessary constraints. For sufficiency set
$$
\ell_l(x)=\min\left\{\sum_p\lambda_p l_p:\ \lambda\ge0,\ \sum_p\lambda_p=1,\ \sum_p\lambda_pp=x\right\}.
$$
The minimum is attained on a nonempty compact polytope. The epigraph is the convex hull of the finitely many upward rays at $(p,l_p)$, a polyhedron, so this defines a finite convex piecewise-affine function on the relative domain. At a sample $z$ the vertex inequalities imply the same inequality for every representation, giving $\ell_l(z)\ge l_z$. The singleton representation gives equality. Its values are at least $\min_p r_p>0$ everywhere.

A constant $l\ge\max r_z$ is feasible. The projection of $\mathcal L$ to the $A$ coordinates is a closed polyhedron. In it all coordinates have positive lower bounds and a sublevel of $\sum_A l_z^q$ is bounded, so the minimum is attained by a projected vector with a feasible lift. The empty-subset objective is constant. The sum of positive parts is the maximum over subsets of the corresponding signed sum. Maximizing first over $l$ for each of these finitely many subsets gives the stated formula and attainment.

At a minimizer $l^*$ for $V_A$, the objective is differentiable on a neighborhood because every coordinate of $l^*$ is positive. The negative gradient is in the cone of active constraint normals. Otherwise a separating direction has nonpositive scalar products with these normals and strictly negative directional derivative for the objective. A sufficiently short positive step stays feasible for every active and inactive row, contradicting minimality. Thus there are multipliers $\eta,\lambda\ge0$ with
$$
\eta-D^\mathsf{T}\lambda=q\mathbf1_A(l^*)^{q-1},\qquad
\eta_z(l_z^*-r_z)=0,\qquad \lambda_j(Dl^*)_j=0.
$$
For $q=1$ the right side of the first equation is $\mathbf1_A$, and dotting with $l^*$ gives $\eta\cdot r=V_A$. For $q>1$, set $a$ to the right side. The same scalar product gives $\eta\cdot r=qV_A$ and the formula for $\kappa$ gives $\kappa\sum_A a_z^p=(q-1)V_A$. These multipliers attain the proposed objective in both cases, including when $A$ is empty.

For any feasible $l$ and dual multipliers, $\eta\cdot r\le\eta\cdot l=(a+D^\mathsf{T}\lambda)\cdot l\le a\cdot l$, taking $a=\mathbf1_A$ for $q=1$. For $q>1$ use $a_z l_z\le l_z^q+\kappa a_z^p$ on $A$, obtained by maximizing $a_z t-t^q$ over $t\ge0$. This gives weak duality and completes both formulas.
