## Statement

Fix $n,d,N\ge1$ and a nonempty finite lattice-convex $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ with $z_0\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}P\cap P$. Let a compatible family attain minimum normalized maximal closed-halfspace depth $\beta$ in the shared-plane class with $|K_{z_0}|_d=1$, and assume all its fibers are positive. Denote its total summed $d$-volume by $M$. Select whole critical groups from fiberwise maximizing marks and assign each $p\in P$ to one selected group covering the whole plane over $p$. At most $2^n$ deepest-origin groups suffice on the deepest bases. Assign each nondeep base to its own additional slack group. No group of mass below $\beta M$ covers a deepest base. Each group $i$ has positive fixed circuit weights $\rho_{ij}$, unit initial continuous normals $u_{ij}$, and projected margins $C_{ip}=\sum_j\rho_{ij}(v_{ij}\cdot p-b_{ij})\ge0$ for its assigned bases.

There are nonnegative cap multipliers $\lambda_{ij}$, zero for slack caps, nonnegative multipliers $\eta_{ip}$ of the assigned coverage inequalities, zero for strict inequalities, and one objective allocation $\omega$ and cap allocations $(\alpha_{ij},\nu_{ijz})$ of the owner-derivative lemma. For every shared body plane $f$ the measure $dW_f=(\omega_f-\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}\alpha_{ijf})\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$ summed over its facet traces at their integer bases satisfies $\int dW_f=\int z\,dW_f=\int x\,dW_f=0$. Write $S_{ij},Z_{ij},X_{ij}$ for the mass, base first moment, and continuous first moment of $\sum_z\delta_z\otimes\nu_{ijz}$. For some $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and $t_i=\sum_p\eta_{ip}$ the same choices satisfy
$$
\lambda_{ij} S_{ij}=\rho_{ij}t_i,\qquad
\lambda_{ij} Z_{ij}=\rho_{ij}\sum_p\eta_{ip}p,\qquad
\lambda_{ij} X_{ij}=\rho_{ij}q_i,\qquad
\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}=\frac1\beta.
$$
For each stressed group $t_i>0$, all its caps carry stress and have a common effective-section centroid on their cap planes. Its base is $\sum_p\eta_{ip}p/t_i$, and its continuous coordinate differs from the $\eta$-weighted average of deepest marks at these bases by a vector in $\bigcap_j\ker u_{ij}$. In rank $d$ the two points coincide and their ambient depth, normalized by $M$, is $\beta$.

## Proof

For existence and initial selection use the first part of 0354aa9a30918591 for the positive compatible fibers. In this lattice-convex case $h(P)\le2^n$: include $\operatorname{conv}P$ when applying Doignon's integer Helly theorem to the open complements of the projected covering halfspaces. On finite $P$ those complements may be shifted to closed halfspaces having the same traces. The resulting subfamily uses at most $2^n$ group complements. An original group has one common cap mass, the restricted maximum at its origin, and a cap passes through its interior origin mark. No slack group covers a deepest base by the same fact. For deepest bases keep the deepest-origin groups of the compressed cover and assign each deepest $p$ to one for which $C_{ip}\ge0$. Assign each nondeep $p$ to its own original slack group, which has $C_{ip}=0$ there. Only the at most $2^n$ deepest-origin groups can carry stress.

Allow all body coefficient directions and cap coefficient changes with $\sum_j\rho_{ij}u_{ij}=0$ and $C_{ip}\ge0$. Keep the positive weights fixed and keep continuous normals unnormalized after the initial point. Their original nonzero columns and independent minors persist locally. The assigned weighted cap margin at $(p,x)$ is $C_{ip}$, so an admissible change still covers every assigned whole plane. By shared-plane-closure, small free body changes are legitimate, up to common dilation restoring central unit volume. Any change satisfying all old cap budgets $\beta M$ with increased total volume $M'$ would give normalized maximum depth at most $\beta M/M'<\beta$ even after that normalization. Thus no such small competitor exists. The value $\beta$ is positive because the center of any interior fiber ball has positive depth.

Let $D$ be the cone of directions satisfying the active coverage inequalities to first order and the circuit equations. These constraints are linear, so every such direction is realized by the small coefficient line. Let $G_0,G_{ij}$ be the compact convex sets of the owner-derivative lemma, restricted to the relevant coefficient space. With caps fixed, lower all body thresholds $b_f$ at unit speed, an erosion direction $e\in D$. It has $r_f=-1$ for every owner. Every active cap has positive mass and therefore some fiber whose cap linear functional has maximum strictly larger than its threshold. A facet incident to a maximizing vertex has a relatively open patch strictly inside the cap. That patch gives a strictly negative contribution for every cap allocation, and every other body contribution is nonpositive. Consequently $g(e)\le-\epsilon$ for all active cap covectors, for some $\epsilon>0$.

The sum of the convex conic hull of the active $G_{ij}$ and the polar $D^\circ=\{\ell:\ell(\delta)\le0\ \forall\delta\in D\}$ is closed. For a convergent sequence of sums, pairing with $e$ bounds the total cap coefficient, because the polar terms are nonpositive there. Compactness of each cap set bounds and gives convergence of a subsequence of the cap parts. The remaining polar parts converge in the closed polar. If this sum cone were disjoint from compact convex $G_0$, strict separation would give $\delta\in D$ with $\min_{g\in G_0}g(\delta)>0$ and every active cap covector nonpositive at $\delta$. Add a small positive multiple of $e$. The objective minimum remains positive and all active cap derivative bounds become strictly negative. By the one-sided derivative formulas, a small positive coefficient step increases total volume and meets every active budget strictly. Slack budgets persist by continuity, contradicting the volume comparison.

Thus one $g_0\in G_0$ lies in the cone. By polyhedral polarity write its identity as $\sum_{ij}\lambda_{ij}g_{ij}-\sum_{ip}\eta_{ip}DC_{ip}-\sum_i q_i\cdot D(\sum_j\rho_{ij}u_{ij})$, setting inactive coefficients to zero. Free body threshold, base-normal, and continuous-normal variations give the three moments of $W_f$. The coefficients of each cap threshold, base normal, and continuous normal give respectively the stated three section equations.

Evaluate this linear identity on joint continuous homothety. Multiply the body $(a_f,b_f)$ and cap $(v_{ij},b_{ij})$ by the common factor while leaving their continuous normals fixed. On each original facet all owner velocities agree at $n_F\cdot x$. On a coincident facet the velocities satisfy $r=\tau_j$ in the body-inside case or $r+\tau_j=0$ in the body-outside case. Hence every allowed allocation evaluates to the actual dilation derivative, which is $dM$ for the objective and $d\mu(H_{ij})$ for the cap. The active homogeneous coverage rows and the circuit rows vanish on this direction. All nonzero cap multipliers have $\mu(H_{ij})=\beta M$, proving the normalization.

Every selected cap has a proper positive-area section at its own interior maximizing mark. So $S_{ij}>0$ for every allocation. The first section equation implies that $t_i>0$ exactly for a stressed group and forces all its cap weights positive. Dividing the section equations gives a common centroid with base $\sum_p\eta_{ip}p/t_i$ and continuous coordinate $q_i/t_i$. Its supporting effective sections lie on each respective cap plane, so the common point lies on all group cap planes. At an assigned deepest mark, every critical cap margin is nonpositive: strict inclusion in a cap of mass $\beta M$ would let its boundary shift to the mark, losing a positive strip in the mark's own interior fiber. The assigned weighted margin is nonnegative, so every margin is zero. Every base assigned to a stressed group is deepest. The $\eta$-weighted average of deepest marks lies on all their planes and has the same base as the common centroid. Subtracting the plane equations proves the kernel identity. In rank $d$ the kernel is zero. The ambient depth region at level $\beta M$ is convex because for any closed halfspace containing a convex combination, at least one of its marks belongs to that halfspace. The average therefore has depth at least $\beta M$, and equality follows from contact with a critical cap.
