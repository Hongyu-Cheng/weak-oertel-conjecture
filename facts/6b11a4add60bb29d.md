## Statement

Let
\[
\Delta_{30}
=
\left\{u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{30})\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{31}:
\sum_{i=0}^{30}u_i=1\right\}.
\]
For \(q\in\Delta_{30}\), write
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_{30}}(q)
:=
\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30})}
\inf_{\substack{H\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
\text{of the affine hull},\ q\in H}}
\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30}\cap H).
\]
For every integer \(d\geq2\), define
\[
F_{d,2}(s)
:=
\sum_{j=2}^{d}\binom dj s^j(1-s)^{d-j}
\qquad(0\leq s\leq1).
\]
Label the twenty-nine regular indices by
\[
R:=\{0,\ldots,28\}
\]
and the two exceptional indices by
\[
E:=\{29,30\}.
\]
For \(0<b\leq1/31\), put
\[
a:=\frac{1-2b}{29},
\qquad
q_i=a\quad(i\in R),
\qquad
q_{29}=q_{30}=b.
\]

For every integer \(k\) with \(0\leq k\leq28\), choose a set
\(B_k\subseteq R\) of cardinality \(k\), put
\[
r_k:=\frac{2b}{1-ka},
\]
and define the proper closed halfspace of the affine hull
\[
H_k:=
\left\{
u:
u_{29}+u_{30}
+r_k\sum_{i\in B_k}u_i
\leq r_k
\right\}.
\]
Then \(q\in\partial H_k\), \(0<r_k<1\), and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30}\cap H_k)}
{\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30})}
=
F_{30-k,2}(r_k).
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently the true normalized depth satisfies the exact finite upper
bound
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_{30}}(q)
\leq
\min_{0\leq k\leq28}
F_{30-k,2}\left(\frac{2b}{1-ka}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
The term \(k=0\) in (2) is \(F_{30,2}(2b)\).

Now specialize to
\[
b=\frac1{50},
\qquad
a=\frac{24}{725}.
\tag{3}
\]
The fully oblique proper closed halfspace
\[
H_{\mathrm{obl}}
:=
\left\{
u:
\frac{145}{144}\sum_{i=1}^{28}u_i
+\frac53(u_{29}+u_{30})
\leq1
\right\}
\tag{4}
\]
contains \(q\) on its boundary, its cutting hyperplane contains no simplex
vertex, and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30}\cap H_{\mathrm{obl}})}
{\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30})}
=
\left(\frac{144}{145}\right)^{28}
\left(\frac35\right)^2
<\frac3{10}.
\tag{5}
\]
At the same parameter, the two proposed values satisfy
\[
(1-a)^{30}
=
\left(\frac{701}{725}\right)^{30}
>\frac13
>\frac3{10},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
F_{30,2}(2b)
=
F_{30,2}\left(\frac1{25}\right)
>\frac{41}{125}
>\frac3{10}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\Delta_{30}}(q)
\leq
\left(\frac{144}{145}\right)^{28}
\left(\frac35\right)^2
<
\min\left\{
(1-a)^{30},
F_{30,2}(2b)
\right\},
\]
which disproves the registered formula.

For a distinct multi-vertex member of (1), take \(k=27\),
\(B_{27}=\{2,\ldots,28\}\), and the same parameter (3).  Then
\[
r_{27}=\frac{29}{77},
\]
and the halfspace may be written with integer coefficients as
\[
H_{27}
=
\left\{
u:
29\sum_{i=2}^{28}u_i
+77(u_{29}+u_{30})
\leq29
\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
It has two regular vertices strictly inside the cap, twenty-seven regular
vertices on the cutting hyperplane, and the two exceptional vertices
outside.  Its exact normalized volume is
\[
F_{3,2}\left(\frac{29}{77}\right)
=
\frac{145493}{456533},
\tag{9}
\]
which is also strictly below both quantities in (6)-(7).

All volumes in (1), (2), (5), and (9) are normalized relative volumes.
For the Euclidean Hausdorff measure on the affine hull,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30})=\frac{\sqrt{31}}{30!}.
\]
Thus, for example, the absolute volume in (5) is
\[
\frac{\sqrt{31}}{30!}
\left(\frac{144}{145}\right)^{28}
\left(\frac35\right)^2.
\]

## Proof

First note that \(a>0\) and
\[
29a+2b=1,
\]
so \(q\in\operatorname{relint}\Delta_{30}\).  Fix
\(0\leq k\leq28\).  Since
\[
1-ka=(29-k)a+2b>2b>0,
\]
one has \(0<r_k<1\).  Moreover,
\[
u_{29}(q)+u_{30}(q)
+r_k\sum_{i\in B_k}u_i(q)
=2b+r_kka
=r_k,
\]
where the last equality is the definition of \(r_k\).  Hence
\(q\in\partial H_k\).  The affine functional defining \(H_k\) has value
\(0\) at every regular vertex outside \(B_k\), value \(r_k\) at every
regular vertex in \(B_k\), and value \(1\) at each exceptional vertex.
Because \(k\leq28\), at least one zero-valued regular vertex exists.
The functional is therefore nonconstant, and \(H_k\) is a proper closed
halfspace of the affine hull.  This also checks the endpoint
\(b=1/31\), where all displayed inequalities remain strict because
\(r_k=2/(31-k)<1\).

We next compute its full thirty-dimensional volume.  Write
\[
X:=u_{29}+u_{30},
\qquad
Y:=\sum_{i\in B_k}u_i,
\qquad
Z:=\sum_{i\in R\setminus B_k}u_i.
\]
Then \(X+Y+Z=1\), and the cap inequality is
\[
X+r_kY\leq r_k.
\]
Substituting \(Y=1-X-Z\) shows that this is exactly
\[
(1-r_k)X\leq r_kZ,
\]
or, away from the null face \(X+Z=0\),
\[
\frac{X}{X+Z}\leq r_k.
\tag{10}
\]

Here is a direct normalized-volume calculation for (10).  In barycentric
coordinate Lebesgue measure, aggregation of the two exceptional
coordinates contributes a factor proportional to \(X^{2-1}=X\).
Aggregation of the \(29-k\) zero-level regular coordinates contributes a
factor proportional to
\[
Z^{29-k-1}=Z^{28-k}.
\]
For \(k>0\), aggregation of the remaining \(k\) coordinates contributes a
factor proportional to \(Y^{k-1}\); for \(k=0\), that group is absent.
Set
\[
S:=X+Z,
\qquad
\rho:=\frac{X}{X+Z}.
\]
The change \((X,Z)=(S\rho,S(1-\rho))\) has Jacobian \(S\), so every factor
depending on \(\rho\) is exactly
\[
\rho(1-\rho)^{28-k}.
\]
All factors depending on \(S\), on \(Y=1-S\), and on the internal
barycentric coordinates of the three groups cancel between the cap volume
and the total volume.  Since
\[
\int_0^1\rho(1-\rho)^{28-k}\,d\rho
=
\frac{(28-k)!}{(30-k)!},
\]
the normalized volume of (10) is
\[
\frac{(30-k)!}{(28-k)!}
\int_0^{r_k}\rho(1-\rho)^{28-k}\,d\rho
=F_{30-k,2}(r_k).
\]
For completeness, if \(d=30-k\), direct integration and the binomial
theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
d(d-1)\int_0^r\rho(1-\rho)^{d-2}\,d\rho
&=
1-(1-r)^d-dr(1-r)^{d-1}\\
&=
\sum_{j=2}^d\binom djr^j(1-r)^{d-j}
=F_{d,2}(r),
\end{aligned}
\]
which justifies the last equality without invoking a predecessor.
This proves (1).  The hyperplane in (10) has thirty-dimensional volume
zero because its defining affine functional is nonconstant.  Hence using
a closed halfspace, including all \(k\) simplex vertices on its boundary,
does not change the integral.  Since every \(H_k\) is admissible in the
depth infimum, (2) follows.

We now verify the fully oblique cap.  Under (3),
\[
29a+2b
=
\frac{696}{725}+\frac1{25}
=1.
\]
The twenty-eight regular edge-intercept fractions from the regular apex
\(u_0=1\) are
\[
t_i=30a=\frac{144}{145}
\qquad(1\leq i\leq28),
\]
and the two exceptional edge-intercept fractions are
\[
t_{29}=t_{30}=30b=\frac35.
\]
All thirty fractions lie strictly between zero and one.  The corresponding
simplex cap is
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{30}\frac{u_i}{t_i}\leq1,
\]
which is exactly (4).  It passes through \(q\), since
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{28}\frac{q_i}{30a}
+\frac{q_{29}}{30b}
+\frac{q_{30}}{30b}
=\frac{28}{30}+\frac1{30}+\frac1{30}
=1.
\]
After multiplication by \(432\), the affine functional in (4) has vertex
values
\[
0,\qquad
\underbrace{435,\ldots,435}_{28\ {\rm times}},
\qquad
720,\qquad720,
\]
and cutting level \(432\).  It is nonconstant and no vertex value equals
the cutting level.  Thus (4) is a genuine proper closed oblique cap and its
cutting hyperplane contains no simplex vertex.

In the affine chart based at the apex \(u_0=1\), the diagonal map
\[
(y_1,\ldots,y_{30})
\longmapsto
(u_1,\ldots,u_{30})
=
(t_1y_1,\ldots,t_{30}y_{30})
\]
maps the standard coordinate simplex bijectively onto
\(\Delta_{30}\cap H_{\mathrm{obl}}\).  Indeed,
\(\sum_i y_i\leq1\) is exactly the cap inequality after this substitution,
and \(0<t_i<1\) ensures
\(\sum_i u_i\leq\sum_i y_i\leq1\).  Conversely, every point of the cap
gives \(y_i=u_i/t_i\geq0\) with \(\sum_i y_i\leq1\).  Its determinant is
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{30}t_i
=
\left(\frac{144}{145}\right)^{28}
\left(\frac35\right)^2,
\]
which proves the equality in (5).

For the strict upper bound in (5), the first three positive terms in the
binomial expansion give
\[
\left(\frac{145}{144}\right)^{28}
>
1+\frac{28}{144}+\frac{\binom{28}{2}}{144^2}
=
\frac{1397}{1152}
>
\frac65,
\]
where
\[
5\cdot1397-6\cdot1152=73>0.
\]
Taking reciprocals and multiplying by \(9/25\) gives
\[
\left(\frac{144}{145}\right)^{28}
\left(\frac35\right)^2
<
\frac56\cdot\frac9{25}
=\frac3{10}.
\]

It remains to verify both proposed values without using a numerical
approximation.  Since
\[
\frac{701}{725}>\frac{29}{30}
\]
and
\[
6\cdot29^5-5\cdot30^5
=1566894>0,
\]
one has
\[
\left(\frac{701}{725}\right)^{30}
>
\left(\frac{29}{30}\right)^{30}
>
\left(\frac56\right)^6
>
\frac13
>
\frac3{10}.
\]
The last two strict inequalities are certified by
\[
3\cdot5^6-6^6=219>0,
\qquad
10\cdot5^6-3\cdot6^6=16282>0.
\]
This proves (6).

For the second displayed value, the binomial definition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{30,2}\left(\frac1{25}\right)
&=
1-
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^{30}
-30\left(\frac1{25}\right)
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^{29}\\
&=
1-\frac{54}{25}
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^{29}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The exact integer inequalities
\[
2\cdot25^{10}-3\cdot24^{10}
=524720385122>0
\]
and
\[
7\cdot25^9-10\cdot24^9
=284805457135>0
\]
give
\[
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^{10}<\frac23,
\qquad
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^9<\frac7{10}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^{29}
<
\left(\frac23\right)^2\frac7{10}
=\frac{14}{45},
\]
and hence
\[
F_{30,2}\left(\frac1{25}\right)
>
1-\frac{54}{25}\frac{14}{45}
=\frac{41}{125}
>
\frac3{10}.
\]
This proves (7) and completes the fully oblique counterexample.

Finally, at (3),
\[
r_{27}
=
\frac{2b}{1-27a}
=
\frac{29}{77}.
\]
The general cap \(H_{27}\) is exactly (8), and (1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{3,2}\left(\frac{29}{77}\right)
&=
3\left(\frac{29}{77}\right)^2
-2\left(\frac{29}{77}\right)^3\\
&=
\frac{145493}{456533}.
\end{aligned}
\]
It is less than \(1/3\), because
\[
456533-3\cdot145493=20054>0,
\]
and it is less than \(41/125\), because
\[
41\cdot456533-125\cdot145493=531228>0.
\]
Together with (6)-(7), this proves the strict claim after (9) and supplies
a distinct exact multi-vertex cap mechanism.

To check the absolute-volume normalization, omit any one barycentric
coordinate to identify \(\Delta_{30}\) with the standard coordinate
simplex of Lebesgue volume \(1/30!\).  The derivative vectors of the
embedding into the affine hyperplane have Gram determinant \(31\), so all
thirty-dimensional Hausdorff volumes acquire the constant factor
\(\sqrt{31}\).  This proves
\(\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\Delta_{30})=\sqrt{31}/30!\) and confirms every
normalized-to-absolute conversion stated above.
