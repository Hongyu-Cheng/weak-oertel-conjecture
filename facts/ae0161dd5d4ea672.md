## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
Assume \(S\neq\varnothing\).
For \((z,y)\in C\), define its absolute depth inside its fiber by
\[
\delta_C(z,y):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L):
L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }y\in L
\right\}.
\]
For \(p\in S\), let
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\left\{
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H
\right\},
\]
where \(H_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\), and set
\[
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\]

Let \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty. For every \(z\in I\), let \(D_z\subseteq C_z\) be a nonempty compact convex set and let \(w_z\geq0\) satisfy
\[
\delta_C(z,y)\geq w_z
\qquad(y\in D_z).
\tag{1}
\]
For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}I\), define the nonempty compact polytope
\[
\Lambda_m(I):=
\left\{
\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^I:
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z=1,
\quad
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z z=m
\right\}
\]
and the barycentric Minkowski core
\[
\mathcal K_m(D):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)}
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zD_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Then every \(x\in\mathcal K_m(D)\) satisfies \((m,x)\in C\) and
\[
h_S(m,x)
\geq
b_m(w),
\qquad
b_m(w):=
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in I\cap J}w_z.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, suppose that \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) is affine and \(s(z)\in D_z\) for every \(z\in I\). Then \(s(m)\in\mathcal K_m(D)\) for every \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}I\), so (3) holds with \(x=s(m)\). If \(T:=\sum_{z\in I}w_z>0\), there is some \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}\{z\in I:w_z>0\}\) such that
\[
(m,s(m))\in C,
\qquad
h_S(m,s(m))\geq2^{-n}T.
\tag{4}
\]

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) and \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), define
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_K y f(y)\,dy:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dy=t
\right\},
\]
and put \(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d\). Let
\[
I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0\}.
\]
This set is finite. For every nonempty \(E\subseteq I_+\), every choice of strictly positive masses
\[
0<t_z\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\qquad(z\in E),
\tag{5}
\]
and every \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), set
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\tag{6}
\]
If this core is nonempty, then
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\gamma_d
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C):=
\sup\left\{
\sum_{z\in E}t_z:
\begin{array}{l}
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq I_+,\ 0<t_z\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\\
\text{there exists }m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E
\text{ with }\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\neq\varnothing\text{ and}\\
\displaystyle
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z\geq2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
\text{ for every proper closed halfspace }J\ni m
\end{array}
\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
where the supremum of the empty set is defined to be zero, one has the metronoid-core master bound
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\tag{9}
\]
Only the positive masses in (5) occur in (6)-(9); no zero-mass metronoid is defined or used.

Finally, assume that every positive-volume lattice fiber has one common volume: there is \(a>0\) such that
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\in\{0,a\}
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{10}
\]
Then, also allowing the vacuous case \(W=0\),
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\tag{11}
\]

## Proof

We first prove (3). Since \(m\in\operatorname{conv}I\), the polytope \(\Lambda_m(I)\) is nonempty. Fix \(x\in\mathcal K_m(D)\) and choose any \(\lambda^0\in\Lambda_m(I)\). Membership in the corresponding Minkowski sum supplies points \(y_z^0\in D_z\) such that
\[
x=\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z^0y_z^0.
\]
Therefore
\[
(m,x)=
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z^0(z,y_z^0)\in C
\tag{12}
\]
by convexity of \(C\). This proves the required ambient membership before any depth estimate.

For later use, (1) implies
\[
0\leq w_z\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\qquad(z\in I).
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, for any \(y\in D_z\), every cap in the infimum defining \(\delta_C(z,y)\) has volume at most \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\). In particular, if \(C_z\) has zero \(d\)-volume, then necessarily \(w_z=0\).

Let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \((m,x)\). Translating its boundary parallel toward \((m,x)\) produces a closed halfspace \(H^0\subseteq H\) whose boundary contains \((m,x)\). Thus there are \(u\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), not both zero, such that
\[
H^0=
\left\{
(z,y):u\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+v\mathbin{\cdot}(y-x)\geq0
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
For each \(z\in I\), compactness of \(D_z\) makes the support value
\[
q_z:=h_{D_z}(v):=\max_{y\in D_z}v\mathbin{\cdot}y
\tag{15}
\]
finite and attained.

For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)\), the inclusion \(x\in\sum_z\lambda_zD_z\) implies
\[
v\mathbin{\cdot}x
\leq
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zq_z.
\tag{16}
\]
Consider the finite linear program
\[
\theta:=
\min\left\{
\sum_{z\in I}q_z\lambda_z:
\lambda\geq0,\ \sum_z\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_z\lambda_zz=m
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Its feasible region is the nonempty compact polytope \(\Lambda_m(I)\), so its minimum is finite and attained, and (16) gives \(\theta\geq v\cdot x\). Its finite-dimensional dual is
\[
\max\left\{
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}m:
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq q_z\quad(z\in I)
\right\},
\tag{18}
\]
with variables \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\) and \(g\in\mathbb R^n\). Finite-dimensional strong linear-programming duality applies because (17) is feasible with finite optimum; it gives a dual optimizer \((\alpha,g)\) satisfying
\[
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}z\leq q_z\quad(z\in I),
\qquad
\alpha+g\mathbin{\cdot}m=\theta.
\tag{19}
\]

The last equality need not equal \(v\cdot x\). This is the dual slack that must be removed. Put
\[
\sigma:=\theta-v\mathbin{\cdot}x\geq0,
\qquad
\ell(z):=\alpha-\sigma+g\mathbin{\cdot}z.
\tag{20}
\]
Lowering only the constant term preserves every dual inequality, while repairing the value at \(m\):
\[
\ell(z)\leq q_z\quad(z\in I),
\qquad
\ell(m)=v\mathbin{\cdot}x.
\tag{21}
\]

Define
\[
J_0:=
\left\{
z\in\mathbb R^n:(u+g)\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)\geq0
\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(u+g\neq0\), this is a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m\). If \(u+g=0\), it is the whole base space; this possible whole-space pullback will be handled directly rather than incorrectly called proper.

For each \(z\in I\cap J_0\), choose \(y_z\in D_z\) attaining (15). Equations (21) and (22) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
u\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+v\mathbin{\cdot}(y_z-x)
&=u\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+q_z-v\mathbin{\cdot}x\\
&\geq u\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)+\ell(z)-\ell(m)\\
&=(u+g)\mathbin{\cdot}(z-m)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(y_z\in C_z\cap H_z^0\).

If \(v\neq0\), then \(H_z^0\) is a proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^d\) containing \(y_z\), so (1) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z^0)\geq w_z.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(v=0\), then (23) says \(u\cdot(z-m)\geq0\), and hence \(H_z^0=\mathbb R^d\); in this zero-continuous-normal case (24) follows from (13). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
&\geq
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z^0)\\
&\geq
\sum_{z\in I\cap J_0}w_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
When \(J_0\) is proper, the last sum is at least \(b_m(w)\) by definition. When \(J_0=\mathbb R^n\), it equals \(\sum_{z\in I}w_z\), which is also at least \(b_m(w)\) because the weights are nonnegative. If the whole ambient space is admitted as an improper closed halfspace, its mixed volume is \(W\), and (13) gives
\[
W\geq\sum_{z\in I}w_z\geq b_m(w).
\]
Thus every allowed closed halfspace containing \((m,x)\) has mixed volume at least \(b_m(w)\). Taking the infimum proves (3), including boundary, zero-normal, zero-volume, and whole-space cases.

We next prove the affine special case. For any \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)\), affineness gives
\[
s(m)=s\left(\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zz\right)
=\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zs(z)
\in\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zD_z.
\tag{26}
\]
Hence \(s(m)\in\mathcal K_m(D)\), and (3) applies.

Suppose now that \(T=\sum_{z\in I}w_z>0\), and apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite measure
\[
\nu:=\sum_{z\in I}w_z\delta_z
\]
supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\). It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) has
\[
\sum_{z\in I\cap J}w_z=\nu(J)\geq2^{-n}T.
\tag{27}
\]
This \(m\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}\{z\in I:w_z>0\}\): otherwise strict separation from that finite convex hull would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) and no positive-weight point, contradicting (27). Equations (3), (26), and (27) prove (4). Notice that (12), rather than any assumption that \(m\in I\), proves \((m,s(m))\in C\).

We now establish the metronoid assertions. Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty, so \(I_+\) is finite. If \(z\in I_+\), then \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` states that for every mass in (5), \(\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(C_z\), and every one of its points has absolute fiber depth at least \(\gamma_dt_z\). Apply (3) with
\[
D_z=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\qquad
w_z=\gamma_dt_z.
\]
Whenever (6) is nonempty, this gives (7). Taking the supremum over the configurations in (8) proves (9). The strict inequalities \(t_z>0\) ensure that only the metronoids covered by the accepted fact are used. A zero-volume fiber is omitted, and a zero selected mass can simply be omitted from \(E\); neither requires a definition of \(\mathsf M_0\).

It remains to prove the equal-positive-volume conclusion. If \(W=0\), then \(h_S(p)\geq0=\gamma_d2^{-n}W\) for every \(p\in S\), so assume \(W>0\). Let
\[
I:=I_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=a\}.
\]
This is a finite nonempty set and \(W=a|I|\). Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the counting measure \(\sum_{z\in I}\delta_z\). It gives \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that
\[
|I\cap J|\geq2^{-n}|I|
\tag{28}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\). As above, (28) and strict separation imply \(m\in\operatorname{conv}I\), so \(\Lambda_m(I)\neq\varnothing\).

For \(z\in I\), let
\[
c_z:=\frac1a\int_{C_z}y\,dy
\]
be the fiber centroid. The full-mass metronoid is exactly the centroid singleton:
\[
\mathsf M_a(C_z)=\{c_z\}.
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed, if \(0\leq f\leq1\) and \(\int_{C_z}f=a=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), then the nonnegative function \(1-f\) has integral zero, so \(f=1\) almost everywhere, and its normalized first moment is \(c_z\). Conversely, \(f=1\) realizes \(c_z\).

Fix any \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)\), and put \(s_0:=a^{1/d}>0\). Accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` proves, before the closure used to include zero-mass boundary points, the following exact positive-mass interpolation: the set
\[
G:=\{(z,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_z)\}
\]
is convex. Applying that binary conclusion finitely many times to the points \((z,c_z,s_0)\in G\) with coefficients \(\lambda_z\) gives
\[
\left(m,\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zc_z,s_0\right)\in G,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zc_z\in\mathsf M_a(C_m).
\tag{30}
\]
This use is entirely at the fixed positive level \(s_0\); no conclusion about the \(s=0\) closure is used.

Membership in \(\mathsf M_a(C_m)\) supplies a density of mass \(a\) bounded by \(1_{C_m}\), so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_m)\geq a>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Because \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), hypothesis (10) now forces
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_m)=a,
\qquad
m\in I.
\tag{32}
\]
The same argument as in (29) therefore gives
\[
\mathsf M_a(C_m)=\{c_m\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Combining (30) and (33), for every barycentric representation \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)\),
\[
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_zc_z=c_m.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus the intersection of the centroid singletons is itself exactly the centroid singleton:
\[
\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_a)
=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(I)}
\sum_{z\in I}\lambda_z\{c_z\}
=\{c_m\}.
\tag{35}
\]
This proves core nonemptiness without asserting that \(z\mapsto c_z\) is globally affine; only the identities (34) at the selected base point \(m\) are proved.

Finally apply (7) with \(E=I\), \(t_z=a\), and \(x=c_m\). Equation (28) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C)
&\geq h_S(m,c_m)\\
&\geq\gamma_d a
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}|I\cap J|\\
&\geq\gamma_da2^{-n}|I|
=\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is (11).
