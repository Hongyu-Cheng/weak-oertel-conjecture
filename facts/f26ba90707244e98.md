## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$, $H>0$ and $0<U<H$. Put $B=H+1$ and $r(z)=(H-z)/B$. On $-1\le z\le U$, $|x|\le r(z)$, use the measure of density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$ with respect to $dx\,dz$. Set $a=r(U)$, $T=U/a$, $t=a^d=[H/(B+T)]^d$, $V_0=B/d$, and $V=(1-t)V_0$. The associated cone has the same density formula on $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, and mass $V_0$.

For a finite slope $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$. Let $C(s)$ and $M(s)$ be the integrals of $f$ and $zf$, respectively, over the section $z+sx=0$ inside the support, against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. A law's record is the average of $(P,C,M)$, and a law is centered if its averaged $M$ is zero.

For $T\ge1$, let $\mu,\nu$ be separately centered probability laws on cone slopes $[0,T]$. Use $P_\mu^c,P_\nu^c,C_\mu,C_\nu$ for their averaged cone cap and coarea records. If $P_\mu^f,P_\nu^f$ denote the corresponding frustum cap records, the coareas are the same, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta_c&=\frac{C_\nu P_\mu^c+C_\mu(V_0-P_\nu^c)}{V_0(C_\mu+C_\nu)},&
\omega&=\frac{C_\nu}{C_\mu+C_\nu},\\
\beta_f&=\frac{C_\nu P_\mu^f+C_\mu(V-P_\nu^f)}{V(C_\mu+C_\nu)}
       =\frac{\beta_c-t\omega}{1-t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For arbitrary separately centered frustum laws, use their record-preserving representatives on $[0,T]\cup\{\infty\}$ and write $\lambda_\mu,\lambda_\nu$ for their finite masses. If both are positive, let $\beta_f$ in the next formula be the bounded frustum ratio of their conditional finite laws, and $C_\mu,C_\nu$ their conditional finite coareas. The ratio for the original pair is

$$
\frac12+\frac{\lambda_\mu\lambda_\nu(C_\mu+C_\nu)}
{\lambda_\mu C_\mu+\lambda_\nu C_\nu}\left(\beta_f-\frac12\right).
$$

If exactly one law has zero coarea, the ratio is $1/2$. If both have zero coarea, the literal quotient has zero denominator, and its continuous extension has value $1/2$. Each cap record in that case is $V/2$. For $T\le1$ the positive-denominator ratio for any separately centered pair is $1/2$, with the same extension at zero total coarea.

In the $T\ge1$ case, any common correction $c$ giving $P-cC\in[\beta V,(1-\beta)V]$ on the centered bounded frustum laws extends to all centered laws when $\beta\le1/2$.

## Proof

For bounded laws, the record identities give $P_\mu^f=P_\mu^c-tV_0$, $P_\nu^f=P_\nu^c-tV_0$ and equal cone and frustum coareas, with $C_\mu+C_\nu>0$. Since $V=V_0-tV_0$, the second oriented term contains $V-P_\nu^f=V_0-P_\nu^c$. The first oriented term loses $tV_0C_\nu$, and the denominator is multiplied by $1-t$. This gives the identity.

For general centered laws with $T\ge1$, the bounded-law lemma preserves each average record and centers each conditional finite law whenever defined. The new average frustum cap and coarea for $\mu$ are $\lambda_\mu P_\mu^f+(1-\lambda_\mu)V/2$ and $\lambda_\mu C_\mu$, respectively, with the corresponding expressions for $\nu$. For any pair with positive total coarea, subtract $1/2$ from the balanced quotient by replacing its numerator with $C_\nu(P_\mu-V/2)-C_\mu(P_\nu-V/2)$, using the actual average records here. If both finite masses are positive, this centered numerator scales by $\lambda_\mu\lambda_\nu$ while the denominator becomes $V(\lambda_\mu C_\mu+\lambda_\nu C_\nu)$. This gives the displayed formula. If just one law has zero coarea, its cap mass is $V/2$, so the centered numerator is zero. The mixing coefficient in the displayed formula is a weighted average of $\lambda_\nu$ and $\lambda_\mu$, with respective weights $\lambda_\mu C_\mu$ and $\lambda_\nu C_\nu$. It tends to zero as both finite masses tend to zero, and $\beta_f\in[0,1]$ since each cap fraction is in $[0,1]$. This also gives continuity at zero total coarea in the record variables. Indeed, for $s\le T$ and $b\in[-T,1]$ the energy kernel is bounded below by $(1+T/B)^{-d}$ and each uniform layer puts at least half its mass in $b\le1$. The conditional finite coareas are therefore bounded below by $C_0(1+T/B)^{-d}/2$ with $C_0=(H/B)^{d-1}$, so convergence to zero total coarea forces both finite masses to tend to zero.

For $T\le1$, the bounded-law lemma gives $P_\mu-V/2=cC_\mu$ and $P_\nu-V/2=cC_\nu$ with the same $c$. The centered numerator cancels, giving the constant quotient when the denominator is positive. Positivity of finite coarea identifies the zero-denominator case with two pure infinity records. Finally, when a common $c$ works on the bounded centered laws, the infinity record has corrected cap $V/2$ in the same interval for $\beta\le1/2$. Averaging proves the extension.
