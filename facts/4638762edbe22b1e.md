## Statement

Use exactly the canonical hard-branch setting, concentrated occurrence transports, and notation of accepted fact `3f66097ebb48c25b`, and write \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).  In particular, \(\mathcal M(v)\) is the set of distinct positive-stress midpoint bases, \(\mathscr L(v)\) is the set of positive cross-occurrence labels, a label \(\ell\) has weight \(\lambda_\ell>0\), base \(m_\ell\), and target atom \(X_\ell\), and
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)
 =2\gamma_d\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L(v)}
 \lambda_\ell\delta_{(m_\ell,X_\ell)},
 \qquad
 \nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
 \tag{1}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\).  For \(m\in\mathcal M(v)\), put
\[
 \mathscr L_m:=\{\ell\in\mathscr L(v):m_\ell=m\},
 \qquad
 L_m:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}\lambda_\ell,
 \qquad
 \zeta_m:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}
 \lambda_\ell\delta_{X_\ell}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(L_m>0\) and
\[
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}L_m=T_*.
 \tag{3}
\]
Let
\[
 A_m:=\operatorname{aff}\{X_\ell:\ell\in\mathscr L_m\},
 \qquad
 s_m:=\dim A_m,
 \tag{4}
\]
where coincident atoms are aggregated in the support used to form \(A_m\), while their weights remain added in \(\zeta_m\).  Define the relative weighted-centerpoint set
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}_m:=
 \left\{q\in A_m:
 \zeta_m(J)\geq\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}
 \text{ for every relative closed halfspace }J\subseteq A_m
 \text{ with }q\in J\right\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Cent}_m\) is nonempty and
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}_m
 \subseteq\operatorname{conv}\{X_\ell:\ell\in\mathscr L_m\}
 \subseteq C_m.
 \tag{6}
\]
The constant in (5) is the intrinsic centerpoint constant \(1/(s_m+1)\), including the value \(1\) when \(s_m=0\).

Every \(q\in\operatorname{Cent}_m\) is a genuine mixed-integer point \((m,q)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and its ambient mixed halfspace depth satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 h_S((m,q))
 \geq 2\gamma_d\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}.}
 \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, in the normalization \(D=2^nh_{\max}(C)\),
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq 2^{n+1}\gamma_d
 \max_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}.}
 \tag{8}
\]
In the canonical hard branch \(D<Q/100\), this forces the simultaneous local mass-rank obstruction
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{L_m}{s_m+1}
 <\frac{Q}{200\gamma_d2^n}
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M(v).}
 \tag{9}
\]
The contributions in (7) are not summed over different midpoint bases.

There is a different, generally weaker conclusion that does give one legal fiber-depth point.  For \(q\in\operatorname{Cent}_m\), define
\[
 \chi_m(q):=
 \max\left\{
 \left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}
 p_\ell\lambda_\ell^{1/d}\right)^d:
 p_\ell\geq0,\quad
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}p_\ell=1,\quad
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}p_\ell X_\ell=q
 \right\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The maximum is attained.  It is the largest fiber mass certified at \(q\) by a single positive-root convex combination of the accepted labeled realizations, and
\[
 \boxed{
 q\in\mathsf M_{\chi_m(q)}(C_m),
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m,q)\geq\gamma_d\chi_m(q),
 \qquad
 0<\chi_m(q)\leq b_m.}
 \tag{11}
\]
Here \(b_m=\max_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}\lambda_\ell\) for the concentrated transport family of `3f66097ebb48c25b`.

Retain \(A_{\mathrm{occ}}=\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M(v)\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}=\dim A_{\mathrm{occ}}\) from `3f66097ebb48c25b`.  For a simultaneous family
\(q_\bullet=(q_m)_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}\) with
\(q_m\in\operatorname{Cent}_m\), put
\[
 r(q_\bullet):=
 \min_{a:A_{\mathrm{occ}}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q_m-a(m):m\in\mathcal M(v)\},
 \tag{12}
\]
and define the optimized centerpoint-compression score
\[
 \mathfrak S_{\mathrm{cp}}(v):=
 \sup_{q_m\in\operatorname{Cent}_m\ (m\in\mathcal M(v))}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{\sum_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}\chi_m(q_m)}
 {r(q_\bullet)+1}.
 \tag{13}
\]
For each family in (13), the points \(q_m\), with the one family of weights
\(\gamma_d\chi_m(q_m)\), form exactly one legal residual selector.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\geq
 \gamma_d\mathfrak S_{\mathrm{cp}}(v).}
 \tag{14}
\]
In the stress-frame normalization of `3f66097ebb48c25b`, the corresponding HSF sufficient condition is
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{4\gamma_d\mathfrak S_{\mathrm{cp}}(v)}{T_*}
 +\frac{8\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*}\geq1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T_*\leq4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+8\mathfrak F_*(v).}
 \tag{15}
\]
If HSF fails, then the reverse inequality in (15) is strict.  More explicitly, for every simultaneous centerpoint family define
\[
 \pi_m:=\frac{L_m}{T_*},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_m(q_m):=
 \frac{(s_m+1)\chi_m(q_m)}{L_m}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Failure of HSF forces, for every such family,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{4\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}}
 {r(q_\bullet)+1}
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}
 \pi_m\frac{\varepsilon_m(q_m)}{s_m+1}
 +\frac{8\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*}<1.}
 \tag{17}
\]
Together, (9) and (17) are the exact local-rank/global-residual-rank obstruction delivered by this architecture.

The comparison with the max-label score is one-sided in retained mass.  For every simultaneous centerpoint family,
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{cp}}(q_\bullet):=
 \frac{\sum_m\chi_m(q_m)}{T_*}
 \leq
 \frac{\sum_mb_m}{T_*}
 =\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v).
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus centerpoint compression never repairs occurrence fragmentation or midpoint congestion in its certified numerator.  It can improve the residual-selector lower bound from `3f66097ebb48c25b` only through a smaller global residual rank.  For a fixed centerpoint family its numerical score is larger than the displayed max-label score of that accepted fact exactly when
\[
 \frac{\sum_m\chi_m(q_m)}{r(q_\bullet)+1}
 >\frac{B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)}{r_\sharp+1};
 \tag{19}
\]
because of (18), this requires the rank reduction to compensate for the entire numerator loss.

There is no universal constant \(c>0\) for which the local source-budget, target-metronoid, coincident-atom, and global-domination premises (1)--(6) alone imply
\[
 \delta_C(m,q)\geq
 c\gamma_d\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}
 \tag{20}
\]
for a weighted centerpoint \(q\).  This remains false when all target atoms coincide, every labeled atom has an actual full-mass metronoid realization, the full occurrence measure is globally halfspace-dominated, and \(s_m=0\).  Thus the factor \(L_m/(s_m+1)\) in (7) is an ambient mixed-depth certificate only; replacing it by a fiber-depth weight in a residual selector is invalid without a new target-fiber compatibility theorem.

## Proof

Fix \(m\in\mathcal M(v)\).  Apply the continuous specialization of accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the nonzero finite measure \(\zeta_m\) on the \(s_m\)-dimensional affine space \(A_m\).  The Helly number of that affine space is \(s_m+1\), so it supplies a point satisfying (5), including when \(s_m=0\).  If such a point lay outside the convex hull of the atoms, strict affine separation inside \(A_m\) would give a relative closed halfspace containing the point and no atom, contradicting the positive lower bound in (5).  Hence the first inclusion in (6) holds.  Accepted fact `3f66097ebb48c25b` gives
\(X_\ell\in\mathsf M_{\lambda_\ell}(C_m)\subseteq C_m\) for every label over \(m\).  Convexity of the fiber then gives the second inclusion in (6).  This proves in particular that \((m,q)\) is an actual point of the mixed-integer body, rather than merely a point of the affine hull of the atoms.

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace containing \((m,q)\).  Its section
\[
 J:=\{x\in A_m:(m,x)\in H\}
 \tag{21}
\]
is a relative closed halfspace of \(A_m\) containing \(q\); it may be all of \(A_m\), which causes no problem.  The centerpoint property gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\in\mathscr L_m\\(m,X_\ell)\in H}}
 \lambda_\ell
 =\zeta_m(J)
 \geq\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}.
 \tag{22}
\]
All contributions from other midpoint bases are nonnegative.  Therefore (1) and (22) give
\[
 \mu_C(H)
 \geq\nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)(H)
 \geq2\gamma_d\frac{L_m}{s_m+1}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Taking the infimum over ambient halfspaces proves (7).  Taking the maximum over \(m\), multiplying by \(2^n\), and then using \(D<Q/100\) proves (8) and (9).  Notice that this argument uses the full local mass only inside one chosen ambient depth computation.  It neither adds the depths obtained at different bases nor conditions global domination on the event that the base equals \(m\).

Now fix \(q\in\operatorname{Cent}_m\).  By (6), the coefficient set in (10) is a nonempty compact polytope.  Its displayed objective is continuous, so the maximum is attained.  For a maximizing coefficient vector \(p\), accepted fact `3f66097ebb48c25b` supplies the positive-root triples
\[
 (m,X_\ell,\lambda_\ell^{1/d})
 \quad(\ell\in\mathscr L_m)
 \tag{24}
\]
in the generating set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`.  That accepted fact proves the positive-root generating set itself convex before closure.  Taking the convex combination with coefficients \(p_\ell\) gives
\[
 \left(m,q,
 \sum_{\ell\in\mathscr L_m}
 p_\ell\lambda_\ell^{1/d}\right)
 \tag{25}
\]
in the same generating set.  This is exactly the first assertion in (11).  The target fiber has positive \(d\)-volume because it supports a positive-mass metronoid realization.  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore applies and gives the fiber-depth inequality in (11).

For the concentrated transports of `3f66097ebb48c25b`, the largest label weight over \(m\) is \(b_m\).  Concavity of \(t\mapsto t^{1/d}\) gives, for every feasible \(p\),
\[
 \sum_\ell p_\ell\lambda_\ell^{1/d}
 \leq\left(\sum_\ell p_\ell\lambda_\ell\right)^{1/d}
 \leq b_m^{1/d}.
 \tag{26}
\]
Raising to the \(d\)-th power proves \(\chi_m(q)\leq b_m\); positivity is immediate.  Equation (10) is exact for the stated interpolation architecture because every iterated application of convexity to the fixed triples (24) is one convex combination of them, and conversely every feasible coefficient vector is allowed by (25).

Choose one centerpoint \(q_m\) at each distinct base.  Equations (6) and (11) show that
\[
 0<\gamma_d\chi_m(q_m)
 \leq\delta_C(m,q_m).
 \tag{27}
\]
The bases are pairwise distinct by the definition of \(\mathcal M(v)\).  Thus (27) is one residual selector in the sense of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`, with one point and one weight at each base.  Its score is
\[
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{\sum_m\chi_m(q_m)}{r(q_\bullet)+1}.
 \tag{28}
\]
Take the supremum over simultaneous centerpoint families to obtain (14).  Multiplying (14) by four and adding \(8\mathfrak F_*(v)\) proves (15).  If HSF fails, then
\(T_*>4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+8\mathfrak F_*(v)\), so (14) makes the normalized reverse inequality strict.  For a fixed family, substitution of (16) into (28) gives exactly the first term of (17), proving the joint obstruction.

Summing the pointwise bounds \(\chi_m(q_m)\leq b_m\), using (3), and invoking the definitions of \(B_{\mathrm{occ}}\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}\) in `3f66097ebb48c25b` proves (18).  The two selector scores have the same factor \(\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}\); cancelling it gives (19).  This proves the stated comparison without adding the two selectors.

It remains to prove that the desired full-local-mass fiber-depth step is genuinely false, not merely absent from the preceding proof.  Fix an integer \(N\geq3\) and a number \(\lambda>0\), take \(n=d=1\), and consider the convex body
\[
 C^{(N)}:=[-2N,2N]\times[0,2\lambda].
 \tag{29}
\]
At each of the \(N\) endpoint-disjoint same-parity pairs
\[
 e_i:=\{-2i,2i\},
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq N,
 \tag{30}
\]
put one labeled cell of weight \(\lambda\).  On every used source fiber, the density
\(g=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{[0,2\lambda]}\) has mass \(\lambda\), barycenter \(\lambda\), and obeys the pointwise half-budget exactly.  Thus every label has midpoint base \(m=0\), target atom \(X=\lambda\), and an actual full-mass target realization
\[
 \lambda\in\mathsf M_\lambda([0,2\lambda])
 \tag{31}
\]
represented by the same half-density.  This is an exact local source-budget and target-metronoid model for the collision step; no incompatible target densities are added.

Here \(\gamma_1=1/2\), so the full occurrence measure in the normalization (1) is
\[
 \nu^{(N)}=N\lambda\,\delta_{(0,\lambda)}.
 \tag{32}
\]
It is globally halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C^{(N)}\).  Indeed, that mixed measure is centrally symmetric about \((0,\lambda)\) and has total mass
\[
 W=(4N+1)2\lambda.
 \tag{33}
\]
Every closed halfspace containing the center contains a parallel boundary-through-the-center halfspace.  Central symmetry makes the two opposite boundary-through-the-center halfspaces have equal mass, and their union is the whole space, so each has mass at least \(W/2=(4N+1)\lambda\geq N\lambda\).  Thus every halfspace containing the atom in (32) has mixed mass at least the mass of (32), while halfspaces missing the atom have zero \(\nu^{(N)}\)-mass.

All \(N\) target atoms coincide.  Hence \(s_m=0\), the unique weighted centerpoint is \(q=\lambda\), and \(L_m=N\lambda\).  Nevertheless
\[
 \delta_{C^{(N)}}(0,\lambda)=\lambda,
 \qquad
 \chi_m(\lambda)=\lambda=b_m,
 \qquad
 \frac{\chi_m(\lambda)}{L_m}=\frac1N.
 \tag{34}
\]
The hoped-for right side with constant \(c\) in (20) is
\(cN\lambda/2\).  Since \(N\) is arbitrary, (34) disproves every universal positive \(c\).  At the same time, the ambient argument (7) remains valid: it asks only for mixed halfspace mass \(2\gamma_1L_m=N\lambda\), which (33) supplies.  The example is a calibration of the compression inference, not a canonical hard-branch optimizer and not a counterexample to HSF or to the locked weak Oertel statement.  It proves that a new theorem making the target-fiber realizations compatible is necessary before the full local occurrence mass can be used as one fiber-depth weight.
