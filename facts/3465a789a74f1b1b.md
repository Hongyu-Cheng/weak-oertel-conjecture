## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite nonempty and $K_z=[l_z,r_z]$ with $l_z<r_z$. Let $\mu$ be the sum of their lengths with total mass $M$, and consider finite $N$-cap full closed covers of $S=\bigcup_z(\{z\}\times K_z)$ with margins $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i x-b_i$, $u_i\in\{-1,1\}$, and mass at most $\tau M$ per cap for $0<\tau<1$. At every local minimum of $\sum_i\Psi_i$ with $\Psi_i=\int_S(L_i)_+\,d\mu$, the contact stress has objective multiplier one.

In detail, write $(c_i,P_i,m_i)$ for cap mass and base and continuous first moments. There are nonnegative budget and cover multipliers $r_i',\eta_z$ and consistent actual section moments $(S_i,P_i^s,m_i^s)$, computed on the cap plane by counting measure with density one at proper sections, density in $[0,1]$ at supporting endpoints, and zero for an empty section. The triple $(P_i^s,m_i^s,-S_i)$ is a single consistent generalized gradient of the cap mass in the unrestricted affine coefficients. At each fiber choose optimal minimax weights $\lambda_z\in\Delta_N$. If $\eta_z>0$, the cover margin is zero and there is a primal minimax contact $x_z\in K_z$ with $L_i(z,x_z)\le0$, $\lambda_{zi}L_i(z,x_z)=0$ for all $i$, and $-\sum_i\lambda_{zi}u_i\in N_{K_z}(x_z)$. The balances, ignoring terms with zero multipliers, are
$$
\begin{aligned}
c_i+r_i'S_i&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi},\\
P_i+r_i'P_i^s&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}z,\\
m_i+r_i'm_i^s&=\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}x_z+\Psi_i u_i .
\end{aligned}
$$
If $r_i'>0$ the $i$th cap budget is saturated.

## Proof

The signs are locally fixed. In the affine parameters $p=(a_i,b_i)_i$ put $t_{iz}=b_i-a_i\cdot z$. For a positive sign the cap length in $K_z$ is $(r_z-\max(l_z,t_{iz}))_+$, and for a negative sign it is $(\min(r_z,-t_{iz})-l_z)_+$. These and their sums are continuous piecewise affine. The coverage margin is $F_z=\min_{x\in K_z}\max_i L_i(z,x)$. If both signs occur, set $s_z=\min_{u_i=1} t_{iz}$ and $q_z=\max_{u_i=-1}(-t_{iz})$. The pointwise maximum is $\max(x-s_z,q_z-x)$, minimized by clipping $(s_z+q_z)/2$ to $K_z$. For a single sign an endpoint minimizes. Thus $F_z$ is continuous piecewise affine as well. The constraint functions $g_j$ are the cap masses minus their budgets and the cover constraints $-F_z$. A common finite closed polyhedral subdivision makes all of them affine on each cell.

For any such finite piecewise-affine inequalities there is a local bound $\operatorname{dist}(p,X)\le C\max_j(g_j(p))_+$ at each $p_*\in X=\{g\le0\}$. To see this, first consider a nonempty polyhedron $Y=\{Ap\le b\}$ and let $y$ be the nearest point to $p$. The residual $v=p-y$ belongs to the cone generated by the active rows at $y$. Indeed, separation from the cone would give a direction feasible for a small step at $y$ and with positive inner product with $v$, contradicting projection. Eliminating dependent generators from a nonnegative representation gives $v=\sum_{j\in I}\theta_j A_j$ with independent active rows and $\theta_j\ge0$. To eliminate a dependence, subtract a multiple of its coefficient vector with sign chosen so at least one coefficient decreases, until the first coefficient becomes zero. For an independent row set the inverse on its span gives $\sum_{j\in I}\theta_j\le C_I\lVert v\rVert_2$. Then $\lVert v\rVert_2^2=\sum_{j\in I}\theta_j(A_jp-b_j)\le C_I\lVert v\rVert_2\max_j(A_jp-b_j)_+$. Maximizing over the finitely many row sets proves the polyhedral bound, including the trivial $v=0$ case. Near $p_*$ only cells containing $p_*$ can occur. Apply that bound within each such cell to its nonempty intersection with $X$, counting its own defining inequalities among the rows. A point in the cell violates none of those extra inequalities. The maximum of the finitely many bounds gives the claimed local estimate.

The hinge $\Psi$ is continuously differentiable in $p$ because each cap plane has zero fiber length. Dominated convergence gives the derivative $(P_i,-c_i)_i$, and continuity follows by the same null-boundary argument. A local Lipschitz constant $B$ and the error bound give $\Psi(p)\ge\Psi(p_*)-BC\max_j(g_j(p))_+$ for all sufficiently near $p_*$, since a nearest feasible point stays in the local-minimum neighborhood. Thus $\Psi+BC\sum_j(g_j)_+$ has a local minimum at $p_*$.

Refine the finite polyhedral subdivision by the signs of the affine pieces of all $g_j$. For each full-dimensional cell containing $p_*$ take the vector equal to $\nabla\Psi(p_*)$ plus the gradient of the affine penalty term there. Zero belongs to the convex hull of these finitely many vectors. Otherwise strict separation would give a direction making every pairing negative. A short ray in that direction lies in the closure of at least one full-dimensional cell containing $p_*$, so by continuity its directional derivative is the pairing for that cell and is negative. Group a convex combination giving zero by constraint. It yields stationarity for $\nabla\Psi(p_*)+\sum_j t_j G_j=0$, with $t_j\ge0$ vanishing on inactive constraints, and $G_j$ a convex combination of limiting ordinary gradients of $g_j$. Only cells with positive $g_j$ contribute that constraint. A zero multiplier needs no gradient choice.

For a cap-mass constraint, the piece gradients are sums of $(z,-1)$ over proper interval sections. At a limiting supporting endpoint the count is zero or one, and convex combinations give a single density shared by all the section moments. At a proper section the count is one, and at an empty section it is zero in a neighborhood. The same sequence may be chosen away from every endpoint equation, since those equations are proper affine hyperplanes in the fixed-sign parameter space. At these points the unrestricted cap mass also has an ordinary gradient with continuous-moment component given by integrating $x$ at the same proper sections. The convex combinations above therefore lift to single consistent generalized gradients in the unrestricted parameters. Thus the budget part has coefficient $r_i'$ times $(P_i^s,-S_i)$ as stated.

For a cover constraint, use accepted fact 828211a20fa24681. For fixed signs and a fixed probability vector, its weighted minimum over $K_z$ is affine in $p$, with coefficient $(\lambda_{zi}z,-\lambda_{zi})_i$. At a differentiability point of $F_z$, the affine function from any optimizing probability vector agrees with $F_z$ there and is a lower bound everywhere. Their gradients must agree. Passing from interior points of a cell to $p_*$ gives limiting optimizing weights by compactness of the simplex and continuity of the weighted minima. The optimizing set is convex by concavity of the dual objective, so the convex combination defining each positive cover multiplier is represented by one optimizing $\lambda_z$. At $F_z=0$ the accepted minimax fact gives the stated common contact and normal-cone condition. The $(a_i,b_i)$ stationarity equations are exactly the first two balances.

Finally the affine cap identity gives $u_i m_i=\Psi_i+b_i c_i-a_i\cdot P_i$. The section and positively weighted contacts have zero margin, so the first two balances cancel their offset and base terms in the corresponding identity for the continuous moments. This gives $u_i(m_i+r_i'm_i^s-\sum_z\eta_z\lambda_{zi}x_z)=\Psi_i$. Since $u_i^2=1$, this is the last balance.
