## Statement

Let $r\ge1$ and let $r+1$ continuous covectors $u_1,\ldots,u_{r+1}$ on $\mathbb R^d$ have rank $r$ and a positive linear dependence $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=0$, $\sum_j\rho_j=1$. Fix $p,y$ and base covectors $a_j$. For $m_j(z,x)=a_j\cdot(z-p)+u_j\cdot(x-y)$ and $s(z)=\sum_j\rho_j a_j\cdot(z-p)$ there is an affine map $c$ with $c(p)=y$ such that
$$
m_j(z,x)=u_j\cdot(x-c(z))+s(z)\qquad\text{for all }j,z,x.
$$
Any two choices differ by an affine map vanishing at $p$ and valued in $\bigcap_j\ker u_j$. If $s(z)\ge0$ and $c(z)\in\operatorname{int}K_z$, that point has nonnegative margin for every cap.

## Proof

The rank-$r$ map $v\mapsto(u_j\cdot v)_j$ has image the orthogonal complement of the nonzero vector $\rho$, since its image is contained there and both spaces have dimension $r$. The vector with entries $s(z)-a_j\cdot(z-p)$ belongs to this image because its pairing with $\rho$ is zero. Apply a linear right inverse on the image to define $c(z)-y$. This gives an affine map with $c(p)=y$. Substituting gives the identity; taking differences proves the stated kernel freedom. At $x=c(z)$ each margin equals $s(z)$. For an exposed target, this verifies the common-interior-target hypothesis if the inclusion holds, but the linear algebra does not imply that inclusion.
