## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C),
\qquad
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}
\quad (z\in P).
\]
Write \(|A|_d\) for \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. For \((z,x)\in C\), define the absolute fiber Tukey depth
\[
\delta_C(z,x)
:=
\inf\bigl\{|C_z\cap H|_d:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in H\bigr\}.
\]
For \(u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\), set
\[
H_u(x):=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:\langle u,y-x\rangle\geq0\}.
\]
Then the following hold.

1. Boundary reduction and attainment:
\[
\delta_C(z,x)
=
\min_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}|C_z\cap H_u(x)|_d.
\]
Thus some minimizing halfspace has \(x\) on its boundary.

2. The function
\[
f:C\longrightarrow[0,\infty),
\qquad
f(z,x):=\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d},
\]
is jointly concave and upper semicontinuous.

3. For every \(s\geq0\), the absolute-depth bundle
\[
G_s:=\{(z,x)\in C:\delta_C(z,x)\geq s\}
\]
is a compact convex set, possibly empty.

4. For every \(z\in P\), the maximum
\[
M(z):=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)
\]
is attained. Moreover,
\[
\rho(z):=M(z)^{1/d}
=\max_{x\in C_z}f(z,x)
\]
is concave and upper semicontinuous on \(P\).

If \(|C_z|_d=0\), in particular if \(C_z\) is lower-dimensional, then
\(\delta_C(z,x)=0\) for every \(x\in C_z\), and \(M(z)=\rho(z)=0\).

## Proof

Each fiber \(C_z\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^d\).
Every proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\) can be written as
\[
H=\{y:\langle u,y\rangle\geq a\}
\]
for some \(u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\) and \(a\in\mathbb R\). If \(x\in H\), then
\(a\leq\langle u,x\rangle\), and hence
\[
H_u(x)=\{y:\langle u,y\rangle\geq\langle u,x\rangle\}
\subseteq H.
\]
Replacing \(H\) by \(H_u(x)\) therefore cannot increase
\(|C_z\cap H|_d\). Since every \(H_u(x)\) is itself an admissible halfspace,
\[
\delta_C(z,x)
=
\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}|C_z\cap H_u(x)|_d.
\tag{1}
\]

For fixed \((z,x)\in C\), the function
\[
u\longmapsto |C_z\cap H_u(x)|_d
\tag{2}
\]
is continuous on \(\mathbb S^{d-1}\). Indeed, if \(u_k\to u\), choose a
bounded measurable set \(B\) containing \(C_z\). For Lebesgue-almost every
\(y\in B\), namely for every \(y\) outside the hyperplane
\(\{y:\langle u,y-x\rangle=0\}\), one has
\[
\mathbf 1_{\{\langle u_k,y-x\rangle\geq0\}}
\longrightarrow
\mathbf 1_{\{\langle u,y-x\rangle\geq0\}}.
\]
Multiplication by \(\mathbf 1_{C_z}(y)\) and dominated convergence prove
continuity of (2). Compactness of \(\mathbb S^{d-1}\) turns the infimum in
(1) into a minimum. This proves assertion 1, also when \(|C_z|_d=0\).

Fix \(u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\), and define
\[
A((z,x),u):=C_z\cap H_u(x),
\qquad
F_u(z,x):=|A((z,x),u)|_d^{1/d}.
\]
Take \(p_i=(z_i,x_i)\in C\) for \(i=0,1\), let \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), and
write
\[
p_\lambda=(z_\lambda,x_\lambda)
:=(1-\lambda)p_0+\lambda p_1.
\]
If \(y_i\in A(p_i,u)\), convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\bigl(z_\lambda,(1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1\bigr)\in C,
\]
while
\[
\left\langle
u,(1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1-x_\lambda
\right\rangle
=
(1-\lambda)\langle u,y_0-x_0\rangle
+\lambda\langle u,y_1-x_1\rangle
\geq0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
(1-\lambda)A(p_0,u)+\lambda A(p_1,u)
\subseteq A(p_\lambda,u).
\tag{3}
\]
The sets in (3) are nonempty compact sets because \(x_i\in A(p_i,u)\).
For \(0<\lambda<1\), the \(d\)-dimensional Brunn-Minkowski inequality and
monotonicity of Lebesgue measure give
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_u(p_\lambda)
&\geq
\bigl|(1-\lambda)A(p_0,u)+\lambda A(p_1,u)\bigr|_d^{1/d}\\
&\geq
(1-\lambda)F_u(p_0)+\lambda F_u(p_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
The endpoint cases are identities. Brunn-Minkowski allows either compact set
to have zero \(d\)-volume, so (4) also covers lower-dimensional fibers.
Thus \(F_u\) is concave on \(C\) for every fixed \(u\).

By (1) and monotonicity of the \(d\)-th root,
\[
f(p)=\inf_{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}}F_u(p).
\tag{5}
\]
Using (4) with the same normal \(u\) at all three points,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(p_\lambda)
&=\inf_u F_u(p_\lambda)\\
&\geq
\inf_u\bigl((1-\lambda)F_u(p_0)+\lambda F_u(p_1)\bigr)\\
&\geq
(1-\lambda)\inf_uF_u(p_0)
+\lambda\inf_uF_u(p_1)\\
&=(1-\lambda)f(p_0)+\lambda f(p_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This proves joint concavity.

It remains to establish the regularity needed for the maxima below. Consider
the cap-volume map
\[
V(p,u):=|A(p,u)|_d,
\qquad
(p,u)\in C\times\mathbb S^{d-1}.
\]
It is jointly upper semicontinuous. To see this, suppose
\((p_k,u_k)\to(p,u)\), where \(p_k=(z_k,x_k)\) and \(p=(z,x)\), and put
\(A_k=A(p_k,u_k)\), \(A=A(p,u)\). All these sets lie in the compact vertical
projection \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^d}(C)\). For every
\(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
A_k\subseteq A+\varepsilon\overline{\mathbb B}_2^d
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }k.
\tag{7}
\]
Otherwise, after passing to a subsequence, there would be \(y_k\in A_k\)
whose distance from \(A\) is at least \(\varepsilon\) and such that
\(y_k\to y\). Closedness of \(C\) would give \((z,y)\in C\), and continuity
of the scalar product would give
\(\langle u,y-x\rangle\geq0\). Hence \(y\in A\), a contradiction.

From (7),
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}|A_k|_d
\leq |A+\varepsilon\overline{\mathbb B}_2^d|_d.
\]
As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the compact parallel sets on the right
decrease to \(A\), so continuity from above of Lebesgue measure yields
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}V(p_k,u_k)\leq V(p,u).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(V\), and hence \(V^{1/d}\), is jointly upper semicontinuous.
For each fixed \(u\), \(F_u\) is therefore upper semicontinuous. Equation
(5) now shows that \(f\) is upper semicontinuous, since a pointwise infimum
of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous. This completes
assertion 2.

For \(s\geq0\),
\[
G_s=\{p\in C:f(p)\geq s^{1/d}\}.
\]
Concavity of \(f\) makes this superlevel set convex. Upper semicontinuity of
\(f\) makes it closed, and it is compact because \(C\) is compact. This
proves assertion 3.

Fix \(z\in P\). The restriction of \(f\) to compact \(C_z\) is upper
semicontinuous, so it attains its maximum. Since \(t\mapsto t^d\) is
increasing on \([0,\infty)\), the same point maximizes \(\delta_C=f^d\), and
\[
\rho(z)=\max_{x\in C_z}f(z,x).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(z_0,z_1\in P\), choose maximizers \(x_i\in C_{z_i}\). If
\(z_\lambda=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1\), then
\(x_\lambda=(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1\) belongs to \(C_{z_\lambda}\).
Joint concavity of \(f\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho(z_\lambda)
&\geq f(z_\lambda,x_\lambda)\\
&\geq
(1-\lambda)f(z_0,x_0)+\lambda f(z_1,x_1)\\
&=(1-\lambda)\rho(z_0)+\lambda\rho(z_1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\rho\) is concave.

To prove upper semicontinuity, let \(z_k\to z\) in \(P\), and choose
\(x_k\in C_{z_k}\) satisfying \(f(z_k,x_k)=\rho(z_k)\). Pass to a subsequence
along which \(\rho(z_k)\) tends to its limsup. Compactness of \(C\) permits a
further subsequence with \((z_k,x_k)\to(z,x)\in C\). Upper semicontinuity of
\(f\) and (9) imply
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\rho(z_k)
=\limsup_{k\to\infty}f(z_k,x_k)
\leq f(z,x)
\leq\rho(z).
\]
This proves assertion 4.

Finally, if \(|C_z|_d=0\), every cap \(C_z\cap H\) has zero
\(d\)-dimensional measure. Hence \(\delta_C(z,x)=0\) for all \(x\in C_z\),
and the asserted values of \(M(z)\) and \(\rho(z)\) follow. A compact convex
subset of \(\mathbb R^d\) with affine dimension less than \(d\) has zero
\(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure, so this includes every lower-dimensional
fiber.

## External sources

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, pp. 362-363, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: if \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets, \(0<\lambda<1\), and \((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\) is measurable, then
\[
\bigl|(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B\bigr|_d^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)|A|_d^{1/d}+\lambda|B|_d^{1/d}.
\]
Here \(A=A(p_0,u)\) and \(B=A(p_1,u)\) are nonempty compact sets, and their Minkowski convex combination is compact and therefore measurable. The theorem explicitly permits zero-volume input sets.
