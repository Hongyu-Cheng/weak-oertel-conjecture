## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $U$ be uniform on $\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R_+^{d+1}:\sum_j u_j=1\}$, and for $x\ge0$ put $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$ and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0} t/F_x(t)^{1/d}$. Assume $d\ge2$. Let $X$ be a local maximizer of $H(X)=\sum_i c_i\phi_d(X_i)$ over nonnegative column-stochastic $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ matrices, where $X_i$ is row $i$ and every $c_i>0$. Suppose at least one strictly positive row has a proper minimizing threshold. Let $\lambda>0$ be its weighted capacity gradient and put $S=\sum_j\lambda_j$. For a row $i$ whose only zero is in column $h$, put $g_i=\phi_d(X_i)$ and $\mu_i=c_i g_i/d$. Then
$$
X_{ij}=\frac{\mu_i}{\lambda_j}\quad(j\ne h),\qquad
c_i=d\,\operatorname{GM}_d((\lambda_j)_{j\ne h}),\qquad
d\max_{j\ne h}\lambda_j\le S.
$$
Two such rows with distinct zero columns imply $\lambda_j\le S/d$ for every $j$. For an identically zero row $i$ one has $c_i\le d\,\operatorname{GM}_d((\lambda_j)_{j\ne h})$ for every $h$.

## Proof

The funding row has a locally differentiable capacity and positive gradient by fact d2ba4e3cbd6eb730 and positivity of its actual proper-section centroid, since the section meets the relative interior of the simplex. Consider a one-zero row $i$. Its capacity with its zero fixed equals the geometric mean of its other entries by fact c80576b633835713, final proof paragraph. The derivative of that mean at a positive coordinate $X_{ij}$ is $g_i/(dX_{ij})$. A two-sided transfer between that entry and the funding row's same column therefore gives $\lambda_j=c_i g_i/(dX_{ij})=\mu_i/X_{ij}$. Taking geometric means in $X_{ij}=\mu_i/\lambda_j$ and cancelling $g_i>0$ gives the asserted equality for $c_i$.

A small increase of the zero entry funded in the same column costs $\lambda_h$ to first order in the weighted objective of the funding row. By the one-zero derivative and the positive-coordinate formulas, the weighted gain to first order in row $i$ is
$$
c_i g_i\left(\frac1{\min_{j\ne h} X_{ij}}-\frac1d\sum_{j\ne h}\frac1{X_{ij}}\right)
=d\max_{j\ne h}\lambda_j-\sum_{j\ne h}\lambda_j .
$$
Local maximality bounds this gain by $\lambda_h$. With two distinct zero columns, each index is in at least one of the two complementary index sets, giving the bound for all $j$.

For an identically zero row, transfer $\epsilon z_j$ from the funding row in each column to row $i$ for any $z\ge0$. The cost to first order is $\epsilon\lambda\cdot z$ and the gain is exactly $\epsilon c_i\phi_d(z)$ by homogeneity. In particular choose $z_h=0$ and $z_j=1/\lambda_j$ for $j\ne h$. The one-zero capacity formula gives $c_i/\operatorname{GM}_d((\lambda_j)_{j\ne h})\le d$. This test is equivalent to the transfer inequality restricted to directions with zero at $h$, because for every such direction with all other entries positive, AM–GM gives $\sum_{j\ne h}\lambda_j z_j\ge d\,\operatorname{GM}_d((\lambda_j)_{j\ne h})\phi_d(z)$, and directions with additional zeros have capacity zero.
