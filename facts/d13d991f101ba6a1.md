## Statement

Let \(m=2\), \(L=3\), and \(D=10\). On the complete weak-composition grid \(\mathcal K_3=\lbrace(0,3),(1,2),(2,1),(3,0)\rbrace\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z(a)&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_3}(a\cdot k)^{10},\\
M_0(a)&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_3}k_0(a\cdot k)^{10},\\
F_0(a)&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_3}k_0(k_0-1)(a\cdot k)^{10}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(a=(a_0,a_1)\); the intended weight laws use any \(a_0,a_1>0\), and these expressions are polynomials in those two indeterminates. The registered regularized polynomial at coordinate zero has coefficient
$$
[a_0^{10}a_1^{10}]\bigl(2M_0(a)^2+Z(a)M_0(a)-Z(a)F_0(a)\bigr)
=-1889022724<0.
$$
Thus it is false that all coefficients are nonnegative for every legal \(m,L,D\). Every composition in the original support is included once.

## Proof

Write \(u_j=(j a_0+(3-j)a_1)^{10}\) for \(j=0,1,2,3\). These are exactly the four original composition weights, so exhaustive enumeration gives
$$
Z=u_0+u_1+u_2+u_3,\qquad
M_0=u_1+2u_2+3u_3,\qquad
F_0=2u_2+6u_3.
$$
In particular, \(M_0-F_0=u_1-3u_3\). Expanding only the requested regularized polynomial gives the exact identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
2M_0^2+ZM_0-ZF_0
={}&u_0u_1-3u_0u_3+3u_1^2+9u_1u_2\\
&+10u_1u_3+8u_2^2+21u_2u_3+15u_3^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
The four factors are \(u_0=(3a_1)^{10}\), \(u_1=(a_0+2a_1)^{10}\), \(u_2=(2a_0+a_1)^{10}\), and \(u_3=(3a_0)^{10}\). The binomial theorem therefore supplies every required product coefficient as follows:

| Product | Coefficient of \(a_0^{10}a_1^{10}\) |
| --- | ---: |
| \(u_0u_1\) | \(3^{10}\) |
| \(u_0u_3\) | \(9^{10}\) |
| \(u_1^2\) | \(\binom{20}{10}2^{10}\) |
| \(u_1u_2\) | \(\sum_{r=0}^{10}\binom{10}{r}^2 4^r\) |
| \(u_1u_3\) | \(6^{10}\) |
| \(u_2^2\) | \(\binom{20}{10}2^{10}\) |
| \(u_2u_3\) | \(3^{10}\) |
| \(u_3^2\) | \(0\) |

For the middle product, choosing the exponent \(r\) of \(a_0\) from \(u_1\) forces exponent \(10-r\) from \(u_2\). Its coefficient is initially \(\sum_{r=0}^{10}\binom{10}{r}^2 4^{10-r}\), which equals the displayed sum by replacing \(r\) with \(10-r\). The other rows follow directly by choosing the unique possible exponent or by expanding a single twentieth power.

For complete exact arithmetic, the summands in that finite sum, in order \(r=0,\ldots,10\), are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&1,\ 400,\ 32400,\ 921600,\ 11289600,\ 65028096,\\
&180633600,\ 235929600,\ 132710400,\ 26214400,\ 1048576.
\end{aligned}
$$
Their sum is \(653808673\). Also \(\binom{20}{10}=184756\), \(\binom{20}{10}2^{10}=189190144\), \(9^{10}=3486784401\), \(6^{10}=60466176\), and \(3^{10}=59049\). Taking the coefficient in (1) and grouping the equal entries in the table now gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&[a_0^{10}a_1^{10}]\bigl(2M_0^2+ZM_0-ZF_0\bigr)\\
&\quad=-3\cdot9^{10}
+9\sum_{r=0}^{10}\binom{10}{r}^2 4^r
+11\binom{20}{10}2^{10}
+10\cdot6^{10}+22\cdot3^{10}\\
&\quad=-10460353203+5884278057+2081091584+604661760+1299078\\
&\quad=-1889022724<0.
\end{aligned}
$$
All arithmetic is integer arithmetic. A negative polynomial coefficient is exactly a counterexample to the registered coefficientwise assertion; no evaluation of this polynomial at a positive vector is claimed to be negative.
