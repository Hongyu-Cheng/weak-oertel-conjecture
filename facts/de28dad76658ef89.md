## Statement

Let $C$ be a compact convex set and $L>0$ on $C$ be a nonconstant finite minimum of affine functions. For every $t>0$, $L^{1+t}$ fails concavity on $C$. For every real $c$, $L\log L+cL$ fails concavity on $C$. Moreover, on a fixed suitable segment in $\operatorname{relint} C$, $L\log L$ cannot be the uniform first derivative at $0^+$ of concave feasible paths whose domains retain that segment.

## Proof

Discard coincident relative affine restrictions and separate the finitely many remaining ties by their relative hyperplanes. On the relative-open cells in the complement, $L$ is affine. Some such cell has varying affine value: if each had constant value then continuity and density of the complement would put the image of connected $C$ in a finite set, making $L$ constant. Take a short segment in that cell with $L=A$ strictly varying affine. Along it, $A^{1+t}$ and $A\log A$ are strictly convex by the positive second derivatives of the scalar functions. Addition of $cA$ cannot change the midpoint strict inequality. If concave paths $U_t$ on domains retaining the segment satisfied $U_t=L+tL\log L+o(t)$ uniformly there, their nonnegative midpoint gaps divided by $t$ would converge to the strictly negative midpoint gap of $L\log L$, since the old affine gap is zero. This is impossible. In particular the same argument covers small Hausdorff changes of convex domains that contain a fixed relative-interior neighborhood of the segment.
