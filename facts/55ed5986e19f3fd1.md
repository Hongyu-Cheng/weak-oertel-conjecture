## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be a compact convex body. Let $\mu=\mathcal H^1|_{C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)}$ have mass $M>0$ and suppose exactly $N\le6$ integer fibers have positive length. Over actual integer-base centers $Y$, set $R_*=\max_Y\mu(C\cap(2Y-C))$. Then $R_*\ge M/2$. For exactly four positive fibers the inequality is strict if every fiber has mass strictly below $M/2$, and $R_*/M=1/2$ is attained with four positive fibers. For exactly five positive fibers, $R_*\ge4M/7$ and $\inf R_*/M=4/7$. In particular some actual point has original raw closed-halfspace depth at least $M/4$, or at least $2M/7$ for five positive fibers.

## Proof

Actual convex compatibility makes the positive bases consecutive, and their widths form a concave sequence. For each base the reflection objective is a finite sum of continuous interval overlaps as the center moves on the compact own interval, so a maximum exists. Every own midpoint supplies its own width. An anchor with zero-length own fiber cannot receive positive reflection mass: a positive reflected pair there would give a positive Minkowski midpoint interval in its own fiber. For three symmetric positive bases, use the optimized-triple lemma and ignore the other nonnegative contributions.

For one or two positive fibers, a largest width pays $M/2$. For three, write endpoint widths $l\le A$ and middle width $b$. Unless an own width is already at least $M/2$, one has $b<A+l$ and $A<b+l$. If $b\ge A$, the triple bound supplies $A+2l>(A+b+l)/2$. If $b<A$, it supplies $b+2l>(A+b+l)/2$.

For four, write widths $(a,b,c,d)$ in order, reversing if needed so that $b\ge c$. Then $d\le c$ by concavity. If $a\ge c$, set $L=\max(a,b)$ and $v=\min(a,b)$. The first middle triple gives $R_*\ge v+2c$ and an own fiber gives $R_*\ge L$. If $L<M/2$, then $M/2<v+c+d\le v+2c$. If $a\le c$, the largest width is $b$ and the two middle triples give $R_*\ge c+2\max(a,d)$. If $b<M/2$, then $M/2<a+c+d\le c+2\max(a,d)$. Aligned intervals $[0,w_i]$ with widths $(1,5,3,1)$ are realized by a concave linear interpolation of the upper endpoints. Each middle base has just one reflected pair, so the exact aligned triple formula gives maxima $5,5$ there. The endpoint maxima are $1,1$. Thus $M=10$ and $R_*=5$.

For either five or six positive fibers let $t$ be the third-largest width. The non-strict superlevel set at $t$ is consecutive and contains three consecutive indices, so the triple bound gives $R_*\ge3t$. The strict superlevel set is consecutive and has at most two members. For five fibers use indices $0,\ldots,4$. If $w_2\le t$, paired concavity about $2$ gives $M\le5w_2\le5t$. Otherwise a second largest width can be chosen adjacent to the central index: two strict exceeders are consecutive, while a unique largest at index $2$ has maximum among the other indices at a neighbor by concavity. After reversal the two largest indices are $1,2$. Write the widths $(a,b,c,d,e)$ and $T=\max(a,d)$. Then $b,c\ge T$ and $e\le d$. The triples at bases $1$ and $2$ give $R_*\ge\min(b,c)+2T$. If $b\ge c$, concavity gives $b\le2c-d$, $e\le2d-c$, and thus $M\le a+2c+2d\le2c+3T$. Also $c\le2d\le2T$, so $2c+3T\le(7/4)(c+2T)$. If $c\ge b$, concavity gives $c+e\le2d$ and hence $M\le a+b+3d\le b+4T\le(5/3)(b+2T)$, using $b\ge T$. This proves $R_*\ge4M/7$.

For sharpness with five positive fibers take $[0,w_i]$ with widths $(1+\varepsilon,3+\varepsilon,2+\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ for $\varepsilon>0$. The upper slopes $2,-1,-1,-1$ are concave. At bases $1,3$ the exact aligned-triple maxima are $4+3\varepsilon$ and $1+3\varepsilon$. At base $2$ the inner triple gives at most $4+3\varepsilon$ and the outer pair adds at most $2\varepsilon$. The extremes retain at most $1+\varepsilon$. Thus $R_*\le4+5\varepsilon$ and $M=7+5\varepsilon$, proving the infimum.

For six fibers use indices $0,\ldots,5$ and put $q_i=w_i-2w_{i+1}+w_{i+2}\le0$ for $0\le i\le3$. Direct expansion gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M-3(w_2+w_3)&=q_0+3q_1+3q_2+q_3,\\
M-(w_0+5w_3)&=q_1+3q_2+q_3,\\
M-3(w_1+w_4)&=q_0+q_3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Place the strict superlevel set above $t$ in an adjacent two-index block. Up to reversal the block is $\{0,1\}$, $\{1,2\}$, or $\{2,3\}$. In these cases the first, second, or third identity respectively gives $M\le6t$, since all widths subtracted in that identity are outside the block. This gives $R_*\ge3t\ge M/2$.

At a maximizing center the intersection $C\cap(2Y-C)$ has reflection-invariant mixed-length measure. Every closed ambient halfspace through $Y$ captures at least half that mass, including any mass on its boundary. A containing halfspace contains its translate through $Y$. The original mass in each cap dominates the retained mass, proving the claimed actual depths.
