## Statement

Let n,d be positive integers, R>0, and, for each i in {1,...,n}, let psi_i:[0,infinity) -> [0,infinity) be finite, convex, and nondecreasing, with psi_i(0)=0 and psi_i(1)>0. Let B be a real d-by-n matrix with |B_li|<=1. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
V(x)&=(\psi_1(|x_1|),\ldots,\psi_n(|x_n|))^{\mathsf T},
&E(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^n V_i(x),\\
a(x)&=R-E(x),&c_B(x)&=BV(x),\\
C_B&=\{(x,y):E(x)\le R,\quad
 c_B(x)-a(x)\mathbf1_d\le y\le c_B(x)+a(x)\mathbf1_d\},\\
S_B&=C_B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
&D_+&=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:E(z)<R\},\\
a_z&=R-E(z),&C_{B,z}&=c_B(z)+[-a_z,a_z]^d,\\
T_z&=2a_z I_d,&w_z&=(2a_z)^d,\\
W&=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z,&p_z&=w_z/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Vector inequalities are coordinatewise. The symbols a_z and w_z are respectively the actual cube radius and actual d-dimensional fiber volume. In particular, C_{B,z}=c_B(z)+T_z[-1/2,1/2]^d. When all psi_i equal a single psi, these objects agree exactly with their namesakes in accepted facts 284ed29f0cbbaaa9 and 347548daa8f1f4e7; E is the total energy denoted L in 284ed29f0cbbaaa9. The original volume law p is the a_z^d law q^(d) of 284ed29f0cbbaaa9, with its common factor 2^d cancelled. No other probability law replaces p.

The set C_B is a compact full-dimensional convex body, D_+ is exactly its positive-volume integer support, and W=H_d(S_B)>0, with H_d and halfspace depth h_{S_B} defined as in the locked problem.

Set

\[
\kappa_1=\frac{\exp(-72e^8)}{2304e^8},
\qquad c=\frac{\kappa_1}{4}.
\]

Then c is an absolute constant with 0<c<1/e, and every body in this statement admits one point y^star in S_B, selected before the containing closed halfspace, such that

\[
h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c\,2^{-n}W.
\]

More precisely, if d>=16n^2, the point y^star=(0_n,0_d) has depth at least cW. If d<16n^2, a centerpoint of the continuous-coordinate marginal gives y^star=(0_n,m) with depth at least W/[2(d+1)] and hence at least 2^{-n}W/64.

## Proof

We first check the body and the original normalization. For each i, convexity and psi_i(0)=0 give psi_i(t)<=t psi_i(1) when 0<=t<=1, and psi_i(t)>=t psi_i(1) when t>=1. Thus psi_i is continuous at zero, continuous at positive arguments by finite convexity, and coercive. The function x_i -> psi_i(|x_i|) is convex. For each row l, the lower endpoint of its y interval is

\[
-R+\sum_i(1+B_{li})\psi_i(|x_i|),
\]

which is convex, and the upper endpoint is R-sum_i(1-B_li)psi_i(|x_i|), which is concave. This proves convexity of C_B. Its constraints are closed; its base is bounded by coercivity. Moreover |c_B(x)_l|<=E(x), so |y_l|<=R on C_B. It is therefore compact. All defining inequalities are strict at (0_n,0_d), and continuity gives an ambient neighborhood of that point inside C_B. Hence C_B is full-dimensional.

For E(z)<R the displayed fiber is an actual cube with volume (2a_z)^d. For E(z)=R it is a singleton and has zero d-volume, since d>0. For E(z)>R it is empty. The support D_+ is finite and contains 0_n, with a_0=R and positive volume (2R)^d. The locked mixed-volume formula gives W=H_d(S_B)>0, including all boundary fibers with their zero contributions.

Let Z have exactly the law p, let U be independent and uniform on [-1/2,1/2]^d, and put

\[
Y=BV(Z)+2a_Z U.
\]

Conditional on Z=z, Y is uniform on the actual positive fiber. Therefore, for every measurable ambient set A,

\[
\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in A)=\frac{\mathcal H_d(S_B\cap A)}{W}.
\tag{1}
\]

All subsequent fiber-mass estimates use this original law. Since V(-z)=V(z), the joint law is invariant under (Z,Y) -> (-Z,Y). Its Y marginal is a finite mixture of positive cubes, is absolutely continuous, and is supported on [-R,R]^d. That cube is exactly the origin fiber C_{B,0_n}.

For any m in that origin fiber and any alpha in R^n and beta in R^d, the elementary indicator inequality

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf1_{\{\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{-\alpha\cdot Z+\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}\\
&\hspace{20mm}\ge\mathbf1_{\{\beta\cdot(Y-m)\ge0\}}
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

holds pointwise: if the last scalar is nonnegative, at least one of the first two is nonnegative. Taking expectations and using the invariance proves that every ambient halfspace through (0_n,m) has probability at least one half of its parallel Y-marginal halfspace through m. When beta=0, the latter probability is one. Any closed halfspace containing (0_n,m) contains its parallel halfspace through that point, so the bound holds for all containing halfspaces.

We will use two purely continuous auxiliary assertions proved in accepted fact 347548daa8f1f4e7. Its elementary centerpoint argument shows that an absolutely continuous probability measure supported on the compact cube [-R,R]^d has a point m in that cube at which every containing closed halfspace has probability at least 1/(d+1). Its cube-cap calculation, equation (10) in its Proof, shows that, for the same normalized uniform U used here and any displacement eta satisfying ||eta||_infinity<=1/4 and ||eta||_2<=1,

\[
\mathbb P(v\cdot(U-\eta)\ge0)
\ge \frac{1}{2304e^8}\exp(-72e^8\|\eta\|_2^2)
\ge\kappa_1
\quad(v\ne0).
\tag{3}
\]

This is applicable because that accepted cube calculation depends only on normalized cube coordinates and the displacement, not on a penalty or an exchangeability property. Its allowed norm bound is L_star=sqrt(406272), which includes the bound 1 here, and its displayed inequality retains the actual norm in the exponent. The centerpoint argument likewise uses only the compact support and absolute continuity, both checked above. The normalized cube [-1/2,1/2]^d, the factor T_z=2a_z I_d, and the variable U are exactly the corresponding objects of 347548daa8f1f4e7. Our displacement eta_z below is from the fiber center to the selected origin; it is not the displacement delta_z to B mu used in that predecessor.

To obtain the needed anisotropic contraction moment, fix i and let H_i z replace z_i by sgn(z_i) floor(|z_i|/2), leaving every other coordinate unchanged (and sending zero to zero). Convexity and monotonicity of this particular psi_i give

\[
V_i(H_i z)\le\tfrac12 V_i(z),
\qquad a_{H_i z}\ge a_z+\tfrac12 V_i(z).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus H_i maps D_+ into D_+. Its zero coordinate image has possible preimages -1,0,1, and a nonzero coordinate image has at most two preimages of the same sign. Hence each image has at most four preimages. This is the coordinate contraction used in 284ed29f0cbbaaa9; (4) and the multiplicity statement have just been checked for different psi_i, without using any permutation symmetry.

Suppose d>=4 and write r=V_i(z)/a_z. From (4),

\[
a_z^d r^2\le a_{H_i z}^d\frac{r^2}{(1+r/2)^d}.
\]

For d>2, the logarithmic derivative of r^2/(1+r/2)^d on r>0 is 2/r-d/(2+r), so its maximum occurs at r=4/(d-2) and equals 16(d-2)^{d-2}/d^d. Summing over all preimages and dividing by sum_{z in D_+}a_z^d gives

\[
\mathbb E_p\frac{V_i(Z)^2}{a_Z^2}
\le\frac{64}{d^2}\left(1-\frac2d\right)^{d-2}
\le\frac{16}{d^2}.
\tag{5}
\]

For the last inequality, the logarithmic derivative in real d>2 of (1-2/d)^{d-2} is log(1-2/d)+2/d<0; its value at d=4 is 1/4. This proves the anisotropic version of the coordinate second-moment bound of 284ed29f0cbbaaa9 from its original-volume contraction. None of that fact's pairwise covariance assertions is invoked.

Pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz and (5) imply

\[
\mathbb E_p\frac{E(Z)^2}{a_Z^2}
\le n\sum_i\mathbb E_p\frac{V_i(Z)^2}{a_Z^2}
\le\frac{16n^2}{d^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

Now assume d>=16n^2 and fix y^star=(0_n,0_d) in the origin fiber. For every positive fiber define its normalized displacement to this point by

\[
\eta_z=\frac{0_d-c_B(z)}{2a_z}=-\frac{BV(z)}{2a_z}.
\]

The coefficient bounds imply ||BV(z)||_infinity<=E(z) and ||BV(z)||_2^2<=d E(z)^2. Consequently (6) gives

\[
\mathbb E_p\|\eta_Z\|_\infty^2\le\frac{4n^2}{d^2},
\qquad
\mathbb E_p\|\eta_Z\|_2^2\le\frac{4n^2}{d}.
\]

Markov's inequality and the union bound therefore show

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_p(\|\eta_Z\|_\infty>1/4)&\le\frac{64n^2}{d^2}\le\frac14,\\
\mathbb P_p(\|\eta_Z\|_2^2>1)&\le\frac{4n^2}{d}\le\frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the set G of positive fibers on which ||eta_z||_infinity<=1/4 and ||eta_z||_2<=1 has original mass at least 1/2. For every nonzero beta and z in G,

\[
\mathbb P(\beta\cdot Y\ge0\mid Z=z)
=\mathbb P(\beta\cdot(U-\eta_z)\ge0)\ge\kappa_1,
\]

because Y=2a_z(U-eta_z) and 2a_z>0. Averaging under the original p gives P(beta dot Y>=0)>=kappa_1/2. For beta=0 the probability is one. Equation (2) therefore gives probability at least kappa_1/4=c for every ambient closed halfspace containing the single fixed origin. Equation (1) proves h_{S_B}(y^star)>=cW in this regime.

For every n,d, the marginal centerpoint from 347548daa8f1f4e7, whose hypotheses were checked above, gives m in [-R,R]^d with marginal depth at least 1/(d+1). Fix this m before any halfspace and select y^star=(0_n,m), an actual point of the origin fiber. Equations (2) and (1) give h_{S_B}(y^star)>=W/[2(d+1)].

If d<16n^2, integrality gives d+1<=16n^2, so this depth is at least W/(32n^2). For all positive integers n one has n^2<=2^{n+1}: check n=1,2,3 directly, and for n>=3 use (n+1)^2<=2n^2 for the induction step. Hence

\[
\frac{h_{S_B}(\mathbf y^\star)}{W}
\ge\frac1{32n^2}\ge\frac{2^{-n}}{64}.
\]

Finally 0<c<=1/(9216e^8)<1/64 and c<1/e. The high-dimension lower bound cW is at least c2^{-n}W, and the complementary lower bound 2^{-n}W/64 is also at least c2^{-n}W. Every selected point belongs to S_B and is fixed before all containing halfspaces, and every probability is transferred by the original mixed-volume identity (1). This proves the stated theorem for the whole anisotropic separable cube class.
