## Statement

Let $D$ be a $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$, and let $K\subseteq D$ be a convex body of positive $d$-volume. For distinct indices $j\in J$, take levels $0<c_j\le1/(d+1)$ with $K\subseteq\{\lambda_j\le c_j\}$. Write
$$
r_j=\frac{\mathcal H^{d-1}(K\cap\{\lambda_j=c_j\})}
          {\mathcal H^{d-1}(D\cap\{\lambda_j=c_j\})},\qquad
\alpha_d=1-(1-1/d)^{d-1}.
$$
For $k=|J|\ge1$, every $r_j\le\alpha_d^{k-1}$. Consequently $\sum_{j\in J}r_j\le 2\alpha_d<4/3$, with equality attained by truncating at the centroid levels in exactly two coordinates. For $d=1$ the analogous bound for the two barycentric midpoint cuts is one, attained by a closed half interval, using $\mathcal H^0$ counting measure.

## Proof

On $\lambda_j=c_j$, dividing the remaining barycentric coordinates by $1-c_j$ sends normalized section area to the uniform law $U$ on the standard probability simplex with $d$ coordinates. Under this law, $\mathbb P(U_i>a)=(1-a)^{d-1}$ for $0\le a\le1$, by translating that coordinate by $a$ and dilating the remaining simplex.

For distinct coordinate constraints $U_i\le a_i$, their joint probability is at most the product of their individual probabilities. To see this, condition on one coordinate $U_i=t$. The remaining coordinates are distributed as $(1-t)V$ for a uniform point $V$ on the simplex of the remaining coordinates, by affine disintegration along the sections. The conditional probability of satisfying all the other upper bounds is nondecreasing in $t$, and $\mathbf 1_{\{t\le a_i\}}$ is nonincreasing. Their covariance is nonpositive by writing twice the covariance as the expected product of their increments between two independent copies of $t$. This bounds the joint probability by the probability of the first constraint times the unconditional probability of the rest. Iteration in the original law proves the product bound.

The other $k-1$ outside cuts restrict $U_i\le c_i/(1-c_j)\le1/d$ on $K$'s section. Thus $r_j\le\alpha_d^{k-1}$. The binomial theorem gives $(1+1/(d-1))^{d-1}\ge2$, while $\log(1+x)<x$ for $x>0$ gives $(1-1/d)^{d-1}>e^{-1}>1/3$. Hence $1/2\le\alpha_d<2/3$. For $k\ge2$, the ratio of successive $k\alpha_d^{k-1}$ is at most $3\alpha_d/2<1$, and $2\alpha_d\ge1$ also covers $k=1$. The empty set has sum zero. For the truncation with two centroid levels the interior remains nonempty, since the two chosen barycentric coordinates can be positive and below their levels while the rest are positive. On each imposed section exactly one constraint remains, so the marginal formula gives $r_j=\alpha_d$. In dimension one the two outside constraints have intersection equal to the midpoint, so a positive-length interval has at most one such constraint and its contact counts at most one. A half interval attains one.

For the maximal truncation with $k$ equal levels $c$, the same section computation also gives the exact ratio
$$
r_j=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(-1)^\ell\binom{k-1}{\ell}
       \left(1-\frac{\ell c}{1-c}\right)_+^{d-1}.
$$
Indeed the intersection of any $\ell$ violations translates $\ell$ normalized coordinates down by $c/(1-c)$ and leaves a simplex of the displayed scale, or a null set if the remaining mass is nonpositive. Inclusion–exclusion gives the expression.
