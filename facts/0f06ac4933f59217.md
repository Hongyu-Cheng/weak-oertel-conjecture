## Statement

There is an absolute constant \(c>0\) with the following property. Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer, put \(n=N-1\), and let \(A<B\) be real numbers. For \(1\le i\le n\), let \(u_i:[A,B]\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous and concave, with \(u_i(t)>0\) for \(A<t<B\). Define

\[
P=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
A\le t\le B,\quad 0\le x_i\le u_i(t)\text{ for }1\le i\le n\right\},
\qquad D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N,
\]

and assume \(D\ne\varnothing\). For arbitrary \(h\in\mathbb R\) and arbitrary real \(a_i\ge0\), without any upper bound, assign the original weights

\[
w_{(x,s)}=\exp\!\left(-(s-h)^2-\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i\right),
\qquad W=\sum_{I\in D}w_I.
\]

Then some original integer height \(s^\star\in[A,B]\) and some original point \(J\in D\) with \(J_N=s^\star\) satisfy

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s^\star}}w_I
\ge \frac{cW}{2^N}
\quad\text{for every closed halfspace }H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ containing }J.
\tag{1}
\]

The constant is independent of \(N,A,B,h\), all widths, and all tilt magnitudes. The statement includes noninteger widths, all boundary atoms, zero endpoint widths, and \(N=1\); empty products are one.

## Proof

We use the common-midpoint width construction of accepted `57987d5f01c36f91`, the scalar Gaussian estimate in the proof of accepted `1b53556bfbd3383a`, and the pointwise product-capacity estimates of accepted `ac9b8b86eb2548d3`. For the first two predecessors, their total dimension \(N\) and horizontal dimension \(n\) equal ours, their interval endpoints \(a,b\) correspond to our \(A,B\), and their lower bounds are specialized to \(l_i=0\). Their width \(L_i=u_i-l_i\) is therefore our \(L_i=u_i\). Their original-row symbols \(\mathcal T,E,p_i,n_i,w_s,W_s\) have the same meanings here, with \(p_i(s)=0\) and \(E=\mathcal T\). The horizontal coefficients called \(\beta_i\) in the tilted predecessors correspond to our \(a_i\), and their geometric ratios correspond to our \(q_i=e^{-a_i}\in(0,1]\). From `1b53556bfbd3383a` we use only its scalar estimate (13), whose hypotheses contain no horizontal tilt bound.

Fix \(\theta=1/2+e^{-4}/256\). By (8) of `ac9b8b86eb2548d3`, there is one absolute \(\varepsilon>0\) such that its average capacity \(B_{m,q}(\varepsilon)\) is at least \(\theta\) for every \(m\ge2\) and \(0<q\le1\). Fix this same \(\varepsilon\) in every coordinate and every row. Let \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\in(0,1]\) denote the absolute prefactor in its normalized pointwise depth estimate (23). Define

\[
\alpha=\frac{\log(2\theta)}{2\log12},
\qquad C_\alpha=\frac{2}{1-e^{-\alpha}},
\qquad \rho=\frac{(7/6)^\alpha}{2\theta}.
\tag{2}
\]

These constants are fixed independently of all data. Since \(1/2<\theta<3/4\), we have \(0<\alpha<1/4\). Moreover, \(7/6<12\), so \(\rho<12^\alpha/(2\theta)=1/\sqrt{2\theta}<1\).

Continuity of the widths on a compact interval makes \(D\) finite. Put \(\mathcal T=[A,B]\cap\mathbb Z\). Every height in \(\mathcal T\) is occupied because every coordinate interval contains zero, so \(E=\mathcal T\ne\varnothing\). Define \(n_i(s)=\lfloor u_i(s)\rfloor+1\ge1\). The exact original row at height \(s\in\mathcal T\) is \(\prod_{i=1}^n\{0,\ldots,n_i(s)-1\}\times\{s\}\). For \(m\ge0\), write \(Z_m(q)=\sum_{j=0}^m q^j\), extending the same notation in `ac9b8b86eb2548d3` to \(m=0\). Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_s&=e^{-(s-h)^2},
&T_i(s)&=Z_{n_i(s)-1}(q_i),
&W_s&=w_s\prod_{i=1}^n T_i(s),\\
Q_i(s)&=
\begin{cases}
1,&n_i(s)\in\{1,2\},\\
\max\{1,\theta T_i(s)\},&n_i(s)\ge3,
\end{cases}
&&&Q_s&=w_s\prod_{i=1}^n Q_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(W_s\) is the original row mass and \(W=\sum_{s\in\mathcal T}W_s>0\). The symbols \(Q_i,Q_s\) denote the modified weighted capacities in (3): the maximum with one changes the capacities used in `1b53556bfbd3383a`. They do not denote odd-core cardinalities or masses from `57987d5f01c36f91`.

For every \(s\in\mathcal T\), we claim that some original row point \(J_s\) has true same-row depth at least \(c_{\mathrm{box}}Q_s\). Normalize the original row by its mass \(W_s\). A coordinate with \(m=n_i(s)-1\ge1\) then has exactly the probability \(p_{m,q_i}(j)=q_i^j/Z_m(q_i)\) in `ac9b8b86eb2548d3`. Its endpoint identity (2) gives \(\mathcal R_{m,q_i,\varepsilon}(0)=1/T_i(s)\). If \(m=1\), choose the endpoint zero, with raw capacity \(T_i(s)\mathcal R_{m,q_i,\varepsilon}(0)=1=Q_i(s)\). If \(m\ge2\), choose an integer center maximizing \(\mathcal R_{m,q_i,\varepsilon}\). Its value is at least both the endpoint value \(1/T_i(s)\) and its \(p_{m,q_i}\)-weighted average, which is at least \(\theta\). Its raw capacity is consequently at least \(Q_i(s)\). A singleton coordinate is fixed at zero and contributes its whole mass one.

Apply (23) of `ac9b8b86eb2548d3` to the nonsingleton coordinates with these chosen centers. In that application its dimension is the number of nonsingleton coordinates, its length parameters are the corresponding \(m_i=n_i(s)-1\), and its normalized product measure is the projection of our normalized row onto those coordinates. Thus multiplying its pointwise bound by \(W_s\) gives

\[
\inf_{H\ni J_s}
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s}}w_I
\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}Q_s.
\tag{4}
\]

Indeed, restriction of any containing closed halfspace to the row, and then to its nonsingleton coordinates, is a containing closed halfspace or the entire remaining affine space. This preserves all boundary atoms. If every coordinate is a singleton, the containing halfspace contains the whole row point and (4) follows from \(c_{\mathrm{box}}\le1\). This also handles \(n=0\).

We next compare the masses and capacities coordinatewise. Always \(1\le Q_i(s)\le T_i(s)\le n_i(s)\). If \(n_i(s)\le12\), then

\[
T_i(s)\le2Q_i(s)^{1-\alpha}.
\tag{5}
\]

For one or two sites this follows from \(Q_i(s)=1\) and \(T_i(s)\le2\). For three through twelve sites, \(Q_i(s)\ge\theta T_i(s)\) and \(Q_i(s)\le12\) give \(T_i(s)/Q_i(s)^{1-\alpha}\le12^\alpha/\theta=2/\sqrt{2\theta}<2\).

For \(L\ge0\), introduce the continuous geometric-sum profile

\[
\Psi_q(L)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1-q^{L+1}}{1-q},&0<q<1,\\
L+1,&q=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]

It is positive, increasing, and concave in \(L\). If \(L=L_i(s)\ge6\), the integer site count satisfies \(L\le n_i(s)\le L+1\), and we have the uniform comparison

\[
\frac67\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(s))\le T_i(s)\le\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(s)).
\tag{7}
\]

For \(q_i<1\), apply monotonicity and concavity of \(r\mapsto(1-q_i^r)/(1-q_i)\), which vanishes at zero, at the two arguments \(n_i(s)\) and \(L_i(s)+1\). The upper bound follows from monotonicity; concavity gives the lower bound with factor \(n_i(s)/(L_i(s)+1)\ge L_i(s)/(L_i(s)+1)\ge6/7\). For \(q_i=1\), the same argument is the identity function \(r\mapsto r\). This proves (7) for all permitted tilts, including zero tilt.

At such a wide coordinate there are at least three sites, so \(Q_i(s)\ge\theta T_i(s)\). Since \(0<\alpha<1\), (7) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_i(s)
&\le\theta^{\alpha-1}Q_i(s)^{1-\alpha}
\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(s))^\alpha,\\
\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(s))
&\le\frac{7}{6\theta}Q_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The first inequality is obtained from \(T_i=T_i^{1-\alpha}T_i^\alpha\), using \(T_i\le Q_i/\theta\) in the first factor and \(T_i\le\Psi_{q_i}(L_i)\) in the second. Thus it remains valid when \(Q_i>\theta T_i\).

We now apply the width-pattern construction of `57987d5f01c36f91`, with the aliases already stated. Let \(\tau=(A+B)/2\), \(M_i=\max_{t\in[A,B]}L_i(t)\), and \(G=\{i:M_i\ge12\}\). For \(i\in G\), nonnegativity and concavity give \(L_i(\tau)\ge M_i/2\ge6\), as in its (12). To see the factor one half, join a maximizer on the left of \(\tau\) to \(B\), or a maximizer on the right to \(A\); at \(\tau\) the coefficient of the maximum is at least one half, and the other endpoint value is nonnegative. Therefore every superlevel set \(\{t:L_i(t)\ge6\}\), \(i\in G\), is a closed interval containing \(\tau\).

Put \(A_s=\{i\in G:L_i(s)\ge6\}\). These sets are nested increasing on the integer heights \(s\le\tau\) and nested decreasing on the heights \(s>\tau\). Partition each side into its maximal nonempty consecutive integer blocks on which \(A_s\) is constant. Call the resulting partition \(\mathcal B\). We denote an individual block by \(B_0\), corresponding to the predecessor's block \(B\), to distinguish it from our upper endpoint \(B\). Write \(A_{B_0}\) for its fixed coordinate set and \(k_{B_0}=|A_{B_0}|\). Distinct successive sets in either nested chain have distinct cardinalities. Consequently there are at most two blocks with any given \(k_{B_0}=k\), for \(0\le k\le n\). An integer midpoint belongs only to the left chain, and the threshold inequality includes equality rows.

For \(s\in B_0\), a coordinate outside \(A_{B_0}\) has at most twelve sites. Indeed, if \(i\notin G\), then \(L_i(s)<12\), so \(n_i(s)\le12\). If \(i\in G\setminus A_{B_0}\), then \(L_i(s)<6\), so \(n_i(s)\le6\). Thus (5) applies outside \(A_{B_0}\) and (8) applies inside it.

For each block \(B_0\), define a new weighted profile on \([\min B_0,\max B_0]\) by

\[
\Phi_{B_0}(t)=e^{-(t-h)^2}
\prod_{i\in A_{B_0}}\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(t)).
\tag{9}
\]

This replaces the different profiles \(F_B\) and \(\Phi_B\) of the predecessors. Every active width is at least six throughout this interval, by concavity and its values at the endpoints. Each composition \(\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(t))\) is positive, continuous, and concave: use that \(\Psi_{q_i}\) is increasing and concave and \(L_i\) is concave. Its logarithm is concave because the scalar logarithm is increasing and concave. Hence
\(\log\Phi_{B_0}(t)+t^2=2ht-h^2+\sum_{i\in A_{B_0}}\log\Psi_{q_i}(L_i(t))\) is concave.

The scalar estimate (13) in `1b53556bfbd3383a` states that, for a positive continuous function \(\Phi\) on a compact interval with \(\log\Phi(t)+t^2\) concave, every nonempty subset \(E'\) of its integer points satisfies \(\sum_{s\in E'}\Phi(s)^\alpha\le C_\alpha\max_{s\in E'}\Phi(s)^\alpha\). Its exponent \(\alpha\) and constant \(C_\alpha\) agree exactly with (2). Apply this estimate to (9) with \(E'=B_0\); all these rows are occupied. It also covers singleton blocks.

Let \(Q_* =\max_{s\in\mathcal T}Q_s>0\). Fix a block \(B_0\) and write \(k=k_{B_0}\). Multiplying (5) and (8), while keeping the Gaussian as \(w_s=w_s^{1-\alpha}w_s^\alpha\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_s&\le2^{n-k}\theta^{(\alpha-1)k}
Q_s^{1-\alpha}\Phi_{B_0}(s)^\alpha,\\
\Phi_{B_0}(s)&\le\left(\frac{7}{6\theta}\right)^k Q_s
\qquad(s\in B_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

For the second inequality, use \(Q_i(s)\ge1\) outside the active set and the second inequality in (8) inside it. The Gaussian estimate and (10) now imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\in B_0}W_s
&\le2^{n-k}\theta^{(\alpha-1)k}Q_*^{1-\alpha}
\sum_{s\in B_0}\Phi_{B_0}(s)^\alpha\\
&\le C_\alpha2^{n-k}\theta^{(\alpha-1)k}Q_*^{1-\alpha}
\left(\left(\frac{7}{6\theta}\right)^kQ_*\right)^\alpha\\
&=C_\alpha2^n\rho^kQ_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

There are at most two blocks of each cardinality. Summing (11) over the exact partition of \(\mathcal T\) yields \(W\le C_\alpha2^nQ_*\sum_{k=0}^n2\rho^k\le[2C_\alpha/(1-\rho)]2^nQ_*\).

Choose \(s^\star\in\mathcal T\) attaining \(Q_*\), and take the original center \(J=J_{s^\star}\) from (4). For every containing closed halfspace,

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s^\star}}w_I
\ge c_{\mathrm{box}}Q_*
\ge\frac{c_{\mathrm{box}}(1-\rho)}{2C_\alpha}\frac{W}{2^n}
=\frac{c_{\mathrm{box}}(1-\rho)}{C_\alpha}\frac{W}{2^N}.
\]

Thus (1) holds with the absolute positive constant \(c=c_{\mathrm{box}}(1-\rho)/C_\alpha\). For \(N=1\), all horizontal products are empty, the rows and their centers are original singleton points, and the partition has at most two blocks of cardinality zero, so the same argument applies. A zero endpoint width contributes its original coordinate point zero; all other row endpoints are included by the exact floor formula. Every center and every weight used above belongs to the original support.
