## Statement

Use the hypotheses and notation of the saturated fixed-facet response lemma and the saturated menu loss-envelope lemma. Suppose the common old raw budget $Q$ is the global maximal raw depth, and the ordinary raw endpoint depth maxima satisfy $m_A<Q$ and $m_B=Q$. Write $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $b=|B|$, and $r=Q/(a+k+b)$. For each $\theta\in\Theta$ define
$$
\mathcal L_\theta(p)=\frac{\sum_i w_i^\theta(dU_i+\theta_i\sigma_i c_i)}{W_\theta},
\qquad
\mathcal J_\theta(q)=\frac{\sum_i w_i^\theta\theta_i\sigma_i e_i}{W_\theta}.
$$
Let $\theta^\infty$ be one on proper and full-at-minimum cuts and zero on empty and strict exterior cuts. For fixed actual centers $p,q$ there exist nonnegative rates, at least one positive, for which both the normalized middle menu bound and the normalized tied endpoint bound strictly decrease to first order if and only if
$$
\Phi(daQ;a+k,a)<0\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathcal J_{\theta^\infty}(q)>drb.
$$
A strictly negative boundary trial allows positive $u$ and a finite $v/u>a/(a+k)$. The second alternative is exactly the test for $u=0,v=1$. If all A cuts are proper, the boundary alternative is $(a+k)\mathcal L_\theta(p)+a\mathcal J_\theta(q)>daQ$ with the sole branch $\theta_i=1$.

## Proof

Every endpoint's actual mixed depth equals its ordinary depth, since a large finite base tilt extends any containing cap on the endpoint to miss both other compact fibers and every ambient cap pays its own trace. Homothetic contraction scales the ordinary endpoint maxima by $(1-ut)^d$ and $(1-vt)^d$ respectively. The middle menu provides an upper bound $Q(t)$ on the middle maximum. The A bound has a strict initial gap, and the other two bounds start at $Q$. The total mass derivative is $-d(au+bv)$, so the two required normalized derivative inequalities are
$$
L(u,v)>dr(au+bv),\qquad Qv>r(au+bv).
$$
For $u>0$, divide by $u$ and write $\tau=v/u$. The second condition becomes $\tau>\tau_0=a/(a+k)$. The envelope gives the first condition as
$$
\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigl[\mathcal L_\theta(p)-dra+\tau(\mathcal J_\theta(q)-drb)\bigr]>0.
$$
A finite maximum of affine functions is positive somewhere to the right of $\tau_0$ exactly when a branch is positive at $\tau_0$ or a branch has strictly positive slope. If the boundary value is positive, continuity gives a nearby finite admissible ratio. A positive slope eventually makes its branch positive. If both fail, every branch is nonpositive at $\tau_0$ and nonincreasing on the ray.

By homogeneity the boundary test uses rates $(a+k,a)$, where the trial loss is $dr[a(a+k)+ba]=daQ$. Monotonicity of $\Phi$ in its first argument makes strict boundary improvement equivalent to the stated negative trial value. Equivalently calculate the trial using the sign of $H_i=2daQ-2d(a+k)U_i+k_i[(a+k)c_i+a e_i]$ to select $\theta_i=1$ for $H_i>0$ and zero for $H_i<0$ on full-at-minimum cuts. The two branches agree at zero. Their sum is $\sum_i w_i^\theta[daQ-d(a+k)U_i-\theta_i\sigma_i((a+k)c_i+a e_i)]$. In the all-proper case the unique weights are independent of the trial, giving the claimed specialization.

For the eventual-slope condition, the maximum of the $\mathcal J_\theta$ equals $L(0,1)$ by the envelope. On this actual nonnegative loss, every full-at-minimum hinge has argument $2L+k_i e_i\ge0$, so $\theta^\infty$ is a valid inverse branch even at a tie and $L(0,1)=\mathcal J_{\theta^\infty}(q)$. This is the slope test and gives the actual B-only alternative, whose tied-endpoint inequality holds automatically because $Q>rb$.
