## Statement

For a uniform barycentric point $\Xi$ of a tetrahedron let $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot\Xi\le t)$ and $\phi_3(x)=\inf_{t>0,\,F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$. If $x=(a,a,b,b)$, $0<a<b$, and $c=b-a$, the unique minimizing threshold has
$$
u_*=\frac{t_*-a}{c}=\frac{2a}{a+b},\qquad
t_*=\frac{a(3b-a)}{a+b},\qquad
F_x(t_*)=\frac{4a^2(3b-a)}{(a+b)^3},\qquad
\phi_3(x)^3=\frac{a(3b-a)^2}{4}.
$$
The optimized capacity is $C^2$ near $x$. Its Hessian bilinear form has mutually orthogonal subspaces spanned by the two split directions $(1,-1,0,0)$, $(0,0,1,-1)$ and the common-pair plane $(da,da,db,db)$. The split second derivatives are $-t_*F_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}/[3F_x(t_*)^{4/3}]$, where $F_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}$ is $2(1-u_*)^2(1-4u_*)/c^2$ and $2u_*^2(3-4u_*)/c^2$, respectively. The common-pair quadratic form is $-(2\phi_3/9)[da/a-(3db-da)/(3b-a)]^2$.

Inertia in the order positive, negative, zero is $(0,3,1),(0,2,2),(1,2,1),(1,1,2),(2,1,1)$ as $a/b$ ranges over $(0,1/7),\{1/7\},(1/7,3/5),\{3/5\},(3/5,1)$, respectively. At $(a,b)=(1/16,1/2)$ one has $u_*=2/9,t_*=23/144,F_x(t_*)=92/729,\phi_3(x)^3=529/16384$ and actual section centroid $(7/18,7/18,1/9,1/9)$.

## Proof

By accepted fact 290ddd8516d76865 with $d=3,k=2$, $U=\Xi_3+\Xi_4$ has distribution function $H(u)=3u^2-2u^3$ on $[0,1]$, with indices numbered from one. For $a<t<b$, write $u=(t-a)/c$. The derivative of $t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$ has the sign of
$$
F_x(t)-tF_x'(t)/3=u[u-2(a/c)(1-u)].
$$
This changes sign strictly from negative to positive at $u_*$ with positive derivative there. For $t\le a$ the distribution has zero mass and for $t\ge b$ the objective is $t$. This proves uniqueness and a nondegenerate minimum, and substitution gives the displayed values.

Within each pair condition on its proportion $R_{\rm low}=\Xi_1/(\Xi_1+\Xi_2)$ and $R_{\rm high}=\Xi_3/(\Xi_3+\Xi_4)$. In these coordinates the uniform tetrahedral density is proportional to $U(1-U)$ times the two uniform proportion densities by the barycentric change of variables, so the proportions are independent uniform variables independent of $U$. For any perturbed row $x'$ let $L$ and $B$ be its two conditional pair heights, the convex combinations of its low and high entries with these proportions. In a neighborhood of $(x,t_*)$, $L<t<B$ uniformly in the proportions. Thus $F_{x'}(t)=\mathbb E H((t-L)/(B-L))$ is jointly smooth there, and the implicit-function theorem applies to its objective's threshold stationarity equation.

To see that the local branch still minimizes globally, let $\eta=\lVert x'-x\rVert_\infty$. Then $F_x(t-\eta)\le F_{x'}(t)\le F_x(t+\eta)$. For positive-mass thresholds $t\le a+\delta$ the objective is at least $(a-\eta)/F_x(a+\delta+\eta)^{1/3}$, which exceeds the objective at $t_*$ for small fixed $\delta>0$ and all sufficiently small $\eta$. Thresholds above $b+1$ also exceed it. On the remaining compact interval $[a+\delta,b+1]$ the inequalities imply uniform convergence of the objective, as the baseline CDF is continuous and bounded away from zero on a small enlargement. All minimizers are therefore near the unique baseline minimum. They coincide with the unique implicit-function branch there. This proves local $C^2$ regularity of the optimized capacity.

For the low-pair split, take $L=a+\varepsilon D$, $B=b$ where $D=2R_{\rm low}-1$ has $\mathbb E D=0$, $\mathbb E D^2=1/3$. The first and second derivatives of $(t-L)/(B-L)$ at zero are $(u-1)D/c$ and $2(u-1)D^2/c^2$. Differentiating the expectation and using $H'(u)=6u(1-u)$, $H''(u)=6(1-2u)$ gives $2(1-u)^2(1-4u)/c^2$. For the high-pair split use $L=a$, $B=b+\varepsilon D$ and the other proportion. The ratio derivatives are $-uD/c$ and $2uD^2/c^2$, giving $2u^2(3-4u)/c^2$. In either split the objective is even in $\varepsilon$ for every nearby $t$, so its mixed threshold-split derivative vanishes at zero and the optimizing threshold has zero first derivative. Differentiating the optimized value twice then gives the split formula in the statement.

The pair exchanges leave the capacity unchanged, decoupling the two split lines and common-pair plane in its Hessian. Differentiating its restriction $4^{-1/3}a^{1/3}(3b-a)^{2/3}$ twice gives the stated common-pair quadratic form, with only the radial kernel in that plane. The split signs switch from negative to positive at $u_*=1/4$ and $u_*=3/4$, equivalent to $a/b=1/7$ and $a/b=3/5$. This gives the five inertias. Finally the section $U=u_*$ has conditional centroid $((1-u_*)/2,(1-u_*)/2,u_*/2,u_*/2)$ by exchange symmetry and the fixed pair totals. The calibration follows by substitution.
