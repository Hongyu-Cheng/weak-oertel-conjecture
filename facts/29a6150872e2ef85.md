## Statement

Let $1/2\le\theta<1$, $0\le b\le1-\theta$, $a\ge0$, and let $m\ge0$ be an integer with $am\le1$. For $0\le h\le m$ put
$$
\rho_h=1-ah,\qquad L_h=1-\frac{a(1-b)h}{\theta},\qquad B_h=1-\frac{ab}{\theta}-\frac{a(1+b)h}{\theta}.
$$
For every $x\in(0,1]$ the combined lists $(L_h)$ and $(B_h)$ have at least as many entries at least $x$ as $(\rho_h)$, counting multiplicities. Hence for every real $p>0$,
$$
\sum_{h=0}^m\big[(L_h)_+^p+(B_h)_+^p\big]\ \ge\ \sum_{h=0}^m\rho_h^p ,
\qquad v_+=\max(v,0).
$$
For $m=0$, equality in the power sum holds exactly when $ab\ge\theta$. For $m\ge1$, equality holds exactly when $m=1$, $a=1$ and $b=\theta=1/2$.

## Proof

First, for integers $k\ge1$ and $0\le b\le1/2$,
$$
\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2(1-b)}\right\rfloor+
\left\lfloor\frac{k-2b}{2(1+b)}\right\rfloor\ \ge\ k-1.
$$
Otherwise let $j$ and $\ell$ be the respective floors plus one. Then $j+\ell\le k$, while the strict upper bounds by the next integers give
$$
b<1-\frac{k}{2j},\qquad b>\frac{k-2\ell}{2(\ell+1)}.
$$
The first and $b\ge0$ imply $j>k/2$, so $\ell<k/2$ and $k-\ell\ge\ell+1$. Since $j\le k-\ell$ and $k-2\ell>0$,
$$
b<\frac{k-2\ell}{2(k-\ell)}\le\frac{k-2\ell}{2(\ell+1)},
$$
a contradiction. Both denominators in the floor inequality are positive, including at $b=0$ and $b=1/2$.

If $a=0$, all entries in each list are one, and the count and strict power inequality follow. Otherwise fix $x\in(0,1]$ and set $k=\min(m,\lfloor(1-x)/a\rfloor)$. There are exactly $k+1$ target entries at least $x$. For $k=0$ the term $L_0=1$ suffices. For $k\ge1$, since $(1-x)/a\ge k$, there are at least
$$
1+\left\lfloor\frac{\theta k}{1-b}\right\rfloor,\qquad
1+\left\lfloor\frac{\theta k-b}{1+b}\right\rfloor
$$
entries at least $x$ from the two lists, respectively. Both arguments of the floors belong to $[0,k]$ because $b\le1-\theta\le\theta$ and $k\ge1$. These counts therefore use only indices up to $m$. Each argument is at least its value at $\theta=1/2$, so the floor inequality makes the combined count at least $k+1$. Integrating the count with weight $p x^{p-1}$ over $(0,1)$ gives the stated power inequality, since all positive entries are at most one and $\rho_h\ge0$.

For equality when $m=0$, the only extra power is $(1-ab/\theta)_+^p$. Suppose $m\ge1$ and $a>0$. If $b<\theta$, then $B_0>1-a\ge0$. On the positive-length interval $(1-a,B_0]$ the target count is one, while $L_0$ and $B_0$ already give two. The integral is strict. The remaining possibility $b=\theta$ forces $\theta=b=1/2$. Then $L_h=\rho_h$ for every $h$ and $B_0=1-a$. If $a<1$ its power is positive. If $a=1$, the prefix condition forces $m=1$ and all $B_h$ are nonpositive, giving equality.
