## Statement

Let \(D\geq1\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^D\) be a convex body of volume \(a>0\), let
\[
c:=\frac1a\int_Kx\,dx,
\]
and, for \(0<r\leq1\), use the canonical metronoid normalization
\[
\mathsf M_{ra}(K):=
\left\{
\frac1{ra}\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_Kf\,dx=ra
\right\}.
\]
For \(0<r<1\), set
\[
\psi(r):=-\frac{1-r}{r}\log(1-r).
\]
Then, for every \(0<p<q<1\),
\[
c+\frac{\psi(p)}{\psi(q)}
   \bigl(\mathsf M_{qa}(K)-c\bigr)
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{pa}(K).
\tag{1}
\]
The zero-mass endpoint of (1) is
\[
c+\frac1{\psi(q)}
   \bigl(\mathsf M_{qa}(K)-c\bigr)
\subseteq K.
\tag{2}
\]
For each fixed \(0<p<q<1\), the coefficient \(\psi(p)/\psi(q)\) in (1) is the largest coefficient valid simultaneously in every dimension and for every convex body.  Likewise \(1/\psi(q)\) is the largest dimension-free coefficient in (2).  In particular,
\[
c+\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}
   \bigl(\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)-c\bigr)
\subseteq\mathsf M_{a/4}(K),
\tag{3}
\]
and hence
\[
c+\frac65\bigl(\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)-c\bigr)
\subseteq\mathsf M_{a/4}(K),
\qquad
c+\frac1{\log2}\bigl(\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)-c\bigr)
\subseteq K.
\tag{4}
\]

Now let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the canonical notation
\[
C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
\]
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}
\qquad(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ Borel}),
\]
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{y\in S}\inf_{H\ni y}\mu_C(H),\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and assume \(W>0\).  Here the infimum is over ambient closed halfspaces.  For \(z\in Z\), let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\), and put
\[
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\tag{5}
\]
By accepted facts `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` and `107b9182522633f9`, every \(D_z\) is an origin-symmetric convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\).

Let \(q=(q_e)\) be a finite family of nonnegative weights on unordered off-diagonal same-parity pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z\), so \(z\ne z'\) and \(z\equiv z'\pmod2\), and suppose
\[
d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\leq\gamma_da_z
\qquad(z\in Z).
\tag{6}
\]
Write
\[
F:=\{e:q_e>0\},\qquad Q:=|q|:=\sum_eq_e,
\]
and, for \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F\), define
\[
m_e:=\frac{z+z'}2\in\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
\bar c_e:=\frac{c_z+c_{z'}}2,
\]
\[
D_e:=\frac12(D_z+D_{z'}),\qquad
L_e:=\bar c_e+D_e
     =\frac12(\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'}).
\tag{7}
\]
The Minkowski functional \(\|\cdot\|_{D_e}\) is a norm.  Define the weighted affine defect by
\[
\Delta_C(q):=
\min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\sum_{e\in F}q_e
\left(\|s(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\right)_+,
\tag{8}
\]
with \(\Delta_C(q)=0\) when \(F=\varnothing\).  The minimum in (8) is attained.

For \(0<p<\gamma_d\), put
\[
\delta_p:=\frac{\psi(p)}{\log2}-1>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq
p\,2^{-n}
\left(Q-\frac{\Delta_C(q)}{\delta_p}\right)_+ .}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq
\frac14\,2^{-n}\bigl(Q-5\Delta_C(q)\bigr)_+.}
\tag{11}
\]

For the final consequence, define the absolute depth of \(x\in C_z\) in its fiber by
\[
\delta_C(z,x):=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ a proper closed halfspace},\ x\in J\}.
\]
Define the accepted residual-selector capacity explicitly by
\[
R_{\rm sel}(C):=
\sup 2^{n-\rho_E}
\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{r_s+1},
\tag{12}
\]
where the supremum is over nonempty \(E\subseteq Z\), points \(x_z\in C_z\), weights \(0<w_z\leq\delta_C(z,x_z)\), and affine maps \(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\), with
\[
\rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),\qquad
r_s:=\dim\operatorname{span}\{x_z-s(z):z\in E\}.
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
B_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}\gamma_da_z,\qquad
\beta_\xi:=\max_{z\equiv\xi}\gamma_da_z,
\qquad
U_\xi:=\max\{2\beta_\xi-B_\xi,0\},
\tag{13}
\]
with all three quantities zero for an empty class.  Call \(q\) an exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching if there are unmatched weights \(u_z\geq0\) such that
\[
\gamma_da_z=u_z+\sum_{e\ni z}q_e,
\qquad
\sum_{z\equiv\xi}u_z=U_\xi
\quad\text{for every }\xi.
\tag{14}
\]
Accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` proves existence of such matchings, the exact identity
\[
Q=\frac12\left(\gamma_dW-\sum_\xi U_\xi\right),
\tag{15}
\]
and the two inequalities
\[
Q\geq\frac{\gamma_dW-R_{\rm sel}(C)}2,
\qquad
h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}R_{\rm sel}(C).
\tag{16}
\]

Let \((C_j)\) be any sequence, with possibly varying \(n_j,d_j\), such that \(W_j>0\) and
\[
H_j:=\frac{2^{n_j}h_{\max}(C_j)}{W_j}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]
For each \(j\), let \(q_j\) be any exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching and define \(\Delta_{C_j}(q_j)\) by (8).  Then \(Q_j:=|q_j|>0\) for all sufficiently large \(j\), and
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{j\to\infty}
\frac{\Delta_{C_j}(q_j)}{Q_j}
\geq\frac1{\log2}-1.}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus every sequence witnessing failure of an absolute weak-Oertel constant has a fixed positive relative affine margin; zero-margin tangency is impossible.

## Proof

We first prove the metronoid nesting theorem, including all marginal regularity and endpoint points used below.

Let \(X\) be uniform on \(K\).  Fix \(u\in\mathbb R^D\setminus\{0\}\), put \(Y:=\langle u,X\rangle\), let \(F\) be its distribution function, and let
\[
Q(s):=\inf\{t:F(t)\geq s\},\qquad 0<s<1,
\]
be its quantile.  The support of \(Y\) is a nondegenerate compact interval \([A,B]\), and \(Q\) is bounded.

We record carefully why the one-dimensional density used below has the required regularity.  If \(D=1\), then \(K\) is an interval and \(Y\) is uniform on an interval.  Its density is constant on the interior of its support.  Suppose \(D\geq2\).  After identifying each hyperplane \(\{x:\langle u,x\rangle=t\}\) with \(u^\perp\), write \(K_t\) for the corresponding section.  Convexity gives
\[
(1-\lambda)K_{t_0}+\lambda K_{t_1}
\subseteq K_{(1-\lambda)t_0+\lambda t_1}.
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality in \(u^\perp\cong\mathbb R^{D-1}\) therefore shows that
\[
t\longmapsto\operatorname{vol}_{D-1}(K_t)^{1/(D-1)}
\]
is concave on \((A,B)\).  A nonnegative concave function is log-concave by weighted arithmetic-geometric mean, so the normalized section-volume density \(f\) of \(Y\) is log-concave.  It is positive and continuous on \((A,B)\).  This also holds in the already separated \(D=1\) case.

On \((0,1)\), set
\[
I(s):=f(Q(s)).
\tag{19}
\]
The function \(I\) is positive and concave.  Here is a nonsmooth justification.  On every compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), \(Q=F^{-1}\) is absolutely continuous and
\[
Q'(s)=\frac1{f(Q(s))}=\frac1{I(s)}
\quad\text{for a.e. }s.
\tag{20}
\]
The finite concave function \(\log f\) is locally Lipschitz and has nonincreasing one-sided slopes.  Since \(F'=f>0\) and \(f\) is continuous on the support interior, \(Q\) is continuously differentiable locally.  The one-sided chain rule gives
\[
I'_\pm(s)
=\frac{f'_\pm(Q(s))}{f(Q(s))}
=(\log f)'_\pm(Q(s)).
\]
Since \(Q\) is increasing and the one-sided slopes of \(\log f\) are nonincreasing, the one-sided slopes of \(I\) are nonincreasing in \(s\); the one-sided-slope characterization of concavity gives concavity of \(I\).  In the uniform case \(I\) is constant, so the same conclusion holds.  The finite nonnegative endpoint limit \(I(0+)\) may be adjoined.  Hence, for \(0<s<t<1\), concavity gives
\[
I(s)\geq\frac{s}{t}I(t),
\]
and consequently
\[
g(s):=\frac{s}{I(s)}
\quad\text{is nondecreasing on }(0,1).
\tag{21}
\]

For \(0<r<1\), define the centered upper-tail average
\[
T_r(Y):=
\frac1r\int_{1-r}^1Q(s)\,ds-
\int_0^1Q(s)\,ds.
\tag{22}
\]
Let
\[
k_r(s):=
\begin{cases}
s,&0<s\leq1-r,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{1-r}{r}(1-s),&1-r<s<1.
\end{cases}
\]
Integration by parts first on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), followed by \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), is legitimate because \(Q\) is bounded, \(Q\) is locally absolutely continuous, and \(k_r(0)=k_r(1)=0\).  It gives
\[
T_r(Y)=\int_0^1k_r(s)Q'(s)\,ds
=\int_0^1A_r(s)g(s)\,ds,
\tag{23}
\]
where
\[
A_r(s):=
\begin{cases}
1,&0<s\leq1-r,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{(1-r)(1-s)}{rs},&1-r<s<1.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
\]
This also proves integrability at both endpoints rather than assuming it.

Fix \(0<p<q<1\).  The ratio \(R:=A_p/A_q\) is nondecreasing, because
\[
R(s)=
\begin{cases}
1,&s\leq1-q,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{qs}{(1-q)(1-s)},&1-q<s\leq1-p,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{q(1-p)}{p(1-q)},&s>1-p.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]
Both \(R\) and \(g\) are nondecreasing.  Applying the elementary covariance identity
\[
\int Rg\,d\nu-
\left(\int R\,d\nu\right)
\left(\int g\,d\nu\right)
=\frac12\iint(R(s)-R(t))(g(s)-g(t))\,d\nu(s)d\nu(t)\geq0
\]
to the probability measure proportional to \(A_q(s)\,ds\), we obtain
\[
T_p(Y)\geq
\frac{\int_0^1A_p(s)\,ds}{\int_0^1A_q(s)\,ds}
T_q(Y).
\tag{26}
\]
A direct endpoint-safe computation gives
\[
\int_0^1A_r(s)\,ds
=1-r+\frac{1-r}{r}
\int_{1-r}^1\left(\frac1s-1\right)ds
=-\frac{1-r}{r}\log(1-r)
=\psi(r).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus
\[
T_p(Y)\geq\frac{\psi(p)}{\psi(q)}T_q(Y).
\tag{28}
\]

Accepted fact `6e64ae6cfa5cab7e`, applied to the uniform probability law on \(K\), gives the exact support formula
\[
h_{\mathsf M_{ra}(K)}(u)
=\frac1r\int_{1-r}^1Q(s)\,ds,
\qquad
\langle u,c\rangle=\int_0^1Q(s)\,ds.
\tag{29}
\]
Therefore (28) is precisely
\[
h_{\mathsf M_{pa}(K)-c}(u)
\geq\frac{\psi(p)}{\psi(q)}
h_{\mathsf M_{qa}(K)-c}(u).
\]
It is trivial for \(u=0\).  Since the metronoids are compact convex sets by the same accepted fact, support-function comparison proves (1).

As \(p\downarrow0\), the upper-\(p\) quantile average in (29) tends to the essential supremum of \(Y\), which equals \(h_K(u)\), and
\[
\psi(p)=\int_0^1\frac{1-p}{1-pt}\,dt\longrightarrow1.
\tag{30}
\]
Taking the limit in the support inequality proves (2), with no assertion that a zero-mass metronoid exists.  Formula (30) also shows directly that \(\psi\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\), so \(\delta_p>0\) whenever \(p<1/2\).

We next prove sharpness.  For every integer \(D\geq2\), consider the \(D\)-dimensional cone
\[
K_D:=\{(y,x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{D-1}:
0\leq y\leq1,\ \|x\|_2\leq y\}.
\]
For the height functional \(u(y,x)=y\), the uniform marginal has density \(D y^{D-1}\) on \([0,1]\), hence quantile \(Q_D(s)=s^{1/D}\) and mean \(D/(D+1)\).  Its centered upper-tail excess is
\[
T_r^{(D)}
=\frac{D}{D+1}\frac{1-r}{r}
\left(1-(1-r)^{1/D}\right).
\tag{31}
\]
Consequently
\[
\lim_{D\to\infty}
\frac{T_p^{(D)}}{T_q^{(D)}}
=\frac{\psi(p)}{\psi(q)}.
\tag{32}
\]
Any larger dimension-free coefficient in (1) would violate the support inequality in direction \(u\) for all sufficiently large \(D\).  Also
\[
h_{K_D-c_D}(u)=\frac1{D+1},
\qquad
\lim_{D\to\infty}
\frac{h_{K_D-c_D}(u)}{T_q^{(D)}}
=\frac1{\psi(q)},
\]
which proves endpoint sharpness.  Finally, \(\psi(1/2)=\log2\) and \(\psi(1/4)=3\log(4/3)\), proving (3).  The inequality
\[
\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}>\frac65
\]
is equivalent to \(2^{24}>3^{15}\), so (4) follows from (2)-(3).

We turn to the affine-defect application.  Accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` proves that \(D_z=\mathcal K_z-c_z\) is compact, convex, and origin-symmetric.  Accepted fact `107b9182522633f9` gives an origin-symmetric covariance-polar convex body \(\mathcal E_z\subseteq D_z\).  Hence \(D_z\), and therefore every \(D_e=(D_z+D_{z'})/2\), has the origin in its interior and is the unit ball of a norm.

For \(F\ne\varnothing\), let \(Y:=(\mathbb R^d)^F\), let
\[
V:=\{(s(m_e))_{e\in F}:s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\text{ affine}\},
\qquad
L:=\prod_{e\in F}L_e,
\]
and equip \(Y\) with the norm
\[
N_q((r_e)_e):=\sum_{e\in F}q_e\|r_e\|_{D_e}.
\]
Since the distance from a point \(y\) to \(\bar c_e+D_e\) in the norm with unit ball \(D_e\) is
\[
\operatorname{dist}_{D_e}(y,L_e)
=\left(\|y-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\right)_+,
\]
coordinatewise minimization gives
\[
\Delta_C(q)=\inf_{v\in V,\ \ell\in L}N_q(v-\ell).
\tag{33}
\]
The set \(V\) is a closed finite-dimensional subspace and \(L\) is compact.  For each \(\ell\in L\), the continuous coercive function \(v\mapsto N_q(v-\ell)\) on \(V\) attains its minimum; the resulting distance-to-\(V\) function is continuous in \(\ell\), so compactness of \(L\) proves joint attainment in (33).  An attaining evaluation vector belongs to \(V\), hence is induced by an affine map.  This proves the attainment assertion in (8), including lower-rank midpoint configurations.  The empty case was defined separately.

We need one lower-mass version of the affine matching argument.  Let \(0<r\leq1\), let \(\widehat q\) be an off-diagonal same-parity matching with
\[
d_z(\widehat q)\leq r a_z,
\tag{34}
\]
and suppose one affine map \(s\) satisfies, on every positive edge \(e=\{z,z'\}\),
\[
s(m_e)\in\frac12
\left(\mathsf M_{ra_z}(C_z)+\mathsf M_{ra_{z'}}(C_{z'})\right).
\tag{35}
\]
Then
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}|\widehat q|.
\tag{36}
\]
Indeed, choose endpoint witnesses in the two metronoids in (35).  The base-centerpoint step in the affine matching proof of accepted fact `8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5` applies to the finite measure \(\sum_e\widehat q_e\delta_{m_e}\): it gives an integral base point \(m_0\) having at least \(2^{-n}|\widehat q|\) in every containing base halfspace.  The graph point \((m_0,s(m_0))\) lies in \(C\), exactly as in that accepted proof.  For any ambient closed halfspace containing this graph point, pull it back to the graph, keep the edges whose midpoints lie in the resulting base halfspace, and orient each such edge toward one endpoint witness lying in the ambient halfspace.  The oriented load \(R_z\) entering fiber \(z\) satisfies
\[
R_z\leq d_z(\widehat q)\leq r a_z.
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives absolute fiber depth at least \(\gamma_d r a_z\geq\gamma_dR_z\) at every point of \(\mathsf M_{ra_z}(C_z)\).  Summing once over the fibers gives ambient halfspace mass at least
\[
\gamma_d\sum_zR_z
\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}|\widehat q|.
\]
This proves (36), including vertical halfspaces and boundary containment, by the same orientation calculation as the accepted half-mass case.

Because
\[
\gamma_d=(1+1/d)^{-d},
\]
the binomial theorem and \(\log(1+x)<x\) give
\[
e^{-1}<\gamma_d\leq\frac12.
\tag{37}
\]
Fix \(0<p<\gamma_d\), and let \(s\) attain (8).  Call an edge good if
\[
\left(\|s(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\right)_+\leq\delta_p.
\]
The weighted Markov inequality gives
\[
\sum_{e\text{ bad}}q_e\leq\frac{\Delta_C(q)}{\delta_p}.
\tag{38}
\]
For a good edge,
\[
s(m_e)\in\bar c_e+(1+\delta_p)D_e.
\tag{39}
\]
Apply (1) in each endpoint fiber with \(q=1/2\).  Since \(1+\delta_p=\psi(p)/\log2\),
\[
c_z+(1+\delta_p)D_z
\subseteq\mathsf M_{pa_z}(C_z).
\tag{40}
\]
Taking the half-sum of (40) for the endpoints of (39) gives
\[
s(m_e)\in\frac12
\left(\mathsf M_{pa_z}(C_z)+\mathsf M_{pa_{z'}}(C_{z'})\right)
\quad(e\text{ good}).
\tag{41}
\]
Restrict \(q\) to its good edges and scale it by \(p/\gamma_d\):
\[
\widehat q_e:=\frac p{\gamma_d}q_e\mathbf1_{\{e\ {\rm good}\}}.
\]
By (6), \(d_z(\widehat q)\leq pa_z\), so (36), (38), and (41) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C)
&\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}|\widehat q|\\
&=p2^{-n}\sum_{e\ {\rm good}}q_e\\
&\geq p2^{-n}
\left(Q-\frac{\Delta_C(q)}{\delta_p}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
When the last parenthesis is negative, the asserted positive-part form follows from \(h_{\max}(C)\geq0\).  This proves (10).

For \(p=1/4\), equation (3) gives \(\delta_{1/4}>1/5\), and (37) gives \(1/4<\gamma_d\).  Therefore
\[
Q-\frac{\Delta_C(q)}{\delta_{1/4}}
\geq Q-5\Delta_C(q),
\]
and (11) follows.

It remains to prove the counterexample-sequence conclusion.  The exact minimum-unmatched matching assertions (13)-(16), including their quantifiers over every choice attaining the minimum unmatched mass, are the direct conclusions of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`.  Divide the second inequality in (16) by \(2^{-n}W\).  For the sequence (17), it gives
\[
\frac{R_{\rm sel}(C_j)}{W_j}\leq H_j.
\tag{42}
\]
The first inequality in (16), (37), and (42) then give
\[
x_j:=\frac{Q_j}{W_j}
\geq\frac{\gamma_{d_j}-H_j}{2}
\geq\frac1{2e}-o(1).
\tag{43}
\]
Thus \(Q_j>0\) eventually.

Fix any \(p\in(0,1/e)\).  By (37), \(p<\gamma_{d_j}\) for every \(j\), so (10) gives
\[
H_j\geq p
\left(x_j-
\frac{\Delta_{C_j}(q_j)}{\delta_pW_j}
\right)_+.
\tag{44}
\]
Whether or not the expression in parentheses is positive, (44) implies
\[
\frac{\Delta_{C_j}(q_j)}{Q_j}
\geq\delta_p
\left(1-\frac{H_j}{p x_j}\right).
\tag{45}
\]
Indeed, if the parenthesis in (44) is nonpositive then the left side of (45) is at least \(\delta_p\); otherwise (45) is the rearrangement of (44).  Equations (17) and (43) yield
\[
\liminf_{j\to\infty}
\frac{\Delta_{C_j}(q_j)}{Q_j}\geq\delta_p.
\]
Finally let \(p\downarrow0\).  Equation (30) gives
\[
\delta_p\longrightarrow\frac1{\log2}-1,
\]
which proves (18).

## External sources

The only external theorem used is the Brunn-Minkowski inequality in section dimension.  Exact form used: if \(k\geq1\), \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^k\) are nonempty measurable sets of finite positive volume, and \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_k((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B)^{1/k}
\geq
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_k(A)^{1/k}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_k(B)^{1/k}.
\]
See Richard J. Gardner, “The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355-405, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2.  The exact displayed form above is the classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality reviewed there.  It applies with \(k=D-1\) to the compact sections \(K_{t_0},K_{t_1}\subseteq u^\perp\); interior sections have positive finite \((D-1)\)-volume.  The case \(D=1\), where \(k=0\), was handled directly and does not invoke the theorem.
