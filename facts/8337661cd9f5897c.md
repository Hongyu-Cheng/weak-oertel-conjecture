## Statement

Fix any integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) specialization of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, with the rooted-tree construction of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, put
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor
\quad(8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}b_j,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
and set
$$
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\qquad N=n+1.
$$
Set \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) whenever \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). Here \(k\) is the height parameter, \(Q\) is tree depth, \(m\) is the branching number, \(n\) is the number of spatial coordinates, and \(N\) is the number of all integer coordinates.

Choose any partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) into symmetric saturated chains. Let \(D_m\) consist of its nonterminal vectors and let \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) be its injective chain successor, so \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\). Spatial coordinates are indexed by
$$
\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace^r.
$$
For a node \(v\), including the empty root, write \(x[v]=(x_{v1},\ldots,x_{vm})\) for its child-coordinate block. For each Boolean source \(b\), its forward path starts at the root and proceeds from \(v\) to \(vi_m(b[v])\) when \(b[v]\in D_m\). It stops at the first failure or upon reaching depth \(Q\). Denote its length by \(h(b)\) and its selected coordinates by \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\). Retain the exact spatial support and every original column:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{m,Q}
&=\left\lbrace z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}:
 b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},\quad
 0\le s\le h(b)\right\rbrace,\\
\widehat A
&=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
 a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
The spatial level is the number of coordinates equal to two. For every vector \(\xi=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_Q)\in\mathbb R^Q\), with no sign or magnitude restriction, set
$$
w_{(z,t)}=
\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{Q}\xi_r\sum_{|u|=r}z_u\right)2^{-t}
\quad((z,t)\in\widehat A),\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
p_{\mathbf I}=\frac{w_{\mathbf I}}W.
$$
All other lattice weights are zero. At every original index define
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=
\inf\left\lbrace
\sum_{\mathbf J\in H\cap\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I
\right\rbrace.
$$
Then, uniformly over every \(k\), every allowed chain partition, and every such vector \(\xi\),
$$
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}
 p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3
\ge 2^{-65}8^{-N}.
$$
The support, column heights, normalizer, sampling law, and family of containing closed ambient halfspaces in this conclusion are the full original ones. This is a theorem about this specified family and makes no assertion that the locked general mixed-integer conjecture is proved.

## Proof

The source, path, successor, inverse reconstruction, and lattice support retain exactly their meanings in accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df. In particular, the Boolean source of a spatial point is recovered by replacing its twos by zeros. Thus each fixed spatial level contains at most \(2^n\) points. Put
$$
\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}.
$$
These predecessors give
$$
|D_m|=|\operatorname{im}F_m|=2^m\eta,
\qquad \eta^Q>\frac34.
\tag{1}
$$
Also \(b_8=4\), so \(Q\ge4\) and \(m\ge1250\). Write
$$
T_s=\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}\le2\,2^{-a_s}
\qquad(0\le s\le Q).
\tag{2}
$$
At any atom every containing closed halfspace includes that atom. Consequently
$$
M_3(p)\ge S_4(p):=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}^{\,4}.
\tag{3}
$$

First we exploit the equality of the coefficients within each tree depth. Define
$$
\mathcal D(x)=\sum_{c\in D_m}e^{x\sum_i c_i},\qquad
Z_s(\xi)=\sum_{\substack{z\in A_{m,Q}\\\operatorname{level}(z)=s}}
 \exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{Q}\xi_r\sum_{|u|=r}z_u\right).
$$
There is the exact factorization
$$
Z_s(\xi)=
\prod_{r=1}^{s}
 \left[e^{2\xi_r}\mathcal D(\xi_r)
 (1+e^{\xi_r})^{m^r-m}\right]
\prod_{r=s+1}^{Q}(1+e^{\xi_r})^{m^r}.
\tag{4}
$$
Indeed, a source reaching level \(s\) visits one child block at each depth \(1,\ldots,s\). Choose its successive patterns in \(D_m\); each choice determines the next visited location. All remaining coordinates are unrestricted Boolean bits. Conversely these choices and remaining bits recover the source uniquely. At depth \(r\le s\), exactly \(m\) coordinates belong to the visited block and the other \(m^r-m\) coordinates are free. All coefficients there equal \(\xi_r\), so the weighted sum is independent of the selected block's location. Finally the selected zero at that depth becomes two, supplying the factor \(e^{2\xi_r}\). This proves (4), including \(s=0\).

For \(x\in\mathbb R\), put
$$
\mathcal R(x)=\frac{8(1+e^{4x})}{(1+e^x)^4}.
$$
The fourth-power inequality gives
$$
\frac{\mathcal D(4x)}{\mathcal D(x)^4}
\ge |D_m|^{-3}\ge8^{-m},\qquad
\mathcal R(x)\ge1.
\tag{5}
$$
The first inequality follows from applying \((\sum_{i=1}^{L}y_i)^4\le L^3\sum_{i=1}^{L}y_i^4\) to the positive numbers indexed by \(D_m\). Apply (5) in (4); the spike factors cancel from the fourth-power ratio. Every depth \(r\ge2\) has at least \(m^r-m\) free Boolean coordinates, whether or not its block was visited. Therefore
$$
\frac{Z_s(4\xi)}{Z_s(\xi)^4}
\ge8^{-n}e^V
\quad(0\le s\le Q),\qquad
V=\sum_{r=2}^{Q}(m^r-m)\log\mathcal R(\xi_r).
\tag{6}
$$
Here \(4\xi\) means componentwise multiplication by four.

Choose \(s\) maximizing the original level mass \(Z_s(\xi)T_s\). The full original normalizer obeys
$$
W=\sum_{j=0}^{Q}Z_j(\xi)T_j
\le(Q+1)Z_s(\xi)T_s.
$$
The original atoms at that level and its entrance height have unnormalized fourth-power sum \(2^{-4a_s}Z_s(4\xi)\). Equations (2) and (6) yield
$$
8^N S_4(p)
\ge\frac{e^V}{2(Q+1)^4}.
\tag{7}
$$
In particular, if \(V\ge\log(2(Q+1)^4)\), (3) and (7) prove the claimed theorem with the stronger coefficient one.

Suppose henceforth that
$$
V<\log(2(Q+1)^4)<5Q.
\tag{8}
$$
We use precisely the scalar estimate proved in accepted fact d63d54549cda4df4: \(\mathcal R\) is even and nondecreasing in \(|x|\), and
$$
\log\mathcal R(x)\ge x^2/3
\qquad(|x|\le\log2).
\tag{9}
$$
The elementary inequalities \(2/3<\log2<1\) follow by integrating \(1/x\) on \([1,2]\), using its tangent at \(3/2\) for the lower bound. Further,
$$
m-1>30Q,
$$
since \(m=2(Q+1)^4>2Q^4\ge128Q\) for \(Q\ge4\). If some \(r\ge2\) had \(|\xi_r|\ge\log2\), monotonicity and (9) would imply
$$
V\ge(m^2-m)\frac{(\log2)^2}{3}
>(m^2-m)\frac4{27}>5Q,
$$
contradicting (8). Thus (9) applies to every \(\xi_r\) with \(r\ge2\). Because \(m^r-m\ge m^r/2\) for \(r\ge2\), we obtain
$$
\sum_{r=2}^{Q}m^r\xi_r^2\le6V<30Q.
\tag{10}
$$
Define a spatial field by
$$
\lambda_u=
\begin{cases}
0,&|u|\le2,\\
\xi_{|u|},&|u|\ge3.
\end{cases}
$$
For any field \(\theta\), define its path variation, over all rooted paths of length \(Q\), by
$$
\Pi(\theta)=\max_{v_0,\ldots,v_Q}
 \sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\theta_{v_ri}|,
\qquad v_0=\varnothing,\quad v_{r+1}\text{ a child of }v_r.
$$
For this depth-dependent \(\lambda\), Cauchy--Schwarz and (10) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Pi(\lambda)
&=m\sum_{r=3}^{Q}|\xi_r|\\
&\le m\left(\sum_{r=3}^{Q}m^r\xi_r^2\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\sum_{r=3}^{Q}m^{-r}\right)^{1/2}\\
&<\sqrt{\frac{30Q}{m-1}}<1,
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
where \(\sum_{r=3}^{\infty}m^{-r}=1/(m^2(m-1))\).

We now establish the required first-two-depth interface directly. Let
$$
\mathcal B_2=\lbrace u:|u|\le2\rbrace,\qquad n_2=m+m^2,
\qquad \pi(z)=(z_u)_{u\in\mathcal B_2}.
$$
We prove the following estimate for the same original support: if \(\lambda\) vanishes on \(\mathcal B_2\), \(P_\lambda:=\Pi(\lambda)\le1\), and \(f\) is any positive function on the finite set of prefixes occurring in \(A_{m,Q}\), then the weights
$$
w_{(z,t)}=f(\pi(z))e^{\lambda\cdot z}2^{-t}
\tag{12}
$$
with their full original normalizer satisfy \(M_3(p)\ge2^{-65}8^{-N}\). This will apply to our original weights by choosing
$$
f(\pi(z))=
\exp\left(\xi_1\sum_{|u|=1}z_u+
          \xi_2\sum_{|u|=2}z_u\right),
$$
and using (11). This definition depends only on the displayed prefix, so (12) is an exact identity, not a comparison of probability laws.

Partition the original support into three classes, of spatial levels zero, one, and at least two. Write their unnormalized measures as \(\nu_0,\nu_1,\nu_{\ge2}\), their positive total masses as \(W_0,W_1,W_{\ge2}\), and their normalized laws as \(q_0,q_1,q_{\ge2}\). All their depths are defined using all closed halfspaces of the same ambient space \(\mathbb R^N\). These three auxiliary laws will be transferred back to the original law at the end.

For either fixed early level \(j=0,1\), put \(v_z=f(\pi(z))e^{\lambda\cdot z}>0\) at that level. Its full class normalizer is \(W_j=T_j\sum_zv_z\). Its atoms at height \(a_j\) give, using (2) and the at most \(2^n\) spatial points,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q_j)
&\ge\frac{2^{-4a_j}\sum_zv_z^4}
 {T_j^4(\sum_zv_z)^4}\\
&\ge\frac1{16}|\lbrace z:\operatorname{level}(z)=j\rbrace|^{-3}
\ge\frac1{16}8^{-n}
=\frac12\,8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
No restriction on the prefix multiplier was needed here.

For the tail class let
$$
\mathcal L_2=\lbrace\pi(z):\operatorname{level}(z)=2\rbrace.
$$
Every spatial point of level at least two has a prefix in \(\mathcal L_2\). In a prefix \(\omega\in\mathcal L_2\), its two coordinates equal to two recover the first two selected nodes; replacing them by zero recovers the unique Boolean prefix source. In particular,
$$
0<|\mathcal L_2|\le2^{n_2}.
\tag{14}
$$
At level two, every such prefix occurs with every Boolean assignment outside \(\mathcal B_2\): those outside coordinates cannot affect the first two forward tests. Put
$$
F=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal L_2}f(\omega),\qquad
F_4=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal L_2}f(\omega)^4,
\qquad
Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}=
 \prod_{u\notin\mathcal B_2}(1+e^{\theta_u})
$$
for fields \(\theta\) vanishing on \(\mathcal B_2\). To make the normalization explicit, the full Boolean product is
$$
Z_\theta=\prod_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}(1+e^{\theta_u})
=2^{n_2}Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}.
\tag{15}
$$
We use the outside product only with this map. Equations (14) and the fourth-power inequality imply
$$
\frac{F_4}{F^4}\ge8^{-n_2},\qquad
\frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}
\ge8^{-(n-n_2)}.
\tag{16}
$$

For a fixed prefix label and level \(s\ge2\), the unique-source map parametrizes the spatial points by some of the Boolean assignments outside \(\mathcal B_2\). Changing its selected prefix zeros to twos changes the field factor by at most \(e^{2P_\lambda}\); the first two depths have zero field, and the remaining selected coordinates lie in the blocks on one rooted path. Dropping the success conditions on the outside assignments therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\ge2}
&\le e^{2P_\lambda}FZ_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}
  \sum_{s=2}^{Q}T_s
\le e^{2P_\lambda}FZ_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}R_k,\\
R_k:=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}T_s
&\le2+2\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell2^{-\ell}
\le2+\frac{H_k-H_7}{4}
\le\frac{9+\log k}{4},
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
where \(H_j=\sum_{r=1}^{j}1/r\) and \(H_k\le1+\log k\). This includes every original tail column and height.

For \(s\ge2\) and \(\omega\in\mathcal L_2\), let \(\mathcal C_s(\omega)\) be the level-\(s\) spatial points with that prefix whose inverse continuation from accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 reaches depth \(Q\). We claim that every field \(\theta\) vanishing on \(\mathcal B_2\) satisfies
$$
\sum_{z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega)}e^{\theta\cdot z}
\ge\eta^{Q-2}e^{-3\Pi(\theta)}Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}.
\tag{18}
$$
Here is the complete census. Fix the recovered first two forward source blocks and their selected nodes from \(\omega\). Give all outside Boolean coordinates their independent product law with mass proportional to \(e^{\theta\cdot b}\), normalized by \(Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}\). Starting at the second selected node, require the next \(s-2\) blocks to lie in \(D_m\), with their forward selections, and require the remaining \(Q-s\) blocks to lie in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\), with the injective inverse selections. There are \(Q-2\) fresh blocks: their coordinate depths are strictly increasing from three to \(Q\). A complete successful transcript fixes exactly those patterns at their adaptively determined locations and leaves every other outside bit free. Distinct transcripts define disjoint cylinder events, since their first differing pattern occurs at the same location determined by their common earlier patterns.

Under fair outside bits, each next success has conditional probability \(\eta\), so the total success probability is \(\eta^{Q-2}\). For a specified bit \(d\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) at coordinate \(u\), its product-law probability divided by its fair-bit probability is
$$
\frac{2e^{\theta_ud}}{1+e^{\theta_u}}\ge e^{-|\theta_u|}.
$$
A transcript's ratio is at least \(e^{-\Pi(\theta)}\), since the revealed blocks lie on one rooted path. Unused coordinates integrate to one. Summing the disjoint cylinder comparisons proves success probability at least \(e^{-\Pi(\theta)}\eta^{Q-2}\).

On success, replace the selected zero by two in the first \(s\) forward blocks, using the fixed prefix for the first two. This is a bijection onto \(\mathcal C_s(\omega)\): replacing twos by zeros recovers the source, and the forward prefix and unique inverse continuation recover the entire mixed transcript. The field weight multiplier is at least \(e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}\), because the first two depths have zero field and all remaining selected coordinates lie on the revealed path. Multiplying the probability bound by \(Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}\) proves (18), including \(s=2\) and \(s=Q\).

Suppose first that \(k\ge2^{16}\), and put \(L=\log k\), \(u=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\). Then \(L>9\) and \(u\ge256\). The scalar entrance estimate proved in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba is
$$
U_k:=\sum_{\ell=8}^{u}b_\ell2^{-\ell}\ge\frac L{32}.
$$
Removing only spatial level one gives exactly
$$
U_{\ge2}:=\sum_{\substack{2\le s\le Q\\8\le a_s\le u}}2^{-a_s}
=U_k-2^{-8}\ge\frac L{64}.
\tag{19}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(L>9\). Also (17) gives
$$
W_{\ge2}\le e^{2P_\lambda}FZ_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}\frac L2.
\tag{20}
$$
Consider the original indices
$$
\mathcal G_{\ge2}=
\left\lbrace(z,a_s+1):
 z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega),\ \omega\in\mathcal L_2,
 \ 2\le s\le Q,\ 8\le a_s\le u\right\rbrace.
$$
Their indicated heights are original heights, since \(a_s+1\le u+1\le k\).

Fix \(\mathbf I=(z,a_s+1)\) in this set, with prefix \(\omega\). We use the pointwise actual-halfspace selection proved in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba, before division by its normalizer. If \(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_Q\) is the complete inverse continuation and \(V_r=\sum_{q=s+1}^{r}e_{u_q}\), then inside every closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf I\) that argument selects distinct original indices of the forms
$$
(z-e_{u_r},a_s),\qquad
(z-V_r,a_r),\qquad(z+V_r,a_r),\qquad r>s,
\tag{21}
$$
whose total untilted weight \(2^{-t}\) is at least \(L/128\). Its proof covers a nonnegative height coefficient by high-entrance endpoint pairs. For a negative height coefficient it uses either the lower neighbors or the remaining high-entrance endpoints after its bounded partial-sum discard. Every containing halfspace contains a parallel one through \(\mathbf I\), so that pointwise conclusion applies to the full family in our definition.

Every point selected in (21) has level at least \(s\ge2\). Every changed coordinate has depth at least \(s+1\ge3\); hence the entire first-two-depth prefix remains exactly \(\omega\). Each coordinate changes by at most one, and all changes lie on the complete rooted path through \(z\). Thus each selected point has spatial factor at least \(e^{\lambda\cdot z-P_\lambda}\) and the same multiplier \(f(\omega)\). Its weight in the tail measure is therefore at least this common factor times its untilted weight. We have proved, for every such containing closed halfspace,
$$
h_{\nu_{\ge2}}(\mathbf I)
:=W_{\ge2}\tau_{q_{\ge2}}(\mathbf I)
\ge f(\omega)e^{\lambda\cdot z-P_\lambda}\frac L{128}.
\tag{22}
$$
In particular this uses the actual tail measure on all its original indices and allows all ambient directions.

Apply (18) to \(\theta=4\lambda\); its path variation is \(4P_\lambda\). Summing (22) cubed against the original tail atom weights, and using (19), (20), and (1), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q_{\ge2})
&=\frac1{W_{\ge2}^4}
 \sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_{\ge2}}
 w_{\mathbf I}h_{\nu_{\ge2}}(\mathbf I)^3\\
&\ge
\frac{e^{-3P_\lambda}L^3}{128^3W_{\ge2}^4}
\sum_{\substack{2\le s\le Q\\8\le a_s\le u}}
 2^{-(a_s+1)}
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal L_2}f(\omega)^4
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal C_s(\omega)}e^{4\lambda\cdot z}\\
&\ge
\frac{\eta^{Q-2}e^{-15P_\lambda}
 F_4Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}U_{\ge2}L^3}
 {2\cdot128^3W_{\ge2}^4}\\
&\ge3\cdot2^{-26}e^{-23P_\lambda}
 \frac{F_4}{F^4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}
 {(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}\\
&\ge3\cdot2^{-23}e^{-23}8^{-N}
>2^{-60}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The constant in the penultimate inequality uses \(\eta^{Q-2}\ge\eta^Q>3/4\), \(U_{\ge2}\ge L/64\), and the factor \(16\) from the fourth power of (20). Equation (16) gives the last product bound \(8^{-n}\), with \(8^{-n}=8\,8^{-N}\). For the final numerical comparison, \(e<3\), \(3^5<2^8\), and \(3^3<2^5\) give \(e^{23}<2^{37}\). The exponent \(23P_\lambda\) consists of \(12P_\lambda\) from the census, \(3P_\lambda\) from the cubed depth, and \(8P_\lambda\) from the fourth normalizer.

If instead \(8\le k<2^{16}\), then \(\log k<16\) and (17) gives
$$
W_{\ge2}<8e^{2P_\lambda}FZ_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
Since \(b_8=4\), we have \(a_2=8\). For every \(\omega\in\mathcal L_2\) and every Boolean assignment outside \(\mathcal B_2\), the spatial point of level two with this prefix and that assignment is original. Its atom at height eight is in the tail class. The fourth powers of just these atom weights sum exactly to
$$
2^{-32}F_4Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
Using their own masses as depth lower bounds and then (16),
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q_{\ge2})
&\ge\frac{2^{-32}F_4Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{W_{\ge2}^4}\\
&>2^{-44}e^{-8P_\lambda}
 \frac{F_4}{F^4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}
 {(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}\\
&\ge2^{-41}e^{-8}8^{-N}
>2^{-54}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-60}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
Here \(e^8<3^8<2^{13}\). This argument also covers \(k=8\), with the height-eight atom at the top of its original column.

Finally let \(\alpha_j=W_j/W\) for the three classes \(j\in\lbrace0,1,\ge2\rbrace\). They are positive and sum to one. At an index in class \(j\), every original containing halfspace has \(p\)-mass at least \(\alpha_j\) times its \(q_j\)-mass. Taking infima over the same ambient closed halfspaces gives \(\tau_p\ge\alpha_j\tau_{q_j}\) there. Since \(p=\alpha_jq_j\) on that class, (13), (23), and (24) imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{j\in\lbrace0,1,\ge2\rbrace}
 \alpha_j^4M_3(q_j)\\
&\ge2^{-60}8^{-N}
 (\alpha_0^4+\alpha_1^4+\alpha_{\ge2}^4)\\
&\ge\frac{2^{-60}}{27}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-65}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fourth-power inequality for three numbers gives the factor \(1/27\). This proves the first-two-depth interface for every positive \(f\) and \(P_\lambda\le1\), including the boundary. It applies to the field decomposition (11), so it proves the original theorem in the remaining case (8). Together with (7), all real depth-dependent coefficient vectors are covered. Every comparison was made before returning to the single full normalizer \(W\), and the final expectation uses precisely the original law and all original columns.
