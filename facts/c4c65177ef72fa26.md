## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex with nonempty $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$, $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$ and $M=\mu(S)>0$. Write $M_r$ for the masses in the $p$ represented positive-mass base parities. The function $R(y)=\mathcal H_q(S\cap(2y-S))$ is upper semicontinuous on $S$ and attains its maximum $R_{\max}$ there. Every maximizer lies in a $q$-dimensional integer fiber and
$$
R_{\max}\ge \frac{\sum_r M_r^2}{2^q M}\ge\frac{M}{2^q p}\ge\frac{M}{2^{n+q}}.
$$
At every actual $y\in S$, each closed ambient halfspace containing $y$ has $\mu$-mass at least $R(y)/2$. In particular the normalized depth at a reflection maximizer is at least $\sum_r M_r^2/(2^{q+1}M^2)\ge2^{-(n+q+1)}$.

## Proof

At an actual integer-base center $y$, reflection preserves $\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$, so $R(y)=\int_S\mathbf1_C(2y-a)\,d\mu(a)$. For $y_i\to y$ in $S$, closedness of $C$ gives $\limsup_i\mathbf1_C(2y_i-a)\le\mathbf1_C(2y-a)$ pointwise. Reverse Fatou for these bounded indicators gives upper semicontinuity. The domain $S$ is compact, so the maximum is attained.

The correlation identity gives $\int R\,d\mu=2^{-q}\sum_r M_r^2$. A maximum is at least the average and is positive. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum_r M_r^2\ge M^2/p$ and $p\le 2^n$. As shown in the correlation proof, positive reflection requires an ordered pair of positive $q$-fibers and its actual midpoint fiber has dimension $q$.

The set $S\cap(2y-S)$ is invariant under the isometric reflection about $y$. Each of its opposite closed through-$y$ caps has the same measure and they cover the retained set, so each has mass at least $R(y)/2$, including any charged boundary. Each closed halfspace containing $y$ contains its parallel through-$y$ cap. This yields the depth bound for the original measure, with counting included when $q=0$.
