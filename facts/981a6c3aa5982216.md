## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and set
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z.
\]
Assume \(W>0\). Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) be a weighted lattice centerpoint with the two properties
\[
w_{z_0}>0
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_z\ge 2^{-n}W
\tag{2}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z_0\). Such a \(z_0\) is supplied by the weighted lattice centerpoint construction proved in accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8`.

For \(y\in C_{z_0}\), define
\[
g(y):=h_S((z_0,y)),
\qquad
t:=\max_{y\in C_{z_0}}g(y).
\]
Then there is a maximizer \(y_0\in\operatorname{int}C_{z_0}\) with the following property. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are an integer \(r\) with \(1\le r\le d+1\), unit vectors
\[
u_i=(a_i,b_i)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d
\qquad(1\le i\le r),
\]
and numbers \(\lambda_i>0\), \(\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1\), such that the ordinary closed halfspaces
\[
H_i:=
\left\{(z,x):
a_i^\top(z-z_0)+b_i^\top(x-y_0)\ge0
\right\}
\tag{3}
\]
satisfy
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_i)\le t+\varepsilon
\qquad(1\le i\le r),
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i b_i=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
\bar a:=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i a_i,
\qquad
Q_\varepsilon:=
\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\bar a^\top(z-z_0)\ge0\}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\bar a=0\), interpret \(Q_\varepsilon=\mathbb R^n\). Then the exact union cover
\[
\bigl(Q_\varepsilon\times\mathbb R^d\bigr)\cap S
\subseteq
\bigcup_{i=1}^r H_i
\tag{7}
\]
holds. Consequently,
\[
2^{-n}W
\le
\mathcal H_d\!\left(S\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^rH_i\right)
\le
r(t+\varepsilon)
\le
(d+1)(t+\varepsilon),
\tag{8}
\]
and hence
\[
t\ge\frac{W}{2^n(d+1)}.
\tag{9}
\]
All caps in (3) are ordinary halfspaces through \((z_0,y_0)\); the statement makes no attainment or continuity assumption at a vertical normal.

The rank-drop threshold cannot be inferred merely from a near-vertical active sequence. Namely, for every \(N\ge1\), the cylinder
\[
C^{(N)}:=[-N,N]\times[-1,1]\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R
\tag{10}
\]
has \(W_N=2(2N+1)\), weighted lattice centerpoint \(z_0=0\), and fiberwise maximizer \(y_0=0\) of depth \(t_N=W_N/2\). For every \(0<\eta<1\), both ordinary caps
\[
H_\eta^\pm:=\{(z,x):z\pm\eta x\ge0\}
\tag{11}
\]
have mass \(t_N\), and their continuous normals balance. Their common primary vertical limit has base boundary \(\{0\}\), whose slice mass is only \(2\), whereas the codimension-one hypothesis in accepted fact `314b32bda8a0b01e` would require at least \(W_N/2=2N+1\).

## Proof

Define the finite Borel measure
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d\!\left(
C_z\cap\{x:(z,x)\in A\}
\right).
\tag{12}
\]
Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty. Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` proves that
\[
g(y)=
\inf_{(a,b)\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1}}
M_y(a,b),
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
M_y(a,b):=
\mu\!\left(
\{(z,x):
a^\top(z-z_0)+b^\top(x-y)\ge0\}
\right).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus only ordinary boundary-through-\((z_0,y)\) halfspaces occur in (13).

We first establish a uniform slab bound. Choose \(R<\infty\) such that every continuous fiber is contained in the ball \(B_d(0,R)\), and let \(Z\) be the finite set of lattice points with nonempty fibers. Slicing \(B_d(0,R)\) orthogonally to a unit vector shows that there is \(L<\infty\), depending only on \(d,R,\lvert Z\rvert\), such that
\[
\sum_{z\in Z}
\mathcal H_d\{x\in C_z:e^\top x\in I\}
\le L|I|
\tag{15}
\]
for every unit \(e\in\mathbb R^d\) and every interval \(I\subseteq\mathbb R\). Indeed, every orthogonal section of the containing ball has uniformly bounded \((d-1)\)-volume, and Cavalieri integration gives (15). The same statement for \(d=1\) is the elementary interval-length bound.

It follows that for every unit \((a,b)\) and all \(y,y'\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
|M_{y'}(a,b)-M_y(a,b)|
\le L\|y'-y\|_2.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(b=0\), both cap sets are identical. For \(b\ne0\), on every fixed fiber the scalar threshold in direction \(e=b/\|b\|_2\) moves by
\(e^\top(y'-y)\), so the symmetric difference is contained in a slab of width at most \(\|y'-y\|_2\), and (15) applies. Taking infima in (13) shows in particular that \(g\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz on \(C_{z_0}\).

We next show that every boundary point of the anchor fiber has depth zero. Put \(K:=C_{z_0}\), and fix \(y\in\partial K\). Choose a unit supporting normal \(e\in\mathbb R^d\) with
\[
e^\top(x-y)\le0\qquad(x\in K).
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(p=(z_0,y)\) and \(q_\delta=p+\delta(0,e)\) for \(\delta>0\). Then \(q_\delta\notin C\). Let \(c_\delta\) be a nearest point of \(C\) to \(q_\delta\), and put
\[
u_\delta=(a_\delta,b_\delta)
:=
\frac{q_\delta-c_\delta}
{\|q_\delta-c_\delta\|_2}.
\tag{18}
\]
The nearest-point inequality
\[
u_\delta^\top(v-c_\delta)\le0
\qquad(v\in C)
\tag{19}
\]
follows, for example, by differentiating at \(s=0\) the squared distance from \(q_\delta\) to \(c_\delta+s(v-c_\delta)\). Since \(p\in C\), the number
\[
\Delta_\delta:=
u_\delta^\top(c_\delta-p)
\tag{20}
\]
satisfies
\[
0\le\Delta_\delta
<
u_\delta^\top(q_\delta-p)
=\delta\,b_\delta^\top e
\le\delta\|b_\delta\|_2.
\tag{21}
\]
In particular, \(b_\delta\ne0\). Every point of \(C\) lying in the boundary-through-\(p\) cap with normal \(u_\delta\) satisfies
\[
0\le
u_\delta^\top(v-p)
\le\Delta_\delta.
\tag{22}
\]
On each lattice fiber, (22) is a slab in direction
\(b_\delta/\|b_\delta\|_2\) of width at most
\(\Delta_\delta/\|b_\delta\|_2<\delta\). Hence (15) gives
\[
g(y)\le M_y(u_\delta)\le L\delta.
\]
Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves
\[
g(y)=0\qquad(y\in\partial K).
\tag{23}
\]

By (1), \(K\) has positive \(d\)-volume and hence nonempty interior. If \(y\in\operatorname{int}K\), choose \(\rho>0\) with
\(B_d(y,\rho)\subseteq K\). For every unit \((a,b)\) with \(b\ne0\), the \(z_0\)-fiber section of its cap contains one half of this ball. If \(b=0\), the section contains the whole ball. Therefore
\[
g(y)\ge\frac12\mathcal H_d(B_d(0,\rho))>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (23) and (24), together with compactness of \(K\) and continuity of \(g\), show that
\[
t:=\max_Kg>0
\tag{25}
\]
and every maximizer of \(g\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}K\).

Although cap mass can jump at a vertical normal, vertical normals do not change the infimum in (13). Indeed, fix a vertical unit normal \((a,0)\), choose any unit \(e\in\mathbb R^d\), and consider the nonvertical normals proportional to \((a,\delta e)\), \(\delta>0\). On every lattice fiber with
\(a^\top(z-z_0)>0\), the cap eventually contains the whole fiber; on every fiber with
\(a^\top(z-z_0)<0\), it eventually contains none; and on a boundary fiber it cuts out
\[
\{x:e^\top(x-y)\ge0\}.
\]
Thus its limiting mass is no larger than the mass of the vertical closed halfspace, which contains every boundary fiber in full. It follows that
\[
g(y)=
\inf_{\substack{(a,b)\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1}\\b\ne0}}
M_y(a,b).
\tag{26}
\]

For \(k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), define the compact truncated normal set
\[
\mathcal U_k:=
\{(a,b)\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1}:\|b\|_2\ge1/k\}
\tag{27}
\]
and the truncated depth
\[
g_k(y):=\min_{u\in\mathcal U_k}M_y(u).
\tag{28}
\]
The map \((y,u)\mapsto M_y(u)\) is continuous on
\(K\times\mathcal U_k\). To see this, take a convergent sequence of pairs. On each of the finitely many positive-mass fibers, the limiting cap boundary is a genuine affine hyperplane because its continuous normal has norm at least \(1/k\), and therefore has zero \(d\)-volume. The cap indicators converge away from that boundary, so dominated convergence applies fiberwise and then after the finite sum. Consequently every \(g_k\) is continuous. Equation (16) also makes every \(g_k\) \(L\)-Lipschitz.

The sets \(\mathcal U_k\) increase with \(k\), and their union is the set of all nonvertical unit normals. Therefore (26) gives
\[
g_k(y)\downarrow g(y)
\qquad(y\in K).
\tag{29}
\]
The convergence is uniform. For completeness, if it were not uniform, there would be \(\alpha>0\), a subsequence, and \(y_k\in K\) with
\[
g_k(y_k)-g(y_k)\ge\alpha.
\]
Pass to a subsequence with \(y_k\to y_\ast\). For every fixed \(m\) and all sufficiently large \(k\), monotonicity gives
\(g_k(y_k)\le g_m(y_k)\). Continuity then gives
\[
\alpha\le g_m(y_\ast)-g(y_\ast)
\]
for every \(m\), contradicting (29) as \(m\to\infty\). Write
\[
\eta_k:=\max_{y\in K}|g_k(y)-g(y)|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{30}
\]

Let \(y_k\) maximize \(g_k\) on \(K\), and put \(t_k:=g_k(y_k)\). For all sufficiently large \(k\), the point \(y_k\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}K\). Indeed, (23) and (30) give
\[
g_k(y)\le\eta_k\qquad(y\in\partial K),
\]
whereas at any maximizer of \(g\), one has \(g_k\ge g=t\). Since \(t>0\), the boundary cannot contain a maximizer of \(g_k\) once \(\eta_k<t\). Also,
\[
t\le t_k\le t+\eta_k,
\tag{31}
\]
so \(t_k\to t\).

Fix such a large \(k\). Let
\[
\mathcal A_k:=
\{(a,b)\in\mathcal U_k:M_{y_k}(a,b)=t_k\}
\tag{32}
\]
be the nonempty compact set of active truncated normals. We claim
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}
\{b:(a,b)\in\mathcal A_k\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Suppose otherwise. Strong separation from this compact convex hull gives a unit vector \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(\gamma>0\) such that
\[
v^\top b\ge\gamma
\qquad((a,b)\in\mathcal A_k).
\tag{34}
\]
By continuity, the open subset
\[
\mathcal V:=
\{(a,b)\in\mathcal U_k:v^\top b>\gamma/2\}
\]
contains \(\mathcal A_k\). If
\(\mathcal U_k\setminus\mathcal V\) is nonempty, compactness supplies
\(\sigma>0\) such that
\[
M_{y_k}(u)\ge t_k+\sigma
\qquad(u\in\mathcal U_k\setminus\mathcal V).
\tag{35}
\]
If that complement is empty, fix any \(\sigma>0\), and interpret (35) as
vacuous.

Choose \(\rho>0\) with \(B_d(y_k,\rho)\subseteq K\), and move to
\[
y_{k,s}:=y_k-sv
\]
for sufficiently small \(s>0\). If \(u=(a,b)\in\mathcal V\), this motion adds \(s\,b^\top v>0\) to the cap inequality on every fiber, so the old cap is contained in the new one. Inside the anchor ball, the newly added part contains the slab
\[
-s\gamma/2
\le
\frac{b^\top(x-y_k)}{\|b\|_2}
<0,
\tag{36}
\]
because \(\|b\|_2\le1\). If \(s\gamma/2<\rho/2\), every orthogonal section of the anchor ball occurring in (36) has a positive lower bound depending only on \(d,\rho\). Hence there is \(c=c(d,\rho)>0\) such that
\[
M_{y_{k,s}}(u)
\ge M_{y_k}(u)+cs\gamma
\ge t_k+cs\gamma
\qquad(u\in\mathcal V).
\tag{37}
\]
For \(u\notin\mathcal V\), (16) and (35) give
\[
M_{y_{k,s}}(u)
\ge t_k+\sigma-Ls.
\tag{38}
\]
Taking \(s\) smaller if necessary so that \(y_{k,s}\in K\) and
\(Ls<\sigma/2\), equations (37) and (38) imply
\[
g_k(y_{k,s})>t_k,
\]
contradicting the definition of \(y_k\). This proves (33).

By the standard affine-dependence reduction in \(\mathbb R^d\), a convex representation of \(0\) using the vectors in (33) can be chosen with at most \(d+1\) terms: if more terms occur, perturb the weights along a nonzero affine dependence until one becomes zero, and repeat. After deleting zero weights, there are
\[
1\le r_k\le d+1,\qquad
u_{k,i}=(a_{k,i},b_{k,i})\in\mathcal A_k,
\qquad
\lambda_{k,i}>0,
\tag{39}
\]
with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{r_k}\lambda_{k,i}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^{r_k}\lambda_{k,i}b_{k,i}=0,
\qquad
M_{y_k}(u_{k,i})=t_k.
\tag{40}
\]

Pass to a subsequence for which \(y_k\to y_0\in K\). From (16), (30), and (31),
\[
g(y_k)\ge g_k(y_k)-\eta_k=t_k-\eta_k\longrightarrow t.
\]
Continuity of \(g\) yields \(g(y_0)=t\), so \(y_0\) is a fiberwise maximizer and lies in \(\operatorname{int}K\) by (23)-(25).

Now fix \(\varepsilon>0\), and choose \(k\) sufficiently far along this subsequence that
\[
t_k+L\|y_k-y_0\|_2\le t+\varepsilon.
\tag{41}
\]
Keep the normals and weights in (39)-(40), but move every ordinary cap boundary from \((z_0,y_k)\) to \((z_0,y_0)\). Equation (16) and (41) give (4), while the exact vector identity in (40) is unchanged and gives (5). This proves the asserted exact ordinary balance for every \(\varepsilon\), including the regime in which the ambient unit normals approach vertical ones.

It remains to prove the union cover. Let \(\bar a\) and
\(Q_\varepsilon\) be as in (6). If \(z\in Q_\varepsilon\) and a point \((z,x)\) lay outside every \(H_i\), then all inequalities in (3) would be strict in the opposite direction. Multiplying them by the positive \(\lambda_i\), summing, and using (5) would give
\[
\bar a^\top(z-z_0)
=
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i
\bigl(
a_i^\top(z-z_0)+b_i^\top(x-y_0)
\bigr)
<0,
\]
contrary to \(z\in Q_\varepsilon\). This proves (7). If \(\bar a\ne0\), property (2) applies to the proper base halfspace \(Q_\varepsilon\); if \(\bar a=0\), its base mass is \(W\). Thus the left inequality in (8) follows from (7). The remaining inequalities in (8) follow from subadditivity, (4), and \(r\le d+1\). Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves (9).

Finally, consider the calibration (10). Its lattice fibers are the \(2N+1\) intervals \([-1,1]\), so
\[
W_N=2(2N+1).
\]
The point \(0\) is a weighted lattice centerpoint. Central symmetry shows that every boundary-through-\((0,0)\) halfspace has mass at least \(W_N/2\), while the continuous halfspace \(\{x\ge0\}\) has exactly that mass. Thus \(g(0)=W_N/2\). For every \(y\in[-1,1]\), the two continuous halfspaces \(\{x\ge y\}\) and \(\{x\le y\}\) both contain \((0,y)\), their common boundary has zero mixed mass, and their masses sum to \(W_N\). Hence \(g(y)\le W_N/2\), proving \(t_N=W_N/2\) and that \(y_0=0\) is a fiberwise maximizer. For \(0<\eta<1\), each cap in (11) contains all fibers with \(z\ge1\), no fiber with \(z\le-1\), and one half of the fiber \(z=0\). Its mass is therefore
\[
2N+1=W_N/2=t_N.
\]
The two continuous normal components are \(\eta\) and \(-\eta\), so equal weights balance them. Both normals tend to the primary vertical normal \(z\ge0\), whose boundary slice \(z=0\) has mass \(2\). Accepted fact `314b32bda8a0b01e` requires a codimension-one primitive slice to have mass at least \(2^{-1}W_N=2N+1\) before its rank-drop conclusion applies. Thus near-vertical activity alone gives no such threshold, and the exactification above does not rely on one.
