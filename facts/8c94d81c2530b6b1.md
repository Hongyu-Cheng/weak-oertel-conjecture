## Statement

Use exactly the tree and source construction of 032423921efad6c8. Thus \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\) indexes the spatial coordinates, the empty word is the root, and \(x[v]=(x_{v1},\ldots,x_{vm})\) is the child block of a node \(v\) of depth less than \(Q\). Choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\); let \(D_m\) be its nonterminal Boolean vectors and \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) its successor. A Boolean source \(b\) selects successive children \(u_r=u_{r-1}i_m(b[u_{r-1}])\), starting at \(u_0=\varnothing\), while the encountered block belongs to \(D_m\), and stops otherwise or at depth \(Q\). Write \(h(b)\) for its path length and \(z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}\) for \(0\le s\le h(b)\). Their union is \(A_{m,Q}\); \(\mathcal L_s\) is its level-\(s\) subset, where the level is the number of coordinates equal to two. The complete original support is

$$
For every real spatial field \(\gamma=(\gamma_u)_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\) and every real height field \(\beta\), retain the entire original law
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_s(\gamma)&=\sum_{z\in\mathcal L_s}e^{\gamma\cdot z},
&Z&=\sum_{(z,t)\in\widehat A}e^{\gamma\cdot z+\beta t},\\
p_{(z,t)}&=\frac{e^{\gamma\cdot z+\beta t}}{Z},
&A_0&=4^N\sum_{\mathbf J\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}^3.

The scalar \(\beta\) is reserved for the height field. Define the new cubic entropy quantities
$$
R_3(x)=\frac{4(1+e^{3x})}{(1+e^x)^3}
=1+3\tanh(x/2)^2,
\qquad \zeta_u=\log R_3(\gamma_u),

Choose any set \(T_0\subseteq\mathcal T_{m,Q}\) of exactly \(mQ\) coordinates having the largest \(\zeta_u\), with arbitrary tie breaking. Define

Then \(0\le E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}\le E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\), and simultaneously for every \(0\le s\le Q\),

\frac{Z_s(3\gamma)}{Z_s(\gamma)^3}

## Proof

The parameter specialization and complete original columns are those of 604889294a17d7df, and the path construction is the one of 032423921efad6c8. In particular, \(b_8=4\), so \(Q\ge4\). The integers \(m\) and \(Q\) are positive, every entrance height is at most \(k\), and every level is nonempty: the chain containing the empty Boolean set has at least one nonterminal vertex, so \(D_m\ne\varnothing\), and any ordered list of \(s\) patterns in \(D_m\) extends to a Boolean source reaching level \(s\). Moreover \(n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\ge mQ\), proving that \(T_0\) exists.

For \(y=e^x\), expanding the numerator gives \(4(1+y^3)=(1+y)^3+3(y-1)^2(1+y)\), proving the stated formula for \(R_3\). It follows that \(R_3\) is even, is at least one, and is nondecreasing as \(|x|\) increases. In particular every \(\zeta_u\) is nonnegative. A full rooted path visits child blocks at distinct depths, so their coordinate sets are disjoint and their union has exactly \(mQ\) elements. The largest-\(mQ\) choice of \(T_0\) implies that the \(\zeta\)-sum on any such union is at most its sum on \(T_0\). Taking the maximum and subtracting from the full sum proves \(0\le E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}\le E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\). The maximum is attained because the set of full rooted paths is finite. Since \(\zeta\ge0\), for any maximizing path its union \(S_*\) satisfies \(\sum_{u\notin S_*}\zeta_u=E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\) exactly.

We now prove the cubic estimate, retaining the adaptive transcript mechanism of dbbd63823b5f4870 but deriving its exponent-three normalization directly. Fix \(s\in\lbrace0,\ldots,Q\rbrace\). An ordered transcript \(\vartheta=(c_1,\ldots,c_s)\in D_m^s\) recursively determines nodes \(u_0=\varnothing\), \(u_r=u_{r-1}i_m(c_r)\), and source assignments \(b[u_{r-1}]=c_r\). Let \(\mathcal V_\vartheta\) be the union of these \(s\) child blocks, and let \(c_{\vartheta,u}\) be the assigned Boolean value at \(u\in\mathcal V_\vartheta\). These blocks occur at distinct depths, so \(|\mathcal V_\vartheta|=ms\). Every selected coordinate \(u_r\) belongs to \(\mathcal V_\vartheta\).

Every Boolean extension outside \(\mathcal V_\vartheta\) has these first \(s\) path steps, by induction on the construction. Conversely, each source with \(h(b)\ge s\) yields a unique such transcript. Reduction of \(z_s(b)\) modulo two recovers \(b\), so distinct extensions and distinct transcripts give disjoint level points. Thus these classes partition \(\mathcal L_s\), with no constraint on any unassigned bit and no stopping constraint after step \(s\). For the empty transcript at \(s=0\), all coordinates are unassigned; at \(s=Q\), reaching tree depth \(Q\) requires no additional block condition.

For one transcript, its exact contribution to \(Z_s(\gamma)\) is
$$
Y_\vartheta(\gamma)
=C_\vartheta(\gamma)\prod_{u\notin\mathcal V_\vartheta}(1+e^{\gamma_u}),
\qquad
C_\vartheta(\gamma)=
\exp\left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal V_\vartheta}\gamma_uc_{\vartheta,u}
+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}\gamma_{u_r}\right).
$$
All spike coordinates are fixed by the transcript, and \(C_\vartheta(3\gamma)=C_\vartheta(\gamma)^3\). The fixed factors therefore cancel, giving
$$
\frac{Y_\vartheta(3\gamma)}{Y_\vartheta(\gamma)^3}
=4^{-(n-ms)}\exp\left(\sum_{u\notin\mathcal V_\vartheta}\zeta_u\right).
$$
The partial path underlying \(\mathcal V_\vartheta\) extends to a full rooted path. Nonnegativity of \(\zeta\) shows that its partial block sum is at most \(\Pi(\zeta)\). Hence each free-coordinate sum is at least \(E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\), even though the free-coordinate set depends on the transcript.

For any \(J\ge1\) positive numbers \(y_1,\ldots,y_J\), the finite cubic inequality is \((\sum_jy_j)^3\le J^2\sum_jy_j^3\). For completeness, Cauchy-Schwarz gives \((\sum_jy_j)^2\le J\sum_jy_j^2\) and \((\sum_jy_j^2)^2\le(\sum_jy_j^3)(\sum_jy_j)\); combining and dividing by \(\sum_jy_j>0\) proves it. There are \(J_s=|D_m|^s\) transcripts, including \(J_0=1\), and \(1\le J_s\le2^{ms}\). Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_s(3\gamma)
&\ge4^{-(n-ms)}e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}
\sum_{\vartheta\in D_m^s}Y_\vartheta(\gamma)^3\\
&\ge4^{-(n-ms)}e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}J_s^{-2}Z_s(\gamma)^3
\ge4^{-n}e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}Z_s(\gamma)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step uses \(J_s^2\le4^{ms}\). This proves the asserted spatial bound for every level, including both endpoints, and its weaker residual version follows from \(E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\ge E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}\).

The admissible levels at height \(t\) are exactly \(s=0,\ldots,Q_t\): this is the definition of the entrance heights, including the Boolean level at height zero. Therefore the cells \((s,t)\) with \(a_s\le t\le k\) are exactly \(H=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(Q_t+1)\) nonempty cells. To check the uniform bound on \(H\), first consider \(k=8\). Here \(Q_t=0\) for \(0\le t<8\) and \(Q_8=4\), whence \(H=8+5=13\le25=(Q+1)^2\). For \(k\ge9\), one has \(b_9=\lfloor512/72\rfloor=7\) and \(Q_9=11\). The quantity \(2^\ell/(8\ell)\) is increasing for integers \(\ell\ge1\), because the ratio at consecutive indices is \(2\ell/(\ell+1)\ge1\). Thus \(b_\ell\ge1\) for every \(\ell\ge10\), and \(Q\ge11+(k-9)=k+2\). Since \(Q_t\le Q\), it follows that \(H\le(k+1)(Q+1)\le(Q+1)^2\). This treats the small endpoint without the false inequality \(k+1\le Q+1\) at \(k=8\).

Let \(x_{s,t}=e^{\beta t}Z_s(\gamma)>0\) on these \(H\) cells. The full original normalizer is exactly \(Z=\sum_{s,t}x_{s,t}\). Applying the spatial bound cell by cell gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf J\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}^3
&=\frac1{Z^3}\sum_{\substack{0\le s\le Q\\a_s\le t\le k}}e^{3\beta t}Z_s(3\gamma)\\
&\ge4^{-n}e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}
\frac{\sum_{s,t}x_{s,t}^3}{(\sum_{s,t}x_{s,t})^3}
\ge\frac{4^{-n}e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}}{H^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality is the same finite cubic inequality, now for the \(H\) cell masses. Multiplying by \(4^N\), with \(N=n+1\), proves the stated activity lower bounds. No estimate in this argument depends on the sign or size of \(\beta\); all allowed heights and their exact weights remain in \(Z\).

If \(A_0<1\), the stronger bound implies \(4e^{E_{3,\mathrm{path}}}/H^2<1\), so taking logarithms proves the two residual thresholds and the stated off-block entropy consequence. Conversely, \(E_{3,\mathrm{path}}\ge2\log H-\log4\) makes that lower bound at least one.

It remains to prove the strong-set estimate for an active law. For \(0\le x\le\log2\), put \(v=(e^x-1)/(e^x+1)=\tanh(x/2)\). The elementary inequalities \(e^x-1\ge x\) and \(e^x+1\le3\) give \(v\ge x/3\), while \(v\le1/3\). Hence \(R_3(x)-1=3v^2\ge x^2/3\) and \(R_3(x)\le4/3\). Integrating \(1/t\) on \([1,y]\) gives \(\log y\ge(y-1)/y\) for \(y\ge1\), and consequently
$$
\log R_3(x)\ge\frac{R_3(x)-1}{R_3(x)}\ge\frac{x^2}{4}
\qquad(0\le x\le\log2).
$$
Here \(\varepsilon\le1/8<\log2\), since \(m\ge2\), \(Q\ge4\), and \(\log2=\int_1^2dt/t\ge1/2\). By evenness and monotonicity of \(R_3\), each \(u\notin T_0\) with \(|\gamma_u|>\varepsilon\) has \(\zeta_u\ge\varepsilon^2/4\). Summing these nonnegative contributions within \(E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}\) proves
$$
|T\setminus T_0|\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\le E_{3,\mathrm{rem}},
\qquad |T|\le mQ+4m^2Q^2E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}.
$$
This also handles \(E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}=0\), which forces \(T=T_0\). For the numerical constant, use the active threshold \(E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}<4\log(Q+1)-\log4<4\log(Q+1)\) and \(\log(Q+1)\le Q\). Substituting \(m=2(Q+1)^4\) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
mQ+4m^2Q^2E_{3,\mathrm{rem}}
&<2Q(Q+1)^4+64Q^3(Q+1)^8\\
&\le64(1+Q^3)(Q+1)^8
\le64(Q+1)^{11}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The middle inequality uses \(2Q(Q+1)^4\le64(Q+1)^8\); the last uses \(1+Q^3\le(Q+1)^3\). Thus no asymptotic estimate or unhandled finite \(k\) is hidden in the constant. Outside \(T\), the definition gives \(|\gamma_u|\le\varepsilon\). Every full rooted path has exactly \(mQ\) coordinates in its child blocks, so its contribution to \(\Pi(\gamma\mathbf1_{\mathcal T_{m,Q}\setminus T})\) is at most \(mQ\varepsilon=1\). Taking the maximum proves the required path-variation bound.

Finally, use the one-factor map to 8ab782832d1c7da2 specified in the statement. For the singleton row at \(\mathbf J\), its geometric weight and row mass both equal \(p_{\mathbf J}\), and its log score is zero. Therefore \(b^{(\mathrm m)}=\sum_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf J}^3\), \(\nu^{(\mathrm m)}=0\), and \(B=4^Nb^{(\mathrm m)}=A_0\). When \(A_0\ge1\), this is exactly the required inactive certificate. The active conclusion proved here is only the entropy and coordinate reduction already stated.
