## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge8\), define
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\quad(8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_t=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}b_\ell,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\quad(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell),\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,\quad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\quad N=n+1.
$$
Choose any symmetric saturated-chain partition \(\mathcal C\) of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\). Let \(D_m\) be its nonterminal set and \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) its injective successor, as in 032423921efad6c8. Spatial coordinates are indexed by
\(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\). For a Boolean source \(b\), follow its successive chosen children while each queried child block lies in \(D_m\), stopping at the first failure or depth \(Q\). Write its resulting path as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and put
$$
z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r},\qquad
A_{m,Q}=\bigcup_b\lbrace z_s(b):0\le s\le h(b)\rbrace,
$$
$$
\widehat A_k=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
Here \(\operatorname{lev}(z)\) is the number of coordinates equal to two. This is precisely the complete fixed canonical lift of 604889294a17d7df in its log-affine specialization; every original height atom is present.

Choose \(i\in[m]\), viewed also as a depth-one node. Let \(\theta\) be an arbitrary real field supported on the union of the whole root child block \([m]\) and the whole child block \(\lbrace ij:1\le j\le m\rbrace\) of node \(i\). Let \(\lambda\) vanish on both these blocks and satisfy exactly the whole-child-block variation normalization of dbbd63823b5f4870:
$$
\Pi(\lambda)=\max_{\substack{u_0=\varnothing,\ u_r\in[m]^r\\u_r\text{ child of }u_{r-1}\ (1\le r\le Q)}}
\sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\lambda_{u_rj}|\le1.
$$
For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), let \(\gamma=\theta+\lambda\), and retain the full original law
$$
w_J=e^{\gamma\cdot z_J+\beta t_J},\qquad
Z=\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}w_J,\qquad p_J=w_J/Z.

Use precisely the mixed-row convention of 8ab782832d1c7da2. At each supported center \(J\), a row \(R_J\) consists of its separately fixed singleton and pairwise disjoint pairs or triples of distinct other supported points, each with arithmetic mean \(J\). Rows at different centers may overlap. If \(\mathcal O\) is the chosen cycle containing \(I\), define
$$
g_J(I)=\left(\prod_{I'\in\mathcal O}p_{I'}\right)^{1/|\mathcal O|},\qquad

$$

$$
$$
b_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jp_JH_J^2,\qquad
q_J=p_JH_J^2/b_{\mathrm m},\qquad
\nu_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jq_JV_J,
\qquad A_0=4^N\sum_Jp_J^3,\quad B=4^Nb_{\mathrm m}.
$$
Thus every row object here is exactly the superscript \((\mathrm m)\) object of 8ab782832d1c7da2, with the superscript suppressed; these are not identified with full reflected rows unless those rows are selected.

There is one finite absolute constant \(C\ge4\), independent of \(k,\mathcal C,i,\theta,\lambda,\beta\), such that every active law \(A_0<1\) admits such rows with

## Proof

Write
$$
\delta=2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}.
$$
Fact 032423921efad6c8 gives \(0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2}\), the source-to-level injectivity, and exactly \(2^m\delta\) symmetric chains. For \(j\in[m]\), let
$$
\mathcal L_j=\lbrace c\in D_m:i_m(c)=j\rbrace.
$$
A saturated chain adds coordinate \(j\) at most once, so \(|\mathcal L_j|\le2^m\delta\). The chain containing the empty set runs through every cardinality up to the full set and adds every coordinate, so \(|\mathcal L_j|\ge1\). The same upper bound applies to inverse edges having a prescribed label. At most two words in each chain lack two successive predecessors. These counts hold for every allowed \(\mathcal C\).

First suppose \(k\ge64\), and put \(x=e^\beta\). Retain the scalar notation of 1af079babe686d6e:
$$
q_t=Q_t,\qquad d_t=\min(t,k-t),\qquad r_t=1+2d_t,
$$
$$
c_0=1,\qquad c_r=1+3\cdot2^{r-1}\quad(r\ge1),
$$
$$
H_0(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^t,\qquad
U_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}q_tx^t,\qquad
T_{\mathrm v}(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}r_t^2x^{3t},
$$
$$
T_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^{3t}\sum_{s=1}^{q_t}c_{q_t-s}^2.
\tag{4}
$$
The height-indexed \(q_t\) is the canonical level count, distinct from the energy probability \(q_J\). Define the two additional scalar channels by
$$
V_1(x)=\sum_{t=8}^{k}x^t,\qquad
W_1(x)=\sum_{t=8}^{k}x^{3t},
$$
$$
U_1(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(q_t-1)_+x^t,\qquad
T_1(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^{3t}\sum_{s=2}^{q_t}c_{q_t-s}^2.
\tag{5}
$$
Here \(V_1,W_1\) are auxiliary scalar height sums, not row variances. The pair \(U_1,T_1\) has exactly the depth-one entry meaning of the canonical entry sums. Empty inner sums are zero. All sums appearing in denominators below are positive, since \(q_8=4\).

We use four disjoint classes of original centers. A Boolean center has spatial level zero. An avoidance center has positive level and first root successor label different from \(i\). An early center has level exactly one and root successor label \(i\). An entry center has level at least two and root successor label \(i\). The Boolean centers use all vertical reflection pairs and their singleton, giving row size \(r_t\). Every early center uses only its singleton.

At an avoidance or entry center of level \(s\) and height \(t\), put \(r=q_t-s\). If \(r=0\), use the singleton. If \(r\ge1\), test the two-predecessor and two inverse-continuation-path conditions of equations (4)-(5) of abbc24783c13e0f6 below the existing level-\(s\) prefix. On success use its \(2^{r-1}\) triples and the singleton at the same actual height \(t\), giving row size \(c_r\); on failure use only the singleton. That accepted local construction verifies distinctness, disjointness, the center-mean identity, and membership. Its sources have spatial levels \(s+r,s+r,s\), and \(s+r=q_t\), so all are present at this height in the complete support. The construction and its checks use only symmetric saturated-chain properties and thus apply to every allowed partition, as also used in 1af079babe686d6e.

These rows preserve both strong blocks. Boolean rows change only height. Avoidance rows modify only descendants of their existing prefix, whose root label differs from \(i\). Entry rows preserve their entire existing prefix and modify coordinates at depths greater than \(s\ge2\), thus preserving both the root block and the block of children of \(i\). Early rows are singletons. No row modifies a strong block whose coefficients have not been bounded.

We next justify the bounded-field census used for the avoidance and entry classes. Let \(P_a\), for \(a\ge0\), denote the Boolean product sum over precisely the coordinates outside both strong blocks:
$$
P_a=\prod_{u\notin[m]\,\cup\,\lbrace ij:j\in[m]\rbrace}(1+e^{a\lambda_u}).
\tag{6}
$$
The corresponding auxiliary product law assigns probability proportional to \(e^{a\lambda\cdot b}\) on these Boolean coordinates. It does not replace the original law. At a fresh weak child block of a node \(v\), put \(A_v=\sum_j|\lambda_{vj}|\le1\). The density of its product law relative to the fair law is between \(e^{-aA_v}\) and \(e^{aA_v}\). The label counts above imply that a forward or inverse successor has any prescribed label with probability at most \(e^a\delta\). A forward-successor or one-predecessor test fails with probability at most \(e^a\delta\); a two-predecessor test fails with probability at most \(2e^a\delta\).

These statements remain valid conditionally at adaptive fresh queries, since the queried block's location is determined before its independent bits are exposed. For a stopped forward path wholly below the already fixed strong prefix, let \(S\) sum \(|\lambda_u|\) at its successfully selected coordinates. Then
$$
0\le S\le1,\qquad \mathbb E S\le e^a\delta.
\tag{7}
$$
Indeed, the conditional expected contribution at a queried block is at most \(e^a\delta A_v\), and the sum of \(A_v\) on each realized queried path is at most \(\Pi(\lambda)\le1\). Summing the conditional expectations proves (7). This is precisely the adaptive bounded-field mechanism of 1af079babe686d6e, now applied only after the strong prefix is fixed. No future success event is conditioned on in (7).

For an avoidance center, fix its exact root source and label. A level-\(s\) prefix then needs \(s-1\) weak forward steps. For an entry center fix additionally its exact strong child-block source and the selected child of \(i\). Its remaining level-\(s\) prefix needs \(s-2\) weak forward steps. In either case the local triple test uses one two-predecessor test and \(2(r-1)\) one-predecessor tests when \(r\ge1\). Their total failure probability under the auxiliary cubic product law is at most \(2Qe^3\delta\). For \(r=0\), only the forward prefix is required, and the same bound holds.

Let \(u\) be the signed sum of selected weak coefficients on the stopped forward prefix. Since \(|u|\le S\le1\),
$$
|e^{2u}-1|\le2e^2S,\qquad |e^{6u}-1|\le6e^6S.

Equation (7), first with \(a=1\) and then with \(a=3\), gives the uniform bounds
$$
\mathbb E\left[e^{2u}\mathbf1_{\lbrace\text{forward prefix succeeds}\rbrace}\right]
\le1+2e^3\delta,

$$
\mathbb E\left[e^{6u}\mathbf1_{\lbrace\text{forward prefix and row test succeed}\rbrace}\right]
\ge1-2Qe^3\delta-6e^9\delta.

$$
The stopped sums are defined on all outcomes before these success indicators are imposed. For the cubic estimate, use \(e^{6u}\mathbf1_G\ge\mathbf1_G-|e^{6u}-1|\). Thus no density comparison is applied to either arbitrary strong field.

Hence (8) holds with \(1+\varepsilon\) and \(1-\varepsilon\), uniformly in every fixed strong source, label, level, and height.

These are normalized finite sums, not normalizers of a replacement law. The Boolean and full-successor sums are the \(\mathfrak B,\mathfrak E\) sums of 1af079babe686d6e on the indicated block, evaluated at the scaled field. The root-successor sum is split into the exact classes entering \(i\) and avoiding \(i\). Every sum is positive: the full chain supplies every label and \(m>1\).

The four disjoint center classes give the following upper bound for the full original denominator:

\begin{aligned}

The Boolean contribution is exact. For an avoidance point, its strong child block at \(i\) is still Boolean and never queried by that prefix; the root successor gives the exact \(\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,\ne i}(1)\). The remaining weak prefix is bounded by (8)-(9), and at height \(t\) there are \(q_t\) eligible positive levels, producing \(U_*\).

For an early point, the level is exactly one, so its strong child block is Boolean regardless of whether it has a successor. Its root source contributes exactly \(\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,i}(1)\), and there are no weak spikes. At height \(t\), the number of entry levels is \((q_t-1)_+\), giving \(U_1\). These are exhaustive disjoint classes, and (11) retains every original source and height in \(Z\).

For positive numbers \(y_c\) on a nonempty finite set \(S\), cubic Hölder says
$$
\left(\sum_{c\in S}y_c\right)^3\le |S|^2\sum_{c\in S}y_c^3.
\tag{14}
$$
Apply it to all Boolean weights, all child-successor weights, and the non-\(i\) root-successor weights in (10), each on at most \(2^m\) words. The resulting inequalities are
$$
\mathfrak B_{\varnothing}(3)\ge\mathfrak B_{\varnothing}(1)^3,\quad
\mathfrak B_i(3)\ge\mathfrak B_i(1)^3,\quad
\mathfrak E_i(3)\ge\mathfrak E_i(1)^3,\quad
\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,\ne i}(3)\ge\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,\ne i}(1)^3.
$$
For the exact entering root class \(\mathcal L_i\), the stronger count \(|\mathcal L_i|\le2^m\delta\) gives instead
$$
\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,i}(3)
\ge\frac{2^{2m}}{|\mathcal L_i|^2}\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,i}(1)^3
\ge\delta^{-2}\mathfrak E_{\varnothing,i}(1)^3.
\tag{15}
$$
This bound holds for arbitrary signs and magnitudes of the root field, even if its entering class has essentially all weighted root-successor mass. It is a finite-sum cubic inequality using the original class weights, not a rarity assertion under that weighted law.

Every coordinate factor obeys \((1+y)^3\le4(1+y^3)\). Since \(P_a\) includes exactly \(n-2m\) weak coordinates,
$$
\mathcal D:=4^{n-2m}\frac{P_3}{P_1^3}\ge1.

$$
\left(\sum_j a_jh_j\right)^3
\le\left(\sum_j a_j^3t_j\right)
\left(\sum_j\frac{h_j^{3/2}}{\sqrt{t_j}}\right)^2.
\tag{17}
$$

$$
v_*(x)=\frac{H_0(x)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{T_{\mathrm v}(x)}},\qquad
e_*(x)=\frac{U_*(x)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{T_*(x)}},\qquad

+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}\delta e_1\right]^2}.
