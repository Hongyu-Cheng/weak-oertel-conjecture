## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
 \quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
 \varnothing\ne A\subseteq\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}
\]
be finite, put
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
\tag{2}
\]
and choose weights
\[
 0<w_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in A).
\tag{3}
\]
For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), set
\[
 A_\xi:=\{y\in A:y\equiv\xi\pmod2\},
 \qquad
 M_\xi:=\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the full-product masses from accepted fact `35ca05c7683b7d12` by
\[
 B_{\xi,m}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac1{M_\xi}
 \sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
 w_uw_v,&M_\xi>0,\\[2ex]
 0,&M_\xi=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad m\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{5}
\]
The sum is over ordered pairs and includes the diagonal. Accepted fact
`35ca05c7683b7d12` gives finite support, exact mass conservation, an
actual common-tube realization for every nonzero \((\xi,m)\), and the
target-fiber bound \(B_{\xi,m}\leq a_m/4\). The conclusions below add a
source-feasible reflected-square atlas to these accepted full-product
labels.

For \(M_\xi>0\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), let
\[
 \mathcal H_{\xi,m}:=
 \bigl\{[h]:=\{h,-h\}:h\in\mathbb Z^n,
          \ m+h,m-h\in A_\xi\bigr\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Choose one representative \(h\in[h]\) for each orbit, taking \(h=0\)
for \([h]=[0]\), and define
\[
 b_{\xi,m,[h]}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{w_m^2}{M_\xi},&[h]=[0],\\[2ex]
 \displaystyle\frac{2w_{m+h}w_{m-h}}{M_\xi},&[h]\ne[0].
 \end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
The second expression is independent of the chosen representative. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{[h]\in\mathcal H_{\xi,m}}b_{\xi,m,[h]}
 =B_{\xi,m}.}
\tag{8}
\]

Put
\[
 \alpha_m:=\sum_\xi B_{\xi,m},
 \qquad
 B_m^{\max}:=\max_\xi B_{\xi,m},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathrm{col}}:=
 \sum_m(\alpha_m-B_m^{\max}).
\tag{9}
\]
Fix any total order on the parity classes. For
\(\xi<\eta\), \([h]\in\mathcal H_{\xi,m}\), and
\([k]\in\mathcal H_{\eta,m}\), define, whenever \(\alpha_m>0\),
\[
 \theta_{m;\xi,[h];\eta,[k]}
 :=\frac{2b_{\xi,m,[h]}b_{\eta,m,[k]}}{\alpha_m}.
\tag{10}
\]
Terms with zero mass are omitted; when \(\alpha_m=0\), there are no
square labels over \(m\). If
\[
 \Theta_m:=
 \sum_{\xi<\eta}
 \sum_{[h]\in\mathcal H_{\xi,m}}
 \sum_{[k]\in\mathcal H_{\eta,m}}
 \theta_{m;\xi,[h];\eta,[k]},
 \qquad
 \Theta_\square:=\sum_m\Theta_m,
\tag{11}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_m=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \alpha_m-\frac{\sum_\xi B_{\xi,m}^2}{\alpha_m},
 &\alpha_m>0,\\[2ex]
 0,&\alpha_m=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \Theta_\square\geq C_{\mathrm{col}}.}
\tag{12}
\]

For one positive square label
\(\lambda=(m;\xi,[h];\eta,[k])\), retain multiplicities in the source
occurrence multiset
\[
 \mathcal O_\lambda
 :=[m+h,m-h,m+k,m-k].
\tag{13}
\]
In particular, a zero direction contributes two occurrences of the same
source. For \(y\in A\), let
\[
 D_y:=
 \sum_\lambda
 \theta_\lambda\,
 \operatorname{mult}_y(\mathcal O_\lambda).
\tag{14}
\]
If \(\xi\) is the parity class of \(y\), then \(M_\xi>0\) and
\[
 \boxed{
 D_y\leq
 4w_y\frac{M_\xi-w_y}{M_\xi}
 +\frac{4w_y^2}{M_\xi}
 =4w_y\leq a_y.}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently all source occurrences are simultaneously realizable. View
each entry of (13) as a tagged occurrence \(o\), and write \(y(o)\) for
its source. For every tagged occurrence in a square \(\lambda\), the
density
\[
 f_{\lambda,o}(x):=
 \frac{\theta_\lambda}{a_{y(o)}}\mathbf1_{C_{y(o)}}(x)
\tag{16}
\]
has mass \(\theta_\lambda\), normalized barycenter \(c_{y(o)}\), and the
sum of all occurrence densities in \(C_y\), with repetitions in (13)
counted separately, is pointwise at most one.

Let
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{17}
\]
For a positive square label, suppose that the chosen representatives
\(h,k\) are linearly independent. Define
\[
 \Phi_\lambda(s,t)
 :=m+h+s(k-h)+t(-k-h),
 \qquad(s,t)\in\mathbb R^2.
\tag{18}
\]
This is an integral affine injection of rank two, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_\lambda(0,0)&=m+h,&
 \Phi_\lambda(1,0)&=m+k,\\
 \Phi_\lambda(0,1)&=m-k,&
 \Phi_\lambda(1,1)&=m-h.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
At these four product vertices choose, respectively,
\[
 X_{00}:=c_{m+h},
 \quad X_{10}:=c_{m+k},
 \quad X_{01}:=c_{m-k},
 \quad X_{11}:=c_{m-h}.
\tag{20}
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), write
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\bigl\{
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace},\ x\in H
 \bigr\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then every corner satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_C\bigl(\Phi_\lambda(i,j),X_{ij}\bigr)
 \geq\gamma_d\theta_\lambda
 \qquad(i,j\in\{0,1\}).}
\tag{22}
\]
In the normalization of the factorized lattice-polysimplex theorem in
accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`, this gives an actual rank-two packet of
raw energy
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal E_\lambda=4\gamma_d\theta_\lambda.}
\tag{23}
\]
If \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), define, for Borel
\(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\),
\[
 \mu_C(E):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in E\},
\]
and put
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in S}
 \inf\{\mu_C(G):G\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in G\},
\tag{24}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\theta_\lambda.}
\tag{25}
\]

If either reflected direction is zero or if \(h,k\) are linearly
dependent, the occurrence realization (15)-(16) remains valid, but the
label is only a degenerate rank-at-most-one reflected packet and
(23)-(25) are not asserted from it. Even in the independent case, no
affine parallelogram identity among the four continuous centroids in
(20) is assumed or needed. Finally, (12) and simultaneous source
feasibility do not imply
\(h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\Theta_\square\), nor do they produce one
packet of energy \(4\gamma_d\Theta_\square\): different square labels
use different charts, and their local packet energies are not additive
without a separate aggregation theorem.

## Proof

Fix \(\xi,m\) with \(M_\xi>0\). Every ordered pair
\((u,v)\) occurring in (5) has the unique integral half-difference
\(h=(u-v)/2\), because \(u,v\) have the same parity, and it satisfies
\(u=m+h\), \(v=m-h\). The diagonal pair corresponds to \(h=0\) and
occurs once. Every nonzero orbit \([h]=\{h,-h\}\) corresponds to the
two ordered pairs
\((m+h,m-h)\) and \((m-h,m+h)\). Their respective contributions give
exactly the two cases in (7), proving (8). This also shows directly that
the reflected-direction labels have finite support.

For a direction label \(r=(\xi,m,[h])\), let \(L_r\) be the sum of the
masses of all square labels containing \(r\). If \(\alpha_m>0\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_r
 &=\frac{2b_{\xi,m,[h]}}{\alpha_m}
   \sum_{\eta\ne\xi}B_{\eta,m}\\
 &=\frac{2b_{\xi,m,[h]}
          (\alpha_m-B_{\xi,m})}{\alpha_m}
 \leq2b_{\xi,m,[h]}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
When \(\alpha_m=0\), there is no such positive label. Summing (10) first
over direction orbits and using (8) gives, for \(\alpha_m>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Theta_m
 &=\frac2{\alpha_m}\sum_{\xi<\eta}
   B_{\xi,m}B_{\eta,m}\\
 &=\frac{\alpha_m^2-\sum_\xi B_{\xi,m}^2}{\alpha_m}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sum_\xi B_{\xi,m}^2
 \leq B_m^{\max}\sum_\xi B_{\xi,m}
 =B_m^{\max}\alpha_m.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(\Theta_m\geq\alpha_m-B_m^{\max}\). This is also true when
\(\alpha_m=0\), and summing over \(m\) proves (12).

We now prove the simultaneous source bound. Fix \(y\in A_\xi\). Every
nonloop reflected direction containing \(y\) has a unique other endpoint
\(z\in A_\xi\setminus\{y\}\), midpoint \(m=(y+z)/2\), and mass
\[
 b_{\xi,m,[(y-z)/2]}=\frac{2w_yw_z}{M_\xi}.
\tag{29}
\]
The source \(y\) occurs once in that reflected pair. By (26), the total
occurrence demand charged through this direction is at most
\(4w_yw_z/M_\xi\). Summing over all such \(z\) gives
\[
 4w_y\frac{M_\xi-w_y}{M_\xi}.
\tag{30}
\]
The loop direction is based at \(m=y\) and has mass
\(b_{\xi,y,[0]}=w_y^2/M_\xi\). It contributes two occurrences of \(y\)
to every square containing it. Equation (26) therefore bounds its total
occurrence demand by
\[
 2L_{(\xi,y,[0])}
 \leq4b_{\xi,y,[0]}
 =\frac{4w_y^2}{M_\xi}.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (30)-(31) prove (15). For each tagged occurrence \(o\) based
at \(y\), (16) is between zero and one because
\(\theta_\lambda\leq D_y\leq a_y\), and
\[
 \int_{C_y}f_{\lambda,o}=\theta_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \frac1{\theta_\lambda}
 \int_{C_y}x f_{\lambda,o}(x)\,dx=c_y.
\tag{32}
\]
After summing over occurrences at \(y\), the pointwise density is the
constant \(D_y/a_y\leq1\) on \(C_y\). This proves the simultaneous
feasibility claim, including the loop multiplicity.

Fix a positive square label with independent representatives \(h,k\).
The two direction columns of (18) are
\[
 U_1=k-h,
 \qquad
 U_2=-k-h.
\tag{33}
\]
They are integral. If \(rU_1+sU_2=0\), then
\[
 (-r-s)h+(r-s)k=0.
\]
Independence of \(h,k\) gives \(r+s=r-s=0\), hence \(r=s=0\).
Therefore (18) is an integral rank-two affine injection, and direct
substitution proves (19).

For every corner source \(y\) of this square, (16) gives
\[
 c_y\in\mathsf M_{\theta_\lambda}(C_y).
\tag{34}
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applies because
\(0<\theta_\lambda\leq a_y\), and gives
\[
 \delta_C(y,c_y)\geq\gamma_d\theta_\lambda.
\tag{35}
\]
This proves (22), including all four actual fiber points.

Apply accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5` with two rank-one factors,
\[
 \Delta_1=\Delta_2=[0,1],
 \qquad
 U_1=k-h,
 \qquad
 U_2=-k-h,
\tag{36}
\]
with affine base point \(m+h\) and the four points (20). For both
vertices of both factors take the constant root
\[
 (\gamma_d\theta_\lambda)^{1/(2d)}.
\tag{37}
\]
The product of the two roots is
\((\gamma_d\theta_\lambda)^{1/d}\), so (22) verifies the theorem's
product-vertex depth hypothesis. Each interval has exactly two lattice
points. Each factor energy is therefore
\(2(\gamma_d\theta_\lambda)^{1/2}\), and their product is
\(4\gamma_d\theta_\lambda\), which proves (23). The depth conclusion of
that accepted theorem, with total rank two, is
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq2^{-2}\mathcal E_\lambda
 =\gamma_d\theta_\lambda,
\]
proving (25).

When \(h\) or \(k\) is zero or they are linearly dependent, the two
columns in (33) have rank at most one, so the rank-two application just
made is unavailable. The source ledger did not use independence, so it
continues to hold in those cases. The factorized theorem uses arbitrary
actual points at the four product vertices and does not require an
affine identity among their continuous coordinates. Finally, its
conclusion is labelwise. Neither that theorem nor the occurrence ledger
adds the energies of packets on different charts. This proves all stated
caveats.
