## Statement

For every even integer \(k\geq2\), put
\[
d:=k^2,\qquad N:=k^4,
\]
and define
\[
C_k:=
\left\{
(t,x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d:
-N\leq t\leq N,\quad
0\leq x_j\leq1+\frac tN\quad(1\leq j\leq d)
\right\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z\), let
\[
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C_k\}.
\]
For \(a,b\geq0\), define
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\left(\frac{2a^{1/d}b^{1/d}}{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}\right)^d,
&ab>0,\\[2mm]
0,&ab=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
E_k:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{-z}),
\qquad
P_k:=w_0+\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}\min\{w_v,w_{-v}\}.
\]
Then \(C_k\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body and
\[
w_z=
\begin{cases}
\left(1+\dfrac zN\right)^d,&-N\leq z\leq N,\\[1mm]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover,
\[
E_k=
1+2\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}
\left(1-\frac{v^2}{N^2}\right)^d,
\qquad
P_k=
1+\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}
\left(1-\frac vN\right)^d,
\]
and, for every even \(k\geq2\),
\[
\frac{P_k}{E_k}<\frac{5}{3k}.
\]
The exact asymptotic constants are
\[
\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\ k\ {\rm even}}}\frac{P_k}{k^2}=1,
\qquad
\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\ k\ {\rm even}}}\frac{E_k}{k^3}=\sqrt{\pi},
\qquad
\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\ k\ {\rm even}}}
k\frac{P_k}{E_k}=\frac1{\sqrt{\pi}}.
\]

More generally, for a convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\), write \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\). For \(m\in\mathbb Z\) with \(w_m>0\), let \(\mathcal R_m\) contain one representative from every unordered nonzero reflected pair \(\{v,-v\}\) with \(w_{m+v}w_{m-v}>0\), and put
\[
P(C,m):=w_m+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\min\{w_{m+v},w_{m-v}\},
\qquad
E(C,m):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z}).
\]
Then
\[
\inf_{\substack{
d\geq1,\ C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\ {\rm a\ convex\ body}\\
m\in\mathbb Z,\ w_m>0}}
\frac{P(C,m)}{E(C,m)}=0.
\]
Consequently there is no positive absolute constant \(c\) such that \(P(C,m)\geq cE(C,m)\) for every such \(d,C,m\).

## Proof

The set \(C_k\) is the intersection of the finitely many closed affine halfspaces
\[
t\geq-N,\quad t\leq N,\quad x_j\geq0,\quad
x_j-\frac tN\leq1\quad(1\leq j\leq d).
\]
It is therefore closed and convex. The inequalities imply \(0\leq x_j\leq2\), so it is bounded and hence compact. The point
\[
\left(0,\frac12,\ldots,\frac12\right)
\]
satisfies every defining inequality strictly. Thus \(C_k\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\), so it is full-dimensional.

If \(-N\leq z\leq N\), the fiber over \(z\) is exactly
\[
\prod_{j=1}^d\left[0,1+\frac zN\right].
\]
Its \(d\)-volume is \((1+z/N)^d\), including the degenerate endpoint \(z=-N\), whose volume is \(0\). If \(z\notin[-N,N]\), the fiber is empty. This proves the asserted formula for \(w_z\), and in particular \(w_0=1>0\).

For \(1\leq v\leq N-1\), both reflected fibers have positive volume and
\[
w_v^{1/d}=1+\frac vN,\qquad
w_{-v}^{1/d}=1-\frac vN.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_v,w_{-v})
=
\left(
\frac{2(1+v/N)(1-v/N)}
{(1+v/N)+(1-v/N)}
\right)^d
=
\left(1-\frac{v^2}{N^2}\right)^d.
\]
The two endpoint terms \(z=\pm N\) vanish because one of the two reflected volumes is zero, as do all terms outside the support. The central term is
\(\operatorname{HM}_d(w_0,w_0)=1\). Since the denominator is an ordered sum, every noncentral unordered pair occurs twice. This gives
\[
E_k=
1+2\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}
\left(1-\frac{v^2}{N^2}\right)^d.
\]
Also \(w_v>w_{-v}\) for \(1\leq v\leq N\), and the \(v=N\) minimum is zero. Hence the unordered pair mass is
\[
P_k=
1+\sum_{v=1}^{N-1}
\left(1-\frac vN\right)^d.
\]

We next prove the uniform quantitative bound. The function
\[
f(x):=\left(1-\frac xN\right)^d,\qquad 0\leq x\leq N,
\]
is decreasing. Comparing its right-endpoint sum with its integral gives
\[
P_k
=f(0)+\sum_{v=1}^{N}f(v)
\leq
1+\int_0^N f(x)\,dx
=1+\frac{N}{d+1}
<1+k^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(f(N)=0\), so inserting that term does not change \(P_k\), and the last inequality uses \(N=k^4\) and \(d=k^2\).

Because \(k\) is even,
\[
M:=\frac{k^3}{2}
\]
is an integer. For \(1\leq v\leq M\),
\[
\frac{v^2}{N^2}
\leq
\frac{M^2}{N^2}
=\frac1{4k^2}
=\frac1{4d}.
\]
Bernoulli's inequality yields
\[
\left(1-\frac1{4d}\right)^d
\geq1-\frac d{4d}
=\frac34.
\]
Retaining only the first \(M\) positive terms in the exact formula for \(E_k\) therefore gives
\[
E_k
\geq1+2M\cdot\frac34
=1+\frac{3k^3}{4}.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining (1) and (2),
\[
\frac{P_k}{E_k}
<
\frac{1+k^2}{1+3k^3/4}.
\]
All denominators are positive, and
\[
5\left(1+\frac{3k^3}{4}\right)
-3k(1+k^2)
=
5+\frac{3k}{4}(k^2-4)
>0
\]
for \(k\geq2\). Thus
\[
\frac{P_k}{E_k}<\frac5{3k}.
\]

For completeness, the sharper asymptotics can also be obtained by exact sum-integral comparisons. The same monotonicity comparison, now using both left and right endpoint sums, gives
\[
\frac{N}{d+1}\leq P_k\leq1+\frac{N}{d+1}.
\]
After division by \(k^2\), the two bounds converge to \(1\), because
\[
\frac{N}{(d+1)k^2}=\frac{k^2}{k^2+1}.
\]
Hence \(P_k/k^2\to1\).

Let
\[
g(x):=\left(1-\frac{x^2}{N^2}\right)^d,\qquad 0\leq x\leq N,
\]
and \(I_k:=\int_0^N g(x)\,dx\). Since \(g\) is decreasing and
\[
E_k=2\sum_{v=0}^{N-1}g(v)-1,
\]
the left and right endpoint comparisons give
\[
2I_k-1\leq E_k\leq2I_k+1.
\tag{3}
\]
Using \(N=k^4\), \(d=k^2\), first \(u=x/N\), and then \(s=ku\), we obtain
\[
\frac{2I_k}{k^3}
=2k\int_0^1(1-u^2)^{k^2}\,du
=2\int_0^k
\left(1-\frac{s^2}{k^2}\right)^{k^2}\,ds.
\tag{4}
\]
Extend the integrand in (4) by zero for \(s>k\). For each fixed \(s\geq0\), it converges to \(e^{-s^2}\). Moreover,
\[
0\leq
\mathbf 1_{[0,k]}(s)
\left(1-\frac{s^2}{k^2}\right)^{k^2}
\leq e^{-s^2},
\]
because \(1-r\leq e^{-r}\) for \(0\leq r\leq1\). Dominated convergence in (4) therefore gives
\[
\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\ k\ {\rm even}}}
\frac{2I_k}{k^3}
=2\int_0^\infty e^{-s^2}\,ds
=\sqrt{\pi}.
\]
The last equality follows, for example, by squaring the positive integral and using polar coordinates:
\[
\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-s^2}\,ds\right)^2
=
\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2}r\,dr\,d\theta
=\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
Equation (3) now implies \(E_k/k^3\to\sqrt{\pi}\). Combining this with \(P_k/k^2\to1\) proves
\[
k\frac{P_k}{E_k}\longrightarrow\frac1{\sqrt{\pi}}.
\]

For the final infimum, apply the general definitions to \(C=C_k\) and \(m=0\). The only nonzero positive reflected pairs are \(\{v,-v\}\) for \(1\leq v\leq N-1\), so \(P(C_k,0)=P_k\), while the ordered root-harmonic energy is \(E(C_k,0)=E_k\). Both are positive because their central terms equal \(w_0=1\). Given any \(c>0\), choose an even \(k>5/(3c)\). Then
\[
0<\frac{P(C_k,0)}{E(C_k,0)}
<\frac5{3k}<c.
\]
All such ratios are nonnegative, so their infimum is exactly \(0\). This disproves every positive absolute fixed-midpoint scalar conversion \(P(C,m)\geq cE(C,m)\). It makes no claim that \(m=0\) is the midpoint selected by a global harmonic-synchronization argument, nor that the same body lacks a different midpoint with a stronger conversion.
