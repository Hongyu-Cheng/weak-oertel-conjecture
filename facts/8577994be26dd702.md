## Statement

Fix integers $n\ge1,d\ge2$ and $R>n\varepsilon>0$. With $B_d$ the closed Euclidean unit ball of volume $\kappa_d$, take
$$
C=\{(z,x):z\in[0,1]^n,\ \lVert x\rVert\le R-\varepsilon\textstyle\sum_i z_i\}.
$$
Use the sum of the $d$-volumes on its integer fibers, of radii $R_z=R-\varepsilon\sum_i z_i$ at $z\in\{0,1\}^n$. At every sampled point the raw closed-halfspace depth is its own-ball depth. The unique global maximizer is $(0,0)$, of depth $Q=\kappa_d R^d/2$. Its complete critical cap menu consists of the closed caps $a\cdot z+u\cdot x\ge0$ with $u\ne0$ and $a\cdot z\le-R_z\lVert u\rVert$ at every nonzero sampled $z$. Each has a proper own cut and only empty or $\mathcal H^{d-1}$-null supporting traces elsewhere. Their union covers the entire base-zero fiber and misses the interior of every other fiber. The missed fraction of the mixed volume is exactly
$$
1-\frac{R^d}{\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^n}R_z^d},
$$
which tends to $1-2^{-n}$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ with $R$ fixed.

## Proof

The norm constraint with positive affine right side and the cube constraint give a full-dimensional compact convex body with exactly the listed integer fibers. Every cube vertex is strictly exposed, for example by taking signs of the base coefficients according to its binary coordinates. Apply dec855d1498a4e64 to the singleton exposed face at each vertex, using the finite sum of ball-volume measures supported on the sampled compact fibers. In a ball of radius $r$, symmetry pairs every central half-ball with its complement up to a null hyperplane and implies center depth $\kappa_d r^d/2$. At every noncentral mark the outward radial cap through it removes a positive slab from a corresponding central half-ball and has strictly smaller mass. The radii are uniquely maximal at base zero, so the claimed point is the only global maximizer.

A closed cap containing $(0,0)$ has threshold $b\le0$. A purely base cap contains the entire large fiber, and for a nonzero continuous normal a strictly negative threshold takes more than its central half-ball. Thus criticality requires $u\ne0$ and $b=0$. The large fiber already contributes $Q$, so each other trace must have zero volume. For a ball this holds exactly when the threshold on it is at least its support value $R_z\lVert u\rVert$, giving the stated inequalities. Conversely those inequalities ensure criticality. At strict inequality the trace is empty. At equality it is one point, which is $\mathcal H^{d-1}$-null as $d\ge2$. Every $u\ne0$ is realized, for example by $a_i=-R\lVert u\rVert$ for all $i$. The central half-balls for all directions cover the large fiber, and the other traces collectively lie on the boundaries of their respective balls. Those finitely many boundaries have zero mixed volume, so summing the missed interior volumes gives the exact fraction and its limit. For $n=1$ the full smaller boundary is attained by choosing $u=x/r$ and $a=-r$ at any boundary point $x$ of its radius-$r$ ball.
