## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,
\]
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
and, for \(p\in S\), write
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \(a,b\geq0\), define
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\left(\frac{2a^{1/d}b^{1/d}}{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}\right)^d,
&ab>0,\\[2mm]
0,&ab=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Then the following three assertions hold.

1. There is \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_m>0\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
\geq 2^{-n}W.
\]
For every \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\),
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
\leq W.
\]

2. Fix \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_m>0\). For every \(z\) with
\(w_z>0\), let \(\lambda_z\) be normalized Lebesgue measure on \(C_z\).
Choose one representative from each unordered pair
\(\{v,-v\}\), with \(v\neq0\), for which
\(w_{m+v}w_{m-v}>0\), and denote the resulting finite set by
\(\mathcal R_m\). Put
\[
\alpha_v:=\min\{w_{m+v},w_{m-v}\},\qquad
M(x,x'):=\frac{x+x'}2,
\]
\[
\kappa_m:=
w_m\lambda_m+
\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\alpha_v\,M_\#(\lambda_{m+v}\otimes\lambda_{m-v}).
\]
For a finite Borel measure \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
\operatorname{HD}(\nu):=
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R^d}
\inf_{\substack{
G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
y\in G}}
\nu(G).
\]
Then every midpoint component of \(\kappa_m\) is supported in \(C_m\),
a maximizing center in the definition of \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\)
may be chosen in \(C_m\), and
\[
\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m).
\]

3. Let \(m\) be a midpoint furnished by assertion 1 and let \(K\geq1\).
Suppose that, for every \(v\in\mathcal R_m\),
\[
K^{-1}\leq\frac{w_{m+v}}{w_{m-v}}\leq K,
\]
and, writing \(c_z\) for the centroid of every positive-volume fiber
\(C_z\),
\[
\frac{c_{m+v}+c_{m-v}}2=c_m.
\]
Then
\[
h_S((m,c_m))
\geq
\frac{1}{2e\sqrt K}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

## Proof

The projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is compact
and therefore meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points. Hence
only finitely many fibers are nonempty and all sums below are finite.
An empty or nonempty lower-dimensional fiber has \(w_z=0\) and
contributes zero. A fiber with \(w_z>0\) is a full-dimensional compact
convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\).

We first prove assertion 1. For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
A_m:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_zw_{2m-z}.
\]
Accepted fact `6a9b0f7ac15a23be` gives an \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with
\(w_m>0\) and
\[
A_m\geq2^{-n}Ww_m.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix any \(z\). If \(w_zw_{2m-z}=0\), then both
\[
\frac{w_zw_{2m-z}}{w_m}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
\]
are zero. If \(w_zw_{2m-z}>0\), convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac12C_z+\frac12C_{2m-z}\subseteq C_m.
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality and monotonicity of volume therefore give
\[
w_m^{1/d}
\geq
\frac{w_z^{1/d}+w_{2m-z}^{1/d}}2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{w_zw_{2m-z}}{w_m}
\leq
\left(
\frac{2w_z^{1/d}w_{2m-z}^{1/d}}
{w_z^{1/d}+w_{2m-z}^{1/d}}
\right)^d
=\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z}).
\tag{2}
\]
Summing (2) and using (1) proves
\[
\sum_z\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
\geq\frac{A_m}{w_m}
\geq2^{-n}W.
\]
This also records explicitly why the selected midpoint has positive-volume
fiber.

For arbitrary \(m\), the ordinary harmonic mean of two positive numbers is
at most their geometric mean. Hence, including the zero cases,
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b)\leq\sqrt{ab}\leq\frac{a+b}{2}
\qquad(a,b\geq0).
\tag{3}
\]
The reflection \(z\mapsto2m-z\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb Z^n\).
Therefore
\[
\sum_z\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
\leq
\frac12\sum_z(w_z+w_{2m-z})
=W.
\]
This proves assertion 1.

We next prove assertion 2. The set \(\mathcal R_m\) is finite. Since
\(w_m>0\), the measure \(\kappa_m\) is a nonzero finite Borel measure.
For every \(v\in\mathcal R_m\), convexity gives
\[
\frac12C_{m+v}+\frac12C_{m-v}\subseteq C_m,
\tag{4}
\]
so the corresponding midpoint component is supported in \(C_m\).
The central component \(w_m\lambda_m\) has support exactly \(C_m\).
Thus
\[
\operatorname{supp}\kappa_m=C_m.
\tag{5}
\]

We verify attainment of the Tukey-depth supremum instead of assuming it.
For any finite Borel measure \(\nu\), set
\[
D_\nu(y):=
\inf_{\substack{
G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\
y\in G}}\nu(G).
\]
The function \(D_\nu\) is upper semicontinuous. Indeed, suppose
\(D_\nu(y)<t\). Choose
\[
G=\{x:\ell(x)\geq0\}
\]
with \(y\in G\) and \(\nu(G)<t\), where \(\ell\) is a nonconstant affine
functional. The enlarged halfspaces
\[
G_\varepsilon:=\{x:\ell(x)\geq-\varepsilon\}
\]
decrease to \(G\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Continuity from above
of the finite measure \(\nu\) gives an \(\varepsilon>0\) with
\(\nu(G_\varepsilon)<t\). The point \(y\) lies in the interior of
\(G_\varepsilon\), so every point sufficiently near \(y\) belongs to
\(G_\varepsilon\) and consequently has \(D_\nu<t\). Thus
\(\{D_\nu<t\}\) is open.

Apply this to \(\nu=\kappa_m\). Upper semicontinuity gives a maximizer of
\(D_{\kappa_m}\) on the compact set \(C_m\). If \(y\notin C_m\), strict
separation of the point \(y\) from the compact convex set \(C_m\) gives
a proper closed halfspace containing \(y\) and disjoint from \(C_m\).
By (5), \(D_{\kappa_m}(y)=0\). Since depth is nonnegative, the maximum
over all of \(\mathbb R^d\) equals the maximum over \(C_m\). Therefore
there is \(y_0\in C_m\) with
\[
D_{\kappa_m}(y_0)=\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m).
\tag{6}
\]
In particular \(p:=(m,y_0)\) belongs to \(S\).

We prove a pointwise lifting inequality that will also be used in
assertion 3. Let \(y\in C_m\), let \(p_y:=(m,y)\), and let \(H\) be a
proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(p_y\). Write
\[
H=
\{(z,x):
a^\top(z-m)+b^\top(x-y)\geq0\},
\qquad(a,b)\neq(0,0).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(b\neq0\), put
\[
G:=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:b^\top(u-y)\geq0\}.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(b=0\), put \(G:=\mathbb R^d\). In the first case \(G\) is a proper
closed halfspace containing \(y\); in the second case
\(\kappa_m(G)=|\kappa_m|\geq D_{\kappa_m}(y)\).

For the central component, (7) at base point \(m\) gives
\[
w_m\lambda_m(G)
=
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_m:(m,x)\in H\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This identity also holds when \(b=0\), because the entire central fiber
then lies on the boundary of \(H\).

Fix \(v\in\mathcal R_m\), and abbreviate
\[
X^+\sim\lambda_{m+v},\qquad X^-\sim\lambda_{m-v},
\]
independently. Pointwise, if \(M(X^+,X^-)\in G\), then the two endpoint
scores in (7) have sum
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl[a^\top v+b^\top(X^+-y)\bigr]
+\bigl[-a^\top v+b^\top(X^--y)\bigr]\\
&\hspace{25mm}
=2b^\top(M(X^+,X^-)-y)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus at least one endpoint belongs to \(H\), including the equality case
because \(H\) is closed. It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\alpha_v
M_\#(\lambda_{m+v}\otimes\lambda_{m-v})(G)\\
&\quad\leq
\alpha_v\,
\mathbb P((m+v,X^+)\in H)
+
\alpha_v\,
\mathbb P((m-v,X^-)\in H)\\
&\quad\leq
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_{m+v}:(m+v,x)\in H\}\\
&\qquad+
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_{m-v}:(m-v,x)\in H\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
where the last inequality uses
\(\alpha_v\leq w_{m+v}\) and \(\alpha_v\leq w_{m-v}\).

The set \(\mathcal R_m\) contains only one representative of each
unordered nonzero pair. Hence the endpoint fibers on the right side of
(10) are pairwise distinct as \(v\) varies, and none is the central
fiber. Summing (9) and (10), then retaining the uncharged fibers in
\(\mu_C(H)\), gives
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\kappa_m(G).
\tag{11}
\]
This proves the lifting without charging any fiber more than once. It
also covers vertical ambient halfspaces \(b=0\) and makes no assumption
that their boundaries have zero mixed mass.

Take \(y=y_0\) from (6). If \(b\neq0\), then the definition of
\(D_{\kappa_m}(y_0)\) and (8) give
\(\kappa_m(G)\geq\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\). If \(b=0\), the same
inequality holds because \(G=\mathbb R^d\). Thus every proper closed
halfspace through \(p=(m,y_0)\) has mixed mass at least
\(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\).

If a proper closed halfspace contains \(p\) but its boundary does not
pass through \(p\), translate its boundary parallel toward \(p\) until
it passes through \(p\). The resulting closed halfspace is contained in
the original one, so monotonicity gives the same bound. If the whole
space is admitted as a closed halfspace, its mixed mass is \(W\), while
\[
\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\leq|\kappa_m|
=w_m+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}\alpha_v\leq W;
\]
the last inequality follows by charging each \(\alpha_v\) to the two
distinct endpoint fibers and using
\(\alpha_v\leq w_{m+v}+w_{m-v}\). Therefore
\[
h_S((m,y_0))\geq\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m),
\]
and taking the supremum over \(p\in S\) proves assertion 2.

We finally prove assertion 3. Put
\[
P_m:=|\kappa_m|
=w_m+\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\min\{w_{m+v},w_{m-v}\}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(a,b>0\) and \(K^{-1}\leq a/b\leq K\), then
\[
\operatorname{HM}_d(a,b)\leq\sqrt{ab}
\leq\sqrt K\,\min\{a,b\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Reflection about \(m\) partitions every noncentral positive-positive
reflected pair into the two ordered indices represented by one
\(v\in\mathcal R_m\). Indices for which one reflected volume is zero
contribute zero to the harmonic sum. The central index contributes
\(\operatorname{HM}_d(w_m,w_m)=w_m\). Consequently,
\[
\sum_z\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})
=
w_m+
2\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_{m+v},w_{m-v}).
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (12) through (14), and \(K\geq1\), give
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_m
&\geq
w_m+
\frac1{\sqrt K}
\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_m}
\operatorname{HM}_d(w_{m+v},w_{m-v})\\
&\geq
\frac1{2\sqrt K}
\sum_z\operatorname{HM}_d(w_z,w_{2m-z})\\
&\geq
\frac1{2\sqrt K}\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The second inequality in (15) explicitly handles the unpaired central
term: \(w_m\geq w_m/(2\sqrt K)\).

Each normalized uniform density \(\lambda_z\) is log-concave. A positive
linear scaling and the convolution of log-concave densities are
log-concave, so every midpoint law
\[
M_\#(\lambda_{m+v}\otimes\lambda_{m-v})
\]
has a log-concave probability density. Its centroid is
\[
\frac{c_{m+v}+c_{m-v}}2=c_m.
\tag{16}
\]
The central law \(\lambda_m\) is also log-concave and has centroid \(c_m\).

Let \(H\) be a proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains
\((m,c_m)\), and define \(G\) from (8) with \(y=c_m\). If \(b\neq0\),
the log-concave Grünbaum inequality applied separately to every component,
using (16), gives at least \(1/e\) of that component's mass to \(G\).
If \(b=0\), \(G=\mathbb R^d\) and every component gives all its mass.
In both cases,
\[
\kappa_m(G)\geq\frac1eP_m.
\tag{17}
\]
The pointwise lifting inequality (11), which was proved for every
\(y\in C_m\), now gives
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\kappa_m(G)
\geq\frac1eP_m
\geq
\frac{1}{2e\sqrt K}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{18}
\]
The centroid \(c_m\) belongs to the positive-volume compact convex fiber
\(C_m\), so \((m,c_m)\in S\). Translating the boundary toward this point
extends (18) from boundary-through-point halfspaces to all proper closed
halfspaces containing it. The whole-space case follows from
\(W\geq(2e\sqrt K)^{-1}2^{-n}W\). Hence (18) is exactly
\[
h_S((m,c_m))
\geq
\frac{1}{2e\sqrt K}\,2^{-n}W,
\]
which completes the proof.

## External sources

R. J. Gardner, “The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American
Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355–405, Theorem 4.1,
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: for
nonempty bounded measurable \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<\lambda<1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_d(A)^{1/d}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_d(B)^{1/d}.
\]
Applicability: when \(w_zw_{2m-z}>0\), the two fibers are nonempty compact
convex sets, their Minkowski midpoint is compact and therefore measurable,
and (by convexity of the ambient body) it is contained in \(C_m\).

László Lovász and Santosh Vempala, “The Geometry of Logconcave Functions
and Sampling Algorithms,” Random Structures & Algorithms 30 (2007),
307–358, Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 5.12,
https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20135,
author-hosted primary PDF
https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~vempala/papers/logcon.pdf. Exact forms used:
the convolution of two log-concave functions is log-concave; and every
closed halfspace containing the centroid of a log-concave probability
density has probability at least \(1/e\). Applicability: every
positive-volume fiber has normalized indicator density
\(w_z^{-1}\mathbf1_{C_z}\), which is log-concave; the midpoint law is a
positive linear image of the convolution of the two fiber densities and
is therefore a full-dimensional log-concave probability density; its
centroid is computed in (16); and the halfspace \(G\) in (8) contains
that centroid. The case \(b=0\) uses the whole space and does not invoke
the inequality.
