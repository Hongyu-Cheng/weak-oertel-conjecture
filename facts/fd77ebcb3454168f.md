## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty with \(\operatorname{conv}D\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), and let \(\phi:D\to[0,\infty)\) have minimum zero and be convex-admissible: for every finite nonempty \(E\subseteq D\), every positive coefficient vector of sum one, and every \(\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\), one has \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\). Let \(w_{\max}>0\), put \(w_{\mathbf I}=w_{\max}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\) and zero outside \(D\), and put \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), \(B=\max_D\phi\), and \(T=2^{-n}W\). Use exactly the canonical roof \(\widehat\phi\), integral hitting mass \(m_{\log}\), and score \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. In particular, its closed-cap formula is
\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
=\min_{\substack{(\mathbf b,t)\in(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R)\setminus\{(\mathbf0,0)\}\\t\ge0}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+t(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I})\ge0}}
w_{\mathbf I}\qquad(\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0).
\]
For input potentials that were not normalized, the exact normalization map is \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\phi^{\mathrm{input}}_{\mathbf I}-\min_D\phi^{\mathrm{input}}\) and \(w_{\max}=e^{-\min_D\phi^{\mathrm{input}}}\); the original weights and all gaps \(G_E\) are unchanged.

For every real mesh \(\delta>0\), define the geometric ratio and the integer starting heights by
\[
q_\delta=e^{-\delta},\qquad
\ell^-_{\mathbf I}=\left\lceil\frac{\phi_{\mathbf I}}\delta\right\rceil,
\qquad
\rho_{\mathbf I}=\delta\ell^-_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf I}\in[0,\delta).
\]
The subscripted \(q_\delta\) is a geometric ratio, distinct from the continuous realization dimension \(q\) in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Define the finite Borel measure \(\nu_\delta\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\), supported on \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), by assigning mass \(w_{\max}q_\delta^s\) to \((\mathbf I,s)\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\) and every integer \(s\ge\ell^-_{\mathbf I}\), with zero mass elsewhere. Its total mass is
\[
M_\delta=\frac1{1-q_\delta}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}e^{-\rho_{\mathbf I}},
\qquad
e^{-\delta}W\le(1-q_\delta)M_\delta\le W.
\tag{1}
\]

Call an integer point \((\mathbf J,k)\) certified if every closed halfspace containing it has \(\nu_\delta\)-mass at least \(M_\delta/2^{n+1}\). Such points exist by the \(d=0\), integer-dimension \(n+1\) specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c. Every certified point has \(\mathbf J\in D\) and \(L=\delta k-\phi_{\mathbf J}\ge0\). For every certified point,
\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}
\ge
\Gamma_\delta(\mathbf J,k)
:=\frac{(1-q_\delta)M_\delta}{2^{n+1}(1+L)}
\ge\frac{e^{-\delta}}{2(1+L)}T.
\tag{2}
\]
The middle quantity \(\Gamma_\delta\) is the original-weight score certified by this centerpoint guarantee and the lower-cap comparison proved below.

Nevertheless, for every integer \(n\ge2\), take the exact finite lattice-convex support and potential
\[
D=\{-100,-99,\ldots,100\}^{n},\qquad
\phi_{\mathbf I}=\frac1{10000}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_i^2,
\qquad w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}.
\tag{3}
\]
These have \(w_{\max}=1\), \(B=n\), and
\[
T\ge\left(\frac{201}{2e}\right)^n>33^n.
\tag{4}
\]
For this family, simultaneously for every \(\delta>0\) and every certified \((\mathbf J,k)\),
\[
\frac{\Gamma_\delta(\mathbf J,k)}T
\le
\max\left\{
\left(\frac29\right)^n,
\frac1{2(1+n/20000)}
\right\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The right side tends to zero as \(n\to\infty\). Thus even optimizing the mesh and the certified center cannot make this specific geometric-height transfer yield an absolute positive coefficient of \(T\).

## Proof

Accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc supplies the lattice-convex support, the roof \(\widehat\phi\) equal to \(\phi\) on \(D\), and the exact closed lower-cap expression for \(m_{\log}\). No root-volume, reflected-pair, or symmetric-table score is substituted for that expression.

Summing the geometric series above each base site gives exactly
\[
\nu_\delta(\{\mathbf I\}\times\mathbb R)
=\frac{w_{\max}q_\delta^{\ell^-_{\mathbf I}}}{1-q_\delta}
=\frac{w_{\mathbf I}e^{-\rho_{\mathbf I}}}{1-q_\delta}.
\]
Because \(D\) is finite and \(0<q_\delta<1\), \(\nu_\delta\) is a nonzero finite Borel measure. Its total mass and \(0\le\rho_{\mathbf I}<\delta\) prove (1).

Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to this measure on \(\mathbb Z^{n+1}\). Its hypotheses allow infinitely many atoms with finite total mass and arbitrary hyperplane atoms. Its conclusion gives the stated certified points. The measure is supported in the closed convex set
\[
Q_\delta=\{(x,s):x\in\operatorname{conv}D,
\ \delta s\ge\widehat\phi(x)\}.
\]
Every point of positive closed-halfspace depth belongs to \(Q_\delta\). Indeed, if a point \(p\) lies outside this nonempty closed convex set, a closest point \(p_0\) exists: a distance-minimizing sequence is bounded and has a convergent subsequence in the closed set. Differentiating the squared distance along segments from \(p_0\) to points of \(Q_\delta\) gives
\((p-p_0)\cdot(x-p_0)\le0\) for every \(x\in Q_\delta\). Consequently the closed halfspace
\(\{x:(p-p_0)\cdot(x-p)\ge0\}\) contains \(p\) and misses \(Q_\delta\), so has measure zero. This contradicts positive depth. For a certified integer point, membership in \(Q_\delta\) now implies \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{conv}D\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) and \(\delta k\ge\widehat\phi(\mathbf J)=\phi_{\mathbf J}\). This proves that its mapped cutoff \(L\) is admissible.

Here is the exact cap comparison, including its rounding and equality atoms. Fix any certified \((\mathbf J,k)\), write \(\delta k=\phi_{\mathbf J}+L\), and take any \((\mathbf b,t)\ne(\mathbf0,0)\) with \(t\ge0\). The lifted closed lower halfspace is
\[
H_{\mathbf b,t}=\{(x,s):
\mathbf b\cdot(x-\mathbf J)+t(\delta k-\delta s)\ge0\}.
\]
It contains \((\mathbf J,k)\). Its corresponding original closed cap is
\[
A_{\mathbf b,t}=\{\mathbf I\in D:
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
+t(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I})\ge0\}.
\]
For \(t>0\), define the integer upper height
\[
\ell^+_{\mathbf I}=\left\lfloor
k+\frac{\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}{\delta t}
\right\rfloor.
\]
The contribution of site \(\mathbf I\) to \(\nu_\delta(H_{\mathbf b,t})\) is exactly
\[
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{w_{\max}}{1-q_\delta}
\bigl(q_\delta^{\ell^-_{\mathbf I}}-q_\delta^{\ell^+_{\mathbf I}+1}\bigr),
&\ell^+_{\mathbf I}\ge\ell^-_{\mathbf I},\\
0,&\ell^+_{\mathbf I}<\ell^-_{\mathbf I}.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
For \(t=0\), its exact contribution is
\[
\frac{w_{\mathbf I}e^{-\rho_{\mathbf I}}}{1-q_\delta}
\mathbf 1_{\{\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0\}}.
\tag{7}
\]
If a site contributes in (6), some \(s\ge\ell^-_{\mathbf I}\) obeys the cap inequality. Since \(\delta s\ge\phi_{\mathbf I}\) and \(t\ge0\), that site belongs to \(A_{\mathbf b,t}\). In (7) membership is exactly that same cap condition. Thus in both cases
\[
\nu_\delta(H_{\mathbf b,t})
\le\frac1{1-q_\delta}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in A_{\mathbf b,t}}
w_{\mathbf I}e^{-\rho_{\mathbf I}}
\le\frac1{1-q_\delta}\sum_{\mathbf I\in A_{\mathbf b,t}}w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{8}
\]
All inequalities are weak at the boundary. In particular, a site whose original cap score is zero stays in \(A_{\mathbf b,t}\), whether or not rounding leaves a lifted atom below that boundary. Vertical normals \(t=0\) are explicitly retained.

Certification gives \(\nu_\delta(H_{\mathbf b,t})\ge M_\delta/2^{n+1}\). Therefore each original cap in (8) has weight at least \((1-q_\delta)M_\delta/2^{n+1}\). Taking the minimum over exactly the normals in the closed-cap formula of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc proves (2). In particular, the cap mass compared here is the original full-occurrence integral hitting mass.

We now prove the obstruction with an all-center calculation on (3). The support is the integer points of the closed box \([-100,100]^n\), so is finite, nonempty, and lattice-convex. For every barycentric representation \(\mathbf J=\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\), with coefficients summing to one,
\[
\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I\|^2-\|\mathbf J\|^2
=\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|^2\ge0.
\]
Hence the potential is convex-admissible. Its minimum is zero at the origin and its maximum is \(n\) at box corners, proving \(w_{\max}=1\) and \(B=n\). There are \(201^n\) sites, each with weight at least \(e^{-n}\), so \(W\ge201^ne^{-n}\). The elementary estimate \(e<3\), obtained from the exponential series and \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\) with some strict inequalities, proves (4).

Fix any \(\mathbf J\in D\) and any \(0\le L\le n/20000\). In the exact original cap formula choose
\(\mathbf b=2\mathbf J/10000\) and \(t=1\). Its atom score is exactly
\[
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
+\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I}
=L-\frac{\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|^2}{10000}.
\]
Thus it is the closed ball cap \(\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|^2\le10000L\). Bounding each original atom weight by one and using \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&\le\#\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|z\|^2\le10000L\}\\
&\le e^{10000L}\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-\|z\|^2}
=e^{10000L}\left(\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}e^{-r^2}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The one-dimensional sum is less than four. Indeed, monotonicity of \(e^{-x^2}\) on \([0,\infty)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}e^{-r^2}
&\le1+2\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\,dx\\
&\le1+2\left(1+\int_1^\infty x e^{-x^2}\,dx\right)
=3+e^{-1}<4.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with \(10000L\le n/2\) and the lower bound on \(W\), equation (9) yields
\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}W
\le\left(\frac{4e^{3/2}}{201}\right)^n<9^{-n}.
\tag{10}
\]
For the last inequality, \(e<3\) and \(\sqrt3<7/4\) imply \(4e^{3/2}<21<201/9\). This proves (10) for every base center, not merely the origin, and every cutoff in the indicated interval.

For an arbitrary mesh \(\delta>0\), define the exact marginal-retention factor
\[
\beta_\delta=\frac{(1-q_\delta)M_\delta}{W}
=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}e^{-\rho_{\mathbf I}}}{W}
\in(0,1].
\]
For every certified \((\mathbf J,k)\), (2) before division by \(1+L\) gives
\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
\ge\frac{\beta_\delta W}{2^{n+1}},
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma_\delta(\mathbf J,k)}T
=\frac{\beta_\delta}{2(1+L)}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(L\le n/20000\), combine (10) and (11) to get
\(\beta_\delta\le2^{n+1}9^{-n}=2(2/9)^n\), and hence \(\Gamma_\delta/T\le(2/9)^n\). If \(L>n/20000\), use \(\beta_\delta\le1\) in (11) to get \(\Gamma_\delta/T\le1/[2(1+n/20000)]\). These two cases prove (5) for all meshes and all certified centers. They also identify both possible losses: a small mapped cutoff forces exponentially small exact retained marginal mass, while a retained marginal mass bounded below forces a cutoff proportional to \(n\).

This last bound concerns the number supplied by the standard weighted integer centerpoint guarantee. It does not bound the actual depth of a specially selected lifted point by that number, and it does not bound \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\) from above. Completing the full scalar route therefore requires an additional depth-versus-height improvement beyond this theorem's uniform \(2^{-(n+1)}\) certificate.
