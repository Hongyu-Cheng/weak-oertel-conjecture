## Statement

In the opposed planar flow, fix a nonzero continuous normal $u$, middle anchor $j=0$, threshold $t$, and the scaled tilt $(1-2s)\beta$. Assume that both initial endpoint cutoffs lie in their closed support intervals. Then all four original upper and lower endpoint traces are nonempty, though some may have zero area. Let $p,r$ be the original upper areas, $a,c$ the whole endpoint areas, $\Delta=2V(A,C)-a-c$, and $\chi=s(1-s)$. The total upper endpoint area $P_s$ satisfies
$$
p+r-2\chi(\sqrt p-\sqrt r)^2
\le P_s
\le p+r+2\chi\bigl[\Delta+(\sqrt{a-p}-\sqrt{c-r})^2\bigr].
$$
If $f$ is the original normalized full cap mass and $(1-f)\Delta+(\sqrt{a-p}-\sqrt{c-r})^2\le0$, then this continued cap has normalized mass at most $f$ for every $s\in[0,1]$.

## Proof

At base $-1$ the cutoff is $(1-s)(t+\beta)+s(t-\beta)$, and at base $1$ it is $s(t+\beta)+(1-s)(t-\beta)$. By linearity of $u\cdot x$, the weighted Minkowski combinations of the original endpoint upper traces lie in the corresponding new upper caps. The original traces are nonempty. Ordinary planar Brunn–Minkowski gives the two lower bounds $[(1-s)\sqrt p+s\sqrt r]^2$ and $[s\sqrt p+(1-s)\sqrt r]^2$. If one trace has zero area the corresponding bound follows by using one point of that trace and the scaled other trace. Their sum is $p+r-2\chi(\sqrt p-\sqrt r)^2$.

The same inclusions hold for the lower traces, whose original areas are $a-p$ and $c-r$ because the cuts have nonzero continuous normal. Their new combined mass is at least $(a-p)+(c-r)-2\chi(\sqrt{a-p}-\sqrt{c-r})^2$. The endpoint mass $a_s+c_s=a+c+2\chi\Delta$ is given by the opposed-flow mass lemma, and the new cut lines are area null. Subtracting gives the stated upper bound on $P_s$. For the continuous inequality see [Gardner, The Brunn–Minkowski Inequality, Theorem 4.1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2).

The middle cap is constant. Thus the full continued mass is at most $F_0+2\chi[\Delta+(\sqrt{a-p}-\sqrt{c-r})^2]$. With $f=F_0/M_0$ one has $fM_s=F_0+2\chi f\Delta$. The proposed sufficient condition makes the former bound at most the latter, and $M_s>0$. An empty trace is outside the stated hypotheses, since no point of such a trace can be used in the Minkowski inclusion.
