## Statement

Let \(\sigma\) be a nonzero compactly supported finite measure on
\(\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^s\), with \(r\ge0\), \(s\ge1\), and let \(E\subset\mathbb R^s\) be nonempty, compact, and convex. Let \(h_E\) be its support function, let \(\pi_Z(\mathbf X,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z\), and define the projected measure
\[
\bar\sigma=(\pi_Z)_\#\sigma.
\]
Define
\[
D_{\sigma,E}(\mathbf x,\mathbf z)
=\inf_{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\ne0}
\sigma\{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf X+\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf Z
\ge\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf z-h_E(\mathbf v)\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
D_{\bar\sigma,E}(\mathbf z)
=\inf_{\mathbf v\ne0}\bar\sigma\{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf Z
\ge\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf z-h_E(\mathbf v)\}.
\]
For a finite measure \(\mu\) on a Euclidean space, write
\[
D_\mu(\mathbf p)=\inf_{\mathbf w\ne0}
\mu\{\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf q\ge\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf p\}
\]
for its ordinary halfspace depth; thus \(D_{\sigma,\{0\}}=D_\sigma\). If
\(D_{\bar\sigma,E}(\mathbf z_0)\ge\delta>0\), then some
\[
(\mathbf x_0,\mathbf z_0)\in
\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\sigma)+(\{0\}\times E)
\]
satisfies \(D_{\sigma,E}(\mathbf x_0,\mathbf z_0)\ge\delta/(r+1)\).
For \(E=\{0\}\), this is ordinary halfspace-depth lifting.

Now let \(n,d\ge1\), let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex body, and let \(\ell_i^\pm:T\to[0,\infty)\) be bounded upper-semicontinuous concave functions. Define
\[
C=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in T,\ -\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)\},
\quad S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
\[
a_i=\lfloor\ell_i^-\rfloor,\quad b_i=\lfloor\ell_i^+\rfloor,\quad
N_i=a_i+b_i+2,\quad
A=\prod_iN_i,\quad B=\prod_i(N_i-1),
\]
\[
W=\int_TA,\qquad M=\int_TB.
\]
Thus \(A=A_{\mathrm{asym}}\) and \(B=B_{\mathrm{asym}}\) in the project notation. Let
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H),
\qquad
\tau_B(\mathbf y)=\inf_{L\ni\mathbf y}\int_{T\cap L}B,
\qquad
\Delta_B=\max_{\mathbf y\in T}\tau_B(\mathbf y),
\]
using proper closed halfspaces \(H\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) and \(L\subset\mathbb R^d\).

Put
\[
P=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in T,\ -\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)\},
\]
and let \(\nu\) be mixed measure on the integer sections of \(P\), namely
\[
\nu(Q)=\int_T\#\{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z^n:(\mathbf k,\mathbf y)\in P\cap Q\}\,d\mathbf y
\]
for measurable \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). All vector floors, ceilings, and inequalities below are coordinatewise.

The following conclusions hold.

(a) \(C=P+([0,1]^n\times\{0\})\), \(\nu\) has total mass \(M\) and base projection \(B\,d\mathbf y\), and
\[
h_S((\lceil\mathbf u\rceil,\mathbf y_0))
\ge D_\nu((\mathbf u,\mathbf y_0))
\quad((\mathbf u,\mathbf y_0)\in P).
\]
In particular, for every \(\mathbf y_0\in T\), some feasible \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))
\ge\tau_B(\mathbf y_0)/(n+1).
\]

(b) At every feasible mixed point \((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0)\in S\),
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))\ge\mathcal C(\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0),
\]
where
\[
\mathcal C(\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0)
=\inf_{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\ne0}
\nu\{(\mathbf k,\mathbf y):
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf k+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf y_0)
\ge\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf J-\sum_i(u_i)_+\}.
\]

(c) Let \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), \(\mathbf b=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)\), and
\(\mathbf c(\mathbf y)=(\mathbf b(\mathbf y)-\mathbf a(\mathbf y))/2\). For a finite measure \(\mu\) on \(T\), define the essential affine rank of \(\mathbf c\) with respect to \(\mu\) to be
\[
\operatorname{arank}_\mu(\mathbf c)
=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{essran}_\mu\mathbf c),
\]
where \(\operatorname{essran}_\mu\mathbf c\) is the essential range of \(\mathbf c\) with respect to \(\mu\). Equivalently, this is the least dimension of an affine subspace containing \(\mathbf c(\mathbf y)\) for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\mathbf y\); set it equal to \(0\) when \(\mu=0\). If
\[
r_0=\operatorname{arank}_{B(\mathbf y)d\mathbf y}(\mathbf c),
\]
then for every \(\mathbf y_0\in T\),
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))
\ge\tau_B(\mathbf y_0)/[2(r_0+1)]
\]
for some feasible \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\). More generally, if \(E_0\subseteq T\) is measurable and
\[
\operatorname{arank}_{\mathbf1_{E_0}(\mathbf y)B(\mathbf y)d\mathbf y}(\mathbf c)\le r_0,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\int_{T\setminus E_0}B,
\]
then
\[
\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)
\ge(\Delta_B-\varepsilon)_+/[2(r_0+1)].
\]
If the coordinates form \(q\) groups and the pair \((\ell_i^-,\ell_i^+)\) is shared by all coordinates in each group, one may take \(r_0\le q\) and choose \(\mathbf J\) constant within each group.

(d) Partition the coordinates into \(I\cup K\), with \(|I|=r_1\), and assume \(a_j=0\) almost everywhere for \(j\in K\). Put
\[
E_K=\tfrac12\max_{j\in K}\int_TBb_j,
\qquad E_\varnothing=0.
\]
For every \(\mathbf y_0\in T\), some feasible
\((\mathbf J_I,\mathbf1_K,\mathbf y_0)\in S\) satisfies
\[
h_S((\mathbf J_I,\mathbf1_K,\mathbf y_0))
\ge(\tau_B(\mathbf y_0)-E_K)_+/(r_1+1).
\]
Individual reflections \(x_j\mapsto1-x_j\) allow the opposite one-sided orientation.

(e) There is a measure \(\lambda\le\mathcal H_d|_S\) of mass exactly \(M\), whose barycenter is a feasible mixed point \((\mathbf J,\bar{\mathbf y})\in S\). At that point, for every \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), the centered unnormalized exponential moment of the original mixed measure satisfies
\[
\int_S
\exp\!\left[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf k-\mathbf J)
+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\bar{\mathbf y})\right]
\,d\mathcal H_d(\mathbf k,\mathbf y)
\ge M.
\]

(f) If \(T\) is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf y_0\) and
\(\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)=\ell_i^+(2\mathbf y_0-\mathbf y)\), then
\[
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))\ge M/2\ge\Delta_B
\quad\text{for every }\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n.
\]

## Proof

We first prove the expanded projection assertion. Write \(Z=\sigma(\mathbb R^{r+s})\) and \(t=\delta/(r+1)\). For every source halfspace
\[
K_i=\{(\mathbf X,\mathbf Z):
\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf X+\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf Z\le c_i\}
\]
of mass greater than \(Z-t\), impose the fiber constraint
\[
\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf x
\le c_i+h_E(\mathbf b_i)-\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf z_0. \tag{1}
\]

We use the following finite linear-inequality alternative. If
\(\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf x\le d_i\) is infeasible in \(\mathbb R^r\), there are nonnegative \(\lambda_i\), supported on at most \(r+1\) indices, with
\(\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf a_i=0\) and \(\sum_i\lambda_id_i<0\).
Indeed, infeasibility says that \(\mathbf d\) lies outside the closed polyhedral cone \(A\mathbb R^r+\mathbb R_+^k\); separation gives the usual nonnegative alternative. Normalize \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) and minimize \(\sum_i\lambda_id_i\) over the resulting compact polytope. An extreme minimizer has at most \(r+1\) positive entries, by linear dependence among the vectors \((\mathbf a_i,1)\).

If a finite collection of (1) were infeasible, this alternative would give at most \(r+1\) selected constraints and
\[
\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf a_i=0,
\qquad
c+\sum_i\lambda_ih_E(\mathbf b_i)<\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf z_0,
\]
where \(\mathbf b=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf b_i\) and \(c=\sum_i\lambda_ic_i\).
The source intersection has mass greater than \(Z-\delta\) and projects into \(\{\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf Z\le c\}\). Support-function subadditivity gives
\(c+h_E(\mathbf b)<\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf z_0\), contradicting the assumed projected expanded depth. If \(\mathbf b=0\), the source intersection is empty, again a contradiction.

Thus every finite collection intersects. Include source-containing coordinate bounds to obtain compact fiber bounds, and use the finite-intersection property. The constraints from all source-containing halfspaces place the resulting point in
\(\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\sigma)+(\{0\}\times E)\).
A cap in the definition of \(D_{\sigma,E}\) with mass below \(t\) has a nearby closed complementary halfspace of mass above \(Z-t\), whose expanded constraint excludes the point. This contradiction proves the lemma.

The roof identity \(C=P+([0,1]^n\times\{0\})\) is sectionwise exact. The anchor section is
\(\prod_i\{-a_i,\ldots,b_i\}\), with cardinality \(B\).

For any normal \(\mathbf u\), choose \(\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^n\) maximizing \(\mathbf u\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\). Translation by this vertex maps anchor points injectively and measure-preservingly into \(S\). It counts every anchor whose unit cell intersects the target halfspace, proving (b).

If \(\mathbf J-\mathbf u_0\in[0,1]^n\), then
\(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf J-\mathbf u_0)\le\sum_i(u_i)_+\).
The same injection proves
\(h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0))\ge D_\nu((\mathbf u_0,\mathbf y_0))\).
Taking \(\mathbf J=\lceil\mathbf u_0\rceil\), then applying ordinary projection through \(n\) coordinates, proves (a). If the projected depth is zero, any core vertex is sufficient.

For (c), form the graph measure \(\sigma_G\) by
\[
d\sigma_G(\mathbf c(\mathbf y),\mathbf y)=B(\mathbf y)d\mathbf y.
\]
Each anchor box is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf c(\mathbf y)\). Whenever its center belongs to a halfspace, at least half its anchor points belong to it. Thus \(D_\nu\ge D_{\sigma_G}/2\) pointwise. Apply ordinary projection through the \(r_0\)-dimensional affine hull of the essential range of the centers. The lifted point is in \(P\), because every graph point outside a \(B(\mathbf y)d\mathbf y\)-null set is in \(P\), \(P\) is closed and convex, and the support of \(\sigma_G\) is contained in \(P\). Then use (a).

For the restricted assertion, restrict both the graph and anchor measures to \(E_0\). Every base halfspace loses at most \(\varepsilon\), so a maximizer of \(\tau_B\) has restricted base depth at least \((\Delta_B-\varepsilon)_+\). The same proof applies because the restricted graph's convex hull remains in \(P\). Shared roof groups place the center vectors in the span of the group indicators; upward rounding preserves equality within each group.

For (d), use the intermediate measure whose sections are
\[
\prod_{i\in I}\{-a_i,\ldots,b_i\}
\times\prod_{j\in K}\{0,\ldots,b_j+1\}.
\]
Consider its projection onto \((\mathbf x_K,\mathbf y)\), and a halfspace through \((\mathbf1_K,\mathbf y_0)\) with normal \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\).
Translate each anchor coordinate in \(K\) by \(1\) when \(u_j\ge0\), and by \(0\) otherwise. Above the favorable base halfspace, the resulting integer contribution is
\[
\sum_{u_j\ge0}u_jk_j+\sum_{u_j<0}|u_j|(1-k_j).
\]
The first sum is nonnegative. If negative coefficients occur, failure requires
\(\sum\theta_jk_j>1\), where
\(\theta_j=|u_j|/\sum_{u_\ell<0}|u_\ell|\).
Conditional on the base, \(\mathbb E k_j=b_j/2\). Markov's inequality bounds the lost anchor mass by
\[
\tfrac12\sum\theta_j\int_TBb_j\le E_K.
\]
Thus projected depth is at least \(\tau_B(\mathbf y_0)-E_K\). Ordinary projection lifts the remaining \(r_1\) coordinates, and upward cube rounding in those coordinates loses no depth. Feasibility follows from convexity of the intermediate anchor/original-bounds body.

For (e), let \((\bar{\mathbf u},\bar{\mathbf y})\) be the barycenter of \(\nu/M\). Set \(\mathbf J=\lceil\bar{\mathbf u}\rceil\), and choose a random cube vertex with mean \(\mathbf J-\bar{\mathbf u}\). Average the corresponding translates of \(\nu\). Each translate is dominated by the original mixed measure; their convex combination \(\lambda\) has mass \(M\) and barycenter \((\mathbf J,\bar{\mathbf y})\). Convexity and \(C=P+([0,1]^n\times\{0\})\) give feasibility. For every \((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\), domination and Jensen's inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_S\exp\!\left[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf k-\mathbf J)
+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\bar{\mathbf y})\right]
\,d\mathcal H_d(\mathbf k,\mathbf y)\\
&\quad\ge
\int\exp\!\left[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf k-\mathbf J)
+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\bar{\mathbf y})\right]d\lambda(\mathbf k,\mathbf y)\\
&\quad\ge M\exp\!\left(
\frac1M\int
[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf k-\mathbf J)
+\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf y-\bar{\mathbf y})]d\lambda
\right)=M.
\end{aligned}
\]
No halfspace-probability lower bound is inferred from this Laplace statement.

For (f), the anchor measure is invariant under
\((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\mapsto(-\mathbf x,2\mathbf y_0-\mathbf y)\).
Translate it by any core vertex \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\). This produces a centrally symmetric mass-\(M\) submeasure of the original measure, centered at \((\mathbf J,\mathbf y_0)\). Each containing halfspace has at least half its mass. Finally \(\Delta_B\le M/2\), because the base measure \(B(\mathbf y)d\mathbf y\) is atomless: through any point one can choose a hyperplane of zero base measure and then take the lighter of its two closed sides.
