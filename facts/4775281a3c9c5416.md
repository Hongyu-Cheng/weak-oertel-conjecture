## Statement

There is a full-dimensional mixed body with $n=d=1$ whose original mixed length is $\mu$, and a probability $\nu\le 2\mu/\mu(S)$ with centroid $y\in S$ such that the normalized original closed-halfspace depth at $y$ is at most $5/22<2^{-1}\gamma_1=1/4$. Here $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. The measure $\nu$ can be the normalized mixed length of a convex subset of the original body.

## Proof

Take $C=\{(z,x):z,x\ge0,\ z+x\le10\}$ and let $S$ be its actual mixed sample. Its integer fiber lengths are $10-z$ for $0\le z\le10$, of sum $55$. Restrict $\mu$ to $T=S\cap\{z+x\le7\}$ and divide by $28$, the sum of the restricted lengths $7-z$ for $0\le z\le7$. The density relative to $\mu/55$ is $55/28$ on $T$ and zero elsewhere, and $55/28<2$. The raw base and continuous first moments of the restricted measure are respectively $\sum_{z=0}^7 z(7-z)=56$ and $\sum_{z=0}^7(7-z)^2/2=70$, so $y=(2,5/2)$ is in $S$. The closed cap $\{z+x\le9/2\}$ through $y$ contributes the original lengths $9/2,7/2,5/2,3/2,1/2$ from the first five bases and zero elsewhere. Its mass divided by $55$ is $(25/2)/55=5/22$.
