## Statement

For the actual planar triangle $\operatorname{conv}\{(2,-1),(-1,2),(-1,-1)\}$ at $0$, the period-three sector form is $\sum_i x_i^2-\sum_{i<j}x_i x_j$. Replacing each opposite pair of its positive terms by the Cauchy lower bound
$$
\frac{(\sigma_i x_i+\sigma_{i+1}x_{i+1})^2}{2(W_i+W_{i+3})},\qquad \sigma_i=R_i+R_{i+3},
$$
with $i=0,1,2$ and the variables cyclic, yields the form $(2/3)\sum_i x_i^2-(4/3)\sum_{i<j}x_i x_j$. It is negative at $(1,1,1)$, where its value is $-2$.

## Proof

The triangle is $\{x\ge-1,\ y\ge-1,\ x+y\le1\}$ and contains the origin strictly. Its six canonical ray endpoints have scalar radius $1$. The double sectors are the cores, of area $1/2$. In each intervening unique sector the vertex is the sum of its two unit ray endpoints, so splitting at that vertex's ray gives area $1$. Thus each pair of opposite positive terms in the compressed energy has coefficient $1+1/2=3/2$ on $(x_i+x_{i+1})^2$. Subtracting $2(x_0+x_1+x_2)^2$ as in the period-three compression gives the exact form, equal to $\tfrac12\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2$.

Weighted Cauchy gives the displayed lower replacement on each pair, with $\sigma_i=2$ and $W_i+W_{i+3}=3/2$. This changes the coefficient of $(x_i+x_{i+1})^2$ from $3/2$ to $4/3$. Subtract the same $2(x_0+x_1+x_2)^2$ to obtain the asserted negative value. Each through-origin cap captures one unique and two double sectors, so each cap has fraction $2/(9/2)=4/9$.
