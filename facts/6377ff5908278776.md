## Statement

Let $X,Y$ be independent uniform points on a $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$. For the closed centroid halfspace pointing toward a chosen vertex in its opposite facet's barycentric coordinate, the midpoint cap probability is
$$
\beta_d
=1-d^2\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i+j}\binom{d-1}{i}\binom{d-1}{j}
     \left(\frac{2}{d+1}\right)^{i+j+2}\frac{i!j!}{(i+j+2)!}.
$$
In particular $\beta_1=1/2$, $\beta_2=115/243$, and $\lim_{d\to\infty}\beta_d=3/e^2$. Any universal lower bound for centroid halfspace probabilities of iid midpoints of uniform $d$-dimensional convex bodies is at most $\beta_d$.

## Proof

The barycentric coordinate of the chosen vertex has survival probability $(1-u)^d$ on $[0,1]$, since its upper cap is a homothetic simplex with linear ratio $1-u$. Thus the coordinate has density $d(1-u)^{d-1}$ and mean $1/(d+1)$. The two coordinates $U,V$ of the independent points have independent copies of that law. The cap event is $U+V\ge a=2/(d+1)$, and $a\le1$. On the complementary threshold triangle, expansion of the two densities gives monomials $u^iv^j$ whose integrals are $a^{i+j+2}i!j!/(i+j+2)!$ by the beta integral. The boundary has probability zero. This gives the formula, with its first two values by direct substitution.

For $s\ge0$, $\Pr(dU\ge s)=(1-s/d)_+^d\to e^{-s}$. By independence the pair $(dU,dV)$ converges jointly to independent unit-rate exponentials. The sum of the limiting pair has the continuous density $s e^{-s}$ on $[0,\infty)$ and $da\to2$, so the cap probability tends to $\int_2^\infty s e^{-s}\,ds=3/e^2$. The simplex itself is a full-dimensional convex body with the prescribed halfspace containing its centroid, so its value is an upper bound for a universal lower constant.
