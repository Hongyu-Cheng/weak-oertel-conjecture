## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and let \(w=(w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be an admissible table: it is nonzero, nonnegative and finitely supported, and has a finite-valued extension \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying \(f(z)=w_z\) at every lattice point, including every zero, and \(f((1-t)x+ty)\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(y)^t\) for all \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\), with a zero endpoint making the right side zero. Use exactly the normalization of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160: \(W=\sum_z w_z>0\), \(p_z=w_z/W\), \(H_j=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\), \(H_*=\max_jH_j\), and \(G=\log(2^nH_*/W)\). The displacement sums are oriented and include zero, and logarithms are natural.

For \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put \(\pi_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}p_z\) and \(q=\sum_b\pi_b^2\). Let \(\Gamma\) be the joint law of independent \(p\)-distributed \(X,Y\), conditioned on \(X\equiv Y\pmod2\), and under this law put \(J=(X+Y)/2\). Write \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}=\mathcal L_\Gamma(J)\) and \(p^{\mathrm{end}}_z=p_z\pi_{z\bmod2}/q\), exactly as in accepted facts 2f62583dd7ec54de and a41dc1e8d1a966c2. The expectation aliases \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{mid}}=\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{mid}}}\) and \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{end}}=\mathbb E_{p^{\mathrm{end}}}\) refer to these marginals, not to the original law \(p\).

Use the original mean and covariance of accepted fact 2f62583dd7ec54de, namely \(m=\mathbb E_pX\) and \(\Sigma=\mathbb E_p[(X-m)(X-m)^T]\). Thus the variance notation of a41dc1e8d1a966c2 maps exactly to \(\operatorname{Var}_p(a\cdot X)=a^T\Sigma a\). The vector \(m\) is not the slicing dimension called \(m\) in part (iv) of ab57fac07c0d3160; that parameter is unused here. The symbol \(\Gamma\) throughout this packet denotes the conditional joint probability law, never a covariance matrix. Any legacy notation calling the original covariance \(\Gamma\) maps to \(\Sigma\) here, while the predecessor joint-law notation \(\Gamma\) is unchanged.

Assume a finite real \(\kappa\ge1\) satisfies, for every \(a\in\mathbb R^n\), \(\mathbb E_p[(a\cdot(X-m))^4]\le\kappa(a^T\Sigma a)^2\). For every class with \(\pi_b>0\), define \(\mu_b=\mathbb E_p[X\mid X\bmod2=b]\) and \(\Sigma_b=\operatorname{Cov}_p(X\mid X\bmod2=b)\). Classes of zero mass are omitted from all sums involving these objects. Define the two different matrices

\[
T_{\mathrm{sq}}=\sum_{b:\pi_b>0}\frac{\pi_b^2}{q}\Sigma_b,
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{orig}}=\sum_{b:\pi_b>0}\pi_b\Sigma_b,
\qquad
\varepsilon_\kappa(G)=\frac9{\sqrt2}\sqrt\kappa\,e^{G/2}(e^G-1)^{1/4}.
\]

For symmetric matrices, \(T\preceq U\) means \(a^TTa\le a^TUa\) for all \(a\in\mathbb R^n\). Then the exact identities are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\Gamma[(X-Y)(X-Y)^T]&=2T_{\mathrm{sq}},\\
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{end}}[(X-m)(X-m)^T]
-\mathbb E_{\mathrm{mid}}[(J-m)(J-m)^T]&=\frac12T_{\mathrm{sq}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The endpoint and midpoint means agree, although neither is assumed to equal \(m\). Consequently their covariances about their own means also differ by \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}/2\). Moreover,

\[
0\preceq T_{\mathrm{sq}}
\preceq2\left[\varepsilon_\kappa(G)
+\sqrt{(\kappa-1)(e^G-1)}\right]\Sigma.
\]

The original weights admit the separate comparison and bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{orig}}
&\preceq T_{\mathrm{sq}}+\sqrt{\kappa/2}\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}\Sigma,\\
0\preceq T_{\mathrm{orig}}
&\preceq\left[2\varepsilon_\kappa(G)
+2\sqrt{(\kappa-1)(e^G-1)}
+\sqrt{\kappa/2}\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}\right]\Sigma,\\
\Sigma-T_{\mathrm{orig}}
&=\sum_{b:\pi_b>0}\pi_b(\mu_b-m)(\mu_b-m)^T\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, for every \(a\), \(a^TT_{\mathrm{orig}}a=\mathbb E_p[(a\cdot(X-\mu_{X\bmod2}))^2]\). Thus these bounds control within-class mean-square displacement in every direction under both the collision-conditioned class distribution and the original class distribution. Their coefficients tend to zero as \(G\downarrow0\) with \(\kappa\) fixed, independently of dimension. No equality or unpriced ordering between \(T_{\mathrm{orig}}\) and \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}\) is asserted. All identities and inequalities include \(G=0\), singular \(\Sigma\), and \(\kappa=1\), without an inverse covariance. Conditional barycenters need not be atoms or lattice points, and no largest-atom bound is asserted.

## Proof

All moments are finite because the table has finite support. Accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160, part (i), gives \(q\ge2^{-n}>0\), \(2^nq\le e^G\), and \(G\ge0\), so conditioning and the displayed coefficients are well-defined. More explicitly,

\(\Gamma\{X=x,Y=y\}=p_xp_y/q\) when \(x\equiv y\pmod2\), and zero otherwise. Hence the common parity class \(B=X\bmod2=Y\bmod2\) under \(\Gamma\) has probability \(\pi_b^2/q\). Conditional on \(B=b\) with positive mass, \(X,Y\) are independent, each with the original conditional class law \(p_z/\pi_b\) on that class. This is the squared-weight mixture used in the proof of a41dc1e8d1a966c2. Summing out either endpoint also verifies that its marginal is exactly \(p^{\mathrm{end}}\), not in general \(p\).

Conditional on this class, both endpoints have mean \(\mu_b\) and covariance \(\Sigma_b\). Their centered cross moments vanish by conditional independence. Expanding \(X-Y=(X-\mu_b)-(Y-\mu_b)\) and \(J-\mu_b=((X-\mu_b)+(Y-\mu_b))/2\) therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\Gamma[(X-Y)(X-Y)^T\mid B=b]&=2\Sigma_b,\\
\mathbb E_\Gamma[(X-m)(X-m)^T\mid B=b]
&=\Sigma_b+(\mu_b-m)(\mu_b-m)^T,\\
\mathbb E_\Gamma[(J-m)(J-m)^T\mid B=b]
&=\frac12\Sigma_b+(\mu_b-m)(\mu_b-m)^T.
\end{aligned}
\]

Averaging with the probabilities \(\pi_b^2/q\) proves both requested matrix identities, including the factors \(2\) and \(1/2\). These formulas also give \(\mathbb E_\Gamma X=\mathbb E_\Gamma J=\sum_b(\pi_b^2/q)\mu_b\). Subtracting the same outer product of the common mean shift from both moments about \(m\) proves the covariance identity about their own means. Every \(\Sigma_b\) is positive semidefinite, so both weighted matrices are positive semidefinite.

Fix \(a\in\mathbb R^n\). The endpoint comparison in accepted fact a41dc1e8d1a966c2, with its variance interpreted as \(a^T\Sigma a\), says

\(\left|\mathbb E_{\mathrm{end}}[(a\cdot(X-m))^2]-a^T\Sigma a\right|\le\varepsilon_\kappa(G)a^T\Sigma a\).

The sharper midpoint quadratic-moment comparison in accepted fact 2f62583dd7ec54de says

\(\left|\mathbb E_{\mathrm{mid}}[(a\cdot(J-m))^2]-a^T\Sigma a\right|\le\sqrt{(\kappa-1)(e^G-1)}\,a^T\Sigma a\).

They apply to the identical admissible table, original center, fourth-moment hypothesis and conditional midpoint law specified here. Taking the quadratic form of the exact moment difference and then applying these two comparisons gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le\frac12a^TT_{\mathrm{sq}}a
&=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{end}}[(a\cdot(X-m))^2]
-\mathbb E_{\mathrm{mid}}[(a\cdot(J-m))^2]\\
&\le\left[\varepsilon_\kappa(G)
+\sqrt{(\kappa-1)(e^G-1)}\right]a^T\Sigma a.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves the squared-weight bound.

To transfer the conclusion to the original weights, first record the endpoint total variation estimate. With \(\tau=\frac12\sum_z|p^{\mathrm{end}}_z-p_z|\), grouping sites by their class gives \(2q\tau=\sum_b\pi_b|\pi_b-q|\). The parity calculation in the proof of a41dc1e8d1a966c2 can also be checked directly: since \(\sum_b\pi_b=1\) and there are \(2^n\) classes,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_b(\pi_b-q)^2&=q(2^nq-1),\\
2q\tau
&\le\left(\sum_b\pi_b^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_b(\pi_b-q)^2\right)^{1/2}
=q\sqrt{2^nq-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Dividing only by the positive number \(q\), and using ab57fac07c0d3160, gives \(\tau\le\frac12\sqrt{e^G-1}\).

For each positive class, the variance decomposition gives \(a^T\Sigma_ba\le\mathbb E_p[(a\cdot(X-m))^2\mid X\bmod2=b]\). In the difference of weighted conditional variances, classes with \(\pi_b\ge q\) have nonpositive coefficients. Dropping those terms and using the preceding inequality on the remaining classes yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
a^T(T_{\mathrm{orig}}-T_{\mathrm{sq}})a
&=\sum_{b:\pi_b>0}\pi_b(1-\pi_b/q)a^T\Sigma_ba\\
&\le\sum_{b:0<\pi_b<q}\pi_b(1-\pi_b/q)
\mathbb E_p[(a\cdot(X-m))^2\mid X\bmod2=b]\\
&=\sum_z(p_z-p^{\mathrm{end}}_z)_+(a\cdot(z-m))^2\\
&\le\left[\tau\sum_z(p_z-p^{\mathrm{end}}_z)_+
(a\cdot(z-m))^4\right]^{1/2}\\
&\le\sqrt{\kappa\tau}\,a^T\Sigma a
\le\sqrt{\kappa/2}\,(e^G-1)^{1/4}a^T\Sigma a.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \((u)_+=\max\{u,0\}\); the positive difference table has mass \(\tau\) and is bounded by \(p\). The penultimate inequality is precisely the original fourth-moment hypothesis, so no endpoint or conditional fourth-moment premise has been added. This proves the separate one-sided comparison of the two matrices. Combining it with the squared-weight bound proves the stated original-weight bound.

Finally, expand \(X-m=(X-\mu_b)+(\mu_b-m)\) under the original law conditional on each class. The cross terms vanish, and averaging now uses \(\pi_b\), not \(\pi_b^2/q\). This proves \(\Sigma=T_{\mathrm{orig}}+\sum_b\pi_b(\mu_b-m)(\mu_b-m)^T\) and the stated original-law mean-square interpretation.

No argument divides by \(G\), \(\kappa-1\), or a directional variance. If \(a^T\Sigma a=0\), the nonnegative finite sum \(\sum_zp_z(a\cdot(z-m))^2\) vanishes, so that projection is zero at every positive site. Both endpoints and all their midpoints have zero centered projection, and every positive class has zero conditional variance in that direction. Thus every directional identity and bound remains valid. This also covers \(\Sigma=0\). If \(G=0\), both accepted moment discrepancies and \(\tau\) are zero, so the identities force \(T_{\mathrm{sq}}=0\), and the reweighting bound gives \(T_{\mathrm{orig}}=0\). If \(\kappa=1\), the accepted midpoint discrepancy coefficient is zero; the endpoint comparison and the reweighting estimate still apply exactly as written. The coefficient limits follow from \(e^G-1\to0\) and \(e^{G/2}\to1\) for fixed finite \(\kappa\).
