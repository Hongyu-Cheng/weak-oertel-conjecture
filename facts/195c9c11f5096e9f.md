## Statement

Use the exact notation of accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d. Let

\[
\Phi(a)=\sum_{k\ge1}(1-k/a)_+^2,\qquad
2\le a\le5,\qquad b\ge a,
\]

and put \(U=\Phi(a)\), \(V=\Phi(b)\), and \(T=1+U+V\). Assume \(U\le V\le U+1\) and \(T>2\). Define the finitely supported profile by \(g_0=1\), \(g_{-k}=(1-k/a)_+\), and \(g_k=(1-k/b)_+\) for \(k\ge1\). Then

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)
=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb R}
\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}\min\{g_i^2,(\sqrt{s}+\beta i)_+^2\}
\le \frac{5T}{9}
+\frac{s(s-1/12)}{1/2-s}
\qquad\left(0\le s\le\frac6{17}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

Thus \(\mathrm R_{1/2}(\mathbf g,0)\) holds with precisely the normalization and interval of accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d.

For the actual-body consequence, let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)\), \(a_i=\mathcal L^2(C_i)\), \(r_i=\sqrt{a_i}\), and \(W=\sum_i a_i>0\). Suppose an area-weighted median \(j\), with \(A=a_j>0\), is nonheavy: \(A<W/2\). Reflect the integer coordinate if necessary so that

\[
U=\frac1A\sum_{i<j}a_i
\le V=\frac1A\sum_{i>j}a_i,
\qquad \frac14\le U\le\frac65.
\tag{2}
\]

There exists one \(\mathbf p\in\{j\}\times C_j\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge2W/9\). Zero-area integer fibers remain in \(S\).

## Proof

Write \(x=\sqrt s\) and \(P(s)=P_{1/2,1}(s)=s(s-1/12)/(1/2-s)\). This is the rational function of accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d, not a differently normalized allowance. Throughout the proof,

\[
0\le s\le6/17<1/2,\qquad
0\le x\le\sqrt{6/17}<3/5,\qquad
\frac14\le U\le\frac65,\qquad
\frac12<V\le\frac{11}{5}.
\tag{3}
\]

The last strict lower bound follows from \(U\le V\) and \(U+V>1\). All denominators used below are positive.

Nonnegative cap slopes suffice. If \(0\le L\le R\) and \(0\le p\le q\), then
\(\min\{L,p\}+\min\{R,q\}\ge\min\{L,q\}+\min\{R,p\}\), since \(\min\{R,z\}-\min\{L,z\}\) is nondecreasing in \(z\). Apply this to each pair of indices \(-k,k\), with \(L=g_{-k}^2\), \(R=g_k^2\), \(p=(x-\beta k)_+^2\), and \(q=(x+\beta k)_+^2\). Since \(b\ge a\), the retained sum at \(\beta\ge0\) is at least its value at \(-\beta\). This includes all zero roots and clipping equalities.

We first establish the steep-slope bound, including its scalar allowance. Accepted fact d773b98105d1ae6c proves that neighboring root caps \(m\le M\), with cap intercept \(x\), retain total area at most \(M^2+\min\{m^2,(2x-M)_+^2\}\). For \(\beta\ge x/2\), every negative index at most \(-2\) has nonpositive cap value. Apply that accepted inequality with \(m=g_{-1}\) and \(M=g_1=1-1/b\), and retain every positive index at least two in full. This gives

\[
\sum_i\min\{g_i^2,(x+\beta i)_+^2\}
\le V+s+(2x-1+1/b)_+^2.
\tag{4}
\]

To bound (4), put \(\zeta=5T/9-V=(5+5U-4V)/9\). The three inequalities \(U\ge1/4\), \(U\ge V-1\), and \(U>1-V\) imply \(\zeta\ge Z(V)\), where

\[
Z(V)=
\begin{cases}
10/9-V,&V\le3/4,\\
25/36-4V/9,&3/4\le V\le5/4,\\
V/9,&V\ge5/4.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, this is the maximum of those three affine lower bounds; their relevant intersections are \(3/4\) and \(5/4\).

For \(z>1\), the finite-sum identity for \(\Phi\) in accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d gives, with \(m=\lfloor z\rfloor\),

\[
z^2\Phi'(z)
=m(m+1)-\frac{m(m+1)(2m+1)}{3z}.
\tag{6}
\]

The expression is nondecreasing in \(z\), including the integer boundaries. At an integer, the newly appearing summand and its first derivative vanish, so both sides are continuous there. Direct substitution gives

\[
\Phi(8/3)=29/64<1/2,\qquad
(8/3)^2\Phi'(8/3)=9/4,\qquad
\Phi(7/2)=5/7<3/4,\qquad
(7/2)^2\Phi'(7/2)=4.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus \(b>8/3\), and \(V\ge3/4\) implies \(b>7/2\). Consider
\(Z(\Phi(b))-s-(2x-1+1/b)_+^2+P(s)\).
After multiplication by \(b^2\), its derivative on the successive pieces in (5) is

\[
\begin{cases}
-b^2\Phi'(b)+2(2x-1+1/b)_+,\\
-(4/9)b^2\Phi'(b)+2(2x-1+1/b)_+,\\
(1/9)b^2\Phi'(b)+2(2x-1+1/b)_+.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

The positive contribution is less than \(7/5\), since \(x<3/5\) and \(1-1/b>1/2\). The first two derivatives are therefore negative by (7); the third is positive. The squared positive part has derivative zero at its clipping boundary, so no extra boundary is omitted. Consequently the minimum occurs at the unique \(b_*>1\) satisfying \(\Phi(b_*)=5/4\). Since \(\Phi(5)=6/5\), we have \(b_*>5\). It is enough to prove

\[
P(x^2)-x^2+\frac5{36}-(2x-4/5)_+^2>0
\qquad\left(0\le x\le\sqrt{6/17}\right).
\tag{9}
\]

For \(x\le2/5\), multiplication by \(1/2-s\) gives \(2s^2-(13/18)s+5/72\), whose leading coefficient is positive and whose discriminant is \(-11/324\). For \(x\ge2/5\), the corresponding numerator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
6x^4-\frac{16}{5}x^3-\frac{937}{450}x^2
+\frac85x-\frac{451}{1800}
&=\frac7{1800}+\frac{53}{450}w
+\frac{953}{450}w^2+\frac{44}{5}w^3+6w^4,\\
w&=x-\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

For \(w\ge0\) every term is nonnegative and the constant is positive. For \(-1/10\le w\le0\), it is at least
\(7/1800+(53/450)w+(557/450)w^2>0\): use \((44/5)w^3\ge-(22/25)w^2\), and note that the last quadratic has discriminant \(-1090/202500\). This proves (9). Hence (4) is at most \(5T/9+P(s)\) for every steep slope.

For the remaining slopes \(0\le\beta\le x/2\), use the repeated abbreviations

\[
c=x-\beta,\qquad y=x-2\beta,\qquad
z=x+2\beta,\qquad
k(y)=\frac{3-5y}{9},\qquad
\delta(y)=\frac{(y-1/2)_+}{3}.
\tag{11}
\]

Here \(0\le y\le c\le x<3/5\), \(k(y)\ge0\), \(x=2c-y\), and \(\beta=(x-y)/2\). We shall need the following left-loss estimate:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{left}}(a)
&:=\frac49\Phi(a)
-\bigl((1-1/a)^2-c^2\bigr)_+
-\bigl((1-2/a)^2-y^2\bigr)_+\\
&\le-\frac5{36}+c^2-k(y)\bigl(\Phi(a)-1/4\bigr)+\delta(y)
\qquad(2\le a\le5).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

We prove (12) analytically, including its switches. First observe that each of the following two functions is concave for \(u\ge1/4\):

\[
u\longmapsto
\left(1-\frac1{\Phi^{-1}(u)}\right)^2,
\qquad
u\longmapsto
\left(1-\frac1{\Phi^{-1}(u)}\right)^2+
\left(1-\frac2{\Phi^{-1}(u)}\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]

To check this directly, put \(\rho=1/a\) on a support interval \(m\le a\le m+1\), \(m\ge2\). The function
\(\Phi(1/\rho)=m-m(m+1)\rho+m(m+1)(2m+1)\rho^2/6\)
has negative derivative there. For \(J=\{1\}\) or \(J=\{1,2\}\), differentiation of the ratio of first derivatives gives

\[
\frac{d}{d\rho}
\left(
\frac{d\sum_{j\in J}(1-j\rho)^2/d\rho}
{d\Phi(1/\rho)/d\rho}
\right)
=
\frac{
-2m(m+1)\sum_{j\in J}j^2
+\frac23m(m+1)(2m+1)\sum_{j\in J}j
}{
\bigl(d\Phi(1/\rho)/d\rho\bigr)^2
}.
\tag{14}
\]

The numerator is \((4/3)m(m+1)(m-1)\ge0\) for \(J=\{1\}\), and \(4m(m+1)(m-2)\ge0\) for \(J=\{1,2\}\). Dividing once more by the negative derivative of \(\Phi(1/\rho)\) proves the claimed concavity. The derivatives with respect to \(u\) agree at integer values of \(a\), so the concavity holds across every support change.

As \(a\) increases, the first left loss in (12) becomes active at \(1-1/a=c\), and the second at \(1-2/a=y\). Their order is as stated, because \(c\le(1+y)/2\) is equivalent to \(x\le1\). Between these switches, (13) makes \(F_{\mathrm{left}}\), and therefore \(F_{\mathrm{left}}+k(y)(\Phi(a)-1/4)\), convex as a function of \(U=\Phi(a)\). It suffices to check the two endpoints and the two switches which lie in the interval.

At \(a=2\), \(F_{\mathrm{left}}=-5/36+c^2\) if \(c\le1/2\), and \(F_{\mathrm{left}}=1/9\le-5/36+c^2\) otherwise. At the first switch, necessarily \(1/2\le c\le3/5\) and \(a\le5/2\). Write \(\varepsilon=c-1/2\in[0,1/10]\). Then \(U-1/4=\varepsilon+5\varepsilon^2\), while \(y=2c-x\ge2c-3/5\) implies \(k(y)\le(1-10\varepsilon)/9\). The left side of (12) plus \(k(y)(U-1/4)\), minus \(-5/36+c^2\), is at most

\[
\frac{(5-10\varepsilon)(\varepsilon+5\varepsilon^2)}9
-(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2)
=\frac{\varepsilon(-4+6\varepsilon-50\varepsilon^2)}9\le0.
\tag{15}
\]

At the second switch, \(a=2/(1-y)\) and \(1-1/a=(1+y)/2\). Substituting the finite formula for \(\Phi\), the same difference is respectively

\[
\begin{cases}
-y(4-16y+25y^2)/36,&0\le y\le1/3,\\
(2y-1)(-35y^2+37y-7)/36,&1/3\le y\le1/2,\\
(35-183y+301y^2-150y^3)/36,&1/2\le y\le3/5.
\end{cases}
\tag{16}
\]

The first expression is nonpositive since \(4-16y+25y^2>0\). In the second, the first factor is nonpositive and the second is positive: its concavity bounds it below by its two positive endpoint values \(13/9\) and \(11/4\). In the third interval, subtracting \(\delta(y)=(y-1/2)/3\) gives
\((2y-1)(-75y^2+113y-41)/36\le0\).
The second factor is increasing on that interval and has value \(-1/5\) at \(y=3/5\).

Finally, at \(a=5\) the same difference is \(y^2-19y/36-1/90\). On \([0,1/2]\), convexity bounds it above by its endpoint maximum, and the two endpoint values are \(-1/90\) and \(-1/40\). On \([1/2,3/5]\), subtract \(\delta(y)\); the resulting quadratic \(y^2-31y/36+7/45\) is convex and has endpoint values \(-1/40\) and \(-1/900\). This proves (12). Every equality between a switch and an endpoint is included in these calculations.

In particular, when \(x\le1/2\), \(\delta(y)=0\), and (12) implies \(F_{\mathrm{left}}(a)\le F_{\mathrm{left}}(2)=-5/36+(x-\beta)^2\). The stronger term involving \(U-1/4\) is what will preserve the median coupling for the longer right tails.

Three elementary lower bounds for the allowance will simplify the remaining estimates:

\[
P(s)\ge3s-\frac35,\qquad
P(s)\ge4s-\frac89,\qquad
P(s)\ge\frac73s-\frac5{12}.
\tag{17}
\]

For the first two, multiply the differences by \(1/2-s\). The respective numerators are
\(4s^2-(131/60)s+3/10\) and \(5s^2-(107/36)s+4/9\), with positive leading coefficients and discriminants \(-119/3600\) and \(-71/1296\). The third follows from the exact identity

\[
P(s)-\frac73s+\frac5{12}
=\frac{(10/3)(s-1/4)^2}{1/2-s}.
\tag{18}
\]

For later use, the convexity in \(y\) of \((x-y)^2/2+\delta(y)\) also gives

\[
2\beta^2+\delta(y)
=\frac{(x-y)^2}{2}+\delta(y)
\le\frac{x^2}{2}
\qquad(0\le y\le x\le3/5).
\tag{19}
\]

At the two endpoints in \(y\), the values are \(x^2/2\) and \((x-1/2)_+/3\); the latter is at most \(x^2/2\), since \(3x^2-2x+1>0\).

We next prove one uniform scalar estimate which will handle the longest permitted right tail. Define

\[
H(x,y)=
3x^2+\frac{(x-y)^2}{2}
-P(x^2)-\frac{767}{960}
+\frac{19y}{36}+\delta(y)
-\left(\frac9{16}-(2x-y)^2\right)_+.
\tag{20}
\]

We claim \(H(x,y)<0\) whenever \(0\le x\le\sqrt{6/17}\) and \(0\le y\le x\). For \(x\le1/2\), use the third bound in (17); here \(\delta=0\). For fixed \(y\), the resulting upper bound is nondecreasing in \(x\) on \(y\le x\le1/2\). Away from the clipping boundary, its derivative is \(7x/3-y\), plus \(4(2x-y)\) when the positive part is active; both contributions are nonnegative. At \(x=1/2\) it is

\[
-\frac{29}{320}+\frac y{36}+\frac{y^2}{2}
-\left(\frac9{16}-(1-y)^2\right)_+.
\tag{21}
\]

On \([0,1/4]\) this increases to \(-151/2880\). On \([1/4,1/2]\) it equals \(111/320-71y/36+3y^2/2\), a convex quadratic whose endpoint values are \(-151/2880\) and \(-761/2880\). Thus (20) is negative for \(x\le1/2\).

For \(x\ge1/2\), the clipping boundary in (20) is \(y=2x-3/4\), which lies in \([0,x]\) and is less than \(1/2\). On \(0\le y\le2x-3/4\), \(H\) is a convex quadratic in \(y\), and \(\delta=0\). On the remaining interval it is strictly decreasing in \(y\): its one-sided derivatives are at most
\(3y-5x+19/36+1/3\le31/36-2x<0\).
Consequently its maximum is at \(y=0\) or \(y=2x-3/4\). The latter value exceeds the former by \(11x/36-11/96>0\). It remains to bound

\[
H(x,2x-3/4)
=\frac72x^2+\frac{11x}{36}-\frac{877}{960}-P(x^2).
\tag{22}
\]

For \(1/2\le x\le8/15\), the first bound in (17) makes (22) at most
\(x^2/2+11x/36-301/960\). This increases with \(x\), and at \(8/15\) equals \(-361/43200\). For \(x\ge8/15\), the second bound in (17) makes (22) at most
\(-x^2/2+11x/36-71/2880\). This decreases for \(x\ge8/15\), and its value there is \(-169/43200\). This proves (20) on the full interval.

We now apply the loss estimates to the retained sum. Write \(t=g_1=1-1/b\) and \(v=g_2=1-2/b\). Both are positive, by \(b>8/3\). Retain every root at distance at least three in full and subtract exactly the four losses at indices \(-2,-1,1,2\). Their cap values are \(y,c,x+\beta,z\), all nonnegative. Subtracting \(5T/9+P(x^2)\), applying (12), and using \((t^2-(x+\beta)^2)_+\ge t^2-(x+\beta)^2\), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_i\min\{g_i^2,(x+\beta i)_+^2\}
-\frac{5T}{9}-P(x^2)\\
&\quad\le
3x^2+2\beta^2-P(x^2)-\frac{25}{36}
+\delta(y)-k(y)(U-1/4)
+\frac49V-t^2-(v^2-z^2)_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

No distant fiber has been deleted or assigned negative loss. Its full area in this upper bound is included in the exact original totals \(U,V\).

As a function of \(V=\Phi(b)\), the expression
\(qV-t^2-(v^2-z^2)_+\) is convex on either side of the possible switch \(v=z\), for every fixed coefficient \(q\). Indeed, the subtracted root function is \(t^2\) on one side and \(t^2+v^2\) on the other, and both are concave by (13). Thus its maximum on a closed interval of \(V\) occurs at the endpoints or at that switch if the switch lies in the interval. This is an analytic convexity argument for real parameters; it uses no enumeration of parameter values.

First suppose \(5/4\le V\le11/5\). The actual median constraint \(U\ge V-1\), with \(k(y)\ge0\), bounds the right side of (23) by

\[
3x^2+2\beta^2-P(x^2)-\frac{25}{36}
+\delta(y)+\frac54k(y)
+\frac{1+5y}{9}V-t^2-(v^2-z^2)_+.
\tag{24}
\]

We verify its two mass endpoints and its possible saturation switch.

At \(V=5/4\), \(b=b_*>5\), so \(t>4/5\). Discarding the last nonpositive term and using the first inequality in (17), followed by (19), bounds (24) above by

\[
2\beta^2+\delta(y)-\frac{161}{900}
\le\frac3{17}-\frac{161}{900}
=-\frac{37}{15300}<0.
\tag{25}
\]

Here the constant before applying (17) is \(-5/36-16/25=-701/900\).

At \(V=11/5\), \(\Phi(8)=35/16<11/5\), so \(b>8\), \(t>7/8\), and \(v>3/4\). Combining the linear terms in (24) gives \(-1/30+19y/36\). Thus (24) is at most \(H(x,y)\), because \(1/30+49/64=767/960\). It is negative by (20).

At a saturation switch, \(v=z\) and \(t=(1+z)/2\). If \(z\ge3/4\), the coefficient \((1+5y)/9\) in (24) is positive; bound \(V\le11/5\) and \(t\ge7/8\). The result is again at most \(H(x,y)\), with its last positive part equal to zero.

If \(z\le3/4\), then \(b\in[5,8]\), since \(V\ge5/4>\Phi(5)\). The function \(\rho\mapsto\Phi(1/\rho)=\sum_{j\ge1}(1-j\rho)_+^2\) is convex. On \([1/8,1/5]\), the affine function \(58/15-40\rho/3\) equals \(\Phi(5)\) at \(\rho=1/5\) and has value \(11/5>\Phi(8)\) at \(\rho=1/8\). Convexity therefore gives

\[
V=\Phi(b)\le\frac{58}{15}-\frac{40}{3b}
=\frac{20}{3}z-\frac{14}{5}.
\tag{26}
\]

Substituting (26), \(z=2x-y\), and \(t=(1+z)/2\) into (24) gives the upper bound

\[
\frac52x^2+\frac{13x}{27}-\frac{151}{180}-P(x^2)
+\left(\frac{200x}{27}-\frac{269}{108}\right)y
-\frac{373}{108}y^2+\delta(y).
\tag{27}
\]

For \(x\le1/2\), \(\delta=0\), and the third inequality in (17) makes (27) at most

\[
-\frac{151}{1080}+\frac{131}{108}y-\frac{373}{108}y^2
\le-\frac{151}{1080}+\frac{17161}{161136}<0.
\tag{28}
\]

The last strict comparison is exact: \(17161\cdot1080=18533880<24331536=151\cdot161136\). For \(x\ge1/2\), the condition \(z\le3/4\) implies \(y\ge2x-3/4\). The one-sided derivatives of (27) with respect to \(y\) are at most

\[
\frac{800x-269-746y}{108}+\frac13
\le\frac{653-1384x}{216}<0.
\tag{29}
\]

Hence (27) is maximized at \(y=2x-3/4\), where \(\delta=0\), the bound in (26) is \(11/5\), and (27) equals (22). That value is negative. This proves (24), and therefore (23), whenever \(V\ge5/4\).

It remains to treat \(1/2<V\le5/4\). For \(x\le1/2\), (12) implies \(F_{\mathrm{left}}\le-5/36+c^2\). The neighboring-pair inequality of accepted fact d773b98105d1ae6c, now with the auxiliary left root cap \(x\) and right root cap \(t\), gives

\[
c^2-\bigl(t^2-(x+\beta)^2\bigr)_+\le(2x-t)_+^2,
\tag{30}
\]

because \(c\le x\le t\). Consequently the retained sum minus \(5T/9+P(x^2)\) is at most
\(x^2-25/36+4V/9+(2x-t)_+^2-P(x^2)\).
To see that this is negative, differentiate its negative as a function of \(b\). After multiplication by \(b^2\), the derivative is
\(-(4/9)b^2\Phi'(b)+2(2x-t)_+\), which is negative for \(b>8/3\): the first magnitude is at least one by (7), while the second is at most \(3/4\). The negative is therefore minimized at \(V=5/4\), where it is bounded below by the positive quantity in (9). This proves the required inequality for this range.

Finally suppose \(x\ge1/2\) and \(1/2<V\le5/4\). Drop the nonpositive term \(-k(y)(U-1/4)\) in (23). By the convexity in \(V\) just established, only the mass endpoints \(1/2,5/4\) and the possible switch \(v=z\) require bounds. The endpoint \(5/4\) is exactly (25).

At \(V=1/2\), (7) gives \(b>8/3\) and \(t>5/8\). Using (17), (19), and discarding the last nonpositive term in (23), we get

\[
\text{right side of (23)}
\le\frac3{17}+\frac35-\frac{497}{576}
=-\frac{4229}{48960}<0.
\tag{31}
\]

Here \(-25/36+2/9-25/64=-497/576\). The strict original constraint \(V>1/2\) is covered by bounding on its closed enlargement.

At a switch \(v=z\), the direct finite value \(\Phi(16/3)=335/256>5/4\) gives \(b<16/3\), hence \(z<5/8\). Since \(z=x+2\beta\ge x\ge1/2\), it follows that \(\beta\le1/16\), \(t=(1+z)/2\ge3/4\), and \(\delta(y)\le1/30\). Bound \(V\le5/4\) in (23), again omit \(-k(y)(U-1/4)\), and use the first inequality in (17). The result is at most

\[
\frac1{128}+\frac35-\frac5{36}-\frac9{16}+\frac1{30}
=-\frac{347}{5760}<0.
\tag{32}
\]

The endpoint and switch estimates, with the continuous convexity argument, prove (23) for every remaining \(V\) and shallow slope. Together with (4)--(10) and the reflection comparison, they prove (1) for every real slope. Taking the supremum preserves the inequality. The proof used the exact finite-sum formula and continuous derivatives at support changes, so all real tent lengths, integer support changes, zero roots, coincident switches, and boundary equalities have been included. In particular \(s=0\) is already covered by the steep-slope argument.

We conclude with the actual-fiber implication. Accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d supplies root concavity on the positive-area integer support, the original-mass-preserving replacement of each tail, the exact normalization of the envelope, and its fixed-fiber rational consumer. Apply its separate variable-length tail replacements at the actual anchor in (2), after translation and division of the roots by \(\sqrt A\). There are unique \(a,b>1\) with \(\Phi(a)=U\) and \(\Phi(b)=V\). By (2), \(\Phi(2)=1/4\), and \(\Phi(5)=6/5\), we have \(2\le a\le5\) and \(b\ge a\). The area-weighted median property is \(V\le U+1\), and nonheaviness is \(T=W/A>2\). The replacement is exactly the profile in the statement, with both lengths measured from the anchor, and preserves \(U,V,A,W\).

For an actual retained area \(0\le s\le6A/17\), its envelope therefore satisfies

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf r,j}(s)
\le A\,\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s/A)
\le\frac{5W}{9}+A\,P(s/A)
=\frac{5W}{9}+P_{1/2,A}(s).
