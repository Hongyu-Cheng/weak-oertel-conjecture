## Statement

Let \(p,q\ge1\) and \(m\ge0\) be integers, put \(r=p+q+m\), and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional polytope affinely isomorphic to \(\Delta_p\times\Delta_q\times[0,1]^m\). Assume \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\), and set \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\) and \(B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\). Then \(B(P)<4^r\), whether or not the vertices of \(P\) are lattice points.

More precisely, pull back the barycentric coordinate functions of every factor and divide each by its value at \(\mathbf0\). Denote the resulting normalized affine facet slacks by \(f_0,\ldots,f_p\), \(g_0,\ldots,g_q\), and \(h_{a,0},h_{a,1}\) for \(1\le a\le m\). Denote their positive denominators by \(\beta_i,\gamma_j,\eta_{a,b}\), respectively, and label a maximal denominator in each factor by \(0\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=0}^p\beta_i f_i(\mathbf x)&=1,
&\beta_0&\ge\beta_i,\\
\sum_{j=0}^q\gamma_j g_j(\mathbf x)&=1,
&\gamma_0&\ge\gamma_j,\\
\eta_{a,0}h_{a,0}(\mathbf x)+\eta_{a,1}h_{a,1}(\mathbf x)&=1,
&\eta_{a,0}&\ge\eta_{a,1}\qquad(1\le a\le m).
\end{aligned}
\]

The explicit tilt

\[
\boldsymbol\theta=-(\log2)\left(
\sum_{i=1}^p\nabla f_i+\sum_{j=1}^q\nabla g_j+
\sum_{a=1}^m\nabla h_{a,1}\right)
\]

satisfies \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}<4^r\).

## Proof

We extend the normalized-slack argument of accepted fact 481900be71a5c26c. Its \(f_i,g_j,\beta_i,\gamma_j\) retain their meanings for the two simplex factors; here \(r=p+q+m\), and we write \(N\) for a bin-fiber cardinality, reserving \(m\) for the number of interval factors. The full argument follows.

The assumed affine product representation provides the stated barycentric functions. Since \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), their values at \(\mathbf0\) are positive. Each factor's barycentric coordinates sum to one, giving the displayed identities and showing that its denominators also sum to one. All normalized slacks equal one at \(\mathbf0\). The polytope \(P\) is exactly the set where all these slacks are nonnegative, and its interior is exactly the set where they are all positive. In particular, every such slack \(s\) satisfies

\[
s\bigl(\pm(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\bigr)
=1\pm\bigl(s(\mathbf I)-s(\mathbf J)\bigr).
\]

Select the \(r\) slacks \(f_1,\ldots,f_p,g_1,\ldots,g_q,h_{1,1},\ldots,h_{m,1}\). For \(\mathbf I\in D\), let \(\mathbf k(\mathbf I)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\) be their coordinatewise floors, in this order. Write their respective fractional parts as \(u_i(\mathbf I),v_j(\mathbf I),w_a(\mathbf I)\in[0,1)\). Fix a nonempty fiber \(D_{\mathbf k}=\{\mathbf I\in D:\mathbf k(\mathbf I)=\mathbf k\}\), of cardinality \(N\), and define

\[
\rho(\mathbf I)=\sum_{i=1}^p\frac{\beta_i}{\beta_0}u_i(\mathbf I),
\qquad
\omega(\mathbf I)=\sum_{j=1}^q\frac{\gamma_j}{\gamma_0}v_j(\mathbf I).
\]

For \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\in D_{\mathbf k}\), the barycentric identities imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0(\mathbf I)-f_0(\mathbf J)&=-\bigl(\rho(\mathbf I)-\rho(\mathbf J)\bigr),\\
g_0(\mathbf I)-g_0(\mathbf J)&=-\bigl(\omega(\mathbf I)-\omega(\mathbf J)\bigr),\\
h_{a,0}(\mathbf I)-h_{a,0}(\mathbf J)
&=-\frac{\eta_{a,1}}{\eta_{a,0}}\bigl(w_a(\mathbf I)-w_a(\mathbf J)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Every selected slack difference has absolute value strictly less than one. Each omitted interval slack difference also has absolute value strictly less than one: its coefficient ratio is at most one, and the difference of its two fractional parts has absolute value strictly less than one. This remains strict when the two interval denominators are equal. The affine slack formula therefore makes all selected slacks and all omitted interval slacks positive on both \(\mathbf I-\mathbf J\) and \(\mathbf J-\mathbf I\).

If \(\mathbf I\ne\mathbf J\), both difference vectors are nonzero standard lattice points, so neither is interior to \(P\). Only \(f_0\) and \(g_0\) can exclude them. Consequently one of the two omitted simplex slack differences is at most \(-1\), and one is at least \(1\). They must be different slacks. Equivalently, either

\[
\rho(\mathbf I)-\rho(\mathbf J)\ge1,
\qquad
\omega(\mathbf I)-\omega(\mathbf J)\le-1,
\]

or these inequalities hold with \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\) interchanged. Equality is allowed: a nonzero lattice difference may lie on a facet, but cannot lie in the interior.

The \(\rho\)-values are distinct. Order the fiber as \(\mathbf I_1,\ldots,\mathbf I_N\) with increasing \(\rho\). Consecutive \(\rho\)-values increase by at least one and consecutive \(\omega\)-values decrease by at least one. Since \(\rho,\omega\ge0\), this gives \(\rho(\mathbf I_\ell)\ge\ell-1\) and \(\omega(\mathbf I_\ell)\ge N-\ell\). Maximality of \(\beta_0,\gamma_0\) and nonnegativity of the interval fractional parts now give

\[
\sum_{i=1}^p u_i(\mathbf I_\ell)+\sum_{j=1}^q v_j(\mathbf I_\ell)
+\sum_{a=1}^m w_a(\mathbf I_\ell)
\ge\rho(\mathbf I_\ell)+\omega(\mathbf I_\ell)\ge N-1.
\]

Therefore the fiber's discounted sum satisfies

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf k}}
2^{-\sum_i u_i(\mathbf I)-\sum_j v_j(\mathbf I)-\sum_a w_a(\mathbf I)}
\le N2^{-(N-1)}\le1.
\]

The last inequality holds for every integer \(N\ge1\): it is equality for \(N=1\), and for \(N\ge2\) the binomial expansion gives \(2^{N-1}\ge1+(N-1)=N\). Thus singleton fibers are included.

Each selected slack has constant term one, so the displayed tilt obeys

\[
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I
=(\log2)\left(r-\sum_{i=1}^p f_i(\mathbf I)
-\sum_{j=1}^q g_j(\mathbf I)-\sum_{a=1}^m h_{a,1}(\mathbf I)\right).
\]

The support \(D\) is finite because \(P\) is compact. Summing the fiber bound yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
&\le2^r\sum_{\mathbf k:D_{\mathbf k}\ne\varnothing}
2^{-\sum_{b=1}^r k_b}\\
&<2^r\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}
2^{-\sum_{b=1}^r k_b}
=2^r\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\right)^r
=4^r.
\end{aligned}
\]

Strictness follows from finitely many occupied bins, an infinite nonnegative integer grid, and strictly positive summands. Taking the infimum over tilts proves the claim. Empty interval sums handle \(m=0\). All boundary lattice points were retained, and the only lattice property used was closure under subtraction, so no factorwise lattice splitting or lattice-vertex assumption is needed.
