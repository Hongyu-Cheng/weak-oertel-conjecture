## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $\mu$ be uniform probability on $F=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\}$, and let $g=(1/d,\ldots,1/d)$. Independently take $Z\sim\mu$ and a uniform vertex index $I$. For $0\le b\le1/d$ put $Y_b=(1-b)Z+be_I$ and $a=1-db$. The largest constant for which
$$
\mathbb E\psi(Y_b)-\mathbb E\psi(Z)
\ge c_d(b)\bigl(\psi(g)-\mathbb E\psi(Z)\bigr)
$$
holds for every continuous concave $\psi$ on $F$ is
$$
c_d(b)=\frac{a-a^d/(1-b)^{d-1}}{(d-1)^2}.
$$
It is positive for $0<b<1/d$ and vanishes at both endpoints. The test $\psi(x)=\min_i x_i$ attains equality with a positive Jensen gap throughout the parameter interval. In particular,
$$
c_d\bigl(1/(d+1)\bigr)=\frac{1-d^{1-d}}{(d-1)^2(d+1)}.
$$

**Facet consequence.** On any facet of a $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, let $\mu$ be normalized facet volume, $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ its barycentric coordinates, and $g_F$ its centroid. Set $m=d+1$, $\Gamma=(d/m)^d$, $c=c_d(1/m)$, $N(s)=\#\{i:s_i\ge1/m\}$, and $E_d(\psi)=\int_F(N-m\Gamma)\psi\,d\mu$. The uniform point $Z$ and averaged point $Y_{1/m}$ on the facet obey
$$
E_d(\psi)=m\Gamma\bigl(\mathbb E\psi(Y_{1/m})-\mathbb E\psi(Z)\bigr).
$$
For every continuous concave $\psi$ and every supporting affine majorant $\ell$ at $g_F$,
$$
\int_F(\ell-\psi)\,d\mu=\psi(g_F)-\int_F\psi\,d\mu\le\frac{E_d(\psi)}{m\Gamma c}.
$$
The coefficient is sharp, and $E_d(\psi)=0$ exactly for affine $\psi$. In convex form, every continuous convex $f\ge0$ with $f(g_F)=0$ satisfies $\int_F Nf\,d\mu\le m\Gamma(1-c)\int_F f\,d\mu$.

## Proof

**Disintegration.** Assume $0<b<1/d$ and put $h=1-b$ and $\theta=db$. The map $z\mapsto hz+be_i$ scales $(d-1)$-dimensional volume by $h^{d-1}$ and maps onto $\{y\in F:y_i\ge b\}$. Thus the density of $Y_b$ relative to $\mu$ is $N_b(y)/(dh^{d-1})$, where $N_b(y)=\#\{i:y_i\ge b\}$. Tied minima are null under both laws, and the minimum label $i$ has probability $1/d$ under each by symmetry. In its cell write $y=tg+(1-t)w$, where $t=dy_i$ and $w$ is on the facet $w_i=0$. Under the uniform cell law, $w$ is uniform and independent of $t$, whose density is $p(t)=(d-1)(1-t)^{d-2}$ on $(0,1)$. Indeed the cell is the simplex with vertices $g$ and $e_j$ for $j\ne i$, and its slices at $t$ scale opposite-facet volume by $(1-t)^{d-2}$.

For $t<\theta$, the condition $y_j\ge b$ for $j\ne i$ becomes $w_j\ge s(t)$ with $s(t)=(b-t/d)/(1-t)\in[0,b]$, while $y_i<b$. The conditional $w$ density is proportional to the number of its coordinates above $s(t)$. Its normalizer is $(d-1)(1-s(t))^{d-2}$, by the same volume scaling on the facet. This gives the lower-dimensional vertex averaging law at step $s(t)$. For $d=2$ it is the point law. For $d\ge3$, $s(t)\le b<1/d<1/(d-1)$, so the subcritical comparison of accepted fact 68c5513d4b6cae29 in $d-1$ coordinates applies. For $t>\theta$ all coordinates exceed $b$, so $w$ is uniform. Hence for any continuous convex test, replacing the conditional $w$ by uniform volume at the same $i,t$ gives an upper bound.

The density $q(t)$ of $t$ under $Y_b$ has likelihood ratio
$$
\frac{q(t)}{p(t)}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{d-1}{dh^{d-1}}\left(1-s(t)\right)^{d-2},&0<t<\theta,\\
h^{-(d-1)},&\theta<t<1.
\end{cases}
$$
Here $(1-s(t))'=(1/d-b)/(1-t)^2>0$, and $1-s(t)\to1$ as $t\uparrow\theta$. The upward jump at $\theta$ and the nonnegative power imply $q/p$ is nondecreasing, also for $d=2$.

**Radial bound.** Let $f$ be continuous, convex, and nonnegative with $f(g)=0$. Put $R=1-t$ and write $p_R,q_R$ for the corresponding radial densities. For each fixed $w$ let $F_w(r)=f(g+r(w-g))$. By convexity $F_w(r)/r$ is nondecreasing for $r>0$, between zero and $F_w(1)$. The ratio $q_R/p_R$ is bounded and nonincreasing. Under the probability density $rp_R(r)/\mathbb E_p R$, these two functions have nonpositive covariance: the product of their increments between two independent samples is nonpositive. Multiplying out this covariance inequality gives
$$
\mathbb E_q F_w(R)\le\frac{\mathbb E_q R}{\mathbb E_p R}\,\mathbb E_p F_w(R).
$$
Apply the conditional facet comparison before this radial bound and then integrate over the uniform minimum label and facet point. This proves $\mathbb E f(Y_b)\le(\mathbb E_q R/\mathbb E_p R)\mathbb E f(Z)$.

**Optimal coefficient.** Translating the constrained coordinates of $Z$ within $Y_b=hZ+be_I$ gives
$$
\Pr(\min_i(Y_b)_i\ge u)=
\begin{cases}
(1-(d-1)u/h)^{d-1},&0\le u\le b,\\
((1-du)/h)^{d-1},&b\le u\le1/d,\\
0,&u>1/d.
\end{cases}
$$
In the first range the raised coordinate is automatic. In the second range all coordinates have lower bounds, whose sum in $Z$ is $(du-b)/h$. The two integrals over $[0,b]$ and $[b,1/d]$ are respectively $h[1-(a/h)^d]/[d(d-1)]$ and $a^d/(d^2h^{d-1})$. Thus
$$
\mathbb E\min_i(Y_b)_i=\frac{h}{d(d-1)}-\frac{a^d}{d^2(d-1)h^{d-1}}.
$$
For $Z$, the survival function is $(1-du)^{d-1}$ on $[0,1/d]$, so $\mathbb E\min_i Z_i=1/d^2$ and $\mathbb E_pR=(d-1)/d$. Since $\mathbb E_qR=1-d\mathbb E\min_i(Y_b)_i$, the expression $1-\mathbb E_q R/\mathbb E_pR$ equals $c_d(b)$. Positivity follows from $0<a<h$ in the open interval.

A continuous concave $\psi$ has a supporting affine majorant $\ell$ at the relative interior point $g$ by the supporting hyperplane theorem. A supporting plane of the hypograph there has a nonzero height component, since $g$ is interior in the affine hull of $F$. Both laws have mean $g$, so for $f=\ell-\psi$ we have $\mathbb E f(Z)=\psi(g)-\mathbb E\psi(Z)$ and $\mathbb E f(Z)-\mathbb E f(Y_b)=\mathbb E\psi(Y_b)-\mathbb E\psi(Z)$. The radial bound gives the desired estimate. At $b=0$ the laws agree, and at $b=1/d$ the critical comparison of 68c5513d4b6cae29 supplies the inequality with coefficient zero. For $\psi=\min_i x_i$ the positive Jensen gap is $(d-1)/d^2$. The minimum expectation formula, valid at both endpoints by the same survival calculation, gives equality throughout the interval. This proves optimality. Substituting $a=1/(d+1)$ and $h=d/(d+1)$ gives the particular coefficient.

**Facet consequence.** In barycentric coordinates on any facet, the law of $Y_{1/m}$ relative to $\mu$ has density $N/(dh^{d-1})=N/(m\Gamma)$ with $h=d/m$. This proves the identity for $E_d$, and the sharp bound follows by applying the quantitative estimate to $\psi$. Affine $\psi$ gives zero because both laws have the facet centroid as mean. Conversely, $E_d(\psi)=0$ forces the nonnegative continuous function $\ell-\psi$ to have zero integral and therefore vanish everywhere. The minimum-coordinate test attains the claimed coefficient. Taking $\psi=-f$ gives the convex form.
