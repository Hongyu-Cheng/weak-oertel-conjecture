## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed-integer mass, and use the following objects and normalization from accepted fact `3f83f851f6547bc6`:
\[
 P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C,
 \qquad
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\]
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf_{\substack{L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\x\in L}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L),
 \qquad
 g(z):=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d},
\]
\[
 B:=\{(z,u):z\in P,\ 0\le u\le g(z)\},
 \qquad
 \eta(E):=
 \sum_{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}
 \int_0^{g(z)}\mathbf 1_E(z,u)\,d\,u^{d-1}\,du.
\]
For a closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), let
\[
 \mathcal A_H:=
 \{(z,u)\in B:\text{ some }x\in C_z\text{ satisfies }(z,x)\in H
 \text{ and }\delta_C(z,x)\ge u^d\},
\]
\[
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z),
 \qquad
 D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q):=
 \inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed ambient halfspace}\\q\in\mathcal A_H}}
 \eta(\mathcal A_H),
\]
where the whole space is allowed as an improper closed halfspace. Put
\[
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
 \qquad
 h_S(p):=\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed ambient halfspace}\\p\in H}}\mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C):=
 \sup_{q\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)}D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q).
\]

For every \(m\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
 M_m:=\max_{x\in C_m}\delta_C(m,x)=g(m)^d,
 \qquad
 q_m^{\mathrm{top}}:=(m,g(m)),
\]
\[
 \mathcal T_m:=\{x\in C_m:\delta_C(m,x)=M_m\},
 \qquad
 \kappa_m:=\inf_{x\in\mathcal T_m}h_S((m,x)).
\]
Then \(\mathcal T_m\) is a nonempty compact convex set, and
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 =\max_{m\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_m^{\mathrm{top}}).
 }
 \tag{1}
\]
For every such \(m\),
\[
 q_m^{\mathrm{top}}\in\mathcal A_H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H\cap(\{m\}\times\mathcal T_m)\ne\varnothing,
 \tag{2}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_d\kappa_m
 \le D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_m^{\mathrm{top}})
 \le\kappa_m,
 \qquad
 D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_m^{\mathrm{top}})\ge M_m.
 }
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_d\max_m\kappa_m
 \le S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \le\max_m\kappa_m.
 }
 \tag{4}
\]

For the profile bound, write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 D_+:=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 N_+:=|D_+|,
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in D_+}a_z>0,
 \qquad
 \alpha:=2^{-n},
 \qquad
 \theta_d:=1-\gamma_d,
\]
and, for \(t>0\),
\[
 Q_a(t):=\sum_{z\in D_+}(a_z-\theta_dt)_+.
 \tag{5}
\]
For every \(t>0\) with \(Q_a(t)>0\), there is \(m_t\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) such that
\[
 D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q_{m_t}^{\mathrm{top}})
 \ge
 \begin{cases}
 \gamma_d\alpha Q_a(t),&a_{m_t}\le t,\\[1mm]
 M_{m_t}>\gamma_dt,&a_{m_t}>t.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 \gamma_d\sup_{t>0}\min\{\alpha Q_a(t),t\}.
 }
 \tag{7}
\]

The following consequences hold.

1. Since
\[
 \gamma_dW\le|\eta|=\sum_zM_z\le\frac12W,
 \tag{8}
\]
one has the support-sensitive estimates
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 \frac{\gamma_d2^{-n}W}
 {1+(1-\gamma_d)2^{-n}N_+}
 \ge
 \frac{2\gamma_d}
 {1+(1-\gamma_d)2^{-n}N_+}\,2^{-n}|\eta|.
 }
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus, if \(N_+\le K2^n\), then
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 \frac{2\gamma_d}{1+(1-\gamma_d)K}\,2^{-n}|\eta|
 >
 \frac{2}{e+(e-1)K}\,2^{-n}|\eta|.
 \tag{10}
\]

2. If
\[
 r:=\frac{\min_{z\in D_+}a_z}{\max_{z\in D_+}a_z}>1-\gamma_d,
 \tag{11}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 2\gamma_d\left(1-\frac{1-\gamma_d}{r}\right)
 2^{-n}|\eta|.
 }
 \tag{12}
\]
In particular, equal positive fiber volumes give
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge2\gamma_d^2\,2^{-n}|\eta|
 >\frac2{e^2}\,2^{-n}|\eta|.
 \tag{13}
\]

3. Put \(\tau_d:=(1-\gamma_d)/\gamma_d\) and
\[
 Q_M(t):=\sum_z(2M_z-\tau_dt)_+.
 \]
Then, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 Q_M(t)\le Q_a(t/\gamma_d),
 \tag{14}
\]
so (7) already implies
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 \sup_{t>0}\min\{t,\gamma_d2^{-n}Q_M(t)\}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Thus the deepest-mass variational profile (15) is dominated by the volume profile (7), not an additional mechanism.

4. For the accepted shattering family \(C_R\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{N_R}\) of `01bace1a2c4ef916`, let \(r_R\) be the ratio in (11). Then
\[
 r_R>\frac78,
\]
and (12), with \(\gamma_{N_R}>e^{-1}\), gives the corrected calibration
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C_R)
 >
 \frac2e\,\frac{8/e-1}{7}\,2^{-2}|\eta|
 =0.204229378492\ldots\,2^{-2}|\eta|.
 }
 \tag{16}
\]
Hence that family is a no-go for the bounded-complexity templates proved in `01bace1a2c4ef916`, but it is not a counterexample to target-scale projected-hit depth.

## Proof

Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` says that \(x\mapsto\delta_C(m,x)\) is upper semicontinuous and that every absolute-depth superlevel set is compact and convex. It also says that the maximum \(M_m\) is attained. Hence every \(\mathcal T_m\) is nonempty, compact, and convex; if \(M_m=0\), then \(\mathcal T_m=C_m\).

Every hit range is a lower ideal in its last coordinate: if \(0\le u\le v\le g(m)\) and \((m,v)\in\mathcal A_H\), the same witness also gives \((m,u)\in\mathcal A_H\). Thus
\[
 D_{\mathrm{hit}}((m,u))\le D_{\mathrm{hit}}((m,v)).
\]
The compact set \(P\) has only finitely many lattice points, and positive mixed mass makes this finite set nonempty. Maximizing separately in every lifted lattice fiber proves (1).

At the top level, a witness \(x\in C_m\cap H_m\) must satisfy
\(
 \delta_C(m,x)\ge g(m)^d=M_m.
\)
By maximality this is equivalent to \(x\in\mathcal T_m\), proving (2), including the case \(M_m=0\). From the definition of \(h_S\), merely combining the two infima gives
\[
 \kappa_m
 =
 \inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed ambient halfspace}\\
 H\cap(\{m\}\times\mathcal T_m)\ne\varnothing}}
 \mu_C(H).
 \tag{17}
\]
Accepted fact `3f83f851f6547bc6` gives, for every one of these same halfspaces,
\[
 \gamma_d\mu_C(H)\le\eta(\mathcal A_H)\le\mu_C(H).
\]
Taking infima and using (2) proves the two-sided part of (3). If \(H\) hits \(q_m^{\mathrm{top}}\), the hit range in its lifted \(m\)-fiber contains every level from \(0\) to \(g(m)\), whose exact \(\eta\)-mass is
\[
 \int_0^{g(m)}d\,u^{d-1}\,du=M_m.
\]
This proves the last inequality in (3). Maximizing the two-sided estimate proves (4).

We next prove (6). Fix \(t>0\) with \(Q_a(t)>0\), and apply the \(d=0\) specialization of the weighted lattice-centerpoint theorem `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the atomic weights
\[
 w_t(z):=(a_z-\theta_dt)_+.
\]
It gives \(m_t\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m_t\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in J}w_t(z)\ge\alpha Q_a(t).
 \tag{18}
\]
The point \(m_t\) belongs to the convex hull of the positive-weight support: otherwise strict separation supplies a proper closed base halfspace containing \(m_t\) and missing that support, contradicting (18). Brunn-Minkowski applied along a convex representation of \(m_t\) shows that \(a(\cdot)^{1/d}\) is concave on the base projection of \(C\); since every point in that representation has positive volume, \(a_{m_t}>0\). In particular, \(m_t\in D_+\).

First suppose \(a_{m_t}\le t\). Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace with \(q_{m_t}^{\mathrm{top}}\in\mathcal A_H\). If \(H\) is the whole space, the lower sandwich in `3f83f851f6547bc6` gives
\[
 \eta(\mathcal A_H)\ge\gamma_dW\ge\gamma_d\alpha Q_a(t).
 \tag{19}
\]
Suppose that \(H\) is proper. By (2), choose \(y\in\mathcal T_{m_t}\) with \((m_t,y)\in H\), and move the boundary of \(H\) parallel toward \((m_t,y)\) until it passes through that point. The resulting proper closed halfspace \(H^0\) satisfies
\[
 H^0\subseteq H,
 \qquad
 q_{m_t}^{\mathrm{top}}\in\mathcal A_{H^0},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{H^0}\subseteq\mathcal A_H.
 \tag{20}
\]

If \(H^0\) is vertical, it equals \(J\times\mathbb R^d\) for a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m_t\). Thus
\[
 \mu_C(H^0)=\sum_{z\in J}a_z
 \ge\sum_{z\in J}w_t(z)
 \ge\alpha Q_a(t).
 \tag{21}
\]

Suppose instead that \(H^0\) is nonvertical. Let \(H^{0,-}\) be the complementary closed side and set
\[
 b(z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H^{0,-}_z).
\]
The body \(C\cap H^{0,-}\) is compact and convex. Brunn-Minkowski shows that \(b^{1/d}\) is concave on its base projection. The bad lattice set
\[
 \mathcal D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:b(z)>b(m_t)\}
\]
is finite. Moreover, \(m_t\notin\operatorname{conv}\mathcal D\): otherwise a convex representation by points of \(\mathcal D\), followed by strict Jensen inequality for the concave function \(b^{1/d}\), would give
\[
 b(m_t)^{1/d}
 \ge\sum_i\lambda_i b(z_i)^{1/d}
 >b(m_t)^{1/d}.
\]
If \(\mathcal D\ne\varnothing\), strict separation therefore gives a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m_t\) disjoint from \(\mathcal D\). If \(\mathcal D=\varnothing\), choose any proper closed base halfspace through \(m_t\). In both cases,
\[
 b(z)\le b(m_t)
 \qquad(z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n).
 \tag{22}
\]

The continuous-coordinate normal of \(H^0\) is nonzero, so its boundary has zero \(d\)-volume in every lattice fiber. Since \(H^0_{m_t}\) contains the deepest point \(y\),
\[
 a_{m_t}-b(m_t)
 =\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{m_t}\cap H^0_{m_t})
 \ge M_{m_t}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Grünbaum's centroid halfspace inequality gives
\(
 M_{m_t}\ge\gamma_da_{m_t}.
\)
Using \(a_{m_t}\le t\), (23) yields
\[
 b(m_t)\le(1-\gamma_d)a_{m_t}\le\theta_dt.
 \tag{24}
\]
For every \(z\in J\cap\mathbb Z^n\), equations (22), (24), and the zero boundary mass give
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H^0_z)
 =a_z-b(z)
 \ge(a_z-\theta_dt)_+.
\]
Consequently, (18) implies
\[
 \mu_C(H^0)
 \ge\sum_{z\in J}(a_z-\theta_dt)_+
 \ge\alpha Q_a(t).
 \tag{25}
\]

In both the vertical and nonvertical cases, (20), the lower sandwich in `3f83f851f6547bc6`, and (21) or (25) give
\[
 \eta(\mathcal A_H)
 \ge\eta(\mathcal A_{H^0})
 \ge\gamma_d\mu_C(H^0)
 \ge\gamma_d\alpha Q_a(t).
\]
Taking the infimum over all \(H\) proves the first branch of (6).

If instead \(a_{m_t}>t\), then every hit range containing \(q_{m_t}^{\mathrm{top}}\) has exact target-fiber hit mass \(M_{m_t}\). Grünbaum's inequality gives
\[
 M_{m_t}\ge\gamma_da_{m_t}>\gamma_dt,
\]
which proves the second branch of (6). Thus for every \(t\) with \(Q_a(t)>0\),
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)\ge\gamma_d\min\{\alpha Q_a(t),t\}.
\]
When \(Q_a(t)=0\), the right side is zero. Taking the supremum proves (7).

For (8), accepted fact `3f83f851f6547bc6` applied to the whole space gives \(|\eta|\ge\gamma_dW\), and the defining integral gives \(|\eta|=\sum_zM_z\). To prove the upper bound, fix a positive-volume fiber and a point in it. Two complementary closed halfspaces through that point have zero-volume common boundary and cap volumes summing to \(a_z\), so one cap has volume at most \(a_z/2\). Taking first the infimum over caps and then the maximum over points gives \(M_z\le a_z/2\). Zero-volume fibers contribute zero. Summation proves (8).

Since \((x)_+\ge x\),
\[
 Q_a(t)\ge W-\theta_dN_+t.
\]
Set
\[
 t_*:=\frac{\alpha W}{1+\theta_d\alpha N_+}.
\]
Then \(Q_a(t_*)>0\) and \(\alpha(W-\theta_dN_+t_*)=t_*\). Substitution in (7), followed by \(W\ge2|\eta|\), proves (9). The first implication in (10) is immediate; the strict dimension-free estimate follows from \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\) and monotonicity of \(\gamma\mapsto2\gamma/[1+(1-\gamma)K]\).

For (12), let \(a_{\max}:=\max_{z\in D_+}a_z\) and use \(t=a_{\max}\). Condition (11) makes every summand in \(Q_a(t)\) positive. The selected point in (6) belongs to \(D_+\), hence \(a_{m_t}\le a_{\max}=t\), so the first branch applies. Moreover,
\[
 Q_a(a_{\max})
 =\sum_{z\in D_+}(a_z-\theta_da_{\max})
 \ge\left(1-\frac{\theta_d}{r}\right)W.
\]
Equation (7) and \(W\ge2|\eta|\) prove (12). Setting \(r=1\) gives (13).

The inequality \(M_z\le a_z/2\) proved above gives, for \(s=t/\gamma_d\),
\[
 (2M_z-\tau_dt)_+
 \le(a_z-\theta_ds)_+.
\]
Summing proves (14). Applying (7) at \(s=t/\gamma_d\) gives
\[
 S_{\mathrm{hit}}(C)
 \ge
 \gamma_d\min\{2^{-n}Q_a(t/\gamma_d),t/\gamma_d\}
 =\min\{\gamma_d2^{-n}Q_a(t/\gamma_d),t\},
\]
which dominates \(\min\{\gamma_d2^{-n}Q_M(t),t\}\). Taking the supremum proves (15).

Finally, accepted fact `01bace1a2c4ef916` constructs \(C_R\) with continuous dimension \(N_R\) and simplex lattice fibers homothetic with scale parameters denoted \(\sigma_R(z)\) here (these are called \(S(z)\) in the proof of that accepted fact), satisfying
\[
 N_R+\frac78\le \sigma_R(z)\le N_R+1.
\]
Since fiber volume is proportional to \(\sigma_R(z)^{N_R}\),
\[
 r_R
 \ge
 \left(1-\frac1{8(N_R+1)}\right)^{N_R}
 \ge1-\frac{N_R}{8(N_R+1)}
 >\frac78.
\]
Use (12) with \(n=2\), \(d=N_R\), \(r_R>7/8\), and \(\gamma_{N_R}>e^{-1}\). Since both factors are positive,
\[
 2\gamma_{N_R}
 \left(1-\frac{1-\gamma_{N_R}}{r_R}\right)
 >
 \frac2e\left(1-\frac{1-1/e}{7/8}\right)
 =\frac2e\frac{8/e-1}{7}.
\]
This proves (16) and retains the factor \(2\gamma_d\) from (12).

## External sources

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, “The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, pp. 362-363, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: for nonempty bounded measurable \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), \(|(1-\lambda)A+\lambda B|_d^{1/d}\ge(1-\lambda)|A|_d^{1/d}+\lambda|B|_d^{1/d}\). Applicability: the fiber sets used above are nonempty compact sets, their Minkowski combination is compact and contained in the relevant convex-body fiber, and the inequality permits zero-volume inputs.

Grünbaum centroid halfspace inequality: Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body with centroid \(c(K)\), every closed halfspace containing \(c(K)\) has volume at least \(\gamma_d|K|_d\). Applicability: it is invoked only for positive-volume fibers \(C_z\), which are full-dimensional convex bodies; their centroids therefore certify \(M_z\ge\gamma_da_z\).
