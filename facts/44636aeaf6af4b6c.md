## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\), let \(q_0,\ldots,q_n\in\mathbb Z^n\) be affinely independent, and put \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{q_0,\ldots,q_n\}\) and \(F=\operatorname{conv}\{q_1,\ldots,q_n\}\). The symbols \(q_j\) are exactly the generic simplex vertices called \(p_j\) in the assignment. They avoid a collision with the accepted tetrahedron's census notation: in the canonical specialization from accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7, the exact map is \(q_0=p_0=(0,0,0)\), \(q_1=p_1=(1,0,0)\), \(q_2=p_2=(0,1,0)\), and \(q_3=p_{19}=(31,53,78)\), whereas its census point \(p_3=(1,1,1)\) is not a simplex vertex.

Let \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\), write its unique affine barycentric coordinates as \(a=\sum_{j=0}^n\alpha_jq_j\), \(\sum_{j=0}^n\alpha_j=1\), and define \(P'=\operatorname{conv}((P\times\{0\})\cup(F\times\{1\})\cup\{(a,2)\})\). Then the following statements hold.

For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the point \((q_i,1)\) is not a vertex of \(P'\) if and only if

\[
\alpha_j\le0\quad\text{for every }j\ne i,
\qquad \alpha_0\ge-1.
\]

At most one such upper point is not a vertex. All \(n+1\) bottom points \((q_j,0)\) and the apex \((a,2)\) are vertices. Thus \(P'\) has \(2n+1\) vertices if the displayed criterion holds for some \(i\), and \(2n+2\) vertices otherwise. Its dimension is \(n+1\). In particular, the sign condition alone is insufficient: \(a=3q_i-2q_0\) has \(\alpha_j\le0\) for all \(j\ne i\), but \((q_i,1)\) remains a vertex because \(\alpha_0=-2\).

Put \(A=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), \(B=P'\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), and

\[
D_1:=\operatorname{conv}\!\left(F\cup\frac{P+a}{2}\right)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad \frac{P+a}{2}:=\left\{\frac{u+a}{2}:u\in P\right\}.
\]

Then \(B=(A\times\{0\})\cup(D_1\times\{1\})\cup\{(a,2)\}\), so \(|B|=|A|+|D_1|+1\). Let \(v_B\) be the number of vertices of \(\operatorname{conv}(B)=P'\), using exactly the vertex-count notation of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7. Use its oriented reflection convention in ambient dimension \(n+1\): \(r_B(j)=|\{v\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}:j+v,j-v\in B\}|\), with zero counted once at every supported center. If \(\max_jr_B(j)\le3\), then

\[
|A|+|D_1|+1=|B|\le3\cdot2^{n+1}-v_B.
\]

Under this reflection hypothesis, equality holds if and only if every ambient parity class of \(B\) has size two or three and every nonvertex point of \(B\) has reflection count three.

In the canonical tetrahedron of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7, \(A=\{p_0,\ldots,p_{19}\}\) has twenty points and maximum reflection count three. The exact old-support alias used here is \(D:=A\), not \(D_1\). For \(F=\operatorname{conv}\{p_1,p_2,p_{19}\}\), every choice \(i\in\{1,2,19\}\), \(x\in A=D\), and \(a=2p_i-x\) gives exactly seven vertices of \(P'\). Consequently, if its lifted support \(B\subset\mathbb Z^4\) has maximum reflection count at most three, then \(|B|\le41\) and \(|D_1|\le20\). Saturation is exactly \(|B|=41\), \(|D_1|=20\); it requires nine parity classes of size three, seven of size two, and reflection count three at every nonvertex point. In particular, the first-step count \(2|A|+1=41\) can satisfy the vertex budget only in the seven-vertex case.

For comparison, any apex for this same canonical \(P,F\) that fails the criterion for all three facet vertices produces eight vertices. Under maximum reflection count at most three its budget is \(|B|\le40\), \(|D_1|\le19\). Equality in that budget requires eight parity classes of each size two and three, as well as reflection count three at every nonvertex point.

Finally, for any canonical reflected apex \(a=2p_i-x\) above, maximum reflection count at most three requires that \(p_i\) is not the midpoint of two distinct points of \(D_1\). Thus satisfying the seven-vertex criterion alone does not establish the reflection condition required for saturation.

## Proof

The only possible vertices of a convex hull of finitely many points are among those generating points. The height-zero face of \(P'\) is exactly \(P\times\{0\}\), so every \((q_j,0)\) is a vertex. The apex is the unique point of height two and hence is a vertex. The bottom simplex has dimension \(n\), and the apex lies outside its affine hull, so \(P'\) has dimension \(n+1\). This leaves only the \(n\) points \((q_i,1)\) to classify.

Fix \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). A point in a finite generating set is not a vertex precisely when it belongs to the convex hull of the other generating points. Suppose therefore that \((q_i,1)\) is a convex combination of the bottom points, the other upper points, and the apex. Write their nonnegative coefficients as \(b_j\) for \((q_j,0)\), \(c_j\) for \((q_j,1)\) with \(j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i\}\), and \(t\) for \((a,2)\). For coordinate comparisons set \(c_0=c_i=0\). The total-mass and height equations give \(\sum_jb_j=t\) and \(\sum_jc_j=1-2t\). If \(t=0\), all bottom coefficients vanish and \(q_i\) is a convex combination of the other facet vertices, contradicting affine independence. Hence \(t>0\).

Uniqueness of affine barycentric coordinates now gives \(\delta_{ij}=b_j+c_j+t\alpha_j\) for every \(j\). For \(j\ne i\), this forces \(\alpha_j\le0\). At \(j=0\), it gives \(b_0=-t\alpha_0\). Since \(b_0\le\sum_jb_j=t\), it also forces \(\alpha_0\ge-1\). This proves necessity, including the omitted-facet constraint.

Conversely, assume the displayed criterion. Put \(t=(1+\alpha_0+\alpha_i)^{-1}\). Since \(\alpha_0+\alpha_i=1-\sum_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\alpha_j\ge1\), this gives \(0<t\le1/2\). The following expression uses only other generating points:

\[
(q_i,1)
=t(a,2)-t\alpha_0(q_0,0)
+t(1+\alpha_0)(q_i,0)
+\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le n\\j\ne i}}(-t\alpha_j)(q_j,1).
\]

All its coefficients are nonnegative. Their sum is \(t(2-\sum_{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\alpha_j)=t(1+\alpha_0+\alpha_i)=1\). The height sum equals this same quantity, and the spatial affine coordinates cancel at every vertex except \(q_i\), whose coefficient is \(t(\alpha_i+1+\alpha_0)=1\). This proves the equality and sufficiency. Boundary cases \(\alpha_0=-1\), \(\alpha_0=0\), and vanishing other coordinates are covered by the nonnegative coefficients.

If the criterion holds for \(i\), then \(\alpha_i=1-\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_j\ge1\). If it also held for a distinct \(k\), it would require \(\alpha_i\le0\), a contradiction. Thus at most one upper point disappears, giving the stated vertex counts and the sign-only counterexample.

We next determine the integer-height layers. A convex representation of a point at height one can be grouped as \(\lambda(u,0)+\mu(f,1)+t(a,2)\), where \(u\in P\), \(f\in F\), and \(\lambda,\mu,t\ge0\), \(\lambda+\mu+t=1\), \(\mu+2t=1\). Thus \(\lambda=t=(1-\mu)/2\), and its spatial coordinate is \(\mu f+(1-\mu)(u+a)/2\). If one group has zero weight its representative can be chosen arbitrarily. This proves that the height-one section is contained in \(\operatorname{conv}(F\cup(P+a)/2)\). The reverse inclusion follows because \(F\times\{1\}\subseteq P'\) and every \(((u+a)/2,1)\) is the midpoint of \((u,0)\) and \((a,2)\). The height-zero section is \(P\), and the height-two section is the singleton \(a\). There are no other integer heights in \(P'\), proving the layer decomposition and cardinality formula.

The finite set \(B=P'\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\) is lattice-convex. All the displayed generating points of \(P'\) are lattice points in \(B\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(B)=P'\). Applying the vertex-deficit inequality of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7 in the ambient lattice dimension \(n+1\) gives \(|B|\le3\cdot2^{n+1}-v_B\) when \(\max_jr_B(j)\le3\).

For completeness, the equality audit follows by retaining the nonnegative terms in the elementary deficit argument. Let \(m_\varepsilon=|B\cap(\varepsilon+2\mathbb Z^{n+1})|\), for \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{n+1}\). The bijection between ordered same-parity endpoint pairs and integer-centered oriented reflected pairs gives \(\sum_\varepsilon m_\varepsilon^2=\sum_{j\in B}r_B(j)\). Every midpoint in this bijection belongs to \(B\) by lattice-convexity. At every vertex the reflection count is one. It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
2\bigl(3\cdot2^{n+1}-v_B-|B|\bigr)
&=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{n+1}}(m_\varepsilon-2)(m_\varepsilon-3)
+\sum_{\substack{j\in B\\j\text{ is not a vertex of }P'}}\bigl(3-r_B(j)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first sum is nonnegative because \(m_\varepsilon\) is an integer; each of its terms vanishes exactly at size two or three. The second sum is nonnegative under the reflection hypothesis and vanishes exactly when every nonvertex has reflection count three. This proves both directions of the equality characterization.

Now specialize to the accepted tetrahedron. For \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in A\subseteq P\), its exact barycentric coordinates with respect to \(q_0=p_0,q_1=p_1,q_2=p_2,q_3=p_{19}\) are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_0&=1-x_1-x_2+83x_3/78,&
\beta_1&=x_1-31x_3/78,\\
\beta_2&=x_2-53x_3/78,&
\beta_3&=x_3/78.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are precisely the barycentric formulas in accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7, with the explicit vertex-index map above. All four are nonnegative and sum to one, so \(0\le\beta_0\le1\). For \(i=1,2,19\), let respectively \(\ell=1,2,3\), so \(p_i=q_\ell\). The affine coordinates of \(a=2p_i-x=2q_\ell-x\) are exactly \(\alpha_j=2\delta_{j\ell}-\beta_j\). Therefore \(\alpha_j=-\beta_j\le0\) for \(j\ne\ell\), and \(\alpha_0=-\beta_0\ge-1\). The criterion holds at the distinguished upper point \((p_i,1)\), and at no other upper point. This proves the seven-vertex claim for every \(x\in A\), without a middle-layer lattice census. The same geometric reasoning works for every real \(x\in P\); integrality here follows from \(x,p_i\in\mathbb Z^3\).

Accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7 supplies \(|A|=20\). Hence in the seven-vertex case the dimension-four deficit gives \(|B|\le3\cdot16-7=41\). Since \(|B|=20+|D_1|+1\), saturation requires \(|D_1|=41-21=20\). There are sixteen ambient parity classes. If each has size two or three, the cardinality is thirty-two plus the number of size-three classes; cardinality forty-one therefore gives nine size-three and seven size-two classes. The eight-vertex case instead gives \(|B|\le48-8=40\), so equality requires \(|D_1|=40-21=19\) and eight classes of each size. In particular, eight vertices are incompatible with the first-step count \(2|A|+1=41\) under the reflection hypothesis.

Finally, fix a canonical reflected apex \(a=2p_i-x\). The point \((p_i,1)\) belongs to \(B\), and its two distinct reflected endpoints \((x,0)\) and \((a,2)\) belong to \(B\). Their two orientations, together with the zero displacement, already contribute three to \(r_B((p_i,1))\). If distinct \(y,z\in D_1\) had \(y+z=2p_i\), the two orientations of \((y,1),(z,1)\) would be additional displacements, distinct from the preceding ones by their zero height component. The count would then be at least five. This proves the necessary middle-layer obstruction and completes the first-lift audit.
