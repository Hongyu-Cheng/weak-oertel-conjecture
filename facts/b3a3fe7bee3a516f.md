## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $v_0,\ldots,v_d$ span $\mathbb R^d$ with sole linear dependence generated by $\sum_{i=0}^d v_i=0$. Let $A,K,B$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$ satisfying $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $B\subseteq\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\text{ for all }i\}$. Write $|\cdot|$ for $d$-volume, $a=|A|$, and $b=|B|$. Define
$$
R_i=A\cap\bigcap_{j\ne i}\{x:v_j\cdot x<-1\},\qquad
Z_i=K\cap\bigcap_{j\ne i}\{x:v_j\cdot x<0\},\qquad r_i=|R_i|.
$$
For every $i$ with $r_i>0$, $(R_i+B)/2\subseteq Z_i$ and $|Z_i|\ge2^{-d}(r_i^{1/d}+b^{1/d})^d$. The $Z_i$ are pairwise disjoint. For every subset $J\subseteq\{i:r_i>0\}$,
$$
\left|K\cap\bigcap_{\ell\notin J}\{x:v_\ell\cdot x<0\}\right|
\ge 2^{-d}\sum_{i\in J}(r_i^{1/d}+b^{1/d})^d.
$$
The intersection on the left is $K$ if $J$ is the full index set. With $U_j=|A\cap\{x:v_j\cdot x\ge-1\}|$, one also has $r_i\ge a-\sum_{j\ne i}U_j$.

In the planar case, place $A,K,B$ at the only positive-area integer fibers $-1,0,1$. If the three ambient caps $\{(z,x):v_i\cdot x\ge z\}$ have common mixed area $Q$ and zero captures on $B$, then for distinct $i,j$ with $r_i,r_j>0$ and remaining index $\ell$,
$$
|K|-Q+U_\ell\ge\frac{(\sqrt{r_i}+\sqrt b)^2+(\sqrt{r_j}+\sqrt b)^2}{4}.
$$

## Proof

For $x\in R_i$ and $y\in B$, each $j\ne i$ has $v_j\cdot(x+y)<-1+1=0$. Thus the actual midpoint is in $Z_i$.

For the volume comparison, replace the strict inequalities defining $R_i,Z_i$ by weak ones. The original and closed versions of each region differ only on finitely many hyperplanes, which have zero volume because the normals are nonzero. If $r_i>0$, the closed version of $R_i$ is compact, convex and full-dimensional, hence a convex body. Its Minkowski average with $B$ is compact and lies in the closed version of $Z_i$ by the corresponding weak inequalities and midpoint compatibility. The Brunn–Minkowski inequality in the form recorded in accepted fact `03f650d1c4f1b86d`, applied to these two convex bodies with weight $1/2$, gives the stated bound for $|Z_i|$.

If a point lay in both $Z_i$ and $Z_j$ for $i\ne j$, all its normal coordinates would be strictly negative, contradicting their zero sum. Each $Z_i$ for $i\in J$ satisfies the strict inequalities indexed outside $J$, so summing their disjoint volumes proves the bound for $J$. The union bound on the other $A$ caps gives $r_i\ge a-\sum_{j\ne i}U_j$.

For the planar assertion use $J=\{i,j\}$. The left side is the part of $K$ strictly missing its $\ell$-th trace. The closed $\ell$-th ambient cap has area $Q$ and receives only $U_\ell$ from $A$ and its middle trace from $K$, so the latter has area $Q-U_\ell$. This yields the formula.
