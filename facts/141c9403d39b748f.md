## Statement

Let \(s\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_s\ge2\) be integers. Partition \(k=s+L\) coordinates, where \(L=\sum_{\ell=1}^sn_\ell\), into centers \(h_1,\ldots,h_s\) and disjoint leaf groups \(G_\ell\) of sizes \(n_\ell\). Put \(N=k+1\), \(Q=2^k\), and \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\). Let \(E^{(\ell)}\) consist of every point with twos at \(h_\ell\) and exactly one leaf of \(G_\ell\), and arbitrary binary entries elsewhere. Define
$$
E=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^sE^{(\ell)},\qquad
D=\operatorname{conv}(B_k\cup E)\cap\mathbb Z^k.
$$
Use the original field
$$
\beta_{h_\ell}=\ell\log2,\qquad \beta_i=0\quad(i\in G_\ell),
\qquad
w_x=e^{-\beta\cdot x},\qquad Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
\qquad p_{(x,0)}=p_x=w_x/Z
$$
on \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\). The abbreviation \(p_x\) always refers to this original law.

The support is complete, contains \(B_k\), is ternary, and has at most two twos per point. Let
$$
D_1=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace,\qquad
D_2=D\setminus D_1,\qquad
\alpha=p(D_1),\qquad S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4.
$$
For \(j\ge0\), use exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
t_0&=2^{-10}/Q,\qquad t_j=t_02^{-j},\\
E_j&=\lbrace x\in D_2:t_j/2<p_x\le t_j\rbrace,\qquad
n_j=|E_j|,\qquad m_j=p(E_j),\\
\mathcal J&=\lbrace j\ge0:n_j\ge2Q\rbrace,\\
q_j&=|\lbrace x\bmod2:x\in E_j\rbrace|,\\
C_j&=\left\lbrace(x+y)/2:x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\right\rbrace,
\qquad \omega_j=t_j|C_j|.
\end{aligned}
$$
If
$$
\alpha<1/4,\qquad S_4Q^3<2^{-40},
\tag{1}
$$
then \(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\), its total stock is positive, and
$$
\mathcal A:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^2\frac{Q}{q_j}\ge2^{-15},
\qquad
0<\Omega:=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j\le2\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal A^3}{\Omega^2}\ge2^{-47}\alpha^{-2}.
\tag{2}
$$
Thus every sequence in this precise source class with \(\alpha\to0\) and \(S_4Q^3\to0\) has \(\mathcal A^3/\Omega^2\to+\infty\).

The residual regime is nonempty along a concrete compensated sequence. For every \(s\ge12\), take
$$
\nu=4^s,\qquad n_\ell=\nu4^\ell\quad(1\le\ell\le s).
\tag{3}
$$
All actual endpoint bands are then retained and dense, and
$$
\alpha\le\frac4{\nu s},\qquad
S_4Q^3\le\frac{162}{8^ss^4}<2^{-40},\qquad
\frac{\mathcal A^3}{\Omega^2}
\ge\frac{128^s}{4\cdot36^3s^4}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{4}
$$

## Proof

We first determine the complete support and all actual band data. No accepted predecessor is needed.

Let \(F^{(\ell)}\) consist of points whose unique two is at \(h_\ell\), whose leaves in \(G_\ell\) are binary with at least two ones, and whose remaining coordinates are arbitrary binary entries. Then
$$
D=B_k\mathbin{\dot\cup}\coprod_{\ell=1}^s
\bigl(E^{(\ell)}\mathbin{\dot\cup}F^{(\ell)}\bigr),
\qquad
D_1=B_k\mathbin{\dot\cup}\coprod_{\ell=1}^sF^{(\ell)},
\qquad D_2=E.
\tag{5}
$$
Indeed, every generating point is ternary and has exactly two twos or none. If a coordinate of an integral point in the generating convex hull is two, extremality in \([0,2]\) forces every positive-coefficient generator to have a two there. Three twos are therefore impossible. Two twos force one prescribed center-leaf pair, and all remaining integral coordinates are binary, so the point lies in its corresponding \(E^{(\ell)}\).

A single two at a leaf is impossible: its unique group's center would also be two in every representing generator. A single two at \(h_\ell\) restricts every generator to \(E^{(\ell)}\). On \(G_\ell\), all those generators have coordinate sum at least two, so a binary leaf vector must have at least two ones. Conversely, choose two of its ones and replace them by \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\), respectively, in two endpoints while keeping every other coordinate fixed. Their midpoint is the proposed point. This realizes exactly \(F^{(\ell)}\). Integral points without twos lie in \(B_k\). These observations prove (5), including disjointness.

The original generating set is contained in \(D\), and \(D\) lies in its convex hull. Hence their convex hulls coincide, proving \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). This also proves all stated support hypotheses.

Put
$$
\rho_\ell=2^{-\ell},\qquad
f(n)=1-(n+1)2^{-n},\qquad
P=\prod_{\ell=1}^s(1+\rho_\ell),\qquad
a_\ell=\frac{\rho_\ell^2}{1+\rho_\ell},
$$
and define
$$
\mathcal F=\sum_{\ell=1}^sa_\ell f(n_\ell),
\qquad
V=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^sa_\ell\left(f(n_\ell)+\frac{n_\ell}{2}\right).
$$
Counting the possible two-leaves and the binary leaf patterns in (5) gives the exact original formulas
$$
Z=2^LPV,\qquad
\alpha=\frac{1+\mathcal F}{V}.
\tag{6}
$$
For example, the \(E^{(\ell)}\) weight is
\(2^LP\,a_\ell n_\ell/2\), and the \(F^{(\ell)}\) weight is
\(2^LP\,a_\ell f(n_\ell)\). The Boolean weight is \(2^LP\).

Hereafter \(r\) is an integer energy index, not a midpoint cardinality. Define the finite coefficients
$$
u_{\ell,r}=[z^{\,r-2\ell}]
\prod_{\substack{1\le a\le s\\a\ne\ell}}(1+z^a),
$$
taking coefficients with negative exponent to be zero. Define
$$
A_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^sn_\ell u_{\ell,r},\qquad
B_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^s(1-2^{-n_\ell})u_{\ell,r},\qquad
K_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^sf(n_\ell)u_{\ell,r}.
\tag{7}
$$
Every endpoint in \(E^{(\ell)}\), with other center bits \(b_a\), has weight
$$
2^{-r},\qquad r=2\ell+\sum_{a\ne\ell}ab_a.
$$
Thus the complete list of nonempty endpoint energy levels is
$$
\mathcal R=\lbrace r:A_r>0\rbrace
\subseteq\left\lbrace2,\ldots,\frac{s(s+3)}2\right\rbrace.
$$
Their common probabilities and cardinalities are
$$
p_r=\frac{2^{-r}}{2^LPV},\qquad
|\lbrace x\in D_2:p_x=p_r\rbrace|=2^{L-1}A_r.
\tag{8}
$$

For exact band assignment, put
$$
b_*=\left\lfloor\log_2(t_0Z)\right\rfloor,\qquad
c=t_0Z\,2^{-b_*}\in[1,2).
$$
An energy \(r\in\mathcal R\) is retained precisely when \(j_r=b_*+r\ge0\). In that case it is the only energy in band \(j_r\), and \(t_{j_r}=cp_r\). This follows directly from
$$
\frac{t_{j_r}}{p_r}=t_0Z\,2^{r-j_r}=c\in[1,2).
$$
The lower band endpoint is open, so this also handles \(c=1\). Every nonempty band is obtained this way. It is dense precisely when
$$
A_r\ge2^{s+2}.
\tag{9}
$$

We next count actual endpoint parities, including their possible coincidences. A parity vector fixes all center bits \(b=(b_1,\ldots,b_s)\). An endpoint in star \(\ell\) has \(b_\ell=0\) and energy
$$
r=\sum_{a=1}^sab_a+2\ell.
$$
For fixed \(b,r\), at most one \(\ell\) satisfies this equation. Hence different stars never occupy the same parity in one exact energy level. For this unique possible star, a leaf parity occurs exactly when some coordinate of \(G_\ell\) is zero. There are
\(2^L(1-2^{-n_\ell})\) such leaf parities. The \(u_{\ell,r}\) choices of other center bits are distinct. Therefore the actual parity count of band \(j_r\) is exactly
$$
q_{j_r}=2^LB_r.
\tag{10}
$$

We also determine the full actual midpoint set. Two same-parity endpoints from distinct stars \(\ell\ne a\) have zero at each other's center: both center-two coordinates are even, while the other endpoint's coordinate is binary. Their other center bits agree. Equality of their energy levels would therefore require \(2\ell=2a\), which is impossible. Thus every distinct same-parity pair inside one band comes from a single star.

Inside star \(\ell\), distinct same-parity endpoints must use distinct two-leaves. Their midpoint has its center equal to two and at least two leaf ones; all its other coordinates are binary. Conversely, every point of \(F^{(\ell)}\) is obtained from two such endpoints by the replacements used in proving (5), with both endpoints having the midpoint's exact energy. It follows that
$$
C_{j_r}
=\coprod_{\ell=1}^s
\lbrace x\in F^{(\ell)}:w_x=2^{-r}\rbrace,
\qquad
|C_{j_r}|=2^LK_r.
\tag{11}
$$
These are actual sets of distinct midpoints, with no multiplicity substituted for cardinality. Combining (8)--(11) gives the complete band formulas
$$
n_{j_r}=2^{L-1}A_r,\qquad
m_{j_r}=\frac{2^{-r}A_r}{2PV},\qquad
q_{j_r}=2^LB_r,\qquad
\omega_{j_r}=\frac{c\,2^{-r}K_r}{PV}.
\tag{12}
$$
All coefficients in (7) are finite explicit generating formulas. Retention and density are given by \(j_r\ge0\) and (9).

In particular, for the dense retained energies,
$$
\mathcal A=\frac{2^{s-2}}{P^2V^2}
\sum_{\substack{r\in\mathcal R\\j_r\in\mathcal J}}
4^{-r}\frac{A_r^2}{B_r}.
\tag{13}
$$
Also \(f(n_\ell)>0\) for every \(n_\ell\ge2\), so every nonempty band has actual midpoints. Summing (11) over all energy levels gives
$$
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}2^{-r}K_r
=\sum_{\ell=1}^sf(n_\ell)\rho_\ell^2
\prod_{a\ne\ell}(1+\rho_a)=P\mathcal F.
$$
Consequently
$$
0<\Omega\le c\,\frac{\mathcal F}{V}\le2\alpha
\tag{14}
$$
once there is a dense band. Positivity will follow from the mass estimate below.

We now prove a uniform bound on \(\mathcal A\), allowing completely arbitrary sizes \(n_\ell\ge2\). Under the original normalized restriction to \(E^{(\ell)}\), the other center bits are independent with
$$
\Pr(b_a=1)=\frac{2^{-a}}{1+2^{-a}}\quad(a\ne\ell).
$$
The size of \(G_\ell\) and the choice of its two-leaf do not change this distribution. Thus
$$
\mathbb E\left[\sum_{a\ne\ell}ab_a\right]
\le\sum_{a=1}^\infty a2^{-a}=2.
$$
Markov's inequality shows that at least \(3/4\) of this component's original mass has \(r\le2\ell+8\le2s+8\). Summing the disjoint components, the original double-endpoint mass at energies \(r\le2s+8\) is at least \(3(1-\alpha)/4>9/16\).

The double endpoints of probability greater than \(t_0\) have original mass at most \(t_0^{-3}S_4<2^{-10}\), by (1). The total mass in all retained bands with \(n_j<2Q\) is at most
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}2Qt_j=4Qt_0=2^{-8}.
$$
It follows that the dense retained bands with energy \(r\le2s+8\) carry total original mass
$$
\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}m_{j_r}
>\frac9{16}-2^{-10}-2^{-8}>\frac12.
\tag{15}
$$
In particular this collection is nonempty.

It remains to bound the sum of its actual parity fractions. Write \(S_b=\sum_{a=1}^sab_a\), and let
$$
H_s(B)=|\lbrace b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^s:S_b\le B\rbrace|.
$$
Equation (10), the bound \(1-2^{-n_\ell}\le1\), and the definition of \(u_{\ell,r}\) imply
$$
\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}\frac{q_{j_r}}Q
\le s2^{-s}H_s(2s+8).
\tag{16}
$$
Indeed, for each \(\ell\), every center-bit vector counted on the left satisfies \(S_b\le2s+8\), after forgetting \(b_\ell=0\) and the additional nonnegative term \(2\ell\). This establishes the bound using the actual parity counts, rather than a list of independently chosen energy capacities.

Set \(z=2^{-1/4}\). Since \((7/6)^4<2\), we have \(z<6/7\), and hence \(\sum_{a\ge1}z^a<6\). Therefore
$$
\prod_{a=1}^s(1+z^a)
\le\exp\left(\sum_{a=1}^sz^a\right)
<e^6<3^6<2^{10}.
$$
For \(S_b\le2s+8\), the inequality \(1\le z^{-(2s+8)}z^{S_b}\) gives
$$
H_s(2s+8)
\le z^{-(2s+8)}\prod_{a=1}^s(1+z^a)
<2^{s/2+12}.
$$
The elementary integer bound \(s2^{-s/2}\le2\) follows by checking \(s=1,2,3\), then using \((s+1)/s\le4/3<\sqrt2\) for \(s\ge3\). Equation (16) consequently yields
$$
\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}\frac{q_{j_r}}Q
\le2^{13}.
\tag{17}
$$
Cauchy's inequality, applied to these nonempty actual bands, now gives
$$
\left(\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}m_{j_r}\right)^2
\le
\left(\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}
m_{j_r}^2\frac Q{q_{j_r}}\right)
\left(\sum_{\substack{j_r\in\mathcal J\\r\le2s+8}}\frac{q_{j_r}}Q\right).
$$
Equations (15) and (17) prove \(\mathcal A\ge2^{-15}\). Combining this with (14) proves
\(\mathcal A^3/\Omega^2\ge2^{-47}\alpha^{-2}\), establishing (2). Along any sequence with the stated two limits, condition (1) eventually holds, so the ratio diverges as claimed.

For completeness, the explicit compensation in (3) verifies that the preceding obstruction occurs with both residual quantities tending to zero. Here
$$
a_\ell n_\ell=\frac{\nu}{1+\rho_\ell},\qquad
\mathcal F\le\sum_{\ell\ge1}4^{-\ell}=\frac13,\qquad
\frac{\nu s}{3}\le V\le\nu s\quad(s\ge3).
\tag{18}
$$
The lower bound uses \(\rho_\ell\le1/2\), and the upper bound uses
\(V\le4/3+\nu s/2\le\nu s\). Also \(1\le P<3\): the first factor is \(3/2\), while the remaining factors have positive increments with sum less than \(1/2\), so their product is at most \(\sum_{r\ge0}(1/2)^r=2\). Equations (6) and (18) give \(\alpha\le4/(\nu s)\).

To compute \(S_4\) at its original normalization, put
$$
P_4=\prod_{\ell=1}^s(1+\rho_\ell^4),\qquad
V_4=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^s
\frac{\rho_\ell^8}{1+\rho_\ell^4}
\left(f(n_\ell)+\frac{n_\ell}{2}\right).
$$
The same disjoint census, with the field multiplied by four, gives exactly
$$
S_4Q^3
=8^s\frac{P_4}{P^4}\frac{V_4}{V^4}.
$$
Here \(P_4/P^4\le1\), and
$$
V_4\le1+\sum_{\ell\ge1}2^{-8\ell}
+\frac{\nu}{2}\sum_{\ell\ge1}2^{-6\ell}
=1+\frac1{255}+\frac{\nu}{126}\le2\nu.
$$
Using (18) and \(\nu=4^s\) therefore gives
$$
S_4Q^3\le\frac{162\,8^s}{\nu^3s^4}
=\frac{162}{8^ss^4}.
$$
For \(s\ge12\), this is less than \(2^{-40}\): \(162<2^8\) and \(12^4>2^{14}\) already give a bound below \(2^{-42}\). Thus (1) holds throughout this sequence.

The largest endpoint weight is \(1/4\), at energy \(r=2\), so
$$
Q\max_{D_2}p_x=\frac{2^s}{4PV}
\le\frac{3}{4s2^s}<2^{-10}\quad(s\ge12).
$$
Every endpoint is retained. Whenever \(A_r>0\), it is at least
\(\min_\ell n_\ell=4\nu\ge2^{s+2}\), so (9) makes every occupied band dense.

Finally, \(r=2\) has only \(\ell=1\) and all other center bits zero. Its exact data are
$$
A_2=4\nu,\qquad B_2=1-2^{-4\nu},\qquad K_2=f(4\nu),
$$
and hence
$$
m_{j_2}=\frac{\nu}{2PV}\ge\frac1{6s},
\qquad
\frac{q_{j_2}}Q=2^{-s}(1-2^{-4\nu}).
$$
Thus
$$
\mathcal A\ge\frac{2^s}{36s^2},\qquad
\Omega=c\frac{\mathcal F}{V}\le\frac2{\nu s},
$$
where equality in the stock formula follows because all endpoint bands are dense and retained. Consequently
$$
\frac{\mathcal A^3}{\Omega^2}
\ge\frac{8^s\nu^2}{4\cdot36^3s^4}
=\frac{128^s}{4\cdot36^3s^4}\longrightarrow+\infty.
$$
This proves (4) and pinpoints the failed compensation step: cancelling endpoint-energy factors in aggregate star weights does not preserve actual endpoint parity fractions. The first band still occupies only the fraction \(2^{-s}(1-2^{-4\nu})\) of all parities. The uniform argument (15)--(17) shows that arbitrary group-size adjustments within this same activity pattern cannot repair the failure.

The remaining general step is whether other center-activity spacings or repeated activities can distribute mass across many exact bands while giving those bands sufficiently large actual parity fractions and retaining \(\alpha\to0\) and \(S_4Q^3\to0\). Neither their impossibility nor a countersequence is proved here. No failure of an original depth or reflection cubic, or of the locked conjecture, is asserted.
