## Statement

Let a finite root-colored forest have roots $v_c$ of colors $c=1,\ldots,J$. For a nonroot $p$ of color $c$, suppose $G_p=2G_{F(p)}-G_{v_c}$ and $r_p=2r_{F(p)}-r_{v_c}$, where $F(p)$ is its parent and each path terminates at a root. Define $k_{v_c}=e_c$ and $k_p=2k_{F(p)}-e_c$. Then $k_p\in\mathbb Z^J$, $\sum_i k_{p,i}=1$, $k_p\bmod2=e_c$, $G_p=\sum_i k_{p,i}G_{v_i}$ and $r_p=\sum_i k_{p,i}r_{v_i}$. If $G$ retains distinct bases, the assignment is injective; affine independence of the roots is not required.

An integer vector $k$ of sum one admits the parent step $P(k)=(k+e_c)/2$, with $c$ its unique odd coordinate, at every stage until terminating at $e_j$ exactly when it can be written $k=e_j+\sum_{i\ne j}m_i(e_j-e_i)$ with nonnegative integers $m_i$ whose binary supports are pairwise disjoint. Its linear radius is $r_j+\sum_{i\ne j}m_i(r_j-r_i)$ for root radii $r_i$, and $r(k)\le r_c$ holds exactly when $r(P(k))\ge r(k)$.

## Proof

The recursion along each terminating path determines the vector unambiguously. Induction gives integrality, sum one, parity and both affine identities, with the root basis vectors as initial values. Equality of vectors implies equality of represented bases, giving injectivity when the bases are distinct.

A legal parent step preserves the set of positive coordinates: a positive even entry halves to a positive integer, a positive odd entry $k_c$ goes to $(k_c+1)/2\ge1$, and nonpositive entries stay nonpositive. Thus a path terminating at $e_j$ starts with exactly one positive coordinate, at $j$. The sum constraint then gives the stated unique representation with $m_i=-k_i$ for $i\ne j$. If no $m_i$ is odd, the positive coordinate is odd; if exactly one $m_i$ is odd, the positive coordinate is even and that negative coordinate is the unique odd one; and two or more odd magnitudes are forbidden. In either legal case the parent replaces each $m_i$ by $\lfloor m_i/2\rfloor$. Iteration is therefore legal exactly when each binary column contains at most one nonzero digit. It terminates when all digits have been discarded. The radius formula follows by substitution, and $r(P(k))=(r(k)+r_c)/2$ proves the last equivalence.
