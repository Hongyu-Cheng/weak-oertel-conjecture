## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$. Let $\mu$ sum $d$-volume on a finite family of positive-volume compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ at bases $z\in\mathbb R^n$. Suppose the nonvertical closed cap $H=\{L\ge0\}$ minimizes $\mu(H)$ among all closed halfspaces containing $y_e$ for each of two distinct points $y_0,y_1$ in their own fiber interiors. Assume $L(y_0)=L(y_1)=0$, and normalize the continuous component of the affine margin $L$ to norm one. The bases may coincide.

On the boundary sections $K_z\cap\{L(z,\cdot)=0\}$ use the summed $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measure $\sigma$, with counting measure for $d=1$. Label nonempty sections by $P$ if the plane cuts the fiber interior, by $E$ if the fiber lies in $\{L\le0\}$, and by $F$ if the fiber lies in $\{L\ge0\}$. For every ambient affine $h$ with $h(y_0)=0$ and $h(y_1)=1$, put $\delta=\operatorname{dist}(h,[0,1])$ and $A=\sigma(P)$. Then
$$
0<A,\qquad A+\int_F(1+\delta)\,d\sigma\le\int_E\delta\,d\sigma.
$$
If $h$ depends only on the base, each integral is a sum of its integrand at the base times the corresponding section measure.

## Proof

For $e=0,1$ set $g_e=h-h(y_e)$ and consider the cap with margin $L+t g_e$. It still passes through $y_e$ and has nonzero continuous normal for small $|t|$. Its mass has a local minimum at $0$, so its left and right derivatives satisfy $D_e^-\le0\le D_e^+$.

At a fixed fiber choose orthonormal continuous coordinates $(\xi,s)$ with $s=L(z,x)$, and write $g_e(z,x)=g_{e,0}(\xi)+bs$. The perturbed threshold is $s\ge -t g_{e,0}(\xi)/(1+tb)$ for small $|t|$. On a line with $\xi$ in the relative interior of a proper section, the fiber contains an interval on both sides of $s=0$. In fact a point in the relative interior of the proper section must lie in the fiber interior, since otherwise a supporting plane there would contain the section plane. On a positive-measure supporting section, the line through each relative-interior point contains an interval on the permitted side of $s=0$. To see this, choose an interior point of the fiber with coordinates $(\xi_*,s_*)$. For sufficiently small $\theta>0$, $(\xi-\theta\xi_*)/(1-\theta)$ still lies in the section face, so convex interpolation with that interior point gives $(\xi,\theta s_*)$ in the fiber. Relative boundaries of full-dimensional sections in their cutting planes are null. For $\xi$ outside the section, the compact line intersection has positive distance from $s=0$ if nonempty, so the pointwise derivative is zero. A zero-measure section contributes zero by the same almost-everywhere argument.

On a proper line the derivative of the captured length is $g_{e,0}$ from both sides. On an empty-side supporting line the right and left derivatives are $(g_{e,0})_+$ and $\min(g_{e,0},0)$, respectively. On a full-side supporting line the right and left derivatives are $\min(g_{e,0},0)$ and $(g_{e,0})_+$, respectively. Indeed the threshold shift clips an interval extending to only one side of zero in the supporting cases. For all these limits the length difference divided by $|t|$ is bounded by $2|g_{e,0}(\xi)|$ for sufficiently small $|t|$. This is bounded on the compact fiber projection, so dominated convergence integrates the line derivatives. For $d=1$ the tangential space is a single point with measure one and the same calculation is the interval clipping rule. A fiber with empty boundary section has zero derivative.

With $m=\int_P h\,d\sigma$, the two derivatives required are
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_0^-&=m+\int_E\min(h,0)\,d\sigma+\int_F h_+\,d\sigma,\\
D_1^+&=m-A+\int_E(h-1)_+\,d\sigma+\int_F\min(h-1,0)\,d\sigma .
\end{aligned}
$$
Pointwise, $(h-1)_+-\min(h,0)=\delta$ and $\min(h-1,0)-h_+=-1-\delta$. Thus $0\le D_1^+-D_0^-=-A+\int_E\delta\,d\sigma-\int_F(1+\delta)\,d\sigma$. The section through either interior endpoint is proper and has positive section measure, so $A>0$.
