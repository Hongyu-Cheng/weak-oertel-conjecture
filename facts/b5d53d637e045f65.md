## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $d\ge1$. Suppose $D=K_{-1}\subset K=K_0$ are positive-volume fibers, $K$ is a polytope, and $D$ meets every facet hyperplane of $K$. Then every nonempty real fiber $K_t$ with $t\ge0$ is contained in $K$.

Under the preceding contact hypotheses, suppose $K_1$ has positive volume, and fix a finite bundle of caps through $(0,y)$ with margins $a z+u\cdot(x-y)$, $u\ne0$, and widths $w=h\Gamma(u)>0$. Use the uniform outward ramp $\varphi_w(s)=\min(1,\max(0,1+s/w))$. Suppose each cap has zero ramp on $D$ and on $K_1$. For a cap write $b(u)=2u\cdot y-h_D(u)-2w$ and set $K_{\mathcal B}=K\cap\bigcap_{u\in\mathcal B}\{x:u\cdot x\le b(u)\}$, where each intersection term uses the width and normal of its cap. Then $K_1\subset K_{\mathcal B}$. If even one $b(u)<[h_K(u)+\min_{x\in K}u\cdot x]/2$, no nonempty fiber at an integer base $\ge2$ exists, so base $1$ is strictly exposed among the nonempty sampled bases. The complete miss on $K_1$ follows if the strip $-w<a+u\cdot(x-y)<0$ has zero volume there and the margin is strictly negative at one mark there. In particular this negativity holds if the cap attains the common deepest value at $(0,y)$, has positive own strip volume there, and $(1,y_1)$ is another deepest mark for the same uniform outward model with width fixed for each $u$.

## Proof

Write $h_A(u)=\max_{x\in A}u\cdot x$ for nonempty compact $A$. Actual convexity gives
$$
(1-\lambda)K_s+\lambda K_t\subset K_{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t}
$$
whenever $K_s,K_t$ are nonempty. Thus $z\mapsto h_{K_z}(v)$ is concave for each direction $v$ on the real base interval. For each facet inequality $v\cdot x\le \beta$ of $K$, the hypothesis gives $h_D(v)=h_K(v)=\beta$. For $t>0$ with $K_t\ne\varnothing$, express $0$ as the combination of $-1,t$ with weights $t/(1+t),1/(1+t)$. Concavity gives $h_{K_t}(v)\le\beta$. Intersecting the facet inequalities proves the first conclusion, including at $t=0$.

A continuous ramp with zero transition-strip volume on a full-dimensional convex fiber has constant value $0$ or $1$ there. Indeed, a point of the interior where the ramp lies strictly between $0$ and $1$ would give a relatively open strip of positive volume. The interior is connected, and continuity extends its constant value to the closure. A strictly negative margin at any point excludes the value $1$.

For the stated sufficient condition for strict negativity, put $c=u\cdot y$ and
$$
q(A,c')=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\int_{K_z}\varphi_w(Az+u\cdot x-c')\,dx
$$
at the fixed $u$ and $w$. The active cap has $q(a,c)$ equal to the common deepest value. An own-interior mark has positive own strip volume, since a ball about it intersects the open strip in positive volume. Positive own strip volume gives $\partial_{c'}q(a,c)<0$ at $c$, and $q(a,\cdot)$ is nonincreasing. If $c_1=a+u\cdot y_1>c$, the same normal through $(1,y_1)$ gives a strictly smaller mass, a contradiction. If $c_1=c$, the same cap attains the depth at both marks. For $p=0,1$ vary its base tilt through the corresponding mark, so the threshold is $A p+u\cdot y_p$ with $y_0=y$. At $A=a$ the derivative vanishes at each minimum. The derivatives are respectively $w^{-1}\sum_z(z-p)V_z$, where $V_z$ is the volume of the strip $-w<az+u\cdot x-c<0$ in $K_z$. Differentiation follows by dominated convergence from the Lipschitz ramp and the fact that its two break planes have zero $d$-volume on each fiber since $u\ne0$. The two derivatives differ by $w^{-1}\sum_z V_z>0$, an impossibility. Hence $c_1<c$.

For any cap completely missing the two fibers its largest margin on each is at most $-w$. Therefore
$$
-a+h_D(u)-u\cdot y\le-w,\qquad
 a+h_{K_1}(u)-u\cdot y\le-w.
$$
Adding gives $h_{K_1}(u)\le b(u)$. The containment in $K_{\mathcal B}$ follows. If $K_2$ were nonempty, concavity at the midpoint $1$ of $0,2$ would give
$$
h_{K_2}(u)\le 2h_{K_1}(u)-h_K(u)\le2b(u)-h_K(u)<\min_{x\in K}u\cdot x.
$$
But $K_2\subset K$ and is nonempty, which is a contradiction. Since the projection is an interval containing $1$, a nonempty fiber beyond $2$ would force $K_2$ to be nonempty. Thus $z\mapsto z$ exposes base $1$ among all nonempty sampled bases.
