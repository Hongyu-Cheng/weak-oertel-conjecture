## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and write \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(\pi:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n\) for the integer-coordinate projection. Let \(\operatorname{vol}_j\) denote \(j\)-dimensional Euclidean Lebesgue measure, also on a \(j\)-dimensional affine subspace. For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), define \(A_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in A\}\) and \(\mu(A):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(A_{\mathbf z})\). Put \(\gamma_j:=(j/(j+1))^j\) for positive integers \(j\).

Let \(K,P\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be nonempty compact convex sets, not necessarily full-dimensional, such that \(C:=K+P\) is a convex body. Put \(k:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(P)\), and suppose \(\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^n\subseteq\pi(P)\) for some \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and cube radius \(R>3/2\). Set \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\), \(W:=\mu(C)\), and \(h_S(\mathbf p):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mu(C\cap H)\), where the infimum is over all closed ambient halfspaces. This is the locked problem's depth because \(\mu(C\cap H)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\).

Then \(n\le k\le m\), \(W>0\), and there is one point \(\mathbf p\in S\), fixed independently of the halfspace, such that

\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge
\gamma_m\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k W.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus every closed halfspace containing this same point has at least the mass on the right of (1).

With \(q:=2^{-n/k}\), the exact radius condition making the profile factor in (1) at least \(2^{-n}\) is \(R\ge(3+q)/(2(1-q))\). In particular, either this condition or the simpler sufficient condition \(R\ge(7/2)k/n\) gives \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge\gamma_m2^{-n}W>e^{-1}2^{-n}W\). The sufficient condition \(R\ge(57/14)k/n\) gives the strong-coefficient consequence \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}W\).

For a genuine rank-\(n\) summand, the weak and strong sufficient radii are respectively \(7/2\) and \(57/14\), independent of \(d\). Taking \(P=C\) and \(K=\{\mathbf0\}\) gives \(k=m\): the projected-body profile has exponent \(m\), and sufficient radii for the weak and strong coefficients are respectively \((7/2)m/n\) and \((57/14)m/n\). No optimality of these sufficient conditions is asserted.

## Proof

Since \(\pi(P)\) contains an \(n\)-dimensional cube, the dimension of \(\pi(\operatorname{aff}(P))\) is \(n\). A linear projection cannot increase affine dimension, so \(n\le k\le m\).

Put \(\alpha:=3/(2R)\), \(\beta:=1/(2R)\), \(\lambda:=1-\alpha\), and \(E:=K+\lambda P\). Then \(0<\lambda<1\). For any \(\mathbf k_0\in K\), convexity gives \(\lambda C+(1-\lambda)\mathbf k_0\subseteq E\). Consequently \(E\) is a compact convex set containing a positive homothetic copy of the convex body \(C\), hence is itself a convex body. Convexity of \(P\) also gives \(E+\alpha P=K+(\lambda+\alpha)P=C\).

Apply accepted fact a4e3dbc02619d36f with its \(n,d,m,\pi,C,P,E,\mathbf a,R,\alpha,\beta,\Lambda,\mu,S,W,\gamma_m\) exactly as defined here. Its centroid point \(\mathbf p\) is the point used in this proof. Conclusion (3) of that fact supplies \(\mathbf p\in S\), independent of the halfspace, with

\[
h_S(\mathbf p)\ge\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(E)
\ge
\gamma_m\frac{\operatorname{vol}_m(E)}
{\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)}W.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular \(0<\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(E)\le h_S(\mathbf p)\le W\), proving \(W>0\). Finiteness of \(W\) follows from compactness of \(C\). The coefficient in (2) is \(\gamma_m\), not \(\gamma_k\), since \(E\) is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^m\).

We next compare the two Lebesgue volumes in (2) using only \(k\) directions. Choose \(\mathbf p_0\in P\), and put \(P_0:=P-\mathbf p_0\) and \(\mathcal L:=\operatorname{span}(P-P)\), so \(P_0\subseteq\mathcal L\) and \(\dim\mathcal L=k\). For every \(t>0\), the identity \(K+tP=(K+tP_0)+t\mathbf p_0\) gives \(\operatorname{vol}_m(K+tP)=\operatorname{vol}_m(K+tP_0)\). These translations are used only to evaluate Lebesgue volumes: neither the body nor the mixed lattice in the application of (2) is translated.

For \(\mathbf y\in\mathcal L^\perp\), define \(A_{\mathbf y}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathcal L:\mathbf y+\mathbf x\in K\}\). This is compact and convex, possibly empty or lower-dimensional. The section of \(K+tP_0\) above \(\mathbf y\) is \(A_{\mathbf y}+tP_0\). If \(A_{\mathbf y}\ne\varnothing\), choose any \(\mathbf v\in A_{\mathbf y}\). Convexity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
t(A_{\mathbf y}+P_0)+(1-t)\mathbf v
&\subseteq A_{\mathbf y}+tP_0 &&(0<t\le1),\\
A_{\mathbf y}+tP_0
&\subseteq t(A_{\mathbf y}+P_0)+(1-t)\mathbf v &&(t\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The first inclusion follows from \(tA_{\mathbf y}+(1-t)\mathbf v\subseteq A_{\mathbf y}\). For the second, if \(\mathbf x\in A_{\mathbf y}\) and \(\mathbf b\in P_0\), then \(\mathbf x+t\mathbf b=t(\mathbf x'+\mathbf b)+(1-t)\mathbf v\), where \(\mathbf x':=t^{-1}\mathbf x+(1-t^{-1})\mathbf v\in A_{\mathbf y}\).

Taking \(k\)-dimensional section volumes in (3) gives a lower bound by \(t^k\operatorname{vol}_k(A_{\mathbf y}+P_0)\) when \(t\le1\), and an upper bound by that quantity when \(t\ge1\). Empty sections give zero on both sides. No measurable choice of \(\mathbf v\) is required, since each choice establishes only a pointwise scalar inequality for section volumes. The sets being integrated are compact, so their section-volume functions are measurable. Fubini on \(\mathcal L\oplus\mathcal L^\perp\), followed by the translation identity above, therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(K+tP)
&\ge t^k\operatorname{vol}_m(C) &&(0<t\le1),\\
\operatorname{vol}_m(K+tP)
&\le t^k\operatorname{vol}_m(C) &&(t\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

When \(k=m\), the transverse space is \(\{\mathbf0\}\) with zero-dimensional measure one, so the same argument is simply the whole-space comparison in (3). Thus (4) includes lower-dimensional \(K\), arbitrarily tilted \(P\), and \(k=m\).

Applying (4) at \(t=\lambda\) and \(t=1+\beta\), and using \(C+\beta P=K+(1+\beta)P\), yields

\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_m(E)}
{\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)}
\ge\frac{\lambda^k\operatorname{vol}_m(C)}
{(1+\beta)^k\operatorname{vol}_m(C)}
=\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k.
\tag{5}
\]

The denominator is positive because \(C+\beta P\) contains a translate of \(C\). Combining (2) and (5) proves (1) with the point already chosen.

For the weak-coefficient consequences, \(q=2^{-n/k}\) lies in \((0,1)\). The inequality \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\ge q\) is equivalent to \(R\ge(3+q)/(2(1-q))\). It is exactly the condition that the positive profile base raised to the \(k\)-th power is at least \(2^{-n}\). Also \(\gamma_m=(1+1/m)^{-m}>e^{-1}\), since \(m\log(1+1/m)<1\); multiplication by \(2^{-n}W>0\) gives the asserted strict comparison.

Put \(\theta:=n/k\in(0,1]\). Accepted fact 0790372855abe672 proves in its proof, equation (29), the scalar inequality \((7-3\theta)/(7+\theta)\ge2^{-\theta}\) for every \(\theta\in[0,1]\), including equality at both endpoints. Here its scalar argument \(\theta\) is evaluated at \(n/k\), rather than at that fact's specialization \(n/m\); only this dimension-free scalar inequality is used from that predecessor. Since the derivative of \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\) in \(R\) is \(8/(2R+1)^2>0\), the condition \(R\ge7/(2\theta)\) gives

\[
\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^k
\ge\left(\frac{7-3\theta}{7+\theta}\right)^k
\ge2^{-\theta k}=2^{-n}.
\tag{6}
\]

This proves the simpler weak sufficient radius, including the endpoint \(k=n\).

For the strong coefficient, define \(\ell(t):=t\log(t/(t+1))\) for \(t>0\). Then \(\ell(j)=\log\gamma_j\), \(\ell'(t)=\log(t/(t+1))+1/(t+1)\), and \(\ell''(t)=1/(t(t+1)^2)>0\). Thus the increments \(\ell(t+1)-\ell(t)\) are increasing in \(t\). Since \(d\ge1\), every increment in the sum from \(d\) to \(d+n\) is at least \(\ell(2)-\ell(1)=\log(8/9)\). Hence

\[
\log\frac{\gamma_m}{\gamma_d}
=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
\bigl(\ell(d+i+1)-\ell(d+i)\bigr)
\ge n\log(8/9),
\qquad
\gamma_m\ge\gamma_d(8/9)^n.
\tag{7}
\]

If \(R\ge57/(14\theta)\), monotonicity of the profile base gives \((2R-3)/(2R+1)\ge(57-21\theta)/(57+7\theta)\). On \([0,1]\), define \(\chi(\theta):=\log(57-21\theta)-\log(57+7\theta)+\theta\log(16/9)\). Its endpoints satisfy \(\chi(0)=0\) and \(\chi(1)=\log(36/64)+\log(16/9)=0\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi'(\theta)
&=-\frac{21}{57-21\theta}
-\frac7{57+7\theta}+\log(16/9),\\
\chi''(\theta)
&=-\frac{441}{(57-21\theta)^2}
+\frac{49}{(57+7\theta)^2}<0
\qquad(0\le\theta\le1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Both denominators are positive, and \(21/(57-21\theta)>7/(57+7\theta)\), proving the strict sign in (8). Concavity and the zero endpoint values give \(\chi(\theta)\ge0\). Therefore \((57-21\theta)/(57+7\theta)\ge(9/16)^\theta\), so the \(k\)-th power of the profile base is at least \((9/16)^n\). Together with (1) and (7), this proves

\[
h_S(\mathbf p)
\ge\gamma_d(8/9)^n(9/16)^nW
=\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\tag{9}
\]

All radius consequences concern the single point from (2). If \(k=n\), then \(q=1/2\), so the exact weak radius is \(7/2\), and the strong sufficient radius is \(57/14\). If \(P=C\) and \(K=\{\mathbf0\}\), then \(k=m\) and the displayed profile and radius conditions give the stated general projected-body specialization. These substitutions preserve the original decomposition and projection hypotheses.
