## Statement

Take the compatible simplex fibers and marked potential class of the exposed-vertex hinge lemma. At one deepest source take a finite menu of regular nonvertical depth-attaining closed caps $H_l$ through that source, with nonzero continuous normals $u_l$ and positive total coareas
$$
S_l=\sum_{z\in P}\frac{\mathcal H^{d-1}(K_z\cap\{x:(z,x)\in\partial H_l\})}{\lVert u_l\rVert}.
$$
Let $g_l=S_l u_l$ and suppose $T=\{\theta\ge0:\sum_l\theta_l=1,\ \sum_l\theta_l g_l=0\}$ is nonempty. For each actual lifted simplex vertex $X$ exposed in some incident facet bundle put $h_{X,l}=\mathbf1_{\{X\in\operatorname{int}H_l\}}$. If the set of such vertices is nonempty, an achievable efficiency using an actual single positive circuit of at most $d+1$ menu caps is at least

$\max_X\ \max_{\theta\in T}\ \sum_l\theta_l h_{X,l}.$

For each $X$ the inner maximum is attained with support at most $d+1$ and equals $\inf_{v\in\mathbb R^d}\max_l(h_{X,l}-v\cdot g_l)$. In particular an inner value at least $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ certifies a paying circuit.

## Proof

Fix $X$. The nonempty feasible set $T$ is a compact polytope. Choose an extreme maximizer of the linear objective. The columns $(1,g_l)$ on its positive support are linearly independent: otherwise a nonzero linear dependence gives a perturbation with either sign keeping the equalities and, for small perturbations, nonnegativity, contradicting extremality. Thus the support has size at most $d+1$. The kernel of the columns $g_l$ on that support is one-dimensional: it contains the positive vector of chosen weights, and any independent kernel vector could be adjusted by that vector to have coordinate sum zero, contradicting independence of $(1,g_l)$. A nontrivial dependence on a proper subset is therefore impossible. Scaling the chosen weights by $S_l$ and renormalizing gives the positive geometric dependence of the $u_l$, with the same support. Conversely, dividing geometric weights by $S_l$ and renormalizing gives the corresponding response weights.

The equality-constrained linear program has dual objective $\inf t$ subject to $t+v\cdot g_l\ge h_{X,l}$ for all $l$ with $t$ and $v$ unrestricted. The primal has an optimum and the dual is feasible (take $v=0$ and $t=\max_l h_{X,l}$), so finite linear programming duality gives equality. Minimizing out $t$ gives the asserted formula.

The contact lemma identifies the exposed vertex with one lacking a simultaneous representation. For the chosen weights, apply the exposed-vertex hinge to the actual caps: its efficiency is at least the objective at this $X$. There are finitely many $X$, so the largest such bound can be achieved or exceeded by one potential with one circuit. No assertion follows from an empty exposed-vertex list.
