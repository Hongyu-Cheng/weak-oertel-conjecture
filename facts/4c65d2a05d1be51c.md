## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$. Write $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$, $t=1/(d+1)$, and $\Gamma=(dt)^d$. For $a>0$, $0\le\beta\le at$, and $h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ put $r_h=(1-ah)_+$, $q_h=t-\beta h$, and $p_h=t+\beta(h+1)$. Define $E(u,r)=d^d u^{d-1}(r-du)_+$ when $u,r>0$ and $E(u,0)=0$ for all real $u$. The finite deficit is
$$
D(a,\beta)=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0}r_h^d-\sum_{h\ge0}\bigl[E(q_h,r_h)+E(p_h,r_h)\bigr].
$$

For every $0<\epsilon\le1$, let $P_\epsilon=\{(a,\beta):\epsilon\le a\le1,\ 0\le\beta\le at\}$. The deficit is continuous there and its maximum is attained. Let $K_\epsilon$ consist of the points in $P_\epsilon$ of the form
$$
a=\frac{t(H+J+1)}{H(2J+1)},\qquad
\beta=\frac{t(H-J)}{dH(2J+1)},
\qquad H,J\in\mathbb Z,\quad H\ge1,\quad 0\le J\le H\le(2d+1)J+d.
$$
Then $K_\epsilon$ is finite, includes boundary coincidences, and
$$
\max_{(a,\beta)\in P_\epsilon}D(a,\beta)
=\max\left(\{0,\ \max_{0\le\beta\le\epsilon t}D(\epsilon,\beta)\}
      \cup\{D(a,\beta):(a,\beta)\in K_\epsilon\}\right).
$$
Throughout the domain $a>0$, $0\le\beta\le at$, the deficit is nonpositive on $\beta=0$, on $\beta=at$, and for $a\ge1$.

## Proof

**Activation and continuity.** Put $s_h=1-ah$. Both thresholds $u_h=q_h,p_h$ satisfy $u_h\ge t s_h$ on the parameter domain. In particular, if $s_h>0$, then $u_h\ge t r_h>0$ and the original envelope is defined. Also $s_h-du_h\le t s_h$, so positive slack implies both $s_h>0$ and $u_h>0$. On the whole domain we have the exact identity
$$
E(u_h,r_h)=d^d(u_h)_+^{d-1}(s_h-du_h)_+.
$$
Indeed it is the defining formula at positive radius and vanishes when $s_h\le0$. A zero threshold has $s_h\le0$, and contributes zero, even when it coincides with a slack or radius switch. On an active piece, $0<s_h-du_h\le u_h$ by $s_h\le(d+1)u_h$. The identity is continuous, and on $P_\epsilon$ all terms with $h>1/\epsilon$ vanish. Thus compactness gives attainment.

The left slack is $t-(a-d\beta)h$ and the right slack is $t-ah-d\beta(h+1)$. The central left slack is $t$, and the central right activation is $\beta=t/d$. For $h\ge1$ the activation lines are
$$
a=d\beta+t/h\quad\text{on the left},\qquad
a=t/h-d\beta(1+1/h)\quad\text{on the right}.
$$
At fixed $\beta$, each envelope is affine or zero on an interval between these lines. An active term has positive radius throughout the interior of its piece, and a term with nonpositive slack is identically zero there. The central right term is independent of $a$, including when $\beta=t/d$. Every mass term $\Gamma(s_h)_+^d$ is convex even through $s_h=0$. Hence $D$ is convex on each such interval and, by continuity, on its closure. Its maximum on that fixed-$\beta$ slice can be taken at a domain endpoint or on one of the displayed activation lines.

**Curvature along an activation line.** Use $\beta$ as the line parameter. For an active envelope with affine radius $s$ and affine threshold $u$, write $\ell=s-du$, $x=\ell/u\in(0,1]$, and use primes for derivatives in $\beta$. If $u'\ne0$, let $\eta=s'/u'$. Differentiating the product $d^d u^{d-1}\ell$ and the mass term gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(u,s)''}{d^d(d-1)u^{d-2}(u')^2}
 &= (d-2)x+2\ell'/u',\\
\frac{(\Gamma s^d-E(u,s))''}{d^d(d-1)u^{d-2}(u')^2}
 &= A\eta^2-2\eta+2d-(d-2)x,\qquad
 A=\frac{d(d+x)^{d-2}}{(d+1)^d}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0\le\eta\le d$, the second expression decreases in $\eta$ because $Ad\le d^2/(d+1)^2<1$. Its value at $\eta=d$ is $B(x)=\Gamma d(1+x/d)^{d-2}-(d-2)x$. For $d=2$, $B$ is constant. For $d\ge3$, $B'(x)=(d-2)[\Gamma(1+x/d)^{d-3}-1]<0$ on $[0,1]$, since $\Gamma(1+1/d)^{d-3}=(d/(d+1))^3<1$. Thus $B(x)\ge B(1)=(3d+2)/(d+1)^2>0$.

On the left line with index $H$, the left slack is $t(1-h/H)$, so the left terms are active precisely for $h<H$. For $1\le h<H$ one has $s_h'=-dh$ and $q_h'=-h$, giving $\eta=d$. At $h=0$, the mass and left envelope are constant. For a right term active on this line, $\ell=t(1-h/H)-d\beta(2h+1)$ and $p_h'=h+1$. Hence $\ell'/p_h'=-d(2h+1)/(h+1)\le-d$, and the first displayed curvature formula has right-hand side at most $(d-2)-2d<0$. The active right envelope is concave.

On the right line with index $J\ge1$, the right slack is $(1-h/J)(t-d\beta)$. Here $a>0$ gives $\beta<t/[d(J+1)]$, so the right terms are active precisely for $h<J$. For all such terms, $s_h'=dh(1+1/J)$ and $p_h'=h+1$, giving $0\le\eta=dh(1+1/J)/(h+1)\le d$. For a left term active on this line with $h\ge1$, its slack is $\ell=t(1-h/J)+d\beta h(2+1/J)$. As $q_h'=-h$, we have $\ell'/q_h'=-d(2+1/J)\le-d$. The first formula again proves concavity of the active opposite envelope. The central left envelope is constant.

Thus on either line, mass minus the same-side envelope is convex, with inactive same-side indices contributing just the convex mass term. Between consecutive opposite-side activations, each opposite-side term is concave or identically zero, so $D$ is convex. Continuity extends this convexity to each closed piece, including endpoints with zero thresholds or radii. The pieces on a left line include the split at the central right activation whenever it lies on the segment. On a right line the central left term causes no split. Radius and threshold switches in inactive terms require no additional cuts.

**Intersections.** Each activation line meets $P_\epsilon$ in a closed segment, a point, or the empty set. Its maximum can therefore be taken on the domain boundary or at an intersection with an opposite activation. The right equation is $Ja+d\beta(J+1)=t$, which also describes the central right activation when $J=0$. Solving it together with $a=d\beta+t/H$ yields the stated coordinates. At those coordinates, $\beta\ge0$ is equivalent to $J\le H$, and $\beta\le at$ is equivalent to $(d+1)(H-J)\le d(H+J+1)$, or $H\le(2d+1)J+d$. At a left activation $s_H=dq_H$ and $q_H\ge t s_H$ imply $s_H\ge0$, so $H\le1/a$. At a right activation with $J\ge1$, $s_J=dp_J>0$ implies $J<1/a$. Thus all relevant indices are bounded on $P_\epsilon$. A left knot can meet $s_H=q_H=0$ on $\beta=at$, and this point is already included in the closed pieces. The argument also applies to degenerate segments when $\epsilon=1$.

**Outer edges and equality.** On $\beta=at$, each positive-radius left threshold is $q_h=tr_h$, so $E(q_h,r_h)=\Gamma r_h^d$. For $a\ge1$, only $r_0=1$ is positive, and its left envelope is $\Gamma$. Both cases give $D\le0$. At $(a,\beta)=(1,t/d)$ the central right slack is zero, so $D=0$ there.

On $\beta=0$, the envelope sum is $2\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0}(1-(d+1)ah)_+$, and the mass divided by $\Gamma$ is $\sum_{h\ge0}(1-ah)_+^d$. Monotonicity bounds the latter above by $1+\int_0^\infty(1-ax)_+^d\,dx=1+1/[(d+1)a]$. For the convex compactly supported function $g(x)=(1-(d+1)ax)_+$ on $[0,\infty)$, the integral on each unit interval is at most the mean of its endpoint values. Summing gives $2\sum_{h\ge0}g(h)\ge g(0)+2\int_0^\infty g(x)\,dx=1+1/[(d+1)a]$. These comparisons include a radius or envelope support ending at an integer. This proves $D\le0$ on the zero-tilt edge.

The slice and line reductions give the upper bound by the stated maximum of zero, the edge $a=\epsilon$, and the knots. Every value on the right is attained in $P_\epsilon$, including zero at $(1,t/d)$, so the reverse inequality follows.
