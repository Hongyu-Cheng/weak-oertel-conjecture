## Statement

For \(n\geq 1\), let \(B=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|\leq 1\}\). For every \(\beta\geq 0\), let \(\mu\) be the probability measure on \(B\) with density proportional to \(\exp(\beta x_1)\). Thus the tilt \(\exp(na x_1)\) in the subgoal has \(\beta=na\); a negative tilt is covered by reflection. For every probability measure \(\sigma\) used below, define its normalized closed-halfspace depth by \(\tau_\sigma(x)=\inf_{H\ni x}\sigma(H)\). Then

\[
\int_B \tau_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x)\geq \frac{1}{64e\,2^n}.
\]

For positive integers \(m\), set \(D_m=mB\cap\mathbb Z^n\), \(P_m=\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\), and \(w_J=\exp(\beta J_1/m)\) for \(J\in D_m\). These are exact complete-lattice supports: \(D_m=P_m\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and their weights have the affine potential \(\ell_m(J)=-\beta J_1/m\). With the registered normalization

\[
W_m=\sum_{J\in D_m}w_J,\qquad
\tau_w(J)=\inf_{H\ni J}\sum_{I\in D_m\cap H}w_I,\qquad
A_m=\sum_{J\in D_m}w_J\tau_w(J),
\]

one has, for every fixed \(n\) and \(\beta\),

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{A_m}{W_m^2}
=\int_B\tau_\mu(x)\,d\mu(x).
\]

In particular, there is \(m_0(n,\beta)\) such that \(2^n A_m/W_m^2\geq 1/(128e)\) for all \(m\geq m_0(n,\beta)\). The continuous bound is uniform in both parameters. The lattice conclusion permits any joint choice of dimensions and tilts provided each mesh is at least this sufficiently fine threshold; it makes no assertion about a threshold independent of those parameters.

## Proof

First, two elementary comparisons will be used. If \(K\subset\mathbb R^n\) is a convex body and \(\nu_K\) is its uniform probability measure, then

\[
\int_K\tau_{\nu_K}(x)\,d\nu_K(x)\geq 2^{-n-1}.
\]

Indeed, \(K\cap(2x-K)\) is centrally symmetric about \(x\). Every closed halfspace containing \(x\) contains at least half its volume. Therefore \(\tau_{\nu_K}(x)\geq |K\cap(2x-K)|/(2|K|)\), where bars denote Lebesgue volume. Convexity implies that \(K\cap(2x-K)\) is empty when \(x\notin K\). Fubini's theorem and the substitution \(z=2x-y\) now give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_K |K\cap(2x-K)|\,dx
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
 \mathbf 1_K(y)\mathbf 1_K(2x-y)\,dy\,dx\\
&=2^{-n}|K|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Second, if probability measures satisfy \(\sigma\geq\rho\nu_K\) as measures, with \(\rho>0\), then \(\tau_\sigma(x)\geq\rho\tau_{\nu_K}(x)\) for every \(x\). Integrating this inequality and using the same domination once more shows

\[
\int\tau_\sigma\,d\sigma
\geq\rho^2\int\tau_{\nu_K}\,d\nu_K
\geq\rho^2 2^{-n-1}.
\]

We next change coordinates projectively. Put \(N=n+1\), and choose \(p\in[0,1)\) to satisfy \(\beta(1-p^2)=Np\). For \(\beta=0\), take \(p=0\). For \(\beta>0\), existence and uniqueness follow because \(Np/(1-p^2)\) increases continuously from zero to infinity on \([0,1)\). Write \(y=(y_1,y_\perp)\), with the perpendicular coordinate absent when \(n=1\), and define

\[
T_p(y)=\left(
\frac{y_1+p}{1+py_1},
\frac{\sqrt{1-p^2}\,y_\perp}{1+py_1}
\right).
\]

The denominator is strictly positive on \(B\). Direct substitution gives \(T_p^{-1}=T_{-p}\) and

\[
1-\|T_p(y)\|^2
=\frac{(1-p^2)(1-\|y\|^2)}{(1+py_1)^2},\qquad
\det DT_p(y)
=\frac{(1-p^2)^{N/2}}{(1+py_1)^N}.
\]

Thus \(T_p\) is a smooth bijection of \(B\) onto itself. Its inverse image of \(B\cap\{x:a\cdot x\geq t\}\) is

\[
B\cap\left\{y:
(a_1-tp)y_1+\sqrt{1-p^2}\,a_\perp\cdot y_\perp
\geq t-a_1p\right\}.
\]

This is the intersection with a closed halfspace, except that a zero coefficient vector can give the whole ball or the empty set. If the original halfspace contains \(T_p(y)\), the empty case is impossible; the whole-ball case has probability one. Applying the same observation to \(T_{-p}\) proves that, if \(\sigma\) denotes the distribution of \(T_p^{-1}(X)\) for \(X\) distributed according to \(\mu\), then \(\tau_\mu(T_p(y))=\tau_\sigma(y)\) for every \(y\in B\). Hence the average depth is unchanged by this transformation.

An unnormalized density of \(\sigma\) is

\[
g(y)=
\exp\left(\beta\frac{y_1+p}{1+py_1}\right)
\frac{(1-p^2)^{N/2}}{(1+py_1)^N}.
\]

Using \(\beta(1-p^2)=Np\), and putting \(u=py_1\) only in the following scalar calculation, we obtain

\[
\log\frac{g(y)}{g(0)}
=N\left(\frac{u}{1+u}-\log(1+u)\right).
\]

The function \(\log(1+u)-u/(1+u)\) is nonnegative for \(-1<u<\infty\): its derivative is \(u/(1+u)^2\), so its minimum is at zero. On \(|u|\leq 1/2\), integrating that derivative also gives

\[
0\leq\log(1+u)-\frac{u}{1+u}\leq 2u^2.
\]

It follows that \(g(y)\leq g(0)\) throughout \(B\). On the convex body \(K=B\cap\{|y_1|\leq h\}\), where \(h=1/(2\sqrt N)\), the same calculation gives \(g(y)\geq e^{-1/2}g(0)\). In particular, since \(\int_B g\leq g(0)|B|\),

\[
\sigma\geq e^{-1/2}\frac{|K|}{|B|}\,\nu_K.
\]

For completeness, \(|K|/|B|\) has a dimension-independent lower bound. Let \(Y\) be uniform on \(B\). Rotational symmetry and radial integration give \(\mathbb E Y_1^2=1/(n+2)\): indeed \(\mathbb E\|Y\|^2=n/(n+2)\). The density of \(Y_1\) is proportional to \((1-s^2)^{(n-1)/2}\) on \([-1,1]\), and is even and nonincreasing on \([0,1]\). For \(r=\sqrt{2/(n+2)}\), Markov's inequality gives \(\mathbb P(|Y_1|\leq r)\geq 1/2\). Since \(0<h\leq r\leq 1\), monotonicity of this density gives

\[
\frac{|K|}{|B|}
=\mathbb P(|Y_1|\leq h)
\geq\frac{h}{r}\mathbb P(|Y_1|\leq r)
\geq\frac{1}{4\sqrt 2}.
\]

Thus the domination comparison applies with \(\rho=e^{-1/2}/(4\sqrt 2)\). Projective invariance and the uniform-convex-body estimate yield

\[
\int_B\tau_\mu\,d\mu
=\int_B\tau_\sigma\,d\sigma
\geq\rho^2 2^{-n-1}
=\frac{1}{64e\,2^n}.
\]

All constants used here are independent of \(p\), and hence of \(n\) and \(\beta\). In particular, no separate large-tilt limit or boundedness assumption on \(\beta/n\) has been used.

We finish with the complete-lattice approximation, including uniform control of every halfspace. Since \(P_m\subseteq mB\) and \(D_m\subseteq P_m\), the equality \(P_m\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_m\) is immediate. Moreover \(P_m\) is a compact polytope and contains \(\pm e_i\), so it has nonempty interior.

Let \(f(x)=e^{\beta x_1}\), \(Z=\int_B f\), and let \(\mu_m\) put mass \(w_J/W_m\) at \(J/m\). The ordinary grid Riemann sums give \(m^{-n}W_m\to Z\). In fact the weighted sums over closed halfspaces converge uniformly over all such halfspaces. Here is a direct justification that also covers grid atoms on their boundary.

Let \(\varepsilon=\sqrt n/(2m)<1\). Tile space by half-open cubes of side \(1/m\), centered at the grid points \(J/m\). For a halfspace \(H=\{x:v\cdot x\geq t\}\), with \(\|v\|=1\), form the union of the cubes whose centers lie in \(B\cap H\). Its symmetric difference with \(B\cap H\), up to cube boundaries of volume zero, is contained in the union of

\[
\{x:1-\varepsilon\leq\|x\|\leq 1+\varepsilon\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{x:\|x\|\leq1+\varepsilon,\ |v\cdot x-t|\leq\varepsilon\}.
\]

If \(V_j\) denotes the volume of the unit ball in \(\mathbb R^j\), with \(V_0=1\), the sum of the volumes of these two sets is at most

\[
V_n\bigl((1+\varepsilon)^n-(1-\varepsilon)^n\bigr)
+2\varepsilon V_{n-1}(1+\varepsilon)^{n-1}.
\]

For fixed \(n\), this tends to zero uniformly in \(v\) and \(t\). The function \(f\) is bounded and uniformly continuous on the ball of radius two. Replacing its value at each cube center by its integral over that cube therefore has an error tending to zero uniformly in the chosen union of cubes. This proves

\[
\sup_H\left|
m^{-n}\sum_{J/m\in B\cap H}f(J/m)-\int_{B\cap H}f(x)\,dx
\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]

Normalizing by \(m^{-n}W_m\to Z>0\) yields \(\delta_m:=\sup_H|\mu_m(H)-\mu(H)|\to0\). Taking infima over the same halfspaces containing any specified center gives \(|\tau_{\mu_m}(x)-\tau_\mu(x)|\leq\delta_m\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^n\). Thus the estimate controls all lattice centers simultaneously, without selecting candidate cutting directions.

The function \(\tau_\mu\) is continuous. To verify this directly, a halfspace in the infimum can always be moved parallel to itself until its boundary contains the center. For any unit vector \(v\), changing the center from \(x\) to \(x'\) changes the corresponding threshold by at most \(\|x-x'\|\). The resulting slab inside \(B\) has volume at most \(V_{n-1}\|x-x'\|\), and \(\mu\) has a bounded density. Consequently these halfspace masses, and their infimum, have a common finite Lipschitz bound. Riemann sums can therefore also be applied to \(f\tau_\mu\), giving \(\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu_m\to\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu\). Combining this with the bound by \(\delta_m\) proves

\[
\int\tau_{\mu_m}\,d\mu_m\longrightarrow\int\tau_\mu\,d\mu.
\]

Finally, dilation by \(m\) preserves halfspaces and gives \(\tau_{\mu_m}(J/m)=\tau_w(J)/W_m\). Hence the integral on the left equals \(A_m/W_m^2\), as required. Taking its error smaller than \(1/(128e\,2^n)\) gives the stated \(m_0(n,\beta)\).
