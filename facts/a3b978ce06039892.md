## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and $\delta=1/8$. Form the lifted convex hull of $A=T$ at $-1$, $B=(-\delta,-\delta)+(7/4)T$ at $0$, and $C=2T$ at $1$. These are the exact three integer fibers, with areas $1/2$, $49/32$, and $2$, and total mixed area $M=129/32$. No fiber has half the total mass. The endpoints are unequal positive homothets, $B,C$ are incomparable by inclusion, and $D=(A+C)/2=(3/2)T\subset B$.

For $0<t\le1/3$, the actual mark $(0,t,t)$ lies strictly inside $D$ on the middle plane and has exact raw ambient depth
$$
P(t)=4t^2+2(t+\delta)^2.
$$
The square root is strictly convex along the segment. At $t=1/16,1/8,3/16$ the values are $11/128,24/128,43/128$, certifying strict failure of root concavity on $D$. At the separate actual mark $(0,1/2,1/2)$ the raw depth is at least $1>43/48=(4/9)M/2$.

## Proof

The middle body consists exactly of $x_1,x_2\ge-1/8$ and $x_1+x_2\le3/2$. It contains $D$ and the point $(-1/8,-1/8)$ outside $C$, while $(2,0)\in C\setminus B$. A convex combination of the lifted fibers at base zero uses equal endpoint weights. It is therefore a mixture of a point of $D$ and one of $B$. The extreme bases cannot mix, so the three sections are exact. Area scaling gives $1/2,49/32,2$, with $2<129/64=M/2$.

For $y=(t,t)$ in the stated interval we have $y\in\operatorname{int}D$. It suffices for a lower bound to check caps through $(0,y)$, since any containing cap contains the parallel through-point cap with the same orientation. Both endpoint bodies contain $L_t=3tT$, whose centroid is $y$ and whose area is $9t^2/2$. A through cap with nonzero continuous normal cuts the two copies at opposite scalar thresholds relative to this centroid. By 6a7d56e450b3126f for a uniform planar convex body, the combined capture is at least $2(4/9)|L_t|=4t^2$. A pure base cap contains a whole endpoint copy, which also meets the bound. The middle body contains $E_t=(-\delta,-\delta)+3(t+\delta)T$ because $3(t+\delta)\le11/8<7/4$. Its centroid is $y$. The full-volume choice in 6769574fc1bd9f8d, or the whole middle section for a pure base cap, gives a middle payment of at least $(4/9)|E_t|=2(t+\delta)^2$. Summing yields the raw lower bound for every cap. The closed ambient cap $x_1+x_2\le2t$ takes exactly two triangles of area $2t^2$ and one of area $2(t+\delta)^2$, so the lower bound is attained.

For $P(t)=6t^2+4\delta t+2\delta^2$, the numerator of the second derivative of its square root after multiplication by $4P(t)^{3/2}$ is $2PP''-(P')^2=32\delta^2>0$. Direct evaluation gives the three rational depths. The concavity inequality at the midpoint would give $2\sqrt{24}\ge\sqrt{11}+\sqrt{43}$, hence $21\ge\sqrt{473}$ by squaring and rearranging. This contradicts $441<473$.

For the separate mark $y=(1/2,1/2)$, the contained middle triangle $D$ has centroid $y$ and pays at least $(4/9)|D|=1/2$ by the same centroid fact. Reflection through $y$ sends $C$ to $\{x_1\le1,\ x_2\le1,\ x_1+x_2\ge0\}$, which contains $A$. With a nonzero continuous normal the paired traces, restricted to $A$ after reflection, are complementary closed halfspaces with overlap of zero area, so together they pay at least $|A|=1/2$. A pure base cap contains an entire endpoint and the middle fiber. Thus the stated mark has raw depth at least $1$. The explicit target is $(4/9)(129/32)/2=43/48$.
