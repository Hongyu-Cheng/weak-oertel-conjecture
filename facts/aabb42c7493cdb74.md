## Statement

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a nondegenerate triangle with centroid zero, vertices $p_i$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$. In $C=T\times[0,1]$ take the closed cap $H=\{t\ge0\}$ and write $T_i=\{\lambda_i\ge9/10\}\cap T$. The measure $\tau=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf 1_{T_i}\,dx$ on the bottom facet is a consistent effective coarea trace for a Clarke generalized gradient of the physical cap mass in its free affine coefficients. Its mass is $|T|/100$, its centroid is zero, and its maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth in the section plane is exactly $1/3<4/9$.

## Proof

Perturb the margin $t$ to $t+\delta(9/10-\lambda_i(x))$ with $\delta>0$ small. In a neighborhood of each such perturbed cap, write the variable margin as $s t+g(x)$ with $s>0$ and $g$ affine. Its threshold $-g(x)/s$ is uniformly below $1$ on $T$. The captured length on the vertical line at $x$ is $1-\max(0,-g(x)/s)$. At the displayed perturbation the threshold vanishes on a line of planar area zero. Differentiating the length integral therefore gives the cap-mass derivative in direction $\dot s\,t+\dot g(x)$ as the integral of $(\dot s\,t+\dot g(x))/s$ at $t=-g(x)/s$ over the subset where this threshold is positive. This is a full derivative by dominated convergence for varying difference quotients: the local lengths are uniformly Lipschitz in the coefficients and for almost every $x$ the clipping branch is locally fixed. In the limit $\delta\downarrow0$ the derivative converges to $\int_{T_i}\dot g(x)\,dx$. The boundary $\lambda_i=9/10$ is null, and $s=1$ on this sequence. Thus the moment covector of $\mathbf 1_{T_i}\,dx$ is a limit of ordinary cap gradients. The Clarke gradient is the convex hull of such limits, so their average is a single consistent trace as asserted. If coefficients are expressed with unit continuous normals, dividing each perturbed margin by the norm of its linear part gives the same cap and limits, by positive scaling and convergence of that norm to one.

Each $T_i=(9/10)p_i+(1/10)T$ has area $|T|/100$, and they are pairwise disjoint. Their centroids are $(9/10)p_i$ and sum to zero, giving the mass and centroid statements. The three closed planar caps $\{\lambda_i\ge1/3\}$ cover all of $\mathbb R^2$ because $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Each contains exactly the $i$th cluster from the trace support, as all other coordinates on a cluster are at most $1/10$. Every point therefore has depth at most one third. For a nonzero linear functional $\ell$ put $b=\max_i\ell(p_i)>0$, which is positive because the vertices span the plane and sum to zero. Then $\ell(p_j)\ge-2b$ for every $j$. For a maximizing vertex $p_i$, every point in $T_i$ has $\ell$ value at least $(9/10)b-(2/10)b>0$. Every closed halfspace containing zero therefore contains one whole cluster, proving equality at the centroid.
