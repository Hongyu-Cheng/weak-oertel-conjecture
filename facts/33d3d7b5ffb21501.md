## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $C$ be a compact convex family with fibers $C_z$ over a compact interval of $\mathbb R$. Suppose $C_z\subset y(z)+\ell(z)D$, with $D$ a convex $d$-body, $y$ affine, and $\ell(z)=a+bz>0$ on the base interval, where $b>0$. Put $D_z=(C_z-y(z))/\ell(z)$ and $A_\xi=\{z:\xi\in D_z\}$. Assume every nonempty $A_\xi$ contains a fixed interval with left integer endpoint $p_-$. Let
$$
f(\xi)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z\text{ in the base}}\ell(z)^d\mathbf1_{D_z}(\xi),
\qquad G_b(\xi)=\int_{A_\xi}(\ell(z)-b)_+^d\,dz .
$$
Then $G_b\le f+(\ell(p_-)-b)_+^d$ pointwise. The function $g=(G_b-(\ell(p_-)-b)_+^d)_+$ is a pointwise minorant of $f$ with convex extended inverse $(d+1)$-st root. The corresponding result for negative slope uses reflection of the base and the reflected left integer endpoint.

## Proof

The set $A_\xi$ is a compact interval when nonempty, by intersecting $C$ with the graph of the affine function $z\mapsto y(z)+\ell(z)\xi$. Write $k_0=\lceil\inf A_\xi\rceil$ and $k_1=\lfloor\sup A_\xi\rfloor$. The common integer point gives $k_0\le p_-\le k_1$. For each $k=k_0,\ldots,k_1$, the part of $[k,k+1]$ in $A_\xi$ has length at most one and on it $(\ell(z)-b)_+=(\ell(z-1))_+\le\ell(k)$, since $k\in A_\xi$ and $\ell(k)>0$. Hence all such cells together cost at most $f(\xi)$. The only remaining part is the interval from $\inf A_\xi$ to $k_0$, on which the weight is at most $(\ell(k_0)-b)_+^d\le(\ell(p_-)-b)_+^d$. The same inequality holds for empty $A_\xi$, where $G_b=0$.

For the density class, restrict the original family to $\ell\ge b$ and add $v\in[0,1-b/\ell(z)]^d$. The added coordinate bound is nonnegative and concave on this restriction, so the lifted set is compact convex. The accepted projective marginal theorem applies with affine coordinates $(x-y(z),\ell(z),v)$ and denominator $\ell(z)$. Its pointwise density is $G_b$: changing $x$ to $y(z)+\ell(z)\xi$ at fixed $z$ multiplies the volume element by $\ell(z)^d$, and integration in $v$ supplies $(1-b/\ell(z))^d$. If $\max\ell\le b$, $G_b$ is zero. If $\max\ell>b$ but $C$ has empty interior, use closed $\epsilon$-neighborhoods of $C$ with $\ell>0$. Each lift has interior, and its weighted section integrals decrease pointwise to $G_b$ by bounded convergence. The corresponding extended inverse roots increase to a convex function. The positive-part closure with $\beta=d+1$ proves the assertion for $g$. The pointwise bound and $f\ge0$ imply $g\le f$. Reflection $z\mapsto-z$ preserves Lebesgue measure and the set of sampled integer points and converts a negative slope to a positive one.
