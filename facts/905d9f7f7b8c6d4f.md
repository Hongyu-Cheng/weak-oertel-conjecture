## Statement

For $L\ge0$, $0\le\alpha\le L$, define $\beta_d(L,\alpha)=\inf_{F,\eta}D_{\mathbf1_Fdx+\eta}(g_{\mathbf1_Fdx+2\eta})$, where $F$ is a convex $d$-body of volume one, $0\le\eta\le L\mathbf1_Fdx$, $\eta(F)=\alpha$, $D$ is raw depth for closed containing halfspaces and $g$ is the ordinary centroid. Then $\beta_d(L,\alpha)\le\gamma_d(1+\alpha)$ and $\beta_d(0,0)=\gamma_d$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For $d=2$, $\alpha\in[0,7/8]$, and $L\ge\ell(\alpha)$ one has $\beta_2(L,\alpha)\le136/225$, where $\ell(\alpha)$ equals $16\alpha/9$ on $[0,9/16]$, $4\alpha-5/4$ on $[9/16,13/16]$, and $16\alpha-11$ on $[13/16,7/8]$.

For every $n\ge1$ there is an actual convex body $C_n$ with $2^{n-1}$ binary blocks of seven positive triangle fibers, of original mixed area $M_n=2^{n-1}11/4$, with the following property. At any positive integer anchor of own area $k$, consider all distinct unordered reflected pairs of positive endpoint fibers with areas $a_j,b_j$. Choose any $t_j\in[0,\min(a_j,b_j)]$ and set $\alpha=\sum_jt_j/k$ and $L=\sum_jt_j/((\sqrt{a_j}+\sqrt{b_j})/2)^2$. The supremum of $k\beta_2(L,\alpha)$ over every such allocation and anchor, including the option $\gamma_2 k$, is at most $136/225$. At a noncentral chain base the bound is $7/18$. The scalar guarantee is at least $1/150$ below $\gamma_2 M_n/2^n=11/18$.

## Proof

For the uniform upper test take a volume-one simplex $F$ and $\eta=\alpha\mathbf1_Fdx$. Its centroid is the indicated source, and a canonical simplex centroid cap has volume $\gamma_d$. The captured mass is $\gamma_d(1+\alpha)$. At zero density the reverse bound is the ordinary centroid case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d.

The uniform test proves the planar bound for $\alpha\le9/25$. For $\alpha\ge9/25$ take an area-one triangle of apex height one and opposite edge height zero. Its cap above height $x$ has area $(1-x)^2$. Thus for uniform area the variable $v=(1-x)^2$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. Write $s=4/9$, $r=4/25$, and $l=\ell(\alpha)$. Take $\eta$ with density $l$ on the rank bands $[0,r/l]$ and $[s,s+(\alpha-r)/l]$, and zero elsewhere. The bands are disjoint up to endpoints and contained in $[0,1]$: the inequalities $r\le ls$, $\alpha-r\le l(1-s)$ hold at $\alpha=9/25,9/16,13/16,7/8$ by substitution and are affine on each intervening interval. The measure has mass $\alpha$ and density at most $L$. The closed cap $x\ge1/3$ has total objective mass $s+r=136/225$.

Since the uniform mean height is $1/3$, the cap contains the centroid of $\mathbf1_Fdx+2\eta$ precisely when $\int\sqrt v\,d\eta\le2\alpha/3$. Integrating on the two rank bands and multiplying by $3\sqrt l/2$ gives the equivalent inequality $J\sqrt l\ge8/125+R^{3/2}$, where $J=\alpha+8l/27$ and $R=\alpha+4l/9-4/25$. Here $J,l,R>0$. Put $C=lJ^2-R^3-64/15625$ and $P=15625C^2-256R^3$. Strict positivity of $C,P$ implies $C>(16/125)R^{3/2}$ and hence the required squared inequality and the original one.

For each interval $[a,b]$ write $\alpha=a+(b-a)t$, $0\le t\le1$. The following rows give numerators of the Bernstein coefficients of $C$ in degree $3$ and $P$ in degree $6$, divided by the displayed common denominator. The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one on the closed unit interval. This proves the strict inequalities throughout the three closed cells and finishes the universal upper test.

For the actual body let $T$ be an area-one triangle of centroid zero, with vertex $p_*$, and let $C_1$ be the convex hull of $\{-4\}\times\{p_*\}$, $\{0\}\times T$, and $\{4\}\times\{p_*\}$, truncated to $|z|\le3$. For a convex combination at height $z$ the total weight of the two apices is at least $|z|/4$. The continuous copies $(1-t)T+tp_*$ are nested decreasing with $t$ since $p_*\in T$. Weight exactly $|z|/4$ is attained using only the apex with sign matching $z$. Therefore every actual fiber is $K_z=(1-|z|/4)T+(|z|/4)p_*$. The positive sampled bases are precisely $-3,\ldots,3$, and their areas are $V_z=(1-|z|/4)^2$, with sum $11/4$.

For any anchor, the equal-coefficient two-fiber coupling in 793210f8a4be3647 supplies each midpoint law on its actual own fiber with mass $t_j$ and density at most $t_j/((\sqrt{a_j}+\sqrt{b_j})/2)^2$. The pair endpoint marginals are the corresponding uniform fractions, and distinct reflected pairs use disjoint endpoints. For every halfspace, the allocated pair pays at least its midpoint cap, since a midpoint in the halfspace requires a captured endpoint. Adding the own mass and the summed midpoint law gives exactly the density class in the definition after homothetically normalizing the own area to one and dividing mass by $k$. The allocated centroid is the centroid of own volume plus twice that midpoint law. The summed density ceiling gives $\alpha\le L$. Thus a universal scalar lower guarantee using only these data at this centroid is bounded above by the infimum defining $\beta_2$.

At chain base zero, $k=1$ and the three pair areas are $9/16,1/4,1/16$, with equal endpoints in each pair. At fixed total mass $\alpha\le7/8$, the minimum cost $L$ fills the pairs in decreasing order of those areas. Moving mass from a smaller-area pair to available capacity in a larger one strictly lowers the cost. The piecewise minimum is exactly $\ell(\alpha)$, so the planar upper test bounds every central allocation. For a positive chain base $p=1,2,3$ the smaller area of each pair $\{p-h,p+h\}$ is $V_{p+h}$ and $k+\sum_jt_j\le\sum_{z=p}^3V_z\le7/8$. The uniform upper test gives at most $\gamma_2(7/8)=7/18$, and the negative bases follow by reflection. The zero allocation accounts for the own-centroid guarantee.

Finally let $C_n=[0,1]^{n-1}\times C_1$, interpreting the binary coordinates as integer coordinates. A reflected pair at a binary anchor must agree with it in every binary coordinate, since two binary entries averaging to zero or one both equal that value. Thus each anchor has the same allocation menu as its counterpart in $C_1$. All sampled fibers are positive and the original mass is $2^{n-1}11/4$. The comparison $11/18-136/225=1/150$ proves the claimed gap.
