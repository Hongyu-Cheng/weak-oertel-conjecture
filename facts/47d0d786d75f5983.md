## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $d\ge1$, with positive mixed $d$-volume, and suppose its mixed base centroid is an integer translated to zero. Let $K_z$ be its fiber and $\mu_z$ its $d$-volume. For any finite coupling $\pi$ of the labelled torque marginals $(i\mu_{-i})_{i\ge1}$ and $(j\mu_j)_{j\ge1}$, put $T(i,x,j,y)=(jx+iy)/(i+j)$ and $w(i,j)=1/i+1/j$. The measure $\nu=T_\#(w\pi)$ is supported in $K_0$ and satisfies
$$
\nu(K_0)=\sum_{z\ne0}\mu_z(K_z),\qquad
\int T'\,d\nu(T')=\sum_{z\ne0}\int x\,d\mu_z(x).
$$
Placing $\nu+\mu_0$ at base zero preserves the total mass and the full original mixed centroid.

## Proof

The base moment equation is $\sum_{i\ge1}i|K_{-i}|=\sum_{j\ge1}j|K_j|$, so the two torque marginals have the same finite mass and admit a coupling. If that mass is zero take the zero coupling. The segment between $(-i,x)$ and $(j,y)$ has its base-zero point at $(0,T)$, so convexity gives support in $K_0$. Integrating $w=1/i+1/j$ against the two labelled marginals gives the noncentral mass. The pointwise identity $wT=x/i+y/j$ gives the noncentral continuous first moment by the same marginal integration. Adding $\mu_0$ gives the asserted centroid and mass.
