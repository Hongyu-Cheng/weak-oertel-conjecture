## Statement

Let \(r\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(f\) be a measurable, globally log-concave, full-dimensional probability density on \(\mathbb R^r\), with \(\int_{\mathbb R^r}f(x)\,dx=1\). Lebesgue volume in \(\mathbb R^r\) is denoted by \(|\cdot|\). For \(s>0\), define \(K_s=\{x:f(x)\ge s\}\), \(V_s=|K_s|\), and
\[
Q_f=4\int_0^\infty s^3V_s^4\,ds.
\]
Then \(Q_f\ge1/[32(r+1)^3]\), and therefore also \(Q_f\ge1/[128(r+1)^3]\).

More precisely, \(M=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}f\) is positive and finite and equals the pointwise supremum of the given globally log-concave version. Define \(\phi(x)=-\log(f(x)/M)\) where \(f(x)>0\), and \(\phi(x)=+\infty\) where \(f(x)=0\). The exact level normalization is \(\{\phi\le t\}=K_{Me^{-t}}\) and \(V(t)=|\{\phi\le t\}|=V_{Me^{-t}}\) for \(t\ge0\). The auxiliary level law \(\psi(t)=Me^{-t}V(t)\) satisfies
\[
\int_0^\infty\psi(t)\,dt=1,
\qquad
\int_0^\infty t\psi(t)\,dt\le r+1,
\qquad
Q_f=4\int_0^\infty\psi(t)^4\,dt.
\]
The original density \(f\) is not rescaled, tilted, or replaced by \(\psi\). The sets \(K_s\) are auxiliary continuous level sets, not the locked convex body \(C\) or mixed set \(S\). No new meaning is assigned to the project's \(\tau_p\), \(\mathcal M_q\), or capacity \(\mathsf C\).

## Proof

First we justify the mode normalization directly for the given version of \(f\), without replacing it by an upper-semicontinuous representative. Probability normalization implies that \(\{f>0\}\) has positive Lebesgue measure and that any infinite-valued set has measure zero. Consequently, for some \(a>0\), the set \(\{a\le f<\infty\}\) has positive measure. Choose \(r+1\) affinely independent points in this set. Such a choice is possible successively because each proper affine subspace has measure zero. Their convex hull \(\Delta\) is a simplex of positive finite volume. Iterating the log-concavity inequality shows that \(f\ge a\) everywhere on \(\Delta\).

For any \(x\) with \(f(x)>0\), log-concavity gives \(f((x+z)/2)\ge\sqrt{a f(x)}\) for all \(z\in\Delta\). Integrating over the translated and dilated simplex \((x+\Delta)/2\) yields
\[
1\ge 2^{-r}|\Delta|\sqrt{a f(x)}.
\]
In particular, \(f(x)\le4^r/(a|\Delta|^2)\). If densities are allowed to have the value \(+\infty\), the same midpoint inequality would make \(f\) infinite on this positive-volume simplex whenever \(f(x)=+\infty\), contradicting its finite integral. Thus all values are finite, the displayed bound holds everywhere, and \(0<M<\infty\).

We also have \(f(x)\le M\) at every point, including points in a null set. Indeed, if \(f(x)>M\), choose \(0<\theta<1\) sufficiently small that \(f(x)^{1-\theta}a^\theta>M\). Log-concavity would give \(f>M\) throughout \((1-\theta)x+\theta\Delta\), whose volume is \(\theta^r|\Delta|>0\). This contradicts the definition of the essential supremum. Hence the essential and pointwise suprema agree. The function \(\phi\) is therefore nonnegative and convex as an extended-valued function, directly by taking logarithms in the global log-concavity inequality. No lower semicontinuity or attained minimum is asserted.

For \(s>0\), normalization gives \(sV_s\le1\); hence every such level volume is finite. The stated level aliases follow pointwise from the definition of \(\phi\). Layer cake, Tonelli's theorem, and the substitution \(s=Me^{-t}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
1
&=\int_{\mathbb R^r}\int_0^M\mathbf 1_{K_s}(x)\,ds\,dx
=\int_0^M V_s\,ds
=\int_0^\infty Me^{-t}V(t)\,dt,\\
Q_f
&=4M^4\int_0^\infty e^{-4t}V(t)^4\,dt
=4\int_0^\infty\psi(t)^4\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(K_s\) is empty for \(s>M\); the endpoints of the substitutions do not affect the integrals. Also \(0\le\psi(t)\le1\), so its fourth-power integral is finite.

We next prove the volume scaling needed for the first-moment estimate using approximate modes. For each \(\delta>0\), equality of the suprema supplies a point \(x_\delta\) with \(f(x_\delta)>Me^{-\delta}\), and hence \(\phi(x_\delta)<\delta\). Fix \(0<t<u\) and choose \(0<\delta<t\). With \(\theta=(t-\delta)/(u-\delta)\), convexity implies
\[
(1-\theta)x_\delta+\theta\{\phi\le u\}\subseteq\{\phi\le t\},
\qquad
V(t)\ge\left(\frac{t-\delta}{u-\delta}\right)^rV(u).
\]
Indeed, the value of \(\phi\) at each point on the left is at most \((1-\theta)\delta+\theta u=t\). Volume under this homothety is multiplied by \(\theta^r\). Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) in the numerical inequality gives \(V(t)\ge(t/u)^rV(u)\). Thus \(V(t)/t^r\) is nonincreasing on \((0,\infty)\), even if the supremum of \(f\) is not attained.

An integration argument now bounds the first moment without differentiating \(V\), assuming a mode exists, or introducing another root density. For any \(\varepsilon>0\), the scaling inequality with \(t=u/(1+\varepsilon)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty e^{-\varepsilon t}\psi(t)\,dt
&=M\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+\varepsilon)t}V(t)\,dt\\
&=\frac{M}{1+\varepsilon}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}V\left(\frac{u}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\,du\\
&\ge(1+\varepsilon)^{-(r+1)}M\int_0^\infty e^{-u}V(u)\,du
=(1+\varepsilon)^{-(r+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with \(\int\psi=1\), this proves
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{1-e^{-\varepsilon t}}{\varepsilon}\psi(t)\,dt
\le\frac{1-(1+\varepsilon)^{-(r+1)}}{\varepsilon}
=\frac1\varepsilon\int_0^\varepsilon(r+1)(1+v)^{-(r+2)}\,dv
\le r+1.
\]
The identity \((1-e^{-\varepsilon t})/\varepsilon=\int_0^t e^{-\varepsilon v}\,dv\) shows that these nonnegative integrands increase pointwise to \(t\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Monotone convergence therefore yields \(\int_0^\infty t\psi(t)\,dt\le r+1\). This argument requires neither differentiability of level volumes nor bounded support.

Finally, the first-moment estimate implies
\[
\int_{2(r+1)}^\infty\psi(t)\,dt
\le\frac1{2(r+1)}\int_0^\infty t\psi(t)\,dt
\le\frac12.
\]
Thus \(\int_0^{2(r+1)}\psi(t)\,dt\ge1/2\). Hölder's inequality on this interval gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{16}
&\le\left(\int_0^{2(r+1)}\psi(t)\,dt\right)^4
\le[2(r+1)]^3\int_0^{2(r+1)}\psi(t)^4\,dt,\\
Q_f
&=4\int_0^\infty\psi(t)^4\,dt
\ge\frac{4}{16[2(r+1)]^3}
=\frac1{32(r+1)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All level sets and all pointwise inequalities above concern the original globally log-concave version. No changes on null sets, upper-semicontinuous regularization, or attained-mode hypothesis were used.
