## Statement

Fix \(c\in(0,1/e)\). For positive integers \(m,\delta\), let \(\mathrm{WO}_c(m,\delta)\) be the following assertion. For every convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+\delta}\), put
\[
\Lambda_{m,\delta}:=\mathbb Z^m\times\mathbb R^\delta,
\qquad
S_K:=K\cap\Lambda_{m,\delta},
\]
and define mixed-integer measure by summing \(\delta\)-dimensional volume over the lattice fibers. If \(S_K\neq\varnothing\), then there is \(y_K\in S_K\) such that every closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+\delta}\) containing \(y_K\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_\delta(S_K\cap G)
\geq c\,2^{-m}\mathcal H_\delta(S_K).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and suppose that \(\mathrm{WO}_c(m,\delta)\) holds for every \(1\leq m<n\) and every \(\delta\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body and
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing.
\]
Let \(1\leq r\leq n\), let \(L\leq\mathbb Z^n\) be a primitive sublattice of rank \(n-r\), meaning
\[
L=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\]
let \(a\in\mathbb Z^n\), and set
\[
A:=a+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(L).
\]
Thus \(A\) is a proper primitive affine lattice subspace of codimension \(r\) and \(A\cap\mathbb Z^n=a+L\). Suppose its slice
\[
S_A:=C\cap\bigl((A\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d\bigr)
\]
satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S_A)\geq 2^{-r}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{2}
\]
Then there exists \(y^\star\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(y^\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq c\,2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

Write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
v_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
If \(\mathcal H_d(S)=0\), any \(y^\star\in S\) satisfies (3). Hence assume \(\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). By (2), \(\mathcal H_d(S_A)>0\).

The compactness of \(C\) implies that only finitely many \(C_z\) are nonempty. Define the nonempty finite set of positive-mass base points in the slice by
\[
D_+:=\{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n:v_z>0\},
\]
and let
\[
B:=\operatorname{aff}(D_+),
\qquad
q:=\dim B.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(B\subseteq A\),
\[
0\leq q\leq n-r,
\qquad
q+r\leq n,
\qquad
q<n.
\tag{5}
\]
Every \(z\in(A\cap\mathbb Z^n)\setminus B\) has \(v_z=0\), since every positive-mass point belongs to \(D_+\subseteq B\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal H_d(S_A)
=\sum_{z\in D_+}v_z
=\sum_{z\in B\cap\mathbb Z^n}v_z.
\tag{6}
\]

We first prove that
\[
K:=C\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)
\tag{7}
\]
is a convex body in the relative affine space \(B\times\mathbb R^d\). Choose affinely independent \(z_0,\ldots,z_q\in D_+\). Each \(C_{z_i}\) is a compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^d\) of positive \(d\)-volume, so it has nonempty interior. Choose \(x_i\in\operatorname{int}(C_{z_i})\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
x_0+\varepsilon e_j\in C_{z_0}
\qquad(1\leq j\leq d),
\]
where \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\) is the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^d\). The \(q+d\) vectors obtained by subtracting \((z_0,x_0)\) from
\[
(z_0,x_0+\varepsilon e_j)\quad(1\leq j\leq d),
\qquad
(z_i,x_i)\quad(1\leq i\leq q)
\]
are linearly independent: their horizontal components first force all coefficients of the last \(q\) vectors to vanish, and their vertical components then force the remaining \(d\) coefficients to vanish. All these points belong to \(K\). Hence \(K\) contains a \((q+d)\)-dimensional simplex in \(B\times\mathbb R^d\). Since \(K\) is also compact and convex, it is a convex body in that relative affine space.

The group
\[
\Gamma:=(B-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n
\tag{8}
\]
is a full lattice in the \(q\)-dimensional vector space \(B-z_0\). Indeed, it is discrete as a subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\), and it contains the \(q\) linearly independent vectors \(z_i-z_0\), \(1\leq i\leq q\). Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\) of \(\Gamma\), with the empty basis when \(q=0\), and define
\[
Uu:=\sum_{i=1}^q u_i g_i,
\qquad
T(u,x):=(z_0+Uu,x).
\tag{9}
\]
Then \(T\) is an affine bijection from \(\mathbb R^q\times\mathbb R^d\) onto \(B\times\mathbb R^d\), and
\[
T(\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb R^d)
=(B\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{10}
\]
Set
\[
K':=T^{-1}(K),
\qquad
S':=K'\cap(\mathbb Z^q\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{11}
\]
The relative full dimensionality proved above shows that \(K'\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{q+d}\). Moreover, for every \(u\in\mathbb Z^q\), the map \(T\) sends the fiber \(\{u\}\times\mathbb R^d\) to \(\{z_0+Uu\}\times\mathbb R^d\) by the identity on the continuous coordinate. It therefore preserves \(d\)-dimensional fiber volume exactly. By (6),
\[
\mathcal H_d(S')=\mathcal H_d(S_A).
\tag{12}
\]

Suppose first that \(q\geq1\). By (5), \(q<n\), so the assumed assertion \(\mathrm{WO}_c(q,d)\) applied to \(K'\) gives \(y'\in S'\) such that every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^{q+d}\) containing \(y'\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S'\cap J)
\geq c\,2^{-q}\mathcal H_d(S').
\tag{13}
\]

Now suppose that \(q=0\). Then \(K'=C_{z_0}\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Let \(y'\) be its centroid. Grünbaum's inequality says that every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(y'\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(K'\cap J)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\mathcal H_d(K').
\tag{14}
\]
Since
\[
d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1,
\]
we have \((d/(d+1))^d>e^{-1}>c\). Also \(S'=K'\) when \(q=0\). Thus (13) again holds, now with \(2^{-q}=1\). In either case we have a point \(y'\in S'\) satisfying (13).

Put \(y^\star:=T(y')\). By (10) and (11), \(y^\star\in S\). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any closed halfspace containing \(y^\star\), and pull its restriction to the relative slice back by setting
\[
J:=T^{-1}\bigl(H\cap(B\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
The restriction to \(B\times\mathbb R^d\) of an affine functional defining \(H\) is either nonconstant or constant. Accordingly, \(J\) is either a closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{q+d}\) or all of \(\mathbb R^{q+d}\); it cannot be empty because it contains \(y'\). In the first case (13) applies. In the second case the same lower bound is immediate from \(c\,2^{-q}<1\). Hence
\[
\mathcal H_d(S'\cap J)
\geq c\,2^{-q}\mathcal H_d(S').
\tag{16}
\]

The fiberwise identity in (9) gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S'\cap J)
=
\mathcal H_d\!\left(
C\cap H\cap\bigl((B\cap\mathbb Z^n)\times\mathbb R^d\bigr)
\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Every fiber indexed by \((A\cap\mathbb Z^n)\setminus B\) has zero \(d\)-volume, and intersecting it with \(H\) still has zero \(d\)-volume. Thus the right side of (17) equals \(\mathcal H_d(S_A\cap H)\), which is at most \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\). Combining (2), (5), (12), and (16) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&\geq c\,2^{-q}\mathcal H_d(S_A)\\
&\geq c\,2^{-(q+r)}\mathcal H_d(S)\\
&\geq c\,2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (3) with exactly the same constant \(c\).

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf. Exact form used: if \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(g(D)\), then every closed halfspace \(J\) containing \(g(D)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{vol}_d(D\cap J)\geq(d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(D)\). Applicability: in the \(q=0\) case, \(K'=C_{z_0}\) is proved above to be a \(d\)-dimensional convex body, \(y'\) is its centroid, and each proper pullback \(J\) is a closed halfspace containing \(y'\). The ratio statement is unchanged under the normalization of \(\mathcal H_d\).
