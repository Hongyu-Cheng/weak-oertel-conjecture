## Statement

Fix an integer \(N\geq3\). Let \(K:=B_2^N\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), let \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_N\) be the standard basis, and put
\[
 y_i:=(2i,2i^2)\in\mathbb Z^2,
 \qquad c_i:=4\mathbf e_i
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq N).
\tag{1}
\]
Define the full-dimensional convex body
\[
 C_N:=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{i=1}^N
 \bigl(\{y_i\}\times(K+c_i)\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^N.
\tag{2}
\]
Let
\[
 \Delta^{N-1}:=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^N:\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i=1\right\},
 \qquad
 Y\lambda:=\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i y_i,
\tag{3}
\]
and, for \(z\in\mathbb R^2\), let
\[
 \Lambda_z:=\{\lambda\in\Delta^{N-1}:Y\lambda=z\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
 L_N(u):=\max_{1\leq i<N}
 \bigl((2i+1)u-2i(i+1)\bigr),
 \qquad
 U_N(u):=(N+1)u-2N,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 Z_N:=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:
 2\leq u\leq2N,\quad L_N(u)\leq v\leq U_N(u)\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the exact positive integer-fiber support and the exact fibers are
\[
 \boxed{
 \{z\in\mathbb Z^2:\operatorname{vol}_N((C_N)_z)>0\}=Z_N,
 \qquad
 (C_N)_z=K+4\Lambda_z\quad(z\in Z_N).}
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(4\Lambda_z\) is viewed in the continuous space \(\mathbb R^N\). In particular, every point of the lattice polygon contributes to the mixed mass, not only the displayed vertices, and \((C_N)_{y_i}=K+4\mathbf e_i\).

Write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_N(K+4\Lambda_z)
 \quad(z\in Z_N),
 \qquad
 W_N:=\sum_{z\in Z_N}a_z.
\tag{8}
\]
Equivalently, if \(\kappa_j:=\operatorname{vol}_j(B_2^j)\), with \(\kappa_0=1\), and \(V_j\) denotes the \(j\)-th intrinsic volume in \(\mathbb R^N\), then the Steiner formula gives the finite exact expression
\[
 a_z=\sum_{j=0}^N\kappa_{N-j}4^jV_j(\Lambda_z).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), set
\[
 Z_{N,\xi}:=\{z\in Z_N:z\equiv\xi\pmod2\},
 \qquad
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\in Z_{N,\xi}}a_z,
 \qquad
 M_\xi:=\max_{z\in Z_{N,\xi}}a_z,
\tag{10}
\]
where \(M_\xi:=0\) if the class is empty, and call \(\xi\) good when \(A_\xi>0\) and \(M_\xi\leq A_\xi/4\). With the canonical normalization of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66, the actual half-strength product-reservoir mass is
\[
 \boxed{
 Q(C_N)=
 \sum_{\substack{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\\
                  \xi\ {\rm good}}}
 \frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\in Z_{N,\xi}}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}.}
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{0\leq Q(C_N)\leq\frac{W_N}{8}.}
\tag{12}
\]
For \(N=1600\), if \(t:=\operatorname{vol}_N(K)/4\) and \(Q_0:=1601t\), then \(Q_0\) is only a comparison parameter. The canonical mass is the finite quantity in (11), formed from all fibers in (7); \(Q_0\) cannot be substituted for it without separately proving equality.

Let \(G(C_N):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_N)\). Accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c gives
\[
 \boxed{G(C_N)\geq\frac{W_N}{64}\geq\frac{Q(C_N)}8.}
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(r\) be the canonical reservoir and put \(R:=R_{C_N}(r)\), with \(R=0\) when \(Q(C_N)=0\) and hence \(r=0\). Use the remaining canonical capacities of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66. With
\[
 \gamma_N:=\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^N,
 \qquad
 D:=2^2h_{\max}(C_N),
\tag{14}
\]
they obey the rigorous bounds
\[
 0\leq R\leq Q(C_N),
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N),M_{\mathrm{off}}(C_N),R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_N)\geq0,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{D\geq\gamma_NG(C_N)\geq\frac{\gamma_N}{8}Q(C_N).}
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore, for the canonical hard-ledger sum
\[
 \mathcal A(C_N):=
 R+G(C_N)+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C_N)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_N)+D,
\tag{17}
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal A(C_N)\geq G(C_N)\geq\frac{Q(C_N)}8.}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(C_N\) cannot satisfy \(\mathcal A(C_N)<Q(C_N)/100\). This remains true when \(Q(C_N)=0\), because then that strict inequality would require the nonnegative quantity \(\mathcal A(C_N)\) to be negative. In particular, the \(N=1600\) body never enters the literal canonical hard branch, so it cannot be a genuine counterexample to the synchronized hereditary rank-density alternative.

## Proof

We first prove the exact Cayley-fiber identity. Every point of \(C_N\) is a convex combination of points \((y_i,k_i+4\mathbf e_i)\) with \(k_i\in K\). Grouping the coefficients by \(i\), it has the form
\[
 \left(Y\lambda,\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_i k_i+4\lambda\right)
 \qquad(\lambda\in\Delta^{N-1}).
\tag{19}
\]
Convexity of \(K\) gives \(\sum_i\lambda_i k_i\in K\). Conversely, for any \(\lambda\in\Delta^{N-1}\) and any \(k\in K\), choose \(k_i=k\) for every \(i\) with positive coefficient. This gives the exact global representation
\[
 C_N=\{(Y\lambda,k+4\lambda):
 \lambda\in\Delta^{N-1},\ k\in K\}.
\tag{20}
\]
Taking the fiber of (20) at \(z\) proves \((C_N)_z=K+4\Lambda_z\) whenever \(\Lambda_z\ne\varnothing\), and the fiber is empty otherwise. A nonempty \(\Lambda_z\) contains some \(\lambda\), so the fiber contains the translate \(K+4\lambda\) and has positive \(N\)-volume. Thus positive volume is equivalent to \(z\in P_N:=\operatorname{conv}\{y_1,\ldots,y_N\}\).

It remains to identify this polygon. For \(1\leq i<N\), the line through \(y_i\) and \(y_{i+1}\) is
\[
 v=(2i+1)u-2i(i+1).
\tag{21}
\]
At the vertex \(y_j\), the vertical difference between \(2j^2\) and the right side of (21) at \(u=2j\) is
\[
 2(j-i)(j-i-1)\geq0,
\tag{22}
\]
with equality exactly for \(j=i,i+1\). Hence these segments form the lower boundary \(v=L_N(u)\). The line through \(y_1\) and \(y_N\) is \(v=U_N(u)\), and
\[
 U_N(2j)-2j^2=2(j-1)(N-j)\geq0.
\tag{23}
\]
It is therefore the upper boundary. This proves
\[
 P_N=\{(u,v):2\leq u\leq2N,\ L_N(u)\leq v\leq U_N(u)\},
\tag{24}
\]
and intersecting with \(\mathbb Z^2\) proves (6)-(7). Each \(y_i\) is a vertex, so \(\Lambda_{y_i}=\{\mathbf e_i\}\), which gives the endpoint-fiber assertion. Three vertices are noncollinear, and vertical differences within one copy of \(K\) span \(\mathbb R^N\); hence \(C_N\) has full affine dimension \(N+2\). Compactness and convexity are immediate from (2), so it is a convex body.

Equation (9) is the standard Steiner identity for the Minkowski sum of the unit ball with the compact convex set \(4\Lambda_z\), using \(V_j(4\Lambda_z)=4^jV_j(\Lambda_z)\). It supplies an exact finite expression for every physical fiber mass in (8).

We next compute the canonical reservoir. Accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 assigns, in a good parity class \(\xi\), the edge weight
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \qquad(u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2),
\tag{25}
\]
and assigns zero in a bad class. Therefore the contribution of a good class is
\[
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq Z_{N,\xi}}\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 =\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\in Z_{N,\xi}}a_z^2}{8A_\xi},
\tag{26}
\]
which proves (11). Each numerator in (26) is nonnegative, and its value is at most \(A_\xi^2\). Summing over good classes gives
\[
 Q(C_N)\leq\frac18\sum_{\xi\ {\rm good}}A_\xi
 \leq\frac{W_N}{8},
\tag{27}
\]
proving (12).

The positive support contains the three noncollinear points \(y_1,y_2,y_3\), so its affine hull is all of \(\mathbb R^2\). Apply the global rank-two conclusion of accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c with the affine slice \(A=\mathbb R^2\). Its intrinsic rank is \(q=n=2\), its slice mass is \(W_N\), and its constant is \(\alpha_2=1/64\). Hence
\[
 G(C_N)\geq\frac{W_N}{64}.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27) and (28) proves (13). The same accepted fact gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C_N)\geq\gamma_N2^{-2}G(C_N),
\tag{29}
\]
so multiplication by \(4\) proves (16). The quantities in (15) are nonnegative by their definitions, and a realizable submatching of \(r\) has mass between zero and \(|r|=Q(C_N)\). Thus (15) holds and (17) is at least its \(G(C_N)\) summand. Equation (18) follows. For positive \(Q(C_N)\), one has \(Q(C_N)/8>Q(C_N)/100\); the zero case was handled in the statement. This is the first canonical preflight failure, so no downstream hard-branch construction is applicable to this family.
