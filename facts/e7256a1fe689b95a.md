## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a compact convex body of positive mixed $d$-volume with true mixed centroid $(0,0)$. Write $K_z$ for its fiber at $z$. For $k\ge1$ set $Q_k^+=K_k/k$ and $Q_k^-=-K_{-k}/k$, with missing fibers represented by empty sets. Each family is decreasing. Set
$$
f_\pm(s)=\sum_{k\ge1} k^d\mathbf1_{Q_k^\pm}(s),\qquad
g_\pm(s)=\sum_{k\ge1} k^{d+1}\mathbf1_{Q_k^\pm}(s).
$$
For any closed halfspace $H=\{az+u\cdot x\ge0\}$, its mixed mass in $C$ is
$$
|K_0\cap\{u\cdot x\ge0\}|+
\int_{a+u\cdot s\ge0} f_+(s)\,ds+
\int_{a+u\cdot s\le0} f_-(s)\,ds .
$$
The centroid equations become
$$
\int g_+=\int g_-,\qquad
\int s(g_+-g_-)\,ds+\int_{K_0}x\,dx=0.
$$
Writing $F_j(m)=\sum_{k=1}^m k^j$ and $R_\pm(s)=\max\{r\ge0:(\pm r,\pm rs)\in C\}$, one has $f_\pm=F_d(\lfloor R_\pm\rfloor)$ and $g_\pm=F_{d+1}(\lfloor R_\pm\rfloor)$, including exact integer endpoints.

## Proof

The mixed centroid belongs to the compact convex body because it is a barycenter of a measure supported on $C$. Convexity and inclusion of the origin imply that if a point $(\pm(k+1),\pm(k+1)s)$ belongs to $C$, so does $(\pm k,\pm ks)$. They also imply that the closed radial intersection in either chart is exactly the interval from zero to $R_\pm(s)$. These observations give the decreasing families and the floor rules.

On the positive fiber $k$, put $x=ks$ and on the negative fiber $-k$, put $x=-ks$. Both changes of variables have absolute Jacobian $k^d$. The cap inequality reduces to $a+u\cdot s\ge0$ on the positive chart and $a+u\cdot s\le0$ on the negative chart. At base zero the test is $u\cdot x\ge0$, so the first term retains the entire central fiber when $u=0$. The base moment of the positive and negative fibers has the extra signed factor $\pm k$, and the continuous moment has factor $\pm ks$. Summing gives the displayed equations. All sums are finite and zero-volume fibers contribute zero.

As a scalar consequence, let $\Phi_d$ be the piecewise-affine interpolant through $(F_d(m),F_{d+1}(m))$ for integers $m\ge0$. Its successive slopes are $m$ for $m\ge1$, since successive differences of the two power sums are $m^d$ and $m^{d+1}$. Hence it is convex and $g_\pm=\Phi_d(f_\pm)$ pointwise. For every measurable $E$ of finite positive Lebesgue measure, Jensen gives $\int_Eg_\pm\ge |E|\Phi_d(|E|^{-1}\int_E f_\pm)$, with equality on any single radial plateau.
