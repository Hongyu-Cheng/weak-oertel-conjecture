## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and affinely span $\mathbb R^n$. Suppose positive-volume compact polytopes $K_r\subset\mathbb R^d$, $r\in P$, $d\ge1$, are the actual sampled fibers of their lifted convex hull. Write $h_r$ for their support functions, and fix $p\in P$. For $q\ne p$, take the extreme non-self representations $\lambda$ of $q$ as a convex combination of $P\setminus\{q\}$, and let $u$ range over the outward facet unit normals of $K_q$. Define
$$
R_p=\bigcap_{\substack{q\ne p,\ \lambda\ {\rm extreme},\ \lambda_p>0\\u\ {\rm a\ facet\ normal\ of}\ K_q}}
\left\{x:u\cdot x\le
\frac{h_q(u)-\sum_{r\ne p,q}\lambda_r h_r(u)}{\lambda_p}\right\}.
$$
An empty intersection is $\mathbb R^d$. Then $K_p\subseteq R_p$, and replacing only $K_p$ by a compact convex $L\supseteq K_p$ is actual on the same sampled set if and only if $L\subseteq R_p$.

In particular, if a facet $G$ of $K_p$ is contained in $\operatorname{int}R_p$, shifting only its supporting inequality outward by a sufficiently small positive distance gives a bounded actual enlargement of this single fiber.

## Proof

For any labeled fibers, the section of their lifted hull at a sampled base $q$ is the union of the Minkowski combinations of the labeled fibers over all representations of $q$. Therefore exact realization is equivalent to inclusion of every anchored Minkowski combination in its target fiber. At a target $q\ne p$, testing the old and new inclusions at its facet normals shows respectively that $K_p\subseteq R_p$ and that $L\subseteq R_p$ is necessary.

Conversely, for $q\ne p$ every extreme non-self representation obeys each target facet bound: if $\lambda_p>0$ this follows from the defining inequality for $R_p$, and if $\lambda_p=0$ it is the unchanged old inclusion. For any other non-self representation the same support inequality holds by linearity in the representation weights. A representation using its own target with weight less than one reduces to a non-self representation by dividing by one minus that weight, and the weight-one case is trivial. At target $p$ the non-self inputs are unchanged, and their combination is in $K_p\subseteq L$. Thus the new hull has exactly the stated fibers over $P$. Its projection is still $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, so completeness excludes other sampled bases. It remains full dimensional since the bases span and the positive fibers contain open continuous balls.

For the facet consequence, keep all other own-fiber inequalities unchanged. The normals are fixed and the recession cone is still trivial. The resulting polytopes are uniformly bounded for small shifts: otherwise normalizing a sequence of unbounded feasible points gives a unit direction in that recession cone. Every accumulation point of points in the new set but outside $K_p$, as the shift tends to zero, lies in $G$ because they violate only its old facet inequality. A neighborhood of compact $G$ lies in $R_p$ by the interior assumption, so all the new points lie in $R_p$ for sufficiently small shifts. Together with $K_p\subseteq R_p$ this proves feasibility. The interior assumption is equivalently strict slack in each of the finitely many reserve inequalities on every vertex of $G$.
