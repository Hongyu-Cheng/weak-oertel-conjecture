## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), let \(\mu_C\) be its mixed-volume measure, and let \(h_{\max}(C)\) and the absolute fiber depth \(\delta_C(z,y)\) have the same definitions and normalizations as in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Put
\[
 G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\tag{2}
\]
where this is the complete metronoid-core capacity with the exact definition and normalization in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`.

For later comparison, define the parity-midpoint residual capacity as follows. If \(\sigma\) is a nonzero finite positive measure carried by \(S\), put
\[
 A_\sigma:=\operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(\operatorname{supp}\sigma),
 \qquad
 \rho_\sigma:=\dim A_\sigma,
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 r_\sigma:=
 \min_{s:A_\sigma\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{y-s(z):(z,y)\in\operatorname{supp}\sigma\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(\mathscr N_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)\) consist of all nonzero positive submeasures of either of the following two types of generating measures, with exactly the same normalizations as in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`:

1. Given a finite \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), points \(q_z\in C_z\), and weights \(0<b_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\), set
\[
 \rho_0:=\sum_{z\in I}b_z\delta_{(z,q_z)},
 \qquad
 \rho_{0,\xi}:=\rho_0\big|_{\{(z,q):z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)\}},
 \qquad
 B_\xi:=|\rho_{0,\xi}|,
\]
and, for \(T(p,p')=(p+p')/2\), use the same-parity midpoint measure
\[
 \nu:=
 \sum_{\xi:B_\xi>0}
 \frac1{2B_\xi}
 T_\#(\rho_{0,\xi}\otimes\rho_{0,\xi}).
\tag{5}
\]

2. Use any genuine minimum-unmatched off-diagonal centroid measure \(\nu_{\mathrm{off}}\) in equations (23)-(29) of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`: thus its source weights are \(b_z=\gamma_da_z\), where \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), its edges join distinct same-parity base points, and its total unmatched mass in parity class \(\xi\) is \((2\max_{z\equiv\xi}b_z-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}b_z)_+\).

Define
\[
 M_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)
 :=
 \sup_{\sigma\in\mathscr N_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)}
 2^{n-\rho_\sigma}\frac{|\sigma|}{r_\sigma+1},
\tag{6}
\]
with supremum zero if the class is empty. The dominated-measure theorem in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}M_{\mathrm{mid}}(C).
\tag{7}
\]

Assume the strict no-rank-drop and small-core conditions
\[
 W_L<2^{\dim L-n}W
 \quad\text{for every proper primitive affine lattice subspace }
 L\subsetneq\mathbb R^n,
 \qquad
 G(C)<\frac W4.
\tag{8}
\]
Here primitive has exactly the meaning in accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb`.

Let \(\Pi\) be an admissible factorized lattice-polysimplex packet in the exact sense of accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`, with \(k\geq2\) factors of positive ranks \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\), total rank
\[
 r_1+\cdots+r_k=n,
\tag{9}
\]
and injective integral affine base map
\[
 \Phi(t_1,\ldots,t_k)=b+\sum_{i=1}^kU_it_i.
\tag{10}
\]
Write \(D_i=\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i}\), let \(g_i\) be the nonnegative affine factor functions of that accepted packet, and put
\[
 \omega_i(u):=g_i(u)^d,
 \qquad
 A_i:=\sum_{u\in D_i}\omega_i(u),
 \qquad
 E:=\mathcal E(\Pi)=\prod_{i=1}^kA_i>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(x(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\) be the canonical multiaffine selector constructed in accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5`, and abbreviate
\[
 P_u:=(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \quad\text{for }u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i.
\tag{12}
\]

For each factor \(i\), perform a minimum-unmatched fractional matching separately in every parity class of \(D_i\). More explicitly, there are nonnegative edge weights \(\lambda_i(e)\) on unordered pairs \(e=\{u,v\}\subseteq D_i\) with \(u\ne v\) and \(u\equiv v\pmod2\), and unmatched weights \(s_i(u)\geq0\), such that
\[
 \omega_i(u)=s_i(u)+d_i(u),
 \qquad
 d_i(u):=\sum_{e\ni u}\lambda_i(e).
\tag{13}
\]
If
\[
 B_{i,\xi}:=\sum_{\substack{u\in D_i\\u\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}\omega_i(u),
 \qquad
 \beta_{i,\xi}:=\max_{\substack{u\in D_i\\u\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}\omega_i(u),
\tag{14}
\]
with both quantities zero for an empty class, then the minimum total unmatched mass and total edge mass are
\[
 \mathsf U_i
 :=\sum_\xi(2\beta_{i,\xi}-B_{i,\xi})_+,
 \qquad
 \sum_{u\in D_i}s_i(u)=\mathsf U_i,
 \qquad
 L_i:=\sum_e\lambda_i(e)=\frac{A_i-\mathsf U_i}{2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Put
\[
 \theta_i:=\frac{\mathsf U_i}{A_i}\in[0,1],
 \qquad
 \chi(\Pi):=
 \sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\\|J|\geq2}}
 \prod_{\ell\in J}r_\ell
 =\prod_{i=1}^k(r_i+1)-1-n.
\tag{16}
\]

Then the following conclusions hold.

First, every factor \(j\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 A_j\prod_{i\ne j}\mathsf U_i\leq G(C),
 \qquad
 \prod_{i\ne j}\theta_i\leq\frac{G(C)}E.
 }
\tag{17}
\]

Second, if \(E>G(C)\), there are distinct factors \(i,j\) such that
\[
 \boxed{
 (1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)
 \geq
 \left[1-\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}\right]^2.
 }
\tag{18}
\]

For any such pair \(i,j\), one has a genuine rectangular measure as follows. For matching edges
\[
 e_i=\{u_0,u_1\},
 \qquad
 e_j=\{v_0,v_1\},
\]
and fixed remaining coordinates \(\widehat u=(u_\ell)_{\ell\ne i,j}\), let \(P_{ab}\) be the point (12) with factor coordinates \(u_a\) and \(v_b\) in positions \(i,j\), and put
\[
 Y_+:=\frac{P_{00}+P_{11}}2,
 \qquad
 Y_-:=\frac{P_{01}+P_{10}}2,
 \qquad
 K_{\widehat u}:=\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}\omega_\ell(u_\ell).
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
 \sigma_{ij}:=
 \sum_{\widehat u}\sum_{e_i,e_j}
 K_{\widehat u}\lambda_i(e_i)\lambda_j(e_j)
 (\delta_{Y_+}+\delta_{Y_-})
\tag{20}
\]
is carried by \(S\), is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\), and has
\[
 \boxed{
 |\sigma_{ij}|=
 \frac E2(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j),
 \qquad
 r_{\sigma_{ij}}\leq\chi(\Pi).
 }
\tag{21}
\]
For each rectangle, \(Y_+\) and \(Y_-\) lie in one common integral base fiber, each has absolute fiber depth at least
\[
 K_{\widehat u}\lambda_i(e_i)\lambda_j(e_j),
\tag{22}
\]
the entire segment joining them has at least that depth, and their continuous-coordinate difference is the actual mixed discrete derivative
\[
 \operatorname{cont}(Y_+)-\operatorname{cont}(Y_-)
 =\frac12\bigl(x_{00}+x_{11}-x_{01}-x_{10}\bigr).
\tag{23}
\]

Distinct from (20), use the source points \(P_u\) and source weights
\[
b_u:=d_i(u_i)d_j(u_j)
\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}\omega_\ell(u_\ell).
\tag{24}
\]
Omit every source with \(b_u=0\).
The canonical all-pairs same-parity measure (5) generated by these data has mass \(E(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)/2\), belongs to \(\mathscr N_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)\), and has residual rank at most \(\chi(\Pi)\). Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 M_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{2(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 (1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{2(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \left[1-\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}\right]^2.
 }
\tag{25}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{2^{n+1}(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \left[1-\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}\right]^2.
 }
\tag{26}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5` gives, for every \(u\in\prod_iD_i\),
\[
 P_u\in S,
 \qquad
 \delta_C(P_u)
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i).
\tag{27}
\]
Here and below \(\delta_C(P_u)\) means the absolute depth of the continuous coordinate of \(P_u\) in its base fiber.

We first justify the minimum-unmatched matchings in (13)-(15). For any finite set of vertices with nonnegative capacities \(c_v\), total capacity \(B\), and maximum \(\beta\), a fractional matching in the complete graph has minimum unmatched mass
\[
 (2\beta-B)_+.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(B=0\), take every edge and unmatched weight to be zero. If \(B>0\) and \(v_*\) has capacity \(\beta>B/2\), match \(v_*\) to every other vertex with edge weight \(c_v\). This saturates every other vertex and leaves \(2\beta-B\) at \(v_*\), which is optimal because all edges incident with \(v_*\) have total weight at most \(B-\beta\). If \(B>0\) and \(\beta\leq B/2\), partition a circle of circumference \(B\) into half-open consecutive arcs \(I_v\) of lengths \(c_v\). Translation by \(B/2\) is a measure-preserving involution, and no arc overlaps its translate except on null endpoints. Assign to \(\{u,v\}\) the measure of points of \(I_u\) whose translate lies in \(I_v\). These symmetric edge weights have degree \(c_v\) at every vertex and no loops. Thus the unmatched mass is zero. Applying (28) independently in every factor parity class proves (13)-(15), including zero-weight points and singleton classes.

Fix a factor \(j\). In each parity class of every factor \(i\ne j\) having positive unmatched mass, choose a point \(u_{i,\xi}\) of maximum weight, and let \(R_i\) be the set of chosen points. Formula (28) gives
\[
 |R_i|\leq2^{r_i},
 \qquad
 \sum_{u_i\in R_i}\omega_i(u_i)\geq\mathsf U_i.
\tag{29}
\]
If some \(R_i\) with \(i\ne j\) is empty, then \(\mathsf U_i=0\) and the first inequality in (17) is immediate. Otherwise fix a tuple \(u_{-j}\in\prod_{i\ne j}R_i\). Holding those factor coordinates fixed in the canonical selector leaves an admissible one-factor packet of rank \(r_j<n\): its integral affine base map is
\[
 t_j\longmapsto b+U_jt_j+\sum_{i\ne j}U_iu_i,
\]
and its vertex depth-root data are the original factor-\(j\) data multiplied by \(\prod_{i\ne j}g_i(u_i)\). Hence its energy is exactly
\[
 A_j\prod_{i\ne j}\omega_i(u_i).
\tag{30}
\]
Under (8), accepted fact `3d1f63abf09b2abb` gives its ambiently normalized one-factor bound
\[
 2^{n-r_j}A_j\prod_{i\ne j}\omega_i(u_i)\leq G(C).
\tag{31}
\]
Sum (31) over \(u_{-j}\in\prod_{i\ne j}R_i\). Since (9) and (29) give
\[
 \left|\prod_{i\ne j}R_i\right|
 \leq2^{\sum_{i\ne j}r_i}=2^{n-r_j},
\]
we obtain
\[
 2^{n-r_j}A_j
 \prod_{i\ne j}\left(\sum_{u_i\in R_i}\omega_i(u_i)\right)
 \leq2^{n-r_j}G(C).
\]
After division and (29), this proves the first inequality in (17). Dividing it by \(E=\prod_iA_i\) proves the second.

Assume \(E>G(C)\), and reorder factors so that
\[
 \theta_1\leq\theta_2\leq\cdots\leq\theta_k.
\]
Taking \(j=1\) in (17) yields
\[
 \prod_{\ell=2}^k\theta_\ell\leq\frac{G(C)}E.
\]
Since every factor on the left is at least \(\theta_2\),
\[
 \theta_1\leq\theta_2
 \leq\left(\frac{G(C)}E\right)^{1/(k-1)}.
\]
Choosing \(i=1,j=2\) proves (18).

We next construct and verify (20). Same-parity factor edges imply
\[
 \frac{u_0+u_1}{2}\in\mathbb Z^{r_i},
 \qquad
 \frac{v_0+v_1}{2}\in\mathbb Z^{r_j}.
\]
Consequently the two points in (19) have the same integral base coordinate. They belong to \(C\) by convexity. Joint concavity of \(\delta_C^{1/d}\) from accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, together with (27), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_C(Y_+)^{1/d}
 &\geq\frac12\left[
 \bigl(K_{\widehat u}\omega_i(u_0)\omega_j(v_0)\bigr)^{1/d}
 +\bigl(K_{\widehat u}\omega_i(u_1)\omega_j(v_1)\bigr)^{1/d}
 \right]\\
 &\geq
 \left[
 K_{\widehat u}^2
 \omega_i(u_0)\omega_i(u_1)
 \omega_j(v_0)\omega_j(v_1)
 \right]^{1/(2d)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Because each edge weight is at most the matched degree, hence at most the capacity, of each endpoint, raising (32) to the \(d\)-th power gives
\[
 \delta_C(Y_+)\geq K_{\widehat u}\lambda_i(e_i)\lambda_j(e_j).
\]
The same argument gives the bound for \(Y_-\), proving (22). The depth superlevel sets are convex by accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, so the whole segment between the two points has the same lower bound. Equation (23) follows directly from (19).

The mass of (20) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\sigma_{ij}|
 &=2\left(\sum_{e_i}\lambda_i(e_i)\right)
       \left(\sum_{e_j}\lambda_j(e_j)\right)
       \prod_{\ell\ne i,j}A_\ell\\
 &=2L_iL_j\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}A_\ell
 =\frac E2(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
For any closed halfspace \(H\), each of the two disjoint diagonal pairs gives
\[
 \mathbf1_H(Y_+)+\mathbf1_H(Y_-)
 \leq\sum_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}\mathbf1_H(P_{ab}),
\tag{34}
\]
because a halfspace containing a midpoint must contain at least one endpoint. After multiplying (34) by the rectangle weight and summing, the coefficient of a source point \(P_u\) is precisely the number \(b_u\) in (24). Equations (13) and (27) give
\[
 0\leq b_u\leq\prod_{\ell=1}^k\omega_\ell(u_\ell)
 \leq\delta_C(P_u).
\tag{35}
\]
The base map \(\Phi\) is injective. Therefore the fiberwise definition of depth gives
\[
 \sum_u b_u\mathbf1_H(P_u)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{36}
\]
Combining (34)-(36) proves \(\sigma_{ij}(H)\leq\mu_C(H)\).

It remains to control residual rank. Choose origins in the factor coordinate spaces. The multiaffine extension of the canonical selector has an expansion
\[
 x(t)=x_\varnothing+\sum_{i=1}^kB_i(t_i)
 +\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\\|J|\geq2}}
 B_J\left(\bigotimes_{\ell\in J}t_\ell\right),
\tag{37}
\]
where each \(B_J\) is linear on the displayed tensor product. Let
\[
 V_{\mathrm{mix}}:=\sum_{|J|\geq2}\operatorname{im}B_J.
\]
Then
\[
 \dim V_{\mathrm{mix}}
 \leq\sum_{|J|\geq2}\prod_{\ell\in J}r_\ell
 =\chi(\Pi).
\tag{38}
\]
Since (9)-(10) make \(\Phi\) an affine isomorphism onto its affine image, the constant and one-factor terms in (37) define an affine map \(s\) on that image. Every packet graph point satisfies
\[
 x(u)-s(\Phi(u))\in V_{\mathrm{mix}}.
\tag{39}
\]
By affineness of \(s\), the same is true for every midpoint of two packet graph points. Thus (4) and (38) give \(r_{\sigma_{ij}}\leq\chi(\Pi)\), completing (21).

Finally consider the source weights (24). Their total mass is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B&:=\sum_u b_u\\
 &=\left(\sum_{u_i}d_i(u_i)\right)
   \left(\sum_{u_j}d_j(u_j)\right)
   \prod_{\ell\ne i,j}A_\ell\\
 &=E(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
By (35), these are admissible source weights in the same-parity construction (5). Accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` shows that the resulting canonical all-pairs midpoint measure \(\nu_{ij}\) has mass \(B/2\), is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\), and is carried by \(S\). Equations (38)-(39) apply to every midpoint in its support, so
\[
 r_{\nu_{ij}}\leq\chi(\Pi),
 \qquad
 \rho_{\nu_{ij}}\leq n.
\tag{41}
\]
This \(\nu_{ij}\), unlike the separately constructed rectangular measure \(\sigma_{ij}\), is the canonical all-pairs measure (5), and hence belongs to \(\mathscr N_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)\). From (6), (40), and (41),
\[
 M_{\mathrm{mid}}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_{\nu_{ij}}}
 \frac{|\nu_{ij}|}{r_{\nu_{ij}}+1}
 \geq\frac{E(1-\theta_i)(1-\theta_j)}{2(\chi(\Pi)+1)}.
\]
Together with (18), this proves (25). Equation (7) then proves (26).
