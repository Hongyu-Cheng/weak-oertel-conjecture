## Statement

For each integer \(m\ge4\), set
$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=4^m,& b&=2^m,& T&=4bm,& \beta&=0,& \Phi&\equiv0,\\
B_i&=b,& C_{4,i}&=2b-1\quad(i\in[k]),&
\epsilon&=2^{-k},&
\rho_i&=\begin{cases}1,&1\le i\le m,\\ \epsilon,&m<i\le k.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
Use the canonical cost functions \(g_i(0)=0\), \(g_i(1)=1\), \(g_i(2)=B_i\), \(g_i(3)=C_{4,i}\), excess \(r(\mathbf x)=\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\), energy \(\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)=\sum_i g_i(x_i)+\Phi(r(\mathbf x))\), and occupation \(Q(\mathbf x)=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|\) of accepted fact 21a3de2e8e8e3fd6. Here \(\Gamma=\Gamma_\Phi=\sum_i g_i\), because \(\Phi=0\). For every nonnegative integer \(E\), retain its complete original height tail
$$
f_E=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^{T}e^{-\beta t}=T-E+1,&E\le T,\\
0,&E>T.
\end{cases}
$$
The individual-activity replacements of the weights and normalizers in that accepted fact are
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf x)
&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^k\rho_i^{x_i}\right)f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},\\
Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf x),\\
Z_{3,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf x),\\
Z_{{\rm bin},\boldsymbol{\rho}}
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}w_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathbf x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, when every \(\rho_i=\rho\), these are exactly \(w_{\Phi,\rho}\), \(Z_{4,\Phi,\rho}\), \(Z_{3,\Phi,\rho}\), and \(Z_{{\rm bin},\rho}\) of 21a3de2e8e8e3fd6. Each spatial vector has multiplicity one, and every feasible integer height, including both endpoints, remains present.

Every member has \(b=\sqrt{k}\), \(b\le B_i\le2b\), and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\), and satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
T&\le\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\rho_i}{1+\rho_i}
       +b[1+\log^2(k+1)],\\
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{Z_{3,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}}
&\ge\frac16\left(\frac43\right)^m.
\end{aligned}
$$
All logarithms are natural. In particular, the ratio is unbounded along this family, so the dimension-uniform conclusion (7) of 21a3de2e8e8e3fd6 is false with arbitrary \(\rho_i\in(0,1]\) and the proposed binary-mean cap baseline, even for the fixed \(a=D=1\).

Define the original atom support and law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,\quad
                    t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}
&=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^k\rho_i^{x_i}e^{-\beta t}}
        {Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\qquad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_T),\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),&
M_3(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(H\) ranges over all closed affine halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero normal. The set \(A_T\) is the full lattice intersection of a full-dimensional compact convex body, and also \(A_T=\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}\); its affine hull is \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). The original atom weights are positive and log-affine on this complete support.

Let \(\kappa_{\rm allwindow}>0\) be exactly the absolute constant specified in (7) of accepted fact bd644bc98ce0e229. Despite the unbounded normalizer ratio, every original law in this family satisfies
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm allwindow}}{16}\,8^{-(m+1)}
       \ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm allwindow}}{16}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Thus the counterexample concerns the proposed normalizer extension and retains the actual original moment bound.

## Proof

All specified parameters and costs are integers where required, \(b\ge16\), and \(0<\epsilon\le1\). The zero spatial vector at height zero is feasible, so every normalizer used below is positive. The identities \(k=b^2\), \(B_i=b\), and \(C_{4,i}=2B_i-1\) verify the cost hypotheses with \(a=1\). Moreover,
\(\sum_i\rho_i/(1+\rho_i)=m/2+(k-m)\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)\ge0\).
The elementary integral inequality \(\log2=\int_1^2u^{-1}\,du>1/2\) gives \(\log4>1\), whence \(\log(k+1)\ge\log k=m\log4\ge m\). Since \(m^2\ge4m\) for \(m\ge4\),
$$
T=4bm\le bm^2\le b[1+\log^2(k+1)]
  \le\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\rho_i}{1+\rho_i}
       +b[1+\log^2(k+1)].
$$
This proves the stated cap with the fixed \(D=1\).

To verify convex completeness directly, extend each discrete \(g_i\) to \([0,3]\) by the piecewise-linear interpolation
$$
g_i(u)=\begin{cases}
u,&0\le u\le1,\\
1+(b-1)(u-1),&1\le u\le3.
\end{cases}
$$
Its successive slopes are \(1,b-1,b-1\), so it is convex, continuous, nonnegative, and has exactly the prescribed four integer values. Consequently
\(\mathcal C_T=\lbrace(\mathbf u,s)\in[0,3]^k\times\mathbb R:
\sum_i g_i(u_i)\le s\le T\rbrace\)
is convex and closed. Its heights lie in \([0,T]\), so it is compact. The elementary induction \(m\le2^{m-2}\) for \(m\ge4\) gives \(0<m/b\le1/4\). At the concrete point \(\mathbf u=(m/b)\mathbf1\), \(s=T/2\), every coordinate is strictly between zero and three and
\(\sum_i g_i(u_i)=km/b=bm=T/4<T/2<T\).
Continuity therefore gives an open Euclidean neighborhood inside \(\mathcal C_T\), proving full dimensionality.

At integer spatial coordinates in \([0,3]^k\), this interpolation has exactly the original energy \(\Gamma\). Hence \(\mathcal C_T\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}=A_T\), with every endpoint retained. Since \(A_T\subset\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\subset\mathcal C_T\), taking lattice intersections proves \(A_T=\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}\). Also \((\mathbf0,0)\), \((\mathbf0,1)\), and \((\mathbf e_i,1)\) for \(1\le i\le k\) all belong to \(A_T\), where \(\mathbf e_i\) are the standard spatial coordinate vectors. Their differences from \((\mathbf0,0)\) are linearly independent, so the support itself has full affine dimension. Its unnormalized atom weights equal
\(\exp(\sum_i(\log\rho_i)x_i-\beta t)\), which are positive and log-affine on every atom of this complete support.

We use an exact elementary bound for the inactive-coordinate products. For every integer \(k\ge5\), \(2^k\ge6k\): it holds at \(k=5\), and doubling preserves it because \(12k\ge6(k+1)\). Here \(k\ge256\), so \(k\epsilon\le1/6\). For \(u\ge0\) with \(ku<1\), the binomial formula and \(\binom{k}{j}\le k^j\) give
$$
(1+u)^k=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom{k}{j}u^j
       \le\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(ku)^j=\frac1{1-ku}.
$$
Because \(\epsilon\le1\), we have
\(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2\le1+2\epsilon\) and
\(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2+\epsilon^3\le1+3\epsilon\).
Using \(2k\epsilon\le1/3\) and \(3k\epsilon\le1/2\) in the preceding bound proves
$$
(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2)^{k-m}\le2,\qquad
(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2+\epsilon^3)^{k-m}\le2.
\tag{1}
$$

Call the first \(m\) coordinates active. For \(\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^m\), define \(\Gamma^{\rm act}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^m g_i(x_i)\), and let
\(Z_4^{\rm act}=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^m}f_{\Gamma^{\rm act}(\mathbf x)}\).
This is exactly the original mass on the restriction where all inactive coordinates are zero. Every active vector satisfies
\(\Gamma^{\rm act}(\mathbf x)\le(2b-1)m\le T/2\).
Thus its complete tail has at least \(T/2\) heights, and
\(Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ge Z_4^{\rm act}\ge(T/2)4^m\).
For the ternary sum, every tail is at most \(T+1\); summing the spatial weights and then using (1) gives
$$
Z_{3,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}
\le(T+1)3^m(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2)^{k-m}
\le2(T+1)3^m.
$$
As \(T\ge2\), these inequalities imply
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{Z_{3,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}}
\ge\frac{T}{4(T+1)}\left(\frac43\right)^m
\ge\frac16\left(\frac43\right)^m.
$$
The last expression tends to infinity as \(m\) tends to infinity.

For the retained original mass, write a full spatial vector as \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\), with \(m\) active coordinates and \(k-m\) inactive coordinates. Nonnegativity of every inactive cost gives
\(\Gamma(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\ge\Gamma^{\rm act}(\mathbf x)\).
Since the original tails \(f_E\) are nonincreasing, including empty tails,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^m}
  \sum_{\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^{k-m}}
       \epsilon^{\sum_jy_j} f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)}\\
&\le Z_4^{\rm act}(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2+\epsilon^3)^{k-m}
\le2Z_4^{\rm act}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the original probability of the inactive-zero restriction is
\(\vartheta=Z_4^{\rm act}/Z_{4,\Phi,\boldsymbol{\rho}}\ge1/2\).

Define
\(A_T^{\rm act}=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^m,\quad
t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma^{\rm act}(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace\)
and \(p^{\rm act}_{(\mathbf x,t)}=1/Z_4^{\rm act}\) on this set. This is exactly the original law (1) of accepted fact bd644bc98ce0e229 under the explicit map
\(k_{\rm predecessor}=m\), \(b_{\rm predecessor}=b\),
\((B_i)_{\rm predecessor}=b\), \((C_{4,i})_{\rm predecessor}=2b-1\),
\(T_{\rm predecessor}=T\), and \(\beta_{\rm predecessor}=0\).
Its \(g_i,\Gamma,A_T,Z_4,p\) become respectively the present \(g_i\) on the active coordinates, \(\Gamma^{\rm act},A_T^{\rm act},Z_4^{\rm act},p^{\rm act}\). The predecessor imposes no cap condition; its cost window and all remaining hypotheses hold. Its theorem therefore gives
\(M_3(p^{\rm act})\ge\kappa_{\rm allwindow}8^{-(m+1)}\), with depths computed using nonzero-normal halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\).

For completeness, these lower-dimensional depths equal the ambient depths of the embedded conditional law. Let
\(\iota(\mathbf x,t)=(x_1,\ldots,x_m,0,\ldots,0,t)\)
and let \(p^{\rm core}\) be the law on \(\iota(A_T^{\rm act})\) assigning mass \(p^{\rm act}_{\mathbf z}\) to \(\iota(\mathbf z)\). Any ambient closed halfspace has the form
\(H=\lbrace(\mathbf u,s):\sum_{i=1}^k a_i u_i+a_t s\ge h\rbrace\)
with \((a_1,\ldots,a_k,a_t)\ne\mathbf0\). If its projected normal
\((a_1,\ldots,a_m,a_t)\) is nonzero, its inverse image under \(\iota\) is exactly a permitted halfspace of \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\). If that projected normal is zero and \(H\) contains \(\iota(\mathbf z)\), then \(0\ge h\), so the inverse image is all of \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\), of conditional probability one. Conversely, every permitted halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\) extends to an ambient permitted halfspace by putting zero coefficients on the inactive coordinates. Taking infima over halfspaces containing the corresponding root consequently yields the exact equality
\(\tau_{p^{\rm core}}(\iota(\mathbf z))=\tau_{p^{\rm act}}(\mathbf z)\);
the extra projected-zero halfspaces cannot reduce the infimum, because their probability is one.

At every core atom, its original probability is
\(p_{\iota(\mathbf z)}=\vartheta p^{\rm act}_{\mathbf z}\).
Every ambient halfspace containing that atom has full original probability at least \(\vartheta\) times its \(p^{\rm core}\) probability. Taking infima and using the just-proved depth equality gives
\(\tau_p(\iota(\mathbf z))\ge\vartheta\tau_{p^{\rm act}}(\mathbf z)\).
Sampling only these original roots, and retaining all original probabilities, proves the fourth-power transfer
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T^{\rm act}}
       \vartheta p^{\rm act}_{\mathbf z}
       [\vartheta\tau_{p^{\rm act}}(\mathbf z)]^3\\
&=\vartheta^4M_3(p^{\rm act})
\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm allwindow}}{16}\,8^{-(m+1)}
\ge\frac{\kappa_{\rm allwindow}}{16}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality uses \(m\le k\). Relative to the ambient scale, the core estimate gains the factor \(8^{k-m}\); one power of \(\vartheta\) comes from original root sampling and three from the depth comparison. This proves the stated original moment bound for the complete family.
