## Statement

Use the canonical definitions of \(D,W,w_{\max},\phi,\mathcal E_{\mathbf J},G_E,m_{\log}\), and \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) from accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The unadorned \(B\) retains its meaning \(\max_D\phi\). No reflected-pair proxy is used.

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \(w=(w_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be any globally extendable nonzero finite table in the sense of `488f0a583886b5fc`. There exist a globally extendable nonzero finite table \(v=(v_{\mathbf X})_{\mathbf X\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}}\), invariant under every coordinate reflection \(X_i\mapsto1-X_i\), an integer \(R\), and an integer affine injection
\[
\iota:\mathbb Z^n\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{n+1}
\]
with the following properties. Put \(h(\mathbf X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}X_i\), let \(D_v=\{\mathbf X:v_{\mathbf X}>0\}\), and set
\[
\begin{aligned}
F&=\{\mathbf X\in D_v:h(\mathbf X)=R\}=\iota(D),\\
\omega^{(t)}_{\mathbf X}&=v_{\mathbf X}\exp\bigl(t(h(\mathbf X)-R)\bigr)\qquad(t\ge0),\\
r_t&=\sum_{\mathbf X\in D_v\setminus F}\omega^{(t)}_{\mathbf X}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(F\) is an exposed lattice face, rather than a hitting set. The table \(\omega^{(t)}\) is a positive common scaling of the allowed affine tilt \(v_{\mathbf X}\exp(t\mathbf1\cdot\mathbf X)\). Its canonical positive support is \(D_v\), its total mass is \(W+r_t\), its maximum atom is \(w_{\max}\), and
\[
\omega^{(t)}_{\iota(\mathbf I)}=w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad
0\le r_t\le |D_v\setminus F|w_{\max}e^{-t}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(\mathbf J\in D\), every \(L\ge0\), and every \(t\ge0\),
\[
m_{\log}(\iota(\mathbf J),L;\omega^{(t)})
=m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w).
\tag{2}
\]
For every \(\mathbf X\in D_v\setminus F\), every \(L\ge0\), and every \(t\ge0\),
\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf X,L;\omega^{(t)})\le r_t.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\le\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(\omega^{(t)})
\le\max\{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w),r_t\}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, the two scores in (4) are exactly equal for every sufficiently large finite \(t\), and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}
\frac{2^{n+1}\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(\omega^{(t)})}{W+r_t}
=2\,\frac{2^n\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}{W}.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\gamma_{\mathrm{all}}\) be the infimum of \(2^n\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)/W\) over all dimensions \(n\ge1\) and all globally extendable nonzero finite tables. Let \(\gamma_{\mathrm{tilt}}\) be the same infimum restricted to tables \(w_{\mathbf I}=v_{\mathbf I}e^{\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\), where \(v\) satisfies the complete coordinate-reflection hypothesis and \(\theta\in\mathbb R^n\) is arbitrary. Then
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{all}}\le\gamma_{\mathrm{tilt}}\le2\gamma_{\mathrm{all}}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus either class has a universal positive full cap coefficient if and only if the other does. More specifically, an arbitrary-tilt coefficient \(c>0\) implies the unrestricted coefficient \(c/2\) in every original integer dimension. Any sequence of unrestricted normalized full cap scores tending to zero gives a sequence in the affine-tilted reflection class with normalized full cap scores tending to zero.

## Proof

By accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, \(D\) is lattice-convex: \(\operatorname{conv}D\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). Its canonical potential values are nonnegative and convex-admissible at every integer barycenter, and \(w_{\mathbf I}=w_{\max}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\).

For \(1\le i\le n\), choose
\[
a_i=1-\min_{\mathbf I\in D}I_i,
\qquad
R=1+\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\sum_{i=1}^n(I_i+a_i),
\]
and define
\[
\iota(\mathbf x)=
\left(x_1+a_1,\ldots,x_n+a_n,
R-\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i+a_i)\right).
\tag{7}
\]
This is an affine injection on \(\mathbb R^n\), maps integer points to integer points, and satisfies \(h(\iota(\mathbf x))=R\). All coordinates of every \(\iota(\mathbf I)\), \(\mathbf I\in D\), are at least one.

Let \(\Sigma\) be the finite group generated by the \(n+1\) coordinate reflections about \(1/2\). Form the set of generators
\[
\mathcal A=\{\sigma\iota(\mathbf I):\mathbf I\in D,\ \sigma\in\Sigma\},
\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal A.
\]
Each generator determines \(\sigma\) uniquely from which coordinates are at most zero, because every unreflected coordinate is at least one. After recovering \(\sigma\), the injection recovers \(\mathbf I\) uniquely. Therefore assigning the generator \(\sigma\iota(\mathbf I)\) the value \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\) is unambiguous. Define its convex roof on \(Q\) by
\[
V(\mathbf x)=\min\left\{
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D,\,\sigma\in\Sigma}
\lambda_{\mathbf I,\sigma}\phi_{\mathbf I}:
\lambda_{\mathbf I,\sigma}\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I,\sigma}\lambda_{\mathbf I,\sigma}=1,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I,\sigma}\lambda_{\mathbf I,\sigma}
\sigma\iota(\mathbf I)=\mathbf x
\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The minimum exists because its feasible set is a nonempty closed subset of a finite probability simplex. Combining feasible coefficient vectors proves convexity of \(V\), and its values lie in \([0,B]\). Applying any coordinate reflection permutes the generators and preserves their assigned values. The reflections are affine, so they preserve each barycentric relation in (8). Applying a reflection to a feasible representation and then applying it again proves \(V(\sigma\mathbf x)=V(\mathbf x)\). The same argument gives \(\sigma Q=Q\).

If \(\sigma\) reflects a nonempty set \(T\) of coordinates, then
\[
h(\sigma\iota(\mathbf I))
=R-\sum_{i\in T}(2\iota(\mathbf I)_i-1)
\le R-1.
\tag{9}
\]
Every unreflected generator has height \(R\). It follows, by taking the height of a convex combination, that
\[
Q\cap\{h=R\}=\iota(\operatorname{conv}D),
\qquad Q\subseteq\{h\le R\}.
\tag{10}
\]
An integer point in the face in (10) has integer first \(n\) coordinates. Subtracting \(\mathbf a\) recovers an integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}D\), hence a member of \(D\). Therefore
\[
Q\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\cap\{h=R\}=\iota(D).
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover, every representation in (8) of \(\iota(\mathbf J)\), \(\mathbf J\in D\), uses only unreflected generators, since all other generators have strictly smaller height. Such a representation is exactly an affine image of a barycentric representation of \(\mathbf J\) by original sites. Convex-admissibility gives objective at least \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\), while the singleton achieves that objective. Thus
\[
V(\iota(\mathbf J))=\phi_{\mathbf J}.
\tag{12}
\]

Define on all of \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\)
\[
f_v(\mathbf x)=
\begin{cases}
w_{\max}e^{-V(\mathbf x)},&\mathbf x\in Q,\\
0,&\mathbf x\notin Q,
\end{cases}
\qquad v_{\mathbf X}=f_v(\mathbf X)\quad(\mathbf X\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}).
\tag{13}
\]
This is finite-valued and log-concave: for two points in \(Q\), use its convexity and that of \(V\); if either endpoint lies outside \(Q\), the right side of the log-concavity inequality is zero. Since \(Q\) is compact, \(D_v=Q\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\) is finite. Equations (8) and (13) give reflection invariance at every lattice point, including all zeros. Equations (11) and (12) give the asserted face and its original weights. Also \(v_{\mathbf X}\le w_{\max}\) on its support. A face site corresponding to an original maximum has \(V=0\), so it has weight \(w_{\max}\).

Multiplication of (13) by \(\exp(t(h-R))\) provides a global log-concave extension of \(\omega^{(t)}\), since \(h-R\) is affine. Its support remains \(D_v\). Equations (10) and (12) show that its face weights are the original weights, and every off-face lattice site has integer height at most \(R-1\). Thus \(\omega^{(t)}_{\mathbf X}\le w_{\max}e^{-t}\) off the face, proving (1). The maximum atom remains exactly \(w_{\max}\). Its canonical potential, denoted \(\phi^{(t)}_{\mathbf X}\) to distinguish it from the original dimension's values, is consequently
\[
\phi^{(t)}_{\mathbf X}=V(\mathbf X)+t(R-h(\mathbf X)).
\tag{14}
\]
For any barycentric support at any center, the affine term in (14) cancels in its gap. At a face center, something stronger holds: every positive-coefficient barycentric support must lie entirely in the face. Indeed, its average height is \(R\), each endpoint height is at most \(R\), and a positive coefficient on a lower-height site would make that average strictly less than \(R\).

The affine injection (7) preserves and reflects affine independence, positive barycentric coordinates, and membership in the relative interior of the convex hull of a support. Consequently, at \(\iota(\mathbf J)\), the full family \(\mathcal E_{\iota(\mathbf J)}\) is in bijection with the original family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\). Equation (12), or equivalently (14) on the face, makes each associated gap exactly its original \(G_E\). Thus their low-cost families agree under this bijection at every cutoff. Their site capacities also agree exactly. A set hits this new family if and only if its intersection with \(F\), pulled back by \(\iota\), hits the original family. Off-face atoms never help to hit a face support and have positive weights. Minimizing the full-occurrence integral hitting cost therefore gives (2), with no change of capacities or cost normalization.

At a center \(\mathbf X\notin F\), every barycentric support has at least one off-face atom, because a barycenter of face atoms remains on the face. Therefore the set \(D_v\setminus F\) hits every barycentric support at this center, including every member of every low-cost family. Its total original tilted capacity is \(r_t\). This proves (3) uniformly over all these centers and all cutoffs, including the singleton and cutoff zero.

Dividing (2) and (3) by \(1+L\), optimizing over cutoffs, and then optimizing over all centers gives (4). Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge w_{\max}>0\) by choosing an original maximum as center and using its compulsory singleton. Since (1) gives \(r_t\to0\), inequality (4) is an equality for every sufficiently large finite \(t\). The total tilted mass is exactly \(W+r_t\), which proves (5).

Common positive table scaling changes neither a barycentric gap nor a low-cost family, multiplies every hitting mass and score by that scalar, and multiplies total mass by the same scalar. Thus the normalized value in (5) is also the normalized value for the exactly prescribed tilt \(v_{\mathbf X}\exp(t\mathbf1\cdot\mathbf X)\).

Every affine tilt of a globally log-concave extension is globally log-concave, so the tilted reflection class is a subclass of the unrestricted class. This proves the first inequality of (6). Conversely, for every original table the tilted reflection infimum is at most each normalized value of its construction, hence at most the limit (5). Taking the infimum over original tables proves the second inequality of (6).

If the tilted reflection class satisfies coefficient \(c>0\), apply it in dimension \(n+1\) to this construction, and let \(t\to\infty\). Equations (4) and (1) give \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge c2^{-(n+1)}W\). This is precisely the claimed unrestricted coefficient \(c/2\). For a sequence of unrestricted scores tending to zero, choose a sufficiently large finite tilt separately for each table so that the scores in (4) agree exactly. Its normalized score in dimension \(n+1\) is then at most twice the original normalized score, because \(W+r_t\ge W\). This yields the asserted asymptotic transfer with all centers included.
