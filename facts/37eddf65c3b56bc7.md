## Statement

Let $\mu$ be the mixed volume of a compact convex $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, of positive mass $M$, and let $k$ be an integer median of its base marginal. Write $L,R$ for the restrictions to the strict sides, of masses $A,B$, and $\mu_k$ for the restriction to base $k$. For $A,B>0$ let $P$ be the law of the point at base $k$ on the segment between independent $X\sim L/A$ and $Y\sim R/B$. Then $\eta=\mu_k+\min(A,B)P$ is carried by the actual fiber $C_k$, with mass $W=M-\max(A,B)\ge M/2$, and $\eta(H)\le\mu(H)$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$. If one side has zero mass use $\eta=\mu_k$. For any finite menu of closed ambient halfspaces $H_i$ and $\lambda_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf1_{H_i}\ge1$ on the actual fiber at $k$, one has $\sum_i\lambda_i\mu(H_i)\ge W$.

## Proof

For endpoints at bases $p<k<q$, the segment image is $Z=((q-k)X+(k-p)Y)/(q-p)$. It belongs to the actual fiber by convexity. The complement of a closed halfspace is convex, so $\mathbf1_H(Z)\le\mathbf1_H(X)+\mathbf1_H(Y)$. Integrating and multiplying by $\min(A,B)$ gives $\min(A,B)P(H)\le L(H)+R(H)$, since both ratios $\min(A,B)/A$ and $\min(A,B)/B$ are at most one. Adding the full central mass yields the halfspace inequality. Its total mass is $M-A-B+\min(A,B)=W$, also valid when one side vanishes. The median condition gives $A,B\le M/2$, so the probability $\eta/W$ is dominated by $2\mu/M$ on halfspaces.

For the weighted cover, integrate the pointwise inequality against $\eta$. Its support condition yields $W\le\sum_i\lambda_i\eta(H_i)\le\sum_i\lambda_i\mu(H_i)$.
