## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, and let its compatible compact convex $d$-fibers have positive volumes $V_z$, $d\ge1$, and total mass $M$. Let $k$ be the number of occupied parities. Use the invariant buffered depth of fact f2ead43a963c7026 at fixed $\varepsilon>0$ with positive affine multiplier $\ell$ satisfying $\sum_z V_z\log\ell(z)=0$. Let $Q$ be its global raw maximum and $I$ the set of bases with a maximizing mark. Suppose every $p\in I$ is a vertex of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ and its atomic base depth equals $Q$.

Contract all $p$-fibers for $p\in I$ by a common factor $1-t$ about arbitrary points in their interiors, and use the scalar-renormalized affine multiplier. The new family is actual, positive and compatible. With $r=(1-t)^d$ and $s=|I|$, its normalized maximum is at most
$$
\frac{rQ}{M-sQ(1-r)}
$$
for all sufficiently small $t>0$. This is strictly below $Q/M$ if $I\ne P$. If $I=P$, then $Q/M=1/k>(d/(d+1))^d/k$.

## Proof

A vertex can be isolated by a closed base halfspace, and any halfspace containing the base includes its own weight. Thus $V_p=b_{\rm base}(p)=Q$ for $p\in I$. A vertex has no nontrivial convex representation by other bases. Thus every anchored compatibility inclusion into a changed target is tautological, and every inclusion into an unchanged target is preserved because every changed input shrinks. Consequently the new actual fibers, realized by the convex hull of their lifts, have total volume $M(t)=M-sQ(1-r)$ and every changed fiber has volume $rQ$.

Let $\ell_t=c(t)\ell$ with
$$
c(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{\sum_z V_z(t)\log\ell(z)}{M(t)}\right).
$$
This gives the required weighted geometric normalization and $c(t)\to1$. The covariance and projective energy in the objective are those of the changed family. Fact f2ead43a963c7026 applies: the fixed labeled positive fibers converge in Hausdorff distance and the multipliers converge uniformly on matching marks because $\ell_t$ depends only on the base and $c(t)\to1$. The outside bases have a strict old gap, since there are finitely many bases and the depth on each compact fiber attains its maximum. For sufficiently small $t$ their new maxima are less than $rQ$, by this uniform depth continuity and $rQ\to Q$. Every new mark over $I$ has depth at most $rQ$ by the pure-base singleton cap. Thus the new global raw maximum is at most $rQ$. If $I\ne P$, then $M>sQ$ and $r<1$, which gives the strict normalized comparison.

If $I=P$, all sites are vertices and have equal volume $Q$. Two distinct vertices of the same parity would have an integer midpoint in $P$ by lattice completeness. The midpoint would fail to be a vertex, a contradiction. Hence $s=k$ and $M=kQ$, which yields the stated comparison since $(d/(d+1))^d<1$.
