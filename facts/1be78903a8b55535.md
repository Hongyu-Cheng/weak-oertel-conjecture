## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Retain the exact six-site data of accepted fact `6aa711c2cd5e2a52`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf a&=(2,-1),&\mathbf b&=(-2,1),&\mathbf c&=(0,1),&
\mathbf u&=(1,0),&\mathbf v&=(-1,1),\\
D&=\{\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c,\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v\},&
P&=\operatorname{conv}D,\\
p_{\mathbf a}=p_{\mathbf b}&=\frac1{16},&
p_{\mathbf0}&=\frac18,&
p_{\mathbf u}=p_{\mathbf v}=p_{\mathbf c}&=\frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]

Set \(p=0\) off \(D\). Define \(\tau:D\to\{\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}\) by \(\tau(\mathbf a)=\tau(\mathbf b)=\tau(\mathbf0)=\mathbf0\) and \(\tau(\mathbf K)=\mathbf K\) for \(\mathbf K\in\{\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}\).

For every integer \(m\ge1\), work in the original block Euclidean lattice \(\mathbb Z^{2m}=(\mathbb Z^2)^m\). Put \(D^{(m)}=D^m\), \(P^{(m)}=P^m\), and \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}=\prod_{h=1}^m p_{\mathbf I_h}\) for \(\mathbf I=(\mathbf I_1,\ldots,\mathbf I_m)\in D^{(m)}\), with zero mass elsewhere. Write \(\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I)=(\tau(\mathbf I_1),\ldots,\tau(\mathbf I_m))\). This is a probability table with \(D^{(m)}=\operatorname{conv}(D^{(m)})\cap\mathbb Z^{2m}\) and a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with all lattice values, including every zero. Its normalization to accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` is \(w=p^{(m)}\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=4^{-m}\), and
\(\phi^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}=\log(4^{-m}/p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I})=\sum_{h=1}^m\log((1/4)/p_{\mathbf I_h})\).

Apply the canonical objects of accepted fact `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc` with \((n,D,p)=(2m,D^{(m)},p^{(m)})\), attaching the superscript \((m)\) to distinguish them from the one-block objects. Explicitly, for indices in \(D^{(m)}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F^{(m)}&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\ (\mathbf I\in D^{(m)}),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\ (\mathbf J\in D^{(m)})\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
V^{(m)}(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2,\\
H^{(m)}(r)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}},\qquad 0\log0=0.

Let \(\mathcal F^{(m)}_{\min}\) be the entire minimizing face of \(V^{(m)}\), and let \(r^{(m),\star}\) maximize \(H^{(m)}\) on that face. As in the predecessor, \(\mathcal T^{(m)}\) comprises the ordered pairs positive in some minimizing array, \(\mathcal J^{(m)}\) their target projection, and \(D^{(m)}_{\mathbf J}=\{\mathbf I:(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T^{(m)}\}\). \[

\min_{r\in\mathcal F^{(m)}}V^{(m)}(r)&=m,\qquad
r^{(m),\star}_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\,\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf J=\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I)\}},\\
\mathcal T^{(m)}&=\{(\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I),\mathbf I):\mathbf I\in D^{(m)}\},\qquad
\mathcal J^{(m)}=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\}^m,\\
q^{(m),\star}_{\mathbf J}&=4^{-m}\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J^{(m)}),\qquad
q^{(m),\star}_{\mathbf J}=0\quad(\mathbf J\notin\mathcal J^{(m)}),\\
D^{(m)}_{\mathbf J}&=\prod_{h=1}^m\tau^{-1}(\{\mathbf J_h\})
\quad(\mathbf J\in\mathcal J^{(m)}).

Thus there are \(4^m\) positive targets and \(6^m\) positive array entries; every other entry is forced to vanish throughout the optimal face. If \(z(\mathbf J)=|\{h:\mathbf J_h=\mathbf0\}|\), its original source capacity is \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf J}=4^{-m}2^{-z(\mathbf J)}\), its row support has size \(3^{z(\mathbf J)}\), and its original logarithmic gap is \(z(\mathbf J)\log2/2\). ## Proof

Accepted fact `6aa711c2cd5e2a52` proves \(P\cap\mathbb Z^2=D\), with \(P=\{(x,y):y\le1,\ x+y\le1,\ x+2y\ge0\}\). A tuple of convex combinations of points in \(D\) is a convex combination of points in \(D^m\), using products of the coefficients. Hence \(\operatorname{conv}(D^m)=P^m\), whose complete intersection with \(\mathbb Z^{2m}\) is \((P\cap\mathbb Z^2)^m=D^m\). The product masses sum to one. On \(P^m\), define

\[
f^{(m)}((x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_m,y_m))
=4^{-m}\exp\!\left[-(\log2)\sum_{h=1}^m
\max\{0,1-x_h-2y_h,-2x_h-2y_h,-2y_h\}\right],
\]

and set it equal to zero outside \(P^m\). The one-block maximum has values two at \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\), one at \(\mathbf0\), and zero at \(\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf c\). This proves agreement with every product mass. Agreement at all other lattice points follows from the complete support equality. The exponent's negative is convex, being a sum of maxima of affine functions. Therefore \(f^{(m)}\) is log-concave between points of \(P^m\); if either endpoint is outside \(P^m\), the required right-hand side is zero. It is finite-valued everywhere and has maximum \(4^{-m}\), proving the stated normalization and the hypotheses of `57c57a5fe0d4d1cc` and `488f0a583886b5fc`.

Denote the one-block optimizer by \(r^{(1),\star}\), an explicit alias for \(r^\star\) in `6aa711c2cd5e2a52`. That fact proves that its entire variance-minimal face is a singleton, its minimum is one, and
\(r^{(1),\star}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}=p_{\mathbf L}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf K=\tau(\mathbf L)\}}\), with four row masses \(1/4\).

Take any \(r\in\mathcal F^{(m)}\), without assuming any product structure. For each block \(h\), define its two-coordinate assignment marginal by
\(r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}=\sum_{\mathbf J:\mathbf J_h=\mathbf K}\sum_{\mathbf I:\mathbf I_h=\mathbf L}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), where \(\mathbf K,\mathbf L\in D\). Its column sums and vector row moments satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf K\in D}r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I:\mathbf I_h=\mathbf L}p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf L},\\
\sum_{\mathbf L\in D}r^{[h]}_{\mathbf K,\mathbf L}(\mathbf L-\mathbf K)
&=\sum_{\mathbf J:\mathbf J_h=\mathbf K}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D^{(m)}}
r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I_h-\mathbf J_h)=\mathbf0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equality uses the \(h\)-th two-dimensional block of each full row moment equation. Thus \(r^{[h]}\) is a feasible one-block array. The actual block Euclidean norm gives

\[
V^{(m)}(r)
=\sum_{h=1}^m\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf J_h\|^2
=\sum_{h=1}^m V^{(1)}(r^{[h]})\ge m.
\]

The trial array \(\overline r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=\prod_{h=1}^m r^{(1),\star}_{\mathbf J_h,\mathbf I_h}\) attains this lower bound. Indeed, its column sums factor into \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\), and the \(h\)-th block of its row moment is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I}\overline r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I_h-\mathbf J_h)
=\left(\sum_{\mathbf L\in D}r^{(1),\star}_{\mathbf J_h,\mathbf L}(\mathbf L-\mathbf J_h)\right)
\prod_{\ell\ne h}q^{(1),\star}_{\mathbf J_\ell}=\mathbf0.
\]

Each of its assignment marginals equals \(r^{(1),\star}\), so its objective is \(m\). Empty products are one, so these statements also apply when \(m=1\).

Now let \(r\) be any minimizing array. Since its objective is a sum of \(m\) quantities each at least one, every block marginal has objective one. The exact one-block uniqueness forces \(r^{[h]}=r^{(1),\star}\) for every \(h\). If \(\mathbf J\ne\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I)\), choose a block with \(\mathbf J_h\ne\tau(\mathbf I_h)\). Then \(r^{[h]}_{\mathbf J_h,\mathbf I_h}=0\), and its defining sum of nonnegative entries includes \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\). Hence that full entry is zero. Only the target \(\tau^{(m)}(\mathbf I)\) remains possible for each source \(\mathbf I\), and its column sum forces the remaining entry to equal \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\). This proves uniqueness on the entire face and every asserted structural zero. The argument uses the original Euclidean objective without a coordinate change.

The inverse images of the four one-block targets have total source mass \(1/4\): at zero it is \(1/16+1/8+1/16\), and at each other target it is its singleton mass. Thus the product formula gives every positive target mass \(4^{-m}\) and the stated row supports. All \(6^m\) product source masses are positive. Since every positive assignment share \(r^{(m),\star}_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\) is one, the conditional assignment entropy is zero. Uniformity on \(4^m\) targets gives target entropy \(\log(4^m)=2m\log2\).

The normalized row distribution at \(\mathbf J\) is the product of its normalized one-block row distributions, because both the entries and row masses factor. A zero block assigns probabilities \(1/2,1/4,1/4\) to \(\mathbf0,\mathbf a,\mathbf b\); every other block is its target singleton. The former block's original gap equals
\(\log(1/8)-\tfrac12\log(1/8)-\tfrac14\log(1/16)-\tfrac14\log(1/16)=\tfrac12\log2\), and each singleton gap is zero. Since \(\log p^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}=\sum_h\log p_{\mathbf I_h}\), the full row gap is \(z(\mathbf J)\log2/2\). Under the uniform target law each block is zero with probability \(1/4\), so the mean of \(z(\mathbf J)\) is \(m/4\). This proves \(\mathcal G_m=m\log2/8\). The entropy diagnostic pair is exactly \((0,0)\), whereas this gap tends to infinity. Taking \(m>8c/\log2\) disproves the constant repair; the same divergence disproves every allowance finite at \((0,0)\).

Finally, use exactly the logarithmic support family and hitting definition of `488f0a583886b5fc` on the original table \(p^{(m)}\). The singleton \(\{\mathbf J_u\}\) is affinely independent, has barycentric coefficient one, and has logarithmic cost zero. Every set hitting all original supports at cutoff zero must therefore contain \(\mathbf J_u\). Its weight, computed with all original capacities, is at least \(p^{(m)}_{\mathbf J_u}=4^{-m}\). Optimizing the center and cutoff preserves this lower bound, whose cutoff denominator is one. This proves the hitting calibration without modifying capacities or asserting exact body realizability of the scalar table.
