## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $\ell>0$, $0\le c\le\ell$, and let $L\subseteq\mathbb R^{d-1}$ be a centrally symmetric convex body centered at zero, of volume $V>0$. Define the compact convex body
$$
C=\{(z,x,w): |z|\le1,\ -\ell\le x\le\ell+4c(1-|z|),\ w\in L\}
$$
and let $\mu$ sum $d$-volume over its integer fibers. Its mass is $M=(6\ell+4c)V$. The point $Y=(0,c,0)$ attains global maximum raw closed-halfspace depth $B=(3\ell+c)V$. If $v$ is a nonzero linear functional on $\mathbb R^{d-1}$, $\varepsilon>0$, $\varepsilon\max_{w\in L}|v(w)|<\ell+c$ and $A>\ell+c+\varepsilon\max_{w\in L}|v(w)|$, the three closed ambient caps
$$
H_0=\{x\ge c\},\qquad H_\pm=\{x\le c\pm(Az+\varepsilon v(w))\}
$$
are an irredundant cover of the mixed set, each with mass $B$ and critical at $Y$. Their continuous normals admit a positive linear dependence and span dimension two. Every fiber has mass strictly below $M/2$ exactly when $c<\ell/2$.

## Proof

The roof in $x$ is concave in $z$. The integer slices are exactly the products of $L$ with the three intervals in the paired-threshold lemma. Their volumes are $2\ell V,(2\ell+4c)V,2\ell V$.

To lower bound the depth of $Y$, move any containing closed halfspace parallel to itself until its boundary passes through $Y$, decreasing its mass. If its coefficient of $x$ is positive, divide by that coefficient so that for each $w$ its fiber intervals are tested by $x-c+az\ge s(w)$, where $a$ is fixed and $s$ is linear in $w$. Central symmetry pairs the integrals at $w$ and $-w$. Since $s(-w)=-s(w)$, the paired-tail lemma with $r=|s(w)|$ gives mass at least $(M_0-2c)V/2=B$. For a negative coefficient of $x$, normalization instead gives $x-c+az\le s(w)$ with $a$ fixed and $s$ linear. Every equality section is length-null in the $x$ interval, so its paired lower-tail mass before integrating is $2M_0-G_a(r)-G_a(-r)$. The upper bound of the same lemma gives the same $B$. When the coefficient of $x$ is zero the pushforward measure on $(z,w)$ is centrally symmetric because the endpoint weights coincide and $L=-L$. Every closed halfspace through its center then takes at least $M/2\ge B$. This includes pure-base cuts and keeps their full boundary mass.

The margins $m_0=x-c$, $m_\pm=c\pm(Az+\varepsilon v(w))-x$ satisfy $2m_0+m_++m_-=0$, so their closed nonnegative halfspaces cover the ambient space. Their continuous normals inherit the dependence and have rank two because $v\ne0$ and $\varepsilon>0$. All three boundaries contain $Y$. The cap $H_0$ has retained lengths $\ell-c,\ell+3c,\ell-c$. At $z=\pm1$ the tilted caps are oppositely full or empty, since $A>\ell+c+\varepsilon\max_L|v|$. At $z=0$ their thresholds stay strictly inside $[-\ell,\ell+4c]$, and their retained lengths average to $\ell+c$ over $L$ by the zero mean of $v(w)$. Each of the three caps therefore has mass $B$. The cover places every candidate mixed point in a cap of mass $B$, so the global maximum is at most $B$. The lower bound at $Y$ proves equality and criticality of the entire listed menu. Irredundancy follows by taking $w=0$, $z=0$, $x>c$ in the middle fiber to witness $H_0$ alone and taking $w=0$, $x=-\ell/2$ at $z=1,-1$ to witness $H_+$ and $H_-$ alone. Finally the middle fiber is a largest one, and $(2\ell+4c)V<M/2$ is equivalent to $c<\ell/2$.
