## Statement

Use finitely many positive, full-dimensional common-normal simplex fibers of an actual compatible body on a lattice-complete finite base. Let $M$ be their summed $d$-volume, $\beta$ their normalized maximum depth and $Q=\beta M$. Take a finite-dimensional polyhedral cone $V$ of actual one-sided compatible variations with uniform $O(t)$ radial displacements about old interior centers, regular radial first derivatives and outward normal speeds $r_l(v)$ constant on each old facet $F_l$ and linear in $v\in V$. Write $A_l=|F_l|_{d-1}$ and $m(v)=\sum_l A_l r_l(v)$. Count endpoints when $d=1$.

For each of finitely many deepest marks take a finite menu of circuits of actual nonvertical mass-$Q$ caps through the mark, each with positive weights $\rho_{cj}$ and balanced unit continuous normals. Require the old weighted margin of each listed circuit $c$ to be nonnegative on its assigned base planes. A cap occurrence is an independently adjustable copy within the selected circuit. Let $S_j>0$ be the sum of proper section areas, let $I_j,O_j$ be its inside and outside supporting facet lists, and let $B_j(v)$ be its strict-captured boundary flux, omitting coincidences. The raw response to raising the threshold with speed $s_j$ is
$$
D_j(v,s_j)=B_j(v)-S_js_j+
\sum_{l\in I_j} A_l\min(r_l(v),-s_j)+
\sum_{l\in O_j} A_l\max(r_l(v)-s_j,0).
$$
For each circuit there is a unique common response $q_c(v)$ for shifts with $\sum_{j\in c}\rho_{cj}s_j=0$. It is continuous, positively homogeneous and piecewise linear. There are shifts satisfying $\sum_j\rho_{cj}s_j\le0$ and $D_j(v,s_j)<\beta m(v)$ on every cap of $c$ exactly when $q_c(v)<\beta m(v)$. The finite-menu test is
$$
\exists v\in V\qquad
\max_i\min_{c\ {\rm in\ menu}\ i}\,[q_c(v)-\beta m(v)]<0.
$$
If a finite family of cheaper full-plane covers on the remaining bases retains uniform slack for the actual variation, a successful test strictly decreases the actual normalized maximum depth.

Equivalently, choose one circuit per group and one binary body selector $\alpha_{jl}$ per inside supporting facet. For this choice solve one linear feasibility problem in $v,s$ with $v\in V$, the weighted shift inequalities, and the normalized response upper bounds $\le-1$ for every binary outside branch on each chosen cap. Failure for a fixed selection and inside choice is equivalent to an opposing normalized nonnegative stress. If $\lambda_j$ is its cap weight and $\sigma_{jl}$ the averaged outside selector, set
$$
T_j=S_j+\sum_{l\in I_j}A_l(1-\alpha_{jl})+
\sum_{l\in O_j}A_l\sigma_{jl}.
$$
For every group $i$ there is $k_i\ge0$ with $\lambda_j T_j=k_i\rho_{c_i j}$ on its caps. The allocated body trace is the strict cap bit off coincidences, $\alpha_{jl}$ inside and $\sigma_{jl}$ outside, with the same $\sigma$ used in $T_j$. In a positive group the weights normalized within the group are proportional to $\rho_{c_i j}/T_j$. Absence of descent means existence of an opposing stress for every selected circuit tuple and every binary inside choice.

Without supporting facets, for finitely many admissible nonnegative potentials $\Phi_h$ with outward speeds $-\Phi_h$ and unit marked-boundary integral, let $A_{ic,h}$ be their efficiencies against the regular profile of circuit $c$ in group $i$. The best common efficiency over the simplex of mixtures $a$ is $\max_{(c_i)}\max_a\min_i\sum_h A_{ic_i,h}a_h$, a finite maximum of LP values.

## Proof

Apply the radial moving-cap calculation in 93cbd92127937644 to the actual paths, with margin speed $-s_j$ and constant body speed on each facet. Its body-inside minimum, body-outside positive part and ordinary proper-section term give the formula for $D_j$. The total derivative is the integral of the normal speed, as follows from the same signed radial shell with no cap restriction. All formulas are summed over actual fibers. For each selected circuit, the weighted new margin on an assigned base equals the old margin minus $t\sum_j\rho_{cj}s_j$. Thus the shift inequality gives exact closed full-plane coverage for $t\ge0$.

For fixed $v$, each $D_j(v,s)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing onto $\mathbb R$, with decrease at least $S_j$ times the shift increment. Denote its inverse in $s$ at response $q$ by $s_j(v,q)$. The positive weighted sum of the inverse functions decreases continuously from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$, so its zero gives the unique equalization. Choose one linear branch of every minimum or maximum and impose the corresponding weak branch inequalities. The threshold coefficients of the branch equations are $-T_j$ with $T_j\ge S_j$. Solving $D_j=q$ and the weighted shift equation in that chamber has a unique linear solution, since the coefficient of $q$ in the substituted shift equation is $-\sum_j\rho_{cj}/T_j<0$. The resulting finitely many closed polyhedral regions cover $V$, and uniqueness makes their formulas agree on overlaps. This proves continuity, piecewise linearity and positive homogeneity.

A favorable common response gives the equalized shifts. Conversely, if all selected cap responses are below $\beta m(v)$, each $s_j$ exceeds $s_j(v,\beta m(v))$. Feasibility of the weighted shift inequality then makes the weighted inverse sum at $\beta m(v)$ strictly negative, so $\beta m(v)>q_c(v)$. Independent finite menu choices give the stated maximum of group minima. Once a strict direction is found, every selected cap has mass $Q+tD_j+o(t)$ and $M(t)=M+tm+o(t)$. Their ratios are strictly below $\beta$ for small positive $t$. All remaining bases have a finite slack cover by hypothesis, so every actual point has a containing cap with normalized mass below $\beta$.

The inside contribution is a minimum of the two choices $\alpha r_l-(1-\alpha)s_j$. The outside contribution is the maximum of $\kappa(r_l-s_j)$ over $\kappa\in\{0,1\}$. For a successful actual direction choose its minimizing inside branch on each facet and then require all outside branch bounds. In the reverse direction, every chosen inside branch is an upper bound on its true minimum, and imposing all outside bounds tests the true maximum. This proves the order of quantifiers. For a fixed tuple and fixed inside choice let $f_{j\kappa}(v,s)$ be the resulting linear branch response minus $\beta m(v)$. On the polyhedral cone of feasible shifts and body directions, requiring every $f_{j\kappa}<0$ is equivalent by homogeneity and finiteness to requiring each $\le-1$.

For this fixed tuple and inside choice, if no such direction exists, separate the linear image of the cone from the open strictly negative orthant. The separating functional has nonnegative coefficients and is nonzero, so normalize them to $\lambda_{j\kappa}\ge0$ with sum one and $\sum_{j,\kappa}\lambda_{j\kappa}f_{j\kappa}\ge0$ on the feasible cone. Conversely this functional precludes simultaneous strict negativity. Aggregate $\lambda_j=\sum_\kappa\lambda_{j\kappa}$ and average the outside binary selectors on each cap using these conditional weights, choosing any values on zero-weight caps. Because the branches are additive in $\kappa$, only their averages $\sigma_{jl}$ matter. Any such averages are obtainable as convex mixtures of the binary vectors. Testing the shifts alone, the coefficients $-\lambda_jT_j$ must be a nonpositive multiple of the one weighted shift row in that group: they vanish on its nullspace, and testing strictly negative weighted shifts fixes the sign. This gives $\lambda_jT_j=k_i\rho_{c_i j}$. If $k_i=0$, all its weights vanish because $T_j>0$. Otherwise, setting $\Lambda_i=\sum_{j\in c_i}\lambda_j$, one has $\lambda_j=\Lambda_i(\rho_{c_i j}/T_j)/\sum_h(\rho_{c_i h}/T_h)$ and $\sum_i\Lambda_i=1$. Testing $s=0$ leaves the weighted body flux minus $\beta m(v)$ nonnegative for all $v\in V$. On the potential cone $r=-\Phi$ this says $\sum_i\Lambda_i\int\Phi W_i\,d\eta\le\beta\int\Phi\,d\eta$, where $W_i$ is the group-normalized weighted allocated trace on the marked boundary $\eta$. Signed body directions impose their corresponding linear tests, including equality when both signs are permitted.

In the regular case $T_j=S_j$ and equalization directly gives $q_c=\sum_j(\rho_{cj}/S_j)B_j/\sum_j\rho_{cj}/S_j$. Thus the efficiency of the mixture $\sum_h a_h\Phi_h$ is $\sum_hA_{ic,h}a_h$ for that circuit. At fixed $a$, $\min_i\max_c\sum_h A_{ic,h}a_h$ equals $\max_{(c_i)}\min_i\sum_h A_{ic_i,h}a_h$, since each group can choose its own maximizer. Maximizing over $a$ now commutes with the finite maximization over the tuple. For a fixed tuple, maximize a scalar bounded above by each group's linear efficiency. This is a single LP. For example, two identical rows $(b,b)$, $b>0$, give common value $b$, while keeping just the two diagonal payments and splitting one unit of mass equally would guarantee $b/2$. The matrix example uses only the algebraic comparison.

For a nonsmooth geometric check take the actual body $[-1,1]\times[-1,1]$ with three integer interval fibers and the critical caps $x-z\ge0$ and $-x+z\ge0$ through $(0,0)$. Reflection gives raw depth $3$ there, and the complementary cap pair of mass $3$ bounds the depth everywhere. Each cap has one proper section, an inside supporting endpoint and an outside supporting endpoint. With cover-equality shifts $s,-s$ the moved caps remain complementary up to mixed-volume-zero boundary, so their responses always sum to $m$. The equalized response is $m/2$. Prescribe inside body selectors $A$ on the left endpoint at $z=-1$ for the plus cap and $B$ on the right endpoint at $z=1$ for the minus cap. Set the outside body selector for the other cap at the same endpoint to $1-A$ or $1-B$, respectively. Then both effective section coefficients are $3-A-B>0$, the two weights $1/2$ satisfy the shift balance, and their allocated body traces sum to one at each endpoint. The stress inequality is equality for every body direction and every inside pair in $[0,1]^2$.
