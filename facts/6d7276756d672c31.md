## Statement

For a finite probability law \(p\) with support \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^N\), define its closed ambient-halfspace depth and normalized second moment by
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
:=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}p(D\cap H),
\qquad
M_2(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^2,
\qquad
\Phi_N(p):=4^N M_2(p).
\]

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Z\) with \(a_i\le b_i\), and put
\[
D=\prod_{i=1}^n\{a_i,\ldots,b_i\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf j)=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf j}}
{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}
\quad(\mathbf j\in D),
\]
where \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^n\) is arbitrary. Let
\[
r:=\bigl|\{i:b_i-a_i\ge2\}\bigr|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_n(p)
\ge
\frac14\exp\!\left(
r\log\frac{32}{27}-2\sqrt{\frac{2r}{3}}
\right)
\ge \frac{e^{-4}}4 .}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(\Phi_n(p)\to\infty\) along every sequence for which \(r\to\infty\), even when all interval lengths and tilts vary. Singleton factors still count in the ambient exponent \(4^n\); no full-dimensionality assumption is made.

The constant in (1) is preserved under the following heterogeneous exposed joins. Let \(h,m\ge1\), let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^h\) be compact and convex, and suppose that the finite nonempty complete label support
\[
V=P\cap\mathbb Z^h
\]
consists entirely of exposed vertices of \(\operatorname{conv}V\). For each \(\mathbf v\in V\), let \(q_{\mathbf v}\) be a finite probability law on a support \(D_{\mathbf v}\subseteq\mathbb Z^m\), let \(\alpha_{\mathbf v}>0\), and suppose \(\sum_{\mathbf v}\alpha_{\mathbf v}=1\). On
\[
D^*=\bigcup_{\mathbf v\in V}
\bigl(\{\mathbf v\}\times D_{\mathbf v}\bigr)
\subseteq\mathbb Z^{h+m}
\]
define
\[
p^*(\mathbf v,\mathbf x)
=\alpha_{\mathbf v}q_{\mathbf v}(\mathbf x).
\]
Then
\[
\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf v,\mathbf x)
=\alpha_{\mathbf v}\tau_{q_{\mathbf v}}(\mathbf x)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Phi_{h+m}(p^*)
=4^h\sum_{\mathbf v\in V}\alpha_{\mathbf v}^3
\Phi_m(q_{\mathbf v}).
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if every \(q_{\mathbf v}\) satisfies
\(\Phi_m(q_{\mathbf v})\ge\kappa\), then
\[
\Phi_{h+m}(p^*)\ge\kappa.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, (3) holds with \(\kappa=e^{-4}/4\) when the \(q_{\mathbf v}\) are arbitrary, label-dependent log-affine laws on \(m\)-dimensional Cartesian products of finite integer intervals, with arbitrary endpoints, lengths, tilts, and singleton factors. Repeated application gives the same constant for every iterated exposed join of this type.

## Proof

Translation of an interval and reflection about its midpoint are affine automorphisms that carry closed halfspaces to closed halfspaces and preserve all relevant masses. It is therefore enough for the one-dimensional estimates to consider
\[
q_j=\frac{t^j}{Z_L(t)},
\qquad
0\le j\le L,
\qquad
Z_L(t)=\sum_{j=0}^L t^j,
\qquad
0<t\le1.
\]
Write
\[
a=-\log t,
\qquad
m_j=\min(j,L-j),
\qquad
s_j=2m_j+1,
\qquad
k_L(t)=\sum_{j=0}^Lq_j^3s_j^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Define a probability law on triples \((J,V,U)\) by
\[
\mathbb P(J=j,V=v,U=u)=\frac{q_j^3}{k_L(t)}
\quad
(-m_j\le v,u\le m_j).
\tag{5}
\]
The normalization in (5) follows directly from (4).

We first prove the two reflection-energy estimates
\[
k_L(t)\ge\frac14\quad(L\ge0),
\qquad
k_L(t)\ge\frac8{27}\quad(L\ge2).
\tag{6}
\]
For \(L=0\), \(k_L=1\). For \(L=1\), both reflected sets are atomic, and
\[
k_L=q_0^3+q_1^3
=1-3q_0q_1\ge\frac14.
\]

Suppose \(L\ge2\), set \(N=L+1\ge3\) and \(z=t^3\), and write
\[
A(z)=1+6z+z^2,
\qquad
S_N(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
\bigl(2\min(j,N-1-j)+1\bigr)^2z^j.
\]
For \(0<z<1\), the infinite geometric sum is
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^2z^j=\frac{A(z)}{(1-z)^3},
\]
and hence, for \(0<t<1\), the corresponding infinite reflection energy is
\[
k_\infty(t)
=(1-t)^3\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^2t^{3j}
=\frac{1+6t^3+t^6}{(1+t+t^2)^3}.
\tag{7}
\]
The exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
&27(1+6t^3+t^6)-8(1+t+t^2)^3\\
&\qquad=(1-t)^2
\left(19(1-t^2)^2+14t(1-t)^2+27t^2\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
shows that \(k_\infty(t)\ge8/27\) for \(0<t<1\); its rational expression in (7) extends continuously to the value \(8/27\) at \(t=1\).

It remains to verify, on every finite interval, that truncation and folding do not decrease the energy below (7). For \(0<z<1\), splitting the sum at the midpoint and summing the two finite quadratic-geometric series gives
\[
S_N(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{A(z)(1-z^N)-4Nz^{N/2}(1-z^2)}{(1-z)^3},
&N\text{ even},\\[1.1em]
\displaystyle
\frac{A(z)(1-z^N)-8Nz^{(N+1)/2}(1-z)}{(1-z)^3},
&N\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
For completeness, (9) can be checked without an infinite-series argument: multiply each asserted equality by \((1-z)^3\); the third finite differences of the quadratic sequence \((2j+1)^2\) vanish away from the endpoints and the fold, and its endpoint and fold coefficients are exactly the two correction terms displayed in (9).

Since
\[
k_L(t)=\frac{S_N(t^3)}{Z_L(t)^3}
=\frac{(1-t)^3S_N(t^3)}{(1-t^N)^3},
\]
the inequality \(k_L(t)\ge k_\infty(t)\) is equivalent to
\[
(1-z)^3S_N(z)\ge A(z)(1-t^N)^3.
\tag{10}
\]
Use
\[
1-t^{3N}-(1-t^N)^3=3t^N(1-t^N).
\tag{11}
\]
In the even case, substitution of (9) into (10), followed by division by \(t^N>0\), reduces (10) to
\[
3A(t^3)(1-t^N)
\ge4Nt^{N/2}(1-t^6).
\tag{12}
\]
If \(t=e^{-c}\) with \(c>0\), then
\[
\frac{1-t^N}{Nt^{N/2}}
=\frac{2\sinh(Nc/2)}{N}
\]
is increasing in real \(N>0\). Indeed, the derivative has the sign of
\(y\cosh y-\sinh y\), whose derivative is \(y\sinh y\ge0\) and whose value at zero is zero. Thus it is enough to prove (12) for the smallest even value \(N=4\). After cancelling \(1-t^2>0\), that case is
\[
3(1+6t^3+t^6)(1+t^2)
\ge16t^2(1+t^2+t^4).
\tag{13}
\]
The difference in (13) equals \((1-t)^2Q(t)\), where
\[
Q(t)=3(1+t^6)+4t^3+2(t^2+t^4)
+6t(1-t)(1-t^3)\ge0.
\]

In the odd case, (9)--(11) reduce (10) to
\[
3A(t^3)(1-t^N)
\ge8Nt^{N/2+3/2}(1-t^3).
\tag{14}
\]
The same monotonicity reduces this to the smallest odd value \(N=3\). After cancellation, the required statement is
\[
A(t^3)\ge8t^3,
\]
which is exactly \((1-t^3)^2\ge0\). This proves (10) for every \(0<t<1\). At \(t=1\), \(k_L(t)=N^{-3}S_N(1)\) is a finite polynomial quotient continuous in \(t\), while the right side of (7) is continuous and equals \(8/27\). Letting \(t\uparrow1\) therefore proves the same finite-interval inequality at \(t=1\). Thus \(k_L\ge8/27\) for \(L\ge2\), and (6) follows in all cases.

We next prove the exact uniform variance estimate needed below:
\[
\operatorname{Var}(aV)\le\frac23,
\tag{15}
\]
with variance zero when \(L\le1\) or \(t=1\). Conditional on \(J=j\), \(V\) is uniform on the integer interval \(\{-m_j,\ldots,m_j\}\), and hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}(aV)
=\frac{a^2}{3}\,
\mathbb E[m_J(m_J+1)].
\tag{16}
\]
Assume \(L\ge2\) and \(0<t<1\), and retain \(z=t^3\in(0,1)\). Let \(G\) have the infinite geometric law
\(\mathbb P(G=j)=(1-z)z^j\), condition it on \(G\le L\), and fold the conditioned variable to
\[
M=\min(G,L-G).
\]
If \(m<L/2\), its folded probability is
\[
\mathbb P(M=m)
=\frac{(1-z)(z^m+z^{L-m})}{1-z^{L+1}}
\ge(1-z)z^m=\mathbb P(G=m),
\tag{17}
\]
and if \(L\) is even and \(m=L/2\), the same assertion follows from
\[
\mathbb P(M=m)
=\frac{(1-z)z^m}{1-z^{L+1}}.
\tag{18}
\]
The folded law has no mass above \(\lfloor L/2\rfloor\). Equations (17)--(18) therefore show that \(M\) is stochastically dominated by \(G\).

Let \(w(m)=(2m+1)^2\), an increasing function. Stochastic domination gives
\(\mathbb E w(M)\le\mathbb E w(G)\). After size-biasing both laws by \(w\), their probability ratio on the finite support is
\[
\frac{\mathbb P_w(M=m)}{\mathbb P_w(G=m)}
=\frac{\mathbb P(M=m)}{\mathbb P(G=m)}
\frac{\mathbb Ew(G)}{\mathbb Ew(M)}\ge1.
\tag{19}
\]
The size-biased folded law is exactly the law of \(m_J\) under (5). Equation (19), together with the absent finite tail, shows that it too is stochastically dominated by the \(w\)-biased infinite geometric law. Since \(m(m+1)\) is increasing,
\[
\mathbb E[m_J(m_J+1)]
\le
\frac{\sum_{m\ge0}(2m+1)^2m(m+1)z^m}
{\sum_{m\ge0}(2m+1)^2z^m}.
\tag{20}
\]
Direct differentiation of the geometric series gives
\[
\sum_{m\ge0}(2m+1)^2m(m+1)z^m
=\frac{6z(3+10z+3z^2)}{(1-z)^5}.
\]
Combining this with the denominator in (7), equations (16) and (20) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(aV)
\le
v_\infty(a)
:=
\frac{2a^2z(3+10z+3z^2)}
{(1-z)^2(1+6z+z^2)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Put \(b=3a=-\log z\). Then
\[
v_\infty(a)
=\frac29
\left(\frac{b}{2\sinh(b/2)}\right)^2
\frac{3+10z+3z^2}{1+6z+z^2}
\le\frac29\cdot1\cdot3=\frac23.
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(2\sinh(b/2)\ge b\), and the final rational factor is at most \(3\) because
\(3(1+6z+z^2)-(3+10z+3z^2)=8z\ge0\). If \(t=1\), then \(a=0\), so (15) holds directly; if \(L\le1\), every \(m_j=0\). Reflection proves (6) and (15) for tilts of either sign. This completes all finite-interval estimates, including arbitrary truncation and the case \(t=1\).

We now pass to the product. The general reflected-displacement comparison in accepted fact ad475d6d41d953b3 applies with the full reflected interval at every center. We spell out its specialization to retain the single global prefactor. Translate the box to
\(D=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,L_i\}\), write \(p=\bigotimes_i p_i\), and, for a target \(\mathbf j\), set
\[
S_{\mathbf j}
=\prod_{i=1}^n\{-m_{i,j_i},\ldots,m_{i,j_i}\}.
\]
If \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf x\ge d\}\) is any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\), then for every \(\mathbf v\in S_{\mathbf j}\), at least one of \(\mathbf j+\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf j-\mathbf v\) belongs to \(H\): choose the sign having nonnegative scalar product with \(\mathbf c\). The zero displacement contributes the target itself. Pairing all nonzero antipodes and undercounting the zero displacement by one half therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{\mathbf v\in S_{\mathbf j}}
\min\{p(\mathbf j+\mathbf v),p(\mathbf j-\mathbf v)\}\\
&=\frac{p(\mathbf j)}2
\sum_{\mathbf v\in S_{\mathbf j}}
e^{-|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Thus (23) treats every closed ambient halfspace, including boundaries not passing through the target and normals mixing all coordinates.

For factor \(i\), let \(k_i\) be (4), and let \((J_i,V_i,U_i)\) have the law (5) for that factor. The coordinate triples are independent. Squaring (23), multiplying by \(p(\mathbf j)\), summing over \(\mathbf j\), and normalizing the resulting product triple sum gives
\[
M_2(p)
\ge
\frac14\left(\prod_{i=1}^n k_i\right)
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(
-\left|\sum_i\ell_iV_i\right|
-\left|\sum_i\ell_iU_i\right|
\right).
\tag{24}
\]
The factor \(1/4\) occurs once for the entire product, not once per coordinate.

Put \(S=\sum_i\ell_iV_i\) and \(T=\sum_i\ell_iU_i\). Conditional symmetry makes every summand centered. Independence across coordinates and (15) show
\[
\operatorname{Var}S=\operatorname{Var}T
\le\frac{2r}{3},
\tag{25}
\]
because factors of cardinality one or two contribute zero. Jensen's inequality for the convex exponential and Cauchy--Schwarz now yield
\[
\mathbb E e^{-|S|-|T|}
\ge e^{-\mathbb E|S|-\mathbb E|T|}
\ge\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{\frac{2r}{3}}\right).
\tag{26}
\]
By (6),
\[
4^n\prod_i k_i
=\prod_i(4k_i)
\ge\left(\frac{32}{27}\right)^r.
\tag{27}
\]
Substitution of (26)--(27) into (24) proves the first inequality in (1).

To obtain the absolute constant without a numerical approximation, put \(x=5/27\). Since \(1/(1+s)>1-s\) for \(s>0\),
\[
\log\frac{32}{27}
=\log(1+x)
>x-\frac{x^2}{2}
=\frac{245}{1458}>\frac16.
\tag{28}
\]
With \(u=\sqrt r\), (28) gives
\[
r\log\frac{32}{27}-2\sqrt{\frac{2r}{3}}
\ge\frac{u^2}{6}-2\sqrt{\frac23}\,u
=\frac{(u-2\sqrt6)^2}{6}-4
\ge-4.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves the second inequality in (1), and the positive linear term in \(r\) proves the asserted divergence.

It remains to prove the exposed-join extension. The exact exposed-face depth identity in accepted fact 1f002486f983941a applies to each label slice; we verify all its hypotheses here. Since every \(\mathbf v\in V\) is an exposed vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}V\), a linear functional of the label coordinates is uniquely maximized at \(\mathbf v\). Its lift to \(\mathbb R^{h+m}\) exposes the entire slice
\(\{\mathbf v\}\times D_{\mathbf v}\) in the convex hull of \(D^*\). Therefore
\[
\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf v,\mathbf x)
=\alpha_{\mathbf v}\tau_{q_{\mathbf v}}(\mathbf x).
\tag{30}
\]
Indeed, restriction of any containing ambient halfspace to the slice gives the lower bound in (30). Conversely, a minimizing conditional halfspace exists because a finite support realizes only finitely many intersection patterns. Add a sufficiently large positive multiple of a label functional which is zero at \(\mathbf v\) and strictly negative at every other label; the resulting closed ambient halfspace has the chosen intersection with the \(\mathbf v\)-slice and excludes every other supported slice. This proves the reverse bound, including singleton and lower-affine-rank slices and all boundary cases. Restriction and extension also show that ambient and intrinsic conditional depths agree when a slice is lower-dimensional.

Summing the probability times the squared identity (30) proves (2). We finally establish the two estimates that prevent any loss of constant. If two distinct labels \(\mathbf v,\mathbf w\in V\) had the same coordinatewise parity, their midpoint would be integral and would belong to \(P\) by convexity, hence to \(V=P\cap\mathbb Z^h\). That midpoint is a nontrivial convex combination of \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\), so it cannot be an exposed vertex, contradicting the hypothesis on every member of \(V\). Thus there is at most one label in each parity class and
\[
|V|\le2^h.
\tag{31}
\]
Hölder's inequality gives the cubic-mass estimate
\[
1=\left(\sum_{\mathbf v\in V}\alpha_{\mathbf v}\right)^3
\le |V|^2\sum_{\mathbf v\in V}\alpha_{\mathbf v}^3,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf v\in V}\alpha_{\mathbf v}^3\ge|V|^{-2}.
\tag{32}
\]
If every \(\Phi_m(q_{\mathbf v})\ge\kappa\), equations (2), (31), and (32) yield
\[
\Phi_{h+m}(p^*)
\ge4^h\kappa\sum_{\mathbf v}\alpha_{\mathbf v}^3
\ge\frac{4^h}{|V|^2}\kappa
\ge\kappa.
\]
Taking \(\kappa=e^{-4}/4\) and using (1) for every heterogeneous rectangular conditional proves (3); induction proves the assertion for iterated exposed joins.
