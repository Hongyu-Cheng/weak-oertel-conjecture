## Statement

For every integer \(r\ge2\), set \(J=2^r\), \(q=J^2-1\), \(n=2r+3\), and \(K=2^n=8(q+1)\). There is a finite set \(\widehat A\) of formal atoms, partitioned into classes \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_\eta\) indexed by all \(\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), with positive weights \(\widehat w_x\in\lbrace9,3,1\rbrace\). Denote its weight levels by \(\widehat{\mathrm H}\), \(\widehat{\mathrm B}\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm L}\), respectively. The census is

| Class type | Number of classes | Atoms in each class |
|---|---:|---|
| Pure \(\widehat{\mathrm H}\) | \(q-J\) | \(3q\) at weight \(9\) |
| Pure \(\widehat{\mathrm B}\) | \(4q+4\) | \(9q\) at weight \(3\) |
| Pure \(\widehat{\mathrm L}\) | \(3q+4\) | \(27q\) at weight \(1\) |
| Mixed | \(J\) | \(2q\) at weight \(9\) and \(9q\) at weight \(1\) |

The classes of the middle level have \(\eta_1=1\), and all other classes have \(\eta_1=0\). Formal heights \(0,1,2\) on the three respective levels are compatible with these first parity coordinates. The label counts, heights, and orders below can simultaneously be represented by distinct lattice points in \(\mathbb Z^n\), with height the primitive first-coordinate functional and the orders induced by the second-coordinate functional. This representation is asserted only for those data, not for the assigned pair centers or multipliers.

Use the canonical notation of e7a5a5229669c789 and 3c2a0244e1cbb074 through the following explicit formal aliases. Here \(K\) counts formal classes, and hats distinguish formal objects from actual full-row quantities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Z&=\sum_{x\in\widehat A}\widehat w_x=27qK,
&\widehat p_x&=\widehat w_x/\widehat Z,
&\widehat t_x&=K\widehat p_x,\\
\widehat t_{\mathrm H}&=\frac1{3q},
&\widehat t_{\mathrm B}&=\frac1{9q},
&\widehat t_{\mathrm L}&=\frac1{27q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

There are positive odd multipliers \(\widehat h_x\) and an assignment \(e\mapsto\widehat j_e\in\widehat A\) of every unordered pair \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\) of distinct atoms in the same formal class such that

$$
\widehat h_x=1+2|\lbrace e:\widehat j_e=x\rbrace|,
\qquad
\widehat w_{\widehat j_e}^{\,2}=\widehat w_{u_e}\widehat w_{v_e}.
\tag{1}
$$

The assignment is into formal slots. Distinct pairs assigned to a center are not asserted to have disjoint endpoints, to avoid that center as an endpoint, or to have that center as their arithmetic midpoint. Each formal row consists of a central token and two oriented tokens for each assigned pair; it is not a set of distinct endpoint atoms.

Precisely \(18q^2J\) pairs have unequal endpoint weights. For a distinguished set \(\widehat{\mathrm B}_*\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm B}\) of \(18q^2\) centers, exactly \(J\) such pairs are assigned to each center, whose unchanged multiplier is \(9q+2J\). All other assigned pairs have equal endpoint weights. Define the same-level multiplier by

$$
\widehat h_x^{\mathrm{same}}
=\widehat h_x-2J\,\mathbf 1_{\lbrace x\in\widehat{\mathrm B}_*\rbrace}.
\tag{2}
$$

Thus \(\widehat h_x^{\mathrm{same}}=\widehat h_x\) on both extreme levels. This notation is separate from the canonical row probability \(\kappa_x(y)\).

The level sizes and total multipliers are

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm H}&=3q^2-qJ,
&\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathrm H}}\widehat h_x
&=9q^3-5q^2J,\\
N_{\mathrm B}&=36q^2+36q,
&\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathrm B}}\widehat h_x
&=324q^3+324q^2+36q^2J,\\
N_{\mathrm L}&=81q^2+108q+9qJ,
&\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathrm L}}\widehat h_x
&=2187q^3+2916q^2+81q^2J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

These are exactly the oriented same-class pair counts, including diagonals, sorted by the geometric-mean weight of their assigned centers. In particular, for every real \(\alpha\),

$$
\sum_{x\in\widehat A}\widehat h_x\widehat p_x^{\,2\alpha}
=\sum_{\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}
\left(\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_\eta}\widehat p_x^{\,\alpha}\right)^2.
\tag{4}
$$

Each level \(\widehat W\in\lbrace\widehat{\mathrm H},\widehat{\mathrm B},\widehat{\mathrm L}\rbrace\) has an ordering in which every atom of rank \(s\) satisfies the complete numerical bound

$$
\widehat h_x^{\mathrm{same}}\le\widehat h_x
\le2\min\lbrace s-1,|\widehat W|-s\rbrace+1.
\tag{5}
$$

For \(e\) as in (1), choose either endpoint order and put \(\widehat z_e=\tfrac12\log(\widehat p_{v_e}/\widehat p_{u_e})\). Then \(|\widehat z_e|\le\log3\). Define the formal aliases of the row mass, energy, score variance, and auxiliary score average by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_{\widehat p}(x)&=\widehat h_x\widehat p_x,
&\widehat b&=\sum_{x\in\widehat A}\widehat p_x\widehat H_{\widehat p}(x)^2,
&\widehat A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2\widehat b,\\
\widehat\Psi_x&=\widehat t_x(\widehat h_x\widehat t_x-1)^2,
&\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\mathcal J}&=\frac1K\sum_x\widehat\Psi_x,
&\widehat{\mathcal D}&=\frac1K\sum_\eta
\left(\sum_{x\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_\eta}\widehat t_x-1\right)^2,\\
\widehat V_x&=\frac2{\widehat h_x}\sum_{e:\widehat j_e=x}\widehat z_e^2,
&\widehat\nu&=\frac1{\widehat b}
\sum_x\widehat p_x\widehat H_{\widehat p}(x)^2\widehat V_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Write \(E=\sum_x\widehat\Psi_x\), and define \(G=\sum_e2\widehat h_{\widehat j_e}\widehat t_{\widehat j_e}^{\,3}\widehat z_e^2\). The exact formulas are

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{64J^2}{243q}
+\sum_{a\in\lbrace3q,9q,27q\rbrace}\frac{2(a^2-1)}{3a^2}
=2+\frac{64J^2}{243q}-\frac{182}{2187q^2},\\
G&=\frac{4J(9q+2J)}{81q}(\log3)^2,
&\widehat{\mathcal D}&=0,\\
\widehat A_{\mathcal J}&=1+\frac EK,
&\widehat A_{\mathcal J}\widehat\nu&=\frac GK.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

In particular, \(0<E<7/3\), and

$$
\lim_{r\to\infty}
\frac1J\frac{\widehat\nu}{\log\widehat A_{\mathcal J}}
=\frac{54}{275}(\log3)^2>0.
\tag{8}
$$

Thus no finite constant independent of \(J\) bounds \(\widehat\nu/\log\widehat A_{\mathcal J}\) for this aggregate model with score bound \(\log3\), even after all the repairs in (5).

## Proof

Since \(J\ge4\), we have \(q\ge15\), \(q\) odd, and \(J\le q\). The class counts are nonnegative, and their sum is
\((q-J)+(4q+4)+(3q+4)+J=8(q+1)=K\).
Each class has raw mass \(27q\); for a mixed class this is \((2q)9+9q=27q\). Therefore \(\widehat Z=27qK\), every class has probability \(1/K\), and the displayed scaled weights follow. The atom counts are obtained by adding the pure and mixed contributions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm H}&=(q-J)3q+J2q=3q^2-qJ,\\
N_{\mathrm B}&=(4q+4)9q=36q^2+36q,\\
N_{\mathrm L}&=(3q+4)27q+J9q=81q^2+108q+9qJ.
\end{aligned}
$$

There are exactly \(4q+4=K/2\) middle classes and \((q-J)+(3q+4)+J=K/2\) other classes. Assign the former bijectively to the labels with \(\eta_1=1\) and the latter to those with \(\eta_1=0\). Give heights \(0,1,2\) to the three levels. Then height modulo two is the first class coordinate, and the weight equals \(9\cdot3^{-\mathrm{height}}\). Equal-level pairs and mixed outer pairs have the required midpoint height at their respective assigned center levels. This proves the stated height and parity stock compatibility.

Construct the base multipliers as follows. Select \(q^2\) atoms of \(\widehat{\mathrm H}\) and give them multiplier \(3q-2J\). Select \(18q^2\) atoms of \(\widehat{\mathrm B}\), denoted by \(\widehat{\mathrm B}_*\), and give them multiplier \(9q+2J\). Select \(81q^2\) atoms of \(\widehat{\mathrm L}\) and give them multiplier \(27q-2J\). Every other atom has its level's baseline multiplier \(a\), where \(a=3q,9q,27q\), respectively. All multipliers are positive odd integers: \(a\) is odd, \(2J\) is even, and \(3q-2J\ge q>0\). The baseline stocks in the three levels are

$$
q(2q-J),\qquad18q(q+2),\qquad9q(J+12),
\tag{9}
$$

respectively. In particular the distinguished sets exist. These stocks also suffice for the proposed repairs. Indeed,

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(2q-J)&\ge q^2\ge15q\ge6q-2,\\
18q(q+2)&\ge18q\ge18q-2,\\
9q(J+12)&\ge144q\ge54q-2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

The rightmost expressions are \(2(a-1)\) at the corresponding levels.

At each level, choose \(2(a-1)\) distinct baseline atoms. For each odd \(k\in\lbrace1,3,\ldots,a-2\rbrace\), choose four of these atoms and replace their four multipliers \(a,a,a,a\) by \(k,2a-k,k,2a-k\). There are \((a-1)/2\) choices of \(k\), so all selected atoms are used once. This is exactly the proposed two copies of the pair replacement \((a,a)\mapsto(k,2a-k)\). It changes no distinguished atom, preserves each level's multiplier sum, and leaves all multipliers positive and odd. In particular, it produces two distinct atoms with each low multiplier \(1,3,\ldots,a-2\), and two with each compensating multiplier \(a+2,a+4,\ldots,2a-1\).

Before and after repair, the level sums are

$$
\begin{aligned}
3qN_{\mathrm H}-2Jq^2&=9q^3-5q^2J,\\
9qN_{\mathrm B}+2J(18q^2)&=324q^3+324q^2+36q^2J,\\
27qN_{\mathrm L}-2J(81q^2)&=2187q^3+2916q^2+81q^2J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

Independently, the oriented same-class pair counts with geometric-mean center weights \(9,3,1\) are, respectively,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(q-J)(3q)^2+J(2q)^2
&=9q^3-5q^2J,\\
(4q+4)(9q)^2+2J(2q)(9q)
&=324q^3+324q^2+36q^2J,\\
(3q+4)(27q)^2+J(9q)^2
&=2187q^3+2916q^2+81q^2J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

This verifies (3), with diagonals included. There are \(M=J(2q)(9q)=18q^2J\) unordered unequal-weight pairs, all joining the two outer levels in a mixed class. Their geometric-mean center weight is \(3\).

We next give the complete formal slot allocation. At every center \(x\), create \((\widehat h_x-1)/2\) unordered-pair slots and one central token. Reserve \(J\) slots at each \(x\in\widehat{\mathrm B}_*\) for unequal-weight pairs. This is possible because those centers retain multiplier \(9q+2J\), and their remaining slot count is \((9q-1)/2\). There are exactly \(J|\widehat{\mathrm B}_*|=M\) reserved slots, so any bijection from the \(M\) unequal-weight pair labels fills them.

Let the three totals in (11) be \(T_{\mathrm H},T_{\mathrm B},T_{\mathrm L}\). The number of unordered equal-weight pair labels at the extreme levels is \((T_{\mathrm H}-N_{\mathrm H})/2\) and \((T_{\mathrm L}-N_{\mathrm L})/2\), respectively, exactly their slot counts. At the middle level, the number of equal-weight pair labels is

$$
(4q+4)\binom{9q}{2}
=\frac{T_{\mathrm B}-N_{\mathrm B}}2-M,
\tag{13}
$$

exactly the number of unreserved slots there. Choose a bijection for each of these three equal-weight inventories to its same-level slots. Assign the diagonal pair \((x,x)\) to the central token at \(x\). Every unordered noncentral pair is now assigned once, has its correct center weight and height, and contributes two oriented tokens. This proves (1) and (2), including the exact scored-center counts. It proves a bijection of labels with slots; it imposes no endpoint-disjointness or spatial midpoint property.

For every real \(\alpha\), a diagonal token contributes \(\widehat p_x^{2\alpha}\), while the two oriented tokens of a pair assigned to \(x\) each contribute \((\widehat p_{u_e}\widehat p_{v_e})^\alpha=\widehat p_x^{2\alpha}\). Summing first by center and then by class proves (4). All powers are defined because the weights are positive. Multiplication by \(\widehat Z^{2\alpha}\) gives the same identity for raw weights.

We now construct orders satisfying every inequality in (5). At any one level, write \(N\) for its cardinality, \(a\) for its baseline multiplier, and \(s_0=(a-1)/2\). For each \(1\le s\le s_0\), put one of the repaired atoms with multiplier \(2s-1\) at rank \(s\), and the other at rank \(N-s+1\). These use precisely the \(a-1\) low repaired atoms and saturate the rank bound at those positions.

Place all \(a-1\) compensating atoms in the central rank interval \([a,N-a+1]\). At the middle level also place all \(18q^2\) distinguished atoms in this interval. Every multiplier placed there is at most \(2a-1\): this is true for the compensating multipliers, and for distinguished middle atoms it follows from \(2J\le2q\le9q-1=a-1\).

There is enough room in this interval. At the upper level,
\(N_{\mathrm H}\ge2q^2\ge30q\ge3(3q)-3\).
At the lower level,
\(N_{\mathrm L}\ge81q^2\ge3(27q)-3\).
Thus their central intervals have at least \(a-1\) positions. At the middle level, the required stronger inequality is

$$
N_{\mathrm B}-(18q^2+3(9q)-3)
=18q^2+9q+3>0.
\tag{14}
$$

It gives at least \(18q^2+a-1\) central positions. The central interval is disjoint from the two tails. Every remaining multiplier is at most \(a\), because the middle distinguished atoms have already been placed and the distinguished outer multipliers are below \(a\). Fill the remaining positions arbitrarily with the remaining atoms.

For a tail rank the bound is the assigned value \(2s-1\). At a central rank, \(\min\lbrace s-1,N-s\rbrace\ge a-1\), so its capacity is at least \(2a-1\). At every other occupied rank, the two tails have been excluded, so the capacity is at least \(2s_0+1=a\). These three checks cover every atom and prove the bound for \(\widehat h_x\). The same-level bound follows from \(0<\widehat h_x^{\mathrm{same}}\le\widehat h_x\); positivity at a distinguished middle atom is \(\widehat h_x^{\mathrm{same}}=9q\). Thus the repair handles the full tails, not just the first and last atoms. The actual extreme-level bound in e7a5a5229669c789 is exactly the numerical constraint passed here, although its geometric derivation is not reversed.

For completeness, these three orders, the class labels, and the heights admit a single lattice placement. Concatenate the three level orders and enumerate the atoms by \(x_1,\ldots,x_{|\widehat A|}\). If \(x_i\) has class label \(\eta\) and height \(d_i\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), set

$$
\iota(x_i)=(d_i,\,2i+\eta_2,\,\eta_3,\ldots,\eta_n)\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{15}
$$

The first coordinate has the required parity; every other coordinate also has its specified parity. The second coordinates increase strictly with \(i\), so this placement is injective and induces each of the chosen orders. The functional \(z\mapsto z_1\) is primitive and realizes the three heights. No claim is made that \(\iota(\widehat j_e)=(\iota(u_e)+\iota(v_e))/2\), or that the prescribed \(\widehat h_x\) are the full-row sizes of this placement.

It remains to compute the formal energy and score price. At a level with baseline multiplier \(a\), \(\widehat t_x=1/a\), so \(\widehat\Psi_x=(\widehat h_x-a)^2/a^3\). The base energy before repair is therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_0
&=4J^2\left(\frac{q^2}{(3q)^3}
+\frac{18q^2}{(9q)^3}+\frac{81q^2}{(27q)^3}\right)\\
&=\frac{4J^2}{q}\left(\frac1{27}+\frac2{81}+\frac1{243}\right)
=\frac{64J^2}{243q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

Each repaired group \(k,2a-k,k,2a-k\) replaces four baseline atoms of zero energy and adds \(4(a-k)^2/a^3\). With \(s_0=(a-1)/2\), the total addition at that level is

$$
\frac4{a^3}\sum_{\substack{1\le k\le a-2\\k\text{ odd}}}(a-k)^2
=\frac{16}{a^3}\sum_{j=1}^{s_0}j^2
=\frac{2(a^2-1)}{3a^2}.
\tag{17}
$$

Adding the three repairs gives the formula for \(E\) in (7), since
\(1/(3q)^2+1/(9q)^2+1/(27q)^2=91/(729q^2)\).
The energy is positive already in (16). Also \(J^2=q+1\) and \(q\ge15\), so

$$
E<2+\frac{64}{243}\frac{16}{15}
=2+\frac{1024}{3645}<\frac73.
\tag{18}
$$

The exponent-one case of (4), together with class probabilities \(1/K\), gives \(\sum_x\widehat h_x\widehat p_x^2=1/K\). Hence \(\sum_x\widehat h_x\widehat t_x^2=K\), while \(\sum_x\widehat t_x=K\). Expanding \(E\) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
E
&=\sum_x\widehat h_x^2\widehat t_x^3
-2\sum_x\widehat h_x\widehat t_x^2+\sum_x\widehat t_x\\
&=K\widehat A_{\mathcal J}-K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$

Every scaled class mass is one, so \(\widehat{\mathcal D}=0\). This proves the formal energy identity in (7), with exactly the normalization of (6).

On the oriented tokens in a formal row, give the two tokens of \(e\) scores \(\widehat z_e\) and \(-\widehat z_e\), and give its central token score zero. The row has \(\widehat h_x\) tokens. Every geometric-mean weight is \(\widehat p_x\) by (1), so the formal row law is uniform and its variance is exactly \(\widehat V_x\) in (6). This is the token version of the canonical \(\kappa_x\) and \(Y_x\) definitions, not a probability law on distinct reflected atoms. All nonzero scores have magnitude \(\tfrac12\log9=\log3\); there are no other unequal-weight pairs. The scored centers were not repaired, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
G
&=18q^2J\,\frac{2(9q+2J)}{(9q)^3}(\log3)^2\\
&=\frac{4J(9q+2J)}{81q}(\log3)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

Substituting (6) and \(\widehat H_{\widehat p}(x)=\widehat h_x\widehat p_x\) gives

$$
\widehat A_{\mathcal J}\widehat\nu
=K^2\sum_x\widehat p_x\widehat H_{\widehat p}(x)^2\widehat V_x
=\frac1K\sum_e2\widehat h_{\widehat j_e}\widehat t_{\widehat j_e}^{\,3}\widehat z_e^2
=\frac GK.
\tag{21}
$$

Since \(E>0\), the logarithm in (8) is positive. Equations (19) and (21) yield

$$
\frac{\widehat\nu}{\log\widehat A_{\mathcal J}}
=\frac{G}{(K+E)\log(1+E/K)}.
\tag{22}
$$

As \(r\to\infty\), we have \(J\to\infty\), \(q=J^2-1\), \(K=8J^2\),
\(E\to550/243\), and \(G/J\to(4/9)(\log3)^2\).
For \(u>0\), integration of \(1/(1+t)\) over \([0,u]\) gives
\(u/(1+u)\le\log(1+u)\le u\). Applying this with \(u=E/K\) shows

$$
E\le(K+E)\log(1+E/K)\le E(1+E/K).
\tag{23}
$$

The denominator of (22) therefore tends to \(550/243\). Dividing (22) by \(J\) proves (8), because \((4/9)/(550/243)=54/275\).

The proved obstruction is to deriving a uniform price from precisely these formal counts, token slots, and scalar rank constraints. Actual midpoint closure would require every same-parity pair to have an arithmetic midpoint in one common support, with its assigned center equal to that midpoint. At each center, genuine noncentral reflection pairs must use disjoint endpoints, avoid the center itself, and occupy opposite sides in every strict linear ordering induced by a linear functional. The aggregate bijections do not establish these incidences. The parity labels also do not establish the coordinatewise congruences modulo four forced by the midpoint equations. Placement (15) establishes only the census, primitive height, and selected orders; it is not a realization of those equations or of the formal rows. Consequently no actual full-row price, original-law depth moment, or Weak Oertel statement is refuted. In particular, 3c2a0244e1cbb074 retains a coefficient depending on \(J\), and is not contradicted by (8).
