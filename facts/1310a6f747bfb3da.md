## Statement

In $\mathbb Z^3$ take
$$
a=(1,0,0),\ b=(-1,0,0),\ c=(0,1,0),\ d=(0,-1,0),\ o=(0,0,0),\ m=(0,0,1),\ t=(0,0,2)
$$
and let $P$ be the set of these seven sites. For $s>0$ write $S_s=[-s/2,s/2]^2$, put $e=(203/220,0)$ and $g=e/2$, and use vertex fibers $S_1,S_{1/10},e+S_1,e+S_{1/10}$ over $a,b,c,d$ and $K_t=g+(4/5)(K_o-g)$ over $t$, where $K_o=\operatorname{conv}(S_{11/20}\cup(e+S_{11/20}))$. Their placed hull has the exact positive fibers $K_o$ and $K_m=(K_o+K_t)/2$ at the remaining two sites and $P$ is its entire integer base. The total area is $M=8009/2000$ and there are four occupied parities. For every nonempty proper closed minor $U=P\cap\operatorname{conv}U$, with $k(U)$ occupied parities, the gap $k(U)M/4-\sum_{z\in U}|K_z|$ is at least $9/8000$, attained at $U=\{a\}$ and $U=\{c\}$.

There is no affine map on the exposed bottom face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ selecting a point in each of its sampled fibers. Every real scalar function preserving all anchored convex representations on $P$ is an ordinary affine restriction, so any faithful full-rank representation-preserving lift retains the same obstruction.

## Proof

The base hull is the square pyramid $0\le z\le2$, $|x|+|y|\le1-z/2$. Its integer sections contain precisely the five stated bottom sites, the midpoint $m$ at level one, and the apex $t$. In a bottom mean-zero representation the coefficients of $a,b$ agree, as do those of $c,d$. Hence the attainable fiber at $o$ is exactly the convex hull of the opposite-pair averages $S_{11/20}$ and $e+S_{11/20}$. At $m$ the coefficient of $t$ must be $1/2$, and the remaining bottom combination has mean zero. Thus $K_m=(K_o+K_t)/2=g+(9/10)(K_o-g)$. The vertex sections are exact because vertices have no other representations. The hull is compact and full dimensional, since the bases span three dimensions and a vertex has a full planar fiber. All anchored Minkowski inclusions follow from convexity of this hull.

The rectangle $K_o$ has width $81/55$ and height $11/20$. The seven areas in the displayed site order are
$$
1,\quad 1/100,\quad 1,\quad 1/100,\quad 81/100,\quad 6561/10000,\quad 324/625.
$$
The four parity classes are $\{a,b\},\{c,d\},\{o,t\},\{m\}$. A subset is closed exactly when it obeys the implications $\{a,b\}\to o$, $\{c,d\}\to o$, and $\{o,t\}\to m$. The only points that can be newly produced in its hull are $o$ and $m$. The bottom mean-zero calculation gives the first two implications and shows that $m$ can be produced only from $t$ with an opposite pair or with $o$. If $o$ is absent there are $3^2$ possible bottom choices and four choices of membership of $t,m$. If $o$ is present there are $4^2$ choices of membership in the two opposite pairs and three choices for $t,m$. This gives $36+48-2=82$ nonempty proper closed subsets.

Compute the quota gap in units of $1/40000$, so each occupied class pays $40045$ before subtracting its fiber areas. Each opposite-pair class contributes $-355$ when full, at least $45$ when occupied at one site, and zero when absent. The class $\{o,t\}$ contributes $7645$ for $o$ alone and $19309$ for $t$ alone. The singleton $m$ contributes $13801$ when present. If $\{o,t\}$ is fully occupied, closedness forces $m$, and the two classes together contribute $710$. A proper subset can then have at most one full opposite pair, giving at least $355$. If $o$ is present and $t$ absent, the gap is at least $7645-2(355)>45$. If $o$ is absent, neither opposite pair is full, and any nonempty subset has gap at least $45$ because each occupied class contributes at least $45$. Thus the lower bound $45/40000=9/8000$ holds and either unit-area singleton attains it.

An affine selection would have to take at $o$ both averages $(s(a)+s(b))/2$ and $(s(c)+s(d))/2$. They lie in the two midpoint squares, whose horizontal projections are separated by $203/220-11/20=41/110>0$. This is impossible even for boundary selections. For any scalar function preserving all anchored representations, the equations
$$
h(a)+h(b)=2h(o),\qquad h(c)+h(d)=2h(o),\qquad h(t)+h(o)=2h(m)
$$
are independent. They leave four dimensions, exactly the dimension of the affine restrictions, which are contained in that kernel and preserve every representation. Consequently every coordinate of a representation-preserving lift is already affine on $P$. A faithful full-rank lift is an affine isomorphism on the base hull and keeps both diagonal relations, so the same midpoint contradiction applies.
