## Statement

Let \(L\ge1\), and for every \(1\le\ell\le L\) let \(n_\ell\ge1\) and let \(A_\ell\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty. Assume separately in every factor:

1. Each coordinatewise parity class has at most three members of \(A_\ell\).
2. The midpoint of each unordered distinct same-parity pair belongs to \(A_\ell\).
3. Different unordered distinct same-parity pairs have different midpoints.

Let \(\mathcal J_\ell\) be the set of these midpoints, and denote the unique endpoint pair of \(j\in\mathcal J_\ell\) by \(\lbrace u_j,v_j\rbrace\). Choose positive weights on the entire support and use exactly their original laws and normalizers:
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=\frac{w_{\ell,x}}{Z_\ell},\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,\qquad
N=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell.
\tag{1}
$$
Extend each law by zero off its support, and require the exact equalities
$$
p_{\ell,j}^{\,2}=p_{\ell,u_j}p_{\ell,v_j}
\qquad(j\in\mathcal J_\ell).
\tag{2}
$$
These are equivalent to the same equalities for the weights.

Retain the canonical meanings of the actual ambient halfspace depth and reflected quantities in accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705, 56593018dd7cfa85, e974512801a94ae5, and ee640ebdfbcb632d:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\left\lbrace p(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j
\right\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},&
b(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,&
\chi(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,&
M_3(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in \(H_p\), and zero occurs once. The ambient space for the product depth is \(\mathbb R^N\), even for lower-dimensional supports.

For a single factor, suppress \(\ell\), and define
$$
\mathcal P(A)=\lbrace\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n:
A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\ne\varnothing\rbrace,
\qquad K=|\mathcal P(A)|.
\tag{4}
$$
This auxiliary scaling deliberately differs from the predecessors' \(K=2^n\): here \(1\le K\le2^n\) is the occupied parity count. Every assertion involving this changed scaling is proved below. No original probability, normalizer, dimension, or quantity in (3) is changed.

For each supported center set
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,&
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)}\quad(y\in R_x),\\
Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},&
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,&
q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},\qquad
\nu=\sum_{x\in A}q_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
These geometric-row quantities have the same meanings and normalization as in 445a9e668ee1a705. Define the atom sum \(A_0=K^2\sum_xp_x^3\), which is distinct from the canonical ratio \(a(p)=\chi(p)/b(p)\). With natural logarithms and
$$
C=3\cdot2^{15},\qquad c=\frac14e^{-C},
\tag{6}
$$
every such factor satisfies the global scalar estimate
$$
K^2b(p)\ge1,\qquad
\nu\le C\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{7}
$$
In particular (7) holds on the requested active region \(A_0<1\). Every original product (1) satisfies the stronger occupied-count estimates
$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3},
\tag{8}
$$
and therefore
$$
M_2(P)\ge c4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{9}
$$
There is no hypothesis on the midpoint graph coefficient. Singleton supports and empty sets of nontrivial rows are allowed. No endpoint is required to have a singleton row, and no convex completeness assumption is used.

## Proof

First work in a single factor. Midpoint closure and midpoint injectivity imply
$$
R_x=
\begin{cases}
\lbrace u_x,x,v_x\rbrace,&x\in\mathcal J,\\
\lbrace x\rbrace,&x\in A\setminus\mathcal J.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed a noncentral reflected pair has same-parity endpoints, and two different such pairs about the same center would violate injectivity. Each atom is a center of at most one nontrivial row. In a parity class with \(m\le3\) atoms, it is an endpoint of exactly \(m-1\le2\) distinct pairs. Thus each atom belongs to at most three nontrivial rows, counting its center role and all endpoint roles together. This includes every overlap of these roles. Also \(|A|\le3K\).

Let \(h_x=3\) for \(x\in\mathcal J\), and \(h_x=1\) otherwise. By exact equality (2), (10) gives
$$
H_p(x)=h_xp_x.
\tag{11}
$$
For each \(\eta\in\mathcal P(A)\), put \(r_\eta=p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\). Expanding the parity squares counts every unordered distinct same-parity pair exactly once, so (2) and midpoint injectivity give
$$
\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}r_\eta^2
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}p_{u_j}p_{v_j}
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}p_j^2
=\chi(p).
\tag{12}
$$
This rederives the full-row parity identity with occupied-count scaling; it is not obtained by substituting a new value of \(K\) into a predecessor's result.

Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),\\
\mathcal D&=K\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}(Kr_\eta-1)^2\ge0,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\sum_{x\in A}p_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2,\qquad
\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}=\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
The equality for \(\mathcal D\) uses exactly \(K\) occupied classes whose masses sum to one. By expanding the deviation square and using (12),
$$
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}=A_{\mathcal J}-2K\chi(p)+1,
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D
=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{14}
$$
In particular \(A_{\mathcal J}\ge1\). For comparison with the active-region normalization in 56593018dd7cfa85, Jensen under the uniform law on \(A\), followed by (11), gives directly
$$
\frac19\le\frac{K^2}{|A|^2}
\le A_0\le A_{\mathcal J}\le9A_0.
\tag{15}
$$
Thus the active region also has \(1\le A_{\mathcal J}<9\). The proof below will not need this upper bound.

We prove the local score charge explicitly. For \(h\in\lbrace1,3\rbrace\) and \(t>0\), define
$$
\Psi_h(t)=t(ht-1)^2.
$$
For every \(\xi,\upsilon,\omega>0\) with \(\upsilon\omega=\xi^2\), put
$$
z=\frac12\log\frac{\omega}{\upsilon},\qquad
G=6\xi^3z^2,
\qquad
F=\Psi_3(\xi)+\Psi_{h_u}(\upsilon)+\Psi_{h_v}(\omega),
\tag{16}
$$
where \(h_u,h_v\) may be either 1 or 3 independently. We claim
$$
G\le2^{15}F.
\tag{17}
$$
Write \(M=\max(\xi,\upsilon,\omega)\). The product constraint in (16) gives \(\upsilon=\xi e^{-z}\), \(\omega=\xi e^z\).

If \(M>2\), the nonnegative quadratic term in the series for \(\cosh(3z)\) gives \(z^2\le(2/9)\cosh(3z)\). Therefore
$$
G\le\frac43\xi^3\cosh(3z)
=\frac23(\upsilon^3+\omega^3)
\le\frac43M^3.
\tag{18}
$$
For any row atom with value \(M\), its own deviation is at least
\(M(M-1)^2\ge M^3/4\), because its multiplier is at least 1. Consequently \(G\le(16/3)F<6F\), proving (17) in this case. This estimate allows arbitrarily large \(|z|\).

Henceforth suppose \(M\le2\). Then
$$
|z|\le\log(2/\xi).
\tag{19}
$$
For every \(0<\xi\le2\), writing \(t=\log(2/\xi)\ge0\) and maximizing \(t^2e^{-t}\) gives
$$
\xi\log^2(2/\xi)=2t^2e^{-t}\le\frac8{e^2}<2.
\tag{20}
$$
If \(\xi\le1/4\), then \(\Psi_3(\xi)\ge\xi/16\), while (19)-(20) give
\(G\le12\xi^2\le3\xi\). Hence \(G\le48F\).
If instead \(\xi\ge4/9\), then \(3\xi-1\ge3\xi/4\), so
\(\Psi_3(\xi)\ge9\xi^3/16\). In this case (19) gives
\(|z|\le\log(9/2)<\log8<3\), and consequently \(G\le54\xi^3\le96F\).

It remains that
$$
\frac14<\xi<\frac49,\qquad M\le2.
\tag{21}
$$
Both endpoints exceed \(1/32\), since their product exceeds \(1/16\) and each endpoint is at most 2. Also \(|z|<\log8<3\), so
$$
G<6\left(\frac49\right)^3 9
=\frac{128}{27}<5.
\tag{22}
$$
If \(F\ge2^{-12}\), (22) gives \(G\le5\cdot2^{12}F<2^{15}F\).

Suppose finally \(F<2^{-12}\). For either endpoint value \(t\in\lbrace\upsilon,\omega\rbrace\),
$$
\Psi_h(t)\ge t\min\lbrace(t-1)^2,(3t-1)^2\rbrace,
\qquad t>\frac1{32}.
$$
Thus the smaller of \(|t-1|\) and \(|3t-1|\) is less than
\(\sqrt{32F}<1/8\). Each endpoint therefore lies in
$$
\left(\frac78,\frac98\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\left(\frac7{24},\frac38\right).
\tag{23}
$$
If one endpoint lay in the first interval, the other would exceed \(7/24\), giving
$$
\upsilon\omega>\frac{49}{192}>\frac{16}{81}>\xi^2,
$$
contrary to (16) and (21). Hence both endpoints lie in \((7/24,3/8)\). For any such endpoint \(t\), one has \(t<1/2\), and therefore
$$
(t-1)^2-(3t-1)^2=4t(1-2t)>0.
$$
Consequently, for either choice of its multiplier,
$$
\Psi_h(t)\ge t(3t-1)^2
\ge\frac{21}{8}\left(t-\frac13\right)^2
\ge2\left(t-\frac13\right)^2.
\tag{24}
$$
The derivative of \(\log t\) is at most 4 on the interval between the two endpoints, because both exceed \(7/24>1/4\). The mean value theorem and (24) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
z^2&\le4(\omega-\upsilon)^2\\
&\le8\left[\left(\omega-\frac13\right)^2
+\left(\upsilon-\frac13\right)^2\right]\\
&\le4\bigl(\Psi_{h_u}(\upsilon)+\Psi_{h_v}(\omega)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
It follows that
$$
G\le24\left(\frac49\right)^3
\bigl(\Psi_{h_u}(\upsilon)+\Psi_{h_v}(\omega)\bigr)
<3F.
$$
This proves (17) in the final case. The proof covers all positive masses and all real scores; it uses neither compactness nor a bound on an endpoint's own row type.

Apply (17) to each nontrivial row with
$$
t_x=Kp_x,\qquad
\xi=t_j,\quad\upsilon=t_{u_j},\quad\omega=t_{v_j},\qquad
z_j=\frac12\log\frac{p_{v_j}}{p_{u_j}}.
\tag{26}
$$
Equation (2) makes the product constraint exact. By (11), the full geometric row law \(\kappa_j\) is uniform on its triple, with score values \(0,-z_j,z_j\). Thus
$$
V_j=\frac23z_j^2,\qquad
p_jH_p(j)^2V_j=6p_j^3z_j^2.
\tag{27}
$$
Singleton rows have zero score variance. The exact sums, with the original probabilities unchanged, are
$$
\sum_{x\in A}\Psi_{h_x}(t_x)=K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J},
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}\nu=\frac1K\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}6t_j^3z_j^2.
\tag{28}
$$
Each atom is charged by at most three rows, by the incidence accounting following (10). Summing (17) and using (28) proves
$$
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
\le3\cdot2^{15}\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}
=C\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{29}
$$
Now (14) and the elementary inequality
\(\log a\ge(a-1)/a\) for \(a\ge1\) imply
$$
\nu\le C\frac{\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}}{A_{\mathcal J}}
\le C\frac{A_{\mathcal J}-1}{A_{\mathcal J}}
\le C\log A_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{30}
$$
For example, the logarithmic inequality follows by integrating \(1/t\ge1/a\) over \([1,a]\). This proves (7) globally. Unlike the intensity charge analyzed in e974512801a94ae5, (29) vanishes when all endpoint scores vanish at triple resonance; no bound on that fact's intensity \(\beta_{\mathcal J}\) is asserted or used.

We next make the requested active/inactive row choice and convert it to actual product depth. Define \(K_\ell\) by (4) in every original factor and define \(A_{0,\ell}=K_\ell^2\sum_xp_{\ell,x}^3\). If \(A_{0,\ell}<1\), retain every full reflected row. If \(A_{0,\ell}\ge1\), retain only the singleton row at every supported center. Denote these symmetric auxiliary rows by \(\widehat R_{\ell,x}\), and define, using the same original probabilities,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_\ell(x)&=\sum_{y\in\widehat R_{\ell,x}}
\sqrt{p_{\ell,y}p_{\ell,2x-y}},&
\widehat b_\ell&=\sum_xp_{\ell,x}\widehat H_\ell(x)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{\ell,x}(y)&=
\frac{\sqrt{p_{\ell,y}p_{\ell,2x-y}}}{\widehat H_\ell(x)},&
\widehat Y_{\ell,x}(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{\ell,y}}{p_{\ell,2x-y}},\\
\widehat V_{\ell,x}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{\ell,x}}
\widehat Y_{\ell,x}^{\,2},&
\widehat q_{\ell,x}&=
\frac{p_{\ell,x}\widehat H_\ell(x)^2}{\widehat b_\ell},\qquad
\widehat\nu_\ell=\sum_x\widehat q_{\ell,x}\widehat V_{\ell,x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$
These have exactly the restricted-row meanings in ee640ebdfbcb632d and 56593018dd7cfa85. The occupied-count change affects only explicit scalings by \(K_\ell\), not any definition in (31). All denominators are positive since each row contains its supported center.

In an active factor, \(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell=K_\ell^2b(p_\ell)\ge1\) and (30) bounds \(\widehat\nu_\ell\). In an inactive factor, \(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell=A_{0,\ell}\ge1\) and \(\widehat\nu_\ell=0\). It follows that
$$
S:=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\log(K_\ell^2\widehat b_\ell)\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat\nu_\ell\le CS.
\tag{32}
$$
Here \(S\) is a new nonnegative scalar log sum, not the mixed-integer set denoted \(S\) in the locked problem; no mixed-integer set is being formed in this proof.

For completeness, prove the original-product conversion rather than invoking a product conclusion whose hypotheses differ. Fix a supported target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), and put
$$
\widehat R_J=\prod_\ell\widehat R_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_\ell\widehat\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
\widehat Y_J(X)=\sum_\ell\widehat Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),\qquad
\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\widehat V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\tag{33}
$$
Reflection preserves each row law and negates its score, so every score has mean zero. Direct factorization and independence give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_P(J)&:=\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}
=\prod_\ell\widehat H_\ell(j_\ell),\\
P_X&=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{\widehat Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\in\widehat R_J),\\
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J&=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}\widehat Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
$$
In particular the likelihood identity is for
\(P_X=\prod_\ell w_{\ell,x_\ell}/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\), without replacing the original law by a conditioned or restricted law.

Every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) contains at least one point of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\) in \(\widehat R_J\), and contains the central atom. Counting each noncentral orbit once, and using half of the central mass, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|\widehat Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
$$
The final inequality is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. This holds for every ambient containing halfspace, regardless of the affine dimension of the support.

Let \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_\ell\widehat q_\ell\) solely as an auxiliary center law. Squaring (35), averaging over all original product atoms with their original probabilities, and using
$$
P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2
=\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
\prod_\ell\widehat q_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell,
$$
gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
\mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell\widehat b_\ell\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\widehat\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
$$
This is the same reflection-orbit conversion mechanism as 445a9e668ee1a705, now explicitly proved for the selected rows.

Combining (32) and (36),
$$
M_2(P)
\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2}\right)
\exp\bigl(S-2\sqrt{CS}\bigr)
\ge\frac14e^{-C}\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2}.
\tag{37}
$$
The last step is \(S-2\sqrt{CS}=(\sqrt S-\sqrt C)^2-C\ge-C\). Jensen under the original product law then gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P\bigl[(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\bigr]
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge c^{3/2}\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-3}.
$$
This proves (8). Since \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\), (9) follows. One may also retain every full row in every factor, because (30) is global; the requested active/inactive choice already proves all conclusions. Empty nontrivial-row sets, zero scores, and singleton supports are covered by the same identities, with no division by a score variance.
