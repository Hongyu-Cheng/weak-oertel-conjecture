## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $K=-K$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R^d$ of volume $V$, and let $R\ge1$. The lifted hull of the three bodies $K,RK,K$ at bases $-1,0,1$ has exactly those three integer fibers. Its raw depth maximum, both at the fixed middle base and globally over the mixed set, equals $V(1+R^d/2)$ and is attained at $(0,0)$. Its normalized maximum is $1/2$.

Componentwise Minkowski interpolation linearly interpolates $R$. The $d$th root of the raw maximum is strictly convex in $R$, so every distinct pair of radii and every interior interpolation parameter violate root concavity. For every positive power $q$, the same violation at a midpoint holds for the three radii $1$, $1+1/(4d)$, $1+1/(2d)$. In dimension two the root violation also holds for the radii $1,2,3$ with $V=1$, giving raw maxima $3/2,3,11/2$.

## Proof

The only nontrivial sampled compatibility condition is that the middle fiber contain the endpoint midpoint $K$, which holds. Indeed in any representation of base zero the endpoint weights agree, and averaging their bodies gives $K$. The projection is $[-1,1]$, so there are no other integer fibers.

The total mixed mass is $V(2+R^d)$. Reflection about $(0,0)$ exchanges every closed through-origin cap with its complement closed across the same hyperplane, preserving the measure. Such a cap captures at least half the total, even when its boundary has positive mixed mass. An arbitrary containing cap contains its parallel through-origin cap. Conversely at any candidate two opposite closed caps with a nonzero continuous normal through that candidate have masses summing to the total, since their common boundary has zero $d$-volume on every fiber. The exact maxima follow.

The only varying component is $RK$, and convexity gives $(1-t)R_0K+tR_1K=((1-t)R_0+tR_1)K$. For $S=1+R^d/2$,
$$
\frac{d^2}{dR^2}S^q
=\frac{qd}{2}R^{d-2}S^{q-2}\left[(d-1)+\frac{dq-1}{2}R^d\right].
$$
For $q=1/d$ the bracket is $d-1>0$ on the entire range. For every $q>0$ and $1\le R\le1+1/(2d)$, the binomial bound $R^d\le\sum_{k=0}^d2^{-k}<2$ makes the bracket positive since $d\ge2$. Strict convexity gives the stated midpoint failures. In the explicit planar case the contrary inequality $2\sqrt3\ge\sqrt{3/2}+\sqrt{11/2}$ would give $5\ge\sqrt{33}$ after squaring, which is false.
