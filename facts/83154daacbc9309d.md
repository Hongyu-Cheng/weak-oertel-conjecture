## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $A,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies, and let $t$ be a nonzero linear height. Let $S\subset A$ be a segment with distinct endpoint heights, and let $c\in\mathbb R$ satisfy $\min_B t\ge-c$. In ordinary $d$-dimensional volume,
$$
|(A+B)\cap\{t\ge0\}|-|(S+B)\cap\{t\ge0\}|\ \ge\ |A\cap\{t\ge c\}|.
$$
The coefficient one is sharp even when $t(A)=[\ell,h]$ with $\ell<-1$, $h>c>0$, $t(B)\subset[-c,1]$, and $S$ joins the extreme heights of $A$. The inequality can fail in dimension one.

## Proof

Let $s_-,s_+$ be the segment endpoints and let $t(s_+)>t(s_-)$. Choose coordinates $(x,t)$ with $x$ transverse. Subtracting $t$ times the transverse slope of $S$ from $x$ is a linear shear of determinant one. It preserves height and Minkowski sums, and makes the transverse coordinate of $S$ constant, say $x_0$. Fix these coordinates.

First let $B$ be a polytope. Write $P$ for its transverse projection, a full-dimensional polytope in dimension $d-1\ge1$. For each vertex $p_i$ of $P$ choose a lift $b_i=(p_i,t_i)\in B$. Put $\Omega_i=\{v:v\cdot p_i>v\cdot p_j\text{ for all }j\ne i\}$. The $\Omega_i$ cover transverse space outside a finite union of hyperplanes, and $0$ is in none of them. For $v\in\Omega_i$, $v\cdot(v+p_i)=|v|^2+h_P(v)>h_P(v)$, where $h_P$ is the support function. Thus $v+p_i\notin P$. Moreover the regions $\Omega_i+p_i$ are pairwise disjoint. If $v+p_i=w+p_j$ for $v\in\Omega_i$, $w\in\Omega_j$ and $i\ne j$, the strict maximizing inequalities give $(v-w)\cdot(p_i-p_j)>0$, whereas the equality gives $(v-w)\cdot(p_i-p_j)=-|p_i-p_j|^2$.

Partition $A\cap\{t\ge c\}$, up to a volume-null set, into pieces with $x-x_0\in\Omega_i$. Translate the $i$th piece by $b_i$. It lands in $A+B$ at height at least $c+t_i\ge0$, with transverse coordinate outside $x_0+P$, the projection of $S+B$. The image pieces are pairwise disjoint by the transverse argument. Since $S+B\subset A+B$, the difference of the captured volumes is at least the total volume of these translated pieces, which equals the volume of the original upper tail.

For general $B$, take increasing full-dimensional inscribed polytopes $B_n$ with Hausdorff distances $\varepsilon_n\to0$, obtained by taking successive convex hulls of a dense subset including a full-dimensional simplex. Fix an inner ball of radius $\rho>0$ and center $z$ in $B_1$. For $\lambda_n=\varepsilon_n/(\rho+\varepsilon_n)$, the support-function bounds $h_B(u)\le h_{B_n}(u)+\varepsilon_n|u|$ and $h_{B_n}(u)\ge u\cdot z+\rho|u|$ give $(1-\lambda_n)B+\lambda_n z\subset B_n$ by separation. For each $D=A,S$, fix $d_0\in D$. Convexity then gives
$$
(1-\lambda_n)(D+B)+\lambda_n(d_0+z)\subset D+B_n\subset D+B .
$$
The missing volume is at most $[1-(1-\lambda_n)^d]|D+B|$, and this also bounds the missing captured volume. The finite proof applies to every $B_n$ with the same threshold and upper tail. Taking the limit proves the claim.

For sharpness use $m=d-1$, $A=[-1,1]^m\times[\ell,h]$, $S=\{0\}\times[\ell,h]$, and $B_\varepsilon=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^m\times[-c,-c+\varepsilon]$ for small $\varepsilon>0$. The captured difference is $[(2+2\varepsilon)^m-(2\varepsilon)^m](h-c+\varepsilon)$, and the tail volume is $2^m(h-c)$. Their ratio tends to one. In $d=1$, taking $A=S=[-2,3]$, $B=[-1,0]$, $t(x)=x$ and $c=1$ makes the difference zero and the tail length two.
