## Statement

Let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, full-dimensional, and complete, meaning
\(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). For
\(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},
\qquad
p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x)
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}}{Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)}.
\]
A positive log-affine law on \(D\) is a law of this form. For any positive law
\(p\) on \(D\), any \(E\subseteq D\), and any closed halfspace
\(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), write
\(p(E)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}p_{\mathbf x}\) and
\(p(H)=p(D\cap H)\). All logarithms below are natural. Define
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),\qquad
M_q(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q,
\qquad
A_q(p;n)=2^{qn}M_q(p),
\]
where the minimum is over closed halfspaces. A minimizing trace at
\(\mathbf z\) is a set \(D\cap H\) attaining this minimum; uniqueness means
uniqueness of this subset of \(D\). Define entropy capacity, its mean, and the
cubic objective by
\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf z)
=\inf_{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}
e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)},
\qquad
\mathcal A(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf z),
\qquad
F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)=M_3(p_{\boldsymbol\theta}).
\]
If \(\mathbf X\) has law \(p_{\boldsymbol\theta}\), write
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf X)
=\mathbb E[(\mathbf X-\mathbb E\mathbf X)
(\mathbf X-\mathbb E\mathbf X)^{\mathsf T}].
\]

Let \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\theta^{\rm src}}\) be a positive log-affine law on
\(D\). Given a finite \(Q\subset(0,\infty)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there are
an integer \(N\), a finite full-dimensional complete support
\(\widetilde D\subset\mathbb Z^N\), and a finite parameter
\(\boldsymbol\theta^\sharp\in\mathbb R^N\) such that
\(\boldsymbol\theta^\sharp\) is a strict local minimum of
\(F_{\widetilde D}\), and, for \(p^\sharp=p_{\boldsymbol\theta^\sharp}\),
every minimizing trace is unique and
\[
|A_q(p^\sharp;N)-A_q(p;n)|<\varepsilon
\quad(q\in Q),
\]
\[
|2^N\mathcal A(p^\sharp)-2^n\mathcal A(p)|<\varepsilon,
\qquad
\left|8^N\sum_{\mathbf x\in\widetilde D}(p^\sharp_{\mathbf x})^4
-8^n\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}^4\right|<\varepsilon.
\]
At \(\boldsymbol\theta^\sharp\), the unique traces make
\(F_{\widetilde D}\) smooth on a neighborhood, and its ordinary parameter
Hessian satisfies
\[
\nabla^2\log F_{\widetilde D}(\boldsymbol\theta^\sharp)
\succ8\operatorname{Cov}_{p^\sharp}(\mathbf X),
\]
where \(A\succ B\) means that \(A-B\) is positive definite.

For later use, if \(p\) is a positive law on a finite
\(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\) and \(B\subseteq D\), let
\(B_c=\{\mathbf x\in B:\mathbf x\equiv c\pmod2\}\). For each occupied
\(B\)-parity class \(c\), meaning \(B_c\ne\varnothing\), list the distinct
values of \(\log p_{\mathbf x}\) represented in \(B_c\) as
\(y_{c,1}<\cdots<y_{c,m_c}\), aggregating repeated values, and put
\[
\mathcal L_B(p)
=\kappa_{\mathrm{par},B}(p)
:=\sum_{c:B_c\ne\varnothing}
\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{m_c-1}
\tanh\!\left(\frac{y_{c,i+1}-y_{c,i}}4\right)\right).
\]
Empty \(B\)-parity classes are omitted, and a represented class with one
distinct value contributes one. This is the canonical parity-energy capacity
on the retained classes under the exact alias \(w_{\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf x}\),
\(e_{\mathbf x}=-\log p_{\mathbf x}\): reversing the order from the distinct
log-probabilities to the distinct energies preserves every adjacent gap.

Consequently, there are strict local minima \(p_k^\sharp\) on finite
full-dimensional complete supports in dimensions \(N_k\), with unique
minimizing traces and the displayed Hessian slack, such that
\[
2^{N_k}M_1(p_k^\sharp)\to0,
\qquad
8^{N_k}M_3(p_k^\sharp)\to\infty,
\]
and, for every fixed \(\rho>0\),
\[
\inf_{B\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(p_k^\sharp):\,
p_k^\sharp(B)\ge\rho}
\frac{\mathcal L_B(p_k^\sharp)}{2^{N_k}}\to\infty.
\]
For \(\rho>1\), the infimum is over the empty family and is defined to be
\(+\infty\).

There is also a sequence in this strict-local-minimum class satisfying
\[
\frac{\mathcal A(p_k^\sharp)}
{M_3(p_k^\sharp)^{1/3}}\to\infty,
\qquad
8^{N_k}\sum_{\mathbf x}(p_k^\sharp(\mathbf x))^4\to0,
\qquad
8^{N_k}M_3(p_k^\sharp)\to\infty.
\]

## Proof

Adjoining a fair Boolean coordinate sends \(p\) to
\(\widehat p(\mathbf z,b)=p(\mathbf z)/2\). Its two slices are exposed, so
\(\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf z,b)=\tau_p(\mathbf z)/2\). Consequently it
preserves every \(A_q\), and the exposed-fiber formulas below show that it also
preserves \(2^n\mathcal A(p)\) and
\(8^n\sum p_{\mathbf z}^4\). We may therefore assume \(n\ge2\).

Approximate the source parameter by a nonzero rational parameter
\(\boldsymbol\theta_0\) for which no two distinct halfspace traces have equal
mass. This is possible because an equality between the masses of two distinct
subsets is a nonzero exponential-polynomial equation: distinct exponential
monomials are linearly independent. The finitely many such zero sets have empty
interior. Depth moments and the two other displayed source statistics are
continuous in the probability table, so the approximation can be made as
small as needed.

Write
\[
Z_0=Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta_0),\qquad
f=M_3(p_{\boldsymbol\theta_0}),\qquad
c=1/32,\qquad
\gamma=(f/c)^{1/3}.
\]
Choose integer vectors
\(\mathbf u_1,\ldots,\mathbf u_{n-1}\) spanning the kernel of
\(\boldsymbol\theta_0\), with \(\mathbf u_1\) primitive, and an integer
\(\mathbf v_0\) satisfying
\(\boldsymbol\theta_0\cdot\mathbf v_0=-a<0\).

Choose \(s\) with \(2^s>n\). Partition the Boolean cube
\(\{0,1\}^{3s}\) according to the parity of each consecutive triple. Each of
the \(2^s\) classes contains \(4^s\) labels. Within every class, all bits are
fair and pairwise independent, so its mean and covariance are
\[
\tfrac12\mathbf1,\qquad\tfrac14I_{3s}. \tag{1}
\]
Choose \(n\) classes as auxiliary types and all remaining classes as the main
type. Set
\[
J=2^s-n,\qquad A=J4^s,\qquad\lambda=1/J.
\]

For large integers \(R\), define
\[
M_R=\left\lfloor\frac{Z_0\gamma}{2}e^{aR}\right\rfloor,
\qquad L_R=M_R^3,
\]
\[
\mathbf v_{j,R}=R\mathbf v_0+L_R\mathbf u_j\quad(j<n),
\qquad
\mathbf v_{n,R}=R\mathbf v_0-L_R\sum_{j<n}\mathbf u_j,
\]
and
\[
C_{j,R}=\mathbf v_{j,R}
+\{1-M_R,\ldots,M_R\}\mathbf u_1.
\]
Place a copy of \(D\) at each main Boolean label and a copy of \(C_{j,R}\)
at each label of auxiliary type \(j\). Explicitly, the resulting
\(\widetilde D_R\subset\mathbb Z^{3s+n}\) is the union of the pairs
\((\boldsymbol\epsilon,\mathbf x)\) with the indicated label and fiber point.

The support is complete. Indeed, every lattice point of the Boolean projection
\([0,1]^{3s}\) is a vertex, and a convex representation projecting to that
vertex uses only its own fiber. Main fibers are complete by hypothesis, and
auxiliary intervals are complete because \(\mathbf u_1\) is primitive. A main
parity class affinely spans the label space by (1), and \(D\) spans the other
coordinates, so the support is full-dimensional.

Every fiber is exposed by its Boolean label. If a point in such a fiber has
fiber mass \(\alpha\) and conditional law \(q\), restriction of any containing
halfspace to the fiber gives the lower bound \(\alpha\tau_q\). Conversely, add
a sufficiently large multiple of a functional exposing the Boolean label to
a conditional minimizing halfspace; on the finite support this excludes every
other fiber without changing the selected trace in the chosen fiber. Thus the
actual depth is exactly \(\alpha\tau_q\).

Use all parameters \((\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\theta)\), with
weights
\(e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon+
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}\). For an integer \(M\ge1\), let
\[
z_M(t)=\sum_{k=1-M}^Me^{tk},\qquad
q_{M,t}(k)=\frac{e^{tk}}{z_M(t)}
\quad(1-M\le k\le M),\qquad
f_M(t)=M_3(q_{M,t}),
\]
where the one-dimensional depth in \(f_M\) uses closed rays. Put
\[
r_{j,R}(\boldsymbol\theta)
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf v_{j,R}}
z_{M_R}(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u_1)}
{Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)}.
\]
The exposed-fiber depth identity gives, at zero label tilt, the exact objective
\[
F_{\widetilde D_R}(0,\boldsymbol\theta)
=A^{-3}
\frac{F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)
+\lambda\sum_jr_{j,R}(\boldsymbol\theta)^4
f_{M_R}(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf u_1)}
{(1+\lambda\sum_jr_{j,R}(\boldsymbol\theta))^4}. \tag{2}
\]

The vectors \(\mathbf v_{1,R},\ldots,\mathbf v_{n,R}\) are independent. To
see this, a linear dependence first has coefficient sum zero after taking the
inner product with \(\boldsymbol\theta_0\), and then its coefficients of the
independent \(\mathbf u_j\)'s force all coefficients to be equal, hence zero.
Introduce
\[
h_j=(\boldsymbol\theta-\boldsymbol\theta_0)
\cdot\mathbf v_{j,R},\qquad
\bar h=n^{-1}\sum_jh_j.
\]
Then
\[
(\boldsymbol\theta-\boldsymbol\theta_0)\cdot\mathbf v_0=\bar h/R,
\qquad
(\boldsymbol\theta-\boldsymbol\theta_0)\cdot\mathbf u_j
=(h_j-\bar h)/L_R.
\]
Thus bounded \(\mathbf h\) implies
\(\boldsymbol\theta\to\boldsymbol\theta_0\), while the interval tilt is
\(t=O(M_R^{-3})\).

At zero tilt, an even interval has unique minimizing endpoint caps and
\[
f_M(0)=\frac{(M+1)^2}{32M^2}\longrightarrow c.
\]
The competing unnormalized endpoint-cap masses differ by nonzero odd integers
at zero, and their derivatives are \(O(M^2)\). Hence all traces stay fixed for
\(|t|\le c'/M^2\), with an absolute \(c'>0\). Fixed-branch derivatives of
orders one and two are \(O(M)\) and \(O(M^2)\), since the relevant sample-sum
statistics are bounded by constant multiples of \(M\). Composition with
\(t(\mathbf h)=O(M_R^{-3})\) therefore gives \(C^2\) convergence on bounded
\(\mathbf h\)-sets:
\[
f_{M_R}(t)\to c,
\qquad z_{M_R}(t)/(2M_R)\to1,
\qquad r_{j,R}\to\gamma e^{h_j}.
\]
All source minimizing traces stay fixed near \(\boldsymbol\theta_0\) by its
choice. Thus (2), multiplied by \(A^3\), converges in \(C^2\) to
\[
G_\infty(\mathbf h)
=\frac{f+\lambda c\gamma^4\sum_je^{4h_j}}
{(1+\lambda\gamma\sum_je^{h_j})^4}.
\]
Since \(c\gamma^3=f\), zero is stationary. With
\[
b_j=\frac{\lambda\gamma}{1+\lambda n\gamma},
\qquad
C_b=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf b)-\mathbf b\mathbf b^{\mathsf T},
\]
one has
\[
G_\infty(0)=\frac f{(1+\lambda n\gamma)^3},
\qquad
\nabla^2\log G_\infty(0)=12C_b\succ0.
\]
Uniform \(C^2\) convergence on a sufficiently small closed ball gives finite
interior local minima \(\mathbf h_R\to0\), and their Hessians are positive
definite.

It remains to check every label direction. At
\(\boldsymbol\beta=0\), both the ordinary label law and the label law
conditioned by the four-point event defining the cubic depth objective are
mixtures of uniform type classes. Each mixture has the mean and covariance in
(1). On the event, all four observations share the same exposed-fiber label.
Differentiating the ordinary and event log-partition functions therefore gives,
for \(F=F_{\widetilde D_R}\) and every nearby \(\boldsymbol\theta\),
\[
\nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta}\log F=0,
\qquad
\nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta}^2\log F=3I,
\qquad
\nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\theta}^2\log F=0.
\]
Thus the parameter corresponding to
\((0,\mathbf h_R)\) is a strict local minimum in every ambient tilt direction.

In the score coordinates
\((\boldsymbol\beta,\mathbf h)\), within-fiber fluctuations vanish in the
limit. Main points have limiting \(\mathbf h\)-score zero, and auxiliary type
\(j\) has score \(\mathbf e_j\). Consequently the ordinary covariance
converges to
\[
\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac14I_{3s},C_b),
\]
whereas the log-Hessian converges to twelve times that matrix. For all
sufficiently large finite \(R\), the log-Hessian therefore exceeds eight times
the covariance. The invertible change from score coordinates to the original
parameter coordinates preserves this inequality by congruence. Source and
interval trace uniqueness, together with exposed-fiber isolation, shows that
every minimizing trace at the resulting \(p_R^\sharp\) is unique. It also
makes \(F_{\widetilde D_R}\) analytic on a neighborhood, so the preceding
Hessian is the one stated in the theorem.

For \(q>0\), the uniform even-interval moment tends to
\[
c_q=\frac1{2^q(q+1)}.
\]
The same is true at the auxiliary tilts because \(M_R|t|\to0\). If
\(m_q=M_q(p_{\boldsymbol\theta_0})\), exact fiber decomposition gives
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}A_q(p_R^\sharp;n+3s)
=2^{qn}\left(\frac{2^s}{J}\right)^q
\frac{m_q+(n/J)\gamma^{q+1}c_q}
{(1+(n/J)\gamma)^{q+1}}.
\]
This tends to \(2^{qn}m_q\) as \(s\to\infty\). Choose \(s\), then a finite
\(R\), and finally include the initial source approximation to obtain all the
requested estimates for the finite collection \(Q\).

For the two additional statistics, first observe the fixed-support identity
\[
\mathsf C_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf z)
\inf_{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)}.
\]
It follows by the change of variables
\(\mathbf b=\mathbf a+\boldsymbol\theta\), and proves continuity in the
source parameter. On an exposed fiber of mass \(\alpha\) and conditional law
\(q\), restriction of the exponential sum gives
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\mathsf C_q(\mathbf z)\); sending an exposing
label tilt to infinity gives the reverse inequality. Therefore
\[
\mathcal A(p)=\sum_i\alpha_i^2\mathcal A(q_i),
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^4
=\sum_i\alpha_i^4\sum_{\mathbf z}q_i(\mathbf z)^4.
\]
Both conditional quantities are at most one. The normalized auxiliary
contributions contain an additional factor \(n/J\) and vanish as
\(s\to\infty\), while the normalized main contributions converge to the
source values by the displayed identity. More explicitly, after taking
\(R\to\infty\), the two normalized expressions are
\[
2^n\frac{2^s/J}{(1+(n/J)\gamma)^2}
\left(\mathcal A(p_{\boldsymbol\theta_0})+O(n/J)\right)
\]
and
\[
8^n\frac{(2^s/J)^3}{(1+(n/J)\gamma)^4}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\boldsymbol\theta_0}(\mathbf z)^4+O(n/J)\right),
\]
respectively; the \(O(n/J)\) terms are bounded using the preceding
conditional bounds. Both coefficients tend to one as \(s\to\infty\). This
proves the two simultaneous approximations.

Accepted fact `001a95dc6616c6b9` supplies source laws with
\(2^nM_1\to0\). Accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97` proves
\(4^nM_2\to\infty\) for that same sequence, with the exact translation and
tilt normalization recorded there. Apply the embedding with
\(Q=\{1,2,3\}\) and errors tending to zero. Monotonicity of \(L^r\)-norms for
the nonnegative random variable \(\tau_p(\mathbf X)\) gives
\[
8^NM_3\ge(4^NM_2)^{3/2}.
\]
The asserted first- and third-moment limits follow.

For the retained-capacity conclusion, completeness and log-affinity give
\[
2M_1(p)-\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2
\ge\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D\\
\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod2}}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
e^{-|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|/2}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, for each center \(\mathbf z\), every containing closed halfspace
contains the center and at least the lighter endpoint of each antipodal pair.
Thus its mass is at least half the sum of the central atom and the oriented
sum of the lighter endpoint masses. For same-parity endpoints, completeness
supplies the integral midpoint, and log-affinity gives
\(p_{(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2}^2=p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}\), which converts
the summed reflected bound exactly into (3).

Restrict the right side of (3) to \(B\times B\). In an occupied retained
parity class, aggregate the masses at each distinct log-probability
\(y_1<\cdots<y_m\), and call the resulting mass vector
\(\boldsymbol\beta\). The kernel matrix
\(K_{ij}=e^{-|y_i-y_j|/2}\) is positive definite. If
\(\rho_i=e^{-(y_{i+1}-y_i)/2}\), direct multiplication gives the
tridiagonal precision identity
\[
\mathbf v^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}\mathbf v
=v_1^2+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}
\frac{(v_{i+1}-\rho_iv_i)^2}{1-\rho_i^2}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}\mathbf1
=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{1-\rho_i}{1+\rho_i}
=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}
\tanh\!\left(\frac{y_{i+1}-y_i}{4}\right).
\]
For \(m=1\), the same identity reads \(1\). Cauchy--Schwarz in the
\(K\)-inner product, followed by weighted Cauchy--Schwarz across precisely the
occupied retained classes, gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in B\\
\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod2}}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
e^{-|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|/2}
\ge\frac{p(B)^2}{\mathcal L_B(p)}.
\]
Since the discarded square sum in (3) is nonnegative,
\[
M_1(p)\ge\frac{p(B)^2}{2\mathcal L_B(p)}.
\]
At the embedded first-moment witnesses this yields, uniformly over
\(p(B)\ge\rho\),
\[
\frac{\mathcal L_B(p)}{2^N}
\ge\frac{\rho^2}{2\,2^NM_1(p)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Finally construct the source sequence for the mean-capacity separation. Put
\(K=2^m\), \(L=K\), and \(T=\lfloor K^{7/4}\rfloor\). Let
\[
D_m^0=(\{1\}\times\{0,1\}^m)\cup\{\mathbf0\}
\cup(\{-1\}\times\{0,-1\}^m),
\qquad V_m=D_m^0\setminus\{\mathbf0\}.
\]
Its convex hull is given by
\(-1\le t\le1\) and
\((t-1)/2\le x_i\le(t+1)/2\), so it is complete. In dimension \(n=m+2\),
define
\[
D_m=(V_m\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\})
\cup(\{\mathbf0\}\times\{0,\ldots,T\}).
\]
This is exactly the integer support of
\[
\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{conv}(D_m^0)\times[0,L-1],
(\mathbf0,T))
\]
and hence is complete; it is visibly full-dimensional. Let \(p\) be uniform
and put \(Z=|D_m|=2KL+T+1\).

Each outer vertical fiber is exposed, so the depth of its point at height
\(j\) is
\(\min(j+1,L-j)/Z\). For a central point with \(L\le j\le T\), the depth is
\((T-j+1)/Z\). Moving a containing halfspace boundary to the center can only
decrease its trace, so take the boundary through the center. The upward cap
attains this value. A cap whose height normal
is nonnegative contains all central heights \(j,\ldots,T\). A cap whose height
normal points downward contains at least one endpoint from each of the \(K\)
outer antipodal pairs at every outer height, hence at least \(KL>T\) points.
Therefore the claimed upward cap is minimal.

The outer contribution to \(M_3\) is at most
\[
\frac{2KL}{Z}\left(\frac{L}{2Z}\right)^3.
\]
The \(L\) lower central points contribute at most \(L/Z\), and the remaining
central points contribute exactly
\[
\frac1{Z^4}\sum_{r=1}^{T-L+1}r^3.
\]
Using \(L=K\), \(T\asymp K^{7/4}\), and \(Z\ge2K^2\) shows that their sum is
less than \(1/K\) for all sufficiently large \(K\).

For every central height \(L/4\le j<3L/4\), reflection about
\((\mathbf0,j)\) gives at least \(KL/2\) disjoint outer antipodal pairs.
Thus the depth there is at least \(KL/(2Z)\), and there are at least \(L/2\)
such centers. Since \(Z\le4K^2\) for all sufficiently large \(K\),
\[
M_3(p)
\ge\frac{L}{2Z}\left(\frac{KL}{2Z}\right)^3
\ge\frac1{4096K}.
\]
Since \(8^n=64K^3\) and the law is uniform,
\[
8^nM_3(p)\asymp K^2,
\qquad
8^n\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^4
=\frac{64K^3}{Z^3}\le\frac8{K^3}.
\]

If a probability law \(q\) on this support has mean \(\mathbf z\), the
entropy variational inequality, applied to every exponential tilt, gives
\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf z)\ge e^{-D(q\|p)}
\]
because Jensen's inequality yields
\(\log\mathbb E_p e^{\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf z)}
\ge-D(q\|p)+\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbb E_q\mathbf X-\mathbf z)\).
Let \(q_0\) be uniform on the outer points and \(q_1\) uniform on the
central heights \(L,\ldots,T\). For
\[
L\le j\le\left\lfloor\frac{T}{16\log K}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\lambda_j=\frac{2j-L+1}{T+1},
\]
one has \(0\le\lambda_j\le1/(8\log K)<1\), and
\(q_j=(1-\lambda_j)q_0+\lambda_jq_1\) has mean
\((\mathbf0,j)\). Because the two component supports are disjoint and \(p\)
is uniform, its relative entropy is
\[
D(q_j\|p)
=(1-\lambda_j)\log\frac{(1-\lambda_j)Z}{2KL}
+\lambda_j\log\frac{\lambda_jZ}{T-L+1}
\le1
\]
for all sufficiently large \(K\): \(Z/(2KL)\to1\), while
\(\log(Z/(T-L+1))=\tfrac14\log K+O(1)\). There are at least
\(T/(32\log K)\) such centers. Hence, for an absolute \(c>0\),
\[
\mathcal A(p)\ge cK^{-1/4}/\log K,
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal A(p)}{M_3(p)^{1/3}}
\ge cK^{1/12}/\log K\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Apply the simultaneous embedding to normalized mean capacity, cubic moment,
and atomic fourth moment, with errors tending to zero. The preceding normalized
limits and the identity
\[
\frac{\mathcal A(p)}{M_3(p)^{1/3}}
=\frac{2^n\mathcal A(p)}{(8^nM_3(p))^{1/3}}
\]
show that all three asserted limits persist. This proves the final sequence.
