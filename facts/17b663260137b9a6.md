## Statement

Put \(\ell:=\log 64\). For every integer \(n\ge 2\), let

\[
d:=n^2,\qquad
\eta_n:=64^{-1/d}=e^{-\ell/n^2},\qquad
R_n:=n^3\sqrt{1-\eta_n},
\]

and define the convex body

\[
C_n:=\left\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\|\mathbf u\|_2\le R_n,
\quad \mathbf x\ge\mathbf0,
\quad \frac{\|\mathbf u\|_2^2}{n^6}+\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\le1
\right\}.
\]

Let

\[
S_n:=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
D_n:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|\mathbf z\|_2\le R_n\},
\]

and, for \(\mathbf z\in D_n\), put

\[
r_{\mathbf z}:=1-\frac{\|\mathbf z\|_2^2}{n^6},\qquad
K_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C_n\},\qquad
w_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}).
\]

Then \(C_n\) is compact and full-dimensional, its nonempty integer fibers are exactly

\[
K_{\mathbf z}=r_{\mathbf z}\Delta_d,\qquad
\Delta_d:=\{\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\le1\},
\qquad \mathbf z\in D_n,
\]

and all of them have positive \(d\)-volume. More precisely,

\[
\eta_n\le r_{\mathbf z}\le1,\qquad
\frac1{64d!}\le w_{\mathbf z}=\frac{r_{\mathbf z}^d}{d!}\le\frac1{d!}.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the ratio of any two positive integer-fiber volumes is at most \(64\), including any fiber whose base lies on \(\|\mathbf z\|_2=R_n\).

Write

\[
W_n:=\mathcal H_d(S_n)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_n}w_{\mathbf z}.
\]

For \(\mathbf p=(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in S_n\), define its relative depth in its own fiber by

\[
\alpha_n(\mathbf p):=
\frac1{w_{\mathbf z}}
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf x\in J
\right\}.
\]

Set

\[
\rho_n:=n^3\sqrt{1-e^{-1/n^2}},\qquad
b_n:=\frac{2(\rho_n+\sqrt n/2)}{R_n-\sqrt n/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

The denominator in (2) is positive. Every \(\mathbf p\in S_n\) with \(\alpha_n(\mathbf p)\ge e^{-1}\) satisfies the uniform bound

\[
\frac{h_{S_n}(\mathbf p)}{2^{-n}W_n}
\le64\,b_n^n,
\tag{3}
\]

and

\[
b_n\longrightarrow\frac2{\sqrt{\log64}}<1.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf p\in S_n\\ \alpha_n(\mathbf p)\ge e^{-1}}}
\frac{h_{S_n}(\mathbf p)}{2^{-n}W_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

For each \(\mathbf z\in D_n\), the original fiber centroid is

\[
\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}=\frac{r_{\mathbf z}}{d+1}\mathbf1,
\]

and its relative own-fiber depth is exactly
\(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d>e^{-1}\). Hence

\[
\max_{\mathbf z\in D_n}
\frac{h_{S_n}((\mathbf z,\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}))}{2^{-n}W_n}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

Nevertheless, let \(\tau_n\) be the smallest attained value among
\(\{r_{\mathbf z}:\mathbf z\in D_n\}\) such that

\[
\sum_{r_{\mathbf z}\le\tau_n}w_{\mathbf z}\ge\frac{W_n}{2},
\]

and define

\[
\mathbf p_n^*:=\left(\mathbf0,\frac{\tau_n}{d+1}\mathbf1\right).
\]

Then \(\mathbf p_n^*\in S_n\) and

\[
h_{S_n}(\mathbf p_n^*)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4}W_n>\frac1{4e}W_n.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus (5)--(6) obstruct the fixed-relative-depth and original-centroid witness rules, while (7) explicitly shows that this family is not a counterexample to the locked weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

The number \(R_n\) is positive. The body \(C_n\) is the intersection of the Euclidean ball constraint in the base, the coordinate halfspaces \(x_i\ge0\), and a sublevel set of the convex function
\((\mathbf u,\mathbf x)\mapsto\|\mathbf u\|_2^2/n^6+\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\). Hence it is convex and closed. It is bounded because \(\|\mathbf u\|_2\le R_n\), while \(\mathbf x\ge0\) and the roof inequality imply \(0\le x_i\le1\). The point
\((\mathbf0,(1/(2d))\mathbf1)\) satisfies all defining inequalities strictly, so \(C_n\) has nonempty interior and is full-dimensional. This proves compactness and full dimension.

Fix an integer base \(\mathbf z\). If \(\|\mathbf z\|_2>R_n\), its fiber is empty. If \(\|\mathbf z\|_2\le R_n\), direct substitution gives

\[
K_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\le r_{\mathbf z}\}
=r_{\mathbf z}\Delta_d.
\]

Moreover,

\[
r_{\mathbf z}\ge1-\frac{R_n^2}{n^6}=\eta_n>0.
\]

In particular, equality \(\|\mathbf z\|_2=R_n\) produces a positive-volume fiber of height \(\eta_n\), rather than a zero-volume boundary fiber. Successive integration of the simplex gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(r\Delta_d)=r^d/d!\). Since
\(\eta_n^d=64^{-1}\), all assertions in (1), including the exact support and the volume-ratio bound, follow.

We next construct a small ambient halfspace through any locally deep point. Fix
\(\mathbf p=(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf x_0)\in S_n\) with
\(\alpha_n(\mathbf p)\ge e^{-1}\), and write

\[
r_0:=r_{\mathbf z_0},\qquad s_0:=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x_0.
\]

The closed halfspace \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\le s_0\}\) contains \(\mathbf x_0\), and its intersection with \(K_{\mathbf z_0}\) is \(s_0\Delta_d\). Therefore

\[
e^{-1}\le\alpha_n(\mathbf p)
\le\left(\frac{s_0}{r_0}\right)^d,\qquad
s_0\ge e^{-1/d}r_0.
\tag{8}
\]

Consider the closed ambient halfspace

\[
H_{\mathbf p}:=
\left\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf x):
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x
\ge s_0-\frac{2}{n^6}\mathbf z_0^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf u-\mathbf z_0)
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Its boundary contains \(\mathbf p\). At a lattice base \(\mathbf z\in D_n\), the fiber roof minus the threshold in (9) is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{\mathbf z}
-\left(s_0-\frac{2}{n^6}\mathbf z_0^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)\right)
&=r_0-s_0-\frac{\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0\|_2^2}{n^6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

If the right side of (10) is negative, the fiber section is empty. If it is zero, the section is contained in the facet
\(\{\mathbf x\ge0:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x=r_{\mathbf z}\}\), which has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Hence a fiber contributes positive mixed volume to \(H_{\mathbf p}\) only under the strict condition

\[
\|\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0\|_2^2
<n^6(r_0-s_0)
\le n^6(1-e^{-1/d})r_0
\le n^6(1-e^{-1/n^2})=\rho_n^2.
\tag{11}
\]

This establishes both the tangent-halfspace identity and the required strict boundary.

Let \(\kappa_n:=\operatorname{vol}_n(B_2^n)\). The unit cubes centered at lattice points in a ball of radius \(r\) have disjoint interiors and lie in the concentric ball of radius \(r+\sqrt n/2\). Thus

\[
\#\bigl(\mathbb Z^n\cap(\mathbf z_0+\rho_nB_2^n)\bigr)
\le\kappa_n(\rho_n+\sqrt n/2)^n.
\tag{12}
\]

Conversely, rounding each coordinate to a nearest integer shows that the unit cubes centered at the points of \(D_n\) cover the ball of radius \(R_n-\sqrt n/2\), provided this radius is positive. Therefore

\[
|D_n|\ge\kappa_n(R_n-\sqrt n/2)^n.
\tag{13}
\]

We verify the positivity without a numerical approximation. Strict convexity of \(t\mapsto1/t\) and its tangent at \(t=3/2\) give

\[
\log2=\int_1^2\frac{dt}{t}
>\int_1^2\left(\frac23-\frac49\left(t-\frac32\right)\right)dt
=\frac23.
\]

Hence \(\ell=6\log2>4\). Also
\(\eta_n=64^{-1/n^2}\ge64^{-1/4}>1/4\). From
\(1-e^{-t}=\int_0^t e^{-s}ds\ge te^{-t}\) we obtain

\[
R_n^2=n^6(1-e^{-\ell/n^2})
\ge n^4\ell e^{-\ell/n^2}
=n^4\ell\eta_n>n^4.
\]

Thus \(R_n>n^2>\sqrt n/2\), as required in (2) and (13).

Every contributing fiber in (11) has volume at most \(1/d!\). Equations (11)--(12) therefore give

\[
h_{S_n}(\mathbf p)
\le\mathcal H_d(S_n\cap H_{\mathbf p})
\le\frac{\kappa_n(\rho_n+\sqrt n/2)^n}{d!}.
\tag{14}
\]

On the other hand, (1) and (13) give

\[
W_n\ge\frac{|D_n|}{64d!}
\ge\frac{\kappa_n(R_n-\sqrt n/2)^n}{64d!}.
\tag{15}
\]

Dividing (14) by (15) and multiplying by \(2^n\) proves (3). Its right side is independent of \(\mathbf z_0\) and \(\mathbf x_0\), so the estimate is uniform over all eligible points.

It remains to justify the limit analytically. For every fixed \(a>0\),

\[
e^{-a/n^2}
\le\frac{1-e^{-a/n^2}}{a/n^2}\le1
\]

by the same integral representation. Hence

\[
n^2(1-e^{-a/n^2})\longrightarrow a.
\]

Applying this with \(a=1\) and \(a=\ell\) yields

\[
\frac{\rho_n}{n^2}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{R_n}{n^2}\longrightarrow\sqrt\ell,\qquad
\frac{\sqrt n}{2n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

This proves the first part of (4), while \(\ell>4\) proves
\(2/\sqrt\ell<1\). Choose any \(q\) strictly between these two numbers. Then \(b_n\le q<1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), so
\(64b_n^n\le64q^n\to0\). This proves (5) without a numerical asymptotic step.

We now treat the original fiber centroids. Applied with \(m=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), accepted fact \(\texttt{6769574fc1bd9f8d}\) says that the centroid of every \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) has closed-halfspace depth at least
\(\gamma_d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), where
\(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). For \(K_{\mathbf z}=r_{\mathbf z}\Delta_d\), the centroid is
\(\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}=r_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1/(d+1)\). The closed halfspace
\(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf x\le d r_{\mathbf z}/(d+1)\}\) contains this centroid and cuts the fraction
\((d/(d+1))^d=\gamma_d\). Thus its relative own-fiber depth is exactly \(\gamma_d\). Finally,

\[
-\log\gamma_d=d\log(1+1/d)<1,
\]

so \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\). Every original centroid is therefore included among the points in (5), and (6) follows.

For completeness, we exhibit the promised globally deep point. The definition of \(\tau_n\) is valid because \(D_n\) is finite and nonempty. Minimality of this attained weighted median gives both

\[
\sum_{r_{\mathbf z}\le\tau_n}w_{\mathbf z}\ge\frac{W_n}{2},\qquad
\sum_{r_{\mathbf z}\ge\tau_n}w_{\mathbf z}\ge\frac{W_n}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]

Indeed, the mass strictly below \(\tau_n\) is less than \(W_n/2\), so its complement has mass greater than \(W_n/2\). Since \(0<\tau_n\le1\), the point \(\mathbf p_n^*\) lies in the zero-base fiber: its coordinates are nonnegative and their sum is \(d\tau_n/(d+1)<1=r_{\mathbf0}\).

Consider first a proper closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(\mathbf p_n^*\), written as

\[
H=\left\{(\mathbf u,\mathbf x):
\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u+
\boldsymbol\beta^{\mathsf T}\left(\mathbf x-\frac{\tau_n}{d+1}\mathbf1\right)\ge0
\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

At the fiber centroid \((\mathbf z,\mathbf c_{\mathbf z})\), the defining expression is

\[
\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z+b(r_{\mathbf z}-\tau_n),\qquad
b:=\frac{\boldsymbol\beta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1}{d+1}.
\tag{18}
\]

If \(b\ge0\), select the bases with \(r_{\mathbf z}\ge\tau_n\); if \(b<0\), select those with \(r_{\mathbf z}\le\tau_n\). In either case the selected set has weight at least \(W_n/2\) by (16). It is centrally symmetric, and paired bases have equal weights because
\(r_{-\mathbf z}=r_{\mathbf z}\) and \(w_{-\mathbf z}=w_{\mathbf z}\). Pairing \(\mathbf z\) with \(-\mathbf z\) shows that the selected bases additionally satisfying
\(\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z\ge0\) have weight at least \(W_n/4\). For each such base, (18) is nonnegative, so its fiber centroid belongs to \(H\).

If \(\boldsymbol\beta\ne\mathbf0\), the section of (17) in that fiber is a closed halfspace containing its centroid, and accepted fact \(\texttt{6769574fc1bd9f8d}\) gives section volume at least \(\gamma_dw_{\mathbf z}\). If \(\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf0\), the whole selected fiber lies in \(H\), which gives the same lower bound. Summing over the selected bases proves

\[
\mathcal H_d(S_n\cap H)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4}W_n.
\]

Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p_n^*\) contains the parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf p_n^*\); the whole-space case is immediate. Therefore the same bound holds for every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p_n^*\), proving (7). Since (7) is far stronger than the locked \(2^{-n}\) scale, the construction cannot disprove weak Oertel. It only proves the stated obstruction to locally deep and original-centroid witness selection.
