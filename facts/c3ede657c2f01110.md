## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\), and let \(D\subset\mathbb R^N\) be finite and nonempty. For \(\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb R^N\), define
\[
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=\frac{e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf z}}
{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf x}},
\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
=\min_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}p(H),
\]
and
\[
M_q(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q
\quad(q>0).
\]
The parameter \(\bm{\theta}\) is the parameter denoted \(\boldsymbol\ell\) in the declared predecessors; the probability and depth normalizations are unchanged.

The following assertions hold.

(a) Every law in the closure of this exponential family is positive and log-affine on \(D\cap F\) for some face \(F\) of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\). A law with proper support is not a local minimum of \(M_q\) relative to that closure. Therefore \(\inf_{\bm{\theta}}M_q(p_{\bm{\theta}})\) is attained at a finite parameter.

(b) Put \(F_D(\bm{\theta})=M_3(p_{\bm{\theta}})\). At every finite local minimum \(\bm{\theta}_*\), each supported point has a unique depth-minimizing trace \(A_{\mathbf z}=D\cap H_{\mathbf z}\). Uniqueness refers to the trace, not to its representing normal. All these traces remain fixed in a neighborhood of \(\bm{\theta}_*\), and \(F_D\) is real analytic there.

(c) At such a local minimum, let \(\mathbf X_0,\ldots,\mathbf X_3\) be independent with law \(p=p_{\bm{\theta}_*}\), and put
\[
E=\{\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf X_3\in A_{\mathbf X_0}\},
\qquad
\mathbf U=\mathbf X_0+\mathbf X_1+\mathbf X_2+\mathbf X_3.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb P(E)=M_3(p),\qquad
\mathbb E[\mathbf U\mid E]=4\mathbb E_p\mathbf X,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf U\mid E)\succeq4\operatorname{Cov}_p(\mathbf X).
\]
More generally, throughout the fixed-trace neighborhood,
\[
\nabla\log F_D
=\mathbb E[\mathbf U\mid E]-4\mathbb E_p\mathbf X,
\]
\[
\nabla^2\log F_D
=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf U\mid E)-4\operatorname{Cov}_p(\mathbf X).
\]

(d) Fix one number \(0<\kappa\le1\). The following two dimension-uniform assertions are equivalent. Here a finite support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^m\) is complete when \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^m\).

\(\mathrm L_\kappa\): For every integer \(m\ge1\), every full-dimensional nonempty finite complete support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^m\), and every finite local minimum \(\bm{\theta}_*\in\mathbb R^m\) of \(F_D\),
\[
F_D(\bm{\theta}_*)\ge\kappa8^{-m}.
\]

\(\mathrm A_\kappa\): For every integer \(m\ge1\), every nonempty finite complete support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^m\), including lower-dimensional supports, and every log-affine probability law \(p\) on \(D\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-m}.
\]

If either, and hence both, of these dimension-uniform assertions holds, then the locked weak Oertel conclusion follows with the absolute constant
\[
c_0=\min\left\{
\frac{\kappa^{1/3}}{12\,16^{1/3}},\frac1{2e}
\right\}.
\]

## Proof

First describe the exponential-family closure. Suppose \(p_{\bm{\theta}_j}\to p^*\), let \(A=\{\mathbf z:p^*_{\mathbf z}>0\}\), and fix \(\mathbf z_0\in A\). Set \(V=\operatorname{span}(A-\mathbf z_0)\), and let \(\mathbf u_j\) be the orthogonal projection of \(\bm{\theta}_j\) onto \(V\).

For each \(\mathbf z\in A\),
\[
\bm{\theta}_j\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)
=\log\frac{p_{\bm{\theta}_j}(\mathbf z)}
{p_{\bm{\theta}_j}(\mathbf z_0)}
\]
has a finite limit. Applying this to a basis selected from \(A-\mathbf z_0\) shows that \(\mathbf u_j\to\mathbf u\in V\).

Put \(\mathbf v_j=\bm{\theta}_j-\mathbf u_j\). This vector is constant on \(A\) as an affine functional. For each \(\mathbf x\in D\setminus A\),
\[
\mathbf v_j\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z_0)\to-\infty.
\]
Because \(D\setminus A\) is finite, one sufficiently large \(j\) gives strict negativity simultaneously for every such \(\mathbf x\). Thus \(\mathbf v_j\) exposes a face \(F\) with \(A=D\cap F\). The limiting probability ratios give \(p^*_{\mathbf z}\propto e^{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z}\) on \(A\).

Now let \(p_F\) be a closure law with proper support \(D\cap F\). Extend its affine logarithm to the ambient space and tilt along an exposing normal of \(F\). This gives finite-parameter laws \(p_t\) satisfying
\[
p_t(\,\cdot\mid F)=p_F,\qquad
\varepsilon_t=p_t(D\setminus F)\to0.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in F\), a minimizing cap in the affine hull of \(F\) extends to an ambient cap excluding every support point outside \(F\): add a sufficiently large exposing component. Consequently,
\[
\tau_{p_t}(\mathbf z)
=(1-\varepsilon_t)\tau_{p_F}(\mathbf z).
\]
For \(\mathbf z\notin F\), a halfspace parallel to the exposing hyperplane contains \(\mathbf z\) and excludes \(F\), so \(\tau_{p_t}(\mathbf z)\le\varepsilon_t\). Hence
\[
M_q(p_t)
\le(1-\varepsilon_t)^{q+1}M_q(p_F)+\varepsilon_t^{q+1}.
\]
Since \((1-\varepsilon)^{q+1}\le1-\varepsilon\), this is strictly below \(M_q(p_F)\) whenever \(\varepsilon_t^q<M_q(p_F)\).

Each depth is the minimum of finitely many linear functions of the probability table. Thus \(M_q\) is continuous on the closed probability simplex. The exponential-family closure is compact, so it has a minimizer; the preceding argument excludes proper-support minimizers. The closure description shows that a full-support minimizer has a finite parameter. This proves (a), including singleton and lower-dimensional supports.

For (b), fix any selection of active minimizing traces at \(\bm{\theta}_*\), and define
\[
\Phi_A(\bm{\theta})
=\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in A_{\mathbf z}}
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf x)\right)^3.
\]
One has \(F_D\le\Phi_A\) everywhere and equality at \(\bm{\theta}_*\). Therefore every active branch \(\Phi_A\) is locally minimized there.

Suppose one center \(\mathbf z\) has distinct active traces \(A,B\) with common mass \(t\). Changing only this trace gives a difference of branch gradients equal to
\[
3p_{\mathbf z}t^2
\left(
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x
-\sum_{\mathbf x\in B}p_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x
\right).
\]
The two gradients must both vanish.

Accepted fact c16327a288b708bd supplies a generic minimizing halfspace representing \(A\), with boundary through \(\mathbf z\) and no other supported point on the boundary. Orient its normal \(\mathbf a\) toward the included side. Its value on \(\mathbf x-\mathbf z\) is positive on \(A\setminus B\) and negative on \(B\setminus A\). Equal positive masses and \(A\ne B\) imply that both difference sets are nonempty. The inner product of \(\mathbf a\) with the displayed difference of first moments is therefore strictly positive, a contradiction. The terms involving \(\mathbf z\) cancel because \(p(A)=p(B)\).

There are finitely many possible traces. Uniqueness gives a positive gap from every competing trace, so all minimizing traces remain fixed in a common neighborhood. The fixed branch is analytic.

For (c), in that neighborhood,
\[
F_D(\bm{\theta})
=
\frac{
\sum_{(\mathbf z_0,\ldots,\mathbf z_3)\in E}
e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot(\mathbf z_0+\cdots+\mathbf z_3)}
}{
Z_D(\bm{\theta})^4
}.
\]
Differentiating its logarithm gives the stated mean and covariance identities. At a local minimum the gradient vanishes and the Hessian of the logarithm is positive semidefinite.

For (d), \((\mathrm A_\kappa)\) immediately implies \((\mathrm L_\kappa)\), because the latter considers a subclass of the laws covered by the former.

Conversely, assume \((\mathrm L_\kappa)\), with its one coefficient \(\kappa\) valid in every integer dimension. Fix an integer \(m\ge1\), a nonempty finite complete support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^m\), and a log-affine law \(p\) on \(D\). Let
\[
r=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D).
\]
If \(r=m\), part (a) allows minimization of \(F_D\) at a finite parameter. This global minimizer is a finite local minimum, so \((\mathrm L_\kappa)\) in dimension \(m\) bounds the minimum by \(\kappa8^{-m}\), and therefore bounds \(M_3(p)\) by the same quantity.

Suppose \(1\le r<m\). The intrinsic affine lattice
\[
L=\operatorname{aff}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^m
\]
has rank \(r\). Choose an affine lattice isomorphism \(\psi:L\to\mathbb Z^r\), and put \(D'=\psi(D)\). Completeness of \(D\) gives
\[
D'=\operatorname{conv}(D')\cap\mathbb Z^r,
\]
and \(D'\) is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^r\). Restriction of an ambient affine functional to \(\operatorname{aff}(D)\), extension of an intrinsic affine functional back to \(\mathbb R^m\), and transport by \(\psi\) show that the containing-halfspace traces on \(D\) are exactly the inverse images of the containing-halfspace traces on \(D'\). Thus \(\psi\) preserves actual depths. It also carries \(p\) to a log-affine law \(p'\) on \(D'\), and hence
\[
M_3(p)=M_3(p').
\]
Part (a), now in dimension \(r\), gives a finite global minimizer for \(F_{D'}\). Applying \((\mathrm L_\kappa)\) in that same dimension \(r\) gives
\[
\min_{\bm\theta\in\mathbb R^r}F_{D'}(\bm\theta)\ge\kappa8^{-r}.
\]
The arbitrary transported log-affine law \(p'\) therefore satisfies
\[
M_3(p)=M_3(p')\ge\kappa8^{-r}\ge\kappa8^{-m}.
\]
If \(r=0\), then \(D\) is a singleton and \(M_3(p)=1\ge\kappa8^{-m}\). This proves \((\mathrm A_\kappa)\) in every dimension \(m\), so the two dimension-uniform assertions are equivalent.

Finally assume either equivalent assertion. If \(Q\subset\mathbb R^M\) is compact and convex and \(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^M\) is nonempty, then \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^M\), because \(\operatorname{conv}(A)\subseteq Q\). Hence, by \((\mathrm A_\kappa)\), for every arithmetic dimension \(M\ge2\), every nonempty complete lattice support in \(\mathbb Z^M\), and every log-affine law on it,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-M}=\kappa2^{-3M}.
\]
This is precisely hypothesis \((\mathrm H_{3,M}(\kappa))\) of accepted fact c5f6533ba1c11991, with the same \(\kappa\) for every \(M\ge2\). For a locked mixed-integer instance with integer dimension \(n\ge1\), apply that accepted fact at arithmetic dimension \(M=n+1\), with \(q=3\) and \(C_3=16\). It yields one mixed-integer point, selected before the containing closed halfspace, whose depth is at least
\[
\frac{\kappa^{1/3}}{12\,16^{1/3}}\,2^{-n}
\]
times the total mixed-integer volume. The zero-volume case is automatic, as in the locked problem. Taking the displayed minimum with \(1/(2e)\) gives the stated absolute \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) and proves the conditional locked weak Oertel implication.
