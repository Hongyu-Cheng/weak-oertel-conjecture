## Statement

Put $d=6$, $r=6/7$, $\gamma=r^6$, $I=\{0,\ldots,9\}$, and $i=8$. There are positive affine arrays $(a_j)_{j\in I}$ and $(v_j)_{j\in I}$ with $0<a_j<v_j$, $v_i=1$, $a_i=r$, and $\sum_j(j-i)a_j^5=0$. Writing $B=\sum_j a_j^6$ and $M=\sum_jv_j^6$, they satisfy
$$
B\le r^6+\sum_{j<i}v_j^6,\qquad
B\le r^6+\sum_{j>i}v_j^6,\qquad
B\ge\gamma\max_j v_j^6,
$$
yet $M>2B/\gamma$.

These data occur on the ten actual fibers $v_j T$ of a full-dimensional body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^6$, where $T$ is a volume-one simplex centered at zero. At $(i,0)$ the realizing cap captures $a_j^6$ on each fiber, has ordinary own-fiber mass $\gamma$, and is a global minimum among all caps through $(i,0)$ with its fixed nonzero continuous normal and arbitrary real base coefficient. The two step budgets are realized by finite base coefficients in this same cap family. The actual point $(6,0)$ has raw mixed depth at least $\gamma M/2>B$.

## Proof

Let
$$
P(t)=35t^5+375t^4+2550t^3+8670t^2+9195t-11805.
$$
Its derivative is strictly positive on $[0,\infty)$. The values $P(2/3)=-239945/243$ and $P(3/4)=1198965/1024$ give a unique positive root $c\in(2/3,3/4)$. Set $a_j=r(j+c)/(8+c)$ and $v_j=(67+j)/75$. Both arrays are positive affine, with $v_8=1$, $a_8=r$. The endpoint bounds $a_0<18/245<v_0$ and $a_9<87/91<v_9$ give $a_j<v_j$ everywhere. Expanding by the binomial theorem gives $\sum_{j=0}^9(j-8)(j+t)^5=-P(t)$, so the root $c$ gives the stated moment identity.

For $t>0$ put $B(t)=r^6\sum_{j=0}^9((j+t)/(8+t))^6$. Its derivative is $6r^6 P(t)/(8+t)^7$, so $B=B(c)\le B(2/3)=837979292565/567869252041<369/250$. Direct rational comparison gives $\gamma>79/200$ and $(67/75)^6>1/2$. The binomial expansion gives
$$
\gamma+v_9^6>79/200+1+6/75+15/75^2=369/250+1/600>B.
$$
The eight left fibers have total volume greater than $4>B$. Also $B>2\gamma$ since $a_9>a_8=r$, whereas $v_9^6<(11/10)^6<2$. Finally $M=273705308393/35595703125>23/3$ and $\gamma>9/23$, so $\gamma M>3>2B$.

Take a volume-one six-simplex $T$ of centroid zero. The vertex values $r,-1/7,\ldots,-1/7$ sum to zero and determine a linear functional $\ell$ by affine interpolation. For $v>0$, the cap of $vT$ above $\ell(x)=h$ has volume $\min\{v,\max(0,rv-h)\}^6$. Indeed the range of $\ell$ has width $v$, and a proper cap is a homothetic copy of $vT$ scaled by $(rv-h)/v$ about its apex. At support levels the same formula counts the full or zero volume, as appropriate.

Use the body $C=\{(t,x):0\le t\le9,\ x\in ((67+t)/75)T\}$. The scale is positive and affine, so convexity follows by taking convex combinations within $T$ with weights proportional to the scales. Compactness and a nonempty interior follow from compactness and positive volume of $T$ and the positive scale on the interval. The integer fibers are exactly the stated ten. The cap $\ell(x)\ge (r/75-s)(t-8)$ has raw mixed mass
$$
Q(s)=\sum_{j=0}^9\min\{v_j,\max(0,r+s(j-8))\}^6 .
$$
At $s_*=r/(8+c)$ its roots are exactly the proper roots $a_j$. For sufficiently large positive $s$, the left traces are empty and the right trace is full, with own mass $\gamma$. For sufficiently negative $s$, all eight left traces are full and the right trace is empty, again with own mass $\gamma$. Finiteness of the base and $|j-8|\ge1$ for $j\ne8$ give finite such values of $s$.

For any $s<0$, the nine roots at $j\le8$ are at least $r$ before clipping, and $v_j\ge67/75>r$, so $Q(s)\ge9\gamma>B$. For $0\le s\le v_9-r$ there is no upper clipping. The function $\sum_j\max(0,r+s(j-8))^6$ is convex and differentiable there, and the interior point $s_*$ has derivative $6\sum_j(j-8)a_j^5=0$. For $s\ge v_9-r$, the right fiber is full, giving $Q(s)\ge\gamma+v_9^6>B$. These cases prove global minimality for the fixed continuous normal.

For the competing center, use the original weights $v_j^6$. Direct summation gives $M-2\sum_{j<6}v_j^6=3911779367/177978515625>0$ and $M-2\sum_{j>6}v_j^6=99585557737/59326171875>0$. Every closed ambient halfspace containing $(6,0)$ intersects the centroid line in a closed halfline containing $6$ or the whole line, hence includes centroids of total original weight at least $M/2$. For each included centroid, the restricted halfspace captures at least $\gamma v_j^6$ by the full-volume case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d. A constant restriction containing the centroid captures the full fiber. The depth at $(6,0)$ is therefore at least $\gamma M/2>3/2>B$, while the displayed cap gives depth at most $B$ at $(8,0)$.
