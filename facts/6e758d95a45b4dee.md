## Statement

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite set in an affine lattice, equal to the lattice points of $\operatorname{conv}P$, and let $d\ge1$. At each $z\in P$ let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, with $\sum_z\lambda_zK_z\subseteq K_p$ whenever $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$ is a convex representation. Write $C$ for the canonical convex hull of the lifted fibers, $C_z$ for its fiber over $z$, and $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathbf1_{K_z}\,dX)$ for the measured fibers. Put $M=\sum_z|K_z|$, let $g_z$ be the centroid of $K_z$, and define
$$
N=d+1,\qquad
\Sigma=\frac1M\sum_z\int_{K_z}(X-g_z)(X-g_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dX,\qquad
\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u},\qquad
f(z,x)=\left(\frac{E_{C_z}(x)}{M}\right)^{1/N}.
$$
Here $E_K(y)$ is the minimum of $\int_K[1+a\cdot(X-y)]^{-N}\,dX$ over parameters with positive denominator on $K$ when $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, and is zero on the own boundary. At a sampled mark $(p,y)$, the raw buffered depth $b_\varepsilon(p,y)$ is the infimum of $\mu$-masses of the closed caps
$$
a\cdot(Z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\varepsilon f(p,y)\Gamma(u),\qquad u\ne0,
$$
and of the closed pure-base caps containing $p$ (with value $M$ in base rank zero). Write $\beta_\varepsilon(K)=\max_{p\in P,\ y\in K_p}b_\varepsilon(p,y)/M$. The case $\varepsilon=0$ gives ordinary normalized closed-halfspace depth.

Fix $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and let $k(P)$ be the number of occupied parity classes in the inherited affine lattice. Suppose $\beta_0(K^{\rm ref})<b<\gamma_d/k(P)$ for a positive compatible family on $P$. Assume there is a compact set $\mathcal A$ of positive compatible families for the simultaneous Hausdorff metric with $M=1$, $K_z\subseteq R B_d$ and $\beta_0\le b$, such that every positive compatible family with $\beta_0\le b$ can be taken into $\mathcal A$ by an invertible map $(z,X)\mapsto(z,AX+Bz+c)$. Here $R>0$, $B_d$ is the Euclidean unit ball, and $L=|P||B_{d-1}|R^{d-1}$ with $|B_0|=1$.

For every $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that $\varepsilon_0LR<b-\beta_0(K^{\rm ref})$, and every $0\le\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$, the infimum $\beta_\varepsilon^*=\inf_K\beta_\varepsilon(K)$ over all positive compatible families on the fixed $P$ is attained, with a representative in $\mathcal A$. Moreover
$$
\beta_0^*\le\beta_\varepsilon^*\le\beta_0^*+\varepsilon LR .
$$
For $\varepsilon_j\to0$ in that interval, any sequence of minimizers can be represented in $\mathcal A$ and has subsequences converging there to ordinary minimizers. If also $\varepsilon_0 LR<\gamma_d/(4|P|)$, every buffered maximizing mark of every family in $\mathcal A$ has a proper own shifted trace in every nonvertical direction.

## Proof

The canonical fiber at a sampled base is $K_p$ by anchored compatibility. For an invertible continuous linear map $A$, an affine base-dependent translation $Bz+c$, and the resulting change of bodies, the energy law of [d90ba05577427854](d90ba05577427854.md) gives $E'=|\det A|E$, and $M'=|\det A|M$. Hence $f'=f$ at corresponding marks. Within each fiber the affine shift cancels in the centered second moment, so $\Sigma'=A\Sigma A^{\mathsf T}$. Under $u'=A^{-\mathsf T}u$ and $a'=a-B^{\mathsf T}u'$, the nonvertical cap expression at corresponding marks agrees exactly, including the shift since $\Gamma'(u')=\Gamma(u)$. Pure-base caps select the same fibers. Thus the normalized objective is invariant. Unimodular affine base changes also preserve it by reindexing bases and transporting the base covector.

The multiplier in [f2ead43a963c7026](f2ead43a963c7026.md) is identically one, so its comparison condition holds and the raw and normalized maxima are jointly continuous in the positive bodies and $\varepsilon$. On $\mathcal A$, $0\le f(p,y)\le1$ by $E_{K_p}(y)\le|K_p|\le M=1$. In each unit continuous direction each fiber variance is at most its uncentered second moment, which is bounded by $R^2$ since $|u\cdot X|\le R$ throughout the fibers. Hence $\Gamma(u)\le R$. Each shifted threshold expands its old cap by a slab of width at most $\varepsilon R$ on every fiber, regardless of the base tilt. The sum of the section bounds is $L$, and pure-base caps do not change. Taking infima and then maxima gives the markwise and maximum bounds $0\le b_\varepsilon-b_0\le\varepsilon LR$ and $0\le\beta_\varepsilon-\beta_0\le\varepsilon LR$ on $\mathcal A$. Monotonicity $\beta_\varepsilon\ge\beta_0$ holds on the entire positive class by cap inclusion.

Normalize $K^{\rm ref}$ into $\mathcal A$. Its buffered value is below $b$ for the whole scale interval. A minimizing sequence for any such scale is eventually below $b$ in buffered value and hence in the original sublevel. Move it to $\mathcal A$ using the invariant maps. Compactness and continuity give an attaining positive limit. This also works at scale zero. Testing an ordinary minimizing representative in the uniform upper bound gives the inequality for the infima. Every buffered minimizer has ordinary value below $b$, so it admits a representative in $\mathcal A$. Joint continuity and the inequality for the infima identify each subsequential limit as an ordinary minimizer.

In [481a0b2cb463b75b](481a0b2cb463b75b.md) take $M=1$, the stated $L$, and the bound $G=R$. If the additional small-scale bound holds, the ordinary raw depth at a buffered maximizing mark is at least $\gamma_d/|P|-\varepsilon LR$, and its own boundary distance is at least this quantity divided by $L$. The shifted hyperplane has displacement at most $\varepsilon R$, strictly less than that distance.
