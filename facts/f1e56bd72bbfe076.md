## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be the convex body in the canonical dyadic setting retained by accepted fact `d0338841f89a9c80`, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\]
For a convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<m\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), use the metronoid normalization
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
  f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\
  \int_Kf(x)\,dx=m
 \right\}.
\]
For every positive-volume matching-endpoint fiber, retain its centroid and centered half-mass body
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 D_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)-c_y.
\]
Retain the canonical endpoint-disjoint same-parity matching \(\mathcal M_2\), its pooled endpoint weights \(\tau_y\), and its matching weights
\[
 t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal M_2),
\]
and, for any dual-feasible stress \(v=(v_e)_{e\in\mathcal M_2}\) in the normalization of accepted fact `d0338841f89a9c80`, put
\[
 U_y:=\frac12v_{e(y)},
 \qquad
 \sigma_y:=h_{D_y}(U_y),
 \qquad
 Y_+:=\{y\in\textstyle\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal M_2}e:\sigma_y>0\},
\tag{1}
\]
where endpoint-disjointness makes \(e(y)\) unique. Accepted fact `d0338841f89a9c80` gives
\[
 0\leq\sigma_y\leq t_{e(y)}\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in\textstyle\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal M_2}e).
\tag{2}
\]

For every same-parity affine basis
\[
 B=\{y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_n\}\subseteq Y_+,
\]
order the basis so that
\[
 \sigma_{y_0}=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\sigma_{y_i}.
\]
For every nontrivial partition
\[
 I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,n\},
 \qquad I\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad J\ne\varnothing,
\]
define, in the convention retained by accepted facts `d0338841f89a9c80` and `3100bf8082ca09d5`,
\[
 F_*(B;I,J):=
 \frac{
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}\right)
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}\right)}
 {\sigma_{y_0}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(\mathfrak F_*\) be the maximum of (3) over all such bases, orderings, and partitions, and set \(\mathfrak F_*:=0\) if there is no admissible basis-partition pair.

Let \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For Borel \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
 \mu_C(A):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\},
\]
and, for \(p\in S\), put
\[
 h_S(p):=
 \inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), use the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap K):K\subseteq\mathbb R^d
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in K\}.
\]
Set
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p),
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{4}
\]
with the normalization of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`. Then every dual-feasible stress satisfies
\[
 \boxed{D\geq\gamma_d\mathfrak F_*.}
\tag{5}
\]
For every admissible basis-partition pair, the proof constructs one full-rank two-factor lattice-polysimplex packet \(\Pi\) in pairwise distinct integral target fibers such that
\[
 D\geq\mathcal E(\Pi)\geq\gamma_dF_*(B;I,J).
\tag{6}
\]
The packets for different frames are alternatives and are maximized, not summed.

## Proof

We first verify the centroid witnesses. Fix \(y\in Y_+\). By (2), \(a_y>0\) and
\[
 0<\frac{\sigma_y}{a_y}\leq\frac14<1.
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore the constant density
\[
 f_y(x):=\frac{\sigma_y}{a_y}\mathbf1_{C_y}(x)
\tag{8}
\]
is an admissible metronoid density: it satisfies \(0\leq f_y\leq1\), has mass
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}f_y(x)\,dx=\sigma_y,
\]
and has normalized first moment
\[
 \frac1{\sigma_y}\int_{\mathbb R^d}xf_y(x)\,dx
 =\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx
 =c_y.
\]
Thus
\[
 c_y\in\mathsf M_{\sigma_y}(C_y).
\tag{9}
\]
The upper bound \(\sigma_y\leq a_y/4\) is exactly what makes (8) legal; no centroid-depth assertion is being substituted for metronoid membership.

Fix an admissible basis \(B=\{y_0,\ldots,y_n\}\) and partition \(I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Write
\[
 M:=\sigma_{y_0},
 \qquad
 I_0:=\{0\}\cup I,
 \qquad
 J_0:=\{0\}\cup J,
 \qquad
 r:=|I|,
 \qquad
 s:=|J|.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(M>0\), \(r,s\geq1\), \(r+s=n\), and
\[
 0<\sigma_{y_i}\leq M
 \qquad(0\leq i\leq n).
\tag{11}
\]

We construct the full-rank lattice chart. For \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\), put
\[
 h_i:=\frac{y_i-y_0}{2},
 \qquad
 k_j:=\frac{y_j-y_0}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
All these vectors are integral because the basis has one parity. Affine independence of \(y_0,\ldots,y_n\) says that their union is linearly independent. Hence, with
\[
 V_1:=\operatorname{span}\{h_i:i\in I\},
 \qquad
 V_2:=\operatorname{span}\{k_j:j\in J\},
\tag{13}
\]
one has
\[
 \dim V_1=r,
 \qquad
 \dim V_2=s,
 \qquad
 V_1\oplus V_2=\mathbb R^n.
\tag{14}
\]
Choose matrices
\[
 \Lambda_1\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_2\in\mathbb Z^{n\times s}
\tag{15}
\]
whose columns are lattice bases of \(V_1\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and \(V_2\cap\mathbb Z^n\), respectively. There are integral coordinate vectors
\[
 \xi_i\in\mathbb Z^r\quad(i\in I),
 \qquad
 \eta_j\in\mathbb Z^s\quad(j\in J)
\tag{16}
\]
such that
\[
 h_i=\Lambda_1\xi_i,
 \qquad
 k_j=\Lambda_2\eta_j.
\tag{17}
\]
Set \(\xi_0:=0\in\mathbb Z^r\) and \(\eta_0:=0\in\mathbb Z^s\). The vectors \(\xi_i\), \(i\in I\), form a real basis of \(\mathbb R^r\), and the vectors \(\eta_j\), \(j\in J\), form a real basis of \(\mathbb R^s\). Thus
\[
 \Delta_1:=\operatorname{conv}\{\xi_i:i\in I_0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^r,
 \qquad
 \Delta_2:=\operatorname{conv}\{\eta_j:j\in J_0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^s
\tag{18}
\]
are full-dimensional lattice simplices. Put
\[
 \mathcal D_1:=\Delta_1\cap\mathbb Z^r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_2:=\Delta_2\cap\mathbb Z^s,
\tag{19}
\]
and let \(\alpha_i\), \(i\in I_0\), and \(\beta_j\), \(j\in J_0\), be the respective barycentric-coordinate functions.

Define the integral affine chart
\[
 \Phi(\xi,\eta):=y_0+\Lambda_1\xi+\Lambda_2\eta.
\tag{20}
\]
By (14), the concatenated matrix \([\Lambda_1\ \Lambda_2]\) has rank \(r+s=n\). Hence \(\Phi\) is injective and has full base rank. Equations (12), (17), and (20) give
\[
 \Phi(\xi_i,\eta_j)
 =y_0+h_i+k_j
 =\frac{y_i+y_j}{2}
 \qquad(i\in I_0,\ j\in J_0),
\tag{21}
\]
where \(h_0=k_0=0\) and \(y_0\) is used for either zero index. The bases in (21) are integral because the \(y_i\) have one parity. The coordinate pairs \((\xi_i,\eta_j)\) are pairwise distinct, and injectivity of \(\Phi\) therefore shows that all \((r+1)(s+1)\) target fibers in (21) are pairwise distinct.

We next construct an actual metronoid point in every product-vertex fiber. For \(i\in I_0\) and \(j\in J_0\), put
\[
 t_{ij}:=\min\{\sigma_{y_i},\sigma_{y_j}\}>0,
 \qquad
 Z_{ij}:=\frac{c_{y_i}+c_{y_j}}2.
\tag{22}
\]
Scaling (8) by \(t_{ij}/\sigma_{y_i}\) gives an admissible density on \(C_{y_i}\) of mass \(t_{ij}\) and barycenter \(c_{y_i}\); the analogous scaling at \(y_j\) has the same mass and barycenter \(c_{y_j}\). Equivalently,
\[
 (y_i,c_{y_i},t_{ij}^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (y_j,c_{y_j},t_{ij}^{1/d})
\tag{23}
\]
belong to the positive-root metronoid generating set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. Its positive-root convexity, in the same normalization used in accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`, puts the midpoint of the two triples in that generating set. Using (21), this says exactly
\[
 \boxed{
 Z_{ij}\in
 \mathsf M_{t_{ij}}
 \left(C_{\Phi(\xi_i,\eta_j)}\right).}
\tag{24}
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` then gives the absolute fiber-depth bound
\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_C\left(\Phi(\xi_i,\eta_j),Z_{ij}\right)
 \geq\gamma_dt_{ij}.}
\tag{25}
\]
Every point in (24) is therefore an actual point of the displayed integral target fiber. The two scaled densities used for one pair \((i,j)\) establish only that cell's membership. Densities belonging to different pairs are never added, even when their source fibers coincide. The packet below uses the pairwise distinct target fibers proved after (21).

We now give the rank-one normalization. For \(i\in I_0\) and \(j\in J_0\), define
\[
 \pi_i:=\frac{\sigma_{y_i}}{\sqrt M},
 \qquad
 \varrho_j:=\frac{\sigma_{y_j}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{26}
\]
By (11),
\[
 \pi_i\varrho_j
 =\frac{\sigma_{y_i}\sigma_{y_j}}M
 \leq\min\{\sigma_{y_i},\sigma_{y_j}\}
 =t_{ij}.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus \((\pi_i\varrho_j)_{i,j}\) is a positive rank-one minorant of the full array of actual cell masses. Take the two factor roots
\[
 a_{1,i}:=\gamma_d^{1/d}\pi_i^{1/d},
 \qquad
 a_{2,j}:=\varrho_j^{1/d}.
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (25) and (27) imply, for every product vertex,
\[
 \delta_C\left(\Phi(\xi_i,\eta_j),Z_{ij}\right)^{1/d}
 \geq a_{1,i}a_{2,j}.
\tag{29}
\]

Define
\[
 \mathcal B_1:=
 \sum_{\xi\in\mathcal D_1}
 \left(
  \sum_{i\in I_0}\alpha_i(\xi)\pi_i^{1/d}
 \right)^d,
\tag{30}
\]
\[
 \mathcal B_2:=
 \sum_{\eta\in\mathcal D_2}
 \left(
  \sum_{j\in J_0}\beta_j(\eta)\varrho_j^{1/d}
 \right)^d.
\tag{31}
\]
All hypotheses of the factorized lattice-polysimplex theorem in accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5` hold with two factors, dimensions \(r,s\), total rank \(r+s=n\), the theorem's two matrix variables instantiated as \(\Lambda_1,\Lambda_2\), chart (20), product-vertex points (24), and factor roots (28). Under this substitution, the theorem's first factor sum is \(\gamma_d\mathcal B_1\) and its second factor sum is \(\mathcal B_2\), because the factor root \(\gamma_d^{1/d}\) in (28) is raised to the \(d\)-th power in every summand of the first factor. The theorem therefore produces one mixed-integer point \(p\in S\), equivalently one full-rank two-factor packet \(\Pi\), such that
\[
 h_S(p)
 \geq2^{-n}\gamma_d\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2.
\tag{32}
\]
In the packet normalization of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`, set
\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi):=\gamma_d\mathcal B_1\mathcal B_2.
\tag{33}
\]
Taking the supremum in (4) and multiplying (32) by \(2^n\) yields the explicit packet-energy bound
\[
 D=2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq\mathcal E(\Pi).
\tag{34}
\]

It remains to estimate the energy. Every simplex vertex belongs to its lattice domain. At \(\xi_i\), the barycentric coordinate \(\alpha_i\) is one and all other \(\alpha\)'s are zero. Since every summand in (30) is nonnegative,
\[
 \mathcal B_1
 \geq\sum_{i\in I_0}\pi_i
 =\frac{M+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{35}
\]
The same argument in the second factor gives
\[
 \mathcal B_2
 \geq\sum_{j\in J_0}\varrho_j
 =\frac{M+\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}}{\sqrt M}.
\tag{36}
\]
Combining (33), (35), and (36), and recalling \(M=\sigma_{y_0}\), gives
\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 \geq
 \gamma_d
 \frac{
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i}\right)
  \left(\sigma_{y_0}+\sum_{j\in J}\sigma_{y_j}\right)}
 {\sigma_{y_0}}
 =\gamma_dF_*(B;I,J).
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (34) and (37) prove (6) for the fixed frame and partition.

This construction is repeated separately for each admissible frame-partition pair. The resulting packets, cell densities, and energies are not combined. Since \(D\) bounds each score individually, taking the maximum of the numerical right sides of (37), rather than summing them, proves
\[
 D\geq\gamma_d\mathfrak F_*.
\]
If there is no admissible basis-partition pair, then \(\mathfrak F_*=0\) by definition and the conclusion follows from \(D\geq0\). In particular, for the zero stress one has \(U_y=0\), \(\sigma_y=h_{D_y}(0)=0\), and \(Y_+=\varnothing\), so the same no-frame argument applies. No high-support aggregation inequality and no weak Oertel conclusion is used or proved.
