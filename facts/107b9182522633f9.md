## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
a:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)>0,
\qquad
c:=\frac1a\int_Kx\,dx,
\qquad
\Sigma_K:=\frac1a\int_K(x-c)(x-c)^{\mathsf T}\,dx.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(0<t\leq a\), define
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dx=t
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The covariance matrix \(\Sigma_K\) is positive definite.  For \(r\in\mathbb R^d\), set
\[
h_r(x):=\left\langle\Sigma_K^{-1}r,x-c\right\rangle,
\qquad
p_K(r):=\max_{x\in K}h_r(x),
\qquad
q_K(r):=\max_{x\in K}(-h_r(x)).
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(p_K(r),q_K(r)\geq0\).  If \(q_K(r)\leq1\), then
\[
c+r\in\mathsf M_{a/(1+p_K(r))}(K),
\tag{4}
\]
and this membership is realized by the density
\[
f_r(x):=\frac{1+h_r(x)}{1+p_K(r)}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the symmetric covariance-polar set
\[
\mathcal E(K):=
\left\{
r\in\mathbb R^d:
\max_{x\in K}
\left|\left\langle\Sigma_K^{-1}r,x-c\right\rangle\right|
\leq1
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
This is a nonempty compact origin-symmetric convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\), and
\[
c+\mathcal E(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_{a/2}(K).
\tag{7}
\]
More precisely, for \(r\in\mathcal E(K)\), the density
\[
g_r(x):=\frac{1+h_r(x)}2
\tag{8}
\]
has values in \([0,1]\), mass \(a/2\), and normalized barycenter \(c+r\).

Now let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{9}
\]
For \((z,y)\in C\), define its absolute depth in its fiber by
\[
\delta_C(z,y):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L):
L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }y\in L
\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\left\{
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H
\right\},
\tag{11}
\]
where \(H_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\), and put
\[
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p),
\qquad
I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0\}.
\tag{12}
\]
The set \(I_+\) is finite.  For every \(z\in I_+\), write
\[
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,
\qquad
\Sigma_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}(y-c_z)(y-c_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dy
\tag{13}
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal E_z:=
\left\{
r\in\mathbb R^d:
\max_{y\in C_z}
\left|\left\langle\Sigma_z^{-1}r,y-c_z\right\rangle\right|
\leq1
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Each \(\Sigma_z\) is positive definite, each \(\mathcal E_z\) is a compact origin-symmetric convex body, and
\[
c_z+\mathcal E_z
\subseteq\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)
\subseteq C_z.
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(E\subseteq I_+\) be nonempty and put
\[
W_E:=\sum_{z\in E}a_z>0.
\tag{16}
\]
There exists \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\) such that
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\geq2^{-n}W_E
\tag{17}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\).  For every \(m\) with these properties, let
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{
\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,
\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m
\right\}
\tag{18}
\]
and define the covariance Minkowski core
\[
\mathcal I_m(E):=
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z(c_z+\mathcal E_z).
\tag{19}
\]
The set \(\Lambda_m(E)\) is a nonempty compact polytope, and \(\mathcal I_m(E)\) is a compact convex set, possibly empty.  If it is nonempty, then every \(x\in\mathcal I_m(E)\) satisfies \((m,x)\in S\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(m,x)
&\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z\\
&\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}W_E,
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular,
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}W_E.
\tag{21}
\]

The following Chebyshev-radius condition is sufficient for the hypothesis in (20).  Let \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body satisfying \(B=-B\), so that its Minkowski functional
\[
\|u\|_B:=\inf\{s\geq0:u\in sB\}
\tag{22}
\]
is a norm.  Suppose numbers \(r_z\geq0\) are chosen so that
\[
r_zB\subseteq\mathcal E_z
\qquad(z\in E).
\tag{23}
\]
Define the barycentric centroid polytope
\[
\mathcal C_m(E):=
\left\{
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zc_z:
\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)
\right\},
\tag{24}
\]
its \(B\)-Chebyshev radius, and the least available barycentric radius by
\[
R_B(\mathcal C_m(E))
:=
\min_{x\in\mathbb R^d}
\max_{q\in\mathcal C_m(E)}\|x-q\|_B,
\qquad
\rho_m(r):=
\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zr_z.
\tag{25}
\]
Both minima are attained.  If
\[
R_B(\mathcal C_m(E))\leq\rho_m(r),
\tag{26}
\]
then \(\mathcal I_m(E)\neq\varnothing\), and consequently (20) and (21) hold with the exact factor \(\gamma_d/2\).

## Proof

We first verify the covariance assertions.  The matrix \(\Sigma_K\) in (1) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.  If \(u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), then
\[
u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Ku
=\frac1a\int_K\langle u,x-c\rangle^2\,dx.
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(K\) is full-dimensional, it contains a nonempty open ball.  The nonzero affine function \(x\mapsto\langle u,x-c\rangle\) cannot vanish on that ball, and its square is positive on a subset of positive volume.  The right-hand side of (27) is therefore positive.  Thus \(\Sigma_K\) is positive definite and invertible.

For fixed \(r\), the score \(h_r\) is continuous on compact \(K\), so the maxima in (3) are finite and attained.  Its average is zero:
\[
\int_Kh_r(x)\,dx
=\left\langle\Sigma_K^{-1}r,\int_K(x-c)\,dx\right\rangle
=0.
\tag{28}
\]
It follows that neither \(h_r\) nor \(-h_r\) can have a strictly negative maximum.  Hence \(p_K(r),q_K(r)\geq0\).

Assume \(q_K(r)\leq1\), and abbreviate \(p:=p_K(r)\), \(q:=q_K(r)\).  Since \(-q\leq h_r(x)\leq p\) on \(K\), (5) satisfies
\[
0\leq\frac{1-q}{1+p}
\leq f_r(x)\leq1.
\tag{29}
\]
The denominator is positive because \(p\geq0\).  Equation (28) gives the exact mass
\[
\int_Kf_r(x)\,dx
=\frac1{1+p}\int_K(1+h_r(x))\,dx
=\frac a{1+p}.
\tag{30}
\]
The covariance normalization gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_K(x-c)h_r(x)\,dx
&=\int_K(x-c)(x-c)^{\mathsf T}\,dx\,\Sigma_K^{-1}r\\
&=a\Sigma_K\Sigma_K^{-1}r
=ar.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (28) and (31),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_Kx f_r(x)\,dx
&=\frac1{1+p}
\left(\int_Kx\,dx+\int_Kx h_r(x)\,dx\right)\\
&=\frac1{1+p}(ac+ar)
=\frac a{1+p}(c+r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
Dividing (32) by (30) proves (4), including the cases \(p=0\) and \(q=1\).

We next verify the geometry and compactness of (6).  The function
\[
N_K(r):=\max_{x\in K}
\left|\left\langle\Sigma_K^{-1}r,x-c\right\rangle\right|
\tag{33}
\]
is absolutely homogeneous and subadditive.  If \(N_K(r)=0\), then \(\Sigma_K^{-1}r\) is orthogonal to every vector \(x-c\) with \(x\in K\).  Full dimensionality of \(K\) implies that these vectors span \(\mathbb R^d\), so \(\Sigma_K^{-1}r=0\) and hence \(r=0\).  Thus \(N_K\) is a norm.  Its unit ball is exactly \(\mathcal E(K)\), which is consequently nonempty, compact, convex, origin-symmetric, and has nonempty interior.

If \(r\in\mathcal E(K)\), then \(p_K(r),q_K(r)\leq1\).  The mass in (30) satisfies
\[
\frac a{1+p_K(r)}\geq\frac a2.
\tag{34}
\]
In general, if \(y\in\mathsf M_t(K)\) is represented by a density \(f\) and \(0<s\leq t\), then \((s/t)f\) is still bounded between zero and one, has mass \(s\), and has the same normalized barycenter.  Hence
\[
\mathsf M_t(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_s(K)
\qquad(0<s\leq t\leq a).
\tag{35}
\]
Apply (35) to (4) with \(t=a/(1+p_K(r))\) and \(s=a/2\).  The scaled density is exactly
\[
\frac{a/2}{a/(1+p_K(r))}f_r
=\frac{1+p_K(r)}2f_r
=\frac{1+h_r}2
=g_r.
\tag{36}
\]
Because \(-1\leq h_r\leq1\), it is admissible.  Equations (28) and (31) also directly give
\[
\int_Kg_r\,dx=\frac a2,
\qquad
\int_Kxg_r(x)\,dx=\frac a2(c+r).
\tag{37}
\]
This proves (7) and (8), with the exact half-mass scaling.

We turn to the mixed setting.  Compactness of the projection of \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) implies that only finitely many lattice points index nonempty fibers, so \(I_+\) is finite.  For \(z\in I_+\), the slice \(C_z\) is compact and convex and has positive \(d\)-dimensional volume, hence is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.  Applying (27) to \(C_z\) proves that \(\Sigma_z\) is positive definite.  Applying the conclusions (6)-(8) to \(C_z\) proves the first inclusion in (15) and all asserted compactness properties of \(\mathcal E_z\).  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` says that \(\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\) is a compact convex subset of \(C_z\), proving the second inclusion in (15).  It also gives
\[
\delta_C(z,y)\geq\gamma_d\frac{a_z}{2}
\qquad
(y\in c_z+\mathcal E_z).
\tag{38}
\]

Fix a nonempty \(E\subseteq I_+\).  Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the nonzero finite measure
\[
\nu_E:=\sum_{z\in E}a_z\delta_z
\tag{39}
\]
on \(\mathbb Z^n\).  It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfying (17).  This point lies in \(\operatorname{conv}E\).  Otherwise strict separation from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}E\) would produce a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E\), whose \(\nu_E\)-mass would be zero, contradicting (17) because \(W_E>0\).  Hence \(\Lambda_m(E)\) is nonempty.  It is the intersection of the finite-dimensional probability simplex with affine equalities, so it is a compact polytope.

For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), the set
\[
A_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z(c_z+\mathcal E_z)
\tag{40}
\]
is nonempty, compact, and convex.  The intersection (19) is convex and closed.  It is contained in \(A_{\lambda^0}\) for any fixed \(\lambda^0\in\Lambda_m(E)\), so it is compact, with the empty case allowed.  Moreover, every \(y\in A_\lambda\) has a representation
\[
y=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zy_z,
\qquad
y_z\in c_z+\mathcal E_z\subseteq C_z,
\tag{41}
\]
and therefore
\[
(m,y)=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z(z,y_z)\in C
\tag{42}
\]
by convexity.  In particular, any \(x\in\mathcal I_m(E)\) gives \((m,x)\in S\).

Apply accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` with
\[
D_z:=c_z+\mathcal E_z,
\qquad
w_z:=\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}
\qquad(z\in E).
\tag{43}
\]
The sets \(D_z\) are nonempty compact convex subsets of \(C_z\), and (38) verifies every required fiber-depth inequality.  Its barycentric core is exactly \(\mathcal I_m(E)\).  Thus every \(x\) in that core satisfies
\[
h_S(m,x)
\geq
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}.
\tag{44}
\]
Factoring out \(\gamma_d/2\) and using (17) proves (20).  Taking the supremum over points of \(S\) proves (21).  This calculation retains the exact constant
\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
=\frac12\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d;
\tag{45}
\]
no replacement by \(1/(2e)\) is made.

It remains to prove the Chebyshev-radius criterion.  Because \(B=-B\) is a convex body, it contains the origin in its interior and (22) is a norm with unit ball \(B\).  The map
\[
\lambda\longmapsto\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zc_z
\tag{46}
\]
is linear, so its image \(\mathcal C_m(E)\) of the nonempty compact polytope \(\Lambda_m(E)\) is a nonempty compact convex polytope.  Consequently
\[
x\longmapsto\max_{q\in\mathcal C_m(E)}\|x-q\|_B
\tag{47}
\]
is continuous.  It is coercive because, for any fixed \(q_0\in\mathcal C_m(E)\), it is at least \(\|x-q_0\|_B\).  Hence its minimum is attained.  The second minimum in (25) is attained because it is the minimum of a continuous linear function on \(\Lambda_m(E)\).

Assume (26), and choose a Chebyshev center \(x_*\) attaining the first minimum in (25).  For arbitrary \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), put
\[
q_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zc_z,
\qquad
s_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zr_z.
\tag{48}
\]
Then
\[
\|x_*-q_\lambda\|_B
\leq R_B(\mathcal C_m(E))
\leq\rho_m(r)
\leq s_\lambda.
\tag{49}
\]
Thus \(x_*-q_\lambda\in s_\lambda B\).  For nonnegative scalars and a convex set \(B\),
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zr_zB
=\left(\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zr_z\right)B
=s_\lambda B,
\tag{50}
\]
including the case \(s_\lambda=0\).  Using (23), (48)-(50) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_*
&\in q_\lambda+s_\lambda B\\
&=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z(c_z+r_zB)\\
&\subseteq\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z(c_z+\mathcal E_z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{51}
\]
This holds for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), so \(x_*\in\mathcal I_m(E)\).  The core is nonempty, and (20)-(21) follow.  This proves the stated sufficient condition and completes the proof.
