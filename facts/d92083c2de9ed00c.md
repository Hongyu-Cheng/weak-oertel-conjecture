## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the hypotheses and canonical normalizations of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. In particular, let \(r=(r_e)\) be the half-strength canonical product reservoir, and put
\[
 Q:=|r|>0,\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,\qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D.
\]
Assume the canonical hard inequality
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
 \bar\nu_r:=\sum_er_e\,\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}
\]
be the full reservoir centroid measure, and use its intrinsic ranks
\[
 \rho_{\bar\nu_r}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:r_e>0\},
\]
\[
 r_{\bar\nu_r}:=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:r_e>0\}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{\bar c_e-a(m_e):r_e>0\}.
\]
Define
\[
 x:=\frac{2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}}{r_{\bar\nu_r}+1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Accepted fact `a3e5d5377e0dc367` gives
\[
 0<x<\frac1{200},
 \qquad
 d\ge r_{\bar\nu_r}\ge200\,2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Use the load-minimal optimizer, load \(\Omega\), retained matching \(s=(s_e)\), and retained edge points \(F_e^\flat\) of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. Then \(s\ne0\), and its mass satisfies the exact bounds
\[
 \boxed{
 |s|\ge\frac{11}{16}Q-\frac34R-\frac{51}{128}G
 >\frac{17}{25}Q.}
\tag{4}
\]
Let
\[
 \sigma_s:=\gamma_d\sum_es_e\,\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and let \(\rho_s,r_s\) be its canonical intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. Define
\[
 u:=\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_s}}{r_s+1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{0<u<\frac1{68}},
 \qquad
 \boxed{x+\frac{11}{16}u<\frac1{100}}.
\tag{6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 u<\frac4{275}(1-100x),}
\tag{7}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 r_s+1>
 \frac{275\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_s}}
 {4(1-100x)}
 >\frac{275\gamma_d}{4}\,2^{n-\rho_s}.}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular,
\[
 r_s\ge
 \left\lfloor
 \frac{275\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_s}}
 {4(1-100x)}
 \right\rfloor.
\tag{9}
\]
The two base-rank deficiencies satisfy the further exact tradeoff
\[
 \boxed{
 2^{n-\rho_s}
 <\frac{4\bigl(d+1-100\,2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}\bigr)}
 {275\gamma_d}.}
\tag{10}
\]
The numerator in (10) is positive by (3).

Equations (6)--(10) concern the full centroid measure and the full positive support of the retained edge-point measure. They do not assert a lower bound on the base or residual ranks of the actual I-bad edge subfamily, of a triangle subfamily, or of the selected or averaged labelled-output points used in the O-branch.

## Proof

The hard inequality (1) implies \(R<Q/100<Q\). Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives
\[
 \Omega\ge Q-R,
 \qquad
 Q\ge\frac3{32}(W-4G),
\tag{11}
\]
as well as
\[
 \sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{retained}}L_\xi
 \ge2\Omega-G-\frac W{64},
 \qquad
 |s|_\xi\ge\frac38L_\xi.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular \(\Omega>0\), so the retained construction is defined. Summing (12), using (11), and rearranging the second inequality in (11) as
\[
 W\le\frac{32}{3}Q+4G,
\tag{13}
\]
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |s|
 &\ge\frac38\left(2\Omega-G-\frac W{64}\right)\\
 &\ge\frac38\left(2Q-2R-G-\frac W{64}\right)\\
 &\ge\frac{11}{16}Q-\frac34R-\frac{51}{128}G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
All summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\) are nonnegative, so (1) gives
\[
 R+G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(51/128<3/4\), equations (14)--(15) yield
\[
 |s|
 \ge\frac{11}{16}Q-\frac34(R+G)
 >\left(\frac{11}{16}-\frac3{400}\right)Q
 =\frac{17}{25}Q.
\tag{16}
\]
This proves (4), including \(s\ne0\).

Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` proves for the full retained edge-point measure that
\[
 D\ge
 \frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_s}|s|}{r_s+1}
 =u|s|.
\tag{17}
\]
Because \(D\) is a nonnegative summand of \(\mathcal A(C)\), equations (1), (16), and (17) give
\[
 u<\frac{Q}{100|s|}<\frac1{68}.
\tag{18}
\]
This proves the preliminary bound in (6).

By the full-reservoir visibility inequality in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` and the definition (2),
\[
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\ge xQ.
\tag{19}
\]
In the following accounting, \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\) and \(D\) are two distinct summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\). The single \(D\)-summand is bounded only by (17); no second lower bound is added inside \(D\). Equations (14), (17), and (19) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A(C)
 &\ge M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)+D+R+G\\
 &\ge xQ+u|s|+R+G\\
 &\ge\left(x+\frac{11}{16}u\right)Q
   +\left(1-\frac34u\right)R
   +\left(1-\frac{51}{128}u\right)G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The preliminary bound \(u<1/68\) implies the exact positive-coefficient audits
\[
 1-\frac34u>1-\frac3{272}=\frac{269}{272}>0,
\]
\[
 1-\frac{51}{128}u
 >1-\frac{51}{8704}=\frac{8653}{8704}>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Discarding the last two nonnegative terms in (20), and then using (1), proves
\[
 x+\frac{11}{16}u<\frac1{100}.
\]
This is the second inequality in (6). Solving it for \(u\) gives (7). Because (3) implies \(0<x<1/200\), the denominator \(1-100x\) is positive. Substituting (5) into (7), and using \(x>0\) for the second strict comparison, proves (8). Since \(r_s\) is an integer, (8) implies (9).

It remains to derive the base-rank tradeoff. The zero affine map is admissible in both residual-rank definitions, so
\[
 r_s\le d,
 \qquad
 r_{\bar\nu_r}\le d.
\tag{22}
\]
The second inequality gives
\[
 x=
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}}{r_{\bar\nu_r}+1}
 \ge\frac{2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}}{d+1}.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining the first inequality in (22) with the first inequality in (8) yields
\[
 d+1>
 \frac{275\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_s}}
 {4(1-100x)}.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation (23) implies
\[
 1-100x
 \le\frac{d+1-100\,2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}}{d+1}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substituting (25) into (24), cancelling \(d+1>0\), and rearranging proves (10). Accepted fact `a3e5d5377e0dc367` gives
\(d\ge200\,2^{n-\rho_{\bar\nu_r}}\), so the numerator in (10) is indeed positive.

Finally, high residual rank on a full support does not pass to an arbitrary positive-mass subfamily, and affine averaging can further lower rank. The I-branch uses an actual bad-edge or triangle subfamily, while the O-branch uses labelled averages \(q_{\xi,c}^{\circ}\). Neither accepted predecessor supplies a mass-sensitive rank-inheritance theorem from \(\bar\nu_r\) or \(\sigma_s\) to those objects. Therefore no I/O rank conclusion beyond the explicit global statements above is used or claimed.
