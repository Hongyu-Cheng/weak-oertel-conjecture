## Statement

There is a convex body in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2$ with exactly three positive integer fibers and a three-cap cover of all ambient space by closed cylindrical halfspaces with a strictly positive dependence of continuous normals, each of mixed mass $M/3<\Gamma_2M$, where $\Gamma_2=4/9$ and $M$ is the total mixed mass.

## Proof

Use integer bases $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ and in their continuous planes place disks of radius $0<\rho<1/2$ centered at the three unit vectors $u_i$ at angles $0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3$. Take the convex hull of these three lifted disks. The base triangle has only its vertices as integer sites, and each lifted vertex fiber is its own disk. The hull has ambient interior since the bases affinely span the base plane and a disk has interior in its continuous plane.

The caps $u_i\cdot x\ge0$ cover the ambient space because $\sum_i u_i=0$. The dot product on their own disk is at least $1-\rho>0$. On either other disk it is at most $-1/2+\rho<0$. Each cap therefore has exactly one disk's mass, which is $M/3$.
