## Statement

Let \(L\ge1\), and let \(m_1,\ldots,m_L\ge1\) be arbitrary integers. Independently in each block \(\ell\), choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(B_{m_\ell}=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{m_\ell}\). Use the objects of accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d for that partition, with explicit block aliases
$$
(B_\ell,D_\ell,F_\ell,i_\ell,E_\ell,T_\ell,C_\ell^{\mathrm{bool}})
=(B_{m_\ell},D_{m_\ell},F_{m_\ell},i_{m_\ell},E_{m_\ell},T_{m_\ell},C_{m_\ell}^{\mathrm{bool}}).
$$
Thus \(D_\ell\) consists of nonterminal chain elements, \(F_\ell(c)=c+e_{i_\ell(c)}\), \(c_{i_\ell(c)}=0\), and
$$
E_\ell=\lbrace c+2e_{i_\ell(c)}:c\in D_\ell\rbrace,\qquad
T_\ell=\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\cap\mathbb Z^{m_\ell}
=E_\ell\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_\ell^{\mathrm{bool}}.
$$
The coordinate unit vector \(e_i\) belongs to the current block. The partitions may differ even when two block widths agree. For arbitrary fields \(\gamma_\ell\in\mathbb R^{m_\ell}\), put
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in T_\ell}e^{\gamma_\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=Z_\ell^{-1}e^{\gamma_\ell\cdot x}\quad(x\in T_\ell),\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell.
$$
Every law is extended by zero off its specified support. Retain exactly the definitions \(\tau_p,H_p,b(p),M_2(p)\) of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc: for a finite law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_j p_jH_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_2(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displacement sum is oriented, with zero occurring once. Then, uniformly in the widths, partitions, fields, and number of blocks,
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)
\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell).
\tag{1}
$$
In particular, the requested bound with prefactor \(1/16\) and exponent \(4\sqrt{2L/3}\) holds.

More precisely, for \(j\in T_\ell\) define the actual reflected row and its uniform law by
$$
R_{\ell,j}=\lbrace x\in T_\ell:2j-x\in T_\ell\rbrace,\qquad
r_{\ell,j}=|R_{\ell,j}|,\qquad
\nu_{\ell,j}(x)=\frac1{r_{\ell,j}}\quad(x\in R_{\ell,j}).
$$
With \(V_{\ell,j}=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\ell,j}}(\gamma_\ell\cdot(X-j))\), the exact likelihood mean and the uniform weighted variance estimate are
$$
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\ell,j}}\log\frac{\nu_{\ell,j}(X)}{p_{\ell,X}}
=-\log H_{p_\ell}(j),\qquad
\sum_{j\in T_\ell}\frac{p_{\ell,j}H_{p_\ell}(j)^2}{b(p_\ell)}V_{\ell,j}
\le\frac23.
\tag{2}
$$
The variance of the displayed log-likelihood is exactly \(V_{\ell,j}\). For the original cubic moment \(M_3(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3\), an immediate consequence is
$$
M_3(P)\ge\frac18\exp\left(-3\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right)
\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)^{3/2}.
\tag{3}
$$
All depths in (1) and (3) range over all closed halfspaces in the full original ambient space \(\mathbb R^{\sum_\ell m_\ell}\), and their averaging law is the original product \(P\).

## Proof

Fix one block, suppress its block index, and retain its field \(\gamma\). Accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d supplies the decomposition \(T=E\mathbin{\dot\cup}C^{\mathrm{bool}}\), the displayed successor construction, and injectivity of \(F\). The set \(E\) is nonempty because the symmetric chain containing the rank-zero element has a successor when \(m\ge1\). Thus \(T\) is finite and nonempty, \(Z>0\), and \(p_x>0\) for every \(x\in T\).

We first determine every reflected row and account for its endpoints. Each point of \(E\) has exactly one coordinate equal to two and all others Boolean; its coordinatewise reduction modulo two is its unique source \(c\in D\). Therefore two Boolean points with equal parity coincide, and two points of \(E\) with equal parity also coincide. If a Boolean point and a point of \(E\) have equal parity, they are precisely \(c\) and \(c+2e_{i(c)}\) for the source of that spike. Their midpoint is \(F(c)\). Distinct integer endpoints can have an integer midpoint only when they have equal parity, so these exhaust all distinct reflected pairs in \(T\).

Such a pair lies in \(T\) exactly when \(c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D\). Its midpoint \(F(c)\) then also lies in \(T\), by convexity and completeness. Injectivity of \(F\) ensures that different such pairs have different centers. Consequently every row at a supported target \(j\in T\) is either the singleton \(\lbrace j\rbrace\), or, uniquely for some \(c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D\),
$$
j=F(c),\qquad
R_j=\lbrace c,j,c+2e_{i(c)}\rbrace.
\tag{4}
$$
The endpoint sets \(\lbrace c,c+2e_{i(c)}\rbrace\) in (4) are pairwise disjoint: Boolean sources are distinct, spike endpoints are distinct because their parities recover their sources, and a Boolean source is never a spike endpoint. Centers are allowed to be endpoints of other pairs; no disjointness of centers is needed. For an integer target outside \(T\), its reflected row is empty, since any two endpoints in \(T\) would have that target as an integer midpoint in \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\). No uniform row or likelihood is defined at such unsupported targets, and they contribute zero to every original-law sum.

At a supported target, \(R_j\) is invariant under \(x\mapsto2j-x\), so its uniform law \(\nu_j\) has mean \(j\). Log-affinity gives \(\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=p_j\) throughout \(R_j\), hence
$$
H_p(j)=r_jp_j,\qquad
b(p)=\sum_{j\in T}r_j^2p_j^3>0,\qquad
\log\frac{\nu_j(x)}{p_x}
=-\log H_p(j)-\gamma\cdot(x-j).
\tag{5}
$$
This proves the likelihood mean assertion in (2). A singleton row has \(V_j=0\); in a row (4), the centered field score takes values \(-\gamma_{i(c)},0,\gamma_{i(c)}\), each with probability \(1/3\), so
$$
V_{F(c)}=\frac23\gamma_{i(c)}^2.
\tag{6}
$$
In particular the likelihood variance is \(V_j\), including when a nontrivial row has zero field score.

Define the auxiliary center weights \(q_j=p_jH_p(j)^2/b(p)=r_j^2p_j^3/b(p)\). Equation (5) proves that they sum to one. They are used only for analysis and do not change the source law \(p\). For each row (4), log-affinity gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_c^3+p_{c+2e_{i(c)}}^3-2p_{F(c)}^3
&=2p_{F(c)}^3\bigl(\cosh(3\gamma_{i(c)})-1\bigr)\\
&\ge9p_{F(c)}^3\gamma_{i(c)}^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
where \(\cosh t-1\ge t^2/2\) follows, for example, from its power series with nonnegative even coefficients. Endpoint disjointness and nonnegativity imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
9\sum_{c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D}p_{F(c)}^3\gamma_{i(c)}^2
&\le\sum_{c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D}
\bigl(p_c^3+p_{c+2e_{i(c)}}^3-2p_{F(c)}^3\bigr)\\
&\le\sum_{x\in T}p_x^3\le b(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Using (6), \(r_{F(c)}=3\), and the fact that all remaining rows have zero variance yields
$$
\sum_{j\in T}q_jV_j
=\frac6{b(p)}\sum_{c\in C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D}
p_{F(c)}^3\gamma_{i(c)}^2
\le\frac23.
\tag{9}
$$
An empty set \(C^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D\) makes these sums zero, so this argument covers blocks having no nontrivial row. This proves (2) for each block, without any restriction on its width or field.

Now fix a product target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\in\prod_\ell T_\ell\). Its exact reflected row is \(R_J=\prod_\ell R_{\ell,j_\ell}\), whose uniform law is \(\nu_J=\bigotimes_\ell\nu_{\ell,j_\ell}\). Write
$$
Y_J(X)=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\gamma_\ell\cdot(X^{(\ell)}-j_\ell),\qquad
\sigma_J^2=\sum_{\ell=1}^L V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
$$
Independence and the block means give \(\mathbb E_{\nu_J}Y_J=0\) and \(\mathbb E_{\nu_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2\). The exact reflected-mass product identity of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc, together with (5), gives
$$
H_P(J)=\prod_\ell H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell),\qquad
P_X=H_P(J)\nu_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}\quad(X\in R_J).
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed, the rightmost expression equals \(\prod_\ell p_{\ell,j_\ell}e^{\gamma_\ell\cdot(X^{(\ell)}-j_\ell)}\), which is exactly the original product probability \(P_X=\exp(\sum_\ell\gamma_\ell\cdot X^{(\ell)})/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\). Thus every source normalizer in (10) is the original one.

Let \(\mathcal H\) be any closed halfspace in the full product ambient space containing \(J\). For each distinct antipodal pair \(X,2J-X\) in \(R_J\), at least one endpoint belongs to \(\mathcal H\): otherwise the linear inequality defining the halfspace would also exclude their midpoint \(J\). The central point \(J\) also belongs to \(\mathcal H\). Summing over these reflection orbits therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\nu_J}e^{-|Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\nu_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The first sum counts each distinct pair twice and the central point once; at the central orbit the right side uses only half its available original mass, so the inequality holds there too. For the equality use (10), central symmetry of \(\nu_J\), and \(Y_J(2J-X)=-Y_J(X)\). The next step is Jensen's inequality for the convex function \(t\mapsto e^{-t}\); the last is Cauchy-Schwarz. These statements remain valid when \(\sigma_J=0\), since then \(Y_J=0\) throughout its finite positive row. No truncation, division by variance, or limiting argument is used. Taking the infimum over all such original ambient halfspaces proves \(\tau_P(J)\ge H_P(J)e^{-\sigma_J}/2\).

For each block use its auxiliary center weights \(q_{\ell,j}=p_{\ell,j}H_{p_\ell}(j)^2/b(p_\ell)\), and let \(q=\bigotimes_\ell q_\ell\). Squaring (11) and summing against the original target law \(P\), the exact factorization (10) shows
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_q e^{-2\sigma_J}.
\tag{12}
$$
In particular \(q\) appears only after factoring the original summand \(P_JH_P(J)^2\); its normalizer is exactly \(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\). A second application of Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz, followed by (9), gives
$$
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}
\ge e^{-2\mathbb E_q\sigma_J}
\ge e^{-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2}}
\ge\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3}}\right).
\tag{13}
$$
Equations (12) and (13) prove (1). If every contributing variance is zero, the same calculation gives the stronger factor \(1/4\) with no exponential loss. The displayed argument covers singleton supports and lower-dimensional block supports because its halfspace step always took place in the full original ambient space.

Finally Jensen's inequality for \(t\mapsto t^{3/2}\), under the original probability \(P\), gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}
\ge\bigl(\mathbb E_P\tau_P(J)^2\bigr)^{3/2}
=M_2(P)^{3/2}.
$$
Substituting (1) proves (3), completing the proof.
