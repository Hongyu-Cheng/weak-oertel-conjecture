## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, and let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. Put $p=(d+1)/(d+2)$ and let $E_K(y)$ be the minimum of $\int_K[1+a\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-1}\,dx$ over affine denominators strictly positive on $K$. Then
$$
E_K(y)=\max_{\substack{\rho\ge0,\ \int_K\rho\,dx=1\\\int_K x\rho(x)\,dx=y}}
\left(\int_K\rho(x)^p\,dx\right)^{d+2}.
$$
The maximum is over ordinary Lebesgue densities on $K$ with the displayed mass and mean. Its unique maximizer up to Lebesgue null sets is $\rho=L^{-d-2}/E_K(y)$, where $L(x)=1+a_*\cdot(x-y)$ is the attained optimal denominator.

## Proof

For any density $\rho$ in the maximum and any admissible denominator $L_a=1+a\cdot(x-y)$, the mass and mean constraints give $\int_K\rho L_a\,dx=1$. Hölder with exponents $1/p$ and $d+2$ applied to $\rho^p=(\rho L_a)^p L_a^{-p}$ gives
$$
\int_K\rho^p\,dx
\le \left(\int_K \rho L_a\,dx\right)^p
     \left(\int_K L_a^{-d-1}\,dx\right)^{1/(d+2)}
=\left(\int_K L_a^{-d-1}\,dx\right)^{1/(d+2)}.
$$
Minimizing over $a$ yields the upper bound. Let $E=E_K(y)$ and use the attained denominator $L$. The escort lemma gives feasibility of $\rho_*=L^{-d-2}/E$, and $\int_K \rho_*^p\,dx=E^{-p}\int_K L^{-d-1}\,dx=E^{1/(d+2)}$. Equality in Hölder with this $L$ requires $\rho L$ to be proportional to $L^{-d-1}$ almost everywhere. The normalization fixes the proportionality constant as $1/E$, proving uniqueness.
