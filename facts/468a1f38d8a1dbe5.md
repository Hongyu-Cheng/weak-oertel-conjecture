## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-branch of accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70` and `7ec63df101c30343`. Use all their canonical normalizations, in particular
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
\mathcal A(C):=
R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
+R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained matching, let \(\mathcal E_I\) be the canonical family of \(I\)-bad opposite edges, and write
\[
I:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I}s_e>\frac Q4.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(t_e\) be the actual opposite-edge triangle masses. Let \(\mathcal B\) be the exceptional family from accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`, so that
\[
\sum_{e\in\mathcal B}s_e\le G,
\qquad
t_e\ge\frac18s_e\quad(e\notin\mathcal B).
\tag{3}
\]

For \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
\Theta_I(\{c\}):=
\sum_\xi\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi,\ m_e=c\\e\in\mathcal E_I}}t_e,
\qquad
E:=\{c\in\mathbb Z^n:\Theta_I(\{c\})>0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus
\[
\Theta_I(E):=\sum_{c\in E}\Theta_I(\{c\})>\frac{3Q}{100}.
\tag{5}
\]
At each \(c\in E\), choose a retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing its contribution \(\Theta_{\xi,c}\), put \(q_c:=q^\circ_{\xi(c),c}\), and define
\[
\rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
\]
\[
r_E:=
\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-a(c):c\in E\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Assume
\[
\rho_E=2,
\qquad
r_E\le2.
\tag{7}
\]

Remove the exceptional edges and put
\[
J:=\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B,
\qquad
S:=\sum_{e\in J}s_e.
\tag{8}
\]
Use the canonical centroid notation \(\bar c_e\) and edge points \(F_e^\flat\) from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, and define
\[
\sigma_M:=\sum_{e\in J}\frac{s_e}{16}\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)},
\qquad
\sigma_F:=\gamma_d\sum_{e\in J}s_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(\rho_M,r_M\) and \(\rho_F,r_F\) be their intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks in the exact sense of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Then \(S>0\), both measures in (9) are nonzero, and
\[
\rho_M\le2,
\qquad
\rho_F\le2,
\qquad
r_M,r_F\le d.
\tag{10}
\]
Put
\[
H_{M,F}:=
\frac{2^{n-\rho_M-4}}{r_M+1}
+\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_F}}{r_F+1}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then every configuration satisfying (1)--(10) necessarily obeys the strict harmonic-rank escape inequality
\[
\boxed{
H_{M,F}<\frac{13-16\gamma_d}{397}.}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{2^{n-6}}{r_M+1}
+\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-2}}{r_F+1}
<\frac{13-16\gamma_d}{397}.}
\tag{13}
\]
Equivalently, no canonical hard \(I\)-branch configuration satisfying (7) can have the reverse weak inequality in either (12) or (13).

The automatic bounds \(r_M,r_F\le d\) give the all-\((n,d)\) scalar consequence
\[
\boxed{
d+1>
397\,2^{n-6}\frac{1+16\gamma_d}{13-16\gamma_d}.}
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover, the right-hand side of (14) is strictly larger than \(6\,2^n\). Hence
\[
\boxed{d+1>6\,2^n.}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently, for arbitrary positive integers \(n,d\), the rank-\((2,\le2)\) subcase is impossible whenever
\[
d+1\le
397\,2^{n-6}\frac{1+16\gamma_d}{13-16\gamma_d},
\tag{16}
\]
and in particular whenever \(d+1\le6\,2^n\). Since (7) forces \(n\ge2\), and the hard branch in fact forces \(n\ge3\), the latter condition recovers \(d\le47\) and extends it uniformly to all \((n,d)\). No conclusion is asserted when (14) holds.

## Proof

Accepted fact `e2263a787ace07a4`, applied to the full positive target support \(E\), gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
\ge
\frac{4\gamma_d}{2^{\rho_E}(r_E+1)}\Theta_I(E)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{3}\Theta_I(E)
>\frac{\gamma_d}{100}Q.
\tag{17}
\]
Accepted fact `07889c9b4686d8b4`, applied to the occupied rank-two plane \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\), gives
\[
\Theta_I(E)\le16G.
\tag{18}
\]
The equality between its plane mass and \(\Theta_I(E)\) holds because \(E\) is the complete positive target support in (4). Combining (5) and (18), and writing
\[
g:=\frac GQ,
\tag{19}
\]
gives
\[
\frac3{1600}<g<\frac1{100}.
\tag{20}
\]

For completeness, the hard configuration has \(n\ge3\). Assumption \(\rho_E=2\) first gives \(n\ge2\). If \(n=2\), then \(E\) spans \(\mathbb R^2\) and is contained in the occupied base support. The full-rank clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` gives \(G\ge W/64\). Accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` gives \(Q\le W/8\), so \(G\ge Q/8\), contradicting (1). Thus
\[
n\ge3.
\tag{21}
\]

Equations (2)--(3) give
\[
S
=I-\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I\cap\mathcal B}s_e
\ge I-G
>Q\left(\frac14-g\right)>0.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(e\in J\) and \(s_e>0\), then (3) gives \(t_e>0\). Hence \(\Theta_I(\{m_e\})>0\), so
\[
m_e\in E
\qquad(e\in J,\ s_e>0).
\tag{23}
\]
This proves the base-rank bounds in (10). The residual ranks are at most the continuous ambient dimension \(d\).

Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives \(s_e/16\le r_e\) and embeds the reservoir centroid measure into an exact minimum-unmatched centroid measure. Therefore \(\sigma_M\) is an admissible nonzero submeasure in the definition of \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
&\ge
2^{n-\rho_M}\frac{|\sigma_M|}{r_M+1}\\
&=
\frac{2^{n-\rho_M-4}}{r_M+1}S.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
The same accepted fact proves that the full edge-point measure
\(\gamma_d\sum_es_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}\) is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\). Its restriction \(\sigma_F\) is therefore halfspace-dominated as well. The dominated-measure residual theorem in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\ge
\frac{|\sigma_F|}{2^{\rho_F}(r_F+1)}
=\frac{\gamma_dS}{2^{\rho_F}(r_F+1)},
\]
and consequently
\[
D=2^nh_{\max}(C)
\ge
\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_F}}{r_F+1}S.
\tag{25}
\]

The quantities \(G\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and \(D\) are four distinct nonnegative summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\). Discarding only the other nonnegative summands and using (17), (22), (24), and (25) yields
\[
\frac{\mathcal A(C)}Q
>
g+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
+H_{M,F}\left(\frac14-g\right).
\tag{26}
\]

We now minimize the right-hand side exactly over the entire allowed interval (20). First note that
\[
0<\frac{13-16\gamma_d}{397}<1.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, the binomial theorem gives
\((1+1/d)^d\ge2\), hence \(\gamma_d\le1/2\), so the numerator in (27) is at least \(5\); it is also less than \(13\).

Suppose contrary to (12) that
\[
H_{M,F}\ge h_d,
\qquad
h_d:=\frac{13-16\gamma_d}{397}.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(H_{M,F}\ge1\), then the right-hand side of (26) is at least
\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{100}+g+\frac14-g
>\frac1{100},
\]
which contradicts (1). Suppose instead that \(h_d\le H_{M,F}<1\). The affine function
\[
g\longmapsto
g+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
+H_{M,F}\left(\frac14-g\right)
\tag{29}
\]
has slope \(1-H_{M,F}>0\). By (20), its value is strictly larger than its value at \(g=3/1600\), and (28) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
g+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
+H_{M,F}\left(\frac14-g\right)
&>
\frac3{1600}+\frac{\gamma_d}{100}
+h_d\frac{397}{1600}\\
&=
\frac{3+16\gamma_d+13-16\gamma_d}{1600}
=\frac1{100}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Again (26) contradicts (1). This proves (12). Since \(\rho_M,\rho_F\le2\), equation (11) gives
\[
H_{M,F}
\ge
\frac{2^{n-6}}{r_M+1}
+\frac{\gamma_d2^{n-2}}{r_F+1},
\]
so (13) follows. Using \(r_M,r_F\le d\) once more gives
\[
H_{M,F}
\ge
\frac{2^{n-6}(1+16\gamma_d)}{d+1}.
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (12) and (31), then using the positivity in (27), proves (14) by exact rearrangement.

It remains to verify the uniform rational comparison in (15). The sequence \(\gamma_d\) is strictly decreasing: the expansion
\[
\gamma_d^{-1}
=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
=\sum_{j=0}^d\frac1{j!}
\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\left(1-\frac{i}{d}\right)
\tag{32}
\]
strictly increases from \(d\) to \(d+1\), because every pre-existing factor is nondecreasing, every term with \(j\ge2\) strictly increases, and one positive terminal term is added. Also
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e,
\qquad
\gamma_d>\frac1e.
\tag{33}
\]
For a completely rational lower bound, the exponential series satisfies
\[
e
=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac1{j!}
<\frac{163}{60}
+\frac{1/720}{1-1/7}
=\frac{11743}{4320}
<\frac{87}{32}.
\tag{34}
\]
Here \(163/60\) is the sum through \(j=5\), and every successive ratio in the tail beginning at \(j=6\) is at most \(1/7\). Hence
\[
\gamma_d>\frac1e>\frac{32}{87}>\frac{4595}{12496},
\tag{35}
\]
where the last comparison is the exact integer calculation
\[
32\cdot12496-87\cdot4595=107>0.
\tag{36}
\]
The rational function
\[
x\longmapsto\frac{1+16x}{13-16x}
\]
is strictly increasing on \((0,1/2]\), since its derivative is
\[
\frac{224}{(13-16x)^2}>0.
\tag{37}
\]
The defining identity \(12496\cdot(4595/12496)=4595\) is equivalent to
\[
397\left(1+16\frac{4595}{12496}\right)
=384\left(13-16\frac{4595}{12496}\right).
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (35)--(38) therefore give
\[
397\,2^{n-6}
\frac{1+16\gamma_d}{13-16\gamma_d}
>384\,2^{n-6}
=6\,2^n.
\tag{39}
\]
Combining (14) and (39) proves (15). The impossibility assertions (16) and its simpler sufficient condition are the contrapositives of (14) and (15). Finally, (21) and \(d\le47\) imply \(d+1\le48=6\cdot2^3\le6\cdot2^n\), so the accepted bounded-dimensional exclusion is recovered as a special case. No step proves a contradiction once the strict escape inequality (14) is allowed.
