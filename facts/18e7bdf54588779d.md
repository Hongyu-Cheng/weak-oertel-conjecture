## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and $H>0$. Put $B=H+1$ and $r(z)=(H-z)/B$. On $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, take density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$ with respect to $dx\,dz$. The total mass is $V=B/d$. For finite $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of the cap $z+sx\ge0$. Let $C(s)$ and $M(s)$ be the integrals of $f$ and $zf$, respectively, over the section $z+sx=0$ inside the support, against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. The records of a probability law are the averages of $(P,C,M)$, and the law is centered if its averaged $M$ is zero. Write $C_0=(H/B)^{d-1}$ and $p_0=(H/B)^d$.

For a probability law on finite slopes, let $\phi$ be the mixture of uniform probability laws on $[-s,s]$ with the given slope weights and with $\delta_0$ at $s=0$. Put $v=\phi((-\infty,1])$, $a_0=1/B$, and $e(q)=\int_{b\le1}(1-qb)^{-d}\,d\phi(b)$ for $0<q<1$. Then $1/2<v\le1$ and the averaged records are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline P/V &= p_0e(a_0)+1-v,\\
\overline C/C_0 &= e(a_0),\\
\overline M/C_0 &= -\frac HB\int_{b\le1} b(1-b/B)^{-d-1}\,d\phi(b).
\end{aligned}
$$

The function $e$ is convex and differentiable on $(0,1)$. Centering is equivalent to $e'(a_0)=0$, or equivalently to $a_0$ minimizing $e$ on $(0,1)$. Every such centered law satisfies $2v-1\le e(a_0)\le(2v-1)/v$.

## Proof

For each finite slope, apply fact 1f86e3e352aa0225 to the point mass at that slope in the frustum with the same $d,H$ and upper endpoint $0<U<H$. As $U\uparrow H$, its $T=U/r(U)$ tends to infinity and its $t=r(U)^d$ tends to zero. The section and cap converge to their cone counterparts by restriction to the increasing axial intervals, with the omitted apex contributing zero mass and zero section length. The signed moment also converges, since $|z|\le\max(1,H)$ and the coarea is finite. In the ray formulas, $tw$ is bounded by $t$ and the cutoff $[-T,1]$ tends to $(-\infty,1]$. The coarea and moment kernels $(1-b/B)^{-d}$ and $b(1-b/B)^{-d-1}$ are bounded on that halfline. Taking the limit gives the displayed formulas for a single layer, and averaging the bounded formulas gives them for every law of finite slopes. Each finite layer has more than half its mass in $b\le1$ and has zero mass at $b=1$, so both properties hold for $\phi$.

On a compact subinterval of $0<q<1$, the functions $|b|^j(1-qb)^{-d-j}$ for $j=0,1,2$ are bounded uniformly on $b\le1$. On $[0,1]$ the denominators have a positive lower bound, and on $b<0$ they grow at least linearly in $|b|$ for large $|b|$. Differentiating under the integral gives $e''(q)=d(d+1)\int_{b\le1}b^2(1-qb)^{-d-2}\,d\phi(b)\ge0$. The moment formula gives $\overline M/C_0=-(H/B)e'(a_0)/d$, yielding the equivalences.

The mass of $[-1,1]$ is $2v-1$ by symmetry. Jensen's inequality on the normalized restriction to this interval gives an inverse-power mean of at least one, because its mean $b$ is zero. This proves the lower bound even without centering. If $v=1$, symmetry gives support in $[-1,1]$ and continuity at $q=0$ gives $e(a_0)\le e(0)=1$ by minimality.

For $v<1$, set $k=1-v\in(0,1/2)$ and compare at $q=2k/d\in(0,1)$. For each $b\le1$, $(1-(2k/d)b)^{-d}\le(1-kb)^{-2}$. Indeed for $A=-2kb>-2$ the logarithmic derivative of $(1+A/p)^{-p}$ with respect to $p\ge2$ is $-\log(1+A/p)+(A/p)/(1+A/p)\le0$. The sign follows from $\log(1+y)\ge y/(1+y)$ for $y>-1$, since the derivative of their difference is $y/(1+y)^2$ and the difference is zero at $y=0$.

On a layer of slope $s$, put $x=\min(1/s,1)$ for $s>0$ and $x=1$ for $s=0$. The layer puts mass $(1+x)/2$ in $b\le1$, so the layer expectation of $x$ is $2v-1$. The exponent-two integral on this captured part equals $I_k(x)=x(1+x)/[2(1-k)(x+k)]$ for $s\ge1$, by integrating $(1-kb)^{-2}$ from $-s$ to $1$ against $db/(2s)$. For $0<s<1$ it equals $(1-k^2s^2)^{-1}\le I_k(1)$, and for $s=0$ it equals $1\le I_k(1)$. Since $I_k''(x)=-k/(x+k)^3<0$ on $[0,1]$, Jensen gives

$$
e(a_0)\le e(2k/d)\le\int_{b\le1}(1-kb)^{-2}\,d\phi(b)
\le I_k(2v-1)=\frac{2v-1}{v}.
$$
