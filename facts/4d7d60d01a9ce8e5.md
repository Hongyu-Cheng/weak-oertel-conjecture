## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge0\) be integers, let \(j_1,\ldots,j_n\) be nonnegative integers, and let \(A=(a_{ri})\in\mathbb R^{k\times n}\) be arbitrary. Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_n\) be independent, with \(V_i\) uniform on the integers \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Write \(a_rV:=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}V_i\), and let \(\sigma_r\) be the nonnegative number satisfying \(\sigma_r^2=\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}^2j_i(j_i+1)/3\). Then
\[
\mathbb P\bigl(|a_rV|\le\sigma_r\text{ for every }r\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\bigr)
\ge \exp(-C_{\mathrm{slab}}k),
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{slab}}:=\frac{2\log 64}{1-\sqrt{7/8}}.
\]
Here \(\log\) is the natural logarithm. In particular, rows with \(\sigma_r=0\) are included.

## Proof

Use the particular symmetric joint laws \(K_j\) and their common marginals \(q_j\) constructed in accepted fact 7f35aa79638b5745. These are the predecessor's objects with the same notation and normalization: both coordinates take values in \(\{0,\ldots,j\}\), their difference is exactly uniform on \(\{-j,\ldots,j\}\), and \(K_j(x,y)\ge\tfrac18q_j(x)q_j(y)\).

We first establish the additional centering property of that construction. Its constant component \(1/[2(j+1)^2]\) is invariant under the central reflection \((x,y)\mapsto(j-x,j-y)\). The residual mass indexed by a difference \(h\) has the same weight as the residual mass indexed by \(-h\), because its weight depends only on \(|h|\). If \(j+h\) is even, reflection sends its placement \(((j+h)/2,(j-h)/2)\) to the placement for \(-h\). If \(j+h\) is odd, reflection sends
\[
\left(\frac{j+h-1}{2},\frac{j-h-1}{2}\right)
\quad\text{to}\quad
\left(\frac{j-h+1}{2},\frac{j+h+1}{2}\right),
\]
and sends the placement with both signs \(+1\) to the placement for \(-h\) with both signs \(-1\). The two odd-parity placements have equal weights. Thus the construction satisfies \(K_j(j-x,j-y)=K_j(x,y)\), and summing over the second coordinate gives \(q_j(j-x)=q_j(x)\). Therefore, for \(X\) of law \(q_j\), \(\mathbb E X=j/2\). Since \(|X-j/2|\le j/2\), we also have \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\le j^2/4\). This includes \(j=0\).

Choose independent paired coordinates \((X_i,Y_i)\), \(1\le i\le n\), with joint laws \(K_{j_i}\). Let \(\Omega:=\prod_{i=1}^n\{0,\ldots,j_i\}\) and \(q:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^nq_{j_i}\). Both \(X=(X_i)\) and \(Y=(Y_i)\) have law \(q\). Here these are exactly the objects in accepted fact 58a99d8c7edb912b under the aliases \(\Omega_i=\{0,\ldots,j_i\}\), \(q_i=q_{j_i}\), \(K_i=K_{j_i}\), and \(\beta=1/8\). That fact consequently gives, for every event \(E\subseteq\Omega\),
\[
\mathbb P(X\in E,Y\in E)\ge q(E)^\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma:=\frac{2}{1-\sqrt{7/8}}.
\]
Thus \(\gamma\) is the predecessor's exponent \(C_\beta\) at \(\beta=1/8\), and \(C_{\mathrm{slab}}=\gamma\log64\).

Let \(I:=\{r:\sigma_r>0\}\) and \(s:=|I|\). If \(\sigma_r=0\), every nonnegative summand in its defining sum vanishes. Hence \(a_{ri}=0\) whenever \(j_i>0\), while \(V_i=0\) whenever \(j_i=0\). It follows that \(a_rV=0\) almost surely for every such row. If \(s=0\), all required inequalities hold with probability one, proving the claim, including the case \(k=0\). We may therefore assume \(s\ge1\).

For \(x\in\Omega\) and \(r\in I\), define the normalized centered image
\(Z_r(x):=\sigma_r^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}(x_i-j_i/2)\). The preceding mean calculation and independence of the coordinates under \(q\) show that \(\mathbb E Z_r(X)=0\) and
\[
\mathbb E Z_r(X)^2
=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}^2\operatorname{Var}(X_i)}{\sigma_r^2}
\le\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n a_{ri}^2j_i^2/4}{\sigma_r^2}
\le\frac34.
\]
The last inequality uses \(j_i^2\le j_i(j_i+1)\). By Cauchy-Schwarz, \(\mathbb E|Z_r(X)|\le\sqrt3/2\le1\). Consequently \(\mathbb E\sum_{r\in I}|Z_r(X)|\le s\). Markov's inequality implies that the event
\(B:=\{x\in\Omega:\sum_{r\in I}|Z_r(x)|\le2s\}\) satisfies \(q(B)\ge1/2\).

Partition \(\mathbb R^I\) into the half-open unit bins \(Q_m:=\prod_{r\in I}[m_r,m_r+1)\), indexed by \(m\in\mathbb Z^I\). If a bin contains a vector \(z\) with \(\sum_{r\in I}|z_r|\le2s\), then \(m_r=\lfloor z_r\rfloor\) and \(|m_r|\le|z_r|+1\), giving \(\sum_{r\in I}|m_r|\le3s\). There are at most
\[
2^s\binom{4s}{s}\le2^s2^{4s}=32^s
\]
such indices. Indeed, the nonnegative tuples \((|m_r|)_{r\in I}\) with total at most \(3s\) are counted by \(\binom{4s}{s}\): adjoining their nonnegative slack yields \(s+1\) entries summing to \(3s\), and the usual placement of \(s\) separators among \(4s\) positions counts them. Each tuple has at most \(2^s\) choices of signs. Finally \(\binom{4s}{s}\le2^{4s}\) because this binomial coefficient is one term in the sum of all binomial coefficients of order \(4s\).

The images of points of \(B\) therefore occupy at most \(32^s\) bins. By the pigeonhole principle, one of those bins, say \(Q_m\), satisfies \(q(\{x\in B:Z(x)\in Q_m\})\ge1/(2\cdot32^s)\). For its full preimage \(E:=\{x\in\Omega:Z(x)\in Q_m\}\), we obtain \(q(E)\ge1/(2\cdot32^s)\ge64^{-s}\), where the last inequality uses \(s\ge1\).

If \(X,Y\in E\), then \(|Z_r(X)-Z_r(Y)|<1\) for every \(r\in I\), because both numbers belong to the same half-open interval of length one. The common centering cancels, so \(|a_r(X-Y)|<\sigma_r\) for all \(r\in I\). The zero-variance rows also vanish on \(X-Y\): their coefficients vanish on coordinates with \(j_i>0\), and both coordinates equal zero when \(j_i=0\). It follows that
\[
\mathbb P\bigl(|a_r(X-Y)|\le\sigma_r\text{ for all }r\bigr)
\ge\mathbb P(X\in E,Y\in E)
\ge64^{-\gamma s}
=\exp(-C_{\mathrm{slab}}s)
\ge\exp(-C_{\mathrm{slab}}k).
\]

Finally, accepted fact 7f35aa79638b5745 gives the exact uniform law of every difference \(X_i-Y_i\) on \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Independence of the paired coordinates makes these differences independent. Thus the entire vector \(X-Y\) has exactly the stipulated law of \(V\), so the last probability is the one in the statement. This proves the bound for all the specified matrices and side lengths.
