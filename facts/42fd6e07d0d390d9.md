## Statement

Use exactly the standard recursive symmetric chain partition and the block objects of accepted facts 6fee4107100a027d, 7538f1751c45475c, and a2c2d2b38e2fa409. Thus \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), \(D_m\) is its nonterminal set, \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) is its injective successor map, and
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\qquad
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m
=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}.
$$
The standard partition here is precisely the recursion in 7538f1751c45475c and a2c2d2b38e2fa409; no assertion is made for arbitrary symmetric chain partitions. Write \(e_m=|E_m|\) and \(v_m=|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|\). The new cardinality symbol \(v_m\) does not replace the point \(c_m\) in 6fee4107100a027d. For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), define the full law
$$
Z_{m,\varepsilon}=v_m+\varepsilon e_m,\qquad
p_{m,\varepsilon}(x)
=\frac{\varepsilon^{\mathbf1_{\{x\in E_m\}}}}{Z_{m,\varepsilon}}
\quad(x\in T_m).
$$
The parameter \(\varepsilon\) is a spike penalty, not a last-spike-height parameter. These weights have the convex potential
\( (-\log\varepsilon)\psi_m(x)\), where
\(\psi_m(x)=\max(0,x_1-1,\ldots,x_m-1)\).

For any finite probability law \(p\) on \(\mathbb Z^N\), retain exactly the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\), reflected mass \(H_p\), reflected moment \(b(p)\), first reflected moment \(\chi(p)\), and second actual moment \(M_2(p)\) of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc. In particular,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^N}\sqrt{p_{j+z}p_{j-z}},\qquad
b(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum is oriented, with the zero displacement counted once. Probabilities outside the full support are zero.

Set \(\kappa=1/2048\), and define the absolute constants
$$
\mathcal D=8\kappa^{-2}+2\kappa^{-1}\log4,
\qquad
\eta=\exp\left(-24\mathcal D\left(2+12(\log2)^2\right)\right)>0.
$$
Then for every integer \(L\ge1\), every \(m_1,\ldots,m_L\ge1\), and every independently chosen \(0<\varepsilon_i\le1\), put \(N=\sum_{i=1}^L m_i\) and \(p^{\Pi}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_{m_i,\varepsilon_i}\). The original full law satisfies
$$
M_2(p^{\Pi})\ge\eta\,4^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(p^{\Pi})\ge\eta^{3/2}\,8^{-N}.
$$

## Proof

We first establish the exact block geometry and reflection formulas needed below. Completeness and the decomposition \(T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) are supplied by 6fee4107100a027d and 7538f1751c45475c. The function \(\psi_m\) is convex, being a maximum of affine functions. It equals one at every spike in \(E_m\) and zero at every point of \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). Hence its displayed potential gives exactly the stated full law. In particular, whenever supported endpoints \(x,y\) have integer midpoint \(j\), completeness gives \(j\in T_m\), and convexity gives
\(p_{m,\varepsilon}(j)\ge\sqrt{p_{m,\varepsilon}(x)p_{m,\varepsilon}(y)}\).

Define \(J_m=F_m(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m)\) and \(h_m=|J_m|\). We claim that \(J_m\) consists exactly of the Boolean words whose prefix walk reaches height four. The matching description proved in 7538f1751c45475c gives a reduced unmatched-symbol word \(1^p0^q\), and its successor changes the first unmatched zero to one, producing \(1^{p+1}0^{q-1}\). The matched intervening words remain matched. By a2c2d2b38e2fa409, membership in \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) is exactly the condition \(p\ge3\). Thus every image in \(J_m\) has at least four unmatched ones. Conversely, for a word with \(p\ge4\) unmatched ones, change its last unmatched one to zero. Matching the resulting word reduces to \(1^{p-1}0^{q+1}\), with the intervening matches preserved. Its first unmatched zero is precisely the changed position, so its successor is the original word. This predecessor is nonterminal and has at least three unmatched ones, hence belongs to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). This proves the claim. Consequently \(J_m\subseteq C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), and, for \(m\ge4\), every word beginning with \(1111\) belongs to \(J_m\); therefore \(h_m/2^m\ge1/16\). Similarly every word beginning with \(111\) belongs to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) for \(m\ge3\), so \(v_m/2^m\ge1/8\).

Every spike has exactly one coordinate equal to two and the other coordinates Boolean. Its parity vector is its source \(c\in D_m\), so distinct spikes have distinct parity vectors. Two Boolean points have the same parity vector only if they are equal. Therefore a distinct pair of supported points with integer midpoint must be precisely a spike \(c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) paired with its Boolean source \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\). Its midpoint is \(F_m(c)\). Injectivity of \(F_m\) makes this the only unordered nontrivial reflected pair at that midpoint. No spike is such a midpoint.

For this block write \(t=\sqrt\varepsilon\), \(Z=Z_{m,\varepsilon}\), and \(p=p_{m,\varepsilon}\). The preceding classification gives the exact formulas
$$
H_p(j)=
\begin{cases}
t^2/Z,&j\in E_m,\\
(1+2t)/Z,&j\in J_m,\\
1/Z,&j\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\setminus J_m,
\end{cases}
\qquad
b(p)=\frac{e_m t^6+v_m-h_m+h_m(1+2t)^2}{Z^3}.
\tag{1}
$$
All atoms, including those outside reflected triples, remain in these formulas.

We next record a uniform atom bound. For \(m\ge3\), put \(u=e_m/2^m\) and \(v=v_m/2^m\). The accepted cardinality formula in 6fee4107100a027d gives \(u\le1\), while the prefix certificate above gives \(1/8\le v\le1\). Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^m\sum_jp_j^3
&=\frac{v+ut^6}{(v+ut^2)^3}
\ge v^{-2}\left(1+\frac uv t^2\right)^{-3}\\
&\ge(1+8t^2)^{-3}
\ge e^{-24t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Here the last inequality uses \(\log(1+s)\le s\). For \(m=1,2,3\), the exact counts in 6fee4107100a027d and a2c2d2b38e2fa409 give \(|T_m|=1,2,6\), respectively. For any probability law on \(s\) points, Jensen gives \(\sum p_j^3\ge s^{-2}\). Therefore \(4^m\sum_jp_j^3\) is at least \(4,4,16/9\), respectively. In particular (2) also holds for \(m=1,2\). Every actual depth is at least its own atom, so every finite product of these blocks has the corresponding product atom lower bound.

We now prove a strict reflected gain uniform in the width and penalty:
$$
4^m b(p_{m,\varepsilon})\ge1+\frac{t}{1024},
\qquad
\log\bigl(4^m b(p_{m,\varepsilon})\bigr)\ge\kappa t.
\tag{3}
$$
For the second inequality it suffices to use
\(\log(1+s)\ge s/2\) for \(0\le s\le1\). For \(m\le3\), the first inequality follows from \(H_p(j)\ge p_j\) and the atom slack just established. Suppose henceforth that \(m\ge4\), and additionally write \(w=h_m/2^m\).

Integer midpoints and convexity give a parity lower bound without any affine-weight assumption. Indeed, the correspondence \((j,z)\leftrightarrow(x,y)=(j+z,j-z)\) is a bijection between supported reflected triples and ordered supported pairs of the same coordinatewise parity. At each such midpoint, \(p_j\ge\sqrt{p_xp_y}\). Every term in \(\chi(p)\) therefore dominates \(p_xp_y\). The \(2h_m\) oriented nontrivial pairs classified above have excess exactly \(t(1-t)/Z^2\) apiece. All other terms have nonnegative excess. Cauchy-Schwarz for the at most \(2^m\) parity-class masses consequently gives
$$
2^m\chi(p)
\ge1+\frac{2w t(1-t)}{(v+ut^2)^2}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular \(2^m\chi(p)\ge1\). Since \(w\ge1/16\) and \(v+ut^2\le2\), (4) gives \(2^m\chi(p)\ge1+t/64\) for \(0<t\le1/2\). Jensen then yields \(4^m b(p)\ge(2^m\chi(p))^2\ge1+t/32\), which is stronger than (3).

For \(1/2\le t\le1\), let \(X\) have the original law \(p\), and put \(Y=2^mH_p(X)\). Thus \(\mathbb EY=2^m\chi(p)\ge1\) and \(\mathbb EY^2=4^m b(p)\). We have \(u\ge1/2\): the formula \(e_m=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) from 6fee4107100a027d has this consequence, since each binomial coefficient is at most its parity sum \(2^{m-1}\). Under the original law, the probabilities of \(J_m\) and \(E_m\) are therefore at least
\(w/(v+ut^2)\ge1/32\) and \(ut^2/(v+ut^2)\ge1/16\), respectively. Formula (1) shows that the two corresponding values of \(Y\) differ by
$$
\frac{1+2t-t^2}{v+ut^2}\ge\frac78.
$$
Using an independent copy \(Y'\), the identity \(\operatorname{Var}Y=\tfrac12\mathbb E(Y-Y')^2\) gives
\(\operatorname{Var}Y\ge(1/32)(1/16)(7/8)^2=49/32768\). It follows that
\(4^m b(p)\ge1+49/32768\ge1+t/1024\). This completes the proof of (3).

We need the actual-depth price of the reflected gain, not a capacity comparison. For one block use the symmetric experiment associated with \(b(p)\): choose a center with probability \(p_jH_p(j)^2/b(p)\), then choose an oriented displacement with conditional probability \(\sqrt{p_{j+z}p_{j-z}}/H_p(j)\). Define
\(T=\tfrac12\log(p_{j+z}/p_{j-z})\) on this experiment. The sign symmetry of the displacement makes \(T\) symmetric and mean zero. Put \(R=\log(1/t)\). By (1), all nonzero values of \(T\) can occur only at centers in \(J_m\), and conditional on such a center the displacement probabilities are \(1/(1+2t)\) at zero and \(t/(1+2t)\) at each of the two nonzero orientations. The latter orientations give values \(R\) and \(-R\). Thus (also when \(R=0\))
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}T
&=\frac{2h_m t(1+2t)R^2}
{e_m t^6+v_m-h_m+h_m(1+2t)^2}\\
&\le2tR^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
If \(h_m=0\), the numerator is zero and the same conclusion holds. Otherwise the denominator is at least \(h_m(1+2t)^2\), proving the inequality.

For completeness, the symmetric experiment gives the following original actual bound for any collection \(I\) of factors. Use independent copies of the preceding experiments and write \(b_i=b(p_{m_i,\varepsilon_i})\), \(t_i=\sqrt{\varepsilon_i}\), and \(R_i=\log(1/t_i)\). Then
$$
M_2\left(\bigotimes_{i\in I}p_{m_i,\varepsilon_i}\right)
\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{i\in I}b_i\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{2\sum_{i\in I}t_iR_i^2}\right).
\tag{6}
$$
To check this directly, let \(q\) denote that product law and let \(\mathbf j\) be a product center. Put
\(S_q(\mathbf j)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\min(q_{\mathbf j+\mathbf z},q_{\mathbf j-\mathbf z})\). Every original ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains the center and at least one endpoint of each antipodal pair. Accounting for both orientations gives \(\tau_q(\mathbf j)\ge(S_q(\mathbf j)+q_{\mathbf j})/2\ge S_q(\mathbf j)/2\). The identity
\(\min(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}\exp(-\tfrac12|\log(a/b)|)\) gives
$$
S_q(\mathbf j)
=\left(\prod_{i\in I}H_{p_{m_i,\varepsilon_i}}(j_i)\right)
\mathbb E\left[e^{-|\sum_{i\in I}T_i|}\mid\mathbf j\right].
$$
Squaring and averaging over the original target law produces the factor \(\prod b_i\) and the independent center laws specified above. Jensen for the conditional square gives a lower bound
\(\tfrac14(\prod b_i)(\mathbb E e^{-|\sum T_i|})^2\), as in the reflected-to-actual argument of ce8cccfdbee6f1cc. Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz then give
\(\mathbb E e^{-|\sum T_i|}\ge\exp(-\sqrt{\sum\operatorname{Var}T_i})\). Substituting (5) proves (6). In particular no one-copy capacity-to-depth conversion has been used.

It remains to join (2) and (6) uniformly for independent penalties. Partition the finitely many factors into the dyadic bands
\(I_k=\lbrace i:2^{-(k+1)}<t_i\le2^{-k}\rbrace\), \(k=0,1,\ldots\). Only finitely many bands are nonempty. For each nonempty band put
\(\alpha_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i\), \(s_k=2^{-k}\), \(r_k=(k+1)\log2\), and \(N_k=\sum_{i\in I_k}m_i\), and let \(q_k\) be its product law. By (2), actual depths retaining their atoms give
$$
4^{N_k}M_2(q_k)
\ge\exp\left(-24\sum_{i\in I_k}t_i^2\right)
\ge\exp(-24s_k\alpha_k).
\tag{7}
$$
By (3), (5), and (6), since \(R_i\le r_k\) in this band, we also have
$$
4^{N_k}M_2(q_k)
\ge\frac14\exp\left(\kappa\alpha_k
-2\sqrt2\,r_k\sqrt{\alpha_k}\right).
\tag{8}
$$
The scalar inequality
\(2\sqrt2\,r\sqrt\alpha\le(\kappa/2)\alpha+4r^2/\kappa\)
follows by completing a square. If
\(\alpha_k\ge\mathcal D(1+r_k^2)\), the exponent in (8) is at least
\(\kappa\alpha_k/2-4r_k^2/\kappa\ge\log4\), by the stated value of \(\mathcal D\). In this case \(4^{N_k}M_2(q_k)\ge1\). In the other case, (7) gives the lower bound \(\exp(-24\mathcal D s_k(1+r_k^2))\). Consequently in both cases
$$
4^{N_k}M_2(q_k)
\ge\exp\bigl(-24\mathcal D s_k(1+r_k^2)\bigr).
\tag{9}
$$
The actual pointwise depth supermultiplicativity proved in ce8cccfdbee6f1cc implies \(M_2(p^{\Pi})\ge\prod_{k:I_k\ne\varnothing}M_2(q_k)\). Multiplying (9) and using the elementary convergent sums
\(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}=2\) and \(\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^22^{-k}=12\) proves
$$
4^N M_2(p^{\Pi})
\ge\exp\left(-24\mathcal D
\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}\bigl(1+(k+1)^2(\log2)^2\bigr)\right)
=\eta.
$$
Finally, Jensen for the nonnegative variable \(\tau_{p^{\Pi}}(X)^2\), with \(X\) sampled from the original full law, gives
\(M_3(p^{\Pi})\ge M_2(p^{\Pi})^{3/2}\ge\eta^{3/2}8^{-N}\). This retains every original atom and every original ambient closed halfspace throughout, and proves both stated uniform inequalities.
