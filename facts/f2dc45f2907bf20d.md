## Statement

Let \(d\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(T,L\subset\mathbb R^d\) be convex bodies, meaning compact convex sets with nonempty interior, with \(L\subseteq T\). Write \(|K|\) for the ordinary \(d\)-dimensional volume of a measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\). Set \(n=2\), \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d\), and

\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,1]^2\times T,[-1,2]^2\times L\bigr),
\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,
\qquad \mu(E)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap E).
\]

For \(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^2\), let \(A_{\mathbf t}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in C\}\). For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^2\), put \(a_{\mathbf z}=|A_{\mathbf z}|\), and let \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^2}a_{\mathbf z}=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Use the prescribed functions

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_1(\mathbf t)&=t_1+t_2,&d_2(\mathbf t)&=1-t_1+t_2,\\
d_3(\mathbf t)&=1+t_1-t_2,&d_4(\mathbf t)&=2-t_1-t_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\), let \(I_j\) be a finite nonempty index set. For every \(s\in I_j\), let \(\beta_{j,s}:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) be a nonconstant affine function, let \(\alpha_{j,s}\in\mathbb R\), and let \(b_{j,s}>0\). Suppose that, separately for each \(j\), one has \(\sum_{s\in I_j}\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf x)=1\) for every \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(\sum_{s\in I_j}\alpha_{j,s}<1\). The original open caps are

\[
H_{j,s}=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^{2+d}:
\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf x)>\alpha_{j,s}+b_{j,s}d_j(\mathbf t)\}.
\]

All actual integer fibers are

\[
A_{\mathbf z}=
\begin{cases}
T,&\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^2,\\
L,&\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^2\setminus\{0,1\}^2,\\
\varnothing,&\mathbf z\notin\{-1,0,1,2\}^2.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(W=4|T|+12|L|>0\). For every \(j\),

\[
\max_{s\in I_j}\mu(H_{j,s})
\ge\frac{|T|}{3}+|L|=\frac W{12}.
\tag{2}
\]

More precisely, let \(\mathbf g_T=|T|^{-1}\int_T\mathbf x\,d\mathbf x\), put \(c_{j,s}=\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf g_T)\), and let \(\widehat H_{j,s}\) be the cap obtained from \(H_{j,s}\) by replacing \(\alpha_{j,s}\) with \(c_{j,s}\). Then \(\mu(\widehat H_{j,s})\ge W/12\) for every \(j,s\). The projection-density hypotheses of accepted fact \(be3097c8a79095ef\) hold automatically: if \(U\) is uniform on \(T\), each random variable \(\beta_{j,s}(U)-c_{j,s}\) has a compactly supported log-concave Lebesgue probability density, no atoms, and mean zero. Consequently, for every real \(\eta\), if every original cap has mass less than \(\eta\), then \(W<12\eta\).

## Proof

Accepted fact \(ec83eaa61a715c72\) treats the same body construction when the continuous space is \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(T\) is a triangle. Its geometric symbols \(C,S,\Lambda,A_{\mathbf t},a_{\mathbf z},W,d_j,\mathbf g_T\) retain their meanings here, with \(d\)-dimensional volume replacing planar area and \(\mathbb R^d\) replacing its continuous space. At a fixed group \(j\), its symbols \(\beta_s,\alpha_s,b_s,F_{K,s}\), and \(M_s\) correspond respectively to our \(\beta_{j,s},\alpha_{j,s},b_{j,s},F_{K,j,s}\), and \(\mu(H_{j,s})\), where the cap-volume functions \(F_{K,j,s}\) are defined below. Its centroid threshold \(h=\beta_s(\mathbf g_T)\) corresponds to our \(c_{j,s}\). We now reprove its geometric census and cap formula in the present dimension and with these group-dependent data. Thus its triangle-specific analytic estimate and its equality of masses across different groups are not being assumed here.

The set \(C\) is a convex body: it is the convex hull of two compact sets and contains \([0,1]^2\times T\), which has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{2+d}\). Convexity of \(T\) and \(L\subseteq T\) give \(C\subseteq[-1,2]^2\times T\). Thus \(A_{\mathbf z}=T\) at every \(\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^2\), since the inner product already contains the entire copy of \(T\) there.

Because each of the two products defining \(C\) is convex, any point of \(C\) is a convex combination of one point from each product. If its lattice base has a coordinate equal to \(-1\) or \(2\), the coefficient of the point in \([0,1]^2\times T\) must be zero: its coordinate lies in \([0,1]\), while the corresponding coordinate of the other point lies in \([-1,2]\), so positive weight on the first point cannot attain that extreme value. The continuous coordinate then belongs to \(L\). Conversely, the whole copy of \(L\) at any base of \([-1,2]^2\) belongs to \(C\). Every integer base in this square outside \(\{0,1\}^2\) has such an extreme coordinate, and no integer base outside the square can meet \(C\). This proves (1), including the eight outer edge fibers as well as the four outer corner fibers. The lattice slices are disjoint isometric copies of \(\mathbb R^d\), so their Hausdorff volumes add and give \(W=4|T|+12|L|\).

For each fixed \(j\), the multiplicities of \(q=d_j(\mathbf z)\) on these sixteen actual fibers are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
q&-2&-1&0&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
A_{\mathbf z}=T&0&0&1&2&1&0&0\\
A_{\mathbf z}=L&1&2&2&2&2&2&1.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]

For \(j=1\), addition of the coordinates on \(\{-1,0,1,2\}^2\) gives total multiplicities \(1,2,3,4,3,2,1\). The four inner sites give multiplicities \(1,2,1\) at \(q=0,1,2\), and subtraction gives the second row of (3). Replacing \(t_1\) with \(1-t_1\), replacing \(t_2\) with \(1-t_2\), and making both replacements convert \(d_1\) into \(d_2,d_3,d_4\), respectively. These bijections preserve both the sixteen-site square and its four inner sites. Hence (3) holds for each group regardless of that group's affine family.

For \(K=T,L\), define the unnormalized cap volume \(F_{K,j,s}(u)=|K\cap\{\beta_{j,s}>u\}|\). Formula (3) gives the exact original mass

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(H_{j,s})={}&F_{T,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s})
+2F_{T,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s}+b_{j,s})
+F_{T,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s}+2b_{j,s})\\
&+F_{L,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s}-2b_{j,s})
+2\sum_{q=-1}^{3}F_{L,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s}+q b_{j,s})
+F_{L,j,s}(\alpha_{j,s}+4b_{j,s}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

This formula counts every actual lattice fiber. Replacing \(\alpha_{j,s}\) with \(c_{j,s}\) gives the exact mass of \(\widehat H_{j,s}\). No relation between the masses for different groups is needed. All cap traces retain the strict inequality in their definition. A level set of any \(\beta_{j,s}\) is an affine hyperplane with zero \(d\)-dimensional volume, so there is also no mass at a threshold on either \(T\) or \(L\), including supporting thresholds.

Fix any \(j\) and \(s\in I_j\). We check the projection law before applying the scalar fact. Write \(\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf x)=\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf0)+\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf x\rangle\), where \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0\). The uniform law \(U\) on \(T\) has density \(\mathbf1_T/|T|\). This is log-concave: when two points lie in \(T\), their convex combinations lie in \(T\), and when either point lies outside \(T\), the right side of the defining log-concavity inequality is zero. Compactness gives a finite first moment, and affinity gives \(\mathbb E\beta_{j,s}(U)=\beta_{j,s}(\mathbb E U)=c_{j,s}\). Therefore \(X=\beta_{j,s}(U)-c_{j,s}\) has mean zero. Its values lie in the compact interval \([\min_T\beta_{j,s}-c_{j,s},\max_T\beta_{j,s}-c_{j,s}]\). That interval is nondegenerate because \(T\) has nonempty interior and \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0\).

When \(d\ge2\), choose an orthogonal linear map \(R\) whose first row is \(\mathbf v^T/\|\mathbf v\|\). The vector \(Y=RU\) has the log-concave Lebesgue probability density \(p(\mathbf y)=\mathbf1_{RT}(\mathbf y)/|T|\); the normalization is unchanged because \(|\det R|=1\). Prékopa's marginal-density theorem, stated precisely under EXTERNAL SOURCES, gives a log-concave density \(\rho(r)=\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}p(r,\mathbf y')\,d\mathbf y'\) for \(Y_1\). This integral is finite because \(RT\) is bounded, and it integrates to one by Fubini's theorem. Since \(X=\|\mathbf v\|Y_1+\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf0)-c_{j,s}\), its density is \(f(u)=\|\mathbf v\|^{-1}\rho((u+c_{j,s}-\beta_{j,s}(\mathbf0))/\|\mathbf v\|)\). Positive scaling and affine composition preserve the defining log-concavity inequality, so \(f\) is log-concave. When \(d=1\), \(T\) is a nondegenerate compact interval; a nonconstant affine image of its uniform law is uniform on a nondegenerate compact interval, and centering preserves that property. Its density is a positive constant on the resulting interval and zero outside, hence is log-concave directly. In either case \(X\) has a compactly supported Lebesgue probability density and consequently has no atoms. Thus all the hypotheses of accepted fact \(be3097c8a79095ef\) hold in every stated dimension.

For the rest of this fixed-pair calculation, abbreviate \(c=c_{j,s}\) and \(b=b_{j,s}>0\). The exact notation and normalization map to fact \(be3097c8a79095ef\) is: its random variable \(X\) is the centered projection just constructed, its nonnegative step \(h\) is our \(b\), and its probability tails are

\[
F(t)=\Pr(X>t)=\frac{F_{T,j,s}(c+t)}{|T|},
\qquad
P(t)=\Pr(X\le t)=\frac{|T\cap\{\beta_{j,s}\le c+t\}|}{|T|}.
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, its conclusion is \(F(0)+2F(b)+F(2b)-P(-2b)\ge1/3\). These are normalized probability tails, whereas the functions \(F_{K,j,s}\) in (4) are volumes.

Retain in the centroid-threshold version of (4) the four inner-fiber contributions and the single outer-fiber contribution at \(q=-2\). All other outer-fiber terms are nonnegative. The inclusion \(L\subseteq T\) gives exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{L,j,s}(c-2b)
&=|L|-|L\cap\{\beta_{j,s}\le c-2b\}|\\
&\ge |L|-|T\cap\{\beta_{j,s}\le c-2b\}|\\
&=|L|-|T|P(-2b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The complementary lower tail is closed, exactly as stipulated in the scalar fact; (6) also holds when the threshold lies at or outside a support endpoint. Combining (4), (5), (6), and that accepted inequality yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu(\widehat H_{j,s})
&\ge F_{T,j,s}(c)+2F_{T,j,s}(c+b)
+F_{T,j,s}(c+2b)+F_{L,j,s}(c-2b)\\
&\ge |T|\bigl(F(0)+2F(b)+F(2b)-P(-2b)\bigr)+|L|\\
&\ge\frac{|T|}{3}+|L|=\frac{4|T|+12|L|}{12}=\frac W{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The pair \((j,s)\) was arbitrary, so this proves the claimed bound at every centroid threshold.

Finally fix a group \(j\). Evaluating its affine identity at \(\mathbf g_T\) gives \(\sum_{s\in I_j}c_{j,s}=1>\sum_{s\in I_j}\alpha_{j,s}\). Hence at least one \(s\in I_j\) satisfies \(\alpha_{j,s}<c_{j,s}\). For this index the defining thresholds show \(\widehat H_{j,s}\subseteq H_{j,s}\), throughout \(\mathbb R^{2+d}\). Thus its original cap satisfies \(\mu(H_{j,s})\ge\mu(\widehat H_{j,s})\ge W/12\), proving (2) separately for every group. The chosen index may differ from group to group. If every original mass is less than \(\eta\), any one of these indices gives \(W/12\le\mu(H_{j,s})<\eta\), and therefore \(W<12\eta\).
