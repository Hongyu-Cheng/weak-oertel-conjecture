## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and let \(\operatorname{vol}_k\) denote \(k\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Write \(\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(\pi:\mathbb R^{n+d}\to\mathbb R^n\) for the integer-coordinate projection. For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) and \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\), set \(A_{\mathbf q}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)\in A\}\), and define \(\mu(A):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(A_{\mathbf z})\).

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body and let \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a nonempty compact convex set, possibly lower-dimensional. Suppose that \(\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^n\subseteq\pi(P)\) for some \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(R>0\). Put \(\beta:=1/(2R)\), \(\alpha:=3/(2R)\), \(\gamma_m:=(m/(m+1))^m\), \(S:=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(W:=\mu(C)\). For \(t>0\), use Minkowski addition \(C+tP:=\{\mathbf c+t\mathbf v:\mathbf c\in C,\mathbf v\in P\}\) and erosion \(C\ominus tP:=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf y+tP\subseteq C\}\), with volume zero for an empty erosion. Then

\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(C\ominus\beta P)
\le W
\le\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P).
\tag{1}
\]

For every convex body \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) satisfying \(E+\alpha P\subseteq C\), there is a point \(\mathbf w\in P\) such that the translate \(D:=E+\alpha\mathbf w\) has centroid \(\mathbf p\in S\), and

\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)\le\mu(C\cap H)
\quad\text{for every closed halfspace }H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}.
\tag{2}
\]

The choices of \(D\) and \(\mathbf p\) are independent of \(H\). With the locked problem's depth \(h_S(\mathbf p):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf p}\mu(C\cap H)\), where the infimum is over closed halfspaces, this gives

\[
h_S(\mathbf p)
\ge\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(E)
\ge\gamma_m
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_m(E)}
     {\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)}\,W.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, if \(\operatorname{vol}_m(C\ominus\alpha P)>0\), one may take \(E=C\ominus\alpha P\) in (2) and (3). If additionally \(P\subseteq C\) and \(R>3/2\), this erosion contains \((1-\alpha)C\), and the resulting point satisfies \(h_S(\mathbf p)\ge\gamma_m((2R-3)/(2R+1))^m W\).

No assumption that \(P\) contains the origin, is centrally symmetric, or is contained in \(C\) is made except for the last specialization. If the \(\alpha\)-erosion has zero volume, its zero depth lower bound is automatic whenever \(S\ne\varnothing\); the positive-volume construction is not invoked in that case.

## Proof

The measure \(\mu(C)\) is finite because \(C\) is bounded and only finitely many integer fibers can meet \(C\). For every \(t>0\),
\(C\ominus tP=\bigcap_{\mathbf v\in P}(C-t\mathbf v)\).
This is closed and convex, and, after fixing any \(\mathbf v_0\in P\), is contained in the compact set \(C-t\mathbf v_0\). It is therefore compact, including when empty. Also \(C+tP\) is compact and contains the translate \(C+t\mathbf v_0\), so \(\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)>0\). These observations justify the sets and the denominator in the statement without requiring \(P\) to have positive \(m\)-dimensional volume.

We first construct a lift for the two volume comparisons. Let \(Q:=[-1/2,1/2)^n\) and \(\overline Q:=[-1/2,1/2]^n\). For each \(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n\), choose \(\mathbf b_{\boldsymbol\sigma}\in P\) with \(\pi(\mathbf b_{\boldsymbol\sigma})=\mathbf a+R\boldsymbol\sigma\). Define, on the closed cube,

\[
\mathbf b(\mathbf t):=
\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{1+2\sigma_i t_i}{2}\right)
\mathbf b_{\boldsymbol\sigma},
\qquad \mathbf t\in\overline Q.
\]

The weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so \(\mathbf b(\mathbf t)\in P\). They have weighted sign average \(2t_i\) in coordinate \(i\). Consequently \(\pi(\mathbf b(\mathbf t))=\mathbf a+2R\mathbf t\), or equivalently \(\beta\pi(\mathbf b(\mathbf t))=\beta\mathbf a+\mathbf t\). The map \(\mathbf b\) is continuous and multi-affine. Write its continuous-coordinate component as \(\mathbf b_C(\mathbf t)\in\mathbb R^d\).

Every \(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n\) has the unique decomposition \(\mathbf q=\boldsymbol\nu(\mathbf q)+\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf q)\), where \(\nu_i(\mathbf q):=\lfloor q_i+1/2\rfloor\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf q)\in Q\). Both maps are Borel. Define the Borel jitter set \(U:=\bigcup_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}((\mathbf z+Q)\times C_{\mathbf z})\). The base cells are disjoint, each with volume one. Thus Tonelli's theorem gives \(\operatorname{vol}_m(U)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})=W\).

We will use an elementary shear observation. If \(\boldsymbol\eta\in\mathbb R^n\) is fixed and \(\boldsymbol\psi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\) is Borel, then
\(T(\mathbf q,\mathbf x):=(\mathbf q+\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\psi(\mathbf q))\)
is a Borel bijection with Borel inverse
\(T^{-1}(\mathbf q',\mathbf x')=(\mathbf q'-\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf x'-\boldsymbol\psi(\mathbf q'-\boldsymbol\eta))\).
In particular, it sends Borel sets to Borel sets. For any Borel set \(A\), its image section is
\((T(A))_{\mathbf q'}=A_{\mathbf q'-\boldsymbol\eta}+\boldsymbol\psi(\mathbf q'-\boldsymbol\eta)\).
Translation invariance in each continuous fiber and then in the base, together with Tonelli's theorem, give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(T(A))
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}
  \operatorname{vol}_d(A_{\mathbf q'-\boldsymbol\eta})\,d\mathbf q'\\
&=\operatorname{vol}_m(A).
\end{aligned}
\]

The two volume comparisons use the following Borel shears:

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_+(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)&:=
\bigl(\mathbf q+\beta\mathbf a,\,
      \mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_C(\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf q))\bigr),\\
T_-(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)&:=
\bigl(\mathbf q+\beta\mathbf a,\,
      \mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_C(-\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf q+\beta\mathbf a))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

For the upper bound in (1), let \((\mathbf q,\mathbf x)\in U\) and write \(\mathbf q=\mathbf z+\mathbf t\) with \(\mathbf t\in Q\); then \((\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\). The projection identity for \(\mathbf b\) gives
\(T_+(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)=(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)+\beta\mathbf b(\mathbf t)\in C+\beta P\).
The shear observation therefore gives \(W=\operatorname{vol}_m(T_+(U))\le\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)\).

For the lower bound, the required phase has the opposite sign. For \((\mathbf q,\mathbf x)\in C\ominus\beta P\), write uniquely
\(\mathbf q=\mathbf z-\beta\mathbf a+\mathbf t\) with \(\mathbf t\in Q\).
The point \(-\mathbf t\) lies in \(\overline Q\), even if some \(t_i=-1/2\). Because \(\mathbf b(-\mathbf t)\in P\), the definition of erosion yields
\((\mathbf q,\mathbf x)+\beta\mathbf b(-\mathbf t)=(\mathbf z,\mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_C(-\mathbf t))\in C\).

It follows that
\(T_-(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)=(\mathbf z+\mathbf t,\mathbf x+\beta\mathbf b_C(-\mathbf t))\in U\).
Measure preservation gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_m(C\ominus\beta P)=\operatorname{vol}_m(T_-(C\ominus\beta P))\le\operatorname{vol}_m(U)=W\),
proving (1). The same inclusion also gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_d((C\ominus\beta P)_{\mathbf z-\beta\mathbf a+\mathbf t})\le\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\);
integrating over the disjoint cells \(\mathbf z-\beta\mathbf a+Q\) gives the same bound. Empty or zero-volume erosions cause no exception. The closed domain of \(\mathbf b\) includes every centered-cell endpoint. In particular, \(\mathbf b(-\mathbf t)\) is a point selected in \(P\), not an assumed negative of \(\mathbf b(\mathbf t)\); no symmetry of \(P\) has been used.

Now fix a convex body \(E\) with \(E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). Let \(\mathbf c_E=(\mathbf c_{E,I},\mathbf c_{E,C})\) be its Lebesgue centroid, and put \(r:=\alpha^{-1}=2R/3\). Choose \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) by coordinatewise nearest-integer rounding of \(\mathbf c_{E,I}+\alpha\mathbf a\), resolving ties by \(\lfloor t+1/2\rfloor\). Define \(\mathbf u:=(\mathbf z_0-\mathbf c_{E,I})/\alpha\). Then \(\|\mathbf u-\mathbf a\|_\infty\le1/(2\alpha)=R/3\), and therefore \(\mathbf u+r[-1,1]^n\subseteq\mathbf a+R[-1,1]^n\subseteq\pi(P)\).

Choose \(\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma}\in P\) with \(\pi(\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma})=\mathbf u+r\boldsymbol\sigma\), for each \(\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n\), and use the corner interpolation

\[
\mathbf s(\mathbf u+r\boldsymbol\xi):=
\sum_{\boldsymbol\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{1+\sigma_i\xi_i}{2}\right)
\mathbf s_{\boldsymbol\sigma},
\qquad \boldsymbol\xi\in[-1,1]^n.
\]

By the same interpolation argument, this is a continuous multi-affine map into \(P\), with \(\pi(\mathbf s(\mathbf t))=\mathbf t\) throughout its closed domain. Set \(\mathbf w:=\mathbf s(\mathbf u)\), \(D:=E+\alpha\mathbf w\), and \(\mathbf p:=\mathbf c_E+\alpha\mathbf w\). Since the centroid of a convex body belongs to that body, \(\mathbf p\in D\subseteq E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). Translation of the centroid gives that \(\mathbf p\) is the centroid of \(D\), while
\(\pi(\mathbf p)=\mathbf c_{E,I}+\alpha\mathbf u=\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\).
Thus \(\mathbf p\in S\), and \(\operatorname{vol}_m(D)=\operatorname{vol}_m(E)>0\). All these choices have been made without choosing a halfspace.

To prove (2), let \(H=\{\mathbf y:L(\mathbf y)\ge b\}\), where \(L\) is any nonzero linear functional on \(\mathbb R^m\) and \(b\in\mathbb R\). Define \(f(\mathbf t):=L(\mathbf s(\mathbf t))\) on \(\mathbf u+r[-1,1]^n\). Apply accepted fact 92e80d28d7127cde with its dimension \(n\), center \(\mathbf u\), radius \(r=\alpha^{-1}\), scalar multi-affine function \(f=L\circ\mathbf s\), and rounding map denoted here by the same \(\mathcal R_f\). Thus these predecessor symbols retain their meanings and normalization. It supplies a Borel map \(\mathcal R_f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb Z^n\) with coordinatewise floor-or-ceiling values, such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
f\bigl(\mathbf u+r(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)-\mathbf q)\bigr)
&\ge f(\mathbf u)
&&(\mathbf q\in\mathbb R^n),\\
\int_{\mathbb R^n}\phi(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\phi(\mathbf z)
&&(\phi:\mathbb Z^n\to[0,\infty]).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular each Borel basin \(\mathcal R_f^{-1}(\mathbf z)\) has base volume one. Define \(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q):=\mathbf u+r(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)-\mathbf q)\). The floor-or-ceiling property puts this point in the closed domain of \(\mathbf s\) for every \(\mathbf q\), and \(\mathbf t_H\) is Borel.

For \(\mathbf y=(\mathbf q,\mathbf x)\in D\), write \(\mathbf e:=\mathbf y-\alpha\mathbf w\in E\). Define

\[
\Phi_H(\mathbf y):=
\mathbf y+\alpha\bigl(\mathbf s(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q))-\mathbf w\bigr)
=\mathbf e+\alpha\mathbf s(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q)).
\]

This is a Borel map, and its value lies in \(E+\alpha P\subseteq C\). Its base coordinate and its change in the halfspace functional satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi(\Phi_H(\mathbf y))
&=\mathbf q+\alpha(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q)-\mathbf u)
 =\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q),\\
L(\Phi_H(\mathbf y))-L(\mathbf y)
&=\alpha\bigl(f(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q))-f(\mathbf u)\bigr)
 \ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\Phi_H\) maps the \(\mathbf q\)-fiber of \(D\cap H\) into the \(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q)\)-fiber of \(C\cap H\). For fixed \(\mathbf q\), it acts on the continuous coordinate by translation by
\(\alpha(\mathbf s_C(\mathbf t_H(\mathbf q))-\mathbf w_C)\),
where subscripts \(C\) denote the \(\mathbb R^d\) components. This translation preserves \(d\)-dimensional volume.

Set \(g_H(\mathbf q):=\operatorname{vol}_d((D\cap H)_{\mathbf q})\) and \(h_H(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d((C\cap H)_{\mathbf z})\). The section-volume functions are Borel: they are integrals of Borel indicators against \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The fiber translation proves \(g_H(\mathbf q)\le h_H(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\) for every \(\mathbf q\). Tonelli's theorem and the rounding pushforward identity now give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}g_H(\mathbf q)\,d\mathbf q\\
&\le\int_{\mathbb R^n}h_H(\mathcal R_f(\mathbf q))\,d\mathbf q\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}h_H(\mathbf z)
 =\mu(C\cap H).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (2) for all proper closed halfspaces, without requiring \(H\) to contain \(\mathbf p\). No injectivity across different base fibers is asserted or needed: the exact unit-volume basins provide the integral comparison. No condition on the continuous-coordinate component of \(L\) is needed, and \(f\) is allowed to be constant. The rounding fact includes ties and all cell boundaries, and the functional comparison is non-strict, as required for closed halfspaces. If the whole space is allowed as a degenerate closed halfspace, apply the same construction with \(f\equiv0\) and omit the halfspace restriction; the fiber comparison gives \(\operatorname{vol}_m(D)\le\mu(C)\), so (2) also includes that case.

For every proper closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p\), the continuous centroid-halfspace inequality of Grünbaum in dimension \(m\) gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_m(D\cap H)\ge\gamma_m\operatorname{vol}_m(D)\).
Its applicability is recorded under EXTERNAL SOURCES below. If the boundary of \(H=\{L\ge b\}\) does not pass through \(\mathbf p\), the parallel halfspace \(\{L\ge L(\mathbf p)\}\) lies inside \(H\) and has its boundary through \(\mathbf p\), so the same bound follows. Combining this inequality with (2), using \(\operatorname{vol}_m(D)=\operatorname{vol}_m(E)\), and taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves the first inequality in (3). The whole-space case, if included, follows from \(\mu(C)\ge\operatorname{vol}_m(D)\) and \(0<\gamma_m\le1\). The upper bound in (1) and the positive denominator \(\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)\) give the second inequality in (3).

If \(C\ominus\alpha P\) has positive \(m\)-dimensional volume, its already proved compactness and convexity imply that it has nonempty interior. It is therefore a convex body, and its defining property gives \((C\ominus\alpha P)+\alpha P\subseteq C\). This proves the stated erosion choice. If it has zero volume, the corresponding depth lower bound is zero and follows from nonnegativity of \(\mu\) for any \(\mathbf p\in S\), when \(S\ne\varnothing\). No existence of such a point is inferred from a zero-volume or empty erosion.

Finally suppose \(P\subseteq C\) and \(R>3/2\), so \(0<\alpha<1\). Convexity gives
\((1-\alpha)C+\alpha P\subseteq(1-\alpha)C+\alpha C=C\).
Hence \((1-\alpha)C\subseteq C\ominus\alpha P\), and this erosion has positive volume. Similarly,
\(C+\beta P\subseteq C+\beta C=(1+\beta)C\).
Consequently

\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_m(C\ominus\alpha P)}
     {\operatorname{vol}_m(C+\beta P)}
\ge
\frac{(1-\alpha)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C)}
     {(1+\beta)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(C)}
=\left(\frac{2R-3}{2R+1}\right)^m.
\]

Using this in (3) with \(E=C\ominus\alpha P\) proves the specialized depth bound. These are ordinary scalar homotheties justified by convexity; the general proof used only the stated Minkowski containments.
