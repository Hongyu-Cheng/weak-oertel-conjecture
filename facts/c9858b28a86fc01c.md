## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the hypotheses and canonical notation of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70. Thus \(Q>0\) is the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir, \(R\) is its realizable-submatching capacity,

\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D.
\tag{1}
\]

Assume that the load \(\Omega\) of the load-minimal optimizer is positive. It then supplies endpoint loads \(\beta_z\), points \(p_z^\flat\), a full-dimensional origin-symmetric body \(D_*\), and, for every \(\beta_z>0\),

\[
 p_z^\flat+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z).
\tag{2}
\]

On the retained parity classes \(\xi\), use the canonical matching

\[
 s_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{\beta_u\beta_v}{L_\xi},
 \qquad
 d_z(s):=\sum_{e\ni z}s_e\le\beta_z,
\tag{3}
\]

and, for a positive edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put

\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

For every positive retained label \((\xi,c)\), put

\[
 S_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
 \qquad
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ:=\frac1{S_{\xi,c}}
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_eF_e^\flat.
\tag{5}
\]

Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives

\[
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c).
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(\ell(e):=(\xi,m_e)\) denote the unique retained label of a positive edge \(e\subseteq\xi\), and define the canonical good-edge family

\[
 \mathcal G:=
 \left\{e:s_e>0,
 \ \|F_e^\flat-q_{\ell(e)}^\circ\|_{D_*}\le\frac14\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(w=(w_e)_{e\in\mathcal G}\) be arbitrary weights satisfying

\[
 0\le w_e\le s_e,
\tag{8}
\]

and put

\[
 W_{\xi,c}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal G,\ e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}w_e,
 \qquad
 B_w:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal G}w_e
 =\sum_{\xi,c}W_{\xi,c}.
\tag{9}
\]

Then the finite positive measure

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_w:=\gamma_d\sum_{\xi,c}W_{\xi,c}
 \delta_{(c,q_{\xi,c}^\circ)}}
\tag{10}
\]

is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\sigma_w(H)\le\mu_C(H)}
\tag{11}
\]

for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), where

\[
 H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\},
 \qquad
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]

If \(B_w=0\), then \(\sigma_w=0\) and (11) is trivial. If \(B_w>0\), define

\[
 \rho_w:=\dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{c:W_{\xi,c}>0\text{ for some }\xi\},
\tag{12}
\]

\[
 r_w:=
 \min_{a\text{ affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c):W_{\xi,c}>0\},
\tag{13}
\]

where \(a\) is defined on the affine hull in (12). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\ge
 \frac{\gamma_dB_w}{2^{\rho_w}(r_w+1)},
 \qquad
 D\ge
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_w}\frac{B_w}{r_w+1}.}
\tag{14}
\]

There is also an exact fixed-target multiplicity charge. Fix \(c\) and suppose

\[
 B_{w,c}:=\sum_\xi W_{\xi,c}>0,
 \qquad
 k_{w,c}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{q_{\xi,c}^\circ:W_{\xi,c}>0\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 D\ge\gamma_d2^n\frac{B_{w,c}}{k_{w,c}+1}
 \ge\gamma_dB_{w,c}.}
\tag{16}
\]

The second inequality uses only that there are at most \(2^n\) positive parity labels over one physical target. Equations (10)--(16) retain their literal labelled atoms, including several distinct continuous atoms with the same physical base \(c\). They do not assert the existence of target-fiber densities of total mass \(\sum_\xi W_{\xi,c}\), and they do not pool such densities.

Now specialize to

\[
 w_e=s_e\qquad(e\in\mathcal G).
\tag{17}
\]

Write \(W^g_{\xi,c}\), \(B_g\), \(\sigma_g\), \(\rho_g\), and \(r_g\) for the resulting quantities, and define its unscaled labelled affine defect by

\[
 O_g:=
 \inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}}.
\tag{18}
\]

With \(I\) and \(O\) in their canonical normalizations from accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, one has

\[
 \boxed{B_g=|s|-I,
 \qquad O_g\ge O-I.}
\tag{19}
\]

Finally assume the exact hard inequality and the \(O\)-only side condition

\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\le\frac Q4.
\tag{20}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 B_g>\frac{43}{100}Q,
 \qquad
 O_g>\frac1{50}Q.}
\tag{21}
\]

In particular \(\sigma_g\ne0\), and its residual ranks obey the exact escape

\[
 \boxed{
 r_g+1>
 43\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_g}
 >\frac{1075}{68}\,2^{n-\rho_g}.}
\tag{22}
\]

No two lower bounds on \(D\) are added in (14), (16), or (22); each displayed consequence is obtained from one halfspace-dominated measure at a time.

## Proof

We first prove the source-coupled domination. Fix \(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal G\), abbreviate \(\ell(e)=(\xi,c)\), and put

\[
 h_e:=q_{\xi,c}^\circ-F_e^\flat.
\tag{23}
\]

By (7),

\[
 h_e\in\frac14D_*\subseteq\frac12D_*.
\tag{24}
\]

Positivity of \(s_e\) implies \(\beta_u,\beta_v>0\). Equations (2) and (24) therefore give the two shifted source memberships

\[
 x_{e,u}:=p_u^\flat+h_e\in\mathsf M_{\beta_u}(C_u),
 \qquad
 x_{e,v}:=p_v^\flat+h_e\in\mathsf M_{\beta_v}(C_v).
\tag{25}
\]

Their graph midpoint is exactly the labelled output atom:

\[
 \frac12\bigl((u,x_{e,u})+(v,x_{e,v})\bigr)
 =\left(m_e,F_e^\flat+h_e\right)
 =\left(c,q_{\xi,c}^\circ\right).
\tag{26}
\]

In particular, the atom in (26) belongs to \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\): the base is integral because \(u,v\) have the same parity, and the continuous point belongs to \(C_c\) either by convexity applied to (25) or directly by (6).

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace. For every edge \(e\in\mathcal G\) whose atom (26) lies in \(H\) and for which \(w_e>0\), at least one of the two shifted endpoints in (26) lies in \(H\). Indeed, an affine functional defining \(H\) takes at the midpoint the average of its two endpoint values, so both endpoint values cannot be strictly below the defining level. Orient \(e\) toward one shifted endpoint lying in \(H\), making an arbitrary choice when both do. For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(b_z(H)\) be the total \(w\)-weight oriented toward \(z\). Then

\[
 b_z(H)\le\sum_{e\ni z}w_e
 \le\sum_{e\ni z}s_e
 =d_z(s)\le\beta_z.
\tag{27}
\]

If \(b_z(H)>0\), the fiber section \(H_z\) contains at least one point \(x_{e,z}\in\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z)\). If \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace, accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \ge\gamma_d\beta_z
 \ge\gamma_db_z(H).
\tag{28}
\]

If \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), the same conclusion follows from

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\ge\beta_z\ge b_z(H),
\]

and \(\gamma_d\le1\). If \(H_z=\varnothing\), no shifted endpoint over \(z\) lies in \(H\), so \(b_z(H)=0\). These cases also cover vertical ambient halfspaces, boundary atoms, the whole space if admitted as a degenerate halfspace, and the empty set if admitted.

Summing (28) over the pairwise distinct source fibers gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\ge\sum_{z:b_z(H)>0}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\\
 &\ge\gamma_d\sum_zb_z(H)\\
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal G\\(m_e,q_{\ell(e)}^\circ)\in H}}w_e
 =\sigma_w(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

This proves (11), including collisions: if several underlying edges or parity labels give the same atom, their weights are all present on the last two lines of (29), while the source-degree inequality (27) is used only once in each physical source fiber.

Suppose \(B_w>0\). The mass of (10) is \(\gamma_dB_w\), and its intrinsic base and continuous residual ranks in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 are exactly (12)--(13). Its dominated-measure residual theorem applied to (11) gives the first inequality in (14); multiplication by \(2^n\) gives the second.

For (16), restrict (10) to the atoms over the single base \(c\). A positive submeasure of a halfspace-dominated measure remains halfspace-dominated. Its base rank is zero, its continuous residual rank is \(k_{w,c}\), and its mass is \(\gamma_dB_{w,c}\). The same dominated-measure theorem gives

\[
 D\ge\gamma_d2^n\frac{B_{w,c}}{k_{w,c}+1}.
\tag{30}
\]

If \(m_{w,c}:=|\{\xi:W_{\xi,c}>0\}|\), then

\[
 k_{w,c}+1\le m_{w,c}\le2^n.
\tag{31}
\]

Equations (30)--(31) prove (16), including coincident label points and rank zero.

We next prove (19). By definition, the complement of \(\mathcal G\) inside the positive support of \(s\) is exactly the edge family counted by \(I\). Therefore

\[
 B_g=\sum_{e\in\mathcal G}s_e=|s|-I.
\tag{32}
\]

Fix an affine map \(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and call a label \((\xi,c)\) bad for \(a\) when

\[
 \|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>\frac14.
\]

The total \(s\)-mass removed from all bad labels when \(S_{\xi,c}\) is replaced by \(W^g_{\xi,c}\) is at most the total removed mass \(I\). Hence

\[
 \sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{(\xi,c)\text{ bad for }a\}}
 \ge
 \sum_{\xi,c}S_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{(\xi,c)\text{ bad for }a\}}-I
 \ge O-I.
\tag{33}
\]

Taking the infimum over \(a\) proves \(O_g\ge O-I\). This affine defect is the ordinary threshold defect of the actual measure \(\sigma_g/\gamma_d\): distinct label points at one base are distinct atoms, while coincident label points have their weights added.

It remains to audit the hard constants. Under (20), all six summands in (1) are nonnegative, so

\[
 R+G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{34}
\]

The zero-load conclusion in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 is \(\mathcal A(C)\ge Q\), so (20) indeed forces the positive-load case assumed above.

Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives

\[
 \Omega\ge Q-R,
 \qquad
 Q\ge\frac3{32}(W-4G),
\tag{35}
\]

\[
 \sum_{\xi\ {\rm retained}}L_\xi
 \ge2\Omega-G-\frac W{64},
 \qquad
 |s|_\xi\ge\frac{3L_\xi}{8}.
\tag{36}
\]

The second inequality in (35) gives

\[
 W\le\frac{32}{3}Q+4G.
\tag{37}
\]

Substituting (35) and (37) into (36) yields

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\xi\ {\rm retained}}L_\xi
 &\ge2Q-2R-G-\frac Q6-\frac G{16}\\
 &=\frac{11}{6}Q-2R-\frac{17}{16}G\\
 &>\left(\frac{11}{6}-\frac1{50}\right)Q
 =\frac{136}{75}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]

Here \(2R+(17/16)G\le2(R+G)<Q/50\). Summing the last inequality in (36) and using (38) gives the strengthened hard lower bound

\[
 |s|>\frac38\cdot\frac{136}{75}Q
 =\frac{17}{25}Q.
\tag{39}
\]

The exact defect inequality in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 is

\[
 I+O\ge
 \frac{11}{16}Q-\frac{67}{4}R-\frac{51}{128}G.
\tag{40}
\]

Since \(51/128<67/4\), equations (34) and (40) give

\[
 I+O>
 \left(\frac{11}{16}-\frac{67}{400}\right)Q
 =\frac{13}{25}Q.
\tag{41}
\]

Using \(I\le Q/4\) in (32), (33), (39), and (41) gives

\[
 B_g=|s|-I>
 \left(\frac{17}{25}-\frac14\right)Q
 =\frac{43}{100}Q,
\tag{42}
\]

and

\[
 O_g\ge O-I=(I+O)-2I>
 \left(\frac{13}{25}-\frac12\right)Q
 =\frac1{50}Q.
\tag{43}
\]

This proves (21).

Apply (14) to \(\sigma_g\). The final summand \(D\) in (1) and (20) give \(D<Q/100\), while (42) gives \(B_g>43Q/100\). Therefore

\[
 \frac Q{100}>D
 \ge\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_g}\frac{B_g}{r_g+1}
 >\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_g}
 \frac{43Q/100}{r_g+1},
\]

and hence

\[
 r_g+1>43\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_g}.
\tag{44}
\]

For the numerical lower bound, the binomial theorem gives

\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e.
\]

Moreover,

\[
 e=\sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac1{j!}+\sum_{j=6}^{\infty}\frac1{j!}
 <\frac{163}{60}+\frac1{720}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{7^k}
 =\frac{11743}{4320}<\frac{68}{25}.
\tag{45}
\]

Thus \(\gamma_d>25/68\). Substitution in (44) proves the second strict inequality in (22). The proof used the \(D\)-summand only through the single lower bound furnished by \(\sigma_g\). It has constructed no simultaneous target-fiber densities, and it makes no assertion that the rank escape in (22) is impossible.
