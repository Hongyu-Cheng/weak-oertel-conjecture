## Statement

Let $\mu$ be a nonzero finite positive Borel measure of compact support $S$ in a Euclidean space, and let $p>0$ be real. Assume for every pair of compact sets $A,B$ of positive $\mu$-mass and every $0<\theta<1$ that
$$
\mu((1-\theta)A+\theta B)^{1/p}\ge (1-\theta)\mu(A)^{1/p}+\theta\mu(B)^{1/p}.
$$
Then $S$ is convex and $q=\dim\operatorname{aff}S$ satisfies $q\le p$. For $q=0$ the law is one positive atom. For $q>0$ it is absolutely continuous with respect to intrinsic $\mathcal H^q$, gives no mass to the relative boundary of $S$, and has a finite positive continuous density $g$ on $\operatorname{relint}S$. If $q<p$, $g^{1/(p-q)}$ is concave there. If $q=p$, $g$ is constant there.

## Proof

Inner regularity extends the input inequality to open balls with the output the interpolated open ball. Balls around support points have positive mass, so this gives convexity of $S$. The case $q=0$ follows from the definition of support. Identify the affine span isometrically with $\mathbb R^q$ and put $O=\operatorname{relint}S$ for $q>0$.

For each $\varepsilon>0$, define $f_\varepsilon(x)=\mu(B_\varepsilon(x))/(\omega_q\varepsilon^q)$ on the affine span, where $\omega_q$ is unit $q$-ball volume. The function $h_\varepsilon=f_\varepsilon^{1/p}$ is finite, positive and concave on $O$ by the equal-radius inequality. In particular it is continuous there. By Fubini $\int f_\varepsilon=\mu(S)$. When $B_{2r}(x)\subset O$, concavity between $x$ and $2y-x$ for $y\in B_r(x)$ gives $h_\varepsilon(y)\ge h_\varepsilon(x)/2$. It follows that $h_\varepsilon(x)\le2(\mu(S)/(\omega_q r^q))^{1/p}$. Uniformly choosing $r$ on larger compact neighborhoods gives local uniform bounds and local equicontinuity by extending secant lines a fixed distance in both directions. Arzelà–Ascoli on a compact exhaustion gives a locally uniform subsequential limit $h$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The measures $f_\varepsilon\,dX$ converge weakly to $\mu$ by averaging any continuous compactly supported test over shrinking balls. Hence $\mu|_O=h^p\,dX$. The limit $h$ is nonnegative and concave, and vanishing at an interior point would force vanishing everywhere by extending segments through that point. This would contradict positive mass in interior balls. Set $g=h^p$.

If the relative boundary had positive mass, take it for $A$ and take a compact positive-mass relative ball in $O$ for $B$. Each $(1-\theta)A+\theta B$ lies in $O$, since convexity retains an interior ball of radius proportional to $\theta$ around every combined point. It is within $\theta\operatorname{diam}S$ of the relative boundary and has mass at least $(1-\theta)^p\mu(A)$. The interior collars with this width shrink to the empty set in $O$, so their mass tends to zero. This contradiction excludes boundary mass.

At arbitrary $x,y\in O$ use radii $\varepsilon a,\varepsilon b$ with $a,b>0$ and $\theta=1/2$. Divide by $\omega_q\varepsilon^q$ in the powered inequality and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Continuity gives
$$
g((x+y)/2)((a+b)/2)^q
\ge \left[\frac{g(x)^{1/p}a^{q/p}+g(y)^{1/p}b^{q/p}}2\right]^p.
$$
Take $x=y$, $a+b=2$ and $b\downarrow0$ to get $q\le p$. For $q<p$, fix $a+b=2$ and maximize the concave sum in brackets. Its maximizing ratio is $a:b=g(x)^{1/(p-q)}:g(y)^{1/(p-q)}$, giving midpoint concavity of $g^{1/(p-q)}$. Continuity gives full concavity. For $q=p$ the endpoint choices of the linear sum give $g((x+y)/2)\ge\max(g(x),g(y))$. If $x,y$ are close in an interior ball and both reflections $2x-y$ and $2y-x$ still lie in $O$, applying this inequality to each reflected pair yields $g(x)=g(y)$. The continuous density is therefore locally constant and constant on the connected $O$.
