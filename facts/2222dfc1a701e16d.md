## Statement

Let $K_l$ be positive-volume compact convex fibers of a convex body at distinct integer bases $z_l$, $1\le l\le k$. Let $t_l>0$ sum to one, put $s=\sum_l t_lz_l$, and let $A=\sum_l t_lK_l$. Suppose the finitely many ambient affine cap margins $L_j(z,x)=a_j\cdot z+u_j\cdot x-b_j$ vanish at $(s,y)$ and their nonzero continuous normals $u_j$ span $V$ of rank $r$ and have a strictly positive balancing relation. On the product space the lifted margins are
$$
\sum_l t_l L_j(z_l,x_l)=u_j\cdot\Big(\sum_l t_lx_l-y\Big).
$$
Their normals retain rank $r$ and the same relation space, and their closed caps cover the whole product ambient space. Their common boundary flat meets the interior of $\prod_l K_l$ precisely when $\pi_V y\in\operatorname{int}_V(\pi_V A)$, where $\pi_V$ is orthogonal projection. If $r=d$, this is $y\in\operatorname{int} A$, which places $(s,y)$ in the interior of the continuous fiber of the original body at $s$.

## Proof

Expand the left side using $\sum_l t_l=1$, $\sum_l t_lz_l=s$, and $L_j(s,y)=0$. The map taking $u$ to $(t_lu)_l$ is linear and injective, so all linear relations and the rank are preserved, including minimality when the original balancing relation is a minimal positive circuit. The positively weighted sum of the lifted margins is identically zero, so one margin is nonnegative at each ambient product point.

The common flat is the inverse image of $\pi_V y$ under the surjective linear map $(x_l)_l\mapsto\pi_V\sum_l t_lx_l$. The image of the product is $\pi_V A$. A surjective linear map is open, so the image of the interior is contained in the interior of the image. For the reverse inclusion, fix an interior source point with image $a$. If a desired image point is interior and differs from $a$, extend the segment from $a$ past the desired point to another point of the image. A strict convex combination of a preimage of that farther point and the original interior source point is interior and maps to the desired point. The case of image $a$ uses the original point itself. Finally $A$ is contained in the actual fiber at $s$ by convexity.
