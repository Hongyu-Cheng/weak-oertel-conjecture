## Statement

For a real number \(x\), write \(x_+:=\max\{x,0\}\). Let \(0<\theta<1\), and let \((u_k)_{k\geq 0}\) be a nonnegative summable sequence with \(u_0=1\). Assume that it is log-concave in the standard discrete sense: its positive support \(\{k\geq 0:u_k>0\}\) is an interval of integers, and
\[
u_k^2\geq u_{k-1}u_{k+1}\qquad(k\geq 1).
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{k\geq 0}(u_k-\theta)_+
\geq
\left(1-\theta-\theta\log\frac1\theta\right)\sum_{k\geq 0}u_k.
\]
The coefficient \(1-\theta-\theta\log(1/\theta)\) is best possible: for \(u_k=r^k\), the ratio of the left-hand side to \(\sum_{k\geq0}u_k\) tends to this coefficient as \(r\uparrow1\).

## Proof

First suppose that \(u_k>0\) for every \(k\). Put
\[
\phi(k):=-\log u_k
\]
at the nonnegative integers and extend \(\phi\) piecewise linearly to \([0,\infty)\). Log-concavity says that the successive slopes of this interpolation are nondecreasing, so \(\phi\) is convex. Since \(\sum_k u_k<\infty\), one has \(u_k\to0\), hence \(\phi(k)\to\infty\).

Set
\[
a:=\log\frac1\theta>0.
\]
The strict sublevel set \(\{x\geq0:\phi(x)<a\}\) is an interval containing \(0\), because \(\phi(0)=0<a\), and it is bounded because \(\phi(x)\to\infty\). Thus it has the form \([0,x_*)\) for some \(x_*>0\), and continuity gives \(\phi(x_*)=a\). Convexity and the chord from \((0,0)\) to \((x_*,a)\) give
\[
\phi(x)\leq \frac{a}{x_*}x\qquad(0\leq x\leq x_*).
\]
For \(x\geq x_*\), monotonicity of secant slopes from \(0\) gives
\[
\phi(x)\geq \frac{a}{x_*}x.
\]
Let
\[
r:=e^{-a/x_*}\in(0,1),\qquad w_k:=r^k.
\]
It follows that
\[
u_k\geq w_k\quad(k<x_*),\qquad
u_k\leq w_k\quad(k>x_*),
\]
with equality \(u_k=w_k=\theta\) if \(k=x_*\) is an integer. Moreover,
\[
u_k>\theta\ \Longleftrightarrow\ k<x_*
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ w_k>\theta.
\]

This argument does not assume that \((u_k)\) decreases from \(k=0\). Indeed, its positive-term ratios are nonincreasing, but they may initially exceed \(1\); equivalently, \(\phi\) may initially decrease below \(0\). The chord inequality above remains valid on such an increasing head, and the comparison below uses only a lower bound on the whole head and an upper bound on the tail.

Let \(m:=\lceil x_*\rceil\), and define
\[
H_u:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}u_k,\quad
T_u:=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}u_k,\quad
H_w:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}w_k,\quad
T_w:=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}w_k.
\]
The pointwise comparison gives \(H_u\geq H_w\) and \(T_u\leq T_w\). Since every \(w_k\) in the head is strictly larger than \(\theta\), \(H_w-m\theta>0\). For
\[
F(H,T):=\frac{H-m\theta}{H+T},
\]
one has
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial H}
=\frac{T+m\theta}{(H+T)^2}>0,\qquad
\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}
=-\frac{H-m\theta}{(H+T)^2}<0
\]
whenever \(H\geq H_w\) and \(T\geq0\). Consequently,
\[
\frac{\sum_k(u_k-\theta)_+}{\sum_k u_k}
=F(H_u,T_u)
\geq F(H_w,T_w)
=\frac{\sum_k(w_k-\theta)_+}{\sum_k w_k}.
\]
This is the required head/tail ratio comparison.

Write \(t:=a/x_*=-\log r\). Direct summation of the geometric sequence gives
\[
\frac{\sum_k(w_k-\theta)_+}{\sum_k w_k}
=R_m(t):=1-e^{-mt}-m\theta(1-e^{-t}).
\]

Suppose first that \(m\geq2\). Since \(m-1<x_*\leq m\),
\[
\frac{a}{m}\leq t<\frac{a}{m-1}.
\]
On this interval,
\[
R_m'(t)
=m\bigl(e^{-mt}-\theta e^{-t}\bigr)
=m e^{-t}\bigl(e^{-(m-1)t}-e^{-a}\bigr)
\geq0.
\]
Therefore, using \(1-e^{-s}\leq s\),
\[
R_m(t)
\geq R_m(a/m)
=1-\theta-m\theta(1-e^{-a/m})
\geq1-\theta-\theta a.
\]

The case \(m=1\) must be treated separately because there is no upper endpoint \(a/(m-1)\). Here \(x_*\leq1\), so \(t\geq a\), and
\[
R_1(t)=(1-\theta)(1-e^{-t})
\geq(1-\theta)^2.
\]
The elementary inequality \(a=-\log\theta\geq1-\theta\) yields
\[
(1-\theta)^2
=1-\theta-\theta(1-\theta)
\geq1-\theta-\theta a.
\]
Thus the claimed inequality holds whenever all terms are positive.

It remains to treat zeros and finite support. By the interval-support part of log-concavity and \(u_0=1\), if a zero occurs then the support is \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\) for some finite \(N\). If \(N\geq1\), put \(q_N:=u_N/u_{N-1}>0\). For \(0<\delta<\min\{q_N,1\}\), define
\[
u_k^{(\delta)}
:=
\begin{cases}
u_k,&0\leq k\leq N,\\
u_N\delta^{\,k-N},&k>N.
\end{cases}
\]
The positive-term ratios of this sequence are the original nonincreasing ratios through \(q_N\), followed by \(\delta,\delta,\ldots\). Hence \(u^{(\delta)}\) is positive, summable, log-concave, and has initial term \(1\). The already proved case applies to it. Its added tail has total mass
\[
\sum_{k>N}u_k^{(\delta)}
=\frac{u_N\delta}{1-\delta}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(\delta\downarrow0).
\]
The difference between the threshold-excess sums for \(u^{(\delta)}\) and \(u\) is nonnegative and at most this added tail mass. Therefore both sides of the asserted inequality for \(u^{(\delta)}\) converge to the corresponding sides for \(u\), and the inequality passes to the limit. If \(N=0\), use \(u_k^{(\delta)}=\delta^k\) and the same argument. This rigorously covers all zeros and finite supports allowed by log-concavity.

Finally, fix \(\theta\) and take \(u_k=r^k\) with \(0<r<1\). Let \(t=-\log r\), \(a=\log(1/\theta)\), and \(m=\lceil a/t\rceil\). Then
\[
\frac{\sum_k(r^k-\theta)_+}{\sum_k r^k}
=1-r^m-m\theta(1-r).
\]
As \(r\uparrow1\), one has \(t\downarrow0\), \(a\leq mt<a+t\), and consequently
\[
r^m=e^{-mt}\to e^{-a}=\theta,\qquad
m(1-r)=mt\,\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}\to a.
\]
The ratio therefore tends to
\[
1-\theta-\theta a
=1-\theta-\theta\log\frac1\theta.
\]
No larger uniform coefficient can hold, proving sharpness.
