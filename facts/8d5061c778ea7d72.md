## Statement

For a full-dimensional convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, write $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. For $y\in K$, a unit vector $u$ and $s\ge0$, put $H_\pm(y,u,s)=\{x:u\cdot(x-y)\ge\pm s\}$ and
$$
p_K(y)=\min_{\lVert u\rVert_2=1,\ s\ge0}\frac{|K\cap H_+(y,u,s)|+|K\cap H_-(y,u,s)|}{2|K|},\qquad
P(K)=\max_{y\in K}p_K(y).
$$
Here $|\cdot|$ is ordinary $d$-dimensional volume.

For every $d\ge3$ there is a finite $A_d>0$ depending only on $d$ such that for every full-dimensional convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ and every minimum-volume enclosing simplex $S$,
$$
1-\frac{|K|}{|S|}\le A_d\,[P(K)-\Gamma].
$$
Such enclosing simplices always exist. With the local coefficient $\Gamma c/4$ and the local Hausdorff radius $\varepsilon_d$ of the relative paired stability theorem, one can take $A_d=\max\{4/(\Gamma c),1/\beta_d\}$, where $\beta_d$ is the attained positive paired-depth gap on the normalized class at distance at least $\varepsilon_d$ from the standard simplex, as defined below. If that subclass is empty, set $\beta_d=1$.

## Proof

**Normalized class.** A maximum-volume inscribed simplex exists by compactness of the vertex tuples and continuity of the volume determinant. Its volume is positive because $K$ has interior. Map any such simplex affinely to $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$. By the normalization in fact 03bc771bddd39a8a, replacing a vertex of $T$ by any $x\in K$ bounds the corresponding barycentric coordinate in absolute value by one, so $T\subseteq K\subseteq[-1,1]^d$. Let $v=|T|$ and $s(C)$ be the maximum volume of a simplex inscribed in $C$. The resulting normalized class is
$$
\mathcal A_d=\{C:\ C\text{ compact convex},\ T\subseteq C\subseteq[-1,1]^d,\ s(C)=v\}.
$$
The two inclusions are Hausdorff closed, and $s(C)$ is Hausdorff continuous on these bounded nonempty compact convex sets by fact 03bc771bddd39a8a. Blaschke selection in the common box gives compactness. Every member contains $T$ and thus has positive volume. If a member is itself a simplex, its volume is $s(C)=v$. A point outside $T$ would contribute the positive-volume cone over a violated facet of $T$, so that member must be $T$.

**The gap on the complement.** Fact 7764d50d02b167bf gives $P(C)\ge\Gamma$ and equality exactly for simplices in $d\ge3$. Its total-variation argument also gives Hausdorff continuity on $\mathcal A_d$. In detail, the normalized uniform laws converge in total variation along every Hausdorff-convergent sequence in the class, since the indicators converge away from the boundary of the full-dimensional limit in a common box, and the volumes are bounded below by $v$. Each cap average changes by at most the total-variation distance, uniformly in the center and parameters. The supremum over all ambient centers equals the maximum over the body, since a center outside the body has a zero-mass separating cap at $s=0$. Thus the uniform bound passes through the minima and maxima.

Let $\mathcal A_{d,\mathrm{far}}=\{C\in\mathcal A_d:d_{H,\infty}(C,T)\ge\varepsilon_d\}$. The distance is continuous, so this set is compact. If it is nonempty, let $\beta_d=\min_{C\in\mathcal A_{d,\mathrm{far}}}(P(C)-\Gamma)$. This minimum exists and is positive, since equality would force $C=T$ and $\varepsilon_d>0$. If the set is empty, take $\beta_d=1$.

**Combining the bounds.** Every normalized body has a minimizing enclosing simplex by fact a839bfd22b7f11a2. For $d_{H,\infty}(K,T)\le\varepsilon_d$, the Hausdorff consequence of the relative paired theorem gives the asserted inequality for every such $S$ with coefficient $4/(\Gamma c)$. Otherwise $K\in\mathcal A_{d,\mathrm{far}}$ and $1-|K|/|S|\le1\le(P(K)-\Gamma)/\beta_d$. The constant is therefore finite, with the stated dimension dependence. The paired affine invariance of the relative theorem and the common determinant factor for the volumes transfer the estimate back for every minimizing enclosure.
