## Statement

Fix \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) and \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). For a convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\tag{1}
\]
and, for a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
 H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\},
 \qquad
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), put
\[
 h_C(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H),
\tag{3}
\]
where \(H\) ranges over closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Assume the following induction hypothesis, vacuous when \(n=1\): for every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), every \(e\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and every convex body \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^e\) with nonempty mixed-integer section and positive total mixed mass \(W_D\), there is
\[
 p_D\in D\cap(\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^e)
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 h_D(p_D)\ge c_0 2^{-k}W_D.
\tag{4}
\]
Suppose that \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\), \(W>0\), and \(C\) is a counterexample at base dimension \(n\), namely
\[
 h_C(p)<c_0 2^{-n}W
 \qquad
 \text{for every }p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{5}
\]

A primitive affine lattice subspace of rank \(q\) means a set
\[
 L=z_L+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Gamma,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(z_L\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(\Gamma\leq\mathbb Z^n\) has rank \(q\), and
\[
 \Gamma=(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Gamma)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(L\cap\mathbb Z^n=z_L+\Gamma\). For such an \(L\), put
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\tag{8}
\]
Then every proper primitive affine lattice subspace \(L\) of rank \(q<n\) satisfies the strict inequality
\[
 \boxed{W_L<2^{q-n}W.}
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently, if a counterexample exists and \(n\) is its least positive base dimension, every counterexample at that least dimension satisfies (9) with the same fixed \(c_0\).

## Proof

Fix a proper primitive affine lattice subspace \(L\) of rank \(q<n\), and suppose for contradiction that
\[
 W_L\geq 2^{q-n}W.
\tag{10}
\]
Because \(W>0\), equation (10) gives \(W_L>0\). The boundedness of the base projection of \(C\) makes
\[
 I:=\{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}
\tag{11}
\]
a finite nonempty set. Let
\[
 A:=\operatorname{aff}_{\mathbb R}I,
 \qquad r:=\dim A.
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(I\subseteq L\), one has \(0\leq r\leq q<n\). Choose \(z_0\in I\) and put
\[
 \Gamma_A:=(A-z_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{13}
\]
The integer differences of points of \(I\) span \(A-z_0\) over \(\mathbb R\), so \(\Gamma_A\) has rank \(r\). It is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^n\): if \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(mv\in\Gamma_A\) for an integer \(m\ne0\), then the linearity of \(A-z_0\) gives \(v\in A-z_0\), hence \(v\in\Gamma_A\). Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(b_1,\ldots,b_r\) of \(\Gamma_A\), and let
\[
 B:\mathbb R^r\longrightarrow A-z_0,
 \qquad
 Bu:=\sum_{i=1}^r u_i b_i.
\tag{14}
\]
Then
\[
 A\cap\mathbb Z^n=z_0+B\mathbb Z^r.
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(E:=A\times\mathbb R^d\) and use the affine chart
\[
 \Phi:\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\longrightarrow E,
 \qquad
 \Phi(u,x):=(z_0+Bu,x).
\tag{16}
\]
Define
\[
 D:=\Phi^{-1}(C\cap E).
\tag{17}
\]
We verify that \(D\) is a convex body in its stated ambient space, rather than merely a lower-dimensional convex set. Choose affinely independent \(z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_r\in I\). Each \(C_{z_i}\) has positive \(d\)-dimensional volume, so it is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. The affine hull of \(C\cap E\) contains all vertical directions \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d\) from the fiber over \(z_0\). Choosing one point in each other positive fiber supplies, modulo those vertical directions, the independent base directions \(z_i-z_0\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(C\cap E)=A\times\mathbb R^d=E.
\tag{18}
\]
The set \(C\cap E\) is compact and convex, so (18) gives it nonempty relative interior in \(E\). Since \(\Phi\) is an affine isomorphism, \(D\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^{r+d}\).

The chart preserves every continuous fiber coordinate and parametrizes the affine lattice exactly. Thus
\[
 D_u=C_{z_0+Bu}
 \qquad(u\in\mathbb R^r)
\tag{19}
\]
and its total mixed mass is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_D
 &=\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z^r}\operatorname{vol}_d(D_u)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
 =W_L.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
For the last equality, every positive-mass integer fiber of \(C\) whose base lies in \(L\) belongs to \(I\subseteq A\); hence each integer base in \(L\setminus A\) has \(a_z=0\).

Assume first that \(r\geq1\). Since \(r<n\), the induction hypothesis applied to \(D\) gives a point
\[
 p_D=(u_*,x_*)\in D\cap(\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d)
\tag{21}
\]
with intrinsic depth at least
\[
 \tau:=c_0 2^{-r}W_D=c_0 2^{-r}W_L.
\tag{22}
\]
Put
\[
 p:=\Phi(p_D)=(z_0+Bu_*,x_*)
 \in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{23}
\]
We transfer the intrinsic slice depth to ambient depth. Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any closed halfspace containing \(p\). Its intersection with \(E\) contains \(p\). If \(H\cap E=E\), then the slice contribution is \(W_L\geq\tau\), since \(c_0 2^{-r}<1\). Otherwise, the restriction to \(E\) of a defining affine functional for \(H\) is nonconstant, and
\[
 G:=\Phi^{-1}(H\cap E)
\tag{24}
\]
is a proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^{r+d}\) containing \(p_D\). Equations (19)-(24) then give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\geq
 \sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\\
 &=
 \sum_{u\in\mathbb Z^r}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(D_u\cap G_u)
 \geq \tau.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
This includes ambient halfspaces whose boundary contains \(p\). Taking the infimum over \(H\ni p\) and using (10), \(r\leq q\), and (22), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h_C(p)
 &\geq c_0 2^{-r}W_L\\
 &\geq c_0 2^{-r}2^{q-n}W
 =c_0 2^{-n}2^{q-r}W
 \geq c_0 2^{-n}W,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
contradicting (5).

It remains to treat intrinsic rank \(r=0\), including the case \(q=0\). Then \(A=\{z_0\}\), and (20) says that the convex body
\[
 K:=C_{z_0}\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\tag{27}
\]
has volume \(W_L>0\). Apply accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` to \(K\) with \(m=W_L=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\). Its full-mass metronoid is nonempty, and every \(x_*\) in it satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap J)
 \geq \gamma_d W_L,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{28}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(x_*\), including boundary-containing halfspaces. Moreover,
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1}>c_0,
\tag{29}
\]
where the strict inequality follows from \(d\log(1+1/d)<1\).

Put \(p=(z_0,x_*)\). For every ambient closed halfspace \(H\ni p\), its section \(H_{z_0}\) is either all of \(\mathbb R^d\) or a closed halfspace containing \(x_*\). Thus (28), also trivially in the whole-space case, gives
\[
 \mu_C(H)\geq\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap H_{z_0})
 \geq\gamma_dW_L.
\tag{30}
\]
Using (10), \(q\geq0\), and (29), we conclude
\[
 h_C(p)\geq\gamma_dW_L
 >c_0W_L
 \geq c_0 2^{q-n}W
 \geq c_0 2^{-n}W,
\tag{31}
\]
again contradicting (5).

Both possible intrinsic ranks lead to contradictions. Therefore (10) is impossible for every proper primitive affine lattice subspace \(L\), which proves the strict inequality (9). If \(n\) is the least positive base dimension of a counterexample for the fixed \(c_0\), the induction hypothesis (4) holds by minimality, so the final consequence follows without changing \(c_0\).
