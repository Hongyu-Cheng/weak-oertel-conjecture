## Statement

For $d\ge1$ and $m=d+1$, let $D_d$ consist of all $v\in\mathbb R_+^m$ for which there exists a nonnegative zero-diagonal column-stochastic $m\times m$ matrix $Y$ satisfying $v_i\le \operatorname{GM}_d((Y_{ij})_{j\ne i})$ for every $i$, where $\operatorname{GM}_d$ is the geometric mean of $d$ entries. The set $D_d$ is compact, convex, and downward in the nonnegative orthant. For every $c\ge0$ its support function satisfies the attained identity
$$
h_{D_d}(c)=\min\left\{\sum_j\lambda_j:\ \lambda\ge0,\ c_i\le d\left(\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j\right)^{1/d}\ \text{for every }i\right\}.
$$
In particular, $v\ge0$ belongs to $D_d$ if and only if
$$
\sum_jp_j^d-d\sum_i v_i\prod_{j\ne i}p_j\ge0\qquad\text{for every }p\ge0.
$$
This gives $D_1=[0,1]^2$ and $D_2=\{v\ge0:\sum_i v_i^2+2v_1v_2v_3\le1\}$. For positive $\lambda$ with $\sum_j\lambda_j=dT$ and each $\lambda_i\le T$, the choice $c_i=d(\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j)^{1/d}$ gives an attaining primal matrix $Y_{ii}=0$ and $Y_{ij}=(T-\lambda_i)/\lambda_j$ for $i\ne j$.

## Proof

The geometric mean of $z\ge0$ is the infimum of $d^{-1}\sum_j a_jz_j$ over $a_j>0$ with $\prod_j a_j=1$. AM–GM gives the lower bound and equality when $z>0$ by taking $a_j=\operatorname{GM}_d(z)/z_j$. If $z_\ell=0$, take all $a_j=\delta$ for $j\ne\ell$ and $a_\ell=\delta^{-(d-1)}$, then let $\delta\downarrow0$. This gives concavity. The allowed matrix set is compact and convex, and all feasible $v$ lie in $[0,1]^m$, so the defining closed inequalities give compactness by projection. Concavity gives convexity and the defining coordinatewise upper bounds give downward closure. Concavity and homogeneity also give $\operatorname{GM}_d(u+v)\ge\operatorname{GM}_d(u)+\operatorname{GM}_d(v)$.

For feasible $\lambda$ and any allowed $Y$,
$$
c_i\operatorname{GM}_d((Y_{ij})_{j\ne i})\le d\operatorname{GM}_d((\lambda_jY_{ij})_{j\ne i})\le\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_jY_{ij}.
$$
Summing gives weak duality. For the reverse inequality, fix $\epsilon>0$ and maximize
$$
H_\epsilon(Y)=\sum_i c_i\operatorname{GM}_d((Y_{ij}+\epsilon)_{j\ne i})
$$
over allowed matrices. At a maximizing matrix set $\lambda_j=\max_{i\ne j}\partial_{ij}H_\epsilon(Y)$, where derivatives are in the off-diagonal entries. Each positive entry has maximal derivative in its column, since a transfer from a positive entry to another off-diagonal entry is feasible in the forward direction. Thus $\sum_j\lambda_j=\sum_{i\ne j}Y_{ij}\partial_{ij}H_\epsilon(Y)\le H_\epsilon(Y)$. For the inequality, all partial derivatives are nonnegative, and replacing each multiplier $Y_{ij}$ by $Y_{ij}+\epsilon$ in the derivative pairing makes its sum exactly $H_\epsilon(Y)$. In row $i$ the $d$ partial derivatives are $c_i\operatorname{GM}_d((Y_{ij}+\epsilon)_{j\ne i})/[d(Y_{ij}+\epsilon)]$, with product $(c_i/d)^d$. The column maxima therefore make $\lambda$ dual feasible, including when $c_i=0$. Uniform continuity of the geometric mean on the compact entry range gives uniform convergence of $H_\epsilon$ to $H_0$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The dual infimum is consequently at most $\max H_0=h_{D_d}(c)$, and the feasible set is closed, nonempty by taking sufficiently large constant $\lambda$, and compact on any bounded sum sublevel. The dual infimum is attained.

For necessity of the polynomial condition, take $\lambda_j=p_j^d$ and $c_i=d\prod_{j\ne i}p_j$ in weak duality. Conversely, for arbitrary $c\ge0$ take a dual minimizer and $p_j=\lambda_j^{1/d}$. The polynomial condition implies $c\cdot v\le\sum_j\lambda_j=h_{D_d}(c)$, using $v\ge0$. These inequalities for nonnegative $c$ suffice: if $v\ge0$ lies outside $D_d$, let $w\in D_d$ be its Euclidean nearest point. Then $c=v-w$ strictly separates $v$ from $D_d$ by the projection inequality. The same inequality applied to a small coordinatewise decrease at any $w_j>0$ gives $c_j\ge0$. For $w_j=0$ one has $c_j=v_j\ge0$ directly.

For $d=1$, testing the two coordinate vectors gives $v_i\le1$, which is sufficient. For $d=2$, the quadratic form has symmetric matrix with diagonal $1$ and with off-diagonal entries $-v_i$ in the complementary pair of indices. Replacing any real vector by its coordinatewise absolute values can only decrease that quadratic, so testing nonnegative vectors is equivalent to positive semidefiniteness. Its determinant is $1-\sum_i v_i^2-2v_1v_2v_3$. Nonnegativity of this determinant with $v\ge0$ already implies $v_i\le1$, so all principal minors are nonnegative and the matrix is positive semidefinite.

Finally, the proposed explicit $Y$ has in column $j$ off-diagonal sum $(dT-\sum_{i\ne j}\lambda_i)/\lambda_j=1$. Its weighted row value is $d(T-\lambda_i)$, including when that row is zero. Summing yields $dT=\sum_j\lambda_j$, matching the dual bound.
