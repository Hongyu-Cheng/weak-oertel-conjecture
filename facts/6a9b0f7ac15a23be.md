## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,
\]
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
and, for \(p\in S\), put
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\{\mu_C(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
A_m:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\,w_{2m-z}.
\]

Then:

1. There is \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_m>0\) such that
\[
A_m\geq 2^{-n}Ww_m.
\]

2. Fix arbitrary
\[
L\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},\qquad
m\in\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z).
\]
Let
\[
T_L(z,x):=(z,x-Lz),\qquad K:=T_L(C),
\]
\[
D_{L,m}:=
\{(v,y):(m+v,y)\in K,\ (m-v,y)\in K\},
\]
\[
Q_U:=U[-1/2,1/2]^n,
\]
\[
D^-_{L,m,U}:=
\{(v,y):(v+q,y)\in D_{L,m}\text{ for every }q\in Q_U\},
\]
and
\[
V_{L,m,U}:=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D^-_{L,m,U}).
\]
If \(V_{L,m,U}>0\), there is
\[
p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)
\]
such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p\) satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\frac{1}{2e}V_{L,m,U}.
\]
This includes vertical halfspaces, whole-space halfspaces, and closed
halfspaces whose boundary carries positive mixed mass. Consequently, for
\[
\mathfrak P(C):=
\sup_{\substack{
L\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},\ m\in\mathbb Z^n\\
U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)}}
V_{L,m,U},
\]
one has
\[
\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq\frac{1}{2e}\mathfrak P(C).
\]

## Proof

The projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is compact
and hence meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points. Therefore
only finitely many fibers \(C_z\) are nonempty. In particular, every sum
below is finite. Empty fibers and nonempty lower-dimensional fibers have
\(w_z=0\) and contribute zero.

We first prove assertion 1. For each parity class
\(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod 2}w_z.
\]
Every ordered pair \((z,z')\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z^n\) with
\(z\equiv z'\pmod 2\) has the unique integer midpoint
\(m=(z+z')/2\). Conversely, every term \(w_z w_{2m-z}\) has two
same-parity endpoints. Thus exact same-parity midpoint counting gives
\[
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^n}A_m
=
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}W_r^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz over all \(2^n\) parity classes, including those of zero
mass, gives
\[
\sum_rW_r^2\geq 2^{-n}\left(\sum_rW_r\right)^2
=2^{-n}W^2.
\tag{2}
\]

We must ensure that the mass in (1) is supported only at positive-volume
midpoint fibers. Suppose a term in \(A_m\) is positive. Then
\(w_z>0\) and \(w_{2m-z}>0\) for its two endpoint fibers. A
positive-volume compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^d\) has nonempty
interior, so there are open Euclidean balls contained in \(C_z\) and
\(C_{2m-z}\). Their Minkowski midpoint contains an open ball. Convexity
of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac12C_z+\frac12C_{2m-z}\subseteq C_m.
\]
Hence \(w_m>0\). The contrapositive is
\[
w_m=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_m=0.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(D_+:=\{m\in\mathbb Z^n:w_m>0\}\). This is a nonempty finite set
because \(W>0\). By (1) through (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{m\in D_+}\frac{A_m}{w_m}
&\geq
\frac{\sum_{m\in D_+}A_m}{\sum_{m\in D_+}w_m}\\
&=
\frac{\sum_mA_m}{W}
\geq 2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
Choosing a maximizer proves assertion 1, including \(w_m>0\).

We now prove assertion 2. Fix \(L,m,U\) as in the statement and abbreviate
\[
D:=D_{L,m},\qquad E:=D^-_{L,m,U},\qquad V:=V_{L,m,U}.
\]
The shear \(T_L\) is an invertible linear map with inverse
\[
T_L^{-1}(z,y)=(z,y+Lz).
\tag{4}
\]
It preserves the base coordinate, translates each continuous lattice
fiber, has determinant one, maps closed halfspaces bijectively to closed
halfspaces, and preserves every fiber's \(d\)-dimensional volume.
Therefore \(K=T_L(C)\) is a convex body and mixed halfspace mass is
unchanged under \(T_L\).

The set \(D\) is compact and convex, and it is invariant under
\[
\rho(v,y):=(-v,y).
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(0\in Q_U\), one has \(E\subseteq D\). Moreover,
\[
E=\bigcap_{q\in Q_U}\bigl(D-(q,0)\bigr),
\tag{6}
\]
so \(E\) is compact and convex. It is also invariant under \(\rho\).
Indeed, if \((v,y)\in E\) and \(q\in Q_U\), then
\((v-q,y)\in D\) because \(-q\in Q_U\); applying (5) gives
\((-v+q,y)\in D\). This is exactly \((-v,y)\in E\).

Assume first that \(V>0\). Then \(E\) is a full-dimensional convex body
in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Let its centroid be
\[
g_E=(\bar v,y_0)
:=\frac1V\int_E(v,y)\,d(v,y).
\]
The symmetry (5) gives \(\bar v=0\), so
\[
g_E=(0,y_0).
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, the centroid of a full-dimensional compact convex set
belongs to its interior. It belongs to the set because its defining
integral is a limit of convex combinations and the set is closed. If it
lay on the boundary, a supporting affine functional would be at most its
value at the centroid everywhere on the set and strictly smaller on a
nonempty open subset of the set. Averaging that functional would then
give a value strictly smaller than its value at the centroid, a
contradiction. Hence
\[
(0,y_0)\in\operatorname{int}E\subseteq D.
\tag{8}
\]
The definition of \(D\) at \(v=0\) now gives \((m,y_0)\in K\). Therefore
\[
p:=T_L^{-1}(m,y_0)=(m,y_0+Lm)
\tag{9}
\]
belongs to \(C\) and has integer base coordinate \(m\), so \(p\in S\).
This proves admissibility of the point selected below.

For \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), define the compact section and its
\(n\)-dimensional volume by
\[
E_y:=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:(v,y)\in E\},
\qquad
f(y):=\operatorname{vol}_n(E_y).
\tag{10}
\]
The indicator of the compact set \(E\) is Borel measurable, so Fubini's
theorem makes \(f\) Borel measurable. It has compact support, and
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}f(y)\,dy=V,\qquad
\frac1V\int_{\mathbb R^d}y f(y)\,dy=y_0.
\tag{11}
\]
The indicator \(\mathbf 1_E(v,y)\) is log-concave in the extended
nonnegative sense because \(E\) is convex. Prékopa's marginal theorem,
stated precisely under EXTERNAL SOURCES, applied by integrating out
\(v\in\mathbb R^n\), shows that \(f\) is log-concave. Thus \(f/V\) is a
log-concave probability density on \(\mathbb R^d\) with centroid \(y_0\).

We next establish the exact lattice counting supplied by the erosion.
Put
\[
F_U:=U[-1/2,1/2)^n.
\tag{12}
\]
The standard half-open cubes \(j+[-1/2,1/2)^n\), \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\),
partition \(\mathbb R^n\). Applying \(U\), and using
\(U\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z^n\), shows that
\[
\{k+F_U:k\in\mathbb Z^n\}
\tag{13}
\]
is a disjoint half-open partition of \(\mathbb R^n\). Since
\(|\det U|=1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(F_U)=1.
\tag{14}
\]
Also \(F_U\subseteq Q_U\), and \(q\in F_U\) implies \(-q\in Q_U\).

For \(k\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(y\in\mathbb R^d\), set
\[
\beta_k(y):=
\operatorname{vol}_n\bigl(E_y\cap(k+F_U)\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
These functions are Borel measurable by Fubini, and (13) through (14)
give, for every \(y\),
\[
0\leq\beta_k(y)\leq1,\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^n}\beta_k(y)=f(y).
\tag{16}
\]
Only finitely many cells can meet the compact set \(E\).

If \(\beta_k(y)>0\), choose
\[
v=k+q\in E_y\cap(k+F_U)
\]
with \(q\in F_U\). Since \(-q\in Q_U\), the definition of \(E\) gives
\[
(k,y)=(v-q,y)\in D.
\]
By the definition of \(D\), both endpoints
\[
(m+k,y)\in K,\qquad (m-k,y)\in K
\tag{17}
\]
then hold. This implication is exact even when a cell boundary is
involved, because the erosion uses the closed cell \(Q_U\), while the
half-open cells in (13) merely assign every \(v\) to one tile.

Let \(\widetilde H_0\) be a proper closed halfspace whose boundary
contains \((m,y_0)\). It has a representation
\[
\widetilde H_0
=
\{(z,y):
a^\top(z-m)+b^\top(y-y_0)\geq0\},
\tag{18}
\]
where \((a,b)\neq(0,0)\). Define
\[
Y_+:=
\begin{cases}
\{y:b^\top(y-y_0)\geq0\},&b\neq0,\\
\mathbb R^d,&b=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
If \(b\neq0\), the log-concave Grünbaum inequality, stated precisely
under EXTERNAL SOURCES, applied to the probability density \(f/V\) and
the closed halfspace in (19) gives
\[
\int_{Y_+}f(y)\,dy\geq\frac Ve.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(b=0\), (11) gives the stronger identity
\(\int_{Y_+}f=V\), so (20) still holds. This is the vertical-normal case.

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let
\[
\iota_z(y):=
\mathbf 1_{\{(z,y)\in K\cap\widetilde H_0\}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Fix \(y\in Y_+\) and \(k\in\mathbb Z^n\). If \(\beta_k(y)>0\), (17)
places both endpoints in \(K\), while their two affine-functional values
in (18) are
\[
a^\top k+b^\top(y-y_0),
\qquad
-a^\top k+b^\top(y-y_0).
\]
Their sum is \(2b^\top(y-y_0)\geq0\), so at least one endpoint belongs
to the closed halfspace \(\widetilde H_0\). Equality causes no loss
because the halfspace is closed. Using also \(\beta_k(y)\leq1\), we get
\[
\beta_k(y)\leq\iota_{m+k}(y)+\iota_{m-k}(y).
\tag{22}
\]
When \(\beta_k(y)=0\), (22) is automatic.

Sum (22) over \(k\). Both maps \(k\mapsto m+k\) and \(k\mapsto m-k\)
are bijections of \(\mathbb Z^n\), so (16) gives
\[
f(y)
\leq
2\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\iota_z(y)
\qquad(y\in Y_+).
\tag{23}
\]
Integrating (23), using (20), and then enlarging the \(y\)-domain on the
right gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac Ve
&\leq\int_{Y_+}f(y)\,dy\\
&\leq
2\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\int_{Y_+}\iota_z(y)\,dy\\
&\leq2\mu_K(\widetilde H_0),
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
where \(\mu_K\) is the mixed measure of \(K\). Therefore
\[
\mu_K(\widetilde H_0)\geq\frac{V}{2e}.
\tag{25}
\]

Now let \(\widetilde H\) be any proper closed halfspace containing
\((m,y_0)\). Translating its boundary parallel toward \((m,y_0)\) until
the boundary passes through that point produces a closed halfspace
\(\widetilde H_0\subseteq\widetilde H\) of the form (18). Hence (25) and
monotonicity give
\[
\mu_K(\widetilde H)\geq\frac{V}{2e}.
\tag{26}
\]
No null-boundary assumption was made anywhere, so this includes
halfspaces whose boundary carries positive mixed mass.

If the whole space is admitted as a halfspace, its mixed mass also
satisfies the desired estimate. Indeed, omit the halfspace condition in
(21). For every \(y,k\) with \(\beta_k(y)>0\), both points in (17) lie in
\(K\), so the analogue of (22) through (24), now integrated over all
\(y\), gives
\[
V\leq2\mu_K(\mathbb R^{n+d})=2W.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus \(W\geq V/2\geq V/(2e)\).

Finally, let \(H\) be any closed halfspace in the original coordinates
containing the point \(p\) in (9). Then
\(\widetilde H:=T_L(H)\) is a closed halfspace containing \((m,y_0)\).
By the fiberwise translations in (4),
\[
\mu_C(H)=\mu_K(\widetilde H).
\]
Equations (26) through (27) therefore prove
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\frac{V}{2e}
\]
for every such \(H\), and hence
\[
h_S(p)\geq\frac{V}{2e}.
\]
This proves assertion 2 for every \(V>0\), every real shear \(L\), every
integer midpoint \(m\), and every unimodular \(U\).

It remains to justify the supremum consequence, including \(V=0\). If a
given triple \(L,m,U\) has \(V=0\), then \(W>0\) supplies at least one
point of \(S\), and nonnegativity of mixed mass gives
\[
\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq0=\frac{V}{2e}.
\]
If \(V>0\), the point constructed above gives the same inequality.
Therefore
\[
\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq\frac{1}{2e}V_{L,m,U}
\]
for every triple \(L,m,U\). Taking the supremum over the triples proves
\[
\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq\frac{1}{2e}\mathfrak P(C).
\]
The supremum on the right is finite: \(E\subseteq D\), and the
volume-preserving injection
\[
(v,y)\longmapsto(m+v,y)
\]
maps \(D\) into \(K\), so
\[
V\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(D)
\leq\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(K)
=\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(C).
\]
This completes the proof.

## External sources

András Prékopa, "On logarithmic concave measures and functions," Acta
Scientiarum Mathematicarum 34 (1973), 335-343, Theorem 6, primary-source
PDF: https://rutcor.rutgers.edu/Prekopa/pdf/SCIENT2.pdf. Exact form used:
if \(g(x,y)\) is a log-concave function on
\(\mathbb R^a\times\mathbb R^b\) and \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^b\) is
convex, then
\[
x\longmapsto\int_A g(x,y)\,dy
\]
is log-concave on \(\mathbb R^a\). Applicability: the indicator
\(\mathbf 1_E\) of the compact convex set \(E\) is a nonnegative Borel
log-concave function, the integrated set is \(A=\mathbb R^n\), and its
marginal is exactly the finite Borel function \(f(y)=\operatorname{vol}_n(E_y)\).

László Lovász and Santosh Vempala, "The Geometry of Logconcave Functions
and Sampling Algorithms," Random Structures & Algorithms 30 (2007),
307-358, Lemma 5.12, https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20135,
author-hosted primary PDF:
https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~vempala/papers/logcon.pdf. Exact form used:
if \(g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R_+\) is a log-concave probability density
with centroid \(c\), then every halfspace containing \(c\) has
\(g\)-mass at least \(1/e\). Applicability: \(f/V\) is proved above to be
a log-concave probability density, (11) shows that its centroid is
\(y_0\), and for \(b\neq0\) the set
\(\{y:b^\top(y-y_0)\geq0\}\) is a closed halfspace containing that
centroid.
