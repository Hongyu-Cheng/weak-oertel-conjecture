## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), and put \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). Write \(C_i=\{\mathbf y:(i,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(a_i=\mathcal L^2(C_i)\), \(r_i=\sqrt{a_i}\), and \(W=\mathcal H_2(S)=\sum_i a_i>0\). Suppose at least five integer fibers have positive area and every \(a_i<W/2\). Choose an area-weighted median among the positive-area fibers. Translate its index to zero and, if necessary, reflect the integer coordinate so that, with \(\widehat r_i=r_i/r_0\),

\[
U=\sum_{i<0}\widehat r_i^2
\le V=\sum_{i>0}\widehat r_i^2,
\qquad 0<U<\frac14.
\tag{1}
\]

There exists one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), in the median fiber or its adjacent fiber toward the heavier tail, such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge2W/9\). The conclusion concerns the original body and its original total \(W\), including any zero-area fibers.

Here is the complete scalar alternative used in the proof. Use exactly the envelope and tail function of accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)
&=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\sum_i
\min\{v_i^2,(\sqrt s+\beta(i-j))_+^2\},\\
\Phi(b)&=\sum_{k\ge1}(1-k/b)_+^2,\qquad
P_{\kappa,A}(s)=
\frac{s\bigl(s+(\kappa/2-1/3)A\bigr)}{\kappa A-s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(u,b,t\) satisfy

\[
0<u<\frac12,\qquad b>2,\qquad t\ge\frac b{b+1},\qquad
1-u^2<V:=t^2(1+\Phi(b))\le1+u^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Define \(g_{-1}=u\), \(g_0=1\), \(g_{1+k}=t(1-k/b)_+\) for integers \(k\ge0\), and \(g_i=0\) at all other indices. Put \(T=T_{\mathbf g}=1+u^2+V\) and \(A=t^2\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
5V\le4(1+u^2)+A
&\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)
\le\frac{5T}{9}+P_{1/2,1}(s)
&&\left(0\le s\le\frac6{17}\right),\\
5V\ge4(1+u^2)+A
&\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,1}(s)
\le\frac{5T}{9}+P_{8/9,A}(s)
&&\left(0\le s\le\frac{4A}{9}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

These are precisely \(\mathrm R_{1/2}(\mathbf g,0)\) and \(\mathrm R_{8/9}(\mathbf g,1)\) in accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`. Both assertions apply at equality in the split. The proof also establishes the exact median envelope \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)=V+s+\min\{u^2,(2\sqrt s-t)_+^2\}\) for \(0\le s\le1\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` supplies root concavity for actual bodies, the simultaneous two-anchor tail comparison, the exact area normalization, and the fixed-fiber rational-test consumer in the locked closed-halfspace convention. Accepted fact `d773b98105d1ae6c` supplies its neighboring-pair bound and the two elementary inequalities

\[
\begin{aligned}
s+(2\sqrt s-\sqrt A)_+^2-\frac{9s^2}{4A}&\le\frac A9
&& (0\le s\le A),\\
P_{8/9,A}(s)&\ge\frac{9s^2}{4A}
&& (0\le s\le4A/9).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

The root caps, areas, and envelopes in these uses have exactly the meanings in (2). Every other estimate below is proved explicitly.

We first prove the scalar alternative. From \(b>2\), strict increase of \(\Phi\), and \(\Phi(2)=1/4\), we have

\[
\frac54t^2<V\le1+u^2<\frac54,
\qquad
t<1,\qquad
t^2<\frac45(1+u^2).
\tag{6}
\]

The positive samples of \(\mathbf g\) are concave: the slope from \(-1\) to zero is \(1-u>0\), the next slope is \(t-1<0\), and the outer right slope is \(-t/b\le t-1\) by (3). In particular \(g_i\le1+i(t-1)\) at every positive right index. Consequently \(V\le\Phi(1/(1-t))\). If \(t\le18/25\), monotonicity of \(\Phi\) would give
\(V\le\Phi(25/7)=461/625<3/4\), contrary to \(V>1-u^2>3/4\). Thus

\[
\frac{18}{25}<t<1.
\tag{7}
\]

The recurrence for \(\Phi\) in accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` and the lower bound on \(t\) give
\(\Phi(b+1)=b^2(1+\Phi(b))/(b+1)^2\le V<5/4\). Since \(\Phi(21/4)=565/441>5/4\), it follows that

\[
2<b<\frac{17}{4},\qquad
\Phi(b)<\Phi(17/4)=\frac{276}{289}<1.
\tag{8}
\]

At the median the neighboring root caps satisfy \(u<t\). The neighboring-pair bound of accepted fact `d773b98105d1ae6c`, with all more distant right terms bounded by their full areas, gives
\(\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)\le V+s+\min\{u^2,(2\sqrt s-t)_+^2\}\).
This is attained. Set \(x=\sqrt s\le1\) and take cap slope \(\beta=t-x\). Its cap root at index one is \(t\), and at every positive right index \(i\) it is
\(it-(i-1)x\ge it-(i-1)\ge g_i\), by the concavity bound just proved. Thus it retains the entire right tail. At index minus one its cap root is \(2x-t\). We have proved

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)
=V+s+\min\{u^2,(2\sqrt s-t)_+^2\}
\qquad(0\le s\le1).
\tag{9}
\]

We next establish the analytic inequality for the median. For \(0\le s\le6/17\), put

\[
J(s)=s-P_{1/2,1}(s)
=\frac{s(7/12-2s)}{1/2-s}.
\tag{10}
\]

We claim that whenever \(0<u<1/2\), \(18/25\le t\le1\), and \(t^2\le4(1+u^2)/5\),

\[
J(s)+\min\{u^2,(2\sqrt s-t)_+^2\}
\le\frac{1+u^2}{5}-\frac{4t^2}{45}
\qquad(0\le s\le6/17).
\tag{11}
\]

All denominators in this calculation are positive. First, \(J(s)<23/180\). After multiplying by \(1/2-s\), this is the positivity of
\(2s^2-(32/45)s+23/360\), whose discriminant is \(-11/2025\) and whose leading coefficient is positive. If \(2\sqrt s\le t\), the minimum in (11) is zero, whereas its right side is at least \(29(1+u^2)/225\ge29/225>23/180\). This proves (11) on that whole region, including its clipping boundary.

Suppose \(x=\sqrt s\) satisfies \(2x>t\). For any nonnegative \(u\) and \(z\),
\(\min\{u^2,z^2\}-u^2/5\le4z^2/5\): use \(u^2\le z^2\) and \(u^2\ge z^2\) separately. It therefore suffices to prove

\[
J(x^2)+\frac45(2x-t)^2+\frac{4t^2}{45}\le\frac15.
\tag{12}
\]

For fixed \(x\), the left side is convex in \(t\). Its allowed interval is \([18/25,\min\{1,2x\}]\), so it suffices to check its endpoints.

At the endpoint \(t=2x\), multiplication of (12) by \(1/2-s\), with \(s=x^2\), gives the positivity of
\((106/45)s^2-(173/180)s+1/10\). Its discriminant is \(-599/32400\), so this endpoint is proved.

The endpoint \(t=1\) is relevant only when \(x\ge1/2\). For \(1/4\le s\le6/17\), direct subtraction gives

\[
P_{1/2,1}(s)-P_{8/9,1}(s)
=\frac{7s(9s-2)}{(6-12s)(8-9s)}\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]

Using (5), we obtain
\(J(s)+(4/5)(2\sqrt s-1)^2\le s+(2\sqrt s-1)^2-P_{8/9,1}(s)\le1/9\). Adding \(4/45\) proves (12) at this endpoint.

For the fixed endpoint \(t=18/25\), let

\[
F(x)=\left(\frac12-x^2\right)
\left(\frac{481}{3125}-\frac45\left(2x-\frac{18}{25}\right)^2\right)
-x^2\left(\frac7{12}-2x^2\right).
\tag{14}
\]

The endpoint inequality (12) is exactly \(F(x)\ge0\) on \(9/25\le x\le\sqrt{6/17}\). With \(y=x-1/2\), direct expansion gives the identity

\[
F(x)=\frac{59}{30000}
+\frac{761}{7500}y
+\frac{18161}{7500}y^2
+\frac{1012}{125}y^3
+\frac{26}{5}y^4.
\tag{15}
\]

If \(y\ge0\), every term in (15) is nonnegative and its constant term is positive. If \(-1/10\le y\le0\), then
\(18161/7500+(1012/125)y\ge12089/7500>8/5\), while \(59/30000>1/600\) and \(761/7500<51/500\). Taking account of the sign of \(y\), (15) gives

\[
F(x)\ge\frac1{600}+\frac{51}{500}y+\frac85y^2>0.
\tag{16}
\]

The last quadratic is positive because its discriminant is \(-197/750000\). Finally, on \([9/25,2/5]\), the derivative \(F'\) is convex, since
\(F'''(x)=(624/5)x-1728/125>0\). Its endpoint values are
\(F'(9/25)=-24621/156250<0\) and \(F'(2/5)=-1507/9375<0\). Convexity makes \(F'\) negative throughout this interval, so \(F(x)\ge F(2/5)>0\), the last inequality already following from (16). These intervals cover the entire required range. This proves the fixed endpoint, hence (12), and hence (11).

If \(5V\le4(1+u^2)+A\), then

\[
\frac{5T}{9}-V
=\frac{5+5u^2-4V}{9}
\ge\frac{1+u^2}{5}-\frac{4A}{45}.
\tag{17}
\]

Equations (6), (7), (9), and (11) now prove the first line of (4).

We turn to the neighbor. The following bound covers its full required cap range:

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,1}(s)
\le1+u^2+s+(2\sqrt s-1)_+^2
\qquad(0\le s\le4A/9).
\tag{18}
\]

To prove it, normalize the roots once more by dividing by \(t\), solely for this auxiliary calculation at anchor one. Put \(q=1/t\), \(\omega=\Phi(b)\), \(p=1-1/b\), and \(c=1-2/b=2p-1\). Equations (3), (6), and (8) imply

\[
2<b<\frac{17}{4},\quad 0<\omega<1,\quad
1\le q\le1+\frac1b,\quad
q^2\ge\frac45(1+\omega),\quad
0<c<\frac9{17}.
\tag{19}
\]

For \(0\le z\le2/3\), define the off-anchor retained sum

\[
M_z(\beta)=\min\{q^2,(z-\beta)_+^2\}
+\sum_{k\ge1}\min\{(1-k/b)_+^2,(z+k\beta)_+^2\}.
\tag{20}
\]

We will prove

\[
M_z(\beta)\le q^2+(2z-q)_+^2
\qquad(\beta\in\mathbb R,\ 0\le z\le2/3).
\tag{21}
\]

At \(z=0\), any slope retains terms on at most one side, so \(M_0(\beta)\le\max\{q^2,\omega\}=q^2\). Suppose henceforth that \(z>0\). Every nonnegative slope satisfies
\(M_z(\beta)\le z^2+\omega\le4/9+\omega<q^2\), since (19) gives \(q^2-(4/9+\omega)\ge16/45-\omega/5>0\).

It remains to bound negative slopes. Between consecutive cap-saturation slopes, the saturated terms in (20) are constant and the other terms are squares of positive parts of affine functions. Thus (20) is convex on each such interval. Its supremum occurs at a saturation slope or one of the two infinite-slope limits. The limits are \(q^2\) and \(\omega\), both covered by (21). Zero cap roots contribute identically zero and create no additional saturation case.

The left saturation slope is \(\beta=z-q<0\). At every right index \(k\ge2\), its cap root is \((k+1)z-kq\le3z-2q\le0\). Hence the retained sum there is
\(q^2+\min\{p^2,(2z-q)_+^2\}\), proving (21).

A negative right saturation slope at a positive root with index \(k\) is
\(\beta=((1-k/b)-z)/k\). We can discard every such candidate with \(k\ge3\), by the following derivative argument. At this slope, all right indices at least \(k\) are saturated and all indices \(1,\ldots,k-1\) are positive and unsaturated. This follows directly by subtracting the two affine roots: their difference at index \(i\) is \((1-z)(1-i/k)\). Immediately to the right of this slope, the derivative of the retained sum, divided by two, is

\[
z\sum_{i\in I}i+\beta\sum_{i\in I}i^2,
\qquad
I=\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\
\text{or}\
I=\{-1,1,\ldots,k-1\}.
\tag{22}
\]

The first choice applies when the left cap remains saturated; the second applies when it is unsaturated, including a simultaneous left saturation, since increasing \(\beta\) then makes the left term unsaturated. Since the root at index \(k\) is positive, \(\beta>-z/k\). In the first case, (22) is therefore strictly greater than \(z(k^2-1)/6\). In the second, it is strictly greater than
\(z(k-3)(k+1)(k+2)/(6k)\). These identities follow from the sums of the first integers and their squares. Both lower bounds are nonnegative for \(k\ge3\), and the comparisons are strict. The right derivative is positive, so such a slope cannot maximize (20). This argument also covers coincident cap-saturation slopes.

Only right indices one and two remain. For index one, a negative saturation slope requires \(z\ge p\ge1/2\); hence \(b\le3\), \(p\le2/3\), and \(\omega=p^2+(2p-1)^2\). The left cap root is \(2z-p\le5/6<q\), so its retained sum is \(\omega+(2z-p)^2\). For fixed \(z\le2/3\), the expression \(q^2+(2z-q)_+^2\) is increasing in \(q\ge1\). It is enough to use \(q=1\). Direct subtraction, and then \(z\le2/3\), give

\[
\begin{aligned}
1+(2z-1)^2-\omega-(2z-p)^2
&=2-4(1-p)z-p^2-\omega\\
&\ge\frac{-18p^2+20p-5}{3}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

The last quadratic is concave on \([1/2,2/3]\), and its endpoint values are \(1/6\) and \(1/9\). This proves (21) at index one, including the zero-slope boundary.

At index two, a negative saturation slope requires \(z\ge c\). Its left cap root is \((3z-c)/2\le1\le q\), and its first right cap root is \((z+c)/2\le p\). All later right terms are saturated. Thus the retained sum is

\[
B_2(z)=\frac52z^2-cz+\frac12c^2+\omega-p^2,
\qquad
B_2(2/3)=\frac{31}{36}-\frac76c+\frac14c^2+\omega.
\tag{24}
\]

This function increases for \(z\ge c\). If \(q\ge4/3\), the joining constraint in (19) implies \(b\le3\) and hence \(\omega\le5/9\). Therefore
\(B_2(z)\le B_2(2/3)\le31/36+5/9=17/12<16/9\le q^2\), proving (21).

Assume now \(1\le q\le4/3\). On the portion \(z\ge q/2\), the difference
\(q^2+(2z-q)^2-B_2(z)\) has derivative \(3z+c-4q<0\). It suffices to check it at \(z=2/3\). The identity

\[
q^2+(4/3-q)^2
=\frac23q^2+\frac49+\frac43(q-1)^2
\ge\frac{44}{45}+\frac8{15}\omega
\tag{25}
\]

uses (19). Subtracting (24) bounds the required endpoint difference below by

\[
\frac7{60}+\frac76c-\frac14c^2-\frac7{15}\omega.
\tag{26}
\]

For \(2<b\le3\), we have \(0<c\le1/3\) and \(\omega=(1+2c+5c^2)/4\). Expression (26) is then \(c(14/15-5c/6)>0\).

For \(3\le b<17/4\), we have \(1/3\le c<9/17\) and \(\omega\le2c\). Here is a direct verification of the latter estimate, including the change at \(b=4\):

\[
\omega-2c=
\begin{cases}
(7c^2-6c+1)/2,&1/3\le c\le1/2,\\
(15c^2-14c+3)/2,&1/2\le c\le9/17.
\end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]

These formulas are just the sums of the two, three, or four positive affine-root squares; a root at an integer endpoint contributes zero. Each displayed quadratic is convex. The first has endpoint values \(-1/9,-1/8\), and the second has endpoint values \(-1/8,-30/289\). Both are negative on their full intervals. Thus (26) is at least
\(7/60+7c/30-c^2/4\ge(7-c)/60>0\), using \(0<c<1\).

The endpoint difference in (25) is now proved in every case. It proves (21) on \(z\ge q/2\) by the decreasing-difference argument. On the remaining portion \(c\le z\le q/2\), monotonicity of \(B_2\) gives \(B_2(z)\le B_2(q/2)\le q^2\); if this portion is empty no assertion is needed. This proves (21) at index two. The limiting slopes, the left saturation slope, every nonnegative slope, and all right saturation slopes have now been covered, so (21) holds for all slopes.

In the original units of \(\mathbf g\), set \(z=\sqrt s/t\). The remote left fiber at index minus one contributes at most \(u^2\). After dropping that term to its full-area upper bound, (20) is exactly the remaining off-anchor sum divided by \(t^2\). Equation (21), multiplied by \(t^2\) and with the anchor contribution \(s\) restored, gives

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,1}(s)
\le u^2+s+t^2\bigl(q^2+(2z-q)_+^2\bigr)
=1+u^2+s+(2\sqrt s-1)_+^2.
\]

This is (18). Since \(t<1\), (5) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
s+(2\sqrt s-1)_+^2
&\le s+(2\sqrt s-t)_+^2\\
&\le\frac A9+\frac{9s^2}{4A}
\le\frac A9+P_{8/9,A}(s)
\qquad(0\le s\le4A/9).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]

If \(5V\ge4(1+u^2)+A\), then \(1+u^2+A/9\le5T/9\). Combining (18) and (28) proves the second line of (4). The scalar alternative is complete, including equality in its split and every zero or clipped retained cap.

We finish by verifying that every actual body in the statement enters this domain, and by transferring the resulting test to its original fibers. Accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` makes the positive-area integer support consecutive and its normalized roots concave. A nonheavy median lies strictly inside this support. If its left side had \(m\ge2\) positive roots, concavity between its root one and the far left positive root would imply \(\widehat r_{-1}\ge1-1/m\ge1/2\), contradicting \(U<1/4\). Thus there is exactly one positive left root, \(u=\widehat r_{-1}=\sqrt U\in(0,1/2)\).

Write \(t=\widehat r_1\). At least three positive roots lie on the right, since there are at least five positive roots in total. Concavity between indices one and three gives \(\widehat r_2>t/2\). Hence \(V>(5/4)t^2\). The median inequalities give \(V\le1+u^2\); nonheaviness of the median gives \(1<(1+u^2+V)/2\), or \(V>1-u^2\). In particular \(t<1\). There are at most five positive right roots: if their number were \(m\ge6\), the chord bound from the median root to the positive endpoint would give \(V\ge\Phi(m)\ge\Phi(6)=55/36>5/4\), a contradiction.

Apply the simultaneous variable-length replacement in accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`, preserving the roots at indices zero and one. The left parameter is \(a=1/(1-u)\in(1,2)\), since \(\Phi(a)=u^2\), so this replacement leaves the one positive left root equal to \(u\). The right outer mass determines \(b>1\) by
\(t^2\Phi(b)=V-t^2\). The preceding strict lower bound on \(V\) implies \(\Phi(b)>1/4\), hence \(b>2\). The simultaneous comparison gives \(t\ge b/(b+1)\), the profile \(\mathbf g\) in (3), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathbf g}&=1+u^2+V
=T_{\widehat{\mathbf r}}=\frac W{a_0},\\
\mathcal E_{\widehat{\mathbf r},0}(s)
&\le\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(s)
&& (0\le s\le1),\\
\mathcal E_{\widehat{\mathbf r},1}(s)
&\le\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,1}(s)
&& (0\le s\le t^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

Every hypothesis in (3) has now been derived with the original normalized masses unchanged. Equation (4) supplies one of the two rational tests, and (29) transfers it to the same anchor of the actual normalized profile.

Finally the exact normalization in accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` is
\(\mathcal E_{\mathbf r,j}(a_0s)=a_0\mathcal E_{\widehat{\mathbf r},j}(s)\) for \(j=0,1\), while \(P_{\kappa,a_0A}(a_0s)=a_0P_{\kappa,A}(s)\). The anchor area is therefore \(a_0\) or \(a_0t^2=a_1\), and the total on the right side of the transferred test is exactly \(W\). The fixed-fiber consumer in that accepted fact gives one point in the selected original fiber whose depth is at least \(2W/9\), with the point fixed before all containing closed ambient halfspaces are quantified. Undoing the initial integer translation or reflection preserves this assertion. Zero-area original fibers remain part of \(S\) and contribute zero to every halfspace-intersection area; the accepted consumer also covers closed boundaries containing a whole fiber. This proves the stated actual-body theorem.
