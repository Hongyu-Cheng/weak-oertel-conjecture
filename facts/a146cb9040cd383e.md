## Statement

Use the exact two-hub tables of accepted fact a87effc3cf58f392. Explicitly, let a finite atom set \(X\) be partitioned into cells of size one or two, with distinguished distinct atoms \(c_0,c_1\) in distinct cells. Every two-atom cell has a label \(a\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), there are \(m_a\ge1\) cells of label \(a\), and a cell of label \(a\) does not contain \(c_a\). Every nondistinguished atom belongs to a two-atom cell. A two-atom cell containing \(c_a\) has label \(1-a\). Let \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}\in\lbrace1,2\rbrace\) be the number of distinguished atoms in two-atom cells, so the number of singleton cells is \(2-q_{\mathrm{ov}}\). Set
$$
K=m_0+m_1+2-q_{\mathrm{ov}},
\qquad
R_a=\lbrace c_a\rbrace\cup
\lbrace\text{endpoints of cells of label }a\rbrace.
$$
Here \(K\) is exactly the occupied cell count, and \(m_a\) is the total noncentral pair count, not an additional-axis count.

Give every atom a positive original weight \(w_x\), with the single original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in X}w_x\) and original law \(p_x=w_x/Z\). Require \(p_up_v=p_{c_a}^2\) in every cell \(\lbrace u,v\rbrace\) of label \(a\). Define \(a_a=p_{c_a}\), \(S_a=\sum_{x\in R_a\setminus\lbrace c_a\rbrace}p_x\), and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_a&=\frac{S_a/a_a+2}{2(m_a+1)},
&A&=K^2\sum_{x\in X}p_x^3,
&\Delta_a&=4m_a(m_a+1)K^2a_a^3,\\
A_{\mathrm{full}}&=A+\Delta_0+\Delta_1,
&d&=-\log A,
&g&=\log A_{\mathrm{full}},\\
r_a&=\frac12\left[
\log\frac{A+\Delta_a}{A}
+\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{full}}}{A+\Delta_{1-a}}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
The energy \(A\) here is the atomic energy called \(A_0\) in a87effc3cf58f392 and 4bb26a33ac78ee45; it is the energy called \(A\) in a9f4cb83f6711543. All means, endpoint sums, and increments have exactly their original-law meanings in those predecessors.

Assume \(A<1\), \(\mu_0\ge2\mu_1\), and \(A_{\mathrm{full}}>9/8\). Then the absolute constant \(B=2^{24}\) satisfies
$$
d\le\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{B}{B+2\mu_a}\,r_a.
\tag{2}
$$
Every larger value of \(B\) also satisfies (2).

This includes the exact forced-spine domains of a9f4cb83f6711543 without a restriction on their pair totals. For \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1\), either hub may be the singleton: if \(h\) denotes the core containing the other center, its forced pair has probabilities \(a_{1-h},a_h^2/a_{1-h}\). For \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\), both forced pairs occur. The additional-axis counts \((k_0,k_1)\) in 4bb26a33ac78ee45, whose statement denotes those counts by \((m_0,m_1)\), map to the total counts of this statement as follows. They are \((m_0-1,m_1)\) for \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1,h=0\), \((m_0-1,m_1-1)\) for \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=2\), and \((m_1-1,m_0)\) after interchanging that construction's core labels for \(q_{\mathrm{ov}}=1,h=1\). In the first two cases its anchored hub ratio is \(\tau=a_1/a_0\); in the last case it is \(\tau=a_0/a_1\). If \(W\) is the original raw weight of the hub anchored at one in that construction, its raw weights and normalizer are the original raw weights divided by \(W\) and \(Z/W\), respectively. Thus its normalized probabilities are exactly the original \(p_x\), as in the explicit map of a9f4cb83f6711543. No original atom is discarded, duplicated, or conditionally renormalized, and \(K\) is never replaced by \(2^n\).

## Proof

Only the cell description and exact pair products are needed. In particular the proof applies before any equalization of nonforced pair totals, so it includes the exact Jensen minima as well as arbitrary heterogeneous fields.

Put \(\delta=1-A\). The endpoint sets of the two labels are disjoint, and their complement consists precisely of the singleton hubs. There are \(2K+q_{\mathrm{ov}}-2\le2K\) atoms, so Holder's inequality gives \(A\ge1/4\). For a pair of label \(a\), its total probability \(s\) satisfies \(s\ge2a_a\), and its endpoint cubic sum is exactly \(s^3-3a_a^2s\). Consequently \(\mu_a\ge1\), and the definition of the mean gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_a&=2a_a((m_a+1)\mu_a-1)\ge2m_a\mu_a a_a,\\
\sum_{x\in R_a\setminus\lbrace c_a\rbrace}p_x^3
&\ge\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}-3a_a^2S_a
\ge\left(1-\frac3{4\mu_a^2}\right)\frac{S_a^3}{m_a^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Since \(\mu_1\le\mu_0\), bound both endpoint groups below by the last coefficient in (3) with \(\mu_1\), and bound any singleton cube below by the same coefficient times itself. These groups have total probability one and capacities \(m_0,m_1\), and one for each singleton, whose sum is \(K\). Weighted Holder therefore yields
$$
A\ge1-\frac3{4\mu_1^2},
\qquad
\delta\le\frac3{4\mu_1^2}.
\tag{4}
$$
Here and below the weighted Holder inequality used is \(\sum_j v_j^3/c_j^2\ge(\sum_jv_j)^3/(\sum_jc_j)^2\) for positive capacities.

For \(1/4\le A<1\), the integral identity
\(d/\delta=\int_0^1[A+t(1-A)]^{-1}\,dt\) shows that this ratio is at most \((4/3)\log4<2\). The strict numerical inequality follows from \(e^{3/2}>1+3/2+9/8+27/48>4\). Combining this with (4) gives
$$
d<2\delta\le\frac3{2\mu_1^2},
\qquad 2\mu_1d<3,
\qquad g>\log(9/8)>\frac19.
\tag{5}
$$
The last inequality uses \(\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)\) for \(x>0\).

Write \(\lambda_a=B/(B+2\mu_a)\). Since \(B\ge1\) and \(\mu_a\ge1\), one has \(\lambda_a\ge1/(4\mu_a)\). The exact Shapley identity and a useful individual lower bound are
$$
r_0+r_1=d+g,
\qquad
r_a\ge\frac12\log(1+\Delta_a/A)
\ge\frac12\log(1+\Delta_a)>0.
\tag{6}
$$
Also, if \(r_1\ge d+1/16\), then the low-mean row alone proves (2): indeed
$$
\lambda_1r_1-d
\ge\frac{B/16-2\mu_1d}{B+2\mu_1}>0
\tag{7}
$$
by (5) and \(B/16>3\).

We use the fixed constants \(T=2^{18}\), \(Q=2^{16}\), and \(D=512\) only to split the unbounded scalar domain. Suppose first that \(\mu_0\le T\) or \(\mu_0\le Q\mu_1^2\). The first alternative gives \(2\mu_0d<4T\), while the second gives \(2\mu_0d<3Q<4T\), by (5). Since \(B/9>4T\), it follows that \(Bg>2\mu_0d\). The means are ordered, so \(\lambda_a\ge\lambda_0\); hence
$$
\sum_a\lambda_ar_a
\ge\lambda_0(d+g)\ge d.
\tag{8}
$$
It remains to consider
\(\mu_0>T\) and \(\mu_0>Q\mu_1^2\).

First handle every table in this remaining region with \(\Delta_0\le1/128\). Set \(H=A+\Delta_1\), so \(H=A_{\mathrm{full}}-\Delta_0>143/128\). Directly from (1),
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1-d
&=\log H+\frac12\log
 \frac{1+\Delta_0/H}{1+\Delta_0/A}\\
&\ge\log H-\frac{\Delta_0}{2A}
>\frac{15}{143}-\frac1{64}
>\frac1{16}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
The second line uses \(A\ge1/4\), \(\log(1+x)\le x\), and \(\log H>\log(143/128)>15/143\). The final comparison is \(960>715\) after common denominators. Thus (7) proves (2) in this case.

We may now assume \(\Delta_0>1/128\). For either row, the endpoint cubic sum is also at least \(S_a^3/(4m_a^2)\), by applying convexity to its \(2m_a\) atoms. Activity and (3) therefore imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_a
&\le\frac{m_a+1}{2m_a^2\mu_a^3}K^2S_a^3
<\frac{2(m_a+1)}{\mu_a^3}
\le\frac{4m_a}{\mu_a^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
If \(m_0\le D\mu_0^2\), (10) and \(\mu_0>T\) would give \(\Delta_0<4D/T=1/128\), a contradiction. Hence
\(m_0>D\mu_0^2\).

Suppose next that \(m_1<D\mu_1^2\). Keep the high-mean endpoint group as one cubic group of capacity \(m_0\), with the coefficient \(1-3/(4\mu_0^2)\) from (3). Give the entire low-mean endpoint group its actual atom capacity \(2m_1\), and give each singleton capacity one. Lowering all these cubic bounds by the same coefficient and applying weighted Holder uses total capacity \(K+m_1\), and proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&\ge\left(1-\frac3{4\mu_0^2}\right)
       \frac{K^2}{(K+m_1)^2},\\
\delta&\le\frac3{4\mu_0^2}+\frac{2m_1}{K}
<\frac{11\mu_1^2}{4\mu_0^2},\\
d&<\frac{6\mu_1^2}{\mu_0^2}
<\frac6{Q\mu_0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
For the second line use \(1-(1+x)^{-2}\le2x\), \(K\ge m_0>D\mu_0^2\), \(m_1<D\mu_1^2\), and \(\mu_1\ge1\). The third line uses (5) and \(11/2<6\). Since \(\Delta_0>1/128\), (6) gives
$$
\lambda_0r_0
>\frac1{4\mu_0}\cdot\frac12\log(129/128)
>\frac1{2048\mu_0}
>\frac6{Q\mu_0}>d.
\tag{12}
$$
Indeed \((1/2)\log(129/128)>1/258>1/512\), and \(Q=2^{16}>6\cdot2048\). This proves (2) throughout the sparse-low-count case.

The only remaining case has both \(m_a\ge D\mu_a^2\), as well as \(\mu_0>T\). Define the cubic repair terms \(E_a=3K^2a_a^2S_a\). Collapsing each pair to its total probability changes its cubic sum by exactly \(3a_a^2s\). The collapsed pair totals and the singleton masses form exactly \(K\) cells of total probability one. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta&\le E_0+E_1,\\
\frac{E_a}{\Delta_a}
&=\frac{3S_a}{4m_a(m_a+1)a_a}
\le\frac{3\mu_a}{2m_a}
\le\frac{3}{2D\mu_a},\\
E_a&\le\frac3{4\mu_a^2}\frac{K^2S_a^3}{m_a^2}
<\frac3{\mu_a^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
For the last line, (3) bounds the endpoint cubic sum below by \((1-3/(4\mu_a^2))S_a^3/m_a^2\), its scaled value is less than \(A<1\), and \(1-3/(4\mu_a^2)\ge1/4\). These are algebraic groupings of the original probabilities, not changes of law or normalizer.

If \(\Delta_1>16\), the first lower bound in (6) gives
$$
r_1-d\ge\frac12\log[A(A+\Delta_1)]
>\frac12\log(65/16)>\log2>\frac12.
\tag{14}
$$
Here \(A\ge1/4\), the function \(A\mapsto A(A+\Delta_1)\) is increasing for positive \(A\), and \(\log2>1/2\) follows by integrating \(1/t\) from one to two. Thus (7) proves (2).

Finally assume \(\Delta_1\le16\). From (6), \(\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\), and (13),
$$
\lambda_1r_1
\ge\frac{\Delta_1}{136\mu_1}
\ge\frac{3\Delta_1}{512\mu_1}
\ge2E_1,
\tag{15}
$$
where \(512>3\cdot136\). If \(\Delta_0\le1\), the same lower bound gives
$$
\lambda_0r_0
\ge\frac{\Delta_0}{16\mu_0}
\ge\frac{3\Delta_0}{512\mu_0}
\ge2E_0.
\tag{16}
$$
If instead \(\Delta_0>1\), then (6), \(\log2>1/2\), (13), and \(\mu_0>T>96\) give
$$
\lambda_0r_0
>\frac1{16\mu_0}
>\frac6{\mu_0^2}>2E_0.
\tag{17}
$$
Equations (13), (15)-(17), and \(d<2\delta\) show \(\sum_a\lambda_ar_a\ge2(E_0+E_1)>d\). This completes the exhaustive split and proves (2).

For the last assertion, each coefficient \(B/(B+2\mu_a)\) increases with \(B\), while \(r_a>0\), so every larger \(B\) preserves the inequality. All estimates used the original cubic sum, the original two endpoint groups, and the original singleton masses. The exact forced-spine and count maps in the statement, supplied by a9f4cb83f6711543 and 4bb26a33ac78ee45, therefore transfer the conclusion without any relaxation or normalization change.
