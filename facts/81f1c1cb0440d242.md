## Statement

For a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, let $\mu_K$ be the measure obtained by summing $d$-dimensional volume on its integer fibers. Let $B$ be the standard $4$-simplex, of volume $V$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_4$, where $\lambda_1=x_1$. Put
$$
\alpha=\frac{17}{100},\qquad \beta=\frac{199999}{1000000},\qquad L=B\cap\{\alpha\le\lambda_1\le\beta\},\qquad
m=\frac{|L|}{V}=\left(\frac{83}{100}\right)^4-\left(\frac{800001}{1000000}\right)^4.
$$
Set $Q=m^{-1/4}$, $r=2-Q$, $A=rL$, $C_+=QL$, and $a=(2m^{1/4}-1)^4$. Let $K$ be the convex hull of the lifts of $A,B,C_+$ at levels $-1,0,1$, respectively. Its integer fibers are precisely $A,B,C_+$ with masses $aV,V,V$ and $0<a<10^{-8}$.

Define $v$ by $\lambda_1(v)=37/200$ and $\lambda_i(v)=163/800$ for $i\ne1$, and put $h=a^{1/4}$, $C'=2v-A$, and $B_h=B\cap\{\lambda_1\le1-h\}$. The convex hull $K'$ of the lifts of $A,B_h,C'$ at levels $-1,0,1$ is a full-dimensional subbody of $K$ with these exact integer fibers. Its mixed mass is $(1+a)V>(2+a)V/2$ and its mixed centroid is $(0,c)$, where
$$
\lambda_1(c)=\frac15-4e,\qquad \lambda_i(c)=\frac15+e\ (i\ne1),\qquad
e=\frac{(83/400-h/5)a}{1+a},\qquad \frac a5\le e\le\frac{83a}{400}.
$$

Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Every closed ambient halfspace containing $(0,c)$ has $\mu_{K'}$-mass at least $\Gamma_4(1+a)V$. The normalized depth of $(0,c)$ in $K'$ is at least $\Gamma_4$, and its normalized depth in $K$ is at least $\Gamma_4(1+a)/(2+a)>\Gamma_4/2$.

## Proof

**The bodies and centroid.** The cap $\{\lambda_1\ge t\}$ of $B$ is the copy of $B$ scaled by $1-t$ about vertex $1$, giving the slab volume by subtraction. Exact rational arithmetic gives
$$
m-\frac1{16}=\frac{2481161996159996799999}{10^{24}}>0,\qquad
\left(\frac{101}{200}\right)^4-m=\frac{56588628840003200001}{10^{24}}>0.
$$
Thus $200/101<Q<2$, $0<r<2/101$, $0<h=2m^{1/4}-1<1/100$, and $0<a<10^{-8}$. The endpoint volumes are $r^4mV=aV$ and $Q^4mV=V$. Since $r+Q=2$ and $L$ is convex, $(A+C_+)/2=L\subset B$. The extreme levels give the specified endpoints. At level zero any convex combination has equal weights on the two endpoints and is therefore a mixture of a point of $B$ and, when the endpoint weights are nonzero, a point of $L$. Thus the middle fiber is exactly $B$.

For $u\in L$, affineness gives $\lambda_i((2v-ru)/Q)=(2\lambda_i(v)-r\lambda_i(u))/Q$ for all five coordinates, since $(2-r)/Q=1$. For $i\ne1$ the numerator is at least $163/400-(2/101)(83/100)>0$. The two strict inequalities
$$
2(37/200-\alpha)>(2/101)(\beta-\alpha),\qquad
2(\beta-37/200)>(2/101)(\beta-\alpha)
$$
give $\alpha\le\lambda_1((2v-ru)/Q)\le\beta$. Hence $C'\subset C_+$. The lifted endpoint fibers of $K'$ are paired by the volume-preserving reflection $(z,x)\mapsto(-z,2v-x)$ and both have mass $aV$. Also $(A+C')/2\subset L\subset B_h$ since $\beta<1-h$. The same equal-weight argument at level zero gives the exact fibers of $K'$, and containment of each generating fiber yields $K'\subset K$. Both bodies are full-dimensional since their middle fibers have interior and they meet other levels.

The removed cap is, up to its null boundary, a simplex of mass $aV$, with barycentric centroid $1-4h/5$ at index $1$ and $h/5$ elsewhere by homothety about vertex $1$. Equal endpoint masses give base centroid zero and their continuous first moments sum to $2aVv$. The retained mass is $(1-a+2a)V$. For $i\ne1$, subtracting the removed first moment and adding the endpoint moments gives
$$
\lambda_i(c)=\frac{1/5-ah/5+2a(163/800)}{1+a}=\frac15+e.
$$
The coordinates sum to one, giving $\lambda_1(c)=1/5-4e$. Since $(83/400-1/500)/(1+10^{-8})>1/5$, the stated bounds on $e$ follow, in particular $e<a/4$. All coordinates are positive and $\lambda_1(c)<1-h$, so $c$ is interior to $B_h$.

**One strictly positive vertex.** Divide all cap masses by $V$. Move a containing halfspace's boundary parallel to itself through $(0,c)$, which can only decrease its mass. If the continuous normal is zero, it takes all of $B_h$, giving $1-a>\Gamma_4(1+a)$, since the decreasing gap is $36899999119/62500000000>0$ at $a=10^{-8}$. Otherwise the affine margin on $B$ is nonconstant and vanishes at the interior point $c$, so its vertex values include both strict signs.

If only vertex $i$ has positive margin, let $t_j\in(0,1]$ be the edge intercept fraction from $i$ to $j$ for $j\ne i$, with $t_j=1$ for a zero-margin vertex. Dividing by the positive margin at $i$ makes the trace $\{\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j/t_j\le1\}$, a simplex of relative volume $P=\prod_{j\ne i}t_j$. Put $b_j=\lambda_j(c)/t_j$. The through-point condition gives $\sum_{j\ne i}b_j=1$, so AM–GM and $\lambda_j(c)\ge1/5-a$ give
$$
P=\frac{\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(c)}{\prod_{j\ne i}b_j}
\ge4^4\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(c)\ge\Gamma_4(1-5a)^4\ge\Gamma_4(1-20a).
$$

If $i=1$, the complementary coordinates are at least $(1+a)/5$, so even subtracting the entire removed mass gives
$$
P-a\ge\Gamma_4(1+a)^4-a\ge\Gamma_4(1+a)+(3\Gamma_4-1)a>\Gamma_4(1+a),
$$
since $3\Gamma_4-1=143/625$. For $i\ne1$, if some $b_j$ lies outside $[6/25,13/50]$, fixing it at $t$ bounds the product of the four $b_j$ by $t((1-t)/3)^3$. This increases to $1/4$ and decreases thereafter. At the two box endpoints its ratios to $4^{-4}$ are $3511808/3515625$ and $10535824/10546875$, both below $999/1000$. Thus $P-a\ge(1000/999)\Gamma_4(1-20a)-a>\Gamma_4(1+a)$; the decreasing gap equals $25593999881/62437500000000>0$ at $10^{-8}$.

For $i\ne1$ and all $b_j\in[6/25,13/50]$, $t_1\le(1/5)/(6/25)=5/6<1-h$. The cap misses the removed region $\{\lambda_1>1-h\}$ and retains all of $P$. Put $R_1=(37/200)/(1/5-4e)$ and $R_o=(163/800)/(1/5+e)$; the small bounds on $a,e$ give $R_1<1<R_o$. Consequently
$$
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\lambda_j(v)}{t_j}
=b_1R_1+(1-b_1)R_o
\le\frac6{25}\frac{37/200}{1/5-10^{-8}}+\frac{19}{25}\frac{163/800}{1/5}
=\frac{79699996903}{79999996000}<1.
$$
Thus $(0,v)$ is in the ambient halfspace for every coefficient of $z$. The halfspace and its reflection about $(0,v)$ cover the two endpoints and have equal endpoint masses, giving at least $a$ in endpoint mass. With $t=5e\le83a/80<2a$,
$$
P/\Gamma_4\ge(1-4t)(1+t)^3=1-t-9t^2-11t^3-4t^4\ge1-\frac{21a}{20},
$$
since $9t^2+11t^3+4t^4\le36a^2+88a^3+64a^4\le a/80$ for $a\le10^{-8}$. The gap after adding the endpoints is at least $a(1-(41/20)\Gamma_4)=(501/3125)a>0$.

**At least two strictly positive vertices.** Use the following comparison on the original simplex $B$.

The barycentric coordinates of a uniform point in a $d$-simplex have the law of $E_i/\sum_j E_j$ for $d+1$ independent mean-one exponentials: changing variables $E_i=t\lambda_i$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$ gives the joint density $e^{-t}t^d$ in $t$ and the $d$ free coordinates. If the affine vertex margins have $k$ strictly positive values $p_i$ and $\nu$ strictly negative values $-q_j$, the simplex cap fraction is
$$
P=\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^k p_i E_i\ge\sum_{j=1}^{\nu} q_j F_j\right)
$$
with independent mean-one exponentials, dropping zero-margin vertices. Write $P_+=\sum_i p_i$, $P_-=\sum_j q_j$. When the through-point barycentric coordinates lie in $[l,u]$, the weighted margin balance implies $uP_+\ge lP_-$ and $P_+/P_-\ge l/u$. If $P_+\ge P_-$, reducing the positive coefficients by a common factor to balance the totals only shrinks the event. The balanced scalar bound of accepted fact `564e798ecec2b61f` gives $P\ge\Gamma_\nu$.

If $P_+<P_-$, scale the $k$ positive exponentials by $R=P_-/P_+>1$. This balances the two coefficient totals. The new joint density of the positive variables is $R^{-k}\exp(-\sum_i x_i/R)$ on the positive orthant, at least $R^{-k}$ times the original density. The unchanged independent negative variables preserve this dominance. The two full laws have common mass at least $R^{-k}$, so the probability of the same event differs by at most $1-R^{-k}$. The accepted balanced bound and Bernoulli's inequality give
$$
P\ge\Gamma_\nu-[1-(P_+/P_-)^k]\ge\Gamma_\nu-k(1-l/u).
$$

Here $k\le4$, $1\le\nu\le3$, and $l=1/5-a$, $u=1/5+a$ are valid. Since $\Gamma_\nu\ge\Gamma_3$ for $1\le\nu\le3$ and $1-l/u=10a/(1+5a)\le10a$, both cases give $P\ge\Gamma_3-40a$. Even after subtracting the entire removed middle mass, $\Gamma_3-41a>\Gamma_4(1+a)$: the decreasing difference equals $767161619/62500000000>0$ at $10^{-8}$.

These cases exhaust all traces and allow arbitrary base tilts. Nonzero continuous normals have volume-null boundaries on every fiber, while a purely base-normal cut through the point contains all of the middle fiber. The stated retained depth follows. Inclusion gives $\mu_K(H)\ge\mu_{K'}(H)$ for each cap; division by $(2+a)V$ proves the original normalized bound.
