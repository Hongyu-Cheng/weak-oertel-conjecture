## Statement

Let $\mu$ be original mixed volume on a compact convex body in $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, $n,d\ge1$. Fix a positive-volume integer fiber $K=K_j$ of volume $w$, and let $T$ be the maximum dominated mass at base mean $j$. For $0<t<T$ let $M_t(j)$ be the continuous centroids of dominated sources of mass $t$ and base mean $j$. For every $0<s<\min(t,w)$ this body contains a translate of
$$
\frac{(T-t)s}{t(T-s)}\left[1-(s/w)^{1/d}\right]K.
$$
Each point of the translate is represented by a dominated original source. In particular, for any Euclidean inball of $K$ of radius $r>0$, the same coefficient times $r$ is a lower bound on the inradius of $M_t(j)$.

## Proof

Apply the finite balanced-capacity theorem to the positive fiber volumes. Any feasible row of masses is realized by densities constant on the fibers, and every dominated source induces such a row. Thus the finite maximum is $T$ and is attained by a source $\sigma_T$. Write $g_T$ for its continuous mean, $g_K$ for the centroid of $K$, and $\theta=(s/w)^{1/d}$. For each $p\in K$ the homothet $(1-\theta)p+\theta K$ lies in $K$, has volume $s$, and has centroid $(1-\theta)p+\theta g_K$. Let $\sigma_p$ be its volume measure on the single fiber at $j$. Set $\lambda=(t-s)/(T-s)$ and $\nu=(T-t)/(T-s)$. The source $\lambda\sigma_T+\nu\sigma_p$ remains dominated because the weights sum to one, and it has mass $t$ and base first moment $tj$. Its continuous centroid is
$$
\frac{\lambda T}t g_T+\frac{\nu s}t\big[(1-\theta)p+\theta g_K\big].
$$
As $p$ ranges over $K$ these are exactly the indicated translate. Scaling the given inball by its positive coefficient proves the radius bound. The moment section is compact and convex by weak-* compactness of the dominated densities with closed moment constraints.
