## Statement

For integers \(m\ge2\), \(L\ge1\), \(D\ge0\), and \(a\in\mathbb R_{>0}^m\), define the full unit-multiplicity composition grid and its law by
$$
\mathcal K_L=\left\lbrace k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}k_j=L\right\rbrace,
\qquad
Z=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L}(a\cdot k)^D,
\qquad
\nu(k)=\frac{(a\cdot k)^D}{Z}.
$$
Let \(K\) have law \(\nu\), put \(\mu_i=\mathbb E K_i\) and \(F_i=\mathbb E[K_i(K_i-1)]\), and conditionally on \(K\) let \(U\) have Dirichlet parameters \((K_0+1,\ldots,K_{m-1}+1)\).

Take
$$
m=3002,\qquad L=2,\qquad D=10,
\qquad
a_0=20,\quad a_1=\cdots=a_{3000}=1,\quad a_{3001}=30.
$$
Then
$$
F_0>2\mu_0^2+\frac{2(\mu_0+1)^2}{L+m},
\qquad
\mathbb E U_0^2>2(\mathbb E U_0)^2.
$$
Here \(L\) is the composition total, unrelated to any adaptive-radius notation. The auxiliary count below is \(q=3000\), so no arithmetic \(N\) is repurposed. In the simplex entropy notation, the composition variables are exactly \(K_i=k_i(\mathbf z)=Lp_i(\mathbf z)\), with \(m=n+1\). An affine function with vertex values \(a_i\) satisfies \(\varphi(\mathbf z)=L^{-1}a\cdot K\); for \(D=d-1\), its normalized derivative weights are therefore exactly \(\nu\), since the factor \(L^{-D}\) cancels. This identification asserts no optimality of that affine function. The locked set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and its measure are not changed by this counterexample.

## Proof

We first establish the posterior representation and its precise moment equivalence, without a log-concavity premise. Write \(\mathsf t=L+m\) and
\(h_j=\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_L,\ k_i=j}(a\cdot k)^D\).
For nonnegative integers \(r,s\), integration by parts gives
\(\int_0^1 u^r(1-u)^s\,du=r!s!/(r+s+1)!\).
Iterating this identity over the simplex gives
$$
\int_{\substack{u_j\ge0\ (0\le j<m-1)\\\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}u_j\le1}}
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}u_j^{k_j}\,du_0\cdots du_{m-2}
=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}k_j!}{(L+m-1)!},
\qquad u_{m-1}=1-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}u_j.
$$
Indeed, fixing one coordinate at \(u\) and rescaling the remaining simplex by \(1-u\) produces the factor \((1-u)^{L-k_i+m-2}\); induction on \(m\), with the displayed one-dimensional identity as its base, proves the formula. Consequently the Dirichlet density proportional to \(\prod_j u_j^{k_j}\) has normalizer \((\mathsf t-1)!/\prod_jk_j!\). The same slicing calculation gives the conditional coordinate density
$$
f_{U_i\mid K=k}(u)
=\frac{(\mathsf t-1)!}{k_i!(\mathsf t-k_i-2)!}
u^{k_i}(1-u)^{\mathsf t-k_i-2}
=(\mathsf t-1)\binom{\mathsf t-2}{k_i}
u^{k_i}(1-u)^{\mathsf t-k_i-2},
\quad 0<u<1.
$$
All factorial arguments are nonnegative, because \(k_i\le L\) and \(m\ge2\). Averaging this density over the finite original law proves exactly
$$
\rho_i(u)=\frac{L+m-1}{Z}
\sum_{j=0}^L h_j\binom{L+m-2}{j}
u^j(1-u)^{L+m-2-j}.
$$
Ratios of the same Beta integrals give
\(\mathbb E[U_i\mid K]=(K_i+1)/\mathsf t\) and
\(\mathbb E[U_i^2\mid K]=(K_i+1)(K_i+2)/(\mathsf t(\mathsf t+1))\).
Thus
$$
\mathbb E U_i=\frac{\mu_i+1}{\mathsf t},
\qquad
\mathbb E U_i^2=\frac{F_i+4\mu_i+2}{\mathsf t(\mathsf t+1)}.
$$
Multiplying by positive denominators shows
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E U_i^2\le2(\mathbb E U_i)^2
&\iff \mathsf t(F_i+4\mu_i+2)\le2(\mathsf t+1)(\mu_i+1)^2\\
&\iff F_i\le2\mu_i^2+\frac{2(\mu_i+1)^2}{\mathsf t}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This proves the claimed exact equivalence for every legal law, independently of any joint or marginal log-concavity.

We now enumerate the entire support of the stated witness. Set \(q=3000\), the number of coefficients equal to one, and use \(i=0\). When \(k_0=0\), the two units both lie among these \(q\) coordinates, one lies there and one in coordinate \(3001\), or both lie in coordinate \(3001\). The numbers of compositions are respectively \(\binom{q+1}{2}\), \(q\), and \(1\), and their scores \(a\cdot k\) are respectively \(2\), \(31\), and \(60\). When \(k_0=1\), the remaining unit has \(q\) choices with score \(21\), or the single choice with score \(50\). When \(k_0=2\), there is one composition, with score \(40\). These cases are disjoint and exhaustive; their total count is
\(\binom{q+1}{2}+2q+3=\binom{q+3}{2}=4507503=|\mathcal K_2|\).
Every composition, including repeated units and boundary sites, is counted once. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
h_0&=\binom{3001}{2}2^{10}+3000\cdot31^{10}+60^{10}
    =3063546625551939000,\\
h_1&=3000\cdot21^{10}+50^{10}=147695892934603000,\\
h_2&=40^{10}=10485760000000000,\\
Z&=h_0+h_1+h_2=3221728278486542000.
\end{aligned}
$$
These evaluations use \(\binom{3001}{2}=4501500\), \(2^{10}=1024\), \(21^{10}=16679880978201\), \(31^{10}=819628286980801\), \(50^{10}=97656250000000000\), and \(60^{10}=604661760000000000\).

Write \(\mathsf M=h_1+2h_2=168667412934603000\), the unnormalized first moment. Since \(K_0\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), the normalized moments are exactly \(\mu_0=\mathsf M/Z\) and \(F_0=2h_2/Z\). Also \(\mathsf t=L+m=3004\). Hence the proposed inequality is equivalent to
\(\mathsf t h_2Z\le\mathsf t\mathsf M^2+(Z+\mathsf M)^2\).
For a shorter exact integer calculation, divide \(h_0,h_1,h_2,Z,\mathsf M\) by their common factor \(1000\) and denote the corresponding scaled normalizer, first-moment sum, and second section weight by
$$
\mathsf z=3\,221\,728\,278\,486\,542,
\qquad
\mathsf v=168667412934603,
\qquad
\mathsf b=10485760000000.
$$
No probabilities are changed by this common scaling. Direct integer multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
3004\,\mathsf b\mathsf z&=101481937618322820096327680000000,\\
3004\,\mathsf v^2&=85459883342899857698617721897436,\\
(\mathsf z+\mathsf v)^2&=11494782944407063867749753111025,\\
3004\,\mathsf b\mathsf z-3004\,\mathsf v^2-(\mathsf z+\mathsf v)^2
&=4527271331015898529960204991539>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the proposed inequality fails strictly on this legal positive coefficient vector. More explicitly, its left side minus its right side is
$$
F_0-2\mu_0^2-\frac{2(\mu_0+1)^2}{3004}
=\frac{2\cdot4527271331015898529960204991539}
{3004\,(3\,221\,728\,278\,486\,542)^2}>0.
$$
The posterior identities already proved then give the strict coordinate second-moment failure
$$
\mathbb E U_0^2-2(\mathbb E U_0)^2
=\frac{2\cdot4527271331015898529960204991539}
{3004^2\cdot3005\cdot(3\,221\,728\,278\,486\,542)^2}>0.
$$
All entries of \(a\) are positive integers, all original support multiplicities were retained, and the strict signs are exact integer comparisons.
