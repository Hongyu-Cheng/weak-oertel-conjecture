## Statement

Retain the canonical hard-branch objects and notation of accepted facts 036a276c54ec5e66, 3016926516cc39df, 3100bf8082ca09d5, and bf0d5c250b8f688c. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
 \qquad
 D<\frac Q{100},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(Q>0\) is the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir.

The positive transverse atlas labels of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 are
\[
 \sigma=(z;e,f),\qquad
 e=\{z,a(\sigma)\},\qquad
 f=\{z,b(\sigma)\},\qquad
 \theta_\sigma>0,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(a(\sigma)\ne b(\sigma)\) are the two opposite endpoints. Write
\[
 m_\sigma:=\frac{a(\sigma)+b(\sigma)}2,
 \qquad
 X_\sigma:=\frac{x_{e,a(\sigma)}+x_{f,b(\sigma)}}2,
 \qquad
 P:=\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma.
\tag{3}
\]
For every physical opposite endpoint \(y\in E_\perp\), accepted fact 3100bf8082ca09d5 gives the exact pooled weight
\[
 \tau_y=
 \sum_{\sigma:\ y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}
 \theta_\sigma.
\tag{4}
\]
The atlas also supplies source densities \(g_{\sigma,y}\), of mass \(\theta_\sigma\) and barycenter \(x_{e,y}\) or \(x_{f,y}\), such that for every subfamily and every physical source \(y\), their pointwise sum is at most \(1/2\).

Retain the endpoint-disjoint dyadic matching \(\mathcal M_2\), its weights
\[
 t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal M_2),
\tag{5}
\]
and the fixed primal missed-edge block \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}\) of accepted fact bf0d5c250b8f688c. Put
\[
 L_{\mathrm{miss}}:=
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}t_e,
 \qquad
 S_{\mathrm{miss}}:=
 \bigcup_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}e.
\tag{6}
\]
The accepted fixed-block estimate is
\[
 L_{\mathrm{miss}}>
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q.
\tag{7}
\]

Let
\[
 \mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 \{\sigma:a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\},
 \qquad
 \Theta_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{\mathrm{int}}>
 \left(\frac9{100}-\frac1{50\gamma_d}\right)Q
 >\frac7{200}Q.}
\tag{9}
\]

Every \(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\) lies in a unique dyadic edge, denoted \(e_2(y)\). Define
\[
 \alpha_y:=\frac{t_{e_2(y)}}{\tau_y},
 \qquad
 \eta_\sigma:=
 \theta_\sigma
 \min\{\alpha_{a(\sigma)},\alpha_{b(\sigma)}\}
 \quad(\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}),
\tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 H_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}}\eta_\sigma.
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\frac12<\alpha_y\leq1
 \quad(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}),}
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 H_{\mathrm{int}}>
 \frac12\Theta_{\mathrm{int}}>
 \frac7{400}Q,}
\tag{12}
\]
and the rescaled internal atlas obeys the exact downstream endpoint capacities
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
 \eta_\sigma
 \leq t_{e_2(y)}
 \quad(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}).}
\tag{13}
\]

For an internal label and one of its two opposite endpoints, set
\[
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y}:=
 \frac{\eta_\sigma}{\theta_\sigma}g_{\sigma,y}.
\tag{14}
\]
This density has mass \(\eta_\sigma\), has the same barycenter as \(g_{\sigma,y}\), and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y}
 \leq\frac12
 \quad\text{pointwise on }C_y.}
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 X_\sigma\in\mathsf M_{\eta_\sigma}(C_{m_\sigma}),
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)
 \geq\gamma_d\eta_\sigma.}
\tag{16}
\]

The single positive measure
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}}
 \eta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)}
\tag{17}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), including after coincident atoms are aggregated, and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\mathrm{int}}(K)\leq\mu_C(K)
 \quad\text{for every closed ambient halfspace }K,}
\tag{18}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 |\nu_{\mathrm{int}}|
 =2\gamma_dH_{\mathrm{int}}
 >\frac{7\gamma_d}{200}Q.}
\tag{19}
\]

Let
\[
 A_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 \operatorname{aff}
 \{m_\sigma:\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\},
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{int}}:=\dim A_{\mathrm{int}},
\tag{20}
\]
and define, equivalently before or after coincident atoms are aggregated,
\[
 r_{\mathrm{int}}:=
 \min_{s:A_{\mathrm{int}}\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{X_\sigma-s(m_\sigma):
 \sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 2\gamma_dH_{\mathrm{int}}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{int}}}}
 {r_{\mathrm{int}}+1},}
\tag{22}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathrm{int}}+1>
 \frac{7\gamma_d}{2}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{int}}},}
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \operatorname{supp}\nu_{\mathrm{int}}
 =\rho_{\mathrm{int}}+r_{\mathrm{int}}
 \geq n+1.}
\tag{24}
\]
In particular, the measure has at least \(n+2\) distinct actual atoms.

The conclusion does not assert that an internal label has opposite pair equal to a downstream dyadic edge, that the atlas points \(X_\sigma\) align with the downstream centroid field or stress directions, or that different internal atoms form one Cartesian packet. Thus (24) is a forced rank escape, not Canonical-CCE or HSF.

## Proof

Split all atlas labels into three classes according to whether their two opposite endpoints lie both in \(S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), one in \(S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), or both outside. Denote their total weights by
\[
 \Theta_{SS},\qquad
 \Theta_{S\bar S},\qquad
 \Theta_{\bar S\bar S},
\tag{25}
\]
respectively. Thus
\[
 P=\Theta_{SS}+\Theta_{S\bar S}
   +\Theta_{\bar S\bar S},
 \qquad
 \Theta_{SS}=\Theta_{\mathrm{int}}.
\tag{26}
\]
The exact pooling identity (4) counts each internal label twice and each crossing label once. Hence
\[
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}}\tau_y
 =2\Theta_{SS}+\Theta_{S\bar S}.
\tag{27}
\]
Subtracting (26) from (27) gives the exact cut identity
\[
 \Theta_{SS}
 =\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}}\tau_y-P
  +\Theta_{\bar S\bar S}
 \geq\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}}\tau_y-P.
\tag{28}
\]

The dyadic matching is endpoint-disjoint and \(t_e\leq\tau_y\) at both endpoints of every edge. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}}\tau_y
 \geq2L_{\mathrm{miss}}.
\tag{29}
\]
Accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 gives
\[
 \Omega=2P+|\lambda^\perp|,
 \qquad
 \Omega\leq Q,
 \qquad
 |\lambda^\perp|\geq0,
\tag{30}
\]
and hence \(P\leq Q/2\). Combining (7), (28), (29), and (30) yields
\[
 \Theta_{\mathrm{int}}>
 2\left(\frac{59}{200}
       -\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q
 -\frac Q2
 =\left(\frac9{100}
       -\frac1{50\gamma_d}\right)Q.
\tag{31}
\]

For completeness, \(\gamma_d>4/11\). Indeed,
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1}
\tag{32}
\]
by \(\log(1+x)<x\), while
\[
 e=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <\frac83+\frac1{24}
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{5^j}
 =\frac{87}{32}<\frac{11}{4}.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus \(1/(50\gamma_d)<11/200\). Substitution in (31) proves (9).

Fix \(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\). Its unique dyadic edge \(e_2(y)=\{u,w\}\) was formed inside one half-open dyadic bin in accepted fact 3016926516cc39df. Therefore
\[
 t_{e_2(y)}\leq\tau_y<2t_{e_2(y)},
\tag{34}
\]
which proves the first part of (12). Consequently every internal label satisfies
\[
 \eta_\sigma>\frac12\theta_\sigma.
\tag{35}
\]
Summing (35) and using (9) proves the second part of (12).

For a fixed \(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), equation (10) gives
\[
 \eta_\sigma\leq
 \alpha_y\theta_\sigma
 \quad
 \text{whenever }y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}.
\tag{36}
\]
Use (4), allowing all labels rather than only the internal ones, to obtain
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
 \eta_\sigma
 \leq\alpha_y\tau_y
 =t_{e_2(y)}.
\tag{37}
\]
This proves (13).

The scaling in (14) preserves the source barycenter and changes the mass from \(\theta_\sigma\) to \(\eta_\sigma\). Moreover, (36) and the simultaneous source bound of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 give, pointwise on \(C_y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y}
 &\leq
 \alpha_y
 \sum_{\sigma:\ y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}
 g_{\sigma,y}\\
 &\leq\frac{\alpha_y}{2}
 \leq\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
This proves (15).

Accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 gives
\[
 X_\sigma\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_\sigma}(C_{m_\sigma}),
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)
 \geq\gamma_d\theta_\sigma.
\tag{39}
\]
Scaling any representing target-fiber density by \(\eta_\sigma/\theta_\sigma\leq1\) preserves its barycenter and proves the membership in (16). The depth inequality in (39) is stronger than the one in (16), because \(\eta_\sigma\leq\theta_\sigma\).

The full atlas measure in accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 is
\[
 \nu_\perp=
 2\gamma_d\sum_\sigma
 \theta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)},
\tag{40}
\]
and is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\). Since every internal coefficient satisfies \(0<\eta_\sigma\leq\theta_\sigma\), equation (17) defines a positive submeasure of \(\nu_\perp\), including after coincident atoms are aggregated. Hence
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{int}}(K)
 \leq\nu_\perp(K)
 \leq\mu_C(K)
\tag{41}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(K\), proving (18). Its mass formula is immediate from (17), and (12) gives (19).

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 to \(\nu_{\mathrm{int}}\). Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (20)-(21), and the theorem gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{|\nu_{\mathrm{int}}|}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathrm{int}}}
  (r_{\mathrm{int}}+1)}.
\tag{42}
\]
Multiplying (42) by \(2^n\) and using the mass identity in (19) proves (22). Combining (1), (19), and (22) gives
\[
 \frac Q{100}>D>
 \frac{7\gamma_dQ}{200}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{int}}}}
 {r_{\mathrm{int}}+1},
\tag{43}
\]
which rearranges to (23).

For every integer \(r\geq0\), one has \(r+1\leq2^r\). Hence (23) and \(\gamma_d>4/11\) imply
\[
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{int}}+r_{\mathrm{int}}}
 \geq
 2^{\rho_{\mathrm{int}}}
 (r_{\mathrm{int}}+1)
 >\frac{7\gamma_d}{2}2^n
 >\frac{14}{11}2^n
 >2^n.
\tag{44}
\]
The exponent on the left is an integer, so it is at least \(n+1\). The intrinsic-rank identity in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 identifies this exponent with the affine dimension of the support, proving (24). A finite set of affine dimension at least \(n+1\) has at least \(n+2\) distinct points. This completes the proof.
