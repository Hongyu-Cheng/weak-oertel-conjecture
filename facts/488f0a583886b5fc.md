## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer. A globally extendable finite table means a nonzero, nonnegative, finitely supported table \(w=(w_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}\) with a finite-valued extension \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying \(f(\mathbf I)=w_{\mathbf I}\) at every lattice point, including every zero of the table, and \(f((1-t)x+tx')\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(x')^t\) for \(x,x'\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\), with zero on the right if either endpoint value is zero. Use the canonical positive support \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\) and total mass \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}>0\) from accepted facts `793210f8a4be3647` and `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. Set

\[
w_{\max}=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\mathbf I}},\qquad
B=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\phi_{\mathbf I},\qquad
P=\operatorname{conv}D.
\tag{1}
\]

All logarithms are natural. Then \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), \(\min_D\phi=0\), and \(\phi\) satisfies \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\) for every finite nonempty \(E\subseteq D\), every positive coefficient vector of sum one, and every \(\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\). Conversely, any finite nonempty lattice-convex \(D\), any such convex-admissible potential values, and any \(w_{\max}>0\) give a globally extendable table by \(w_{\mathbf I}=w_{\max}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\) and zero elsewhere.

The notation map to the accepted general realization `b563a1eb51a20cd2` is \(A=D\), its normalization \(M=w_{\max}\), its lattice heights \(h_z=\phi_z\), and its convex roof \(V=\widehat\phi\), defined below. Its auxiliary affine lattice chart, also denoted \(\phi(u)\) there, is not used here. In the archived logarithmic-height source the normalized concave heights are \(h_{\mathbf I}=-\phi_{\mathbf I}\), and their concave roof is \(-\widehat\phi\); before normalization, \(h_{\mathbf I}=\log w_{\max}-\phi_{\mathbf I}\). The source's positive support \(A\) is \(D\); its nonnegative amplitude called \(A\) in the entropy response is \(B\). The integer \(q\) below is the new continuous dimension of the constructed body. It replaces the original body's continuous dimension \(d\) only for that constructed body, and is unrelated to the thinning coefficients \(q_v\) in `03f071403cdfc69f` or the proof abbreviation \(q=1-\gamma_d\) in `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`.

Explicitly, the roof used in the construction is

\[
\widehat\phi(x)=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}:
\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\quad\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=x\right\}\qquad(x\in P).
\]

As in the accepted realization, it is attained, convex, continuous relative to all of \(P\), piecewise affine, and equal to \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\) at every \(\mathbf I\in D\).

For each \(\mathbf J\in D\), retain exactly the finite family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) of `793210f8a4be3647`: its members are nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), equipped with their unique positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J},
&\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)&=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:G_E\le L\},\\
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\max\left\{\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E\le w_{\mathbf I}
\ (\mathbf I\in D)\right\},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing\ \ (E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L))\right\},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L},
&\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here and below \(L\ge0\). Every source occurring with positive coefficient in a component of mass \(x_E\) is charged the full \(x_E\). All components share the capacities \(w_{\mathbf I}\), and the singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\) is the central mass. These are precisely the full-occurrence conventions of `793210f8a4be3647`; the symbol \(W\) always denotes total table weight. All extrema in (2) are attained, the cutoff maxima need only the finitely many values \(G_E\), and the same values result if arbitrary finite barycentric supports and positive coefficients are allowed. The source's fractional \(\mathcal B_{\log}(h)\) is \(\mathcal B_{\log}(w)\) here, and its potential-based integral score is \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\).

For an actual convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), with an integer \(d\ge1\), retain \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{y:(\mathbf I,y)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), \(D\), and \(W\) with the meanings above; empty or lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. The locked set remains \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), with \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Its canonical root cost is

\[
L_E=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}{
\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}.
\tag{3}
\]

Using the family \(\{E:L_E\le L\}\) instead of \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\) in (2) gives exactly \(P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\), \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\), \(\mathcal B_d(w)\), and \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\) of `793210f8a4be3647` and `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. A semicolon merely makes the table argument explicit. For every actual table with \(W>0\), at every center and cutoff,

\[
P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w),\qquad
m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w).
\tag{4}
\]

The following one approximation applies to all the scores. For every integer \(q>B\), there is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope \(C^{(q)}\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^q\) with complete integer fibers

\[
C^{(q)}_{\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\left[0,w_{\max}^{1/q}(1-\phi_{\mathbf I}/q)\right]^q,&\mathbf I\in D,\\
\varnothing,&\mathbf I\notin D.
\end{cases}
\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}^{(q)}=
\begin{cases}
w_{\max}(1-\phi_{\mathbf I}/q)^q,&\mathbf I\in D,\\
0,&\mathbf I\notin D.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

This is the cube-roof realization underlying `b563a1eb51a20cd2`, with isotropic vertical normalization and exact lattice-preserving transverse thickening when \(\dim P<n\). No integer coordinate is removed, and no lattice-index or determinant factor changes the weights. Rank zero is included. Define

\[
\kappa_q=1-B/q>0,\qquad
\varepsilon_q=-q\log(1-B/q)-B,
\qquad
0\le\varepsilon_q\le\frac{B^2}{2(q-B)}.
\tag{6}
\]

For every lattice index, \(e^{-\varepsilon_q}w_{\mathbf I}\le w_{\mathbf I}^{(q)}\le w_{\mathbf I}\); hence \(e^{-\varepsilon_q}W\le W^{(q)}\le W\), where \(W^{(q)}=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{(q)}\). For any finite barycentric component at any \(\mathbf J\in D\), not just an affinely independent one, write \(\overline\phi_\lambda=\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\) and \(G_\lambda=\overline\phi_\lambda-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). Its root cost in (5) obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_\lambda^{(q)}
&=q\log\frac{q-\phi_{\mathbf J}}{q-\overline\phi_\lambda},\\
G_\lambda
&\le L_\lambda^{(q)}
\le\min\{G_\lambda+\varepsilon_q,\ G_\lambda/\kappa_q\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, zero-cost components stay exactly at zero. Put \(t_q(L)=\min\{L+\varepsilon_q,L/\kappa_q\}\). Uniformly for every \(\mathbf J\in D\) and every \(L\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_q(\mathbf J,L;w^{(q)})&\le P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w),
&P_q(\mathbf J,t_q(L);w^{(q)})&\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w),\\
m_q(\mathbf J,L;w^{(q)})&\le m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w),
&m_q(\mathbf J,t_q(L);w^{(q)})&\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently, for each of the two corresponding pairs \((\mathcal B_q,\mathcal B_{\log})\) and \((\mathcal B_q^{\mathrm{hit}},\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}})\), writing that pair as \((\mathcal B_q^\ast,\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast)\) only in the following display,

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\varepsilon_q}\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w)
&\le\mathcal B_q^\ast(w^{(q)})\le\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w),\\
e^{-\varepsilon_q}\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w)}W
&\le\frac{\mathcal B_q^\ast(w^{(q)})}{W^{(q)}}
\le e^{\varepsilon_q}\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w)}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus both normalized root scores converge to their corresponding normalized logarithmic scores. The same inequalities hold if optimization is over the cutoffs at one prescribed center, because (8) is pointwise in the center.

For the curvature refinement, retain the exact notation of `03f071403cdfc69f`: for a fixed \(j\in D\), \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\), \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\), \(B_j^\Delta(L)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\), \(K_j(w)=\max_{L\ge0}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\), and \(K(w)=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w)\). The suppressed center in \(\alpha\) is always the displayed \(j\). Displacements are oriented and include zero. Give the superscripted symbols the same definitions for \(w^{(q)}\). For every \(j\in D\), \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), and \(L\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\varepsilon_q}\alpha(v)&\le\alpha^{(q)}(v)\le\alpha(v),
&0&\le\Delta_j^{(q)}(v)-\Delta_j(v)\le2\varepsilon_q,\\
B_j^{\Delta^{(q)}}(L;w^{(q)})&\le B_j^\Delta(L;w),

\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last two lines also hold with \(K_j\) at each fixed center. If the complete original table satisfies \(w_z=0\) outside \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\) and \(w_u\ge w_v\) whenever \(0\le u\le v\) coordinatewise, including comparisons involving zeros, then \(w^{(q)}\) satisfies these same conditions. Thus the infima of \(2^nK(w)/W\) for this monotone class agree between globally extendable tables and actual bodies, in every fixed integer dimension and after taking the infimum over integer dimensions. The unrestricted \(K\) optimal-constant equivalence was already accepted in `b563a1eb51a20cd2`; the new assertions here are the one-sided curvature and score comparisons and preservation of the monotone class. When \(B=0\), the realization is exact and all comparisons (7)-(10) are equalities.

There is also quantitative stability and rational sufficiency for the two logarithmic scores. Suppose \(\phi,\phi'\) are convex-admissible potentials on the same lattice-convex \(D\), \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\), \(w'_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi'_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\), and \(\max_D|\phi'-\phi|\le\tau\) for \(\tau\ge0\). For either logarithmic score, denoted \(\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast\) as in (9), this gives

&\le\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w')}{W'}
\le e^{2\tau}(1+2\tau)\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^\ast(w)}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}

Every normalized real convex-admissible potential on \(D\) is a uniform limit of normalized nonnegative rational convex-admissible potentials on that exact \(D\). For rational potential values and \(w_{\max}=1\), the bodies (5), including their transverse thickening, can be chosen to be rational polytopes. is then rational. The assertion concerns side lengths; the fiber volumes converge to the possibly nonconstant values \(e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\).

Precisely, for every fixed \(n\ge1\), the following two equalities hold separately:

\[
\begin{aligned}

Here \(w(C)\) is the body's actual complete integer fiber table and \(W(C)\) its total weight. ## Proof

The support and interpolation assertions at the start are the globally extendable-table conclusions of accepted fact `b563a1eb51a20cd2`, with the notation map stated above. Briefly, repeated application of the extension inequality gives \(f(\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I)\ge w_{\max}\exp(-\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I})>0\). Every lattice point of \(P\) must therefore lie in \(D\), since the extension agrees with all table zeros. At an integer barycenter \(\mathbf J\), taking logarithms gives the displayed convex-admissibility inequality. We give the roof and rational interpolation details needed below as well.

For any real values \(r_{\mathbf I}\) on the finite set \(D\), define on \(P\) the lower roof

\[
R(x)=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf I}:
\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\quad\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=x\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

For each \(x\), the feasible set is a nonempty closed subset of a finite probability simplex, so the minimum is attained. The roof lies between \(\min_D r\) and \(\max_D r\). Combining minimizing coefficient vectors for two points proves convexity. It is continuous and piecewise affine even at the relative boundary of \(P\). To check this and the rational assertion without an interpolation assumption, its epigraph is exactly the set of \((x,s)\) admitting nonnegative \(\lambda\) of sum one and barycenter \(x\), with \(s\ge\sum\lambda r\). Eliminate the finitely many coefficient variables from these linear inequalities, replacing equalities by two inequalities. At one elimination step, existence of a variable is equivalent to every lower bound for it being at most every upper bound, together with the inequalities independent of it. If a lower or upper class is empty, only the independent inequalities and the existing one-sided bounds need be accommodated, which is always possible. Repeating gives finitely many linear inequalities in \((x,s)\).

Every such valid inequality has nonpositive coefficient on \(s\), because the epigraph contains arbitrarily large \(s\) at each \(x\in P\). The inequalities with zero coefficient constrain only the domain. Each negative coefficient gives an affine lower bound on \(s\). There is at least one lower bound, since the roof is bounded below and the domain is nonempty. Consequently \(R\) is the maximum of finitely many affine functions on \(P\), proving continuity and the piecewise-affine assertion on its entire domain. If the values \(r_{\mathbf I}\) are rational, all coefficients in the initial system and throughout this finite elimination are rational, so these affine functions and the epigraph inequalities can be chosen rational.

Apply (17) to \(r=\phi\) and denote the result by \(\widehat\phi\). Convex-admissibility says every representation at \(\mathbf J\in D\) has objective at least \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\), while its singleton representation has that value. Hence \(\widehat\phi(\mathbf J)=\phi_{\mathbf J}\). The roof takes values in \([0,B]\). Conversely, starting with the admissible data stated before (2), define \(f=w_{\max}e^{-\widehat\phi}\) on \(P\) and zero elsewhere. Convexity of \(\widehat\phi\) proves its log-concavity when both endpoints lie in \(P\); otherwise the right side of the extension inequality is zero. Its values on every lattice point are precisely the prescribed table because \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). This proves the claimed equivalence of data descriptions.

We next justify the exact finite logarithmic formulation. Given any barycentric component supported on a finite \(E\subseteq D\), minimize \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\) over all its representations of \(\mathbf J\). Choose an optimizer with the smallest positive support \(E'\). If \(E'\) were affinely dependent, there would be a nonzero vector \(v\) on it with \(\sum v_{\mathbf I}=0\) and \(\sum v_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=0\). Both sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations of the optimizer along \(v\) are feasible. Minimality forces \(\sum v_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}=0\). Because \(v\) has both signs, moving in one direction until the first positive coordinate vanishes preserves feasibility and the objective but reduces support, a contradiction. Thus \(E'\) is affinely independent, has at most \(n+1\) sites, and belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\).

The new logarithmic cost is at most the old cost. Retaining the component's mass and replacing \(E\) by \(E'\subseteq E\) weakly decreases every full-occurrence source usage. Repeated indices may first be merged by adding their coefficients; zero coefficients are omitted, and identical reduced supports may be grouped. A reduced singleton is exactly central mass. This proves that the packing program (2) gives the maximum mass even over arbitrary finite components. For hitting, every low-cost arbitrary support contains a low-cost reduced support, so hitting all the displayed independent supports is equivalent to hitting all arbitrary low-cost barycentric supports.

The packing feasible region is nonempty and compact: each variable is bounded by the minimum of the finitely many positive capacities on its nonempty support. The hitting minimum ranges over finitely many subsets, with \(D\) always feasible. The singleton has cost zero, giving \(w_{\mathbf J}\le P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\le W\) and \(w_{\mathbf J}\le m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\le W\). At any cutoff, replace \(L\) by the largest value \(G_E\le L\); that value exists because of the singleton. The allowed family is unchanged and the denominator weakly decreases. There are finitely many targets and support costs, proving all attainment assertions for (2). These arguments are the logarithmic counterparts of the root-support reduction and finite-cutoff conventions in accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`.

Every actual volume table with positive total mass is globally extendable by accepted fact `b563a1eb51a20cd2`; this includes all zero-volume and boundary fibers. Accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647` supplies nonnegativity of every actual root cost (3). Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean gives

\[
d\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I},
\qquad 0\le L_E\le G_E.
\tag{18}
\]

Hence the root cutoff family contains the logarithmic one at the same center and cutoff. More allowed components weakly increase fractional packing, and more supports to hit weakly increase integral hitting. The capacities are identical in this comparison, which proves (4).

Here is the precise full-dimensional construction for (5), including rational thickening. Let \(r=\dim P\), fix \(\mathbf I_0\in D\), and put \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D-\mathbf I_0)\). Choose a rational linear map \(T:\mathbb R^{n-r}\to\mathbb R^n\) whose image complements \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\). Such a map exists: select \(r\) independent integer differences spanning \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) and complete them to a basis of \(\mathbb R^n\) with standard coordinate vectors. The added vectors are columns of \(T\). No claim that this basis is unimodular is needed.

There is a positive distance \(\rho=\operatorname{dist}(P,\mathbb Z^n\setminus D)>0\). If the distance were zero, a sequence of lattice points outside \(D\) with distances to the compact \(P\) tending to zero would be bounded. A subsequence would be a single lattice point in the closed set \(P\), contradicting \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). Choose a positive rational \(\delta_0\) so small that \(\|Tt\|<\rho\) for every \(t\in[-\delta_0,\delta_0]^{n-r}\). Then

\[
\bigl(P+T[-\delta_0,\delta_0]^{n-r}\bigr)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D.
\tag{19}
\]

Indeed, an extra integer point in that sum would be at distance less than \(\rho\) from \(P\). Every point of \(D\) is present with \(t=0\). The decomposition \(x+Tt\), with \(x\in\operatorname{aff}P\), is unique because the linear subspaces are complementary. Define

\[
C^{(q)}=\left\{(x+Tt,y):
x\in P,\ t\in[-\delta_0,\delta_0]^{n-r},\quad
0\le y_\ell\le w_{\max}^{1/q}\bigl(1-\widehat\phi(x)/q\bigr)
\ (1\le\ell\le q)\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]

The height is continuous, concave, and at least \(w_{\max}^{1/q}\kappa_q>0\). This proves convexity and compactness of (20). The relative interior of \(P\), the interior of the transverse box, and the open vertical cube \((0,w_{\max}^{1/q}\kappa_q)^q\) map to a nonempty open subset of \(\mathbb R^{n+q}\), proving full dimension. The piecewise-affine roof also makes (20) a polytope. If \(r=n\), there are no transverse coordinates and this is the original cube roof. If \(r=0\), then \(D=\{\mathbf I_0\}\), \(B=0\), and it is a small base box times a cube of volume \(w_{\max}\); one can choose \(T\) to be the identity and \(\delta_0<1/2\).

At a lattice base \(\mathbf I\in D\), uniqueness of the complementary decomposition forces \(x=\mathbf I\) and \(t=0\). Its fiber is exactly the cube in (5). Equation (19) makes every other integer fiber empty. This treats all boundary sites and proves the complete table, rather than a selected subtable. The map in (20) leaves the vertical coordinates unchanged, so it changes no vertical volume or source capacity. It does not replace the original lattice with the group generated by some support differences.

When \(w_{\max}=1\) and the potentials are rational, (17) gives rational affine inequalities for the roof. To verify rationality of (20) also in lower rank, write \(x=\mathbf I_0+Uu\), where the columns of \(U\) are the chosen independent integer differences. The square matrix with columns \(U,T\) is rational and invertible, and its inverse is rational, as follows from the determinant formula for matrix inversion. The polytope \(\{u:\mathbf I_0+Uu\in P\}\), the roof inequalities in these coordinates, the transverse box, and the vertical inequalities all have rational coefficients.
