## Statement

Let \(n_1,n_2,n_3\ge1\) be integers, put \(r=n_1+n_2+n_3\), and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional polytope affinely isomorphic to the Cartesian product of three simplices of dimensions \(n_1,n_2,n_3\). Suppose \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). No vertex-integrality or factorwise lattice-splitting hypothesis is imposed. For the complete finite support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\), including every boundary point, define the unnormalized exponential capacity by

\[
B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathbb R^r}Z(\boldsymbol\vartheta),
\qquad
Z(\boldsymbol\vartheta)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
e^{\boldsymbol\vartheta\cdot\mathbf I}.
\]

For each block \(b\in\{1,2,3\}\), let \(f_{b,0},\ldots,f_{b,n_b}\) be the affine slacks of its facets, nonnegative on \(P\) and normalized by \(f_{b,i}(\mathbf0)=1\). Let \(\beta_{b,i}>0\) be the barycentric coefficients satisfying \(\sum_{i=0}^{n_b}\beta_{b,i}f_{b,i}\equiv1\), so \(\sum_{i=0}^{n_b}\beta_{b,i}=1\). Label any maximal coefficient in each block by \(0\), and set

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{b,i}&=\frac{\beta_{b,i}}{\beta_{b,0}}
\quad(1\le i\le n_b),
&\mu_b&=\sum_{i=1}^{n_b}a_{b,i},\\
c_b&=\max_{1\le i\le n_b}a_{b,i},
&q_b&=2^{-1/c_b},
&Q&=q_1+q_2+q_3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(0<a_{b,i}\le1\), \(0<c_b\le1\), and \(0<q_b\le1/2\). The explicit tilt obtained by omitting the maximal-coefficient facet from each block is

\[
\boldsymbol\theta
=-(\log2)\sum_{b=1}^3\sum_{i=1}^{n_b}\nabla f_{b,i}.
\]

It satisfies \(B(P)\le Z(\boldsymbol\theta)<4^r\). More precisely,

\[
\frac{Z(\boldsymbol\theta)}{4^r}
\le
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\prod_{b=1}^3\left(1-\frac{q_b}{4}\right)<1,
& Q\le1,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle\frac{3391}{3456}<1,
& Q>1.
\end{cases}
\]

## Proof

Because \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), the barycentric coordinate functions of the three simplex factors, pulled back to \(P\), have positive values at \(\mathbf0\). Dividing each function by its value there constructs the stated \(f_{b,i}\), with \(\beta_{b,i}\) equal to that value. Their block sums give the required identities. Maximality of \(\beta_{b,0}\) gives all the coefficient bounds above. Consequently \(P,D,f,\beta,a,\mu,c,q,Q\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta\) satisfy exactly the hypotheses and normalizations of accepted fact \(\mathtt{b3e3bd3a844d633a}\).

Let all \(K_{b,i}\) be independent, with \(\mathbb P(K_{b,i}=k)=2^{-k-1}\) for \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), and put \(Y_b=\sum_{i=1}^{n_b}a_{b,i}K_{b,i}\), so \(\mathbb EY_b=\mu_b\). Apply the final scalar-tail statement of accepted fact \(\mathtt{2caddd7f62f0f071}\) separately to each block with the exact parameter map \(m=n_b\), \(a_i=a_{b,i}\), \(K_i=K_{b,i}\), \(Y=Y_b\), \(\mu=\mu_b\), and \(c=c_b\). Its hypotheses hold because \(n_b\ge1\), all coefficients lie in \((0,1]\), and the variables have its independent geometric law. Taking its parameter \(t=0\) and then \(t=1/c_b\) gives

\[
\mathbb P(Y_b\le\mu_b)\le\frac34,
\qquad
\mathbb P(Y_b>\mu_b+1)
\ge\frac{2^{-1/c_b}}4=\frac{q_b}{4}.
\]

The latter bound is stronger than the denominator-6 premise in \(\mathtt{b3e3bd3a844d633a}\). Using the unconditional global bound from that fact and the two inequalities just established gives, with \((Q-1)_+=\max\{Q-1,0\}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z(\boldsymbol\theta)}{4^r}
&\le\prod_{b=1}^3\mathbb P(Y_b\le\mu_b+1)
+(Q-1)_+\prod_{b=1}^3\mathbb P(Y_b\le\mu_b)\\
&\le\prod_{b=1}^3\left(1-\frac{q_b}{4}\right)
+(Q-1)_+\frac{27}{64}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(Q\le1\), the excess term vanishes. Every factor \(1-q_b/4\) lies in \([7/8,1)\), so the stated product bound is strictly below one, including when \(Q=1\).

If \(Q>1\), then \(Q\le3/2\) and \((Q-1)_+\le1/2\). Applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the three positive factors \(1-q_b/4\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z(\boldsymbol\theta)}{4^r}
&\le\left(1-\frac{Q}{12}\right)^3+\frac{27}{128}\\
&\le\left(\frac{11}{12}\right)^3+\frac{27}{128}
=\frac{3391}{3456}<1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The global bound in \(\mathtt{b3e3bd3a844d633a}\) applies to the complete support \(D\), with every boundary bin retained. Its cutoff events use \(\le\), exactly the complements of the strict-tail events supplied above, so equality at either cutoff is included. That fact assumes neither integral vertices nor a split ambient lattice. Finally, evaluating the infimum at this explicit \(\boldsymbol\theta\) gives \(B(P)\le Z(\boldsymbol\theta)\); no attainment of the infimum is required. This proves the theorem for every stated affine product.
