## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and, for \(y\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 C_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_y:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y),
 \qquad
 Z_+:=\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}.
\]
For \(y\in Z_+\), let
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 L_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y),
 \qquad
 D_y:=L_y-c_y,
\]
where, for every positive-volume convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and every \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
  0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
  \int_Kf(x)\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\]

Retain the exact diagonal endpoint objects of accepted fact `f025d631bd2f3444`.  Thus \(I\subseteq Z_+\) is finite and, for every \(y\in I\), there are numbers \(\pi_y,\alpha_y\) and a compact convex set \(K'_y\) satisfying
\[
 0<\pi_y\leq\frac{a_y}{8},
 \qquad
 \frac12\leq\alpha_y\leq1,
 \qquad
 K'_y=c_y+\alpha_yD_y,
\]
and every \(D_y\) is a full-dimensional compact convex origin-symmetric body.  For \(z\in\mathbb R^d\), write
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}(z,K'_y)
 :=\inf_{x\in K'_y}\|z-x\|_{\alpha_yD_y}.
\]
If \(I\neq\varnothing\), define
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}I\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{y\in I}\pi_y
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}\bigl(s(y),K'_y\bigr),
\]
and, if \(I=\varnothing\), put \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}:=0\).  Put
\[
 \Pi:=\sum_{y\in I}\pi_y,
 \qquad
 \delta_*:=\frac{-7\log(7/8)}{\log2}-1.
\]

For completeness, use the complete-core capacity of accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` with the following exact normalization.  If \(\varnothing\neq E\subseteq Z_+\), \(0<t_y\leq a_y\) for \(y\in E\), and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), put
\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{
  \lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
  \sum_{y\in E}\lambda_y=1,
  \quad
  \sum_{y\in E}\lambda_yy=m
 \right\}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{y\in E}\lambda_y\mathsf M_{t_y}(C_y).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) is the supremum of \(\sum_{y\in E}t_y\) over all such data for which \(\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\neq\varnothing\) and
\[
 \sum_{y\in E\cap J}t_y
 \geq2^{-n}\sum_{y\in E}t_y
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\); the supremum of the empty family is zero.

Let
\[
 \mu_C(H):=
 \sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_y:(y,x)\in H\},
\]
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
where the infimum is over ambient closed halfspaces.  Then the minimum defining \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\) is attained, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
 \geq
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+
 \geq
 (\Pi-3\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}})_+ .
 }
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+
 \geq
 \gamma_d(\Pi-3\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}})_+ .
 }
\]
Although accepted fact `f025d631bd2f3444` also gives \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\geq\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\), no occurrence of \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\) in these lower bounds may be replaced by \(\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) on the basis of that comparison.

## Proof

We first record attainment.  Assume \(I\neq\varnothing\), and consider the finite product space
\[
 \mathcal E:=(\mathbb R^d)^I
\]
and its linear subspace
\[
 \mathcal L:=
 \{(s(y))_{y\in I}:s:\operatorname{aff}I\to\mathbb R^d
 \text{ is affine}\}.
\]
The space \(\mathcal L\) is a finite-dimensional linear subspace of \(\mathcal E\), hence is closed.  Since every \(\alpha_yD_y\) is a full-dimensional compact convex origin-symmetric body, its gauge is a norm.  Since \(K'_y\) is compact, the function
\[
 z\longmapsto
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}(z,K'_y)
\]
is continuous and tends to infinity as \(\|z\|_2\to\infty\).  All \(\pi_y\) are strictly positive, so
\[
 (z_y)_{y\in I}\longmapsto
 \sum_{y\in I}\pi_y
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}(z_y,K'_y)
\]
is continuous and coercive on \(\mathcal E\).  Its restriction to the closed set \(\mathcal L\) therefore attains a minimum.  By the definition of \(\mathcal L\), a minimizing evaluation vector is induced by an affine map on \(\operatorname{aff}I\).  This also reproves the attainment assertion supplied for these exact objects by accepted fact `f025d631bd2f3444`.  Fix such an attaining affine map \(s\).  For each fixed \(y\), compactness of \(K'_y\) also shows that the infimum in the corresponding distance is attained.

Next compute the nesting constant exactly.  With
\[
 \psi(r):=-\frac{1-r}{r}\log(1-r),
\]
one has
\[
 1+\delta_*
 =\frac{-7\log(7/8)}{\log2}
 =\frac{\psi(1/8)}{\psi(1/2)}.
\]
Accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b`, applied to each positive-volume fiber \(C_y\) with \(p=1/8\) and \(q=1/2\), therefore gives
\[
 c_y+(1+\delta_*)D_y
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{a_y/8}(C_y).
 \tag{1}
\]
The same accepted fact proves this inclusion for every fiber dimension and without any boundary or smoothness hypothesis beyond convexity.

We will also use metronoid monotonicity in the direction in which decreasing the selected mass enlarges the metronoid.  Namely, if \(0<m\leq t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\) and \(x\in\mathsf M_t(K)\) is represented by a density \(f\), then \(g:=(m/t)f\) satisfies \(0\leq g\leq1\), has integral \(m\), and has the same normalized first moment as \(f\).  Hence
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_m(K)
 \qquad(0<m\leq t).
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, because \(0<\pi_y\leq a_y/8\),
\[
 \mathsf M_{a_y/8}(C_y)
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y).
 \tag{3}
\]

The numerical margin is strictly larger than \(1/3\).  Indeed, because \(\log2>0\),
\[
 \delta_*>\frac13
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 21\log\frac87>\log16
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left(\frac87\right)^{21}>16.
\]
The last inequality follows without numerical approximation from the positive
binomial expansion:
\[
 \left(1+\frac17\right)^{21}
 >\sum_{k=0}^{6}\binom{21}{k}7^{-k}
 =\frac{1920968}{117649}
 >16.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\delta_*>1/3>0\).

For \(y\in I\), set
\[
 e_y:=
 \operatorname{dist}_{\alpha_yD_y}\bigl(s(y),K'_y\bigr),
\]
and retain the low-distance set
\[
 E:=\{y\in I:e_y\leq\delta_*\},
 \qquad
 T:=\sum_{y\in E}\pi_y.
\]
Weighted Markov gives
\[
 \sum_{y\in I\setminus E}\pi_y
 \leq\frac1{\delta_*}
 \sum_{y\in I\setminus E}\pi_ye_y
 \leq\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 T\geq
 \Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*},
 \qquad
 T\geq
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+.
 \tag{5}
\]

Fix \(y\in E\).  Distance attainment supplies \(k_y\in K'_y\) with
\[
 \|s(y)-k_y\|_{\alpha_yD_y}=e_y\leq\delta_*.
\]
Since \(K'_y=c_y+\alpha_yD_y\), convexity of \(D_y\) and \(\alpha_y\leq1\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s(y)
 &\in K'_y+\delta_*\alpha_yD_y\\
 &=c_y+(1+\delta_*)\alpha_yD_y\\
 &\subseteq c_y+(1+\delta_*)D_y.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining this with (1) and then with the correctly directed monotonicity (3) yields
\[
 s(y)\in\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y)
 \qquad(y\in E).
 \tag{6}
\]

We now prove complete-core admissibility.  If \(T=0\), then, because every \(\pi_y>0\), the retained set \(E\) is empty.  Equation (5) then forces
\[
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+=0,
\]
and the first desired lower bound follows from \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq0\).  Thus assume \(T>0\), so \(E\neq\varnothing\).

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the nonzero finite Borel measure
\[
 \nu:=\sum_{y\in E}\pi_y\delta_y
\]
supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\).  It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), and in particular every proper one, satisfies
\[
 \sum_{y\in E\cap J}\pi_y=\nu(J)\geq2^{-n}T.
 \tag{7}
\]
Moreover \(m\in\operatorname{conv}E\).  Otherwise strict separation of \(m\) from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}E\) would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E\), contradicting (7) because \(T>0\).

The original minimizer is affine on \(\operatorname{aff}I\), and therefore its restriction to \(\operatorname{aff}E\) is affine.  To match literally the ambient-domain affine special case in accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`, one may extend this restriction to all of \(\mathbb R^n\): choose \(y_0\in E\), extend the linear part of \(s-s(y_0)\) from \(\operatorname{span}(E-y_0)\) to \(\mathbb R^n\), and add back \(s(y_0)\).  Denote the resulting affine extension by \(\widetilde s\).  It satisfies \(\widetilde s(y)=s(y)\) for every \(y\in E\).

For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), affineness and (6) give
\[
 \widetilde s(m)
 =\sum_{y\in E}\lambda_y\widetilde s(y)
 \in\sum_{y\in E}\lambda_y\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \widetilde s(m)
 \in
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{y\in E}\lambda_y\mathsf M_{\pi_y}(C_y)
 =\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_\pi),
 \tag{8}
\]
so this complete core is nonempty.  Every selected mass is strictly positive and satisfies \(\pi_y\leq a_y/8\leq a_y\); equation (7) is exactly the required weighted base-centerpoint condition.  Thus \((E,(\pi_y)_{y\in E},m)\) is admissible in the definition of \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\), and
\[
 \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
 \geq T
 \geq
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+.
 \tag{9}
\]

If \(I=\varnothing\), the conventions in accepted fact `f025d631bd2f3444` give \(\Pi=\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}=0\), while the complete-core capacity is nonnegative by definition.  Hence (9) also holds in the empty case.  This covers both the empty diagonal family and the possibly zero retained mass without defining any zero-mass metronoid.

Accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` proves the complete-core master bound
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) and applying (9) gives
\[
 2^nh_{\max}(C)
 \geq\gamma_d
 \left(\Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}\right)_+.
 \tag{10}
\]

Finally, (4) gives \(1/\delta_*<3\).  Since \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\geq0\),
\[
 \Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}
 \geq\Pi-3\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}.
\]
The positive-part map is nondecreasing, which proves the second inequality in each displayed chain.

Accepted fact `f025d631bd2f3444` proves
\(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\geq\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\).  Because the defect is subtracted in (9)-(10), this comparison has the opposite direction from what would be needed to replace \(\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}\) by \(\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\): it gives
\[
 \Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}}{\delta_*}
 \leq
 \Pi-\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\delta_*}.
\]
Thus the proof makes no such replacement and supplies no bound on a large diagonal defect.  In particular, it does not prove the locked weak Oertel statement.
