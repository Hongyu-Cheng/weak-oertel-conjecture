## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, and let each $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a compact convex body of positive $d$-volume equal to the $p$-section of $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$. Mark the fiber boundary area sum $\eta$ by the unique regular outward continuous unit normal $u$, using endpoint counting if $d=1$. Let $\nu=f\eta$ be finite and nonnegative and in equal-mass physical convex order below $\eta$. Take any physical martingale coupling $\Pi$ from $\nu$ to $\eta$ and write $w=(p,x)$ and $w'$ for its input and output. Such a coupling exists and preserves the mark.

Write $r=\dim\operatorname{aff}P$. At each regular input one may select a supporting equation $a(\theta)\cdot z+u\cdot X=b(\theta)$ of $C$ through $w$, with $\theta=(j,u)$ where $j$ indexes an affine interpolant through $r+1$ affinely independent bases at their $u$-support heights. The coefficients are bounded and the selection is Borel. Let $\kappa$ be the source label marginal of $\Pi$, and $\Pi_\theta$ its conditional probabilities with physical marginals $\nu_\theta,\eta_\theta$. For $\kappa$-almost every $\theta$, both points lie in the same selected exposed face, the conditional martingale property holds, and $W_\theta=\eta_\theta-\nu_\theta$ has zero mass and first moments. In particular
$$
Q_\theta=\int ww^{\mathsf T}\,dW_\theta(w)=\int(w'-w)(w'-w)^{\mathsf T}\,d\Pi_\theta(w,w')\succeq0,\qquad
Q_\theta\binom{a(\theta)}{u}=0 .
$$
Writing $B_\theta=Q_\theta^{px}$ gives $Q_\theta^{pp}a+B_\theta u=0$ and $B_\theta^{\mathsf T}a+Q_\theta^{xx}u=0$. The labeled target law is $\eta(dw)\Omega_w(d\theta)$ for a probability kernel induced by $\Pi$, while the labeled source law is $\nu(dw)\delta_{\theta(w)}(d\theta)$.

## Proof

The stated family is actual and both measures are supported on regular boundaries as required in bf891d8f55d6abf7. That fact gives existence under equal-mass convex order and normal preservation for every physical martingale coupling. The compact fiber boundaries have finite area and a Borel regular normal. For example, a convex body has local Lipschitz boundary charts, so its exceptional nonregular points are surface-null, and the normal on the regular set is relatively continuous by closedness of the unit normal graph. For intervals these statements hold at both endpoints.

Write $h_p(u)=\max_{x\in K_p}u\cdot x$. The largest $\sum_q\alpha_q h_q(u)$ over $\alpha_q\ge0$ with $\sum_q\alpha_q=1$ and $\sum_q\alpha_q q=p$ equals $h_p(u)$. The singleton at $p$ gives one direction, and the opposite direction follows by choosing a maximizing point in each used fiber and using that their convex combination lies in the actual fiber at $p$. The dual of this finite linear program therefore has an affine majorant of all the $h_q(u)$ that touches at $p$. In the space of affine functions on $\operatorname{aff}P$, the dual feasible polyhedron has no line because the evaluations on $P$ span the dual space. Its nonempty optimal face has a vertex. One can see this by starting at a minimizer, moving in a direction annihilating all active evaluations when their rank is deficient, and choosing the sign to encounter a new constraint. Such a direction also annihilates the objective evaluation at $p$, since both signs are feasible for small steps at a minimizer. Some inactive evaluation varies because $P$ spans, and the first new constraint increases the rank. Repeating gives $r+1$ independent active evaluations. Thus a touching majorant is one of finitely many affine interpolants through bases with those evaluations.

Write these interpolants as $b_j(u)-a_j(u)\cdot z$, extending their linear parts by zero orthogonal to the direction space of the affine hull. Each coefficient is a fixed linear combination of finitely many continuous support heights, hence is continuous and bounded on the unit sphere. Choose the first interpolant majorizing all sampled heights and touching at the input base. The tests are finite inequalities and equalities of continuous functions, which give a Borel selection. In rank zero this reduces to the one constant interpolant. Every point of $C$ obeys the resulting affine inequality, and the regular input belongs to the exposed face.

The output plane gap is nonnegative. Since the input gap vanishes, its integral is zero by testing the coordinatewise martingale identities with the bounded Borel coefficients of the source label. Hence it vanishes almost everywhere. Disintegrate by the source label on the finite index set times the sphere. To retain the martingale property conditional on the label, localize the input tests with bounded label tests and first take a countable determining family of continuous physical input tests on the compact support. After discarding one $\kappa$-null set, each coordinate signed input measure for the displacement integrates every continuous test to zero by uniform density, so vanishes for every Borel test. The source and target both lie in the selected closed face for almost every label by the almost-everywhere gap identity. With $\delta=w'-w$, expand $w'w'^{\mathsf T}-ww^{\mathsf T}$ into $\delta\delta^{\mathsf T}+w\delta^{\mathsf T}+\delta w^{\mathsf T}$. The last two integrals vanish under each conditional martingale; the first is positive semidefinite and kills $(a,u)$ by tangency of every increment. The conditional fields are Borel versions and bounded on the compact physical set. Disintegrating the pushed-forward law of $(w',\theta)$ by the target point gives the kernel with physical marginal $\eta$. The law of $(w,\theta)$ uses the deterministic selector. Both disintegrations are of finite Borel measures on standard Borel spaces.

For a curved example take $P=\{-1,0,1\}$, $d=2$, $K_p=pt+r_0B_2$ for $t\ne0$ and $r_0>0$. The hull is exactly $-1\le z\le1,\ x-zt\in r_0B_2$, by interpolating between endpoint disks with a common translated disk point. At each normal $u$ put source density three on the central circle and zero on the others. Send $(0,r_0u)$ equally to $(p,pt+r_0u)$ for the three bases. The target is ordinary boundary area on every circle, each having element $r_0\,d\varphi$ in its angular coordinate $\varphi$. This is a physical martingale and preserves $u$. All support heights are $r_0+p(u\cdot t)$, so every interpolant is that affine function and the first index is fixed. Its exposed face is exactly a line segment, with plane coefficients $a=-u\cdot t$, $b=r_0$. Here $d\kappa=3r_0\,d\varphi$ and $Q_\theta=(2/3)(1,t)(1,t)^{\mathsf T}$ because the increments are $p(1,t)$. This matrix is nonzero on every label.
