## Statement

Let a planar compact convex body have exactly $N$ nonempty sampled integer fibers, at consecutive sites. Suppose all have positive length, with affine midpoint heights and affine widths. Use the sum of fiber lengths as mass, and closed ambient halfspaces for depth. Subtract the affine center graph, reverse the base when needed, and for a nonconstant width profile divide by its positive increment. Write the resulting lengths as $w_i=q+i$ for $0\le i<N$, with $q>0$. At base $j$ the midpoint attains the largest raw depth in its interval, namely
$$
D_j(q)=\min\left\{\sum_{i<j}w_i+w_j/2,\ \sum_{i>j}w_i+w_j/2,\ T_{j,k}(q)\ (k\ne j)\right\},
$$
where every sum uses $0,\ldots,N-1$ and
$$
T_{j,k}(q)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{i<k}\min\{q+i,(k-i)(q+j)/(2(k-j))\},&k>j,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i>k}\min\{q+i,(i-k)(q+j)/(2(j-k))\},&k<j.
\end{cases}
$$
Each $D_j$ is concave and piecewise affine with rational coefficients. The normalized maximum $\max_j D_j/[Nq+N(N-1)/2]$ is piecewise linear-fractional with finitely many rational breakpoints and is monotone or constant on each piece. For constant positive widths the normalized maximum is $1/2$ for odd $N$ and $(N-1)/(2N)$ for even $N$.

## Proof

The center shear preserves all closed halfspaces and fiber lengths. Common continuous scaling multiplies all raw masses by one constant. After centering, reflection in $x=0$ preserves the sampled measure. Every halfspace containing a convex combination of two points contains at least one of them, so depth at the midpoint of a reflected pair is at least their common depth. It suffices to evaluate all fiber midpoints.

Translate each containing halfspace inward through the midpoint. In one nonvertical orientation its mass at $(j,0)$ is $\sum_i [w_i/2+v(i-j)]_0^{w_i}$, where $[u]_0^w=\min(w,\max(0,u))$. Reflection gives the other orientation. The two far slope tails keep the full fibers on one side plus half of the anchoring fiber. The corresponding purely base cap keeps its full anchoring fiber and has no smaller mass.

By the finite interval slope formula, between successive knots the mass is affine. At a knot where a trace changes between empty and proper the slope jumps up, and at a knot where a trace changes between full and proper it jumps down. In the absence of an empty event a knot therefore has a strictly downward net jump, which cannot start an interior minimum or a flat minimum plateau. Choose the leftmost minimizing knot unless an outer constant region already minimizes. This yields a tail or an empty event. At the event for fiber $k\ne j$, $v=-(q+k)/(2(k-j))$, and the unclipped length at $i$ equals $(k-i)(q+j)/(2(k-j))$. Clipping gives the two displayed sums.

Every term of $T_{j,k}$ is the minimum of two affine functions, and finite minima of concave functions are concave. All coefficients are rational. The pointwise maximum of the $D_j$ still has finitely many affine pieces with rational endpoints when pieces change, as intersections of affine functions with rational coefficients occur at rational parameters unless they coincide. The ratio of each affine piece with $Nq+N(N-1)/2$ has a derivative of constant sign or vanishes identically, giving the stated behavior.

For common width $w$, set $m=\min(j,N-1-j)$. The centered block of bases $j-m,\ldots,j+m$ is centrally symmetric about $(j,0)$, so any closed containing halfspace contains at least half its mass, $(2m+1)w/2$. The steep oblique cap keeping the shorter side and half the anchoring fiber attains this value. Maximizing over $j$ and dividing by $Nw$ gives both parity cases.
