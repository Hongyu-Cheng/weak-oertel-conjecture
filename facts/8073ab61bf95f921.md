## Statement

In every $d\ge2$, small opposite translates of any centered $d$-simplex realize simultaneous proper varying captured-side Brunn–Minkowski equality with whole-body equality in all $d+1$ balanced barycentric directions through its centroid.

Two planar nonsimplex families with endpoint weights $1/2$ and exact whole-body equality show separate limitations. First take $A=[0,1]^2$, $B=A+(1/10,0)$, $K=(A+B)/2$ and caps $x+y\ge3/2$, $x-y\ge1/2$ through the common middle mark $(1,1/2)$. They have independent normals without positive balance. Both directions have proper unequal endpoint fractions and exact cap equality, with areas $(1/8,9/50,121/800)$ on $A,B,K$.

Second take $D=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(-1,1),(-2,0),(-1,-1)\}$, $A=D+(0,1/10)$, $B=D-(0,1/10)$, $K=D$. Through zero take normals $(1,1/4),(1,-1/4),(-1,0)$, positively balanced with weights $(1,1,2)$. All cuts are proper and satisfy cap equality. The first two directions have varying endpoint fractions and the same apex, while the third has constant fractions.

## Proof

For the simplex, let $\ell_i$ be the linear parts of the barycentric functions on the centered simplex $D$. They sum to zero. Choose a sufficiently small vector $v$ off the finitely many hyperplanes $\ell_i(v)=0$ and use $D\pm v$ and midpoint $D$. In the direction $\ell_i$ the two normalized thresholds are $\pm\ell_i(v)$. In ordinary barycentric coordinates their corresponding levels lie strictly between zero and one, so both caps are proper cones at vertex $i$ and the fractions vary. The one-direction classification gives cap equality, and the midpoint is $D$ since the shifts cancel.

For the square, the midpoint is $A+(1/20,0)$ and $(1,1/2)$ is interior to it. The two cut regions are right triangles at vertices of each translated square, with leg lengths $1/2,3/5,11/20$ on $A,B,K$, respectively. Hence the areas are as stated and their square roots satisfy midpoint equality. The two normals $(1,1),(1,-1)$ are independent, and the first coordinate rules out a positive balance.

For the quadrilateral, zero is interior, all translates have area $3$ by the shoelace formula, and the midpoint is $D$. In each of the first two directions the maximal height is one at $(1,0)$ and the other vertices have height at most $-3/4$. The two normalized thresholds are $\pm1/40$ in either order. The larger cap is an actual cone at $(1,0)$, so the cuts are proper and the one-direction classification gives exact cap equality. The middle cap triangle at height zero has area $32/63$. For instance its two edge parameters from the apex on the edges ending at $(-1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$ are $4/7$ and $4/9$, and the triangle on the three vertices has area two. Hence the endpoint areas are $(32/63)(1\pm1/40)^2$. In the third direction the translation does not change the level, so the cap equality also holds with constant fractions. The area there is $5/2$ because the complementary triangle $x>0$ has area $1/2$. The four vertices remain distinct extreme points.

For the constant-fraction case alone, take any convex body $D$ with $0$ interior, choose concentric positive dilates about zero, and use cuts through zero. Their normalized thresholds are all zero, so the equal-threshold branch gives every corresponding cap equality even if $D$ is a nonsimplex.
