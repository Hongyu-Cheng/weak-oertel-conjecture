## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) and \(T\ge0\) be integers, let \(\beta\ge0\) be real, and let \(B_i\ge2\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) be integers for \(i\in[k]:=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\). Define
$$
g_i(0)=0,\qquad g_i(1)=1,\qquad g_i(2)=B_i,\qquad g_i(3)=C_{4,i},
\qquad
\Gamma(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i).
$$
For every integer \(E\ge0\), define the complete height tail
$$
f_E=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&E\le T,\\
0,&E>T,
\end{cases}
$$
and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_4&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},
&Z_3&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},\\
b&=\min_{i\in[k]}B_i,
&P&=\lbrace i\in[k]:B_i=b\rbrace,\\
m&=|P|\ge1,
&R&=\lfloor T/b\rfloor.
\end{aligned}
$$
The meanings of \(g_i,\Gamma,T,\beta,f_E,Z_4,Z_3,R\) are exactly those in accepted fact `9f6ebd76c13f58a8`, with its \(B_{\min}\) renamed \(b\). Then
$$
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le\exp\!\left(\frac{38R^2}{m}\right).
\tag{1}
$$
For the full original law
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T^{(4)}&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}&=e^{-\beta t}/Z_4\qquad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_T^{(4)}),
\end{aligned}
$$
retain the original ambient-halfspace definitions: every halfspace is a closed affine halfspace of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero normal, and
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T^{(4)}\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T^{(4)}}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
$$
With these original atoms, heights, halfspaces, and full normalizer, one has
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\exp(-152R^2/m)}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$
In particular, for every real \(A\ge0\),
$$
R\le A\sqrt m
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{38A^2},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-152A^2}}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$
The accepted three-site input is `cd9532b1d59c5977`, with its \(k,T,\beta,B_i\) identical to the present data, its \(Z\) equal to the present \(Z_3\), and its ambient space and halfspace family unchanged. The locked \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) retain their locked meanings; no symbol here redefines them.

## Proof

Call a spatial vector feasible when its energy is at most \(T\), and weight it by its actual complete height tail \(f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\). The zero vector gives \(Z_3\ge f_0>0\). For all nonnegative integer energies \(E'\le E\),
$$
f_E\le f_{E'}.
\tag{4}
$$
Thus energy-decreasing exchanges compare the entire original height tails, without requiring equal energies within a count class. This includes \(\beta=0\) and every atom at height \(T\).

We use the elementary weighted graph comparison from `9f6ebd76c13f58a8`, with the vertex weights mapped literally to these same \(f_{\Gamma}\). For completeness, if every edge from a source \(\mathbf x\) to a target \(\mathbf y\) satisfies \(w(\mathbf x)\le w(\mathbf y)\), every source has at least \(D>0\) distinct targets, and every target has at most \(I\) sources, then
$$
D\sum_{\mathbf x}w(\mathbf x)
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y}w(\mathbf x)
\le\sum_{\mathbf x\sim\mathbf y}w(\mathbf y)
\le I\sum_{\mathbf y}w(\mathbf y).
\tag{5}
$$
No uniformity of the vertex weights is assumed.

Every feasible spatial vector has at most \(R\) coordinates in \(\lbrace2,3\rbrace\), since each such coordinate costs at least \(b\). Clipping each three to two decreases energy because \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge B_i\). A feasible ternary target with \(q\) twos has at most \(2^q\) four-site preimages, obtained by independently replacing any subset of those twos by threes. Every preimage has weight at most the target's weight by (4), and \(q\le R\). Therefore
$$
Z_4\le2^R Z_3.
\tag{6}
$$
If \(R=0\), no feasible vector contains a two or a three, so \(Z_4=Z_3\), proving (1) in that case.

Suppose now that
$$
1\le R\le m/16.
\tag{7}
$$
For each integer \(\ell\ge1\), let \(Z_\ell^{\mathrm{hi}}\) and \(Z_\ell^{\mathrm{lo}}\) denote the masses of feasible sources with exactly \(\ell\) threes and, respectively, at least \(m/4\) or fewer than \(m/4\) ones inside \(P\). Only \(\ell\le R\) can occur. In the following graphs every target is ternary and feasible.

First consider a high-pool-one source \(\mathbf x\), and let
$$
a=|\lbrace i\in P:x_i=1\rbrace|\ge m/4.
$$
For each \(\ell\)-element subset \(J\) of these pool one-coordinates, change all \(\ell\) threes to twos and all ones in \(J\) to twos. This gives \(\binom a\ell\) distinct targets. The energy change is at most
$$
\ell(b-1)-\sum_{i:x_i=3}(C_{4,i}-B_i)
\le\ell(b-1)-\sum_{i:x_i=3}(B_i-1)
\le0,
\tag{8}
$$
because every \(B_i\ge b\). Thus all pool ones are eligible regardless of the positions and costs of the threes, and every edge has the weight comparison required in (5).

For a target with \(q\) twos, an inverse source is specified uniquely by two disjoint \(\ell\)-element subsets of its two-coordinates: the positions formerly equal to three and the positions formerly equal to one. The second subset must in fact lie in \(P\), but ignoring that restriction gives the upper bound
$$
\binom q\ell\binom{q-\ell}\ell
\le\frac{R^{2\ell}}{(\ell!)^2}
\tag{9}
$$
when \(q\ge2\ell\); when \(q<2\ell\) the indegree is zero. There is no matching or pairing factor, because the two subsets determine every source coordinate. By (7) and \(\ell\le R\), the outdegree satisfies
$$
\binom a\ell
\ge\frac{(a-\ell+1)^\ell}{\ell!}
\ge\frac{(3m/16)^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{10}
$$
Applying (5) with all feasible ternary vectors allowed as targets gives
$$
Z_\ell^{\mathrm{hi}}
\le\frac1{\ell!}\left(\frac{16R^2}{3m}\right)^\ell Z_3
\le\frac1{\ell!}\left(\frac{6R^2}{m}\right)^\ell Z_3.
\tag{11}
$$

For low-pool-one sources, fix the common integer
$$
h=\min\!\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac{m}{8R}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace.
\tag{12}
$$
Here \(h\ge1\), since (7) gives \(m/(8R)\ge2\). For fixed \(\ell\le R\), partition these sources by the three integers
$$
s=|\lbrace i\in[k]:x_i=2\rbrace|,
\qquad
a=|\lbrace i\in P:x_i=1\rbrace|,
\qquad
z=|\lbrace i\in P:x_i=0\rbrace|.
\tag{13}
$$
Crucially, \(s\) counts twos globally, whereas \(a,z\) count only pool coordinates. The number of threes inside \(P\) is not fixed. In any nonempty such class,
$$
a<m/4,\qquad s+\ell\le R,\qquad
m-a-z=|\lbrace i\in P:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|\le R.
$$
Consequently
$$
z>11m/16,
\qquad
h\ell\le m/8.
\tag{14}
$$
For each \(h\ell\)-element subset \(J\) of the pool zero-coordinates, change all \(\ell\) threes to twos and the zeros in \(J\) to ones. This produces exactly \(\binom z{h\ell}\) distinct targets. Since \(h\le b-1\),
$$
\Gamma(\mathbf y)-\Gamma(\mathbf x)
=h\ell-\sum_{i:x_i=3}(C_{4,i}-B_i)
\le h\ell-\ell(b-1)\le0.
\tag{15}
$$
The targets therefore remain feasible and have at least the source weight. Their three relevant counts are exactly
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\lbrace i\in[k]:y_i=2\rbrace|&=s+\ell,\\
|\lbrace i\in P:y_i=1\rbrace|&=a+h\ell,\\
|\lbrace i\in P:y_i=0\rbrace|&=z-h\ell.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
The equalities hold regardless of how many source threes lie in \(P\): clipping a three inside the pool leaves that coordinate in \(\lbrace2,3\rbrace\), so it changes neither pool one-count nor pool zero-count.

Let \(\mathcal Y_{\ell,s,a,z}\) be the entire feasible ternary class with counts (16), and let its mass be the sum of its individual \(f_{\Gamma(\mathbf y)}\). A target in this class has at most
$$
\binom{s+\ell}\ell\binom{a+h\ell}{h\ell}
\tag{17}
$$
inverse sources: choose the \(\ell\) target twos formerly equal to three, then the \(h\ell\) pool target ones formerly equal to zero. Those choices uniquely determine the source. Invalid choices can only lower this indegree. By (5), the mass of the fixed source class is at most the mass of \(\mathcal Y_{\ell,s,a,z}\) times
$$
\binom{s+\ell}\ell
\frac{\binom{a+h\ell}{h\ell}}{\binom z{h\ell}}.
\tag{18}
$$

For every nonempty source class put
$$
\theta=\frac{a+h\ell}{z-h\ell+1}.
$$
All denominators below are positive by (14), and the product formula gives
$$
\frac{\binom{a+h\ell}{h\ell}}{\binom z{h\ell}}
=\prod_{r=0}^{h\ell-1}\frac{a+h\ell-r}{z-r}
\le\theta^{h\ell}.
\tag{19}
$$
The count bounds give
$$
\theta\le\frac{3m/8}{9m/16}=\frac23.
\tag{20}
$$
There is also a bound using the source's actual energy. Since each of its \(a\) pool ones costs one,
$$
a\le\Gamma(\mathbf x)\le T<b(R+1).
$$
Together with \(h\le b-1\), \(\ell\le R\), and \(R\ge1\), this gives \(a+h\ell\le3bR\). By (14), therefore,
$$
\theta\le\frac{16bR}{3m}\le\frac{6bR}{m}.
\tag{21}
$$

We claim
$$
\theta^h\le\frac{32R}{m}.
\tag{22}
$$
If \(h=b-1\), set \(u=R/m>0\). Equations (20)-(21) imply
$$
\theta^h\le\min\lbrace2/3,6bu\rbrace^{b-1}\le12u.
\tag{23}
$$
To check the last inequality, for \(u\ge1/(9b)\), division of \((2/3)^{b-1}\) by \(u\) is at most \(9b(2/3)^{b-1}\). For \(0<u\le1/(9b)\), the expression \((6bu)^{b-1}/u\) is nondecreasing in \(u\), because \(b\ge2\), and at the endpoint has that same value. For integers \(b\ge2\), \(9b(2/3)^{b-1}\le12\): its value at \(b=2\) is \(12\), and the ratio of the value at \(b+1\) to the value at \(b\) is \(2(b+1)/(3b)\le1\).

If \(h=\lfloor m/(8R)\rfloor\), then \(m/(8R)\ge2\) implies \(h\ge m/(16R)\). The binomial expansion, or Bernoulli's inequality for the positive integer \(h\), yields \((3/2)^h\ge1+h/2\ge h/2\). Thus
$$
\theta^h\le(2/3)^h\le2/h\le32R/m.
\tag{24}
$$
At least one of these two cases always holds by (12), including equality between its two entries. This proves (22).

Using \(s+\ell\le R\), equations (18)-(19) and (22) show that every class multiplier is at most
$$
\frac{R^\ell}{\ell!}(\theta^h)^\ell
\le\frac1{\ell!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{m}\right)^\ell.
\tag{25}
$$
For fixed \(\ell\) and the common \(h\), the target classes in (16) are disjoint as \((s,a,z)\) varies. Indeed, a target's global two-count, pool one-count, and pool zero-count recover the source counts uniquely by subtracting \(\ell\), subtracting \(h\ell\), and adding \(h\ell\), respectively. This is an injective map of count triples; it makes no assumption about the number of source threes inside the pool. Source classes with no sources contribute zero, and empty target classes have zero mass. Summing (18) over the nonempty source classes, using disjointness and (25), gives
$$
Z_\ell^{\mathrm{lo}}
\le\frac1{\ell!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{m}\right)^\ell Z_3.
\tag{26}
$$
Targets may be used again for a different value of \(\ell\), which is harmless because (26) is a separate estimate for each \(\ell\). Within each such estimate every target class is counted at most once.

The sources with no threes contribute exactly \(Z_3\). Letting \(x=R^2/m\), summing (11) and (26) over all positive integers \(\ell\), and extending each nonnegative series if necessary, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}
&\le1+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{(6x)^\ell}{\ell!}
+\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac{(32x)^\ell}{\ell!}\\
&=e^{6x}+e^{32x}-1
\le e^{38x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The last inequality is equivalent to \((e^{6x}-1)(e^{32x}-1)\ge0\). This proves (1) under (7).

If instead \(R>m/16\), the universal clipping estimate (6) gives
$$
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{(\log2)R}
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{16(\log2)R^2}{m}\right)
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{38R^2}{m}\right).
\tag{28}
$$
Together with the \(R=0\) case and (27), this proves (1) for every \(m\ge1\) and every rank \(R\), including \(R\ge k\). No assumption on the distribution of the other costs has been imposed.

For the moment consequence, apply accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977` to the exact restriction
$$
A_T^{(3)}=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace
\subseteq A_T^{(4)}.
$$
Its probability law, denoted here by \(p^{(3)}\), has probabilities \(e^{-\beta t}/Z_3\), and its accepted original ambient moment bound is
$$
M_3(p^{(3)})\ge\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{29}
$$
The parameter and normalizer map in the statement identifies every object used in (29); the halfspaces for both laws are the same closed affine halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). Put \(\alpha=Z_3/Z_4\). For every original atom \(\mathbf z\in A_T^{(3)}\), one has \(p_{\mathbf z}=\alpha p^{(3)}_{\mathbf z}\). For every ambient halfspace \(H\),
$$
\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T^{(4)}\cap H}p_{\mathbf v}
\ge\alpha\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T^{(3)}\cap H}p^{(3)}_{\mathbf v}.
$$
Taking infima over the same halfspaces containing \(\mathbf z\) gives \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge\alpha\tau_{p^{(3)}}(\mathbf z)\). Summing only over these original atoms yields
$$
M_3(p)
\ge\alpha^4 M_3(p^{(3)})
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{30}
$$
There are exactly four normalizer comparison factors: one from sampling the atom and three from its depth. In particular the law on the left always uses the full original \(Z_4\). Equation (1) gives \(\alpha^4\ge\exp(-152R^2/m)\), proving (2), and substituting \(R^2/m\le A^2\) proves (3).

All exchanges and counts above use integer energies and weak feasibility inequalities. Cost ties create no ambiguity in the fixed pool \(P\), and energy ties preserve (4). No distinctness or upper bound on the \(B_i\) or \(C_{4,i}\) is used. When \(T=0\), \(R=0\) and both original supports are the singleton \(\lbrace(\mathbf0,0)\rbrace\), so (1)-(2) hold directly as well. The proof of (4), the weighted graph argument, and the accepted three-site bound all include \(\beta=0\). These observations cover all stated boundary cases.
