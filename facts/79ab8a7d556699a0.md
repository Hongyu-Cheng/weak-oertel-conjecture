## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer and let $A,K,B$ be compact convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interiors and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Write $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$, using $d$-volume. Suppose all three volumes are at most $M/2$ and both endpoint volumes are at least $h|K|$, where $0\le h\le1$. Let $B_d\in(1,2)$ be the unique solution of $B_d^d-(2-B_d)^d=1$, let $y_d=(2-B_d)^d$, and put $L(h)=\max\{2-B_d,h^{1/d}\}$. Then the exact maximum of $M/|K|$ under these conditions is
$$
1+L(h)^d+(2-L(h))^d.
$$
It is attained by compatible homothetic bodies with relative roots $(L(h),1,2-L(h))$. At $h=0$ the maximum is $2(1+y_d)<1+\sqrt5$, and for $d=1$ it is $3$ for every $h$.

## Proof

The continuous function $x^d-(2-x)^d$ is strictly increasing on $[1,2]$ with endpoint values $0$ and $2^d$, so $B_d$ exists and is unique. Divide all volume roots by $|K|^{1/d}$ and exchange endpoints if necessary so that the resulting endpoint roots are $a\le b$. Brunn–Minkowski and the midpoint inclusion give $a+b\le2$. If $b\le1$, then $a^d+b^d\le2\le L(h)^d+(2-L(h))^d$ by power convexity. If $b\ge1$, then $b^d\le1+a^d\le1+(2-b)^d$ by nonheaviness and $b\le2-h^{1/d}$ by the smaller endpoint floor. Thus $b\le2-L(h)$. The function $x^d+(2-x)^d$ is nondecreasing on $[1,2]$, so $a^d+b^d\le (2-b)^d+b^d\le L(h)^d+(2-L(h))^d$.

Take one volume-one convex body $K$ containing the origin in its interior and set $A=L(h)K$, $B=(2-L(h))K$. The midpoint is exactly $K$. Both endpoint floors hold, and the larger endpoint is nonheavy because $2-L(h)\le B_d$. The smaller endpoint is nonheavy because it is no larger than the larger one. The middle is nonheavy because the sum of endpoint powers is at least $2$. These are actual sections at $-1,0,1$ in the hull of their lifts. Any convex combination at base zero has equal endpoint coefficients, so its continuous point is a mixture of a point of $K$ and a point of $(A+B)/2$.

For $h=0$, the larger endpoint power is $B_d^d=1+y_d$. Since $B_d(2-B_d)=1-(B_d-1)^2<1$, we have $y_d(1+y_d)=[B_d(2-B_d)]^d<1$. Hence $2(1+y_d)<1+\sqrt5$. In dimension one the endpoint power sum at the maximizing triple is $2$.
