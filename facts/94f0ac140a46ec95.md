## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $d\ge2$, and let $B\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a compact convex body whose complete integer sample $P=B\cap\mathbb Z^n$ contains $0$ and is centrally symmetric. Let $r^0:B\to(0,\infty)$ be continuous and concave with $r^0(z)=r^0(-z)$ at the sampled bases. Put $L=\operatorname{span}P$, $v_0=(-1,\ldots,-1)$, $v_i=v_0+(d+1)e_i$ for $1\le i\le d$, $T_d=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}$, $V_d=(d+1)^d/d!$, and $c_d=d^d/d!$. For positive continuous concave $r$ on $B$ write $r_z=r(z)$ and take $C_r=\{(z,x):z\in B,\ x\in r(z)T_d\}$ with actual mixed set $S_r=C_r\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. Use summed fiber $d$-volume $\mu_r$, and let $h_r(y)$ be its infimum over closed ambient halfspaces containing $y$. Work in the validated neighborhood of the even sample from the all-normal radial simplex theorem. Its unique deepest point is $Y=(0,0)$, of depth $\tau(r)=F_r(\beta(r))$, where $F_r(\beta)=\sum_z(d r_z+\beta\cdot z)^d/d!$ and $\beta(r)$ is its unique small critical coefficient in $L$. Put $R_0=\sum_z(r_z^0)^d$ and $Q_0=c_dR_0$. At each nonzero base $z_0\in P$ take any attaining cap through $(z_0,0)$. Its orbit under the full simplex vertex-permutation group has the same raw base coefficient $\alpha$ and equal actual mass $h_r(z_0,0)<\tau(r)$, with $\alpha\cdot z_0>0$. Its nonzero continuous normals sum to zero and span $\mathbb R^d$. The orbit covers the entire continuous space at its own base. Assign it to $z_0$ and assign the canonical minimizing caps at $Y$ to base zero, giving a finite complete cover with strict slack at each nonzero base. Baseline auxiliary caps can be kept fixed for nearby coverage and slack, or reselected to remain critical. In dimension two the order-three cyclic orbit suffices and is a full-rank positive circuit of three caps.

## Proof

The all-normal radial simplex theorem gives an attained nonvertical centroid cap, a strict gap at $z_0$, and the minimizing caps at $Y$. Write the auxiliary cap as $u\cdot x+\alpha\cdot(z-z_0)\ge0$. A linear vertex permutation acts on the continuous normals by the inverse transpose, preserves every fiber and its volume, and leaves the base coefficient unchanged. Thus every orbit member is attaining and has the same mass. This cap excludes $Y$ because its mass is strictly below $h_r(Y)$. Evaluating its margin at $Y$ gives $\alpha\cdot z_0>0$.

The vertex-height map $u\mapsto (u\cdot v_i)_i$ identifies continuous normals with the $d$-dimensional zero-sum coordinate subspace. The permutation orbit averages to zero. Its span contains a nonzero multiple of a coordinate difference by subtracting a transposed array with two unequal heights, and then contains all coordinate differences by permutation. The actual distinct orbit members have equal multiplicities in the group enumeration, so their sum is zero as well. In dimension two, cyclically permuting a nonzero zero-sum triple gives three distinct arrays summing to zero. Their span has dimension two, since an invariant real line for a map of order three would be fixed pointwise, and the zero-sum subspace has no nonzero fixed vector. This gives a positive circuit in the plane.

Above $z_0$ the margins sum to zero, so at least one is nonnegative at every continuous point. At base zero the canonical normals likewise sum to zero. Assigning each family only its own base covers all original fibers. With fixed baseline auxiliary caps, incidences and coverage remain unchanged as the body moves on the fixed base set. The uniform mass variation bound from the radial simplex theorem, together with continuity of $\tau$, keeps all such budgets strictly slack. Current criticality follows by selecting an attained minimum again. If different groups contain the same plane, the shared cap has the same single actual mass wherever it occurs.
