## Statement

Let $K=\operatorname{conv}(P_0,\ldots,P_d)$ be a $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$, with interior source $y$ of barycentric coordinates $\lambda_j$. For $v\ne0$ write $t_j=v\cdot(P_j-y)$. Consider decompositions $v=\sum_r v^{(r)}$ into finitely many normals through the same source, each with exactly one strictly positive vertex value $h_r$ and all other vertex values nonpositive. Let $Q^{(r)}$ be its cap volume divided by $|K|$ and $S^{(r)}$ its section $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ divided by $|K|\|v^{(r)}\|$. Then
$$
\min_{\{v^{(r)}\}}\sum_r \frac{Q^{(r)}}{S^{(r)}}=\frac1d\sum_j(t_j)_+.
$$
Zero margins are allowed in the minimum, and the lower bound still holds if all other margins are required to be strictly negative. Positive mixture coefficients can be absorbed into the normals.

## Proof

Each cap is a pyramid from its unique strictly captured vertex to the full cut section, at height $h_r/\|v^{(r)}\|$. Its ratio is $h_r/d$ by the pyramid formula, also for an interval with a counting-measure cut point. Applying the positive-part triangle inequality at each vertex gives the lower bound.

Let $I=\{i:t_i>0\}$ and $P=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_it_i>0$. For each $i\in I$ take a vector of vertex values with value $t_i$ at $i$, value $(\lambda_i t_i/P)t_j$ at vertices with $t_j<0$, and zero at all other vertices. It has barycentric mean zero because $\sum_j\lambda_jt_j=0$. The unique affine functional with these values vanishes at $y$, so it is $v^{(i)}\cdot(x-y)$ for an actual normal. Their sum has all the original vertex values and hence is the original functional. The unique positive margins sum to $\sum_j(t_j)_+$, so the lower bound is attained. Multiplying a normal by a positive coefficient multiplies its margin and the ratio by that coefficient.
