## Statement

For every integer \(N\ge2\), put \(m=2^N-1\), \(b_i=2^{i-1}\), and \(r=0\). For a real number \(q\in(0,1)\), let

\[
D=\left\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N:
\sum_{i=1}^N2^{i-1}z_i\equiv0\pmod{2^N-1}\right\},
\qquad
w_z=\begin{cases}q^{\sum_i z_i},&z\in D,\\0,&z\notin D,\end{cases}
\qquad W=\sum_{z\in D}w_z.
\]

This is the registered notation with \(a_i=-\log q\) for every \(i\). For \(J\in D\), retain the full original score \(S_J=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^N}\min\{w_{J+v},w_{J-v}\}\), with both orientations and zero included. Then \(0<W<\infty\), and the maximum of \(S_J\) over \(J\in D\) is attained.

Write \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb Z^N\). For every integer \(\ell\ge1\) and every real \(s\) satisfying \(q<s<1\), define \(\theta=2(2\ell+1)q^{3\ell}(1-qs)\). The original center \(J=3\ell\mathbf1\) belongs to \(D\) and satisfies

\[
\frac{S_{3\ell\mathbf1}}{2^{-N}W}
\ge
\frac{1-q/s}{1+q}\,
\frac{\theta^N}{2\ell N+1}.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, whenever \(\theta>1\), (1) supplies a positive bound independent of \(N\), and its right side tends to infinity as \(N\to\infty\). For the specified family \(q=9/10\), take \(\ell=3\) and \(s=903/1000\). For every \(N\ge2\), the fixed original center \(J=9\mathbf1\) satisfies

\[
\frac{\max_{J\in D}S_J}{2^{-N}W}
\ge \frac{S_{9\mathbf1}}{2^{-N}W}
\ge \frac{1}{600(6N+1)}
\left(\frac{203}{200}\right)^N
\ge \frac1{240000}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently the maximum normalized score in this entire cyclic family tends to infinity, rather than to zero.

## Proof

First verify the whole-lattice chart. Let \(P\) be the cyclic predecessor map, so \((Pk)_i=k_{i-1}\), with \(k_0=k_N\), and set \(F=2I-P\). The identity \(P^N=I\) gives

\[
F^{-1}=\frac1{2^N-1}
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}2^{N-1-j}P^j,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^N2^{i-1}(Fk)_i=(2^N-1)k_N,
\qquad
\sum_i(Fk)_i=\sum_i k_i.
\tag{3}
\]

The inverse formula follows by multiplying the finite geometric sum by \(2I-P\); its coefficients are nonnegative. The middle identity follows by subtracting the two sums involving \(k_i\) and \(k_{i-1}\), leaving only \((2^N-1)k_N\). If an integer vector \(z\) satisfies the congruence in the definition of \(D\), its inverse under \(F\) is integral: begin with \(k_N=(\sum_i2^{i-1}z_i)/(2^N-1)\), and successively solve \(k_{i-1}=2k_i-z_i\) for \(i=N,N-1,\ldots,2\). The remaining equation \(2k_1-k_N=z_1\) follows from the middle identity of (3), applied to the vector so constructed. Thus \(F\) maps \(\mathbb Z^N\) bijectively onto the full congruence lattice, including its vectors with negative coordinates.

If additionally \(z\ge0\), the nonnegative inverse in (3) gives \(k\ge0\). Conversely, \(Fk\ge0\) is precisely the collection of inequalities \(k_{i-1}\le2k_i\). Therefore the complete original support is in bijection with

\[
K_N=\{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N:k_{i-1}\le2k_i\text{ for }1\le i\le N\},
\qquad
W=\sum_{k\in K_N}q^{\sum_i k_i}.
\tag{4}
\]

All these sums converge, since the original support is a subset of the nonnegative orthant and \(W\le(1-q)^{-N}\); also \(W\ge w_0=1\).

We next bound the full infinite sum in (4). Index matrices by nonnegative integers \(x,y\), and define \(T_{xy}=q^y\mathbf1_{\{x\le2y\}}\). Expanding a diagonal entry of \(T^N\) sums over paths whose final index equals their initial index. Its product of entries is exactly \(q^{\sum_i k_i}\), with the cyclic inequalities in (4). Hence \(W=\sum_{x\ge0}(T^N)_{xx}\). Here and below a matrix power means its nonnegative multiple series; regrouping can be justified by increasing finite partial sums.

Use \(q<s<1\) to define \(M_{xy}=s^{-x}T_{xy}s^y\) and \(\rho=(1-qs)^{-1}\). On every closed path the powers of \(s\) cancel, so \(W=\sum_{x\ge0}(M^N)_{xx}\), term by term. The row sums are explicit. For \(u\ge0\),

\[
\sum_{y\ge0}M_{2u,y}
=\rho\left(\frac qs\right)^u,
\qquad
\sum_{y\ge0}M_{2u+1,y}
=q\rho\left(\frac qs\right)^u.
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, the row with index \(x\) is the geometric sum \(s^{-x}\sum_{y\ge\lceil x/2\rceil}(qs)^y\). Thus every row sum is at most \(\rho\), and the sum of all row sums is \(\rho(1+q)/(1-q/s)\). Induction using nonnegative summation gives \(\sum_y(M^h)_{xy}\le\rho^h\) for every integer \(h\ge0\). In particular every entry of \(M^{N-1}\) is at most \(\rho^{N-1}\), so

\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=\sum_{x,y\ge0}M_{xy}(M^{N-1})_{yx}\\
&\le\rho^{N-1}\sum_{x,y\ge0}M_{xy}
=\frac{1+q}{1-q/s}\,(1-qs)^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The finite majorant in (6) accounts for all infinite tails. The argument requires neither a spectral-radius calculation nor an operator trace theorem.

Now take the original center \(J=3\ell\mathbf1\). Since \(F\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), (3) shows \(J\in D\). For each integer vector \(\delta\in\{-\ell,\ldots,\ell\}^N\), put \(v=F\delta\). Each coordinate obeys \(|v_i|=|2\delta_i-\delta_{i-1}|\le3\ell\), and \(v\) belongs to the original congruence lattice by (3). Therefore both \(J+v\) and \(J-v\) belong to the original \(D\). The map \(\delta\mapsto v\) is injective, and the last identity of (3) gives

\[
\min\{w_{J+F\delta},w_{J-F\delta}\}
=q^{3\ell N+|\sum_i\delta_i|}.
\tag{7}
\]

In particular each such vector with \(\sum_i\delta_i=0\) contributes exactly \(q^{3\ell N}\). These are distinct permitted original displacements; the set contains \(\delta\) and \(-\delta\), and contains zero exactly once. Thus the orientation and zero normalization of \(S_J\) is preserved.

To count the balanced vectors, let \(c_h(t)\) be the number of vectors in \(\{-\ell,\ldots,\ell\}^h\) with coordinate sum \(t\). These counts are symmetric in \(t\) and nonincreasing for \(t\ge0\). For completeness, this is true for \(h=1\), and the convolution recurrence implies

\[
c_{h+1}(t+1)-c_{h+1}(t)
=c_h(t+\ell+1)-c_h(t-\ell)\le0
\qquad(t\ge0).
\tag{8}
\]

The inequality follows from the induction hypothesis and \(|t+\ell+1|\ge|t-\ell|\). Since all \((2\ell+1)^N\) vectors have sums in the \(2\ell N+1\) integer positions from \(-\ell N\) to \(\ell N\), it follows that \(c_N(0)\ge(2\ell+1)^N/(2\ell N+1)\). Using only these balanced terms of the full nonnegative reflection sum proves

\[
S_{3\ell\mathbf1}
\ge q^{3\ell N}c_N(0)
\ge\frac{[(2\ell+1)q^{3\ell}]^N}{2\ell N+1}.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (6) and (9) gives (1). If \(\theta>1\), Bernoulli's inequality gives \(\theta^N\ge1+N(\theta-1)\), so (1) is bounded below, independently of \(N\), by

\[
\frac{1-q/s}{1+q}
\min\left\{1,\frac{\theta-1}{2\ell}\right\}>0.
\tag{10}
\]

The right side of (1) tends to infinity. This can already be seen by retaining the quadratic term \(\binom N2(\theta-1)^2\) in the binomial expansion of \(\theta^N\).

For the exact parameters \(q=9/10\), \(s=903/1000\), and \(\ell=3\), one has

\[
\frac{1-q/s}{1+q}=\frac{10}{5719}>\frac1{600},
\qquad
\theta=14\left(\frac9{10}\right)^9\frac{1873}{10000}
=\frac{10158940062558}{10000000000000}
>\frac{203}{200}.
\tag{11}
\]

The last inequality is the integer comparison \(10158940062558>10150000000000\). Bernoulli's inequality also gives \((203/200)^N\ge1+3N/200\). Since \(400(1+3N/200)=400+6N\ge1+6N\), we have \((203/200)^N/(6N+1)\ge1/400\). Equations (1) and (11) now imply (2), including its uniform coefficient. They also imply the asserted divergence as \(N\to\infty\).

Finally, the maximum in the statement is an actual maximum over all original centers. If a displacement contributes to \(S_J\), nonnegativity of both endpoints requires \(|v_i|\le J_i\). Its minimum weight is \(q^{\sum_iJ_i+|\sum_i v_i|}\le q^{\sum_iJ_i}\). Consequently

\[
0<w_J\le S_J
\le q^{\sum_iJ_i}\prod_{i=1}^N(2J_i+1)
=\prod_{i=1}^N\bigl[q^{J_i}(2J_i+1)\bigr].
\tag{12}
\]

For fixed \(N\), each one-coordinate factor is bounded and tends to zero when its coordinate tends to infinity. Thus the right side tends to zero as \(\|J\|_\infty\to\infty\). At \(J=0\), the score equals one. All centers with score at least one therefore lie in a finite box, and a maximum is attained. No center or tail has been omitted from this assertion or from the upper bound for \(W\).
