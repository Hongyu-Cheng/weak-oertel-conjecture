## Statement

Let $Y_i$ be independent unit-rate exponentials and $V=\sum_i c_iY_i$ have coefficients of both signs. Write $f$ for its continuous density, $\mu_j$ for the coarea conditional mean of $Y_j$ at $V=0$, and $B(t)=\int_{-\infty}^0 e^{tv}f(v)\,dv$. For every positive selected coefficient $c_j=p$, maximal or otherwise, $B(1/p)<\infty$ and
$$
 \mu_j=\frac{B(1/p)}{p f(0)}.
$$
Also $v\mapsto e^{v/p}f(v)$ is nondecreasing on $\mathbb R$.

## Proof

Let $\nu$ be the law of $W=V-pY_j$. Conditioning on $W$ gives
$$
 f(v)=\frac1p\int_{w\le v}e^{-(v-w)/p}\,d\nu(w).
$$
Here $W$ has at least one negative nonzero term, so it has no atoms, the formula is continuous and gives the actual density at zero. It is positive at zero because $\Pr(W<0)>0$ by independence and the unbounded negative coordinate. Multiplication by $e^{v/p}$ shows the monotonicity. By Tonelli,
$$
 B(1/p)=\frac1p\int_{w<0}(-w)e^{w/p}\,d\nu(w),
$$
which is finite since the integrand is bounded. The coarea first-moment integral at $V=0$ has the same exponential factor, raw Jacobian $1/p$, and forced value $Y_j=-w/p$, so it is $f(0)\mu_j=p^{-2}\int_{w<0}(-w)e^{w/p}\,d\nu(w)$. These two formulas give the endpoint identity even if this selected endpoint is outside the common open tilt interval.
