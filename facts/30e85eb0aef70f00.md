## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \(w=(w_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be a nonzero nonnegative table supported in \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}^n\). Assume it is globally extendable in the sense of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`: there is a finite-valued nonnegative log-concave function on \(\mathbb R^n\) agreeing with every lattice value, including every zero of the table. Assume also that \(w\) is invariant under each coordinate reflection \(I_i\mapsto1-I_i\), with all other coordinates fixed.

Use the canonical positive support \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\), total weight \(W=\sum_Dw_{\mathbf I}\), maximum weight \(w_{\max}\), potential \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\), and amplitude \(B=\max_D\phi_{\mathbf I}\). For \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) consist of the nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\); denote their positive barycentric coefficients by \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). Set \(G_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). The canonical original-weight quantities of `488f0a583886b5fc` are

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:F\subseteq D,
\ F\cap E\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\text{ with }G_E\le L\right\},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\,L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define the absolute constants \(\kappa=\log(4)/\log(3)>1\) and
\(c_6=\frac12((\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1))^{1/\kappa}>0\), where all logarithms are natural. Then every \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\) belongs to \(D\), and

\[
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}
\ge c_6\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge c_6\,2^{-n}W.
\]

More explicitly, for each such \(\mathbf J\) there is one \(L\ge0\), chosen before any cap normal, for which every \((\mathbf b,s)\in(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R)\setminus\{(\mathbf0,0)\}\) with \(s\ge0\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)+s(\phi_{\mathbf J}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I})\ge0}}w_{\mathbf I}
\ge c_6(1+L)2^{-n}W.
\]

This includes vertical caps and all equality atoms.

Consequently, let \(d\ge1\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let its complete original fiber-volume table \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\) satisfy the hypotheses above. With \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), for every \(\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^n\) there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in C_{\mathbf J}\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf J,\mathbf y^\star)\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\frac{c_6}{6}\,2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

The point is chosen before \(H\). No symmetry of the actual fibers is required. If the complete table is identically zero and \(S\ne\varnothing\), the same zero bound holds at any point of \(S\), without any assertion about the central fibers.

All positive-table conclusions persist under transport by \(T(\mathbf x)=U\mathbf x+\mathbf v\), where \(U\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\), \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n\), with transformed weights \(\widetilde w_{T(\mathbf I)}=w_{\mathbf I}\), and central targets replaced by \(T(\mathbf J)\). For bodies the transport is \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\mapsto(T(\mathbf x),\mathbf y)\). Global extendability remains an assumption for the six-point class; no claim is made that arbitrary decreasing ternary folded weights have a log-concave extension.

## Proof

For a scalar coordinate \(z\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}\), define its folded radius \(r(z)\in\{0,1,2\}\) by \(r(0)=r(1)=0\), \(r(-1)=r(2)=1\), and \(r(-2)=r(3)=2\). Apply this map coordinatewise. Each reflection preserves the folded radius, and the coordinate reflections act transitively on the box points having any prescribed folded-radius vector. Hence a unique function \(h:\{0,1,2\}^n\to[0,\infty)\) satisfies \(w_{\mathbf I}=h(r(\mathbf I))\) throughout the box. Each folded-radius vector has exactly \(2^n\) preimages.

We verify monotonicity from the extension, including zeros. Fix all but one coordinate at box lattice values, and fix integers \(0\le u<v\le2\). The two remaining-coordinate values \(-v\) and \(1+v\) have the same weight, namely the folded value at radius \(v\). The value \(-u\) lies strictly between these two coordinates and has folded radius \(u\). If the common endpoint weight is positive, log-concavity of the extension on that segment gives that the weight at \(-u\) is at least this common weight. If the common endpoint weight is zero, the same inequality follows from nonnegativity. Thus \(h\) is decreasing in each coordinate. In particular, because the table is nonzero, \(h(\mathbf0)>0\), \(w_{\max}=h(\mathbf0)\), and all central vertices are in \(D\). The positive folded support is a down-set. We use no converse extension assertion.

We now implement the layer-cake and Hölder mechanism of accepted fact `43f845f17a377be1` with the new adjacency exponent supplied by `fb81dd9234b820a1`; every analytic step and the changed reflection correspondence are given below. The symbol \(\kappa\) here has the value \(\log4/\log3\), not the four-point theorem's value \(\log3/\log2\).

Put \(A=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\{0,1,2\}^n}h(\mathbf u)>0\). The preimage count gives \(W=2^n A\). Temporarily divide all table weights and folded weights by \(A\), continuing to denote the normalized weights by \(w,h\). Potentials, gaps, and barycentric support families are unchanged by this division; hitting masses and scores are divided by \(A\). In this normalization \(\sum h=1\) and \(W=2^n\). Write \(h_0=h(\mathbf0)>0\) and \(\Phi(\mathbf u)=\log(h_0/h(\mathbf u))\) only where \(h(\mathbf u)>0\).

For \(t\ge0\), let \(R_t=\{\mathbf u:h(\mathbf u)>0,\ \Phi(\mathbf u)\le t\}\), let \(N(t)=|R_t|\), and let \(d(t)\) count ordered pairs in \(R_t\times R_t\) adjacent in every coordinate for the relation
\(\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,2),(2,1)\}\).
Monotonicity makes each \(R_t\) a nonempty down-set, even when some folded weights vanish. Thus accepted fact `fb81dd9234b820a1` gives \(d(t)\ge N(t)^\kappa\). Also \(1\le N(t)\le3^n\). Finite-sum integration gives

\[
Z:=\int_0^\infty N(t)e^{-t}\,dt
=\sum_{\mathbf u:h(\mathbf u)>0}e^{-\Phi(\mathbf u)}
=\frac1{h_0}.
\]

For the exact reflection correspondence, both \(\mathbf z\) and \(-\mathbf z\) lie in the six-point box if and only if \(\mathbf z\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^n\). In one coordinate, the respective values of \((r(z),r(-z))\), as \(z=-2,-1,0,1,2\), are
\((2,1),(1,0),(0,0),(0,1),(1,2)\).
These are precisely the five distinct allowed ordered adjacency pairs. Taking coordinate products proves that \(\mathbf z\mapsto(r(\mathbf z),r(-\mathbf z))\) is a bijection from this intersection box to the ordered adjacent folded pairs. Negation corresponds to reversal, and the only vector fixed by negation is \(\mathbf0\), corresponding to the ordered pair \((\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\). In particular no other self-reversing adjacent pair has been omitted.

If the two folded endpoints lie in \(R_t\), the corresponding points \(\mathbf z,-\mathbf z\) have positive weights and potentials at most \(t\). For \(\mathbf z\ne\mathbf0\), their two-point set is affinely independent, has barycenter \(\mathbf0\) with coefficients one half, and has gap at most \(t\) because \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\). Every eligible hitting set must therefore contain at least one endpoint. All distinct unordered reflection pairs are disjoint, and none contains the compulsory singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\). Define

\[
\Sigma(t)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in R_t\\\mathbf u\sim\mathbf v}}
\min\{h(\mathbf u),h(\mathbf v)\}.
\]

The bijection and disjointness prove \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,t;w)\ge(h_0+\Sigma(t))/2\). Indeed every noncentral reflection pair contributes its minimum weight, while the single central ordered pair contributes \(h_0\) to \(\Sigma(t)\) and the additional \(h_0\) restores the full compulsory central mass. In particular \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,0;w)\ge h_0\).

For each ordered pair counted in \(\Sigma(t)\), put \(b=\max\{\Phi(\mathbf u),\Phi(\mathbf v)\}\le t\). Its minimum weight is \(h_0e^{-b}\). Summing the elementary identity \(e^{-b}=e^{-t}+\int_b^t e^{-\tau}\,d\tau\) over these finitely many pairs gives

\[
\Sigma(t)=h_0\left(e^{-t}d(t)+\int_0^t d(\tau)e^{-\tau}\,d\tau\right).
\]

This identity retains all threshold ties, including at \(t=0\). Define \(\Psi(t)=\int_0^t N(\tau)^\kappa e^{-\tau}\,d\tau\). Discarding nonnegative terms and using \(d(\tau)\ge N(\tau)^\kappa\) gives \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,t;w)\ge\Psi(t)/(2Z)\).

Let \(q=\sup_{t\ge0}m_{\log}(\mathbf0,t;w)/(1+t)\). It is finite since the hitting mass is at most \(2^n\), and positive since \(q\ge h_0=1/Z\). For every \(t\ge0\), we therefore have \(\Psi(t)\le2qZ(1+t)\). The function \(\Psi\) is absolutely continuous, starts at zero, is bounded by \(3^{n\kappa}\), and has derivative \(N(t)^\kappa e^{-t}\) almost everywhere. Set \(\kappa'=\kappa/(\kappa-1)\), and choose \(a=(\kappa-1)/(2\kappa)>0\). Then \(a\kappa'=1/2<1\). Integration by parts, first on a finite interval and then letting its upper endpoint tend to infinity, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty N(t)^\kappa e^{-t-a\kappa t}\,dt
&=a\kappa\int_0^\infty\Psi(t)e^{-a\kappa t}\,dt\\
&\le2qZ\,a\kappa\int_0^\infty(1+t)e^{-a\kappa t}\,dt
=2qZ\left(1+\frac1{a\kappa}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The boundary terms vanish by \(\Psi(0)=0\), boundedness of \(\Psi\), and \(a>0\). Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(\kappa,\kappa'\), applied to \(N(t)e^{-at}\) and \(e^{at}\) under measure \(e^{-t}dt\), now yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z
&\le\left(\int_0^\infty N(t)^\kappa e^{-t-a\kappa t}\,dt\right)^{1/\kappa}
\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-(1-a\kappa')t}\,dt\right)^{1/\kappa'}\\
&\le(2qZ)^{1/\kappa}
\left(1+\frac1{a\kappa}\right)^{1/\kappa}
(1-a\kappa')^{-1/\kappa'}.
\end{aligned}
\]

All integrals are finite by the displayed bounds and \(a\kappa'<1\). Divide by \(Z^{1/\kappa}\) and use \(Z\ge1/q\) to obtain

\[
q^{-1/\kappa'}\le Z^{1/\kappa'}
\le(2q)^{1/\kappa}
\left(1+\frac1{a\kappa}\right)^{1/\kappa}
(1-a\kappa')^{-1/\kappa'}.
\]

Because \(1/\kappa+1/\kappa'=1\), rearrangement gives
\(q\ge2^{-1/\kappa}(1+1/(a\kappa))^{-1/\kappa}(1-a\kappa')^{1/\kappa'}\).
Our choice has \(a\kappa=(\kappa-1)/2\) and \(1-a\kappa'=1/2\); the last lower bound is exactly \(c_6\).

The supremum is attained. The family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) is finite, and global extendability ensures \(0\le G_E\le B\) by `488f0a583886b5fc`. Membership in the cutoff family changes only at these finitely many gap values, including zero from the singleton. Between successive values the hitting mass is constant, with its new value already taken at the left endpoint, while division by \(1+t\) decreases. Past the largest value the same conclusion holds. This proves attainment, also when \(B=0\). Undoing normalization multiplies \(q\) by the original \(A=2^{-n}W\). It proves the stated fixed-center inequality at \(\mathbf0\). Coordinate reflections preserve weights, potentials, affine independence, and barycentric coefficients; they send \(\mathbf0\) to any prescribed central vertex. They thus prove the same bound there. The exact closed-cap formula (15) of `488f0a583886b5fc` converts the attaining cutoff into the asserted inequality for all normals, including vertical ones and boundary atoms.

For an actual body, keep the original weights throughout. At a center \(\mathbf J\) and the just obtained cutoff \(L\), define its canonical root costs by
\(L_E=\log\bigl(w_{\mathbf J}/(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d})^d\bigr)\).
The original root hitting mass \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\) uses exactly the same hitting definition with the family \(L_E\le L\). Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\). Explicitly, the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives \((\sum\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d})^d\ge\prod w_{\mathbf I}^{\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}}\), so \(L_E\le G_E\). Thus the root cutoff family contains the logarithmic one, and its minimum hitting weight can only increase, with no replacement of source weights. By the fixed-fiber, attained depth inequality (4) in accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in C_{\mathbf J}\) satisfies

\[
h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y^\star))
\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)}{6(1+L)}
\ge\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{6(1+L)}
\ge\frac{c_6}{6}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

Since depth is the infimum over all containing closed ambient halfspaces, this is exactly the claimed point-before-halfspace statement. The coefficient is independent of both dimensions. It is positive and less than \(1/e\), since \(c_6<1/2\) and \(e<3<12\). If \(W=0\), nonnegativity of mixed volume proves the stipulated zero case directly.

Finally, a unimodular affine map bijects the lattice, transports the extension by composition with its inverse, and bijects every barycentric support while preserving its coefficients, weight, and potential. It therefore preserves \(W\), every corresponding logarithmic hitting mass, and the optimized score. For bodies the stated ambient map leaves each continuous fiber coordinate unchanged, preserves its \(d\)-dimensional volume, bijects the integer fibers, and bijects containing halfspaces. Hence it preserves mixed-volume depth and carries each chosen witness to the claimed transformed target. This proves the transport assertions using the original measures.
