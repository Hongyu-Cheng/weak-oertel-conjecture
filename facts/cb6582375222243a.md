## Statement

There is a full-dimensional planar triangle with nonnegative coordinates and a uniform point $X$ such that for $T=X_1+X_2$ both conditional coordinate means are increasing and $1$-Lipschitz on the interior projection interval, yet its scalar Stein kernel $\tau$ has $\tau'>1$ on an open subinterval. Its density is strictly decreasing on a nonempty open interior interval lying below $\mathbb ET-1$. The number one is the largest coefficient of the projection $X_1+X_2$.

## Proof

First use $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-3,3),(3,-3),(3,3)\}$, of area $18$. In coordinates $(t,z)=(x_1+x_2,x_1)$, whose inverse has absolute Jacobian one, the triangle is $0\le t\le6$, $t-3\le z\le3$. Therefore on $(0,6)$ the density is $f(t)=(6-t)/18$, the mean is $2$ by integration, and the conditional means are both $t/2$ by uniformity on the displayed segment.

Use the accepted scalar closed-tail formula with one fiber and $L(x)=x$ on the law of $T$. Its tail numerator $\kappa(t)=\int_t^6(s-2)f(s)\,ds$ has derivative $(2-t)f(t)$ and vanishes at six. The function $t(6-t)^2/54$ has that derivative and endpoint value, so $\tau(t)=\kappa(t)/f(t)=t(6-t)/3$. Its derivative is $2-2t/3>1$ for $0<t<3/2$. The density is strictly decreasing on all of $(0,6)$, including $(0,1)$, which lies below $\mathbb ET-1$.

Translate the triangle by $(3,3)$ to obtain vertices $(0,6),(6,0),(6,6)$. The sum shifts by six, so the conditional coordinate means are still $t/2$, the mean is eight, the density on $(6,12)$ is $(12-t)/18$, and the kernel is $(t-6)(12-t)/3$. Translation preserves both interior failures, independently of density values at the endpoints.
