## Statement

Fix $n,d,N\ge1$, finite nonempty lattice-convex $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ and $z_0\in P\cap\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}P$. A family is compatible if its nonempty compact convex fibers are the actual sections of the convex hull of their lifts over $P$. In the compatible class with $|K_{z_0}|_d=1$, impose the requirement that a common list of at most $N$ inequalities $n_f\cdot x+a_f\cdot z\le b_f$, with $n_f\ne0$, represent every positive-volume fiber as their full intersection in $\mathbb R^d$. The class is closed under simultaneous Hausdorff convergence, with no representation condition on null fibers. If every fiber is positive, sufficiently small changes in all the coefficients give positive bounded actual fibers over $P$ with the same list size. The coefficient assertion allows the central volume to change before common rescaling restores it.

## Proof

Let $K_{z,\ell}\to K_z$. All the sets lie in some fixed ball, and finite anchored Minkowski inclusions pass to the limit by taking limits of points with fixed weights. This is compatibility, and the projection is exactly $P$ because the base hull is $\operatorname{conv}P$. Convex-set volume is continuous in this convergence. For a positive limit, support-function convergence retains a small ball $B(q_z,r_z)\subset K_{z,\ell}$ for every sufficiently large $\ell$, and each point off the boundary has eventual indicator convergence. For a null limit, the volumes of its shrinking closed neighborhoods tend to zero. Thus the central normalization passes to the limit, and each site with a positive limit is eventually positive. Only these finitely many sites need a representation.

Repeat rows if necessary so the list has length $N$, and divide each inequality by $\lVert n_{f,\ell}\rVert$. Pass to a subsequence with $n_{f,\ell}\to n_f$ of unit length and affine evaluations $t_{f,\ell}(z)=b_{f,\ell}-a_{f,\ell}\cdot z$ tending simultaneously to extended real values at all the positive limiting sites. The retained interior ball bounds each threshold below, so the limits are finite or $+\infty$. At a positive site every point of $K_z$ satisfies every finite limiting inequality by convergence. Conversely, for any point of the finite limiting intersection, mix it with $q_z$ using any positive weight on $q_z$. This satisfies the finite limiting inequalities strictly because the ball gives slack at least $r_z$. The divergent rows also hold eventually at this fixed point. The mixed point lies in the old intersections for all sufficiently large $\ell$ and hence in $K_z$. Letting the weight decrease to zero proves equality globally with the finite limiting intersection.

Apply 0a73ba4a9c7b24d1 separately to each row with normal $-n_{f,\ell}$ and affine threshold $-t_{f,\ell}$, on the finite set of positive limiting sites. Take the radius $R$ strictly larger than the common containing radius. The affine evaluation step in its proof preserves the finite thresholds exactly and gives a finite threshold $t_f(z)>R$ at each originally positive divergence. These new rows are redundant on $K_z$, which already equals the full finite limiting intersection. This yields the required one list simultaneously at all positive sites.

In the all-positive case, fix a representation. Small coefficient changes keep a fixed smaller ball in each fiber by strict slack. The original normals satisfy $\min_{\lVert y\rVert=1}\max_f n_f\cdot y>0$: otherwise a nonzero common recession direction contradicts boundedness of any old fiber. This inequality persists by compactness of the sphere, and a uniform upper bound on the changed thresholds at the finite sites then bounds the new fibers. All are sections of the common convex polyhedron cut by $\operatorname{conv}P\times\mathbb R^d$. Their lifted hull is contained in that polyhedron and contains all their lifts, so these are also its exact sampled fibers. A common positive continuous dilation preserves the number of rows and can restore central unit volume.
