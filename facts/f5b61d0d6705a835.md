## Statement

For integers \(n,L\ge1\), set \(D_{n,L}=\{0,\ldots,L\}^n\subset\mathbb Z^n\). This support is complete: \(D_{n,L}=[0,L]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n=\operatorname{conv}(D_{n,L})\cap\mathbb Z^n\). A simplex here means the convex hull of \(r+1\) affinely independent vertices in \(D_{n,L}\), for any \(0\le r\le n\), including a single point when \(r=0\). Define its largest original uniform capture fraction by
\[
\max_T\frac{\#(D_{n,L}\cap T)}{\#D_{n,L}}.
\]
The maximum is over this finite nonempty collection of simplices. For every such \(T\),
\[
\frac{\#(D_{n,L}\cap T)}{(L+1)^n}
\le
\left(\frac{L}{L+1}\right)^n
\left[
\frac{n^{n/2}}{n!}
+\frac{n(n+1)}{L}
\left(1+\frac{\sqrt n}{L}\right)^{n-1}
\right].
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\dim T<n\), the stronger bound omitting the volume term and replacing \(n(n+1)\) by \(n\) holds. In particular, for the explicit even integers \(L_n=2^{n^2}\),
\[
2^n\max_T\frac{\#(D_{n,L_n}\cap T)}{(L_n+1)^n}
\le
\left(\frac{2e}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+e\,n(n+1)2^{n-n^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently there is no \(a>0\) such that every finite nonempty complete-lattice support in every integer dimension \(n\ge1\) contains a lattice simplex capturing at least \(a2^{-n}\) of its original uniform mass.

For calibration against the locked problem, let \(d\ge1\), let \(B_d\subset\mathbb R^d\) be the closed Euclidean unit ball centered at \(\mathbf0\), and take the genuine full-dimensional convex body \(C=[0,L_n]^n\times B_d\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Use the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(h_S\), and put \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)=(L_n+1)^n\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)>0\). Then the legal point \(\mathbf y^\star=((L_n/2)\mathbf1,\mathbf0)\in S\), with \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^n\), satisfies \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)=W/2\).

## Proof

First fix \(n,L\) and a simplex \(T\) as in the statement. The normalization used only for the counting argument is \(\widehat D=L^{-1}D_{n,L}\subset[0,1]^n\) and \(\widehat T=L^{-1}T\). Thus the map \(\mathbf z\mapsto\mathbf z/L\) bijects \(D_{n,L}\cap T\) with \(\widehat D\cap\widehat T\); all final fractions retain the original counting normalization.

Around each \(\mathbf x\in\widehat D\cap\widehat T\), take the centered closed cell \(E_{\mathbf x}=\mathbf x+[-1/(2L),1/(2L)]^n\), and let \(U\) be their union. Different cells have disjoint interiors; their intersections lie in coordinate hyperplanes and have \(n\)-dimensional volume zero. Hence, writing \(m=\#(D_{n,L}\cap T)\), we have \(\operatorname{vol}_n(U)=m/L^n\). Put \(\varepsilon=\sqrt n/(2L)\). Each point of \(E_{\mathbf x}\) is at Euclidean distance at most \(\varepsilon\) from its center \(\mathbf x\), and every cell lies in the expanded cube \(E=[-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]^n\).

We will use the following elementary slab estimate, uniformly over every unit normal \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and every \(b\in\mathbb R\):
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n\bigl(E\cap\{\mathbf y:|\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf y-b|\le\varepsilon\}\bigr)
\le2\varepsilon\sqrt n(1+2\varepsilon)^{n-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, choose a coordinate \(k\) with \(|a_k|\ge1/\sqrt n\), which exists since \(\sum_k a_k^2=1\). For fixed values of all other coordinates, the slab inequality allows an interval of length \(2\varepsilon/|a_k|\le2\varepsilon\sqrt n\) in coordinate \(k\); intersecting with that coordinate's cube interval can only decrease its length. The other coordinates range over a box of volume \((1+2\varepsilon)^{n-1}\). Fubini proves (3), including \(n=1\).

Suppose \(\dim T=n\). Its normalized simplex has \(n+1\) facets, so it is the intersection of their closed supporting halfspaces \(\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf y\le b_i\), with \(\|\mathbf a_i\|_2=1\), for \(1\le i\le n+1\). For any \(\mathbf y\in U\setminus\widehat T\), choose a cell center \(\mathbf x\in\widehat T\) containing \(\mathbf y\). Some facet inequality is violated, and for that facet
\[
0<\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf y-b_i
\le\mathbf a_i\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)
\le\|\mathbf y-\mathbf x\|_2
\le\varepsilon.
\]
Thus \(U\setminus\widehat T\) is contained in the union of the \(n+1\) slabs in (3). Volume subadditivity gives
\[
\frac m{L^n}
\le\operatorname{vol}_n(\widehat T)
+(n+1)2\varepsilon\sqrt n(1+2\varepsilon)^{n-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
For the first term, write the vertices of \(\widehat T\) as \(\mathbf v_0,\ldots,\mathbf v_n\in[0,1]^n\). Each edge column \(\mathbf v_i-\mathbf v_0\) has norm at most \(\sqrt n\). The absolute determinant of the edge matrix is at most the product of these column norms: Gram-Schmidt expresses it as the product of the perpendicular column lengths, each no greater than the original column length. The simplex volume is this determinant divided by \(n!\). This volume formula follows by applying the edge matrix to the standard simplex \(\{\mathbf t\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i t_i\le1\}\), whose volume is \(1/n!\) by repeated integration. Therefore \(\operatorname{vol}_n(\widehat T)\le n^{n/2}/n!\). Substituting \(2\varepsilon\sqrt n=n/L\) into (4) and dividing by \((L+1)^n/L^n\) proves (1) in the full-dimensional case.

If \(\dim T<n\), its affine hull is contained in some hyperplane \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x=b\) with \(\|\mathbf a\|_2=1\). For every \(\mathbf y\in U\), a cell center \(\mathbf x\in\widehat T\) satisfies \(|\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf y-b|=|\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)|\le\varepsilon\). Hence (3) bounds all of \(U\), and
\[
\frac m{L^n}\le\frac nL\left(1+\frac{\sqrt n}{L}\right)^{n-1}.
\]
This proves the asserted stronger bound and, consequently, (1) in every remaining rank, including point simplices.

We now verify the asymptotic quantitatively. Since \(\log\) is increasing,
\[
\log(n!)=\sum_{k=2}^n\log k
\ge\int_1^n\log t\,dt=n\log n-n+1,
\]
also for \(n=1\). In particular \(n!\ge(n/e)^n\), and \(2^n n^{n/2}/n!\le(2e/\sqrt n)^n\). Furthermore, \((n-1)\sqrt n\le n^2\le2^{n^2}\) for every integer \(n\ge1\). Using \(1+t\le e^t\) for \(t\ge0\) gives
\[
\left(1+\frac{\sqrt n}{2^{n^2}}\right)^{n-1}
\le\exp\left(\frac{(n-1)\sqrt n}{2^{n^2}}\right)
\le e.
\]
Since \((L_n/(L_n+1))^n\le1\), (1) proves (2). The first term in (2) is at most \(2^{-n}\) whenever \(n\ge16e^2\). For the second term, the elementary inequalities \(n\le2^n\) and \(n+1\le2^n\), valid for \(n\ge1\), show that it is at most \(e2^{3n-n^2}\), which tends to zero. Thus (2) is a rigorous limit, uniform over all simplex vertices and all ranks. For any proposed \(a>0\), sufficiently large \(n\) has \(2^n\max_T\#(D_{n,L_n}\cap T)/(L_n+1)^n<a\), refuting the stated universal capture assertion on that exact complete-lattice cube.

Finally, the product \(C=[0,L_n]^n\times B_d\) is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Its lattice support is exactly \(D_{n,L_n}\), and every occupied fiber has the identical positive \(d\)-volume \(\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)\), so the displayed value of \(W\) follows directly from the locked mixed-integer measure. The integer \(L_n\) is even, hence \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\).

The reflection \((\mathbf z,\mathbf u)\mapsto(L_n\mathbf1-\mathbf z,-\mathbf u)\) preserves \(S\) and its mixed-integer measure and reflects about \(\mathbf y^\star\). For any ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), the parallel closed halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf y^\star\) and has the same orientation is contained in it. This latter halfspace and its reflection cover \(S\), and their \(S\)-masses are equal. Their common mass is therefore at least \(W/2\). This proves \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge W/2\), with no assumption that their shared boundary has zero mixed-integer measure. To attain equality, take the closed ambient halfspace \(\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf u):u_1\ge0\}\). It contains \(\mathbf y^\star\) and bisects every ball fiber; its boundary has \(d\)-volume zero in each fiber since \(d\ge1\). Its mixed-integer mass is exactly \(W/2\), proving the claimed equality. In particular, the vanishing simplex-capture fraction occurs on bodies satisfying a much larger actual depth bound than the locked target asks for.
