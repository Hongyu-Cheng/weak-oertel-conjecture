## Statement

Fix $d\ge1$ and $\tau>0$. For a finite nonempty base $P$ let $D_P(K)$ be normalized maximum closed-halfspace depth on the full compatible positive $d$-volume fibers $K$, and let $b_d(P)=\inf_K D_P(K)$ over all such families. Write $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Use one of the following budgets consistently on closed subsets and their canonical lifts.

- For an arbitrary finite base $P$, use its relative Helly number $q(P)=h(P)$.
- For a complete finite affine-lattice base $P$, use the number $q(P)=k(P)$ of occupied parity classes. Each closed subset uses the inherited affine lattice, and each canonical lift uses its complete integer graph realization with the inherited graph lattice.

Suppose $D_P(K)<\tau/q(P)$ for some positive compatible family. Then among the canonical lifts of nonempty closed subsets of $P$ with $b_d(\Phi_U(U))<\tau/q(U)$, choose one with the fewest sites. Write $B=\Phi_U(U)$ for any such choice, using the integer realization in the parity case. It is dependence-connected, $q(B)=q(U)\le q(P)$, every representation-preserving function on $B$ is affine, and $\dim\operatorname{aff}(B)\le h(B)-1$. In the parity case $B$ is complete in its inherited affine lattice, even if its affine rank exceeds the original rank.

On this $B$ the unrestricted infimum $\beta=b_d(B)<\tau/q(B)$ is attained by a compatible family of total volume one with every fiber of positive $d$-volume, and $\beta\ge\gamma_d/|B|>0$. More precisely, for every $0<\delta<\tau/q(B)$, the mass-one sublevel $D_B(K)\le\delta$ has a uniform positive lower bound on all fiber volumes and is sequentially compact for simultaneous fiber Hausdorff convergence after common continuous affine normalizations and affine shears on $B$. For every nonempty proper closed $T\subset B$ every member of this sublevel satisfies
$$
M_T\ \le\ \frac{D_B(K)}{\tau}\,q(T)\ \le\ \frac{\delta}{\tau}\,q(T)\ <\ \frac{q(T)}{q(B)},
\qquad M_T=\sum_{p\in T}|K_p|_d.
$$
For $\tau=\gamma_d$, the Helly case is the auxiliary finite-base reduction. In the parity case an actual mixed-integer body on $\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$ with positive total mixed $d$-volume and normalized maximum depth below $\tau/2^n$ supplies a strict bad input on its complete positive-fiber base. This includes $\tau=\gamma_d$ and any fixed $c_0\in(0,1/e)$ by taking $\tau=c_0$.

## Proof

The canonical lift preserves compatibility, and its affine projection pulls each original containing halfspace back while preserving its mass. This gives $D_{\Phi_P(P)}(K)\le D_P(K)$. The chosen budget is preserved by the real lift in the Helly case and by the integer graph realization in the parity case. Closed minors have no greater budget, by Helly monotonicity or inclusion of occupied parity classes. For an affine lattice $a+L$, on any retained affine subspace spanned by the retained sites the new direction lattice is the old lattice intersected with the direction space. A difference of retained sites lies in twice that intersection exactly when it lies in $2L$, since the half difference stays in the direction space. Thus changing to the minor's inherited affine lattice does not change the parity count used in the comparison.

There is consequently at least one bad lift, and the collection of canonical lifts of closed minors of $P$ is finite up to affine equivalence. On $B$ every nonempty proper closed $T$ has $b_d(\Phi_T(T))\ge\tau/q(T)$ by minimality, since its canonical lift is also in that collection with the same budget. In the lattice case this applies to each minor's actual integer realization by the congruence lemma. If $B$ had more than one dependence component $T_i$, each would be a proper closed minor and $\sum_i q(T_i)=q(B)$ by exact budget additivity. In a bad family of mass $M$ some component has $M_{T_i}/q(T_i)\ge M/q(B)$. The depth restriction and lift inequalities would give
$$
D_{\Phi_{T_i}(T_i)}(K|_{T_i})\ \le\ D_B(K)M/M_{T_i}\ <\ \tau/q(T_i),
$$
contradicting minimality. The dimension bound follows because the $r+1$ facets of a simplex with $r+1$ affinely independent vertices in a rank-$r$ base form a minimal infeasible family of convex traces, giving $h(B)\ge r+1$ for $r\ge1$. In rank zero $h(B)=1$. The affine-function assertion follows from the canonical lift.

On a family normalized to mass one, the same restriction and lift inequality for any proper nonempty closed $T$ gives $D_B(K)\ge M_Tb_d(\Phi_T(T))\ge M_T\tau/q(T)$, proving the displayed quota. To get the uniform positive floor, suppose a sequence in the sublevel has least fiber volume tending to zero. Along a subsequence all masses converge. The support $T$ of the positive limiting masses is nonempty, proper, and closed by compatibility and the scaled-source volume bound of the diameter lemma. Its retained mass tends to one. This contradicts $M_T\le \delta q(T)/\tau\le\delta q(B)/\tau<1$. Thus all volumes have a common positive floor on this sublevel.

All volumes are also at most one. Apply the common determinant-one anchor normalization and diameter propagation to the connected base $B$. The affine residual lemma bounds positions after an actual affine shear, because $B$ is canonical. The normalizations preserve the family, the mass, and the depth under the corresponding invertible affine maps of the ambient product. The resulting fibers lie in a common closed ball. Blaschke selection on that ball and finiteness of the base give a simultaneous Hausdorff limit along a subsequence. Compatibility survives by approximating each source point in a fixed anchored combination. Volume continuity preserves the positive floor and total volume one. The depth continuity of the residual lemma, applied on the fixed canonical base, keeps the limit in the sublevel.

Choose $\delta$ strictly between $\beta$ and $\tau/q(B)$ and scale a minimizing sequence by a common continuous dilation to normalize its total mass. A tail lies in this sublevel, so the compactness just proved gives a limit attaining $\beta$. For every family a largest fiber has at least $1/|B|$ of the total mass. Its volume centroid has own-fiber depth at least $\gamma_d$ times that fiber's volume by accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with the full-fiber mass parameter. Every containing ambient cap retains at least this contribution, giving $\beta\ge\gamma_d/|B|$.

For the mixed-integer input, the positive-volume bases are complete: at an integer base in their convex hull, the original fiber contains a translate of a positively scaled positive-volume source by convexity, so its volume is positive. The retained fibers preserve the full measured $d$-volume of the original body and reduce the candidate set, so their maximum is at most the original maximum. Their occupied parity count is at most $2^n$, giving the strict input. A positive compatible family on a complete graph base is realized as the full integer trace of the hull of its placed fibers in its inherited affine lattice, by the compatibility equivalence and lattice completeness.
