## Statement

Let $\mu$ be ordinary mixed volume on a finite family of positive compact convex $d$-fibers $K_z$ at integer bases, $d\ge1$. At fixed feasible mass $t>0$, write $B_t$ for the joint body of base and continuous means of dominated sources, $Q_t$ for its real base projection, and $M_t(j)$ for its section. Let $\rho_t(j)$ be the $d$th root of the section's John volume, with value zero if it has no interior. At a base $j$ with $0<t<T_j$, where $T_j$ is the maximum balanced mass at $j$, take the John ellipsoid $c_j+A_jB_d$ and its finite contact data $v_s,\lambda_s,u_s=A_j^{-1}v_s,a_s,\beta_s$. Thus the original support caps have margins $u_s\cdot x+a_s\cdot(z-j)-\beta_s$, and $\sum_s\lambda_s v_s=0$, $\sum_s\lambda_s v_sv_s^{\mathsf T}=I_d$. For $\bar a=\sum_s\lambda_s a_s$, every $j'\in Q_t$ satisfies
$$
\rho_t(j')\le \rho_t(j)\left[1-\frac{\bar a\cdot(j'-j)}d\right].
$$
Equality holds only at $j$, and $\max_{j'\in Q_t}\bar a\cdot(j'-j)<d$.

Under $z'=Uz+b$, $x'=Lx+Bz+c$ with $U$ unimodular, $b$ integral and $L$ invertible, let $\Delta=|\det L|$ and let $S$ denote the affine map. Ordinary transformed mixed volume is $\mu'=\Delta S_\#\mu$, and $B'_{\Delta t}=S(B_t)$. At $j'=Uj+b$ the center maps to $Lc_j+Bj+c$, the canonical shape is $A'=(LA_j^2L^{\mathsf T})^{1/2}$, and the volume and its root scale by $\Delta$ and $\Delta^{1/d}$. The normalized contact vectors are $(A')^{-1}LA_jv_s$ with the same weights. The new cap coefficients and weighted aggregates satisfy
$$
u_s'=L^{-\mathsf T}u_s,\qquad
a_s'=U^{-\mathsf T}(a_s-B^{\mathsf T}u_s'),\qquad
\beta_s'=\beta_s+u_s'\cdot(Bj+c),\qquad
\sum_s\lambda_s a_s'=U^{-\mathsf T}\bar a,\qquad
\sum_s\lambda_s\beta_s'=\sum_s\lambda_s\beta_s .
$$
The source masses and hinge integrals scale by $\Delta$.

## Proof

The fixed-section contact and hinge theorem supplies actual original-source cap data because $0<t<T_j$. For any dominated density of mass $t$ and base mean $j'$, integrating its product with each margin is bounded by the corresponding original hinge. The cap at $j$ attains the bound for $u_s$, so
$$
h_{M_t(j')}(u_s)\le h_{M_t(j)}(u_s)-a_s\cdot(j'-j).
$$
In the coordinates $A_j^{-1}(x-c_j)$ the right support at $j$ is the unit-ball contact value. If the target section has positive volume, use its maximizing ellipsoid in these coordinates with center $b_0$ and symmetric positive definite shape $H$. If it has no interior, use an arbitrary point as center $b_0$ and $H=0$. The support inequalities give $v_s\cdot b_0+\|Hv_s\|\le1-a_s\cdot(j'-j)$. Multiply by $\lambda_s$ and sum. Vector balance removes the center, while matrix balance and $\|v_s\|=1$ give
$$
\operatorname{tr}H=\sum_s\lambda_s v_s^{\mathsf T}Hv_s\le \sum_s\lambda_s\|Hv_s\|\le d-\bar a\cdot(j'-j).
$$
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives $(\det H)^{1/d}\le\operatorname{tr}H/d$. Here $(\det H)^{1/d}=\rho_t(j')/\rho_t(j)$ also for the zero-shape convention, proving the majorant.

Apply the accepted raw strict-interpolation theorem to $Q(t,tj)$ at common mass $t$. Division by $t$ gives the continuous centroid sections, and multiplication by the common unit-ball volume root converts determinant root to volume root. Hence $\rho_t$ is strictly concave between any two distinct feasible bases, including zero-volume endpoints. The affine majorant touches at $j$, and contact at any other base would contradict strict concavity at intermediate bases. The majorant is therefore positive on all of $Q_t$, since it is positive at $j$ and strictly exceeds the nonnegative $\rho_t(j')$ elsewhere. The compactness of $Q_t$ from the raw graph gives a positive minimum, hence the strict maximum inequality.

The base map bijects integer fibers, and change of variables in each fiber gives $\mu'=\Delta S_\#\mu$. Transport a source density by $f'=f\circ S^{-1}$. Its mass is $\Delta t$, and its normalized joint mean is transported by $S$, so invertibility gives $B'_{\Delta t}=S(B_t)$. If the convention instead keeps $S_\#\mu$, it keeps $t$ and has density $1/\Delta$ relative to ordinary transformed volume. At fixed base, the map sends each ellipsoid to an ellipsoid and scales all volumes by $\Delta$, so it sends the unique John ellipsoid to the new one. The canonical shape squares to $LA_j^2L^{\mathsf T}$ and $(A')^{-1}LA_j$ is orthogonal, which proves the contact-vector and volume laws. The general uniqueness proof in 636ab2c480ef41f7 applies to these full-dimensional compact convex sections.

Substitute $x=L^{-1}(x'-Bz-c)$ and $z-j=U^{-1}(z'-(Uj+b))$ in the old margin. This gives the displayed three cap coefficients and preserves its value at corresponding points. Weighted continuous normals sum to zero before and after the map, so summing cancels the shear and threshold corrections, proving the aggregate laws. The same pointwise margin identity and volume change give the mass and hinge laws. Because $U^{-\mathsf T}$ is invertible, a nonzero weighted aggregate tilt remains nonzero.
