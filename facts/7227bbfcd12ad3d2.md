## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, with a compact convex body $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ of nonempty interior for every $p\in P$, where $d\ge1$. Let $Q=\operatorname{conv}P$ and $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$. For every $z\in Q$ let $C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}$. The fibers $C_z$ are convex bodies with a uniform positive inradius. If $h_K(u)=\max_{x\in K}u\cdot x$, then
$$
h_{C_z}(u)=\max_{\substack{\lambda_p\ge0,\ \sum_p\lambda_p=1\\ \sum_p\lambda_p p=z}}
\sum_p\lambda_p h_{K_p}(u).
$$
There is a finite constant $L$ depending on the given family such that $d_H(C_z,C_{z'})\le L\|z-z'\|$ for all $z,z'\in Q$, including its relative boundary. In particular, for each $y\in\operatorname{int}C_z$, the fibers at all sufficiently close bases in $Q$ contain a common ball centered at $y$.

## Proof

Grouping a convex combination in $C$ by the sampled bases gives exactly the union, over the feasible weights in the statement, of the corresponding Minkowski sums. Each support is the weighted sum of input supports. The feasible weight set is compact, so its continuous linear objective attains the displayed maximum. The fibers are compact and convex since $C$ is the convex hull of a compact set in finite dimension. Choose closed balls of one positive radius inside all the finitely many $K_p$. Every feasible convex combination contains a ball of this radius centered at the corresponding combination of the ball centers. This proves the uniform inradius assertion.

Use orthonormal coordinates on the base affine span and write $r=\dim\operatorname{aff}(P)$. For each fixed unit $u$, finite linear programming duality expresses the support value as the minimum at $z$ of $c+A\cdot z$ over $c+A\cdot p\ge h_{K_p}(u)$ for all $p\in P$. The dual is feasible, for example with a sufficiently large constant, and the minimum is attained because the feasible primal has a finite optimum. The dual polyhedron is pointed, because any line direction would be an affine function vanishing on all the points of a full affine span. Its nonempty optimal face therefore has a vertex. Each vertex is determined by $r+1$ independent tight rows of the fixed matrix with rows $(1,p^{\mathsf T})$. There are finitely many such independent row sets. The associated vertex coefficients are fixed linear functions of the selected values $h_{K_p}(u)$, which are uniformly bounded over unit $u$ and $p\in P$. Thus all vertex slopes are bounded in norm by one finite constant $L$. This includes $r=0$, when the slope is zero.

For fixed $u$, the support is the minimum of the affine functions at the feasible dual vertices, all with slopes bounded by $L$. Evaluating a minimizing vertex at the other base and reversing the roles gives $|h_{C_z}(u)-h_{C_{z'}}(u)|\le L\|z-z'\|$. Taking the supremum over unit $u$ gives the Hausdorff estimate by the support characterization of Hausdorff distance for compact convex sets. If $y+RB_d\subset C_z$ for $R>0$ and the closed Euclidean unit ball $B_d$, then $h_{C_{z'}}(u)\ge u\cdot y+R-d_H(C_z,C_{z'})$ for every unit $u$. This implies $y+(R/2)B_d\subset C_{z'}$ whenever $d_H(C_z,C_{z'})\le R/2$.
