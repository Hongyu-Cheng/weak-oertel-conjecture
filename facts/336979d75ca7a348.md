## Statement

For every real \(s>0\), define
\[
F_3(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj},
\qquad
Q_s(j)=\frac{(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}}{F_3(s)}\quad(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}),
\qquad
\mu_3(s)=\mathbb E_{Q_s}J.
\]
The law \(Q_s\) is a new auxiliary law, distinct from any original finite-support law \(p\). The accepted predecessor `609407d6db81b18b` uses the geometric law \(P_\rho(j)=(1-\rho)\rho^j\), the generating function \(F(t)=(1+6t+t^2)/(1-t)^3\), and the quadratic auxiliary law \(Q_\rho(j)=(2j+1)^2\rho^{3j}/F(\rho^3)\). The exact rate alias is \(\rho=e^{-s}\). Thus the present \(Q_s\) replaces the weights \((2j+1)^2e^{-3sj}\) by \((2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}\); it is not the predecessor's \(Q_{e^{-s}}\). Below the rational-function argument is \(z=e^{-4s}\), whereas the predecessor's quadratic argument at the same rate is \(e^{-3s}\).

For \(z=e^{-4s}\), the exact formulas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_3(s)&=\frac{1+23z+23z^2+z^3}{(1-z)^4},\\
\mu_3(s)&=\frac{27z+115z^2+49z^3+z^4}
{(1-z)(1+23z+23z^2+z^3)},\\
\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2&=\frac{27z+338z^2+492z^3+102z^4+z^5}
{(1-z)^2(1+23z+23z^2+z^3)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mu_3(s)\le\frac{5}{2s},\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2\le\frac{23}{3s^2}<\frac8{s^2}\quad(s>0),
\qquad
\mu_3(s)\ge\frac1{20s}\quad(0<s\le\log3).
\tag{2}
\]
Writing \(\psi_3(s)=\tfrac14\log F_3(s)\), one has
\[
\psi_3'(s)=-\mu_3(s),\qquad
\psi_3''(s)=4\operatorname{Var}_{Q_s}J>0,
\qquad
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\psi_3(s)=+\infty.
\tag{3}
\]
For every polynomial \(f\),
\(\frac{d}{ds}\mathbb E_{Q_s}f(J)=-4\operatorname{Cov}_{Q_s}(f(J),J)\).
For all real \(u,a\ge0\), the exact exponential tilt identity is
\[
\frac{e^{-uaj}Q_s(j)}{\mathbb E_{Q_s}e^{-uaJ}}
=Q_{s+ua/4}(j)\qquad(j\ge0).
\tag{4}
\]
For every integer \(\kappa\ge1\), the rate inflation ratio satisfies
\[
\frac{F_3(\kappa s)}{F_3(s)}\ge\frac1{48\kappa^4}\quad(s>0).
\tag{5}
\]
Finally the fourth-power geometric partition factor satisfies, for every \(s>0\),
\[
(1-e^{-s})^4F_3(s)\ge\left(\frac8{27}\right)^{3/2}>\frac18.
\tag{6}
\]
At the cutoff used in (2), the corresponding deterministic-zero coordinate factor also obeys
\((1-e^{-s})^4\ge16/81>(8/27)^{3/2}\) for every \(s\ge\log3\).

## Proof

All the defining series converge absolutely for \(s>0\), and \(F_3(s)>0\), so \(Q_s\) is a probability law. Every polynomial moment is finite. On any compact interval of positive rates, multiplication of a summand by a fixed power of \(j\) still leaves a uniformly summable exponential majorant. Thus all the differentiations below are justified term by term.

For \(0<z<1\), apply the operator \(z\frac{d}{dz}\) to the geometric series. Expanding \((2j+1)^3=8j^3+12j^2+6j+1\) gives
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^3z^j
=\left(8\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^3
+12\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\right)^2
+6z\frac{d}{dz}+1\right)\frac1{1-z}
=\frac{1+23z+23z^2+z^3}{(1-z)^4}.
\]
Applying \(z\frac{d}{dz}\) once and twice to this last expression gives the respective unnormalized moment numerators
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}j(2j+1)^3z^j
=\frac{27z+115z^2+49z^3+z^4}{(1-z)^5},
\qquad
\sum_{j\ge0}j^2(2j+1)^3z^j
=\frac{27z+338z^2+492z^3+102z^4+z^5}{(1-z)^6}.
\]
Dividing by the displayed generating function proves (1).

The probability generating function, valid for \(0\le t<e^{4s}\), factors as
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_s}t^J
=\frac{1+23zt+23z^2t^2+z^3t^3}{1+23z+23z^2+z^3}
\left(\frac{1-z}{1-zt}\right)^4.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently \(J\) has the same law as \(Y+K\), with independence, where \(Y\) is a sum of four independent geometric variables of masses \((1-z)z^j\), and \(K\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) has probabilities proportional to \(1,23z,23z^2,z^3\). Equality of the probability generating functions proves this distributional identity. The geometric formulas and \(e^v-1\ge v\), \(\sinh v\ge v\) for \(v\ge0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY&=\frac4{e^{4s}-1}\le\frac1s,\\
\operatorname{Var}Y&=\frac{4z}{(1-z)^2}
=\frac1{\sinh^2(2s)}\le\frac1{4s^2},\\
\mathbb EY^2&\le\frac5{4s^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

For the finite law of \(K\), its expectation of every increasing function increases with \(z\). Indeed differentiation with respect to \(\log z\) gives the covariance with \(K\), and for an independent copy \(K'\) this covariance is
\(\tfrac12\mathbb E[(f(K)-f(K'))(K-K')]\ge0\).
At \(z=1\) the four weights sum to 48. Therefore, for \(0<z\le1\),
\[
\mathbb EK\le\frac{23+46+3}{48}=\frac32,
\qquad
\mathbb EK^2\le\frac{23+92+9}{48}=\frac{31}{12}.
\tag{9}
\]
These bounds imply \(\mathbb EK\le3/(2s)\) and \(\mathbb EK^2\le31/(12s^2)\) when \(0<s\le1\). For \(s\ge1\), the defining finite weights give instead
\(\mathbb EK\le72e^{-4s}\) and \(\mathbb EK^2\le124e^{-4s}\).
Both \(s e^{-4s}\) and \(s^2e^{-4s}\) decrease on \([1,\infty)\), so their values there are at most \(e^{-4}\). The elementary bound \(e>8/3\) gives \(e^4>48\). Hence
\(s\mathbb EK\le72e^{-4}<3/2\) and
\(s^2\mathbb EK^2\le124e^{-4}<31/12\).
Thus for every \(s>0\),
\[
\mathbb EK\le\frac3{2s},\qquad
\mathbb EK^2\le\frac{31}{12s^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10) proves the upper mean bound in (2). The elementary inequality \((Y+K)^2\le2Y^2+2K^2\) gives
\(\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2\le(5/2+31/6)s^{-2}=23/(3s^2)<8/s^2\), proving the upper second moment bound.

For the lower mean bound, \(\mu_3(s)\ge\mathbb EY=4/(e^{4s}-1)\). The function \(v/(e^v-1)\) strictly decreases on \(v>0\): the numerator of its derivative is \(e^v(1-v)-1\), which vanishes at zero and has derivative \(-ve^v<0\). Thus for \(0<s\le\log3\),
\[
s\mu_3(s)\ge\frac{4s}{e^{4s}-1}
\ge\frac{4\log3}{3^4-1}=\frac{\log3}{20}>\frac1{20}.
\]
Here \(8/3<e<3\), and hence \(\log3>1\), follow from the exponential series: the first four terms sum to \(8/3\), while \(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\), with strict inequality for \(k\ge3\), gives \(\sum_{k\ge0}1/k!<3\). This completes (2) with exact positive constants and its stated range.

Differentiating the normalized summands gives
\(\frac{d}{ds}Q_s(j)=-4(j-\mu_3(s))Q_s(j)\).
Summing against a polynomial \(f\) proves the covariance identity. In particular, \(F_3'(s)/F_3(s)=-4\mu_3(s)\) and \(\mu_3'(s)=-4\operatorname{Var}_{Q_s}J\), proving the derivative identities in (3). The variance is positive because the masses at both zero and one are positive. Formula (1) shows \(F_3(s)\to+\infty\) as \(s\downarrow0\), which proves the final assertion in (3). Multiplying the unnormalized weights by \(e^{-uaj}\) changes their rate from \(s\) to \(s+ua/4>0\), and the exact normalizing expectation is \(F_3(s+ua/4)/F_3(s)\). This proves (4), including \(u=0\) or \(a=0\).

For (5), the numerator polynomial in (1) lies between 1 and 48 on \([0,1]\). Moreover, for an integer \(\kappa\ge1\),
\(1-z^\kappa=(1-z)(1+z+\cdots+z^{\kappa-1})\le\kappa(1-z)\).
Applying (1) at \(s\) and \(\kappa s\), so that their arguments are \(z\) and \(z^\kappa\), gives the claimed ratio \(1/(48\kappa^4)\).

It remains to prove the partition-factor lower bound from the accepted input with its exact normalization. Set \(\rho=e^{-s}\), and use precisely the predecessor's geometric probability law \(P_\rho(j)=(1-\rho)\rho^j\). Its half-line oriented reflected Hellinger mass at \(j\) is
\(H_s(j)=(2j+1)P_\rho(j)\): there are \(2j+1\) displacements with both endpoints nonnegative, and their log-affine geometric means all equal \(P_\rho(j)\). Accepted fact `609407d6db81b18b` supplies
\[
\mathbb E_{P_\rho}H_s(J)^2
=\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^2P_\rho(j)^3
=(1-\rho)^3F(\rho^3)=L(\rho)\ge\frac8{27}.
\tag{11}
\]
Its third moment under the same geometric law is exactly
\(\mathbb E_{P_\rho}H_s(J)^3=(1-\rho)^4F_3(s)\).
These moments are finite by their exponentially summable expressions. Hölder's inequality, with exponents \(3/2\) and 3 under the probability law \(P_\rho\), gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P_\rho}H_s(J)^2
=\mathbb E_{P_\rho}[(H_s(J)^3)^{2/3}\,1]
\le\bigl(\mathbb E_{P_\rho}H_s(J)^3\bigr)^{2/3}.
\]
Together with (11), this proves
\((1-e^{-s})^4F_3(s)\ge(8/27)^{3/2}\).
The strict comparison with \(1/8\) is exact: after squaring and clearing positive denominators it is the integer inequality \(32768>19683\). Finally \(s\ge\log3\) implies \((1-e^{-s})^4\ge(2/3)^4=16/81\); the comparison \(16/81>(8/27)^{3/2}\), squared and cleared of positive denominators, is \(768>512\). This proves (6) and the deterministic-zero coordinate assertion.
