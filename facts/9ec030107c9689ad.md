## Statement

For every pair of integers \(n,k\ge1\), every \(\kappa\ge1\), and every collection of nonnegative rows \(w_r=(w_{r1},\ldots,w_{rn})\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^n\), \(1\le r\le k\), there exists \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
|I|&\le\kappa\sqrt{nk}+k,\\
\sum_{i\notin I}w_{ri}^2
&\le\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\left(\sum_{i\in I}w_{ri}\right)^2
\qquad(1\le r\le k).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently, in the requested range \(k\le n\), one may take \(C=1\) in \(|I|\le C(\kappa\sqrt{nk}+\kappa^2k)\). The theorem applies, in particular, to strictly positive rational rows. No normalization of the given rows is imposed.

## Proof

Let \(\mathcal R=\{r:w_r\ne0\}\) and \(m=|\mathcal R|\le k\). If \(m=0\), take \(I=\varnothing\). Suppose henceforth that \(m>0\). Zero rows will impose no condition.

We first construct a fractional selection. For \(\xi\in[0,1]^n\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\), define the selected mass \(L_r(\xi)=\sum_iw_{ri}\xi_i\) and the residual energy \(E_r(\xi)=\sum_iw_{ri}^2(1-\xi_i)\ge0\). The residual energy is positive whenever \(\xi\in[0,1)^n\). For \(0\le t<1\), let
\[
f(t)=\int_0^t\frac{u^2}{(1-u)^2}\,du
=\frac{1}{1-t}+2\log(1-t)+t-1,
\qquad
\Phi(\xi)=\frac1{\kappa^2}\sum_{i=1}^nf(\xi_i)
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\log E_r(\xi).
\tag{1}
\]
Here every logarithm is natural. The function \(f\) is nonnegative and strictly increasing on \((0,1)\).

The potential \(\Phi\) attains its minimum in \([0,1)^n\). To check the excluded boundary explicitly, put \(\varepsilon=\min_i(1-\xi_i)>0\). Then \(\sum_i f(\xi_i)\ge f(1-\varepsilon)\), and \(E_r(\xi)\ge\varepsilon\sum_iw_{ri}^2\). Therefore
\[
\Phi(\xi)\ge
\frac{1}{\kappa^2\varepsilon}
+\left(m+\frac2{\kappa^2}\right)\log\varepsilon
-\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa^2}
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\log\left(\sum_iw_{ri}^2\right)
\longrightarrow+\infty
\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\downarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus a sublevel containing \(\xi=0\) is contained in some compact cube \([0,1-\varepsilon_0]^n\). Continuity on that cube gives a minimizer \(\xi^\star\).

If column \(i\) is nonzero in at least one row, the one-sided derivative of \(\Phi\) at \(\xi_i=0\), with the other coordinates fixed, is \(-\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}w_{ri}^2/E_r(\xi)<0\). Hence \(\xi_i^\star>0\). Since \(\xi_i^\star<1\), the partial derivative at the minimum vanishes. If column \(i\) is zero in every row, \(\Phi\) depends on that coordinate only through \(f\), so \(\xi_i^\star=0\). Both cases give the same identity:
\[
\frac{\xi_i^\star}{1-\xi_i^\star}
=\kappa\left(\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
\frac{w_{ri}^2}{E_r(\xi^\star)}\right)^{1/2}
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{3}
\]
All denominators are positive, including in rows with some zero entries.

For each \(r\in\mathcal R\), identity (3) implies \(\xi_i^\star\ge\kappa w_{ri}(1-\xi_i^\star)/\sqrt{E_r(\xi^\star)}\). Multiplying by \(w_{ri}\) and summing gives the pointwise row margin
\[
L_r(\xi^\star)\ge
\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{E_r(\xi^\star)}}
\sum_iw_{ri}^2(1-\xi_i^\star)
=\kappa\sqrt{E_r(\xi^\star)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Squaring (3) after multiplication by \(1-\xi_i^\star\), and then summing in \(i\), also gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i(\xi_i^\star)^2
&=\kappa^2\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
\frac{\sum_iw_{ri}^2(1-\xi_i^\star)^2}{E_r(\xi^\star)}
\le\kappa^2m,\\
\sum_i\xi_i^\star
&\le\sqrt{n\sum_i(\xi_i^\star)^2}
\le\kappa\sqrt{nm}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The first inequality uses \((1-\xi_i^\star)^2\le1-\xi_i^\star\), and the second line is Cauchy--Schwarz.

We now round while retaining every row margin. For \(r\in\mathcal R\), put \(t_r=\sqrt{E_r(\xi^\star)}>0\) and define nonnegative coefficients \(b_{ri}=w_{ri}+\kappa w_{ri}^2/(2t_r)\). Consider the compact nonempty polytope
\[
P=\left\{\eta\in[0,1]^n:
\sum_i b_{ri}\eta_i\ge\sum_i b_{ri}\xi_i^\star
\text{ for every }r\in\mathcal R\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
It contains \(\xi^\star\). Choose a minimizer of \(\sum_i\eta_i\) on \(P\) having as many coordinates in \(\{0,1\}\) as possible; denote it by \(\eta^\star\).

At most \(m\) coordinates of \(\eta^\star\) lie strictly between zero and one. Indeed, if there were more than \(m\), the \(m\) homogeneous equations \(\sum_i b_{ri}\Delta_i=0\), \(r\in\mathcal R\), would have a nonzero solution \(\Delta\) supported on those fractional coordinates. For sufficiently small positive \(a\), both \(\eta^\star+a\Delta\) and \(\eta^\star-a\Delta\) lie in \(P\): their row sums are unchanged. Minimality then forces \(\sum_i\Delta_i=0\), since otherwise one sign decreases the objective. Move in either direction until the first fractional coordinate reaches zero or one. This preserves all row sums and the objective, leaves every previously integral coordinate unchanged, and produces a minimizer with more integral coordinates, a contradiction.

Take \(I=\{i:\eta_i^\star>0\}\), and write \(z_i=1\) for \(i\in I\) and \(z_i=0\) otherwise. Thus \(z\ge\eta^\star\) coordinatewise. The nonnegative coefficients in (6) imply \(z\in P\), and rounding at most \(m\) fractional coordinates upward gives
\[
|I|=\sum_i z_i
\le\sum_i\eta_i^\star+m
\le\sum_i\xi_i^\star+m
\le\kappa\sqrt{nm}+m
\le\kappa\sqrt{nk}+k.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to verify the actual tail inequality rather than only the linear rounding constraints. Fix \(r\in\mathcal R\). From \(z\in P\), the definition of \(b_{ri}\), and \(E_r(\eta)=\sum_iw_{ri}^2-\sum_iw_{ri}^2\eta_i\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_r(z)
&\ge L_r(\xi^\star)
+\frac{\kappa}{2t_r}\bigl(E_r(z)-E_r(\xi^\star)\bigr)\\
&\ge\kappa t_r+
\frac{\kappa}{2t_r}\bigl(E_r(z)-t_r^2\bigr)\\
&=\frac{\kappa}{2t_r}\bigl(t_r^2+E_r(z)\bigr)
\ge\kappa\sqrt{E_r(z)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The second line uses (4); the last inequality is \((t_r-\sqrt{E_r(z)})^2\ge0\), so it also covers \(E_r(z)=0\). Since \(z\) is the indicator of \(I\), \(L_r(z)=\sum_{i\in I}w_{ri}\) and \(E_r(z)=\sum_{i\notin I}w_{ri}^2\). Squaring (8) proves the asserted inequality for this row. Each discarded zero row satisfies it identically. Finally, \(k\le\kappa^2k\) because \(\kappa\ge1\), proving the requested bound with \(C=1\).
