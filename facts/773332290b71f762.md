## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite nonempty, $d\ge1$, and $w_p>0$ with $w_p\ge\sum_j\lambda_jw_j$ for every anchored representation $p=\sum_j\lambda_j p_j$. For $c\in\mathbb R^P$, set $K_p(c)=[c_p-w_p/2,c_p+w_p/2]\times[-1/2,1/2]^{d-1}$. The convex hull of the lifted boxes has exactly the prescribed integer fibers if and only if all anchored representations satisfy
$$
|c_p-\sum_j\lambda_jc_j|\le\frac12(w_p-\sum_j\lambda_jw_j).
$$
Only the extreme anchored representations need be tested. Let $L_0$ contain the extreme rows with $w_p=\sum_j\lambda_jw_j$. The feasible center polyhedron is contained in $\ker L_0$ and contains a relative neighborhood of zero there, so its dimension is $\dim\ker L_0$.

## Proof

In a convex combination at base $p$, group the points from any one lifted box using its convexity. The integer fiber over $p$ is exactly the union of Minkowski averages over anchored coefficients at $p$. Lattice completeness ensures that these are all integer bases. Each average of boxes has the same last $d-1$ intervals, first-coordinate center $\sum_j\lambda_jc_j$ and width $\sum_j\lambda_jw_j$. Requiring both of its endpoints to lie in the specified first-coordinate interval at $p$ gives the displayed inequality. This proves necessity and sufficiency. Each of the two signed inequalities is linear in the coefficients, so the finite extreme-point checks suffice. The zero-slack rows force the stated equations; all other extreme rows have strictly positive slack at zero. There are finitely many, so all of their inequalities hold on a relative neighborhood of zero in the kernel. This proves the dimension statement, including zero or redundant rows.
