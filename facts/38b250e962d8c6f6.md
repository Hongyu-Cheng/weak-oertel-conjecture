## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, and let $q\ge1$ be the maximum affine dimension of a nonempty integer fiber. List all its positive $\mathcal H^q$ integer fibers as $K_i=C_{p_i}$, with $V_i=\mathcal H^q(K_i)$, and let $\mu$ sum their intrinsic $q$-volumes. Given finitely many original closed ambient halfspaces $H_a$ and an integer $t\ge0$, set
$$
U_J=C\cap\bigcap_{a\in J}H_a^c,\qquad
e_i=\min_{|J|\le t}\mathcal H^q((U_J)_{p_i})^{1/q}.
$$
Use these obstacles in the finite sampled convex-majorant program with power $q$.

For an optimal pair and any nonnegative KKT multipliers of its supporting-slope formulation, let $\lambda_i$ denote the obstacle multipliers. At each $\lambda_i>0$, choose one minimizing subset $J_i$ in the obstacle definition and write $c_i^*=V_i-e_i^q$ for its captured volume in that fiber. Put $\rho_i=\lambda_i$ for $q=1$, and for $q>1$ set $\rho_i=\lambda_i/(q e_i^{q-1})$ at positive multipliers and zero elsewhere. For each original cap $a$ put $L_a=\max(\{\rho_i:\lambda_i>0,\ a\in J_i\}\cup\{0\})$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\sum_i\rho_i e_i^q,\\
\sum_i\rho_i V_i-R
 &=\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\rho_i c_i^*
 \le\sum_a L_a\,\mu(H_a).
\end{aligned}
$$
If each original cap has $\mu(H_a)\le B$, the last sum is at most $B\sum_aL_a$. If the caps cover the mixed set and $t\ge1$, then $R\le(1-\gamma_q)\sum_i\rho_i V_i$, where $\gamma_q=(q/(q+1))^q$.

## Proof

The residual roots are nonnegative and the sampled bases are finite and distinct, so the finite residual martingale dual theorem applies. Its obstacle complementarity gives $e_i=h_i$ whenever $\lambda_i>0$, and its zero-height conclusion excludes $h_i=0$ at positive multipliers when $q>1$. Hence every denominator used for $q>1$ is positive. In either case $\rho_i e_i^q=\lambda_i e_i/q$ at positive multipliers and is zero otherwise. The equality $\sum_i\lambda_i e_i=qR$ gives the first identity, and the equality for the captured volumes follows by subtraction.

At each selected contact the captured union has measure at most the sum of the measures of its actual constituent cap traces. Thus the contribution of original label $a$ after weighting is at most
$$
L_a\sum_i\mathcal H^q(\{x\in K_i:(p_i,x)\in H_a\})=L_a\mu(H_a).
$$
Sum over the original labels to obtain the stated bound and its uniform-budget consequence. In the covering case each fiber centroid lies in some original cap. Identify its $q$-dimensional affine span isometrically with $\mathbb R^q$. At $m=V_i$ in 6769574fc1bd9f8d the constant density one gives that centroid, so a proper closed trace containing it captures at least $\gamma_q V_i$. A trace containing the entire fiber gives the same bound directly. Thus the local minimum after at most $t\ge1$ caps has $e_i^q\le(1-\gamma_q)V_i$. Multiply by $\rho_i$ and sum. This argument uses the contact-weighted total exactly as written.
