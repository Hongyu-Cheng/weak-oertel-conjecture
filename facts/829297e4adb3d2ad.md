## Statement

Let
\[
P:=\{z=(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb R^2:
z_1\geq0,\ z_2\geq0,\ z_1+z_2\leq3\}
\]
and
\[
C:=P\times[-1,1]\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(C\) is an exact compact full-dimensional convex body with
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z)=20.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(r=1,2\), every primitive affine lattice slice of codimension \(r\)
has mass strictly below \(2^{-r}W\), while
\[
\mathfrak P(C)=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently neither alternative in
`partial-symmetry-rank-drop-dichotomy` holds for \(C\), for any proposed
absolute constant \(\eta>0\).

## Proof

The triangle \(P\) and interval \([-1,1]\) are compact convex sets with
nonempty interiors in their respective ambient spaces. Their product
\(C\) is therefore a compact full-dimensional convex body in
\(\mathbb R^3\).

The complete positive lattice-fiber support is
\[
\begin{aligned}
D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^2
=\{&
(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),\\
&(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),\\
&(0,2),(1,2),\\
&(0,3)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(z\in D\),
\[
C_z=[-1,1],
\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z)=2,
\tag{5}
\]
whereas \(C_z=\varnothing\) and \(w_z=0\) for \(z\notin D\). There are
ten points in (4), so (2) follows:
\[
W=10\cdot2=20.
\tag{6}
\]

We first eliminate every primitive affine slice. A primitive rank-one
sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^2\) has the form \(\mathbb Zu\) for a primitive
nonzero vector \(u=(u_1,u_2)\in\mathbb Z^2\). Consider any coset
\(a+\mathbb Zu\), and suppose it contains \(N\) points of \(D\). Write
those points as \(a+k u\), and let \(k_{\min}\) and \(k_{\max}\) be the
least and greatest occurring parameters. Since the parameters are
distinct integers,
\[
k_{\max}-k_{\min}\geq N-1.
\tag{7}
\]
Choose \(i\in\{1,2\}\) with \(u_i\neq0\). Every coordinate of every point
of \(D\) lies between \(0\) and \(3\). Hence
\[
3
\geq
\left|(k_{\max}-k_{\min})u_i\right|
\geq
k_{\max}-k_{\min}
\geq N-1.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(N\leq4\). By (5), every primitive affine line has mass at most
\[
4\cdot2=8<10=2^{-1}W.
\tag{9}
\]

For \(r=2\), the only rank-zero sublattice is \(\{0\}\), which is
primitive, and every corresponding affine slice is a singleton. Its
mass is either \(2\) or \(0\). Therefore every such slice has mass at
most
\[
2<5=2^{-2}W.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9) and (10) prove that alternative 1 of the registered
dichotomy fails strictly in every allowed codimension.

It remains to prove (3) with all quantifiers in the definition of
\(\mathfrak P\). Fix arbitrary
\[
L\in\mathbb R^{1\times2},
\qquad
m\in\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z),
\tag{11}
\]
and put
\[
Q_U:=U[-1/2,1/2]^2.
\]
Under the accepted definition, the shear is
\[
T_L(z,x)=(z,x-Lz),
\qquad
K:=T_L(C).
\]
Writing the continuous coordinate of \(K\) as \(y\), (1) gives the exact
description
\[
K=\{(z,y):z\in P,\ |y+Lz|\leq1\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Define the symmetric base core at \(m\) by
\[
B_m:=(P-m)\cap(m-P)
=\{v:m+v\in P,\ m-v\in P\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The un-eroded pair body is
\[
D_{L,m}
=\{(v,y):(m+v,y)\in K,\ (m-v,y)\in K\}.
\]
Make the continuous translation
\[
t:=y+Lm.
\tag{14}
\]
It has Jacobian one. By (12), membership in \(D_{L,m}\) is equivalent to
\[
v\in B_m,
\qquad
|t+Lv|\leq1,
\qquad
|t-Lv|\leq1.
\]
For real numbers \(s,t\),
\[
\max\{|t+s|,|t-s|\}=|t|+|s|.
\]
Thus, in the coordinates \((v,t)\), the pair body is exactly
\[
\widehat D_{L,m}
=\{(v,t):v\in B_m,\ |t|+|Lv|\leq1\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Apply the same translation (14) to the eroded pair body
\[
D^-_{L,m,U}
=\{(v,y):(v+q,y)\in D_{L,m}\ \text{for every }q\in Q_U\}.
\]
Equation (15) gives
\[
\widehat D^-_{L,m,U}
=
\left\{
(v,t):
\begin{array}{l}
v+q\in B_m,\\
|t|+|L(v+q)|\leq1
\end{array}
\text{ for every }q\in Q_U
\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, its base projection is contained in the inner base
erosion
\[
R_{m,U}:=\{v:v+Q_U\subseteq B_m\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Also \(0\in Q_U\), so (16) implies \(|t|\leq1\). Consequently
\[
\widehat D^-_{L,m,U}\subseteq R_{m,U}\times[-1,1].
\tag{18}
\]
This reduction is valid for the arbitrary real shear \(L\) in (11).

We now prove that \(R_{m,U}\) has planar Lebesgue measure zero for every
integer \(m\) and every unimodular \(U\). If \(B_m\neq\varnothing\), then
there is a \(v\) with \(m+v,m-v\in P\). Since their midpoint is \(m\),
convexity of \(P\) gives \(m\in P\). Hence
\[
m\notin P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B_m=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_{m,U}=\varnothing.
\tag{19}
\]

Suppose next that \(m\in\partial P\). There is a nonzero supporting
normal \(a\) such that
\[
P\subseteq\{z:a^\top z\leq a^\top m\}.
\]
For \(v\in B_m\), both \(m+v\) and \(m-v\) lie in \(P\), so
\[
a^\top v\leq0,
\qquad
-a^\top v\leq0.
\]
Therefore \(a^\top v=0\), and \(B_m\) lies in the line \(a^\perp\).
The set \(Q_U\) is a full-dimensional parallelogram of area
\(|\det U|=1\). No translate of it can lie in a line. Thus
\[
m\in\partial P\cap\mathbb Z^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{m,U}=\varnothing.
\tag{20}
\]

The only integer point in the interior of \(P\) is
\[
m_0:=(1,1).
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed, an interior integer point has positive integer coordinates and
coordinate sum strictly less than \(3\), forcing both coordinates to be
\(1\). For \(v=(v_1,v_2)\), the six inequalities
\(m_0\pm v\in P\) reduce exactly to
\[
|v_1|\leq1,
\qquad
|v_2|\leq1,
\qquad
|v_1+v_2|\leq1.
\]
Hence
\[
B_{m_0}
=B
:=
\bigcap_{a\in\mathcal A}\{v:|a^\top v|\leq1\},
\qquad
\mathcal A:=\{e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\}.
\tag{22}
\]

We prove the required unimodular width obstruction. For
\(a\in\mathbb R^2\), symmetry of \(Q_U\) gives its support value
\[
h_{Q_U}(a)
:=\max_{q\in Q_U}a^\top q
=\frac12\|U^\top a\|_1,
\tag{23}
\]
so its width in direction \(a\) is
\[
\operatorname{width}_{Q_U}(a)=\|U^\top a\|_1.
\tag{24}
\]
Let
\[
r_1:=U^\top e_1,
\qquad
r_2:=U^\top e_2.
\]
These are linearly independent integer vectors. If either has
\(\ell_1\)-norm at least \(2\), then the corresponding member of
\(\{e_1,e_2\}\) has the desired property. Otherwise
\[
\|r_1\|_1=\|r_2\|_1=1.
\]
Each \(r_i\) is then a signed coordinate vector. Linear independence
forces them to use different coordinate axes, and therefore
\[
\|U^\top(e_1+e_2)\|_1
=\|r_1+r_2\|_1
=2.
\]
We have proved that for every \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)
there exists \(a\in\mathcal A\) such that
\[
\|U^\top a\|_1\geq2.
\tag{25}
\]

Now let \(v\in R_{m_0,U}\). Then \(v+Q_U\subseteq B\), so (22) and the
symmetry of \(Q_U\) imply, for every \(a\in\mathcal A\),
\[
\max_{q\in Q_U}|a^\top(v+q)|
=|a^\top v|+h_{Q_U}(a)
=|a^\top v|+\frac12\|U^\top a\|_1
\leq1.
\tag{26}
\]
Choose \(a\) from (25). If \(\|U^\top a\|_1>2\), equation (26) is
impossible, so \(R_{m_0,U}=\varnothing\). If
\(\|U^\top a\|_1=2\), equation (26) forces
\[
a^\top v=0.
\]
In that case \(R_{m_0,U}\) is contained in a line. In either case,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_2(R_{m_0,U})=0.
\tag{27}
\]
Together, (19), (20), (21), and (27) prove
\[
\operatorname{vol}_2(R_{m,U})=0
\quad
\text{for every }m\in\mathbb Z^2
\text{ and }U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z).
\tag{28}
\]

By (18) and (28),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_3(\widehat D^-_{L,m,U})=0.
\]
The coordinate change (14) preserves three-dimensional volume, so
\[
V_{L,m,U}
=\operatorname{vol}_3(D^-_{L,m,U})
=0
\tag{29}
\]
for every triple in (11). Since every \(V_{L,m,U}\) is nonnegative,
taking their supremum gives
\[
\mathfrak P(C)
=
\sup_{\substack{
L\in\mathbb R^{1\times2},\ m\in\mathbb Z^2\\
U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)}}
V_{L,m,U}
=0.
\tag{30}
\]
Equations (9), (10), and (30) disprove the registered dichotomy.

Finally, this route counterexample is not a target counterexample. Put
\[
p:=((1,1),0)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R).
\]
Let \(H\) be any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\), and
write
\[
H=\{(z,x):a^\top z+b x\geq c\}.
\]
Containment of \(p\) says \(a^\top(1,1)\geq c\). On the fiber
\(\{(1,1)\}\times[-1,1]\), if \(b=0\), all of the fiber lies in \(H\).
If \(b>0\), its intersection with \(H\) is an interval containing
\([0,1]\); if \(b<0\), it is an interval containing \([-1,0]\). In all
cases the one-dimensional volume contributed by this fiber alone is at
least \(1\). The whole-space halfspace has even larger mass. Therefore
\[
h_S(p)\geq1
=\frac15\,2^{-2}W.
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(1/5\in(0,1/e)\), this particular body satisfies a positive
locked-target-scale bound. The counterexample establishes only the
failure of the proposed partial-symmetry/rank-drop completion bridge.
