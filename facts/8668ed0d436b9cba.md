## Statement

Let \(r,s,T\ge0\) be integers. Let \(D_{\rm core}\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\) be finite and nonempty, let \(h_{\rm core}:D_{\rm core}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) have minimum zero, and let \(q_{\mathbf x}>0\) for every \(\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core}\). Fix a finite real \(\beta\ge0\). For every integer \(0\le U\le T\), define the complete core law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{{\rm core},U}
&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t'): \mathbf x\in D_{\rm core},\ t'\in\mathbb Z,
\ h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)\le t'\le U\rbrace,\\
Z_{\rm core}(U)
&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},U}}q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t'},
\qquad
p_{{\rm core},(\mathbf x,t')}^{(U)}
=\frac{q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t'}}{Z_{\rm core}(U)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For \(i\in[s]\), let \(B_i\ge2\) be an integer and put \(g_i^{(3)}=(0,1,B_i)\) on \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\). Set \(\Gamma(\mathbf y)=\sum_{i=1}^s g_i^{(3)}(y_i)\), with the empty sum equal to zero. Define the combined original law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t):\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core},\
\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s,\ t\in\mathbb Z,
\ h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)+\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)\in A_T}q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t},
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)}=\frac{q_{\mathbf x}e^{-\beta t}}Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
For a finite probability law \(\rho\) in its indicated ambient Euclidean space, let \(\tau_\rho(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\rho(H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed affine halfspaces with nonzero ambient normal, and let \(M_3(\rho)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\rho_{\mathbf z}\tau_\rho(\mathbf z)^3\). The ambient spaces are \(\mathbb R^{r+1}\) for (1) and \(\mathbb R^{r+s+1}\) for (2).

These are exactly the core and combined objects of accepted `6fe9007fe1ba4c4b`, with its three-site \(g_i\) renamed \(g_i^{(3)}\); its \(r,s,T,\beta,D_{\rm core},h_{\rm core},q_{\mathbf x},Z_{\rm core}(U),p_{\rm core}^{(U)},\Gamma,Z,p\) have their same meanings. We additionally allow \(r=0\), using the singleton space \(\mathbb Z^0\). The present \(s\) counts three-site coordinates and is not an auxiliary exponential rate. If these tails are obtained from canonical four-site costs \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,C_{4,i})\), then \(g_i^{(3)}\) means their restriction to \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), without altering \(B_i\), \(\beta\), or \(T\). The locked \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S,W,c_0\), arithmetic \(N\), spatial cost count \(k\), full auxiliary minimizer \(s_\star\), and imbalance second moment \(\sigma^b\) retain their existing meanings.

For \(\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T\), put
$$
\pi_{\mathbf y}
=\frac{e^{-\beta\Gamma(\mathbf y)}Z_{\rm core}(T-\Gamma(\mathbf y))}{Z}
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x,t:\ (\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)\in A_T}}
p_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)}.
\tag{3}
$$
Thus \(\pi\) is the law of the three-site vector \(\mathbf Y\) under the full original law, and its displayed entries sum to one. With \(c_{\rm ext}=1/(8\cdot9^4)=1/52488\), the following bound holds without any hypothesis on the core moments:
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_{\rm ext}\,8^{-s}
\left(
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s\\\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T}}
\pi_{\mathbf y}
M_3\bigl(p_{\rm core}^{(T-\Gamma(\mathbf y))}\bigr)^{1/4}
\right)^4.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular, let \(\mathcal G\subseteq\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace\) be any set, and suppose that, for some \(\kappa>0\), every \(U\in\mathcal G\) satisfies \(M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(U)})\ge\kappa8^{-(r+1)}\). Its retained original mass
$$
\alpha
=\frac1Z
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s\\
\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T,\ T-\Gamma(\mathbf y)\in\mathcal G}}
e^{-\beta\Gamma(\mathbf y)}Z_{\rm core}(T-\Gamma(\mathbf y))
\tag{5}
$$
then gives \(M_3(p)\ge c_{\rm ext}\kappa\alpha^4\,8^{-(r+s+1)}\). No condition is imposed at cutoffs outside \(\mathcal G\), and \(\mathcal G\) need not be an interval or contain every feasible residual cutoff.

For an integer gate \(0\le E_0\le T\), one may take \(\mathcal G=\lbrace T-\Gamma(\mathbf y):\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s,\ \Gamma(\mathbf y)\le E_0\rbrace\). The required core bounds then concern only residual cutoffs at least \(T-E_0\), and (5) is exactly the original probability of \(\Gamma(\mathbf Y)\le E_0\). The theorem includes \(r=0\), \(s=0\), \(T=0\), \(\beta=0\), \(E_0=0\), and \(E_0=T\).

## Proof

All supports are finite, and every core support contains a zero-energy core site at height zero. Thus their normalizers are positive and finite. The combined support contains the same site with \(\mathbf y=\mathbf0\). Define the core tail for integer \(E\ge0\) by
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\rm core}(E)
&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\rm core}}q_{\mathbf x}
\sum_{t=h_{\rm core}(\mathbf x)+E}^{T}e^{-\beta t}\\
&=e^{-\beta E}Z_{\rm core}(T-E)
\qquad(0\le E\le T),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
and set \(A_{\rm core}(E)=0\) for \(E>T\). The identity is the substitution \(t=t'+E\). In particular this is the core tail \(A_{\rm core}\) of `6fe9007fe1ba4c4b`, including its spatial weights, rather than a scalar height tail. Partitioning (2) by \(\mathbf y\) gives \(Z=\sum_{\mathbf y}A_{\rm core}(\Gamma(\mathbf y))\), which proves (3).

We use only the following Boolean assertion of accepted `c13fd517ed177886`: if \(\mathcal D\subseteq2^L\) is a downset on a finite ground set \(L\), then the number \(\mathfrak D(\mathcal D,\mathcal D)\) of ordered disjoint pairs in \(\mathcal D^2\), allowing empty sets, is at least \(|\mathcal D|^{3/2}\). Its ground set is the present \(L\), its downset is the present \(\mathcal D_L(E)\), and its ordered count will be the present \(R_L(E)\). The empty ground set is included.

For \(L\subseteq[s]\), put \(\ell=|L|\), \(\mathbf y_L=\mathbf1_L\), and \(a_i=B_i-1\ge1\). Every three-site vector is uniquely \(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I\), where \(I\subseteq L\); its energy is \(E_L(I)=\ell+\sum_{i\in I}a_i\). For an integer \(\ell\le E\le T\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_L(E)&=\lbrace I\subseteq L:\sum_{i\in I}a_i\le E-\ell\rbrace,
\qquad m_L(E)=|\mathcal D_L(E)|,\\
R_L(E)&=|\lbrace(P,Q)\in\mathcal D_L(E)^2:P\cap Q=\varnothing\rbrace|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Positivity of the increments makes \(\mathcal D_L(E)\) a downset, so \(R_L(E)\ge m_L(E)^{3/2}\). The displacement \(\boldsymbol\delta=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q\) recovers \(P,Q\) from its positive and negative supports. Hence these pairs give \(R_L(E)\) distinct displacements, with a set invariant under negation. Both endpoint energies satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\boldsymbol\delta)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in P}a_i-|Q|\le E,\\
\Gamma(\mathbf y_L-\boldsymbol\delta)
&=\ell+\sum_{i\in Q}a_i-|P|\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Both endpoint vectors belong to the three-site box. All heterogeneous costs remain unchanged.

Fix \(L,E\) as in (7), and let \(U=T-E\). For each of the displacements just constructed, the map
\(\iota_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\mathbf x',t'')=(\mathbf x',\mathbf y_L+\boldsymbol\delta,t''+E)\)
sends \(A_{{\rm core},U}\) into \(A_T\), by (8) and the bounds on \(t''\). Distinct displacements give disjoint copies. At every atom of a copy the original mass equals
$$
p_{\iota_{\boldsymbol\delta}(\mathbf x',t'')}
=\frac{A_{\rm core}(E)}Z\,
p_{{\rm core},(\mathbf x',t'')}^{(U)}.
\tag{9}
$$
For \(r\ge1\), the following argument is the core-copy estimate (14) of accepted `6fe9007fe1ba4c4b` with exactly these objects. We give the argument also for \(r=0\).

Take an original root \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)\) with \((\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},U}\). Write a closed containing halfspace as
\(H=\lbrace(\mathbf x',\mathbf y',t''): \boldsymbol\nu_x\cdot\mathbf x'+\boldsymbol\nu_y\cdot\mathbf y'+\nu_t t''\ge b_H\rbrace\)
with nonzero ambient normal. Negation symmetry gives at least \(R_L(E)/2\) displacements with \(\boldsymbol\nu_y\cdot\boldsymbol\delta\ge0\). In each corresponding copy, all core atoms satisfying
\(\boldsymbol\nu_x\cdot(\mathbf x'-\mathbf x)+\nu_t(t''-t')\ge0\)
map into \(H\). If \((\boldsymbol\nu_x,\nu_t)\ne0\), this condition defines a closed core halfspace through \((\mathbf x,t')\) and has core mass at least its core depth. If the core normal is zero, it defines the entire core space and has mass one, again at least its depth. Summing the disjoint copies using (9), and taking the infimum over \(H\), proves
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)
\ge\frac{R_L(E)}2\frac{A_{\rm core}(E)}Z\,
\tau_{p_{\rm core}^{(T-E)}}(\mathbf x,t').
\tag{10}
$$
This uses the given core weights without any independence assumption. It applies when the support has lower affine rank, and every boundary inequality is weak.

The zero displacement in (9) gives the exact sampling mass of the root. Cube (10), multiply by that mass, and sum over the core support. This gives the variable-moment estimate
$$
\sum_{(\mathbf x,t')\in A_{{\rm core},T-E}}
p_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)}
\tau_p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y_L,t'+E)^3
\ge
\frac{R_L(E)^3 A_{\rm core}(E)^4}{8Z^4}
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-E)}).
\tag{11}
$$
There is one full original normalizer factor for root sampling and three for depth. In particular no residual core normalizer replaces any of the four factors of \(Z\).

Let \(\mathcal E_L=\lbrace E_L(I):I\subseteq L,\ E_L(I)\le T\rbrace\), and define the weighted stock, distinct from the unweighted \(V_L\) of `6fe9007fe1ba4c4b`, by
$$
V_L^{\rm wt}=\max_{E\in\mathcal E_L}
R_L(E)^{3/4} A_{\rm core}(E)
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-E)})^{1/4},
\tag{12}
$$
with \(V_L^{\rm wt}=0\) if \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty. Select one maximizing threshold per nonempty label. Different labels have different binary tail roots \(\mathbf y_L\); within a label only one threshold is used. Thus their sampled root sets are disjoint. Equation (11) and nonnegativity of all other sampled contributions imply
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{8Z^4}\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}(V_L^{\rm wt})^4.
\tag{13}
$$
Copy atoms used to estimate different roots' depths may overlap, which does not duplicate any sampled root in (13).

For a label \(L\) with \(\ell\le T\), list all subsets with \(E_L(I)\le T\) as \(I_1,\ldots,I_{m_L(T)}\) in nondecreasing order of energy, breaking ties arbitrarily. At the energy of the \(j\)-th subset, the downset includes all first \(j\) subsets, even in a tie. Equations (7) and (12) therefore give
$$
V_L^{\rm wt}\ge j^{9/8} A_{\rm core}(E_L(I_j))
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-E_L(I_j))})^{1/4}
\qquad(1\le j\le m_L(T)).
\tag{14}
$$
If \(\ell>T\), there are no feasible subsets and this list is empty; in that case no value of \(m_L(T)\) outside its defining range is needed. The series estimate
\(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-9/8}\le1+\int_1^\infty x^{-9/8}\,dx=9\)
implies, for every label,
$$
\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq L\\E_L(I)\le T}}
A_{\rm core}(E_L(I))
M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-E_L(I))})^{1/4}
\le9V_L^{\rm wt}.
\tag{15}
$$
Crucially, (14) does not require the core moment to be monotone in its cutoff. The Boolean downset counts all earlier source energies, while its stock uses the core moment at the single selected threshold.

Use the unique tail-vector representation and (3), then sum (15) and apply Hölder on the \(2^s\) labels. The result is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left(
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf y\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^s\\\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T}}
\pi_{\mathbf y}M_3(p_{\rm core}^{(T-\Gamma(\mathbf y))})^{1/4}
\right)^4\\
&\qquad\le\frac{9^4}{Z^4}
\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}V_L^{\rm wt}\right)^4
\le\frac{9^4\,8^s}{Z^4}\sum_{L\subseteq[s]}(V_L^{\rm wt})^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
Combining (13) and (16) proves (4).

On the cutoffs in \(\mathcal G\), the fourth root of the core moment is at least \(\kappa^{1/4}8^{-(r+1)/4}\). The corresponding \(\pi\)-mass is exactly (5). Restricting the nonnegative sum in (4) to those vectors and taking fourth powers proves the stated \(c_{\rm ext}\kappa\alpha^4\,8^{-(r+s+1)}\) bound. This restriction allows an arbitrary good cutoff set; it imposes no certificates on the other cutoffs. For the gate \(E_0\), its retained vectors are precisely those with \(\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le E_0\), so its probability and residual-cutoff range are exactly as stated.

When \(s=0\), the empty label and the empty tail vector are the only ones. The combined law is the core law at \(T\), \(\pi=1\), and every step above is valid, though (4) then gives a weaker bound than this identity. When \(r=0\), the core spatial set is the singleton in \(\mathbb Z^0\), and the core halfspace argument takes place in the one height coordinate; the zero-core-normal case was explicitly covered. When \(T=0\), only the empty tail vector is feasible, and core sites of energy zero at height zero are retained with their given weights. If \(\beta=0\), every exponential is one and no step changes. The gate endpoints \(E_0=0\) and \(E_0=T\) retain respectively the zero tail vector and all feasible tail vectors, with \(\alpha=\pi_{\mathbf0}\) and \(\alpha=1\). Empty good sets give \(\alpha=0\) and a valid zero lower bound. Thus every stated zero case, cutoff endpoint, tie, and support or halfspace boundary is included.
