## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be convex bodies at the only integer bases $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use planar area on each fiber. Fix $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and nonzero spanning vectors $v_1,v_2,v_3$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. With $h_i=\max_{x\in B}v_i\cdot x$, assume that each closed ambient cap
$$
v_i\cdot(x-y)+(v_i\cdot y-h_i)z\ge0
$$
attains the actual mixed halfspace depth $Q$ at $(0,y)$ and that its cuts on $A$ and $K$ are proper, meaning that the cutting lines meet the interiors. Its trace on $B$ is a supporting face, so $Q=U_i+q_i$, where $U_i$ is the cap area on $A$ and $q_i=|K\cap\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}|$. Let $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $b=|B|$, $M=a+k+b$, and $D=|\det(v_1,v_2)|$. Write $a_i,k_i$ for the lengths of the respective $A,K$ chords divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, and set $s_i=k_i+2a_i$, $\lambda_i=1/s_i$, $\Lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i$, $p_i=\lambda_i/\Lambda$, and $t_i=a_i/s_i$.


Let $J$ be a quarter turn. Both endpoint distances of the $i$-th middle chord in the parameter $x=y+uJv_i$ lie between $s_i(1-4t_i+6t_i^2)/2$ and $s_i(1/2-3t_i^2)$, with $0<t_i\le1/3$. For each permutation of $i,j,\ell$,
$$
\frac{p_\ell}{1-2t_\ell}\le
\frac{p_i}{1-4t_i+6t_i^2}+\frac{p_j}{1-4t_j+6t_j^2},
\qquad p_\ell<\frac34.
$$
Write $Z_i=K\cap\{x:v_j\cdot(x-y)<0,\ v_\ell\cdot(x-y)<0\}$. If $p_\ell>3p_i$, then
$$
|Z_i|\Lambda^2<\frac{D}{8p_\ell(p_\ell-3p_i)}.
$$
In particular, if all $q_i\le ck$ for some $c<1/2$, put $\epsilon=1-2c$. Then $k\Lambda^2<D/[8\epsilon p_\ell(p_\ell-3p_*)]$ whenever $p_\ell>3p_*$, where $p_*=\min(p_i,p_j)$.

For distinct $i,j,\ell$ satisfying $p_\ell>p_i+3p_j$ and $p_\ell>p_j+3p_i$, put $p_*=\min(p_i,p_j)$ and
$$
T_i=\frac{2-\sqrt{6p_i/(p_\ell-3p_j)-2}}6,\qquad
T_j=\frac{2-\sqrt{6p_j/(p_\ell-3p_i)-2}}6,\qquad
X=\frac{4p_\ell(p_\ell-3p_*)T_iT_j}{p_ip_j}.
$$

In the domain of these two stronger weight inequalities, $T_i,T_j\in(0,1/3)$ and $a\Lambda^2>DT_iT_j/(2p_ip_j)$. Under the additional cap bound $q_i\le ck$ for all three indices, $a/k>\epsilon X$.

## Proof

The assumptions of ba445d671279810a hold with the original $A,K,B$, the mark $y$, and the same cap on each index. Indeed the cutting thresholds on $A,K,B$ are $2v_i\cdot y-h_i$, $v_i\cdot y$, and $h_i$, respectively. In the original geometry that theorem gives $k_i\ge a_i$ and the middle-chord center estimate $|c_i|\le a_i(k_i-a_i)/s_i$ in the parameter $u$. Its two endpoint distances are $k_i/2\pm c_i$. Using $a_i=s_it_i$ and $k_i=s_i(1-2t_i)$ gives the stated exact endpoint estimates. As $f(t)=1-4t+6t^2$ decreases from $1$ to $1/3$ on $[0,1/3]$, every endpoint distance is at least $s_i/6$ and strictly less than $s_i/2$. Equality in the lower coarse estimate occurs exactly when $t_i=1/3$, in which case $k_i=a_i$ and the center estimate forces $c_i=0$.

The gauge $N$ of the centrally symmetric body $(K-y)\cap(y-K)$ is a norm by convexity and interiority. Its value $N(Jv_i)$ is the reciprocal of the nearer endpoint distance in the parameter $u$, which is at most $k_i/2$. Thus
$$
\frac{2\lambda_i}{1-2t_i}\le N(Jv_i)\le\frac{2\lambda_i}{f(t_i)}.
$$
Using $Jv_\ell=-Jv_i-Jv_j$ in this one norm and dividing by $2\Lambda$ gives the displayed gauge inequality. Its left side is strictly larger than $p_\ell$ since $t_\ell>0$, and its right side is at most $3(p_i+p_j)$.

For the sector estimate, relabel the normals so the desired indices $i,\ell$ are $1,3$. All pairs are independent and have the same absolute determinant $D$, by $\sum_i v_i=0$. Let $L$ be the linear map with rows $v_1^{\mathsf T},v_2^{\mathsf T}$, and take coordinates $L(x-y)$. The transformed normals $\widehat v_r=L^{-\mathsf T}v_r$ are $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$. Each planar area is multiplied by $D$. Since $LJL^{\mathsf T}=(\det L)J$, a chord parameter in direction $Jv_r$ becomes the same parameter multiplied by $\det L$ in direction $J\widehat v_r$. Thus normalized chord lengths and $s_r$ are multiplied by $D$, $\Lambda$ is divided by $D$, and $p_r,t_r$ are invariant. The affine map leaves $z$ unchanged and carries the actual cap inequalities to the transformed inequalities, with $h_i-v_i\cdot y$ as the new support value. It is a bijection on ambient halfspaces and rescales all their masses by $D$.

In canonical coordinates the sector $Z_1$ is the one from the sector lemma. In these coordinates let $\Lambda'$ be the transformed $\Lambda$. The two endpoints in that lemma obey $\beta\ge 1/(6p_1\Lambda')$ and $\rho<1/(2p_3\Lambda')$. If $p_3>3p_1$, then $\rho/\beta<3p_1/p_3<1$. By the sector lemma, its transformed area is at most $\rho^2/[2(1-\rho/\beta)]$, which is strictly smaller than $1/[8(\Lambda')^2p_3(p_3-3p_1)]$. Returning to the original areas yields the factor $D$. This argument applies to every permutation by choosing the coordinate rows after relabeling. When $p_\ell=3p_i$, the exact endpoints still give $\beta>\rho$ and the geometric bound in terms of $\beta,\rho$ still applies. The displayed bound using only weights has zero denominator there.

Let $H_r^K=\{x:v_r\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$. Inclusion-exclusion gives $|Z_i|\ge k-q_j-q_\ell$. Under the cap-fraction hypothesis this is at least $\epsilon k$. Selecting the sector for the index of the smaller weight gives the upper bound on $k\Lambda^2$.

For the lower scale, use $p_\ell/(1-2t_\ell)>p_\ell$ and $p_j/f(t_j)\le3p_j$ to get $p_\ell-3p_j<p_i/f(t_i)$. In the stated domain, $1/3\le f(t_i)<p_i/(p_\ell-3p_j)<1$. The root defining $T_i$ is therefore real and lies strictly between $0$ and $1/3$. Inverting the decreasing $f$ gives $t_i>T_i$, with $t_j>T_j$ by interchanging $i,j$. At a boundary such as $p_\ell=p_i+3p_j$, the corresponding root is $T_i=0$ and yields no positive lower bound for that index.

For the area estimate on $A$, use coordinates $(v_i\cdot x,v_j\cdot x)$ with area factor $D$. The two cutting chords become vertical and horizontal segments of lengths $Da_i$ and $Da_j$. If the vertical segment straddles the horizontal line, the two triangles with the horizontal base and the vertical endpoints as apices have disjoint interiors and total area $D^2a_ia_j/2$. If the vertical segment lies on one side, its farther endpoint has height at least its length, so one triangle suffices. This includes endpoint contact and does not require the chords to intersect. The transformed $A$ contains these triangles. Transforming the area back gives $a\ge Da_ia_j/2$. Substituting $a_r=t_r/(p_r\Lambda)$ gives the strict lower scale. The stronger weight inequalities imply $p_\ell>3p_*$, so dividing the two scales proves $a/k>\epsilon X$.
