## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the following canonical notation from accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70:
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
\]
\[
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),\qquad
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z.
\]
Call \(\xi\) good when \(A_\xi>0\) and \(\max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\le A_\xi/4\), and define the half-strength canonical product reservoir on unordered distinct pairs by
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi},&
 u,v\equiv\xi\ \text{and }\xi\text{ is good},\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad Q:=|r|.
\]
Assume the hypotheses of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, in particular \(W>0\) and \(G<W/4\), and use its load-minimal optimizer, with realizable-submatching capacity \(R\), load \(\Omega\), endpoint loads \(\beta_z\), points \(p_z^\flat\), and common origin-symmetric body \(D_*\). For a positive-volume bounded measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<m\le\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), use
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{\frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\le f\le1\ {\rm a.e.},\quad \int_Kf=m\right\}.
\]
Thus, whenever \(\beta_z>0\),
\[
 p_z^\flat+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq \mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z),
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathsf M_m\) is the canonical metronoid used in accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 1c54f5dc74c15059.

For each retained parity class \(\xi\), put
\[
 L_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}\beta_z,
 \qquad
 s_{\{u,v\}}:=\frac{\beta_u\beta_v}{L_\xi}
 \quad(u<v,\ u,v\equiv\xi),
\tag{2}
\]
where retention means
\[
 \max_{z\equiv\xi}\beta_z\le\frac{L_\xi}{4},
 \qquad
 L_\xi\ge\frac{A_\xi}{64}.
\]
Put \(s_e=0\) elsewhere. For a positive edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},\qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
For every retained label \((\xi,c)\), put
\[
 S_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
 \qquad
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ:=\frac1{S_{\xi,c}}
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_eF_e^\flat
 \quad(S_{\xi,c}>0),
\tag{4}
\]
and define
\[
 I:=\sum_{\xi,c}\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}
 s_e\mathbf1_{\{\|F_e^\flat-q_{\xi,c}^\circ\|_{D_*}>1/4\}},
\tag{5}
\]
\[
 O:=\inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{\xi,c}S_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}}.
\tag{6}
\]
The matching denoted \(s^{\mathrm{ret}}\) in the research reply is exactly the matching \(s\) in (2); there is no rescaling. The symbol \(G(C)\) in accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c is exactly \(G\) above, and its fiber masses and total mass are exactly \(a_z\) and \(W\). The metronoid \(\mathsf M_m\) in accepted fact 1c54f5dc74c15059 is exactly the metronoid in (1); none of that fact's separate matching symbols \(q^\times,\omega\) is used here.

Use the canonical total capacity
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+2^nh_{\max}(C)
\tag{7}
\]
with exactly the normalizations of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, and assume
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{8}
\]
Then \(n\ge2\), \(\Omega>0\), and \(\max\{I,O\}>Q/4\).

For a retained class \(\xi\), let \(\mathcal T_\xi\) be the family of labels
\[
 \tau=(z;\{a,b\})
\tag{9}
\]
such that \(z,a,b\in Z\) are distinct, all lie in \(\xi\), \(\beta_z\beta_a\beta_b>0\), the pair \(\{a,b\}\) is unordered, and \(z,a,b\) are not collinear. Define
\[
 \theta_\tau:=\frac{\beta_z\beta_a\beta_b}{2L_\xi^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
The two input edges of \(\tau\) are \(\{z,a\}\) and \(\{z,b\}\), and its opposite edge is \(\{a,b\}\). Put
\[
 t_{\{a,b\}}:=
 \sum_{\substack{z:\ (z;\{a,b\})\in\mathcal T_\xi}}
 \theta_{z;\{a,b\}},
 \qquad
 P_\triangle:=\sum_et_e=\sum_\tau\theta_\tau.
\tag{11}
\]
Then the \(\theta_\tau\) form a feasible transverse line-graph pairing with input capacities \(s\), and
\[
 0\le t_e\le\frac12s_e,
 \qquad
 P_\triangle>\frac{8397}{51200}Q.
\tag{12}
\]
There is an explicit exceptional edge family \(\mathcal B\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal B}s_e\le G,
 \qquad
 t_e\ge\frac18s_e\quad(e\notin\mathcal B).
\tag{13}
\]

If \(I>Q/4\), let
\[
 \mathcal E_I:=\{e:\|F_e^\flat-q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ\|_{D_*}>1/4\},
\tag{14}
\]
where \(\xi\) is the retained class containing \(e\), and retain the triangles whose opposite edge lies in \(\mathcal E_I\). Their total mass satisfies
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\tau:\operatorname{opp}(\tau)\in\mathcal E_I}}
 \theta_\tau>\frac{3Q}{100}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every such \(\tau=(z;\{a,b\})\), put \(e=\{a,b\}\), \(\theta=\theta_\tau\), and define the integral affine rank-two chart
\[
 \Phi_\tau(j,k):=z+j\frac{a-z}{2}+k\frac{b-z}{2}
 \qquad((j,k)\in\{0,1\}^2)
\tag{16}
\]
and the four points
\[
 X_{00}:=p_z^\flat,\qquad
 X_{10}:=F_{\{z,a\}}^\flat,\qquad
 X_{01}:=F_{\{z,b\}}^\flat,\qquad
 X_{11}:=q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ.
\tag{17}
\]
They are actual common-mass metronoid realizations,
\[
 X_{jk}\in\mathsf M_\theta(C_{\Phi_\tau(j,k)})
 \qquad((j,k)\in\{0,1\}^2),
\tag{18}
\]
and their mixed second difference obeys
\[
 \left\|X_{00}-X_{10}-X_{01}+X_{11}\right\|_{D_*}>\frac14.
\tag{19}
\]

If \(O>Q/4\), define
\[
 T_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}t_e
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
 O_\triangle:=
 \inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{\xi,c}T_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}},
\tag{21}
\]
where only positive \(T_{\xi,c}\) are included. Then
\[
 0<T_{\xi,c}\le\frac12S_{\xi,c},\qquad
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2T_{\xi,c}}(C_c),
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
 O_\triangle>\frac{3Q}{100}.
\tag{23}
\]
Every summand of \(T_{\xi,c}\) is the sum of the explicit triangle masses (10) whose opposite edge has label \((\xi,c)\). Hence (22)--(23) concern actual conditioned outputs backed by the canonical triangle construction, not an abstract stress system.

## Proof

All summands in (7) are nonnegative. Thus (8) gives
\[
 R<\frac Q{100},\qquad G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{24}
\]
From the reservoir formula in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, the reservoir mass in a good parity class is
\[
 \frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}
 \le\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 Q\le\frac W8.
\tag{25}
\]
If \(n=1\), positivity of \(Q\) gives at least two positive-volume fibers. Apply accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c to the affine line \(\mathbb R\). Its rank-one bound gives \(G\ge W/4\ge2Q\), contradicting (8). Therefore \(n\ge2\). The zero-load conclusion of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 is \(R=Q\), also contradicting (8); hence \(\Omega>0\). Its hard alternative then gives \(\max\{I,O\}>Q/4\).

The same accepted fact gives
\[
 \Omega\ge Q-R,
 \qquad
 Q\ge\frac3{32}(W-4G),
 \qquad
 \sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{retained}}L_\xi
 \ge2\Omega-G-\frac W{64}.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (24) and (26) imply
\[
 W\le\frac{32}{3}Q+4G<\frac{803}{75}Q
\tag{27}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \sum_{\xi\ \mathrm{retained}}L_\xi
 >\frac{198}{100}Q-\frac1{100}Q-\frac{803}{4800}Q
 =\frac{8653}{4800}Q.
\tag{28}
\]
Since accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives \(|s|_\xi\ge3L_\xi/8\),
\[
 |s|>\frac{8653}{12800}Q.
\tag{29}
\]

We next record the line-mass estimate needed below. Let \(\ell\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be an affine line containing two points of \(Z\), and put
\[
 W_\ell:=\sum_{z\in Z\cap\ell}a_z.
\]
Apply accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c to \(A=\ell\). Its intrinsic rank is one, and, since \(n\ge2\), its affine hull is proper. Equation (15) of that accepted fact gives
\[
 G\ge
 \min\left\{\frac{W_\ell}{4}+\frac{W-W_\ell}{2},
 2^{n-3}W_\ell\right\}.
\tag{30}
\]
The first entry in the minimum is at least \(W/4\), while (24)--(25) give \(G<W/4\). Hence
\[
 W_\ell\le2^{3-n}G.
\tag{31}
\]
Also, from the reservoir degrees in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70,
\[
 \beta_z\le d_z(r)
 =\frac{a_z}{4}\left(1-\frac{a_z}{A_\xi}\right)
 \le\frac{a_z}{4}.
\tag{32}
\]

Fix a retained class and abbreviate \(L=L_\xi\). For an input edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), the triangle variables using \(e\) at the endpoint \(u\) have total mass at most
\[
 \sum_w\frac{\beta_u\beta_v\beta_w}{2L^2}
 \le\frac{\beta_u\beta_v}{2L}
 =\frac12s_e.
\tag{33}
\]
The same estimate holds at \(v\). Thus the total use of \(e\) is at most \(s_e\), proving feasibility.

For a positive edge \(e=\{a,b\}\), let \(\ell(a,b)\) be its affine line and put
\[
 B_{\ell(a,b)}:=
 \sum_{z\equiv\xi,\ z\in\ell(a,b)}\beta_z.
\]
Directly from (10)--(11),
\[
 t_{\{a,b\}}
 =\frac{s_{\{a,b\}}}{2}
 \left(1-\frac{B_{\ell(a,b)}}{L}\right),
\tag{34}
\]
so \(0\le t_e\le s_e/2\). If collinear centers were also allowed, but the two endpoints were still excluded, the corresponding output weight would be
\[
 \frac{s_{\{a,b\}}}{2}
 \left(1-\frac{\beta_a+\beta_b}{L}\right)
 \ge\frac14s_{\{a,b\}},
\tag{35}
\]
because retention gives \(\beta_a,\beta_b\le L/4\).

For every line through two positive \(\beta\)-atoms, (31)--(32) give
\[
 B_\ell\le\frac{W_\ell}{4}\le2^{1-n}G.
\tag{36}
\]
The total collinear loss in this retained class is at most
\[
 \frac{2^{1-n}G}{2L^2}
 \sum_{a<b}\beta_a\beta_b
 \le2^{-n-1}G.
\tag{37}
\]
There are at most \(2^n\) retained parity classes. Summing (35)--(37), and then using (24) and (29), gives
\[
 P_\triangle
 \ge\frac14|s|-\frac12G
 >\left(\frac{8653}{51200}-\frac1{200}\right)Q
 =\frac{8397}{51200}Q.
\tag{38}
\]

Call a line in a retained class dominant if \(B_\ell>3L/4\). There is at most one dominant line in a class. Indeed, two distinct lines intersect in at most one point, whose \(\beta\)-mass is at most \(L/4\), so their masses would satisfy both
\[
 B_{\ell_1}+B_{\ell_2}>\frac{3L}{2}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 B_{\ell_1}+B_{\ell_2}\le L+\frac L4,
\]
a contradiction. Let \(\mathcal B\) consist of all edges lying on the dominant line of their class, when it exists. In one class, (36) gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\ell\\e\in\mathcal B}}s_e
 \le\frac{B_\ell^2}{2L}
 \le\frac{B_\ell}{2}
 \le2^{-n}G.
\tag{39}
\]
Summing over the parity classes proves the first inequality in (13). If \(e\notin\mathcal B\), then \(B_{\ell(e)}\le3L/4\), and (34) proves the second inequality in (13).

Assume first that \(I>Q/4\). Equations (5), (13), and (14) give
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_I}t_e
 \ge\frac18\left(I-\sum_{e\in\mathcal B}s_e\right)
 \ge\frac18(I-G)
 >\frac{3Q}{100}.
\tag{40}
\]
By (11), the left side of (40) is exactly the left side of (15).

Fix one retained triangle \(\tau=(z;\{a,b\})\) in this subfamily. Because its three bases lie in one parity class, the two vectors \((a-z)/2,(b-z)/2\) are integral; noncollinearity makes them linearly independent. Thus (16) is an integral affine rank-two chart. Moreover,
\[
 \theta_\tau\le
 \min\{\beta_z,\beta_a,\beta_b,
 s_{\{z,a\}},s_{\{z,b\}},s_{\{a,b\}}\}.
\tag{41}
\]
For the three \(\beta\)-bounds this follows directly from (10), and for an edge, for example,
\[
 \frac{\theta_{z;\{a,b\}}}{s_{\{a,b\}}}
 =\frac{\beta_z}{2L}\le\frac18;
\]
the other two edge bounds are identical.

By (1), scaling a representing density shows that each \(p_y^\flat\), \(y\in\{z,a,b\}\), lies in \(\mathsf M_{\theta_\tau}(C_y)\). The positive-root metronoid interpolation argument proved in accepted fact 1c54f5dc74c15059 then applies to each relevant pair of these equal-mass realizations and gives
\[
 F_{\{z,a\}}^\flat\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_\tau}(C_{(z+a)/2}),\qquad
 F_{\{z,b\}}^\flat\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_\tau}(C_{(z+b)/2}),
\tag{42}
\]
and also
\[
 F_{\{a,b\}}^\flat\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_\tau}(C_{(a+b)/2}).
\tag{43}
\]
Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 gives
\[
 q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,m_e}}(C_{m_e}).
\tag{44}
\]
Since \(e=\{a,b\}\) occurs in that label and (41) gives \(\theta_\tau\le s_e\le S_{\xi,m_e}\), scaling a density in (44) gives
\[
 q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_\tau}(C_{m_e}).
\tag{45}
\]
Equations (16)--(17), (42), and (45) prove (18). Finally,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_{00}-X_{10}-X_{01}+X_{11}
 &=p_z^\flat-\frac{p_z^\flat+p_a^\flat}{2}
 -\frac{p_z^\flat+p_b^\flat}{2}+q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ\\
 &=q_{\xi,m_e}^\circ-F_e^\flat.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{46}
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal E_I\) proves (19).

Assume now that \(O>Q/4\). Equation (12) gives
\[
 0\le T_{\xi,c}\le\frac12S_{\xi,c}.
\tag{47}
\]
When \(T_{\xi,c}>0\), scale every representing density for every point in the inclusion
\[
 q_{\xi,c}^\circ+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c)
\]
by \(T_{\xi,c}/S_{\xi,c}\le1/2\). This preserves its normalized first moment and proves (22).

Fix an affine map \(a\), and let \(E(a)\) be the union of the edge labels \((\xi,c)\) for which
\[
 \|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>\frac14.
\]
Then (13) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\xi,c}T_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}}
 &=\sum_{e\in E(a)}t_e\\
 &\ge\frac18\left(\sum_{e\in E(a)}s_e-G\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(a\) gives
\[
 O_\triangle\ge\frac18(O-G)>\frac{3Q}{100},
\tag{49}
\]
which proves (23). Finally, (11) and (20) show directly that every \(T_{\xi,c}\) is the sum of the stated triangle masses. This completes all assertions.
