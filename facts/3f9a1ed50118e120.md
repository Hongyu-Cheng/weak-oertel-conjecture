## Statement

For a compact convex body $B\subset\mathbb R^d$ with nonempty interior, $d\ge1$, write $A(B)$ for the maximum over $g\in B$ of the minimum $d$-volume of a closed halfspace trace on $B$ through $g$. If $K,L$ are such bodies and $K\subsetneq L$, then $A(K)<A(L)$.

## Proof

Depth at a given mark is monotone under inclusion. Let $g$ maximize depth in $K$. Such a point exists by continuity of cap volume in the mark and direction and compactness of $K$ and the sphere. Assume $A(K)=A(L)$. Then the depth of $g$ in $L$ is between these two equal values, so $g$ is also an actual maximizer in $L$.

Apply the exact active-normal cover in fact 755cb08c379113cc to the compatible one-base family $\{0\}\times L$ in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$. The anchored capacity in direction $u$ is exactly the ordinary cap volume, since the base-slope term is zero at the only base. Thus finitely many ordinary closed halfspaces $H_i$ through $g$ cover $\mathbb R^d$ and satisfy $|L\cap H_i|=A(L)$. For each $i$,
$A(K)\le |K\cap H_i|\le |L\cap H_i|=A(K)$.
Both traces are compact convex bodies of positive volume, because an interior ball gives $A(K)>0$. Their inclusion and equal volumes force their equality. Indeed, given a point in the larger body outside the smaller closed body, joining it to an interior point of the larger gives interior points still outside the smaller, hence a positive volume difference. Taking unions over the cover gives $K=L$, a contradiction.
