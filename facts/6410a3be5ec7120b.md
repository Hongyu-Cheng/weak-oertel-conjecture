## Statement

Use \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}>0\) for the rank-budget prefactor, exactly the assignment's \(B\) and the prefactor in accepted fact `0ea9778d4ccdcc84`. Our piece name \(E_i\) is the alias for that fact's piece name \(D_i\); its intrinsic rank is \(r_i:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E_i)\), exactly the piece rank used there. Integer affine charts have the same meaning as in `0ea9778d4ccdcc84` and `8db3ba8c8761ea6c`: \(\Phi_i(\mathbf y)=\mathbf v_i+B_i\mathbf y\), with \(\mathbf v_i\in\mathbb Z^n\) and a full-column-rank matrix \(B_i\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r_i}\). Here saturation is not required.

An interval-box piece of positive rank \(r_i\) means
\[
E_i=\Phi_i\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r_i}\{0,\ldots,L_{i,j}\}\right),
\qquad L_{i,j}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1},
\quad B_i\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r_i},
\quad \operatorname{rank}B_i=r_i.
\]
The exact interval-length alias to `8db3ba8c8761ea6c` is that its coordinate length \(m_j\) is \(L_{i,j}+1\), and its intrinsic rank \(R\) is this \(r_i\). General integer intervals are covered by absorbing their lower endpoints into \(\mathbf v_i\); singleton factors can be deleted. The radius \(R\) below is a new radius parameter, not that predecessor's rank \(R\).

A standard-simplex piece of positive rank \(r_i\) means \(E_i=\Phi_i(D_{m_i,r_i})\), where \(m_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\) and
\[
D_{m,r}:=\left\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{r}:\sum_{j=1}^{r}y_j\le m\right\}.
\]
This is exactly the support \(D_{m,r}\) of accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`, with its dilation \(m=m_i\) and rank \(r=r_i\). In either family, a rank-zero piece means an integer singleton. Since the positive-rank domains contain \(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_{r_i}\), their image ranks are exactly the specified \(r_i\). These definitions include all saturated charts, as well as arbitrary nonsaturated charts of full column rank.

For every \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}>0\) and \(\delta>0\), specify the even dimension and integer dilation threshold by
\[
\begin{aligned}
n&:=2\left\lceil\frac{8B_{\mathrm{bud}}}{\delta}\right\rceil,\\
R_0&:=n^2+1+
\left\lceil
\frac{4B_{\mathrm{bud}}}{\delta}
(n^2+2^n)3^{n-1}\left(\frac n6\right)^{n/2}
\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every integer \(R\ge R_0\), let
\[
D_{n,R}:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|\mathbf z\|_2^2\le R^2\},
\qquad
p_{\mathbf z}:=\frac1{|D_{n,R}|}\quad(\mathbf z\in D_{n,R}).
\]
Then \(D_{n,R}\) is finite, nonempty, complete in the sense \(D_{n,R}=\operatorname{conv}(D_{n,R})\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and has affine rank \(n\). Its probability law \(p\) is the original normalized log-affine law with tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell=\mathbf0\), using the normalization of `0ea9778d4ccdcc84`.

For every finite collection of pieces \(E_1,\ldots,E_s\subseteq D_{n,R}\) of the two types above, including the empty collection, if \(\sum_{i=1}^{s}2^{r_i}\le B_{\mathrm{bud}}2^n\), then
\[
p\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}E_i\right)<\delta.
\]
No disjointness assumption is necessary, and no conclusion about an arbitrary certified graph or polytope support family is asserted.

## Proof

Write \(\operatorname{vol}_n\) for ordinary \(n\)-dimensional Euclidean volume. All norms in the proof are Euclidean. The specified \(n\) is a positive even integer, hence \(n\ge2\), and \(R\ge R_0>n^2\).

First verify the support hypotheses. The real closed ball of radius \(R\) is convex, so it contains \(\operatorname{conv}(D_{n,R})\). Thus every integer point of that convex hull belongs to \(D_{n,R}\). The reverse inclusion in completeness is automatic. The support contains \(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\) because \(R\ge1\), so its affine rank is \(n\). Finiteness follows from containment in \([-R,R]^n\), and uniformity is precisely the tilt-zero log-affine formula in the statement. In particular, mass is cardinality divided by \(|D_{n,R}|\), with no change of the root law.

We next prove a useful lower bound on real ball volume without a factorial asymptotic or an external volume formula. A planar disk of radius \(t\) contains its inscribed regular dodecagon. The twelve triangles from its center each have area \(t^2\sin(\pi/6)/2=t^2/4\), so the disk has area at least \(3t^2\). In \(\mathbb R^n\), the Cartesian product of \(n/2\) planar disks of radius \(t\sqrt{2/n}\) lies in the radius-\(t\) ball: the squared norm of any point of the product is at most \((n/2)t^2(2/n)=t^2\). Taking product volumes therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n\{\mathbf x:\|\mathbf x\|_2\le t\}
\ge\left(\frac6n\right)^{n/2}t^n
\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
For every point of the radius-\(R-\sqrt n/2\) ball, choose a nearest integer in each coordinate. The resulting \(\mathbf z\) satisfies \(\|\mathbf x-\mathbf z\|_2\le\sqrt n/2\) and hence \(\mathbf z\in D_{n,R}\). Consequently, the unit cubes centered at points of \(D_{n,R}\) cover this smaller ball. Their interiors are disjoint and each cube has volume one, giving
\[
|D_{n,R}|\ge
\left(\frac6n\right)^{n/2}(R-\sqrt n/2)^n.
\]
Since \(R\ge n^2\ge n\sqrt n\), Bernoulli's inequality gives
\(\bigl(1-\sqrt n/(2R)\bigr)^n\ge1-n\sqrt n/(2R)\ge1/2\). Thus
\[
|D_{n,R}|\ge\frac12\left(\frac6n\right)^{n/2}R^n.
\tag{1}
\]

Consider a full-rank interval-box piece, so \(r_i=n\). Write \(\mathbf b_{i,j}\) for column \(j\) of \(B_i\). Its convex hull is the parallelepiped \(\Phi_i(\prod_j[0,L_{i,j}])\): its vertices belong to the piece, and the parameter box is the convex hull of its vertices. All these vertices have norm at most \(R\). Averaging their squared norms, and canceling all mixed sign terms about their common center, gives
\[
\left\|\mathbf v_i+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^nL_{i,j}\mathbf b_{i,j}\right\|_2^2
+\frac14\sum_{j=1}^nL_{i,j}^2\|\mathbf b_{i,j}\|_2^2
\le R^2.
\]
In particular, \(\sum_j\|L_{i,j}\mathbf b_{i,j}\|_2^2\le4R^2\). The determinant of the edge matrix is bounded by the product of its column norms: this follows, for example, by Gram-Schmidt, since each perpendicular component is no longer than its original column. Applying arithmetic-geometric mean to their squared norms yields
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}E_i)
=|\det B_i|\prod_{j=1}^nL_{i,j}
\le\prod_{j=1}^n\|L_{i,j}\mathbf b_{i,j}\|_2
\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n.
\tag{2}
\]
The two endpoints of each edge belong to the radius-\(R\) ball, so \(L_{i,j}\|\mathbf b_{i,j}\|_2\le2R\). Every column is a nonzero integer vector and therefore has norm at least one; hence \(L_{i,j}\le2R\). Injectivity of the chart gives \(|E_i|=\prod_j(L_{i,j}+1)\). Also, the nonzero integer determinant satisfies \(|\det B_i|\ge1\). Telescoping the two products, one factor at a time, proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_i|&\le\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}E_i)
+\prod_{j=1}^n(L_{i,j}+1)-\prod_{j=1}^nL_{i,j}\\
&\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+n(2R+1)^{n-1}\\
&\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+n^2(2R+n)^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, each of the \(n\) telescoping summands is a product of \(n-1\) factors, each at most \(2R+1\).

Now consider a full-rank standard-simplex piece, \(E_i=\Phi_i(D_{m_i,n})\). Its convex hull has vertices \(\mathbf w_0=\mathbf v_i\) and \(\mathbf w_j=\mathbf v_i+m_i\mathbf b_{i,j}\), \(1\le j\le n\). To see this, the real parameter simplex \(\{\mathbf y\ge\mathbf0:\sum_jy_j\le m_i\}\) contains every point of \(D_{m_i,n}\) and has all its vertices in that integer support. Its volume is \(m_i^n/n!\), obtained by successive integration; affine volume scaling then gives \(\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}E_i)=|\det B_i|m_i^n/n!\).

Let \(\overline{\mathbf w}=(n+1)^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^n\mathbf w_j\) and let
\(G_i=\sum_{j=0}^n(\mathbf w_j-\overline{\mathbf w})(\mathbf w_j-\overline{\mathbf w})^{\mathsf T}\).
All the vertices have norm at most \(R\), so
\(\operatorname{tr}G_i=\sum_j\|\mathbf w_j\|_2^2-(n+1)\|\overline{\mathbf w}\|_2^2\le(n+1)R^2\).
Writing \(\mathbf1\) for the all-ones vector of length \(n\), direct expansion about the centroid gives
\[
G_i=m_i^2 B_i\left(I_n-\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}}{n+1}\right)B_i^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\det G_i=\frac{m_i^{2n}(\det B_i)^2}{n+1}.
\]
The middle matrix has eigenvalue \(1/(n+1)\) along \(\mathbf1\) and eigenvalue one on its orthogonal complement, which proves the determinant identity. Arithmetic-geometric mean on the positive eigenvalues of \(G_i\) now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}E_i)
&=\frac{\sqrt{(n+1)\det G_i}}{n!}\\
&\le\frac{(n+1)^{(n+1)/2}}{n!\,n^{n/2}}R^n
\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Here the last numerical inequality has an elementary exact verification. For \(1\le j\le n\), \(j(n+1-j)\ge n\), since their difference is \((j-1)(n-j)\). Thus \((n!)^2=\prod_{j=1}^n j(n+1-j)\ge n^n\). Moreover, \(n+1\le2n\) and \(n+1\le2^n\), the latter by induction. Consequently,
\((n+1)^{n+1}\le(2n)^n2^n=4^n n^n\le4^n(n!)^2\), proving the inequality in (4).

The edge from \(\mathbf w_0\) to \(\mathbf w_j\) has length \(m_i\|\mathbf b_{i,j}\|_2\le2R\), so \(m_i\le2R\). Appending the nonnegative integer slack coordinate \(m_i-\sum_jy_j\) identifies the domain with compositions of \(m_i\) into \(n+1\) parts. Choosing the \(n\) separator positions among \(m_i+n\) positions gives \(|E_i|=\binom{m_i+n}{n}\). Since \(|\det B_i|\ge1\), telescoping products again gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E_i|&\le\operatorname{vol}_n(\operatorname{conv}E_i)
+\frac{\prod_{j=1}^n(m_i+j)-m_i^n}{n!}\\
&\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+\frac{n(n+1)}{2n!}(m_i+n)^{n-1}\\
&\le\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+n^2(2R+n)^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
For the second line, replacing the \(j\)-th factor \(m_i\) by \(m_i+j\) increases that factor by \(j\), while every other factor in the corresponding telescoping summand is at most \(m_i+n\). The sum of these increases is \(n(n+1)/2\). The last line uses \(n(n+1)/(2n!)\le n^2\). Thus (3) and (5) are cardinality estimates uniform over all integer chart matrices, including matrices of nontrivial lattice index.

For a piece of rank \(1\le r_i<n\), choose \(r_i\) coordinate positions in which \(B_i\) has a nonzero \(r_i\)-by-\(r_i\) minor. Projection onto these positions is injective on \(\mathbf v_i+B_i\mathbb R^{r_i}\): equality of the projections forces equality of the parameter vectors by invertibility of that minor. Every integer point of \(D_{n,R}\) has each coordinate in \(\{-R,\ldots,R\}\). Therefore
\[
|E_i|\le(2R+1)^{r_i}\le(2R+1)^{n-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
For rank zero, the same estimate holds because the piece is a singleton; no matrix minor is needed.

There are at most \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}\) full-rank pieces, because each spends \(2^n\) of the budget. There are at most \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}2^n\) pieces in total, because each spends at least one. The union cardinality is at most the sum of cardinalities, even when the pieces overlap. Applying (3), (5), and (6), then using \(R\ge n\) to bound both \(2R+n\) and \(2R+1\) by \(3R\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\bigcup_i E_i\right|
&\le B_{\mathrm{bud}}\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+B_{\mathrm{bud}}n^2(2R+n)^{n-1}
+B_{\mathrm{bud}}2^n(2R+1)^{n-1}\\
&\le B_{\mathrm{bud}}\left(\frac{2R}{\sqrt n}\right)^n
+B_{\mathrm{bud}}(n^2+2^n)3^{n-1}R^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Dividing by (1) and using the original uniform law yields
\[
p\!\left(\bigcup_i E_i\right)
\le 2B_{\mathrm{bud}}\left(\frac23\right)^{n/2}
+\frac{2B_{\mathrm{bud}}(n^2+2^n)3^{n-1}(n/6)^{n/2}}{R}.
\tag{7}
\]
The first term is strictly less than \(\delta/2\). Indeed, Bernoulli's inequality and the definition of \(n\) give
\((3/2)^{n/2}\ge1+n/4\ge1+4B_{\mathrm{bud}}/\delta>4B_{\mathrm{bud}}/\delta\).
The second term is strictly less than \(\delta/2\), because \(R\ge R_0\) is strictly greater than
\((4B_{\mathrm{bud}}/\delta)(n^2+2^n)3^{n-1}(n/6)^{n/2}\).
This proves the strict mass bound. For the empty collection its mass is zero, so the conclusion holds there as well.

The argument concerns only the existence of a budgeted representation. The interval support and rank aliases from `8db3ba8c8761ea6c`, the standard-simplex support alias from `cfb2a3f2d408f191`, and the budget normalization from `0ea9778d4ccdcc84` are unchanged; no moment lower bound for a new support family has been assumed. The obstruction persists if one additionally requires disjointness, saturation, or any further certification restricting the allowed pieces. It makes no assertion that the depth or moment of the uniform lattice-ball law is small.
