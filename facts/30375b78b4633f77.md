## Statement

Let \(k\) be a nonnegative integer, let \(T,\beta\ge0\) be real, and, when \(k>0\), let \(2\le B_1\le\cdots\le B_k\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) be integers. Put \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,C_{4,i})\) on \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\). Write \(T\) for the integer part of the cap henceforth. Define the original complete law
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),\\
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z,\\
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(H\) ranges over closed affine halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero ambient normal. All support and halfspace inequalities are weak.

Here are the exact prefix laws and adaptive objectives used in the hypothesis and proof. For \(0\le j\le k\), let \(D_i^{(j)}=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\) if \(i\le j\) and \(D_i^{(j)}=\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) if \(i>j\). Restrict the same \(g_i\) to these alphabets, let \(\Gamma_j\) be their sum, and let \(A_T^{(j)},Z^{(j)},p^{(j)}\) be the resulting epigraph support, original normalizer, and normalized law in the same \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). In particular \((\Gamma_k,Z^{(k)},p^{(k)})=(\Gamma,Z,p)\). Set
$$
f_E=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\quad(0\le E\le T),\qquad
s_{\max}=12\log2,
$$
and \(f_E=0\) for integer \(E>T\). For each prefix, coordinate, and allowed root \(\lambda\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}&=\lbrace h\in\mathbb Z:\lambda+h,\lambda-h\in D_i^{(j)}\rbrace,\\
e_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]-g_i(\lambda),\\
b_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)-g_i(\lambda-h)],\\
K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}}
 e^{-(2s/3)[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]},\\
H_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}}e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)},\\
Z_i^{(j)}(s)&=\sum_{x\in D_i^{(j)}}e^{-s g_i(x)},\qquad
P_i^{(j)}(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in D_i^{(j)}}K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)^3,\\
\delta_i^{(j)}(s)&=\log\frac{8P_i^{(j)}(s)}{Z_i^{(j)}(s)^4},\qquad
Q_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)=K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)^3/P_i^{(j)}(s).
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(J_i\) have law \(Q_{s,i}^{(j)}\), and conditionally on \(J_i=\lambda\) let \(V_i\) have probabilities \(e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)}/H_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar e_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,\lambda}(V_i)\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\sigma_{s,i}^{e,(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,\lambda}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\sigma_{s,i}^{b,(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[b_{i,\lambda}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\alpha_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=g_i(\lambda)+\bar e_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda),\qquad
\mu_i^{(j)}(s)=\mathbb E\alpha_{s,i}^{(j)}(J_i),\\
R_i^{(j)}(s)&=s^2\left(1+\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)^2+
 e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2]\right),\\
V_s^{(j)}&=\sum_iR_i^{(j)}(s),\qquad
\mu^{(j)}(s)=\sum_i\mu_i^{(j)}(s),\\
L_j(s)&=40\sqrt{\sum_i\left(1+\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)^2+
 e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2]\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus \(L_j\) is defined continuously at zero by the displayed finite expectations and \(L_j(s)=40\sqrt{V_s^{(j)}}/s\) for \(s>0\). In particular \(\sigma^b\) denotes the imbalance second moment, with exactly the convention in (12) of 69083720ffaaff7c. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(s)&=\max_{0\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z}
       [\log(f_t/f_0)+st],\\
\mathcal J(s)&=\lbrace t:\log(f_t/f_0)+st=\mathcal F(s)\rbrace,\\
\Psi_j(s)&=\mathcal F(s)+\tfrac14\sum_i\log P_i^{(j)}(s)
                 -\int_0^sL_j(y)\,dy\qquad(0\le s\le s_{\max}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The full law hypothesis is that some global minimizer \(s_\star\) of \(\Psi_k\) on \([0,s_{\max}]\) satisfies \(0<s_\star<s_{\max}\) and has an active integer \(t\in\mathcal J(s_\star)\) with \(t\le T/2\). For an initial real cap, this integer-height condition is unchanged by taking its integer part.

There is an absolute \(\kappa_{\rm low}>0\) such that every law satisfying this hypothesis has
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\rm low}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
One finite specification is the following. Let \(\varepsilon\) be exactly the positive compact minimum (2) of 69083720ffaaff7c, so \(0<\varepsilon\le1/8\). Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_\diamond&=\min\lbrace1/1024,\ \varepsilon/2,\ (3/2)\operatorname{sech}^2 2\rbrace,\\
D_\diamond&=256e^{16},\qquad
K_\diamond=260^2D_\diamond/a_\diamond^2,\\
N_\diamond&=\left\lceil\left(\frac{4096+4s_{\max}}{a_\diamond}\right)^2\right\rceil,\\
\kappa_{\rm low}&=\min\left\lbrace\frac{2^{-5N_\diamond}}{448},\
 \frac{\exp(-152e^2K_\diamond)}{2939328}\right\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
All these quantities are absolute. The locked \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S,W,c_0\) retain their original meanings; neither the scalar rate \(s_\star\) nor the cutoff \(N_\diamond\) changes any locked notation.

## Proof

We use only the declared predecessors. From 69083720ffaaff7c we use its positive \(\varepsilon\) in (2) and its one-coordinate assertions (11)-(14). The exact map, separately for each four-site coordinate, is its \(s,u,v_j,w,\xi,\eta_j,\zeta_i\) equal to the present \(s,u=s,v_i=sB_i,sC_{4,i},e^{-s},e^{-sB_i},e^{-sC_{4,i}}\). Its \(g_i,Z_i,P_i,\delta_i,R_i,\mu_i\), root law, conditional displacement law, \(\bar e,\alpha,\sigma^e,\sigma^b\) are the present coordinate quantities, at any prefix which retains that four-site coordinate. These assertions are scalar assertions with arbitrary \(B_i\ge2\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\), so they apply individually without any grouping hypothesis. We reprove below the changes for binary coordinates, the prefix comparison, the product consumer, and the mass transfer. From cd9532b1d59c5977 we use its scalar geometric-height assertion (4), with the explicit translations below, and its three-site original-law theorem for the binary fallback. No full two-group theorem or extension theorem is used.

The support is nonempty because \((\mathbf0,0)\) is present. Taking the integer part of the cap leaves the law and every adaptive objective unchanged. If a nonnegative integer \(t\) satisfies \(t\le T_{\rm original}/2\), it satisfies \(2t\le\lfloor T_{\rm original}\rfloor\), and the converse holds, so it also leaves the stated branch condition unchanged. For \(T=0\) the support is a singleton and \(M_3(p)=1\), which proves the conclusion. Henceforth \(T\ge1\). All \(f_E\) with \(0\le E\le T\) are positive, and \(f_E\) strictly decreases with \(E\).

We first give a height consumer that also settles bounded \(k\) uniformly in cap and tilt. For any prefix, take one spatial root \(\boldsymbol\lambda\) and an integer \(E\) with \(\Gamma_j(\boldsymbol\lambda)\le E\le T\). Let
$$
r_E^{(j)}(\boldsymbol\lambda)=
\#\lbrace\mathbf h\in\mathbb Z^k:
 \boldsymbol\lambda\pm\mathbf h\in\textstyle\prod_iD_i^{(j)},\
 \Gamma_j(\boldsymbol\lambda\pm\mathbf h)\le E\rbrace.
$$
For \(E\le t\le T\), combine these spatial displacements with all height displacements \(\lvert h\rvert\le\min(t-E,T-t)\). Reflection about \((\boldsymbol\lambda,t)\) pairs their endpoints, with disjoint nonfixed pairs and the sole fixed zero displacement. Every closed containing ambient halfspace contains at least one endpoint of each pair. The smaller endpoint mass is \(e^{-\beta(t+\lvert h\rvert)}/Z^{(j)}\), including equality. Charging half of the fixed root mass if it occurs gives
$$
\tau_{p^{(j)}}(\boldsymbol\lambda,t)
\ge\frac{r_E^{(j)}(\boldsymbol\lambda)}{2Z^{(j)}}
 \sum_{\lvert h\rvert\le\min(t-E,T-t)}e^{-\beta(t+\lvert h\rvert)}.
\tag{1}
$$
Apply the scalar assertion (4) of cd9532b1d59c5977 with its \(M=T-E\), \(r=e^{-\beta}\), \(j=t-E\), and \(q_j=e^{-\beta t}/f_E\). Its reflected mass \(F_q(j)\) is the sum in (1) divided by \(f_E\). Cubing (1), multiplying by the original root mass \(e^{-\beta t}/Z^{(j)}\), and summing over \(t=E,\ldots,T\) therefore gives
$$
\sum_{t=E}^T p^{(j)}_{(\boldsymbol\lambda,t)}
     \tau_{p^{(j)}}(\boldsymbol\lambda,t)^3
\ge\frac{r_E^{(j)}(\boldsymbol\lambda)^3 f_E^4}
            {3584(Z^{(j)})^4}.
\tag{2}
$$
This application includes \(\beta=0\) and \(E=T\). It uses original masses throughout.

For the full law take \(\boldsymbol\lambda=\mathbf0\) and \(E=0\). There is exactly one allowed spatial displacement, so (2) gives \(M_3(p)\ge f_0^4/(3584Z^4)\). Each of at most \(4^k\) spatial vectors contributes at most \(f_0\) to \(Z\). Consequently
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{4^{-4k}}{3584}
 =\frac{2^{-5k}}{448}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{3}
$$
For \(k\le N_\diamond\) this proves the claimed coefficient, including \(k=0\) and every lower affine rank. Henceforth \(k>N_\diamond\), in particular \(k\ge1\).

For a binary coordinate, \(I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}=\lbrace0\rbrace\) for both roots \(\lambda=0,1\). Hence all excess and imbalance variables are zero and the exact binary quantities, denoted by a subscript \(\mathrm{bin}\), are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&K_{s,\mathrm{bin}}(0)=1,\quad K_{s,\mathrm{bin}}(1)=e^{-4s/3},
 \quad H_{s,\mathrm{bin}}(0)=H_{s,\mathrm{bin}}(1)=1,\\
&P_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)=1+e^{-4s},\quad Z_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)=1+e^{-s},
 \quad\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)=\frac{e^{-4s}}{1+e^{-4s}},\\
&R_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)=s^2(1+\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)),\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)=\log\frac{8(1+e^{-4s})}{(1+e^{-s})^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
In particular \((\log P_{\mathrm{bin}})'/4=-\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}\). Its \(\alpha\) is the root itself and both \(\sigma^e\) and \(\sigma^b\) vanish, so
$$
s^2\mathbb E\alpha^2\le2R_{\mathrm{bin}},\qquad
s^2\mathbb E(\sigma^e+\sigma^b)\le R_{\mathrm{bin}}.
$$
Also \(R_{\mathrm{bin}}\le2s^2<256\) on \(0<s\le s_{\max}\): indeed \(\log2<3/4\) gives \(s_{\max}<9\). Direct differentiation gives
$$
\delta_{\mathrm{bin}}(0)=0,\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{bin}}'(s)=2[\tanh(2s)-\tanh(s/2)]>0\quad(s>0).
$$
For \(0\le s\le1\), the integral of \(\operatorname{sech}^2\) on \([s/2,2s]\) is at least \((3s/2)\operatorname{sech}^2 2\). Integrating this derivative yields
$$
\delta_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)\ge(3/2)\operatorname{sech}^2 2\,s^2
 \quad(0\le s\le1),\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)\ge(3/2)\operatorname{sech}^2 2
 \quad(s\ge1).
\tag{5}
$$

For a four-site coordinate put \(q_i=e^{-2v_i/3}\), \(v_i=sB_i\), and \(u=s\). The mapped assertions (13)-(14) of 69083720ffaaff7c give
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\delta_i(s)\ge0,\qquad 0<R_i(s)<256\quad(0<s\le s_{\max}),\\
&s^2\mathbb E\alpha_{s,i}(J_i)^2\le2R_i(s),\qquad
s^2\mathbb E[\sigma^e_{s,i}(J_i)+\sigma^b_{s,i}(J_i)]\le R_i(s),\\
&\tfrac14(\log P_i)'(s)=-\mu_i(s),\\
&\delta_i(s)\ge\varepsilon
 \quad\text{if }s\ge1\text{ or if }0\le s\le1,\ v_i\le24,\\
&\delta_i(s)\ge(s^2+q_i)/1024,\qquad
R_i(s)\le2s^2+128v_i^2q_i
 \quad\text{if }0<s\le1,\ v_i\ge24.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
These formulas, together with (4)-(5), establish all scalar identities and estimates used for every prefix, with its own coordinate laws. Since the linear interpolants have slopes \(1,B_i-1,C_{4,i}-B_i\), they are convex; their restrictions to binary alphabets are convex too. Thus every excess \(e\) is nonnegative. Symmetry of \(h\) and \(-h\) gives conditional mean zero for every imbalance \(b\). These observations hold even when some costs are tied.

We next compare the global minimizing rates. Fix a coordinate with four-site costs \(B_i,C_{4,i}\). Its exact polynomial, with \(x=e^{-s}\), \(\eta=e^{-sB_i}\), and \(\zeta=e^{-sC_{4,i}}\), is
$$
P_i(s)=1+(x^{4/3}+2\eta^{2/3})^3
 +(\eta^{4/3}+2(x\zeta)^{2/3})^3+\zeta^4.
\tag{7}
$$
In particular \(P_i=P_{\mathrm{bin}}+P_{\mathrm{extra}}\), where \(P_{\mathrm{extra}}\) is a nonempty positive sum of exponential monomials. In the first cube, beyond the binary monomial \(e^{-4s}\), the monomial with \(r\) factors from \(2\eta^{2/3}\) has energy
$$
\frac43(3-r)+\frac23B_i r
 =4+\frac23(B_i-2)r\ge4\qquad(r=1,2,3).
$$
Every monomial in the second cube has energy at least \(8\), since its two possible factor energies are \(4B_i/3\ge8/3\) and \(2(1+C_{4,i})/3\ge8/3\). The last monomial has energy \(4C_{4,i}\ge12\). Multiplicities in these expansions are positive and irrelevant to these bounds.

By the derivative identity in (6), \(\mu_i\) is one quarter the mean monomial energy under the weights defining \(P_i\). For \(P_{\mathrm{bin}}\) that mean energy is \(4\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}<4\) at every finite \(s\ge0\). All additional monomial energies are at least \(4\). Therefore
$$
\mu_i(s)>\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}(s)\qquad(0\le s\le s_{\max}).
\tag{8}
$$
The zero-root weight in (7) is exactly \(1\), whereas every other root has \(g_i(\lambda)\ge1\). Hence
$$
\mathbb E g_i(J_i)^2\ge\frac{P_i-1}{P_i}
 \ge\frac{P_{\mathrm{bin}}-1}{P_{\mathrm{bin}}}=\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}.
$$
All the other second moments are nonnegative. The unscaled second-moment sum in \(R_i\) is thus at least its binary counterpart, including at \(s=0\). Replacing a binary coordinate by its four-site version gives \(L_{j+1}(s)\ge L_j(s)\) on the entire closed domain. The height maximum \(\mathcal F\) is identical for the two prefixes. Therefore their difference is continuously differentiable and
$$
\frac{d}{ds}(\Psi_{j+1}-\Psi_j)
 =-(\mu_{j+1}(s)-\mu_{\mathrm{bin}}(s))-(L_{j+1}(s)-L_j(s))<0,
\tag{9}
$$
where \(\mu_{j+1}\) on the right denotes only the four-site scalar mean of coordinate \(j+1\). This meaning is distinct from the total mean \(\mu^{(j+1)}\).

For completeness, strict decreasing differences compare arbitrary global minimizers without convexity or differentiability of the separate objectives. If \(a\) minimizes \(\Psi_j\) and \(b\) minimizes \(\Psi_{j+1}\) and \(a>b\), minimality gives
$$
(\Psi_{j+1}-\Psi_j)(a)-(\Psi_{j+1}-\Psi_j)(b)
 =[\Psi_{j+1}(a)-\Psi_{j+1}(b)]
  -[\Psi_j(a)-\Psi_j(b)]\ge0,
$$
contradicting (9). Consequently every minimizer of \(\Psi_j\) is at most every minimizer of \(\Psi_{j+1}\). This proof includes ties among minima and either domain endpoint.

Each objective is continuous on the compact domain. At zero the unique active height is zero, so \(\mathcal F'_+(0)=0\). The scalar derivative identities and continuity give
$$
\Psi'_{j,+}(0)=-\mu^{(j)}(0)-L_j(0)<0,
$$
since \(k\ge1\) implies \(L_j(0)>0\). Choose any global minimizing rate \(s_j\) for each prefix, taking \(s_k=s_\star\) as in the hypothesis. The preceding comparison proves
$$
0<s_0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_k=s_\star<s_{\max}.
\tag{10}
$$
In particular every chosen prefix has its own interior global minimum.

We record the product consumer at these own saddles, including its original normalization. The tail sequence is strictly logconcave: with \(r=e^{-\beta}\), one has \(f_E^2-f_{E-1}f_{E+1}=r^{E+T}>0\) for \(1\le E<T\). Thus its logarithm is concave on the integer interval, so all maximizing indices in \(\mathcal J(s)\) are consecutive. At an interior minimum of \(\Psi_j\), its left and right derivatives give
$$
\min\mathcal J(s_j)\le\mu^{(j)}(s_j)+L_j(s_j)\le\max\mathcal J(s_j).
$$
There is consequently an active integer \(t_\star\) with
$$
\mu^{(j)}(s_j)+40\sqrt{V_{s_j}^{(j)}}/s_j
 \le t_\star
 \le\mu^{(j)}(s_j)+40\sqrt{V_{s_j}^{(j)}}/s_j+1.
\tag{11}
$$
For every \(s\) and active \(t\), the pointwise inequality \(f_{\Gamma_j(\mathbf x)}\le f_t e^{s(t-\Gamma_j(\mathbf x))}\), followed by summation over the prefix spatial box, gives
$$
Z^{(j)}\le f_t e^{st}\prod_i Z_i^{(j)}(s).
\tag{12}
$$
This is a bound for its own complete original law.

Fix \(j\), write \(s=s_j\) and \(V=V_s^{(j)}\), and in the next paragraph suppress only the prefix superscripts of coordinate quantities. Sample independent roots \(J_i\) with their own \(Q_{s,i}\). The scalar moment bounds (4),(6) imply that with probability at least \(1-3/64>1/2\),
$$
\left|s\sum_i\alpha_{s,i}(J_i)-s\mu^{(j)}(s)\right|\le8\sqrt V,
\qquad
s^2\sum_i[\sigma^e_{s,i}(J_i)+\sigma^b_{s,i}(J_i)]\le64V.
\tag{13}
$$
Indeed, Chebyshev bounds the first failure by \(2/64\) and Markov bounds the second by \(1/64\). For a fixed root \(\boldsymbol\lambda\) satisfying (13), sample conditionally independent displacements. Their conditional imbalance means are zero. Chebyshev and a union bound, using the second inequality in (13), give probability at least \(3/4\) to the event
$$
\left|s\sum_i(e_{i,\lambda_i}(V_i)-\bar e_{s,i}(\lambda_i))\right|\le16\sqrt V,
\qquad
\left|s\sum_i b_{i,\lambda_i}(V_i)\right|\le16\sqrt V.
\tag{14}
$$
The sum of the two failure bounds is at most \(64/256\). The endpoint identity
$$
\Gamma_j(\boldsymbol\lambda\pm\mathbf h)
 =\sum_i\left[g_i(\lambda_i)+e_{i,\lambda_i}(h_i)
                    \pm b_{i,\lambda_i}(h_i)\right]
$$
and (11),(13),(14) put both endpoints in the prefix energy sublevel at \(t_\star\). Nonnegativity of the excess also puts the root there.

The conditional probability of \(\mathbf h\) is \(\exp[-(4s/3)\sum_i e_{i,\lambda_i}(h_i)]/\prod_i H_{s,i}(\lambda_i)\). On (14) its reciprocal is at least
$$
\prod_iH_{s,i}(\lambda_i)
 \exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}\sum_i\bar e_{s,i}(\lambda_i)-\frac{64}{3}\sqrt V\right).
$$
As (14) has probability at least \(3/4\), its number of distinct displacement vectors is at least one half this displayed quantity. The endpoint map is injective, so this is a lower bound on \(r_{t_\star}^{(j)}(\boldsymbol\lambda)\). The exact identity
$$
\prod_iH_{s,i}(\lambda_i)^3
 =e^{4s\Gamma_j(\boldsymbol\lambda)}
       \left(\prod_iP_i^{(j)}(s)\right)\prod_iQ_{s,i}(\lambda_i)
$$
then implies, after cubing this count, using the lower side of the first inequality in (13), and summing over the good roots of total probability at least \(1/2\),
$$
\sum_{\boldsymbol\lambda\ \text{good}}
 r_{t_\star}^{(j)}(\boldsymbol\lambda)^3
 \ge\frac1{16}\left(\prod_iP_i^{(j)}(s)\right)
        \exp(4s\mu^{(j)}(s)-96\sqrt V).
$$
Distinct spatial roots have disjoint sampled height lines. Apply (2) to each, then use (11)-(12) to obtain
$$
M_3(p^{(j)})\ge\frac1{3584\cdot16}\,8^{-k}
 \exp\left(\sum_i\delta_i^{(j)}(s_j)
              -256\sqrt{V_{s_j}^{(j)}}-4s_j\right).
\tag{15}
$$
The constants and normalization agree with (17)-(20),(6) of 69083720ffaaff7c. The derivation here proves (15) for the mixed binary/four-site prefixes using their own kernels, their own minimizing rates, and their own original normalizers.

Call prefix \(j\) successful if
$$
\sum_i\delta_i^{(j)}(s_j)\ge256\sqrt{V_{s_j}^{(j)}}+4s_j.
\tag{16}
$$
Then (15) gives \(M_3(p^{(j)})\ge(1/7168)8^{-(k+1)}\).

We bound the activity of every failing positive prefix. First no failing prefix can have \(s_j\ge1\) under our standing \(k>N_\diamond\). In fact (5)-(6) give \(\sum_i\delta_i^{(j)}(s_j)\ge a_\diamond k\), whereas \(V_{s_j}^{(j)}<256k\). Failure would give

a_\diamond k<4096\sqrt k+4s_{\max}
 \le(4096+4s_{\max})\sqrt k,
$$
contradicting \(k>N_\diamond\) and the definition of \(N_\diamond\). Thus every failing prefix has \(0<s_j<1\).

For an arbitrary prefix \(j\ge1\) and \(0<s\le1\), define
$$
Q_j(s)=\sum_{i\le j}e^{-2sB_i/3},\qquad G=ks^2+Q_j(s).
$$
For a retained four-site coordinate with \(v_i\le24\), one has \(s^2+q_i\le2\) and \(\delta_i\ge\varepsilon\); for \(v_i\ge24\) use (6). Together with (5) for the binary coordinates, this proves
$$
\sum_i\delta_i^{(j)}(s)\ge a_\diamond G.
\tag{17}
$$
The number of retained coordinates with \(v_i\le24\) is at most \(e^{16}Q_j(s)\), since each such \(q_i\ge e^{-16}\). Their combined radius is less than \(256e^{16}Q_j(s)\). Every other retained coordinate has \(R_i\le2s^2+128v_i^2q_i\), and \(v_i\le sB_j\) by the sorted costs. Each binary coordinate has \(R_{\mathrm{bin}}\le2s^2\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_s^{(j)}
&\le2ks^2+[256e^{16}+128(sB_j)^2]Q_j(s)\\
&\le D_\diamond[1+(sB_j)^2]G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Also \(V_s^{(j)}\ge ks^2\ge s^2\). If prefix \(j\) fails, substitute \(s=s_j\) in (17)-(18) and absorb \(4s\le4\sqrt{V_s^{(j)}}\). As \(G>0\), squaring and dividing by \(G\) proves
$$
Q_j(s_j)\le G<K_\diamond[1+(s_jB_j)^2]
\qquad\text{for every failing }j\ge1.
\tag{19}
$$
All constants here are independent of the number, ratios, or multiplicities of the costs.

Let \(j_\star\) be the largest successful positive prefix, if one exists, and let \(j_\star=0\) otherwise. If \(j_\star>0\), (15)-(16) give its original moment bound with coefficient \(1/7168\). If \(j_\star=0\), apply the theorem of cd9532b1d59c5977 with its rank equal to the present \(k\), its cap and tilt equal to \(T,\beta\), and every one of its third-site costs equal to the artificial integer \(T+1\ge2\). No artificial third site is feasible, so this is exactly the binary prefix law \(p^{(0)}\), with its same original \(Z^{(0)}\), its same ambient coordinates, and its same ambient depths. Thus in both cases
$$
M_3(p^{(j_\star)})\ge\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{20}
$$

Every positive prefix \(j>j_\star\) fails. By (10) and (19), and monotonicity of each activity summand in \(s\),
$$
Q_j(s_\star)\le Q_j(s_j)
 <K_\diamond[1+(s_jB_j)^2]\le K_\diamond[1+(s_\star B_j)^2]
 \qquad(j>j_\star).
\tag{21}
$$
For \(v\ge0\) put
$$
F(v)=\sum_{i>j_\star:\ s_\star B_i\le v}e^{-2s_\star B_i/3}.
$$
If this sum is nonempty, let \(j\) be the last index with \(s_\star B_j\le v\). Then \(j>j_\star\), so the sum is at most \(Q_j(s_\star)\), and (21) gives \(F(v)\le K_\diamond(1+v^2)\). If it is empty the same bound holds. Since \(s_\star>0\) and \(B_i\ge2\), one has \(F(0)=0\). Stieltjes integration by parts, whose boundary term at infinity vanishes because there are finitely many coordinates, now gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i>j_\star}e^{-s_\star B_i}
 &=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-v/3}\,dF(v)
  =\frac13\int_0^\infty e^{-v/3}F(v)\,dv\\
 &\le\frac {K_\diamond}3\int_0^\infty e^{-v/3}(1+v^2)\,dv
  =19K_\diamond.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
This includes \(j_\star=k\), when the tail is empty.

It remains to charge the tail using the original cap and tilt. Use the active integer \(t\le T/2\) supplied by the full-law hypothesis. It satisfies \(t<T\). Its active comparison with the forward neighbor gives
$$
s_\star\le\log(f_t/f_{t+1}).
$$
With \(r=e^{-\beta}\) and the positive integer \(b=T-t\), the exact ratio and its bound are
$$
\frac{f_t}{f_{t+1}}
 =e^\beta\left(1+\frac{r^b}{\sum_{h=0}^{b-1}r^h}\right)
 \le e^\beta(1+1/b).
$$
The denominator is at least \(br^b\), including \(r=1\). Therefore, including a tie between active indices,
$$
s_\star\le\beta+1/(T-t)\le\beta+2/T.
\tag{23}
$$
For each individually feasible second cost \(B_i\le T\) it follows that
$$
e^{-\beta B_i}\le e^2 e^{-s_\star B_i}.
\tag{24}
$$

We prove the exact original-mass comparison by sectors, without replacing the epigraph law by an independent law. Fix disjoint subsets of tail coordinates to be at sites \(2\) and \(3\), leaving every other tail coordinate binary. Let \(E_{\mathrm{fix}}\) be their fixed total energy. If \(E_{\mathrm{fix}}>T\) the sector is empty. Otherwise shift its height by \(E_{\mathrm{fix}}\). Its unnormalized mass is \(e^{-\beta E_{\mathrm{fix}}}\) times the complete law of all remaining spatial coordinates capped at \(T-E_{\mathrm{fix}}\). Appending zeros in the fixed coordinates embeds this shifted support, with exactly its weights \(e^{-\beta t'}\), into the prefix support \(A_T^{(j_\star)}\). The sector mass is consequently at most \(e^{-\beta E_{\mathrm{fix}}}Z^{(j_\star)}\). A sector using an individual site of cost greater than \(T\) is empty. Summing the sector bounds therefore proves
$$
\frac{Z}{Z^{(j_\star)}}
 \le\prod_{i>j_\star}
 \left(1+\mathbf1_{B_i\le T}e^{-\beta B_i}
          +\mathbf1_{C_{4,i}\le T}e^{-\beta C_{4,i}}\right).
\tag{25}
$$
Since \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge B_i\), every feasible fourth cost has a feasible second cost and \(e^{-\beta C_{4,i}}\le e^{-\beta B_i}\). Applying \(1+x\le e^x\), (22), and (24) gives the single bound
$$
\frac{Z}{Z^{(j_\star)}}
 \le\exp\left(2\sum_{i>j_\star:\ B_i\le T}e^{-\beta B_i}\right)
 \le\exp(38e^2K_\diamond).
\tag{26}
$$
Infeasible expensive sites contributed no mass and were dropped before using (24).

Finally \(A_T^{(j_\star)}\) is a subset of the full original support in the same ambient \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). On that subset the original atom probabilities are exactly \(Z^{(j_\star)}/Z\) times their prefix probabilities. Every containing halfspace has at least that ratio times its prefix mass, and taking infima preserves this inequality. Sampling only prefix atoms hence proves the fourth-power transfer
$$
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac{Z^{(j_\star)}}Z\right)^4
                       M_3(p^{(j_\star)}).
\tag{27}
$$
Equations (20),(26),(27) give the claimed coefficient \(\exp(-152e^2K_\diamond)/2939328\) when \(k>N_\diamond\). Equation (3) gives \(2^{-5N_\diamond}/448\) for \(k\le N_\diamond\), and \(T=0\) was settled separately. Their minimum is exactly \(\kappa_{\rm low}\) in the statement.

Every comparison used complete original epigraph masses and closed ambient halfspaces. The minimizing-rate ordering was proved for arbitrary global minimizers on the entire compact domain, then the given full interior minimizer placed all selected prefix minima in its interior. Only the full law's supplied low active height was used to compare its rate with \(\beta\). Thus all ties, zero tilt, singleton height intervals, lower affine ranks, and support boundary atoms are included under precisely the stated low-height hypothesis.
