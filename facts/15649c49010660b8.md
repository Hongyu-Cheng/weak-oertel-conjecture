## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $U$ be uniform on $\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R_+^{d+1}:\sum_j u_j=1\}$, and for $x\ge0$ put $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$ and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0} t/F_x(t)^{1/d}$. For $b=(b_1,\ldots,b_d)>0$ and $y=\min_j b_j$, one has $\phi_d(0,b)=\operatorname{GM}_d(b)$ and
$$
\left.\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\phi_d(\epsilon,b)\right|_{\epsilon=0+}
=\operatorname{GM}_d(b)\left(\frac1y-\frac1d\sum_{j=1}^d\frac1{b_j}\right).
$$
The formula includes repeated positive entries and $d=1$.

## Proof

Write $M=\max_j b_j$ and use the $d$ barycentric coordinates corresponding to $b$ as coordinates on the simplex. For $0\le\epsilon<y$ and $t>\epsilon$, the cap is
$$
\left\{u\ge0:\ \sum_j u_j\le1,\ \sum_j(b_j-\epsilon)u_j\le t-\epsilon\right\}.
$$
The second inequality alone defines an orthant simplex of relative volume $(t-\epsilon)^d/\prod_j(b_j-\epsilon)$. The first inequality is redundant for $t\le y$. It removes positive volume for $t>y$, since the orthant simplex extends past $\sum_j u_j=1$ along a coordinate attaining $y$. In particular at $\epsilon=0$ the capacity is $\operatorname{GM}_d(b)$, with ratio strictly above it for $t>y$.

For $0<\epsilon<y$ the ratio on $(\epsilon,y]$ equals $A_\epsilon t/(t-\epsilon)$ with $A_\epsilon=(\prod_j(b_j-\epsilon))^{1/d}$ and is strictly decreasing there. Above $M$ the ratio equals $t$. On $[y,M]$ it is continuous with positive cap probability, so some minimizing threshold $t_\epsilon$ belongs to this interval. Along any subsequence with $t_\epsilon\to t_0$, the corresponding cap probabilities converge to $F_{(0,b)}(t_0)$ by dominated convergence: the unperturbed nonconstant linear form has probability zero at equality. Comparing with the ratio at $y$ then forces $t_0=y$. Thus $t_\epsilon\to y$, including the case $y=M$.

The full orthant bound at $t_\epsilon$ and equality at $y$ give
$$
A_\epsilon\frac{t_\epsilon}{t_\epsilon-\epsilon}\le\phi_d(\epsilon,b)\le A_\epsilon\frac{y}{y-\epsilon}.
$$
Both bounds equal $\operatorname{GM}_d(b)\big[1+\epsilon(1/y-d^{-1}\sum_j 1/b_j)\big]+o(\epsilon)$. This yields the stated right derivative.
