## Statement

There exists epsilon>0 independent of n such that for every convex body C in R^n x R^2, n>=1, with a complete positive parity core P of full planar triangular fibers, if V is the largest core fiber area and the total outside-core area in each parity class is at most epsilon V, then some actual mixed-integer point has normalized closed-ambient-halfspace depth at least (4/9)2^(-n). There is no intermediate-target incoming-load hypothesis. Explicitly, for a valid dimension-two one-sided-star constant0<rho<=1/4, take eta_H=rho/8, eta_P=eta_H/34, epsilon=min(eta_P/72,eta_H^2/46656). The proof combines distinct partner and target bands, the exact planar all-ratio actual transfer, and a71/72 versus17/18 retained-mass contradiction. It assumes the specified triangular parity core; it does not assert such a core exists in every convex body.

## Proof

# Complete triangle parity cores need no intermediate incoming-load restriction

Scoped theorem, 6 November 2026. This concerns continuous dimension2 and arbitrary integer dimensionn. It assumes a complete positive parity core consisting of triangles and sufficiently small outside volume in every parity class. It does not assert these hypotheses for arbitrary mixed-integer convex bodies.

Let Gamma=4/9. Choose a valid constant0<rho<=1/4 from the accepted one-sided local-star theorem c77c6c94c4f34008 in dimension2. Its outer-mass coefficient is A=5/9, so kappa_H=Gamma/A=4/5. Define

    eta_H=rho/8,
    eta_P=eta_H/34,
    epsilon=min(eta_P/72,eta_H^2/46656)>0.

Let C be a convex body in R^n times R^2, n>=1, and K_z its integer fibers. Suppose P contains precisely one representative of every parity class in Z^n, every K_w forw in P is a full-dimensional triangle, and put

    L=2^n, V=max_(w in P)|K_w|,
    F_r=sum_(z notin P,z equivalent r mod2)|K_z|.

Assume F_r<=epsilon V for every parityr. Then an actual point of C intersect(Z^n times R^2) has normalized mixed-volume depth at leastGamma/L. In particular there is no additional assumption on how incoming outside mass is distributed among intermediate-volume core fibers.

## 1. Deficit accounting under hypothetical failure

Write V_w=|K_w| and

    E=sum_(z notin P)|K_z|,
    Delta=sum_(w in P)(V-V_w),
    W=L V-Delta+E,
    b_avg=W/L, T=Gamma b_avg.

All sums are finite because C is bounded. Core fibers have positive area, so W>0. Zero-area outside fibers have no effect on these sums. If no actual point has raw depth at leastT, the centroid of a largest core triangle givesGamma V<T. Thus

    E>Delta,       V<b_avg<=V+epsilon V.                    (1)

For each core target set d_w=b_avg-V_w>0. Direct summation gives

    sum_(w in P)d_w=E.                                     (2)

## 2. Partners use a narrower band than high targets

Define

    G_P={v in P:V_v>=(1-eta_P)V},
    H={w in P:V_w>=(1-eta_H)V},
    M=(1-eta_P)V.

For each positive-area outside fiber p, choose its unique parity representative v(p) in P and set w(p)=(p+v(p))/2. Convexity gives the actual midpoint inclusion

    (K_p+K_(v(p)))/2 subset K_(w(p)).

Retain this outside edge only if v(p) belongs to G_P. Its target must belong to P. Indeed fixing any point of K_p gives a translate of half K_(v(p)) inside K_(w(p)), so V_(w(p))>=M/4. If the target were outside P, its area would be at most its parity load epsilon V<M/4, a contradiction. The strict comparison follows from eta_P<=1/2 and epsilon<=eta_P/72: epsilon<=1/144<(1-eta_P)/4.

Every bad partner v outside G_P contributes more than eta_P V to Delta. Its entire outside parity load is at most epsilon V. Hence the mass discarded from outside edges satisfies

    D_bad<= (epsilon/eta_P)Delta<=Delta/72.                 (3)

Let e_w be the retained incoming mass at a core targetw. Then

    sum_(w in P)e_w=E-D_bad>71E/72,                         (4)

using(1). Each retained outside fiber appears exactly once in this sum. For a fixed targetw, its partners v=2w-p are distinct core points, distinct fromw; all incoming fibers are outside P. Every individual local star therefore has pairwise distinct actual bases. No conclusion relies on simultaneously adding partner masses from different stars.

## 3. High targets are repaired by the one-sided local-star theorem

## 5. Contradiction and exact scope

Equations(5) and(9), together with(2), imply
