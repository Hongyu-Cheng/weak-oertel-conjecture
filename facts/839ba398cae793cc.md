## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer and let \(D\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^k\) be nonempty and invariant under every coordinate permutation. Let \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb R^k\). Suppose there is an ordering \(i_1,\ldots,i_k\) of the coordinate indices such that

$$
\beta_{i_{j+1}}-\beta_{i_j}\ge\log 2
\qquad(1\le j<k).
$$

For \(k=1\), this condition is vacuous. Set \(N:=k+1\), let \(A:=D\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), and define the original weights and law by

$$
w_{\mathbf x}:=\exp(-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x),
\qquad
Z:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,0)}:=\frac{w_{\mathbf x}}Z.
$$

The subscript \(\mathbf x\) on \(w_{\mathbf x}\) indexes spatial atoms; the subscript \((\mathbf x,0)\) on \(p\) indexes their embedded original atoms. For \(\mathbf z\in A\), use all closed affine halfspaces of the original ambient space to define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
&:=\inf_{\substack{H\ni\mathbf z\\ H=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\ge b\}\\ \mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}}
\sum_{\mathbf v\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},\\
M_3(p)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

For every coordinate-permutation orbit \(\mathcal O\subseteq D\), let \(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}\) be its unique point whose ordered word \((y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_k})\) is nonincreasing. Then

$$
\sum_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}w_{\mathbf x}
\le16w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}=\max_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal O}w_{\mathbf x}.
\tag{1}
$$

In particular, some original atom has probability at least \(1/[16(k+1)^2]\), and

$$
M_3(p)
\ge\frac1{16^4(k+1)^8}
\ge16^{-5}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$

All quantities in (1) and (2) concern the original law just defined. No predecessor theorem or predecessor object is used as a premise.

## Proof

For a word \(\mathbf u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\{0,1,2\}^k\), define its inversion count relative to nonincreasing order by

$$
I(\mathbf u):=\bigl|\{(a,b):1\le a<b\le k,\ u_a<u_b\}\bigr|.
$$

For \(\mathbf x\in D\), its ordered word is \(u_j:=x_{i_j}\). The coordinate order here only specifies how the finite word is read; the spatial points, embedding, probabilities, and ambient halfspaces are unchanged.

Fix a permutation orbit \(\mathcal O\). A word that is not nonincreasing has an adjacent ascent \(u_j<u_{j+1}\). Swapping this adjacent pair decreases \(I\) by exactly one: its own pair ceases to be counted, and each position outside the pair has the same total number of comparisons with its two entries before and after the swap. The change in the original energy is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(\beta_{i_j}u_j+\beta_{i_{j+1}}u_{j+1}\bigr)
-\bigl(\beta_{i_j}u_{j+1}+\beta_{i_{j+1}}u_j\bigr)\\
&\hspace{25mm}=
(\beta_{i_{j+1}}-\beta_{i_j})(u_{j+1}-u_j)
\ge\log 2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The swap stays in the same orbit. Repeatedly swapping adjacent ascents terminates after exactly \(I(\mathbf u)\) steps, because the inversion count is a nonnegative integer decreasing by one at each step, and a word with no adjacent ascent is nonincreasing and has inversion count zero. Its terminal word is the unique nonincreasing arrangement of the fixed counts, namely the word of \(\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}\). Summing the energy decreases gives

$$
\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x-
\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}
\ge(\log2)I(\mathbf u).
$$

Consequently,

$$
\frac{w_{\mathbf x}}{w_{\mathbf y_{\mathcal O}}}
\le2^{-I(\mathbf u)}.
\tag{3}
$$

This proves the maximum assertion in (1), including an orbit consisting of a single word.

We next prove a uniform finite-word counting bound. Given nonnegative integers \(a,b\), consider all binary words with \(a\) high letters and \(b\) low letters, and let \(I_{\rm bin}\) count pairs with a low letter before a high letter. Define

$$
F(a,b):=\sum_{\text{such binary words}}2^{-I_{\rm bin}}.
$$

If \(a=0\), there is a single word and \(F(0,b)=1\). For \(a\ge1\), number the high letters from left to right and let \(t_j\) be the number of low letters before the \(j\)-th high letter. The word is uniquely specified by

$$
0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_a\le b,
\qquad
I_{\rm bin}=\sum_{j=1}^a t_j.
$$

Indeed, its \(j\)-th high letter has position \(t_j+j\), and the remaining positions are low letters. Dropping only the upper bound \(t_a\le b\), and then putting \(s_1:=t_1\), \(s_j:=t_j-t_{j-1}\) for \(2\le j\le a\), gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(a,b)
&\le\sum_{0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_a}
2^{-\sum_{j=1}^a t_j}\\
&=\sum_{s_1,\ldots,s_a\ge0}
2^{-\sum_{j=1}^a(a-j+1)s_j}
=\prod_{r=1}^a(1-2^{-r})^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The final identity is a product of finitely many convergent geometric series. It is also obtained by taking increasing rectangular finite sums, so no rearrangement of signed terms is involved.

For any finite list \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\in[0,1]\), induction using
\((1-B)(1-b)=1-B-b+Bb\ge1-B-b\) proves
\(\prod_{j=1}^m(1-b_j)\ge1-\sum_{j=1}^m b_j\). Applying this to \(2^{-r}\) for \(2\le r\le a\), with an empty list allowed when \(a=1\), yields

$$
\prod_{r=2}^a(1-2^{-r})
\ge1-\sum_{r=2}^a2^{-r}
=\frac12+2^{-a}
\ge\frac12.
$$

Therefore \(\prod_{r=1}^a(1-2^{-r})\ge1/4\) for \(a\ge1\). Together with the empty-product case \(a=0\), equation (4) proves

$$
F(a,b)\le4\qquad(a,b\ge0).
\tag{5}
$$

Return to a ternary orbit, and let \(m_r\) be the number of occurrences of \(r\), for \(r=0,1,2\), so \(m_0+m_1+m_2=k\). Every ternary word of these counts corresponds bijectively to a pair of binary words: its skeleton records each two as a high letter and each other entry as a low letter, and its second word is obtained by erasing all twos and regarding ones as high and zeros as low. Conversely, put the second word into the low slots of the skeleton to recover the ternary word.

The inversions in which the later entry is two are exactly the inversions of the skeleton. The remaining inversions are precisely the zero-before-one pairs, which are the inversions of the second word. Thus inversion counts add under this bijection, and (5) gives the exact factorization and bound

$$
\sum_{\substack{\text{ternary words of}\\ \text{counts }(m_0,m_1,m_2)}}2^{-I(\mathbf u)}
=F(m_2,m_0+m_1)F(m_1,m_0)
\le16.
\tag{6}
$$

Since \(D\) is permutation invariant, the words from any orbit \(\mathcal O\subseteq D\) are all ternary words of its fixed counts, read in the coordinate order \(i_1,\ldots,i_k\). Summing (3) and applying (6) proves (1). In particular the actual weight of its original mode is at least one sixteenth of that orbit's total weight; this statement also covers any of the counts being zero.

Let \(R\) be the number of permutation orbits present in \(D\). Such an orbit is uniquely specified by its count triple. There are

$$
\sum_{m_2=0}^k(k-m_2+1)
=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}2
\le(k+1)^2
$$

nonnegative count triples with sum \(k\), so \(1\le R\le(k+1)^2\). Choose \(\mathbf x^\star\in D\) maximizing the original weight. Summing (1) over the disjoint present orbits, and using that every orbit maximum is at most \(w_{\mathbf x^\star}\), gives

$$
Z\le16R\,w_{\mathbf x^\star},
\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x^\star,0)}\ge\frac1{16R}
\ge\frac1{16(k+1)^2}.
\tag{7}
$$

For every original atom \(\mathbf z\in A\), every admissible halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\) includes its full atom probability. Hence \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge p_{\mathbf z}\). In particular, restricting the original nonnegative sampling sum to \(\mathbf z^\star:=(\mathbf x^\star,0)\), equation (7) implies

$$
M_3(p)
\ge p_{\mathbf z^\star}\tau_p(\mathbf z^\star)^3
\ge p_{\mathbf z^\star}^4
\ge\frac1{16^4(k+1)^8}.
\tag{8}
$$

This argument uses all the original ambient halfspaces, including those with a nonzero last component of their normal; the containment of an atom is sufficient regardless of the support's affine dimension.

For completeness, for every integer \(m\ge2\) we have

$$
m^8\le16\,8^m.
\tag{9}
$$

For \(m=2,3,4\) this is respectively \(256\le1024\), \(6561\le8192\), and \(65536=65536\). For \(m\ge4\),

$$
\left(\frac{m+1}{m}\right)^8
\le\left(\frac54\right)^8
<8,
$$

where the last strict inequality is \(5^8=390625<524288=8\cdot4^8\). Induction from \(m=4\) now proves (9) for all larger integers. Apply (9) to \(m=k+1\) in (8) to obtain the second inequality in (2), with the absolute coefficient \(\kappa:=16^{-5}>0\).

When \(k=1\), the field condition is empty, every orbit is a singleton, the inversion and binary-word arguments still hold with empty products as specified, and (7)--(9) apply. This verifies that edge case without an additional assumption and completes the proof.
