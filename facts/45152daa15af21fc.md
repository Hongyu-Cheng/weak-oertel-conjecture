## Statement

For every $d\ge2$ there is a probability measure with positive density on the interior of a regular $d$-simplex centered at zero, and $d+1$ vertex-side caps through zero, whose sections have weighted centroid zero and equal positive coarea factors $S_i$, with inward unit normals satisfying $\sum_i S_i u_i/(d+1)=0$. The lifted positive-part potential is convex and its gradient is cyclically monotone. At these cuts its mixed normal-offset Hessian block vanishes; the fixed-mass barycenter supports have the corresponding positive tangent section Hessians. Nevertheless all cap fractions equal
$$
p_i=\epsilon\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d+\frac{1-\epsilon}{d+1}
<\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
\quad(0<\epsilon<1).
$$
For $d=3$ and $\epsilon=1/2$ the common value is $43/128<27/64$.

## Proof

Let $v_0,\dots,v_d$ be the vertices of the centered regular simplex $K$. For the barycentric coordinate $\lambda_i$ use $H_i=\{\lambda_i\ge1/(d+1)\}$. The section has vertices $v_i/(d+1)+d v_j/(d+1)$ for $j\ne i$ and hence centroid zero. The inward unit normals point along the respective $v_i$, have sum zero, and the section areas agree. Fix $0<\delta<1$. At $a_i=(1-\delta)v_i$ the $i$th coordinate is $1-\delta d/(d+1)$ and all other coordinates are $\delta/(d+1)$. Choose equal positive-radius balls $B_i$ centered at the $a_i$ that lie in $\operatorname{int}K$, are disjoint and lie on the indicated strict sides of all cut planes. All the finitely many distances involved are positive. Set $\mu=\epsilon\operatorname{Unif}(K)+(1-\epsilon)\sum_i\operatorname{Unif}(B_i)/(d+1)$.

Every section has a neighborhood disjoint from the balls, so the density there equals $\epsilon/|K|$. This gives the claimed weighted centroids, common coarea and balance. The positive-part integral against $\mu$ is convex, and its gradient exists at all nonzero normals by the nullity of cutting planes. The subgradient inequalities sum to cyclic monotonicity. Near each specified cut only the constant-density part contributes to the change in section or cap integrals, so the normal-offset Hessian is the weighted section moment matrix, by differentiating the cap moment on normal columns. Its mixed block is zero. For any fixed mass at one of these cuts, pointwise threshold exchange gives the maximizing cap barycenter. The derivative of its moving threshold in a tangent direction is the weighted section mean of that direction, zero here. Differentiating the first-moment gradient therefore gives the section second moment weighted by the density and divided by the fixed cap mass. It is positive definite on the tangent plane because the section contains a relative ball about zero. All these comparisons use the same measure.

The uniform simplex vertex cap is homothetic about its vertex by factor $d/(d+1)$. Each small ball lies in its own cap alone, giving the displayed exact fractions. For $d=2$, $1/3<4/9$. For $d\ge3$, the binomial expansion gives $(1+1/d)^{d-1}<\sum_{j\ge0}1/j!<3\le d$, where $j!\ge2^{j-1}$ for $j\ge2$ with strict inequality for $j\ge3$. This is equivalent to $1/(d+1)<(d/(d+1))^d$. Substitution gives the stated $d=3$ value.
