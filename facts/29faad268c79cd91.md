## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be convex bodies at the only positive-area integer fibers $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $0\in\operatorname{int}K$. Set $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$ and $v_3=(-1,-1)$, and assume $B\subseteq T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1,\ i=1,2,3\}$. Suppose each closed ambient cap $H_i=\{(z,x):v_i\cdot x\ge z\}$ attains the true raw mixed area depth $Q$ at $(0,0)$, with proper $A$ cuts and zero $B$ captures. Proper means each cutting line meets the interior of $A$. Write $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $b=|B|$ for the planar areas, and $u\le v\le w$ for the ordered $A$ cap areas. Assume $b\le a+k$ and $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$. If $a-u-w>0$, then $a<b/32$.

## Proof

The three original $A$ halfplanes cover the plane because their thresholds sum to $-3$ and the normals sum to zero. The three middle halfplanes cover the plane by the same argument with thresholds zero. The middle cap areas in decreasing order are $Q-u,Q-v,Q-w$ by the common mixed area and the zero $B$ captures. Write $q=Q-w$, $\delta=a-u-v$, $r=\delta/w$, and $F=u+v+w/4$. The three-cap formula (3) of accepted fact `ac7ba1e1e0ebc955` applies to these ordinary planar bodies and covers with $\gamma_2=4/9$, so
$$
a\le w+v+u/4,\qquad k\le 9Q/4-F.
$$
Using $Q\le2(a+k+b)/9$ with the second inequality gives both $k\le a+b-2F$ and $Q\le4(a+b-F)/9$. Then $b\le a+k$ gives $a\ge F$, so $r\ge1/4$. Also
$$
b\ge 9Q/2-a-k\ge9Q/4+F-a=(9/4)q+(5/2-r)w. \tag{1}
$$

Let $v_\ell$ be the normal of the largest $A$ cap. Replace its threshold $-1$ by $2$ and keep the two other thresholds at $-1$. These three halfplanes still cover the plane because their thresholds and normals both sum to zero. If the shifted cap has area $\tau$, the same three-cap formula gives $\tau\ge\delta+\tfrac34\min(u,\tau)$. If $\tau<u$, this implies $\tau\ge4\delta\ge w\ge u$, a contradiction. Hence $\tau\ge\delta+3u/4$. Put $x=u/w$ and $\theta=r+3x/4$. With $h=\max_{p\in A}v_\ell\cdot p$, positivity of $\tau\ge\theta w$ implies $h>2$ because the line at level $2$ has area zero. Write $H=h+1>3$. Contract the original largest cap about any of its points at level $-1$ by $3/H$. The contracted cap is still in the original cap, lies at levels at most $2$, and has area $9w/H^2$. Thus $\theta\le1-9/H^2$ and
$$
1/4\le r<\theta<1,\qquad H\ge3/\sqrt{1-\theta}. \tag{2}
$$

Take the segment $E$ joining two points of $A$ at the minimum and maximum of $t=v_\ell\cdot p$. Its range in $t$ is $[t_0,h]$ with $t_0<-1$ by properness. Choose a transverse linear coordinate $s$ of unit absolute Jacobian together with $t$, then shear $s$ by a multiple of $t$ so that $E$ has constant $s$. For a nonempty $t$-section of $B$ at fixed $s$, write $[L,U]$ for its interval. The three normal coordinates of $B$ sum to zero and are at most $1$, so $-2\le L\le U\le1$. The corresponding section of $E+B$ is $[t_0+L,h+U]$, with its lower endpoint negative and its upper endpoint positive. Its length at levels $t\ge0$ is $h+U$. The pointwise bound
$$
h+U-\frac{H}{3}(U-L)=\frac{(h-2)(1-U)+H(L+2)}3\ge0
$$
and integration in the area-preserving coordinates give $|(E+B)\cap\{t\ge0\}|\ge Hb/3$. By compatibility, $4q\ge Hb/3$. Combining this bound with (1) and (2) shows
$$
0<(5/2-r)w\le b(1-3H/16)\le b\left(1-\frac{9}{16\sqrt{1-\theta}}\right).
\tag{3}
$$
In particular $\theta<175/256$.

Label the normals by $i,j,\ell$ for the areas $u,v,w$ respectively. For $y=v/w$, the two actual residual regions $R_\ell=A\cap\{v_i\cdot p<-1,\ v_j\cdot p<-1\}$ and $R_j=A\cap\{v_i\cdot p<-1,\ v_\ell\cdot p<-1\}$ have areas at least $rw$ and $(r+y-1)w>0$ respectively by the complement union bound. Thus $1-r<y\le1$. Apply the planar case of the whole residual sector lemma. Its normals span the plane with zero sum, its midpoint and $B$ hypotheses are the present ones, and the mixed cap areas are common with zero $B$ captures. The two midpoint sectors both strictly miss the middle trace of the $i$-th cap and are disjoint. With the two positive area lower bounds the lemma gives
$$
k-Q+u\ge\frac b2+\frac{(2r+y-1)w}{4}
+\frac{\sqrt{bw}}2\left(\sqrt r+\sqrt{r+y-1}\right). \tag{4}
$$
The cap profile gives $k-Q+u\le5Q/4-v-w/4$. Since $Q\le4(a+b-F)/9$ and $a-F=(r-1/4)w$, this is at most $5b/9+[(10r-7)/18-y]w$. Subtract (4) from that bound and set $z=w/b>0$ to obtain
$$
2\ge (5-2r+45y)z+18\sqrt z\left(\sqrt r+\sqrt{r+y-1}\right). \tag{5}
$$

Suppose $a/b\ge1/32$, and put $\alpha=r+x+y=a/w$. Then $z\ge1/(32\alpha)$. Both terms on the right of (5) increase with $z$ since $5-2r+45y>50-47r>0$ by (2). Consequently
$$
2\ge\frac{5-2r+45y}{32(r+x+y)}
+\frac{18(\sqrt r+\sqrt{r+y-1})}{\sqrt{32(r+x+y)}}. \tag{6}
$$
The right side decreases with $x$ for fixed $r,y$. We may therefore increase $x$ to $\bar x=\tfrac43(175/256-r)>0$ by (3). With this value of $x$, the first term increases with $y$ since its derivative has numerator $47r+45\bar x-5>0$. With $t=r+y-1>0$, the derivative of the second term has the sign of $1+\bar x-\sqrt{rt}>0$, since $t\le r<1$. The expression is continuous at $t=0$, so we may lower $y$ to $1-r$. With $L(r)=1+\bar x=367/192-4r/3$, (6) then gives
$$
2\ge f(r):=\frac{50-47r}{32L(r)}+18\sqrt{\frac{r}{32L(r)}}.
$$
But for $1/4\le r<175/256$,
$$
32L(r)^2 f'(r)=-\frac{1483}{64}+9\frac{367}{192}\sqrt{32}\sqrt{\frac{L(r)}r}>0.
$$
Indeed $L(r)>1$, $r<1$, $367/192>1$ and $\sqrt{32}>5$, so the positive term exceeds $45$, while $1483/64<24$. Thus $f(r)\ge f(1/4)=153/202+18/\sqrt{202}>2$. The strict comparison follows from $18\sqrt{202}>251$, whose squared sides are $65448>63001$. This contradicts (6) and proves $a<b/32$.
