## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^D$ be a convex body of volume $V$, and let $H_1,\ldots,H_j$ be proper closed halfspaces covering $K$, where $2\le j\le D$. Assume their normals are linearly independent. With $A_i=|K\cap H_i|$ and $\gamma_q=(q/(q+1))^q$ for $q\ge1$, for every $\alpha>0$,
$$
\sum_{i=1}^j(A_i-\alpha)_+ + \frac{\alpha}{\gamma_{j-1}}>V.
$$
If $a=\min_i A_i>0$, then $\sum_i A_i-V>(j-1/\gamma_{j-1})a$.

## Proof

If a proper subfamily covers $K$, choose an inclusion-minimal one of size $k<j$. The effective cap-count inequality 23a563a735c07c25 gives its profile with coefficient $1/\gamma_{k-1}$, including $\gamma_0=1$ for $k=1$. The sequence $(\gamma_q)_{q\ge0}$ is strictly decreasing. For $q>0$ this follows by differentiating $q\log(q/(q+1))$ and using $\log(1+1/q)>1/(q+1)$. Therefore replacing $1/\gamma_{k-1}$ by $1/\gamma_{j-1}$ strictly increases the left side when $\alpha>0$. Adding the omitted nonnegative terms proves the result in this case.

Now suppose the cover is inclusion-minimal. Write $H_i=\{\ell_i\ge0\}$ for its affine margins. Their joint image of $K$ is convex and compact and avoids the open negative orthant. Separation gives $\lambda_i\ge0$, not all zero, such that $t=\sum_i\lambda_i\ell_i\ge0$ on $K$. Indeed a separating functional has finite supremum on the negative orthant only with nonnegative coefficients, and then that supremum is zero. Every coefficient is positive: if any were zero, the positive-coefficient caps alone would cover $K$. Independence implies that $t$ is nonconstant.

Use affine coordinates $(v,t,y)$ where $v=(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_{j-1})$, and complete these independent coordinates by $y$. On $t>0$ the projective marginal f6c497926ac327fa in dimension $j-1$ preserves the total and all cap masses and has convex inverse power $-1/j$. Its density can be restricted to the open positive interior, since the boundary is null. In coordinates $z=v/t$ the projected margins are $z_i$ for $i<j$ and $(1-\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i z_i)/\lambda_j$ for $i=j$. They are nonconstant, and their weighted sum with weights $\lambda_i$ is one everywhere. Thus their open positive halfspaces cover the whole projected space. The strict open-cover profile applies with $r=j-1$ and $\beta=j$. The resulting masses are the original $A_i$, because $t=0$ is null. This proves the strict profile in the minimal case.

For $a>0$ take $\alpha=a$, so each positive part is $A_i-a$. When $a=0$, the overlap bound need not be strict. For example, take the cube $[-1,1]^D$, the halfspace $x_1\ge-2$, and the halfspaces $x_i\ge2$ for $2\le i\le j$. The first cap has mass $V$ and the others have zero mass.
