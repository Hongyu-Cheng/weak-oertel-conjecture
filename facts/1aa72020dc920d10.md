## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional convex body, $d\ge1$, with uniform probability $\mu$ and centroid $g$. Put $D=K\cap(2g-K)$ and $p=|D|_d/|K|_d$. The map $T(x)=2g-x$ on $D$ and $T(x)=x$ on $K\setminus D$ is a measure-preserving involution. For $X\sim\mu$, the joint law of $(X,T(X))$ has both marginals equal to $\mu$, and its midpoint law is $\nu=\mu|_{K\setminus D}+p\delta_g$. For every proper closed halfspace $H$ through $g$, $\nu(H)=\mu(H)+p/2$. For every closed halfspace containing $g$,
$$
\nu(H)\ge\gamma_d+p/2\ge\gamma_d+\frac{1}{2d^d},
\qquad\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d.
$$
In fact $(K-g)/d\subseteq(K-g)\cap(g-K)$, so $p\ge d^{-d}$. The displayed uniform lower bound equals one when $d=1$.

## Proof

Reflection about $g$ preserves Lebesgue measure and maps $D$ onto itself, while $T$ fixes its complement in $K$. Thus $T$ preserves $\mu$ and is an involution. The midpoint is $g$ on $D$ and equals $X$ off $D$, giving the stated law. Every proper through-$g$ halfspace contains exactly half the mass of $\mu|_D$ by reflection and the null Lebesgue boundary, whereas it contains the new atom in full. Its gain is therefore $p/2$. The full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d, with the density one, gives $\mu(H)\ge\gamma_d$ for each such halfspace. A closed halfspace containing $g$ contains the parallel through-$g$ halfspace, so the lower bound also holds there.

Translate $g$ to zero. The centroid is interior: otherwise a supporting linear functional through it is nonnegative throughout $K$ and strictly positive on a set of positive measure, contradicting zero mean. For a unit direction $u$, let $[-a,b]$ be the projection range of $K$ and choose $v\in K$ with $u\cdot v=b$. The set $v+s(K-v)$ lies in $K$ and above projection $b-s(a+b)$, for $0\le s\le1$, with relative volume $s^d$. Integrating this upper-tail lower bound and using the zero projection mean gives
$$
0=-a+\int_{-a}^b\Pr(u\cdot X\ge y)\,dy
\ \ge\ -a+(a+b)\int_0^1s^d\,ds
=-a+\frac{a+b}{d+1}.
$$
Hence $b\le da$ in every direction, so the support function of $K/d$ is everywhere at most that of $-K$. Separation gives $K/d\subseteq-K$, while convexity and $0\in K$ give $K/d\subseteq K$. Its volume is $d^{-d}|K|_d$, proving the overlap bound. In dimension one the uniform interval is centrally symmetric and $\nu=\delta_g$.
