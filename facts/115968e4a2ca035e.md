## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, and let $P$ be its nonempty finite set of positive $d$-volume integer bases. Work in the saturated lattice coordinates on $\operatorname{aff}P$ after restricting $C$ to $\operatorname{conv}P\times\mathbb R^d$, and write $m=\dim\operatorname{aff}P$ and $M$ for the total mixed $d$-volume. Suppose finitely many closed caps $H_k=\{a_k\cdot z+u_k\cdot x\ge b_k\}$ with unit $u_k$ cover all of $\mathbb R^d$ above every retained base. For a cap $i$ of mass strictly between $0$ and $M$, suppose its selected effective trace $\tau_i$ is the full geometric coarea trace on the retained bases and $S_i=\tau_i(\mathbb R^{m+d})>0$. Rotate continuous coordinates so $u_i=e_d$ and write $x=(\xi,t)$. The shear $t'=t+a_i\cdot z-b_i$ and deletion of $t'=0$ turn the section into a convex body in $\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^{d-1}$, whose mixed $(d-1)$-volume is exactly the image of $\tau_i$. If an unnormalized nonzero continuous normal is used for the defining cap, the coarea trace is the corresponding constant $1/\lVert u_i\rVert$ times mixed section volume after dividing its margin by $\lVert u_i\rVert$. Each cap $k$ restricts in the unit-normal coordinates to
$$
(a_k-u_{k,d}a_i)\cdot z+u_{k,\perp}\cdot\xi\ge b_k-u_{k,d}b_i.
$$
If the depth bound $\gamma_{d-1}/2^m$ holds for ordinary mixed $(d-1)$-volume on convex bodies in this reduced dimension, where $\gamma_0=1$, then some $k\ne i$ satisfies $\tau_i(H_k)\ge(\gamma_{d-1}/2^m)S_i$. In reduced ambient dimension zero the same conclusion holds without the depth hypothesis.

## Proof

Every integer point of $\operatorname{conv}P$ has a positive fiber: take a convex combination of positive-radius open balls from its contributing fibers. Consequently the restriction has exactly the bases $P$, and it preserves their fibers. Apply b6d385eb408fbfb9 to this restricted set. Its saturated affine chart fixes the continuous coordinate, and its transformed hull of occupied fibers is full-dimensional in $\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^d$. The transformed restricted body contains that hull, so is itself full-dimensional there. The integer fiber volumes and all within-fiber section measures are identical in this chart.

Since the cap plane has zero $d$-volume at every sampled fiber, the intermediate mass gives source points on both strict sides. The transformed body is full-dimensional, so its interior is dense in it. Approximate the strict-side points by interior points and use the segment between them to see that the plane meets the interior. Its section is thus a full-dimensional convex body relative to the plane. A continuous rotation and the displayed shear are affine bijections preserving integer bases. On each fixed base the section has $t=b_i-a_i\cdot z$, so dropping that coordinate is a Euclidean isometry from the fiber section to its image. Hence the section measure is the sum of ordinary $(d-1)$-volumes of the image fibers, or of counts for $d=1$. The coarea multiplier for a nonunit margin is $1/\lVert u_i\rVert$ and is constant across all these fibers. Substituting the section value of $t$ gives the stated restriction for each cap, including restrictions that are all space or empty.

At any retained base, the other caps cover the whole plane of cap $i$. Otherwise a point on that plane has strictly negative margins for every $k\ne i$. Moving a sufficiently small positive distance in direction $-u_i$ preserves their strict signs by finiteness and makes the $i$th margin negative, contradicting whole-space coverage. The lower-dimensional depth hypothesis provides one actual mixed point in the section of normalized depth at least $\gamma_{d-1}/2^m$, and its base is retained. One of the other restricted caps contains this point. If that restriction is a genuine halfspace, the depth bound gives the claimed mass, and if it is all space the mass is $S_i$. The same argument in dimension zero uses the only point with its full mass. The constant coarea factor cancels in every fraction.
