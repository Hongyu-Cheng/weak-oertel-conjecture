## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$, $b=1/m$, $\Gamma=(d/m)^d$ and $c=(1-d^{1-d})/[(d-1)^2m]$. There is a constant $\delta_d>0$ depending only on $d$ with the following property. Suppose $S$ is a full-dimensional simplex in $\mathbb R^d$ with centroid $g$, $K$ is a compact convex body, and
$$
g+(1-\delta)(S-g)\subseteq K\subseteq S,\qquad 0\le\delta\le\delta_d.
$$
Assume the centroid of every facet of $S$ belongs to $K$. With $\tau=1-|K|/|S|$, there is one $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ such that for every closed halfspace $H$ containing $y$,
$$
\frac{|K\cap H|}{|K|}\ge\Gamma+\frac{\Gamma c}{4}\tau .
$$
In particular, if $D(K)$ is the maximum normalized ordinary halfspace depth and $\mu_K,\mu_S$ are the normalized uniform laws, then
$$
\sup_{H\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}}|\mu_K(H)-\mu_S(H)|
\le \operatorname{TV}(\mu_K,\mu_S)=\tau
\le \frac{4}{\Gamma c}\bigl(D(K)-\Gamma\bigr).
$$

The loss estimates below use the same nested inclusion and facet-centroid contacts before imposing the depth radius $\delta_d$. Let $\lambda_i$ be the barycentric functions of $S$ and $R_i=|(S\setminus K)\cap\{\lambda_i\ge b\}|/|S|$. For $0\le\delta<1$ one has $\sum_i R_i\le m\Gamma(1-c)(1-\delta)^{-d}\tau$. Put
$$
\eta=\min\left\{\frac{1}{8m},\frac{\Gamma c}{4\cdot8m\cdot2^{d+1}}\right\},
\qquad E=8m\eta\,2^{d+1},\qquad
L_i=\min\{\tau,R_i+E\tau\},\qquad
\bar L=\frac1m\sum_i L_i .
$$
For $\delta\le1/2$, each $L_i$ bounds the deleted fraction relative to $|S|$ for every affine margin within $\eta$ of $\lambda_i-b$ on $S$. For $\delta\le\min\{1/2,c/(4d)\}$, $\bar L\le\Gamma(1-c/2)\tau$. The point in the depth conclusion can be taken with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i(y)=b+(\bar L-L_i)/(m\Gamma)$.

## Proof

**Canonical losses.** Use the radial notation $F_j,\rho,f_j,w_j,\mu_{F_j}$ of the nearby simplex cap-loss lemma. Each facet centroid lies on $\partial S$ and in $K$, so its gauge about $g$ equals one and $f_j$ vanishes there. The convex facet estimate of accepted fact f566248002918e0f applies to $f_j$ with the $d$ barycentric coordinates on $F_j$ and threshold $b=1/(d+1)$. For $z\in F_j$, write $N(z)=\#\{i:\lambda_i(z)\ge b\}$, where $\lambda_j=0$ contributes zero. The facet estimate gives $\int_{F_j}N f_j\,d\mu_{F_j}\le m\Gamma(1-c)\int_{F_j} f_j\,d\mu_{F_j}$. Canonical cap membership is constant on each ray away from $g$, because $\lambda_i(g+a(z-g))-b=a(\lambda_i(z)-b)$. By the cone disintegration and weight comparisons,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i R_i
&=\frac1m\sum_j\int_{F_j}Nw_j\,d\mu_{F_j}\\
&\le\frac{d}{m}\,m\Gamma(1-c)\sum_j\int_{F_j} f_j\,d\mu_{F_j}
\le m\Gamma(1-c)(1-\delta)^{-d}\tau .
\end{aligned}
$$
This bound in fact holds for all $0\le\delta<1$ satisfying the geometric hypotheses. If $\delta\le1/2$, the stated $\eta$ satisfies $\eta\le (1-\delta)/(4m)$ and $8m\eta(1-\delta)^{-d-1}\le E$. The nearby-margin lemma bounds each loss by $R_i+E\tau$, and it is also at most $\tau$. Thus it is at most $L_i$, with $0\le L_i\le\tau$. If also $\delta\le c/(4d)$, then $(1-\delta)^d\ge1-d\delta\ge1-c/4$ by Bernoulli. Since $0<c<1$, $(1-c)/(1-c/4)\le1-3c/4$. Now $E\le\Gamma c/4$ and the bound on $\sum_i R_i$ give $\bar L\le\Gamma(1-c/2)\tau$.

**Localization of all normals.** Invertible affine maps preserve the geometric inclusions, barycentric coordinates, and the relevant volume fractions, so take $S=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$ before choosing any constants. Let $v_i$ be its vertices. For a unit normal $u$ and a point $y$ set $H(y,u)=\{x:u\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$. The normalized simplex cap volume is jointly continuous in $(y,u)$ by dominated convergence off the limiting hyperplane, which has zero $d$-volume. At $g$ the minimum is $\Gamma$ and its only normals are $u_i=\nabla\lambda_i/|\nabla\lambda_i|$. Indeed the centroid inequality and its oriented equality case in accepted fact ea91e021ff1f4416, §1, identify equality with an apex-side cap of a pyramid with base parallel to the cut. In a simplex the apex must be a vertex and all other vertices must lie in its opposite facet, giving exactly these oriented normals. Each resulting cap is a homothetic simplex of scale $1-b$ and has volume fraction $\Gamma$.

Take small disjoint open neighborhoods $U_i$ of the $u_i$ on the unit sphere with nonempty compact complement. They and a number $\chi>0$ can be chosen so that for $u\in U_i$ and $\max_j|\lambda_j(y)-b|\le\chi$, the margin $\phi(x)=|\nabla\lambda_i|u\cdot(x-y)$ differs from $\lambda_i(x)-b$ by at most $\eta$ on $S$. At $(u_i,g)$ the margins agree on $S$, and the uniform estimate follows from continuity on compact $S$. Since $\eta<b$ and $1-b\ge b$, every such cap strictly contains $v_i$ and strictly excludes the other vertices. At $g$ the minimum over the complementary normals is $\Gamma+\rho_0$ for some $\rho_0>0$ by continuity, compactness, and the exact equality directions. Shrink $\chi$ so that at every indicated $y$ and every complementary normal the simplex cap fraction is at least $\Gamma+\rho_0/2$. All these choices depend only on $d$.

**The chosen point.** Let $B=2^d/\Gamma$ and set
$$
\tau_*=\min\{\Gamma/2,\ m\Gamma\chi,\ \rho_0/4,\ \Gamma c/(4B)\},
\qquad \delta_d=\min\{1/4,\ c/(4d),\ \tau_*/d\}.
$$
The inner inclusion gives $\tau\le1-(1-\delta)^d\le d\delta$, so $\tau\le\tau_*$. Choose $y$ by $\lambda_i(y)=b+(\bar L-L_i)/(m\Gamma)$. The coordinates sum to one and differ from $b$ by at most $\tau/(m\Gamma)$. They are at least $b/2>\delta/m$, so $y$ lies in the interior of $g+(1-\delta)(S-g)$, whose barycentric inequalities are $\lambda_i\ge\delta/m$. Thus $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and $\max_j|\lambda_j(y)-b|\le\chi$.

Fix a normal in $U_i$. Its cap through $y$ isolates $v_i$, so in barycentric coordinates it has equation $\sum_{j\ne i} a_j\lambda_j(x)\le1$ with $a_j>1$. In fact if $p_j=u\cdot(v_j-y)$, then $p_i>0>p_j$ for $j\ne i$, and $a_j=1-p_j/p_i$. Its edge intercept fractions from $v_i$ are $a_j^{-1}$, so its volume fraction is $\prod_{j\ne i}a_j^{-1}$ by the simplex determinant. Since $\sum_{j\ne i}a_j\lambda_j(y)=1$, AM–GM gives
$$
\frac{|S\cap H(y,u)|}{|S|}=\prod_{j\ne i}a_j^{-1}\ge d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(y).
$$
Write $\Delta_j=\lambda_j(y)-b$. In the expansion of the product on the right after factoring $\Gamma$, the linear term is $\sum_{j\ne i}m\Delta_j=-m\Delta_i$, and every $|m\Delta_j|\le\tau/\Gamma\le1$. The higher-order terms, after multiplying back by $\Gamma$, have absolute sum at most $\Gamma\sum_{k=2}^d\binom dk(\tau/\Gamma)^k\le B\tau^2$. The simplex cap fraction is therefore at least $\Gamma-m\Gamma\Delta_i-B\tau^2$. Removing the lost fraction at most $L_i$ gives
$$
\frac{|K\cap H(y,u)|}{|S|}
\ge \Gamma-\bar L-B\tau^2
\ge \Gamma-\Gamma(1-c/2)\tau-B\tau^2 .
$$
For each complementary normal the simplex cap fraction is at least $\Gamma+\rho_0/2$ and at most $\tau\le\rho_0/4$ is deleted, so the same lower bound holds. Any closed halfspace containing $y$ contains the parallel through-$y$ halfspace of the same orientation. Finally, divide by $1-\tau=|K|/|S|$ and subtract $\Gamma$. The resulting gain is at least
$$
\frac{\Gamma c\,\tau/2-B\tau^2}{1-\tau}\ge \frac{\Gamma c}{4}\tau
$$
because $\tau\le\Gamma c/(4B)$. These estimates include $\tau=0$, when the chosen point is $g$.

For the cap-distance bound, the signed density of $\mu_K-\mu_S$ with respect to volume is nonnegative on $K$, nonpositive on $S\setminus K$, and zero elsewhere. Its positive mass is $\mu_K(K)-\mu_S(K)=1-|K|/|S|$, so total variation, in the convention $\operatorname{TV}=\sup_A|\mu_K(A)-\mu_S(A)|$, is exactly $\tau$. The supremum over closed halfspaces is bounded by that supremum over measurable sets.
