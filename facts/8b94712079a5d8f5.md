## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $N=d+1$, $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, and let $P$ be a finite complete affine-lattice base with $k$ occupied parities. Range over all actual positive compatible $d$-fibers on $P$. Denote the total sampled volume by $M$, the fiber volumes and centroids by $V_z$ and $g_z$, and the original normalized maximum closed-halfspace depth by $\beta_0(K)$. Let $\beta_0^*=\inf_K\beta_0(K)$. Fix $b$ with $\beta_0^*<b<\gamma_d/k$. Assume the entire original sublevel $\{\beta_0\le b\}$ admits a compact representative frame under invertible maps $(z,x)\mapsto(z,Ax+s(z))$, where $s$ is affine in the base. Its representatives have $M=1$, $V_z\ge v>0$, $K_z\subset R B_d$, and $V(T)\le b k(T)/\gamma_d$ for each nonempty proper closed minor $T=P\cap\operatorname{conv}(T)$.

A positive sampled-concave array $\ell$ satisfies $\ell_q\ge\sum_z\lambda_z\ell_z$ for every anchored convex representation $q=\sum_z\lambda_z z$ in $P$, and is normalized by $\sum_z(V_z/M)\log\ell_z=0$. At a mark $(p,y)$ use the displacement
$$
\varepsilon(\ell_p E_{K_p}(y)/M)^{1/N}G_K(u),\qquad
G_K(u)=\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_z\int_{K_z}(u\cdot(X-g_z))^2\,dX\right)^{1/2},
$$
added to the margin $a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)$ of each through-mark cap with $u\ne0$. Here $E_{K_p}$ is the projective energy of d90ba05577427854, with boundary value zero. Include unchanged pure-base caps, with the harmless formal whole-base trace of mass $M$ in rank zero. Write $\beta_\varepsilon(K,\ell)$ for the normalized maximum buffered depth.

For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$ the sublevel $\{\beta_\varepsilon\le b\}$ is compact in the stated frame. If an admissible reference pair has $\beta_\varepsilon<b$, the unrestricted joint infimum is attained by a positive compatible family and positive array. Such a reference exists for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. For every $\varepsilon>0$ the infimal value lies between $\beta_0^*$ and $\beta_0^*+\varepsilon |P|\kappa_{d-1}R^d$, where $\kappa_j$ is the volume of the Euclidean unit $j$-ball and $\kappa_0=1$.

## Proof

The normalized objectives and parameter constraint are invariant under the stated maps. Each $V_z$ and $M$ gains the same factor $|\det A|$, as does the projective energy by d90ba05577427854 applied to the fiber at $p$. Subtracting the new within-fiber centroid gives the transformed covariance norm $G_K(A^{\mathsf T}u)$. The remaining term from the shear is affine in $z-p$ and is absorbed into the unrestricted base tilt. Pure-base traces are unchanged. All caps enlarge under nonnegative buffering, so every pair in the buffered sublevel can be framed using its original sublevel.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and take a framed sequence in the buffered sublevel. Pass to a simultaneous positive-body limit and hence to weights $w_{j,z}=V_{j,z}/M_j\to w_z\ge v$. Let $h_{j,z}=\ell_{j,z}/\sum_t w_{j,t}\ell_{j,t}$. These sampled-concave arrays satisfy $0<h_{j,z}\le1/v$ and have weighted arithmetic mean one. Pass to an array limit $h\ge0$ with $\sum_z w_z h_z=1$. Geometric normalization yields
$$
\ell_{j,z}=\frac{h_{j,z}}{\prod_t h_{j,t}^{w_{j,t}}}.
$$
If every $h_z$ is positive, this converges to a finite positive normalized sampled-concave array. Otherwise the denominator tends to zero, since at least one $\log h_{j,t}$ tends to $-\infty$ with weight at least $v$ and all other logarithms are bounded above. Hence $\ell_{j,z}\to\infty$ for every $z$ with $h_z>0$.

In the latter case the positive-set parity center lemma applies to $h$ and the limiting weights. Sampled concavity implies midpoint concavity, the limiting volumes satisfy the assumed quotas, and $bk(T)/\gamma_d<k(T)/k$. It provides a fixed base $p$ with $h_p>0$ and closed pure-base depth at least $1/k$ in the limit. The centroids $g_{j,p}$ have $E_{K_{j,p}}(g_{j,p})=V_{j,p}\ge v$ by d90ba05577427854. Positive-body Hausdorff convergence gives convergence of within-fiber covariances to a positive definite covariance, as in f2ead43a963c7026, so $\min_{\|u\|=1}G_{K_j}(u)$ stays positive. Thus the displacement at $(p,g_{j,p})$ is at least $2R$ for every unit $u$ for large $j$. Every nonvertical cap then captures the entire closed base trace $\{z:a\cdot(z-p)\ge0\}$, because both mark and fibers lie in $RB_d$. This includes the whole trace for $a=0$, which is the only trace in rank zero. Since pure-base caps are unchanged, buffered depth at that centroid equals its pure-base depth. The finitely many pure-base traces give convergence of that depth with the weights. This contradicts $\beta_\varepsilon\le b<1/k$.

Every sequence thus has a framed positive limit of bodies and arrays. For a convergent sequence of arrays the corresponding marks at the same base see uniformly convergent multiplier values, so f2ead43a963c7026 applies at fixed $\varepsilon$ and gives continuity of $\beta_\varepsilon$ at the limit. The normalized constraint and every anchored inequality persist. This proves compactness, and a reference of value below $b$ puts a tail of an unrestricted minimizing sequence inside this sublevel, giving attainment.

For $\ell=1$, $E_{K_p}(y)/M\le1$ by the zero energy test or boundary value, and $G_K(u)\le R$ for unit $u$ since the pooled within-fiber variance is at most the pooled uncentered second moment. Thus every cap mass increases by at most $\varepsilon R |P|\kappa_{d-1}R^{d-1}$, using the uniform section-area bound in the common ball. Pure-base masses do not increase. Passing to infima over caps and maxima over marks preserves this upper estimate. Apply it to ordinary near-minimizers in the framed sublevel to obtain the asserted upper bound, and monotonicity gives the lower bound. Applying the same estimate to any fixed ordinary reference of value below $b$ gives the small-$\varepsilon$ reference condition.

In particular the assumptions on the frame hold on a base selected by the cardinality-minimal canonical bad-minor theorem 26d27b0f77ea4ac9, using the parity budget and $\tau=\gamma_d$. That theorem supplies the original whole-sublevel compactness, positive volume floor and stated quotas at any $b$ strictly between its original infimum and $\gamma_d/k$. The base and minor parities in its statement are inherited graph-lattice classes, as required here.
