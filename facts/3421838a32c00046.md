## Statement

Retain the complete canonical hard-branch objects and notation of accepted facts `d01de728d8b5e33c` and `e0dcff96f9b90a96`, and fix \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\).  In particular,
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\frac{Q}{100},
 \qquad n\geq2,
\tag{1}
\]
and the internal synchronized atlas consists of positive whole-label weights \(\eta_\sigma\), two distinct endpoints \(a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), and occurrence densities \(\widetilde g_{\sigma,y}\) on \(C_y\).  For \(y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), retain the actual pooled density and its mass from accepted fact `e0dcff96f9b90a96`:
\[
 F_y
 :=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
 \widetilde g_{\sigma,y},
 \qquad
 R_y:=\int_{C_y}F_y
 =\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}\\
 y\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}\eta_\sigma.
\tag{2}
\]
Use the canonical internal support
\[
 S_{\mathrm{int}}:=\{y\in S_{\mathrm{miss}}:R_y>0\},
\tag{3}
\]
which is the set denoted \(A_0\) in the imported pooled-kernel draft.  For \(y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\), define the pooled source barycenter
\[
 p_y:=\frac1{R_y}\int_{C_y}xF_y(x)\,dx.
\tag{4}
\]
Also retain the unused half-density complement
\[
 B_y:=\frac12-F_y,
 \qquad
 b_y:=\int_{C_y}B_y=\frac{a_y}{2}-R_y,
\tag{5}
\]
and define its barycenter
\[
 \bar q_y:=\frac1{b_y}\int_{C_y}xB_y(x)\,dx.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(\bar q_y\) is the point called \(q_y\) in the imported draft; the bar distinguishes it from the partner points already present in the canonical atlas notation.

Put \(L_0:=\operatorname{aff}(S_{\mathrm{int}})\), and let
\[
 U_0^{\mathrm{base}}
 :=\operatorname{span}\{(1,y):y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}.
\tag{7}
\]
Define the surjective augmented-base map and the pooled first-moment map
\[
 \mathcal B_0:\mathbb R^{S_{\mathrm{int}}}
 \longrightarrow U_0^{\mathrm{base}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_0\lambda
 :=\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\lambda_y(1,y),
\tag{8}
\]
\[
 \mathcal P_0:\mathbb R^{S_{\mathrm{int}}}
 \longrightarrow\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_0\lambda
 :=\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\lambda_yp_y.
\tag{9}
\]
The explicit pooled-kernel space and its dimension are
\[
 \mathcal U_0
 :=\mathcal P_0(\ker\mathcal B_0)
 +\operatorname{span}\{p_y-c_y:y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\},
 \qquad
 \kappa_0:=\dim\mathcal U_0.
\tag{10}
\]
Then the exact common-chart formula is
\[
 \boxed{
 \min_{s:L_0\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{p_y-s(y),\bar q_y-s(y):y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\}
 =\kappa_0.}
\tag{KR}
\]
Moreover, for every nonempty \(A\subseteq S_{\mathrm{int}}\), the canonical centroid residual rank of accepted fact `d01de728d8b5e33c` satisfies
\[
 r_A^c\leq\kappa_0.
\tag{11}
\]

For every nonempty \(A\subseteq\widehat Y(v)\), write
\[
 \Lambda_v(A)
 :=2^{n-\rho_A}
 \frac{\sum_{y\in A}\widehat\tau_y(v)}{r_A^c+1}.
\tag{12}
\]
One has
\[
 \varnothing\ne S_{\mathrm{int}}\subseteq\widehat Y(v),
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\widehat\tau_y(v)
 \geq\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}R_y
 =2H_{\mathrm{int}}>\frac{7Q}{200}.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently, if \(\kappa_0\leq4\), then the single accepted selector on \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\), whether or not that set is proper, gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq4\gamma_d\Lambda_v(S_{\mathrm{int}})
 >\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{14}
\]
contradicting (1).

The following sharper statement produces the proper subset required by the registered rank-density subgoal:
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &S_{\mathrm{int}}\subsetneq\widehat Y(v)
 \quad\text{and}\quad \kappa_0\leq4,
 \\
 &\hspace{22mm}\text{or}
 \\
 &S_{\mathrm{int}}=\widehat Y(v)
 \quad\text{and}\quad \kappa_0\leq3
 \end{aligned}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists\,\varnothing\ne A\subsetneq\widehat Y(v):
 \Lambda_v(A)>\frac{Q}{400\gamma_d}.}
\tag{PKR}
\]
In the first alternative one may take \(A=S_{\mathrm{int}}\), obtaining
\[
 \Lambda_v(A)>\frac{7Q}{1000}.
\tag{15}
\]
In the second alternative one may delete an endpoint of minimum \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\) from \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\), obtaining
\[
 \Lambda_v(A)>\frac{7Q}{960}.
\tag{16}
\]
No assertion that \(\kappa_0\leq4\), or that \(\kappa_0\leq3\) in the full-support case, is part of this theorem.

## Proof

Accepted fact `e0dcff96f9b90a96` proves that every \(\widetilde g_{\sigma,y}\) in (2) is a nonnegative density on \(C_y\), that
\[
 0\leq F_y\leq\frac12
 \quad\text{a.e. on }C_y,
 \qquad
 R_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4},
\tag{17}
\]
and that \(H_{\mathrm{int}}>7Q/400\).  Each internal label has two distinct endpoints.  Therefore summing (2) over \(S_{\mathrm{miss}}\), and then discarding the zero terms, gives
\[
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}R_y
 =2\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}}\eta_\sigma
 =2H_{\mathrm{int}}>\frac{7Q}{200}.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular, \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\ne\varnothing\), and \(R_y>0\) for every \(y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\), so (4) is defined.  Equations (5) and (17) give
\[
 B_y\geq0,
 \qquad
 b_y=\frac{a_y}{2}-R_y\geq\frac{a_y}{4}>0,
\tag{19}
\]
so \(B_y\) is also an actual nonnegative density and (6) is defined.  This checks the factors \(a_y/2\) and \(a_y/4\); neither \(R_y\) nor \(b_y\) is a normalized probability mass.

The pointwise identity
\[
 F_y+B_y=\frac12\mathbf{1}_{C_y}
\tag{20}
\]
and the centroid identity \(\int_{C_y}x\,dx=a_yc_y\) imply
\[
 R_yp_y+b_y\bar q_y=\frac{a_y}{2}c_y,
 \qquad
 R_y+b_y=\frac{a_y}{2}.
\tag{21}
\]
Subtracting the second equality times \(c_y\) from the first gives
\[
 R_y(p_y-c_y)+b_y(\bar q_y-c_y)=0.
\tag{22}
\]
Hence
\[
 \bar q_y-c_y=-\frac{R_y}{b_y}(p_y-c_y),
 \qquad
 \bar q_y-p_y=-\frac{a_y}{2b_y}(p_y-c_y).
\tag{23}
\]
The scalar \(a_y/(2b_y)\) is positive, so
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\bar q_y-p_y:y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\}
 =\operatorname{span}\{p_y-c_y:y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\}.
\tag{24}
\]

We prove (KR).  An affine map \(s:L_0\to\mathbb R^d\) is equivalently a linear map
\[
 S:U_0^{\mathrm{base}}\to\mathbb R^d
 \quad\text{with}\quad S(1,y)=s(y).
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, every linear relation among the vectors \((1,y)\) is an affine dependence, and every affine map preserves such a dependence; hence the displayed linear extension is well-defined, and its restriction is affine.
For such an \(S\), put
\[
 V_S:=\operatorname{span}
 \{p_y-S(1,y),\bar q_y-S(1,y):y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (24) shows that every \(p_y-c_y\) lies in the span of the differences between the two displayed residual fields, and hence in \(V_S\).  Also, if \(\lambda\in\ker\mathcal B_0\), then
\[
 \mathcal P_0\lambda
 =\sum_y\lambda_y\bigl(p_y-S(1,y)\bigr)
 +S\mathcal B_0\lambda
 =\sum_y\lambda_y\bigl(p_y-S(1,y)\bigr)
 \in V_S.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal U_0\subseteq V_S\) for every affine chart, so
\[
 \dim V_S\geq\kappa_0.
\tag{28}
\]

For the reverse inequality, \(\mathcal B_0\) is surjective by the definition of \(U_0^{\mathrm{base}}\).  Finite-dimensional linear algebra therefore supplies a right inverse
\[
 J:U_0^{\mathrm{base}}\to\mathbb R^{S_{\mathrm{int}}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_0J=I_{U_0^{\mathrm{base}}}.
\tag{29}
\]
Set \(S_0:=\mathcal P_0J\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal P_0-S_0\mathcal B_0
 =\mathcal P_0(I-J\mathcal B_0),
\tag{30}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal B_0(I-J\mathcal B_0)=0.
\tag{31}
\]
Applying (30)-(31) to the coordinate vector at \(y\) shows
\[
 p_y-S_0(1,y)\in\mathcal P_0(\ker\mathcal B_0)
 \subseteq\mathcal U_0.
\tag{32}
\]
By (23)-(24),
\[
 \bar q_y-S_0(1,y)
 =p_y-S_0(1,y)+(\bar q_y-p_y)
 \in\mathcal U_0.
\tag{33}
\]
Therefore \(V_{S_0}\subseteq\mathcal U_0\).  Together with (28), this proves (KR), and also verifies that the right-inverse construction is an affine chart on the original affine hull, rather than a map defined only on a coordinate presentation.

Let \(s_0\) be the affine map represented by \(S_0\).  Equations (10) and (32) give
\[
 c_y-s_0(y)
 =\bigl(p_y-s_0(y)\bigr)-(p_y-c_y)
 \in\mathcal U_0.
\tag{34}
\]
For any nonempty \(A\subseteq S_{\mathrm{int}}\), restricting this one map to \(\operatorname{aff}(A)\) gives
\[
 r_A^c
 \leq\dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s_0(y):y\in A\}
 \leq\kappa_0,
\tag{35}
\]
which proves (11).

Accepted fact `d01de728d8b5e33c` preserves every internal whole label with its exact weight:
\[
 \widehat\theta_\sigma(v)=\eta_\sigma
 \quad(\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{int}}).
\tag{36}
\]
At each physical endpoint, the synchronized mass \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\) sums all retained incident labels.  Equations (2) and (36) therefore give
\[
 \widehat\tau_y(v)\geq R_y>0
 \quad(y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}).
\tag{37}
\]
This proves \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\subseteq\widehat Y(v)\), and (18) and (37) prove (13).

For later numerical comparisons, for every integer \(d\geq1\),
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d>\frac4{11}.
\tag{38}
\]
Indeed, the binomial expansion gives \((1+1/d)^d<e\), while
\[
 e
 =1+1+\frac12+\frac16+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(4+j)!}
 \leq\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{5^j}
 =\frac{87}{32}<\frac{11}{4}.
\tag{39}
\]
Taking reciprocals proves (38).

Apply equation (38) of accepted fact `d01de728d8b5e33c` to the single endpoint set \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\).  Since \(\rho_{S_{\mathrm{int}}}\leq n\), equations (11) and (13) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 &\geq4\gamma_d\Lambda_v(S_{\mathrm{int}})\\
 &\geq4\gamma_d
 \frac{\sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\widehat\tau_y(v)}{\kappa_0+1}\\
 &>\frac{7\gamma_d}{50(\kappa_0+1)}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
If \(\kappa_0\leq4\), then (38)-(40) yield
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 >\frac{7\gamma_d}{250}Q
 >\frac{14}{1375}Q
 >\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{41}
\]
where \(14/1375-1/100=1/5500\).  This proves the strengthened direct contradiction (14), including the full-support case.

It remains to produce the promised proper subsets.  First suppose
\[
 S_{\mathrm{int}}\subsetneq\widehat Y(v),
 \qquad
 \kappa_0\leq4.
\tag{42}
\]
Take \(A=S_{\mathrm{int}}\).  This set is nonempty and proper.  Equations (11)-(13) give
\[
 \Lambda_v(A)
 \geq\frac{
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\widehat\tau_y(v)}5
 >\frac{7Q}{1000}.
\tag{43}
\]
By (38),
\[
 \frac{Q}{400\gamma_d}<\frac{11Q}{1600},
 \qquad
 \frac7{1000}-\frac{11}{1600}=\frac1{8000}>0.
\tag{44}
\]
Thus (43) crosses the registered threshold strictly.

Now suppose
\[
 S_{\mathrm{int}}=\widehat Y(v),
 \qquad
 \kappa_0\leq3.
\tag{45}
\]
Accepted fact `d01de728d8b5e33c` gives
\[
 |S_{\mathrm{int}}|=|\widehat Y(v)|\geq n+4\geq6.
\tag{46}
\]
Choose \(y_0\in S_{\mathrm{int}}\) of minimum \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\), and put
\[
 A:=S_{\mathrm{int}}\setminus\{y_0\}.
\tag{47}
\]
This set is nonempty and proper.  Equations (13) and (46), together with the minimum choice, give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{y\in A}\widehat\tau_y(v)
 &\geq
 \left(1-\frac1{|S_{\mathrm{int}}|}\right)
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\widehat\tau_y(v)\\
 &\geq\frac56
 \sum_{y\in S_{\mathrm{int}}}\widehat\tau_y(v)
 >\frac{7Q}{240}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Equations (11), (12), and (45) now give
\[
 \Lambda_v(A)>\frac{7Q}{960}.
\tag{49}
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac7{960}-\frac{11}{1600}=\frac1{2400}>0,
\tag{50}
\]
so (44), (49), and (50) prove the full-support alternative of (PKR).

Every selector bound above is exactly one application of the accepted centroid selector in `d01de728d8b5e33c`, with the accepted physical weights \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\) on one endpoint set and the physical centroids \(c_y\).  That accepted theorem establishes both the selector and the calibration bound \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\leq a_y/4\) at each selected fiber; no second simultaneous density is constructed here.  The pooled masses \(R_y\) are used only as scalar lower bounds in (37); they are never added to \(\widehat\tau_y(v)\), substituted for an atlas label, or realized as a second selector.  Thus no source capacity, target atom, or selector weight is reused.  In either alternative of (PKR), one final application gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq4\gamma_d\Lambda_v(A)
 >\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{51}
\]
contradicting (1).  The theorem proves only the exact formula and these conditional closures; it does not prove the missing upper bound on \(\kappa_0\).
