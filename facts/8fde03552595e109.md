## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and lattice-complete with at least two points. Let $K$ be a full-dimensional $d$-simplex with centroid zero and volume $V$, and let $r_zK$ be positive compatible fibers at $z\in P$. Use the $d+1$ fixed continuous canonical centroid caps on every fiber, with total mass $M=V\sum_zr_z^d$ and $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For $R>\max_{z\in P}\|z\|^2$, set
$$
t_z=r_z+\varepsilon(R-\|z\|^2),\qquad
\lambda=\left(\frac{\sum_zr_z^d}{\sum_zt_z^d}\right)^{1/d},\qquad s_z=\lambda t_z.
$$
For every $\varepsilon\ge0$ the family $s_zK$ is compatible and preserves $M$ and each canonical budget $\gamma_dM$. If $\varepsilon>0$, every anchored radius gap for a representation of its target using only other sites is at least $\lambda\varepsilon\min_{p\ne q}\|p-q\|^2$. Consequently there is an open product neighborhood of independently variable convex fibers around this family, each realized with exactly the original integer bases. An affine shear for the centroid field or use of the induced relative lattice preserves these conclusions.

If $d\ge2$, there are arbitrarily close actual compatible simplex families whose total volume is strictly larger and whose mass in each fixed canonical cap is strictly smaller. At any strictly compatible family, let $V_p,V_q$ be the positive volumes of two distinct fibers, $m=d+1$, and let $c$ be a nonzero zero-sum column vector in $\mathbb R^m$. The opposing weighted vertex lifts used below have aggregate Lagrangian Hessian
$$
H=\frac{d^2-1}{d(dm-1)}\|c\|^2\left(V_p+V_p^2/V_q\right)>0.
$$
A vertex acceleration gives second derivative $-\gamma_dH/2$ in every aggregate cap and $H/2$ in the total volume. For $d=1$ both fixed canonical cap budgets cannot be lowered while increasing total volume.

## Proof

Compatibility of positive homothets of $K$ is the radius inequality $r_z\ge\sum_j\alpha_jr_{p_j}$ whenever $z=\sum_j\alpha_jp_j$ with positive convex coefficients. The radius change gives
$$
s_z-\sum_j\alpha_js_{p_j}
=\lambda\left(r_z-\sum_j\alpha_jr_{p_j}+
          \varepsilon\sum_j\alpha_j\|p_j-z\|^2\right).
$$
This is nonnegative and gives the claimed uniform gap when every $p_j\ne z$. A representation using $z$ itself reduces to one excluding it after cancelling its weight, unless it is trivial. On each unit continuous normal the support function gap is at least the radius gap times $\min_{\|u\|=1}h_K(u)>0$. This uniform margin persists under sufficiently small independent Hausdorff perturbations of the finitely many fibers, since their support functions change uniformly. The convex hull of the placed fibers therefore has precisely those fibers at $P$. Its projection is $\operatorname{conv}P$, whose integer points are exactly $P$. The common rescaling preserves total mass, and every centered homothet contributes its fraction $\gamma_d$ to each fixed canonical cap. Shearing by an affine centroid field maps both the fibers and the canonical halfspaces affinely and preserves the lattice bases. The same argument works in the affine lattice containing $P$.

Work now at a strictly compatible family. In barycentric coordinates of each of the two chosen homothets, the cap formulas and Lagrangian of e42e4ad8eaa4f705 apply with that fiber's volume as prefactor. Let $B$ be the zero-column-sum vertex matrix from its minimum-norm lift of $c$. Its quadratic value per unit volume is $(d^2-1)\|c\|^2/[d(dm-1)]$. Give fiber $p$ velocity $B$, fiber $q$ velocity $-(V_p/V_q)B$, and all others zero. The first derivatives of each aggregate cap cancel because they are proportional to volume and linear in the velocity. The total volume's first derivative cancels for the same reason. The aggregate Lagrangian is total volume minus $(m\gamma_d)^{-1}$ times the sum of aggregate cap masses, so it is stationary and its Hessian on this velocity is exactly $H$.

The first derivative map for the $m$ caps of even one positive fiber is onto. Indeed for a column-sum-zero matrix $D$ the vector of derivatives divided by $\gamma_d$ times that fiber's volume is $\operatorname{tr}(D)\mathbf1+D\mathbf1$. A scalar multiple of $I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/m$ gives any constant part, while $a\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/m$ gives any zero-sum part $a$. Use an acceleration on that one fiber to make every aggregate cap second derivative equal to $-\gamma_dH/2$. By stationarity this leaves the Lagrangian second derivative equal to $H$, so the total volume second derivative is $H/2$. For sufficiently small positive time the resulting degree-two vertex path lies in the open compatible neighborhood and gives both strict inequalities. The initial strictification can be made arbitrarily small. In dimension one the two fixed halfspaces partition each continuous line up to its single boundary point, so the sum of their masses is the total volume.
