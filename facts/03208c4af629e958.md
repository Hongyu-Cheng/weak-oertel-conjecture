## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put \(C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(D_+=\{z:w_z>0\}\), and \(W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)\), with empty fibers having volume zero. Assume that every pair \(x,y\in D_+\) having the same coordinatewise base parity satisfies

\[
w_{(x+y)/2}
=\left(\frac{w_x^{1/d}+w_y^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]

If \(W>0\), define the true original volume centroids \(b_z=w_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}y\,dy\), the actual graph \(G=\{(z,b_z):z\in D_+\}\), and \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). Then, for every pair in (1), the midpoint fiber is positive and

\[
C_{(x+y)/2}=\frac{C_x+C_y}{2},
\qquad
b_{(x+y)/2}=\frac{b_x+b_y}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]

For distinct endpoints, the two endpoint fibers are positive homothets. The Helly number of intersections of \(G\) with ordinary ambient convex sets is at most \(2^n\). There exists one \(z^\star\in D_+\), chosen before any tested halfspace, such that \(\mathbf y^\star=(z^\star,b_{z^\star})\in G\subseteq S\) and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\(z,b_z)\in H}}w_z\ge2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}W\). Since \(\gamma_d>1/e\), this proves the locked conclusion on class (1) with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/(2e)\). If \(W=0\), the locked conclusion holds at every point of \(S\).

The actual-fiber symbols \(C_z,w_z,D_+,W,b_z,\gamma_d\) have the same meanings as in \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\); our \(G\) is the support of its joint centroid law \(\nu_{zb}\). The graph labels and weights in \(\texttt{bee3cc8e50a0dcea}\) are here the actual \(b_z,w_z\), and its location set \(T\) is exactly \(G\). For the pairwise use of \(\texttt{f6641f09c19819a3}\), its \(L=2\), \(A_1=C_x\), \(A_2=C_y\), and \(t_1=t_2=1/2\); its \(M=(C_x+C_y)/2\), \(V_t=w_{(x+y)/2}\) by (1), and \(m_t=(b_x+b_y)/2\). Its \(K\) is the actual midpoint fiber, and \(b_K\) is its true centroid. No transport mean replaces a true centroid without the equality argument below.

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty. Hence all mixed-volume sums are finite. Every zero-volume fiber has zero volume in each of its halfspace sections, so \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). If \(W=0\), nonnegativity proves the claimed zero lower bound at every point of the assumed nonempty \(S\). We therefore assume \(W>0\). The set \(D_+\) is finite and nonempty, and every \(C_z\), \(z\in D_+\), is compact, convex, and full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^d\). The centroids are well-defined and belong to their fibers. The latter assertion is also proved directly in accepted fact \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\). Thus \(G\subseteq S\) is a finite graph with exactly one point per positive integer base.

Fix \(x,y\in D_+\) of the same coordinatewise parity and put \(u=(x+y)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\). If \(x=y\), (2) follows from convexity of \(C_x\), since \((C_x+C_x)/2=C_x\). Suppose henceforth that \(x\ne y\). Convexity of the one original body gives \((C_x+C_y)/2\subseteq C_u\). This average contains a translate of a half-scale copy of \(C_x\), obtained by fixing any point of \(C_y\), so \(C_u\) has positive \(d\)-volume. As a compact convex set of positive volume, it is a convex body. This verifies the target-body hypothesis of \(\texttt{f6641f09c19819a3}\) independently of any centroid conclusion.

Use that accepted transport lemma with exactly the pairwise aliases in the statement. It gives an absolutely continuous probability \(\mu=\rho(v)\,dv\), topologically supported in \((C_x+C_y)/2\), with mean \((b_x+b_y)/2\) and density \(0\le\rho\le1/w_u\) almost everywhere. Since \(\mu\) is supported in \(C_u\) and \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_u)=w_u\),

\[
\int_{C_u}\left(\frac1{w_u}-\rho(v)\right)\,dv
=1-1=0.
\tag{4}
\]

The integrand is nonnegative almost everywhere. It is therefore zero almost everywhere, which identifies \(\mu\) exactly with the uniform probability on the original midpoint fiber. Their means agree, proving \(b_u=(b_x+b_y)/2\). This is an exact equality of true uniform centroids, with no error or covariance tolerance.

The support of \(\mu\) is contained in the compact Minkowski average. The uniform-law identification therefore also gives \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_u\setminus((C_x+C_y)/2))=0\). A full-dimensional compact convex subset of a convex body with the same volume equals that body. To verify the last assertion here, if a point of the larger body lay outside the smaller compact convex set, strict separation by a hyperplane would place that point strictly beyond the smaller set. Combining the point with a small ball in the interior of the smaller set would give a ball of positive radius in the larger body beyond that hyperplane, a positive-volume subset of the difference. This contradicts the zero difference volume. Consequently \(C_u=(C_x+C_y)/2\), completing (2).

This also verifies equality in the Brunn-Minkowski inequality itself: the volume of \((C_x+C_y)/2\) equals \(((w_x^{1/d}+w_y^{1/d})/2)^d\). The positive-volume convex-body equality form recorded and checked in accepted fact \(\texttt{e4253a0d6f78849c}\) applies to these two actual endpoint bodies at parameter \(1/2\). It gives \(C_y=a+\lambda C_x\) for some \(a\in\mathbb R^d\), \(\lambda>0\), with \(\lambda=(w_y/w_x)^{1/d}\) by volume scaling. In dimension one this is also immediate because both fibers are positive-length closed intervals. There is no need for the equal-volume specialization \(\lambda=1\) used in that predecessor. As an additional direct check of the centroid equality, convexity gives \((C_x+C_y)/2=a/2+((1+\lambda)/2)C_x\), and the affine change of variables in the centroid integral gives its centroid \(a/2+((1+\lambda)/2)b_x=(b_x+b_y)/2\).

Equation (2) says exactly that the midpoint of every two graph points with the same base parity is another point of the same actual graph. Apply accepted fact \(\texttt{bee3cc8e50a0dcea}\) with \(D=D_+\), its labels equal to the actual \(b_z\), its \(T=G\), and its finite nonnegative weights equal to the original positive \(w_z\). All hypotheses are now checked: \(D_+\) is finite and nonempty, there is one graph point per base, and exact ambient midpoint closure holds. That fact yields the asserted Helly bound and one \(\mathbf y^\star=(z^\star,b_{z^\star})\in G\) whose containing closed ambient halfspaces all have original graph weight at least \(2^{-n}W\). This is the first inequality in (3). The chosen point is fixed by the graph and original weights before the halfspace is selected. No affine rank bound on the centroids and no global affine dependence of them are required.

For completeness, check the transfer to all actual closed halfspaces at this same point. Let \(H=\{(z,v):\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot v\ge t\}\) be any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\). For each \(z\in D_+\) with \((z,b_z)\in H\), the fiber halfspace \(\{v:\beta\cdot(v-b_z)\ge0\}\) is contained in its section \(H_z\). If \(\beta\ne0\), the original-centroid Grünbaum input in accepted fact \(\texttt{c6b7777f667984de}\) gives this cap volume at least \(\gamma_dw_z\). Its hypotheses hold because \(C_z\) is a positive-volume convex body and \(b_z\) is its true uniform-volume centroid. If \(\beta=0\), the entire fiber lies in \(H\), so its volume is \(w_z\ge\gamma_dw_z\). Summing over exactly these graph-selected bases gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&=\sum_{z\in D_+}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\\
&\ge\gamma_d\sum_{\substack{z\in D_+\\(z,b_z)\in H}}w_z
\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

The argument includes centroids on the boundary of \(H\), vertical halfspaces, and boundaries containing whole fibers. Zero-volume fibers contribute zero and need no centroids. If the whole space is included in the halfspace convention, its volume is \(W\), so it also satisfies (5). Taking the infimum over all closed halfspaces containing this fixed point proves the depth bound. Finally, \(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\) gives \(\log\gamma_d=-d\log(1+1/d)>-1\). Hence \(\gamma_d>1/e\), and the claimed absolute constant follows.

We check the overlap precisely. Accepted fact \(\texttt{e4253a0d6f78849c}\) assumes all positive original fibers have one common volume. In that class, convexity makes every same-parity midpoint positive, after which the common-volume hypothesis forces (1). Thus its class is contained in the present class and its numerical bound agrees exactly with (3). Accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\) supplies the same numerical bound when the root-volume profile agrees with a positive affine function, or more generally when it has an exact positive convex reference on \(\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\). The present condition does not require either kind of global reference. The following exact body shows that neither the common-volume hypothesis nor an exact convex root-volume reference follows from (1).

Let \(n=3,d=1\), let \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,1),(1,2)\}\), and set \(m=(1,1)\). Let \(C'\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\) be the convex hull of the three vertical segments \(\{v\}\times[0,1]\) at the vertices \(v\) of \(P\), together with the point \((m,2)\). Define the actual body \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\) by \(C=\{((x,s),v):(x,v)\in C',\ -2\le s\le2\}\). Both \(C'\) and \(C\) are compact and convex. The prism \(P\times[0,1]\) is contained in \(C'\), so they have nonempty interior in their respective ambient dimensions.

The triangle has exactly the four integer points \((0,0),(2,1),(1,2),m\). To check the census directly, its inequalities are \(y\ge x/2\), \(y\le2x\), and \(x+y\le3\). For integer \(x\), these leave respectively \(y=0\) at \(x=0\), \(y=1,2\) at \(x=1\), \(y=1\) at \(x=2\), and no possibilities at other \(x\). At each vertex, extremality of its base makes its fiber in \(C'\) exactly \([0,1]\). At \(m\), the fiber is exactly \([0,2]\): the vertical segment from \((m,0)\) to \((m,2)\) is present, and every generating height is between zero and two. Thus the complete positive integer support of \(C\) is these four base points times \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\), with weights one at every vertex base and two at \(m\), independent of the third coordinate.

The four triangle points have pairwise different coordinatewise parities. Hence a same-parity pair in this complete support must have the same triangle base and third coordinates of the same parity. Its integer midpoint has that same triangle base and an integer third coordinate in \([-2,2]\). Both endpoint fibers and the midpoint fiber are the identical interval, verifying (1) for every pair, including distinct pairs. The volumes are unequal across triangle bases. Further, \(m\) is the average of the three vertices. If a convex function on \(P\times[-2,2]\) agreed with the root volumes at every supported integer base, convexity at the three bases with third coordinate zero would give its value at \((m,0)\) at most one, whereas agreement requires two. Thus no exact positive convex root-volume reference exists, and in particular no exact affine one exists. This calibration only establishes the distinction between hypotheses; the depth theorem for it is already a consequence of the general proof above.
