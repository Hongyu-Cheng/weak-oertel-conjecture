## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, and let $T$ be a $d$-simplex of volume $V$ and centroid $g$. At the four binary bases in two integer dimensions put $T$. Add $A=g+r(T-g)$ of volume $a=r^dV$ at each of $p=(-1,1)$ and $p'=(1,-1)$, with $0<r\le1$. These are exactly the six integer fibers of the convex hull of the placed bodies. At the nonanchor core point $((0,0),g)$ the exact raw depth is $\Gamma_d V+2\Gamma_d a$, while both binary exposure costs $\chi_q(a)$ and $\Omega_{q,i}$ equal $a$ for every $i$. The gap to the resulting upper certificate is $(1-2\Gamma_d)a>0$. Among canonical caps at this point excluding the other core fibers and taking each addition wholly or with zero mass, the exact least extra mass is $a$.

If $a\le\rho_d V$, the global raw maximum over all sampled points is $\Gamma_d V+a$, attained precisely at $((0,1),g)$ and $((1,0),g)$.

## Proof

The base hull is $\lbrace z:z_1\le1,\ z_2\le1,\ z_1+z_2\ge0\rbrace$. Its integer rows at $z_1=-1,0,1$ have respectively one, two, and three sites. The added bases are exposed by minimizing the coordinate equal to $-1$, so their hull fibers equal $A$. The other four fibers remain exactly $T$ because all continuous generators lie there.

Write $q=(0,0)$, $b=1/(d+1)$ and $h=1-b$. A nonvertical through-cap at $(q,g)$ has opposite shifts from the centroid on the two extra fibers. The paired centroid tail theorem 6a7d56e450b3126f gives at least $2\Gamma_d a$ there, while the own trace gives at least $\Gamma_d V$ by the simplex profile. Vertical through-caps contain the own fiber and at least one of the two opposite extra bases, so their mass is at least $V+a>\Gamma_d V+2\Gamma_d a$. For an upper bound use $\lambda_i(x)-h(z_1+z_2)\ge b$. It captures $\Gamma_d V$ at $q$ and $\Gamma_d a$ at each added base, since the two extra cuts are canonical centroid cuts of the homothetic simplices. The adjacent core traces are supporting and the remaining core fiber is excluded. Thus this cap attains the exact depth. The caps over all $i$ cover the whole $q$ fiber with the same mass.

In the two exposure systems both opposite bases cannot be excluded. In the directional test the inequalities require $\eta\cdot p\ge rh$ and $\eta\cdot p'\ge rh>0$, and in the base-only test both lower bounds are one. Either individual inequality is feasible by taking the difference between the two positive coordinates of $\eta$ sufficiently large in the desired sign. Thus each cost is $a$. In the actual canonical caps a sufficiently unequal positive tilt also captures the other extra fiber wholly, since its $\lambda_i$ range is $[b-rb,b+rh]$. This proves exact minimal binary cost. The gap is linear in $a$ and is positive since $d\ge2$.

For $a\le\rho_d V$ the aggregate credit theorem gives $\Gamma_d V+a$ at the centroids of their two anchors. The affine functions $z_1+1-z_2$ and $1-z_1+z_2$ vanish at these respective anchors, equal $-1$ at their owned base, and are positive integers at all remaining bases. Subtracting twice the appropriate function from a canonical cap gives an upper cover with mass $\Gamma_d V+a$ there. Away from the centroid raise the threshold to an own coordinate $\lambda_i(y)>b$, strictly decreasing the mass. At $q$ the cover above costs $\Gamma_d V+2\Gamma_d a<\Gamma_d V+a$. The cap with twice the tilt $2-z_1-z_2$ isolates the remaining core vertex, and exposed vertical caps of mass $a$ cover the added fibers. These comparisons prove the global statement on all six fibers.
