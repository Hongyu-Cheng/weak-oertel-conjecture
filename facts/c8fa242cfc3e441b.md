## Statement

For every row-stochastic N by N matrix P with zero diagonal and strictly positive off-diagonal entries, and every proper missed-row set J, the retained barycentric polytope {lambda>=0,sum lambda=1,lambda_J<=(P lambda)_J} has exactly |J^c|2^|J| vertices, indexed explicitly by one surviving root and a subset of active missed rows. A proved sum-preserving transform gives a simplex-capacity bound using the N largest distinct vertex norm-squares. For the displayed exact rational6 by6 matrix, all15 two-hit cones have capacity strictly below1/40 by the included exact certificate; hence all qualifying5-simplex sources hit at least three rows and the fixed centroid-fan score is at leastGamma_5+(763/7776)(1-v). No arbitrary-fan or full OC conclusion is claimed.

## Proof

# Finite-vertex stochastic-cone capacity certificates

This note gives a geometric refinement of the absorption-only capacity
bound. Its general lemma applies to every strictly positive off-diagonal
stochastic matrix. The final exact example concerns one 6-by-6 matrix;
it does not prove a statement for all positive fans or Oertel's conjecture.

## 1. Every retained-cone vertex has one surviving root

Let N>=2 and let P be an N-by-N row-stochastic matrix with P_jj=0 and
P_jr>0 for every j!=r. Fix a proper set J of row indices and put R=J^c.
In the standard barycentric simplex

    Delta={lambda>=0: sum lambda_j=1},

consider the retained polytope

    K_J={lambda in Delta: lambda_j<=(P lambda)_j for j in J}.  (1)

For every r in R and every A subset J, define a point v(r,A) by

    h_A=(I-P_AA)^(-1)P_Ar,
    v_r=1/(1+sum_(j in A)h_j),
    v_A=h_A v_r,
    v_j=0 otherwise.                                         (2)

For A empty, this means v(r,empty)=e_r. The inverse in (2) exists:
each row sum of P_AA is strictly below1, so its nonnegative Neumann
series converges. If A is nonempty, h_A is strictly positive.

**Vertex lemma.** The vertices of K_J are exactly the |R|2^|J| distinct
points (2).

**Proof.** The point (2) is feasible. Its inequalities indexed by A are
equalities by construction. At any j in J outside A, its coordinate is
zero, while (P v)_j is positive because v_r>0 and P_jr>0. Fixing all
zero coordinates and the equalities at A leaves a unique point, by the
invertibility in (2) and the sum-one equation. Hence (2) is a vertex.
Its support is A union {r}, so all these points are distinct.

Conversely, let lambda be any vertex and let A be its positive-coordinate
indices in J, and R_+ its positive-coordinate indices in R. First R_+
is nonempty. Indeed, if lambda_R=0, then lambda_J<=P_JJ lambda_J, and the
nonnegative inverse of I-P_JJ gives lambda_J<=0, contradicting sum1.
Every inequality indexed by J outside A is strict: its left side is zero,
and its right side has a positive contribution from any coordinate in
R_+. Within the relative interior of the coordinate face with support
A union R_+, the only potentially active inequalities are consequently
those indexed by A. That face has dimension |A|+|R_+|-1.
If |R_+|>=2 or fewer than |A| of these inequalities are active, their
number is strictly smaller than the face dimension. There is then a
nonzero perturbation preserving the sum and the active equations; both
sufficiently small signs preserve positive coordinates and all strict
inequalities. This contradicts extremality. Thus R_+={r} and every A
inequality is active. The unique solution is exactly (2). QED.

## 2. A rational family of norm certificates

Put

    H=(I-P_JJ)^(-1)P_JR,
    m_j=max_(r in R)H_jr.

The matrix H is nonnegative and row-stochastic, since
(I-P_JJ)1=P_JR 1. For every lambda=(y_J,x_R) in K_J,

    0<=y<=Hx.                                                (3)

Choose arbitrary numbers t_j>=0 satisfying t_j^2 m_j<=1, and define

    T(y,x)=((1+t_j)y_j, x-H^T(t*y)),
    A_T=det T=product_(j in J)(1+t_j)>0.                      (4)

Here t*y is componentwise multiplication. The map preserves the sum
of coordinates. Set X=sum x, Y=sum y, and z=H^T(t*y). Since y_j<=m_j X,

    sum y_j^2<=XY,
    sum t_j^2 y_j^2<=XY,
    ||x||_2<=X,
    ||z||_2<=sum_j t_j y_j ||H_j||_2<=sum_j y_j=Y.             (5)

For the last inequality, use ||H_j||_2^2<=m_j and t_j sqrt(m_j)<=1.
Expanding the norm in (4) and using y_j<=(Hx)_j gives

    ||T lambda||_2^2
      <=sum_j (1+t_j^2)y_j^2+||x||_2^2+||z||_2^2
      <=2XY+X^2+Y^2=1.                                      (6)

The transformed R-coordinates can be negative; no orthant assumption
is used. All t_j may be rational. For example, a rational upper bound
u_j>=sqrt(m_j) gives the permitted rational choice t_j=1/u_j.

## 3. The largest-distinct-vertex-norm bound

List the vertices (2), and arrange their norm squares in decreasing order:

    q_1>=q_2>=...>=q_L,  q_l=||T v_l||_2^2,  L=|R|2^|J|.

Then every N-vertex simplex Q contained in K_J has relative volume at most

    |Q|/|Delta| <= A_T^(-1) sqrt(product_(l=1)^N q_l).         (7)

Here relative volume is the usual (N-1)-dimensional Euclidean volume
ratio, equivalently the absolute determinant of the barycentric vertex
matrix. Formula (7) also covers J empty, when L=N and T is the identity.

**Proof.** The absolute determinant is continuous on compact K_J^N, so it
has a maximum. It is convex in each one of its vertex columns separately.
Starting from a maximizer, move each column in turn to a vertex of K_J
where this convex function takes at least its old value; it still has the
global maximum. Thus the maximum is achieved by vertices from (2). If
it is nonzero, its N columns are distinct. Hadamard's inequality gives

    A_T |det Lambda|=|det(T Lambda)|
        <=product_columns ||T lambda||_2
        <=sqrt(product_(l=1)^N q_l).

If the determinant is zero, (7) is immediate. QED.

Unlike the bound A_T^(-1), formula (7) keeps the defects of the actual
retained-cone vertices. It also accounts for the fact that a simplex cannot
reuse a single best vertex as several independent columns. It is still an
upper bound; no assertion of attainability is made.

## 5. The actual matrix fan has a three-hit certificate

Let B be any5-simplex of volume vV with 0<v<=1 and use the centroid fan
with vertex values I-P, where P is(8). Every simplex source P_i subset B
of volume at least V/32 strictly hits at least THREE cuts. Otherwise its
hit set extends to a two-element R, so it lies in K_(R^c), whose capacity
by(9) is less than |B|/40<=V/40<V/32, a contradiction. Strict contact
is treated as a miss; hence the argument respects closed retained cones.

If normalized source weights sum to E=1-v, their scores therefore satisfy

    max_j [v p_j+sum_i e_i 1{P_i strictly hits row j}]
      >=Gamma_5 v+E/2
      =Gamma_5+(763/7776)E,                                 (12)

because every own vertex cap has p_j>=Gamma_5 by arithmetic-geometric
mean. For the relaxation's v=9/10, the positive surplus is763/77760.
Thus the particular rational absorption-only obstruction cannot be
geometrically realized with the required source volumes, even if one
allows arbitrary choices of actual simplices and weights. The conclusion
comes from source vertex geometry and distinctness, not from absorption
maxima alone. The general sharp all-fan inequality remains unproved.
