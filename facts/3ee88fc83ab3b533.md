## Statement

Retain the convex-admissible potentials and the lower convex roof \(\widehat\phi\) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The potential values here need not have minimum zero, exactly as in that fact's stability assertion. Throughout, \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), and \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). All logarithms are natural.

First let \(r\ge1\), let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope with \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\). Let \(\mathcal K_D\) be the cone of all real convex-admissible value vectors on this complete support. For an arbitrary value vector \(u\in\mathbb R^D\), let \(\boldsymbol\theta(u)\) be the unique vector for which the law proportional to \(e^{-u_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta(u)\cdot\mathbf I}\) has mean zero. Write this law as \(p^u\), and define

\[
\mathcal G(u)
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p^u_{\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}-u_{\mathbf0}.
\tag{1}
\]

This is also the original-mean energy gap of the effective convex-admissible potential \(u_{\mathbf I}-\boldsymbol\theta(u)\cdot\mathbf I\) whenever \(u\in\mathcal K_D\). It is unchanged when an affine function is added to \(u\). At an originally centered potential \(\phi\), the vector \(\boldsymbol\theta(\phi)\) is zero, so \(\mathcal G(\phi)=g\) for the original law in the open subgoal.

At such a \(\phi\), use expectation with respect to its original law \(p\), and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma&=\mathbb E_p[\mathbf I\mathbf I^{\mathsf T}],
&\mathbf a_h&=\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb E_p[\mathbf I h_{\mathbf I}],\\
(Rh)_{\mathbf I}
&=h_{\mathbf I}-\mathbb E_p h-\mathbf a_h\cdot\mathbf I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

For arbitrary real value directions \(h,k\), the exact variations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
D\boldsymbol\theta(\phi)[h]&=\mathbf a_h,\\
D^2\boldsymbol\theta(\phi)[h,k]
&=-\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb E_p[\mathbf I(Rh)(Rk)],\\
D\mathcal G(\phi)[h]
&=\mathbb E_p h-h_{\mathbf0}-\mathbb E_p[(R\phi)(Rh)],\\
D^2\mathcal G(\phi)[h,k]
&=\mathbb E_p[((R\phi)-2)(Rh)(Rk)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

All these derivatives exist in the ambient vector space; feasible derivatives are their restrictions to the relevant face or tangent cone of \(\mathcal K_D\). If \(\phi\) is stationary in the ambient quotient by affine functions, its Hessian is strictly negative in every nonaffine direction. Such a stationary point can lie in the interior of a cone having dimension greater than one after quotienting by affine functions.

More precisely, let
\(Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,0),(1,-1)\}\), with these four vertices denoted cyclically by \(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\). Then \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^2=\{\mathbf0,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\}\), and \(\mathbf0\) is its only interior lattice point. There is a centered convex-admissible potential on this nonsymmetric support which is a strict global maximum of \(\mathcal G\) modulo affine functions and lies in the interior of its two-dimensional cone modulo affine functions. In particular, its class is not an extreme ray.

For the stronger support obstruction, retain the capacity notation \(B(P)\) from accepted fact `c38daee277a73322`, using dilation parameter one. For every full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) with sole interior lattice point \(\mathbf0\), the maximum original-mean energy gap over all real potentials on \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\) whose original law has mean zero is exactly the unique positive number \(m\) satisfying

\[
m e^{m+1}=B(P)-1.
\tag{4}
\]

The maximizing law has a convex-admissible potential. Thus this is also the maximum over the convex-admissible class. The capacity bound in `c38daee277a73322` implies \(m e^{m+1}\le4^r-1\). In particular, every such law in rank at most two, including a law supported on only part of the boundary together with zero, has energy gap strictly less than \(3/2\).

On the other hand, set

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_\Delta&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,-1),(2,-1),(-1,2)\},\\
D&=5P_\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^2,
&\phi_{(x,y)}&=(\log2)\max\{-x,-y,x+y\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

This complete nonsymmetric support and globally convex potential have original weighted mean zero and satisfy

\[
g=\frac{1269}{545}\log2>\frac32.
\tag{6}
\]

Consequently no maximization-preserving reduction can replace every centered convex-admissible law by a law on a one-interior lattice polytope of the same or smaller rank. On the fixed support in (5), there is also no sequence of centered convex-admissible laws, all with energy gap at least (6), whose limiting positive support is a one-interior lattice polytope in its intrinsic lattice.

## Proof

We first verify the variations, keeping the original mean throughout. For arbitrary \(u\), consider the finite exponential sum \(Z(u,\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{-u_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\). Since \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), there is \(\varepsilon>0\) for which \(\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I\ge\varepsilon\|\boldsymbol\theta\|\). Consequently \(Z(u,\boldsymbol\theta)\ge e^{-\max_D u+\varepsilon\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}\), so it is coercive in \(\boldsymbol\theta\). The Hessian of \(\log Z\) in \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is the covariance matrix of a positive law on the full-span set \(D\), hence is positive definite. Thus the minimizer exists and is unique; its derivative equation is exactly the zero-mean equation. The positive definite covariance also makes the implicit zero-mean equation locally smooth of every order. These facts prove the asserted existence, uniqueness, and differentiability of \(\boldsymbol\theta(u)\). Adding an affine function to \(u\) translates its linear tilt and changes the minimized log partition function by its constant part, without changing \(p^u\) or \(\mathcal G(u)\).

At the originally centered \(\phi\), \(\Sigma\) in (2) is positive definite. By construction, \(Rh\) has mean zero and is orthogonal to every coordinate function: \(\mathbb E_p Rh=0\) and \(\mathbb E_p[\mathbf I Rh]=\mathbf0\). Differentiate the zero-mean equation in direction \(h\). It gives \(\Sigma D\boldsymbol\theta[h]=\mathbb E_p[\mathbf I h]\), proving the first line of (3). Differentiation of the normalized law then gives \(Dp_{\mathbf I}[h]=-p_{\mathbf I}(Rh)_{\mathbf I}\).

Put \(F(u)=\log Z(u,\boldsymbol\theta(u))\). The zero-mean equation cancels all first derivatives of the tilt in the envelope derivative. Direct differentiation, followed by the orthogonality just proved, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
DF(u)[h]&=-\mathbb E_{p^u}h,\\
D^2F(\phi)[h,k]&=\mathbb E_p[(Rh)(Rk)],\\
D^3F(\phi)[h,k,\ell]&=-\mathbb E_p[(Rh)(Rk)(R\ell)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

For the last identity, when the base potential changes, the derivative of \(Rh\), with \(h\) fixed, is an affine function of \(\mathbf I\). Its inner product with \(Rk\) is zero. The analogous term for the derivative of \(Rk\) is also zero. Thus differentiation of their inner product leaves only the derivative of the probability weights, which is multiplication by \(-R\ell\). This proves the third derivative in (7), including all derivatives of the centering tilt.

The mixed second derivative of the normalized law is
\(D^2p_{\mathbf I}[h,k]=p_{\mathbf I}((Rh)_{\mathbf I}(Rk)_{\mathbf I}+D^2\boldsymbol\theta[h,k]\cdot\mathbf I-\mathbb E_p[(Rh)(Rk)])\). Multiplying this by \(\mathbf I\), summing, and using the twice differentiated mean-zero equation gives the second line of (3). Finally, \(\mathcal G(u)=-DF(u)[u]-u_{\mathbf0}\). Differentiating once and twice and inserting (7) gives the last two lines of (3). Therefore the formulas do not omit the quadratic correction from maintaining the original mean.

Here is the exact relation to the convex-roof cone. For every \(\mathbf J\in D\), use the finite family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) of affinely independent barycentric representations in `488f0a583886b5fc`. Its inequalities are \(A_{\mathbf J,E}(u):=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}u_{\mathbf I}-u_{\mathbf J}\ge0\). They describe precisely \(\mathcal K_D\). Equivalently, to check all barycentric inequalities, minimize a proposed barycentric average over its coefficient polytope. An optimizer with least positive support is affinely independent, since any affine dependence would permit a perturbation preserving feasibility and objective until a coefficient vanishes. This also proves directly that the displayed finite family suffices. Its lower roof is the exact extension \(\widehat\phi\) of that accepted predecessor.

Let \(T\) be the linear space of directions \(h\) for which \(A_{\mathbf J,E}(h)=0\) for every equality active at \(\phi\). Every \(h\in T\) admits both \(\phi+th\) and \(\phi-th\) in \(\mathcal K_D\) for sufficiently small positive \(t\): the finitely many inactive inequalities have strictly positive slack. Thus a relative local maximum must obey \(D\mathcal G[h]=0\) and \(D^2\mathcal G[h,h]\le0\) on \(T\). On the full one-sided tangent cone, described by \(A_{\mathbf J,E}(h)\ge0\) at the active rows, it must obey \(D\mathcal G[h]\le0\), and the second derivative must be nonpositive whenever this first derivative is zero. These are necessary conditions; neither (3) nor the cone geometry makes \(\mathcal G\) convex or forces an extreme ray.

Indeed, since \(R\phi\) is orthogonal to affine functions, the first derivative in (3) can be written as \(\mathbb E_p[(1-R\phi)h]-h_{\mathbf0}\). Ambient stationarity is therefore equivalent to

\[
p_{\mathbf I}(1-(R\phi)_{\mathbf I})=\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf0\}}.
\tag{8}
\]

At a stationary point, \((R\phi)_{\mathbf I}=1\) at every nonzero site, while \((R\phi)_{\mathbf0}=-g=1-1/p_{\mathbf0}\). In particular \(p_{\mathbf0}(g+1)=1\). Inserting these values in (3) gives

\[
D^2\mathcal G(\phi)[h,h]
=-\mathbb E_p[(Rh)^2]-(Rh)_{\mathbf0}^{2}.
\tag{9}
\]

This is strictly negative for every nonaffine value vector \(h\). Thus ambient stationarity provides local maxima, not a direction that can push the objective to a boundary face. It is also informative about additional interior sites. Equation (8) forces \(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}=g+1+\mathbf a_\phi\cdot\mathbf I\) at every nonzero site. If a nonzero site were interior to \(P\), it could be represented using \(\mathbf0\) with positive coefficient and boundary vertices with total coefficient less than one. Its convex-admissibility inequality would require \(g+1\le t(g+1)\) for some \(t<1\), which is impossible because \(g+1=1/p_{\mathbf0}>0\). Hence a local maximum on a support with additional interior sites must have active constraints. This observation does not provide an ascent direction inside the resulting active face.

We next prove (4), which also constructs the stated stationary example. For a one-interior polytope \(P\), let \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\) minimize its unweighted capacity. The origin contributes one independently of the tilt, so the same vector minimizes the sum over the nonzero sites, and
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\ne\mathbf0}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}=B(P)-1\), with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\ne\mathbf0}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). This sum is positive. For any mean-zero probability law \(q\) on the nonzero sites, concavity of the logarithm gives \(H(q)\le\log(B(P)-1)\): write its entropy as \(\sum q_{\mathbf I}\log(e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}/q_{\mathbf I})\) and use its zero mean. The argument permits zero coordinates of \(q\), restricting that sum to its positive support.

For any mean-zero law \(p\) on \(D\) with \(0<p_{\mathbf0}<1\), its conditional law on the nonzero sites still has mean zero. Write \(t=p_{\mathbf0}\). The elementary identity \(g=H(p)+\log p_{\mathbf0}\), also stated in `c38daee277a73322`, therefore gives

\[
g\le (1-t)\log\frac{(B(P)-1)t}{1-t}.
\tag{10}
\]

The function on the right has derivative \(1/t-\log((B(P)-1)t/(1-t))\) and strictly negative second derivative \(-1/t^2-1/t-1/(1-t)\). It tends to negative infinity as \(t\downarrow0\), and to zero as \(t\uparrow1\). Consequently it has a unique maximum at \(t=1/c\), where \(c>1\) is the unique solution of \(c-1=(B(P)-1)e^{-c}\). The value of this maximum is \(c-1\), proving the upper bound in (4). A law concentrated at zero has energy zero and satisfies the same upper bound.

For equality, take \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=c-\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I\) at every nonzero site. Its original mean is zero, its total unnormalized weight is \(1+(B(P)-1)e^{-c}=c\), and its gap is \(c-1\). Its potential is convex-admissible. To check the latter assertion after removing its affine part, every nonzero site lies on the boundary of \(P\), and any barycentric representation of that site must stay in one supporting face of \(P\); such a face cannot contain zero. The represented potential and every value in the representation are therefore all \(c\). At zero the barycentric inequality is \(0\le c(1-\lambda_{\mathbf0})\), which holds. All barycentric inequalities, including representations with zero included, have thus been checked. This proves equality and convex admissibility without an assumption about an extremal ray.

For the explicit planar interior maximum, the vertices of \(Q\) all have second coordinate between minus one and one. The integer row at height one consists only of \((0,1)\); the row at height minus one consists only of \((1,-1)\); and the row at height zero consists of \((-1,0),(0,0),(1,0)\). These observations verify its entire lattice set. The four edge inequalities are \(x+y\le1\), \(y-x\le1\), \(x+2y\ge-1\), and \(x\le1\). They are all strict at zero, which proves that zero is its unique interior lattice point. The polygon is not centrally symmetric: \((0,1)\) belongs to it but \((0,-1)\) does not.

For an entirely exact description of its capacity tilt, let \(a\) be the unique positive root of \(a^4+a^3=1\), and take \(\boldsymbol\theta_*=(2\log a,\log a)\). The four unnormalized boundary weights are \(a^2,a,a^{-2},a\). Their vector sum is zero because \(a^{-2}=a^2+a\). Strict convexity of the partition function identifies this as its minimizing tilt. Thus \(B(Q)-1=2a^2+3a\). With \(c>1\) determined by \(c-1=(2a^2+3a)e^{-c}\), the preceding construction supplies the exact centered global maximizer.

All four nonzero sites of this support are vertices, so the only nontrivial barycentric inequalities have their target at zero. At the value vector equal to zero at zero and to \(c\) at each vertex, every member of the finite family with target zero other than the trivial singleton has slack \(c>0\). Hence this potential, and its centered affine translate, are in the interior of \(\mathcal K_D\). The full value space has dimension five and its affine subspace has dimension three, leaving two independent directions modulo affine functions. Strict concavity in (10), and the equality condition for the entropy step, give a unique maximizing law; two value vectors give that same centered law exactly when they differ by an affine function. The maximizer is therefore strict globally modulo affine functions, and (9) gives its strict local Hessian certificate. Its class cannot be an extreme ray: one can perturb in both signs along a direction independent of that class and remain inside the cone. This proves the claimed failure of the naive extreme-ray argument in a nonsymmetric, genuinely two-dimensional value problem.

For clarity, the Hessian is not globally nonpositive either. On this same support, vary the common nonzero height \(c\), keeping the capacity tilt above. Put \(A=2a^2+3a<5\). The original zero-mean law has \(t=p_{\mathbf0}=1/(1+Ae^{-c})\), and its gap along this ray is \(c(1-t)\). Its second derivative in \(c\) is \(t(1-t)(c(2t-1)-2)\). At \(c=4\), the elementary inequality \(e^4>1+4+4^2/2+4^3/6>15\) gives \(t>3/4\), so this second derivative is positive. Both signs of curvature therefore occur for centered convex-admissible potentials on the same nonsymmetric support.

The capacity bound \(B(P)\le4^r\) used next is exactly the dilation-one conclusion of accepted fact `c38daee277a73322`, whose full-dimensional lattice-polytope and sole-interior-point hypotheses are the present ones. In rank at most two it gives \(B(P)-1\le15\). The function \(x e^{x+1}\) is strictly increasing for \(x\ge0\). Moreover, the exponential series with positive remainder gives
\(e^{5/2}>1+5/2+25/8+125/48+625/384=4169/384>10\). Thus \((3/2)e^{5/2}>15\), and (4) implies that the energy of every law considered in (10) is strictly less than \(3/2\). The rank-zero law has energy zero. The entropy argument proves the same bound for laws with vanishing boundary coordinates, as long as their origin mass is positive.

It remains to verify the counterexample (5) directly. The function \(\max\{-x,-y,x+y\}\) is globally convex, nonnegative, and zero only at the origin. At integer sites its values are nonnegative integers, and its sublevel set at integer \(k\ge0\) is \(kP_\Delta\). It follows that (5) gives every lattice point of its support its prescribed convex value, with no omitted interior or boundary site.

The map \((x,y)\mapsto(k-x-y,k+x,k+y)\) is a bijection between \(kP_\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^2\) and the triples of nonnegative integers summing to \(3k\). Its cardinality is \(\binom{3k+2}{2}\). Permuting the three coordinates preserves the latter set, so the uniform average of each coordinate is \(k\), and the sum of \((x,y)\) over the entire lattice sublevel set is zero. Subtracting consecutive sublevel sums shows that every complete shell has vector sum zero. The shell at height \(k\ge1\) has size
\(\binom{3k+2}{2}-\binom{3k-1}{2}=9k\). Each site on that shell has weight \(2^{-k}\), so the original weighted mean is exactly zero; no affine tilt is being silently substituted for the original law in this example.

The exact finite sums are \(\sum_{k=1}^5 k2^{-k}=57/32\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^5 k^2 2^{-k}=141/32\). Therefore \(W=1+9(57/32)=545/32\), \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), and the original energy numerator is \((\log2)9(141/32)\). This proves the equality in (6). To verify its strict inequality without numerical logarithms, integrate the geometric series for \(1/(1-z^2)\) between zero and \(1/3\), obtaining \(\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}(1/3)^{2j+1}/(2j+1)>2/3\). Consequently \(g>846/545>3/2\), since \(1692>1635\). The support is nonsymmetric, as \((10,-5)\in5P_\Delta\) but its negative does not belong to that polytope. The potential is a maximum of three different linear forms in two variables; neither the zero-mean verification nor the energy comparison uses a product decomposition.

Combining (6) with (10) and the strict rank-two upper bound proves that a replacement by any one-interior support of rank at most two cannot preserve or increase this energy. This conclusion concerns the energy objective itself. The example still satisfies \(g<2\log4\) and supplies no counterexample to the open universal energy bound.

Finally, we verify the limit assertion, so support loss does not evade the obstruction. Let \(p^{(j)}\) be any centered convex-admissible laws on the fixed finite set in (5), and suppose they converge to \(p^*\). Their mean equation passes to the limit. Let \(E=\{\mathbf I:p^*_{\mathbf I}>0\}\). The limit law has total mass one, and its mean zero expresses the origin as a convex combination of points of \(E\). Convex admissibility of each original law gives \(p^{(j)}_{\mathbf0}\ge\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}(p^{(j)}_{\mathbf I})^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\) for that fixed barycentric representation, ignoring zero coefficients. Hence \(p^*_{\mathbf0}>0\). The same argument at every lattice point in \(\operatorname{conv}E\) proves that \(E\) is lattice-convex. All convex-admissibility inequalities within its positive support pass to the limit as well. Since every point of \(E\) has positive weight, its mean zero lies in the relative interior of its convex hull.

The identity \(g(p)=H(p)+\log p_{\mathbf0}\), with \(0\log0=0\), is continuous at this limit because \(p^*_{\mathbf0}>0\). If \(\operatorname{conv}E\) is one-interior in its intrinsic lattice, choose a basis of that full lattice in its span and apply the rank-at-most-two bound just proved. It gives \(g(p^*)<3/2\). This contradicts \(g(p^{(j)})\ge(1269/545)\log2>3/2\) for all \(j\). The claimed limit reduction is therefore impossible too.
