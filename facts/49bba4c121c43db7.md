## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge2\), put \(k=2m\), and regard the first \(m\) coordinates as active coordinates and the last \(m\) coordinates as neutral coordinates. Write a point as \((a,t)\), with \(a,t\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^m\). Define \(E_m\) to consist of all points having exactly one active coordinate equal to two, at an index \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\), and exactly one neutral coordinate equal to two, at an index \(j\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\), subject to
$$
a_h\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\quad(h\ne i),
\qquad t_h\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\quad(h\ne j),
\qquad \sum_{h\ne j}t_h=i-1.
$$
Define \(F_m\) to consist of all points having exactly one active coordinate equal to two, at an index \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m-1\rbrace\), and satisfying
$$
a_h\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\quad(h\ne i),
\qquad t\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,
\qquad \sum_{h=1}^m t_h=i+1.
$$
Set \(D=B_k\cup E_m\cup F_m\), where \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\). Then \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\), and every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two. Use the original affine field \(\beta=0\), so that \(Z=|D|\) and \(p_x=1/Z\) for every \(x\in D\).

Let \(D_1\) be the points of \(D\) with at most one coordinate equal to two, let \(\alpha=p(D_1)\), and let \(S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4\). Let
$$
L_B=
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2\\(x+y)/2\in B_k}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6},
$$
with ordered pairs in the sum. For each nonempty \(I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), let \(p_I(u)=\sum_{x\in D:x_I=u}p_x\), and let
$$
P_{\mathrm{block}}(p)=
\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace}
8^{|I|}\sum_{\substack{u\in\pi_I(D)\\u\text{ has a coordinate equal to }2}}p_I(u)^4.
$$
The following bounds hold simultaneously:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&\le\frac6m,\\
P_{\mathrm{block}}(p)&\le\frac{1024}{m^{3/2}}+\frac{1600}{m^3},\\
2^{k/3}L_B&\le 2^{8/3}\frac{4m+4}{m^{4/3}},\\
8^kS_4&\le\frac{64}{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently \(\alpha+P_{\mathrm{block}}(p)+2^{k/3}L_B+8^kS_4\to0\) as \(m\to\infty\). No absolute positive constant in the registered inequality exists.

## Proof

We first verify the exact support, including its convex completeness. Put \(G=B_k\cup E_m\). Every generator lies in \([0,2]^k\), so every lattice point of \(\operatorname{conv}(G)\) is ternary. If a coordinate of a convex combination equals two, every generator having positive coefficient has that coordinate equal to two, because all generator coordinates are at most two. Thus such a lattice point has at most two coordinates equal to two. If it has two, they must be one active coordinate \(i\) and one neutral coordinate \(j\). All generators in its representation then have these same two coordinates equal to two. Every remaining coordinate of these generators is binary. An integer convex combination of binary coordinates can equal zero or one only when all positively weighted generators agree on that coordinate. Hence a lattice point with two coordinates equal to two is already in \(E_m\).

There cannot be a lattice point with exactly one neutral coordinate \(j\) equal to two. Indeed, all its representing generators would belong to \(E_m\) and have this neutral coordinate equal to two. All other neutral coordinates are binary, and their integer values therefore force them to be the same in every representing generator. Their sum fixes the active index \(i=1+\sum_{h\ne j}t_h\). All representing generators consequently also have this active coordinate equal to two, a contradiction.

If a lattice point has exactly one active coordinate \(i\) equal to two, its representing generators all have that active coordinate equal to two. For each such generator the sum of the neutral coordinates is \(i+1\). The lattice point has Boolean neutral coordinates, so their sum must be \(i+1\). This is possible only when \(i\le m-1\), and the point belongs to \(F_m\). Points with no coordinate equal to two are in \(B_k\). We have proved \(\operatorname{conv}(G)\cap\mathbb Z^k\subseteq D\).

Conversely, every point of \(F_m\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\). Its Boolean neutral vector \(t\) has \(i+1\ge2\) entries equal to one. Choose two distinct such indices \(j,h\). Keep its active vector fixed and replace the neutral vector by \(t+e_j-e_h\), respectively \(t-e_j+e_h\), where these unit vectors act in the neutral block. Both resulting points belong to \(E_m\): each has one neutral two and exactly \(i-1\) neutral ones. Their midpoint is the original point. Thus \(D\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(G)\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(D)=\operatorname{conv}(G)\), and the preceding classification proves \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\). This also verifies that the displayed description includes every lattice point of the convex hull. The inclusion of \(B_k\) makes the hull full dimensional.

For each choice of the neutral two-coordinate \(j\), replace that two by zero to obtain a Boolean neutral source \(v\) with \(v_j=0\). There are \(2^{m-1}\) such sources. Its weight \(|v|=\sum_hv_h\) fixes the active two-coordinate uniquely as \(i=1+|v|\), which belongs to \(\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\). The other \(m-1\) active coordinates can be chosen freely. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
|E_m|&=m2^{2m-2}=m2^{k-2},\\
|F_m|&=2^{m-1}\sum_{w=2}^m\binom mw
=2^{m-1}(2^m-m-1)\le2^{k-1},\\
Z&=2^k+m2^{k-2}+2^{m-1}(2^m-m-1)
\ge m2^{k-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The sets in this union are disjoint. Since \(D_1=B_k\cup F_m\), these counts give \(\alpha\le6/m\). Uniformity gives \(S_4=Z^{-3}\), and hence \(8^kS_4\le64/m^3\).

We next bound \(L_B\). Fix a parity class, with Boolean source \(b\in B_k\), and let \(w\) be the number of ones in its neutral block. Every \(E_m\)-point in this parity class has active two-coordinate \(i=w+1\), since replacing its neutral two by zero recovers the neutral block of \(b\). There are at most \(m\) such points. Any two of them share this active coordinate equal to two, so their midpoint is not Boolean. There is at most one \(F_m\)-point in the parity class, because its active two-coordinate must be \(i=w-1\). Along with the unique Boolean point, this leaves at most two points outside \(E_m\) in the class. Every ordered pair contributing to \(L_B\) must have at least one endpoint among those two points. Its number is consequently at most \(4m+4\), including a harmless upper count of diagonal pairs. Every actual summand equals \(Z^{-4/3}\). Summing over the \(2^k\) parity classes gives
$$
2^{k/3}L_B
\le 2^{4k/3}(4m+4)Z^{-4/3}
\le 2^{8/3}\frac{4m+4}{m^{4/3}}.
$$

It remains to control the full supremum over coordinate blocks. We will use the elementary binomial bound
$$
M_t:=\max_{0\le s\le t}2^{-t}\binom ts\le\frac1{\sqrt{t+1}}
\qquad(t\ge0).
$$
Here is a proof. Let \(c_n=4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n}\). The recurrence \(c_{n+1}=c_n(2n+1)/(2n+2)\), the initial value \(c_0=1\), and
$$
\frac{2n+1}{(2n+2)^2}\le\frac1{2n+3}
$$
prove inductively that \(M_{2n}=c_n\le(2n+1)^{-1/2}\). Also \(M_{2n+1}=c_n(2n+1)/(2n+2)\), whose square is at most \((2n+1)/(2n+2)^2\le1/(2n+2)\). This proves the stated bound in both parities.

Let \(U_m\) be all ternary points with exactly one active two and all remaining coordinates binary. In particular \(F_m\subseteq U_m\). Fix any nonempty coordinate block \(I\). Let \(I_a\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\) be its active indices and \(J\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\) its neutral indices. Put
$$
a=|I_a|,\qquad b=|J|,\qquad t=m-b,\qquad r=a+b=|I|.
$$
For projected patterns define the unnormalized submasses
$$
e_I(u)=\frac{|\lbrace x\in E_m:x_I=u\rbrace|}{Z},
\qquad
v_I(u)=\frac{|\lbrace x\in U_m:x_I=u\rbrace|}{Z}.
$$
For every pattern containing a two, the Boolean part contributes zero and \(p_I(u)\le e_I(u)+v_I(u)\). The inequality \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\) therefore gives
$$
8^r\sum_{u\text{ containing }2}p_I(u)^4
\le8\left(
8^r\sum_{u\text{ containing }2}e_I(u)^4
+8^r\sum_{u\text{ containing }2}v_I(u)^4
\right).
$$
We may sum over all ternary projected patterns; nonactual patterns contribute zero on the left and cannot invalidate the upper bound.

For \(U_m\), a projected pattern containing a two must have exactly one active two and binary values in its other coordinates. There are \(a2^{r-1}\) such patterns, each with \(2^{k-r}\) completions in \(U_m\). If \(a=0\), there are none. Consequently
$$
8^r\sum_{u\text{ containing }2}v_I(u)^4
=8^r a2^{r-1}\left(\frac{2^{k-r}}Z\right)^4
\le\frac{128a}{m^4}
\le\frac{128}{m^3}.
$$

There are two exhaustive types of projected \(E_m\)-patterns containing a two. First consider patterns with no neutral two. Their active two-coordinate \(i\) must lie in \(I_a\), and their neutral two-coordinate is hidden outside \(J\). Fix the observed Boolean neutral vector \(c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^b\). For \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\), set
$$
\begin{aligned}
q_i(c)&=2^{-t}|\lbrace v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^t:1+|c|+|v|=i\rbrace|,\\
C_i(c)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^t\\1+|c|+|v|=i}}(t-|v|).
\end{aligned}
$$
Each hidden zero of \(v\) can be chosen as the hidden neutral two-coordinate, so the exact number of completions for a fixed observed active pattern with two at \(i\) is \(2^{m-a}C_i(c)\). We have \(C_i(c)\le t2^tq_i(c)\), \(\sum_{i=1}^m q_i(c)\le1\), and \(\max_iq_i(c)\le M_t\). The last two assertions remain valid when \(1+|c|+|v|=m+1\) for some hidden vector: this value is omitted from the sum and has no hidden zero that could generate an \(E_m\)-point. Since \(Z\ge m2^{k-2}\), each such projected submass satisfies
$$
e_I(u)\le\frac{4t}{m2^r}q_i(c).
$$
For each \(i\in I_a\) and \(c\) there are \(2^{a-1}\) observed active patterns. Thus, when \(a\ge1\), the score of this first type is at most
$$
\begin{aligned}
8^r2^{a-1}\sum_c\sum_{i\in I_a}
\left(\frac{4t}{m2^r}q_i(c)\right)^4
&=128\left(\frac tm\right)^4
2^{-b}\sum_c\sum_{i\in I_a}q_i(c)^4\\
&\le128\left(\frac tm\right)^4M_t^3\\
&\le\frac{128}{m^{3/2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first type is absent if \(a=0\) or \(t=0\). The last inequality follows from \(t^4/(t+1)^{3/2}\le t^{5/2}\le m^{5/2}\), with the zero case interpreted directly.

For the second type, fix an observed neutral two-coordinate \(j\in J\). Let \(c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{b-1}\) be the other observed neutral bits. The underlying Boolean source has value zero at \(j\). Define
$$
q_i(c)=2^{-t}|\lbrace v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^t:1+|c|+|v|=i\rbrace|,
\qquad i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace.
$$
These probabilities sum to one, since \(|c|+|v|\le m-1\). If \(i\in I_a\), the active two is observed. A fixed active pattern then has \(2^{m-a}2^tq_i(c)\) completions, so its submass is at most \(4q_i(c)/(m2^r)\). Counting its \(2^{a-1}\) active patterns and \(2^{b-1}\) neutral vectors, the score for this subcase is at most
$$
\frac{64}{m^4}\,
2^{-(b-1)}\sum_c\sum_{i\in I_a}q_i(c)^4
\le\frac{64}{m^4}.
$$
This subcase is absent if \(a=0\). If instead \(i\notin I_a\), the observed active pattern is entirely Boolean. Put \(Q(c)=\sum_{i\notin I_a}q_i(c)\). For a fixed observed active pattern, the number of completions is \(2^{m-a-1}2^tQ(c)\), summed over the possible hidden active two-coordinates. Its submass is at most \(2Q(c)/(m2^r)\). There are \(2^a\) observed active patterns, and the score for this subcase is consequently at most
$$
\frac8{m^4}\,
2^{-(b-1)}\sum_c Q(c)^4
\le\frac8{m^4}.
$$
This subcase is absent if \(a=m\). The two subcases have different observed active patterns. Summing their bounds and then summing over the \(b\) possible observed neutral two-coordinates gives a total at most \(72b/m^4\le72/m^3\) for the second type. There are no further types, since every \(E_m\)-point has exactly one active two and one neutral two.

We have therefore proved, for every nonempty block \(I\) without restriction on its size or composition,
$$
\begin{aligned}
8^r\sum_{u\text{ containing }2}p_I(u)^4
&\le8\left(\frac{128}{m^{3/2}}+\frac{72}{m^3}+\frac{128}{m^3}\right)\\
&=\frac{1024}{m^{3/2}}+\frac{1600}{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the maximum over all blocks proves the claimed bound on \(P_{\mathrm{block}}\). Every displayed upper bound in the statement tends to zero. This is an exact counterfamily with the original real affine field \(\beta=0\), so it refutes an absolute positive lower bound over all original real affine fields.
