## Statement

Use the actual canonical hull, summed mass $M$, fixed norm and signed left-quantile radius $r_Q$ of the fixed-body sublevel law. Fix $\varepsilon>0$, and write $E(w)=E_{C_z}(y)$ for projective energy at $w=(z,y)\in C$, with zero value on each own-fiber boundary. Define
$$
T_Q(w)=\frac M{\varepsilon^{d+1}}\frac{[r_Q(w)_+]^{d+1}}{E(w)}
$$
when $E(w)>0$ and $r_Q(w)<\infty$, and $T_Q(w)=\infty$ otherwise. This is convex and lower semicontinuous on $C$ and can be extended by infinity outside $C$. At every actual real mark and every positive finite $\ell$,
$$
B\bigl(w,\varepsilon[\ell E(w)/M]^{1/(d+1)}\bigr)\le Q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell\le T_Q(w).
$$
In particular, for a finite positive concave weight $L$ on $C$ the maximum of the corresponding buffered depths over the sampled fibers is at most $Q$ exactly when $L(w)\le T_Q(w)$ at every sampled mark.

## Proof

If $y$ is on the boundary of its real fiber, choose an outward nonzero supporting covector and rescale it to $\Gamma$-unit length. By 7227bbfcd12ad3d2 the support-height linear program admits a finite affine majorant touching at $z$. The resulting ambient supporting cap through $w$ has zero volume on every sampled fiber. Its projection tail is continuous and is zero at the supporting threshold, while $Q>0$. Its left $Q$-quantile lies strictly below that threshold, so $r_Q(w)>0$ or $r_Q(w)=\infty$. Its zero buffered displacement at these marks gives depth zero. At a positive-energy mark the signed-radius law gives the equivalence directly on raising the positive displacement to the power $d+1$, including the case $r_Q\le0$ where no positive parameter can satisfy the inequality.

By 5aeb5916857f97a9, the function $F=E^{1/d}$ is jointly concave on own-fiber interiors. Hausdorff continuity of the actual real fibers in 7227bbfcd12ad3d2 and the marked energy continuity of b3c0088cf09b2894 give continuity on $C$, including zero at fiber boundaries. Concavity extends there by approaching each endpoint along its own fiber interior. The closed perspective of $x^{d+1}$ on $x\ge0$ is $g(x,s)=x^{d+1}/s^d$ for $s>0$, with $g(x,0)=\infty$ for $x>0$ and $g(0,0)=0$. It is increasing in $x$ and decreasing in $s$. On points with finite $T_Q$, combine convexity of $(r_Q)_+$, concavity of $F$, and perspective convexity to get the desired convexity inequality. If an endpoint has infinite $T_Q$, the same convexity inequality at a nontrivial combination is automatic. For lower semicontinuity use the lower semicontinuity of $(r_Q)_+$, continuity of $F$, and this monotone closed perspective. In particular, at an own-fiber boundary the positive lower limit of $(r_Q)_+$ forces divergence as $F\to0$, and at an infinite radius its lower limit is infinite while $F$ is bounded. Thus the perspective agrees with the specified extension everywhere.

The markwise equivalence gives the assertion at all sampled marks. For attainment of their maximum, a finite positive concave $L$ is bounded above on $C$. Indeed fix a relative interior point $o$ and a small fixed $\lambda>0$ so that $(o-\lambda w)/(1-\lambda)\in C$ for every $w\in C$. Positivity and concavity give $L(w)\le L(o)/\lambda$. On each sampled fiber, $L$ is continuous in its interior. Its boundedness and the zero boundary energy extend $LE$ continuously there. The buffered depth is then an infimum of continuous oblique tail masses and the constant pure-base value on each compact sampled fiber, hence upper semicontinuous and attaining a maximum on the finite union.
