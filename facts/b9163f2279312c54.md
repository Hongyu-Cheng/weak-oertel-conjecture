## Statement

Let \(m,h\) be positive integers, let \(L\) be a nonnegative integer, and let \(\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) be arbitrary finite real tilts. Define
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
                    0\le x_i\le L-hy\text{ for every }i\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x+\theta y}}
 {\sum_{(\mathbf u,v)\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf u+\theta v}}.
\]
For a probability law \(\pi\) on a nonempty finite support \(A\subset\mathbb R^s\), write
\[
\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\pi(A\cap H),
\qquad
M_q(\pi)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}\pi(\mathbf z)\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^q
\quad(q=2,3),
\]
where the infimum uses closed halfspaces. On a singleton the depth is one. Thus \(M_q\) is exactly \(\mathcal M_q\) of 8db3ba8c8761ea6c, and \(M_3\) agrees with ac4157e446425a3d; neither the probabilities nor the halfspace masses are reweighted.

Retain the row constants of 8db3ba8c8761ea6c:
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{\mathrm{lik}}&=4+2\max\{16,(131/27)e^8,65e^3\},
&K_{\mathrm{row}}&=20288,\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}&=(8K_{\mathrm{row}})^{-1},
&\beta_{\mathrm{row}}&=\sqrt{8K_{\mathrm{row}}c_{\mathrm{lik}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\) is its conversion constant, distinct from every source coordinate \(\beta_i\). Put \(g=32/27\), \(\delta=(3/2)\log g>0\), and retain \(c_1=60000\) from ac4157e446425a3d. One absolute positive choice is
\[
\kappa=\min\left\{\frac1{32},\;
 \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^3}{64c_1^4}
 \exp\left(-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{row}}^2}{2\delta}-\frac\delta2\right)
 \left(\frac\delta{16}\right)^8\right\}.
\]
Then \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-(m+1)}\), uniformly over all the parameters above, including zero coordinate scores, zero height score after flipping, and all tilt ties.

## Proof

We first record two actual-depth comparisons, also proved in ac4157e446425a3d. For laws \(P_1,P_2\) in separate coordinate blocks,
\[
\tau_{P_1\otimes P_2}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)
 \ge\tau_{P_1}(\mathbf z_1)\tau_{P_2}(\mathbf z_2),
\qquad
M_3(P_1\otimes P_2)\ge M_3(P_1)M_3(P_2).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, translate the boundary of a containing halfspace to the root, obtaining a contained halfspace. Its centered inequality is the sum of one linear functional in each block being nonnegative. The product event where both functionals are nonnegative lies inside that halfspace. Each block event has mass at least its block depth, and independence proves the first inequality. A zero coefficient block contributes the whole support and has mass one. Cube and average with the original product root law to obtain the second inequality.

If \(P\ge\rho V\) pointwise for a probability law \(V\) on a subset of the support of \(P\), then \(\tau_P\ge\rho\tau_V\) at every root of \(V\). Retaining the original root probabilities gives
\[
M_3(P)\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in\operatorname{supp}V}
 P(\mathbf z)(\rho\tau_V(\mathbf z))^3
 \ge\rho^4M_3(V).
\tag{2}
\]
These comparisons also apply to laws supported on an affine slice of the ambient space.

Every probability law \(\pi\) on the real line satisfies \(M_3(\pi)\ge1/32\). To check this directly, order its atoms and let \(a_j\) be the mass strictly before atom \(j\), and \(b_j\) the mass up to and including it. The actual depth there is \(\min\{b_j,1-a_j\}\). For \(v\in[a_j,b_j]\), \(\min\{v,1-v\}\le\min\{b_j,1-a_j\}\). Integrating over all these intervals gives
\(M_3(\pi)\ge\int_0^1\min\{v,1-v\}^3\,dv=1/32\).
In particular this holds for every uniform nonempty consecutive integer interval, including a singleton.

We next retain the interval-product surplus before its absorption. For a nonnegative integer \(R\) and arbitrary \(\gamma_i\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
Q_R(\mathbf z)=\prod_{i=1}^m
 \frac{e^{\gamma_i z_i}}{\sum_{u=0}^R e^{\gamma_i u}},
\qquad \mathbf z\in\{0,\ldots,R\}^m.
\]
When \(R\ge2\), in 8db3ba8c8761ea6c the interval cardinalities are here \(m_i=R+1\), its scalar tilts are \(\theta_i=\gamma_i\), its retained rank is \(m\), and its number \(b_{\ge3}\) is also \(m\). The product-conversion inequality established in its proof before the square-completion step is
\[
M_2(Q_R)\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^2
 e^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m}
 \prod_{i=1}^m\mathcal B_2(p_i),
\tag{3}
\]
where \(p_i(u)=e^{\gamma_i u}/\sum_{v=0}^R e^{\gamma_i v}\). The capacity second moment in (3) is
\(\mathcal B_2(p_i)=\sum_jp_i(j)\mathsf C_{p_i}(j)^2\), with
\(\mathsf C_{p_i}(j)=\inf_{w\in\mathbb R}\sum_up_i(u)e^{w(u-j)}\), exactly as in both 8db3ba8c8761ea6c and 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9. Applying 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9 with its scalar cardinality \(R+1\ge3\) and tilt \(\gamma_i\) gives \(\mathcal B_2(p_i)\ge8/27=g/4\). Thus (3) gives
\(M_2(Q_R)\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^2e^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m}g^m4^{-m}\).
Jensen applied to \((\tau_{Q_R}^2)^{3/2}\), under the original law \(Q_R\), now yields
\[
M_3(Q_R)\ge M_2(Q_R)^{3/2}
 \ge\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^3
 e^{\delta m-3\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m}8^{-m}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular (4) concerns actual depths; the capacities enter only through the accepted actual-depth conversion (3).

Now flip precisely the coordinates whose original scores are positive. Define \(z_i=L-hy-x_i\) if \(\beta_i>0\), and \(z_i=x_i\) otherwise. Leave \(y\) unchanged at this step. This is an invertible integer affine map of the whole ambient space: the displayed formula is also its inverse in each flipped coordinate. It takes the original support bijectively to \(0\le z_i\le L-hy\). Set
\(\gamma_i=-|\beta_i|\le0\) and
\(\eta=\theta-h\sum_{i:\beta_i>0}\beta_i\).
The exact score identity is
\[
\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x+\theta y
 =L\sum_{i:\beta_i>0}\beta_i+\eta y
   +\sum_{i=1}^m\gamma_i z_i.
\tag{5}
\]
Write \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\), \(\ell=L-hK\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\), and \(t=K-y\). This is a further invertible integer affine map. Put \(R_t=\ell+ht\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(R)&=\prod_{i=1}^m\sum_{u=0}^R e^{\gamma_i u},
&\mathcal Z&=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\eta t}A(R_t),\\
p'(\mathbf z,t)&=\frac{e^{-\eta t+\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf z}}{\mathcal Z},
&q(t)&=\frac{e^{-\eta t}A(R_t)}{\mathcal Z}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(R\) is a nonnegative integer, and \(p'\) is the pushforward of the original \(p\). The common score constant in (5), followed by \(\eta K\), cancels exactly from its normalization. Every invertible affine map identifies all closed halfspaces and corresponding roots, so \(M_3(p')=M_3(p)\).

The box partition \(A(R)\) is positive and nondecreasing. Moreover \(A(R)/(R+1)^m\) is nonincreasing. For an exact coupling proof of the latter assertion, take independent uniform random variables \(T_i\) on \([0,1)\), and put \(U_{R,i}=\lfloor(R+1)T_i\rfloor\). The vector \(\mathbf U_R\) is uniform on \(\{0,\ldots,R\}^m\). When \(R\) increases, every \(U_{R,i}\) is nondecreasing. Since every \(\gamma_i\le0\), \(e^{\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf U_R}\) is nonincreasing pointwise. Its expectation is precisely \(A(R)/(R+1)^m\). Consequently
\[
\frac{A(R_2)}{A(R_1)}
 \le\left(\frac{R_2+1}{R_1+1}\right)^m
 \qquad(0\le R_1\le R_2).
\tag{7}
\]
Monotonicity of \(A\) itself also follows directly because each partial sum in its definition adds positive terms.

Separate the low-budget heights \(J_{\mathrm{low}}=\{t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}:R_t\le1\}\). The values \(R_t\) are distinct nonnegative integers, so \(|J_{\mathrm{low}}|\le2\). If \(q(J_{\mathrm{low}})\ge1/2\), some \(t\in J_{\mathrm{low}}\) has \(q(t)\ge1/4\). At this height the conditional transverse law is \(Q_{R_t}\) with the tilts from (6). For \(R_t=0\) it is a singleton with moment one. For \(R_t=1\), every halfspace containing a root contains that root atom, and hence
\[
M_3(Q_1)\ge\sum_{\mathbf z}Q_1(\mathbf z)^4
 =\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(p_i(0)^4+p_i(1)^4\bigr)
 \ge8^{-m}.
\tag{8}
\]
The last inequality is convexity of the fourth power applied to the two probabilities summing to one. Embedding this conditional law at height \(t\), the original \(p'\) dominates \(q(t)(Q_{R_t}\otimes\delta_t)\). Equations (1), (2), and (8), with the singleton case included, therefore give
\[
M_3(p)=M_3(p')\ge(1/4)^4\,8^{-m}
 =\frac1{32}\,8^{-(m+1)}.
\tag{9}
\]
This argument uses the original binary root weights and holds for every binary tilt.

It remains to consider \(q(J_{\mathrm{low}})<1/2\). The high-budget set
\(J_{\mathrm{high}}=\{t:R_t\ge2\}\) is then a nonempty suffix \(\{t_0,\ldots,K\}\) with mass \(r=q(J_{\mathrm{high}})>1/2\). Condition on this set and shift the height to \(s=t-t_0\). Let \(K'=K-t_0\), \(R_{\min}=R_{t_0}\ge2\), and \(b_s=A(R_{\min}+hs)\) for \(0\le s\le K'\). The conditional joint law is exactly
\[
p_{\mathrm{high}}(\mathbf z,s)
 =\frac{e^{-\eta s+\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf z}}{Z_{\mathrm{high}}},
\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{high}}=\sum_{s=0}^{K'}e^{-\eta s}b_s,
\quad 0\le z_i\le R_{\min}+hs.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(1\le u\le v\le K'\), (7) implies
\[
\frac{b_v}{b_u}
 \le\left(\frac{R_{\min}+hv+1}{R_{\min}+hu+1}\right)^m
 \le(v/u)^m.
\tag{11}
\]
The last comparison follows by cross multiplication using \(R_{\min}+1\ge0\). The \(b_s\) are positive and nondecreasing.

The auxiliary height-band assertion proved in ac4157e446425a3d says the following, with exactly its \(c_1=60000\): if positive nondecreasing \(b_0,\ldots,b_J\), \(J\ge1\), satisfy \(b_v/b_u\le(v/u)^\nu\) for \(1\le u\le v\le J\), where \(\nu\ge1\) is an integer, then for every real \(\eta\) there is a nonempty consecutive integer interval \(I=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq\{0,\ldots,J\}\) such that
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm{high}}
 :=\frac{|I|b_a\min_{s\in I}e^{-\eta s}}
 {\sum_{s=0}^J e^{-\eta s}b_s}
 \ge\frac1{c_1(\nu+1)^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Apply this accepted assertion with \(J=K'\) and \(\nu=m\), using (11). If \(K'=0\), take \(I=\{0\}\); then \(\lambda_{\mathrm{high}}=1\), so the same lower bound holds without invoking the positive-length assertion.

Set \(R=R_{\min}+ha\ge2\). Every height \(s\in I\) has transverse box containing \(\{0,\ldots,R\}^m\). Let \(u_I\) be uniform on \(I\). On the support of \(Q_R\otimes u_I\), the exact probability ratio in (10) is
\[
\frac{p_{\mathrm{high}}(\mathbf z,s)}{Q_R(\mathbf z)u_I(s)}
 =\frac{|I|A(R)e^{-\eta s}}{Z_{\mathrm{high}}}
 \ge\lambda_{\mathrm{high}}.
\tag{13}
\]
Extending this product law by zero elsewhere and shifting it back to height \(t_0+s\), the original \(p'\) therefore dominates it with coefficient
\(r\lambda_{\mathrm{high}}\ge[2c_1(m+1)^2]^{-1}\).
Equations (1), (2), (4), and the line-moment bound give
\[
M_3(p)=M_3(p')
 \ge\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^3}{512c_1^4}
 \frac{e^{\delta m-3\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m}}{(m+1)^8}
 8^{-m}.
\tag{14}
\]
There is one factor \((r\lambda_{\mathrm{high}})^4\) from the original law comparison, and the additional factor \(1/32\) is the height interval's actual third depth moment.

Finally absorb the polynomial band loss. Completing a square gives
\[
\delta m-3\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m
 \ge\frac\delta2m-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{row}}^2}{2\delta}.
\]
Applying \(e^u\ge u\) at \(u=\delta(m+1)/16>0\) and raising to the eighth power yields
\[
\frac{e^{\delta m-3\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt m}}{(m+1)^8}
 \ge\exp\left(-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{row}}^2}{2\delta}-\frac\delta2\right)
 \left(\frac\delta{16}\right)^8.
\tag{15}
\]
Substitute (15) in (14) and use \(8^{-m}=8\,8^{-(m+1)}\). This gives the second coefficient in the stated \(\kappa\). Equation (9) gives its first coefficient. The alternatives exhaust all laws, and the affine maps preserve the original root probabilities and every actual halfspace depth. The stated uniform bound follows.
