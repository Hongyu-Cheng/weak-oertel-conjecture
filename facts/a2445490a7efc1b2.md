## Statement

For a convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), retain the notation of accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041: \(C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), and \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For every positive-volume fiber, \(\lambda_z\) is its normalized Lebesgue probability measure. In particular, \(\lambda_z\) is a measure, not the weight \(w_z\).

When \(w_0>0\), let \(\mathcal R_0\) contain one representative of each unordered pair \(\{v,-v\}\) with \(v\ne0\) and \(w_vw_{-v}>0\). With \(M(x,x')=(x+x')/2\) and \(\alpha_v=\min\{w_v,w_{-v}\}\), use exactly the canonical measure
\[
\kappa_0
=w_0\lambda_0+
\sum_{v\in\mathcal R_0}\alpha_v
M_\#(\lambda_v\otimes\lambda_{-v}),
\qquad
\Lambda_0:=\kappa_0(\mathbb R^d).
\]
Thus the scalar \(\Lambda_0\) is exactly the predecessor's total mass \(P_m\) at \(m=0\); there is no rescaling of \(\kappa_0\). It is distinct from the mixed-integer set \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) in the locked problem. Define the reflected overlap by \(S_0:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{w_z,w_{-z}\}\). Retain \(\operatorname{HD}(\nu):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^d}\inf_{G\ni x}\nu(G)\), where the infimum is over proper closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^d\), as in accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041.

For every integer \(k\ge3\), there exist integers \(d=k-1\) and \(n\) with \(k/2\le n\le4k\), and a full-dimensional rational polytope \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), such that \(w_0>0\), \(W>0\), \(S_0=W\), and
\[
\frac{\Lambda_0}{2n}
\le \frac{\Lambda_0}{k}
\le \operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)
\le \frac{2\Lambda_0}{k+1}
\le \frac{8\Lambda_0}{n}.
\]
The supremum defining \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)\) is attained at a point of \(C_0\). Consequently no absolute \(c>0\) guarantees \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_m)\ge c\,\kappa_m(\mathbb R^d)\) for every convex body and every prescribed \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_m>0\), even if one additionally requires \(\sum_z\min\{w_z,w_{2m-z}\}=W\).

## Proof

Fix an integer \(k\ge3\) and put \(d=k-1\). Take the standard simplex \(\Delta=\{y\in\mathbb R^{k-1}:y_s\ge0\ (1\le s<k),\ \sum_{s=1}^{k-1}y_s\le1\}\). Its barycentric coordinate functions, defined on all of \(\mathbb R^{k-1}\), are \(\beta_s(y)=y_s\) for \(s<k\) and \(\beta_k(y)=1-\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}y_s\). Let \(q=1/(k+1)\) and, for \(1\le i\le k\), set \(P_i=\{y\in\Delta:\beta_\ell(y)\le q\text{ for all }\ell\ne i\}\).

The coordinates \((\beta_\ell)_{\ell\ne i}\) range independently over \([0,q]^{k-1}\) on \(P_i\): their sum is at most \((k-1)/(k+1)<1\), so the remaining coordinate \(\beta_i=1-\sum_{\ell\ne i}\beta_\ell\) is positive. The affine map from \(y\) to these coordinates has Jacobian determinant of absolute value one. For \(i=k\) it is the identity. For \(i<k\), up to row permutation its linear part is the identity with row \(i\) replaced by \((-1,\ldots,-1)\), which has determinant \(-1\). Therefore every \(P_i\) is a full-dimensional rational parallelotope of volume \(w=q^{k-1}\).

The vertex of \(\Delta\) at which \(\beta_i=1\) belongs to \(P_i\). Since every \(P_i\subseteq\Delta\), it follows that \(\operatorname{conv}(\bigcup_{i=1}^kP_i)=\Delta\). The volume of the standard simplex is \(V=1/(k-1)!\).

Choose the integers
\[
R=\left\lceil\frac Vw\right\rceil
=\left\lceil\frac{(k+1)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}\right\rceil,
\qquad N=kR,\qquad n=1+\lceil\log_2N\rceil.
\]
Here \(V/w>1\), since it is the product of the factors \((k+1)/s>1\) for \(1\le s\le k-1\). In particular \(N\ge2\). The elementary inequality \(2^{N-1}\ge N\) for integer \(N\ge2\) gives \(n\le N\), while the definition of \(n\) gives \(2^{n-1}\ge N\). Thus one can choose \(N\) distinct vectors \(b_{i,r}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\), indexed by \(1\le i\le k\) and \(1\le r\le R\), including \(0,e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1}\). Indeed, these are \(n\) distinct cube vertices, and there are enough remaining cube vertices to extend them to \(N\) vectors. Set \(z_{i,r}=(b_{i,r},1)\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(Q=\operatorname{conv}\{b_{i,r}\}\), and \(B=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm z_{i,r}\}\).

We first determine all lattice points of \(B\). If \((u,t)\in B\cap\mathbb Z^n\), then \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}\). The face at \(t=1\) is \(Q\times\{1\}\). An integer point of \(Q\subseteq[0,1]^{n-1}\) is binary. If a binary vector \(u\) is a convex combination of the chosen \(b_{i,r}\), then a coordinate of \(u\) equal to zero forces that coordinate of every positively weighted vector to be zero, and a coordinate equal to one forces all of those coordinates to be one. Hence all positively weighted vectors equal \(u\), and \(u\) is one of the chosen vectors. The face at \(t=-1\) gives their negatives.

At \(t=0\), the total weight on the positive generators equals the total weight on the negative generators, namely \(1/2\). Therefore \(u\in(Q-Q)/2\subseteq[-1/2,1/2]^{n-1}\). Its only integer possibility is \(u=0\). Conversely the origin and all generators belong to \(B\). We have proved the exact census
\[
B\cap\mathbb Z^n
=\{0\}\cup
\{\pm z_{i,r}:1\le i\le k,\ 1\le r\le R\}.
\]
The same binary-coordinate argument shows that each chosen \(b_{i,r}\) is a vertex of \(Q\), so each \(\pm z_{i,r}\) is a vertex of \(B\). Also the chosen vectors include \((0,1)\) and \((e_s,1)\) for \(1\le s<n\). Their differences supply \((e_s,0)\), so the \(z_{i,r}\) span \(\mathbb R^n\), and \(B\) is full-dimensional.

Now define
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}
\bigcup_{\substack{1\le i\le k\\1\le r\le R}}
\left[
\bigl(\{z_{i,r}\}\times P_i\bigr)
\cup
\bigl(\{-z_{i,r}\}\times P_i\bigr)
\right].
\]
This is a compact rational polytope, since each generating fiber is a rational polytope and there are finitely many of them. Differences within any one generating fiber span \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d\). For every \(p\in P_i\), the difference between \((z_{i,r},p)\) and \((-z_{i,r},p)\) is \((2z_{i,r},0)\). The preceding spanning property consequently supplies \(\mathbb R^n\times\{0\}\) as well. The affine dimension of \(C\) is therefore \(n+d\), so \(C\) is a genuine convex body in the required ambient space.

Its projection to the first factor is exactly \(B\). Since each \(\pm z_{i,r}\) is a vertex of \(B\), any convex representation of a point of \(C\) with that base coordinate can use only generators with the same base coordinate. It follows that \(C_{z_{i,r}}=C_{-z_{i,r}}=P_i\). At base coordinate zero, each \(p\in P_i\) is realized by the midpoint of \((z_{i,r},p)\) and \((-z_{i,r},p)\). Hence \(C_0\) contains every \(P_i\), and convexity gives \(\Delta\subseteq C_0\). Conversely all continuous coordinates of the generators lie in \(\Delta\), so \(C_0\subseteq\Delta\). Thus \(C_0=\Delta\).

The census of \(B\) proves that all other integer fibers are empty, not merely of zero volume. We have therefore determined every integer fiber and its volume:
\[
C_0=\Delta,\quad w_0=V,\qquad
C_{\pm z_{i,r}}=P_i,\quad w_{\pm z_{i,r}}=w,\qquad
W=V+2kRw.
\]
The central and outer fiber volumes are positive. All integer fibers have equal-volume reflected partners, so \(S_0=\sum_z\min\{w_z,w_{-z}\}=W\).

We now apply the normalization in assertion 2 of accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041, with its reflection center \(m=0\). Choose \(\mathcal R_0=\{z_{i,r}\}\). Then \(\alpha_{z_{i,r}}=w\), and both \(\lambda_{z_{i,r}}\) and \(\lambda_{-z_{i,r}}\) are the uniform probability measure on \(P_i\). Let \(\nu_i\) be the law of \((Y_i+Y_i')/2\), where \(Y_i,Y_i'\) are independent uniform points of \(P_i\). It is a probability measure supported in \(P_i\), by convexity. The central probability measure \(\lambda_0\) is uniform on \(\Delta\). Substitution in the canonical definition gives the exact formulas
\[
\kappa_0=Rw\sum_{i=1}^k\nu_i+V\lambda_0,
\qquad
\Lambda_0=kRw+V=\frac{S_0+w_0}{2}.
\]
Each unordered nonzero reflection pair is counted once in \(\kappa_0\), whereas each of its two fibers contributes to \(S_0\). The central term is counted once in both expressions.

For any \(x\in C_0=\Delta\), choose \(i\) with \(\beta_i(x)=\max_\ell\beta_\ell(x)\). Since the barycentric coordinates sum to one, \(\beta_i(x)\ge1/k>q\). The set \(G_x=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:\beta_i(y)\ge\beta_i(x)\}\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(x\); the affine function \(\beta_i\) is nonconstant for every \(i\). For \(\ell\ne i\), every point of \(P_\ell\) has \(\beta_i\le q<\beta_i(x)\). Hence \(G_x\cap P_\ell=\varnothing\), so every component \(\nu_\ell\) with \(\ell\ne i\) gives zero mass to \(G_x\). The remaining midpoint component contributes at most \(Rw\), and the central component contributes at most \(V\). Therefore \(\kappa_0(G_x)\le Rw+V\).

Assertion 2 of accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041 applies because \(C\) is a convex body and \(w_0>0\). It states that the maximal depth of this canonical measure is attained in \(C_0\). The preceding halfspace bound, valid for every \(x\in C_0\), consequently gives \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)\le Rw+V\). By the choice of \(R\), one has \(Rw\ge V\). The identity \(2(kRw+V)-(k+1)(Rw+V)=(k-1)(Rw-V)\ge0\) proves \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)\le2\Lambda_0/(k+1)\).

For completeness, the integer dimension has the required bounds uniformly in \(k\). Put \(A=V/w=(k+1)^{k-1}/(k-1)!\). The first \(\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor\) factors in \(A=\prod_{s=1}^{k-1}(k+1)/s\) are strictly greater than two, and the remaining factors exceed one. Therefore \(A>2^{\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor}\), so \(n=1+\lceil\log_2(kR)\rceil>1+\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor\ge k/2\).

For the upper bound, \(A>1\) gives \(R=\lceil A\rceil\le2A\). The quantity \(A\) is one term of the exponential series for \(e^{k+1}\), hence \(A\le e^{k+1}<3^{k+1}\). The elementary bound \(e<3\) follows from \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge1\), with strict inequality for \(j\ge3\), by summing the series for \(e\). Also \(\log_2k\le k-1\), and \(\log_2 3<5/3\) because \(3^3<2^5\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
n
&\le 2+\log_2(kR)
\le 3+\log_2k+\log_2A\\
&<3+(k-1)+\frac53(k+1)
=\frac{8k+11}{3}
\le4k ,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final inequality uses \(k\ge3\). In particular \(2/(k+1)\le8/n\).

To obtain the lower bound, apply the continuous specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c. In that fact's mixed-integer measure assertion, set its integer dimension to zero, its continuous dimension to the present \(d=k-1\), and its measure \(\nu\) to the present \(\kappa_0\). This measure is a nonzero finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^d=\mathbb Z^0\times\mathbb R^d\), with total mass \(\Lambda_0\), so the fact gives a point whose every closed halfspace has mass at least \(\Lambda_0/(d+1)=\Lambda_0/k\). Thus \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)\ge\Lambda_0/k\); the attainment-in-\(C_0\) assertion already used places a maximizer in \(C_0\). Since \(k\le2n\), this is at least \(\Lambda_0/(2n)\). All claimed two-sided inequalities now follow.

As \(k\) tends to infinity, \(n\ge k/2\) also tends to infinity, whereas \(\operatorname{HD}(\kappa_0)/\Lambda_0\le2/(k+1)\) tends to zero. This refutes precisely the asserted absolute-constant bound at every prescribed reflection center, even under \(S_0=W\).

It does not refute weak Oertel. Indeed, assertion 2 of accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041 and the lower bound just proved give \(\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\ge\Lambda_0/k\ge W/(4n)\), using \(\Lambda_0=(W+V)/2\ge W/2\) and \(k\le2n\). Along this family \(W/n\) is larger than \(2^{-n}W\) by the unbounded factor \(2^n/n\). The construction limits a normalized fixed-center reflected-measure theorem, not the locked joint choice of an integer fiber and a point at the weak Oertel scale.
