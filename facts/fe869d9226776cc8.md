## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be positive $d$-dimensional convex bodies at integer bases $-1,0,1$, with $M_0=(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. For unit $n$ define
$$
\Psi(n,z,x)=\left[n\cdot x-\frac{(1-z)h_A(n)+(1+z)h_B(n)}2\right]_+ .
$$
This is a compatible convex inward roof speed. It vanishes on the measured endpoint boundaries and equals $\Delta(n)=h_K(n)-h_{M_0}(n)$ at a middle boundary point with outward normal $n$. Its total boundary integral is $d|K|-\int h_{M_0}\,dS_K$, where $S_K$ is the surface area measure, and its integral weighted by a bounded measurable boundary profile $W$ is $\int_{\partial K}\Delta(n)W\,dS$. Multiplying by a continuous nonnegative function of $n$ gives an actual speed with the corresponding directional localization. The denominator of the weighted-to-total ratio can be zero even when $M_0$ is a proper subset of $K$.

## Proof

The expression inside the positive part is affine in $(z,x)$ and continuous in $n$. Thus the field and its nonnegative continuous normal multiples satisfy the hypotheses of the actual roof realization 340f949c484a612f on the compatible lifted hull. At either endpoint its support subtraction equals the support of that fiber and the boundary value at its own outward normal is zero. At the middle, additivity and homogeneity of supports give the subtracted support $h_{M_0}(n)$. Because $M_0\subseteq K$, no clipping occurs at that boundary normal. Integrating $\Delta$ and using $\int h_K\,dS_K=d|K|$ by the divergence theorem gives the total. The weighted formula follows by the same boundary evaluation.

In dimension two take $K=\{x,y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\}$ and $A=B=K\cap\{x+y\ge h\}$ with $0<h<1$. The midpoint $M_0=A$ is positive and is a proper subset of $K$. The three fibers are exact in the lifted hull by their midpoint inclusion and exposed endpoints. The smaller body meets all three supporting lines of $K$, so their supports agree at all three normals carrying $S_K$. Hence the total integral is zero. Here proper inclusion of sets does not provide strict support slack on any set of positive $S_K$ measure.
