## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^k$ be a finite lattice-complete set spanning $\mathbb R^k$ affinely. Write $m=r+s$ with $r\ge1$ and $s\ge0$, and take compatible convex $m$-bodies $K_{\ell,z}\subset\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^s$ at the sites of $P$. Let $M_\ell=\sum_z|K_{\ell,z}|_m$ and assume each ratio $|K_{\ell,z}|_m/M_\ell$ has a positive limit. In the $r$ fine coordinates use one lattice coset $\Lambda_\ell+a_\ell$ of covolume $v_\ell$, with original fine law $\mu_\ell$ obtained by summing $v_\ell$ times the $s$-dimensional section volumes. Use counting for $s=0$.

The full-shape intrinsic lift can be made with component normalizations $T_{\ell,i}(u,x)=(A_{\ell,i}u,B_{\ell,i}u+C_{\ell,i}x)$ of absolute determinant $1/M_\ell$. In the bounded residual fibers $R_{\ell,z}=T_{\ell,i}K_{\ell,z}-g_{\ell,z}$ the fine coset is $A_{\ell,i}(\Lambda_\ell+a_\ell)-g_{\ell,z}^{u}$, with covolume $v_\ell|\det A_{\ell,i}|$. The covolume-weighted residual fine law pushes forward under the lift map to $\mu_\ell/M_\ell$. The condition that the covering radii $\rho_{\ell,i}$ of $A_{\ell,i}\Lambda_\ell$ tend to zero is independent of the chosen uniformly round block frame of the same component anchor.

Assume $\rho_{\ell,i}\to0$ for each component. Along any subsequence giving a full-shape limit lift with intrinsic law $\nu_*$, $\mu_\ell(\mathrm{total})/M_\ell\to1$. The liminf of maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth in the original fine trace is at least the maximum depth for $\nu_*$. The comparison is realized to arbitrary accuracy by actual original fine-feasible points. If this recovery is applied to a retained support, the corresponding bound for the full fine trace is multiplied by the limiting retained fine mass fraction, whenever the latter exists.

## Proof

In the construction of e961a354bb3cb9b8, postcompose each component's original shape map by an orthogonal map taking the image of $\{0\}\times\mathbb R^s$ to that subspace. This preserves its absolute determinant and diameter bounds. The resulting map has the asserted block form with both diagonal blocks invertible. Use the same representation-preserving translation construction in e961a354bb3cb9b8 for the rotated shapes. The placed residual fibers are then uniformly bounded in their fixed isometric component planes, and the construction gives a global affine recovery map with restriction $T_{\ell,i}^{-1}(w+g_{\ell,z})$ at each base $z$ of component $i$.

For two block frames $T,\widetilde T$ normalizing the same anchor, put $S=\widetilde T T^{-1}$. If the anchor images contain balls of radii $\rho,\widetilde\rho$ and lie in balls of radii $R,\widetilde R$, width comparison gives $\|S\|\le\widetilde R/\rho$ and $\|S^{-1}\|\le R/\widetilde\rho$. Their induced fine-coordinate blocks $\widetilde A A^{-1}$ and $A\widetilde A^{-1}$ have norm at most these respective bounds. A linear image multiplies the covering radius by at most its operator norm, by transporting a nearest lattice point. Applying this in both directions shows the radii compare by factors $\widetilde\rho/R$ and $\widetilde R/\rho$, as claimed for uniformly round frames.

Under the block map the sample condition is the stated residual coset. For fixed original $u$, the section map on $x$ has absolute Jacobian $|\det C_{\ell,i}|$. Thus residual covolume times residual section volume equals original covolume times original section volume divided by $M_\ell$, including the zero-dimensional case. Summing proves the pushforward identity for the residual fine law, denoted by $\sigma_\ell$.

Choose a common box $[-R,R]^m$ containing the residual fibers. Each residual coset has a measurable Voronoi fundamental cell in $[-\rho_{\ell,i},\rho_{\ell,i}]^r$. The convex-set estimate in the proof of e4ef1ac154c5d280 applies in this box to every residual fiber and every trace of a closed ambient halfspace on its fixed plane, including whole, empty and lower-dimensional traces. Write $\nu_\ell$ for the intrinsic coarse law of mass one. Summation bounds both $|\sigma_\ell(\mathrm{total})-1|$ and $|\sigma_\ell(H)-\nu_\ell(H)|$ by
$$
e_\ell=\sum_i |P_i|\,2r\rho_{\ell,i}(2R+2\rho_{\ell,i})^{m-1}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Here $P_i$ is the set of sites in component $i$. Once $e_\ell<1$, normalization gives uniform halfspace error at most $2e_\ell/(1-e_\ell)$. The pushforward identity gives the claimed fine mass convergence.

Take a point of the limiting lift with depth arbitrarily close to its maximum. By within-fiber continuity in e961a354bb3cb9b8, move it slightly into the interior of its positive residual fiber with arbitrarily small loss. Write the new residual coordinate as $(U,X)$. Hausdorff convergence of the residual fiber gives a fixed positive-radius ball about $(U,X)$ contained in every sufficiently late fiber at that site. Replace $U$ by a nearest point $U_\ell$ of the actual residual coset, and keep $X$ fixed. The displacement is at most $\rho_{\ell,i}$, so the rounded point lies in the late fiber and in the limiting fiber. Its image under the recovery map is an actual original fine point.

The intrinsic coarse laws $\nu_\ell$ converge to $\nu_*$ in total variation by almost-everywhere convergence of the indicators in each fixed plane and boundedness. Within-fiber continuity for $\nu_*$ and the uniform halfspace estimate therefore give the limiting lower bound for the normalized $\sigma_\ell$ depth at the rounded points. An original containing closed halfspace pulls back under the affine recovery map to a containing closed halfspace or the whole space, with exactly the corresponding normalized mass. Hence the same lower bound holds at their original images. Letting the interior perturbation and initial depth error tend to zero proves the liminf assertion.

For retained fine mass $T_+>0$ and full fine mass $T$, raw cap mass can only increase on adding the omitted fibers. Dividing the raw inequality by $T$ gives full normalized depth at least $T_+/T$ times retained normalized depth at every retained fine point. This gives the last assertion and the factor $1/(1+R_0)$ if $T_+/M_+\to1$ and $\limsup(T-T_+)/M_+\le R_0<\infty$.
