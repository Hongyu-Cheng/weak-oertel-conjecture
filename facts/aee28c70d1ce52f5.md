## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), let \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), and let \(D\) be a finite support satisfying
$$
B_k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k.
$$
Suppose every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two. The double-extension graph in each parity class is arbitrary. For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), use the original embedding and affine probabilities
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=k+1,& A&=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N,\\
w_x&=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),& Z&=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
&p_{(x,0)}&=w_x/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) only as an abbreviation. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{K\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in K}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap K}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum is ordered, with its diagonal counted once. Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; adding the whole space leaves the infimum unchanged. Let
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=\left\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\right\rbrace,\\
D_1^{\mathrm{full}}&=\left\lbrace x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k:
\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\right\rbrace,\\
W_1&=\sum_{x\in D_1}w_x,
&W_1^{\mathrm{full}}&=\sum_{x\in D_1^{\mathrm{full}}}w_x,
&\delta&=\frac{W_1}{W_1^{\mathrm{full}}}\in(0,1].
\end{aligned}
$$
Then both original cubics satisfy
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)},
\qquad
T(p)\ge2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
In particular, if the entire one-step core is contained in \(D\), then \(\delta=1\), and for every affine field
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-62}8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
If the entire one-step core is contained in \(D\) and all activities satisfy \(r_i=\exp(-\beta_i)\le16\), then the stronger bounds hold:
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-58}8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
No positive lower bound on \(\delta\) independent of the support and field is assumed or asserted. The notation and normalization are those of accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\). Only its assertion that a nonempty complete ternary support with at most one two and \(q\) occupied parity classes has both original cubics at least \(1/(512q^3)\) is used.

## Proof

Put \(\alpha=W_1/Z\) and \(\eta=W_1^{\mathrm{full}}/Z=\alpha/\delta\). We first establish the original core consumer
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\,8^k}.
\tag{1}
$$
The actual core \(D_1\) is complete. An integer point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) is ternary. If two of its coordinates were two, every generator having positive coefficient in a convex representation would have both coordinates equal to two, which no point of \(D_1\) does. Hence the point has at most one two. It is also in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\), and therefore belongs to \(D_1\). Since \(B_k\subseteq D_1\), its occupied parity count is exactly \(2^k\).

Condition on this actual core in the same ambient embedding. Its probabilities are \(\nu_{(x,0)}=w_x/W_1=p_x/\alpha\). Apply the one-step assertion of accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\) with dimension \(k\), support \(D_1\), field \(\beta\), partition function \(W_1\), probabilities \(\nu\), and parity count \(2^k\). Both conditional cubics are at least \(1/(512\,8^k)\). Every containing halfspace has original mass at least \(\alpha\) times its conditional core mass. At a core center, restricting the original reflection sum to pairs with both endpoints in the actual core also gives at least \(\alpha\) times its conditional reflection sum. Averaging just over core centers gives (1). The fourth power includes one factor from the original center probability and three from the depth or reflection sum. No missing one-step point is treated as an original atom.

We will also use a direct consumer at Boolean centers. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_B&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x\equiv y\pmod2\\(x+y)/2\in B_k}}
\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6},\\
R_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y)
\qquad(c\in B_k).
\end{aligned}
$$
Affine weights with the original normalization give \(p_c^2=p_xp_y\) whenever \(x+y=2c\). Thus each summand of \(L_B\) equals \(p_c^{1/3}\min(p_x,p_y)\), where \(c=(x+y)/2\). Grouping by its unique midpoint and applying Hölder on the \(2^k\) Boolean centers yields
$$
L_B=\sum_{c\in B_k}p_c^{1/3}R_p(c),
\qquad
L_B^3\le4^k\sum_{c\in B_k}p_cR_p(c)^3.
\tag{2}
$$
All these centers are actual because \(B_k\subseteq D\).

The full reflection sum satisfies \(s_p(c)=p_c+R_p(c)\). Distinct unordered reflected pairs about \(c\) have disjoint endpoints, since the partner of an endpoint \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\); their endpoints are also distinct from \(c\). Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains at least one endpoint of each pair. If both endpoints were strictly outside its defining affine inequality, their midpoint would also be strictly outside. Summing the minimum probabilities of these disjoint unordered pairs and including the center atom proves
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\frac12R_p(c).
$$
This argument covers every containing closed halfspace, including those whose boundary contains the center. It requires no affine rank assumption. If a halfspace restricts to a zero normal on the affine span of the embedded support and contains the center, it contains the whole support and satisfies the same inequality. Retaining only Boolean centers in the two original cubics consequently gives the common bound
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge\frac{L_B^3}{8\,4^k}.
\tag{3}
$$

We next prove a finite graph estimate. Let \(G=(U,E)\) be any finite simple graph, assign \(0<u_i\le1\) to its vertices, and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V&=\sum_{i\in U}u_i,
&a_e&=u_i u_j\quad(e=\lbrace i,j\rbrace\in E),\\
\mathcal W&=\sum_{e\in E}a_e,
&\mathcal K&=\sum_{\substack{e,f\in E\\e\cap f=\varnothing}}
\min(a_e,a_f)^{7/6}\max(a_e,a_f)^{1/6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last sum is ordered. We claim
$$
\mathcal W\le4\mathcal V+\frac32\sqrt{\mathcal K}.
\tag{4}
$$
For \(0<t\le1\), let \(E(t)=\lbrace e:a_e\ge t\rbrace\), let \(m(t)=|E(t)|\), let \(n(t)=|\lbrace i:u_i\ge t\rbrace|\), and let \(J(t)\) count ordered pairs of disjoint edges in \(E(t)\). Both endpoints of an edge in \(E(t)\) have weight at least \(t\), because all vertex weights are at most one. This threshold graph therefore has at most \(n(t)\) vertices. Each edge intersects at most \(2n(t)\) of its edges, counting itself within this upper bound. Thus
\(J(t)\ge m(t)(m(t)-2n(t))\).
If \(m(t)\ge4n(t)\), this gives \(J(t)\ge m(t)^2/2\). If \(m(t)<4n(t)\), the direct bound suffices. Hence in either case
$$
m(t)\le4n(t)+\sqrt{2J(t)}.
\tag{5}
$$
Finite-sum integration gives \(\int_0^1m(t)\,dt=\mathcal W\) and \(\int_0^1n(t)\,dt=\mathcal V\). Each summand of \(\mathcal K\) is at least \(\min(a_e,a_f)^{4/3}\), so integration of the corresponding pair indicators gives
$$
\mathcal K\ge\frac43\int_0^1t^{1/3}J(t)\,dt.
$$
Cauchy--Schwarz and the integrable factor \(t^{-1/3}\) yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\sqrt{J(t)}\,dt
&\le\left(\int_0^1t^{1/3}J(t)\,dt\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int_0^1t^{-1/3}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le\frac{3}{2\sqrt2}\sqrt{\mathcal K}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(\int_0^1t^{-1/3}\,dt=3/2\). Integrating (5) proves (4), also for empty graphs.

It follows that
$$
\mathcal W\ge8\mathcal V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal K\ge\mathcal W^{4/3}.
\tag{6}
$$
If \(\mathcal W=0\), this is immediate. Otherwise (4) and \(4\mathcal V\le\mathcal W/2\) imply \(\mathcal K\ge\mathcal W^2/9\). Simplicity of the graph gives \(\mathcal W\le\mathcal V^2/2\). Together with \(\mathcal W\ge8\mathcal V>0\), this implies \(\mathcal V\ge16\) and \(\mathcal W\ge128\). In particular \(\mathcal W\ge27\), so \(\mathcal W^2/9\ge\mathcal W^{4/3}\), proving (6).

For the exact scaling needed below, suppose \(0<v_i\le R\). Write \(\mathcal V_v=\sum_i v_i\), \(\mathcal W_v=\sum_{\lbrace i,j\rbrace\in E}v_i v_j\), and let \(\mathcal K_v\) be the same ordered disjoint-edge kernel using edge weights \(v_i v_j\). Applying (6) to \(u_i=v_i/R\), for which
\(\mathcal V=\mathcal V_v/R\), \(\mathcal W=\mathcal W_v/R^2\), and \(\mathcal K=\mathcal K_v/R^{8/3}\), gives
$$
\mathcal W_v\ge8R\mathcal V_v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal K_v\ge\mathcal W_v^{4/3}.
\tag{7}
$$

Return to the original support and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_i&=\exp(-\beta_i),
&B&=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+r_i),
&a_i&=\frac{r_i^2}{1+r_i},\\
H&=\lbrace i:r_i>16\rbrace,
&J&=H^c,
&h&=|H|,
&s&=|J|.
\end{aligned}
$$
The hypothetical full-core partition satisfies
$$
W_1^{\mathrm{full}}=B\left(1+\sum_i a_i\right).
\tag{8}
$$
Indeed the Boolean part contributes \(B\), and fixing its unique two at \(i\) contributes \(Ba_i\). This hypothetical full core is used only to bound sums of weights; it does not alter the original support or law.

Let \(p_H\) be the original marginal on the high block and define
$$
m_H=p(\exists i\in H:x_i=2),
\qquad
P_H=8^h\sum_{\substack{u\in\pi_H(D)\\u\text{ contains a two}}}p_H(u)^4.
$$
For empty \(H\), set \(m_H=P_H=0\). For each Boolean parity source \(b\in B_k\), form a graph using only its low zero coordinates:
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_b&=\lbrace i\in J:b_i=0\rbrace,\\
E_b&=\left\lbrace\lbrace i,j\rbrace\subseteq U_b:
b+2e_i+2e_j\in D\right\rbrace,\\
\mathcal V_b&=\sum_{i\in U_b}r_i^2,
&\mathcal W_b&=\sum_{\lbrace i,j\rbrace\in E_b}r_i^2r_j^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Let \(\mathcal K_b\) be its ordered disjoint-edge kernel. All its vertex weights \(r_i^2\) are at most \(256\). The original probability of the double point corresponding to an edge \(\lbrace i,j\rbrace\) is exactly \(p_b r_i^2r_j^2\). Every double point with both its two-indices in \(J\) has a unique such source and edge. Therefore its total original mass, say \(d_J\), is \(\sum_b p_b\mathcal W_b\). The complement of the high-two event consists of such double points and actual core points without a high two. Hence
$$
d_J\ge1-\alpha-m_H.
\tag{9}
$$
The full Boolean cube is present, so direct summation of its original weights gives the exact identity and upper bound
$$
\sum_{b\in B_k}p_b\mathcal V_b
=\frac{B}{Z}\sum_{j\in J}\frac{r_j^2}{1+r_j}
\le\frac{W_1^{\mathrm{full}}}{Z}=\eta.
\tag{10}
$$
This remains valid when any or all of the corresponding single extensions are missing; the left side is a sum at actual Boolean sources, with hypothetical single weights used only in its evaluation.

Let \(\mathcal G=\lbrace b:\mathcal W_b\ge2048\mathcal V_b\rbrace\), and let \(m=\sum_{b\in\mathcal G}p_b\mathcal W_b\) be the mass of its low-low double points. The mass in the other classes is at most \(2048\sum_b p_b\mathcal V_b\le2048\eta\). By (9),
$$
m\ge1-\alpha-m_H-2048\eta.
\tag{11}
$$
For each class in \(\mathcal G\), (7) with \(R=256\) proves \(\mathcal K_b\ge\mathcal W_b^{4/3}\). This also holds for empty graphs, where all the relevant quantities are zero.

An ordered pair of disjoint edges \(\lbrace i,j\rbrace,\lbrace a,\ell\rbrace\in E_b\) gives two distinct original double points with Boolean midpoint \(b+e_i+e_j+e_a+e_\ell\). The map to the ordered endpoint pair is injective, including across different parity classes, and its contribution to \(L_B\) is the graph kernel multiplied by \(p_b^{4/3}\). Hölder on at most \(2^k\) parity sources gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_B&\ge\sum_{b\in\mathcal G}p_b^{4/3}\mathcal K_b
\ge\sum_{b\in\mathcal G}(p_b\mathcal W_b)^{4/3}\\
&\ge2^{-k/3}m^{4/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Together with (3), this proves a second universal consumer on the present class:
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge\frac{m^4}{8}\,8^{-k}.
\tag{12}
$$
No actual single-extension point was required anywhere in this graph argument.

We next prove the high-activity cap
$$
m_H\le4096\eta+512P_H^{1/4}.
\tag{13}
$$
It is immediate for empty \(H\). The finite estimate for \(h\ge1\) is proved here. Take an arbitrary list \(r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_h>16\), put \(B_H=\prod_{i=1}^h(1+r_i)\) and \(a_H=\sum_{i=1}^h r_i^2/(1+r_i)\), and let \(X\) be all ternary patterns in these coordinates with exactly one or two twos. Define
$$
g_u=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\prod_i r_i^{u_i}}{B_H},&u\text{ has one two},\\[4pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\prod_i r_i^{u_i}}{B_H(1+a_H)},&u\text{ has two twos}.
\end{cases}
$$
With \(t=2^{-h}\), we claim
$$
\sum_{u\in X}\min(g_u,t)\le4096.
\tag{14}
$$
Let \(\rho=5/(4\sqrt2)<9/10\). For a pattern whose unique two is at \(i\), let \(S\) be its set of zero coordinates. The two-coordinate factor is at most \(r_i\), each zero-coordinate factor at most \(1/r_j\), and each one-coordinate factor at most one, so \(g_u\le r_i/\prod_{j\in S}r_j\). If \(S\) has no index less than \(i\), there are \(2^{h-i}\) such patterns, whose total truncated contribution over all \(i\) is at most \(\sum_i2^{h-i}t\le1\). Otherwise some \(j\in S\) has \(j<i\), so \(r_j\ge r_i\), and \(g_u\le16^{1-|S|}\). Using \(\min(v,t)\le\sqrt{vt}\) and enlarging the latter sum to all zero sets for every \(i\) bounds its contribution by
$$
\begin{aligned}
h\sum_{S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,h-1\rbrace}\sqrt{t\,16^{1-|S|}}
&=4h2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-1}\\
&=2\sqrt2\,h\rho^{h-1}<300.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed \(h\rho^{h-1}\le\sum_{n\ge1}n(9/10)^{n-1}=100\) and \(2\sqrt2<3\). Thus the one-two contribution to (14) is at most \(301\).

For a pattern with twos at \(i<j\), let \(S\) again be its zero set, now disjoint from \(\lbrace i,j\rbrace\). Since \(1+a_H\ge r_i^2/(1+r_i)\ge r_i/2\), the same factor bounds give \(g_u\le2r_j/\prod_{\ell\in S}r_\ell\). If \(S\) has no index less than \(j\) other than the already excluded index \(i\), there are \(2^{h-j}\) such patterns for this pair. Their total truncated contribution is at most \(\sum_{j=2}^h(j-1)2^{-j}\le2\). Otherwise there is \(\ell\in S\) with \(\ell<j\) and \(\ell\ne i\), giving \(g_u\le2\,16^{1-|S|}\). For \(h\ge2\), enlargement to all zero sets for every pair and the square-root bound give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\binom h2\sum_{S\subseteq\lbrace1,\ldots,h-2\rbrace}
\sqrt{t\,2\,16^{1-|S|}}
&=\binom h2\sqrt{32}\,2^{-h/2}(5/4)^{h-2}\\
&=\sqrt2\,h(h-1)\rho^{h-2}<3000.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(h(h-1)\rho^{h-2}\le\sum_{n\ge2}n(n-1)(9/10)^{n-2}=2000\) and \(\sqrt2<3/2\). The two-two contribution is absent for \(h=1\); otherwise it is at most \(3002\). Since \(301+3002<4096\), (14) holds for all \(h\ge1\). This is a finite activity estimate with no graph or support assumption.

Apply it after sorting the original high block only for this estimate. Put \(B_H=\prod_{i\in H}(1+r_i)\), \(B_J=\prod_{i\in J}(1+r_i)\), \(a_H=\sum_{i\in H}a_i\), and \(a_J=\sum_{i\in J}a_i\). Empty products are one and empty sums zero. A high pattern with one two has low completions with at most one two, whose total weight is at most \(B_J(1+a_J)\); one with two twos has only binary low completions, whose total weight is at most \(B_J\). These upper bounds use the full hypothetical completion sets. Since \(\eta=B_HB_J(1+a_H+a_J)/Z\), they give, respectively,
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_H(u)&\le\eta\frac{1+a_J}{1+a_H+a_J}
\frac{\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{u_i}}{B_H}\le\eta g_u,\\
p_H(u)&\le\eta\frac{\prod_{i\in H}r_i^{u_i}}{B_H(1+a_H+a_J)}
\le\eta g_u.
\end{aligned}
$$
Set \(p_H(u)=0\) on nonactual patterns of \(X\). The part where \(g_u\le t\) has marginal mass at most \(4096\eta\), by (14). The number of patterns where \(g_u>t\) is at most \(4096/t=4096\,2^h\). Hölder on those patterns yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_H&\le4096\eta+(4096\,2^h)^{3/4}
\left(\sum_{u\in X}p_H(u)^4\right)^{1/4}\\
&=4096\eta+512P_H^{1/4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This proves (13) without a star hypothesis or a lower bound on actual core coverage.

For nonempty \(H\) we also have the original section consumer
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge\frac{P_H}{512\,8^k}.
\tag{15}
$$
To check every residual hypothesis and normalization, fix an actual high pattern \(u\) containing a two and set
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_u&=\lbrace x\in D:x_H=u\rbrace,
&D_u&=\lbrace y\in\mathbb Z^J:(u,y)\in D\rbrace,\\
Z_u&=\sum_{y\in D_u}\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y),
&\lambda_u&=p_H(u)=\frac{\exp(-\beta_H\cdot u)Z_u}{Z},\\
\sigma^{(u)}_{(y,0)}&=\frac{\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y)}{Z_u}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Insertion into the original coordinate positions is understood. Let \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(D)\) and \(Q_u=\lbrace y\in\mathbb R^J:(u,y)\in Q\rbrace\). This is compact and convex. Completeness gives \(Q_u\cap\mathbb Z^J=D_u\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(D_u)\cap\mathbb Z^J=D_u\). The residual support is nonempty, ternary, and has at most one two, because the fixed high pattern already contains at least one two. It occupies at most \(2^s\) parity classes.

When \(s\ge1\), apply the one-step assertion of accepted fact \(\texttt{f27ee487069e27e7}\) with dimension \(s\), support \(D_u\), residual field \(\beta_J\), partition \(Z_u\), and conditional probabilities \(\sigma^{(u)}\) on \(D_u\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^{s+1}\). It gives both residual cubics at least \(1/(512\,8^s)\). If \(s=0\), an actual residual support is the singleton empty vector. Its conditional law is a single atom in \(\mathbb R^1\); its depth, full reflection sum, and both cubics are exactly one, so the same lower bound holds. The accepted result also covers residual singletons and every lower affine rank when \(s\ge1\).

Let \(\iota_u(y,v)=((u,y),v)\). Then \(p_{\iota_u(y,0)}=\lambda_u\sigma^{(u)}_{(y,0)}\). The pullback of a containing original halfspace with inequality \(\ell\cdot x+\gamma v\ge b_0\) has inequality \(\ell_J\cdot y+\gamma v\ge b_0-\ell_H\cdot u\). If the residual normal \((\ell_J,\gamma)\) is nonzero, it is a containing closed affine halfspace in the residual space. If that normal is zero, containment of the center implies that the pullback is the whole residual space and has conditional mass one. Thus the original containing-halfspace depth is at least \(\lambda_u\) times the residual depth. For reflections, the pairs with both endpoints in \(F_u\) are exactly the lifted residual reflected pairs, and the minimum endpoint probabilities have the common factor \(\lambda_u\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in F_u}p_x\tau_p((x,0))^3
&\ge\lambda_u^4M_3(\sigma^{(u)}),\\
\sum_{c\in F_u}p_c s_p(c)^3
&\ge\lambda_u^4T(\sigma^{(u)}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Summing over the disjoint high-pattern sections containing a two proves (15), since
$$
\frac1{512\,8^s}\sum_{u\text{ containing a two}}p_H(u)^4
=\frac{P_H}{512\,8^k}.
$$
In particular no fourth-power factor is lost through conditioning.

We finish the weighted theorem by a mass split. If \(\eta\ge2^{-14}\), then \(\alpha=\delta\eta\ge2^{-14}\delta\), and (1) gives
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge2^{-65}\delta^4 8^{-k}
=2^{-62}\delta^4 8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Suppose instead that \(\eta<2^{-14}\). If \(m_H\ge1/2\), necessarily \(H\ne\varnothing\), and (13) gives \(512P_H^{1/4}>1/4\). Thus \(P_H>2^{-44}\), and (15) gives
\(\min(M_3(p),T(p))>2^{-53}8^{-k}=2^{-50}8^{-(k+1)}\).
This is stronger than the desired bound because \(\delta\le1\).

If \(m_H<1/2\), use \(\alpha\le\eta\) and (11) to get
$$
m>\frac12-2049\eta
>\frac12-\frac{2049}{16384}>\frac14.
$$
Then (12) yields
$$
\min\bigl(M_3(p),T(p)\bigr)
\ge2^{-11}8^{-k}=2^{-8}8^{-(k+1)},
$$
again stronger than the desired bound. If \(H\) is empty, \(m_H=0\), so this argument still applies. If \(J\) is empty, its low graph has no edges; the derived lower bound on \(m\) shows that this last branch is impossible. The other branches already cover that case, using the residual singleton calculation when needed. These observations cover both empty coordinate blocks without invoking an estimate in positive dimension where it does not apply.

If the entire one-step core is present, then \(W_1=W_1^{\mathrm{full}}\) and \(\delta=1\); this proves the stated arbitrary-field full-core corollary. Finally assume both full-core presence and \(r_i\le16\) for all coordinates. Here \(\eta=\alpha\), \(H\) is empty, and \(m_H=0\). If \(\alpha\ge2^{-13}\), (1) gives
\(\min(M_3(p),T(p))\ge2^{-61}8^{-k}=2^{-58}8^{-(k+1)}\).
If \(\alpha<2^{-13}\), (11) gives \(m\ge1-2049\alpha>1-2049/8192>1/2\). Equation (12) then gives the stronger common bound \(2^{-7}8^{-k}=2^{-4}8^{-(k+1)}\). This proves the original bounded-field full-core corollary as well.
