## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a compact convex body of volume $V$, with ordinary deepest point $g$ and maximum raw ordinary depth $q$. Take compact convex bodies $A,C\subset K\cap(2g-K)$ of equal volume $a>0$, and take the hull of $A,K,K,C$ over the respective bases $0,1,2,3$. These are the exact sampled fibers. The only globally deepest mixed points are $(1,g),(2,g)$, the global raw maximum is $Q=q+a$, and $M=2(V+a)$.

There are finite positive ordinary critical circuits at $g$ with at most $d+1$ inward unit normals $u_j$, all through-$g$ cap masses $q$, and positive coefficients balancing the normals. For any such circuit, take a common sufficiently large $L>0$. The caps $u_j\cdot(x-g)-L(z-1)\ge0$ at the first middle fiber and $u_j\cdot(x-g)+L(z-2)\ge0$ at the second form regular globally critical menus. Each cap strictly contains its paying endpoint ($A$ or $C$), cuts its own middle fiber by the ordinary cap, and strictly misses the other two fibers. Their sole proper sections have full mixed centroid at the respective marked point. The respective menus cover all planes at $z\le1$ and $z\ge2$.

If $2g-A,2g-C\subset\operatorname{int}K$, every critical halfspace through $(1,g)$ strictly contains $A$ at $0$, and every critical halfspace through $(2,g)$ strictly contains $C$ at $3$.

## Proof

All continuous coordinates lie in $K$, so the inner hull sections are exactly $K$ and the exposed endpoint sections are the prescribed bodies. The projection is $[0,3]$. For any halfspace containing $(1,g)$, the placed copy of $A$ at base zero and its reflected copy $2g-A$ inside the base-two $K$ form a centrally symmetric submeasure around $(1,g)$ of mass $2a$. Such a closed halfspace captures at least $a$ of that pair. The base-one $K$ contributes at least $q$. The reflected copy of $C$ in the base-one $K$ proves the same bound at $(2,g)$. This handles pure-base halfspaces as well.

For any mark $(1,x)$ in the first middle fiber choose a minimizing ordinary through-$x$ cap of $K$ and give it a sufficiently large negative finite base tilt about base one. It strictly captures $A$ and strictly misses the other two nonanchor fibers. Therefore the mixed depth there is at most $a+\operatorname{depth}_K(x)$. The same argument with positive tilt applies at base two. At either exposed endpoint a sufficiently large outward base tilt isolates the ordinary minimum cap of that fiber; conversely its contribution is unavoidable. The endpoint depth is thus its ordinary depth, at most $a/2<q+a$.

Ordinary cap mass through a mark is jointly continuous in the mark and in the continuous unit normal, by dominated convergence and the zero volume of each hyperplane. The ordinary depth attains its maximum on $K$ by compactness. It is positive at an inball center and zero at boundary points. At an interior maximizing mark every active ordinary cap has a proper section and positive coarea. View the ordinary body as one sampled layer in the regular first variation and singleton exposed-contact calculation of 1269c554ca9a41b8 §§2–3. This makes its section centroid equal the mark, and puts every other deepest mark strictly outside each critical cap. The convex hull of the active unit normals contains zero. Otherwise strict separation of that compact set gives a continuous direction having positive dot product with every active normal. In a small neighborhood of the active normals all those dot products stay positive, and every old plane crosses a common small ball about the mark. Moving a small positive distance opposite to that direction strictly increases their through-mark cap masses by a uniform positive bound for fixed distance. All other normals have a uniform positive old cap-mass gap and retain it by continuity. This contradicts maximality. Carathéodory and removal of zero weights now give a positive circuit with at most $d+1$ members. No other deepest mark can be strictly outside each cap of that circuit, as the positively weighted sum of their margins is zero. This proves ordinary uniqueness and hence the claimed mixed uniqueness on each middle layer.

Choose $L$ greater than all absolute values $|u_j\cdot(x-g)|$ on the four bodies. The stated mixed caps have the exact strict statuses and mass $q+a$, and are regular because the only proper section is on the source layer. Its ordinary section centroid is $g$, so the full mixed section centroid is the marked point. At each $z\le1$ the weighted sum of the first list's margins is nonnegative by the positive normal balance, giving coverage of that plane. The second list covers each $z\ge2$ by the same computation with the positive base tilt.

For strict reflected containment consider a critical cap through $(1,g)$. A pure-base cap has own contribution $V>q$ and paired contribution at least $a$, so cannot be critical. For a cap with nonzero continuous normal, equality in the lower-bound decomposition forces no volume from outside $2g-A$ in the base-two $K$. If its base-two trace had positive volume, its threshold would be below the support of $K$ in that normal. Strict containment of $2g-A$ gives strictly smaller support there. The cap would then capture positive volume near the $K$ support, beyond both thresholds and outside $2g-A$. Therefore the base-two trace has zero volume and its threshold is at least the support of $K$. It strictly misses $2g-A$, and ambient reflection around $(1,g)$ shows that the original cap strictly contains the placed $A$. Reflection around $(2,g)$ gives the other assertion.
