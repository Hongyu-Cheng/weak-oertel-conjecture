## Statement

On $\Delta_3=\{u\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4:\sum_j u_j=1\}$ let $U$ be uniform, $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$, and $\phi_3(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$. For fixed weights $\omega_i>0$, maximize $\sum_{i=1}^4\omega_i\phi_3(X_i)$ over nonnegative $4\times4$ matrices with column sums one, and take a maximizer with the fewest nonzero rows. Suppose its active rows consist of two strictly positive rows $x,y$ and one row $z$ whose only zero is at column $h$. Write $b$ for the common actual centroid of the full rows' proper minimizing sections, label $x$ as the row strictly capturing $h$, and write $c^x=t_x\mathbf1-x,\ c^y=t_y\mathbf1-y$ for their actual margins. The two cuts are strict quadrilaterals with complementary captured pairs. Moreover $z_j=w/b_j$ for $j\ne h$ and some $w>0$, and
$$
c^x+c^y=-w m_h,\qquad (m_h)_h=3,\quad (m_h)_j=3-1/b_j\quad(j\ne h).
$$
The actual vertex-$h$ section with oriented margin $m_h$ also has centroid $b$.

## Proof

By cd8f8e2a7b4c9d2b the two full rows have proper cuts and common actual centroid $b$, $b_h>1/3$, and exactly one row strictly captures $h$ while the other strictly excludes it. The common gradient divided by its coordinate sum is $b$, so 5097879468363a7e gives $z_j=w/b_j$ with $w>0$ and $b_j\le1/3$ for $j\ne h$. Since $t_x=b\cdot x$, $t_y=b\cdot y$ and $b\cdot z=3w$, summing the columns gives
$$
(c^x+c^y)_h=-3w,\qquad (c^x+c^y)_j=w(1/b_j-3)\quad(j\ne h).
$$
This is the asserted margin identity. The cut of $m_h$ meets each edge from $h$ at coordinate $3b_j$ of its other endpoint $j$, because its affine value on that edge at parameter $s$ equals $3-s/b_j$. These parameters belong to $(0,1]$. Averaging the three section vertices proves the centroid is $b$.

A proper triangular section can be oriented toward a vertex it isolates, with the three section vertices on that vertex's incident edges, allowing a vertex on the plane. Each nonisolated centroid coordinate is an edge intercept divided by three, hence at most $1/3$. A triangular section with centroid $b$ must therefore isolate $h$. If it were the section of $x$, it would strictly capture only $h$, contrary to the two strictly captured vertices guaranteed by 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9. Thus $x$ is a strict quadrilateral capturing $\{h,j\}$ for some $j\ne h$. The two coordinates excluded by $x$ must be strictly captured by $y$, by the displayed balance. If $y$ also strictly excludes $j$, the conclusion follows. Otherwise its section is triangular, with $h$ isolated and possibly a plane vertex at $j$.

For that triangular alternative, the vertex-$h$ cap has volume fraction $V=27\prod_{i\ne h}b_i$ by the three intercepts $3b_i$. The actual row-$y$ margin is the opposite orientation with margin $-r$ at $h$ and $r(1/(3b_i)-1)$ at $i\ne h$, for some $r>0$. The density at zero of the associated independent unit exponential sum is $V/r$: for the reversed margin condition on its nonpositive phases, so the survival at zero is the expectation of an exponential with scale $r$ and the density there is this expectation divided by $r$. The presence of a strictly negative phase ensures continuity at zero; a zero phase does not affect the conditioning. Reversing the sign preserves the zero density. Threshold stationarity gives $t_y=(1-V)/(V/r)$, since the simplex projection density at zero is three times the exponential density as in 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9. Hence with $L=r/V$ one has $y_h=L$ and $y_i=L[1-V/(3b_i)]$ off $h$.

Column sums now give $x_h=1-L$ and $x_i=1-L+\gamma/b_i$ off $h$, where $\gamma=LV/3-w$. Therefore $t_x=1-L+3\gamma$ and the margins of $x$ are $3\gamma$ at $h$ and $\gamma(3-1/b_i)$ off $h$. Strict capture of $h$ implies $\gamma>0$, making every other margin nonpositive. This contradicts 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9. Thus the two cuts are strict complementary quadrilaterals, including when a central centroid coordinate equals $1/3$.
