## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)\subset\mathbb R^2$, let $V=|T|_2$, and let $\gamma_2=4/9$. Define $C=\operatorname{conv}((\{-1\}\times(3T/4))\cup(\{0\}\times T)\cup(\{1\}\times(3T/4)))$ and $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)$. For each affine upper majorant of the three ordinary maximal-depth roots, define the retained contact bound $B_Q/k_Q$ by summing raw ordinary maxima over its positive contact set $Q$ and dividing by the number $k_Q$ of occupied base parities. The maximum over nonempty contact sets is $\gamma_2 V$, strictly below $(\gamma_2/2)\mathcal H_2(S)=(17/16)\gamma_2 V$.

## Proof

The endpoint fibers are exactly $3T/4$. In a convex combination with base zero the total weights on the two endpoint bases agree, and the middle section of their hull alone is $3T/4\subset T$. Hence the exact middle fiber is $T$. The sum of the areas is $(1+2(3/4)^2)V=17V/8$.

By 03bc771bddd39a8a each simplex has ordinary maximal depth equal to $\gamma_2$ times its area. The roots here are therefore proportional to $(3/4,1,3/4)$. An affine sampled upper majorant cannot touch both endpoints, because its value at the middle would then be below the middle root. Each nonempty contact set is a singleton or an adjacent pair. The bound is at most $\gamma_2 V$ for a singleton. Adjacent bases have different parities and their two depths give $(1+9/16)\gamma_2 V/2=(25/32)\gamma_2 V$. The constant majorant at the middle root has the middle singleton as contact and attains $\gamma_2 V$.
