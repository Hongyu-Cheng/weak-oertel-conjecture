## Statement

Let $U$ be a nonempty finite complete subset of an affine lattice with direction lattice $L$, with $k(U)$ occupied classes modulo $2L$. Let positive compact convex $d$-fibers $K_u$, $d\ge1$, form a compatible family on $U$, with total mass $M>0$ and normalized maximum closed-halfspace depth strictly below $\gamma_d/k(U)$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Then $|K_u|_d<M/k(U)$ for every $u$, so $|U|>k(U)$. In any Euclidean norm, a shortest distinct same-parity pair has the form $p,p+2v$ with $v$ primitive in $L$. Its midpoint $p+v$ belongs to $U$, has different parity, and has fiber containing $(K_p+K_{p+2v})/2$.

## Proof

In accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d take $m=|K_u|_d$, so the full-fiber centroid belongs to its trimmed barycenter set and has own-fiber depth at least $\gamma_d |K_u|_d$. Every closed ambient halfspace containing that marked point restricts to a containing closed halfspace or the whole fiber plane. Its mixed mass is at least its mass on the marked fiber. The strict mixed depth hypothesis therefore forces $|K_u|_d<M/k(U)$ for each site. Summing gives $|U|>k(U)$ and a repeated parity class.

Let $p,p+2v$ be any closest distinct pair of equal parity. If $v=mw$ for $w\in L$ and an integer $m\ge2$, then $p+2w$ is a lattice point on their segment, and is in $U$ by completeness. It forms a strictly closer same-parity pair with $p$, a contradiction. Thus $v$ is primitive. The integer midpoint is in $U$ by completeness, and its difference from $p$ is not in $2L$ by primitivity. Compatibility at this midpoint gives the claimed inclusion.
