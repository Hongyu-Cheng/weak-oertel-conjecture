## Statement

Use raw summed volume of a finite family of compact convex fibers with positive $d$-dimensional volume, and let $Y$ be interior to its own fiber with raw depth $Q$. Let finitely many nonvertical critical closed caps through $Y$ have mass $Q$ and continuous normals $u_j$ positively balanced by weights $\rho_j$ with $\sum_j\rho_j=1$. Linearly perturb their full normals to $N_j+sN'_j$ with continuous parts $u_j+s w_j$. Define $m_j(W)=N_j\cdot(W-Y)$, $\ell_j(W)=N'_j\cdot(W-Y)$ and $F_j(s,c)=\mu\{m_j+s\ell_j\ge c\}$. Assume $F_j$ is $C^2$ near $(0,0)$ and $S_j=-\partial_cF_j(0,0)>0$. Put $A_j=\partial_{ss}F_j(0,0)$. Then $\partial_sF_j(0,0)=0$, $A_j\ge0$, and the actual raw $Q$-quantile threshold is
$$
c_j(s)=\frac{A_j}{2S_j}s^2+o(s^2).
$$
For $s>0$ the coefficients $\rho_j/s$ combine the continuous normals exactly to $\sum_j\rho_jw_j$ and the thresholds to $(s/2)\sum_j\rho_j A_j/S_j+o(s)$. If the missing components of unit representatives have magnitude $O(s)$, recovering a nonzero component there requires total positive coefficient $\Omega(1/s)$. With a $C^1$ cap map the scalar inversion still gives $c_j(s)=o(s)$ and the same normal identity with threshold sum $o(1)$.

Under local coarea moment regularity, writing $d\sigma$ for the sum of fiber-section areas divided by $|u_j|$, the first normal derivative is $\int_{m_j=0}\ell_j\,d\sigma$, and $A_j=-\partial_c\int_{m_j=c}\ell_j^2\,d\sigma|_{c=0}$. In particular the identities hold for stable proper, strictly full or strictly empty cuts of finite polytopal fibers whose varying planes avoid the vertices. If a continuous direction $e$ satisfies $u_j\cdot e=0$, $w_j\cdot e\ne0$ and $A_j>0$ for some $j$, then
$$
h_\mu(Y+he)\le Q-\frac{S_j^2(w_j\cdot e)^2}{2A_j}h^2+o(h^2).
$$

## Proof

All the through-$Y$ caps contain $Y$, so $F_j(s,0)\ge Q=F_j(0,0)$. Two-sided differentiability gives zero first derivative and nonnegative second derivative. The scalar implicit function theorem applies by $-S_j<0$ and its resulting quantile has $c'_j(0)=0$ and $c''_j(0)=A_j/S_j$. The proper own cut guarantees the strict threshold decrease locally; thresholds farther away cannot give the same mass by monotonicity. Multiplying the normals and quantiles by the stated coefficients proves both sums. Projection onto the missing subspace gives the necessary growth bound, and the $C^1$ version follows by first differentiation only.

For the moment identities, test $F_j(s,\cdot)$ against a smooth threshold test function $\varphi$ of compact support in the neighborhood. The resulting integral is $\int\int_{-\infty}^{m_j(W)+s\ell_j(W)}\varphi(c)\,dc\,d\mu(W)$. Differentiate once and twice in $s$ by boundedness of the support and coarea on each fiber. The results are respectively the integrals of $\varphi(c)$ against the first section moment and of $\varphi'(c)$ against the second section moment. Hence the identities hold as distributions in $c$. Where the first section moment is continuous and the second is locally $C^1$, equality holds pointwise. For the stable polytope case, intersections along crossed edges depend smoothly on the plane and the clipping incidence stays fixed; triangulating the resulting sections and clipped polytopes by face flags gives smooth integrals of polynomial moments. Strictly full or empty cuts contribute constants to cap mass and zero to the section moments. This supplies the claimed sufficient regularity.

For the displaced source, its threshold in the tilted cap about $Y$ is exactly $sh(w_j\cdot e)$ by $u_j\cdot e=0$. Write $s=S_j(w_j\cdot e)h/A_j$. The joint Taylor expansion has linear threshold term $-S_j sh(w_j\cdot e)$ and tilt term $A_js^2/2$; all mixed and quadratic threshold terms are $o(h^2)$. Their sum is the displayed negative quadratic coefficient. This is the mass of an actual cap containing the displaced point, which proves the upper bound.
