## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), put \(K=2^n\). Let \(B\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\) contain \(0\), set
$$
A=B\cup(-B),\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}(A),
$$
and choose any \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\). Retain the entire original affine law and its normalizer:
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in A}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}Z\quad(x\in A),
$$
with \(p_x=0\) off \(A\). Then \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\).

Use exactly the full canonical rows and normalizations of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,
&\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},\\
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},
&V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in each row sum, and the central atom occurs once. Define
$$
A_0=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3.
$$
This \(A_0\) is the atom quantity, not the ratio \(\chi(p)/b(p)\). All logarithms are natural. With the absolute constant \(C=2^{70}\),
$$
A_0<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0\le\nu\le C\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{1}
$$
The full-cube case of (1) is already accepted in e2506c0626ec02e6 and is imported rather than reproved. The assertion here adds every proper subset \(B\).

There is the following immediate actual-depth consequence. Let \(L\ge1\), choose arbitrary integers \(n_i\ge1\), subsets \(B_i\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n_i}\) containing zero, and vectors \(\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\). Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_i&=B_i\cup(-B_i),&
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}e^{\ell_i\cdot x},&
p_{i,x}&=\frac{e^{\ell_i\cdot x}}{Z_i},\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i,&
c_*&=\frac14e^{-2^{70}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For every supported \(J\), use the original ambient closed-halfspace depth
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace.
$$
Then the entire original law satisfies
$$
M_2(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^2\ge c_*4^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3\ge c_*^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{17}
$$
There is no activity restriction on any factor in (17). Each \(A_i\) is its complete convex lattice census, and their product is also a complete convex lattice census. The claim concerns this Boolean-subset family and its products only.

## Proof

Write \(M=|B|\) and \(h=|A|=2M-1\). First verify the geometry and all normalizations. Every point of \(Q\) can be written
$$
\lambda u-(1-\lambda)v,
\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1,\qquad u,v\in\operatorname{conv}(B)\subseteq[0,1]^n.
$$
For \(0<\lambda<1\), all its coordinates lie strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\), so an integral point must be \(0\in A\). At either endpoint \(\lambda=0,1\), an integral point is Boolean or negative Boolean. A Boolean vertex in \(\operatorname{conv}(B)\) must itself belong to \(B\): each of its coordinates forces every point in a convex representation with positive coefficient to have that same coordinate. Thus \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\).

Every nonzero parity class represented in \(A\) consists of the pair \(b,-b\) for a unique \(b\in B\setminus\lbrace0\rbrace\); the zero class consists only of \(0\). Consequently every distinct supported pair with an integral midpoint is such an antipodal pair. Hence
$$
R_0=A,\qquad R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\quad(j\ne0).
\tag{2}
$$
Define, only to express the exact original finite sums,
$$
Z_3=\sum_{x\in A}e^{3\ell\cdot x}.
$$
Affineness gives \(\sqrt{p_xp_{-x}}=1/Z\). Therefore the objects from 445a9e668ee1a705 have exactly the following values, with no change of law or normalizer:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(0)&=\frac hZ,&H_p(j)&=p_j\quad(j\ne0),\\
A_0&=\frac{K^2Z_3}{Z^3},&
K^2b(p)&=\frac{K^2(Z_3-1+h^2)}{Z^3},\\
\kappa_0(x)&=\frac1h\quad(x\in A),&Y_0(x)&=\ell\cdot x,\\
V_0&=\frac2h\sum_{b\in B}(\ell\cdot b)^2,&
q_0&=\frac{h^2}{Z_3-1+h^2},\qquad \nu=q_0V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Every other row score and variance is zero. These are identity maps on all the canonical objects, including the original \(p,Z,H,b,\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu\).

If \(B=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), our \(A\) is exactly the support \(A_n\) of e2506c0626ec02e6, with the same \(n,K,\ell,Z,p\) and all the same full canonical rows and quantities. Thus that accepted theorem proves (1) with the smaller constant \(16384\). Assume henceforth that \(B\) is proper and that \(A_0<1\). Hölder's inequality for the \(h\) original probabilities gives
$$
1<\frac{h^2}{K^2},\qquad h>K,\qquad M>\frac K2.
\tag{4}
$$
Indeed \(\sum_xp_x^3\ge h^{-2}\), whereas \(K^2\sum_xp_x^3<1\).

We prove a bound for the exact original normalizer. For uniform \(b\in B\), set
$$
c_b=\cosh(\ell\cdot b),\qquad
\mu=\mathbb E_Bc_b,\qquad \mathcal V=\operatorname{Var}_B(c_b).
$$
Here \(\mathbb E_B\) means the uniform average over \(B\), not a replacement for the original probability law \(p\). Since \(c_b\ge1\), one has \(\mu\ge1\). The central atom is subtracted only once in the exact identities
$$
Z=2M\mu-1,\qquad
Z_3=8M\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-6M\mu-1.
\tag{5}
$$
The second identity uses \(\cosh(3t)=4\cosh^3t-3\cosh t\). Moreover
$$
\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-\mu^3
=\mathbb E_B\bigl[(c_b-\mu)^2(c_b+2\mu)\bigr]
\ge2\mu\mathcal V.
$$
Substitute this inequality and (5) into the exact active condition \(K^2Z_3<Z^3\), expand, and divide by \(8K^2M\mu>0\). This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\mathcal V+\left(1-\frac{M^2}{K^2}\right)\mu^2
&<\frac34+\frac1{8M\mu}-\frac{3M\mu}{2K^2}
  +\frac3{4K^2}-\frac1{8K^2M\mu}\\
&\le\frac34+\frac18+\frac3{16}
=\frac{17}{16}<2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
We used \(K\ge2\), \(M\mu\ge1\), and dropped only negative terms. Since \(M\le K\), both terms on the left are nonnegative. In particular
$$
\mathcal V<1,\qquad
\left(1-\frac{M^2}{K^2}\right)\mu^2<2.
\tag{7}
$$

We now use the Boolean ambient representation. We claim
$$
\mu\le12n.
\tag{8}
$$
This is immediate if \(\mu<8\). Otherwise let
$$
z=\operatorname{arcosh}\mu,\qquad \delta=\frac4\mu,
\qquad
G=\lbrace b\in B:|c_b-\mu|\le2\rbrace.
$$
By (7), \(M/K>3/4\). Chebyshev's inequality and \(\mathcal V<1\) give
$$
|G|\ge\frac34M>\frac9{16}K>\frac K2.
\tag{9}
$$
For \(u\in[\mu-2,\mu+2]\), with \(\mu\ge8\),
$$
\frac{d}{du}\operatorname{arcosh}u
=\frac1{\sqrt{u^2-1}}\le\frac2\mu.
$$
It follows that every \(b\in G\) satisfies
$$
\bigl||\ell\cdot b|-z\bigr|\le\delta.
\tag{10}
$$
For each coordinate \(i\), the cube involution that flips coordinate \(i\) sends a set of size greater than \(K/2\) to another set of that size. Thus some edge in direction \(i\) has both endpoints in \(G\). Their two linear-form values, each satisfying (10), differ by exactly \(\ell_i\), up to the orientation of the edge. Consequently there is \(d_i\in\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\) such that
$$
|\ell_i-2zd_i|\le2\delta.
\tag{11}
$$
Choose these integers for all coordinates. Fix any \(b\in G\). Then \(k_b=\sum_i d_i b_i\in\mathbb Z\), and (11) gives
$$
|\ell\cdot b-2zk_b|\le2n\delta.
$$
Together with (10), this implies \(|(2k_b-\varepsilon)z|\le(2n+1)\delta\) for some \(\varepsilon\in\lbrace-1,1\rbrace\). The integer \(2k_b-\varepsilon\) is odd and nonzero. Hence
$$
z\le(2n+1)\delta,\qquad
\mu z\le8n+4\le12n.
$$
Since \(z=\operatorname{arcosh}\mu>1\) when \(\mu\ge8\), (8) follows. This argument used intersections of the good set with its coordinate flips; it made no comparison with the normalized full-cube law.

The function \(u\mapsto(\operatorname{arcosh}u)^2\) is increasing and concave for \(u\ge1\), as proved in 445a9e668ee1a705. Applying Jensen to the uniform average over \(B\), and then using (3) and (8), gives
$$
0\le\nu\le V_0
=\frac{2M}{h}\mathbb E_B(\ell\cdot b)^2
\le2(\operatorname{arcosh}\mu)^2
\le2\log^2(2\mu)
\le2\log^2(24n).
\tag{12}
$$
In addition, (4), (5), and (8) imply \(h>K\) and \(Z<24Kn\). Since \(Z_3-1\ge0\), formula (3) therefore gives, with \(g=\log(K^2b(p))\),
$$
g>n\log2-3\log(24n).
\tag{13}
$$

For \(n\ge64\), the right side of (13) is at least \(n\log2/2\). Indeed the difference at \(n=64\) is
$$
32\log2-3\log(1536)=\log(32/27)>0,
$$
and its derivative in real \(n\ge64\) is \(\log2/2-3/n>0\). Also
$$
\log(24n)\le\sqrt n\qquad(n\ge64):
$$
the difference \(\sqrt n-\log(24n)\) is positive at \(64\) and has positive derivative there and thereafter. For an explicit check at \(64\), \(e>8/3\) implies \(e^8>(8/3)^8>1536\). Thus (12)--(13) give
$$
\nu\le2n\le\frac4{\log2}\,g<8g
\qquad(n\ge64).
\tag{14}
$$

It remains to give a uniform positive gap for the finitely many smaller dimensions. We use the canonical quantity \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\) from 445a9e668ee1a705. There are exactly \(M\) represented parity classes. Every same-parity pair has its integer midpoint in \(A\), and global affinity gives equality \(p_{(x+y)/2}=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\). The ordered-pair expansion of \(\chi\) therefore yields
$$
\chi(p)
=\sum_{\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}
 p(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)^2
\ge\frac1M.
$$
Jensen under the entire original law gives \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\). Since the subset is proper, \(M\le K-1\), so
$$
g\ge2\log(K/M)
\ge2\log\frac K{K-1}
\ge\frac2K>0.
\tag{15}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(-\log(1-u)\ge u\) at \(u=1/K\). For \(1\le n\le63\), one has \(\log(24n)<8\), and (12), (15) imply
$$
\frac\nu g\le64K\le2^{69}<2^{70}.
\tag{16}
$$
Equations (14)--(16) prove (1) for every proper subset. The already accepted full-cube bound from e2506c0626ec02e6 completes the stated class. All estimates use \(A_0=K^2\sum p^3<1\) and the original affine normalizer \(Z\); no ratio condition, deletion monotonicity, or general complete-convex-support price has been substituted.

We finish by deriving (17) with the original probabilities and arbitrary ambient halfspaces. This is the generic symmetric-row argument underlying 445a9e668ee1a705 and the active/inactive selection used in e2506c0626ec02e6; the following calculation verifies its applicability to the present subsets directly.

In factor \(i\), put \(K_i=2^{n_i}\) and \(A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\). When \(A_{0,i}<1\), select its full canonical rows \(R_{i,j}\); otherwise select the singleton \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) in each row. Denote the selected rows by \(\widehat R_{i,j}\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}
 \sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
\widehat b_i&=\sum_jp_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=
 \frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_i(j)},&
\widehat q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2}{\widehat b_i},\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},&
\widehat V_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
\widehat\nu_i&=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j},&
g_i&=\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
For an active factor, these hatted objects equal the unhatted canonical ones exactly, and (1) gives \(\widehat\nu_i\le Cg_i\), with \(g_i\ge0\). For an inactive factor, \(\widehat H_i(j)=p_{i,j}\), \(\widehat V_{i,j}=0\), and \(g_i=\log A_{0,i}\ge0\). Thus, with \(S_g=\sum_i g_i\),
$$
S_g\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\widehat\nu_i\le CS_g.
\tag{19}
$$
The selection changes auxiliary rows only. In particular the original probability at \(X=(x_i)\) is still
$$
P_X=\prod_i p_{i,x_i}
=\frac{\prod_i e^{\ell_i\cdot x_i}}{\prod_i Z_i}.
$$

For a supported target \(J=(j_i)\), take the selected product row
$$
\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i},\qquad
\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i),\qquad
\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}.
$$
Every selected row is symmetric about its center. Reflection negates \(Y_{i,j}\) and preserves \(\widehat\kappa_{i,j}\), so its score has mean zero. Therefore, for
$$
Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i),\qquad
\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i},
$$
conditional independence gives
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,\qquad
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}.
\tag{20}
$$
The last identity follows by direct multiplication of (18); it uses the original \(P_X\), including all original normalizers.

Let \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be any closed halfspace containing \(J\). It contains at least one endpoint of each distinct reflected pair \(X,2J-X\), and it contains \(J\). Counting both orientations in the selected row and using only half of the available central mass gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}
 \min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
 \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
The equality uses (20) and reflection symmetry. The last inequality uses Jensen and then Cauchy--Schwarz. This argument applies to every original ambient containing closed halfspace, including when selected rows are singletons.

Take the infimum over \(\mathcal H\), square (21), and average over the entire original \(P\). The auxiliary product center law is \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), and the exact factorization
$$
P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2
=\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}
$$
gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
$$
Here \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\); Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz prove the second inequality. Since \(\prod_iK_i^2=4^N\), (19)--(22) yield
$$
4^NM_2(P)
\ge\frac14\exp\bigl(S_g-2\sqrt{CS_g}\bigr)
\ge\frac14e^{-C}=c_*.
$$
The final inequality is
\(S_g-2\sqrt{CS_g}=(\sqrt{S_g}-\sqrt C)^2-C\ge-C\).
Finally Jensen under the original \(P\) gives
$$
M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2},
$$
which proves the other bound in (17). The already proved lattice-census identity for each factor, together with the identity between the convex hull of a Cartesian product and the product of the convex hulls, verifies the product census assertion. No product law has been conditioned, reweighted, or replaced.
