## Statement

For an integer $d\ge1$, let $F_+$ be the closed tail of the density $d x^{d-1}$ on $[0,1]$ centered at $A=d/(d+1)$, and set $F_-(q)=1-F_+(-q)$. These are the two opposing axial tails of a full $d$-simplex in a fixed facet-normal direction, with its scalar support before centering scaled to length one. For $0<r\le s$, write
$$
E=r^d-\left[\frac{(dr-s)_+}{d+1}\right]^d,\qquad
V_*=2\left(\frac{d}{d+1}\right)^d\left(\frac{r+s}{2}\right)^d.
$$
For the cost $r^dF_\pm(t/r)+s^dF_\pm(-t/s)$ over real $t$, the two respective minima are $E$ and $\min\{r^d,V_*\}$. Thus the minimum over those two orientations and thresholds is $\min\{E,V_*\}$.

## Proof

Sections of a simplex along its height from the opposite vertex to its facet have volumes proportional to $x^{d-1}$ after scaling the height to one. Its normalized density is $d x^{d-1}$. The mean is $A=d/(d+1)$ and the two tails are reflections without atoms. Put $B=1/(d+1)$, so $F_+(q)=1-\operatorname{clip}(A+q,0,1)^d$, where clipping projects onto the indicated interval.

For $d=1$ the distribution is a centered interval. For each threshold pair the smaller scaled interval gives complementary opposite tails of total $r$, and enlarging one of the two intervals can only increase its cap length. A sufficiently negative threshold makes the first cap full and the other empty, attaining $r$. Both claimed formulas give $r$.

Suppose $d\ge2$. Denote the increasing-density cost by $C(t)$. Its breakpoints are $-rA,rB,-sB,sA$, and its far plateaus are $r^d,s^d$. On the common proper interval $[\max(-rA,-sB),rB]$, including its endpoint limits,
$$
C(t)=r^d+s^d-(rA+t)^d-(sA-t)^d.
$$
This expression is concave. Outside the common interval each nonsaturated piece has one proper cap and is monotone. The left junction value is $E$ when $s\le dr$ and at least $r^d=E$ when $s\ge dr$. At $t=-sB$ in the latter case the first cap is full, giving the value $E$. The right junction value is $J=s^d-[(ds-r)/(d+1)]^d$. For $\lambda=s/r$ in $[1,d]$ put $D(\lambda)=(J-E)/r^d$. Then $D(1)=0$, and
$$
\frac{D'(\lambda)}{d}
=\lambda^{d-1}-\frac{d(d\lambda-1)^{d-1}+(d-\lambda)^{d-1}}{(d+1)^d}
\ge \lambda^{d-1}\left[1-\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^{d-1}\right]>0.
$$
Both bases in the numerator are nonnegative and at most $d\lambda$. For $s\ge dr$, $J$ increases in $s$ since its derivative is $d[s^{d-1}-A(sA-rB)^{d-1}]>0$, so $J\ge J(dr)=[d^d-(d-1)^d]r^d>r^d$ for $d\ge2$. Since $E\le r^d\le s^d$, this proves $\min C=E$.

Reflection makes the other orientation's cost $r^d+s^d-C(-t)$. The proper-interval maximum of $C$ can exceed both plateaus only at $t_*=A(s-r)/2$, when the two powered terms are equal. This nonnegative point lies in the proper interval exactly when $s/r\le1+2/d$. Its value is then $r^d+s^d-V_*$. In that case the proper endpoints are at most this value and the one-proper pieces are monotone toward a plateau or a proper endpoint. If $t_*>rB$, $C$ increases on the common proper interval, whose right endpoint is $J\le s^d$. The left endpoint and the one-proper pieces are then also bounded by the larger plateau $s^d$. In the infeasible case $A(r+s)/2>r$, so $V_*>2r^d$. In both cases reflection gives the minimum $\min\{r^d,V_*\}$. Combining the two minima proves the result.
