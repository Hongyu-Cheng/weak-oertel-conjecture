## Statement

The integer \(n\ge1\) denotes the lattice dimension, the integer \(d\ge1\) denotes the continuous fiber dimension, and \(Q\ge1\) denotes rooted-tree depth as in accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. No quadratic form is denoted by \(Q\) here. All logarithms are natural.

Retain the cutoff normalization of accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and d7c8f1d06792b00b. Specifically, for a nonempty finite set \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and a table positive on \(D\) and zero outside, put \(W=\sum_{z\in D}w_z\). At each supported center \(j\in D\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\},
&\mathcal U_j&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v,j-v\in D\},\\
\Delta_j(v)&=\log\frac{w_j^2}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}
\quad(v\in\mathcal U_j),
&B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
\quad(L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\]

The dependence of \(\alpha\) on the fixed center is suppressed. Assume, whenever these quantities are used below, that \(w_j^2\ge w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\) for every \(j\in D\) and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). Thus the curvatures are nonnegative. Displacements are oriented, both signs of a nonzero displacement are counted, and zero is counted once. The maxima are attained at actual curvature values. A tilde on these objects means that they are formed from the corresponding table \(\widetilde w\) with this same normalization.

Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) be a bounded, full-dimensional rational polytope, let \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be an open convex set containing \(P\), and let \(E:U\to\mathbb R\) be finite and convex. For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), there exist a globally real-analytic function \(V:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\), a finite real \(R\), and finite constants \(0<a\le b\) such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
2aI_n&\preceq\nabla^2V(x)\preceq2bI_n
&& (x\in\mathbb R^n),\\
|V(x)-E(x)|&\le\varepsilon,
\qquad V(x)<R
&& (x\in P),\\
V(z)&>R
&& (z\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus P).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

The function \(V\) is coercive, its minimum \(V_{\min}\) is attained, and \(R>V_{\min}\). In particular, with \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), its complete sublevel has exactly the prescribed support:

\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:V(z)\le R\}=D.
\tag{2}
\]

This regularization assertion also allows \(D\) to be empty. For \(D\ne\varnothing\), the table having weight \(e^{-E(z)}\) at each \(z\in D\) and zero outside, and the analogous table formed from \(V\), both satisfy the midpoint condition above. The proof supplies explicit global Hessian bounds in terms of finitely many affine slopes and positive smoothing parameters.

There is a stability assertion independent of the regularization construction. Let \(w,\widetilde w\) be two tables satisfying the stated midpoint condition on the same nonempty finite support \(D\). If, for some \(\varepsilon\ge0\),

\[
e^{-\varepsilon}w_z\le\widetilde w_z\le e^{\varepsilon}w_z
\quad(z\in D),
\tag{3}
\]

then for every \(j\in D\) and every \(L\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde B_j^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)
&\le e^\varepsilon B_j^\Delta(L+4\varepsilon),\\
K_j(\widetilde w)&\le e^\varepsilon(1+4\varepsilon)K_j(w),\\
\frac{2^nK(\widetilde w)}{\widetilde W}
&\le e^{2\varepsilon}(1+4\varepsilon)\frac{2^nK(w)}{W}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\widetilde W=\sum_{z\in D}\widetilde w_z\). The same inequalities hold after interchanging the two tables.

For the sequence \(m_Q=2(Q+1)^4\) and \(n_Q=\sum_{\ell=1}^Q m_Q^\ell\), there exist globally real-analytic potentials \(V_Q:\mathbb R^{n_Q}\to\mathbb R\), levels \(R_Q>V_{Q,\min}\), and finite \(0<a_Q\le b_Q\) satisfying the global Hessian bounds in (1), such that the complete tables

\[
\widetilde w_z^{[Q]}
=e^{-V_Q(z)}\mathbf1_{\{V_Q(z)\le R_Q\}},
\qquad
\widetilde W_Q=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{n_Q}}\widetilde w_z^{[Q]}
\tag{5}
\]

have exactly the rooted-tree support \(A_Q=P_{m_Q,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_Q}\) of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. With \(\varepsilon=1/10\) and \(H_{Q+1}=\sum_{k=0}^Q1/(k+1)\), they satisfy

\[
0<\frac{2^{n_Q}K(\widetilde w^{[Q]})}{\widetilde W_Q}
<\frac{2e^{2\varepsilon}(1+4\varepsilon)}
{H_{Q+1}-1/(Q+1)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]

Hence no absolute \(c>0\) makes \(K(w)\ge c\,2^{-n}W\) valid for all complete finite sublevels of globally real-analytic potentials with \(2aI_n\preceq\nabla^2V\preceq2bI_n\), when \(0<a\le b<\infty\) may depend on the table. Every choice of such comparison constants for the sequence (5) has \(\kappa_Q=b_Q/a_Q\to\infty\).

Every regularized table with \(D\ne\varnothing\) in (1) has the following exact realization, using the realization clause of accepted fact d7c8f1d06792b00b. Set \(\kappa=b/a\), let \(d\) be any positive integer with \(d\ge2\kappa(R-V_{\min})\), and put \(P_R=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:V(x)\le R\}\). Then

\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
x\in P_R,\quad
|y_i|\le\frac12e^{-V(x)/d}\quad(1\le i\le d)\right\}
\tag{7}
\]

is a compact, full-dimensional convex body. For \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), its actual volumes are exactly \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=e^{-V(z)}\mathbf1_D(z)\). All fibers on \(P_R\), including its boundary, are retained with positive volume. In particular, every lattice point on the boundary of the original polytope \(P\) retains its positive fiber.

Write \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_z w_z\), and \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For \(p\in S\), let \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. There is one \(J\in D\), chosen before the halfspace, such that

\[
h_S((J,\mathbf0))\ge\frac12\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently, each table (5) is realized by such a body \(C_Q\) in some finite positive continuous dimension \(d_Q\), and it has one point \((J_Q,\mathbf0)\) satisfying (8) with \(n=n_Q\) and \(W=\widetilde W_Q\), simultaneously with the scalar limit (6).

## Proof

We first construct the regularization. A finite convex function on an open convex set is continuous and has a supporting affine function at every point. The facts needed here follow directly from local convexity and separation. For continuity at \(x_0\in U\), choose \(h>0\) with \(x_0+[-h,h]^n\subset U\). Let \(M_{\mathrm{loc}}\) be the maximum of the finitely many values of \(E\) at the vertices of this cube. Convexity bounds \(E\) by \(M_{\mathrm{loc}}\) throughout the cube, including at \(x_0\). For \(0<\|v\|_\infty\le h\), put \(t=\|v\|_\infty/h\). Convexity on the segments from \(x_0\) to \(x_0\pm v/t\) gives \(E(x_0\pm v)\le E(x_0)+t(M_{\mathrm{loc}}-E(x_0))\). Combining this with \(2E(x_0)\le E(x_0+v)+E(x_0-v)\) yields \(|E(x_0+v)-E(x_0)|\le t(M_{\mathrm{loc}}-E(x_0))\), proving continuity.

The open epigraph \(\{(x,t):x\in U,\ t>E(x)\}\) is open and convex. Finite-dimensional hyperplane separation at its boundary point \((x_0,E(x_0))\) gives a nonzero normal \((p,q)\) such that \(p^{\mathsf T}(x-x_0)+q(t-E(x_0))\ge0\) on that epigraph. Necessarily \(q\ge0\), by letting \(t\) increase. If \(q=0\), the openness of \(U\) around \(x_0\) forces \(p=0\), a contradiction. Thus \(q>0\). Letting \(t\) decrease to \(E(x)\) gives the supporting affine function \(E(x_0)-(p/q)^{\mathsf T}(x-x_0)\), which equals \(E\) at \(x_0\) and lies below it throughout \(U\).

For each \(x_0\in P\), choose such a supporting affine function. Its difference from \(E\) is continuous and vanishes at \(x_0\), so it approximates \(E\) from below to within \(\varepsilon/4\) on a neighborhood of \(x_0\). A finite subcover of \(P\) gives affine functions \(\ell_k(x)=s_k^{\mathsf T}x+c_k\), \(1\le k\le N\), with

\[
F(x):=\max_{1\le k\le N}\ell_k(x),
\qquad
E(x)-\varepsilon/4\le F(x)\le E(x)
\quad(x\in P).
\tag{9}
\]

Here \(N\ge1\), and the same formula defines the continuous convex function \(F\) on all of \(\mathbb R^n\). Choose \(\delta>0\) with \(\delta\max_{x\in P}\|x\|_2^2\le\varepsilon/4\), for example \(\delta=\varepsilon/[4(1+\max_{x\in P}\|x\|_2^2)]\). Define

\[
\widehat V(x)=F(x)+\delta\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad
m_0=\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}\widehat V(x),
\qquad
M_0=\max_{x\in P}\widehat V(x).
\tag{10}
\]

These extrema exist and are finite. Indeed, \(\widehat V\ge\ell_1+\delta\|x\|_2^2\) is coercive, and \(P\) is compact. In particular \(M_0\ge m_0\).

Take a rational halfspace description of \(P\) and multiply each inequality by a positive common denominator. Write the resulting description as

\[
P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:u_i^{\mathsf T}x\le b_i,\ 1\le i\le M\},
\qquad u_i\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad b_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad M\ge1.
\tag{11}
\]

The barrier

\[
\Psi(x)=\max\{0,u_1^{\mathsf T}x-b_1,\ldots,u_M^{\mathsf T}x-b_M\}
\tag{12}
\]

vanishes on \(P\). Every integer point \(z\notin P\) violates at least one inequality in (11), and that positive violation is an integer. Hence \(\Psi(z)\ge1\) for every such \(z\), with no restriction on its distance from \(P\).

Put \(\lambda=M_0-m_0+3\ge3\). Choose positive \(\tau,\xi\) with \(\tau\log N+\lambda\xi\log(M+1)\le\varepsilon/4\). For example, \(\tau=\varepsilon/[8(1+\log N)]\) and \(\xi=\varepsilon/[8\lambda\log(M+1)]\) have this property, including \(N=1\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\tau(x)&=\tau\log\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{\ell_k(x)/\tau},\\
\Psi_\xi(x)&=\xi\log\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{M}
e^{(u_i^{\mathsf T}x-b_i)/\xi}\right),\\
V(x)&=F_\tau(x)+\delta\|x\|_2^2+\lambda\Psi_\xi(x),
\qquad R=M_0+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

For finitely many real numbers, their maximum is at most their log-sum-exp at temperature \(\theta>0\), which is at most their maximum plus \(\theta\) times the logarithm of their number. This follows by factoring out the largest exponential and bounding the remaining sum between one and the number of terms. Thus \(F\le F_\tau\le F+\tau\log N\) and \(\Psi\le\Psi_\xi\le\Psi+\xi\log(M+1)\) everywhere. On \(P\), where \(\Psi=0\), we obtain

\[
\widehat V\le V\le\widehat V+\varepsilon/4,
\qquad
E-\varepsilon/4\le V\le E+\varepsilon/2,
\qquad
V\le M_0+\varepsilon/4<R.
\tag{14}
\]

At every integer point outside \(P\), the global lower bounds give

\[
V(z)\ge\widehat V(z)+\lambda\Psi(z)
\ge m_0+\lambda=M_0+3>R.
\tag{15}
\]

This proves the approximation and the complete support assertion, including every lattice point on \(\partial P\). The coercive lower bound \(V\ge\widehat V\) also shows that \(V\) attains a finite minimum. Since \(P\) is nonempty and \(V<R\) there, \(V_{\min}<R\).

We give an explicit global Hessian estimate. For \(F_\tau\), let \(p_k(x)=e^{\ell_k(x)/\tau}/\sum_{h=1}^N e^{\ell_h(x)/\tau}\) and let \(\overline s(x)=\sum_{k=1}^N p_k(x)s_k\). Differentiating the finite sum gives

\[
\nabla^2F_\tau(x)
=\frac1\tau\left(\sum_{k=1}^Np_k(x)s_ks_k^{\mathsf T}
-\overline s(x)\overline s(x)^{\mathsf T}\right).
\tag{16}
\]

For every real vector \(h\), its quadratic form is the variance of the finitely many numbers \(h^{\mathsf T}s_k\), divided by \(\tau\). In particular,

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le h^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2F_\tau(x)h
&=\frac1\tau\left(\sum_kp_k(x)(h^{\mathsf T}s_k)^2
-(h^{\mathsf T}\overline s(x))^2\right)\\
&\le\frac{\max_k\|s_k\|_2^2}{\tau}\|h\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The identical calculation for \(\Psi_\xi\) uses the slopes \(0,u_1,\ldots,u_M\) and gives \(0\preceq\nabla^2\Psi_\xi(x)\preceq[\max_i\|u_i\|_2^2/\xi]I_n\). Consequently, the explicit finite constants

\[
a=\delta,
\qquad
b=\delta+\frac{\max_k\|s_k\|_2^2}{2\tau}
+\frac{\lambda\max_i\|u_i\|_2^2}{2\xi}
\tag{18}
\]

satisfy \(0<a\le b\) and \(2aI_n\preceq\nabla^2V(x)\preceq2bI_n\) for every real \(x\). Each exponential sum in (13) is strictly positive and real analytic everywhere. The real logarithm is analytic on the positive reals, so \(V\) is globally real analytic. The positive quadratic term gives the asserted strong convexity. This completes (1) and (2).

We next prove stability. At every \(z\in D\), put \(\eta_z=\log(\widetilde w_z/w_z)\), so \(|\eta_z|\le\varepsilon\). Equality of supports gives equality of the two displacement domains at every supported center. For every \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), taking the minimum of the endpoint bounds in (3) and subtracting the two logarithmic curvatures gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\varepsilon}\alpha(v)
&\le\widetilde\alpha(v)\le e^\varepsilon\alpha(v),\\
\widetilde\Delta_j(v)-\Delta_j(v)
&=2\eta_j-\eta_{j+v}-\eta_{j-v},
\qquad
|\widetilde\Delta_j(v)-\Delta_j(v)|\le4\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus \(\widetilde\Delta_j(v)\le L\) implies \(\Delta_j(v)\le L+4\varepsilon\). Summing nonnegative weights over that inclusion proves the first inequality in (4) for each \(j,L\), including equality at a cutoff. Dividing it by \(1+L\) gives

\[
\frac{\widetilde B_j^{\widetilde\Delta}(L)}{1+L}
\le e^\varepsilon\frac{1+L+4\varepsilon}{1+L}K_j(w)
\le e^\varepsilon(1+4\varepsilon)K_j(w).
\tag{20}
\]

Taking the maximum over \(L\) and then \(j\) is therefore valid without choosing a center or cutoff in advance. Also \(\widetilde W\ge e^{-\varepsilon}W>0\), so (20) gives the normalized inequality in (4). Interchanging \(w,\widetilde w\) preserves hypothesis (3), proving the reverse comparisons. To justify every maximum used here, at each center the finite curvature list contains \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). For any \(L\ge0\), the largest actual curvature \(t\le L\) has \(B_j^\Delta(t)=B_j^\Delta(L)\) and \(1+t\le1+L\). The maximum over cutoffs is attained on this finite list; the finite center set then gives attainment of \(K\). The same reasoning applies to the other table. Zero has weight \(\alpha(0)=w_j>0\), so \(K(w)>0\).

Apply the construction to the rooted-tree family. Use the exact notation of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8: \(P_Q:=P_{m_Q,Q}\), \(A_Q=P_Q\cap\mathbb Z^{n_Q}\), and \(w^{[Q]}\) and \(W_Q\) for its original table and total weight. The predecessor proves that \(P_Q\subseteq[0,2]^{n_Q}\) is full-dimensional and is the convex hull of integer points, hence is a rational polytope. Its coordinate indices are the tree words \(u\in\mathcal T_{m_Q,Q}\), with depths \(1\le|u|\le Q\), and its positive concave function is

\[
\rho_Q(x)=\min\left\{1,\ \min_{u\in\mathcal T_{m_Q,Q}}
\left[1-\frac{|u|}{|u|+1}(x_u-1)\right]\right\}
\quad(x\in P_Q).
\tag{21}
\]

Every affine expression in (21) is at least \(1/(Q+1)\) on \(P_Q\). Let \(U_Q\) be the set on which all those affine expressions are strictly positive. This is a finite intersection of open affine halfspaces, hence is open and convex, and it contains \(P_Q\). Extend \(\rho_Q\) to \(U_Q\) by the same formula. It is positive there, and it is concave because a minimum of affine functions is concave. Therefore \(E_Q=-\log\rho_Q\) is finite and convex on \(U_Q\): for \(x,x'\in U_Q\) and \(0\le t\le1\), positivity, the decrease of \(-\log\), and its convexity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\log\rho_Q((1-t)x+tx')
&\le-\log\bigl((1-t)\rho_Q(x)+t\rho_Q(x')\bigr)\\
&\le(1-t)(-\log\rho_Q(x))+t(-\log\rho_Q(x')).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

This supplies an open convex neighborhood before regularization is invoked. On every \(z\in A_Q\), \(e^{-E_Q(z)}=\rho_Q(z)=w_z^{[Q]}\). Apply (1) with \(P=P_Q\), \(E=E_Q\), and \(\varepsilon=1/10\), obtaining \(V_Q,R_Q,a_Q,b_Q\). Its strict inside and outside inequalities show that the complete sublevel table is exactly (5), supported on \(A_Q\), and (3) holds between \(w^{[Q]}\) and \(\widetilde w^{[Q]}\). Both curvature arrays are nonnegative by convexity of their respective potentials at supported midpoints. The predecessor supplies

\[
K(w^{[Q]})\le2,
\qquad
\frac{W_Q}{2^{n_Q}}>H_{Q+1}-\frac1{Q+1}.
\tag{23}
\]

Combining (23) with (4) proves the strict upper bound in (6). Its right-hand side tends to zero since \(H_{Q+1}\ge\int_1^{Q+2}dt/t=\log(Q+2)\). Positivity follows from the zero displacement. The normalized limit contradicts every proposed absolute positive coefficient for this class of complete sublevels.

For the assertion about Hessian ratios, take any constants \(0<a_Q\le b_Q<\infty\) satisfying the displayed global bounds for these same \(V_Q\). The accepted all-radius theorem d7c8f1d06792b00b, with its positive-definite comparison matrix \(A=a_QI_{n_Q}\) and ratio \(\kappa_Q=b_Q/a_Q\), applies to the exact complete support at \(R_Q\). It gives

\[
\frac{2^{n_Q}K(\widetilde w^{[Q]})}{\widetilde W_Q}
\ge\frac1{100\kappa_Q}.
\tag{24}
\]

Since the left-hand side is positive and tends to zero, (24) forces \(\kappa_Q\to\infty\). This uses the already accepted estimate and does not establish that its dependence on \(\kappa_Q\) is optimal.

It remains to verify the realization and the positive depth claim. Let \(V,R,a,b\) be produced by (1), with \(D\ne\varnothing\). In the realization clause of accepted fact d7c8f1d06792b00b, substitute \(aI_n\) for its matrix \(A\), take \(\kappa=b/a\), and use this unchanged potential \(V\) and level \(R\). All hypotheses hold: \(V\) is \(C^2\), its global Hessian lies between \(2aI_n\) and \(2bI_n\), its complete lattice sublevel is nonempty, and \(R>V_{\min}\). That clause proves that \(e^{-V/d}\) is concave on \(P_R\) for every integer \(d\ge1\) with \(d\ge2\kappa(R-V_{\min})\), and that (7) is a compact, full-dimensional convex body. Such a finite integer \(d\) exists because all the parameters are finite.

At every real \(x\in P_R\), the box fiber in (7) has side length \(e^{-V(x)/d}>0\) in all \(d\) directions, so its volume is exactly \(e^{-V(x)}\). At \(V(x)=R\) this volume is \(e^{-R}>0\). Outside \(P_R\) the fiber is empty. Equality (2) therefore proves the exact table at every integer point, and (14) preserves all the original boundary lattice points as well. The potential has not been shifted by \(V_{\min}\), so this is the original weight normalization. Compactness gives finitely many integer fibers, and consequently \(\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_z w_z=W>0\).

The weighted integer-centerpoint argument proved in accepted fact bb2b5f9519ae23e8 applies to these actual positive fiber weights. It supplies one \(J\in D\) for which every proper closed base halfspace containing \(J\) has weight at least \(2^{-n}W\). In that predecessor the chosen point is first in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\); membership in \(D\) also follows here directly from \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq P_R\) and \(D=P_R\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Fix \(J\) now. The point \((J,\mathbf0)\) belongs to \(S\).

Let \(H\) be any proper closed ambient halfspace containing this point. For some real vectors \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), not both zero, and some \(t\le0\), write \(H=\{(x,y):u^{\mathsf T}(x-J)+v^{\mathsf T}y\ge t\}\). It contains the through-point halfspace \(H_0\) obtained by replacing \(t\) with zero. Put \(G=\{x:u^{\mathsf T}(x-J)\ge0\}\). If \(u\ne0\), the centerpoint property gives \(\sum_{z\in D\cap G}w_z\ge2^{-n}W\); if \(u=0\), then \(G=\mathbb R^n\) and the same bound follows from its full mass \(W\).

For each \(z\in D\cap G\), the fiber section of \(H_0\) contains \(\{y:v^{\mathsf T}y\ge0\}\). The cube \(C_z\) is centrally symmetric about zero. If \(v\ne0\), reflection \(y\mapsto-y\) preserves its volume and interchanges its intersections with the two closed halfspaces \(v^{\mathsf T}y\ge0\) and \(v^{\mathsf T}y\le0\). Those two intersections cover \(C_z\), so each has volume at least \(w_z/2\). If \(v=0\), the whole fiber is retained, and the same lower bound holds. Therefore

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12\sum_{z\in D\cap G}w_z
\ge\frac12\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{25}
\]

The full ambient space, if allowed as a halfspace, has mass \(W\) and satisfies the same bound. All inequalities defining the sections were weak, so vertical halfspaces and boundaries carrying entire positive fibers are included. The point \(J\) was fixed before any halfspace was chosen. Taking the infimum proves (8), and applying this to each \(V_Q,R_Q\) proves the simultaneous realization and depth assertions.
