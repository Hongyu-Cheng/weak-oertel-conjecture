## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $m=d+1$, $r=d/m$, $\kappa=(1-r^d)^{1/d}$, $\psi(q)=\min\{1,\max\{0,1-q\}\}$ and $\phi(q)=(1-\psi(q)^d)^{1/d}$.

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional simplex of centroid zero and volume $V>0$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i=1/m+\ell_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. The convex hull of the lifts of $A=aT+\delta$ at $-1$ and $D=bT-\delta$ at $1$, with $a,b>0$, has actual midpoint fiber $K=((a+b)/2)T$. Take ambient halfspaces $H_i=\{(z,x):\ell_i(x)+\beta_i z\ge0\}$, with $B=\sum_i\beta_i$. Their endpoint barycentric cutoffs on the reference $T$ are
$$
q_i^A=1/m-\ell_i(\delta)/a+\beta_i/a,\qquad
q_i^D=1/m+\ell_i(\delta)/b-\beta_i/b.
$$
They satisfy
$$
\sum_iq_i^A=1+B/a,\qquad\sum_iq_i^D=1-B/b,\qquad
a q_i^A+b q_i^D=(a+b)/m.
$$
The endpoint capture fractions are $\psi(q)^d$. The actual volume roots of the residuals are $aV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^A)$ and $bV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^D)$. The closed residual is empty for $q<0$ and tangent for $q=0$, while the capture is tangent for $q=1$ and empty for $q>1$. For $0<q<1$ both have positive volume.

Define the normalized residual-root overshoots
$$
\Delta_i=\frac{a\phi(q_i^A)+b\phi(q_i^D)-\kappa(a+b)}2,
\qquad \widehat\Delta_i=V^{1/d}\Delta_i,
$$
where $\widehat\Delta_i$ is the actual overshoot in volume-root units.

If $B=0$, then
$$
\sum_i\Delta_i=-[aJ(q^A)+bJ(q^D)]/2\le0,
\qquad
\sum_i(\Delta_i)_+\le\frac d2[\max(a,b)-\kappa(a+b)]_+,
$$
with $J(q)=m\kappa-\sum_i\phi(q_i)$ for sum-one vectors. The first inequality is an equality for $d\ge2$ exactly when $\ell_i(\delta)=\beta_i$ for all $i$, and always for $d=1$. If $b>\kappa(a+b)$, then with $C_A=\sum_i|A\cap(H_i)_{-1}|$ the positive-part bound improves to
$$
\frac{b-\kappa(a+b)}2\min\{d,C_A/(a^dV)\}.
$$
The symmetric improvement holds if $a>\kappa(a+b)$. Both bounds for the positive part are attained, with effective shifts $\ell_i(\delta)-\beta_i=(br,\ldots,br,-dbr)_i$ when $b$ dominates.

## Proof

The endpoint identities give both sums equal to one. The middle root in each canonical direction is $V^{1/d}\kappa(a+b)/2$, and the definition of $J$ gives the exact signed-sum formula. The envelope lemma proves its sign and its equality cases.

When both thresholds for a direction are nonnegative, concavity of $\phi$ on the nonnegative half-line and the paired weighted mean $1/m$ give $a\phi(q_i^A)+b\phi(q_i^D)\le\kappa(a+b)$. Hence a positive overshoot requires a negative threshold. Since $\kappa\ge1/2$, at most one endpoint scale exceeds $\kappa(a+b)$, and if neither does no positive overshoot is possible. Suppose the larger one is $b>\kappa(a+b)$. For a positive overshoot the negative threshold must be $q_i^A$, since otherwise $\phi(q_i^D)=0$ and the remaining term is at most $a\le\kappa(a+b)$. Its excess is at most $(b-\kappa(a+b))/2$. There are at most $d$ such thresholds since $q^A$ sums to one. Each such direction fully captures $A$, of volume $a^dV$, so their number is also at most $C_A/(a^dV)$. Interchanging endpoints treats the other dominance case.

For attainment let the effective shifts be $t_i=br$ for the first $d$ indices and $t_m=-dbr$. These shifts sum to zero and the barycentric linear map realizes them by one translation with $\beta_i=0$. Since $b>\kappa(a+b)$ implies $b>a$ and thus $db>a$, the first $d$ thresholds have $q_i^A<0$ and $q_i^D=1$, while the last has $q_m^A>1$ and $q_m^D=1-d\le0$. The endpoint residual-root vectors in units of $V^{1/d}$ are $(0,\ldots,0,a)$ and $(b,\ldots,b,0)$, and $C_A=d a^d V$. These give equality in both positive-part bounds. The last signed overshoot is $(a-\kappa(a+b))/2$, and the signed sum is $-[(m\kappa-1)a+(m\kappa-d)b]/2$. If neither endpoint dominates, zero effective shifts attain the zero bound.
