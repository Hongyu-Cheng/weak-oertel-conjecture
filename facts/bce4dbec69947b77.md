## Statement

Let \(r,s,h_x,h_y\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(n=r+s\), and retain the geometric objects of accepted fact `105a0951ae3ea2b6`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,h_x]^r\cup\{-\mathbf1_r\}\bigr),
&Q&=[-h_y,h_y]^s,\\
P&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((R\times\{\mathbf0\})\cup(\{\mathbf0\}\times Q)\bigr),
&D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Write \(\mathbf I=(x,y)\), with first integer block \(x\in\mathbb Z^r\) and second integer block \(y\in\mathbb Z^s\). Let \(B\in\mathbb R^{s\times r}\) be arbitrary, let \(V:R\to\mathbb R\) be finite and convex with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\), and let \(g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, convex, and nondecreasing with \(g(0)=0\). Define the original potential and complete table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi(x,y)&=V(x)+g(\|y-Bx\|_\infty)\quad((x,y)\in P),
&\phi_{\mathbf I}&=\phi(x,y),\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
&w_{\mathbf I}&=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),
&W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Assume that the original weighted mean is \(\mathbf J_0=W^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). This hypothesis uses exactly the support and weights in (1)-(2).

At target \(\mathbf0\), use the finite family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`: its members are nonempty affinely independent subsets \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), with their unique positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). For \(L\ge0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing
\text{ for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\text{ with }G_E\le L\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Each selected source is charged its full original weight. The same accepted fact identifies this quantity with hitting all arbitrary finite positive barycentric supports of gap at most \(L\). All support and cutoff boundary inequalities here are weak inequalities.

The table in (2) is globally extendable in the sense of `488f0a583886b5fc`, \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), \(\dim P=n\), \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\). Its exact complete support is

\[

\cup\left\{(x,y):
x\in\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r,\quad y\in\mathbb Z^s,\quad
\frac{\|x\|_\infty}{h_x}+\frac{\|y\|_\infty}{h_y}\le1

\tag{4}
\]

Use the notation of accepted fact `f9b3c1c36d1df760` with its \(h=h_y\), its \(s=s\), and its \(g=g\):

\[
\begin{aligned}

For the conditional actual-body conclusion, let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{z\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,z)\in C\}\). Suppose that, for some \(\alpha>0\), its complete volume table satisfies

Thus (7) includes all zero-volume fibers. Put \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(W_C=\mathcal H_d(S)=\alpha W\). There is one \(z^\star\in C_{\mathbf0}\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf0,z^\star)\) satisfies

\tag{8}

For explicit actual realizations of approximating tables, set \(w_{\max}=e^{-\min_D\phi}\), \(\psi_{\mathbf I}=\phi_{\mathbf I}-\min_D\phi\), and \(\beta=\max_D\psi\). Define

\[
\widehat\psi(\xi)=\min\left\{
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}:

\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\xi

\tag{9}
\]

For every integer \(q>\beta\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
C^{(q)}&=\left\{(\xi,z)\in P\times\mathbb R^q:
0\le z_j\le w_{\max}^{1/q}(1-\widehat\psi(\xi)/q)
\quad(1\le j\le q)\right\},\\
w^{(q)}_{\mathbf I}&=
\begin{cases}
w_{\max}(1-\psi_{\mathbf I}/q)^q,&\mathbf I\in D,\\
0,&\mathbf I\notin D,
\end{cases}
&W^{(q)}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w^{(q)}_{\mathbf I},\\
\kappa_q&=1-\beta/q,
&\varepsilon_q&=-q\log(1-\beta/q)-\beta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The set \(C^{(q)}\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope in \(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^q\), and \(w^{(q)}\) is exactly its complete integer-fiber volume table. Every integer fiber outside \(D\) is empty, and every integer fiber in \(D\), including boundary sites, is a positive cube. The integer dimension and positive support remain exactly \(n\) and \(D\). Moreover,

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le\varepsilon_q\le\frac{\beta^2}{2(q-\beta)},
&e^{-\varepsilon_q}w_{\mathbf I}&\le w^{(q)}_{\mathbf I}\le w_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n),\\
e^{-\varepsilon_q}W&\le W^{(q)}\le W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Put \(\Lambda^{(q)}=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^q\) and \(S^{(q)}=C^{(q)}\cap\Lambda^{(q)}\). There is one \(z_q^\star\in C^{(q)}_{\mathbf0}\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf0,z_q^\star)\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_q(S^{(q)}\cap H)
\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}
\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\frac{W^{(q)}}{216\,2^n}.
\tag{12}
\]

The multiplier before \(W^{(q)}/(216\,2^n)\) tends to one as \(q\to\infty\). No mean-zero condition on \(w^{(q)}\) is required; the target in (12) is the original integer point \(\mathbf0\).

For an exact stationary quadratic example, take \(r=2\), \(s=1\), \(B=(\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)\), \(g(t)=t^2\), and any integers \(h_x,h_y\ge2\). Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac1{2h_x(h_x+1)^2(2h_y+1)},\\
c_-&=\sum_{\substack{(x,y)\in D\\x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2}}
x_1u^{x_1+x_2}e^{-(y-\sqrt2(x_1-x_2))^2},
&\delta&=-\log(c_-u^2),\\
V(x)&=-(\log u)(x_1+x_2)
+\delta\max\{0,-x_1,-x_2\}\quad(x\in R).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

These formulas give \(0<c_-\le1/2\), \(\delta>0\), and a finite convex \(V\) with \(V(\mathbf0)=0\). The resulting original table has exactly \(\mathbf J_0=\mathbf0\), \(w_{\max}=1\), and \(\psi=\phi\) on \(D\). Its potential values on \(D\) cannot be written as \(U(x)+T(y)\). For this example there is no convex body in any finite continuous dimension whose complete integer-fiber volume table is proportional to the original \(w\). Nevertheless (10) gives actual convex bodies for every integer \(q>\beta\), and (12) applies. In each such finite-dimensional table \(w^{(q)}\), the first two coordinate moments are equal and strictly negative, and the third coordinate moment is zero. Thus these actual tables do not have mean zero.

## Proof

The support geometry is unchanged from accepted fact `105a0951ae3ea2b6` and is independent of the radial cost. We check it directly. A point of \(R\) has the form \(t v-(1-t)\mathbf1_r\), where \(0\le t\le1\) and \(v\in[0,h_x]^r\). Every coordinate is at least \(t-1\ge-1\) and at most \((h_x+1)t-1\le h_x\). If an integer coordinate is negative, it is \(-1\), and the first bound forces \(t=0\). Thus the lattice points of \(R\) are precisely \(-\mathbf1_r\) and \(\{0,\ldots,h_x\}^r\).

A point of \(P\) can be written as \((x,y)=(\lambda v,(1-\lambda)v')\), with \(v\in R\), \(v'\in Q\), and \(0\le\lambda\le1\). Its first projection lies in \(R\), because \(\mathbf0\in R\). If that projection is the integer point \(-\mathbf1_r\), then \(x_i\ge-\lambda\) forces \(\lambda=1\), so \(y=\mathbf0\). Otherwise an integer first projection is nonnegative, and the coordinate bounds give \(\|x\|_\infty/h_x\le\lambda\) and \(\|y\|_\infty/h_y\le1-\lambda\). Conversely, for a nonnegative integer pair satisfying (4), take \(\lambda=\|x\|_\infty/h_x\). When \(0<\lambda<1\), the points \(x/\lambda\in[0,h_x]^r\) and \(y/(1-\lambda)\in Q\) give a representation in \(P\). At \(\lambda=0\) one has \(x=\mathbf0\); at \(\lambda=1\) the inequality forces \(y=\mathbf0\). This proves (4), including all boundaries.

All integer vertices generating \(P\) belong to \(D\), so \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). The origin is the barycenter with coefficients \(1/(r+1)\) of the affinely independent points \(-\mathbf1_r,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\) in \(R\). Therefore it is interior to \(R\). It is also interior to \(Q\), and \((R/2)\times(Q/2)\subseteq P\). Consequently \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\) and \(\dim P=n\).

The function \((x,y)\mapsto g(\|y-Bx\|_\infty)\) is convex. Indeed, the norm of a convex combination is at most the corresponding convex combination of norms; monotonicity of \(g\) permits that comparison inside \(g\), and convexity of \(g\) then bounds it by the convex combination of the two costs. Adding the convex function \(V(x)\) proves convexity of \(\phi\) on \(P\). This uses a real linear map and places no arithmetic condition on \(B\). The function \(f=e^{-\phi}\) on \(P\), extended by zero outside \(P\), is finite-valued and log-concave. When both endpoints are in \(P\), this follows from convexity of \(P\) and \(\phi\); when an endpoint is outside, the right side of the extension inequality is zero. Since \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), the extension agrees with the entire table, including every zero. It proves global extendability, convex-admissibility of the normalized \(\psi\), and \(G_E\ge0\). The identities \(V(\mathbf0)=g(0)=0\) give \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(w_{\mathbf0}=1\).

We now prove the mass comparison for the original nonlinear table. Accepted fact `f9b3c1c36d1df760`, with the map in (5), proves \(M\ge1\), the stated cutoff attainment, and

\[
Z_g(v)\le9\,2^s M\quad\text{for every }v\in\mathbb R^s.
\tag{14}
\]

Retain the auxiliary first-block law \(\rho_x=2^{-(r+1)}2^{-(x_1+\cdots+x_r)}\) on \(\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r\) from `105a0951ae3ea2b6`. Its mass at \(-\mathbf1_r\) is \(1/2\), and its total mass on the nonnegative orthant is \(2^{-(r+1)}(\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j})^r=1/2\). Hence \(\sum_x\rho_x=1\). Use \(\pi\) for the present auxiliary comparison numbers, keeping \(q\) reserved for the constructed continuous dimension:

\[
\pi_{(x,y)}=
\frac{\rho_x e^{-g(\|y-Bx\|_\infty)}}{9\,2^s M}
\quad((x,y)\in D).
\tag{15}
\]

Every original first projection is in the domain of \(\rho\), and every second projection is in \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\). Applying (14) with the exact real shift \(v=Bx\), including at the negative apex, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
0<\sum_{(x,y)\in D}\pi_{(x,y)}
&\le\sum_{x\in\{-\mathbf1_r\}\cup\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}
\rho_x\frac{\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-g(\|y-Bx\|_\infty)}}{9\,2^s M}\\
&\le\sum_x\rho_x=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Only this auxiliary sum has been extended. The original support, mean, and hitting capacities remain (1)-(2).

Let \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) be the original probability law. Jensen's inequality for the logarithm gives \(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf I}\log(\pi_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\le\log\sum_D\pi_{\mathbf I}\le0\). Substitution cancels the entire nonlinear coupled cost exactly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\log\frac{p_{(x,y)}}{\pi_{(x,y)}}\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W
 +(r+1)\log2+\log(9\,2^s M)
 +(\log2)\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\\
&=-\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)-\log W+\log(18\,2^n M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The last equality uses the first-block coordinates of the stipulated original mean, with \(n=r+s\). At that same law, convexity of \(V\) gives \(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}V(x)\ge V(\sum_Dp_{(x,y)}x)=V(\mathbf0)=0\). Thus (17) proves \(W\le18\,2^nM\). Neither a shifted lattice nor the auxiliary capacities \(\pi\) have entered this mean calculation or the hitting problem.

At first block zero, the complete table is exactly \(w_{(\mathbf0,y)}=e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)}\). The singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) is eligible at every cutoff and forces the payment of its full weight one. For each nonzero \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), the unordered pair \(\{(\mathbf0,y),(\mathbf0,-y)\}\) is affinely independent, has coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), and has gap \(g(\|y\|_\infty)\). Its endpoints have equal original weights. These unordered pairs are disjoint from each other and from the singleton. Every set hitting the full eligible family must therefore pay

\[
1+\frac12\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s,\ y\ne\mathbf0\\g(\|y\|_\infty)\le L}}
e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)}
=\frac{Z_g(0;L)+1}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]

The factor \(1/2\) groups equal-weight endpoints into unordered pairs. Each selected endpoint still costs its full original weight; its charge has not been multiplied by its barycentric coefficient. This proves the second line of (6).

Choose \(L_\star\in\{g(0),\ldots,g(h_y)\}\) attaining \(M\), as supplied by `f9b3c1c36d1df760`. Then (18) and (17) imply

\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L_\star;w)}{1+L_\star}
\ge\frac{Z_g(0;L_\star)}{2(1+L_\star)}
=\frac M2\ge\frac{W}{36\,2^n}.
\tag{19}
\]

Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` applies to the globally extendable table already verified and supplies attainment of the logarithmic cutoff maximum and equality of the independent-support and arbitrary-support definitions. This proves (6). When \(g\) vanishes identically, or has plateaus, the same argument uses its attained weak cutoff without deleting any zero-cost pair.

For the conditional actual conclusion, suppose that \(C\) satisfies (7). Its positive support is exactly \(D\), its target fiber has volume \(\alpha>0\), and \(W_C=\alpha W\). Its normalized logarithmic potential is
\(\log(w^C_{\max}/w^C_{\mathbf I})=\log(\alpha w_{\max}/(\alpha w_{\mathbf I}))=\psi_{\mathbf I}\).
Thus the logarithmic support costs and eligible families are identical, while the original capacity of each source is multiplied by \(\alpha\). Consequently

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)=\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
\quad(L\ge0).
\tag{20}
\]

For \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\), its actual root cost in continuous dimension \(d\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_E^C
&=\log\frac{w^C_{\mathbf0}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}(w^C_{\mathbf I})^{1/d}\right)^d}\\
&=-d\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}/d}\right)
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}=G_E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

The scale \(\alpha\) cancels exactly; the inequality is weighted arithmetic-geometric mean. These actual root costs are nonnegative by `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. Every logarithmically eligible support is root eligible at the same target and cutoff. Hence every set hitting the root family hits the logarithmic family, with the same actual capacities \(w^C\), and
\(m_d(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\ge m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w^C)\).
This is also the pointwise actual-table comparison of `488f0a583886b5fc`.

Apply the original-fiber inequality (4) of `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` to this actual \(C\), target \(\mathbf0\), and cutoff \(L_\star\). Equations (19)-(21) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}

&\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf0,L_\star;w^C)}{6(1+L_\star)}\\
&\ge\frac{\alpha m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L_\star;w)}{6(1+L_\star)}
\ge\frac{W_C}{216\,2^n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

The same accepted fact proves that this fiberwise maximum is attained. Choose an attaining \(z^\star\) before the halfspace. The definition of \(h_S\) then gives (8) for every containing closed ambient halfspace, including vertical halfspaces and all boundary mass.

We next verify the actual roof construction and its fixed-center transfer. Apply `488f0a583886b5fc` with normalized potential \(\psi\), scalar amplitude \(\beta\), and the remaining map stated above. The roof (9) is attained, continuous on all of \(P\), convex, piecewise affine, and equal to \(\psi\) on \(D\). The equality at a lattice site also follows directly: convex-admissibility bounds every representing objective below by the prescribed value, and the singleton achieves it. Its range is contained in \([0,\beta]\). Thus the common upper side length in (10) is positive, continuous, concave, and piecewise affine, with lower bound \(w_{\max}^{1/q}\kappa_q>0\).

Since \(P\) is full dimensional and compact, these observations prove that \(C^{(q)}\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope. For example, the product of an open subset of \(P\) with \((0,w_{\max}^{1/q}\kappa_q)^q\) is contained in it. At every integer \(\mathbf I\in D\), its fiber is precisely the cube \([0,w_{\max}^{1/q}(1-\psi_{\mathbf I}/q)]^q\), of volume \(w^{(q)}_{\mathbf I}\). At every other integer index the fiber is empty, since \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). There is no transverse thickening in this full-rank case, no change of lattice, and no determinant factor in the fiber volumes.

For \(0\le t\le\beta<q\), put \(b_q(t)=-q\log(1-t/q)-t\), the scalar distortion function used in the proof of `488f0a583886b5fc`. Directly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_q'(t)&=\frac{t}{q-t}\ge0,
&b_q(t)&=\int_0^t\frac{v}{q-v}\,dv,\\
0\le b_q(t)&\le b_q(\beta)=\varepsilon_q
\le\frac{\beta^2}{2(q-\beta)},
&w^{(q)}_{\mathbf I}&=w_{\mathbf I}e^{-b_q(\psi_{\mathbf I})}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D).
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

This proves all capacity and total-weight bounds (11), including the common zeros outside \(D\).

For \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf0}\), write \(\overline\psi_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}\). Convex-admissibility gives \(0\le\psi_{\mathbf0}\le\overline\psi_E\le\beta\) and \(G_E=\overline\psi_E-\psi_{\mathbf0}\). Because the actual volume roots in dimension \(q\) are affine functions of these potential values, their actual root cost is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_E^{(q)}
&=q\log\frac{q-\psi_{\mathbf0}}{q-\overline\psi_E}\\
&=G_E+b_q(\overline\psi_E)-b_q(\psi_{\mathbf0})
=\int_{\psi_{\mathbf0}}^{\overline\psi_E}\frac{q}{q-t}\,dt,\\
G_E&\le L_E^{(q)}\le\min\{G_E+\varepsilon_q,G_E/\kappa_q\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The first upper bound uses the range and monotonicity of \(b_q\); the second uses \(q/(q-t)\le1/\kappa_q\) on \([0,\beta]\). In particular, zero-cost supports remain at cost zero. These formulas are the pointwise component comparison of `488f0a583886b5fc`, here checked in the actual constructed continuous dimension and at the unchanged target.

Let \(t_q(L)=\min\{L+\varepsilon_q,L/\kappa_q\}\). Equation (24) implies that the original logarithmic family at cutoff \(L\) is contained in the actual root family at cutoff \(t_q(L)\). Any set hitting that root family consequently hits the logarithmic family. Its full new weight is at least \(e^{-\varepsilon_q}\) times its full original scalar weight, by (23). Taking minima gives

\[
m_q(\mathbf0,t_q(L);w^{(q)})
\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
\quad(L\ge0).
\tag{25}
\]

This preserves the integer center and specifies the exact comparison of source capacities. It imposes no condition on the mean of \(w^{(q)}\).

Since \(0<\kappa_q\le1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
1+t_q(L)&\le(1+\varepsilon_q)(1+L),
&1+t_q(L)&\le\frac{1+L}{\kappa_q},\\
\frac{1+L}{1+t_q(L)}
&\ge\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Apply `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` to the actual body \(C^{(q)}\), with continuous dimension \(q\), target \(\mathbf0\), and cutoff \(t_q(L_\star)\). By (19), (25), (26), and \(W^{(q)}\le W\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{z\in C^{(q)}_{\mathbf0}}h_{S^{(q)}}((\mathbf0,z))
&\ge\frac{m_q(\mathbf0,t_q(L_\star);w^{(q)})}{6(1+t_q(L_\star))}\\
&\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}
\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L_\star;w)}{6(1+L_\star)}\\
&\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}
\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\frac{W}{216\,2^n}\\
&\ge e^{-\varepsilon_q}
\max\left\{\kappa_q,\frac1{1+\varepsilon_q}\right\}
\frac{W^{(q)}}{216\,2^n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
