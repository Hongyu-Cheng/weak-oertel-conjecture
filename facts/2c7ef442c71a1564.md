## Statement

Retain one common actual canonical hard instance and the common notation of accepted facts 4aa2d3090ea613b8, 6b09aae189415c70, 8aac56c49321466c, b5739a77433de383, d5d5de35c3f25af9, and f83d08a46c19ca2f. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body, and
\[
 C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\tag{1}
\]
\[
 I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in I_+}a_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(Q>0\) be the common half-strength canonical product-reservoir mass and let
\[
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
\tag{3}
\]
be the global complete metronoid-core capacity. Retain the hard inequality
\[
 G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{4}
\]

Assume that the larger provenance color in the retained-mass alternative of accepted fact f83d08a46c19ca2f is the off-diagonal color, and let
\[
 q^*=(q_e^*)
\tag{5}
\]
be its nonzero balanced optimizer. Thus every positive edge is an unordered off-diagonal pair \(e=\{r,s\}\), its physical target is
\[
 m_e:=\frac{r+s}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 0\le q_e^*\le\bar q_e^{\ne}\le q_e^{\ne}.
\tag{7}
\]
For every target put
\[
 x_t:=\sum_{e:m_e=t}q_e^*,
 \qquad
 T_*:=\{t\in\mathbb Z^n:x_t>0\},
 \qquad
 |q^*|:=\sum_eq_e^*=\sum_tx_t.
\tag{8}
\]
Assume
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(T_*)=2.
\tag{9}
\]
The retained-mass estimate is
\[
 |q^*|>\frac{553}{51200}Q.
\tag{10}
\]

Put
\[
 A:=\operatorname{aff}(T_*),
 \qquad
 D_A:=I_+\cap A,
 \qquad
 W_A:=\sum_{z\in D_A}a_z.
\tag{11}
\]
Then every physical target is charged only once and
\[
 \boxed{x_t\le\frac{a_t}{8}\quad(t\in T_*),}
\tag{12}
\]
\[
 \boxed{W_A\ge8|q^*|.}
\tag{13}
\]

The case \(n=2\) is impossible under (4), (9), and (10). If \(n>2\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 G\ge\frac{2^{n-2}}{64}W_A
 \ge2^{n-5}|q^*|,}
\tag{14}
\]
and equivalently
\[
 \boxed{W_A\le2^{8-n}G.}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently every \(n\ge5\) is impossible under the same hard inequalities. The only ambient dimensions not excluded by this lemma are \(n=3,4\), where (14)-(15) give exactly
\[
 n=3:
 \qquad
 G\ge\frac{|q^*|}{4},
 \qquad
 W_A\le32G,
 \qquad
 |q^*|<\frac Q{25},
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 n=4:
 \qquad
 G\ge\frac{|q^*|}{2},
 \qquad
 W_A\le16G,
 \qquad
 |q^*|<\frac Q{50}.
\tag{17}
\]
No exclusion of \(n=3,4\), and no coefficient larger than those in (16)-(17), is asserted.

## Proof

We first verify the targetwise physical-fiber charge, including every collision at one target. Fix \(t\in T_*\). In the notation of accepted facts b5739a77433de383 and d5d5de35c3f25af9, the positive dominant target has load \(b_t\), stress load \(0\le\omega_t\le b_t\), ordered probability cell
\[
 \mathcal P_t:=\{(r,s):r,s\in A_{\xi(t)},\ r+s=2t\},
 \qquad
 \sum_{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t}p_{t;r,s}=1,
\tag{18}
\]
and symmetric ordered probabilities
\[
 p_{t;r,s}=p_{t;s,r}.
\tag{19}
\]
For an unordered off-diagonal edge \(e=\{r,s\}\) over \(t\), accepted fact f83d08a46c19ca2f gives the exact provenance coefficient
\[
 q_e^{\ne}=p_{t;r,s}\omega_t.
\tag{20}
\]
Pairing the two ordered orientations of each unordered off-diagonal pair, while retaining all distinct edges colliding at \(t\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 x_t
 &\le\sum_{e:m_e=t}q_e^{\ne}\\
 &=\frac{\omega_t}{2}
 \sum_{\substack{(r,s)\in\mathcal P_t\\r\ne s}}
 p_{t;r,s}\\
 &\le\frac{\omega_t}{2}
 \le\frac{b_t}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Accepted fact d5d5de35c3f25af9 gives the actual dominant target-fiber bound
\[
 b_t\le\frac{a_t}{4}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (21)-(22) prove (12). In particular, \(a_t>0\) for every \(t\in T_*\), so \(T_*\subseteq D_A\). Summing (12) over the distinct physical targets proves
\[
 W_A
 \ge\sum_{t\in T_*}a_t
 \ge8\sum_{t\in T_*}x_t
 =8|q^*|,
\]
which is (13). This is the same exact collision accounting retained in accepted fact 4aa2d3090ea613b8; no target fiber is reused.

We next record the canonical reservoir-to-volume comparison. In the notation of accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, for a parity class \(\xi\) put
\[
 A_\xi^{\mathrm{vol}}:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(\xi\) is good, its exact half-strength product-reservoir mass is
\[
 Q_\xi
 =\frac{(A_\xi^{\mathrm{vol}})^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}
 {8A_\xi^{\mathrm{vol}}}
 \le\frac{A_\xi^{\mathrm{vol}}}{8}.
\tag{24}
\]
Bad classes contribute zero. Summing (24) over the parity classes gives
\[
 Q\le\frac W8,
 \qquad
 W\ge8Q.
\tag{25}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \frac W{64}\ge\frac Q8>\frac Q{100}>G,
\tag{26}
\]
where the final strict inequality is (4).

Because \(T_*\subseteq D_A\) and \(T_*\) affinely spans \(A\),
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(D_A)=A,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(D_A)=2.
\tag{27}
\]
Suppose first that \(n=2\). Then \(A=\mathbb R^2\), so \(D_A=I_+\) and \(W_A=W\). The full-rank clause of accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c, with its exact rank-two coefficient \(1/64\), gives
\[
 G\ge\frac{W}{64}.
\tag{28}
\]
This contradicts (26). Hence \(n=2\) is impossible.

Assume now that \(n>2\). Then the two-plane \(A\) is a proper affine subspace of \(\mathbb R^n\). The exact one-sided augmentation conclusion of accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c gives
\[
 G\ge
 \min\left\{
 \frac{W_A}{64}+\frac{W-W_A}{2},
 \frac{2^{n-2}W_A}{64}
 \right\}.
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(0<W_A\le W\), the first entry in (29) satisfies
\[
 \frac{W_A}{64}+\frac{W-W_A}{2}
 =\frac W2-\frac{31}{64}W_A
 \ge\frac W{64}
 >G
\tag{30}
\]
by (26). If the second entry in (29) were also larger than \(G\), then the minimum would be larger than \(G\), contradicting (29). Therefore
\[
 G\ge\frac{2^{n-2}W_A}{64}.
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (31) with (13) proves (14), and rearranging (31) proves (15).

If \(n\ge5\), equation (14) gives \(G\ge|q^*|\). Equations (10) and (4) would then imply
\[
 \frac Q{100}>G\ge|q^*|>\frac{553}{51200}Q.
\tag{32}
\]
This is impossible because
\[
 \frac{553}{51200}-\frac1{100}
 =\frac{41}{51200}>0.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus every \(n\ge5\) is excluded.

For \(n=3\), equations (14)-(15) become
\[
 G\ge\frac{|q^*|}{4},
 \qquad
 W_A\le32G.
\]
Using (4) in the first inequality gives \(|q^*|\le4G<Q/25\), proving (16). For \(n=4\), the same equations become
\[
 G\ge\frac{|q^*|}{2},
 \qquad
 W_A\le16G,
\]
and hence \(|q^*|\le2G<Q/50\), proving (17). These estimates do not contradict (10), so no further conclusion is claimed.
