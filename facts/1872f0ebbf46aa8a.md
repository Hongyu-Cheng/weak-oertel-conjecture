## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(L\ge1\) and \(k\ge6\) be integers, and set
$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=k2^L,& B&=q2^q,& t&=2^{-q}\log2,\\
m&=2^{2q+8}B^2,& n&=m+1,& r&=\frac{m}{8B},\\
\lambda&=e^{-t},&\beta&=e^{-tB}=2^{-q},&K_{\mathrm a}&=2^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
In \(\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U
&=\left\lbrace u\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m:
\left|\sum_i u_i-\frac m2\right|\le r\right\rbrace,
&N&=|\mathcal U|,\\
Q_{\mathrm{old}}
&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
\cup(\mathcal U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2B\rbrace)\bigr),
&j_0&=(0,B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
For \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(S_\ell\) be the first \(m/2^{\ell+1}\) coordinates, let \(s_\ell\) be its indicator vector, and put
$$
c_0=0,\qquad c_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}s_h,\qquad
v_\ell=(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell,0),\qquad
j_\ell=(c_\ell,B/2^\ell).
\tag{3}
$$
Define the exact support and its original probability law by
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(Q_{\mathrm{old}}\cup\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L\rbrace\bigr),
&A&=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n,\qquad H(x,z)=z,\\
w_a&=e^{-tH(a)},&Z&=\sum_{a\in A}w_a,\qquad p_a=\frac{w_a}{Z},\\
K_{\mathrm o}&=\bigl|\lbrace a+2\mathbb Z^n:a\in A\rbrace\bigr|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Here \(Q\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope and \(H\) is primitive. In particular \(A\) is a complete finite lattice support. Every same-parity pair has its midpoint in \(A\), and its weights satisfy the exact midpoint identity.

The following is a containment for the entire support, not a claim that all displayed candidate points are present. For \(e=(e_1,\ldots,e_L)\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L\), put
$$
x(e)=\sum_{h=1}^Le_hs_h,\qquad
\sigma(e)=\sum_{h=1}^L\frac{e_h}{2^h},\qquad
h_e=B(1-\sigma(e)).
\tag{5}
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{old}}
&=(\mathcal U\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
\cup(\mathcal U\times\lbrace1\rbrace)
\cup\lbrace j_0\rbrace
\cup(-\mathcal U\times\lbrace2B\rbrace),\\
A_{\mathrm{old}}\cup\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L,j_1,\ldots,j_L\rbrace
&\subseteq A\subseteq
A_{\mathrm{old}}\cup\lbrace v_1,\ldots,v_L\rbrace
\cup\bigcup_{e\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L}
\lbrace(x(e),h_e),(x(e),h_e+1)\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Every parity class contains at most two points. Its complete list of two-point classes is
$$
\lbrace(u,0),(-u,2B)\rbrace\quad(u\in\mathcal U),
\qquad
\lbrace j_{\ell-1},v_\ell\rbrace\quad(1\le\ell\le L).
\tag{7}
$$
Their midpoints are respectively \(j_0\) and \(j_\ell\). All other occupied classes are singletons.

For an occupied class \(\mathcal C\), write \(\mu(\mathcal C)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal C}p_a\), and call it balanced when \(K_{\mathrm o}\mu(\mathcal C)\in[1/2,2]\). Every class in (7) is balanced. For any fixed \(u\in\mathcal U\), consider the ordered classes
$$
\mathcal C_0=\lbrace(u,0),(-u,2B)\rbrace,\qquad
\mathcal C_h=\lbrace j_{h-1},v_h\rbrace\quad(1\le h\le L).
\tag{8}
$$
They are distinct balanced two-point classes, and the midpoint of \(\mathcal C_h\) belongs to \(\mathcal C_{h+1}\) for every integer \(0\le h<L\). Thus (8) is a chain of \(L\) balanced-to-balanced midpoint links. Its final class also has midpoint \(j_L\); no balance assertion is made about the own class of \(j_L\).

With the original law (4), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace a\in A:2j-a\in A\rbrace,&
H_p(j)&=\sum_{a\in R_j}\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}},\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace a\in R_j:H(a)=H(j)\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{a\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_ap_{2j-a}},\\
b_{\mathrm{equal}}&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2,&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
Every same-weight row is the singleton \(\widehat R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\), and
$$
E_{\mathrm a}:=K_{\mathrm a}^2b_{\mathrm{equal}}
=K_{\mathrm a}^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3<1.
\tag{10}
$$
The occupied count, full normalizer, and pair scores satisfy
$$
\begin{gathered}
2N\le K_{\mathrm o}\le2N+2^{L+1},\qquad
N(1+\lambda)\le Z\le
N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+L+2^{L+1}\le4N,\\
\left\lbrace\frac12\left|\log\frac{p_a}{p_b}\right|:
a,b\in A,\ a\ne b,\ a\equiv b\pmod2\right\rbrace
=\lbrace k2^h\log2:h\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le h\le L\rbrace.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
$$
In particular the minimum nonzero pair score is \(k\log2\), the maximum is \(k2^L\log2\), and their ratio is \(2^L\). The integers \(L\) and \(k\) can be chosen independently.

For \(L=1\), the support is exactly \(A=A_{\mathrm{old}}\cup\lbrace v_1,j_1\rbrace\), with occupied heights \(\lbrace0,1,B/2,B,2B\rbrace\) and \(K_{\mathrm o}=2N+2\). Writing \(b=B/2=k2^{2k}\) gives the original five heights \(\lbrace0,1,b,2b,4b\rbrace\). For \(k\ge42\), one has \(t<\log64\) and \(tb\ge3\log16384\), while \(tb\) is unbounded with \(k\). The source class at heights \(0,4b\) and the own two-point class of its midpoint at height \(2b\) are both balanced, and the latter class has its midpoint at height \(b\).

For quantitative scope, the full reflected energy satisfies
$$
K_{\mathrm o}^2b(p)\ge\frac{N\beta^3}{4}
\ge2^{m-3q-3}>1,
\qquad K_{\mathrm a}^2b(p)\ge K_{\mathrm o}^2b(p).
\tag{12}
$$
For each fixed upper bound on \(L\), accepted fact 5e2dee70e6a96862 continues to give its actual product-moment bounds, with occupancy two and the corresponding finite score width. The counterexample concerns a uniform geometric chain-length exclusion under (10).

## Proof

Apply accepted fact 493ce33699090e90 at its parameter \(k=q\). Its height parameter \(b\) is the present \(B\), its \(K\) is \(K_{\mathrm a}\), its \(j_\star\) is \(j_0\), and its \(Q,A,Z\) are denoted here by \(Q_{\mathrm{old}},A_{\mathrm{old}},Z_{\mathrm{old}}\). Its \(m,n,r,t,\lambda,\beta,\mathcal U,N\) retain their meanings. We use its exact lattice census, full dimension, band-density estimate, bounds on \(\lambda,\beta\), and strict ambient energy inequality. All claims about the added vertices and the enlarged support will be proved below. For raw cubic sums use \(W_{3,\mathrm{old}}\) and \(W_3\); these are not the pair-count symbol \(J=N\) appearing in that predecessor.

Since \(q=k2^L\),
$$
\frac{B}{2^L}=k2^q,\qquad
\frac{m}{2^{L+1}}=k^2 2^{4q+L+7},\qquad
r=q2^{3q+5}.
\tag{13}
$$
These are integers, and \(B/2^L\) is even. In particular all sets and points in (1)--(3) are defined over the integer lattice, and every \(B/2^\ell\), \(0\le\ell\le L\), is even. The predecessor applies because \(q\ge12\). It gives the first line of (6), full dimension of \(Q_{\mathrm{old}}\), and
$$
\begin{gathered}
N\ge2^m\left(1-\frac{\beta^2}{16}\right)\ge2^{m-1},
\qquad \lambda>\frac{63}{64},\qquad 0<\beta\le\frac1{64},\\
Z_{\mathrm{old}}=N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)+\beta,\qquad
W_{3,\mathrm{old}}=N(1+\lambda^3+\beta^6)+\beta^3,\qquad
\frac{K_{\mathrm a}^2W_{3,\mathrm{old}}}{Z_{\mathrm{old}}^3}<1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{14}
$$
The new polytope contains \(Q_{\mathrm{old}}\), so it is full-dimensional. Its generating points are integral and finite. All their heights lie in \([0,2B]\), and \(H\) is primitive because \(H(0,1)=1\). Completeness is the definition \(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\). If two points of \(A\) have the same parity, their midpoint is integral and belongs to \(Q\) by convexity. For such a midpoint \(j=(a+b)/2\), the affine height gives \(w_j^2=w_aw_b\) and \(p_j^2=p_ap_b\).

We first prove the support containment, including points in the simplex spanned only by the added vertices. Partition \(S_1\) into the nonempty blocks
\(D_h=S_h\setminus S_{h+1}\) for \(1\le h<L\) and \(D_L=S_L\). Their sizes are \(m/2^{h+2}\) for \(h<L\) and \(m/2^{L+1}\) for \(h=L\); the complementary block has size \(3m/4\). Every added horizontal vertex is zero outside \(S_1\), and its common value on \(D_h\) is
$$
V_\ell(h)=
\begin{cases}
h,&h<\ell,\\
\ell+1,&h\ge\ell.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
$$
The map that assigns these block values identifies integer profiles in \(\mathbb Z^L\) with all integer vectors that are zero outside \(S_1\) and constant on each \(D_h\). Nonemptiness of each block is used here.

For \(L=1\), \(\operatorname{conv}(V_1)\) is a single integer point. Suppose \(L\ge2\). For \(1\le\ell<L\), the difference \(V_\ell-V_{\ell+1}\) has zeros before coordinate \(\ell\), value \(1\) at coordinate \(\ell\), and value \(-1\) at every later coordinate. The matrix of their first \(L-1\) coordinates is lower triangular with diagonal entries one. Thus these differences are linearly independent, and every integer vector in their real span has integer coefficients in this basis: solving the first \(L-1\) coordinates successively proves that assertion directly. They consequently form a basis of the full lattice in this \((L-1)\)-dimensional span. The vectors \(V_\ell-V_L\), \(1\le\ell<L\), are their successive tail sums, another integer basis with determinant one. This proves unimodularity in the affine lattice of the added simplex.

More explicitly, let \(y=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\delta_\ell V_\ell\) be an integer profile, where \(\delta_\ell\ge0\) and \(\sum_\ell\delta_\ell=1\). Its coefficient along \(V_h-V_{h+1}\) in \(y-V_L\) is \(\delta_1+\cdots+\delta_h\). Each of these coefficients is an integer by the preceding triangular argument. They form a nondecreasing sequence in \([0,1]\). Hence all but one of the \(\delta_\ell\) vanish and the remaining coefficient is one. Therefore the added simplex has exactly its vertices as lattice points, including in its boundary.

Let \(P=\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal U)\). Every point of \(Q\) has a representation
$$
\begin{gathered}
x=\alpha u+\gamma u'-\theta w+
\sum_{\ell=1}^L\delta_\ell(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell),
\qquad z=\gamma+2B\theta,\\
u,u',w\in P,\qquad
\alpha,\gamma,\theta,\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_L\ge0,
\qquad \alpha+\gamma+\theta+\delta=1,
\qquad \delta=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\delta_\ell.
\end{gathered}
\tag{16}
$$
Zero coefficients cause no restriction on the unused choices from the nonempty set \(P\). If \(\delta=0\), every integral point in (16) belongs to \(A_{\mathrm{old}}\) by (14). If \(\delta=1\), then \(\alpha=\gamma=\theta=0\), and the added-simplex argument gives exactly the vertices \(v_\ell\).

It remains to consider an integral point represented with \(0<\delta<1\). Then \(0\le\theta\le1-\delta<1\). Outside \(S_1\), each coordinate belongs to \([-\theta,1-\theta-\delta]\), whose lower endpoint is greater than \(-1\) and upper endpoint is less than \(1\). Therefore every such integer coordinate is zero. On \(D_h\), put \(a_h=\sum_\ell\delta_\ell V_\ell(h)\). Every coordinate in that block belongs to
\([a_h-\theta,a_h+1-\theta-\delta]\), an interval of length \(1-\delta<1\). Since the coordinates are integral, they have a single common integer value, denoted \(g_h\).

Formula (15) gives \(a_1=\delta+\delta_1\). The lower bound for \(g_1\) is greater than \(-1\), and its upper bound is \(1-\theta+\delta_1<2\). Thus \(g_1\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\). For \(1\le h<L\),
$$
a_{h+1}-a_h=2\delta_{h+1}+\sum_{\ell>h+1}\delta_\ell\in[0,2\delta].
\tag{17}
$$
Subtracting the two coordinate intervals shows
$$
-1< (a_{h+1}-a_h)-(1-\delta)
\le g_{h+1}-g_h
\le(a_{h+1}-a_h)+(1-\delta)<2.
\tag{18}
$$
Consequently \(g_{h+1}-g_h\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\). Set \(e_1=g_1\) and \(e_h=g_h-g_{h-1}\) for \(h\ge2\). Then \(e\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^L\), and \(x=x(e)\) from (5). This argument includes \(\theta=0\) and all equality boundaries in (17).

The horizontal coordinate sum of each added vertex is exactly \(m/2\), because
$$
\sum_i(c_{\ell-1}+2s_\ell)_i
=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell-1}\frac{m}{2^{h+1}}+\frac{2m}{2^{\ell+1}}
=\frac m2.
\tag{19}
$$
Every point of \(P\) has coordinate sum in \([m/2-r,m/2+r]\). Taking the coordinate sum in (16) therefore yields
$$
\left|\sum_i x_i-\frac m2(1-2\theta)\right|
\le r(\alpha+\gamma+\theta)=r(1-\delta)\le r.
\tag{20}
$$
For \(x=x(e)\), its sum is \((m/2)\sigma(e)\). Substituting this and \(r/m=1/(8B)\) into (20) gives \(|2B\theta-h_e|\le1/4\). Since \(0\le\gamma\le1\),
$$
h_e-\frac14\le z\le h_e+\frac54.
\tag{21}
$$
The numbers \(h_e\) are the distinct positive multiples of \(B/2^L\) from \(B/2^L\) through \(B\): the integer \(\sum_h e_h2^{L-h}\) runs once through \(0,\ldots,2^L-1\). By (13), every \(h_e\) is even and their spacing is at least two. The only possible integral heights in (21) are \(h_e\) and \(h_e+1\). They all lie strictly between \(1\) and \(2B\), and the two-element height sets for different \(e\) are disjoint. This proves the upper containment in (6). It makes no membership claim about either candidate height of an arbitrary profile.

Membership of the required points does not depend on that containment. The old hub \(j_0\) belongs to \(Q_{\mathrm{old}}\). For each \(1\le\ell\le L\), the exact identity
$$
\frac{j_{\ell-1}+v_\ell}{2}
=\left(c_{\ell-1}+s_\ell,\frac{B}{2^\ell}\right)=j_\ell
\tag{22}
$$
and induction show that \(j_\ell\in Q\). They are integral by (13), hence belong to \(A\). The vertices \(v_\ell\) and \(A_{\mathrm{old}}\) also belong to \(A\). This proves the lower containment in (6).

We next classify all parity collisions. Distinct Boolean vectors have distinct parities. The old layers at heights zero and \(2B\) have exactly the \(N\) paired classes \(\lbrace(u,0),(-u,2B)\rbrace\); the height-one layer gives another \(N\) classes. Every horizontal parity in these three layers has Hamming weight at least \(m/2-r>m/4\). On the other hand every staircase profile and every added vertex is zero outside \(S_1\), whose size is \(m/4\). Their parities therefore cannot collide with those \(2N\) old classes.

The horizontal parities of the \(2^L\) staircase profiles are mutually distinct. Indeed their values on \(D_h\) are \(g_h=e_1+\cdots+e_h\). The parity of \(g_1\) determines the bit \(e_1\), and the parity of \(g_h-g_{h-1}\) determines each later bit. The two possible heights of one profile have opposite parities. Thus any two distinct atoms in the profile part of (6) lie in different parity classes. This includes the old hub, which is the profile \(e=0\) at height \(B\).

For every \(\ell\), the horizontal parity of \(v_\ell\) is that of \(c_{\ell-1}\). This is the staircase profile with its first \(\ell-1\) bits equal to one and all later bits zero. These \(L\) parities are distinct. Its even candidate height from (5) is \(B/2^{\ell-1}\), exactly the height of the known point \(j_{\ell-1}\); its other candidate height is odd. Since \(v_\ell\) has height zero, it has the same parity as \(j_{\ell-1}\) and cannot share parity with any other candidate profile atom. Consequently (7) is the complete list of all two-point classes, and no class contains three points. The possible presence or absence of every other profile atom leaves this conclusion unchanged.

At height zero the old points \((u,0)\) and the \(v_\ell\) have mutually distinct parities. The height-one and top layers are also parity-injective. At every other possible height, (21) permits at most one profile atom. Hence every individual height is injective modulo \(2\mathbb Z^n\). If \(a\in\widehat R_j\), then \(a\) and \(2j-a\) are same-parity points at the same height, so they coincide. This forces \(a=j\), proving the singleton-row assertion in (10). Because \(t>0\), height equality is exactly weight equality.

The old \(2N\) classes remain distinct. The profile part of (6) contributes at most \(2^{L+1}\) classes, and every added vertex shares a class with the known profile point \(j_{\ell-1}\). This proves the bounds for \(K_{\mathrm o}\) in (11). The three nonhub old layers contribute exactly \(N(1+\lambda+\beta^2)\) to the raw normalizer. There are \(L\) added vertices, of raw weight one. Every other atom is a profile point from (6), with at most \(2^{L+1}\) such points, including the old hub. Since all heights in \(Q\) are nonnegative, each of those weights is at most one. This gives the upper bound for \(Z\) in (11) without discarding or conditioning any unknown additional mass. The lower bound follows just from the two old lower layers.

For the simpler bounds used below, \(2^L\ge L+1\) implies \(q\ge L+4\); also \(m=q^2 2^{4q+8}\ge q\). Thus \(m\ge L+4\), and (14) gives \(N\ge2^{L+3}\). Since \(L\le2^L\), it follows that
$$
L+2^{L+1}\le N,\qquad 2^{L+1}\le N/2,\qquad
Z\le4N,\qquad K_{\mathrm o}\le\frac52N.
\tag{23}
$$
Here \(1+\lambda+\beta^2<3\), as \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(0<\beta<1\). Together with (14), (23) yields
$$
\frac12\le\frac{K_{\mathrm o}}{Z}
<\frac{(5/2)N}{(127/64)N}=\frac{160}{127}.
\tag{24}
$$

We prove the ambient selected-energy inequality using the full unknown extra mass. Let
\(W_3=\sum_{a\in A}w_a^3\),
\(\Delta Z=Z-Z_{\mathrm{old}}\), and
\(\Delta W_3=W_3-W_{3,\mathrm{old}}\).
The new vertex \(v_1\), which has coordinate value two on the nonempty set \(S_1\), is not in the old height-zero Boolean layer. Hence \(\Delta Z>0\). Every newly added atom has weight at most one, so
\(0<\Delta W_3\le\Delta Z\). From (14) and \(\lambda<1\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
3W_{3,\mathrm{old}}-Z_{\mathrm{old}}
&=N(2+3\lambda^3-\lambda+3\beta^6-\beta^2)
+3\beta^3-\beta\\
&\ge N(1-\beta^2)-\beta
\ge1-\frac1{4096}-\frac1{64}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
The exact cubic expansion consequently gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&W_{3,\mathrm{old}}(Z_{\mathrm{old}}+\Delta Z)^3
-(W_{3,\mathrm{old}}+\Delta W_3)Z_{\mathrm{old}}^3\\
&\quad\ge
\Delta Z\,Z_{\mathrm{old}}^2
(3W_{3,\mathrm{old}}-Z_{\mathrm{old}})
+3W_{3,\mathrm{old}}Z_{\mathrm{old}}(\Delta Z)^2
+W_{3,\mathrm{old}}(\Delta Z)^3>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
Thus \(W_3/Z^3<W_{3,\mathrm{old}}/Z_{\mathrm{old}}^3\). The singleton same-weight rows give \(b_{\mathrm{equal}}=W_3/Z^3\). Multiplying by the unchanged ambient capacity \(K_{\mathrm a}^2\) and applying (14) proves (10). All original central atoms and every unclassified extra atom remain in \(Z,W_3\).

The raw mass of an old two-point class is \(1+\beta^2\). The raw mass of the class \(\lbrace j_{\ell-1},v_\ell\rbrace\) is
\(1+e^{-tB/2^{\ell-1}}\). Among the smaller endpoints of all these classes, the largest weight is
\(e^{-tB/2^{L-1}}=2^{-2k}\le1/4096\).
By (24), every class in (7) therefore satisfies
$$
\frac12\le K_{\mathrm o}\mu(\mathcal C)
<\frac{160}{127}\left(1+\frac1{4096}\right)<2.
\tag{27}
$$
The last strict inequality follows by multiplying the positive denominators. This proves balance using the occupied count and the complete original normalizer. The parity classification proves that the classes in (8) are distinct. Their midpoint relations follow from the old opposite pairs and (22). This proves all \(L\) links in (8); the use of the final pair \(\lbrace j_{L-1},v_L\rbrace\) guarantees balance of the last target class without asserting balance for \(j_L\)'s own class.

By (7), there are no further pairs of distinct same-parity atoms. Their absolute scores are exactly
$$
\frac12\left|\log\frac{p_{(u,0)}}{p_{(-u,2B)}}\right|
=tB=q\log2,
\qquad
\frac12\left|\log\frac{p_{v_\ell}}{p_{j_{\ell-1}}}\right|
=\frac{tB}{2^\ell}=k2^{L-\ell}\log2.
\tag{28}
$$
Both members of every listed pair are present and distinct. This proves the exact score set in (11). Given any prescribed positive score threshold and any prescribed chain length, choosing the integer \(k\) sufficiently large and using that length as \(L\) meets both requirements independently.

For completeness, we now sharpen the support containment when \(L=1\). Write \(s=s_1\). In (16) with \(0<\delta<1\), every coordinate on \(S_1\) belongs to \([2\delta-\theta,1-\theta+\delta]\). If \(\delta<\theta\), its upper bound is strictly less than one and its lower bound is greater than \(-1\), so all such coordinates are zero. If \(\delta\ge\theta\), its lower bound is at least \(\delta>0\) and its upper bound is less than two, so all such coordinates are one. The boundary \(\delta=\theta\) is included in the latter case. Thus the only mixed-representation horizontal vectors are zero and \(s\).

For \(x=0\), (20) implies \(|\theta-1/2|\le1/(8B)\), while \(\gamma\le1-\theta\). Hence
$$
B-\frac14\le z\le
1+(2B-1)\left(\frac12+\frac1{8B}\right)
=B+\frac34-\frac1{8B}.
\tag{29}
$$
The only integer height here is \(B\). For \(x=s\), its coordinate sum is \(m/4\), so \(|\theta-1/4|\le1/(8B)\). This is the case \(\delta\ge\theta\), which gives \(\gamma\le1-\theta-\delta\le1-2\theta\). Since \(2B-2>0\),
$$
\frac B2-\frac14\le z\le
1+(2B-2)\left(\frac14+\frac1{8B}\right)
=\frac B2+\frac34-\frac1{4B}.
\tag{30}
$$
The only integer height is \(B/2\). The \(\delta=0\) and \(\delta=1\) cases were already exhausted. Both remaining points are present by (22), proving the exact census \(A=A_{\mathrm{old}}\cup\lbrace v_1,j_1\rbrace\).

For this specialization, the new vertex joins the old hub's class, and \(j_1\) has the distinct nonzero horizontal parity of \(s\). Thus \(K_{\mathrm o}=2N+2\). Put \(b=B/2=k2^{2k}\), so \(e^{-tb}=2^{-k}\). Then \(q=2k\), \(m=2^{4k+10}b^2\), \(r=m/(16b)\), and the exact unchanged raw sums are
$$
Z=N(1+\lambda+2^{-4k})+1+2^{-k}+2^{-2k},
\qquad W_3=N(1+\lambda^3+2^{-12k})+1+2^{-3k}+2^{-6k}.
\tag{31}
$$
All five claimed heights occur. The pairs, midpoint link, full dimension, primitive height, occupancy, ambient energy, and occupied balance have already been proved, now with an exact census. If \(k\ge42\), then \(tb=k\log2\ge42\log2=3\log16384\), whereas \(t=2^{-2k}\log2<\log64\). This settles the original five-height link test within the same family.

Finally, the full row at \(j_0\) contains its central atom and both endpoints of each of the \(N\) old pairs. Every such geometric row summand equals \(p_{j_0}=\beta/Z\). Therefore, using only the original law and (23),
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm o}^2b(p)
&\ge K_{\mathrm o}^2(2N+1)^2\left(\frac\beta Z\right)^3\\
&\ge\frac{(2N)^2(2N+1)^2\beta^3}{(4N)^3}
\ge\frac{N\beta^3}{4}
\ge2^{m-3q-3}>1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
