## Statement

For an integer $d\ge1$, $0<a\le b$, and $h\ge0$, put $R(h)=[a-(2h^{1/d}-b^{1/d})_+^d]_+$, where $x_+=\max(x,0)$, and $v=2^{-d}(a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})^d$. The function is $a$ on $[0,b/2^d]$, zero on $[v,\infty)$, and strictly decreasing between. Its optimal global Lipschitz constant on $[0,\infty)$ is
$$
\kappa_d(a,b)=2\left(\frac{2a^{1/d}}{a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}}\right)^{d-1}\le2.
$$

## Proof

The formula gives the two constant branches and is continuous at both junctions. The intervening branch has positive inner argument and strictly positive residual credit. If $d=1$ it is affine with slope $-2$. If $d>1$, the magnitude of its derivative is $2(2-(b/h)^{1/d})^{d-1}$, strictly increasing there, with left limit at $v$ equal to the displayed constant. The mean value theorem on each branch, and addition across the junctions, give a global Lipschitz bound. Any global Lipschitz constant bounds the magnitude of the derivative at every interior differentiability point, hence also that left limit. Finally $a\le b$ makes the base in the displayed power at most one.
