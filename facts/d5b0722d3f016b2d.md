## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\) be positive integers with \(L\ge h\), let \(\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)\in\mathbb R^m\), and let \(\theta\in\mathbb R\). On
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\}
\]
give every point its original probability
\[
p(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{\exp(\sum_{i=1}^m\beta_i x_i+\theta y)}{\sum_{(\mathbf u,v)\in D}\exp(\sum_{i=1}^m\beta_i u_i+\theta v)}.
\]
For any finite probability law \(\pi\) in a real coordinate space, write \(\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)\) for the infimum of the \(\pi\)-masses of all closed halfspaces containing \(\mathbf z\), and put \(M_3(\pi)=\sum_{\mathbf z}\pi(\mathbf z)\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^3\). A singleton has depth and moment one. These are exactly the actual-depth quantities used in ac4157e446425a3d and the quantity called \(\mathcal M_3\) in f1b85039a5de43a6, with no rescaling.

There is one absolute constant \(\kappa_{\mathrm{arb}}>0\) such that \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{arb}}8^{-(m+1)}\) for every choice above, including arbitrary unequal, zero, positive, and negative transverse scores.

Here is an explicit choice in terms of the accepted constants. Let \(c_1=60000\) be exactly the height-band constant of ac4157e446425a3d. Let \(a\in(0,1)\), \(g>1\), \(\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}=1/(8\cdot45648)\), and \(\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}=\sqrt{8\cdot45648\,C_{\mathrm{price}}}\) be exactly the constants of f1b85039a5de43a6. Thus \(g\) and \(C_{\mathrm{price}}\) have that fact's stated definitions and normalizations. Define
\[
\xi=\frac12\log\min\{2,g^{3/2}\}>0,
\qquad
c_* =\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3\exp\left(-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}^2}{4\xi}\right).
\]
One may take
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{arb}}=\min\left\{a^3,\frac{c_*}{4c_1^4e^\xi}\left(\frac{\xi}{8}\right)^8\right\}.
\]
All constants here are independent of \(m,h,L,\boldsymbol\beta,\theta\).

## Proof

When \(h=1\), this is already a standard-simplex specialization of f1b85039a5de43a6: take one simplex factor with that fact's dilation \(m_1=L\), rank \(r_1=m+1\), and source tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell_1=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m,\theta)\). Its support and its original probability are precisely \(D\) and \(p\) above. Hence \(M_3(p)\ge a^3 8^{-(m+1)}\), which implies the asserted bound. We prove the remaining case \(h\ge2\).

Put \(K=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\ge1\), \(\ell=L-hK\in\{0,\ldots,h-1\}\), \(t=K-y\), and \(R_t=\ell+ht\). Adjoin the integer slack \(s_0=L-\sum_i x_i-hy\), and set \(s_i=x_i\) for \(1\le i\le m\). At height \(t\), these are the complete compositions \(\mathbf s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}\) of \(R_t\). Extend the scores by \(\beta_0=0\), and choose an index \(f\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) attaining \(\beta_*:=\max_{0\le i\le m}\beta_i\). Let \(\mathbf z=(z_i)_{i\ne f}\) consist of the \(m\) retained coordinates \(z_i=s_i\), in increasing index order, and let \(\delta_i=\beta_i-\beta_*\le0\) for each retained index. These scores are fixed independently of the height.

The chart \(\Phi:(\mathbf x,y)\mapsto(\mathbf z,t)\) is an invertible integer affine map of \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\). If \(f=0\), its inverse is \(x_i=z_i\), \(y=K-t\). If \(f\ge1\), its inverse is
\[
y=K-t,\qquad x_i=z_i\ (1\le i\le m,\ i\ne f),\qquad x_f=\ell+ht-\sum_{i\ne f}z_i,
\]
where the last sum includes retained index zero. In either case its transformed support is
\[
\widetilde D=\{(\mathbf z,t):0\le t\le K,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \mathbf z\in\Delta_{R_t,m}\},
\qquad
\Delta_{R,m}:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i z_i\le R\}.
\]
Retained coordinates here always use the increasing-index order of the chart. Eliminating \(s_f\) gives the exact identity
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\beta_i x_i+\theta y
=\theta K+\beta_*\ell-(\theta-h\beta_*)t+\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i z_i.
\]
Consequently, with \(\eta=\theta-h\beta_*\) and
\[
A(R)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\Delta_{R,m}}\exp\left(\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i z_i\right),
\qquad
\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\eta t}A(R_t),
\]
the pushforward \(\widetilde p(\mathbf z,t)=p(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf z,t))\) is exactly
\[
\widetilde p(\mathbf z,t)=\frac{e^{-\eta t}\exp(\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i z_i)}{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{1}
\]
The original normalizing sum is \(e^{\theta K+\beta_*\ell}\mathcal Z\), so no weights have been reassigned. The real invertibility of \(\Phi\) identifies all closed-halfspace traces and their root-containing traces. Therefore it preserves their original probabilities, actual depths, and \(M_3\).

We prove the needed partition growth for arbitrary nonpositive \(\delta_i\). Let \(N_R=\binom{R+m}{m}\), the number of complete compositions of \(R\) into \(m+1\) coordinates. There is a coordinatewise increasing coupling of their uniform laws for all integers \(R\ge0\). Start at the zero composition. Given a composition \(\mathbf s\) of \(R\), increment coordinate \(i\) with probability \((s_i+1)/(R+m+1)\). If the law at \(R\) is uniform, then for every composition \(\mathbf d\) of \(R+1\), the probability of reaching it is
\[
\sum_{i:d_i\ge1}\frac1{N_R}\frac{d_i}{R+m+1}
=\frac{R+1}{N_R(R+m+1)}
=\frac1{N_{R+1}}.
\]
Thus induction proves uniformity at every budget, and every coordinate only increases along the coupled path. Since each \(\delta_i\le0\), the function \(\exp(\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i s_i)\) is nonincreasing along that path. Removing coordinate \(f\) bijects complete compositions with \(\Delta_{R,m}\), so
\[
\frac{A(R)}{N_R}=\mathbb E\exp\left(\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i s_i^{(R)}\right)
\]
is nonincreasing in \(R\). On the other hand \(A(R)\) is positive and nondecreasing, because its supports are nested and its atom weights are fixed and positive. For integers \(1\le R_1\le R_2\) we therefore have
\[
\frac{A(R_2)}{A(R_1)}
\le\frac{N_{R_2}}{N_{R_1}}
=\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{R_2+j}{R_1+j}
\le\left(\frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)^m.
\tag{2}
\]
The coupling and these bounds include score ties and zero retained scores exactly.

Apply the generalized height-band assertion proved in ac4157e446425a3d, with its \(\nu=m\), its \(K\) equal to the present \(K\), its height weights \(b_t=A(R_t)\), and its exponential parameter equal to the present \(\eta\). Its positivity and monotonicity hypotheses hold. For \(1\le u\le v\le K\), (2) gives
\[
\frac{b_v}{b_u}\le\left(\frac{\ell+hv}{\ell+hu}\right)^m\le(v/u)^m,
\]
as required. Thus a nonempty consecutive integer interval \(I=[a_I,b_I]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq\{0,\ldots,K\}\) exists such that
\[
\lambda:=\frac{|I|A(R_{a_I})\min_{t\in I}e^{-\eta t}}{\mathcal Z}
\ge\frac1{c_1(m+1)^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Write \(R=R_{a_I}\), let \(u_I\) be uniform on \(I\), and give \(\Delta_{R,m}\) its original tilted-simplex conditional law
\[
Q_R(\mathbf z)=\frac{\exp(\sum_{i\ne f}\delta_i z_i)}{A(R)}.
\]
Its support is contained in the transverse support at every \(t\in I\). The ratio of (1) to \(Q_R(\mathbf z)u_I(t)\) on this product support is \(|I|A(R)e^{-\eta t}/\mathcal Z\). Thus (3) proves the pointwise comparison
\[
\widetilde p\ge\lambda(Q_R\otimes u_I),
\tag{4}
\]
with the product extended by zero outside its support. This comparison uses the original probability in (1).

We next retain an exponential rank surplus for \(Q_R\), uniformly including small budgets. If \(R\ge2\), specialize the certified-product estimate before completion of the square in the proof of f1b85039a5de43a6 to a single simplex. Its dilation \(m_1\) is the present \(R\), its rank \(r_1\) and \(S_{\mathrm{rank}}\) are the present \(m\), its source tilt is the retained vector \((\delta_i)_{i\ne f}\), and its original law \(P_{\mathrm{cert}}\) is exactly \(Q_R\). That estimate is
\[
M_2(Q_R)\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^2
\exp(m\log g-2\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt m)4^{-m},
\]
where \(M_2(Q_R)=\sum_{\mathbf z}Q_R(\mathbf z)\tau_{Q_R}(\mathbf z)^2\), exactly \(\mathcal M_2\) of that fact. Its Jensen step, still under the original root law, yields
\[
M_3(Q_R)\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3
\exp\left(\tfrac32 m\log g-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt m\right)8^{-m}.
\]
By the definition of \(\xi\), \(2\xi\le\tfrac32\log g\). Also
\[
\xi m-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt m
=\left(\sqrt{\xi m}-\frac{3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}}{2\sqrt\xi}\right)^2
-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}^2}{4\xi}.
\]
It follows that
\[
M_3(Q_R)\ge c_*e^{\xi m}8^{-m}.
\tag{5}
\]
The constants satisfy \(e^\xi\le\sqrt2\le2\) and \(0<c_*\le\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3<1/2\).

For \(R=1\), \(Q_R\) is an arbitrary positive law on the \(m+1\) simplex vertices. Every root-containing halfspace contains the root atom, so convexity of the fourth power gives
\[
M_3(Q_1)\ge\sum_{\mathbf z}Q_1(\mathbf z)^4\ge(m+1)^{-3}.
\]
The inequality \((m+1)^3\le2\cdot4^m\) holds at \(m=1\) and persists by induction because \(((m+2)/(m+1))^3\le27/8<4\). Hence
\[
M_3(Q_1)\ge\tfrac12\,2^m8^{-m}\ge c_*e^{\xi m}8^{-m},
\]
which proves (5) in this case without using a row certificate. For \(R=0\), \(Q_R\) is a rank-zero singleton, its actual depth moment is one, and (5) follows directly from \(c_*<1/2\) and \(e^\xi\le2\). Thus (5) holds for all integer \(R\ge0\), with the same constants.

For completeness, the two actual-depth operations used with (4) keep the root weights exactly. For any two finite laws \(P_1,P_2\), a halfspace through a product root has a centered inequality \(\mathbf u_1\cdot(\mathbf X_1-\mathbf z_1)+\mathbf u_2\cdot(\mathbf X_2-\mathbf z_2)\ge0\). It contains the product event in which both summands are nonnegative. Each factor event has probability at least its corresponding actual depth, including a zero coefficient block, whose event has probability one. Any containing halfspace contains its parallel translate through the root. Taking the infimum and then averaging cubes with the product root law proves
\[
\tau_{P_1\otimes P_2}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)
\ge\tau_{P_1}(\mathbf z_1)\tau_{P_2}(\mathbf z_2),
\qquad
M_3(P_1\otimes P_2)\ge M_3(P_1)M_3(P_2).
\tag{6}
\]
Likewise, if \(P\ge\rho V\) pointwise, with \(V\) a probability law on a subset of the support of \(P\), then every containing halfspace at a root of \(V\) has \(P\)-mass at least \(\rho\) times its \(V\)-mass. Consequently
\[
M_3(P)\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in\operatorname{supp}V}P(\mathbf z)
(\rho\tau_V(\mathbf z))^3
\ge\rho^4M_3(V).
\tag{7}
\]
There is one factor of \(\rho\) from the original root probability and three factors from its original actual depth. These arguments include singleton factors and do not use exponential capacity as actual depth.

The finite-line depth estimate proved in ac4157e446425a3d gives \(M_3(u_I)\ge1/32\) for every nonempty consecutive interval, including a singleton. Combining (3)--(7) therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)=M_3(\widetilde p)
&\ge\lambda^4M_3(Q_R)M_3(u_I)\\
&\ge\frac{c_*e^{\xi m}}{32c_1^4(m+1)^8}\,8^{-m}\\
&=\frac{c_*}{4c_1^4}\frac{e^{\xi m}}{(m+1)^8}\,8^{-(m+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally, \(e^v\ge v\), applied to \(v=(m+1)\xi/8>0\) and raised to the eighth power, implies
\[
\frac{e^{\xi m}}{(m+1)^8}\ge e^{-\xi}\left(\frac{\xi}{8}\right)^8.
\]
This proves the second coefficient in the stated \(\kappa_{\mathrm{arb}}\) for \(h\ge2\). Together with the already accepted \(h=1\) specialization it proves the theorem for every allowed parameter. All depths and moments throughout are measured with original halfspace probabilities and original root weights.
