## Statement

Take three compatible positive-area triangles at $0,1,2$ with strict midpoint containment, at a local minimum of their maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth $\beta$ among such triangle triples. Write $M$ for the total area and $b$ for the middle area. Choose a minimal positive circuit of $k=2$ or $3$ active unit planar normals $u_j$ at an interior middle maximizing mark, with actual ambient caps of mass $\beta M$. Translate the mark to $0$ in planar coordinates. Let $C_j$ be the area of the middle cap, $S_j$ its chord length, and $X_j$ the chord first moment about $0$. There exist positive multipliers $\lambda_j$ with
$$
\sum_j\lambda_j S_j u_j=0,\qquad
\sum_j\lambda_j C_j=b,\qquad
\sum_j\lambda_j X_j u_j^{\mathsf T}=0.
$$
These identities also hold for the middle cap multipliers of any objective-one joint stress for the full independently varied triangles and cap parameters, using the groups of the middle circuit and strict endpoint isolating caps, provided it satisfies cap and coverage stationarity. For a pair the multipliers are equal and the normalized weighted middle cap fraction is $1/2$. For a triple every middle chord is bisected at the mark.

## Proof

The middle is deepest by strict-interior-three-triangle-deepest-fibers. Fact 755cb08c379113cc applies to these three positive compatible fibers: the Minkowski midpoint inclusion guarantees that the lifted hull has the same middle fiber, since a convex combination at base $1$ has equal weights at $0$ and $2$. The fact supplies interior maximizing marks and minimal active circuits. Any chosen circuit covers its entire base-$1$ plane because its positive weighted sum of margins vanishes there. At each endpoint take the three ordinary centroid vertex caps and add large exposing base tilts to exclude the other two fibers strictly. Their masses are $4/9$ of that endpoint area and hence at most $\beta M$ by the same deepest-fiber lemma. Their traces cover the entire endpoint plane because some barycentric coordinate is at least $1/3$.

Freeze both endpoint triangles and all cap parameters. Vary only the middle triangle by a small affine perturbation. Strict compatibility persists by the positive distance from the compact midpoint sum to the middle boundary. The endpoint caps still exclude the middle strictly, and the entire three-plane cover is unchanged. If the middle area increased and each middle cap mass were at most its old value $\beta M$, the cover would imply that the new maximum normalized depth is at most $\beta M/M'<\beta$, where $M'$ is the new total area. Thus the middle area is locally maximal under these finitely many middle cap constraints.

Under the affine change $x\mapsto x+\varepsilon(Ax+c)$ the derivative of the middle area is $b\operatorname{tr}A$, while the derivative of the $j$-th cap area is
$$
G_j(A,c)=C_j\operatorname{tr}A+u_j^{\mathsf T}A X_j+S_j u_j\cdot c.
$$
For the latter, pull the cut back to the fixed triangle and write $x=t u_j+s u_j^\perp$. Its perturbed threshold in $t$ is $-\varepsilon u_j\cdot(A(su_j^\perp)+c)/(1+\varepsilon u_j^{\mathsf T}A u_j)$. This produces the stated signed strip flux along the chord. In each transverse slice, the threshold change is uniformly $O(|\varepsilon|)$ over the bounded projection. At all but the two chord endpoints the limiting first variation of slice length is the threshold speed with its negative sign on the interior chord and zero off the chord. Dominated convergence gives the flux even if the proper chord passes through a vertex. Multiplying by the determinant adds the trace term. Endpoint contributions to the middle cap masses are constant in this test.

The objective functional $b\operatorname{tr}A$ is a nonnegative linear combination of the $G_j$. To see this, use the direction $(-I,0)$, for which $G_j=-2C_j<0$ since the mark is interior. The cone generated by the finitely many $G_j$ is closed: evaluation in this direction bounds the sum of the nonnegative coefficients in every converging sequence of conic combinations. If the objective were not in this cone, separation would give a direction with positive objective and all $G_j\le0$. Adding a small multiple of $(-I,0)$ gives all $G_j<0$ and keeps the objective positive. A sufficiently small affine step in that direction contradicts local maximality. This proves the claimed representation with nonnegative multipliers. For a given full objective-one joint stress the same representation follows by testing independent middle affine directions only: the endpoint caps miss the middle strictly, the linear coverage rows only involve the fixed cap parameters, and compatibility has strict slack.

The translation part of the representation gives the first identity. Since $u_j\cdot X_j=0$ for each chord through $0$, the test $A=I$ gives the second. Subtraction leaves $\sum_j\lambda_j u_j^{\mathsf T} A X_j=0$ for every $A$, giving the third. At least one multiplier is positive by the second identity; all are positive by the first and minimality of the circuit, since $S_j>0$. For a pair the two opposite unit normals share the same chord, so the weights coincide and the cap areas sum to $b$. For a triple the space of relations among the unit normals is one-dimensional. Set $m_j=X_j/S_j$. For each coordinate of $m_j$, the third identity is a relation with coefficients $\lambda_j S_j(m_j)_\ell$. It is a multiple of the first identity, with coefficients $\lambda_j S_j>0$. Thus all three $m_j$ coincide. They lie on all three rank-two concurrent lines, whose common intersection is $0$.
