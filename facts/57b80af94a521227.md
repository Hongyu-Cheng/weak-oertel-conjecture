## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(b_0\ge b_1\ge\cdots\ge b_n\ge0\), with \(b_0>0\). For \(z\in\{0,1,2\}^n\), write \(r(z):=|\{i:z_i=2\}|\), and define \(w_z:=b_{r(z)}\). Define \(w_z:=0\) at all other integer points, and put \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\). These weights have nonempty coordinatewise downset positive support and are coordinatewise nonincreasing there.

For each \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), define \(\alpha_j(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha_j(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). For \(L\ge0\), define \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha_j(v)\). All logarithms are natural, and displacements are oriented and include zero. At fixed \(j\), \(\alpha_j(v)\) is the quantity denoted \(\alpha(v)\) in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; the other scalar definitions have the same normalization. The curvatures of the profiles in this statement are nonnegative. Writing \(\mathbf 1:=(1,\ldots,1)\), one has

\[
\max\left\{B_0^\Delta(0),\frac{B_{\mathbf 1}^\Delta(2n)}{1+2n}\right\}
\ge\frac1{10}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

In particular, the same bound holds for the maximum over all positive centers and nonnegative cutoffs.

An admissible jointly log-concave subclass is obtained as follows. Let \(g:[0,n]\to[0,\infty)\) be nonincreasing and log-concave, with \(g(0)>0\). On \(\mathbb R^n\), define

\[
F(x):=
\begin{cases}
g\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\max\{x_i-1,0\}\right),&x\in[0,2]^n,\\
0,&x\notin[0,2]^n.
\end{cases}
\]

Then \(F\) is jointly log-concave and coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative orthant. Its integer restriction is the preceding profile with \(b_r=g(r)\), so it satisfies the displayed cutoff bound. This includes \(g(s)=e^{-t s^2}\) for every \(t>0\), and \(g(s)=e^{-t s^2}\mathbf 1_{\{s\le R\}}\) for every \(t>0\) and \(R\in[0,n]\). The former is nonproduct when \(n\ge2\); the latter has nonproduct positive integer support when \(R\) is an integer with \(1\le R<n\).

For the actual-body consequence, let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), and \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). Suppose these actual integer fiber volumes have exactly the scalar form above for some \(b_0\ge\cdots\ge b_n\ge0\), \(b_0>0\). These actual-body objects have their meanings in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. With \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\), there exists one point \(p^\star\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge (\ell/160)2^{-n}W\). The constant \(\ell/160\) is absolute and belongs to \((0,1/e)\).

## Proof

If \(y\in\mathbb Z^n\), \(0\le y\le z\) coordinatewise, and \(z\in\{0,1,2\}^n\), then \(y\in\{0,1,2\}^n\) and \(r(y)\le r(z)\). Hence \(w_y\ge w_z\). In particular, every integer point below a positive point is positive. The origin and every binary point have weight \(b_0>0\), so the asserted positive support is nonempty. All relevant sums are finite.

For any positive reflected pair about any positive integer center \(j\), every coordinate with \(j_i=0\) or \(j_i=2\) has \(v_i=0\): both endpoints must lie in \([0,2]^n\). Consequently, each coordinate equal to 2 at the center remains equal to 2 at both endpoints. Thus \(r(j+v),r(j-v)\ge r(j)\), and \(w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\le w_j\). This proves \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\). At the origin the only positive reflected displacement is zero, so \(B_0^\Delta(0)=b_0\).

Normalize by \(a_r:=b_r/b_0\), so \(1=a_0\ge\cdots\ge a_n\ge0\). Define \(t_r:=\binom nr2^{-r}a_r\) and \(T:=2^{-n}W/b_0\). There are exactly \(\binom nr2^{n-r}\) ternary points with \(r\) coordinates equal to 2: choose these coordinates, then choose either 0 or 1 in each remaining coordinate. Therefore \(T=\sum_{r=0}^n t_r\), and \(t_0=1\).

For every integer \(r>n/2\), symmetry of the binomial coefficient, \(2^{-(n-r)}\ge2^{-r}\), and \(a_{n-r}\ge a_r\) give \(t_{n-r}\ge t_r\). Pairing these ranks injectively with ranks below \(n/2\) proves \(\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}t_r\ge T/2\). This also handles the unpaired middle rank when \(n\) is even. After removing the rank-zero contribution, it gives \(\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}t_r\ge T/2-1\).

Let \(I:=\{r\in\mathbb Z:1\le r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\ a_r\ge e^{-n}\}\). The contribution discarded by this threshold, even if summed over all ranks, is at most

\[
\sum_{\substack{0\le r\le n\\a_r<e^{-n}}}t_r
\le e^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr2^{-r}
=\left(\frac{3}{2e}\right)^n<1.
\]

Here \(e>2\) suffices for the last inequality. In particular, \(\sum_{r\in I}t_r\ge T/2-2\). A zero weight is discarded at this step, and no logarithm of a zero weight is used.

Use the center \(j=\mathbf 1\), whose weight is \(b_0\). For every \(r\in I\), retain all displacements \(v\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\) having exactly \(r\) coordinates equal to \(+1\) and exactly \(r\) coordinates equal to \(-1\). Both endpoints then have exactly \(r\) coordinates equal to 2, so their weights are both \(b_0a_r>0\). Thus each retained displacement has mass \(\alpha_{\mathbf 1}(v)=b_0a_r\) and curvature \(\Delta_{\mathbf 1}(v)=2\log(1/a_r)\le2n\).

There are \(\binom nr\binom{n-r}{r}\) such displacements. This counts both orientations, as required: a nonzero displacement and its negative are distinct members of the sum. Displacements belonging to different ranks are also distinct. For every \(1\le r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\),

\[
2^r\binom{n-r}{r}
\ge 2^r\frac{n-r}{r}
\ge 2(n-r)
\ge n.
\]

The first inequality follows from \(\binom{n-r}{r}=((n-r)/r)\binom{n-r-1}{r-1}\) and \(\binom{n-r-1}{r-1}\ge1\). The second uses \(2^r\ge2r\) for integers \(r\ge1\), which follows by induction from equality at \(r=1\). These arguments include \(n=2,r=1\). If \(n=1\), the range of positive balanced ranks is empty.

The retained displacements therefore give

\[
B_{\mathbf 1}^\Delta(2n)
\ge b_0\sum_{r\in I}\binom nr\binom{n-r}{r}a_r
=b_0\sum_{r\in I}t_r\,2^r\binom{n-r}{r}
\ge b_0 n\sum_{r\in I}t_r.
\]

If \(T\le10\), the origin already gives \(B_0^\Delta(0)=b_0\ge b_0T/10\). If \(T>10\), then \(T/2-2\ge3T/10>0\), and the last display gives

\[
\frac{B_{\mathbf 1}^\Delta(2n)}{1+2n}
\ge b_0\frac{n}{1+2n}\left(\frac T2-2\right)
\ge\frac{b_0}{3}\left(\frac T2-2\right)
\ge\frac{b_0T}{10}.
\]

Here \(n/(1+2n)\ge1/3\) for \(n\ge1\). Since \(b_0T=2^{-n}W\), these two cases prove the scalar bound, including every zero-weight and small-dimension case. The maximum over all positive centers and cutoffs exists as well: there are finitely many centers, and at each center the numerator only changes at one of finitely many nonnegative curvature values, including zero; replacing any cutoff by the largest such value not exceeding it preserves the numerator and decreases the denominator.

We next verify the jointly log-concave subclass. On the box \([0,2]^n\), the function \(x\mapsto\sum_i\max\{x_i-1,0\}\) is convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing, with values in \([0,n]\). For two points in the positive support of \(F\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), convexity of this sum and the nonincreasing property of \(g\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)
&\ge g\!\left(\lambda\sum_i\max\{x_i-1,0\}+(1-\lambda)\sum_i\max\{y_i-1,0\}\right)\\
&\ge F(x)^\lambda F(y)^{1-\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second inequality is log-concavity of \(g\). If one endpoint has zero value, the log-concavity inequality is automatic; the box is convex, so extending by zero outside it preserves the same inequality. Coordinatewise monotonicity on the nonnegative orthant follows from the monotonicity of the sum and of \(g\); if a point leaves the box through an upper face, its value becomes zero. At an integer point of the box, the sum is exactly \(r(z)\). Thus \(F(z)=g(r(z))\), and the scalar theorem applies.

For \(t>0\), the function \(-t s^2\) is concave and nonincreasing on \([0,n]\), proving the stated properties of \(g(s)=e^{-t s^2}\). Restricting its positive support to the interval \([0,R]\) preserves those properties. To see nonproduct behavior without relying on terminology alone, let \(e_1,e_2\) be distinct coordinate unit vectors. In the untruncated quadratic example, \(w_0=1\), \(w_{2e_1}=w_{2e_2}=e^{-t}\), and \(w_{2e_1+2e_2}=e^{-4t}\). A product profile would satisfy \(w_{2e_1+2e_2}w_0=w_{2e_1}w_{2e_2}\), whereas here the two sides are \(e^{-4t}\) and \(e^{-2t}\), which differ. For an integer \(1\le R<n\), the truncated positive integer support is exactly \(\{z\in\{0,1,2\}^n:r(z)\le R\}\). Each coordinate projection contains all of \(\{0,1,2\}\), but the all-2 point is absent. This support cannot be a Cartesian product. The scalar proof covers the resulting coupled support and curvature simultaneously.

Finally, assume the stated actual-body hypotheses. Its actual \(w_z,W,C_z,S\) are exactly the objects in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`, and \(W\ge b_0>0\). At fixed \(j\), the substitution \(\alpha(v)=\alpha_j(v)\) identifies the reflected weights; \(\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j(v),B_j^\Delta(L)\) and oriented-displacement normalization are unchanged. That accepted fact supplies one \(p^\star\in S\) with \(h_S(p^\star)\ge(\ell/16)\max_{j,L}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\), where \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\). Substituting the proved scalar estimate gives \(h_S(p^\star)\ge(\ell/160)2^{-n}W\). The point is selected before the halfspace, so the bound holds for every closed ambient halfspace containing that same point. Finally, \(e>2\) implies \(0<\ell<1\), and \(e<3\) implies \(\ell/160<1/160<1/e\).
