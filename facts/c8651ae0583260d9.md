## Statement

Fix $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$ and $N\ge2$ copies of the reference $d$-simplex in the physical canonical cap Hessian setting. The $m$ budgets are the sums, over the copies, of the volumes in each corresponding fixed canonical cap, with budgets equal to their reference values. On the independent vertex tangent space the aggregate Lagrangian has positive index $dN$. Its positive subspace meets the kernel of the first derivative of the $m$ aggregate caps in dimension at least $dN-m>0$, and the first derivative map onto these caps is surjective. If the copies can vary independently in a neighborhood of their reference positions in the actual fiber family, then there is a degree-two polynomial vertex path making all aggregate cap volumes strictly smaller and the total volume strictly larger for every sufficiently small positive time.

## Proof

Let $F_i^{\rm tot}$ be each aggregate cap volume and $M$ the total simplex volume. By physical-simplex-canonical-cap-hessian-inertia the aggregate Lagrangian $L^{\rm tot}=M-(m\gamma)^{-1}\sum_iF_i^{\rm tot}$ is stationary and its Hessian is a direct sum with positive index $dN$. The kernel of the first-cap map cuts a positive subspace in codimension at most $m$. In one copy, a translation has barycentric velocity matrix $v\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. Its trace is zero and its row sums are $mv_i$, so the cap derivatives span the zero-sum subspace by the accepted derivative formula. A dilation with velocity $I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/m$ has trace $d$ and zero row sums, giving a nonzero common cap derivative. Varying this one copy thus proves surjectivity for the aggregate map.

Choose a nonzero velocity $B$ in a positive subspace and in the kernel of the cap derivative. Along a path with this velocity, $M'$ and all cap first derivatives vanish, by stationarity of $L^{\rm tot}$. By surjectivity, choose an acceleration $C$ such that along the physical vertex path $I+tB+\tfrac12t^2C$, componentwise for the copies, all cap second derivatives are $-\varepsilon$. This is possible because the acceleration adds exactly the first-cap map applied to $C$ to the second derivatives at velocity $B$. The Lagrangian second derivative remains its positive Hessian value on $B$ because its first derivative is zero. Thus $M''=(L^{\rm tot})''-\varepsilon/\gamma>0$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. Smoothness of the exact cap formula and the strict signs give the conclusion. The assumed open compatibility keeps this polynomial path inside the actual family.
