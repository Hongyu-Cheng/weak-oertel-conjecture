## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). Use the mixed-integer volume and closed-halfspace depth of the locked problem. In particular, for measurable \(F\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\),
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap F)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in F\},\qquad
h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H),
\]
where the infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces. Retain the canonical actual-fiber objects
\[
C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\},\quad w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\quad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\},\quad W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S),
\]
and, for \(z\in D_+\),
\[
b_z=w_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}y\,dy,\qquad
\Sigma_z=w_z^{-1}\int_{C_z}(y-b_z)(y-b_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dy,\qquad
\gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
There is one \(p^\star\in S\), selected before any halfspace, such that
\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac1{10^{15}e}\,2^{-2}W=\frac{W}{4\cdot10^{15}e}.
\]
The constant \(c_0=1/(10^{15}e)\) belongs to \((0,1/e)\) and is independent of \(d\) and \(C\). No assumptions on fiber shapes, centroid affine rank, or the size or lattice width of the projection are imposed. If \(W=0\), every point of \(S\) satisfies the inequality.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes the collection of nonempty integer fibers finite. Every fiber is compact and convex, and every positive-volume fiber is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\). The canonical volumes, centroids, and covariances above are the actual ones, with precisely the normalization in accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c. Zero-volume fibers contribute zero to every mixed-integer halfspace mass. If \(W=0\), choose any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\); nonnegativity of mixed-integer volume proves the result. Henceforth \(W>0\).

1. Real-base root-volume concavity, including zero fibers.

Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^2\) be the projection of \(C\) onto its base coordinates. This is nonempty, compact, and convex. For every real \(x\in P\), put \(C_x=\{y:(x,y)\in C\}\), \(w_x=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)\), and \(r(x)=w_x^{1/d}\). This extends the canonical integer objects without changing their values. All these volumes are finite and nonnegative, and all these fibers are nonempty.

For \(x_0,x_1\in P\) with both volumes positive and \(t\in[0,1]\), accepted fact f6641f09c19819a3, with its two bodies \(A_1=C_{x_0}\), \(A_2=C_{x_1}\), its coefficients \(t_1=1-t,t_2=t\), and its containing body \(K=C_{(1-t)x_0+tx_1}\), gives
\[
r((1-t)x_0+tx_1)\ge(1-t)r(x_0)+tr(x_1).
\tag{1}
\]
Here the applicability of its containing-body conclusion can also be read directly from its fiber specialization: convexity gives \((1-t)C_{x_0}+tC_{x_1}\subseteq C_{(1-t)x_0+tx_1}\), and its unit-mass transport with the displayed density bound proves that the target fiber has positive volume. Thus no prior positivity assumption on the target is being used.

If \(w_{x_0}>0\) and \(w_{x_1}=0\), choose any \(y_1\in C_{x_1}\). Convexity gives
\((1-t)C_{x_0}+t\{y_1\}\subseteq C_{(1-t)x_0+tx_1}\). Its volume is \((1-t)^d w_{x_0}\), so (1) follows, including the endpoints. The case \(w_{x_0}=0<w_{x_1}\) follows by interchanging the two endpoints. If both volumes vanish, the right side of (1) is zero and (1) follows from nonnegativity. Consequently \(r:P\to[0,\infty)\) is a finite-valued concave function on the whole real projection, with zero fibers included. This derivation uses the self-contained transport in f6641f09c19819a3 and elementary convexity and scaling; it does not assume a real-index extension of an integer-only interpolation statement.

The finite weighted sum on \(P\cap\mathbb Z^2\) is exactly \(\sum_z r(z)^d=W\). Choose its integer radius maximum
\(R=\max_{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^2}r(z)>0\). Set \(\delta=1/(10000d)\) and \(\omega=2^{27}\). Apply accepted fact b295de5f23afdbe8 with its \(P,r,W,R,\delta,\omega\) equal to these objects and constants. It gives either a closed primitive integer slab of width \(\omega\) carrying at least \(W/2\), or the original top-radius core carrying at least \(W/(3\cdot10^9)\). These choices precede all halfspaces.

2. The slab branch and its actual slice.

Suppose there are a primitive \(u\in\mathbb Z^2\setminus\{0\}\) and an integer \(m_{\rm slab}\) such that
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\m_{\rm slab}\le u\cdot z\le m_{\rm slab}+\omega}}w_z\ge\frac W2.
\]
There are at most \(\omega+1\) integer levels in this closed slab. Choose a heaviest level \(q\in\{m_{\rm slab},\ldots,m_{\rm slab}+\omega\}\), and write
\[
W_q=\sum_{z:\ u\cdot z=q}w_z\ge\frac{W}{2(\omega+1)}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(u\) is primitive, Bezout's identity supplies an integer row vector that, together with \(u\) as the second row, forms a matrix \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\). Define the actual slice, in its intrinsic coordinates, by
\[
K_q=\{(s,y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d:(U^{-1}(s,q),y)\in C\}.
\tag{3}
\]
It is compact and convex. The map \(s\mapsto U^{-1}(s,q)\) bijects integer \(s\) with the original lattice bases at level \(q\). Its fibers are exactly \((K_q)_s=C_{U^{-1}(s,q)}\), and therefore its mixed-integer volume is exactly \(W_q\). All continuous coordinates remain unchanged, so there is no volume Jacobian on these \(d\)-dimensional fibers.

The real base projection of \(K_q\) is a compact interval. If it is nondegenerate, \(K_q\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\): a positive integer fiber contains a \(d\)-dimensional ball, and any slice point at a different real base, together with \(d+1\) affinely independent points of that ball, yields \(d+2\) affinely independent slice points. Their simplex has nonempty ambient interior. Accepted fact 2c2a5d2f1c5eb626 consequently applies with its body \(C\) mapped to \(K_q\), its integer index \(z\) mapped to \(s\), its actual fiber \(C_z\) mapped to \(C_{U^{-1}(s,q)}\), and its volume \(W\) mapped to \(W_q\). It gives one actual point \((s^\star,y^\star)\) of the mixed slice with depth at least \(W_q/(100000e)\) in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\).

If that interval is instead a singleton, (2) ensures that its unique base \(s_0\) is integer. Put \(z_0=U^{-1}(s_0,q)\). The entire slice is \(\{s_0\}\times C_{z_0}\), and \(w_{z_0}=W_q>0\). Apply accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with \(K=C_{z_0}\), \(a=m=w_{z_0}\), and density \(f=1\). Its metronoid contains the true centroid \(b_{z_0}\), so every proper closed continuous halfspace containing that centroid captures at least \(\gamma_d W_q\). The centroid belongs to the fiber. Since
\(d\log(1+1/d)\le1\), one has \(\gamma_d\ge1/e\). Thus the point \((s_0,b_{z_0})\) has slice depth at least \(W_q/e\), in particular at least \(W_q/(100000e)\). A containing slice halfspace with zero continuous normal contains the whole fiber, so the same bound includes that case.

In either case embed the selected slice point by \((s,y)\mapsto(U^{-1}(s,q),y)\), obtaining one actual \(p^\star\in S\). To check the transfer for every ambient halfspace, write
\(H=\{(z,y):\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot y\ge a\}\). Its pullback under this embedding is a closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\) if its normal is nonzero. If that normal is zero, the pullback is either empty or the whole space; because \(H\) contains the selected point, it is the whole space. The pullback contains the selected slice point, and the original mixed volume on \(H\) is at least
\[
\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{vol}_d
\{y\in(K_q)_s:\alpha\cdot U^{-1}(s,q)+\beta\cdot y\ge a\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is exactly the mixed volume of the slice in its pullback. For a whole-space pullback it equals \(W_q\); for a proper pullback the established slice depth applies. Every containing original halfspace therefore has mass at least \(W_q/(100000e)\). By (2),
\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{W}{200000e(2^{27}+1)}
\ge\frac{W}{4\cdot10^{15}e},
\tag{5}
\]
where \(200000(2^{27}+1)=26843545800000<4\cdot10^{15}\). All slab boundaries and all halfspace boundaries were retained.

3. The top-radius core and its complete lattice simplex.

It remains to treat the alternative
\[
E=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^2:r(z)\ge(1-\delta)R\},\qquad
W_E=\sum_{z\in E}w_z\ge\frac{W}{3\cdot10^9}.
\tag{6}
\]
The real superlevel \(\{x\in P:r(x)\ge(1-\delta)R\}\) is convex by (1). Hence \(E\) is finite, nonempty, and satisfies \(E=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\): its convex hull lies in the superlevel, so every integer point of that hull lies in \(E\), and the reverse inclusion is immediate. Every point of \(E\) has positive fiber volume. At integer points the definition of \(R\) and Bernoulli's inequality give
\[
(1-\delta)^dR^d\le w_z\le R^d\quad(z\in E),
\qquad(1-\delta)^d\ge1-d\delta=\frac{9999}{10000}\ge\frac12.
\tag{7}
\]

Apply accepted fact 6af93f68fb881fc3 with its complete lattice set \(D\) mapped to \(E\). In affine rank two choose its maximum-area triangle \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{z_1,z_2,z_3\}\), with the three noncollinear vertices in \(E\). It satisfies \(\#(E\cap T)\ge\#E/8\). In affine rank one choose the two endpoints of \(\operatorname{conv}(E)\), and let \(T\) be their segment, which captures all of \(E\). In rank zero let \(T\) be the unique point, again capturing all of \(E\). In all cases \(T\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E)\), so \(T\cap\mathbb Z^2=E\cap T\), and all its integer fibers are the unchanged original positive fibers. Put \(W_T=\sum_{z\in T\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\).

In rank zero, write \(T=\{z_1\}\). Then \(W_T=W_E=w_{z_1}\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, with exactly the full-mass centroid application made in the singleton slice above, gives the actual point \((z_1,b_{z_1})\) for which every containing original halfspace captures at least \(\gamma_d w_{z_1}\). For a nonzero continuous normal its fiber section is a closed continuous halfspace containing the centroid; for zero continuous normal that section is all of \(\mathbb R^d\). Other fibers add nonnegative mass. Thus its original depth is at least \(W_E/e\ge W/(3\cdot10^9e)\), which implies the target.

In rank one, write the endpoint difference as \(z_2-z_1=h v\), where \(h\ge1\) is integer and \(v\in\mathbb Z^2\) is primitive. Bezout's identity gives \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) with \(Uv=(1,0)\). For clarity, if \(v=(v_1,v_2)\) and \(k_1v_1+k_2v_2=1\), one may take the rows of \(U\) to be \((k_1,k_2)\) and \((-v_2,v_1)\). Both endpoints have the same integer second coordinate \(q\); write their first coordinates as \(s_-<s_+\). The compact convex set
\[
K_T=\{(s,y):s_-\le s\le s_+,\ (U^{-1}(s,q),y)\in C\}
\]
is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\), since its two distinct endpoint fibers have positive volume. Its integer fibers correspond bijectively to \(T\cap\mathbb Z^2=E\), and its mixed volume is \(W_T=W_E\). Apply accepted fact 2c2a5d2f1c5eb626 with its body, fibers, indices, and volume mapped respectively to \(K_T\), \(C_{U^{-1}(s,q)}\), \(s\), and \(W_E\). Its selected actual point embeds into \(S\). The pullback and volume calculation (4), with the extra restriction \(s_-\le s\le s_+\), transfers every containing original halfspace exactly as before, including a zero pullback normal. Therefore
\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{W_E}{100000e}
\ge\frac{W}{3\cdot10^{14}e}
\ge\frac{W}{4\cdot10^{15}e}.
\tag{8}
\]
These two cases exhaust the degenerate affine ranks, regardless of the affine rank of the original centroids.

4. The rank-two core: one affine map and the own-covariance criterion.

Assume now that \(T\) is the nondegenerate triangle chosen above. By (7) and the count bound,
\[
W_T\ge\frac12R^d\,\#(T\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\ge\frac{R^d\#E}{16}
\ge\frac{W_E}{16}
\ge\frac{W}{48\cdot10^9}.
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(W_E\le R^d\#E\), so the third inequality has the displayed direction.

Retain the actual restricted body \(C^T=C\cap(T\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let \(S^T=C^T\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)\). It is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{2+d}\). Indeed, choose balls \(B(y_i,\rho)\subset C_{z_i}\) at the three vertices with a common \(\rho>0\). The unique affine map sending \(z_i\) to \(y_i\) maps every interior base \(x\in T\) to a center whose radius-\(\rho\) ball belongs to \(C_x\), by convex combinations of the three endpoint balls. This supplies an open set in \(\mathbb R^{2+d}\) inside \(C^T\). Compactness and convexity are immediate. Its positive integer support is exactly \(T\cap\mathbb Z^2\), its mixed volume is \(W_T\), and each of its integer fibers, centroids, and covariances agrees with the original \(C_z,b_z,\Sigma_z\) at that index.

Define the unique affine map \(z\mapsto Lz+b\), with \(L\in\mathbb R^{d\times2}\), by \(Lz_i+b=b_{z_i}\) for \(i=1,2,3\). This \(L\) and this intercept \(b\) are exactly the affine-approximation matrix and vector of accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c; neither denotes a centroid affine space. For \(z\in T\), let \(t_i(z)\ge0\) be its barycentric coordinates, with sum one. Then \(Lz+b=\sum_i t_i(z)b_{z_i}\).

For every integer \(z\in T\), apply accepted fact f6641f09c19819a3 with its body-count parameter equal to three, its \(A_i=C_{z_i}\), its coefficients \(t_i=t_i(z)\), and its containing body \(K=C_z\). Its transported mean \(m_t\) is precisely \(Lz+b\), its \(b_K\) is the true \(b_z\), and its \(\Sigma_K\) is the actual own covariance \(\Sigma_z\). Zero barycentric coefficients on faces or vertices are allowed by that lemma. The root-volume denominator in the lemma is
\[
V_z=\left(\sum_{i=1}^3 t_i(z)r(z_i)\right)^d
\ge(1-\delta)^dR^d\ge\frac{9999}{10000}R^d.
\]
Since \(w_z\le R^d\) for every integer \(z\in T\), the lemma's own-covariance inequality gives, simultaneously for every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\bigl|\beta\cdot(b_z-Lz-b)\bigr|
\le\sqrt{\frac{w_z}{V_z}-1}\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}
\le\frac1{\sqrt{9999}}\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}.
\tag{10}
\]
The quantity inside the first square root is nonnegative by that same accepted lemma. No comparison between different fibers' covariances is made.

Set \(\epsilon=1/\sqrt{9999}\). Since \(9999>144\) and \(e<3\), one has \(\epsilon<1/12<1/(4e)\). The elementary bound \(e<3\) follows from its series, using \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\), with strict inequality for \(j=3\). Equation (10) therefore verifies all hypotheses of accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c for \(C^T\), with its \(n=2\), its \(D_+=T\cap\mathbb Z^2\), its \(W=W_T\), its actual \(C_z,b_z,\Sigma_z\) equal to the unchanged original objects on that support, and its affine map exactly \(Lz+b\). It provides one
\(z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(T\cap\mathbb Z^2)\cap\mathbb Z^2\) such that \(p^\star=(z^\star,Lz^\star+b)\in S^T\) and
\[
h_{S^T}(p^\star)\ge\frac{\gamma_d-\epsilon}{4}W_T\ge\frac{W_T}{8e}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the graph point is an actual point of the restricted body; membership is part of the accepted theorem, rather than an unproved assumption about interpolated centroids. The theorem fixes that point before any halfspace and includes all containing closed ambient halfspaces, continuous or vertical.

Since \(S^T\subseteq S\), each such halfspace has original mixed volume at least its mixed volume on \(S^T\). Consequently (9) and (11) yield
\[
h_S(p^\star)\ge\frac{W_T}{8e}
\ge\frac{W}{384\cdot10^9e}
\ge\frac{W}{4\cdot10^{15}e}.
\tag{12}
\]
The last comparison uses \(384\cdot10^9<4\cdot10^{15}\).

The slab branch, all three core ranks, and \(W=0\) exhaust the alternatives. Every restriction kept actual full fibers at its retained integer indices, every embedding preserved their original continuous volume, and discarded fibers only added nonnegative halfspace mass on transfer to \(S\). No normalization was assigned to a zero-volume fiber, including real zero fibers in (1). Every selected point belongs to the original \(S\) and was fixed before the arbitrary containing halfspace. Equations (5), (8), and (12), and the rank-zero cap bound prove the statement.
