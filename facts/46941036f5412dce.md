## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let an actual finite positive family have common-normal simplex fibers $K_z=\{x:\lambda_j(x)\ge b_j(z)\ \forall j\}$ with $r_z=1-\sum_j b_j(z)>0$, in fixed barycentric affine coordinates. Suppose one regular canonical circuit through $(p,y)$ consists of the $d+1$ caps $\lambda_j(x)\ge L_j(z)$, where $L_j$ are affine in the base and $L_j(p)=\lambda_j(y)$. Every trace is proper and every cap has the same raw mass $Q=\beta M$, with $M$ the original total mixed volume. Use the circuit's positive coarea response weights $\theta_j$ normalized to sum to one. Suppose every single-facet offset is two-sided feasible and the common circuit's first response relative to $\beta M$ vanishes on each such offset. Then for every $j,z$,
$$
\frac{L_j(z)-b_j(z)}{r_z}=\frac1{d+1},\qquad
\theta_j=\frac1{d+1},\qquad
\beta=\left(\frac{d}{d+1}\right)^d.
$$
The centroid $g_z$ of $K_z$ satisfies $\lambda_j(g_z)=L_j(z)$, so the actual centroid graph is affine.

## Proof

Put $u_j=\nabla\lambda_j$, with $\sum_j u_j=0$. Coarea in the scalar $\lambda_j$ coordinate is total section measure divided by $\lVert u_j\rVert$, denoted by $S_j>0$. In the equal-budget inverse the weights are $(1/S_j)/\sum_h(1/S_h)$, since the unscaled normals balance with coefficients one. Equivalently, normalizing the continuous normals to unit length replaces these balance coefficients by $\lVert u_j\rVert$, yielding the same weights. For each individual signed offset motion use the regular response formula of single-free-simplex-facet-menu-response, or its inverse calculation for the given two-sided facet motion. The weighted boundary trace on that facet must have mean $\beta$.

Write $q_j(z)=(L_j(z)-b_j(z))/r_z\in(0,1)$ and denote the vertices of $K_z$ by $v_j(z)$, with relative barycentric coordinate $j$ equal to one. On facet $i$ cap $i$ is absent. For $j\ne i$ the cap's trace is $q_j(z)v_j(z)+(1-q_j(z))F_i(z)$, of relative $(d-1)$-area $(1-q_j(z))^{d-1}$. Its whole-fiber fraction is similarly $(1-q_j(z))^d$. The facet equalities give
$$
\sum_{j\ne i}\theta_j(1-q_j(z))^{d-1}=\beta
\quad\text{for every }i,z.
$$
For each $z$, subtract the equations for two choices of $i$ and substitute back to get $\theta_i(1-q_i(z))^{d-1}=\beta/d$. As $d\ge2$ and each $\theta_i>0$, $q_i(z)$ is independent of $z$. The common global cap budget is now exactly $(1-q_i)^d M$ for each $i$, so all $q_i$ coincide. At $p$, $\sum_i q_i(p)=(\sum_i\lambda_i(y)-\sum_i b_i(p))/r_p=1$. This gives the displayed threshold, cap fraction, and weights. The ordinary simplex centroid has barycentric coordinates $b_i(z)+r_z/(d+1)=L_i(z)$. The first $d$ independent barycentric coordinates determine a point by an affine inverse, so their affine dependence on $z$ gives an affine centroid graph.
