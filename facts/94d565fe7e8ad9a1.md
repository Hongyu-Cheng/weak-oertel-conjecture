## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge2\), put \(K=2^n\) and define the full-dimensional convex body and its complete lattice support by
$$
Q_n=\lbrace x\in[-1,1]^n:x_i-x_j\le1\text{ for every }1\le i,j\le n\rbrace,
\qquad A_n=Q_n\cap\mathbb Z^n.
$$
Then
$$
A_n=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\cup\bigl(-\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\bigr),
\qquad Z:=|A_n|=2K-1.
$$
Give every point original weight \(w_x=1\) and original probability \(p_x=w_x/Z=1/Z\), and extend \(p\) by zero off \(A_n\). The support is midpoint-closed whenever the midpoint is integral, every parity class has at most two points, and \(p\) is midpoint-log-concave.

For a supported center \(x\), define precisely the auxiliary restricted mass and its second moment by
$$
\widehat H(x)=p_x+2\max_{\substack{u,v\in A_n,\ u\ne v\\u+v=2x}}\sqrt{p_up_v},
\qquad \widehat b=\sum_{x\in A_n}p_x\widehat H(x)^2,
$$
where the maximum is zero for an empty collection and each unordered endpoint pair is considered once. Then
$$
K^2\widehat b=\frac{K^2(2K+7)}{(2K-1)^3}<1,
\qquad \lim_{n\to\infty}K^2\widehat b=\frac14.
$$
For any finite nonempty \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) with every parity class of size at most two, and any positive probability law \(p\) on \(A\), the same definition always satisfies \(K^2\widehat b\ge1/4\), whether or not midpoint closure or midpoint log concavity holds. Thus \(1/4\) is the best universal constant for the selector in the question.

Retain exactly the actual depth and canonical reflected notation of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(x)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
 \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }x\rbrace,\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_xp_x\tau_p(x)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_xp_x\tau_p(x)^3,\\
H_p(x)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{x+v}p_{x-v}},\qquad
b(p)=\sum_xp_xH_p(x)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(H_p\) is the full oriented zero-inclusive sum, whereas the hat denotes only a selected singleton or triple. The definition of \(M_3\) is the original third depth moment; it introduces no change to the predecessor objects. For the displayed family,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(0)&=\frac KZ>\frac12,\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\frac1Z\quad(x\in A_n\setminus\lbrace0\rbrace),\\
M_2(p)&=\frac{K^2+2K-2}{(2K-1)^3},\qquad
M_3(p)=\frac{K^3+2K-2}{(2K-1)^4},\\
K^2M_2(p)&\ge1,\qquad K^3M_3(p)>1.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Proof

First check the support exactly. An integer point in \([-1,1]^n\) has every coordinate in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\). The difference inequalities in \(Q_n\) forbid the simultaneous occurrence of a coordinate \(1\) and a coordinate \(-1\). Therefore every integer point of \(Q_n\) is either Boolean or the negative of a Boolean point. Conversely, all such points satisfy every displayed inequality. The two Boolean cubes meet only at the origin, proving \(|A_n|=2K-1\). The set \(Q_n\) is compact and convex and contains the cube \([-1/4,1/4]^n\) in its interior, so it is a full-dimensional convex body. This proves that \(A_n\) is a complete convex lattice support.

The zero parity class consists only of the origin. For every nonzero \(c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), its parity class consists exactly of the two points \(c,-c\). In particular every parity class has size at most two, and every distinct pair in a common parity class has midpoint zero. Two lattice points have an integral midpoint exactly when their parities agree. These observations prove midpoint closure and show that zero is the midpoint of all \(K-1\) distinct endpoint pairs. The uniform original weights give equality in every required midpoint log-concavity inequality.

If two distinct points \(u,v\in A_n\) are reflected about a lattice center, they have equal parity. Hence they must be \(c,-c\) for some nonzero Boolean \(c\), and their center must be zero. Consequently the full supported reflected rows are
$$
R_0=A_n,\qquad R_x=\lbrace x\rbrace\quad(x\ne0).
$$
At zero all endpoint geometric means are \(1/Z\), so choosing any maximizing pair gives exactly the same restricted mass. Thus
$$
\widehat H(0)=\frac3Z,\qquad
\widehat H(x)=\frac1Z\quad(x\ne0),\qquad
\widehat b=\frac{(Z-1)+9}{Z^3}=\frac{2K+7}{(2K-1)^3}.
$$
Allowing the singleton at zero cannot increase this moment. Thus the displayed value is already the largest moment obtainable by retaining at most one endpoint pair per center.

For \(K\ge4\), the exact difference is
$$
(2K-1)^3-K^2(2K+7)
=K^2(6K-19)+6K-1
\ge5K^2+6K-1>0.
$$
This proves the failure of threshold \(1\) for every \(n\ge2\). Dividing numerator and denominator of \(K^2\widehat b\) by \(K^3\) gives its limit \(1/4\).

For the universal lower bound, let \(m=|A|\). The parity-class hypothesis gives \(m\le2K\). Since every restricted row retains its central atom, \(\widehat H(x)\ge p_x\). Holder's inequality gives \(1=(\sum_xp_x)^3\le m^2\sum_xp_x^3\). Hence
$$
K^2\widehat b\ge K^2\sum_xp_x^3\ge\frac{K^2}{m^2}\ge\frac14.
$$
Together with the complete convex family whose gains tend to \(1/4\), this proves sharpness of the universal constant.

We now verify the actual depths in the original ambient space, keeping exactly the definitions of ce8cccfdbee6f1cc. Every closed halfspace containing zero contains at least one endpoint of each antipodal pair \(c,-c\), as otherwise the linear inequality defining it would also exclude their midpoint zero. It also contains the origin itself. These are \(K\) disjoint required atoms, so its mass is at least \(K/Z\). The closed halfspace \(\sum_i x_i\ge0\) contains precisely the \(K\) Boolean points and no nonzero negative Boolean point. It therefore attains that lower bound and proves \(\tau_p(0)=K/Z\).

Every nonzero supported point is exposed among the support points, as the following explicit calculation shows. For a nonzero Boolean \(c\), put \(I_c=\lbrace i:c_i=1\rbrace\), which is nonempty. Define
$$
f_c(x)=n\sum_{i\in I_c}x_i-\sum_{i\notin I_c}x_i.
$$
On the Boolean cube, the unique maximizer is \(c\), with value \(n|I_c|\). On the negative Boolean cube, the maximum is \(n-|I_c|\). Since \(n|I_c|\ge n>n-|I_c|\), the point \(c\) is also the unique maximizer over all \(A_n\). Therefore the closed halfspace \(f_c(x)\ge n|I_c|\) contains only \(c\) from the support. For \(-c\) use the negative of the same functional. Each nonzero supported point consequently has depth at most \(1/Z\), and the mass of the target atom gives the reverse inequality. Thus \(\tau_p(x)=1/Z\) for every \(x\ne0\).

There are \(2K-2\) nonzero supported points. Averaging their computed depths and the origin depth against exactly the original uniform law gives
$$
M_2(p)=\frac{2K-2+K^2}{Z^3},\qquad
M_3(p)=\frac{2K-2+K^3}{Z^4}.
$$
For the second moment, the exact numerator comparison is
$$
K^2(K^2+2K-2)-(2K-1)^3
=(K-1)^2(K^2-4K+1)>0\qquad(K\ge4).
$$
For the third moment,
$$
K^3M_3(p)\ge\frac{K^6}{(2K-1)^4}
>\frac{K^2}{16}\ge1\qquad(K\ge4).
$$
This proves all the asserted actual depth calibrations.

In the smallest example \(n=2\), the body \(Q_2\) is the hexagon defined by \(|x_1|\le1\), \(|x_2|\le1\), \(|x_1-x_2|\le1\), and
$$
A_2=\lbrace(0,0),\ \pm(1,0),\ \pm(0,1),\ \pm(1,1)\rbrace,
\qquad K=4,\quad Z=7.
$$
The exact values are \(K^2\widehat b=240/343<1\), \(M_2(p)=22/343\), \(K^2M_2(p)=352/343>1\), and \(K^3M_3(p)=640/343>1\). To distinguish the full canonical reflection from the selected triple explicitly, \(H_p(0)=1\), \(H_p(x)=1/7\) for \(x\ne0\), and \(b(p)=55/343\) in this example. The failed threshold therefore concerns only the restricted selector. It neither contradicts a lower bound for the full canonical reflected mass nor supplies a failure of actual depth or of the locked weak Oertel statement.
