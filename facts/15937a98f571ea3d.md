## Statement

Let $Q$ be a full-dimensional convex pentagon in the plane. Assume some invertible affine map $A$ gives $\delta=d_{H,\infty}(AQ,T)\le10^{-6}$, where $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$. Use the enclosing triangle $S$ obtained, in the normalized frame, by choosing actual sides through the leftmost point of $AQ$ on $y=1/2$, the bottommost on $x=1/2$, and the last point on $x=y$. Set $\tau=1-|Q|/|S|$ in the original frame. The two unused side inequalities remove two positive-area vertex triangles $R_1,R_2$ up to null boundaries. Each incident edge fraction is at most $1/2+44\delta<501/1000$, and at least one per piece is at most $\sqrt{\tau}<1/100$. For $t_j=|R_j|/|S|$, one has $t_j\le\tau\le81\delta$ and $\tau=t_1+t_2-|R_1\cap R_2|/|S|$, with zero intersection area when the deleted vertices differ. For the uniform probability measures and the supremum $d_{\rm cap}$ of their absolute probability difference over closed halfspaces, the same $S$ satisfies
$$
D(Q)-4/9\ge\tau/180,\qquad
\tau/2\le d_{\rm cap}(\mu_Q,\mu_S)\le\tau ,
$$
where $D(Q)$ is maximal normalized ordinary halfspace depth. Consequently $\inf_{S'}d_{\rm cap}(\mu_Q,\mu_{S'})\le180[D(Q)-4/9]$ over full-dimensional triangles.

## Proof

In the normalized frame take an actual pentagon side at each of the three contacts used in 890c4a5b1a27a090. At a vertex either incident side is a support, and the predecessor's slope bounds apply to every such choice. The bounds make the three lines distinct and give a nondegenerate triangle. In its barycentric frame the retained edge contacts are each within $44\delta$ of the respective midpoints in the corresponding edge parameter. For the diagonal edge this parameter is the first standard coordinate, and for the other edges it is the nonconstant standard coordinate. The same proof gives $\tau\le81\delta<1/10000$.

A full-dimensional pentagon is the intersection of its five actual side halfplanes. Each unused side strictly excludes a point of $S$: otherwise the three selected and the other unused inequality would already define $Q$, contradicting its five actual sides. It therefore strictly excludes a vertex of $S$. If another vertex on either incident edge were outside or on that cut, its retained interior contact would be strictly outside. Thus each cut strictly retains both other vertices and removes a positive-area vertex triangle. The incident contacts give the claimed intercept bounds. Their product is the relative area $t_j$, and $t_j\le\tau$ by containment of the strict deletion in the missing set. If the two removed vertices differ, let $a,b$ be their intercepts on their common edge, each measured from its removed vertex. The selected side of $Q$ on that edge has positive length, so $a+b<1$. At a common point with third barycentric coordinate $v<1$, the other coordinates are bounded above by $a(1-v)$ and $b(1-v)$ because the two off-edge intercepts are at most one. This contradicts their sum $1-v$. The third vertex is strictly retained by both cuts. Inclusion–exclusion now gives the area assertion in both incidence types.

The same triangle in 890c4a5b1a27a090 has $D(Q)-4/9\ge\tau/180$ and $d_{\rm cap}(\mu_Q,\mu_S)\le\tau$. Indeed its proof used any support at each of these three contacts, and all hypotheses hold for this positive-area pentagon with the same infinity Hausdorff radius. For each unused side the corresponding exterior closed halfspace has zero $Q$-area and $S$-probability $t_j$. Thus the cap distance is at least $\max_j t_j\ge\tau/2$. All normalized areas and cap distances are invariant under the inverse affine map. The infimum bound follows by using this $S$.
