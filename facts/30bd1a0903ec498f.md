## Statement

For each $d\ge2$ there are actual mixed-integer convex bodies with one integer coordinate, all fibers strictly below half the total mixed mass $M$, and arbitrarily many distinct source fibers with failing residual midpoint rows pointing to the same unique surviving endpoint of volume one. Every edge uses a genuine closed oblique cap of own-fiber fraction $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and original mixed cap mass strictly below $\gamma_d M/2$. The total incoming original source volume is unbounded at each fixed $d$.

## Proof

For integer $L\ge2$ let $N=40dL$ and
$$
C=\{(z,x):-N\le z\le0,\quad 0\le x_\ell\le1+z/N\ \ (1\le\ell\le d)\}.
$$
This is a rational full-dimensional compact convex body. At integer $z=k-N$ its fiber is $(k/N)[0,1]^d$ for $0\le k\le N$. The total mass is $M=\sum_{k=0}^N(k/N)^d\ge N/(d+1)>2$ by the right Riemann sum, and the largest fiber has volume one. Set $i_j=-N/2+j$ and $c_j=1+i_j/N$ for $1\le j\le2L$. The midpoint of bases $2i_j$ and $0$ is $i_j$; their original volume roots are $2j/N$ and $1$, with mean $c_j$. In particular $1/2<c_j\le1/2+1/(20d)\le21/40$.

Use the closed cap
$$
H_j=\{(z,x):x_1-2(z-i_j)\ge(1-\gamma_d)c_j\}.
$$
It captures fraction $\gamma_d$ at $i_j$ by the cube coordinate distribution. Every lower integer fiber is captured (the left side is at least two) and every higher one missed (the left side is at most minus one). The residual roots at $2i_j,i_j,0$ are $0,c_j(1-\gamma_d)^{1/d},1$. To see strict failure, $\gamma_d>1/3$ since $(1+1/d)^d<\sum_{k\ge0}1/k!<3$, the last inequality following from $1/k!\le2^{1-k}$ for $k\ge2$ with strict terms. The concavity tangent at one gives
$$
2c_j(1-\gamma_d)^{1/d}
<(1+1/(10d))(1-1/(3d))<1.
$$
Thus zero is the unique surviving endpoint of every row. The strict volume-growth factor in the existing forest theorem is also explicit: $1>2^d(1-\gamma_d)c_j^d$.

Write $m_j=N+i_j$. The original cap mass is $\sum_{k=0}^{m_j-1}(k/N)^d+\gamma_dc_j^d$. The left Riemann sum bounds the sum by $Nc_j^{d+1}/(d+1)$. Since $d\ge2$, $\gamma_d<1/2$ by the binomial formula, and $N\ge30(d+1)$, we get
$$
\frac{\mu(H_j)}M\le c_j^{d+1}+\frac{\gamma_d(d+1)}N
< (21/40)^3+1/60
=\frac{30983}{192000}<\frac16<\frac{\gamma_d}2.
$$
The continuous cap boundary has zero volume on every positive-volume fiber. There are $2L$ incoming sources and their total volume exceeds $2L/2^d$. Each of the selected caps misses base zero, so these caps do not form a cover. All counts and volumes are in the original body.
