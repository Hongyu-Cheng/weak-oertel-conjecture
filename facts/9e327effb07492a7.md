## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) be finite and nonempty, with \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). Suppose every point of \(D\) has at most two coordinates equal to two. No inclusion \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\subseteq D\) is assumed. Put \(N=k+1\) and \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\). For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), define
$$
w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad
p_{(x,0)}=\frac{w_x}{Z}\quad(x\in D).
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) solely as an abbreviation. For \(z\in A\), define the depth using all containing closed affine halfspaces and define its cubic moment by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; allowing the whole space additionally does not change the infimum. Define the full ordered-reflection sum and its cubic by
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y)
=\sum_{\substack{x\in D\\2c-x\in D}}\min(p_x,p_{2c-x}),\\
T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c\,s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here the diagonal \((c,c)\) is counted once, and each unordered distinct reflected pair is counted twice. The symbols \(k,D,A,N,\beta,w,Z,p,\tau_p,M_3,s_p,T\) agree with accepted fact \(f27ee487069e27e7\).

For \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), put \(V_b=\lbrace i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace:b_i=0\rbrace\). Given \(\theta>0\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z\), define a simple graph \(G_{b,j}(D,p,\theta)\) on \(V_b\) by putting an edge \(\lbrace i,\ell\rbrace\), where \(i\ne\ell\), precisely when
$$
x=b+2e_i+2e_\ell\in D,
\qquad
\theta 2^{-j-1}<p_x\le\theta 2^{-j}.
$$
Thus its edges correspond exactly to the double points of parity \(b\) in that probability band. Say that \((D,p)\) has the band-paritywise-star property if there is a single \(\theta>0\) such that, for every \(b\) and every \(j\), all edges of each nonempty \(G_{b,j}(D,p,\theta)\) contain a common vertex. That vertex may depend on both \(b\) and \(j\); an empty graph satisfies the property.

At least one of the following alternatives holds:

(i) Both original cubics satisfy
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N},\qquad
T(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N}.
$$

(ii) There is a nonempty set \(E_*\subseteq D\) of points with exactly two coordinates equal to two such that, with
$$
D_*=\operatorname{conv}(E_*)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad
\lambda=\sum_{x\in D_*}p_x,
\qquad
p_x^*=\frac{p_x}{\lambda}\quad(x\in D_*),
$$
one has \(D_*\subseteq D\), \(D_*=\operatorname{conv}(D_*)\cap\mathbb Z^k\), and \(\lambda\ge1/32\). The double points of \(D_*\) are exactly \(E_*\). In particular every point of \(D_*\) has at most two twos. The probabilities \(p^*\) are the original affine law with the same \(\beta\) restricted to the actual subset \(D_*\), and \((D_*,p^*)\) has the band-paritywise-star property for some common scale \(\theta>0\).

Consequently suppose there is an absolute \(\kappa>0\) such that, for every \(k\ge1\), every finite nonempty complete max-two ternary support, and every original affine law on it admitting the band-paritywise-star property, both cubics, defined in ambient dimension \(k+1\) as above, are at least \(\kappa8^{-(k+1)}\). Then every original law in the present statement satisfies
$$
M_3(p)\ge\min(2^{-40},2^{-20}\kappa)\,8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge\min(2^{-40},2^{-20}\kappa)\,8^{-N}.
$$
All assertions hold for every affine rank of the support.

## Proof

Throughout write \(\tau_p(x)=\tau_p((x,0))\), and put \(Q=2^k\). We first record the restriction and reflection comparisons with the original masses.

For a nonempty subset \(U\subseteq D\), let \(\lambda_U=\sum_{x\in U}p_x\), and let \(p^U_x=p_x/\lambda_U\) be its conditioned law. Its normalization is \(Z_U=\sum_{x\in U}w_x=\lambda_U Z\), so its affine field is exactly \(\beta\). Define all of its depths and reflection sums on \(U\times\lbrace0\rbrace\) in the same ambient \(\mathbb R^N\). Every containing halfspace has original mass at least \(\lambda_U\) times its conditional mass. Taking infima, and restricting original reflection sums to pairs from \(U\), gives, for \(c\in U\),
$$
\tau_p(c)\ge\lambda_U\tau_{p^U}(c),\qquad
s_p(c)\ge\lambda_U s_{p^U}(c).
$$
All summands are nonnegative, and \(p_c=\lambda_U p_c^U\). Therefore
$$
M_3(p)\ge\lambda_U^4 M_3(p^U),\qquad
T(p)\ge\lambda_U^4 T(p^U).
$$
In particular no conditional contribution is transferred with less than its fourth power of original mass.

For \(c\in D\), put
$$
R(c)=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
\qquad
S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4.
$$
Every containing closed halfspace contains the center atom. Its depth is therefore at least \(p_c\), and \(s_p(c)\ge p_c\). Hence \(M_3(p)\ge S_4\) and \(T(p)\ge S_4\). Also, distinct unordered reflected pairs about \(c\) have disjoint endpoints, because the partner of an endpoint \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\). Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains at least one endpoint of each such pair: two endpoints strictly outside would have their average strictly outside. Consequently \(\tau_p(c)\ge R(c)/2\), while \(s_p(c)\ge R(c)\).

Let \(B=D\cap\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), and define the global Boolean-midpoint sum
$$
L_B=\sum_{c\in B}p_c^{1/3}R(c).
$$
Hölder's inequality and \(|B|\le Q\) give
$$
L_B^3\le Q^2\sum_{c\in B}p_cR(c)^3,
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac{L_B^3}{8Q^2},\qquad
T(p)\ge\frac{L_B^3}{Q^2}.
$$
These inequalities also hold when \(B\) is empty, with \(L_B=0\). For any reflected endpoints, the unchanged affine weights give \(p_c^2=p_xp_y\). Thus the summand contributed to \(L_B\) by an ordered distinct pair with Boolean midpoint is
$$
p_c^{1/3}\min(p_x,p_y)
=\min(p_x,p_y)^{7/6}\max(p_x,p_y)^{1/6}.
$$
This counts ordered pairs exactly as in \(R(c)\).

If \(S_4\ge2^{-40}Q^{-3}\), the atomic comparisons already yield both cubics at least \(2^{-37}8^{-N}\), since \(Q^{-3}=8\,8^{-N}\). This implies (i). We henceforth assume
$$
S_4<2^{-40}Q^{-3}.
$$

Let \(D_1\) be the actual subset of points with at most one two, and let \(\alpha=\sum_{x\in D_1}p_x\). If \(D_1\) is nonempty, it is complete. Indeed, an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_1)\) belongs to \(D\) by completeness of \(D\). If it had two coordinates equal to two, every point with positive coefficient in a convex representation would equal two in both coordinates, since every coordinate is at most two. This contradicts the definition of \(D_1\). It therefore belongs to \(D_1\).

If \(\alpha\ge1/4\), apply the final, no-Boolean-containment, one-step assertion of accepted fact \(f27ee487069e27e7\) to \(D_1\) and the conditioned law \(p^{D_1}\). The exact normalization map is \(D\mapsto D_1\), \(Z\mapsto Z_{D_1}=\alpha Z\), \(p\mapsto p^{D_1}=p/\alpha\), with unchanged \(k,N,\beta\) and the same definitions of depth and full reflection sums. If \(q_{D_1}\) is its number of occupied parity classes, that accepted assertion gives both conditional cubics at least \(1/(512q_{D_1}^3)\ge1/(512Q^3)\). The restriction comparisons give both original cubics at least
$$
\frac{\alpha^4}{512Q^3}\ge2^{-17}Q^{-3}=2^{-14}8^{-N}.
$$
Again (i) follows. Thus it remains to treat \(\alpha<1/4\), allowing \(D_1\) to be empty.

Set \(t_0=2^{-10}/Q\). The original mass of all points with \(p_x>t_0\), and hence of all such double points, is bounded by
$$
\sum_{\substack{x\in D\\p_x>t_0}}p_x
\le t_0^{-3}S_4<2^{-10}.
$$
Let \(F\) be the set of double points with \(p_x\le t_0\). Since all other points belong to \(D_1\), its original mass satisfies
$$
\sum_{x\in F}p_x>\frac34-2^{-10}.
$$
For \(j\ge0\), write \(t_j=t_0 2^{-j}\), and partition \(F\) by its parity \(b\) and the bands \((t_j/2,t_j]\). Every positive probability at most \(t_0\) lies in exactly one such band, including probabilities equal to a band endpoint by the stated half-open convention. Every double point of parity \(b\) has the unique form \(b+2e_i+2e_\ell\) with \(i,\ell\in V_b\), \(i\ne\ell\). Consequently each nonempty part is the edge set of the simple graph \(G_{b,j}(D,p,t_0)\).

For one such graph let \(n\) be its number of edges and \(\Delta\) its maximum degree. Call the part rich if \(\Delta\ge n/4\), and diffuse otherwise. In a rich part choose a maximum-degree vertex and retain precisely its incident edges. This retains at least \(n/4\) edges. Since the probabilities of the double points corresponding to these edges lie in \((t_j/2,t_j]\), the retained mass is at least \(1/8\) of the entire part's original mass: the former is at least \(nt_j/8\), while the latter is at most \(nt_j\).

We give the needed diffuse count explicitly. If the degrees of a simple graph are \(d_i\), the number of ordered pairs of distinct edges that meet is \(\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)\), since distinct simple edges share at most one vertex. Thus the number of ordered disjoint edge pairs is
$$
\begin{aligned}
n(n-1)-\sum_i d_i(d_i-1)
&=n^2+n-\sum_i d_i^2\\
&\ge n^2+n-2n\Delta.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(\sum_i d_i=2n\). In a diffuse graph \(\Delta<n/4\), so this count is greater than \(n^2/2+n\), in particular at least \(n^2/4\). Empty parts are ignored. For \(1\le n\le4\) one has \(\Delta\ge1\ge n/4\), so every such part is rich; there is no exceptional small diffuse graph.

First suppose that the total original mass in rich parts is at least \(1/4\). Apply the incident-star selection in each rich part, and let \(E_*\) be the set of all selected double points. Its original mass is at least \(1/32\), so it is nonempty. Define \(D_*=\operatorname{conv}(E_*)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). Since \(E_*\subseteq D\) and \(D\) is complete, \(D_*\subseteq D\). Also \(E_*\subseteq D_*\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E_*)\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(D_*)=\operatorname{conv}(E_*)\) and \(D_*\) is complete. Its original mass \(\lambda\) is at least the mass of \(E_*\), hence at least \(1/32\).

Convex completion adds no double points. To see this, let \(z\in D_*\) have twos at distinct coordinates \(i,\ell\), and take a convex representation of \(z\) by points of \(E_*\), retaining only positive coefficients. Every such point must have twos at both \(i,\ell\), because its coordinates are at most two. Its remaining coordinates are binary, by the max-two budget. The remaining coordinates of \(z\) are therefore binary as well. An average of binary entries equal to zero or one forces every positive-coefficient entry to have that same value. Every point in the representation is consequently exactly \(z\), proving \(z\in E_*\). The reverse inclusion is immediate. This proves that the double points of \(D_*\) are exactly \(E_*\).

Conditioning uses \(p_x^*=p_x/\lambda=w_x/(\lambda Z)\) with the same field \(\beta\). Choose the single scale \(\theta=t_0/\lambda\). For every \(j\ge0\),
$$
\theta 2^{-j-1}<p_x^*\le\theta 2^{-j}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_j/2<p_x\le t_j.
$$
In each parity and each such band, the double points are exactly the edges selected from that part, which all contain the selected vertex, or the graph is empty. Every double point of \(D_*\) has original probability at most \(t_0\); thus all its conditional probabilities are at most \(\theta\), and the bands with \(j<0\) contain no double points. This proves the property for all integer bands, with any boundary equalities preserved by the rescaling, and proves alternative (ii). No Boolean or other point absent from \(D\) has been added.

It remains to show (i) when the total original mass in rich parts is less than \(1/4\). The diffuse parts then have total original mass
$$
m_{\mathrm{diff}}>\frac12-2^{-10}>\frac14.
$$
For each \(j\ge0\), let \(n_j\) be their total number of edge points in band \(j\), and \(m_j\) their total original mass. Write \(n_{b,j}\) for the edge count in a diffuse part, and take it to be zero for a rich or empty part. There are at most \(Q\) parities. The graph count and Cauchy-Schwarz give at least
$$
\frac14\sum_b n_{b,j}^2\ge\frac{n_j^2}{4Q}
$$
ordered disjoint edge pairs, all within individual diffuse parts in band \(j\).

Each such pair corresponds to distinct double points \(x=b+2e_i+2e_\ell\) and \(y=b+2e_a+2e_d\), where the two edges are disjoint. Their midpoint is \(c=b+e_i+e_\ell+e_a+e_d\), which is Boolean because all four indices lie in \(V_b\). It is integral and belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\), so completeness puts this actual Boolean point in \(B\). Thus the pair contributes to the global sum \(L_B\). Both endpoint probabilities exceed \(t_j/2\), so its contribution is at least \((t_j/2)^{4/3}\ge t_j^{4/3}/4\). No ordered endpoint pair is counted in two of these selections: its endpoints determine its band and parity. Multiple selected pairs with the same Boolean midpoint are all correctly included in \(R(c)\).

Since \(m_j\le n_jt_j\), summing these contributions gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_B
&\ge\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{t_j^{4/3}}4\frac{n_j^2}{4Q}\\
&\ge\frac1{16Q}\sum_{j\ge0}m_j^2t_j^{-2/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
There are only finitely many nonempty parts, since \(D\) is finite. Cauchy-Schwarz, using the full summable sequence of band weights, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sum_{j\ge0}m_j\right)^2
&\le\left(\sum_{j\ge0}m_j^2t_j^{-2/3}\right)
\left(\sum_{j\ge0}t_j^{2/3}\right),\\
\sum_{j\ge0}t_j^{2/3}
&=\frac{t_0^{2/3}}{1-2^{-2/3}}<3t_0^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last strict inequality follows from \(2^{-2/3}<2/3\), equivalently \(27<32\) after cubing. Because \(\sum_jm_j=m_{\mathrm{diff}}>1/4\), it follows that
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}m_j^2t_j^{-2/3}>\frac1{48t_0^{2/3}}.
$$
Moreover \(t_0^{2/3}=2^{-20/3}Q^{-2/3}\le2^{-6}Q^{-2/3}\). Therefore
$$
L_B>\frac1{768Q\,t_0^{2/3}}
\ge\frac1{12}Q^{-1/3}
>\frac1{16}Q^{-1/3}.
$$
Applying the global Boolean-midpoint inequalities proves
$$
M_3(p)>2^{-15}Q^{-3}=2^{-12}8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)>2^{-12}Q^{-3}=2^{-9}8^{-N}.
$$
Both imply (i). This completes the dichotomy.

Finally, under the stated star-class premise, case (i) gives the claimed coefficient directly. In case (ii), apply that premise to the actual complete support \(D_*\) and its law \(p^*\), using the common scale just constructed. This law has the same \(k,N,\beta\) as required and needs no Boolean containment. The original-mass restriction inequalities yield both original cubics at least
$$
\lambda^4\kappa8^{-N}\ge2^{-20}\kappa8^{-N}.
$$
The two cases give the minimum in the statement. All convex-completion, closed-halfspace, reflection, and conditioning arguments were made in the original ambient space and do not assume full affine rank. The cases with no double points fall in the one-step branch, and all empty graphs and bands were explicitly harmless. The conclusion is only the stated conditional reduction; it supplies no proof of the uniform star-class premise.
