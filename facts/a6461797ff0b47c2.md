## Statement

In the local-minimum setting of the exact fixed-normal hinge chamber theorem, suppose there is exactly one cover group. Restrict to a linear subspace of directions on which $v_F(-h)=-v_F(h)$ at every sampled facet. For a direction $(h,s)$ let $(L,r_j)$ be its equalization with contact budget $\alpha=\min_a A_a(h,s)$, and let $\sigma=\max_a A_a(h,s)-\alpha$. With the exact cap losses as in that theorem, set
$$
E_j=R_j(h,r_j)+R_j(-h,-r_j),\qquad
\Delta_j=R_j(-h,\cdot)^{-1}(-L)+r_j.
$$
Then $\sum_j\rho_j\Delta_j\ge-\sigma$. Each $\Delta_j$ is the signed integral of the reciprocal threshold slope (evaluated at the reversed inverse threshold) from loss $-L+E_j$ to loss $-L$. In particular $\operatorname{sign}\Delta_j=-\operatorname{sign}E_j$. With one contact, at least one selected cap has $E_j\le0$ for every direction. On fixed branches for the equalization and both inverse evaluations the inequality is linear.

## Proof

The maximum of finitely many linear inward speeds is convex. Oddness on the subspace makes it concave there too, so each $v_F$ is linear. The total loss and each individual contact response are then odd, since they are linear in the speeds and in $s$. Thus the budget on reversing $(h,s)$ is $-\max_a A_a=-\alpha-\sigma$. Let $L^-$ denote that reversed equalized loss. Absence of a strictly descending direction in the one group gives both $L-\beta D+M\tau s\le0$ and $L^-+\beta D-M\tau s\le0$, with the scale terms omitted at fixed scale. Hence $L+L^-\le0$.

The exact loss formula also makes the sign content explicit: the ordinary linear loss terms cancel in $E_j$, adding the two inside maxima gives $\sum_{\rm in}|F||v_F-r_j/b_j|$, and adding the two outside minima gives $-\sum_{\rm out}|F||v_F+r_j/b_j|$.

The function $\ell\mapsto\sum_j\rho_jR_j(-h,\cdot)^{-1}(\ell)$ is strictly increasing and has value $-\alpha-\sigma$ at $L^-$. At $-L\ge L^-$ it is at least that value. Add $\sum_j\rho_j r_j=\alpha$ to obtain the asserted bound. At threshold $-r_j$ the reversed loss is $-L+E_j$. The inverse is piecewise affine with positive finite slopes, equal almost everywhere to the reciprocal forward slope at the inverse threshold. Integrating this derivative gives exactly $\Delta_j$ and its sign. For a single contact $\sigma=0$, and all $E_j>0$ would give all $\Delta_j<0$, contradicting the bound. With fixed branch choices the equations for the forward and inverse losses are linear and each threshold slope is a fixed positive constant, which gives the linear inequality on those branches.
