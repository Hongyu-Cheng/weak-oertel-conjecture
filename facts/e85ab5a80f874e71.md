## Statement

Let $\mathcal T$ be the ordinary strict triangle row image, equivalently the set of $f$ with $f_i=2\sqrt{u_i(1-u_{i+1})}$ for cyclic indices, $0<u_i<1$, and $1<\sum_i u_i<2$. For $f\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^3$ define $B(f)$ to have zero diagonal and symmetric cyclic entries $B(f)_{i,i+1}=B(f)_{i+1,i}=f_i/2$. Then
$$
D:=\{f\ge0:I-B(f)\succeq0\}=\left\{f\ge0:\sum_i f_i^2+f_1f_2f_3\le4\right\}
$$
is both the closed coordinatewise downward hull and the closed downward convex hull of $\mathcal T$, taken in the nonnegative orthant. Every strictly positive $f\in D$ has an attained single strict triangle dominator $f'=f/\rho\in\mathcal T$, where $\rho=\lambda_{\max}(B(f))\in(0,1]$.

## Proof

The determinant of $I-B(f)$ is $1-(\sum_i f_i^2+f_1f_2f_3)/4$. For $f\ge0$, the cubic bound forces $f_i\le2$, so the two-by-two principal minors $1-f_i^2/4$ are nonnegative. With diagonal entries one, the principal-minor criterion proves the PSD equivalence including zero coordinates. The region is closed and convex by the affine matrix condition. It is downward closed in the nonnegative orthant: if $0\le h\le f\in D$, then for every $x\in\mathbb R^3$ the nonpositive off-diagonal entries give
$$
x^{\mathsf T}(I-B(h))x\ge |x|^{\mathsf T}(I-B(h))|x|\ge |x|^{\mathsf T}(I-B(f))|x|\ge0,
$$
with $|x|$ taken coordinatewise. The strict triangle cubic deficit gives $\mathcal T\subseteq D$.

For $f>0$ in $D$, a unit vector maximizing the Rayleigh quotient of $B(f)$ can be taken nonnegative by replacing its coordinates with absolute values. The resulting maximizing eigenvector $w$ is strictly positive: a zero coordinate would force the other two to vanish by the eigenvector equation and positive edge weights. Its eigenvalue $\rho$ is positive by testing the all-ones vector and is at most one because $I-B(f)$ is PSD. Put $f'=f/\rho$ and $c_i=f'_i/2$. Then $B(f')w=w$. Define $u_i=c_iw_{i+1}/w_i$. The two positive summands in each eigenvector equation give $0<u_i<1$ and $1-u_{i+1}=c_iw_i/w_{i+1}$. Hence $u_i(1-u_{i+1})=c_i^2$ and $\prod_i u_i=\prod_i c_i=\prod_i(1-u_i)$.

Expanding the last product equality gives $\sum_i u_i=1+\sum_{i<j}u_i u_j-2\prod_i u_i>1$, since each pair product exceeds the triple product. Applying the same argument to the complements yields $\sum_i u_i<2$. The exact triangle image theorem now realizes $f'$ by a single strict triangle. Since $\rho\le1$, this vector dominates $f$.

For a nonnegative $f\in D$, the positive vectors $(1-\varepsilon)f+\varepsilon(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ belong to $D$ for $0<\varepsilon<1$ by convexity, since $(1/2,1/2,1/2)$ satisfies the cubic bound. Each has a strict triangle dominator by the positive case. They converge to $f$, so $D$ is contained in the closed coordinatewise downward hull. Equivalently a subsequence of their dominators converges in the compact closed triangle image and its limit dominates $f$. The other inclusion follows from $\mathcal T\subseteq D$ and closed downwardness. As $D$ is convex, the closed downward convex hull also equals $D$. For example, $(2,0,0)$ cannot have a strictly positive actual triangle dominator, since such a dominator violates the cubic bound.
