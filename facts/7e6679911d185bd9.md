## Statement

Interior-uniform and logarithmic $L^1$ convergence of positive continuous concave log-normalized parameters, even with a common positive lower bound, need not allow replacement by their limit in a first-order shell integral. On $K=[0,1]$ let $L=1$, $c=1-\log2$, and $U_t(x)=e^{ct}\min(1,1/2+x/(2t))$, $0<t<1/2$. For the actual removed shell $R_t=[0,t]$ of $K_t=[t,1]$, the integral $\int_{R_t}\log U_t=-ct+ct^2$ has normalized limit $-c$ after division by $t$, whereas $\int_{R_t}\log L=0$. The current log renormalization of $U_t$ on $K_t$ is identically one.

## Proof

The minimum of the two displayed affine functions is positive, continuous and concave on $[0,1]$, and its minimum is $1/2$. With $s=1/2+x/(2t)$ we have
$$
\int_0^t\log(1/2+x/(2t))\,dx
=2t\int_{1/2}^1\log s\,ds=t(\log2-1).
$$
The logarithm of the minimum is zero on $[t,1]$. The added constant $ct$ therefore makes the full log integral zero, and on $[0,t]$ it adds exactly $ct^2$. Away from the shrinking shell, $U_t=e^{ct}\to1$ uniformly. On the shell the log integrand is uniformly bounded, which gives logarithmic $L^1$ convergence, and the parameters have a common positive floor $1/2$. On the retained interval $K_t$ the raw parameter is the constant $e^{ct}$, whose geometric normalization is one.
