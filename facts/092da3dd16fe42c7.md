## Statement

For $p\ge0$, $\ell>0$ and $j\in\{1,2\}$ let $S_j(p,\ell)=\sum_{h\ge0}(p-\ell h)_+^j$, where the indices are integers. With $\delta=\{p/\ell\}$ and $B_3(u)=u(u-1/2)(u-1)$,
$$
S_2(p,\ell)=\frac{p^3}{3\ell}+\frac{p^2}{2}+\frac{p\ell}{6}-\frac{\ell^2}{3}B_3(\delta)
\le \frac{p^3}{3\ell}+\frac{p^2}{2}+\frac{3p\ell}{16},
\qquad
S_1(p,\ell)\ge\frac{p^2}{2\ell}+\frac p2 .
$$

## Proof

Write $p/\ell=N+\delta$ with $N=\lfloor p/\ell\rfloor$ and $0\le\delta<1$. Reversing the finite sum gives
$$
S_2(p,\ell)=\ell^2\sum_{j=0}^N(j+\delta)^2
=\ell^2\left[\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}6+\delta N(N+1)+(N+1)\delta^2\right].
$$
Substitute $N=p/\ell-\delta$ and collect the powers of $p/\ell$ to obtain the identity. It includes integral phases and $p=0$. For $\delta\le1/2$ we have $B_3(\delta)\ge0$. For $\delta>1/2$ the two nonnegative factors $\delta-1/2$ and $1-\delta$ have sum $1/2$, so $-B_3(\delta)\le\delta/16$. Since $\ell\delta\le p$, the phase term is at most $p\ell/48$. Adding it to $p\ell/6$ proves the upper bound.

The function $x\mapsto(p-\ell x)_+$ is convex on $[0,\infty)$, and the sum of the trapezoids over the unit intervals is $S_1(p,\ell)-p/2$. Its integral is $p^2/(2\ell)$ and is at most that trapezoid sum.
