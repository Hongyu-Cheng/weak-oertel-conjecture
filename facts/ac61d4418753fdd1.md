## Statement

Fix a finite nonempty real base $P$ and $d\ge1$. Let the convex bodies $K_z$ be the actual sampled fibers of their canonical hull, and use their summed $d$-volume as mixed measure. Suppose the total mass is one, each $K_z\subset R B_d$ contains a ball of radius $r_b>0$, and $|K_z|\ge v>0$. Let $q_z$ be the maximum raw ordinary closed-halfspace depth on fiber $z$, $Q=\max_z q_z$, $F_p$ the smallest face of $\operatorname{conv}P$ containing $p$, and $\beta_*$ the infimum of normalized ordinary maximum depth over all positive compatible families on $P$. Set $g=Q-\beta_*$, $\omega_j=|B_j|$ with $\omega_0=1$, and
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=|P|\omega_{d-1}R^{d-1},& q_*&=\omega_d r_b^d/2,\\
C_V&=(d/r_b)(1+1/r_b)^{d-1},& c_m&=dv/(2R),\\
C_D&=C_V+A,& c_F&=q_*c_m/[2C_D(1+C_V)].
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0<\eta\le2C_D$, if the faces of the bases with $q_p\ge Q-\eta$ miss a sampled base, then $g\ge c_F\eta$. In particular, for $0\le g\le(c_F/2)\min(2C_D,q_*/2)$, the minimal faces of the bases with $q_p\ge Q-2g/c_F$ cover $P$, and each maximizing mark on these fibers contains an own inball centered there of radius $q_*/(4A)$.

## Proof

The ordinary depth at the center of any displayed inball is at least $q_*$, because nonvertical through-mark caps take half the ball and pure-base caps take its whole fiber. The uniform same-base slab comparison of 43ed87d91b76249c uses the section bound $A$ and gives continuity, attainment on each compact fiber, and $A$-Lipschitz continuity of ordinary depth in its mark. At an own-boundary mark, an own supporting direction has a finite ambient supporting tilt by 155691fc3768c06f, since compatibility gives its anchored inclusion hypothesis. The supporting cap then has zero mixed volume.

If $u$ is a missed base, replace only input $K_u$ by $K_u+tB_d$ and take the canonical hull. The actual fiber inclusions of b22db3535d2a902a apply, including equality $K_u(t)=K_u+tB_d$ by the anchored representations. For $0<t\le1$, a radius-$r_b$ inball with center $o_z$ gives $K_z+tB_d\subset o_z+(1+t/r_b)(K_z-o_z)$, and $o_u+(1+t/(2R))(K_u-o_u)\subset K_u+tB_d$ because $\|X-o_u\|\le2R$ on $K_u$. The added total mass $\Delta_t$ thus obeys $c_m t\le\Delta_t\le C_Vt$ by the volume scaling formula, the mass-one assumption, and $|K_u|\ge v$.

Compare each new mark to an old mark within $t$. First compare the two measures at fixed cap and then move the threshold on the old measure. By the same slab estimate the change is bounded above by $\Delta_t+At$ uniformly over all tilts, including pure-base caps, where there is only the added mass. Thus $q_z(t)\le q_z+C_Dt$. For every $p$ with $q_p\ge Q-\eta$, all fibers on $F_p$ stay fixed by anchored face membership. The exact raw face restriction in dec855d1498a4e64 applies to both mixed-volume families on that face, so the entire depth function on it is fixed. Take $t=\eta/(2C_D)\le1$. All other bases remain strictly below $Q-\eta/2$, and hence the enlarged family has exactly the old raw maximum $Q$. This is an admissible unrestricted ordinary competitor, so
$$
g\ge Q-\frac{Q}{1+\Delta_t}=\frac{Q\Delta_t}{1+\Delta_t}\ge c_F\eta .
$$
For $g>0$ in the stated range, set $\eta=2g/c_F$. A missed base would give $g\ge2g$. For $g=0$, the actual family is an unrestricted global minimum, so apply the exact exhaustion of b22db3535d2a902a. Each selected maximizing mark has depth at least $q_*/2$. Boundary zero and the $A$-Lipschitz bound put its distance to the own boundary at least $q_*/(2A)$, which supplies the asserted smaller closed inball.
