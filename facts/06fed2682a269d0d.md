## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ nonempty, $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$, and $P$ the bases of the positive $\mathcal H_q$-measure fibers $K_z$, with $w_z=\mathcal H_q(K_z)$, $M=\sum_{z\in P}w_z$ and $m=2^n$. For a finite list of original closed ambient halfspaces $H_i$ and a nonempty group $I$ of their indices, write $U_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i$, $e_I(z)=\mathcal H_q(K_z\setminus (U_I)_z)$, and $B_I=\mu(U_I)$.

For a nonempty finite family $\mathcal G$ of groups and positive constants $a_I$ (which may all be one), set
$$
V(\mathcal G)=\max_{z\in P}\min_{I\in\mathcal G}\frac{e_I(z)}{a_I}.
$$
Then
$$
V(\mathcal G)=\min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\mathcal J\subseteq\mathcal G\\|\mathcal J|\le m}} V(\mathcal J).
$$
In particular an attaining subfamily exists, without any cover hypothesis.

## Proof

Deleting groups can only increase the minimum at each base, so it can only increase $V$. For the converse set $t=V(\mathcal G)$, which is nonnegative and attained. For $q\ge1$, the strict superlevel bases of $e_I(z)>a_I t$ are the trace on $P$ of the convex real-base set $E_I$ in the proof of f47790a5ad162036, with constant threshold $\ell=(a_I t)^{1/q}$. The section-convexity argument in that proof uses compact convexity and the intrinsic dimension, with no ambient interior requirement. It applies separately for each group, including when $t=0$. For $q=0$ the remainder is either zero or one; its strict superlevel trace is empty if $a_I t\ge1$, and otherwise is the trace on $P$ of the convex projection $C\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}H_i^c$ onto the base. The common intersection of the strict superlevels misses $P$ by the choice of $t$.

The positive-fiber base set is closed under integer same-parity midpoints: the midpoint fiber contains a translate of a half-scaled $q$-fiber and so has dimension at least $q$ and at most $q$. For $q=0$ the same inclusion proves nonemptiness. By the finite-set witness lemma in bee3cc8e50a0dcea, applied to $P$, at most one site per parity occurs in any empty vertex subset, hence the Helly bound is at most $m$. Select at most $m$ groups whose strict superlevels still have common intersection missing $P$. This subfamily is nonempty since $P$ is nonempty. Each base then has a selected group with $e_I(z)/a_I\le t$, so $V(\mathcal J)\le t$, proving equality and attainment.
