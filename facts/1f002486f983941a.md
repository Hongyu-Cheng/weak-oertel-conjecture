## Statement

For a finite probability law \(p\) with support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\), use the canonical closed-halfspace probability depth and its second moment:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf z)&=\inf\{p(H):H\subset\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf z\},\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^2,
\qquad \Phi_N(p)=4^NM_2(p),\\
v_p(\mathbf z)&=p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

A complete log-affine law on a polytope \(Q\subset\mathbb R^N\) has support exactly \(D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\) and probabilities proportional to the restriction of one exponential affine function. The codimension of a nonempty face \(F\) below is its intrinsic codimension in \(Q\), namely \(N-\dim F\); all constructed \(Q\) are full-dimensional and satisfy \(Q=\operatorname{conv}D\).

Here is the explicit construction, for every integer \(r\ge2\). Set \(K=2^r\), \(L=rK\), \(M=r\), \(q=\lceil\log_2r\rceil+5\), \(T=2^q\), \(s=T-1\), and \(N_r=q+r+1\). The scalar \(L\) is the exponential weight base. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_r&=\{0,1\}^r\cup\{0,-1\}^r,\\
P_r&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{-1\}\times\operatorname{conv}A_r)\cup\{(1,\mathbf0)\}\bigr),\\
G_r&=\left\{(t,\mathbf x):
0\le t\le1,\quad
0\le x_1\le LM-1-(LM-M)t,\quad
0\le x_i\le1\ (2\le i\le r)\right\},\\
U&=\{0,1\}^q,\\
Q_r&=\operatorname{conv}\left(
(\{\mathbf0\}\times P_r)
\cup\bigcup_{\mathbf u\in U\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
(\{\mathbf u\}\times G_r)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Coordinates on \(Q_r\) are \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf u,t,\mathbf x)\). Its complete support is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_r={}&\{(\mathbf0,-1,\mathbf x):\mathbf x\in A_r\}
\cup\{(\mathbf0,0,\mathbf0),(\mathbf0,1,\mathbf0)\}\\
&\cup\{(\mathbf u,0,j,\boldsymbol\epsilon):
\mathbf u\in U\setminus\{\mathbf0\},\ 0\le j\le LM-1,\ j\in\mathbb Z,
\ \boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{r-1}\}\\
&\cup\{(\mathbf u,1,j,\boldsymbol\epsilon):
\mathbf u\in U\setminus\{\mathbf0\},\ 0\le j\le M-1,\ j\in\mathbb Z,
\ \boldsymbol\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{r-1}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Put \(Z_c=L+1+(2K-1)/L\), \(Z_g=LMK\), \(Z=sZ_g+Z_c\), and \(p_r(\mathbf u,t,\mathbf x)=L^t/Z\) on \(D_r\). These laws satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{N_r}\max_{\mathbf z\in D_r}p_r(\mathbf z)&\longrightarrow0,\\
\sup_{\substack{F\text{ nonempty proper}\\\text{face of }Q_r}}
2^{\operatorname{codim}F}p_r(F)&\le 1+\varepsilon_r,
\qquad \varepsilon_r\longrightarrow0,\\
\max_{F\text{ nonempty proper face of }Q_r}p_r(F)&\longrightarrow\frac12,\\
\Phi_{N_r}(p_r)&\longrightarrow\frac13.
\end{aligned}
\]

One explicit admissible error is
\(\varepsilon_r=T/s-1+2^{N_r}Z_c/Z\).
For \(k\ge1\), define
\(\theta_k=(\sqrt{12\cdot4^k-3}-3)/(2(4^k-1))\).
For all sufficiently large \(r\), every nonempty proper face satisfies
\(p_r(F)\le\theta_{\operatorname{codim}F}\).

The supported triple
\(\mathbf a_r=(\mathbf0,-1,\mathbf0)\),
\(\mathbf b_r=(\mathbf0,0,\mathbf0)\), and
\(\mathbf c_r=(\mathbf0,1,\mathbf0)\)
satisfies \(\mathbf b_r=(\mathbf a_r+\mathbf c_r)/2\) and the exact identities

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf b_r)}
{\sqrt{\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf a_r)\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf c_r)}}
&=\frac{1+1/r}{2^{r/2}},\\
\frac{v_{p_r}(\mathbf b_r)}
{\sqrt{v_{p_r}(\mathbf a_r)v_{p_r}(\mathbf c_r)}}
&=\frac{(1+1/r)^2}{2^r}.
\end{aligned}
\]

There is also an exact convex-body realization up to a vanishing multiplicative perturbation. Set \(d_r=r^4\), \(\lambda=\log L\), and

\[
\mathcal C_r=\left\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^{N_r+d_r}:
\mathbf z=(\mathbf u,t,\mathbf x)\in Q_r,\quad
0\le y_i\le1+\frac{\lambda t}{d_r}\ (1\le i\le d_r)\right\}.
\]

This is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its fibers at integer \(\mathbf z\) have exactly the volumes
\(w_{\mathbf z}=(1+\lambda t/d_r)^{d_r}\) for \(\mathbf z\in D_r\), with no other nonempty integer fibers. Let \(\widehat p_r\) be these volumes normalized to total mass one, and put
\(\delta_r=\lambda^2/(2d_r(1-\lambda/d_r))\).
Then \(\delta_r=O(r^{-2})\), and for every supported \(\mathbf z\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\delta_r}p_r(\mathbf z)&\le\widehat p_r(\mathbf z)\le e^{\delta_r}p_r(\mathbf z),\\
e^{-\delta_r}\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf z)&\le\tau_{\widehat p_r}(\mathbf z)\le e^{\delta_r}\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf z),\\
e^{-3\delta_r}M_2(p_r)&\le M_2(\widehat p_r)\le e^{3\delta_r}M_2(p_r).
\end{aligned}
\]

All four preceding asymptotic properties, and eventually the exact \(\theta_k\) facial bounds, hold for \(\widehat p_r\), using the facial error \(e^{\delta_r}(1+\varepsilon_r)-1\) in place of \(\varepsilon_r\). Its midpoint ratio for \(v_{\widehat p_r}\) lies between \(e^{-6\delta_r}(1+1/r)^2 2^{-r}\) and \(e^{6\delta_r}(1+1/r)^2 2^{-r}\). The depths in these assertions are depths of the base probability tables; no conclusion about small actual mixed-integer depth of \(\mathcal C_r\) is asserted.

## Proof

We first record an elementary fact about finite supports. If \(F\) is an exposed face of the convex hull of a finite support and \(p_F\) is the law conditional on \(F\), then, for every supported \(\mathbf z\in F\),

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=p(F)\tau_{p_F}(\mathbf z),
\]

where the conditional depth is computed in \(\operatorname{aff}F\), with value one for a singleton. Indeed, intersecting a containing halfspace with \(\operatorname{aff}F\) gives a containing intrinsic halfspace or all of \(\operatorname{aff}F\), so the left side is at least the right side. Conversely, a halfspace attaining conditional depth exists: there are finitely many subsets of the conditional support, so the infimum of their realized masses is a minimum. Extend an affine inequality defining this halfspace from \(\operatorname{aff}F\) to the ambient space. Let \(\psi\) be an affine exposing function equal to zero on \(F\) and strictly negative at supported points outside \(F\). Adding a sufficiently large positive multiple of \(\psi\) to the extended inequality excludes every supported point outside \(F\), without changing its intersection with \(F\). This yields the reverse inequality. The singleton case follows directly by an exposing halfspace. For the whole support the identity is tautological. This argument uses closed halfspaces throughout, retaining points on the cutting boundary.

We next prove the core lattice census. Every point of \(\operatorname{conv}A_r\) lies in \([-1,1]^r\) and satisfies \(x_i-x_j\le1\) for every \(i,j\): all points of each of the two cubes defining \(A_r\) satisfy these inequalities, which are preserved by convex combinations. Consequently an integer point in this convex hull cannot have both a coordinate equal to one and a coordinate equal to minus one. Its coordinates are therefore either all in \(\{0,1\}\) or all in \(\{0,-1\}\). Thus
\((\operatorname{conv}A_r)\cap\mathbb Z^r=A_r\), whose cardinality is \(2K-1\).
The section of \(P_r\) at height \(t\in[-1,1]\) is
\(((1-t)/2)\operatorname{conv}A_r\). At integer height zero it lies in \([-1/2,1/2]^r\), whose only integer point is zero. The section at height one is the apex. These are all possible integer heights, proving

\[
P_r\cap\mathbb Z^{r+1}
=(\{-1\}\times A_r)\cup\{(0,\mathbf0),(1,\mathbf0)\}.
\]

Both \(P_r\) and \(G_r\) are full-dimensional of dimension \(r+1\). For \(P_r\), the base contains the affinely independent points \(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r\), and the apex is outside its affine span. For \(G_r\), its defining coordinate intervals have positive length for \(0<t<1\), since \(M\ge2\). Its only integer heights are zero and one, giving exactly the two ordinary sets of lattice points in the statement.

The outer projection of \(Q_r\) is \([0,1]^q\). Every integer point of that cube is a Boolean vertex. In a convex combination of generating fibers whose outer projection equals a Boolean vertex \(\mathbf u\), every positive-coefficient term has the same outer label \(\mathbf u\): in each coordinate an average of numbers in \(\{0,1\}\) equals an endpoint only when all those numbers equal that endpoint. Hence the section of \(Q_r\) at \(\mathbf u\) is exactly \(P_r\) when \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\), and exactly \(G_r\) otherwise. This proves the full census \(D_r\) with no extra integer points introduced by convex completion.

The base of \(P_r\) and its apex are convex hulls of integer points. The two-dimensional trapezoid in the \((t,x_1)\) coordinates of \(G_r\) has vertices
\((0,0),(0,LM-1),(1,0),(1,M-1)\), all integer, and its remaining factor is a Boolean cube. Thus all generating polytopes are convex hulls of their integer points, proving \(Q_r=\operatorname{conv}D_r\). Its affine span projects onto \(\mathbb R^q\), and its direction space contains the \(r+1\) independent inner directions furnished by any ordinary fiber. Rank and nullity therefore give \(\dim Q_r=q+r+1=N_r\). In particular every face is spanned by its supported lattice vertices, so geometric face dimension and the affine rank of the corresponding supported face agree. The law \(p_r\) is globally log-affine because \(L^t=e^{(\log L)t}\).

For exact depths, initially use the unnormalized core weights \(L^t\), with total \(Z_c\), and denote the least weight of a containing halfspace by \(d_c\). At the base point \(a=(-1,\mathbf0)\), any containing halfspace contains at least \(K\) supported base points. To see this, the base support consists of its origin and \(K-1\) opposite pairs, and every containing closed halfspace contains the origin and at least one member of every pair. Each base point has weight \(1/L\). The halfspace
\(\sum_i x_i-(t+1)\ge0\) contains exactly the \(K\) base points with \(\mathbf x\in\{0,1\}^r\), and excludes both other core points. Hence \(d_c(a)=K/L\). The apex \(c=(1,\mathbf0)\) is isolated by \(t\ge1\), giving \(d_c(c)=L\).

To compute depth at \(b=(0,\mathbf0)\), one may restrict to halfspaces whose boundary passes through \(b\), because moving a containing boundary to the target only removes points. Write such a halfspace as \(\alpha t+\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x\ge0\). If \(\alpha\ge0\), it contains both \(b\) and the apex, of total weight \(1+L\ge1+K/L\). If \(\alpha<0\), its base condition is \(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x\ge\alpha\). This contains the origin and at least one member of each opposite pair, because at least one of two opposite scores is nonnegative. It therefore contains base weight at least \(K/L\), in addition to the middle atom of weight one. The halfspace \(-t/2+\sum_i x_i\ge0\) attains this bound: it excludes the apex, includes \(b\), and includes exactly the positive Boolean cube at the base. Indeed, on a nonzero negative Boolean point the coordinate sum is an integer at most minus one. Thus

\[
d_c(a)=K/L,\qquad d_c(b)=1+K/L,\qquad d_c(c)=L.
\]

Every outer-label fiber is exposed in \(Q_r\), by a linear functional on \(\mathbf u\) uniquely maximized at that Boolean label. The exposed-face identity therefore gives global depths \(d_c(a)/Z,d_c(b)/Z,d_c(c)/Z\) on the displayed triple. Its probabilities are \(L^{-1}/Z,1/Z,L/Z\), so its middle probability is exactly the geometric mean of its endpoint probabilities. Consequently the depth ratio is \((1+K/L)/\sqrt K\), and the \(v_p\) ratio is its square. Substituting \(K=2^r\) and \(K/L=1/r\) proves both claimed exact midpoint identities.

We now establish the ordinary face and moment estimates. Write \(G_r=J_r\times[0,1]^{r-1}\), where \(J_r\) is the trapezoid in coordinates \((t,x_1)\). Its integer section at height zero has \(LM\) points of weight one; its section at height one has \(M\) points of weight \(L\). Each height has total weight \(LM\), so \(J_r\) has total weight \(2LM\), and \(G_r\) has total weight \(Z_g=LMK\). The normalized law \(g_r\) on \(G_r\cap\mathbb Z^{r+1}\) is the product of the normalized trapezoid law and the uniform law on the other \(r-1\) Boolean coordinates.

Every horizontal edge of \(J_r\) has probability \(1/2\). Each of its two side edges has integer points only at its endpoints, and therefore probability \((1+L)/(2LM)\le1/2\), since \(M\ge2\) and \(L\ge1\). Each vertex has probability at most \(L/(2LM)=1/(2M)\le1/4\). Including the whole trapezoid, these are all its nonempty faces. Maximizing a linear functional on a product maximizes its factor functionals separately. Thus every face of a product is a product of faces, with additive codimensions. A Boolean-cube face fixing \(h\) coordinates has probability \(2^{-h}\). We conclude, also allowing the whole \(G_r\) as a codimension-zero face, that

\[
g_r(J)\le2^{-\operatorname{codim}_{G_r}J}
\quad\text{for every nonempty face }J\text{ of }G_r.
\]

For the uniform law on \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), the exact depth at \(j\) is \(\min(j+1,m-j)/m\), since a closed halfspace of the line is a closed ray. Define its moment
\(c_m=m^{-3}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\min(j+1,m-j)^2\).
Then \(c_m\to1/12\): writing it as the average of the squared normalized depths gives a Riemann sum for \(\min(x,1-x)^2\) on \([0,1]\). More explicitly, the normalized depth differs from \(\min(j/m,1-j/m)\) by at most \(1/m\); the resulting difference of squared terms is at most \(2/m\), while the continuous limiting function has integral \(2\int_0^{1/2}x^2\,dx=1/12\).

Fixing a height in \(\{0,1\}\) and all the other \(r-1\) Boolean coordinates gives an exposed line face of \(G_r\), of total probability \(1/K\). Conditional on that line its law is uniform on \(LM\) points at height zero, or on \(M\) points at height one. The exposed-face identity, followed by summing over the \(K/2\) lines at each height, gives the exact formula

\[
M_2(g_r)=\frac{K/2}{K^3}(c_{LM}+c_M)
=\frac{c_{LM}+c_M}{2K^2}.
\]

Let \(\pi_r\) be the normalized core law, and put
\(\rho=Z_c/Z_g\), \(\alpha=Z_g/Z=1/(s+\rho)\), and
\(\eta=Z_c/Z=\rho/(s+\rho)\).
The values \(\alpha\) and \(\eta\) are the probability masses of each ordinary fiber and the core fiber, respectively. Applying the exposed-face identity independently on every fiber proves

\[
M_2(p_r)=s\alpha^3\frac{c_{LM}+c_M}{2K^2}
+\eta^3M_2(\pi_r).
\]

We give bounds sufficient to justify each limit, including the exceptional contribution. For \(r\ge2\),
\(Z_c=L+1+(2K-1)/L\le L+2\le2L\).
Consequently
\(0<\rho\le2/(MK)\) and \(\eta\le2/(sMK)\).
Also \(32r\le T<64r\), \(s=T-1\to\infty\), and \(T/s\to1\). Since \(2^{N_r}=2TK\), the largest atom obeys

\[
2^{N_r}\max p_r
=\frac{2TKL}{Z}
\le\frac{2T}{sM}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Because \(4^{N_r}=4T^2K^2\), the normalized ordinary moment is exactly

\[
2\left(\frac Ts\right)^2
\left(1+\frac\rho s\right)^{-3}(c_{LM}+c_M)
\longrightarrow2\cdot\frac16=\frac13.
\]

Here both \(M\) and \(LM\) tend to infinity, and \(\rho/s\to0\). Since \(0\le M_2(\pi_r)\le1\), the normalized core moment is at most

\[
4T^2K^2\left(\frac2{sMK}\right)^3
=\frac{32(T/s)^2}{sM^3K}\longrightarrow0.
\]

This proves the moment limit without requiring any omitted depth calculation for other core points.

We prove the uniform estimate for every face codimension. First, an affine subspace of dimension \(f\) in \(\mathbb R^q\) contains at most \(2^f\) Boolean vertices. Indeed, among the coordinate linear functionals choose \(f\) whose restrictions form a basis of the dual of its direction space. Projection to these coordinates is injective on the affine subspace, and Boolean points have only \(2^f\) possible projected values. This includes \(f=0\).

Let \(F\) be a nonempty proper face of \(Q_r\), exposed by maximizing a linear functional \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf u+\mathbf b\cdot(t,\mathbf x)\). Let \(h=\dim F\), \(k=N_r-h\), and let \(f\) be the dimension of the affine span of its outer projection. If \(F\) meets an ordinary fiber, its intersection with that fiber is exactly \(\{\mathbf u\}\times J\), where \(J\) is the maximizing face of \(\mathbf b\) on \(G_r\). This is the same \(J\) at every ordinary label met by \(F\). In fact, if the global maximum is \(b_*\) and the maximum of the inner functional on \(G_r\) is \(b_g\), an intersection forces
\(b_*=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf u+b_g\); equality then holds at precisely the points of \(J\). The assertion also covers \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), for which \(J=G_r\).

Put \(j=\dim J\). The outer projection of the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}F\) has rank \(f\). Its kernel contains the \(j\)-dimensional direction space of one copy of \(J\) in \(F\), so rank and nullity give \(h\ge f+j\). At most \(2^f\) ordinary Boolean labels meet \(F\), by the preceding affine-subspace bound. The probability contributed by each is \(\alpha g_r(J)\le\alpha2^{-(r+1-j)}\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^k p_r(F\cap\{\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\})
&\le2^{q+r+1-h}\,2^f\alpha\,2^{-(r+1-j)}\\
&=T\alpha\,2^{f+j-h}\le T\alpha\le T/s.
\end{aligned}
\]

If no ordinary label meets \(F\), its ordinary contribution is zero and the same upper bound holds. The entire core has probability \(\eta\), so its contribution at any codimension is at most \(2^{N_r}\eta\). In particular this estimate applies to vertices as well as positive-dimensional faces. The previous bounds imply

\[
2^{N_r}\eta\le\frac{4T}{sM}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
2^kp_r(F)\le T/s+2^{N_r}\eta=1+\varepsilon_r.
\]

All dimensions in this argument are affine dimensions of the actual faces and their projections, rather than counts of fixed coordinates. As already proved, \(Q_r\) and \(G_r\) have full affine dimension and are convex hulls of their lattice supports, so these are precisely the intrinsic codimensions appearing in the statement.

Since \(k\ge1\) on proper faces, the last estimate gives \(p_r(F)\le(1+\varepsilon_r)/2\). The face \(u_1=1\) contains exactly \(T/2\) ordinary fibers and no core fiber, so its mass is \(T\alpha/2\to1/2\). This proves the limit of the maximum proper-face mass.

For the exact \(\theta_k\) restrictions, no reduction theorem is needed. The displayed \(\theta_k\) is the unique positive root of
\((4^k-1)x^2+3x-3=0\), whose left side is strictly increasing for \(x\ge0\). Substitute \(x=(6/5)2^{-k}\) and write \(u=2^{-k}\in(0,1/2]\). The polynomial becomes

\[
\frac{-39+90u-36u^2}{25}\le-\frac3{25}<0.
\]

The inequality holds because its numerator has positive derivative \(90-72u\) on this interval and takes value minus three at \(u=1/2\). Thus \(\theta_k>(6/5)2^{-k}\) uniformly for all \(k\ge1\). For sufficiently large \(r\), \(1+\varepsilon_r\le6/5\). The uniform face estimate then gives every claimed \(\theta_k\) restriction simultaneously.

Finally consider \(\mathcal C_r\). Since \(\lambda=\log(r2^r)<2r\) for \(r\ge2\) and \(d_r=r^4\), one has \(0<\lambda/d_r<1/4\). The fiber side lengths are strictly positive for all \(-1\le t\le1\). All defining inequalities are affine, and the base \(Q_r\) is compact, so \(\mathcal C_r\) is closed, bounded, and convex. An interior base point together with fiber coordinates strictly between their two bounds has an ambient open neighborhood in \(\mathcal C_r\); hence it is full-dimensional. Its projection is exactly \(Q_r\). The integer base support is therefore exactly \(D_r\), and the product-box formula gives its exact \(d_r\)-dimensional fiber volumes stated above.

For any \(|x|<1\), elementary integration yields

\[
0\le x-\log(1+x)\le\frac{x^2}{2(1-|x|)}.
\]

For example, integrate the derivative \(x/(1+x)\) between zero and \(x\); for negative \(x\) reverse the integration limits, and in either case bound the denominator below by \(1-|x|\). Set \(x=\lambda t/d_r\) and multiply by \(d_r\). Uniformly on \(-1\le t\le1\), this gives

\[
e^{-\delta_r}L^t\le(1+\lambda t/d_r)^{d_r}\le L^t,
\qquad
\delta_r=\frac{\lambda^2}{2d_r(1-\lambda/d_r)}=O(r^{-2}).
\]

In particular \(\delta_r\le8/(3r^2)\) follows from \(\lambda<2r\) and \(1-\lambda/d_r>3/4\). The sum of the new unnormalized weights lies between \(e^{-\delta_r}Z\) and \(Z\). Dividing numerator and denominator proves the asserted pointwise comparison between \(\widehat p_r\) and \(p_r\). Summation proves the same comparison for the probability of any subset of the support, including every closed-halfspace intersection and every face. Taking infima over the same halfspaces preserves both inequalities, proving the depth comparison. Multiplying one probability factor by two depth factors, and then summing, proves the moment comparison and the pointwise comparison
\(e^{-3\delta_r}v_{p_r}\le v_{\widehat p_r}\le e^{3\delta_r}v_{p_r}\).
Applying this last comparison to the numerator and geometric-mean denominator of the midpoint ratio gives exactly the two factors \(e^{-6\delta_r}\) and \(e^{6\delta_r}\) claimed in the statement.

The vanishing normalized atom follows by its \(e^{\delta_r}\) comparison with that of \(p_r\). Uniformly in all proper-face codimensions,
\(2^k\widehat p_r(F)\le e^{\delta_r}(1+\varepsilon_r)=1+o(1)\).
The face \(u_1=1\), together with the reverse comparison, still has mass tending to one half. The moment comparison gives \(\Phi_{N_r}(\widehat p_r)\to1/3\). Finally \(e^{\delta_r}(1+\varepsilon_r)\le6/5\) for sufficiently large \(r\), so the same directly proved lower bound on \(\theta_k\) establishes all exact facial restrictions for \(\widehat p_r\).

Since \(N_r=r+O(\log r)\), the midpoint ratios tend to zero exponentially in the base dimension, for both the log-affine laws and the exact fiber laws. Thus no positive dimension-independent constant can lower-bound \(v_p((\mathbf a+\mathbf c)/2)/\sqrt{v_p(\mathbf a)v_p(\mathbf c)}\) throughout either stated family, even with all the proved small-atom and facial conditions. The normalized second moments tend to a positive constant, so this obstruction supplies neither a sequence with \(\Phi_N\to0\) nor a counterexample to weak Oertel.
