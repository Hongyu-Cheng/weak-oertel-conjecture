## Statement

Let $w=(z,X)$ and let a compactly supported probability row of $w'=(z',X')$ have mean $w$. Suppose that all targets and the source lie on the common plane $a\cdot z+n\cdot X=b$. For interior fiber centers $c_z$ let $r(w)=n\cdot(X-c_z)>0$ and $r(w')=n\cdot(X'-c_{z'})>0$, with the center values bounded and measurable on the row. Write $\Delta w=w'-w$. Target weighting by $r$ changes the row mass relative to source weighting by
$
\mathbb E r(w')-r(w)=-n\cdot(\mathbb E c_{z'}-c_z)
$
and has unnormalized barycentric defect
$
\mathbb E[r(w')\Delta w]=-\mathbb E[\Delta w\{a\cdot(z'-z)+n\cdot(c_{z'}-c_z)\}].
$
If $c_z=Az+c_0$ is affine, the mass is preserved and the defect is $-Q(a+A^\top n,0)$, where $Q=\mathbb E[\Delta w\,\Delta w^\top]$. In this affine case the weighted row retains its barycenter exactly when $r(w')=r(w)$ almost surely.

## Proof

Subtract the support-plane equalities to get $n\cdot(X'-X)=-a\cdot(z'-z)$, hence
$
r(w')-r(w)=-a\cdot(z'-z)-n\cdot(c_{z'}-c_z).
$
Take expectation and use $\mathbb E(z'-z)=0$ to get the mass identity. Multiply by $\Delta w$ and take expectation using $\mathbb E\Delta w=0$ to obtain the barycenter identity. For affine centers the weight difference is $-(a+A^\top n,0)\cdot\Delta w$. Its mean is zero and its second moment is the quadratic form of $Q$ on this vector. Because $Q$ is positive semidefinite, the vanishing vector condition is equivalent to this second moment being zero, which is exactly constancy of the weight. Positivity permits normalizing the weighted row when talking about its barycenter.
