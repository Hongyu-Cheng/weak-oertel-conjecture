## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer, and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^N\) be a compact full-dimensional convex polytope with nonempty complete support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Let \(h\in\mathbb R\) and let \(\ell:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R\) be an arbitrary real affine function. The original height is the last coordinate \(I_N\); at \(N=3\) this is exactly the registered height \(I_3\). For every \(I\in D\) and every \(s\in\mathbb Z\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_I&=\exp\bigl(-(I_N-h)^2-\ell(I)\bigr),\\
W&=\sum_{I\in D}w_I,
&W_s&=\sum_{\substack{I\in D\\I_N=s}}w_I.
\end{aligned}
\]

Empty sums are zero. Then \(W>0\) and \(\max_{s\in\mathbb Z}W_s\ge W/2^{N+1}\). In particular, \(\max_sW_s\ge W/16\) in the entire registered class \(N=3\).

Now specialize to \(N=3\). For an occupied height \(s\) and an original center \(J\in D\) with \(J_3=s\), define its true singleton-height restricted depth by

\[
\tau_{[s,s]}(J)
=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\text{ a closed halfspace}\\J\in H}}
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_3=s}}w_I.
\]

There exist \(s_0\in\mathbb Z\) and \(J\in D\) with \(J_3=s_0\) such that \(W_{s_0}\ge W/16\) and \(\tau_{[s_0,s_0]}(J)\ge W/64\). The singleton interval has width zero and hence meets every nonnegative squared-width bound. All weights and sums are those of the original complete support, including boundary points. The only accepted predecessor used is the integer centerpoint consequence of \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\), with integer dimension two and continuous dimension zero, for this depth conclusion.

## Proof

Compactness makes \(D\) finite. It is nonempty and every \(w_I\) is positive, so \(W>0\). Set \(W_*:=\max_{s\in\mathbb Z}W_s>0\).

For \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^N\) and \(s\in\mathbb Z\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{s,\varepsilon}
&=\{I\in D:I_N=s,\ I_i\equiv\varepsilon_i\pmod 2\text{ for }1\le i\le N\},\\
W_{s,\varepsilon}&=\sum_{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon}}w_I.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(0\le W_{s,\varepsilon}\le W_s\le W_*\), and \(W_{s,\varepsilon}>0\) is possible only when \(s\equiv\varepsilon_N\pmod 2\). These are subdivisions of the original rows, and \(W=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^N}\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z}W_{s,\varepsilon}\).

Fix one \(\varepsilon\) and heights \(s,t\) for which both \(W_{s,\varepsilon}\) and \(W_{t,\varepsilon}\) are positive. Their common height parity makes \(m=(s+t)/2\) an integer. For every ordered pair \(I\in D_{s,\varepsilon}\), \(I'\in D_{t,\varepsilon}\), the midpoint \(J=(I+I')/2\) is an integer point. Convexity puts \(J\) in \(P\), so completeness puts it in \(D\), at height \(m\). Affinity of \(\ell\) and expansion of the squares give the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log w_J-\tfrac12(\log w_I+\log w_{I'})
&=\tfrac12\bigl((s-h)^2+(t-h)^2\bigr)-(m-h)^2\\
&=\frac{(s-t)^2}{4},\\
w_J&=\exp\!\left(\frac{(s-t)^2}{4}\right)\sqrt{w_Iw_{I'}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

For a fixed midpoint \(J\), the other point of a contributing pair is uniquely determined by either point, through \(I'=2J-I\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon},\ I'\in D_{t,\varepsilon}\\I+I'=2J}}
\sqrt{w_Iw_{I'}}
&\le
\left(\sum_{\substack{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon}\\2J-I\in D_{t,\varepsilon}}}w_I\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{\substack{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon}\\2J-I\in D_{t,\varepsilon}}}w_{2J-I}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le\sqrt{W_{s,\varepsilon}W_{t,\varepsilon}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

In the second factor the map \(I\mapsto2J-I\) is injective into \(D_{t,\varepsilon}\). This is why arbitrarily many pairs sharing one midpoint do not invalidate the comparison.

Group the product of the two row sums by the midpoint of each ordered pair. All those midpoints are among the original points of height \(m\). Equations (1) and (2) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{s,\varepsilon}W_{t,\varepsilon}
&=\sum_{\substack{J\in D\\J_N=m}}
\sum_{\substack{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon},\ I'\in D_{t,\varepsilon}\\I+I'=2J}}w_Iw_{I'}\\
&=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(s-t)^2}{4}\right)
\sum_{\substack{J\in D\\J_N=m}}w_J
\sum_{\substack{I\in D_{s,\varepsilon},\ I'\in D_{t,\varepsilon}\\I+I'=2J}}
\sqrt{w_Iw_{I'}}\\
&\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{(s-t)^2}{4}\right)
W_m\sqrt{W_{s,\varepsilon}W_{t,\varepsilon}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Dividing by the positive square root proves the direct original-mass inequality

\[
W_{(s+t)/2}
\ge\exp\!\left(\frac{(s-t)^2}{4}\right)
\sqrt{W_{s,\varepsilon}W_{t,\varepsilon}}.
\tag{3}
\]

This includes \(s=t\), and every intermediate point used belongs to the original support. No restriction on the directions or dimensions of the individual height sections was needed.

For this fixed \(\varepsilon\) and \(v\ge0\), consider the occupied heights with \(W_{s,\varepsilon}\ge e^{-v}W_*\). If \(s,t\) are two such heights, (3) and the definition of \(W_*\) imply

\[
W_*
\ge W_{(s+t)/2}
\ge\exp\!\left(\frac{(s-t)^2}{4}-v\right)W_*.
\]

Consequently these heights have diameter at most \(2\sqrt v\). They all lie in the progression \(\varepsilon_N+2\mathbb Z\), whose spacing is two. There are therefore at most \(\lfloor\sqrt v\rfloor+1\le\sqrt v+1\) of them. This also bounds an empty set of such heights.

For each nonzero \(W_{s,\varepsilon}\), its potential \(\log(W_*/W_{s,\varepsilon})\) is nonnegative. Apply \(e^{-a}=\int_a^\infty e^{-v}\,dv\) to these finitely many potentials and sum. The preceding count and \(\sqrt v\le(v+1)/2\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z}W_{s,\varepsilon}}{W_*}
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-v}
\bigl|\{s\in\mathbb Z:W_{s,\varepsilon}\ge e^{-v}W_*\}\bigr|\,dv\\
&\le\int_0^\infty e^{-v}(\sqrt v+1)\,dv\\
&\le\int_0^\infty e^{-v}\left(\frac v2+\frac32\right)\,dv
=2.
\end{aligned}
\]

If the parity class has no original points, its left side is zero and the same bound holds. There are \(2^N\) full parity classes. Summing over them proves \(W\le2^{N+1}W_*\) for every \(N\ge1\). In dimension three this gives \(W\le16W_*\), establishing the registered absolute constant.

For the depth conclusion, specialize to \(N=3\). Choose \(s_0\) with \(W_{s_0}=W_*\), and let \(K=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:(x,s_0)\in P\}\). This is a nonempty compact convex set; it need not have positive area. Define the finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^2\) by

\[
\nu=\sum_{\substack{x\in\mathbb Z^2\\(x,s_0)\in D}}
w_{(x,s_0)}\,\delta_x.
\]

Its support lies in \(K\cap\mathbb Z^2\), and its total mass is \(W_*\). Apply accepted fact \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\) with its integer dimension \(n=2\), continuous dimension \(d=0\), lattice set \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2\), and measure equal to this \(\nu\). Its total measure in this application is exactly \(W_*\). It supplies \(x_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) such that every closed planar halfspace containing \(x_0\) has \(\nu\)-mass at least \(W_*/4\).

In fact \(x_0\in K\). Otherwise strict separation from the compact convex set \(K\) would give a closed planar halfspace containing \(x_0\) and disjoint from \(K\), with \(\nu\)-mass zero, contradicting \(W_*/4>0\). Thus \(J=(x_0,s_0)\) belongs to the original complete support \(D\).

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) be any closed halfspace containing \(J\). Restrict its defining affine inequality to the plane of height \(s_0\). If the two horizontal coefficients are both zero, the restriction is the entire plane, because it contains \(J\). Otherwise it is a closed planar halfspace containing \(x_0\). The integer centerpoint conclusion, or the full row mass in the first case, then gives

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_3=s_0}}w_I
\ge\frac{W_*}{4}
\ge\frac{W}{64}.
\]

Taking the infimum over all such original halfspaces proves the claimed singleton-height depth. All inequalities use closed halfspaces and keep atoms on their boundaries. The selected interval \([s_0,s_0]\) has width zero, as asserted.
