## Statement

Let k>=1 and T>=0 be integers, let beta>=0, and let B_i>=2 and C_{4,i}>=2B_i-1 be integers for i in [k]. Define
\[
g_i(0)=0,\qquad g_i(1)=1,\qquad g_i(2)=B_i,\qquad g_i(3)=C_{4,i},
\qquad \Gamma(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i).
\]
For every integer E>=0, put
\[
f_E=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\quad(E\le T),\qquad f_E=0\quad(E>T),
\]
and put
\[
Z_4=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\{0,1,2,3\}^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},\qquad
Z_3=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\{0,1,2\}^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},\qquad
B_{\min}=\min_i B_i,\qquad R=\lfloor T/B_{\min}\rfloor.
\]
Fix a real rho with 0<rho<=1/4. Assume the following eligibility condition: every vector x in {0,1,2,3}^k satisfying Gamma(x)<=T, having at least k/4 coordinates equal to 1, and having at least one coordinate equal to 3 satisfies
\[
\left|\left\{j:x_j=1,\ B_j\le\min_{i:x_i=3} B_i\right\}\right|\ge\rho k.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le\exp\!\left(\frac{10R^2}{\rho k}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
In the small-R regime 1<=R<=rho*k/4, the proof gives the sharper intermediate bound
\[
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le
\exp\!\left(\left(\frac2\rho+32\right)\frac{R^2}{k}\right).
\tag{3}
\]
In particular, for every real A>=0,
\[
R\le A\sqrt{k}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{10A^2/\rho}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the complete original law
\[
A_T^{(4)}=\{(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\{0,1,2,3\}^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z_4,
\]
let tau_p and M_3(p) have the original ambient-halfspace meanings in accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977`: halfspaces are closed affine halfspaces of R^{k+1}, tau_p(z) is the infimum of their p-masses over those containing z, and M_3(p)=sum_z p_z tau_p(z)^3. Then
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-40R^2/(\rho k)}}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)},
\qquad
R\le A\sqrt{k}\ \Longrightarrow\
M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-40A^2/\rho}}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{5}
\]

A checkable sufficient condition for (1) with rho=1/8 is
\[
|\{i:B_i=B_{\min}\}|\ge 7k/8.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, under (6), (2) and (5) give Z_4/Z_3<=exp(80R^2/k) and M_3(p)>=exp(-320R^2/k)8^{-(k+1)}/2939328. This allows genuinely unequal B_i: for example, when k is a positive multiple of 8, take 7k/8 of the B_i equal to any fixed integer B_min>=2, and the remaining k/8 equal to any larger integer, with all C_{4,i} arbitrary integers at least 2B_i-1. The locked symbols n,d,C,W,c_0 retain their locked meanings.

## Proof

The zero spatial vector gives Z_3>0. For all integer energies E'<=E,
\[
f_E\le f_{E'}.
\tag{7}
\]
This is the only property of the height weights used in the mass proof. In particular all comparisons retain the entire tail through T, including beta=0 and cap atoms.

We will use elementary weighted double counting in the following form. Suppose a finite bipartite graph has source weights w(x)>=0 and target weights w(y)>=0, every edge x~y satisfies w(x)<=w(y), every source has at least D>0 distinct targets, and every target has at most I sources. Then
\[
D\sum_xw(x)\le\sum_{x\sim y}w(x)
\le\sum_{x\sim y}w(y)\le I\sum_yw(y).
\tag{8}
\]
Our vertex weights will always be the original spatial masses f_{Gamma(x)}. No equality of energies within an occupation class will be used.

Every feasible four-site vector has at most R coordinates in {2,3}, because each such coordinate costs at least B_min. Clipping every 3 to 2 weakly lowers energy. A feasible three-site target y having q coordinates equal to 2 has exactly 2^q possible four-site preimages before feasibility restrictions: independently retain 2 or replace it by 3 at each of these q sites. Since q<=min(R,k) and every feasible preimage has weight at most f_{Gamma(y)}, summing over clipping fibers gives
\[
Z_4\le 2^{\min(R,k)}Z_3\le2^RZ_3.
\tag{9}
\]
If R=0, no vector containing a 3 is feasible, since C_{4,i}>=B_min>T. Thus Z_4=Z_3 in this case.

For now suppose
\[
1\le R\le\rho k/4\le k/16.
\tag{10}
\]
For m>=1, partition the feasible sources with exactly m threes into the high-one sources, having m_1>=k/4 ones, and the low-one sources, having m_1<k/4 ones. Write their total original masses as Z_m^{hi} and Z_m^{lo}. All sources have m<=R and m_2+m<=R, where m_2 counts their twos.

For a high-one source x, let
\[
L(\mathbf x)=\{j:x_j=1,\ B_j\le\min_{i:x_i=3}B_i\},\qquad
\ell(\mathbf x)=|L(\mathbf x)|.
\]
By (1), ell(x)>=rho*k. For each m-element subset J of L(x), construct a target y by changing every three of x to two, and changing precisely the ones in J to twos. Different J give different targets. If I={i:x_i=3}, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf y)-\Gamma(\mathbf x)
&=\sum_{j\in J}(B_j-1)-\sum_{i\in I}(C_{4,i}-B_i)\\
&\le\sum_{j\in J}B_j-\sum_{i\in I}B_i\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The last inequality follows because all m costs B_j are at most min_{i in I}B_i. Thus every target is feasible and has at least the original weight of its source by (7). This proves the energy comparison for the actual heterogeneous fourth costs.

For a fixed feasible target y with q twos, every inverse source is specified by two disjoint m-element subsets I,J of its two-coordinates: the coordinates in I were threes and those in J were ones. All remaining coordinates are unchanged. These choices determine the source vector uniquely; there is no additional pairing or matching multiplicity. The indegree is zero if q<2m. Otherwise it is at most
\[
\binom qm\binom{q-m}m\le\binom Rm\binom{R-m}m
\le\frac{R^{2m}}{(m!)^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The source outdegree is exactly binom(ell(x),m), and by (10) it is at least
\[
\binom{\ell(\mathbf x)}m
\ge\frac{(\ell(\mathbf x)-m+1)^m}{m!}
\ge\frac{(3\rho k/4)^m}{m!}.
\tag{13}
\]
The graph in (8), with all feasible three-site vectors allowed as targets, now gives
\[
Z_m^{hi}\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{2R^2}{\rho k}\right)^m Z_3.
\tag{14}
\]
Here 2 is a convenient upper bound for 4/3. If 2m>R, (12) simply says the high-one graph is empty; this causes no exception to (14).

For the low-one sources fix the integer
\[
h=\min\left\{B_{\min}-1,\left\lfloor\frac{k}{8R}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
This h is common to all these graphs, is at least 1, and satisfies hm<=k/8 whenever m<=R. Fix m and also fix the source counts a=m_1 and b=m_2. Let m_0=k-a-b-m. A low-one source in this class satisfies
\[
a<k/4,\qquad b+m\le R\le k/16,\qquad
m_0>11k/16,\qquad hm\le k/8.
\tag{16}
\]
For each hm-element subset J of its zero-coordinates, change all its threes to twos and the zeros in J to ones. These binom(m_0,hm) choices give distinct targets, now with b+m twos and a+hm ones. The actual heterogeneous energy change satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf y)-\Gamma(\mathbf x)
&=hm-\sum_{i:x_i=3}(C_{4,i}-B_i)\\
&\le hm-\sum_{i:x_i=3}(B_i-1)\\
&\le-m(B_{\min}-1-h)\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus these edges also compare the original full tails in the correct direction.

A target in this graph has at most
\[
\binom{b+m}m\binom{a+hm}{hm}
\tag{18}
\]
inverse sources: choose the m two-coordinates formerly equal to 3, and the hm one-coordinates formerly equal to 0. These choices again specify the source uniquely. Some specified sources may be infeasible or fail the class restrictions, which can only decrease the indegree. Weighted counting (8) therefore bounds the mass of this source class by the mass of the entire feasible target class with counts (b+m,a+hm), multiplied by
\[
\binom{b+m}m\frac{\binom{a+hm}{hm}}{\binom{m_0}{hm}}.
\tag{19}
\]
This is a ratio of actual graph degree bounds, rather than a ratio of equal-energy multinomial masses.

Set
\[
\vartheta=\frac{a+hm}{m_0-hm+1}.
\]
Expanding the two binomial coefficients as products gives
\[
\frac{\binom{a+hm}{hm}}{\binom{m_0}{hm}}
=\prod_{r=0}^{hm-1}\frac{a+hm-r}{m_0-r}
\le\vartheta^{hm}.
\tag{20}
\]
The bounds (16) give
\[
\vartheta\le\frac{3k/8}{9k/16}=\frac23.
\tag{21}
\]
There is also a bound sensitive to the available energy, valid for these heterogeneous costs. Indeed, a<=Gamma(x)<=T<B_min(R+1), and h<=B_min-1, m<=R. Since R>=1,
\[
a+hm\le B_{\min}(R+1)+(B_{\min}-1)R\le3B_{\min}R.
\]
Together with m_0-hm+1>=9k/16 this implies
\[
\vartheta\le\frac{6B_{\min}R}{k}.
\tag{22}
\]
No upper bound on any individual B_i or C_{4,i} has entered this estimate.

We next prove
\[
\vartheta^h\le\frac{32R}{k}.
\tag{23}
\]
If h=B_min-1, put u=R/k. From (21)-(22),
\[
\vartheta^h\le\min\{2/3,6B_{\min}u\}^{B_{\min}-1}\le12u.
\tag{24}
\]
For the last inequality, if u>=1/(9B_min), division of (2/3)^{B_min-1} by u is at most 9B_min(2/3)^{B_min-1}. If u<=1/(9B_min), division of (6B_min*u)^{B_min-1} by u is nondecreasing in u, because B_min>=2, and at that endpoint has the same upper bound. For every integer B_min>=2 this upper bound is at most 12: its value at 2 is 12, and the ratio of consecutive values is 2(B_min+1)/(3B_min)<=1.

If h=floor(k/(8R)), (10) implies k/(8R)>=2 and therefore h>=k/(16R). Bernoulli's inequality gives (3/2)^h>=1+h/2>=h/2. Consequently
\[
\vartheta^h\le(2/3)^h\le2/h\le32R/k.
\tag{25}
\]
At least one of these cases holds, including when the two terms in (15) tie. This proves (23).

As b+m<=R, (19)-(20) and (23) are at most
\[
\frac{R^m}{m!}(\vartheta^h)^m
\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{k}\right)^m.
\tag{26}
\]
For the fixed m and h, the target counts (b+m,a+hm) determine (a,b) uniquely. Thus distinct low-one source count classes use disjoint target count classes. Summing the weighted estimate (19) over these classes and using (26) proves
\[
Z_m^{lo}\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{32R^2}{k}\right)^m Z_3.
\tag{27}
\]
In this summation targets of different energies retain their individual weights. The disjointness is solely a statement about their coordinate counts, so it does not presume homogeneous costs.

The part of Z_4 with no threes is exactly Z_3. Summing (14) and (27), extending the nonnegative series over all m>=1, yields under (10)
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}
&\le e^{2R^2/(\rho k)}+e^{32R^2/k}-1\\
&\le\exp\!\left(\left(\frac2\rho+32\right)\frac{R^2}{k}\right)
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{10R^2}{\rho k}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The second inequality follows from (e^u-1)(e^v-1)>=0 for u,v>=0. The last uses rho<=1/4. This proves (3) and the small-R part of (2).

If R>rho*k/4, (9) gives
\[
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le e^{(\log2)R}
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{4(\log2)R^2}{\rho k}\right)
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{10R^2}{\rho k}\right).
\tag{29}
\]
Together with the R=0 case this proves (2) for all parameters, including small k and R>=k. Substitution of R^2/k<=A^2 proves (4).

To obtain (5), use accepted `cd9532b1d59c5977` with its k,T,beta and B_i identical to the present parameters and with its original spatial coordinates and ambient halfspaces unchanged. Its support is exactly
\[
A_T^{(3)}=A_T^{(4)}\cap(\{0,1,2\}^k\times\mathbb Z),
\]
and its normalizer Z is the present Z_3. Let p^{(3)} denote that accepted law and put alpha=Z_3/Z_4. For every z in A_T^{(3)}, the full original law satisfies p_z=alpha*p^{(3)}_z. Every ambient halfspace has p-mass at least alpha times its p^{(3)}-mass. Taking the infima over the same containing halfspaces gives tau_p(z)>=alpha*tau_{p^{(3)}}(z). Thus the accepted moment bound and summation over these original atoms give
\[
M_3(p)\ge\alpha^4 M_3(p^{(3)})
\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{30}
\]
The one sampling factor and three depth factors account for the fourth power. Applying (2) to alpha proves the first inequality in (5); applying (4) proves the second. In particular the denominator throughout is the full original Z_4, with Z_3 used only as an explicitly compared mass portion.

To verify (6), at most k/8 coordinates have B_i>B_min. Any source with at least k/4 ones therefore has at least k/8 one-coordinates with B_j=B_min. Each of them satisfies B_j<=min_{i:x_i=3}B_i whenever a three is present. Hence (1) holds with rho=1/8, regardless of all fourth costs and all other second costs. This proves the stated genuinely heterogeneous special case.

For clarity about the necessary scope of the result, the eligibility condition cannot be omitted from an estimate exp(KR^2/k) with any fixed K. Here is a complete exact example. For every integer k>=2 set beta=0, B_1=k^2, T=100k^2, B_i=T+1 for i>=2, and C_{4,i}=2B_i for all i. Then B_min=k^2 and R=100. Coordinates i>=2 can only be 0 or 1 in a feasible vector. If s is their number of ones, the energies at the four values of coordinate 1 are s,s+1,s+k^2,s+2k^2, all at most T. The sum of s over their 2^{k-1} binary choices is 2^{k-1}(k-1)/2. Summing their complete beta=0 tails T-E+1 exactly gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_3&=2^{k-1}\left(299k^2+\frac{7-3k}{2}\right),\\
Z_4&=2^{k-1}(397k^2+5-2k),\\
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}&=\frac{397k^2+5-2k}{299k^2+(7-3k)/2}
\longrightarrow\frac{397}{299}>1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
By contrast exp(KR^2/k)=exp(10000K/k) tends to 1. This family fails (1) for every rho>0: take coordinate 1 equal to 3 and ceil(k/4) of the other coordinates equal to 1. The vector is feasible, but all its one-coordinates have B_j=T+1>B_1 and hence none is eligible. Therefore (31) does not conflict with (2); it verifies the substantive need to retain (1) in the conclusion.
