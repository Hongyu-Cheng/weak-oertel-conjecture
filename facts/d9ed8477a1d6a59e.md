## Statement

Let \(P_{\mathrm{tet}}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(31,53,78)\}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{tet}}=P_{\mathrm{tet}}\cap\mathbb Z^3\). The alias map from accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7 is \(P\mapsto P_{\mathrm{tet}}\) and its three-dimensional support \(A\mapsto B_{\mathrm{tet}}\); its census points \(p_0,\ldots,p_{19}\) retain their coordinates and indices. In this statement \(A\) denotes the new four-dimensional lift, not the predecessor's base support.

For every affine function \(\ell:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) with rational coefficients that is nonnegative on \(P_{\mathrm{tet}}\), put \(h_i=\lfloor\ell(p_i)\rfloor\) and

\(A=\{(p_i,t):0\le i\le19,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le t\le h_i\}\).

For an integer center \(w\in\mathbb Z^4\), define \(r_A(w)=|\{v\in\mathbb Z^4:w+v,w-v\in A\}|\), with oriented displacements and zero included once at a supported center. Let \(s(A)\) be the number of classes in \(\mathbb Z^4/2\mathbb Z^4\) containing exactly one point of \(A\). If \(\max_{w\in\mathbb Z^4}r_A(w)\le3\), then \(|A|+s(A)\le40\). In particular, no such roof can have a singleton vertical fiber, maximum reflection count at most three, and \(|A|+s(A)>40\) simultaneously.

The last table lists every unordered distinct base pair with an integer midpoint, each exactly once. Its displayed relation is \(p_i+p_k=2p_j\), where \(j\) is the center index and \((i,k)\) its endpoint pair. Thus the four vertex indices \(0,1,2,19\) have no nonzero base pair, while each of the sixteen other indices has exactly one.

For clarity about the rational roofs being quantified over, write \(\ell(x,y,z)=a+bx+cy+dz\), where \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Q\). Nonnegativity on the tetrahedron is exactly the four inequalities \(a\ge0\), \(a+b\ge0\), \(a+c\ge0\), and \(a+31b+53c+78d\ge0\). The exact floor conditions are \(h_i\le a+bx_i+cy_i+dz_i<h_i+1\) for all twenty displayed points \(p_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i)\). No restriction on the denominators is imposed. In particular, for every row of the midpoint table, affinity gives \(\ell(p_i)+\ell(p_k)=2\ell(p_j)\). Combining this identity with the floor inequalities gives the precise necessary pair-sum bounds

\(2h_j-1\le h_i+h_k\le2h_j+1\).

Indeed, \(h_i+h_k\le2\ell(p_j)<2h_j+2\), while \(h_i+h_k>2\ell(p_j)-2\ge2h_j-2\); the claimed bounds follow because the sum of floors is integral. We use the full affine identity below, not an identity obtained by replacing the roof values with their floors.

The set \(Q=\{(x,t):x\in P_{\mathrm{tet}},\ 0\le t\le\ell(x)\}\) is convex and has \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^4=A\). Consequently an integer midpoint of any two points of \(A\) belongs to \(A\), and every integer center outside \(A\) has reflection count zero. This also shows that \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^4\), without requiring \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(A)\).

For such a roof, \(\max_{w\in\mathbb Z^4}r_A(w)\le3\) holds if and only if the following three conditions hold:

1. \(0\le h_i\le3\) for every \(i\).
2. \(h_j\le1\) for every nonvertex index \(3\le j\le18\).
3. For every endpoint pair \((i,k)\) in the midpoint table, if both \(h_i\) and \(h_k\) are positive, then \(h_i=h_k=1\).

We first prove necessity. If \(h_i\ge4\), the center \((p_i,2)\) has its zero displacement and the four vertical displacements with last coordinate \(\pm1,\pm2\), so its count is at least five. This proves condition 1.

For condition 2, suppose \(p_j\) has the distinct base pair \(p_i,p_k\) and \(h_j\ge2\). Affinity gives \(\ell(p_i)+\ell(p_k)=2\ell(p_j)\ge4\). Hence \(h_i+h_k>2\), so \(h_i+h_k\ge3\). The sums of integer heights in the two endpoint fibers fill every integer from zero to \(h_i+h_k\). In particular there are \(u\in\{0,\ldots,h_i\}\) and \(v\in\{0,\ldots,h_k\}\) with \(u+v=2\). The center \((p_j,1)\) then has both the vertical unordered pair \(\{(p_j,0),(p_j,2)\}\) and the distinct unordered pair \(\{(p_i,u),(p_k,v)\}\), in addition to its zero displacement. Their horizontal projections distinguish the two pairs. The count is at least five, proving condition 2.

For condition 3, suppose, after interchanging the endpoints if necessary, that \(h_i\ge2\) and \(h_k\ge1\). The two unordered lifted pairs

\(\{(p_i,1),(p_k,1)\}\) and \(\{(p_i,2),(p_k,0)\}\)

are distinct and have the common integer midpoint \((p_j,1)\). This midpoint belongs to \(A\) by the convexity already established. Its zero displacement and these two pairs give a count at least five. Thus condition 3 is necessary.

For sufficiency and a full enumeration of possible repeated lifted pairs, assume conditions 1–3. Above a base vertex, every reflected pair is vertical, because there is no distinct base pair centered there. The count at a supported point \((p_i,t)\) is exactly \(1+2\min\{t,h_i-t\}\), which is at most three when \(h_i\le3\).

Above a nonvertex \(p_j\), the fiber has height zero or one, so it has no nonzero vertical pair. At height zero, the unique nonzero base pair has only its bottom-bottom lift, and the reflection count is exactly three. If \(h_j=1\), a nonzero pair centered at \((p_j,1)\) must project to the unique pair \((i,k)\) from the table. The number of its unordered lifts is exactly

\(\max\{0,\min(h_i,2)-\max(0,2-h_k)+1\}\),

because the height of its endpoint above \(p_i\) determines the other endpoint height by their sum being two. The precise pair-sum bounds give \(h_i+h_k\in\{1,2,3\}\). The following table lists every possible ordered floor pair and all the corresponding ordered endpoint-height pairs summing to two:

```text
(h_i,h_k)    lifted height pairs (u,v)
(0,1)        none
(1,0)        none
(0,2)        (0,2)
(1,1)        (1,1)
(2,0)        (2,0)
(0,3)        (0,2)
(1,2)        (0,2), (1,1)
(2,1)        (1,1), (2,0)
(3,0)        (2,0)
```

Here the base endpoints are distinct and fixed, so different ordered height pairs in a row give different unordered pairs of points. Exactly the rows \((1,2)\) and \((2,1)\) would create repeated nonzero pairs at that center. Condition 3 excludes them; all other rows give at most one unordered pair and hence reflection count at most three. This verifies every supported center. Centers outside \(A\) have count zero as proved above, so the criterion is sufficient as well as necessary.

It remains to bound \(|A|+s(A)\) under these conditions. Fix one base parity class and let \(N\) be its size, which is two or three. Its points contribute only to the two lifted parity classes having this same base parity, distinguished by the parity of the last coordinate. Since \(h_i\le3\), the number of points of its fiber with even last coordinate is \(1+\mathbf1_{\{h_i\ge2\}}\), and the number with odd last coordinate is \(\mathbf1_{\{h_i\ge1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{h_i=3\}}\). The even lifted class therefore has at least \(N\ge2\) points and never contributes to \(s(A)\). The odd lifted class contributes to \(s(A)\) precisely when its size is one.

Each base parity class of size two consists entirely of nonvertices, as the displayed parity table shows. Its two floor heights therefore belong to \(\{0,1\}\). If \(m\in\{0,1,2\}\) of them are one, the class contributes exactly \(2+m+\mathbf1_{\{m=1\}}\le4\) to \(|A|+s(A)\).

Each base parity class of size three consists of one vertex and two nonvertices. If the vertex floor height is at most one, all three heights belong to \(\{0,1\}\). If \(m\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) of them are one, its contribution is \(3+m+\mathbf1_{\{m=1\}}\le6\).

Otherwise the vertex height is \(H\in\{2,3\}\). Every pair of distinct points in this base parity class has an integer midpoint and is included in the complete midpoint table. Condition 3 applied to the vertex and each of its two class mates forces both nonvertex floor heights to be zero. The lifted even class then has four points. The odd class has one point if \(H=2\) and two if \(H=3\). Thus the contribution to \(|A|+s(A)\) is exactly \(H+3+\mathbf1_{\{H=2\}}=6\).

There are exactly four base parity classes of size two and four of size three. They partition all sixteen ambient lifted parity classes into disjoint pairs. Summing the proved contributions gives \(|A|+s(A)\le4\cdot4+4\cdot6=40\). This covers all rational affine roofs satisfying the reflection hypothesis; any singleton vertical fiber is simply a floor height equal to zero and does not change the argument. The requested strict inequality is therefore impossible. The proof establishes only this fixed-base ceiling and has no dimension-growing conclusion.
