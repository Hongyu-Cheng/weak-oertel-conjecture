## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $v_0,\ldots,v_d$ span $\mathbb R^d$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$ as their unique linear dependence up to scale. Set $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. Let $A\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body with every cut $v_i\cdot x=-1$ proper, and let $g$ be its centroid. Put $U_i=|A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge-1\}|$ and $\ell_i=\min_A v_i\cdot x$. If every fixed-cap loss derivative under contraction about $g$ equals $\frac{d^2}{d+1}|A|^{1/d}U_i^{(d-1)/d}$, then $A=\{x:v_i\cdot x\ge\ell_i\ \forall i\}$ is a simplex. In particular $-T\subset\operatorname{int} A$ and $|A|>|T|$. If $|A|\le|T|$, every positively weighted sum with a strictly positive weight at each index has strict surplus over the sum of the sharp loss bounds.

## Proof

By the single-cut equality characterization in the sharp centroid contraction theorem, $A=\operatorname{conv}(\{p_i\}\cup F_i)$ for each $i$, where $p_i$ is the unique maximizer of $v_i$ and $F_i=A\cap\{v_i\cdot x=\ell_i\}$ has dimension $d-1$. In $d=1$ the two normals are opposite and the endpoints are distinct. In $d\ge2$ every pair of normals is linearly independent by the unique dependence. If $p_i=p_j=p$, then representing a point of $F_i$ using $p$ and $F_j$ forces its coefficient on $p$ to be zero, since $v_i\cdot p>\ell_i$ while $v_i\cdot y\ge\ell_i$ for all $y\in F_j$. This would put $F_i$ in the intersection of the two independent base hyperplanes, contradicting its dimension. Thus the $p_i$ are distinct.

Each $p_j$ is extreme in $A$ since it uniquely maximizes $v_j$. The pyramid representation at $i\ne j$ then forces $p_j\in F_i$. Write $L=-\sum_i\ell_i>0$, since each $\ell_i<-1$. The affine functions $\beta_i(x)=(v_i\cdot x-\ell_i)/L$ sum to one and take the values $\beta_i(p_j)=\delta_{ij}$. The affine map given by the $\beta_i$ is injective by spanning. Each $x$ satisfying the stated halfspace inequalities therefore equals $\sum_i\beta_i(x)p_i$: the two points have the same affine coordinates. The $p_i$ are affinely independent by those coordinates. The halfspace intersection is their simplex, lies in $A$ by convexity, and contains $A$ by the definitions of $\ell_i$.

The set $T$ contains a ball about zero and is bounded, since the only direction with all $v_i\cdot x\le0$ has all values zero and hence is zero. At each point of $-T$ all the $\beta_i$ are strictly positive because $\ell_i<-1$. Thus the compact set $-T$ is inside the interior of $A$, implying strictly smaller volume. Each loss surplus is nonnegative by the sharp bound. A sum with all weights positive can vanish only if all individual equalities hold, which forces the strict volume comparison just proved.
