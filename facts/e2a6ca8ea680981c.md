## Statement

Fix finitely many positive $d$-simplex fibers $K_z$ with vertices $x_{zi}$, $0\le i\le d$, a point $y=(z_0,x_0)$, and an ambient normal $p=(a,u)$. Put $w_z=|K_z|$, let $A_z$ have rows $(z-z_0,x_{zi}-x_0)$, and define $P(c)=\Pr(\sum_i c_iE_i\ge0)$ for independent unit exponentials. The cap through $y$ has mass $F(p)=\sum_z w_zP(A_zp)$. On a strict sign chamber, where every margin is nonzero, $F$ is $C^2$ and
$
\nabla F(p)=\sum_z w_zA_z^\top\nabla P(A_zp),\qquad
\nabla^2F(p)=\sum_z w_zA_z^\top\nabla^2P(A_zp)A_z .
$
Full and empty fibers contribute zero derivatives. For a proper margin vector in such a chamber, $\nabla^2P(c)c=-\nabla P(c)$ and for $i\ne j$, $c_i\ne c_j$, one has $P_{ij}=(P_i-P_j)/(c_i-c_j)$, allowing either sign. In particular the statement applies to common-oriented simplex homothets $x_{zi}=t_z+s_zv_i$.

## Proof

For independent unit exponentials, the change to their sum and their ratios has Jacobian equal to the $d$-th power of the sum. The ratios therefore have the uniform barycentric law, giving the cap formula. Simplices with the same oriented facet normals are positive homothets up to translation: their $d+1$ support values modulo the rank-$d$ translation subspace have one degree of freedom, with positivity determined by the positive dependence of the normals and the strict support inequalities at an interior point.

Here is a strict-chamber justification of the general signed divided difference. Let $V=\sum_k c_kE_k$, with every coefficient bounded away from zero in a compact parameter neighborhood in its strict sign chamber. Write $f_i$ for the density of $V+c_iE'$ and $g_{ij}$ for the density of $V+c_iE'+c_jE''$, also when $i=j$, with independent unit exponentials $E',E''$. Their characteristic functions are products of factors $(1-itc_k)^{-1}$ for the original and added phases. The inverse Fourier integrals for $f_i$ and $g_{ij}'$ are absolutely convergent and their integrands dominated locally uniformly in $c$, since $d\ge1$. They give bounded continuous functions jointly in location and $c$.

For a smooth probability mollifier of compact support $\varphi_\varepsilon$, replace $\mathbf1_{\{V\ge0\}}$ by its smooth step with derivative $\varphi_\varepsilon$. Differentiate in $c$ under expectation, justified by the finite exponential moments. The first derivative is $\mathbb E[E_i\varphi_\varepsilon(V)]=\int\varphi_\varepsilon f_i$ by exponential size bias. The distinct-coordinate second derivative is $\mathbb E[E_iE_j\varphi_\varepsilon'(V)]=-\int\varphi_\varepsilon g_{ij}'$, by two independent size biases and integration by parts. On the diagonal the size bias by $E_i^2$ adds two phases at coefficient $c_i$ and a factor two, giving $-2\int\varphi_\varepsilon g_{ii}'$. These derivatives converge locally uniformly as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, by joint continuity of the indicated densities and their derivatives. The smoothed functions converge pointwise to $P$ since $V$ has no atom at zero. Integrating the derivatives along coordinate segments in the chamber then gives $P\in C^2$, $P_i=f_i(0)$ and $P_{ij}=-g_{ij}'(0)$ for $i\ne j$.

The scaled exponential density at any nonzero scale $c$ has distributional resolvent $(1+c\,d/dx)k_c=\delta_0$, checked by integrating by parts on its half-line support. Hence $(1+c_i\,d/dx)g_{ij}=f_j$ and $(1+c_j\,d/dx)g_{ij}=f_i$. Subtracting proves the divided difference for distinct coefficients, including opposite signs. Positive homogeneity of degree zero gives $\nabla P(c)\cdot c=0$, whose derivative gives the Hessian identity. Full and empty traces are constant on their strict chambers. The displayed joint derivatives now follow by the ordinary linear chain rule.
