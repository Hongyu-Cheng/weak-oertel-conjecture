## Statement

Let $d=m+1\ge2$, let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a centered full-dimensional ellipsoid, and let $r$ be positive and concave on $[a,b]$ with $a<0<b$. Put $K=\{(X,z):a\le z\le b,\ X\in r(z)D\}$. For a finite menu of unit normals $u_i$, let $S_i=\mathcal H^{d-1}(K\cap u_i^\perp)$ and $p_i=|K\cap\{Y:u_i\cdot Y\ge0\}|/|K|$. Suppose each section has centroid $0$, and $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\theta_i=1$, $\sum_i\theta_i S_i u_i=0$. Then
$$
\sum_i\theta_i p_i\ge c_d,\qquad
c_2=\frac49,\qquad
c_d=\frac1{1+\max\{4/3,\sqrt{2(d-1)/d}\}}\quad(d\ge3).
$$
In particular, $c_d=3/7$ for $3\le d\le9$, $c_d>2/5$ for $d\ge10$, and $c_d\ge(d/(d+1))^d$.

## Proof

Positivity and concavity on the interior give $0\in\operatorname{int}K$. If necessary, replace the two endpoint values by the one-sided limits from the interior to make the profile upper semicontinuous. The changes lie on $z=a$ and $z=b$, which are volume-null and meet each central cut in a set of section measure zero because $a<0<b$. This leaves all the cap fractions, section areas, and centroids unchanged. The resulting $K$ is a compact convex body by upper semicontinuity and concavity. Apply a block-diagonal invertible map $T$ that sends $D$ to the Euclidean unit ball and fixes the vertical coordinate. A unit normal $u$ is carried to the unit normal $T^{-\mathsf T}u/\lVert T^{-\mathsf T}u\rVert$. The hyperplane area multiplier is $|\det T|\lVert T^{-\mathsf T}u\rVert$. Indeed, adjoining $u$ to an orthonormal basis of $u^\perp$, the perpendicular component of $Tu$ along the new unit normal is $1/\lVert T^{-\mathsf T}u\rVert$, and the full volume multiplier is $|\det T|$. Thus $S_i u_i$ is carried to $|\det T|T^{-\mathsf T}(S_i u_i)$. Each cap fraction is invariant under $T$, and each section centroid is carried to its image because its section area multiplier is constant. Balance is therefore preserved with the same weights.

In the transformed coordinates write the unit normal of cap $i$ as $(h_i,v_i)$. The two unit normals $(\lVert h_i\rVert e_1,v_i)$ and $(-\lVert h_i\rVert e_1,v_i)$ are obtained from it by horizontal orthogonal transformations. The transformed body is invariant under these transformations, so both caps retain the transformed cap fraction and section area and have section centroid $0$. Assign each weight $\theta_i/2$. Their contributions to the horizontal balance cancel, and their combined vertical contribution is the original transformed one. This also covers $h_i=0$ or $v_i=0$. The reflected menu has the same weighted mean and lies in the plane spanned by $e_1$ and the vertical axis. The transformed body contains $0$ in its interior, so its projection onto this plane does too. Full section centering implies projected centering. Fact 03ec0bc313e02df5 now gives exactly the stated bounds.
