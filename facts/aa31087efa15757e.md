## Statement

Use the half-mass metronoid, weighted affine-defect dual, and affine-retainable mass in the normalization of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d. Use the endpoint stress field and its loads in the normalization of accepted fact 9c47a4888e8a2947, the load-minimal optimizer face \(\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\) from accepted fact f78198b3e936e0b3, the stress-frame score \(\mathfrak F_*\) from accepted fact d0338841f89a9c80, and the centroid-selector score supplied by accepted fact 844dca4a76115be3.

Fix an integer \(k\geq402\), put
\[
 n:=2,
 \qquad
 d:=2k,
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and decompose the continuous space as
\[
 \mathbb R^d=H_1\oplus H_2,
 \qquad
 H_1=H_2=\mathbb R^k.
\]
Let \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k\) be the standard basis of \(H_1\), let \(\mathbf 1:=\sum_i\xi_i\), and define
\[
 u_i:=\sqrt{\frac{k}{k-1}}
 \left(\xi_i-\frac1k\mathbf 1\right)
 \in H_1
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq k).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_k\) be the standard basis of \(H_2\).

Put
\[
 S:=\left(k,\frac{k(k+1)}2\right)\in\mathbb Z^2
\]
and, for \(1\leq i\leq k\), put
\[
 p_i:=-S+2\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(1,j)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Then the cyclic list
\[
 p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_k,-p_1,-p_2,\ldots,-p_k
\tag{3}
\]
is the vertex list of a centrally symmetric strictly convex lattice polygon, and all its vertices have one common parity. Define
\[
 y_i^+:=p_i,
 \qquad
 y_i^-:=-p_i,
 \qquad
 e_i:=\{y_i^+,y_i^-\},
 \qquad
 m_{e_i}:=0.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(B_d\) be the Euclidean unit ball in \(\mathbb R^d\). There is a number \(R_d>0\) such that, if
\[
 a:=\operatorname{vol}_d(R_dB_d),
\]
then
\[
 \mathsf M_{a/2}(R_dB_d)=B_d.
\tag{5}
\]
Define endpoint centroids
\[
 c_i^+:=-2u_i+\eta_i,
 \qquad
 c_i^-:=-2u_i-\eta_i
\tag{6}
\]
and the convex body
\[
 C:=\operatorname{conv}
 \bigcup_{i=1}^k
 \left(
   \{y_i^+\}\times(c_i^++R_dB_d)
   \ \cup\
   \{y_i^-\}\times(c_i^-+R_dB_d)
 \right)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{7}
\]
Give every edge the common weight
\[
 t_{e_i}:=t:=\frac a4.
\tag{8}
\]

Then all of the following hold.

1. The set \(C\) is a full-dimensional convex body. At every displayed endpoint its fiber is exactly the displayed translated ball. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal K_{y_i^\pm}=c_i^\pm+B_d,
 \qquad
 D_{y_i^\pm}=B_d,
 \qquad
 D_{e_i}=B_d,
 \qquad
 L_{e_i}=-2u_i+B_d.
\tag{9}
\]
The family \(\{e_i\}\) with weights (8) is an arbitrary legal endpoint-disjoint off-diagonal same-parity matching and satisfies the stronger endpoint bound
\[
 t_{e_i}=\frac{a_{y_i^+}}4=\frac{a_{y_i^-}}4.
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover
\[
 -2u_i\in L_{e_i}\cap\mathsf M_t(C_0)
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq k).
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, one may choose the endpoint half-mass contacts \(c_i^\pm\in\mathcal K_{y_i^\pm}\), whose edgewise averages are the points \(-2u_i\) in (11).

2. The stress
\[
 v_{e_i}^*:=t u_i
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq k)
\tag{12}
\]
is the unique dual optimizer. Hence
\[
 \mathcal O(t)=\mathcal V_{\min}(t)=\{v^*\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The point \(v^*\) is exposed even in the full dual-feasible set. The affine primal optimizer with value zero at the common midpoint misses every \(L_{e_i}\). All edge loads and endpoint loads are
\[
 \omega_{e_i}=t,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{y_i^+}=\sigma_{y_i^-}=\frac t2,
 \qquad
 T_*=kt.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus every missed-edge pair sum is fixed and saturated:
\[
 \sigma_{y_i^+}+\sigma_{y_i^-}=t.
\tag{15}
\]
The \(k\) sets \(L_{e_i}\) are pairwise disjoint, and the exactly affine-retainable matching mass is
\[
 R_C(t)=t,
 \qquad
 Q:=\sum_i t_{e_i}=kt.
\tag{16}
\]

3. Let
\[
 Y:=\{y_i^+,y_i^-:1\leq i\leq k\}.
\]
For every nonempty \(A\subseteq Y\), let
\[
 q(A):=|\{i:A\cap e_i\ne\varnothing\}|,
 \qquad
 \rho_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A),
\]
and define its centroid residual rank by
\[
 r_A^c:=
 \min_{s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s(y):y\in A\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 r_A^c\geq
 \max\{0,q(A)-\rho_A-1\}
 \geq
 \max\{0,q(A)-3\}.}
\tag{18}
\]
For the full endpoint set, the stronger bound
\[
 \boxed{r_Y^c\geq k-2}
\tag{19}
\]
holds.

4. For a nonempty endpoint subset \(A\subseteq Y\), define the exact aligned centroid-selector score
\[
 \mathcal S_{v^*}(A):=
 2^{2-\rho_A}
 \frac{4\gamma_d\sum_{y\in A}\omega_{e(y)}}{r_A^c+1}.
\tag{20}
\]
Accepted fact 844dca4a76115be3 shows that (20) is the score of one legal centroid selector and is at most \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\). In this example
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal S_{v^*}(A)<96t
 \quad\text{for every nonempty }A\subseteq Y.}
\tag{21}
\]
All endpoints have one parity and all endpoint loads equal \(t/2\), so the frame convention of accepted fact d0338841f89a9c80 gives
\[
 \boxed{\mathfrak F_*=2t.}
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{T_*-8\mathfrak F_*}{4}
 =\frac{k-16}{4}t
 \geq96.5t,
\tag{23}
\]
and hence no nonempty subset satisfies
\[
 \mathcal S_{v^*}(A)
 \geq
 \frac{T_*-8\mathfrak F_*}{4}.
\tag{CCE}
\]

The construction is a genuine-convex-body example for the broader arbitrary-legal-matching class. It is not asserted that the weights, endpoint contacts, or matching are produced by the canonical atlas, pooling, endpoint-core, or dyadic-bin construction retained in accepted fact 9c47a4888e8a2947. Failure of (CCE) is not failure of HSF: \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) may be large because of selector points or integer fibers not used in (20). No statement above proves or disproves the weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

We first audit the base polygon. Put \(a_j:=(1,j)\). From (2), the consecutive edge vectors from \(p_1\) through \(p_k\) to \(-p_1\) are
\[
 2a_1,2a_2,\ldots,2a_k,
\tag{24}
\]
because \(S=\sum_{j=1}^ka_j\). The remaining consecutive edge vectors are
\[
 -2a_1,-2a_2,\ldots,-2a_k.
\tag{25}
\]
The arguments of \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\) are strictly increasing between \(0\) and \(\pi/2\). The turn from \(a_k\) to \(-a_1\), and the closing turn from \(-a_k\) to \(a_1\), both have angle
\[
 \pi+\arg(a_1)-\arg(a_k)\in(0,\pi).
\]
Thus every cyclic turn in (24)-(25) lies strictly between zero and \(\pi\). The closed polygon is strictly convex, so all points in (3) are distinct vertices. Every \(p_i\) is congruent to \(-S\) modulo \(2\), and negation preserves parity, proving the parity assertion. No \(p_i\) is zero: its first coordinate can vanish only when \(k\) is even and \(i-1=k/2\), while at that index its second coordinate differs from zero by \(-k^2/4\). Thus every edge in (4) is off diagonal.

We next prove (5). Let \(a_0:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_d)\) and
\[
 K_0:=\mathsf M_{a_0/2}(B_d).
\]
The metronoid definition in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d shows that \(K_0\) is compact and convex. Orthogonal changes of variables preserve the admissible densities on \(B_d\), so \(K_0\) is invariant under every orthogonal transformation. The constant density \(1/2\) shows that \(0\in K_0\), and the indicator of a half-ball cut by any central hyperplane has a nonzero mean in its normal direction. Hence \(K_0=\beta_dB_d\) for some \(\beta_d>0\). Metronoids commute with dilations, so choosing
\[
 R_d:=\beta_d^{-1}
\]
gives (5).

The projection of \(C\) to the base space is the strictly convex polygon above. If \((y_i^\pm,x)\in C\), then any convex representation of its base coordinate as a combination of the displayed vertices uses only the vertex \(y_i^\pm\), because that base point is extreme. Therefore the fiber at \(y_i^\pm\) is exactly \(c_i^\pm+R_dB_d\). Conversely that entire translated ball is included by construction. Three noncollinear base vertices and positive barycentric coefficients produce, over the interior of their triangle, a Minkowski combination containing a translate of \(R_dB_d\). Hence \(C\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) and is a convex body.

Translation covariance of the metronoid and (5) now give
\[
 \mathcal K_{y_i^\pm}=c_i^\pm+B_d,
 \qquad
 D_{y_i^\pm}=B_d.
\]
Equations (6) then give all identities in (9). Global endpoint-disjointness is immediate from the distinct vertex pairs. The common-parity and off-diagonal properties were proved above, and each endpoint has matching degree \(t=a/4\leq a/2\). This proves legality and (10).

Convexity of \(C\) gives, at the common midpoint zero,
\[
 \frac12(c_i^++R_dB_d)+\frac12(c_i^-+R_dB_d)
 =-2u_i+R_dB_d
 \subseteq C_0.
\tag{26}
\]
The concentric ball in the right side of (26) with radius \(4^{-1/d}R_d\) has volume \(a/4=t\). Its indicator is an admissible density of mass \(t\), and its normalized first moment is its center \(-2u_i\). This proves (11). The constant half-density on each endpoint fiber has normalized first moment equal to its centroid, so \(c_i^\pm\in\mathcal K_{y_i^\pm}\), proving the contact assertion.

We now analyze the dual exactly. From (1),
\[
 \lVert u_i\rVert_2=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^ku_i=0,
 \qquad
 \lVert u_i-u_j\rVert_2
 =\sqrt{\frac{2k}{k-1}}
 \quad(i\ne j).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus (12) satisfies both affine moment cancellations, because all edge midpoints are zero, and it satisfies every capacity constraint with equality:
\[
 h_{D_{e_i}}(v_{e_i}^*)=\lVert tu_i\rVert_2=t.
\]
For any dual-feasible \(v=(v_{e_i})\), equations (9) and the translation formula for support functions give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J(v)
 &:=-\sum_i h_{L_{e_i}}(v_{e_i})\\
 &=\sum_i\left(2\langle u_i,v_{e_i}\rangle
                 -\lVert v_{e_i}\rVert_2\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
For each coordinate,
\[
 2\langle u_i,v_{e_i}\rangle-\lVert v_{e_i}\rVert_2
 \leq\lVert v_{e_i}\rVert_2
 \leq t.
\tag{29}
\]
Equality throughout (29) holds if and only if \(v_{e_i}=tu_i\). Therefore \(J(v)\leq kt\), with equality only at \(v=v^*\). This proves uniqueness of the dual optimizer.

The affine primal map with \(s(0)=0\) has, for every edge,
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{B_d}(0,-2u_i+B_d)
 =\lVert2u_i\rVert_2-1=1.
\]
Its weighted defect is \(kt\), equal to the dual value in (28). Exact duality from accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d proves that this primal map is optimal. It misses every edge set. Since the dual optimizer is unique, it is automatically the unique load-minimal optimizer, proving (13). This also realizes directly the fixed missed-edge saturation conclusion of accepted fact f78198b3e936e0b3.

In fact, the linear functional
\[
 \Phi(v):=\sum_i\langle u_i,v_{e_i}\rangle
\]
satisfies
\[
 \Phi(v)\leq\sum_i\lVert v_{e_i}\rVert_2\leq kt
\]
throughout the full dual-feasible set, with equality only at \(v^*\). Thus \(v^*\) is exposed before restricting to the optimizer face.

Using the endpoint convention of accepted fact 9c47a4888e8a2947 and (9),
\[
 \omega_{e_i}=h_{B_d}(tu_i)=t,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{y_i^\pm}=h_{B_d}(tu_i/2)=t/2.
\]
This proves (14)-(15). By (27), the centers of distinct sets \(L_{e_i}\) have distance
\[
 2\lVert u_i-u_j\rVert_2
 =2\sqrt{\frac{2k}{k-1}}>2,
\]
so the unit balls \(L_{e_i}\) are pairwise disjoint. Since every midpoint is zero, an affine map can hit at most one of them. Taking its value at zero to be one center hits one edge, so the definition in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d gives (16).

It remains to prove the residual-rank and CCE claims. Let \(P_2:\mathbb R^d\to H_2\) be the second-block projection. For a nonempty \(A\subseteq Y\), form the matrix whose columns are the centroids \(c_y\), \(y\in A\). Its projected columns are \(\pm\eta_i\), one or two for each represented edge index. They span a space of dimension exactly \(q(A)\). Hence the centroid matrix itself has rank at least \(q(A)\).

For any affine \(s:\operatorname{aff}(A)\to\mathbb R^d\), extend it affinely to \(\mathbb R^2\). The matrix with columns \(s(y)\), \(y\in A\), has rank at most \(\rho_A+1\). The rank inequality
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M-N)
 \geq\operatorname{rank}(M)-\operatorname{rank}(N)
\]
applied to the centroid and affine-value matrices gives
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_y-s(y):y\in A\}
 \geq q(A)-\rho_A-1.
\]
Taking the minimum over \(s\), and using nonnegativity and \(\rho_A\leq2\), proves (18).

For the stronger full-support estimate, write an affine map as \(s(y)=b+Ly\) and let \(W_s\) be the span of all \(2k\) residuals. For every \(i\),
\[
 \frac12P_2\left(
  (c_i^+-s(p_i))-(c_i^--s(-p_i))
 \right)
 =\eta_i-P_2(Lp_i).
\tag{30}
\]
Let \(E:=P_2(L\mathbb R^2)\), so \(\dim E\leq2\). Modulo \(E\), the vectors on the right side of (30) have the same classes as \(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_k\), and these span \(H_2/E\). Therefore their span has dimension at least \(k-\dim E\geq k-2\). Since they lie in \(P_2(W_s)\), one has \(\dim W_s\geq k-2\). Minimizing over \(s\) proves (19).

For every nonempty \(A\), all represented edge loads equal \(t\), so
\[
 \sum_{y\in A}\omega_{e(y)}=|A|t
 \leq2q(A)t.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (18), (20), and \(2^{2-\rho_A}\leq4\) give
\[
 \mathcal S_{v^*}(A)
 \leq16\gamma_dt
 \frac{2q(A)}{\max\{1,q(A)-2\}}.
\tag{32}
\]
For every integer \(q\geq1\),
\[
 \frac{2q}{\max\{1,q-2\}}\leq6:
\]
the values for \(q=1,2,3\) are \(2,4,6\), while for \(q\geq4\) the ratio is \(2q/(q-2)\leq4\). Since \(\gamma_d<1\), equation (32) proves the strict bound (21).

All \(2k\) endpoints have the same parity, and the strictly convex base polygon is not contained in a line, so it contains a same-parity affine basis of three endpoints. Every positive endpoint load is \(t/2\). In base dimension two the only nontrivial partition of the two nonpivot basis elements has one element in each part. Thus every legal basis-frame score in the convention of accepted fact d0338841f89a9c80 is
\[
 \frac{(t/2+t/2)(t/2+t/2)}{t/2}=2t.
\]
This proves (22). Finally, \(T_*=kt\), and \(k\geq402\) gives (23). Combining (21) with (23) proves strict failure of (CCE) for every endpoint subset.

The proof used an arbitrary legal matching chosen as part of the construction. It did not derive that matching or its weights from canonical atlas occurrences, pooled weights, the endpoint core, or dyadic bins. It also bounded only the centroid scores (20), not the supremum over all legal selectors defining \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\). Therefore the asserted scope limitations follow, and the proof is complete.
