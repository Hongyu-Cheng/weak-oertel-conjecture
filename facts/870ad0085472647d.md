## Statement

Let $k\ge1$ be an integer and $p\in[0,1]$. Let $X\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)$ (shape $k$, scale one) and let $E,E_0$ be mean-one exponential random variables, with all three independent. Then
$$
G_{k,p}:=\Pr(X+pE\ge E_0+k+p-1)>\frac9{20}.
$$

## Proof

**Dependence on the fractional coefficient.** Let $W_k=X-E_0$, with density $f_k$ and survival function $S_k$, and write $m=k-1$. Conditioning on $E$ gives
$$
G_{k,p}=\mathbb E[S_k(m-p(E-1))].
$$
The densities $f_k$ are bounded and continuous. For $k\ge3$ the gamma density $g_k$ extended by zero has a continuous integrable derivative. With Fourier transform convention $\widehat g_k(t)=\int e^{itx}g_k(x)\,dx$, we have $\widehat g_k(t)=(1-it)^{-k}$ and $\widehat{g_k'}(t)=-it(1-it)^{-k}$. The latter is integrable, so Fourier inversion gives
$$
\lVert g_k'\rVert_\infty
\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|t|(1+t^2)^{-k/2}\,dt
=\frac1{\pi(k-2)}<\frac1{3(k-2)}.
$$
Convolving with the law of $-E_0$ preserves the Lipschitz bound. Direct convolution gives $f_1(w)=e^{-|w|}/2$ and
$$
f_2(w)=\begin{cases}e^w/4,&w<0,\\e^{-w}(1+2w)/4,&w\ge0.\end{cases}
$$
The first is $1/2$-Lipschitz. For the second, the derivative on the negative side has magnitude at most $1/4$. On the positive side, $|1-2w|e^{-w}$ is at most one: this holds on $[0,1/2]$ and the maximum on $[1/2,\infty)$ is $2e^{-3/2}<1$. Continuity at zero then gives the global bound. Thus we may use the Lipschitz constants
$$
C_1=\tfrac12,\qquad C_2=\tfrac14,\qquad C_k=\frac1{3(k-2)}\quad(k\ge3).
$$
The conditional survival formula is continuous on $[0,1]$ by dominated convergence, including both endpoints. Its derivative for $0<p<1$ is $\mathbb E[(E-1)f_k(m-p(E-1))]$; differentiation under the expectation uses the integrable bound $\lVert f_k\rVert_\infty |E-1|$. Since $\mathbb E(E-1)=0$ and $\mathbb E(E-1)^2=1$, subtracting $f_k(m)$ inside the expectation gives
$$
|\partial_p G_{k,p}|\le p C_k,\qquad
|G_{k,p}-G_{k,0}|\le \tfrac12 p^2 C_k,\qquad
|G_{k,p}-G_{k,q}|\le C_k|p-q|\quad(p,q\in[0,1]).
$$
The last two bounds follow by integration and continuity. In particular this argument only uses Lipschitz continuity of $f_1$, even at its nondifferentiable point.

**The integer endpoint and large shapes.** For $s\ge0$, let
$$
Q_k(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{s^j}{j!}\left(1-2^{-(k-j)}\right).
$$
In the convolution for $W_k$, integrate the gamma density at $w+y$ against $e^{-y}$ over $y\ge\max(0,-w)$. Substitution for $w<0$ and the binomial expansion for $w\ge0$ give
$$
f_k(w)=\begin{cases}
e^w/2^k,&w<0,\\
e^{-w}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{w^j}{j!\,2^{k-j}},&w\ge0.
\end{cases}
$$
Integrating the second expression from $s$ to infinity gives $S_k(s)=e^{-s}Q_k(s)$: the coefficient at $s^j/j!$ is the finite geometric sum $\sum_{l=j}^{k-1}2^{-(k-l)}=1-2^{-(k-j)}$. In particular, $G_{m+1,0}=e^{-m}Q_{m+1}(m)$.

For $m\ge1$, put $q_m=e^{-m}m^m/m!$. The sum $e^{-m}\sum_{j=0}^m m^j/j!$ is the survival probability of $\operatorname{Gamma}(m+1,1)$ at $m$. For its density $h$, at $0<u<m$,
$$
\log\frac{h(m+u)}{h(m-u)}
=m\log\frac{1+u/m}{1-u/m}-2u>0,
$$
since the derivative of $\log((1+x)/(1-x))$ exceeds $2$ for $0<x<1$. Pairing the densities on either side of $m$ and retaining the positive tail beyond $2m$ shows the survival exceeds $1/2$. The part subtracted from this sum in $G_{m+1,0}$ is
$$
q_m\sum_{l=0}^m 2^{-(l+1)}\frac{m!}{(m-l)!\,m^l}<q_m.
$$
Concavity of $\log$ on each interval centered at the integers $1,\ldots,m$ gives
$$
\log(m!)\ge\int_{1/2}^{m+1/2}\log x\,dx
\ge (m+\tfrac12)\log m-m+\tfrac12\log(2e).
$$
The second step uses $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)$ at $x=1/(2m)$, which follows by integrating $(1+t)^{-1}$ on $[0,x]$. Hence $q_m\le(2em)^{-1/2}<\sqrt{3/(16m)}$, using $e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3$. The bound on $|G_{k,p}-G_{k,0}|$ now gives, for $k\ge83$,
$$
G_{k,p}>\tfrac12-\sqrt{\frac3{16(k-1)}}-\frac1{6(k-2)}>\tfrac9{20}.
$$
Both errors decrease with $k$. At $k=83$, the strict inequality follows from $1/20-1/486=233/4860>0$ and $(233/4860)^2-3/(16\cdot82)=23023/1936807200>0$.

**Intermediate shapes.** For $12\le k\le82$, use $e<87/32$: the series through degree five is $163/60$, and the tail is at most $(1/720)\sum_{r\ge0}7^{-r}=7/4320$, so their sum is $87/32-1/2160$. With $m=k-1$ define
$$
Z_m=\sum_{j=0}^m(2^{m+1}-2^j)\frac{m!}{j!}m^j.
$$
The endpoint formula gives
$$
G_{m+1,0}>\frac{Z_m\,32^m}{2^{m+1}m!\,87^m}\ge\frac{46684}{100000}\qquad(11\le m\le81).
$$
The first comparison uses positivity of $Q_{m+1}(m)$ and the strict upper bound on $e$. For the second, the finite certificate is the integer comparison $100000 Z_m32^m\ge46684\cdot2^{m+1}m!87^m$ at each of the 71 indicated integers. It can be checked by the sum defining $Z_m$ and integer multiplication, as in the executable linked below. Consequently $G_{k,p}>46684/100000-1/60=9/20+13/75000$ for $12\le k\le82$.

**Small shapes: exact mesh and interpolation.** For $0<p<1$, set $s=k-1+p$ and $c=(1-p)/p$. Conditioning on $W_k$ gives
$$
G_{k,p}=S_k(s)+e^{-s/p}\int_{-\infty}^s e^{w/p}f_k(w)\,dw.
$$
The negative part of the integral is $p/[2^k(1+p)]$. For the positive part, integration by parts gives $\int_0^s w^j e^{cw}\,dw=e^{cs}F_j+D_j$, where
$$
F_0=1/c,\quad D_0=-1/c,\qquad
F_j=(s^j-jF_{j-1})/c,\quad D_j=-jD_{j-1}/c.
$$
Using $-s/p+cs=-s$ therefore gives $G_{k,p}=A e^{-s}+B e^{-s/p}$ with
$$
A=Q_k(s)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{F_j}{j!\,2^{k-j}},
\qquad B=\frac{p}{2^k(1+p)}+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{D_j}{j!\,2^{k-j}}.
$$
At $p=0$ use $G_{k,0}=e^{-(k-1)}Q_k(k-1)$. At $p=1$ the independent positive phases combine to shape $k+1$, giving $G_{k,1}=e^{-k}Q_{k+1}(k)$.

For rational $x\ge0$ write $x=h+r$ with $h$ a nonnegative integer and $0\le r<1$. Let $U(r)$ and $L(r)$ be the Taylor sums of $e^{-r}$ through degrees 32 and 33, respectively. On $[0,1]$, the alternating remainder gives $0<L(r)\le e^{-r}\le U(r)$. For positivity, pair the terms after degree three to obtain $L(r)\ge1-r+r^2/2-r^3/6\ge1/3$; the last polynomial decreases to $1/3$ on this interval. Thus
$$
L(1)^hL(r)\le e^{-x}\le U(1)^hU(r).
$$
Use these enclosures for each exponential in the mesh formulas, using the upper endpoint in the lower bound whenever its coefficient is negative. Let $\ell_{k,i}$ be the resulting rational lower bound at $p=i/32$. The finite certificate is $10^6\operatorname{num}(\ell_{k,i})\ge r_k\operatorname{den}(\ell_{k,i})$ for every $0\le i\le32$, with positive denominators and the following integers.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $r_k$ | 459849 | 456756 | 456756 | 457418 | 458844 | 460391 | 461880 | 463263 | 464533 | 465695 | 466758 |

The [exact certificate executable](../assets/pk-nov6/computations/verify_deterministic_fractional_gamma_uniform_bound_coherent.py) checks each of the 363 inequalities by rational arithmetic, as well as the 71 intermediate comparisons and all cutoff gaps. The mesh values exceed $459/1000$ for $k=1$ and $57/125$ for $2\le k\le11$. Every $p\in[0,1]$ is at distance at most $1/64$ from a mesh point. The Lipschitz estimate therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_{1,p}&>459/1000-1/128=9/20+19/16000,\\
G_{2,p}&>57/125-1/256=9/20+67/32000,\\
G_{k,p}&>57/125-1/192=9/20+19/24000\qquad (3\le k\le11).
\end{aligned}
$$
All ranges are strict, which completes the proof.
