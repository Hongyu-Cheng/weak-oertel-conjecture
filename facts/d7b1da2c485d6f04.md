## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the exact canonical objects and normalizations of accepted facts `0c5bcff6d676252e`, `23a7bea6c72cc440`, and `6b631757dd07968c`. Put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\rm sel}(C)+M_{\rm off}(C)+R_{\rm cloud}(C)+D,
\tag{1}
\]
and assume the canonical hard \(I\)-dominant inequalities
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\ge O.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(s=(s_e)\) be the retained product matching, let
\[
 J:=\{e\in\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B:s_e>0\},
\tag{3}
\]
and let the load-minimal optimizer have edge loads \(\omega_e\), source loads
\[
 \beta_z:=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e,
\tag{4}
\]
source points \(p_z^\flat\), and common origin-symmetric full-dimensional body \(D_*\). Write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
 \qquad
 d_z(s):=\sum_{e\ni z}s_e.
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
 \chi_e:=
 \begin{cases}
  \min\{\omega_e,s_e/16\},&e\in J,\\
  0,&e\notin J,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 X:=\sum_e\chi_e>\frac Q{160}.
\tag{6}
\]
For every source incident with a \(\chi\)-positive edge, the accepted source conclusions give
\[
 d_z^\chi:=\sum_{e\ni z}\chi_e
 \le\frac{d_z(s)}{16}
 \le\frac{\beta_z}{16}
 \le\frac{a_z}{64},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
 x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z,
 \qquad
 p_z^\flat+\frac12D_z\subseteq\mathcal K_z,
\tag{8}
\]
so in particular \(p_z^\flat\in\mathcal K_z\).

For every \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(\chi_e>0\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 \ell_e:=\frac{x_{e,u}+x_{e,v}}2,
 \qquad
 F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 y_e:=\frac{F_e^\flat+\ell_e}{2}

  \frac{p_u^\flat+x_{e,u}}2+

\tag{10}

These edges have same-parity endpoints, so \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\), and the accepted contact separation is
\[
 \|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_*}\ge\frac12.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(\mu_C\) denote the mixed-volume measure of \(C\).

### 1. Clipped simultaneous domination

Let \(K\) be a finite index set. For every \(k\in K\) and every \(\chi\)-positive edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), choose
\[
 q_{e,u}^{(k)}\in\mathcal K_u,
 \qquad
 q_{e,v}^{(k)}\in\mathcal K_v,
 \qquad
 Q_e^{(k)}:=\frac{q_{e,u}^{(k)}+q_{e,v}^{(k)}}2.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(c_k\ge0\) and
\[
 \sum_{k\in K}c_k\le32\gamma_d,
\tag{13}
\]
then the positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma:=
 \sum_{k\in K}c_k
 \sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,Q_e^{(k)})}}
\tag{14}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\sigma(H)\le\mu_C(H)}
\tag{15}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\).

In particular, each of the following five measures is carried by the mixed-integer body and halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\):
\[
 32\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)},
\tag{16}
\]
\[
 32\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)},
\tag{17}
\]
\[
 16\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e
 \left(
  \delta_{(m_e,\ell_e)}+
  \delta_{(m_e,F_e^\flat)}
 \right),
\tag{18}
\]
\[
 32\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,y_e)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Moreover, for every affine map \(a:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\), where
\[
 A_\chi:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:\chi_e>0\},
 \qquad
 \rho_\chi:=\dim A_\chi,
\tag{20}
\]
choose, for every \(\chi\)-positive edge, one point
\[
 q_e^a\in\{\ell_e,F_e^\flat\}
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 \|q_e^a-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}\ge\frac14.
\tag{21}
\]
Such a choice always exists by (11), and
\[
 32\gamma_d\sum_e\chi_e\delta_{(m_e,q_e^a)}
\tag{22}
\]
is carried and halfspace-dominated.

### 1.1 Corrected strengthened single-use screen

\[
 r(q):=
 \min_{b:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q_e-b(m_e):\chi_e>0\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Set
\[
 r_\ell:=r((\ell_e)_e),
 \qquad
 r_F:=r((F_e^\flat)_e),
 \qquad
 r_y:=r((y_e)_e),
 \qquad
 r_a^\dagger:=r((q_e^a)_e),
\tag{24}
\]
and define the residual rank of the two-atom support in (18) by

 r_*:=
 \min_{b:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}

All five measures (16)-(19) and (22) have intrinsic base rank \(\rho_\chi\). Put

 \boxed{

Retain the loaded-source score \(x\) and the centroid score \(y_{\bar c}\) of accepted fact `6b631757dd07968c`. Explicitly, with

### 1.2 Individual edge cap and common affine-defect anchor

 \boxed{
 D\ge2^n32\gamma_d\chi_e,}
\tag{39}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \chi_e<
 \frac{Q}{3200\gamma_d2^n}

\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 |\{e:\chi_e>0\}|>
 20\gamma_d2^n>
 \frac{20}{3}2^n.}
\tag{41}

Let \(\rho:=\rho_\chi\). Choose \(\rho+1\) edges \(e_0,\ldots,e_\rho\) whose midpoint bases are affinely independent and span \(A_\chi\), and let \(a_0:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\) be the unique affine map satisfying

 a_0(m_{e_i})=F_{e_i}^\flat

 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\chi_{e_i}

Thus the nonanchor edges have total \(\chi\)-weight greater than \(71X/80\).

 q_e\in\{\ell_e,F_e^\flat\}

## Proof

For (14)-(15), fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\). Whenever \((m_e,Q_e^{(k)})\in H\), the point \((m_e,Q_e^{(k)})\) is the midpoint of
\[
 (u,q_{e,u}^{(k)})
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (v,q_{e,v}^{(k)}).
\]
Linearity of a defining affine functional for \(H\) implies that at least one of these two endpoint points belongs to \(H\). Orient that occurrence toward one such endpoint. At a fixed source \(z\), the total oriented weight is at most
\[
 \left(\sum_{k\in K}c_k\right)d_z^\chi
 \le32\gamma_d\frac{a_z}{64}
 =\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2}.
\tag{47}
\]
If this weight is positive, the fiber halfspace
\[
 H_z:=\{q\in\mathbb R^d:(z,q)\in H\}
\]
contains one of the oriented points in \(\mathcal K_z=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_da_z}{2},
\tag{48}
\]
which covers the total oriented weight at that source. Summing (47)-(48) over the distinct source fibers proves (15). Each endpoint point belongs to its corresponding fiber of \(C\), and convexity gives \(Q_e^{(k)}\in C_{m_e}\). Since \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\), (14) is carried by the mixed-integer body.

For (16), use one index \(k\), coefficient \(32\gamma_d\), and endpoint choices \(x_{e,u},x_{e,v}\). For (17), use endpoint choices \(p_u^\flat,p_v^\flat\). For (18), use both choices with coefficient \(16\gamma_d\) each. For (19), convexity of \(\mathcal K_z\) gives
\[
 \frac{p_z^\flat+x_{e,z}}2\in\mathcal K_z,
\]
and these two endpoint points have midpoint \(y_e\). For (22), (11) and the triangle inequality imply that at least one of \(\ell_e,F_e^\flat\) has distance at least \(1/4\) from \(a(m_e)\). Use one index \(k\), coefficient \(32\gamma_d\), and choose the endpoint pair \((x_{e,u},x_{e,v})\) or \((p_u^\flat,p_v^\flat)\) according to the selected target point \(q_e^a\). This proves all five specializations.

Each measure in (16)-(19) and (22) has total mass \(32\gamma_dX\), and its base projection is exactly \(\{m_e:\chi_e>0\}\). Applying the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` separately to each measure gives (28). This is a maximum of valid lower bounds for the one capacity \(D\), not a sum of those bounds.

Accepted fact `6b631757dd07968c` gives the distinct-capacity lower bounds
\[
 R_{\rm sel}(C)\ge4x(Q-R),
 \qquad
 M_{\rm off}(C)\ge Xy_{\bar c},
\tag{49}
\]
and accepted fact `23a7bea6c72cc440` gives the correlated overlap estimate
\[
 \frac XQ\ge
 \frac{11}{512}
 -\frac{195}{128}\frac RQ
 -\frac{307}{4096}\frac GQ.
\tag{50}
\]
Put
\[
 r_0:=\frac RQ,
 \qquad
 g_0:=\frac GQ,
 \qquad
 q:=\frac XQ.
\tag{51}
\]
Using (28), (49), the distinct capacities \(R,G,R_{\rm sel}(C),M_{\rm off}(C),D\), and discarding only the nonnegative \(R_{\rm cloud}(C)\), the hard inequality gives
\[
 r_0+g_0+4(1-r_0)x+q\widehat Z_a<\frac1{100}.
\tag{52}
\]
Substitute (50) into (52). With
\[
 A_R:=1-4x-\frac{195}{128}\widehat Z_a,
 \qquad
 A_G:=1-\frac{307}{4096}\widehat Z_a,
\tag{53}
\]
one obtains
\[
 4x+\frac{11}{512}\widehat Z_a
 +r_0A_R+g_0A_G<\frac1{100},
\tag{54}
\]
while
\[
 A_G-A_R
 =4x+
 \left(\frac{195}{128}-\frac{307}{4096}\right)
 \widehat Z_a>0.
\tag{55}
\]
If \(A_R<0\), then \(r_0+g_0<1/100\) and (55) imply
\[
 r_0A_R+g_0A_G
 \ge(r_0+g_0)A_R
 >\frac1{100}A_R.
\]
The left side of (54) would then be strictly larger than
\[
 4x+\frac{11}{512}\widehat Z_a+\frac1{100}A_R
 =\frac1{100}+\frac{99}{25}x+\frac1{160}\widehat Z_a
 >\frac1{100},
\]
a contradiction. Hence \(A_R\ge0\), and then (55) gives \(A_G>0\). Dropping the last two nonnegative terms in (54) yields
\[
 4x+\frac{11}{512}\widehat Z_a<\frac1{100}.
\]
Multiplication by \(512/11\) proves (34).

Since \(x>0\), (34) gives \(\widehat Z_a<128/275\). Each of the five terms inside the maximum in (33) therefore satisfies
\[
 32\gamma_d\frac{2^{n-\rho_\chi}}{r+1}<\frac{128}{275},
\]
which rearranges to (35). By contrast, the centroid term has coefficient one, so it gives exactly
\[
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_\chi}}{r_{\bar c}+1}<\frac{128}{275},
\]
which rearranges to (37), not (35). For every integer \(r\ge0\), one has \(r+1\le2^r\). Since \(\gamma_d>1/3\),
\[
 \frac{275\gamma_d}{4}>16,
 \qquad
 \frac{275}{128}>2.
\]
Thus (35) implies
\[
 2^{\rho_\chi+r}>2^{n+4},
\]
and (37) implies
\[
 2^{\rho_\chi+r_{\bar c}}>2^{n+1}.
\]
Integrality of the affine dimensions proves (36) and (38). This also isolates the exact reason the stronger centroid claim is unavailable.

Fix a \(\chi\)-positive edge \(e\). A one-atom submeasure of (16) is halfspace-dominated and has intrinsic ranks \(0,0\). The dominated-measure residual theorem gives (39). Since \(D<Q/100\) by (1)-(2), the first inequality in (40) follows. The strict bound \(X>Q/160\) gives the second. If \(N:=|\{e:\chi_e>0\}|\), summing the second strict bound in (40) over all \(N\) edges gives
\[
 X<N\frac{X}{20\gamma_d2^n},
\]
and hence the first inequality in (41); the second follows from \(\gamma_d>1/3\).

For the anchor step, \(\rho\le n\), and each selected anchor edge satisfies the second inequality in (40). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\chi_{e_i}
 <\frac{\rho+1}{20\gamma_d2^n}X
 <\frac{3(n+1)}{20\,2^n}X.
\]
For \(n\ge2\), the last coefficient is at most \(9/80\), with equality only in this final comparison when \(n=2\), proving (43). The complement consequently has weight greater than \(71X/80\).

Finally, for every nonanchor edge, (11) and the triangle inequality give
\[
 \frac12
 \le\|\ell_e-F_e^\flat\|_{D_*}
 \le\|\ell_e-a_0(m_e)\|_{D_*}
   +\|F_e^\flat-a_0(m_e)\|_{D_*},
\]
which proves that a choice (44) exists. The affine coordinates satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\alpha_i(m)=1,
 \qquad
 m=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\alpha_i(m)m_{e_i},
\]
and (42) gives
\[
 a_0(m)=\sum_{i=0}^{\rho}\alpha_i(m)F_{e_i}^\flat.
\]
Substitution proves (45)-(46). The coefficients \(\alpha_i(m_e)\) need not be nonnegative, and the anchor points need not share a physical source; no stronger packet or chart conclusion is asserted.
