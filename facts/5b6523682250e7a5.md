## Statement

For a finite nonempty set \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\), a weight function \(\omega:D\to[0,\infty)\), and \(c\in\mathbb R^r\), define the exact weighted halfspace depth
\[
 \kappa(D,\omega;c)
 :=
 \inf\left\{
 \sum_{u\in D\cap K}\omega(u):
 K\subseteq\mathbb R^r\text{ is a closed halfspace and }c\in K
 \right\},
\tag{1}
\]
where the whole space is allowed as a closed halfspace.

The following product-depth counting lemma holds. Let \(k\geq1\). For \(1\leq i\leq k\), let \(r_i\geq1\), let \(D_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{r_i}\) be finite and nonempty, let \(\omega_i:D_i\to[0,\infty)\), choose \(c_i\in\mathbb R^{r_i}\), and put
\[
 \kappa_i:=\kappa(D_i,\omega_i;c_i).
\]
If \(F:\prod_{i=1}^k\mathbb R^{r_i}\to\mathbb R\) is affine in each block separately and
\[
 F(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\geq0,
\]
then
\[
 \sum_{\substack{(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i\\
 F(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\geq0}}
 \prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i)
 \geq
 \prod_{i=1}^k\kappa_i.
\tag{2}
\]

Here is the factorized lattice-polysimplex consequence. Let \(n,d,k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and define
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For a Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), let
\[
 \mu_C(A):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \left|\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\right|_d,
\]
where \(|\cdot|_d\) is \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure. For \(p\in S\), set
\[
 h_S(p):=
 \inf\{\mu_C(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\{|C_z\cap K|_d:K\subseteq\mathbb R^d
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in K\}.
\tag{4}
\]

For each \(i\), let \(r_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
 \Delta_i=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{i,0},\ldots,v_{i,r_i}\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^{r_i}
\]
be a full-dimensional lattice simplex, and put
\[
 D_i:=\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i},
 \qquad
 q:=\sum_{i=1}^k r_i.
\]
Let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(U_i\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r_i}\), and assume that the concatenated matrix
\[
 U=[U_1\ \cdots\ U_k]
\]
has rank \(q\). Thus \(q\leq n\), and the affine map
\[
 \Phi(t_1,\ldots,t_k):=
 m+\sum_{i=1}^kU_it_i
\tag{5}
\]
is injective.

For every product vertex \(j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\), with \(0\leq j_i\leq r_i\), suppose there is a point
\[
 x_j\in C_{\Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k})}.
\]
Suppose also that numbers \(a_{i,j_i}\geq0\) are given such that
\[
 \delta_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j
 \right)^{1/d}
 \geq
 \prod_{i=1}^k a_{i,j_i}
\tag{6}
\]
for every product vertex \(j\). Let \(\lambda_{i,j}\) denote the barycentric coordinate of \(v_{i,j}\) on \(\Delta_i\), and define
\[
 g_i(t_i):=
 \sum_{j=0}^{r_i}\lambda_{i,j}(t_i)a_{i,j},
 \qquad
 A_i:=\sum_{u\in D_i}g_i(u)^d.
\tag{7}
\]
Then there exists \(p\in S\) such that
\[
 h_S(p)
 \geq
 2^{-q}\prod_{i=1}^k A_i
 =
 2^{-q}\prod_{i=1}^k
 \left(\sum_{u\in D_i}g_i(u)^d\right).
\tag{8}
\]

In particular, if there is a common \(s\geq0\) such that every product vertex has a point \(x_j\) satisfying
\[
 \delta_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j
 \right)\geq s,
\tag{9}
\]
then some \(p\in S\) satisfies the uniform product-simplex bound
\[
 h_S(p)
 \geq
 2^{-q}s\prod_{i=1}^k|D_i|.
\tag{10}
\]

There is also the following exact asymmetric-box corollary. Let \(q\leq n\), let \(u_1,\ldots,u_q\in\mathbb Z^n\) be linearly independent, let \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(L_1,\ldots,L_q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Put
\[
 B:=\left\{m+\sum_{i=1}^q k_i u_i:
 0\leq k_i\leq L_i,\ k_i\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
If, for every \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^q\), the corner fiber over
\[
 m+\sum_{i=1}^q\varepsilon_iL_i u_i
\]
contains a point of absolute fiber depth at least \(s\geq0\), then some \(p\in S\) satisfies
\[
 h_S(p)
 \geq
 s\prod_{i=1}^q
 \left(\left\lfloor\frac{L_i}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)
 \geq
 2^{-q}s|B|.
\tag{12}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the product-depth counting lemma. The case \(k=1\) follows directly from (1). Indeed, the set
\[
 \{u_1\in\mathbb R^{r_1}:F(u_1)\geq0\}
\]
is either the whole space or a closed halfspace containing \(c_1\), because \(F(c_1)\geq0\). Its \(\omega_1\)-mass is therefore at least \(\kappa_1\).

Suppose the result holds for \(k-1\) blocks, and define
\[
 G(u_2,\ldots,u_k):=F(c_1,u_2,\ldots,u_k).
\]
This function is affine in each of its blocks separately and satisfies
\[
 G(c_2,\ldots,c_k)=F(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\geq0.
\]
The induction hypothesis gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{(u_2,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_{i=2}^kD_i\\
 G(u_2,\ldots,u_k)\geq0}}
 \prod_{i=2}^k\omega_i(u_i)
 \geq
 \prod_{i=2}^k\kappa_i.
\tag{13}
\]
Fix a tuple \(v=(u_2,\ldots,u_k)\) counted on the left of (13). The function
\[
 u_1\longmapsto F(u_1,v)
\]
is affine and is nonnegative at \(c_1\), since its value there is \(G(v)\geq0\). Its nonnegative superlevel set is therefore either the whole space or a closed halfspace containing \(c_1\). Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{u_1\in D_1\\F(u_1,v)\geq0}}
 \omega_1(u_1)\geq\kappa_1.
\tag{14}
\]
All weights are nonnegative, so restricting the outer sum to the tuples in (13), and then using (14), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{\substack{(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i\\
 F(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\geq0}}
 \prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i)\\
 &\qquad\geq
 \kappa_1
 \sum_{\substack{(u_2,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_{i=2}^kD_i\\
 G(u_2,\ldots,u_k)\geq0}}
 \prod_{i=2}^k\omega_i(u_i)
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^k\kappa_i.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (2), including zero weights and zero values of some \(\kappa_i\).

We next construct the continuous selector used in the mixed theorem. For \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\in\prod_i\Delta_i\) and a product vertex \(j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\), put
\[
 \Lambda_j(t):=\prod_{i=1}^k\lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i),
 \qquad
 x(t):=\sum_j\Lambda_j(t)x_j.
\tag{15}
\]
The barycentric coordinates are affine and continuous. Thus \(x(t)\) is continuous and affine in each block separately. On the product of the simplices, all \(\Lambda_j(t)\) are nonnegative and sum to one. Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_j\Lambda_j(t)
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k})
 &=m+\sum_{i=1}^kU_i
 \sum_{j_i=0}^{r_i}\lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i)v_{i,j_i}\\
 &=m+\sum_{i=1}^kU_it_i
 =\Phi(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
It follows from convexity of \(C\) that
\[
 (\Phi(t),x(t))
 =\sum_j\Lambda_j(t)
 \bigl(\Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j\bigr)
 \in C.
\tag{17}
\]

Accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 proves that
\[
 (z,x)\longmapsto\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}
\]
is jointly concave on \(C\), including at points where the depth is zero. Applying that concavity to (17), then using (6) and the product form of the weights, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_C(\Phi(t),x(t))^{1/d}
 &\geq
 \sum_j\Lambda_j(t)
 \delta_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j
 \right)^{1/d}\\
 &\geq
 \sum_j\prod_{i=1}^k
 \lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i)a_{i,j_i}\\
 &=\prod_{i=1}^k
 \left(\sum_{j_i=0}^{r_i}
 \lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i)a_{i,j_i}\right)
 =\prod_{i=1}^kg_i(t_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Every \(g_i\) is nonnegative on \(\Delta_i\). Raising (18) to the \(d\)-th power therefore gives
\[
 \delta_C(\Phi(t),x(t))
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^kg_i(t_i)^d.
\tag{19}
\]

Set
\[
 \omega_i(u):=g_i(u)^d
 \qquad(u\in D_i),
\tag{20}
\]
so that \(A_i=\sum_{u\in D_i}\omega_i(u)\). We first handle zero factor energy. If \(A_i=0\) for at least one \(i\), then the right side of (8) is zero. Choose any product vertex \(j\). Its base point is integral, and the given point
\[
 p=\bigl(\Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j\bigr)
\]
lies in \(S\). Since \(h_S(p)\geq0\), (8) follows. No centerpoint theorem is applied to a zero measure.

Assume henceforth that every \(A_i>0\). Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to the nonzero finite atomic measure
\[
 \nu_i:=\sum_{u\in D_i}\omega_i(u)\,\delta_u
\]
on \(\mathbb R^{r_i}\), supported on \(\mathbb Z^{r_i}\). That fact, with continuous dimension zero, gives \(c_i\in\mathbb Z^{r_i}\) such that every closed halfspace containing \(c_i\) has \(\nu_i\)-mass at least
\[
 2^{-r_i}A_i.
\tag{21}
\]
The point \(c_i\) belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\nu_i)\). Otherwise, strict separation from this nonempty compact convex hull would give a closed halfspace containing \(c_i\) and disjoint from the support, contradicting the positive lower bound (21). Hence
\[
 c_i\in\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\nu_i)
 \subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D_i)=\Delta_i.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves both properties needed below: \(c_i\) is integral, and the selector \(x(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\) is defined at it.

Let
\[
 \kappa_i:=\kappa(D_i,\omega_i;c_i).
\]
By (21),
\[
 \kappa_i\geq2^{-r_i}A_i.
\tag{23}
\]
Set \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\) and
\[
 p:=(\Phi(c),x(c)).
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (17) and (22) give \(p\in C\). Since \(m\), the matrices \(U_i\), and the points \(c_i\) are integral, \(\Phi(c)\in\mathbb Z^n\). Thus \(p\in S\).

We now bound every closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\). First consider the whole space \(H=\mathbb R^{n+d}\). The rank assumption on \(U\) implies
\[
 \Phi(u_1,\ldots,u_k)=\Phi(u'_1,\ldots,u'_k)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 (u_1,\ldots,u_k)=(u'_1,\ldots,u'_k).
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the tuples in \(\prod_iD_i\) index distinct integral base fibers. By (19), each such full fiber has volume at least \(\prod_i\omega_i(u_i)\). Consequently,
\[
 \mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})
 \geq\sum_{(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i}
 \prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i)
 =\prod_{i=1}^kA_i
 \geq2^{-q}\prod_{i=1}^kA_i.
\tag{26}
\]

Let \(H\) now be a proper closed halfspace containing \(p\). Write
\[
 H=\{(z,y):a\mathbin{\cdot}z+b\mathbin{\cdot}y\geq\beta\},
 \qquad (a,b)\ne(0,0),
\tag{27}
\]
and define
\[
 F(t_1,\ldots,t_k):=
 a\mathbin{\cdot}\Phi(t_1,\ldots,t_k)
 +b\mathbin{\cdot}x(t_1,\ldots,t_k)-\beta.
\tag{28}
\]
The affine barycentric coordinates extend to all of \(\mathbb R^{r_i}\), so (15) and (28) define a function on \(\prod_i\mathbb R^{r_i}\) which is affine in each block separately. Since \(p\in H\),
\[
 F(c_1,\ldots,c_k)\geq0.
\tag{29}
\]
Apply (2) to \(F\), the weights (20), and the exact depths \(\kappa_i\). It gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i\\F(u)\geq0}}
 \prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i)
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^k\kappa_i.
\tag{30}
\]

For every tuple \(u\) counted in (30), the section of \(H\) in the fiber over \(\Phi(u)\) contains \(x(u)\). If \(b\ne0\), that section is the proper closed halfspace
\[
 \{y\in\mathbb R^d:b\mathbin{\cdot}y
 \geq\beta-a\mathbin{\cdot}\Phi(u)\},
\]
so (4) and (19) show that the fiber contributes at least
\[
 \delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^k\omega_i(u_i)
\tag{31}
\]
to \(\mu_C(H)\). If \(b=0\), the halfspace is vertical. The condition \(F(u)\geq0\) means that its section over \(\Phi(u)\) is the whole of \(\mathbb R^d\), including when equality holds in (27). The full fiber volume is again at least the right side of (31). By injectivity (25), these contributions come from distinct fibers and are not counted repeatedly. Hence (30), (31), and (23) give
\[
 \mu_C(H)
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^k\kappa_i
 \geq2^{-\sum_i r_i}\prod_{i=1}^kA_i
 =2^{-q}\prod_{i=1}^kA_i.
\tag{32}
\]
Together with (26), this treats every closed halfspace containing \(p\). Taking the infimum proves (8). Notice that (29), rather than boundary equality, is exactly what is needed in the counting lemma.

For the uniform product-simplex corollary, assume (9). In (6), take
\[
 a_{1,j}:=s^{1/d}\quad(0\leq j\leq r_1),
 \qquad
 a_{i,j}:=1\quad(2\leq i\leq k).
\]
Then \(g_1\equiv s^{1/d}\), every other \(g_i\equiv1\), and therefore
\[
 A_1=s|D_1|,
 \qquad
 A_i=|D_i|\quad(i\geq2).
\]
Substitution into (8) gives (10), including \(s=0\) through the zero-energy case.

It remains to derive the exact constants in the asymmetric-box corollary. Regard each interval
\[
 \Delta_i=[0,L_i],
 \qquad
 D_i=\{0,1,\ldots,L_i\},
\]
as a one-dimensional lattice simplex, and take \(U_it_i=t_iu_i\). The corner construction (15), applied to the given corner points, and accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 show directly that
\[
 \delta_C\!\left(
 m+\sum_{i=1}^qt_iu_i,x(t)
 \right)\geq s
 \qquad(0\leq t_i\leq L_i).
\tag{33}
\]
For each \(i\), choose the integer median
\[
 c_i:=\left\lfloor\frac{L_i}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
With unit weights on \(D_i\), its exact one-dimensional halfspace depth is
\[
 \kappa_i
 =\min\{c_i+1,L_i-c_i+1\}
 =\left\lfloor\frac{L_i}{2}\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{34}
\]
Indeed, the two rays with endpoint \(c_i\) give the two displayed counts, and every closed ray containing \(c_i\) contains one of those endpoint rays.

Take
\[
 p=\left(m+\sum_{i=1}^q c_i u_i,x(c_1,\ldots,c_q)\right)\in S.
\]
For any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\), construct \(F\) as in (28). The product-depth lemma with the unit weights and (34) supplies at least \(\prod_i\kappa_i\) grid tuples with \(F\geq0\). Linear independence of the \(u_i\) makes their base fibers distinct, and (33) makes each such fiber contribute at least \(s\). A vertical halfspace is handled by its whole selected fiber exactly as above, while the whole ambient space contains every grid fiber. Therefore
\[
 h_S(p)\geq s\prod_{i=1}^q\kappa_i
 =s\prod_{i=1}^q
 \left(\left\lfloor\frac{L_i}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right).
\tag{35}
\]
Finally, injectivity gives \(|B|=\prod_i(L_i+1)\), and
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{L_i}{2}\right\rfloor+1
 \geq\frac{L_i+1}{2}
\]
for every \(i\). Multiplying these inequalities proves the second bound in (12). The proof supplies no no-affine calibration claim; that separate statement is not used here.
