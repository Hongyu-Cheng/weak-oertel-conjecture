## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge2$, be compact convex bodies of positive volume at bases $-1,0,1$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Fix $y\in K$ and let finitely many closed caps $H_i=\{u_i\cdot(x-y)+\beta_i z\ge0\}$, $u_i\ne0$, attain actual raw halfspace depth $Q$ at $(0,y)$. Suppose their middle traces cover $K$, and for each cap the $d$-th root of its captured middle volume equals the mean of the two endpoint capture roots. Then each cap captures the same volume $q=Q/3>0$ in each of the three fibers. If the caps cover all three fibers, the normalized depth at $(0,y)$ is at least $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$.

If positive weights $\lambda_i$ satisfy $\sum_i\lambda_i u_i=0$, put $\Delta=\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i$. The caps cover every entire continuous plane over $z$ with $z\Delta\ge0$, including both endpoints when $\Delta=0$. Any actual fiber $C_z$ with volume above $q/\Gamma_d$ lies on a negative-drift side. At its volume centroid $g_z$ each functional gap $-m_i(z)$ is strictly positive, where $m_i(z)=u_i\cdot(g_z-y)+\beta_i z$, and
$$
\sum_i\lambda_i[-m_i(z)]=-z\Delta>0 .
$$
Equivalently this is the weighted sum of the ordinary distances to the cutting planes in that fiber with weights $\lambda_i\lVert u_i\rVert$. No rank or minimality is required.

## Proof

One middle capture has positive volume because finitely many cover $K$. Thus $Q>0$. Apply 03c59c70402b66a2 to each actual depth-attaining cap, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and the exact captured-side equality in $d>1$. It gives equal volumes $q=Q/3$ on all three fibers for every cap, including any initial saturation cases.

On a covered actual fiber at least one closed trace contains its ordinary volume centroid. The full-volume centroid case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives that trace volume at least $\Gamma_d|C_z|$. Since the trace mass is $q$, the whole fiber has mass at most $q/\Gamma_d$. When all three actual fibers are covered, summation gives $|A|+|K|+|B|\le3q/\Gamma_d$, so the depth at the given mark divided by the full mass is at least $\Gamma_d$.

Positive continuous balance makes the weighted sum of margins at any $(z,x)$ equal $z\Delta$. If this is nonnegative, at least one closed cap contains $(z,x)$. If a fiber is above the centroid volume bound, every cap misses its centroid, since all captures equal $q$. Each margin there is strictly negative, and their weighted sum is again $z\Delta$. This proves the gap identity and the negative-side conclusion. Dividing each functional gap by its continuous normal norm gives the signed Euclidean distance, now positive, within the fiber.
