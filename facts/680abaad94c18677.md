## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact, $y_0$ interior, and let $d+1$ branches $F_j(y,r)$ be continuous on $K$ for parameters near $r_0\in\mathbb R^m$, jointly continuous in $(y,r)$ and $C^1$ near $(y_0,r_0)$. Suppose their values at $(y_0,r_0)$ agree, $y_0$ uniquely maximizes their minimum on $K$ at $r_0$, and their gradients in $y$ are affinely independent with zero in the interior of their convex hull. The equalities $F_j(y,r)=U$ have a unique local $C^1$ solution $(y(r),U(r))$ through the old tie. For all nearby $r$, $U(r)=\max_{y\in K}\min_jF_j(y,r)$. Thus the profile is an upper bound for raw fiber depth whenever the branches are genuine containing cap tests covering the chart.

## Proof

The Jacobian in $(y,U)$ has rows $(\nabla_yF_j,-1)$, invertible by affine independence. The implicit function theorem yields the local solution. The gradients along it remain a full positive circuit. There is a uniform $c>0$ such that for every displacement $v$ some branch has gradient pairing at most $-c\|v\|$. Continuity of all gradients makes its Taylor remainder in a uniform neighborhood at most $(c/2)\|v\|$, uniformly for nearby $r$, so the branch minimum drops strictly away from $y(r)$ there. Outside a smaller fixed old neighborhood the unique maximum at $r_0$ gives a strict compact gap, which persists by joint continuity and continuity of $U(r)$. Shrink the parameter neighborhood so the local and exterior regions cover $K$. This proves the exact envelope identity. If the $F_j$ are raw masses of containing caps, actual raw depth is pointwise no larger than their minimum.
