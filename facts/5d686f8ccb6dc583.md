## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $p>0$, and let $g$ be a bounded nonnegative measurable density of compact support in $\mathbb R^d$ with positive integral. Suppose $h=g^{-1/p}$, with value $+\infty$ where $g=0$, is convex. Then the symmetric decreasing rearrangement $g^*$ admits an equimeasurable representative with convex extended inverse $p$th root that is radial and nondecreasing in radius. In particular $\int g^*=\int g$. If $g\le f$ almost everywhere for a radially nonincreasing nonnegative $f$, then $g^*\le f$ almost everywhere.

## Proof

Let $A_t=\{h<t\}$ and $t_0=\inf\{t:|A_t|>0\}$. The positive-integral and boundedness hypotheses give $0<t_0<\infty$. For $t>t_0$ let $R(t)=(|A_t|/\omega_d)^{1/d}$, where $\omega_d$ is the unit-ball volume. It is finite, positive and nondecreasing. By convexity, $(1-\theta)A_s+\theta A_t\subseteq A_{(1-\theta)s+\theta t}$. Brunn–Minkowski implies that $R$ is concave on $(t_0,\infty)$, hence continuous there. The set of pairs $(x,t)$ with $t>t_0$ and $\|x\|<R(t)$ is open and convex by continuity, the triangle inequality and concavity. On each vertical line it is either an open upper ray or empty, by monotonicity of $R$. Thus it is the strict epigraph of an extended convex function $h^*$, obtained by taking the infimum of the heights on each such line and assigning $+\infty$ to empty lines. The function is radial and nondecreasing in radius and satisfies $h^*\ge t_0>0$. This construction covers flat initial levels and both finite and infinite boundary values.

Every strict sublevel of $h^*$ has the same volume as that of $h$, by the defining radii for $t>t_0$ and zero volume for the other levels. Therefore $g^*=(h^*)^{-p}$, with value zero where $h^*=+\infty$, has the same strict superlevel volumes as $g$ and the same integral by layer-cake. If $g\le f$ almost everywhere, then each superlevel volume of $g$ is bounded by the corresponding volume of $f$. The superlevel sets of both $g^*$ and $f$ are centered balls modulo null sets, so the superlevel of $g^*$ lies in that of $f$ modulo null sets. Apply this inclusion at positive rational levels to obtain $g^*\le f$ almost everywhere.
