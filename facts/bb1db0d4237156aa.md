## Statement

For every \(\eta,c>0\), there exist integers \(n,d\ge1\), a compact full-dimensional convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) of positive mixed mass \(W\), and a positive-mass lattice point \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) whose normalized fiber-mass marginal \(\pi\) has halfspace depth
\[
\tau_\pi(z_0)=:\tau\ge 2^{-n},
\]
such that
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\pi_{z_0+kv}<\eta\tau
\quad\text{for every primitive }v\in\mathbb Z^n,
\]
but every \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) has normalized mixed halfspace depth strictly less than \(c\tau\). Consequently, the universal constants requested by `marginal-centerpoint-line-spread-lifting` do not exist.

## Proof

Fix an integer \(m\ge2\), and put \(r=m+1\). In the \(m\)-dimensional subspace
\[
E=\left\{u\in\mathbb R^{m+1}:\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}u_j=0\right\},
\]
identified isometrically with \(\mathbb R^m\), define
\[
b_i=\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{m}}\left(e_i-\frac{\mathbf1}{m+1}\right),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,r.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r b_i=0,\qquad \lVert b_i\rVert_2=1,\qquad
b_i^{\mathsf T}b_j=-\frac1m\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Thus \(\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{b_1,\ldots,b_r\}\) is an \(m\)-dimensional regular simplex containing \(0\). Let \(B\) be the unit Euclidean ball in \(\mathbb R^m\), let \(v_m=\operatorname{vol}_m(B)\), set \(R=m+1\), and define
\[
A_m=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_m(R\Delta+B)}{v_m}.
\]
Since \(0\in\Delta\) and \(\Delta\subseteq B\),
\[
B\subseteq R\Delta+B\subseteq (R+1)B,
\qquad
1\le A_m\le(m+2)^m.
\tag{1}
\]
Write \(M=(m+2)^m\), and set
\[
L=m(M+2),\qquad n=rL=m(m+1)(M+2),\qquad d=m.
\]
Index the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^n\) by \(e_{i,\ell}\), where \(1\le i\le r\) and \(1\le\ell\le L\). Define
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\left\{(s e_{i,\ell},Rb_i):
1\le i\le r,\ 1\le\ell\le L,\ s\in\{-1,1\}\right\}
\]
and
\[
C=P+(\{0\}\times B)\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^m.
\tag{2}
\]
The differences
\[
(e_{i,\ell},Rb_i)-(-e_{i,\ell},Rb_i)=(2e_{i,\ell},0)
\]
span \(\mathbb R^n\times\{0\}\). Moreover, the midpoints \((0,Rb_i)\) belong to \(P\), and their differences span \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^m\). Hence \(P\), and therefore \(C\), is full-dimensional. It is compact and convex by construction.

The base projection of \(C\) is
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_{i,\ell}\}
=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\lVert z\rVert_1\le1\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{0\}\cup\{\pm e_{i,\ell}:1\le i\le r,\ 1\le\ell\le L\}.
\tag{3}
\]
We next compute the fibers. If a convex combination of the generating points of \(P\) has base coordinate \(0\), then the coefficients of \(e_{i,\ell}\) and \(-e_{i,\ell}\) agree separately for every \((i,\ell)\). Its continuous coordinate is therefore an arbitrary convex combination of \(Rb_1,\ldots,Rb_r\), and every such combination is obtained in this way. Thus \(P_0=R\Delta\). Each \(\pm e_{i,\ell}\) is an extreme point of \(Q\), so the corresponding fiber of \(P\) is the singleton \(\{Rb_i\}\). Since the second summand in (2) has zero base coordinate,
\[
C_0=R\Delta+B,\qquad
C_{\pm e_{i,\ell}}=Rb_i+B.
\tag{4}
\]
It follows that the central fiber has mass \(A_m v_m\), every remote fiber has mass \(v_m\), and the total mixed mass is
\[
W=(A_m+2n)v_m.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\pi_z=\operatorname{vol}_m(C_z)/W\), and take \(z_0=0\). Every closed base halfspace through \(0\), written as \(\{z:a^{\mathsf T}z\ge0\}\) with \(a\ne0\), contains the central fiber and at least one point from each pair \(\{e_{i,\ell},-e_{i,\ell}\}\). It therefore has marginal mass at least
\[
\frac{A_m+n}{A_m+2n}.
\]
Taking \(a\) with every coordinate nonzero includes exactly one point from each pair, so this lower bound is attained. Hence
\[
\tau=\tau_\pi(0)=\frac{A_m+n}{A_m+2n}>\frac12\ge2^{-n}.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, \(0\) is a positive-mass weighted marginal centerpoint.

A primitive lattice line through \(0\) contains a remote support point from (3) only when its primitive direction is \(\pm e_{i,\ell}\), in which case it contains exactly the pair \(\{\pm e_{i,\ell}\}\). Hence the largest such line mass, divided by \(\tau\), is
\[
\max_{v\ {\rm primitive}}
\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\pi_{kv}}{\tau}
=\frac{A_m+2}{A_m+n}
\le\frac{M+2}{n}
=\frac1{m(m+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to bound the depth of every mixed point. First let \(p=(0,x)\) with \(x\in C_0\). Since \(\sum_i b_i=0\), at least one index \(i\) satisfies \(b_i^{\mathsf T}x\ge0\). The closed halfspace
\[
H=\{(z,y):b_i^{\mathsf T}y\ge b_i^{\mathsf T}x\}
\tag{8}
\]
contains \(p\). For \(j\ne i\), every \(y\in Rb_j+B\) satisfies
\[
b_i^{\mathsf T}y
\le Rb_i^{\mathsf T}b_j+1
=-\frac{m+1}{m}+1
=-\frac1m
<0
\le b_i^{\mathsf T}x.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus (8) excludes every remote fiber of color \(j\ne i\), including its boundary. It can meet only the central fiber and the \(2L\) remote fibers of color \(i\), so
\[
\mathcal H_m(S\cap H)\le(A_m+2L)v_m.
\tag{10}
\]

Next let \(p=(s e_{i,\ell},x)\) be any point of a remote fiber, where \(s\in\{-1,1\}\). Writing \(z_{i,\ell}\) for the corresponding base coordinate, the closed halfspace
\[
H_{i,\ell,s}=\{(z,y):s z_{i,\ell}\ge1\}
\tag{11}
\]
contains the entire fiber through \(p\). By (3), it contains no other positive-mass lattice fiber, so
\[
\mathcal H_m(S\cap H_{i,\ell,s})=v_m.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (3), (10), and (12) cover every point of \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^m)\). Therefore
\[
\sup_{p\in S}\frac{h_S(p)}{\tau W}
\le\frac{A_m+2L}{A_m+n}
\le\frac{(2m+1)(M+2)}{m(m+1)(M+2)}
=\frac{2m+1}{m(m+1)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]
Here the second inequality uses \(A_m\le M\), \(L=m(M+2)\), and \(A_m+n\ge n\).

Finally, given arbitrary \(\eta,c>0\), choose \(m\) so large that
\[
\frac1{m(m+1)}<\eta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{2m+1}{m(m+1)}<c.
\]
Then (7) gives the strict light-line hypothesis for every primitive direction, while (13) shows that every mixed point has normalized depth strictly less than \(c\tau\). This proves the claimed counterexample family.
