## Statement

Let \(s,h\ge1\) be integers, let \(Q=[-h,h]^s\), and let \(g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, convex, and nondecreasing, with \(g(0)=0\). For \(v\in\mathbb R^s\) and \(L\ge0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_g(v)&=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-g(\|y-v\|_\infty)},\\
Z_g(0;L)&=\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\\g(\|y\|_\infty)\le L}}
e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)},&
M&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{Z_g(0;L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This maximum exists and is attained at a value in \(\{g(0),g(1),\ldots,g(h)\}\). Uniformly over the stated data,

\[
\sup_{v\in\mathbb R^s}Z_g(v)\le 9\,2^s M.
\]

Plateaus are allowed, and every radius and cost boundary is included.

The notation map to accepted fact f55232eea7862d8c is \(s=s\), \(h=h\), \(Q=Q\), and \(v=v\); when \(g(t)=at\) with \(a\ge0\), its \(Z_Q(a;v)\) is exactly \(Z_g(v)\). In accepted fact 105a0951ae3ea2b6, take its \(h_y=h\), its second integer block \(y\) to be the present \(y\), and its cube \(Q\) to be the present \(Q\). For that same linear specialization, its \(Z_{\mathrm{cube}}\) is \(Z_g(0)\), and the mass in its central cost cutoff is \(Z_g(0;L)\). The present statement uses no first-block variable or continuous-fiber variable from that fact. The estimates below establish the nonlinear extension directly.

## Proof

Every central radius \(\|y\|_\infty\), for \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), is one of the integers \(0,\ldots,h\). Consequently the numerator \(Z_g(0;L)\) changes only when \(L\) reaches one of the finitely many values \(g(0),\ldots,g(h)\). Between consecutive distinct values it is constant, while \(1+L\) increases. It is also constant for \(L\ge g(h)\). Since the cutoff includes equality, its ratio with \(1+L\) attains a maximum at one of these values, including when some values coincide. The origin belongs to the cutoff at \(L=0\) and has weight one, so \(M\ge1\).

We first prove a cost cutoff estimate for the central sum. Use the same coordinate contraction as in the proof of accepted fact 105a0951ae3ea2b6:
\(\eta(y)_j=\operatorname{sgn}(y_j)\lfloor |y_j|/2\rfloor\), where \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\).
This is a map from \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) into itself and satisfies \(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty\le \|y\|_\infty/2\). A zero image coordinate has at most the three preimages \(-1,0,1\); a nonzero image coordinate has at most two preimages. Restricting to the finite cube can only remove preimages, so each image vector has at most \(3^s\) preimages. Monotonicity, convexity, and \(g(0)=0\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
g(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty)
&\le g(\|y\|_\infty/2)\le \tfrac12 g(\|y\|_\infty),\\
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)/2}
&\le\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-g(\|\eta(y)\|_\infty)}
\le 3^s Z_g(0).
\end{aligned}
\]

It follows that the strict complement of the cutoff at \(4s\) obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_g(0)-Z_g(0;4s)
&=\sum_{\substack{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\\g(\|y\|_\infty)>4s}}
e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)}\\
&\le e^{-2s}
\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}e^{-g(\|y\|_\infty)/2}\\
&\le (3e^{-2})^s Z_g(0)<\tfrac12 Z_g(0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(e^2>1+2+2+8/6>6\), and \(s\ge1\). Thus
\(Z_g(0;4s)\ge Z_g(0)/2\), and hence
\(Z_g(0)\le2(1+4s)M\).
This argument uses all the original central weights and includes sites with cost exactly \(4s\).

We next establish the shift estimate

\[
Z_g(v)\le 2^s+(4/3)^s Z_g(0)
\qquad(v\in\mathbb R^s).
\tag{1}
\]

For \(t\ge0\), let \(N_v(t)\) be the number of \(y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) satisfying \(\|y-v\|_\infty\le t\). This has the same meaning as \(N_v(t)\) in the proof of accepted fact f55232eea7862d8c. To check the needed bound directly, an interval of length \(2t\) contains at most \(\lfloor2t\rfloor+1\) integers: with \(m\ge1\) integers, the difference between the largest and smallest is at least \(m-1\). Each coordinate also has at most \(2h+1\) choices in \(Q\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_v(t)&\le\min(2h+1,\lfloor2t\rfloor+1)^s,\\
N_0(t)&=\min(2h+1,2\lfloor t\rfloor+1)^s.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(0\le t<1\), the first bound gives \(N_v(t)\le2^s\). If \(t\ge1\), put \(k=\lfloor t\rfloor\ge1\). When \(k\ge h\), \(N_0(t)=(2h+1)^s\) and \(N_v(t)\le N_0(t)\). When \(1\le k<h\), one instead has

\[
\frac{N_v(t)}{N_0(t)}
\le\left(\frac{2k+2}{2k+1}\right)^s
\le(4/3)^s.
\]

These bounds apply at integer and half-integer radii as well as between them; in particular \(t=1\) belongs to the second case. For every finite \(t\ge0\) they imply
\(N_v(t)\le2^s+(4/3)^sN_0(t)\).

If \(g\) is identically zero, then \(Z_g(v)=Z_g(0)=(2h+1)^s\), so (1) holds. Suppose now that \(g\) is not identically zero. The function is continuous: for each \(b>0\) and \(0\le t_1<t_2\le b\), interpolation between \(t_1\) and \(2b\), together with nonnegativity and monotonicity, gives
\(0\le g(t_2)-g(t_1)\le (t_2-t_1)g(2b)/b\).
Choose \(b>0\) with \(g(b)>0\). Convexity between \(0\) and \(t\) gives \(g(t)\ge (t/b)g(b)\) for \(t\ge b\), so \(g(t)\) tends to infinity. Thus for every \(L\ge0\) its nonempty sublevel set is a closed bounded interval \([0,R_L]\). This interval includes a possible zero-cost plateau and its endpoint.

For every \(t\ge0\), the elementary layer identity
\(e^{-g(t)}=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}\mathbf{1}_{\{g(t)\le L\}}\,dL\)
holds. Summing over the finite lattice cube and using the sublevel interval just proved yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_g(v)
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}N_v(R_L)\,dL\\
&\le 2^s\int_0^\infty e^{-L}\,dL
 +(4/3)^s\int_0^\infty e^{-L}N_0(R_L)\,dL\\
&=2^s+(4/3)^sZ_g(0).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (1) for every real \(v\), including shifts outside \(Q\). The sublevel intervals and the counting functions both use weak inequalities, so no radius or cost boundary has been dropped.

Finally, combine (1), \(M\ge1\), and the central cutoff estimate:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_g(v)
&\le2^s+(4/3)^sZ_g(0)\\
&\le2^sM+2(1+4s)(4/3)^sM\\
&=2^s\bigl[1+2(1+4s)(2/3)^s\bigr]M.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(s=1\) the quantity \((1+4s)(2/3)^s\) equals \(10/3\), and for \(s=2\) it equals \(4\). For every integer \(s\ge2\), the ratio of its value at \(s+1\) to its value at \(s\) is
\(2(4s+5)/[3(4s+1)]\le1\), because \(4s\ge7\). Hence \((1+4s)(2/3)^s\le4\) for all \(s\ge1\). The last displayed bound is therefore at most \(9\,2^sM\). Since the estimate is uniform in \(v\), it also bounds the stated supremum.
