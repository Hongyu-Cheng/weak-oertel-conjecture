## Statement

For every integer $d\ge2$ and real numbers $1/2\le b\le c\le1$,
$$
1+[(1+1/d)b+2c-2]_+^d\ge (2b-1)^d+b^d+c^d,
$$
where $[r]_+=\max\{r,0\}$.

## Proof

Put $s=1+1/d$, and write $F(b,c)=1+[s b+2c-2]_+^d-(2b-1)^d-b^d-c^d$. At fixed $b$ the derivative in $c$ is negative in the inactive region. Where the positive part is active its sign is the sign of $2(sb+2c-2)^{d-1}-c^{d-1}$. The ratio $(sb+2c-2)/c$ increases strictly, because $sb<2$. With $q=2^{-1/(d-1)}$, the only possible interior minimum therefore occurs at $c_*(b)=(2-sb)/(2-q)$. Its value is
$$
F(b,c_*(b))=1-(2b-1)^d-b^d-\frac{(2-sb)^d}{2(2-q)^{d-1}}.
$$
This is concave in $b$. The feasibility condition $b\le c_*(b)\le1$ gives the interval $[\max(1/2,q/s),\,2/(s+2-q)]$. If this interval is nonempty, its endpoints give $c=b$, $c=1$ or $b=1/2$. Concavity shows it suffices to check these three edges, including their corners.

At $c=1$, $F(b,1)=(s^d-1)b^d-(2b-1)^d\ge0$ since $s^d\ge2$ and $0\le2b-1\le b$. At $c=b$, divide by $b^d$ and put $u=1/b-1\in[0,1]$. The desired inequality becomes
$$
(1+u)^d-(1-u)^d+[1+1/d-2u]_+^d\ge2.
$$
The odd terms in the binomial difference give at least $2du$. For $v=2du$, Bernoulli gives $[1+(1-v)/d]_+^d\ge2-v$ when $v\le d+1$. When $v\ge d+1$, the $v$ term alone exceeds $2$. This proves the second edge.

For $b=1/2$ and $d\ge3$, $q\ge1/\sqrt{2}>2/3\ge s/2$, so $c_*(1/2)\ge1$ and $F(1/2,c)$ is nonincreasing up to the first edge. For $d=2$, $F(1/2,c)=3/4+[2c-5/4]_+^2-c^2$. It is at least $23/64$ when $c\le5/8$, and equals $3(c-5/6)^2+11/48$ when $c\ge5/8$. This proves the remaining edge and the inequality.
