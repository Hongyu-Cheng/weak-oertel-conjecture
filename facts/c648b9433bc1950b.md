## Statement

Let $C$ have base affine dimension $n\ge1$ and actual positive $d$-dimensional sampled fibers. Let $F$ lie in a nonvertical supporting plane $a_F\cdot z+v\cdot x=b_F$ of $C$, with $v\ne0$ and $C$ on the $\le$ side. Consider an actual cap with normal $(a,u)$, $u\ne0$, and threshold $c=(a,u)\cdot(p,y)-b$, where $b\ge0$ and the source has an own inball $y+r_s B_d\subseteq C_p$, $r_s>0$. Suppose the cap boundary coincides with a positive-area own-facet patch in $F$ at some sampled base. Write $u=s\alpha v$ with $s\in\{-1,1\}$, $\alpha>0$. In the case $s=-1$ suppose $b<\alpha r_s\lVert v\rVert$. All sampled supporting incidences of this cap on $F$ lie over the proper affine section
$$
\{z:(a-s\alpha a_F)\cdot z+s\alpha b_F-c=0\}.
$$
It excludes $p$ and has dimension at most $n-1$. For a roof plane $v\cdot x=\ell(z)$ this conclusion also holds when $p$ is outside the contact cell of $\ell$.

## Proof

A positive $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ coincidence of two nonvertical planes restricted to one fiber requires the same within-fiber plane, so $u=s\alpha v$. This is also valid for the endpoint case $d=1$. On $F$ the cap margin reduces to the displayed affine function of $z$. Evaluating at $p$ gives $s\alpha(b_F-a_F\cdot p-v\cdot y)+b$. Testing the global supporting inequality on the own inball bounds the bracket below by $r_s\lVert v\rVert$, so this value is positive for $s=1$ and negative for $s=-1$. Consequently the affine function is nonzero at $p$ and vanishes at every incidence. Its zero set in the base affine hull is proper, and the assumed incidence makes it nonempty. The argument evaluates the global supporting inequality at $p$ and needs no contact there. The section can cross the base hull.
