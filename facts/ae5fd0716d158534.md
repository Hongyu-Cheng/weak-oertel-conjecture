## Statement

Let a plane meet the interior of a nondegenerate tetrahedron. Let $F$ be the relative three-dimensional volume of either closed cap and $b$ the barycentric centroid of the actual section with its uniform two-dimensional area measure. Then for every index $j$,
$$
F(1-F)\le27\prod_{i\ne j}b_i.
$$
Equality holds precisely when the section contains a tetrahedral edge and $j$ is an endpoint of that edge. In particular every strict quadrilateral has strict inequality, and the constant $27$ is sharp.

## Proof

In a strict quadrilateral orient the cap so the omitted index belongs to the positive pair. This exchanges $F$ and $1-F$ if needed and preserves the actual centroid. If the two positive heights differ, apply the two-positive simplex variance theorem (baef4d408413a817) with $d=3$, two negative heights, and omission of the selected positive index. Its strict conclusion uses exactly the three actual centroid factors.

For a tie rescale to heights $(1,1,-r,-s)$ with $r,s>0$. Set $D=r+s+2rs$ and $\theta=s(1+r)/D$. The raw-height part of tetrahedral-quadrilateral-section-calculus (1462fa9ce6ced54e) gives
$$
3b_1=\frac{s}{1+s}+(1-\theta)\frac r{1+r},\quad
3b_2=\frac{2-\theta}{1+r},\quad
3b_3=\frac{1+\theta}{1+s},\quad
F=\frac{1+2(r+s)+3rs}{(1+r)^2(1+s)^2}.
$$
The first two centroid coordinates agree by exchange of the equal positive heights, so it suffices to omit index $0$. Multiply $(3b_1)(3b_2)(3b_3)-F(1-F)$ by $(1+r)^4(1+s)^4D^3$. With $M_{ij}=r^is^j+r^js^i$ for $i>j$ and $M_{ii}=r^is^i$, substitution and expansion gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&2M_{40}+7M_{31}+9M_{22}\\
&+3M_{50}+29M_{41}+64M_{32}\\
&+27M_{51}+141M_{42}+220M_{33}\\
&+96M_{52}+328M_{43}\\
&+177M_{53}+376M_{44}\\
&+2M_{63}+174M_{54}\\
&+7M_{64}+78M_{55}+8M_{65}+3M_{66}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is strictly positive for $r,s>0$.

For every remaining proper incidence, at least one sign occurs at just one vertex $h$. Orient toward it. The section is the triangle on the three incident edge intercepts, at fractions $0<\eta_i\le1$ from $h$, $i\ne h$. At least one fraction is below one. Determinants and the triangle centroid give $F=\prod_{i\ne h}\eta_i$, $3b_i=\eta_i$ for $i\ne h$, and $3b_h=\sum_{i\ne h}(1-\eta_i)$. Omitting $h$ gives the strictly larger bound $F$. Omitting $j\ne h$ and dividing the desired inequality by $F/\eta_j>0$ gives
$$
\eta_j(1-\textstyle\prod_{i\ne h}\eta_i)\le\sum_{i\ne h}(1-\eta_i).
$$
The product gap $1-abc=(1-a)+a(1-b)+ab(1-c)$ is at most the sum of the three deficits, with equality exactly when at most one deficit is positive. Since $F<1$, the extra factor requires $\eta_j=1$ for equality. Thus there is equality exactly when two intercepts are vertices and the omitted index is one of them, equivalent to the stated edge condition. Taking intercepts $(z,1,1)$ with $0<z<1$ attains equality for either of those indices, proving sharpness. Proper section planes have volume-zero boundary, so cap reversal preserves $F(1-F)$ also for these incidences.
