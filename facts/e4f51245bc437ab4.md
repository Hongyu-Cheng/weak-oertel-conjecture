## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`. In particular, \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
and there are a finite physical endpoint support \(E_\perp\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), weights \(\tau_y>0\), and points \(p_y\in C_y\) such that the positive endpoint measure
\[
 \nu_E:=\gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_\perp}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}
\tag{2}
\]
satisfies
\[
 \nu_E(H)\leq\mu_C(H)
\tag{3}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Let \(F\) be any nonempty family of unordered pairs
\[
 e=\{u,v\}\subseteq E_\perp,
 \qquad
 u\ne v,
 \qquad
 u\equiv v\pmod 2.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(e=\{u,v\}\in F\), define
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 q_e:=\frac{p_u+p_v}{2}\in C_{m_e}.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(x=(x_e)_{e\in F}\) be any nonzero family of nonnegative real numbers satisfying the endpoint-capacity constraints
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{e\in F:\ y\in e}}x_e\leq\tau_y
 \qquad(y\in E_\perp).}
\tag{6}
\]
Then the positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_x:=\gamma_d\sum_{e\in F}
 x_e\delta_{(m_e,q_e)}}
\tag{7}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\):
\[
 \boxed{\sigma_x(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{8}
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). This conclusion allows different edges of positive \(x_e\) to share endpoints and to have different physical midpoints.

Let
\[
 P_x:=\operatorname{supp}\sigma_x,
 \qquad
 A_x:=\operatorname{aff}
 \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(P_x),
 \qquad
 \rho_x:=\dim A_x,
\tag{9}
\]
and define the continuous residual rank
\[
 r_x:=
 \min_{s:A_x\to\mathbb R^d\ \text{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q-s(m):(m,q)\in P_x\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives the exact intrinsic-rank screen
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_x}
 \frac{\sum_{e\in F}x_e}{r_x+1}.}
\tag{11}
\]

Define the \(\tau\)-capacitated fractional-matching value of \(F\) by
\[
 \mathfrak B_\tau(F):=
 \max\left\{
 \sum_{e\in F}x_e:
 x_e\geq0,\quad
 \sum_{\substack{e\in F:\ y\in e}}x_e\leq\tau_y
 \ \text{for every }y\in E_\perp
 \right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
It has the exact fractional-vertex-cover dual
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_\tau(F)=
 \min\left\{
 \sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_yz_y:
 z_y\geq0,\quad
 z_u+z_v\geq1
 \ \text{for every }\{u,v\}\in F
 \right\}.}
\tag{13}
\]
Both optima are attained. If \(z^*\) is a dual optimizer, then
\[
 H^*:=\{y\in E_\perp:z_y^*\geq1/2\}
\tag{14}
\]
is a vertex cover of \(F\) and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{y\in H^*}\tau_y
 \leq2\mathfrak B_\tau(F).}
\tag{15}
\]
Conversely, every vertex cover \(H\subseteq E_\perp\) of \(F\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_\tau(F)
 \leq\sum_{y\in H}\tau_y.}
\tag{16}
\]

In particular, one may take \(F\) to be the family of pairs contributing to the \(74\)-th-and-later tail \(E_{74}\) of accepted fact `8d0a0563ad180fca`. Its accepted lower bound
\[
 E_{74}>\frac{37}{300}Q
\tag{17}
\]
is a lower bound for a sum of geometric-mean pair weights \(\sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}\), not for the capacity-constrained value \(\mathfrak B_\tau(F)\). No inequality that lower-bounds \(\mathfrak B_\tau(F)\) in terms of \(E_{74}\) or \(Q\) is asserted here. Equations (8), (11), and (13) therefore do not prove an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality or the locked final theorem.

## Proof

For every \(y\in E_\perp\), accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` gives \(p_y\in C_y\). Thus \((u,p_u),(v,p_v)\in C\) for \(e=\{u,v\}\in F\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
 (m_e,q_e)
 =\frac12(u,p_u)+\frac12(v,p_v)
 \in C.
\tag{18}
\]
The same-parity hypothesis in (4) gives \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\). Hence every atom in (7) lies in \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

Fix a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). For every edge \(e=\{u,v\}\in F\) whose midpoint atom \((m_e,q_e)\) lies in \(H\), equation (18) implies that at least one of \((u,p_u)\) and \((v,p_v)\) lies in \(H\). Indeed, an affine functional defining \(H\), evaluated at the average in (18), cannot be on the closed side of its threshold when both endpoints are strictly on the opposite side. Choose one such endpoint and denote it by \(y(e,H)\). The choice may send many different edges to the same endpoint. Grouping by these chosen endpoints and using (6) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_x(H)
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e\in F:\ (m_e,q_e)\in H}}x_e\\
 &\leq\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in E_\perp:\ (y,p_y)\in H}}
 \sum_{\substack{e\in F:\ y(e,H)=y}}x_e\\
 &\leq\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\in E_\perp:\ (y,p_y)\in H}}
 \tau_y
 =\nu_E(H)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
This proves (8). In particular, endpoint reuse is charged only once through its total load in (6); no endpoint-disjointness across different midpoints was used.

The mass of (7) is
\[
 |\sigma_x|=\gamma_d\sum_{e\in F}x_e>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to (8), with the intrinsic ranks (9)--(10). It gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d
 \frac{\sum_{e\in F}x_e}
 {2^{\rho_x}(r_x+1)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Multiplication by \(2^n\) proves (11).

The feasible set in (12) is nonempty. It is bounded because summing the endpoint constraints counts each edge twice and gives
\[
 2\sum_{e\in F}x_e
 \leq\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_y.
\tag{22}
\]
The usual finite-dimensional linear-program dual assigns one nonnegative variable \(z_y\) to each endpoint constraint. The coefficient of \(x_{\{u,v\}}\) in the resulting dual constraint is \(z_u+z_v\), so the dual is exactly the minimization problem in (13). The primal has a feasible point and a finite optimum, and the dual has the feasible point \(z_y=1\) for every \(y\). Finite-dimensional strong duality and attainment therefore prove (13).

For every edge \(\{u,v\}\in F\), dual feasibility gives \(z_u^*+z_v^*\geq1\). Hence at least one endpoint belongs to (14), so \(H^*\) is a vertex cover. Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{y\in H^*}\tau_y
 \leq
 2\sum_{y\in H^*}\tau_yz_y^*
 \leq
 2\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_yz_y^*
 =2\mathfrak B_\tau(F),
\tag{23}
\]
which proves (15). If \(H\) is any vertex cover, set \(z_y=1\) on \(H\) and \(z_y=0\) off \(H\). This is dual feasible, so (13) gives (16).

Finally, accepted fact `8d0a0563ad180fca` defines the tail quantity in (17) using the geometric-mean weights \(\sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}\) and explicitly does not supply endpoint-capacity feasibility across different midpoints. The present argument neither replaces those weights by a feasible loading nor bounds the fractional-cover dual. This proves all stated conclusions and the stated limitation.
