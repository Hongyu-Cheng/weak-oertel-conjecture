## Statement

Thus, for every positive-volume lattice fiber, put
\[
 C_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_y:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y),
 \qquad
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
\tag{1}
\]
and put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 \gamma_{2d}:=\left(\frac{2d}{2d+1}\right)^{2d},
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C).
\tag{2}
\]
For a positive-volume bounded measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<m\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), use the canonical metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \mathrm{a.e.},\quad
 \int_Kf=m
 \right\},
\]
and, for a Borel set \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), use the mixed-volume measure
\[
 \mu_C(H):=
 \sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_y:(y,x)\in H\}.
\]
Let \(Q>0\) be the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir.  The canonical hard ledger is
\[
 R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{3}

For an original canonical reservoir edge \(g=\{u,v\}\), retain its reservoir weight \(r_g\), original load-minimal load \(\omega_g\), original midpoint and centroid midpoint

and, when \(\omega_g>0\), its original exposed contact

Retain also the canonical target multipliers \(0<\chi_m\leq1\) and the quantities

 \widehat\Omega:=\sum_g\chi_{m_g}\omega_g,

if \(B_{\mathrm{cong}}=0\), set \(P_{\mathrm{anc}}:=0\) without assigning ancestral ranks.  Then
\[
 A:=\max\{P_{\mathrm{pool}},P_{\mathrm{anc}}\}
 <\frac Q{200}.
\tag{10}
\]

Retain the fixed missed dyadic matching and the rescaled internal atlas.  For every physical opposite endpoint \(y\), let \(e_2(y)\) be its unique missed dyadic edge and let \(t_{e_2(y)}>0\) be that edge's matching weight.  Every internal label

\tag{11}

Let \(\mathscr B\subseteq\mathscr A_{\mathrm{cross}}\) be a whole-label cross subfamily and put

Retain a nonzero dyadic load-minimal stress \((v_E^{(2)})\) saturating every \(E\in\mathcal J\), in the notation of accepted fact `bf0d5c250b8f688c`.  The superscript \((2)\) is an alias used only here to distinguish this derived stress from the original reservoir optimizer in (4)-(7):

 r_{\mathrm{par}}:=
 \min_{s:A_{\mathrm{par}}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}

 \boxed{|\nu_{\mathscr B}^{\mathrm{par}}|=4\gamma_dM.}

## Proof

We first audit the dyadic reflected-pair multiplicities.  Since \(\mathscr B\subseteq\mathscr A_{\mathrm{cross}}\), accepted fact `e0dcff96f9b90a96` says that the two opposite endpoints of every \(\sigma\in\mathscr B\) lie on two distinct missed dyadic edges.  Thus each label contributes \(\eta_\sigma\) to exactly two distinct terms in the left side of (18), proving
\[
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal J}b_E=2M.
\tag{40}
\]
Fix \(E=\{y,y^\star\}\in\mathcal J\).  Aggregate first over all labels using the physical endpoint \(y\), and then over those using \(y^\star\).  The endpoint inequality (13), supplied by accepted fact `2fe46f47d19112ea`, bounds each aggregate by \(t_E\).  A cross label cannot occur in both endpoint aggregates for the same \(E\).  Therefore
\[
 b_E\leq t_E+t_E=2t_E.
\tag{41}
\]
Summing (41) and using (40) gives \(2M\leq2T\), which proves all of (18).  This calculation counts labels before any target collision and counts every whole label exactly twice, once for each distinct supporting edge.

We prove the common-body cloud bound.  Equation (13) at the two endpoints of \(E\) gives
\[
 \alpha_E=\min\{a_y,a_{y^\star}\}\geq4t_E.
\tag{42}
\]
The dyadic edges are endpoint-disjoint, so different members of \(\mathcal J\) are different unordered reflected pairs.  By (15), every one of them is reflected about the same lattice base \(\bar m\).  Use exactly these reflected directions, and no central index, in the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`.  That definition permits coincident continuous cloud points: their masses remain summed, while \(k_c\) is computed from the aggregated physical support.  It gives the first inequality in (20).  Equations (18) and (42) give
\[
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal J}\alpha_E
 \geq4\sum_{E\in\mathcal J}t_E
 =4T\geq4M,
\tag{43}
\]
which proves the second inequality in (20).

We next audit the original parent charge.  For an original reservoir edge \(g\), every label counted in (21) is one of the transverse-pairing variables incident with \(g\).  Accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66` gives the original feasibility constraint
\[
 \sum_{\sigma:\,g\in\{e_\sigma,f_\sigma\}}
 \theta_\sigma\leq\omega_g.
\tag{44}
\]
Since \(\mathscr B\) is a subfamily and \(\eta_\sigma\leq\theta_\sigma\), equations (21) and (44) give
\[
 q_g^{\mathscr B}\leq\omega_g.
\tag{45}
\]
Equation (6) then gives the remaining coordinatewise inequality in (22).  A transverse pair consists of two distinct parent edges.  Therefore, even if many labels reuse one parent edge and are aggregated in \(q_g^{\mathscr B}\), every label contributes its weight once to each of its two distinct parents and
\[
 \sum_gq_g^{\mathscr B}
 =2\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr B}\eta_\sigma
 =2M.
\tag{46}
\]
This proves (22) with no parent-edge multiplicity loss.

Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` supplies the canonical reservoir centroid measure
\[
 \bar\nu_r:=\sum_gr_g\delta_{(m_g,\bar c_g)}
\tag{47}
\]
and states that every nonzero positive submeasure \(\sigma\leq\bar\nu_r\) has
\[
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 \geq2^{n-\rho_\sigma}
 \frac{|\sigma|}{r_\sigma+1}.
\tag{48}
\]
Equations (22)-(23) give \(0<\bar\nu_{\mathscr B}\leq\bar\nu_r\) and
\[
 |\bar\nu_{\mathscr B}|
 =\sum_gq_g^{\mathscr B}=2M.
\tag{49}
\]
Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (24)-(25), including after all equal atoms are aggregated.  Substitution of (49) into (48) proves (26).

For the contact measure, apply the general original-contact hybrid theorem of accepted fact `7b2a94c842113bf3` with its midpoint charge \(\beta_g\) identified exactly as
\[
 \beta_g=q_g^{\mathscr B},
\tag{50}
\]
and with both endpoint-incidence charges equal to zero.  The ownership condition of that theorem is \(\beta_g\leq\omega_g\), which is exactly (45).  It therefore proves that (27) is carried by the mixed lattice section and is halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\).  Its mass is
\[
 |\nu_{\mathscr B}^{\mathrm{par}}|
 =2\gamma_d\sum_gq_g^{\mathscr B}
 =4\gamma_dM,
\tag{51}
\]
proving (30).  If different parent edges give the same physical contact atom, the atomic coefficients add; domination was proved before aggregation by the one-edge ownership inequality, so neither mass nor source capacity is lost.

Apply the halfspace-dominated residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to the nonzero measure (27).  Its intrinsic ranks are precisely (28)-(29), and (51) gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{4\gamma_dM}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathrm{par}}}(r_{\mathrm{par}}+1)}.
\tag{52}
\]
Multiplication by \(2^n\) proves (31).

Every capacity in (20), (26), and (31) is a nonnegative summand of, or equals \(D\) in, the hard ledger (3).  Hence each is strictly smaller than \(Q/100\).  Rearranging (20), (26), and (31) separately gives the first inequalities in (32), (33), and (34).  The strict mass bound (14) gives
\[
 400\frac{M}{Q}>\frac72,
 \qquad
 200\frac{M}{Q}>\frac74,
\tag{53}
\]
which proves their second inequalities.  These are three simultaneous rank screens, not three additively combined capacity charges.

It remains to prove the upstream channel split.  Equations (35)-(36) and \(0<\chi_{m_g}\leq1\) give nonnegative charges and the exact edgewise identity
\[
 q_g^{\mathrm{pool}}+q_g^{\mathrm{cong}}
 =q_g^{\mathscr B}.
\tag{54}
\]
Summing (54) and using (22) proves the first equality in (37).  Also, (45) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{\mathrm{pool}}^{\mathscr B}
 &=\sum_g\chi_{m_g}q_g^{\mathscr B}
 \leq\sum_g\chi_{m_g}\omega_g
 =\widehat\Omega,\\
 M_{\mathrm{cong}}^{\mathscr B}
 &=\sum_g(1-\chi_{m_g})q_g^{\mathscr B}
 \leq\sum_g(1-\chi_{m_g})\omega_g
 =B_{\mathrm{cong}}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{55}
\]
This proves all of (37), including arbitrary repetitions of original target midpoints, since \(\chi_m\) is constant on every such target and both sides of (55) are grouped by the same original edges.  Since two nonnegative numbers sum to \(2M\), at least one is at least \(M\).

Suppose first that \(M_{\mathrm{pool}}^{\mathscr B}\geq M\).  Equations (8) and (10) give
\[
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_J}
 \frac{\widehat\Omega}{r_J+1}
 =P_{\mathrm{pool}}<\frac Q{200}.
\tag{56}
\]
All quantities are positive.  Rearranging (56), using (37), and then (14) proves (38).

Suppose instead that \(M_{\mathrm{cong}}^{\mathscr B}\geq M\).  Equations (14) and (37) imply \(B_{\mathrm{cong}}>0\), so the ancestral support and ranks in (9) are defined.  Equations (9)-(10) give
\[
 4\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{anc}}}
 \frac{B_{\mathrm{cong}}}{r_{\mathrm{anc}}+1}
 =P_{\mathrm{anc}}<\frac Q{200}.
\tag{57}
\]
Rearranging (57), using (37), and then (14) proves (39).

The common-midpoint hypothesis and dyadic saturation were retained with the exact distinction (16).  None of the proofs above substitutes \(t_E\) for \(\omega_g\) or \(v_E^{(2)}\) for an original reservoir dual vector.  Equations (20), (26), (31), and (38)-(39) contain no assertion about a quotient-lattice point set or about \(\mathcal B_\eta\).  This completes exactly the registered three-rank and upstream-channel ledger without claiming the missing arithmetic coupling.
