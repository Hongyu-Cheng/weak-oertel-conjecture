## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) and \(0\le r_{\max}\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor\) be integers. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{k,r_{\max}}
&:=\left\{\mathbf x\in[0,2]^k:
\sum_{i=1}^k\max\lbrace x_i-1,0\rbrace\le r_{\max},\quad
\sum_{i=1}^k x_i\le k\right\},\\
D&:=P_{k,r_{\max}}\cap\mathbb Z^k
=\left\{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k:
\sum_i x_i\le k,\quad
\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le r_{\max}\right\},\\
A&:=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace,\qquad N:=k+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

For arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), write
\(w_{\mathbf x}:=e^{-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x}\),
\(Z:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x}\), and
\(p_{(\mathbf x,0)}:=w_{\mathbf x}/Z\). Keep the original depth and moment

$$
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf a\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf a},
\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
$$

where \(H\) ranges over all closed affine halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero normal. For \(L\subseteq[k]\), put \(\mathbf y_L:=\mathbf1_L\in D\), and define the oriented stock

$$
\mathcal K_L
:=\sum_{\substack{E,F\subseteq L,\ E\cap F=\varnothing\\
|E|=|F|\le r_{\max}}}
\exp\left(-\left|\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(\mathbf1_E-\mathbf1_F)\right|\right).
$$

The sum is over ordered pairs and includes \((\varnothing,\varnothing)\). The symbol \(\mathcal K_L\) denotes this field-dependent stock, not the rank-profile quantities \(K_j\) of accepted fact 803f398e39c669b9. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x}^4
+\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}w_{\mathbf y_L}^4
\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal K_L+1}{2}\right)^3-1\right],\\
B_{\mathrm{stock}}&:=\frac{V}{Z^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For every such choice,

$$
B_{\mathrm{stock}}\le M_3(p).
\tag{1}
$$

Nevertheless, for every integer \(k\ge3\), choose

$$
r_{\max}:=\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\qquad
R_k:=2^k,\qquad
\boldsymbol\beta:=(-\log R_k,\log R_k,0,\ldots,0).
\tag{2}
$$

All activities \(e^{-\beta_i}\) are positive rational numbers, and
\(w_{\mathbf x}=R_k^{x_1-x_2}\) is rational. On this sequence,

$$
0<8^{k+1}B_{\mathrm{stock}}
\le128\cdot3^8\left[\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k
+\left(\frac{14}{81}\right)^k\right]
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3}
$$

In particular, the infimum of \(8^{k+1}B_{\mathrm{stock}}\) over the full registered parameter range is zero. At the same time, the actual original moment on (2) satisfies

$$
8^{k+1}M_3(p)
\ge\frac{64}{271^3}\left(1+\frac{18}{2^k}\right)^{-4}.
\tag{4}
$$

## Proof

First prove (1), including the sampling accounting. Every binary root belongs to \(D\). Fix \(L\), and for an index \((E,F)\) in \(\mathcal K_L\), let

$$
\mathbf z_+:=\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_E-\mathbf1_F,
\qquad
\mathbf z_-:=\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_E+\mathbf1_F.
$$

Both endpoints have \(|E|=|F|\le r_{\max}\) twos and coordinate sum \(|L|\le k\), so they belong to \(D\). The map \((E,F)\mapsto\mathbf z_+\) is injective: the twos recover \(E\), and the zeros inside \(L\) recover \(F\). Swapping \(E,F\) is an involution whose only fixed index is \((\varnothing,\varnothing)\). Thus its nontrivial orbits give disjoint pairs of distinct original atoms with midpoint \((\mathbf y_L,0)\).

A closed halfspace containing this midpoint contains at least one endpoint of each pair, and it contains the root itself. Moreover,

$$
\min\lbrace w_{\mathbf z_+},w_{\mathbf z_-}\rbrace
=w_{\mathbf y_L}
\exp\left(-\left|\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(\mathbf1_E-\mathbf1_F)\right|\right).
$$

The two orientations have identical summands. Therefore every admissible halfspace containing the root has probability mass at least

$$
\frac{w_{\mathbf y_L}}{Z}
\left(1+\frac{\mathcal K_L-1}{2}\right)
=p_{(\mathbf y_L,0)}\frac{\mathcal K_L+1}{2}.
\tag{5}
$$

For every original atom \((\mathbf x,0)\), the singleton baseline gives
\(\tau_p((\mathbf x,0))\ge p_{(\mathbf x,0)}\). In the sampling sum defining \(M_3(p)\), use (5) at each binary root and the singleton bound at every other atom. The roots \(\mathbf y_L\) are distinct as \(L\) varies. This yields exactly

$$
M_3(p)\ge
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{(\mathbf x,0)}^4
+\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}p_{(\mathbf y_L,0)}^4
\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal K_L+1}{2}\right)^3-1\right]
=B_{\mathrm{stock}}.
$$

There is no duplicate sampling: each atom has one baseline term, and each binary root has one replacement increment. The endpoint sets used to bound different roots may overlap, but they occur inside separate valid depth inequalities, not as additional sampling terms. The midpoint argument applies in the original ambient space, including halfspaces with a nonzero height component.

Now take (2). The asymptotic construction was chosen analytically; no numerical minimum or floating-point witness is used. With \(r_{\max}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\), the quota restriction on twos is redundant, since each two contributes two to the nonnegative coordinate sum. Consequently

$$
D=\left\{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k:
\sum_i x_i\le k\right\}.
\tag{6}
$$

Put \(m:=k-2\), and let

$$
T:=\left\{\mathbf u\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^m:
\sum_i u_i\le m\right\},\qquad
F:=\lbrace(2,0,\mathbf u):\mathbf u\in T\rbrace\subseteq D.
$$

Write \(n_m:=|T|\). The involution \(\mathbf u\mapsto2\mathbf1-\mathbf u\) exchanges sums below and above \(m\). Hence

$$
n_m\ge\frac{3^m}{2}=\frac{3^{k-2}}2.
\tag{7}
$$

Every point of \(F\) has weight \(R_k^2\), so

$$
Z\ge R_k^2n_m\ge\frac{R_k^2\,3^{k-2}}2.
\tag{8}
$$

Since \(|D|\le3^k\) and \(x_1-x_2\le2\), the complete diagonal numerator satisfies

$$
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x}^4\le3^kR_k^8.
\tag{9}
$$

For any \(L\), every summand of \(\mathcal K_L\) is at most one, and its index maps injectively into \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace^L\). Thus

$$
1\le\mathcal K_L\le3^{|L|},\qquad
0\le\left(\frac{\mathcal K_L+1}{2}\right)^3-1
\le\mathcal K_L^3\le27^{|L|}.
\tag{10}
$$

Using the binary-root weights from (2), the entire additional stock numerator is bounded by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}w_{\mathbf y_L}^4
\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal K_L+1}{2}\right)^3-1\right]
&\le\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}
27^{|L|}R_k^{4(\mathbf1_{1\in L}-\mathbf1_{2\in L})}\\
&=(1+27R_k^4)(1+27R_k^{-4})28^{k-2}\\
&\le R_k^4\,28^k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

The last inequality uses \(R_k\ge1\), so
\(1+27R_k^4\le28R_k^4\) and \(1+27R_k^{-4}\le28\).
Combining (8), (9), and (11) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
8^{k+1}B_{\mathrm{stock}}
&\le\frac{16\,8^{k+1}}{R_k^8\,3^{4k-8}}
\left(3^kR_k^8+R_k^4\,28^k\right)\\
&=128\cdot3^8
\left[\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k
+R_k^{-4}\left(\frac{224}{81}\right)^k\right]\\
&=128\cdot3^8
\left[\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k
+\left(\frac{14}{81}\right)^k\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Positivity follows from the nonzero diagonal numerator. Both displayed bases are strictly below one, proving (3). This argument concerns the repaired proxy with every original diagonal term present.

Finally calibrate the actual moment. Equality \(x_1-x_2=2\) holds exactly when \((x_1,x_2)=(2,0)\), and (6) then identifies precisely the face \(F\). Every atom outside \(F\) has weight at most \(R_k\). Therefore its total weight is at most \(3^kR_k\), and the mass of the face satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(F\times\lbrace0\rbrace)
&=\frac{R_k^2n_m}{Z}
\ge\left(1+\frac{3^k}{R_kn_m}\right)^{-1}\\
&\ge\left(1+\frac{18}{R_k}\right)^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

Conditioned on this face, the original law is uniform. The set \(T\) is exactly the spatial support from accepted fact 803f398e39c669b9 with spatial dimension \(m\), quota \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and rank profile identically one. Its quota is again redundant. The affine embedding
\((\mathbf u,h)\mapsto(2,0,\mathbf u,h)\) identifies its supported atoms with \(F\times\lbrace0\rbrace\). The pullback of a closed ambient halfspace containing one of these atoms is either a closed halfspace with nonzero normal in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\), or the whole space. It follows that, for each atom of this face, its original depth is at least its conditional depth multiplied by the face mass.

Restricting the original sampling sum to the face gives the fourth power of that mass: one factor from sampling and three from depth. Applying 803f398e39c669b9 and then (12) therefore yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge p(F\times\lbrace0\rbrace)^4\,
\frac{1}{271^3}\,8^{-(m+1)}\\
&\ge\left(1+\frac{18}{2^k}\right)^{-4}
\frac{1}{271^3}\,8^{-(k-1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplication by \(8^{k+1}\) proves (4). Here \(m\ge1\) because \(k\ge3\), so every hypothesis of the accepted calibration applies.
