## Statement

Let $n\ge0$ and $d\ge1$ be integers. Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, with $\operatorname{aff}(P)=\mathbb R^n$, and let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$, $p\in P$, be compact convex bodies with nonempty interior. For each $q\in P$, let
$$
E_q=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{P\setminus\{q\}}:\quad
\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p=1,\quad \sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p p=q\right\}.
$$
Call the family strictly compatible if for each extreme point $\lambda$ of each nonempty $E_q$, the Minkowski combination $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p K_p$ is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_q$. Under this condition there is a common $\kappa>0$ such that $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p K_p+\kappa B_d\subseteq K_q$ for every such extreme point, where $B_d$ is the closed unit ball. For any family of convex bodies $K'_p$ with $d_H(K'_p,K_p)<\eta<\kappa/2$ for all $p$, the convex hull of the lifted bodies $\{p\}\times K'_p$ has exactly the prescribed integer fibers $K'_p$ at $p\in P$. In particular the original family is actual.

## Proof

There are finitely many extreme points across the nonempty coefficient polytopes. Compact containment gives a positive distance to the complement of $K_q$ for every displayed sum, so choose a common positive $\kappa$. When there are no rows, choose any $\kappa>0$.

For every unit $u$, the extreme-point inequalities read $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p h_{K_p}(u)\le h_{K_q}(u)-\kappa$. By linearity in $\lambda$ they hold on all of $E_q$. Hausdorff distance bounds the sup norm of the difference of support functions, so $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p h_{K'_p}(u)\le h_{K'_q}(u)-\kappa+2\eta\le h_{K'_q}(u)$. Hence every such Minkowski combination is contained in $K'_q$.

Group any lifted convex combination at base $q$ by its base indices. If its coefficient at $q$ is one, its continuous point lies in $K'_q$ by convexity. Otherwise, remove that coefficient and renormalize the rest to obtain a vector in $E_q$. The already proved inclusion and convexity with the contribution from $q$ put the continuous point in $K'_q$. The reverse inclusion follows because each lifted fiber generates the hull. Its projection is $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, whose integer bases are exactly $P$. Applying the same argument to $K'_p=K_p$ gives actual original fibers.
