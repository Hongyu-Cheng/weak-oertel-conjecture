## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and $H>0$. Put $B=H+1$ and $r(z)=(H-z)/B$. On $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, take density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$ with respect to $dx\,dz$. The total mass is $V=B/d$. For finite $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of the cap $z+sx\ge0$. Let $C(s)$ and $M(s)$ be the integrals of $f$ and $zf$, respectively, over the section $z+sx=0$ inside the support, against $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. The records of a probability law are the averages of $(P,C,M)$, and the law is centered if its averaged $M$ is zero. Write $C_0=(H/B)^{d-1}$ and $p_0=(H/B)^d$.

Allow also an atom at $s=\infty$, whose record is the limit $(V/2,0,0)$ as $s\to\infty$. Put $c=(V/C_0)(p_0-4/9)$. Every centered probability law on $[0,\infty]$ satisfies $4V/9\le\overline P-c\overline C\le5V/9$, with this same $c$.

For any two separately centered probability laws $\mu,\nu$ with $\overline C_\mu+\overline C_\nu>0$,

$$
\frac{\overline C_\nu\overline P_\mu+\overline C_\mu(V-\overline P_\nu)}
{V(\overline C_\mu+\overline C_\nu)}\ge\frac49\ge
\Gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
$$

If exactly one coarea is zero, the quotient is $1/2$. If both are zero, both cap fractions are $1/2$ and the quotient has continuous extension $1/2$ in the records.

## Proof

For a centered finite-slope law, apply the centered cone energy lemma with its $v,e(a_0)$. Its cap and coarea formulas give $(\overline P-c\overline C)/V=1-v+(4/9)e(a_0)$. The lower energy bound gives at least $(5-v)/9\ge4/9$. The upper energy bound gives at most $1-v+(4/9)(2v-1)/v$, whose gap from $5/9$ is $(3v-2)^2/(9v)$.

For a single slope $s\to\infty$, its uniform layer has $v(s)\to1/2$. The coarea and moment kernels are absolutely integrable with respect to $db$ on $(-\infty,1]$. Their absolute values decay as $O(|b|^{-d})$ at $-\infty$ with $d\ge2$. The layer density is $1/(2s)$, so the coarea and moment both tend to zero. The cap formula then gives $P(s)\to V/2$. A centered law on $[0,\infty]$ with positive finite mass has a centered conditional law on finite slopes, because $M(\infty)=0$. Its corrected average cap is the convex combination of the finite conditional law's corrected cap and $V/2$. A pure infinity law has corrected cap $V/2$.

Multiply the lower bound for $\mu$ by $\overline C_\nu$ and the upper bound for $\nu$ by $\overline C_\mu$, using nonnegative coareas. Subtraction cancels the common $c$ and gives the quotient inequality. The derivative of $-x\log(1+1/x)$ is $-\log(1+1/x)+1/(1+x)<0$ for $x>0$, so $\Gamma_d\le\Gamma_2=4/9$.

The energy kernel is strictly positive on the captured part of every finite layer, so a zero coarea identifies a pure infinity law. Its cap is $V/2$, which gives the quotient when exactly one coarea is zero. For a law on finite slopes, the lower energy bound, valid without centering, and the cap identity give
$|\overline P/V-1/2|\le (p_0+1/2)\overline C/C_0$.
The same estimate extends by averaging with the infinity record. For positive total coarea the quotient is a weighted average of $\overline P_\mu/V$ and $1-\overline P_\nu/V$, so it tends to $1/2$ as both coareas tend to zero.
