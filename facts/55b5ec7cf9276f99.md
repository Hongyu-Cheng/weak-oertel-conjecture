## Statement

Let \(k\ge2\) be an integer, and let \(A,B\) partition \(\{1,\ldots,2k\}\), with \(|A|=|B|=k\). Define
\[
J_k:=\left\{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2k}:
\sum_{i=1}^{2k}n_i=2k,
\quad k-1\le\sum_{i\in A}n_i\le k+1\right\}.
\]
For \(\mathbf j\in J_k\), let \(F_{\mathbf j}\) be the minimal face of \(\operatorname{conv}J_k\) containing \(\mathbf j\), and set \(J_{\mathbf j}:=J_k\cap F_{\mathbf j}\) and \(I:=\{i:j_i>0\}\). Suppose \(\mathbf N\) has a positive log-affine entropy row on this face, meaning that, for positive count weights \(q_i\), \(i\in I\),
\[
\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf n)
=\frac{\prod_{i\in I}q_i^{n_i}}
{\sum_{\mathbf u\in J_{\mathbf j}}\prod_{i\in I}q_i^{u_i}}
\quad(\mathbf n\in J_{\mathbf j}),
\qquad \mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\mathbf N=\mathbf j.
\]
The law is zero off \(J_{\mathbf j}\). The count weights \(q_i\) have exactly the meaning of the composition weights in accepted fact `128175cd66587a67`, not that of any face probability. Such a row is the unique maximum-entropy law with mean \(\mathbf j\).

Then, for every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^{2k}\), with \(Y:=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf N-\mathbf j)\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Y^4
\le 9089024\bigl(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}Y\bigr)^2.
\]
The constant is independent of \(k\), the active block sizes, the count weights, the mean, and the direction, and includes every minimal face and every zero-variance direction.

More specifically, if \(\sum_{i\in A}j_i=k\), put \(S_A:=\sum_{i\in A}N_i\), \(p_s:=\Pr(S_A=s)\), and \(\mu_i(s):=\mathbb E[N_i\mid S_A=s]\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{k-1}=p_{k+1}=\alpha,&\qquad p_k=\beta=1-2\alpha,
&\alpha\ge\frac27,&\qquad \beta\ge\frac13,\\
\mu_i(s)&\ge\frac{j_i}{6}\ge\frac16
&&\left(i\in I,\ s\in\{k-1,k,k+1\}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(\sum_{i\in A}j_i\) is either endpoint \(k-1\) or \(k+1\), the row is a product of two integer-mean complete-composition laws and the sharper constant \(45648\) applies.

## Proof

First identify the face support. The convex hull of \(J_k\) is
\[
\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2k}:
\sum_i x_i=2k,\quad k-1\le\sum_{i\in A}x_i\le k+1\right\}.
\]
Indeed, for a point of this real polytope, write its block totals as \(s\) and \(2k-s\). Both are positive. Keep its normalized proportions within each block and replace \(s\) by the two endpoints \(k-1,k+1\). The original point is a convex combination of these two endpoint points. Each endpoint section is a product of two simplices whose vertices put the entire block total in one coordinate of each block. Those vertices are integer points of \(J_k\). This proves the convex-hull identity.

Consequently, \(F_{\mathbf j}\) is obtained by setting all coordinates outside \(I\) to zero and, only when the mean block total is an endpoint, fixing that block total. Every active \(j_i\) is a positive integer, hence at least one. Each block has at least one active coordinate, because both block totals are at least \(k-1\ge1\). Any probability law of mean \(\mathbf j\) on \(J_k\) is supported on \(J_{\mathbf j}\): mean-zero nonnegative coordinates vanish almost surely, and an endpoint mean of a bounded block total forces that total to equal the endpoint almost surely. On this support, the expectation of \(\log\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is the same for every law of mean \(\mathbf j\), since \(\log\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is affine. Nonnegativity of relative entropy therefore proves the maximum-entropy assertion and its uniqueness.

We next prove the conditional-mean comparison. Use the complete homogeneous partition sums \(h_t(\mathbf q)\) exactly as defined in accepted fact `128175cd66587a67`: they sum \(\prod_iq_i^{n_i}\) over all nonnegative compositions of total \(t\), without multinomial coefficients. This \(h_t\) is not the halfspace-depth function \(h_S\) in the locked problem. Fix a nonempty positive tuple \(\mathbf q\) of length \(u\), and let \(\mu_i^{(t)}\) be the mean of coordinate \(i\) under its complete-composition law of total \(t\ge1\).

The tail identity
\[
\Pr_t(N_i\ge r)=q_i^r\frac{h_{t-r}(\mathbf q)}{h_t(\mathbf q)}
\qquad(1\le r\le t)
\]
follows by subtracting \(r\) units from coordinate \(i\). The log-concavity of \(h_t\), proved in `128175cd66587a67`, says that the positive ratios \(h_{v+1}/h_v\) decrease with \(v\). Thus, for \(1\le r\le t\),
\[
\frac{h_{t+1-r}}{h_{t-r}}\ge\frac{h_{t+1}}{h_t},
\qquad
\Pr_{t+1}(N_i\ge r)\ge\Pr_t(N_i\ge r).
\]
The extra tail at \(r=t+1\) is nonnegative, so summing tails proves \(\mu_i^{(t+1)}\ge\mu_i^{(t)}\).

For the reverse comparison, let \(q_{\max}:=\max_iq_i\). For any positive tuple of length \(v\) and any integer \(t\ge1\), counting each composition once for each of its \(t\) units gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
t h_t(\mathbf q)
&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf n\ge0\\\sum n_i=t-1}}
\mathbf q^{\mathbf n}\sum_iq_i(n_i+1)\\
&\le q_{\max}(t+v-1)h_{t-1}(\mathbf q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Also, adding a unit to a coordinate of maximal weight injects the total-\(t\) compositions into the total-\(t+1\) compositions, so \(h_{t+1}(\mathbf q)\ge q_{\max}h_t(\mathbf q)\).

Let \(\mathbf q^{[i]}\) denote \(\mathbf q\) with one additional copy of \(q_i\). Splitting the \(n_i-1\) remaining units between these two copies in \(n_i\) ways proves
\[
\mu_i^{(t)}
=q_i\frac{h_{t-1}(\mathbf q^{[i]})}{h_t(\mathbf q)}.
\]
The enlarged tuple has length \(u+1\) and the same maximal weight. Combining the last three identities proves the uniform neighboring-total bound
\[
1\le\frac{\mu_i^{(t+1)}}{\mu_i^{(t)}}
=\frac{h_t(\mathbf q^{[i]})}{h_{t-1}(\mathbf q^{[i]})}
\frac{h_t(\mathbf q)}{h_{t+1}(\mathbf q)}
\le\frac{t+u}{t}.
\]
All means and denominators here are positive.

For \(u\le k\), applying this bound at \(t=k-1,k\) yields
\[
\frac{\mu_i^{(k+1)}}{\mu_i^{(k-1)}}
\le\frac{k-1+u}{k-1}\frac{k+u}{k}
\le\frac{2k-1}{k-1}\,2\le6.
\]
Thus all three means at totals \(k-1,k,k+1\) lie between \(\mu_i^{(k-1)}\) and \(6\mu_i^{(k-1)}\). This comparison uses no coupling or independence between different total levels.

Now assume \(\sum_{i\in A}j_i=k\). The face support contains all three levels. Put \(\mathbf q_A:=(q_i)_{i\in A\cap I}\) and \(\mathbf q_B:=(q_i)_{i\in B\cap I}\). Both tuples are nonempty and have length at most \(k\). Conditional on \(S_A=s\), the two active blocks are independent complete-composition laws with totals \(s\) and \(2k-s\), respectively. Their conditional coordinate means therefore satisfy the preceding comparison. Since \(j_i=\sum_s p_s\mu_i(s)\) is a convex combination of these means, it follows that
\[
\min_s\mu_i(s)\ge\frac16\max_s\mu_i(s)\ge\frac{j_i}{6}\ge\frac16
\qquad(i\in I).
\]

We also require a uniform lower bound on the three level masses. For any nonempty positive tuple, take independent unit-rate exponential variables \(G_i\). The multinomial expansion and \(\mathbb E G_i^r=r!\) give
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_iq_iG_i\right)^t=t!h_t(\mathbf q).
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz, applied to the powers \((k-1)/2\) and \((k+1)/2\) of this positive random variable, therefore gives
\[
\frac{h_{k-1}(\mathbf q)h_{k+1}(\mathbf q)}{h_k(\mathbf q)^2}
\ge\frac{k}{k+1}.
\]
The upper bound for this ratio is one by `128175cd66587a67`. The unnormalized level weights are
\(w_s:=h_s(\mathbf q_A)h_{2k-s}(\mathbf q_B)\). Applying both bounds to the two tuples yields
\[
\frac{k}{k+1}
\le\frac{\sqrt{w_{k-1}w_{k+1}}}{w_k}\le1.
\]
The condition \(\mathbb ES_A=k\) implies \(p_{k-1}=p_{k+1}=\alpha\). Writing \(p_k=\beta\), the displayed ratio is \(\alpha/\beta\), so \(2/3\le\alpha/\beta\le1\) and \(2\alpha+\beta=1\). Hence \(\alpha\ge2/7\) and \(\beta\ge1/3\). In particular every \(p_s\ge2/7\). The proof used only nonemptiness of the active tuples, so deleting inactive coordinates causes no change.

For use inside each level, we prove the real-mean extension of the gamma mechanism in accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`. Consider a positive complete-composition law of total \(m\ge1\), with count vector \(\mathbf N\) and real coordinate means \(\mu_i\ge c>0\). The exact alias to that fact's gamma construction is that its active augmented counts \(\mathbf N_I\) are these counts, its active augmented mean \(\mathbf j_I^+\) is \(\boldsymbol\mu\), and its fixed total \(m\) is the present \(m\). Its integer-mean theorem is not being applied to these possibly noninteger means; the extension is proved below.

For any direction \((a_i)\), set \(b_i:=a_i-m^{-1}\sum_h a_h\mu_h\). Then \(\sum_i b_i\mu_i=0\), and the centered projection is \(U:=\sum_i b_i(N_i-\mu_i)\). Given the counts, let \(T_i\) be conditionally independent with densities \(e^{-t_i}t_i^{N_i}/N_i!\) for \(t_i>0\). Integration by parts gives conditional means and variances both equal to \(N_i+1\). The marginal density is
\[
f_{\mathbf T}(\mathbf t)
=\frac{e^{-\sum_i t_i}}{m!h_m(\mathbf q)}
\left(\sum_iq_it_i\right)^m
\mathbf1_{\{t_i>0\ \text{for all }i\}},
\]
which is log-concave. Conditional on \(\mathbf T\), the count law is multinomial with \(m\) trials and probabilities \(W_i:=q_iT_i/\sum_hq_hT_h\). Thus \(m\mathbb EW_i=\mu_i\), and total covariance gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf N)
=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\mu)
-\frac{\boldsymbol\mu\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathsf T}}m
+m(m-1)\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf W).
\]
Evaluating on \(\mathbf b\) proves \(\operatorname{Var}U\ge\sum_i b_i^2\mu_i\), including \(m=1\). No integrality of the means entered this identity.

Put \(\widetilde U:=\sum_i b_i(T_i-\mu_i-1)\). Conditional expectation and total variance now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\widetilde U\mid\mathbf N]&=U,\\
\operatorname{Var}\widetilde U
&=\operatorname{Var}U+\sum_i b_i^2(\mu_i+1)\\
&\le\operatorname{Var}U+(1+1/c)\sum_i b_i^2\mu_i
\le(2+1/c)\operatorname{Var}U.
\end{aligned}
\]
The log-concave marginal integration argument proved in `cfb2a3f2d408f191` applies to the displayed density of \(\mathbf T\), independently of its count means. Its sectional integrals, including after an orthogonal change of coordinates, are finite because the density is bounded on its orthant by a constant times \((1+\|\mathbf t\|)^m e^{-\|\mathbf t\|}\). Hence any nonzero linear projection of \(\mathbf T\), and its translate \(\widetilde U\), has a log-concave density. Jensen's inequality conditional on \(\mathbf N\), followed by the scaled scalar fourth-moment bound of accepted fact `8bb84543f325a261`, proves
\[
\mathbb EU^4\le\mathbb E\widetilde U^4
\le5072\bigl(\operatorname{Var}\widetilde U\bigr)^2
\le5072(2+1/c)^2\bigl(\operatorname{Var}U\bigr)^2.
\]
If \(\operatorname{Var}U=0\), the conclusion follows directly from \(U=0\) almost surely. Otherwise \(\mathbf b\ne0\), so the required scalar density exists. This establishes the real-mean extension, with the same mean-weighted coefficient gauge as in `cfb2a3f2d408f191`.

For independent centered variables satisfying a common fourth-moment bound \(\kappa\ge3\), the two-variable expansion is
\[
\mathbb E(U_1+U_2)^4
=\mathbb EU_1^4+\mathbb EU_2^4
+6\operatorname{Var}U_1\operatorname{Var}U_2
\le\kappa\bigl(\operatorname{Var}U_1+\operatorname{Var}U_2\bigr)^2.
\]
In the present interior case, conditional on any level, both blocks have all their active means at least \(c=1/6\). Apply the real-mean extension and then this independent-block expansion with
\(\kappa:=5072(2+6)^2=324608\). For
\(M_s:=\mathbb E[Y\mid S_A=s]\) and \(v_s:=\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid S_A=s)\), it follows that
\[
\mathbb E[(Y-M_s)^4\mid S_A=s]\le\kappa v_s^2.
\]
Only the two blocks at the same fixed level are taken independent. The gamma variables have been constructed separately inside these complete-composition laws, not for the unconditioned capped-support row.

To combine the three levels, set \(V_{c}:=\sum_s p_s v_s\) and \(V_{m}:=\sum_s p_s M_s^2\). Since \(\mathbb EY=0\), total variance gives \(\operatorname{Var}Y=V_{c}+V_{m}\). For any nonnegative three numbers \(z_s\), the mass bound proves
\[
\sum_s p_s z_s^2
=\sum_s\frac{(p_s z_s)^2}{p_s}
\le\frac72\sum_s(p_s z_s)^2
\le\frac72\left(\sum_s p_s z_s\right)^2.
\]
Apply this first to \(z_s=v_s\) and then to \(z_s=M_s^2\). The pointwise inequality \(|x+y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY^4
&\le8\left(\kappa\sum_s p_s v_s^2+\sum_s p_s M_s^4\right)\\
&\le28\left(\kappa V_{c}^2+V_{m}^2\right)
\le28\kappa(V_{c}+V_{m})^2\\
&=9089024\bigl(\operatorname{Var}Y\bigr)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This explicitly controls the fourth moment of the between-level means by their own variance, with no coordinate-count loss.

Finally, suppose the mean block total is an endpoint. Then \(S_A\) is fixed, and the row factors into two complete-composition laws. Each active block mean is its corresponding subvector of \(\mathbf j\), hence integer. If a block has a single active coordinate its centered projection is zero. Otherwise delete one active coordinate \(i_\star\) as slack. The exact alias to the theorem `cfb2a3f2d408f191` is: its \(r\) is the block's active coordinate count minus one, its \(m\) is this fixed block total, its \(\mathbf X\) and \(\mathbf j\) are the remaining counts and their means, and its augmented counts and mean \(\mathbf j^+\) restore the entire active block. The theorem's direction coefficients are \((a_i-a_{i_\star})_{i\ne i_\star}\), which give exactly the present centered block projection. The positive composition law is its maximum-entropy law by the same log-affine relative-entropy argument used above. Therefore that accepted theorem gives the block constant \(45648\). The independent-block expansion preserves this constant for their sum. It is smaller than \(9089024\), completing all endpoint, face, and zero-variance cases.
