## Statement

In the visible-facet-star setting, fix finitely many groups of original closed ambient caps $H_j=\{\ell_j\ge0\}$, with a single affine margin for each original cap even when it occurs in several groups. The caps in each group $i$ pass through one point $(p_i,y_i)$ on an actual fiber and all original cap masses equal $Q$. In each group choose $\rho_{ij}>0$ with $\sum_{j\in i}\rho_{ij}v_j=0$, where $v_j\ne0$ is the continuous normal of $\ell_j$. Suppose each old threshold tail $s\mapsto M(C\cap\{\ell_j\ge s\})$ is continuously differentiable near zero with derivative $-S_j<0$ at zero, where $S_j$ is the summed old section measure divided by $\|v_j\|$. Assume $\nu(\{\ell_j=0\})=0$ for every cap. Put $B_j=\int\mathbf1_{H_j}\,d\nu$, $\beta=Q/M(C)$ and
$$
\theta_{ij}=\frac{\rho_{ij}/S_j}{\sum_{h\in i}\rho_{ih}/S_h},
\qquad W_i=\sum_{j\in i}\theta_{ij}\mathbf1_{H_j}.
$$
For each group there is a unique nearby common mass $\tau_i(\epsilon)$ with threshold shifts $s_{ij}$ having weighted sum zero. Those thresholds give the old whole-plane cover on each base where its old weighted margin is nonnegative. They satisfy
$$
\tau_i'(0+)=\sum_{j\in i}\theta_{ij}B_j,\qquad
\tau_i'(0+)-\beta M'(0+)=\int(W_i-\beta)\,d\nu .
$$
For the fixed original cap normals and the constraints $\sum_{j\in i}\rho_{ij}s_j\le0$ for all groups, the smallest nearby common upper mass budget with a single shift per original cap is $\max_i\tau_i(\epsilon)$. Here the constraints mean the old weighted margin on each base is preserved or increased. If every weighted shift sum must instead equal zero and every group must keep its own equal-mass budget, the separate group roots are realizable by single original thresholds exactly when their masses agree across each shared original label.

If the original groups together with fixed strictly slack whole-plane covers elsewhere cover all actual fiber planes and the old global maximum raw depth equals $Q$, negativity of all displayed normalized response numerators strictly decreases the actual maximum normalized depth for small positive $\epsilon$.

## Proof

For each cap let $F_j(\epsilon,s)=M(C_\epsilon\cap\{\ell_j\ge s\})$. Thresholds near zero have continuous tails because the continuous normal is nonzero and section planes have zero $d$-volume. The old threshold slopes are strictly negative on a common neighborhood, and nesting $C\subseteq C_\epsilon$ means each tail's slab decrease is at least the corresponding old slab decrease. Hence the perturbed tails are strictly decreasing there. By the star mass formula they differ from the old tails uniformly by $O(\epsilon)$. Their inverses $s_j(\epsilon,q)$ exist on a common mass neighborhood of $Q$, are continuous and strictly decreasing in $q$, and converge at fixed nearby $q$ to their old inverses by the strict tail bounds. Thus each strictly decreasing weighted inverse sum crosses zero uniquely near $Q$ for small $\epsilon$, by bracketing on both sides of $Q$.

For bounded $s/\epsilon$, the same star integral as for a fixed cap gives added mass $\epsilon B_j+o(\epsilon)$ uniformly. Indeed, the only differing indicators are within a band $|\ell_j(w)|\le C_0\epsilon$ on the old boundary for a uniform constant $C_0$, using $0\le s_{\rm roof}\le1$ in each pyramid parametrization. The $\nu$-mass of that band tends to zero by the null coincidence hypothesis. The old tail has expansion $Q-S_js+o(|s|)$. It follows by bracketing the strictly decreasing tail that
$$
s_j(\epsilon,Q+\epsilon q)=\epsilon(B_j-q)/S_j+o(\epsilon)
$$
for bounded $q$, uniformly on compact ranges. The weighted sums at any two fixed $q$ on opposite sides of $\sum_j\theta_{ij}B_j$ have opposite signs for small $\epsilon$, proving the derivative of each root. The normalized response follows from $M'(0+)=\nu(\mathrm{total})$. The weighted margin is constant in the continuous coordinates on each base, and is unchanged after shifts with sum zero, so at least one closed cap contains each point on its original covered plane.

For a common proposed budget $q$, a fixed normal requires shift at least $s_j(\epsilon,q)$ for its mass to be at most $q$. This follows from the strict local decrease and global monotonicity, even if an admissible shift lies beyond the inverse interval. All circuit weights are positive. If $q<\max_i\tau_i(\epsilon)$, one of the weighted sums of inverse thresholds is positive and every shift vector meeting that budget violates that group's margin constraint. At $q=\max_i\tau_i(\epsilon)$ each weighted inverse sum is nonpositive. The shifts $s_j(\epsilon,q)$ thus give one cap per original label, and all its uses have the same inequality. Positive rescalings of an original margin merely rescale its shift and its balancing coefficient inversely, so the conclusion uses the same oriented original halfspace in every group. If group weighted sums must instead be exactly zero with equal cap mass in each group, strict inverse monotonicity forces the group root. On a shared original label the two shifts coincide if and only if the root masses coincide. Agreement for every shared label also suffices by assigning these forced shifts.

When all response numerators are negative, finiteness gives $\max_i\tau_i(\epsilon)<\beta M(C_\epsilon)$ for small positive $\epsilon$. The original margin constraints preserve whole-plane coverage. Any fixed slack covers elsewhere keep slack by continuity of their masses and total mass, with unchanged fiber bases. Thus every mixed point lies in a cap of normalized mass strictly below the old maximum, with a uniform bound given by the maximum of finitely many cap masses.
