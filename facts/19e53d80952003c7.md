## Statement

On positive actual planar fibers let $W=\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf1_{H_j}$ be a finite profile with positive weights summing to one, and assume each cap cuts an anchor fiber properly. The positive part of cap $j$'s affine margin is a continuous nonnegative convex test whose marked boundary efficiency for $W$ is at least $\theta_j$. In particular a weight at least $4/9$ pays the planar target, as does some cap of every two-row profile. If all three weights of a three-row profile are below $4/9$, then each is above $1/9$. If an exposed point of the convex hull of the finite positive-fiber union lies strictly within two caps, one exposing hinge pays at least the sum of their weights, which exceeds $5/9$ in that three-row case.

## Proof

A proper cut has an open boundary patch on the positive side. To see positivity of its surface measure, use a radial parametrization from an interior point of the fiber in the cutting plane. Directions in the positive open hemisphere give positive margins, and projection back to the sphere is Lipschitz on the boundary away from that interior point. The open image has positive measure, so the corresponding boundary patch does also. Thus the margin hinge has positive denominator and is supported where $W\ge\theta_j$. This gives the first result. Two weights summing to one have a weight at least $1/2$. For three weights, each pair has sum less than $8/9$, so the remaining one exceeds $1/9$. Each pair also has sum greater than $5/9$ by the upper bound on the remaining weight.

An exposed point $w$ of the finite compact-fiber hull is in one of its measured fibers, since any convex representation of its unique maximizing value uses only maximizers of the exposing affine function. In that fiber $w$ is a boundary point: a function exposing only one point is nonconstant on its full-dimensional continuous fiber. If $w$ lies in the strict double intersection, compactness places a sufficiently shallow positive level set of its exposing function inside that intersection. On its own fiber this neighborhood contains a relatively open boundary patch of positive surface measure, again by radial parametrization from an inball center. The positive part of that shifted affine function is thus a convex test of positive denominator, supported where both caps are present. Its efficiency is at least their weight sum.
