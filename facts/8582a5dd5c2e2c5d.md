## Statement

Let $P=\{m,m+1,\ldots,M\}\subset\mathbb Z$ be nonempty and let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of volume $V>0$ and centroid $g$, with $d\ge1$. Assume $K$ has a finite volume-preserving affine symmetry group whose average orbit is $g$, as for a simplex. Use the sum of fiber volumes on $P\times K$ and let $Q$ be the maximum raw closed-halfspace depth on that set.

Fix an actual deepest point $(p,y)$ and a prescribed finite list of caps $H_j=\{a_j(z-p)+u_j\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$ of mass $Q$, with $u_j\ne0$ and positive $\rho_j$ satisfying $\sum_j\rho_ju_j=0$. For each cap assume differentiability in the full hyperplane parameters with the usual mixed coarea derivative. If there is a deepest point at a different base, or if $P$ is a singleton, there is one exposed endpoint $e$ and one continuous nonnegative potential $\Phi(n,z,x)$, convex in $(z,x)$ for each fixed unit $n$, supported on the actual boundary bundle over $e$. With $\eta$ the sum of boundary $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ lifted by the outward continuous unit normal, its integral is $dV$ and
$\int\Phi\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf 1_{H_j}\,d\eta\ge dV(d/(d+1))^d$ for every $\theta_j\ge0$ with $\sum_j\theta_j=1$. In particular the conclusion holds if the deepest bases meet every parity class occupied by $P$.

More generally, in any finite base dimension with a common fiber, the same localization conclusion holds for any finite list of nonvertical caps through $(p,g)$ if an exposed base $e$ satisfies $a_j\cdot(e-p)\ge0$ for every row. In that implication the list need not be critical or balanced.

## Proof

A deepest point lies in the interior of its own common fiber: an ordinary supporting cap at a boundary point has zero mass in every fiber, whereas a ball centered at any interior point in its own fiber gives positive volume in every halfspace through that point. Thus each listed cap has a proper own trace and strictly positive total coarea. For a cap through $Y=(p,y)$ with full normal $A=(a,u)$, let $\sigma$ be the sum of its trace $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measures divided by $\lVert u\rVert$, with total $S>0$ and centroid $B$. The assumed derivative of cap mass under $(A,b)\mapsto(A+\tau W,b+\tau c)$ is $S(W\cdot B-c)$. Through $Y$, take $c=W\cdot Y$. Every such cap has mass at least $Q$, and the two-sided derivative at zero vanishes. This gives $B=Y$. If another deepest mark $Y'$ is strictly in the cap, increasing its threshold a little retains $Y'$ and loses positive mass from the proper own trace. If $Y'\ne Y$ lies on the cap boundary, use $c=W\cdot Y'$ and $W=Y'-Y$. The derivative is then $-S\lVert Y'-Y\rVert^2<0$, contradicting deepestness. Thus every other deepest mark is strictly outside each prescribed cap.

The ambient depth superlevels are convex since a halfspace containing a convex combination contains at least one summand. Affine symmetries preserve every ambient cap mass and act only on $K$. Hence orbit averaging shows that $(q,g)$ is deepest at every deepest base $q$. On base $p$, a distinct deepest mark would have $u_j\cdot(x-y)<0$ for each $j$, contradicting their positive balance. Therefore $y=g$. If $q\ne p$ is another deepest base, strict exclusion of $(q,g)$ gives $a_j(q-p)<0$ for all $j$. For $q>p$ choose $e=m$, and for $q<p$ choose $e=M$. Then $a_j(e-p)\ge0$ for all $j$. For the singleton take $e=p$, giving the same weak inequalities.

In fact the following localization works for any finite base with a common fiber and any exposed base $e$ with $a_j\cdot(e-p)\ge0$ for caps through $(p,g)$. Choose an affine exposer $\ell$ with $\ell(e)=0$ and $\ell<0$ on the remaining bases. In the singleton case take $\ell=0$. Choose $B_0\ge0$ so $-B_0\ell(z)\ge\max_{x\in K}\lVert x-g\rVert$ at each other base. Then $\Phi(n,z,x)=[n\cdot(x-g)+B_0\ell(z)]_+$ has the stated continuity, convexity and support. On $\partial K$ at $e$ it equals the nonnegative radial flux density $n(x)\cdot(x-g)$. Its integral there is $dV$ by Gauss–Green applied to the vector field $x-g$. The actual $j$th trace over $e$ contains the through-centroid cap $K\cap\{u_j\cdot(x-g)\ge0\}$. Apply Gauss–Green to this proper cut. Its cut plane has zero radial flux, so the part of its flux on $\partial K$ is $d$ times its volume. The cut is through the interior, so the intersection of its cut plane with $\partial K$ has zero $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measure. In $d=1$ the two divergence identities are the corresponding endpoint identities. By 6769574fc1bd9f8d with retained mass $m=V$ and density one, the through-centroid volume is at least $V(d/(d+1))^d$. Nonnegativity of radial flux gives at least that payment times $d$ for every actual row, and summing with the given weights proves the result. A nonsingleton integer interval occupies both parities, so meeting both supplies a second deepest base.
