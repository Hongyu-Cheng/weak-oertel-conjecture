## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for every
real base point \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
C_u:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(u,y)\in C\},
\qquad
a(u):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_u).
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), abbreviate \(a_z:=a(z)\), and put
\[
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\]
For \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in H\},
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\]

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), let
\[
\mathsf M_t(K):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_K y f(y)\,dy:
f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},
\int_K f\,dy=t
\right\},
\]
and put
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

Let \(E\subseteq D_+\) be finite and nonempty, let
\[
m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E,
\]
and define
\[
\Lambda_m(E):=
\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\quad
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z z=m
\right\}.
\]
Suppose that there is an affine function
\(\ell:\operatorname{aff}E\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
0<\ell(z),
\qquad
t_z:=\ell(z)^d\leq a_z
\qquad(z\in E),
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\ell(m)^d=a_m.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(a_m>0\). Writing
\[
c_m:=\frac1{a_m}\int_{C_m}y\,dy,
\]
one has, for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), the exact equality
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
=\mathsf M_{a_m}(C_m)
=\{c_m\}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\) and every simultaneous
choice
\[
q_z\in\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in E),
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zq_z=c_m.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, every representation-dependent sum and their complete
barycentric Minkowski core coincide:
\[
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
=\{c_m\}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the weighted base depth at \(m\) by
\[
b_m(t):=
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z.
\tag{6}
\]
Then the same target point satisfies the pointwise mixed-depth estimate
\[
\boxed{
h_S(m,c_m)\geq\gamma_d b_m(t).
}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently, if \(T:=\sum_{z\in E}t_z\) and
\(b_m(t)\geq\alpha T\) for some \(0\leq\alpha\leq1\), then
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq h_S(m,c_m)
\geq\gamma_d\alpha T.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, weighted lattice depth \(2^{-n}\) gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z.
\tag{9}
\]

As a bounded affine-root-complexity corollary, fix \(K\geq1\), assume
\(S\neq\varnothing\), and assume that, when \(W>0\), the positive
lattice-fiber set has a partition into \(K\) nonempty cells
\[
D_+=E_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup E_K
\tag{10}
\]
such that for every \(j\) there is an affine function
\(\ell_j:\operatorname{aff}E_j\to\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
a(u)^{1/d}=\ell_j(u)
\qquad\bigl(u\in\operatorname{conv}E_j\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{K}\,2^{-n}W
\geq\frac1{eK}\,2^{-n}W.
}
\tag{12}
\]
If \(W=0\), the same inequality is automatic for every point of \(S\). Thus,
for a class in which \(K\leq K_0\) uniformly, the locked weak Oertel
statement holds with any fixed \(c_0\in(0,1/(eK_0))\).

## Proof

Because \(m\in\operatorname{conv}E\), the polytope
\(\Lambda_m(E)\) is nonempty. Fix any
\(\lambda^0\in\Lambda_m(E)\). Affineness on the possibly
lower-dimensional space \(\operatorname{aff}E\) gives
\[
\ell(m)=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z^0\ell(z)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Together with (2), this proves \(a_m>0\). Thus \(C_m\) is a
\(d\)-dimensional convex body and its centroid \(c_m\) is defined. Moreover,
(1) says that every source mass is strictly positive and does not exceed its
fiber capacity. Hence each source metronoid in (3) is defined and nonempty.
No assertion concerning \(\mathsf M_0\) is needed.

Fix arbitrary \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\) and arbitrary points
\[
q_z\in\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in E).
\tag{14}
\]
By (1),
\[
(z,q_z,\ell(z))
\in
G:=\{(u,q,s):s>0,\ q\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_u)\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e proves that the positive-level set \(G\)
itself is convex, before taking the closure that appears in that fact's
statement. Applying this convexity finitely many times gives
\[
\left(
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z z,
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zq_z,
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z\ell(z)
\right)
\in G.
\tag{16}
\]
The first coordinate is \(m\), and affineness of \(\ell\) on
\(\operatorname{aff}E\) makes the last coordinate \(\ell(m)\), independently
of which barycentric representation \(\lambda\) was chosen. Therefore (2)
and (16) imply
\[
\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zq_z
\in\mathsf M_{\ell(m)^d}(C_m)
=\mathsf M_{a_m}(C_m).
\tag{17}
\]

The last metronoid is the full-mass metronoid of \(C_m\), so it is exactly
the centroid singleton. Indeed, if \(f\) is admissible for
\(\mathsf M_{a_m}(C_m)\), then
\[
0\leq1-f\quad\text{a.e. on }C_m,
\qquad
\int_{C_m}(1-f)\,dy=a_m-a_m=0.
\]
Thus \(f=1\) almost everywhere and its normalized first moment is \(c_m\).
Conversely, \(f=1\) realizes \(c_m\). This also agrees with the full-mass
case of accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d. Hence (17) proves (4).

Every source metronoid in (14) is nonempty, so every Minkowski sum in (3)
is nonempty. Since every one of its points is \(c_m\), that sum equals
\(\{c_m\}\). This proves (3), and intersecting the identical nonempty
singletons proves (5). Notice that this proves equality for each
representation, rather than merely finding one point in their intersection.
Moreover, accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives
\(\mathsf M_{a_m}(C_m)\subseteq C_m\), so \(c_m\in C_m\) and
\((m,c_m)\in S\).

Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d also says that each
\(\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(C_z\) and
that every one of its points has absolute fiber depth at least
\(\gamma_dt_z\). Apply the general barycentric-core depth conclusion (3) of
accepted fact ae0161dd5d4ea672 with
\[
D_z:=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\qquad
w_z:=\gamma_dt_z,
\qquad
x:=c_m.
\]
The required core membership is (5). That accepted conclusion gives
\[
h_S(m,c_m)
\geq
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\m\in J}}
\sum_{z\in E\cap J}\gamma_dt_z
=\gamma_db_m(t),
\]
which is (7). Equations (8) and (9) follow immediately. This use of the
accepted core theorem includes vertical ambient halfspaces, nonvertical
ambient halfspaces, boundary-containing halfspaces, and whole-space
conventions; no additional boundary or general-position assumption is being
made here.

We prove (12). The case \(W=0\) was already disposed of, so assume \(W>0\).
Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes \(D_+\) finite. For each
cell in (10), put
\[
W_j:=\sum_{z\in E_j}a_z>0
\]
and apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c, with continuous dimension zero,
to the finite measure
\[
\nu_j:=\sum_{z\in E_j}a_z\delta_z
\]
supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\). It gives \(m_j\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every
proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m_j\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in E_j\cap J}a_z\geq2^{-n}W_j.
\tag{18}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
m_j\in\operatorname{conv}E_j.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, otherwise strict separation of \(m_j\) from the compact polytope
\(\operatorname{conv}E_j\) would give a proper closed halfspace containing
\(m_j\) and no atom of \(\nu_j\), contradicting (18).

By (11), the function \(\ell_j\) is positive at every point of \(E_j\),
satisfies
\[
\ell_j(z)^d=a_z\qquad(z\in E_j),
\]
and, using (19), touches the target fiber root:
\[
\ell_j(m_j)^d=a(m_j).
\]
Thus (1)-(2) hold on \(E_j\) with \(t_z=a_z\). Equations (9) and (18) give
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d2^{-n}W_j
\qquad(1\leq j\leq K).
\tag{20}
\]
Choose \(j\) with \(W_j\geq W/K\), which is possible because the cells
partition all positive lattice mass. This proves the first inequality in
(12). Finally,
\[
\gamma_d
=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}
>e^{-1},
\]
so the second inequality follows.

For completeness, the previously considered upper-volume and
minimum-volume formulations are literal specializations, not additional
hypotheses or weaker conclusions. If \(0<a_m\leq a_z\) for every \(z\in E\),
take the constant affine function
\(\ell\equiv a_m^{1/d}\); then (3) applies at the common submass \(a_m\).
This covers an integral minimum-volume target in any upper-volume level set.
Likewise, if an affine function \(L\) majorizes the concave root profile
\(a(\cdot)^{1/d}\) on \(\operatorname{conv}E\) and agrees with it on \(E\),
then for every \(m\in\operatorname{conv}E\), concavity and any barycentric
representation give
\[
a(m)^{1/d}
\geq\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z a_z^{1/d}
=L(m)
\geq a(m)^{1/d}.
\]
Thus equality holds at \(m\), and the same theorem applies with
\(\ell=L\) and the full source masses \(t_z=a_z\). This is the affine
root-face case.

All zero-volume lattice fibers are excluded from \(D_+\) and contribute zero
to \(W\). Every metronoid used above has strictly positive mass, and the only
zero-total-mass case is handled before any metronoid or weighted centerpoint
is invoked. This completes the proof.
