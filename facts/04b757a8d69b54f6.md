## Statement

On $C=[0,1]\times[-1,1]$ with raw mixed length, the maximum closed-halfspace depth over $C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$ is $Q=1$, attained at both $Y_0=(0,0)$ and $Y_1=(1,0)$. The two cap pairs $\pm x-2z\ge0$ and $\pm x+2(z-1)\ge0$ consist of proper actual mass-$Q$ caps through their respective maximizing marks. Their groups have continuous rank one and cover the full fiber planes on $z\le0$ and $z\ge1$, respectively. The total mixed mass is $4=4Q$ and the outward mean base coefficients are $-2$ and $2$.

## Proof

Only bases 0 and 1 occur; each has length two. Any containing closed cap at a midpoint cuts at least half of its own interval, including the case of a pure-base cap. Each displayed cap cuts exactly one source half-interval, and misses the other fiber because its margin is at most $1-2<0$ there. Thus both marks attain depth one. At any other point of either interval, a cap through it in the smaller own direction has own length less than one. Add a sufficiently large finite base tilt pointing away from the other base; it leaves that own cut unchanged and excludes the compact other interval. Thus there is no greater depth anywhere on the mixed source, and outside $C$ strict separation gives zero depth.

Within each pair the continuous normals are $+1,-1$ and balance with weights $1/2,1/2$. The weighted margins are $-2z$ and $2(z-1)$, respectively. Wherever a weighted margin is nonnegative one cap has nonnegative margin, giving the stated whole-plane cover and its count equality. The base coefficient of the weighted margin is the indicated outward normal at the corresponding endpoint. In either group the two full normals have nonzero determinant, so ambient rank is two even though continuous rank is one.
