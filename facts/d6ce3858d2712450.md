## Statement

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a nondegenerate triangle of area $V$, and let $y\in T$ have barycentric coordinates $A\ge B\ge C$. Write $f_T(y)$ for the infimum of $|T\cap H|$ over closed halfplanes $H$ containing $y$, where $|\cdot|$ denotes area. For $u\ne0$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, set
$$
P_T(y;u,t)=|T\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge u\cdot y+t\}|+
           |T\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge u\cdot y-t\}|.
$$
Then $P_T(y;u,t)\ge 8BCV$ for every $u\ne0$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, and
$$
\min_{u\ne0,\ t\in\mathbb R}P_T(y;u,t)=8BCV=2f_T(y).
$$
The minimum is attained at shift zero, including when $y\in\partial T$.

## Proof

Translate $y$ to the origin and apply Part 1, (2)–(3), of accepted fact 431423848c9bacf8 to this translated triangle, with $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(C,B,A)$. It gives $f_T(y)=4BCV$ for every nondegenerate triangle, including a point on its boundary. Moving a containing halfplane boundary to $y$ only decreases its cap area. For an interior point a minimizing through-$y$ cap is the corner cap from a vertex of coordinate $A$ with edge fractions $2B$ and $2C$, as in that fact. At a boundary point a supporting through-$y$ cap has zero area. Choosing either minimizing cap twice at shift zero gives the upper bound and attainment. On the boundary, $C=0$ and the lower bound follows from nonnegativity. Henceforth $y$ is interior. Opposite through-$y$ halfplanes have cap areas summing to $V$ because their common line is null, so $2f_T(y)/V\le1$.

Fix a nonzero normal. Its vertex heights have positive range. Subtract their minimum, divide by their range, and divide the shift by the same range. In height order the normalized vertex heights are $(0,a,1)$, with $0\le a\le1$. Write $(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)$ for the barycentric coordinates in this vertex order and $m=a\lambda_1+\lambda_2$ for the height of $y$. For $0<a<1$, the normalized upper tail of area is
$$
F_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
1 & x\le0,\\
1-x^2/a & 0\le x\le a,\\
(1-x)^2/(1-a) & a\le x\le1,\\
0 & x\ge1.
\end{cases}
$$
For $x$ below the middle height and above zero, the excluded corner has edge fractions $x/a$ and $x$. For $x$ above the middle height and below one, the retained corner has edge fractions $(1-x)/(1-a)$ and $1-x$. The two proper formulas have the same value and derivative at $a$, and the derivatives vanish at the adjacent support endpoints. Thus $F_a$ is continuously differentiable, with $F_a'(x)<0$ for $0<x<1$.

The normalized paired cost is $F_a(m-t)+F_a(m+t)$, where $t$ now denotes the normalized shift. The terms interchange under $t\mapsto-t$, and the sum equals one for $t\ge\max(m,1-m)$. Consequently it attains a minimum on $[0,\infty)$. At zero shift its value is at least $2f_T(y)/V$. At any positive minimizing shift the derivative $F_a'(m+t)-F_a'(m-t)$ vanishes. If either threshold is outside $(0,1)$, the derivative condition forces both to be outside $(0,1)$. Since $0<m<1$, one cap is then full and one is null. If the proper thresholds both lie in $[0,a]$ or both in $[a,1]$, the derivative is respectively $-4t/a$ or $4t/(1-a)$, including when a threshold equals $a$. This leaves only proper thresholds strictly straddling $a$.

In that regime the cost is $1-(m-t)^2/a+(1-m-t)^2/(1-a)$. It is strictly concave in $t$ for $a<1/2$. At $a=1/2$ it is affine and has zero slope only when $m=1/2$, in which case its value is one. For $a>1/2$ the stationary shift is $t_*=(a-m)/(2a-1)$. Write $q=(2m-1)/(2a-1)$. At this shift the two thresholds are $a q$ and $1-(1-a)q$. They satisfy $0\le m-t_*\le a\le m+t_*\le1$ and $t_*\ge0$ exactly when $0\le q\le1$, or $1/2\le m\le a$. The corresponding value is
$$
J=1-(2a-1)q^2=1-\frac{(2m-1)^2}{2a-1}.
$$

For the fixed vertex order let $v=2a-1$, $b=\lambda_1$, and $c=\lambda_2-\lambda_0$. Then $2m-1=bv+c$ and
$$
1-J=b^2v+2bc+\frac{c^2}{v}.
$$
This is convex for $v>0$, and the conditions $0<v\le1$ and $0\le bv+c\le v$ give the interval from $v_0=\max\{0,-c/b,c/(1-b)\}$ to $1$, when nonempty, with zero initially excluded. Here $0<b<1$. If $v_0=0$ then $c=0$ and the formula extends continuously to $v=0$. The concave function $J$ is therefore bounded below by the lesser of its two endpoint values.

At the left endpoint $J=1$ for $c<0$ because $bv_0+c=0$, and the same value holds by continuity for $c=0$. For $c>0$, positivity of $\lambda_0$ gives $v_0=c/(1-b)<1$. There $m=a$ and the value $1-v_0$ is $2F_a(a)$ at zero shift. Those heights define an affine functional on $T$ with nonzero linear part, so the corresponding through-$y$ cap gives $2F_a(a)\ge2f_T(y)/V$. At the right endpoint the formula gives
$$
J=1-(1-2\lambda_0)^2=4\lambda_0(1-\lambda_0)\ge4C(1-C)\ge8BC.
$$
The first inequality holds because $\lambda_0\in[C,1-C]$ and $x(1-x)$ has its minimum on this interval at either endpoint. The second follows from $1-C=A+B\ge2B$. Thus every possible shift minimum costs at least $8BC$ in units of $V$.

For $a=0$ or $a=1$, use the affine functionals with vertex heights $(0,a',1)$ in the chosen order, with $0<a'<1$ tending to $a$. The functionals converge since their vertex values do, and each limiting level set is a line because the height range remains one. Keep the normalized shift fixed. The two cap indicators converge outside their null limiting boundary lines, so their areas converge by dominated convergence. This proves the same lower bound for repeated vertex heights and completes the proof.

For the nonzero-shift example, take $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and $y=(2/5,2/5)$. The barycentric coordinates are $(1/5,2/5,2/5)$, giving $f_T(y)=8V/25$ and paired minimum $16V/25$. With $u=(1,1)$ and $t=1/5$, the two thresholds are $1$ and $3/5$. The first cap has zero area and the second has area $[1-(3/5)^2]V=16V/25$, by the excluded corner area. In that same direction the zero-shift cost is $2[1-(4/5)^2]V=18V/25$. Two barycentric coordinates exceed $1/3$, so $y$ is outside the three vertex-to-centroid segments, on each of which the two coordinates corresponding to the other vertices are at most $1/3$.
