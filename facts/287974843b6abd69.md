## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, put
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\qquad
W:=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0,
\]
where \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), with empty fibers assigned volume zero. For \((z,x)\in C\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
\delta_C(z,x):=
\inf\bigl\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap L):
L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in L
\bigr\}.
\]
For an affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), set
\[
q_s(z):=
\begin{cases}
\delta_C(z,s(z)),&(z,s(z))\in C,\\
0,&(z,s(z))\notin C,
\end{cases}
\qquad
Q_C(s):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}q_s(z).
\]
If \(Q_C(s)>0\), then there is \(z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that \((z^\star,s(z^\star))\in C\) and
\[
h_S(z^\star,s(z^\star))\geq 2^{-n}Q_C(s).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)
\geq 2^{-n}\sup_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \operatorname{affine}}Q_C(s).
\]

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), let
\[
M(z):=
\begin{cases}
\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x),&C_z\neq\varnothing,\\
0,&C_z=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\]
When \(n=1\), one moreover has
\[
\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)
\geq \frac18\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}M(z)
\geq \frac{\gamma_d}{8}W,
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

## Proof

Fix an affine map \(s\) with \(Q_C(s)>0\), and define the finite positive-depth hit set
\[
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:q_s(z)>0\}.
\]
The set is finite because every member lies in the bounded projection of \(C\), and it is nonempty because \(Q_C(s)>0\). Give \(z\in E\) weight \(q_s(z)\). The weighted lattice-centerpoint argument proved in accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8` applies to every finite set of positive real weights. It yields
\[
z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E)
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z^\star\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q}q_s(z)\geq 2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}q_s(z)
=2^{-n}Q_C(s).
\tag{1}
\]

The graph-feasibility set
\[
P_s:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:(z,s(z))\in C\}
\]
is convex, since it is the inverse image of the convex set \(C\) under the affine graph map \(z\mapsto(z,s(z))\). Every point of \(E\) belongs to \(P_s\), so
\[
z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\subseteq P_s.
\]
Thus \(p^\star:=(z^\star,s(z^\star))\) belongs to \(S\). Notice that \(z^\star\) need not itself lie in \(E\): it is enough that it lies in the graph-feasibility set, and its own fiber depth is allowed to be zero.

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be an arbitrary closed halfspace containing \(p^\star\), and let
\[
Q_H:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:(z,s(z))\in H\}
\]
be its pullback to the affine graph. The pullback is a closed affine halfspace containing \(z^\star\). If its defining affine functional is nonconstant, then \(Q_H\) is a proper closed halfspace, and (1) applies. If that functional is constant, containment of \(z^\star\) forces \(Q_H=\mathbb R^n\), and then
\[
\sum_{z\in E\cap Q_H}q_s(z)=Q_C(s)\geq2^{-n}Q_C(s).
\tag{2}
\]
This includes the case in which a proper ambient halfspace contains the entire affine graph, as well as the harmless whole ambient space if it is admitted as a halfspace.

For each \(z\in E\cap Q_H\), the point \(s(z)\) belongs to both \(C_z\) and the fiber section
\[
H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\}.
\]
If the continuous-coordinate normal of \(H\) is nonzero, then \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(s(z)\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\geq\delta_C(z,s(z))=q_s(z).
\tag{3}
\]
If the continuous-coordinate normal is zero, then \(z\in Q_H\) makes \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\); in that whole-fiber case (3) again holds because \(q_s(z)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\). An empty fiber section can occur only for \(z\notin Q_H\), so it is irrelevant to this sum. Feasible graph hits of depth zero were excluded from \(E\) and contribute zero to both the objective and the required lower bound.

Summing (3), and using either (1) or (2), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\\
&\geq\sum_{z\in E\cap Q_H}q_s(z)\\
&\geq2^{-n}Q_C(s).
\end{aligned}
\]
This holds for every containing ambient closed halfspace \(H\), without moving its boundary. Hence
\[
h_S(p^\star)\geq2^{-n}Q_C(s),
\]
which proves the first assertion.

For completeness, the maximum of \(h_S\) over \(S\) exists. Indeed, \(S\) is a finite union of compact fibers and is compact, and its mixed measure is finite. For any \(\alpha\), if \(h_S(p)<\alpha\), choose a proper closed halfspace \(H\ni p\) of mixed measure less than \(\alpha\). Translate its boundary outward by \(\varepsilon>0\). The enlarged halfspaces decrease to \(H\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), so continuity from above of the finite mixed measure gives a sufficiently small enlargement of measure still below \(\alpha\), with \(p\) in its interior. The same halfspace then witnesses \(h_S(p')<\alpha\) for all nearby \(p'\). Thus \(h_S\) is upper semicontinuous on compact \(S\) and attains a maximum.

If \(Q_C(s)=0\), the inequality for that particular \(s\) is automatic because depths are nonnegative. If some affine map has positive objective, the first assertion bounds the attained maximum for each such map. Taking the supremum over them gives
\[
\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)\geq2^{-n}\sup_s Q_C(s).
\]
If all affine objectives vanish, the right-hand side is zero and the same conclusion is again automatic. Also \(Q_C(s)\leq W\) for every \(s\), so the supremum is finite.

It remains to prove the folded assertion for \(n=1\). Let
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R}(C),
\qquad
\rho(u):=\left(\max_{x\in C_u}\delta_C(u,x)\right)^{1/d}
\quad(u\in P).
\]
Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` says that the maximum is attained for every \(u\), that \(\rho\) is concave and upper semicontinuous on the compact interval \(P\), and that
\[
f(u,x):=\delta_C(u,x)^{1/d}
\]
is jointly concave on \(C\). On integer points of \(P\), one has \(\rho(z)^d=M(z)\). Accepted fact `4bd2e6d22487dfef` gives
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}M(z)\geq\gamma_dW>0.
\tag{4}
\]

Apply accepted fact `62f2ec156b347dfe` with integer dimension one, the interval \(P\), and the concave function \(\rho\). There exists \(t>0\) such that, writing
\[
D_t:=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z:\rho(z)\geq t\},
\qquad N(t):=\#D_t,
\]
one has
\[
t^dN(t)\geq\frac14\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\rho(z)^d
=\frac14\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}M(z).
\tag{5}
\]
The factors here are exactly \(2^{-1-1}=1/4\). The real superlevel set
\[
I_t:=\{u\in P:\rho(u)\geq t\}
\]
is a compact interval: convexity follows from concavity of \(\rho\), and closedness follows from its upper semicontinuity. Therefore \(D_t=I_t\cap\mathbb Z\) is a nonempty consecutive interval of lattice indices. Let
\[
a:=\min D_t,
\qquad b:=\max D_t.
\]

Suppose first that \(a<b\). By attainment in accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, choose \(x_a\in C_a\) and \(x_b\in C_b\) with
\[
f(a,x_a)=\rho(a)\geq t,
\qquad
f(b,x_b)=\rho(b)\geq t.
\]
Let \(s:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^d\) be the affine map interpolating these two endpoint maximizers. For every integer \(z\in D_t\), write \(z=(1-\lambda)a+\lambda b\) with \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\). Convexity of \(C\) puts
\[
(z,s(z))=(1-\lambda)(a,x_a)+\lambda(b,x_b)
\]
in \(C\), while joint concavity from `4e868f774de77608` gives
\[
f(z,s(z))
\geq(1-\lambda)f(a,x_a)+\lambda f(b,x_b)
\geq t.
\]
Consequently \(q_s(z)\geq t^d\) for every one of the \(N(t)\) lattice indices in \(D_t\).

If \(a=b\), choose an attained maximizer \(x_a\in C_a\) and take the constant affine map \(s(u):=x_a\). Then \(D_t=\{a\}\), so \(N(t)=1\), and
\[
q_s(a)=M(a)=\rho(a)^d\geq t^d=t^dN(t).
\]
Thus the singleton level requires no second endpoint. In both the singleton and nonsingleton cases, the constructed affine map satisfies
\[
Q_C(s)\geq t^dN(t)>0.
\tag{6}
\]

Applying the already proved affine-graph lift with \(n=1\), then (6), (5), and (4), yields
\[
\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)
\geq\frac12Q_C(s)
\geq\frac12t^dN(t)
\geq\frac18\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}M(z)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{8}W.
\]
This records separately the \(1/4\) parity single-level factor, the \(1/2\) affine-graph lifting factor, and the fiberwise \(\gamma_d\) factor, and completes the proof.
