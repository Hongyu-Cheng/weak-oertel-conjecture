## Statement

Let $Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,2)\}$ and $P=Q\cap\mathbb Z^2$. At its three vertices prescribe intervals $K_z=[0,1/2]$ and at its three edge midpoints prescribe $K_z=[0,1]$. Write $b_z$ for the right endpoint of $K_z$. The convex hull of the six lifted intervals is a full-dimensional rational polytope with exactly these integer fibers. Let $S$ be its mixed-integer source and $\mu=\mathcal H_1|_S$.

For any choice $x_z\in K_z$ define $\nu_x=\sum_{z\in P}\min(x_z,b_z-x_z)\delta_{(z,x_z)}$ and write $h_{\nu_x}$ for depth under this atomic measure, using closed ambient halfspaces. Every ambient closed halfspace $H$ satisfies $\mu(H)\ge\nu_x(H)$, but
$$
\sup_{(x_z)\in\prod_{z\in P}K_z}\ \max_{z\in P}h_{\nu_x}(z,x_z)
=\frac12
<\frac{\gamma_1}{4}\mu(S)=\frac9{16},\qquad \gamma_1=\frac12 .
$$
The supremum is attained by choosing the center of each interval. Each maximal face of $Q$ with an integer base in its relative interior is an edge. For any compact convex subset of the polytope whose base projection lies in one such edge, with positive total integer-fiber length $W'$ and $k'$ occupied parities among its positive-length fibers, its scalar bound $\gamma_1 W'/k'$ is at most $1/2$, with equality for the full edge.

## Proof

The inequalities $z_1,z_2\ge0$ and $z_1+z_2\le2$ give exactly the three vertices and three edge midpoints as integer bases. At a vertex the base of a convex combination forces every term to have that same base. At an edge midpoint, an exposing affine functional for the edge forces every term to use one of the three bases on that edge. The weights on the two edge endpoints must then agree. Their averaged interval is $[0,1/2]$, contained in the prescribed midpoint interval $[0,1]$. Thus every listed interval is an exact fiber. The base projection is $Q$, so there are no further integer fibers. The hull contains the three noncollinear lifted points at height zero over the vertices and a fourth point over one vertex at height $1/2$, so it has nonempty interior. Its total integer-fiber length is $9/2$, with maximum fiber length $1<9/8$.

The vertices have parity $(0,0)$, and the midpoints have the three other parities. There is no interior integer base in $Q$, and every edge has its midpoint in its relative interior. Thus the maximal faces specified in the statement are precisely the edges. An edge has total fiber length $2$ and two occupied parities. A convex subpiece on it with two occupied positive parities still has total length at most $2$. A subpiece with one occupied positive parity can have at most one positive fiber: two positive endpoint intervals would have a positive-length Minkowski midpoint contained in the subpiece over the other parity. Thus in this case total length is at most $1$. The bound $\gamma_1 W'/k'\le1/2$ follows in both cases. If occupied parities are instead counted using all nonempty fibers of the subpiece, including singleton fibers, the same bound follows because the denominator can only increase.

The ordinary interval depth of $x_z$ in $K_z$ is $t_z=\min(x_z,b_z-x_z)$. Whenever $H$ contains the representative $(z,x_z)$, its trace in that fiber is either the full interval or a closed one-dimensional halfspace containing $x_z$ intersected with $K_z$. Its length is at least $t_z$. Summing over the captured representatives proves $\mu(H)\ge\nu_x(H)$, including representatives of weight zero and pure base normals.

For the optimized atomic upper bound, a pure base normal isolates any vertex from the other five bases. Its representative therefore has depth at most $t_z\le1/4$. At an edge midpoint, use dec855d1498a4e64 with the six compact singleton fibers $\{x_z\}$ and the finite nonnegative Borel measure $\nu_x$. This gives exactly the restricted atomic depth over the edge, with its three selected points. The same depth can be computed in the relative base affine hull with height. If the three selected points are not collinear, the middle-base point is an exposed vertex of their triangle, and its atomic depth is its own weight $t_0\le1/2$. This uses a closed halfspace retaining precisely that vertex among the three points.

If the three points are collinear, parameterize the edge bases by $-1,0,1$. Then $x_0=(x_-+x_+)/2\le1/2$, so $t_0=x_0$. Every halfspace containing the middle point contains at least one of the other two. By cutting the line at the middle point with either orientation one can retain just the middle and the chosen endpoint. Thus the restricted atomic depth is $t_0+\min(t_-,t_+)$. Using $t_\pm\le1/2-x_\pm$, this is at most
$$
x_0+\frac12-\max(x_-,x_+)\le\frac12 .
$$
All representative selections have therefore been covered. For attainment, choose $x_z=1/4$ at the vertices and $x_z=1/2$ at the midpoints. Every halfspace containing a midpoint representative includes its weight $1/2$, so these marks attain the bound. The raw target equals $(1/2)(9/2)/4=9/16$.
