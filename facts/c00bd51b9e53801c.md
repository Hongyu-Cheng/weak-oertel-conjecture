## Statement

Fix an integer \(D\ge0\), and put
$$
H_\ast=2D+1,\qquad
L_D=2^{22}H_\ast^9,\qquad
C_D=(D+3)L_D,\qquad
c_D=\frac14e^{-C_D},\qquad
\gamma_D=\frac18e^{-3C_D/2}=c_D^{3/2}.
\tag{1}
$$
Let \(L\ge1\). For every \(1\le\ell\le L\), let \(n_\ell\ge1\) and let \(A_\ell\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_\ell}\) be finite and nonempty. Assume in each factor that every coordinatewise parity class contains at most three members, that the midpoint of every unordered distinct same-parity pair belongs to \(A_\ell\), and that at most \(D\) such pairs have any one midpoint. Choose arbitrary positive weights on the entire supports and retain the original normalizers and laws
$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=w_{\ell,x}/Z_\ell,\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,\qquad
N=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell.
\tag{2}
$$
Extend each law by zero outside its support. For every unordered distinct same-parity pair \(e=\lbrace u_e,v_e\rbrace\), write \(j_e=(u_e+v_e)/2\), and require all the exact equalities \(p_{\ell,j_e}^{\,2}=p_{\ell,u_e}p_{\ell,v_e}\) in that factor.

For one factor, suppress \(\ell\). Use the same occupied-class scaling as 1067298acf6f67ac:
$$
\mathcal P(A)=\lbrace\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n:
A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\ne\varnothing\rbrace,
\qquad K=|\mathcal P(A)|.
\tag{3}
$$
Thus \(K\) is the actual occupied parity count, not the ambient number \(2^n\). Retain exactly the original-law, full-row quantities of 1067298acf6f67ac and 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_xp_xH_p(x)^2,
&\chi(p)&=\sum_xp_xH_p(x),\\
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)},
&Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,
&q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},
\qquad \nu=\sum_xq_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in every row sum, and the central atom occurs once. In particular, no row is truncated. The integer \(D\) here bounds pairs sharing a midpoint; it is not the endpoint-incidence parameter called \(D_i\) in 445a9e668ee1a705.

Every factor satisfies, with natural logarithms,
$$
K^2b(p)\ge1,\qquad
\nu\le C_D\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{5}
$$
For any law on a finite lattice support in \(\mathbb R^n\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of its mass over every ambient closed halfspace containing \(j\), and let \(M_k(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^k\). These have precisely the meanings in the two cited predecessors. The original product (2), with depth taken in \(\mathbb R^N\), satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge c_D\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2},\\
M_3(P)&\ge \gamma_D\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Since \(0<\gamma_D\le c_D\) and \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\), one common coefficient gives
$$
M_2(P)\ge\gamma_D4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\gamma_D8^{-N}.
\tag{7}
$$
All constants depend only on the fixed integer \(D\). The hypotheses allow singleton and lower-dimensional supports and impose no graph expansion or comparison of different atom masses.

## Proof

We first prove the scalar bound in one factor. Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of all unordered distinct same-parity pairs in \(A\), let \(\mathcal J=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr Q\rbrace\), and put
$$
m_x=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q:j_e=x\rbrace|,
\qquad h_x=2m_x+1,\qquad t_x=Kp_x.
\tag{8}
$$
Here \(m_x\le D\), so every \(h_x\) is an odd positive integer at most \(H_\ast\). A noncentral reflected pair about \(x\) is exactly a pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(x\). Different such pairs about a fixed center have disjoint endpoint sets. The exact pair equalities therefore imply
$$
|R_x|=h_x,\qquad H_p(x)=h_xp_x.
\tag{9}
$$
This generalizes the row multiplier \(h_x\in\lbrace1,3\rbrace\) of 1067298acf6f67ac without changing any probability, row normalization, or occupied-count scaling. A nontrivial center may itself be an endpoint of other pairs.

For \(\eta\in\mathcal P(A)\), define its original mass \(r_\eta\), its scaled mass \(R_\eta=Kr_\eta\), and its squared mass deviation \(d_\eta=(R_\eta-1)^2\). Expanding each parity square counts every pair, including all pairs that share a midpoint. Consequently
$$
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}p_{u_e}p_{v_e}
=\sum_x(2m_x+1)p_x^2
=\chi(p).
\tag{10}
$$
Use the full-row meanings of the energy symbols in 1067298acf6f67ac:
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),\\
\mathcal D&=K\sum_\eta r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_\eta d_\eta\ge0,\\
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\sum_xp_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2,
\qquad \mathcal E_{\mathcal J}=\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The equality for \(\mathcal D\) uses the fact that exactly \(K\) occupied class masses sum to one. Expanding \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\) and using (10) gives
$$
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}=A_{\mathcal J}-2K\chi(p)+1,
\qquad A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}\ge1.
\tag{12}
$$
In particular, the energy identity does not require injectivity of pair midpoints.

For every odd positive integer \(h\le H_\ast\) and \(t>0\), write \(\Psi_h(t)=t(ht-1)^2\), and abbreviate \(\Psi_x=\Psi_{h_x}(t_x)\). Then \(\sum_x\Psi_x=K\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\). Fix a pair \(e\in\mathscr Q\), let \(\eta(e)\) be its endpoint parity, and write \(\mathcal C_e=A\cap(\eta(e)+2\mathbb Z^n)\). This class has two or three atoms. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\xi&=t_{j_e},\quad \upsilon=t_{u_e},\quad \omega=t_{v_e},
\quad z_e=\frac12\log\frac{\omega}{\upsilon},\\
F_e&=\Psi_{j_e}+\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_e}\Psi_x+d_{\eta(e)},
\qquad G_e=2h_{j_e}\xi^3z_e^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
The exact pair equality is \(\upsilon\omega=\xi^2\), and hence \(\upsilon=\xi e^{-z_e}\), \(\omega=\xi e^{z_e}\). We claim the explicit local bound
$$
G_e\le L_DF_e.
\tag{14}
$$
All terms of the endpoint class occur in this charge, including a possible third atom of arbitrarily small mass. If the center is itself in this class, its deviation appears twice in \(F_e\), as specified by (13).

Let \(M=\max(\lbrace\xi\rbrace\cup\lbrace t_x:x\in\mathcal C_e\rbrace)\). First suppose \(M>2\). The series for \(\cosh(3z)\) implies \(z^2\le(2/9)\cosh(3z)\). Therefore
$$
G_e\le\frac{4h_{j_e}}9\xi^3\cosh(3z_e)
=\frac{2h_{j_e}}9(\upsilon^3+\omega^3)
\le\frac{4H_\ast}{9}M^3.
\tag{15}
$$
An atom attaining \(M\) contributes its deviation to \(F_e\), and that deviation is at least \(M(M-1)^2\ge M^3/4\), since its multiplier is at least one. Thus \(G_e\le(16H_\ast/9)F_e\), which is stronger than (14). This case allows unbounded masses and unbounded scores.

Suppose now that \(M\le2\). Then \(|z_e|\le\log(2/\xi)\). For \(0<\xi\le2\), substituting \(a=\log(2/\xi)\ge0\) and maximizing \(a^2e^{-a}\) gives \(\xi\log^2(2/\xi)\le8/e^2<2\). If \(\xi\le1/(4H_\ast)\), then \(h_{j_e}\xi\le1/4\), and consequently
$$
\Psi_{j_e}\ge\frac9{16}\xi,
\qquad
G_e\le4H_\ast\xi^2\le\xi\le\frac{16}{9}F_e.
\tag{16}
$$
This proves (14) even when the center tends to zero.

It remains to consider \(M\le2\) and \(\xi>1/(4H_\ast)\). Both endpoints are then strictly larger than
\(\rho=1/(32H_\ast^2)\), because their product is \(\xi^2>1/(16H_\ast^2)\) and each is at most two. Also \(|z_e|<\log(8H_\ast)\). Thus the crude bound
$$
G_e\le16H_\ast\log^2(8H_\ast)\le2^{10}H_\ast^3
\tag{17}
$$
holds, using \(\log(8H_\ast)\le8H_\ast\). Put \(\varepsilon=2^{-12}H_\ast^{-6}\). If \(F_e\ge\varepsilon\), (17) gives \(G_e\le2^{22}H_\ast^9F_e=L_DF_e\).

Assume finally that \(F_e<\varepsilon\). For every \(h\ge1\) and \(t>0\),
$$
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(t,\lbrace0,1/h\rbrace\bigr)^2
\le2\Psi_h(t).
\tag{18}
$$
Indeed, for \(t\le1/(2h)\), one has \(\Psi_h(t)\ge t/4\ge(h/2)t^2\ge t^2/2\). For \(t\ge1/(2h)\), one has \(\Psi_h(t)=h^2t(t-1/h)^2\ge(h/2)(t-1/h)^2\ge(t-1/h)^2/2\). These prove (18) in the two cases.

Choose for every \(x\in\mathcal C_e\) a root \(a_x\in\lbrace0,1/h_x\rbrace\) with \(|t_x-a_x|\le\sqrt{2\Psi_x}\). The endpoint roots are nonzero: their masses exceed \(\rho\), whereas
$$
\sqrt{2F_e}<\frac{\sqrt2}{64H_\ast^3}
<\frac1{32H_\ast^2}=\rho.
\tag{19}
$$
Since \(d_{\eta(e)}\le F_e\) and the class has at most three atoms,
$$
\left|\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_e}a_x-1\right|
\le\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_e}|a_x-t_x|+|R_{\eta(e)}-1|
\le(3\sqrt2+1)\sqrt{F_e}
<\frac1{H_\ast^3}.
\tag{20}
$$
Every nonzero \(a_x\) is the reciprocal of an odd positive integer at most \(H_\ast\). A sum of at most three such roots, with zero roots allowed, is rational with a denominator dividing the product of the nonzero denominators, which is at most \(H_\ast^3\). If the sum differs from one, its distance from one is therefore at least \(H_\ast^{-3}\). Equation (20) forces the exact equality \(\sum_xa_x=1\).

At least two roots are positive, by (19). None can equal one, since another positive root would then make their sum exceed one. All positive roots are consequently at most \(1/3\). With at most three roots and sum one, there must be exactly three roots, each equal to \(1/3\). In particular, the endpoint roots are both \(1/3\), and (18) gives
\((\upsilon-1/3)^2\le2\Psi_{u_e}\) and \((\omega-1/3)^2\le2\Psi_{v_e}\). Equations (19) and the choice of \(\varepsilon\) also give \(\upsilon,\omega>1/6\). The derivative of \(\log t\) is at most six between the endpoints, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
z_e^2&\le9(\omega-\upsilon)^2\\
&\le18\bigl[(\omega-1/3)^2+(\upsilon-1/3)^2\bigr]\\
&\le36(\Psi_{u_e}+\Psi_{v_e})\le36F_e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
Since \(\xi\le2\), this implies \(G_e\le576H_\ast F_e\le L_DF_e\). All cases of (14) are now proved. The argument uses explicit bounds near zero and infinity rather than a compactness assertion.

By (9), the geometric row law \(\kappa_x\) is uniform on its \(h_x\) atoms. Its scores are zero at the center and the two values \(\pm z_e\) for every pair with midpoint \(x\). Thus
$$
V_x=\frac2{h_x}\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q\\j_e=x}}z_e^2,
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}\nu=\frac1K\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}G_e.
\tag{22}
$$
An atom's center deviation occurs as the first term of \(F_e\) at most \(D\) times. A parity class of size at most three supplies at most three unordered distinct pairs, so each of its atom deviations occurs in the class term of (13) at most three times, and its mass deviation occurs at most three times. This counts all overlaps, including centers that lie in endpoint classes. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}F_e
&\le(D+3)\sum_x\Psi_x+3\sum_\eta d_\eta,\\
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
&\le L_D\bigl[(D+3)\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+3\mathcal D\bigr]
\le C_D\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The last inequality uses \(3\le2(D+3)\). With (12) and \(\log a\ge(a-1)/a\) for \(a\ge1\), it follows that
$$
\nu\le C_D\frac{A_{\mathcal J}-1}{A_{\mathcal J}}
\le C_D\log A_{\mathcal J}.
\tag{24}
$$
This proves (5). If \(\mathscr Q\) is empty, the same sums give \(\nu=0\) directly; in particular the proof includes \(D=0\).

We finish with the original-product conversion, using the reflection mechanism of 445a9e668ee1a705 and 1067298acf6f67ac. All symbols in (2)--(4) retain exactly their predecessor normalizations. For a supported product center \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), the full row is \(R_J=\prod_\ell R_{\ell,j_\ell}\). Direct factorization gives
$$
H_P(J)=\prod_\ell H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell),\qquad
\kappa_J=\bigotimes_\ell\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\qquad
Y_J(X)=\sum_\ell Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),
\qquad \sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\tag{25}
$$
Reflection preserves each \(\kappa\) and negates its score. The scores therefore have mean zero, and independence gives \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2\). There is also the exact likelihood identity
\(P_X=H_P(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}\) for \(X\in R_J\), where \(P_X=\prod_\ell w_{\ell,x_\ell}/\prod_\ell Z_\ell\) is the original law.

Every ambient closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\), and contains \(J\) itself. Summing the smaller mass of each noncentral orbit and half the central mass, then using Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz, proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
This holds regardless of the support's affine dimension. The auxiliary center law is \(q=\bigotimes_\ell q_\ell\); it does not replace \(P\). The factorization
\(P_JH_P(J)^2=(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell))q_J\), followed by Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q\), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_\ell\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
Here \(\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2=\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\). Put
\(s_{\log}=\sum_\ell\log(K_\ell^2b(p_\ell))\ge0\), so (5) gives \(\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\le C_Ds_{\log}\). This scalar does not denote the mixed-integer set \(S\) of the locked problem. Equation (27) now yields
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2}\right)
\exp\bigl(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_Ds_{\log}}\bigr)
\ge c_D\prod_\ell K_\ell^{-2},
\tag{28}
$$
because \(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_Ds_{\log}}=(\sqrt{s_{\log}}-\sqrt{C_D})^2-C_D\ge-C_D\). The coefficient is paid once for the entire product. Finally, Jensen under the original probability \(P\) gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving (6), and then (7) follows as stated.
