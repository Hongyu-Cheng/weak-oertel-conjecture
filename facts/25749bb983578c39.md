## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, nonempty, and complete, meaning \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let \(w_{\mathbf I}>0\) on \(D\), with zero values off \(D\), be a globally extendable table in the sense of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Retain its original total weight \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), maximum \(w_{\max}\), and potential \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\). Put \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). The exact normalization map to the original arrays of accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc and the certificate of accepted fact 1fb82b36348c8b3d is their probability table \(p=w/W\), their normalized total mass one, and their potential \(\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})=\phi_{\mathbf I}\). No original source weight is changed. When \(w\) comes from an actual convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\), its meaning remains \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\) for the locked \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

Use the following probability arrays and entropies:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(p)&=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\ (\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\ (\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\\
H(r)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\qquad 0\log0=0,\\
S(q)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose that a nonempty \(\mathcal J_0\subseteq D\), with \(K_0=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal J_0\), satisfies \(K_0\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J_0\), every member of \(\mathcal J_0\) is a vertex of \(K_0\), and \(\phi_{\mathbf J}=\beta+\boldsymbol u\cdot\mathbf J\) on \(\mathcal J_0\), for some \(\beta\in\mathbb R\) and \(\boldsymbol u\in\mathbb R^n\).

Let \(\mathscr A\) be a finite set of distinct ordered pairs \((\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\) of different core members, with \(\mathbf I_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}=2\mathbf J-\mathbf K\in D\setminus\mathcal J_0\), and let \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}>0\) be their normalized assignment amounts. Suppose their original core capacities and circulation balance satisfy

\[
t_{\mathbf K}:=\sum_{\mathbf J:(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
=\sum_{\mathbf L:(\mathbf L,\mathbf K)\in\mathscr A}a_{\mathbf L\mathbf K}
\le p_{\mathbf K}\qquad(\mathbf K\in\mathcal J_0).
\]

In addition, let \(\mathscr Z\) be a finite family of components. A component \(b\in\mathscr Z\) has a target \(\mathbf J_b\in\mathcal J_0\), a nonempty support \(E_b\subseteq D\setminus\mathcal J_0\), a mass \(\alpha_b>0\), and positive coefficients \(\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\) satisfying

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_b}\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}=1,\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_b}\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J_b,\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_b}\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}=\phi_{\mathbf J_b}.
\]

No affine-independence assumption is imposed. Require the exact original external-column equalities

\[
\sum_{\substack{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A\\2\mathbf J-\mathbf K=\mathbf I}}a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}
+\sum_{\substack{b\in\mathscr Z\\\mathbf I\in E_b}}\alpha_b\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}
=p_{\mathbf I}\qquad(\mathbf I\in D\setminus\mathcal J_0).
\]

Empty families and empty sums are allowed. The amounts \(\alpha_b\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\) in this equality are charges in the barycentric array only. They are not the full-occurrence charges of the logarithmic packing in 488f0a583886b5fc: a packing component of mass \(x_E\) there charges every occurring source the full \(x_E\). No packing feasibility is inferred here from these array equalities.

Then there is a finite globally strongly convex function \(F\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), equal to zero on \(\mathcal J_0\) and positive at all other sites of \(D\), whose entire \(V_F(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}F(\mathbf J)\)-minimizing face is
\(\mathcal F_0=\{r\in\mathcal F(p):q_{\mathbf I}=0\text{ for }\mathbf I\notin\mathcal J_0\}\).
The original energy \(G(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J})\) is constant on this entire face. For its unique entropy maximizer \(r_F\), with target law \(q_F\),

\[
0\le G(r_F)\le H(r_F)\le n\log2-S(q_F)+H(r_F).
\]

This is a genuine enlargement of the reflection-only class. To state explicit examples, take integers \(k,m\ge2\), put \(n=k+1\), \(\rho_k=2^{1/k}-1\), \(L_k=\log(1/\rho_k)>0\), and let \(h(\mathbf x)\) count the coordinates of \(\mathbf x\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^k\) equal to \(-1\) or \(2\). In the original integer coordinates \((\mathbf x,t)\in\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb Z\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D^{(k,m)}&=\bigl(\{-1,0,1,2\}^k\times\{0\}\bigr)
\ \cup\ \bigl(\{0,1\}^k\times([-m,m]\cap\mathbb Z)\bigr),\\
\mathcal J_0^{(k,m)}&=\{0,1\}^k\times\{0\},\\
w_{(\mathbf x,t)}&=\begin{cases}
1,&\mathbf x\in\{0,1\}^k,\ |t|\le m,\\
\rho_k^{h(\mathbf x)},&\mathbf x\notin\{0,1\}^k,\ t=0.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

All other lattice values are zero. For this complete globally extendable table,

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=2^{k+1}(m+1),&
\sum_{\mathbf J\in\mathcal J_0^{(k,m)}}p_{\mathbf J}&=\frac1{2(m+1)},\\
G(r_F)&=\frac{k(1-2^{-1/k})L_k}{m+1},&
S(q_F)&=k\log2,\\
H(r_F)&\ge G(r_F).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the explicit strongly convex cost is \(F(\mathbf x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^k(x_i-1/2)^2+t^2-k/4\). Every possible empty vertex core in this original table has mass at most \(1/(m+1)<1/2\), so none can satisfy the reflection-only certificate. Taking \(k=2^{m^2}\) makes \(G(r_F)\to\infty\) while the displayed core mass and the upper bound for every alternative core mass tend to zero.

These conclusions also have exact actual-body examples, rather than only a limiting table interpretation. Write \(P^{(k,m)}=\operatorname{conv}D^{(k,m)}\) and
\(\Phi(\mathbf x,t)=L_k\sum_{i=1}^k\max\{-x_i,0,x_i-1\}\). For any integer \(d>kL_k\), define the body in the original integer and continuous dimensions \(n=k+1\) and \(d\) by

\[
C^{(k,m,d)}=\left\{(\mathbf x,t,\mathbf y):
(\mathbf x,t)\in P^{(k,m)},\quad
0\le y_\ell\le1-\Phi(\mathbf x,t)/d\ (1\le\ell\le d)\right\}.
\]

Its complete positive integer support is exactly \(D^{(k,m)}\). Its original fiber volumes equal one on the binary cylinder and \((1-h(\mathbf x)L_k/d)^d\) at the exterior sites with \(t=0\). Its original potential there is \(-d\log(1-h(\mathbf x)L_k/d)\), and is zero on the binary cylinder. The same certificate and cost expose the same entire face and give the same coefficient-one energy inequality. Every possible empty vertex core has normalized original volume at most \(2/(2m+1)<1/2\). For \(k=2^{m^2}\), a sufficiently large integer \(d=d_m\) for each \(m\) gives positive original selector energy tending to infinity and core mass tending to zero.

## Proof

First construct an array \(\overline r\) from the certificate, retaining every original source column. Begin with the diagonal entries \(\overline r_{\mathbf K,\mathbf K}=p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K}\) for core sites. For each arrow \((\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\), put \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\) into entry \((\mathbf J,\mathbf K)\), and add \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\) to entry \((\mathbf J,2\mathbf J-\mathbf K)\). For each zero-energy component \(b\), add \(\alpha_b\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\) to entry \((\mathbf J_b,\mathbf I)\) for every \(\mathbf I\in E_b\). All remaining entries are zero.

No zero-energy component uses a core source column. Distinct arrows have distinct entries \((\mathbf J,\mathbf K)\) in those columns, and these entries do not coincide with a diagonal because \(\mathbf K\ne\mathbf J\). Thus every core column has sum \(p_{\mathbf K}-t_{\mathbf K}+t_{\mathbf K}=p_{\mathbf K}\). Every external column has its full mass by the stipulated equality, even if contributions from several components or arrows coincide. Nonnegativity follows from the core capacity bounds. Each arrow has row moment zero because its two source sites have midpoint \(\mathbf J\). Each component has row moment zero by its barycenter identity, and the diagonal has zero moment. Consequently \(\overline r\in\mathcal F(p)\), with all target rows in \(\mathcal J_0\). This proves nonemptiness of the required face without assuming a universal core or using a selected subtable.

For the cost construction, apply the exact strict Jensen interpolation theorem of accepted fact c300e9d112cefeb0 to the complete lattice set \(\mathcal J_0\). Every member is a vertex of its hull, so its finite strict Jensen cone has no nonsingleton test. The all-zero cost vector on this set therefore has an exact finite globally strongly convex extension \(g\) to \(\mathbb R^n\). This is precisely the same justified interpolation mechanism used by 1fb82b36348c8b3d, now with the feasible array just constructed.

Let \(d_0(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,K_0)\). This function is finite and convex: using nearest points of the compact convex set \(K_0\), convexity of the set and the triangle inequality give \(d_0((1-s)x+sx')\le(1-s)d_0(x)+sd_0(x')\) for \(0\le s\le1\). It vanishes on core sites. Every external lattice site has strictly positive distance, since \(K_0\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J_0\). If there are external sites, choose

\[
\varepsilon=\min\left\{1,\min_{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\mathcal J_0}
\frac{d_0(\mathbf I)}{2(1+|g(\mathbf I)|)}\right\}>0,
\qquad F=d_0+\varepsilon g.
\]

Then \(F\) is globally strongly convex, is zero on the core, and has \(F(\mathbf I)>d_0(\mathbf I)/2>0\) at every external site. If there are no external sites, use \(F=g\). At every site in \(D\) its cost is nonnegative, so \(V_F(r)\ge0\) for every feasible array. Equality holds exactly when all rows outside the core vanish. The comparison array attains equality. Thus this description is the entire minimizing face, including every tied array, not merely one minimizing array.

The face is nonempty, compact, and convex. Indeed each feasible entry belongs to \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\), and all constraints are closed and linear. The continuity and strict concavity argument for the original conditional entropy in accepted fact 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc therefore gives its unique maximizer \(r_F\), with \(H(r_F)\ge H(\overline r)\). No row log-concavity assertion for a new objective is needed.

For every feasible array, summing row moments gives \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J=\boldsymbol\mu\), where \(\boldsymbol\mu=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\). Also \(\sum q=1\). On the entire exposed face the target-potential expectation is consequently \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\phi_{\mathbf J}=\beta+\boldsymbol u\cdot\boldsymbol\mu\), while the source-potential expectation is fixed. This proves constancy of \(G\) throughout the whole face. We may compute it using \(\overline r\).

The zero-energy components contribute \(\alpha_b[\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{b,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J_b}]=0\). Diagonal entries contribute zero. Each arrow contributes \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}(\phi_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}+\phi_{\mathbf K}-2\phi_{\mathbf J})\), which is nonnegative by the original convex-admissibility from accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Thus \(G\ge0\). Circulation balances the logarithm of the original probability at every core site, giving the following exact computation and payment:

\[
\begin{aligned}
G(r_F)=G(\overline r)
&=\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log\frac{p_{\mathbf J}^2}{p_{\mathbf K}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}}\\
&=\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log\frac{p_{\mathbf K}}{p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}}\\
&\le\sum_{(\mathbf K,\mathbf J)\in\mathscr A}
a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\log\frac{p_{\mathbf K}}{a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}}\\
&\le H(\overline r)\le H(r_F).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality uses \(a_{\mathbf K\mathbf J}\le p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf K}\), which follows from the full external-column equality with nonnegative terms. The second uses the actual entries in the original core columns: they are exactly the arrow amounts and the nonnegative diagonal residuals. Their arrow terms are the displayed entropy terms. Every residual term and every external-column entropy term is nonnegative, since no entry exceeds its original column mass. Possible collisions in external entries therefore cause no problem. The external-only condition on the zero-energy components is what preserves these paying core entries.

For the final allowance, two distinct core vertices cannot have the same parity. Their integer midpoint would belong to \(K_0\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathcal J_0\) and would not be a vertex. Thus \(|\mathcal J_0|\le2^n\). Any probability law supported on at most \(2^n\) sites has entropy at most \(n\log2\): relative entropy against the uniform law is nonnegative, as follows from \(\log x\le x-1\). Hence \(S(q_F)\le n\log2\), proving the asserted inequality. This also covers an empty circulation and zero energy.

We next verify the tapered family completely. The coordinates of any point of \(P^{(k,m)}\) obey \(-1\le x_i\le2\) and \(-m\le t\le m\). If an integral point in this hull has any coordinate \(x_i=-1\) or \(2\), every positively weighted generator in a convex representation must have that extreme coordinate. Such generators all have \(t=0\), since the binary cylinder has only zero or one in that coordinate. Thus the integral point has \(t=0\), and belongs to the first displayed part of \(D^{(k,m)}\). Otherwise its integral \(\mathbf x\) is binary, and its integral \(t\) is in \([-m,m]\), so it belongs to the cylinder. The reverse containment is immediate. This proves \(P^{(k,m)}\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}=D^{(k,m)}\), including every boundary site. The hull contains \([0,1]^k\times[-m,m]\), so it has full dimension \(k+1\).

The convex function \(\Phi\) in the statement equals \(h(\mathbf x)L_k\) on the exterior base sites and zero on the cylinder. Therefore \(e^{-\Phi}\) on \(P^{(k,m)}\), extended by zero outside it, is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with all the specified original lattice weights and every lattice zero. This verifies the extension convention of 488f0a583886b5fc directly. In particular \(w_{\max}=1\) and \(\phi=\Phi\) at the positive lattice sites.

In the base layer, each coordinate has two possible weights one and two possible weights \(\rho_k\). Its total weight is \([2(1+\rho_k)]^k=2^{k+1}\). The additional \(2m\) binary layers each have weight \(2^k\). This proves \(W=2^{k+1}(m+1)\) and the stated core mass. The core is the vertex set of \([0,1]^k\times\{0\}\), with exactly that complete lattice intersection, and its original potential is zero.

Use all arrows from \((\mathbf K,0)\) to \((\mathbf J,0)\) with \(\mathbf K,\mathbf J\in\{0,1\}^k\) different, and assign \(a_{(\mathbf K,0),(\mathbf J,0)}=\rho_k^{h(2\mathbf J-\mathbf K)}/W\). The four coordinate values \(-1,0,1,2\) correspond respectively to \((K_i,J_i)=(1,0),(0,0),(1,1),(0,1)\). Consequently every exterior base site is supplied by exactly one arrow. For a fixed core source, the sum over all possible destinations, including itself, is \((1+\rho_k)^k/W=2/W\). Removing the diagonal destination leaves \(t_{(\mathbf K,0)}=1/W=p_{(\mathbf K,0)}\). The same coordinate calculation gives the identical incoming sum. Thus every core capacity and every circulation balance is exact.

For each binary \(\mathbf J\) and each integer \(s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), take a zero-energy component with target \((\mathbf J,0)\), source support \(\{(\mathbf J,s),(\mathbf J,-s)\}\), coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), and normalized mass \(2/W\). Its sources have zero potential, have the target as their midpoint, and each receives its full normalized source mass \(1/W\). These components supply precisely the external cylinder sites not supplied by the reflection arrows. They have no core source entries. This proves every certificate hypothesis.

At a core site the stated quadratic cost equals zero. At an exterior base site it equals \(2h(\mathbf x)>0\), and at a nonzero cylinder height it equals \(t^2>0\). It is globally strongly convex. The constructed feasible array therefore shows directly that its entire minimizing face is exactly the stated core-row face. Every source and target law, feasible condition, this face, and the entropy are invariant under each reflection \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\). Uniqueness of the entropy maximizer implies that its target law is invariant under this group, which acts transitively on the core. Thus \(q_F\) is uniform there and \(S(q_F)=k\log2\).

The target potential is zero on the whole face, so its original energy is the fixed source expectation. Differentiating the elementary polynomial identity \((1+\rho)^k=\sum_{h=0}^k\binom{k}{h}\rho^h\), or multiplying out and counting a marked exterior coordinate, gives

\[
G(r_F)=\frac{2^k k\rho_k(1+\rho_k)^{k-1}L_k}{2^{k+1}(m+1)}
=\frac{k(1-2^{-1/k})L_k}{m+1}.
\]

For the explicit comparison array all exterior columns are deterministic, and all core diagonal residuals are zero. Its core-column arrow entropy is \(\sum_{\mathbf I\text{ exterior base}}p_{\mathbf I}\log(1/w_{\mathbf I})=G(r_F)\). Hence \(H(\overline r)=G(r_F)\), in addition to the already proved \(H(r_F)\ge G(r_F)\).

Any empty vertex core in dimension \(n=k+1\) has at most \(2^{k+1}\) members by the parity proof above. Every original weight in this table is at most one. Its normalized core mass is therefore at most \(2^{k+1}/W=1/(m+1)<1/2\), irrespective of its geometry or original potential. For any reflection-only certificate of 1fb82b36348c8b3d, summing its full external-column equalities gives external mass equal to \(\sum a=\sum t_{\mathbf K}\), which is at most core mass. Such a certificate necessarily has core mass at least one half. Thus no alternative choice of core can place the tapered table in that accepted class.

To check the stated unbounded-energy sequence, let \(k=2^{m^2}\) and put \(\delta=(\log2)/k\). Then \(0<\delta\le\log2\), \(\rho_k=e^\delta-1\le2\delta\), and \(k(1-e^{-\delta})\ge(\log2)/2\), by integrating respectively \(e^s\le2\) and \(e^{-s}\ge1/2\) over \([0,\delta]\). Therefore

\[
G(r_F)\ge\frac{\log2}{2(m+1)}
\left[m^2\log2-\log(2\log2)\right]\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

At the same time both core-mass bounds tend to zero. This is not obtained by multiplying all weights by a common scalar or merely specializing a reflection-only parameter: the new neutral columns have total normalized mass \(m/(m+1)\) and are completed exclusively by the zero-energy components.

Finally verify the actual bodies. On \(P^{(k,m)}\), the continuous function \(\Phi\) lies in \([0,kL_k]\), so \(1-\Phi/d\) is concave, continuous, and strictly positive for the stated integer \(d\). The body defined by the closed inequalities in the statement is compact and convex. Its base has full dimension and its cube height has a positive lower bound, so it has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\). Completeness of the base lattice intersection proves that every integer fiber outside \(D^{(k,m)}\) is empty, while every site of that support has exactly the displayed closed cube as its fiber. All cylinder volumes are one; at an exterior base site its original volume is exactly \((1-hL_k/d)^d\). Thus all original columns and all boundary fibers are retained, and their sum is exactly \(\mathcal H_d(S)\).

For every exterior site, \((1-hL_k/d)^d\le e^{-hL_k}=\rho_k^h\), using \(\log(1-u)\le-u\). Supply its full original normalized mass through its unique reflection arrow. For each core vertex, incoming and outgoing sums are equal, because these original volumes depend only on the number of differing binary coordinates. Each sum is at most \(\sum_{\mathbf J\ne\mathbf K}\rho_k^{h(2\mathbf J-\mathbf K)}/W=1/W\), its original core capacity. Use the same zero-energy pairs, now with masses \(2/W\) for this body's original \(W\). Their potentials are still zero, and they supply every remaining external cylinder column exactly. All hypotheses of the proved certificate hold with the actual original potential, so the entire-face conclusion and entropy payment follow. The same quadratic cost and the same symmetry argument also give the same uniform target law.

The cylinder alone has original weight \((2m+1)2^k\). Every original fiber volume is at most one. Hence every possible empty vertex core has normalized original volume at most \(2^{k+1}/[(2m+1)2^k]=2/(2m+1)<1/2\). No reflection-only certificate is possible for these actual bodies either. The displayed core has mass at most \(1/(2m+1)\), which tends to zero.

For each fixed \(k,m\), as integers \(d\to\infty\), all finitely many original volumes converge to the tapered table volumes and all original potentials converge to \(hL_k\). The total weights and the fixed source-potential expectation therefore converge to their already computed table values. The latter is the original energy on the entire face for every \(d\). For \(k=2^{m^2}\), choose an integer \(d_m>kL_k\) large enough that this positive original energy is at least half the corresponding table energy. Such an integer exists by the finite-sum convergence. The lower bound just proved then makes the actual energies tend to infinity, while their core masses tend to zero. This proves the exact actual-body extension without replacing original volumes by proxy weights.
