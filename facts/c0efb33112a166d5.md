## Proof

We first audit the ordered-pair multiplicities.  If \(r\ne s\), then the two ordered pairs \((r,s)\) and \((s,r)\) occur in the same cell \(t=(r+s)/2\), have the same probability, have the same outside-outside classification, and satisfy
\[
 \lambda_{t;r,s}=\lambda_{t;s,r}.
\tag{16}
\]
For the unordered edge \(e=\{r,s\}\) associated with such an outside-outside pair, put
\[
 q_e^{\ne}:=\lambda_{t;r,s}.
\tag{17}
\]
At endpoint \(r\), use
\[
 f_{e,r}^{\ne}
 :=g_{(t;r,s;1)}+g_{(t;s,r;2)},
 \qquad y_{e,r}^{\ne}:=c_r+h_t,
\tag{18}
\]
and define the analogous density at \(s\) from the other two tagged occurrences.  Each summand in (18) has mass \(\lambda_{t;r,s}/2\) and barycenter \(c_r+h_t\).  Hence \(f_{e,r}^{\ne}\) has mass \(q_e^{\ne}\) and barycenter \(y_{e,r}^{\ne}\).  The off-diagonal part has total matching mass
\[
 |q^{\ne}|=\frac{B_{\ne}}2.
\tag{19}
\]

For a diagonal pair there is one ordered pair and two tagged endpoint occurrences.  Their aggregate density is
\[
 f_r^\Delta:=g_{(r;r,r;1)}+g_{(r;r,r;2)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Its mass is the full pair load \(\delta_r\), not \(\delta_r/2\), and its barycenter is
\[
 y_r^\Delta:=c_r+h_r.
\tag{21}
\]
This audits the two-tag normalization requested in the statement.  The source ledger of `b5739a77433de383` gives \(0\leq f_r^\Delta\leq1\), so \(y_r^\Delta\in\mathsf M_{\delta_r}(C_r)\).  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives fiber halfspace depth at least \(\gamma_d\delta_r\).  Every ambient halfspace containing \((r,y_r^\Delta)\) has a fiber section containing \(y_r^\Delta\), so
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\delta_r,
 \qquad
 \delta_r\leq\frac{D}{\gamma_d2^n}
 <\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n}.
\tag{22}
\]

We use the following elementary fractional matching lemma.  If finitely many nonnegative vertex masses \((u_i)\) lie in one parity class, with total \(U\) and maximum \(u_*\), then there is a loopless fractional matching with degrees at most \(u_i\) and mass
\[
 \min\left\{\frac U2,U-u_*\right\}
 \geq\frac{U-u_*}{2}.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(u_*>U/2\), join the maximum vertex to every other vertex with edge weight equal to that other vertex's mass.  If \(u_*\leq U/2\), arrange consecutive intervals of lengths \(u_i\) on a circle of circumference \(U\), pair every point with its antipode, and assign to \(\{i,j\}\) half the measure of points whose two labels are \(i,j\).  No interval meets its antipodal translate in positive measure, every vertex degree is exactly \(u_i\), and the total matching mass is \(U/2\).  This proves (23), including singleton and boundary cases.

Apply (23) to \((\delta_r)_{r\equiv\xi}\) in every parity class, and call the resulting union \(q^\Delta\).  If \(m_\xi:=\max_{r\equiv\xi}\delta_r\), with \(m_\xi=0\) for an empty class, then (22) and the fact that there are at most \(2^n\) classes give
\[
 \sum_\xi m_\xi
 <\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}.
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 |q^\Delta|
 \geq\frac12\left(B_\Delta-\sum_\xi m_\xi\right)
 >\frac12\left(B_\Delta-\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}\right).
\tag{25}
\]

For every edge \(e\) of \(q^\Delta\) incident to \(r\), put
\[
 f_{e,r}^\Delta:=\frac{q_e^\Delta}{\delta_r}f_r^\Delta,
 \qquad y_{e,r}^\Delta:=y_r^\Delta.
\tag{26}
\]
The degree bound in (23) makes the sum of (26) over the new diagonal-rematching edges at \(r\) at most \(f_r^\Delta\).  Equations (18) and (26) use disjoint parts of the original occurrence list.  Hence their union \(q:=q^{\ne}+q^\Delta\) satisfies the pointwise ledger (6) and the degree bound (3).  If the same unordered edge receives weight from both summands, add its two endpoint densities and define \(y_{e,r}\) to be their normalized aggregate barycenter; otherwise retain the already defined density and point.  Every constituent barycenter is strictly outside \(H\), so convexity of the strict complement \(\{\Phi<0\}\) keeps the aggregate barycenter strictly outside.  Convexity of \(C\) then proves (7).

Combining (2), (19), (25), the accepted strict load bound, and \(\gamma_d>4/11\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |q|
 &>\frac12\left(
 L_H^{\mathrm{out,out}}-\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}
 \right)\\
 &>\frac12\left(\frac{901}{6400}-\frac{11}{400}\right)Q
 =\frac{725}{12800}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
This proves (3)-(7).  Notice that neither a diagonal loop nor either orientation of an off-diagonal pair has been charged twice.

We next transfer most of \(q\) to a canonical centroid host.  We prove a second elementary matching lemma.  Let \((b_i)\) be nonnegative degrees on a finite complete loopless graph, and let \(p\) be any fractional matching with \(d_i(p)\leq b_i\).  There is a submatching \(p'\leq p\), losing at most \(b_*:=\max_i b_i\), which is contained in an exact minimum-unmatched matching for \((b_i)\).

Indeed, put \(B:=\sum_i b_i\).  If \(b_*>B/2\), retain only the edges of \(p\) incident to a maximum vertex.  Complete them by joining the residual degree of every other vertex to the maximum vertex.  The result saturates all nonmaximum degrees and is exact minimum-unmatched.  The discarded mass is the mass of \(p\) among nonmaximum vertices.

Every discarded edge has both endpoints among the nonmaximum vertices, so this discarded mass is at most
\[
 \frac12\sum_{i\ne *}d_i(p)
 \leq\frac{B-b_*}{2}<\frac{b_*}{2}<b_*,
\tag{28}
\]
where \(b_*>B/2\) was used.  If instead \(b_*\leq B/2\), put \(P:=|p|\) and residual degrees \(b_i-d_i(p)\).  If they are balanced, complete them by (23).  Otherwise there is a unique residual-dominant vertex \(i\).  Put
\[
 \varepsilon:=b_i-d_i(p)-\frac{B-2P}{2}>0.
\tag{29}
\]
Original balance gives \(\varepsilon\leq P-d_i(p)\), the mass of edges of \(p\) not incident to \(i\).  Delete exactly \(\varepsilon\) from those edges.  The new residual degree at \(i\) is exactly half the new residual total, and no other residual degree exceeds it; complete the residual sequence by (23).  The loss \(\varepsilon<b_i\leq b_*\).  This proves the lemma in both cases.

Apply this lemma separately in each parity class to
\[
 b_z=b_z^{\mathrm{cen}}=\gamma_da_z,
 \qquad p=q|_\xi.
\tag{30}
\]
The hypothesis follows from (3) and \(\gamma_d>1/4\).  To bound the total loss, note that the fiber centroid \(c_z\) is the unique point of \(\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)\).  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_da_z,
 \qquad
 \gamma_da_z\leq\frac D{2^n}<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n}.
\tag{31}
\]
There are at most \(2^n\) parity classes.  Thus the classwise host-completion losses total less than \(Q/100\).  The union of the completed matchings is an exact global minimum-unmatched centroid matching \(\lambda^{\mathrm{cen}}\), and their retained parts give \(q'\leq q\) with
\[
 |q'|>|q|-\frac Q{100}
 >\left(\frac{725}{12800}-\frac{128}{12800}\right)Q
 =\frac{597}{12800}Q.
\tag{32}
\]
This proves (8).  Definition (9) is therefore a legal positive centroid-host submeasure in the exact normalization of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, which proves (12).  Combining (12) with \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)<Q/100\), a consequence of the nonnegative hard ledger in `637b5f7874d8c875`, proves (13).

Finally fix \(t\) with \(E_t\ne\varnothing\).  Every edge \(e=\{r,s\}\in E_t\) satisfies
\[
 q'_e\leq q_e\leq d_r(q)\leq\frac{a_r}{4},
 \qquad
 q'_e\leq\frac{a_s}{4}.
\tag{33}
\]
Moreover, convexity gives \((C_r+C_s)/2\subseteq C_t\).  The two endpoint fibers have positive \(d\)-volume, so their Minkowski average has positive \(d\)-volume and hence \(a_t>0\).  Thus this \(t\) is an admissible center in the canonical reflected-cloud definition.
Use in the defining reflected cloud of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` the noncentral directions corresponding to the distinct pairs \(e=\{t+v,t-v\}\in E_t\).  Equation (33) yields
\[
 \sum_{e\in E_t}\min\{a_{t+v},a_{t-v}\}
 \geq4S_t.
\tag{34}
\]
The reflected centroid points are exactly \(\bar c_e\).  Substitution in that accepted definition proves the first inequality in (15).  The strict bound \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)<Q/100\) from the hard ledger gives the second.  This completes the construction, the centroid-host transfer, and both exact rank screens.
