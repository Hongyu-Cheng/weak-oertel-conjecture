## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and maximize $\sum_i \omega_i\phi_d(X_i)$ locally over nonnegative $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ matrices with each column sum equal to one, where $X_i$ is row $i$, $\omega_i>0$, and $\phi_d$ is the uniform-simplex row capacity. If a strictly positive row $X_r$ has a full-cut minimum, it has maximum entry one and saturates at least $d$ columns. The capacity vector then equals $e_r$ and is realized also by a zero-diagonal column-stochastic matrix. If the capacity vector is no unit vector, every strictly positive row has a unique proper minimizing threshold and locally differentiable capacity.

## Proof

By the full-cut classification, a strictly positive row $r$ at a full-cut minimum is constant or has one low entry and $d$ high entries equal to its maximum $m$. Suppose $m<1$ and choose a high column $j$. Its remaining mass $1-m$ gives a positive entry $X_{ij}$ in a different row $i$. Move a signed amount $\epsilon$ from that entry to $X_{rj}$. The line is feasible in both signs near zero. The row-$r$ right derivative minus left derivative of capacity along the line is strictly positive by the coordinate-jump lemma. For row $i$ it is nonnegative, since reversing the parameter exchanges and negates its one-sided derivatives. Positive weights and constant other rows therefore give a strictly positive jump in the objective. A local maximum requires its left derivative to be nonnegative and its right derivative to be nonpositive, a contradiction.

It follows that $m=1$. At least $d$ columns are saturated by row $r$, and every other row is supported in at most one column. Its capacity is zero by the fixed-support boundary calculation of the coordinate-jump lemma, while row $r$ has capacity one. To realize $e_r$ with zero diagonal, put $1$ in every off-diagonal entry in row $r$ and put the remaining column's unit mass in any other row. This is column-stochastic and has capacities $1$ in row $r$ and $0$ elsewhere by the one-zero and at-least-two-zero formulas. The unique-threshold lemma gives the proper-row conclusion outside the unit-vector case.
