## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$, $V=|T|=1/2$, and let $C$ be the hull of the planar fibers $(7/15)T,T,T$ placed at the bases $-1,0,1$ in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2$. These are its exact integer fibers. They have total mixed area $(499/225)V$, with each area strictly smaller than half that total. The point $(1,p)$, where $p=(1/300,1/300)$, is in the interior of its own fiber. For each $z\in\{-1,0\}$ push the area on $C_z$ forward by $x\mapsto (x-zp)/(1-z)$. The resulting measure has density
$$
f=\mathbf1_T+4\mathbf1_{(7/30)T+(1/600,1/600)}
$$
and mass $(274/225)V$. At every point in $\mathbb R^2$ its normalized closed-halfspace depth is at most $6566561/15618000=27/64-89131/62472000<\Gamma_3$, where $\Gamma_3=27/64$.

Put $B_t=(1-t)C_{-t/(1-t)}+tp$. For $0\le s<t<1$ such that the remote fiber $C_{-t/(1-t)}$ is nonempty, one has $B_t+(t-s)(T-p)\subseteq B_s$, and every line through zero has a positive-length intersection with $T-p$. The raw mixed depth of the anchor $(1,p)$ is at most $V/22500$, whereas the centroid of the middle fiber has raw mixed depth at least $(4/9)V$.

## Proof

The hull is compact and full dimensional, since it contains a full planar triangle at one height and a point at a different height. The endpoint heights use only their own fibers, and any contribution from the two endpoints at height zero uses their midpoint combination $(11/15)T\subset T$. Thus the middle section is $T$ and the projection $[-1,1]$ gives exactly the three claimed integer sites. The areas are $(49/225)V,V,V$. At height zero the pushforward is the identity. At $-1$ it is $x\mapsto (x+p)/2$, with area Jacobian $1/4$. The claimed density and total mass follow.

Write $s_0=27/50$, $r=4/15$, $k=99/500=(1-r)s_0/2$, $c=7/30$, and $\tau=1/600$. The closed halfplanes
$$
\{x_1+x_2\le s_0\},\qquad
\{x_1-rx_2\ge k\},\qquad
\{x_2-rx_1\ge k\}
$$
cover the plane, since adding the strict failures of the last two gives $x_1+x_2<s_0$. The first has mass $V(s_0^2+4c^2)=V(11461/22500)$ because $c+2\tau<s_0$. For a triangle $aT$ and $0<h<a$, the cap $x_1-rx_2\ge h$ has relative area $(a-h)^2/(1+r)$ in units of $V$. Its side on $x_2=0$ has length $a-h$, and the opposite vertex, on $x_1+x_2=a$, has height $(a-h)/(1+r)$. Use $(a,h)=(1,k)$ for the outer triangle and $(a,h)=(c,k-(1-r)\tau)$ after translating the inner triangle to the origin. The latter threshold is $1771/9000\in(0,c)$. The second cap therefore has mass
$$
V\,\frac{(1-k)^2+4(c-k+(1-r)\tau)^2}{1+r}
=V\,\frac{6566561}{12825000}.
$$
The third has the same mass by coordinate exchange. This exceeds the first by $V(33791/12825000)$. Divide the largest cap mass by $(274/225)V$ to obtain the claimed depth bound and subtract it from $27/64$ for the strict gap.

For the erosion inclusion, the base $-s/(1-s)$ is the average of $-t/(1-t)$ and $1$ with weights $(1-t)/(1-s)$ and $(t-s)/(1-s)$. Convexity gives $(1-t)C_{-t/(1-t)}+(t-s)T\subseteq(1-s)C_{-s/(1-s)}$. Add $sp$ to both sides. The point $p$ is interior to $T$, so $T-p$ contains a ball about zero. In the original ambient space the closed cap $x_1+x_2+(1-z)\le 1/150$ contains $(1,p)$ and misses both other fibers. It has area $V/22500$. By the full-mass centroid case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d with $d=2$, every closed planar halfspace containing the centroid of $T$ contains area at least $4V/9$. An ambient halfspace containing the middle centroid restricts there to such a halfspace or the entire fiber plane, so the same bound holds for its mixed mass.
