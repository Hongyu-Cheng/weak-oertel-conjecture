## Statement

Let \(R,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and define
\[
C_{R,d}:=
\left\{
(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d:
0\leq x_j\leq R-\|z\|_\infty
\quad(1\leq j\leq d)
\right\}.
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C_{R,d}\}.
\]
Given a finite nonempty
\[
E\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0\},
\qquad
0\in\operatorname{conv}(E),
\]
and an affine map
\[
s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
s(z)\in C_z\quad(z\in E),
\]
put
\[
D_E:=\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2
\]
and
\[
\delta_{E,s}(z):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }s(z)\in J
\right\}.
\]
If \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)>0\), define
\[
\alpha_0(E,s):=
\inf_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(E)\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
0\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in D_E\cap Q}\delta_{E,s}(z).
\]
If \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)=0\), necessarily \(D_E=\{0\}\), and define
\[
\alpha_0(E,s):=\delta_{E,s}(0).
\]
Finally put
\[
\mathfrak A_0(C_{R,d}):=\sup_{E,s}\alpha_0(E,s).
\]

For every integer \(a\) with \(0\leq a<R\), let
\[
E_a:=\{(\varepsilon_1a,\varepsilon_2a):
\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{-1,1\}\}
\]
as a set, so \(E_0=\{0\}\), and define the constant affine map
\[
s_a(z):=\frac{R-a}{2}(1,\ldots,1).
\]
Then \((E_a,s_a)\) is admissible and
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_0(E_a,s_a)
\geq
\frac{(R-a)^d\bigl((2a+1)^2+1\bigr)}4.
}
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathfrak A_0(C_{R,d})
\geq
\max_{0\leq a<R,\ a\in\mathbb Z}
\frac{(R-a)^d\bigl((2a+1)^2+1\bigr)}4.
\]

In particular, take \(d=10\), \(R=2100\), and put
\[
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{10}(C_z),
\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z.
\]
The complete positive profile is
\[
D=[-2099,2099]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
w_z=(2100-\|z\|_\infty)^{10}\quad(z\in D),
\]
with \(w_z=0\) outside \(D\), and
\[
W
=2100^{10}
+8\sum_{m=1}^{2099}m(2100-m)^{10}
=445808283959994019181783268863960700000.
\]
This specialization satisfies
\[
w_z<\frac W4
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^2),
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in(b+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\]
for every coset \(b+L\) of every primitive rank-one sublattice
\(L\leq\mathbb Z^2\), every proper closed base halfspace containing zero
has \(w\)-mass at least \(W/4\), and
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}D.
\]
For the parity masses
\[
W_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z
\]
and
\[
Q^\circ:=
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}W_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2,
\]
one has
\[
Q^\circ>\frac1{16}W^2>
\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\]

Set
\[
\gamma:=\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10},
\qquad
S_0:=2(1-\gamma)^{1/10}2100.
\]
For \(A,B\geq0\), define
\[
\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B):=
A^{10}+B^{10}
-
\max_{\substack{0\leq u\leq A,\ 0\leq v\leq B\\u+v\leq S_0}}
(u^{10}+v^{10}),
\]
\[
\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B):=
\min\{\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B),A^{10},B^{10}\}.
\]
Using one representative from each nonzero symmetric pair
\(\{v,-v\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\), define
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)
:=
\gamma w_0
+
\sum_{v/\pm}
\Psi_{10,S_0}
\left(w_v^{1/10},w_{-v}^{1/10}\right).
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)<\frac W{64},
\]
but the general certificate above with \(a=350\) gives
\[
\mathfrak A_0(C_{2100,10})
\geq
\alpha_0(E_{350},s_{350})
\geq
33094621255182743072509765625000000000
>
\frac W{64}.
\]
Thus this exact residual low-energy cube bundle satisfies
\[
\max\{
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0),
\mathfrak A_0(C_{2100,10})
\}
>\frac W{64}.
\]

## Proof

For each \(j\), the defining constraints of \(C_{R,d}\) are
\[
x_j\geq0,\qquad x_j+\|z\|_\infty\leq R.
\]
They are convex constraints. They imply
\(\|z\|_\infty\leq R\) and \(0\leq x_j\leq R\), so the body is compact.
It contains a neighborhood of
\((0,(R/2,\ldots,R/2))\), so it is full-dimensional. Its real fiber is
\[
C_z=
\begin{cases}
[0,R-\|z\|_\infty]^d,&\|z\|_\infty\leq R,\\
\varnothing,&\|z\|_\infty>R.
\end{cases}
\]

Fix an integer \(a\) with \(0\leq a<R\). When \(a\geq1\),
\[
\operatorname{conv}(E_a)=[-a,a]^2,
\qquad
D_{E_a}=[-a,a]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{aff}(E_a)=\mathbb R^2.
\]
When \(a=0\), all four displayed vertices coincide and
\(E_0=D_{E_0}=\{0\}\). In either case \(E_a\) consists of positive-fiber
lattice points and contains zero in its convex hull.

For every \(z\in[-a,a]^2\),
\[
R-\|z\|_\infty\geq R-a.
\]
Thus \(C_z\) contains the cube
\[
K_a:=[0,R-a]^d,
\]
whose center is \(s_a(z)\). In particular \(s_a\) is feasible on the whole
convex hull.

Fix \(z\in D_{E_a}\), and let \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be any proper
closed halfspace containing \(s_a(z)\). Write
\[
J=\{x:u^\top x\geq b\},
\qquad
u\neq0,\quad b\leq u^\top s_a(z).
\]
Then
\[
J_0:=\{x:u^\top(x-s_a(z))\geq0\}
\subseteq J.
\]
Reflection through the center \(s_a(z)\) preserves \(K_a\) and exchanges
the two open sides of the boundary hyperplane of \(J_0\). This hyperplane
has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Hence, with the closed boundary included,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_a\cap J_0)
=\frac12\operatorname{vol}_d(K_a)
=\frac12(R-a)^d.
\]
Since \(K_a\subseteq C_z\),
\[
\delta_{E_a,s_a}(z)\geq\frac12(R-a)^d
\qquad(z\in D_{E_a}).
\]

Suppose first that \(a\geq1\). Let
\(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be any proper closed halfspace containing zero.
Write
\[
Q=\{z:u^\top z\geq b\},
\qquad
u\neq0,\quad b\leq0.
\]
The boundary-through-zero halfspace
\[
Q_0:=\{z:u^\top z\geq0\}
\]
is contained in \(Q\). The finite set \(D_{E_a}\) is centrally symmetric,
contains zero, and has \((2a+1)^2\) points. Pairing every nonzero lattice
point with its negative shows
\[
|D_{E_a}\cap Q|
\geq
|D_{E_a}\cap Q_0|
\geq
\frac{(2a+1)^2+1}{2}.
\]
If an antipodal pair lies on the boundary, both of its points are counted,
so the closed-boundary convention can only increase the left side.
Combining this count with the uniform fiber-depth bound gives
\[
\sum_{z\in D_{E_a}\cap Q}\delta_{E_a,s_a}(z)
\geq
\frac{(R-a)^d\bigl((2a+1)^2+1\bigr)}4.
\]
Taking the infimum over every such \(Q\) proves the boxed estimate for
\(a\geq1\).

For \(a=0\), the registered rank-zero definition gives
\[
\alpha_0(E_0,s_0)
=\delta_{E_0,s_0}(0)
\geq\frac12R^d
=\frac{R^d((2\cdot0+1)^2+1)}4.
\]
Thus the same formula holds for every permitted \(a\), and taking the
supremum proves the general capacity bound.

It remains to audit the exact \(R=2100,d=10\) corollary. The positive
lattice fibers are precisely those with \(\|z\|_\infty\leq2099\), and the
shell \(\|z\|_\infty=m\) has \(8m\) points for \(m\geq1\). This proves the
displayed complete profile and total mass.

The largest fiber has mass \(2100^{10}\), and
\[
W-4\cdot2100^{10}
=445801612007602738781783268863960700000>0.
\]
Hence every singleton inequality is strict.

If a primitive rank-one coset contains a lattice point, write its lattice
points as \(b+kv\), with \(b\in\mathbb Z^2\) and primitive
\(v\neq0\). Choose a coordinate \(i\) with \(v_i\neq0\). The values
\(b_i+kv_i\) are distinct integers, and the fiber mass at such a point is
at most
\((2100-|b_i+kv_i|)^{10}\) when it is positive. Thus every such coset has
mass at most
\[
L:=2100^{10}+2\sum_{k=1}^{2099}k^{10}
=636869506603755599191663828920818500.
\]
The exact comparison
\[
W-2L
=444534544946786507983399941206119063000>0
\]
proves every strict primitive-line-coset inequality. A coset without a
lattice point has zero mass.

The profile is centrally symmetric about zero. Moving the boundary of any
proper closed base halfspace containing zero parallel toward zero produces
a contained boundary-through-zero halfspace. Antipodal pairing shows that
this smaller halfspace has mass at least \(W/2\), including all boundary
mass. This proves the required \(W/4\) marginal bound. Also
\[
\operatorname{conv}D=[-2099,2099]^2,
\]
so zero is in its interior.

The parity counts on the shell \(\|z\|_\infty=m\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&00&01&10&11\\ \hline
m\ {\rm even}&4m&2m&2m&0\\
m\ {\rm odd}&0&2m&2m&4m.
\end{array}
\]
Including the origin in class \(00\), exact summation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{00}
&=111452487991278022493124290927539200000,\\
W_{01}=W_{10}
&=111451653992974049770445817215990175000,\\
W_{11}
&=111452487982767897147767343504441150000.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover
\[
\sum_z w_z^2
=2100^{20}
+8\sum_{m=1}^{2099}m(2100-m)^{20}
\]
\[
=212458637162246231376821690558283916815485397959173429255778975250372000.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
256Q^\circ-16W^2
\]
\[
=9539706861039969972699522321315018990184919174525721775813936964484575904768000
>0.
\]
Thus \(Q^\circ>W^2/16>(16-e)W^2/256\).

For the support-safe energy, put
\[
q:=\left(1-\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}\right)^{1/10}.
\]
The exact integer inequality
\[
11^{10}21^{10}-10^{10}21^{10}-20^{10}11^{10}
=235117529492562122801>0
\]
implies \(q>20/21\), and therefore
\[
S_0=4200q>4000.
\]
On the shell \(\|v\|_\infty=m\), the two endpoint root volumes are
\[
A=B=2100-m,
\]
and there are \(4m\) unordered symmetric pairs. For
\(100\leq m\leq2099\), one has \(A+B\leq4000<S_0\), so
\(\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=0\). For
\(1\leq m\leq99\), the feasible point
\[
(u,v)=(2100-m,1900+m)
\]
has \(u+v=4000<S_0\) and \(0\leq u,v\leq A=B\). Hence
\[
\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,A)
\leq
(2100-m)^{10}-(1900+m)^{10}.
\]
Pairs outside the positive support contribute zero. Therefore
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)\leq U,
\]
where
\[
U=
\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10}2100^{10}
+
\sum_{m=1}^{99}
4m\left((2100-m)^{10}-(1900+m)^{10}\right).
\]
Writing \(U=N_U/11^{10}\), exact summation gives
\[
N_U
=178662440347030185963710415414319990620120000000,
\]
and
\[
11^{10}W-64N_U
=128722569503610671260984662365812182789500700000
>0.
\]
This proves \(\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)<W/64\).

Finally, the general certificate with \(a=350\) has
\[
R-a=1750,\qquad
\frac{(2a+1)^2+1}{2}
=\frac{701^2+1}{2}
=245701.
\]
Thus
\[
\alpha_0(E_{350},s_{350})
\geq
\frac{1750^{10}(701^2+1)}4
\]
\[
=33094621255182743072509765625000000000.
\]
The exact comparison
\[
64\cdot33094621255182743072509765625000000000-W
\]
\[
=1672247476371701537458841731136039300000>0
\]
proves that this anchored value is strictly greater than \(W/64\). No
optimality assertion about \(a=350\) or about the anchored capacity is
used.
