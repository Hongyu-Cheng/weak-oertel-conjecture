## Statement

Let \(p\ge7\) be an odd prime, put \(n=2p-2\), \(Q=2^n-1\), and \(m=Q/p\), and choose any primitive element \(\alpha\) of \(\mathbb F_{2^n}\). Put \(\omega=\alpha^m\). The element \(1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha\) is nonzero, so there is a unique exponent \(c\) modulo \(Q\) such that \(\alpha^c=1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha\).

Start with labels \(X_{i,t}\), for \(i\in\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z\) and \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\), and equations

\[
2X_{i,t}=X_{i-d_t,s}+X_{i-d_t,u},
\qquad (d_0,d_1,d_2)=(1,m-1,c),
\qquad \{s,u,t\}=\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Delete the labels \(X_{jm,0}\) and \(X_{jm-1,1}\), for \(0\le j<p\), and every equation with a deleted endpoint. Use the aliases \(U_i=X_{i,0}\), \(V_i=X_{i-1,1}\), and \(Z_i=X_{i,2}\). The retained labels without equations are exactly the \(2p\) sinks

\(A_j=Z_{c-1+jm}\) and \(B_j=Z_{c+jm}\), for \(0\le j<p\).

There is a unique harmonic boundary solution. For a retained label \(Y\), let \(\mu_Y(s)\) be its coefficient at sink \(s\), equivalently the probability of absorption at \(s\) for the walk that chooses either parent with probability one half. Then some index \(i\not\equiv0\pmod m\) satisfies, for every sink \(s\),

\[
0<\mu_{V_i}(s)<3\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(s),
\qquad
0<\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(s)<3\mu_{V_i}(s).
\]

This index depends only on the graph. For any assignment of rational positions to its sinks in a finite-dimensional real coordinate space, realize the retained labels by the unique harmonic solution. Let \(\mathcal L\) be the additive group generated by all realized positions, let \(P\) be their convex hull, and put \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\). Define \(r_B^{\mathcal L}(U_i):=|\{v\in\mathcal L:U_i+v,U_i-v\in B\}|\), counting oriented displacements and counting the zero displacement once. For every such assignment, either \(V_i=Z_{i-1}\), or \(V_i\ne Z_{i-1}\) and \(B\) contains five distinct collinear points centered at \(U_i\), with \(r_B^{\mathcal L}(U_i)\ge5\) in this second alternative.

The conclusions about the graph and its rational realizations also hold if \(c\) is chosen arbitrarily modulo \(Q\), without imposing the field identity.

## Proof

First the field data are defined for every permitted primitive element. Write \(q=2^{p-1}\), so \(\mathbb F_{2^n}=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and let \(K=\mathbb F_q\). Fermat's theorem gives \(p\mid q-1\), so \(\omega\in K\). If \(\gamma=1+\alpha+\omega/\alpha\) belonged to \(K\), then \(\alpha\notin K\) would have the monic quadratic minimal polynomial \(X^2+(1+\gamma)X+\omega\) over \(K\). Its norm would be \(\omega\), of order \(p\). Its actual norm \(\alpha^{q+1}\) has order \(q-1>p\), a contradiction. Thus \(\gamma\notin K\), in particular \(\gamma\ne0\), and \(c\) exists. Also \(c\not\equiv0\pmod m\), although the analytic argument below does not require this additional restriction.

In the aliases of the statement, all retained equations are precisely

\[
\begin{aligned}
2U_i&=V_i+Z_{i-1},&
2V_i&=U_{i-m}+Z_{i-m}
&& (i\not\equiv0\pmod m),\\
2Z_{i-1+c}&=U_{i-1}+V_i
&&&& (i\not\equiv0,1\pmod m).
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, the deleted labels are \(U_{jm},V_{jm}\). A deleted \(U_i\) removes the equation at the already deleted \(V_{i+m}\) and the equation at \(Z_{i+c}\). A deleted \(V_i\) removes the equation at the already deleted \(U_i\) and the equation at \(Z_{i-1+c}\). This proves the sink classification and shows that each remaining nonsink has exactly two retained parents.

Direct original equations from their centers to their parents. Closed type paths \(0\to1\to0\), \(0\to2\to0\), and \(0\to1\to2\to0\) change the exponent by \(-m\), \(-(1+c)\), and \(-(m+c)\), respectively. In a finite cyclic group the semigroup generated by these increments is their generated subgroup, because the inverse of an increment is a nonnegative multiple of it. The subgroup contains \(m\) and \(m-1\), hence contains one. Thus the original graph is strongly connected, for every \(c\).

Every retained nonsink reaches a retained sink. Follow an original path from it to a deleted label and stop at the first retained center whose equation was lost. Such a center must occur before the deleted label, and the preceding classification makes it a sink. All preceding edges belong to retained equations. The maximum principle now gives uniqueness for scalar harmonic boundary values: a positive maximum of a solution with zero boundary would propagate along a retained path to a zero sink; applying the same argument to its negative excludes a negative minimum. The square integer system is therefore invertible over \(\mathbb Q\). Its single-sink boundary solutions are nonnegative and sum to one. Also absorption occurs almost surely: finiteness and the paths just proved give a common finite path-length bound and a positive lower bound on absorption within that many steps, from every nonsink. Iterating this bound makes the survival probability tend to zero. Hence the harmonic coefficients have the stated probabilistic interpretation.

We next eliminate the retained \(U,V\) cycles probabilistically. Fix \(i\not\equiv0\pmod m\). Starting from \(U_i\), two successive parent choices either exit to a \(Z\) label or return to \(U_{i-m}\). A return has probability \(1/4\). Thus the number \(K_0\) of complete returns before exit has distribution \(\Pr(K_0=k)=3/4^{k+1}\), for \(k\ge0\). It is independent of which exit is taken. The exit laws are

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_i&\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
Z_{i-K_0m-1},&\text{with probability }2/3,\\
Z_{i-(K_0+1)m},&\text{with probability }1/3,
\end{cases}\\
V_i&\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
Z_{i-(K_0+1)m},&\text{with probability }2/3,\\
Z_{i-(K_0+1)m-1},&\text{with probability }1/3.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

Every such cycle is retained because its residue is nonzero. At a nonsink \(Z_r\), its two parents are \(U_{r-c}\) and \(V_{r-c+1}\), both in retained cycles. Combining their exit laws shows that one \(Z\)-to-\(Z\) excursion has the following form: independently sample \(K_0\) as above and choose uniformly among

\[
Z_{r-c-1-K_0m},\qquad
Z_{r-c-(K_0+1)m},\qquad
Z_{r-c+1-(K_0+1)m}.
\]

Different excursions use independent samples. On residues modulo \(m\), this is the walk with increments \(-c-1,-c,-c+1\), each with probability \(1/3\), killed at the two adjacent residues \(c-1,c\).

Let \(h(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\mu_{Z_r}(A_j)\). Translation by \(m\) preserves the graph and each sink orbit, so uniqueness makes \(h\) a function on \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). It satisfies \(h(c-1)=1\), \(h(c)=0\), and, with \((\mathcal A h)(r)=(h(r-1)+h(r)+h(r+1))/3\),

\((\mathcal A h)(r)=h(r+c)\) for every \(r\not\equiv-1,0\pmod m\).

Both \(h\) and \(1-h\) are strictly positive off their respective zero boundary residues. Here is an elementary check that rules out additional zeros. If the set \(\mathcal Z_0\) of nonsink zeros of \(h\) were nonempty, harmonicity would give \((\mathcal Z_0-c)+\{-1,0,1\}\subseteq \mathcal Z_0\cup\{c\}\). A nonempty subset of a cyclic group with at least two points missing gains at least two points when its two neighboring translates are adjoined: the boundary of its cyclic intervals contains at least two distinct outside points. Therefore the left side has at least \(|\mathcal Z_0|+2\) points, while the right side has at most \(|\mathcal Z_0|+1\). This is impossible. The same argument applies to \(1-h\).

The next calculation bounds the number of adjacent scalar values with a large relative change. Put \(\kappa=(\mathcal A h)(0)\). Since \(\mathcal A\) and the shift by \(c\) each preserve the sum of an array, the two fault equations give \((\mathcal A h)(-1)=1-\kappa\). The strict bounds just proved imply \(0<\kappa<1\). For \(\varepsilon>0\), set

\[
J_\varepsilon(r)=
\log\bigl((\mathcal A h)(r)+\varepsilon\bigr)
-\frac13\sum_{e=-1}^{1}\log\bigl(h(r+e)+\varepsilon\bigr).
\]

Concavity of logarithm makes every \(J_\varepsilon(r)\) nonnegative. Summing on the cyclic residue group, using the harmonic equations at all indices except \(-1,0\), gives the exact identity

\[
\sum_r J_\varepsilon(r)
=\log\frac{(\kappa+\varepsilon)(1-\kappa+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)}.
\]

The only triples containing the zero value \(h(c)=0\) have centers \(c-1,c,c+1\). Put \(a=h(c-2)\) and \(b=h(c+1)\); these and \(h(c+2)\) are strictly positive. Subtract those three terms from the last identity, and then let \(\varepsilon\) decrease to zero. Writing \(J(r)\) for the finite limit at every other center gives

\[
\sum_{r\notin\{c-1,c,c+1\}}J(r)
=\log\frac{27\kappa(1-\kappa)a^{1/3}b^{2/3}h(c+2)^{1/3}}
{(1+a)(1+b)(b+h(c+2))}.
\]

This subtraction is from the global regularized identity. It therefore remains valid when a fault index \(-1\) or \(0\) coincides with one of the three subtracted centers, for any value of \(c\).

For the positive values \(a,b,h(c+2)\), elementary one-variable maximization gives

\[
\frac{a^{1/3}}{1+a}\le\frac{2^{2/3}}3,
\qquad
\frac{b^{2/3}h(c+2)^{1/3}}{b+h(c+2)}\le\frac{2^{2/3}}3.
\]

The maxima occur at \(a=1/2\) and \(h(c+2)=b/2\), respectively. Using also \(\kappa(1-\kappa)\le1/4\) and \(1/(1+b)\le1\), we obtain

\(\sum_{r\notin\{c-1,c,c+1\}}J(r)\le\log(3/2^{2/3})\).

If any two positive entries of a triple have ratio greater than two, its logarithmic Jensen difference exceeds \(\frac13\log(9/8)\). Indeed, after scaling those entries to \(1,t\), with \(t>2\), the difference is minimized over the third entry at \((1+t)/2\). Its value there is \(\frac23\log((1+t)/(2\sqrt t))>\frac13\log(9/8)\).

The exact inequality

\(\log(3/2^{2/3})<17\cdot\frac13\log(9/8)\)

is equivalent to \(3^{31}>2^{49}\); it follows, for example, by multiplying the square of \(3^{12}=531441>524288=2^{19}\) by \(3^7=2187>2048=2^{11}\). Consequently at most sixteen of the nonsubtracted centers have a triple with ratio greater than two. Apply the same calculation to \(1-h\), reflecting the residue coordinate so that its adjacent boundary values are in the same order. At most sixteen of its nonsubtracted centers are bad as well.

The exceptional center sets for the two laws are \(\{c-1,c,c+1\}\) and \(\{c-2,c-1,c\}\), whose union has four elements. Associate to the adjacent pair \((i-1,i)\) the triple centered at \(i-1\). We have proved that, except for at most thirty-six indices \(i\) modulo \(m\), both

\[
\frac12\le\frac{h(i)}{h(i-1)}\le2,
\qquad
\frac12\le\frac{1-h(i)}{1-h(i-1)}\le2.
\]

We now control the individual sink translates, rather than just their two orbit totals. Reduction of \(K_0\) modulo \(p\) has Fourier transform \(3/(4-e^{2\pi\mathrm i k/p})\) at frequency \(k\). Its modulus is

\(\rho_k=(1+(16/9)\sin^2(\pi k/p))^{-1/2}\).

Fourier inversion on the finite cyclic group shows that the sum of \(t\) independent such rotations has probability at any prescribed residue between

\(p^{-1}(1-\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\rho_k^t)\) and \(p^{-1}(1+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\rho_k^t)\).

Conditional on an entire quotient path and its exit choices, the successive \(K_0\)'s remain independent with this law. The additional multiples of \(m\) in the displayed exit formulas merely translate their sum modulo \(p\). Thus the same bounds hold conditionally for the final sink translate within its chosen orbit. Since \(0<\rho_k<1\) at every nonzero frequency, if absorption cannot occur before the \(L\)th cycle exit, and \(\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\rho_k^L<2/7\), then every sink \(s\) in an orbit \(\mathcal O\) of positive total absorption probability satisfies

\[
\frac{5}{7p}\mu_Y(\mathcal O)
<\mu_Y(s)
<\frac{9}{7p}\mu_Y(\mathcal O),
\qquad
\mu_Y(\mathcal O):=\sum_{s\in\mathcal O}\mu_Y(s).
\]

For primes \(p\ge11\), take \(L=p^2\). For \(1\le k\le(p-1)/2\), concavity gives \(\sin(\pi k/p)\ge2k/p\) and \(\log(1+(16/9)u)\ge u\log(25/9)\), for \(0\le u\le1\). Therefore

\(\rho_k^{p^2}\le(81/625)^{k^2}\).

Pairing conjugate frequencies and putting \(q_0=81/625\), we get

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\rho_k^{p^2}
\le2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_0^{k^2}
\le\frac{2q_0}{1-q_0^3}
<\frac27.
\]

Here \(k^2\ge1+3(k-1)\) for every positive integer \(k\). The final strict inequality is equivalent to \(q_0^3+7q_0<1\), which follows already from \(q_0<2/15\), since \((2/15)^3+14/15<1\). This is a full frequency-sum estimate; no factor growing with \(p\) is hidden in it.

For \(p=7\), take \(L=22\). To give an exact trigonometric bound, \(x=\cos(2\pi/7)\) is the root in \((1/2,1)\) of \(8x^3+4x^2-4x-1\). The polynomial is strictly increasing there, and its value at \(5/8\) is \(1/64>0\), so \(x<5/8\). The polynomial identity follows by expanding \(1+2\cos(2\pi/7)+2\cos(4\pi/7)+2\cos(6\pi/7)=0\). Every nonzero frequency has cosine at most \(x\), hence \(\rho_k^2<3/4\), and

\(\sum_{k=1}^{6}\rho_k^{22}<6(3/4)^{11}<2/7\).

The last inequality is the integer comparison \(21\cdot3^{11}=3720087<4194304=4^{11}\).

It remains to find a scalar-good index whose two parent walks cannot be absorbed too early. Starting at a \(Z\) residue \(r\), after \(t\) quotient steps its residue is \(r-tc+e\), with \(-t\le e\le t\). A walk from \(V_i\) first exits at residue \(i\) or \(i-1\). Thus it is sufficient to exclude the following set of indices:

\[
\bigcup_{t=0}^{L-1}
\bigl\{(t+1)c+e\pmod m:-t-1\le e\le t+1\bigr\}.
\]

Its cardinality is at most \(\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}(2t+3)=L(L+2)\). Outside it, the walk starting at \(Z_{i-1}\), and the walk from either first exit of \(V_i\), need at least \(L\) quotient steps to reach a sink residue. Each quotient step is one retained cycle exit, and the initial exit from \(V_i\) only increases that number.

Exclude also the at most thirty-six scalar-bad indices and the one deleted-cycle residue \(i=0\). There remains an admissible index whenever \(m>L(L+2)+37\). For \(p=7\), this is \(585>22\cdot24+37=565\). For \(p\ge11\), it follows from

\(4^{p-1}>p^5+2p^3+37p+1\).

At \(p=11\), the two sides are \(1048576\) and \(164121\). The inequality persists when the integer \(p\) increases: the polynomial on the right grows by at most \(((p+1)/p)^5\le(12/11)^5<4\), while the left side grows by four. This proves the required bound for every larger prime as well.

Choose such an \(i\). For orbit \(\{A_j\}\), the retained cycle equations and invariance under translation by \(m\) give

\(\mu_{V_i}(\{A_j\})=(2h(i)+h(i-1))/3\).

For the other orbit, replace \(h\) by \(1-h\). The scalar-good property therefore gives, for each orbit \(\mathcal O\),

\[
\frac23\le
\frac{\mu_{V_i}(\mathcal O)}{\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(\mathcal O)}
\le\frac53.
\]

Both orbit totals are positive. Apply the individual-translate bounds to both parents. For each individual sink \(s\in\mathcal O\), they yield

\[
\frac{\mu_{V_i}(s)}{\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(s)}
<\frac95\cdot\frac53=3,
\qquad
\frac{\mu_{Z_{i-1}}(s)}{\mu_{V_i}(s)}
<\frac95\cdot\frac32=\frac{27}{10}<3.
\]

This proves the claimed comparison for the retained equation \(2U_i=V_i+Z_{i-1}\).

Finally assign arbitrary rational sink positions. Every realized label is a convex combination of those positions, and all sink positions remain among the labels; hence their common convex hull is \(P\). Rational invertibility of the harmonic system makes every label rational. A common denominator therefore puts \(\mathcal L\) inside a dilation of the ambient integer lattice, so \(B=P\cap\mathcal L\) is finite.

The coefficient vectors of \((3V_i-Z_{i-1})/2\) and \((3Z_{i-1}-V_i)/2\) are strictly positive and sum to one, by the comparison. Both extrapolations lie in \(P\). They lie in \(\mathcal L\) as well, since they equal \(2V_i-U_i\) and \(2Z_{i-1}-U_i\). Set \(v=V_i-U_i\in\mathcal L\). The five points \(U_i-2v,U_i-v,U_i,U_i+v,U_i+2v\) all belong to \(B\). If the two parent positions do not coincide, then \(v\ne0\), the five points are distinct, and the five oriented displacements \(-2v,-v,0,v,2v\) establish \(r_B^{\mathcal L}(U_i)\ge5\). Otherwise \(V_i=Z_{i-1}\), which is the first alternative.
