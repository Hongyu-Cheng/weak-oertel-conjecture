## Statement

For every pair of integers \(n,d\geq 1\), let
\[
N:=3^n,\qquad T:=3^{n/2},\qquad P:=[-1,1]^n,
\]
and, with \(t\) denoting one continuous coordinate, define
\[
\widetilde C_n:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(P\times[-1,0],\,\{(\mathbf 0,T)\}\bigr)
\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R.
\]
With the convention that \([-1,1]^0\) is a singleton of \(0\)-dimensional volume \(1\), put
\[
C_{n,d}:=\widetilde C_n\times[-1,1]^{d-1}
\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d.
\]
Then \(C_{n,d}\) is a full-dimensional convex body. Let \(\mu_{n,d}\) be the probability measure obtained by normalizing the mixed volume on
\[
S_{n,d}:=C_{n,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
where, for measurable \(A\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{\mathrm{mix}}(A)
:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in A\}.
\]
The barycenter of \(\mu_{n,d}\) is \(p=(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\in S_{n,d}\), but its closed-halfspace Tukey depth satisfies
\[
\operatorname{depth}_{\mu_{n,d}}(p)
\leq \frac{1}{3^{n/2}+1}
=o(1/n).
\]
Consequently there is no universal \(c>0\) such that every such centered mixed measure has barycenter depth at least \(c/n\). On the other hand,
\[
\frac{1}{3^{n/2}+1}\bigg/2^{-n}\longrightarrow+\infty,
\]
so this family is not a counterexample to a \(c_0 2^{-n}\) lower bound.

## Proof

The set \(P\times[-1,0]\) already has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\), so \(\widetilde C_n\) is a compact, full-dimensional convex set. Hence \(C_{n,d}\), its product with the full-dimensional box \([-1,1]^{d-1}\), is a full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

For \(y\in P\), the vertical fiber of \(\widetilde C_n\) at \(y\) is
\[
(\widetilde C_n)_y
=\bigl[-1,\,T(1-\|y\|_\infty)\bigr].
\]
Indeed, the base prism supplies every height in \([-1,0]\). A point of positive height \(t\) belongs to the convex hull precisely when it can be written as
\[
(y,t)=(1-\lambda)(y',0)+\lambda(\mathbf0,T)
\]
with \(y'\in P\), which is equivalent to \(\lambda=t/T\) and
\(\|y\|_\infty\leq 1-\lambda\), or \(t\leq T(1-\|y\|_\infty)\). No point of the convex hull has height below \(-1\), and the same inequality gives the upper bound for every convex combination involving a point of \(P\times[-1,0]\).

The only lattice points of \(P\) are the \(N=3^n\) points of
\(\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Therefore the exact continuous fibers of \(C_{n,d}\) are
\[
(C_{n,d})_{\mathbf0}=[-1,T]\times[-1,1]^{d-1}
\]
at the origin and
\[
(C_{n,d})_z=[-1,0]\times[-1,1]^{d-1}
\]
at each of the other \(N-1\) lattice points \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\). All other lattice fibers are empty. Writing \(V:=2^{d-1}\), the central fiber has \(d\)-volume \(V(T+1)\), every other nonempty fiber has volume \(V\), and hence the total mixed volume is
\[
W=V\bigl((T+1)+(N-1)\bigr)=V(N+T).
\]

The numerator of the barycenter in the integer coordinates is zero. The central fiber has integer coordinate \(\mathbf0\), while the other fibers have equal weights and
\[
\sum_{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n}z=\mathbf0.
\]
The last \(d-1\) continuous coordinates also have zero barycenter because every fiber is a product with the centrally symmetric box \([-1,1]^{d-1}\). In the \(t\)-coordinate, the unnormalized first moment equals
\[
V\left(\int_{-1}^{T}t\,dt
+(N-1)\int_{-1}^{0}t\,dt\right)
=\frac V2\bigl(T^2-1-(N-1)\bigr)
=\frac V2(T^2-N)=0,
\]
because \(T^2=N\). Thus the mixed barycenter is
\(p=(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\), which belongs to the central fiber.

Consider the closed halfspace
\[
H:=\{(z,t,u):t\geq0\}.
\]
It contains \(p\) on its boundary. Its intersection with the central fiber has \(d\)-volume \(VT\). In every noncentral fiber, its intersection is
\(\{0\}\times[-1,1]^{d-1}\), which has \(d\)-volume zero. Consequently
\[
\mu_{n,d}(H)
=\frac{VT}{V(N+T)}
=\frac{T}{T^2+T}
=\frac1{T+1}
=\frac1{3^{n/2}+1}.
\]
Taking the infimum over all closed halfspaces containing \(p\) proves the asserted depth upper bound. Exponential decay gives
\[
n\,\operatorname{depth}_{\mu_{n,d}}(p)
\leq \frac{n}{3^{n/2}+1}\longrightarrow0,
\]
which rules out a universal \(c/n\) centered-barycenter lower bound. Finally,
\[
\frac{1/(3^{n/2}+1)}{2^{-n}}
=\frac{2^n}{3^{n/2}+1}
\sim\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}\right)^n\longrightarrow+\infty,
\]
so the displayed cap is still much heavier than the scale \(2^{-n}\) relevant to the locked target.
