## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For integers \(m,r\ge1\), define
\[
D_{m,r}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{r}:\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_i\le m\},
\qquad
p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x)
:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}}{Z(\boldsymbol\theta)},
\qquad
Z(\boldsymbol\theta):=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{m,r}}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},
\]
where \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r\) is arbitrary. For every \(\mathbf j\in D_{m,r}\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(D_{m,r}\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), and write \(H(\nu):=-\sum_{\mathbf x:\nu(\mathbf x)>0}\nu(\mathbf x)\log\nu(\mathbf x)\).

The exact aliases for accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 are \(A=D_{m,r}\), \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\theta\), \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}\), and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{D_{m,r},\mathbf j}\). The ambient dimension denoted by \(m\) in that fact is \(r\) here; throughout this statement, \(m\) is the simplex dilation. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathbf j}&:=\mathsf C_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf j)
=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)e^{H(\nu_{\mathbf j})},\\
B_2&:=\mathcal B_2(p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_{m,r}}p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^{2},\\
\omega_{\mathbf j}&:=\frac{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j)C_{\mathbf j}^{2}}{B_2},\\
V_{\mathbf j}&:=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
\left(\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)}
{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf X)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed capacity identity follows from bf1ff94487296374. Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_{m,r}}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}
\le \left(4+\frac{16}{3}e^2\right)r.}
\]
Moreover, every entropy row satisfies \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)\le2r\), including all proper faces. At each vertex, \(V_{\mathbf j}=0\).

## Proof

First, for every mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\) on \(D_{m,r}\), log-affinity gives
\[
D(\nu\|p_{\boldsymbol\theta})
=-H(\nu)-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j+\log Z(\boldsymbol\theta)
=-H(\nu)-\log p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf j).
\]
Thus the entropy minimizer in bf1ff94487296374 is precisely \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\), and that fact proves the stated capacity identity, also on proper faces. In particular, \(B_2>0\) and \(\omega\) is a probability law.

Introduce composition coordinates \(N_i=x_i\) for \(1\le i\le r\) and \(N_{r+1}=m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_i\). Their sum is always \(m\). When a law is evaluated at a full composition \(\mathbf n\), this means its pushforward under this bijection: its mass at \(\mathbf n\) is its original mass at \((n_1,\ldots,n_r)\). A permutation of these \(r+1\) coordinates gives an affine bijection of \(D_{m,r}\), preserves mean constraints, and preserves entropy. Under the resulting bijection of source laws and entropy rows, the capacity formula above shows that \(C_{\mathbf j}\), \(\omega_{\mathbf j}\), and \(V_{\mathbf j}\) are preserved at corresponding roots.

To specify the normalization exactly, put \((\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{r+1})=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_r,0)\), and choose a permutation \(\sigma\) with \(\beta_{\sigma(r+1)}=\max_h\beta_h\). The new root coordinates are \(\widetilde j_i=j_{\sigma(i)}\), where \(j_{r+1}:=m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}j_i\); the new point coordinates are \(\widetilde x_i=N_{\sigma(i)}\). The new source tilt is
\(\widetilde\theta_i=\beta_{\sigma(i)}-\beta_{\sigma(r+1)}=-a_i\), with \(a_i\ge0\). Subtracting the common coefficient only subtracts a constant from the composition score. Henceforth retain the canonical symbols for this relabeled law and its roots. We may therefore assume \(\theta_i=-a_i\le0\) for \(1\le i\le r\), with the source-favored slack at coordinate \(r+1\).

We prove the needed properties of exponential composition laws before using them. If a mean composition has positive coordinates precisely on an index set \(I\), every law with this mean has \(N_h=0\) almost surely for \(h\notin I\). An exponential law on its face has, for some \(q_h>0\), mass proportional to \(\prod_{h\in I}q_h^{N_h}\) on the nonnegative integer compositions of \(m\) using \(I\). Multiplying all \(q_h\) by a common number does not change this law. The maximum-entropy representation on every such face, and its extension to real means, are established below.

For a nonempty list of positive weights \(q_1,\ldots,q_s\), let
\(h_n(q_1,\ldots,q_s):=\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_s=n}\prod_{h=1}^{s}q_h^{n_h}\) for \(n\ge0\), where the sum is over nonnegative integer \(n_h\). This positive sequence is log-concave: \(h_n^2\ge h_{n-1}h_{n+1}\) for \(n\ge1\). Here is a direct proof. For one weight it is the sequence \(q_1^n\). Suppose \((b_n)\) is obtained by adding a weight \(q>0\) to a positive log-concave sequence \((d_n)_{n\ge0}\), so \(b_n=\sum_{t=0}^{n}q^td_{n-t}\). Since \(b_n=d_n+qb_{n-1}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_n^2-b_{n-1}b_{n+1}
&=d_nb_n-d_{n+1}b_{n-1}\\
&=d_nd_0q^n+
\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}q^t
\bigl(d_nd_{n-t}-d_{n+1}d_{n-1-t}\bigr)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, log-concavity makes \(d_{k+1}/d_k\) nonincreasing, and therefore \(d_{n-t}/d_{n-1-t}\ge d_{n+1}/d_n\) in every summand. Induction proves the assertion for all nonempty weight lists.

For an exponential composition law with at least two active coordinates, the marginal mass of \(N_i=k\), for an active \(i\), is proportional to \(q_i^k h_{m-k}((q_h)_{h\in I\setminus\{i\}})\), \(0\le k\le m\). The preceding log-concavity, reversal of the index, and the geometric factor show that this marginal is log-concave. Inactive coordinates are deterministic zero; with only one active coordinate, all coordinates are deterministic.

We record the moment consequence with its proof. For a finite nonnegative integer-valued law of mean \(\mu\), whose positive support is consecutive and whose masses are log-concave, write its mass as \(f_k\) and its survival probabilities as \(s_k=\mathbb P(Y\ge k)\), so \(s_0=1\). On the positive support, the ratios \(f_{k+1}/f_k\) are nonincreasing. For every fixed nonnegative \(t\), the products expressing \(f_{k+t}/f_k\) are consequently nonincreasing in \(k\), with zero terms beyond the support. Hence \(s_k/f_k\) is nonincreasing. It follows that \(f_k/s_k\) is nondecreasing and \(s_{k+1}/s_k=1-f_k/s_k\) is nonincreasing wherever defined. Before the positive support starts, the latter ratios are \(1\); once a survival probability is zero, all later ones are zero. Comparing products of survival ratios gives \(s_{u+v}\le s_us_v\) for all nonnegative integers \(u,v\). The tail-sum identities imply
\[
\frac{\mathbb E[Y(Y-1)]}{2}
=\sum_{k\ge2}(k-1)s_k
=\sum_{u,v\ge1}s_{u+v}
\le\left(\sum_{u\ge1}s_u\right)^2=\mu^2.
\]
Thus \(\mathbb EY^2\le2\mu^2+\mu\) and \(\operatorname{Var}Y\le\mu(\mu+1)\). The deterministic cases satisfy the latter inequality directly. In particular, every coordinate of every exponential composition law satisfies
\(\operatorname{Var}N_i\le(\mathbb EN_i)(\mathbb EN_i+1)\).

Distinct composition counts also have nonpositive covariance. Inactive coordinates contribute zero. With exactly two active coordinates, their sum is constant, so their covariance is minus either variance. With at least three active coordinates, fix distinct active \(i,j\), and let \(f(k,l)\) be their joint probability mass, extended by zero outside \(k,l\ge0\), \(k+l\le m\). On this triangle it is proportional to
\(q_i^kq_j^l h_{m-k-l}((q_h)_{h\in I\setminus\{i,j\}})\).
For nonnegative integers \(k<k'\) and \(l<l'\), this joint mass satisfies
\[
f(k,l)f(k',l')\le f(k,l')f(k',l).
\]
If \(k'+l'>m\), the left-hand side is zero. Otherwise all four points lie in the triangle. Put \(n=m-k-l\), \(d=k'-k\), and \(e=l'-l\). The geometric factors cancel, and the desired inequality is \(h_nh_{n-d-e}\le h_{n-d}h_{n-e}\). It follows by comparing the products of \(e\) successive decreasing ratios in \(h_n/h_{n-e}\) and \(h_{n-d}/h_{n-d-e}\). Finally, expansion with an independent copy of the joint pair gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(N_i,N_j)
=\sum_{k<k',\ l<l'}(k'-k)(l'-l)
\bigl[f(k,l)f(k',l')-f(k,l')f(k',l)\bigr]\le0.
\]
This identity is also obtained by grouping the terms in one half of the expectation of \((N_i-N_i')(N_j-N_j')\). All sums are finite. Thus no independence of counts within a row is assumed.

There is a further elementary comparison valid relative to any active slack coordinate, whether or not it is favored by the law. Fix distinct active \(i,s\), and put \(q=q_i/q_s\). For every integer \(k\ge1\), the map that transfers \(k\) units from \(N_i\) to \(N_s\) is an injection from \(\{N_i\ge k\}\) into the composition support. The mass of each point in the domain is \(q^k\) times the mass of its image. Therefore \(\mathbb P(N_i\ge k)\le q^k\). If \(q<1\), summing gives \(\mathbb EN_i\le q/(1-q)\). Consequently, whenever \(\mathbb EN_i=t>0\),
\[
\frac{q_i}{q_s}\ge\frac{t}{t+1}.
\]
For \(q\ge1\), this last inequality is immediate. In particular, if \(q_i\le q_s\), then \(\log(q_s/q_i)\le\log(1+1/t)\le1/t\).

We next justify the maximum-entropy representation and the entropy profile on all real means, including its boundary continuity. Extend \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) to \(\nu_{\mathbf y}\) for every real \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{conv}D_{m,r}\) by the same maximum-entropy problem, and define the profile \(H(\mathbf y):=H(\nu_{\mathbf y})\). Its composition mean has coordinates \(y_1,\ldots,y_r\) and \(y_{r+1}=m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}y_i\). Let \(I\) be the indices with \(y_h>0\). The feasible laws form a nonempty compact polytope: nonemptiness follows already by assigning probability \(y_h/m\) to the composition vertex \(m\mathbf e_h\). Entropy is continuous and strictly concave on the finite probability simplex, so it has a unique maximizer. Every feasible law is supported on the face with inactive coordinates zero.

There is a feasible law positive at every point of this face support. To construct it, put a sufficiently small positive multiple of the uniform law on the face support into the law. Subtracting its contribution from the mean leaves strictly positive coordinates on \(I\). The remaining normalized mean is in the same face and can be realized by a law on its vertices. The resulting mixture is positive throughout the face support. The entropy maximizer itself is positive there: mixing a maximizer having a zero mass with the preceding positive feasible law produces a positive contribution of order \(-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon\) at a zero mass, while changes at its positive masses are of order \(\varepsilon\), contradicting maximality for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\).

When \(|I|\ge2\), choose any \(s\in I\) as slack. The Lagrange multiplier equations, applied to the normalization and the mean constraints on the positive face support, now give
\[
\nu_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf n)
=\frac{\exp(\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{s\}}\lambda_i n_i)}
{Z_I(\boldsymbol\lambda)},
\qquad
Z_I(\boldsymbol\lambda)
:=\sum_{\substack{n_h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ h\in I\\
\sum_{h\in I}n_h=m}}
\exp\left(\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{s\}}\lambda_i n_i\right).
\]
Thus this is exactly the exponential composition law considered above, with \(q_s=1\) and \(q_i=e^{\lambda_i}\). The normalization and coordinate mean constraints are linearly independent, as can be checked on the vertices \(m\mathbf e_h\); hence these multiplier equations use precisely the independent face coordinates. Conversely, any such exponential law is the maximum-entropy law at its own mean: for any mean-matched law \(\nu\), its affine log density gives \(D(\nu\|\nu_{\boldsymbol\lambda})=H(\nu_{\boldsymbol\lambda})-H(\nu)\ge0\). Here \(\nu_{\boldsymbol\lambda}\) denotes the exponential law with the displayed parameter, and nonnegativity of relative entropy follows from \(\log t\le t-1\).

Writing \(F_I=\log Z_I\), direct differentiation of this finite sum gives \(\nabla F_I=\mathbb E\mathbf N\) in the coordinates indexed by \(I\setminus\{s\}\), and \(\nabla^2F_I=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf N)\) in those coordinates. This covariance matrix is positive definite. Indeed, a vector with zero quadratic form would give a linear combination of the independent coordinates constant at every support point, since the law is positive everywhere. Its value at \(m\mathbf e_s\) is zero, and its values at the other vertices force every coefficient to be zero. The parameter representing a given positive law is unique in this chart, also by evaluating score differences at these vertices. Strict concavity of entropy makes the row unique, so its parameter is unique as well.

The inverse function theorem applied to \(\nabla F_I\) now gives a smooth local parameter \(\boldsymbol\lambda(\mathbf y)\) around every mean in the relative interior of this face. The existence and uniqueness just proved identify the local inverse with the maximum-entropy parameter. In this chart,
\(H(\mathbf y)=F_I(\boldsymbol\lambda)-\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{s\}}\lambda_i y_i\). Differentiating, and using \(\nabla F_I=\mathbf y\), proves
\[
\frac{\partial H}{\partial y_i}=-\lambda_i
=-\log(q_i/q_s)
\qquad(i\in I\setminus\{s\}),
\]
where the slack mean adjusts to keep the sum of composition means equal to \(m\).

For completeness, \(H\) is continuous on the entire closed simplex, not just face interiors. Let real means \(\mathbf y^{(n)}\) tend to \(\mathbf y\), and append their slack coordinates. All vector operations in this continuity argument use these full \((r+1)\)-coordinate composition vectors. A convergent subsequence of their maximizing laws has a feasible limit with mean \(\mathbf y\); continuity of entropy on the finite probability simplex proves \(\limsup H(\mathbf y^{(n)})\le H(\mathbf y)\). For the converse, when \(\mathbf y^{(n)}\ne\mathbf y\), put
\(\varepsilon_n:=\max_{h:y_h>0}(y_h-y_h^{(n)})_+/y_h\).
Equality of the sums of all composition coordinates implies \(\varepsilon_n>0\). Also \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), and for sufficiently large \(n\) it is less than \(1\). The composition vector
\(\mathbf z^{(n)}=[\mathbf y^{(n)}-(1-\varepsilon_n)\mathbf y]/\varepsilon_n\)
is nonnegative and has sum \(m\). Choose a vertex-supported law of mean \(\mathbf z^{(n)}\). Its mixture with \(\nu_{\mathbf y}\), in proportions \(\varepsilon_n\) and \(1-\varepsilon_n\), has mean \(\mathbf y^{(n)}\) and converges to \(\nu_{\mathbf y}\). Its entropy tends to \(H(\mathbf y)\), so maximality proves the matching lower limit. Terms with \(\mathbf y^{(n)}=\mathbf y\) need no mixture. This proves continuity.

We can now bound both variance terms in an integer entropy row. In that row choose \(f\in I\) maximizing its exponential weight \(q_f\), and set \(u_h=\log(q_f/q_h)\ge0\) for \(h\in I\setminus\{f\}\). This is a choice depending on the row, distinct from the fixed source-favored slack. On its support,
\(\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)=\text{constant}-\sum_{h\in I\setminus\{f\}}u_hN_h\).
All active composition means \(j_h\), including \(j_{r+1}\), are positive integers. The transfer comparison proves \(u_h\le1/j_h\). The proved marginal variance and covariance inequalities therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)
&\le\sum_{h\in I\setminus\{f\}}u_h^2\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}N_h\\
&\le\sum_{h\in I\setminus\{f\}}
\frac{j_h(j_h+1)}{j_h^2}
\le2(|I|-1)\le2r.
\end{aligned}
\]
For a singleton \(I\) the variance is zero and the same bound holds. In the fixed source coordinates, \(a_i\ge0\), so nonpositive covariances likewise yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}\log p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf N)
&=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_iN_i\right)\\
&\le\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_i^2j_i(j_i+1)
\le2\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_ij_i)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(j_i\ge1\) whenever \(j_i>0\); a zero-mean coordinate is deterministic. The elementary inequality \(\operatorname{Var}(U-W)\le2\operatorname{Var}U+2\operatorname{Var}W\) consequently proves the row estimate
\[
V_{\mathbf j}\le4r+4\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_ij_i)^2.
\]

It remains to average the source terms. Fix \(i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\) and fix all the other root coordinates at a feasible nonnegative integer vector. Put \(M=m-\sum_{h\ne i,\ h\le r}j_h\). The possible values of \(j_i\) are exactly \(0,1,\ldots,M\). If \(M=0\), the contribution \((a_ij_i)^2\) is zero. Suppose \(M>0\), and consider the real profile along this line: \(y_i=t\), \(y_{r+1}=M-t\), with the other means fixed, for \(0\le t\le M\). The other zero means restrict to the appropriate face. For \(0<t<M\), both \(i\) and the source slack \(r+1\) are active, even if the row favors a different coordinate. The profile differentiation formula and the transfer comparison relative to this particular slack prove
\[
\frac{d}{dt}H(\mathbf y(t))
=-\log(q_i/q_{r+1})
\le\log(1+1/t).
\]
Define \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)=(t+1)\log(t+1)-t\log t\) for \(t>0\), and \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(0)=0\). Its derivative is \(\log(1+1/t)\). Thus \(H(\mathbf y(t))-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)\) is nonincreasing on \((0,M)\). The proved continuity of \(H\) and continuity of \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}\) extend this monotonicity to both endpoints.

The capacity formula shows that the unnormalized weights of \(\omega\) are \(e^{-3\sum_{h=1}^{r}a_hj_h+2H(\mathbf j)}\); the common factor \(Z(\boldsymbol\theta)^{-3}\) cancels. Therefore, conditional on the other root coordinates, the mass at \(j_i=k\) is proportional to
\[
e^{-3a_ik+2h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k)}g(k),
\qquad
g(k):=
\begin{cases}
e^{2[H(\mathbf y(k))-h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k)]},&0\le k\le M,\\
0,&k>M.
\end{cases}
\]
The function \(g\) on the nonnegative integers is bounded, nonnegative, nonincreasing, and positive at \(0\). If \(a_i=0\), the desired conditional cost is zero. Otherwise, for \(a=a_i>0\), let \(\pi_a\) be the probability law on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) whose mass is proportional to \(e^{-3ak+2h_{\mathrm{geom}}(k)}\).

This is a well-defined law with finite polynomial moments. Indeed, for \(t>0\),
\(h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)=\log(t+1)+t\log(1+1/t)\), whence \(\log(t+1)\le h_{\mathrm{geom}}(t)\le\log(t+1)+1\); the same bounds hold at \(t=0\). Weighting \(\pi_a\) by \(g\) can only decrease the expectation of an increasing function. To verify this directly, for independent \(K,K'\) of law \(\pi_a\) and any increasing integrable \(F\) with \(Fg\) integrable,
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_a}(F(K),g(K))
=\mathbb E[(F(K)-F(K'))(g(K)-g(K'))]\le0.
\]
Division by \(\mathbb E_{\pi_a}g(K)>0\) proves the comparison. Apply it with \(F(k)=(ak)^2\).

Put \(z=e^{-3a}\in(0,1)\). The bounds on \(h_{\mathrm{geom}}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_a}(aK)^2
\le e^2a^2\frac{Q(z)}{P(z)},
\qquad
P(z):=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)^2z^k,
\quad
Q(z):=\sum_{k\ge0}k^2(k+1)^2z^k.
\]
These sums are exact:
\[
P(z)=\frac{1+z}{(1-z)^3},
\qquad
Q(z)=\frac{4z(1+4z+z^2)}{(1-z)^5}.
\]
For example, apply \((z\,d/dz+1)^2\) to \((1-z)^{-1}\) to obtain \(P\), and then apply \((z\,d/dz)^2\) to obtain \(Q\); the power series are absolutely differentiable for \(|z|<1\). For \(0<z<1\), the inequality \((1+4z+z^2)/(1+z)\le3\) follows from \(2-z-z^2=(1-z)(2+z)\ge0\). Also
\[
\frac{z}{(1-z)^2}
=\frac{1}{4\sinh^2(3a/2)}
\le\frac{1}{9a^2},
\]
since \(\sinh u\ge u\) for \(u\ge0\), which follows by integration of \(\cosh u\ge1\). Consequently \(a^2Q(z)/P(z)\le4/3\). We have proved, for every feasible choice of the other integer root coordinates,
\[
\mathbb E_{\omega}\bigl[(a_ij_i)^2\mid(j_h)_{h\ne i,\ h\le r}\bigr]
\le\frac43e^2.
\]
This also holds for \(M=0\) or \(a_i=0\), as those costs vanish. Averaging and summing over the \(r\) source coordinates yields
\(\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}\sum_{i=1}^{r}(a_ij_i)^2\le(4/3)e^2r\).
Combining this with the row estimate proves
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}\omega_{\mathbf j}V_{\mathbf j}
\le4r+4\cdot\frac43e^2r
=\left(4+\frac{16}{3}e^2\right)r.
\]
All row estimates were on the active face support, so they include every integer boundary root. At a vertex, that support is a singleton and the likelihood random variable is constant, giving \(V_{\mathbf j}=0\).
