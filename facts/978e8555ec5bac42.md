## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), and let \(S=C\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing\). For every Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), define
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal H_d\!\left(S\cap A\cap
(\{\mathbf z\}\times\mathbb R^d)\right),
\]
so that \(\mu(A)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)\) in the mixed-integer-volume notation of the locked problem. For \(\mathbf y\in S\), let
\[
h_S(\mathbf y):=\inf\{\mu(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf y\in H\}.
\]
Then, writing \(m=n+d\) and \(\mathbb S^{m-1}=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^m:\|\mathbf u\|_2=1\}\), one has for every \(\mathbf y\in S\)
\[
h_S(\mathbf y)
=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{m-1}}
\mu\!\left(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x
\geq \mathbf u^\top\mathbf y\}\right).
\]
Equivalently, the defining infimum may be restricted to closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y\). Moreover, \(S\) is compact, \(h_S:S\to[0,\mu(\mathbb R^m)]\) is upper semicontinuous in the relative topology, and there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that
\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)=\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y).
\]

## Proof

The projection \(P=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(C)\) is compact, so \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite. For each \(\mathbf z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), the fiber
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}
\]
is compact and bounded, and hence has finite \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Thus the displayed sum defining \(\mu\) has only finitely many nonzero summands and defines a finite Borel measure. Also, \(\Lambda\) is closed, so \(S=C\cap\Lambda\) is compact; it is nonempty by hypothesis.

Fix \(\mathbf y\in S\). Every proper closed halfspace can be written as
\[
H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf a^\top\mathbf x\geq c\}
\quad\text{with }\mathbf a\neq\mathbf 0.
\]
If \(\mathbf y\in H\), then \(\mathbf a^\top\mathbf y\geq c\), and
\[
H_{\mathbf y}:=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf a^\top\mathbf x
\geq\mathbf a^\top\mathbf y\}
\]
has \(\mathbf y\in\partial H_{\mathbf y}\) and \(H_{\mathbf y}\subseteq H\). Hence \(\mu(H_{\mathbf y})\leq\mu(H)\). The boundary-through-\(\mathbf y\) halfspaces form a subfamily of all halfspaces containing \(\mathbf y\), so the infimum over all containing halfspaces is at most the infimum over that subfamily. The preceding construction gives the reverse inequality, because for every containing \(H\) it supplies a member \(H_{\mathbf y}\) of the subfamily with no larger measure. The two infima are equal. Normalizing \(\mathbf a\) gives the asserted infimum over \(\mathbb S^{m-1}\).

No zero-boundary-mass assertion is used here. Indeed, if
\[
H^+=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x\geq t\},
\qquad
H^-=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x\leq t\},
\]
then in general
\[
\mu(H^+)+\mu(H^-)
=\mu(\mathbb R^m)+
\mu(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x=t\}),
\]
because the two closed halfspaces both contain their common boundary. The boundary term may be positive for mixed-integer volume. Boundary reduction above uses only the inclusion \(H_{\mathbf y}\subseteq H\), so such mass causes no loss or double-counting.

It remains to prove upper semicontinuity. Fix \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{m-1}\) and set
\[
A_{\mathbf u}(t):=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x\geq t\},
\qquad
F_{\mathbf u}(t):=\mu(A_{\mathbf u}(t)).
\]
Suppose \(t_k\to t\). For each \(j\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), eventually \(t_k\geq t-1/j\), and therefore
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}F_{\mathbf u}(t_k)
\leq F_{\mathbf u}(t-1/j).
\]
The sets \(A_{\mathbf u}(t-1/j)\) decrease to \(A_{\mathbf u}(t)\). Since \(\mu\) is finite, continuity from above yields
\[
F_{\mathbf u}(t-1/j)\downarrow F_{\mathbf u}(t).
\]
For completeness, this continuity follows by applying countable additivity from below to
\(A_{\mathbf u}(t-1)\setminus A_{\mathbf u}(t-1/j)\), whose union is
\(A_{\mathbf u}(t-1)\setminus A_{\mathbf u}(t)\), and then subtracting from the finite number \(\mu(A_{\mathbf u}(t-1))\). Letting \(j\to\infty\) proves
\[
\limsup_{k\to\infty}F_{\mathbf u}(t_k)\leq F_{\mathbf u}(t),
\]
so \(F_{\mathbf u}\) is upper semicontinuous. Crucially,
\[
\bigcap_{j\geq1}A_{\mathbf u}(t-1/j)=A_{\mathbf u}(t)
\]
includes the boundary hyperplane \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u^\top\mathbf x=t\}\); any mass on that hyperplane is retained in the limit.

Define
\[
\phi_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf y)
:=F_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf u^\top\mathbf y).
\]
The map \(\mathbf y\mapsto\mathbf u^\top\mathbf y\) is continuous, hence every \(\phi_{\mathbf u}\) is upper semicontinuous. By boundary reduction,
\[
h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{m-1}}
\phi_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf y).
\]
For every \(\alpha\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\{\mathbf y\in S:h_S(\mathbf y)\geq\alpha\}
=\bigcap_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{m-1}}
\{\mathbf y\in S:\phi_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf y)\geq\alpha\},
\]
an intersection of relatively closed subsets of \(S\). Thus \(h_S\) is upper semicontinuous.

Finally, let \(M=\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)\), which is finite because \(0\leq h_S\leq\mu(\mathbb R^m)\). Choose \(\mathbf y_k\in S\) with \(h_S(\mathbf y_k)\to M\). Compactness of \(S\) gives a subsequence converging to some \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\). Upper semicontinuity gives
\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\geq
\limsup_{k\to\infty}h_S(\mathbf y_k)=M,
\]
so \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)=M\), as claimed.
