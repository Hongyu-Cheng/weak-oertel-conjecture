## Statement

Let $N\ge3$, $d=N-1$, $J=\lceil N/2\rceil$, $q=N-J+1$, and let $P$ be an $N\times N$ nonnegative column-stochastic matrix. Write its row maxima in decreasing order as $r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_N$. Then
$$|\det P|\le N^{N/2}2^{-d}\prod_{i=2}^N r_i\le N^{N/2}2^{-d}r_J^q.$$
Consequently, if $|\det P|\ge2^{-d}a$ with $a>0$, then $r_J\ge a^{1/q}N^{-N/(2q)}$. Put $b=1/N$, $\delta_0=N^{-N/(2q)}-b>0$, and $\delta=\delta_0/2$. If $a\ge((b+\delta)/(b+\delta_0))^q$, at least $J$ rows have maximum at least $b+\delta$.

If a $d$-simplex $S$ lies inside a $d$-simplex $T$, its vertex matrix in the barycentric coordinates of $T$ is nonnegative column-stochastic and has absolute determinant $|S|/|T|$.

## Proof

Permute the rows to arrange their maxima, changing at most the sign of the determinant. Replace the first row by the sum of all rows, which is the all-ones row. This replacement preserves the determinant. From each remaining row $i$, subtract $r_i/2$ times the first row. Every entry then has absolute value at most $r_i/2$, so the row's Euclidean norm is at most $\sqrt N r_i/2$. Hadamard's inequality gives the first bound. Since entries of a column-stochastic nonnegative matrix are at most one, $r_i\le1$. The first $J-2$ factors in the product are at most one, and the remaining $q$ are at most $r_J$, proving the second bound. The determinant lower bound yields the assertion about $r_J$ by cancellation and taking a positive $q$-th root. Because $q=\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1>N/2$, one has $N^{-N/(2q)}>1/N$, so $\delta_0>0$. Substituting the stated lower bound on $a$ yields $r_J\ge b+\delta$.

For the simplex assertion, form augmented vertex matrices with a top row of ones. The augmented vertex matrix of $S$ is that of $T$ multiplied by the barycentric matrix. Absolute augmented determinants equal $d!$ times the respective simplex volumes. Their ratio is the claimed determinant. Nonnegativity and unit column sums are exactly the barycentric characterization of points in $T$.
