## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be a convex body, and put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
\[
w_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0.
\]
For every Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\), write
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}
\mathcal H_d\bigl(\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\bigr),
\]
and for \(p\in S\) write
\[
h_S(p):=\inf\{\mu(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) satisfy \(w_{z_0}>0\) and
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W4
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(z_0\). Assume \(w_z<W/4\) for every \(z\). Let \(y_0\in C_{z_0}\) maximize \(h_S((z_0,y))\) over \(y\in C_{z_0}\), and put
\[
t:=h_S((z_0,y_0)).
\]
If \(t<W/64\), then there are constants \(\eta>0\) and \(\varepsilon_0>0\) such that every Euclidean unit normal \(u=(a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) for which
\[
H_u:=\{(z,x):a^\top(z-z_0)+b^\top(x-y_0)\geq0\}
\]
satisfies
\[
\mu(H_u)\leq t+\varepsilon_0
\]
also satisfies
\[
\|b\|_2\geq\eta.
\]

## Proof

The projection of \(C\) to \(\mathbb R^2\) is compact, so
\[
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}
\]
is finite. Put \(w_0:=w_{z_0}\), let \(c_0\) be the centroid of the \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(C_{z_0}\), and define
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
\theta_d:=1-\gamma_d,
\]
\[
q_d:=\gamma_d-\theta_d\log\frac1{\theta_d}
=\gamma_d+(1-\gamma_d)\log(1-\gamma_d).
\tag{1}
\]
Every ambient boundary-through-\((z_0,c_0)\) halfspace contains at least \(\gamma_dw_0\) from the anchor fiber. Indeed, if its continuous normal is zero, it contains that fiber in full; otherwise its restriction to the anchor fiber is a closed halfspace through \(c_0\), and Grünbaum's centroid inequality applies. Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` permits the defining infimum to be restricted to boundary-through-point halfspaces. Since \(y_0\) maximizes depth on \(C_{z_0}\),
\[
t\geq h_S((z_0,c_0))\geq\gamma_dw_0.
\tag{2}
\]

We record the strict constant needed below. The inequality
\[
d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1
\]
gives \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\). For \(0<x<1\), set
\[
q(x):=x+(1-x)\log(1-x).
\]
Then \(q(0)=0\), \(q'(x)=-\log(1-x)>0\), and integration of the power series for \(-\log(1-x)\) gives
\[
q(x)=\sum_{m\geq2}\frac{x^m}{m(m-1)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
e
=1+1+\frac12+\frac16+\sum_{j\geq0}\frac1{(4+j)!}
<\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{j\geq0}5^{-j}
=\frac{87}{32}
<\frac{11}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_d
&=q(\gamma_d)>q(e^{-1})\\
&>\frac1{2e^2}+\frac1{6e^3}\\
&>\frac8{121}+\frac{32}{3993}
=\frac{296}{3993}
>\frac1{15}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \(0<q_d<\gamma_d<1\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that the asserted pair \((\eta,\varepsilon_0)\) does not exist. For every \(j\geq1\), choose a Euclidean unit normal
\[
u_j=(a_j,b_j)
\]
such that
\[
\|b_j\|_2<\frac1j,\qquad
\mu(H_{u_j})\leq t+\frac Wj.
\tag{6}
\]
Every \(H_{u_j}\) is a closed halfspace containing \((z_0,y_0)\), so the definition of \(t\) and (6) give
\[
t\leq\mu(H_{u_j})\leq t+\frac Wj.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(\|u_j\|_2=1\) and \(b_j\to0\), after passing to a subsequence,
\[
a_j\longrightarrow a,\qquad \|a\|_2=1.
\tag{8}
\]
Define only the positive open-side mass
\[
P:=\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\a^\top(z-z_0)>0}}w_z.
\tag{9}
\]
This is the mass that passes safely to the limit. To see this, put
\[
R:=\max\{\|x-y_0\|_2:(z,x)\in C\}<\infty.
\]
For each \(z\in D\) occurring in (9), the positive number
\(a^\top(z-z_0)\) dominates both
\(\|(a_j-a)\|_2\|z-z_0\|_2\) and \(\|b_j\|_2R\) for all sufficiently large \(j\). Hence the entire fiber \(\{z\}\times C_z\) is contained in \(H_{u_j}\) eventually. There are only finitely many such fibers, so (7) yields
\[
P\leq t.
\tag{10}
\]
No closed-boundary mass has been put into \(P\); such mass need not converge along (8).

Consider the lattice kernel
\[
L:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^2:a^\top v=0\}.
\tag{11}
\]
It is saturated: if \(mv\in L\) for a nonzero integer \(m\), then \(v\in L\). Since \(a\neq0\), its rank is zero or one.

First suppose \(\operatorname{rank}L=0\), the irrational-normal case. Then the boundary line
\[
\{z:a^\top(z-z_0)=0\}
\]
contains exactly the one lattice point \(z_0\). The proper closed halfspace
\[
Q:=\{z:a^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
therefore has weighted mass exactly \(P+w_0\). The marginal centerpoint condition and (10) give
\[
w_0\geq\frac W4-P\geq\frac W4-t.
\tag{12}
\]
Combining (2), (5), and (12) gives
\[
t\geq\gamma_d\left(\frac W4-t\right)
>\frac1{15}\left(\frac W4-t\right).
\]
Thus \(16t>W/4\), or \(t>W/64\), contrary to the hypothesis.

It remains to treat \(\operatorname{rank}L=1\), the rational-normal case. Let \(\ell\in\mathbb Z^2\) be a primitive generator of \(L\), so
\[
\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:a^\top(z-z_0)=0\}
=z_0+\mathbb Z\ell.
\tag{13}
\]
Choose \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(c^\top\ell=1\). Since \(D\) is finite, there is a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\) such that \(a+\delta c\) and \(a-\delta c\) are nonzero and have the same sign as \(a\) at every \(z-z_0\) with \(z\in D\setminus(z_0+\mathbb R\ell)\). On the boundary line, the two perturbed functionals have signs respectively equal to the signs of \(k\) and \(-k\) at \(z_0+k\ell\). Thus the two proper closed halfspaces
\[
Q_+:=\{z:(a+\delta c)^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\},
\qquad
Q_-:=\{z:(a-\delta c)^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
have weighted masses exactly
\[
P+A_+,\qquad P+A_-,
\]
where the two closed-ray masses, each retaining the anchor fiber, are
\[
A_+:=\sum_{k\geq0}w_{z_0+k\ell},
\qquad
A_-:=\sum_{k\leq0}w_{z_0+k\ell}.
\tag{14}
\]
Applying the marginal centerpoint condition to these two finite tilts and using (10) gives
\[
A_+\geq\frac W4-P\geq\frac W4-t,
\qquad
A_-\geq\frac W4-P\geq\frac W4-t.
\tag{15}
\]
This counts the closed boundary fibers exactly: \(k=0\) occurs in both ray sums.

We now prove the required anchored rank-one bound directly. Restrict \(C\) to
\[
E:=(z_0+\mathbb R\ell)\times\mathbb R^d
\]
and use coordinates \((s,x)\mapsto(z_0+s\ell,x)\). Put
\[
D_k:=C_{z_0+k\ell},\qquad
\alpha_k:=\mathcal H_d(D_k)\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z),
\tag{16}
\]
so \(\alpha_0=w_0\). We claim that every ambient boundary-through-\((z_0,c_0)\) halfspace has mass at least
\[
q_d\min\{A_+,A_-\}
\tag{17}
\]
on the lattice fibers of \(E\).

The restriction of such a halfspace to \(E\) is all of \(E\), or is a relative boundary-through-\((0,c_0)\) halfspace
\[
G:=\{(s,x):\rho s+b^\top(x-c_0)\geq0\}
\tag{18}
\]
with \((\rho,b)\neq(0,0)\). The all-of-\(E\) case contains both rays. If (18) is proper and \(b=0\), it contains one entire closed ray. In either case its line-slice mass is at least
\(\min\{A_+,A_-\}\), and hence at least (17), because \(0<q_d<1\).

Suppose \(b\neq0\). Let the opposite cap in the relative convex slice be
\[
B:=C\cap E\cap
\{(s,x):\rho s+b^\top(x-c_0)\leq0\},
\]
and define
\[
B_k:=D_k\cap
\{x:\rho k+b^\top(x-c_0)\leq0\},
\qquad
\beta_k:=\mathcal H_d(B_k).
\tag{19}
\]
Convexity gives the midpoint inclusions
\[
\frac12(D_{k-1}+D_{k+1})\subseteq D_k,
\qquad
\frac12(B_{k-1}+B_{k+1})\subseteq B_k.
\tag{20}
\]
Brunn--Minkowski followed by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
\alpha_k^2\geq\alpha_{k-1}\alpha_{k+1},
\qquad
\beta_k^2\geq\beta_{k-1}\beta_{k+1}.
\tag{21}
\]
If an outer term vanishes, the corresponding inequality is immediate. If two sections in either sequence have positive \(d\)-volume, convexity and Brunn--Minkowski at every intermediate parameter show that all intermediate integer sections have positive \(d\)-volume. Thus both positive supports are intervals. Compactness of \(C\) makes both sequences finitely supported. Hence the zero-extended sequences \((\alpha_k)\) and \((\beta_k)\) are nonnegative summable log-concave sequences in the standard discrete sense.

The cutting hyperplane in (18) has zero \(d\)-volume in every fiber because \(b\neq0\). Applying Grünbaum's inequality to both closed sides through the centroid \(c_0\) of \(D_0\) gives
\[
\beta_0\geq\gamma_d\alpha_0>0,
\qquad
\alpha_0-\beta_0\geq\gamma_d\alpha_0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\beta_0\leq\theta_d\alpha_0.
\tag{22}
\]
Log-concavity at zero gives
\[
\beta_0^2\geq\beta_{-1}\beta_1.
\]
Thus at least one of \(\beta_{-1},\beta_1\) is at most \(\beta_0\). Choose \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) accordingly. If the chosen neighbor is zero, interval support makes every later term on that ray zero. Otherwise, the successive-ratio characterization of log-concavity shows that the ratios along the chosen ray are nonincreasing and start at most at one. In either case,
\[
\beta_{\sigma k}\leq\beta_0\leq\theta_d\alpha_0
\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{23}
\]
The \(G\)-mass in fiber \(\sigma k\) is
\(\alpha_{\sigma k}-\beta_{\sigma k}\). From (23),
\[
\alpha_{\sigma k}-\beta_{\sigma k}
\geq(\alpha_{\sigma k}-\theta_d\alpha_0)_+
\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{24}
\]

Apply accepted fact `772688f4504b1b5b` with threshold \(\theta_d\) to
\[
u_k:=\frac{\alpha_{\sigma k}}{\alpha_0}\qquad(k\geq0).
\]
The preceding interval-support, summability, and log-concavity checks verify all its hypotheses, and \(u_0=1\). Its coefficient is exactly \(q_d\) from (1). Summing (24) and applying that fact gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu\bigl(G\cap((z_0+\mathbb Z\ell)\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr)
&\geq\sum_{k\geq0}
(\alpha_{\sigma k}-\theta_d\alpha_0)_+\\
&\geq q_d\sum_{k\geq0}\alpha_{\sigma k}\\
&\geq q_d\min\{A_+,A_-\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
This proves (17) in every case.

The line-slice mass in (17) is a submass of the full ambient mixed mass. Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` again reduces the depth at \((z_0,c_0)\) to ambient boundary-through-point halfspaces. Therefore (15), (17), and fiberwise maximality give
\[
t\geq h_S((z_0,c_0))
\geq q_d\min\{A_+,A_-\}
\geq q_d\left(\frac W4-t\right).
\tag{26}
\]
By (5) and \(W/4-t>0\),
\[
t>\frac1{15}\left(\frac W4-t\right),
\]
which again rearranges to \(t>W/64\), a contradiction.

Both possible ranks of (11) are impossible. Hence the assumed failure of uniformity is impossible. Indeed, failure for every \(\eta,\varepsilon_0>0\) produced the diagonal sequence (6), while the preceding compactness argument excludes every such sequence. Consequently some \(\eta>0\) and \(\varepsilon_0>0\) have the asserted property.

\geq
(1-\lambda)\mathcal H_d(X)^{1/d}
+\lambda\mathcal H_d(Y)^{1/d}.
\]
Applicability: all positive-volume sets to which it is applied above are compact convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\), their Minkowski combinations are compact, and convexity supplies the containments in the indicated middle fibers. Vanishing outer volumes are handled separately.
