## Statement

Take a positive compatible family and fixed energy-only buffered cap groups as in the preceding parity-face theorem. In particular, $\varepsilon>0$ is fixed, $Q$ is the old raw maximum, $I$ is the set of old deepest bases, $J_i$ cover $I$, and the selected caps have fixed normals $N_{ij}=(a_{ij},u_{ij})$ and actual mass $Q$ at thresholds $c_{ij}$. Use the same $P_i,h_i,\rho_{ij}$ as in that theorem. Let $K_z(t)$ be positive compatible bodies on the same bases with $K_z(t)\to K_z$ in Hausdorff distance as $t\downarrow0$. Write $\mu_t,M_t,\Gamma_t,f_t,b_{\varepsilon,t}$ for their actual measure, mass, covariance norm, energy-only profile and raw buffered depth. Put
$$
A_i(t)=\sum_j\rho_{ij}\Gamma_t(u_{ij}),\qquad
\alpha_i(t)=\min_{z\in J_i,\ x\in K_z(t)}
[P_i(z,x)-h_i-\varepsilon A_i(t)f_t(z,x)].
$$
Then $A_i(t)\to1$, $\alpha_i(t)\to0$, and the minimizing marks approach the finite old contact set $\{(z,y_z):z\in J_i\}$. For each $i,j$ let $c_{ij}(t,q)$ be the right upper $q$-quantile of $\mu_t$ in the fixed normal $N_{ij}$. For small $t$ there is a unique solution $q_i(t)$ near $Q$ to
$$
\sum_j\rho_{ij}c_{ij}(t,q_i(t))=h_i+\alpha_i(t).
$$
It tends to $Q$, and for all small $t$ one has
$$
b_{\varepsilon,t}(z,x)\le q_i(t)\quad (z\in J_i,\ x\in K_z(t)),\qquad
M_t\beta_\varepsilon(K(t))\le\max_i q_i(t).
$$

## Proof

Positive Hausdorff limits give convergence of the masses and all first and second moments, hence of $\Gamma_t(u_{ij})$. The old normals are $\Gamma$-unit, so $A_i(t)\to1$. The minimum exists by continuity of marked energy [b3c0088cf09b2894](b3c0088cf09b2894.md) and compactness. Its lower limit is nonnegative by taking a convergent subsequence of minimizing marks at one base, applying marked continuity, and using the nonnegative old margin. Each old contact is own-interior and stays in the corresponding new fiber by support-function convergence, so testing it gives upper limit at most zero. Any subsequential limit of minimizing marks is an old zero, and the old zeros on the sampled assigned fibers are precisely the stated finite set.

For each selected cap its old cutting plane crosses an interior ball in its source fiber. Nearby fibers retain a smaller such ball, so their cap tails in this normal are continuous and strictly decreasing on a common threshold neighborhood of $c_{ij}$. Continuity follows because the nonzero continuous normal gives zero mass to any plane. Strict decrease follows from the positive mass of each intervening slab in the retained ball. Symmetric-difference convergence of the bodies gives uniform convergence of these tails in the threshold. In particular, the neighboring inverse intervals contain a common open neighborhood of $Q$ for small $t$, and the local inverse thresholds are the right quantiles since their tails strictly decrease locally. They are continuous and strictly decreasing in $q$ there. At any fixed $q$ in a sufficiently small such interval, the inverses converge to the old inverses, by bracketing their thresholds and tail convergence. At $t=0$, the weighted sum of the inverses equals $h_i$ at $Q$ and is strictly decreasing. Bracket $Q$ between two fixed nearby levels and use $\alpha_i(t)\to0$ and the intermediate value theorem to obtain the unique solution between them. Arbitrarily close brackets give $q_i(t)\to Q$.

For a current assigned point, the definition of the minimum and the scalar equation give
$$
\sum_j\rho_{ij}
[N_{ij}\cdot(z,x)-\varepsilon f_t(z,x)\Gamma_t(u_{ij})
-c_{ij}(t,q_i(t))]\ge0.
$$
Some term is nonnegative. In that direction the actual through-mark buffered cap is contained in the cap of mass $q_i(t)$, so gives the claimed bound throughout the current fiber. At each base outside $I$, the old maximum is strictly below $Q$. Apply [f2ead43a963c7026](f2ead43a963c7026.md) at multiplier one to the same finite positive support. Its uniform comparison on matched marks controls the current maximum at each base by the old one plus $o(1)$, since each new mark has an old mark within the fiber Hausdorff distance. The finitely many bases outside $I$ therefore retain a common strict gap below $Q$. As the $J_i$ cover $I$ and all $q_i(t)\to Q$, the bound for the full maximum follows.
