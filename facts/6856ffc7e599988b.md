## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $P,Q$ be disjoint finite integer base sets. For small $\varepsilon\ge0$, suppose there is an actual compact convex body with complete sampled fibers $K_z$ at $z\in P$ and $x_q+\varepsilon^{1/d}D_q$ at $q\in Q$, where the $K_z,D_q$ are compact convex bodies of positive $d$-volume and $\sum_q |D_q|=1$. Put $M=\sum_z|K_z|>0$ and $m_q=|D_q|$. Then the mixed mass is $M+\varepsilon$. If $q$ is exposed in $\operatorname{conv}(P\cup Q)$, every point in its new fiber has raw depth at most $\varepsilon m_q$ both for ordinary halfspace depth and for outward smoothing in which pure-base caps remain ordinary.

Suppose all $q$ are exposed, and the zero-birth body $C_{\rm ext}$ contains a compact convex body $C_{\rm old}$ whose only sampled bases are $P$, with the same old fibers. Then $(1-t)C_{\rm old}+tC_{\rm ext}$, $0\le t\le1$, has the same mixed measure and global maximum depth throughout, for ordinary depth or the fixed outward-smoothing model with width determined by the sampled measure.

## Proof

The dilation of each $D_q$ multiplies $d$-volume by $\varepsilon$. At zero the new fibers are singletons of zero $d$-volume. By exposure there is a linear functional $\ell$ with $\ell(q)>\ell(z)$ for every other sampled base $z$. The pure-base cap $\ell(z)\ge\ell(q)$ contains every point at $q$ and has mass exactly $\varepsilon m_q$. This also proves the stated bound in the smoothed model.

Each body on the preliminary Minkowski segment contains $C_{\rm old}$ (use the same point in both terms) and is contained in $C_{\rm ext}$ by convexity and nesting. The two endpoint bodies have identical fibers on $P$, so all intermediate bodies have them too. No other sampled base can occur except those in $Q$. At a base in $Q$ the fiber can only be empty or its prescribed singleton. Thus the ambient measure and any smoothing widths determined by it are unchanged. Every appearing singleton has depth zero by the same exposure cap, while every old point has its original depth. Hence the maximum is unchanged. The argument allows singleton fibers to appear at intermediate times.
