## Statement

Let $m\ge1$ be an integer, let $d\ge1$, and let $\Xi$ have any probability law on $\{u\in\mathbb R_+^m:\sum_j u_j=1\}$. For $x\in\mathbb R_+^m$ put $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot\Xi\le t)$ and
$$
\phi(x)=\inf_{\substack{t>0\\F_x(t)>0}}\frac{t}{F_x(t)^{1/d}}.
$$
Then $\phi$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing, positively homogeneous of degree one, continuous on $\mathbb R_+^m$, and satisfies $0\le\phi(x)\le\max_j x_j$. For each fixed finite positive number of rows and fixed nonnegative weights $\omega_i$, the objective $\sum_i\omega_i\phi(X_i)$ attains a maximum over the nonnegative matrices with $m$ columns whose column sums all equal one. It has a maximizer with the fewest nonzero rows.

## Proof

If $x\le y$ coordinatewise then $F_x(t)\ge F_y(t)$ for every $t$. Each threshold with $F_y(t)>0$ is admissible for $x$ and gives a ratio for $x$ at most its ratio for $y$. This proves monotonicity. For $\alpha>0$, the identity $F_{\alpha x}(\alpha t)=F_x(t)$ gives $\phi(\alpha x)=\alpha\phi(x)$. At $x=0$ all positive thresholds have probability one, so $\phi(0)=0$. The threshold $\max_j x_j$ captures probability one when $x\ne0$, giving the stated upper bound.

Suppose $x^{(n)}\to x$ and let $\epsilon_n=\max_j|x_j^{(n)}-x_j|$. For each fixed $t>0$ with $F_x(t)>0$, coordinatewise monotonicity and $\sum_j\Xi_j=1$ give
$$
\phi(x^{(n)})\le\phi(x+\epsilon_n\mathbf 1)
 \le\frac{t+\epsilon_n}{F_{x+\epsilon_n\mathbf 1}(t+\epsilon_n)^{1/d}}
 =\frac{t+\epsilon_n}{F_x(t)^{1/d}}.
$$
Take the upper limit and then the infimum in $t$. If $x\ne0$, let $\delta_n=\epsilon_n/\min_{j:x_j>0}x_j$. For sufficiently large $n$, $0\le\delta_n<1$ and $x^{(n)}\ge(1-\delta_n)x$ coordinatewise, including at each zero entry. Thus $\phi(x^{(n)})\ge(1-\delta_n)\phi(x)$, giving the lower limit. If $x=0$, use $0\le\phi(x^{(n)})\le\max_j x_j^{(n)}$.

The stochastic domain is a finite product of compact simplices, so the continuous objective attains its maximum. The row counts of its maximizers form a nonempty finite set of integers with a minimum.
