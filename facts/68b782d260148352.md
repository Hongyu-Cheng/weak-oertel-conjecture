## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body of area $M$. Suppose three open halfplanes cover $C$, their corresponding closed halfplanes have caps of common area $a=\tau M$, and the common closed intersection $T$ of the halfplanes is contained in $C$. The area $E$ covered exactly once is
$$
E=2M-3a+|T|.
$$
Thus $E\le3a/2$ is equivalent to $|T|\le(9\tau/2-2)M$.

If $T$ is a nondegenerate triangle, the three halfplanes are given by its barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i\ge0$. For $h\ge0$, the body $C=\{x:\lambda_i(x)\ge-h\text{ for all }i\}$ has $M=(1+3h)^2|T|$ and $a=(1+2h)^2|T|$. In this family $\sqrt{|T|}=3\sqrt a-2\sqrt M$.

## Proof

The boundary lines have area zero. The areas covered exactly once, twice and three times are therefore $E$, some $D\ge0$, and $|T|$, respectively. The cover gives $E+D+|T|=M$, while integrating multiplicity gives $E+2D+3|T|=3a$. Eliminate $D$ to get the claimed identity and equivalence.

The affine coordinates $\lambda_i$ identify the plane with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. For the outer body the lower bounds on all coordinates are $-h$. Shifting by these lower bounds leaves nonnegative coordinates summing to $1+3h$, so this body is homothetic to the standard coordinate simplex by that scale factor. In the cap with index $i$, the lower bound on $\lambda_i$ is instead zero, and the other two are still $-h$. The same translation and scaling argument gives scale $1+2h$. This proves the two area formulas, and their square roots give $3\sqrt a-2\sqrt M=\sqrt{|T|}$. Every point is in an open halfplane, since the barycentric coordinates sum to one. The formulas include $h=0$.
