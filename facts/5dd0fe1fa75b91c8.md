## Statement

Let \(m,k,h\ge1\) and \(L\ge0\) be integers, let \(\boldsymbol\beta\in\mathbb R^m\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^k\) be arbitrary finite vectors, and put \(N=m+k\). Define
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^k:
             \textstyle\sum_i x_i+h\sum_j y_j\le L\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=
 \frac{e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}}
 {\sum_{(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf u+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf v}}.
\]
Use the actual closed-halfspace depth and its original-root moment, with exactly the normalization of ac4157e446425a3d and f1b85039a5de43a6:
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\]
where \(H\) ranges over closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\). There is an absolute \(\kappa>0\) such that \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-N}\) for every choice above.

One explicit choice uses exactly the constants \(a,\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}},\beta_{\mathrm{complex}},g\) defined in f1b85039a5de43a6. Set
\[
C_{\mathrm{band}}=50000000,
\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{gain}}=\min\{\tfrac32\log g,\log2\}>0,
\]
and take
\[
\kappa=\min\left\{a^3,
 \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3}{16C_{\mathrm{band}}^4}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{9\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}^2}{2\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}}
                  -\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}}{2}\right)
 \left(\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}}{24}\right)^{12}\right\}.
\]
The parameter \(g\) here is the minimum rank-gain constant defined in that predecessor, not a partition function. In the application of its simplex notation \(D_{m_i,r_i}\), the dilation \(m_i\) will be a selected conditional budget, while the rank \(r_i\) will be \(m\) or \(k-1\).

## Proof

We first prove the required one-dimensional band estimate. Let \(K\ge1\), let \(r,s\ge0\) be integers, put \(n_*=r+s+1\), and let \(A,B\) be positive nondecreasing functions on \(\{0,\ldots,K\}\) such that
\[
\frac{A(v)}{A(u)}\le(v/u)^r,
\qquad \frac{B(v)}{B(u)}\le(v/u)^s
\quad(1\le u\le v\le K).
\tag{1}
\]
For any \(\eta\ge0\), let \(\mathcal Z=\sum_{t=0}^K e^{-\eta t}A(t)B(K-t)\) and let \(q(t)=e^{-\eta t}A(t)B(K-t)/\mathcal Z\). We claim there is a nonempty consecutive integer interval \(J=[v,w]\cap\mathbb Z\) satisfying
\[
\lambda_J:=\frac{|J|A(v)B(K-w)e^{-\eta w}}{\mathcal Z}
 \ge \frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{band}}n_*^3}.
\tag{2}
\]

If \(\eta\ge1/4\), then for \(t\ge8r+1\) with \(t+1\le K\), (1) and monotonicity give \(q(t+1)/q(t)\le e^{-\eta}(1+1/t)^r\le e^{-1/8}\). Shifting the tail by 32 therefore gives \(q(t\ge8r+33)\le e^{-4}<1/16\). At least \(15/16\) of the mass lies on at most \(8r+33\le33n_*\) sites. A singleton at a largest such atom has \(\lambda_J=q(J)\ge1/(36n_*)\), which proves (2) in this case.

Suppose \(0\le\eta<1/4\), and define \(\sigma=K\) if \(\eta=0\), and \(\sigma=\min\{K,1/\eta\}\) otherwise. If \(0<\eta<1/4\), the mean obeys \(\mathbb E_q t\le2(r+1)/\eta\le2n_*/\eta\). Here is a proof including a possible atom at zero. By (1), \(A(t)/t^r\) is nonincreasing for \(t\ge1\), as is \(B(K-t)\). Their product is thus a decreasing likelihood weight relative to \(t^r e^{-\eta t}\). Multiplication by a nonincreasing positive function cannot increase the mean: for independent copies \(T,T'\), the identity \(2\operatorname{Cov}(T,f(T))=\mathbb E[(T-T')(f(T)-f(T'))]\) gives this assertion. Truncation at \(K\) also cannot increase the mean of a nonnegative variable. Finally, if \(S_j=\sum_{t\ge1}t^j e^{-\eta t}\), integration over \([t-1,t]\) and \([t,t+1]\) gives
\[
S_r\ge e^{-\eta}\frac{r!}{\eta^{r+1}},
\qquad S_{r+1}\le e^\eta\frac{(r+1)!}{\eta^{r+2}},
\qquad S_{r+1}/S_r\le e^{2\eta}(r+1)/\eta<2(r+1)/\eta.
\]
Conditioning on \(t\ge1\) gives the mean comparison, and an atom at zero can only reduce the unconditional mean.

Take \(B_*=K\) when \(\eta=0\), or when \(\eta>0\) and \(K\le1/\eta\); otherwise take \(B_*=\min\{K,\lceil72n_*/\eta\rceil\}\). The mean bound and Markov's inequality give \(q(t>B_*)\le1/36\), including the cases where the tail is empty. In every case \(B_*\le73n_*\sigma\). If \(\sigma\le4096n_*\), at least \(35/36\) of the mass lies on at most \(299009n_*^2\) sites. A singleton with probability at least \(35/(36\cdot299009n_*^2)\) proves (2).

It remains to consider \(\sigma>4096n_*\). Put \(a_*=\lfloor\sigma/(4096n_*)\rfloor\) and \(d_*=\lfloor K/(4096n_*)\rfloor\), so \(1\le a_*\le d_*\). For \(0\le t<a_*\) and \(1\le j\le64\), the site \(t+ja_*\) lies below \(K\). Monotonicity of \(A\), the growth bound for \(B\), and \(\eta\sigma\le1\) give
\[
\frac{q(t+ja_*)}{q(t)}
 \ge e^{-\eta ja_*}
 \left(\frac{K-t-ja_*}{K-t}\right)^s
 \ge e^{-1/(64n_*)}
       \left(1-\frac{65}{4096n_*}\right)^s
 >\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]
For the last inequality use \(e^{-x}\ge1-x\) and \((1-x)^s\ge1-sx\); the product is at least \((63/64)(1-65/4096)>1/2\). The 64 shifted copies of \(\{0,\ldots,a_*-1\}\), together with the original set, are disjoint. Hence \(q(t<a_*)\le1/33\).

For the other endpoint, use \(u=K-t\). For \(0\le u<d_*\) and \(1\le j\le64\), monotonicity of \(B\), the growth bound for \(A\), and the favorable exponential factor give
\[
\frac{q(K-u-jd_*)}{q(K-u)}
 \ge\left(1-\frac{65}{4096n_*}\right)^r>\frac12.
\]
All these sites are valid, because \(u+jd_*\le65K/(4096n_*)<K\). Disjoint shifted copies again give \(q(K-t<d_*)\le1/33\). Thus the consecutive central set \(a_*\le t\le\min\{B_*,K-d_*\}\) has mass at least \(1-2/33-1/36>7/8\).

Partition that central set into consecutive blocks of at most \(n=\max\{1,\lfloor a_*/(16n_*)\rfloor\}\) sites. One has \(n-1\le a_*/(16n_*)\), \(n\ge a_*/(32n_*)\), and \(a_*\ge\sigma/(8192n_*)\). Consequently the number of blocks is at most
\[
\frac{B_*}{n}+1
 \le4672\cdot4096\,n_*^3+1
 <20000000\,n_*^3.
\]
One block \(J=[v,w]\cap\mathbb Z\) has \(q(J)\ge7/(160000000n_*^3)\). On that block, (1) gives \(A(w)/A(v)\le e^{1/16}\) and \(B(K-v)/B(K-w)\le e^{1/16}\), because \(v\ge a_*\), \(K-w\ge d_*\ge a_*\), and \(w-v\le n-1\). Also \(\eta(w-v)\le1/16\). Comparing the minimum product in (2) with the maximum weight on the block yields \(\lambda_J\ge e^{-3/16}q(J)\ge q(J)/2\). Since \(7/(320000000)>1/50000000\), this proves (2). The band estimate is proved for all its parameters.

We next establish the partition-function growth used in that estimate. For an integer \(r\ge0\), a vector \(\boldsymbol\zeta\in(-\infty,0]^r\), and an integer budget \(R\ge0\), write
\(F_{\boldsymbol\zeta}(R)=\sum_{\mathbf z\ge0,\,\sum z_i\le R}e^{\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\mathbf z}\).
This is nondecreasing in \(R\). For \(1\le R_1\le R_2\), it satisfies
\[
\frac{F_{\boldsymbol\zeta}(R_2)}{F_{\boldsymbol\zeta}(R_1)}
 \le\frac{\binom{R_2+r}{r}}{\binom{R_1+r}{r}}
 \le(R_2/R_1)^r.
\tag{4}
\]
To prove the first inequality, augment a uniform simplex point by its slack, obtaining a uniform composition of \(R\) into \(r+1\) coordinates. Given a composition \(\mathbf n\) of \(R\), add one to coordinate \(i\) with probability \((n_i+1)/(R+r+1)\). The resulting composition is uniform at total \(R+1\): for any composition \(\mathbf n'\) of \(R+1\), its probability is
\(\binom{R+r}{r}^{-1}\sum_i n'_i/(R+r+1)=\binom{R+r+1}{r}^{-1}\).
This couples successive uniform simplex points with every retained coordinate nondecreasing. Therefore their expectation of \(e^{\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\mathbf z}\) is nonincreasing. Since that expectation equals \(F_{\boldsymbol\zeta}(R)/\binom{R+r}{r}\), the first inequality follows. The second follows by taking the product of \((R_2+i)/(R_1+i)\le R_2/R_1\) for \(1\le i\le r\). The argument includes \(r=0\), with empty products and partition function one.

We now give the exact affine reduction of the original law. Introduce the light slack \(x_0=L-\sum_i x_i-h\sum_j y_j\), and assign it score \(\beta_0=0\). Choose a favorite light index \(i_*\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) with score \(\beta_{i_*}=\max\{0,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m\}\). Let \(\mathbf z\) consist of the other \(m\) light coordinates, including \(x_0\) if \(i_*\ne0\), and let their tilt vector be \(\boldsymbol\zeta=(\beta_i-\beta_{i_*})_{i\ne i_*}\le\mathbf0\). Choose a favorite heavy index \(j_*\) with \(\theta_{j_*}=\max_j\theta_j\). Put \(U=\sum_j y_j\), let \(\mathbf v=(y_j)_{j\ne j_*}\), and put \(\boldsymbol\xi=(\theta_j-\theta_{j_*})_{j\ne j_*}\le\mathbf0\) and \(\gamma=\theta_{j_*}-h\beta_{i_*}\).

The map \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\mapsto(\mathbf z,\mathbf v,U)\) is invertible affine. Its inverse recovers \(y_{j_*}=U-\sum v_j\) and \(x_{i_*}=L-hU-\sum z_i\) when \(i_*\ne0\), or the original \(\mathbf x=\mathbf z\) when \(i_*=0\). Its support is exactly
\[
0\le U\le K:=\lfloor L/h\rfloor,
\qquad \mathbf z\ge0,\ \sum z_i\le L-hU,
\qquad \mathbf v\ge0,\ \sum v_j\le U.
\tag{5}
\]
The canceled constant in the weight is \(e^{\beta_{i_*}L}\). Thus the original probability in these affine coordinates, with no root reweighting, is
\[
P(\mathbf z,\mathbf v,U)=
 \frac{e^{\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\mathbf z+\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\mathbf v+\gamma U}}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{U=0}^K e^{\gamma U}F(L-hU)G(U),
\tag{6}
\]
where \(F=F_{\boldsymbol\zeta}\) has nominal rank \(m\), and \(G=F_{\boldsymbol\xi}\) has nominal rank \(k-1\). Empty heavy vectors when \(k=1\) cause no change. Invertible affine maps identify all containing closed-halfspace traces and their original probabilities, so \(M_3(P)=M_3(p)\).

If \(K=0\), the heavy coordinates are zero and the law is a tilted standard simplex of budget \(L\) and rank \(m\), or a singleton when \(L=0\). The standard-simplex and singleton cases of f1b85039a5de43a6 give \(M_3(p)\ge a^3 8^{-m}\ge a^3 8^{-N}\). Hence suppose \(K\ge1\), and write \(\ell=L-hK\ge0\).

If \(\gamma\ge0\), apply the band estimate with \(t=K-U\), \(\eta=\gamma\), \(A(t)=F(\ell+ht)\), \(B(t)=G(t)\), \(r=m\), and \(s=k-1\). The common factor \(e^{\gamma K}\) cancels from the total law. By (4), \(A(v)/A(u)\le((\ell+hv)/(\ell+hu))^m\le(v/u)^m\), and \(B\) has the required growth bound. If \(\gamma<0\), instead use \(t=U\), \(\eta=-\gamma\), \(A(t)=G(t)\), \(B(t)=F(\ell+ht)\), \(r=k-1\), and \(s=m\). In both cases \(n_*=N\). Translating the resulting interval back to \(U\) gives a nonempty consecutive interval \(I=[a_I,b_I]\cap\mathbb Z\) with
\[
\lambda:=\frac{|I|F(L-hb_I)G(a_I)\min_{U\in I}e^{\gamma U}}{Z}
 \ge\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{band}}N^3}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(Q_1\) be the original log-affine simplex law proportional to \(e^{\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\mathbf z}\) on \(\sum z_i\le R_1:=L-hb_I\), and let \(Q_2\) be the law proportional to \(e^{\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\mathbf v}\) on \(\sum v_j\le R_2:=a_I\). Let \(u_I\) be uniform on \(I\). The product \(V=Q_1\otimes Q_2\otimes u_I\) is supported inside (5). Equation (6) shows pointwise that \(P\ge\lambda V\): its atom ratio to the product law is exactly \(|I|F(R_1)G(R_2)e^{\gamma U}/Z\), whose minimum is (7).

We record the actual-depth operations, including their root weights. For any product law, a centered containing halfspace contains the event that each coordinate-block linear functional is nonnegative. Independence gives \(\tau_{Q_1\otimes Q_2}\ge\tau_{Q_1}\tau_{Q_2}\), and averaging cubes under the product root law gives \(M_3(Q_1\otimes Q_2)\ge M_3(Q_1)M_3(Q_2)\). A vanishing block normal has event probability one. Also, from \(P\ge\lambda V\), every containing halfspace has \(P\)-mass at least \(\lambda\) times its \(V\)-mass. Keeping the original root mass \(P(\mathbf z)\ge\lambda V(\mathbf z)\) yields
\[
M_3(P)\ge\lambda^4 M_3(V).
\tag{8}
\]
These are exactly the actual-depth operations in the proof of ac4157e446425a3d, with its domination coefficient replaced by the present \(\lambda\), and its two coordinate blocks replaced by the indicated three blocks. The one-dimensional calculation in that fact gives \(M_3(u_I)\ge1/32\), including singleton intervals.

We use the quantitative rank gain before its absorption in f1b85039a5de43a6. Here \(M_2(Q)=\sum_{\mathbf z}Q(\mathbf z)\tau_Q(\mathbf z)^2\) is exactly its \(\mathcal M_2(Q)\), while the present \(M_3\) is its \(\mathcal M_3\). Among \(Q_1,Q_2\), certify each positive-rank simplex with budget at least two, and let \(S_{\mathrm{cert}}\) be the sum of their ranks. The certified-product estimate proved there, with precisely these original laws, gives
\[
M_2(Q_{\mathrm{cert}})\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^2
 e^{S_{\mathrm{cert}}\log g-2\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cert}}}}
 4^{-S_{\mathrm{cert}}}.
\]
Jensen's inequality under the same original root law therefore gives
\[
M_3(Q_{\mathrm{cert}})\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3
 e^{\frac32S_{\mathrm{cert}}\log g-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{cert}}}}
 8^{-S_{\mathrm{cert}}}.
\tag{9}
\]
For an empty certified group its law is a singleton, and (9) still holds because \(\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}<1\).

Handle all other factors exactly by atoms. A nominal-rank-\(r\) simplex with budget zero has one atom and moment one, as does a rank-zero simplex with any budget. With budget one it has \(r+1\) affine-independent vertices; every vertex can be isolated by a closed halfspace, so its moment is exactly \(\sum_i p_i^4\ge(r+1)^{-3}\). For every integer \(r\ge1\), \((r+1)^3\le2\cdot4^r\): equality holds at \(r=1\), and the successive left-hand ratio is at most \(27/8<4\). It also holds at \(r=0\). Thus each uncertified factor, including nominal rank zero, satisfies \(M_3\ge\tfrac12\,2^r8^{-r}\). This bound is valid for budget zero as well, since its right side is \(\tfrac12 4^{-r}\le1\). At most two factors are uncertified. Their total nominal rank is \(N-1-S_{\mathrm{cert}}\), even when an actual support is a singleton.

Applying actual-depth supermultiplicativity to (9) and these atom bounds gives
\[
M_3(Q_1\otimes Q_2)\ge
 \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3}{4}
 e^{\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}(N-1)-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{N-1}}
 8^{-(N-1)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(\tfrac32\log g\ge\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}\), \(\log2\ge\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}\), and \(S_{\mathrm{cert}}\le N-1\). The factors \(1/4\) are harmless if fewer than two atom factors occur. Budgets zero and one have therefore been handled without any row certificate or capacity-to-depth substitution.

Combining (7), (8), (10), and the interval moment gives, with \(R=N-1\),
\[
M_3(P)\ge
 \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^3}{16C_{\mathrm{band}}^4}
 \frac{e^{\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}R-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt R}}{(R+1)^{12}}
 8^{-N}.
\tag{11}
\]
To absorb the polynomial and square-root losses, completing the square gives
\(\tfrac12\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}R-3\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt R\ge-9\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}^2/(2\rho_{\mathrm{gain}})\).
The inequality \(e^u\ge u\), applied to \(u=\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}(R+1)/24\) and raised to the twelfth power, gives
\[
\frac{e^{\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}R/2}}{(R+1)^{12}}
 \ge e^{-\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}/2}
       (\rho_{\mathrm{gain}}/24)^{12}.
\]
Substitution in (11) proves the second coefficient in the stated \(\kappa\); the \(K=0\) case proves the first. Returning through the invertible affine chart gives the bound for the original \(p\) and its actual containing closed halfspaces. Throughout, the original probability is used for every root average and every cap probability. No capacity-reweighted root law is introduced.
