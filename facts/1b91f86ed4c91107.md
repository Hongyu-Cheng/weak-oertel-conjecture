## Statement

Let \(L\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(1\le i\le L\), let \(n_i\ge1\) be an integer, let \(Q_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_i}\) be convex, and suppose that \(A_i=Q_i\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) is finite, nonempty, and centrally symmetric about zero. Assume that whenever distinct \(x,y\in A_i\) have \((x+y)/2\in\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), their midpoint is zero. No full-dimensionality or coordinate bound is imposed. For arbitrary \(\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\), retain the original laws and normalizers
$$
Z_i=\sum_{x\in A_i}e^{\ell_i\cdot x},\qquad
p_{i,x}=\frac{e^{\ell_i\cdot x}}{Z_i},\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^L n_i.
$$
All laws are extended by zero off their supports. With the exact actual ambient depth and moments of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
 \text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace,\\
M_2(P)&=\sum_J P_J\tau_P(J)^2,
&M_3(P)&=\sum_J P_J\tau_P(J)^3,
\end{aligned}
$$
the single absolute constant \(c_* =\frac14\exp(-48\cdot2^{80})\) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_*4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_*^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{1}
$$
The product support is its complete convex lattice census. Singleton factors, factors of lower affine dimension, and ambient dimensions \(n_i=1\) are included.

There are also the following one-factor scalar estimates, used in the proof. Suppress the factor index and put \(K=2^n\). Choose one representative of each nonzero antipodal pair and adjoin zero, obtaining \(B\), and put \(M=|B|\), \(h=2M-1\). Retain the full canonical reflected quantities of 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,
&\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},\\
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},
&V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_jq_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
The row sums include both nonzero orientations and the central atom once. Define \(A_0=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3\), \(\mu=M^{-1}\sum_{b\in B}\cosh(\ell\cdot b)\), and \(t=\log(2\mu)\). If \(A_0<1\), then, with \(d=-\log A_0\), \(g=\log(K^2b(p))\), and the absolute constant \(C=2^{40}\),
$$
g>0,\qquad d\le C e^{-t}g,\qquad \nu\le C t^2g.
\tag{2}
$$
These estimates use only the stated one-center hypotheses and the original affine law. All logarithms are natural.

## Proof

First fix one factor and suppress its index. Convexity, nonemptiness, and \(A=-A\) imply \(0\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\). If two distinct members of \(A\) have the same coordinatewise parity, their midpoint is integral and hence, by hypothesis, they are antipodal. The zero parity class consists only of zero: an even nonzero point and zero would have a nonzero integral midpoint. Each other occupied parity class consists of exactly one antipodal pair. Thus the representative set \(B\) is parity-injective and \(M\le K\). Moreover \(A=B\cup(-B)\), \(|A|=h=2M-1\), and
$$
R_0=A,\qquad R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\quad(j\ne0).
\tag{3}
$$
No Boolean-coordinate representation of \(B\) is being assumed.

Put \(Z_3=\sum_{x\in A}e^{3\ell\cdot x}\). Substitution into the canonical quantities gives the following identities with exactly the original normalizer \(Z\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(0)&=h/Z,&H_p(j)&=p_j\quad(j\ne0),\\
A_0&=K^2Z_3/Z^3,&K^2b(p)&=K^2(Z_3-1+h^2)/Z^3,\\
\kappa_0(x)&=1/h,&Y_0(x)&=\ell\cdot x,\\
V_0&=\frac2h\sum_{b\in B}(\ell\cdot b)^2,
&q_0&=\frac{h^2}{Z_3-1+h^2},\qquad \nu=q_0V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Every nonzero-center row has zero score and variance. Indeed \(\sqrt{p_xp_{-x}}=1/Z\), while every other row is a singleton by (3).

Assume for the scalar argument that \(A_0<1\). Hölder applied to the \(h\) original probabilities gives \(\sum_xp_x^3\ge h^{-2}\); hence \(h>K\), \(M>K/2\), and \(M\ge2\). The same inequality and \(h<2K\) give
$$
\frac14<A_0<1,\qquad 0<d<\log4<2.
\tag{5}
$$
In particular \(K\ge2\). For the auxiliary uniform average over \(B\), let \(c_b=\cosh(\ell\cdot b)\) and \(\mathcal V=\mathbb E_B(c_b-\mu)^2\). This average does not replace the original law \(p\). Since \(c_b\ge1\), \(\mu\ge1\), and the exact finite-sum identities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=2M\mu-1,\\
Z_3&=8M\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-6M\mu-1,\\
\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-\mu^3
&=\mathbb E_B[(c_b-\mu)^2(c_b+2\mu)]\ge2\mu\mathcal V.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
The second identity uses \(\cosh(3u)=4\cosh^3u-3\cosh u\), subtracting the central atom once. Activity and (6) imply
$$
K^2(8M\mu^3+16M\mu\mathcal V-6M\mu-1)
\le K^2Z_3<Z^3<(2M\mu)^3.
$$
Rearranging, and using \(M\le K\), gives
$$
\mathcal V<\frac12\left(\frac{M^2}{K^2}-1\right)\mu^2
 +\frac38+\frac1{16M\mu}\le\frac7{16}<1.
\tag{7}
$$
The summand at \(b=0\) in \(\mathcal V\) gives \((\mu-1)^2/M\le\mathcal V<1\). Consequently \(\mu<\sqrt M+1\le2\sqrt M\), so \(\mu^2<4M\le4K\).

Jensen gives \(\mathbb E_Bc_b^3\ge\mu^3\). The expression \(8M\mu^3-6M\mu-1\) is positive for \(M\ge1\), \(\mu\ge1\). Thus (6) and \(Z<2M\mu\) give
$$
A_0>\frac{K^2}{M^2}
 \left(1-\frac3{4\mu^2}-\frac1{8M\mu^3}\right)
\ge1-\frac7{8\mu^2}.
\tag{8}
$$
For the last inequality the parenthesis is positive, \(K/M\ge1\), and \(M\mu\ge1\).

Write \(E=K^2(h^2-1)/Z^3\), so \(K^2b(p)=A_0+E\). From \(M\ge2\), \(h^2-1=4M(M-1)\ge2M^2\), and (7),
$$
E>\frac{K^2}{4M\mu^3}\ge\frac M{4\mu^3}>\frac1{16\mu}.
\tag{9}
$$
Also \(2M/h\le4/3\). The increasing concave function \(u\mapsto\operatorname{arcosh}(u)^2\) on \([1,\infty)\) was proved concave in 445a9e668ee1a705. Applying it to the auxiliary uniform average over \(B\), and using \(\operatorname{arcosh}\mu\le\log(2\mu)=t\), proves
$$
V_0=\frac{2M}{h}\mathbb E_B(\ell\cdot b)^2
\le\frac43\operatorname{arcosh}(\mu)^2\le\frac43t^2.
\tag{10}
$$
This also covers \(\mu=1\), when every score vanishes.

We next establish a positive gap for \(g\), including the possibility \(M=K\). Use the canonical \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\) of 445a9e668ee1a705. The ordered-pair midpoint expansion, completeness, and affinity give the exact identity
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(p)
&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
 p_{(x+y)/2}\sqrt{p_xp_y}\\
&=\sum_{\rho\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}p(\rho+2\mathbb Z^n)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
For each same-parity pair its midpoint is in \(A\), and \(p_{(x+y)/2}=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\) under the original affine law. The zero parity class has mass \(p_0=1/Z\le1/h<1/M\). Cauchy--Schwarz on the other \(M-1\) occupied classes therefore yields
$$
\chi(p)\ge p_0^2+\frac{(1-p_0)^2}{M-1}
\ge\frac{4M-3}{h^2}.
\tag{12}
$$
For the second inequality, the function \(u^2+(1-u)^2/(M-1)\) decreases on \([0,1/M]\), and its value at \(u=1/h\) is \((4M-3)/h^2\). Since \(K\ge M\ge2\),
$$
K\chi(p)\ge\frac{M(4M-3)}{h^2}
=1+\frac{M-1}{h^2}\ge1+\frac1{8K}.
$$
Indeed \(M-1\ge M/2\) and \(h^2\le4M^2\). Jensen under the entire original \(p\) gives \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\), and consequently
$$
g=\log(K^2b(p))\ge2\log\left(1+\frac1{8K}\right)
\ge\frac1{8K}>0.
\tag{13}
$$
Here \(2\log(1+u)\ge u\) for \(0\le u\le1\).

For \(\mu\ge64\), let \(\delta=1-A_0\). Equations (8)--(9) imply \(0<\delta<7/(8\mu^2)\le E/4\), since \(\mu\ge64>56\). It follows that
$$
K^2b(p)=1-\delta+E\ge1+\frac34E,
\qquad
g\ge\log\left(1+\frac3{64\mu}\right)\ge\frac1{32\mu}.
\tag{14}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(\log(1+u)\ge u/(1+u)\), because \(3/(64\mu+3)\ge1/(32\mu)\) for \(\mu\ge64\). Equation (8) also gives \(d\le2/\mu^2\), by \(-\log(1-u)\le2u\) for \(0\le u\le1/2\). Thus
$$
d\le\frac{64}{\mu}g=128e^{-t}g.
\tag{15}
$$
For the price, use (4) and the definition of \(E\) to get
$$
q_0=\frac{h^2}{h^2-1}\frac E{K^2b(p)}
\le\frac98\frac E{K^2b(p)}
\le\frac32\frac{K^2b(p)-1}{K^2b(p)}
\le\frac32 g.
\tag{16}
$$
The middle inequality uses \(K^2b(p)-1\ge3E/4\); the last uses \(1-u^{-1}\le\log u\) for \(u\ge1\). Equations (10) and (16) prove \(\nu=q_0V_0\le2t^2g\) in this case.

For \(1\le\mu<64\), we have \(e^{-t}=1/(2\mu)>2^{-7}\). Since \(Z_3\ge1\), \(h>K\), \(M\le K\), and \(Z<2M\mu\), formula (4) gives
$$
K^2b(p)\ge\frac{K^2h^2}{Z^3}>\frac K{8\mu^3}.
\tag{17}
$$
If \(K\ge2^{22}=16\cdot64^3\), then (17) gives \(g>\log2>1/2\). In this subcase (5) implies \(d/(e^{-t}g)<2^9\), while (10) and \(q_0\le1\) imply \(\nu/(t^2g)<8/3\). If \(K<2^{22}\), (13) gives \(g>2^{-25}\). Then (5) gives \(d/(e^{-t}g)<2^{33}\), and (10) gives \(\nu/(t^2g)<(4/3)2^{25}<2^{26}\). Division by \(t^2\) is harmless because \(t\ge\log2>0\). These estimates together with (15)--(16) establish (2) with the common constant \(C=2^{40}\).

Return to the finite product. For each factor let \(K_i=2^{n_i}\) and \(A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\). Call it active if \(A_{0,i}<1\). For every inactive factor select singleton auxiliary rows at every center. The selected scaled energy is then \(A_{0,i}\ge1\), and its selected price is zero.

For each active factor retain \(t_i,d_i,g_i,\nu_i\) from (2), and group the active indices into bins
$$
I_m=\lbrace i:A_{0,i}<1,\ m\le t_i<m+1\rbrace,
\qquad G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i,\qquad m=0,1,2,\ldots.
\tag{18}
$$
Because \(t_i\ge\log2\), these bins contain all active indices. Only finitely many are nonempty. If \(G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2\), select the full canonical rows in every factor of \(I_m\); otherwise select singleton rows in every factor of \(I_m\). For a bin selected in full, its total selected price \(T_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}\nu_i\) obeys
$$
T_m\le C(m+1)^2G_m\le\frac14G_m^2,
\qquad G_m\ge2\sqrt{T_m}.
\tag{19}
$$
For a bin selected as singletons its lost logarithmic energy is bounded by
$$
\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i
\le C e^{-m}G_m
<4C^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2.
\tag{20}
$$
The bound is also valid as a weak inequality for an empty bin. The sum of the right sides over all nonnegative integers is at most \(D=48C^2\). Indeed, for \(0\le r<1\), differentiating the geometric series gives \(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\). With \(r=e^{-1}<1/2\), this is less than \(12\).

We now check the selected-row source for the actual original law. This is the reflection mechanism of 445a9e668ee1a705, reproved for the chosen auxiliary rows. Denote the selected rows by \(\widehat R_{i,j}\), equal either to \(R_{i,j}\) or to \(\lbrace j\rbrace\) according to the selection of its entire factor. Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}
 \sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat b_i&=\sum_jp_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_i(j)},
&\widehat q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2}{\widehat b_i},\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat V_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
\widehat\nu_i&=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j},
&S_g&=\sum_i\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
$$
All hatted quantities are auxiliary. The unhatted original probability remains, at every supported \(X=(x_i)\),
$$
P_X=\prod_i p_{i,x_i}=\frac{\prod_i e^{\ell_i\cdot x_i}}{\prod_i Z_i}.
\tag{22}
$$
A factor selected in full has \(\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i\) and \(\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i)=g_i\). A factor selected as singletons has \(\widehat H_i(j)=p_{i,j}\), \(\widehat\nu_i=0\), and \(K_i^2\widehat b_i=A_{0,i}\). Hence (19)--(20), together with the nonnegative logarithmic energies of inactive factors, imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g&\ge\sum_{m:\,G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2}G_m-D\\
&\ge2\sum_{m:\,G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2}\sqrt{T_m}-D
\ge2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}-D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
The last inequality is \(\sum_m\sqrt{T_m}\ge\sqrt{\sum_mT_m}\), including an empty sum.

For a supported center \(J=(j_i)\), put
$$
\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i},\quad
\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i),\quad
\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i},\quad
Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i),\quad
\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}.
$$
Every selected row is symmetric about its center. Its reflection preserves its auxiliary law and reverses its score. Conditional independence and direct substitution therefore give
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,\qquad
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\in\widehat R_J).
\tag{24}
$$
The last identity uses exactly (22), with every original normalizer preserved.

Any ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each distinct reflected pair \(X,2J-X\), and contains \(J\) itself. Summing over these disjoint orbits, counting both nonzero orientations and using half of the available central mass, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2 e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
The equality follows from (24). Jensen for \(e^{-u}\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, proves the final inequality. The bound holds for every containing closed halfspace and thus for \(\tau_P(J)\), with no rank assumption.

Let \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\). The exact factorization \(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b_i)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}\) and the identity \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\) yield, by squaring (25), averaging under the entire original \(P\), and applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz once more,
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
Since \(\prod_iK_i^2=4^N\), (23)--(26) give
$$
4^NM_2(P)\ge\frac14
 \exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right)
\ge\frac14e^{-D}=c_*.
$$
Jensen under the same original \(P\) gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving (1). If there are no active factors, the argument simply selects all singleton rows and has zero total price; if \(\mu=1\) in an active factor, (10) gives zero price. Thus these cases, as well as singleton factors, cause no exception.

Finally \(\operatorname{conv}(A_i)\subseteq Q_i\) gives \(\operatorname{conv}(A_i)\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i}=A_i\). The convex hull of the finite Cartesian product is the product of the factor convex hulls. Intersecting that product with \(\mathbb Z^N\) proves the complete product census. The proof used only parity counting and antipodal score algebra in each factor, without a Boolean representation or a quantitative cap-stability assertion.
