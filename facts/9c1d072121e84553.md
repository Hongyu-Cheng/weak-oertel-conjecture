## Statement

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be compact and convex. Equip $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$ with the sum of the original integer-fiber lengths and assume its total mass $M$ is positive. If its true mixed-length centroid has integer base coordinate, it belongs to $S$ and has raw mixed length strictly greater than $M/3$ in the infimum over all closed ambient halfspaces containing it. This allows any finite number of positive integer fibers and also allows zero-length fibers.

## Proof

The mixed centroid is an average of points in the compact convex set $C$. Its integer base makes it a point of $S$. Translate it to $(0,0)$. Zero-length fibers do not affect this centroid or any cap mass. The positive-width fibers form a consecutive integer interval by convexity. If just one has positive width, its midpoint has depth $M/2$.

Apply 1c6a3d4ab63f450c to the same mixed-length centroid. It supplies a finite nonvertical minimizing cap and a choice of that cap with either no wholly captured positive fiber, or a cusp-free symmetric proper-cut block $I=[-h,h]\cap\mathbb Z$ with homogeneous outside tails and at least one wholly captured exterior tail. In the first case the same fact gives strict depth greater than $M/3$. It also supplies the strict quadratic width estimate $Q>(2/3)mM$ for $Q=\sum_i w_i^2$ and $m=\max_i w_i$.

Consider the second case. Tighten the cap through the centroid if necessary, as in the minimizing-cap reduction. Shear or reflect the continuous coordinate to write it as $x\ge0$, preserving the integer base and length. Write $[l_i,r_i]$ for the positive fibers and $w_i=r_i-l_i$. The sequences $w_i$ and $r_i$ are concave on these sites by convexity. The true centroid gives
$$
\sum_i i w_i=0,\qquad \sum_i w_i r_i=Q/2,
$$
where the second equality follows by summing the continuous first moments $w_i(r_i-w_i/2)$. For $i\in I$ one has $l_i<0<r_i$. Put $k=2h+1$. The minimizing cap has mass $\sum_{i\in I}r_i$ plus the mass of its fully captured exterior fibers.

If $km\ge M$, the case $h=0$ would force a single positive fiber, already covered. For $h\ge1$ let $L_i=c+bi$ interpolate $r_{-h},r_h$, so $c>0$. Write $e_i=r_i-L_i$. Concavity gives $e_i\ge0$ on $I$ and $e_i\le0$ off $I$. The weighted base mean vanishes, and $\sum_{i\in I}i=0$, so
$$
\frac Q2=\sum_iw_i r_i
\le Mc+m\sum_{i\in I}e_i
=m\sum_{i\in I}r_i+c(M-km)
\le m\sum_{i\in I}r_i.
$$
The proper traces alone therefore exceed $M/3$.

If $km\le M$, compare the weights $w_i/M$ with the uniform law on $I$. Their signed difference is nonpositive on $I$, nonnegative outside and has total mass and first moment zero. For $h\ge1$, subtract the same affine chord $L_i$ from $r_i$ in the sum against that difference. Every term of the resulting sum is nonpositive by the chord sign on each region. Thus $\sum_{i\in I}r_i/k\ge\sum_iw_i r_i/M$. For $h=0$ this comparison is Jensen at the base mean zero. The proper mass is therefore at least $kQ/(2M)>km/3$. By centered-concave-slab-tail-one-third, each strict outer tail of these actual widths has mass at least $(M-km)/3$, using concavity on the positive finite interval and $\sum_i i w_i=0$. The minimizing cap captures one entire such tail, so the total is strictly greater than $M/3$.

The finite minimizer in 1c6a3d4ab63f450c already compares all closed containing halfspaces, including vertical ones with whole boundary fibers. Thus the strict inequality holds for the infimum itself.
