## Statement

Let $E_1,E_2,E_3,F_1,F_2$ be independent exponential random variables of mean one. Define $X=(E_1+E_2+18E_3)/20$, $X'=(2E_1+18E_3)/20$, and $Y=(F_1+F_2)/2$. Both positive coefficient totals and the negative coefficient total are one. The two smallest positive coefficients of $X$ are merged in $X'$, and
$$
\Pr(X'\ge Y)-\Pr(X\ge Y)=\frac{25}{1173942}>0.
$$
This refutes the claim that merging any two positive coefficients of a balanced signed-exponential sum can only decrease its nonnegative sign probability.

## Proof

The survival function of $Y$ on $x\ge0$ is $e^{-2x}(1+2x)$ by convolution of the two exponential densities of rate two. If $U=\sum_k a_k E_k$ and $L_U(t)=\prod_k(1+t a_k)^{-1}$ is its Laplace transform, then independence gives
$$
\Pr(U\ge Y)=1-L_U(2)\left(1+2\sum_k\frac{a_k}{1+2a_k}\right).
$$
Here $\mathbb E[Ue^{-2U}]=-L_U'(2)$ follows by differentiating the Laplace integral. For $X$ the product is $250/847$ and the sum $\sum_k a_k/(1+2a_k)$ is $127/308$. For $X'$ the product is $25/84$ and the sum is $17/42$. Substitution yields $\Pr(X\ge Y)=30094/65219$ and $\Pr(X'\ge Y)=407/882$. Their difference in the stated order is $25/1173942$.
