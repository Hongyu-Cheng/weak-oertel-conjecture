## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex body. Retain the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and \(h_S\). For every real base point \(\mathbf x\), set \(C_{\mathbf x}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in C\}\). At every integer index define

\[
\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}},\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I}),\qquad
D=\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf I}>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

Assume \(W>0\), so \(D\) is finite and nonempty. For \(\mathbf I\in D\), let \(U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}/w_{\mathbf I}\), exactly as in accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647. Empty and zero-volume fibers retain weight zero and are never assigned a uniform probability law. All logarithms are natural.

At a target \(\mathbf J\in D\), a finite scalar feasible scheme has components \(k\), each with a nonempty set \(E_k\subseteq D\) of distinct indices, positive coefficients \(\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\), and mass \(x_k\ge0\), such that

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}=1,\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J,\qquad
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le w_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D).
\tag{1}
\]

Supports and coefficient vectors may be arbitrary and may repeat between components. Every positive-coefficient source occurrence is charged the full \(x_k\); all these charges share the single system (1). The singleton \(E_k=\{\mathbf J\}\), with coefficient one, includes exactly the central mass allowed in 793210f8a4be3647.

A coupled scheme additionally chooses, for each component, an arbitrary joint probability law \(\Pi_k\) on \(\prod_{\mathbf I\in E_k}C_{\mathbf I}\) whose marginal at \(\mathbf I\) is exactly \(U_{\mathbf I}\). Thus the mass-\(x_k\) joint measure \(x_k\Pi_k\) has marginal exactly \(x_kU_{\mathbf I}\) at each occurring source. Let \(P_k\) be the law of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf X_{\mathbf I}\) under \(\Pi_k\). For \(M=\sum_kx_k>0\), set

\[
P=\frac1M\sum_kx_kP_k,\qquad
\delta=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})
=\int_{C_{\mathbf J}} f\log f\,dU_{\mathbf J},
\qquad f=\frac{dP}{dU_{\mathbf J}},
\tag{2}
\]

with \(0\log0=0\). A coupled scheme is admissible when its final law \(P\) has a bounded measurable Lebesgue density. No density, independence, or prescribed dependence structure is imposed on individual joint laws. Convexity puts every \(P_k\), and hence \(P\), in \(C_{\mathbf J}\); admissibility makes (2) finite and nonnegative. Define

\[
\mathcal E(C;\mathbf J)
=\sup_{\substack{\text{admissible finite coupled schemes at }\mathbf J\\M>0}}
\frac{M}{1+\delta},
\qquad
\mathcal E(C)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathcal E(C;\mathbf J).
\tag{3}
\]

This is the actual-mixture entropy score called \(E(C)\) in the source prompt. It is distinct from the exponential-capacity function \(E(p)\) used elsewhere. In particular, (3) is not restricted to the affinely independent support family denoted \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) in 793210f8a4be3647.

For the same scalar scheme, retain the canonical root cost

\[
L_k=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\right)^d}
\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\) be the maximum scalar mass at \(\mathbf J\) subject to (1) and \(L_k\le L\) whenever \(x_k>0\), for \(L\ge0\). Accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 proves that this unrestricted value is attained and equals its finite affinely independent packing program. With the notation of 839747fd50cbcae6, set

\[
\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L},
\qquad
\mathcal B_d(w)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w).
\tag{5}
\]

The semicolon in \(P_d\) merely displays its table argument. The continuous dimension in \(\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}\) is the fixed \(d\). The scalar \(a=\log2\) in 839747fd50cbcae6 is written \(\log2\) here; the function \(a(\mathbf x)\) below has the different, explicitly defined root-volume meaning.

Let \(\Omega=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C\). The function \(a(\mathbf x)=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf x})^{1/d}\) is finite, nonnegative, concave, and upper semicontinuous on all of \(\Omega\). Define

\[

\mathbf x\in\Omega,\quad 0\le y_i\le a(\mathbf x)\ (1\le i\le d)\}.
\tag{6}
\]

Then \(\widehat C\) is a compact convex body in the same \(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), and \(\mathcal L^d(\widehat C_{\mathbf I})=w_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\), including every zero weight. For each positive target, and then globally,

The upper bounds hold over all the schemes and couplings in (3). The same upper proof applies to any actual body whose positive integer fibers are \([0,w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}]^d\), whether or not its other fibers are specified by (6).

## Proof

The definitions of positive fibers and full-occurrence capacities agree with 793210f8a4be3647 and 839747fd50cbcae6. Summing (1) over \(D\) gives \(\sum_k|E_k|x_k\le W\), and therefore \(M\le W\). Moreover, at every source the aggregate marginal measure is

\[
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}(x_k\Pi_k)_{\mathbf I}
=\left(\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\right)U_{\mathbf I}
\le w_{\mathbf I}U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{15}
\]

This is one global measure-capacity inequality for each source, including the target when it occurs. It uses no barycentric coefficient in the charge.

Convexity gives \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}C_{\mathbf I}\subseteq C_{\mathbf J}\), so every joint law in the definition has its output supported in \(C_{\mathbf J}\). For an admissible final law with bounded Lebesgue density \(p\), its \(U_{\mathbf J}\)-density is \(f=w_{\mathbf J}p\) on that fiber. This density is bounded. The function \(s\log s\), extended by zero at zero, is bounded above and below on any bounded nonnegative interval, proving finiteness of \(\delta\). Jensen's inequality and \(\int f\,dU_{\mathbf J}=1\) give \(\delta\ge0\). The singleton of mass \(w_{\mathbf J}\) has output \(U_{\mathbf J}\) and \(\delta=0\). Thus the supremum in (3) is nonempty and \(w_{\mathbf J}\le\mathcal E(C;\mathbf J)\le W\). In particular all the score normalizations used below are legitimate.

We first establish the real-fiber regularity needed for (6), including zero-volume boundary fibers. Compactness of \(C\) makes \(\Omega\) compact and convex and puts every real fiber in one fixed box \([-R,R]^d\). If \(\mathbf x_j\to\mathbf x\) in \(\Omega\), closedness of \(C\) implies
\(\limsup_j\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf x_j}}(\mathbf y)\le\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf x}}(\mathbf y)\) for every \(\mathbf y\). Indeed, membership of \(\mathbf y\) in infinitely many of the fibers would put \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) in \(C\) by taking a subsequence. Fatou's lemma applied to the nonnegative functions \(\mathbf1_{[-R,R]^d}-\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf x_j}}\) therefore gives

\[
\limsup_j\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf x_j})
\le\int_{[-R,R]^d}\limsup_j\mathbf1_{C_{\mathbf x_j}}\,d\mathcal L^d
\le\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf x}).
\tag{16}
\]

Taking \(d\)-th roots proves upper semicontinuity of \(a\) on the entire \(\Omega\).

The root concavity can be obtained from the declared transport predecessor without a new external input. Fix distinct \(\mathbf x,\mathbf z\in\Omega\) with both fibers of positive volume. Choose an invertible affine map of the base sending \(\mathbf x\) to \(-\mathbf e_1\) and \(\mathbf z\) to \(\mathbf e_1\). Such a map exists by extending \(\mathbf z-\mathbf x\) to a basis. Applying this map to the base coordinates alone gives another actual convex body with the same vertical fibers at those two integer sites and with fiber \(C_{(\mathbf x+\mathbf z)/2}\) at the integer site zero. Accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647, with the two coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), gives

\[
a((\mathbf x+\mathbf z)/2)\ge\frac{a(\mathbf x)+a(\mathbf z)}2.
\tag{17}
\]

The auxiliary affine map is used only for this universally quantified application of the accepted theorem. It does not change the integer coordinates of the final construction (6).

If exactly one endpoint fiber has volume zero, choose any point in that nonempty fiber. Convexity includes its midpoint translate of one half of the positive-volume fiber in the middle fiber. The included set has volume \(2^{-d}\) times the positive endpoint volume, which proves (17) in this case also. If both endpoint volumes are zero, (17) follows from nonnegativity. Coincident endpoints are immediate. Iterating the midpoint inequality proves
\(a((1-t)\mathbf x+t\mathbf z)\ge(1-t)a(\mathbf x)+ta(\mathbf z)\) for dyadic \(t\in[0,1]\). Approximate any \(t\in[0,1]\) by dyadic values and use upper semicontinuity at the limiting base point, as in (16). The same inequality follows for every \(t\), proving the stated concavity.

For two points of (6), each coordinate of their convex combination is nonnegative and at most the corresponding convex combination of the two root values. Concavity bounds that combination by the root value at the new base point. Thus \(\widehat C\) is convex. It is bounded, and is closed because \(\Omega\) is closed and \(a\) is upper semicontinuous. Hence it is compact.

To check full dimension, take an open Euclidean ball of radius \(r>0\) about \((\mathbf x_0,\mathbf y_0)\) contained in \(C\). For \(\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0\|<r/2\), the fiber contains the \(d\)-dimensional ball of radius \(r/2\) about \(\mathbf y_0\), because the product of these two open balls lies in the original ball. Its positive volume has a \(d\)-th root \(b>0\) independent of \(\mathbf x\). Consequently \(\widehat C\) contains the open set \(\{\mathbf x:\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0\|<r/2\}\times(0,b)^d\). It is a convex body in the same full dimension.

At every \(\mathbf x\in\Omega\), the fiber of (6) is exactly \([0,a(\mathbf x)]^d\), with volume \(a(\mathbf x)^d=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf x})\). Outside \(\Omega\) both fibers are empty. If \(\mathbf x\in\Omega\) and the old fiber has volume zero, the new fiber is the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\); because \(d\ge1\), its volume is still zero. This proves exact equality of every integer weight, not merely agreement on \(D\). The dimensions, \(D,W\), every scalar capacity, every root cost, and every cutoff packing value consequently agree between \(C\) and \(\widehat C\).

For the general lower bound in (7), fix \(\mathbf J\in D\), \(L\ge0\), and a scalar feasible scheme at that target with \(M>0\) whose positive-mass costs are at most \(L\). Accepted fact 793210f8a4be3647 supplies a coupling with exactly the prescribed uniform marginals for each component, with output domination \(P_k\le e^{L_k}U_{\mathbf J}\). The singleton is already \(U_{\mathbf J}\). These choices do not change any mass or capacity in (1), and (15) checks their globally shared measure capacities. The finite final mixture obeys \(P\le e^LU_{\mathbf J}\), so it has a bounded measurable density and \(\delta\le L\). Its score is therefore at least \(M/(1+L)\). Take a scalar cutoff optimizer, then maximize over \(L\), to obtain \(\mathcal E(C;\mathbf J)\ge\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)\). This argument applies to \(\widehat C\) as an actual body with exactly the same table, proving both lower comparisons. It uses only the accepted uniform-marginal coupling theorem, with no new transport assertion.

We now prove the upper comparison directly for every admissible coupled scheme on a body with positive integer fibers \([0,w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}]^d\). In particular this applies to \(\widehat C\). Write \(a_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\), fix the target \(\mathbf J\), and let \(Q\) be the uniform law on \([0,a_{\mathbf J}]^d\). For component \(k\), put \(r_k=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}>0\). Each source point has every coordinate between zero and \(a_{\mathbf I}\). Therefore, for every coupling under consideration, its output satisfies the almost-sure containment

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf X_{\mathbf I}
\in[0,r_k]^d
=[0,a_{\mathbf J}e^{-L_k/d}]^d.
\tag{18}
\]

The root costs are nonnegative by 793210f8a4be3647. The containment (18) uses no bound on support size, no reduction of coefficients, and no property of the dependence in \(\Pi_k\).

For any \(t>0\), set \(A_t=[0,a_{\mathbf J}e^{-t/d}]^d\) and \(p_t=P(A_t)\). Then \(Q(A_t)=e^{-t}\in(0,1)\). Every component with \(L_k>t\) is supported in \(A_t\), so

\[
\frac1M\sum_{k:L_k>t}x_k\le p_t.
\tag{19}
\]

Let \(f=dP/dQ\). Apply Jensen's inequality to \(s\log s\) under \(Q\) conditioned on \(A_t\) and separately on its complement in the target cube. This gives the binary relative-entropy inequality for the final mixture:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta
&\ge p_t\log\frac{p_t}{e^{-t}}
+(1-p_t)\log\frac{1-p_t}{1-e^{-t}}\\
&=p_t t+p_t\log p_t+(1-p_t)\log(1-p_t)
-(1-p_t)\log(1-e^{-t})\\
&\ge p_t t-\log2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

The last step uses \(-\log(1-e^{-t})>0\) and
\(s\log s+(1-s)\log(1-s)\ge-\log2\) on \([0,1]\), whose minimum occurs at \(s=1/2\) by differentiation on \((0,1)\) and continuity at the endpoints. The convention \(0\log0=0\) includes \(p_t=0\) and \(p_t=1\). Both conditional reference probabilities are positive, so no undefined conditioning occurs. This is an inequality for \(P\) itself, and does not average component entropies.

Combining (19) and (20) yields
\(\sum_{k:L_k>t}x_k\le M(\delta+\log2)/t\). Choose
\(t=2(\delta+\log2)\). Since \(\delta\ge0\), this is strictly positive even when \(\delta=0\), and at least \(M/2\) of the component mass has cost at most \(t\). Retain exactly those components. For each source,

\[
\sum_{\substack{k:\mathbf I\in E_k\\L_k\le t}}x_k
\le\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{21}
\]

Thus all original capacities remain valid together, and all retained supports and coefficients remain unchanged. Costs tied at \(t\) are retained; only costs strictly greater than \(t\) are discarded. The scalar packing definition, including arbitrary supports through the accepted exact correspondence, gives

\[
P_d(\mathbf J,t;w)\ge\frac M2,\qquad
\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)\ge\frac{M}{2(1+2\delta+2\log2)}.
\tag{22}
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{M}{1+\delta}
\le\frac{2+4\delta+4\log2}{1+\delta}\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w)
\le(2+4\log2)\mathcal B_{\mathbf J}(w).
\tag{23}
\]

For the last inequality, \(\log2=\int_1^2s^{-1}\,ds>1/2\), and the difference between \((2+4\log2)(1+\delta)\) and \(2+4\delta+4\log2\) is \((4\log2-2)\delta\ge0\). Thus (23) includes zero entropy without a limit argument. Taking the supremum over every admissible scheme and coupling at \(\mathbf J\), and then the maximum over the finite set \(D\), proves all of (7) and the stated extension to any body with these positive integer cube fibers.

We next verify the infimum implications without assuming any optimizing body exists. The classes in (8) are nonempty, as shown by \([-\tfrac14,\tfrac14]^n\times[0,1]^d\), whose total integer-fiber mass is one. \le\frac{2^n\mathcal E(\widehat C)}{W(C)}
\le(2+4\log2)\frac{2^n\mathcal B_d(w(C))}{W(C)}.
\tag{24}
\]

Taking the infimum over \(C\) proves the first line of (9). Taking infima over \(d\) and then over \(n\) proves the other lines.
