## Statement

Let $h$ be a finite nonnegative concave function on an interval of length $L>0$, positive on its interior. For integers $1\le p\le m$ put $U=\int h^m$. Then
$$
\int h^p\ge \frac{(m+1)^{p/m}}{p+1}L^{1-p/m}U^{p/m}.
$$
Equality is attained by a tent with affine sides vanishing at its interval endpoints and by a one-sided affine function vanishing at one endpoint.

## Proof

Let $H=\sup h$. This is finite since two fixed interior points bound $h$ above on the outer portions by extrapolating their chord, and $h$ is continuous on the interior. For $0\le s<H$, let $\ell(s)=|\{h>s\}|$. The superlevel sets are intervals, and their weighted Minkowski combinations lie in the corresponding interpolated strict superlevel set by concavity. Thus $\ell$ is concave on $[0,H)$ and $\ell(0)=L$. Comparing with a superlevel at height tending to $H$ shows $\ell(s)\ge L(1-s/H)$. Layer cake gives $U\ge LH^m/(m+1)$.

Set $c=((m+1)U/L)^{1/m}\ge H$ and $\ell_0(s)=L(1-s/c)_+$. Extend $\ell$ by zero for $s\ge H$. The difference $g=\ell-\ell_0$ is concave on $[0,H)$, with $g(0)=0$, so $g(s)/s$ is nonincreasing there. On $[H,\infty)$ the difference is nonpositive. Thus its only possible sign change is from nonnegative to nonpositive, with a jump at $H$ permitted. By layer cake $\int s^{m-1}g(s)\,ds=0$. If both signs occur, choose a crossing $s_0>0$. For $p<m$ the product $s^{m-1}g(s)(s^{p-m}-s_0^{p-m})$ is nonnegative for $s>0$. Integrating and using the zero moment gives $\int s^{p-1}g(s)\,ds\ge0$. If one sign is absent the equal moment gives $g=0$ almost everywhere. The case $p=m$ is the defining equality. Layer cake now gives $\int h^p\ge Lc^p/(p+1)$. For the indicated tent and the one-sided affine example the strict superlevel length is exactly $L(1-s/H)_+$, giving equality.
