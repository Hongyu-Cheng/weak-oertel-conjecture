## Statement

Let \(n\ge2\).  The following two discrete assertions hold for every finite nonempty set \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), where \(N:=|D|\).

1. Adaptive modulus \(n+1\).  Let \(E\) be a largest coordinatewise congruence class of \(D\) modulo \(n+1\), and put \(q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)\).  Then \(|E|\ge N/(n+1)^n\), and exactly one of the following affine-rank cases applies:

   (a) If \(q=0\), then \(E\) is a singleton and \(N\le(n+1)^n\).

   (b) If \(1\le q\le n-1\), there is a \(q\)-dimensional lattice simplex \(T\), with vertices in \(E\), such that
   \[
    |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge |E|\ge\frac{N}{(n+1)^n}.
   \]

   (c) If \(q=n\), there is an \(n\)-dimensional lattice simplex \(T\), with vertices in \(E\), such that
   \[
    |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge\frac{2}{n}|E|
    \ge\frac{2N}{n(n+1)^n}.
   \]

2. Fixed modulus \(n+2\).  Let \(E\) be a largest coordinatewise congruence class of \(D\) modulo \(n+2\), and again put \(q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)\).  Then \(|E|\ge N/(n+2)^n\).  If \(q=0\), then \(N\le(n+2)^n\).  If \(1\le q\le n\), there is a \(q\)-dimensional lattice simplex \(T\), with vertices in \(E\), such that
\[
 |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge |E|\ge\frac{N}{(n+2)^n}.
\]

Now let \(d\ge1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume
\[
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing.
\]
Write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 W:=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z|_d.
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), let
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\{|C_z\cap J|_d:J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in J\},
\]
and for \(p\in S\), let
\[
 h_S(p):=
 \inf\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
 \text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
Put
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 B_d:=d2^d\int_0^1\frac{x^{d-1}}{(1+x)^d}\,dx.
\]
If \(W>0\), then there is a point \(p\in S\) such that
\[
 h_S(p)\ge
 \frac{\gamma_d}{B_d}\max\left\{
 \frac{1}{n2^{2n-1}(n+1)^n},
 \frac{1}{2^{2n}(n+2)^n}
 \right\}W.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 h_{\max}(C)>
 \max\left\{
 \frac{1}{e\,n\,2^{2n}(n+1)^n},
 \frac{1}{e\,2^{2n+1}(n+2)^n}
 \right\}W.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(n=3\), the adaptive term is the larger term, and (1) gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{6144B_d}W
 >\frac{\gamma_d}{12288}W
 >\frac{1}{12288e}W.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(W=0\), every point of the nonempty set \(S\) satisfies the corresponding non-strict zero bound \(h_S(p)\ge0\).  No universal-in-\(n\) constant is asserted.

## Proof

We first prove the two discrete assertions.

Maximum-simplex barycentric bound

Let \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite of affine dimension \(q\ge1\).  Among all \(q\)-simplices with vertices in \(E\), choose one of maximum \(q\)-dimensional volume:
\[
 T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(x\in E\), write its barycentric coordinates relative to \(T\) as
\[
 x=\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(x)v_i,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(x)=1.
\tag{5}
\]
Replacing \(v_i\) by \(x\) multiplies the absolute \(q\)-volume of the simplex by \(|\lambda_i(x)|\).  The replacement simplex still has all vertices in \(E\).  Maximality of (4) therefore gives
\[
 |\lambda_i(x)|\le1
 \qquad(x\in E,\ 0\le i\le q).
\tag{6}
\]
This argument takes place in the intrinsic affine hull of \(E\), so it applies without any full-dimensionality assumption in the ambient space.

Fixed modulus \(n+2\)

Suppose first that all points of \(E\) are coordinatewise congruent modulo \(n+2\), and that \(1\le q\le n\).  Use the maximum simplex (4).  Choose positive integers \(k_0,\ldots,k_q\) with
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^q k_i=n+1;
\tag{7}
\]
for example, take \(k_0=n-q+1\) and \(k_i=1\) for \(1\le i\le q\).  Define
\[
 \Phi(x):=
 \frac{x+\sum_{i=0}^q k_iv_i}{n+2}
 \qquad(x\in E).
\tag{8}
\]
The numerator in (8) is the sum of \(n+2\) points counted with multiplicity, all in one residue class modulo \(n+2\).  Hence \(\Phi(x)\in\mathbb Z^n\).  Equations (5) and (7) give
\[
 \Phi(x)=
 \sum_{i=0}^q
 \frac{\lambda_i(x)+k_i}{n+2}v_i.
\tag{9}
\]
Every coefficient in (9) is nonnegative by (6) and \(k_i\ge1\), and the coefficients sum to one.  Thus \(\Phi(x)\in T\cap\mathbb Z^n\).  The map is injective because
\[
 \Phi(x)-\Phi(x')=\frac{x-x'}{n+2}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge|E|.
\tag{10}
\]

For an arbitrary finite nonempty \(D\), partition it into its \((n+2)^n\) coordinatewise residue classes and choose a largest one \(E\).  Then \(|E|\ge N/(n+2)^n\).  If its affine rank is positive, (10) proves the stated fixed-modulus extraction.  If its affine rank is zero, then \(E\) is a singleton, so \(1=|E|\ge N/(n+2)^n\), equivalently \(N\le(n+2)^n\).  This proves every affine-rank case of the fixed-modulus assertion.

Adaptive modulus \(n+1\), ranks below \(n\)

Now suppose that all points of \(E\) are coordinatewise congruent modulo \(n+1\).  If \(1\le q\le n-1\), choose positive integers \(k_0,\ldots,k_q\) with
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^q k_i=n;
\tag{11}
\]
one may take \(k_0=n-q\) and \(k_i=1\) for \(1\le i\le q\).  The map
\[
 \Phi(x):=
 \frac{x+\sum_{i=0}^q k_iv_i}{n+1}
\tag{12}
\]
is integral because its numerator is the sum, with multiplicity, of \(n+1\) congruent points.  Its barycentric coefficients are
\[
 \frac{\lambda_i(x)+k_i}{n+1},
\]
which are nonnegative by (6), and they sum to one by (11).  Thus (12) is an injection from \(E\) into \(T\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and
\[
 |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|\ge|E|.
\tag{13}
\]

Adaptive modulus \(n+1\), full affine rank

It remains to prove the full-rank edge count.  Suppose \(q=n\), retain the maximum simplex
\[
 T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_n\},
\]
and write \(\lambda_0(x),\ldots,\lambda_n(x)\) for the barycentric coordinates of \(x\in E\).  For every index \(j\) with \(\lambda_j(x)\ge0\), define
\[
 \psi_j(x):=
 \frac{x+\sum_{i\ne j}v_i}{n+1}.
\tag{14}
\]
The point in (14) is integral because its numerator is a sum of \(n+1\) congruent lattice points.  It also lies in \(T\), because
\[
 \psi_j(x)=
 \frac{\lambda_j(x)}{n+1}v_j
 +\sum_{i\ne j}
 \frac{\lambda_i(x)+1}{n+1}v_i,
\tag{15}
\]
where every coefficient is nonnegative by the choice of \(j\) and (6), and the coefficients sum to one.

Every \(x\in E\) has at least two indices \(j\) with \(\lambda_j(x)\ge0\).  Indeed, if at most one barycentric coordinate were nonnegative, the sum of all coordinates would be strictly smaller than that sole nonnegative coordinate, which is at most one by (6), contradicting \(\sum_j\lambda_j(x)=1\).

Form a bipartite graph.  Its left vertex set is \(E\); its right vertex set \(Y\) consists of all points \(\psi_j(x)\) from (14); and an admissible pair \((x,j)\) is an edge from \(x\) to \(\psi_j(x)\).  For a fixed \(x\), different indices give different neighbors, since
\[
 \psi_j(x)-\psi_k(x)=\frac{v_k-v_j}{n+1}\ne0
 \qquad(j\ne k).
\tag{16}
\]
Hence every left vertex has degree at least two.

For a fixed right vertex \(y\) and a fixed label \(j\), equation (14) determines at most one preimage:
\[
 x=(n+1)y-\sum_{i\ne j}v_i.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus every right vertex has degree at most \(n+1\).  We next identify the equality case exactly.  Let \(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_n\) be the barycentric coordinates of \(y\in T\).  If \(y\) has a preimage with label \(j\), then the \(j\)-th barycentric coordinate of that preimage is \((n+1)\mu_j\).  If \(y\) has preimages for all \(n+1\) labels, (6) gives
\[
 0\le\mu_j\le\frac1{n+1}
 \qquad(0\le j\le n).
\]
Since the \(\mu_j\) sum to one, they all equal \(1/(n+1)\).  Therefore the only possible right vertex of degree \(n+1\) is the simplex centroid
\[
 c:=\frac{v_0+\cdots+v_n}{n+1}.
\tag{18}
\]
Conversely, \(c\) is integral by the congruence assumption, and its preimage with label \(j\) is exactly \(v_j\).  Hence \(c\) has degree exactly \(n+1\), its preimages are precisely the simplex vertices, and every other right vertex has degree at most \(n\).

Delete the \(n+1\) centroid edges.  A nonvertex of \(E\) loses no edge and retains degree at least two.  Each simplex vertex originally has all \(n+1\) labels admissible, and after deletion it retains exactly \(n\) edges.  Consequently the number of remaining edges is at least
\[
 2\bigl(|E|-(n+1)\bigr)+n(n+1)
 =2|E|+(n-2)(n+1)
 \ge2|E|,
\tag{19}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(n\ge2\).  All remaining edges end in \(Y\setminus\{c\}\), and each such right vertex has degree at most \(n\).  Therefore
\[
 n(|Y|-1)\ge2|E|+(n-2)(n+1)\ge2|E|.
\tag{20}
\]
In particular,
\[
 |T\cap\mathbb Z^n|
 \ge|Y|
 \ge\frac{2}{n}|E|.
\tag{21}
\]
This includes the edge case \(n=2\): then (19) has exactly the lower bound \(2|E|\), and every noncentroid right vertex has degree at most two.

For arbitrary \(D\), choose a largest of the \((n+1)^n\) residue classes.  Then \(|E|\ge N/(n+1)^n\).  Equations (13) and (21) give the positive-rank alternatives.  If \(q=0\), the largest class is a singleton and \(N\le(n+1)^n\).  This completes every affine-rank case of the adaptive extraction.

Intrinsic-lattice simplex lift

We record how either extracted simplex enters accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5, including nonprimitive and lower-rank affine hulls.  Let
\[
 T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\}\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\]
be a \(q\)-dimensional lattice simplex, with \(q\ge1\), and put \(F:=\operatorname{aff}(T)\).  Its intrinsic lattice is
\[
 \Gamma:=(F-v_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{22}
\]
The lattice generated by the differences \(v_i-v_0\) has rank \(q\) and is contained in \(\Gamma\); since \(\Gamma\) lies in the \(q\)-dimensional space \(F-v_0\), \(\Gamma\) has rank exactly \(q\).  Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) of \(\Gamma\), let \(U\in\mathbb Z^{n\times q}\) have these basis vectors as columns, and write
\[
 v_i=v_0+Uu_i,
 \qquad u_i\in\mathbb Z^q.
\]
Then
\[
 \Delta:=\operatorname{conv}\{u_0,\ldots,u_q\}
\]
is a full-dimensional lattice simplex in \(\mathbb R^q\), and the affine map
\[
 \phi(u):=v_0+Uu
\tag{23}
\]
is injective.  Moreover, (22) and the fact that \(U\mathbb Z^q=\Gamma\) show that (23) restricts to a bijection
\[
 \Delta\cap\mathbb Z^q
 \longleftrightarrow
 T\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{24}
\]

Suppose that for every vertex \(v_i\) there is \(x_i\in C_{v_i}\) with
\[
 \delta_C(v_i,x_i)\ge s
\tag{25}
\]
for a common \(s\ge0\).  Apply accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 with one simplex factor \(\Delta\), base map (23), and common vertex depth \(s\).  Its uniform one-simplex conclusion and (24) produce a point \(p\in S\) satisfying
\[
 h_S(p)\ge2^{-q}s|\Delta\cap\mathbb Z^q|
 =2^{-q}s|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus (26) is valid in the intrinsic rank \(q\), regardless of whether the affine hull is a primitive coordinate subspace.  The cited fact already treats proper, vertical, boundary, and whole-space halfspaces.

There is also a direct rank-zero observation.  If \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(x\in C_z\), then
\[
 h_S((z,x))\ge\delta_C(z,x).
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, every ambient closed halfspace containing \((z,x)\) has, in the fiber over \(z\), either a proper closed halfspace containing \(x\) or the whole fiber.  That single fiber contributes at least \(\delta_C(z,x)\); all other fiber contributions are nonnegative.

Exact parity-layer selection

Assume now that \(W>0\).  Let
\[
 P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C,
 \qquad
 M(z):=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x),
 \qquad
 \rho(z):=M(z)^{1/d}
 \quad(z\in P),
\tag{28}
\]
and extend \(M\) by zero off \(P\).  Accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 gives attainment of every maximum in (28), concavity of \(\rho\) on \(P\), and \(M(z)=0\) for every zero-volume or lower-dimensional fiber.  Because \(P\) is compact, \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite.  Put
\[
 S_M:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}M(z).
\tag{29}
\]
Accepted fact 4bd2e6d22487dfef gives
\[
 S_M\ge\gamma_dW>0.
\tag{30}
\]

We now fully reprove the sharper \(B_d\) version of the level selection underlying accepted fact 62f2ec156b347dfe.  For \(s>0\), put
\[
 D_s:=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n:\rho(z)\ge s\},
 \qquad N(s):=|D_s|,
\]
and define
\[
 R:=\max_{s>0}s^dN(s).
\tag{31}
\]
This maximum is finite and attained at a positive lattice value of \(\rho\).  Indeed, the positive values of \(\rho\) on the finite set \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) form a finite nonempty set.  If \(N(s)>0\), let \(v\) be its least member satisfying \(v\ge s\).  Then \(N(v)=N(s)\) and \(v^dN(v)\ge s^dN(s)\); if \(N(s)=0\), the score is zero.  Hence (31) is the maximum over finitely many positive lattice values.

Partition \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) into its \(2^n\) coordinatewise parity classes.  Some parity class \(E\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in E}\rho(z)^d\ge2^{-n}S_M>0.
\tag{32}
\]
Choose \(z_0\in E\) maximizing \(\rho\) on \(E\), put \(h:=\rho(z_0)>0\), and for \(0<t\le h\) set
\[
 A(t):=|\{z\in E:\rho(z)\ge t\}|.
\]
For every point counted by \(A(t)\), the midpoint
\[
 \phi(z):=\frac{z+z_0}{2}
\]
is integral, belongs to \(P\) by convexity, and depends injectively on \(z\).  Concavity of \(\rho\) gives
\[
 \rho(\phi(z))\ge\frac{\rho(z)+h}{2}\ge\frac{t+h}{2}.
\]
Therefore, by the definition of \(R\),
\[
 A(t)\le N\left(\frac{t+h}{2}\right)
 \le R\left(\frac{2}{t+h}\right)^d.
\tag{33}
\]
The finite layer-cake identity, including zero values and with endpoint conventions irrelevant to the integral, now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{z\in E}\rho(z)^d
 &=\int_0^h d\,t^{d-1}A(t)\,dt\\
 &\le R\,d2^d\int_0^h\frac{t^{d-1}}{(t+h)^d}\,dt\\
 &=RB_d.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Combining (32) and (34) yields
\[
 R\ge\frac{S_M}{2^nB_d}.
\tag{35}
\]
For completeness, the substitution \(u=2x/(1+x)\) gives
\[
 B_d
 =2d\int_0^1\frac{u^{d-1}}{2-u}\,du
 <2d\int_0^1u^{d-1}\,du
 =2.
\tag{36}
\]
The inequality is strict because \((2-u)^{-1}<1\) on a positive-measure subset of \([0,1]\).

Choose a positive level \(t\) attaining \(R\), and put
\[
 D_t:=\{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n:\rho(z)\ge t\},
 \qquad N:=|D_t|.
\]
Then
\[
 t^dN=R\ge\frac{S_M}{2^nB_d}.
\tag{37}
\]
The set \(D_t\) is finite and nonempty.  Since \(t>0\), zero-volume and lower-dimensional fibers do not occur in \(D_t\).  At every \(z\in D_t\), attainment in accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 gives a point \(x_z\in C_z\) with
\[
 \delta_C(z,x_z)=M(z)\ge t^d.
\tag{38}
\]

Adaptive mixed-depth bound

Apply the adaptive modulus-\(n+1\) extraction to \(D_t\).

If its largest residue class has affine rank zero, then \(N\le(n+1)^n\).  For its unique point \(z\), equations (27) and (38) give a point of depth at least \(t^d\), and hence at least
\[
 \frac{t^dN}{n2^{n-1}(n+1)^n}.
\tag{39}
\]

If \(1\le q\le n-1\), equations (13), (26), and (38) give a point of depth at least
\[
 2^{-q}t^d|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{2^{n-1}(n+1)^n}
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{n2^{n-1}(n+1)^n}.
\tag{40}
\]

If \(q=n\), equations (21), (26), and (38) give a point of depth at least
\[
 2^{-n}t^d|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{n2^{n-1}(n+1)^n}.
\tag{41}
\]
Thus every adaptive affine-rank case supplies some \(p_{\mathrm{ad}}\in S\) with
\[
 h_S(p_{\mathrm{ad}})
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{n2^{n-1}(n+1)^n}
 \overset{(37)}{\ge}
 \frac{S_M}{n2^{2n-1}B_d(n+1)^n}.
\tag{42}
\]

Fixed-modulus mixed-depth bound

Apply instead the fixed modulus-\(n+2\) extraction to the same set \(D_t\).  In affine rank zero, \(N\le(n+2)^n\), and (27), (38) give depth at least \(t^d\), which is at least \(2^{-n}t^dN/(n+2)^n\).  In every positive affine rank \(q\le n\), equations (10), (26), and (38) give a point of depth at least
\[
 2^{-q}t^d|T\cap\mathbb Z^n|
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{2^n(n+2)^n}.
\tag{43}
\]
Therefore every fixed-modulus affine-rank case supplies some \(p_{\mathrm{fix}}\in S\) with
\[
 h_S(p_{\mathrm{fix}})
 \ge
 \frac{t^dN}{2^n(n+2)^n}
 \overset{(37)}{\ge}
 \frac{S_M}{2^{2n}B_d(n+2)^n}.
\tag{44}
\]

Choose whichever of \(p_{\mathrm{ad}}\) and \(p_{\mathrm{fix}}\) corresponds to the larger coefficient.  Combining (30), (42), and (44) proves (1).

Finally,
\[
 d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1,
\]
because \(\log(1+x)<x\) for \(x>0\).  Hence
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>e^{-1}.
\tag{45}
\]
Since \(W>0\), equations (1), (36), and (45) give the strict inequality (2).  For \(n=3\), the adaptive denominator before the common factor \(B_d\) is
\[
 3\cdot2^5\cdot4^3=6144,
\]
whereas the fixed denominator before \(B_d\) is
\[
 2^6\cdot5^3=8000.
\]
Thus (3) follows.

If \(W=0\), choose any point of the assumed nonempty set \(S\).  Mixed-integer halfspace mass is nonnegative, so its depth is at least zero.  Accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 also confirms that every zero-volume or lower-dimensional fiber has maximal absolute fiber depth zero, consistent with the absence of a positive level in this case.

The normalized coefficient multiplying \(2^{-n}W\) in (2) is at most a quantity of order \(2^{-n}(n+1)^{-n}\), and therefore tends to zero with \(n\).  The result closes each fixed integer rank uniformly in \(d\), including \(n=3\), but does not prove the universal weak Oertel target.
