## Statement

Let $f$ be bounded, positive and continuous on a bounded open convex set $D$ and zero off $D$, and suppose $f^{-1/\beta}$ is convex on $D$ for $\beta\ge1$. Integrating out one coordinate yields a bounded, positive and continuous function $F$ on the open projected set, extended by zero off it. If $\beta>1$, $F^{-1/(\beta-1)}$ is convex there. If $\beta=1$, $F$ is quasiconcave there.

Let a compact convex flattened contact set have tangent dimension $m=d-1\ge1$, with at least one positive $m$-volume tangent fiber and positive affine $H=H(z)$ and $L=L(z,\xi)$ on the whole set. The weight $f=HL^{-m-1}$ has jointly convex inverse power $f^{-1/m}=H(L/H)^{(m+1)/m}$. Integrating out $r<m$ orthonormal tangent coordinates gives a marginal with convex inverse power $-1/(m-r)$ in the interior of its projection, taken relative to the base affine span and the remaining tangent coordinates. The full tangent marginal is quasiconcave on the real base, including boundary bases with positive fiber mass. If $H$ is constant, the initial exponent improves to $-1/(m+1)$ and the full tangent marginal has convex reciprocal on the set where it is positive.

## Proof

Write the coordinates as $(z,x)$ with $x$ the scalar to be integrated. For each $z$ in the open projection, the section of $D$ is a nonempty bounded open interval $I_z$, and its mass $F(z)=\int_{I_z}f(z,x)\,dx$ is positive and bounded. Fix such a $z$ and a sequence $z_j$ tending to it. At each $x$ with $(z,x)\in D$, continuity and openness give $f(z_j,x)\to f(z,x)$. At $x\notin\overline{I_z}$, one has $(z,x)\notin\overline D$: a point of $\overline D$ at that base could be joined to an interior point at the same base, giving interior points of $D$ on the open segment and putting $x$ in $\overline{I_z}$. Thus for those $x$, $f(z_j,x)=0$ eventually. Only the two endpoints of $I_z$ can be exceptions. Bounded dominated convergence in a fixed bounded interval gives continuity of $F$ on the open projection.

For two bases $z_0,z_1$ in that projection, let $F_i=F(z_i)$ and let $X_i(s)$ be the quantile of $f(z_i,x)\,dx/F_i$ for $0<s<1$. The quantile is differentiable with $X_i'=F_i/f(z_i,X_i)$, since its distribution function has positive continuous derivative on the open interval. At $z_\lambda=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1$ put $X_\lambda=(1-\lambda)X_0+\lambda X_1$ and $u_i=f(z_i,X_i)^{-1/\beta}$. For $0<\lambda<1$, convexity gives
$$
f(z_\lambda,X_\lambda)\ge[(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1]^{-\beta},
\qquad
X_\lambda'=(1-\lambda)F_0u_0^\beta+\lambda F_1u_1^\beta.
$$
When $\beta>1$, weighted Hölder gives
$$
[(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1]^\beta
\le [(1-\lambda)F_0u_0^\beta+\lambda F_1u_1^\beta]
[(1-\lambda)F_0^{-1/(\beta-1)}+\lambda F_1^{-1/(\beta-1)}]^{\beta-1}.
$$
Consequently $f(z_\lambda,X_\lambda)X_\lambda'$ is at least the power mean of $F_0,F_1$ with exponent $-1/(\beta-1)$. For $\beta=1$, the analogous lower bound is the quotient of $(1-\lambda)F_0u_0+\lambda F_1u_1$ by $(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1$, which is at least $\min(F_0,F_1)$. The increasing map $X_\lambda$ parametrizes an interval contained in the intermediate section. Substitution on compact subintervals of $(0,1)$ and monotone exhaustion give the same lower bound for the total mass $F(z_\lambda)$. This proves inverse-power convexity or quasiconcavity, as asserted.

For the contact application, the flattened set is full-dimensional relative to the base affine span times the tangent space. Indeed a full tangent fiber and points at additional affinely independent bases span that space. On its relative interior the initial inverse power $H(L/H)^{(m+1)/m}$ is convex, since it is the perspective of the convex function $t\mapsto t^{(m+1)/m}$ composed with two affine functions. Equivalently, in a convex combination, normalize the weights by their positive $H$ contributions and apply Jensen to $L/H$. Positivity on the compact set bounds the density and its inverse. The relative interior is an open convex domain in these coordinates. Apply the one-coordinate result $r$ times in orthonormal tangent coordinates, choosing axes for the coordinates integrated out. The continuity and boundedness just proved permit iteration. At $r=m$ the last step uses $\beta=1$, giving quasiconcavity. For constant $H$ the initial inverse power with $\beta=m+1$ is a positive multiple of $L$, and after $m$ steps the reciprocal of the full marginal is convex.

It remains to track the full tangent marginal on boundary bases. At a relative-interior base, the open section of the relative interior has closure equal to the actual closed section: the projection of the relative interior is the relative interior of the projection, and interpolation with an interior point in that section approximates every point of the closed section from within. Thus the open and closed section integrals agree there. Fix a relative-interior base $z_*$ and a point $\xi_*$ of its tangent fiber. For any base $z$ and $t\downarrow0$, set $z_t=(1-t)z+t z_*$. By convexity the tangent section at $z_t$ contains $(1-t)F_z+t\xi_*$, where $F_z$ is the actual closed tangent section at $z$. Integrating the continuous density on this image shows that the lower limit of the mass at $z_t$ is at least the mass at $z$, using tangent Jacobian $(1-t)^m$. For the upper limit, compactness ensures points outside the closed section at $z$ are eventually outside the sections at $z_t$. The bounded density on the compact contact set then gives the reverse limit inequality by dominated upper convergence. This proves convergence along this segment. Apply it to each of two positive-mass endpoint bases and their interpolated base to extend the interior inequality to them. Their midpoint fibers, and all strict interpolants, have positive tangent volume by convexity of the contact set. In the quasiconcave case an endpoint of zero mass gives a trivial bound.
