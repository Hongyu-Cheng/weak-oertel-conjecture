## Statement

Let n be a nonnegative integer. A set R contained in {0,1,2}^n is a down-set if x in R and y in {0,1,2}^n with y_i<=x_i for every coordinate imply y in R. On {0,1,2}, define the symmetric adjacency relation by the five ordered pairs (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,2), and (2,1). Two vectors are adjacent if their entries are adjacent in every coordinate. For down-sets F,G contained in {0,1,2}^n, let DP(F,G) be the number of ordered adjacent pairs (x,y) in F times G, and put D(R)=DP(R,R). The empty product {0,1,2}^0 has one point, which is adjacent to itself.

Set g=log(2)/log(3), where logarithms are natural. Then, including empty down-sets,

\[
\operatorname{DP}(F,G)\ge (|F||G|)^g,
\qquad
D(R)\ge |R|^{2g}=|R|^{\log(4)/\log(3)}.
\]

Moreover, g is the largest exponent for which the first inequality can hold for all such F,G and all n. This optimality claim concerns the two-family inequality; it does not assert optimality of the displayed self-pair exponent.

## Proof

We first prove a scalar inequality. Let a_0>=a_1>=a_2>=0 and b_0>=b_1>=b_2>=0 be arbitrary real numbers. We claim

\[
a_0^g b_0^g+a_0^g b_1^g+a_1^g b_0^g+a_1^g b_2^g+a_2^g b_1^g
\ge \bigl((a_0+a_1+a_2)(b_0+b_1+b_2)\bigr)^g.
\tag{1}
\]

Put p=1/g>1. For vectors A,B with three nonnegative entries indexed by 0,1,2, define

\[
\mathcal L(A,B)=A_0B_0+A_0B_1+A_1B_0+A_1B_2+A_2B_1,
\qquad
\|A\|_p=(A_0^p+A_1^p+A_2^p)^{1/p}.
\]

For r in {1,2,3}, let s_r be the indicator vector of the initial segment {0,...,r-1}. The nine values of this bilinear form are

\[
\bigl(\mathcal L(s_r,s_s)\bigr)_{r,s=1}^3
=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\2&3&4\\2&4&5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]

Every entry in (2) is at least (rs)^g. Here is an exact check of all six entries up to symmetry. For (r,s)=(1,1), both quantities are 1. For (1,2), use g<1 to obtain 2^g<2. For (1,3), use 3^g=2. For (2,2), observe that 2^3<3^2 implies g<2/3, so 4^g<4^{2/3}<3; the last strict inequality follows by cubing and using 16<27. For (2,3), one has 6^g=2 times 2^g<4. Finally, for (3,3), one has 9^g=4<5.

For any nonincreasing nonnegative A, define alpha_1=A_0-A_1, alpha_2=A_1-A_2, and alpha_3=A_2. These coefficients are nonnegative, and A=sum_{r=1}^3 alpha_r s_r. We show directly that

\[
\|A\|_p\le\sum_{r=1}^3\alpha_r r^g.
\tag{3}
\]

If the sum on the right is zero, then A=0 and the assertion holds. Otherwise denote that sum by S and define lambda_r=alpha_r r^g/S. Then lambda_r>=0, sum_r lambda_r=1, and A/S=sum_r lambda_r(s_r/r^g). The function t mapsto t^p is convex on the nonnegative real numbers, since p>1. Applying its convexity separately in each of the three coordinates and summing gives

\[
\sum_{i=0}^2(A_i/S)^p
\le\sum_{r=1}^3\lambda_r\sum_{i=0}^2(s_{r,i}/r^g)^p
=\sum_{r=1}^3\lambda_r\,r/r^{gp}=1.
\]

Taking the p-th root proves (3). Apply the same construction to a nonincreasing nonnegative B, with coefficients beta_s. Bilinearity, (2), its entrywise bound, and (3) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L(A,B)
&=\sum_{r,s=1}^3\alpha_r\beta_s\mathcal L(s_r,s_s)\\
&\ge\sum_{r,s=1}^3\alpha_r\beta_s(rs)^g
=\left(\sum_r\alpha_r r^g\right)\left(\sum_s\beta_s s^g\right)
\ge\|A\|_p\|B\|_p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Take A_i=a_i^g and B_i=b_i^g. They are nonincreasing and nonnegative. Since gp=1, their p-norms are respectively (a_0+a_1+a_2)^g and (b_0+b_1+b_2)^g. This proves (1), including zero entries and zero vectors.

We now induct on n to prove the two-family counting inequality. For n=0, every down-set is either empty or the one-point empty product. If either set is empty, both sides are zero; if both are nonempty, both sides are one.

Suppose the result holds in dimension n-1, where n>=1. For i in {0,1,2}, define F_i={u in {0,1,2}^{n-1}:(u,i) in F}, and define G_i in the same way. All six slices are down-sets, and F_0 contains F_1 contains F_2, while G_0 contains G_1 contains G_2. Set a_i=|F_i| and b_i=|G_i|. These are precisely nonnegative nonincreasing triples. Partitioning adjacent pairs according to their last entries gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{DP}(F,G)
={}&\operatorname{DP}(F_0,G_0)+\operatorname{DP}(F_0,G_1)
+\operatorname{DP}(F_1,G_0)\\
&+\operatorname{DP}(F_1,G_2)+\operatorname{DP}(F_2,G_1).
\end{aligned}
\]

Apply the induction hypothesis to each term and then apply (1). The result is DP(F,G)>=[(sum_i a_i)(sum_i b_i)]^g=(|F||G|)^g. This completes the induction. Substitution F=G=R gives the stated self-pair bound. Its exponent is strictly greater than one because 4>3.

For optimality of the two-family exponent, already in dimension one take F={0} and G={0,1,2}. Both are down-sets. The only adjacent pairs are (0,0) and (0,1), so DP(F,G)=2 while |F||G|=3. Any exponent h satisfying the two-family inequality for all down-sets must therefore satisfy 2>=3^h, or h<=log(2)/log(3)=g. This verifies the claimed optimality exactly.
