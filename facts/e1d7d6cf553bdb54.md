## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), \(m:=n+d\), and identify
\(\mathbb R^m=\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\). Write \(\operatorname{vol}_j\)
for Euclidean \(j\)-volume, with \(\operatorname{vol}_0(\{0\}):=1\).

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be a convex body and let
\(A\subseteq D\) be compact and convex, with
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(A)\geq
\alpha\operatorname{vol}_m(D)>0
\]
for some \(\alpha>0\). Write
\[
K:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D),\qquad
G:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(A),
\]
and suppose that
\[
a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n\subseteq K
\]
for some \(a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\rho>0\), where
\(\mathbb B_\infty^n=[-1,1]^n\).
Then, for every \(q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) and every \(q\)-dimensional
linear subspace \(E\leq\mathbb R^n\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EG)
\geq
\frac{\alpha}{\binom mq}\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EK)
\geq
\frac{\alpha(2\rho)^q}{\binom mq}. \tag{1}
\]

For a nonempty compact \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
\operatorname{width}_u(C):=
\max_{x\in C}\langle u,x\rangle-
\min_{x\in C}\langle u,x\rangle
\]
and, for \(W\geq0\), put
\[
V_W(C):=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{width}_u(C)\leq W\}.
\]
If \(r:=\dim V_W(G)\geq1\), then
\[
W\geq
2\rho\,\alpha^{1/r}\binom mr^{-1/r}. \tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(W=0\) forces \(r=0\). If
\(\alpha=\gamma_m:=(m/(m+1))^m\), then for every \(W\geq0\),
\[
r\leq\frac{e^2mW}{2\rho}. \tag{3}
\]

Moreover, if \(L_t\subseteq L_s\) are nonempty compact subsets of
\(\mathbb R^n\), then
\[
V_W(L_s)\subseteq V_W(L_t).
\]
These are rational subspaces, and if their dimensions are equal, then the
two subspaces are equal.

## Proof

First take \(1\leq q\leq n\), let
\[
F:=E\times\{0\}\leq\mathbb R^m,
\]
and define
\[
M_D:=
\max_{y\in F}
\operatorname{vol}_{m-q}\bigl(D\cap(y+F^\perp)\bigr).
\]
Since \(A\subseteq D\), Fubini's section formula gives, in the required
direction,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(A)
&=\int_F
\operatorname{vol}_{m-q}\bigl(A\cap(y+F^\perp)\bigr)\,dy\\
&\leq \operatorname{vol}_q(P_FA)\,M_D. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]
The Rogers--Shephard projection--section inequality gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_FD)M_D\leq
\binom mq\operatorname{vol}_m(D). \tag{5}
\]
Here it applies because \(D\) is an \(m\)-dimensional convex body and
\(1\leq q\leq n\leq m-1\), the last inequality following from \(d\geq1\).
Also,
\[
P_FD=P_EK\times\{0\},\qquad
P_FA=P_EG\times\{0\}.
\]
Combining the hypothesis, (4), and (5), then cancelling the positive
\(\operatorname{vol}_m(D)\), proves
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EG)
\geq\frac{\alpha}{\binom mq}\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EK). \tag{6}
\]

We next record the zonotope formula in the full generality needed here:
for arbitrary \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\mathbb R^q\), including zero, parallel,
or repeated signed generators,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^N[-x_i,x_i]\right)
=2^q\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[N]\\|I|=q}}
\left|\det(x_i)_{i\in I}\right|. \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, perturb the finite tuple to tuples
\((x_1^{(k)},\ldots,x_N^{(k)})\) in generic position converging to it.
The cited generic-generator formula applies to each perturbed tuple.
The corresponding zonotopes converge in Hausdorff distance, since their
support functions satisfy, uniformly for \(v\in\mathbb S^{q-1}\),
\[
\left|\sum_i|\langle v,x_i^{(k)}\rangle|
-\sum_i|\langle v,x_i\rangle|\right|
\leq\sum_i\|x_i^{(k)}-x_i\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Continuity of convex-body volume under Hausdorff convergence makes the
left sides of (7) converge. The finite determinant sums converge by
continuity of the determinant and absolute value. Passing to the limit
proves (7), including for zero rows and repeated or oppositely signed rows.

Choose an \(n\times q\) matrix \(R\) whose columns form an orthonormal
basis of \(E\), and write \(r_i^\top\) for its \(i\)-th row. Under the
coordinate isometry \(E\to\mathbb R^q\),
\[
P_E\mathbb B_\infty^n
\quad\text{corresponds to}\quad
\sum_{i=1}^n[-r_i,r_i].
\]
Applying (7) to these actual rows and then Cauchy--Binet gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_E\mathbb B_\infty^n)
&=2^q\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[n]\\|I|=q}}|\det R_I|\\
&\geq
2^q\left(\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[n]\\|I|=q}}
(\det R_I)^2\right)^{1/2}\\
&=2^q\det(R^\top R)^{1/2}=2^q. \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
Projection preserves inclusion, and translation does not change volume, so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EK)
\geq\rho^q\operatorname{vol}_q(P_E\mathbb B_\infty^n)
\geq(2\rho)^q. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (6) and (9) prove (1) for \(q\geq1\).

For \(q=0\), both projections have \(0\)-volume one, while
\(\alpha\leq1\) because \(A\subseteq D\); hence (1) holds since
\(\binom m0=(2\rho)^0=1\). The endpoint \(q=n\) was covered because
\(d\geq1\).

Now let \(r:=\dim V_W(G)\geq1\). Choose linearly independent
\(u_1,\ldots,u_r\in\mathbb Z^n\) with
\(\operatorname{width}_{u_i}(G)\leq W\) that span \(V_W(G)\), and let
\(U\in\mathbb Z^{r\times n}\) have rows \(u_i^\top\). Set
\(E:=V_W(G)\). The map
\[
T:E\to\mathbb R^r,\qquad T(x):=Ux,
\]
has \(r\)-dimensional Jacobian
\[
\Delta:=\sqrt{\det(UU^\top)}.
\]
By Cauchy--Binet,
\[
\Delta^2=
\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq[n]\\|I|=r}}(\det U_I)^2
\]
is a positive integer, so \(\Delta\geq1\). Since every \(u_i\in E\),
\(U(P_Ex)=Ux\). Hence \(T(P_EG)=UG\), and this set lies in a rectangular
box whose \(i\)-th side has length at most \(W\). Change of variables gives
\[
\Delta\operatorname{vol}_r(P_EG)
=\operatorname{vol}_r(UG)\leq W^r. \tag{10}
\]
Applying (1) with \(q=r\) and using \(\Delta\geq1\) gives
\[
W^r\geq
\Delta\frac{\alpha(2\rho)^r}{\binom mr}
\geq\frac{\alpha(2\rho)^r}{\binom mr},
\]
which is (2). If \(W=0\), (10) contradicts the positive lower bound in
(1) whenever \(r\geq1\); hence \(r=0\).

Suppose finally that \(\alpha=\gamma_m\). Since
\[
\gamma_m^{1/r}\geq\gamma_m=(1+1/m)^{-m}\geq e^{-1},
\qquad
\binom mr\leq\left(\frac{em}{r}\right)^r,
\]
(2) implies, for \(r\geq1\),
\[
W\geq 2\rho\,e^{-1}\frac{r}{em}
=\frac{2\rho r}{e^2m}.
\]
This is (3); the already proved \(W=0\) case gives the same conclusion.

For the moreover clause, \(L_t\subseteq L_s\) implies
\(\operatorname{width}_u(L_t)\leq\operatorname{width}_u(L_s)\) for every
\(u\in\mathbb Z^n\). Thus every \(W\)-narrow integer direction for
\(L_s\) is \(W\)-narrow for \(L_t\), proving the stated inclusion.
Each space is rational because it is spanned by integer vectors. An
inclusion of finite-dimensional subspaces of equal dimension is equality.

## External sources

C. A. Rogers and G. C. Shephard, "Convex Bodies Associated with a Given
Convex Body," J. London Math. Soc. 33 (1958), 270--281, Theorem 1,
https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-33.3.270.
It states that for an \(M\)-dimensional convex body \(C\) and an
\(\ell\)-subspace \(H\), \(1\leq\ell\leq M-1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_\ell(P_HC)
\max_{y\in H}\operatorname{vol}_{M-\ell}
\bigl(C\cap(y+H^\perp)\bigr)
\leq\binom M\ell\operatorname{vol}_M(C).
\]
It applies in (5) with \((M,\ell,C,H)=(m,q,D,E\times\{0\})\); the
hypotheses are checked there, and \(q=0\) is handled directly.

G. C. Shephard, "Combinatorial Properties of Associated Zonotopes,"
Canad. J. Math. 26 (1974), 302--321, formula (57),
https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1974-032-5, gives the formula in (7) for
generic generators. The limiting argument preceding (8) proves the
arbitrary-generator version needed for the possibly zero, parallel, or
repeated signed rows of \(R\).
