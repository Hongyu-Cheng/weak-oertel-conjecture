## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(T\ge0\), and \(B\ge2\) be integers. For each \(1\le i\le k\), let \(C_{4,i}\ge2B-1\) be an integer, and define
$$
g_i(0)=0,\qquad g_i(1)=1,\qquad g_i(2)=B,\qquad g_i(3)=C_{4,i}.
$$
For \(\beta\ge0\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),\\
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For an original atom \(\mathbf z\in A_T\), let \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) be the infimum of \(p(H)\) over all closed affine halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\) containing \(\mathbf z\), and define
$$
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
$$
There is an absolute \(\kappa>0\), specified in (7) below, such that
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$
In particular this holds when all \(C_{4,i}\) equal a common \(C_4\ge2B-1\), which is the registered homogeneous four-site family. Every probability and moment in (1)-(2) uses the full original \(Z\). The locked symbols \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) retain their locked meanings. The statement includes \(T=0\), \(\beta=0\), singleton height intervals, and equality atoms on spatial-energy, height, and halfspace boundaries.

Here is the explicit compact minimum used to specify the absolute constant. For scalar variables
$$
0\le u\le1,\qquad 2u\le v\le24,\qquad 0\le \zeta\le e^{u-2v},
\tag{3}
$$
put \(\xi=e^{-u}\), \(\eta=e^{-v}\), and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr Z(u,v,\zeta)&=1+\xi+\eta+\zeta,\\
\mathscr P(u,v,\zeta)&=1+(\xi^{4/3}+2\eta^{2/3})^3
 +(\eta^{4/3}+2(\xi\zeta)^{2/3})^3+\zeta^4,\\
\Delta(u,v,\zeta)&=\log\frac{8\mathscr P(u,v,\zeta)}{\mathscr Z(u,v,\zeta)^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Let \(\mathcal K\) be exactly the compact set (3), and define
$$
\varepsilon=\min\left\lbrace\frac18,\min_{(u,v,\zeta)\in\mathcal K}\Delta(u,v,\zeta)\right\rbrace.
\tag{5}
$$
The proof establishes \(\varepsilon>0\), including on the face \(\zeta=0\). Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=\left\lceil\max\left\lbrace2^{1024},(2^{20}/\varepsilon)^2\right\rbrace\right\rceil,
&H&=2^{36},\\
\kappa_{\rm small}&=
\frac{4096}{3584\cdot19683(1+2N\log3)^3},
&\kappa_3&=\frac1{2939328}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Then one permissible coefficient in (2) is
$$
\kappa=\min\left\lbrace
\kappa_{\rm small},\ \frac1{7168},\ \frac{e^{-8}}{448},\
\kappa_3 e^{-4096H^2}\right\rbrace>0.
\tag{7}
$$

## Proof

The support in (1) is finite and contains \((\mathbf0,0)\), so \(0<Z<\infty\). The linear interpolation of each \(g_i\) has nonnegative nondecreasing slopes \(1,B-1,C_{4,i}-B\), since \(C_{4,i}\ge2B-1\). If \(T=0\), the support is a singleton and \(M_3(p)=1\), which proves (2). Henceforth \(T\ge1\).

First we identify the original spatial profile and the accepted consumers. Write \(r=e^{-\beta}\), define \(f_E=\sum_{t=E}^T r^t\) for integers \(0\le E\le T\), and put \(f_E=0\) for integer \(E>T\). Define \(a_{\mathbf x}=f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\) on the spatial box and zero outside it. The exact map to the finite table of accepted `0cd230a98ae241c3` is
$$

A^a_\omega&=\lbrace\mathbf x:a_{\mathbf x}\ge\omega\rbrace,&
r^a_\omega(\mathbf j)&=|A^a_\omega\cap(2\mathbf j-A^a_\omega)|,\\
E(a)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_a}\max_{0<\omega\le a_{\mathbf j}}
\omega^4r^a_\omega(\mathbf j)^3,&
\chi(a)&=\frac1Z\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}a_{\mathbf x}\log\frac{f_0}{a_{\mathbf x}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
There is no assertion that \(E(a)\) has an absolute lower bound in branches using a comparison with a spatial subfamily.

The tail profile is strictly decreasing on its positive indices because \(f_E-f_{E+1}=r^E>0\). Direct multiplication gives \(f_E^2-f_{E-1}f_{E+1}=r^{E+T}>0\) for \(1\le E<T\), also when \(r=1\). All finite-table hypotheses of `0cd230a98ae241c3` hold, with exactly (8). Its attained-value assertion and energy/spread estimate give
$$

\tag{9}
$$

The original-height consumer is
$$

\tag{10}
$$
Here is its precise applicability map and argument. The scalar calculation (45)-(46) of accepted `dbcf5be1e541e0cf` says that the geometric law \(q_j=r^j/\sum_{i=0}^m r^i\) on \(\lbrace0,\ldots,m\rbrace\), extended by zero, satisfies

This calculation has no spatial-potential hypothesis. For completeness, let \(L_j=\sum_{i\le j}q_i\), \(R_j=\sum_{i\ge j}q_i\), and \(d_j=\min(L_j,R_j)\). Fix an original spatial root \(\mathbf j\) and an integer \(E\) with \(\Gamma(\mathbf j)\le E\le T\). Suppose \(R\) is the number of oriented spatial displacements \(\mathbf h\) whose two endpoints have energy at most \(E\). For \(E\le t\le T\) and \(|h|\le\min(t-E,T-t)\), both \((\mathbf j+\mathbf h,t+h)\) and \((\mathbf j-\mathbf h,t-h)\) are original atoms and have midpoint \((\mathbf j,t)\). Apply (11) with its exact map \(m=T-E\), \(j=t-E\), and normalized height masses \(q^{(E)}_t=r^t/f_E\). Cubing this depth bound and multiplying by the original root mass \(r^t/Z\) proves

Apply (12) once and sum over distinct spatial roots. No separate levels are added at the same sampled root. This proves (10).

\tag{34}
$$
In particular \(L(s)=40\sqrt{V_s}/s\) when \(s>0\); its finite value at zero is defined by the displayed exact expectations. For the fixed finite parameters in the theorem, minimize on \([0,s_{\max}]\) the continuous function
$$
\Psi(s)=\mathcal F(s)+\frac14\sum_i\log P_{g_i}(s)
-\int_0^sL(y)\,dy.
\tag{35}
$$
At zero, the only active index of \(\mathcal F\) is zero, since \(f_t<f_0\) for \(t>0\). Its right derivative is therefore zero. Equations (32)-(35) give \(\Psi'_+(0)=-\mu(0)-L(0)<0\). Hence a minimizing rate exists and is positive.

The defining maximum gives, for any \(s>0\) and \(t\in\mathcal J(s)\), the original-mass upper bound
$$
Z\le f_0e^{\mathcal F(s)}\prod_i Z_{g_i}(s)
=f_t e^{st}\prod_i Z_{g_i}(s).
\tag{36}
$$
Indeed \(a_{\mathbf x}\le f_0e^{\mathcal F(s)}e^{-s\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\) at every feasible spatial site, and summing over the whole box only enlarges its right side.

At an interior minimizing rate, the left and right derivatives of \(\mathcal F\) are its smallest and largest active integer indices. The derivative identity (32) therefore brackets \(\mu(s)+L(s)\) between these two indices. Logconcavity of \(f_t\) makes the active indices a consecutive integer interval: the concave interpolation of \(\log(f_t/f_0)+st\) is constant between maximizing indices. We may choose an active integer \(t_\star\) such that
$$
\mu(s)+L(s)\le t_\star\le\mu(s)+L(s)+1.
\tag{37}
$$
If the minimum is at \(s=s_{\max}\), choose the smallest active index. The nonpositive left derivative of \(\Psi\) instead gives
$$
t_\star\le\mu(s)+L(s).
\tag{38}
$$

For an interior minimum sample the roots independently with laws \(Q_{s,i}\), giving product law \(\mathcal Q_s\). Call a root \(\mathbf j\) good when
$$
\left|s\sum_i\alpha_{s,i}(j_i)-s\mu(s)\right|\le8\sqrt{V_s},
\qquad
s^2\sum_i[v_{s,i}^e(j_i)+v_{s,i}^b(j_i)]\le64V_s.
\tag{39}
$$
By (31), independence, Chebyshev, Markov, and a union bound, its probability is at least \(1-3/64>1/2\). Here \(V_s\ge ks^2>0\). At a fixed good root, sample coordinate displacements independently with their conditional laws (25). A second Chebyshev union bound shows that the event
$$
\left|s\left(\sum_i e_{i,j_i}(V_i)-\sum_i m_{s,i}(j_i)\right)\right|\le16\sqrt{V_s},
\qquad
\left|s\sum_i b_{i,j_i}(V_i)\right|\le16\sqrt{V_s}
\tag{40}
$$
has probability at least \(3/4\), hence at least \(1/2\). In fact the sum of its two failure bounds is at most \(64V_s/(256V_s)=1/4\); conditional independence of excess and imbalance in one coordinate is not needed.

The exact endpoint energies are
$$
\Gamma(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf h)=\Gamma(\mathbf j)
+\sum_i e_{i,j_i}(h_i)\pm\sum_i b_{i,j_i}(h_i).
$$
By (37), (39), and (40), both are at most \(\mu(s)+40\sqrt{V_s}/s\le t_\star\). The root also has energy at most \(t_\star\), since all conditional excess means are nonnegative. Thus these roots and displacements are legal in the actual weak level \(A^a_{f_{t_\star}}\).

The reciprocal conditional density on event (40), using its lower excess bound, shows that the number of its distinct displacements at this root is at least
$$
\frac12\left(\prod_i H_{s,i}(j_i)\right)
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}\sum_i m_{s,i}(j_i)-\frac{64}{3}\sqrt{V_s}\right).
\tag{41}
$$
The map \(\mathbf h\mapsto\mathbf j+\mathbf h\) is injective, so this is also a lower bound on its reflected count. The exact product identity is
$$
\prod_iH_{s,i}(j_i)^3
=e^{4s\Gamma(\mathbf j)}\left(\prod_iP_{g_i}(s)\right)\mathcal Q_s(\mathbf j).
$$
Cube (41), use the single legal threshold \(f_{t_\star}\) at each good root, sum, and use the lower side of (39) and good-root probability at least \(1/2\). The result is
$$
E(a)\ge\frac1{16}f_{t_\star}^4
\left(\prod_iP_{g_i}(s)\right)
\exp\left(4s\mu(s)-96\sqrt{V_s}\right).
\tag{42}
$$
The threshold need not itself be an attained spatial energy: it is a permitted weak level in the definition (8). Divide by (36) to the fourth power and use (37), which costs at most \(160\sqrt{V_s}+4s\) in the exponent. We obtain
$$
\frac{E(a)}{Z^4}\ge\frac1{16}
\exp\left(\sum_i\delta_{g_i}(s)-256\sqrt{V_s}-4s\right)8^{-k}.
\tag{43}
$$

We first settle the endpoint alternative (38). In (27) there is exactly one zero-energy tuple, at root 0, and all other tuples have energy at least 4. Thus
$$
P_{g_i}(s/2)-1\le55e^{-2s}.
$$
At \(s=s_{\max}\), its right side is \(55\cdot2^{-24}<1\). Since \(ye^{-y}\le(2/e)e^{-y/2}\), (27) and (32) give
$$
s\mu_{g_i}(s)=\frac1{4P_{g_i}(s)}\sum_{\Xi>0}(s\Xi)e^{-s\Xi}<\frac14.
$$
Also \(g_i(1)=1\), \(B\ge2\), and \(C_{4,i}\ge3\) imply
$$
Z_{g_i}(s)\le1+2^{-12}+2^{-24}+2^{-36},
\qquad \log Z_{g_i}(s)<1/16.
$$
Using \(f_{t_\star}\le f_0\), (28), (36), and (38), we find
$$
\log(Z/f_0)\le st_\star+\sum_i\log Z_{g_i}(s)
\le\frac5{16}k+40\sqrt{V_s}
<\frac5{16}k+640\sqrt{k}.
\tag{44}
$$
The original spatial root zero has its legal self-reflection at level \(f_0\), so \(E(a)\ge f_0^4\). Since \(k\ge N\ge2^{1024}\), (44) gives
$$
E(a)/Z^4\ge e^{-(5/4)k-2560\sqrt{k}}
\ge e^{-3k/2}\ge8^{-k}.
$$
Equation (10) proves (2) in this case.

For an interior minimum, (28) gives \(V_s<256k\). If \(s\ge1\), or if \(s\le1\) and \(v=sB\le24\), equations (21)-(22) give \(\sum_i\delta_{g_i}(s)\ge k\varepsilon\). The definition of \(N\) ensures
$$
k\varepsilon\ge2^{20}\sqrt{k}>4096\sqrt{k}+48
>256\sqrt{V_s}+4s.
$$
Consequently (43) gives \(E(a)/Z^4\ge8^{-k}/16\). Combined with (10), this is (2) with coefficient \(1/7168\). The same conclusion holds in the near-binary phase whenever
$$
\sum_i\delta_{g_i}(s)\ge256\sqrt{V_s}+4s.
\tag{45}
$$

It remains to consider an interior minimum with \(0<s\le1\), \(v=sB\ge24\), at which (45) fails. Define
$$
U=ks^2,\qquad Q=k e^{-2v/3},\qquad G=U+Q.
$$
From (23) and (29),
$$
\sum_i\delta_{g_i}(s)\ge G/1024,
\qquad V_s\le2U+128v^2Q.
$$
Also \(\sqrt{V_s}\ge\sqrt{k}s\ge s\), so failure of (45) yields
$$
G<2^{19}\sqrt{V_s}\le2^{19}\sqrt{2U+128v^2Q}.
$$
Squaring and using \(U,Q\le G\) and \(v\ge24\) gives
$$
G<2^{38}(2+128v^2)<2^{46}v^2.
\tag{46}
$$
In particular
$$
\frac{k}{B}=\frac{\sqrt{k}\sqrt U}{v}<2^{23}\sqrt{k},
\qquad
Q=\sqrt{k}\sqrt Q\,e^{-v/3}<2^{25}\sqrt{k}.
\tag{47}
$$
For the second bound use \(\sqrt Q<2^{23}v\) from (46) and \(ve^{-v/3}\le3/e<2\). Similarly
$$
\frac{\sqrt{V_s}}v
\le\sqrt{2U/v^2}+\sqrt{128Q}
<2^{24}+16\sqrt{k}<2^{25}\sqrt{k}.
\tag{48}
$$
Summing the mean bound in (29) gives \(\mu(s)/B\le k/B+64Q\). Equations (37), (47), and (48), with \(B\ge2\), therefore imply
$$
\frac{t_\star}{B}
\le\frac{k}{B}+64Q+40\frac{\sqrt{V_s}}v+\frac1B
<H\sqrt{k},\qquad H=2^{36}.
\tag{49}
$$
This is the only occupation consequence needed from a bad near-binary saddle.

If \(T/B\le2H\sqrt{k}\), then \(R_T\le2H\sqrt{k}\), so (17) with \(A=2H\) proves (2) with coefficient \(\kappa_3e^{-4096H^2}\).

Finally suppose \(T/B>2H\sqrt{k}\). By (49), \(t_\star<T/2<T\). Its being an active integer index in (33), compared with its forward neighbor, implies
$$
s\le\log\frac{f_{t_\star}}{f_{t_\star+1}}
\le\beta+\frac1{T-t_\star}\le\beta+\frac2T.
\tag{50}
$$
For the middle inequality put \(\ell=T-t_\star\ge1\). The tail ratio is \(e^\beta(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}r^j)/(\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}r^j)\); its latter factor is at most \(1+1/\ell\). This verifies (50) including \(\beta=0\). By (16),
$$
v=sB\le\beta B+2B/T<\log k+1,
\qquad Q>e^{-2/3}k^{1/3}.
\tag{51}
$$
But (46) says \(Q<2^{46}(\log k+1)^2\), contradicting \(k\ge N\ge2^{1024}\). To check the numerical cutoff without an asymptotic assertion, set \(\ell=\log k>512\), using \(\log2>1/2\). The function \(e^{\ell/3}/(\ell+1)^2\) is increasing for \(\ell>5\). At 512, \(e>2\), \(e^{2/3}<4\), and \(513^2<2^{20}\) give
$$
\frac{e^{-2/3}e^{512/3}}{513^2}>2^{148}>2^{46}.
$$
Thus (51) and (46) cannot simultaneously hold. The last case is empty.

All cases are covered with the coefficient (7). Bounded dimension used the complete original spatial mass \(M_a=Z\). The binary and three-site branches explicitly compared portions of that mass and paid their fourth-power ratios in the original moment. The saddle used only original feasible roots, weak levels, reflected endpoints, and heights, and retained \(Z^4\) throughout (10), (36), and (43). The original-height reflection argument used closed halfspaces and the scalar geometric law with its singleton and zero-tilt cases. Hence no cap, equality, or ambient-halfspace boundary atom was lost. This proves (2) for coordinate-dependent fourth costs and, by taking \(C_{4,i}=C_4\), for the registered homogeneous family.
