## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $d\ge1$, with a nonempty finite positive $d$-volume integer fiber support $P$. Write $K_z=C_z$ for $z\in P$, and replace $C$ by the convex hull of those lifted fibers. Set $Q=\operatorname{conv}P$ and $r_z=|K_z|^{1/d}$. Then $P=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n$, and the finite concave polyhedral roof
$$
R(x)=\max\left\{\sum_z\lambda_z r_z:\ \lambda\ge0,\ \sum_z\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_z\lambda_z z=x\right\}
$$
is attained for every $x\in Q$ and equals $r_z$ at every sampled base. Its maximal affine patches form a subdivision of $Q$. For any supporting affine majorant $\ell$ on the relative base, its equality patch is the hull of $E=\{z\in P:r_z=\ell(z)\}$. At every integer base in this patch the section of the hull of the lifted centroids over $E$ is exactly the corresponding actual centroid singleton.

If such a patch contains an integer point in its relative interior, the entire real centroid hull on the patch is the graph of one affine section. Without this hypothesis, the conclusion can fail even for a roof that is globally affine. Over the four binary bases in $\mathbb Z^2$, take four intervals of length one with centroid heights $+1$ at $00,11$ and $-1$ at $10,01$. Their lifted hull has one root patch, the entire square, and no affine map realizes its four centroid values. The centroid hull over the real center of the square has section $[-1,1]$, while the actual interval there is $[-3/2,3/2]$.

## Proof

Taking the indicated hull preserves every $K_z$ at a sampled base because the hull is contained in the original $C$ and contains all sampled fibers. If an integer $w$ is a convex combination of bases in $P$, the corresponding weighted Minkowski sum has positive $d$-volume and belongs to the hull fiber at $w$. Thus $w\in P$. The nonempty compact representation polytopes give attainment. The hypograph is the convex hull of the downward vertical rays starting at the sampled heights, so $R$ is concave and polyhedral. At an integer base $w$, Brunn–Minkowski and the anchored Minkowski inclusion give $r_w\ge\sum_z\lambda_zr_z$ for every representation, and the singleton attains equality.

In the relative affine space the hypograph is a polyhedron with full dimension, including when the base has rank zero. Its inequalities with positive height coefficient give finitely many affine upper bounds, and at every point of $Q$ some one of these is tight at the upper endpoint. Indeed the remaining facet inequalities only constrain the base, since the hypograph extends downwards without limit. The maximal affine patches are the projections of the upper facets, and intersections are faces because the graph projects affinely and injectively on each upper face. For any affine supporting majorant, the difference $\ell(x)-R(x)$ is the weighted sum of the nonnegative sample gaps in any maximizing representation. Equality therefore forces support only on $E$, and a mixture supported on $E$ conversely gives equality.

Extend $\ell$ affinely to the original base space. In the replaced hull every nonempty integer fiber has dimension $d$ and its root is the sampled root. Thus 1f98d2e726475e3a applies with intrinsic dimension $q=d$ and with exactly this positive contact set $E$. Its anchored centroid identity shows that the section of the centroid hull at each integer $w\in\operatorname{conv}E$ equals $\{g_w\}$.

If $w$ is in the relative interior of the patch and the centroid hull had two distinct points over any real base $x$ in the patch, choose $s\in(0,1)$ small enough that $x'=(w-sx)/(1-s)$ also lies in the patch. Combine each of the two points with one fixed point of the hull above $x'$ to obtain two distinct points over $w$, a contradiction. The hull therefore gives a single-valued graph on the patch. Its convexity gives the affine interpolation identity for every pair of real bases and every weight in $[0,1]$, so the section is affine.

For the square, take $L=4$ in accepted fact a4bfc45c35ac42b8 and apply the affine shear $(s,t,u)\mapsto(s,t,u+1/2-2s-2t)$. This preserves the base and each interval's length, and maps the four interval centers $(1/2,1/2,1/2,9/2)$ in order $00,10,01,11$ to $(1,-1,-1,1)$. The new roof is constant one since all four sampled lengths are one. The centroid sums on the two diagonals differ, ruling out affinity. At base $(1/2,1/2)$ the preimage interval is $[0,3]$ by the accepted exact real fiber formula, so the new section is $[-3/2,3/2]$. In the centroid hull all heights lie in $[-1,1]$, and the two diagonal averages attain both endpoints over the real center. Hence the centroid hull has the claimed interval there. The full dimension also follows directly from the four centroid points, which are affinely independent because the two diagonal sums differ.
