## Statement

Let $n,d\ge0$, let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, and let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ be nonempty. Put $q=\dim_H S$ and let $P$ be the finite set of integer bases whose fibers $C_z$ have affine dimension $q$, equivalently whose fibers have positive $\mathcal H_q$ measure. Let $k$ be the number of coordinate parity classes occupied by $P$. Fix a finite family of closed ambient halfspaces and an integer $t\ge1$. If for every $z\in P$ some at most $t$ of the original halfspaces cover the entire fiber at $z$, then at most $kt$ of the original halfspaces simultaneously cover every fiber over $P$.

## Proof

There are finitely many nonempty integer fibers, each compact convex. Their maximum affine dimension is $q$ by the finite union property of Hausdorff dimension. If $z,w\in P$ have the same coordinate parity, the integer midpoint fiber contains $(C_z+C_w)/2$. Fixing one point of $C_w$ shows that this Minkowski sum has dimension at least $q$, and the integer fiber cannot have larger dimension. Therefore the midpoint belongs to $P$.

The finite-set lemma proved in fact bee3cc8e50a0dcea applies directly to $P$. Every subset $R\subseteq P$ consisting of vertices of its own hull and with $\operatorname{conv}R\cap P=R$ has at most $k$ points: two points of the same parity would have a midpoint in $\operatorname{conv}R\cap P$ that is not a vertex. The lemma consequently gives a Helly bound of $k$ for arbitrary convex sets tested on $P$, without requiring closedness.

For each nonempty list $J$ of at most $t$ original indices form $E_J=\pi(C\cap\bigcap_{i\in J}H_i^c)$, where $\pi$ projects onto the base. This set is convex and may be nonclosed. For $z\in P$, membership $z\in E_J$ means that a point of the actual fiber misses every cap in $J$. The local premise thus makes the intersection of all $E_J$ disjoint from $P$. The Helly bound selects at most $k$ lists whose residual projections still have intersection disjoint from $P$. At each $z$ some selected list covers its entire fiber. Their union uses at most $kt$ original halfspaces and has the stated simultaneous coverage. This argument includes $q=0$, where each nonempty integer fiber is a singleton.
