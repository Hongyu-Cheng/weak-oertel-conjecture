## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\) be integers, let \(1<R<2\), and put \(a=1-1/R\in(0,1/2)\). Let

\[
\Delta_d=\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^d:y_j\geq0\text{ for all }j,\ \sum_{j=1}^d y_j\leq1\},
\qquad
C=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):\mathbf y\in\Delta_d,\ |x_i|\leq R(1-y_1)\text{ for all }i\}.
\]

Write \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), let \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\), and let \(Y\) have the normalized original continuous marginal of mixed-integer volume on \(S\). Thus its density relative to Lebesgue measure on \(\Delta_d\) is proportional to the actual horizontal lattice count

\[
N(\mathbf y)=
\begin{cases}
3^n,&y_1\leq a,\\
1,&y_1>a.
\end{cases}
\]

The value on the boundary \(y_1=a\) has no effect on this distribution. Let \(\mathbf m=\mathbb E Y\), the centroid before any cap is imposed. Then every closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(\mathbf m\) satisfies
\(\mathbb P(Y\in G)\geq 3^{-n/2}/432\geq 2^{-n}/432\).
Moreover \((\mathbf0,\mathbf m)\in S\), and its depth in the notation of the locked problem satisfies
\(h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf m))\geq 3^{-n/2}W/864\geq 2^{-n}W/864\).
No accepted predecessors are used.

## Proof

Set \(M=3^n\). Under normalized Lebesgue measure on \(\Delta_d\), the first coordinate has density \(g(t)=d(1-t)^{d-1}\) on \([0,1]\). Indeed, its slice at \(t\) is the \((d-1)\)-simplex scaled by \(1-t\); this also gives \(g(t)=1\) when \(d=1\). Define \(p=1-(1-a)^d\), \(q=(1-a)^d\), and \(Z=Mp+q=1+(M-1)p\). Consequently \(W=Z/d!>0\), and \(T=Y_1\) has density

\[
f(t)=\frac{g(t)}Z
\begin{cases}
M,&0\leq t\leq a,\\
1,&a<t\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]

Put \(\mu=\mathbb E T\). Integration gives

\[
\mu
=\frac{M-(M-1)q(1+da)}{(d+1)Z}
=\frac1{d+1}-\frac{(M-1)da q}{(d+1)Z}
\leq\frac1{d+1}.
\]

The remaining coordinates, including the slack coordinate \(Y_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^dY_j\), are exchangeable conditionally on \(T\), and their sum is \(1-T\). Therefore \(m_1=\mu\) and \(m_j=(1-\mu)/d\) for \(2\leq j\leq d\). In particular \(\mathbf m\in\Delta_d\) and \((\mathbf0,\mathbf m)\in S\).

We first prove the two one-dimensional mean-tail bounds
\(\mathbb P(T\leq\mu)\geq1/2\) and \(\mathbb P(T\geq\mu)\geq1/(12\sqrt M)\).
The density \(f\) is nonincreasing, including its downward jump at \(a\). Its cumulative distribution function \(F\) is thus concave on \([0,1]\). The distribution is continuous and strictly increasing there, so \(F(T)\) is uniform on \([0,1]\). Jensen's inequality yields \(F(\mu)\geq\mathbb E F(T)=1/2\).

For the other tail, if \(Z\leq2\sqrt M\), then \(f\geq g/Z\) and the upper bound on \(\mu\) gives

\[
\mathbb P(T\geq\mu)
\geq\frac{(1-\mu)^d}{Z}
\geq\frac1Z\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
\geq\frac1{3Z}
\geq\frac1{6\sqrt M}.
\]

Here \((1+1/d)^d\leq e<3\), so the penultimate inequality holds in every positive dimension.

Suppose instead that \(Z>2\sqrt M\). Let \(L\) have the baseline density \(g(t)/p\) on \([0,a]\), and put \(\ell=\mathbb E L\). Its survival function on \([0,a)\) is \(s(t)=((1-t)^d-q)/p\). The function \(B(t)=-\log s(t)\) is convex: explicitly,

\[
B'(t)=\frac d{(1-t)-q(1-t)^{1-d}},
\]

whose positive denominator decreases with \(t\), because its derivative is \(-1-(d-1)q(1-t)^{-d}<0\). Since \(s(L)\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), \(\mathbb E B(L)=1\). Jensen's inequality therefore implies \(B(\ell)\leq1\), and hence \(\mathbb P(L\geq\ell)\geq e^{-1}>1/3\).

Let \(u\) be the mean of the baseline distribution conditioned on \([a,1]\). Substituting \(t=a+(1-a)r\) gives \(u=a+(1-a)/(d+1)\). The original distribution is the mixture with weights \(Mp/Z\) and \(q/Z\) of these lower and upper conditional distributions. Consequently
\(\mu-\ell=(q/Z)(u-\ell)\geq0\), while its mass above \(\ell\) is at least \((Mp/Z)/3+q/Z\geq1/3\).
Also \(f(t)\leq Md/Z\) everywhere. It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(T\geq\mu)
&\geq\frac13-\frac{Md}{Z}(\mu-\ell)\\
&\geq\frac13-\frac{Mqd u}{Z^2}
\geq\frac13-\frac M{Z^2}
>\frac1{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last estimate involving \(u\), note that \(du\leq da+1\) and \(q(da+1)\leq1\). The latter follows directly from
\((1-a)^{-d}=(1+a/(1-a))^d\geq1+da/(1-a)\geq1+da\).
As \(M\geq1\), this proves the desired upper tail bound in the second case as well. These tail bounds concern the original mean \(\mu\), not a smoothed or conditioned centroid.

We next give an elementary uniform estimate for the conditional simplex noise. For \(d\geq2\), let \((U_0,U_2,\ldots,U_d)\) be uniform on the barycentric simplex of \(d\) nonnegative coordinates summing to one. For arbitrary real coefficients \(c_j\) with \(\sum_{j\ne1}c_j=0\), put \(V=\sum_{j\ne1}c_jU_j\), \(p_2=\sum_{j\ne1}c_j^2\), and \(p_4=\sum_{j\ne1}c_j^4\). Then both \(\mathbb P(V\geq0)\) and \(\mathbb P(V\leq0)\) are at least \(1/36\).

To verify this, elementary integration of monomials over the simplex gives, for nonnegative integers \(k_j\) summing to \(r\),
\(\mathbb E\prod_{j\ne1}U_j^{k_j}=(d-1)!\prod_{j\ne1}k_j!/(d+r-1)!\).
This formula follows by repeated integration using \(\int_0^1t^k(1-t)^l\,dt=k!l!/(k+l+1)!\). Expanding the second and fourth powers and using \(\sum c_j=0\) gives

\[
\mathbb E V=0,\qquad
\sigma^2:=\mathbb E V^2=\frac{p_2}{d(d+1)},\qquad
\mathbb E V^4=\frac{3p_2^2+6p_4}{d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}\leq9\sigma^4.
\]

If \(p_2=0\), then \(V=0\) and the assertion is immediate. Otherwise Hölder's inequality gives
\(\sigma^2\leq(\mathbb E|V|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E V^4)^{1/3}\), so \(\mathbb E|V|\geq\sigma/3\). Since \(\mathbb E V=0\), \(\mathbb E V_+=\mathbb E(-V)_+=\mathbb E|V|/2\geq\sigma/6\). Cauchy-Schwarz then gives
\(\mathbb E V_+\leq\sigma\sqrt{\mathbb P(V>0)}\), and the analogous inequality for \(-V\). Thus each strict sign probability is at least \(1/36\). When \(d=1\), the conditional simplex is the single coordinate \(U_0=1\), and its centered noise is identically zero; the same non-strict sign bounds hold.

In the original distribution, \((Y_0,Y_2,\ldots,Y_d)=(1-T)(U_0,U_2,\ldots,U_d)\), with \(U\) independent of \(T\) and with exactly the preceding uniform simplex law. This follows from the slice description and the fact that the weight \(N(\mathbf y)\) depends only on \(T\).

Let a continuous normal be \(\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_d)\), and set \(\beta_0=0\), \(\bar\beta=d^{-1}\sum_{j\ne1}\beta_j\), \(b=\beta_1-\bar\beta\), and \(c_j=\beta_j-\bar\beta\) for \(j\ne1\). These coefficients sum to zero. With \(V\) as above, the exact centered projection is

\[
\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(Y-\mathbf m)=b(T-\mu)+(1-T)V.
\]

If \(b\geq0\), the event \(\{T\geq\mu,V\geq0\}\) lies in \(\{\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(Y-\mathbf m)\geq0\}\). If \(b<0\), the same is true of \(\{T\leq\mu,V\geq0\}\). Independence, the two mean-tail bounds, and the noise bound therefore show in every case that
\(\mathbb P(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(Y-\mathbf m)\geq0)\geq1/(432\sqrt M)\).
This includes degenerate noise and \(d=1\). Any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\) contains the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf m\), so the continuous conclusion follows for every such halfspace.

Finally, under normalized mixed-integer volume on \(S\), conditionally on \(Y=\mathbf y\), the integer vector \(X\) is uniform on \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\) when \(y_1\leq a\), and equals \(\mathbf0\) when \(y_1>a\). Both laws are invariant under \(X\mapsto-X\). Thus for every integer-coordinate normal \(\boldsymbol\alpha\), at least half of this conditional law satisfies \(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot X\geq0\). For any mixed normal \((\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)\), this proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\bigl(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot X+\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(Y-\mathbf m)\geq0\bigr)
&\geq\frac12\mathbb P\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta\cdot(Y-\mathbf m)\geq0\bigr)\\
&\geq\frac1{864\sqrt M}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality restricts to continuous points with nonnegative continuous contribution, where every integer point with nonnegative integer contribution is retained. Translating a halfspace containing \((\mathbf0,\mathbf m)\) to the parallel boundary through that point can only decrease its mass. Multiplying the resulting probability bound by \(W\), and using \(\sqrt M=3^{n/2}\leq2^n\), gives the asserted actual depth bound.
