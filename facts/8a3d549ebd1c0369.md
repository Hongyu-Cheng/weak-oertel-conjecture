## Statement

Let $D=\{(z,x):-1\le z\le1,\ 0\le x\le3-2|z|\}$ and $C=\{(z,x,t):(z,x)\in D,\ -50\le t\le31/10-x\}$, with $z$ integer. The section $t=0$ has total mixed length $5$ and centroid $(0,11/10)$. At its lift $(0,11/10,0)$, the cap $t\ge0$ cuts every positive fiber properly and attains raw depth $10$ out of total mixed area $260$. The section's unique deepest point is $(0,1)$ with raw section depth $2$, strictly more than the section depth of the centroid. The lift $(0,1,0)$ has original raw depth at most $799/80<10$. The minimizing section direction $x\ge1$ at $(0,1)$ captures a fraction $2/5$ of section length but only $11/50<2/9$ of the original positive cap area, and it captures a fraction $2/5$ of the negative cap area.

## Proof

The affine roof $g(z,x)=31/10-x$ is positive on $D$. The integer bases are exactly $-1,0,1$, with section intervals $[0,1],[0,3],[0,1]$. Their lengths and first moments give total length $5$ and centroid $(0,11/10)$. In centered coordinates $X=(z,s)$ with $s=x-11/10$, the section support has full affine span and its radius is $R=19/10$. The central interval attains this radius, and the squared norm of every outer point is at most $221/100<(19/10)^2$. For a unit vector $(a,b)$, zero mean gives a normalized positive moment of $\frac1{10}\int|az+bs|\,d\nu$. Pairing the two outer intervals at equal $s$ gives at least $2|a|$ in the integral. The subinterval $[-11/10,11/10]$ in the middle gives at least $(121/100)|b|$. Therefore the minimum normalized positive moment is at least $121/1000$, using $|a|+|b|\ge1$. With $L=50$, this gives $Lm/R\ge121/38>31/10=\max_Dg$. The centered-section roof realization theorem applies and proves that $t\ge0$ minimizes over all original containing halfspaces. The cap's two outer fiber areas are $13/5$ each and its middle fiber area is $24/5$. The total body adds lower areas $50,150,50$, so all three fibers are positive, each cut is proper, and the total is $260$.

For the section point $(0,1)$, a through-point upward cap $x+a z\ge1$ takes at least two from the middle. A downward cap $x+a z\le1$ takes one from the middle and at least one full outer interval, because one outer threshold is at least one. A pure-base cap takes at least four. The untilted upward cap attains two. At a middle point with $x<1$, a sufficiently large finite tilt of the downward cap takes exactly $x+1<2$. For $x>1$, the untilted upward cap takes $3-x<2$. On either outer base, a finite base tilt can isolate an own interval cap of length at most $1/2$. Its shifted base coefficient is zero at that base and strictly negative at the other two bases, and a large positive multiple excludes their bounded intervals. Thus the section maximizer is unique. At the section centroid the untilted upward cap has mass $19/10<2$.

The original cap $t\ge(x-1)/40$ contains $(0,1,0)$ and its threshold stays between $-1/40$ and $1/20$, strictly between the lower roof $-50$ and the upper roof $g\ge1/10$ on every fiber. Its area is $10-\frac1{40}\int_D(x-1)\,d\nu=10-\frac1{40}(11/2-5)=799/80$. In the direction $x\ge1$ only the middle interval contributes positive length. Its upper cap area there is $\int_1^3(31/10-x)\,dx=11/5$ out of $10$, while the negative area there is $50(3-1)=100$ out of $250$. These give the stated fractions. The original source is shallower than the actual point $(0,3/2,-25)$, whose own fiber contains a rectangle of area $150$ symmetric about that point.
