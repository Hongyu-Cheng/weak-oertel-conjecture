## Statement

Let $K$ range over nondegenerate planar triangles, $y$ over their interior points, and $v_1,v_2,v_3$ over nonzero spanning normals with $\sum_i v_i=0$. Require that the cap $v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0$ strictly capture vertex $P_i$ and strictly exclude the other two vertices, with the $P_i$ distinct. Let $k=|K|$, let $Q_i$ be the cap areas, let $\sigma_i$ be the cutting chord lengths divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, and set
$$
 f_i=\frac{2\sqrt{kQ_i}}{\sigma_i\sum_\ell Q_\ell/\sigma_\ell}.
$$
With indices modulo three, the set of these $f$-vectors is exactly
$$
 f_i=\sqrt{g_i^2-d_i^2},\qquad g_i=1-d_{i+1}+d_{i+2},\qquad \sum_i|d_i|<1.
$$
Equivalently, the set is exactly
$$
f_i=2\sqrt{u_i(1-u_{i+1})},\qquad
0<u_i<1,\qquad 1<\sum_i u_i<2 .
$$
Its closure is the image given by replacing the strict inequalities in either domain with weak inequalities.

## Proof

Use the normalized signed heights $a_i,b_{ij}$ from the signed-row theorem for these strict triangle caps. Its chord–height relation gives $Q_i/\sigma_i=Ha_i/2$, where $H$ is the total unnormalized diagonal height. Since $\sum_i a_i=1$, the $f_i$ here equals $2a_i/\sqrt{Q_i/k}$, as in the signed-row theorem. Put $s_i=b_{i,i+1}+b_{i,i+2}$ and $d_i=b_{i,i+1}-b_{i,i+2}$. Then $\sum_i s_i=1$ by the column identities and $|d_i|<s_i$ by strict signs. The same theorem yields $f_i=\sqrt{g_i^2-d_i^2}$ with the stated $g_i$. This proves necessity.

Conversely, let $\varepsilon=1-\sum_i|d_i|>0$ and set
$$
s_i=|d_i|+\varepsilon/3,\qquad
b_{i,i+1}=(s_i+d_i)/2,\qquad b_{i,i+2}=(s_i-d_i)/2,\qquad a_i=(g_i-s_i)/2.
$$
All $b_{ij}>0$. Since $g_i-|d_i|\ge\varepsilon$, each $a_i\ge\varepsilon/3>0$. The sums of the $s_i$ and $g_i$ are $1$ and $3$, respectively, so $\sum_i a_i=1$. Substitution gives $2\sum_{\ell\ne i}b_{\ell i}=s_{i+1}-d_{i+1}+s_{i+2}+d_{i+2}=g_i-s_i=2a_i$.

Let $M$ be the matrix with diagonal $a_i$ and off-diagonal entries $-b_{ij}$. Its columns sum to zero. Each principal two-by-two minor, for distinct $i,j$ and the remaining index $\ell$, equals
$$
a_i a_j-b_{ij}b_{ji}=(b_{ji}+b_{\ell i})(b_{ij}+b_{\ell j})-b_{ij}b_{ji}>0.
$$
Thus $M$ has rank two. Each row of $\operatorname{adj} M$ is in the left kernel of $M$, which is spanned by $(1,1,1)$. Hence the columns of $\operatorname{adj} M$ coincide, and their entries are the positive principal cofactors. Normalizing this common column gives a positive right null vector of $M$ with coordinate sum one.

Take $P_j=(M_{1j},M_{2j})$ in the plane. An affine dependence among the $P_j$ would give a vector in $\ker M$ with zero coordinate sum, because row three is minus the first two rows. The right kernel is one-dimensional and spanned by the positive vector of coordinate sum one, so there is no nonzero affine dependence. The same vector expresses $0$ as a strict convex combination of the $P_j$. With source $0$ and normals $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$, the signed margins are exactly $M$. In particular all incidences are strict, the total positive diagonal height is one, and the signed-row formula yields the prescribed $f$.

For the other coordinates let $u_i=(g_i+d_i)/2$. Then $(g_i-d_i)/2=1-u_{i+1}$ and $d_i=u_i+u_{i+1}-1$, giving the asserted formula. The eight sign inequalities $\sum_i \epsilon_i d_i<1$ for $\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}$ describe $\sum_i|d_i|<1$. The pair for the all-plus and all-minus signs gives $1<\sum_i u_i<2$ because $\sum_i u_i=(3+\sum_i d_i)/2$. The other six give $0<u_i<1$ because $2u_i-1=d_i-d_{i+1}+d_{i+2}$. The same equivalence holds with weak inequalities.

On the closed $\ell_1$ ball, $g_i\ge|d_i|$ so the formula is continuous with a compact image. Conversely, any boundary parameter is a limit of its contractions toward zero, all in the open ball. Thus the closed image is exactly the closure of the strict triangle image.
