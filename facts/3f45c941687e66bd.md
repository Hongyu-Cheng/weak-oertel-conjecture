## Statement

Put $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$, $r_h=1-h/10$ for $h=0,\ldots,9$, $t=1/3$ and $\beta=1/60$. Use the complete integer fibers $r_hT$ at bases $-h$ and $h+1$ of the body defined by the piecewise-affine radius interpolation. If the law of $w\cdot X$ for $X$ uniform on $T$, $w_i\ge0$ and $w_1+w_2=1$ is relaxed to an arbitrary $1/2$-concave probability law supported in $[0,1]$ of mean $1/3$, the bound
$$
\sum_{h=0}^9 r_h^2\left[\mathbb P(r_hY\ge t-\beta h)+\mathbb P(r_hY\ge t+\beta(h+1))\right]\ge \frac49\sum_{h=0}^9 r_h^2
$$
fails. The uniform law on $[1/3-1/600,1/3+1/600]$ is nonatomic and log-concave as well as $1/2$-concave, and gives the left side $1/2$ versus the right side $77/45$. Every genuine normalized nonnegative projection has zero in its support and variance at least $1/72$. This relaxed law has variance $1/1080000$ and its support excludes zero.

## Proof

The real radius interpolation on $[-9,10]$ is positive and concave, with slopes $1/10,0,-1/10$ in that order. Its simplex subgraph is a compact convex body with interior and the stated integer fibers. The normalization by the area of $T$ accounts for the multiplier $r_h^2$ in each tail.

The uniform law on the stated interval $I$ has constant positive density there and zero elsewhere, hence is log-concave. It is also a $1$-concave measure on sets of positive measure. For compact nonempty $A,B\subseteq I$ and $0\le s\le1$, the subsets $(1-s)A+s\min B$ and $(1-s)\max A+sB$ of $(1-s)A+sB$ meet only at their common endpoint. Their lengths add to $(1-s)|A|+s|B|$. Divide by $|I|$ and use compact inner approximation for positive-measure sets after intersecting with $I$. This proves the $1$-concavity inequality. Applying the concavity of the square root to that inequality gives $1/2$-concavity. The midpoint is its mean.

For $h\ge1$ the left threshold exceeds $r_h/3$ by $h/60$, and for every $h\ge0$ the right threshold exceeds $r_h/3$ by $(2h+1)/60$. Both exceed the largest upward deviation $r_h/600\le1/600$. Thus the only nonzero tail is the left central term at its exact mean, of probability $1/2$. The sum of the squared radii is $(1^2+\cdots+10^2)/100=77/20$.

For the uniform probability on $T$, direct integration against density two gives $\mathbb E X_1=\mathbb E X_2=1/3$, $\mathbb E X_i^2=1/6$ and $\mathbb E X_1 X_2=1/12$. Consequently
$$
\operatorname{Var}(w\cdot X)=\frac{3(w_1^2+w_2^2)-1}{36}\ge\frac1{72}.
$$
In the inequality we used $w_1^2+w_2^2\ge1/2$. The relaxed interval has length $1/300$ and variance $(1/300)^2/12=1/1080000$. Moreover every relative neighborhood of the origin in the triangle has positive area and projects arbitrarily close to zero under $w\cdot X$. Thus zero lies in the support of every genuine projection. Both checks distinguish the relaxed probability law from the projection of the actual fiber volumes.
