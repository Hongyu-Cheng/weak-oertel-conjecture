## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with $0\in\operatorname{int}K$, and let $u_1,\ldots,u_m$ be unit vectors. Write $H_i=\{x:u_i\cdot x\ge0\}$, $V=|K|$, and $\Phi(K)=\sum_i\theta_i|K\cap H_i|/V$ for weights $\theta_i>0$ with $\sum_i\theta_i=1$. Let $h$ be the support function of $K$ on the unit sphere. Take a nonzero $C^2$ function $\psi\ge0$ on the sphere such that on an open neighborhood of $\operatorname{supp}\psi$, $h$ is $C^2$ and $A_h=\nabla_S^2 h+hI$ is positive definite on each spherical tangent space. Suppose the support points $X(n)=h(n)n+\nabla_S h(n)$ for $n\in\operatorname{supp}\psi$ satisfy $s_i u_i\cdot X(n)>0$ for fixed signs $s_i\in\{-1,1\}$ and every $i$.

For all sufficiently small signed $t$, $h_t=h+t\psi$ is the support function of a convex body $K_t$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}K_t$. The changed radial intervals are in $\{x:s_i u_i\cdot x>0\text{ for all }i\}$ and stay uniformly away from the cutting planes. In particular $K_t\cap u_i^\perp=K\cap u_i^\perp$ for all $i$. Set $V_t=|K_t|$, $\Delta V(t)=V_t-V$, $e_i=\mathbf1_{\{s_i=1\}}$, and $W=\sum_i\theta_i e_i$. Then
$$
|K_t\cap H_i|-|K\cap H_i|=e_i\Delta V(t),\qquad
\Phi(K_t)-\Phi(K)=(W-\Phi(K))\,\frac{\Delta V(t)}{V_t}.
$$
The volume $V_t$ is $C^1$ near $0$, $\Delta V(t)$ has the sign of $t$ for $t\ne0$, and
$$
V'_0=\int_{\partial K}\psi(n(x))\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}(x)>0
$$
where $n(x)$ is the outward unit normal wherever it is unique.

At a Hausdorff local minimum of $\Phi$ subject to the same section traces, every such patch has $W=\Phi(K)$. Group the $u_i$ by their unoriented planes. A positive-curvature patch crossing exactly one such plane at a point on no other distinct plane excludes a local minimum if the two orientations in that class have unequal total weights. If $h$ is $C^2$ and $A_h>0$ on the entire sphere, an imbalanced class always gives such a patch. If all classes balance, $\Phi(K)=1/2$ for every $K$.

## Proof

Choose an open neighborhood of the compact bump support with closure in the region where $h$ is $C^2$ and $A_h>0$. By continuity and compactness it can be chosen so the strict sign inequalities have a uniform positive margin there. On this neighborhood $A_{h_t}=A_h+t(\nabla_S^2\psi+\psi I)$ stays positive definite for small $|t|$. Also $\min h_t>0$ since $K$ contains a ball about zero. The one-homogeneous extension of $h_t$ is locally convex away from zero. On the neighborhood of the bump support its Hessian at $rn$ has tangential block $A_{h_t}(n)/r$ and zero radial and mixed blocks, by differentiating the homogeneous extension. Off the support it agrees locally with the original support function. Its restriction to any line avoiding zero is locally convex and thus convex. On lines through zero the two linear pieces have ordered slopes because $h_t(n)+h_t(-n)>0$. The extension is therefore convex on all of $\mathbb R^d$. A finite convex one-homogeneous function is the support function of the bounded closed convex set of linear forms it dominates, by the supporting-hyperplane theorem. Since $h_t$ has a positive minimum, this set contains a ball about zero. The bound $\|h_t-h\|_\infty=|t|\|\psi\|_\infty$ gives Hausdorff convergence by support comparison with the two outer parallel bodies.

At a normal in the chosen neighborhood the support face is the singleton
$$
X_t(n)=h_t(n)n+\nabla_S h_t(n)=X(n)+t(\psi(n)n+\nabla_S\psi(n)),
$$
since every point of the support face is a subgradient of the homogeneous extension and the latter is differentiable there. Thus the support points for normals in $\operatorname{supp}\psi$ retain a uniform positive margin from all the planes, in the same strict signature. Off $\operatorname{supp}\psi$ the old and new extensions agree on a neighborhood of the normal, so their subdifferentials, hence support faces, agree. Indeed for a support function a subgradient at a nonzero normal is a linear form dominated by the support function and attaining it at that normal, by homogeneity and the subgradient inequality.

Each body has just one boundary point on any ray from zero. If its radial endpoint changes, every supporting normal of either endpoint must lie in $\operatorname{supp}\psi$, since any normal outside it would retain that same endpoint for both bodies. Both endpoints are then among the indicated support points in the strict signature, with a uniform margin. By linearity of the signed cutting forms their whole connecting segment has the same margin. Thus all changed radial intervals avoid the cutting planes. The cap indicators are constant on the changed set, giving the exact cap mass changes and the stated quotient identity. Support ordering gives $K_t\supsetneq K$ for $t>0$ and $K_t\subsetneq K$ for $t<0$, since $\psi\ge0$ and is nonzero. Strict inclusion between convex bodies of positive volume is strict in volume, by taking points of the larger body's interior outside the smaller closed body.

For the volume derivative, on the smooth neighborhood the tangent derivative of $X_t$ at $n$ is $A_{h_t}(n)$ with image $n^\perp$. Each point there has a unique normal. If it had a second normal, the intervening normal arc would support the same point, contradicting local injectivity of $X_t$ from its full-rank derivative. The map $n\mapsto X_t(n)/|X_t(n)|$ is locally invertible because projection to $X_t(n)^\perp$ is invertible on $n^\perp$ when $n\cdot X_t(n)=h_t(n)>0$. The directions of all potentially changed radial intervals belong to the compact image of $\operatorname{supp}\psi$ under $n\mapsto X_0(n)/|X_0(n)|$. The implicit function theorem and a finite cover of this compact image give a fixed open neighborhood of these directions where the radial endpoint $\rho_t(\omega)\omega$ is $C^1$ in $t$ for all sufficiently small $|t|$, with uniformly bounded derivative on a smaller fixed neighborhood still containing the compact image. On overlapping inverse patches the radial endpoints agree by uniqueness. For a ray there, differentiate $X_t(n_t)=\rho_t(\omega)\omega$ and take the scalar product with $n_t$. The derivative through $n_t$ is tangential, so
$$
\frac{\partial\rho_t(\omega)}{\partial t}=\frac{\psi(n_t)}{n_t\cdot\omega}.
$$
The denominator is bounded away from zero by the positive minimum of $h_t$ and an upper bound on $|X_t|$. Outside the fixed neighborhood the radial endpoint is unchanged. Differentiating the radial volume integral $d^{-1}\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\rho_t(\omega)^d\,d\omega$ now gives a continuous derivative near zero. Radial projection from the smooth boundary to the sphere has tangential Jacobian $(n\cdot\omega)/\rho_0^{d-1}$, since it is orthogonal projection between tangent planes followed by scaling by $\rho_0^{-1}$. This gives the stated surface integral. The integrand is zero off the bump patch wherever the normal is unique. It is positive on an open smooth boundary patch of positive area, because $\psi$ is positive on an open normal patch and $A_h$ is positive definite there.

The two signs of $\Delta V(t)$ and the quotient identity imply $W=\Phi(K)$ at a local minimum with fixed section traces. When $W<\Phi(K)$ take $t>0$ for descent, and when $W>\Phi(K)$ take $t<0$. Keeping each section as a set also keeps its area and first moment, and thus preserves all original section centroids and any coarea balance with the fixed weights and normals.

On an unoriented plane the two oppositely oriented caps have total mass $V$. If their combined weights are equal in each class, the weighted mean is $1/2$. Otherwise a crossing of a single imbalanced class produces two nearby signatures whose $W$ differ by the difference of its two total orientation weights. At least one of their values differs from $\Phi(K)$. When the smooth positive support curvature is global, choose a nonzero ray in the imbalanced plane avoiding every other distinct plane, which is possible for $d\ge2$ by the finite union of proper subspaces property. Its radial endpoint lies on just that plane. The plane crosses the smooth boundary transversely there since a tangent cutting plane through $0$ would support $K$ through an interior point. Equivalently the derivative of $n\mapsto u_i\cdot X(n)$ at this normal is nonzero, since the tangent image of $D X$ is $n^\perp$ and $u_i$ cannot be parallel to $n$. Hence each adjacent signature has an open normal patch with a compactly supported nonnegative bump. This proves the local exclusion, with the same argument for an isolated crossing in a smooth positive-curvature patch of a nonsmooth body.
