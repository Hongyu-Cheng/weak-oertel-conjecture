## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(B\) be a nonempty compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional real affine space, and let

\[
 \nu_0,\ldots,\nu_d\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}
\]

be an irredundant bounded simplex normal system. Suppose that functions \(h_j:B\to\mathbb R\) define, for every \(x\in B\), the full-dimensional simplex

\[
 C_x:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:\langle\nu_j,y\rangle\le h_j(x),\ 0\le j\le d\},
\tag{1}
\]

with every displayed inequality facet-defining. Assume that

\[
 \mathcal C:=\{(x,y):x\in B,\ y\in C_x\}
\tag{2}
\]

is convex and that

\[
 F(x):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)^{1/d}
\tag{3}
\]

is affine on \(B\), in the sense that it preserves every convex combination of two points of \(B\).

Then there are a fixed full-dimensional simplex \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), an affine map \(b:B\to\mathbb R^d\), and a positive affine function \(s:B\to(0,\infty)\) such that

\[
 \boxed{C_x=b(x)+s(x)K\qquad(x\in B).}
\tag{4}
\]

Every \(h_j\) is affine on \(B\), and the centroid field

\[
 c(x):=\operatorname{cg}(C_x)=b(x)+s(x)\operatorname{cg}(K)
\tag{5}
\]

is affine on \(B\).

For the mixed consequence, let additionally \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and suppose that \(C:=\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) is a convex body. Put

\[
 C_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,
\tag{6}
\]

let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let \(h_{\max}(C)\) be its maximum mixed-integer halfspace depth. With

\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{7}
\]

one has the stronger explicit estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\ge \frac{\gamma_d}{5}\,2^{-n}W
 \ge \frac1{5e}\,2^{-n}W.}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular this class satisfies the locked statement with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/(5e)\), and therefore also with \(c_0=1/(8e)\).

Moreover, if \(G=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) is the global complete metronoid-core capacity of accepted fact 3d1f63abf09b2abb, then the following independent certificate split holds:

\[
 G\ge \frac W8
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 h_{\max}(C)\ge \frac{\gamma_d}{8}\,2^{-n}W,
\tag{9}
\]

whereas if \(G<W/8\), the canonical product matching of accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b is affinely half-metronoid realizable and gives

\[
 h_{\max}(C)>
 \frac{7\gamma_d}{32}\,2^{-n}W
 >\frac{\gamma_d}{8}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

Fix \(x^0\in B\), put \(h^0=(h_0(x^0),\ldots,h_d(x^0))\), and let \(K:=C_{x^0}\). We first determine the exact finite-dimensional support-number geometry of the fixed normal system.

The normals span \(\mathbb R^d\). Otherwise there would be a nonzero vector \(v\) orthogonal to all of them, and every nonempty set cut out by these inequalities would contain the entire line through each of its points in direction \(v\), contradicting boundedness. Hence the linear map

\[
 N:\mathbb R^{d+1}\to\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad N(\eta):=\sum_{j=0}^d\eta_j\nu_j,
\tag{11}
\]

has rank \(d\) and one-dimensional kernel.

There is a vector \(\alpha=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_d)\) spanning this kernel with every coordinate strictly positive. Let \(F_j\) be the \(j\)-th facet of \(K\), let \(A_j>0\) be its \((d-1)\)-dimensional volume, and let \(u_j=\nu_j/\|\nu_j\|\) be its unit outward normal. Applying the divergence theorem to every constant vector field gives

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d A_j u_j=0.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus \(\alpha_j:=A_j/\|\nu_j\|>0\) satisfies

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d\alpha_j\nu_j=0.
\tag{13}
\]

After a common rescaling, assume \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\). Since \(\ker N\) is one-dimensional, this strictly positive dependence is unique up to scale.

Define the translation-support map

\[
 T:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^{d+1},
 \qquad
 T(v):=(\langle\nu_0,v\rangle,\ldots,\langle\nu_d,v\rangle).
\tag{14}
\]

It is injective because the normals span \(\mathbb R^d\). Equation (13) gives \(\alpha\cdot T(v)=0\), so the \(d\)-dimensional image of \(T\) is contained in the \(d\)-dimensional hyperplane

\[
 H_\alpha:=\{q\in\mathbb R^{d+1}:\alpha\cdot q=0\}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{im}T=H_\alpha.}
\tag{15}
\]

This is the exact one-dimensional support-vector quotient modulo translations.

For \(x\in B\), write \(h(x)=(h_0(x),\ldots,h_d(x))\) and set

\[
 \lambda(x):=\alpha\cdot h(x).
\tag{16}
\]

This number is strictly positive. Indeed, choose an interior point \(y_x\in C_x\). Every slack

\[
 r_j(x):=h_j(x)-\langle\nu_j,y_x\rangle
\]

is positive, and (13) gives

\[
 \lambda(x)
 =\sum_j\alpha_jr_j(x)
 +\left\langle\sum_j\alpha_j\nu_j,y_x\right\rangle
 =\sum_j\alpha_jr_j(x)>0.
\tag{17}
\]

Put \(\lambda_0:=\lambda(x^0)>0\) and

\[
 s(x):=\frac{\lambda(x)}{\lambda_0}>0.
\tag{18}
\]

The support vector \(h(x)-s(x)h^0\) lies in \(H_\alpha\), so (15) supplies a unique \(b(x)\in\mathbb R^d\) such that

\[
 h_j(x)=\langle\nu_j,b(x)\rangle+s(x)h_j^0
 \qquad(0\le j\le d).
\tag{19}
\]

Since \(s(x)>0\), the defining inequalities show directly that

\[
 y\in C_x
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{y-b(x)}{s(x)}\in K.
\]

Thus the pointwise homothety (4) already follows, without using an infinitesimal deformation or a Taylor expansion. It also gives

\[
 F(x)=s(x)F(x^0)
 =\frac{F(x^0)}{\lambda_0}\lambda(x).
\tag{20}
\]

It remains to prove that the parameters are affine in the base.

Convexity of \(\mathcal C\) implies, for \(x_0,x_1\in B\), \(0\le\theta\le1\), and \(x_\theta=\theta x_0+(1-\theta)x_1\), that

\[
 \theta C_{x_0}+(1-\theta)C_{x_1}\subseteq C_{x_\theta}.
\tag{21}
\]

Because every displayed inequality in (1) is active, \(h_j(x)\) is the actual support value of \(C_x\) in direction \(\nu_j\). Taking support values in (21) gives

\[
 h_j(x_\theta)\ge
 \theta h_j(x_0)+(1-\theta)h_j(x_1),
\tag{22}
\]

so every \(h_j\) is concave. Define

\[
 g_j:=h_j(x_\theta)
 -\theta h_j(x_0)-(1-\theta)h_j(x_1)\ge0.
\tag{23}
\]

By (20), the hypothesis that \(F\) is affine says exactly that \(\lambda\) is affine. Taking the corresponding gap in (16) therefore yields

\[
 0=\sum_{j=0}^d\alpha_jg_j.
\tag{24}
\]

Every \(\alpha_j\) is strictly positive, so every \(g_j=0\). Since \(x_0,x_1,\theta\) were arbitrary, each \(h_j\) is affine on \(B\). Equations (18) and (20) make \(s\) affine. Finally, (19) and the linear inverse of \(T:\mathbb R^d\to H_\alpha\) show that \(b\) is affine. Centroids commute with translations and positive dilations, so (5) follows and is affine. This proves finite-amplitude rigidity independently from the first-order fixed-normal theory.

We now prove the mixed-depth assertions. Keep (6)-(7), and write

\[
 c_z:=c(z)=\operatorname{cg}(C_z),
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z).
\tag{25}
\]

The constant density \(1/2\) on \(C_z\) has mass \(a_z/2\) and barycenter \(c_z\), hence

\[
 c_z\in\mathcal K_z\qquad(z\in Z).
\tag{26}
\]

Because \(C\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), its base \(B\) is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^n\). Thus the affine map (5) extends uniquely from \(B\) to an affine map on \(\mathbb R^n\), whose value at every \(z\in Z\) is \(c_z\). Therefore assertion 4 of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 applies: every free off-diagonal same-parity matching is affinely half-metronoid realizable. Its assertion 1 then gives

\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C).
\tag{27}
\]

We first give the requested complete-core/product-matching split. Let \(G=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) be as in accepted fact 3d1f63abf09b2abb. We record explicitly the complete-core master estimate used here:

\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\gamma_d2^{-n}G.
\tag{28}
\]

For completeness, (28) follows from the complete-core definition and the metronoid fiber-depth input recorded in 3d1f63abf09b2abb. Take any admissible tuple \((E,(t_z),m)\) in the definition of \(G\), put \(Q_z=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\), let \(\tau=\sum_{z\in E}t_z\), and choose

\[
 y\in\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zQ_z.
\]

For any \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), this membership represents \((m,y)\) as a convex combination of points \((z,q_z)\in C\), so \(p=(m,y)\in S\). Let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\), and set

\[
 R:=\{z\in E:Q_z\cap H_z\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad F:=E\setminus R.
\]

One has \(m\notin\operatorname{conv}F\). Otherwise a representation \(m=\sum_{z\in F}\lambda_zz\), together with complete-core membership for that \(\lambda\), would write \(p\) as a convex combination of points of \(C\setminus H\). Every point used is strictly outside the closed halfspace \(H\), so their finite convex combination is strictly outside \(H\), a contradiction. If \(F\ne\varnothing\), strict separation supplies a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) disjoint from \(F\). If \(F=\varnothing\), choose any proper closed base halfspace through \(m\). In either case \(E\cap J\subseteq R\), and the balance condition gives

\[
 \sum_{z\in R}t_z
 \ge\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z
 \ge2^{-n}\tau.
\tag{29}
\]

For each \(z\in R\), choose \(q_z\in Q_z\cap H_z\). The metronoid depth property used in 3d1f63abf09b2abb gives

\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\ge\gamma_dt_z;
\]

when \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from \(a_z\ge t_z\ge\gamma_dt_z\). Summing and using (29) proves that \(p\) has mixed depth at least \(\gamma_d2^{-n}\tau\). Taking the supremum over admissible tuples proves (28), including a nonattained supremum.

If \(G\ge W/8\), (28) proves (9). Suppose instead that \(G<W/8\). In particular \(G<W/2\), so accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b supplies its canonical product matching \(p^\times\), which is a legal free matching and satisfies

\[
 4|p^\times|\ge W-G>\frac{7W}{8}.
\tag{30}
\]

By (26) and the affinity of the centroid field, this matching is affinely half-metronoid realizable. Applying the affine matching certificate in 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 gives

\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \ge\gamma_d2^{-n}|p^\times|
 >\frac{7\gamma_d}{32}\,2^{-n}W,
\tag{31}
\]

which proves (10). Thus the large-core versus small-core/free-product split alone gives \(c_0=1/(8e)\).

We finish with the sharper heavy-fiber/free-matching optimization. Let

\[
 A_*:=\max_{z\in Z}a_z,
 \qquad x:=\frac{2^nA_*}{W}.
\tag{32}
\]

At a maximizing fiber \(z_*\), its centroid is the unique full-mass metronoid point. The metronoid depth input from 3d1f63abf09b2abb, now with \(t=a_{z_*}\), shows that every fiber halfspace containing \(c_{z_*}\) has volume at least \(\gamma_dA_*\). An ambient halfspace containing \((z_*,c_{z_*})\) has either such a proper fiber section or the whole fiber. Hence

\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\gamma_dA_*
 =\gamma_d2^{-n}xW.
\tag{33}
\]

On the other hand, assertions 1, 2, and 4 of 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5, together with (26), give

\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \ge\gamma_d2^{-n}\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C)
 \ge\gamma_d2^{-n}\frac{W-2^nA_*}{4}
 =\gamma_d2^{-n}\frac{1-x}{4}W.
\tag{34}
\]

If \(x\ge1/5\), (33) is at least \(\gamma_d2^{-n}W/5\). If \(x<1/5\), (34) is strictly larger than \(\gamma_d2^{-n}W/5\). Thus

\[
 h_{\max}(C)\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{5}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{35}
\]

Finally,

\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}\ge e^{-1},
\]

because \(d\log(1+1/d)\le1\). Substitution into (35) proves (8). Since \(1/(5e)\in(0,1/e)\), this is an admissible absolute constant for the locked statement on the stated class.
