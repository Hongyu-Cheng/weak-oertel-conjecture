## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(b\ge2\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers, let \(\beta\ge0\) be real, and let the integer costs satisfy \(b\le B_i\le2b\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) for every \(i\in[k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\). Let \(\Phi:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be nondecreasing with \(\Phi(0)=0\). Give every spatial vector multiplicity one and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
r(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k\max(x_i-1,0),&
\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i)+\Phi(r(\mathbf x)),\\
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^{T}e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}&
Q(\mathbf x)&=|\lbrace i:x_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,\\
Z_{4,\Phi}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},&
Z_{3,\Phi}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)},\\
Z_{\rm bin}&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Every argument \(E\) of \(f_E\) is a nonnegative integer. All integer heights from \(E\) through \(T\), including both endpoints, are retained. Define \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H}\) and \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H}\) by imposing the indicated occupation restriction in the sum for \(Z_{4,\Phi}\).

The notation maps to the accepted predecessors as follows. At \(\Phi=0\), the objects \(\Gamma_\Phi,Z_{4,\Phi},Z_{3,\Phi}\) become \(\Gamma,Z_4,Z_3\) of `f91d0a2ca70a4beb`; its full and selected normalizers are respectively these \(Z_4\) and \(Z_3\). The definitions of \(g_i,f_E,Q,k,b,T,\beta,B_i,C_{4,i}\) are unchanged. The binary normalizer in (1) is exactly \(Z_{\rm bin}\) of `deb724bb2b6dd465`, since \(r=0\) on every binary vector. The cap parameter \(L_{\rm cap}\) below is the parameter called \(L\) in that accepted fact. The locked objects \(n,d,C,S,c_0\) retain their locked meanings.

For every integer \(H\) with \(1\le H\le k/128\), set
$$
h_H=\min\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac{k-H}{16H}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace,
\qquad
D_H=\frac{100H^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k}}+2H\left(\frac23\right)^{h_H}.
\tag{2}
$$
Then \(h_H\ge1\) and
$$
Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H}\le e^{D_H}Z_{3,\Phi}.
\tag{3}
$$
For every fixed \(a>0,A>0,p\ge0\), uniformly over these models with \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\) and integers \(1\le H\le A(1+\log k)^p\), the bound is \(e^{o(1)}Z_{3,\Phi}\) as \(k\to\infty\). In particular there is \(k_0(a,A,p)\) such that \(H\le k/128\) and \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H}\le eZ_{3,\Phi}\) for all \(k\ge k_0(a,A,p)\). No cap restriction is needed for this conclusion.

Suppose additionally that \(a>0,L_{\rm cap}\ge0\) are real and \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\), \(T\le k/2+L_{\rm cap}b\). For every integer
\(H\ge\max\lbrace1,4L_{\rm cap},(4/a^2)\log(2k)\rbrace\), one has
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le\frac{2(k+1)\exp(-a^2H^2/4)}{1-\exp(-a^2H/4)}.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular, set
$$
K_a=4(1+a^{-2}),\qquad
H_0=\left\lceil K_a\bigl[L_{\rm cap}+\log(k+1)+1\bigr]\right\rceil.
\tag{5}
$$
Then \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H_0}\le Z_{\rm bin}/2\). For every fixed \(D\ge0\), the condition \(L_{\rm cap}\le D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\) implies
$$
H_0\le K_{a,D}[1+\log^2(k+1)],
\qquad K_{a,D}=K_a(D+2)+1.
\tag{6}
$$
Consequently, for fixed \(a>0,D\ge0\), all the models above with \(k\) sufficiently large depending only on \(a,D\), \(b\ge a\sqrt{k}\), \(T\le k/2+L_{\rm cap}b\), and \(0\le L_{\rm cap}\le D[1+\log^2(k+1)]\) satisfy
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi}}{Z_{3,\Phi}}\le e+\frac12.
\tag{7}
$$
All statements hold for every \(\beta\ge0\), including \(\beta=0\), every permitted endpoint cost, and \(T=0\). Every normalizer is an original complete-height sum. If \(\Phi\) is also discrete convex, its convex nondecreasing interpolation gives a convex epigraph for this spatial energy. The example \(\Phi(j)=\lambda j^2\), with \(\lambda\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), belongs to this class and is nonseparable when \(\lambda>0\) and \(k\ge2\).

## Proof

The binary zero vector at height zero contributes one, so \(Z_{\rm bin}>0\) and \(Z_{3,\Phi}\ge Z_{\rm bin}>0\). For any nonnegative integers \(E'\le E\), the tails in (1) satisfy \(f_E\le f_{E'}\), including the case \(E>T\). Hence, for any nonnegative edge kernel \(K(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) supported on pairs with \(\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf y)\le\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)\),
$$
\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y}K(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)}
\le\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y}K(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)f_{\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf y)}.
\tag{8}
$$
In particular, row sums one and column sums at most \(L_*\) bound total source mass by \(L_*\) times allowed target mass. Here \(L_*\) is only a kernel bound, unrelated to \(L_{\rm cap}\).

We first extend the single-third estimate underlying `f91d0a2ca70a4beb`. Work on any \(\nu\) coordinates satisfying the same cost window, with any nonnegative integer cap and the same original height tails. Allow any nondecreasing integer-valued penalty \(\Psi\ge0\) with \(\Psi(0)=0\) on the local excess \(r\). For \(1\le u\le\nu/16\), let \(V_{1,\Psi}(u)\) be its mass with exactly one three and occupation at most \(u\), and let \(V_{0,\Psi}(u+1)\) be its all-ternary mass with occupation at most \(u+1\). Put \(h(\nu,u)=\min\lbrace b-1,\lfloor\nu/(8u)\rfloor\rbrace\). We prove
$$
V_{1,\Psi}(u)\le
\left[\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}+u\left(\frac23\right)^{h(\nu,u)}\right]
V_{0,\Psi}(u+1).
\tag{9}
$$
All counts in this local argument refer only to its \(\nu\) coordinates.

Order the coordinates by nondecreasing \(B_i\), breaking ties in a fixed manner. Let \(m=\lceil\sqrt{\nu u}\rceil\), let \(I_{\rm low}\) be the first \(m\) labels, and let \(I_{\rm high}\) contain the rest. Since \(\nu\ge16u\), \(\sqrt{\nu u}\ge4\), and \(u\ge1\), one has
$$
\sqrt{\nu u}\le m\le\frac54\sqrt{\nu u},\qquad
m\ge4u,\qquad m-u\ge\frac34m,\qquad m<\nu.
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed, the ceiling adds at most one, and \(m\le\nu/4+1\le5\nu/16<\nu\).

For a source, let \(i\) be its unique three-position, let \(v\) be its one-count, and let \(q\le u\) be its occupation. First consider \(v\ge\nu/4\) and \(i\in I_{\rm low}\). For every source one-position \(j\), change \(3\) at \(i\) to \(0\), and \(1\) at \(j\) to \(2\). The additive energy change is at most
\(B_j-1-C_{4,i}\le2b-1-(2B_i-1)\le0\). The excess \(r\) changes by \(-2+1=-1\), so the penalty also weakly decreases. There are \(v\ge\nu/4\) distinct targets, all ternary and all with occupation \(q\). Each target has at most \(mQ(\mathbf y)\le mu\) inverse sources, obtained by choosing a zero-position in \(I_{\rm low}\) to restore to three and a two-position to restore to one. Applying (8) with unit edge weights bounds this source mass by \((4mu/\nu)V_{0,\Psi}(u+1)\).

Next consider \(v\ge\nu/4\) and \(i\in I_{\rm high}\). For every source one-position \(j\in I_{\rm low}\), change \(3\) at \(i\) to \(2\), and \(1\) at \(j\) to \(2\). The additive change is
\((B_j-1)-(C_{4,i}-B_i)\le B_j-B_i\le0\). The excess changes by \(-1+1=0\), so the penalty stays fixed. The target is ternary with occupation \(q+1\le u+1\).

To bound the source-weighted outdegree, fix \(i\), the source two-position set \(I_2\), and the integer \(v\ge\nu/4\). Here \(q=|I_2|+1\le u\). The one-positions range over all \(v\)-subsets of the \(\nu-q\) remaining labels. The excess is always \(|I_2|+2\); thus every such placement has the identical coupled energy
\(C_{4,i}+\sum_{j\in I_2}B_j+v+\Psi(|I_2|+2)\), and the identical original tail weight, including feasibility. Their average number of ones in \(I_{\rm low}\) is
$$
\frac{v\bigl(m-|I_2\cap I_{\rm low}|\bigr)}{\nu-q}
\ge\frac{m-q+1}{4}\ge\frac{m-u}{4}.
\tag{11}
$$
Each available position occurs in the fraction \(v/(\nu-q)\ge1/4\) of all these subsets. Since occupation and one-count restrictions hold throughout every class, (11) is also the required lower bound after averaging with the actual source weights. A target has at most \(Q(\mathbf y)^2\le(u+1)^2\le4u^2\) inverse sources: choose its two-position in \(I_{\rm high}\) to restore to three and its two-position in \(I_{\rm low}\) to restore to one. Therefore (8) bounds this second source mass by \(16u^2/(m-u)\) times \(V_{0,\Psi}(u+1)\). The sum of the two high-one coefficients is at most
$$
\frac{4mu}{\nu}+\frac{16u^2}{m-u}
\le\left(5+\frac{64}{3}\right)\frac{u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}
\le\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu},
\tag{12}
$$
using (10).

Finally consider all sources with \(v<\nu/4\), irrespective of \(i\). Put \(h=h(\nu,u)\). Then \(1\le h\le b-1\) and \(h\le\nu/(8u)\le\nu/8\). Their zero-count \(z=\nu-v-q\) obeys
$$
z>\frac{11\nu}{16},\qquad z-h>\frac{9\nu}{16},\qquad v+h<\frac{3\nu}{8}.
\tag{13}
$$
Change the unique three to two and any \(h\) source zeros to ones. The additive energy change is at most
\(h-(C_{4,i}-B_i)\le(b-1)-(B_i-1)\le0\). The excess drops by one; zero-to-one changes do not affect it. Thus the coupled energy weakly decreases. These \(\binom zh\) distinct ternary targets preserve occupation. Give each edge kernel entry \(1/\binom zh\), so every row sum is one.

For a fixed target admitting an inverse, its one-count \(v+h\) and zero-count \(z-h\) determine \(v,z\), because \(h\) is common. Each inverse chooses a target two-position to restore to three and \(h\) target one-positions to restore to zero. Its column sum is consequently at most
$$
Q(\mathbf y)\frac{\binom{v+h}{h}}{\binom zh}
\le Q(\mathbf y)\left(\frac{v+h}{z-h+1}\right)^h
\le u\left(\frac23\right)^h.
\tag{14}
$$
The product formula for the binomial ratio gives the first bound, and (13) gives the last. Equation (8) bounds the low-one source mass by \(u(2/3)^hV_{0,\Psi}(u+1)\). These three source cases are disjoint and exhaustive. Combining them with (12) proves (9). This derivation proves, rather than assumes, that every exchange and the original one-placement averaging in the accepted uncoupled argument survive the penalty.

We now condition and iterate, retaining the increase in occupation cutoff. For \(0\le\ell\le k\) and integer \(J\ge0\), let \(Z_{\Phi,\le J}^{(\ell)}\) be the original mass in (1) with exactly \(\ell\) threes and occupation at most \(J\). At \(\Phi=0\) this is \(Z_{\le J}^{(\ell)}\) of `f91d0a2ca70a4beb`. Fix \(1\le\ell\le H\) and an integer \(J\) with \(J\ge\ell\) and \(u:=J-\ell+1\le2H\). Fix a set \(F\subseteq[k]\) of size \(\ell-1\) at three. In this coupled model the conditioning quantities are
$$
r_F=2|F|,\qquad
E_F=\sum_{i\in F}C_{4,i}+\Phi(r_F),\qquad
T_F=T-E_F,\qquad
\Phi_F(s)=\Phi(r_F+s)-\Phi(r_F).
\tag{15}
$$
Thus \(E_F\) is the accepted uncoupled conditioning energy with the additional constant \(\Phi(r_F)\). The residual penalty \(\Phi_F\) is integer-valued, nonnegative, nondecreasing, and zero at zero. If \(\mathbf x'\) denotes the vector on the \(\nu=k-\ell+1\) remaining coordinates, the exact energy decomposition is
$$
\Gamma_\Phi(\mathbf x)=E_F+
\sum_{i\notin F}g_i(x_i)+\Phi_F(r(\mathbf x')).
\tag{16}
$$
In particular all residual energies are nonnegative integers.

Let \(U_{1,\Phi}(F,J)\) be the mass with these fixed threes, exactly one additional three, and total occupation at most \(J\). Let \(U_{0,\Phi}(F,J+1)\) be the mass with exactly these threes, all other coordinates ternary, and total occupation at most \(J+1\). If \(T_F<0\), both vanish. If \(T_F\ge0\), for every residual energy \(E\), the translation \(t'=t-E_F\) gives the exact identity
$$
f_{E_F+E}=e^{-\beta E_F}\sum_{t'=E}^{T_F}e^{-\beta t'},
\tag{17}
$$
where an empty sum is zero. Every height endpoint is translated, without dropping a height. The residual model therefore has the same cost window, penalty \(\Phi_F\), cap \(T_F\), source occupation cutoff \(u\), target cutoff \(u+1\), and common multiplicative factor \(e^{-\beta E_F}\).

Because \(H\le k/128\), \(\nu\ge k-H\), and \(u\le2H\), one has \(u/\nu\le2/127<1/16\), so (9) applies. Moreover
$$
\begin{aligned}
h(\nu,u)&\ge\min\left\lbrace b-1,\left\lfloor\frac{k-H}{16H}\right\rfloor\right\rbrace=h_H,\\
\frac{32u^{3/2}}{\sqrt\nu}+u\left(\frac23\right)^{h(\nu,u)}
&\le\frac{32(2H)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{k-H}}+2H\left(\frac23\right)^{h_H}\le D_H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Here \(k-H\ge127k/128\) and \(32\cdot2^{3/2}\sqrt{128/127}<100\). Also \((k-H)/(16H)\ge127/16\), so \(h_H\ge1\). Equations (9) and (17), including the zero-mass case, yield \(U_{1,\Phi}(F,J)\le D_H U_{0,\Phi}(F,J+1)\).

Summing over all sets \(F\) of size \(\ell-1\) gives the exact multiplicities
$$
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_{1,\Phi}(F,J)=\ell Z_{\Phi,\le J}^{(\ell)},\qquad
\sum_{|F|=\ell-1}U_{0,\Phi}(F,J+1)=Z_{\Phi,\le J+1}^{(\ell-1)}.
\tag{19}
$$
A source with \(\ell\) threes occurs for the \(\ell\) subsets obtained by omitting one of its three-positions. A target with \(\ell-1\) threes occurs only for its entire set of three-positions. Although the conditional factors in (17) may vary with \(F\), each occurrence is its identical original coupled height tail, so these are identities for the original masses. We have proved
\(\ell Z_{\Phi,\le J}^{(\ell)}\le D_H Z_{\Phi,\le J+1}^{(\ell-1)}\) for the stated \(\ell,J\).

For fixed \(1\le\ell\le H\), iterate this inequality at current third-count \(s=\ell,\ell-1,\ldots,1\) and cutoff \(J_s=H+\ell-s\). One has \(J_s\ge s\) and \(1\le J_s-s+1=H+\ell-2s+1\le2H-1\), so every application is justified. Thus
$$
Z_{\Phi,\le H}^{(\ell)}
\le\frac{D_H^\ell}{\ell!}Z_{\Phi,\le H+\ell}^{(0)}
\le\frac{D_H^\ell}{\ell!}Z_{3,\Phi}.
\tag{20}
$$
The final all-ternary cutoff may reach \(H+\ell\le2H\). A vector of occupation at most \(H\) has at most \(H\) threes. Summing (20), with the \(\ell=0\) term bounded by \(Z_{3,\Phi}\), proves (3).

For the uniform low-occupation consequence, let \(a,A,p\) be fixed as stated. The upper bound \(H\le A(1+\log k)^p\) implies \(H/k\to0\), \(H^{3/2}/\sqrt{k}\to0\), and \(\sqrt{k}/H\to\infty\), uniformly over the permitted positive integers \(H\). For all sufficiently large \(k\) depending only on \(a,A,p\), it follows that \(H\le k/128\), \(b-1\ge(a/2)\sqrt{k}\), and \(\lfloor(k-H)/(16H)\rfloor\ge(a/2)\sqrt{k}\). Consequently
$$
D_H\le\frac{100A^{3/2}(1+\log k)^{3p/2}}{\sqrt{k}}
+2A(1+\log k)^p\left(\frac23\right)^{(a/2)\sqrt{k}}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
$$
This proves the uniform \(e^{o(1)}\) assertion and, by increasing \(k_0(a,A,p)\) so that \(D_H\le1\), the bound by \(eZ_{3,\Phi}\).

For the high-occupation comparison, we use the exact binary-height comparisons proved in `deb724bb2b6dd465`. Define its auxiliary normalizer, with the identical meaning and normalization,
$$
Z_{\rm bin}(m,U;\beta)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol\epsilon\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}
\sum_{t=\sum_i\epsilon_i}^{U}e^{-\beta t},&U\ge0,\\
0,&U<0,
\end{cases}
\tag{22}
$$
for every integer \(m\ge0\) and integer \(U\). An inner sum above its upper endpoint is zero; for \(m=0\) there is one empty binary vector. Thus \(Z_{\rm bin}=Z_{\rm bin}(k,T;\beta)\) exactly, because \(\Phi(0)=0\). The accepted binary height-tilt inequality states, for integers \(m,\delta,T\ge0\) and \(\beta\ge0\),
$$
\frac{e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\beta)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\beta)}
\le\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}.
\tag{23}
$$
Both denominators are positive. The two accepted uniform-count bounds for \(m\ge1,\delta\ge1\) are
$$
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}
\le
\begin{cases}
\exp(-2\delta^2/(m+1)),&0\le T\le m/2,\\
2(m+1)\exp(-\delta^2/(2m)),&T>m/2,\ T-\delta\le m/2-\delta/2.
\end{cases}
\tag{24}
$$
When \(T-\delta<0\), the numerator is zero. These are precisely the binary statements of that accepted fact, with no coupled energy or conditional law inserted into their denominators.

Fix \(1\le q\le k\), a high-coordinate set \(I\) of size \(q\), and labels \(\eta_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\) for \(i\in I\). Put \(m=k-q\) and \(\delta=bq\). The excess is constant on this sector: the coordinates outside \(I\) are binary and contribute zero to \(r\). Hence its high-coordinate energy is the fixed integer
\(E_{I,\boldsymbol\eta}=\sum_{i\in I}g_i(\eta_i)+\Phi(\sum_{i\in I}(\eta_i-1))\ge bq=\delta\).
Here each high-site cost is at least \(b\), since \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge b\), and \(\Phi\ge0\). The energy for a remaining binary vector is exactly \(E_{I,\boldsymbol\eta}+\sum_{i\notin I}x_i\). Its complete sector mass, by original height translation, is therefore
$$
e^{-\beta E_{I,\boldsymbol\eta}}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-E_{I,\boldsymbol\eta};\beta)
\le e^{-\beta\delta}Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;\beta).
\tag{25}
$$
The inequality follows term by term from \(f_{E_{I,\boldsymbol\eta}+j}\le f_{\delta+j}\) at each binary one-count \(j\). Setting the coordinates in \(I\) to zero embeds the complete \(m\)-coordinate binary-height support into the \(k\)-coordinate binary support. Thus \(Z_{\rm bin}(k,T;\beta)\ge Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;\beta)\). There are \(\binom kq2^q\) choices of \(I,\boldsymbol\eta\). Using (23) in (25) yields
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q=q}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le\binom kq2^q
\frac{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)}{Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)}.
\tag{26}
$$

If \(q\ge4L_{\rm cap}\), then \(L_{\rm cap}b\le\delta/4\), and \(q/2\le\delta/4\) since \(b\ge2\). The assumed cap gives
$$
T-\delta\le m/2+q/2+L_{\rm cap}b-\delta\le m/2-\delta/2.
\tag{27}
$$
For \(m=0\), the last quantity is negative, so this sector has zero mass. For \(m\ge1\), either case of (24), with \(m+1\le k\), implies
\(Z_{\rm bin}(m,T-\delta;0)/Z_{\rm bin}(m,T;0)\le2(k+1)\exp(-b^2q^2/(2k))\). In the first case the exponent \(-2\delta^2/(m+1)\) is at most \(-\delta^2/(2k)\); in the second case \(m\le k\) gives the same weaker exponent. The zero-mass case also obeys this bound. Combining it with (26), \(b^2/k\ge a^2\), and \(\binom kq2^q\le(2k)^q\) gives
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q=q}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le2(k+1)\exp\left(q\log(2k)-\frac{a^2q^2}{2}\right)
\quad\text{for }q\ge\max\lbrace1,4L_{\rm cap}\rbrace.
\tag{28}
$$
For every \(q\ge H\) under the hypothesis of (4), \(q\log(2k)\le a^2q^2/4\). Summing the disjoint sectors and using \((H+j)^2\ge H^2+Hj\) yields
$$
\frac{Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H}}{Z_{\rm bin}}
\le2(k+1)\sum_{q=H}^k e^{-a^2q^2/4}
\le\frac{2(k+1)e^{-a^2H^2/4}}{1-e^{-a^2H/4}},
\tag{29}
$$
which proves (4). If \(H>k\), the tail is empty and the conclusion also holds.

For the threshold in (5), write \(\xi=\log(k+1)\). Since \(\log(2k)\le\xi+1\), \(K_a\ge4\), and \(K_a\ge4/a^2\), the integer \(H_0\) satisfies every hypothesis of (4). In particular \(e^{-a^2H_0/4}\le(2k)^{-1}\le1/2\). Moreover, \(aK_a=4(a+a^{-1})\ge8\), so
$$
\frac{a^2H_0^2}{4}\ge16(\xi+1)^2\ge\xi+\log8.
\tag{30}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(16(\xi+1)^2-(\xi+3)=16\xi^2+31\xi+13\ge0\) and \(\log8<3\). Substitution in (29) proves \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H_0}/Z_{\rm bin}\le4(k+1)e^{-a^2H_0^2/4}\le1/2\). Also \(\xi+1\le2(1+\xi^2)\), so the ceiling and the hypothesis on \(L_{\rm cap}\) give
\(H_0\le K_a[D(1+\xi^2)+\xi+1]+1\le[K_a(D+2)+1](1+\xi^2)\), proving (6).

For fixed \(a,D\), (6) implies \(H_0\le2K_{a,D}(1+\log k)^2\), since \(\log(k+1)\le1+\log k\) for \(k\ge1\). Apply the uniform low-occupation conclusion with \(A=2K_{a,D}\) and \(p=2\). For all sufficiently large \(k\) depending only on \(a,D\), it gives \(Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H_0}\le eZ_{3,\Phi}\). The complementary mass has \(Q\ge H_0+1\), and is at most the tail with \(Q\ge H_0\). Thus
$$
Z_{4,\Phi}
=Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\le H_0}+Z_{4,\Phi}^{Q\ge H_0+1}
\le eZ_{3,\Phi}+\frac12Z_{\rm bin}
\le\left(e+\frac12\right)Z_{3,\Phi},
\tag{31}
$$
as required. This compares original masses directly and uses no selected-moment premise or monotonicity assertion for the full-to-selected ratio as \(\Phi\) varies.

For the convexity assertion, interpolate each \(g_i\) linearly on the intervals between sites zero, one, two, and three. Its slopes are \(1,B_i-1,C_{4,i}-B_i\), which are nondecreasing by the cost assumptions, so the interpolation is convex. If \(\Phi\) has nondecreasing successive differences, its linear interpolation on nonnegative integers is convex and nondecreasing. The real-variable excess \(\sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\) is convex. Convexity and monotonicity of the interpolated penalty therefore make its composition with this excess convex; adding the convex interpolated coordinate costs gives a convex energy on \([0,3]^k\). Its epigraph is convex and agrees with every energy in (1) at integer spatial vectors. For \(\Phi(j)=\lambda j^2\), the convex nondecreasing extension \(s\mapsto\lambda s^2\) can also be used. When \(\lambda>0\) and \(k\ge2\), take two coordinates independently at sites one and two, with all other coordinates at one. The mixed difference of the four energies is \(4\lambda-\lambda-\lambda+0=2\lambda>0\), whereas a separable sum of coordinate functions has mixed difference zero. Thus this example is genuinely coupled.
