## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d
\]
be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0.
\]
Assume the strict singleton inequalities
\[
w_z<\frac W4\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W4
\tag{2}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(z_0\).

For every \(z\) with \(w_z>0\), define
\[
M(z):=
\max_{x\in C_z}
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
a_d:=\frac{W}{64\gamma_d},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
D_{\geq a_d}:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z\geq a_d\},
\qquad
D_{<a_d}:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:0<w_z<a_d\}.
\tag{5}
\]

For \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), let \(v_0,\ldots,v_q\) be affinely independent
positive-mass lattice points, put
\[
T:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\},
\]
and suppose \(z_0\in T\).  If \(q=0\), define
\[
\kappa(T,z_0):=1.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(q>0\), define
\[
\kappa(T,z_0):=
\min_{\substack{
Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(T)\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
z_0\in Q}}
\#(T\cap Q\cap\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{7}
\]

Then the following dichotomy holds.

1. If
\[
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}D_{\geq a_d},
\tag{8}
\]
there exist \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\) and a simplex \(T\) as above such that
\[
\min_{0\leq i\leq q}M(v_i)\,\kappa(T,z_0)
\geq\frac W{64}.
\tag{9}
\]
If a convex representation in (8) places \(z_0\) on a proper face, the
conclusion uses that face and its lower rank; thus all rank and face cases
are included.

2. If (8) fails, then
\[
\sum_{z\in D_{<a_d}}w_z\geq\frac W4.
\tag{10}
\]
For \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}w_z,
\qquad
Q^\circ:=\sum_rW_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{Q^\circ}{W^2}
>
\frac{16-\gamma_d^{-1}}{256}
>
\frac{16-e}{256}.
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently, in every instance of the registered subgoal
`n2-low-energy-uniform-simplex`, either (9) already proves its requested
uniform-simplex conclusion or (10) holds; in the latter branch its assumed
strict inequality \(Q^\circ>(16-e)W^2/256\) is automatic.  No conclusion
about the existence of a simplex is asserted from (10) alone.

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many
integer fibers have positive volume.  Hence every sum and convex hull below
is finite.

We first record the exact fiberwise depth bound.  Accepted fact
`4e868f774de77608` proves that the maximum in (3) is attained.  Fix \(z\)
with \(w_z>0\).
Then \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.  Let \(c_z\) be its
centroid.  Grünbaum's centroid halfspace theorem states that every closed
halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c_z\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
=\gamma_dw_z.
\]
The depth at \(c_z\) is therefore at least \(\gamma_dw_z\), and maximizing
over \(x\in C_z\) in (3) gives
\[
M(z)\geq\gamma_dw_z.
\tag{13}
\]
Only the lower bound obtained from the specific point \(c_z\) is needed
below.

Assume (8).  Choose a convex representation
\[
z_0=\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_iv_i,\qquad
\lambda_i>0,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i=1,
\qquad
v_i\in D_{\geq a_d},
\tag{14}
\]
whose positive support is inclusion-minimal.  That support is affinely
independent.  Indeed, if it were affinely dependent, there would be real
numbers \(\alpha_i\), not all zero, satisfying
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i=0,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_iv_i=0.
\]
After replacing \((\alpha_i)\) by its negative if necessary, some
\(\alpha_i>0\).  With
\[
t:=\min_{\{\alpha_i>0\}}\frac{\lambda_i}{\alpha_i},
\]
the coefficients \(\lambda_i-t\alpha_i\) would remain nonnegative, would
still sum to one and represent \(z_0\), and at least one would vanish,
contradicting minimality.  Since an affinely independent subset of
\(\mathbb R^2\) has at most three members, \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\).

Put \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\}\).  If an initial
representation of \(z_0\) used a simplex on whose proper face \(z_0\)
lay, the preceding minimal-support operation deletes the vertices outside
that face.  Hence the resulting \(q\) is its correct affine rank, including
the rank-zero and rank-one face cases.

When \(q=0\), definition (6) gives \(\kappa(T,z_0)=1\).  When \(q>0\),
every proper closed relative halfspace occurring in (7) contains the
lattice point \(z_0\in T\cap\mathbb Z^2\).  Consequently it contains at
least one lattice point of \(T\), and again
\[
\kappa(T,z_0)\geq1.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (4), (13), (14), and (15) now yield
\[
\min_iM(v_i)\,\kappa(T,z_0)
\geq
\gamma_d\min_iw_{v_i}
\geq
\gamma_da_d
=\frac W{64}.
\]
This proves (9) in all three ranks.

Suppose next that (8) fails.  If \(D_{\geq a_d}\) is empty, every
positive-mass fiber belongs to \(D_{<a_d}\), so the left side of (10) is
\(W\) and (10) is immediate.  Otherwise,
\(\operatorname{conv}D_{\geq a_d}\) is a nonempty compact convex set not
containing \(z_0\).  Strict separation supplies a nonzero
\(u\in\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
u^\top(z-z_0)<0
\qquad(z\in D_{\geq a_d}).
\tag{16}
\]
The proper closed halfspace
\[
Q:=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\tag{17}
\]
contains \(z_0\) and no point of \(D_{\geq a_d}\).  Every
positive-mass integer fiber indexed by \(Q\) therefore belongs to
\(D_{<a_d}\).  Applying (2) to the closed halfspace (17) gives
\[
\sum_{z\in D_{<a_d}}w_z
\geq
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
\geq\frac W4,
\]
which proves (10), including the closed-boundary case.

It remains to prove both strict inequalities in (12).  Normalize
\[
\pi_z:=\frac{w_z}{W},\qquad
\alpha_d:=\frac{a_d}{W}=\frac1{64\gamma_d},\qquad
\lambda:=\sum_{\{z:0<\pi_z<\alpha_d\}}\pi_z.
\tag{18}
\]
Equation (10) gives
\[
\lambda\geq\frac14.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict inequality
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e
\tag{20}
\]
is elementary from the exponential series:
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
=\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk d^{-k}
\leq\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}
<\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
=e.
\]
It gives \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\).  Also \(k!\geq2^{k-1}\) for \(k\geq2\),
with strict inequality for \(k\geq3\), so
\[
e
=2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
<
2+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{2^j}
=3<4.
\]
Consequently
\[
0<\alpha_d<\frac1{16}<\frac14.
\tag{21}
\]

For every light atom in (18),
\[
\pi_z^2<\alpha_d\pi_z.
\]
There is at least one positive light atom by (19), so summing this strict
inequality gives
\[
\sum_{\{0<\pi_z<\alpha_d\}}\pi_z^2
<\alpha_d\lambda.
\tag{22}
\]
For every remaining positive atom, the strict singleton hypothesis (1)
gives
\[
\pi_z^2<\frac14\pi_z.
\]
If there is at least one such atom, summing is strict; if there is none,
both sides of the corresponding sum are zero.  In either case, the
strictness already present in (22) gives
\[
\sum_z\pi_z^2
<
\alpha_d\lambda+\frac14(1-\lambda).
\tag{23}
\]

For each parity class \(r\), put
\[
P_r:=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}\pi_z.
\]
There are four classes and \(\sum_rP_r=1\), including any empty classes,
so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_rP_r^2\geq\frac14.
\tag{24}
\]
Using (11), (18), (19), (21), (23), and (24), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Q^\circ}{W^2}
&=\sum_rP_r^2-\sum_z\pi_z^2\\
&>
\frac14-\alpha_d\lambda-\frac14(1-\lambda)\\
&=
\lambda\left(\frac14-\alpha_d\right)\\
&\geq
\frac14\left(\frac14-\frac1{64\gamma_d}\right)\\
&=
\frac{16-\gamma_d^{-1}}{256}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Finally, (20) is precisely
\[
\gamma_d^{-1}
=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<e.
\]
Subtracting from \(16\) and dividing by \(256\) proves
\[
\frac{16-\gamma_d^{-1}}{256}
>
\frac{16-e}{256}.
\]
Together with (25), this proves (12) and completes the dichotomy.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, “Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), pages 1257 to 1261, Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pages 1259 to 1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257.  Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c(K)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap J)\geq(d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\).  Applicability: for every \(z\) with \(w_z>0\), the compact convex fiber \(C_z\) has positive \(d\)-volume and hence is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body; its centroid is an admissible point in (3).
