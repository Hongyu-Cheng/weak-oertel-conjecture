## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\]
\[
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{y\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\ni y\\H\ \mathrm{closed\ halfspace}}}\mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C).
\]
For a positive-volume convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), put
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kxg(x)\,dx:
 0\leq g\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_Kg(x)\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\]

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh hard instance of accepted facts
7ab0a03cf9189637, 6b09aae189415c70, and
6bfe50fed9a5540b. Thus \(Q>0\),
\[
 D<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 Q\geq\frac3{32}(W-4G).
\tag{1}
\]
There are physical target degrees \(d_m\), target-fiber volumes \(a_m\),
target centroids \(c_m\), and an exact off-diagonal same-parity target
matching of mass \(P_{\mathrm{tar}}\) such that
\[
 0\leq d_m\leq\frac{a_m}{2},
 \qquad
 \sum_md_m=2P_{\mathrm{tar}},
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{tar}}>\frac{381}{800}Q.
\tag{2}
\]
For every positive-volume physical target fiber put
\[
 \mathcal K_m:=\mathsf M_{a_m/2}(C_m),
 \qquad
 D_m:=\mathcal K_m-c_m.
\tag{3}
\]
Every \(D_m\) is a full-dimensional compact origin-symmetric convex body.

For each parity class
\(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 L_\xi:=\sum_{m\equiv\xi\pmod2}d_m.
\]
For distinct \(m,m'\) in a class with \(L_\xi>0\), define
\[
 q_{\{m,m'\}}:=\frac{d_md_{m'}}{L_\xi},
\]
and put all other \(q_f=0\). Let \(F:=\{f:q_f>0\}\), and write
\[
 d_m(q):=\sum_{f\ni m}q_f,
 \qquad
 |q|:=\sum_{f\in F}q_f.
\]
The accepted product-rematching theorem 7ab0a03cf9189637 gives
\[
 d_m(q)=d_m\left(1-\frac{d_m}{L_\xi}\right)
 \quad(L_\xi>0),
 \qquad
 \frac{381}{1600}Q<|q|\leq\frac Q2.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(f=\{m,m'\}\in F\), put
\[
 \widehat m_f:=\frac{m+m'}2,
 \qquad
 \bar c_f:=\frac{c_m+c_{m'}}2,
 \qquad
 \bar D_f:=\frac12(D_m+D_{m'}),
 \qquad
 \eta_f:=\frac{q_f}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
The same accepted fact supplies vectors
\(\theta_f\in\mathbb R^d\) such that
\[
 h_{\bar D_f}(\theta_f)\leq\eta_f,
 \qquad
 \sum_f\theta_f=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_f\theta_f\widehat m_f^{\mathsf T}=0,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_{1/2}:=
 \sum_f\left(
 \langle\theta_f,\bar c_f\rangle
 -\frac12h_{\bar D_f}(\theta_f)
 \right)
 >\frac{293}{16000}Q.
\tag{7}
\]

For every physical endpoint \(m\), with an empty incident sum interpreted as
zero, define
\[
 \ell_m:=\sum_{f\ni m}\eta_f=\frac12d_m(q),
 \qquad
 U_m:=\frac12\sum_{f\ni m}\theta_f,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
 \pi_m:=\frac14\sum_{f\ni m}h_{D_m}(\theta_f),
 \qquad
 \tau_m:=h_{D_m}(U_m),
 \qquad
 S:=\{m:\ell_m>0\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Then \(S\ne\varnothing\), and
\[
 \sum_mU_m=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_mU_mm^{\mathsf T}=0,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
 0\leq\tau_m\leq2\pi_m\leq\ell_m\leq\frac{a_m}{4},
 \qquad
 \Delta_{1/2}=\sum_m
 \bigl(\langle U_m,c_m\rangle-\pi_m\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, if \(\ell_m=0\), then
\(U_m=\pi_m=\tau_m=0\), and no endpoint body or atom is defined at \(m\).

For every \(m\in S\), choose \(e_m\in D_m\) satisfying
\[
 \langle U_m,e_m\rangle=\tau_m
\]
when \(\tau_m>0\), and put \(e_m:=0\) when \(\tau_m=0\). Define
\[
 x_m:=c_m-\frac12e_m,
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_m:=x_m+\frac1{80}D_m.
\tag{12}
\]
Then every \(\mathcal R_m\) is a full-dimensional compact convex body of
positive radius and
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal R_m\subseteq\mathcal K_m\subseteq C_m.}
\tag{13}
\]

For every simultaneous choice \(z=(z_m)_{m\in S}\) with
\(z_m\in\mathcal R_m\), put
\[
 \nu_z:=2\gamma_d\sum_{m\in S}\ell_m
 \delta_{(m,z_m)}.
\tag{14}
\]
Then \(\nu_z\) is carried by
\(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and for every closed ambient
halfspace \(H\),
\[
 \boxed{\nu_z(H)\leq\mu_C(H),}
 \qquad
 \boxed{|\nu_z|=2\gamma_d|q|>
 \frac{381\gamma_d}{800}Q.}
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(2\gamma_d\) is a valid uniform all-selection endpoint coefficient,
with no reuse of a physical endpoint fiber.

The endpoint forces retain the macroscopic defect. For every simultaneous
choice \(z_m\in\mathcal R_m\) and every affine map
\(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{m\in S}
 \langle U_m,z_m-a(m)\rangle
 \geq\Delta_{1/2}-\frac{|q|}{80}
 >\frac{193}{16000}Q.}
\tag{16}
\]
For \(v\in\mathbb R^d\), use the gauge
\[
 \|v\|_{D_m}:=\inf\{t\geq0:v\in tD_m\},
\]
and put
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_m}(y,\mathcal R_m)
 :=\inf_{z\in\mathcal R_m}\|y-z\|_{D_m}.
\]
Then (16) also gives the actual-body affine-distance defect
\[
 \boxed{
 \inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \sum_{m\in S}\ell_m
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_m}\bigl(a(m),\mathcal R_m\bigr)
 >\frac{193}{16000}Q.}
\tag{17}
\]

Put
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{end}}:=\dim\operatorname{aff}S,
\]
and, for a simultaneous selection \(z_m\in\mathcal R_m\), define
\[
 r_{\mathrm{end}}(z):=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}S\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{z_m-a(m):m\in S\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Every simultaneous selection obeys the residual-rank screen
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathrm{end}}(z)+1>
 \frac{381\gamma_d}{8}\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}.}
\tag{19}
\]

Assume, only for the following lattice-rank conclusion, that there is a fixed
\(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) for which \(C\) is a counterexample at the least positive
base dimension \(n\), in the exact sense of accepted fact
1e3269a0e9b5695a. Then
\[
 \boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\geq n-2.}
\tag{20}
\]
Equivalently, the smallest primitive affine lattice subspace containing the
positive endpoint support has rank at least \(n-2\).

No assertion is made about pooling endpoint bodies at midpoint targets,
extracting a low-residual-rank selection, combining different parity classes,
closing a positive-fresh branch, or proving the locked theorem.

## Proof

We first apply the accepted reflected-defect endpoint reduction
f025d631bd2f3444 to the edge labels \(f\in F\). Its exact alias map is
\[
 (\sigma,\eta_\sigma,a_\sigma,b_\sigma,m_\sigma,
 q_\sigma,B_\sigma)
 \longleftrightarrow
 (f,\eta_f,m,m',\widehat m_f,\bar c_f,\bar D_f).
\tag{21}
\]
Here the predecessor's center \(q_\sigma\) maps to \(\bar c_f\) and is
unrelated to the product weight \(q_f\). Every edge is off-diagonal and
same-parity. By (4) and (8),
\[
 \ell_m=\frac12d_m(q)\leq\frac12d_m\leq\frac{a_m}{4},
\tag{22}
\]
so all load hypotheses of f025d631bd2f3444 hold. Equations (6)-(7)
say precisely that the supplied \(\theta_f\) is a maximizing force family
for the predecessor's threshold-one-half reflected defect. Its endpoint
conclusions give (10)-(11).

If \(\ell_m=0\), then every nonnegative incident weight \(\eta_f\) is zero,
so there is no incident label in \(F\). Definitions (8)-(9) therefore give
\(U_m=\pi_m=\tau_m=0\), proving the stated zero-load convention. Equation
(4) gives \(S\ne\varnothing\).

For \(m\in S\), compactness supplies the exposing point \(e_m\) when
\(\tau_m>0\). If \(\tau_m=0\), full dimensionality of \(D_m\) implies
\(U_m=0\), and \(e_m=0\) has the required zero pairing. Origin symmetry and
convexity give
\[
 -\frac12e_m+\frac1{80}D_m
 \subseteq\frac12D_m+\frac1{80}D_m
 =\frac{41}{80}D_m
 \subseteq D_m.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus (12) gives
\(\mathcal R_m\subseteq c_m+D_m=\mathcal K_m\).
Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives
\(\mathcal K_m\subseteq C_m\). Since \(D_m\) is full-dimensional and the
dilation factor \(1/80\) is positive, \(\mathcal R_m\) is a full-dimensional
compact convex body of positive radius. This proves (13).

Fix a simultaneous selection \(z_m\in\mathcal R_m\) and a closed ambient
halfspace \(H\). If \((m,z_m)\in H\), then the fiber section \(H_m\) is a
closed halfspace, possibly the whole space, containing
\(z_m\in\mathsf M_{a_m/2}(C_m)\). The metronoid depth theorem
6769574fc1bd9f8d gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_m\cap H_m)
 \geq\gamma_d\frac{a_m}{2}
 \geq2\gamma_d\ell_m,
\tag{24}
\]
where the last inequality is (11). The selected bases \(m\in S\) are
distinct physical fibers. Summing (24) over exactly those selected atoms
that lie in \(H\) proves the halfspace domination in (15). Also
\[
 \sum_m\ell_m
 =\sum_m\sum_{f\ni m}\eta_f
 =2\sum_f\eta_f
 =|q|.
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (4), (14), and (25) prove the mass formula and strict lower bound
in (15).

The endpoint contact in (12) satisfies the exact identity
\[
 \langle U_m,x_m\rangle
 =\langle U_m,c_m\rangle-\frac12\tau_m.
\tag{26}
\]
This includes \(\tau_m=0\). Summing (26), then using (11), gives
\[
 \sum_{m\in S}\langle U_m,x_m\rangle
 \geq\sum_m(\langle U_m,c_m\rangle-\pi_m)
 =\Delta_{1/2}.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(z_m\in x_m+D_m/80\), origin symmetry yields
\[
 \langle U_m,z_m\rangle
 \geq\langle U_m,x_m\rangle-\frac1{80}\tau_m.
\tag{28}
\]
The two moment identities in (10) annihilate every affine map, so
\[
 \sum_m\langle U_m,a(m)\rangle=0.
\tag{29}
\]
Summing (28), and using (7), (11), (25), and
\(|q|\leq Q/2\), proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{m\in S}\langle U_m,z_m-a(m)\rangle
 &\geq\Delta_{1/2}-\frac1{80}\sum_m\tau_m\\
 &\geq\Delta_{1/2}-\frac{|q|}{80}\\
 &>\frac{293}{16000}Q-\frac1{160}Q
 =\frac{193}{16000}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (16).

To prove (17), fix an affine \(a\). Compactness of every
\(\mathcal R_m\) gives a point \(z_m\in\mathcal R_m\) attaining
\(\operatorname{dist}_{D_m}(a(m),\mathcal R_m)\). Since \(D_m\) is
origin-symmetric,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle U_m,z_m-a(m)\rangle
 &\leq h_{D_m}(U_m)\|z_m-a(m)\|_{D_m}\\
 &=\tau_m\|z_m-a(m)\|_{D_m}\\
 &\leq\ell_m
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_m}(a(m),\mathcal R_m).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Summing (31) and applying the stronger uniform lower bound in (16) shows
that every affine \(a\) has distance objective at least
\(\Delta_{1/2}-|q|/80>193Q/16000\). Taking the infimum proves (17).

The support of \(\nu_z\) has base projection exactly \(S\), because all
\(\ell_m\) in (14) are positive. Its intrinsic continuous residual rank in
accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 is exactly \(r_{\mathrm{end}}(z)\).
Applying that accepted dominated-measure theorem to (15) gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{2\gamma_d|q|}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}(r_{\mathrm{end}}(z)+1)}.
\tag{32}
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\), then using (1) and (4), yields
\[
 \frac Q{100}>D\geq
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}
 \frac{2\gamma_d|q|}{r_{\mathrm{end}}(z)+1}
 >2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}
 \frac{381\gamma_d Q/800}{r_{\mathrm{end}}(z)+1}.
\tag{33}
\]
Rearrangement proves (19).

It remains to prove the conditional lattice-rank conclusion. The target
degrees in (2) are degrees of an off-diagonal matching. Hence, whenever
\(d_m>0\), the total degree at all other vertices of its parity class is at
least \(d_m\), so \(d_m\leq L_\xi/2\). Equation (4) then gives
\[
 d_m(q)\geq\frac{d_m}{2}>0.
\]
The reverse implication is immediate from the same formula. Therefore
\[
 S=\{m:d_m>0\}.
\tag{34}
\]
Equations (2) and (34) give
\[
 \sum_{m\in S}a_m
 \geq2\sum_md_m
 =4P_{\mathrm{tar}}
 >\frac{381}{200}Q.
\tag{35}
\]
Accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 6bfe50fed9a5540b, on the same
canonical hard instance, give the last two inequalities in (1). Thus
\[
 W\leq\frac{32}{3}Q+4G
 <\left(\frac{32}{3}+\frac1{25}\right)Q
 =\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{36}
\]

For \(n\leq2\), (20) is immediate from
\(\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\geq0\). Suppose \(n\geq3\) and, toward a
contradiction, \(\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\leq n-3\). The saturation of the
lattice generated by differences of points of \(S\) gives a primitive affine
lattice subspace \(L\) of rank \(\rho_{\mathrm{end}}\) containing \(S\).
Under the stated least-base-dimension counterexample hypothesis, accepted
fact 1e3269a0e9b5695a gives
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
 <2^{\rho_{\mathrm{end}}-n}W
 \leq\frac W8
 <\frac{803}{600}Q.
\tag{37}
\]
But (35) and \(S\subseteq L\) give
\[
 W_L\geq\sum_{m\in S}a_m
 >\frac{381}{200}Q
 =\frac{1143}{600}Q
 >\frac{803}{600}Q,
\tag{38}
\]
contradicting (37). Hence (20) holds. The construction uses one body per
distinct endpoint only, and none of its conclusions supplies the excluded
cross-parity or final extraction step.
