## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and set
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}),
\]
where the volume of the empty set is zero. Let the selector domain be precisely the positive-mass fiber set
\[
D_+:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf z}>0\},
\]
and assume that the total mixed-integer volume is positive:
\[
W:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}
=\mathcal H_d(S)>0.
\]
Suppose there are \(\beta\in(0,1]\), a matrix \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\), and a vector \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^d\) such that the affine map
\[
q(\mathbf z):=A\mathbf z+\mathbf b,\qquad \mathbf z\in D_+,
\]
is a selector, meaning \(q(\mathbf z)\in C_{\mathbf z}\) for every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\), and such that it has uniform relative fiber depth \(\beta\): for every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\) and every proper closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(q(\mathbf z)\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap L)\geq
\beta\,w_{\mathbf z}.
\]
Then there is a point \(\mathbf z^\star\in D_+\), in particular \(w_{\mathbf z^\star}>0\), such that
\[
\mathbf y^\star:=(\mathbf z^\star,q(\mathbf z^\star))\in S
\]
and every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq
\frac{\beta}{2^n}\,\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

As a corollary, suppose that for every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\), the centroid \(\mathfrak c(C_{\mathbf z})\) is affine in \(\mathbf z\): there are \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^d\) with
\[
\mathfrak c(C_{\mathbf z})=A\mathbf z+\mathbf b
\quad\text{for every }\mathbf z\in D_+.
\]
Then the preceding conclusion holds with
\[
\beta=\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
and hence
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2^n}\,\mathcal H_d(S)
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\) containing the resulting \(\mathbf y^\star\).

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that its projection onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded. Consequently only finitely many integer vectors have nonempty fibers, so \(D_+\) is finite. It is nonempty because \(W>0\).

We first prove the weighted lattice centerpoint statement needed below. Define a finite measure on \(\mathbb R^n\) by
\[
\nu(E):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+\cap E}w_{\mathbf z},
\]
so \(\nu(\mathbb R^n)=W\). Put \(h:=2^n\), and consider the finite family of compact convex sets
\[
\mathscr K:=
\left\{
\operatorname{conv}(Y):
Y\subseteq D_+,\
\nu(Y)>\left(1-\frac1h\right)W
\right\}.
\]
Take any \(r\leq h\) members \(\operatorname{conv}(Y_1),\ldots,\operatorname{conv}(Y_r)\) of \(\mathscr K\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^rY_i\right)
&=W-\nu\left(D_+\setminus\bigcap_{i=1}^rY_i\right)\\
&\geq W-\sum_{i=1}^r\nu(D_+\setminus Y_i)\\
&>W-\frac r hW
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\bigcap_{i=1}^rY_i\) contains a point of \(D_+\), which is an integer point common to the corresponding \(r\) convex hulls. Doignon's lattice Helly theorem therefore gives
\[
\mathbf z^\star\in
\mathbb Z^n\cap\bigcap_{K\in\mathscr K}K.
\]
Since \(Y=D_+\) is one of the sets defining \(\mathscr K\), we also have
\[
\mathbf z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(D_+).
\]

We claim that every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(\mathbf z^\star\) satisfies
\[
\nu(Q)\geq\frac Wh.
\]
Otherwise \(Y:=D_+\setminus Q\) would satisfy
\[
\nu(Y)=W-\nu(Q)>
\left(1-\frac1h\right)W,
\]
so \(\operatorname{conv}(Y)\in\mathscr K\) and hence \(\mathbf z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(Y)\). But \(Y\) is finite and lies in the open halfspace complementary to \(Q\), so its convex hull also lies in that open halfspace and cannot contain \(\mathbf z^\star\in Q\). This contradiction proves the claim. Thus the weighted lattice centerpoint guarantee is the required \(h^{-1}=2^{-n}\); no integrality or rationality of the positive weights was used.

It remains essential to check that \(\mathbf z^\star\) belongs to the selector domain. Because \(\mathbf z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\), write
\[
\mathbf z^\star=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\mathbf z_i,
\qquad
\mathbf z_i\in D_+,\quad
\lambda_i>0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1.
\]
Fix an index \(i_0\), and for every \(i\neq i_0\) choose \(\mathbf x_i\in C_{\mathbf z_i}\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\lambda_{i_0}C_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}
+\sum_{i\neq i_0}\lambda_i\mathbf x_i
\subseteq C_{\mathbf z^\star}.
\]
The set on the left is a translate of a positive scaling of \(C_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}\), and therefore
\[
w_{\mathbf z^\star}
\geq\lambda_{i_0}^{\,d}w_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}>0.
\]
Hence \(\mathbf z^\star\in D_+\). The selector is defined there, and
\[
\mathbf y^\star=(\mathbf z^\star,q(\mathbf z^\star))
\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)=S.
\]

By the accepted boundary-reduction fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\), to lower-bound the mixed-integer volume of every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), it suffices to treat a closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y^\star\). Let such a halfspace be
\[
H_0=
\left\{
(\mathbf z,\mathbf x):
\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+\mathbf c^\top(\mathbf x-q(\mathbf z^\star))\geq0
\right\},
\]
where \((\mathbf a,\mathbf c)\neq(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\). Set
\[
\mathbf r:=\mathbf a+A^\top\mathbf c,
\qquad
Q:=
\left\{
\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n:
\mathbf r^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)\geq0
\right\}.
\]
If \(\mathbf r\neq\mathbf0\), then \(Q\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z^\star\), so \(\nu(Q)\geq2^{-n}W\). If \(\mathbf r=\mathbf0\), then \(Q=\mathbb R^n\) and the same inequality holds because \(\nu(Q)=W\).

For every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\cap Q\), affineness of \(q\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+\mathbf c^\top(q(\mathbf z)-q(\mathbf z^\star))\\
&\qquad=
(\mathbf a+A^\top\mathbf c)^\top
(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
=\mathbf r^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the fiber section
\[
L_{\mathbf z}:=
\left\{
\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:
\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+\mathbf c^\top(\mathbf x-q(\mathbf z^\star))\geq0
\right\}
\]
contains \(q(\mathbf z)\). If \(\mathbf c\neq\mathbf0\), this is a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\), and the fiber-depth hypothesis gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap L_{\mathbf z})
\geq\beta w_{\mathbf z}.
\]
If \(\mathbf c=\mathbf0\), then \(L_{\mathbf z}=\mathbb R^d\) for every \(\mathbf z\in Q\), so the same inequality follows from \(\beta\leq1\). Summing only these nonnegative contributions yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap L_{\mathbf z})\\
&\geq
\beta\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+\cap Q}w_{\mathbf z}
=\beta\,\nu(Q)\\
&\geq\frac{\beta}{2^n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]
For an arbitrary closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\) supplies such an \(H_0\subseteq H\), and hence
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\geq
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)\geq
\frac{\beta}{2^n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\]
This proves the affine-selector theorem.

For the centroid corollary, every \(C_{\mathbf z}\) with \(\mathbf z\in D_+\) is a \(d\)-dimensional compact convex set of positive volume, and its centroid belongs to \(C_{\mathbf z}\). Thus the affine centroid map is an affine selector on exactly \(D_+\). Grünbaum's inequality says that every closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(\mathfrak c(C_{\mathbf z})\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap L)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})
=\gamma_dw_{\mathbf z}.
\]
The affine-selector theorem applied with \(\beta=\gamma_d\) gives the asserted corollary.

## External sources

Amitabh Basu and Timm Oertel, "Centerpoints: A Link between Optimization and Convex Geometry," SIAM Journal on Optimization 27(2) (2017), 866-889, Theorem 15, https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1092908 (author manuscript: https://www.ams.jhu.edu/~abasu9/papers/centerpoints.pdf). Exact statement used for verification: if \(T\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) is nonempty and closed, a probability measure is supported on \(T\), and the Helly number \(h(T)\) is finite, then its centerpoint value \(F(T,\mu)\), defined as the maximum over \(\mathbf x\in T\) of the infimum of \(\mu\) over closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(\mathbf x\), is at least \(1/h(T)\). Every closed halfspace containing the selected point contains its boundary-through-the-point translate, so the same lower bound holds for all containing closed halfspaces. Applicability check: with \(T=\mathbb Z^n\), \(h(T)=2^n\), and \(\mu=W^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}w_{\mathbf z}\delta_{\mathbf z}\), this is precisely the weighted lattice centerpoint assertion. The proof above independently derives the finite-support specialization and additionally proves the needed facts \(\mathbf z^\star\in\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\) and \(w_{\mathbf z^\star}>0\).

Jean-Paul Doignon, "Convexity in cristallographical lattices," Journal of Geometry 3 (1973), 71-85, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01949705. Exact theorem used: for a finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^n\), if every subfamily of at most \(2^n\) members has a common point in \(\mathbb Z^n\), then the whole family has a common point in \(\mathbb Z^n\); equivalently \(h(\mathbb Z^n)=2^n\). Applicability check: \(\mathscr K\) is a finite family of compact convex hulls, and the displayed strict weight calculation gives a common point of \(D_+\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) for every subfamily of at most \(2^n\) members.

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of mass-distributions and of convex bodies by hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257-1261, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(\mathfrak c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(L\) containing \(\mathfrak c(K)\) satisfies \(\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap L)\geq(d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\). This exact formulation is also recorded as Theorem 2.2 in the local Cheng-Basu source manuscript at lines 123-130. Applicability check: every \(C_{\mathbf z}\) with \(\mathbf z\in D_+\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body and has the centroid specified in the corollary.
