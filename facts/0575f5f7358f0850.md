## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, and let $A_i$ for $1\le i\le k$ be nonempty compact subsets of $\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$. Let $M_i$ be their raw mixed $d$-volumes and $a_i$ their peak fiber volumes. Let $\lambda_i>0$ sum to one and put $T=\sum_i\lambda_i A_i$. For any such $T$, $\mu(T)$ measures its integer fibers, each as a union.

If every $M_i>0$ and every weighted base combination of positive-volume input fibers is integral, then
$$
\mu(T)\ge\left(\sum_i M_i/a_i-k+1\right)
\left(\sum_i\lambda_i a_i^{1/d}\right)^d.
$$
For two inputs the first factor is at least $\max(M_1/a_1,M_2/a_2)$. If all weighted combinations of nonempty input bases are integral, then also for possibly zero input masses,
$$
\mu(T)^{1/d}\ge\sum_i\lambda_i M_i^{1/d}.
$$
Zero coefficients are handled by deleting those inputs. The root inequality has equality for single-fiber homothetic convex inputs at a common base. The peak bound is attained for equal weights on arithmetic progressions of step $k$ on an integer line, with identical fibers within each input and homothetic convex fibers across inputs.

For $d=0$ and nonempty finite input sets with integral weighted combinations, $|T|\ge\sum_i|A_i|-k+1$ for positive weights. In particular $|T|\ge\max(|A_1|,|A_2|)$ for two inputs. If inputs lie in a common compact convex body $C$ and the respective caps $\ell\ge t_i$ for one ambient affine functional $\ell$, the applicable bounds also hold for the raw mixed measure of the actual cap of $C$ at $\ell\ge\sum_i\lambda_i t_i$.

## Proof

Suppose first that all input masses are positive. At $0<q\le1$ let $P_i(q)$ be the bases with fiber volume at least $q a_i$. Each is nonempty. Put $L=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i^{1/d}$. For any selected bases, the fiber at their weighted sum contains the Minkowski combination of their compact fibers. Ordinary $d$-dimensional Brunn–Minkowski gives volume at least $qL^d$.

For any two finite nonempty sets of vectors $U,V$, list each in increasing order of a generic linear functional injective on each set. The sums obtained by keeping the first element of $U$ and traversing $V$, then holding the last element of $V$ and traversing the remaining elements of $U$, are strictly increasing in that functional. Thus $|U+V|\ge|U|+|V|-1$. Iterate this inequality and use injectivity of positive scalar multiplication to get $|\sum_i\lambda_iP_i(q)|\ge\sum_i|P_i(q)|-k+1$. Every counted output base is integral by the stated hypothesis, and each counts only once even if several input combinations produce it. Integrating the count by layer cake, restricted to volume thresholds between zero and $L^d$, gives
$$
\mu(T)\ge L^d\int_0^1\left(\sum_i|P_i(q)|-k+1\right)dq
=L^d\left(\sum_i M_i/a_i-k+1\right).
$$
Here the integral of each input indicator is its fiber volume divided by $a_i$. This proves the bound. Since all effective counts are at least one, their sum minus $k-1$ is at least every one of them. Thus multiplying $L$ by the $d$th root of that factor gives at least $\sum_i\lambda_i M_i^{1/d}$.

When some inputs have zero mass, fix one actual point in each of them. The positive inputs now have exactly the same level sets as before, while the weighted fixed points give one translation. The weighted output bases after that translation are integral by the all-nonempty-base hypothesis. The distinct-sum argument and layer cake for the positive inputs therefore still apply with their original coefficients and with $k$ replaced by their number. A common fixed translation does not affect the volumes in their fibers. The estimate gives the root inequality with only the positive terms. When every mass is zero the root inequality is immediate. Deleting zero weights includes the two-input endpoints.

For sharpness at equal weights, take the bases $0,k,\ldots,k(N_i-1)$ on a primitive integer line for input $i$, with a fixed fiber $r_i K$, where $K$ is compact convex of volume one and $r_i>0$. The output bases form an interval of exactly $\sum_iN_i-k+1$ integer sites on that line, all of whose fibers equal $(\sum_i r_i/k)K$. The formula is equality. For single-fiber homothetic convex bodies at a common integral base, the root inequality is ordinary Brunn–Minkowski equality.

In $d=0$ the same finite distinct-sum inequality gives the claimed counting bound directly. Finally any weighted combination of points from the cap inputs lies in $C$ by convexity and has affine functional value at least the weighted threshold. The integral output fibers therefore contribute their union volumes to the actual output cap. This containment applies to every ambient affine functional, including oblique cuts and closed cuts carrying entire boundary fibers.
