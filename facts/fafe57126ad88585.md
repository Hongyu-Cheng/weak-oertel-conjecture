## Statement

Let a nondegenerate triangle have vertices $v_1,v_2,v_3$ and let $y$ be interior, with barycentrics $y_j$. For $j=1,2,3$ a closed halfplane $H_j$ through $y$ contains $v_j$ strictly and no other vertex strictly. Let $t_{jk}\in(0,1]$ be its initial edge fraction from $v_j$ on $v_jv_k$. Let $\theta_j>0$ sum to one and $\beta>0$. Put $W=\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf1_{H_j}$. Suppose on each edge, with length measure,
$$
\int (W-\beta)\psi=0\quad\text{for all affine }\psi,
\qquad
\int (W-\beta)\Phi\le0\quad\text{for all nonnegative convex }\Phi.
$$
Then the data are precisely those given by $r_1,r_2,r_3>0$ with $\sum_{j<k}r_jr_k=1$. Writing $P=\sum_jr_j$ and $R=r_1r_2r_3$, they satisfy
$$
\beta=\frac{P-R}{2P},\qquad
\theta_j=\frac{\beta}{1+r_j^2},\qquad
y_j=\frac{r_j(r_k+r_l)}2,\qquad
t_{jk}=\frac{r_k(1+r_j^2)}{r_j+r_k}<1,
$$
where $k,l$ in the formula for $y_j$ are the other two indices. Every such triple gives a positive rank-two circuit of cap normals. In particular $\beta\ge4/9$, with equality exactly at the centroid with uniform weights and all edge fractions $2/3$.

## Proof

On edge $v_jv_k$ the third cap is null for length, and caps $j,k$ occupy $[0,t_{jk}]$ and $[1-t_{kj},1]$ in relative edge coordinates. The constant and centered linear tests give
$$
x_jt_{jk}+x_kt_{kj}=1,\qquad
x_jt_{jk}(1-t_{jk})=x_kt_{kj}(1-t_{kj}),\qquad x_j=\theta_j/\beta.
$$
Testing the convex hinge $(h-s)_+$ in the edge coordinate $s$ with $0<h<t_{jk}$ shows $x_j\le1$, since $W\ge\theta_j$ there. Set $b_{jk}=(1-t_{jk})/t_{jk}$. The cap margin in barycentrics is $L_j=\lambda_j-\sum_{k\ne j}b_{jk}\lambda_k$, so $y_j=\sum_{k\ne j}b_{jk}y_k$. Each row has a positive term. Contact at one end means $t_{jk}=1$, which forces $t_{kj}=1$ and $x_j+x_k=1$. On a strict edge, writing $b=b_{jk}$ and $c=b_{kj}$, the two moment equations solve to
$$
x_j=\frac{c(1+b)^2}{b+c+2bc},\quad
x_k=\frac{b(1+c)^2}{b+c+2bc},\quad
1-x_j=\frac{b(1-bc)}{b+c+2bc},\quad
1-x_k=\frac{c(1-bc)}{b+c+2bc}.
$$
If some $x_j=1$, neither incident edge can be a contact, since both neighboring weights are positive. The strict-edge formulas then give $x_k=1$ and $b_{jk}b_{kj}=1$ for either neighbor $k$. Both rows have their two terms positive, giving $y_j>b_{jk}y_k>b_{jk}b_{kj}y_j=y_j$. Thus $0<x_j<1$.

Put $z_j=1-x_j$ and $r_j=(z_j/x_j)^{1/2}$. On a strict edge the preceding equations give $b/z_j=c/z_k=\sigma$ and $z_jz_k(1+\sigma)^2=x_jx_k$: the latter follows by substituting $b=\sigma z_j,c=\sigma z_k$ in $z_j=b(1-bc)/(b+c+2bc)$. The positive root gives
$$
b_{jk}=\frac{r_j/r_k-r_j^2}{1+r_j^2}.
$$
On a contact edge $x_j+x_k=1$ gives $r_jr_k=1$, so the same formula gives the correct zero odds. Substitution into the common-source equations gives $2y_j=r_j\sum_k y_k/r_k-r_j^2$. Dividing by $r_j$ and summing yields $\sum_k y_k/r_k=P$, and summing without division yields $\sum_{j<k}r_jr_k=1$. Hence every product $r_jr_k$ is strictly below one, so all odds are positive and the fractions have the stated values. Normalizing the weights gives $\beta^{-1}=\sum_j(1+r_j^2)^{-1}$. Expanding with $\sum_{j<k}r_jr_k=1$ gives $\prod_j(1+r_j^2)=(P-R)^2$ and $\sum_{j<k}(1+r_j^2)(1+r_k^2)=2P(P-R)$. Here $P-R>0$, yielding the stated $\beta$. Also $P/R=3+\sum_{j<k}(r_j/r_k+r_k/r_j)\ge9$, with equality only for equal $r_j$, proving the bound and equality classification.

Conversely define the margins by the displayed odds from a permitted triple. The odds are positive, the $y_j$ are positive and sum to one, and substitution into the common-source equations shows $L_j(y)=0$. The strict-edge formulas give the moment equations: for example the two mass contributions are $x_jt_{jk}=r_k/(r_j+r_k)$ and $x_kt_{kj}=r_j/(r_j+r_k)$, and the two centered moment magnitudes agree since $1-t_{jk}=r_j(1-r_jr_k)/(r_j+r_k)$. The edge intervals overlap properly, because their fractions sum to $1+r_jr_k$. On the overlap the profile is positive since $x_j+x_k-1=(1-r_j^2r_k^2)/[(1+r_j^2)(1+r_k^2)]>0$. It is negative on both outer intervals. The affine secant to a convex test at the overlap endpoints is above that test inside and below it outside. Multiplying their difference by $W-\beta$ gives a nonpositive integrand, and the secant integral vanishes by the affine moments. This establishes the full convex stress.

The coefficients $\rho_j=y_j/z_j$ are positive and satisfy $\sum_j\rho_j L_j=0$, since $b_{jk}/z_j$ is symmetric and $y_j=\sum_{k\ne j}b_{jk}y_k$. The normals span the plane: otherwise at most two oriented parallel normals give at most two halfplanes through the same point, whereas the three halfplanes isolate distinct vertices. In particular $r=(1/2,1/2,3/4)$ is an actual noncanonical ordinary example. It gives $\beta=25/56$, $\theta=(5/14,5/14,2/7)$, $y=(5/16,5/16,3/8)$, and $t_{12}=t_{21}=t_{31}=t_{32}=5/8$, $t_{13}=t_{23}=3/4$, with margin relation proportional to $(3,3,2)$.
