## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge2\), let \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^n\), write \(\mathbf v_-:=-\mathbf1_n\), and set

\[
P_n:=\operatorname{conv}(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,\mathbf v_-),
\qquad
D_n:=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,\mathbf v_-\}.
\]

Here \(P_n\) denotes a polytope, not the product probability law \(P\) in accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374`. Use the canonical definitions of that fact with the exact aliases \(A=D_n\) and \(m=r=n\). Let \(p\) be uniform on \(D_n\), so \(p_{\mathbf x}=1/(n+2)\); this is its log-affine law with tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell=\mathbf0\). Explicitly,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\substack{H\text{ a proper closed halfspace in }\mathbb R^n\\
                         \mathbf j\in H}}p(H),
&
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_n}p_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},\\
\mathcal B_2(p)
&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_n}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2,
&
\mathcal M_2(p)
&:=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_n}p_{\mathbf j}\tau_p(\mathbf j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For each \(\mathbf j\in D_n\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}:=r_{D_n,\mathbf j}\), meaning exactly the maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) row denoted \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) with \(E=D_n\) in the accepted fact. For this uniform \(p\), it is also the relative-entropy minimizer denoted \(r_{\mathbf j}\) there. Define the capacity weights and the all-row likelihood variance by

\[
\omega_{\mathbf j}:=\frac{p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2}{\mathcal B_2(p)},
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{allrows}}(p):=
\max_{\mathbf j\in D_n}
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
\left[\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)}{p_{\mathbf X}}\right].
\]

The logarithm is evaluated only on the positive support of \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\). Thus \(V_{\mathrm{allrows}}(p)\) is exactly \(\sigma_p^2\) from equation (31) of `bf1ff94487296374`, under the alias \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{\mathbf j}\).

The support is complete: \(P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_n\). Its only nonvertex support point is the interior point \(\mathbf0\), and \(P_n\) is not affinely equivalent to a product of two positive-dimensional polytopes. Its entire row and capacity census is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{\mathbf0}&=p,
&\mathsf C_p(\mathbf0)&=1,
&\tau_p(\mathbf0)&=\frac2{n+2},
&\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf0}}(\mathbf0)&=\frac2{n+2},\\
\nu_{\mathbf v}&=\delta_{\mathbf v},
&\mathsf C_p(\mathbf v)&=\frac1{n+2},
&\tau_p(\mathbf v)&=\frac1{n+2},
&\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf v}}(\mathbf v)&=1
\quad(\mathbf v\in\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,\mathbf v_-\}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the depth of a row law is defined by the same closed-halfspace infimum, on its positive support. All likelihood variances vanish: \(V_{\mathrm{allrows}}(p)=0\).

For \(\mathbf X\sim\nu_{\mathbf0}\) and direction \(\mathbf a=\mathbf1_n\), the scalar \(Y:=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf0)\) is centered and satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=\frac{n(n+1)}{n+2},
&\mathbb E Y^4&=\frac{n+n^4}{n+2},\\
\frac{\mathbb E Y^4}{(\mathbb E Y^2)^2}
&=\frac{(n+2)(n^2-n+1)}{n(n+1)}
=n-\frac{n-2}{n(n+1)}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]

The capacity-to-actual-moment ledger is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_2(p)&=\frac{n^2+5n+5}{(n+2)^3},
&\mathcal M_2(p)&=\frac{n+5}{(n+2)^3},\\
\omega_{\mathbf0}&=\frac{(n+2)^2}{n^2+5n+5}\longrightarrow1,
&\omega_{\mathbf v}&=\frac1{n^2+5n+5},\\
\frac{\mathcal M_2(p)}{\mathcal B_2(p)}
&=\frac{n+5}{n^2+5n+5}\longrightarrow0,
&\frac{\tau_p(\mathbf0)}{\mathsf C_p(\mathbf0)}&=\frac2{n+2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently, over all dimensions, complete finite lattice supports, and positive log-affine laws, no absolute \(\kappa<\infty\) bounds every nondegenerate entropy-row directional kurtosis. Even restricted to laws with \(V_{\mathrm{allrows}}(p)=0\), there is no absolute \(c>0\) such that \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\ge c\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)\) at every row, and no absolute \(c>0\) such that \(\mathcal M_2(p)\ge c\mathcal B_2(p)\) for every law.

## Proof

The vertices defining \(P_n\) are affinely independent. Indeed, an affine dependence with coefficients \(\alpha_-,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\) has \(\alpha_i=\alpha_-\) from each coordinate of \(\sum_i\alpha_i\mathbf e_i+\alpha_-\mathbf v_-=\mathbf0\); its zero coefficient sum then gives \((n+1)\alpha_-=0\). Thus \(P_n\) is a full-dimensional simplex. For any \(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n\), its unique barycentric coordinates are

\[
\lambda_-:=\frac{1-\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n+1},
\qquad
\lambda_i:=x_i+\lambda_-\quad(1\le i\le n).
\]

They sum to one and satisfy \(\mathbf x=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf e_i+\lambda_-\mathbf v_-\). Membership in \(P_n\) is therefore equivalent to their nonnegativity. If \(\mathbf x\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\), then \(0\le\lambda_-\le1\). When \(\lambda_-=1\), all \(\lambda_i\) are zero and \(\mathbf x=\mathbf v_-\). When \(\lambda_-<1\), each integer \(x_i=\lambda_i-\lambda_->-1\) is nonnegative. Also \(\lambda_-\ge0\) gives \(\sum_i x_i\le1\). Hence \(\mathbf x\) is either \(\mathbf0\) or one of the \(\mathbf e_i\). Conversely all these listed points belong to \(P_n\): at \(\mathbf0\), every barycentric coordinate is \(1/(n+1)>0\). This proves completeness and the full interior/boundary census.

A nontrivial product of polytopes of positive dimensions \(r_1,r_2\), with \(r_1+r_2=n\), has at least \((r_1+1)(r_2+1)=n+1+r_1r_2>n+1\) vertices. A factor has at least its dimension plus one vertices, and vertices of a product are exactly pairs of factor vertices. Affine equivalence preserves vertex count. Since \(P_n\) has exactly \(n+1\) vertices, it is not such a product.

The sum of the \(n+1\) vertices is zero, so the uniform law on \(D_n\) has mean zero. For any law \(\nu\) on \(D_n\), its relative entropy to \(p\) is \(\log(n+2)+\sum_{\mathbf x}\nu_{\mathbf x}\log\nu_{\mathbf x}\). It is nonnegative, with equality only at \(\nu=p\): apply Jensen's inequality to the strictly convex function \(t\log t\), extended continuously at zero, with weights \(p_{\mathbf x}\) and arguments \(\nu_{\mathbf x}/p_{\mathbf x}\). Thus \(p\), being feasible at mean zero, uniquely maximizes entropy there. At a vertex \(\mathbf v\), the only mean-\(\mathbf v\) law is \(\delta_{\mathbf v}\), as follows by taking an expectation of a linear functional uniquely maximized at that vertex. These functionals can be chosen as \(x_i\) at \(\mathbf e_i\) and \(-\mathbf1_n\cdot\mathbf x\) at \(\mathbf v_-\). This proves all row assertions. The entropy-capacity identity, including boundary centers, in `bf1ff94487296374` gives \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf0)=e^0=1\) and \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf v)=e^{-\log(n+2)}=1/(n+2)\).

The boundary capacities can also be checked directly, without assuming an attained ambient exponential infimum. Every exponential sum centered at a vertex contains the unchanged term \(p_{\mathbf v}\), so its infimum is at least that term. At \(\mathbf e_i\), choosing \(\mathbf u=t\mathbf e_i\), and at \(\mathbf v_-\), choosing \(\mathbf u=-t\mathbf1_n\), yields respectively

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_n}p_{\mathbf x}e^{t\mathbf e_i\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf e_i)}
&=\frac{1+n e^{-t}+e^{-2t}}{n+2},\\
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_n}p_{\mathbf x}e^{-t\mathbf1_n\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf v_-)}
&=\frac{1+e^{-nt}+n e^{-(n+1)t}}{n+2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Letting \(t\to\infty\) proves the claimed capacities. Neither boundary infimum is attained at a finite vector, since all other summands remain strictly positive. At \(\mathbf0\), Jensen's inequality and mean zero give every exponential sum at least one; \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\) attains one.

Every closed halfspace containing a support point includes its atom, so each vertex has depth at least \(1/(n+2)\). The proper closed halfspaces \(\{\mathbf x:x_i\ge1\}\) and \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf1_n\cdot\mathbf x\le-n\}\) isolate \(\mathbf e_i\) and \(\mathbf v_-\), respectively. Their vertex depths are therefore exactly their atom masses. For a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf0\), write it as \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge b\}\), where \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\) and \(b\le0\). Because the vertex projections sum to zero, at least one vertex has projection nonnegative. That vertex and \(\mathbf0\) both belong to the halfspace, giving mass at least \(2/(n+2)\). The halfspace \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf1_n\cdot\mathbf x\le0\}\) contains exactly \(\mathbf0\) and \(\mathbf v_-\) from \(D_n\), attaining this mass. Thus \(\tau_p(\mathbf0)=2/(n+2)\). The entropy-row depths follow because the root row is \(p\) and every vertex row is a Dirac law.

Under \(\nu_{\mathbf0}=p\), the projection \(Y\) takes value \(1\) at each of \(n\) sites, value \(-n\) at \(\mathbf v_-\), and value zero at the root, all with atom mass \(1/(n+2)\). Its mean is \((n-n)/(n+2)=0\). Its second and fourth moments are respectively \((n+n^2)/(n+2)\) and \((n+n^4)/(n+2)\). Their quotient simplifies using \(n^3+1=(n+1)(n^2-n+1)\) to the displayed kurtosis. For \(n\ge2\) this quotient is at least \(n-1\), proving divergence and ruling out an absolute directional fourth-moment constant.

On the root row the likelihood logarithm is identically zero. On a vertex row its only value is \(\log(n+2)\). Hence every likelihood variance is zero. The minimum positive entropy-row atom on \(D_n\) is nevertheless \(1/(n+2)\). In the notation of the accepted fact, even the sufficient minimum-atom hypothesis (21) cannot have a dimension-independent \(\kappa\) on these supports. More precisely, for the singleton family \(\mathscr A=\{D_n\}\), all proper face supports consist only of simplex vertices, so all their mean-at-support-point entropy rows are Dirac laws. Accordingly its constant \(\rho\) in (13) is exactly \(1/(n+2)\) and its \(\kappa=\max\{3,\rho^{-1}\}=n+2\). This is consistent with, rather than a contradiction to, the support-dependent fixed-family theorem.

There is one root of probability \(1/(n+2)\) and \(n+1\) vertices of that same probability. Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_2(p)
&=\frac1{n+2}+\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^3}
=\frac{n^2+5n+5}{(n+2)^3},\\
\mathcal M_2(p)
&=\frac4{(n+2)^3}+\frac{n+1}{(n+2)^3}
=\frac{n+5}{(n+2)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The stated capacity weights and moment ratio follow by division. In particular, the root depth-to-capacity ratio and the moment ratio both tend to zero despite zero likelihood variance. This disproves each asserted support-independent positive prefactor.

For this family, at every row one has the exact identity \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)/\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\mathbf j)\). Thus the capacity-weighted row-depth ledger is

\[
\frac{\mathcal M_2(p)}{\mathcal B_2(p)}
=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_n}\omega_{\mathbf j}\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\mathbf j)^2
=\frac4{n^2+5n+5}+\frac{n+1}{n^2+5n+5}.
\]

The first term is the root contribution, and the second is the aggregate vertex contribution. Although \(\omega_{\mathbf0}\to1\), its entropy-row depth is only \(2/(n+2)\). The ledger also shows why this obstruction is not a normalized-moment counterexample:

\[
4^n\mathcal B_2(p)
=4^n\frac{n^2+5n+5}{(n+2)^3}\sim\frac{4^n}{n},
\qquad
4^n\mathcal M_2(p)
=4^n\frac{n+5}{(n+2)^3}\sim\frac{4^n}{n^2}.
\]

Both diverge. The normalized capacity surplus can absorb the polynomial conversion loss in this witness. The direct next quantity to test on further complete supports is entropy-row halfspace depth on rows carrying substantial capacity weight, considered together with the normalized capacity surplus, rather than likelihood variance alone. This packet supplies the exact obstruction and ledger for this family; it asserts no universal surplus-versus-row-depth coupling bound.
