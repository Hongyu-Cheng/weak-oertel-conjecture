## Statement

Let $f:[0,L]\to[0,\infty)$ be integrable with positive integral, and suppose its positive set is convex and either $f^{1/r}$ is concave on that set for some $r>0$, or $\log f$ is concave there. Write $m=L^{-1}\int_0^L f$. If $0<q<L/2$ and $\int_0^q f=\int_{L-q}^L f=qm$, then $f=m$ almost everywhere.

## Proof

On each tail there is a point with $f\ge m$. Otherwise the positive function $m-f$ on that tail would have strictly positive integral, contradicting its specified mean. The interval joining these two points is in the positive set. Concavity of the specified increasing transform of $f$ makes $f\ge m$ on the middle interval $[q,L-q]$. The two tail equalities give $\int_q^{L-q} f=(L-2q)m$. Hence $f=m$ almost everywhere there, and equality holds throughout its open interior by continuity of the concave transform on the interior of its domain.

A concave function constant on a nondegenerate interval cannot be greater than that constant at any other point of its domain. For example, if it were greater at a point to the left, take two interior plateau points and apply concavity at the earlier point as a strict convex combination of the left point and the later plateau point. The same argument applies to the right. Thus $f\le m$ on the positive set, and also off it. Its prescribed tail integrals force $f=m$ almost everywhere on both tails.
