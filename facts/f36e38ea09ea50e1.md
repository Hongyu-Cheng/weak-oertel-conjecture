## Statement

Let $p\ge2$, $d=p+1$, and $T_p=\operatorname{conv}(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_p)$ with $v_0=(-1,\ldots,-1)$ and $v_i=v_0+(p+1)e_i$. Set $V_p=(p+1)^p/p!$, $\delta=(p/(p+1))^p$, and let
$$
K_p=\{(x,t): |t|\le1,\ x\in(1-|t|)T_p\},
\qquad V=2V_p/(p+1).
$$
Write $u_i=e_i$ for $1\le i\le p$ and $u_0=-\sum_{i=1}^p e_i$.

The body $K_p$ has centroid zero, volume $V$, and $d+2$ exposed vertices. For every $(u,v)\ne0$ and real $h$, the two closed caps with $u\cdot x+vt\ge h$ and $u\cdot x+vt\ge-h$ have total volume at least $2\delta V$. The maximal ordinary normalized depth is $\delta$. At the centroid the minimizing normal rays are precisely the rays of $(u_i,0)$. In each of those directions the paired inequality is strict for nonzero $h$.

## Proof

The body is the convex hull of the equatorial simplex and the two tips $(0,\pm1)$: combinations with the tip of the same sign as $t$ give every specified section, and any combination using both tips has equatorial coefficient at most $1-|t|$. Integrating section volumes gives $V$; section centroids are zero in $x$ and reflection makes the $t$ centroid zero. The normals $(u_i,0)$ and $(0,\pm1)$ expose the $p+3=d+2$ listed vertices.

For $u=0$ the $t$ marginal is symmetric and nonatomic, so the paired sum is $V>2\delta V$; the binomial expansion gives $(1+1/p)^p>2$ for $p\ge2$. For $u\ne0$, at $t$ and $-t$ the four equatorial thresholds are $h-vt,-h-vt,h+vt,-h+vt$. Pair the opposites. In each simplex slice of radius $1-t$ for $0<t<1$, the two pairs together have volume at least $4\delta V_p(1-t)^p$ by 6a7d56e450b3126f. Integrating gives the assertion. At $h=0$ it gives a centroid lower bound. Each canonical cylinder has exactly $\delta V$ by the vertex cap formula on every simplex slice, and the normals sum to zero, so these cylinders cover all space and give the global upper bound.

If $u\ne0$ is noncanonical, at the equator $t=0$ its centroid cap in $T_p$ strictly exceeds $\delta V_p$ by 53fe12b742a7608d. Slice volumes are continuous in $t$ near zero by scaling to the fixed simplex and using nullity of hyperplane levels. The strict gap in the paired sum therefore persists on an interval, and elsewhere the paired bound prohibits cancellation. For a canonical $u$ it suffices to scale to $u_i$. On a slice of radius $\rho>0$ the support of $u_i\cdot x$ is $[-\rho,p\rho]$, with one high vertex and all other vertices at $-\rho$. While both opposite thresholds $\pm s$ are proper, the sum is $\delta V_p[(\rho-s/p)^p+(\rho+s/p)^p]$, strictly above $2\delta V_p\rho^p$ if $s\ne0$. When $|s|\ge\rho$ a full slice alone exceeds that bound. A nonzero $v$ creates such a strict pair on every $t\in(0,1)$ at $h=0$. Likewise for $v=0$ and $h\ne0$ the canonical pairs are strictly above the bound on every positive-radius slice. This proves all equality statements.
