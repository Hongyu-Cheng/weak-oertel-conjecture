## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, $d\ge1$, and let $f:K\to\mathbb R$ be bounded, lower semicontinuous and convex. For its ordinary uniform mean $m$, the closed sublevel set satisfies $|\{x\in K:f(x)\le m\}|\ge\Gamma_d|K|$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. The same bound holds for the closed mean superlevel of a bounded upper semicontinuous concave function. The constant is sharp, with equality for $f(x)=\sum_i x_i$ on the standard $d$-simplex.

## Proof

Let $F(t)=|\{x\in K:f(x)\le t\}|/|K|$. The sublevels are compact and convex, and a convex combination of the sublevels at $t$ and $u$ lies in the sublevel at the corresponding combination of thresholds. Define $q(0)=\min_K f$ and $q(r)=\inf\{t:F(t)\ge r^d\}$ for $0<r\le1$. Boundedness gives finite quantiles. Right continuity of $F$ by continuity of measure from above ensures $F(q(r))\ge r^d$. Each required sublevel is nonempty, including the one at $q(0)$, whether or not it has positive volume. Applying Brunn–Minkowski to the sublevels at $q(r),q(s)$ shows that their threshold interpolation has probability at least $((1-\lambda)r+\lambda s)^d$ for $0\le\lambda\le1$. By the quantile definition this proves convexity of $q$ on $[0,1]$, with the all-zero interpolation immediate.

For a random $R$ with distribution $\Pr(R\le r)=r^d$ on $[0,1]$, the relation $q(r)\le t$ iff $r^d\le F(t)$ for $0<r\le1$ implies that $q(R)$ has distribution function $F$. In particular an atom of $f$ at its minimum appears as a constant initial interval of $q$. The mean of $R$ is $d/(d+1)$, so Jensen gives $m=\mathbb E q(R)\ge q(d/(d+1))$ and hence $F(m)\ge(d/(d+1))^d$. Apply this to the negative of the concave function for the superlevel assertion. On the standard simplex $F(t)=t^d$ for $0\le t\le1$ for the specified $f$, with mean $d/(d+1)$, proving sharpness.
