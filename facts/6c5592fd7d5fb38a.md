## Statement

For every positive integer \(m\), put \(k=16m^4\), \(r_{\max}=k/4\), \(u=1+1/m\), \(h=\log u\), and \(N=k+1\). Let \(\varepsilon_i=1\) for \(i\le k/2\) and \(\varepsilon_i=-1\) otherwise. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\left\{x\in[0,2]^k:\sum_i x_i\le k,\quad
 \sum_i\max(x_i-1,0)\le r_{\max}\right\},\\
D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^k
 =\left\{x\in\{0,1,2\}^k:\sum_i x_i\le k,
 \quad\#\{i:x_i=2\}\le r_{\max}\right\},\\
A&=D\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^N,\qquad
\beta_i=-\log 2-\varepsilon_i h,\\
w_x&=e^{-\beta\cdot x},\qquad Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x.
\end{aligned}
$$

For \(E\subseteq[k]\), let \(D_E=\{x\in D:\{i:x_i\ne1\}=E\}\), \(V_E=\sum_{x\in D_E}w_x\), and let \(\sigma_E^2\) be the variance of \(\beta\cdot X\) under the original conditional probabilities \(w_x/V_E\) on \(D_E\). All these classes are nonempty. Set

$$
C_m=\frac{2^k}{Z^2}
 \sum_{E\subseteq[k]}\frac{V_E^2}{\sqrt{1+\sigma_E^2}}.
$$

There exist absolute constants \(C>0\), \(0<\rho<1\), and \(m_0\) such that for \(m\ge m_0\),

$$
\frac{2^k}{Z^2}\sum_E V_E^2\le C,
\qquad
0\le C_m\le \frac{C}{h\sqrt{k}}+Ck\rho^{k/2}
\le\frac{C}{2m}+Ck\rho^{k/2}.
$$

In particular \(C_m\longrightarrow0\). Here the spatial dimension is \(k\), the embedded set is \(A\), and the canonical mixed set of the locked problem is not renamed.

## Proof

All unspecified constants below are positive absolute constants; their values may change between inequalities. Only sufficiently large \(m\) are needed. We use \(0<h\le1/4\), \(kh^4\le16\), and \(h\sqrt{k}\ge2m\). The first holds eventually, the second follows from \(\log(1+1/m)\le1/m\), and the third follows from \(\log(1+1/m)\ge1/(m+1)\ge1/(2m)\).

The set \(P\) is compact and convex, and its integer points are exactly the displayed \(D\). It has nonempty interior, for example around \((1/2,\ldots,1/2)\). Its defining constraints are invariant under every coordinate permutation. Appending the zero coordinate gives the stated \(A\), without changing any of the weight or parity quantities considered here.

For a ternary vector write \(Q(x)=\#\{i:x_i=2\}\), \(R_0(x)=\#\{i:x_i=0\}\), and \(T_{\mathrm{sum}}(x)=\sum_i x_i\). Then \(T_{\mathrm{sum}}=k+Q-R_0\), so the two original quota constraints are precisely \(Q\le r_{\max}\) and \(R_0\ge Q\).

We first establish two elementary estimates that will be used with uniformly bounded probabilities and activities.

For a three-category multinomial law with \(n\) trials and category probabilities at least a fixed \(a>0\), every integer type \((n_1,n_2,n_3)\) satisfying \(\sum n_j=n\) and \(|n_j-n\pi_j|\le B\sqrt n\) has probability at least \(c(a,B)/n\), for all sufficiently large \(n\). Here is an elementary proof. Each count has variance at most \(n\); Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound put probability at least \(2/3\) in the box \(|n_j-n\pi_j|\le3\sqrt n\). There are at most \(C n\) integer types in that box, since two counts determine the third. Some type there therefore has probability at least \(c/n\). Connect this type to the specified type by transferring one unit at a time from a surplus category to a deficit category. There are at most \(C(B)\sqrt n\) moves, and every count stays between its initial and final values. For a move from category \(j\) to category \(i\), the ratio of the new probability to the old is \(\pi_i n_j/(\pi_j(n_i+1))\). Along this path its logarithm has absolute value at most \(C(a,B)/\sqrt n\), because all counts are within \(C(B)\sqrt n\) of their means and all \(\pi_i\ge a\). Multiplying these ratios proves the assertion.

The second estimate concerns the distribution

$$
\mathbb P(B_+=b)\ \propto\
 \binom{L_+}{b}\binom{L_-}{q-b}t^b,
\qquad L_++L_-=L,
\tag{1}
$$

where \(L_+,L_-\ge L/8\), \(L/4\le q\le L/2\), and \(1\le t\le e\). For sufficiently large \(L\), uniformly over these parameters,
\(\operatorname{Var}(B_+)\ge cL\).
To prove this, denote its unnormalized weights by \(v_b\). Their successive ratio on the interior of the support is

$$
\frac{v_{b+1}}{v_b}
 =t\frac{(L_+-b)(q-b)}{(b+1)(L_--q+b+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$

Every mode \(b_0\) is an absolute positive fraction of \(L\) away from all four cell boundaries: each of
\(b_0,L_+-b_0,q-b_0,L_--q+b_0\) is at least \(\delta L\), with \(\delta=10^{-4}\), once \(L\) is sufficiently large. For explicit verification, if \(b\le\delta L\) or \(L_--q+b\le\delta L\), the ratio (2) is at least \(1/(256\delta)>1\). The product of its numerator factors is at least \(L^2/64\): in the first case use \((1/8-\delta)(1/4-\delta)>1/64\), and in the second use \((1/2-\delta)(1/8-\delta)>1/64\). Its denominator is at most \(4\delta L^2\). If \(L_+-b\le\delta L\), the ratio \(v_b/v_{b-1}\) is at most \(256e\delta<1\); its denominator is at least \(L^2/64\) and its numerator at most \(4e\delta L^2\). If \(q-b\le\delta L\), the corresponding bound is \(512e\delta<1\), with denominator at least \(L^2/128\). Thus none of these four boundary regions can contain a mode.

On \(|b-b_0|\le2\sqrt L\), all four cell counts are at least \(\delta L/2\). The change of the logarithm of (2) in one step is consequently at most \(C/L\) in absolute value. At a mode the ratios immediately before and after it bracket one; hence the logarithm of either adjacent ratio is at most \(C/L\) in absolute value. Summing the successive logarithms shows \(v_b\ge c v_{b_0}\) for \(|b-b_0|\le\sqrt L\). There are at least \(c\sqrt L\) such integers, so every probability in (1) is at most \(C/\sqrt L\). For any real center, an interval of radius \(s\sqrt L\) therefore has probability at most \((2s\sqrt L+2)C/\sqrt L\). Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(s>0\); this bound is at most \(1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(L\). Applying it at the mean gives \(\operatorname{Var}(B_+)\ge s^2L/2\), proving the estimate.

We next obtain a lower bound for the original \(Z\). Define base weights on a coordinate of sign \(\varepsilon\) by
\(b_\varepsilon(0)=1\), \(b_\varepsilon(1)=2e^{\varepsilon h}\), and \(b_\varepsilon(2)=e^{2\varepsilon h}\). Their product \(b(x)\) satisfies the exact identity \(w_x=4^{Q(x)}b(x)\). The unrestricted base partition function is

$$
G=\prod_i(1+e^{\varepsilon_i h})^2
 =(2+2\cosh h)^k.
\tag{3}
$$

Under the normalized base product law, a coordinate of sign \(+\) has the \(\operatorname{Binomial}(2,p)\) distribution, where \(p=e^h/(1+e^h)\); a coordinate of sign \(-\) has the \(\operatorname{Binomial}(2,1-p)\) distribution. Put \(n=k/2\). The expectations of the total two-count and zero-count are both
\(r_{\max}+\Delta\), where

$$
\Delta=\frac{k}{4}\tanh^2(h/2)
 \le\frac{kh^2}{16}\le\frac{\sqrt k}{4}.
\tag{4}
$$

Let \(\mu_{+,2},\mu_{-,2}\) be the two group means of the two-counts and \(\mu_{+,0},\mu_{-,0}\) the group means of the zero-counts. Choose the group \(+\) two-count in an integer interval of radius \(\sqrt k/100\) about \(\mu_{+,2}-\Delta/2\), and set the group \(-\) two-count equal to \(r_{\max}\) minus it. Independently choose each group zero-count in an interval of radius \(\sqrt k/100\) about \(\mu_{\pm,0}-\Delta/2+\sqrt k/2\). There are at least \(c k^{3/2}\) such triples of free integer choices. They give \(Q=r_{\max}\) and \(R_0\ge r_{\max}\), since the total zero-count is within \(2\sqrt k/100\) of \(r_{\max}+\sqrt k\). All six category counts differ from their respective base means by at most \(2\sqrt k\), including the one-counts determined by the group totals. They are valid nonnegative counts for sufficiently large \(k\), because all three coordinate probabilities have an absolute positive lower bound for \(h\le1/4\).

The multinomial estimate proved above gives probability at least \(c/n\) for each group type, and independence gives probability at least \(c/k^2\) for each type pair. Summing over the disjoint choices yields
\(\mathbb P_b(Q=r_{\max},R_0\ge r_{\max})\ge c/\sqrt k\).
These are original feasible points with factor \(4^{Q}=4^{r_{\max}}\). Therefore

$$
Z\ge\frac{c}{\sqrt k}\,4^{r_{\max}}G.
\tag{5}
$$

For the parity collision consider two unrestricted base vectors \(X,Y\), conditioned coordinate by coordinate to have equal parity, and normalize their product base weights. Denote this independent-coordinate pair law by \(\mathbb P_{\mathrm{pair}}\). Its partition function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\prod_i\left(1+6e^{2\varepsilon_i h}
                         +e^{4\varepsilon_i h}\right)
   =(6+2\cosh(2h))^k,\\
\frac{2^kH}{G^2}
 &=\left[\frac{2(1+\cosh^2h)}{(1+\cosh h)^2}\right]^k
   =\left[1+\tanh^4(h/2)\right]^k\le e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

The final inequality uses \(\tanh(h/2)\le h/2\) and \(kh^4\le16\). This is the exact quartic cancellation needed here.

Under this pair law the one-coordinate vector of two-indicators
\((\mathbf1_{X_i=2},\mathbf1_{Y_i=2})\) takes the four values \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\), with respective unnormalized probabilities
\(1+4e^{2\varepsilon_i h},e^{2\varepsilon_i h},e^{2\varepsilon_i h},e^{4\varepsilon_i h}\).
All four normalized probabilities exceed an absolute \(a>0\), uniformly for \(h\le1/4\). Consequently

$$
\sup_{v,w\in\mathbb Z}
 \mathbb P_{\mathrm{pair}}(Q(X)=v,Q(Y)=w)\le\frac{C}{k}.
\tag{7}
$$

For completeness, subtract mass \(a\) at each of the four values. This expresses each coordinate law as a mixture with weight \(4a\) of the uniform law on \(\{0,1\}^2\). Its characteristic function consequently has absolute value at most
\(1-4a+4a\cos(t_1/2)\cos(t_2/2)\) on \([-\pi,\pi]^2\). Both cosines are nonnegative there, and
\(1-\cos(t_1/2)\cos(t_2/2)\ge(t_1^2+t_2^2)/(4\pi^2)\), by \(xy\le(x+y)/2\) for \(x,y\in[0,1]\) and the elementary bound \(1-\cos s\ge2s^2/\pi^2\) for \(|s|\le\pi/2\). Thus the product characteristic function is bounded by \(e^{-ck(t_1^2+t_2^2)}\). Fourier inversion, obtained by integrating the finite integer-valued characteristic-function sum term by term, bounds each atom by the integral of this function divided by \((2\pi)^2\), which is at most \(C/k\). This proves (7).

The exact equality \(\sum_E V_E^2=\sum_{x,y\in D,\ x\equiv y\ (2)}w_xw_y\) counts ordered endpoint pairs. Dropping the two original sum constraints, but retaining the two-count caps, and then using (7), gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_E V_E^2
&\le H\sum_{v,w=0}^{r_{\max}}4^{v+w}
   \mathbb P_{\mathrm{pair}}(Q(X)=v,Q(Y)=w)\\
&\le\frac{C}{k}\,4^{2r_{\max}}H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

Together, (5), (6), and (8) prove the claimed bounded scaled collision.

Call a parity class good if its numbers \(L_+,L_-\) of coordinates in the two field groups both satisfy \(L_+,L_-\ge k/8\). Under \(\mathbb P_{\mathrm{pair}}\), the common even-parity set \(E\) has independent membership indicators with probability
\(\theta=\cosh^2h/(1+\cosh^2h)\ge1/2\). This probability is the same in both field groups. Thus each group count has the \(\operatorname{Binomial}(k/2,\theta)\) distribution. With
\(\rho=2^{1/4}(3/4)<1\), the exponential Markov inequality gives, for either group count \(B\),
\(\mathbb P(B<k/8)\le2^{k/8}\mathbb E2^{-B}\le\rho^{k/2}\).
For original feasible pairs \(4^{Q(X)+Q(Y)}\le4^{2r_{\max}}\), so

$$
\sum_{E\text{ bad}}V_E^2
 \le2\,4^{2r_{\max}}H\rho^{k/2},
\qquad
\frac{2^k}{Z^2}\sum_{E\text{ bad}}V_E^2
 \le Ck\rho^{k/2}.
\tag{9}
$$

It remains to lower-bound the original conditional energy variance in a good class. Put \(L=L_++L_-\), \(d_E=L_+-L_-\), and \(q=\min(r_{\max},\lfloor L/2\rfloor)\). A point of \(D_E\) fixes all coordinates off \(E\) at one and selects a subset of \(E\), of size at most \(q\), to have value two. The rest have value zero. If that subset has \(a\) elements in the plus group and \(b\) in the minus group, its original weight is exactly

$$
2^{k-L}u^{-d_E}\,4^{a+b}u^{2(a-b)}.
\tag{10}
$$

In particular this is the original conditional law, without a change of its activities or a replacement energy. To see that its top size \(Q=q\) has probability at least \(1/2\), let \(c_j\) be the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(j\) in the \(L\) activities \(4e^{2\varepsilon_i h}\), \(i\in E\). Counting extensions of each \((j-1)\)-subset gives
\(j c_j\ge(L-j+1)(4e^{-2h})c_{j-1}\).
Since \(j\le q\le L/2\) and \(4e^{-2h}>2\), we have \(c_{j-1}/c_j<1/2\). Summing the resulting geometric bound gives \(\sum_{j=0}^q c_j\le2c_q\), as asserted.

Conditional on \(Q=q\), the plus group two-count \(B_+\) has exactly the law (1) with \(t=e^{4h}\in[1,e]\). Goodness implies \(L\ge k/4\), \(L_+,L_-\ge L/8\), and \(L/4\le q\le L/2\). The second elementary estimate therefore gives \(\operatorname{Var}(B_+\mid Q=q)\ge cL\).

The full original energy on this conditional slice is

$$
\beta\cdot X
 =-(\log2)(k-L+2q)-h(4B_+-2q-d_E).
\tag{11}
$$

The common-count term in (11) is retained; it becomes constant only after conditioning on the original top count. The conditional-variance identity now yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_E^2
&\ge\mathbb P(Q=q\mid D_E)
      \operatorname{Var}(\beta\cdot X\mid D_E,Q=q)\\
&\ge\tfrac12\,16h^2
      \operatorname{Var}(B_+\mid Q=q)
 \ge c h^2L\ge c h^2k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

Finally split the defining sum for \(C_m\) into good and bad classes. Apply (12) and the bounded scaled collision to the good part, and use the denominator bound \(\sqrt{1+\sigma_E^2}\ge1\) with (9) for the bad part. This gives
\(C_m\le C/(h\sqrt k)+Ck\rho^{k/2}\), exactly as claimed. Since \(h\sqrt k\ge2m\) and \(0<\rho<1\), both terms tend to zero. Every weight, normalization, parity class, and conditional variance in this calculation is the one in the statement.
