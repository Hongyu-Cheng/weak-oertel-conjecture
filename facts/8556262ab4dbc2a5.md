## Statement

For $L\ge1$, let $C_L$ be the convex hull of the intervals $[L256^{k(z)},L256^{k(z)}+1]$ placed at the binary bases $z\in\{0,1\}^4$, where $k(z)=z_1+2z_2+4z_3+8z_4$. These are the exact integer fibers and the total mixed length is $16$. The integer-fiber difference body is $[-1,1]$. Every two-cap closed cover of the mixed trace, with continuous coefficients $|u_i|=1$, has total hinge $\sum_i\int (a_i\cdot z+u_i x-b_i)_+\,d\mu\ge L/120$. There is a two-cap cover with each cap of mass $8$, and the minimum hinge at this budget is attained.

The same-real-base difference body is $[-W_L,W_L]$, with $W_L\ge1+(L/2)255^2\cdot257$. A closed cover by $32$ caps with integer-fiber normalization has each cap of mass $1/2$ and total hinge $4$. Rescaling its margins to the same-real-base normalization gives hinge divided by total mass $1/(4W_L)\to0$.

## Proof

The projection is the binary cube and every integer point of it is an extreme vertex. A convex combination projecting to such a vertex uses only that base, so all sixteen fibers are the indicated unit intervals. The integer-fiber difference body is therefore $[-1,1]$. Two opposite horizontal closed cuts at a threshold strictly between the upper endpoint of code $7$ and the lower endpoint of code $8$ each contain eight full fibers and give the required cover. Apply dd9bb9379a876e89 at cap count two and $\tau=1/2$ for attainment, since each interval is full dimensional in its continuous line and the feasible class is nonempty.

Assign every interval midpoint to one of the two covering caps. Some cap receives at least eight, from which choose six. The six columns $(1,z)$ in $\mathbb R^5$ are dependent. A minimal dependent subfamily of rank $r\le5$ has $r+1$ columns. Select $r$ independent rows of that submatrix and take its signed maximal minors. They give a nonzero integer relation $(c_z)$ on the six columns after extending by zero, with $|c_z|\le r!\le120$ by the determinant expansion of a zero-one matrix. Thus $\sum c_z=\sum c_z z=0$. If $K$ is the largest code with nonzero coefficient, then
$$
\left|\sum_z c_z256^{k(z)}\right|
\ge256^K-120\sum_{j<K}256^j>0.
$$
This integer has absolute value at least one. At the chosen midpoints the margins $m_z=a\cdot z+u(L256^{k(z)}+1/2)-b$ are nonnegative. Since $|u|=1$, affine cancellation gives $|\sum c_zm_z|\ge L$. Jensen's inequality on each unit interval gives hinge at least $m_z$ for that cap. Summing the disjoint interval contributions yields $L\le120\sum m_z\le120\sum_i\int(L_i)_+\,d\mu$, where $L_i$ denotes the margin of cap $i$.

At the real base $(1/2,1/2,0,0)$ compare the midpoint of the upper endpoints at codes $0,3$ with the midpoint of the lower endpoints at codes $1,2$. Their positive difference is $1+(L/2)(256^3-256^2-256+1)=1+(L/2)255^2\cdot257$. The same-real-base difference body is a compact symmetric interval containing this difference. Finally, over each binary vertex use the two opposite half-interval caps through its midpoint. Each is extended by subtracting a sufficiently large multiple of the nonnegative affine binary distance from that vertex, leaving its own trace unchanged and excluding all other fibers. Each cap has mass $1/2$ and hinge $1/8$, so the total hinge is $4$. Dividing every margin by $W_L$ preserves the traces and gives the stated second normalization.
