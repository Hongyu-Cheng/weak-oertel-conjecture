## Statement

Let $T$ be a nondegenerate tetrahedron in $\mathbb R^3$, and let a prescribed axis be parallel to an edge of $T$. Either one-sided shake in this axis, obtained by translating each chord to share its lower or upper height, is the image of $T$ under an invertible affine shear and translation along the axis. In particular it preserves ordinary halfspace depth normalized by volume at corresponding points and preserves its maximum.

## Proof

Choose a height coordinate along the edge and project onto a complementary plane. The two endpoints of the chosen edge project to one point $a$. The other two vertices project to $b,c$, and $a,b,c$ are affinely independent because the tetrahedron is nondegenerate. For a projected point with barycentric coordinates $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ in that triangle, the coefficients at the two edge endpoints in any lifted point must sum to $\alpha$, while the coefficients at the other two vertices are $\beta,\gamma$. Thus the chord endpoints are the affine interpolations of the three projected-vertex heights, using the lower or upper of the edge heights at $a$ and the unique heights at $b,c$. In particular both endpoint heights are affine functions of the projected point. Subtracting the lower or upper affine height function from the continuous height is an invertible affine map and exactly realizes the corresponding shake, including on the projection boundary. An invertible affine map bijects the containing closed halfspaces and scales body and cap volumes by the same determinant factor, proving the depth identities.
