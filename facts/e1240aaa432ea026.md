## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For a finite probability law \(v\), define \(S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(v)=-\sum_xv_x\log v_x\), with zero summands interpreted by continuity. This is exactly the Shannon entropy denoted \(S(v)\) in accepted facts `464815b935df9890` and `543c0d1e38bd08bf`; it is not the locked mixed set \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). Conditional entropies \(\operatorname{Ent}(\cdot\mid\cdot)\) have the same Shannon normalization. The locked convex body \(C\) is not used to name a constant or an entropy quantity below.

For each integer \(k\ge32\), take exactly the support and original weights of accepted fact `001a95dc6616c6b9`, also used in `543c0d1e38bd08bf`. Its dimension \(n\) is renamed \(N=k^2\). Put \(t=k^{-1/4}\), \(M=2^k\), \(L=2M-1\), \(\theta=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^k\), and let \(\beta\in\mathbb R^N\) consist of \(k\) copies of \(\theta\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_k&=\{0,1\}^k\cup\{0,-1\}^k,
&K_k&=\operatorname{conv}(\Omega_k),\\
P&=\mathbf1_N+K_k^k,
&D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^N=\mathbf1_N+\Omega_k^k,\\
w_{\mathbf I}&=e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
&W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I},
&p_{\mathbf I}&=w_{\mathbf I}/W.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(P\) is \(P_k\) in `001a95dc6616c6b9`, \(K_k^k\) is a Cartesian product, and \(W\) is the original total weight, not a replacement normalization. Extend \(p\) by zero off \(D\). Define the actual depth and its original-root moments by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf J)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf J\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf I},\\
\mathcal M_m(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}p_{\mathbf J}\tau_p(\mathbf J)^m
\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\tau_p=\tau_w/W\), with \(\tau_w\) as in `001a95dc6616c6b9`. The legacy moment \(M_m(p)\) is \(\mathcal M_m(p)\); in particular \(M_3(p)\) in `543c0d1e38bd08bf` is exactly \(\mathcal M_3(p)\). No entropy-capacity proxy or maximum-entropy source row is substituted for \(\tau_p\).

Use precisely the feasible arrays of `464815b935df9890`:
\[
\mathcal F(p)=\left\{r\ge0:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I},\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
\quad(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\in D)\right\}.
\]
For \(r\in\mathcal F(p)\), write \(q_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), and let \((\mathbf J,\mathbf X)\) have joint law \(r\), with root marginal \(q\) and original source marginal \(p\). Keep the predecessor's definitions
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(r)&=-\sum_{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
=\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J\mid\mathbf X),\\
I(r)&=\sum_{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{q_{\mathbf J}p_{\mathbf I}},\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(v\|z)&=\sum_{v_x>0}v_x\log(v_x/z_x),
\end{aligned}
\]
where relative entropy is used only when \(z_x>0\) on the positive support of \(v\). Zero root rows have no conditional-entropy contribution.

Let \(\pi_c=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D,\ \mathbf I\equiv c\pmod2}p_{\mathbf I}\). Write \(r_{\mathrm{can}}\) and \(q_{\mathrm{can}}\) for the explicit array and root law denoted \(r,q\) in `464815b935df9890` and `543c0d1e38bd08bf`:
\[
(r_{\mathrm{can}})_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=\frac{p_{\mathbf I}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}{\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}},
\qquad
(q_{\mathrm{can}})_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}(r_{\mathrm{can}})_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}.
\]
Put \(\delta=N\log2-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi)\). For every feasible \(r\), the following identities hold:
\[
\begin{aligned}
I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)
&=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf J\mid\mathbf X),\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r_{\mathrm{can}})
&=H(r_{\mathrm{can}})-H(r)\\
&=I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi).
\end{aligned}
\]
The joint relative entropy in this display is finite. Moreover \(0\le\delta\le5/2\), and every \(r\in\mathcal F(p)\) satisfying \(I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)\le N\log2\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_q\log\frac1{\tau_p(\mathbf J)}-I(r)
\ge
\left(1-\frac{104}{k}\right)_+
\left[\frac14\log k-\log(6\sqrt8)\right]_+-8,
\tag{1}
\]
where \([x]_+=\max\{x,0\}\). The right side tends to \(+\infty\). The budget-feasible set is nonempty for every \(k\ge32\), since it contains \(r_{\mathrm{can}}\). Consequently no choice of feasible couplings, even depending arbitrarily on \(k\), can satisfy both that budget and a finite absolute upper bound on the gap.

The conditional source entropy instead gives \(I(r)=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X\mid\mathbf J)\), not the budget identity. In particular the product of the blockwise blends of the diagonal and canonical arrays, with canonical fraction \(8/k\), has \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)>0\), so the proposed replacement of the budget by this conditional-source formula fails even on those explicit feasible blends.

In contrast, the same original laws satisfy \(\mathcal M_2(p)\ge4^{-N}\) by the arbitrary-tilt antipodal-product theorem in accepted fact `290dcf5cce104a97`, and satisfy \(\mathcal M_3(p)\ge2^{-3N}\). Thus (1) does not refute their actual third-moment bound.

## Proof

The support completeness and full-dimensionality are supplied by `001a95dc6616c6b9`. The zero extension of \(e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf y}\) from the convex polytope \(P\) is a finite-valued globally log-concave extension of the original weight table. Thus the construction in `464815b935df9890` applies. Alternatively, its stated weaker midpoint hypotheses hold because equal-parity endpoints have an integral midpoint in the complete convex support and log-affinity gives equality of midpoint weight and geometric mean.

The exact coordinate change is \(\mathbf x=\mathbf I-\mathbf1_N\) for a source and \(\mathbf j=\mathbf J-\mathbf1_N\) for a root. Since \(\beta\cdot\mathbf1_N=0\), the translated weight is exactly \(e^{-\beta\cdot\mathbf x}\), without even a common multiplicative factor. Translation preserves containing closed halfspaces, sends reflected endpoints to reflected endpoints, permutes parity classes, and preserves the row barycenter equations. Below \(x_b,j_b\in\Omega_k\) denote centered source and root blocks. Every array entry in centered coordinates means the entry at its original translated indices, not a new law. The original source law factors into \(k\) copies of
\[
p^0_x=\frac{e^{-\theta\cdot x}}{W_0},
\qquad
u=\cosh t,
\qquad
W_0=M(1+u)-1,
\qquad
W=W_0^k.
\]
The symbol \(u\) in this display is the scalar \(\cosh t\), as in `001a95dc6616c6b9` and `543c0d1e38bd08bf`.

For completeness, the \(M\) centered block parity classes are the singleton \(\{0\}\) and the pairs \(\{\mathbf1_E,-\mathbf1_E\}\) for nonempty \(E\subseteq[k]\). Among all subsets \(E\), \(M/2\) have both or neither of the first two coordinates, \(M/4\) have just the first, and \(M/4\) have just the second. Their values \(\theta\cdot\mathbf1_E\) are \(0,t,-t\), respectively; the first count includes the empty subset. This gives the displayed \(W_0\). Product parity classes and their probabilities factor, so substitution in the canonical-array formula shows that \(r_{\mathrm{can}}\) is the product of its centered block arrays. Fact `464815b935df9890` gives its source marginal, feasibility, full root support, and \(H(r_{\mathrm{can}})=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi)\).

We first check the two entropy orientations for any feasible array. Its row equations and source marginal give \(\mathbb E_q\mathbf J=\mathbb E_p\mathbf X\). The original formula \(\log p_{\mathbf I}=-\beta\cdot\mathbf I-\log W\) is affine. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_q\log p_{\mathbf J}
&=\mathbb E_p\log p_{\mathbf X}=-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p),\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)&=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(q),\\
I(r)&=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(q)-H(r)
=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X\mid\mathbf J),\\
I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)&=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(p)-H(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The middle entropy equality follows by expanding the joint Shannon entropy in both orders. These are identities of finite sums, including arrays with zero entries or zero rows.

We next identify the common joint support and prove the new joint-entropy identity. Every nonzero point of \(\Omega_k\) is an exposed vertex, as established in `001a95dc6616c6b9`: for \(\mathbf1_E\), the functional having coefficient \(2k\) on \(E\) and \(-1\) off \(E\) has that unique maximizer; its negative exposes \(-\mathbf1_E\). If a feasible row has a nonzero centered root block \(j_b\), its conditional source block has mean \(j_b\). Taking the expectation of an exposing functional forces its source block to equal \(j_b\) at every positive entry of that row. A zero row makes no assertion and has no positive entries. Hence all feasible positive entries lie in the set of pairs satisfying \(j_b\in\{0,x_b\}\) in every block. In particular \(x_b=0\) forces \(j_b=0\).

For a nonzero centered block source \(x\), put \(s_x=\theta\cdot x\). Directly from the two-point parity class, the canonical conditional assignment probabilities are
\[
\Pr_{\mathrm{can}}(j_b=0\mid x_b=x)=\frac{e^{s_x}}{2\cosh s_x},
\qquad
\Pr_{\mathrm{can}}(j_b=x\mid x_b=x)=\frac{e^{-s_x}}{2\cosh s_x}.
\tag{3}
\]
At source zero the root is zero deterministically. All displayed probabilities are positive. Taking their products proves that \(r_{\mathrm{can}}\) is positive on every pair allowed above, and thus on the positive support of every feasible \(r\). On this allowed set, its log conditional assignment probability is
\[
\log\frac{(r_{\mathrm{can}})_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
=\sum_{b:x_b\ne0}\bigl(-\theta\cdot x_b-\log(2\cosh(\theta\cdot x_b))\bigr)
+2\beta\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j).
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, a nonzero block with \(j_b=x_b\) gives the second probability in (3), whereas \(j_b=0\) adds \(2s_{x_b}\) and gives the first; a zero source block contributes zero.

The first sum in (4) depends only on the source, whose marginal is always \(p\). The expectation of the last term is zero by feasibility. Thus the expectation of (4) under \(r\) equals its expectation under \(r_{\mathrm{can}}\), namely \(-H(r_{\mathrm{can}})\). Expanding relative entropy gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r_{\mathrm{can}})
&=\sum_{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\left(\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
-\log\frac{(r_{\mathrm{can}})_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}\right)\\
&=H(r_{\mathrm{can}})-H(r)
=I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)-S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
This also proves that the canonical array maximizes conditional assignment entropy among all feasible arrays, not just product arrays. It does not assert maximal conditional source entropy at each root.

We use only elementary relative-entropy inequalities. For finite probability laws \(\mathbb P_1,\mathbb P_0\), with \(\mathbb P_0>0\) wherever \(\mathbb P_1>0\), the inequality \(-\log z\ge1-z\) yields
\(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb P_1\|\mathbb P_0)\ge1-\sum_{x:(\mathbb P_1)_x>0}(\mathbb P_0)_x\ge0\).
Equality holds only when the two laws agree, by the equality condition of the logarithm inequality and the total masses. This applies to the common support just proved. Combining (5) with the information budget gives
\[
0\le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r_{\mathrm{can}})\le\delta.
\tag{6}
\]
Also (2) and the accepted value of \(H(r_{\mathrm{can}})\) give \(I(r_{\mathrm{can}})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_{\mathrm{can}}\|p)=S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi)\le N\log2\). The last inequality follows from nonnegative relative entropy of \(\pi\) against the uniform law on all \(2^N\) parity classes. All those classes are present here. This proves nonemptiness of the budget-feasible set and \(\delta\ge0\).

To verify the numerical upper bound on \(\delta\), let \(\pi^0\) be the block parity law and let \(U_M\) be uniform on its \(M\) classes. Expanding the logarithm of the product parity probabilities gives \(S_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi)=kS_{\mathrm{Sh}}(\pi^0)\). The block masses are \(1/W_0\) and \(2\cosh(\theta\cdot\mathbf1_E)/W_0\). The preceding counts give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_c(\pi_c^0)^2&=\frac{M(3+\cosh(2t))-3}{W_0^2},\\
\delta&=kD_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi^0\|U_M)\\
&\le k\left(\frac{M\bigl(M(3+\cosh(2t))-3\bigr)}{W_0^2}-1\right)
\le k\left(t^4+\frac3M\right)\le\frac52.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For the first inequality, multiply \(\log z\le z-1\) by the probabilities \(\pi_c^0\). For the second, use the exact expansion
\[
M\bigl(M(3+\cosh(2t))-3\bigr)-W_0^2
=M^2(u-1)^2+M(2u-1)-1.
\]
Here \(W_0\ge M\), \(u<2\), and \(u-1\le t^2\) for \(0<t\le1\). The latter two elementary estimates are also proved in `001a95dc6616c6b9`: the power series gives \(\cosh1<2\), and termwise \(\cosh t-1\le t^2(\cosh1-1)\le t^2\). Finally \(kt^4=1\) and \(M=2^k\ge2k\), the latter by induction from \(k=2\). This verifies (7) also at \(k=32\).

We now bound conditional source entropy for every budget-feasible array, without assuming its rows or its root law factor. The canonical block zero-root entries are
\[
(r^0_{\mathrm{can}})_{0,0}=\frac1{W_0},
\qquad
(r^0_{\mathrm{can}})_{0,\pm\mathbf1_E}=\frac1{2W_0\cosh s_E},
\qquad
s_E=\theta\cdot\mathbf1_E.
\]
Consequently its zero-root probability and conditional source law are
\[
a=\frac{M(1+u^{-1})}{2W_0},
\qquad
\rho_x=\frac{v_x}{\sum_{y\in\Omega_k}v_y},
\qquad
v_0=2,\quad v_{\pm\mathbf1_E}=\frac1{\cosh s_E}.
\tag{8}
\]
These are precisely the block quantities \(a,\rho\) in `543c0d1e38bd08bf`, derived here from the original array. Since \(W_0<M(1+u)\) and \(u<2\), \(a>1/(2u)>1/4\). Every nonzero block root has positive canonical mass, so also \(a<1\).

For an original root \(\mathbf J\), let \(Z(\mathbf J)\) be the number of zero blocks in \(\mathbf J-\mathbf1_N\). This is the zero-block count denoted \(Z\) in `543c0d1e38bd08bf`, not the energy sum called \(Z\) in the proof of `001a95dc6616c6b9`. Under \(q_{\mathrm{can}}\), it has distribution \(\operatorname{Binomial}(k,a)\). The canonical conditional source row at \(\mathbf J\), denoted here by \(\mu^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbf J}\), is the product of one copy of \(\rho\) at each zero block and a point mass at each nonzero block. This notation refers only to the canonical row, not to a maximum-entropy source row.

Let \(U_L\) be uniform on the \(L\) block sites, and define
\(\chi^2(\rho\|U_L)=L\sum_x\rho_x^2-1\).
From (8), \(v_x\ge1/u\). Its noncentral values are (1) or \(1/u\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2(\rho\|U_L)
&=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{U_L}(v)}{(\mathbb E_{U_L}v)^2}
\le u^2\mathbb E_{U_L}(v-1)^2\\
&\le\frac{u^2}{L}+(u-1)^2
\le\frac4L+t^4,
\qquad
k\chi^2(\rho\|U_L)\le3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The central site contributes \(1/L\) to the squared deviation before multiplication by \(u^2\), and all other contributions are at most \((u-1)^2/u^2\). The last bound uses \(L=2^{k+1}-1\ge2k\) and \(kt^4=1\).

On the common allowed joint support define
\(\Psi(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)=\sum_{b:j_b=0}\log(L\rho_{x_b})\).
Independence of the canonical blocks, but no independence assumption on \(r\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{r_{\mathrm{can}}}e^\Psi
&=\left(1-a+aL\sum_x\rho_x^2\right)^k\\
&=\left(1+a\chi^2(\rho\|U_L)\right)^k\le e^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
To justify the comparison with arbitrary \(r\), for finite laws \(\mathbb P_1,\mathbb P_0\), where \(\mathbb P_1\) is supported on the positive support of \(\mathbb P_0\), and a real function \(F\), normalize \((\mathbb P_0)_xe^{F(x)}\) to a probability law \(\mathbb P_0^F\). Expansion and nonnegativity of \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb P_1\|\mathbb P_0^F)\) give
\(\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_1} F\le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbb P_1\|\mathbb P_0)+\log\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_0}e^F\).
Using (6) and (10), we obtain \(\mathbb E_r\Psi\le\delta+3\).

For each positive \(q_{\mathbf J}\), define the arbitrary conditional source row \(\mu^r_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/q_{\mathbf J}\), and put
\(D_{\mathrm{row}}=\sum_{\mathbf J:q_{\mathbf J}>0}q_{\mathbf J}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu^r_{\mathbf J}\|\mu^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbf J})\).
Canonical root masses are all positive, and the common-support result makes all these relative entropies finite. Factoring positive joint entries into root mass and conditional source mass gives the exact chain rule
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r_{\mathrm{can}})
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|q_{\mathrm{can}})+D_{\mathrm{row}}.
\tag{11}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative, so each is at most \(\delta\). On allowed entries the product row has
\(\log\mu^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=-Z(\mathbf J)\log L+\Psi(\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\).
Expansion of \(D_{\mathrm{row}}\), followed by (7), proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X\mid\mathbf J)
&=\mathbb E_qZ\log L-\mathbb E_r\Psi-D_{\mathrm{row}}\\
&\ge\mathbb E_qZ\log L-2\delta-3
\ge\mathbb E_qZ\log L-8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
No conditional row was assigned to a zero \(q\)-row.

We use the exact original closed-halfspace depths, not row depths. For \(s\ge1\) put
\(T_s=\sum_{\mathbf y\in\Omega_k^s}e^{-|\beta_s\cdot\mathbf y|}\), where \(\beta_s\) consists of \(s\) copies of \(\theta\), and put \(T_0=1\). Accepted fact `001a95dc6616c6b9` proves
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf J)=p_{\mathbf J}\frac{T_{Z(\mathbf J)}+1}{2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Here is also the closed-cap verification relevant to this use. Freeze the nonzero blocks of the centered root and vary its zero blocks over \(\Omega_k^s\). Every containing closed halfspace contains the center and at least one endpoint of every nonzero antipodal pair in this subset. The sum of the lighter endpoint weights is \(w_{\mathbf J}(T_s-1)/2\), proving the lower bound \(w_{\mathbf J}(T_s+1)/2\). For attainment when \(s>0\), choose \(\gamma_i=2^{i-1}\) on the \(ks\) varying coordinates. It has nonzero scalar product with every nonzero vector in this ternary support. A sufficiently small positive perturbation \(b=\beta_s+\varepsilon\gamma\) preserves all nonzero energy signs and breaks every other antipodal tie. The closed condition \(b\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\) selects exactly the lighter endpoint of each unequal-weight pair, one endpoint of each equal-weight nonzero pair, and the origin. Its varying-block mass is \((T_s+1)/2\).

For each nonzero root block choose the integer exposing functional described above; every other block site loses at least one. The sum of their exposing differences is zero exactly on the frozen-block subset and at most \(-1\) elsewhere. Adding \((1+\max_{\mathbf y\in\Omega_k^s}|b\cdot\mathbf y|)^{-1}b\cdot\mathbf y\) on the varying coordinates, and requiring the resulting affine functional to be nonnegative, excludes every support point outside that subset and realizes the preceding cap on it. This is one closed halfspace in the full original space after translation. If \(s=0\), the exposing sum alone selects exactly the root; if all blocks are zero, no exposing sum is needed. Thus all zero-energy ties and the central atom are included exactly as claimed in (13), with normalization by the same original \(W\).

We verify the finite-sum constant used for (13). For a block uniform on \(\Omega_k\), its energy divided by \(t\) is a random variable \(V\in\{-1,0,1\}\) with
\(\Pr(V=1)=\Pr(V=-1)=\lambda=M/(2L)\) and \(\Pr(V=0)=1-2\lambda\).
The counts are \(M/2\) sites at each of the two nonzero energies and \(M-1\) at zero. Hence \(T_s=L^s\mathbb E e^{-t|V_1+\cdots+V_s|}\) for independent copies. Since \(1/4\le\lambda\le1/3\), the characteristic function \(\varphi(\xi)=1-2\lambda(1-\cos\xi)\) satisfies, for \(|\xi|\le\pi\) and \(\zeta=1-\cos\xi\in[0,2]\),
\[
\varphi(\xi)^2=1-4\lambda\zeta(1-\lambda\zeta)\le1-\zeta/3,
\qquad
|\varphi(\xi)|\le e^{-\zeta/6}\le e^{-\xi^2/(3\pi^2)}.
\]
Here \(4\lambda(1-\lambda\zeta)\ge4(1/4)(1/3)=1/3\). The last inequality uses the concavity chord bound for sine, which gives \(1-\cos\xi\ge2\xi^2/\pi^2\). Fourier inversion here follows simply by integrating the finite expansion in integer powers of \(e^{i\xi}\). For \(s\ge1\), it gives for every integer \(h\)
\[
\Pr(V_1+\cdots+V_s=h)
\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\varphi(\xi)|^s\,d\xi
\le\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{2\sqrt{s}}<\frac2{\sqrt{s}}.
\]
The Gaussian integral is obtained by scaling \(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-x^2}\,dx=\sqrt\pi\); the latter identity follows by squaring and integrating in polar coordinates. Since \(e^t-1\ge t\) and \(t\le1\),
\(\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}e^{-t|h|}=1+2/(e^t-1)\le3/t\).
Therefore \(T_s\le L^s\min\{1,6/(t\sqrt{s})\}\). Every \(T_s\ge1\), so \(T_s+1\le2T_s\). Define \(f(0)=0\) and \(f(s)=[\log(t\sqrt{s}/6)]_+\) for integer \(s\ge1\), exactly as in `543c0d1e38bd08bf`. We have proved, including \(s=0\),
\[
\log(T_s+1)\le s\log L+\log2-f(s).
\tag{14}
\]
The absolute value in \(T_s\) is taken after summing all block energies, so no product of individually minimized caps has replaced the actual depth.

Combining the affine-mean identity (2), the original depth (13), and the entropy estimate (12), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_q\log\frac1{\tau_p(\mathbf J)}-I(r)
&=\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbf X\mid\mathbf J)
-\mathbb E_q\log(T_Z+1)+\log2\\
&\ge\mathbb E_qf(Z)-8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
This holds for the complete arbitrary root law \(q\).

It remains to show that budget-feasible \(q\) cannot avoid large \(Z\). Put \(B=\{\mathbf J:Z(\mathbf J)<k/8\}\). Under the canonical root law \(Z\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,a)\), with \(1/4<a<1\). Applying Markov's inequality to \(2^{-Z}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_{\mathrm{can}}(B)
&\le2^{k/8}(1-a/2)^k\\
&\le\exp\left(\frac{k\log2}{8}-\frac{k}{8}\right)
\le e^{-k/32}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Here \(1-z\le e^{-z}\), and \(\log2\le3/4\): integrate the chord upper bound for the convex function \(1/x\) on \([1,2]\). Both \(B\) and its complement have positive canonical mass, since \(Z=0\) and \(Z=k\) each have positive mass.

Write \(\alpha=q(B)\) and \(b_0=q_{\mathrm{can}}(B)\in(0,1)\). Splitting relative entropy over \(B\) and its complement gives its binary relative entropy plus nonnegative conditional relative entropies. This remains valid when \(\alpha=0\) or \(\alpha=1\), by omitting zero-mass conditional laws. The binary entropy is at most \(\log2\), as follows from its nonnegative relative entropy against the fair binary law. Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|q_{\mathrm{can}})
&\ge \alpha\log\frac1{b_0}-\log2
\ge\frac{k}{32}q(B)-\log2,\\
q(B)&\le\frac{32(\delta+\log2)}{k}\le\frac{104}{k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
