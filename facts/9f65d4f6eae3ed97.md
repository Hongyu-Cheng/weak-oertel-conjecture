## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ be an integer, $p=d/(d-1)$, and $U,V$ nonnegative random variables on a probability space with $U+V\le1$ almost surely. Let $a=\inf_{t>0:\Pr(U\le t)>0}t/\Pr(U\le t)^{1/d}$ and define $b$ with $V$ in place of $U$. Then $a^p+b^p\le1$, including laws with atoms. Consequently, for any integer $d\ge1$ and any nonnegative $(d+1)$-square column-stochastic matrix $X$ with at most two nonzero rows, its row capacities $\phi_d(X_i)=\inf_{t>0:\Pr(X_i\cdot\Xi\le t)>0}t/\Pr(X_i\cdot\Xi\le t)^{1/d}$ for uniform barycentric $\Xi$ belong to $D_d$, the downward set of off-diagonal row geometric means of zero-diagonal column-stochastic matrices of the same size.

## Proof

Testing $t=1$ gives $a,b\le1$, so either zero capacity settles the inequality. Otherwise the defining infima give $\Pr(U\le t)\le(t/a)^d$ and $\Pr(V\le s)\le(s/b)^d$ for all positive thresholds, including when the probabilities are zero. For each $0<t<1$, the two events $U\le t$ and $V\le1-t$ cover almost surely, so $1\le (t/a)^d+((1-t)/b)^d$. The strictly convex right hand side has minimum $(a^p+b^p)^{1-d}$ at $t=a^p/(a^p+b^p)$ by differentiation. This gives the inequality without an attainment or density hypothesis.

In the matrix case, permute row labels so the possible nonzero rows are $1,2$. At every simplex point their evaluations sum to one by column stochasticity, and every remaining capacity is zero. For $d\ge2$ choose $\theta\in[a^p,1-b^p]$. Set $Y_{12}=Y_{21}=1$ and $Y_{1j}=\theta$, $Y_{2j}=1-\theta$ in columns $j\ge3$ with other entries zero. This has zero diagonal and column sums one, and its off-diagonal means dominate $a,b$ because they are $\theta^{1/p},(1-\theta)^{1/p}$. In $d=1$ the two-cross-entry matrix has means $(1,1)$, which dominate the capacities as each is at most one.
