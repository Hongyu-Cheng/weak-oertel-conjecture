## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge8\), use exactly the specialization \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, with any symmetric saturated chain partition permitted by accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, put \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), \(b_\ell=\lfloor2^\ell/(8\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r\), \(Q=Q_k\), \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), \(n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\), \(N=n+1\), and \(\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}\). The parameter \(k\) is the height cutoff, \(Q\) is the tree depth, \(m\) is the branching number, and \(n\) is the spatial dimension. Retain the exact rooted-tree objects \(D_m,F_m,A_{m,Q},P_{m,Q},\widehat P,\widehat A\) from these predecessors, without changing their supports or weights. The entrance heights are \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). Thus
$$
\widehat A
=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace,
\qquad
w_{(z,t)}=2^{-t}.
$$
Weights vanish off \(\widehat A\). Let \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I}\) and \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\), exactly as in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba.

For \(0\le s\le Q\), define the spatial level and its full original height class by
$$
\mathcal L_s=\lbrace z\in A_{m,Q}:\operatorname{level}(z)=s\rbrace,
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal L}_s
=\lbrace(z,t):z\in\mathcal L_s,\quad
t\in\mathbb Z,\quad a_s\le t\le k\rbrace.
$$
Write
$$
K_s=|\mathcal L_s|=2^n\eta^s,
\qquad
R_s=\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t},
\qquad
W_s=K_sR_s,
\qquad
\alpha_s=p(\widehat{\mathcal L}_s)=\frac{W_s}{W}.
$$
Here \(R_s\) is a height tail, whereas \(H_j=\sum_{r=1}^{j}1/r\) continues to denote a harmonic number. Let \(q_s\) be the actual conditional law of \(p\) on \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_s\), extended by zero to the rest of \(\widehat A\), so that
$$
(q_s)_{(z,t)}=\frac{2^{-t}}{W_s}
\quad\bigl((z,t)\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_s\bigr).
$$
For any of these probability laws \(r\), define
$$
\tau_r(\mathbf I)
=\inf\lbrace r(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(r)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}r_{\mathbf I}\tau_r(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Thus all conditional depths use every closed halfspace in the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^N\).

For every \(k\ge8\) and every \(0\le s\le Q\),
$$
\frac1{16K_s^3}\le M_3(q_s)\le\frac1{K_s^3},
\qquad
\frac12\le8^N M_3(q_s)\le\frac{512}{27}.
$$
In particular, for the scalar transfer expression
$$
J_k=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\alpha_s^4M_3(q_s),
$$
one has
$$
\frac12\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\alpha_s^4
\le8^N J_k
\le\frac{512}{27}\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\alpha_s^4
\le\frac{512}{27}
\left(\frac{64}{3(H_k-H_7)}\right)^3
\longrightarrow0.
$$
Nevertheless the original law satisfies
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-22}8^{-N},
\qquad
\frac{J_k}{M_3(p)}\longrightarrow0.
$$
These assertions concern the unchanged original probability law and its exact spatial-level conditionals. In particular, they refute both a uniform positive lower bound for \(8^N J_k\) and a comparison \(J_k\ge cM_3(p)\) with an absolute \(c>0\).

## Proof

Accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 gives \(A_{m,Q}\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^n\), identifies level with the number of coordinates equal to two, and gives the exact level count \(K_s=2^n\eta^s\). Accepted fact 604889294a17d7df gives the displayed exact height columns and weights, together with
$$
\eta^s>\frac34\quad(0\le s\le Q),
\qquad
\frac W{2^N}\ge\frac3{64}(H_k-H_7).
$$
All classes are nonempty, all \(R_s\) are positive, and the classes partition \(\widehat A\). Consequently \(W=\sum_sW_s\), \(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\), and the formulas for \(q_s\) are the exact conditional normalizations.

We first expose each spatial point within its fixed level. Fix \(s\) and \(z\in\mathcal L_s\), and write \(S_x=\lbrace u:x_u=2\rbrace\) for every \(x\in\mathcal L_s\), where \(u\) runs through the canonical tree coordinates. Define the linear functional
$$
\ell_z(x)
=2\sum_{u\in S_z}x_u
+\sum_{u:z_u=1}x_u
-\sum_{u:z_u=0}x_u.
$$
We claim that \(\ell_z(x)<\ell_z(z)\) for every \(x\in\mathcal L_s\setminus\lbrace z\rbrace\). If \(S_x\ne S_z\), both sets have cardinality \(s\), so \(|S_z\setminus S_x|=|S_x\setminus S_z|\ge1\). The contribution of coordinates in \(S_z\) to \(\ell_z(x)-\ell_z(z)\) is at most \(-2|S_z\setminus S_x|\). Outside \(S_z\), a positive contribution is possible only when \(z_u=1\) and \(x_u=2\), and each such contribution equals one. There are at most \(|S_x\setminus S_z|\) such coordinates. Hence \(\ell_z(x)-\ell_z(z)\le-|S_z\setminus S_x|<0\). If \(S_x=S_z\), both points are Boolean on the remaining coordinates. Each disagreement there decreases \(\ell_z(x)-\ell_z(z)\) by one, and at least one disagreement occurs. This proves the claim, including the case \(s=0\).

The functional is nonzero, so
$$
G_z=\lbrace(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
\ell_z(x)\ge\ell_z(z)\rbrace
$$
is a proper closed halfspace of the original ambient space. Its intersection with \(\widehat{\mathcal L}_s\) is exactly the entire column \(\lbrace(z,t):a_s\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace\). Every sample point in that column belongs to \(G_z\), and
$$
q_s(G_z)=\frac{R_s}{W_s}=\frac1{K_s}.
$$
Other spatial levels possibly contained in \(G_z\) have zero \(q_s\)-mass. Therefore this is an admissible upper bound for the actual conditional depth with all ambient halfspaces allowed:
$$
\tau_{q_s}(z,t)\le\frac1{K_s}
\quad\bigl((z,t)\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_s\bigr),
\qquad
M_3(q_s)\le\frac1{K_s^3}.
$$

Conversely, every closed halfspace containing an atom contains that atom's probability mass. Thus \(\tau_{q_s}(\mathbf I)\ge(q_s)_{\mathbf I}\) at every atom of \(q_s\). Retaining just the original entrance-height atoms, one for each of the \(K_s\) spatial points, gives
$$
M_3(q_s)
\ge\sum_{z\in\mathcal L_s}(q_s)_{(z,a_s)}^4
=\frac{2^{-4a_s}}{K_s^3R_s^4}.
$$
The exact finite height tail satisfies
$$
2^{-a_s}\le R_s
=2^{1-a_s}\left(1-2^{-(k-a_s+1)}\right)
\le2^{1-a_s}.
$$
Consequently \(M_3(q_s)\ge1/(16K_s^3)\). This bound also covers \(a_s=k\), when the column has only its entrance atom.

Since \(N=n+1\),
$$
8^N K_s^{-3}=8\eta^{-3s}.
$$
Using \(3/4<\eta^s\le1\) in the two proved conditional bounds yields
$$
\frac12\le8^N M_3(q_s)
\le8\left(\frac43\right)^3=\frac{512}{27}.
$$
Multiplication by \(\alpha_s^4\) and summation establishes the two-sided comparison of \(8^N J_k\) with \(\sum_s\alpha_s^4\). In addition,
$$
W_s=2^n\eta^sR_s
\le2^n\,2^{1-a_s}\le2^N,
\qquad
\max_s\alpha_s\le\frac{2^N}{W}
\le\frac{64}{3(H_k-H_7)}.
$$
Because \(\sum_s\alpha_s=1\),
$$
\sum_s\alpha_s^4
\le(\max_s\alpha_s)^3\sum_s\alpha_s
\le\left(\frac{64}{3(H_k-H_7)}\right)^3.
$$
The harmonic numbers diverge, proving \(8^N J_k\to0\) with the claimed quantitative bound.

For clarity, the scalar transfer is a valid lower bound for the original moment. For \(\mathbf I\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_s\) and every original ambient closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf I\), one has \(p(H)\ge\alpha_sq_s(H)\). Taking infima over that same halfspace family gives \(\tau_p(\mathbf I)\ge\alpha_s\tau_{q_s}(\mathbf I)\). Summing its cube against the original sampling law yields
$$
M_3(p)
\ge\sum_s\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat{\mathcal L}_s}
\alpha_s(q_s)_{\mathbf I}
\bigl(\alpha_s\tau_{q_s}(\mathbf I)\bigr)^3
=J_k.
$$
What fails is a uniform lower bound of the required scale for this transfer expression, even with the true conditional moments. Accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba applies to exactly this untilted law, including all original heights and normalization, and gives \(M_3(p)\ge2^{-22}8^{-N}\). Therefore
$$
0\le\frac{J_k}{M_3(p)}\le2^{22}8^N J_k\longrightarrow0.
$$
No upper bound on the original depth or original moment is asserted, and this scalar-transfer obstruction supplies no disproof of the locked weak Oertel conjecture.
