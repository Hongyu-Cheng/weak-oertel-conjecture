## Statement

For each integer \(m\ge1\), let \(k=4m\), \(r=m\), \(N=k+1\), and

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\left\{\mathbf x\in[0,2]^k:
\sum_{i=1}^k x_i=k,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^k\max\{x_i-1,0\}\le m\right\},\\
D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^k
=\left\{I\in\{0,1,2\}^k:
\sum_i I_i=k,\quad \#\{i:I_i=2\}\le m\right\},\\
A&=D\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb R^N,\\
\beta_i&=\begin{cases}
-\log2,&1\le i\le2m,\\
\log2,&2m<i\le4m,
\end{cases}\\
w_I&=e^{-\beta\cdot I},\qquad
Z=\sum_{I\in D}w_I,\qquad p_I=w_I/Z.
\end{aligned}
$$

The support \(P,D,A\), spatial dimension \(k\), height zero, and cutoff \(m=r=r_{\max}\) are exactly those of accepted fact \(\texttt{cd14b2b69fa17e0f}\). The original law here is changed from its uniform law to the displayed log-affine law. For \(J\in D\), let \(\nu_J\) be the unique maximum-Shannon-entropy law on \(D\) with barycenter \(J\), and retain the original definitions

$$
R_1(J)=\left(\sum_{I\in D}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}\right)^{-1},
\qquad B_1=\sum_{J\in D}p_JR_1(J).
$$

Then

$$
B_1\le (m+1)^5(2m+1)^5\,2^{-4m}
\left(\frac{117}{125}\right)^{2m}.
\tag{1}
$$

Consequently \(2^kB_1\longrightarrow0\) along this family. There is no absolute \(c>0\) for which \(B_1\ge c2^{-k}\) holds for all balanced truncated ternary layers and all fields.

For completeness, the original fixed-law depth consequences still hold. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p((J,0))&=\inf_{\substack{H\ni(J,0)\\
H=\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf y\ge b\}\\
\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}}
\sum_{(I,0)\in A\cap H}p_I,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p((J,0))^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

All the halfspaces in this definition are closed affine halfspaces of the original ambient space. For every law in the displayed family,

$$
\tau_p((J,0))\ge R_1(J)/4\quad(J\in D),
\qquad
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p((J,0))\ge B_1/4,
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge(B_1/4)^3.
\tag{2}
$$

An upper bound on \(B_1\) does not give an upper bound on these actual depths or moments.

## Proof

The convexity, completeness, and nonemptiness of \(P,D\) are also established for exactly this support in accepted fact \(\texttt{cd14b2b69fa17e0f}\). Directly, the defining function of the cutoff is convex, its value at a ternary integer point is its number of twos, and the all-ones vector is feasible. We first establish the entropy law and its normalization for the present nonuniform original law.

For \(J\in D\), let \(q=\#\{i:J_i=2\}=\#\{i:J_i=0\}\) and let \(t=m-q\). A law with barycenter \(J\) must fix each coordinate at which \(J_i=0\) or \(J_i=2\), since its values lie in \([0,2]\). On the remaining \(b=k-2q\) coordinates, write a point as \(I_i=1+u_i\). Its permissible displacements are precisely

$$
u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{b},\qquad
\sum_i u_i=0,\qquad
\#\{i:u_i=1\}\le t.
\tag{3}
$$

The set in (3) is invariant under \(u\mapsto-u\). Extended by zero displacement on the fixed coordinates, it is exactly \(D\cap(2J-D)\): the reverse inclusion follows because \(I,2J-I\in[0,2]^k\) force every endpoint coordinate of \(J\) to remain fixed. Thus its uniform law has barycenter \(J\). It is the unique maximum-entropy law, since every feasible barycenter law is supported there and uniform measure uniquely maximizes entropy on a finite set. This is the same entropy law as in the cited accepted fact, because changing the original \(p\) does not change this entropy maximization problem.

Write

$$
\mathcal M_J=D\cap(2J-D),\qquad
H_J=|\mathcal M_J|,\qquad
T_J=\sum_{I\in\mathcal M_J}e^{-\beta\cdot(I-J)}.
$$

Here \(\mathcal M_J\) is a reflected set, not the locked mixed set \(S\). Reflection gives \(\sum_{I\in\mathcal M_J}e^{\beta\cdot(I-J)}=T_J\). As \(w_I=w_Je^{-\beta\cdot(I-J)}\), exact substitution into the original normalization yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{I\in D}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}
&=\frac{Z}{H_J^2w_J}T_J,\\
R_1(J)&=\frac{H_J^2w_J}{ZT_J},\\
B_1&=\frac{\mathcal U}{Z^2},\qquad
\mathcal U:=\sum_{J\in D}\frac{H_J^2w_J^2}{T_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The two coordinate blocks both have size \(2m\). For \(J\) with \(q\) zeros and \(q\) twos, let \(s\) be the number of its endpoint coordinates in the first block. The numbers of active coordinates in the two blocks are \(b_1=2m-s\) and \(b_2=2m-2q+s\). They satisfy \(b_1,b_2\ge2t\), where \(t=m-q\), and \(b_1+b_2=b=4m-2q\). The cardinality in (3) is

$$
H_J=H_q:=\sum_{a=0}^{t}\binom{b}{2a}\binom{2a}{a}.
\tag{5}
$$

To lower-bound \(T_J\), use only displacements with exactly \(t\) nonzero coordinates in each block. For each \(0\le\ell\le t\), give the first block \(\ell\) positive and \(t-\ell\) negative displacements, and give the second block \(t-\ell\) positive and \(\ell\) negative displacements. These displacements belong to (3). Their weight is \(4^{2\ell-t}\). Thus, on defining \(V_t=\sum_{\ell=0}^t\binom t\ell^2 4^{2\ell-t}\), we have \(T_J\ge\binom{b_1}{t}\binom{b_2}{t}V_t\). Cauchy-Schwarz and the binomial theorem give

$$
V_t=\sum_{\ell=0}^t
\left(\binom t\ell 2^{2\ell-t}\right)^2
\ge\frac1{t+1}
\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^t\binom t\ell2^{2\ell-t}\right)^2
=\frac{(5/2)^{2t}}{t+1}.
\tag{6}
$$

All these assertions include \(t=0\).

For fixed endpoint locations of \(J\), drop the requirement that their signs give \(q\) positive and \(q\) negative centered coordinates. This can only increase their total squared weight. Since \(\sum_i\beta_i=0\), the squared weight at each endpoint has the two possibilities \(4\) and \(1/4\), in either block. The total squared weight over the original balanced sign assignments is consequently at most \((17/4)^{2q}\). There are \(\binom{2m}{s}\binom{2m}{2q-s}\) choices of the endpoint locations. Combining this observation with (4)-(6) yields

$$
\mathcal U\le\sum_{q=0}^m
\frac{H_q^2(t+1)(17/4)^{2q}}{(5/2)^{2t}}
\sum_{s=0}^{2q}
\frac{\binom{2m}{s}\binom{2m}{2q-s}}
{\binom{2m-s}{t}\binom{2m-2q+s}{t}},
\qquad t=m-q.
\tag{7}
$$

We bound the inner sum by its central term times its number of terms. Put \(f(s)=\binom{2m}{s}/\binom{2m-s}{t}\) for integers \(0\le s\le2q\). Its successive ratios satisfy

$$
\frac{f(s+1)}{f(s)}
=\frac{(2m-s)^2}{(s+1)(2m-s-t)}.
$$

These ratios decrease. Indeed, for \(t>0\), regard the right-hand side as a positive function of real \(s\) on the relevant interval. Its logarithmic derivative is

$$
\frac{2t-(2m-s)}{(2m-s)(2m-s-t)}-\frac1{s+1}\le0,
$$

because \(2m-s\ge2m-2q=2t\). For \(t=0\), the ratios are those of the binomial coefficients and decrease as well. Hence \(f\) is log-concave. Moving two indices inward while preserving their sum increases the product, so \(f(s)f(2q-s)\le f(q)^2\). Thus

$$
\sum_{s=0}^{2q}
\frac{\binom{2m}{s}\binom{2m}{2q-s}}
{\binom{2m-s}{t}\binom{2m-2q+s}{t}}
\le(2q+1)
\frac{\binom{2m}{q}^{\!2}}{\binom{2m-q}{t}^{\!2}}.
\tag{8}
$$

For (5), \(b\ge4t\). Its summands increase with \(a\) up to \(t\), since the ratio of the summand at \(a+1\) to the summand at \(a\) is \((b-2a)(b-2a-1)/(a+1)^2>1\) when \(0\le a<t\). Therefore \(H_q\le(t+1)\binom b{2t}\binom{2t}{t}\). Set \(d=b/2=2m-q\). Vandermonde's identity and the log-concavity of binomial coefficients give

$$
\binom b{2t}
=\sum_{a=0}^{2t}\binom da\binom d{2t-a}
\le(2t+1)\binom dt^2.
$$

The product inequality here follows directly from the decreasing successive binomial ratios by moving the indices toward their mean \(t\). Also \(\binom{2t}{t}\le4^t\). Consequently

$$
H_q\le(t+1)(2t+1)\binom{2m-q}{t}^{\!2}4^t.
\tag{9}
$$

Substituting (8)-(9) into (7), and using \(q+t=m\), proves

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U
&\le\sum_{q=0}^m
(2q+1)(t+1)^3(2t+1)^2
\left(\binom{2m}{q}\binom{2m-q}{t}\right)^2
\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)^{2q}
\left(\frac85\right)^{2t}\\
&\le(m+1)^3(2m+1)^3\binom{2m}{m}^{\!2}
\sum_{q=0}^m\binom mq^2
\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)^{2q}
\left(\frac85\right)^{2(m-q)}\\
&\le(m+1)^3(2m+1)^3\binom{2m}{m}^{\!2}
\left(\frac{117}{20}\right)^{2m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

For the second line, the exact factorial identity is \(\binom{2m}{q}\binom{2m-q}{m-q}=\binom{2m}{m}\binom mq\). For the last line, the sum of squares of positive numbers is at most the square of their sum, and their sum is \((17/4+8/5)^m=(117/20)^m\).

For a lower bound on the original \(Z\), use only points with exactly \(m\) nonzero centered coordinates in each block, and mirrored signs between the two blocks as in the construction preceding (6). They have exactly \(m\) positive and \(m\) negative centered coordinates, so they belong to \(D\). Their original weights, without squaring, sum to \(\binom{2m}{m}^2V_m\). It follows from (6) that

$$
Z\ge\binom{2m}{m}^{\!2}\frac{(5/2)^{2m}}{m+1}.
\tag{11}
$$

Equations (4), (10), and (11) give

$$
B_1\le
\frac{(m+1)^5(2m+1)^3}{\binom{2m}{m}^{\!2}}
\left(\frac{117/20}{(5/2)^2}\right)^{2m}.
$$

The largest of the \(2m+1\) binomial coefficients of order \(2m\) is the central one. Their sum is \(2^{2m}\), so \(\binom{2m}{m}\ge2^{2m}/(2m+1)\). Also \((117/20)/(5/2)^2=117/125\). This proves (1). Since a fixed polynomial times \((117/125)^{2m}\) tends to zero, the asserted failure of every positive absolute lower constant follows.

Finally, central symmetry of \(\nu_J\) about \(J\), pushed forward to \(A\), puts at least half its mass in every closed affine halfspace containing \((J,0)\). Pair a point with its reflection: their average is \((J,0)\), so at least one belongs to such a halfspace. Cauchy-Schwarz for the original positive probabilities gives

$$
\frac12\le\nu_J(H)
\le\left(\sum_{I\in D}\frac{\nu_J(I)^2}{p_I}\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{(I,0)\in A\cap H}p_I\right)^{1/2}.
$$

Taking the infimum over exactly the containing closed ambient halfspaces yields \(\tau_p((J,0))\ge R_1(J)/4\). Averaging against the unchanged original \(p_J\), and then applying Jensen's inequality to \(x\mapsto x^3\) on the nonnegative depths, gives (2). This proves no upper bound on those depths or their moments.
