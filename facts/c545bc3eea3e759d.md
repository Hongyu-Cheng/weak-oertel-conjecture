## Statement

Let $\ell>0$ and $c\ge0$. Place $[-\ell,\ell]$, $[-\ell,\ell+4c]$, $[-\ell,\ell]$ at bases $-1,0,1$. For real $a,s$ let $G_a(s)$ be the sum of lengths captured by $x-c+az\ge s$, and set $M_0=6\ell+4c$. For every $a\in\mathbb R$ and $r\ge0$,
$$
M_0-2c\le G_a(r)+G_a(-r)\le M_0+2c.
$$

## Proof

For $L>0$ write $T_L(t)=\min(2L,(L-t)_+)$ for the upper tail length of $[-L,L]$. Put $D_L(b,r)=T_L(r-b)-T_L(r+b)$, an odd function of $b$. It is piecewise affine and its derivative in $b$ is $\mathbf1_{\{|b-r|<L\}}+\mathbf1_{\{|b+r|<L\}}$ almost everywhere, hence it is nondecreasing and $2$-Lipschitz.

In the coordinate $x-c+az$ the intervals have centers $a-c,c,-a-c$ and half-lengths $\ell,R,\ell$ with $R=\ell+2c$. The identity $T_L(-t)=2L-T_L(t)$ makes the contribution of an interval centered at $b$ to the paired sum equal to $2L+D_L(b,r)$. By oddness,
$$
G_a(r)+G_a(-r)-M_0
=D_R(c,r)-\bigl[D_\ell(a+c,r)-D_\ell(a-c,r)\bigr].
$$
The middle term $D_R(c,r)$ is between $0$ and $2c$, and the bracket is between $0$ and $4c$, by the derivative bound. The upper bound follows. If $r\le\ell+c=R-c$, both arguments $r-c$ and $r+c$ lie in $[-R,R]$, so $D_R(c,r)=2c$ and the difference is at least $-2c$. If $r>\ell+c$, the two open intervals contributing to the derivative of $D_\ell$ have a gap $2(r-\ell)>2c$. The integration interval $[a-c,a+c]$ has length $2c$ and intersects at most one of them. Its integral of the derivative is at most $2c$. Since $D_R(c,r)\ge0$, this also gives the lower bound. The formulas include equality at the clipping thresholds by continuity.
