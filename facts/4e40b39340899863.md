## Statement

Use the notation of accepted fact 1abd4035daee7fe2. In particular, the alias to accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7 is \(D_3=D=A\), with the same oriented-displacement reflection function \(r_{D_3}=r_A\). For any finite lattice-convex \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), let \(r_E(j)=|\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:j+u,j-u\in E\}|\), including the zero displacement at supported centers. All dimensions below are ambient lattice dimensions.

For a full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P_n\), its complete census \(D_n=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\), a facet \(F_n\), and an integer apex \(a_n\), use the accepted update

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{n+1}&=P_n'=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((P_n,0)\cup(F_n,1)\cup\{(a_n,2)\}\bigr),\\
D_{n+1}&=D_n'=P_{n+1}\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1},\\
D_{n,1}&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(F_n,(P_n+a_n)/2\bigr)\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
M_{a_n}&=\{(x+a_n)/2:x\in D_n,\ x\equiv a_n\pmod2\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Write \(N_n=|D_n|\), \(v_n=|\operatorname{vert}(P_n)|\), \(t_n=|\{j\in D_n:r_{D_n}(j)=1\}|\), \(q_n=3\cdot2^n-N_n-t_n\), \(e_n=t_n-v_n\), and \(\delta_n=3\cdot2^n-N_n-v_n=q_n+e_n\). The step-indexed aliases to 1abd4035daee7fe2 are \(k_n=k\), \(m_n=m_{a_n}\), \(t_{n,1}=t_1\), and \(q_{n,1}=q_1=3\cdot2^n-|D_{n,1}|-t_{n,1}\). Thus \(k_n\) counts surviving height-one vertices, \(m_n=|M_{a_n}|\), and \(t_{n,1}\) counts middle reflection-one points before the cross-layer contribution.

Suppose the old and middle reflection maxima are at most three and \(r_{D_{n,1}}=1\) on \(M_{a_n}\), equivalently the lifted reflection maximum is at most three. If \(s_{n,b}=|D_n\cap(b+2\mathbb Z^n)|\), with all \(2^n\) classes included, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_n&=\frac12\sum_b(s_{n,b}-2)(s_{n,b}-3)\ge0,\\
q_{n+1}&=q_n+q_{n,1}+m_n-2,\\
t_{n+1}&=t_n+t_{n,1}+1-m_n,\\
e_{n+1}&=e_n+h_n,\qquad h_n:=t_{n,1}-k_n-m_n\ge0,\\
\delta_{n+1}&=\delta_n+q_{n,1}+h_n+m_n-2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For any census, write \(\varepsilon\) for its number of empty parity classes and \(\sigma\) for its number of singleton parity classes; subscripts \(n\) and \((n,1)\) specify the old and middle censuses. Their exact transport is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{n+1}&=\varepsilon_n+\varepsilon_{n,1}-\mathbf1_{\{m_n=0\}},\\
\sigma_{n+1}&=\sigma_n+\sigma_{n,1}+\mathbf1_{\{m_n=0\}}-\mathbf1_{\{m_n=1\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

An empty class contributes three units to \(q\), a singleton contributes one, sizes two and three contribute zero, and size \(s\ge4\) contributes \((s-2)(s-3)/2\). For a selected singleton, \(q_{n+1}=q_n+q_{n,1}-1\), so nonnegativity requires \(q_n+q_{n,1}\ge1\); this is automatic because \(q_n\ge\sigma_n\ge1\). Its even-height singleton class is consumed, not carried into the lift. Conversely a selected empty class creates an even-height singleton class. Middle singleton classes are copied to odd height parity independently of these operations.

Here is a concrete sufficient condition. Start at the accepted calibration of 1abd4035daee7fe2:
\(P_4=P_3'\) with \(a_3=(-10,-15,-25)=2p_2-p_{14}\), \(D_4=P_4\cap\mathbb Z^4\), and \(F_4=(P_3,0)\), the bottom tetrahedral facet. Assume that for every \(n\ge4\) an apex \(a_n\) can be chosen with the following properties:

3. The selected old parity class has size \(m_n\in\{1,2\}\). Every middle parity class has size one, two, or three, with \(\sigma_{n,1}\le2\).
4. \(0\le h_n=t_{n,1}-n-m_n\le2\).

Then every old parity class continues to have size one, two, or three, the reflection maximum continues to equal three, and the following invariant and quantitative bounds hold for all \(n\ge4\):

\[
\begin{aligned}

This criterion does not require \(\delta_n=0\). The same proof permits any fixed bound \(C\) in place of both occurrences of two in properties 3 and 4: replace the linear upper bounds for \(\sigma_n,e_n\) by \(2+C(n-4)\) and the bound for \(\delta_n\) by \(4+2C(n-4)\). For the normalizations, retain the canonical quantities of 768d9712b73c4ab7, \(W=\sum_z w_z\), \(M=\max_z w_z\), \(H_j=\sum_u\sqrt{w_{j+u}w_{j-u}}\), \(H_*=\max_jH_j\), \(\rho=2^nM/W\), and \(G=\log(2^nH_*/W)\). Its uniform-center definition is the logarithm of the second moment of \(H_j\) divided by the square of its first moment, with centers sampled uniformly from \(D_n\):

\[

Under the sufficient condition above,
\(N_n/2^n\to3\), \(t_n/N_n\to0\), the canonical \(\rho_n\to1/3\), \(G_n\to0\), \(2^n\rho_n^{\mathrm{par}}\to1\), and \(G_n^{\mathrm{mom}}\to0\). The maximum atom is \(1/N_n\sim(1/3)2^{-n}\). These are conditional consequences for uniform lattice tables, not an assertion about halfspace depths or a resolution of the locked conjecture.

## Proof

First consider an arbitrary complete finite lattice census \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) with reflection maximum at most three. Lattice-convexity implies that every integer midpoint of its points belongs to \(E\), so centers outside \(E\) have count zero. At a supported center the zero displacement occurs once and nonzero displacements occur in opposite pairs; hence the count is one or three. If \(N=|E|\) and \(t\) counts its reflection-one points, the sum of all reflection counts is \(3N-2t\). Ordered pairs of same-parity endpoints are in bijection with reflected pairs via \((x,y)\mapsto((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)\). Thus \(\sum_b s_b^2=3N-2t\). Since \(\sum_b s_b=N\) and the number of ambient classes is \(2^n\), expansion gives

\[
\frac12\sum_b(s_b-2)(s_b-3)
=\frac12(3N-2t-5N+6\cdot2^n)
=3\cdot2^n-N-t.
\]

The summands are nonnegative at every nonnegative integer size, with the costs stated in the claim. Vertices cannot be midpoints of distinct census points, so their counts are one and \(e=t-v\ge0\). This proves all the nonnegativity statements used here without requiring any predecessor beyond the specified definitions. It also recovers \(\delta=q+e\).

By accepted fact 1abd4035daee7fe2 the lifted census is exactly \((D_n,0)\cup(D_{n,1},1)\cup\{(a_n,2)\}\). Let \(u_{n,b}\) be the middle parity sizes and let \(b_n\) be the parity of \(a_n\). The full \((n+1)\)-dimensional parity sizes, indexed by the horizontal parity \(b\) and height parity, are exactly

\[
s_{n+1,(b,0)}=s_{n,b}+\mathbf1_{\{b=b_n\}},
\qquad s_{n+1,(b,1)}=u_{n,b}.
\]

There are \(2^n\) even-height and \(2^n\) odd-height classes. In particular the old and middle contributions already account for all \(2^{n+1}\) classes, so no additional dimensional offset remains. Put \(f(s)=(s-2)(s-3)/2\); direct subtraction gives \(f(m+1)-f(m)=m-2\). Summing the parity costs proves \(q_{n+1}=q_n+q_{n,1}+m_n-2\). The only change to the even-height profile is the selected class's transition \(m_n\mapsto m_n+1\); the claimed empty and singleton transport follows immediately. In particular, even for a selected empty class the negative two-unit change in total cost cannot make it negative: that old class alone had cost three. For a selected singleton it had cost one, proving the more precise nonnegativity constraint in the statement.

The accepted all-center reflection formulas show that bottom counts are unchanged, top count is one, and precisely the \(m_n\) distinct points of \(M_{a_n}\) have their middle counts changed from one to three. Every other middle count is unchanged. This proves \(t_{n+1}=t_n+t_{n,1}+1-m_n\). The height-one vertices and \(M_{a_n}\) are disjoint: every point of the latter, at height one, is a midpoint of a bottom point and the apex, so is not extreme. Each of the \(k_n\) height-one vertices is a middle reflection-one point. Hence \(t_{n,1}\ge k_n+m_n\) and \(h_n\ge0\), as also proved in 1abd4035daee7fe2. Subtracting its vertex update \(v_{n+1}=v_n+k_n+1\) proves \(e_{n+1}=e_n+h_n\). Adding the two defect updates gives the stated exact update of \(\delta\). Notice that singleton selection can reduce \(q\), but it cannot reduce \(e\).

Next suppose the sufficient condition holds at a step \(n\ge4\) and the old profile consists only of sizes one, two, and three. Its middle profile has the same property, and \(m_n\in\{1,2\}\) changes one even class from one to two or from two to three. The full profile therefore remains in these sizes. Its parity cost is exactly its singleton count, so \(q_{n+1}=\sigma_{n+1}\). For a profile in these three sizes, let \(\tau_n\) count size-two classes. Its cardinality is \(N_n=3\cdot2^n-2\sigma_n-\tau_n\). Since \(q_n=\sigma_n=3\cdot2^n-N_n-t_n\), it follows that \(t_n=\sigma_n+\tau_n\) and hence \(\tau_n=v_n+e_n-\sigma_n\). This is the stated invariant linking singleton defects to excess count-one points. It also proves that a size-one or size-two class is always available at the parity level: if \(\sigma_n>0\), there is a singleton; otherwise \(\tau_n=t_n\ge v_n>0\). It does not guarantee that an apex of that parity satisfies the middle reflection and census conditions.

Within this profile the precise recurrences simplify to

\[
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
\]

Thus selection of a singleton consumes one unit of the singleton reservoir, while middle singleton classes replenish it. Middle count-one excess is added irreversibly. Starting from \(\sigma_4=e_4=2\) and \(v_4=7\), induction using \(\sigma_{n,1},h_n\le2\) gives all the displayed bounds; summing the vertex recurrence gives \(v_n=n(n+1)/2-3\). The version with a fixed bound \(C\) follows by the same summation. As an additional census consistency check, properties 3 and 4 force the middle numbers of singleton, double, and triple classes to be respectively

\[
\sigma_{n,1},\qquad n+m_n+h_n-\sigma_{n,1},\qquad 2^n-n-m_n-h_n.
\]

For \(n\ge4\), \(m_n\in\{1,2\}\), \(h_n\in\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\sigma_{n,1}\in\{0,1,2\}\), all these are nonnegative integers: the second is at least \(n-1\), and the third is at least \(2^n-n-4\ge0\). They sum to \(2^n\). Thus the bounded-loss conditions have no contradiction at the level of the complete parity census, and the initial census realizes the specified defect mechanism. This arithmetic compatibility is not a geometric existence proof.

Finally the bounds give \(v_n=O(n^2)\), \(\delta_n=O(n)\), and \(t_n=O(n^2)\), so \(v_n+\delta_n=o(2^n)\). The identity \(N_n=3\cdot2^n-v_n-\delta_n\) proves \(N_n/2^n\to3\), and consequently \(t_n/N_n\to0\). Once \(e_4=2\), its monotonicity shows \(\delta_n\ge e_n\ge2\): sharp zero-deficit restoration is impossible along these updates, but is unnecessary for the normalized limit.

For completeness derive the probabilistic quantities directly. The uniform table has \(W=1\), \(M=1/N_n\), and \(H_j=r_{D_n}(j)/N_n\), since every supported endpoint contributes \(\sqrt{(1/N_n)(1/N_n)}=1/N_n\). Extending it to \(N_n^{-1}\mathbf1_{P_n}\) preserves every zero lattice value and is log-concave by convexity of \(P_n\): for an interpolation parameter strictly between zero and one, two positive endpoints give the same positive value on their segment, and a zero endpoint makes the required lower bound zero. Since the reflection maximum is exactly three, the canonical definitions give \(\rho_n=2^n/N_n\) and \(G_n=\log(3\cdot2^n/N_n)\), tending to \(1/3\) and zero respectively.

The same ordered-pair bijection already proved gives \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{par}}=(3N_n-2t_n)/N_n^2\). Therefore \(2^n\rho_n^{\mathrm{par}}=(2^n/N_n)(3-2t_n/N_n)\to1\). For the uniform-center Hellinger moments, \(H_j=r_j/N_n\) gives first moment \((\sum_jr_j)/N_n^2\) and second moment \((\sum_jr_j^2)/N_n^3\); their ratio is exactly the formula in the statement. Equivalently, normalizing the Hellinger center weights to probabilities \(H_j/\sum_iH_i\) gives \(\exp(G_n^{\mathrm{mom}})=N_n\sum_j(H_j/\sum_iH_i)^2\). Both calculations use only the definitions, not an undeclared Hellinger theorem.

There are \(t_n\) counts one and \(N_n-t_n\) counts three, so \(\sum_jr_j=3N_n-2t_n\) and \(\sum_jr_j^2=9N_n-8t_n\). Direct subtraction of numerator and denominator proves

\[
G_n^{\mathrm{mom}}
=\log\left(1+\frac{4t_n(N_n-t_n)}{(3N_n-2t_n)^2}\right),
\qquad 0\le G_n^{\mathrm{mom}}\le\frac{4t_n}{N_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Here the bound uses \(0\le t_n\le N_n\), hence \(3N_n-2t_n\ge N_n\), and \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x\ge0\). The maximum atom is \(1/N_n\), proving its stated asymptotic.
