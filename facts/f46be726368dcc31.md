## Statement

Let \(K=\operatorname{conv}\{a,b,c,d\}\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a full-dimensional tetrahedron with \(a,b,c,d\in\mathbb Z^3\), and put \(B=K\cap\mathbb Z^3\). Use the oriented, zero-inclusive reflection convention of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7: \(r_B(j)=|\{v\in\mathbb Z^3:j+v,j-v\in B\}|\) for every \(j\in\mathbb Z^3\).

If \(|B|=20\) and \(\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^3}r_B(j)\le3\), then \(B\) affinely generates the ambient integer lattice. Explicitly, \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{x-a:x\in B\}=\mathbb Z^3\). Its normalized volume \(D=|\det(b-a,c-a,d-a)|\), equal to six times its ordinary volume and with the same meaning as \(D\) in accepted fact dd4516fafb98226b, satisfies \(34\le D\le4096\).

## Proof

First, every affine line contains at most four points of \(B\). If a line contained at least five, its lattice points could be written in increasing order along a primitive integer direction \(u\). Convexity would supply five consecutive points \(x,x+u,x+2u,x+3u,x+4u\) of \(B\). At the integer center \(x+2u\), the five distinct displacements \(0,u,-u,2u,-2u\) would then be counted by \(r_B\), contradicting its upper bound.

Apply the non-spanning classification stated in EXTERNAL SOURCES. If \(K\) did not affinely generate \(\mathbb Z^3\), its complete lattice-point set would fall into one of that classification's three alternatives. The two-segment alternative permits at most \(4+4=8\) points. The one-segment alternative permits at most \(4+4=8\) points. Each exceptional polytope also has at most eight points. Affine-unimodular equivalence preserves both lattice-point counts and the line bound. Since \(|B|=20\), none of the alternatives is possible. Consequently \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{x-a:x\in B\}=\mathbb Z^3\).

We next extract all equality information from the vertex-deficit argument of accepted fact 768d9712b73c4ab7. For each ambient parity class \(\epsilon\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3\), let \(N_\epsilon=|\{x\in B:x\bmod2=\epsilon\}|\), including a zero value if a class is empty. Ordered pairs of same-parity points correspond bijectively to reflected pairs with integer center, via \((x,y)\mapsto((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)\). Every such center belongs to \(B\), by convexity. Each of the four vertices has reflection count one, since an extreme point cannot be a midpoint of two distinct points of \(K\). Therefore

\[
52=5\cdot20-6\cdot8
\le \sum_{\epsilon}N_\epsilon^2
=\sum_{j\in B}r_B(j)
\le4+3\cdot16=52.
\]

The first inequality sums \(m^2-5m+6=(m-2)(m-3)\ge0\) over the eight nonnegative integer class sizes. Equality throughout implies that every \(N_\epsilon\) belongs to \(\{2,3\}\), and that every nonvertex of \(B\) has reflection count exactly three. Since the class sizes sum to twenty, four classes have size two and four have size three.

Label the points \(p_1,\ldots,p_{20}\), with \(p_1=a,p_2=b,p_3=c,p_4=d\), and let \(I=\{5,\ldots,20\}\). For each \(i\in I\), there is a unique unordered pair of distinct indices \(\{s(i),t(i)\}\) such that \(2p_i=p_{s(i)}+p_{t(i)}\). Neither parent index can equal \(i\). Conversely, every unordered distinct pair of points in a common parity class has its midpoint at one of these sixteen nonvertices and is that midpoint's unique pair. Indeed, its midpoint is an integer point of \(K\), cannot be a vertex, and two different pairs at one midpoint would give reflection count at least five. Thus the sixteen parent pairs are exactly all pairs within the eight parity classes: four classes contribute one pair each and four contribute three pairs each.

Consider the directed graph with arrows \(i\to s(i)\) and \(i\to t(i)\) for \(i\in I\); the four vertex indices have no outgoing arrows and will be called sinks. Every index in \(I\) has a directed path to a sink. Otherwise, starting at an index without such a path, the set \(U\) of all reachable indices would be a nonempty subset of \(I\) closed under both parent maps. The finite set \(\{p_i:i\in U\}\) has an extreme point of its convex hull. That point would be the midpoint of its two distinct parents, both also in the set, contradicting extremality.

Let \(\mathbf f_i\), \(i\in I\), be the standard row basis of \(\mathbb Z^{16}\), and set \(\mathbf f_1=\mathbf f_2=\mathbf f_3=\mathbf f_4=0\). Define the integer \(16\) by \(16\) matrix \(H\) by giving its row indexed by \(i\in I\) as \(2\mathbf f_i-\mathbf f_{s(i)}-\mathbf f_{t(i)}\).

This matrix is nonsingular. To see this, suppose \(Hh=0\) for a real vector indexed by \(I\), and extend \(h\) to the sinks by zero. At every \(i\in I\), the equation says \(h_i=(h_{s(i)}+h_{t(i)})/2\). If \(h\ne0\), choose an index where \(|h_i|\) is maximal and positive. Both parent values must equal \(h_i\): their absolute values are no larger, and their average attains the same positive absolute maximum. Propagating this equality along a directed path to a sink gives a nonzero value at a sink, a contradiction. Thus \(h=0\).

We now keep track of the integral relations. Let \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_{20}\) denote the standard basis of \(\mathbb Z^{20}\), let

\[
R=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}
\{\,2\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_{s(i)}-\mathbf e_{t(i)}:i\in I\,\},
\qquad
\mathcal A=\mathbb Z^{20}/R,
\]

and write \(\bar{\mathbf e}_i\) for the image of \(\mathbf e_i\) in \(\mathcal A\). Restricting the sixteen relation vectors to their \(I\)-coordinates gives the rows of \(H\). Since \(H\) is nonsingular, these relations are independent over \(\mathbb Q\), so the finitely generated abelian group \(\mathcal A\) has free rank four.

The assignment \(\Phi(\bar{\mathbf e}_i)=\binom{p_i}{1}\) defines a homomorphism \(\Phi:\mathcal A\to\mathbb Z^4\), because each midpoint relation is annihilated. It is surjective in the ambient integer lattice: the differences \(p_i-a\) generate \(\mathbb Z^3\), as proved above, so the differences of the corresponding homogeneous vectors generate \(\mathbb Z^3\times\{0\}\). Together with \(\binom a1\), they generate \(\mathbb Z^4\).

The kernel of \(\Phi\) is precisely the torsion subgroup \(T\) of \(\mathcal A\). All torsion maps to zero because \(\mathbb Z^4\) is torsion-free. Conversely, the source and target both have free rank four, so the kernel is a finitely generated group of rank zero and is finite. The surjection onto the free group \(\mathbb Z^4\) splits by lifting its standard basis. Consequently \(\mathcal A\cong\mathbb Z^4\oplus T\).

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the subgroup of \(\mathcal A\) generated by the four sink generators \(\bar{\mathbf e}_1,\ldots,\bar{\mathbf e}_4\). Their images under \(\Phi\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), since \(K\) is full-dimensional. Thus these four elements freely generate \(\mathcal V\), the restriction of \(\Phi\) to \(\mathcal V\) is injective, and \(\mathcal V\cap T=\{0\}\). Moreover, \(\Phi(\mathcal V)\) has index \(D\) in \(\mathbb Z^4\): the absolute determinant of its four homogeneous vertex columns is \(D\), by subtracting the first column from the other three and expanding along the last row.

The induced map \(\mathcal A/\mathcal V\to\mathbb Z^4/\Phi(\mathcal V)\) is surjective. Its kernel consists exactly of the images of \(T\). Indeed, if \(\Phi(x)\in\Phi(\mathcal V)\), choose \(v\in\mathcal V\) with \(\Phi(v)=\Phi(x)\); then \(x-v\in T\). Conversely every torsion element maps to zero, and \(T\) injects into \(\mathcal A/\mathcal V\) because \(T\cap\mathcal V=\{0\}\). Hence there is an exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow T\longrightarrow\mathcal A/\mathcal V
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^4/\Phi(\mathcal V)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Quotienting by \(\mathcal V\) kills the four sink generators. The resulting presentation is \(\mathbb Z^{16}\) modulo the subgroup generated by the rows of \(H\), so \(|\mathcal A/\mathcal V|=|\det H|\). The exact sequence therefore gives the integral index identity \(|\det H|=|T|D\). This identity uses the established surjectivity onto the ambient \(\mathbb Z^4\).

It remains to bound the two factors in that identity. Modulo two, the relation \(2\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_{s(i)}-\mathbf e_{t(i)}\) reduces to \(\mathbf e_{s(i)}+\mathbf e_{t(i)}\). By the equality bookkeeping, these are exactly the incidence vectors of all unordered pairs within the eight parity classes. For a class with \(m\) elements, those vectors span the coordinate-sum-zero subspace of its \(\mathbb F_2^m\), of dimension \(m-1\). Explicitly, fixing one class element, its pairs with the other \(m-1\) elements give an independent spanning set. The classes have disjoint coordinates and are all nonempty, so the relation matrix has rank \(\sum_\epsilon(N_\epsilon-1)=20-8=12\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Therefore

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(\mathcal A/2\mathcal A)=20-12=8,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(T/2T)=8-4=4.
\]

The second equality uses \(\mathcal A\cong\mathbb Z^4\oplus T\). Thus the finite group \(T\) has a quotient \(T/2T\) with sixteen elements, and \(|T|\ge16\).

Finally, each row of \(H\) is the sum \((\mathbf f_i-\mathbf f_{s(i)})+(\mathbf f_i-\mathbf f_{t(i)})\). Multilinearity of the determinant expands \(\det H\) into \(2^{16}\) terms, one for each choice of one parent at every index of \(I\). Each term is the determinant of a matrix whose row at \(i\) is \(\mathbf f_i-\mathbf f_{q(i)}\), where \(q(i)\) is the chosen parent.

If the chosen directed graph has a cycle entirely in \(I\), the matrix rows on that cycle sum to zero, so its determinant is zero. If it has no such cycle, every chosen trajectory reaches a sink, since the graph is finite and every non-sink has one chosen outgoing arrow. Order the indices of \(I\) by their distance to the sink along these trajectories, with smaller distances first. After making the same permutation of rows and columns, the matrix is triangular with every diagonal entry one: a chosen parent in \(I\) always occurs earlier than its child. This simultaneous permutation does not change the determinant. Hence each acyclic term has determinant one.

Every term is therefore either zero or one, giving \(\det H\le2^{16}\). Nonsingularity and this nonnegative expansion also give \(\det H>0\). Combining the bounds with the integral index identity yields \(D=\det H/|T|\le2^{16}/16=4096\).

Accepted fact dd4516fafb98226b proves \(D\ge34\) for every full-dimensional twenty-point lattice tetrahedron with exactly these reflection hypotheses, independently of its additional source-polytope assumptions. Its stated normalization is the present determinant \(D\). This gives the asserted lower bound and completes the proof.

The precise consequence used from Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2, the lattice-point description immediately after Lemma 1.2, and Theorem 1.3 is this: every full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P\subset\mathbb R^3\) of affine lattice index greater than one is, up to an affine automorphism of \(\mathbb Z^3\), in one of the following alternatives.

- Its entire set \(P\cap\mathbb Z^3\) lies on two lattice segments in consecutive parallel lattice planes.
- Its entire set \(P\cap\mathbb Z^3\) consists of one segment's lattice points and three or four additional points.
- It is one of six exceptions, whose respective numbers of lattice points are \(5,6,7,8,8,8\).

The source defines index using the affine lattice generated by all of \(P\cap\mathbb Z^3\). Thus it is the ambient index required here. A prospective non-spanning \(K\) satisfies every hypothesis; no assumption concerning hollowness, normality, or tetrahedron shape is needed.
