## Statement

Normalize the larger endpoint volume to one and put $0<a\le1$, $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, $v=2^{-d}(1+a^{1/d})^d$, and $k\ge v$. For a compatible middle-symmetric three-fiber source at $-1,0,1$ with volumes $a,k,1$, consider the two lower certificates
$$
B_0=k/2+a(1-k/(2v))_+,\qquad B_1=\Gamma
$$
at the middle symmetry center and the larger endpoint centroid. These can both miss the target $\Gamma(1+a+k)/2$. In dimension one this holds along $0<a<1/3$ with $k=(1-2a)(1+a)/(1-a)$; at $a=1/4,\ k=5/6$ the gap is $1/48$. In dimension two it holds for $1/3<x<(2+\sqrt{19})/15$ with $a=x^2$ and $k=2(4/9-a)/(1-a/v)$; at $a=4/25,\ k=6272/7425$ the gap is $14/13365$. Throughout these regimes $v<k<2v$ and $B_0=B_1=\Gamma$.

For every admissible $a,k$ in this setup the centered homothetic cube fibers $a^{1/d}B,k^{1/d}B,B$ with $B=[-1/2,1/2]^d$ are exactly realized by their lifted convex hull. Their actual middle center has raw depth at least $k/2+a$. In the rational dimension-one example that gives $2/3>25/48$, and in dimension two it gives a surplus of $9146/66825$ above the target.

## Proof

Apply the two-input equal-weight case of 793210f8a4be3647 to the original endpoint uniform laws, then multiply the joint law by $a$. Its two marginals are dominated by the two original volume measures and its midpoint law is dominated by $(a/v)\mathbf1_K\,dx$. In a nonvertical through-center ambient cap, the missed middle trace has volume $k/2$. Thus the midpoint law puts at least $a(1-k/(2v))_+$ into the captured trace. An ambient halfspace containing a coupled midpoint contains at least one of the endpoints, so the original endpoint cap volumes sum to at least that payment. The middle contributes $k/2$. Pure-base through-center caps contain the full middle and one or both endpoints, so also satisfy $B_0$. For the larger endpoint centroid apply 6769574fc1bd9f8d at full selected mass one in its own fiber. Tightening a containing cap parallel to the mark reduces to through-mark caps. This verifies that the two formulas are lower bounds.

For $d=1$, $v=(1+a)/2$. At the stated $k$,
$$
k-v=\frac{(1+a)(1-3a)}{2(1-a)},\quad
2v-k=\frac{a(1+a)}{1-a},\quad
k-(1-a)=\frac{a(1-3a)}{1-a}.
$$
All are positive. Since $B_0=a+(1-a/v)k/2$ when $k<2v$, substitution gives $B_0=1/2=B_1$, whereas the target is larger because $k>1-a$. The rational values give $25/48-1/2=1/48$.

For $d=2$ write $a=x^2,\ v=(1+x)^2/4$. On the displayed interval $0<x<1/2$, so $v-a>0$ and $v+a<13/16<8/9$. Also $x>1/3$ gives $v>4/9$. With the chosen $k$, these facts give $v<k<2v$ and $B_0=4/9=B_1$. Finally
$$
4[v(1+a-8/9)-a(1-a)]=\frac{(x+1)(3x-1)(15x^2-4x-1)}9<0
$$
on this interval, and the expression inside brackets equals $-(v-a)[k-(1-a)]$. Thus again $k>1-a$ and the target is strictly larger. At $x=2/5$ the substitution yields the stated $k$ and total $14885/7425$, giving the gap $14/13365$.

For the actual realizations, the endpoint midpoint body is $(1+a^{1/d})B/2\subseteq k^{1/d}B$. Equal endpoint weights in each combination at base zero give the exact middle fiber and the exposed endpoints give the other two. Reflection about the actual middle center takes the smaller endpoint to an equal volume subset of the larger. Each of its points or the reflected point belongs to a through-center closed cap. This gives at least $a$ actual endpoint mass, and symmetry of the middle gives $k/2$ in every nonvertical through-center cap. Pure-base caps contain the whole middle and at least $a$ from the endpoints. The two rational surpluses follow by substitution.
