## Statement

For tetrahedral raw margins $c=(p,q,-1,-1)$ with $p,q>0$, let $P(c)$ be the uniform tetrahedral cap probability, $f_V(0)$ the density at zero of $V=\sum_i c_i E_i$ for independent unit exponentials $E_i$, and $J=\nabla^2P(c)[e_3-e_4,e_3-e_4]/[3f_V(0)]$. Put $s=p+q$, $v=pq$ and $N=s^2+4sv+6v^2-v-v^3$. Then $J=2N/[3(s+2v)(1+s+v)^2]$. Along $p=1/10+t,\ q=1/10-t$, $|t|<1/10$, the reciprocal $R=1/J$ is defined near zero, with $R(0)=363/29$ and $R''(0)=-270000/841$. At $t=0$ the section centroid has barycentric coordinates $(5/11,5/11,1/22,1/22)$.

## Proof

The raw cap formula of 1462fa9ce6ced54e applies since the two positive and two negative margins are strict. For a perturbation by $\delta(e_3-e_4)$ with $|\delta|<1$, put $r=1-\delta$ and $w=1+\delta$. Bringing the accepted expression $p(\alpha+\delta_{\rm sec})+q^2/[(q+r)(q+w)]$, with $\delta_{\rm sec}=qr/[(p+r)(q+w)(q+r)]$ and $\alpha=pw/[(p+r)(p+w)(q+w)]$, to a common denominator gives
$$
P(p,q,-r,-w)=
\frac{v^2+vs(r+w)+(s^2-v)rw}{(p+r)(p+w)(q+r)(q+w)}.
$$
Writing $m=(1+p)(1+q)=1+s+v$, the numerator along the perturbation is $A-(s^2-v)\delta^2$, with $A=(s+v)^2-v$. The denominator is $m^2-U\delta^2+\delta^4$, where $U=(1+p)^2+(1+q)^2=2+2s+s^2-2v$. The second derivative at zero is $2[AU-(s^2-v)m^2]/m^4=2N/m^4$ by expansion.

For the density, $E_3+E_4$ has density $te^{-t}$ for $t\ge0$, by convolution. It is independent of $S=pE_1+qE_2$. Hence $f_V(0)=\mathbb E[Se^{-S}]=m^{-1}[p/(1+p)+q/(1+q)]=(s+2v)/m^2$, using the first moments of the two tilted exponential densities. Division gives $J$.

At fixed $s=1/5$ write $D=3(s+2v)(1+s+v)^2$ so that $R=D/(2N)$ and $v=1/100-t^2$. At $v=1/100$ the values of $(N,\partial_vN,D,\partial_v D)$ are $(38599/10^6,-803/10^4,483153/500000,51909/5000)$. Thus $J$ is nonzero in a neighborhood of zero. The two reciprocal values follow from $R(0)=D/(2N)$ and $R''(0)=-(\partial_vD\,N-D\,\partial_vN)/N^2$. Both margins are positive on the specified segment, and their sum is $1/5<1$. At its midpoint the section equation gives first-pair sum $10/11$ and second-pair sum $1/11$. Swapping either pair is an affine automorphism of the section and preserves its normalized area, so its centroid bisects both sums.
