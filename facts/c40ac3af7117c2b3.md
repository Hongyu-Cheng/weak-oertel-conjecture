## Statement

For every integer \(N\ge2\), let
\(P_N=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,-1),(-1,N),(1,N)\}\subset\mathbb R^2\), and retain the complete support \(D_N=P_N\cap\mathbb Z^2\) in the whole lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\).
Then \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P_N\), but there is no full-dimensional lattice polytope \(Q\subseteq P_N\) satisfying \(\operatorname{int}(Q)\cap\mathbb Z^2=\{\mathbf0\}\). In fact, the only contained lattice polytope with \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}Q\) is \(P_N\) itself.

For \(N=2\), put \(D=D_2\), \(P=P_2\), and let \(\phi\) be the restriction to \(D\) of the real-valued convex function \(\phi(x,y)=(\log3)\max\{y,0\}\). With the original weights and law
\(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), and \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\), one has

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0,
\qquad
W=\frac83,
\qquad
g:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}
=\frac38\log3>0.
\]

Consequently the proposed universal inequality

\[
g\le
\sup\left\{
\log B(Q):
\begin{array}{l}
Q\subseteq P\text{ is a full-dimensional lattice polytope},\\
\operatorname{int}(Q)\cap\mathbb Z^2=\{\mathbf0\}
\end{array}
\right\},
\qquad
B(Q)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I},
\]

is false with the standard extended-real convention \(\sup\varnothing=-\infty\). Without an empty-set convention, its right-hand side is not a real number on this valid input. It remains false if the empty supremum is instead assigned the value zero. The same example has \(g<2\log4\), so it is not a counterexample to the open sharp energy estimate.

## Proof

The triangle has the exact description
\(P_N=\{(x,y):y\le N,\ (N+1)|x|\le y+1\}\). For each real \(y\in[-1,N]\), its horizontal section is the interval \(|x|\le(y+1)/(N+1)\). These sections also verify the displayed description directly from its three vertices.

If \(y\) is an integer with \(-1\le y<N\), this interval has radius strictly less than one, so its only integer abscissa is zero. At \(y=N\), the integer abscissae are exactly \(-1,0,1\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_N&=\{(0,j):-1\le j\le N,\ j\in\mathbb Z\}
       \cup\{(-1,N),(1,N)\},\\
\operatorname{int}(P_N)\cap\mathbb Z^2
&=\{(0,j):0\le j\le N-1,\ j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the second equality, both side inequalities and the upper inequality are strict exactly at the indicated integer sites. This proves that zero is interior and that there are exactly \(N\) interior lattice points. The lattice throughout these calculations is all of \(\mathbb Z^2\). The support has affine rank two, since it contains \((0,0),(0,1),(1,N)\).

Suppose that a lattice polytope \(Q\subseteq P_N\) has zero in its ordinary interior in \(\mathbb R^2\). A ball about zero lies in \(Q\). Consequently its minimum first coordinate is negative, its maximum first coordinate is positive, and its minimum second coordinate is negative. Each of these extrema is attained at a vertex, and each vertex is an element of \(D_N\), because \(Q\) is a lattice polytope contained in \(P_N\).

The complete list of \(D_N\) shows that its only point with negative first coordinate is \((-1,N)\), its only point with positive first coordinate is \((1,N)\), and its only point with negative second coordinate is \((0,-1)\). All three points must therefore belong to \(Q\). Their convex hull is \(P_N\), so convexity gives \(P_N\subseteq Q\). Together with the assumed containment this gives \(Q=P_N\). Since \(N\ge2\), the displayed interior count excludes the required sole-interior-point property. This checks every possible \(Q\), rather than excluding only one selected core.

It remains to verify the probability law on the exact complete support for \(N=2\). The function \(\phi\) is the maximum of the two affine functions \(0\) and \((\log3)y\), so it is convex on all of \(\mathbb R^2\). Its values therefore satisfy every convex-admissibility constraint on \(D\). The six sites and their original weights are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\mathbf I&\phi_{\mathbf I}&w_{\mathbf I}\\ \hline
(0,-1)&0&1\\
(0,0)&0&1\\
(0,1)&\log3&1/3\\
(-1,2)&2\log3&1/9\\
(0,2)&2\log3&1/9\\
(1,2)&2\log3&1/9
\end{array}
\]

All six weights are strictly positive. Their first-coordinate moment cancels between the two nonzero abscissae. Their second-coordinate moment and total weight are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}I_2
&=-1+\frac13+3\cdot\frac29=0,\\
W&=1+1+\frac13+3\cdot\frac19=\frac83.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the original weighted mean is zero, with no change of potential or centering tilt. Since \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), its energy is
\(g=(\frac13\log3+3\cdot\frac19\,2\log3)/(8/3)=\frac38\log3>0\), as asserted. Finally, \(\frac38\log3<2\log4\) follows from \(0<\log3<\log4\) and \(3/8<2\). The support is nonsymmetric, since \((1,2)\in P\) but \((-1,-2)\notin P\).
