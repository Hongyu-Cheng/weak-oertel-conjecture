## Statement

Assume the complete triangular-homothetic saturated three-circuit setting and notation of accepted fact d33ef001609a8cc8, including the saturated maximum free same-parity matching \(p\) from accepted fact f23ff6dd60fbbc37, the selected masses

\[
 t_z:=2d_z(p),
 \qquad
 U:=\{z\in Z:t_z<a_z\},
\]

the fully selected clipped family \(B\subseteq Z\setminus U\), the normalized thresholds \(\xi_{i,z}\), the cap losses \(\Delta_{i,z}\), the admissible integral center \(m\), and an actual minimum-support balanced negative circuit of cardinality three with directions \(u_1,u_2,u_3\), exact affine minorants \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\), and \(u_1+u_2+u_3=0\). For \(z\in B\), put

\[
 L_z:=\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z},
 \qquad
 L_B:=\sum_{z\in B}L_z,
 \qquad
 U_0:=\sum_{u\in U}a_u.
\tag{1}
\]

For a parity class \(\omega\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), write

\[
 Z_\omega:=\{z\in Z:z\equiv\omega\pmod 2\}.
\]

Let \(\mathcal P_U\) be the set of parity classes meeting \(U\), and split

\[
 B_U:=B\cap\bigcup_{\omega\in\mathcal P_U}Z_\omega,
 \qquad
 B_0:=B\setminus B_U,
\]

\[
 L_U:=\sum_{z\in B_U}L_z,
 \qquad
 L_0:=\sum_{z\in B_0}L_z.
\tag{2}
\]

Then the following conclusions hold.

1. For every \(\omega\in\mathcal P_U\), the set \(U\cap Z_\omega\) consists of one point \(u_\omega\), and

\[
 a_{u_\omega}>
 \sum_{z\in Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}}a_z.
\tag{3}
\]

Every positive matching edge of \(p\) in this parity class is incident with \(u_\omega\), and

\[
 t_{u_\omega}
 =\sum_{z\in Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}}a_z.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, source ownership is literal and has no reuse across the saturated vertices in this class.

2. The clipped loss in the dominant-star classes satisfies

\[
 L_U<\frac{10}{9}U_0,
\tag{5}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \frac14L_B-U_0
 <\frac14L_0-\frac{13}{18}U_0
 \leq\frac14L_0.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus it is enough for the registered dispersion-charge inequality to prove the stronger reduced bound using only fully saturated parity classes,

\[
 \sup_{\rho\in\Delta_3}
 \sup_{\substack{B'\subseteq B_0,\ 0<\tau_z\leq a_z\\
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z)}}
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z
 \geq\frac14L_0-\frac{13}{18}U_0.
\tag{7}
\]

Here the common affine slack map is defined equivalently by

\[
 \langle u_i,q^\rho(z)\rangle
 =\ell_i(z)-\rho_i\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_j(z)
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{8}
\]

3. Define \(a_*:=0\) when \(B_0=\varnothing\), and otherwise define

\[
 a_*:=\max_{z\in B_0}a_z.
\tag{9}
\]

The left side of (7) is at least \(a_*\). Consequently the full registered dispersion-charge inequality holds whenever

\[
 L_0\leq4a_*+\frac{26}{9}U_0.
\tag{10}
\]

In particular it holds when \(B_0=\varnothing\). Any counterexample to the registered inequality must satisfy the strict high-dispersion condition

\[
 B_0\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 L_0>4a_*+\frac{26}{9}U_0.
\tag{11}
\]

4. Let \(E_p:=\{z:t_z>0\}\), let

\[
 D_z:=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
 \qquad(z\in E_p),
\]

and use the representation polytope \(\Lambda_m(E_p)\) and support envelope \(\phi_t\) of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb. For \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_p)\), put

\[
 K_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E_p}\lambda_zD_z.
\tag{12}
\]

Then

\[
 K_\lambda\cap K_\mu\ne\varnothing
 \qquad
 (\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_m(E_p)).
\tag{13}
\]

Moreover \(U\cap E_p\ne\varnothing\). If \(c_z\) denotes the centroid of \(C_z\), put

\[
 P_c:=\left\{\sum_{z\in E_p}\lambda_zc_z:
 \lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_p)\right\},
 \qquad
 E_u:=D_u-c_u
 \quad(u\in U\cap E_p).
\tag{14}
\]

For \(w\in\mathbb R^2\), choose \(\lambda^-\) and \(\lambda^+\) minimizing and maximizing, respectively, \(\langle w,\sum_z\lambda_zc_z\rangle\) over \(\Lambda_m(E_p)\). Then the exact nonsaturated-width charge is

\[
 \operatorname{width}_{P_c}(w)
 \leq
 h_{\sum_{u\in U\cap E_p}\lambda^-_uE_u}(w)
 +h_{\sum_{u\in U\cap E_p}\lambda^+_uE_u}(-w).
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the first remaining unsupported bridge after (11) is a volume-weighted conversion of the representation-coefficient width charge (15) into common-\(\rho\) metronoid mass on the balanced-parity family \(B_0\); neither an uncharged aggregation nor reuse of a dominant-star source is needed or permitted.

## Proof

We first prove the exact source-ownership assertion. The matching problem decomposes over parity classes, so the restriction of the global maximum matching \(p\) to one nonempty \(Z_\omega\) is maximum in that class. Put

\[
 A_\omega:=\sum_{z\in Z_\omega}a_z,
 \qquad
 M_\omega:=\max_{z\in Z_\omega}a_z.
\]

Accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 gives the exact matching mass in this class as

\[
 \min\left\{\frac{A_\omega}{4},
 \frac{A_\omega-M_\omega}{2}\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]

Accepted fact f23ff6dd60fbbc37 gives at most one nonsaturated vertex in the class and saturates every other vertex. Suppose the class meets \(U\), and denote that unique vertex by \(u_\omega\). If \(M_\omega\leq A_\omega/2\), then (16) equals \(A_\omega/4\). The sum of all endpoint degrees is then \(A_\omega/2\), equal to the sum of all endpoint capacities \(a_z/2\), so every endpoint is saturated, a contradiction. Hence \(M_\omega>A_\omega/2\).

The vertex attaining \(M_\omega\) must be \(u_\omega\). Otherwise that maximum vertex is saturated, so its degree is \(M_\omega/2\), but every vertex degree is at most the total matching mass \((A_\omega-M_\omega)/2<M_\omega/2\), a contradiction. This proves (3). Formula (16) now gives

\[
 |p|_\omega=\frac{A_\omega-a_{u_\omega}}2.
\tag{17}
\]

The saturated vertices other than \(u_\omega\) have total degree

\[
 \sum_{z\in Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}}d_z(p)
 =\frac12\sum_{z\in Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}}a_z
 =|p|_\omega.
\tag{18}
\]

An edge joining two vertices other than \(u_\omega\) would be counted twice on the left of (18), while an edge incident with \(u_\omega\) is counted once. Since every edge is counted once in \(|p|_\omega\), equality (18) forces the total weight of edges internal to \(Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}\) to be zero. Thus every positive edge is incident with \(u_\omega\), and its degree is \(|p|_\omega\). Multiplying (17) by two proves (4).

For \(z\in B\), at least one normalized threshold is negative. Accepted fact d33ef001609a8cc8 gives \(0\leq\Delta_{i,z}\leq5a_z/9\) for every \(i\), while a negative threshold has zero loss. Therefore

\[
 L_z\leq\frac{10}{9}a_z
 \qquad(z\in B).
\tag{19}
\]

For every \(\omega\in\mathcal P_U\), the clipped set \(B\cap Z_\omega\) omits \(u_\omega\), because \(B\subseteq Z\setminus U\). Equations (3) and (19) give

\[
 \sum_{z\in B\cap Z_\omega}L_z
 \leq\frac{10}{9}
 \sum_{z\in Z_\omega\setminus\{u_\omega\}}a_z
 <\frac{10}{9}a_{u_\omega}.
\tag{20}
\]

Summing (20) over the distinct parity classes and using that their \(u_\omega\) are distinct members of \(U\) proves (5). Substitution into

\[
 \frac14L_B-U_0
 =\frac14L_0+\frac14L_U-U_0
\]

proves (6). The objective for \(B\) is at least the same objective restricted to \(B_0\), so (7) indeed suffices for the registered inequality.

We next prove the one-fiber common-slack lower bound. Fix \(z\in B\), and use the local slack from accepted fact d33ef001609a8cc8,

\[
 \varrho_z:=1-\sum_{i=1}^3\xi_{i,z}
 =-\frac{\sum_i\ell_i(z)}{\kappa_z}.
\tag{21}
\]

Every \(\xi_{i,z}\leq1/3\), and at least one is strictly negative, so \(\varrho_z>0\). Define

\[
 \rho_{i,z}:=
 \frac{1/3-\xi_{i,z}}{\varrho_z}
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{22}
\]

The three entries are nonnegative and sum to one. Equations (8), (21), and the barycentric-coordinate calculation in d33ef001609a8cc8 give

\[
 \beta_{i,z}(q^{\rho_z}(z))
 =\xi_{i,z}+\rho_{i,z}\varrho_z
 =\frac13
 \qquad(i=1,2,3).
\tag{23}
\]

Hence \(q^{\rho_z}(z)=c_z\). The full-mass metronoid is the centroid singleton,

\[
 \mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)=\{c_z\},
\tag{24}
\]

because an admissible density of mass equal to \(a_z\) must equal one almost everywhere. Taking \(B'=\{z\}\) and \(\tau_z=a_z\) proves that the objective in (7) is at least every \(a_z\) with \(z\in B_0\), and hence at least \(a_*\). Combining this with (6) proves (10), and its contrapositive gives (11). When \(B_0=\varnothing\), (6) makes the registered right side strictly negative, while its left side is nonnegative, so the inequality holds.

It remains to prove the structural three-circuit assertions. For \(w\ne0\), the two directions \(w,-w\) with weights \(1/2,1/2\) form a positive balanced family. Since the actual circuit has minimum possible support cardinality three, no such pair is negative. Therefore

\[
 \phi_t(w)+\phi_t(-w)\geq0
 \qquad(w\in\mathbb R^2).
\tag{25}
\]

For arbitrary \(\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_m(E_p)\), definition of the support envelope in accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb gives

\[
 h_{K_\lambda}(w)+h_{K_\mu}(-w)
 \geq\phi_t(w)+\phi_t(-w)
 \geq0.
\tag{26}
\]

If \(K_\lambda\) and \(K_\mu\) were disjoint, strict separation of two nonempty compact convex sets would supply a vector \(w\ne0\) for which

\[
 h_{K_\lambda}(w)<
 \inf_{x\in K_\mu}\langle w,x\rangle
 =-h_{K_\mu}(-w),
\]

contradicting (26). This proves (13).

For every \(u\in U\cap E_p\), the constant density \(t_u/a_u\) proves \(c_u\in D_u\), so \(0\in E_u\). For every saturated \(z\in E_p\setminus U\), one has \(t_z=a_z\) and \(D_z=\{c_z\}\). Consequently

\[
 K_\lambda
 =c_\lambda+
 \sum_{u\in U\cap E_p}\lambda_uE_u,
 \qquad
 c_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E_p}\lambda_zc_z.
\tag{27}
\]

If \(U\cap E_p\) were empty, all \(K_\lambda\) would be singletons. Their pairwise intersection in (13) would force all of them to be the same singleton, making their total intersection nonempty. This contradicts the empty degree-saturated core required for the circuit branch of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb. Hence \(U\cap E_p\ne\varnothing\).

Finally choose \(x\in K_{\lambda^-}\cap K_{\lambda^+}\), which exists by (13). From (27), write

\[
 x=c_{\lambda^-}+e^-
 =c_{\lambda^+}+e^+,
\]

where

\[
 e^-\in\sum_{u\in U\cap E_p}\lambda^-_uE_u,
 \qquad
 e^+\in\sum_{u\in U\cap E_p}\lambda^+_uE_u.
\]

The definitions of \(\lambda^-\) and \(\lambda^+\) now give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{width}_{P_c}(w)
 &=\langle w,c_{\lambda^+}-c_{\lambda^-}\rangle\\
 &=\langle w,e^--e^+\rangle\\
 &\leq
 h_{\sum_u\lambda^-_uE_u}(w)
 +h_{\sum_u\lambda^+_uE_u}(-w),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (15). Every use of an unsaturated body is through one actual Minkowski coefficient in one representation; every matching source in (3)-(4) is owned by one parity class and is never duplicated. This completes the proof.
