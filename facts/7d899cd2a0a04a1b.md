## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope with \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{\mathbf0\}\). Put \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), retaining every boundary lattice point, and write \(N=|D|-1\). This \(N\) is the scalar count denoted \(S\) in the registered subgoal. The locked notation \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) is unchanged.

Use the logarithmic score and full source occurrence convention of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Explicitly, let \(\phi:D\to[0,\infty)\) satisfy \(\min_D\phi=0\) and be convex-admissible: for every finite positive barycentric representation \(\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\), one has \(\phi_{\mathbf J}\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\). Set \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\) on \(D\), set all other lattice weights to zero, and put \(W=\sum_D w_{\mathbf I}\) and \(T=W/2^n\). All logarithms are natural.

For \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) consist of all nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), carrying their unique positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\). For \(\ell\ge0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J},\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,\ell;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing
\text{ for every }E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\text{ with }G_E\le\ell\right\},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ \ell\ge0}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,\ell;w)}{1+\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\ell\) is the cutoff called \(L\) in 488f0a583886b5fc; it is distinct from the single-well amplitude \(L\) below. The predecessor's positive support, total weight, potential, and integral score retain exactly the meanings \(D,W,\phi,\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}\) displayed here.

Assume that on the lattice points of each nonempty proper face of \(P\), the potential \(\phi\) agrees with an affine function on \(\mathbb R^n\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,0;w)
&\ge\frac{W-w_{\mathbf0}}{2^{n+1}},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&\ge\max\left\{w_{\mathbf0},\frac{W-w_{\mathbf0}}{2^{n+1}}\right\}
\ge\frac{W}{1+2^{n+1}}
\ge\frac25\,T.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, for every real amplitude \(L\ge0\), the single-well values \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=L\) for \(\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\) are convex-admissible and satisfy this bound. In this specialization the predecessor's amplitude \(B=\max_D\phi\) equals the present \(L\), and \(W=1+Ne^{-L}\). Define the ordinary closed count depths

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=\min_{\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}
\left|\{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf I\ge0\}\right|,\\
b&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\min_{\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}
\left|\{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}:
\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0\}\right|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every equality point is included in these halfspaces. The complete original score is exactly

\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
=\max\left\{1,\ e^{-L}b,\ \frac{1+e^{-L}a}{1+L}\right\},
\qquad b\ge\frac{N}{2^{n+1}}.
\]

## Proof

First consider a boundary target \(\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\). There is a nonzero supporting normal \(\mathbf v\) and a real number \(\alpha\) such that \(\mathbf v\cdot x\le\alpha\) on \(P\) and \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf J=\alpha\). Because \(\mathbf0\) is interior, \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf0<\alpha\). If \(\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\) with all coefficients positive and of sum one, then every \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf I\le\alpha\), while their barycentric average is \(\alpha\). Hence every source lies in the proper face \(P\cap\{x:\mathbf v\cdot x=\alpha\}\). In particular no source is \(\mathbf0\).

By the affine restriction assumed on the lattice points of this face, every such support has \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}=\phi_{\mathbf J}\), so its logarithmic cost is exactly zero. This is true for every positive barycentric support, including those that are not affinely independent. Consequently all cutoffs \(\ell\ge0\) have the same complete support family at every boundary target.

We record the finite weighted hitting identity used here. For a finite set of points with positive weights and a target in its convex hull, the minimum weight of a set hitting all affinely independent positive barycentric supports of the target equals the minimum original weight of a closed halfspace through the target. Indeed, any such halfspace hits every support, since the barycentric average of its defining scores is zero. Conversely, let \(F\) be a minimum hitting set. The target is outside the convex hull of the retained points. Otherwise choose a representation on retained points with smallest positive support. An affine dependence allows a nonzero coefficient perturbation preserving the barycenter and the sum of the coefficients; moving until a coefficient first vanishes contradicts minimality. Thus the smallest support is affinely independent and would be an unhit support. If some points are retained, strict separation from their compact convex hull gives a closed halfspace through the target containing only deleted points. Its weight is at most the weight of \(F\). If no points are retained, any proper closed halfspace through the target has weight at most that of \(F\). These observations prove the reverse inequality in both cases. There are finitely many hitting sets, so a minimum exists; the argument also produces a minimizing closed halfspace. No equality atom is discarded.

Apply this identity at a boundary target to the weighted set \(D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\). Its barycentric support family is exactly the original one, as proved by the supporting-face argument. Including \(\mathbf0\) in a hitting set cannot help and has positive cost. Therefore, for every boundary target and every \(\ell\ge0\),

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf J,\ell;w)
=\min_{\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\\
\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0}}w_{\mathbf I}.
\]

Choose a linear functional \(\mathbf u\cdot x\) that is nonzero at every point of \(D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\). Such a choice exists because a finite union of proper linear hyperplanes does not cover \(\mathbb R^n\). Explicitly, each nonzero lattice vector \(\mathbf I\) gives a nonzero polynomial \(\sum_{k=1}^n I_k t^{k-1}\); a real \(t\) outside the finitely many roots of these polynomials supplies \(\mathbf u=(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1})\). After replacing \(\mathbf u\) by \(-\mathbf u\) if needed, the set
\(D_+=\{\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf I>0\}\)
has original weight at least \((W-w_{\mathbf0})/2>0\).

Apply the pure integer \(d=0\) specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to the finite Borel measure \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_+}w_{\mathbf I}\delta_{\mathbf I}\) supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\). It gives an integer point \(\mathbf J\) such that every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf J\) has retained measure at least \((\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_+}w_{\mathbf I})/2^n\). This point belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}D_+\): if it did not, strict separation from the compact convex hull would give a containing closed halfspace with retained measure zero, contradicting the positive lower bound.

Since \(D_+\) is finite and every \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf I\) on \(D_+\) is strictly positive, every point in \(\operatorname{conv}D_+\) has strictly positive \(\mathbf u\)-coordinate. Thus \(\mathbf J\ne\mathbf0\). Also \(\operatorname{conv}D_+\subseteq P\), so the original-lattice identity \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) gives \(\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\). The unique-interior hypothesis makes this an original boundary target. Every closed halfspace through it has full boundary weight at least its retained weight. The preceding hitting identity consequently gives
\(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,0;w)\ge(W-w_{\mathbf0})/2^{n+1}\).
This uses the original weights on the retained sites and then the original complete boundary weights; no lattice change or weight renormalization is involved.

The singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) has cost zero and forces every hitting set at target \(\mathbf0\) to contain \(\mathbf0\). Hence \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge w_{\mathbf0}\). Combining this with the boundary target just obtained proves the first maximum lower bound in the statement. Both bounds give \(W=w_{\mathbf0}+(W-w_{\mathbf0})\le(1+2^{n+1})\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\). The same inequality holds with the displayed maximum in place of the score. Finally \(n\ge1\) gives \(2^n/(1+2^{n+1})\ge2/5\), proving all stated weighted lower bounds.

We now check the single-well specialization and its full optimization. Every positive barycentric support at a boundary target lies in a proper face, so all its potentials equal \(L\) and its cost is zero. At target \(\mathbf0\), nonnegativity of all source potentials gives convex-admissibility. Together these statements check convex-admissibility at every site of the complete \(D\); normalization holds because \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\). The same data are the restriction of \(L\) times the gauge \(x\mapsto\inf\{t\ge0:x\in tP\}\): that gauge is zero at \(\mathbf0\) and one at every boundary point. Convexity of the gauge follows by combining representations in \(sP\) and \(tP\) and using convexity of \(P\); when \(L=0\), the potential is simply zero. Thus these are exactly the permitted single-well tables. Accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc applies to their complete lattice support and convex-admissible potentials, with the unchanged hitting convention displayed in the statement.

The boundary hitting identity already proved gives, for each \(\mathbf J\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\) and every \(\ell\ge0\), the count depth at \(\mathbf J\) multiplied by \(e^{-L}\). Its optimized ratio therefore uses cutoff zero, and the maximum over all original boundary targets is exactly \(e^{-L}b\). Applying the weighted boundary lower bound with \(W-w_{\mathbf0}=Ne^{-L}\), and dividing by the strictly positive \(e^{-L}\), yields \(b\ge N/2^{n+1}\).

At the interior target \(\mathbf0\), every support avoiding \(\mathbf0\) has cost exactly \(L\). Thus if \(0\le\ell<L\), every eligible support contains \(\mathbf0\); its singleton is eligible, and the hitting minimum is exactly \(1\). If \(\ell\ge L\), every support is eligible, since every source potential lies in \([0,L]\). Apply the finite weighted hitting identity to the complete set \(D\) with target \(\mathbf0\). Every closed halfspace through \(\mathbf0\) includes its weight \(1\), including all boundary equality points, and the minimum boundary contribution is \(e^{-L}a\). Therefore

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,\ell;w)=
\begin{cases}
1,&0\le\ell<L,\\
1+e^{-L}a,&\ell\ge L.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(L>0\), the first interval optimizes at \(\ell=0\) and the second at \(\ell=L\). For \(L=0\), the first interval is empty, every cutoff has hitting mass \(1+a\), and cutoff zero is optimal; the extra entry \(1\) in the stated maximum is then redundant. Combining these interior cases with the boundary optimization proves the exact formula for every \(L\ge0\), every original target, and every cutoff. The previously proved weighted lower bound supplies the claimed uniform coefficient \(2/5\) for the entire registered class.
