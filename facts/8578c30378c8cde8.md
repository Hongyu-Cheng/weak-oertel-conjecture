## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, with a full-dimensional compact convex fiber $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ at each $z\in P$, $d\ge1$. Fix $p\in P$, $y\in\operatorname{int}K_p$, and a unit vector $u\in\mathbb R^d$. Let $L=\operatorname{span}(P-p)$, $v_z=z-p$, $c_z=u\cdot y-a\cdot v_z$ for $a\in L$, and let $F(a)$ be the sum of the volumes of the closed traces $K_z\cap\{u\cdot x\ge c_z\}$. Write $l_z,r_z$ for the support endpoints in direction $u$ and $w_z(s)=\mathcal H^{d-1}(K_z\cap\{u\cdot x=s\})$ on $[l_z,r_z]$.

At tilt $a$, let $I$ be the indices of proper cuts, $E$ those with $c_z=r_z$, and $B$ those with $c_z=l_z$. Put $g=\sum_{z\in I}w_z(c_z)v_z$, $U=\sum_{z\in E}[0,w_z(r_z)v_z]$ and $V=\sum_{z\in B}[0,w_z(l_z)v_z]$, where segment sums are Minkowski sums. For $b_z=h\cdot v_z$, the right derivative is
$$
D^+F(a;h)=g\cdot h+\sum_{z\in E}w_z(r_z)(b_z)_++\sum_{z\in B}w_z(l_z)\min(b_z,0)
=h_{g+U}(h)-h_{-V}(h).
$$
It is nonnegative in every direction exactly when $-V\subseteq g+U$. In positive rank, strict positivity on the unit sphere of $L$ implies a strict local minimum.

A global minimizing tilt can be chosen with a supporting threshold or with $\{v_z:z\in I\}$ spanning $L$. At every support-free local minimum $g=0$. In the spanning alternative with $\dim L>0$, the weights $w_z(c_z)$ on non-own proper fibers normalize to positive coefficients representing $p$ as a convex combination of their bases. Those bases affinely span $p+L$, $p$ is in their relative interior, and the union of supports of the extreme representations on those bases still spans $p+L$.

At a support-free tilt in $d\ge2$, for $t\downarrow0$ the expansion is $F(a+th)=F(a)+t g\cdot h+t^2Q_a(h)/2+o(t^2)$, where
$$
Q_a(h)=-\sum_{\substack{z\in I\\b_z>0}} b_z^2 w'_{z,-}(c_z)
        -\sum_{\substack{z\in I\\b_z<0}} b_z^2 w'_{z,+}(c_z).
$$
A local minimum requires $Q_a(h)\ge0$ for every $h$. If all proper densities are differentiable at their cuts, this is the positive-semidefiniteness on $L$ of $-\sum_{z\in I}w'_z(c_z)v_zv_z^{\mathsf T}$. In $d=1$, $F$ is affine on each support-free trace cell and in positive rank a support-free global minimum can move to a supporting threshold at the same mass.

## Proof

Ordinary section Brunn–Minkowski gives a positive concave function $w_z^{1/(d-1)}$ on the open support interval for $d\ge2$. Thus $w_z$ is continuous there and has finite left and right derivatives at every interior point. At an endpoint, identify the parallel section planes by translation to $u^\perp$. Compactness gives upper containment of nearby sections in every neighborhood of the endpoint face. Their volumes have upper limit at most the volume of that face by continuity of measure from above on closed neighborhoods. In the other direction, joining the entire endpoint face to an interior point gives at level $s$ a translated scaled copy of the face with scale tending to one as $s$ approaches the endpoint. This gives the opposite lower bound. For $d=1$ the fiber is an interval and $w_z=1$ at all levels of the interval, including its endpoints.

Extend the density by zero outside the support interval. Fubini gives the change of the $z$ cap along $a+th$ as the oriented integral $\int_{c_z-tb_z}^{c_z} w_z(s)\,ds$. At a proper cut its linear term is $t b_z w_z(c_z)$. At an upper support only $b_z>0$ contributes and at a lower support only $b_z<0$ contributes. At a strictly saturated trace there is no change for small $t$. This proves the displayed formula and its support-function expression, using $\min(b,0)=-(-b)_+$. The support-function inclusion criterion gives the equivalence. Each scalar integral has error $o(|t b_z|)$ at first order using the corresponding one-sided limit. Finiteness yields an $o(\|\delta a\|)$ error for increments $\delta a$ uniformly in direction. The positive continuous directional derivative then has a positive minimum on the unit sphere when it is strictly positive there, giving the asserted local minimum.

A finite global minimizing tilt exists by 1ec728c26c11b368 with threshold $u\cdot y$, projecting the coefficient to $L$. Suppose the chosen minimizer is support-free and the proper vectors do not span $L$. A nonzero $h\in L$ perpendicular to them fixes every proper threshold. Some saturated fiber has $h\cdot v_z\ne0$, because all the $v_z$ span $L$. Choose the sign of $h$ that moves this fiber toward its support endpoint. The first supporting threshold along that ray is reached in finite time. Before it, all proper thresholds and all saturated statuses are fixed. Continuity of the cap masses gives the same minimum at the first transition even if the transition creates a nonempty null face. If a support-free minimizer is chosen, the first variation is linear in $h$, so both signs give $g=0$. With positive rank and spanning proper vectors there is at least one non-own proper fiber. Dividing $g=0$ by the sum of their positive areas gives the claimed combination. The positive representation puts $p$ in their convex hull, whose affine span is $p+L$ because the vectors span $L$. A proper supporting affine hyperplane through $p$ would require at least one strictly positive gap at a base and therefore a positive averaged gap, contradicting the representation. Finally the positive coefficient vector is a convex combination of vertices of the compact polytope of anchored coefficient vectors. Each positive coordinate occurs in the support of some such vertex, proving the spanning-union assertion.

For the quadratic formula the oriented integral traverses the left side of $c_z$ if $b_z>0$ and its right side if $b_z<0$. Expanding the density on that side to first order and integrating gives the term $-t^2 b_z^2 w'_{z,\pm}(c_z)/2$. When $b_z=0$ the integral vanishes. A support-free local minimum has $g=0$, so a negative quadratic coefficient would contradict local minimality along that ray. If the side slopes agree on every proper cut the formula gives the stated matrix. For $d=1$, all proper densities are identically one, so zero gradient makes the entire support-free cell constant. A nonzero direction in $L$ changes some threshold. One of its signs reaches a first support endpoint in finite time, whether that changed threshold starts proper or saturated. Continuity again gives the same mass at the endpoint.
