## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,\qquad
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\},\qquad
\pi_z:=\frac{w_z}{W}\quad(z\in D).
\]
For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in D}\operatorname{vol}_d
\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}.
\]

Suppose that coefficients \((\eta_z)_{z\in D}\) satisfy
\[
0\leq\eta_z\leq\pi_z,\qquad
\sigma:=\sum_{z\in D}\eta_z>0,\qquad
\sum_{z\in D}\eta_z(z-z_0)=0
\]
for some \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\). Put
\[
I:=\{z\in D:\eta_z>0\},\qquad
q_z:=\frac{\eta_z}{\sigma}\quad(z\in I),
\]
let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\), and put
\[
p:=\left(z_0,\sum_{z\in I}q_zc_z\right).
\]
Then \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and
\[
\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
\mu_C(H)
\geq
\frac{W}{e}\min_{z\in I}\pi_z.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(D)\), define
\[
A_\pi(z_0):=
\max\left\{
\min_{z\in I}\pi_z:
\varnothing\neq I\subseteq D,\
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(I)
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(a>0\), put
\[
D_{\geq a}:=\{z\in D:\pi_z\geq a\}.
\]
Then the maximum in (2) is attained and
\[
A_\pi(z_0)
=
\max\left\{
a\in\{\pi_z:z\in D\}:
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\geq a})
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
There is a set \(I\subseteq D\) with \(|I|\leq n+1\), positive coefficients
\[
q_z>0,\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}q_z=1,\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}q_zz=z_0,
\tag{4}
\]
and \(\min_{z\in I}\pi_z=A_\pi(z_0)\). For
\[
p_I:=\left(z_0,\sum_{z\in I}q_zc_z\right)
\]
one has
\[
\frac1W
\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\p_I\in H}}
\mu_C(H)
\geq
\frac1e A_\pi(z_0).
\tag{5}
\]

If
\[
\tau:=
\inf_{u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}}
\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0}}\pi_z>0
\tag{6}
\]
is the marginal closed-halfspace depth of \(z_0\), and
\[
L_\pi(a):=\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\\pi_z<a}}\pi_z,
\tag{7}
\]
then
\[
L_\pi(a)<\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_\pi(z_0)\geq a.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, for \(N:=|D|\),
\[
A_\pi(z_0)\geq\frac{\tau}{N},
\qquad
\frac1W
\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\p_I\in H}}
\mu_C(H)
\geq\frac{\tau}{eN}
\tag{9}
\]
for a suitable \(p_I\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

The \(N\)-dependence in this \(A_\pi\)-branch is genuine. For any \(R\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
D_R:=\{-R,\ldots,R\}^n,\qquad N_R:=(2R+1)^n,
\qquad \pi_z:=\frac1{N_R},\qquad z_0:=0.
\]
Then
\[
\tau=\frac{N_R+1}{2N_R},
\qquad
A_\pi(0)=\frac1{N_R}.
\tag{10}
\]
This marginal is realized by the convex body
\[
C_R=[-R,R]^n\times K
\]
for any full-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\). If \(K=-K\), the normalized mixed halfspace depth of \((0,0)\) is \(1/2\), while (5) supplies only \(1/(eN_R)\).

## Proof

Apply accepted fact `ecdcb5a24b2eac2c` with the displayed coefficients \(\eta_z\). It proves that \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and, for every closed halfspace \(H\) whose boundary contains \(p\), its fiber-capture fractions
\[
r_z(H):=
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}}{w_z}
\]
satisfy
\[
\sum_{z\in I}r_z(H)\geq\frac1e.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(H\) contains \(p\) but its boundary does not, translate its boundary parallel toward \(p\) to obtain a closed halfspace \(H'\subseteq H\) whose boundary contains \(p\). Fiberwise monotonicity gives \(r_z(H)\geq r_z(H')\), so (11) holds for every closed halfspace containing \(p\).
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_C(H)
&=W\sum_{z\in D}\pi_zr_z(H)\\
&\geq W\sum_{z\in I}\pi_zr_z(H)\\
&\geq W\left(\min_{z\in I}\pi_z\right)
\sum_{z\in I}r_z(H)\\
&\geq\frac{W}{e}\min_{z\in I}\pi_z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves (1). Since \(\eta_z\leq\pi_z\), (1) is at least as strong as the accepted consequence involving \(\min_{z\in I}\eta_z\), and it can be strictly stronger when the common scale \(\sigma\) is small.

The set \(D\) is finite because the projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded. Thus only finitely many nonempty supports \(I\subseteq D\) occur in (2), so its maximum is attained. If \(z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(I)\) and \(\min_{z\in I}\pi_z\geq a\), then \(I\subseteq D_{\geq a}\) and hence
\[
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\geq a}).
\]
Conversely, if \(z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\geq a})\), a finite convex representation of \(z_0\) has a positive support \(I\subseteq D_{\geq a}\), and therefore
\[
\min_{z\in I}\pi_z\geq a.
\]
Since the sets \(D_{\geq a}\) change only when \(a\) crosses one of the finitely many values \(\pi_z\), this proves (3), including the boundary convention \(\pi_z\geq a\).

Let \(a=A_\pi(z_0)\). By (3),
\[
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\geq a}).
\]
Choose a convex representation of \(z_0\) by points of \(D_{\geq a}\) having minimal positive support \(I\). If \(|I|>n+1\), those points are affinely dependent, so there are real numbers \((\alpha_z)_{z\in I}\), not all zero, with
\[
\sum_{z\in I}\alpha_z=0,\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}\alpha_zz=0.
\]
Writing the positive coefficients of the representation as \(q_z\), replace them by
\[
q'_z=q_z-t\alpha_z,\qquad
t:=\min_{\substack{z\in I\\\alpha_z>0}}\frac{q_z}{\alpha_z}.
\]
The set over which the minimum is taken is nonempty because the nonzero \(\alpha_z\) sum to zero. The coefficients \(q'_z\) are nonnegative, still sum to one, still represent \(z_0\), and at least one vanishes, contradicting minimality. Hence \(|I|\leq n+1\), (4) holds, and
\[
\min_{z\in I}\pi_z\geq a.
\]
The reverse strict inequality would contradict the maximality in (2), so equality holds. Choose any
\[
0<\sigma\leq\min_{z\in I}\frac{\pi_z}{q_z}
\]
and set
\[
\eta_z:=\sigma q_z\quad(z\in I),\qquad
\eta_z:=0\quad(z\notin I).
\]
Then \(0\leq\eta_z\leq\pi_z\), its total mass is \(\sigma\), and
\[
\sum_{z\in D}\eta_z(z-z_0)
=\sigma\left(\sum_{z\in I}q_zz-z_0\right)=0.
\]
The point \(p\) in (1) is exactly \(p_I\), and (1) proves (5). This scaling step also shows that the capacities impose no further restriction on a fixed finite convex representation.

We next prove (8). Suppose instead that
\[
z_0\notin\operatorname{conv}(D_{\geq a}).
\]
Strong separation from the finite compact convex hull gives a nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^n\) such that
\[
u^\top z_0>
\sup_{z\in D_{\geq a}}u^\top z.
\]
Therefore the closed halfspace
\[
\{z\in\mathbb R^n:u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
contains none of \(D_{\geq a}\). Its \(\pi\)-mass is at most \(L_\pi(a)\), whereas (6) says its mass is at least \(\tau\). Thus \(L_\pi(a)\geq\tau\). The contrapositive proves (8).

Take \(a=\tau/N\). Every term in \(L_\pi(a)\) is strictly less than \(\tau/N\), and there are at most \(N\) terms, so
\[
L_\pi(\tau/N)<\tau.
\]
Equations (8) and (5) give (9).

Finally consider the uniform box. Every positive atom has weight \(1/N_R\), so every nonempty support in (2) has minimum weight \(1/N_R\). Since \(0\in D_R\), this proves
\[
A_\pi(0)=\frac1{N_R}.
\]
The distribution is centrally symmetric and has an atom of mass \(1/N_R\) at the origin. Hence every closed halfspace through the origin contains the origin and at least one member of every nonzero pair \(\{z,-z\}\), so its mass is at least
\[
\frac1{N_R}+\frac{N_R-1}{2N_R}
=\frac{N_R+1}{2N_R}.
\]
Choose a vector \(u\) with \(u^\top z\neq0\) for every nonzero \(z\in D_R\). The halfspace \(\{z:u^\top z\geq0\}\) then contains the origin and exactly one member of every nonzero pair, so the lower bound is attained and (10) follows.

For \(C_R=[-R,R]^n\times K\), precisely the fibers indexed by \(D_R\) have positive \(d\)-volume, and they all equal \(K\), so the marginal is the stated uniform distribution. If \(K=-K\), the normalized mixed measure is centrally symmetric about \((0,0)\); every halfspace containing \((0,0)\) has mass at least \(1/2\), and a halfspace through \((0,0)\) determined by a generic continuous-coordinate normal has boundary of mixed measure zero and mass exactly \(1/2\). Thus the actual depth is \(1/2\), whereas (5) gives \(1/(eN_R)\), which proves the claimed calibration.
