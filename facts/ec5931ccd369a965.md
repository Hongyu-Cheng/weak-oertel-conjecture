## Statement

Use the triangle product, actual unique deepest mark and three-cap balanced menu of the positive-width triangle-product singleton theorem. Attach the outward normal to each fiber edge and let $\eta$ be the sum of the four edge arclength measures on this marked bundle. The boundary profile $W_{\epsilon,h}$ is the average of the three cap kernels. Let $W^0$ be the own-fiber average of the three ordinary canonical vertex caps at the centroid, and let $\eta_{\epsilon,h}$ be the maximum over own edges of the fraction of edge length on which the own restriction of $W_{\epsilon,h}$ differs from $W^0$. Then $\eta_{\epsilon,h}\to0$. For every finite nonnegative normal-dependent field $\Phi(n,z,x)$ convex in $(z,x)$ at fixed normal and with positive denominator,
$$
\frac{\int\Phi W_{\epsilon,h}\,d\eta}{\int\Phi\,d\eta}
\le\frac{13}{36}+\frac{\eta_{\epsilon,h}}2
<\frac49
$$
for all sufficiently small positive $\epsilon,h$. The supremum tends to $13/36$, as does the normalized smooth depth. This applies in particular to the actual tied projective-energy buffer of 32418f8d50e792af on the same product.

## Proof

By the singleton theorem the own smooth minimizing direction and its cyclic orbit approach the three ordinary canonical directions, up to relabeling. At each edge, each limiting margin either has constant nonzero sign or has its zero at edge parameter $1/3$ or $2/3$ with nonzero slope. Uniform convergence of the normalized margins at $g$, together with the vanishing width and buffer, implies that the smooth profile agrees with $W^0$ outside bands whose normalized total lengths tend to zero on all edges. More explicitly, off a shrinking neighborhood of these zeros the limiting margins have absolute value larger than the sum of the uniform margin error and $h$, so every ramp is saturated at its limiting indicator. The disagreement set $E$ on each edge thus lies in parameters $[1/4,3/4]$ for small $\epsilon,h$.

Parameterize an edge by $t\in[0,1]$ and let $F(t)$ be the nonnegative convex restriction of the own field at the edge's fixed normal. For $t\in[1/4,3/4]$, Jensen on $[t-1/4,t+1/4]$ gives $F(t)\le2\int_0^1F(s)\,ds$. It follows that $\int_EF\le2\eta_{\epsilon,h}\int_0^1F$. On the edge $W^0=1/3+(1/3)\mathbf1_{[1/3,2/3]}$ up to endpoints. Integrating the midpoint convexity inequality
$$
2F(t+1/3)\le F(t)+F(t+2/3),\qquad 0\le t\le1/3,
$$
bounds the middle-third integral by one third of the full edge integral. Hence $\int_0^1FW^0\le(4/9)\int_0^1F$. Since the two profiles lie in $[0,1]$, the discrepancy contributes at most $2\eta_{\epsilon,h}$ times the full field integral on the edge. Restoring arclength and summing, the own numerator is at most $(4/9+2\eta_{\epsilon,h})I_0$, where $I_0=\int_{\partial K}\Phi(n,0,x)\,dS$.

On each outer fiber the profile is the constant $1/3$ by the saturation of the three matched tilts. Put $R=\sum_j\int_{\partial K}\Phi(n,v_j,x)\,dS$. Since the four fibers and their normal marks coincide at each physical $x$ and $\sum_jv_j/3=0$, physical Jensen gives $I_0\le R/3$. For $I_0+R>0$ we obtain
$$
\frac{(4/9+2\eta_{\epsilon,h})I_0+R/3}{I_0+R}
=\frac13+\left(\frac19+2\eta_{\epsilon,h}\right)\frac{I_0}{I_0+R}
\le\frac{13}{36}+\frac{\eta_{\epsilon,h}}2.
$$
The right side is below $4/9$ when $\eta_{\epsilon,h}<1/6$. For the constant field the own edge averages converge to $4/9$ and the three equal-perimeter outer averages are $1/3$, so the limiting full-cone supremum is exactly $13/36$. The singleton theorem gives normalized depth $(V+q_{\epsilon,h})/(4V)\to13/36$.

For the tied case every real fiber of the product is exactly $K$, so the defining volume and energy in 32418f8d50e792af make the profile independent of the base. That fact gives continuity, nonnegativity, and strict own-interior concavity. Nonnegativity and strict concavity at the midpoint of two distinct interior points around $g$ give positivity at $g$ and hence near it. The own-interior formula in df51254e978fb6a2 gives differentiability. Every barycentric permutation of $K$ preserves its volume. Changing variables by that permutation in the energy integral permutes the admissible affine denominators and preserves their values at corresponding points, so the tied profile is invariant. This checks all hypotheses of the singleton theorem for the actual tied choice.
