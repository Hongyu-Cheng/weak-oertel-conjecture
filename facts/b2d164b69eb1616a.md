## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing$, $q=\dim_H S\ge1$, and $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$, with $M=\mu(S)$. Let $P$ be the bases of the $q$-dimensional integer fibers, and $k$ their number of occupied coordinate parities. Join each distinct same-parity pair of bases in $P$ to its integer midpoint in one hyperedge; the midpoint also belongs to $P$. Write $c(P)$ for the number of connected components.

Suppose $r$ original closed ambient halfspaces of positive $\mu$-mass cover $S$ up to $\mu$-null sets and have pairwise $\mu$-null intersections. Suppose also that no one of them has full $\mathcal H_q$-measure on any fiber over $P$. On each positive fiber exactly two traces have positive measure; they are complementary cuts and their unordered pair of original cap indices is constant on each component. In particular $r\le2c(P)\le2k$. If $|P|>k$, then $r\le2(k-1)$. For equal actual cap mass $D$, one has $M=rD$ and $D\ge M/(2c(P))\ge M/(2k)\ge \Gamma_qM/k$, where $\Gamma_q=(q/(q+1))^q$.

## Proof

There are finitely many integer fibers. A nonempty convex fiber has Hausdorff dimension equal to its affine dimension, and the maximal-dimensional ones carry all $\mu$-mass, measured by intrinsic $q$-volume. The partition hypotheses thus hold on each $K_z$ for $z\in P$. A finite closed almost-everywhere cover is pointwise on that fiber, since a relatively open missed neighborhood has positive intrinsic volume. On the affine plane of each positive fiber use the local argument of dc2f64b066063869 with unit weights and constant incidence one. No full trace is allowed, so that argument yields two opposite restricted halfspaces on a common cutting hyperplane through the relative interior. They cover the plane, and any third positive trace would overlap one of the two in positive measure. Denote the two labels by $I_z$ and choose $x_z$ on the common cutting plane in the relative interior of $K_z$. Their ambient margins vanish at $(z,x_z)$; all others are strictly negative there. Indeed, any margin vanishing at a relative interior point either cuts positive volume or vanishes on the entire fiber plane, in either case contradicting a null trace.

Take distinct same-parity $z,w\in P$ and their integer midpoint $h$. Actual convexity gives $(K_z+K_w)/2\subseteq K_h$. The Minkowski average has affine direction space the sum of the two $q$-dimensional directions, and $K_h$ has dimension at most $q$. Hence both directions agree, $K_h$ has this same direction and dimension $q$, and $h\in P$. An intrinsic ball around each marked point averages to an intrinsic ball around $(x_z+x_w)/2$ in $K_h$. At this midpoint all margins outside $I_z\cap I_w$ are strictly negative, and all in that intersection vanish. No common label would leave the point uncovered. Exactly one common label would leave a relative open half-ball uncovered: all other caps remain absent nearby, and the common cap has nonzero normal on the common direction space because it is a proper positive cut in a source fiber. Thus $I_z=I_w$. Both of those labels cut positive volume in the middle ball and the local two-trace conclusion gives $I_h=I_z=I_w$.

Every positive global cap appears on some positive fiber. The pair of labels is constant along hyperedges, so $r\le2c(P)$. Every parity lies in a single component, proving $c(P)\le k$. If equality holds the assignment of parities to components is bijective, so each component is of one parity and closed under pairwise midpoints. A closest distinct pair would give a midpoint at half their separation, contradicting finiteness and the minimum separation. Thus all components are singletons and $|P|=k$. With common mass $D$, null overlaps and full measured coverage give $M=rD$; use the two component bounds and $(1+1/q)^q\ge2$ for the last inequality.
