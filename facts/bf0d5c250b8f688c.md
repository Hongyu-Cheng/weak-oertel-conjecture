## Statement

Retain the canonical hard-branch hypotheses, objects, and normalizations of accepted facts 036a276c54ec5e66, 3016926516cc39df, and 9c47a4888e8a2947. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
and \(Q>0\) is the total mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir \(r\) in accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66. The canonical hard ledger gives
\[
 D<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 n\geq2.
\tag{1}
\]

Retain the globally endpoint-disjoint same-parity dyadic matching \(\mathcal M_2\), its pooled endpoint weights \(\tau_y\), and its matching weights
\[
 t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal M_2).
\]
Write
\[
 S_2:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}t_e.
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(S_2\) is the mass of the derived dyadic matching, while \(Q\) is the mass of the upstream canonical reservoir. They are different objects; the only comparison used below is
\[
 S_2>\frac{59}{200}Q.
\tag{3}
\]
Every matching endpoint satisfies
\[
 \tau_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n},
 \qquad
 t_e<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{4}
\]

Use the weighted affine-frustration objects of accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d, with its matching weights denoted by \(t=(t_e)\). Let \(s_*\) be one fixed primal optimizer, let
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}
 :=\{e\in\mathcal M_2:s_*(m_e)\notin L_e\},
\tag{5}
\]
and let \(R_C(t)\) be the maximum exactly affine-retainable \(t\)-mass. Put \(Y_2:=\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal M_2}e\). Retain the optimizer face \(\mathcal O(t)\), its load-minimal exposed face \(\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\), and the edge and endpoint loads from accepted fact f78198b3e936e0b3:
\[
 \omega_e(v):=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 \sigma_y(v):=h_{D_y}\!\left(\frac{v_{e(y)}}2\right),
 \qquad
 T_*(v):=\sum_{y\in Y_2}\sigma_y(v)=\sum_e\omega_e(v).
\tag{6}
\]
For \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\), put
\[
 k(v):=|\{e\in\mathcal M_2:\omega_e(v)>0\}|,
 \qquad
 Y_+(v):=\{y\in Y_2:\sigma_y(v)>0\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Then the fixed block (5) is saturated throughout the whole load-minimal face:
\[
 \boxed{\omega_e(v)=t_e>0
 \quad(e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}},\ v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)).}
\tag{8}
\]
Moreover, uniformly for every \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\),
\[
 \boxed{
 T_*(v)>
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q,}
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 k(v)>
 2^n\left(118-\frac4{\gamma_d}\right).}
\tag{10}
\]
In fact, the number of edges in the single fixed saturated block already satisfies the right-hand side of (10).

For \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\), define the positive stress-centroid ranks
\[
 \rho_c(v):=\dim\operatorname{aff}Y_+(v),
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 r_c(v):=
 \min_{a:\operatorname{aff}Y_+(v)\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_y-a(y):y\in Y_+(v)\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 r_c(v)+1>
 (236\gamma_d-8)\,2^{n-\rho_c(v)}.}
\tag{13}
\]

Finally, let \(v\) be any dual-feasible stress, not necessarily optimal or load-minimal, and let \(\mathfrak F_*(v)\) be the stress-frame maximum in the basis, pivot, and nontrivial-partition convention of accepted fact d0338841f89a9c80. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak F_*(v)<
 \left\lfloor\frac{(n+2)^2}{4}\right\rfloor
 \frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.}
\tag{14}
\]

In the exact diagnostic cell \(n=3,d=200\), every \(v\in\mathcal V_{\min}(t)\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}|\geq858,
 \qquad k(v)\geq858,
 \qquad |Y_+(v)|=2k(v)\geq1716,}
\tag{15}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_c(v)\geq2,
 \qquad
 \rho_c(v)=2\Longrightarrow r_c(v)\geq158,
 \qquad
 \rho_c(v)=3\Longrightarrow r_c(v)\geq79,}
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak F_*(v)<\frac{3Q}{1600},
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*(v)}<\frac7{1000}.}
\tag{17}
\]
No assertion above proves the high-support stress-frame inequality, produces the unsupported subset in ADVANCES, or proves the locked weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

Accepted fact 3016926516cc39df gives (3) and the first strict inequality in (4); the second follows from \(t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}\). Accepted fact 9c47a4888e8a2947 shows that \(t\) is a nonzero free off-diagonal same-parity half-metronoid matching and gives the lossless identity in (6). The hard ledger (1) is the inequality \(\mathcal A(C)<Q/100\) in accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66, applied to its distinct nonnegative summands \(D\) and \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\); that fact also gives \(n\geq2\).

We first bound the mass missed by the fixed primal optimizer. By the definition and attainment statement for \(R_C(t)\) in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d, the \(t\)-mass of the edges hit by \(s_*\) is at most \(R_C(t)\). Hence
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}t_e
 \geq S_2-R_C(t).
\tag{18}
\]
Accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 gives \(D\geq\gamma_d\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\). The characterization of \(R_C(t)\) in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d says that \(R_C(t)\) is the mass of an affinely realizable submatching, so
\[
 D\geq\gamma_dR_C(t).
\tag{19}
\]
Together with (1), this yields
\[
 R_C(t)<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d}.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (3), (18), and (20) give the strict fixed-block estimate
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}t_e
 >\left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q.
\tag{21}
\]

Accepted fact f78198b3e936e0b3 proves that every edge missed by this one fixed \(s_*\) is saturated by every dual optimizer. This proves (8). The lossless identity (6), nonnegativity of every load, and (21) give
\[
 T_*(v)=\sum_e\omega_e(v)
 \geq\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}\omega_e(v)
 =\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}t_e
 >\left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q,
\]
which is (9). If \(N_{\mathrm{miss}}:=|\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}|\), then (4) and (21) give
\[
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q
 <\sum_{e\in\mathcal E_{\mathrm{miss}}}t_e
 <N_{\mathrm{miss}}\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\]
Since \(Q>0\),
\[
 N_{\mathrm{miss}}>
 400\,2^n\left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)
 =2^n\left(118-\frac4{\gamma_d}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
Every edge of this block has positive load by (8), so \(k(v)\geq N_{\mathrm{miss}}\), proving (10).

We next prove the centroid-rank screen. Positive edge load is equivalent to two positive endpoint loads in the endpoint-disjoint matching, as proved in accepted fact 844dca4a76115be3; in particular, (9) makes \(Y_+(v)\) nonempty. The whole-support centroid selector in that accepted fact gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 8\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_c(v)}
 \frac{T_*(v)}{r_c(v)+1}.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (1) and (23), then using (9), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_c(v)+1
 &>800\gamma_d\frac{T_*(v)}Q\,2^{n-\rho_c(v)}\\
 &>(236\gamma_d-8)\,2^{n-\rho_c(v)},
 \end{aligned}
\]
which is (13).

It remains to prove the frame cap. If there is no admissible frame, then \(\mathfrak F_*(v)=0\), and (14) follows because \(Q>0\). Otherwise fix an admissible same-parity affine basis
\[
 B=\{y_0,\ldots,y_n\}\subseteq Y_+(v)
\]
and order it with
\[
 M:=\sigma_{y_0}(v)=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\sigma_{y_i}(v)>0.
\]
For a nontrivial partition \(I\mathbin{\dot\cup}J=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), write \(j:=|I|\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). Since every basis load is at most \(M\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_*(B;I,J)
 &=\frac{
  (M+\sum_{i\in I}\sigma_{y_i})
  (M+\sum_{j'\in J}\sigma_{y_{j'}})}M\\
 &\leq(j+1)(n-j+1)M.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
The edgewise lossless identity in accepted fact 9c47a4888e8a2947 gives
\[
 M\leq\omega_{e(y_0)}(v)\leq t_{e(y_0)}
 <\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{25}
\]
For integer \(1\leq j\leq n-1\),
\[
 (j+1)(n-j+1)
 =\frac{(n+2)^2-(2j-n)^2}{4}
 \leq\left\lfloor\frac{(n+2)^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (24)--(26), followed by maximization over all frames and partitions, prove (14). Notice that the strict sign in (14) comes from the strict dyadic cap in (4), not from replacing a floor by a real maximum.

We finish with a wholly rational audit of the \(n=3,d=200\) conclusions. First,
\[
 \frac1{\gamma_{200}}=\left(1+\frac1{200}\right)^{200}<e.
\tag{27}
\]
The standard exponential series gives the explicit rational bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 e
 &=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}\\
 &<\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac1{5^m}
 =\frac83+\frac5{96}
 =\frac{87}{32}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Indeed, for \(k=4+m\), the reciprocal factorial is at most \(1/(24\,5^m)\), and the inequality is strict for some \(m\). Substituting (27)--(28) into (10) gives
\[
 k(v)>8\left(118-\frac4{\gamma_{200}}\right)
 =944-\frac{32}{\gamma_{200}}
 >944-87=857.
\]
Since \(k(v)\) is an integer, \(k(v)\geq858\). Every positive edge has two distinct positive-load endpoints, and different matching edges have disjoint endpoints, so
\[
 |Y_+(v)|=2k(v)\geq1716.
\]
This proves (15) without a decimal approximation.

For the rank conclusions and the ratio in (17), we need the following slightly sharper exact comparison:
\[
 \boxed{\gamma_{200}>\frac{87}{236}.}
\tag{29}
\]
Put \(\delta:=299/120000\). The alternating series for \(\log(1+x)\), truncated after its positive cubic term, gives
\[
 200\log\left(1+\frac1{200}\right)
 <1-\frac1{400}+\frac1{120000}
 =1-\delta.
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(0<\delta<1\), the alternating series for \(e^{-\delta}\), truncated after \(\delta^2/2\), and (28) give
\[
 \left(\frac{201}{200}\right)^{200}
 <e\,e^{-\delta}
 <\frac{87}{32}\left(1-\delta+\frac{\delta^2}{2}\right)
 =\frac{87\cdot28\,728\,329\,401}{921\,600\,000\,000}.
\tag{31}
\]
The last rational number is strictly smaller than \(236/87\), because the exact cross-multiplied difference is
\[
 236\cdot921\,600\,000\,000
 -87^2\cdot28\,728\,329\,401
 =52\,874\,763\,831>0.
\tag{32}
\]
Taking reciprocals in (31)--(32) proves (29).

Consequently,
\[
 236\gamma_{200}-8>79.
\tag{33}
\]
If \(\rho_c(v)\leq1\), then (13) and (33) give \(r_c(v)+1>4\cdot79=316\), contradicting \(r_c(v)\leq d=200\). Hence \(\rho_c(v)\geq2\). If \(\rho_c(v)=2\), then (13) and (33) give \(r_c(v)+1>158\), so the integer \(r_c(v)\) is at least \(158\). If \(\rho_c(v)=3\), they give \(r_c(v)+1>79\), so \(r_c(v)\geq79\). This proves (16), with all strict inequalities and integer roundings explicit.

For \(n=3\), the floor in (14) is \(\lfloor25/4\rfloor=6\), and hence
\[
 \mathfrak F_*(v)<6\frac{Q}{400\cdot8}=\frac{3Q}{1600}.
\tag{34}
\]
Moreover, (29) gives
\[
 \frac1{\gamma_{200}}<\frac{236}{87}<\frac{19}{7},
\]
where the last strict comparison is \(236\cdot7=1652<1653=19\cdot87\). Equation (9) therefore yields
\[
 T_*(v)>
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac{19}{700}\right)Q
 =\frac{15}{56}Q.
\tag{35}
\]
Combining (34)--(35),
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*(v)}
 <\frac{3/1600}{15/56}
 =\frac7{1000},
\]
which completes (17). Every conclusion proved here is a support, rank, or one-frame calibration; none supplies the unsupported single-subset extraction in ADVANCES.
