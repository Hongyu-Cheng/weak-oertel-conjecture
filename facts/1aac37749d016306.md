## Statement

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\) be finite, let \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\) have dimension two, and assume \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^2\). For any \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^2\), define the original weights and probability law by \(w_{\mathbf x}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}\), \(Z=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x}\), and \(p_{\mathbf x}=w_{\mathbf x}/Z\). Suppose \(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}\mathbf xw_{\mathbf x}=\mathbf0\) exactly. Set
\[
Z_{\mathrm{ref}}:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap(-D)}e^{-|\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x|}.
\]
Then the absolute constant \(K=3362e^4\) satisfies \(Z\leq KZ_{\mathrm{ref}}\), and \(\tau_p(\mathbf0)\geq1/(2K)=1/(6724e^4)\).

The exact aliases to accepted fact `1ed6b6d9eb994c90` are \(\nu=p\), \(\boldsymbol\lambda=\boldsymbol\theta\), and \(\mathbf j=\mathbf0\). To accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374` they are \(A=D\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\theta\), with the original law \(p\) unchanged. Thus \(p=\nu_{\mathbf0}=r_{D,\mathbf0}\), the unique maximum-entropy mean-zero row, and \(\tau_p\) has that fact's actual proper closed-halfspace definition. Here \(P\) denotes the support polygon, not the product probability law denoted by \(P\) in that predecessor. All counting uses the full saturated lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\).

## Proof

First \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\). Indeed, its expression as the mean with strictly positive coefficients places it in \(P\). If it were on the boundary, a supporting line would give a nonzero functional nonpositive on \(D\) and zero at the mean. Positivity of every coefficient would force that functional to vanish on every support point, contradicting dimension two. Completeness therefore also gives \(\mathbf0\in D\).

Write \(\mathcal R=P\cap(-P)\) and \(N_{\mathrm{cen}}=|\mathcal R\cap\mathbb Z^2|=|D\cap(-D)|\). The equality follows directly from completeness. This centrally symmetric convex set contains the origin, and \(N_{\mathrm{cen}}\geq1\).

We prove the entropy comparison \(Z\leq41e^2N_{\mathrm{cen}}\). Normalize the outward normals of the finitely many polygon edges to obtain
\[
P=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x\leq1\text{ for every }i\},
\qquad
\mathcal R=\{\mathbf x:|\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x|\leq1\text{ for every }i\}.
\]
The normalization is possible because the origin is interior, so every edge's supporting offset is strictly positive. The edge halfplanes have intersection exactly \(P\): for a point outside \(P\), the ray from the origin first meets its boundary on an edge or vertex, and an edge inequality tight there is violated farther along that ray. The normals span \(\mathbb R^2\), since otherwise their common perpendicular would give an unbounded line in the displayed intersection.

Relabel the normals so that \(|\det(\mathbf u_1,\mathbf u_2)|>0\) is maximal among all pairs. Write \(\mathbf u_i=c_{i,1}\mathbf u_1+c_{i,2}\mathbf u_2\). Taking determinants with \(\mathbf u_2\) and \(\mathbf u_1\), respectively, proves \(|c_{i,1}|\leq1\) and \(|c_{i,2}|\leq1\). Consequently the function \(\Phi(\mathbf x)=|\mathbf u_1\cdot\mathbf x|+|\mathbf u_2\cdot\mathbf x|\) satisfies
\[
\{\mathbf x:\Phi(\mathbf x)\leq t\}\subseteq t\mathcal R
\quad(t\geq0),
\qquad
\mathbb E_p\Phi(\mathbf X)\leq4.
\]
For the inclusion, \(|\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf x|\leq\Phi(\mathbf x)\) for every \(i\); at \(t=0\), independence of the two selected normals gives \(\mathbf x=\mathbf0\). For the expectation, each selected scalar \(\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf X\) has mean zero and is at most one. Its expected absolute value is twice its expected positive part, hence at most two. Adding the two bounds proves the claim.

For every real \(t\geq0\), the exact lattice count satisfies
\[
|t\mathcal R\cap\mathbb Z^2|\leq(2t+1)^2N_{\mathrm{cen}}.
\]
To prove this, take the integer \(m_t=\lfloor2t\rfloor+1>2t\) and partition \(t\mathcal R\cap\mathbb Z^2\) into its \(m_t^2\) residue classes modulo \(m_t\mathbb Z^2\). In a nonempty class fix \(\mathbf z\). The map \(\mathbf x\mapsto(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)/m_t\) is integral and injective on the class. Since \(\mathcal R\) is centrally symmetric and convex, its image belongs to \((2t/m_t)\mathcal R\subseteq\mathcal R\). Each class thus has at most \(N_{\mathrm{cen}}\) points. Finally \(m_t\leq2t+1\), proving the count, including \(t=0\).

Define the reference normalizer on the whole lattice by \(B_{\mathrm{ref}}=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^2}e^{-\Phi(\mathbf z)/2}\). Summing nonnegative integrals and applying the preceding inclusion and count give
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{\mathrm{ref}}
&=\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}
    |\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^2:\Phi(\mathbf z)\leq t\}|\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{N_{\mathrm{cen}}}{2}\int_0^\infty e^{-t/2}(2t+1)^2\,dt
=41N_{\mathrm{cen}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last integral follows by expanding the square and integrating by parts: the integrals of \(1,t,t^2\) against \(\frac12e^{-t/2}\,dt\) are \(1,2,8\), respectively. In particular \(B_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is finite and positive. The new reference law \(g_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf z)=B_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}e^{-\Phi(\mathbf z)/2}\) is a probability on \(\mathbb Z^2\), not the original law or an entropy row.

Nonnegativity of relative entropy against this reference gives
\[
H(p)\leq\log B_{\mathrm{ref}}+\tfrac12\mathbb E_p\Phi(\mathbf X)
\leq\log(41N_{\mathrm{cen}})+2.
\]
For completeness, it holds even though the reference has infinite support: \(-\log u\geq1-u\), applied to \(u=g_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf x)/p_{\mathbf x}\) and summed on \(D\), gives \(\sum_Dp_{\mathbf x}\log(p_{\mathbf x}/g_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf x))\geq1-\sum_Dg_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathbf x)\geq0\). The exact mean-zero entropy identity of `1ed6b6d9eb994c90` is \(H(p)=\log Z\); directly, \(-\log p_{\mathbf x}=\log Z-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x\) and its expectation is \(\log Z\). Exponentiation proves \(Z\leq41e^2N_{\mathrm{cen}}\).

Cauchy--Schwarz now turns this unweighted central count into the required weighted count:
\[
N_{\mathrm{cen}}^2
\leq\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap(-D)}e^{|\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x|}\right)Z_{\mathrm{ref}}
\leq2ZZ_{\mathrm{ref}}.
\]
For the second inequality, \(e^{|s|}\leq e^s+e^{-s}\), and negation permutes \(D\cap(-D)\). Thus the parenthesized sum is at most twice \(\sum_{D\cap(-D)}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}\), which is at most \(2Z\). Combining this with the entropy comparison yields
\[
\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{Z}
\geq\frac{N_{\mathrm{cen}}^2}{2Z^2}
\geq\frac1{2(41e^2)^2}
=\frac1{3362e^4}.
\]

Finally assign the submeasure mass \(Z^{-1}e^{-|\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x|}\) to each \(\mathbf x\in D\cap(-D)\), and zero elsewhere. It is dominated by the original law \(p\), is invariant under negation, and has total mass \(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}/Z\). Every proper closed halfspace containing the origin contains its parallel translate through the origin. Such a translate contains at least one point of each pair \(\{\mathbf x,-\mathbf x\}\), whose two submeasure masses are equal, and retains all boundary points. Its submeasure mass is at least half the total. Taking the infimum in the actual-depth definition of `bf1ff94487296374` proves \(\tau_p(\mathbf0)\geq Z_{\mathrm{ref}}/(2Z)\geq1/(6724e^4)\).
