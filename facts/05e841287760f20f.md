## Statement

Assume the complete triangular-homothetic saturated three-circuit setting and notation of accepted facts `d33ef001609a8cc8` and `6ce65197bbcae680`.  Thus
\[
 C_z=c_z+\kappa_z\mathcal T,
 \qquad
 a_z=A\kappa_z^2,
 \qquad
 A:=\operatorname{area}(\mathcal T),
\tag{1}
\]
the normalized thresholds and cap losses on a fully selected fiber satisfy
\[
 \xi_{i,z}\leq\frac13,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,z}
 =a_z\bigl(2\xi_{i,z}^+-(\xi_{i,z}^+)^2\bigr),
\tag{2}
\]
and \(B_0\) is the clipped family in parity classes disjoint from the nonsaturated set \(U\).  Put
\[
 L_z:=\sum_{i=1}^3\Delta_{i,z}
 \quad(z\in B_0),
 \qquad
 L_0:=\sum_{z\in B_0}L_z,
 \qquad
 U_0:=\sum_{u\in U}a_u.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\rho\in\Delta_3\), retain the common affine slack map
\[
 \langle u_i,q^\rho(z)\rangle
 =\ell_i(z)-\rho_i\sum_{j=1}^3\ell_j(z),
\tag{4}
\]
and let \(M\) be exactly the reduced objective in conclusion 4 of accepted fact `f67005b3d1b63d77`,
\[
 M:=
 \sup_{\rho\in\Delta_3}
 \sup_{\substack{B'\subseteq B_0,\ 0<\tau_z\leq a_z\\
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z)}}
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z.
\tag{5}
\]

For each \(z\in B_0\) with \(L_z>0\), choose an index \(i=i(z)\) for which \(\Delta_{i,z}\) is maximal, and put
\[
 s_z:=1-\sum_{h=1}^3\xi_{h,z},
 \qquad
 r_{h,z}:=\frac{1/3-\xi_{h,z}}{s_z}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(j,k\) are the indices different from \(i\), define
\[
 \bar\rho_i:=0,
 \qquad
 \bar\rho_j:=r_{j,z}+\frac12r_{i,z},
 \qquad
 \bar\rho_k:=r_{k,z}+\frac12r_{i,z}.
\tag{7}
\]
Choose \(h\in\{j,k\}\) with \(\bar\rho_h\geq1/2\), let \(h'\) be the other index, and define the closed interval
\[
 J_z:=\left\{\rho(t):0\leq t\leq\frac1{6s_z}\right\}
 \subseteq\{\rho\in\Delta_3:\rho_i=0\},
\tag{8}
\]
where
\[
 \rho_i(t)=0,
 \qquad
 \rho_h(t)=\bar\rho_h-t,
 \qquad
 \rho_{h'}(t)=\bar\rho_{h'}+t.
\tag{9}
\]
Then the following conclusions hold.

1. The construction is well defined, \(s_z>1/3\), and \(J_z\) has intrinsic edge-coordinate length
\[
 |J_z|=\frac1{6s_z}<\frac12.
\tag{10}
\]
For every \(\rho\in J_z\) and every \(0<r\leq L_z\),
\[
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{r/3}(C_z).
\tag{11}
\]

2. Form the finite graph with vertex set
\[
 \mathcal Z_+:=\{z\in B_0:L_z>0\}
\tag{12}
\]
and an edge \(\{z,z'\}\) exactly when
\(J_z\cap J_{z'}=\varnothing\).  Let \(\pi=(\pi_e)\) be any maximum fractional matching with loss capacities
\[
 \pi_e\geq0,
 \qquad
 d_z(\pi):=\sum_{e\ni z}\pi_e\leq L_z,
 \qquad
 \Pi:=\sum_e\pi_e.
\tag{13}
\]
Then the intervals with positive residual
\[
 R_z:=L_z-d_z(\pi)>0
\tag{14}
\]
have one common point \(\rho_*\in\Delta_3\).  Consequently
\[
 M\geq\frac13\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+}R_z
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{L_0\leq3M+2\Pi.}
\tag{16}
\]
This includes the cases \(B_0=\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Z_+=\varnothing\), an empty residual family, and every threshold boundary \(\xi_{i,z}=1/3\).

3. In particular, if the additional global gluing charge
\[
 \Pi\leq G+U_0
\tag{17}
\]
holds for the matching in conclusion 2, then
\[
 L_0\leq3M+2G+2U_0.
\tag{18}
\]
Under the hard descendant hypotheses in conclusion 4 of accepted fact `f67005b3d1b63d77`, equation (18) implies the registered dispersion-charge inequality stated there.

4. Equation (17) is not proved here.  The following exact facts delimit the remaining step.

(a) The graph in conclusion 2 is not parity constrained.  An edge joining different parity classes has no integral midpoint.  If edges are restricted to same-parity pairs, maximality gives pairwise intersection of residual intervals only within each parity class, not one common point for all residual intervals.

(b) The matching capacities in (13) are loss capacities, not physical source capacities.  The threshold table
\[
 (\xi_{1,z},\xi_{2,z},\xi_{3,z})
 =\left(\frac13,\frac13,-K\right)
 \qquad(K>0)
\tag{19}
\]
satisfies the local clipped threshold restrictions and has
\[
 L_z=\frac{10}{9}a_z.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus (13) can have \(d_z(\pi)>a_z\), whereas a no-reuse physical transport through \(C_z\) cannot.

(c) For a same-parity edge \(e=\{z,z'\}\), put
\[
 w_e:=\frac{z+z'}2\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 H_z:=-\sum_{i=1}^3\ell_i(z)=\kappa_zs_z.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
 c_z=q^{r_z}(z),
\tag{22}
\]
and the full source triangles force at \(w_e\) a triangle centered at
\[
 \frac{c_z+c_{z'}}2
 =q^{\rho_e}(w_e),
 \qquad
 \rho_e:=
 \frac{H_zr_z+H_{z'}r_{z'}}{H_z+H_{z'}}.
\tag{23}
\]
The interval-disjointness relation does not make the values \(\rho_e\) common across matching edges.  Accepted fact `f67005b3d1b63d77` charges two different source pairs when they have one common midpoint, but it does not add the separate singleton or segment bounds for arbitrarily many distinct midpoints.  Therefore (23) is not yet one common-\(\rho\) profile or one affine complete-core profile of total mass \(\Pi\).

No unconditional assertion of (17), (18), or the registered dispersion-charge inequality is part of this lemma.

## Proof

The set of positive lattice fibers is finite, so all interval families and matching linear programs below are finite.

Fix \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\).  Since \(z\in B_0\subseteq B\), at least one threshold is negative.  Equation (2), which is conclusion 1 of accepted fact `d33ef001609a8cc8`, shows that a loss is positive exactly when its threshold is positive.  Hence at most two losses are positive.  The maximizing index in (6) therefore satisfies
\[
 0<\xi_{i,z}\leq\frac13,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{i,z}\geq\frac12L_z.
\tag{24}
\]
The negative threshold and the upper bounds on the other two thresholds give
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^3\xi_{h,z}<\frac23,
\]
which proves \(s_z>1/3\).  Each \(r_{h,z}\) is nonnegative, and
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^3r_{h,z}
 =\frac{1-\sum_h\xi_{h,z}}{s_z}=1.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(\bar\rho\in\Delta_3\), \(\bar\rho_i=0\), and one of the other two coordinates is at least \(1/2\).  Since
\[
 \frac1{6s_z}<\frac12,
\tag{26}
\]
the donor coordinate in (9) remains nonnegative.  The coordinates continue to sum to one, so \(J_z\subseteq\Delta_3\).  Equations (10) and (26) follow directly.

For any common slack \(\rho\), the barycentric coordinates of \(q^\rho(z)\) in \(C_z\) are
\[
 \lambda_{m,z}(\rho)
 :=\xi_{m,z}+s_z\rho_m
 \qquad(m=1,2,3).
\tag{27}
\]
At the initial point \(\bar\rho\), equations (6)-(7) give
\[
 \lambda_{i,z}=\xi_{i,z},
 \qquad
 \lambda_{j,z}=\lambda_{k,z}
 =\frac{1-\xi_{i,z}}2\geq\frac13.
\tag{28}
\]
Along (9), the donor barycentric coordinate decreases by at most
\(s_z/(6s_z)=1/6\), while the recipient increases by the same amount.  Hence, throughout \(J_z\),
\[
 \lambda_{i,z}=\xi_{i,z},
 \qquad
 \min\{\lambda_{j,z},\lambda_{k,z}\}\geq\frac16,
 \qquad
 \max\{\lambda_{j,z},\lambda_{k,z}\}\geq\frac13.
\tag{29}
\]

Apply the simplex transportation theorem in accepted fact `75e9cb08300c59fb`.  If \(\xi_{i,z}<1/3\), the \(i\)-th factor in its relative-area product is \(3\xi_{i,z}\).  Among the other two barycentric coordinates, at most one is below \(1/3\), and any factor from it is at least \(1/2\) by (29).  If \(\xi_{i,z}=1/3\), the \(i\)-th coordinate contributes no factor, and any factor from the other two is again at least \(1/2\).  In both cases the relative-area product is at least
\[
 \frac32\xi_{i,z}.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(C_z\) contains a subtriangle centered at \(q^\rho(z)\) and of area at least
\[
 \frac32a_z\xi_{i,z}.
\tag{31}
\]
From (2),
\[
 \Delta_{i,z}
 =a_z(2\xi_{i,z}-\xi_{i,z}^2)
 \leq2a_z\xi_{i,z}.
\tag{32}
\]
Equations (24), (31), and (32) give
\[
 \frac32a_z\xi_{i,z}
 \geq\frac34\Delta_{i,z}
 \geq\frac38L_z
 >\frac13L_z.
\tag{33}
\]
For any \(0<r\leq L_z\), shrink this subtriangle homothetically about its centroid to area exactly \(r/3\).  Its indicator is an admissible metronoid density, proving (11).  This also handles \(\xi_{i,z}=1/3\) without a limiting argument.

We next prove the matching reduction.  A maximum fractional matching in (13) exists because its feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope.  Suppose \(R_z,R_{z'}>0\) and \(J_z\cap J_{z'}=\varnothing\).  Then \(\{z,z'\}\) is a graph edge, and increasing its matching weight by
\[
 \min\{R_z,R_{z'}\}>0
\]
preserves all vertex capacities and increases \(\Pi\), a contradiction.  Therefore the positive-residual intervals are pairwise intersecting.

We verify that their total intersection is nonempty.  If they all lie on one edge of \(\Delta_3\), this is the one-dimensional Helly property for closed intervals.  Suppose intervals occur on two different triangle edges.  An interval on one edge and an interval on the other can intersect only at the common vertex of those two edges.  Pairwise intersection therefore forces every interval on both edges to contain that vertex.  Finally, intervals cannot occur on all three edges: choosing one from each edge would force each chosen interval to contain both endpoints of its edge, whereas (10) gives length strictly less than \(1/2\).  This proves the existence of \(\rho_*\) in conclusion 2.  If the residual family is empty, choose any \(\rho_*\in\Delta_3\) and the following argument is vacuous.

For every \(z\) with \(R_z>0\), apply (11) at \(\rho_*\) with \(r=R_z\).  Using these masses on the residual support gives one admissible profile in (5), so
\[
 M\geq\sum_{R_z>0}\frac{R_z}{3}
 =\frac13\sum_zR_z.
\tag{34}
\]
Also
\[
 \sum_zd_z(\pi)=2\sum_e\pi_e=2\Pi.
\tag{35}
\]
Zero-loss fibers contribute nothing, and equations (3), (14), (34), and (35) give
\[
 L_0
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+}L_z
 =2\Pi+\sum_zR_z
 \leq2\Pi+3M.
\tag{36}
\]
This proves conclusions 1 and 2, including the empty cases.

If (17) holds, substituting it into (36) proves (18).  The conditional reduction in conclusion 4 of accepted fact `f67005b3d1b63d77` then proves the registered dispersion-charge inequality under its stated hard descendant hypotheses.  This proves conclusion 3.

It remains to verify the exact scope assertions in conclusion 4.  If \(z,z'\) have different parities in any coordinate, their midpoint has a half-integral coordinate, proving (a).  If matching edges are limited to same-parity pairs, the augmentation argument above compares residual vertices only inside the same parity class, so it supplies no cross-class intersection statement.

For (b), substitute (19) into (2).  The first two losses are each \(5a_z/9\), while the negative third threshold has zero loss.  This proves (20).  Hence a degree constrained only by \(L_z\) need not be bounded by the physical area \(a_z\).

For (c), equation (6) and the normalized threshold definition give
\[
 H_zr_{i,z}
 =\kappa_z\left(\frac13-\xi_{i,z}\right)
 =\langle u_i,c_z\rangle-\ell_i(z).
\tag{37}
\]
Comparison with (4) proves (22).  Since every \(\ell_i\) is affine,
\[
 -\sum_i\ell_i(w_e)=\frac{H_z+H_{z'}}2.
\tag{38}
\]
Taking the average of (37) for \(z,z'\) and using (38) proves (23).  Convexity of the genuine body also gives
\[
 \frac12C_z+\frac12C_{z'}\subseteq C_{w_e},
\tag{39}
\]
so (23) is the center of the actual triangle forced by the two source fibers.  Its parameter \(\rho_e\) depends on the edge.  Interval disjointness imposes no equality between the weighted averages in (23).  The common-midpoint conclusion of accepted fact `f67005b3d1b63d77` applies when two such pairs share \(w_e\); its lattice-simplex conclusion applies to one simplex profile.  Neither conclusion permits separate lower bounds on the same supremal capacity \(G\) to be added over unrelated distinct midpoint profiles.  This establishes (a)-(c) and the asserted boundary of the lemma.  In particular, no proof of (17) or of the unconditional registered conclusion has been inserted.
