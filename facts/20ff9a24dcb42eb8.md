## Statement

Let G be a finite oriented loopless graph, with parallel edges and isolated vertices allowed. Write V and E for its vertex and edge sets and N:=|E|. For an edge directed from u to v, let its column in the full signed incidence matrix \(\widehat A\) be \(\mathbf e_v-\mathbf e_u\). A loop in any graph produced below has incidence column \(\mathbf0\). Components are counted in the underlying undirected graph, including isolated vertices. Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r&:=|V|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G),\qquad
 n:=N-r=N-|V|+\#\operatorname{comp}(G),\\
 D&:=\{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E}:\widehat A\mathbf z=\mathbf0\},\\
 Q&:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x},\qquad
 p_{\mathbf z}:=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}/Q
 \quad(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^{E}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Deleting one vertex row from \(\widehat A\) in each component gives a full-row-rank matrix A with the same kernel. This A is the matrix object denoted A in accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2; N, r, n, D, \(\boldsymbol\theta\), Q, p, \(\tau_p\), and \(M_3\) retain its exact meanings and normalizations. In particular, for \(\mathbf z\in D\),
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=
\min_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{E}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\]
where genuine halfspaces have nonzero normals. In a zero-dimensional singleton realization, use normalized depth 1 and capacity depth equal to the singleton's capacity; these agree with the depths in every positive-dimensional ambient embedding.

Perform any finite sequence of the following operations, retaining the indicated orientations and tilts. Each local rule uses the edge incidences and tilt entries of the current graph.

1. Delete a bridge b, retaining the other edge tilts and initially retaining all vertices. The deleted coordinate is reconstructed as \(z_b=0\).

2. At a vertex v incident with exactly two distinct nonloop edges e and f and no other edge, let u and w be their other endpoints, respectively. Let \(\varepsilon_e\) and \(\varepsilon_f\) be their incidence entries at v (1 for an entering edge and -1 for a leaving edge), and put \(\sigma:=-\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f\in\{-1,1\}\). Remove v, e, and f and add one edge g between u and w. When u differs from w, orient g from u to w if \(\varepsilon_e=1\), and from w to u if \(\varepsilon_e=-1\). When u=w, retain g as a loop, with incidence column zero. Its coordinate \(\xi:=z_g\) reconstructs \(z_e=\xi\) and \(z_f=\sigma\xi\), and its tilt is
\(\theta_g:=\theta_e+\sigma\theta_f\). All other coordinates and tilts are unchanged. Degrees count a loop twice; a vertex incident only with one loop is not eligible for this operation.

3. Discard an isolated vertex, without changing the edge-coordinate space or tilts.

Let \(G_*\) be the resulting graph, which need not be loopless, and let \(V_*\) and \(E_*\) be its vertex and edge sets. Let \(\widehat A_*\), \(D_*:=\{\mathbf w\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E_*}:\widehat A_*\mathbf w=\mathbf0\}\), \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\), \(Q_*\), and \(p^*\) be the corresponding objects with these conventions. There are an injective integer linear map \(L:\mathbb R^{E_*}\to\mathbb R^E\) and a coordinate projection \(P:\mathbb R^E\to\mathbb R^{E_*}\), with edge relabeling understood, such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 PL&=I,\qquad
 L(\ker\widehat A_*)=\ker\widehat A,\\
 L(\ker\widehat A_*\cap\mathbb Z^{E_*})
   &=\ker\widehat A\cap\mathbb Z^E,\qquad
 L(D_*)=D,\\
 \boldsymbol\theta_*&=L^T\boldsymbol\theta,\qquad
 Q_*=Q,\qquad p^*_{\mathbf w}=p_{L\mathbf w}
 \quad(\mathbf w\in D_*).
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the reduced incidence constraints give all and only the ternary points corresponding to the original constraints; there are no additional points, multiplicities, or lattice-index factors. The intrinsic cycle rank of \(G_*\) is exactly n.

There is a saturated integral linear chart \(\Phi_*:\mathbb R^n\to\ker\widehat A_*\), meaning a linear isomorphism onto the real kernel whose restriction maps \(\mathbb Z^n\) bijectively onto the integer kernel. With \(\Phi:=L\Phi_*\), both laws have the identical finite probability table on
\(\widehat D:=\Phi_*^{-1}(D_*)=\Phi^{-1}(D)\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\).
The set \(\widehat D\) is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^n\) and satisfies
\(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\).
If \(\widehat p_{\mathbf u}:=p_{\Phi\mathbf u}=p^*_{\Phi_*\mathbf u}\), then, at every corresponding support point,
\[
\tau_p(L\mathbf w)=\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w),\qquad
\tau_p(\Phi\mathbf u)=\tau_{\widehat p}(\mathbf u),\qquad
M_3(p)=M_3(p^*)=M_3(\widehat p).
\]
These are equalities of actual closed-halfspace depths, with all boundary atoms retained. When n=0, each support is a singleton and all three depths and cubic moments equal 1.

Every loop in \(G_*\) is a free ternary coordinate, with its assigned tilt. In particular, a component that is a pure cycle, or a cycle with trees attached, reduces after bridge deletion, isolated-vertex deletion, and series suppression to one loop. Choose a traversal of its cycle that agrees with a selected edge's orientation, and let \(\sigma_e=1\) for an edge oriented along the traversal and \(\sigma_e=-1\) otherwise. The surviving free coordinate \(\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}\) can be chosen so that \(z_e=\sigma_e\xi\) on the cycle, all attached-tree coordinates are zero, and its tilt is \(\sum_{e\text{ on the cycle}}\sigma_e\theta_e\). This also covers a two-edge cycle with parallel edges. A loop created from coincident endpoints is retained even when its vertex has other incident edges.

Finally, let positive original capacities satisfy \(c_{\mathbf z}=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\) for a constant a>0, and put \(c^*_{\mathbf w}:=c_{L\mathbf w}=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w}\). Using the canonical capacity depth \(h_c\) of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2, the total capacity is the same number \(T:=\sum_D c_{\mathbf z}=\sum_{D_*}c^*_{\mathbf w}\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 h_c(L\mathbf w)&=h_{c^*}(\mathbf w)
   =T\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w)=T\tau_p(L\mathbf w),\\
 \sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
   &=\sum_{\mathbf w\in D_*}c^*_{\mathbf w}h_{c^*}(\mathbf w)^3
    =T^4M_3(p^*).
\end{aligned}
\]
No extra scaling is introduced by deletion, subdivision, suppression, or charting.

## Proof

We first verify the incidence and lattice hypotheses. The left nullspace of \(\widehat A\) consists exactly of vertex vectors that are constant on each component: the equation for a nonloop edge says that its two endpoint values agree, and a loop column gives no condition. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}\widehat A=|V|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G)=r\). The sum of the vertex rows in any component is zero. Consequently deleting one row per component does not change the kernel. The retained rows are independent: a relation among them, extended by zero coefficients at the omitted rows, is a vector in the left nullspace, hence constant on each component and zero at its omitted vertex, hence identically zero. These arguments remain valid for all reduced graphs, including loops and isolated vertices.

The full incidence matrix, and hence A, is totally unimodular. Indeed, consider any square submatrix. If a column has no nonzero entry its determinant is zero. If a column has exactly one nonzero entry, expansion along that column reduces the determinant, up to sign, to that of a smaller square submatrix. If every column has two nonzero entries, those entries are 1 and -1, so the sum of its rows is zero and its determinant is zero. Induction, starting with the empty determinant 1, proves that every square minor is 0, 1, or -1. Zero loop columns cause no exception. Thus \(\dim\ker\widehat A=N-r=n\).

Consider a bridge b of the current graph. Its deletion separates its component into two vertex sets U and W with b as the sole edge crossing between them. For every real circulation of this graph, summing the incidence equations over U gives \(\pm z_b=0\), because all other edge contributions cancel or vanish. Hence \(z_b=0\) for every real circulation, not merely for ternary ones. Let \(L_b\) insert a zero at b and let \(P_b\) forget coordinate b. A vector on the remaining edges is a circulation in the graph with b deleted if and only if its zero extension is a circulation in the previous graph. We therefore have \(P_bL_b=I\), an isomorphism of the real kernels, and a bijection of their integer kernels. Zero insertion and deletion also give a bijection of their ternary supports. The deleted tilt contributes \(\theta_bz_b=0\), so it supplies neither a weight factor nor a partition factor. Discarding an isolated vertex merely removes a zero incidence row; the edge maps for that step are identities.

For a series suppression, conservation at v is exactly
\(\varepsilon_e z_e+\varepsilon_f z_f=0\). Both incidence entries are 1 or -1, so this is equivalent, over the reals as well as the integers, to \(z_f=\sigma z_e\), where \(\sigma=-\varepsilon_e\varepsilon_f\). There are no other incident edges at v. Write \(\xi=z_e\). At the remaining vertices the combined incidence contribution of e and f is
\[
 -\varepsilon_e\xi\mathbf e_u
 -\varepsilon_f\sigma\xi\mathbf e_w
 =\varepsilon_e\xi(\mathbf e_w-\mathbf e_u).
\]
This is exactly the contribution of the specified oriented new edge g. If u=w, it is zero, exactly the column of the new loop. Thus the incidence equations at all remaining vertices become precisely the reduced incidence equations, while the equation at v is already satisfied by the reconstruction.

Let \(L_v\) retain every unaffected coordinate and replace the g-coordinate \(\xi\) by the pair \((\xi,\sigma\xi)\) at e and f. Let \(P_v\) retain the unaffected coordinates and use the e-coordinate as the g-coordinate. Then \(P_vL_v=I\); both maps have integer entries, and they restrict to mutually inverse maps between the real kernels and between the integer kernels. If \(\xi\in\{-1,0,1\}\), both reconstructed coordinates are ternary. Conversely, any original ternary circulation satisfies \(z_f=\sigma z_e\), and its projected coordinate is ternary. The unchanged incidence equations impose exactly the reduced constraints. This proves a bijection of complete ternary supports, in both directions, rather than only an embedding of some support points.

For a reconstructed vector, the changed part of its logarithmic weight is
\(\theta_e\xi+\theta_f\sigma\xi=(\theta_e+\sigma\theta_f)\xi\). Hence the stated tilt rule gives exact equality of unnormalized weights. In the coincident-endpoint case, the g-column is zero even if u has other incident edges. Its ternary coordinate is therefore unconstrained and independent of the remaining coordinates under the exponential law. More generally, all loop columns are zero, so the support is the Cartesian product of the nonloop support with one ternary set per loop, and the unnormalized weights factor accordingly. In particular, a loop may not be dropped as though it were a forced-zero edge.

Compose the reconstruction maps of all operations to obtain L, and compose their coordinate projections in the reverse order to obtain P. The stepwise identities imply \(PL=I\). The real-kernel and integer-kernel isomorphisms, and the ternary bijections, also compose. Each coordinate of P is a retained original coordinate with its current edge name, so P remains a coordinate projection. The map L is injective, integer, and has an integer left inverse. Its image lattice has no index defect: if \(m\mathbf z\in L(\mathbb Z^{E_*})\) for a positive integer m and \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^E\), then \(m\mathbf z=L\mathbf a\) implies \(\mathbf a=mP\mathbf z\) and consequently \(\mathbf z=LP\mathbf z\in L(\mathbb Z^{E_*})\). Thus its image lattice is saturated. The integer-kernel equality is the stronger, already established assertion that every original integer circulation is reconstructed from its integer projection.

For completeness, track the cycle rank directly. A bridge deletion changes the edge count from N to N-1, leaves the vertex count fixed, and increases the number of components by one. Thus \(N-|V|+\#\operatorname{comp}(G)\) is unchanged. A series suppression decreases both the edge count and the vertex count by one and leaves the number of components unchanged. Connectivity among surviving vertices is unchanged, since the path through v is replaced by g; if u=w, removal of the degree-two vertex v leaves its surviving neighbor u and replaces the two parallel edges by a loop in the same component. Thus the rank is unchanged in this case as well. Discarding an isolated vertex decreases both the vertex count and the component count by one, again leaving the rank unchanged. Applying these counts step by step proves that the final intrinsic rank is n, including when bridge deletion is followed by removal of isolated vertices. No uniqueness or global normal form for the reduced graph is needed.

A pure cycle of length at least two can be successively suppressed until the last two distinct edges join the same pair of vertices, at which point suppression produces one loop. A component consisting of a cycle with attached trees first loses all tree edges, since each is a bridge; discarded isolated vertices do not affect its remaining cycle. Alternatively, conservation around the cycle directly shows that every circulation has the form \(z_e=\sigma_e\xi\), using the traversal signs in the statement, while the bridge argument forces every attached-tree coordinate to zero. Choosing the selected edge's coordinate as \(\xi\) and retaining it through the successive coordinate projections realizes this identification by the specified suppression rules. The tilt identity becomes \(\sum_e\theta_ez_e=\xi\sum_e\sigma_e\theta_e\), with only cycle edges in the sum. The resulting component therefore has exactly the three weights \(e^{-\sum_e\sigma_e\theta_e}\), 1, and \(e^{\sum_e\sigma_e\theta_e}\). Its normalized law is the free ternary law with this signed total tilt. This handles the two-edge cycle as well as cycles with subdivisions and trees; a single-loop terminal component is retained as a free coordinate.

For the full graph, the stepwise logarithmic-weight identities give \(\boldsymbol\theta_* = L^T\boldsymbol\theta\). Since \(L:D_*\to D\) is a bijection, exact change of the finite summation index yields
\[
 Q=\sum_{\mathbf w\in D_*}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot L\mathbf w}
   =\sum_{\mathbf w\in D_*}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w}=Q_*.
\]
Division by this same partition gives \(p_{L\mathbf w}=p^*_{\mathbf w}\). No independent-product partition, conditional probability, or multiplicity is used.

Now construct the common chart. If n>0, the reduced row-deleted incidence matrix \(A_*\) is integer, totally unimodular, and has full row rank \(|V_*|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G_*)<|E_*|\). Its edge count is positive. Apply the saturated-chart assertion of accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2 with A there equal to \(A_*\), with N there equal to \(|E_*|\), with r there equal to \(|V_*|-\#\operatorname{comp}(G_*)\), and with t there equal to its positive number of distinct column vectors. Its n equals the same intrinsic n proved above; its tilt is \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\). This gives \(\Phi_*\) with an integer coordinate-projection inverse on the kernel, and a full-dimensional complete support \(\widehat D:=\Phi_*^{-1}(D_*)\). The reconstruction isomorphisms imply that \(\Phi:=L\Phi_*\) maps \(\mathbb R^n\) isomorphically onto \(\ker\widehat A\) and maps \(\mathbb Z^n\) bijectively onto its integer kernel. Its inverse on that kernel is \(\Phi_*^{-1}P\), again a coordinate projection. Thus it is a saturated chart of exactly the same type, with \(\Phi^{-1}(D)=\widehat D\). Equality of the finite probability tables follows from \(p_{L\mathbf w}=p^*_{\mathbf w}\). Equivalently, their coordinate tilts agree exactly because \(\Phi^T\boldsymbol\theta=\Phi_*^T\boldsymbol\theta_*\). Full dimension and \(\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=\widehat D\) are the chart properties furnished by the accepted fact. We use no depth lower bound from that fact or from any other predecessor.

If n=0, the real kernels on both graphs are \(\{\mathbf0\}\); hence D and \(D_*\) are each \(\{\mathbf0\}\). The unique map from \(\mathbb R^0\) onto the zero kernel is the saturated chart, with \(\widehat D=\{\mathbf0\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^0\). It is full-dimensional in dimension zero and complete. Every containing genuine halfspace in a positive-dimensional ambient realization has the entire singleton's probability, namely 1. The zero-dimensional convention gives the same answer. Thus the asserted table, depth, and moment equalities hold in this case, including a graph with no edges.

It remains to check actual depth for n>0; we give the restriction-and-lift argument so that neither lower-dimensional restrictions nor boundary atoms are overlooked. For an original closed halfspace \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x\ge b\}\) with \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\) containing \(L\mathbf w\), its inverse image under L has the inequality
\((L^T\mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf y\ge b\). If \(L^T\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\), this is a genuine reduced halfspace containing \(\mathbf w\), with exactly the same atoms and probability as \(D\cap H\). If \(L^T\mathbf a=\mathbf0\), containment implies \(b\le0\), so the restriction is the whole reduced space and has probability 1. Since any depth is at most 1, this constant restriction cannot decrease the minimum. It follows that \(\tau_p(L\mathbf w)\ge\tau_{p^*}(\mathbf w)\).

Conversely, a reduced halfspace \(\{\mathbf y:\mathbf c\cdot\mathbf y\ge b\}\), with \(\mathbf c\ne\mathbf0\), lifts to the original halfspace with normal \(P^T\mathbf c\). This normal is nonzero because \(L^TP^T\mathbf c=(PL)^T\mathbf c=\mathbf c\). Its restriction under L is the given halfspace, exactly, and it contains \(L\mathbf w\) whenever the reduced halfspace contains \(\mathbf w\). The probabilities therefore agree, proving the reverse depth inequality. Finite supports have only finitely many possible intersection subsets with halfspaces, so the minima in these comparisons exist. The use of the same weak inequality \(\ge\) throughout proves equality with all atoms on the bounding hyperplanes included.

The identical argument applies to \(\Phi:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^E\) and its coordinate-projection left inverse, or to \(\Phi_*\) in the reduced ambient space. Restrictions with zero chart normal give the whole chart law, and every genuine chart halfspace lifts via the transpose of the left inverse to a genuine ambient halfspace. Hence both ambient depths equal the actual chart depth. Equality of the probability tables and pointwise depths then gives \(M_3(p)=M_3(p^*)=M_3(\widehat p)\) by reindexing the defining finite sums.

Finally, exact equality of logarithmic weights and the support bijection give \(c^*_{\mathbf w}=c_{L\mathbf w}=a e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf w}\) and the same total capacity T. The normalization in accepted fact 7781e8f7b246c4d2 is \(p_{\mathbf z}=c_{\mathbf z}/T\) and \(h_c(\mathbf z)=T\tau_p(\mathbf z)\). It also follows directly here, since every halfspace's capacity sum is T times its probability sum; minimizing preserves this factor. Apply this on both graphs and use the established equality of depths. Reindexing once more gives the capacity-depth sum equality and its value \(T^4M_3(p^*)\). Thus exactly the original T, with no lattice index or multiplicity factor, scales every transferred depth and cubic-moment assertion.
