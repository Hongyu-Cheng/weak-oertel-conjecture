## Statement

Let $d\ge1$. Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, let each $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ for $z\in P$ be a convex body, and assume $\sum_z\lambda_z K_z\subset K_p$ whenever $\lambda_z\ge0$, $\sum_z\lambda_z=1$, and $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$. Set $Q=\operatorname{conv}P$ and $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$. For the positive attained energy
$E_K(y)=\min_a\int_K[1+a\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-1}\,dx$ over denominators positive on $K$, put $F(z,x)=E_{C_z}(x)^{1/d}$ on $\Omega_C=\{(z,x):z\in Q,\ x\in\operatorname{int}C_z\}$.

Fix $p\in P$ and $y\in\operatorname{int}K_p$, let $a_y$ be the optimizer at this mark in $K_p$, and put $F_0=F(p,y)$. The function $F$ is locally Lipschitz relative to $\operatorname{aff}(Q)\times\mathbb R^d$ on its domain near $(p,y)$, and there is a finite $B\in\operatorname{span}(Q-Q)$ such that
$$
F(z,x)\le F_0\left[1+B\cdot(z-p)+\frac{d+1}{d}a_y\cdot(x-y)\right]\qquad((z,x)\in\Omega_C).
$$
Every supergradient at $(p,y)$ has continuous component $\frac{d+1}{d}F_0a_y$. The associated affine chart
$$
L(z,x)=1+a_y\cdot(x-y)+\frac{d}{d+1}B\cdot(z-p)
$$
is at least $1/(d+1)$ on $C$ and strictly greater on $\Omega_C$. The base point $p$ may lie on the relative boundary of $Q$. For arbitrary compact convex ambient bodies a finite boundary-base energy supergradient can fail, even with full-dimensional ball fibers.

## Proof

Grouping convex combinations by sampled bases and using the anchored hypothesis gives $C_p=K_p$ at every $p\in P$. The joint energy theorem for compact convex fibers gives concavity on the convex domain $\Omega_C$. The canonical-fiber Hausdorff lemma gives a common ball $y+R B_d$ with $R>0$ in every nearby $C_z$, as well as a Lipschitz estimate for $d_H(C_z,C_{z'})$. By shrinking the base and mark neighborhoods, every marked point $x$ in the resulting product neighborhood has an inball of one fixed radius $r>0$ centered at $x$ in its corresponding $C_z$. All bodies are contained in a fixed bounded ball.

The envelope and denominator lemma gives the lower bound $1/(d+1)$ for the optimizer's denominator on its own body. Every optimizer has norm at most $1/r$ by positivity on its marked inball. For two nearby pairs $(K,x)$ and $(K',x')$ of fibers and marks in the product neighborhood, put $\eta=d_H(K,K')+\|x-x'\|$. A parameter minimizing at either pair is admissible at both pairs once $\eta/r<1/[2(d+1)]$. In fact its denominators at either mark are at least $1/[2(d+1)]$ on $K\cup K'$, by matching any point to the minimizing parameter's original body within Hausdorff distance and accounting for the displacement of the mark. The same lower bound persists when interpolating the two marks with that parameter fixed. Thus the inverse-power integrands and their derivatives in the mark are uniformly bounded on this union for both tested parameters.

The symmetric-difference volume is bounded by a constant times $d_H(K,K')$. Both bodies contain $y+R B_d$, and for $\delta=d_H(K,K')$ convexity gives
$K+\delta B_d\subset y+(1+\delta/R)(K-y)$ and the analogous inclusion for $K'$. The Hausdorff inclusions in both directions, bounded volumes and bounded $\delta$ then give the estimate by subtracting the original volumes from the dilated ones. For a parameter minimizing at $(K,x)$, comparing its two integrals over the common and differing parts gives $E_{K'}(x')-E_K(x)\le c\eta$ with a uniform constant $c$. Testing the other minimizer gives the reverse inequality. The energies have a uniform positive lower bound because each denominator has mean one on the marked inball and its inverse power has average at least one there by Jensen. Taking the $d$-th root preserves the local Lipschitz bound. Together with the canonical-fiber Hausdorff estimate, this proves Lipschitz regularity on a sufficiently small closed product neighborhood $D_0$ of $(p,y)$ relative to $Q\times\mathbb R^d$.

Take $f=-F$ on the compact convex set $D_0$, with Lipschitz constant $L_0$. In affine coordinates on $\operatorname{aff}(Q)\times\mathbb R^d$, the function
$$
g(v)=\inf_{w\in D_0}\bigl[f(w)+L_0\|v-w\|\bigr]
$$
is finite and convex by joint convexity of the expression in $(v,w)$. The Lipschitz inequality and the choice $w=v$ show that it agrees with $f$ on $D_0$. A finite convex function on the whole finite-dimensional space has a subgradient at $(p,y)$. Negating it gives a local affine upper support to $F$. Any segment from $(p,y)$ to another point of $\Omega_C$ has a short initial interval in $D_0$, and concavity along the segment extends the upper bound to the other endpoint. Both signs of every continuous-coordinate displacement are available at $(p,y)$, so every such support has continuous component equal to the own-fiber derivative $\frac{d+1}{d}F_0 a_y$. Dividing the base component by $F_0$ gives the stated $B$ in the relative base coordinates.

The bracket in the upper support is positive wherever $F>0$. The chart $L$ is $1/(d+1)$ plus $d/(d+1)$ times this bracket, proving the strict lower bound on $\Omega_C$. Every real fiber is full-dimensional, so its interior is dense in it. Applying continuity of the affine chart in each fiber gives the bound on all of $C$.

For the failure on general convex ambient bodies take $C=\{(z,x):0\le z\le1,\ \|x\|\le1+\sqrt z\}$. Concavity of the radius and the triangle inequality give convexity. On each fiber centered at zero, Jensen gives energy equal to the ball volume, attained at the zero parameter. Thus $F(z,0)=|B_d|^{1/d}(1+\sqrt z)$. Any affine upper support touching at $(0,0)$ would bound $\sqrt z$ by a finite multiple of $z$ for all small positive $z$, which is impossible. The canonical hull of its sampled endpoint fibers at $0,1$ has radius $1+z$. The constant chart $L=1$ is positive in both constructions.
