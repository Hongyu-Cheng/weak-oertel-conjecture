## Statement

Use the admissibility and notation of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. Explicitly, an admissible table in integer dimension \(n\ge1\) is a nonzero, nonnegative, finitely supported table \(w=(w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) with a finite-valued extension \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing at every lattice point, including every zero, and satisfying \(f((1-\theta)x+\theta y)\ge f(x)^{1-\theta}f(y)^\theta\) for all real \(x,y\) and \(0<\theta<1\). A zero endpoint makes the right side zero. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=\sum_z w_z,& M&=\max_z w_z,& p_z&=w_z/W,\\
H_j(w)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},&
H_*(w)&=\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^n}H_j(w),\\
G(w)&=\log\frac{2^nH_*(w)}W,&
\rho(w)&=\frac{2^nM}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The sums are oriented and include displacement zero; all logarithms are natural. Let \(\pi_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}p_z\), \(q=\sum_{b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}\pi_b^2\), and let \(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\) be the law of \((X+Y)/2\) for independent \(X,Y\) of law \(p\), conditioned on equal parity. Use \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\|v)=\sum_{j:u_j>0}u_j\log(u_j/v_j)\) and \(\operatorname{TV}(u,v)=\frac12\sum_j|u_j-v_j|\). Scaling a table does not change \(G\), \(\rho\), or these normalized laws.

(i) If constants \(g_0>0\) and \(a>0\), independent of dimension and table, satisfy \(G(w)\le g_0\Longrightarrow\rho(w)\ge a\) for every admissible table, then \(g_0<\log(4/e)\). More precisely, for every integer \(m\ge3\), the one-dimensional table

\[
t_m=\frac{2m-1}{2m+1},\qquad
w^{(m)}_j=t_m^j\mathbf1_{\{0,\ldots,m^3\}}(j)
\]

is admissible. Writing \(M_m=\max_jw^{(m)}_j\) and \(W_m=\sum_jw^{(m)}_j\), it satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_m&=1,&
W_m&=\frac{2m+1}{2}\left(1-t_m^{m^3+1}\right),&
H_*(w^{(m)})&=(2m+1)t_m^m,\\
e^{G(w^{(m)})}&=\frac{4t_m^m}{1-t_m^{m^3+1}},&
\rho(w^{(m)})&=\frac{4}{(2m+1)(1-t_m^{m^3+1})}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every such \(m\), \(0\le G(w^{(m)})<\log(4/e)\), while \(G(w^{(m)})\to\log(4/e)\) and \(\rho(w^{(m)})\to0\).

(ii) For each even integer \(L\ge4\), take the unnormalized seed \(w\) and normalized seed \(p\) of accepted fact ce82c43b769ded34, with exactly its parameters

\[
t=2^{-L},\qquad m_i=L2^{i-1}-1\ (1\le i\le L),\qquad
\nu=1+L(2^L-2),\qquad R=1+2^L.
\]

To specify this seed independently, use coordinates \(z=(x,(y_{i,h})_{1\le i\le L,\,1\le h\le m_i})\) on \(\mathbb R^\nu\). Let \(\mathcal P\) be given by \(0\le x\le R\) and

\[
-\frac{(R-1)x}{R2^i}\le y_{i,h}\le1,
\qquad y_{i,h}\le1-\frac{x-1}{2^i}
\quad(1\le i\le L,\ 1\le h\le m_i).
\]

Set \(w_z=t^{(x-1)_+}\mathbf1_{\mathcal P}(z)\), where \((x-1)_+=\max\{x-1,0\}\), and put \(W=\sum_z w_z\), \(p=w/W\). Let \(T\) be its mass outside the Boolean core \(\mathcal C=\{0,1\}^\nu\). Then \(M=1\), \(W=2^\nu+T\), \(t\le T\le3t\), and the following sharpened seed estimates hold:

\[
H_*(w)=1+2\sqrt t,\qquad
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\|p)\le\frac{15t^2}{2^\nu}.
\]

The integer \(k_L=2^\nu t^{-3/2}=2^{\nu+3L/2}\) defines \(P_L=p^{\otimes k_L}\) in integer dimension \(n_L=\nu k_L\). These are admissible probability tables, with a global extension at all lattice zeros. Let \(q_L\) and \(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}}\) be their parity-collision probabilities and conditional-midpoint laws. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho(P_L)&=\left(\frac{2^\nu}{2^\nu+T}\right)^{k_L}
 \le\exp\left(-\frac1{2\sqrt t}\right)\longrightarrow0,\\
0\le2^{n_L}q_L-1&\le e^{9\sqrt t}-1\longrightarrow0,\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}}\|P_L)&\le15\sqrt t\longrightarrow0,\\
G(P_L)&=k_L\log\frac{2^\nu(1+2\sqrt t)}{2^\nu+T}
 \ge\frac{2^\nu}{2t}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(\operatorname{TV}(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}},P_L)\to0\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(a>0\) some admissible probability table therefore satisfies both \(2^nq-1<\varepsilon\) and \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\|p)<\varepsilon\), but has \(\rho<a\). This conclusion imposes no small-gain hypothesis. The families in (i) and (ii) have respectively positive limiting gain and diverging gain, and give no admissible sequence with both \(G\to0\) and \(\rho\to0\).

## Proof

For (i), the function \(f_m(x)=t_m^x\mathbf1_{[0,m^3]}(x)\) is finite-valued, has convex positive support, and has affine logarithm on that support. If an endpoint is outside the support the global log-concavity inequality has zero right side; if both are inside it holds with equality. This also verifies every lattice zero. The displayed formulas for \(M_m\) and \(W_m\) follow from \(0<t_m<1\) and the finite geometric sum.

At an integer center \(0\le j\le m^3\), a reflected pair is supported precisely when \(|v|\le\min(j,m^3-j)\); each such Hellinger summand equals \(t_m^j\). Thus \(H_j=(2\min(j,m^3-j)+1)t_m^j\), and the sum is zero at other centers. On nonnegative integers the sequence \((2j+1)t_m^j\) has successive ratio \(t_m(2j+3)/(2j+1)\), which is greater than one for \(j<m-1\), equal to one at \(j=m-1\), and less than one thereafter. Every \(H_j\) is bounded by this sequence at \(j\), and equality holds at \(j=m-1,m\), since \(m^3\ge2m\). Its maximum is consequently \((2m+1)t_m^m\). Substitution proves both exact normalized formulas.

The elementary identity \(\log((1-u)/(1+u))=-2\int_0^u(1-s^2)^{-1}\,ds\), with \(u=1/(2m)\), gives

\[
-\frac1{1-1/(4m^2)}\le m\log t_m
\le-2m\left(\frac1{2m}+\frac1{3(2m)^3}\right)
=-1-\frac1{12m^2}.
\]

Here the lower bound uses \((1-s^2)^{-1}\le(1-u^2)^{-1}\), and the upper bound uses \((1-s^2)^{-1}\ge1+s^2\). In particular \(m\log t_m\to-1\) and \(t_m^{m^3+1}\le e^{-m^2}\). Because \(e^{-m^2}<1/2\), integration of \((1-s)^{-1}\le2\) on \([0,e^{-m^2}]\) yields

\[
G(w^{(m)})
\le\log(4/e)-\frac1{12m^2}+2e^{-m^2}
<\log(4/e).
\]

The strict inequality is valid for every \(m\ge3\): the function \(e^x/x\) increases for \(x\ge1\), and \(e^{m^2}/m^2\ge e^9/9>2^9/9>24\). Nonnegativity of the gain is the admissible-table conclusion of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. The exact formulas and the proved limits give \(G(w^{(m)})\to\log(4/e)\) and \(\rho(w^{(m)})\to0\). If \(g_0\ge\log(4/e)\), every member satisfies the gain restriction and sufficiently large \(m\) violates any prescribed positive atomic lower bound. This proves (i), including exclusion of the endpoint.

For (ii), we first record the full seed census and midpoint identities of accepted fact ce82c43b769ded34 in its own normalization. The objects \(t,m_i,\nu,R,\mathcal P,w,W,p,\mathcal C,T,F,\mathcal A_k,c,\tau_b,B,D,\mathcal R,U\) below have exactly their meanings in that fact. In particular \(B\) denotes the squared tail parity masses, not the number \(2^\nu\) of parity classes. The symbol \(R\) is the scalar height bound from that fact; the unnormalized midpoint table \(\mathcal R_j\) below equals \(W^2R_j\) for the normalized \(R_j\) of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160.

The identity \(1+\sum_{i=1}^Lm_i=\nu\) verifies the dimension. The polytope \(\mathcal P\) is compact, convex, and contains \([0,1]^\nu\). The function

\[
f(z)=W^{-1}\exp\bigl(-L(\log2)(x-1)_+\bigr)\mathbf1_{\mathcal P}(z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb R^\nu)
\]

is finite-valued and agrees with \(p\) at every lattice point. Convexity of \((x-1)_+\) makes its logarithm concave on \(\mathcal P\); if either endpoint lies outside \(\mathcal P\), the required right side is zero. Thus it is a global extension including all zeros. Multiplication by \(W\) gives the corresponding extension of \(w\). Every core value is one and every tail value is a positive power of \(t<1\), so \(M=1\).

At heights \(x=0,1\), each transverse coordinate has exactly the integer choices \(0,1\); these are precisely the core \(\mathcal C\). At a tail height \(x=r+1\), \(1\le r\le2^L\), write \(\beta=1-(r+1)/R\in[0,1)\). The allowed interval in block \(i\) is \([-(r+\beta)/2^i,1-r/2^i]\). Writing \(r=a2^i+s\), \(0\le s<2^i\), shows that its full set of integers is

\[
I_i(r)=
\begin{cases}
\{-r/2^i,\ 1-r/2^i\},&2^i\mid r,\\
\{-\lfloor r/2^i\rfloor\},&2^i\nmid r.
\end{cases}
\]

Indeed, when \(s=0\) the ceiling of the lower endpoint is \(-a\) and the upper endpoint is \(1-a\); when \(s>0\), the inequality \(0<s+\beta<2^i\) makes both the ceiling lower endpoint and floor upper endpoint equal to \(-a\). Once the height is fixed all coordinates are independently constrained, so this accounts for every supported lattice point.

If \(r=(2\ell+1)2^k\) with \(0\le k\le L\), its fiber has \(2^{L(2^k-1)-k}\) points, each of weight \(t^r\). Denote the fiber \(r=2^k\) by \(\mathcal A_k\). Its mass is \(t/2^k\). The total nonpeak tail mass \(F\) obeys

\[
\begin{aligned}
F&=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{t}{2^k}
 \sum_{\substack{\ell\ge1\\(2\ell+1)2^k\le2^L}}t^{2\ell2^k}\\
&\le\frac{t^2}{1-t^2}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{t}{2^k}
\le\frac{2t^3}{1-t^2}\le3t^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The peak masses sum to \(2t-t^2\). Hence \(T=2t-t^2+F\), \(t\le T\le3t\), \(W=2^\nu+T\), and the last peak \(\mathcal A_L\) has mass \(t^2\). These bounds use only \(t\le1/4\), which holds throughout.

For a tail point \(z\), let \(b(z)\) be its unique same-parity core point and put \(\sigma(z)=(z+b(z))/2\). Coordinatewise this map is the ceiling of half the coordinate. For \(1\le k\le L\), it maps \(\mathcal A_k\) onto \(\mathcal A_{k-1}\) with multiplicity exactly \(2^{m_k}\). At height it takes \(1+2^k\) to \(1+2^{k-1}\). In block \(i<k\), the choices \(-2^{k-i},1-2^{k-i}\) map bijectively to the two target choices. In block \(k\), both source choices \(-1,0\) map to zero, giving two preimages independently in each of its \(m_k\) coordinates; higher blocks are zero. Thus the ordered core-tail pairs whose tail endpoint lies in \(\mathcal A_k\) give mass

\[
2\cdot2^{m_k}t^{2^k}=t^{2^{k-1}}=w_j
\quad\text{at every }j\in\mathcal A_{k-1}.
\]

The first peak is the single point \((2,\mathbf0)\), whose partner \((0,\mathbf0)\) gives two ordered pairs of product weight \(t\), both with midpoint \(c=(1,\mathbf0)\).

We next prove the new exact Hellinger maximum, including the multiplicities of all reflected pairs. Global log-concavity implies \(H_j(w)=0\) whenever \(w_j=0\), since a positive endpoint product would force a positive value at its midpoint. At height zero, the only endpoint heights are zero and zero. Each coordinate in \(\{0,1\}\) has only the diagonal reflected pair about an integer center, so \(H_j=1\). At height one, the two height-one endpoints again give just the diagonal contribution one. The only other heights are zero and two, in either order; the height-two fiber has transverse coordinates zero. Integer midpoint coordinates force its core partner to be \((0,\mathbf0)\), so these pairs occur only at \(c\). Consequently \(H_c=1+2\sqrt t\) and every other height-one center has sum one.

Consider a supported tail center \(j=(s+1,(y_{i,h}))\), \(1\le s\le2^L\). A pair using a core endpoint at height zero has its other endpoint at height \(2s+2\), with odd tail parameter \(2s+1\). Every transverse interval there is a singleton. For this fixed center and endpoint order the core coordinates are then uniquely determined, if they exist. The total contribution is at most \(2t^{s+1/2}\), and is zero if that height exceeds \(R\).

A pair using a core endpoint at height one has its other tail parameter equal to \(2s\). If \(2s>2^L\), no such pair exists. Otherwise write \(a=2^{v_2(s)}\), where \(v_2(s)\) is the largest integer \(h\ge0\) with \(2^h\mid s\). In block \(i\) with two tail choices, write the core and tail coordinates as \(\epsilon\) and \(-2s/2^i+\epsilon'\), with \(\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{0,1\}\). The equation for their sum is

\[
2y_{i,h}=-2s/2^i+\epsilon+\epsilon'.
\]

There are two solutions only when \(\epsilon+\epsilon'=1\) and \(2s/2^i\) is odd. Thus only block \(i=1+v_2(s)\) can have two choices per coordinate. Blocks with a singleton tail interval have at most one choice. The total multiplicity for either endpoint order is at most \(2^{m_{1+v_2(s)}}\); this block exists because \(2s\le2^L\). Therefore these pairs contribute at most

\[
2\cdot2^{m_{1+v_2(s)}}t^s=t^{s-a}\le1.
\]

For two tail endpoints their positive parameters \(r,r'\) satisfy \(r+r'=2s\), with \(1\le r,r'\le2^L\). There are at most \(2s-1\) ordered height choices, and each supported reflected pair has Hellinger weight \(t^s\). In any coordinate, if at least one of \(I_i(r),I_i(r')\) is a singleton, there is at most one reflected choice. If both have two elements, then \(2^i\) divides both parameters and the coordinate sum takes the form

\[
2y_{i,h}=-2s/2^i+\epsilon+\epsilon',\qquad
\epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Again multiplicity two is possible only at \(i=1+v_2(s)\), and all other blocks have multiplicity at most one. If \(s\) is a power of two, this exceptional divisor is \(2s\). Two positive parameters each divisible by \(2s\) cannot sum to \(2s\). Thus in this case every ordered height choice has transverse multiplicity at most one, and the tail-tail contribution is at most \((2s-1)t^s\). This reasoning also covers the top center \(s=2^L\), where the exceptional index would exceed \(L\).

If \(s\) is not a power of two, put \(a=2^{v_2(s)}\) as above. Here the exceptional block is among \(1,\ldots,L\), and its possible multiplicity is \(2^{m_{1+v_2(s)}}=\tfrac12t^{-a}\). The tail-tail contribution is at most \(\tfrac{2s-1}{2}t^{s-a}\). The integer \(s/a\) is odd and at least three, so \(s-a\ge2\) and \(2s-1<3(s-a)\). The sequence \(u t^u\) decreases on integers \(u\ge2\) when \(t\le1/4\), because its successive ratio is \(((u+1)/u)t\le3/8<1\). Thus the contribution is at most \(\tfrac32(s-a)t^{s-a}\le3t^2\).

In the power-of-two case with \(s\ge2\), the same bound \((2s-1)t^s\le3t^2\) follows because its ratio at successive integers \(s\ge2\) is \(((2s+1)/(2s-1))t\le5/12<1\). When \(s=1\), the only tail-tail pair is the diagonal at \((2,\mathbf0)\), with contribution \(t\). All endpoint types have now been counted, including the diagonal. Combining them gives

\[
H_j(w)\le
\begin{cases}
1+t+2t^{3/2},&s=1,\\
1+3t^2+2t^{s+1/2},&s\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

Both are at most \(1+2\sqrt t\) for \(t\le1/4\). Indeed, \(t+2t^{3/2}=\sqrt t(\sqrt t+2t)\le\sqrt t\), while \(3t^2+2t^{s+1/2}\le\sqrt t(3t^{3/2}+2t^2)\le\sqrt t/2\) for \(s\ge2\). Equality at \(c\) therefore establishes \(H_*(w)=1+2\sqrt t\le2\).

For the KL estimate, define \(\tau_b=\sum_{z\notin\mathcal C,\,z\equiv b\pmod2}w_z\), \(B=\sum_b\tau_b^2\), and the unnormalized midpoint table \(\mathcal R_j=\sum_v w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\). The bijection between \((j,v)\) and ordered equal-parity endpoints gives

\[
D:=\sum_j\mathcal R_j=\sum_b(1+\tau_b)^2
=2^\nu+2T+B,\qquad
0\le B\le T^2\le9t^2,\qquad
p^{\mathrm{mid}}=\mathcal R/D.
\]

Use the same reference table as accepted fact ce82c43b769ded34:

\[
U_j=\mathbf1_{\mathcal C}(j)+2t\mathbf1_{\{c\}}(j)
+w_j\mathbf1_{\bigcup_{k=0}^{L-1}\mathcal A_k}(j).
\]

The core has one point per parity class, so all its equal-parity internal pairs are diagonal and contribute the first term. The proved peak midpoint multiplicities account for the other two terms. The remaining pairs are exactly the ordered core-tail pairs with a nonpeak tail endpoint, of total mass \(2F\), and the equal-parity tail-tail pairs, of total mass \(B\). Thus \(\mathcal R\ge U\) pointwise and \(\sum_j(\mathcal R_j-U_j)=2F+B\), even when the midpoint locations of different types overlap.

For a nonnegative table \(u\) supported where \(w\) is positive, define the unnormalized relative entropy by the following explicit formula:

\[
\Phi(u,w)=\sum_{j:w_j>0}
\left[u_j\log\left(\frac{u_j}{w_j}\right)-u_j+w_j\right],
\qquad 0\log(0/w_j):=0.
\]

Global log-concavity gives \(\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\le w_j\). Multiplying by the same square-root factor and summing, then using the proved Hellinger maximum, yields \(\mathcal R_j\le w_jH_j(w)\le2w_j\). In particular \(\mathcal R\) and \(U\) vanish at every zero of \(w\), so \(\Phi\) applies to both. For \(w_j>0\), the derivative of \(u\log(u/w_j)-u+w_j\) is \(\log(u/w_j)\). On the interval from \(U_j\) to \(\mathcal R_j\) this derivative is at most \(\log2\). The integral is finite also when \(U_j=0\), by continuity of \(u\log u\) at zero. Consequently

\[
\Phi(\mathcal R,w)\le\Phi(U,w)+(2F+B)\log2.
\]

The reference table agrees with \(w\) except at \(c\), the last peak, and the nonpeak tail. The latter two have total mass \(t^2+F\), and \(U_c=1+2t\), \(w_c=1\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(U,w)&=(1+2t)\log(1+2t)-2t+t^2+F\le3t^2+F,\\
\Phi(\mathcal R,w)&\le3t^2+3F+B
\le12t^2+9t^3\le15t^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \((1+x)\log(1+x)-x=\int_0^x\log(1+s)\,ds\le x^2/2\) for \(x\ge0\), and \(\log2<1\), \(F\le3t^3\), \(B\le9t^2\), \(t\le1/4\) give the other inequalities. Normalization satisfies the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\|p)
&=\frac1D\sum_{j:\mathcal R_j>0}\mathcal R_j\log\frac{\mathcal R_j}{w_j}
 +\log\frac WD\\
&=\frac{\Phi(\mathcal R,w)}D+1-\frac WD+\log\frac WD
\le\frac{\Phi(\mathcal R,w)}D
\le\frac{15t^2}{2^\nu}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first inequality is \(\log u\le u-1\) for \(u=W/D>0\); the last uses \(D\ge2^\nu\). This proves the new seed KL estimate with the stated orientation of relative entropy.

For completeness, the same census gives the seed parity bound directly. Since \(q=D/W^2\), Cauchy-Schwarz on the \(2^\nu\) values \(\tau_b\) gives

\[
0\le2^\nu q-1
=\frac{2^\nu B-T^2}{(2^\nu+T)^2}
\le\frac{9t^2}{2^\nu}.
\]

Now let \(k_L=2^{\nu+3L/2}\), an integer because \(L\) is even, and form \(P_L=p^{\otimes k_L}\). The product of the displayed seed extensions on disjoint coordinate blocks is finite-valued and globally log-concave: multiply the seed inequalities when both endpoint products are positive, and use a zero right side otherwise. It agrees with every lattice value, including every tuple with a zero factor. Its support is compact and its lattice support finite.

Before conditioning, the \(k_L\) endpoint pairs are independent. Equal parity of the full vectors is the intersection of the independent blockwise equal-parity events. Conditional probabilities therefore factor, giving \(q_L=q^{k_L}\) and \(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}}=(p^{\mathrm{mid}})^{\otimes k_L}\). Expanding the logarithm of the product density ratio and summing the other probability factors proves additivity of KL divergence. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}}\|P_L)
&=k_LD_{\mathrm{KL}}(p^{\mathrm{mid}}\|p)\le15\sqrt t,\\
0\le2^{n_L}q_L-1
&=(2^\nu q)^{k_L}-1
\le\left(1+\frac{9t^2}{2^\nu}\right)^{k_L}-1
\le e^{9\sqrt t}-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last step uses \(1+u\le e^u\). Since \(M(w)=1\), the product maximum gives \(\rho(P_L)=(2^\nu/(2^\nu+T))^{k_L}\). The bounds \(T\ge t\), \(T\le3t\le2^\nu\), and \(\log(1+u)\ge u/(1+u)\) imply

\[
-\log\rho(P_L)
=k_L\log\left(1+\frac{T}{2^\nu}\right)
\ge\frac{k_LT}{2^\nu+T}
\ge\frac{k_Lt}{2\cdot2^\nu}
=\frac1{2\sqrt t}.
\]

To obtain the TV consequence without an external theorem, the scalar inequality \(r\log r-r+1\ge(\sqrt r-1)^2\) holds for \(r\ge0\), with the zero convention. Indeed, their difference is \(2\sqrt r(\sqrt r\log\sqrt r-\sqrt r+1)\ge0\), since \(u\log u-u+1\) is minimized at \(u=1\). Summation against a reference probability table gives \(\sum_j(\sqrt{u_j}-\sqrt{v_j})^2\le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\|v)\) whenever the latter is finite. Cauchy-Schwarz applied to \(|u_j-v_j|=|\sqrt{u_j}-\sqrt{v_j}|(\sqrt{u_j}+\sqrt{v_j})\), and \(\sum_j(\sqrt{u_j}+\sqrt{v_j})^2\le4\), then gives \(\operatorname{TV}(u,v)\le\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(u\|v)}\). Here it yields \(\operatorname{TV}(P_L^{\mathrm{mid}},P_L)\le\sqrt{15}\,t^{1/4}\to0\).

Finally, the exact seed maximum gives \(e^{G(p)}=2^\nu(1+2\sqrt t)/(2^\nu+T)\ge1+\sqrt t\). To check the last inequality, it suffices that \(T(1+\sqrt t)\le2^\nu\sqrt t\); this follows from \(T\le3t\), \(3\sqrt t(1+\sqrt t)\le9/4\), and \(2^\nu\ge32\). As \(\log(1+u)\ge u/2\) for \(0\le u\le1\), one has \(G(p)\ge\sqrt t/2\). The product reflection sums factor and their attained maxima multiply, exactly as in accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. Consequently

\[
G(P_L)=k_LG(p)
=k_L\log\frac{2^\nu(1+2\sqrt t)}{2^\nu+T}
\ge\frac{2^\nu}{2t}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

All other displayed limits follow as even \(L\to\infty\), hence \(t\to0\). They give the asserted quantified obstruction based on small parity and KL defects.

The first family remains at gain approaching the positive value \(\log(4/e)\), so it is compatible with the one-dimensional positive small-gain result of accepted fact b85cce72a986b40e, whose gain \(g\) is the present \(G\) and whose normalized largest-atom quantity \(2M/W\) is the present \(\rho\). The second family has gain tending to infinity. Neither reaches the simultaneous limits \(G\to0\), \(\rho\to0\) that characterize failure of dimension-uniform small-gain stability in accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. These are limitations on thresholds and on an averaged-midpoint strategy, with no disproof of the locked mixed-integer depth target.
