## Statement

Put
\[
K:=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
(1,0),(0,1),\left(-1,-\frac18\right),
\left(0,-\frac3{11}\right)\right\}\subseteq\mathbb R^2
\]
and let \(p\) be its Minkowski functional.  Explicitly,
\[
p(x,y)=\max\left\{
x+y,\ y-\frac98x,\
-\frac{13}{24}x-\frac{11}{3}y,\
x-\frac{11}{3}y
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(d:=30\), \(R:=500000\), and
\[
B:=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
e_1,\ldots,e_{30},-\sum_{i=1}^{30}e_i
\right\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{30}.
\tag{2}
\]
The centroid of \(B\) is \(0\), and
\[
V:=\operatorname{vol}_{30}(B)=\frac{31}{30!}.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(p_B\) for the Minkowski functional of \(B\), define
\[
C:=\left\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{30}:
p(z)+p_B(x)\leq R\right\},
\qquad z_0:=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body.  Its complete
lattice-fiber profile is
\[
C_z=(R-p(z))B,\qquad
w_z=V(R-p(z))^{30}
\quad\text{if }p(z)\leq R,
\tag{5}
\]
where the fiber at \(p(z)=R\) is the singleton \(\{0\}\), and \(C_z\) is
empty if \(p(z)>R\).  In particular,
\[
w_z=V(R-p(z))_+^{30}.
\tag{6}
\]
If
\[
P(z):=24p(z)
=\max\left\{
24z_1+24z_2,\ 24z_2-27z_1,\
-13z_1-88z_2,\ 24z_1-88z_2
\right\},
\tag{7}
\]
then \(P(z)\in\mathbb Z\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), and
\[
\{z:w_z>0\}
=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:P(z)<12000000\},
\tag{8}
\]
\[
w_z=\frac{V}{24^{30}}(12000000-P(z))^{30}
\quad(P(z)<12000000).
\tag{9}
\]
Thus (8) and (9) are the complete positive lattice profile, while every
lattice point with \(P(z)=12000000\) has an exact zero-volume fiber.

Let
\[
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z,
\qquad
\gamma:=\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30}.
\tag{10}
\]
For every positive fiber, its maximal absolute halfspace depth is
\[
M(z)=\gamma w_z.
\tag{11}
\]
The body and anchor satisfy
\[
w_z<\frac W4\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^2),
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in(a+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\tag{13}
\]
for every coset \(a+L\) of every primitive rank-one sublattice,
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z>\frac W4
\tag{14}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(0\), and
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{z:w_z>0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
For the four parity masses \(W_r\), one has
\[
Q^\circ:=\sum_rW_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2
>\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{16}
\]
The registered support-safe energy satisfies
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{30}(w;0)<\frac W{64}.
\tag{17}
\]
Nevertheless, for every eligible rank-zero, rank-one, or rank-two
lattice simplex \(T\) containing \(0\),
\[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M(v)\,\kappa(T,0)
<\frac W{64}.
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently this \(C\) is an exact counterexample to
`n2-low-energy-uniform-simplex`.

## Proof

The four expressions in (1) are the supporting functionals of the four
successive edges of \(K\), each normalized to equal \(1\) on its edge.
They therefore give its Minkowski functional.  The origin is in the
interior of \(K\).  Likewise, the \(31\) vertices in (2) are affinely
independent and have average \(0\), so \(0\in\operatorname{int}B\).
The determinant of the edge matrix \(I_{30}+\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\) is
\(31\), proving (3).  Both gauges are nonnegative, convex, positively
homogeneous, and coercive.  Hence (4) is compact and convex, and the
strict inequality at \((0,0)\) proves that it is full-dimensional.
Positive homogeneity gives (5) through (9).

We first compute the maximal fiber depths, including sharpness.  If
\(\lambda>0\), the centroid of \(\lambda B\) is \(0\).  Grünbaum's
centroid halfspace inequality gives absolute depth at least
\(\gamma\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\lambda B)\) there.  Conversely, use
barycentric coordinates relative to the \(31\) vertices of \(\lambda B\).
At the centroid every coordinate is \(1/31\).  The halfspace
\(\{\lambda_i\geq1/31\}\) is a cap containing the centroid, homothetic to
the whole simplex with linear ratio \(30/31\), and hence has volume
\(\gamma\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\lambda B)\).  Thus the centroid has
depth exactly this value.

At any other point, some barycentric coordinate \(\lambda_i\) is at
least \(1/31\).  The cap \(\{\lambda_i\geq\lambda_i(x)\}\) contains that
point on its closed boundary and has volume
\[
(1-\lambda_i(x))^{30}\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\lambda B)
\leq\gamma\operatorname{vol}_{30}(\lambda B).
\]
No point has larger depth, proving (11).  Accepted fact
`4e868f774de77608` also gives attainment and states literally that a
zero-volume fiber has \(M(z)=0\).  Thus the singleton boundary fibers in
(5) are not silently treated as positive fibers.

We next record all exact planar constants needed below.  Shoelace gives
\[
\operatorname{area}(K)=\frac{14}{11}.
\tag{19}
\]
We also need the exact continuous halfspace depth of \(0\) in \(K\):
\[
\inf_{\substack{
H\subseteq\mathbb R^2\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\0\in H}}
\operatorname{area}(K\cap H)
=\frac{269}{825}.
\tag{20}
\]
Here is a complete one-variable verification of (20).  Moving a
containing halfspace boundary toward \(0\) reduces its intersection, so
only lines through \(0\) need be considered.  Let the vertices of \(K\)
in counterclockwise order be
\[
A=(1,0),\quad B=(0,1),\quad
C=\left(-1,-\frac18\right),\quad
D=\left(0,-\frac3{11}\right).
\]
Modulo reversing the line, the two boundary intersections fall into the
following three angular cases.

For a direction \((1,m)\), \(0\leq m\leq1/8\), the line meets
\([A,B]\) and \([B,C]\).  The areas of its two closed sides are
\[
\frac{17}{2(m+1)(9-8m)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{-224m^2+28m+65}{22(m+1)(9-8m)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Their derivatives are, respectively,
\[
\frac{17(16m-1)}
{2(m+1)^2(9-8m)^2}
\quad\text{and its negative}.
\tag{22}
\]
Their minima on this interval are \(16/17\) and \(65/198\).

For a direction \((1,m)\), \(m\geq1/8\), the line meets
\([A,B]\) and \([C,D]\).  The two areas are
\[
\frac{112m^2+210m+23}{2(m+1)(88m+13)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{1232m^2+518m+111}{22(m+1)(88m+13)}.
\tag{23}
\]
Their derivatives are
\[
-\frac{(64m-11)(112m+37)}
{2(m+1)^2(88m+13)^2}
\quad\text{and its negative}.
\tag{24}
\]
The first area has minimum \(7/11\), attained as \(m\to\infty\).
The second has its minimum at \(m=11/64\), where it equals \(269/825\).

For a direction \((-1,m)\), \(m\geq0\), the line meets
\([B,C]\) and \([D,A]\).  The two areas are
\[
\frac{1232m^2+826m+195}{22(8m+9)(11m+3)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{112m^2+238m+51}{2(8m+9)(11m+3)}.
\tag{25}
\]
Their derivatives are
\[
\frac{(64m-3)(112m+51)}
{2(8m+9)^2(11m+3)^2}
\quad\text{and its negative}.
\tag{26}
\]
The first has its minimum \(269/825\) at \(m=3/64\), and the second has
minimum \(7/11\).  These three cases exhaust a half-turn of line
directions.  Finally,
\[
\frac{65}{198}-\frac{269}{825}=\frac1{450}>0,
\qquad
\frac7{11}-\frac{269}{825}=\frac{256}{825}>0,
\]
so (20) follows.  The two minimizing lines have normals
\((11,-64)\) and \((3,64)\).  Dividing (20) by (19) gives the exact
uniform cap fraction
\[
\alpha:=\frac{269/825}{14/11}
=\frac{2959}{11550}
=\frac14+\frac{143}{23100}.
\tag{27}
\]

We now compare lattice sums to integrals.  Each supporting functional in
(1) has coefficient \(\ell_1\)-norm at most \(14/3\).  Therefore
\[
|p(x)-p(z)|\leq\frac73
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
x\in z+\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Homogeneity and (19) give, for every \(T\geq0\),
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}(T-p(x))_+^{30}\,dx
=\frac{28}{11\cdot31\cdot32}T^{32}
=\frac7{2728}T^{32}.
\tag{29}
\]
Applying (28) on every unit lattice cell gives
\[
\frac7{2728}V\left(R-\frac73\right)^{32}
\leq W\leq
\frac7{2728}V\left(R+\frac73\right)^{32}.
\tag{30}
\]
Bernoulli's inequality and \(R=500000\) give
\[
\left(1-\frac7{3R}\right)^{32}
\geq1-\frac{224}{3R}
=\frac{1499776}{1500000}
>\frac{999}{1000};
\tag{31}
\]
the last cross-product difference is
\[
1499776\cdot1000-999\cdot1500000=1276000>0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
W>\frac{6993}{2728000}VR^{32}.
\tag{32}
\]

Because \(K\subseteq[-1,1]^2\), its gauge satisfies
\(p(z)\geq\max\{|z_1|,|z_2|\}\).  In particular \(w_z\leq w_0=VR^{30}\).
Equations (32) and \(R=500000\) yield
\[
\frac{w_z}{W}\leq\frac{w_0}{W}
<\frac{341}{218531250000}<\frac1{16}<\frac14.
\tag{33}
\]
This proves the strict singleton condition.

On an affine lattice line, at least one integer coordinate is injective.
Using \(p(z)\geq|z_i|\) for that coordinate gives the uniform line bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\text{ on the line}}w_z
&\leq
V\left[R^{30}+2\sum_{k=1}^{R-1}(R-k)^{30}\right]\\
&<
V\left(R^{30}+\frac{2R^{31}}{31}\right),
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
where the strict inequality follows by comparing the increasing summand
with \(\int_0^R t^{30}\,dt\).  Combining (32) and (34),
\[
\frac{2\sum_{z\text{ on the line}}w_z}{W}
<
\frac{2(1+2R/31)}
{(6993/2728000)R^2}
=\frac{11000341}{109265625000}<1,
\tag{35}
\]
and the last strict gap is
\[
109265625000-11000341=109254624659>0.
\]
This proves (13), for every primitive-line coset and in fact for every
affine lattice line.

We next prove the anchored marginal bound for every closed halfspace,
without sampling its slope.  Shrinking a halfspace containing \(0\)
until its boundary reaches \(0\) can only reduce its lattice mass.  Write
such a boundary-through-zero halfspace as
\[
Q=\{x:a^\top x\geq0\},
\qquad \|a\|_1=1.
\]
If \(x\in z+[-1/2,1/2]^2\) and \(a^\top x\geq1/2\), then
\(a^\top z\geq0\).  Thus the lower cell comparison (28), the cap fraction
(27), and layer cake imply
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
\geq
V\left[
\alpha\frac7{2728}\left(R-\frac73\right)^{32}
-4\left(R-\frac73\right)^{31}
\right].
\tag{36}
\]
For completeness, the subtracted strip is
\(\{0\leq a^\top x<1/2\}\).  Since
\(\|a\|_2\geq1/\sqrt2\) and \(K\subseteq[-1,1]^2\), its intersection
with \((R-7/3)K\) has area less than
\(4(R-7/3)\).  The integrand is at most
\((R-7/3)^{30}\), proving the exact strip error in (36).  This argument
retains every lattice point on the closed boundary of \(Q\).

Let \(T_-:=R-7/3\) and \(T_+:=R+7/3\).  Since
\[
\frac{T_-}{T_+}>1-\frac{14}{3R},
\]
Bernoulli gives
\[
\left(\frac{T_-}{T_+}\right)^{32}
>1-\frac{448}{3R}.
\]
Also \(T_-^{31}/T_+^{32}<1/R\).  Dividing (36) by the upper bound in
(30) therefore gives the fully rational estimate
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in Q}w_z}{W}
>
\frac{2959}{11550}\left(1-\frac{448}{3R}\right)
-\frac{10912}{7R}
=\frac{12452159}{49218750}.
\tag{37}
\]
The exact gap above one quarter is
\[
\frac{12452159}{49218750}-\frac14
=\frac{294943}{98437500}>0.
\tag{38}
\]
This proves (14) for all proper closed halfspaces.  Moreover,
\(\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}\) lies in the positive support, so (15) follows.

For parity, Cauchy-Schwarz and \(w_z\leq w_0\) give
\[
Q^\circ
=\sum_rW_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2
\geq\frac{W^2}{4}-w_0W.
\tag{39}
\]
Equation (33) makes the right side strictly larger than
\(3W^2/16\).  Since \(e>0\),
\[
\frac3{16}>\frac1{16}>\frac{16-e}{256},
\]
which proves the strict registered parity condition (16).

It remains to prove the exact low-energy estimate.  Put
\[
q:=(1-\gamma)^{1/30},\qquad c:=1-q.
\tag{40}
\]
First,
\[
\left(\frac{31}{30}\right)^{30}
>
\sum_{k=0}^4\binom{30}{k}30^{-k}
=\frac{144047}{54000}
=\frac83+\frac{47}{54000}.
\]
Hence \(\gamma<3/8\) and \(q^{30}>5/8\).  The two exact integer
certificates
\[
91\cdot64^6-100\cdot63^6=1122162076>0,
\tag{41}
\]
\[
5\cdot100^5-8\cdot91^5=77428392>0
\tag{42}
\]
show
\[
\left(\frac{63}{64}\right)^{30}
<
\left(\frac{91}{100}\right)^5
<\frac58<q^{30}.
\]
Therefore
\[
c<\frac1{64}.
\tag{43}
\]

Define the symmetric gauge
\[
h(z):=p(z)+p(-z).
\tag{44}
\]
If the four coefficient vectors in (1) are denoted by
\(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_4\), then
\[
h(z)=\max_{i,j}(\ell_i-\ell_j)^\top z.
\]
Solving the adjacent equalities in the six strip constraints
\(|(\ell_i-\ell_j)^\top z|\leq1\) gives the exact unit ball
\[
\{h\leq1\}
=\operatorname{conv}\left\{
\left(\frac8{17},\frac1{17}\right),
\left(0,\frac3{14}\right),
\left(-\frac8{17},0\right),
\left(-\frac8{17},-\frac1{17}\right),
\left(0,-\frac3{14}\right),
\left(\frac8{17},0\right)
\right\}.
\tag{45}
\]
Direct substitution verifies every strip constraint, and shoelace gives
\[
A_h:=\operatorname{area}\{h\leq1\}=\frac{464}{2023}.
\tag{46}
\]
The largest coefficient \(\ell_1\)-norm among the differences
\(\ell_i-\ell_j\) is
\[
\frac{17}{8}+\frac{14}{3}=\frac{163}{24}.
\]
Thus rounding inside a unit lattice cell changes \(h\) by at most
\(163/48\).  Homogeneity and (46) also give
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}(T-h(x))_+\,dx=\frac{A_hT^3}{3}.
\tag{47}
\]

The root radii of the two fibers at \(z,-z\), after removing the common
factor \(V^{1/30}\), are
\[
A=(R-p(z))_+,\qquad B=(R-p(-z))_+.
\]
Since \(q>1/2\), the registered budget \(2qR\) is larger than
\(\max\{A,B\}\).  If one endpoint radius is zero, the truncation in
\(\Psi\) makes its pair contribution exactly zero.  If both are positive,
accepted exact endpoint formula `aa56458a4606be95` says that a nonzero
contribution can occur only when
\[
\Delta:=A+B-2qR=2cR-h(z)>0,
\]
and in that case
\[
\Phi_{30,2qR}(A,B)
=m^{30}-(m-\Delta)^{30}
\leq30R^{29}\Delta,
\tag{48}
\]
where \(m=\min\{A,B\}\).  This is the mean value theorem on
\([m-\Delta,m]\).  Restoring the volume factor and using
\(\Psi\leq\Phi\) gives, for every unordered nonzero pair,
\[
\Psi_{30,S_0}\!\left(w_z^{1/30},w_{-z}^{1/30}\right)
\leq30VR^{29}(2cR-h(z))_+.
\tag{49}
\]

Summing one member of every unordered pair, adding the anchor term, and
then harmlessly inserting the origin into the symmetric lattice sum gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E^\Psi_{30}(w;0)
&\leq
\gamma VR^{30}
+15VR^{29}\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}(2cR-h(z))_+\\
&\leq
V\left[
\gamma R^{30}
+5A_hR^{29}
\left(2cR+\frac{163}{48}\right)^3
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{50}
\]
The second inequality uses the upper lattice-cell comparison and (47).
By (43),
\[
2cR+\frac{163}{48}
<\frac R{32}+\frac{163}{48}<\frac R{31};
\tag{51}
\]
the last exact cross-product certificate is
\[
48R-163\cdot992=23838304>0.
\]
Equations (32), (43), (46), and (50) now reduce (17) to one rational
comparison:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{6993}{2728000}
-64\left(
\frac{3}{8R^2}
+\frac{2320}{2023\cdot31^3}
\right)\\
&\hspace{25mm}
=\frac{2,065,905,351,026,381}
{20716847593750000000}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{52}
\]
Indeed, the first term in parentheses bounds
\(\gamma/R^2\), and the second bounds
\(5A_h(1/31)^3\).  Thus
\(\mathcal E^\Psi_{30}(w;0)<W/64\) exactly.

We finish with the universal simplex bound.  First dispose of all
minimal-face issues.  Let \(F\) be the minimal face of an eligible
simplex \(T\) containing \(0\).  Then
\[
\kappa(T,0)=\kappa(F,0),
\tag{53}
\]
with the registered value \(1\) when \(F=\{0\}\).  For the lower
inequality in (53), intersect any proper closed relative halfspace of
\(\operatorname{aff}(T)\) containing \(0\) with
\(\operatorname{aff}(F)\).  The result is either all of
\(\operatorname{aff}(F)\) or a proper closed relative halfspace
containing \(0\).  For the reverse inequality, choose a minimizing
boundary-through-zero functional on \(F\), extend it to
\(\operatorname{aff}(T)\), and subtract a sufficiently large multiple
of a supporting functional which is zero on \(F\) and positive on
\(T\setminus F\).  Since \(T\cap\mathbb Z^2\) is finite, the resulting
proper closed relative halfspace retains exactly the minimizing lattice
points of \(F\) and none outside \(F\).  If \(F=\{0\}\), the negative of
the supporting functional retains only \(0\).  This proves (53),
including every closed-boundary point.

Because the minimum vertex depth of \(T\) is no larger than that of \(F\),
we may replace \(T\) by \(F\).  Thus \(0\) is in its relative interior.
For a rank-one segment with endpoints \(-au,bu\), where \(u\) is primitive
and \(a,b\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), the two proper closed relative rays based at
\(0\) show exactly that
\[
\kappa(T,0)=\min\{a,b\}+1.
\tag{54}
\]
The anchor is counted on both closed rays.

For any compact convex \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^2\), including a segment or
a point,
\[
\#(A\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\leq
\operatorname{area}(A)+\Delta_x(A)+\Delta_y(A)+1.
\tag{55}
\]
Indeed, attach the half-open unit square
\([-1/2,1/2)^2\) to every lattice point of \(A\).  These cells are
disjoint, their total area is the lattice-point count, and their union is
contained in \(A+[-1/2,1/2]^2\).  Adding first a horizontal and then a
vertical unit segment shows exactly that
\[
\operatorname{area}\left(A+\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right]^2\right)
=\operatorname{area}(A)+\Delta_x(A)+\Delta_y(A)+1.
\]
Thus (55) counts all boundary lattice points, not only interior cells.

For the reduced simplex, put
\[
s:=\max_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}p(v).
\tag{56}
\]
Then \(0\leq s<R\), \(T\subseteq sK\), and
\[
\min_{v\in\operatorname{vert}T}M(v)
=\gamma V(R-s)^{30}.
\tag{57}
\]
If \(T\) has rank two, accepted fact `431423848c9bacf8`, scaled from
\(K\) to \(sK\), supplies a proper closed halfspace containing \(0\)
whose cap \(A=T\cap H\) has
\[
\operatorname{area}(A)<\frac5{32}s^2.
\tag{58}
\]
If \(T\) has rank one, use either minimizing proper closed relative ray
from (54); its cap has planar area zero.  Rank zero has
\(\kappa=1\).  Since
\[
\Delta_x(K)=2,\qquad \Delta_y(K)=\frac{14}{11},
\]
equations (55) and (58) give, simultaneously in all three ranks,
\[
\kappa(T,0)
\leq\frac5{32}s^2+\frac{36}{11}s+1.
\tag{59}
\]

The exact elementary maxima on \(0\leq s\leq R\) are
\[
\max s^2(R-s)^{30}
=\frac{4R^{32}}{32^2}\left(\frac{30}{32}\right)^{30},
\tag{60}
\]
\[
\max s(R-s)^{30}
=\frac{R^{31}}{31}\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^{30},
\qquad
\max(R-s)^{30}=R^{30}.
\tag{61}
\]
Combining (57) through (61), every eligible simplex score is at most
\[
U:=\gamma V\left[
\frac5{32}\frac{4R^{32}}{32^2}
\left(\frac{30}{32}\right)^{30}
+\frac{36}{11}\frac{R^{31}}{31}\gamma
+R^{30}
\right].
\tag{62}
\]
To compare this with \(W\), first note the exact certificates
\[
21\cdot16^{10}-40\cdot15^{10}
=23728558296>0,
\]
\[
\frac3{20}-\left(\frac{21}{40}\right)^3
=\frac{339}{64000}>0.
\]
They imply
\[
\left(\frac{15}{16}\right)^{30}<\frac3{20}.
\tag{63}
\]
Using also \(\gamma<3/8\), equation (62) gives
\[
\frac{U}{VR^{32}}
<
\frac9{262144}
+\frac{81}{5456R}
+\frac3{8R^2}.
\tag{64}
\]
Finally, at \(R=500000\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{6993}{2728000}
-64\left(
\frac9{262144}
+\frac{81}{5456R}
+\frac3{8R^2}
\right)\\
&\hspace{25mm}
=\frac{11291762649}{31000000000000}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{65}
\]
Equations (32), (64), and (65) give \(U<W/64\).  Therefore every score
in (18) is strictly below \(W/64\), completing the exact counterexample.
