## Statement

Let $P=\{0,1\}^2$, $\epsilon_z=(-1)^{z_1+z_2}$, $h_z=\epsilon_z/4$, and take the convex hull $C$ of the four intervals $K_z=[h_z-1,h_z+1]$ at these bases. They are its exact integer fibers and have total mass eight. Among covers by two closed caps each of mass at most four and with unit continuous normals, the unique hinge minimum up to relabeling is $x\ge0$, $x\le0$, with each cap mass four and hinge $17/8$.

For any fixed width normalization $h_{[-W,W]}(u)=1$, $W>0$, the geometric minimizers are the same and the hinges are $17/(8W)$ each. The exact Fritz–John source base marginals and common contact base marginal at the minimum are
$$
\sigma_\pm(z)=\alpha(1\pm h_z)+Wr,\qquad
\omega(z)=A+s\epsilon_z,\qquad A=\alpha+Wr>0,
$$
where $\alpha,r\ge0$, $|s|\le A$, and both budget multipliers equal $r$. Every such choice is a stress with minimax weights $1/2$ each, actual contact $x_z=0$ and cover multiplier $\eta_z=2\omega(z)$. For every normal stress $\alpha>0$, there is a convex function of the base whose integral against $\omega$ exceeds its integral against at least one of $\sigma_+,\sigma_-$ by at least $\alpha/4$. An abnormal stress $\alpha=0$ with $r>0$ and $s=0$ makes the marginals equal. Here the actual integer-fiber and all-real-fiber difference bodies are respectively $[-2,2]$ and $[-5/2,5/2]$.

## Proof

All four sampled bases are extreme, and the square has no other integer sites. The hull has full affine dimension three by varying height in one interval and using three affinely independent bases. Each cover of a length-two interval by two unit-sign caps costs at least one in hinge. A cap covering the whole interval costs at least two. If the signs agree, coverage forces one cap to cover the interval. For opposite signs write the unscaled margins $x-p$ and $q-x$. If $p>q$, coverage puts the interval on one side of the gap, and a cap covers it. If $p\le q$, the hinge sum dominates $|x-t|$ for each $p\le t\le q$, whose interval integral is at least one. Thus a wholly covered fiber forces total hinge at least five. The displayed pair has mass four each, hinge $\tfrac12\sum_z(1\pm h_z)^2=17/8$ each, and total $17/4<5$. It suffices to consider pairs that have opposite signs and cut every interval properly. Coverage and the budgets imply overlap of measure zero, hence the two proper thresholds coincide on every fiber. Their common trace $t_z$ is affine in $z$. The hinge sum equals $4+\sum_z(t_z-h_z)^2=17/4+\sum_z t_z^2$ because $h$ has zero sum and zero first moments in both base coordinates. The unique optimal trace is zero, and the affine function is determined by the four vertices.

For general $W$, both margins are scaled by $1/W$, so the geometric optimum is unchanged. The minimum of the maximum of the two margins on each interval is attained only at $x=0$. By 828211a20fa24681 the dual weights are $1/2$ each: any other mixture has a nonzero continuous slope and a strictly negative minimum because zero is interior. The cap mass derivative section on each fiber is its endpoint-counting coarea $W$, supported at $x=0$. This also follows directly by writing the two thresholds as affine evaluations divided by their respective fixed continuous components. The derivative of the hinge in the free base and offset parameters pairs with the cap's raw base moment and mass, by the same dominated-convergence argument as 0366ccb8fa1881a1. The fixed-width constraint in dimension one has a discrete normalized normal set. Thus the joint Fritz–John equations in the free parameters are exactly the base mass and first-moment equations of c343c9f3dc96957e, with coarea $W$ and the actual contacts just found. The cover-weight equations hold at $0$ because both margins vanish there.

The two source weights at $z$ are $\alpha(1\pm h_z)+Wr_\pm$ and their shared contact mass is $\eta_z/2$. Total-mass equality forces $r_+=r_-=r$. Since $h$ has zero mass and first base moments, the contact array minus the constant $A=\alpha+Wr$ has zero sum and both first moments zero. The kernel on the square is spanned by $\epsilon$. Nonnegativity is equivalent to $|s|\le A$, and $A=0$ would make every multiplier zero. Conversely each displayed choice satisfies these equations and complementarity. The continuous section and contact moments vanish, and the raw cap continuous moments are $\pm17/8$. If the normalization is written as an equality in the continuous normal variable, its gradient is $\pm W$ on the respective sign branch, so a multiplier $-\alpha\,17/(8W)$ supplies the continuous stationarity equation. This verifies the entire stress system directly without a general width-normality premise.

For $p\in P$ the globally convex test
$$
\phi_p(z)=\max\!\left(0,1-\sum_{k=1}^2[(1-p_k)z_k+p_k(1-z_k)]\right)
$$
is exactly the indicator of $p$ on $P$. Now $\sum_z\omega(z)=4A$ and $\sum_z\min(\sigma_+(z),\sigma_-(z))=4A-\alpha$. One $p$ therefore has $\omega(p)-\min(\sigma_+(p),\sigma_-(p))\ge\alpha/4$. This proves the obstruction and the stated abnormal case follows by substitution. The sampled intervals all have length two. Every height in the hull lies between $-5/4$ and $5/4$, and both extremes occur over $(1/2,1/2)$ by averaging the upper endpoints of the positive-parity diagonal and the lower endpoints of the negative-parity diagonal, respectively. These facts give the two width bodies.
