## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and $d\ge2$. A compact convex body has the same full-dimensional simplex $T$ of volume $V$ at every binary base, and outside integer fibers of total $d$-volume $E$. In standard simplex coordinates put $b=1/(d+1)$, $\Gamma=(1-b)^d$, $\tau=1-2b$, and $\eta=\tau/(16\sqrt d)$. Let $g$ be the centroid, $\theta_i$ the unit normals in the directions of the barycentric gradients, and $F(u)=|T\cap\{u\cdot(x-g)\ge0\}|/V$ on the unit sphere. Define $G_d=\min_{\min_i\lVert u-\theta_i\rVert\ge\eta}(F(u)-\Gamma)>0$ on that sphere.

If $(E/V)^{1/d}\le\tau/4$, then for every outside positive fiber $A\subseteq T$, every unit $u$ within $\eta$ of a canonical $\theta_i$, and every pair of thresholds with sum $2u\cdot g$, the two upper caps in $A$ and $T$ have combined mass at least $|A|$. If moreover
$$
E/V\le\min\{2^{-d-1},(\tau/4)^d,G_d/\Gamma\},
$$
then some binary core centroid has raw closed ambient halfspace depth at least $\Gamma(2^nV+E)/2^n$. The mass threshold is independent of $n$. For an affine image of the standard simplex the normal neighborhood is interpreted after mapping the continuous coordinates to the standard simplex.

## Proof

A common invertible affine map of the continuous coordinates changes all masses by one factor and preserves the halfspace problem. Work in the standard simplex. By the fixed-core census, every outside fiber is contained in $T$.

For a unit $u$ with $\lVert u-\theta_i\rVert\le\eta$, rescale its functional by $\lVert\nabla\lambda_i\rVert$ and add the constant term of $\lambda_i$. This gives $f(x)=\lambda_i(x)+v\cdot x$, with $\lVert v\rVert\le\sqrt d\,\eta=\tau/16=:\epsilon$. All points of $T$ have Euclidean norm at most one. Thus if $m=\min_A f$, $h=\max_T f$, and $W=\max_Tf-\min_Tf$, then $m+h-2f(g)\ge\tau-4\epsilon$ and $W\le1+2\epsilon$. Put $r=(|A|/V)^{1/d}\le\tau/4$. We obtain
$$
m+h-2f(g)-Wr
\ \ge\ \frac{3\tau}{4}-(1+\tau/8)\frac{\tau}{4}
\ \ge\ \frac{15\tau}{32}>0.
$$
The $T$-cap at threshold $h-Wr$ contains a copy of $T$ scaled by $r$ about a maximizing point of $f$, so it has mass at least $|A|$. If the actual $T$-cap has smaller mass, its threshold is above $h-Wr$. The $A$ threshold is then below $2f(g)-h+Wr<m$, so that cap captures all of $A$. The affine rescaling preserves the condition that the two thresholds are opposite about the value at $g$. This proves the first claim for all shifts.

For the depth claim, $F$ is continuous by hyperplane nullity and fact 53fe12b742a7608d gives the lower bound $\Gamma$ with equality exactly at the canonical normals. The closed set in the definition of $G_d$ excludes these normals and is nonempty, for instance it contains $-e_1$. Thus its minimum is positive. The fixed-core census applies since $E/V\le 2^{-d-1}<2^{-d}$. Choose an anchor $q$ whose group mass is at least $E/2^n$. The groups include empty groups, so this choice also applies when there are no outside fibers. Its outside bases and their distinct binary partners are opposite about $q$ and none of the partners equals $q$.

Consider a closed through-cap at $(q,g)$ with unit continuous normal $u$. Near any canonical normal, its two thresholds on each paired group of bases have sum $2u\cdot g$. They pay at least the group mass by the first claim and the distinct partners. The own trace pays at least $\Gamma V$, so the cap pays at least $\Gamma V+E/2^n\ge\Gamma V+\Gamma E/2^n$. Away from the neighborhoods the own trace alone has mass at least $(\Gamma+G_d)V\ge\Gamma V+\Gamma E/2^n$ by the displayed threshold. A pure-base through-cap contains the entire own fiber, of mass $V>\Gamma V+\Gamma E/2^n$ because $\Gamma<1/2$, $n\ge1$, and $E/V<1$. Here $\Gamma<1/2$ follows from $(1+1/d)^d>2$ by the binomial expansion for $d\ge2$. Every containing cap contains its parallel through-cap. The total mixed volume is $2^nV+E$, proving the result.
