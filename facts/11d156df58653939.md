## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\) be an integer, let \(\Gamma:\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be coordinatewise nondecreasing, and assume \(\Gamma(\mathbf0)=0\). Let \(T\ge0\) be an integer and \(\beta\ge0\) a real number. Define the complete integer-height table, with one atom at every listed spatial point and height, by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T&:=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&:=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}:=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{Z}\quad((\mathbf x,t)\in A_T).
\end{aligned}
$$

For \(\mathbf z\in A_T\), let

$$
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf v\in A_T\cap H}p_{\mathbf v},
\qquad
M_3(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
$$

where the infimum is over all closed affine halfspaces \(H=\lbrace\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^{k+1}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\ge b\rbrace\) with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). Then

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$

The symbols \(A_T,Z,p,\tau_p,M_3\) have exactly the table, probability, ambient-halfspace, and moment normalizations of accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977`; only its separable energy hypothesis is replaced by the present monotonicity hypothesis. The quantity \(Z\) is an auxiliary table normalizer. The locked problem's \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S\), its mixed volume, and any locked notation \(N\) are not redefined. The only imported mathematical estimates are the Boolean disjoint-pair inequality of accepted fact `c13fd517ed177886` and the auxiliary scalar height estimate proved in accepted fact `cd9532b1d59c5977`, stated with exact maps below.

## Proof

The support is finite and nonempty, since \((\mathbf0,0)\in A_T\) and every supported height belongs to \(\lbrace0,\ldots,T\rbrace\). Thus \(0<Z<\infty\). No convex interpolation or geometric representation of \(\Gamma\) is used.

We first state the precise scalar estimate consumed from `cd9532b1d59c5977`. For an integer \(M\ge0\) and \(0<r\le1\), put \(Z_{\rm aux}:=\sum_{i=0}^M r^i\), let \(q_j:=r^j/Z_{\rm aux}\) for \(0\le j\le M\), and put \(q_j:=0\) otherwise. For \(F_q(j):=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min\lbrace q_{j+h},q_{j-h}\rbrace\), its scalar estimate is

$$
\sum_{j=0}^M q_jF_q(j)^3\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{1}
$$

These are exactly that predecessor's auxiliary \(M,r,Z_{\rm aux},q_j,F_q(j)\); \(Z_{\rm aux}\) does not denote the full table normalizer \(Z\). In particular its estimate includes \(M=0\) and \(r=1\). To recall why no regularity assumption is hidden here, write \(C_j:=\sum_{i=0}^j q_i\) and \(D_j:=\sum_{i=j}^M q_i\). The scalar closed-halfspace depth is \(\min\lbrace C_j,D_j\rbrace\). The consecutive probability intervals \([C_j-q_j,C_j]\) partition \([0,1]\) up to endpoints; for \(v\) in the \(j\)-th interval, \(v\le C_j\) and \(1-v\le D_j\). Therefore

$$
\sum_{j=0}^M q_j\min\lbrace C_j,D_j\rbrace^6
\ge\int_0^1\min\lbrace v,1-v\rbrace^6\,dv
=\frac1{448}.
$$

With \(b_j:=\min\lbrace j,M-j\rbrace\), the reflected mass is \(F_q(j)=q_j(1+2\sum_{h=1}^{b_j}r^h)\). If \(j\le M/2\), this is at least \(r^jC_j\), while \(D_j=r^j(\sum_{i=0}^{M-j}r^i)/Z_{\rm aux}\le r^j\); hence \(F_q(j)\ge C_jD_j\ge\min\lbrace C_j,D_j\rbrace^2\). If \(j\ge M/2\), it is at least \(q_j\sum_{h=0}^{M-j}r^h=D_j\ge\min\lbrace C_j,D_j\rbrace^2\). This verifies (1), including both scalar boundary cases.

For every integer \(E\ge0\), define \(A(E):=\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t}\), with \(A(E)=0\) when \(E>T\). For \(0\le E\le T\), let \(q_t^{(E)}:=e^{-\beta t}/A(E)\) on \(E\le t\le T\), zero elsewhere. The exact scalar normalization map into (1) is

$$
j=t-E,\qquad M=T-E,\qquad r=e^{-\beta},\qquad
Z_{\rm aux}=e^{\beta E}A(E),\qquad q_t^{(E)}=q_{t-E}.
\tag{2}
$$

Define \(F_E(t):=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min\lbrace q_{t+h}^{(E)},q_{t-h}^{(E)}\rbrace=F_q(t-E)\). The same displacement sum gives

$$
F_E(t)=\frac1{A(E)}
\sum_{|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)},
\qquad
\sum_{t=E}^Tq_t^{(E)}F_E(t)^3\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{3}
$$

The summand formula follows because both endpoint heights must lie in \([E,T]\), and the smaller exponential mass occurs at the larger height \(t+|h|\).

The exact Boolean input from `c13fd517ed177886` is the following independent assertion. If \(\mathcal D,\mathcal E\subseteq2^L\) are downsets of a finite ground set \(L\), then the ordered disjoint-pair count satisfies

$$
\mathfrak D(\mathcal D,\mathcal E)
:=|\lbrace(P,Q)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal E:P\cap Q=\varnothing\rbrace|
\ge(|\mathcal D|\,|\mathcal E|)^{3/4}.
\tag{4}
$$

The map from that predecessor's Boolean assertion is literal: its finite ground set is the present \(L\), its downsets are the present \(\mathcal D,\mathcal E\), and its \(\mathfrak D\) counts the same ordered pairs. Empty sets, both orientations, and the empty ground set are included. In particular \(\mathfrak D(\mathcal D,\mathcal D)\ge|\mathcal D|^{3/2}\). No scalar cutoff or actual-body conclusion of that predecessor is invoked.

Write \([k]:=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\), and let \(\mathbf1_L\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\) be the indicator vector of \(L\subseteq[k]\). Every \(\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) has a unique representation \(\mathbf x=\mathbf1_L+\mathbf1_I\), with \(L=\lbrace i:x_i>0\rbrace\) and \(I=\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\subseteq L\). For each \(L\subseteq[k]\), put \(\mathbf y_L:=\mathbf1_L\), \(e_L:=\Gamma(\mathbf y_L)\), and \(E_L(I):=\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I)\). In this extension the binary root's baseline energy is \(e_L\); in the separable predecessor it was \(|L|\). Monotonicity gives \(e_L\le E_L(I)\) for all \(I\subseteq L\). Define the original unnormalized block mass by \(Z_L:=\sum_{I\subseteq L}A(E_L(I))\). The unique representation partitions every original atom, including its complete height interval, so

$$
Z=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}Z_L.
\tag{5}
$$

Fix \(L\) and an integer \(E\ge e_L\). Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_L(E)&:=\lbrace I\subseteq L:E_L(I)\le E\rbrace,\qquad
m_L(E):=|\mathcal D_L(E)|,\\
\mathcal F_L(E)&:=\lbrace(P,Q)\in\mathcal D_L(E)^2:P\cap Q=\varnothing\rbrace,\qquad
R_L(E):=|\mathcal F_L(E)|.
\end{aligned}
$$

If \(J\subseteq I\), then \(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_J\le\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_I\) coordinatewise, so \(E_L(J)\le E_L(I)\). Thus \(\mathcal D_L(E)\) is a downset, and (4) yields \(R_L(E)\ge m_L(E)^{3/2}\). The pair family is invariant under \((P,Q)\mapsto(Q,P)\). For every pair in it, both reflected spatial endpoints lie in the ternary cube, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q&\le\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P,
&\Gamma(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q)&\le E_L(P)\le E,\\
\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q&\le\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_Q,
&\Gamma(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q)&\le E_L(Q)\le E.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

These inequalities use only coordinatewise monotonicity; interaction terms in \(\Gamma\) are unrestricted.

Suppose now \(e_L\le E\le T\). For a fixed integer \(t\in[E,T]\), every \((P,Q)\in\mathcal F_L(E)\) and integer \(h\) with \(|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace\) give original atoms

$$
\mathbf z_+=(\mathbf y_L+\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_Q,t+h),\qquad
\mathbf z_-=(\mathbf y_L-\mathbf1_P+\mathbf1_Q,t-h).
\tag{7}
$$

Their energies are at most \(E\) by (6), and both heights lie in \([E,T]\), so they belong to \(A_T\). Their midpoint is the original supported atom \((\mathbf y_L,t)\).

For this fixed root, the map \((P,Q,h)\mapsto\mathbf z_+\) is injective: the spatial coordinates equal to \(2\) recover \(P\), the zero coordinates within \(L\) recover \(Q\), and the height recovers \(h\). The involution \((P,Q,h)\mapsto(Q,P,-h)\) exchanges the two endpoints. Its only fixed index is \((\varnothing,\varnothing,0)\), which gives the root. Consequently its other orbits give disjoint unordered pairs of distinct original atoms, disjoint also from the fixed root atom.

Every closed affine halfspace containing the root contains at least one endpoint of every such pair: exclusion of both would put the defining affine functional strictly below its threshold at their midpoint. The minimum original mass of the two endpoints is \(e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}/Z\). Sum this minimum once over each two-element orbit and charge half of the fixed root mass. This is at most the halfspace's mass and equals half the sum over all oriented indices. Therefore (3) gives the uniform original-depth estimate

$$
\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)
\ge\frac{R_L(E)}{2Z}
\sum_{|h|\le\min\lbrace t-E,T-t\rbrace}e^{-\beta(t+|h|)}
=\frac{R_L(E)A(E)}{2Z}F_E(t).
\tag{8}
$$

The original sampling probability at this root and height is \(p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}=(A(E)/Z)q_t^{(E)}\). Cube (8), multiply by this probability, sum over \(t=E,\ldots,T\), and apply (3). The result is

$$
\sum_{t=E}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
\ge\frac{R_L(E)^3A(E)^4}{8Z^4}
\sum_{t=E}^Tq_t^{(E)}F_E(t)^3
\ge\frac{R_L(E)^3A(E)^4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{9}
$$

All four factors of the normalizer here are the original full \(Z\). The auxiliary scalar law in (2) has only expressed the sum of original-law contributions in (9).

Let \(\mathcal E_L:=\lbrace E_L(I):I\subseteq L,\ E_L(I)\le T\rbrace\), and define \(V_L:=\max_{E\in\mathcal E_L}R_L(E)^{3/4}A(E)\), with \(V_L:=0\) if \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty. When nonempty, this is a finite maximum at an integer \(E\ge e_L\). Choose one such maximizing threshold. Equation (9) and nonnegativity of the unused root heights imply

$$
\sum_{t=e_L}^T p_{(\mathbf y_L,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf y_L,t)^3
\ge\frac{V_L^4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{10}
$$

If \(\mathcal E_L\) is empty, then \(e_L>T\), since \(I=\varnothing\) would otherwise be feasible. Conversely \(e_L>T\) implies emptiness by monotonicity. Thus in this case the left side of (10) is an empty sum and both sides vanish. The binary spatial roots are distinct for distinct \(L\). We have chosen only one threshold per root, so summing (10) counts each sampled root and height at most once. With \(\mathcal V_4:=\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L^4\), it follows that

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\mathcal V_4}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{11}
$$

Endpoint atoms may be reused in estimates of different roots because each estimate separately bounds that root's depth.

For a fixed \(L\), enumerate the feasible subsets as \(I_1,\ldots,I_{b_L}\) in nondecreasing order of \(E_L(I_j)\), breaking ties arbitrarily. Here \(b_L\) is the number of feasible subsets and is unrelated to any locked count notation. At threshold \(E_L(I_j)\), all the first \(j\) subsets lie in the downset. Thus

$$
V_L\ge R_L(E_L(I_j))^{3/4}A(E_L(I_j))
\ge m_L(E_L(I_j))^{9/8}A(E_L(I_j))
\ge j^{9/8}A(E_L(I_j)).
\tag{12}
$$

Ties cause no loss because every sublevel includes all subsets at equality. Since \(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-9/8}\le1+\int_1^{\infty}x^{-9/8}\,dx=9\), summing (12) gives \(Z_L\le9V_L\). If \(b_L=0\), both quantities are zero and the same estimate holds. Equation (5) and Hölder's inequality for the \(2^k\) nonnegative stock values now give

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z^4
&\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L\right)^4\\
&\le9^4\left(\sum_{L\subseteq[k]}V_L^4\right)(2^k)^3
=9^4\mathcal V_4\,8^k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

For \(L=\varnothing\), its only subset is empty, \(e_L=E_L(\varnothing)=0\), \(R_L(0)=1\), and \(A(0)>0\). Hence \(V_{\varnothing}>0\) and \(\mathcal V_4>0\). Combining (11) and (13) proves

$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{3584\cdot9^4\cdot8^k}
=\frac1{448\cdot9^4}\,8^{-(k+1)}
=\frac1{2939328}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
$$

All energy sublevels, endpoint memberships, height intervals, and halfspaces above use weak inequalities, so every equality boundary is retained. All heights and displacements are integral. The case \(\beta=0\) is \(r=1\) in (2), and a singleton height interval is \(M=0\), both included in (1). Zero energy increments and arbitrary ties do not affect downward closure, the rank estimate, or injectivity. When \(T=0\), the support can contain several zero-energy spatial points; every feasible threshold is then zero, the scalar law is a singleton, and the same proof applies. Empty labels and infeasible roots were handled explicitly. This establishes the theorem for all stated parameters.
