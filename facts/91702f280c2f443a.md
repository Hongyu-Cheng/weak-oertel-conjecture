## Statement

Fix formal planar fiber areas $(a,|K|,b)=(1/10,1,1)$, total $m=21/10$ and $\Gamma=4/9$. Let
$$
q=(37/100,367/800,367/800),\qquad
U=(19/200,1/160,1/160),\qquad V=(0,0,0).
$$
The columns have common sum $Q=93/200$ with $\Gamma\max(a,1,b)<Q<\Gamma m/2=7/15$. Whole volumes are strictly nonheavy and $\sqrt a+\sqrt b<2$. In each column $U_i+V_i\le a$, $Q\le3q_i$, and
$$
\sqrt{a-U_i}+1\le2\sqrt{1-q_i}.
$$
For every $\alpha\ge0$, the ordinary middle and left-endpoint profile expressions satisfy
$$
\sum_i(q_i-\alpha)_++9\alpha/4\ge1,\qquad
\sum_i(U_i-\alpha)_++9\alpha/4\ge1/10.
$$
Their exact minima are $101/100$ and $329/3200$, respectively.

There are no planar convex bodies $A,K,B$ of these areas at bases $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, and three closed ambient caps with these captures whose middle traces cover $K$. This nonrealizability holds without requiring the caps to attain any point's depth.

## Proof

The first and the other column sums are respectively $(74+19)/200$ and $(367+5)/800$, both $93/200$. The comparisons follow from $4/9<93/200<7/15$, the largest whole area $1<21/20$, and $\sqrt{1/10}<1$. Both $q_i$ and $U_i$ are strictly between zero and their corresponding whole-fiber areas. Each $U_i\le a$ and $q_i\ge37/100>Q/3$.

For three positive numbers $s_1\le s_2\le s_3$, the continuous piecewise linear function $\sum_i(s_i-\alpha)_++9\alpha/4$ has slope $-3/4$ below $s_1$ and slope at least $1/4$ above $s_1$ on every interval of linearity, including tied values. Its minimum is $s_3+s_2+s_1/4$. Substitution gives $2(367/800)+(37/100)/4=101/100$ and $19/200+1/160+(1/160)/4=329/3200$. Thus the full two profiles hold, as do the interval-distance consequences derived from them. A captured Brunn–Minkowski condition requiring two positive endpoint captures is inapplicable because every $V_i=0$.

Set $r=a-U_i$ and $t=1-q_i$. The residual inequality $\sqrt r+1\le2\sqrt t$ is equivalent to $4t-r-1\ge2\sqrt r$. For column 1, $(r,t)=(1/200,63/100)$, $4t-r-1=303/200>0$ and $(4t-r-1)^2-4r=91009/40000>0$. For the other columns, $(r,t)=(3/32,433/800)$, $4t-r-1=857/800>0$ and the corresponding squared gap is $494449/640000>0$.

Suppose such bodies and caps exist. For caps 2 and 3 take, on each endpoint, the points strictly outside both. Their areas are at least $1/10-2/160=7/80$ in $A$ and exactly $1$ in $B$, by subadditivity and the specified captures. For small $\varepsilon>0$ intersect each endpoint with the two closed inequalities requiring its ambient margins in caps 2 and 3 to be at most $-\varepsilon$. These compact convex subcuts increase to the strict residuals in area as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and have positive areas for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. The midpoint of the two subcuts lies in $K$ by compatibility. Each ambient affine margin at a midpoint is the average of the original endpoint margins, so this midpoint is still strictly outside both caps. The middle cover therefore puts it in cap 1. Apply planar Brunn–Minkowski to the two compact subcuts and pass to the limit. This forces the middle cap 1 area to be at least
$$
\frac{(\sqrt{7/80}+1)^2}{4}>\frac{25}{64}>\frac{37}{100},
$$
since $7/80>1/16$. This contradicts its specified capture.
