## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let M be any finite real number with M>=1. Let g be a log-concave probability density on the real line, and let f be any probability density such that f(x)<=M g(x) for Lebesgue-almost every x. Both densities have finite first and second absolute moments. Define the true original mean m_f=integral x f(x) dx. Then

\[
\min\left\{\int_{m_f}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx,
             \int_{-\infty}^{m_f}f(x)\,dx\right\}
\ge \frac{1}{512 M(1+\log M)}.
\tag{1}
\]

The caps in (1) integrate f, not g. No lower bound on f/g is assumed, and f need not be log-concave. Closed and open endpoint versions have the same mass. Here f and g denote the original and reference scalar densities, respectively; they do not denote the conditional density called f in accepted predecessor 3c78088dd84bbbdf. The precise scalar-density and standard-deviation aliases for that predecessor are specified in the proof. The notation T_g below is a survival function, distinct from the locked problem's mixed-integer set S.

## Proof

First we establish the moment and support facts without a bounded-support assumption. The positive support of g is an interval of positive length, and its interior I=(a,b) has g positive, finite, and continuous: log g is a finite concave function there. Endpoint values of the density affect none of the integrals. If b=+infinity, fix u in I. Integrability supplies v>u in I with 0<g(v)<g(u); otherwise g(x)>=g(u) for every x>u, contradicting integrability. Concavity gives, for x>=v,

\[
\log g(x)\le \log g(v)
 +\frac{\log g(v)-\log g(u)}{v-u}(x-v).
\tag{2}
\]

The coefficient of x-v is strictly negative, so this is an exponential upper bound on the unbounded right tail. Reflection gives the same assertion on an unbounded left tail. At a finite support endpoint the coordinate x is bounded on its adjacent tail, and integrability already controls its moments there. Combining those observations with integrability on a bounded central interval proves that g has finite first and second absolute moments (indeed every fixed nonnegative power moment). Since f<=M g, the same moments are finite for f.

Put m_g=integral x g(x) dx and sigma_g squared=integral (x-m_g)^2 g(x) dx. The variance is finite and strictly positive: zero variance would force a probability density to be concentrated at the single point m_g, which is impossible. Write sigma_g for its positive square root. The accepted scalar estimate proved and used in 3c78088dd84bbbdf is that a real log-concave probability density has essential supremum at most the reciprocal of its positive standard deviation. Its scalar density called f is here aliased to g, its real projection variable X is aliased to a real variable with density g, and its scalar standard deviation s is aliased to sigma_g. This estimate applies because g is log-concave and its variance has just been shown finite and positive. Thus

\[
g(x)\le \frac1{\sigma_g}\quad\hbox{a.e.},
\qquad f(x)\le \frac{M}{\sigma_g}\quad\hbox{a.e.}
\tag{3}
\]

No other conclusion or fiber hypothesis of that predecessor is used.

We next prove the reference tail estimate needed here directly. Define T_g(x)=integral from x to infinity of g. For x<y in I and u>=0, concavity of log g gives

\[
\log g(x+u)+\log g(y)
 \ge \log g(x)+\log g(y+u)
\tag{4}
\]

whenever y+u is in I. To verify (4), write x+u and y as the two convex combinations of x and y+u with complementary coefficients u/(y+u-x) and (y-x)/(y+u-x), and add their concavity inequalities. If y+u is beyond the upper support endpoint, the resulting ratio inequality g(y+u)/g(y)<=g(x+u)/g(x) still holds almost everywhere in u because its left side is zero. Integrating that ratio inequality over u>=0 yields

\[
\frac{T_g(y)}{g(y)}\le\frac{T_g(x)}{g(x)}.
\tag{5}
\]

These ratios are finite and positive on I. Consequently g/T_g is nondecreasing there, and (log T_g)'=-g/T_g is nonincreasing, so log T_g is concave on I. If a is finite, T_g=1 to the left of a and T_g tends to 1 at a from the right. Extending log T_g by zero there preserves concavity: it is continuous at a and its derivative changes from zero to a nonpositive, nonincreasing derivative. Thus log T_g is concave throughout the interval where T_g>0, including points below the positive support. At and beyond a finite b, T_g is zero; those points need no logarithm in the tail argument.

Chebyshev's inequality, with the actual mean and variance of g, gives

\[
T_g(m_g-2\sigma_g)\ge\frac34,
\qquad T_g(m_g+2\sigma_g)\le\frac14.
\tag{6}
\]

Set alpha=(log 3)/4. If the second survival function value in (6) is zero, all later right tails are zero. Otherwise, monotonicity of secant slopes of the concave function log T_g between m_g-2 sigma_g and m_g+2 sigma_g gives, for t>=2 sigma_g,

\[
T_g(m_g+t)
 \le\frac14\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha}{\sigma_g}(t-2\sigma_g)\right]
 =\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha t}{\sigma_g}\right].
\tag{7}
\]

If m_g+t is outside the upper support, (7) holds with left side zero. For every real t<=2 sigma_g, the elementary bound T_g<=1 is at most 2 exp(-alpha t/sigma_g), since 2 exp(-2 alpha)=2/sqrt3>1. Together with (7) this proves, for EVERY real t,

\[
T_g(m_g+t)\le2\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha t}{\sigma_g}\right].
\tag{8}
\]

Reflection of g gives in addition, for t>=0,

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{m_g-t}g(x)\,dx
 \le2\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha t}{\sigma_g}\right].
\tag{9}
\]

Here and below a random variable X_f has density f. By the nonnegative layer-cake identity and (9),

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_g-m_f
&=\mathbb E(m_g-X_f)
 \le\mathbb E(m_g-X_f)_+\\
&=\int_0^\infty\Pr(X_f\le m_g-t)\,dt\\
&\le\int_0^\infty
       \min\{1,2M e^{-\alpha t/\sigma_g}\}\,dt
 =\frac{\sigma_g}{\alpha}(1+\log(2M)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last equality splits the integral at (sigma_g/alpha) log(2M), a nonnegative number. Applying (10) to the reflected f and g also bounds m_f-m_g by the same quantity. Hence absolute mean displacement is at most (sigma_g/alpha)(1+log(2M)). In particular, (8) and the lower bound on m_f from (10) imply, for t>=0,

\[
\begin{aligned}
M T_g(m_f+t)
&\le2M\exp\left[\frac{\alpha(m_g-m_f)}{\sigma_g}\right]
          e^{-\alpha t/\sigma_g}\\
&\le4e M^2 e^{-\alpha t/\sigma_g}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

This uses (8) for all real arguments, so it is valid also when m_f+t<m_g.

Define the ORIGINAL normalized cap masses L=integral from minus infinity to m_f of f and R=integral from m_f to infinity of f. They satisfy L+R=1 and L,R>0. Indeed, if R=0, the nonnegative variable m_f-X_f has expectation zero and is therefore zero almost surely, contrary to the existence of a probability density; the case L=0 is the reflected argument. Finite first moments and the true-mean identity give the common first moment

\[
J:=\int_{m_f}^\infty(x-m_f)f(x)\,dx
  =\int_{-\infty}^{m_f}(m_f-x)f(x)\,dx.
\tag{12}
\]

By (3), a length-t interval contains f-mass at most Mt/sigma_g. Therefore Pr(X_f<=m_f-t)>=L-Mt/sigma_g for t>=0. The layer-cake identity for the left integral in (12) yields

\[
J\ge\int_0^{L\sigma_g/M}
        \left(L-\frac{Mt}{\sigma_g}\right)dt
 =\frac{L^2\sigma_g}{2M}.
\tag{13}
\]

For the right integral in (12), its tails have original f-mass at most R and, by upper domination and (11), at most 4e M^2 exp(-alpha t/sigma_g). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
J&\le\int_0^\infty
       \min\{R,4e M^2e^{-\alpha t/\sigma_g}\}\,dt\\
 &=\frac{\sigma_g R}{\alpha}
       \left[1+\log\left(\frac{4e M^2}{R}\right)\right]
 =\frac{\sigma_g R}{\alpha}
       \left[2+\log\left(\frac{4M^2}{R}\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The splitting point (sigma_g/alpha) log(4e M^2/R) is nonnegative because 0<R<=1 and M>=1. Combining (13) and (14) cancels the actual standard deviation and proves

\[
\frac{\alpha L^2}{2M}
 \le R\left[2+\log\left(\frac{4M^2}{R}\right)\right].
\tag{15}
\]

If R>1/2, the desired right-cap bound is immediate. Suppose R<=1/2. Then L>=1/2, and (15) implies

\[
\frac{\alpha}{8M}
 \le R\left[2+\log\left(\frac{4M^2}{R}\right)\right].
\tag{16}
\]

Put kappa_M=1+log M and R_0=1/(512 M kappa_M). The elementary inequality log M<=M-1 gives 1<=kappa_M<=M. The function r -> r[2+log(4M^2/r)] is strictly increasing for 0<r<=1, since its derivative is 1+log(4M^2/r)>0. At R_0 its value is

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_0\left[2+\log\left(\frac{4M^2}{R_0}\right)\right]
&=\frac{2+\log2048+3\log M+\log\kappa_M}
        {512 M\kappa_M}\\
&<\frac{13+4\log M}{512 M\kappa_M}
 \le\frac{13}{512M}
 <\frac1{32M}
 <\frac{\log3}{32M}
 =\frac{\alpha}{8M}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

Here log kappa_M<=log M follows from kappa_M<=M; log2048=11 log2<11 and log3>1 follow from 2<e<3. Consequently R<=R_0 contradicts (16), by the stated monotonicity. This proves R>R_0 in all cases.

Finally replace f(x),g(x) by f(-x),g(-x). They are again probability densities with the same upper-domination constant M, the reference density is log-concave, and the original true mean is -m_f. The just-proved right-cap bound becomes the left-cap bound for the original f. This proves (1), including M=1, bounded or unbounded support, gaps in the positive support of f, and arbitrary zero likelihood sets. A density cannot have zero variance or an atom at the mean, so no additional degenerate probability case is hidden.
