## Statement

Let $S$ be the sampled mixed set of a compact convex body, with $q\ge1$ its largest integer fiber dimension. List its positive $q$-volume fibers $K_i$ at distinct bases $p_i$, let $V_i=\mathcal H^q(K_i)$, and let $\mu$ sum their intrinsic $q$-volumes. Suppose a finite family of original closed ambient halfspaces $H_1,\ldots,H_N$ covers these fibers. Set $A_{ai}=\mathcal H^q(K_i\cap H_a)$, $\gamma_q=(q/(q+1))^q$ and $\kappa=1/\gamma_q-2$. For nonnegative weights $\rho_i$ and thresholds $\alpha_i$, put
$$
T=\sum_i\rho_i V_i,\qquad
E_i(\alpha_i)=\sum_a(A_{ai}-\alpha_i)_+ +\alpha_i/\gamma_q-V_i,\qquad
L_a=\max(\{\rho_i:A_{ai}>\alpha_i\}\cup\{0\}).
$$
Then each surplus is nonnegative and
$$
T+\sum_i\rho_i E_i(\alpha_i)
=\sum_a\sum_i\rho_i(A_{ai}-\alpha_i)_+
  +\gamma_q^{-1}\sum_i\rho_i\alpha_i
\le\sum_a L_a\mu(H_a)+\gamma_q^{-1}\sum_i\rho_i\alpha_i.
$$
In particular, the last cap term is at most $B\sum_a L_a$ if each original cap has mass at most $B$.

Write $A_{(s)i}$ for the decreasing ranks of the actual local masses, with a fixed ordering of ties and zero padding. Write $a_s(i)$ for the corresponding original labels when present. Set $e_i=(V_i-A_{(1)i})^{1/q}$, $R=\sum_i\rho_i e_i^q$, and $E_i^{\mathrm{top}}=A_{(1)i}+A_{(2)i}+\kappa A_{(3)i}-V_i$. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_a^{23}&=\max_i\rho_i(\mathbf1_{\{a=a_2(i)\}}+\kappa\mathbf1_{\{a=a_3(i)\}}),\\
D_a^{123}&=\max_i\rho_i(\mathbf1_{\{a=a_1(i)\}}+\mathbf1_{\{a=a_2(i)\}}+\kappa\mathbf1_{\{a=a_3(i)\}}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where a padded rank contributes no indicator. Then $E_i^{\mathrm{top}}\ge0$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
T-R&=\sum_i\rho_i A_{(1)i},\\
R+\sum_i\rho_i E_i^{\mathrm{top}}
 &=\sum_i\rho_i(A_{(2)i}+\kappa A_{(3)i})\le\sum_a D_a^{23}\mu(H_a),\\
T+\sum_i\rho_i E_i^{\mathrm{top}}
 &\le\sum_a D_a^{123}\mu(H_a).
\end{aligned}
$$
If every cap has mass at most $B$, the last sum is at most $B\sum_a D_a^{123}$.

In particular the weights may be the contact weights of the finite one-cap residual optimization. Minimize $\sum_i h_i^q$ subject to $h_i\ge e_i$ and $h_j\ge h_i+g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)$ for all distinct $i,j$. At an optimizer, let $\lambda_i$ be the nonnegative KKT multipliers of $e_i-h_i\le0$. Then the optimum is precisely the above $R$ for $\rho_i=\lambda_i$ if $q=1$, and, if $q>1$, for $\rho_i=\lambda_i/(q e_i^{q-1})$ at $\lambda_i>0$ and zero otherwise. In the latter case every positive multiplier has $e_i>0$.

## Proof

Identify each positive fiber's affine span isometrically with $\mathbb R^q$. A constant trace can be replaced for this local argument by an intrinsic halfspace containing the entire fiber or missing it, preserving all cap memberships in that fiber. Thus ac7ba1e1e0ebc955 applies to its local cover at each $\alpha_i$, giving $E_i(\alpha_i)\ge0$. The weighted definition gives the first identity. For each original label $a$, only $A_{ai}>\alpha_i$ contribute to its sum. On them $\rho_i\le L_a$ and $(A_{ai}-\alpha_i)_+\le A_{ai}$, so the sum is at most $L_a\sum_iA_{ai}=L_a\mu(H_a)$. This proves the weighted bound with original cap labels.

At $\alpha_i=A_{(3)i}$ the sum of positive parts is $A_{(1)i}+A_{(2)i}-2A_{(3)i}$, including ties and padding. Hence $E_i^{\mathrm{top}}=E_i(A_{(3)i})$. The coefficient $\kappa$ is nonnegative by $(1+1/q)^q\ge2$, including equality at $q=1$. The best single-cap residual is exactly $e_i^q$ because removing one original cap leaves $V_i-A_{ai}$. Substitution proves the two ranked identities. For either ranked sum collect the coefficient of each $A_{ai}$ by its original label. The coefficients are nonnegative and bounded by the corresponding $D_a^{23}$ or $D_a^{123}$. Summing the actual areas of a fixed cap gives $\mu(H_a)$. There are finitely many tie orderings, so any of the displayed total load expressions attains its minimum over them.

For the contact identification, the sampled program is feasible by a constant threshold and $g_i=0$. The projection of its finite system of affine inequalities onto the $h$ coordinates is a closed polyhedron by elimination, and objective sublevels bound the nonnegative $h_i$. Thus the minimum is attained. For $q>1$ use the differentiable objective extension $\sum_i(h_i)_+^q$ and for $q=1$ use the linear objective. A direction satisfying the homogeneous inequalities for the active constraints is feasible for a short positive step, so the negative gradient is in the cone of the active normals by finite Farkas. If $\nu_{ij}\ge0$ denotes the multiplier for the $(i,j)$ supporting inequality, stationarity and complementarity give
$$
\begin{gathered}
s_i-\lambda_i+\sum_j\nu_{ij}-\sum_j\nu_{ji}=0,\qquad
\sum_j\nu_{ij}(p_j-p_i)=0,\\
\lambda_i(h_i-e_i)=0,\qquad
\nu_{ij}(h_i+g_i\cdot(p_j-p_i)-h_j)=0,
\end{gathered}
$$
where $s_i=q h_i^{q-1}$ for $q>1$ and $s_i=1$ for $q=1$. Multiplying the row identity by $g_i$ and using complementarity gives $\sum_j\nu_{ij}(h_i-h_j)=0$. Multiplying the stationarity equations by $h_i$ and summing yields $\sum_i\lambda_i e_i=q\sum_i h_i^q$. If $q>1$ and $h_i=0$, the row identity just obtained shows that positive outgoing multipliers go only to other zero-height indices. Summing stationarity on the zero-height indices shows that their total $\lambda_i$ plus the total incoming $\nu$ from positive-height indices is zero, since their $s_i$ vanish. Each such $\lambda_i$ is therefore zero. Consequently every $\lambda_i>0$ has $e_i=h_i>0$ in the power case. In both cases the specified $\rho_i$ are nonnegative and $\sum_i\rho_i e_i^q=\sum_i h_i^q$, as required.
