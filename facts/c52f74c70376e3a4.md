## Statement

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a full-dimensional rational convex polygon with centroid zero. Let $k\in\mathbb Q^2$ satisfy $-1/2<k\cdot x<1$ for $x\in D$, put $B=\{x/(1-k\cdot x):x\in D\}$ and $K=\operatorname{conv}(B\times\{-1\},(0,0,2))$. Then $|K|=|B|$, $g_K=((3/4)g_B,-1/4)$, and $z=3k\cdot x$ cuts the graph over $(2/3)D$ with centroid zero. For the apex-facing cap, with inward unit normal $u$ and section area $S$,
$$
p=(8/27)|D|/|B|,\qquad s=S(u\cdot g_K)/|K|=-|D|(1+9k\cdot g_B)/(9|B|).
$$
A vertical plane over a centered chord of $B$ has centroidal section, the same cap fraction as for $B$, and scalar $s$ equal to $9/8$ times the planar scalar. The oblique nonsimplex case occurs when $k\ne0$ and $D$ has at least four vertices.

## Proof

The projective map $x\mapsto x/(1-k\cdot x)$ is defined on $D$, and it takes segments to segments there because its denominator is positive. Thus $B$ is the convex hull of the images of the vertices of $D$. A ray segment from apex $(0,0,2)$ to $(b,-1)$ has points $(tb,2-3t)$ with $0\le t\le1$. If $b=x/(1-k\cdot x)$, its intersection with $z=3k\cdot y$ is at $t=2(1-k\cdot x)/3$, strictly between zero and one by the bounds on $k\cdot x$. Its projected point is $tb=2x/3$. This gives exactly the stated graph with centroid zero, since the graph area is a constant multiple of planar area. Horizontal slices of the whole cone at parameter $t$ have area $t^2|B|$, so its volume is $|B|$ and the volume-weighted mean of $t$ is $3/4$. Hence $g_K=((3/4)g_B,-1/4)$.

The cap above the graph is the pyramid from the apex to that section. The section area divided by $\lVert(-3k,1)\rVert$ is $4|D|/9$ by the graph Jacobian. The apex raw margin is $2$, giving pyramid volume $(2/3)(4|D|/9)$ and the stated fraction. The centroid raw margin is $-(1+9k\cdot g_B)/4$, giving the stated scalar $s$.

A vertical plane over a centered base chord $[-q,q]$ cuts the triangle with vertices $(q,-1),(-q,-1),(0,0,2)$, with centroid zero. Its cap volume fraction equals the planar fraction because every horizontal slice is a homothetic copy of $B$ about the origin. Its section area is $3/2$ times the base chord length. The horizontal centroid projection is $3/4$ times that for $B$, and the volume normalization equals the planar area normalization, so the scalar ratio is $9/8$. Rational data yield rational fractions and scalars for the oblique section and for vertical chords with rational endpoints. When $k\ne0$ and $D$ has at least four vertices, the section is oblique and the cone has at least five vertices, so is not a simplex.
