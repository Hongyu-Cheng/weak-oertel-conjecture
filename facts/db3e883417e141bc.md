## Statement

For every $d\ge1$ there is an actual polytopal body with one integer base dimension and a positive weighted ambient-cap source on the marked own-fiber boundaries. It admits a physical normal-preserving martingale to the full unweighted boundary measure, with zero incoming mass in each normal sector and affine sampled supports in all directions. After straightening the $e_1$-normal facet, the unweighted reduced volume has mass one at each of the three integer bases $0,1,2$, while the inherited source has masses $(3/4,3/2,3/4)$. Its $z^2$ moment is $9/2$, compared with $5$ for the reduced target. When $d\ge2$, the reduced source gives positive mass to the interiors of the reduced fibers and is singular with respect to their own-fiber boundary measure. Each of the two original generating caps has bulk mass $2$, and their coefficient-weighted bulk budget is the total bulk mass $3$.

## Proof

Take $C=[0,2]\times[-1/2,1/2]^d$ and let $\eta$ be the marked own-fiber boundary measure. Give the source density $f=(3/4)(\mathbf 1_{\{z\le3/2\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{z\ge1/2\}})$ relative to $\eta$. The caps miss integer bases on their boundaries and each contains exactly two unit-volume fibers, proving the bulk claims. For each common continuous boundary point and its normal, keep source mass $3/4$ at each of bases $0,2$ there, and send the base-$1$ mass $3/2$ to $0,1,2$ in amounts $(1/4,1,1/4)$. Each target base receives mass $1$. The middle row has base first moment $3/2$ and therefore mean $1$, and all rows fix the continuous point and its normal. This constructs the coupling directly.

Every marked sector is the same continuous face across the three bases. The rows preserve the mark and cannot enter such a facet from outside. At $u=e_1$ the reduced polytope is $[0,2]\times[-1/2,1/2]^{d-1}$. The fixed-base isometry maps the facet measure to ordinary $(d-1)$-volume, with the singleton-counting convention when $d=1$. The same density yields the claimed masses and the quadratic moments $(3/2)+4(3/4)=9/2$ and $1+4=5$. For $d\ge2$ the reduced fiber interiors have full volume, while their own-fiber boundary measures are supported on the boundary. This proves the singularity.
