## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), put \(\kappa=\log2\) and define the integrable log-concave function
\[
g_n(\mathbf x,t)=e^{-t}\mathbf1_{\{t\ge\kappa\|\mathbf x\|_1\}},
\qquad (\mathbf x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R.
\]
Its scalar mixed-integer density measure and its total mass are
\[
\mu_{g_n}(A)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_A(\mathbf z,t)g_n(\mathbf z,t)\,dt,
\qquad W=\mu_{g_n}(\mathbb R^{n+1}).
\]
Here \(A\) is any measurable ambient set; the measure is supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R\). Its unnormalized halfspace depth is \(h_{g_n}(\mathbf y)=\inf\{\mu_{g_n}(H):H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf y\}\). This notation refers to the weighted density model, not the Hausdorff measure of a convex body from the locked problem.

For each \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define the fiber mass \(a_{\mathbf z}=\int_{\mathbb R}g_n(\mathbf z,t)\,dt\) and the scalar fiber barycenter \(m_{\mathbf z}=a_{\mathbf z}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}t g_n(\mathbf z,t)\,dt\). Define the reflection score \(S_{\mathbf j}=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\min(a_{\mathbf z},a_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf z})\) for \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\).

For real \(4\le p<\infty\), the reflection-power rule first chooses a maximizer \(\mathbf j\) of \(S_{\mathbf j}\), and then uses the probability weights
\[
\lambda_{\mathbf j,\mathbf z}^{(p)}=
\frac{\min(a_{\mathbf z},a_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf z})^p}
{\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^n}\min(a_{\mathbf u},a_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf u})^p}
\]
to take the barycenter of the points \((\mathbf z,m_{\mathbf z})\). For \(p=\infty\), use only the fibers maximizing \(\min(a_{\mathbf z},a_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf z})\), after the same choice of reflection center. Both that center and that fiber are unique in this family, so this endpoint rule is unambiguous.

For these densities, \(a_{\mathbf z}=2^{-\|\mathbf z\|_1}\), \(m_{\mathbf z}=1+\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1\), \(W=3^n\), and \(\mathbf j=\mathbf0\) is the unique maximal reflection center. Write
\[
\mu_p=\frac{2^{1-p}}{1-2^{-2p}}\quad(4\le p<\infty),
\qquad \mu_\infty=0,
\qquad t_p=1+\kappa n\mu_p.
\]
The rule selects \(\mathbf y_{n,p}=(\mathbf0,t_p)\), which is in the support of the mixed-integer density. In particular, \(t_\infty=1\), and all these points lie in the single closed halfspace \(H_n=\{(\mathbf x,t):t\le t_4\}\), where \(t_4=1+(32/255)(\log2)n\).

With \(\delta=\log(45/34)-(32/85)\log2\), one has \(\delta>443/57800>0\), and, simultaneously for every \(n\ge1\) and every \(p\in[4,\infty]\),
\[
\frac{h_{g_n}(\mathbf y_{n,p})}{2^{-n}W}
\le \frac{\mu_{g_n}(H_n)}{2^{-n}W}
\le e^3e^{-\delta n}.
\]
Consequently no positive absolute constant lower-bounds the selected depth divided by \(2^{-n}W\), even if the exponent is chosen separately in every dimension from \([4,\infty]\).

## Proof

The support of \(g_n\) is the convex epigraph of the convex function \(\kappa\|\mathbf x\|_1\). On that support, \(\log g_n(\mathbf x,t)=-t\) is affine, so \(g_n\) is log-concave. Integration first in \(t\) gives \(\int_{\mathbb R^{n+1}}g_n=(2/\kappa)^n<\infty\). Direct one-dimensional integration on each lattice fiber gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathbf z}
&=\int_{\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1}^{\infty}e^{-t}\,dt
=e^{-\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1}=2^{-\|\mathbf z\|_1},\\
\int_{\mathbb R}t g_n(\mathbf z,t)\,dt
&=(1+\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1)e^{-\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(m_{\mathbf z}=1+\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1\). For \(s>0\), define \(Z(s)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}2^{-s|k|}\). Summing the geometric series, with \(q=2^{-s}\), yields \(Z(s)=(1+q)/(1-q)\). The product form of \(a_{\mathbf z}\) therefore gives \(W=Z(1)^n=3^n\). All fiber masses are positive and at most one. The same geometric series show \(\sum_{\mathbf z}(1+\|\mathbf z\|_1)a_{\mathbf z}<\infty\). In particular, for every reflection center and every finite \(p\ge4\), the weight denominator is positive and finite, and the barycentric sums converge absolutely.

Since \(a_{\mathbf z}=a_{-\mathbf z}\), one has \(S_{\mathbf0}=W\). For every \(\mathbf j\), each summand defining \(S_{\mathbf j}\) is at most \(a_{\mathbf z}\), so \(S_{\mathbf j}\le W\). If \(\mathbf j\ne\mathbf0\), the summand at \(\mathbf z=\mathbf0\) is \(a_{2\mathbf j}=2^{-2\|\mathbf j\|_1}<1=a_{\mathbf0}\). All the other differences are nonnegative, whence \(W-S_{\mathbf j}\ge1-a_{2\mathbf j}>0\). Thus \(\mathbf0\) is the unique maximal reflection center.

At this center, \(\lambda_{\mathbf0,\mathbf z}^{(p)}=a_{\mathbf z}^p/Z(p)^n\). These are product probability weights, with one-coordinate law \(\mathbb P_p(K=k)=2^{-p|k|}/Z(p)\). Symmetry and absolute convergence give zero for the mean of each lattice coordinate. Using \(\sum_{k\ge1}kq^k=q/(1-q)^2\), for \(q=2^{-p}\) one obtains
\[
\mathbb E_p|K|
=\frac{2\sum_{k\ge1}kq^k}{Z(p)}
=\frac{2q}{1-q^2}
=\mu_p.
\]
It follows that the continuous coordinate of the selected barycenter is \(\sum_{\mathbf z}\lambda_{\mathbf0,\mathbf z}^{(p)}m_{\mathbf z}=1+\kappa n\mu_p=t_p\). At \(p=\infty\), the reflected mass is \(a_{\mathbf z}\), uniquely maximized by \(\mathbf z=\mathbf0\), so the selected point is \((\mathbf0,m_{\mathbf0})=(\mathbf0,1)\). This also agrees with the limit of the finite-power barycenters. All selected heights are at least one, so the selected points belong to the fiber \(\{\mathbf0\}\times[0,\infty)\) of the density support.

For finite \(p\ge4\), one has \(0<2^{-p}\le1/16\). Therefore \(0<\mu_p\le(1/8)/(255/256)=32/255=\mu_4\); the upper bound also holds for \(\mu_\infty=0\). Hence \(t_p\le t_4\) for all the exponents under consideration. This proves the asserted common-halfspace containment, without assuming that the exponent stays fixed as \(n\) varies.

On a fiber with \(\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1>t_4\), the cap \(H_n\) has zero density mass. On any other fiber its density mass is at most the full fiber mass \(a_{\mathbf z}\). Applying the elementary bound \(\mathbf1_{\{\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1\le t_4\}}\le e^{3t_4-3\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{g_n}(H_n)
&\le\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
a_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_{\{\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1\le t_4\}}\\
&\le e^{3t_4}\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
e^{-4\kappa\|\mathbf z\|_1}
=e^{3t_4}Z(4)^n.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the Chernoff estimate with tilt \(3\log2\) on the lattice norm. Here \(Z(4)=17/15\), \(Z(1)=3\), and \(3\mu_4=32/85\). Dividing by the unnormalized target scale therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mu_{g_n}(H_n)}{2^{-n}W}
&\le e^3\left(\frac{2Z(4)}{Z(1)}e^{3\kappa\mu_4}\right)^n\\
&=e^3\left(\frac{34}{45}e^{(32/85)\log2}\right)^n
=e^3e^{-\delta n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(H_n\) is a closed halfspace containing every \(\mathbf y_{n,p}\), the defining infimum of halfspace depth gives the remaining inequality.

For completeness, the strict positive lower bound on \(\delta\) has an elementary exact certificate. For \(x>0\), the derivative of \(\log(1+x)-x+x^2/2\) is \(x^2/(1+x)>0\), and the expression vanishes at zero. Thus \(\log(1+x)>x-x^2/2\). Also the positive-term exponential series gives
\[
e^{7/10}
>1+\frac7{10}+\frac{(7/10)^2}{2}+\frac{(7/10)^3}{6}
=\frac{12013}{6000}>2,
\]
so \(\log2<7/10\). Substituting \(x=11/34\) yields
\[
\delta
>\frac{11}{34}-\frac12\left(\frac{11}{34}\right)^2
-\frac{32}{85}\frac7{10}
=\frac{443}{57800}>0.
\]
Thus \(e^3e^{-\delta n}\) tends to zero. Given any proposed constant \(c>0\), choose an integer \(n\) large enough that this expression is less than \(c\). In that dimension every exponent \(p\in[4,\infty]\) selects a point with depth less than \(c2^{-n}W\). The bound is uniform over all exponent sequences \(p_n\in[4,\infty]\).

The example has an unbounded epigraph support and an exponential density. It is not a compact convex body equipped with the unweighted mixed-integer volume in the locked problem. Moreover, the argument concerns the specified power-weight and maximal-mass choices only; it makes no claim about other mass-based rules or other possible points of large depth.
