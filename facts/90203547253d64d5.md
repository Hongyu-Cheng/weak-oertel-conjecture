## Statement

Use the packet notation and the exact capacity normalizations of accepted fact `b5919922da1d7a2e`. Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, let \(W=\sum_z a_z\), and let \(\Pi\) be an admissible full-rank multifactor packet with \(k\geq2\), positive factor ranks \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\) satisfying \(\sum_i r_i=n\), and energy \(E=\mathcal E(\Pi)\). Put
\[
 P:=\prod_{i=1}^k(r_i+1),
 \qquad
 \chi(\Pi):=P-1-n.
\]
Then
\[
 \chi(\Pi)+1\geq2^{k-1},
 \qquad
 P\leq2^n.
\]

Assume \(W>0\) and the strict no-rank-drop hypothesis
\[
 W_L<2^{\dim L-n}W
 \quad\text{for every proper primitive affine lattice subspace }
 L\subsetneq\mathbb R^n,
\]
with \(W_L\) as in `b5919922da1d7a2e`. Then every such packet satisfies
\[
 \max\{G(C),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\}
 \geq \frac{\mathcal E(\Pi)}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}.
\]
For every integer \(\Chi\geq0\), define
\[
 \mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C)
 :=\sup\left\{\mathcal E(\Pi):
 \Pi\text{ is full-rank},\ k(\Pi)\geq2,
 \chi(\Pi)\leq\Chi\right\},
\]
where the supremum of the empty family is zero. Under the same no-rank-drop hypothesis,
\[
 \max\{G(C),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\}
 \geq
 \frac{\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C)}{4(\Chi+1)}.
\]

Under the same no-rank-drop hypothesis, let \(\varepsilon,\lambda>0\). If
\[
 G(C)<\varepsilon W,
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)<\varepsilon W,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(\Pi)\geq\lambda W,
\]
then the following inequalities are strict:
\[
 \chi(\Pi)+1>\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 \prod_i(r_i+1)>n+\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 n>\log_2\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon}\right).
\]

In particular, consider a varying-dimension sequence \(C_m\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_m}\times\mathbb R^{d_m}\) with \(W_m>0\), exact strict no-rank-drop for every \(m\), numbers \(0<\varepsilon_m\leq1/64\) with \(\varepsilon_m\to0\), and the exact full hard-capacity hypotheses
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(D_{+,m})=\mathbb R^{n_m},
 \qquad
 G(C_m),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_m),M_{\mathrm{off}}(C_m),R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_m)
 <\varepsilon_mW_m.
\]
If, for one fixed \(\lambda>0\), each \(C_m\) has a full-rank packet \(\Pi_m\) with \(k_m\geq2\) and \(\mathcal E(\Pi_m)\geq\lambda W_m\), then
\[
 \chi(\Pi_m)+1>\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_m},
 \qquad
 n_m>\log_2\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_m}\right),
\]
so \(\chi(\Pi_m)\to\infty\) and \(n_m\to\infty\). Also, for every fixed \(\Chi\geq0\),
\[
 \frac{\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C_m)}{W_m}\longrightarrow0.
\]
These are conditional sequence conclusions: they do not apply to arbitrary counterexamples unless exact no-rank-drop and a macroscopic full-rank packet in the stated hard branch have first been obtained.

For clarity, \(P=\prod_i(r_i+1)\) counts product vertices. If \(D_i=\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i}\) and \(S_i=\{u_i\in D_i:g_i(u_i)^d>0\}\), then \(\prod_i|S_i|\) counts the positive-support product lattice points, while \(\prod_i|D_i|\) counts the full product lattice grid. The first two sets are generally incomparable as subsets of the full grid because positive support can omit product vertices and can include nonvertex lattice points. Under the full hard branch in the preceding sequence, the already accepted positive-grid estimate in `b5919922da1d7a2e`, combined with \(P\leq2^n\), implies
\[
 \frac{\prod_i|S_{m,i}|}{\prod_i(r_{m,i}+1)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
This last sentence is only a comparison with the accepted positive-grid theorem, not a new registration of that theorem.

## Proof

Expand the product using the positivity and integrality of all factor ranks:
\[
 P
 =1+\sum_i r_i
  +\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\\|I|\geq2}}
    \prod_{i\in I}r_i.
\]
Since \(n=\sum_i r_i\) and every \(r_i\geq1\),
\[
 \chi(\Pi)+1=P-n
 \geq1+\sum_{j=2}^k\binom{k}{j}
 =2^k-k.
\]
For \(k\geq2\), one has \(2^{k-1}\geq k\), so \(2^k-k\geq2^{k-1}\). This proves the first combinatorial inequality, including \(k=2\), where the last comparison is equality. For every positive integer \(r\), induction gives \(r+1\leq2^r\). Therefore
\[
 P=\prod_i(r_i+1)
 \leq\prod_i2^{r_i}
 =2^{\sum_i r_i}=2^n.
\]
In particular, \(\chi(\Pi)+1\geq2\), so all later denominators are positive.

Write
\[
 M:=\max\{G(C),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\}.
\]
If \(E=0\), then \(M\geq E/[4(\chi(\Pi)+1)]\) follows from nonnegativity. Assume \(E>0\). Accepted fact `b5919922da1d7a2e` proves \(E\leq W\). If \(G(C)\geq W/4\), then
\[
 M\geq G(C)\geq E/4\geq\frac{E}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}.
\]
Now suppose \(G(C)<W/4\). If \(E\leq G(C)\), the same target follows immediately from \(\chi(\Pi)+1\geq1\). It remains to treat \(E>G(C)\). Put \(x=G(C)/E\). The residual-selector estimate of `b5919922da1d7a2e`, whose hypotheses are now exactly satisfied, gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{E}{\chi(\Pi)+1}
 \left(1-x^{1/(k-1)}\right)^2.
\]
If \(x\geq2^{-(k-1)}\), then
\[
 M\geq G(C)=xE
 \geq2^{-(k-1)}E
 \geq\frac{E}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)},
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(\chi(\Pi)+1\geq2^{k-1}\). If \(x<2^{-(k-1)}\), then \(x^{1/(k-1)}<1/2\), so the square in the residual-selector estimate is strictly larger than \(1/4\). Hence
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)>
 \frac{E}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}.
\]
These exhaustive cases prove the packetwise bound with its stated constant.

Fix \(\Chi\geq0\). Every packet in the family defining \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C)\) has \(\chi(\Pi)\leq\Chi\), and therefore the packetwise bound gives
\[
 M\geq\frac{\mathcal E(\Pi)}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \geq\frac{\mathcal E(\Pi)}{4(\Chi+1)}.
\]
Taking the supremum over this family does not require an attaining packet and proves the claimed bound for \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C)\). If the family is empty, its stipulated supremum is zero and the conclusion is simply \(M\geq0\). In fact, the first combinatorial inequality shows that the family is necessarily empty when \(\Chi=0\).

Under the two strict capacity inequalities, \(M<\varepsilon W\). Combining the packetwise bound with \(E\geq\lambda W\) and \(W>0\) yields
\[
 \frac{\lambda W}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \leq\frac{E}{4(\chi(\Pi)+1)}
 \leq M
 <\varepsilon W.
\]
Division by \(W\) and rearrangement preserve the strict final inequality and give
\[
 \chi(\Pi)+1>\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon}.
\]
Since \(P=n+\chi(\Pi)+1\), this implies
\[
 P>n+\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon}.
\]
Together with \(P\leq2^n\), it gives \(2^n>\lambda/(4\varepsilon)\). Strict monotonicity of \(\log_2\) proves
\[
 n>\log_2\!\left(\frac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon}\right).
\]

Applying these pointwise inequalities with \(C=C_m\), \(\Pi=\Pi_m\), and \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_m\) proves the two explicit sequence bounds and their divergence consequences. Put \(M_m:=\max\{G(C_m),R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_m)\}\). The supremum estimate also gives, for each fixed \(\Chi\),
\[
 0\leq
 \frac{\mathcal F_{\mathrm{multi}}^\Chi(C_m)}{W_m}
 \leq4(\Chi+1)\frac{M_m}{W_m}
 <4(\Chi+1)\varepsilon_m,
\]
which tends to zero.

Finally, the three grid counts are literal Cartesian-product counts because the packet chart is injective. Each \(D_i\) contains its \(r_i+1\) simplex vertices, and \(S_i\subseteq D_i\), but neither the product-vertex set nor the positive-support set need contain the other. Write \(P_m:=\prod_i(r_{m,i}+1)\) and \(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d\). For the stated full hard-branch sequence, the already accepted positive-grid conclusion (24) of `b5919922da1d7a2e`, together with \(P_m\leq2^{n_m}\), gives the comparison
\[
 \frac{\prod_i|S_{m,i}|}{P_m}
 >\frac{\gamma_{d_m}\lambda}{\varepsilon_m}
 >\frac{\lambda}{e\varepsilon_m}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Here the second strict inequality follows from \(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\), which gives \(\gamma_d=(1+1/d)^{-d}>e^{-1}\). Thus no positive-grid theorem is reproved or re-registered. The full grid has count \(\prod_i|D_{m,i}|\geq\prod_i|S_{m,i}|\), but it remains a distinct object.
