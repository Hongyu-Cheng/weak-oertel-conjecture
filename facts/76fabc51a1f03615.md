## Statement

In the same actual three-triangle family, at its deepest mark $Y=(0,y)$, no nonnegative finite field that is convex in $(z,x)$ for each fixed outer normal $n$ can vanish almost everywhere on both endpoint fiber normal bundles and equal the entire own-source cone field $[h_T(n)-n\cdot y]P_y(x)^{-2}$ almost everywhere on the middle normal bundle, where $P_y(x)>0$ is the optimal projective denominator of $T$ at $y$.

## Proof

Each endpoint triangle shares positive-length portions of the bottom facet $x_2=-1$ and the diagonal facet $x_1+x_2=1$ with $T$, because the endpoint triangle is homothetic about their common vertex $A$. On either shared portion, all three positions $(-1,x),(0,x),(1,x)$ have the same fixed regular normal $n$, and the middle point is the average of the endpoints. For each $x$ outside the endpoint exceptional null sets, convexity and endpoint vanishing force the value at $(0,x)$ to be zero. This is a positive-length set. The interior mark gives $h_T(n)-n\cdot y>0$, and the compact energy denominator is positive, so the asserted middle field is strictly positive throughout the shared facet portion. This contradicts the required almost-everywhere equality.
