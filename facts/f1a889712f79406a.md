## Statement

For each integer $s\ge1$ set $r=4s+2$, $R=7s+4$ and
$$
C_s=\{(p,q,x):-1\le q\le1,\ |p|\le r-(r-s)q,\ |x|\le2+q\},
$$
with integer coordinates $p,q$. It has $24s+15$ positive integer fibers and total mixed length $M_s=72s+44$. The exact raw closed-halfspace maximum is $T_s=24s+14$ and its maximizing marks are precisely $(j,0,0)$ with $j=-s,\ldots,s$.

For every $d\ge2$ and every centrally symmetric convex body $L\subset\mathbb R^{d-1}$ centered at zero, $C_s\times L$ has mixed volume $M_s|L|$ and raw maximum $T_s|L|$. Its maximizing integer bases are precisely $(j,0)$ with $-s\le j\le s$, with a maximizing mark at continuous coordinate $(0,0)$ over each such base. In each dimension the normalized maximum is $T_s/M_s=1/3-2/(3M_s)>1/8$. Thus a count of maximizing integer bases depending only on $n,d$ is impossible for arbitrary faceted mixed bodies.

## Proof

The integer rows $q=-1,0,1$ have respective $p$-radii $R,r,s$ and interval half-widths $1,2,3$. Their fiber counts are $14s+9,8s+5,2s+1$, so their total count and mass are as stated. The caps $H_\pm=\{\pm x+q\ge0\}$ each capture $0,2,4$ per fiber in the three rows, with total mass $2(2r+1)+4(2s+1)=T_s$. They cover the middle and top rows. The caps $\{\pm x-3(q+1)\ge0\}$ each capture only the bottom row, with mass $2R+1<T_s$, and cover it. This gives the upper bound everywhere, also after multiplying all masses by $|L|$ in the product.

Write $[u]_0^m=\max(0,\min(m,u))$. Let $F_s(a,b,c)$ denote the mixed length in the cap with margin $x+a(p-s)+bq+c$. Reflection in $p$ gives $F_s(-a,b,c)\ge F_s(a,b,c)$ for $a\ge0$, because it changes the negative slope to the positive one and adds $2as$ to the margin. It suffices to prove
$$
F_s(a,b,c)+F_s(a,b,-c)\ge2T_s
$$
for every $a\ge0$ and all real $b,c$.

For the bottom row let $B_a$ be the distribution of $x+ak$ on its disjoint unit-density intervals $x\in[-1,1]$ with $k=p-s=-8s-4,\ldots,6s+4$. Let $U_a$ be the distribution of $x+aj$ on $x\in[-3,3]$ for $j=0,\ldots,2s$, also unit density. Reflection of $x$ and $j=s-p$ on the top row give the combined outer-row capture
$$
B_a([b-c,\infty))+6(2s+1)-U_a([b+c,\infty)),
$$
where the scalar laws have no atoms. Put $N=4s+2$. For each target interval labeled $j$ in $U_a$, send its three pieces $[-3,-1],[-1,1],[1,3]$ to bottom labels $2j,2j+1,j+N$ by shifting $x$ by $2,0,-2$, respectively. The images fill the labeled receiving intervals. These labels are all distinct and in $0,\ldots,6s+2$ within the actual bottom range. The changes in projected value are $2+ja,(j+1)a,Na-2$. The first two are nonnegative; each third piece can lose at most $(2-Na)_+$ of captured length at any fixed tail threshold. Therefore
$$
B_a([t,\infty))\ge U_a([t,\infty))-(2s+1)(2-Na)_+
$$
for every $t\in\mathbb R$. Apply this at $t=b-c,b+c$ to show that the paired outer-row capture is at least $2[6(2s+1)-(2s+1)(2-Na)_+]$.

The middle-row shifts $k=p-s$ run from $-(5s+2)$ to $3s+2$. In the symmetric block $|k|\le3s+2$, pairing the terms with opposite $k,c$ shows that the combined middle capture at $c,-c$ there is exactly $2(12s+10)$, since $[2+h]_0^4+[2-h]_0^4=4$. For each remaining $k=-K$, put $v=2-Ka\le2$. Then $[v+c]_0^4+[v-c]_0^4\ge2(v)_+$. For $v\le0$ this is immediate; for $v>0$ the sum equals $2v$ if $|c|\le v$, and the larger term alone is at least $2v$ if $|c|>v$. Thus the paired middle capture is at least twice
$$
12s+10+\sum_{K=3s+3}^{5s+2}(2-Ka)_+.
$$
This expression is at least $12s+10$ and at least $16s+10-s(8s+5)a$ by summing the linear lower bounds. For $0\le a\le2/N$, the latter bound added to the outer-row bound per half of the pair gives $T_s+[(2s+1)N-s(8s+5)]a=T_s+(3s+2)a$. For $a\ge2/N$ the former bound gives $12s+10+6(2s+1)=T_s+2$. This proves the paired inequality.

Any closed halfspace containing the outer mark $(s,0,0)$, or its lift with additional coordinate $0$, can be moved parallel through that mark without increasing its mass. A nonzero $x$ coefficient can be made $1$ by reflection and positive scaling. In dimension one the bound with $c=0$ gives mass at least $T_s$. In the product, the margin has $c=v\cdot w$ for a continuous covector $v$ and $w\in L$. Integrating the paired inequality using $L=-L$ and Fubini gives mass at least $T_s|L|$. If the $x$ coefficient is zero, add $\eta x$ to the margin and let $\eta\to0$ through nonzero values. The pointwise upper limit of the perturbed indicators is at most the indicator of the closed original cap, and the mixed measure is finite, so reverse Fatou gives the same bound. The upper cover and reflection in $p$ show that both outer marks are maximizing. Every halfspace containing a mark on the segment between them contains an endpoint, so all the stated intermediate sampled marks maximize as well.

For the census, the bottom row is strictly below $T_s$ by its cover. A point in the top row, or a middle-row point with $x\ne0$, lies strictly inside at least one $H_\pm$. Move that cap's threshold upward slightly while retaining the point. Its proper middle-row traces lose positive length, so the new mass is strictly below $T_s$. This also works for the product by multiplying the lost length by $|L|$. At $(j,0,0)$ with $j\ge s+1$, test the cap $x+t(p-j)\ge0$ with sufficiently large finite $t>0$. The top is entirely excluded, the bottom includes $p>j$ and half the fiber at $p=j$, and the middle includes $p>j$ and half the fiber at $p=j$. At $j=s+1$ this costs $2(6s+3)+1+4(3s+1)+2=24s+13<T_s$. The cost is nonincreasing for greater $j$, and reflection handles $j\le-s-1$. This cap has the same bound times $|L|$ at every lifted mark over the corresponding excluded base with $x=0$. This proves the exact maximizing-base census in all dimensions and the exact mark census in dimension one.
