## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), put \(m:=n+d\), and identify
\(\mathbb R^m=\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\). Write
\(\operatorname{vol}_j\) for Euclidean \(j\)-volume, with
\(\operatorname{vol}_0(\{0\})=1\), and let
\(\mathbb B_\infty^n=[-1,1]^n\).

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) be a convex body, let \(A\subseteq D\)
be compact and convex, and suppose that
\[
\mathbf a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n
\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D)
\]
for some \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\rho>0\). Fix \(W>0\).
For \(E\in\{A,D\}\), \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n\), and \(s>0\), set
\[
E_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in E\},
\qquad
g_E(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(E_{\mathbf z})^{1/d},
\qquad
L_s^E:=\{\mathbf z:g_E(\mathbf z)\geq s\},
\]
where the empty fiber has volume zero. Put
\[
\mathcal S_A:=\{s>0:L_s^A\neq\varnothing\}.
\]

Suppose that \(I_0,\ldots,I_n\) is a measurable partition of
\(\mathcal S_A\) such that, for every \(q\geq1\) and every \(s\in I_q\),
there is a \(q\)-dimensional linear subspace \(E_s\leq\mathbb R^n\)
satisfying
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_{E_s}L_s^A)\leq W^q. \tag{1}
\]
No measurability of the choice \(s\mapsto E_s\) is required. Define
\[
M_q:=d\int_{I_q}s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds
\quad(0\leq q\leq n), \tag{2}
\]
\[
F_0:=1,
\qquad
F_q:=
\left(\frac{2\rho}{W}\right)^q
\left(1-\frac qm\right)^{m-q}
\left(\frac qm\right)^q
\quad(1\leq q\leq n). \tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\geq
\sum_{q=0}^n2^{-q-1}F_qM_q. \tag{4}
\]
For \(1\leq q\leq n\),
\[
F_q\geq
\left(\frac{2\rho q}{emW}\right)^q. \tag{5}
\]
Consequently, for every integer
\[
Q\geq
\max\left\{2,\left\lceil\frac{4emW}{\rho}\right\rceil\right\},
\]
one has, with an empty sum understood as zero,
\[
\sum_{q=Q}^nM_q
\leq\frac83\,4^{-Q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\leq\frac16\operatorname{vol}_m(D). \tag{6}
\]

For a nonempty compact \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
\operatorname{width}_{\mathbf u}(C):=
\max_{\mathbf x\in C}\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf x\rangle
-
\min_{\mathbf x\in C}\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf x\rangle
\]
and
\[
V_W(C):=
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}
\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^n:
\operatorname{width}_{\mathbf u}(C)\leq W\}.
\]
For \(s\in\mathcal S_A\), put
\[
r_W(s):=\dim V_W(L_s^A),
\qquad
I_q^W:=\{s\in\mathcal S_A:r_W(s)=q\}. \tag{7}
\]
The sets \(I_0^W,\ldots,I_n^W\) form a measurable partition satisfying
(1), with \(E_s=V_W(L_s^A)\). Hence (2)-(6) apply to this partition.
Moreover, for every nonempty \(I_q^W\), there is one fixed rational
\(q\)-dimensional subspace \(V_q\leq\mathbb R^n\) such that
\[
V_W(L_s^A)=V_q\qquad(s\in I_q^W). \tag{8}
\]
For \(q=0\), this means \(V_q=\{0\}\).

Finally, suppose that
\[
A=D\cap H,
\]
where \(H\) is a closed halfspace whose boundary contains the centroid
of \(D\). For every integer \(Q\) satisfying the condition above, there
are \(q<Q\) and a fixed rational \(q\)-subspace \(V_q\), possibly
\(q=0\), such that (8) holds on \(I_q^W\) and
\[
d\int_{I_q^W}
s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds
\geq
\frac{1/e-1/6}{Q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D). \tag{9}
\]

## Proof

Accepted fact `e63e7ce292c9ca94` gives compact convex positive levels,
their measurability, the layer-cake identity, and, for every
\(s\geq0\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
(1-\lambda)L_s^A+
\lambda(\mathbf a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n)
\subseteq L_{(1-\lambda)s}^D. \tag{10}
\]
It also covers empty and lower-dimensional levels.

We first prove the pointwise estimate behind (4). Fix \(q\geq1\) and
\(s\in I_q\). If \(\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)=0\), the required
pointwise lower bound has zero right side and is automatic. Suppose
instead that \(\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)>0\), and abbreviate
\(L:=L_s^A\) and \(E:=E_s\). Orthogonal Fubini decomposition gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(L)
=
\int_{P_EL}
\operatorname{vol}_{n-q}\bigl(L\cap(\mathbf y+E^\perp)\bigr)
\,d\mathbf y. \tag{11}
\]
Thus some \(\mathbf y\in P_EL\) has a section
\[
S:=L\cap(\mathbf y+E^\perp)
\]
such that, by (1),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{n-q}(S)
\geq
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(L)}
{\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL)}
\geq
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_n(L)}{W^q}. \tag{12}
\]
This includes \(q=n\): the selected nonempty zero-dimensional section
has volume one.

Let
\[
C_E:=\mathbf a+\rho(\mathbb B_\infty^n\cap E).
\]
Vaaler's cube-section theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(C_E)\geq(2\rho)^q. \tag{13}
\]
Since \(S\subseteq L\) and
\(C_E\subseteq\mathbf a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n\), (10) contains
\((1-\lambda)S+\lambda C_E\) in \(L_{(1-\lambda)s}^D\).
After translations, the two summands lie in the orthogonal complementary
spaces \(E^\perp\) and \(E\). The addition map
\[
E^\perp\times E\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n,
\qquad(\mathbf x,\mathbf z)\longmapsto\mathbf x+\mathbf z,
\]
is an isometry. Therefore product measure, including the
zero-dimensional endpoint factors, gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_n\bigl((1-\lambda)S+\lambda C_E\bigr)
&=(1-\lambda)^{n-q}\lambda^q
\operatorname{vol}_{n-q}(S)\operatorname{vol}_q(C_E).
\end{aligned} \tag{14}
\]
Taking \(\lambda=q/m\) in (12)-(14) yields
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n\!\left(L_{(1-q/m)s}^D\right)
\geq
\left(\frac{2\rho}{W}\right)^q
\left(1-\frac qm\right)^{n-q}
\left(\frac qm\right)^q
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A). \tag{15}
\]
This remains true for the zero-volume levels excluded while deriving
(12). For \(q=0\), (10) with \(\lambda=0\) directly gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^D)
\geq\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A). \tag{16}
\]

Put \(a_q:=1-q/m\), which is positive because \(q\leq n<m\), and
\(T_q:=a_qI_q\). The change of variables \(t=a_qs\), including its
Jacobian and the fiber layer weight, gives for \(q\geq1\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\int_{T_q}t^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_t^D)\,dt
&=
d\int_{I_q}(a_qs)^{d-1}
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_{a_qs}^D)a_q\,ds\\
&\geq
\left(\frac{2\rho}{W}\right)^q
a_q^{n-q+d}\left(\frac qm\right)^qM_q\\
&=F_qM_q,
\end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]
because \(n+d=m\). Equations (16) and \(T_0=I_0\) give the same
conclusion for \(q=0\), with \(F_0=1\).

To account for all possible overlaps among the sets \(T_q\), set
\[
h(t):=dt^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_t^D).
\]
This is nonnegative, and layer cake gives
\(\int_0^\infty h(t)\,dt=\operatorname{vol}_m(D)\). Hence Tonelli and
\(\sum_{q=0}^n2^{-q-1}\leq1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
&\geq
\sum_{q=0}^n2^{-q-1}\int_{T_q}h(t)\,dt\\
&\geq
\sum_{q=0}^n2^{-q-1}F_qM_q.
\end{aligned} \tag{18}
\]
This proves (4). Empty levels never enter the partition. Nonempty
zero-volume levels contribute zero to every \(M_q\), so no \(0^0\)
convention is used here.

For (5), let \(x=q/m\in(0,1)\). The elementary inequality
\(y\log y\geq y-1\) for \(y>0\), applied to \(y=1-x\), gives
\[
(m-q)\log(1-q/m)
=m(1-x)\log(1-x)\geq-mx=-q.
\]
Thus
\[
\left(1-\frac qm\right)^{m-q}\geq e^{-q},
\]
which substituted in (3) proves (5).

Write \(R:=mW/\rho\). If \(q\geq Q\), then \(q\geq4eR\), so (5) gives
\[
F_q\geq\left(\frac{2q}{eR}\right)^q\geq8^q. \tag{19}
\]
The individual \(q\)-summand in (4) now implies
\[
M_q\leq2\,4^{-q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D).
\]
Summing the geometric series gives
\[
\sum_{q=Q}^nM_q
\leq
2\sum_{q=Q}^{\infty}4^{-q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
=\frac83\,4^{-Q}\operatorname{vol}_m(D).
\]
Since \(Q\geq2\), the last coefficient is at most \(1/6\). This proves
(6), including the case \(Q>n\).

We next verify the narrow-rank specialization without using any
projection estimate from accepted fact `e1d7d6cf553bdb54`. Fix
\(s\in I_q^W\) with \(q\geq1\). By the definition of the span, choose
linearly independent
\(\mathbf u_1,\ldots,\mathbf u_q\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that
\[
\operatorname{width}_{\mathbf u_i}(L_s^A)\leq W
\quad(1\leq i\leq q)
\]
and these vectors span \(V_W(L_s^A)\). Let \(U\in\mathbb Z^{q\times n}\)
have rows \(\mathbf u_i^{\mathsf T}\), put
\(E:=\operatorname{row}(U)=V_W(L_s^A)\), and set
\[
\Delta:=\sqrt{\det(UU^{\mathsf T})}.
\]
Cauchy-Binet gives
\[
\Delta^2
=
\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|J|=q}}
(\det U_J)^2.
\]
At least one minor is a nonzero integer, so
\[
\Delta\geq1. \tag{20}
\]

The restriction \(T:=U|_E:E\to\mathbb R^q\) has \(q\)-Jacobian
\(\Delta\), and \(U(P_E\mathbf x)=U\mathbf x\). Consequently
\[
\Delta\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)
=\operatorname{vol}_q(UL_s^A).
\]
The \(i\)-th coordinate of \(UL_s^A\) ranges over an interval of length
at most \(W\), so
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(UL_s^A)\leq W^q.
\]
Together with (20), this proves the required certificate
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(P_EL_s^A)\leq W^q. \tag{21}
\]
The coarea normalization can also be checked directly from (11):
under \(\mathbf t=T\mathbf y\), one has
\(d\mathbf t=\Delta\,d\mathbf y\), while
\(U^{-1}(\mathbf t)=\mathbf y+E^\perp\). Therefore
\[
\Delta\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)
=
\int_{UL_s^A}
\operatorname{vol}_{n-q}
\bigl(L_s^A\cap U^{-1}(\mathbf t)\bigr)\,d\mathbf t. \tag{22}
\]
In particular, (22), the box bound, and \(\Delta\geq1\) recover exactly
the section lower bound \(\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)/W^q\) used in
(12), with no reversed Jacobian.

It remains to check measurability and the common space. Accepted fact
`e1d7d6cf553bdb54` is used only here: for nonempty nested compact levels
\(L_t^A\subseteq L_s^A\), it states that
\[
V_W(L_s^A)\subseteq V_W(L_t^A),
\]
that these spaces are rational, and that equal dimensions force equality.
Thus \(r_W(s)\) is integer-valued and nondecreasing with \(s\) on
\(\mathcal S_A\). Each rank stratum is therefore measurable. If
\(s,t\in I_q^W\), order them and apply the equal-rank clause to conclude
that their spaces coincide. This proves (8), including \(V_0=\{0\}\),
and (21) permits application of the general budget.

Finally, suppose \(A=D\cap H\) as in the statement. Grünbaum's
centroid-halfspace theorem in dimension \(m\) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(A)
\geq
\left(\frac{m}{m+1}\right)^m\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\geq
\frac1e\operatorname{vol}_m(D), \tag{23}
\]
where the last inequality follows from
\((1+1/m)^m\leq e\). Fubini and layer cake give
\[
\sum_{q=0}^nM_q
=d\int_0^\infty
s^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)\,ds
=\operatorname{vol}_m(A). \tag{24}
\]
Combining (6), (23), and (24) shows that
\[
\sum_{q<Q}M_q
\geq
\left(\frac1e-\frac16\right)\operatorname{vol}_m(D).
\]
There are at most \(Q\) integer ranks \(q<Q\). Pigeonhole therefore
gives (9), and (8) supplies the fixed rational space on the selected
stratum. The argument allows the selected rank to be zero.

## External sources

Jeffrey D. Vaaler, "A Geometric Inequality with Applications to Linear
Forms," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 83 (1979), 543-553, Theorem 1
and its corollary,
https://msp.org/pjm/1979/83-2/pjm-v83-n2-p24-p.pdf.
The corollary states that every \(q\)-dimensional central section of
\([-1/2,1/2]^n\) has \(q\)-volume at least one. Scaling first by \(2\)
and then by \(\rho\) gives exactly (13), for every
\(1\leq q\leq n\).

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies
by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257-1261,
Theorem 2 and its proof,
https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf.
The theorem places the centroid in every halfspace-depth region of
volume fraction \((m/(m+1))^m\), which applies to either closed
halfspace bounded by a hyperplane through the centroid and gives (23).
