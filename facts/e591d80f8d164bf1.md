## Statement

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_5\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb Z^5\), put \(v=(1,1,1,1,5)\), and let \(T\) be the integer linear map with columns \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,v\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
B&=\lbrace b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^5:\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^5b_i\le2\rbrace,\qquad
Q=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(TB\cup(-TB)\bigr),\\
a&=e_5,\qquad u=v-a=(1,1,1,1,4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(Q\) is a full-dimensional centrally symmetric rational polytope, and its complete lattice census is
$$
A:=Q\cap\mathbb Z^5=TB\cup(-TB)\cup\lbrace a,-a,u,-u\rbrace,
\qquad |A|=35.
$$
Every distinct pair \(x,y\in A\) whose midpoint is integral satisfies \(y=-x\), so that midpoint is zero. The point \(a\) is a nonvertex of \(Q\), but no set of two or three distinct points in \(A\setminus\lbrace a\rbrace\) has arithmetic mean \(a\). The four distinct other points \(u,-e_1-e_2,-e_3-e_4,0\) do have arithmetic mean \(a\). Consequently four is the least possible size of a distinct-point averaging set excluding \(a\). In particular, any row formed from the fixed singleton \(\lbrace a\rbrace\) and disjoint two-point or three-point sets of other supported points with mean \(a\) must be the singleton row.

Retain the entire original uniform affine law \(w_x=1=\exp(0+0\cdot x)\), \(Z=35\), and \(p_x=1/35\) for all \(x\in A\). This law is active in dimension five:
\(A_0:=4^5\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3=1024/1225<1\).
For its actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and second moment,
$$
\tau_p(j)=\inf\lbrace p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^5\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,
\qquad M_2(p)=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\tau_p(j)^2,
$$
one nevertheless has \(\tau_p(0)\ge18/35\) and \(M_2(p)\ge324/35^3>4^{-5}\).

## Proof

We first give an exact membership criterion for the polytope before applying \(T\). The convex hull of \(B\) is
\(Q_+=\lbrace b\in[0,1]^5:\sum_i b_i\le2\rbrace\).
Here is a direct verification of the nontrivial inclusion. If a point of the displayed set has at least two fractional coordinates, move those two coordinates in opposite directions, preserving their sum, until one reaches zero or one in each direction. The original point is a convex combination of the resulting two points, each with fewer fractional coordinates. Iterating reduces to at most one fractional coordinate. In that case there are at most one coordinates equal to one if a fractional coordinate is present, since the coordinate sum is at most two. Interpolating its remaining fractional coordinate between zero and one expresses the point as a convex combination of points of \(B\). Points with no fractional coordinates are already in \(B\).

For \(y\in\mathbb R^5\), let \(y_i^+=\max(y_i,0)\) and \(y_i^-=\max(-y_i,0)\), and define
$$
\Gamma(y)=
\max\left(\max_i y_i^+,\frac12\sum_i y_i^+\right)
+\max\left(\max_i y_i^-,\frac12\sum_i y_i^-\right).
$$
We claim that \(Q':=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup(-B))\) consists exactly of the points satisfying \(\Gamma(y)\le1\). Indeed, every point of \(Q'\) can be written as \(y=t b-(1-t)c\) with \(b,c\in Q_+\) and \(0\le t\le1\). Coordinatewise this implies
$$
y_i^+\le t,\quad \sum_i y_i^+\le2t,\qquad
y_i^-\le1-t,\quad \sum_i y_i^-\le2(1-t),
$$
which gives \(\Gamma(y)\le1\). Conversely, that inequality permits a \(t\in[0,1]\) lying between the first maximum in \(\Gamma\) and one minus its second maximum. The same four inequalities then hold. For \(0<t<1\), the vectors \(b=y^+/t\) and \(c=y^-/(1-t)\) lie in \(Q_+\) and give the required representation. If \(t=0\), then \(y^+=0\) and use \(b=0,c=y^-\); if \(t=1\), use \(b=y^+,c=0\). This proves the criterion, including its boundary cases. In particular \(|y_i|\le1\) for every \(y\in Q'\).

Since \(\det T=5\), the map is invertible, \(Q=TQ'\), and an ambient integer point \(x=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,z)\) belongs to \(Q\) precisely when
$$
y=T^{-1}x=(x_1-z/5,x_2-z/5,x_3-z/5,x_4-z/5,z/5)
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad\Gamma(y)\le1.
$$
The coordinate bound gives \(-5\le z\le5\). We now check every such integer slice.

If \(z=0\), all coordinates of \(y\) are integers in \([-1,1]\). If both signs occur, the two maxima in \(\Gamma\) are each at least one, which is impossible. Otherwise the nonzero coordinates all have one sign and magnitude one; the sum constraint permits at most two of them. Thus this slice consists exactly of the signed Boolean vectors of rank at most two in the first four coordinates. It has \(1+2(4+6)=21\) points, all belonging to \(TB\cup(-TB)\).

If \(z=5\), then \(y_5=1\). The positive maximum in \(\Gamma\) is at least one, so the negative maximum is zero and the positive coordinate sum is at most two. The other four coordinates are integers equal to zero or one, with at most one equal to one. There are exactly five possibilities, namely the images under \(T\) of the points of \(B\) with fifth coordinate one. The \(z=-5\) slice consists of their five negatives.

For \(0<z<5\), put \(s=z/5\), so \(s\in\lbrace1/5,2/5,3/5,4/5\rbrace\). The bound \(|x_i-s|\le1\) and integrality give \(x_i\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) for \(1\le i\le4\). Let \(j\) be the number of these four coordinates equal to one. The positive coordinates of \(y\) are \(s\) and \(j\) copies of \(1-s\), while its negative magnitudes are \(4-j\) copies of \(s\). Substitution in the criterion gives the following exact values or sufficient lower bounds:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
j&\Gamma(y)\\ \hline
0&3s\\
1&\Gamma(y)\ge(1-s)+3s/2=1+s/2\\
2&\Gamma(y)\ge(2-s)/2+s=1+s/2\\
3&\Gamma(y)\ge(3-2s)/2+s=3/2\\
4&\max(s,2-3s/2)
\end{array}
$$
For the last line, the negative part vanishes, and \((4-3s)/2\ge1-s\), so its expression is exact. Thus \(j=1,2,3\) are impossible. For \(j=0\), the condition is \(s\le1/3\), leaving only \(z=1\), which gives \(x=a\). For \(j=4\), the condition is \(s\ge2/3\), leaving only \(z=4\), which gives \(x=u\). These possibilities do satisfy the criterion. Central symmetry gives exactly \(-a,-u\) in the remaining negative slices and no others. This exhausts all slices.

There are \(|B|=1+5+10=16\) points in \(B\), and \(TB\cap(-TB)=\lbrace0\rbrace\), because \(T\) is invertible and \(B\cap(-B)=\lbrace0\rbrace\). Hence \(|TB\cup(-TB)|=31\). The four extra points are distinct and have fifth coordinates \(1,-1,4,-4\), absent from that union. The census therefore has exactly 35 points. The polytope is compact, rational, and centrally symmetric by its finite integer generating set. It is full-dimensional because it contains the five independent vectors \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,v\), together with zero.

Next check the midpoint assertion exactly. Since \(\det T\) is odd, \(T\) is invertible modulo two. The 16 Boolean points of \(B\) therefore yield distinct parities under \(T\); each nonzero such parity is occupied in \(TB\cup(-TB)\) by exactly the antipodal pair \(Tb,-Tb\), and its zero parity is occupied only by zero. Moreover, modulo two,
$$
a\equiv T(1,1,1,1,1),\qquad
u\equiv T(1,1,1,1,0).
$$
The two Boolean preimages have ranks five and four, respectively, so neither is in \(B\), and they are distinct. Thus the added pairs \(\lbrace a,-a\rbrace\) and \(\lbrace u,-u\rbrace\) occupy two new, distinct nonzero parities. Altogether every occupied nonzero parity contains exactly one antipodal pair, and the zero parity contains only zero. A pair of integer points has an integer midpoint if and only if its points have the same parity. Every distinct such pair in \(A\) is consequently antipodal, with midpoint zero. In particular no distinct pair can have midpoint \(a\ne0\).

To exclude triples of other points, note from the census that their fifth-coordinate values belong to \(\lbrace-5,-4,-1,0,4,5\rbrace\). The value \(-1\) occurs only at \(-a\), and the value \(4\) only at \(u\). The only unordered triples of values from this set, with repetitions allowed, that sum to three are
$$
\lbrace-5,4,4\rbrace,\qquad
\lbrace-1,-1,5\rbrace,\qquad
\lbrace-1,0,4\rbrace.
$$
For completeness, if the least value is \(-5\), the other two must sum to eight and hence are \(4,4\); if it is \(-4\), there is no pair summing to seven; if it is \(-1\), the possible pairs summing to four are \((-1,5)\) and \((0,4)\); and if it is nonnegative, no three allowed values sum to three. This enumerates coordinate multisets, including all possible choices from the five points on each of the \(z=5\) and \(z=-5\) slices.

The first listed multiset requires the unique point \(u\) twice, and the second requires the unique point \(-a\) twice. Neither can arise from distinct sources. In the third case, the triple must be \(\lbrace-a,u,x\rbrace\) with \(x\) in the zero slice. The equation \(-a+u+x=3a\) forces \(x=(-1,-1,-1,-1,0)\), excluded from that slice because it has four nonzero coordinates. This proves the required triple obstruction.

On the other hand, all four points \(u,-e_1-e_2,-e_3-e_4,0\) belong to \(A\), are distinct, and differ from \(a\). Their sum is \((0,0,0,0,4)=4a\). This proves the asserted mean, proves that \(a\) is not a vertex, and establishes the minimum averaging-set size four. The stated singleton-row conclusion follows immediately from the absence of the two permitted nontrivial set sizes.

Finally, the original uniform law has \(\sum_xp_x^3=35/35^3=1/35^2\), yielding \(A_0=1024/1225<1\). The 34 nonzero points form 17 antipodal pairs. Any closed halfspace containing zero contains zero and at least one point of each pair: an affine functional nonnegative at the midpoint cannot be negative at both endpoints. Its original probability is therefore at least \(18/35\). Consequently
$$
M_2(p)\ge p_0\tau_p(0)^2\ge\frac1{35}\left(\frac{18}{35}\right)^2
=\frac{324}{35^3}>\frac1{1024}=4^{-5},
$$
where the strict comparison follows from \(324\cdot1024=331776>42875=35^3\). The center-zero row consisting of zero and all 17 antipodal pairs remains available. Thus the singleton obstruction at \(a\) supplies no upper bound on a row-energy sum over all centers; the refutation concerns only the stated local completeness premise.
