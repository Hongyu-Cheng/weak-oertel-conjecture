## Statement

Use the independent reflected triangle-guard body of w31-axis-guard-active-menus with $L\ge1$, $0<\sigma\le1/4$, and $0<w\le1/100$. Given any balanced actual active menu, let $W$ be its weighted ramp profile. For every finite nonnegative continuous physical field $\Phi(n,z,x)$, convex in $(z,x)$ for each continuous unit boundary normal $n$, take $N=\int W\Phi\,dS$, $D=\int\Phi\,dS>0$, where $dS$ sums all actual fiber boundary lengths with their normals marked. Then
$$
\frac ND\le \frac13+\frac{1/9+w/3}{1+4L\sigma}.
$$
The supremum over such menus and fields is at least $1/3$. For $L=1$ and $w\le\sigma/6$ the upper bound is at most $4/9-\sigma/9$; at $\sigma=1/4$, $w=1/1000$ it is $7/18+1/6000$. For $\sigma=1/4$, fixed allowed $w$, the supremum tends to $1/3$ as $L\to\infty$, and already $L=3$ gives an upper bound $1303/3600<1/e$ in integer dimension nine.

## Proof

The exact active-menu description and guard profile bound are from w31-axis-guard-active-menus. Write $I$ for the total central boundary-field integral and $E$ for the total guard boundary-field integral, so $D=I+E$. On each central edge use its affine parameter $s\in[0,1]$, and let $f(s)$ be the restriction of $\Phi$ at that edge's unit normal. Because the zero barycentric coordinate makes its own ramp vanish, the central edge profile is
$$
W_h(s)=\frac13\left[\Psi((s-1/3)/w)+\Psi((2/3-s)/w)\right].
$$
It is symmetric and nondecreasing on $[0,1/2]$. Its mean is $4/9+w/3$: at width zero the outer thirds have value $1/3$ and the middle third value $2/3$, and each of the two width-$w$ triangles contributes integral $w/6$. The symmetrization $g(s)=(f(s)+f(1-s))/2$ preserves both integrals and is nonincreasing on $[0,1/2]$ by convexity. Integrating $(W_h(s)-W_h(t))(g(s)-g(t))\le0$ over that half interval twice gives nonpositive covariance. Restoring the whole interval and its constant length factor gives central capture at most $(4/9+w/3)I$.

On each central edge the guards of its two endpoint vertices have coincident edge portions of relative length $\sigma$ at its two ends, with the same unit normal. For each reflected pair the sum of its field values at corresponding points is at least twice the field at the central point, by convexity in the physical point with normal fixed. Symmetry and monotonicity of $g$ give $\int_{[0,\sigma]\cup[1-\sigma,1]}f\ge2\sigma\int_0^1 f$. Each endpoint patch is repeated on $L$ independent pairs, so summing gives $E\ge4L\sigma I$. The counted portions are disjoint on the measured boundary except length-null vertices: distinct axes are different fibers, and the two source edges incident to a vertex use different guard edges. Other guard facets add nonnegative cost. The guard capture is at most $E/3$, whence
$$
\frac{N}{D}\le \frac13+(1/9+w/3)\frac{I}{I+E}
\le \frac13+\frac{1/9+w/3}{1+4L\sigma},
$$
also when $I=0$. The estimate applies without any bound on the relative concentration of the test.

For the lower bound choose the canonical menu whose caps capture the positive matching guards. The normal-independent convex field $[2z_{i,r}-1]_+$ is one on the chosen positive guard and zero on every other measured fiber. Its efficiency is exactly $1/3$ because only that direction class pays there. For $L=1$, subtracting the upper bound from $4/9$ gives $(4\sigma/9-w/3)/(1+4\sigma)\ge \sigma/[3(1+4\sigma)]\ge\sigma/9$ when $w\le\sigma/6$. At $\sigma=1/4$ the upper bound is $7/18+w/6$ for $L=1$, and $13/36+w/12\le1303/3600<4/11$ for $L=3$. The exponential series gives $e\le 8/3+(1/24)/(1-1/5)<11/4$. Finally, at fixed $w$ and $\sigma=1/4$ the upper bound converges to the attained lower comparison $1/3$.
