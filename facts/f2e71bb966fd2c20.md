## Statement

For every integer \(N\ge1\), let \(D=\{-1,0,1\}^N=[-1,1]^N\cap\mathbb Z^N\), let \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\log 2)\mathbf1_N\), and give \(D\) the log-affine probability law
\[
p_{\mathbf z}
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}}
{\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}
=\prod_{i=1}^N w(z_i),
\qquad
w(-1)=\frac17,\quad w(0)=\frac27,\quad w(1)=\frac47.
\]
The assignment's \(\theta\) is exactly the canonical tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell\) used here.

For \(\mathbf z\in D\), let \(t_{\mathbf z}=\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) be the minimum probability of a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\), and let \(M_1(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}t_{\mathbf z}\). Choose any depth-minimizing closed halfspaces
\[
H_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a_{\mathbf z}\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\},
\qquad \mathbf a_{\mathbf z}\ne\mathbf0,
\qquad D\cap\partial H_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf z\}.
\]
Such choices exist by accepted fact \(c16327a288b708bd\). Define the genuine matrix using exactly that fact's normalization:
\[
E_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x\in D\cap H_{\mathbf z}:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\},
\qquad
K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf x\in E_{\mathbf z}\}},
\qquad
\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\sum_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x\in D}K_{\mathbf z,\mathbf x}^2.
\]
For every such minimizing-cap selection,
\[
\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\frac12\left[\left(\frac{29}{49}\right)^N+\left(\frac37\right)^N\right],
\qquad
0<M_1(p)\le
\sqrt{\frac{29}{2}}\,
\frac{(29/49)^N}{\sqrt{N+1}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2/M_1(p)\ge\sqrt{(N+1)/58}\). For any proposed finite constant \(C>0\), the exact choice \(N=\lfloor58C^2\rfloor+1\) gives \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2>C M_1(p)\). No local minimality of this law within the log-affine family is assumed or asserted.

## Proof

The normalization of the law is exact: \(e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}=2^{\sum_i z_i}\) and the partition function is \((2^{-1}+1+2)^N=(7/2)^N\). Thus the stated coordinate probabilities are positive and sum to one. The support is complete because its convex hull is \([-1,1]^N\), whose integer points are exactly \(D\).

First, an elementary binomial estimate will be used twice. If \(B\) has the binomial law with integer number of trials \(n\ge0\) and success probability \(a\in(0,1)\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\frac1{B+1}
&=\sum_{m=0}^n\frac{\binom nm a^m(1-a)^{n-m}}{m+1}\\
&=\frac1{(n+1)a}
\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{j}a^j(1-a)^{n+1-j}\\
&=\frac{1-(1-a)^{n+1}}{(n+1)a}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Here the second equality uses \(\binom nm/(m+1)=\binom{n+1}{m+1}/(n+1)\). Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\frac1{\sqrt{B+1}}
\le\sqrt{\mathbb E\frac1{B+1}}
\le\frac1{\sqrt{a(n+1)}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Next, let \(U_1,\ldots,U_k\) be independent uniform random variables on \(\{-1,0,1\}\), where \(k\ge0\), with the empty sum interpreted as zero. We claim that every atom of \(\sum_i U_i\) has probability at most \(\sqrt{3/(2(k+1))}\).

To prove the claim, the sum of \(m\) independent fair signs has maximum atom at a middle binomial coefficient. This follows from the consecutive-coefficient ratio \(\binom m{j+1}/\binom mj=(m-j)/(j+1)\). Put \(c_r=4^{-r}\binom{2r}{r}\) for integers \(r\ge0\). The bound \(c_r^2\le1/(2r+1)\) follows by induction from \(c_0=1\) and
\[
c_{r+1}^2
=c_r^2\left(\frac{2r+1}{2r+2}\right)^2
\le\frac{2r+1}{(2r+2)^2}
\le\frac1{2r+3},
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \((2r+1)(2r+3)\le(2r+2)^2\). For \(m=2r\), the maximum atom is \(c_r\le1/\sqrt{m+1}\). For \(m=2r+1\), it is \(c_r(2r+1)/(2r+2)\), whose square is at most \((2r+1)/(2r+2)^2\le1/(m+1)\). This also covers the empty fair-sign sum when \(m=0\).

The number of nonzero \(U_i\) has binomial law with parameters \(k\) and \(2/3\). Conditional on this number being \(m\), their sum has the law of \(m\) independent fair signs: for any fixed set of nonzero positions, their signs are independent and equiprobable. Hence, for each integer \(s\), applying (3) gives
\[
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{i=1}^k U_i=s\right)
\le\mathbb E\frac1{\sqrt{B+1}}
\le\sqrt{\frac3{2(k+1)}},
\qquad B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k,2/3).
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(Q_k\) be the probability that the sum of \(k\) independent variables with law \(w\) is at most zero; in particular \(Q_0=1\). Since \(w(u)=(2/7)2^u\) for \(u\in\{-1,0,1\}\), exponential change from the uniform ternary law gives the exact identity
\[
Q_k=\left(\frac67\right)^k
\sum_{s=-k}^0 2^s\,
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{i=1}^k U_i=s\right).
\]
Indeed, a particular ternary sequence of sum \(s\) has tilted probability \((2/7)^k2^s\) and uniform probability \(3^{-k}\). Using (4) and \(\sum_{s=-k}^0 2^s\le\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j}=2\), we obtain the universal, exact estimate
\[
Q_k\le\sqrt6\,\frac{(6/7)^k}{\sqrt{k+1}}
\qquad(k\ge0).
\tag{5}
\]

We now construct admissible closed halfspaces at every original root. Fix \(\mathbf z\in D\), let \(I_0=\{i:z_i=0\}\), let \(F=\{1,\ldots,N\}\setminus I_0\), and write \(k=|I_0|\). Define
\[
G_{\mathbf z}:=\left\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^N:
(k+1)\sum_{i\in F}(1-z_i u_i)+\sum_{i\in I_0}u_i\le0\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Its defining affine functional vanishes at \(\mathbf z\). Its linear part is nonzero: when \(F\ne\varnothing\), a coefficient is \(-(k+1)z_i\ne0\); when \(F=\varnothing\), all \(N\ge1\) coefficients are one. Thus \(G_{\mathbf z}\) is a genuine closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\).

For \(\mathbf x\in D\), each summand \(1-z_i x_i\), \(i\in F\), is in \(\{0,1,2\}\), and it is zero exactly when \(x_i=z_i\). If any fixed coordinate differs, the left side of (6) is at least \((k+1)-k=1\), since \(\sum_{i\in I_0}x_i\ge-k\). Such a point is excluded. If all fixed coordinates agree, the left side is exactly \(\sum_{i\in I_0}x_i\). Therefore the exact cap intersection is
\[
D\cap G_{\mathbf z}
=\left\{\mathbf x\in D:
x_i=z_i\ (i\in F),\quad \sum_{i\in I_0}x_i\le0\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
This covers \(k=0\), when the cap consists only of \(\mathbf z\), and \(k=N\), when it is the full ternary-sum cap. By independence and the defining minimum for \(t_{\mathbf z}\),
\[
t_{\mathbf z}\le p(D\cap G_{\mathbf z})
=\left(\prod_{i\in F}w(z_i)\right)Q_k.
\tag{8}
\]
These \(G_{\mathbf z}\) need not be minimizing or generic. They are used only for the depth upper bound (8); they do not replace the actual minimizing caps \(H_{\mathbf z}\) defining \(K\).

For a root with zero set \(I_0\), its own probability is \((2/7)^k\prod_{i\in F}w(z_i)\). Multiply (8) by that probability, and sum over all roots by first choosing their zero set and then their fixed signs. Each fixed coordinate contributes \(w(-1)^2+w(1)^2=17/49\), so every root is accounted for in the exact upper bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_1(p)
&\le\sum_{k=0}^N\binom Nk
\left(\frac27\right)^k\left(\frac{17}{49}\right)^{N-k}Q_k\\
&\le\sqrt6\sum_{k=0}^N\binom Nk
\frac{(12/49)^k(17/49)^{N-k}}{\sqrt{k+1}}\\
&=\sqrt6\left(\frac{29}{49}\right)^N
\mathbb E\frac1{\sqrt{B+1}},
\qquad B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,12/29)\\
&\le\sqrt6\left(\frac{29}{49}\right)^N
\sqrt{\frac{29}{12(N+1)}}
=\sqrt{\frac{29}{2}}\,
\frac{(29/49)^N}{\sqrt{N+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The second line uses (5), and the last uses (3). Positivity is also exact: every admissible halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\) contains its atom, so \(t_{\mathbf z}\ge p_{\mathbf z}>0\) and \(M_1(p)>0\).

Finally, accepted fact \(c16327a288b708bd\), part 4, applies to \(P=[-1,1]^N\), the complete integer support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\), the stated log-affine law, and the chosen generic depth-minimizing caps. In one coordinate, the even parity class has mass \(2/7\) and the odd parity class has mass \(5/7\). Product structure therefore gives
\[
\sum_{\boldsymbol\gamma\in\mathbb Z^N/2\mathbb Z^N}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap(\boldsymbol\gamma+2\mathbb Z^N)}p_{\mathbf x}\right)^2
=\left[\left(\frac27\right)^2+\left(\frac57\right)^2\right]^N
=\left(\frac{29}{49}\right)^N.
\]
Also \(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2=[(1/7)^2+(2/7)^2+(4/7)^2]^N=(3/7)^N\). Substitution into that fact's exact parity identity proves the first equality in (1), for the genuine matrix and independently of the minimizing-cap selection. Combining it with (9) yields
\[
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}{M_1(p)}
\ge\frac{\tfrac12(29/49)^N}
{\sqrt{29/2}\,(29/49)^N/\sqrt{N+1}}
=\sqrt{\frac{N+1}{58}}.
\]
For \(N=\lfloor58C^2\rfloor+1\), we have \(N+1>58C^2\), proving the strict violation claimed in the statement. This proves only the unrestricted first-moment comparison false; no conclusion about tilt minima, the cubic comparison, or the locked target is used or deduced.
