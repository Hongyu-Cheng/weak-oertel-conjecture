## Statement

Use exactly the canonical quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. Thus, for a positive law \(p\) on a finite support \(A\subset\mathbb Z^n\), extended by zero elsewhere, let
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=2^n,&
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,\\
\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},\\
V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,&
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},\\
\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j,&
A_0&=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3,\qquad A_{\mathrm{full}}=K^2b(p).
\end{aligned}
$$
All logarithms are natural. Here \(A_0\) is the displayed atom quantity, not the ratio \(\chi(p)/b(p)\).

For every integer \(n\ge7\), use the convex body and complete lattice support of accepted fact 94d565fe7e8ad9a1:
$$
Q_n=\lbrace x\in[-1,1]^n:x_i-x_j\le1
 \text{ for every }1\le i,j\le n\rbrace,\qquad
A=Q_n\cap\mathbb Z^n
 =\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\cup\bigl(-\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\bigr).
$$
There are explicit positive original weights \(w_x\), with their entire original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\) and law \(p_x=w_x/Z\), such that every parity class contains at most two supported atoms, the support is midpoint-closed whenever the midpoint is integral, and every distinct supported same-parity pair \(\lbrace u,v\rbrace\), with midpoint \(j\), satisfies
$$
\frac{\sqrt{p_up_v}}{p_j}=1.
$$
The quantities computed from all full canonical rows satisfy
$$
A_0<1,\qquad 1\le A_{\mathrm{full}}<6,\qquad
\nu\ge\frac{2}{135}\log^2(2^n-1).
$$
Consequently there is no finite absolute constant \(C\) for which
\(\nu\le C\log A_{\mathrm{full}}\) holds throughout the registered active full-equality class, even after imposing complete convex support.

The displayed weights are not globally affine Gibbs weights. Moreover, writing \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) for the standard basis and \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), they satisfy the strict inequality
$$
w_{\mathbf0}<
 \left(w_{-\mathbf1}\prod_{i=1}^n w_{e_i}\right)^{1/(n+1)},
\qquad
\mathbf0=\frac{e_1+\cdots+e_n-\mathbf1}{n+1}.
$$
Thus they cannot be restrictions of a positive log-concave function on \(Q_n\). Completeness of the support and equality in all integral midpoint constraints therefore do not supply either of these stronger weight premises.

## Proof

We reuse only the geometric objects \(Q_n,A_n\) from 94d565fe7e8ad9a1, with \(A=A_n\). The weights, normalizer, and probability law below replace that predecessor's uniform ones; in particular our \(Z=\sum_xw_x\) need not equal its support cardinality.

We first verify the geometric assertions directly. An integer point of \([-1,1]^n\) has all coordinates in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\). The inequalities \(x_i-x_j\le1\) prohibit the simultaneous occurrence of coordinates \(1\) and \(-1\). Therefore every integer point of \(Q_n\) is Boolean or the negative of a Boolean point. Conversely every such point satisfies the inequalities. This proves the displayed formula for \(A=Q_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The set \(Q_n\) is compact and convex, and every point of \([-1/4,1/4]^n\) satisfies all defining inequalities strictly. Thus \(Q_n\) has nonempty interior and is a full-dimensional convex body. This proves complete convex support, with no inference from midpoint closure.

Put
$$
m=2^n-1,\qquad t=m^{-1/3},\qquad
a_+=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2,\qquad
a_-=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t^2}}2.
$$
Since \(m\ge127\), these weights are positive and satisfy
$$
a_++a_-=1,\qquad a_+a_-=t^2,\qquad
a_+>t,\qquad a_+\ge\frac12,\qquad t^3=\frac1m.
\tag{1}
$$
The strict inequality \(a_+>t\) follows from \(a_+>a_->0\) and \(t=\sqrt{a_+a_-}\). Let
\(\mathcal C=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\setminus\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\), so
\(A=\lbrace\mathbf0\rbrace\cup\mathcal C\cup(-\mathcal C)\). Assign the original weights and normalizer by
$$
w_{\mathbf0}=t,\qquad w_c=a_+,\qquad w_{-c}=a_-
\quad(c\in\mathcal C),\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x=t+m(a_++a_-)=m+t,
\qquad p_x=\frac{w_x}{Z}.
\tag{2}
$$
Thus \(p\) is a positive probability law on the entire complete lattice support.

For every nonzero Boolean vector \(c\), its parity class contains exactly \(c\) and \(-c\). Distinct Boolean vectors have distinct parity classes. The zero parity class contains only \(\mathbf0\). Therefore the only distinct supported pairs with integer midpoint are
\(\lbrace c,-c\rbrace\), and all their midpoints are \(\mathbf0\in A\). This proves the parity bound and midpoint closure. By (1)--(2), every such pair has
$$
\sqrt{p_cp_{-c}}=\frac{\sqrt{a_+a_-}}Z=\frac tZ=p_{\mathbf0}.
\tag{3}
$$
In particular all pair ratios equal one, including every pair in the full row at the origin.

Every nontrivial supported reflected pair has midpoint zero. Hence the full canonical rows, with the conventions of 445a9e668ee1a705 unchanged, are exactly
$$
R_{\mathbf0}=A,\qquad |R_{\mathbf0}|=2m+1,
\qquad R_x=\lbrace x\rbrace\quad(x\ne\mathbf0).
\tag{4}
$$
It follows that
$$
H_p(\mathbf0)=\frac{(2m+1)t}{Z},\qquad
H_p(c)=\frac{a_+}Z,\qquad H_p(-c)=\frac{a_-}Z.
\tag{5}
$$
Both nonzero orientations in every origin pair occur in (5); the central atom occurs once. The identity
\(a_+^3+a_-^3=(a_++a_-)^3-3a_+a_-(a_++a_-)=1-3t^2\) gives the exact atom and reflection formulas
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=\frac{(m+1)^2}{(m+t)^3}
 \bigl[t^3+m(1-3t^2)\bigr],\\
A_{\mathrm{full}}&=\frac{(m+1)^2}{(m+t)^3}
 \bigl[(2m+1)^2t^3+m(1-3t^2)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

We verify the active condition with an explicit inequality. Set
$$
f=1-3m^{-2/3}+m^{-2}.
$$
For \(m\ge8\), one has \(0<f<1\). Rewriting the first line of (6), and using \((1+1/m)^2\le1+3/m\), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0
&=\frac{(1+1/m)^2}{(1+t/m)^3}f
<(1+3/m)f\\
&\le f+\frac3m
=1-3m^{-2/3}+m^{-2}+\frac3m<1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
For the final strict inequality, multiplication by \(m^2\) reduces it to
\(3m^{4/3}>3m+1\), which holds for \(m\ge8\), since then \(m^{1/3}\ge2\).

Subtracting the two exact formulas in (6) and using \(t^3=1/m\) yields
$$
A_{\mathrm{full}}=A_0+4\left(\frac{m+1}{m+t}\right)^3.
\tag{8}
$$
For \(m\ge15\), equations (7)--(8) imply
$$
A_{\mathrm{full}}<1+4\left(\frac{16}{15}\right)^3<6.
\tag{9}
$$
Its required lower bound also follows directly with the same original law. Define only for this calculation
\(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\), as in 445a9e668ee1a705. Expanding (5) gives
$$
\chi(p)=\frac{(2m+1)t^2+m(a_+^2+a_-^2)}{Z^2}
=\frac{m+t^2}{Z^2}.
\tag{10}
$$
The parity masses are \(t/Z\) in the zero class and \(1/Z\) in each of the other \(m\) classes. Thus the last expression in (10) is their sum of squares. Cauchy--Schwarz for the \(m+1=K\) parity masses gives \(K\chi(p)\ge1\). Cauchy--Schwarz under the original probability law \(p\) gives \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\), so
$$
A_{\mathrm{full}}=K^2b(p)\ge(K\chi(p))^2\ge1.
\tag{11}
$$

We now compute the full-row score price. By (3)--(5), \(\kappa_{\mathbf0}\) is the uniform law on all \(2m+1\) atoms of its full row. Put
$$
z=\frac12\log\frac{a_+}{a_-}=\log\frac{a_+}t.
\tag{12}
$$
At the origin the score is zero; at every \(c\) and \(-c\) it is respectively \(z\) and \(-z\). Therefore
$$
V_{\mathbf0}=\frac{2m}{2m+1}z^2,
\qquad V_x=0\quad(x\ne\mathbf0),
\tag{13}
$$
and the exact canonical center probability is
$$
q_{\mathbf0}
=\frac{(2m+1)^2t^3}
 {(2m+1)^2t^3+m(1-3t^2)}.
\tag{14}
$$
Its numerator is \((2m+1)^2/m\ge4m\), and the other positive term in its denominator is at most \(m\). Thus \(q_{\mathbf0}\ge4/5\). Equations (13)--(14) give
$$
\nu=q_{\mathbf0}\frac{2m}{2m+1}z^2\ge\frac8{15}z^2.
\tag{15}
$$
Since \(a_+\ge1/2\), equation (12) also gives
$$
z\ge\frac13\log m-\log2\ge\frac16\log m
\quad(m\ge64).
\tag{16}
$$
Combining (15)--(16) proves
$$
\nu\ge\frac{2}{135}\log^2m.
\tag{17}
$$
All constructions above are finite and satisfy every stated support and midpoint-equality hypothesis for each integer \(n\ge7\). By (9) and (11), their logarithmic full reflected quantity lies between zero and \(\log6\), whereas (17) tends to infinity as \(n\to\infty\). For any proposed finite positive absolute constant \(C\), choosing \(n\) sufficiently large therefore makes \(\nu>C\log A_{\mathrm{full}}\).

The exact formulas also give
$$
A_0\longrightarrow1\text{ from below},\qquad
A_{\mathrm{full}}\longrightarrow5,\qquad
q_{\mathbf0}\longrightarrow\frac45,\qquad
\frac{\nu}{\log^2m}\longrightarrow\frac4{45}.
$$

Finally we verify the stronger weight premises fail, so that the scope of the obstruction is explicit. If the original weights were globally affine Gibbs weights, there would be a scalar \(\gamma>0\) and a vector \(\theta\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(w_x=\gamma e^{\theta\cdot x}\) for every \(x\in A\). Such weights must satisfy
$$
w_{e_1}w_{e_2}=w_{\mathbf0}w_{e_1+e_2}.
$$
All four points belong to \(A\), but the weights in (2) would make this equality
\(a_+^2=t a_+\), contrary to \(a_+>t\). The normalized law cannot be globally affine Gibbs either, since multiplying all weights by a common positive factor preserves this necessary equality.

There is also a direct violation of full convex-combination log concavity at the origin. The \(n+1\) supported points \(e_1,\ldots,e_n,-\mathbf1\) have equal barycentric coefficients \(1/(n+1)\) and average zero. A positive log-concave function with the values (2) would therefore require
$$
t=w_{\mathbf0}\ge
 \left(a_+^n a_-\right)^{1/(n+1)}.
\tag{18}
$$
In fact (1) gives
$$
\left(
 \frac{(a_+^n a_-)^{1/(n+1)}}t
\right)^{n+1}
=\frac{a_+^{n-1}t^2}{t^{n+1}}
=\left(\frac{a_+}t\right)^{n-1}>1.
\tag{19}
$$
Thus (18) fails strictly. The same convex-combination inequality fails for the normalized probabilities, since both its sides acquire the same factor \(1/Z\). The support is complete and convex as proved above, but the weights satisfy only the claimed midpoint equalities and do not meet these stronger global weight premises.
