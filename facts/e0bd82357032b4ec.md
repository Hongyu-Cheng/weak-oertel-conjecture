## Statement

Let $L\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a compact convex body with $0\in\operatorname{int}L$. Let $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ be nonzero vectors with $\pm p_i\in\partial L$. Write $R(x_1,x_2)=(-x_2,x_1)$ and $H_i=\{x:(Rp_i)\cdot x\ge0\}$. Suppose $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, and $\sum_i\lambda_i p_i=0$. For every real $b\ge4/3$ and $q_i\in[1,b]$, put $\theta_i=\lambda_iq_i/\sum_j\lambda_jq_j$. Then
$$
\sum_i\theta_i\frac{|L\cap H_i|}{|L|}\ge\frac1{1+b}.
$$
In particular the lower bounds at $b=4/3$ and $b=3/2$ are $3/7$ and $2/5$.

## Proof

**An elementary reweighting estimate.** If numbers $h_i\in[0,1]$ have mean $P=\sum_i w_i h_i$ for probability weights $w_i$, then multiplication of the weights by factors $k_i\in[1,k]$, followed by normalization, gives mean at least
$$
\frac{P}{P+k(1-P)}.
$$
Indeed, the reweighted numerator $\sum_i w_i k_i h_i$ is at least $P$, and its complementary numerator is at most $k(1-P)$. It suffices to prove the geometric bound for $b=4/3$. For larger $b$, first multiply by $\min(q_i,4/3)$, giving $P\ge3/7$, then by $q_i/\min(q_i,4/3)\in[1,3b/4]$. The displayed bound is increasing in $P$ and equals $1/(1+b)$ at $P=3/7,\ k=3b/4$.

**Reduction to a strict triple.** Fix the vectors and multipliers at $b=4/3$. The feasible probability weights $\theta$ satisfy $\sum_i\theta_i p_i/q_i=0$ and form a compact polytope. A minimum of the linear cap mean occurs at an extreme point. The columns $(1,p_i/q_i)$ in its positive support are linearly independent, since a dependence gives a two-sided feasible perturbation. There are at most three, and a one-point support cannot balance. A two-point support consists of opposite rays. Their endpoints satisfy $p_2=-p_1$ by uniqueness of the boundary point on a ray from $0$. The cap fractions complement one another, and the two normalized weights are $q_1/(q_1+q_2)$ and $q_2/(q_1+q_2)$, both at least $1/(1+b)$. A collinear support cannot have three independent columns.

For a noncollinear triple recover the positive balanced weights $\lambda_i$ by normalizing $\theta_i/q_i$. Each $\lambda_i\le1/2$, since a larger value would express $p_i$ as a convex combination of the points $-p_j$, $j\ne i$, and $0$, with positive weight at the interior point $0$. If some $\lambda_i=1/2$, the exact half-weight case of b82858197fd14145 gives the unmodified mean $1/2$. The elementary reweighting estimate with $P=1/2$ and $k=b$ gives the claimed bound, even when the boundary has a segment through a marked point. We now have three $\lambda_i<1/2$.

**Truncation and triangle coordinates.** The strict triple has the geometry in the proof of b82858197fd14145, under “Reduction to a triangle” and “Corner areas and weights.” Here is the construction with the altered weights checked. Relabeling if necessary, an orientation-preserving linear map gives $p_1=(1,0), p_2=(0,1), p_3=(-a,-c)$, where $a,c>0$, $a+c>1$, $1+a>c$, and $1+c>a$. Area fractions and cap signs are invariant, because $(Rp_i)\cdot x=\det(p_i,x)$. The six marked points, in angular order, are $(1,0),(a,c),(0,1),(-1,0),(-a,-c),(0,-1)$. Consecutive turn determinants are $1+c-a,a+c-1,1+a-c$ and their repeats, so all six are vertices of their centrally symmetric hull $D$.

Away from the three boundary lines, the positive signs in the caps consist of one or two indices. They cannot all agree, by positive balance, and crossing a boundary ray changes one sign. The three two-cap sectors have weights $1-\theta_i$. They are bounded by the pairs of consecutive marks $((1,0),(a,c)), ((0,1),(-1,0)), ((-a,-c),(0,-1))$, as can be checked by the determinant signs at each pair's sum. Each has weight at least $1/(1+b)$ since
$$
\theta_i\le \frac{b\lambda_i}{b\lambda_i+1-\lambda_i}\le\frac{b}{1+b}.
$$
Cut $L$ by the line joining the two marks of each such sector, keeping the halfplane containing $0$, and denote the remainder by $L'$. If the line supports $L$, the cut removes nothing. Otherwise its section of $L$ is the segment of the two marks, since a boundary point in the relative interior of a line section forces that line to support the body. A point of $L$ removed by the cut projects along its ray from $0$ onto that segment. Thus the discarded part lies in the corresponding two-cap sector, up to null boundaries. With $W=\sum_i\theta_i\mathbf1_{H_i}$, it follows that
$$
\int_L\left(W-\frac1{1+b}\right)\ge \int_{L'}\left(W-\frac1{1+b}\right).
$$

The three retained halfplanes have outward normals $(c,1-a),(-1,1),(c-1,-a)$ and right sides $c,1,a$. Their normals span the plane and have a positive dependence with coefficients $1+a-c,a+c-1,1+c-a$. They bound a triangle $T$ containing $D$, with $0$ interior. Their reflections supply the other three edge lines of $D$, so $D=T\cap(-T)$ and $D\subseteq L'\subseteq T$.

Let $v_0,v_1,v_2$ be the vertices of $T$ in counterclockwise order and let $l_i$ be the barycentric coordinates of $0$. With $\ell_i$ the barycentric coordinate functions, the reflected inequalities are $\ell_i\le2l_i$, and each gives an edge of $D$. Thus $0<l_i<1/2$, $t_i=1-2l_i>0$, $\sum_i t_i=1$. Set $e=t_0t_1+t_0t_2+t_1t_2$. The three vertex corners $Q_i=\{x\in T:\ell_i(x)>2l_i\}$ are disjoint and have relative areas $t_i^2$ by the homothety about $v_i$; $|D|/|T|=1-\sum_i t_i^2=2e$. The section of $T$ along each reflected edge line is the segment of its two marks. Radial projection onto that segment places $Q_i$ in the sector opposite the corresponding discarded sector, so it is a singleton-cap sector.

The six endpoints can be written in cyclic indices as
$$
p_i=t_kv_i+(1-t_k)v_k,\qquad -p_i=t_jv_i+(1-t_j)v_j,\qquad (i,j,k)\text{ cyclic}.
$$
These are the edge intersections, and antipodality follows from $2\sum_i l_i v_i=0$. This chooses the sign that makes the cap at $Q_i$ the singleton indexed by $i$. The determinants of $p_i$ against $v_i,v_j,v_k$, divided by $|T|$, are respectively $(1-t_k)(1-t_j),-(1-t_k)t_j,-t_k(1-t_j)$. The original oriented triple must take either all these signs or all the opposite signs, since the three directions have a one-dimensional space of relations and both choices must balance positively. Taking all opposite signs would make the corner sectors two-cap sectors, contrary to the construction. Substituting the displayed endpoints gives
$$
\lambda_i=\frac{t_i(1-t_i)}{2e}.
$$
Indeed the proposed weights sum to one, and the coefficient of $v_i$ in their weighted sum of the $p_i$ is $[t_i(1-t_i)t_k+t_j(1-t_j)(1-t_i)]/(2e)=(1-t_i)/2=l_i$. The weighted sum is zero and the positive balance is unique.

By central symmetry, $\int_D W=|D|/2$. With $a_i=|L'\cap Q_i|/|T|$, the mean on $L'$ is
$$
\frac{e+\sum_i\theta_i a_i}{2e+\sum_i a_i},\qquad 0\le a_i\le t_i^2.
$$
The condition that it be at least $1/(1+b)$ is affine in the $a_i$ after multiplying by its positive denominator. It suffices to check the box vertices $a_i=t_i^2\mathbf1_{i\in I}$.

**Finite inequalities for $b=4/3$.** Put $A_i=t_i(\sum_j t_j-t_i)$ and $Q=\sum_i q_i A_i$, so $\theta_i=q_i A_i/Q$. After multiplying the box-vertex inequality by $(1+b)Q(2e+\sum_{i\in I}t_i^2)$, its left side minus its right side is
$$
E_I=(b-1)eQ+\sum_{i\in I}[(1+b)q_iA_i-Q]t_i^2 .
$$
It is homogeneous of degree four in the $t_i$. For each fixed $I$, the only term of the box-vertex mean depending on the multipliers is the average of $t_i^2\mathbf1_{i\in I}$ with weights $q_iA_i$, times a positive constant. The derivative of this average in $q_i$ has the sign of the $i$-th cost minus the average. A minimum over the closed multiplier box therefore assigns $q_i=b$ below the average and $q_i=1$ above it. Changing a multiplier at a tied cost equal to the average preserves the average, so that multiplier can also be set to an endpoint. When the costs are not all equal, their positive weights place the average strictly between the smallest and largest costs.

For $I=\varnothing$ the mean is $1/2$. For a singleton, put $t$ for its coordinate, $s$ for the sum of the other two and $v$ for their product, with $A=ts$, $e=A+v$, and $0\le v\le s^2/4$. The minimizing pattern is $(1,b,b)$ with the singleton first. Substituting into $E_I$ gives
$$
9E_I=7A^2+15Av+8v^2-24vt^2\ge t^2s^2+15Av+8v^2\ge0.
$$

For two corners put $t_0=x,t_1=x+y,t_2=z$ with $x,y,z\ge0$, the last coordinate omitted. Only $(1,1,b)$ and $(b,1,b)$ are needed. In the first case put $s=2x+y$, $v=x(x+y)$. The identity and square completion are
$$
9E_I=s(3vs-8vz+7sz^2),\qquad
3vs-8vz+7sz^2=7s\left(z-\frac{4v}{7s}\right)^2+vs\left(3-\frac{16v}{7s^2}\right)
$$
for $s>0$, and $v\le s^2/4$; when $s=0$ the polynomial is zero. In the second case,
$$
9E_I=(2x+y)\bigl[7x^2(x+y)-(6x^2+10xy)z+(15x+7y)z^2\bigr].
$$
The bracket has discriminant $-x^2(384x^2+496xy+96y^2)\le0$ as a quadratic in $z$. Its leading coefficient is positive unless $x=y=0$, when the polynomial is zero. If $x=0<y$ the bracket is $7yz^2$.

For all three corners put $t_0=x,t_1=x+y,t_2=x+y+z$ with $x,y,z\ge0$. The threshold argument leaves $(b,1,1)$ and $(b,b,1)$, including equal costs since when all tie either pattern gives the same mean. Expanding $9E_I$ in these two cases gives respectively
$$
\begin{aligned}
&20x^4+34x^3y+17x^3z+34x^2y^2+34x^2yz+14x^2z^2\\
&\quad+32xy^3+48xy^2z+22xyz^2+3xz^3+12y^4+24y^3z+15y^2z^2+3yz^3,\\[2pt]
&22x^4+49x^3y+10x^3z+57x^2y^2+19x^2yz+4x^2z^2\\
&\quad+44xy^3+37xy^2z+4xyz^2+14y^4+21y^3z+7y^2z^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
All coefficients are nonnegative. The identities can be checked directly by substituting the given $t_i$ and multiplier pattern into the homogeneous formula for $E_I$. This proves the box-vertex bounds. The cut comparison gives the bound on $L$, and the two-stage reweighting gives every $b\ge4/3$. Lines are null for planar area, so the use of closed caps does not affect any estimate.
