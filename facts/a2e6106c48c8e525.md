## Statement

Let \(r\ge1\) be an integer and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope satisfying \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). Use its complete lattice support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\), including all boundary lattice points, and define the unnormalized capacity

\[
B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}.
\]

Then \(B(P)\le6^r\). No continuous-centroid hypothesis is imposed on \(P\).

The infimum is attained, and its exact entropy normalization is

\[
\log B(P)=
\max_{\substack{\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\ \sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0}}
H(\lambda),
\qquad
H(\lambda)=-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log\lambda_{\mathbf I},
\]

where logarithms are natural and \(0\log0=0\). An optimizing probability vector has positive mass at every point of \(D\), and it is a limit of rational probability vectors with the same full support and exactly zero mean.

More generally, let \((a_d)_{d\ge1}\) be positive real numbers such that, for every integer \(d\ge1\) and every full-dimensional convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) whose continuous centroid is \(\mathbf0\) and whose interior lattice points consist exactly of \(\mathbf0\),
\(\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^d)\le a_d\). Then

\[
B(P)\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}a_{r(k-1)}^{1/k}.
\]

Consequently, if \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}a_d^{1/d}\le\beta<\infty\), then \(B(P)\le\beta^r\). In particular, conditional on the existence of constants \(C_1>0\) and \(q\ge0\), independent of \(d\) and \(K\), such that every such centered body satisfies \(\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^d)\le C_1(d+1)^q4^d\), one obtains \(B(P)\le4^r\). The same conditional conclusion holds for any universal bound \(a_d\) with \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}a_d^{1/d}\le4\); in particular it holds with a subexponential factor multiplying \(4^d\).

In dimension zero, with \(P=\{\mathbf0\}\subset\mathbb R^0\), the capacity is \(1\), so both numerical bounds hold directly.

## Proof

We first prove the entropy identity and the approximation assertion. Since \(P\) is a lattice polytope, \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\), and \(D\) is finite. The interior hypothesis gives \(\rho>0\) such that \(\{\mathbf x:\|\mathbf x\|\le\rho\}\subset P\). Write \(F(\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\). For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\),

\[
F(\boldsymbol\theta)
\ge \exp\!\left(\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I\right)
=\exp\!\left(\max_{\mathbf x\in P}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x\right)
\ge e^{\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}.
\]

Thus \(F\) is continuous and coercive, so it has a minimizer \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\in\mathbb R^r\). Differentiating at this unconstrained minimizer gives \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\). Hence
\(\lambda^*_{\mathbf I}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot\mathbf I}/F(\boldsymbol\theta_*)\) is a positive probability vector of mean zero, and substitution gives \(H(\lambda^*)=\log F(\boldsymbol\theta_*)=\log B(P)\).

For every mean-zero probability vector \(\lambda\) and every \(\boldsymbol\theta\), concavity of the logarithm gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\lambda)
&=\sum_{\mathbf I:\lambda_{\mathbf I}>0}
\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}}{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\\
&\le\log\!\left(\sum_{\mathbf I:\lambda_{\mathbf I}>0}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\right)
\le\log F(\boldsymbol\theta).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first equality uses the exactly zero mean. Taking \(\boldsymbol\theta=\boldsymbol\theta_*\) proves the claimed maximum identity, including feasible vectors with zero coordinates. In particular there is no division by \(|D|\) in the capacity normalization.

For rational approximation, let \(\mathsf A\) be the integer matrix whose column indexed by \(\mathbf I\in D\) is \((1,\mathbf I)\). Its rank is \(r+1\): the column at \(\mathbf0\), together with columns at any \(r\) linearly independent points of \(D\), is independent. Choose these \(r+1\) columns as an invertible square matrix \(\mathsf A_0\). Approximate the other coordinates of \(\lambda^*\) by rational numbers, and solve for the selected coordinates using
\(\mathsf A_0^{-1}\bigl((1,\mathbf0)-\sum_{\mathbf I\text{ not selected}}\lambda_{\mathbf I}(1,\mathbf I)\bigr)\).
The resulting coordinates are rational and satisfy the probability and mean equations exactly. They converge to the selected coordinates of \(\lambda^*\) because this expression is affine and continuous in the other coordinates. All coordinates are positive once the approximation is sufficiently close, because \(D\) is finite and every coordinate of \(\lambda^*\) is positive. This gives rational, full-support, exactly mean-zero vectors converging to \(\lambda^*\). Entropy is continuous on the finite probability simplex under the stated convention, so their entropies tend to \(\log B(P)\).

Fix any rational full-support mean-zero probability vector \(\lambda\). For an integer \(k\ge2\) with every \(k\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) integral, let \(\mathcal T_k(\lambda)\subset D^k\) consist of all words having exactly \(k\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) occurrences of each \(\mathbf I\). It is exactly the set of distinct block permutations of any one such word. Every label occurs at least once, and every word has block sum zero. Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_k&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_k(\lambda),\\
V_k&=\left\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_k)\in(\mathbb R^r)^k:
\sum_{a=1}^k\mathbf x_a=\mathbf0\right\},\\
L_k&=V_k\cap(\mathbb Z^r)^k.
\end{aligned}
\]

We establish that \(Q_k\) is full-dimensional in \(V_k\), has continuous relative centroid zero, and satisfies \(\operatorname{relint}(Q_k)\cap L_k=\{\mathbf0\}\).

Certainly \(Q_k\subset V_k\cap P^k\). For any two distinct block positions \(a,b\) and two distinct labels \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\in D\), there is a word with labels \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\) at those positions, since their prescribed multiplicities are positive. Subtract from that word the word obtained by swapping those positions. The difference has \(\mathbf I-\mathbf J\) in block \(a\), its negative in block \(b\), and zero elsewhere. Since \(D-D\) spans \(\mathbb R^r\), linear combinations of these differences give the same block vector for every \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r\) in place of \(\mathbf I-\mathbf J\). Taking \(b=k\) and \(1\le a<k\), these vectors span \(V_k\): any \((\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_k)\in V_k\) is their sum with \(\mathbf u=\mathbf x_a\). Thus the difference space of \(Q_k\) is \(V_k\), so \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_k)=V_k\) and \(\dim Q_k=r(k-1)\).

Average a fixed word over all \(k!\) block permutations. Every block has average \(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\), so this average is the zero vector. Every distinct word occurs with positive multiplicity in this average. It follows that \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_k)\). Indeed, if zero were on the relative boundary, a nonzero supporting linear functional on \(V_k\) could be oriented to be nonnegative on \(Q_k\). Its positive-weight average over all words would be zero, forcing its value to be zero on every word. That contradicts \(\operatorname{aff}(Q_k)=V_k\).

Equip \(V_k\) with its Euclidean volume in dimension \(r(k-1)\). Each block permutation acts orthogonally on \(V_k\) and maps \(Q_k\) to itself. Changing variables in the centroid integral therefore shows that the continuous relative centroid of \(Q_k\) is fixed by every block permutation. A fixed vector has all blocks equal to some \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r\). The centroid lies in \(V_k\), which then gives \(k\mathbf u=\mathbf0\). Thus this continuous centroid is zero. This step uses invariance of the volume integral, beyond the preceding average of the finite orbit.

Let \(\mathbf x=(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_k)\in\operatorname{relint}(Q_k)\). If \(\mathbf x=\mathbf0\), every block lies in \(\operatorname{int}(P)\). Otherwise, relative interiority permits a sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(\mathbf y=(1+\varepsilon)\mathbf x\in Q_k\). Every \(\mathbf y_a\) belongs to \(P\), and \(\mathbf x_a=\mathbf y_a/(1+\varepsilon)\). Because \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\), for every \(0\le t<1\) one has \(tP\subset\operatorname{int}(P)\): for \(\mathbf y_a\in P\), the ball of radius \((1-t)\rho\) centered at \(t\mathbf y_a\) is contained in \(tP+(1-t)P\subset P\). Applying this with \(t=1/(1+\varepsilon)\) shows that all \(\mathbf x_a\) lie in \(\operatorname{int}(P)\). If \(\mathbf x\in L_k\), every block is integral and hence every block is zero by the hypothesis on \(P\). Conversely, zero is in \(L_k\) and in \(\operatorname{relint}(Q_k)\), proving the asserted equality. No condition on lattice points of the boundary of \(Q_k\) is needed.

The lattice \(L_k\) is the kernel of the surjective block-sum map \((\mathbb Z^r)^k\to\mathbb Z^r\), so it is a primitive lattice of rank \(r(k-1)\). More explicitly, the linear isomorphism

\[
\Phi_k:(\mathbb R^r)^{k-1}\longrightarrow V_k,
\qquad
(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{k-1})\longmapsto
\left(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{k-1},-\sum_{a=1}^{k-1}\mathbf y_a\right)
\]

maps \(\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)}\) bijectively onto \(L_k\); its inverse retains the first \(k-1\) blocks. Consequently \(K_k=\Phi_k^{-1}(Q_k)\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope in \(\mathbb R^{r(k-1)}\), its sole interior lattice point is zero, and its number of lattice points is exactly \(\#(Q_k\cap L_k)\). The constant volume Jacobian of \(\Phi_k\) cancels in the normalized centroid integral, so the continuous centroid of \(K_k\) is \(\Phi_k^{-1}(\mathbf0)=\mathbf0\). In particular, this chart introduces no lattice-index factor in the count.

Every distinct word belongs to \(Q_k\cap L_k\), and the number of these words is exactly

\[
\#\mathcal T_k(\lambda)
=\frac{k!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(k\lambda_{\mathbf I})!}
\le\#(K_k\cap\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)}).
\]

The equality follows by permuting \(k\) positions and dividing by the independent permutations among equal labels. Distinct words remain distinct in the lattice chart, since their last block is determined by the preceding blocks and their zero block sum.

We next prove the general transfer for a sequence \((a_d)\) satisfying the stated counting hypothesis. The construction applies to every full-support type with exactly zero mean; it does not require one fixed probability vector as \(k\) varies. For the fixed rational \(\lambda\), choose an integer \(N\ge1\) such that \(N\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) is integral for every \(\mathbf I\). For every integer \(k\ge\max(N,2)\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_k&=N\lfloor k/N\rfloor,\qquad 0\le k-b_k<N,\\
\lambda^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
&=\frac{b_k}{k}\lambda_{\mathbf I}+
\frac{k-b_k}{k}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf0\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The word now consists of the prescribed type of length \(b_k\), padded with \(k-b_k\) copies of the label \(\mathbf0\in D\). Its total mean is still exactly zero. Every \(k\lambda^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}\) is a positive integer, so the full-support span argument remains valid for every such \(k\). Moreover \(\lambda^{(k)}\to\lambda\), since fewer than \(N\) zero labels were added. Applying the preceding construction to this type and then the assumed count bound gives

\[
\frac{k!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(k\lambda^{(k)}_{\mathbf I})!}
\le a_{r(k-1)}
\qquad(k\ge\max(N,2)).
\]

Here the left-hand side uses the complete type class, including all labels, and the auxiliary dimension is exactly \(r(k-1)\).

For completeness, comparing the sum \(\sum_{j=1}^m\log j\) with the integral of the increasing function \(\log x\) gives \(\log(m!)=m\log m-m+O(\log(m+1))\). The bound also holds for \(m=0\) with \(0!=1\) and \(0\log0=0\). Applying it to the numerator and to each of the \(|D|\) denominator factorials yields, uniformly over types on the fixed finite set \(D\),

\[
\log\frac{k!}{\prod_{\mathbf I\in D}(k\lambda^{(k)}_{\mathbf I})!}
=kH(\lambda^{(k)})+O\bigl(|D|\log(k+1)\bigr).
\]

After division by \(k\), the error tends to zero and the right-hand entropy tends to \(H(\lambda)\). Thus the \(k\)-th roots of these multinomial counts converge to \(e^{H(\lambda)}\) along all sufficiently large integers \(k\), proving
\(e^{H(\lambda)}\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}a_{r(k-1)}^{1/k}\).
The right-hand side is independent of the chosen rational vector. Letting rational full-support mean-zero vectors tend to \(\lambda^*\) proves the general bound for \(B(P)\).

If \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}a_d^{1/d}\le\beta<\infty\), then for every \(\eta>0\), all sufficiently large \(d\) satisfy \(a_d^{1/d}\le\beta+\eta\). The counting hypothesis implies \(a_d\ge1\), by applying it to \([-1/2,1/2]^d\), so \(\beta\ge1\). Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(k\),
\(a_{r(k-1)}^{1/k}\le(\beta+\eta)^{r(k-1)/k}\).
The general transfer gives \(B(P)\le(\beta+\eta)^r\), and letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) yields \(B(P)\le\beta^r\).

We now invoke Berg and Henk's Proposition 1.1, stated precisely below. If a full-dimensional convex body has continuous centroid zero and sole interior lattice point zero, its first successive minimum is at least one: any nonzero lattice point in \(tK\) with \(t<1\) would belong to \(\operatorname{int}(K)\), since \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}(K)\). The proposition therefore gives the universal count bound \(\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^d)<6^d\). In particular every orbit polytope above satisfies
\(\#\mathcal T_k(\lambda)\le\#(K_k\cap\mathbb Z^{r(k-1)})<6^{r(k-1)}\).
Taking \(a_d=6^d\) in the proved transfer yields
\(B(P)\le\lim_{k\to\infty}6^{r(k-1)/k}=6^r\).
The strict finite-dimensional count inequality gives this non-strict capacity inequality after the limit.

Finally, under the separately stated conditional bound, choose \(a_d=C_1(d+1)^q4^d\). Its \(d\)-th root tends to \(4\), because \((\log C_1+q\log(d+1))/d\to0\). The general transfer then yields \(B(P)\le4^r\). This step establishes an implication and supplies no proof of its centered lattice-count premise. The same argument applies to any premise with \(\limsup a_d^{1/d}\le4\). For \(r=0\), the only point and the only summand are zero and one respectively, so \(B(P)=1\) without applying a positive-dimensional count theorem.

## External sources

Sören Lennart Berg and Martin Henk, “Lattice point inequalities for centered convex bodies,” arXiv:1505.06444v1, Proposition 1.1 and its immediately following specialization: [primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/1505.06444).

For each \(d\ge1\), the proposition states that a full-dimensional convex body \(K\subset\mathbb R^d\) with continuous centroid \(\mathbf0\) satisfies

\[
\mathrm G(K)<2^d\left(\frac{2}{\lambda_1(K)}+1\right)^d,
\qquad
\mathrm G(K)=\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^d),
\qquad
\lambda_1(K)=\min\{t>0:tK\cap\mathbb Z^d\ne\{\mathbf0\}\}.
\]

The source explicitly gives \(\mathrm G(K)<6^d\) when zero is the sole interior lattice point. Its centroid is the Lebesgue integral divided by volume. The dimension, lattice chart, continuous centroid, and unique interior lattice point of \(K_k\) were checked above.
