## Statement

For every $n\ge1,d\ge2$ there is a compact convex mixed-integer source with $3\cdot2^{n-1}$ positive fibers and $2^n$ occupied parities such that each fiber has volume strictly below total mixed volume divided by $2^n$. In each three-fiber column the actual middle fiber equals the Minkowski midpoint of the endpoints, its centroid differs from the midpoint of the endpoint centroids, and neither endpoint admits a strict monotone enlargement with every other fiber fixed. The original two-dimensional column has fiber areas $(1/2,7/8,1)$ and centroid defect $(1/28,1/28)$; its ordinary Brunn–Minkowski volume-root deficit is strictly positive.

## Proof

Take $K_0=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$ and $K_2=[0,1]^2$. Their Minkowski midpoint $K_1$ is $[0,1]^2\cap\{x_1+x_2\le3/2\}$. The containment follows from the defining inequalities, and each of the five vertices is a midpoint of an endpoint vertex pair. The hull of the three lifted fibers at $0,1,2$ has these exact integer fibers: endpoints are extreme, and a convex combination at 1 uses equal endpoint weights, so lies in $K_1$. The only integer bases in the projection are $0,1,2$.

Removing the top-right triangle of area $1/8$ and centroid $(5/6,5/6)$ from the square gives $|K_1|=7/8$ and centroid $(19/42,19/42)$, since each first moment is $1/2-(1/8)(5/6)=19/48$. The endpoint centroids are $(1/3,1/3)$ and $(1/2,1/2)$, giving the stated defect. The largest area is 1, strictly below half the total $19/8$. Strictness of the area-root deficit also follows by squaring: $7/8-[(1/\sqrt2+1)/2]^2=(1-1/\sqrt2)/2>0$.

If an endpoint body $L_0\supseteq K_0$ is feasible while the other fibers stay fixed, midpoint inclusion yields $h_{L_0}(u)\le2h_{K_1}(u)-h_{K_2}(u)=h_{K_0}(u)$ in every direction $u$. Thus $L_0=K_0$, and the argument at endpoint 2 is identical. Product with a centered unit cube in the continuous coordinates preserves midpoint equality, the original centroid coordinates and all volumes. Product in the base with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ has one copy of the column at each binary vertex and no other integer layers. The same midpoint constraint within each layer still forces both endpoints to stay fixed. There are $3\cdot2^{n-1}$ fibers and $2^n$ occupied parities, so the total-volume threshold per parity is the original column mass divided by 2.
