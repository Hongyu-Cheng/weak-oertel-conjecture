## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $r\ge d$, $N=d+1$, and $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Let $P$ be a nonempty finite complete canonical affine-lattice base with $k$ occupied inherited parities, selected by the cardinality-minimal canonical bad-base theorem for the parity budget and target $\Gamma_d$. Range over all actual positive compatible convex $d$-fibers $K_z$ on $P$. Let $\beta_0(K)$ be their original maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth and $\beta_0^*=\inf_K\beta_0(K)$. Choose $\beta_0^*<b<\Gamma_d/k$. Use the original sublevel's compact representative frame under invertible common continuous affine maps and affine continuous shears, with $M=\sum_z|K_z|=1$, all $|K_z|\ge v>0$ and all $K_z\subset RB_d$. Write $L_{\mathrm{slab}}=|P|\kappa_{d-1}R^{d-1}$, with $\kappa_j$ the volume of the Euclidean unit $j$-ball and $\kappa_0=1$.

For a family $K$ with canonical hull $C_K$, let $L$ be affine on $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$, positive on $C_K$, and normalized by $\int L^{-r}\,d\mu_K/M=1$, where $\mu_K$ sums the fiber $d$-volumes. For a mark $(p,y)$ put
$$
f_L(p,y)=(L(p,y)E_{K_p}(y)/M)^{1/N},\qquad
G_K(u)=\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_z\int_{K_z}[u\cdot(X-g_z)]^2\,dX\right)^{1/2},
$$
where $g_z$ is the fiber centroid and $E_{K_p}$ is the marked projective energy with value zero on the own-fiber boundary. Use unchanged pure-base closed caps and nonvertical through-mark caps enlarged by $\varepsilon f_L(p,y)G_K(u)$ in their affine margins. Let $\beta_\varepsilon(K,L)$ be the resulting maximum normalized depth. For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the joint sublevel $\{\beta_\varepsilon\le b\}$ is compact modulo the specified affine normalizations. If a reference pair has $\beta_\varepsilon<b$, the infimum over all admissible pairs is attained. A reference with $L=1$ has this property for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, and for each $\varepsilon>0$,
$$
\beta_0^*\le\inf_{K,L}\beta_\varepsilon(K,L)\le\beta_0^*+\varepsilon L_{\mathrm{slab}}R .
$$

## Proof

Nonnegative buffering gives $\beta_\varepsilon\ge\beta_0$. Affine covariance for the full-space inverse-power-normalized multiplier preserves the constraint, the shift and normalized cap masses under the permitted maps. Consequently a sequence in the joint sublevel can be represented in the original mass-one compact positive frame. The canonical bad-base theorem provides, for each nonempty proper closed minor $T$, $V(T)\le bk(T)/\Gamma_d<k(T)/k$ for its inherited parity count. This passes to limits by continuity of each fiber volume.

Pass to a simultaneous positive Hausdorff limit in that frame. The inverse-power affine escape theorem with exponent $r\ge d$ gives, after passage to a subsequence, either a finite positive normalized affine limit or divergence of $L_j(p,g_{j,p})$ to infinity at all sampled centroids, or at all bases outside a nonempty proper zero face $F$ of the limiting base hull. In the all-base case use the positive-set parity center lemma with $h=1$ on $P$. Its quota premise is vacuous. In the face case choose a nonnegative affine exposing slack $h$ for $F$ on the base hull. It is midpoint concave, nonzero on $P$, and the quotas above give exactly that lemma's hypothesis on its zero set. Both cases supply a fixed base $p$ where the centroid multiplier diverges and where the limiting raw closed base depth is at least $1/k$ with the limiting mass-one weights. Inherited parities in the bad-base theorem and the center lemma agree because for a difference in the affine direction space of a minor, the half difference lies in the same space.

Centroid energy is $E_{K_{j,p}}(g_{j,p})=V_{j,p}\ge v$. The pooled covariance matrices converge to a positive definite matrix by the positive Hausdorff continuity theorem, so $\min_{\|u\|=1}G_{K_j}(u)$ stays positive. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ the enlarged margin at $(p,g_{j,p})$ therefore has displacement at least $2R$ for every unit $u$ for all large $j$. In any such nonvertical cap of base tilt $a$, every fiber with $a\cdot(z-p)\ge0$ is fully captured because $u\cdot(X-g_{j,p})\ge-2R$. That closed base trace has at least the current base depth, with the whole-base trace used for $a=0$. Pure-base caps are unchanged and attain the minimum base-trace mass, so the depth at this actual centroid equals that base depth. The latter is continuous as a minimum of finitely many linear functions of the weights. This gives $\liminf_j\beta_\varepsilon(K_j,L_j)\ge1/k>b$, contradicting the sublevel. Thus the finite positive normalized limit must occur.

Coefficient convergence of $L_j$ in fixed relative affine coordinates gives uniform convergence on same-base corresponding marks at Hausdorff distance, since all bodies are bounded and the limiting affine function is uniformly continuous there. The positive Hausdorff continuity theorem applies with these markwise multipliers and fixed $\varepsilon$, giving continuity of the normalized objective, including pure-base directions and all base tilts. The limit remains in the sublevel. This proves sequential compactness of the representatives in the metric of simultaneous Hausdorff and coefficient convergence. If an admissible reference pair has value below $b$, a tail of any minimizing sequence lies in the joint sublevel and attains its infimum there.

The original minimum is attained in the compact representative frame by the canonical bad-base theorem. On its mass-one representative use the normalized $L=1$. One has $E_{K_p}(y)\le V_p$ for every mark, including the own boundary, and $G_K(u)\le R$ for $\|u\|=1$ since the pooled within-fiber variance is at most the pooled uncentered second moment. The margin displacement is at most $\varepsilon R$. A slab of this width has mass at most $\varepsilon RL_{\mathrm{slab}}$ by the unit-normal section-area bound in $RB_d$ and summing over all fibers, also for $d=1$. Pure-base caps are unchanged. Taking depth infima and then maxima yields the upper comparison for this reference, and the lower comparison follows from $\beta_\varepsilon\ge\beta_0$. Since $\beta_0^*<b$, this reference has value below $b$ for all sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$.
