## Statement

Let $T$ be a nondegenerate triangle, $y$ an interior source, and $v_i$ nonzero spanning normals summing to zero whose through-source caps strictly isolate three distinct vertices $P_i$. Write $k=|T|$, $Q_i$ for cap areas, $\sigma_i$ for chord lengths divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_iM)$, and $\Psi=\sum_i f_i^2+\prod_i f_i$. Assume $\Psi=4$. Fix the edge $P(t)=(1-t)P_i+tP_j$ opposite $P_s$, and let $a$ be the entry into cap $j$ and $b$ the exit from cap $i$. Then $0<a<b<1$.

Take an outward vector $w$ with $|\det(P_j-P_i,w)|=1$. In the coordinates $P(t)+rw$, the first variation under an outward piecewise-affine roof $r=\varepsilon h(t)$ with $h(0)=h(1)=0$ is $D\Psi[h]=\int h\,d\nu$. The density of $\nu$ is $\alpha_i,\beta_s,\alpha_j$ on $(0,a),(a,b),(b,1)$, respectively. With $C=8+\prod_i f_i$, $E_\ell=2f_\ell^2+\prod_i f_i$ and $T_\ell=\sqrt{k/Q_\ell}$,
$$
2k\alpha_\ell=C+E_\ell T_\ell^2-Cf_\ell T_\ell,\qquad
\beta_s=\alpha_i+\alpha_j-\frac C{2k}.
$$
At either crossing $t_0$ of cap $\ell$, there is an atom of weight $(\partial_{\sigma_\ell}\Psi)/|v_\ell\cdot(P_j-P_i)|$, where $\partial_{\sigma_\ell}\Psi=-E_\ell/\sigma_\ell+C Q_\ell/(\sigma_\ell^2 M)$. The moments $\int(1-t)d\nu$ and $\int t\,d\nu$ both vanish.

Put $U(\theta)=\int[\min(t,\theta)-t\theta]\,d\nu(t)$. The unit-height triangular roof with peak $\theta\in(0,1)$ has derivative $U(\theta)/[\theta(1-\theta)]$, where
$$
U(\theta)=-\alpha_i\theta^2/2\quad(0\le\theta\le a),\qquad
U(\theta)=-\alpha_j(1-\theta)^2/2\quad(b\le\theta\le1).
$$
For $\theta=a+z(b-a)$ with $0\le z\le1$,
$$
2U(\theta)=-(1-z)\alpha_i a^2-z\alpha_j(1-b)^2+\beta_s(b-a)^2z(1-z).
$$
Both $\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_j$ are positive. The derivative is nonpositive at every peak exactly when
$$
\beta_s(b-a)^2\le \bigl(a\sqrt{\alpha_i}+(1-b)\sqrt{\alpha_j}\bigr)^2.
$$
Strict inequality gives a negative derivative at every peak. Equality in this criterion gives exactly one zero peak.

## Proof

The margin of cap $s$ is strictly negative along the edge. The other two margins sum to its opposite and hence have positive sum there. Their strict endpoint signs imply $0<a<b<1$, and the three interval signatures are respectively $\{i\},\{i,j\},\{j\}$.

The area Jacobian of the $(t,r)$ coordinates is one. The area derivatives therefore integrate $h$ over the respective fixed sign intervals. For each crossing $t_0$ the cut equation has form $(t-t_0)v_\ell\cdot(P_j-P_i)+rv_\ell\cdot w=0$. Its intersection with the roof has $r=\varepsilon h(t_0)+O(\varepsilon^2)$ because $h$ is Lipschitz and the cut is transverse to the edge. Along the cut the absolute speed in $r$ divided by $\lVert v_\ell\rVert$ is $1/|v_\ell\cdot(P_j-P_i)|$, by the determinant-one condition. This gives the chord derivative, including when the crossing coincides with a breakpoint of $h$. The transverse crossing moves by $O(\varepsilon)$ in $t$, so using fixed sign intervals in the strip changes the area only by $O(\varepsilon^2)$. The cap $s$ is uniformly negative on the edge and has no variation there.

Differentiating $f_\ell$ gives $df_\ell/f_\ell=dk/(2k)+dQ_\ell/(2Q_\ell)-d\sigma_\ell/\sigma_\ell-dM/M$. Since $\sum_\ell E_\ell=2\Psi+\prod_i f_i=C$ at equality, the partials of $\Psi$ in $k,Q_\ell,\sigma_\ell$ are $C/(2k)$, $E_\ell/(2Q_\ell)-C/(\sigma_\ell M)$, and the stated section partial. In a unique sector the unit area increases $k$ and just one $Q_\ell$. In a double sector it increases $k,Q_i,Q_j$. This gives the two density formulas and the atoms by the chain rule.

All nearby triangles formed by moving any single vertex keep the source interior and the strict incidence. By [33488d4938a6ee4a](../facts/33488d4938a6ee4a.md) they satisfy $\Psi\le4$. Since all cut crossings are transverse and away from the vertices, their $\Psi$ is differentiable in the vertex coordinates, so every first derivative vanishes at $T$. Move $P_i$ in the direction parallel to the other edge through $P_i$. That edge stays on the same line, as does the edge opposite $P_i$. The normal displacement along $P_iP_j$ to first order is a nonzero scalar multiple of $(1-t)$, since the two incident edges are independent. For example, if the moved vertex has coordinates $(\varepsilon\delta,\varepsilon\eta)$ and $P_j$ has coordinates $(1,0)$, the moving line has height $\varepsilon\eta(1-t)/(1-\varepsilon\delta)$. The endpoint corners change the strip area by $O(\varepsilon^2)$, and no cut meets those corners. The same strip calculation therefore gives a nonzero multiple of $\int(1-t)d\nu$ for the derivative. Moving $P_j$ parallel to its other incident edge gives a nonzero multiple of $\int t\,d\nu$. Stationarity forces both moments to vanish.

The Green function in the statement vanishes at both endpoints and has distributional second derivative $-\delta_t$ in $\theta$. Thus $U''=-\nu$, and the moment identities give $U'(0)=U'(1)=0$. Integrating on the outer intervals gives the two outer formulas. On the middle interval $U''=-\beta_s$, and matching the continuous endpoint values gives the middle formula. The normalization $1/[\theta(1-\theta)]$ makes its triangular roof have unit height at the peak.

As all $f_\ell>0$ and $\Psi=4$, each $f_\ell<2$. The discriminant identity $4E_\ell-Cf_\ell^2=(\prod_i f_i)(4-f_\ell^2)>0$ proves $\alpha_\ell>0$ by completing the square in $T_\ell$. The outer derivatives are therefore negative for interior peaks. For $0<z<1$, divide the middle formula by $z(1-z)$. For $A=\alpha_i a^2>0$ and $B=\alpha_j(1-b)^2>0$, the minimum of $A/z+B/(1-z)$ is $(\sqrt A+\sqrt B)^2$, uniquely at $z=\sqrt A/(\sqrt A+\sqrt B)$ by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. This proves the criterion and its equality claim.
