## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with volume $V>0$, and let $E$ be a two-dimensional linear subspace. Suppose $0$ lies in the interior of the orthogonal projection of $K$ onto $E$. Let $u_i\in E$ be a finite list of unit normals and set
$$
S_i=\mathcal H^{d-1}(K\cap u_i^\perp),\qquad
p_i=\frac{|K\cap\{x:u_i\cdot x\ge0\}|}{V}.
$$
Suppose the centroid of each section $K\cap u_i^\perp$ has orthogonal projection $0$ onto $E$. If $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\theta_i=1$, and $\sum_i\theta_i S_i u_i=0$, then
$$
\sum_i\theta_i p_i\ge c_d,\qquad
c_2=\frac49,\qquad
c_d=\frac1{1+\max\{4/3,\sqrt{2(d-1)/d}\}}\quad(d\ge3).
$$
For $3\le d\le9$ this is $3/7$. For $d\ge10$ it is strictly greater than $2/5$. In particular $c_d>(d/(d+1))^d$ for $d\ge3$.

Consequently, in any dimension $d\ge1$, if $0\in\operatorname{int}K$, each central section has full centroid $0$, and the positive-weight normals span a space of dimension at most two, then the same weighted mean is at least $(d/(d+1))^d$. In rank one the mean is exactly $1/2$, even without section centering.

## Proof

**Projection and section measures.** Write $\pi$ for orthogonal projection onto $E$. For every $x\in\operatorname{int}(\pi K)$ there is a lift in $\operatorname{int}K$. To see this, choose $z_0\in\operatorname{int}K$ and take $x_1=x+\varepsilon(x-\pi z_0)$ in $\pi K$ for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$. If $z_1\in K$ lifts $x_1$, then $(z_1+\varepsilon z_0)/(1+\varepsilon)$ is an interior lift of $x$. In particular, translating $K$ by the negative of an interior lift of $0$, a vector in $E^\perp$, puts $0$ in its interior. This preserves section volumes, projected centroids, cap fractions, and the balance. All the sections are proper, with $S_i>0$.

When $d=2$, $E=\mathbb R^2$ and the centroid condition says that the line-section segment of length $S_i$ has midpoint $0$. Fix an orientation of $E$, with positive right-angle rotation $R$. Its oriented endpoint is $R^{-1}(S_i u_i/2)$; these endpoints balance with weights $\theta_i$ by the given section-area balance. The sharp planar chord theorem b82858197fd14145 gives $4/9$.

Suppose $d\ge3$, put $m=d-2$, and define a density on $E$ by
$$
f(x)=\mathcal H^m(K\cap(x+E^\perp)).
$$
The inclusion of Minkowski combinations of fibers in the intermediate fiber and Brunn–Minkowski in $E^\perp$ show that $f^{1/m}$ is concave on the interior of $\pi K$, where $f>0$ by the interior-lift property. We set $f=0$ on the boundary of $\pi K$. This changes no planar mass and no integral along a line or ray through $0$, since the boundary is planar null and every such ray meets it at only its endpoint. The density is bounded, compactly supported and positive at $0$, and its power concavity implies log-concavity.

Set $\ell_i=E\cap u_i^\perp$. Orthogonal Fubini in $\mathbb R^d=E\oplus E^\perp$ identifies $V=\int_E f$ and $Vp_i=\int_{\{x\in E:u_i\cdot x\ge0\}} f(x)\,dx$. Orthogonal Fubini separately in the actual fixed cut plane $u_i^\perp=\ell_i\oplus E^\perp$ gives the exact identities
$$
S_i=\int_{\ell_i} f(x)\,d\mathcal H^1(x),\qquad
\int_{K\cap u_i^\perp}\pi z\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}(z)=\int_{\ell_i} x f(x)\,d\mathcal H^1(x)=0 .
$$
In the last equality the left side is $S_i$ times the projected centroid. The orthogonal decompositions have Jacobian one. These identities hold on each specified cut plane, so there is no almost-everywhere exception among the normals. The perpendicular components of the section centroids never enter.

**Radialization and comparison of section weights.** Apply the construction in 8bc17121df970992 with dimension parameter $r=2$ and inverse-power parameter $\beta=4>3$. Its convexity hypothesis holds because $f^{-1/4}=(f^{1/m})^{-m/4}$ is a convex decreasing function of the positive concave root, with extension by $+\infty$ outside its convex domain. The result is a convex body $B\subset E$ of area $V$ and with $0$ interior, whose radius is
$$
\rho_B(v)^2=2\int_0^\infty t f(tv)\,dt
$$
for each unit direction $v\in E$. It preserves the planar density mass in the through-origin caps, so the cap fractions of $B$ are precisely the $p_i$.

Choose a unit vector $v$ on $\ell_i$ and apply d49c8cc7c869ce29 to the line density $t\mapsto f(tv)$. It is integrable, log-concave, positive near zero, has first moment zero by the fixed-cut Fubini identity, and its $1/m$-power is concave on its positive interval. The two ray first moments therefore equal the same $I>0$. Thus the two radii of $B$ along that line both equal $\sqrt{2I}$, and the centered chord length $L_i$ is $2\sqrt{2I}$, exactly the length parameter in the line theorem. Its power-concave bound yields
$$
\sqrt{\frac{d}{2(d-1)}}\le r_i:=\frac{S_i}{\sqrt{f(0)}L_i}\le1.
$$
Define $\lambda_i=\theta_i r_i/\sum_j\theta_jr_j$. Since $r_iL_i=S_i/\sqrt{f(0)}$, we have $\sum_i\lambda_i L_i u_i=0$. The bisected oriented chord endpoints $R^{-1}(L_i u_i/2)$ of $B$ consequently balance with the weights $\lambda_i$. The original $\theta_i$ are exactly the normalization of $\lambda_i/r_i$, and the multipliers $1/r_i$ lie in $[1,\sqrt{2(d-1)/d}]$. Apply the robust chord theorem with $b=\max\{4/3,\sqrt{2(d-1)/d}\}$. The resulting cap mean is at least $c_d$.

**Constants and the rank-two consequence.** The square-root term is at most $4/3$ exactly for $d\le9$. Hence for $3\le d\le9$ the bound is $3/7>27/64$. For $d\ge10$, both terms defining $b$ are strictly below $3/2$, so $c_d>2/5$. The comparison $2/5>(6/7)^6$ is $2\cdot7^6=235298>233280=5\cdot6^6$. The function $x\mapsto x\log(x/(x+1))$ is decreasing for $x>0$, since its derivative is $-\log(1+1/x)+1/(x+1)<0$. Thus the stated comparisons imply $c_d>(d/(d+1))^d$ in every dimension $d\ge3$.

For a positive-weight rank-two family, take $E$ to be the span of those normals, ignoring zero weights. An interior origin projects to an interior origin, and full section centering implies projected centering. This gives the rank-two case of 3ed6256a0896f434 §5, including $d=2$. In rank one the two possible oriented normals have the same positive section measure. The balance puts total weight $1/2$ on each orientation, and their volume fractions add to one, because the hyperplane has zero $d$-volume. This gives the claimed mean $1/2$, including $d=1$ with its $\mathcal H^0$ section measure.
