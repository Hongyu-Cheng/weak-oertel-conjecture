## Statement

For each integer \(m\ge 1\), let
\[
Q_m=[0,m^2]^2,\qquad D_m=Q_m\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad w^{(m)}_{(i,j)}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{i+j}{m}\right),
\]
and put
\[
W_m=\sum_{(i,j)\in D_m}w^{(m)}_{(i,j)}.
\]
For a finite set \(A\subset\mathbb Z^2\) and \(y\in\mathbb R^2\), write
\[
d_A(y)=\inf\{|A\cap H|:H\subseteq\mathbb R^2
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\}.
\]
For \(s>0\), set
\[
A_s^{(m)}=\{j\in D_m:w_j^{(m)}\ge s\}.
\]
Using the actual-depth layer normalization of accepted fact
\(\mathtt{d52c89d30b11ee18}\), define
\[
L(w^{(m)})=3\int_0^\infty s^2
\sum_{j\in A_s^{(m)}}d_{A_s^{(m)}}(j)^2\,ds.
\]
Then
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{L(w^{(m)})}{W_m^3}
=\frac{68}{19683}.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if an absolute \(\kappa>0\) satisfies
\[
L(w)\ge \kappa 4^{-N}W^3
\tag{2}
\]
for every complete convex lattice support \(D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\) and every log-affine positive table on it, then necessarily
\[
\kappa\le \frac{1088}{19683}.
\tag{3}
\]
The family above has a positive limit in (1). Thus (1)--(3) give only an upper calibration of a possible universal layer constant; they do not disprove the existence of a positive \(\kappa\) in (2), and they do not disprove the locked weak Oertel statement.

## Proof

The support and the weights have exactly the required structure:
\(D_m=Q_m\cap\mathbb Z^2\), where \(Q_m\) is compact and convex, and
\[
w_j^{(m)}=e^{\ell_m\cdot j},
\qquad \ell_m=(-1/m,-1/m)\ne0.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, this is a genuinely log-affine table on a complete convex lattice support.

We first record its mass scale. With
\[
S_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m^2}e^{-i/m},
\]
one has \(W_m=S_m^2\), and the geometric-series formula gives
\[
\frac{S_m}{m}
=\frac{1-e^{-(m^2+1)/m}}{m(1-e^{-1/m})}
\longrightarrow 1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{W_m}{m^2}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\frac{W_m^3}{m^6}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{5}
\]

Let
\[
\Delta=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le1\}.
\]
For a nondegenerate triangle \(T\) and \(z\in T\), let
\[
\delta_T(z)=\inf_{H\ni z}\operatorname{area}(T\cap H),
\]
where the infimum is over closed halfplanes. If \(A_T=\operatorname{area}(T)\) and
\(\lambda_{(1)}(z)\le\lambda_{(2)}(z)\le\lambda_{(3)}(z)\) are the ordered barycentric coordinates of \(z\), the general triangle formula proved in accepted fact \(\mathtt{431423848c9bacf8}\), after translating \(z\) to the origin, says
\[
\delta_T(z)=4A_T\lambda_{(1)}(z)\lambda_{(2)}(z).
\tag{6}
\]
The same formula includes boundary points, where its right-hand side is zero. The normalized area depth on \(\Delta\) is consequently
\[
\tau_\Delta(z):=\frac{\delta_\Delta(z)}{|\Delta|}
=4\lambda_{(1)}(z)\lambda_{(2)}(z).
\tag{7}
\]

We next compute its squared moment exactly. A uniform point of \(\Delta\) has density \(2\), and permutations of its three barycentric coordinates preserve this law. On one of the six ordering regions, write the least and middle coordinates as \(x\) and \(y\). That region is
\[
0\le x\le\frac13,
\qquad x\le y\le\frac{1-x}{2}.
\]
Using (7), symmetry over the six regions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\tau_\Delta^2
&=6\cdot2\int_0^{1/3}\int_x^{(1-x)/2}(4xy)^2\,dy\,dx\\
&=192\int_0^{1/3}x^2\int_x^{(1-x)/2}y^2\,dy\,dx\\
&=\frac{34}{1215}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
For an explicit evaluation of the last integral, its value is
\[
\left[
\frac83x^3-6x^4+\frac{24}{5}x^5-12x^6
\right]_{0}^{1/3}
=\frac{34}{1215}.
\]
Since \(|\Delta|=1/2\), equations (7)--(8) imply
\[
\int_\Delta\delta_\Delta(z)^2\,dz
=|\Delta|^3\mathbb E\tau_\Delta^2
=\frac18\frac{34}{1215}.
\tag{9}
\]
Area and halfspace depth both scale quadratically under dilation in the plane. Hence, for every \(t\ge0\),
\[
\int_{t\Delta}\delta_{t\Delta}(z)^2\,dz
=t^6\int_\Delta\delta_\Delta(z)^2\,dz
=\frac{t^6}{8}\frac{34}{1215}.
\tag{10}
\]
At \(t=0\), both sides are interpreted as zero.

We now justify passage from lattice depths to (10), uniformly over the halfplanes used in each depth infimum. We use the following elementary planar discrepancy estimate. For every fixed \(t\ge0\), every integer \(m\ge1\), and every closed halfplane \(H\),
\[
\left|
m^{-2}|(t\Delta\cap H)\cap m^{-1}\mathbb Z^2|
-\operatorname{area}(t\Delta\cap H)
\right|
\le C\left(\frac{t}{m}+\frac1{m^2}\right),
\tag{11}
\]
where \(C\) is an absolute constant, independent of \(H,t,m\).

Indeed, tile the plane by half-open squares of side \(m^{-1}\), centered at the points of \(m^{-1}\mathbb Z^2\). The Riemann sum and the area can differ only on squares meeting the boundary of \(t\Delta\cap H\). This intersection is a convex polygon with at most four sides, including its possible degenerate cases. Every side has length at most the diameter \(\sqrt2t\). A line segment of length \(a\) meets at most \(C_0(1+ma)\) such squares: split at its crossings of the vertical and horizontal grid lines, with a fixed factor covering a segment lying on a grid line. Thus the number of boundary squares is at most \(C_1(1+mt)\). Multiplying by their area \(m^{-2}\) proves (11). In particular, (11) is uniform over all halfplanes, and hence over all halfplanes containing any specified target.

Fix \(t\ge0\). Since
\[
w_{(i,j)}^{(m)}\ge e^{-t}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
i+j\le mt,
\]
for every sufficiently large \(m\) (namely \(m\ge t\)) the upper bounds \(i,j\le m^2\) are redundant and
\[
m^{-1}A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}
=(t\Delta)\cap m^{-1}\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(z\) in the grid set in (12), define
\[
f_{m,t}(z)=m^{-2}d_{A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}(mz),
\qquad
f_t(z)=\delta_{t\Delta}(z).
\]
Scaling by \(m^{-1}\) bijects the halfplanes in the two definitions. Since (11) holds for every halfplane, the elementary inequality
\[
\left|\inf_H a_H-\inf_H b_H\right|
\le\sup_H|a_H-b_H|
\]
gives
\[
\sup_{z\in(t\Delta)\cap m^{-1}\mathbb Z^2}
|f_{m,t}(z)-f_t(z)|
\le C\left(\frac{t}{m}+\frac1{m^2}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
This is the required halfspace-uniform lattice-to-area convergence after taking the depth infimum.

By (6), \(f_t\) is continuous on \(t\Delta\): it is a constant multiple of the product of the two least among three nonnegative affine barycentric-coordinate functions. Thus the ordinary lattice Riemann sums for \(f_t^2\) converge to its integral. The grid cardinalities multiplied by \(m^{-2}\) stay bounded for fixed \(t\), so (13) permits replacement of \(f_{m,t}^2\) by \(f_t^2\) in those sums. It follows from (10) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
m^{-6}\sum_{j\in A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}
d_{A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}(j)^2
&=m^{-2}\sum_{z\in(t\Delta)\cap m^{-1}\mathbb Z^2}
f_{m,t}(z)^2\\
&\longrightarrow
\int_{t\Delta}\delta_{t\Delta}(z)^2\,dz
=\frac{t^6}{8}\frac{34}{1215}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
For \(t=0\), the left side is \(m^{-6}\) and has the same zero limit, so (14) holds there as well.

It remains to justify integration of (14) over all levels. Since every weight in (4) is at most one, the levels \(s>1\) are empty. With the substitution \(s=e^{-t}\), the definition of the layer functional becomes
\[
L(w^{(m)})
=3\int_0^\infty e^{-3t}
\sum_{j\in A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}
d_{A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}(j)^2\,dt.
\tag{15}
\]
For every \(t\ge0\), putting \(k=\lfloor mt\rfloor\) and ignoring the box truncation gives
\[
|A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}|
\le\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}2
\le\frac{(mt+2)^2}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(d_A(j)\le|A|\), equations (16) imply
\[
m^{-6}\sum_{j\in A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}
d_{A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}}(j)^2
\le m^{-6}|A_{e^{-t}}^{(m)}|^3
\le\frac{(t+2)^6}{8}.
\tag{17}
\]
The right-hand side of (17), multiplied by \(3e^{-3t}\), is integrable on \([0,\infty)\). Dominated convergence applied to (14)--(17), followed by (5), therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{L(w^{(m)})}{W_m^3}
&=\frac38\frac{34}{1215}
\int_0^\infty e^{-3t}t^6\,dt\\
&=\frac38\frac{34}{1215}\frac{6!}{3^7}\\
&=\frac{10}{81}\frac{34}{1215}
=\frac{68}{19683}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
This proves (1).

Finally, apply any putative universal inequality (2) to this admissible family with \(N=2\). It gives
\[
\frac{L(w^{(m)})}{W_m^3}\ge\frac{\kappa}{16}.
\]
Taking \(m\to\infty\) and using (18) proves
\[
\kappa\le16\frac{68}{19683}=\frac{1088}{19683},
\]
which is (3). Since the limit in (18) is strictly positive, the family does not make \(4^2L/W^3\) tend to zero. It is therefore an upper calibration only, not a counterexample to positivity and not a counterexample to weak Oertel.
