## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and $0<a<1$. Let $H=(1-a)/a$, $r(z)=(H-z)/(H+1)$ and consider the cone $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, with planar density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$, and $C(s),M(s)$ the section integrals of $f,zf$, respectively, with respect to $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. Put $V_0=(H+1)/d$, $C_0=(1-a)^{d-1}$ and $p=(1-a)^d$. For $s>0$ define
$$
e_s=\frac1{2s}\int_{-s}^{\min(s,1)}(1-ab)^{-d}\,db,\qquad
m_s=\partial_a e_s,\qquad k_s=\frac{(s-1)_+}{2s},\qquad v_s=1-k_s.
$$
At $s=0$ set $(e_0,m_0,k_0)=(1,0,0)$. Thus $e_s=C(s)/C_0$ and $M(s)/C_0=-(1-a)m_s/d$. A centered law is a probability law with average section moment zero.

Take $d=2$, $a=1/4$, and restrict the finite slopes to $[0,3]$. The set of $(\mathbb E e_s,\mathbb E v_s)$ of centered probability laws strictly contains the segment generated by the two individually centered slope records. In particular the centered law $(50/197)\delta_1+(147/197)\delta_3$ has $(\mathbb E e_s,\mathbb E v_s)=(128/197,148/197)$, outside the segment. These records also distinguish the laws by their bounded-slope frustum cap and coarea at cutoff $T=3$.

## Proof

The cone record fact 18e7bdf54588779d gives the stated moment and coarea normalization. For $0<s\le1$, pairing $b$ with $-b$ in the derivative of $e_s$ gives $m_s>0$. For $s\ge1$, integrating at $d=2$ and differentiating gives $e_s=(s+1)/(2s(1-a)(1+as))$ and $m_s=e_s[1/(1-a)-s/(1+as)]$. Thus the only zeros of $m_s$ on $[0,3]$ are $0$ and $2$, with $(e_s,v_s)=(1,1)$ and $(2/3,3/4)$.

At slopes $1$ and $3$ the triples $(e_s,m_s,v_s)$ are $(16/15,128/225,1)$ and $(32/63,-256/1323,2/3)$. Their weighted sum with the stated weights has moment coordinate zero and $(e,v)=(128/197,148/197)$. The singleton segment at $v=148/197$ has $e=1-\frac43(1-148/197)=395/591$, while the mixture has $e=384/591$. All segment points arise by mixing the two centered singletons. At $T=3$, fact f4be2679c83c2fd1 gives the same $C,M$ as the cone for all these slopes and $P/V_0=pe_s+1-v_s-t$ with fixed $t=p/(1+3a)^2$. Since $C_0,V_0>0$, the map from $(e,v)$ to $(C,P)$ is invertible affine, so it preserves the separation.
