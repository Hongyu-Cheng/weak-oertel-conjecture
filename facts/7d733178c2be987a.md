## Statement

Let a proper oriented section of a $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, have exactly two positive affine vertex heights $p\ge q>0$, at least one strictly negative height, and any number of zero heights. Let $F$ be the fraction of simplex volume in the nonnegative halfspace, and $b_i$ the barycentric coordinates of the centroid of the uniform $(d-1)$-dimensional section measure. Let $f$ denote the density at zero of the sum of the vertex heights times independent unit exponentials. Every zero-height index satisfies $db_i=1$.

For $p>q$, write the negative heights as $-r_j$, and put
$$
h=1-q/p,\quad a_j=\frac{r_j}{q+r_j},\quad
s_j=\frac p{p+r_j},\quad t_j=\frac q{q+r_j},\quad
X=\prod_j s_j,\quad Y=\prod_j t_j,\quad
\rho=Y/X,\quad L=\frac{h\rho}{1-\rho},\quad S=\sum_j a_j.
$$
Then $F=(pX-qY)/(p-q)$, $f=(X-Y)/(p-q)$, $db_q=(1-LS)/h$, and $db_j=s_j(1+La_j)$ at each negative index $j$.

For $p=q$, rescale the heights to $p=q=1$ for the formulas, and put $a_j=(1+r_j)^{-1}$, $t_j=1-a_j$, $Z=\sum_jt_j$, and $L=\prod_j a_j$. Then
$$
F=L(1+Z),\qquad f=LZ,\qquad
db_p=db_q=\frac{Z^2+\sum_jt_j^2}{2Z},\qquad db_j=\frac{a_j(Z+t_j)}{Z}.
$$
In either case, if $b_q\le1/d$, then $F>d^d\prod_{j\ne p}b_j$, omitting the specified $p$-vertex. For equal positive heights either captured vertex can be omitted.

## Proof

Let $V=\sum_i c_iE_i$ for the vertex heights $c_i$ and independent unit exponentials $E_i$. Their normalized vector is uniform on the barycentric simplex, so $F=\Pr(V\ge0)$. The proof of 6df0487380ed701a gives the conditional law at $V=0$ (choose a different nonzero elimination coordinate from the coordinate whose mean is computed), with radial law $\operatorname{Gamma}(d,1)$ and uniform angular law on the section. Thus $\mathbb E[E_i\mid V=0]=db_i$ for every coordinate. In 511d89569241b0d6 the derivative $P_i$ of $F$ in $c_i$ is obtained by weighting the exponential law by $E_i$. In the coarea conditional law this gives $P_i=fdb_i$. This can also be seen in that fact's formula $\mathbb E[E_i g_i(-c_iE_i)]$ for the derivative, which integrates the conditional density before dividing by $f$. These facts apply since there are two positive heights and at least one negative height. At zero height $P_i=f$. Deleting zero phases leaves $V$, $F$, and each other $P_i/f$ unchanged. Thus all formulas and the product can use just the negative phases and the two positive ones, even when the ambient $d$ is larger.

For $p>q$, the density of $pE_0+qE_1$ at $u>0$ is $(e^{-u/p}-e^{-u/q})/(p-q)$, by convolution. Its survival function is $(pe^{-u/p}-qe^{-u/q})/(p-q)$. Integrate both at the independent sum $\sum_jr_jE_{j+1}$, whose transforms at $1/p,1/q$ are $X,Y$. This gives the formulas for $F,f$. Differentiating $F$ in $q$ and in $-r_j$ yields
$$
\frac{P_q}{f}=\frac1h-\frac{\rho S}{1-\rho},\qquad
\frac{P_j}{f}=\frac{s_j-\rho t_j}{1-\rho}.
$$
Here $q\,\partial_qY=YS$, $t_j/s_j=1-ha_j$, and $\rho=\prod_j(1-ha_j)$. This proves the stated centroid formulas and makes $b_q\le1/d$ equivalent to $LS\ge1-h$.

We prove the finite scalar estimate needed for these parameters. For $0<x_j<1$ put $\rho=\prod_j(1-x_j)$, $\tau=\rho/(1-\rho)$, and $z=\tau\sum_jx_j$. Then
$$
(1-z)\prod_j(1+\tau x_j)\le1-\rho z,
$$
strictly when there are at least two entries. To prove it, let $u_j=x_j/(1-x_j)$ and let $e_k$ be their elementary symmetric coefficients. Set $A=\prod_j(1+u_j)$, $E=A-1$, and $C=\sum_{k\ge1}(k-1)e_k$. Each squarefree term of degree $k\ge2$ has coefficient $2^k-2>k-1$ in $E^2$, and the nonsquarefree terms also contribute positively, so $C<E^2$ even with a single entry. The law $\Pr(N=k-1)=e_k/E$ has contiguous finite positive support, is log-concave by the elementary-symmetric convolution calculation in the result “A sharp product bound for a finite probability vector”, and has mean $\mu=C/E$. With $T_s=\Pr(N\ge s)$, the ratios of consecutive probabilities decrease, hence $T_s/\Pr(N=s)$ decreases on the positive support by termwise ratio comparison. Consequently $T_{s+1}/T_s$ decreases and $T_{a+b}\le T_aT_b$ for nonnegative integers, including zero tails. Counting positive ordered $r$-tuples of total $s$ gives
$$
\mathbb E\binom Nr=\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_r\ge1}T_{k_1+\cdots+k_r}
\le\left(\sum_{k\ge1}T_k\right)^r=\mu^r.
$$
Therefore for $t\ge0$ with $t\mu<1$, $\mathbb E(1+t)^N\le(1-t\mu)^{-1}$. This is strict when $t\mu>0$ since the binomial expansion is finite. Take $t=1/E$. We have $\rho=1/A$, $\tau=1/E$, $A\sum_j x_j=\sum_k k e_k=E+C$, and direct expansion gives
$$
1-z=(1-\rho)(1-C/E^2),\qquad
\prod_j(1+\tau x_j)=\rho+\mathbb E(1+1/E)^N.
$$
For the product identity use $1+\tau x_j=(1+(A/E)u_j)/(1+u_j)$. The claimed scalar estimate is thus equivalent to $(1-C/E^2)\mathbb E(1+1/E)^N\le1$. It is strict when $C>0$, and $0<z<1$ follows from the identities.

Apply this estimate to $x_j=ha_j$, so that $\tau x_j=La_j$ and $z=LS$. Multiplying the proposed product comparison by $h/X$ gives the two sides
$$
(1-LS)\prod_j(1+La_j)
\le1-\rho LS\le1-(1-h)\rho .
$$
The last inequality is the centroid condition. The first is strict with at least two negative indices. With one negative index $LS=1-ha_1>1-h$ because $a_1<1$, so the second is strict.

For $p=q=1$, the sum of the positive phases has density $ue^{-u}$ and survival function $(1+u)e^{-u}$. Conditioning at the negative sum gives $f=LZ$ and $F=L(1+Z)$. Differentiation in $-r_j$ gives $P_j=L a_j(Z+t_j)$. Symmetry and the degree-zero homogeneity of $F$ in the heights give $2P_p=\sum_jr_jP_j=L(Z^2+\sum_jt_j^2)$. Dividing by $f$ proves the remaining centroid formulas. Put $v_j=t_j/Z$ and $s=\sum_jv_j^2$. The product omitting one of the two positive vertices is $L Z(1+s)\prod_j(1+v_j)/2$, while $b_q\le1/d$ gives $Z(1+s)\le2$. By the result “A sharp product bound for a finite probability vector”,
$$
(1+s)\prod_j(1+v_j)\le3+s\le2(1+1/Z).
$$
The first comparison is strict with at least two negative indices. With one negative index $Z=t_1<1$ and $s=1$, so the second is strict. Multiplying by $LZ/2$ proves the result.
