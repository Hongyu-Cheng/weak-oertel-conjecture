## Statement

Let $C=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2:|z|\le1,\ |x|+|z|\le2\}$, with Euclidean $|x|$. Let $A_r(t)$ be the area of a radius-$r$ disk above height $t$. At $y_t=(0,0,t)$ for $0<t<1$, the globally minimum mixed mass of any closed containing halfspace is $V(t)=A_2(t)+2A_1(t)$, attained by the horizontal cut. Its three sections are proper and have full mixed section centroid $y_t$. For each $p>0$, $(V^p)''(t)>0$ near $1$ from below.

## Proof

The three integer fibers are concentric disks of radii $1,2,1$. Their chord profile is $f_r(s)=2\sqrt{(r^2-s^2)_+}$, even and decreasing in $|s|$. For a unit continuous normal and anchor threshold $s\ge0$, the two endpoint masses under integer tilt $v$ have sum $A_1(s+v)+A_1(s-v)$. For $v\ge0$ its derivative is $f_1(s-v)-f_1(s+v)\ge0$, so its minimum is at zero tilt. For $s\le0$ the endpoint sum is at least $\pi$, since $A_1(s+v)\ge A_1(-s+v)=\pi-A_1(s-v)$. Its middle term is then at least $2\pi$.

A containing cap may be shrunk to pass through $y_t$. The scalar threshold for any unit continuous normal is in $[-t,t]$. For nonnegative thresholds the zero-tilt sum decreases with threshold, while negative thresholds cost at least $3\pi>V(t)$. A vertical cap through $y_t$ costs at least $5\pi$. This proves the global minimum. At the horizontal cut each chord center is $(0,t)$ in its continuous coordinates and the outer chord lengths agree. They therefore give section centroid $y_t$, with all chords proper. Finally $V(t)\to A_2(1)>0$, $V'=-2\sqrt{4-t^2}-4\sqrt{1-t^2}$ is bounded, and $V''=2t/\sqrt{4-t^2}+4t/\sqrt{1-t^2}\to+\infty$. Hence $pV^{p-2}[VV''+(p-1)(V')^2]$ is eventually positive for every fixed $p>0$.
