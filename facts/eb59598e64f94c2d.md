## Statement

Let $\mu$ sum $d$-volume on finitely many positive compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ at bases $z\in\mathbb R^n$, with $d\ge1$. Define the raw depth $D(Y)$ of an ambient point to be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed halfspaces containing $Y$. Let $H=\{w:N\cdot(w-Y)\ge0\}$ attain $Q=D(Y)$, with nonzero continuous component of $N$. Assume its boundary cuts the interior of at least one sampled fiber. Assume also that for every ambient vector $v$ the function
$$
t\longmapsto\mu\{w:(N+t v)\cdot(w-Y)\ge0\}
$$
is differentiable at zero. Then $H\cap\{D\ge Q\}=\{Y\}$.

## Proof

Choose a ball in one proper fiber centered on its old cutting plane, with positive radius. For normals and section levels sufficiently close to the old ones, every intermediate section through that ball contains a $(d-1)$-ball of fixed positive radius, or one point when $d=1$. Integrating those sections bounds from below the mass removed by an upward level shift of size $h$ by a fixed positive constant times $h$. The continuous component of the normal stays bounded above and away from zero in this comparison.

For a point $Y'$ strictly inside $H$, shift the old threshold upward by a small positive amount below $N\cdot(Y'-Y)$. The shifted cap still contains $Y'$ and loses positive mass in the proper fiber. Hence $D(Y')<Q$.

For a point $Y'\ne Y$ on the old boundary, put $v=Y'-Y$ and $N_t=N+t v$. The through-$Y$ cap mass is at least $Q$ for both signs of small $t$. Differentiability at its minimum gives mass $Q+o(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$. The cap with the same $N_t$ through $Y'$ raises its threshold by
$$
N_t\cdot(Y'-Y)=t\lVert v\rVert^2.
$$
The proper-fiber strip bound now gives mass at most $Q+o(t)-c t\lVert v\rVert^2$ for some $c>0$. This is below $Q$ for sufficiently small positive $t$. The cap contains $Y'$ and proves the strict inequality there.
