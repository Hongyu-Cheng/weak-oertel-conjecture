## Statement

Set \(T_*:=\log64\), and define the absolute constants
$$
C_*:=2^{13}T_*^2e^{4T_*},\qquad
c_*:=\frac14e^{-C_*},\qquad
\gamma:=c_*^{3/2}=\frac18e^{-3C_*/2}.
\tag{1}
$$
Let \(L\ge1\) be an integer. For each \(1\le i\le L\), let \(n_i\ge1\) be an integer, let \(Q_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_i}\) be convex with finite nonempty complete lattice support \(A_i=Q_i\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), and assume that every coordinatewise parity class contains at most two points of \(A_i\). Choose one globally affine raw weight law \(w_{i,x}=\exp(c_i+\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot x)\), where \(c_i\in\mathbb R\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\). Retain the original normalizers, probabilities, and product:
$$
Z_i:=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},\qquad
p_{i,x}:=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},\qquad
P:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i,\qquad
N:=\sum_{i=1}^L n_i.
\tag{2}
$$
Extend every law by zero outside its support. Assume that each factor satisfies at least one of the following two conditions:

- (B) For every distinct \(u,v\in A_i\) with \(u\equiv v\pmod2\), one has \(\left|\tfrac12\log(w_{i,v}/w_{i,u})\right|\le T_*\).
- (S) Every two distinct values of \(\log w_{i,x}\), for \(x\in A_i\), differ by at least \(T_*\).

Choose any partition \(\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace=\mathscr B\sqcup\mathscr S\) consistent with these conditions. Thus a factor satisfying both conditions may be assigned to either part. Retain the full-row and score notation of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
R_{i,j}:=\lbrace x\in A_i:2j-x\in A_i\rbrace,
\qquad
Y_{i,j}(x):=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}}.
\tag{3}
$$
Use hats only for selected-row analogues of those quantities, as in accepted fact 4e2de5c00a49d7b9. For every supported center \(j\in A_i\), set
$$
\widehat R_{i,j}:=
\begin{cases}
R_{i,j},&i\in\mathscr B,\\
\lbrace x\in R_{i,j}:w_{i,x}=w_{i,j}\rbrace,&i\in\mathscr S,
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
$$
and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_{p_i}(j)
&:=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat b(p_i)&:=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}\widehat H_{p_i}(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)
&:=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_{p_i}(j)},
&\widehat V_{i,j}&:=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
\widehat q_{i,j}
&:=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_{p_i}(j)^2}{\widehat b(p_i)},
&\widehat\nu_i&:=\sum_{j\in A_i}\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Here \(\widehat\kappa_{i,j}\) is defined on \(\widehat R_{i,j}\); the original center law in the moments remains \(p_i\). All rows in (4) contain their central atom, and every noncentral reflection orbit retained in a row has both orientations.

For an explicit alias map between the two predecessor normalizations, let \(K_i^{\mathrm{occ}}\) be the number of occupied coordinatewise parity classes of \(A_i\), and let \(K_i^{\mathrm{amb}}:=2^{n_i}\). The symbol \(K\) of accepted fact df930bf328963eef is \(K_i^{\mathrm{occ}}\) here; the symbol \(K\) of accepted fact 4e2de5c00a49d7b9 is \(K_i^{\mathrm{amb}}\). Define the selected ambient energy by \(\mathcal A_i:=(K_i^{\mathrm{amb}})^2\widehat b(p_i)=4^{n_i}\widehat b(p_i)\). Then
$$
\mathcal A_i\ge1,\qquad
0\le\widehat\nu_i\le C_*\log\mathcal A_i
\quad(1\le i\le L).
\tag{6}
$$

For \(s>0\), use the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and moments of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\tau_P(J):=\inf_{\substack{\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed halfspace}\\J\in\mathcal H}}P(\mathcal H),
\qquad
M_s(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^s.
\tag{7}
$$
Then the original product (2) satisfies
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_*4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\gamma8^{-N}.
\tag{8}
$$
In particular the one absolute coefficient \(\gamma>0\) satisfies both \(M_2(P)\ge\gamma4^{-N}\) and \(M_3(P)\ge\gamma8^{-N}\), independently of the factor count, dimensions, supports, affine ranks, and weights.

Every factor with at most three distinct raw weight values satisfies (B) or (S), so (8) holds for arbitrary heterogeneous products of all such factors. Uniform laws, singleton or lower-dimensional supports, and factors with no distinct same-parity pair are included. This proves the arithmetic cubic inequality on this restricted product class. It does not establish the all-support hypothesis \((\mathrm H_{3,N}(\gamma))\) of accepted fact c5f6533ba1c11991, does not cover arbitrary weight spectra outside the union of (B) and (S), and does not prove the locked mixed-integer-volume conjecture.

## Proof

Convex completeness implies that the midpoint \(j=(u+v)/2\) of every same-parity pair \(u,v\in A_i\) belongs to \(A_i\). Global affinity, with precisely the original normalization (2), gives
$$
w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,u}w_{i,v},\qquad
p_{i,j}^{\,2}=p_{i,u}p_{i,v},\qquad
\frac{p_{i,v}}{p_{i,u}}=\frac{w_{i,v}}{w_{i,u}}.
\tag{9}
$$
For every full reflected row about \(j\), this also gives \(w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}=w_{i,j}^2\). Consequently (4) is symmetric about \(j\) in either branch. In branch (S), its defining equality forces \(w_{i,x}=w_{i,j}=w_{i,2j-x}\), so its score is zero. Every selected row contains \(j\) and hence \(\widehat H_{p_i}(j)\ge p_{i,j}>0\). Thus every quantity in (5) is well defined, and \(\widehat q_i\) is a probability law on all original centers.

For \(i\in\mathscr B\), apply accepted fact df930bf328963eef with \(T=T_*\). Its hypotheses hold by parity occupancy, (9), and condition (B). Its quantities \(H_{p_i},b(p_i),\kappa_{i,j},V_{i,j},q_{i,j},\nu_i\) equal the corresponding hatted quantities in (5), because (4) retains the entire row. Its occupied normalization is \((K_i^{\mathrm{occ}})^2b(p_i)\), not the ambient normalization \(\mathcal A_i\). It gives
$$
(K_i^{\mathrm{occ}})^2b(p_i)\ge1,\qquad
\widehat\nu_i\le C_*\log\bigl((K_i^{\mathrm{occ}})^2b(p_i)\bigr).
\tag{10}
$$
Since \(K_i^{\mathrm{occ}}\le K_i^{\mathrm{amb}}\), (10) implies \(\mathcal A_i\ge1\) and \(\widehat\nu_i\le C_*\log\mathcal A_i\). This use of the accepted theorem imposes no bound on the number of pairs sharing one midpoint.

For \(i\in\mathscr S\), apply accepted fact 4e2de5c00a49d7b9. Its complete convex support, parity occupancy, global affinity, and spectral-gap hypotheses are exactly the present hypotheses and (S). Its same-weight selected rows are precisely (4), its original \(Z,p\) are (2), and its ambient \(K\) is \(K_i^{\mathrm{amb}}\). It gives \(\widehat b(p_i)\ge4^{-n_i}\) and \(\widehat\nu_i=0\), including the uniform and lower-rank cases. These establish (6) in this branch as well.

The use of selected rows has changed no original atom weight or normalizer. In particular the exact raw-weight formula in either branch is
$$
\widehat b(p_i)=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{11}
$$
All original centers and their central row entries occur in this sum.

We now apply the reflection argument of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 directly to the mixed selected rows. For a supported product center \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), define \(\widehat R_J:=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}\), and define its selected reflected mass by summing \(\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}\) over \(X\in\widehat R_J\). Finite product factorization gives
$$
\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_{p_i}(j_i),\qquad
\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i},\qquad
Y_J(X):=\frac12\log\frac{P_X}{P_{2J-X}}
=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i).
\tag{12}
$$
Here \(\widehat\kappa_J(X)=\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}/\widehat H_P(J)\) on \(\widehat R_J\). The factor-row reflection preserves each geometric law \(\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}\) and negates its score, so \(\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}}Y_{i,j_i}=0\). Independence under (12) therefore gives, with \(\sigma_J:=(\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i})^{1/2}\),
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,\qquad
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}.
\tag{13}
$$
The last equality is the exact original likelihood identity on the selected row, with \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,X_i}/\prod_iZ_i\).

Let \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be any closed halfspace containing \(J\). Each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2J-X\rbrace\) of \(\widehat R_J\) has at least one endpoint in \(\mathcal H\): a linear inequality strictly excluding both endpoints would strictly exclude their midpoint \(J\). The central atom \(J\) also belongs to \(\mathcal H\). Summing original probabilities over these disjoint orbits yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}
\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
e^{-\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral orbit twice before the factor \(1/2\), and uses half the available central mass. Thus no boundary atom or central entry is deleted. The equality uses (13) and the sign reversal of \(Y_J\); the last two steps are Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking the infimum over all containing ambient closed halfspaces proves (14) for \(\tau_P(J)\), without a full-dimensionality assumption.

Use the auxiliary product center law \(\widehat q:=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\). By (5) and (12),
\(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b(p_i))\widehat q_J\), and \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\). Squaring (14), summing over every original center with its original probability \(P_J\), and again applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b(p_i)\right)
\mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b(p_i)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
This is one actual product-halfspace estimate; no actual depth or moment factorization has been used.

Set \(s_{\log}:=\sum_i\log\mathcal A_i\ge0\). By (6), \(\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\le C_*s_{\log}\), while the ambient normalization gives \(\prod_i\widehat b(p_i)=4^{-N}e^{s_{\log}}\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14 4^{-N}
\exp\bigl(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C_*s_{\log}}\bigr)\\
&\ge\frac14e^{-C_*}4^{-N}=c_*4^{-N},
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
because \(s-2\sqrt{C_*s}=(\sqrt s-\sqrt{C_*})^2-C_*\ge-C_*\) for every \(s\ge0\). The coefficient is paid once for the whole mixed product. Under the same original probability law \(P\), Jensen for \(u\mapsto u^{3/2}\) gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P\bigl[(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\bigr]
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge c_*^{3/2}8^{-N}=\gamma8^{-N}.
\tag{17}
$$
Since \(0<c_*\le1/4\), one has \(\gamma=c_*^{3/2}\le c_*\). This proves both (8) and the common-coefficient assertion.

It remains to prove the three-value classification independently. Fix one factor and take any distinct same-parity endpoints \(u,v\). Their midpoint lies in the support, and (9) says that its log weight is the arithmetic mean of the two endpoint log weights. If the endpoint values are unequal, this mean lies strictly between them. With only one or two distinct log-weight values, such endpoints cannot occur. With three distinct values \(a<b<c\), neither an \((a,b)\) pair nor a \((b,c)\) pair has an available strictly intermediate value. Thus every unequal-weight same-parity pair must have endpoint log weights \(a,c\), and its midpoint must have log weight \(b=(a+c)/2\).

If the three values are not an arithmetic progression, there are no unequal-weight same-parity pairs, and every pair score in (B) is zero. If they are an arithmetic progression with gap \(g:=b-a=c-b>0\), every unequal-weight pair score is exactly \((c-a)/2=g\), while every equal-weight pair has score zero. If \(g\le T_*\), condition (B) holds. If \(g\ge T_*\), all distinct log-weight gaps are at least \(T_*\), so condition (S) holds. At equality either branch is available. The one- and two-value cases have all pair scores zero and satisfy (B). This proves the classification, including empty-pair and uniform cases. All applications and the reflection proof allow lower affine rank; a singleton has only its central row and zero score.

Finally, accepted fact c5f6533ba1c11991 defines \((\mathrm H_{3,N}(\kappa_N))\) by quantifying over every compact convex complete lattice support in \(\mathbb Z^N\) and every global affine weight field, with no parity-occupancy or spectral restriction. For the present supports, replacing \(Q_i\) by \(\operatorname{conv}A_i\) preserves completeness, so their products are compact complete supports of the kind considered there. However, (16)-(17) prove its cubic inequality only on the product class specified here. No reduction of every support in that universal hypothesis to these factors is supplied, and arbitrary multilevel spectra outside (B) and (S) are not addressed. Consequently this result alone does not satisfy the premise of that accepted general consumer and does not prove the locked target.
