## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted facts `036a276c54ec5e66`, `3100bf8082ca09d5`, and `d41b002f3091f5c4`. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
and the canonical hard inequality holds. In particular,
\[
 D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(y\in E_\perp\), let \(\tau_y>0\) and \(p_y\in\mathsf M_{\tau_y}(C_y)\) be the pooled endpoint weight and point of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5`. Put
\[
 T_E:=\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_y.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
 T_E>\frac{99}{100}Q,
 \qquad
 \tau_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{4}
\]
The endpoint measure
\[
 \nu_E:=\gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_\perp}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}
\tag{5}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\).

For every positive-fiber base \(y\), let
\[
 d_y^r:=\sum_{e\ni y}r_e.
\tag{6}
\]
This is its degree in the half-strength canonical product reservoir. It is zero outside the good parity classes, while in a good class \(\xi\),
\[
 d_y^r=\frac{a_y}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_y}{A_\xi}\right).
\tag{6a}
\]
Every endpoint belongs to a good parity class, so \(a_y/A_\xi\leq1/4\). Consequently,
\[
 \frac{3a_y}{16}\leq d_y^r\leq\frac{a_y}{4},
 \qquad
 \tau_y\leq d_y^r.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix any real number
\[
 0<c<\frac{99}{200},
\tag{8}
\]
and define the reservoir-degree-regular endpoint core
\[
 E_c:=\{y\in E_\perp:\tau_y\geq c d_y^r\},
 \qquad
 T_c:=\sum_{y\in E_c}\tau_y.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 T_c>\left(\frac{99}{100}-2c\right)Q,}
\tag{10}
\]
and every \(y\in E_c\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{3c}{16}a_y\leq\tau_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}.}
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 |E_c|>(396-800c)\,2^n.}
\tag{12}
\]

The restricted endpoint measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_c:=\gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_c}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}}
\tag{13}
\]
has mass \(\lvert\nu_c\rvert=\gamma_dT_c\), is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and is genuinely halfspace-dominated:
\[
 \boxed{\nu_c(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{14}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Define
\[
 A_c:=\operatorname{aff}E_c,
 \qquad
 \rho_c:=\dim A_c,
\tag{15}
\]
and
\[
 r_c:=
 \min_{a:A_c\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{p_y-a(y):y\in E_c\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_dT_c
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_c}}{r_c+1},}
\tag{17}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 r_c+1>
 (99-200c)\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_c},}
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 2^{\rho_c+r_c}>
 (99-200c)\gamma_d2^n.}
\tag{19}
\]

In particular, take \(c=1/20\) and write \(E_{1/20}\), \(T_{1/20}\), \(\rho_{1/20}\), and \(r_{1/20}\) for the corresponding objects. Then
\[
 \boxed{T_{1/20}>\frac{89}{100}Q,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{
 \frac{3}{320}a_y\leq\tau_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \quad(y\in E_{1/20}),}
\tag{20}
\]
\[
 \boxed{|E_{1/20}|>356\,2^n,}
\tag{21}
\]
so some parity class contains at least \(357\) elements of \(E_{1/20}\). Furthermore,
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{1/20}+1>
 89\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{1/20}},}
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{(y,p_y):y\in E_{1/20}\}
 =\rho_{1/20}+r_{1/20}\geq n+6.}
\tag{23}
\]

The conclusions do not bound \(\tau_u/\tau_v\) for distinct endpoints \(u,v\), do not force a same-parity affine basis to retain a fixed fraction of \(T_c\), and do not produce a balanced orientation of every reservoir edge. They prove neither an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality nor the locked final theorem.

## Proof

Accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` proves, for every \(y\in E_\perp\),
\[
 \tau_y\leq\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_e
 \leq\sum_{e\ni y}r_e=d_y^r.
\tag{24}
\]
Every reservoir edge belongs to one good parity class and contributes its weight to the degree of each of its two endpoints. Therefore
\[
 \sum_y d_y^r=2Q.
\tag{25}
\]
The exact formula in (6) and the good-class inequality \(a_y/A_\xi\leq1/4\) prove (7).

If \(y\in E_\perp\setminus E_c\), then \(\tau_y<c d_y^r\). Equations (3), (4), and (25) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_c
 &=T_E-\sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus E_c}\tau_y\\
 &>\frac{99}{100}Q
   -c\sum_{y\in E_\perp\setminus E_c}d_y^r\\
 &\geq\frac{99}{100}Q-2cQ.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
This proves (10). For \(y\in E_c\), equations (7) and (9) give
\[
 \tau_y\geq c d_y^r\geq\frac{3c}{16}a_y,
\]
while (7) gives the upper bound in (11).

The strict atom cap in (4) and (10) imply
\[
 \left(\frac{99}{100}-2c\right)Q
 <T_c
 <|E_c|\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{27}
\]
Cancelling \(Q>0\) proves (12).

The measure \(\nu_c\) is a positive submeasure of \(\nu_E\). Hence, for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\), accepted halfspace domination of \(\nu_E\) gives
\[
 \nu_c(H)\leq\nu_E(H)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{28}
\]
This proves (13)--(14).

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to \(\nu_c\). Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (15)--(16), and its mass is \(\gamma_dT_c\). Therefore
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_dT_c}{2^{\rho_c}(r_c+1)}.
\tag{29}
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) proves (17). Equations (2), (10), and (17) give
\[
 \frac Q{100}>D>
 \gamma_d\left(\frac{99}{100}-2c\right)Q
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_c}}{r_c+1},
\tag{30}
\]
which rearranges to (18). Since \(r_c+1\leq2^{r_c}\), equation (18) implies (19).

Set \(c=1/20\). Equations (10)--(12) immediately give (20)--(21). The strict support bound in (21), averaged over the \(2^n\) parity classes, gives the integer lower bound \(357\) in one class. Equation (18) gives (22).

Accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` records the elementary estimates
\[
 \gamma_d>e^{-1},
 \qquad
 e<\frac{11}{4},
\tag{31}
\]
and hence \(\gamma_d>4/11\). Using (19) at \(c=1/20\),
\[
 2^{\rho_{1/20}+r_{1/20}}
 >89\gamma_d2^n
 >\frac{356}{11}2^n
 >2^{n+5}.
\tag{32}
\]
The exponent on the left is an integer, so
\(\rho_{1/20}+r_{1/20}\geq n+6\). The intrinsic-rank identity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` identifies this sum with the affine dimension in (23). This completes the proof.
