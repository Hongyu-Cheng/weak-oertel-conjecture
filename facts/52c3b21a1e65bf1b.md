## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body in the common canonical hard setting of accepted facts c81f02314cb34f8b and f6188983e8850f01, and write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\pmod2}a_z
 \quad
 (\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n).
\]
Retain the half-strength canonical product reservoir \(r\): on a good parity class \(\xi\),
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \qquad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2),
\tag{1}
\]
and every edge in an empty or bad class has reservoir weight zero. Choose any load-minimal optimizer for the full reservoir and put
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 0\leq\omega_e\leq r_e.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(e=\{u,v\}\), define
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 \vartheta_e:=2^{-(d+1)}
 \bigl(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}\bigr)^d.
\tag{3}
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\) and physical target \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 \kappa_{\xi,m}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}:\ \omega_e>0,\ m_e=m\\
 u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2}}
 \frac{\omega_e}{\vartheta_e},
 \qquad
 \kappa_m:=\sum_{\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}
 \kappa_{\xi,m},
\tag{4}
\]
where an empty color-target cell contributes zero, and retain the exact canonical multiplier and charge
\[
 \chi_m:=\frac1{\max\{1,\kappa_m\}},
 \qquad
 \beta_e^{\mathrm{cong}}:=(1-\chi_{m_e})\omega_e.
\tag{5}
\]
Then, for every \(\xi\) and \(m\),
\[
 \boxed{\kappa_{\xi,m}\leq\frac14},
\tag{6}
\]
and therefore, for every \(m\),
\[
 \boxed{\kappa_m\leq2^{n-2}}.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, if \(n\leq2\), then
\[
 \boxed{\chi_m=1\quad(m\in\mathbb Z^n)},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\beta_e^{\mathrm{cong}}=0\quad(e)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(B_{\mathrm{cong}}:=\sum_e\beta_e^{\mathrm{cong}}=0\), the fresh total load \(\widetilde\Omega_{\mathrm{cong}}\) is zero under the zero-charge convention of accepted fact 637b5f7874d8c875, and the positive-fresh alternative \(\widetilde\Omega_{\mathrm{cong}}>0\) cannot occur for \(n\leq2\). For \(n\geq3\), assertions (6)-(7) are the only conclusions claimed here.

## Proof

Accepted fact c81f02314cb34f8b gives (1), including zero reservoir on every bad class. Accepted fact f6188983e8850f01 supplies (2)-(5) in the common canonical normalization. Fix \(\xi\) and \(m\).

If \(A_\xi=0\) or \(\xi\) is bad, then every reservoir edge in that class has weight zero. Equation (2) forces every corresponding \(\omega_e\) to be zero, so \(\kappa_{\xi,m}=0\) and (6) holds. It remains to consider a good class with \(A_\xi>0\).

For every edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) contributing to \(\kappa_{\xi,m}\), the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \vartheta_e
 =2^{-(d+1)}
 \bigl(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}\bigr)^d
 \geq\frac12\sqrt{a_ua_v}.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (9) yields
\[
 \frac{\omega_e}{\vartheta_e}
 \leq
 \frac{r_e}{\vartheta_e}
 \leq
 \frac{\sqrt{a_ua_v}}{2A_\xi}.
\tag{10}
\]

The edges in one fixed color-target cell are pairwise endpoint-disjoint. Indeed, if \(u\) is an endpoint of an edge with midpoint \(m\), then its other endpoint must be the unique point \(2m-u\). Hence every fiber mass \(a_z\) occurs in at most one summand below, and another application of the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}:\ \omega_e>0,\ m_e=m\\
 u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2}}
 \sqrt{a_ua_v}
 &\leq
 \frac12
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}:\ \omega_e>0,\ m_e=m\\
 u\equiv v\equiv\xi\pmod2}}
 (a_u+a_v)\\
 &\leq\frac{A_\xi}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Summing (10) and applying (11) proves
\[
 \kappa_{\xi,m}\leq
 \frac1{2A_\xi}\cdot\frac{A_\xi}{2}
 =\frac14,
\]
which is (6).

There are exactly \(2^n\) parity classes. Summing (6), with zero contributions from empty color-target cells, gives
\[
 \kappa_m\leq\frac{2^n}{4}=2^{n-2},
\]
proving (7). If \(n\leq2\), then \(2^{n-2}\leq1\), so (5) gives \(\chi_m=1\) at every nonempty target. Accepted fact f6188983e8850f01 assigns \(\chi_m=1\) at every empty target as well. Thus \(\beta_e^{\mathrm{cong}}=0\) edgewise and \(B_{\mathrm{cong}}=0\). Accepted fact 637b5f7874d8c875 extends the fresh total-load notation by \(\widetilde\Omega_{\mathrm{cong}}:=0\) in exactly this zero-charge case. This proves (8) and excludes the positive-fresh branch for \(n\leq2\). When \(n\geq3\), the upper bound \(2^{n-2}\) can exceed one, so no zero-charge conclusion follows from this argument.
