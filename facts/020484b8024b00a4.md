## Statement

For an integer \(k\ge1\), put \(N=k+1\), \(B_k=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), and \(Q=2^k\). Consider a finite nonempty support satisfying
$$
B_k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad \#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le2\quad(x\in D).
$$
For a field \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), use the original law on \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\): \(w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x)\), \(Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x\), and \(p_{(x,0)}=p_x=w_x/Z\). Define the actual core and double endpoints by
$$
D_1=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace\le1\rbrace,
\qquad D_2=D\setminus D_1,
\qquad \alpha=p(D_1),
\qquad S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4.
$$
Set \(t_0=2^{-10}Q^{-1}\) and \(t_j=t_0 2^{-j}\) for integers \(j\ge0\). The actual endpoint bands, dense-band indices, and midpoint stock are
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_j&=\lbrace x\in D_2:t_j/2<p_x\le t_j\rbrace,
&n_j&=|E_j|,
&m_j&=p(E_j),\\
\mathcal J&=\lbrace j\ge0:n_j\ge2Q\rbrace,
&C_j&=\left\lbrace\frac{x+y}{2}:x,y\in E_j,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\right\rbrace,
&r_j&=|C_j|,
\qquad \omega_j=t_jr_j.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(r_j\) always denotes a midpoint cardinality, not a coordinate activity. There is no absolute \(\eta>0\) such that all these laws with \(\alpha<1/4\) and \(S_4<2^{-40}8^{-k}\) satisfy
$$
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^2\right)^3
\ge\eta\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j\right)^2.
\tag{7}
$$

Explicitly, let the auxiliary integer \(d\ge12\) count free Boolean coordinates, put \(s=2^{2d}\), and let \(a=3s\) be the base dimension, so the canonical dimensions are \(k=a+d\) and \(N=k+1\). Partition the \(a\) base coordinates into disjoint triples \((h_\ell,u_\ell,v_\ell)\), \(1\le\ell\le s\). Let \(E\) consist of every base point whose two coordinates equal to two are either \(\lbrace h_\ell,u_\ell\rbrace\) or \(\lbrace h_\ell,v_\ell\rbrace\) for some \(\ell\), and whose other base coordinates are arbitrary binary entries. Define
$$
D_0=\operatorname{conv}(B_a\cup E)\cap\mathbb Z^a,
\qquad D=D_0\times B_d.
$$
Take \(\beta_i=0\) on base coordinates and \(\beta_i=-\log2\) on free coordinates. For all \(d\ge12\), this law satisfies the stated geometric hypotheses and both residual inequalities. All its double endpoints are retained, exactly \(d+1\) bands are occupied, and every occupied band is dense. Moreover,
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j\ge\frac{s}{8+5s}\ge\frac16,
\qquad
\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^2\right)^3}
{\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j\right)^2}
\le288d^{-3/2}.
$$
Consequently these actual band data refute (7) as \(d\to\infty\).

For calibration on precisely this original law, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_cs_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum is ordered, with its diagonal once. The same family obeys the stronger direct estimates
$$
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac{136}{81}\right)^d8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge8\left(\frac{136}{81}\right)^d8^{-N}.
$$
All these assertions are proved below without a predecessor dependency.

## Proof

First determine the actual convex completion. Let \(F\) consist of the base points having exactly one two, at \(h_\ell\) for some \(\ell\), with \(u_\ell=v_\ell=1\) and every other coordinate binary. We claim the exact disjoint census
$$
D_0=B_a\mathbin{\dot\cup}E\mathbin{\dot\cup}F,
\qquad |E|=s2^{a-1},
\qquad |F|=s2^{a-3},
\qquad |D_0|=2^a\left(1+\frac{5s}{8}\right).
$$
Every point of the generating set \(B_a\cup E\) is ternary and has at most two twos. An integral point of its convex hull is ternary. If a coordinate of such a point is two, every positive-coefficient generator in any convex representation must have that coordinate two, by extremality in \([0,2]\). Thus no integral point has three twos. If an integral point has two twos, every generator used has exactly that same pair of twos. Such generators exist only for one of the prescribed star edges, and their other coordinates are binary. At every other coordinate, integrality gives a binary value and extremality in \([0,1]\) forces each generator to have that value. Thus every double point is already in \(E\).

If an integral point has exactly one two at a leaf \(u_\ell\) or \(v_\ell\), every generator used also has \(h_\ell=2\), which is impossible for a point having only one two. Hence a single two must be at a center \(h_\ell\). The face of the generating convex hull with \(h_\ell=2\), after suppressing this fixed coordinate, is exactly
$$
\operatorname{conv}\lbrace(2,0),(2,1),(0,2),(1,2)\rbrace
\times[0,1]^{a-3},
$$
where the first two coordinates are \((u_\ell,v_\ell)\). Indeed the generators on that face have precisely those four leaf patterns and arbitrary binary remaining coordinates; the convex hull of their Cartesian product is the product of the two convex hulls. Every displayed leaf pattern has coordinate sum at least two. A Boolean leaf pair in this face must therefore be \((1,1)\). Conversely, \((1,1)\) is the midpoint of \((2,0)\) and \((0,2)\). Keeping arbitrary remaining binary entries identical in these two generators realizes every point of \(F\). Integral points without twos are already in \(B_a\). This proves the census. There are \(2s\) disjoint possible two-index pairs and \(2^{a-2}\) assignments for the other coordinates of each double point. For \(F\), there are \(s\) disjoint choices of center and \(2^{a-3}\) other assignments. These counts give the displayed cardinalities.

The generating set lies in \(D_0\), and \(D_0\) lies in its generating convex hull, so \(\operatorname{conv}(D_0)=\operatorname{conv}(B_a\cup E)\). Hence \(D_0\) is complete. For an integral point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_0\times B_d)\), its base projection lies in \(\operatorname{conv}(D_0)\cap\mathbb Z^a=D_0\), and its free projection lies in \([0,1]^d\cap\mathbb Z^d=B_d\). It therefore belongs to \(D\), proving completeness of the product. The census also proves \(B_k\subseteq D\), the max-two condition, and
$$
D_1=(B_a\cup F)\times B_d,
\qquad D_2=E\times B_d.
$$

Next define the full actual base midpoint image of the double endpoints by
$$
C_0=\left\lbrace\frac{x+y}{2}:x,y\in E,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\right\rbrace.
$$
For every point of \(F\), the two generators just used have local triple patterns \((2,2,0)\) and \((2,0,2)\), with identical remaining binary entries. They are distinct points of \(E\), have the same parity, and have the required midpoint. Thus \(F\subseteq C_0\). Completeness gives \(C_0\subseteq D_0\). A midpoint in \(C_0\) cannot have two twos: both endpoints would then use their two twos at the same two coordinates, and their remaining binary coordinates would agree by same parity, making the endpoints identical. Therefore
$$
F\subseteq C_0\subseteq B_a\cup F,
\qquad
\frac{s}{8+5s}\le\frac{|C_0|}{|D_0|}
\le\frac{8+s}{8+5s}.
$$
In particular \(|C_0|/|D_0|\to1/5\). No proxy for the actual midpoint image has been substituted here.

For \(x\in D_0\) and \(b\in B_d\), write \(|b|=\sum_i b_i\). The specified original field gives
$$
Z=|D_0|3^d,
\qquad p_{(x,b)}=\frac{2^{|b|}}{|D_0|3^d},
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{8+s}{8+5s},
\qquad
S_4=|D_0|^{-3}\left(\frac{17}{81}\right)^d.
$$
Here \(p_{(x,b)}\) abbreviates the probability of \(((x,b),0)\in A\), with the final ambient zero coordinate omitted. The normalizer follows from \(\sum_{b\in B_d}2^{|b|}=3^d\). The core mass follows from the census, and the fourth moment follows by summing \(2^{4|b|}\), whose sum is \(17^d\). Thus \(\alpha<1/4\) when \(s>24\). Using \(s=4^d\) gives
$$
8^kS_4
=\frac{(136/81)^d}{(1+5s/8)^3}
\le\frac{512}{125}\left(\frac{17}{648}\right)^d
<2^{3-5d}<2^{-40}\qquad(d\ge12).
$$
The last inequalities use \(512/125<8\) and \(17/648<1/32\). Also the largest double-endpoint probability satisfies
$$
Q\max_{x\in D_2}p_x
=\frac{(4/3)^d}{1+5s/8}
\le\frac85\,3^{-d}
<2^{1-d}\le2^{-11}<2^{-10}\qquad(d\ge12).
$$
Hence every double endpoint is retained below \(t_0\), and both residual conditions hold for every member of the stated family.

The possible endpoint probabilities are exactly
\(p_\ell=2^\ell/(|D_0|3^d)\), \(0\le\ell\le d\). Let \(j_*\ge0\) be the unique index with \(t_{j_*}/2<p_d\le t_{j_*}\); it exists because the half-open bands partition \((0,t_0]\). For \(0\le\ell\le d\), put \(j_\ell=j_*+d-\ell\). Since both the probabilities and the band boundaries change by factors of two,
$$
\frac{t_{j_\ell}}2<p_\ell\le t_{j_\ell},
\qquad
\frac{t_{j_\ell}}{p_\ell}
=\frac{t_{j_*}}{p_d}=:c_d\in[1,2).
$$
This explicitly retains the common dyadic phase, including the possibility of equality at an upper band boundary. Distinct ranks occupy distinct bands. Writing \(B_{d,\ell}=\lbrace b\in B_d:|b|=\ell\rbrace\), the complete list of nonempty bands is
$$
E_{j_\ell}=E\times B_{d,\ell},
\qquad n_{j_\ell}=|E|\binom d\ell,
\qquad 0\le\ell\le d.
$$
Each is dense, since
\(n_{j_\ell}\ge s2^{a-1}\ge2^{a+d+1}=2Q\), where the middle inequality is equivalent to \(2^{2d}\ge2^{d+2}\) and holds for \(d\ge2\). Every other band is empty. Hence \(\mathcal J=\lbrace j_\ell:0\le\ell\le d\rbrace\).

For two binary free vectors, same parity forces equality coordinate by coordinate. Therefore two distinct same-parity endpoints in \(E\times B_{d,\ell}\) have the same free vector and distinct same-parity base endpoints in \(E\). Conversely, every such base pair can be combined with every free vector in \(B_{d,\ell}\). This proves the exact full midpoint images and stocks
$$
C_{j_\ell}=C_0\times B_{d,\ell},
\qquad r_{j_\ell}=|C_0|\binom d\ell.
$$
Define \(\pi_\ell=\binom d\ell2^\ell/3^d\), so \(\sum_{\ell=0}^d\pi_\ell=1\). Then the actual band masses and stocks are exactly
$$
m_{j_\ell}=\frac{|E|}{|D_0|}\pi_\ell,
\qquad
\omega_{j_\ell}=c_d\frac{|C_0|}{|D_0|}\pi_\ell.
$$
In particular, \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j=c_d|C_0|/|D_0|\ge s/(8+5s)\ge1/6\), since \(s\ge8\).

We prove the required decay of the squared binomial masses directly. By the binomial theorem, \(\sum_{\ell=0}^d\pi_\ell e^{i\ell t}=((1+2e^{it})/3)^d\). Expanding its squared modulus and integrating each exponential over \([-\pi,\pi]\) leaves exactly the terms with equal indices. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\ell=0}^d\pi_\ell^2
&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\left(1-\frac89\sin^2(t/2)\right)^d\,dt\\
&\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\exp\left(-\frac{8d}{9\pi^2}t^2\right)\,dt
\le\frac{2}{\sqrt d}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the first inequality, concavity of sine on \([0,\pi/2]\) gives \(\sin(|t|/2)\ge|t|/\pi\), and \(1-u\le e^{-u}\) applies to the nonnegative integrand. To verify the last bound without any asymptotic formula, substitute \(u=\sqrt{8d/(9\pi^2)}\,t\) and enlarge the interval to the real line. The integral of \(e^{-u^2}\) over \([-1,1]\) is at most two, and its two tails have total at most \(2\int_1^\infty e^{-u}\,du=2/e<1\). The resulting bound is \(9/(4\sqrt{2d})<2/\sqrt d\).

Since \(|E|/|D_0|\le1\), the exact mass formula and the stock lower bound yield
$$
\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^2\right)^3}
{\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\omega_j\right)^2}
\le36\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^d\pi_\ell^2\right)^3
\le288d^{-3/2}\longrightarrow0.
$$
The denominator is positive for every \(d\ge12\). Given any \(\eta>0\), an integer \(d\ge12\) large enough that \(288d^{-3/2}<\eta\) supplies a law satisfying all the hypotheses and violating (7).

We next calibrate the actual cubics. Put \(\nu_x=1/|D_0|\) on the base support and \(\mu_b=2^{|b|}/3^d\) on the free Boolean support, so exactly \(p_{(x,b)}=\nu_x\mu_b\). Define the base ordered-reflection sum and its cubic by
$$
s_\nu(c)=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D_0\\x+y=2c}}\min(\nu_x,\nu_y),
\qquad T_0=\sum_{c\in D_0}\nu_cs_\nu(c)^3.
$$
Every same-parity pair of base points, including the diagonal, has an integral midpoint in \(D_0\), by completeness. Conversely every ordered reflected pair is same-parity. Uniformity therefore gives
$$
\sum_{c\in D_0}\nu_cs_\nu(c)
=\frac{\#\lbrace(x,y)\in D_0^2:x\equiv y\pmod2\rbrace}{|D_0|^2}
\ge2^{-a}.
$$
Indeed, if the cardinalities of the \(2^a\) parity classes are \(n_b\), Cauchy's inequality gives \(\sum_bn_b^2\ge(\sum_bn_b)^2/2^a=|D_0|^2/2^a\). Jensen's inequality for the probability weights \(\nu_c\) now gives \(T_0\ge8^{-a}\).

At an original center \((c,b)\in D\), a reflected pair has free endpoints \(b',b''\in B_d\) with \(b'+b''=2b\). Binary extremality forces \(b'=b''=b\). Thus the full original ordered-reflection sum, with no selected-pair restriction, satisfies
$$
s_p((c,b))=\mu_b s_\nu(c),
\qquad
T(p)=T_0\sum_{b\in B_d}\mu_b^4
=T_0\left(\frac{17}{81}\right)^d
\ge8^{-k}\left(\frac{136}{81}\right)^d.
$$
Finally, for each original center, distinct unordered non-diagonal reflected pairs have disjoint endpoints, none equal to the center. Every containing closed halfspace contains an endpoint of each such pair: if both endpoints were strictly outside its defining closed inequality, their midpoint would also be outside. The mass of that endpoint is at least the minimum of the two endpoint masses. Adding the center atom proves \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge(p_c+s_p(c))/2\ge s_p(c)/2\), with \(c\in D\) here a full base-and-free vector. Cubing and summing gives \(M_3(p)\ge T(p)/8\). This proves the two stated cubic estimates, since \(N=k+1\). It also covers containing halfspaces whose boundary contains the center. No affine-rank assumption or changed law has entered.

For a more precise calibration of the band inventory, let \(q_j\) be the number of parity classes actually occupied by \(E_j\), for \(j\in\mathcal J\). A base parity occurs in \(E\) exactly when some triple has \(h_\ell=0\) and at least one of \(u_\ell,v_\ell\) equal to zero. There are five Boolean patterns on one triple avoiding this condition: four with \(h_\ell=1\), and one with \(h_\ell=0,u_\ell=v_\ell=1\). The independence of the disjoint triples therefore gives the exact endpoint parity count \(q_E=2^a-5^s>0\). Every base parity just described is realized by setting one permissible edge to two and all other coordinates to its binary parity values. Free binary vectors represent distinct parities, so
$$
q_{j_\ell}=(2^a-5^s)\binom d\ell.
$$
For the purely numerical quantity formed from these actual band data,
$$
\Psi=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{m_j^6}{q_j^3\omega_j^2},
$$
the preceding exact formulas give
$$
Q^3\Psi=
\frac{(|E|/|D_0|)^6}{c_d^2(|C_0|/|D_0|)^2}
\left(\frac{2^a}{2^a-5^s}\right)^3
\left(\frac{136}{81}\right)^d.
$$
To check the identity, the summand at rank \(\ell\), after extracting the factors independent of \(\ell\), is \(\pi_\ell^4/\binom d\ell^3=\binom d\ell16^\ell/81^d\). Its sum is \((17/81)^d\), and multiplication by \(Q^3=8^{a+d}\) gives the displayed expression. This exhibits in the actual parity counts the same exponential factor as the direct original cubic bound, whereas the ratio in (7) tends to zero.
