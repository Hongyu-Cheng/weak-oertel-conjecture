## Statement

Let $n\ge0$ and $N\ge1$, let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite, and let $D_j\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^D$ be compact convex sets contained in one fixed compact set. All nonempty integer fibers of $D_j$ have bases in $P$. At step $j$ cut by one affine level $A_j(z,x)=s_j$ whose continuous gradient is nonzero. Assume the resulting fibers lie in parallel $N$-dimensional affine planes, with the same Euclidean $N$-volume convention. Let $\mu_{j,z}$ be $\alpha_j>0$ times the $N$-volume restricted to the fiber of this cut at $z$, where $\alpha_j$ is independent of $z$. Assume simultaneous finite weak limits $\mu_{j,z}\to\mu_z$ at every $z\in P$. Put $P_+=\{z\in P:\mu_z\ne0\}$ and assume it is nonempty.

Whenever $z_i\in P_+$ and $\theta_i>0$ have $\sum_i\theta_i=1$ and $z=\sum_i\theta_i z_i$ is integer, $z\in P_+$ and for all compact sets $E_i$ with $\mu_{z_i}(E_i)>0$,
$$
\mu_z\!\left(\sum_i\theta_i E_i\right)^{1/N}
\ \ge\ \sum_i\theta_i\mu_{z_i}(E_i)^{1/N}.
$$
In particular $P_+=\operatorname{conv}(P_+)\cap\mathbb Z^n$.

## Proof

Extend the prelimit and limit measures by zero at integer bases outside $P$. At each $j$, any weighted Minkowski sum of compact subsets of the input slices lies in the output slice by convexity of $D_j$ and affinity of $A_j$. Translate the parallel planes to their common Euclidean direction space. Ordinary $N$-dimensional Brunn–Minkowski and the common multiplier $\alpha_j$ then give the desired inequality for these compact portions with positive prelimit masses.

Fix compact $E_i$ of positive limit mass and write $E=\sum_i\theta_i E_i$. For $\varepsilon>0$ let $U_i$ be the open $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of $E_i$. The Minkowski combination of the $U_i$ lies in the closed $\varepsilon$-neighborhood $E_\varepsilon$ of $E$. Portmanteau gives $\liminf_j\mu_{j,z_i}(U_i)\ge\mu_{z_i}(E_i)>0$. For large $j$, use compact subsets of each $U_i$ intersected with its input slice and approximate its mass by compact inner regularity. The prelimit inequality then gives $\mu_{j,z}(E_\varepsilon)^{1/N}\ge\sum_i\theta_i\mu_{j,z_i}(U_i)^{1/N}$. The upper Portmanteau bound for the closed set $E_\varepsilon$ gives the same inequality with $\mu_z(E_\varepsilon)$ on the left and the $\mu_{z_i}(E_i)$ on the right. Send $\varepsilon$ to zero and use continuity from above for the finite output measure.

The right side is positive, so the integer output belongs to $P_+$. Any integer point of its convex hull has a finite representation of the stated form after zero coefficients are discarded, which proves the census.
