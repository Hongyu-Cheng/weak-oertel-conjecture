## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Fix an integer \(u\ge1\). For every integer \(T\ge1\), let \(E_T:=\{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{u}:\sum_{i=1}^{u}n_i=T\}\). For every real vector \(\mathbf y\ge\mathbf0\) with \(\sum_i y_i=T\), let \(\nu_{T,\mathbf y}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(E_T\) having mean \(\mathbf y\), and define \(H_T(\mathbf y):=H(\nu_{T,\mathbf y})\), where \(H(\nu):=-\sum_{\mathbf n:\nu(\mathbf n)>0}\nu(\mathbf n)\log\nu(\mathbf n)\). The existence and uniqueness of these laws are established below. For integer \(\mathbf j\in E_T\), the exact alias to the maximum-entropy notation of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 is \(\nu_{T,\mathbf j}=r_{E_T,\mathbf j}\).

For every integer \(T\ge1\), every \(\mathbf j\in E_T\), and every \(f\in\{1,\ldots,u\}\),
\[
0\le H_{T+1}(\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f)-H_T(\mathbf j)\le\log16.
\]
Here \(\mathbf e_f\) is the \(f\)-th coordinate unit vector.

Let \(\mathbf q=(q_1,\ldots,q_u)\) have positive entries and \(\max_iq_i=1\). Write \(\mathbf q^{\mathbf n}:=\prod_iq_i^{n_i}\), and define
\[
h_T(\mathbf q):=\sum_{\mathbf n\in E_T}\mathbf q^{\mathbf n},
\qquad
p_{T,\mathbf q}(\mathbf n):=\frac{\mathbf q^{\mathbf n}}{h_T(\mathbf q)},
\qquad
F_T(\mathbf q):=\sum_{\mathbf j\in E_T}\mathbf q^{3\mathbf j}e^{2H_T(\mathbf j)}.
\]
The partition sum \(h_T\) has exactly the normalization of accepted facts 128175cd66587a67 and 55b5ec7cf9276f99: there are no multinomial coefficients. It is not the halfspace-depth function \(h_S\) in the locked problem. The \(q_i\) are source count weights, not face-conditioning probabilities. Independently chosen entropy-row count weights will be denoted by \(w_i\) or \(v_i\); these have the same count-weight meaning as the composition weights in those accepted facts.

With \(A=E_T\), ambient dimension \(m=u\), and \(p=p_{T,\mathbf q}\) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, its entropy capacity and capacity second moment satisfy
\[
\mathsf C_{p_{T,\mathbf q}}(\mathbf j)
=p_{T,\mathbf q}(\mathbf j)e^{H_T(\mathbf j)},
\qquad
F_T(\mathbf q)=h_T(\mathbf q)^3\mathcal B_2(p_{T,\mathbf q}).
\]
Thus \(F_T\) is the unnormalized capacity-weighted numerator, not the source partition sum or source root law. For all such \(\mathbf q\) and all integers \(T\ge1\),
\[
1\le\frac{F_{T+1}(\mathbf q)}{F_T(\mathbf q)}\le256u.
\]

## Proof

First specify the composition-coordinate aliases, including on proper faces. For any integer total \(M\ge1\), the convex hull of \(E_M\) is the simplex \(\{\mathbf y\ge\mathbf0:\sum_i y_i=M\}\), whose vertices are \(M\mathbf e_i\). At a mean \(\mathbf y\), let \(I:=\{i:y_i>0\}\), and let \(E_{M,I}:=\{\mathbf n\in E_M:n_i=0\text{ for }i\notin I\}\). Every law of mean \(\mathbf y\) is supported on \(E_{M,I}\), since a nonnegative coordinate of mean zero vanishes almost surely. This is exactly the support of the minimal face containing \(\mathbf y\).

If \(|I|\ge2\), choose any \(s_0\in I\), delete the count \(N_{s_0}\), and list the other active counts as \(\mathbf X=(N_i)_{i\in I\setminus\{s_0\}}\). This is an affine bijection from \(E_{M,I}\) to \(D_{m,r}\) of accepted fact 81ad175d3c410508 with \(m=M\) and \(r=|I|-1\). Its slack is precisely \(N_{s_0}=M-\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{s_0\}}X_i\); its mean coordinates are the corresponding entries of \(\mathbf y\), with slack mean \(y_{s_0}\). The law \(\nu_{\mathbf y}\) and profile \(H(\mathbf y)\) in the real-mean portion of that accepted proof push forward to \(\nu_{M,\mathbf y}\) and \(H_M(\mathbf y)\) here. Bijections preserve entropy and mean constraints. If \(|I|=1\), the only feasible law is a point mass and its entropy is zero. These maps justify existence and uniqueness for every real mean and smoothness on each face interior. For continuity on the entire closed composition simplex when \(u\ge2\), use the same bijection with the full coordinate set and any fixed slack: the boundary continuity in that accepted proof then applies to all means. For \(u=1\), the profile is identically zero.

In particular, a row with positive means on \(I\) has positive count weights \((w_i)_{i\in I}\) and mass \(\prod_{i\in I}w_i^{n_i}/h_M((w_i)_{i\in I})\) on \(E_{M,I}\). The exact parameter map to the preceding chart of 81ad175d3c410508 is \(\lambda_i=\log(w_i/w_{s_0})\). Multiplying every \(w_i\) by a common positive number leaves the law unchanged. The profile differentiation formula in that accepted proof consequently gives, along any smooth mean curve in this face interior,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}H_M(\mathbf y(t))
=-\sum_{i\in I}\log w_i(t)\,\dot y_i(t).
\]
The formula is independent of the common weight gauge because \(\sum_{i\in I}\dot y_i(t)=0\). In particular, it remains valid after normalizing \(\max_{i\in I}w_i(t)=1\), without requiring the maximizing index to remain fixed.

Now fix \(T,\mathbf j,f\) as in the statement and put \(\mathbf l:=\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f\). Translating \(\nu_{T,\mathbf j}\) by \(\mathbf e_f\) gives a law on \(E_{T+1}\) of mean \(\mathbf l\) with exactly the same entropy. Maximality proves \(H_{T+1}(\mathbf l)\ge H_T(\mathbf j)\).

For the upper bound, take \(I:=\{i:l_i>0\}\) and write \(s:=|I|\). Since \(l_i\) are positive integers on \(I\), \(s\le T+1\). Also \(f\in I\). If \(s=1\), then \(\mathbf j=T\mathbf e_f\) and both entropy rows are point masses, so the bound holds. Henceforth \(s\ge2\).

Represent \(\nu_{T+1,\mathbf l}\) using positive weights \((w_i)_{i\in I}\), normalized by \(\max_iw_i=1\). To apply accepted fact 128175cd66587a67, its total \(m\) is \(T+1\), its simplex rank \(r\) is \(s-1\), and its non-slack coordinates are the counts after deleting an index of maximal \(w_i\); its augmented counts and integer mean are the active counts and \(\mathbf l_I\). Its normalized count weights \(q_i\) are these \(w_i\), and \(Z_{\mathrm{comp}}=h_{T+1}((w_i)_{i\in I})\). Its integer-mean unit-transfer estimate gives
\[
0\le-\log w_i\le\log(1+1/l_i)\le\log2
\qquad(i\in I).
\]
The maximal-weight coordinate itself has \(-\log w_i=0\).

Use exactly the same tuple \((w_i)_{i\in I}\) to define a complete-composition law of total \(T\) on \(E_{T,I}\), and denote its full mean by \(\boldsymbol\mu\), with \(\mu_i=0\) off \(I\). Every \(\mu_i\) on \(I\) is positive: the positive law gives positive mass to the vertex \(T\mathbf e_i\). Every law with this mean is supported on \(E_{T,I}\). By the real-mean converse proved in 81ad175d3c410508, a positive exponential law is maximum entropy at its own mean: its affine log density has the same expectation under every mean-matched law, so nonnegativity of relative entropy proves maximality. Thus the same-weight law is \(\nu_{T,\boldsymbol\mu}\). No integer-mean theorem is being applied at the possibly noninteger mean \(\boldsymbol\mu\).

The neighboring-total coordinate monotonicity proved in 55b5ec7cf9276f99 applies to any positive count-weight tuple. Its exact aliases are its tuple \(\mathbf q=(w_i)_{i\in I}\), tuple length \(u=s\), total \(t=T\), and means \(\mu_i^{(T)}=\mu_i\), \(\mu_i^{(T+1)}=l_i\). Consequently, with \(\Delta_i:=l_i-\mu_i\) on \(I\),
\[
\Delta_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i\in I}\Delta_i=1.
\]
The exact unit-count identity in the same accepted proof, using \(\max_iw_i=1\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(T+1)h_{T+1}((w_i)_{i\in I})
&=\sum_{\mathbf n\in E_{T,I}}
\left(\prod_{i\in I}w_i^{n_i}\right)
\sum_{i\in I}w_i(n_i+1)\\
&\le(T+s)h_T((w_i)_{i\in I}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(h_{T+1}((w_i)_{i\in I})/h_T((w_i)_{i\in I})\le(T+s)/(T+1)\le2\). The entropy formula for these two laws with identical weights now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{T+1}(\mathbf l)-H_T(\boldsymbol\mu)
&=\log\frac{h_{T+1}((w_i)_{i\in I})}{h_T((w_i)_{i\in I})}
-\sum_{i\in I}(\log w_i)\Delta_i\\
&\le\log2+(\log2)\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_i
=2\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to compare the real mean \(\boldsymbol\mu\) with \(\mathbf j\) at the fixed total \(T\). Put \(\delta:=\mu_f-j_f=1-\Delta_f\). Then \(0\le\delta\le1\), while \(j_i-\mu_i=\Delta_i\ge0\) for \(i\ne f\) in \(I\), and \(\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{f\}}\Delta_i=\delta\). If \(\delta=0\), all these differences vanish and \(\boldsymbol\mu=\mathbf j\). Otherwise follow the segment \(\mathbf y(t):=\boldsymbol\mu+t(\mathbf j-\boldsymbol\mu)\), \(0\le t\le1\). Every coordinate in \(I\) remains positive for \(t<1\). At \(t=1\), only coordinate \(f\) can become zero, since \(j_i=l_i\ge1\) for every other \(i\in I\). Coordinates outside \(I\) remain zero throughout.

For \(t<1\), normalize the instantaneous row weights \((v_i(t))_{i\in I}\) by their maximum, so every \(\log v_i(t)\le0\). The real-mean transfer comparison proved in 81ad175d3c410508 gives
\(v_f(t)\ge y_f(t)/(y_f(t)+1)\). This comparison requires only a positive real mean: if \(v_f(t)<1\), transferring \(r\) units from count \(f\) to a maximal-weight count bounds its survival probability by \(v_f(t)^r\), and summing gives \(y_f(t)\le v_f(t)/(1-v_f(t))\); if \(v_f(t)=1\), the comparison is immediate. Hence the derivative formula and the signs of the coordinate motions imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}H_T(\mathbf y(t))
&=-\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{f\}}\log v_i(t)\,\Delta_i
+\delta\log v_f(t)\\
&\ge-\delta\log(1+1/y_f(t)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrate first over \(0\le t\le a<1\), then let \(a\uparrow1\), using the boundary continuity established in 81ad175d3c410508. Since \(y_f(t)=\mu_f-\delta t\), this proves
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_T(\boldsymbol\mu)-H_T(\mathbf j)
&\le\int_{j_f}^{\mu_f}\log(1+1/x)\,dx\\
&\le\int_0^1\log(1+1/x)\,dx
=2\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second inequality uses \(j_f\ge0\), \(\mu_f-j_f=\delta\le1\), and the positivity and decrease of the integrand. The improper integral at zero is finite: an antiderivative is \((x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log x\), with \(x\log x\to0\) as \(x\downarrow0\). Combining the two entropy comparisons gives \(H_{T+1}(\mathbf l)-H_T(\mathbf j)\le4\log2=\log16\). Together with the translation argument, this proves the entropy assertion on every face.

For the capacity interpretation, any probability law \(\rho\) on \(E_T\) with mean \(\mathbf j\in E_T\) satisfies
\[
D(\rho\|p_{T,\mathbf q})
=-H(\rho)-\sum_i j_i\log q_i+\log h_T(\mathbf q)
=-H(\rho)-\log p_{T,\mathbf q}(\mathbf j).
\]
The entropy-capacity identity of bf1ff94487296374 therefore gives \(\mathsf C_{p_{T,\mathbf q}}(\mathbf j)=p_{T,\mathbf q}(\mathbf j)e^{H_T(\mathbf j)}\), also on boundary faces. Substitution into its definition of \(\mathcal B_2\) proves \(F_T=h_T^3\mathcal B_2(p_{T,\mathbf q})\). Every summand of \(F_T\) is positive, so \(F_T>0\).

Finally choose \(f_\star\) with \(q_{f_\star}=1\). The injection \(\mathbf j\mapsto\mathbf j+\mathbf e_{f_\star}\) and the lower entropy bound show that each corresponding summand of \(F_{T+1}\) is at least its summand of \(F_T\), proving \(F_{T+1}\ge F_T\).

For the upper bound, assign to each \(\mathbf l\in E_{T+1}\) any one coordinate \(f\) with \(l_f>0\), and put \(\mathbf j=\mathbf l-\mathbf e_f\in E_T\). The upper entropy bound and \(q_f\le1\) give
\[
\mathbf q^{3\mathbf l}e^{2H_{T+1}(\mathbf l)}
=q_f^3\mathbf q^{3\mathbf j}e^{2H_{T+1}(\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f)}
\le256\,\mathbf q^{3\mathbf j}e^{2H_T(\mathbf j)}.
\]
Each old root \(\mathbf j\) can receive at most \(u\) new roots, because every received root equals \(\mathbf j+\mathbf e_f\) for one of the \(u\) coordinates. Summing proves \(F_{T+1}\le256uF_T\), and division by \(F_T>0\) completes the claimed ratio bounds.
