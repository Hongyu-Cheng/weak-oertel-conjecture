## Statement

For every integer $m\ge1$ put $D_m=[-1,1/(m+1)]\times[-1,1]$ and $f_m(x,y)=(1+x)^m\mathbf1_{D_m}(x,y)$. The density has affine $m$th root on its positive support, full centroid zero, and both coordinate-line sections centered at zero. The planar Ball body with $\rho(u)^2=2\int_0^\infty r f_m(ru)\,dr$ is convex, has centered coordinate chords, and preserves every halfplane mass fraction through zero. Its full centroid has strictly positive first coordinate and second coordinate zero.

## Proof

Write $L=(m+2)/(m+1)$. Substitution $u=1+x$ gives the horizontal first moment $L^{m+2}/(m+2)-L^{m+1}/(m+1)=0$. The vertical section is uniform on $[-1,1]$, and the full mean is zero by the product form of the density.

Put $A(x)=2\int_0^1 t(1+tx)^m\,dt$ and $s(x)=\sqrt{A(x)}$. Integrating the derivative of $t^2(1+tx)^m$ on $[0,1]$ gives $A+xA'/2=(1+x)^m$. Thus the map $T(x,y)=s(x)(x,y)$ has Jacobian $(1+x)^m$. On each ray its radial derivative is $(1+x)^m/s(x)>0$ in the interior. At a unit ray endpoint $Ru$, substituting $r=Rt$ in the radial integral shows the prescribed radius is $R s(Ru_x)$. Consequently $T$ bijects interiors and sends $f_m\,dx\,dy$ to area on the radial body. Its ray preservation proves all the asserted through-origin halfplane fractions. Along each coordinate axis the signed first moment is zero, so the two integrals $\int_0^\infty r f_m(\pm ru)\,dr$ are equal. This gives centered chords.

For convexity, $X=xs(x)$ is strictly increasing in the interior with a continuous inverse up to the endpoints. The image is bounded by the graphs $\pm S(X)$, where $S(X)=s(x(X))$. Its derivative is $A'(x)/(2(1+x)^m)$. For $0<t<1$ and $x>-1$, the logarithmic derivative of $(1+tx)^{m-1}/(1+x)^m$ is
$$
\frac{(m-1)t}{1+tx}-\frac{m}{1+x}
=\frac{t(m-1-x)-m}{(1+tx)(1+x)}<0.
$$
Using the integral formula for $A'$, this proves $S'$ is decreasing. Continuity includes the boundary of the image, so it is convex.

Under the probability density proportional to $f_m$, write $X_0$ for the original first coordinate. The transformed first moment is $\mathbb E[X_0s(X_0)]=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,s(X_0))$. It is strictly positive because $s$ is strictly increasing and the covariance equals half the expectation of $(X_0-X_0')(s(X_0)-s(X_0'))$ for independent copies. Reflection in the second coordinate preserves the image, hence its second mean is zero.
