## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_5\}$ have vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_5$ and centroid $g$. For $0<\delta<1/5$ let $Q_i=v_i+\delta(T-g)$ and let $\mu$ be the sum of the six Lebesgue measures restricted to $Q_i$. Maximizing normalized ordinary closed-halfspace depth over all of $\mathbb R^5$ gives exactly $1/6$, attained at $g$. In particular this is strictly below $\Gamma_5/2$ with $\Gamma_5=(5/6)^5$ and difference $533/15552$.

## Proof

For each barycentric coordinate $\lambda_j$ of $T$ and every $x\in Q_i$ we have $\lambda_j(x)=\mathbf1_{i=j}+\delta(\lambda_j(t)-1/6)$ for some $t\in T$. Thus $\lambda_i(x)\ge1-\delta/6$ and $\lambda_j(x)\le5\delta/6$ for $j\ne i$. These bounds make the six clusters disjoint. Since $5\delta/6<1/6$, the six closed caps $\{\lambda_i\ge1/6\}$ each capture exactly one cluster. They cover all ambient points because $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. The six clusters have equal positive mass, so every point has depth at most $1/6$.

For a nonzero linear margin through $g$, let $h_i$ be its values at the vertices. Their sum is zero. They cannot all vanish because the vertices are affinely independent, so their maximum $m$ is positive and their minimum is at least $-5m$. For a cluster whose vertex attains $m$, the margin of each of its points is at least $m-5\delta m>0$, by linearity on $T-g$. Every through-$g$ closed halfspace therefore contains one whole cluster. A containing halfspace contains its parallel through-$g$ halfspace. This gives depth at least $1/6$ at $g$ and proves equality. Direct subtraction yields $(5/6)^5/2-1/6=533/15552$.
