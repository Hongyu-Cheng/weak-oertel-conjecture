## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^N\). Use the canonical probability and depth normalization
\[
p_{\mathbf x}:=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}}{\sum_{\mathbf y\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf y}},
\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf z):=\min_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace in }\mathbb R^N\\\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}
\quad(\mathbf z\in D).
\]
A halfspace has a nonzero normal. For any positive probability law on a finite subset of \(\mathbb R^N\), its depth is defined by the same formula.

For \(\mathbf z\in D\), define \(R_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\}\), \(k_{\mathbf z}:=|R_{\mathbf z}|\), and \(B_{\mathbf z}:=\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}p_{\mathbf x}\). Let \(r_{\mathbf z}\) be the conditional probability law on \(R_{\mathbf z}\), namely \(r_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf x):=p_{\mathbf x}/B_{\mathbf z}\). For an arbitrary closed halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf z\), write \(E_{\mathbf z,H}:=R_{\mathbf z}\cap H\). For a chosen minimizing cap \(H_{\mathbf z}\), this notation gives exactly the canonical reflected cap \(E_{\mathbf z,H_{\mathbf z}}=E_{\mathbf z}\).

Every such ambient halfspace satisfies
\[
|E_{\mathbf z,H}|\ge\frac{k_{\mathbf z}+1}{2},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}
\ge\frac{|E_{\mathbf z,H}|^2p_{\mathbf z}^2}{B_{\mathbf z}}
\ge\frac{(k_{\mathbf z}+1)^2p_{\mathbf z}^2}{4B_{\mathbf z}}.
\tag{1}
\]
In addition, \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\ge B_{\mathbf z}\tau_{r_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf z)\). If \(R_{\mathbf z}\) is an exposed face of the finite support, in the precise sense that some \(\mathbf c\in\mathbb R^N\) satisfies
\[
\mathbf c\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)=0\quad(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}),
\qquad
\mathbf c\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)<0\quad(\mathbf x\in D\setminus R_{\mathbf z}),
\tag{2}
\]
then \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)=B_{\mathbf z}\tau_{r_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf z)\). The case \(R_{\mathbf z}=D\) is allowed in (2).

In particular, if \(D\) is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf z\in D\), then \(B_{\mathbf z}=1\) and
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
\ge\frac{(|D|+1)^2p_{\mathbf z}^2}{4}
=\frac{(|D|+1)^2}{4\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)}\right)^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
This finite centrally symmetric support statement requires neither a lattice hypothesis nor full dimensionality.

Now specialize to \(D=\{-1,0,1\}^N\). The product parameter \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is an alias of \(\boldsymbol\ell\), and
\(Z_j:=1+e^{\theta_j}+e^{-\theta_j}\), \(p_j(s):=e^{\theta_js}/Z_j\) for \(s\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and \(p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf x):=\prod_{j=1}^Np_j(x_j)\). Thus \(p=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}\) in the preceding normalization.

For any \(\mathbf z\in D\), put \(I:=\{i:z_i=0\}\), \(m:=|I|\), and \(\beta_{\mathbf z}:=\prod_{j\notin I}p_j(z_j)\). Define the conditional product law on \(\{-1,0,1\}^I\) by
\(p^I_{\boldsymbol\theta}(\mathbf y):=e^{\sum_{i\in I}\theta_i y_i}/\prod_{i\in I}Z_i\). If \(I=\varnothing\), this is the unit mass on the single empty tuple, and its depth at that tuple is defined to be \(1\). Empty products are \(1\). Then
\[
\tau_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf z)
=\beta_{\mathbf z}\tau_{p^I_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(\mathbf0)
\ge\frac{\beta_{\mathbf z}(3^m+1)^2}{4\prod_{i\in I}Z_i^2}
\ge\frac{\beta_{\mathbf z}3^{2m}}{4\prod_{i\in I}Z_i^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
All assertions hold for arbitrary signs of the tilt coordinates, zero normal components, and all equality atoms of closed halfspaces.

## Proof

The minima defining depth exist: a finite support has only finitely many subsets, so the nonempty collection of subsets realized by admissible halfspaces has a minimum mass.

Fix \(\mathbf z\in D\). Reflection \(\mathbf x\mapsto2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\) permutes \(R_{\mathbf z}\), fixes only \(\mathbf z\), and pairs all its other points. Thus \(k_{\mathbf z}\) is odd. Write an arbitrary containing halfspace as \(H=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge s\}\), where \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\) and \(s\le0\). Its through-root subcap \(H_0:=\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\}\) is contained in \(H\).

Let \(b:=|\{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)=0\}|\). Each reflected pair outside that hyperplane contributes exactly one point to \(R_{\mathbf z}\cap H_0\). Each pair on the hyperplane contributes both points, and the center contributes once. Therefore
\[
|R_{\mathbf z}\cap H_0|=\frac{k_{\mathbf z}+b}{2},
\qquad
|E_{\mathbf z,H}|\ge\frac{k_{\mathbf z}+b}{2}\ge\frac{k_{\mathbf z}+1}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
For a through-root cap with no reflected-support boundary atom other than \(\mathbf z\), the count is exactly \((k_{\mathbf z}+1)/2\). For a nongeneric through-root cap, its extra boundary points are retained and increase the count according to (5). If the normal vanishes on every difference \(\mathbf x-\mathbf z\) with \(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\), then \(b=k_{\mathbf z}\), and the restriction contains the entire reflected support. For a containing halfspace whose boundary does not pass through the root, (5) uses the through-root subcap; no symmetry of its original boundary atoms is assumed. This treats every containing halfspace, not just a selected minimizing cap.

The log-affine normalization gives the exact reflection identity \(p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf z}^2\) for every \(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\). Put \(E:=E_{\mathbf z,H}\). Cauchy--Schwarz and injectivity of reflection give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E|^2p_{\mathbf z}^2
&=\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}}\right)^2\\
&\le\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}p_{\mathbf x}\right)
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}\right)\\
&\le B_{\mathbf z}\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}p_{\mathbf x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed the reflected image of \(E\) is a subset of \(R_{\mathbf z}\). Its mass is at most \(B_{\mathbf z}\), regardless of tilt signs or boundary multiplicities. Since \(E\subseteq D\cap H\), (5)--(6) prove (1). When \(R_{\mathbf z}=D\), the shifted normalization gives \(p_{\mathbf z}=1/\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)}\), proving (3).

For restriction and face isolation, any ambient containing halfspace satisfies
\(\sum_{D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}\ge\sum_{R_{\mathbf z}\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}\ge B_{\mathbf z}\tau_{r_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf z)\). Taking the minimum proves the asserted restriction inequality. This argument does not discard any ambient halfspace with a degenerate restriction.

Suppose (2) holds. If \(R_{\mathbf z}=D\), the equality is immediate. Otherwise, choose a halfspace minimizing \(\tau_{r_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf z)\). Translating its boundary to \(\mathbf z\) only discards support points and keeps the root, so it remains minimizing. Write it as \(\{\mathbf u:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\}\), with \(\mathbf a\ne\mathbf0\). Because \(D\setminus R_{\mathbf z}\) is finite and the corresponding values in (2) are strictly negative, choose a finite \(M>0\) large enough that
\(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)+M\mathbf c\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)<0\) for every \(\mathbf x\in D\setminus R_{\mathbf z}\). For example, it suffices to take \(M\) larger than \(0\) and every ratio \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)/[-\mathbf c\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)]\) over that finite set.

The closed ambient halfspace \(H_M:=\{\mathbf u:(\mathbf a+M\mathbf c)\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf z)\ge0\}\) contains \(\mathbf z\), excludes every off-face support point, and has exactly the original minimizing intersection on \(R_{\mathbf z}\). Its normal is nonzero, since a zero normal would contradict the strict negative values on the nonempty off-face set. Its mass is therefore \(B_{\mathbf z}\tau_{r_{\mathbf z}}(\mathbf z)\). Together with restriction, this proves exact equality. On-face equality atoms remain included because the exposing term is zero there.

For the ternary specialization, coordinatewise reflection shows
\(R_{\mathbf z}=F_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in D:x_j=z_j\text{ for all }j\notin I\}\). Indeed, if \(z_j=1\), the condition \(2z_j-x_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\) forces \(x_j=1\); if \(z_j=-1\), it forces \(x_j=-1\); and if \(z_j=0\), every ternary value is allowed. Hence \(k_{\mathbf z}=3^m\). The exposing vector with \(c_j=z_j\) for \(j\notin I\) and \(c_i=0\) for \(i\in I\) satisfies
\(\mathbf c\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)=\sum_{j\notin I}(z_jx_j-1)\), which is zero precisely on \(F_{\mathbf z}\) and strictly negative at every off-face point. When \(I\) is all coordinates, \(F_{\mathbf z}=D\), and no isolation is needed.

Product normalization yields \(B_{\mathbf z}=\beta_{\mathbf z}\), \(p_{\mathbf z}=\beta_{\mathbf z}/\prod_{i\in I}Z_i\), and identifies \(r_{\mathbf z}\) with \(p^I_{\boldsymbol\theta}\) under the coordinate map that fixes \(x_j=z_j\) outside \(I\). This map also preserves the relevant depth. To check that assertion explicitly when \(m>0\), an ambient containing halfspace \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\ge s\), \(s\le0\), restricts to \(\sum_{i\in I}a_i y_i\ge s\). If its restricted normal is nonzero, this is an admissible conditional containing halfspace. If its restricted normal is zero, it contains the entire conditional support and has conditional mass \(1\), which is at least conditional depth. Conversely, every conditional containing halfspace lifts to an ambient one by setting all normal components outside \(I\) to zero. Thus the two conditional depths agree. If \(m=0\), both conditional supports consist solely of the root and have depth \(1\).

The face equality and (1), with these exact substitutions, now give (4). More explicitly, in a through-root restricted cap the number of included face sites is \((3^m+b)/2\), where \(b\) is the number of conditional equality sites. A generic restricted normal has \(b=1\); any additional equality sites are included, not removed. If all restricted normal components vanish, \(b=3^m\), and the whole face is included. Zero individual components and arbitrary tilt signs require no further case distinction.

As a direct smallest-dimension check, for \(N=1\) and \(z=\pm1\), face isolation gives \(\tau_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(z)=p_{\boldsymbol\theta}(z)\). For \(z=0\), translating any containing halfline to \(0\) leaves either \(\{-1,0\}\) or \(\{0,1\}\); both retain the closed center atom. Consequently \(\tau_{p_{\boldsymbol\theta}}(0)=(1+e^{-|\theta_1|})/Z_1\). These cases are covered by the same proof, including the empty-product interpretation.
