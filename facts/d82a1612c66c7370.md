## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(w_z>0\) and \(x\in C_z\), put
\[
\delta_z(x):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\},
\tag{2}
\]
and define
\[
M_z:=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_z(x).
\tag{3}
\]
For every empty or zero-volume lattice fiber, define \(M_z:=0\).

Let \(u\in\mathbb Z^2\) be primitive, let \(\ell\) be an affine line
parallel to \(u\) with \(\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
L_\ell:=\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0.
\tag{4}
\]
Then there are one or two positive-fiber lattice points
\[
E\subseteq\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
q(E):=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)\in\{0,1\},
\tag{5}
\]
fiber-depth maximizers at the points of \(E\), and their affine interpolant
\(s\), such that
\[
2^{-q(E)}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\delta_z(s(z))
\geq
\frac3{32}\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z
\geq
\frac{3L_\ell}{32e}.
\tag{6}
\]
In rank zero the sum in (6) is the single fiber depth. Consequently, if
\[
L_\ell\geq\frac{eW}{6},
\tag{7}
\]
then \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)\) contains a point of mixed
halfspace depth at least \(W/64\).

## Proof

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) implies that only finitely many
lattice fibers are nonempty. Thus all sums below have finite support, even
when they are written over an entire affine lattice line.

We first compare maximal fiber depth with fiber mass and record the
zero-fiber convention. Fix \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\) with \(w_z>0\), and define
the finite measure on \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\)
\[
\mu_z:=
\delta_0\otimes
\left(\operatorname{Leb}_d\mathbin{\restriction}C_z\right).
\tag{8}
\]
It is a nonzero compactly supported log-concave measure of total mass
\(w_z\). Its base marginal is \(w_z\delta_0\), so every closed base
halfspace containing \(0\) has mass \(w_z\). Apply accepted fact
`f049539ffd9c9b1d` with base dimension one, \(z_0=0\), and \(\alpha=1\).
The selected submeasure is dominated by \(\mu_z\) and has the same total
mass, so it equals \(\mu_z\). Its barycenter is therefore a point
\((0,c_z)\) with \(c_z\in C_z\), and every closed halfspace in
\(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) containing \((0,c_z)\) has
\(\mu_z\)-mass at least \(w_z/e\).

For every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing
\(c_z\), the product \(\mathbb R\times J\) is a proper closed ambient
halfspace containing \((0,c_z)\). Hence
\[
\delta_z(c_z)\geq\frac{w_z}{e},
\qquad
M_z\geq\frac{w_z}{e}.
\tag{9}
\]
Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` shows that the maximum defining \(M_z\)
is attained. The same fact, applied on the continuous base projection
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^2}(C),
\tag{10}
\]
shows that
\[
\rho(z):=M(z)^{1/d}
\tag{11}
\]
is concave on \(P\), where \(M(z)\) denotes maximal absolute fiber depth,
and that \(M(z)=0\) on every nonempty zero-volume fiber. Together with the
explicit convention \(M_z=0\) on empty fibers, (9) gives
\[
M_z>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w_z>0
\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{12}
\]
Also \(0\leq\delta_z(x)\leq w_z\) for every positive-volume fiber and
every \(x\in C_z\), a fact used below in rank zero.

We next prove the finite chord lemma needed on the line.

**Chord lemma.** Let
\[
r_0\geq r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_m\geq0
\tag{13}
\]
be a finite sequence, and suppose its drops
\[
\Delta_k:=r_{k-1}-r_k
\qquad(1\leq k\leq m)
\tag{14}
\]
are nondecreasing. For \(j\geq1\), put
\[
B_j:=
\sum_{k=0}^j
\left(
\left(1-\frac{k}{j}\right)r_0+\frac{k}{j}r_j
\right)^d,
\qquad
B_0:=r_0^d.
\tag{15}
\]
Then some \(j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) satisfies
\[
B_j\geq\frac38\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d.
\tag{16}
\]

If \(r_0=0\), then every term in (13) is zero, and \(j=0\) proves (16).
Assume henceforth that \(r_0>0\), and put
\[
\theta:=\frac d{d+1},\qquad
\gamma:=\theta^d,\qquad
\eta:=\theta^{d+1}.
\tag{17}
\]
The binomial theorem gives
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d\geq2
\tag{18}
\]
and
\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{d+1}
\geq
1+\frac{d+1}{d}
+\binom{d+1}{2}\frac1{d^2}
=\frac52+\frac{3}{2d}>\frac52.
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore
\[
\gamma\leq\frac12,\qquad \eta<\frac25.
\tag{20}
\]

First suppose that
\[
r_k\geq\theta r_0
\qquad(0\leq k\leq m).
\tag{21}
\]
If \(m=0\), choose \(j=0\). If \(m\geq1\), choose \(j=m\). The chord from
\(r_0\) to \(r_m\) is bounded below at its \(k\)-th lattice point by
\[
r_0\left(1-\frac{k}{m(d+1)}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
The function
\[
\phi(x):=\left(1-\frac{x}{d+1}\right)^d
\qquad(0\leq x\leq1)
\tag{23}
\]
is convex. The composite trapezoidal inequality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_m
&\geq r_0^d\sum_{k=0}^m\phi(k/m)\\
&\geq r_0^d\left[
m\int_0^1\phi(x)\,dx+\frac{\phi(0)+\phi(1)}2
\right]\\
&=r_0^d\left[
m(1-\eta)+\frac{1+\gamma}{2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Since \(\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d\leq(m+1)r_0^d\), equations (20) and (24) give
\[
8B_m-3\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d
\geq
r_0^d\left[m(5-8\eta)+1+4\gamma\right]>0.
\tag{25}
\]
This proves (16) in the first case.

Suppose now that (21) fails, and define
\[
j:=\max\{k:r_k\geq\theta r_0\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Then \(j<m\) and \(r_{j+1}<\theta r_0\). If \(j=0\), then
\[
\Delta_1>\frac{r_0}{d+1}.
\tag{27}
\]
Because the drops are nondecreasing, for every \(1\leq k\leq m\),
\[
r_k<
r_0\left(1-\frac{k}{d+1}\right).
\tag{28}
\]
Nonnegativity forces \(m\leq d\), and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d
&<
r_0^d\sum_{k=0}^{d}
\left(1-\frac{k}{d+1}\right)^d\\
&=
r_0^d\sum_{h=1}^{d+1}
\left(\frac{h}{d+1}\right)^d
\leq2r_0^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
For the last inequality, the summand is nondecreasing in \(h\), so
\[
\sum_{h=1}^{d}\left(\frac{h}{d+1}\right)^d
\leq
\int_1^{d+1}\left(\frac{x}{d+1}\right)^d\,dx
\leq1,
\]
while the last summand equals \(1\). Thus \(B_0=r_0^d\) is more than one
half of the total in (29), proving (16).

It remains to treat \(j\geq1\). Put
\[
h:=\frac{r_0}{(d+1)(j+1)}.
\tag{30}
\]
Monotonicity of the drops and \(r_{j+1}<\theta r_0\) give
\[
\Delta_{j+1}
\geq
\frac{r_0-r_{j+1}}{j+1}
>h.
\tag{31}
\]
Every later drop is at least \(\Delta_{j+1}\). Consequently, for every
existing \(l\in\{0,\ldots,m-j-1\}\),
\[
r_{j+1+l}
\leq
\left(\theta r_0-lh\right)_+.
\tag{32}
\]
The function \(x\mapsto(\theta r_0-hx)_+^d\) is nonincreasing. Its sum
over nonnegative integers is at most its value at zero plus its integral,
so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=j+1}^m r_k^d
&\leq
\gamma r_0^d+
\int_0^\infty(\theta r_0-hx)_+^d\,dx\\
&=
\left[\gamma+\eta(j+1)\right]r_0^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
Adding the trivial prefix bound gives
\[
\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d
\leq
\left[(j+1)(1+\eta)+\gamma\right]r_0^d.
\tag{34}
\]
Since \(r_j\geq\theta r_0\), the same trapezoidal estimate as in (24)
gives
\[
B_j\geq
r_0^d\left[
j(1-\eta)+\frac{1+\gamma}{2}
\right].
\tag{35}
\]
Combining (34) and (35),
\[
8B_j-3\sum_{k=0}^m r_k^d
\geq
r_0^d\left[
j(5-11\eta)+1+\gamma-3\eta
\right].
\tag{36}
\]
Here \(5-11\eta>0\) by (20). Moreover, because
\(\eta=\theta\gamma\), \(\theta<1\), and \(\gamma\leq1/2\),
\[
1+\gamma-3\eta
=1-\gamma(3\theta-1)
>1-2\gamma\geq0.
\tag{37}
\]
The right-hand side of (36) is therefore positive. This completes the
proof of the chord lemma, including the all-zero sequence.

We now apply the chord lemma to \(\ell\). Choose an actual lattice anchor
\[
a\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{38}
\]
Write \(u=(u_1,u_2)\). Primitivity gives \(p,q\in\mathbb Z\) such that
\(pu_1+qu_2=1\). If \(z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2\), then
\(z-a=tu\) for some \(t\in\mathbb R\), and
\[
t=p(z_1-a_1)+q(z_2-a_2)\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The reverse inclusion is immediate, so
\[
\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2=a+\mathbb Zu.
\tag{39}
\]
This is the exact restricted-lattice identification.

Define
\[
I:=\{k\in\mathbb Z:w_{a+ku}>0\}.
\tag{40}
\]
Compactness of \(C\) makes \(I\) finite, while the standing assumption
\(L_\ell>0\) makes it nonempty. It is a consecutive integer interval.
Indeed, if \(k_0<k_1\) lie in \(I\), then (9) makes
\(\rho(a+k_i u)>0\) for \(i=0,1\). Concavity of \(\rho\) on the segment
between these base points makes it positive at every intermediate lattice
point. Equation (12) then makes each intermediate fiber positive-volume.
Thus
\[
I=\{k_-,k_-+1,\ldots,k_+\}.
\tag{41}
\]
The zero convention outside \(I\) gives
\[
\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z
=
\sum_{k=k_-}^{k_+}M_{a+ku}.
\tag{42}
\]

Choose \(k_*\in I\) maximizing \(\rho(a+ku)\) over \(I\). Define the two
one-sided sequences
\[
r_k^+:=\rho(a+(k_*+k)u)
\quad(0\leq k\leq k_+-k_*),
\tag{43}
\]
\[
r_k^-:=\rho(a+(k_*-k)u)
\quad(0\leq k\leq k_*-k_-).
\tag{44}
\]
Both are nonnegative and nonincreasing, and their drops are nondecreasing.
Indeed, concavity of \(\rho\) makes its successive lattice slopes
nonincreasing in either oriented coordinate, while maximality at \(k_*\)
makes each first outgoing slope nonpositive.

Put
\[
\Sigma_+:=\sum_k(r_k^+)^d,\qquad
\Sigma_-:=\sum_k(r_k^-)^d.
\tag{45}
\]
The maximizing term is counted twice, so (42) gives
\[
\Sigma_++\Sigma_-
=
\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z+M_{a+k_*u}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max\{\Sigma_+,\Sigma_-\}
\geq
\frac12\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z.
\tag{46}
\]

Choose \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) for a side attaining the maximum in (46).
Apply the chord lemma to that side. It returns an index \(j\), including
the possibility \(j=0\), whose chord sum satisfies
\[
B_j\geq\frac38\max\{\Sigma_+,\Sigma_-\}.
\tag{47}
\]
The initial term on this side is positive because \(k_*\in I\); the
all-zero part of the scalar lemma was proved separately but is not needed
at this application.

Put
\[
z^{(0)}:=a+k_*u,\qquad
z^{(j)}:=a+(k_*+\sigma j)u.
\tag{48}
\]
If \(j=0\), set \(E:=\{z^{(0)}\}\), choose an actual maximizer
\(x_0\in C_{z^{(0)}}\), and define \(s(z^{(0)}):=x_0\). If \(j\geq1\),
set
\[
E:=\{z^{(0)},z^{(j)}\},
\tag{49}
\]
choose actual fiber-depth maximizers
\[
x_0\in C_{z^{(0)}},\qquad x_j\in C_{z^{(j)}},
\tag{50}
\]
and let \(s\) be their affine interpolant. Every point of \(E\) has a
positive-volume fiber because both indices lie in \(I\).

For \(j\geq1\), the primitive-lattice identity (39) gives exactly
\[
\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2
=
\{z^{(0)}+\sigma ku:0\leq k\leq j\}.
\tag{51}
\]
Every index in (51) lies on the selected side inside \(I\), so every
fiber appearing in the ensuing depth sum has positive volume. Convexity
of \(C\) makes
\[
s(z^{(0)}+\sigma ku)
=
\left(1-\frac{k}{j}\right)x_0+\frac{k}{j}x_j
\in C_{z^{(0)}+\sigma ku}.
\tag{52}
\]
Joint concavity of the fiber-depth root in accepted fact
`4e868f774de77608` then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta_{z^{(0)}+\sigma ku}
\left(s(z^{(0)}+\sigma ku)\right)^{1/d}\\
&\qquad\geq
\left(1-\frac{k}{j}\right)M_{z^{(0)}}^{1/d}
+\frac{k}{j}M_{z^{(j)}}^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{53}
\]
Raising (53) to the \(d\)-th power and summing proves
\[
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\delta_z(s(z))
\geq B_j.
\tag{54}
\]
For \(j=0\), equation (54) also holds, with both sides equal to
\(M_{z^{(0)}}\).

The rank factor is now explicit:
\[
2^{-q(E)}
=
\begin{cases}
1,&j=0,\\
\frac12,&j\geq1.
\end{cases}
\tag{55}
\]
Thus \(2^{-q(E)}\geq1/2\). Combining (46), (47), (54), and (55) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{-q(E)}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\delta_z(s(z))
&\geq\frac12 B_j\\
&\geq\frac3{16}\max\{\Sigma_+,\Sigma_-\}\\
&\geq
\frac3{32}\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{56}
\]
Finally, (9) on every positive-volume line fiber and the zero convention
on all other line fibers give
\[
\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}M_z
\geq
\frac1e\sum_{z\in\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z
=\frac{L_\ell}{e}.
\tag{57}
\]
Equations (56) and (57) prove (6).

It remains to derive the ambient mixed-depth consequence. Assume (7), and
put
\[
\Delta_E:=
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\delta_z(s(z)).
\tag{58}
\]
The standing hypothesis \(L_\ell>0\) is what made the construction of
\(E\) and inequality (6) available. Using it together with (7) gives
\[
2^{-q(E)}\Delta_E
\geq\frac{3L_\ell}{32e}
\geq\frac{W}{64}.
\tag{59}
\]
The other standing hypothesis \(W>0\) makes the last quantity strictly
positive. In particular, \(\Delta_E>0\), as required below.

First suppose \(q(E)=0\). Write \(E=\{z^*\}\) and
\(x^*:=s(z^*)\). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be any closed
halfspace containing \((z^*,x^*)\). If \(H\) is proper, write it with a
nonzero normal as
\[
H=\{(z,x):\langle a,z\rangle+\langle c,x\rangle\geq b\}.
\tag{60}
\]
If \(c\ne0\), its section over \(z^*\) is a proper closed halfspace of
\(\mathbb R^d\) containing \(x^*\), and that section cuts volume at least
\(\delta_{z^*}(x^*)=\Delta_E\) from \(C_{z^*}\). If \(c=0\), membership
over \(z^*\) is constant; because \((z^*,x^*)\in H\), the whole fiber
\(C_{z^*}\) lies in \(H\), and its volume is
\(w_{z^*}\geq\Delta_E\). The same full-fiber bound handles the whole-space
halfspace. All other lattice-fiber contributions are nonnegative, so
\[
\mathcal H_d\!\left(
C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H
\right)
\geq\Delta_E
\geq\frac{W}{64},
\tag{61}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(q(E)=0\) in (59). This proves the
rank-zero case directly. In particular, accepted fact
`b220933817d23295` is not invoked with base rank zero.

Now suppose \(q(E)=1\), so \(j\geq1\). Define
\[
\widetilde C:=
\left\{
(t,x)\in[0,j]\times\mathbb R^d:
(z^{(0)}+\sigma tu,x)\in C
\right\}.
\tag{62}
\]
This set is compact and convex. Both endpoint fibers have positive
\(d\)-volume and therefore contain \(d\)-dimensional open balls. The
convex hull of those balls placed at base coordinates \(0\) and \(j\)
has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\) and lies in
\(\widetilde C\). Hence \(\widetilde C\) is a convex body in
\(\mathbb R^{1+d}\).

The affine embedding
\[
\Phi:\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{aff}(E)\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
\Phi(t,x):=(z^{(0)}+\sigma tu,x),
\tag{63}
\]
identifies the mixed lattice exactly:
\[
\Phi(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)
=
\bigl(\operatorname{aff}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\bigr)\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{64}
\]
This is (39), translated to \(z^{(0)}\) and with the primitive direction
\(\sigma u\).

Define the affine selector
\[
\widetilde s(t):=
\left(1-\frac{t}{j}\right)x_0+\frac{t}{j}x_j.
\tag{65}
\]
Convexity of \(C\) gives
\((t,\widetilde s(t))\in\widetilde C\) for every \(t\in[0,j]\).
Thus the selector is feasible on every positive-volume integer fiber of
\(\widetilde C\). Under (63), the selector depth in the fiber at an
integer \(t\in[0,j]\) is exactly
\(\delta_{z^{(0)}+\sigma tu}(s(z^{(0)}+\sigma tu))\), while all integer
fibers outside \([0,j]\) are empty. Therefore the sum of selector depths
for \(\widetilde C\) is exactly \(\Delta_E>0\).

Apply accepted fact `b220933817d23295` to \(\widetilde C\) with base
dimension one and selector (65). This is the only use of that accepted
fact. It gives an integer \(t^*\in[0,j]\) such that
\[
\widetilde y^*:=(t^*,\widetilde s(t^*))
\tag{66}
\]
has mixed halfspace depth in \(\widetilde C\) at least
\[
\frac12\Delta_E
=2^{-q(E)}\Delta_E
\geq\frac{W}{64}.
\tag{67}
\]
Set \(y^*:=\Phi(\widetilde y^*)\). Equation (64) and feasibility show
\[
y^*\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{68}
\]

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be any closed halfspace containing
\(y^*\). Its affine preimage
\[
\widetilde H:=\Phi^{-1}(H)
\tag{69}
\]
is a closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\), possibly the whole space,
and contains \(\widetilde y^*\). The embedding \(\Phi\) is the identity
on each continuous fiber, so it preserves the \(d\)-dimensional fiber
measure. Write
\[
H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\},\qquad
\widetilde C_t:=\{x:(t,x)\in\widetilde C\},
\tag{70}
\]
and define \((\widetilde H)_t\) analogously. Equations (51) and (64)
therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal H_d\!\left(
C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H
\right)\\
&\qquad\geq
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)\\
&\qquad=
\sum_{t\in\mathbb Z}
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(
\widetilde C_t\cap(\widetilde H)_t
\right)
\geq\frac{W}{64}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{71}
\]
The last inequality is (67). This transfers restricted rank-one depth
monotonically to the original ambient body. Every halfspace in the
argument is closed, its affine preimage is closed, and no boundary mass
is discarded. Equations (61) and (71) complete the proof.
