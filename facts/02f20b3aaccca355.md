## Statement

Let $B$ be the planar unit disk, $C=B\times[0,3]^2$, $R=[-2,2]^2\times[0,3]^2$, $e=1/10$ and $a=\sqrt{1799}/20$. In coordinates $(x_1,x_2,t,s)$, each of the following fourteen proper closed caps has volume $9\pi/4$ on $C$:
$$
\begin{gathered}
t\le3/4,\quad t\ge9/4,\quad s\le3/4,\quad s\ge9/4,\\
t\le3/4+ex_i,\quad t\ge9/4+ex_i,\quad
s\le3/4+ex_i,\quad s\ge9/4+ex_i,\quad
t+s\le a+ex_i\qquad (i=1,2).
\end{gathered}
$$
Among convex bodies $L\subseteq R$ of volume $9\pi$, these measurements uniquely determine $C$. In particular they exclude every polytope in this fixed box. Within the smaller family $L=K\times[0,3]^2\subseteq R$ it suffices to use the two caps $t\ge9/4+ex_i$ and the two caps $t+s\le a+ex_i$ together with total volume.

## Proof

The disk has area $\pi$, centroid zero and coordinate second moments $\pi/4$. On $C$ the untilted intervals have length $3/4$ in the cut coordinate, and the mean of every tilted linear shift is zero. These intervals are unclipped since $|x_i|\le1$. Each corner cap has triangular area $(a+e x_i)^2/2$ at $x$, since $0<a-e<a+e<3$. Its integrated volume is $\pi(a^2+e^2/4)/2=9\pi/4$.

For a matching $L$ the $t$-section volume function, extended by zero on the unoccupied part of $[0,3]$, has convex positive set and concave cube root there by the ordinary Brunn–Minkowski inequality and convex section inclusions. The total volume gives mean $3\pi$ and both $3/4$ tails have this average. The two-tail profile lemma makes the section volume equal $3\pi$ almost everywhere. The same reasoning applies in $s$. In particular the three unit slabs in each variable have volume $3\pi$, so the three-slab prism lemma gives both prisms. In the $(t,s)$ projection the $t$-prism is a parallelogram with vertical sections of fixed positive length. If its cross-shear were nonzero, its lowest horizontal section would be a singleton, contrary to the $s$-prism of constant positive horizontal length. The projection is $[0,3]^2$, so the $s$-prism has zero $t$-shear too. Composing the remaining translations gives $x=y+tv+sw$ over a planar body $K$ of area $\pi$, with $0\le t,s\le3$.

For $b=3/4$ and $b=9/4$ the moving threshold in $t$ is $(b+e(y_i+sw_i))/(1-ev_i)$. Comparing the $t=0,3$ ends at fixed $y,s$ in the box gives $|v_i|\le4/3$. The margins at $t=0,3$ have opposite signs since $|x_i|\le2$, so there is no clipping. The required lower-tail volume at $b=3/4$ and upper-tail volume at $b=9/4$ respectively force the integrated average threshold to equal $b$ at both levels. Writing $m_i$ for the centroid of $K$, the equations are
$$
\frac{b+e(m_i+3w_i/2)}{1-ev_i}=b
\qquad (b=3/4,\ 9/4).
$$
They give $v_i=0$ and $m_i+3w_i/2=0$. The symmetric $s$ equations give $w_i=0$ and $m_i=0$.

With both shears zero, $K\subseteq[-2,2]^2$ and the corner thresholds lie in $(0,3)$ because $a-2e>0$ and $a+2e<3$. Their volumes are
$$
\frac{a^2\pi}{2}+\frac{e^2}{2}\int_K y_i^2\,dy=\frac{9\pi}{4}.
$$
Since $a^2=9/2-e^2/4$, both coordinate moments equal $\pi/4$. The area-$\pi$ radial moment comparison follows by subtracting $\int_B |y|^2\,dy$ and splitting into $K\setminus B$ and $B\setminus K$, with nonnegative integrands $|y|^2-1$ and $1-|y|^2$. Equality forces agreement up to null sets. Compact convex bodies with interiors that differ at a point also differ in positive area, by joining that point toward an interior ball. Hence $K=B$. Projection onto the $x$-plane proves the nonpolytopality. In the product family the shear is zero by hypothesis, so the top equations at $b=9/4$ alone give $m_i=0$. The same two corner computations finish that subcertificate.
