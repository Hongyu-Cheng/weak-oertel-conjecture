## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(a:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) be log-concave with finite positive lattice sum \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a(z)\). For \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(c\in\mathbb R^n\), respectively, define the unnormalized reflection-overlap mass and weighted base halfspace depth by

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_j&:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{a(z),a(2j-z)\},\\
\operatorname{TD}_a(c)&:=\inf\left\{
\sum_{z\in H\cap\mathbb Z^n}a(z):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace},\ c\in H
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The whole space may be included among the closed halfspaces; its mass is \(W\), so including it does not change the infimum over proper closed halfspaces. A positive lattice site means \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(a(j)>0\), not a condition on the signs of its coordinates. Each overlap sum converges because its summands lie between zero and \(a(z)\).

There is no absolute \(\alpha>0\) such that \(S_j\geq\alpha\operatorname{TD}_a(j)\) for every such \(n,a\), and every positive lattice site \(j\). More precisely, let \(T:=\operatorname{conv}\{(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\}\). For every \(r\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), take \(n=2r\), \(a=\mathbf1_{T^r}\) on \(\mathbb R^{2r}\), and \(j=0\). Here \(T^r\) is the Cartesian product of \(r\) copies of \(T\). Its support is a full-dimensional lattice polytope, and

\[
W=4^r,\qquad S_0=1,\qquad
\operatorname{TD}_a(0)\geq2^r,\qquad
\frac{S_0}{\operatorname{TD}_a(0)}\leq2^{-r}.
\]

For this same family, \(2^{-n}W=1=S_0\). Consequently the family satisfies \(\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^n:a(j)>0}S_j\geq2^{-n}W\), and is not a counterexample to an existential bound \(\max_j S_j\geq c2^{-n}W\) with an absolute \(c\in(0,1]\).

## Proof

We first identify the predecessor notation exactly. The scalar \(\kappa(D,\omega;c)\) in accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 is the unnormalized closed-halfspace depth of the finite atomic measure with weights \(\omega\) on \(D\). Thus it equals \(\operatorname{TD}_a(c)\) whenever \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a(z)>0\}\) is finite and \(\omega=a|_D\). There is no division by total mass. The scalar \(\kappa(T,0)\) in accepted fact e6af28280337b587 is the unit-weight lattice counting depth on \(A:=T\cap\mathbb Z^2\). In particular, its exact alias map is \(\kappa(T,0)=\kappa(A,\mathbf1;0)=\operatorname{TD}_{\mathbf1_T}(0)\), where \(\mathbf1\) denotes unit weights on \(A\). That predecessor uses proper closed halfspaces; allowing the whole space has no effect as explained above. Neither predecessor scalar is the reflected midpoint measure denoted by \(\kappa_j\) in reflection-kernel notation. No such continuous measure is used here, and \(S_j\) has only the overlap meaning defined in this statement.

Accepted fact e6af28280337b587 supplies the exact census \(A=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)\}\), the interior inclusion \(0\in\operatorname{int}T\), and the depth value \(\kappa(T,0)=2\). To make the geometry and boundary conventions explicit, the two edge vectors from \((-1,-1)\) to the other vertices are \((2,1)\) and \((1,2)\), with determinant \(3\). The triangle is therefore two-dimensional, and the origin is its interior point with barycentric coordinates \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\).

The triangle lies in \([-1,1]^2\) and has the edge inequalities \(x+y\leq1\), \(2x-y\geq-1\), and \(x-2y\leq1\). On the possible lattice rows \(y=-1,0,1\), these inequalities give, respectively, \(x=-1\), \(x\in\{0,1\}\), and \(x=0\). This verifies the complete census above. The negatives of its three nonzero lattice points are absent from \(A\), so \(A\cap(-A)=\{0\}\).

For the depth assertion, any proper closed halfspace containing the origin can be written as \(\{x:\ell(x)\geq\beta\}\) with a nonzero linear functional \(\ell\) and \(\beta\leq0\). The three vertex values of \(\ell\) sum to zero, so at least one is nonnegative. Such a halfspace therefore contains the origin and at least one vertex. Conversely, the proper closed halfspace \(\{(x,y):2x-y\geq0\}\) contains exactly \((0,0)\) and \((1,0)\) from \(A\). This recovers the accepted exact value \(\operatorname{TD}_{\mathbf1_T}(0)=2\) with its closed-boundary convention.

Fix \(r\geq1\). The product \(T^r\) is a compact convex polytope whose vertices are tuples of vertices of \(T\), hence are integral. The product of \(r\) open neighborhoods of the origin contained in \(T\) is an open neighborhood in \(\mathbb R^{2r}\) contained in \(T^r\). Thus \(T^r\) has dimension \(2r\), and \(0\) is an interior lattice point. Its indicator \(a=\mathbf1_{T^r}\) is log-concave on all of \(\mathbb R^{2r}\): for \(0<t<1\), if both endpoints lie in \(T^r\), their convex combination lies there and the log-concavity inequality has both sides equal to one; if either endpoint lies outside, the right side is zero. The endpoint cases \(t=0,1\) are identities. The positive support of \(a\) is exactly \(T^r\), and \(a(0)=1\).

Membership in the product and in the product lattice is coordinatewise, so \(T^r\cap\mathbb Z^{2r}=A^r\). It follows that \(W=|A^r|=4^r\), which is finite and positive. At the chosen center the overlap has the exact value

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_0
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{2r}}\min\{\mathbf1_{T^r}(z),\mathbf1_{T^r}(-z)\}\\
&=|A^r\cap(-A^r)|
=|(A\cap(-A))^r|
=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

We now apply only the product-depth counting lemma of accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5. In its notation take \(k=r\), \(r_i=2\), \(D_i=A\), \(\omega_i\equiv1\), and \(c_i=0\) for every \(1\leq i\leq r\). The sets are finite and nonempty, the weights are nonnegative, and the preceding alias map gives each exact factor depth \(\kappa(D_i,\omega_i;c_i)=2\).

Let \(H\) be any proper closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^{2r}\) containing \(0\). Write a point in blocks as \((x_1,\ldots,x_r)\), with \(x_i\in\mathbb R^2\), and write \(H=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_r):\sum_{i=1}^r\ell_i(x_i)\geq\beta\}\), where the \(\ell_i\) are linear functionals, not all zero, and \(\beta\leq0\). The function \(F(x_1,\ldots,x_r):=\sum_{i=1}^r\ell_i(x_i)-\beta\) is affine in each block separately and satisfies \(F(c_1,\ldots,c_r)=-\beta\geq0\). The product-depth lemma therefore yields

\[
\sum_{z\in H\cap\mathbb Z^{2r}}a(z)
=\sum_{\substack{(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\in A^r\\F(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\geq0}}
\prod_{i=1}^r\omega_i(u_i)
\geq\prod_{i=1}^r\kappa(D_i,\omega_i;c_i)
=2^r.
\]

The lemma allows zero block functionals and nonnegative boundary values, so no genericity or strict-halfspace assumption is needed. The whole space has mass \(4^r\geq2^r\). Taking the infimum over all containing closed halfspaces proves \(\operatorname{TD}_a(0)\geq2^r>0\). Combining this with \(S_0=1\) gives the claimed ratio bound.

For any proposed absolute \(\alpha>0\), choose an integer \(r\geq1\) with \(2^{-r}<\alpha\). The admissible indicator weight just constructed then satisfies \(S_0/\operatorname{TD}_a(0)\leq2^{-r}<\alpha\), and hence \(S_0<\alpha\operatorname{TD}_a(0)\). This proves the stated failure with the required quantifiers over dimensions, weights, and prescribed positive lattice sites.

Finally, \(2^{-n}W=2^{-2r}4^r=1=S_0\). Thus even the selected site already has target-scale overlap in this family. The vanishing ratio concerns overlap relative to the larger base depth, not overlap relative to \(2^{-n}W\). No upper bound on the best mixed-integer halfspace depth is asserted, so this obstruction does not disprove the locked weak Oertel conjecture.
