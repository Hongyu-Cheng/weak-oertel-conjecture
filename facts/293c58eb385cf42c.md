## Statement

Let two distinct proper plane sections of a nondegenerate tetrahedron have the same actual area centroid in barycentric coordinates $b=(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)$, with $b_0>1/3$ and $b_j\le1/3$ for $j=1,2,3$. Suppose both strictly separate $\{0,1\}$ from $\{2,3\}$, and orient their raw affine vertex values $c,c'$ to be positive on $\{0,1\}$. If $c'=a c+t m$ with $a,t>0$ and $m=(3,3-1/b_1,3-1/b_2,3-1/b_3)$, then $b_2=b_3$ and $c_2=c_3$, $c'_2=c'_3$.

## Proof

For raw values $(p,q,-r,-s)$ with $p,q,r,s>0$, write $D_s=pq(r+s)+rs(p+q)$ and $\tau=ps(q+r)/D_s$. The raw formulas of 1462fa9ce6ced54e for the strict split $\{0,1\}\mid\{2,3\}$ give the actual centroid $B(p,q,r,s)$ as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
3B_0&=\frac r{p+r}+\frac{\tau s}{p+s},&
3B_1&=\frac s{q+s}+\frac{(1-\tau)r}{q+r},\\
3B_2&=\frac p{p+r}+\frac{(1-\tau)q}{q+r},&
3B_3&=\frac q{q+s}+\frac{\tau p}{p+s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed the area weight in that fact is $\alpha/(\alpha+\delta)=ps(q+r)/D_s$, with the same triangulation and orientation, and the four edge intersections give the displayed coordinates. All denominators are positive in the strict section cell and all centroid coordinates are positive.

Scale the first margin to $(p,q,-r,-1)$ by dividing by $-c_3>0$ and the second margin by $a(-c_3)>0$. Then the normalized second section has margin $(p,q,-r,-1)+T m$ with $T=t/(a(-c_3))>0$ and both sections have centroid $b=B(p,q,r,1)$. Hence for $i=1,2$ the rational differences
$$
B_i(p+3T,q+T m_1,r-T m_2,1-T m_3)-B_i(p,q,r,1)
$$
vanish at this $T$, and their denominators are nonzero there by strictness.

Here are the exact determinant identities for these two differences. They are given with the rational input, primitive-part rule and factors to permit coefficient verification. In each difference put $m_j=3-1/B_j(p,q,r,1)$, collect over a common denominator, and divide its numerator by $T$. For the generic identity take the primitive part in $T$ over $\mathbb Z[p,q,r]$, allowing either sign of a primitive part. Denote the resulting quartics by $F_1,F_2$. Using the factored centroid expressions to clear denominators, the cleared numerator factors apart from $F_i$, up to nonzero constants, are $3Tq^2(p+1)(p+r)N_1$ and $3Tq^2r(p+1)(p+r)(q+1)N_2$, respectively. Any canceled denominator factors divide products of the original nonzero denominators. Thus at an actual second strict section with $T>0$, both specialized quartics vanish. This implication does not require that their specialized leading coefficients stay nonzero.

Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_*&=pq^2(r+1)+2pqr+q^2r-r^2,& D&=pqr+pq+pr+qr,\\
A_*&=pqr+2pq+2pr+qr,& E&=2pqr+pq+2pr+qr,& L&=qr+q+2r.
\end{aligned}
$$
The $8\times8$ Sylvester determinant of the quartics is, up to the sign convention for the primitive parts and determinant,
$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{16}p^2q^{32}r^{12}(r-1)^3B_*^2G
(p+r)^{19}(p+1)^{20}(q+r)^{10}(q+1)^6
D^{10}A_*^6E^3LN_1N_2V_1V_2^2 ,
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
G={}&(r^2+3r+1)p^3q^3+3r(r+1)p^3q^2+2r^2p^3q\\
 &+r(r+1)p^2q^3-2r(r+1)^2p^2q^2-5r^2(r+1)p^2q\\
 &-3r^3p^2-3r^2(r+1)pq^2-3r^3pq-r^3q^2,\\
N_1={}&pq^2r^2+pq^2r+pq^2+2pqr^2+2pqr+pr^2+q^2r^2+q^2r+2qr^2,\\
N_2={}&2p^2q^2r+p^2q^2+p^2qr^2+2p^2qr+p^2r^2+pq^2r^2+2pq^2r+pqr^2+q^2r^2,\\
V_1={}&2p^2q^3r^2+5p^2q^3r+2p^2q^3+p^2q^2r^3+10p^2q^2r^2+7p^2q^2r\\
 &+4p^2qr^3+8p^2qr^2+3p^2r^3+4pq^3r^2+4pq^3r+pq^3\\
 &+2pq^2r^3+8pq^2r^2+2pq^2r+2pqr^3+pqr^2+2q^3r^2+q^3r+q^2r^3+2q^2r^2,\\
V_2={}&2p^2q^3r^2+5p^2q^3r+2p^2q^3+7p^2q^2r^2+10p^2q^2r+p^2q^2\\
 &+8p^2qr^2+4p^2qr+3p^2r^2+pq^3r^3+4pq^3r^2+4pq^3r\\
 &+2pq^2r^3+8pq^2r^2+2pq^2r+pqr^3+2pqr^2+q^3r^3+2q^3r^2+2q^2r^3+q^2r^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$
To obtain the identity by expansion, put the coefficients in descending order of $T$, use the four shifts of each coefficient row with zeros filling the other entries, and take the determinant in $\mathbb Z[p,q,r]$. Equivalently, for a matrix of current size $k$ and nonzero pivot $d_{00}$ after row and column swaps, replace its lower-right entries by $d_{00}d_{ij}-d_{i0}d_{0j}$ and divide the resulting determinant by $d_{00}^{k-2}$, keeping the swap signs. These operations give the displayed polynomial identity. A common finite root gives a nonzero kernel vector of descending powers ending in one for the padded Sylvester matrix, including at specializations with degree drops. All the factors besides $r-1$, $B_*$ and $G$ are strictly positive for $p,q,r>0$.

To determine the sign of $G$, put $P=p/\sqrt r$, $Q=q/\sqrt r$, $R=\sqrt r+1/\sqrt r\ge2$. From the centroid formula, $b_0>1/3$ is equivalent to $P^2(QR+1)+2PQ<1$. In fact
$$
(3b_0-1)(p+r)(p+1)D
=p\,[r^2-p^2(q(r+1)+r)-2pqr] .
$$
It follows that $0<P<1$ and $0<Q<Q_{\max}=(1-P^2)/(P(RP+2))$. The polynomial $g=G/r^4$ in these variables is cubic in $Q$, with positive leading coefficient, quadratic coefficient $3RP(P^2-1)-2R^2P^2-1<0$, linear coefficient $P(2P^2-5RP-3)<0$, and constant $-3P^2$. Thus $g/Q^3$ is strictly increasing for $Q>0$. Direct substitution in $G/r^4$ gives
$$
g(P,Q_{\max},R)=-\frac{(P^2+2)(P^2+RP+1)^3}{P^2(RP+2)^3}<0 .
$$
Therefore $G<0$ on the required noncentral domain. If $B_*\ne0$, the vanishing determinant forces $r=1$.

The remaining boundary is exactly $b_1=1/3$, since
$$
b_1-\frac13=-\frac{qB_*}{3(q+1)(q+r)D}.
$$
Here $p=r(r-q^2)/(q((r+1)q+2r))$, with $q^2<r$ by $p>0$. Substituting into the centroid formula gives $b_2=(q^3+r)/(3r(q+1)^2)$ and $b_3=(q^3+r^2)/(3(q+r)^2)$, and $m_1=0$. Make this substitution before taking numerator differences and their primitive parts. After division by $T$ the polynomials $J_1,J_2$ in $T$ are cubics, and the reduced numerator coefficient contents, apart from $T$ and nonzero constants, are $q^3$ and $q^3(q^3+r)$, respectively. Both are positive. The $6\times6$ determinant, using three shifts of each of the two coefficient rows, is up to sign
$$
3^9(r-1)^2 r q^{21}(qr+q+2r)(q+1)^6(q+r)^{10}
(q^2-r)^2(q^2-q-2r)^2(q^2-qr-2r)^4(q^3+r)H_1H_2^2,
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_1&=2q^4r+q^4+2q^3r+q^3-2q^2r^2+2q^2r+qr^3+2qr^2+3r^3,\\
H_2&=q^4r+2q^4+q^3r^2+2q^3r+2q^2r^2-2q^2r+2qr^2+qr+3r^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The same determinant expansion applied to the substituted rational differences gives this identity. As $q^2<r$, the three differences $q^2-r$, $q^2-q-2r$, $q^2-qr-2r$ are strictly negative. The negative monomial of $H_1$ is dominated by $3r^3$, and that of $H_2$ by $3r^2$. Thus only $r-1$ can vanish. The finite common root again gives determinant zero, so $r=1$ on this boundary as well.

Now the first plane has equal negative values at vertices $2,3$, and the affine transposition of these vertices preserves its section. The centroid formulas with $r=s=1$ give $b_2=b_3$. Thus $m_2=m_3$, so the pencil gives equality of the second plane's two negative values as well.
