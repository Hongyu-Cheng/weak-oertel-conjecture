## Statement

Fix an integer \(m\ge1\) and one symmetric saturated chain partition of \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\). Let \(D_m\) be its nonterminal elements and write the successor as \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), where \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\) and \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector. Use exactly the block and serial objects of accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d:
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,
\qquad T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m,
$$
$$
\Gamma_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{s=0}^Q E_m^s\times B_m^{Q-s},
\qquad P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma_{m,Q}),
\qquad A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^{mQ}.
$$
Put \(e_m=|E_m|\) and \(t_m=|T_m|\), and assume \(t_m\ne2^m\). The partition and \(m\) are fixed throughout the following quantifiers.

For every integer \(Q\ge1\), integer \(k\ge0\), and integer schedule
$$
0=a_0\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_Q\le k,
$$
put \(\nu=mQ\), write \(x=(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(Q)})\), and define the convex lift
$$
\phi(x)=\max\left(0,\max_{\substack{1\le j\le Q\\1\le i\le m}}
 a_j(x_i^{(j)}-1)\right),
\qquad
\widehat P^{\mathrm{ser}}
=\lbrace(x,t):x\in P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}},\ \phi(x)\le t\le k\rbrace.
$$
For \(x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\), its last-spike index is
$$
\ell(x)=\max\bigl(\lbrace j:x^{(j)}\in E_m\rbrace\cup\lbrace0\rbrace\bigr).
$$
This is the last spiked block, not the number of spiked blocks. The complete lattice support of the lift is exactly
$$
\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}
=\widehat P^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^{\nu+1}
=\left\lbrace(x,t):x\in A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}},\
 a_{\ell(x)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
\tag{1}
$$
For every independently chosen \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_Q\in\mathbb R^m\) and every \(\zeta\in\mathbb R\), retain all atoms of (1) and put
$$
w_{(x,t)}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^Q\gamma_j\cdot x^{(j)}+\zeta t\right),
\qquad W=\sum_{(x,t)\in\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}}w_{(x,t)},
\qquad p_{(x,t)}=w_{(x,t)}/W.
$$
For every original sample \(I\in\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}\), define
$$
\tau_p(I)=\inf_{H\ni I}p(H),
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{I\in\widehat A^{\mathrm{ser}}}p_I\tau_p(I)^3,
$$
where the infimum is over all closed halfspaces of the original ambient space \(\mathbb R^{\nu+1}\). Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_{m,\mathrm{partition}}\,8^{-(mQ+1)},
\tag{2}
$$
with a positive constant independent of \(Q,k,(a_j),(\gamma_j),\zeta\). Here are explicit admissible choices.

If \(t_m<2^m\), set
$$
K_{<}=1+\frac{e_m/2^m}{1-t_m/2^m},
\qquad c_{m,\mathrm{partition}}=\frac1{16K_{<}^3}.
\tag{3}
$$
If \(t_m>2^m\), let \(r_T=\dim\operatorname{aff}(T_m)\), and use the constants \(\alpha,\beta,g\) of (12)--(14) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 for the singleton fixed family \(\mathscr A=\lbrace T_m\rbrace\). These are the product constants of that fact, not class masses or affine fields. Explicitly, for each nonempty \(B\subseteq T_m\), let \(F_{T_m}(B)\) be the minimal face of \(\operatorname{conv}(T_m)\) containing \(B\), and choose a representation
$$
\frac1{|B|}\sum_{x\in B}x
=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{vert}F_{T_m}(B)}\lambda_v^{T_m,B}v,
\qquad \sum_v\lambda_v^{T_m,B}=1,
\qquad \lambda_v^{T_m,B}>0.
$$
Let \(\lambda_{T_m}\) be the minimum of these coefficients over all such subsets and vertices. Write \(\eta_{\mathrm{prod}}\) for the parameter called \(\eta\) in bf1ff94487296374; explicitly,
$$
\eta_{\mathrm{prod}}=\frac{\exp[-\log(4t_m)/\lambda_{T_m}]}{t_m}.
$$
For every nonempty face support \(E=T_m\cap F\) and every \(j\in E\), let \(r_{E,j}\) be the maximum-entropy probability law on \(E\) with mean \(j\). Define, exactly as in that predecessor,
$$
\rho=\min\lbrace r_{E,j}(x):r_{E,j}(x)>0\rbrace,
\quad \kappa=\max(3,\rho^{-1}),
\quad D_0=\log\frac1{\eta_{\mathrm{prod}}\rho},
$$
$$
\alpha=\frac1{8\kappa},\qquad
\beta=\sqrt{8\kappa}\,D_0,\qquad
g=\min\left(\frac{27}{16},1+\frac{4^{r_T}\eta_{\mathrm{prod}}^3}{4}\right)>1.
$$
The minimum defining \(\rho\) ranges over all the stated face supports, their sample points, and their positive atoms. These are finite fixed-support parameters. Put
$$
K_{>}=1+\frac{\alpha^{-1}\exp(\beta^2/\log g)}{1-g^{-1/4}},
\qquad c_{m,\mathrm{partition}}=\frac1{16K_{>}^3}.
\tag{4}
$$
All logarithms are natural.

For the standard recursive partition of accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c, its bound \(t_7\ge130>128\) gives (2) with \(m=7\) for all the displayed schedules and fields. More generally, each fixed standard \(m\ge7\) satisfies \(t_m>2^m\) and hence has the same conclusion with its own constant (4). No exact enumeration of \(T_7\) is required. The case \(t_m=2^m\) is excluded from this theorem.

## Proof

We first check the exact lift and its full normalizer. With \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m\), accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d gives
$$
T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},
\qquad
\mathcal L_0=B_m^Q,
\qquad
\mathcal L_r=T_m^{r-1}\times E_m\times B_m^{Q-r}
\quad(1\le r\le Q),
\tag{5}
$$
as a disjoint exhaustive decomposition of \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) by last-spike index. It also gives \(P_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\subseteq[0,2]^\nu\) and
$$
1\le e_m=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}<2^m.
\tag{6}
$$
Every block of an integer support point is Boolean or has exactly one coordinate equal to two and all other coordinates Boolean. A Boolean coordinate contributes at most zero to the maximum defining \(\phi\). A coordinate equal to two in block \(j\) contributes \(a_j\). Consequently, monotonicity of the schedule gives \(\phi(x)=a_{\ell(x)}\), including \(\ell(x)=0\). Conversely an integer point of the lift has its spatial coordinate in \(A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\). This proves (1). The lift is convex and compact because \(\phi\) is a maximum of finitely many affine functions and the spatial polytope is compact. On that polytope, \(0\le\phi\le k\), so each spatial support point has a nonempty column. When \(k=0\), every \(a_j\) is zero and every column consists exactly of its height-zero atom; the argument does not require a positive column length or full dimension of the lift.

For \(X\in\lbrace B_m,E_m,T_m\rbrace\), use the predecessor's block sum
$$
Z_X(\gamma)=\sum_{z\in X}e^{\gamma\cdot z},
\qquad
L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)=\sum_{t=a_r}^k e^{\zeta t}.
$$
The latter is the height-tail notation used in accepted fact cdf4b723cb4dc8ec, now with the present entrance schedule. Every such height sum is positive. Summing exactly on (5), without removing any original atom, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
W={}&L_0^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)\prod_{j=1}^Q Z_{B_m}(\gamma_j)\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^Q L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta)
 \left(\prod_{j<r}Z_{T_m}(\gamma_j)\right)
 Z_{E_m}(\gamma_r)
 \left(\prod_{j>r}Z_{B_m}(\gamma_j)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Here \(W\) is the full lifted normalizer; the unlifted normalizer in 6fee4107100a027d is obtained by omitting the height sums. Empty products in (5) and (7) have one element and value one, respectively.

We will use two elementary conditional moment facts, retaining the original ambient halfspaces throughout. The closed-tail estimate proved in cdf4b723cb4dc8ec applies to any probability law \(q\) on a finite ordered set of heights. Writing
$$
d_q(t)=\min\left(\sum_{s\le t}q_s,\sum_{s\ge t}q_s\right),
$$
it states
$$
\sum_t q_t d_q(t)^3\ge\frac1{128}.
\tag{8}
$$
Indeed, the heights whose lower closed tail has mass less than \(1/4\) form an initial segment of mass less than \(1/4\). The heights whose upper closed tail has mass less than \(1/4\) form a final segment of mass less than \(1/4\). Outside their union, which has mass at most \(1/2\), both tails have mass at least \(1/4\). This proves (8), including a singleton height law.

First consider any finite spatial support with \(K\) nonempty columns and any probability law on those columns. Write its law as \(r_xq_x(t)\), where \(r_x\) is the complete column mass and \(q_x\) its conditional height law. A closed halfspace containing \((x,t)\) intersects that same vertical column in a closed upper tail, a closed lower tail, or the whole column, and its intersection contains the sample. Thus
$$
\tau(x,t)\ge r_xd_{q_x}(t),\qquad
M_3\ge\frac1{128}\sum_xr_x^4\ge\frac1{128K^3}.
\tag{9}
$$
The last step is finite Hölder and \(\sum_xr_x=1\). Different columns may have different height laws in (9).

Second, suppose a class has the product law \(\sigma_xq_t\) on its spatial support times a common height set. Let \(\tau_\sigma(x)\) be its actual spatial closed-halfspace depth, computed in the original spatial ambient space. For any containing ambient halfspace, move its boundary parallel to itself through \((x,t)\), leaving a contained halfspace. Write the resulting halfspace as
$$
a\cdot(y-x)+b(s-t)\ge0.
$$
It contains the Cartesian product of the two events \(a\cdot(y-x)\ge0\) and \(b(s-t)\ge0\). Their probabilities are at least \(\tau_\sigma(x)\) and \(d_q(t)\), respectively. A zero block normal gives the whole corresponding support and causes no exception. Independence and (8) therefore give
$$
\tau_{\sigma\otimes q}(x,t)\ge\tau_\sigma(x)d_q(t),
\qquad
M_3(\sigma\otimes q)\ge\frac1{128}M_3(\sigma).
\tag{10}
$$
No sign restriction on \(\zeta\), no height-normalizer estimate, and no top-height restriction have entered (8)--(10).

We also record the exact class transfer, the fourth-power conditional transfer of cdf4b723cb4dc8ec. Partition the original full lifted support into nonempty classes, let \(W_r\) be their complete masses, \(\mu_r=W_r/W\), and let \(p^{[r]}\) be their conditional laws. For a sample in class \(r\), every containing original ambient closed halfspace has full probability at least \(\mu_r\) times its conditional probability. Therefore
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_r\mu_r^4M_3(p^{[r]}).
\tag{11}
$$
There is one power of \(\mu_r\) from the sampling law and three from the cubed depth. In particular, if \(M_3(p^{[r]})\ge8^{-\nu}\vartheta_r\) for positive coefficients \(\vartheta_r\), then Hölder gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge8^{-\nu}\left(\sum_r\vartheta_r^{-1/3}\right)^{-3}.
\tag{12}
$$
To see this, use \(\sum_r\mu_r=1\) and
$$
1\le\left(\sum_r\vartheta_r\mu_r^4\right)^{1/4}
       \left(\sum_r\vartheta_r^{-1/3}\right)^{3/4}.
$$
The notation \(\mu_r\) for class masses and \(\vartheta_r\) for class coefficients leaves \(\alpha,\beta,\kappa\) with exactly their fixed-family meanings above.

Suppose first that \(t_m<2^m\). The exact support cardinality in 6fee4107100a027d is
$$
|A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|
=2^{mQ}+e_m\sum_{r=1}^Q t_m^{r-1}2^{m(Q-r)}.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\frac{|A_{m,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|}{2^{mQ}}
=1+\frac{e_m}{2^m}\sum_{L=0}^{Q-1}(t_m/2^m)^L
\le K_{<}.
\tag{13}
$$
Apply (9) to all original spatial columns at once, with their original conditional height laws and their complete tilted column masses. This is valid even though their entrance heights differ. Equations (9) and (13) give
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-mQ}}{128K_{<}^3}
=\frac1{16K_{<}^3}8^{-(mQ+1)}.
\tag{14}
$$
This proves (2) in the below-threshold case.

For the remaining case \(t_m>2^m\), we establish the spatial class estimates before introducing their height laws. Every point of \(E_m\) is an exposed vertex of \(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\). Here is a direct verification that works for the chosen arbitrary partition. For \(v\in E_m\), let \(i\) be its unique coordinate equal to two, and take the functional vector
$$
b_v=(2m+1)e_i+\sum_{j\ne i}(2v_j-1)e_j.
$$
For another \(v'\in E_m\) with \(v'_i=2\), all other coordinates of both points are Boolean. If \(v'\ne v\), the value of \(b_v\cdot(v-v')\) is their positive Hamming distance outside coordinate \(i\). If \(v'_i\le1\), the coordinate-\(i\) contribution is at least \(2m+1\), and every other coordinate contributes at least \(-2\), so the total is positive. Thus \(v\) is the unique maximizer. Each point of \(B_m\) is similarly exposed by its sign vector \(2v-\mathbf1\). The sum of the separate exposing functionals exposes any chosen site in a Cartesian product of these sets.

We need an exact exposed-fiber observation, rather than an equality assertion for general product depths. Let \(U,V\) be finite supports with positive laws \(P,q\), and suppose a chosen \(v\in V\) is exposed by a functional \(b\), so
$$
b\cdot(v'-v)<0\qquad(v'\in V\setminus\lbrace v\rbrace).
$$
For a target \((u,v)\), restriction of any containing halfspace to the fiber \(U\times\lbrace v\rbrace\) gives a containing halfspace of \(U\), or all of \(U\). Hence
$$
\tau_{P\otimes q}(u,v)\ge q_v\tau_P(u).
$$
Conversely, for a halfspace through \(u\) defined by \(a\cdot(u'-u)\ge0\), the ambient halfspace
$$
a\cdot(u'-u)+M b\cdot(v'-v)\ge0
$$
has precisely that intersection with the fiber at \(v\), and contains no point in any other fiber once \(M\) is sufficiently large. Such a finite \(M\) exists because both supports are finite and the second term is strictly negative off the fiber. Taking the infimum over the spatial halfspaces gives the reverse inequality. Halfspaces through \(u\) suffice, because translating any containing boundary to \(u\) can only decrease the contained support. If \(U\) is the empty Cartesian product, it has one point and depth one by convention; exposing \(v\) directly gives the same result. A singleton total support has depth one in every retained positive-dimensional ambient space. We have proved
$$
\tau_{P\otimes q}(u,v)=q_v\tau_P(u).
\tag{15}
$$
Only the exposed second factor is used in this equality.

For \(L\ge1\), let \(P_L\) be the product of the independently tilted laws on \(T_m\) in blocks \(1,\ldots,L\). Write \(\mathcal M_2(P_L)\) for the actual second depth moment in bf1ff94487296374, and \(M_3(P_L)\) for its analogous third moment. The support \(T_m\) is complete in the sense needed by that fact: \(E_m\subseteq T_m\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\) implies
$$
\operatorname{conv}(T_m)=\operatorname{conv}(E_m),
\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(T_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m.
$$
Its intrinsic rank \(r_T\) satisfies \(r_T\le m\), and
\(t_m>2^m\ge2^{r_T}\). Thus every one of the \(L\) factors is an above-intrinsic-threshold factor in (16) of bf1ff94487296374. With precisely the constants in the statement and without replacing its exponential gain by a uniform product constant, that accepted estimate gives
$$
\mathcal M_2(P_L)
\ge\alpha^2\exp(L\log g-2\beta\sqrt L)\,4^{-r_TL}.
\tag{16}
$$
The predecessor's intrinsic and ambient depth definitions agree: restrictions to the affine hull are halfspaces or the whole support, and intrinsic halfspaces extend to the ambient space. Moreover, for any probability law, Hölder implies
$$
\sum_uP_L(u)\tau_{P_L}(u)^2
\le\left(\sum_uP_L(u)\tau_{P_L}(u)^3\right)^{2/3}.
$$
Raising (16) to the power \(3/2\), and then using \(r_T\le m\), yields
$$
M_3(P_L)\ge
\alpha^3\exp\left(\frac32L\log g-3\beta\sqrt L\right)8^{-mL}.
\tag{17}
$$
For \(L=0\), define \(P_0\) on the empty product to have depth and third moment one. Since \(\kappa\ge3\) implies \(0<\alpha\le1/24<1\), (17) is also valid at \(L=0\). This explicitly covers the empty \(T_m\)-prefix.

Let \(\sigma_r\) be the spatial conditional law on \(\mathcal L_r\) with weights \(\exp(\sum_j\gamma_j\cdot x^{(j)})\). For \(r\ge1\), the Cartesian product in (5) and the separated fields give
$$
\sigma_r=P_{r-1}\otimes q^{[r]},
$$
where \(q^{[r]}\) is the independently tilted law on
\(E_m\times B_m^{Q-r}\). All its sites are exposed, as just proved, and its cardinality is \(e_m2^{m(Q-r)}\). Equation (15) and finite Hölder therefore give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(\sigma_r)
&=M_3(P_{r-1})\sum_v(q^{[r]}_v)^4\\
&\ge M_3(P_{r-1})e_m^{-3}8^{-m(Q-r)}\\
&\ge M_3(P_{r-1})8^{-m(Q-r+1)},
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
where the last line uses (6). With \(L=r-1\), (17)--(18) show
$$
M_3(\sigma_r)\ge
8^{-mQ}\alpha^3
\exp\left(\frac32(r-1)\log g-3\beta\sqrt{r-1}\right).
\tag{19}
$$
For \(r=0\), every point of \(B_m^Q\) is exposed, so its spatial depth equals its atom probability. Hence
$$
M_3(\sigma_0)=\sum_x\sigma_0(x)^4\ge8^{-mQ}.
\tag{20}
$$

Lift each entire stratum to
$$
\widehat{\mathcal L}_r
=\lbrace(x,t):x\in\mathcal L_r,\ a_r\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
These form a nonempty disjoint exhaustive partition of (1). Their masses are exactly the summands of (7). On each class, its full conditional law factors as
$$
p^{[r]}_{(x,t)}=\sigma_r(x)q_r(t),
\qquad q_r(t)=e^{\zeta t}/L_r^{\mathrm{ht}}(\zeta).
$$
This uses the last-spike index in (5); it would not follow merely by counting spikes. Equations (10), (19), and (20) give the valid conditional coefficients
$$
M_3(p^{[r]})\ge8^{-mQ}\vartheta_r,
\qquad
\vartheta_0=\frac1{128},
$$
$$
\vartheta_r=\frac{\alpha^3}{128}
\exp\left(\frac32(r-1)\log g-3\beta\sqrt{r-1}\right)
\qquad(1\le r\le Q).
\tag{21}
$$
Every height atom still occurs in each conditional normalizer. In particular, (21) applies when \(a_r=k\), when several entrance heights coincide, and when \(k=0\).

The inverse cube-root capacities of (21) satisfy
$$
\sum_{r=0}^Q\vartheta_r^{-1/3}
=128^{1/3}\left[1+\alpha^{-1}
\sum_{L=0}^{Q-1}\exp\left(-\frac12L\log g+\beta\sqrt L\right)\right].
\tag{22}
$$
Because \(g>1\) and \(\beta\ge0\), the elementary square inequality gives
$$
\beta\sqrt L\le\frac14L\log g+\frac{\beta^2}{\log g}
\qquad(L\ge0).
$$
It follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{L=0}^{Q-1}\exp\left(-\frac12L\log g+\beta\sqrt L\right)
&\le \exp(\beta^2/\log g)\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}g^{-L/4}\\
&=\frac{\exp(\beta^2/\log g)}{1-g^{-1/4}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
Thus (22) is at most \(128^{1/3}K_{>}\). Apply the fourth-power transfer (11) and its Hölder consequence (12) to the actual class masses from (7). We obtain
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-mQ}}{128K_{>}^3}
=\frac1{16K_{>}^3}8^{-(mQ+1)},
\tag{24}
$$
as required. The exponential dependence on the prefix length in (17) is retained until (23); this is what makes the bound independent of the number of strata.

Finally, accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c supplies, for the standard recursive partition, a certified Boolean family of size \(N_m\), defined by the condition
$$
\max_{0\le j\le m}\left(2\sum_{i=1}^j b_i-j\right)\ge3,
$$
and proves
$$
t_m\ge2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}+N_m,
\qquad N_7=37.
$$
At \(m=7\), the binomial coefficient is 35, so \(t_7\ge128-35+37=130\). This proves the stated seven-coordinate corollary without enumerating the full complete support.

For the optional extension to each fixed standard \(m\ge7\), appending either Boolean bit preserves a prefix witnessing the displayed condition. Thus \(N_{m+1}\ge2N_m\). Put \(c_m^{\mathrm{bin}}=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\), a binomial count distinct from the theorem's constant. The elementary identities
$$
c_{2r+1}^{\mathrm{bin}}=\frac{2r+1}{r+1}c_{2r}^{\mathrm{bin}}
\le2c_{2r}^{\mathrm{bin}},
\qquad
c_{2r+2}^{\mathrm{bin}}=2c_{2r+1}^{\mathrm{bin}}
$$
show \(c_{m+1}^{\mathrm{bin}}\le2c_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\). Hence
$$
N_{m+1}-c_{m+1}^{\mathrm{bin}}
\ge2(N_m-c_m^{\mathrm{bin}}),
$$
and induction from \(37-35=2\) gives
$$
t_m\ge2^m+2^{m-6}>2^m\qquad(m\ge7).
$$
The already proved above-threshold case therefore applies separately to each such fixed block. Neither this induction nor (24) gives a uniform positive lower bound for the constants as \(m\) varies. The equality case \(t_m=2^m\) is not used in either argument. This completes the theorem.
