## Statement

Use the six cyclic rays with endpoints
$$
(P_0,\ldots,P_5)=((A,0),(0,B),(-t,t),(-a,0),(0,-b),(r,-r)),
\qquad (R_0,\ldots,R_5)=(A,B,t,a,b,r)>0.
$$
Indices on rays and sectors are modulo six. Sector $s$ is the cone between $P_s$ and $P_{s+1}$. A feasible datum $(R,W)$ is realized by an ordinary planar convex body with $0$ in its interior, these boundary ray endpoints and sector areas $W_s$.

Fix six positive sector areas $W_s$ for which at least one feasible radius vector exists.

Put $k=\sum_s W_s$ and let $q_1=W_0+W_4+W_5$, $q_2=W_0+W_1+W_2$, $q_3=W_2+W_3+W_4$ be the areas of the caps $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$, $x+y\le0$, respectively. The corresponding coarea chord lengths are $\ell_1=B+b$, $\ell_2=A+a$, $\ell_3=t+r$. Write
$$
M=\sum_{j=1}^3 q_j/\ell_j,\qquad f_j=\frac{2\sqrt{kq_j}}{\ell_jM},
\qquad \Phi_i=f_i+\frac{(f_j+f_l)^2}{4},\quad \{i,j,l\}=\{1,2,3\}.
$$

Set $c_s=1/(2W_s)$ and $h_s=1/(R_sR_{s+1})-c_s$. A core sector has $h_s=0$. Define the symmetric matrix $L$ by $L_{ss}=c_{s-1}+c_s-R_s^{-2}$, $L_{s,s+1}=L_{s+1,s}=-h_s$ and all other off-diagonal entries zero. Its connected blocks are on the components of the graph with edges $s,s+1$ when $h_s>0$.

The feasible radius set is compact, with $(c_{s-1}+c_s)^{-1/2}\le R_s\le 2W_s\sqrt{c_s+c_{s+1}}$, and each selected row $\Phi_i$ attains a maximum. At any local maximum with $W$ fixed, the following hold.

- There are at least two cores. Both endpoints of every chord with positive derivative $\partial_{\ell_j}\Phi_i$ are incident to a core. If all three chord derivatives vanish, every endpoint is incident to a core and there are at least three cores.
- Every positive-definite connected block has at most two nodes. At least two connected blocks are singular, so $\dim\ker L\ge2$.
- If there are exactly two cores, the datum has a unique realizing body. It is a quadrilateral with one vertex strictly inside each of the four positive-excess sectors.

## Proof

The six canonical ray directions have consecutive and next-neighbor determinants equal to one. Thus $D_s=R_sR_{s+1}$ and $E_s=R_{s-1}R_{s+1}$ in the ordinary sector criterion [7d1ee06fa4119930](../facts/7d1ee06fa4119930.md). In its quadratic form $b_s-h_{s-1}-h_s=c_{s-1}+c_s-R_s^{-2}$, and the adjacent off-diagonal entry is $-h_s$. The gaps are strictly less than $\pi$, so the criterion identifies actual feasibility with $h_s\ge0$ for all $s$ and $L\succeq0$. The nonempty compact box bound is the feasible-set part of [82a8acc237f57498](../facts/82a8acc237f57498.md), which concerns exactly these positive fixed sector areas on the canonical rays. Equivalently, each PSD diagonal gives the lower bound, the product at the next sector gives the upper bound, and all inequalities are closed in this positive box. The row is continuous there because the areas and all chords are positive.

### One-coordinate gains

Put $x_j=1/\ell_j$, $\rho_j=\sqrt{q_j/k}$ and $z_j=q_jx_j/M$, so $\sum_j z_j=1$ and $f_j=2z_j/\rho_j$. Vary $x_s$ alone. Then $\tau=z_s$ is strictly increasing in $x_s$ and $z_j=(1-\tau)\bar z_j$ for the two other indices, with fixed positive $\bar z_j$ summing to one. When $s=i$ the row is $2\tau/\rho_i+(1-\tau)^2(\sum_{j\ne i}\bar z_j/\rho_j)^2$, with strictly positive quadratic coefficient. When $s\ne i$, and $l$ is the third index, it is $2(1-\tau)\bar z_i/\rho_i+[\tau/\rho_s+(1-\tau)\bar z_l/\rho_l]^2$. Its quadratic coefficient can vanish only when the derivative in $\tau$ is the strictly negative constant $-2\bar z_i/\rho_i$. Thus whenever the chord derivative is zero, either sufficiently small one-coordinate change in that chord strictly increases the row. For a nonzero derivative the locally improving direction is given by its sign. In particular, if $g=f_j+f_l$ and $H=f_i+g^2/2$, differentiating the weights gives $\partial_{x_i}\Phi_i=(q_i/M)(2/\rho_i-H)$ and $\partial_{x_j}\Phi_i=(q_j/M)(g/\rho_j-H)$ for $j\ne i$.

Write $d_j=\partial_{\ell_j}\Phi_i$. Homogeneity gives $\sum_j\ell_jd_j=0$. Increasing one endpoint radius is feasible up to the minimum of the two incident core bounds by [63f90fd70f747bd0](../facts/63f90fd70f747bd0.md), with three distinct central lines, opposite canonical directions and consecutive determinants one. This increases just its chord. A feasible increase at a local maximum therefore forces $d_j<0$. In particular both endpoints of a chord with $d_j\ge0$ need an incident core. Opposite endpoints cannot share an incident edge, so a positive derivative forces at least two cores. If there is no positive derivative, homogeneity forces all derivatives to vanish and then all six nodes need an incident core, requiring at least three cores.

If $L$ were positive definite, each radius could decrease slightly, improving the two incident core bounds and keeping positive definiteness by openness. At a maximum this would force every $d_j>0$, again impossible. A positive-definite path block with at least three nodes has an internal node with both incident excesses strict. Its radius can change slightly in both directions while the block remains positive definite and the other blocks are unchanged. This contradicts the one-coordinate gain in some direction. There are at least two cores, so all the blocks are paths or singletons.

### Feasible boundary transfers

A separating core product can stay fixed while its radius on one side is decreased by a factor $e^{-\varepsilon}$ and the radius on the other side increased by $e^\varepsilon$. We use this with the decreasing radius in a positive-definite block or in a positive-definite principal complement. Separating edges must stay zero. Within the positive-definite submatrix, openness controls all small changes, including the appearance of an edge at a former core. Decreasing a radius relaxes any core bound internal to that submatrix. Within the other submatrix, the changed entries of $L$ increase. The increased radius is only incident there to edges of strict positive excess in the uses below, so the off-diagonal entries stay nonpositive. If $C'$ is the changed submatrix and $C$ the old one, $v^\top C'v\ge |v|^\top C'|v|\ge |v|^\top C|v|\ge0$. Thus the exact sector criterion supplies an actual realization. The same comparison permits increasing two radii in the singular block simultaneously when both internal core bounds have slack.

### Exactly two cores

Some chord has a positive derivative since the all-zero case needs three cores. Cyclically index the nodes so the positive chord has endpoints $1,4$. This just relabels the adjacent and opposite pairs for the argument. One core is incident to $1$ and one to $4$. Their pairs are $(0,3)$, $(0,4)$, $(1,3)$ and $(1,4)$. The two blocks have sizes $3,3$ or $2,4$. Any block of size at least three is singular by the internal-node argument. In the $2,4$ case, the two nodes of the short block lie on the two chords other than the chosen positive-derivative chord. Each is adjacent across a core to an endpoint of that chosen chord. More explicitly the short blocks are $\{5,0\}$ for cores $(0,4)$ and $\{2,3\}$ for cores $(1,3)$. If the short block were positive definite, each boundary transfer from its endpoint to the chosen chord would force the derivative of that endpoint's chord to be strictly positive. Indeed the first variation for a decrease at $u$ and increase at $v$ is $R_v d_{\operatorname{chord}(v)}-R_u d_{\operatorname{chord}(u)}$, and must be nonpositive. This would make all three derivatives strictly positive. Both blocks are therefore singular.

### At least three cores

There are at most three positive edges, so a unique singular block would have size one through four. Its whole principal complement is positive definite. A decrease at a complement node not adjacent to the singular block leaves all separating zero edges unchanged and is feasible by openness on the complement. Such a decrease forces the corresponding chord derivative to be strictly positive.

- For a singleton at node $s$, decrease separately at $s+2,s+3,s+4$. Their opposite-pair chord labels are all distinct, forcing all three derivatives positive.
- For a block $\{1,2\}$, after cyclic relabeling, separate decreases at $4,5$ force the derivatives of chords $\{1,4\}$ and $\{2,5\}$ positive. The derivative of $\{0,3\}$ is negative by homogeneity. Transfer from node $0$ to $1$ across core $0$, leaving the core at edge $2$ unchanged. Its first variation is strictly positive.
- For a block $\{0,1,2\}$, increasing its internal radius at $1$ is feasible since both incident excesses are strict. Thus the derivative of chord $\{1,4\}$ is negative. A decrease at $4$ in the complement forces that same derivative to be positive.
- For a block $\{0,1,2,3\}$, the internal nodes $1,2$ can separately increase, making the derivatives of chords $\{1,4\}$ and $\{2,5\}$ negative and the derivative of $\{0,3\}$ positive. Increase $R_0,R_3$ by the factor $e^\varepsilon$ and decrease $R_4,R_5$ by $e^{-\varepsilon}$. The separating core products $R_3R_4$ and $R_5R_0$ stay fixed. The core product $R_4R_5$ decreases, and the positive-definite complement tolerates the new off-diagonal entry by openness. The singular block remains PSD by entrywise comparison with its old matrix. All changes contribute positively to the first variation.

Every case contradicts a local maximum. Each singular block contributes a nonzero kernel vector, with disjoint supports for distinct blocks, giving nullity at least two. For the exactly-two-core case, the positive least-eigenvalue vectors of the two connected singular blocks in [2fb8b0785692a5b4](../facts/2fb8b0785692a5b4.md) are null vectors. Their concatenation is positive, so [ffdb2bc33d7a6750](../facts/ffdb2bc33d7a6750.md) applies to the same actual canonical datum, even with the two core equalities. It gives uniqueness of the actual body and exactly four open-sector vertices.
