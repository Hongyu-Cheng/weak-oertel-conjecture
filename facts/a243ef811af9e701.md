## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) and let \(w=(w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be admissible in the exact sense of accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160`: it is nonzero, nonnegative, and finitely supported, and admits a finite-valued globally log-concave extension \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing with every lattice value, including every zero. A zero endpoint makes the right side of the log-concavity inequality zero. Write \(A=\{z:w_z>0\}\), and retain that fact's quantities

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=\sum_z w_z,& M&=\max_z w_z,\\
H_j&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},&
H_*&=\max_{j\in\mathbb Z^n}H_j,\\
G&=\log(2^nH_*/W),& \rho&=2^nM/W.
\end{aligned}
\]

All logarithms are natural. At each positive center \(j\in A\), use exactly the normalization of accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\},&
\mathcal U_j&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\},\\
\Delta_j(v)&=\log\frac{w_j^2}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\quad(v\in\mathcal U_j),\\
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)\quad(L\ge0),&
K(w)&=\max_{j\in A,\ L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The dependence of \(\alpha\) on its displayed center is suppressed as in that predecessor. Every displacement sum is oriented: both nonzero orientations occur, and zero occurs exactly once. Curvature is defined only on positive reflected pairs. The maxima are attained. For \(m\ge1\), let \(w^{\otimes m}_{(z_1,\ldots,z_m)}=\prod_{i=1}^m w_{z_i}\), a table in integer dimension \(mn\).

For every even integer \(m\ge2\),

\[
\frac{H_*^m}{3000n^2m^{3/2}}
\le K(w^{\otimes m})\le H_*^m.
\tag{1}
\]

The upper bound holds for every \(m\ge1\). For every \(m\ge1\), \(K(w^{\otimes(m+1)})\ge M K(w^{\otimes m})\); in particular every odd \(m\ge3\) satisfies

\[
K(w^{\otimes m})\ge
\frac{M H_*^{m-1}}{3000n^2(m-1)^{3/2}}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{m\to\infty}K(w^{\otimes m})^{1/m}&=H_*,\\
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\left(\frac{2^{mn}K(w^{\otimes m})}{W^m}\right)&=G.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

One has \(0\le G\le n\log2\). If \(G=0\), then \(2^{mn}K(w^{\otimes m})/W^m=1\) for every \(m\ge1\). If \(G>0\), this normalized score tends to infinity along all integer powers.

The following moment bounds and sharper estimate are part of the theorem. For each \(j\in A\) and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), define

\[
g_j(v)=\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},\qquad
\mathsf Q_j(v)=g_j(v)/H_j,\qquad
T_j(v)=\tfrac12\log\frac{w_{j+v}}{w_{j-v}}.
\]

Here \(\mathsf Q_j\) is a probability law on integer displacements, not a continuous fiber-midpoint law. At every positive center,

\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}e^{\Delta_j/4}\le4^n,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}\Delta_j\le4n\log4<6n.
\tag{4}
\]

At any center \(j\) with \(H_j=H_*\), one also has

\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}T_j=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}e^{|T_j|}\le\frac{2W}{H_*}\le2^{n+1},\qquad
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}T_j^2\le8n^2.
\tag{5}
\]

Fix such a maximizing center and put \(D=\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}\Delta_j\) and \(V=\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}T_j^2\). Every even \(m\ge2\) satisfies

\[
K(w^{\otimes m})\ge
\frac{H_*^m}{4e\bigl(1+4\sqrt{mV/2}\bigr)(1+4mD)}.
\tag{6}
\]

For the actual-body consequence, let \(n,d\ge1\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). Use its actual weights \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), with empty and lower-dimensional fibers assigned zero, and suppose \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). Let \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{y\in S}h_S(y)\), with closed ambient halfspaces as in the locked problem. Accepted facts `ab57fac07c0d3160` and `03f071403cdfc69f` make the preceding definitions applicable. For Cartesian powers, with integer dimension \(mn\) and continuous dimension \(md\), the resulting quantitative consumer is

\[
\frac{h_{\max}(C^{\times m})}{2^{-mn}W^m}\ge
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{e^{mG}}{36000n^2m^{3/2}},&m\ge2\text{ even},\\[1ex]
\displaystyle\frac{\rho e^{(m-1)G}}{36000n^2(m-1)^{3/2}},&m\ge3\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, for an even \(m\ge2\), the explicit sufficient condition

\[
mG\ge\log(36000/e)+2\log n+\tfrac32\log m
\tag{8}
\]

gives \(h_{\max}(C^{\times m})\ge e^{-1}2^{-mn}W^m\). For an odd \(m\ge3\), the analogous sufficient condition is \((m-1)G+\log\rho\ge\log(36000/e)+2\log n+\tfrac32\log(m-1)\). When \(G=0\), a largest product fiber's centroid gives \(h_{\max}(C^{\times m})\ge e^{-1}2^{-mn}W^m\) for every \(m\ge1\). These are statements with the displayed coefficient \(1/e\).

For comparison with the accepted actual-depth result, put \(p_z=w_z/W\) and use the exact quantity of accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`, equivalently written

\[
E(p)=\max_{j\in A}\inf_{a\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{z\in A}p_z e^{a\cdot(z-j)}.
\]

That fact already gives \(\lim_{m\to\infty}(h_{\max}(C^{\times m})/W^m)^{1/m}=E(p)\ge H_*/W\), as well as its stronger eventual coefficient for fixed normalized tables even when factor shapes and continuous dimensions vary. Equation (3) determines the scalar rate \(H_*/W\) after normalization; equality with \(E(p)\) is not required and can fail. Thus (7)-(8) retain the explicit quantitative consequence of the new scalar estimate without replacing the accepted actual-depth theorem. If \(W=0\), the requested depth lower bound is zero and follows at every point of each nonempty product mixed set, without any of the positive-mass normalizations.

## Proof

First, global log-concavity gives \(\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\le w_j\) at every integer \(j,v\). Thus any positive reflected product has a positive integer center. At \(j\in A\), the set \(\mathcal U_j\) is finite, even, and contains zero; \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\), \(\Delta_j(-v)=\Delta_j(v)\), and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). Also \(H_j\ge w_j>0\). If \(j\notin A\), then \(H_j=0\). Hence \(H_*\) is a positive maximum over finitely many centers. For any cutoff \(L\ge0\), the largest curvature value \(t\le L\) exists because zero is available. The numerator is unchanged at \(t\) and its denominator is no larger. This reduces every cutoff maximization to a finite set and proves attainment, including all zero-curvature cases. It is the same argument and normalization as in accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f`.

For completeness, the Hellinger bounds of accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160` follow here from the same-parity bijection. The map \((j,v)\mapsto(j+v,j-v)\) bijects integer center-displacement pairs with ordered integer endpoint pairs of equal parity, including diagonal pairs. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{-n}W^2
&\le\sum_{b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}
\left(\sum_{z\equiv b\ (2)}w_z\right)^2\\
&=\sum_{j,v}w_{j+v}w_{j-v}
\le\sum_j w_jH_j\le WH_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz for the \(2^n\) parity masses; the next inequality multiplies midpoint log-concavity by its nonnegative geometric-mean term. A separate Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on the two reflected square-root sequences gives \(H_j\le W\). Consequently \(2^{-n}W\le H_*\le W\), giving the stated range of \(G\).

Multiplying the global extensions on separate coordinate blocks gives a finite-valued globally log-concave extension of every product table. If either endpoint product vanishes, the log-concavity inequality is automatic; otherwise multiply the factor inequalities. This also verifies the required zeros at every lattice tuple. The finite sums factor, so \(W(w^{\otimes m})=W^m\), \(M(w^{\otimes m})=M^m\), and

\[
H_{(j_1,\ldots,j_m)}(w^{\otimes m})=\prod_{i=1}^m H_{j_i},
\qquad H_*(w^{\otimes m})=H_*^m.
\tag{15}
\]

These identities agree exactly with accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160`, including its \(G\) and \(\rho\) tensor identities. At every positive product center and every cutoff, each retained minimum is at most its geometric mean and the denominator is at least one. Enlarging to all reflected displacements and applying (15) proves \(K(w^{\otimes m})\le H_*^m\) for every \(m\ge1\).

We next prove the moment estimate at an arbitrary positive center \(j\). Extend \(g_j\) from its lattice definition to real arguments by \(g_j(x)=\sqrt{f(j+x)f(j-x)}\). This function is even and globally log-concave, with \(g_j(0)=w_j\) and \(0\le g_j(x)\le w_j\), the last assertion being the midpoint inequality for \(f\). Define \(\phi_j(x)=-\log(g_j(x)/w_j)\) where \(g_j(x)>0\), and \(\phi_j(x)=+\infty\) elsewhere. It is an even, convex, nonnegative extended-real function with \(\phi_j(0)=0\). At every lattice point in \(\mathcal U_j\), \(\Delta_j(v)=2\phi_j(v)\).

For \(t\ge0\), set \(N_j(t)=|\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\phi_j(v)\le t\}|\). These sets are finite because a positive lattice value of \(g_j\) requires both endpoints to belong to the finite support of \(w\). In each nonempty residue class modulo four within \(\{\phi_j\le2t\}\), choose one point \(a\). For every other point \(v\) of that same class, \((v-a)/4\) is an integer vector and

\[
\phi_j((v-a)/4)
\le\tfrac14\phi_j(v)+\tfrac14\phi_j(-a)+\tfrac12\phi_j(0)
\le t.
\]

This uses convexity at the displayed convex combination, and evenness for \(-a\). The map is injective within its residue class, also sending the chosen point to zero. There are at most \(4^n\) classes, so \(N_j(2t)\le4^nN_j(t)\) for every \(t\ge0\), including \(t=0\). No injectivity between different residue classes is asserted or needed.

For \(s>0\), put \(Z_j(s)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}e^{-s\phi_j(v)}\), with \(e^{-s\infty}=0\). There are finitely many nonzero summands. Integrating each summand's indicator of its threshold gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_j(1/2)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}N_j(2t)\,dt\\
&\le4^n\int_0^\infty e^{-t}N_j(t)\,dt
=4^n Z_j(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Here \(Z_j(1)=H_j/w_j>0\), and \(\mathsf Q_j(v)=e^{-\phi_j(v)}/Z_j(1)\) on its finite support. Consequently \(\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}e^{\Delta_j/4}=Z_j(1/2)/Z_j(1)\le4^n\). Jensen's inequality gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}\Delta_j\le4n\log4\). Since \(\log2<3/4\), this is less than \(6n\). The elementary logarithm inequality follows, for example, by integrating the strictly convex function \(1/x\) under its chord on \([1,2]\). This establishes (4) at every positive center, without any bound on the support cardinality.

Now fix \(j\) with \(H_j=H_*\). The law \(\mathsf Q_j\) is even and \(T_j\) is odd, so its mean is zero. At each \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), \(g_j(v)e^{|T_j(v)|}=\max\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\). Each of the maps \(v\mapsto j+v\) and \(v\mapsto j-v\) is injective. Hence

\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_j}e^{|T_j|}
=\frac1{H_*}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\max\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}
\le\frac{2W}{H_*}\le2^{n+1}.
\tag{17}
\]

Put \(a=2W/H_*\), so \(2\le a\le2^{n+1}\). Markov's inequality implies \(\mathbb P_{\mathsf Q_j}(|T_j|>t)\le\min\{1,ae^{-t}\}\) for every \(t\ge0\). Integrating the finite-support second-moment identity yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
V
&=\int_0^\infty 2t\,\mathbb P_{\mathsf Q_j}(|T_j|>t)\,dt\\
&\le\int_0^{\log a}2t\,dt+\int_{\log a}^\infty2tae^{-t}\,dt\\
&=(\log a)^2+2\log a+2\\
&\le\tfrac94n^2+3n+2\le\tfrac{29}{4}n^2<8n^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

The penultimate inequality uses \(\log a\le(n+1)\log2<3n/2\) for \(n\ge1\); the last uses \(n\le n^2\) and \(1\le n^2\). Together with \(D\le6n\) from (4), this proves all of (5).

To prove (6), let \(m=2r\) with \(r\ge1\). Draw one block \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\) independently with law \(\mathsf Q_j\), and set \(U=\sum_{i=1}^rT_j(v_i)\) and \(Z=\sum_{i=1}^r\Delta_j(v_i)\). These random variables satisfy \(\mathbb EU=0\), \(\mathbb EU^2=rV\), \(Z\ge0\), and \(\mathbb EZ=rD\). If \(D>0\), Markov gives \(\mathbb P(Z>4rD)\le1/4\); if \(D=0\), then \(Z=0\) almost surely. If \(V>0\), Chebyshev gives \(\mathbb P(|U|>2\sqrt{rV})\le1/4\); if \(V=0\), then \(U=0\) almost surely. Thus \(\mathcal A=\{Z\le4rD,\ |U|\le2\sqrt{rV}\}\) has probability at least \(1/2\) in every case.

Partition \([-2\sqrt{rV},2\sqrt{rV}]\) into \(B\) disjoint intervals of diameter at most one, where \(B\le1+4\sqrt{rV}\). For positive length use \(B=\max\{1,\lceil4\sqrt{rV}\rceil\}\), with shared boundary points assigned to exactly one interval. For zero length use the single set \(\{0\}\). If \(p_\ell^{\mathrm{bin}}\) is the probability that \(\mathcal A\) holds and \(U\) lies in the \(\ell\)-th interval, then \(\sum_{\ell=1}^B p_\ell^{\mathrm{bin}}\ge1/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives \(\sum_{\ell=1}^B (p_\ell^{\mathrm{bin}})^2\ge1/(4B)\).

Take an independent second block \(u_1,\ldots,u_r\) with the same law and corresponding sums \(U',Z'\). The event that both blocks satisfy \(\mathcal A\) and their imbalance sums lie in the same interval has probability \(\sum_\ell(p_\ell^{\mathrm{bin}})^2\ge1/(4B)\). On this event the product displacement \((v_1,\ldots,v_r,-u_1,\ldots,-u_r)\) has total imbalance \(U-U'\) of absolute value at most one and total curvature \(Z+Z'\le8rD=4mD\). Because \(\mathsf Q_j\) is even, the law of this product displacement is exactly \(\mathsf Q_j^{\otimes m}\). In particular the sign reversal does not change the probability weights or the counting convention.

At the repeated positive center \((j,\ldots,j)\), positive reflected product displacements are exactly the tuples in \(\mathcal U_j^m\). Write \(\alpha^{(m)}\) for the product table's canonical minimum endpoint weight at this center. If a positive tuple is denoted \((v_1,\ldots,v_m)\), direct multiplication gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{(j,\ldots,j)}(v_1,\ldots,v_m)
&=\sum_{i=1}^m\Delta_j(v_i),\\
\alpha^{(m)}(v_1,\ldots,v_m)
&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^m g_j(v_i)\right)
e^{-\left|\sum_{i=1}^mT_j(v_i)\right|}\\
&=H_*^m\left(\prod_{i=1}^m\mathsf Q_j(v_i)\right)
e^{-\left|\sum_{i=1}^mT_j(v_i)\right|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

The second equality is the identity \(\min\{a,b\}=\sqrt{ab}\,e^{-|\log(a/b)|/2}\) for positive endpoint products. Choose the cutoff \(L=4mD\), which remains allowed when \(D=0\). On the selected event, (19) contributes at least its probability weight times \(e^{-1}H_*^m\). All remaining cutoff terms are nonnegative. Therefore

\[
K(w^{\otimes m})
\ge\frac{H_*^m}{4eB(1+4mD)}
\ge\frac{H_*^m}{4e(1+4\sqrt{mV/2})(1+4mD)},
\]

proving (6), including zero moments and all interval or cutoff boundaries. From \(V\le8n^2\), \(D\le6n\), and \(m,n\ge1\), we have \(1+4\sqrt{mV/2}\le1+8n\sqrt m\le9n\sqrt m\) and \(1+4mD\le1+24mn\le25mn\). Thus the denominator in (6) is at most \(900e n^2m^{3/2}<3000n^2m^{3/2}\), using \(e<3\). This proves the lower bound in (1).

For the odd-power comparison, take a center and cutoff attaining \(K(w^{\otimes m})\), and adjoin a maximum-weight center in one further block. Restrict the final displacement to zero. Every retained term's minimum is multiplied by \(M\), and its curvature and cutoff denominator are unchanged because the new curvature is zero. The product score also includes nonnegative terms with a nonzero final displacement. Consequently \(K(w^{\otimes(m+1)})\ge M K(w^{\otimes m})\) for every \(m\ge1\). Combining this with (1) gives (2); no zeroth tensor power is needed.

All scores are positive because a maximum-weight center's zero displacement gives \(K(w^{\otimes m})\ge M^m>0\). Taking logarithms of (1) along the even integers shows \(m^{-1}\log K(w^{\otimes m})\to\log H_*\) there. Equation (2) gives the same lower logarithmic limit along the odd integers, since \(M,H_*>0\) are fixed and \(\log m/m\to0\); the all-power upper bound gives the matching upper limit. This proves the first equality in (3). Adding \(n\log2-\log W\) proves the second.

For \(G>0\), normalizing (1) gives the lower bound \(e^{mG}/(3000n^2m^{3/2})\) on even powers, and normalizing (2) gives \(\rho e^{(m-1)G}/(3000n^2(m-1)^{3/2})\) on odd powers. The constant \(\rho\) is positive. Both bounds tend to infinity. If \(G=0\), the equality characterization of accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160` gives a uniform support with exactly one point in every parity class and \(M=2^{-n}W\). Two positive reflected endpoints have the same parity, hence must be the same point; their displacement is zero. A product support also contains exactly one point of every product parity class, and its weights are uniformly \(M^m\). Thus its cutoff numerator at a positive center is \(M^m\) for all \(L\ge0\), its maximum is attained at \(L=0\), and \(K(w^{\otimes m})=M^m=(2^{-n}W)^m\). This proves the exact zero-gain case for every power.

For an actual body, the product fiber at \((z_1,\ldots,z_m)\) is the Cartesian product of the factor fibers, so its \(md\)-dimensional volume is \(\prod_i w_{z_i}\). This holds also for empty and lower-dimensional fibers: a zero-volume factor makes the product volume zero. Compactness gives only finitely many relevant lattice bases, and the total mixed volume factors as \(W^m\). Accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160` supplies the global-extension property for the actual table. Apply the actual-depth consumer of accepted fact `03f071403cdfc69f` in dimensions \(mn,md\), giving one point of depth at least \(K(w^{\otimes m})/12\) against every containing closed ambient halfspace. This consumer already includes vertical halfspaces and whole fibers on the boundary. Equations (1)-(2) and the exact definition \(\rho=2^nM/W\) now prove (7). Rearranging the even bound to make its coefficient at least \(1/e\) gives (8), and rearranging the odd bound gives the stated odd threshold. When \(G=0\), the product maximum fiber has volume \(M^m=2^{-mn}W^m\), and the largest-fiber centroid statement in accepted fact `ab57fac07c0d3160`, applied in continuous dimension \(md\), gives depth at least \(M^m/e\).

The definition of \(E(p)\) is the exact exponential infimum in accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`: its \(I(j)\) is the negative logarithm of the inner infimum, so its \(e^{-\min_j I(j)}\) is the displayed maximum. Its normalization map is \(H_*(p)=H_*(w)/W\), and its normalized depth \(\mathcal D\) equals \(h_{\max}/W\) here by attainment.
