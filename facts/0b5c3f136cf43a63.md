## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
\[
w_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0.
\]
Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq \frac W4
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(z_0\), and assume
\[
\max_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W4.
\]
For each \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^2\\z\equiv r\pmod 2}}w_z,
\qquad
Q^\circ:=\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}W_r^2
-\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z^2.
\]
If
\[
Q^\circ\leq\frac{16-e}{256}W^2,
\]
then there exists \(x_0\in C_{z_0}\) such that
\[
h_S((z_0,x_0))\geq\frac W{64}.
\]
Consequently, every \(y_0\in C_{z_0}\) that maximizes \(h_S((z_0,y))\) over \(y\in C_{z_0}\) satisfies
\[
h_S((z_0,y_0))\geq\frac W{64}.
\]

## Proof

Let
\[
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\},\qquad
\pi_z:=\frac{w_z}{W}\quad(z\in D).
\]
The set \(D\) is finite because the projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^2\) is bounded. Moreover,
\[
\sum_{z\in D}\pi_z=1,\qquad 0<\pi_z<\frac14\quad(z\in D).
\]
Set
\[
a:=\frac e{64},\qquad
L:=\{z\in D:\pi_z<a\},\qquad
\lambda:=\sum_{z\in L}\pi_z,
\]
and, for each parity class \(r\), set
\[
P_r:=\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\z\equiv r\pmod2}}\pi_z.
\]
There are exactly four parity classes and \(\sum_rP_r=1\), so Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\sum_rP_r^2\geq\frac14.
\tag{1}
\]

We claim that \(\lambda<1/4\). Suppose instead that \(\lambda\geq1/4\). Then \(L\neq\varnothing\), and the strict definition of \(L\) gives
\[
\sum_{z\in L}\pi_z^2<a\sum_{z\in L}\pi_z=a\lambda.
\tag{2}
\]
The strict singleton hypothesis gives \(\pi_z^2<(1/4)\pi_z\) for every \(z\in D\). In particular, whether or not \(D\setminus L\) is empty,
\[
\sum_{z\in D\setminus L}\pi_z^2
\leq\frac14\sum_{z\in D\setminus L}\pi_z
=\frac14(1-\lambda).
\tag{3}
\]
The inequality in (2) is strict, so adding (2) and (3) yields
\[
\sum_{z\in D}\pi_z^2
<a\lambda+\frac14(1-\lambda).
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(W_r=WP_r\) and \(w_z=W\pi_z\), equations (1) and (4) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Q^\circ}{W^2}
&=\sum_rP_r^2-\sum_{z\in D}\pi_z^2\\
&>\frac14-a\lambda-\frac14(1-\lambda)\\
&=\lambda\left(\frac14-a\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
For \(k\geq2\), one has \(k!\geq2^{k-1}\), with strict inequality for \(k\geq3\). Thus the exact series estimate
\[
e=2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
<2+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{2^j}=3<16
\]
shows that \(1/4-a>0\). Hence \(\lambda\geq1/4\) and (5) give
\[
\frac{Q^\circ}{W^2}
>\frac14\left(\frac14-\frac e{64}\right)
=\frac{16-e}{256},
\]
contrary to the assumed non-strict upper bound. Therefore
\[
\lambda<\frac14.
\tag{6}
\]

We next verify the exact marginal-depth hypothesis needed below. First,
\[
z_0\in\operatorname{conv}(D).
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, otherwise strict separation of \(z_0\) from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) would give \(u\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}\) such that
\[
u^\top(z-z_0)<0\qquad(z\in D).
\]
The proper closed halfspace
\[
Q_u:=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
would contain \(z_0\) but no point of \(D\), and hence would have total fiber mass zero, contradicting the assumed lower bound \(W/4>0\). This proves (7).

Define the normalized marginal closed-halfspace depth
\[
\tau:=
\inf_{u\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}}
\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0}}\pi_z.
\]
For each nonzero \(u\), the set \(Q_u\) above is a proper closed halfspace containing \(z_0\). Dividing the registered marginal hypothesis by \(W\) therefore gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0}}\pi_z\geq\frac14.
\]
Taking the infimum over nonzero \(u\) yields
\[
\tau\geq\frac14.
\tag{8}
\]
With the strict convention in the definition of \(L\),
\[
L_\pi(a):=\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\\pi_z<a}}\pi_z
=\lambda<\frac14\leq\tau
\tag{9}
\]
by (6) and (8).

Accepted fact `a02e76f89b3b8062` applies to (7)--(9). Its capacity/convolution conclusion states that \(L_\pi(a)<\tau\) implies
\[
A_\pi(z_0)\geq a,
\]
and supplies a nonempty set \(I\subseteq D\) with \(|I|\leq3\), positive coefficients \(q_z\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{z\in I}q_z=1,\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}q_zz=z_0,
\]
and, writing \(c_z\) for the centroid of \(C_z\), a point
\[
p_I:=\left(z_0,\sum_{z\in I}q_zc_z\right)
\]
such that
\[
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p_I\in H}}
\mu_C(H)
\geq\frac We A_\pi(z_0)
\geq\frac We\,\frac e{64}
=\frac W{64}.
\tag{10}
\]
Here
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in D}\mathcal H_d
\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}.
\]
Each positive-volume compact convex fiber \(C_z\), \(z\in I\), contains its centroid \(c_z\). Thus \((z,c_z)\in C\), and convexity together with \(\sum q_zz=z_0\) shows directly that
\[
p_I=\sum_{z\in I}q_z(z,c_z)\in C
\quad\text{and}\quad
p_I=(z_0,x_0)
\]
for \(x_0:=\sum_{z\in I}q_zc_z\in C_{z_0}\). This verifies that the point furnished by (10) lies in the actual \(z_0\)-fiber.

For every lattice fiber outside \(D\), \(\mathcal H_d(C_z)=0\), so its intersection with any halfspace also has zero \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure. By the definition of mixed-integer volume, it follows that
\[
\mu_C(H)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\]
for every closed halfspace \(H\). Therefore (10) is exactly
\[
h_S((z_0,x_0))\geq\frac W{64}.
\]
Finally, if \(y_0\in C_{z_0}\) is any maximizer of \(h_S((z_0,y))\) over that fiber, then its defining maximality and the membership \(x_0\in C_{z_0}\) give
\[
h_S((z_0,y_0))
\geq h_S((z_0,x_0))
\geq\frac W{64}.
\]
