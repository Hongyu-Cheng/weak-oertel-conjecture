## Statement

For \(\mathbf x=(x_1,\ldots,x_{10})\in\mathbb R^{10}\), define twelve affine functions by

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_i(\mathbf x)&=1+x_i &&(1\le i\le10),\\
f_{11}(\mathbf x)&=1-\frac{33}{32}x_1-\frac{55}{32}x_2
 -\frac54\sum_{i=3}^{10}x_i,\\
f_{12}(\mathbf x)&=1-\frac{55}{32}x_1-\frac{33}{32}x_2
 -\frac54\sum_{i=3}^{10}x_i.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(P=\{\mathbf x:f_i(\mathbf x)\ge0\text{ for }1\le i\le12\}\) and \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^{10}\). Then \(P\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope with exactly these twelve facets, every \(f_i(\mathbf0)=1\), and \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^{10}=\{\mathbf0\}\).

For every two-row matrix \(G\) whose rows form a basis of the linear relations among \(\nabla f_1,\ldots,\nabla f_{12}\), the unique unordered pair maximizing \(\lvert\det(G_a,G_b)\rvert\) is \(\{1,2\}\). For this pair, the occupied bin \(\mathbf k=(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,12,12)\) of the selected slacks \(f_3,\ldots,f_{12}\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D:\\
 (\lfloor f_3(\mathbf I)\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor f_{12}(\mathbf I)\rfloor)=\mathbf k}}
2^{-\sum_{i=3}^{12}(f_i(\mathbf I)-\lfloor f_i(\mathbf I)\rfloor)}
\ge2^{1/4}>1.
\]

In addition, \(B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^{10}}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}<4^{10}\).

## Proof

Write \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\), and let \(\mathbf e_i\) be the standard basis vectors of \(\mathbb R^{10}\). The integral translation \(\mathbf X=\mathbf x+\mathbf1\) identifies \(P\) with

\[
Q=\left\{\mathbf X\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{10}:
\begin{aligned}
33X_1+55X_2+40\sum_{i=3}^{10}X_i&\le440,\\
55X_1+33X_2+40\sum_{i=3}^{10}X_i&\le440
\end{aligned}\right\}.
\]

All coefficients in the two upper inequalities are positive, so \(Q\) is bounded. The point \(\mathbf X=\mathbf1\) strictly satisfies all twelve inequalities, since both upper left sides equal \(408<440\). Thus \(P\) and \(Q\) have dimension ten.

The complete vertex set of \(Q\) is

\[
\left\{\mathbf0,\ 8\mathbf e_1,\ 8\mathbf e_2,
 5(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2),
 11\mathbf e_i\ (3\le i\le10)\right\}.
\]

Here is a direct verification that the list is complete. A feasible point with at least three positive coordinates has a nonzero direction supported on those coordinates that annihilates both upper left sides. Sufficiently small displacements in both signs of that direction remain feasible, so such a point is not a vertex. At a vertex with exactly two positive coordinates, both upper inequalities must be tight; otherwise there is a nonzero direction on those coordinates preserving the active upper inequality, if any, and sufficiently small displacements in both signs are again feasible. Subtracting the two tight upper equations gives \(X_1=X_2\). Consequently a support consisting of one index in \(\{1,2\}\) and one index in \(\{3,\ldots,10\}\) is impossible. A support of two indices at least three has a direction exchanging those coordinates and is not a vertex. The only remaining two-coordinate support is \(\{1,2\}\), where the equations give \(X_1=X_2=5\). On a one-coordinate support, the nonzero endpoint is \(8\mathbf e_1\), \(8\mathbf e_2\), or \(11\mathbf e_i\), as stated. A point strictly between zero and that endpoint is not a vertex. Finally, every listed point is a vertex: at zero all ten lower equations are active and independent; at each one-coordinate endpoint nine lower equations and one upper equation are independent; and at \(5(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2)\) the eight lower equations and the two upper equations are independent. This proves the exact vertex description. Translating the displayed integer vertices by \(-\mathbf1\) proves that \(P\) is a lattice polytope for the standard lattice.

Every defining inequality is a facet. The face \(X_1=0\) contains \(\mathbf0,8\mathbf e_2,11\mathbf e_3,\ldots,11\mathbf e_{10}\), ten affinely independent points; the face \(X_2=0\) has the analogous list. For \(3\le i\le10\), the face \(X_i=0\) contains zero and the nine independent vectors \(8\mathbf e_1,8\mathbf e_2,11\mathbf e_j\) with \(3\le j\le10\) and \(j\ne i\). The first upper face contains \(8\mathbf e_2,5(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2),11\mathbf e_3,\ldots,11\mathbf e_{10}\). Subtracting \(8\mathbf e_2\) gives the nine independent vectors \(5\mathbf e_1-3\mathbf e_2\) and \(11\mathbf e_j-8\mathbf e_2\) for \(3\le j\le10\). The second upper face is obtained by interchanging the first two coordinates and also has dimension nine. The twelve defining hyperplanes are distinct, and these inequalities already describe the whole polytope, so there are exactly twelve facets.

The origin has all twelve slacks equal to one. If \(\mathbf x\) is an interior standard lattice point, the lower inequalities give \(x_i>-1\), hence \(x_i\ge0\) for every \(i\). If \(\mathbf x\ne\mathbf0\), at least one coordinate is a positive integer. Since each coefficient in \(1-f_{11}\) is at least \(33/32>1\), this would give \(f_{11}(\mathbf x)\le1-33/32<0\). Therefore the origin is the only interior lattice point.

The first ten gradients are the standard basis vectors, so the relation space of all twelve gradients has dimension two. A basis is given by the rows of the matrix whose columns are

\[
G_1=\begin{pmatrix}33/32\\55/32\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G_2=\begin{pmatrix}55/32\\33/32\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G_i=\begin{pmatrix}5/4\\5/4\end{pmatrix}\ (3\le i\le10),\qquad
G_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Indeed, the first row records \(\frac{33}{32}\nabla f_1+\frac{55}{32}\nabla f_2+\frac54\sum_{i=3}^{10}\nabla f_i+\nabla f_{11}=\mathbf0\); the second row gives the same identity with the first two coefficients interchanged and \(f_{12}\) in place of \(f_{11}\). Their entries in columns eleven and twelve show that the rows are independent.

For the first two columns, \(\lvert\det(G_1,G_2)\rvert=(55^2-33^2)/32^2=121/64\).

Every other absolute column determinant belongs to \(\{0,55/64,33/32,55/32,5/4,1\}\), as follows by pairing the five displayed column types. All these numbers are strictly less than \(121/64\). Hence \(\{1,2\}\) is the unique maximizing pair. Any other relation-row basis replaces \(G\) by \(MG\) for an invertible two-by-two real matrix \(M\), multiplying every determinant by the same nonzero factor \(\det M\). Uniqueness of the maximizing pair therefore holds for every permitted matrix in the statement.

The two omitted slacks are explicitly

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=5-\frac5{11}\sum_{i=3}^{10}f_i+\frac6{11}f_{11}-\frac{10}{11}f_{12},\\
f_2&=5-\frac5{11}\sum_{i=3}^{10}f_i-\frac{10}{11}f_{11}+\frac6{11}f_{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In constant-minus-sum form, each row has eight coefficients \(5/11\), one coefficient \(-6/11\), and one coefficient \(10/11\). Thus every coefficient has absolute value strictly less than one; the negative entries occur in the two different selected upper-slack columns.

Now take the two distinct standard lattice points \(\mathbf I=\mathbf e_1-\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf J=\mathbf e_2-\mathbf1\). Their complete slack values are

\[
\begin{aligned}
(f_1(\mathbf I),f_2(\mathbf I))&=(1,0),&
(f_1(\mathbf J),f_2(\mathbf J))&=(0,1),\\
f_i(\mathbf I)=f_i(\mathbf J)&=0 &&(3\le i\le10),\\
(f_{11}(\mathbf I),f_{12}(\mathbf I))&=(407/32,385/32),&
(f_{11}(\mathbf J),f_{12}(\mathbf J))&=(385/32,407/32).
\end{aligned}
\]

All entries are nonnegative, so both points belong to \(D\). After omitting the unique maximizing pair \(\{1,2\}\), their selected floor vectors are both \(\mathbf k\). The two nonzero selected fractional parts are \(23/32\) and \(1/32\), in opposite orders, and their sum is \(3/4\) at each point. The full bin sum, whose summands are all positive, is therefore at least \(2\cdot2^{-3/4}=2^{1/4}>1\). This is an actual bin of the complete lattice support; no assertion about abstract point configurations or closure of a proposed difference set is needed.

For the capacity calibration, set \(\boldsymbol\theta=-(\log2)\mathbf1\). Each \(\mathbf I\in D\) corresponds to a different nonnegative integer vector \(\mathbf I+\mathbf1\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^{10}\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
&=2^{10}\sum_{\mathbf X\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^{10}}2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{10}X_i}\\
&<2^{10}\sum_{\mathbf X\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{10}}2^{-\sum_{i=1}^{10}X_i}
=2^{10}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\right)^{10}
=4^{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The inequality is strict because \(Q\) is bounded, while the full nonnegative integer grid is infinite and all its summands are positive. Taking the infimum over tilts proves the stated capacity bound and completes the mechanism counterexample.
