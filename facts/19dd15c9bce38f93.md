## Statement

For every integer $m\ge2$ and real $p\in[0,1]$, let
$$
P_m(p)=\Pr\!\left(X+pE_+\ge E_0+\frac{1+p}{m}Y\right),
$$
where $X\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1)$, $Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1)$, and $E_+,E_0$ are mean-one exponentials, all independent. The gamma laws have scale one. Then $P_m(p)\ge\min\{P_m(0),P_m(1)\}$.

## Proof

Fix $m\ge2$ and write $b=(1+p)/m$. Set $T=E_++Y$, $U=E_+/T$, $W=X-E_0$, and $Z=W/T$. The change of variables $(E_+,Y)=(t u,t(1-u))$ has Jacobian $t$ and gives the joint density $t^m e^{-t}(1-u)^{m-1}/(m-1)!$ for $t>0$ and $0<u<1$. Thus $T\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m+1,1)$ and $U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,m)$ are independent. Since $W$ is independent of $(T,U)$, the variable $U$ is independent of $Z$. The event defining $P_m$ is $Z+(p+b)U\ge b$. The beta survival function on $[0,1]$ is $(1-u)^m$, so conditioning on $Z$ gives
$$
P_m(p)=\Pr(Z\ge b)+\int_{-p}^{b}
  \left(\frac{p+z}{p+b}\right)^m f_Z(z)\,dz.
$$
The convolution integral for the density of $W$ is $\int_{\max(0,-w)}^\infty (w+v)e^{-w-2v}\,dv$. This equals $e^w/4$ for $w<0$ and $e^{-w}(1+2w)/4$ for $w\ge0$. Averaging $T f_W(Tz)$ over the gamma density of $T$ gives
$$
f_Z(z)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{m+1}{4(1-z)^{m+2}},&z<0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{(m+1)[1+(2m+5)z]}{4(1+z)^{m+3}},&z\ge0.
\end{cases}
$$
For $0<p<1$, the upper moving-endpoint term in the derivative of the integral cancels the derivative of $\Pr(Z\ge b)$, and the lower term vanishes. The integrand and its parameter derivative are continuous and bounded on the moving ranges for $p$ in each compact subinterval of $(0,1)$. Since $m(p+b)-(m+1)(p+z)=1-(m+1)z$, differentiation gives
$$
P_m'(p)=\frac{I_m(p)}{(p+b)^{m+1}},\qquad
I_m(p)=\int_{-p}^{b}(p+z)^{m-1}[1-(m+1)z]f_Z(z)\,dz .
$$

Put $r=m+1$ and define
$$
A=(2r+3)p^2+(3r+1)p-r,\qquad D=p^2+(3r+1)p+r+2.
$$
In the negative part of $I_m$, use $y=(p+z)/(1-z)$. With $z=(y-p)/(1+y)$ and $dz=(1+p)\,dy/(1+y)^2$, the integral is
$$
\int_0^p \frac{m+1}{4(1+p)^2}
 y^{m-1}[1+(m+1)p-my]\,dy
=\frac{p^m[(m+1)+(2m+1)p]}{4m(1+p)^2}.
$$
In the positive part, use $t=(p+z)/(1+z)$, so that $z=(t-p)/(1-t)$ and $dz=(1-p)\,dt/(1-t)^2$. The transformed integrand is
$$
\frac{m+1}{4(1-p)^3}\,t^{m-1}
 [1+(m+1)p-(m+2)t][1-(2m+5)p+(2m+4)t].
$$
The limits are $p$ and $t_*=(1+rp)/(r+p)$. Indeed $t_*-p=(1-p^2)/(r+p)>0$ and $1-t_*=(r-1)(1-p)/(r+p)>0$. Write the product of the two linear factors as $\sum_{j=0}^2 C_jt^j$ and put $Q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^2 C_jt^j/(m+j)$. The primitive after removing the prefactor is $t^m Q(t)$. Substitution and expansion give
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(t_*)&=-\frac{t_*^2 A}{m(m+1)},\\
\frac{(m+1)+(2m+1)p}{4m(1+p)^2}
-\frac{(m+1)Q(p)}{4(1-p)^3}
&=\frac{p^2D}{m(1-p)^3(1+p)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Combining the two parts therefore yields
$$
I_m(p)=
\frac{L(p)}{4m(1-p)^3(1+p)^2(r+p)^{r+1}},\qquad
L(p)=4p^{r+1}(r+p)^{r+1}D-(1+p)^2(1+rp)^{r+1}A.
$$
The denominators in $I_m$ and $P_m'$ are positive for $0<p<1$, so $P_m'$ has the sign of $L$.

For $p\ge0$, $D>0$ and $A$ is strictly increasing. Since $A(0)=-r$ and $A(1)=4r+4$, there is a unique positive root $\alpha\in(0,1)$ of $A$. Hence $L>0$ for $0<p\le\alpha$. For $p>\alpha$ define
$$
R(p)=4\left(\frac{p(r+p)}{1+rp}\right)^{r+1}\frac{D}{(1+p)^2A}.
$$
On this range $L$ has the sign of $R-1$. The logarithmic derivative is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R'}{R}
&=(r+1)\left(\frac1p+\frac1{r+p}-\frac{r}{1+rp}\right)
+\frac{D'}{D}-\frac2{1+p}-\frac{A'}{A}\\
&=\frac{(p-1)^2K_r(p)}{p(1+p)(r+p)(1+rp)DA},
\end{aligned}
$$
where clearing the denominator and expanding gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_r(p)={}&-r^4-3r^3-2r^2
 +(-3r^4-9r^3-8r^2-4r)p\\
&+(r^4-5r^3-25r^2-9r+2)p^2\\
&+(11r^4-5r^3-21r^2-13r+4)p^3\\
&+(6r^4+4r^3-13r^2-3r)p^4
 +(2r^3+r^2-3r)p^5.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first two coefficients are negative. Writing $r=3+s$ with $s\ge0$, the coefficients of $p^3,p^4,p^5$, respectively, are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&11s^4+127s^3+528s^2+914s+532,\\
&6s^4+76s^3+347s^2+675s+468,\\
&2s^3+19s^2+57s+54 .
\end{aligned}
$$
All three are positive. Regardless of the $p^2$ coefficient, there is one sign change after zeros are omitted. Descartes' rule of signs gives at most one positive root, counting multiplicity. Since the constant coefficient is negative and the leading coefficient is positive, there is one such root $c$, and it is simple.

In the cleared logarithmic derivative, every term vanishes at $p=\alpha$ except the one from $-A'/A$. Thus
$$
(\alpha-1)^2K_r(\alpha)
=-\alpha(1+\alpha)(r+\alpha)(1+r\alpha)D(\alpha)A'(\alpha)<0.
$$
This places $c>\alpha$. The denominator of $R'/R$ is positive on $(\alpha,\infty)$, and the isolated zero from $(p-1)^2$ does not change the sign. Therefore $R$ decreases strictly on $(\alpha,c)$ and increases strictly on $(c,\infty)$.

As $p\downarrow\alpha$, $R(p)\to+\infty$. At $p=1$ the powered ratio is one and $D(1)=A(1)=4r+4$, so $R(1)=1$. If $c\ge1$, then $R>1$ on $(\alpha,1)$. If $c<1$, strict increase from $c$ to $1$ gives $R(c)<1$, so $R$ crosses one exactly once on $(\alpha,c)$ and stays below one from that crossing until $1$. Together with $L>0$ on $(0,\alpha]$, this shows that $P_m$ increases throughout the open interval or increases and then decreases. To extend the conclusion to both endpoints, couple $X,E_+,E_0,Y$ for all $p$. The signed variable is continuous in $p$ for every realization. At any fixed $p$, equality has probability zero by conditioning on all variables except $E_0$. Dominated convergence thus gives continuity of $P_m$ on $[0,1]$, so the minimum is the smaller endpoint value.
