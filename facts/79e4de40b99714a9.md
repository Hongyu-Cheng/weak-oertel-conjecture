## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Suppose that \((x,y)\in C\) implies \(((\varepsilon_i x_i)_{i=1}^n,y)\in C\) for every \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\). Set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), with the original mixed-integer volume \(\mathcal H_d\) and closed-halfspace depth \(h_S\) of the locked problem. Write \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(K_y=\{x:(x,y)\in C\}\), and

\[
N(y)=\#(K_y\cap\mathbb Z^n),\qquad
W=\mathcal H_d(S)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}N(y)\,dy,\qquad
m=\frac1W\int_{\mathbb R^d}yN(y)\,dy.
\]

An empty \(K_y\) gives \(N(y)=0\). Under these hypotheses \(W>0\), \(S\ne\varnothing\), and the true centroid of the original mixed-integer measure is \(\mathbf y^\star=(0_n,m)\in S\). It satisfies

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{W}{2(3+\sqrt2)\sqrt{3^n(1+n\log3)}}
>\frac{1}{32\,2^n}\,\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

All logarithms are natural. In particular \(c_0=1/32\in(0,1/e)\) is independent of \(n,d,C\), and every closed halfspace containing the one selected point has at least \(c_0 2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S)\) of the original volume. In the locked problem's separate branch \(W=0\) and \(S\ne\varnothing\), the requested non-strict inequality is automatic at any point of \(S\); that branch cannot occur for the full-dimensional unconditional bodies in this statement.

## Proof

We use the notation and normalizations of accepted fact ef682ae82d115df6 without changing the integer-fiber weights. In particular, let \(D=\{y:K_y\ne\varnothing\}=C_0\), let \(Q=[-1/2,1/2]^n\), and let

\[
V(y)=\operatorname{vol}_n(K_y+Q),\qquad
\widetilde C=C+(Q\times\{0_d\}),\qquad
\widetilde W=\int_{\mathbb R^d}V(y)\,dy.
\]

Here \(V=0\) for an empty section. Accepted fact ef682ae82d115df6 proves that \(D=C_0\) is a compact convex body, \(N,V\) are measurable, \(N\ge1\) on \(D\), and \(N\le V\le3^nN\) everywhere. Thus \(0<W\le\widetilde W\le3^nW<\infty\). It also proves that \(V/\widetilde W\), extended by zero off \(D\), is a log-concave probability density, that \(m\in C_0\), and that \((0_n,m)\) is the centroid of the original mixed-integer measure. Consequently the point is legal and depends only on \(C\), before choosing a normal. This \(m\) is the original \(N/W\) mean throughout the proof, not the mean of \(V/\widetilde W\).

Put \(M=3^n\). Define a measurable likelihood on \(\mathbb R^d\) by \(\ell(y)=MN(y)/V(y)\) on \(D\), and \(\ell(y)=1\) off \(D\). Division is legitimate on \(D\), since \(V\ge N\ge1\) there. The pointwise comparisons give \(1\le\ell\le M\), and the exact identity \(\ell V=MN\) holds on all of \(\mathbb R^d\). In particular the original continuous marginal law is the normalized reweighting

\[
\frac{N(y)}{W}
=\frac{\ell(y)V(y)/\widetilde W}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\frac{\ell(y)V(y)}{\widetilde W}\,dy
=\frac{MW}{\widetilde W}\in[1,M].
\]

Fix any nonzero \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\). Choose an orthonormal basis of \(\beta^\perp\), represented by the columns of a matrix \(U\in\mathbb R^{d\times(d-1)}\), and parameterize \(y\) by

\[
y=\frac{t\beta}{\|\beta\|_2^2}+Uu,
\qquad t=\beta\cdot y,\qquad u\in\mathbb R^{d-1}.
\]

The absolute Jacobian of this linear change of variables is \(1/\|\beta\|_2\). Define the joint reference density and its scalar marginal by

\[
q_\beta(t,u)=\frac{V(t\beta/\|\beta\|_2^2+Uu)}{\|\beta\|_2\widetilde W},
\qquad
g_\beta(t)=\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}q_\beta(t,u)\,du.
\]

The function \(q_\beta\) is measurable, log-concave, compactly supported, and has total integral one: these properties follow from those of \(V/\widetilde W\) and the invertible linear change of variables. Its marginal integrals are finite, since \(V\) is bounded and compactly supported. Prékopa's marginal theorem, stated and checked under EXTERNAL SOURCES, proves that \(g_\beta\) is log-concave. Tonelli and the Jacobian show that it is a probability density. For \(d=1\), the coordinate \(u\) is absent, the integral means the value \(q_\beta(t)\), and log-concavity follows directly from the linear substitution; no marginal theorem in zero integration dimensions is required.

The scalar likelihood must be averaged along these same projection fibers. Set

\[
r_\beta(t)=\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}
\ell(t\beta/\|\beta\|_2^2+Uu)q_\beta(t,u)\,du,
\qquad
w_\beta(t)=
\begin{cases}
r_\beta(t)/g_\beta(t),&g_\beta(t)>0,\\
1,&g_\beta(t)=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Both integrals are measurable and finite. Since \(1\le\ell\le M\), integration gives \(g_\beta\le r_\beta\le Mg_\beta\). Thus \(w_\beta\) is measurable, \(1\le w_\beta\le M\), and \(w_\beta g_\beta=r_\beta\) everywhere, including zero-density fibers. Equivalently, wherever \(g_\beta>0\), it is the conditional mean of \(\ell\) under the reference density on the projection fiber. The pointwise identity \(\ell V=MN\) and the change of variables give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}w_\beta(t)g_\beta(t)\,dt
&=\frac{MW}{\widetilde W}=Z,\\
f_\beta(t):=\frac{w_\beta(t)g_\beta(t)}{Z}
&=\frac1{\|\beta\|_2W}
\int_{\mathbb R^{d-1}}
N(t\beta/\|\beta\|_2^2+Uu)\,du.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly the probability density of \(\beta\cdot Y\) when \(Y\) has the original density \(N/W\). All its moments used here are finite, because \(D\) is compact. In particular its true scalar mean is

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}t f_\beta(t)\,dt
=\frac1W\int_{\mathbb R^d}(\beta\cdot y)N(y)\,dy
=\beta\cdot m.
\]

Apply accepted fact b77de4ea87809841 to \(g=g_\beta\), \(w=w_\beta\), and \(M=3^n\). Its scalar mean, denoted \(m\) within that predecessor, is explicitly \(\beta\cdot m\) in the present notation; its \(Z,f\) are the \(Z,f_\beta\) just computed. Every hypothesis is satisfied, so both closed scalar mean half-lines have probability at least

\[
\frac1{(3+\sqrt2)\sqrt{M(1+\log M)}}
=\frac1{(3+\sqrt2)\sqrt{3^n(1+n\log3)}}.
\]

In particular, writing \(P\) for the original law with density \(N/W\), every nonzero \(\beta\) satisfies this bound for \(P\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}\). No scalar reference mean has been substituted for the original one. These scalar laws are densities, so their mean boundary points have zero probability; equivalently, nonzero \(\beta\) gives zero \(P\)-mass to its boundary hyperplane.

Now take arbitrary normal components \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\), \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\). The original-volume fiber transfer in accepted fact ef682ae82d115df6 gives

\[
\mathcal H_d\{(z,y)\in S:\alpha\cdot z+\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}
\ge\frac12\int_{\{y:\beta\cdot(y-m)\ge0\}}N(y)\,dy.
\]

For \(\beta\ne0_d\), the cap bound makes the right side at least \(W/[2(3+\sqrt2)\sqrt{3^n(1+n\log3)}]\), for every \(\alpha\), including \(\alpha=0_n\). For \(\beta=0_d\), the integration set is all of \(\mathbb R^d\), and transfer gives \(W/2\), which is at least the same displayed lower bound. This includes pure base normals and their possible integer-fiber boundary mass. If both normal components vanish, the displayed set is all of \(S\) and has volume \(W\).

Every proper closed halfspace containing \((0_n,m)\) can be written as \(H=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\ge b\}\), with \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0_n,0_d)\) and \(b\le\beta\cdot m\). It contains the centered halfspace just treated. Its original volume therefore obeys the same bound. The whole space, if included in the halfspace convention, has volume \(W\) and also obeys it. Taking the infimum over these halfspaces proves the first depth inequality in the statement.

It remains to make the constant independent of \(n\). Set \(a=\log(4/3)\). Direct integration gives \(a=\int_1^{4/3}x^{-1}\,dx>1/4\) and \(\log3=\int_1^3x^{-1}\,dx<2\). Since \(e^{an}\ge an\) and \(e^{-an}\le1\),

\[
(1+n\log3)e^{-an}
\le1+\frac{\log3}{a}<9,
\qquad
\frac{2^n}{3^{n/2}\sqrt{1+n\log3}}
=\frac1{\sqrt{(1+n\log3)e^{-an}}}>\frac13.
\]

Multiplying the first depth inequality by this comparison yields

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
>\frac{1}{6(3+\sqrt2)\,2^n}W
>\frac{1}{30\,2^n}W
>\frac{1}{32\,2^n}W,
\]

where \(\sqrt2<2\) gives the middle inequality and \(W>0\) preserves strictness. Finally \(e<3<32\), so \(0<1/32<1/e\). For example \(e=\sum_{k=0}^\infty1/k!<1+1+\sum_{k=2}^\infty2^{-(k-1)}=3\). Thus this is an admissible absolute constant for all stated dimensions. If the locked problem is separately considered with \(W=0\) and nonempty \(S\), every mixed-integer halfspace volume is nonnegative and the required right side is zero, so any point of \(S\) suffices without defining a normalized mean. Under the present hypotheses, the established \(W\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(D)>0\) rules that branch out.
