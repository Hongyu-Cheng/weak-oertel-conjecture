## Statement

Independently tilting both endpoint marginals by a common positive affine function and tilting the old midpoint law by that function can make the old tilted law incomparable in convex order with every midpoint law of the tilted marginals, even with matching total masses. Dividing both sides of a convex-order relation by a common positive affine function and then normalizing can also destroy convex order.

## Proof

Take both marginals to be Lebesgue measure on $[-1,1]$ and the old coupling $(X,-X)$, with midpoint law $2\delta_0$. For $a(x)=1+x/2$ the tilted old midpoint law is still $2\delta_0$. Each tilted marginal $a(x)\,dx$ has mass two and first moment $\int_{-1}^1 xa(x)\,dx=1/3$. Every coupling of them has midpoint first moment $1/3$. Finite convex order forces equal first moments by testing both signs of linear functions, so none is comparable in convex order with the directly tilted old law.

For the inverse operation, Jensen gives $\delta_0\preceq_{\rm cx}(\delta_{-1}+\delta_1)/2$. Division by $a$ gives $\delta_0$ and $\delta_{-1}+\delta_1/3$, with masses one and $4/3$. After normalizing, the second law has mean $-1/2$, whereas the first still has mean zero.
