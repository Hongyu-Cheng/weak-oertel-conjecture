## Statement

Put \(\alpha=\sqrt2\). For each integer \(t\ge100\), let \(M=t^6\) and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_t(x)&=\alpha x-\frac{|x|}{tM},\\
P_t&=\{(x,y):-M\le x\le M,\ -3M\le y\le f_t(x)\},
&D_t&=P_t\cap\mathbb Z^2,\\
\boldsymbol\theta_t&=(-t\alpha,t),
&Z_t&=\sum_{(i,j)\in D_t}e^{t(j-\alpha i)},\\
p_{t,(i,j)}&=Z_t^{-1}e^{t(j-\alpha i)}\quad((i,j)\in D_t),
&p_{\max,t}&=\max_{D_t}p_t,
\end{aligned}
\]
and extend \(p_t\) by zero elsewhere. This is the original root law throughout. The support is finite, complete, and of affine rank two. Let \(\eta_t=\sqrt{p_{\max,t}}\), and choose \(K_t=\operatorname{conv}(E_t)\) of minimum Euclidean area among all subsets \(E_t\subseteq D_t\) with \(p_t(E_t)\ge1-\eta_t\). Put \(F_t=K_t\cap\mathbb Z^2\).

Choose any triangle of maximum area in \(K_t\) whose three vertices \(\mathbf u_{t,0},\mathbf u_{t,1},\mathbf u_{t,2}\) are vertices of \(K_t\). Such a maximizing triangle exists, and \(K_t\) has affine rank two. Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_t&=[\mathbf u_{t,1}-\mathbf u_{t,0}\ \ \mathbf u_{t,2}-\mathbf u_{t,0}],\\
A_t(\mathbf x)&=G_t^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf u_{t,0})-(1/3,1/3),\\
\Gamma_t&=G_t^{-1}\mathbb Z^2,
&\boldsymbol\lambda_t&=G_t^T\boldsymbol\theta_t.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(\mathbf g_{t,1}\) be any shortest nonzero vector of \(\Gamma_t\), and let \(\sigma_t=|\boldsymbol\lambda_t\cdot\mathbf g_{t,1}|\). These are exactly the quantile-core and maximum-triangle normalization conventions of accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`, with its index \(k\) replaced by \(t\), and the shortest-vector score convention of accepted fact `e50598a6051a715a`, whose score \(t_k\) is here \(\sigma_t\). Neither predecessor's near-stationarity hypothesis is imposed or invoked.

Write \(z(x,y)=\alpha x-y\) and \(B_t=40\log t+6\). For every choice just specified,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{\max,t}&=Z_t^{-1},
&\frac{e^{-2}M}{2t}&\le Z_t\le\frac{56M}{t},\\
K_t&\subseteq\{(x,y):|x|\le M,\ 0\le z(x,y)\le B_t/t\},\\
\operatorname{area}(K_t)&\ge\frac{e^{-2}M}{16t},
&|\det G_t|&\ge\frac{e^{-2}M}{64t},\\
\|\mathbf g_{t,1}\|&\le64\sqrt2\,e^2
\left(\frac{3B_t}{t^3}+\frac2{t^2}\right),\\
0\le\sigma_t&\le128e^2B_t
\left(\frac{3B_t}{t^3}+\frac2{t^2}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the conclusion concerns all genuine shortest vectors, including ties, for all minimum-area cores and all the stated maximizing triangles. Identifying an individual minimizer or an individual shortest vector is unnecessary for this uniform certification.

## Proof

The two roof slopes are \(\alpha+1/(tM)\) and \(\alpha-1/(tM)\), so \(f_t\) is concave. Its minimum on \([-M,M]\) is greater than \(-3M\). Thus \(P_t\) is a bounded full-dimensional convex polygon. Since \(D_t=P_t\cap\mathbb Z^2\), its integer hull has exactly \(D_t\) as its lattice points: one inclusion follows from \(\operatorname{conv}(D_t)\subseteq P_t\), and the reverse inclusion follows because every member of \(D_t\) belongs to that hull. The three noncollinear points \((0,-3M),(0,0),(1,0)\) belong to \(D_t\).

The linear shear \((x,y)\mapsto(x,z(x,y))\) has determinant \(-1\), so it preserves area. In these coordinates every support point satisfies \(|x|\le M\) and \(z\ge |x|/(tM)\ge0\), and its unnormalized weight is \(e^{-tz}\le1\). The origin has weight one. Hence \(p_{\max,t}=Z_t^{-1}\). We first establish estimates for the original root normalizer and the mass near both ends of the roof.

Expand \((1+\sqrt2)^n=P_n+Q_n\sqrt2\), for positive integers \(n\). Then \(P_n,Q_n\) are positive integers,
\(P_n^2-2Q_n^2=(-1)^n\), and
\[
|\alpha-P_n/Q_n|
=\frac1{Q_n(P_n+\alpha Q_n)}<Q_n^{-2}.
\tag{1}
\]
The norm identity shows that \(P_n,Q_n\) are coprime. The recurrence is \(P_{n+1}=P_n+2Q_n\), \(Q_{n+1}=P_n+Q_n\). Also \(P_n<2Q_n\): this follows by squaring from \(P_n^2\le2Q_n^2+1<4Q_n^2\) for \(Q_n\ge1\). Consequently \(Q_{n+1}<3Q_n\), and \(Q_n\) increases without bound.

For the counting estimates only, take the first \(q=Q_n\ge t^2\) and put \(r=P_n\). Then
\(t^2\le q<3t^2\), \((r,q)=1\), and \(|\alpha-r/q|<q^{-2}\). In particular \(q/t\ge100\) and \(M\ge4q\). On any block of \(q\) consecutive integers \(i=a+\ell\), \(0\le\ell<q\),
\[
\left|f_t(a+\ell)-f_t(a)-\ell r/q\right|
\le q|\alpha-r/q|+q/(tM)\le2/q.
\tag{2}
\]
Here the inequality \(||a+\ell|-|a||\le\ell\) handles blocks crossing the two facets. The fractional parts of \(f_t(a)+\ell r/q\) form a translated grid of \(q\) points of spacing \(1/q\). Any interval of length \(L\) in \([0,1]\) contains at least \(qL-1\) and at most \(qL+1\) such points. This follows by listing the equally spaced points in increasing order and comparing the interval length with the spacings between the first and last contained points.

Put \(b_i=\lfloor f_t(i)\rfloor\) and \(d_i=f_t(i)-b_i\). At least \(q/t-5\ge q/(2t)\) of the grid points are in \([2/q,1/t-2/q]\). Equation (2) implies \(0\le d_i\le1/t\) for those indices, with no crossing of an integer. If the block is within \([-M,M]\), the top-site weight is
\[
e^{t(b_i-\alpha i)}=e^{-|i|/M}e^{-td_i}\ge e^{-2}.
\tag{3}
\]
The \(2M+1\) indices in the support contain at least \(M/q\) full blocks, since \(M\ge q\). Their top weights give \(Z_t\ge e^{-2}M/(2t)\).

For an upper bound on a block, discard the grid points whose fractional parts are at least \(1-2/q\), of which there are at most three. For every remaining grid point \(g\), (2) gives
\(e^{-td_i}\le e^{2t/q}e^{-tg}\). When \(g<2/q\), this inequality holds because its right side is at least one. In all other retained cases there is no integer crossing, which gives the inequality directly. Sorting the translated grid, its \(\ell\)-th point is at least \(\ell/q\). Since \(2t/q\le1/50\), the power series bound \(e^s\le1/(1-s)\) for \(0\le s<1\) gives \(e^{2t/q}<2\). Thus the block sum is at most
\[
2\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}e^{-t\ell/q}+3
\le2(1+q/t)+3\le7q/t.
\tag{4}
\]
The middle inequality uses \(1/(1-e^{-s})\le1+1/s\), which follows from \(e^s\ge1+s\). A partial block has no larger sum than the full block obtained by extending it, because \(f_t\) is defined on the entire real line. Covering the support indices by at most \((2M+1)/q+1\) blocks gives a top-site sum at most \(7(2M+1+q)/t\le28M/t\). On each vertical line the other sites form a finite geometric progression downward with ratio \(e^{-t}\). Its total is at most its top weight divided by \(1-e^{-t}\), and that factor is at most two. Hence \(Z_t\le56M/t\), completing the claimed normalizer bounds.

Apply the same lower counting argument separately in each of \([-M,-M/2]\) and \([M/2,M]\). Each interval contains at least \(M/2\) integer indices and therefore at least \(M/(4q)\) full blocks. Equation (3) gives unnormalized mass at least \(e^{-2}M/(8t)\) in top sites with \(d_i\le1/t\) in each interval. Those sites satisfy \(0\le z(i,b_i)=|i|/(tM)+d_i\le2/t\). The original mass of each such patch is at least
\[
c=\frac{e^{-2}}{448}.
\tag{5}
\]
Also \(\eta_t\le\sqrt{2e^2}\,t^{-5/2}<5t^{-5/2}\le1/20000\), whereas \(c>1/4032\), using \(e<3\). Thus every eligible core contains at least one lattice point \(\mathbf a_t\) in the left patch and at least one \(\mathbf b_t\) in the right patch. Their abscissas differ by at least \(M\), and both their \(z\)-coordinates lie in \([0,2/t]\). This applies to the actual core without conditioning the root law.

We verify that every eligible hull has rank two. Any lattice line containing more than one lattice point has a primitive integer direction \((a,b)\). Its support sites form a consecutive interval in that direction. Their probabilities are a geometric progression with score increment \(t(b-\alpha a)\), and every term is at most \(p_{\max,t}\). If \(a\ne0\),
\[
|\alpha a-b|\ge\frac1{4|a|}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed this is immediate if the left side is at least one. Otherwise \(|b|\le\alpha|a|+1\), so \(|b+\alpha a|<4|a|\), and the nonzero integer \(b^2-2a^2\) has absolute value at least one. Factorization gives (6). The line has at most \(1+2M/|a|\) support sites, while its geometric sum is at most \(p_{\max,t}(1+4|a|/t)\). Its mass is therefore at most
\[
p_{\max,t}\min\{1+2M/|a|,1+4|a|/t\}
\le p_{\max,t}(1+\sqrt{8M/t}).
\tag{7}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\min(u,v)\le\sqrt{uv}\) for nonnegative \(u,v\). If \(a=0\), primitivity gives \(|b|=1\), and the geometric bound is \(p_{\max,t}(1+1/t)\), which also obeys (7). A singleton obeys (7) as well. From the normalizer bound, \(p_{\max,t}\le2e^2t^{-5}<20t^{-5}\), so the bound in (7) is at most \(80t^{-5/2}<1/2\). Every eligible set has mass \(1-\eta_t>1/2\), proving rank two.

Next impose the true minimizing property. Let \(H_t=10\log t/t\). The original mass in \(z>H_t\) is at most
\[
\frac{(2M+1)e^{-tH_t}}{(1-e^{-t})Z_t}
\le12e^2t^{-9}.
\tag{8}
\]
To see the numerator bound, on each vertical line the first site with \(z>H_t\) has weight at most \(e^{-tH_t}\), and the subsequent weights decay by \(e^{-t}\). There are \(2M+1\le3M\) lines. The lower normalizer bound proves (8). Conversely the upper normalizer bound gives \(\eta_t\ge t^{-5/2}/\sqrt{56}\). Since \(12e^2\sqrt{56}<864<100^{13/2}\), (8) is less than \(\eta_t\) for all \(t\ge100\). Therefore the lattice set with \(z\le H_t\) is eligible. Its hull lies in the rectangle \([-M,M]\times[0,H_t]\) in shear coordinates, of area \(2MH_t\). The actual area-minimizing core consequently satisfies
\[
\operatorname{area}(K_t)\le2MH_t.
\tag{9}
\]

The two mandatory patch points now bound all depths of this actual core. The line through their shear images has \(z\)-coordinate at most \(6/t\) at every abscissa in \([-M,M]\): start with the first endpoint's \(z\le2/t\), use the endpoint difference in \(z\) at most \(2/t\), the abscissa difference at least \(M\), and distance in abscissa from that first endpoint at most \(2M\). For any \(\mathbf v\in K_t\) with \(z(\mathbf v)>6/t\), the triangle with vertices \(\mathbf a_t,\mathbf b_t,\mathbf v\) is contained in \(K_t\), and its area is at least \(M(z(\mathbf v)-6/t)/2\). By (9), \(z(\mathbf v)\le4H_t+6/t=B_t/t\). Points with \(z(\mathbf v)\le6/t\) obey the same bound. Together with \(K_t\subseteq P_t\), this proves the claimed core containment. This is where minimum-area selection, as opposed to whole-support normalization, is essential.

For the lower area bound, completeness gives \(F_t\subseteq D_t\), \(\operatorname{conv}(F_t)=K_t\), and \(p_t(F_t)\ge1-\eta_t>1/2\). Thus
\[
|F_t|\ge Z_t/2\ge\frac{e^{-2}M}{4t}.
\tag{10}
\]
For completeness, every full-dimensional lattice polygon with \(N\) lattice points has area at least \((N-2)/2\). Triangulate the polygon using its vertices, then insert its other lattice points one at a time. Inserting a point inside a triangle replaces that triangle by three; inserting on an interior edge replaces its two adjacent triangles by four; inserting on a boundary edge replaces its adjacent triangle by two. At every step the triangulation remains nondegenerate and gains at least one triangle. Starting with \(v-2\) triangles for \(v\) vertices therefore ends with at least \(N-2\) triangles. Each has area at least \(1/2\), since its doubled area is the absolute value of a nonzero integer determinant. This proves the bound without a point-counting formula. Equation (10) gives \(|F_t|\ge4\), so
\(\operatorname{area}(K_t)\ge |F_t|/4\ge e^{-2}M/(16t)\).

A maximum-area triangle exists by compactness. Its vertices can be chosen among polygon vertices: with two points fixed, the absolute determinant in the third is the absolute value of an affine functional, whose maximum on a polygon occurs at a vertex. Replace the three points successively in a maximizing triple. This is the same construction used in accepted fact `3f23ec160b160e93`; every such triple has positive area and integer vertices. For its matrix \(G_t\), transforming by \(G_t^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf u_{t,0})\) makes the maximizing triangle \(\operatorname{conv}\{0,\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2\}\). Replacing its first or second nonzero vertex with any transformed point and using maximality gives \(|x_1|\le1\), \(|x_2|\le1\). Hence the transformed entire core has area at most four. It follows that
\[
|\det G_t|\ge\operatorname{area}(K_t)/4
\ge\frac{e^{-2}M}{64t}.
\tag{11}
\]
The translation in \(A_t\) has no effect on this determinant or on the transported lattice group.

It remains to certify the shortest-vector score, including all choices of shortest vector. In shear coordinates write the two columns of \(G_t\) as \((X_1,Z_1)\), \((X_2,Z_2)\). The actual core containment gives \(|X_i|\le2M\), \(|Z_i|\le B_t/t\). For any integer vector \(\mathbf v=(a,b)\), its shear image is \((a,\alpha a-b)\). The inverse formula for a two by two matrix, together with (11), gives
\[
\|G_t^{-1}\mathbf v\|
\le64\sqrt2\,e^2
\left(\frac{B_t|a|}{M}+2t|\alpha a-b|\right).
\tag{12}
\]
This is the actual Euclidean norm in the maximum-triangle normalization, not a replacement norm. To check it, each of the two inverse coordinates has numerator bounded by \((B_t/t)|a|+2M|\alpha a-b|\), and denominator at least \(e^{-2}M/(64t)\).

Now take the first Pell denominator \(q=Q_n\ge t^3\), with numerator \(r=P_n\). The elementary identities (1) give
\(t^3\le q<3t^3\) and \(|\alpha q-r|<1/q\). The vector \(G_t^{-1}(q,r)\) is an explicit nonzero member of \(\Gamma_t\). By (12),
\[
\|G_t^{-1}(q,r)\|
\le64\sqrt2\,e^2
\left(\frac{3B_t}{t^3}+\frac2{t^2}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
A shortest nonzero vector of a full-rank lattice exists, since bounded Euclidean balls contain finitely many lattice points. Every choice of \(\mathbf g_{t,1}\) has norm no greater than the certified competitor (13). This proves the stated shortest-length estimate for the exact lattice \(\Gamma_t\).

Finally \(\boldsymbol\theta_t\cdot\mathbf x=-t z(\mathbf x)\). Therefore the two components of \(\boldsymbol\lambda_t=G_t^T\boldsymbol\theta_t\) are \(-tZ_1,-tZ_2\), each of absolute value at most \(B_t\). Cauchy--Schwarz and (13) imply
\[
|\boldsymbol\lambda_t\cdot\mathbf g_{t,1}|
\le\sqrt2 B_t\|\mathbf g_{t,1}\|
\le128e^2B_t
\left(\frac{3B_t}{t^3}+\frac2{t^2}\right).
\]
As \(B_t=40\log t+6\), this tends to zero. This is exactly the shortest-vector score defined in accepted fact `e50598a6051a715a`; extension to a nearest-reduced basis does not change its first vector or this score. All probability and area estimates above concern the original root law and its actual minimum-area quantile core. No canonical defect estimate or near-stationarity assertion was needed.
