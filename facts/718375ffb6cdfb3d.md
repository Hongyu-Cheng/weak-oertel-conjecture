## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty and let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be positive-volume compact convex bodies that are the actual fibers of the convex hull of their lifts. For each $q\in P$, form the polytope of representations $q=\sum_{z\ne q}a_z z$ with $a_z\ge0$ and $\sum_{z\ne q}a_z=1$. Fix $p\in P$. Suppose that for each extreme representation with target $q=p$ or with $a_p>0$, the Minkowski sum $\sum_{z\ne q}a_z K_z$ is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_q$. Every sufficiently small Hausdorff perturbation of $K_p$ alone by a convex body preserves the prescribed actual integer fibers. If $K_p$ is a simplex, independent affine outward normal velocities on its facets are realized by actual two-sided simplex paths, including independent endpoint velocities when $d=1$.

## Proof

Compact containment gives a positive uniform support gap on each affected extreme row. The rows are finite, so one positive gap works for all, with an arbitrary positive choice when there are no affected rows. Changing $K_p$ changes the support difference on any affected row by at most $d_H(K_p,K'_p)$, since $p$ is either its target or an input of weight at most one. Every other extreme row is unchanged. Choose the Hausdorff change smaller than the common gap.

Each representation excluding its target is a convex combination of extreme representations. The support inequality is linear in its coefficients, so all those rows hold after the perturbation. A lifted convex combination landing at base $q$ either uses just $q$ or, after removing its $q$ term and dividing by the total remaining weight, gives such a row. Its continuous point is therefore in the prescribed new $K_q$. The reverse inclusion follows from the lifted generators. The projection remains $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, so the integer census remains $P$.

For each original unit-normal facet inequality $n_i\cdot x\le h_i$, use $n_i\cdot x\le h_i+t\psi_i(x)$ for the prescribed affine velocity $\psi_i$. For sufficiently small signed $t$, the perturbed $d$-fold vertex systems stay nonsingular and each corresponding opposite-vertex inequality stays strict. Their $d+1$ intersection points depend continuously on $t$ and remain affinely independent. The defining functions vanish on the corresponding $d$ points and have the strict inside sign at the opposite point. The perturbed inequalities therefore exactly define their simplex by its barycentric halfspaces. At a relative-interior point of the original facet the signed normal displacement divided by $t$ tends to $\psi_i(x)$, by intersecting its normal line with the new hyperplane. The other facets can be moved independently. The simplices converge in Hausdorff distance, so the first part gives the actual paths.
