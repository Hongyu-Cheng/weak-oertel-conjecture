## Statement

There is no universal constant bounding the total length of positive-area recipient traces on an empty supporting face of a planar triangle by the sum of the recipients' own section lengths, even when the family covers the triangle, both recipients cut it properly, and the three inward unit normals have a strictly positive dependence. More precisely, for $0<\varepsilon<1$ take $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-1,0),(1,0),(0,-1)\}$, $H_0=\{y\ge0\}$, $H_+=\{y\ge-1+\varepsilon\}$ and $H_-=\{y\le-1+\varepsilon\}$. The face $F_0=K\cap\partial H_0$ has length $2$ and the two traces on it have lengths $2,0$. The recipient cap areas are $1-\varepsilon^2,\varepsilon^2$, and their own section lengths sum to $4\varepsilon$.

## Proof

The complementary closed halfspaces $H_+,H_-$ cover the plane. Their common boundary cuts the edges from $(0,-1)$ at fraction $\varepsilon$ of the distance to their endpoints, so the common section has length $2\varepsilon$. The bottom triangle is similar to $K$ at scale $\varepsilon$. Since $K$ has area $1$, the bottom cap has area $\varepsilon^2$ and the top cap has area $1-\varepsilon^2$. The top edge lies strictly in $H_+$, disjoint from $H_-$. Hence the ratio of the recipient trace sum to their own section sum is $1/(2\varepsilon)$, which is unbounded. The three inward unit normals are $(0,1),(0,1),(0,-1)$, with positive dependence coefficients $1,1,2$.
