## Statement

Fix $d\ge1$, $N=d+1$, $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, and a finite complete affine-lattice base $P$ with $k$ occupied parities. Let $\beta_0^*$ be the infimum of the normalized ordinary closed-halfspace maximum over all actual positive compatible $d$-fibers on $P$. Assume $\beta_0^*<b<\gamma_d/k$ and assume the entire original sublevel $\{\beta_0\le b\}$ admits a compact frame of positive representatives under invertible maps $(z,x)\mapsto(z,Ax+s(z))$ with $s$ affine. Each representative has total sampled volume $M=1$, fiber volumes bounded below by $v>0$, and all fibers contained in $R B_d$. Assume every limit of the framed sublevel satisfies $V(T)\le k(T)/k$ for every nonempty proper closed minor $T=P\cap\operatorname{conv}(T)$, with $V(T)=\sum_{z\in T}|K_z|$ and inherited parity count $k(T)$.

For an actual canonical hull $C$ of the sampled fibers let the parameter $L$ be positive continuous concave on $C$ with $\sum_z\int_{K_z}\log L(z,X)\,dX/M=0$. At scale $\varepsilon>0$ use through-mark nonvertical caps with margin $a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)$ increased at mark $(p,y)$ by
$$
\varepsilon\left(\frac{L(p,y)E_{K_p}(y)}{M}\right)^{1/N}G_K(u),\qquad
G_K(u)=\left(\frac1M\sum_z\int_{K_z}(u\cdot(X-g_z))^2\,dX\right)^{1/2},
$$
where $g_z$ is the centroid and projective energy is extended by zero to the own boundary. Pure-base caps are unchanged. Write $\beta_\varepsilon(K,L)$ for the normalized buffered maximum. Let $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ and let $(K_j,L_j)$ be admissible pairs with $\beta_{\varepsilon_j}(K_j,L_j)-\inf_{K,L}\beta_{\varepsilon_j}(K,L)\to0$, taking each infimum over the whole stated class. Then
$$
\beta_{\varepsilon_j}(K_j,L_j)\to\beta_0^*,
\qquad
\max_{z\in P,\ X\in K_{j,z}}\varepsilon_j^N L_j(z,X)\to0.
$$
The physical cap displacements also tend to zero uniformly on sampled fibers and Euclidean unit continuous normals in the bounded representative frame. These assumptions on the original sublevel and quotas hold for a base selected by 26d27b0f77ea4ac9 using the parity budget with $\tau=\gamma_d$.

## Proof

Let $\beta_j=\beta_{\varepsilon_j}(K_j,L_j)$. Monotonicity of every cap gives $\beta_j\ge\beta_0^*$. For a fixed ordinary near-minimizer the constant $L=1$ is admissible, and its displacement on every unit continuous normal is uniformly $O(\varepsilon)$ over sampled marks. Indeed $E_{K_p}(y)\le |K_p|\le M$ by d90ba05577427854 and the covariance norm is bounded. The mass added by a shift of size $s$ is at most $s$ times the common sum of section-area bounds, independently of the base tilt. It follows that the limsup of the joint infima is at most the ordinary depth of this trial body. Sending its ordinary value to $\beta_0^*$ and using the $o(1)$ optimality gives $\beta_j\to\beta_0^*$.

The objectives, logarithmic constraint and sampled maximum of the parameter are invariant under the representative maps if the parameter is pulled back. This follows from the common determinant factor for $d$-volumes and energy, the covariance transformation $G_{K'}(u)=G_K(A^{\mathsf T}u)$, and absorption of the affine shear into the unrestricted base tilt. For large $j$, $\beta_0(K_j)\le\beta_j\le b$, so work in the frame. Write $h_j=\varepsilon_j^N L_j$ and $m_{j,z}=\max_{X\in K_{j,z}}h_j(z,X)$.

First the sampled maxima are bounded. If $A_j=\max_z m_{j,z}\to\infty$ along a subsequence, take a positive Hausdorff limit of the bodies and an array limit $m_{j,z}/A_j\to a_z\in[0,1]$ with $\max_z a_z=1$. The arrays $m_j$ are sampled-concave by choosing maximizing inputs for an anchored representation and applying joint concavity to their actual combination. Their normalized limit is sampled-concave. Apply the positive-set parity center lemma to $a$ and the limiting weights, using the limiting quotas, to find $p$ with $a_p>0$ and limiting pure-base depth at least $1/k$.

For a positive concave function $f$ on a convex $d$-body $K$ with maximum $m$ at $x_0$, the superlevel $\{f\ge tm\}$ contains $tx_0+(1-t)K$ for $0\le t\le1$. Integrating these volume bounds yields $|K|^{-1}\int_K f\ge m/(d+1)$, and Jensen at the centroid gives $f(g_K)\ge m/(d+1)$. Hence $h_j(p,g_{j,p})\to\infty$. The centroid energy is $V_{j,p}\ge v$ by d90ba05577427854. Hausdorff convergence gives convergence of fiber integrals of first and second moments and volumes, so the pooled covariance norms converge uniformly on the unit sphere to a positive definite norm. Consequently the displacement $(h_j E/M)^{1/N}G_{K_j}(u)$ at this centroid exceeds $2R$ for all Euclidean unit $u$ when $j$ is large. Every through-mark nonvertical cap then contains the whole closed trace of its nonnegative base drift, and the unchanged pure-base caps make the depth at that centroid equal its pure-base depth. In rank zero the whole trace has mass one. The finite family of closed base traces gives convergence of the corresponding depths with the volumes. This contradicts $\beta_j\le b<1/k$, proving the uniform sampled bound.

Next take any simultaneous positive Hausdorff limit $K$ along a subsequence. Each compact subset of $\operatorname{int}K_z$ with a small compact collar is contained in $K_{j,z}$ eventually by support convergence. On such a subset the uniform bounds and concavity give a uniform local Lipschitz estimate, by extending each secant in both directions into that collar and bounding its slopes by the oscillation divided by the collar width. A diagonal subsequence over an exhaustion of each of the finitely many interiors gives locally uniform limits $h_z$ there. They are finite, nonnegative and concave. Every anchored convex combination of own-interior inputs is own-interior at its target by compatibility and the inclusion of combined interior balls. Its fixed inputs and output belong to the moving fibers eventually, so the sampled anchored concavity inequality passes to the limit at all such tuples.

The physical displacements converge uniformly between marks at the same base at distance at most the maximal fiber Hausdorff distance, and all Euclidean unit continuous normals. For proof, take any sequence of such mark pairs and extract a subsequence with a fixed base and a common limiting mark. At an own-interior limit the local uniform convergence just proved gives convergence of both parameter evaluations to $h_z$ there. At a boundary limit the uniformly bounded parameters need no boundary convergence, because the marked energy of both moving and limiting fibers tends to zero by b3c0088cf09b2894. The limiting shift is defined to be zero on the boundary. The norm converges uniformly in both cases. This sequential argument implies uniform comparison of the displacements. The mass of a cap at corresponding marks differs by at most the sum of symmetric-difference volumes plus the common sum of section-area bounds times the sum of mark distance and shift discrepancy. This is the unit-normal slab estimate of 43ed87d91b76249c, with the difference of displacements included in the cutoff. The base tilt cancels. Pure-base caps require only the symmetric-difference bound. Both errors tend to zero, so infimizing over normals and using the mark correspondence for maxima gives convergence of the normalized buffered maximum to that of the limiting sampled buffer.

Likewise $\beta_0(K_j)\to\beta_0(K)$ by 43ed87d91b76249c. The inequalities $\beta_0^*\le\beta_0(K_j)\le\beta_j$ show that $K$ is an unrestricted ordinary global minimum. Its limiting buffered supremum is $\beta_0^*$ as well, and both raw maxima use the same mass $M=1$. The zero-buffer rigidity theorem therefore gives $h_z=0$ on every sampled interior.

If some sampled maximum stays at least $\eta>0$ along a subsequence, fix such a base $z$ by finiteness and extract the limits just proved along that subsequence. Fix $g\in\operatorname{int}K_z$ with a ball of radius $r>0$ contained in $K_{j,z}$ about $g$ for all sufficiently large $j$, and bound the distance of all moving points from $g$ by $D$. Given any $X\ne g$ in the moving fiber, write $g$ as a convex combination of $X$ and the radius-$r$ point opposite $X$ about $g$. Nonnegativity gives $h_j(z,X)\le(1+D/r)h_j(z,g)$, also valid for $X=g$. The right side tends to zero because $h_z(g)=0$, contradicting $\eta$. This proves vanishing of the whole sampled maximum. Bounded $E/M$ and the bounded frame's unit-normal covariance norms then give the uniform physical displacement assertion.

For the stated minimal canonical application take the original sublevel in 26d27b0f77ea4ac9 with $\tau=\gamma_d$ and the parity budget. Its whole-sublevel compactness, positive-volume floor and bound $V(T)\le b k(T)/\gamma_d< k(T)/k$ verify all frame and limit assumptions.
