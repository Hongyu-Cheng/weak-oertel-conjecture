## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For \(\mathbf y\in S\), define
\[
h_S(\mathbf y)
:=
\inf\left\{
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf y\in H
\right\}.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}),
\]
where an empty fiber has volume zero, and let
\[
D_+:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_{\mathbf z}>0\}.
\]
For every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\), let \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}\) be the centroid of \(C_{\mathbf z}\). Define
\[
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

Let \(I\subseteq D_+\) be nonempty, and set
\[
W_I:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in I}a_{\mathbf z}>0
\]
and
\[
r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)
:=
\min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\dim\operatorname{span}
\{\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}-s(\mathbf z):\mathbf z\in I\}.
\]
Then there exists \(\mathbf y_I\in S\) such that every closed halfspace
\(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y_I\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2^n\bigl(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)+1\bigr)}W_I.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2^n\bigl(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)+1\bigr)}W_I.
\tag{2}
\]
This holds for every nonempty \(I\subseteq D_+\), including
\(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)=0\) and singleton \(I\).

## Proof

Compactness of the projection of \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) implies that only finitely many lattice vectors index nonempty fibers. Hence \(D_+\), and therefore \(I\), is finite.

We first justify that the minimum in the definition of the residual rank is attained. The set
\[
\mathscr R_I
:=
\left\{
\dim\operatorname{span}
\{\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}-s(\mathbf z):\mathbf z\in I\}:
s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\text{ affine}
\right\}
\]
is nonempty, since it contains the value obtained from \(s\equiv\mathbf0\), and it is a subset of the finite set \(\{0,1,\ldots,d\}\). Its least element is therefore one of its attained values. Choose an affine map
\[
s(\mathbf z)=A\mathbf z+\mathbf b
\]
attaining that least value, write
\[
r:=r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I),
\qquad
\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}:=\mathbf c_{\mathbf z}-s(\mathbf z),
\qquad
V:=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}:\mathbf z\in I\},
\]
and note that \(\dim V=r\).

Apply the invertible affine shear
\[
T:\mathbb R^{n+d}\to\mathbb R^{n+d},
\qquad
T(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)
=
(\mathbf z,\mathbf x-s(\mathbf z)).
\tag{3}
\]
Its inverse is \(T^{-1}(\mathbf z,\mathbf u)=(\mathbf z,\mathbf u+s(\mathbf z))\). In particular, \(T(\Lambda)=\Lambda\). Put
\[
C':=T(C),
\qquad
S':=C'\cap\Lambda=T(S).
\]
The sheared fiber at \(\mathbf z\) is
\[
K_{\mathbf z}:=C'_{\mathbf z}=C_{\mathbf z}-s(\mathbf z).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus, for \(\mathbf z\in I\), its volume is \(a_{\mathbf z}\) and its centroid is \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\in V\).

Choose a linear isometry \(\vartheta:V\to\mathbb R^r\). When \(r=0\), this means the unique linear map from \(V=\{\mathbf0\}\) to \(\mathbb R^0\). Define the finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^{n+r}\)
\[
\nu
:=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in I}
\gamma_d a_{\mathbf z}
\,\delta_{(\mathbf z,\vartheta\mathbf v_{\mathbf z})}.
\tag{5}
\]
It is nonzero, is supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^r\), and has total mass
\[
\nu(\mathbb R^{n+r})=\gamma_dW_I.
\]
Accepted fact \(6d6fcf377e68c79c\), applied with continuous dimension \(r\), gives a point
\[
\overline{\mathbf y}
=(\mathbf z^\star,\mathbf w^\star)
\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^r
\]
such that every closed halfspace \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+r}\) containing \(\overline{\mathbf y}\) satisfies
\[
\nu(G)
\geq
\Delta_I
:=
\frac{\gamma_dW_I}{2^n(r+1)}
>0.
\tag{6}
\]

The point supplied by the centerpoint theorem must lie in the convex hull of the atoms in (5). Indeed, let
\[
P:=\operatorname{conv}
\{(\mathbf z,\vartheta\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}):\mathbf z\in I\}.
\]
If \(\overline{\mathbf y}\notin P\), strict separation of a point from the compact convex set \(P\) would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(\overline{\mathbf y}\) and disjoint from \(P\). That halfspace would have \(\nu\)-mass zero, contradicting (6). Hence
\[
\overline{\mathbf y}\in P.
\tag{7}
\]

Use the linear identification
\[
\Phi:\mathbb R^n\times V\to\mathbb R^{n+r},
\qquad
\Phi(\mathbf z,\mathbf v)=(\mathbf z,\vartheta\mathbf v),
\]
and let
\[
\mathbf y':=\Phi^{-1}(\overline{\mathbf y})
=(\mathbf z^\star,\mathbf v^\star).
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (7) gives
\[
\mathbf y'
\in
\operatorname{conv}
\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}):\mathbf z\in I\}.
\tag{9}
\]

We next verify that this point belongs to the sheared body, rather than merely to the ambient mixed-integer space. For each \(\mathbf z\in I\), the positive volume of \(K_{\mathbf z}\) makes it a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Apply accepted fact \(6769574fc1bd9f8d\) to \(K_{\mathbf z}\) with \(m=a_{\mathbf z}\). The admissible density \(f\equiv1\) has barycenter \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\), so
\(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\in\mathsf M_{a_{\mathbf z}}(K_{\mathbf z})\). The same accepted fact gives both
\[
\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\in K_{\mathbf z}
\tag{10}
\]
and, for every closed halfspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}\cap L)
\geq
\gamma_d a_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{11}
\]
By (10), each point \((\mathbf z,\mathbf v_{\mathbf z})\) in (9) belongs to \(C'\). Convexity of \(C'\) and (9) therefore imply
\[
\mathbf y'\in C'.
\tag{12}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathbf z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\) by construction, so
\[
\mathbf y'\in C'\cap\Lambda=S'.
\tag{13}
\]

We now transfer the atomic centerpoint bound to the mixed-volume measure of \(C'\). Let \(H'\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y'\), and write
\[
H'
=
\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf u):
\boldsymbol\alpha^\top\mathbf z
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf u
\geq t\},
\tag{14}
\]
where \((\boldsymbol\alpha,\boldsymbol\beta)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\). Its restriction to \(\mathbb R^n\times V\), transported by \(\Phi\), is
\[
\overline H
:=
\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf w)\in\mathbb R^{n+r}:
\boldsymbol\alpha^\top\mathbf z
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\vartheta^{-1}\mathbf w
\geq t\}.
\tag{15}
\]
If the restricted normal in (15) is nonzero, \(\overline H\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(\overline{\mathbf y}\), so (6) applies. If the restricted normal is zero, containment of \(\overline{\mathbf y}\) forces \(\overline H=\mathbb R^{n+r}\), and its \(\nu\)-mass is \(\gamma_dW_I\geq\Delta_I\). Thus in both cases
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in I\\
(\mathbf z,\mathbf v_{\mathbf z})\in H'}}
\gamma_da_{\mathbf z}
&=
\nu(\overline H)\\
&\geq\Delta_I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Fix an index \(\mathbf z\in I\) counted in (16), and consider the fiber section
\[
L_{\mathbf z}
:=
\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^d:
\boldsymbol\alpha^\top\mathbf z
+\boldsymbol\beta^\top\mathbf u
\geq t\}.
\tag{17}
\]
The membership \((\mathbf z,\mathbf v_{\mathbf z})\in H'\) says that
\(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\in L_{\mathbf z}\).

If \(\boldsymbol\beta\ne\mathbf0\), then \(L_{\mathbf z}\) is a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\), and (11) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}\cap L_{\mathbf z})
\geq\gamma_da_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf0\), then (17) is independent of \(\mathbf u\). Since it contains \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf z}\), it equals all of \(\mathbb R^d\), and hence
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}\cap L_{\mathbf z})
=a_{\mathbf z}
\geq\gamma_da_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (18) covers every nonvertical halfspace and (19) covers every vertical halfspace. Summing these contributions and discarding the nonnegative contributions from all other fibers gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S'\cap H')
&=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{\mathbf z}\cap L_{\mathbf z})\\
&\geq
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in I\\
(\mathbf z,\mathbf v_{\mathbf z})\in H'}}
\gamma_da_{\mathbf z}\\
&\geq
\Delta_I.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
If the whole space is admitted as an improper closed halfspace, its mixed volume is at least \(W_I\geq\Delta_I\), so (20) also holds in that case. An empty halfspace cannot contain \(\mathbf y'\).

Finally, set
\[
\mathbf y_I:=T^{-1}(\mathbf y')\in S,
\tag{21}
\]
where membership follows from (13) and \(T^{-1}(S')=S\). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be any closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y_I\). The image \(T(H)\) is a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y'\). On every lattice fiber, \(T\) is the translation
\(\mathbf x\mapsto\mathbf x-s(\mathbf z)\), so it preserves \(d\)-dimensional volume. Therefore
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
=
\mathcal H_d(S'\cap T(H))
\geq
\Delta_I
=
\frac{\gamma_dW_I}{2^n(r+1)}.
\tag{22}
\]
This proves (1), and (2) follows from the definition of \(h_{\max}(C)\).

For completeness, no step above assumes \(r>0\). When \(r=0\), one has \(V=\{\mathbf0\}\), the measure (5) is a measure on
\(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^0\cong\mathbb Z^n\), accepted fact
\(6d6fcf377e68c79c\) gives the denominator \(2^n(0+1)\), and a restricted ambient halfspace in (15) is either a base halfspace or the whole lower-dimensional space. The vertical and nonvertical fiber arguments (18)-(19) are unchanged.

If \(I=\{\mathbf z_0\}\), the constant affine map
\(s\equiv\mathbf c_{\mathbf z_0}\) makes the only residual zero, so
\(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)=0\). In this case (7) forces the selected centerpoint to be the sole atom \((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf0)\). Equations (18)-(19) then give at least
\(\gamma_da_{\mathbf z_0}\) from that fiber for every containing halfspace, which is stronger than the required
\(\gamma_d2^{-n}a_{\mathbf z_0}\). Thus the singleton and all zero-residual cases are included without an omitted limiting or positive-dimensional argument.
