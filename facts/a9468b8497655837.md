## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, and place convex bodies $A,B,C\subset\mathbb R^d$ at bases $-1,0,1$. Assume $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$ and write $a=|A|$, $b=|B|$, $c=|C|$, $M=a+b+c$, with $0<a<c$, $c<a+b$, and $b<a+c$. Use summed original fiber volumes $\mu$ and let $f(y)=\inf_{H\ni(0,y)}\mu(H)$ for closed ambient halfspaces. Write $g_K$ for the uniform centroid of $K$ and set
$$
Y=\left\{\frac{b g_B+a g_A+\int x\,d\sigma}{b+2a}:0\le\sigma\le\mathbf 1_C dx,\ \sigma(C)=a\right\}.
$$
This set is nonempty, compact, convex and uniformly inside $B$, and $f$ attains its maximum there. For every maximizer $y_*$ with $D=f(y_*)<M/2$, there are $k\le d+1$ actual minimum caps $H_j=\{(z,x):u_j\cdot(x-y_*)+v_jz\ge0\}$, $\lVert u_j\rVert=1$, $\mu(H_j)=D$, and positive weights $\omega_j$ of sum one. Each cap has effective section measures $\tau_{jz}$ on its trace in $K_z$, where $(K_{-1},K_0,K_1)=(A,B,C)$. They are ordinary $H^{d-1}$ on a proper trace, zero on an empty or $H^{d-1}$-null trace, and $\theta_{jz}H^{d-1}$ on a supporting facet with a measurable $0\le\theta_{jz}\le1$. Write $S_{jz}=\int d\tau_{jz}$, $S_j=\sum_z S_{jz}$ and $X_j=\sum_z\int x\,d\tau_{jz}$. Then
$$
S_j>0,\qquad S_{j,-1}=S_{j,1},\qquad X_j=S_jy_*,\qquad
\bar u\cdot(y-y_*)\ge0\quad(y\in Y),\qquad
\bar u=\sum_j\omega_j S_j u_j .
$$
When $\bar u\ne0$, the selector density producing $y_*$ is uniquely the indicator, up to volume-null sets, of the actual lower $\bar u$-cap in $C$ of volume $a$. When $\bar u=0$, the displayed sum gives a positive section-weighted normal balance.

## Proof

By 6769574fc1bd9f8d, the moments $\int x\,d\sigma$ form the compact nonempty convex set $a\mathsf M_a(C)$, since $0<a<c$. For each such $\sigma$, its mean $g_\sigma$ is in $C$, and $(g_A+g_\sigma)/2\in B$. Hence each $y\in Y$ is the convex combination of $g_B\in\operatorname{int}B$ and this endpoint midpoint, with weight $b/(b+2a)>0$ on $g_B$. A ball about $g_B$ contained in $B$, scaled by that weight and translated to $y$, lies in $B$ uniformly over $Y$.

On a bounded open neighborhood of $Y$ with closure in $\operatorname{int}B$, tighten containing caps through $(0,y)$. A pure-base cap has mass at least $\min(a+b,b+c)>M/2$ using $a<c$ and $c<a+b$. For a unit continuous normal $u$ and real tilt $v$ put
$$
F(y,u,v)=\sum_{z=-1}^1\bigl|K_z\cap\{x:u\cdot(x-y)+vz\ge0\}\bigr|.
$$
Opposite nonvertical caps through $(0,y)$ sum to $M$ because their common boundary has zero $d$-volume in every fiber. Choose $L$ with $L-1>\sup\lVert x-y\rVert$ over these fibers and the closure of the neighborhood. For $v\ge L-1$, the $+1$ fiber is full and the $-1$ fiber is empty, and for $v\le-L+1$ the roles reverse. The middle trace is independent of $v$. Thus $f(y)=\min_{\lVert u\rVert=1,|v|\le L}F(y,u,v)$, and minimizers can be chosen with $|v|\le L-1$. Parameter changes of size $\delta$ change membership in each bounded fiber only in a slab of thickness $O(\delta)$ about the old cut. Enclosing each fiber in a ball bounds its orthogonal projected area uniformly, so $F$ is jointly locally Lipschitz in sphere charts with uniform $y$-Lipschitz control. Consequently $f$ is locally Lipschitz and attains its maximum on $Y$.

Apply clarke-gradient-active-marginal-minimizer with the fixed compact interior subset of parameters $\lVert u\rVert=1$, $|v|\le L-1$. For each gradient limit $g$, this gives a minimizing pair $(u,v)$ and a joint Clarke gradient with $y$ component $g$ and zero tilt and sphere components. We characterize the joint gradients at any such $(y,u,v)$. At points where nonproper traces are $H^{d-1}$-null, 1269c554ca9a41b8 §2 gives the regular first variation. Under the substitutions of base coefficient $v$ and threshold $u\cdot y$, its components in $y,v$ and the tangent space of $u$ are
$$
-Su,\qquad \sum_z z S_z,\qquad \operatorname{proj}_{u^\perp}(X-Sy),
$$
using the section mass and moment $S=\sum_z S_z$ and $X=\sum_z X_z$. One can take the gradient limits off all supporting coincidences: for each fixed $(u,v)$ and fiber, a coincidence requires $u\cdot y$ to have one of the two supporting values shifted by $vz$, so Fubini makes this parameter set null in each chart. The marginal lemma's shrinking-hull argument permits its omission. In a chart the sphere derivative is transported to the limiting tangent space by the continuously varying coordinate differential.

For each approaching regular section, orthogonally project to the limiting fiber plane. The pushforward of its $H^{d-1}$ has density at most $1+o(1)$ on that plane, by the projection Jacobian between planes with converging unit normals. Its support is uniformly bounded and every limit point is in the limiting trace. Thus any weak limit is dominated by $H^{d-1}$ on that trace. For a proper limiting trace the projected indicators tend to one at its relative-interior points, which are interior to the body, and vanish off the trace. Its relative boundary is null, so the limit is the ordinary section measure. The zero-dimensional version uses counting measure on the point of a proper trace and zero or one on an approaching endpoint. The admissible measures on each limiting trace form a compact convex set. The displayed linear differential therefore still represents every joint Clarke gradient at the limit with the same effective trace density in all components. In particular the selected gradients give $\sum_z zS_z=0$ and $\operatorname{proj}_{u^\perp}(X-Sy_*)=0$. Integrating the cutting-plane equation gives $u\cdot(X-Sy_*)+v\sum_z zS_z=0$, so $X=Sy_*$. The middle trace is proper and has $S_0>0$.

At a constrained maximum of $f$ over $Y$, some element of $\partial_C f(y_*)$ lies in the normal cone $N_Y(y_*)=\{q:q\cdot(y-y_*)\le0,\ y\in Y\}$. Indeed, if these sets were disjoint, strict separation of the compact Clarke gradient from the closed cone would give a direction in the polar of the normal cone, positive on every Clarke gradient by a common margin. This polar is the closure of positive multiples of feasible secants, by separation for convex cones. Approximate the direction by such a multiple, keeping the common strict margin. Ordinary gradients near $y_*$ still have uniformly positive dot product with it by the shrinking-hull description. Integration on nearby parallel segments and continuity, as in the marginal lemma, make $f$ increase on a small feasible segment, a contradiction.

Each limiting ordinary $y$-gradient of $f$ has the form $-S u$ just proved. The set of these gradient limits at $y_*$ is compact by the local Lipschitz bound and a diagonal choice of differentiability points. Thus its convex hull already equals $\partial_C f(y_*)$. Carathéodory's theorem in $\mathbb R^d$ represents the selected normal by at most $d+1$ such vectors. Removing zero weights gives the asserted caps and weights. Finally, a nonzero $\bar u$ minimizes the linear functional $\bar u\cdot y$ over $Y$ at $y_*$, which is the support maximization in direction $-\bar u$ on $\mathsf M_a(C)$. Apply dcccecd70b052857 (1), (12)–(13) to the original full-dimensional $C$ with $0<a<c$. Its uniqueness of the representing cap indicator gives the stated selector.
