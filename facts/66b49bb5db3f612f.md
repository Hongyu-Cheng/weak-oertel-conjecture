## Statement

Let $B=[-2,2]^2$, $P=B\cap\mathbb Z^2$, $q(z)=2z_1+z_2$, $r(z)=7-|q(z)|$, and $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(2,-1),(-1,2),(-1,-1)\}$. Put $K_z=r(z)T$ and $C=\{(z,X):z\in B,\ X\in K_z\}$. The mixed measure is the sum of the $2$-areas of the sampled fibers.

The body is the canonical convex hull of all 25 integer fibers, with $k(P)=4$ occupied parities, $\sum_P r=109$, $\sum_P r^2=551$, and total mass $M=4959/2$. Its unique globally deepest mixed point is $Y=(0,0)$, of normalized depth $4/9$. The only oriented critical normals through $Y$, up to positive scaling, are those of $H_j=\{v_j\cdot X\ge0\}$ with $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$ and $v_3=(-1,-1)$. Each cap has mass $1102$.

These three continuous normals form an irreducible positive circuit. Each cap has coarea $2r(z)$ on fiber $z$ in the displayed normal scaling, and the full mixed coarea-section centroid is $Y$ for each cap. The normalized circuit response weights are $\theta_j=1/3$. Put $\gamma=4/9$ and $W=\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf1_{H_j}$ on the sampled boundary, with $\eta$ its summed length measure. There is a nontrivial physical martingale from $(W/\gamma)\eta$ to $\eta$ supported on pairs on the same fiber edge, and hence preserving the edge normal. In particular $\int (W-\gamma)\Phi(n,z,X)\,d\eta\le0$ for every continuous field convex in $(z,X)$ at fixed normal $n$, with equality for such affine fields.

For the fixed continuous normal $-e_1$ the support roof is $r$ on $B$. Among finite covers of $P$ by closed minors $S=P\cap\operatorname{conv}(S)$ each contained in a single affine upper contact face of this roof, the minimum of the sum of their occupied parity counts is $8>k(P)=4$.

## Proof

**Body, depth and active caps.** The function $r=\min(7-q,7+q)$ is concave and is at least one on $B$. The triangle contains zero, so Minkowski interpolation of the homothetic fibers and concavity of $r$ prove convexity of $C$. The polygons $B_\pm=B\cap\{\pm q\ge0\}$ have vertices given by their respective two square corners and the crease endpoints $(-1,2),(1,-2)$. These vertices are sampled, and $r$ is affine on each polygon. Express any real base as a convex combination of the vertices of its polygon. The Minkowski combination of the sampled fibers with those coefficients is exactly $r(z)T$, proving the canonical hull assertion. Counting $|q|=0,1,\ldots,6$ on $P$ gives multiplicities $(3,4,6,4,4,2,2)$. This yields both radius sums and, since $|T|=9/2$, the claimed mass. The square census occupies all four parities.

The triangle centroid is zero. For $u\ne0$ write $F_u(s)=|T\cap\{u\cdot X\ge s\}|/|T|$. By accepted fact 6a7d56e450b3126f at $d=2$, the paired sum $F_u(s)+F_u(-s)$ is at least $8/9$ at every real shift. A through-$Y$ cap $\{a\cdot z+u\cdot X\ge0\}$ sees opposite normalized thresholds in the equal-radius pair $z,-z$, so its pair mass is at least $4/9$ of the pair volume. Its central-fiber fraction is also at least $4/9$. Pure-base through-$Y$ caps contain at least half the mass by reflection. Tightening any containing cap to $Y$ only decreases mass, so this bounds every containing cap. Each of the three displayed caps has exactly fraction $4/9$ at every fiber by similar triangles. These caps cover the entire ambient space since their three margins sum to zero. Thus every mixed point has depth at most $4M/9$, and $Y$ attains it.

For $X\ne0$, some $v_j\cdot X>0$. The corresponding parallel cap through $(p,X)$ has strictly smaller mass than $H_j$, since raising the threshold from zero removes a positive-area strip in the central fiber. For a nonzero integer base $p$ at $X=0$, use the through-$(p,0)$ cap $\{v_j\cdot X+t p\cdot(z-p)\ge0\}$. For small $t$, all normalized cuts are in the isolated-vertex regime where $F_{v_j}(s)=(2-s)^2/9$ for $-1\le s\le2$. Its mass is
$$
\frac{|T|}{9}\sum_{z\in P}(2r(z)+t p\cdot(z-p))^2
=\frac49M-\frac{4|T|}{9}\,109\,t\lVert p\rVert^2+
\frac{|T|}{9}\,t^2\sum_{z\in P}(p\cdot(z-p))^2.
$$
Here $\sum_P r(z)z=0$ by reflection. This is strictly below $4M/9$ for sufficiently small positive $t$, proving uniqueness.

A critical nonvertical through-$Y$ cap must attain equality on the positive central fiber. Its three barycentric coordinates there are $1/3$, so accepted fact 700febf4b7a92759 at $d=2$ requires one of the three canonical normals: the cut isolates one vertex and meets its two incident edges at fraction $2/3$ from that vertex. Rescale to $u=v_j$. The two tails sum to $(8+2s^2)/9$ for $|s|\le1$, and to at least one for $|s|\ge1$ because the lower of the two thresholds is then at or below the minimum projection $-1$. Equality in every reflected pair therefore forces $a\cdot z=0$ for every $z\in P$. Since $P$ spans the base, $a=0$.

**Coareas and transport.** The two axis sections in $rT$ have length $2r$ and the third has length $2\sqrt2\,r$. All are centered at zero. Dividing each length by $\lVert v_j\rVert$ gives $2r$, so both the continuous first moment and the reflected sum of base first moments vanish. The unique positive dependence among the displayed $v_j$ is proportional to $(1,1,1)$. With unit normals the circuit coefficients are proportional to $\lVert v_j\rVert$ and the total threshold derivatives are proportional to the same lengths, so dividing by the derivatives and normalizing gives response weights $1/3$.

On each fiber edge take the affine coordinate $s\in[0,1]$. At its endpoints the triples of margins are permutations of $(2r,-r,-r)$ with different positive indices. Thus $W=1/3$ on the two outer thirds and $W=2/3$ on the middle third, up to length-null endpoints. The source density relative to edge length is $3/4$ outside and $3/2$ in the middle. Keep an outer point fixed. A middle point moves to $s,s-1/3,s+1/3$ with probabilities $2/3,1/6,1/6$. Its mean stays $s$. The stationary branch gives density one inside, and each shifted branch adds $1/4$ on its outer third, bringing its density to one. The kernel stays on the same fiber edge, whose normal is constant except at vertices of zero length. The combined kernel is nontrivial on every middle third. Conditional Jensen in the physical point gives the asserted integral inequality, with conditional equality for affine fields.

**Parity cost.** The support of $r(z)T$ in direction $-e_1$ is $r(z)$. A contact face lies in one of $B_-$ or $B_+$, since on a segment between strict opposite sides the concave roof strictly exceeds the chord at the crease and an affine upper contact plane cannot touch at both endpoints. The points $(-2,0),(-2,-1),(-1,0),(-1,-1)$ on the strict negative side realize the four parities, and their negatives realize the same parities on the strict positive side. For each parity two distinct cover members are needed to cover the two representatives. Summing over the four parities gives eight. Both $P\cap B_\pm$ are closed minors because $B_\pm$ are spanned by sampled vertices. They cover $P$ and each occupies four parities, giving equality.
