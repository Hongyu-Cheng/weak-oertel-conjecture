## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $n\ge0$ and $d\ge1$, with nonempty mixed trace $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. For each nonempty integer fiber $K_z$, let $q$ be the maximum affine dimension of these fibers, and assume $q\ge1$. Put $a_z=\mathcal H_q(K_z)$, $r_z=a_z^{1/q}$ and $M=\mathcal H_q(S)=\sum_z a_z$. Suppose an affine function $\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ satisfies $\ell(z)\ge r_z$ at every nonempty integer fiber. Set $E=\{z:\ell(z)=r_z>0\}$, $W_E=\sum_{z\in E}a_z$ and let $g_z$ be the intrinsic centroid of $K_z$ for $z\in E$.

If $z_i\in E$ and $w=\sum_i\lambda_i z_i\in\mathbb Z^n$ with $\lambda_i>0$ and $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, then $w\in E$, $K_w=\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}$ and $g_w=\sum_i\lambda_i g_{z_i}$. The participating fibers have parallel affine hulls and are positive homothets. In particular $\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^n=E$, and the mixed trace of the convex hull of $T_E=\{(z,g_z):z\in E\}$ is exactly $T_E$.

If $E$ is nonempty, some $y\in T_E$ satisfies $\mathcal H_q(S\cap H)\ge\gamma_q W_E/2^n$ for every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing $y$, where $\gamma_j=(j/(j+1))^j$. Its normalized depth is at least $\gamma_q W_E/(2^n M)\ge\gamma_d W_E/(2^n M)$. In particular $W_E=M$ when all positive fibers are contacts. If $E$ is empty, the zero lower bound holds at any actual point of $S$.

## Proof

The integer fiber set is finite. Each fiber of affine dimension $q$ has positive finite intrinsic measure, lower-dimensional fibers have zero $\mathcal H_q$ measure, and the lifted fibers are disjoint isometric copies of the continuous fibers. This gives the formula for $M>0$.

For an integer-anchored combination as in the statement, convexity gives $K'=\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}\subseteq K_w$. Its direction space is the sum of those of the input fibers: fixing all other summands exhibits each input direction, and the reverse inclusion follows by taking differences. Each input has dimension $q$, and $\dim\operatorname{aff}K_w\le q$, so all these spaces equal one $q$-space. Translate the bodies into this space and apply ordinary Brunn–Minkowski there. The inclusion, affine contact identities, and majorant at $w$ give
$$
r_w\ \ge\ \mathcal H_q(K')^{1/q}\ \ge\ \sum_i\lambda_i r_{z_i}
   \ =\ \ell(w)\ \ge\ r_w.
$$
All terms equal a positive number, so $w\in E$. The containment has equal positive volume in a common affine plane, so $K'=K_w$: a point outside the smaller compact convex set can be strictly separated from it, and small mixtures of that point with an interior ball of the larger set produce a positive-volume ball in the difference.

For $q=1$ the source intervals are positive homothets. For $q>1$ equality in ordinary positive-volume Brunn–Minkowski gives positive homothety for two bodies. For more than two, group the first bodies as their normalized Minkowski average and apply the two-body inequality to that average and the last body. Equality in the full chain implies equality for the grouped average and the outer inequality. Induction makes the grouped bodies homothetic to each other, their Minkowski average is a homothet of each, and the outer equality makes it homothetic to the last. Writing $K_{z_i}=\rho_i B+t_i$ for a common convex body $B$ in the common direction space, with $\rho_i>0$, their sum is $(\sum_i\lambda_i\rho_i)B+\sum_i\lambda_i t_i$. Its centroid is exactly the weighted mean of their centroids. Since $K'=K_w$, this proves the centroid identity. Applying it to each point of the centroid hull over an integer base proves both saturation identities.

For $n\ge1$ and $E\ne\varnothing$, the singleton identity supplies same-parity midpoint closure of $T_E$. Apply bee3cc8e50a0dcea to this graph with weights $a_z$. It selects $y\in T_E$ such that each containing closed halfspace has captured atomic weight at least $W_E/2^n$. When $n=0$ there is only one contact centroid and the same assertion is immediate. For every captured centroid, a nonconstant restriction of the halfspace to its fiber plane captures at least $\gamma_q a_z$ by 6769574fc1bd9f8d applied to the isometric $q$-body with its full volume as the metronoid mass. A constant containing restriction captures all $a_z$. Summing gives the desired raw bound. Finally $q\le d$ and $t\log(t/(t+1))$ is decreasing for $t>0$ since $\log(1+1/t)>1/(t+1)$, obtained by integrating $1/(1+s)$ on $[0,1/t]$. This gives the second normalized inequality.
