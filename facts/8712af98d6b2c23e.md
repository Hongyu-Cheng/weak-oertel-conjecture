## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and put $t=1/(d+1)$ and $\Gamma=(dt)^d$. For $a>0$, $0\le\beta\le at$ and $h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, set $r_h=(1-ah)_+$, $q_h=t-\beta h$ and $p_h=t+\beta(h+1)$, where $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. The diagonal-envelope deficit is
$$
D(a,\beta)=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0}r_h^d
-d^d\sum_{\substack{h\ge0\\r_h>0}}
  \left[q_h^{d-1}(r_h-dq_h)_++p_h^{d-1}(r_h-dp_h)_+\right].
$$
Both sums have only finitely many nonzero terms. Whenever $r_h>0$, one has $q_h\ge tr_h>0$ and $p_h\ge t>0$.

With the constants $C_d$ and $K_d$ of the uniform deficit expansion, define
$$
\varepsilon_d=\min\left\{\frac1{2d},\frac1{4[C_d+(d+1)K_d^2]}\right\}.
$$
For every $0<a\le1/(2d)$ and $0\le\beta\le at$,
$$
\frac{D(a,\beta)}{\Gamma}\le -\frac12+[C_d+(d+1)K_d^2]a.
$$
In particular $D(a,\beta)\le-\Gamma/4$ for all $0<a\le\varepsilon_d$ and $0\le\beta\le at$.

## Proof

For $v=\beta/(at)\in[0,1]$, the paired kernels in the uniform quartic gain theorem are $K_{-v}=L_v$ and $K_v=R_{0,v}$ from the deficit expansion. Their support endpoints and polynomial factors agree. The dimension is an integer $d\ge2$, so the theorem gives $G_d(v)\ge v^2/[4(d+1)]$, including $G_d(0)=0$.

The deficit expansion holds throughout the stated slope and tilt range. Use its bounds $|J_d(v)|\le K_d v$ and $|\rho_d(a,v)|\le C_da$ to obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{D(a,atv)}{\Gamma}
&\le-\frac12-\frac{v^2}{4(d+1)a}+K_d v+C_d a\\
&=-\frac12+[C_d+(d+1)K_d^2]a
 -\frac{[v-2(d+1)aK_d]^2}{4(d+1)a}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last term is nonpositive for every admissible $v$. Since the constants are positive, $a\le\varepsilon_d$ makes the preceding linear correction at most $1/4$.
