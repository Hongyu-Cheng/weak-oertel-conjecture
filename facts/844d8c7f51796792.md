## Statement

Let \(g_0\in(0,1/100]\) be the absolute constant in accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e`. Denote that fact's constant \(C=16+g_0^{-2}\) by \(C_H\) throughout this theorem. Thus \(C_H\) is a scalar constant, distinct from the convex body \(C\) used below. Put \(A_H:=4C_H\) and \(K:=64A_H\). Let \(s_0\) be the least integer at least one for which \(g_*:=2^{-s_0}\le(2\sqrt{C_H})^{-1}\), and set \(a_0:=(g_*/32)\exp(-4/g_*)\) and \(a:=a_0\exp(-24C_HK)\). These are positive absolute constants independent of all dimensions, support lengths, and factor values.

For every \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(b_i:\mathbb Z\to[0,\infty)\), \(1\le i\le n\), have finite, nonempty, consecutive positive support and satisfy \(b_i(k)^2\ge b_i(k-1)b_i(k+1)\) for every integer \(k\). Define \(W_i:=\sum_k b_i(k)\), \(w_z:=\prod_{i=1}^n b_i(z_i)\) for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), and \(W:=\sum_z w_z=\prod_i W_i>0\). All logarithms below are natural.

For each \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), put \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\) and \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\). The dependence of \(\alpha\) on the fixed center \(j\) is suppressed, exactly as in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. For \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), put \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\), and for \(L\ge0\) put \(B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j:\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\). These sums use all oriented displacements, including zero. Then

\[
\max_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z^n,\ w_j>0\\L\ge0}}
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}
\ge a\,2^{-n}W.
\]

The maximum is attained at a positive center \(j\) and a value \(L\in\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\); there are only finitely many such choices.

Consequently, let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Put \(C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), and \(W:=\mathcal H_d(S)\). Suppose \(W>0\) and that for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) its integer fiber volumes satisfy \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)=\prod_i b_i(z_i)\), where the factors satisfy the preceding hypotheses. Let \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\) and \(c_0:=\min\{a\ell/16,1/(2e)\}\). There exists a single point \(p^\star\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(p^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

Here \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\) is absolute, and the point is selected before the halfspace. If \(S\ne\varnothing\) and \(W=0\), the same depth conclusion holds at every point of \(S\).

More generally, keep a convex body \(C\) and let \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\). Suppose there are a product table \(\widetilde w_z:=\prod_i b_i(z_i)\) with the same factor hypotheses, a scale \(\tau>0\), and a comparison parameter \(R\ge1\) such that \(\tau R^{-1}\widetilde w_z\le w_z\le\tau R\widetilde w_z\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\). Then there exists a single \(p^\star\in S\) for which every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing it satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W/[R^2(1+4\log R)]\). Here \(W=\sum_z w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\); the comparison inequalities imply that the actual and model tables have the same positive support. The constant \(c_0\) is the same absolute constant as above, while the displayed loss records the dependence on \(R\).

## Proof

First fix the factors. Each \(W_i\) is positive and finite, and the finite product identity for \(W\) follows by expanding the sums. The positive support of \(w\) is the Cartesian product of the positive supports of the factors, so it is finite and nonempty. Concavity of \(\log b_i\) on its positive integer interval gives \(b_i(k)^2\ge b_i(k+u)b_i(k-u)\) whenever both endpoint weights are positive. Equivalently, this is the nonnegativity of the one-dimensional reflection curvature in accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e`. Multiplying these inequalities over coordinates proves that \(\Delta_j\) is finite, nonnegative, and even on \(\mathcal U_j\), with \(\Delta_j(0)=0\).

For each factor define \(M_i:=\max_k b_i(k)\), \(H_{i,k}:=\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z}\sqrt{b_i(k+u)b_i(k-u)}\), \(H_i:=\max_k H_{i,k}\), and \(g_i:=\log(2H_i/W_i)\). Choose a maximizing center \(k_i\) for \(H_{i,k}\) and a maximizing atom \(m_i\) for \(b_i\). Both choices exist; a center with \(H_{i,k}>0\) lies in the finite positive support, and \(H_{i,m_i}\ge M_i>0\). In particular, \(b_i(k_i)>0\).

To fix the normalization precisely, \(W_i,M_i,H_{i,k},H_i,g_i\) are the objects \(W,M,H_j,H_*,g\) of accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e` for the factor \(b=b_i\), with its scalar constant \(C\) renamed \(C_H\). At its center \(j=k_i\), write its \(\mathcal U_j,Q_j,\Delta_j,T_j\) as \(\mathcal U_i,Q_i,\Delta_i,T_i\). Explicitly, \(\mathcal U_i:=\{u:b_i(k_i+u)b_i(k_i-u)>0\}\) and

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_i(u)&=\frac{\sqrt{b_i(k_i+u)b_i(k_i-u)}}{H_i},\\
\Delta_i(u)&=\log\frac{b_i(k_i)^2}{b_i(k_i+u)b_i(k_i-u)},\\
T_i(u)&=\frac12\log\frac{b_i(k_i+u)}{b_i(k_i-u)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The probability law \(Q_i\) includes zero, is even, and is positive at every point of \(\mathcal U_i\). Define \(D_i:=\mathbb E_{Q_i}\Delta_i\), \(V_i:=\operatorname{Var}_{Q_i}(T_i)\), and \(\delta_i:=(1-2M_i/W_i)_+\). The accepted theorem gives \(0\le g_i\le\log2\), \(\mathbb E_{Q_i}T_i=0\), \(0\le D_i,V_i\le2\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_i&\le C_Hg_i^2,\\
D_i&\le C_Hg_i\log(e/g_i),\\
V_i&\le C_Hg_i[\log(e/g_i)]^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

At \(g_i=0\) the last two right sides mean zero. The same accepted theorem says that \(g_i=0\) holds exactly for a factor supported on two adjacent integers with equal weights \(W_i/2\). Thus such a factor can be fixed at \(m_i\) with weight exactly \(W_i/2\). The moment estimates will be used at the maximizing centers \(k_i\); coordinates fixed later at \(m_i\) use only displacement zero.

We next prove the estimate used for each retained block of coordinates. Let \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) be nonempty, and equip \(\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal U_i\) with the independent product probability law \(Q_I:=\bigotimes_{i\in I}Q_i\). Put \(G_I:=\sum_{i\in I}g_i\), \(D_I:=\sum_{i\in I}D_i\), and \(V_I:=\sum_{i\in I}V_i\). The random sum \(\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_i(v_i)\) is nonnegative and has mean \(D_I\); the random sum \(\sum_{i\in I}T_i(v_i)\) has mean zero and variance \(V_I\). Define \(L_I:=4D_I\) and

\[
E_I:=\left\{v_I\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal U_i:
\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_i(v_i)\le4D_I,
\quad\left|\sum_{i\in I}T_i(v_i)\right|\le2\sqrt{V_I}\right\}.
\]

If \(D_I>0\), Markov's inequality bounds the probability of violating the first condition by \(1/4\). If \(D_I=0\), the nonnegative sum is zero almost surely, so that condition holds with probability one. If \(V_I>0\), Chebyshev's inequality bounds the probability of violating the second condition by \(1/4\). If \(V_I=0\), the mean-zero sum is zero almost surely, so the second condition also holds with probability one. Hence \(Q_I(E_I)\ge1/2\) in every case. Since each \(\Delta_i\) is even and each \(T_i\) is odd, \(E_I\) is invariant under \(v_I\mapsto-v_I\). It contains the zero displacement.

For \(v_I\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal U_i\), define the block reflected weight \(\alpha_I(v_I):=\min\{\prod_{i\in I}b_i(k_i+v_i),\prod_{i\in I}b_i(k_i-v_i)\}\). This is a minimum of the two entire endpoint products. For positive real numbers \(u,v\), the identity \(\min(u,v)=\sqrt{uv}\exp(-|\log(u/v)|/2)\) gives the exact normalization

\[
\alpha_I(v_I)
=\left(\prod_{i\in I}H_i\right)Q_I(v_I)
\exp\left(-\left|\sum_{i\in I}T_i(v_i)\right|\right).
\]

Therefore the block mass \(Z_I:=\sum_{v_I\in E_I}\alpha_I(v_I)\) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_I
&\ge\frac12\exp(-2\sqrt{V_I})\prod_{i\in I}H_i\\
&=\frac12\exp(G_I-2\sqrt{V_I})
\prod_{i\in I}\frac{W_i}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every displacement counted by \(Z_I\) has block curvature at most \(L_I\). All quantities here are finite because all factors have finite positive support and the logarithms are taken only at positive weights.

Now partition the coordinates by their gains. Put \(I_>:=\{i:g_i>g_*\}\). For each integer \(s\ge s_0\), put \(I_s:=\{i:2^{-s-1}<g_i\le2^{-s}\}\), \(S_s:=s+1\), and \(G_s:=\sum_{i\in I_s}g_i\). Every coordinate with \(0<g_i\le g_*\) belongs to exactly one of these bins, including those on a dyadic boundary. Ignore empty bins. There are at most \(n\) nonempty bins, even though their indices can be arbitrarily large. Retain a nonempty bin exactly when \(G_s\ge K S_s^2\); otherwise discard it. Let \(F\) consist of all coordinates with zero gain and all coordinates in discarded bins. These coordinates will be fixed at their maximizing atoms. The remaining coordinates form the large-gain block \(I_>\) and the retained bins.

We bound the loss from fixing the coordinates in \(F\). The definition of \(s_0\) is possible since \(2^{-s}\to0\). It gives \(0<g_*\le1/2\) and \(C_Hg_*^2\le1/4\). Thus every coordinate in a discarded bin has \(\delta_i\le C_Hg_i^2\le1/4\). If \(2M_i/W_i\ge1\), then \(\log(2M_i/W_i)\ge0\). Otherwise \(2M_i/W_i=1-\delta_i\), and

\[
-\log(1-\delta_i)
=\int_0^{\delta_i}\frac{dt}{1-t}
\le\frac{\delta_i}{1-\delta_i}
\le2\delta_i
\le2C_Hg_i^2.
\]

Consequently \(M_i/(W_i/2)\ge\exp(-2C_Hg_i^2)\) in both cases. In a discarded bin,
\(\sum_{i\in I_s}g_i^2\le2^{-s}G_s<K S_s^2 2^{-s}\). The identity \(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(s+1)^2x^s=(1+x)/(1-x)^3\) for \(0\le x<1\), obtained by differentiating the geometric series twice, gives \(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}(s+1)^2 2^{-s}=12\). The actual sum over discarded nonempty bins is finite, so it is bounded by this full convergent series. Including the zero-gain coordinates, whose ratios are exactly one, proves

\[
\prod_{i\in F}M_i
\ge\exp(-24C_HK)\prod_{i\in F}\frac{W_i}{2}.
\]

The empty product convention makes this inequality valid if \(F\) is empty.

For each retained bin, apply the block construction with \(I=I_s\), and write its quantities as \(D_s,V_s,L_s,E_s,Z_s\). When \(i\in I_s\), one has \(\log(e/g_i)<1+S_s\log2\le2S_s\), since \(S_s\ge1\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_s&\le2C_H S_sG_s,\\
V_s&\le4C_H S_s^2G_s=A_H S_s^2G_s,\\
L_s=4D_s&\le8C_H S_sG_s=2A_H S_sG_s.
\end{aligned}
\]

The retained-bin threshold \(G_s\ge64A_H S_s^2\) implies \(G_s\ge16A_H S_s^2\) and \(G_s\ge32A_H S_s\). It therefore gives \(2\sqrt{V_s}\le G_s/2\) and \(L_s\le G_s^2/16\). It also gives \(G_s\ge2\), since \(C_H\ge16\) and \(S_s\ge1\). For every real \(G\ge2\), the elementary exponential bound yields

\[
\frac12e^{G/2}
\ge\frac12+\frac G4+\frac{G^2}{16}
\ge1+\frac{G^2}{16}.
\]

Combining this inequality with the block mass estimate proves, for every retained bin,

\[
Z_s\ge\frac12e^{G_s/2}\prod_{i\in I_s}\frac{W_i}{2}
\ge(1+L_s)\prod_{i\in I_s}\frac{W_i}{2}.
\]

The large-gain block is retained whenever nonempty. Suppose first that \(m:=|I_>|\ge1\). Use the block construction and the notation \(G_>,D_>,V_>,L_>,E_>,Z_>\). The accepted uniform moment bounds give \(D_>\le2m\), \(V_>\le2m\), and \(L_>\le8m\), while \(G_>\ge mg_*\). Thus

\[
\frac{Z_>}{1+L_>}
\ge\frac{\exp(mg_*-2\sqrt{2m})}{2(1+8m)}
\prod_{i\in I_>}\frac{W_i}{2}.
\]

The following two elementary inequalities hold for every \(m\ge0\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{mg_*}{2}-2\sqrt{2m}
&=\left(\sqrt{\frac{mg_*}{2}}-\frac2{\sqrt{g_*}}\right)^2
-\frac4{g_*}
\ge-\frac4{g_*},\\
1+8m
&=\frac{16}{g_*}\left(\frac{g_*}{16}+\frac{mg_*}{2}\right)
\le\frac{16}{g_*}\left(1+\frac{mg_*}{2}\right)
\le\frac{16}{g_*}e^{mg_*/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second uses \(g_*\le1/2\). Splitting the exponent \(mg_*\) into two equal parts therefore gives
\(Z_>\ge a_0(1+L_>)\prod_{i\in I_>}(W_i/2)\).
If \(I_>\) is empty, define \(E_>\) to contain the single empty tuple, \(Z_>:=1\), and \(L_>:=0\). Since \(0<a_0<1\), the same inequality holds with the empty product equal to one. This handles an empty large-gain block without charging its coefficient more than once.

We now combine the choices. Let \(\mathcal I\) be the finite collection consisting of \(I_>\), with the preceding empty convention if necessary, and all retained bins. Its nonempty members and \(F\) partition the coordinates. Choose the global center by \(j_i=m_i\) for \(i\in F\) and \(j_i=k_i\) otherwise. Then \(w_j>0\). Put \(L:=\sum_{I\in\mathcal I}L_I\), and let \(E\) consist of the global displacements with \(v_i=0\) on \(F\) and \(v_I\in E_I\) for every \(I\in\mathcal I\). All endpoint weights for such displacements are positive, so \(E\subseteq\mathcal U_j\). It is a finite even set containing zero. The exact product identity for the curvature gives

\[
\Delta_j(v)
=\sum_{I\in\mathcal I}\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_i(v_i)
\le\sum_{I\in\mathcal I}L_I=L
\qquad(v\in E),
\]

because the fixed coordinates contribute zero curvature. For any finite collection of positive pairs, the minimum of the products is at least the product of the minima: both products separately are at least that product of minima. Applying this observation to the two global endpoint weights gives

\[
\alpha(v)\ge\left(\prod_{i\in F}M_i\right)
\prod_{I\in\mathcal I}\alpha_I(v_I)
\qquad(v\in E),
\]

where the empty block contributes one. Summing over the Cartesian product defining \(E\) and using its curvature bound now yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)
&\ge\sum_{v\in E}\alpha(v)\\
&\ge\left(\prod_{i\in F}M_i\right)
\prod_{I\in\mathcal I}Z_I\\
&\ge a_0e^{-24C_HK}
\left(\prod_{I\in\mathcal I}(1+L_I)\right)
\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{W_i}{2}\\
&\ge a(1+L)\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last inequality follows by expanding the finite product of \(1+L_I\): all terms are nonnegative and it contains the constant term one and every linear term \(L_I\). This proves the scalar inequality at one specific finite center and cutoff, hence also for the supremum over centers and cutoffs. The block and freezing estimates have introduced only \(a_0\) once and the bounded total freezing loss, so the resulting constant is independent of \(n\), the number of bins, and the support lengths.

For completeness, this supremum is the stated attained maximum. There are finitely many positive centers \(j\). For each one, \(\mathcal U_j\) is finite and contains zero, and its finite set of curvature values contains zero. Given any \(L\ge0\), choose the largest such curvature value \(t\le L\). Then \(B_j^\Delta(t)=B_j^\Delta(L)\) and \(1+t\le1+L\). Thus \(B_j^\Delta(t)/(1+t)\ge B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\). Maximizing over the finitely many centers and their finitely many curvature values proves attainment and the asserted scalar maximum.

Finally consider the actual convex body in the statement. Its integer fiber volumes are exactly the scalar weights just treated, and the mixed-integer mass \(\mathcal H_d(S)\) is exactly their sum \(W\). For every fixed positive center \(j\), the quantities \(\alpha(v),\mathcal U_j,\Delta_j(v),B_j^\Delta(L)\) above have exactly the definitions and normalization used in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`, with the same body \(C\), fibers \(C_z\), and weights \(w_z\). In particular, the sum is over oriented displacements including zero, so no factor from unordered pairs is inserted into the scalar estimate. The hypotheses of that accepted theorem hold because \(C\) is a convex body and \(W>0\). Its curvature-cutoff conclusion supplies one point \(p^\star\in S\) with

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(p^\star)
&\ge\frac{\ell}{16}
\max_{\substack{j\in\mathbb Z^n,\ w_j>0\\L\ge0}}
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}\\
&\ge\frac{a\ell}{16}\,2^{-n}W
\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), with the infimum over closed ambient halfspaces, is the same depth as in the accepted theorem and the problem statement. The theorem selects this one point before any halfspace, so the bound holds for every halfspace containing that point. Positivity and absoluteness of \(a\) and \(\ell\) imply \(0<c_0\le1/(2e)<1/e\). If \(W=0\) and \(S\ne\varnothing\), the desired lower bound is zero, so any point of \(S\) satisfies it.

To prove the comparison extension, use tildes for the scalar objects associated with the model table: \(\widetilde W:=\sum_z\widetilde w_z\), \(\widetilde\alpha(v):=\min\{\widetilde w_{j+v},\widetilde w_{j-v}\}\), \(\widetilde\Delta_j(v):=\log(\widetilde w_j^2/(\widetilde w_{j+v}\widetilde w_{j-v}))\), and \(\widetilde B_j^\Delta\) for its cutoff sum. The untilded objects are those of the actual body in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. The comparison bounds imply identical positive supports, hence identical eligible centers and positive reflected-pair sets, and \(W\le\tau R\widetilde W\). At every such center and pair,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&\ge\frac{\tau}{R}\widetilde\alpha(v),\\
\Delta_j(v)
&\le\log\frac{(\tau R\widetilde w_j)^2}
{(\tau R^{-1}\widetilde w_{j+v})(\tau R^{-1}\widetilde w_{j-v})}
=\widetilde\Delta_j(v)+4\log R.
\end{aligned}
\]

The actual curvature is nonnegative by the accepted actual-body theorem. Choose a model center and cutoff \((j,L)\) satisfying \(\widetilde B_j^\Delta(L)\ge a(1+L)2^{-n}\widetilde W\), as supplied by the proved scalar theorem. Put \(L':=L+4\log R\ge0\). Each pair counted by the model cutoff is counted by the actual cutoff at \(L'\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L')
&\ge\frac{\tau}{R}\widetilde B_j^\Delta(L)
\ge\frac{a\tau}{R}(1+L)2^{-n}\widetilde W
\ge\frac a{R^2}(1+L)2^{-n}W,\\
1+L'
&=1+L+4\log R
\le(1+L)(1+4\log R).
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently the actual scalar maximum is at least \(a\,2^{-n}W/[R^2(1+4\log R)]\). Applying the same accepted actual-body cutoff conclusion gives one point of depth at least \((a\ell/16)2^{-n}W/[R^2(1+4\log R)]\), which is at least the stated bound with \(c_0\). Its quantifier order is again one point before all containing halfspaces. The proof of this extension uses only the displayed comparison inequalities and makes no assertion that a general body admits a comparison parameter bounded independently of dimension.
