## Statement

Let \(n\geq1\) be an integer and let \(\mathcal F\) be a nonempty family of subsets of \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) such that \(E'\subseteq E\in\mathcal F\) implies \(E'\in\mathcal F\). For arbitrary \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n\in\mathbb R\), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\{I\in\{-1,0,1\}^n:\operatorname{supp}(I)\in\mathcal F\},
&P&=\operatorname{conv}(D),\\
w_I&=\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^n\beta_i I_i\right)\quad(I\in D),
&W&=\sum_{I\in D}w_I,
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\operatorname{supp}(I)=\{i:I_i\neq0\}\). Define the unnormalized depth against the entire original weight table and its weighted average by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_w(J)&=\inf\left\{\sum_{I\in D\cap H}w_I:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\right\},\\
A&=\sum_{J\in D}w_J\tau_w(J).
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and

\[
A\geq\frac{e^{-1/2}}{8\sqrt\pi}\,\frac{W^2}{2^n}.
\tag{1}
\]

No full-dimensionality assumption is required. In particular, this applies to every nonempty coordinate-sign-invariant lattice polytope contained in \([-1,1]^n\), including every truncated signed Hamming ball, with its complete lattice support and arbitrary weights of the displayed affine form.

## Proof

Nonemptiness and heredity give \(\varnothing\in\mathcal F\), hence \(0\in D\). Clearly \(D\subseteq P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and \(P\subseteq[-1,1]^n\). If \(x\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), then \(x\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\). Write \(x\) as a convex combination of points of \(D\), retaining only positive coefficients. For every \(i\in\operatorname{supp}(x)\), its coordinate \(x_i\) is either \(1\) or \(-1\), an endpoint of the coordinate interval. Each point in this convex combination therefore has coordinate \(x_i\) at \(i\). Thus the support of any one of these points contains \(\operatorname{supp}(x)\). Heredity gives \(\operatorname{supp}(x)\in\mathcal F\), and \(x\in D\). This proves completeness without a rank assumption.

For \(1\leq i\leq n\), put \(b_i=e^{\beta_i}+e^{-\beta_i}=2\cosh\beta_i\geq2\). For each \(E\subseteq[n]\), put \(v_E=\prod_{i\in E}b_i\), with the empty product equal to one, and set \(Q_{\mathrm{par}}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}v_E^2\). On the ternary cube, two points have the same coordinatewise parity exactly when their supports agree. For \(E\in\mathcal F\), the total original weight of this parity class is

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\\\operatorname{supp}(I)=E}}w_I
=\prod_{i\in E}(e^{-\beta_i}+e^{\beta_i})
=v_E,
\qquad W=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}v_E.
\tag{2}
\]

We prove that \(W^2\leq3\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}Q_{\mathrm{par}}\). First construct a partition of all subsets of \([n]\) into chains, each containing one set of every cardinality from some \(k\) through \(n-k\). The construction is by induction, starting with the singleton chain \((\varnothing)\) in dimension zero. Suppose such a partition has been constructed in dimension \(m\). An old chain
\(E_k\subset E_{k+1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{m-k}\), with \(|E_j|=j\), produces in dimension \(m+1\) the chain

\[
E_k\subset E_{k+1}\subset\cdots\subset E_{m-k}
\subset E_{m-k}\cup\{m+1\},
\]

and, when it is nonempty, the second chain

\[
E_k\cup\{m+1\}\subset E_{k+1}\cup\{m+1\}
\subset\cdots\subset E_{m-k-1}\cup\{m+1\}.
\]

The first chain has endpoint cardinalities \(k\) and \(m+1-k\); the second has endpoint cardinalities \(k+1\) and \(m-k\). Each is therefore of the required form. Together they partition the old chain and its copy with the new coordinate present. Old chains were disjoint, so these new chains partition the full Boolean lattice. This completes the induction.

Each resulting chain in dimension \(n\) contains exactly one set of cardinality \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Hence there are exactly \(M=\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\) chains. By heredity, each chain intersects \(\mathcal F\) in a possibly empty initial segment. Let \(u_1,\ldots,u_l\) be the values of \(v_E\) along a nonempty such segment, in increasing order of cardinality. At each step one coordinate is added, so \(u_{j+1}\geq2u_j\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sum_{j=1}^l u_j\right)^2
&=\sum_{j=1}^l u_j^2+2\sum_{j=1}^l u_j\sum_{i<j}u_i\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^l u_j^2
  +2\sum_{j=1}^l u_j^2\sum_{r=1}^{j-1}2^{-r}
\leq3\sum_{j=1}^l u_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For an empty segment both sides are zero. Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the \(M\) segment sums, and then the preceding inequality on each segment, gives \(W^2\leq3M Q_{\mathrm{par}}\).

The elementary estimate \(M\leq2^n/\sqrt{n+1}\) follows as well without an asymptotic formula. Put \(q_m=4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}\) for integers \(m\geq0\). We have \(q_0=1\) and \(q_{m+1}=q_m(2m+1)/(2m+2)\). Induction gives \(q_m^2\leq1/(2m+1)\), because

\[
q_{m+1}^2\leq\frac{2m+1}{(2m+2)^2}
\leq\frac1{2m+3},
\qquad (2m+1)(2m+3)=(2m+2)^2-1.
\]

For even \(n=2m\), this is the asserted bound. For odd \(n=2m+1\),
\(2^{-n}\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}=q_{m+1}\leq1/\sqrt{2m+3}\leq1/\sqrt{n+1}\). We have proved

\[
Q_{\mathrm{par}}\geq\frac{W^2}{3M}
\geq\frac{\sqrt{n+1}}{3\,2^n}W^2.
\tag{3}
\]

For each \(J\in D\), define the oriented, zero-inclusive reflected sum

\[
S_J=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathbb Z^n\\J+v\in D,\ J-v\in D}}
\min(w_{J+v},w_{J-v}).
\]

Fix any closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(J\). For every summand, at least one of \(J+v\) and \(J-v\) belongs to \(H\): writing \(H=\{x:a\cdot x\geq t\}\), their two defining affine values average to \(a\cdot J\geq t\). Therefore
\(w_{J+v}\mathbf1_H(J+v)+w_{J-v}\mathbf1_H(J-v)\geq\min(w_{J+v},w_{J-v})\).
Summing this inequality over all admissible \(v\), its left side is twice the original mass in \(H\) of the reflected source subset \(D\cap(2J-D)\). It is at most twice the mass of \(D\cap H\). This counting also holds for \(v=0\), where the left side is \(2w_J\). Taking the infimum over all original containing halfspaces gives

\[
\tau_w(J)\geq\tfrac12 S_J,
\qquad A\geq\tfrac12\sum_{J\in D}w_JS_J.
\tag{4}
\]

We now reindex this last sum exactly. The assignments \(I=J+v\), \(K=J-v\) give a bijection between its summation pairs \((J,v)\) and ordered endpoint pairs \((I,K)\in D^2\) of the same parity. To check the converse, equal parity in the ternary cube means \(\operatorname{supp}(I)=\operatorname{supp}(K)=E\in\mathcal F\). The midpoint \(J=(I+K)/2\) is ternary and has support contained in \(E\); hence \(J\in D\) by heredity. Also \(v=(I-K)/2\) is integral. These are the unique inverse assignments, including diagonal pairs.

Log-affinity gives \(w_I=w_Je^{-\sum_i\beta_i v_i}\), \(w_K=w_Je^{\sum_i\beta_i v_i}\), and \(w_Iw_K=w_J^2\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{J\in D}w_JS_J
&=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}
\sum_{\substack{I,K\in D\\\operatorname{supp}(I)=\operatorname{supp}(K)=E}}
w_Iw_K\exp\left(-\left|\sum_{i=1}^n\beta_i\frac{I_i-K_i}{2}\right|\right)\\
&=\sum_{E\in\mathcal F}v_E^2\,\mathbb E e^{-|Z_E|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Here, for each fixed \(E\), choose independent endpoints \(X,Y\) from that parity class with probabilities \(w_I/v_E\). Their coordinates in \(E\) are independent, with \(\mathbb P(X_i=s)=\mathbb P(Y_i=s)=e^{-\beta_i s}/b_i\) for \(s\in\{-1,1\}\), and their other coordinates vanish. Put \(V_i=(X_i-Y_i)/2\) for \(i\in E\) and \(Z_E=\sum_{i\in E}\beta_iV_i\). The \(V_i\) are independent and symmetric, with

\[
\mathbb P(V_i=1)=\mathbb P(V_i=-1)=\frac1{b_i^2},
\qquad \mathbb P(V_i=0)=1-\frac2{b_i^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(E=\varnothing\), these definitions give the sole ordered endpoint pair \((0,0)\), \(v_E=1\), and \(Z_E=0\). This verifies every normalization and multiplicity in (5).

We prove a uniform lower bound for the expectations in (5). The real Gaussian integral identity needed below is

\[
e^{-z^2}=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-s^2}\cos(2sz)\,ds
\qquad(z\in\mathbb R).
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, let \(G(z)\) denote the integral on the right before division by \(\sqrt\pi\). Differentiation under the integral is justified by the integrable bound \(2|s|e^{-s^2}\) on the derivative of the integrand. Integration by parts, whose boundary term vanishes, gives

\[
G'(z)=-2\int_{\mathbb R}s e^{-s^2}\sin(2sz)\,ds=-2zG(z).
\]

The positive Gaussian integral is finite, and its square is
\(\int_{\mathbb R^2}e^{-(s^2+t^2)}\,ds\,dt
=2\pi\int_0^\infty r e^{-r^2}\,dr=\pi\), by polar coordinates. Thus \(G(0)=\sqrt\pi\). Solving the displayed differential equation, or differentiating \(e^{z^2}G(z)\), proves (7).

For \(i\in E\), set

\[
h_i(t)=\mathbb E\cos(t\beta_iV_i)
=1-\frac2{b_i^2}+\frac2{b_i^2}\cos(\beta_i t).
\]

Symmetry makes \(\mathbb E\sin(t\beta_iV_i)=0\). Independence and the cosine addition formula therefore imply
\(\mathbb E\cos(2sZ_E)=\prod_{i\in E}h_i(2s)\).
Applying (7) and exchanging a finite expectation with its integral yields

\[
\mathbb E e^{-Z_E^2}
=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-s^2}
\prod_{i\in E}h_i(2s)\,ds.
\tag{8}
\]

Every factor is nonnegative on the whole real line: since \(b_i\geq2\),
\(h_i(t)\geq1-4/b_i^2\geq0\). This permits restricting the integral in (8) to any interval.

The inequalities \(\cosh t\geq1+t^2/2\geq|t|\) give \(\beta_i^2/b_i^2\leq1/4\). The first inequality follows from the even power series for \(\cosh\), and the second follows from \(1+t^2/2-|t|=((|t|-1)^2+1)/2\geq0\). Also \(1-\cos u=2\sin^2(u/2)\leq u^2/2\). Hence

\[
h_i(2s)\geq1-\frac{4\beta_i^2s^2}{b_i^2}\geq1-s^2.
\]

For \(|s|\leq1/(2\sqrt n)\), the numbers \(4\beta_i^2s^2/b_i^2\) lie in \([0,1]\). The inequality \(\prod_i(1-a_i)\geq1-\sum_i a_i\) for numbers \(a_i\in[0,1]\), obtained by induction, now gives

\[
\prod_{i\in E}h_i(2s)
\geq1-\sum_{i\in E}\frac{4\beta_i^2s^2}{b_i^2}
\geq1-ns^2\geq\frac34.
\]

This includes the empty product. On this interval, \(e^{-s^2}\geq e^{-1/4}\), and its length is \(1/\sqrt n\). Equation (8) consequently gives
\(\mathbb E e^{-Z_E^2}\geq3e^{-1/4}/(4\sqrt\pi\sqrt n)\).
Finally, \((|z|-1/2)^2\geq0\) is equivalent to
\(e^{-|z|}\geq e^{-1/4}e^{-z^2}\). We have established, for every \(E\in\mathcal F\),

\[
\mathbb E e^{-|Z_E|}\geq\frac{3e^{-1/2}}{4\sqrt\pi\sqrt n}.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (3), (4), (5), and (9) proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
A
&\geq\frac{3e^{-1/2}}{8\sqrt\pi\sqrt n}Q_{\mathrm{par}}\\
&\geq\frac{e^{-1/2}}{8\sqrt\pi}
\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{n}}\,\frac{W^2}{2^n}
\geq\frac{e^{-1/2}}{8\sqrt\pi}\,\frac{W^2}{2^n},
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (1). All sums are finite, and no step assumes that \(P\) has full dimension. In particular, the case \(\mathcal F=\{\varnothing\}\) is included: it has \(D=\{0\}\), \(W=\tau_w(0)=A=1\).

To verify the final geometric scope assertion, let \(P\subseteq[-1,1]^n\) be any nonempty lattice polytope invariant under independently changing coordinate signs, and let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Its vertices are integral, so \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\), and \(D\subseteq\{-1,0,1\}^n\). If \(I\in D\), changing the sign of one coordinate preserves membership in \(P\); averaging these two points deletes that coordinate and gives another integral point in \(P\). Repeating deletions shows that the family of supports of points in \(D\) is downward closed. Sign invariance supplies every ternary sign choice with each such support. Therefore this \(D\) has precisely the form used in the statement. Taking the family \(\{E\subseteq[n]:|E|\leq r\}\) for any integer \(0\leq r\leq n\) gives the truncated signed Hamming balls as particular cases.
