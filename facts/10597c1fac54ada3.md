## Statement

The anchoring claim is false already in the minimal dimensions \(n=d=1\). Let
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\{(-2,0),(1,-3/2),(1,3/2)\}\subseteq\mathbb R^2,
\qquad z_0=0,\qquad x_0=0,
\]
let \(q_{-1}=q_1=1/2\) and \(q_z=0\) otherwise, and let
\[
H=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2:z+x\leq0\}.
\]
Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body, \(C_{z_0}\) has positive length and centroid \(x_0\), \(q\) is a finitely supported probability distribution with mean \(z_0\), and \(\partial H\) contains \((z_0,x_0)\). Nevertheless, for
\[
h_z=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z\cap H_z),
\qquad b_z=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z)-h_z,
\]
one has
\[
\sum_z q_z b_z=\frac54>1=b_{z_0}.
\]
Thus the asserted \(b\)-inequality fails strictly. For the opposite closed halfspace
\(\widetilde H=\{(z,x):z+x\geq0\}\), the asserted \(h\)-inequality also fails strictly:
\[
\sum_z q_z\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z\cap\widetilde H_z)
=\frac54
>
1
=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_{z_0}\cap\widetilde H_{z_0}).
\]

## Proof

The three displayed vertices are noncollinear, so their convex hull \(C\) is compact, convex, and full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^2\). Its fibers are
\[
C_z=
\begin{cases}
\left[-\dfrac{z+2}{2},\dfrac{z+2}{2}\right],&-2\leq z\leq1,\\
\varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular,
\[
C_{-1}=[-1/2,1/2],\qquad C_0=[-1,1],\qquad
C_1=[-3/2,3/2],
\]
with respective lengths \(1,2,3\). Hence \(C_0\) has positive length and centroid \(x_0=0\). Also
\[
\frac12(-1)+\frac12(1)=0=z_0,
\]
and the boundary line \(z+x=0\) of \(H\) passes through \((0,0)\).

Since \(H_z=(-\infty,-z]\), direct intersection gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
z&-1&0&1\\ \hline
h_z&1&1&1/2\\
b_z&0&1&5/2.
\end{array}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_zq_zb_z
=\frac12\cdot0+\frac12\cdot\frac52
=\frac54
>
1=b_0.
\]
Because \(d=1\), taking \(1/d\)-powers changes nothing, so this is exactly a strict failure of
\(\sum_zq_zb_z^{1/d}\leq b_{z_0}^{1/d}\).

For completeness, \(\widetilde H_z=[-z,\infty)\), and
\[
\operatorname{vol}_1(C_{-1}\cap\widetilde H_{-1})=0,\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_1(C_0\cap\widetilde H_0)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_1(C_1\cap\widetilde H_1)=\frac52.
\]
The first intersection is actually empty, since
\([-1/2,1/2]\cap[1,\infty)=\varnothing\). Thus the same \(C,z_0,x_0,q\), with the opposite boundary-through-anchor halfspace, strictly violates the asserted \(h\)-inequality.

This empty cap identifies the first invalid proof step. Put \(D=C\cap\widetilde H\). Then
\[
D_{-1}=\varnothing,\qquad D_0=[0,1],\qquad D_1=[-1,3/2].
\]
Convexity gives only the vacuous inclusion
\[
\frac12D_{-1}+\frac12D_1=\varnothing\subseteq D_0.
\]
Brunn--Minkowski cannot be applied to this empty weighted Minkowski sum to conclude
\[
\frac12\operatorname{vol}_1(D_{-1})
+\frac12\operatorname{vol}_1(D_1)
\leq\operatorname{vol}_1(D_0);
\]
indeed, its two sides are \(5/4\) and \(1\). Root concavity holds on the positive-support projection of \(D\), but its zero extension across the missing fiber \(z=-1\) is not concave.

The claimed symmetric-pair energy bound fails numerically for the original halfspace \(H\). Here
\[
\gamma_1=\frac12,\qquad \rho_1:=(1-\gamma_1)^{1/1}=\frac12,\qquad
A=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_1)=3,\quad
B=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_{-1})=1,\quad
R=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_0)=2.
\]
Here \(\rho_1\) is the quantity denoted \(q_d\) in the proposed energy. Thus
\(2\rho_1R=2\), and the proposed energy is
\[
\Phi_1(3,1;2)
=3+1-\max\{x+y:0\leq x\leq3,\ 0\leq y\leq1,\ x+y\leq2\}
=2.
\]
But the actual retained pair mass is
\[
h_1+h_{-1}=\frac12+1=\frac32<2=\Phi_1(3,1;2).
\]
Hence the displayed endpoint constraint corresponding to formula (4) and the pair lower bound corresponding to formula (6) in the proposed reduction are false. Consequently the global energy bounds corresponding to formulas (10)--(12), and the rank-drop target built from them, do not follow. Indeed, all other symmetric pairs about \(0\) have zero fiber volume, so the proposed global energy at \(0\) equals
\[
\gamma_1\operatorname{vol}_1(C_0)+\Phi_1(3,1;2)=1+2=3,
\]
whereas this particular boundary-through-anchor halfspace has total mixed-integer mass
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z\cap H_z)
=1+1+\frac12=\frac52<3.
\]
