## Statement

Let an actual planar convex body have positive integer interval fibers $I_i=[l_i,r_i]$ of widths $w_i>0$ at $i=0,\ldots,N-1$; other fibers have zero length or are empty. Put $M=\sum_i w_i$ and $R_*=\max_{j,y\in I_j}\sum_i|I_i\cap(2y-I_{2j-i})|$, with zero terms for missing or null fibers. For each $j$, let $K_j=\min(j,N-1-j)$, $N_j=2K_j+1$, $C_j=\sum_{0\le 2j-i<N}w_iw_{2j-i}$, and $\Delta_j=N_j w_j^2-C_j$. Then
$
(N_jw_j-R_*)_+^2\le N_j\Delta_j,\qquad
R_*\ge N_jw_j-\sqrt{N_j\Delta_j}>\frac{C_j}{2w_j}.
$
With $s_k=(w_{j-k}+w_{j+k})/2$ the defect decomposes as
$
\Delta_j=2\sum_{k=1}^{K_j}(w_j-s_k)(w_j+s_k)
+\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{K_j}(w_{j-k}-w_{j+k})^2.
$
In particular $R_*<M/2$ implies $\Delta_j\ge (N_jw_j-M/2)_+^2/N_j$, strictly when $N_jw_j>M/2$. For an exact limitation of these conditions, the actual fibers $[0,2],[0,6],[0,10],[0,3]$ satisfy the full set of defect inequalities with proposed budget $b=10\ge\max_i w_i$, although $b<M/2$ and their actual $R_*$ equals $12$.

## Proof

Each ordered term of the reflection profile $R_j(y)$ is a convolution of two interval indicators evaluated at $2y$. It is a translated trapezoid with slopes of absolute value at most two. Actual convexity places the half-Minkowski sum of the two source intervals inside $I_j$, so the full profile vanishes at both endpoints of $I_j$ and is supported in $I_j$. It is $2N_j$-Lipschitz. The substitution $s=2y$ and Fubini give $\int_{I_j}R_j(y)\,dy=C_j/2$, including every ordered term. Thus for $0\le x\le w_j$,
$
R_j(l_j+x)\le\min(R_*,2N_jx,2N_j(w_j-x)).
$
The unclipped triangle has integral $N_jw_j^2/2$. If clipped, the removed triangle of height $(N_jw_j-R_*)_+$ has area $(N_jw_j-R_*)_+^2/(2N_j)$. This gives the squared defect inequality and its lower bound for $R_*$. Because $C_j\ge w_j^2>0$, rationalizing yields
$
N_jw_j-\sqrt{N_j\Delta_j}
=\frac{N_j C_j}{N_jw_j+\sqrt{N_j\Delta_j}}>\frac{C_j}{2w_j}.
$
The decomposition follows by pairing $j-k,j+k$ and expanding their product as $s_k^2-(w_{j-k}-w_{j+k})^2/4$. Compatibility gives $w_j\ge s_k$, so the displayed summands are nonnegative. If $R_*<M/2$, the positive-part square is strictly larger with $R_*$ than with $M/2$ whenever $N_jw_j>M/2$.

For the example the roof joining the four upper endpoints has decreasing slopes $4,4,-7$, with lower endpoint zero, so it gives an actual convex body. Here $M=21$, $N_j=(1,3,3,1)$, $C_j=(4,76,136,9)$, and $\Delta_j=(0,32,164,0)$. The only active comparisons at $b=10$ are $64\le96$ and $400\le492$. To check the exact reflection value, let $s=2y$; the ordered overlap of $[0,a]$ and $s-[0,c]$ is $[\min(a,c,s,a+c-s)]_+$. At the two edge bases the maxima are their own widths $2,3$. At base 1 the middle interval contributes at most $6$ and the reflected pair at most $4$, with both attained at $s=6$. At base 2 the own term is $\min(s,20-s)$ on $0\le s\le20$ and the pair adds $2[\min(3,s,9-s)]_+$. For $0\le s\le6$ their sum is at most $s+6\le12$; for $6\le s\le9$ it is $18-s\le12$; for $s\ge9$ it is at most $10$. Equality is attained at $s=6$, proving $R_*=12$.
