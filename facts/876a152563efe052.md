## Statement

Let \(D\geq1\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^D\) be a \(D\)-dimensional convex body of volume \(a>0\), and let
\[
 c:=\frac1a\int_Kx\,dx,
 \qquad
 \Sigma:=\frac1a\int_K(x-c)(x-c)^{\mathsf T}\,dx.
\]
For \(0<t\leq a\), use the canonical metronoid normalization
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\quad 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},
 \quad\int_Kf\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\]
Then \(\Sigma\) is positive definite and
\[
 \boxed{
 c+\frac1{128}\Sigma^{1/2}B_2^D
 \subseteq \mathsf M_{a/2}(K).}
 \tag{1}
\]

Now let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}.
\]
For \(z\in Z\), put
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}(x-c_z)(x-c_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dx.
\]
These covariance matrices are positive definite.  Define the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf\left\{
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace},\ x\in H
 \right\}.
 \tag{2}
\]

For completeness, if \(\varnothing\ne E\subseteq Z\),
\(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), and
\(0<t_z\leq a_z\), put
\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{\alpha\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z=1,\quad
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_zz=m\right\},
\]
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t):=
 \bigcap_{\alpha\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]
Write \(G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) for the complete metronoid-core capacity in accepted fact `fbeb0a501b268d12`, namely the supremum of \(\sum_{z\in E}t_z\) over such data for which
\(\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing\) and
\[
 \sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z
 \geq 2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
 \tag{3}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\).

Also use the residual-selector capacity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`,
\[
 R_{\rm sel}(C):=
 \sup
 2^{n-\rho_E}\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{r_s+1},
 \tag{4}
\]
where the supremum is over nonempty \(E\subseteq Z\), points
\(q_z\in C_z\), weights \(0<w_z\leq\delta_C(z,q_z)\), and affine maps
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^d\), with
\[
 \rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),
 \qquad
 r_s:=\dim\operatorname{span}\{q_z-s(z):z\in E\}.
\]

For an affine map \(s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), define
\[
 \tau_z(s):=
 \sqrt{(s(z)-c_z)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z^{-1}(s(z)-c_z)}
 \qquad(z\in Z),
\]
and
\[
 E_s:=\{z\in Z:\tau_z(s)\leq1/128\}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Then every affine \(s\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 G(C)\geq\frac12\sum_{z\in E_s}a_z.}
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(E_s\ne\varnothing\) and
\(\rho_s:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E_s)\), then, with
\(\gamma_d:=(d/(d+1))^d\),
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\rm sel}(C)
 \geq
 2^{n-\rho_s}\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
 \sum_{z\in E_s}a_z.}
 \tag{7}
\]

## Proof

Because \(K\) has positive \(D\)-dimensional volume, every nonzero linear functional has positive variance under the uniform law on \(K\); hence \(\Sigma\) is positive definite.

Fix \(u\in\mathbb R^D\setminus\{0\}\), let \(X\) be uniform on \(K\), and set
\[
 \sigma^2:=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u>0,
 \qquad
 Z_u:=\frac{\langle u,X-c\rangle}{\sigma}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\mathbb EZ_u=0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(Z_u)=1\).  Accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b` proves that this one-dimensional marginal has a log-concave density \(f\), positive and continuous on the interior of its compact support interval.

We first prove the quantitative bound
\[
 \|f\|_\infty<16.
 \tag{9}
\]
Here and below \(\|f\|_\infty\) means essential supremum.  To avoid an endpoint attainment assumption, replace \(f\) at the two endpoints of its support by its one-sided limits.  This changes neither the law nor its essential supremum.  Those limits exist because \(\log f\) is concave on the support interior, with the value \(-\infty\) allowed when the density tends to zero.  A limit \(+\infty\) is impossible: if \(\log f(x_j)\to+\infty\) as \(x_j\) approaches one endpoint, concavity applied between \(x_j\) and any fixed point near the other endpoint would force \(\log f\) to be infinite at a fixed interior point.  The resulting representative is upper semicontinuous and log-concave on the compact support.  Consequently
\[
 M:=\|f\|_\infty
\]
is finite and attained at a mode \(m_0\).  Log-concavity makes \(f\) nonincreasing away from \(m_0\) on the right and nondecreasing toward \(m_0\) on the left.

Consider first the right side of the mode and let \(L\) be the distance from \(m_0\) to the right endpoint of the support.  If \(f(m_0+s)\geq M/2\) throughout \(0<s<L\), then the total mass bound gives \(L\leq2/M\), and therefore
\[
 \int_{m_0}^{\infty}(x-m_0)^2f(x)\,dx
 \leq M\int_0^Ls^2\,ds
 \leq\frac{8}{3M^2}
 <\frac{112}{M^2}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Otherwise, continuity on the support interior gives a first
\(t\in(0,L)\) such that \(f(m_0+t)=M/2\).  Since
\(f\geq M/2\) on \([m_0,m_0+t]\), again \(t\leq2/M\).  Concavity of \(\log f\), applied to \(m_0,m_0+t,m_0+s\), yields for every \(s\geq t\)
\[
 f(m_0+s)\leq M2^{-s/t}.
 \tag{11}
\]
It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_{m_0}^{\infty}(x-m_0)^2f(x)\,dx
 &\leq M\int_0^t s^2\,ds
       +M\int_t^\infty s^22^{-s/t}\,ds\\
 &=Mt^3\left(\frac13+\int_1^\infty v^22^{-v}\,dv\right).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
\]
Writing \(b:=\log2>1/2\), direct integration gives
\[
 \int_1^\infty v^22^{-v}\,dv
 =\frac1{2b}+\frac1{b^2}+\frac1{b^3}<13.
\]
Together with \(t\leq2/M\), (12) is strictly less than \(112/M^2\).  The same two-case argument on the left of \(m_0\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[(Z_u-m_0)^2]<\frac{224}{M^2}.
 \tag{13}
\]
Since
\(\operatorname{Var}(Z_u)=1\leq\mathbb E[(Z_u-m_0)^2]\), one obtains
\(M<\sqrt{224}<16\), proving (9).

Let \(m\) be a median of \(Z_u\).  The density makes the law atomless, so
\[
 \mathbb P(Z_u\geq m)=\mathbb P(Z_u\leq m)=\frac12.
 \tag{14}
\]
Put \(A_+:=\mathbb E[Z_u\mathbf1_{\{Z_u\geq m\}}]\).  Since
\(\mathbb EZ_u=0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E|Z_u-m|
 &=\mathbb E[(Z_u-m)\mathbf1_{\{Z_u\geq m\}}]
   +\mathbb E[(m-Z_u)\mathbf1_{\{Z_u<m\}}]\\
 &=A_+-\mathbb E[Z_u\mathbf1_{\{Z_u<m\}}]
 =2A_+\\
 &=\mathbb E[Z_u\mid Z_u\geq m].
\end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
By (9),
\[
 \mathbb P\left(|Z_u-m|<\frac1{64}\right)
 \leq\frac2{64}\|f\|_\infty<\frac12.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E|Z_u-m|
 \geq\frac1{64}
 \mathbb P\left(|Z_u-m|\geq\frac1{64}\right)
 \geq\frac1{128}.
 \tag{16}
\]

Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives compactness of the metronoid and its exact top-cap support formula.  Because the marginal is atomless, the top cap of volume \(a/2\) is cut at the median in (14).  Equations (8), (15), and (16) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 h_{\mathsf M_{a/2}(K)-c}(u)
 &=\sigma\,\mathbb E[Z_u\mid Z_u\geq m]\\
 &\geq\frac\sigma{128}
 =\frac1{128}\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
The final quantity is the support function of
\((1/128)\Sigma^{1/2}B_2^D\).  Inequality (17) holds for every nonzero \(u\), and it is trivial for \(u=0\).  Support-function comparison between compact convex sets proves (1).

We turn to the mixed consequences.  Compactness of \(C\) makes \(Z\) finite, and positive \(d\)-volume makes every \(\Sigma_z\) positive definite, as asserted.  If \(z\in E_s\), (1), applied to \(K=C_z\), gives
\[
 s(z)\in\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z).
 \tag{18}
\]

If \(E_s=\varnothing\), (6) is just the nonnegativity of the supremum defining \(G(C)\).  Suppose henceforth that \(E_s\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
 t_z:=a_z/2\qquad(z\in E_s).
\]
Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` with continuous dimension zero to the finite measure
\[
 \nu:=\sum_{z\in E_s}t_z\delta_z
\]
supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\).  It supplies \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every proper closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_s\cap J}t_z
 \geq2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E_s}t_z.
 \tag{19}
\]
Moreover \(m\in\operatorname{conv}E_s\): otherwise strict separation would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(E_s\), contradicting (19).

For every \(\alpha\in\Lambda_m(E_s)\), affineness of \(s\) and (18) give
\[
 s(m)=\sum_{z\in E_s}\alpha_zs(z)
 \in
 \sum_{z\in E_s}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
 \tag{20}
\]
Thus \(s(m)\in\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\), so the core is nonempty.  Equations (19)-(20) are an admissible configuration in the exact complete-core definition of accepted fact `fbeb0a501b268d12`.  Hence
\[
 G(C)\geq\sum_{z\in E_s}t_z
 =\frac12\sum_{z\in E_s}a_z,
\]
which proves (6).

Finally, the metronoid-depth inclusion in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` states
\[
 \mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)
 \subseteq
 \{x\in C_z:\delta_C(z,x)\geq\gamma_da_z/2\}.
 \tag{21}
\]
For \(z\in E_s\), choose in the definition (4)
\[
 q_z:=s(z),
 \qquad
 w_z:=\gamma_da_z/2,
\]
and restrict the same affine map \(s\) to \(\operatorname{aff}(E_s)\).  Equations (18) and (21) make these choices admissible.  All residuals \(q_z-s(z)\) vanish, so \(r_s=0\).  The exact normalization (4), from accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`, now yields
\[
 R_{\rm sel}(C)
 \geq2^{n-\rho_s}\sum_{z\in E_s}w_z
 =2^{n-\rho_s}\frac{\gamma_d}{2}
 \sum_{z\in E_s}a_z,
\]
which is (7).
