## Statement

Let $D_4=\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,-4),(4,0),(0,4)\}$ and $P=D_4\cap\mathbb Z^2$. Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a nondegenerate triangle of area $V$, with centroid $g$ and barycentric functions $\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2$. On $S=P\times K$, let $\mu$ sum planar area over the fibers, and let the raw depth of a point of $S$ be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing that point. Then $\mu(S)=31V$, $(0,g)$ is the unique deepest point of $S$, and its raw and normalized depths are $40V/3$ and $40/93$ respectively.

For $\ell(z)=(-z_1-z_2,2z_1-z_2,2z_2-z_1)$, the three canonical caps $H_j=\{(z,x):\lambda_j(x)-1/3+\ell_j(z)\ge0\}$ cover the ambient space and each attains the depth at $(0,g)$. Each cap has twelve strictly full traces, sixteen strictly empty traces and three proper traces. With the raw margin $\lambda_j-1/3+\ell_j$, its mixed coarea section has mass $4V$ and centroid $(0,g)$, using fiber section length divided by $\|\nabla\lambda_j\|$. The deepest base is in just one of the four occupied parity classes.

## Proof

**Actual lattice census and canonical caps.** The inequalities $\ell_j(z)\ge-4$ describe $D_4$. For each integer $x=z_1$ from $-4$ to $4$, the integer range for $y=z_2$ is $\lceil(x-4)/2\rceil\le y\le\min(4-x,2x+4)$. The consecutive row counts are $1,2,4,5,7,5,4,2,1$, totaling $31$. In the same rows the counts for $\ell_0=-x-y>0$ are $1,2,4,3,2,0,0,0,0$, and the zero slice is $\{(-1,1),(0,0),(1,-1)\}$. The same counts apply to $\ell_1,\ell_2$ by permuting the primitive vertices $e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2$ of $D_4/4$. These permutations act by integer linear maps with integer inverses, since each map and its inverse send $e_1,e_2$ to integer vertices. They preserve $D_4$ and $P$, and permute its three facet forms $\ell_j$.

An integer offset of at least one captures the entire fiber strictly, and one of at most minus one misses it strictly, because $\lambda_j\in[0,1]$ on $K$. The zero offset gives a triangle scaled by $2/3$ about vertex $j$, of area $4V/9$. Thus every $H_j$ has mass $12V+3(4V/9)=40V/3$. The three margins sum to zero everywhere, so these caps cover the ambient space and give a global upper bound of $40V/3$. For each proper trace the section $\lambda_j=1/3$ has midpoint $g$ and length $2/3$ of the opposite side. Since $\|\nabla\lambda_j\|$ is the reciprocal of the corresponding altitude, the raw coarea mass per section is $(2/3)\cdot 2V=4V/3$. The three bases on each zero slice sum to zero by the displayed slice and symmetry. This gives mass $4V$ and centroid $(0,g)$ for each mixed section. The sections meet the interiors of the two incident edges, and all other traces are strictly separated from their boundary.

**All through-center normals.** Any halfspace containing $(0,g)$ contains the parallel halfspace with boundary through $(0,g)$ and the same inward normal. Write its margin as $L(z)+u\cdot(x-g)$. Independently permute the primitive base vertices and the continuous vertices to sort their two triples of margin values in decreasing order. The base action preserves $P$ as above. Each continuous permutation acts affinely on $K$, preserves its area and fixes $g$. The two triples each sum to zero. For a nonzero normal, the sum of their maxima is positive. Divide by that sum. The resulting forms are $L(x,y)=ax+by$ and the continuous vertex heights $(1-a,c,-1+a-c)$, where
$$
0\le a\le1,\qquad -a/2\le b\le a,\qquad -(1-a)/2\le c\le 1-a.
$$
These inequalities give the tetrahedron with vertices $(0,0,-1/2),(0,0,1),(1,-1/2,0),(1,1,0)$. Its barycentric coordinates $\tau$ give $a=\tau_2+\tau_3$, $b=-\tau_2/2+\tau_3$, and $c=-\tau_0/2+\tau_1$. At base $z=(x,y)$ the doubled vertex margins $f_0,f_1,f_2$ are obtained by dotting each displayed row with $\tau$.
$$
(2,2,2x-y,2x+2y),\qquad (-1,2,2x-y,2x+2y),\qquad
(-1,-4,2x-y,2x+2y).
$$
Their ordered differences $g_0=f_1-f_2$, $g_1=f_0-f_2$, $g_2=f_0-f_1$ are given by rows $(0,6,0,0),(3,6,0,0),(3,0,0,0)$ dotted with $\tau$. These are nonnegative throughout the tetrahedron.

**Reduction to a cubic.** When the continuous heights are distinct, the fraction captured in a fiber is
$$
\frac{g_0(f_0)_+^2-g_1(f_1)_+^2+g_2(f_2)_+^2}{g_0g_1g_2},
\qquad t_+=\max(t,0).
$$
If $f_0\ge0\ge f_1$, the captured corner has side ratios $f_0/(f_0-f_i)$ for $i=1,2$. If $f_1\ge0\ge f_2$, the missed corner has side ratios $(-f_2)/(f_i-f_2)$ for $i=0,1$. Its complementary fraction gives the displayed expression by expansion. With all values nonpositive the fraction is zero, and with all values nonnegative the numerator expands to the denominator. This includes thresholds equal to individual vertex values. The doubling scales numerator and denominator by the same factor. It suffices to show that the following expression is nonnegative on the full parameter tetrahedron.
$$
3\sum_{z\in P}\bigl[g_0(f_0(z))_+^2-g_1(f_1(z))_+^2+g_2(f_2(z))_+^2\bigr]
-40g_0g_1g_2. \tag{1}
$$

**Exact terminal tests.** On any sub-tetrahedron, bound (1) below by a homogeneous cubic. Keep the full square for a positive term if its margin is nonnegative at every vertex, and use zero otherwise. For a negative term use zero if its margin is nonpositive at every vertex, and the full negative square otherwise. Keep the last product unchanged. Linear margins have their extrema at vertices, and all $g_i$ are nonnegative, so these replacements give the asserted pointwise lower bound. Use local barycentric variables $\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_3$. The lower cubic is nonnegative if its twenty degree-three power coefficients are nonnegative.

A stored vertex is a nonnegative integer ray $v$ representing $v/\sum_i v_i$. For a retained margin row $f$ the quadratic coefficients at the four integer rays $v_i$ are $(f\cdot v_i)^2$ on $\xi_i^2$ and $2(f\cdot v_i)(f\cdot v_j)$ on $\xi_i\xi_j$ for $i<j$. Multiply these by $g_t\cdot v_k$ for the corresponding difference row $g_t$ and accumulate on the sorted triple $(i,j,k)$ with factor $3$ or $-3$ from (1). Subtract $40(g_0\cdot v_i)(g_1\cdot v_j)(g_2\cdot v_k)$ on the sorted triple for each ordered triple. These integer coefficients have the signs of the normalized local coefficients, since every contribution to the same monomial is divided by the same positive product of vertex coordinate sums.

**Repeated heights and pure-base normals.** For a nonzero continuous component with repeated heights, perturb the continuous component to give distinct heights. The limiting affine margin is nonconstant on each fiber, so its zero set has zero area there. Bounded convergence for the finite sum of fibers gives the same lower bound in the limit. For a pure-base normal, perturb it by continuous components tending to zero. At a point with strictly negative base margin the perturbed indicators eventually vanish, and a point on a boundary fiber is already included in the limiting closed cap. Thus the pointwise limsup is at most the limiting closed-cap indicator. Reverse Fatou for the finite measure $\mu$ gives the lower bound $40V/3$ including the entire mass of each boundary fiber. The origin is in $P$ and $g$ is in $K$, so this proves attainment in $S$.

**Uniqueness.** If a deepest point lies strictly inside a canonical cap, increase that cap's threshold by a sufficiently small positive amount, keeping the point and losing positive area on each of the three proper traces. The depth there would be strictly below $40V/3$. All three canonical margins at a deepest point must therefore be nonpositive, and their sum is zero. Consequently $\lambda_j(x)=1/3-\ell_j(z)$ for each $j$. Since each offset is integral and each barycentric value lies in $[0,1]$, all offsets are zero. Their common kernel is $z=0$, and then $x=g$. The base contains $0,e_1,e_2,(-1,-1)$ and hence all four parities, while the only deepest base is $0$.
