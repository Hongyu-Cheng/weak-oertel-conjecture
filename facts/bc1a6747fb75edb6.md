## Statement

Let $w_0,\ldots,w_N>0$ be log-concave, with zero extension, $S_i=\sum_{h\ge i}w_h$ for integer $i\ge0$, and $M=S_0$. The ratios $S_{i+1}/S_i$ for $0\le i\le N$ decrease strictly when there is more than one, ending at zero. For all integers $i,k\ge0$, $S_{i+k}\le S_i S_k/M$, strictly if $i,k>0$ and $S_i,S_k>0$. Monotonicity of the original weights is unnecessary.

## Proof

Log-concavity makes $w_{i+1}/w_i$ nonincreasing on the positive support. For $i<N$ compare $S_i/w_i$ and $S_{i+1}/w_{i+1}$ termwise as sums of $w_{i+h}/w_i$ and $w_{i+1+h}/w_{i+1}$. Each common term in the latter is no larger, by multiplying consecutive mass ratios. The former has the additional positive term $w_N/w_i$. Therefore $S_{i+1}/w_{i+1}<S_i/w_i$, and $S_{i+1}/S_i=1-w_i/S_i$ decreases strictly as claimed. If $i+k\le N$, compare the product of $k$ successive survival ratios starting at $i$ with the product starting at $0$. They give $S_{i+k}/S_i$ and $S_k/M$; the comparison is strict when $i,k>0$ because each ratio in the second product is positive. If $i+k>N$, the left side is zero, strictly smaller when both tails on the right are positive. If $i=0$ or $k=0$, the submultiplicativity inequality is an identity.
