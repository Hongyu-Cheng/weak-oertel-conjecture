## Statement

Let $f:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)$ be finite and concave with integral one. Replace its endpoint values by the corresponding one-sided limits to obtain a continuous representative $\bar f$. Let $\nu$ be the positive Stieltjes curvature measure of the decreasing right derivative of $\bar f$ on $(0,1)$. Then
$$
\bar f(t)=\bar f(0)(1-t)+\bar f(1)t+\int_{(0,1)}(\min(t,p)-tp)\,\nu(dp).
$$
Equivalently, $\bar f=\int T_p\,d\mu(p)$ for a probability measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$, where $T_0(t)=2(1-t)$, $T_1(t)=2t$ and $T_p(t)=2\min\{t/p,(1-t)/(1-p)\}$ for $0<p<1$. The endpoint masses are $\bar f(0)/2,\bar f(1)/2$ and the interior measure is $p(1-p)\nu(dp)/2$.

## Proof

The two chords from any point $(t,f(t))$ to the endpoint values bound triangles of total area at least $f(t)/2$, so $f(t)\le2$ in the interior. Bounded concavity gives finite one-sided limits. Concavity forces each original endpoint value to be at most its limit, and taking interior limits in the concavity inequalities proves that the new representative is concave and continuous on the closed interval.

For $0<a<t<b<1$, the right derivative $h=\bar f'_+$ is finite, decreasing and right-continuous on the interior. Define $\nu((a,b])=h(a)-h(b)$. On $[a,b]$ the function is Lipschitz and its right derivative equals its derivative almost everywhere. Integrating $h(s)=h(a)-\nu((a,s])$ and using Tonelli gives $\bar f(t)=\bar f(a)+(t-a)h(a)-\int_{(a,t]}(t-p)\nu(dp)$. Subtract the chord using the same formula at $b$. The difference is $\int_{(a,b)}G_{ab}(t,p)\nu(dp)$ for the kernel
$$
G_{ab}(t,p)=\frac{(\min(t,p)-a)(b-\max(t,p))}{b-a}.
$$
Extend the kernel by zero in $p$ outside $(a,b)$. As the interval expands to $(0,1)$ it increases to $\min(t,p)-tp$. Indeed for fixed $m=\min(t,p)$ and $M=\max(t,p)$ its derivatives in $a,b$ are $-(b-M)(b-m)/(b-a)^2$ and $(m-a)(M-a)/(b-a)^2$. Monotone convergence and endpoint continuity give the formula for interior $t$. At the endpoints the kernel is zero, so the formula holds there as well. The identity $\min(t,p)-tp=p(1-p)T_p(t)/2$ and the fact that every tent has integral one give the mixture and its probability normalization by Tonelli. In particular the weighted curvature measure is finite even if $\nu$ is infinite.
