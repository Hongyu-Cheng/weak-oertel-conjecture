## Statement

Let \(d\ge4\), let \(\Delta_d\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a simplex of ordinary \(d\)-dimensional volume one, and let \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{d+1}\) be its affine barycentric coordinates, extended to all of \(\mathbb R^d\). Thus \(\sum_i\beta_i(x)=1\). Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer. Let \(\ell_i:[-N,N]\to\mathbb R\) be continuous convex rational piecewise-affine functions satisfying \(\ell_i(0)=0\) and \(L(t):=1-\sum_i\ell_i(t)>0\) throughout \([-N,N]\). Define

\[
C=\{(t,x):-N\le t\le N,\ \beta_i(x)\ge\ell_i(t)\text{ for every }i\},
\qquad
C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]

Use the actual mixed-integer set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\) and its measure \(\mu(A)=\mathcal H_d(S\cap A)\). Put \(w_z=|C_z|_d\) and \(W=\sum_{z=-N}^Nw_z\). Suppose zero is a weighted median, meaning \(\sum_{z<0}w_z\le W/2\) and \(\sum_{z>0}w_z\le W/2\).

Let \(t_1,\ldots,t_{d+1}>0\) have sum \(1-\delta\), where \(0<\delta<1\), and let \(s_1,\ldots,s_{d+1}\) be rational. These \(t_i\) are the cap thresholds, while \(t\) is the real base coordinate. Define the original open ambient caps \(H_i=\{(t,x):\beta_i(x)>t_i+s_it\}\) and their traces \(H_{i,z}=\{x:(z,x)\in H_i\}\). Define exact overcount by

\[
\Omega_z
=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|C_z\cap H_{i,z}|_d
 -\left|C_z\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{d+1}H_{i,z}\right|_d,
\qquad
\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}=\sum_{\substack{-N\le z\le N\\z\ne0}}\Omega_z.
\]

With \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), one has the quantitative inequality

\[
W\le
2+2\max_{1\le i\le d+1}
 \bigl\{\mu(H_i)-(1-t_i)^d\bigr\}
+\frac{2\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}{(d+1)\gamma_d-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, specialize to the registered thresholds

\[
\delta=(d+1)^{-2},\qquad
t_1=\frac{1-\delta}{2(d+1)},\qquad
t_i=\frac{(1-\delta)(2d+1)}{2d(d+1)}\quad(2\le i\le d+1),
\qquad
\eta=(1-t_1)^d.
\]

For these thresholds, if \(\mu(H_i)\le2\eta\) for every \(i\) and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}\le\eta\), then

\[
\frac W\eta<8+\frac6{d-2}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently the additional requirement \(W\ge d\eta/4\) is impossible for every \(d\ge33\). All volumes and masses in these conclusions retain the original normalization \(|\Delta_d|_d=w_0=1\).

## Proof

The defining inequalities of \(C\) are convex because each \(\ell_i\) is convex. Continuity and compactness of the base bound all the \(\ell_i\); the identity \(\sum_i\beta_i=1\) then bounds every barycentric coordinate on \(C\). Thus \(C\) is compact and convex. At \(t=0\), the barycenter of \(\Delta_d\) satisfies every facet inequality strictly. Continuity gives a neighborhood in \(C\), so \(C\) is a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{d+1}\).

For any real \(t\in[-N,N]\), write \(L_t=L(t)\). Its fiber is the affine image of \(\Delta_d\) whose barycentric coordinates are \(\ell_i(t)+L_t\beta_i(x)\). This map is a translation followed by a dilation of factor \(L_t>0\), since \(\sum_i\ell_i(t)+L_t=1\). Therefore \(w_z=L_z^d\) for every integer \(-N\le z\le N\). All these fibers have positive volume, all other integer fibers are empty, and \(w_0=1\).

Put \(q_i(t)=t_i+s_it-\ell_i(t)\). Each \(q_i\) is concave, and \(q_i(0)=t_i>0\). Subtracting the lower barycentric coordinates in a fiber gives nonnegative coordinates of total sum \(L_t\). The cap condition is that coordinate \(i\) exceed \(q_i(t)\). Consequently, for each nonempty set of distinct indices \(I\), the exact intersection volume is

\[
\left|C_z\cap\bigcap_{i\in I}H_{i,z}\right|_d
=\left[L_z-\sum_{i\in I}(q_i(z))_+\right]_+^d,
\qquad a_+=\max\{a,0\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, subtract the positive lower bounds \((q_i(z))_+\) from their coordinates. If their sum is less than \(L_z\), the remainder is a simplex of that remaining length; if the sum is at least \(L_z\), the intersection has zero \(d\)-volume. The strict inequalities change only subsets of affine hyperplanes, all of which have zero volume in the positive-dimensional fiber. In particular, when \(q_i(z)=0\), the open cap misses only a facet and has the full fiber volume. At the anchor, (3) gives \(|C_0\cap H_{i,0}|_d=(1-t_i)^d\), because \(0<t_i<1\).

For every fiber, the integrand defining \(\Omega_z\) is the number of caps containing a point minus the indicator that at least one contains it. Hence \(\Omega_z\ge0\). Summation also gives the exact general-dimensional ledger

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\mu(H_i)
=\mu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{d+1}H_i\right)
+\sum_{z=-N}^N\Omega_z.
\tag{4}
\]

This uses the exact repeated-coverage integrand, rather than a sum of pairwise intersections.

Choose \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) so that \(\varepsilon\sum_i s_i\le0\). For every \(0\le u\le N\),

\[
\sum_i(t_i+\varepsilon s_i u)
=1-\delta+\varepsilon u\sum_i s_i<1,
\qquad
\sum_iq_i(\varepsilon u)
=L(\varepsilon u)-\delta+\varepsilon u\sum_i s_i<L(\varepsilon u).
\tag{5}
\]

The first inequality proves that the entire cap family covers \(\mathbb R^d\) on this side: a point outside every trace would have \(1=\sum_i\beta_i(x)\le\sum_i(t_i+\varepsilon s_i u)<1\). Coverage on the other side is unnecessary; its exact overcount remains nonnegative. The weighted-median assumption gives

\[
1+\sum_{k=1}^Nw_{\varepsilon k}
=W-\sum_{k=1}^Nw_{-\varepsilon k}\ge\frac W2.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(k_0\) be the smallest integer in \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\) at which some \(q_i(\varepsilon k_0)\le0\). If no such integer exists, put \(k_0=N+1\). For every integer \(1\le k<k_0\), all the numbers \(q_i(\varepsilon k)\) are positive. Their sum is less than \(L_{\varepsilon k}\) by (5), so each ratio \(q_i(\varepsilon k)/L_{\varepsilon k}\) lies in \((0,1)\). The function \(r\mapsto(1-r)^d\) is convex and decreasing on \([0,1]\). Its convexity and (3) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{w_{\varepsilon k}}
 \sum_{i=1}^{d+1}|C_{\varepsilon k}\cap H_{i,\varepsilon k}|_d
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}
 \left(1-\frac{q_i(\varepsilon k)}{L_{\varepsilon k}}\right)^d\\
&\ge(d+1)
 \left(1-\frac{\sum_iq_i(\varepsilon k)}{(d+1)L_{\varepsilon k}}\right)^d\\
&\ge(d+1)\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d
=(d+1)\gamma_d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Because these fibers are covered, subtracting their union volume \(w_{\varepsilon k}\) shows that \(\Omega_{\varepsilon k}\ge((d+1)\gamma_d-1)w_{\varepsilon k}\).

The coefficient is positive. In fact, the binomial theorem and \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\) yield

\[
\gamma_d^{-1}
=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
\le\sum_{j=0}^d\frac1{j!}
\le2+\sum_{j=2}^d2^{1-j}<3.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus \((d+1)\gamma_d-1>(d-2)/3>0\). Summing (7), and using nonnegativity of every other off-anchor overcount, gives

\[
\sum_{1\le k<k_0}w_{\varepsilon k}
\le\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}}{(d+1)\gamma_d-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

If \(k_0\le N\), choose an index \(i_*\) with \(q_{i_*}(\varepsilon k_0)\le0\). Concavity along the oriented interval, applied to \(0,k_0,k\), implies for every integer \(k\ge k_0\) that

\[
q_{i_*}(\varepsilon k)
\le\frac{k}{k_0}q_{i_*}(\varepsilon k_0)
 -\left(\frac{k}{k_0}-1\right)t_{i_*}
\le0.
\tag{10}
\]

For \(k>k_0\) the last inequality is strict because \(t_{i_*}>0\). Formula (3) therefore shows that the same original cap \(H_{i_*}\) contains the full volume of every fiber \(C_{\varepsilon k}\) with \(k_0\le k\le N\), including the possible zero-threshold fiber at \(k_0\). Its anchor contribution must also be charged, so

\[
\sum_{k=k_0}^Nw_{\varepsilon k}
\le\mu(H_{i_*})-(1-t_{i_*})^d
\le\max_i\bigl\{\mu(H_i)-(1-t_i)^d\bigr\}.
\tag{11}
\]

If \(k_0=N+1\), the left side is the empty sum, and the same final bound holds because each expression in the maximum is a sum of nonnegative cap contributions from the other fibers. Combining (6), (9), and (11) proves (1). The argument includes every integer between the anchor and the endpoints, regardless of the locations of the piecewise-affine breakpoints.

For the registered thresholds, their sum is \(1-\delta\), they are positive, and \(t_1<t_i\) for \(i>1\). Their largest anchor cap has mass \(\eta=(1-t_1)^d\), with the prescribed volume-one normalization. Bernoulli's inequality gives

\[
\eta\ge1-dt_1
=1-\frac{d(1-\delta)}{2(d+1)}>\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]

Every anchor cap has positive mass. Consequently the hypotheses \(\mu(H_i)\le2\eta\) and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{off}}\le\eta\), together with (1), (8), and (12), give

\[
\frac W\eta
<\frac2\eta+4+\frac2{(d+1)\gamma_d-1}
<8+\frac6{d-2}.
\tag{13}
\]

For every integer \(d\ge33\), the last expression is at most \(8+6/31=254/31<33/4\le d/4\); the strict rational comparison is \(1016<1023\). This contradicts \(W\ge d\eta/4\), proving the claimed explicit cutoff and resolving the arbitrary-large-dimension test.
