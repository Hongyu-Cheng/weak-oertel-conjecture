## Statement

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a convex body centrally symmetric about zero, $m\ge1$. Let $A=D\cap\{b:a\cdot b\le T\}$ for $a\ne0$, $T>0$, and put $v=|A|/|D|$. Define the projective energy at zero by
$$
E_A(0)=\inf_{\ell:\,1+\ell\cdot b>0\ \text{on }A}\int_A(1+\ell\cdot b)^{-m-1}\,db.
$$
Then
$$
2v-1\le\frac{E_A(0)}{|D|}\le\frac{2v-1}{v},
\qquad
\frac49\le\frac{v}{1+E_A(0)/|D|}\le\frac12.
$$
The lower ratio $4/9$ is attained for $m=1$, $D=[-1,1]$, $A=[-1,1/3]$. For $m\ge2$ and $v<1$, the lower ratio inequality is strict.

## Proof

The marginal density of volume on $D$ under $b\mapsto a\cdot b$ is even, bounded and compactly supported. For $m\ge2$, at a height $s\in[0,t]$ the section contains the appropriate Minkowski combination of the sections at $-t,t$. Those two sections have equal volume by central symmetry. Brunn–Minkowski gives nonincrease of the section volume for nonnegative heights. When $m=1$ the density is constant on its interval. The origin is interior to $A$, so the projective energy is defined. Apply the even-unimodal truncation lemma at $p=m+1$. The axial denominator $1+c_p a\cdot b$ is admissible on $A$, giving the upper energy bound. If $m\ge2$ and $v<1$, the strict exponent comparison holds off $s=0$, a null set for the marginal. Hence that upper energy bound is strict.

The symmetric slab $A\cap(-A)$ has volume $(2v-1)|D|$ because $A$ and $-A$ cover $D$. Its centroid is zero. By monotonicity and the centroid equality in accepted fact d90ba05577427854, $E_A(0)\ge E_{A\cap(-A)}(0)=|A\cap(-A)|$. This proves the lower energy bound. The ratio upper bound follows by substituting it. For the ratio lower bound,
$$
\frac{v}{1+E_A(0)/|D|}\ge\frac{v^2}{3v-1}
=\frac49+\frac{(3v-2)^2}{9(3v-1)}.
$$
The strict upper energy estimate makes this ratio inequality strict in the stated proper truncation case. For $A=[-1,t]$, $0<t\le1$, the integral in the energy is $(1+t)/[(1-\ell)(1+t\ell)]$. The admissible maximum of the denominator occurs at $\ell=(t-1)/(2t)$ and is $(1+t)^2/(4t)$. Thus $E_A(0)=4t/(1+t)$. Substituting $t=1/3$ and $|D|=2$ gives $4/9$.
