## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be planar convex bodies at levels $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, where $K$ is a triangle. Let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and let nonzero spanning normals $v_1,v_2,v_3$ satisfy $\sum_i v_i=0$. Put $h_i=h_B(v_i)-v_i\cdot y$. The caps $v_i\cdot(x-y)-h_i z\ge0$ have $A$ traces $H_i^A=\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge-h_i\}$, middle traces $v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0$, and supporting $B$ traces of zero area. Suppose all $A$ cuts are proper and the three caps have a common raw area $Q$, so $U_i+kq_i=Q$ for the $A$ cap areas $U_i$, the middle cap fractions $q_i$, and the middle area $k=|K|$. With $a=|A|$ and $b=|B|$, assume
$$
Q\le\frac{2(a+k+b)}9,\qquad b\le a+k,\qquad a<b/32.
$$
For the actual cutting-chord lengths divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, write $\sigma_{A,i}$ and $\sigma_{K,i}$. For each $i$ with a nonempty residual corner $R_i=A\setminus\bigcup_{j\ne i}H_j^A$,
$$
p_i:=\frac{1/(\sigma_{K,i}+2\sigma_{A,i})}{\sum_j1/(\sigma_{K,j}+2\sigma_{A,j})}>\frac29.
$$
The common area condition concerns the displayed caps themselves.

## Proof

**Budget and actual sections.** The three $A$ halfplanes cover the plane, since $\sum_i h_i>0$ and the normals sum to zero. The three middle halfplanes also cover the plane by balance. By fact ea91e021ff1f4416 the ordinary maximum depth on the triangle is $4/9$ of its area. A middle trace containing such a maximizer has area at least $4k/9$, so $Q\ge4k/9$. The assumed upper bound on $Q$ then yields $k\le a+b$. Put $\alpha=a/k$, $\beta=b/k$, $r=Q/k$, and $c=128/279$. The strict small-area inequality and $b\le a+k$ yield $\alpha<1/31$, hence $\beta\ge1-\alpha>30/31$ and $q_i\le r\le4(1+\alpha)/9<c$.

Apply fact b08bdaf82b84ef41 to the middle caps with balancing weights one and cap ceiling $c$. The triangle is nondegenerate, the mark interior and the normals spanning, so these cuts isolate distinct strict vertices. Thus fact 2e657e976221a74d applies with the same ceiling, giving the source barycentric coordinates $\alpha_i<5/14$ and $q_i>20/49$. The cap intercepts $u,v$ on the edges toward the other vertices satisfy $q_i=uv$ and $\alpha_j/u+\alpha_k/v=1$, so $q_i\ge4\alpha_j\alpha_k$. Since $\eta_i=5/14-\alpha_i>0$ and $\sum_i\eta_i=1/14$, we have $\sum_i\alpha_i^2=65/196+\sum_i\eta_i^2<33/98$. Consequently $\sum_i q_i\ge2(1-\sum_i\alpha_i^2)>65/49$.

The actual cover gives $E=\sum_i U_i-a\ge0$. With $\Delta=125/93-\sum_i q_i$, the common budget gives
$$
0\le E/k=3r-\sum_i q_i-\alpha\le 4/3+\alpha/3-\sum_i q_i<\Delta,
\qquad 0<\Delta<\frac{80}{4557}.
$$
Apply fact a19fb2ae234afb2a with these distinct strict middle caps and its $f_i$ variables. The moment is $\sum_i f_i\sqrt{q_i}=2$ and $f_i+(f_j+f_k)^2/4\le2$ for each $i$. The actual signed-row area lemma applies under the same hypotheses and cap ceiling, giving $R>201/400$, for its $R=8Dk/H^2$. Thus $L=\sqrt{\beta R}>697/1000$, since $(30/31)(201/400)-(697/1000)^2=14921/31000000$.

Fix a present $R_i$ and put $\delta_i=(a-U_j-U_k)_+$. If $\delta_i>0$ then $\delta_i\le U_i$, and $U_i-\sqrt{U_i\delta_i}\le U_i-\delta_i=E$. If $\delta_i=0$, then $U_i\le E$. The same-cut section-ratio lemma applies since the $A$ cut is proper, the corner is present and both endpoints use the same normals and support values. It gives
$$
t_i:=\sigma_{A,i}/\sigma_{K,i}\le \frac{f_i}{\sqrt{q_i}}\frac{\Delta}{L}.
$$

**Scalar estimate with the budget slack.** Relabel so the chosen index is $1$. Let $x=f_1$, $t=\sqrt{q_1}$, $g=f_2+f_3$, $F=x+g$, $m=\sqrt{20/49}$, $s=\sqrt c$, $\rho=\sqrt{119/279}$, $N=x/t$, and $Z=f_2/\sqrt{q_2}+f_3/\sqrt{q_3}$. The moment gives $F\ge2/s>59/20$. Summing the row inequalities gives $F+(F^2+\sum_i f_i^2)/4\le6$, which, using $\sum_i f_i^2\ge F^2/3$, yields $F\le3$.

The ordinary inverse middle chords are proportional to $f_j/\sqrt{q_j}$ by the signed-row cap formula. Every other endpoint section ratio is nonnegative, so
$$
p_1\ge\frac{N}{N+(1+2t_1)Z}
\ge\frac{xm}{3s\bigl(1+2x\Delta/(mL)\bigr)}.
$$
For the second inequality, $N\ge x/s$, $N+Z\le F/m$ and $t_1\le x\Delta/(mL)$. If $p_1\le2/9$, the last bound implies
$$
x\le\frac{2s/(3m)}{1-4s\Delta/(3m^2L)}<\frac34 .
$$
Indeed $s<33867/50000$, $m>63887/100000$ by squaring, and $m^2=20/49$, $L>697/1000$, $\Delta<80/4557$. These give a positive lower bound for the denominator, and substituting the indicated rational bounds in the whole quotient gives an upper bound $24392142300/32586905977=3/4-192148731/130347623908$.

For both $j=2,3$ we have $f_j>(s/\rho)x$. Otherwise $T=x+f_j<(1+26/25)(3/4)=153/100$ since $s/\rho<26/25$. The row inequality at the remaining index gives $F\le 2+T-T^2/4<117791/40000<59/20$, a contradiction because $T\mapsto 2+T-T^2/4$ increases on $(0,2)$.

Apply the square-root tangent at $119/279$ to $q_1$ and those at $c$ to $q_2,q_3$. Since $119/279+2c=125/93$, the combined linear remainder is
$$
-\frac{x\Delta}{2\rho}+
\sum_{j=2,3}\left(\frac{f_j}{2s}-\frac{x}{2\rho}\right)(q_j-c)
\le-\frac{x\Delta}{2\rho}.
$$
The sign follows from $f_j/s>x/\rho$ and $q_j\le c$. The moment and the row bound $g\le2\sqrt{2-x}$ now give $x\Delta/(2\rho)\le B(x):=\rho x+2s\sqrt{2-x}-2$. On $(0,3/4]$ this $B$ is increasing since $(5/4)\rho^2-s^2=83/1116>0$. For $x_0=67/100$, use the rational bounds $\rho<65309/100000$, $s<33867/50000$, and $\sqrt{2-x_0}<57663/50000$ by squaring. Substituting them gives $B(x_0)<-10269/78125000$. Hence $x_0<x<3/4$. In this interval $B'(x)=\rho-s/\sqrt{2-x}<2/21<1/10$ using $\rho<2/3$, $s>2/3$, and $\sqrt{2-x}<7/6$. Set $z=x-x_0\in(0,2/25)$. Then $B(x)<z/10$, and $4\rho/(mL)<6$ by the rational upper bound $261236000/44529239$. We obtain
$$
1+2t_1\le1+\frac{2x\Delta}{mL}\le1+\frac{4\rho B(x)}{mL}<1+3z/5.
$$

For $m\le u\le s$, the secant bound $1/u\le(m+s-u)/(ms)$ follows from $(u-m)(s-u)\ge0$. Applying it to the two other cap square roots, then using the moment and the row bound, gives $Z\le[2(m+s)\sqrt{2-x}-2+xt]/(ms)$. The term $2(m+s)\sqrt{2-x}-2$ is positive since $m+s>13/10$ and $\sqrt{2-x}>1$. Multiplying by $t/x$ and replacing $t$ by $s$ thus gives
$$
Z/N\le \zeta(x):=\frac{2(m+s)\sqrt{2-x}-2+xs}{mx}.
$$
To check $\zeta(x_0)<7/2$, set $w=57663/50000$, $\underline m=63887/100000$, and $\overline s=33867/50000$. The coefficient $(7/2)x_0-2w=481/12500$ is positive. Then
$$
2-s(2\sqrt{2-x_0}+x_0)+m\bigl((7/2)x_0-2\sqrt{2-x_0}\bigr)
>2-\overline s(2w+x_0)+\underline m\bigl((7/2)x_0-2w\bigr)
=\frac{82692}{9765625}>0.
$$
On the same interval $\zeta'(x)=[2-(m+s)(4-x)/\sqrt{2-x}]/(mx^2)<-328/75<-4$. This follows from $m+s>13/10$, $(4-x)/\sqrt{2-x}=\sqrt{2-x}+2/\sqrt{2-x}\ge2\sqrt2>14/5$, and $mx^2<(2/3)(3/4)^2=3/8$. Thus $Z/N<7/2-4z$, which is positive for $0<z<2/25$. Combining the two estimates gives
$$
(1+2t_1)Z/N<(1+3z/5)(7/2-4z)=7/2-(19/10)z-(12/5)z^2<7/2.
$$
The first lower bound on $p_1$ now gives $p_1>2/9$, contradicting the supposition. The argument uses the actual residual corner only at the chosen index.
