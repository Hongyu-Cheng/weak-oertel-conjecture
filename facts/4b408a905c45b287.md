## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body containing $0$ in its interior. Choose $m\ge3$ distinct rays from $0$ in counterclockwise order with each consecutive angular gap strictly less than $\pi$. Let $P_i$ be the boundary point of $K$ on the $i$th ray, and choose one supporting line of $K$ at each $P_i$, independently. With cyclic indices let $S_i=\operatorname{cone}(P_i,P_{i+1})$.

There is a convex polygon $Q$ containing $0$ in its interior with the same boundary points on the chosen rays, the same areas $|Q\cap S_i|=|K\cap S_i|$, and with every chosen line supporting $Q$ at $P_i$. In the open sector between two consecutive rays, $Q$ has at most one vertex. Thus it has at most $2m$ vertices.

## Proof

In one sector write a point as $xP_i+yP_{i+1}$, so that $x,y\ge0$ and the endpoint coordinates are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. The chosen containing support halfplanes have inequalities $x+py\le1$ and $qx+y\le1$. The constants on the right can be normalized to one because $0$ is strictly inside both halfplanes; because both endpoints belong to $K$, $p,q\le1$. Let $W$ be the intersection of these two halfplanes with the nonnegative quadrant, and let $\alpha$ be the area of the original sector in these coordinates. The original piece is contained in $W$ and contains the core triangle with vertices $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$. In particular $\alpha\ge1/2$.

If $p=1$ or $q=1$, then $W$ is the core triangle: the other inequality follows from $x+y\le1$. Otherwise $p,q<1$. If $1-pq>0$, the two lines meet at
$$
E=\left(\frac{1-p}{1-pq},\frac{1-q}{1-pq}\right),\qquad
E_x+E_y-1=\frac{(1-p)(1-q)}{1-pq}>0.
$$
The positive combination of the inequalities with coefficients $E_y,E_x$, respectively, bounds $x+y$ by $E_x+E_y$. The only boundary intersections of the four defining lines in $W$ are $0,(1,0),E,(0,1)$, so these are its vertices and $|W|=(E_x+E_y)/2$. If $1-pq\le0$, both $p,q$ are negative and $(-p,1)$ is a positive recession direction: the two linear left sides take values $0$ and $1-pq\le0$ there. This includes $1-pq=0$.

Let $M=(1/2,1/2)$. If $\alpha=1/2$, take $(u,v)=M$. For $\alpha>1/2$ and bounded $W$, take the point of the segment $[M,E]$ whose coordinate sum is $2\alpha$, which exists because $\alpha\le |W|$. If $\alpha>1/2$ and $W$ is unbounded, take $(u,v)=M+\frac{2\alpha-1}{1-p}(-p,1)$. In every case $u,v>0$, $(u,v)\in W$, and $u+v=2\alpha$. Equality at the core value makes the extra point redundant; equality at the bounded wedge's upper area selects $E$ itself. In either equality case the original closed sector piece is the corresponding containing or contained polygon, since a proper compact convex subset of a full dimensional polygon has strictly smaller area.

Replace the sector piece by the convex hull of $0,(1,0),(u,v),(0,1)$. It has area $(u+v)/2$, by splitting at the ray through $(u,v)$, and the same axis endpoints. Its gauge on the quadrant is
$$
f_i(xP_i+yP_{i+1})=\max\left\{x+\frac{1-u}{v}y,\frac{1-v}{u}x+y\right\}.
$$
Indeed, when $u+v=1$ the two forms are both $x+y$. Otherwise the coefficients $p'=(1-u)/v$ and $q'=(1-v)/u$ are strictly less than one, $1-p'q'=(u+v-1)/(uv)>0$, and the quadrant sublevel at one is the bounded quadrilateral just computed, with intersection point $(u,v)$. In either case the gauge is positive away from $0$ because the sublevel is bounded and the function positively homogeneous. From $(u,v)\in W$ we have $p\le p'$ and $q\le q'$, so $f_i$ dominates the two normalized endpoint support functionals on the sector.

On a shared ray through $P_i$, the adjacent gauges coincide with the radial parameter $f(tP_i)=t$ for $t\ge0$. They define a continuous piecewise linear function $f$ on the plane. To see convexity, restrict to a line avoiding $0$. At a crossing of the ray through $P_i$, both sides agree with, and locally lie above, the same affine function obtained by restricting the chosen support functional at $P_i$. Thus the left slope is at most that affine slope and the right slope is at least it. Each sector restriction is convex and there are finitely many crossings, so the whole line restriction is convex. Along a line through $0$ of direction $e$, the two slopes are $-f(-e)$ and $f(e)$, in that order, since both values of $f$ are positive. This proves convexity on every line.

The sublevel $Q=\{f\le1\}$ is the union of the sector pieces. It is bounded, contains a disk about $0$ by continuity, and is the convex hull of the finite union of their vertex sets. The prescribed radial values give the exact endpoints and the determinant of each sector coordinate change gives the original area from $\alpha$. Each open sector has at most one vertex. Finally both pieces adjacent to $P_i$ lie in its chosen containing halfplane. If a point of $Q$ violated that inequality, the segment from $P_i$ to that point would violate it arbitrarily close to $P_i$, where only those two sectors occur. Thus the selected line supports all of $Q$.
