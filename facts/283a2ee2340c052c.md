## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, $n\ge0$, $d\ge1$. Let $P=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:K_z\ne\varnothing\}$, where $K_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}$, and let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. Suppose $q=\max_{z\in P}\dim K_z\ge1$. For each $q$-dimensional fiber, let $V_z=\mathcal H_q(K_z)$, let $A_z$ be its raw ordinary intrinsic maximal depth, and let $g_z$ be its unique deepest point. Set $m_z=A_z^{1/q}$ on these fibers and $A_z=m_z=0$ on the other nonempty fibers.

Suppose an affine function $\ell$ satisfies $\ell(z)\ge m_z$ for all $z\in P$ and $Q=\{z\in P:m_z=\ell(z)>0\}$ is nonempty. Then $Q=\operatorname{conv}Q\cap\mathbb Z^n$. Let $k_Q$ be the number of base parities in $Q$ and $B_Q=\sum_{z\in Q} A_z$. There exists $z\in Q$ such that
$$
h_{\mathcal H_q|_S}(z,g_z)\ge \frac{B_Q}{k_Q}
\ge \frac{\gamma_q}{k_Q}\sum_{w\in Q}V_w,\qquad
\gamma_q=\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)^q,
$$
where the depth takes the infimum over closed ambient halfspaces containing the mark. If all contact fibers have the same intrinsic direction space, one can choose a point whose depth is strictly greater than $B_Q/k_Q$ unless the lifted graph consists of $k_Q$ vertices with weights $A_z$ all equal. In particular strictness holds in this common-direction case when $|Q|>k_Q$.

If every $q$-dimensional integer fiber has the same raw maximal depth $A_*>0$, take $P_+=\{z\in P:\dim K_z=q\}$ and let $k$ count its occupied parities. There is an actual mark with raw $\mathcal H_q$ depth at least $|P_+|A_*/k\ge \gamma_q\mathcal H_q(S)/k\ge\gamma_d\mathcal H_q(S)/2^n$. One can choose the mark with depth strictly greater than $|P_+|A_*/k$ whenever the positive fibers share a direction space and $|P_+|>k$.

## Proof

For a full-dimensional body in its affine plane, through-cap volume is jointly continuous in the unit normal and mark, since cutting boundaries have zero intrinsic volume. Compactness of the sphere gives attainment and continuity of the point depth, hence a deepest mark. Boundary marks have zero depth by supporting planes and interior marks have positive depth by inballs. A minimizing through-cap at an interior mark has that mark as its section centroid. Apply the one-fiber calculation of 1269c554ca9a41b8 §2 with fixed base coefficient and $(u',b')=(w,w\cdot y)$ for a cap through $y$. The trace is proper, so the two-sided derivative is its positive coarea times $w$ dotted with the section centroid minus $y$. Since the variation always contains $y$, the derivative vanishes at a minimizing direction for every $w$. A minimum cap at the midpoint of two distinct deepest marks would either have different thresholds at the two marks, or be a minimizing cap through both. In the first case the larger endpoint threshold cuts off a strictly smaller cap, since the open slab contains an interior point of their segment. In the second case the same section would have two distinct centroids. Both contradict maximality.

When $n=0$, the one nonempty fiber is its own contact set, and the statement follows at its deepest point from ordinary Grünbaum in its affine plane. Assume $n\ge1$.

For any convex combination of bases $z_i\in Q$ with positive coefficients $\lambda_i$ and integer barycenter $p$, convexity gives $\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}\subseteq K_p$. The direction space of the affine hull of the sum is the sum of the endpoint direction spaces. Each has dimension $q$, whereas $K_p$ has dimension at most $q$, so all these spaces agree with that of $K_p$. The affine planes can therefore be translated to the same Euclidean $q$-space, choosing the weighted mean of their origins as the origin of the containing fiber.

At the mark $\sum_i\lambda_i g_{z_i}$, the ordinary depth root in $K_p$ is at least $\sum_i\lambda_i m_{z_i}$. To see this when there is more than one endpoint, place the translated $q$-bodies over auxiliary affinely independent real bases, so that their hull is full-dimensional and its fiber at the weighted base has exactly their Minkowski combination. Apply the joint depth-root concavity of 4e868f774de77608 and then containment in $K_p$. A single endpoint gives the same bound directly. In particular $m_p\ge\sum_i\lambda_i m_{z_i}$. Affinity gives $\sum_i\lambda_i m_{z_i}=\ell(p)>0$, while the majorant gives $m_p\le\ell(p)$. Thus $p\in Q$, proving lattice completeness.

In particular for any two distinct sites in the same base parity, the integer midpoint lies in $Q$. The lower bound for the pointwise depth root at the interpolated mark equals $m_p$, so this mark is deepest in the midpoint fiber. Uniqueness of the deepest mark puts the lifted midpoint in the graph $T=\{(z,g_z):z\in Q\}$. The graph lies in the full product space and has the $k_Q$ occupied base parities as labels.

Apply f4547c4166b39683 to $T$ with atom weights $A_z$. It gives a graph point with atomic depth at least $B_Q/k_Q$ and minimal-face weight greater than $B_Q/k_Q$ outside the stated equally weighted vertex exception. The marked-fiber graph transfer applies since the marks are interior in their fiber planes and $0<A_z\le V_z/2<V_z$ by opposite through-mark caps. It gives the first inequality for the contact fibers and hence for all of $S$, including its strictness conclusion when there is a common contact direction space. For the second inequality, apply 6769574fc1bd9f8d to each intrinsic $q$-body with metronoid mass parameter $V_z$, where its ordinary volume centroid belongs to the metronoid. This gives $A_z\ge\gamma_q V_z$.

For the last claim use the constant affine majorant $A_*^{1/q}$, for which $Q=P_+$. Fibers of dimension less than $q$ contribute zero $\mathcal H_q$ measure, so $\mathcal H_q(S)=\sum_{z\in P_+}V_z$. Also $k\le2^n$ and $\gamma_q\ge\gamma_d$ for $q\le d$. The latter follows because the derivative of $t\log(t/(t+1))$ is $-\log(1+1/t)+1/(t+1)<0$ for $t>0$, by integrating $1/(1+x)$ on $[0,1/t]$.
