## Statement

Let $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional simplex of volume $V$, centroid $g$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$. Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For a unit vector $u$, set $b=u\cdot g$, $\ell=\min_T u\cdot x$ and
$$
f(u)=\frac{|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge b\rbrace|}{V},\qquad
R(u)=\frac{|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge 2b-\ell\rbrace|}{V},\qquad
\rho_d=\min_{\lVert u\rVert=1}[f(u)-\Gamma_d+R(u)].
$$
Then $f(u)\ge\Gamma_d$, with equality precisely for the $d+1$ unit normals in the directions of $\nabla\lambda_i$. The constant $\rho_d$ is affine invariant; $\rho_1=0$, and for $d\ge2$,
$$
0<\rho_d\le\left(\frac{d-1}{d+1}\right)^d<\Gamma_d.
$$
Equivalently, $\rho_d$ is the minimum of $\Phi_r(\bar r)+\Phi_r(2\bar r)-\Gamma_d$ over $r=(r_0,\ldots,r_d)\in[0,1]^{d+1}$ with $\min_i r_i=0$, $\max_i r_i=1$, where $\bar r=\sum_i r_i/(d+1)$ and $\Phi_r(s)=\Pr(\sum_i r_i\lambda_i(X)\ge s)$ for $X$ uniform on $T$. In dimension two this minimum equals $2(\sqrt{10}-3)/9$ and its normalized vertex levels are $(0,1-\sqrt{2/5},1)$ up to permutation.

## Proof

**Centroid tail and its equality directions.** Fix $u$ and let $h=\max_T u\cdot x$, $Y=u\cdot X$, and $F(t)=\Pr(Y\ge t)$. This distribution has no atoms because all hyperplanes are volume-null. Its tail is continuous and strictly decreases from one to zero on $[\ell,h]$: every open slab with endpoints strictly between $\ell$ and $h$ meets the interior of $T$. Thus $F(Y)$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. The weighted Minkowski sum of upper caps at levels $s,t$ is contained in the upper cap at the corresponding weighted level. Brunn–Minkowski therefore makes $F^{1/d}$ concave on $[\ell,h]$. Since $b=\mathbb EY$ lies in the interior of that interval, Jensen gives
$$
F(b)^{1/d}\ge \mathbb E[F(Y)^{1/d}]=\int_0^1 x^{1/d}\,dx=\frac{d}{d+1}.
$$
If equality holds, a supporting affine majorant to the concave root at $b$ has zero expected excess over that root. The excess is nonnegative and continuous and $Y$ has full support, so the root agrees with that line throughout the interval. The endpoint values imply $F(t)=((h-t)/(h-\ell))^d$.

Let $m$ vertices attain $\ell$, and put $k=d+1-m$. At each of the $k$ other vertices the level exceeds $\ell$ by at least some $\delta>0$. If $Y\le\ell+\varepsilon$, each of those $k$ barycentric coordinates is at most $\varepsilon/\delta$. Omit one minimizing coordinate; the remaining $d$ coordinates have constant density $d!$ on the standard coordinate simplex. Bounding that simplex by the unit box shows $\Pr(Y\le\ell+\varepsilon)\le d!(\varepsilon/\delta)^k$. If $k\ge2$ this contradicts the positive linear term $d\varepsilon/(h-\ell)$ from the affine-root formula. Hence $k=1$, so $u\cdot x=\ell+(h-\ell)\lambda_i(x)$ for the one nonminimizing vertex $i$. Conversely, in that direction the cap $\lbrace\lambda_i\ge t\rbrace$ is a homothetic simplex of relative volume $(1-t)^d$ for $0\le t\le1$, and $\lambda_i(g)=1/(d+1)$, giving equality.

**Minimum and coefficient domain.** The two cap fractions are continuous in $u$: their thresholds are continuous (the minimum is over the vertices) and their boundary hyperplanes are null, including at supporting levels. At a direction with $f(u)=\Gamma_d$, the reflected threshold is $\lambda_i\ge2/(d+1)$ and its fraction is $((d-1)/(d+1))^d$. For $d\ge2$, at every direction at least one of $f(u)-\Gamma_d$ and $R(u)$ is positive. Their continuous sum attains a positive minimum on the sphere, and evaluating at any canonical direction gives the upper bound. For $d=1$ the canonical direction already gives zero and the sum is nonnegative. Positive affine rescaling of a functional preserves both fractions, since it takes the two thresholds to the corresponding thresholds. Its values at the vertices can thus be normalized to $r_i\in[0,1]$ with minimum zero and maximum one. Conversely every such tuple specifies one affine functional. Its thresholds are $\bar r$ and $2\bar r$. This proves the coefficient description and, since all full-dimensional simplices are affinely equivalent, the dimension-only assertion.

The optional formula $\Phi_r(s)=[r_0,\ldots,r_d](x-s)_+^d$ uses the usual confluent divided difference. For smooth $G$, the divided difference $[r_0,\ldots,r_d]G$ is the integral of $G^{(d)}(\sum_i r_i\lambda_i)$ over the coordinate simplex, with ordinary coordinate measure of mass $1/d!$. For distinct knots this follows by induction: integrate in the last coordinate, moving weight from $r_0$ to $r_d$, and the two boundary integrals give the divided-difference recursion after division by $r_d-r_0$. Continuity gives the identity at repeated knots for smooth $G$. Apply this to smooth mollifications of $(x-s)_+^d$. Their $d$th derivatives are bounded by $d!$ and converge to $d!\,1_{\lbrace x>s\rbrace}$ off $s$. The level set has zero coordinate volume because $r$ is not constant, so the integrals converge to $\Phi_r(s)$. No knot has multiplicity more than $d$, and the derivatives through order $d-1$ of the mollifications converge at the knots to the derivatives of the truncated power. The confluent divided differences therefore converge to the claimed value.

**Exact planar value.** Up to permutation the vertex levels are $(0,r,1)$ with $0\le r\le1$, and $b=(1+r)/3$ in this normalization. For $r\le s\le1$ the upper cap is the triangle at the top vertex and has fraction $(1-s)^2/(1-r)$, by multiplying the two edge intercept ratios. For $0\le s\le r$ the upper cap has fraction $1-s^2/r$ by the complementary bottom triangle. These formulas are used only with nonzero denominators, and the fraction is zero for $s\ge1$. Hence
$$
\Phi_r(b)+\Phi_r(2b)-\frac49=
\begin{cases}
(5r^2-4r+1)/(9(1-r)),&0\le r\le 1/2,\\
(3-r-1/r)/9,&1/2\le r\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
The pieces agree at $1/2$. In the first, put $v=1-r$; the expression becomes $(5v+2/v-6)/9$. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives its minimum $(2\sqrt{10}-6)/9$, attained only at $v=\sqrt{2/5}\in[1/2,1]$. The second piece is increasing and its minimum is $1/18$. Since $\sqrt{10}<13/4$, the first minimum is strictly smaller. This is the threshold for the displayed profile, with no assertion that it is the largest mass fraction permitted by a mixed-depth theorem.
