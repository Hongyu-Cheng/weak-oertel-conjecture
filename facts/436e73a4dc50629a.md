## Statement

Consider a finite cover with one canonical centroid cap of each vertex type of every positive simplex fiber, all strictly empty on every other fiber. For a smooth cap-moment cost $\Phi$ of margin degree zero and source degree $h$ with $h\Phi>0$ on every cap, the fixed-source cap-cover stationarity equations admit an objective multiplier one, zero budget and metric multipliers, and a centroid contact for each fiber, in any fixed positive definite quadratic normalization of the continuous normals. On each fiber the local contact weights are proportional to $S_i=dc/H_i$ and the coverage multiplier is $(h\Phi/c)\sum_i S_i$.

More generally replace the quadratic normalization by $N(c,\Psi,T)=1$ with source and margin degrees $h_N,k_N$ and $k_N\ne0$. Let $F$ have degrees $h_F,k_F$, and let both shape factors be positive at $\kappa_d$. On the strict simplex cone region, the normalization is regular and the reduced cost is
$$
F_{\mathrm{eff}}(c)=f_F(\kappa_d)f_N(\kappa_d)^{-k_F/k_N}c^\rho,
\qquad \rho=h_F-k_Fh_N/k_N.
$$
With objective multiplier one, the normalization multiplier $-k_FF/(k_NN)$ leaves the section coefficient $\rho F_{\mathrm{eff}}(c)/c$. When $\rho>0$, the canonical cover has a normal zero-budget stress with equal local contact weights and coverage multiplier $(d+1)(\rho F_{\mathrm{eff}}/c)dc/H$ on each fiber. For $\rho=0$ the reduced differential is zero.

## Proof

All canonical types on the same simplex have cap mass $c=\Gamma_d|K|$. Each has affine margin on its own fiber a positive multiple of $\lambda_i-1/(d+1)$ and its own section centroid is the simplex centroid $g$. Differentiating the cap survival formula in the raw threshold gives $S_i=dc/H_i$. Thus $S_i u_i=(d+1)c\nabla\lambda_i$ and $\sum_i S_i u_i=0$. The indicated probability weights on the own types give a vanishing weighted margin on the whole own fiber and are supported on the rows that attain the maximum zero margin at $g$. This supplies the cover contact and the stationarity in the contact weights. The objective covector for each row when $k=0$ is $(h\Phi/c)S_i(z,g,-1)$, and $h\Phi/c$ is constant across its own types by homogeneity and their equal mass. The stated coverage multiplier gives exactly the opposite covector, so the cap equations hold with zero budget and metric multipliers. Off-fiber margins can be chosen strictly negative by the hypothesis of this cover. Positive rescaling of each full margin preserves these signs.

For the scalar normalization, the cone formula gives $\partial_HN=k_NN/H\ne0$, and solving it gives $H/(d+1)=f_N(\kappa_d)^{-1/k_N}c^{-h_N/k_N}$. Substitution in $F$ yields the displayed power. Using the derivative formula for both costs shows that the indicated multiplier cancels their $DH$ terms and leaves exactly $\rho F_{\mathrm{eff}}(c)Dc/c$ on $N=1$. Because $c$ is common within each fiber, the normalized apex heights are common there and hence so are the $S_i$. The centroid contact with equal weights therefore balances the continuous normals. The same mass, base and continuous equations then hold with the asserted multiplier and zero budget multipliers. The sign condition makes the coverage multiplier positive. These constructions establish stationary multipliers. For instance $N=\Psi/c$ gives $F_{\mathrm{eff}}=c$ for $F=\Psi$, while $N=\sqrt{T/c}$ gives $F_{\mathrm{eff}}=c/\sqrt{\kappa_d}$ for the same $F$ on the cone.
