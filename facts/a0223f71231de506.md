## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, $d\ge2$, let $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional simplex of volume $V$ and centroid $g$, and let $A\subseteq T$ be compact convex with $0<a=|A|\le\rho_d V$, where $\rho_d$ is the reflected simplex profile minimum. For distinct $q,v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n$, put $p=2q-v$ and form the convex hull of the fibers $\lbrace z\rbrace\times T$ at all binary vertices and $\lbrace p\rbrace\times A$. Its exact integer support is $\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\cup\lbrace p\rbrace$ with the assigned fibers. Using their $d$-dimensional volumes and all closed ambient halfspaces, the raw maximum over the mixed-integer points in the hull is $\Gamma_d V+a$, attained only at $(q,g)$. For $\alpha=a/V$ the normalized maximum is $(\Gamma_d+\alpha)/(2^n+\alpha)$ and its right derivative at zero is $(2^n-\Gamma_d)/4^n$.

## Proof

Flip the base coordinates with $q_i=1$ by $z_i\mapsto1-z_i$. This preserves the lattice, fiber volumes and halfspaces, and reduces to $q=0$, $v=\mathbf 1_I$, $p=-\mathbf 1_I$ for a nonempty coordinate set $I$. Write $k=|I|$. The base projection lies in $[-1,1]^n$ and has nonnegative coordinates outside $I$. Any integer base outside the cube must have a coordinate $-1$. The only generator with such a coordinate is $p$, so attaining $-1$ forces the entire convex weight onto $p$. The fiber there is exactly $A$. All continuous coordinates of the hull lie in $T$, and each cube vertex already carries $T$, proving the census. The hull contains $[0,1]^n\times T$, so is full dimensional.

To bound a closed halfspace through $(q,g)$ from below, restrict it to $(q+\mathbb R(v-q))\times\mathbb R^d$. In the parameter $t$ defined by $z=q+t(v-q)$ the three retained fibers over $t=-1,0,1$ are exactly $A,T,T$. If the restriction is the whole subspace they contribute $2V+a$. Otherwise it is a closed halfspace through the middle centroid of this three-fiber configuration, so the three-fiber theorem gives at least $\Gamma_dV+a$. Its containment and mass hypotheses are exactly those assumed here. Other fibers contribute nonnegative mass. Any closed halfspace containing $(q,g)$ contains a parallel through-point one.

For the upper bound define $\delta_q(z)=\sum_i z_i$, so that $\delta_q(q)=0$, $\delta_q(p)=-k$, and $\delta_q\ge1$ on the other cube vertices. For each cube vertex $w\ne q$, set
$$
\delta_w(z)=(k+1)\sum_{i:w_i=1}(1-z_i)+\sum_{i:w_i=0}z_i.
$$
This is zero at $w$ and at least one at the other cube vertices. If $j=|I\cap\operatorname{supp} w|$ and $s=|\operatorname{supp} w\setminus I|$, then $\delta_w(p)=(2k+3)j+(k+1)s-k\ge1$, since $j+s\ge1$. With barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ of $T$, the caps
$$
\lbrace\lambda_i(x)-2\delta_w(z)\ge1/(d+1)\rbrace,
\qquad w\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n,\quad 0\le i\le d,
$$
cover the whole actual source. For $w=q$ they include all of $A$ and exclude the other cube fibers, giving mass $\Gamma_d V+a$. For $w\ne q$ they isolate the canonical cap on the fiber at $w$, giving mass $\Gamma_d V$. These inclusion and exclusion statements use $0\le\lambda_i\le1$ on $A$ and $T$.

At $(q,y)$ with $y\ne g$ some $\lambda_i(y)>1/(d+1)$. Raising the $q$ cap threshold to $\lambda_i(y)\le1$ preserves the full added endpoint and other exclusions, but strictly reduces the own cap. At points over the other cube vertices a covering cap already has mass strictly below $\Gamma_d V+a$. At any point over $p$, the vertical cap $\sum_{i\in I} z_i\le-k$ isolates $A$, of mass $a$. This proves uniqueness. The exact total is $2^nV+a$. With no added endpoint the canonical caps isolate the cube fibers, and a cube vertex centroid always pays at least $\Gamma_dV$ on its own fiber, so the same value formula extends to the no-addition baseline. The derivative of the normalized quotient at $\alpha=0$ is $(2^n-\Gamma_d)/4^n$.
