## Statement

For every \(0<\varepsilon<2/5\), let
\[
C_\varepsilon:=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb R^2:|t|\leq 1,\ |x|\leq 1-(1-\varepsilon)|t|\}.
\]
Then \(C_\varepsilon\) is a convex body. For the integer midpoint \(m=0\), define, for every \(v\in\mathbb Z\) and \(y\in\mathbb R\),
\[
I_v(y):=\int_{\mathbb R}
\mathbf 1_{C_\varepsilon}(v,y+u)
\mathbf 1_{C_\varepsilon}(-v,y-u)\,du
\]
and
\[
G_0(y):=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}I_v(y).
\]
Writing \((s)_+:=\max\{s,0\}\), every lattice-displacement term is
\[
I_0(y)=2(1-|y|)_+,
\qquad
I_1(y)=I_{-1}(y)=2(\varepsilon-|y|)_+,
\qquad
I_v(y)=0\quad(|v|\geq2).
\]
Consequently,
\[
G_0(y)=2(1-|y|)_++4(\varepsilon-|y|)_+.
\]
Moreover,
\[
G_0(\varepsilon)^2-G_0(0)G_0(2\varepsilon)
=4\varepsilon(5\varepsilon-2)<0,
\]
so \(G_0\) is not log-concave.

## Proof

Fix \(0<\varepsilon<2/5\). Since \(0<1-\varepsilon<1\), the defining conditions for \(C_\varepsilon\) are equivalently the following six linear inequalities:
\[
t\leq1,
\qquad -t\leq1,
\qquad
\sigma x+\tau(1-\varepsilon)t\leq1
\quad(\sigma,\tau\in\{-1,1\}).
\]
Indeed, the last four inequalities are equivalent to
\(|x|+(1-\varepsilon)|t|\leq1\), and under \(|t|\leq1\) this is equivalent to
\(|x|\leq1-(1-\varepsilon)|t|\). Thus \(C_\varepsilon\) is a closed convex set. It is bounded because its definition gives \(|t|\leq1\) and \(|x|\leq1\), hence it is compact. It has nonempty interior: if \(|t|<1/4\) and \(|x|<1/4\), then
\[
|x|+(1-\varepsilon)|t|<\frac14+\frac14=\frac12<1,
\]
and \(|t|<1\), so \((-1/4,1/4)^2\subseteq\operatorname{int}C_\varepsilon\). Therefore \(C_\varepsilon\) is compact, convex, and full-dimensional, hence a convex body.

We now compute every term \(I_v(y)\). If \(v\in\mathbb Z\) satisfies \(|v|\geq2\), then neither \((v,y+u)\) nor \((-v,y-u)\) can lie in \(C_\varepsilon\), because the first coordinate would have absolute value greater than \(1\). Hence
\[
I_v(y)=0\qquad(|v|\geq2).
\]
It remains to consider exactly \(v\in\{-1,0,1\}\). Set
\[
r_v:=1-(1-\varepsilon)|v|,
\]
so that \(r_0=1\) and \(r_1=r_{-1}=\varepsilon>0\). Because \(|-v|=|v|\), the two indicator factors in \(I_v(y)\) equal one precisely when
\[
|y+u|\leq r_v
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|y-u|\leq r_v.
\]
Equivalently, \(u\) belongs to the intersection
\[
[-r_v-y,r_v-y]\cap[y-r_v,y+r_v].
\]
The larger lower endpoint is \(|y|-r_v\), and the smaller upper endpoint is \(r_v-|y|\). The intersection therefore has length
\[
2(r_v-|y|)_+.
\]
Thus
\[
I_0(y)=2(1-|y|)_+,
\qquad
I_1(y)=I_{-1}(y)=2(\varepsilon-|y|)_+.
\]
Together with the vanishing of all \(|v|\geq2\) terms, this proves
\[
G_0(y)=2(1-|y|)_++4(\varepsilon-|y|)_+.
\]

Since \(0<\varepsilon<2/5\), one has \(2\varepsilon<1\). The exact formula gives
\[
G_0(0)=2+4\varepsilon=2(1+2\varepsilon),
\qquad
G_0(\varepsilon)=2(1-\varepsilon),
\qquad
G_0(2\varepsilon)=2(1-2\varepsilon).
\]
All three values are positive. If \(G_0\) were log-concave, its midpoint inequality at the two points \(0\) and \(2\varepsilon\) would imply
\[
G_0(\varepsilon)^2\geq G_0(0)G_0(2\varepsilon).
\]
Instead,
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_0(\varepsilon)^2-G_0(0)G_0(2\varepsilon)
&=4(1-\varepsilon)^2-4(1+2\varepsilon)(1-2\varepsilon)\\
&=4\varepsilon(5\varepsilon-2)<0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality holds for the full range \(0<\varepsilon<2/5\). Hence \(G_0\) is not log-concave, which proves the stated raw-marginal route exclusion.
