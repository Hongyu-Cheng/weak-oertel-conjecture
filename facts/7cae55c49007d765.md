## Statement

For an integer $m\ge1$ and $0\le x_j\le h<1$ with some $x_j>0$, put $\rho=\prod_j(1-x_j)$, $E=\rho^{-1}-1$, and
$$
L=\left(1-\frac{\sum_j x_j}{E}\right)\prod_j(1+x_j/E),\qquad
R=\rho(E+h)\left[1-\frac{(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)}{h^{m+1}}\right].
$$

Let $T$ be a $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, with affine vertex heights $p>q>0$ at two vertices, $-r_j<0$ at $m\ge1$ other vertices, and arbitrary remaining zero heights. Let $F$ be the volume fraction on the closed nonnegative side, $b_i$ the barycentric coordinates of the uniform section centroid, and $V$ the signed sum of independent unit-rate exponentials with these coefficients. Put
$$
h=1-q/p,\quad x_j=\frac{hr_j}{q+r_j},\quad s_j=\frac{p}{p+r_j},
\quad X=\prod_j s_j,\quad \rho=\prod_j(1-x_j),\quad E=\rho^{-1}-1.
$$
Use these $L,R$ at this vector and threshold, let $Q=\prod_j(1+x_j/E)$, $B=\sum_jx_j/(1-x_j)$, $C=B/(1-\rho)-1$, and $W=(1-h)CQ$. Write $\mu_i=d b_i$ and use $p,q$ to denote the two positive indices. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\frac{X\rho(E+h)}h=\frac{(E+h)\prod_j(h-x_j)}{h^{m+1}},
& f_V(0)&=\frac{X(1-\rho)}{p-q},\\
\mu_q&=\frac{1-\sum_j x_j/E}{h},
&\mu_p&=\frac{(1-h)C}{h},\\
\mu_j&=s_j(1+x_j/E)\quad(c_j=-r_j),
&\mu_i&=1\quad(c_i=0),\\
\prod_{i\ne p}\mu_i-F(1-F)&=\frac Xh(L-R),
&\prod_{i\ne q}\mu_i-F(1-F)&=\frac Xh(W-R).
\end{aligned}
$$
For any two negative indices $i,j$, with $h,E$, the other coordinates and $k=(1-x_i)(1-x_j)$ fixed, $W-R$ is a quadratic with strictly negative second derivative in $T_0=(1-x_i)+(1-x_j)$ on its feasible range.

## Proof

Let $G=\sum_iY_i$ for the independent unit-rate exponentials and $U_i=Y_i/G$. The Jacobian factor $G^d$ makes $U$ uniform on the barycentric simplex, independent of $G$, which has the gamma density with shape $d+1$ and rate one. Write $L_0=\sum_i c_iU_i$, so $V=G L_0$ and $F=\Pr(V\ge0)$. By coarea the density of $L_0$ at zero is the positive $(d-1)$-volume of its proper section divided by the full volume and by the height-gradient norm. The weighted density with weight $U_i$ at zero is $b_i$ times that density. These density functions are bounded and continuous at zero: the parallel slices, translated into one hyperplane, lie in a fixed bounded region and converge pointwise off the relative boundary of the section, a null set. Independence yields $f_V(0)=f_{L_0}(0)\mathbb E(1/G)=f_{L_0}(0)/d$. The corresponding density weighted by $Y_i$ at zero is $f_{L_0}(0)b_i$, since $Y_i=GU_i$ cancels the conditional Jacobian $1/G$. Thus the coarea conditional mean equals $\mu_i=d b_i$ in the original dimension. If a coefficient is zero, its independent phase has conditional mean one, and deletion leaves the other conditional means and $F$ unchanged.

Put $A_0=pY_p+qY_q$ and $N_0=\sum_j r_jY_j$. The density of $A_0$ at $u>0$ is $(e^{-u/p}-e^{-u/q})/(p-q)$ by convolution, and its survival probability is $(p e^{-u/p}-q e^{-u/q})/(p-q)$. Put $t_j=q/(q+r_j)$ and $Y=\prod_j t_j$. Conditioning on $N_0$ and using the exponential Laplace transforms gives $F=(pX-qY)/(p-q)$ and $f_V(0)=(X-Y)/(p-q)$. The ratios $t_j/s_j=1-x_j$ and $1-x_j/h=t_j$ give the stated $F$ and $f_V(0)$.

The coefficient derivative of $F$ at a nonzero height $c_i$ is $f_V(0)\mu_i$. For a positive height, condition on the positive phases and differentiate the distribution function of $N_0$ at $A_0>0$; the derivative is the expectation of $Y_i$ times the density of $N_0$ at $A_0$. That density is bounded and continuous on $(0,\infty)$, so dominated convergence applies using the first moment of $Y_i$. The expectation is the coarea density weighted by $Y_i$ at zero, by conditioning and continuity at zero. For a negative height condition on the negative phases and differentiate the survival of $A_0$ at $N_0>0$, again using bounded density and finite first moments. The derivative in $c_i=-r_i$ has the opposite sign from the $r_i$ derivative. These arguments allow repeated negative heights.

Using $X_p/X=\sum_j r_j/[p(p+r_j)]$, $Y_q/Y=\sum_j r_j/[q(q+r_j)]$, and $X_{r_j}/X=-1/(p+r_j)$, $Y_{r_j}/Y=-1/(q+r_j)$, differentiation gives
$$
\mu_q=\frac1h-\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\sum_j\frac{r_j}{q+r_j},\quad
\mu_p=-\frac{1-h}h+\frac{\sum_j r_j/(p+r_j)}{1-\rho},\quad
\mu_j=\frac{s_j-\rho t_j}{1-\rho}.
$$
The identity $r_j/(p+r_j)=(1-h)x_j/[h(1-x_j)]$ and $1/E=\rho/(1-\rho)$ give the desired formulas for the means. Multiplying the negative means gives $XQ$ and every zero mean contributes one. Since $F(1-F)=XR/h$, both gap identities follow.

For the pair motion write $u=1-x_i$, $v=1-x_j$, so $uv=k$ and $u+v=T_0$. The pair's contribution to $B$ is $T_0/k-2$ and its contribution to $E^2Q$ apart from the fixed other factors is $(E+1)^2-(E+1)T_0+k$. Thus $W$ is a positive constant times the product of two affine factors, one with strictly positive slope and one with strictly negative slope. The corresponding product in $R$ is $(h-1)^2+(h-1)T_0+k$, so $R$ is affine and the asserted strict concavity follows.
