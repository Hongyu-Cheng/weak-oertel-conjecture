## Statement

For each integer \(A\ge2\), retain exactly the objects of accepted fact 864a4db51a7ccdd1: \(h=A+1\), \(B=Ah-1=A^2+A-1\),
\[
D_{A,B}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(A,0),(0,B)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad \mathbf j_A=(1,B-h)=(1,A^2-2).
\]
Let \(\nu_{\mathbf j_A}\) be its original maximum-entropy law with full vector mean \(\mathbf j_A\). Put \(T=B-hX-Y\). This \(T\) is exactly the affine coordinate called \(z(X,Y)\) in that fact; it is not a substituted random vector or a latent center. Retain its unique positive parameter pair \((t,q)\) and its partition sum \(Z\), so that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{\mathbf j_A}(x,y)&=\frac{t^xq^{B-hx-y}}{Z},\\
Z&=\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^x\sum_{r=0}^{B-hx}q^r+t^Aq^{-1},\\
\mathbb E X&=1,\qquad \mathbb E T=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
There is an absolute \(\delta>0\) such that, for every \(k\ge1\), every independently chosen integers \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\ge2\), and the original product \(Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k\nu_{\mathbf j_{A_i}}\), every proper closed halfspace containing \(J=(\mathbf j_{A_1},\ldots,\mathbf j_{A_k})\) has \(Q\)-mass at least \(\delta\). Equivalently, for every choice of real block normals \((u_i,v_i)\), including the all-zero choice,
\[
Q\left\{\sum_{i=1}^k\left[u_i(X_i-1)+v_i(Y_i-(B_i-h_i))\right]\ge0\right\}\ge\delta.
\]
No column, floor, parameter, probability, or full vector mean is changed. Constants defined in the proof give the explicit choice \(\delta=(16K_WK_N)^{-1}\). These constants are universal and do not depend on a cap direction.

## Proof

We first establish uniform estimates for the original pair \((t,q)\), using only its exact equations. Set \(M_x=B-hx=h(A-x)-1\) for \(0\le x\le A-1\), and define \(L_x=\sum_{r=0}^{M_x}q^r\), \(R_x=\sum_{r=0}^{M_x}r q^r\), and \(S_A(t)=\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^x\). The exact support and balances from 864a4db51a7ccdd1 become
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{supp}(X,T)
 &=\{(x,r):0\le x\le A-1,\ 0\le r\le M_x\}\cup\{(A,-1)\},\\
\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}(x-1)t^xL_x+(A-1)t^Aq^{-1}&=0,\\
\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^xR_x-t^Aq^{-1}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, eliminating the apex term gives \(\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^x[(x-1)L_x+(A-1)R_x]=0\). Every summand with \(x\ge1\) is nonnegative, and the \(x=1\) summand is strictly positive. Consequently \(R_0/L_0<1/(A-1)\le1\). The mean of a geometric law on \(\{0,\ldots,B\}\) increases strictly with its ratio, because differentiation with respect to its logarithm gives its positive variance. Here \(B\ge5\). At ratio \(2/3\), even its restriction to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) has mean \(66/65>1\); adding larger support points strictly increases that mean. Thus
\[
0<q<\frac23,
\qquad q^2\le t^A.
\tag{2}
\]
The second inequality follows from the last balance in (1), whose right side includes the term \(q\) from \(x=0,r=1\).

We claim that \(1/16<t<6\), and that \(t<4/5\) when \(A\ge8\). To prove the lower bound, suppose \(t\le1/16\). Then (2) gives \(q\le t^{A/2}\le t\). Using \(Z\ge1\), the last balance in (1), and infinite geometric sums gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
1=\mathbb EX
&\le\frac{t}{(1-q)(1-t)^2}
   +\frac{Aq}{(1-q)^2(1-t)}\\
&\le\frac{t+A t^{A/2}}{(1-t)^3}
\le\frac{3t}{(1-t)^3}
\le\frac{768}{3375}<1,
\end{aligned}
\]
a contradiction. For the penultimate inequality, \(A t^{A/2}\le2t\) for every integer \(A\ge2\): successive terms have ratio \(((A+1)/A)\sqrt t\le3/8<1\).

Since \(q<1\), the averages of the initial segments of the decreasing sequence \(1,q,q^2,\ldots\) decrease with their length. Because \(M_x+1=h(A-x)\), this gives \(L_x/L_0\ge(A-x)/A\). For \(A\ge3\), the horizontal balance in (1) implies \(L_0\ge t^2L_2\), hence \(t^2\le A/(A-2)\le3\). For \(A=2\), the last balance reads \(t^2/q=R_0+tR_1\), and \(M_0=5,M_1=2\). Thus \(t^2\le15+3t\), which implies \(t<6\). Finally, for \(A\ge8\), retaining \(x=2,3,4\) in the horizontal balance gives
\[
1\ge\frac12(t^2+2t^3+3t^4).
\]
The right side is strictly increasing for positive \(t\), and at \(t=4/5\) it is \(904/625>1\). This proves the claimed upper bound. Put \(\rho=\sqrt{4/5}<1\). We have proved
\[
\frac1{16}<t<6,
\qquad A\ge8\Longrightarrow t<\frac45\ \text{and}\ q\le\rho^A.
\tag{3}
\]

Call \(T=0\) the line event and \(T\ne0\) the active event. Write their probabilities as \(\beta_A\) and \(s_A=1-\beta_A\). Both are positive. The conditional line coordinate \(U_A\) has its exact original geometric masses \(t^x/S_A(t)\) on \(\{0,\ldots,A-1\}\). From (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z&\le\frac{S_A(t)}{1-q}
       +\frac{qS_A(t)}{(1-q)^2}
 =\frac{S_A(t)}{(1-q)^2},\\
\beta_A&=\frac{S_A(t)}Z\ge(1-q)^2\ge\frac19,\\
s_A&\le2\Pr\{(X,T)=(A,-1)\}
 \le\frac{2q}{(1-q)^2}\le18q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
For the bound on \(s_A\), the equation \(\mathbb ET=0\) says that the apex probability equals \(\mathbb E[T\mathbf1_{\{T>0\}}]\), which is at least \(\Pr(T>0)\), since the positive values of \(T\) are integers. Also \(Z\ge S_A(t)\), and the last balance in (1) bounds the apex numerator by \(qS_A(t)/(1-q)^2\).

The following finite constants will be used to avoid dependence on \(A\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_*&=\frac1{16\cdot6^{14}},\\
L_*&=7^4+\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}(x+1)^4(4/5)^x,\\
Q_*&=7^4+\sum_{a=8}^{\infty}a^4\rho^a,\\
D_1&=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r(2/3)^{r-1}=9,
\qquad D_4=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^4(2/3)^{r-1},\\
E_*&=8(D_4+D_1^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Every series converges by the ratio test. The symbol \(a\) inside the series defining \(Q_*\) is only an integer summation index, not a cap coefficient. Equation (3) implies \(qA^4\le Q_*\).

We record elementary estimates for geometric horizontal laws. The full line law satisfies \(\operatorname{Var}U_A\ge v_*\) and \(\mathbb E(U_A-1)^4\le L_*\). Every geometric law with ratio \(t\) on an initial subinterval of \(\{0,\ldots,A-1\}\) also has raw fourth moment at most \(L_*\). Indeed, if \(A\le7\), all such coordinates belong to \(\{0,\ldots,6\}\). Moreover \(S_A(t)\le\sum_{x=0}^{6}6^x<6^7\). The line probabilities at zero and one are respectively at least \(6^{-7}\) and \(6^{-7}/16\), and \(\operatorname{Var}U_A\ge\Pr(U_A=0)\Pr(U_A=1)\ge v_*\). If \(A\ge8\), then \(S_A(t)\le5\), so these two probabilities are at least \(1/5\) and \(1/80\), giving variance at least \(1/400>v_*\). Its fourth moment about one is bounded by \(\sum_{x\ge0}(x+1)^4(4/5)^x\). For the raw fourth moment on a shorter initial interval, a geometric law of ratio \(t<1\) is the infinite geometric law conditioned to lie in that interval. Removing its upper tail cannot increase the expectation of the increasing function \(x^4\); the infinite geometric fourth moment is at most the same displayed series. These arguments prove all the asserted estimates, including a singleton shorter interval.

We now prove uniform directional fourth-moment regularity of the active law about its own mean, which differs from the full root. Conditional on the active event, let \(p_A\) be the apex probability. Conditional further on \(T>0\), write \((U,R)=(X,T)\), and put \(\mu_A=\mathbb EU\), \(m_A=\mathbb ER\). All expectations in this paragraph are under that positive-excursion conditional law. Its exact support is \(0\le U\le A-1\), \(1\le R\le M_U\), with weights proportional to \(t^Uq^R\). For each \(r\ge1\) let \(S_r=\sum_{0\le x\le A-1,\ M_x\ge r}t^x\). Then \(S_1=S_A(t)\), since the smallest \(M_x\) is \(A\ge2\), and \(S_r\le S_1\). Its normalizer lies between \(qS_1\) and \(qS_1/(1-q)\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(R=r)&\le q^{r-1},\qquad \Pr(R=1)\ge1-q\ge\frac13,\\
1\le m_A&\le D_1=9,\qquad m_A-1\le qD_1,\\
\mathbb E(R-m_A)^4
&\le8\left[\mathbb E(R-1)^4+(m_A-1)^4\right]
\le E_*q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\mathbb E(R-1)^4\le qD_4\), as follows by summing over \(r\ge2\); the bound on \(m_A-1\) is the same calculation with the first power. Also, conditional on any supported value of \(R\), the coordinate \(U\) is geometric with ratio \(t\) on the exact initial interval \(\{0,\ldots,\min(A-1,\lfloor(B-R)/h\rfloor)\}\). Therefore \(\mathbb EU^4\le L_*\), \(\mu_A^4\le L_*\), and \(\mathbb E(U-\mu_A)^4\le16L_*\). At \(R=1\) this conditional law is precisely the full line law \(U_A\).

Let \(\xi_A=\mathbb E[X\mid T\ne0]\). Since \(\mathbb E[T\mid T\ne0]=0\), the active branch probabilities obey \(p_A=m_A/(1+m_A)\), so \(1/2\le p_A\le9/10\), and \(\xi_A=p_AA+(1-p_A)\mu_A\). For any real coefficients \(a,b\), define the centered active projection \(N_A=a(X-\xi_A)+bT\), and put \(d=a(A-\mu_A)-b(1+m_A)\). Its apex branch mean is \((1-p_A)d\), and its positive branch mean is \(-p_Ad\). The law of total variance and the conditional law at \(R=1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}N_A&\ge p_A(1-p_A)d^2\ge\frac{d^2}{100},\\
\operatorname{Var}N_A&\ge(1-p_A)\Pr(R=1)a^2\operatorname{Var}U_A
 \ge\frac{v_*a^2}{30},\\
\operatorname{Var}N_A&\ge\frac{v_*}{60}(a^2+d^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For the last line, average the first two lower bounds and use \(v_*/30\le1/100\).

On the positive branch its fluctuation about the branch mean is \(F=a(U-\mu_A)+b(R-m_A)\). Independence between \(U\) and \(R\) is not assumed. Using \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\), (6), and \(b=[a(A-\mu_A)-d]/(1+m_A)\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EF^4&\le128L_*a^4+8E_*q b^4,\\
b^4&\le8(a^4A^4+d^4),\\
\mathbb EF^4&\le F_*(a^4+d^4),
\qquad F_*=128L_*+64E_*(Q_*+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The second line uses \(0\le\mu_A\le A-1\) and \(1+m_A\ge1\); the third uses \(qA^4\le Q_*\) and \(q<1\). Centering the two branches now gives \(\mathbb EN_A^4\le9d^4+8\mathbb EF^4\le(9+8F_*)(a^4+d^4)\). Define
\[
K_N=\max\left\{3,(9+8F_*)\left(\frac{60}{v_*}\right)^2\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7) and (8) prove \(\mathbb EN_A^4\le K_N(\operatorname{Var}N_A)^2\) for every \(a,b\). Zero coefficients give zero moments; otherwise (7) ensures positive variance. This is regularity about the active law's own mean \((\xi_A,0)\) in \((X,T)\) coordinates, not about \((1,0)\).

Next define an unscaled scalar center \(W_A\) as follows. On the line branch, of probability \(\beta_A\), sample the original line coordinate \(U_A\) and put \(W_A=U_A-1\). On the active branch, of probability \(s_A\), put \(W_A=\xi_A-1\) deterministically. These choices preserve the exact original horizontal mean, so \(\mathbb EW_A=\mathbb E(X-1)=0\). From (4), (5), and the line estimates,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}W_A&\ge\beta_A\operatorname{Var}U_A\ge\frac{v_*}{9},\\
\mathbb EW_A^4&\le L_*+s_A A^4\le L_*+18Q_*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(0\le\xi_A\le A\), so \(|\xi_A-1|\le A\). Define \(K_W=\max\{3,(L_*+18Q_*)(9/v_*)^2\}\). Then \(\mathbb EW_A^4\le K_W(\mathbb EW_A^2)^2\), uniformly in \(A\). Scaling by any real \(a\) retains this bound.

We supply the scalar cap calculation needed for the product composition. If independent centered real variables \(Z_i\) satisfy \(\mathbb EZ_i^4\le K(\mathbb EZ_i^2)^2\), where \(K\ge3\), then their sum \(Z_\Sigma\) satisfies the same bound: writing \(v_i=\mathbb EZ_i^2\), expansion gives
\[
\mathbb EZ_\Sigma^4=\sum_i\mathbb EZ_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j
\le K\left(\sum_iv_i\right)^2.
\]
For any centered real \(Z_\Sigma\) with positive variance \(v\) and this fourth-moment bound, Hölder gives \(\mathbb E|Z_\Sigma|\ge\sqrt{v/K}\). Centering gives \(\mathbb E(Z_\Sigma)_+=\mathbb E|Z_\Sigma|/2\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives \((\mathbb E(Z_\Sigma)_+)^2\le v\Pr(Z_\Sigma\ge0)\). Hence \(\Pr(Z_\Sigma\ge0)\ge1/(4K)\). At variance zero the centered sum is identically zero and this closed event has probability one. The same calculation applies after conditioning on any latent information for which the variables remain independent and centered with this common bound.

Fix the product and arbitrary scalar coefficients \(a_i,b_i\) in \((X_i,T_i)\) coordinates. Independently for each block perform the following exact experiment. Select its original line or active event with probabilities \(\beta_{A_i},s_{A_i}\). On a line event, sample its original line coordinate and set \(W_i=a_i(U_{A_i}-1)\), \(N_i=0\). On an active event, set \(W_i=a_i(\xi_{A_i}-1)\) and sample the entire original active conditional vector, setting \(N_i=a_i(X_i-\xi_{A_i})+b_iT_i\). In both cases,
\[
W_i+N_i=a_i(X_i-1)+b_iT_i.
\tag{11}
\]
Sampling the original branches and their original conditional laws realizes the original row exactly at every atom. Making the block experiments independent therefore realizes \(Q\) exactly.

Let \(\mathcal G\) contain the selected events and the sampled line coordinates, without revealing the active vectors. Then \(W_\Sigma=\sum_iW_i\) is \(\mathcal G\)-measurable. Unconditionally the \(W_i\) are independent, centered, and satisfy the fourth-moment bound with constant \(K_W\), by (10). Therefore \(\Pr(W_\Sigma\ge0)\ge1/(4K_W)\). Conditional on any value of \(\mathcal G\), the \(N_i\) remain independent and centered. They are either deterministic zero or the centered active projections already bounded in (9). Thus \(\Pr(N_\Sigma\ge0\mid\mathcal G)\ge1/(4K_N)\) for every latent pattern, where \(N_\Sigma=\sum_iN_i\). In particular, on \(\{W_\Sigma\ge0\}\),
\[
\Pr(W_\Sigma+N_\Sigma\ge0\mid\mathcal G)
\ge\Pr(N_\Sigma\ge0\mid\mathcal G)
\ge\frac1{4K_N}.
\]
Averaging gives \(Q\{\sum_i[a_i(X_i-1)+b_iT_i]\ge0\}\ge(16K_WK_N)^{-1}=\delta\). This also handles all zero-variance conditional patterns and the all-zero direction.

Finally, the exact change of cap coefficients is \(a_i=u_i-h_iv_i\), \(b_i=-v_i\), because \(Y_i-(B_i-h_i)=-h_i(X_i-1)-T_i\). Thus (11) gives precisely the original full-vector projection in the statement. Every proper closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains its parallel boundary-through-\(J\) translate. The bound for the latter therefore proves the stated bound for every containing halfspace.

The varying active horizontal mean is essential: this construction does not claim that the active and line pieces retain the original full vector mean. It therefore does not reinstate the component hypothesis refuted in 864a4db51a7ccdd1. All mean-cap probabilities just proved are under the original product, and the theorem makes no assertion about arbitrary other right-triangle entropy rows.
