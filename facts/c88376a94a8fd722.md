## Statement

For every integer $m\ge2$ and $0<x<1$, set $E=(1-x)^{-m}-1$, $\rho=(1+E)^{-1}$, $Z=E((m+1)x-1)+mx$, and $h_c=x+Z/E$. On the open interval $x<h<1$, the function
$$
R(h)=\rho(E+h)\left[1-\frac{(E+h)(h-x)^m}{h^{m+1}}\right]
$$
has exactly one critical point, its maximum. That point satisfies $h<h_c$.

## Proof

Put $f(h)=(E+h)^2(h-x)^m/h^{m+1}$, so $R'=\rho(1-f')$. Differentiation gives
$$
f'(h)=\frac{(h-x)^{m-1}(E+h)}{h^{m+2}}
\left[h^2+((m-1)x-E)h+(m+1)Ex\right].
$$
Set $r=x/E\in(0,1/m)$ and $y=x/(h-x)$. On multiplying $f'-1$ by $r^2(1+y)^{m+2}$, substitution of $h=x(1+y)/y$ gives
$$
[r+(r+1)y][r+((m+1)r-1)y+m(r+1)y^2]-r^2(1+y)^{m+2}=y^2 B(r,y),
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(r,y)
&=-\frac{m(m-1)}2r^2+2mr-1+
 \left[m(1+r)^2-\binom{m+2}{3}r^2\right]y\\
&\phantom{={}}-r^2\sum_{k=4}^{m+2}\binom{m+2}{k}y^{k-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed, the constant and linear terms of the cubic and the binomial expression cancel, and the displayed terms are the coefficients of degree two and higher after division by $y^2$. For each $r>0$ this polynomial is strictly concave on $y>0$ and tends to $-\infty$ there, since the coefficients of degrees at least two are strictly negative. At $y_0=x/(1-x)$, corresponding to $h=1$, the formula for $f'$ gives $f'(1)-1=Z/(1-x)>0$. The sign follows from strict convexity of $E(x)$, since $Z=(1-x)(xE'-E)$ and $E(0)=0$. Hence $B(r,y_0)>0$. Continuity and concavity give exactly one zero in $y>y_0$: after any zero the secant slope from $y_0$ is negative and all later secant slopes from that zero are no larger. The function is positive before this zero and negative after it. As $h$ increases from $x$ to $1$, $y$ decreases from infinity to $y_0$, so $R'$ changes once from positive to negative.

If $h_c\ge1$ the asserted location already follows. If $h_c<1$, let
$$
y_c=\frac{x}{h_c-x}=\frac{x}{(m+1)x-1+mr}>y_0 .
$$
The finite geometric sum for $E$ gives $E\ge mx/(1-x)$, hence $mr\le1-x$ and $y_c\ge1/m$. To show $y_c\le2/(m+1)$ it suffices to have $mr\ge1-(m+1)x/2$. The bound is immediate if the right side is nonpositive. On $0<x<2/(m+1)$ the function
$$
\log\frac{2+(m-1)x}{2-(m+1)x}+m\log(1-x)
$$
vanishes at zero and has derivative $m(m-1)(m+1)x^2/[(1-x)(2+(m-1)x)(2-(m+1)x)]>0$. Exponentiating gives $E<mx/[1-(m+1)x/2]$, as needed.

It remains to check $B(r,y)>0$ on $0<r<1/m$, $1/m\le y\le2/(m+1)$. It is concave in $r$ there: its quadratic coefficient is $-m(m-1)/2+[m-\binom{m+2}{3}]y-\sum_{k=4}^{m+2}\binom{m+2}{k}y^{k-2}<0$. At $r=0$ it is $my-1\ge0$. At $r=1/m$ it is concave in $y$, so its two $y$ endpoints suffice. There the cubic before subtraction equals $[1+(m+1)y][1+y+m(m+1)y^2]/m^2$. At $y=1/m$ and $y=2/(m+1)$, positivity is therefore equivalent, respectively, to
$$
(1+1/m)^m<\frac{2(2m+1)}{m+1},\qquad
(1+2/(m+1))^{m+2}<\frac{3(5m+3)}{m+1}.
$$
The first left side is below $e<11/4<10/3$, and the right side is at least $10/3$. The second left side is below $e^2(1+2/(m+1))<(121/16)(5/3)=605/48<13$, and its right side is at least $13$. Here $\log(1+t)<t$ for $t>0$ gives the exponential estimates, and summing the exponential series through degree three and bounding its tail by $(1/24)/(1-1/5)$ gives $e\le87/32<11/4$. Both endpoints are strictly positive, and interpolation by concavity in $y$ and then in $r$ yields the desired positivity. In particular $B(r,y_c)>0$, so $y_c$ lies before the unique zero and $h_c$ lies strictly above the critical point of $R$.
