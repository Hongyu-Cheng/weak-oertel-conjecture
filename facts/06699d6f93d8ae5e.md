## Statement

For integers \(q\ge2\) and \(m\ge2\), set \(\alpha_q=1-1/q\), \(\varepsilon_q=2^{1/q}-1\), and \(N=m^q\). On the complete integer interval \(D_+=\{0,1,\ldots,N\}\), define \(r_j=(j+1)^{\alpha_q}\). These are exact positive real numbers. The sequence is concave and satisfies

\[
r_{(i+k)/2}\le(1+\varepsilon_q)\frac{r_i+r_k}{2}
\quad\text{for every }i,k\in D_+\text{ with }i\equiv k\pmod2.
\]

For every fixed \(q\ge2\) and every finite \(K\ge1\), choosing any integer \(m\ge2\) such that \(m^{\alpha_q}>3K\) gives no positive convex function \(\varphi:[0,N]\to(0,\infty)\) satisfying \(r_j\le\varphi(j)\le K r_j\) for all \(j\in D_+\). Denote the proposed absolute comparison constant from the subgoal by \(C_*\), reserving \(C\) for the convex body below. No absolute constant \(C_*\ge0\) guarantees such a convex, or affine, majorant with \(K=1+C_*\varepsilon\) under the stated midpoint hypothesis. The same failure occurs with arbitrarily small \(\varepsilon>0\), since \(\varepsilon_q\to0\).

For every integer \(d\ge1\), each displayed sequence is an actual root-volume profile: there is a convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) with \(C_x=\{y:(x,y)\in C\}\), \(w_j=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_j)\), positive integer support exactly \(D_+\), and \(r_j=w_j^{1/d}\). Here \(r_j\) has precisely the actual root-volume meaning, and \(\varphi\) is a proposed convex majorant of those roots, so \(\varphi(j)^d\ge w_j\).

## Proof

Extend the sequence on its real interval by \(r(x)=(x+1)^{\alpha_q}\), \(0\le x\le N\). Since \(0<\alpha_q<1\), its second derivative is \(\alpha_q(\alpha_q-1)(x+1)^{\alpha_q-2}<0\). Thus \(r\) is positive and concave, and its integer restriction is a positive concave sequence on a complete interval.

For positive \(a,b\) and \(0<\alpha<1\), put \(s=a/(a+b)\in(0,1)\). The inequalities \(s^\alpha\ge s\) and \((1-s)^\alpha\ge1-s\) show \(a^\alpha+b^\alpha\ge(a+b)^\alpha\). Consequently, for any real \(x,x'\in[0,N]\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
r\!\left(\frac{x+x'}2\right)
&=2^{-\alpha_q}\bigl((x+1)+(x'+1)\bigr)^{\alpha_q}\\
&\le 2^{-\alpha_q}\bigl((x+1)^{\alpha_q}+(x'+1)^{\alpha_q}\bigr)\\
&=2^{1/q}\frac{r(x)+r(x')}2
=(1+\varepsilon_q)\frac{r(x)+r(x')}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For same-parity integer endpoints the midpoint belongs to \(D_+\), giving exactly the required hypothesis. This proof includes identical endpoints. It does not depend on any parity omission.

Let \(L\) be the chord joining the two endpoint root values, so \(L(x)=1+(x/N)((N+1)^{\alpha_q}-1)\). If a convex \(\varphi\) obeyed the proposed inequalities with factor \(K\), convexity and its bounds at \(0,N\) would imply, for every \(x\in[0,N]\),

\[
\varphi(x)
\le\left(1-\frac{x}{N}\right)\varphi(0)+\frac{x}{N}\varphi(N)
\le K L(x).
\]

In particular at the interior integer \(x=m\), since \(N=m^q\) and \(\alpha_q=(q-1)/q\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
L(m)
&=1+m^{1-q}\bigl((m^q+1)^{\alpha_q}-1\bigr)\\
&<1+m^{1-q}(2m^q)^{\alpha_q}
=1+2^{\alpha_q}<3,\\
r_m&=(m+1)^{\alpha_q}>m^{\alpha_q}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(m^{\alpha_q}>3K\), these estimates contradict \(r_m\le\varphi(m)\le K L(m)<3K\). Such an integer \(m\) exists for every fixed \(q\) and finite \(K\), by the unboundedness of \(m^{\alpha_q}\). For a proposed absolute \(C_*\ge0\), take \(K=1+C_*\varepsilon_q\) and choose that \(m\). An affine function is convex, so the same contradiction covers affine majorants. Finally \(2^{1/q}\to1\), proving that the obstruction persists for arbitrarily small positive defects, rather than only for a large-defect regime.

For the actual-fiber realization, fix any \(d\ge1\) and let \(B=[-1/2,1/2]^d\), a convex body of volume one containing the origin. Define

\[
C=\{(x,y):0\le x\le N,\ y\in r(x)B\}.
\]

The set is compact because \(r\) is continuous and bounded. It has nonempty ambient interior because \(r(x)\ge1\) throughout \([0,N]\), so it contains \((0,N)\times(-1/2,1/2)^d\). To check convexity, take \(y_i\in r(x_i)B\) for \(i=0,1\). For \(0\le t\le1\), convexity of \(B\) gives \((1-t)y_0+t y_1\in((1-t)r(x_0)+t r(x_1))B\). Concavity of \(r\), together with \(0\in B\), then puts this set inside \(r((1-t)x_0+t x_1)B\). Thus \(C\) is a convex body.

Its fiber at \(x\in[0,N]\) is exactly \(r(x)B\), and its fibers outside that interval are empty. Hence at the integers its positive support is exactly \(D_+\), \(w_j=r(j)^d\), and \(w_j^{1/d}=r_j\), with the original unscaled volume normalization. Both endpoint roots are strictly positive, namely \(r_0=1\) and \(r_N=(N+1)^{\alpha_q}\); no concavity of a zero extension is asserted. Taking the \(d\)-th power of the established midpoint inequality also gives the actual-volume inequality

\[
w_{(i+k)/2}\le 2^{d/q}\left(\frac{w_i^{1/d}+w_k^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\]

For each fixed \(d\), the multiplier \(2^{d/q}\) also tends to one as \(q\to\infty\).

For completeness, the short-interval parity issue does not supply this obstruction. A singleton admits its exact constant majorant, and two consecutive integer points admit their positive affine interpolant even though only identical-endpoint midpoint tests are available. On three consecutive integer points \(\{a,a+1,a+2\}\), let \(L\) be the positive endpoint chord. Concavity gives \(L(j)\le r_j\) at all three points, and the midpoint hypothesis gives \(r_{a+1}\le(1+\varepsilon)L(a+1)\). Thus \(\varphi=(1+\varepsilon)L\) is a positive affine majorant with \(\varphi(j)\le(1+\varepsilon)r_j\). The constructed failure instead uses a long complete interval and midpoint control valid even for all real pairs.
