## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
For every bounded measurable \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and every
\(t>0\), use the canonical metronoid normalization
\[
\mathsf M_t(E):=
\left\{
\frac1t\int_E x f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
\int_E f(x)\,dx=t
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
\varnothing\ne A\subseteq
\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}
\]
be finite. For \(y\in A\), let
\[
c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
\qquad
D_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)-c_y,
\tag{2}
\]
and choose weights satisfying
\[
0<w_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
\qquad(y\in A).
\tag{3}
\]
Define the common centered body
\[
K:=\bigcap_{y\in A}D_y.
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(K\) is an origin-symmetric convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\); in
particular,
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}K
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_d(K)>0.
\tag{5}
\]

For each parity class
\(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
A_\xi:=\{y\in A:y\equiv\xi\pmod2\},
\qquad
M_\xi:=\sum_{y\in A_\xi}w_y.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(M_\xi=0\), set
\[
B_{\xi,m}:=0
\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(M_\xi>0\), define
\[
B_{\xi,m}:=
\frac1{M_\xi}
\sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
w_uw_v
\qquad(m\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{8}
\]
The sum in (8) is over ordered pairs and includes every diagonal pair
\((u,u)\). If \(B_{\xi,m}>0\), define
\[
q_{\xi,m}:=
\frac1{M_\xi B_{\xi,m}}
\sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
w_uw_v\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(B_{\xi,m}=0\), leave \(q_{\xi,m}\) undefined and omit that label
from every assertion involving \(q_{\xi,m}\).

These quantities have finite support and satisfy exact mass conservation:
\[
\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^n}B_{\xi,m}=M_\xi
\quad\text{for every }\xi,
\qquad
\sum_{\xi}\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^n}B_{\xi,m}
=\sum_{y\in A}w_y.
\tag{10}
\]
Every nonzero labelled output has the simultaneous common-tube realization
\[
\boxed{
q_{\xi,m}+\frac65K
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{B_{\xi,m}}(C_m).}
\tag{11}
\]
It also satisfies the genuine target-fiber capacity bound
\[
\boxed{
0<B_{\xi,m}\leq\frac{a_m}{4}.}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, (11) is an actual metronoid tube in the positive-volume
integer fiber \(C_m\), not merely a formal Minkowski average.

## Proof

Accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b` gives that every \(D_y\) in (2) is an
origin-symmetric convex body. Hence each \(D_y\) contains an open
Euclidean ball about the origin. Since \(A\) is finite, the intersection
in (4) contains the smallest of these finitely many balls. The
intersection is also compact, convex, and origin-symmetric. This proves
(5), including the nondegeneracy of \(K\).

We next establish one input realization that will be used with the same
translation in every fiber. The nested-metronoid inclusion in accepted
fact `11e347c295e9430b` states that
\[
c_y+\frac65D_y
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{a_y/4}(C_y).
\tag{13}
\]
If \(x\in\mathsf M_{a_y/4}(C_y)\), choose a representing density \(f\).
Then
\[
\widetilde f:=\frac{4w_y}{a_y}f
\]
satisfies \(0\leq\widetilde f\leq1\), has mass \(w_y\), and has the
same normalized first moment as \(f\), because (3) gives
\(4w_y/a_y\leq1\). Therefore
\[
\mathsf M_{a_y/4}(C_y)
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{w_y}(C_y).
\tag{14}
\]
For every fixed \(h\in(6/5)K\), equations (4), (13), and (14) give
\[
c_y+h\in\mathsf M_{w_y}(C_y)
\qquad(y\in A).
\tag{15}
\]

Fix a parity class \(\xi\) with \(M_\xi>0\). Apply accepted fact
`fc828b039898bc41` to the finite input family
\[
b_y=w_y,
\qquad
p_y=c_y+h
\qquad(y\in A_\xi).
\tag{16}
\]
The hypotheses of that fact hold by (15), and all pair midpoints are
integral because the inputs have one parity. Its output mass at \(m\) is
exactly (8). For \(B_{\xi,m}>0\), its output point is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{M_\xi B_{\xi,m}}
\sum_{\substack{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\\u+v=2m}}
w_uw_v\frac{p_u+p_v}{2}
&=
\frac1{M_\xi B_{\xi,m}}
\sum_{u+v=2m}
w_uw_v\left(\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}+h\right)\\
&=q_{\xi,m}+h.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The conclusion of accepted fact `fc828b039898bc41` therefore gives
\[
q_{\xi,m}+h
\in\mathsf M_{B_{\xi,m}}(C_m).
\tag{18}
\]
The mass \(B_{\xi,m}\) and center \(q_{\xi,m}\) do not depend on
\(h\). Since (18) holds for every \(h\in(6/5)K\), it proves the set
inclusion (11).

We verify the normalization directly so that the diagonal convention is
explicit. Every ordered pair \((u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi\), including
\((u,u)\), occurs in exactly one sum in (8), at
\(m=(u+v)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\). Hence, when \(M_\xi>0\),
\[
\sum_mB_{\xi,m}
=\frac1{M_\xi}
\sum_{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi}w_uw_v
=\frac{M_\xi^2}{M_\xi}
=M_\xi.
\tag{19}
\]
When \(M_\xi=0\), the same equality follows from the convention (7).
Summing (19) over the finitely many parity classes proves (10).

It remains to prove the quarter-fiber cap. Fix \(\xi,m\) with
\(B_{\xi,m}>0\), and write
\[
P_{\xi,m}:=
\{(u,v)\in A_\xi\times A_\xi:u+v=2m\}.
\]
This set is nonempty. Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac12(C_u+C_v)\subseteq C_m
\qquad((u,v)\in P_{\xi,m}).
\tag{20}
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality applied in \(\mathbb R^d\), followed by
the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_m^{1/d}
&\geq
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(\frac{C_u+C_v}{2}\right)^{1/d}\\
&\geq\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2
\geq(a_ua_v)^{1/(2d)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Thus (3) and (21) imply
\[
\sqrt{w_uw_v}
\leq\frac14\sqrt{a_ua_v}
\leq\frac{a_m}{4}
\qquad((u,v)\in P_{\xi,m}).
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{(u,v)\in P_{\xi,m}}w_uw_v
\leq
\frac{a_m}{4}
\sum_{(u,v)\in P_{\xi,m}}\sqrt{w_uw_v}.
\tag{23}
\]
Let
\[
U:=\{u\in A_\xi:2m-u\in A_\xi\}.
\]
The involution \(u\mapsto2m-u\) maps \(U\) bijectively to itself and
also includes its fixed points, which are exactly the possible diagonal
pairs. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{(u,v)\in P_{\xi,m}}\sqrt{w_uw_v}
&=\sum_{u\in U}\sqrt{w_uw_{2m-u}}\\
&\leq
\left(\sum_{u\in U}w_u\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{u\in U}w_{2m-u}\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\sum_{u\in U}w_u
\leq M_\xi.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Combining (8), (23), and (24) proves
\[
B_{\xi,m}\leq\frac{a_m}{4}.
\]
Positivity is part of the present label hypothesis. It follows at once
that \(a_m>0\), so \(C_m\) is a positive-volume fiber and (11) is an
actual metronoid assertion. This proves (12) and completes the proof.

## External sources

The only external result used directly is the Brunn-Minkowski inequality
in \(\mathbb R^d\): for nonempty measurable sets
\(E,F\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) of finite positive volume,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-t)E+tF)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-t)\operatorname{vol}_d(E)^{1/d}
+t\operatorname{vol}_d(F)^{1/d}
\qquad(0\leq t\leq1).
\]
See Richard J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of
the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), 355-405,
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. It applies in (21) to the
compact convex positive-volume fibers \(C_u,C_v\), with \(t=1/2\).
