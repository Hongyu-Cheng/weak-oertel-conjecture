## Statement

Even with three identical actual square fibers, a same-normal physical martingale need not split into extreme anchored base templates at the unchanged physical source while keeping the full target law.

## Proof

Use $[0,2]\times[-1,1]^2$, normal $u=(0,1)$ and a unit source atom at $(1,(0,1))$. The target has atoms $(0,(-1,1))$, $(1,(1,1))$, $(2,(-1,1))$ of masses $1/4,1/2,1/4$. The normal and both coordinates of the mean are preserved.

The anchored probabilities at base one are $(t,1-2t,t)$ for $0\le t\le1/2$, with vertices the self-template and the equal endpoint template. To supply the base-one target, the self-template rows must have mass $1/2$. They can only use the unique target at base one, with continuous point $(1,1)$, violating the mean of a row at the unchanged source $(0,1)$. The correct intermediate points for the two templates are $(1,1)$ and $(-1,1)$ with equal masses, both on the actual source face.
