## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and $Y_0,\ldots,Y_d$ be independent mean-one exponentials. For arbitrary real coefficients $c_i$, put $C=\{\sum_i c_iY_i\ge0\}$, $P=\Pr(C)>0$, and $\nu_i=\mathbb E(Y_i\mid C)$. For every index $j$,
$$
\prod_{i\ne j}\nu_i\ge P.
$$
When $c_j>0$, equality holds exactly when all nonzero $c_i$ with $i\ne j$ have the same sign, including the case in which all are zero. In a uniform $d$-simplex, a halfspace cap of positive volume fraction $P$ with centroid barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ consequently satisfies $(d+1)^d\prod_{i\ne j}\lambda_i\ge P$ for every $j$.

## Proof

The conditional means are positive and finite. First assume $c_j>0$. Integrating out $Y_j$ gives the conditional density of the remaining coordinates $z$ on the nonnegative orthant as $p(z)=e^{-\psi(z)}/P$, where
$$
\psi(z)=\sum_{i\ne j}z_i+\max\!\left(0,-\sum_{i\ne j}(c_i/c_j)z_i\right).
$$
This is homogeneous of degree one and is bounded above and below by positive multiples of $\sum_{i\ne j}z_i$. Thus $\int e^{-s\psi(z)}\,dz=s^{-d}P$ for $s>0$, and differentiation at $s=1$ gives $\mathbb E_p\psi=d$. Exponential domination near $s=1$ justifies differentiation. Let $q(z)=\prod_{i\ne j}\nu_i^{-1}\exp(-z_i/\nu_i)$ be the product exponential density. Integrating the logarithmic ratio gives
$$
\int p\log(p/q)=\log\!\left(\frac{\prod_{i\ne j}\nu_i}{P}\right),
$$
because $\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb E_p(z_i)/\nu_i=d$. The integral is nonnegative by $\log x\le x-1$ applied to $q/p$, with equality exactly when $p=q$ almost everywhere. If the remaining nonzero coefficients all have one sign or are absent, $\psi$ is linear on the orthant with positive coefficients and $p$ is a product of exponentials. If they have both signs, the two linear formulas for $\psi$ have different gradients on nonempty open sets, precluding equality.

To use the accepted full-product bound for the other indices, let $G=\sum_iY_i$ and $U=Y/G$. Changing variables to $G$ and the first $d$ free barycentric coordinates gives density proportional to $e^{-G}G^d$ times the uniform density on the standard simplex. Thus $U$ is uniform, $G$ is independent of $U$, and $\mathbb EG=d+1$. The event $C$ depends only on $U$, hence $\nu_i=(d+1)\mathbb E(U_i\mid C)$. On that simplex the cap of fraction $P$ has interior centroid $\mathbb E(U\mid C)$ because its positive-volume indicator gives positive weight to strictly positive values of each coordinate. Its indicator is feasible in the retained-volume objective of accepted fact efa4558174ce60cf at this centroid. The retained-volume bound by the simplex energy therefore gives $P\le(d+1)^{d+1}\prod_i\mathbb E(U_i\mid C)=\prod_i\nu_i$.

If $c_j\le0$, the function $g(t)=\Pr(C\mid Y_j=t)$ is nonincreasing. For independent copies $T,T'$ of $Y_j$,
$$
2\operatorname{Cov}(T,g(T))=\mathbb E[(T-T')(g(T)-g(T'))]\le0.
$$
Consequently $\nu_j=\mathbb E[Tg(T)]/P\le1$ and dividing the full product by $\nu_j$ gives the desired bound. A cap of an arbitrary simplex is transferred to the standard simplex by an affine map, which preserves its fraction and the centroid's barycentric coordinates. Its affine defining function becomes $\sum_i c_iU_i$, with $c_i$ the vertex values, so the same exponential representation gives the geometric inequality.
