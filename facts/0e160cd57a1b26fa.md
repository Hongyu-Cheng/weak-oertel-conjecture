## Statement

Let $N\ge2$ and let $A,B\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be full-dimensional polytopes with normal fans $\mathcal F_A,\mathcal F_B$. Put $K_j=(1-j/N)A+(j/N)B$ for $0\le j\le N$, and let $\mathcal F$ be their common refinement. Write $V_{\mathcal F}$ for continuous positively homogeneous functions linear on each cone, $R$ for one generator of each ray, and $\ell_\sigma^f$ for the linear piece of $f$ on a maximal cone $\sigma$. Define $C_{\mathcal F}=\{f\in V_{\mathcal F}:f(r)\ge0\ \forall r\in R\}$. Define $T_A$ and $T_B$ by the inequalities $\eta(r)-\ell_\sigma^\eta(r)\ge0$ for every row $(\sigma,r)$ at which the corresponding endpoint support $h_A$ or $h_B$ has equality.

For $\delta_j\in V_{\mathcal F}$, the supports $h_{K_j}+t\delta_j$ give exactly the full-dimensional integer fibers of their convex hull for every sufficiently small $t\ge0$ if and only if $\eta_0=\delta_0\in T_A$, $\eta_N=\delta_N\in T_B$, and $c_i=2\delta_i-\delta_{i-1}-\delta_{i+1}\in C_{\mathcal F}$ for every $1\le i<N$. The unique decomposition is
$$
\delta_j=(1-j/N)\eta_0+(j/N)\eta_N+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}G_{ji}c_i,\qquad
G_{ji}=\frac{\min(i,j)(N-\max(i,j))}{N}.
$$
Lineality consists precisely of the affine interpolations with $\eta_0\in V_{\mathcal F_A}$, $\eta_N\in V_{\mathcal F_B}$. At fixed endpoints all support velocities are nonnegative, and positive curvature $c_i(u)>0$ at one normal and one interior index gives positive support velocity at that normal at every interior index. In the plane $C_{\mathcal F}$ is the full nonnegative orthant in ray values.

## Proof

A function $f\in V_{\mathcal F}$ is a support exactly when $f(r)-\ell_\sigma^f(r)\ge0$ for all $(\sigma,r)$. In fact each maximal cone is generated by its rays, so the inequalities imply $f=\max_\sigma\ell_\sigma^f$, the support of the convex hull of the linear-piece vectors. Conversely, for a support which is linear on an open cone, its piece is given by a point of the body. At an interior normal of that cone, take a maximizing point and test nearby directions in both signs to force equality with the piece vector. Thus each piece lies everywhere below the support. This argument uses the linear consistency built into $V_{\mathcal F}$ even for a nonsimplicial fan. In the plane the adjacent ray values determine each linear piece and impose no further consistency.

For each interior $j$, the fan is exactly $\mathcal F$. A positive linear combination of the two supports can be linear on a convex cone only if both supports are linear there: each convex Jensen gap is nonnegative, so a zero weighted sum of gaps makes each zero. The converse is immediate. At an interior support the rows with $r\in\sigma$ are identities throughout $V_{\mathcal F}$ and those with $r\notin\sigma$ have strict slack, since the vertex for $\sigma$ is exposed only in its normal cone. Hence all interior perturbations remain supports for sufficiently small $t$. At the endpoints it is exactly the originally active rows which constrain the perturbations. There are finitely many rows and sites. Every feasible small perturbation preserves full dimension because support convergence is uniform on the sphere and the original minimum widths are positive.

Now apply the anchored Minkowski criterion of ba0f3db1bf4f62c6 to the complete lattice base $\{0,\ldots,N\}$. The old support values at every normal form an affine sequence. The anchored inequalities for the new sequence are equivalent to its adjacent midpoint inequalities: the latter say that the piecewise-linear interpolant has nonincreasing slopes and hence is concave, so all anchored inequalities follow. Thus the only other requirements are $c_i\ge0$ pointwise, which are equivalent to the ray tests by linearity on every cone. The convex hull has projection $[0,N]$ and no additional integer sites. A full-dimensional fiber and any point at a different base span the ambient dimension.

The displayed kernel vanishes at $j=0,N$. Its two slopes in $j$ are $(N-i)/N$ to the left of $i$ and $-i/N$ to the right, so its discrete second difference in the specified sign is one at $j=i$ and zero elsewhere. The decomposition therefore has the given endpoint values and second differences. Any difference of two such solutions is affine in $j$ with zero endpoints, and is zero. For lineality $c_i$ and $-c_i$ both lie in the pointed cone $C_{\mathcal F}$, so they vanish. If both signs of an endpoint velocity are feasible, on each old maximal endpoint cone the velocity and its negative are convex, by subtracting the linear old support from the two feasible supports. Thus the velocity is linear on that cone. Conversely a piecewise-linear function in the old endpoint fan retains the old identities and all strict old support rows for both signs near zero. This proves the endpoint lineality and hence the full statement. The interior propagation follows from $G_{ji}>0$ for all interior $i,j$.
