## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $v_1,\ldots,v_{d+1}$ span $\mathbb R^d$ with sole dependence generated by $\sum_i v_i=0$. Set $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. Let $A$ be a convex body with every cut $v_i\cdot x=-1$ proper, so $A$ meets both strict sides, and suppose every full section $A\cap\{v_i\cdot x=-1\}$ lies in $-T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\ge-1\ \forall i\}$. Then the open-side residuals $A\cap\{v_i\cdot x<-1\}$ are pairwise disjoint. In dimension one the analogous statement for the pair of opposite normals holds directly.

## Proof

Suppose $p$ lies in two residuals indexed by $i\ne j$. Properness ensures that the $i$-th cutting section has nonempty relative interior in its cutting hyperplane, since it meets the interior of $A$. The normals $v_i,v_j$ are independent by the sole-dependence hypothesis and $d\ge2$. Hence $v_j\cdot x$ is nonconstant on the $i$-th cutting hyperplane. The entire section satisfies $v_j\cdot x\ge-1$, so it contains a point $q$ with $v_j\cdot q>-1$. Along the segment $[p,q]\subset A$, the $j$-th equality is attained before reaching $q$, while the $i$-th strict inequality still holds. That point belongs to the $j$-th section but lies outside $-T$, a contradiction. In dimension one, the two opposite normal values cannot both be less than $-1$.
