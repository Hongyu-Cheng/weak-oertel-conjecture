## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
For every bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(m>0\), set
\[
\mathsf M_m(K):=
\left\{
\frac1m\int_{\mathbb R^d}x f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq\mathbf 1_K\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_{\mathbb R^d}f=m
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\).  Let
\[
\varnothing\neq I\subseteq
\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:z\equiv\varepsilon\pmod 2\}
\]
be finite, and suppose that
\[
b_z>0,
\qquad
q_z\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in I).
\tag{2}
\]
Extend \(b_z\) by zero to \(\mathbb Z^n\setminus I\), and put
\[
M:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}b_z,
\qquad
S_c:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}b_zb_{2c-z},
\qquad
b'_c:=\frac{S_c}{M}
\quad(c\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
When \(S_c>0\), define
\[
q'_c:=\frac1{S_c}
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\b_zb_{2c-z}>0}}
b_zb_{2c-z}\frac{q_z+q_{2c-z}}2.
\tag{4}
\]
Then, for every \(c\) with \(S_c>0\),
\[
q'_c\in\mathsf M_{b'_c}(C_c).
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z^n}b'_c=M,
\qquad
\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z^n}b'_cc=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}b_zz,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{c:S_c>0}b'_cq'_c
=\sum_{z\in I}b_zq_z.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(Z\) have law \(\mathbb P(Z=z)=b_z/M\), let \(Z_1,Z_2\) be
independent copies of \(Z\), and let \(Z'\) have law
\(\mathbb P(Z'=c)=b'_c/M\).  Then
\[
Z'\stackrel d=\frac{Z_1+Z_2}{2},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(Z')=\frac12\operatorname{Cov}(Z).
\tag{8}
\]

Define the finite atomic measure
\[
\beta':=\sum_{c:S_c>0}b'_c\delta_{(c,q'_c)},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{9}
\]
For a closed ambient halfspace
\(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\}
\]
and define the mixed-volume measure on such a halfspace by
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H_z|_d.
\tag{10}
\]
Then every closed ambient halfspace, including the whole space if
degenerate halfspaces are admitted, satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\gamma_d\,\beta'(H).
\tag{11}
\]

Finally, consider the exact instance
\[
C=[0,2]\times[0,1/2]\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R,
\qquad
b_0=b_2=\frac12,
\qquad
q_0=q_2=\frac14.
\tag{12}
\]
The first repacking in the even class gives masses
\(1/4,1/2,1/4\) at bases \(0,1,2\), respectively.  Splitting this
output by parity and repacking the two classes separately gives mass
\(1/4\) from the even class and mass \(1/2\) from the odd class at
target base \(1\).  Their unscaled merge would require mass \(3/4\)
in the fiber \([0,1/2]\), but
\[
\mathsf M_{3/4}([0,1/2])=\varnothing.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus this parity-split, separately-repack, unscaled-merge operation is not
universally capacity preserving.  No assertion about all possible
iterations is made.

## Proof

All sums below are finite because \(I\) is finite.  Also \(M>0\) by
nonemptiness of \(I\).  If \(b_zb_w>0\), then \(z,w\in I\), so
\(z\equiv w\pmod2\) and \((z+w)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\).  Thus every
nonzero ordered pair occurring below has an integer midpoint.

Fix \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(S_c>0\), and set
\[
J_c:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:b_zb_{2c-z}>0\},
\qquad
w_z:=2c-z,
\qquad
\lambda_z:=\frac{b_zb_{w_z}}{S_c}
\quad(z\in J_c).
\tag{14}
\]
The set \(J_c\) is nonempty, \(\lambda_z>0\), and
\(\sum_{z\in J_c}\lambda_z=1\).  The involution
\(z\mapsto w_z\) maps \(J_c\) bijectively to itself.

Accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` establishes the positive-root convexity
of
\[
G:=\{(y,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_y)\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, its proof constructs the interpolating density and proves directly
that the convex combination of any two points of \(G\) is again in
\(G\); its closure is used only to adjoin zero-root boundary points.
For each \(z\in J_c\), (2) says that
\[
(z,q_z,b_z^{1/d}),
\quad
(w_z,q_{w_z},b_{w_z}^{1/d})
\in G.
\]
Their midpoint therefore belongs to \(G\):
\[
\left(
c,
\frac{q_z+q_{w_z}}2,
\frac{b_z^{1/d}+b_{w_z}^{1/d}}2
\right)\in G.
\tag{16}
\]
Taking the finite convex combination of (16) with weights
\(\lambda_z\) gives
\[
(c,q'_c,r_c)\in G,
\qquad
r_c:=\sum_{z\in J_c}\lambda_z
\frac{b_z^{1/d}+b_{w_z}^{1/d}}2.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular,
\[
q'_c\in\mathsf M_{r_c^d}(C_c).
\tag{18}
\]

We now compare this root mass with the required mass.  By the involutive
symmetry of \(J_c\), the two terms in the average defining \(r_c\)
have equal total sums.  Hence
\[
r_c
=\frac1{S_c}\sum_{z\in J_c}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}.
\tag{19}
\]
Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \((d+1)/d\) and \(d+1\)
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_c
&=\sum_{z\in J_c}
\bigl(b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\bigr)^{d/(d+1)}
b_{w_z}^{1/(d+1)}\\
&\leq
\left(\sum_{z\in J_c}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\right)^{d/(d+1)}
\left(\sum_{z\in J_c}b_{w_z}\right)^{1/(d+1)}\\
&\leq
\left(\sum_{z\in J_c}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\right)^{d/(d+1)}
M^{1/(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{z\in J_c}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}
\geq S_c^{1+1/d}M^{-1/d}.
\tag{21}
\]
Combining (19) and (21) yields
\[
r_c\geq\left(\frac{S_c}{M}\right)^{1/d},
\qquad
r_c^d\geq\frac{S_c}{M}=b'_c.
\tag{22}
\]

Choose a density \(g_c\) representing (18), so
\[
0\leq g_c\leq\mathbf1_{C_c},
\qquad
\int g_c=r_c^d,
\qquad
\frac1{r_c^d}\int xg_c(x)\,dx=q'_c.
\tag{23}
\]
Since \(0<b'_c\leq r_c^d\), the scaled density
\[
f_c:=\frac{b'_c}{r_c^d}g_c
\tag{24}
\]
satisfies \(0\leq f_c\leq\mathbf1_{C_c}\), has mass \(b'_c\),
and has the same normalized first moment \(q'_c\).  This proves (5).
When \(S_c=0\), one has \(b'_c=0\), no atom or metronoid assertion is
made, and every term involving that target has zero weight.  This treats
all zero-extended input and zero-target cases.

We next prove the exact mass and moment identities.  The correspondence
\[
(z,w)\in I\times I
\longleftrightarrow
\left(c=\frac{z+w}2,z\right)
\tag{25}
\]
is a bijection between ordered input pairs and nonzero summands in the
collection of the \(S_c\)'s.  Therefore
\[
\sum_c b'_c
=\frac1M\sum_{z,w\in I}b_zb_w
=\frac{M^2}{M}=M.
\tag{26}
\]
For the base first moment, (25) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_c b'_cc
&=\frac1{2M}\sum_{z,w\in I}b_zb_w(z+w)\\
&=\frac1{2M}
\left(
M\sum_{z\in I}b_zz+M\sum_{w\in I}b_ww
\right)
=\sum_{z\in I}b_zz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Similarly, substituting (4) and using (25),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{c:S_c>0}b'_cq'_c
&=\frac1{2M}\sum_{z,w\in I}b_zb_w(q_z+q_w)\\
&=\frac1{2M}
\left(
M\sum_{z\in I}b_zq_z+M\sum_{w\in I}b_wq_w
\right)
=\sum_{z\in I}b_zq_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Equations (26)-(28) prove (6) and (7), including the exact total mass
and both parts of the full first moment.

By (26), \(b'_c/M\) is a probability law.  Independence and (25) give,
for every \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\),
\[
\mathbb P\left(\frac{Z_1+Z_2}{2}=c\right)
=\frac1{M^2}\sum_zb_zb_{2c-z}
=\frac{S_c}{M^2}
=\frac{b'_c}{M}.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves the law identity in (8).  Since the laws have finite support,
all second moments exist.  Independence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(Z')
&=\operatorname{Cov}\left(\frac{Z_1+Z_2}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_1)+\operatorname{Cov}(Z_2)
\right)
=\frac12\operatorname{Cov}(Z),
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
because the two cross-covariance matrices vanish.  This completes (8).

We prove (11) with all ambient-halfspace cases explicit.  Let
\[
A_H:=\{c:S_c>0,\ (c,q'_c)\in H\}.
\tag{31}
\]
For each \(c\in A_H\), (5) gives
\(q'_c\in\mathsf M_{b'_c}(C_c)\) with \(b'_c>0\).  A representing
density has integral \(b'_c\), so \(|C_c|_d\geq b'_c>0\).  Since
\(C_c\) is a compact convex fiber, it is therefore a
\(d\)-dimensional convex body.

If \(H\) is a proper ambient halfspace, write
\[
H=\{(y,x):\langle u,y\rangle+\langle v,x\rangle\geq a\},
\qquad
(u,v)\neq(0,0).
\tag{32}
\]
If \(v\neq0\), then
\[
H_c=\{x:\langle v,x\rangle\geq a-\langle u,c\rangle\}
\tag{33}
\]
is a proper closed fiber halfspace containing \(q'_c\), even when the
atom lies on the boundary.  Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, including
its boundary case, therefore gives
\[
|C_c\cap H_c|_d\geq\gamma_db'_c.
\tag{34}
\]
If \(v=0\), then \(H_c\) is either empty or all of \(\mathbb R^d\).
It cannot be empty for \(c\in A_H\), so it is all of \(\mathbb R^d\),
and directly
\[
|C_c\cap H_c|_d=|C_c|_d\geq b'_c\geq\gamma_db'_c.
\tag{35}
\]
This is the vertical ambient-halfspace case.  If \(H\) is the whole
space, every \(H_c\) is all of \(\mathbb R^d\), so (35) applies to
every selected target.  If an empty degenerate halfspace is admitted,
then \(A_H=\varnothing\), \(\beta'(H)=0\), and (11) follows from
nonnegativity.

In every case, summing (34) or (35) over the selected, pairwise distinct
integer fibers gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_C(H)
&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H_z|_d\\
&\geq\sum_{c\in A_H}|C_c\cap H_c|_d\\
&\geq\gamma_d\sum_{c\in A_H}b'_c
=\gamma_d\,\beta'(H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
Thus (11) covers proper nonvertical halfspaces, vertical halfspaces,
atoms on boundaries, the whole space, and the optional empty degenerate
case.

It remains to verify the calibration.  In (12), the initial mass is
\(M=1\).  For the first repacking, the only nonzero ordered pairs are
\((0,0),(0,2),(2,0),(2,2)\).  Hence
\[
S_0=\frac14,
\qquad
S_1=\frac12,
\qquad
S_2=\frac14,
\tag{37}
\]
and therefore the first output masses at \(0,1,2\) are
\[
\frac14,\quad\frac12,\quad\frac14.
\tag{38}
\]
Every pair midpoint in the continuous coordinate is \(1/4\), so all
three output points have continuous coordinate \(1/4\).

For the second round, the even class consists of bases \(0,2\), each
with mass \(1/4\), and has total mass \(M_{\rm even}=1/2\).  Its
ordered-pair sums at targets \(0,1,2\) are \(1/16,1/8,1/16\), so its
output masses are
\[
\frac18,\quad\frac14,\quad\frac18.
\tag{39}
\]
In particular, it puts mass \(1/4\) at target \(1\).  The odd class is
the singleton at base \(1\) with mass \(1/2\).  Its total mass is
\(M_{\rm odd}=1/2\), its unique ordered-pair sum is \(1/4\), and it
therefore puts mass
\[
\frac{1/4}{1/2}=\frac12
\tag{40}
\]
at target \(1\).  Again every continuous coordinate remains \(1/4\).
An unscaled merge of these two target-\(1\) outputs would consequently
require metronoid mass
\[
\frac14+\frac12=\frac34
\tag{41}
\]
in \(C_1=[0,1/2]\).  But every measurable
\(0\leq f\leq\mathbf1_{[0,1/2]}\) satisfies
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}f\leq|[0,1/2]|_1=\frac12<\frac34.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus no density in the definition of
\(\mathsf M_{3/4}([0,1/2])\) exists, proving (13).  This is exactly the
capacity failure of the stated unscaled collision merge and proves no
broader impossibility claim.
