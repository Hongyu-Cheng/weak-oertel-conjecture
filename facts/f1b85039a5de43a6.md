## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(\mathcal J\) be any finite index set, including the empty set. For each \(i\in\mathcal J\), choose a support \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) of one of the following types:

- A standard lattice simplex
  \(A_i=D_{m_i,r_i}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{r_i}:\sum_{h=1}^{r_i}x_h\le m_i\}\), with arbitrary integers \(m_i,r_i\ge1\) and \(n_i=r_i\).
- A width-two cube pyramid
  \(A_i=D_{k_i}:=(\{0\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^{k_i})\cup\{(1,\mathbf0),(2,\mathbf0)\}\), with arbitrary integer \(k_i\ge1\), \(n_i=r_i=k_i+1\). Thus \(D_{k_i}=P_{k_i}\cap\mathbb Z^{k_i+1}\), where \(P_k=\operatorname{conv}((\{0\}\times[-1,1]^k)\cup\{(2,\mathbf0)\})\), exactly as in 0114546e99a0a84c and 304370ac28bef485.
- A finite interval \(A_i=I_{L_i}:=\{0,\ldots,L_i-1\}\), with arbitrary integer \(L_i\ge1\), \(n_i=1\), and \(r_i=\mathbf1_{\{L_i\ge2\}}\).
- A rank-zero singleton \(A_i=\{\mathbf0_{n_i}\}\), with arbitrary integer \(n_i\ge0\), and \(r_i=0\).

A rank-one degenerate pyramid \(D_0=\{0,1,2\}\) is allowed as the interval \(I_3\). A rank-zero simplex is a singleton and can be inserted as the fourth type. Let \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{complex}}\) denote the simplex and positive-\(k_i\) pyramid indices, and \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{box}}\) the interval indices. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}

&n_0&:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}n_i,
&R&:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J}r_i,\\

&Z_i&:=\sum_{\mathbf y\in A_i}e^{\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\mathbf y},
&P&:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J}p_i,
\end{aligned}
\]
where all tilts \(\boldsymbol\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}\) are arbitrary finite real vectors. The empty product is the probability-one point in \(\mathbb R^0\). The law \(P\) is the original normalized product law, not a capacity-reweighted root law. Its intrinsic affine dimension and its difference-lattice rank are both \(R\).

For a probability law \(\pi\) on a nonempty finite support \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}

&:=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^n}
 \sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in A\\
 \mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\ge0}}\pi_{\mathbf x},
&\mathcal M_q(\pi)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}\pi_{\mathbf z}\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^q
 \quad(q=2,3),\\

&\mathcal B_2(\pi)&:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}\pi_{\mathbf z}\mathsf C_\pi(\mathbf z)^2.
\end{aligned}

## Proof

We first fix the row and likelihood normalizations. For each simplex or pyramid factor and each root \(\mathbf j\in A_i\), let \(\nu_{i,\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) law on \(A_i\). This is exactly \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) of cfb2a3f2d408f191 for \(A_i=D_{m_i,r_i}\), and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) of 304370ac28bef485 for \(A_i=D_{k_i}\). It is also the row \(r_{\mathbf j}\) of 12850c4a90821cd2 on a pyramid. With \(A_i,n_i,p_i\) as above, it is precisely the relative-entropy minimizing row \(\nu_{i,\mathbf j}\) of c5abe3c72b8b89eb. Indeed, for every mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(\nu\), log-affinity gives
\[
D(\nu\|p_i)=-H(\nu)-\log p_{i,\mathbf j},
\qquad
\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j)
=e^{-D(\nu_{i,\mathbf j}\|p_i)}
=p_{i,\mathbf j}e^{H(\nu_{i,\mathbf j})}.
\]
The capacity identity, including boundary roots, is part of c5abe3c72b8b89eb. Define
\[
V_{i,\mathbf j}:=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}}
 \log\frac{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}(\mathbf X_i)}{p_{i,\mathbf X_i}},
\qquad
\omega_{i,\mathbf j}:=
 \frac{p_{i,\mathbf j}\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j)^2}{\mathcal B_2(p_i)}.
\]
These are exactly \(V_{\mathbf j},\omega_{\mathbf j}\) of 81ad175d3c410508 on a simplex and \(V_p(\mathbf j),\omega_p(\mathbf j)\) of 12850c4a90821cd2 on a pyramid. Thus they are also the quantities with the same block indices in c5abe3c72b8b89eb. The logarithm is evaluated only on the positive row support. Every capacity is at least the positive root atom, so \(\mathcal B_2(p_i)>0\) and \(\omega_i\) is a probability law.

Let \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}\) consist of every simplex index with \(m_i\ge2\) and every pyramid index. Define \(S_{\mathrm{rank}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}}r_i\). We certify all these simplices, including any for which \(|D_{m_i,r_i}|\le2^{r_i}\); no price is charged to an unmodeled block. The accepted inputs give, for each certified index,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}}
 [(\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j))^4]
 &\le\kappa\left(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{i,\mathbf j}}
               [\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf X_i]\right)^2
 &&(\mathbf j\in A_i,\ \mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^{n_i}),\\
\mathbb E_{\omega_i}V_{i,\mathbf j}&\le C_{\mathrm{price}}r_i,
&\mathcal B_2(p_i)&\ge g^{r_i}4^{-r_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For simplices these are respectively cfb2a3f2d408f191, 81ad175d3c410508, and 609407d6db81b18b, with the present \(r_i,m_i,\boldsymbol\ell_i\) equal to their rank, dilation, and source tilt. For pyramids they are respectively 304370ac28bef485, 12850c4a90821cd2, and 0114546e99a0a84c, with their \(k=k_i\), source tilt \((\tau,\boldsymbol\theta)=\boldsymbol\ell_i\), and rank \(k_i+1=r_i\). The inequality from 12850c4a90821cd2 is \(\mathbb E_{\omega_i}V\le K_{\mathrm{price}}k_i\le C_{\mathrm{pyramid}}r_i\); substituting its logarithmic constants gives exactly the stated \(C_{\mathrm{pyramid}}\). These are rank-linear prices and rank-exponential surpluses, not uniform per-block prices.

Suppose first \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}\ne\varnothing\), and let \(P_{\mathrm{cert}}:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}}p_i\). For a certified root tuple \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf j_i)_i\), put \(Q_{\mathbf j}:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}}\nu_{i,\mathbf j_i}\) and \(v(\mathbf j):=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}}V_{i,\mathbf j_i}\). These are the row product and summed likelihood variance in the pointwise change-of-measure proof of c5abe3c72b8b89eb. We use that argument with the price charged by rank, as follows.

For any block direction, set \(Y_i=\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)\), \(s_i^2=\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y_i^2\), and \(Y=\sum_iY_i\). Independence and centering imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y^4
&=\sum_i\mathbb E Y_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}s_i^2s_h^2\\
&\le\kappa\sum_i s_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}s_i^2s_h^2
\le\kappa\left(\sum_i s_i^2\right)^2
=\kappa\left(\mathbb E Y^2\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality uses \(\kappa\ge3\), so neither the number of factors nor their ranks enlarges the fourth-moment constant. All zero-variance directions are included.

Write \(\sigma^2=\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}Y^2\). If \(\sigma>0\), Hölder gives
\(\mathbb E Y^2\le(\mathbb E|Y|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E Y^4)^{1/3}\), hence \(\mathbb E|Y|\ge\sigma/\sqrt\kappa\). Since \(\mathbb E Y=0\), \(\mathbb E Y_+=\mathbb E|Y|/2\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives \((\mathbb E Y_+)^2\le\sigma^2 Q_{\mathbf j}(Y\ge0)\), proving \(Q_{\mathbf j}(Y\ge0)\ge1/(4\kappa)\). If \(\sigma=0\), this probability is one. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains its parallel translate through \(\mathbf j\), whose event is such a \(Y\ge0\). Consequently every containing halfspace has \(Q_{\mathbf j}\)-mass at least \(1/(4\kappa)\).

On the positive support of \(Q_{\mathbf j}\), let \(L_{\mathbf j}=\log(Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf X)/P_{\mathrm{cert}}(\mathbf X))\). The row identity and independence yield
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{\mathbf j}}L_{\mathbf j}
=-\sum_i\log\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i),
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{\mathbf j}}L_{\mathbf j}=v(\mathbf j).
\]
When \(v(\mathbf j)>0\), Chebyshev bounds the event
\(L_{\mathbf j}>\mathbb E L_{\mathbf j}+\sqrt{8\kappa v(\mathbf j)}\)
by \(1/(8\kappa)\). When \(v(\mathbf j)=0\), that event, with threshold zero, is empty. Intersect its complement with a containing halfspace. The intersection has \(Q_{\mathbf j}\)-mass at least \(\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}=1/(8\kappa)\), and on it
\[
P_{\mathrm{cert}}(\mathbf x)
=Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)e^{-L_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)}
\ge Q_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)
 e^{-\sqrt{8\kappa v(\mathbf j)}}
 \prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\]
Summing there and taking the halfspace infimum proves the pointwise actual-depth bound
\[
\tau_{P_{\mathrm{cert}}}(\mathbf j)
\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}
 e^{-\sqrt{8\kappa v(\mathbf j)}}
 \prod_i\mathsf C_{p_i}(\mathbf j_i).
\]
Mass outside the positive row support remains nonnegative and is simply not used.

Squaring and averaging with the original root probabilities \(P_{\mathrm{cert}}(\mathbf j)=\prod_i p_{i,\mathbf j_i}\) gives
\[
\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{cert}})
\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^2
 \left(\prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i)\right)
 \mathbb E_\Omega e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa}\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}},
\qquad
\Omega:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{cert}}}\omega_i.
\]
Here \(\Omega\) is only the algebraic reweighting of this squared-capacity sum. It does not replace the original law either in the moment or in the halfspace probabilities. The rank-linear price gives
\[
\mathbb E_\Omega v(\mathbf j)
=\sum_i\mathbb E_{\omega_i}V_{i,\mathbf j_i}
\le C_{\mathrm{price}}S_{\mathrm{rank}},
\qquad
\mathbb E_\Omega\sqrt{v(\mathbf j)}
\le\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{price}}S_{\mathrm{rank}}}.
\]
Jensen for the convex decreasing function \(z\mapsto e^{-2\sqrt{8\kappa}z}\) therefore gives a lower bound \(e^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}}\) for the last expectation. Capacity factorization in c5abe3c72b8b89eb is exact for these original laws, so
\(\mathcal B_2(P_{\mathrm{cert}})=\prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i)\ge g^{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}4^{-S_{\mathrm{rank}}}\).
In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{cert}})
&\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{complex}}^2
 \exp\!\left(S_{\mathrm{rank}}\log g
              -2\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}\right)
 4^{-S_{\mathrm{rank}}},\\
S_{\mathrm{rank}}\log g-2\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}
&=\log g\left(\sqrt{S_{\mathrm{rank}}}
                  -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}}{\log g}\right)^2
 -\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{complex}}^2}{\log g}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Completing this square once for the entire certified product proves
\(\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{cert}})\ge a_{\mathrm{complex}}^2 4^{-S_{\mathrm{rank}}}\).
There is no coefficient per certified block. If the certified group is empty, its product is a singleton, \(S_{\mathrm{rank}}=0\), and its moment is exactly one; the same uniform inequality holds since \(0<a_{\mathrm{complex}}<1\).

We next restore the simplex factors with \(m_i=1\), without a row certificate. Such a factor has \(r_i+1\) atoms, and \(r_i+1\le2^{r_i}\) for every integer \(r_i\ge1\). For any law \(p\) on \(M\) atoms, every halfspace containing its root contains the root atom, so \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)\ge p_{\mathbf j}\). Convexity of the cube under the uniform average of the \(M\) probabilities yields
\[
\mathcal M_2(p)\ge\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}^3
\ge M\left(\frac1M\right)^3=M^{-2}.
\]
Thus every \(m_i=1\) simplex satisfies \(\mathcal M_2(p_i)\ge4^{-r_i}\) with no prefactor loss. This uses neither a fourth-moment bound nor a price bound on its vertex rows.

The actual-depth supermultiplicativity in c5abe3c72b8b89eb applies to any finite laws, not just the certified factors. Explicitly, a halfspace through a product root \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) has an event
\(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf x)+\mathbf w\cdot(\mathbf Y-\mathbf y)\ge0\).
It contains the product event where both summands are nonnegative. Each event has probability at least the depth of its block, and a zero normal gives probability one. Independence, followed by the halfspace infimum and the original-law average, proves
\[
\tau_{p\otimes q}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
\ge\tau_p(\mathbf x)\tau_q(\mathbf y),
\qquad
\mathcal M_2(p\otimes q)\ge\mathcal M_2(p)\mathcal M_2(q).
\]
Iterating this with the atom bounds proves, for
\(P_{\mathrm{complex}}:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{complex}}}p_i\)
and \(R_{\mathrm{complex}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{complex}}}r_i\),
\(\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{complex}})\ge a_{\mathrm{complex}}^2 4^{-R_{\mathrm{complex}}}\).
If no complex factor is present, this group has moment exactly one. Rank-zero singleton factors likewise have depths, capacities, and moments exactly one, and cause no loss.

Consider the entire interval group
\(P_{\mathrm{box}}:=\bigotimes_{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{box}}}p_i\)
and \(R_{\mathrm{box}}:=\#\{i\in\mathcal J_{\mathrm{box}}:L_i\ge2\}\).
For a nonempty interval group, 8db3ba8c8761ea6c applies with its interval cardinalities \(m_i=L_i\) and scalar tilts \(\theta_i\) equal to the sole coordinates of \(\boldsymbol\ell_i\), giving
\(\mathcal M_2(P_{\mathrm{box}})\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^2 4^{-R_{\mathrm{box}}}\).
This is one coefficient for the whole interval product, uniformly over all lengths and tilts, not a coefficient for each interval. An empty interval group is the probability-one point, and obeys the same bound because \(0<a_{\mathrm{box}}<1\). Apply the preceding supermultiplicativity once to the complex group and the interval group, with all singleton factors harmless. Since \(R=R_{\mathrm{complex}}+R_{\mathrm{box}}\), this proves
\(\mathcal M_2(P)\ge(a_{\mathrm{complex}}a_{\mathrm{box}})^2 4^{-R}\).
Both factors of \(a\) are fixed positive numbers less than one, independent of all choices in the theorem.

Finally, Jensen for \(z\mapsto z^{3/2}\), still under the original root law \(P\), gives
\[
\mathcal M_3(P)
=\mathbb E_P[(\tau_P(\mathbf j)^2)^{3/2}]
\ge(\mathbb E_P\tau_P(\mathbf j)^2)^{3/2}
=\mathcal M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge a^3 8^{-R}.

Taking the indicated roots proves the normalized root-moment bounds. If all factors are singletons, or if the entire product is empty, there is just one root and its depth, capacity, and normalized moments are exactly one. This proves the stated sharper rank-zero conclusion rather than merely its uniform lower bound.

We verify cap normalization and affine transport. A halfspace \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge t\}\) containing \(\mathbf z\) has \(t\le\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\), so it contains the centered cap \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\ge0\). Conversely, every centered cap with a nonzero normal is a containing closed-halfspace trace. The zero normal gives the whole support, a realizable closed-halfspace trace on any finite support in positive ambient dimension, and cannot lower the infimum. On a singleton every containing halfspace has mass one after normalization. This identifies our centered definition with the actual depth used throughout c5abe3c72b8b89eb and 8db3ba8c8761ea6c.

Factorization of the exponential weights gives exactly \(T=b_{\mathrm{cap}}\prod_i Z_i\) and \(c_{\mathbf x}/T=P_{\mathbf x}\). Every unnormalized cap mass is \(T\) times its normalized mass, so its infimum satisfies \(h_c=T\tau_P\). There is one further factor of \(T\) from each root weight, whence
\[
\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)
=\sum_{\mathbf z}T P_{\mathbf z}(T\tau_P(\mathbf z))^3
=T^4\mathcal M_3(P)
\ge T^4a^3 8^{-R}.
\]
This is the same normalization as 8db3ba8c8761ea6c, with actual cap depths inside the cube.

Each simplex is complete in its coordinate lattice, being the integer points of \(\{\mathbf x\ge0:\sum_h x_h\le m_i\}\). Each pyramid is complete in its coordinate lattice by 304370ac28bef485, and each interval is the integer points of its closed real interval. The convex hull of a finite Cartesian product is the product of the convex hulls: one inclusion is immediate, and for the other, multiply the coordinate convex-combination coefficients to obtain a convex combination of product points. Consequently deleting all constant zero coordinates gives
\(D_{\mathrm{red}}=\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathrm{red}})\cap\mathbb Z^R\).
It contains \(\mathbf0\) and every standard unit vector in \(\mathbb Z^R\): a simplex contains them since \(m_i\ge1\); a pyramid contains the height unit vector \((1,\mathbf0)\) and all base unit vectors \((0,\mathbf e_h)\); and each retained interval contains zero and one. Thus its affine hull is \(\mathbb R^R\) and its difference lattice is exactly \(\mathbb Z^R\). These conclusions hold with the empty vectors when \(R=0\). The insertion chart \(\psi\) therefore shows that both intrinsic ranks of \(D\) are exactly \(R\), and that its intrinsic lattice is saturated in its original ambient coordinate space.

The chart \(\psi\) preserves probabilities and unnormalized weights because all removed coordinates are deterministic zero. Its pulled-back weights are \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell_{\mathrm{red}}\cdot\mathbf y}\); the omitted partition-function factors are exactly one. Restricting and extending affine inequalities by deleting or inserting zero normal components identifies all its cap traces. Thus it preserves actual depths and their moments.

For the general chart \(\Phi\), injectivity gives \(\operatorname{aff}(A')=\mathbf v+B\mathbb R^R\), and the zero and unit-vector points just proved give
\(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(A'-A')=B\mathbb Z^R\).
Saturation and the integral translation imply
\(\operatorname{aff}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathbf v+B\mathbb Z^R\).
If \(\mathbf z\in\operatorname{conv}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^n\), then \(\mathbf z=\mathbf v+B\mathbf y\) for some \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathrm{red}})\). Saturation also writes it as \(\mathbf v+B\mathbf h\) with \(\mathbf h\in\mathbb Z^R\). Injectivity forces \(\mathbf y=\mathbf h\), and completeness of \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) forces \(\mathbf y\in D_{\mathrm{red}}\). Hence \(\mathbf z\in A'\), proving ambient integer completeness; the reverse inclusion is immediate.

An ambient affine inequality \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge t\) pulls back to
