## Statement

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite set and let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $z\in P$, be compact convex fibers with $d\ge1$, compatible under all anchored representations on $P$, and with volumes in $[v,V]$ for some $0<v\le V$. Choose one representation at every nonvertex using all other sites of its minimal face with strictly positive coefficients. For a nonsingleton dependence component $P_i$, let $\lambda_*$ be the smallest coefficient among its chosen representations. After one common continuous affine map with linear determinant one, all fibers in $P_i$ have diameter at most
$$
2\sqrt d\,(d!V)^{1/d}
\left[\frac{2}{\lambda_*}\left(1+\frac{2dV}{\lambda_*^d v}\right)\right]^{|P_i|-1}.
$$
The anchor bound $2\sqrt d\,(d!V)^{1/d}$ suffices on a singleton component. Also, in any sequence of positive compatible families on fixed $P$ with convergent normalized fiber volumes, the set of sites with positive limiting normalized volume is closed in $P$.

## Proof

For the anchor normalization, choose a maximum-volume inscribed $d$-simplex in an anchor of volume $a$. A map sending it to the standard simplex has absolute linear determinant $A$ with $Aa\ge1/d!$. The image of the anchor lies in $[-1,1]^d$: replacing vertex $e_j$ by any image point multiplies the absolute simplex determinant by the absolute value of that point's $j$-th coordinate, which is therefore at most one. Compose with dilation by $A^{-1/d}$. Orienting the simplex ordering gives determinant one, and the resulting radius about the image of the first vertex is at most $\sqrt d(d!a)^{1/d}$.

For the propagation estimate, suppose $B\subset L$ are compact convex sets, $B$ is contained in a ball of radius $R>0$ centered at the origin, $|B|_d\ge b>0$ and $|L|_d\le V$. If $x\in L$ has norm $T>R$, sections of $B$ normal to $x$ have essential supremum of their $(d-1)$-area at least $b/(2R)$ by Fubini. The convex hull of any such section with $x$ lies in $L$ and has volume its section area times its height divided by $d$, by integrating homothetic sections. Its height is at least $T-R$. Taking section areas arbitrarily close to that essential supremum yields $V\ge (T-R)b/(2dR)$. Therefore $L$ is contained in the ball of radius $R(1+2dV/b)$. The argument includes $d=1$, with a nonempty point section of measure one.

For a chosen relation with target $z$, fix points in all sources other than a source $u$ whose diameter is bounded by $D$. The target contains a translate $B$ of $\lambda_u K_u$ by compatibility. About any point of $B$ it has radius at most $\lambda_u D$ and volume at least $\lambda_*^d v$. The preceding estimate bounds the target diameter by $2D(1+2dV/(\lambda_*^d v))$. Conversely, a target diameter bound gives a bound on each source by division by its coefficient, since each scaled source has a translate in the target. This also applies if the target is the first bounded node in the hyperedge. Thus each hyperedge expands the bounded sites at a cost of at most the multiplicative factor in the statement. The component characterization gives a sequence of at most $|P_i|-1$ hyperedge expansions, each adding at least one site. Applying the anchor normalization first and propagating gives the claim.

For the last assertion, suppose a target in $P$ is a convex combination of the positive-limit sites and one of its sources is $u$ with coefficient $\lambda>0$. The same translate inclusion gives $|K_z|_d\ge\lambda^d |K_u|_d$ for every family in the sequence. Divide by that family's total mass and pass to the limit. The target then has positive limiting normalized volume, proving closedness.
