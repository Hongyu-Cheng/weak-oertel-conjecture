## Statement

For $p\ge0$, $\ell>0$, and $0<k\le1$, set
$$
S(p,\ell)=\sum_{h\ge0}(p-\ell h)_+^2,\qquad
f_k(p,\ell)=(1-k)\left(\frac{p^3}{3\ell}+\frac{p^2}{2}\right)+kp^2,
$$
where the sum is over integers. For $1/3\le b\le1/2$ and $0\le z\le b$, put $P=1-b$. Then
$$
\frac{f_k(P,k(1-z))+f_k(P-kz,k(1+z))}{2-3b}
>\frac49\left(\frac1{3k}+\frac12+\frac{k}{5}\right).
$$
In particular, on the actual maximal paired affine triangle chain with radii $r_h=(1-kh)_+$ at bases $-h,h+1$ for all positive radii, let $a\in[2/3,1]$ and $0\le\beta<k/3$. The cap with margin $-\beta z_{\mathrm{base}}+aX_1+(1-a)X_2-1/3$ through $(0,1/3,1/3)$ has area divided by $|T|$ exactly
$$
\frac{S(P,k(1-z))+S(P-kz,k(1+z))}{2-3b}>\frac49 S(1,k),
\qquad b=\frac1{3a},\quad z=\frac{\beta}{ak},
$$
where $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$.

## Proof

Write $Q=P-kz$, so $P,Q\ge0$, and both hinge steps are positive. Multiply the difference in the first inequality by the positive factor $270k(2-3b)(1-z^2)$. Direct multiplication gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
N={}&90(1-k)\big[P^3(1+z)+Q^3(1-z)\big]\\
&+135k(1+k)(1-z^2)(P^2+Q^2)\\
&-(40+60k+24k^2)(2-3b)(1-z^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Substitute $b=1/3+x/6$ and $z=by$ with $x,y\in[0,1]$. Cover the $k$ range by $k=k_0+w/2$ with $k_0=0,1/2$ and $w\in[0,1]$. Each mapped numerator is a polynomial of degree at most four in each variable. If its rational power coefficients are $A_{pqr}$, the tensor Bernstein coefficients of coordinate degrees $(4,4,4)$ are
$$
c_{ij\ell}=\sum_{p\le i,\ q\le j,\ r\le\ell}A_{pqr}
\frac{\binom{i}{p}\binom{j}{q}\binom{\ell}{r}}
{\binom4p\binom4q\binom4r}.
$$
Expanding the displayed $N$ and taking this sum for $0\le i,j,\ell\le4$ gives the exact minima $5/2$ for $k_0=0$ and $331/384$ for $k_0=1/2$. The full arrays are in the PK computation half-integer-affine-high-sector-bernstein.json. Equivalently, to reproduce the minima, start with the power coefficients of each mapped $N$, apply the weighted sum $\sum_{j\le i}\binom{i}{j}A_j/\binom4j$ in each coordinate while keeping the other two indices fixed, and take the minimum of the resulting 125 coefficients. The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one, so $N$ is positive on both boxes. Division by the stated factor for $k>0$ proves the scalar inequality.

For the cap identity the two thresholds at positive radius $r_h=1-kh$ are $1/3-\beta h$ and $1/3+\beta(h+1)$. Both are at least $r_h/3\ge (1-a)r_h$, so both use the upper proper tail or the empty tail in b026600b8d8e369b. The tail divided by $|T|$ is
$$
\frac{(r_h-s/a)_+^2}{2-3b}.
$$
This formula includes $a=1$ by the direct coordinate cap. Substitution gives the first affine hinge $P-k(1-z)h$ and the second $P-kz-k(1+z)h$. At $h=1/k$ the first is $z-b<0$ and the second is $-b-z-kz<0$. They decrease with $h$, so the arithmetic sums beyond the positive-radius leg add no area.

The first summand gives $S(p,\ell)\ge p^2$. Convexity of $(p-\ell h)_+^2$ and the trapezoid rule on every unit interval give $S(p,\ell)\ge p^3/(3\ell)+p^2/2$. Thus $S(p,\ell)\ge f_k(p,\ell)$. Fact 092da3dd16fe42c7 with $p=1,\ell=k$ gives $S(1,k)\le1/(3k)+1/2+3k/16\le1/(3k)+1/2+k/5$. The scalar inequality therefore proves the stated strict cap comparison.
