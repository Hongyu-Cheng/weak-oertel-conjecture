## Statement

Let \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), set \(n=2\), \(d=N\), and let \(e_1,\ldots,e_N\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^N\). For \(1\leq j\leq N\), put
\[
 K_j:=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:|x_j|\leq1,
 \ |x_k|\leq1/2\ (k\ne j)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), define the displayed base endpoints
\[
 z_{j,1}^{\epsilon}:=(-1,6j+\epsilon),\qquad
 z_{j,2}^{\epsilon}:=(0,6j+2\epsilon),\qquad
 z_{j,3}^{\epsilon}:=(1,6j+2\epsilon),
\tag{2}
\]
and continuous translations
\[
 a_{j,1}^{\epsilon}=a_{j,3}^{\epsilon}:=0,
 \qquad a_{j,2}^{\epsilon}:=2e_j.
\tag{3}
\]
Consider the one common convex body
\[
 C_N:=\operatorname{conv}\!\bigcup_{\substack{1\leq j\leq N\\
 i\in\{1,2,3\},\ \epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}}
 \bigl(\{z_{j,i}^{\epsilon}\}\times(a_{j,i}^{\epsilon}+K_j)\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^N.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a convex body. Write \(P_N\) for its base projection and, for \(z\in P_N\), write
\[
 (C_N)_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:(z,x)\in C_N\}.
\]

Every actual fiber, including every displayed endpoint fiber and every intermediate fiber created by (4), has the following exact rational-polytopal description. Let \(\lambda=(\lambda_{j,i,\epsilon})\) range over
\[
 \lambda_{j,i,\epsilon}\geq0,\qquad
 \sum_{j,i,\epsilon}\lambda_{j,i,\epsilon}=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j,i,\epsilon}\lambda_{j,i,\epsilon}z_{j,i}^{\epsilon}=z,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
 \alpha_j:=\sum_{i,\epsilon}\lambda_{j,i,\epsilon},
 \qquad
 \beta_j:=\sum_{\epsilon}\lambda_{j,2,\epsilon}.
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 (C_N)_z=
 \left\{2\sum_{j=1}^N\beta_je_j+y:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \lambda\text{ satisfies }(5),\\
 |y_k|\leq(1+\alpha_k)/2\quad(1\leq k\leq N)
 \end{array}\right\}.}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, the base polygon has the six exposed vertices
\[
 (0,4),(1,4),(1,6N+2),(0,6N+2),(-1,6N+1),(-1,5),
\tag{8}
\]
and the actual fibers there are, in the same order,
\[
 2e_1+K_1,\ K_1,\ K_N,\ 2e_N+K_N,\ K_N,\ K_1.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(Z_j:=\mathsf M_1(K_j)\), then the actual half-metronoids at these exposed fibers are the corresponding translates of \(Z_1\) or \(Z_N\). More precisely,
\[
 h_{Z_j}(u)=\frac12\int_{K_j}|\langle u,x\rangle|\,dx,
 \qquad
 h_{Z_j}(e_j)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 h_{Z_j}(e_k)=\frac14\quad(k\ne j).
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)\) be the intrinsic residual-selector capacity in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)\geq
 \begin{cases}
 4,&N=1,\\
 4N-4,&N\geq2,
 \end{cases}}
\tag{11}
\]
and hence \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)\geq N/4\) for every \(N\geq1\). Consequently the second alternative proposed in the subgoal, which requires \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)<N/4\), is impossible for this common body, independently of which load-minimal affine-defect stress is chosen.

## Proof

The finite union in (4) is compact. Its base points span \(\mathbb R^2\), and every attached \(K_j\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^N\), so its convex hull has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{N+2}\). Thus \(C_N\) is a convex body.

We first prove the exact actual-fiber formula (7). A point of the fiber over \(z\) is a convex combination with coefficients satisfying (5). The translation part of that combination is exactly \(2\sum_j\beta_je_j\). For fixed coefficients, the centered part is
\[
 \sum_{j,i,\epsilon}\lambda_{j,i,\epsilon}K_j
 =\sum_{j=1}^N\alpha_jK_j.
\tag{12}
\]
The right side is an axis-parallel box. Its half-width in coordinate \(k\) equals
\[
 \alpha_k+\frac12\sum_{j\ne k}\alpha_j
 =\frac{1+\alpha_k}{2},
\tag{13}
\]
because \(\sum_j\alpha_j=1\). This proves that the left side of (7) is contained in the right side. Conversely, every \(y\) satisfying the coordinate inequalities in (7) lies in the Minkowski sum (12). Choosing one point of each \(K_j\) which realizes that Minkowski representation, and using that same point in every positive-weight copy with block label \(j\), realizes \(2\sum_j\beta_je_j+y\) as a convex combination in (4). This proves the reverse containment and hence (7). Notice that (7) audits interpolation at the level of the single common convex hull; it does not identify a displayed attached box with the actual fiber unless the barycentric constraints force that conclusion.

The base projection is the convex hull of the points (2). At \(x=-1,0,1\), their extreme vertical coordinates are respectively
\[
 [5,6N+1],\qquad[4,6N+2],\qquad[4,6N+2].
\]
Taking the convex hull gives exactly the hexagon with vertices (8). At each vertex the barycentric representation by the displayed base points is unique, and (7) therefore gives (9).

For completeness, we compute the half-metronoids in (10). Each \(K_j\) is centrally symmetric and has volume \(2\). In maximizing the support functional in direction \(u\ne0\) over selectors of mass \(1\), the maximizing selector is the indicator of the half of \(K_j\) on which \(\langle u,x\rangle\geq0\). Symmetry and the fact that the cutting hyperplane has zero \(N\)-volume give
\[
 h_{\mathsf M_1(K_j)}(u)
 =\int_{K_j\cap\{\langle u,x\rangle\geq0\}}
 \langle u,x\rangle\,dx
 =\frac12\int_{K_j}|\langle u,x\rangle|\,dx.
\]
Taking \(u=e_k\) and integrating one coordinate of the box gives \(1/2\) when \(k=j\) and \(1/4\) otherwise. Translation covariance of the metronoid gives the assertion following (9).

It remains to construct one simultaneous legal selector. Assume first that \(N\geq2\). For \(1\leq j\leq N-1\), put
\[
 u_j:=z_{j,2}^{+}=(0,6j+2).
\]
The following three outer, hence untranslated, displayed copies have the indicated barycentric combination:
\[
 u_j=\frac12z_{j,1}^{+}
      +\frac14z_{j,3}^{+}
      +\frac14z_{j+1,3}^{-}.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore (7), or directly (4), gives the centrally symmetric subbox
\[
 B_j^{+}:=\frac34K_j+\frac14K_{j+1}
 \subseteq(C_N)_{u_j}.
\tag{15}
\]
Its half-widths in coordinates \(j,j+1\) are \(7/8,5/8\), and all other half-widths are \(1/2\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_N(B_j^{+})=\frac74\frac54=\frac{35}{16}>2.
\tag{16}
\]

For \(2\leq j\leq N\), put
\[
 \ell_j:=z_{j,2}^{-}=(0,6j-2).
\]
Again using only untranslated outer copies,
\[
 \ell_j=\frac12z_{j,3}^{-}
       +\frac18z_{j-1,1}^{+}
       +\frac38z_{j,1}^{-},
\tag{17}
\]
so
\[
 B_j^{-}:=\frac78K_j+\frac18K_{j-1}
 \subseteq(C_N)_{\ell_j}.
\tag{18}
\]
Its two exceptional half-widths are \(15/16\) in coordinate \(j\) and \(9/16\) in coordinate \(j-1\). Thus
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_N(B_j^{-})=\frac{15}{8}\frac98
 =\frac{135}{64}>2.
\tag{19}
\]

Every \(B_j^{\pm}\) is centrally symmetric about \(0\). If a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^N\) contains \(0\), it contains a parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through \(0\), and that latter halfspace contains exactly half the volume of a centrally symmetric body. From (15)--(19), in the absolute fiber-depth notation of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483,
\[
 \delta_{C_N}(u_j,0)\geq\frac{35}{32}>1,
 \qquad
 \delta_{C_N}(\ell_j,0)\geq\frac{135}{128}>1.
\tag{20}
\]

Let
\[
 E:=\{u_j:1\leq j\leq N-1\}
 \cup\{\ell_j:2\leq j\leq N\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The \(2N-2\) points in (21) are distinct and lie on the line \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R\), so \(\rho_E=1\). In the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)\) from accepted fact be359f8a6c628483, take \(q_z=0\), \(w_z=1\) for every \(z\in E\), and the affine map \(s\equiv0\) on \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\). Equation (20) proves legality, and the residual rank is zero. Therefore
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_N)
 \geq2^{2-1}\frac{2N-2}{0+1}=4N-4.
\tag{22}
\]

If \(N=1\), use the singleton \(E=\{z_{1,1}^{-}\}=\{(-1,5)\}\). Its actual fiber contains the centrally symmetric interval \(K_1=[-1,1]\), so \(\delta_{C_1}(z_{1,1}^{-},0)\geq1\). Taking \(q_z=0\), \(w_z=1\), and \(s\equiv0\) gives \(\rho_E=r_s=0\), and the same accepted definition yields
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_1)\geq2^2=4.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (22)--(23) prove (11). For \(N\geq2\), \(4N-4\geq N/4\), and the \(N=1\) case is immediate as well. Thus convex interpolation forces the selector alternative for the proposed common body and rules out the claimed surviving-stress counterexample.
