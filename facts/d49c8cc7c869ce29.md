## Statement

Let \(f:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)\) be integrable and log-concave, with \(0\) in the interior of its positive interval and \(\int_{\mathbb R}x f(x)\,dx=0\). Put \(c=f(0)>0\), \(S=\int_{\mathbb R} f\), \(I=\int_0^\infty x f(x)\,dx>0\), and \(L=2\sqrt{2I}\). Then
$$
\sqrt{c/2}\ \le\ S/L\ \le\ \sqrt c.
$$
If \(f^{1/m}\) is concave on the positive interval for an integer \(m\ge1\), the lower bound improves to \(\sqrt{c(m+2)/(2(m+1))}\). The upper bound is attained by a uniform interval centered at \(0\), the general lower bound by \(f(x)=c e^{-|x|/a}\), and the power-concave lower bound by \(f(x)=c(1-|x|/a)_+^m\), with \(a>0\). A constant density on an interval with centroid \(0\) has \(S/L=\sqrt c\).

## Proof

The first moments on both rays are finite. Indeed, on an unbounded positive ray integrability gives a point \(b>0\) with \(f(b)<f(0)/2\). Concavity of \(\log f\) bounds the subsequent tail by \(f(0)e^{-x\log(2)/b}\). On a bounded ray integrability suffices. The two first moments are equal by centering. Divide \(f\) by \(c\), so that the ratio \(S/L\) is divided by \(\sqrt c\). We prove the bounds for \(c=1\).

**Entropy sublevel width.** For \(t>0\) let \(F(t)=t\log t-t+1\) and \(G(t)=(t+1)/2-\int_0^1 t^s\,ds\). Both functions and their first derivatives vanish at \(1\), and
$$
\frac{G''(t)}{F''(t)}=\int_0^1 s(1-s)t^{s-1}\,ds
$$
is decreasing. Consequently \(G/F\) is decreasing. For \(t>1\) this ratio is the average of \(G''/F''\) on \((1,t)\) with weights \((t-x)F''(x)\). Increasing \(t\) multiplies weights on the common interval by an increasing function of \(x\) and adds a right interval. For \(t<1\) the weights are \((x-t)F''(x)\) on \((t,1)\). Increasing \(t\) removes a left interval and again multiplies weights on the common interval by an increasing function. Both changes can only reduce the mean of a decreasing function, by the nonpositive covariance of an increasing and a decreasing function under a common probability measure. At \(1\) the two limits equal \(G''(1)/F''(1)\).

For \(0<u<1\), let \(a<1<b\) be the positive solutions of \(F(a)=F(b)=u\) and put \(w=b-a\), \(A=-\log a\), \(B=\log b\). Differentiation gives \(w'=1/A+1/B\). The equations for \(F\) imply
$$
u w'=w-\frac{b-1}{B}+\frac{1-a}{A}.
$$
Since \(G(b)\le G(a)\) and \(\int_0^1 t^s\,ds=(t-1)/\log t\), we have \((b-1)/B-(1-a)/A\ge w/2\). Thus \(w/\sqrt u\) is nonincreasing. The Taylor expansion \(F(1+t)=t^2/2+O(t^3)\) gives its limit \(2\sqrt2\) at zero, so \(w^2\le8u\). This also holds at \(u=1\) by continuity with \(a=0,b=e\).

**Upper bound.** A finite supergradient \(s\) of \(\log f\) at \(0\) gives \(f(x)\le e^{sx}\) on its positive interval, and the bound holds off the interval as well. Reflect if needed to make \(s\ge0\). A density on the positive ray under a given envelope with first moment \(I\) has largest possible mass by filling the envelope from zero to a cutoff having that first moment. To see this, subtract the filled envelope from any competitor. The difference \(h\) is nonpositive below the cutoff \(T\) and nonnegative above it, so \(0=\int_0^\infty xh(x)\,dx\ge T\int_0^\infty h(x)\,dx\). If \(T=\infty\) the envelope bounds the total mass directly. For \(s=0\) the envelope on each ray is \(1\), and each mass is at most \(\sqrt{2I}\).

If \(s>0\), put \(u=s^2 I\in(0,1]\), since the envelope \(e^{-sx}\) on the reflected negative ray has first moment \(1/s^2\). Let the cutoff lengths on the negative and positive rays be \(A/s\) and \(B/s\), respectively. They solve
$$
u=1-(A+1)e^{-A}=1+(B-1)e^B
$$
with \(A=\infty\) allowed when \(u=1\). Summing the filled masses gives \(sS\le e^B-e^{-A}\). The values \(e^{-A}\) and \(e^B\) are the two entropy endpoints at level \(u\), so \(sS\le\sqrt{8u}\). This gives \(S\le L\).

**Lower bounds.** On one ray compare \(f\) to \(g(x)=(1-x/a)_+^m\), with \(a>0\) chosen so \(I=a^2/[(m+1)(m+2)]\). If the positive interval of \(f\) extended past \(a\), concavity of \(f^{1/m}\) and \(f^{1/m}(a)>0\) would give \(f(x)\ge g(x)\) up to \(a\) and positive mass beyond \(a\), contradicting equal first moments. Up to the end of that interval the difference \(f^{1/m}(x)-(1-x/a)\) is concave and vanishes at zero, so its quotient by \(x>0\) is nonincreasing. Extending \(f\) by zero, \(f-g\) can change sign only from positive to negative. If both signs occur, use a crossing point \(T>0\). Then
$$
0=\int_0^\infty x(f-g)(x)\,dx\le T\int_0^\infty (f-g)(x)\,dx.
$$
If only one sign occurs, equal moments force equality almost everywhere. This bounds the ray mass below by \(\int_0^\infty g=a/(m+1)=\sqrt{I(m+2)/(m+1)}\). For general log-concave \(f\), take \(g(x)=e^{-x/a}\) with \(a=\sqrt I\). The concave difference \(\log f(x)+x/a\) has the same one-crossing property before the end of the positive interval of \(f\), and \(f-g<0\) beyond that end. The same moment comparison bounds the ray mass below by \(\sqrt I\).

Apply the lower comparisons on both rays, divide by \(L=2\sqrt{2I}\), and restore \(c\). The displayed sharp examples give equalities by direct integration. For a planar density whose restriction to a line through \(0\) meets the hypotheses, the two ray radii of the planar radial-integral body are \(\sqrt{2I}\) in each direction. Its chord is centered and has length \(L\). This is the comparison needed for that individual section.
