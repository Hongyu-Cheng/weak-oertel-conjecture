## Statement

Let $A,B$ be compact sets of finite positive Lebesgue measures $a,b$, let $G\subseteq A\times B$ be closed, and assume $|G_y|\ge c>0$ for every $y\in B$, where $G_y=\{x\in A:(x,y)\in G\}$. Among finite nonnegative measures supported on $G$ with marginals dominated by the restrictions of Lebesgue measure to $A$ and $B$, the maximum mass is attained and is at least $\min(b,c)$. If $a\ge b$, every feasible matching can be extended to a coupling on $A\times B$ with second marginal exactly $\mathbf1_B\,dy$ and first marginal dominated by $\mathbf1_A\,dx$.

## Proof

Set $m=\min(b,c)$ and take the product-Lebesgue density $\mathbf1_G(x,y)m/(b|G_y|)$ on $A\times B$. Measurability of $y\mapsto |G_y|$ follows from Fubini's theorem. Its second marginal has density $m/b\le1$ on $B$ and total mass $m$. Its first marginal has density at most $\int_B m/(bc)\,dy=m/c\le1$. This proves the lower bound directly.

The feasible measures have uniformly bounded mass on a compact product, hence are relatively weakly compact. They remain supported on the closed set $G$ in weak limits, and each marginal bound passes to weak limits by testing against every nonnegative continuous function on the relevant compact set. Thus the feasible set is weakly compact. The mass is the integral of the continuous function $1$, so it attains its maximum.

For any feasible matching of mass $g$ with $a\ge b$, subtract the used marginals from the original measures to get residual capacities $\alpha,\beta$ of masses $a-g,b-g$. If $g=b$ there is nothing to add. Otherwise $\alpha\otimes\beta/(a-g)$ has right marginal $\beta$ and left marginal $[(b-g)/(a-g)]\alpha\le\alpha$. Add this measure to the matching. It need not be supported on $G$.
