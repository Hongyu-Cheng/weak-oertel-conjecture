## Proof

We first verify the loop/cross split and the base geometry. By (11), every label belongs to exactly one of the two classes, so (12) and accepted fact `2fe46f47d19112ea` give the equality and strict lower bound in (13). The maximum of two nonnegative numbers is at least half their sum, proving the dichotomy.

If \(\sigma\) is a loop, then \(a(\sigma)\) and \(b(\sigma)\) are distinct endpoints lying in one same-parity edge of the endpoint-disjoint matching. Hence their unordered pair is that edge, and its midpoint \(m_E\) is integral. If \(\sigma\) is a cross label, the two missed edges are distinct. Endpoint-disjointness then shows that
\[
 a,
 \ a^\star,
 \ b,
 \ b^\star
\tag{40}
\]
are four distinct bases. The original atlas construction in accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66` gives \(a\equiv b\pmod2\), and accepted fact `3016926516cc39df` gives \(a^\star\equiv a\pmod2\) and \(b^\star\equiv b\pmod2\). All four bases in (40) therefore have the same parity. This proves the integrality in (28) and also proves that \(u_\sigma,v_\sigma\) are nonzero integral vectors. Saturation and the interpretation of these vectors as the two missed-edge directions follow from accepted fact `bf0d5c250b8f688c`.

We next construct the partner witnesses and check the simultaneous source budget. Equations (7) and (18) give
\[
 R_y\leq t_{e_2(y)}.
\tag{41}
\]
Accepted fact `3016926516cc39df` gives
\(
 t_{e_2(y)}\leq a_y/4
\)
at either endpoint. Equations (8), (20), and (41) now give
\[
 B_y\geq0,
 \qquad
 b_y=\frac{a_y}{2}-R_y
 \geq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \geq t_{e_2(y)}.
\tag{42}
\]
The partner \(y^\star\) lies in the same matching edge, so (7) at \(y^\star\) gives
\[
 R_{y^\star}^{\mathrm{cross}}
 \leq R_{y^\star}
 \leq t_{e_2(y^\star)}
 =t_{e_2(y)}.
\tag{43}
\]
This proves (21). The scalar in (22) lies in \([0,1]\), so
\[
 0\leq h_y\leq B_y,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_y}h_y=R_{y^\star}^{\mathrm{cross}}.
\tag{44}
\]
For every cross label incident to \(y\), equations (22) and (24) give
\[
 \int_{C_{y^\star}}k_{\sigma,y^\star}
 =\frac{\eta_\sigma}{R_y^{\mathrm{cross}}}
   R_y^{\mathrm{cross}}
 =\eta_\sigma,
\tag{45}
\]
and normalization of the first moment gives the barycenter \(q_{y^\star}^\star\). Summing (24) over all cross labels incident to \(y^\star\), while using \((y^\star)^\star=y\), gives exactly \(h_y\). Therefore (20), (22), and (44) prove (25). This construction uses the unused part of the pointwise half-density budget. It does not assume that the canonical pooled point at the partner can be reused within that budget.

We prove the loop conclusions. For \(y\in E\), the density \(G_{E,y}\) is a sub-sum of \(F_y\), so it is bounded by \(1/2\). Its mass is \(L_E\), because every loop label on \(E\) occurs once at each of its two endpoints. Its barycenter is \(\bar x_{E,y}\). Consequently
\[
 (u,\bar x_{E,u},L_E^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (w,\bar x_{E,w},L_E^{1/d})
\tag{46}
\]
belong to the positive-root metronoid set in accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. Convexity of that positive-root set, applied at the midpoint of (46), proves
\[
 Y_E\in\mathsf M_{L_E}(C_{m_E}).
\tag{47}
\]
The metronoid-depth conclusion of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives the second assertion in (17). Finally,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y_E
 &=\frac1{2L_E}
   \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr L_E}
   \eta_\sigma(x_{\sigma,a}+x_{\sigma,b})\\
 &=\frac1{L_E}
   \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr L_E}\eta_\sigma X_\sigma,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
where the endpoint order of a label is immaterial. This proves the whole-label barycenter identity and all claims in (17).

Fix a cross label. Equations (6), (24), (27), and (45) provide, for each \(s,t\in\{0,1\}\), two admissible source densities of the same positive mass \(\eta_\sigma\), supported respectively in \(C_{a_s}\) and \(C_{b_t}\), with barycenters \(A_{\sigma,s}\) and \(B_{\sigma,t}\). Thus
\[
 (a_s,A_{\sigma,s},\eta_\sigma^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (b_t,B_{\sigma,t},\eta_\sigma^{1/d})
\tag{49}
\]
belong to the positive-root set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. Midpoint convexity applied to (49) proves the metronoid membership in (29). In particular, the target fiber has positive \(d\)-volume. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applies with selected mass \(\eta_\sigma\) and proves the depth inequality in (29). Definitions (5), (9), and (26)-(28) give (30). Direct expansion gives (33)-(34).

We now prove the simultaneous domination claim. Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). If a loop atom \((m_E,Y_E)\) belongs to \(K\), then at least one of
\[
 (u,\bar x_{E,u}),
 \qquad
 (w,\bar x_{E,w})
\tag{50}
\]
belongs to \(K\), because their midpoint is the loop atom. Orient the loop occurrence toward one such endpoint. If a cross corner \((m_{\sigma,st},Z_{\sigma,st})\) belongs to \(K\), then at least one of
\[
 (a_s,A_{\sigma,s}),
 \qquad
 (b_t,B_{\sigma,t})
\tag{51}
\]
belongs to \(K\). Orient that corner toward one such source occurrence.

For each physical source base \(y\), form a density \(\Psi_y\) on \(C_y\) by adding \(2G_{E,y}\) if the unique loop edge incident to \(y\) was oriented toward \(y\), and by adding the source density of every cross corner oriented toward \(y\), with multiplicity. Each original or partner source occurrence of one cross label is used by exactly two of its four corners. Therefore the pointwise ledger (25) gives
\[
 0\leq\Psi_y
 \leq
 2\left(
  F_y+
  \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr A_{\mathrm{cross}}\\
  y^\star\in\{a(\sigma),b(\sigma)\}}}
  k_{\sigma,y}
  \right)
 \leq1.
\tag{52}
\]
Here loop and cross original densities are disjoint sub-sums of \(F_y\), and endpoint-disjointness gives at most one loop aggregate at \(y\).

Let \(T_y:=\int_{C_y}\Psi_y\). If \(T_y>0\), every barycenter entering \(\Psi_y\) lies in the fiber section
\[
 K_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in K\}.
\tag{53}
\]
Convexity of \(K_y\) shows that the barycenter of \(\Psi_y\) lies in \(K_y\). Equation (52) makes \(\Psi_y\) an admissible selected-mass density of mass \(T_y\). If \(K_y\) is a proper closed halfspace, accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap K_y)\geq\gamma_dT_y.
\tag{54}
\]
If \(K_y=\mathbb R^d\), the same bound follows from \(T_y\leq a_y\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\). An empty section has no oriented source.

The contribution \(\gamma_dT_y\) counts \(2\gamma_dL_E\) for each loop atom oriented to \(y\), and \(\gamma_d\eta_\sigma\) for each cross corner oriented to \(y\). Summing (54) over the distinct physical source fibers therefore gives
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{align}}(K)
 \leq\sum_y\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap K_y)
 \leq\mu_C(K),
\tag{55}
\]
which proves (36). This argument counts labeled occurrences before aggregating equal atoms, so all target collisions are harmless. The memberships (17) and (29) show that every atom is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

The loop masses sum to \(H_{\mathrm{loop}}\), while every cross label contributes four corners of mass coefficient \(\gamma_d\eta_\sigma\). This proves the equalities in (37). Since \(H_{\mathrm{cross}}\geq0\), equations (10) and (13) give
\[
 |\nu_{\mathrm{align}}|
 =2\gamma_d(H_{\mathrm{int}}+H_{\mathrm{cross}})
 >\frac{7\gamma_d}{200}Q.
\tag{56}
\]
The branch-specific mass bounds follow from (13).

It remains only to audit degeneracies. A loop edge contributes one compressed output, so that local output is rank zero. For a cross label, the four endpoints in (40) are distinct, hence \(u_\sigma,v_\sigma\ne0\) and (38) follows. The four relative base positions are
\[
 0,
 \quad u_\sigma,
 \quad v_\sigma,
 \quad u_\sigma+v_\sigma.
\tag{57}
\]
Because neither direction is zero, the only possible equalities among different positions in (57) are precisely \(u_\sigma=v_\sigma\) and \(u_\sigma=-v_\sigma\), proving (39). The graph positions have the analogous two-direction form with directions \((u_\sigma,U_\sigma)\) and \((v_\sigma,V_\sigma)\). Each is nonzero because its base component is nonzero, so their span has dimension one or two. Nothing prevents collisions between unrelated labels or edges, and nothing in the proof sums target densities or local packet energies. This proves every stated qualification.
