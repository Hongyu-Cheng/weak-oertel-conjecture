## Statement

Let one finite limiting actual cap law have weights $\theta_H>0$ summing to one and allocated section-log defects $s_H$ on the physical sampled set. Suppose its nonzero continuous normals $u_H$ form an irreducible positive dependence of full rank $d\ge2$, and fix $c\ne0$ such that $s_H\ne0$ implies $u_H\parallel c$. Then at most one index $E$ has $u_E\parallel c$. The total vector section correction is $\theta_Eu_Es_E$ if that index exists and is zero otherwise. When $E$ exists it vanishes as a vector measure only if $s_E=0$.

The normal hypothesis holds in the finite-plane section classification when the actual limiting contact rows have one surviving continuous slope $c$ and every retained limiting affine component touches at an own-fiber interior mark. In that case each affected fiber has a zero supporting face on the cap plane, and the signs of the proportionality to $c$ distinguish inside from outside support.

## Proof

A positive dependence is irreducible here if no proper subcollection admits strictly positive coefficients with resultant zero. If two normals parallel to $c$ point in the same direction, combine their positive coefficients into one coefficient after accounting for the ratio of their lengths. This deletes one normal while retaining a positive dependence on the others. If they point in opposite directions, their pair already admits a positive dependence, and its rank one makes it a proper subcollection because $d\ge2$ and the whole rank is $d$. Both are impossible. Thus all terms in the section correction vanish except possibly the stated single term. A positive scalar coefficient times a fixed nonzero vector sends a nonzero nonnegative measure to a nonzero vector measure.

For the actual single-slope setting, the ordinary limiting volume rows cover $dm/M$ after multiplication of the inverse rows by $L$. Each such row lies on its touching plane and has continuous gradient label $c$ almost everywhere in mixed volume. Thus $\nabla_X L=c$ almost everywhere on each positive fiber. The restriction of $L$ is affine there with slope $c$, by continuity and its finite affine presentation. If a retained plane touches at an own-fiber interior mark, it majorizes $L$ and its affine difference from this restriction is nonnegative on a neighborhood of the mark with equality there. Its continuous slope is therefore also $c$. This argument covers retained components whose limiting weight is zero. Apply the finite-plane section-normal classification to get the claimed parallelism and signs. The geometric count can be made on any such circuit; the full-law separator inequality does not follow for a conditional law from this count.
