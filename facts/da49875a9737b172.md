## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $N=d+1$, and let finitely many convex bodies $K_z(t)\subset\mathbb R^d$ move through $K_z=K_z(0)$ as $t\downarrow0$. In each old body choose an interior origin $o_z$, and let $R_z,R_{z,t}$ be the old and moving radial functions about it. Suppose $|R_{z,t}-R_z|\le Ct$ and $(R_{z,t}-R_z)/t\to r_z$ almost everywhere on the sphere. At a regular old boundary point $X=o_z+R_z(\omega)\omega$ set $v_z(X)=-r_z(\omega)n_z(X)\cdot\omega$, with $n_z$ the outward unit normal. This is the signed inward speed. Use endpoint counting measure in dimension one. Let
$$
M=\sum_z|K_z|,\quad D=\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}v_z\,dS,\quad
J=\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}(X-g_z)(X-g_z)^{\mathsf T}v_z(X)\,dS
$$
with $g_z$ the old centroids, and define $M_t,\Sigma_t,\Gamma_t$ using actual moving masses and within-fiber centroids as in the invariant energy-only buffer. For a compact $U\subset\operatorname{int}K_z$, write $a_y$ for the unique old parameter minimizing the projective energy at $y$, and set
$$
C_z(y)=\int_{\partial K_z}[1+a_y\cdot(X-y)]^{-N}v_z(X)\,dS.
$$
Then, uniformly for $y\in U$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{K_z(t)}(y)&=E_{K_z}(y)-tC_z(y)+o(t),\\
f_t(z,y)&=f(z,y)+t\,\frac{f(z,y)}N
 \left[-\frac{C_z(y)}{E_{K_z}(y)}+\frac DM\right]+o(t),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $f_t(z,y)=(E_{K_z(t)}(y)/M_t)^{1/N}$. The right derivatives at zero satisfy
$$
\dot M=-D,\qquad \dot\Sigma=(D\Sigma-J)/M,\qquad
\dot\Gamma(u)=\frac{u^{\mathsf T}\dot\Sigma u}{2\Gamma(u)}\quad(u\ne0).
$$

## Proof

For a fiber and a continuous integrand $G$ on a common compact neighborhood, signed radial integration gives the change of integral as
$$
\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}\int_{R_z(\omega)}^{R_{z,t}(\omega)}
G(o_z+s\omega)s^{d-1}\,ds\,d\omega.
$$
After division by $t$, its limit is $\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}G(o_z+R_z\omega)R_z^{d-1}r_z\,d\omega$. The radial projection formula is $(n_z\cdot\omega)\,dS=R_z^{d-1}d\omega$ almost everywhere on a convex boundary with an interior origin. Here $n_z\cdot\omega$ is bounded below almost everywhere by a positive constant from the old inball and outer radius. Thus the derivative is $-\int_{\partial K_z}Gv_z\,dS$, also for the two endpoints when $d=1$. For a uniformly bounded equi-Lipschitz family of $G$, evaluating the integrand and radial factor at the old endpoint costs $O(t^2)$ uniformly, and bounded dominated convergence of the radial difference quotients in $L^1$ makes the remaining first-order error uniform.

The radial bound gives $d_H(K_z(t),K_z)=O(t)$. All $y\in U$ have a common positive centered inball radius in $K_z$ and in the moving bodies for small $t$, by support-function convergence. Positivity of an admissible denominator on that ball bounds its parameter uniformly. By [a56f8711406a3870](a56f8711406a3870.md) each old or new optimizer has denominator at least $1/N$ on its own body. The uniform parameter bound and $O(t)$ Hausdorff motion give a fixed positive floor on the old and new bodies and on a common neighborhood of the old body for all these optimizer kernels. They are consequently uniformly bounded and equi-Lipschitz there.

The moving optimizers converge uniformly on $U$ to the corresponding old ones. Indeed, take any sequence of marks and moving optimizers with a convergent subsequence. Its parameter limit is admissible at the limiting old mark by the uniform floor. Test the old optimizer of the limiting mark in the moving objectives, where it is admissible for large indices by its strictly positive denominator. The floor, convergence of marks and parameters, and vanishing symmetric differences allow passage to the old objective, which makes the parameter limit the unique old optimizer by [d90ba05577427854](d90ba05577427854.md). The continuity of the old optimizer from the denominator-floor fact proves uniform convergence by compactness.

For each $y$ use the old optimizer as a competitor in the new objective and the new optimizer as a competitor in the old objective. The two inequalities sandwich the energy difference between the two signed shell differences at these parameters. The uniform shell estimate and uniform optimizer convergence give the same coefficient $-C_z(y)$, which is continuous in $y$ on $U$. Apply the shell estimate also to volume and to first and second moments. In differentiating the within-fiber centered second moment, the terms from the centroid derivative vanish by the zero old centered first moment. Thus its derivative is the negative corresponding boundary matrix integral. Division by $M_t=M-tD+o(t)$ gives $\dot\Sigma$, and the positive covariance gives the norm derivative. The energy is continuous and positive on $U$, with a positive lower bound there. Differentiating its normalized positive power using the uniform energy expansion gives the asserted formula for $f_t$.
