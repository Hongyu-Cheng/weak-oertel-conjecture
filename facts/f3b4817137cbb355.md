## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge2\), let \(\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^n\), let \(\mathbf e_i\) be the coordinate unit vectors, and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n&=[-2,2]^n,
&D_n&=P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,
&U_n&=\operatorname{int}(P_n)\cap\mathbb Z^n,\\
\mathcal H_n&=\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n:
 n x_1-\sum_{j=2}^n x_j\ge0\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

There are finite real convex-admissible potentials \(\phi^{(n)}\) on these exact supports with original weights \(w^{(n)}_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi^{(n)}_{\mathbf I}}>0\), total weight \(W_n=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_n}w^{(n)}_{\mathbf I}\), and original laws \(p^{(n)}=w^{(n)}/W_n\) satisfying the following assertions. Every boundary site has positive weight; every lattice site outside \(D_n\) has weight zero; and this complete table has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension on \(\mathbb R^n\). Its affine rank is \(n\), and its original mean is exactly \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_n}p^{(n)}_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf0\).

For \(E=D_n\) or \(E=U_n\), use the prescribed-center unnormalized capacity

\[
C_{\phi^{(n)}}(E;\mathbf0)
=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}
 e^{-\phi^{(n)}_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\phi^{(n)}}(D_n;\mathbf0)&=W_n,
&n+3&\le W_n<n+3+\frac14,\\
\alpha_n:=p^{(n)}(\mathcal H_n)&\le\frac4{n+3},
&1-\frac1n&\le\frac{C_{\phi^{(n)}}(U_n;\mathbf0)}{W_n}\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Every equality atom of the displayed closed halfspace is included in \(\alpha_n\). All finite parameters are specified below by integer formulas and unique positive roots of rational polynomials, so all weights are positive real algebraic numbers. In particular,

\[
\frac{C_{\phi^{(n)}}(U_n;\mathbf0)}{W_n}\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha_n}{\sqrt{C_{\phi^{(n)}}(U_n;\mathbf0)/W_n}}
\le\frac{4\sqrt2}{n+3}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently no positive absolute coefficient \(c\) makes
\(p(H)\ge c\sqrt{C_\phi(U;\mathbf J_0)/W}\) valid for all complete globally extendable tables with original integer mean and all closed halfspaces containing that mean. The same sequence rules out any lower bound depending only on \(C_\phi(U;\mathbf J_0)/W\) that is bounded below by a positive constant on some neighborhood of one.

## Proof

Fix \(n\ge2\), and temporarily suppress \(n\) on \(P_n,D_n,U_n,\mathcal H_n\). Define \(\sigma(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\) and the finite convex function

\[
g(\mathbf x)
=\max\left\{\sigma(\mathbf x),
 \sigma(\mathbf x)-(n+1)x_1,\ldots,
 \sigma(\mathbf x)-(n+1)x_n\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The sum of the \(n+1\) linear expressions in (3) is zero. Thus \(g\ge0\), and equality forces every expression to vanish, hence \(\mathbf x=0\). Its sublevel simplex is
\(R:=\{g\le1\}=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,-\mathbf1\}\).
Indeed, the inequalities \(g(\mathbf x)\le1\) are exactly nonnegativity of
\(\lambda_0=(1-\sigma(\mathbf x))/(n+1)\) and
\(\lambda_j=x_j+\lambda_0\) for \(1\le j\le n\). These coefficients have sum one and give
\(\mathbf x=\sum_j\lambda_j\mathbf e_j-\lambda_0\mathbf1\), proving the claimed equality in both directions.

The entire lattice intersection of \(R\) is

\[
A:=R\cap\mathbb Z^n
=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n,-\mathbf1\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here is a direct census. If every coordinate of an integer point in \(R\) is nonnegative, its coordinate sum is at most one, so it is zero or a unit vector. Otherwise let \(t\le-1\) be its smallest coordinate. Then \(nt\le\sigma(\mathbf x)\), while the inequality in (3) at a minimizing coordinate gives \(\sigma(\mathbf x)\le(n+1)t+1\). These imply \(t\ge-1\), so \(t=-1\) and both bounds force \(\sigma(\mathbf x)=-n\). Every coordinate is therefore \(-1\). Conversely, the listed points have \(g(0)=0\) and \(g=1\) at every listed vertex, so all are present. This proves (4) without omitting any boundary point. Since \(g\) is integer valued on \(\mathbb Z^n\), every other lattice point satisfies \(g\ge2\).

The potential \((g-1)\log2\) gives weight two at zero and weight one at each of the \(n+1\) vertices in (4). Write \(W^0=n+3\) for this total. Their original weighted sum is zero, because \(\sum_i\mathbf e_i-\mathbf1=0\). Among these sites the closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) contains precisely zero and \(\mathbf e_1\): its functional has values \(n\) at \(\mathbf e_1\), \(-1\) at each other vertex, and zero at the origin. Thus this centered law has closed-halfspace mass \(3/W^0\). Its potential is convex on \(R\), so exponentiating its negative on \(R\) and putting zero elsewhere already gives a complete globally log-concave extension of this smaller table.

Every point of \(A\) lies in \(U=\{-1,0,1\}^n\), strictly inside the larger box. Put \(v(\mathbf x)=\max\{0,g(\mathbf x)-1\}\), another finite convex function. For each integer \(k\ge2\), define positive rational numbers on all of \(D=\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^n\) by

\[
a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
=2^{1-g(\mathbf I)}k^{-v(\mathbf I)}.
\tag{5}
\]

These equal the old weights on \(A\). At every added site, \(v=g-1\) is a positive integer and \(a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}=(2k)^{-v(\mathbf I)}\). Hence the full un-tilted added weight satisfies

\[
\varepsilon_k:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\setminus A}a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
\le\frac{5^n-n-2}{2k},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}=W^0+\varepsilon_k.
\tag{6}
\]

There is a unique \(q_k>0\) solving the exact finite equation

\[
F_k(q_k)=0,
\qquad
F_k(q):=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}
 \sigma(\mathbf I)a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}q^{\sigma(\mathbf I)}.
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed,
\(F_k'(q)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\sigma(\mathbf I)^2a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}q^{\sigma(\mathbf I)-1}>0\)
for every \(q>0\). As \(q\downarrow0\), the term with exponent \(-2n\) forces \(F_k(q)\to-\infty\), and as \(q\to\infty\), the term with exponent \(2n\) forces \(F_k(q)\to+\infty\). Continuity and strict increase prove existence and uniqueness. Multiplying (7) by \(q^{2n}\) gives a rational polynomial, so its unique positive root is an exact positive algebraic number.

Define the original complete table by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
&=(g(\mathbf I)-1)\log2+(\log k)v(\mathbf I)
  -(\log q_k)\sigma(\mathbf I),\\
w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
&=a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}q_k^{\sigma(\mathbf I)},
&W^{(k)}&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I},
&p^{(k)}&=w^{(k)}/W^{(k)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Every site of \(D\), including every boundary site, has strictly positive finite weight. The formula for \(\phi^{(k)}\) extends to a finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^n\), since \(g,v\) are convex, their displayed coefficients are positive, and the last term is affine. Its restriction to \(D\) is therefore convex-admissible for every finite barycentric representation. The function \(e^{-\phi^{(k)}}\) on \(P\), extended by zero outside \(P\), is finite valued and globally log-concave. For two points of \(P\), the inequality follows from convexity of the potential and of \(P\); if either endpoint is outside \(P\), its right side is zero. This verifies the extension at every lattice point, including all zeros outside \(D\). The box vertices belong to \(D\), so \(\operatorname{conv}D=P\) has affine rank \(n\).

The original mean of (8) is exactly zero. Coordinate permutations preserve \(D,g,v,\sigma\), and hence preserve its weights. Its \(n\) coordinate moments are therefore equal. Their sum is exactly \(F_k(q_k)=0\), so each is zero. This proves the original-mean assertion without substituting a conditional law or a capacity optimizer for the law in (8).

To estimate its weight and capacity, define
\(Z_k(\boldsymbol\eta)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}a^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}e^{\boldsymbol\eta\cdot\mathbf I}\).
For the old sites, the corresponding sum is

\[
Z^0(\boldsymbol\eta)
=2+\sum_{i=1}^n e^{\eta_i}+e^{-\sum_i\eta_i}
\ge n+3=W^0.
\tag{9}
\]

The inequality is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for the \(n+1\) exponential terms, whose product is one. Equality requires all their exponents to be equal, forcing every \(\eta_i=0\). Thus zero is the unique minimizer of the old sum.

The vector \((\log q_k)\mathbf1\) is the unique full-tilt minimizer of \(Z_k\). In fact, for every \(\boldsymbol\eta\), the original mean identity and \(e^s\ge1+s\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_k(\boldsymbol\eta)
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
 e^{(\boldsymbol\eta-(\log q_k)\mathbf1)\cdot\mathbf I}\\
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
 \bigl(1+(\boldsymbol\eta-(\log q_k)\mathbf1)\cdot\mathbf I\bigr)
=W^{(k)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Equality holds at the claimed vector. If a different vector gave equality, equality in each positive summand would force its difference to be orthogonal to every \(\mathbf e_i\in D\), a contradiction. Since the old sites are retained, (6), (9), and (10) now yield the finite bounds

\[
W^0\le Z^0((\log q_k)\mathbf1)
\le W^{(k)}\le Z_k(\mathbf0)=W^0+\varepsilon_k.
\tag{11}
\]

The complete added weight after the mean-restoring tilt is also at most \(\varepsilon_k\), because it equals
\(W^{(k)}-Z^0((\log q_k)\mathbf1)\), and the old sum is at least \(W^0\).

For the interior capacity, every old site belongs to \(U\). Thus for every independent \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n\), (9) gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in U}w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
\ge Z^0(\boldsymbol\theta+(\log q_k)\mathbf1)
\ge W^0.
\tag{12}
\]

Taking the infimum preserves this same lower bound. Zero tilt gives an upper bound by \(W^{(k)}\). Moreover, (10), with \(\boldsymbol\eta=\boldsymbol\theta+(\log q_k)\mathbf1\), shows that the full capacity is \(W^{(k)}\). Therefore, with capacities normalized exactly as in the statement,

\[
C_{\phi^{(k)}}(D;\mathbf0)=W^{(k)},
\qquad
\frac{W^0}{W^{(k)}}
\le\frac{C_{\phi^{(k)}}(U;\mathbf0)}{W^{(k)}}\le1.
\tag{13}
\]

This proves the inner estimate for every tilt and every finite \(k\); it assumes neither continuity nor attainment of an infimum.

For clarity, the whole padding limit is exact as well. For fixed \(n\), (6) and (11) imply \(W^{(k)}\to W^0\) as \(k\to\infty\). They also imply
\(2+nq_k+q_k^{-n}\to n+3\).
The bounds on these positive terms keep \(q_k\) bounded above and away from zero. Every subsequential limit must have equality in (9) with \(\boldsymbol\eta=(\log q)\mathbf1\), so it must be one. Hence \(q_k\to1\). The full law consequently converges at every site of the fixed \(D\) to the old law on \(A\), extended by zero off \(A\): old weights converge to two at zero and one at each vertex, while every added weight tends to zero by (5), the boundedness of \(q_k\), and finiteness of \(D\). Thus the mass of the same closed halfspace tends to \(3/(n+3)\), with every equality atom included. Equation (13) squeezes the interior-capacity ratio to one.

We finish with an explicit finite choice, so no limiting selection is needed for the counterexample sequence. Restore \(n\), set \(k=2\cdot5^n\) in the preceding construction, and take \(\phi^{(n)}\) to be its potential (8). Equation (6) gives \(\varepsilon_k<1/4\). The old sum and (11) give
\(nq_k\le n+1+\varepsilon_k\), hence \(q_k\le1+(1+\varepsilon_k)/n\).
The old sites of \(\mathcal H\) have total tilted weight \(2+q_k\), and all added sites together have weight at most \(\varepsilon_k\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\cap\mathcal H}w^{(k)}_{\mathbf I}
&\le2+q_k+\varepsilon_k\\
&\le3+\frac{1+\varepsilon_k}{n}+\varepsilon_k
<3+\frac{5}{4n}+\frac14
\le\frac{31}{8}<4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

The sum on the left includes every equality site; none was discarded in the bound by all added sites. Divide (14) by \(W^{(k)}\ge W^0=n+3\). Equations (11) and (13) also give

\[
\frac{C_{\phi^{(k)}}(U;\mathbf0)}{W^{(k)}}
\ge\frac{n+3}{n+3+1/4}
=1-\frac1{4n+13}
\ge1-\frac1n.
\tag{15}
\]

These prove (1), and \(1-1/n\ge1/2\) proves (2). For any proposed positive coefficient \(c\), a finite \(n\) with \(4\sqrt2/(n+3)<c\) gives a strict violation at the stated closed halfspace through the exact original integer mean. The same sequence has interior ratio tending to one and halfspace mass tending to zero, proving the final assertion about lower bounds bounded away from zero near one.
