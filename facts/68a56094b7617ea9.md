## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and define
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
\mu_C(B):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\}
\quad
(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ Borel}).
\]
Let \(D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}\). Let \(\mathcal E\) be a finite nonempty set of two-element unordered pairs \(e=\{u,v\}\subseteq D_+\) such that
\[
u\equiv v\pmod 2,
\]
and assign a weight \(s_e>0\) to every \(e\in\mathcal E\). Put
\[
\deg_s(z):=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E:z\in e}s_e,
\qquad
Q:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}s_e,
\]
and assume the source-capacity inequalities
\[
\deg_s(z)\leq\frac{a_z}{2}
\qquad(z\in D_+).
\tag{1}
\]
For \(e=\{u,v\}\), set
\[
m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
T_e(x,y):=\left(m_e,\frac{x+y}{2}\right),
\]
and define the finite Borel measures
\[
\nu_e:=(T_e)_\#\left(
\frac{s_e}{a_ua_v}\,
\mathcal H_d|_{C_u}\otimes\mathcal H_d|_{C_v}
\right),
\qquad
\nu:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\nu_e.
\tag{2}
\]
Then:

1. Each \(\nu_e\) has mass \(s_e\), so \(|\nu|=Q\); every \(\nu_e\), and hence \(\nu\), is supported on
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\),
\[
2\nu(H)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{3}
\]

2. Let \(c_z\) denote the centroid of \(C_z\) for \(z\in D_+\). On its target fiber \(\{m_e\}\times\mathbb R^d\), the measure \(\nu_e\) has Lebesgue density
\[
g_e(q)=
\frac{2^ds_e}{a_ua_v}
\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\mathbf1_{C_u}(x)\mathbf1_{C_v}(2q-x)\,dx.
\tag{4}
\]
The normalized density \(f_e:=g_e/s_e\) is a log-concave probability density with centroid
\[
q_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}\in C_{m_e}.
\tag{5}
\]

3. Suppose there are \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\) such that the affine map
\[
q(z):=Az+b
\tag{6}
\]
satisfies
\[
q(m_e)=q_e
\qquad(e\in\mathcal E).
\tag{7}
\]
Then there is
\[
m_0\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal E\}
\]
such that
\[
p:=(m_0,q(m_0))\in S
\tag{8}
\]
and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni p\) satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\frac{2}{e}\,2^{-n}Q.
\tag{9}
\]

Here the fiber-volume notation \(a_z\) is the notation \(w_z\) used in accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041. The theorem makes no log-concavity claim about any sum of the densities \(g_e\) whose centroids differ.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that only finitely many lattice fibers are nonempty. Every \(z\in D_+\) indexes a full-dimensional compact convex body \(C_z\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), so its normalized Lebesgue measure and centroid are well-defined.

Fix \(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal E\). The product measure inside (2) has mass
\[
\frac{s_e}{a_ua_v}\,a_ua_v=s_e,
\]
and pushforward preserves total mass. Hence \(|\nu_e|=s_e\) and \(|\nu|=Q\). If \((x,y)\in C_u\times C_v\), then convexity gives
\[
\left(\frac{u+v}{2},\frac{x+y}{2}\right)
=\frac12(u,x)+\frac12(v,y)\in C.
\]
The same-parity hypothesis gives \(m_e=(u+v)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\). Thus \(T_e(C_u\times C_v)\subseteq S\), proving the support assertion.

We next prove simultaneous halfspace domination. Let \(H\) be a proper closed halfspace, and write \(H=\{(z,x):\ell(z,x)\geq0\}\) for a nonconstant affine functional \(\ell\). Affineness gives
\[
2\ell\!\left(T_e(x,y)\right)=\ell(u,x)+\ell(v,y).
\]
If \(T_e(x,y)\in H\), at least one of \((u,x)\) and \((v,y)\) lies in \(H\). This remains true when the midpoint lies on the boundary because \(H\) is closed. Therefore
\[
\mathbf1_H(T_e(x,y))
\leq \mathbf1_H(u,x)+\mathbf1_H(v,y).
\]
Writing
\[
\mu_z(H):=\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
integration against the product measure in (2) yields
\[
\nu_e(H)
\leq \frac{s_e}{a_u}\mu_u(H)
+\frac{s_e}{a_v}\mu_v(H).
\tag{10}
\]
Summing (10) over the unordered edges charges each endpoint exactly by its weighted degree, so (1) gives
\[
\nu(H)
\leq\sum_{z\in D_+}\frac{\deg_s(z)}{a_z}\mu_z(H)
\leq\frac12\sum_{z\in D_+}\mu_z(H)
=\frac12\mu_C(H).
\tag{11}
\]
The empty halfspace is immediate. For the whole space, the same conclusion follows either from (11), with every fiber section equal to the full fiber, or directly from
\[
2Q=\sum_z\deg_s(z)\leq\frac12\sum_z a_z.
\]
Thus (3) holds for every closed halfspace, including degenerate conventions.

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be independent random vectors uniformly distributed on \(C_u\) and \(C_v\), respectively. The continuous coordinate of the normalized component \(s_e^{-1}\nu_e\) is \(Z=(X+Y)/2\). If \(h\) is the convolution density of \(X+Y\), then
\[
h(t)=\frac1{a_ua_v}\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\mathbf1_{C_u}(x)\mathbf1_{C_v}(t-x)\,dx.
\]
The change of variables \(t=2q\), whose Jacobian is \(dt=2^d\,dq\), shows that \(Z\) has density
\[
f_e(q)=2^dh(2q)
=\frac{2^d}{a_ua_v}\int_{\mathbb R^d}
\mathbf1_{C_u}(x)\mathbf1_{C_v}(2q-x)\,dx.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, \(\int f_e=1\), and multiplying (12) by \(s_e\) proves (4) with no missing normalization factor.

The normalized indicator densities of \(C_u\) and \(C_v\) are log-concave. The exact convolution-closure and log-concave centroid-halfspace inputs verified in accepted fact 15ec168ab4433041 say that their convolution is log-concave, that composing it with the invertible linear map \(q\mapsto2q\) and multiplying by \(2^d\) preserves log-concavity, and that every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\) containing the resulting density's centroid has mass at least \(1/e\). Thus \(f_e\) is log-concave and has this centroid-halfspace guarantee. Moreover,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}qf_e(q)\,dq
=\mathbb E\frac{X+Y}{2}
=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}=q_e.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(c_u\in C_u\), \(c_v\in C_v\), and
\[
\frac12C_u+\frac12C_v\subseteq C_{m_e},
\]
equation (5), including \(q_e\in C_{m_e}\), follows. Notice that log-concavity was established separately for each \(f_e\); no sum of differently centered components was used.

Assume now (6)-(7), and define the finite atomic base measure
\[
\sigma:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}s_e\delta_{m_e}.
\tag{14}
\]
It is supported on \(\mathbb Z^n\) and has mass \(Q>0\). Apply the \(d=0\) specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to \(\sigma\). It supplies \(m_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that every closed base halfspace \(J\ni m_0\) satisfies
\[
\sigma(J)\geq2^{-n}Q.
\tag{15}
\]
This point lies in the convex hull of the atoms of \(\sigma\). Indeed, otherwise strict separation of \(m_0\) from the compact polytope \(\operatorname{conv}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal E\}\) would give a closed halfspace containing \(m_0\) and disjoint from every atom, contradicting (15). Choose coefficients \(\lambda_e\geq0\) with \(\sum_e\lambda_e=1\) and
\[
m_0=\sum_e\lambda_em_e.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (5)-(7) and convexity of \(C\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
p
&=(m_0,q(m_0))
=\sum_e\lambda_e(m_e,q(m_e))\\
&=\sum_e\lambda_e(m_e,q_e)\in C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(m_0\in\mathbb Z^n\), this proves (8). This convex-hull argument is necessary because the lattice centerpoint need not itself be an atom of \(\sigma\).

Let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\). Translate its boundary parallel toward \(p\) until it passes through \(p\); the resulting closed halfspace \(H_0\) contains \(p\) and satisfies \(H_0\subseteq H\). We may write
\[
H_0=\left\{(z,x):
\alpha^\top(z-m_0)+\beta^\top(x-q(m_0))\geq0
\right\},
\tag{18}
\]
where \((\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)\). Put
\[
r:=\alpha+A^\top\beta.
\]
If \(r\neq0\), let
\[
J:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:r^\top(z-m_0)\geq0\};
\]
if \(r=0\), let \(J:=\mathbb R^n\). In both cases \(J\ni m_0\), and (15) gives
\[
\sum_{e:m_e\in J}s_e=\sigma(J)\geq2^{-n}Q.
\tag{19}
\]

Fix an edge \(e\) counted in (19), and set \(t_e:=r^\top(m_e-m_0)\geq0\). Affineness of \(q\) and (7) give, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\alpha^\top(m_e-m_0)+\beta^\top(x-q(m_0))\\
&\qquad=r^\top(m_e-m_0)+\beta^\top(x-q_e)
=t_e+\beta^\top(x-q_e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\beta=0\), equation (20) is nonnegative on the entire target fiber, so \(\nu_e(H_0)=s_e\). If \(\beta\neq0\), the fiber section of \(H_0\) at \(m_e\) contains the closed halfspace
\[
G_e:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\beta^\top(x-q_e)\geq0\}.
\]
This halfspace contains the centroid \(q_e\) of the log-concave probability density \(f_e\). The centroid-halfspace conclusion cited above therefore gives
\[
\nu_e(H_0)\geq s_e\int_{G_e}f_e(x)\,dx\geq\frac{s_e}{e}.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus (21) is valid in both cases, with the stronger value \(s_e\) when \(\beta=0\). Summing only the edges in (19) gives
\[
\nu(H_0)
\geq\frac1e\sum_{e:m_e\in J}s_e
\geq\frac1e\,2^{-n}Q.
\tag{22}
\]
Finally, monotonicity and the simultaneous domination (3) yield
\[
\mu_C(H)
\geq\mu_C(H_0)
\geq2\nu(H_0)
\geq\frac{2}{e}\,2^{-n}Q.
\]
If the whole space is included as a closed halfspace, summing (1) gives \(2Q\leq\frac12\sum_z a_z\), hence \(Q\leq\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})/4\). Since \(n\geq1\), this implies
\[
\frac{2}{e}\,2^{-n}Q\leq Q\leq\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d}).
\]
Thus the whole space also has at least the required mass. This completes the proof of (9) for all closed halfspaces containing \(p\), and proves the theorem.
