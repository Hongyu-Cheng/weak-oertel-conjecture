## Statement

For each integer \(m\ge 1\), let \(D_m=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(0,4m)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Among probability laws on \(D_m\) with full mean \((1,m)\), the unique law maximizing Shannon entropy is the positive law \(q_m(x,y)=r_m^x/Z_m\), where \(r_m=\sqrt{4m+1}\) and \(Z_m=2(4m+1)+(2m+1)\sqrt{4m+1}\). Thus its original exponential parameters in the coordinates \((x,y)\), with exponent \(\alpha_m x+\beta_m y\), are \(\alpha_m=\tfrac12\log(4m+1)\) and \(\beta_m=0\). The point \((1,m)\) is in the interior of \(\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\), so its minimal face is the whole triangle.

Let \(k\ge 1\), let \(m_1,\ldots,m_k\ge 1\) be arbitrary integers, and let \((X_i,Y_i)\), \(1\le i\le k\), be independent with laws \(q_{m_i}\). Put \(\mu=((1,m_1),\ldots,(1,m_k))\in\mathbb R^{2k}\). For every real block normal \((a_i,b_i)\), \(1\le i\le k\),

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(
\sum_{i=1}^k \bigl[a_i(X_i-1)+b_i(Y_i-m_i)\bigr]\ge 0
\right)\ge \frac14.
\]

Consequently, every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{2k}\) containing \(\mu\) has product probability at least \(1/4\). The vector \(\mu\) itself belongs to the product support. The product law is also the unique maximum-entropy law on that support with full mean \(\mu\).

The scalar principle used here is the following: if \(V\) is a finite-valued random variable, \(T=t(V)\) has a distribution symmetric about zero, and the conditional law of a real random variable \(N\) given \(V=v\) is symmetric about zero for every \(v\) of positive probability, then \(\mathbb P(T+N\ge 0)\ge 1/4\). No independence between \(T\) and \(N\) is required.

## Proof

The integer points of the original triangle are exactly

\[
D_m=
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le y\le 4m\}
\cup\{(1,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le y\le 2m\}
\cup\{(2,0)\}.
\]

Indeed, its inequalities are \(x\ge 0\), \(y\ge 0\), and \(2mx+y\le 4m\). These allow precisely the three listed integer columns. Its vertices belong to \(D_m\), hence \(\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\) is the original triangle. At \((1,m)\), the three inequalities are strict: \(1>0\), \(m>0\), and \(2m+m<4m\). This proves the interior and minimal-face assertions.

First set \(\beta_m=0\) and write \(r=\exp(\alpha_m)>0\). For the weights \(r^x\), the column totals are \(4m+1\), \((2m+1)r\), and \(r^2\). For a law on \(x\in\{0,1,2\}\), the equation \(\mathbb E[X]=1\) is equivalent to \(\mathbb P(X=0)=\mathbb P(X=2)\). Thus the weights yield \(\mathbb E[X]=1\) precisely when \(r^2=4m+1\). Choosing \(r=r_m\) therefore gives the stated normalization \(Z_m\) and law \(q_m\). In particular, with \(B_m=2r_m+2m+1\), its original marginal probabilities are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(X=0)=\mathbb P(X=2)&=\frac{r_m}{B_m},\\
\mathbb P(X=1)&=\frac{2m+1}{B_m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Conditional on \(X=x\), \(Y\) is uniform on the integers from \(0\) through \(L_m(x)=2m(2-x)\). Its conditional mean is \(\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x]=L_m(x)/2=2m-mx\). It follows that \(\mathbb E[Y]=2m-m\mathbb E[X]=m\). Hence \(q_m\) satisfies both prescribed original mean equations. Every point has strictly positive probability.

To prove entropy maximality directly, let \(p\) be any probability law on \(D_m\) with \(\mathbb E_p[X]=1\) and \(\mathbb E_p[Y]=m\). Define its entropy by \(H(p)=-\sum p\log p\), using \(0\log 0=0\), and its relative entropy to \(q_m\) by \(D(p\Vert q_m)=\sum p\log(p/q_m)\). Since \(\log q_m=\alpha_m x-\log Z_m\), the entropy identity is \(H(p)=\log Z_m-\alpha_m-D(p\Vert q_m)\).

For \(u\ge 0\), the function \(u\log u-u+1\) is nonnegative, vanishing only at \(u=1\), with the continuous convention at \(u=0\). Applying this to \(u=p(x,y)/q_m(x,y)\) and summing with the positive weights \(q_m\) gives \(D(p\Vert q_m)\ge 0\), with equality only when \(p=q_m\) at every point. The entropy identity proves uniqueness and maximality. It also proves that the constructed law is the original full-mean entropy law, without presupposing that the optimizer has exponential form. Its exponential parameters are unique: equality of two positive exponential representations would make their difference an affine function constant on \(D_m\); evaluating that function at \((0,0)\), \((1,0)\), and \((0,1)\) forces both parameter differences to vanish. All three points belong to \(D_m\) for \(m\ge 1\).

For the product support, independence gives full mean \(\mu\), and the product probabilities are proportional to \(\exp(\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_{m_i}x_i)\). For any other joint law \(p\) on this finite support with full mean \(\mu\), the same relative-entropy computation yields

\[
H(p)=
\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl[\log Z_{m_i}-\alpha_{m_i}\bigr]
-D\!\left(p\,\middle\Vert\,\bigotimes_{i=1}^k q_{m_i}\right).
\]

The product law is everywhere positive, so the same nonnegativity and equality argument proves its asserted unique entropy maximality. Also \((1,m_i)\in D_{m_i}\) for each \(i\), proving \(\mu\) belongs to the product support.

We next prove the stated scalar principle. Symmetry of \(T\) implies \(\mathbb P(T\ge 0)=(1+\mathbb P(T=0))/2\ge 1/2\). For each \(v\) with \(t(v)\ge 0\), conditional symmetry of \(N\) gives \(\mathbb P(N\ge 0\mid V=v)=(1+\mathbb P(N=0\mid V=v))/2\ge 1/2\). On this event, \(t(v)+N\ge 0\). Summing only over such \(v\) therefore gives \(\mathbb P(T+N\ge 0)\ge \tfrac12\mathbb P(T\ge 0)\ge 1/4\). This argument places no restriction on how the symmetric conditional noise laws vary with \(v\).

Apply the principle to \(V=(X_1,\ldots,X_k)\). Define the conditional centered noises \(\eta_i=Y_i-(2m_i-m_iX_i)\). For every fixed column configuration \(V=x\) having positive probability, the conditional law of the \(Y_i\) is the product of uniform laws on \(\{0,\ldots,L_{m_i}(x_i)\}\). The reflection

\[
(y_1,\ldots,y_k)\longmapsto
\bigl(L_{m_1}(x_1)-y_1,\ldots,L_{m_k}(x_k)-y_k\bigr)
\]

preserves this conditional law and changes every \(\eta_i\) to \(-\eta_i\). Consequently \(N=\sum_{i=1}^k b_i\eta_i\) has a symmetric conditional distribution given \(V=x\), including configurations with degenerate columns.

The marginal law of each \(X_i-1\) is symmetric about zero, as \(\mathbb P(X_i=0)=\mathbb P(X_i=2)\). Independence implies that the joint law of \((X_1-1,\ldots,X_k-1)\) is invariant under simultaneous sign reversal. Therefore \(T=\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i-m_ib_i)(X_i-1)\) is symmetric about zero. Finally, the exact original-coordinate identity

\[
a_i(X_i-1)+b_i(Y_i-m_i)
=(a_i-m_ib_i)(X_i-1)+b_i\eta_i
\]

holds for each factor. Thus the projection centered at the original full mean is \(T+N\), and the scalar principle proves the probability bound \(1/4\) for every choice of block normals.

Write any nontrivial closed halfspace containing \(\mu\) as

\[
\left\{z:
\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl[a_i z_{i,1}+b_i z_{i,2}\bigr]\ge h
\right\}
\]

for real coefficients and \(h\le\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i+b_i m_i)\). It contains the halfspace with the same normal and boundary through \(\mu\), whose probability is at least \(1/4\) by the preceding bound. This proves the claim for every such halfspace. A zero normal, if included in the halfspace convention and containing \(\mu\), gives the whole space and probability one.
