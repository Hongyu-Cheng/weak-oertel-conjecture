## Statement

For $d\ge2$, let $U$ be uniform on $\Delta_d$ and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)>0}t/\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)^{1/d}$. For a strictly positive row $x$, the full threshold $m=\max_jx_j$ minimizes the ratio exactly when $x=m\mathbf 1$ or $x$ is a permutation of $(a,m,\ldots,m)$ with $0<a<m$ and $d$ entries equal to $m$. In these cases the capacity is $m$ and the minimizing threshold is unique.

## Proof

Suppose first that the row is nonconstant, with $r$ entries equal to $m$. If $r\le d-1$, let $g>0$ be the smallest positive gap $m-x_j$. The upper-tail event $x\cdot U>m-\delta$ implies $\sum_{j:x_j<m}U_j<\delta/g$. Put $k=d+1-r\ge2$. These $k$ coordinates have a bounded marginal density near the origin, proportional to $(1-\sum_j u_j)^{r-1}$ there, as follows by integrating the other $r$ simplex coordinates. Thus the upper-tail probability is $O(\delta^k)$. The ratio at $m-\delta$ is $m-\delta+O(\delta^2)<m$ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$.

When $r=d$ the row has a single low entry $a$. Its cap at $a<t\le m$ is a homothetic vertex simplex with fraction $((t-a)/(m-a))^d$. The ratio $t(m-a)/(t-a)$ is strictly decreasing since $a>0$, and at $t\ge m$ the ratio equals $t$. For $x=m\mathbf 1$, the distribution is the atom at $m$ and the ratio is $t$ on its domain $[m,\infty)$. These cases give the claimed minima.
