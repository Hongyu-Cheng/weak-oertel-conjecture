## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be compact convex bodies of positive volume. Write $\mu$ for the sum of their labeled fiber volumes, $M=\int d\mu$, and $\beta$ for the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth on the sampled fibers. Assume this maximum is locally minimized at $L=1$ when the measure on the same sampled family is reweighted by $L(p)^{-d}$ for positive affine $L$ near $1$. Equivalently one can use ordinary fiber volumes of the image family $(p,x)\mapsto(p/L(p),x/L(p))$.

Select finitely many circuits indexed by $i$, at least one at each deepest base. In each circuit the caps
$$
H_{ij}=\{a_{ij}\cdot p+u_{ij}\cdot x\ge b_{ij}\},\qquad \|u_{ij}\|_2=1,
$$
have mass $\beta M$, pass through the same interior deepest mark $(p_i,x_i)$, and have weights $\rho_{ij}>0$ with $\sum_j\rho_{ij}=1$, $\sum_j\rho_{ij}u_{ij}=0$. At every other base assume there is a fixed finite cover of the entire fiber plane by caps of mass strictly below $\beta M$. Define $\delta_{ip}=p-p_i$, $T_{ijp}=b_{ij}-a_{ij}\cdot p$, and $F_{ijp}(s)=|K_p\cap\{u_{ij}\cdot x\ge s\}|$. Assume $F_{ijp}$ is $C^2$ near $T_{ijp}$ for all $i,j,p$. At that threshold set
$$
S_{ijp}=-F'_{ijp},\quad R_{ijp}=-F''_{ijp},\quad S_{ij}=\sum_p S_{ijp}>0,\qquad
\kappa_{ij}=\frac{\rho_{ij}/S_{ij}}{\sum_l \rho_{il}/S_{il}}.
$$
Put $H^*_{ij}=-\sum_p R_{ijp}\delta_{ip}\delta_{ip}^{\mathsf T}$, $V_{ij}=\sum_p S_{ijp}\delta_{ip}\delta_{ip}^{\mathsf T}$, $h_{ij}=\sum_p R_{ijp}\delta_{ip}$, and $k_{ij}=-\sum_p R_{ijp}$. Use matrices on $\operatorname{span}(P-P)$. Then $H^*_{ij}\succeq0$. For an affine direction $A$ with linear part $b$, define
$$
B_{ij}(A)=-d\int_{H_{ij}}A\,d\mu,\quad t_i(A)=\sum_j\kappa_{ij}B_{ij}(A),\quad
g_i(A)=t_i(A)+d\beta\int A\,d\mu,
$$
and put $v_{ij}=(B_{ij}(A)-t_i(A))/S_{ij}$, $w_{ij}=dV_{ij}b-v_{ij}h_{ij}$. The first-order condition is $\max_i g_i(A)\ge0$ for every affine $A$.

Fix any $A$ with $\max_i g_i(A)=0$. Let $I_0(A)=\{i:g_i(A)=0\}$ and $J(A)=\{i\in I_0(A): w_{ij}\in\operatorname{ran}H^*_{ij}\text{ for all }j\}$. For $i\in J(A)$ set
$$
n_i(A)=d(d+1)\left[\sum_j\kappa_{ij}\int_{H_{ij}}A^2\,d\mu-\beta\int A^2\,d\mu\right]
 +\sum_j\kappa_{ij}\left[k_{ij}v_{ij}^2-w_{ij}^{\mathsf T}(H^*_{ij})^\dagger w_{ij}\right].
$$
Here the dagger denotes the inverse on the range and zero on the kernel. Then $J(A)$ is nonempty and there are weights $\pi_i\ge0$ on $J(A)$ with
$$
\sum_i\pi_i=1,\qquad \sum_i\pi_i g_i=0\text{ as an affine-direction functional},\qquad
\sum_i\pi_i n_i(A)\ge0.
$$
Moreover, if $W$ is a linear space of affine directions on which every $g_i$ vanishes, let $E(W)$ be the groups $i$ for which $A\mapsto w_{ij}(A)$ belongs to $\operatorname{ran}H^*_{ij}$ for every $A\in W$ and every $j$. Then $0\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_i:i\in E(W)\}$.

## Proof

For $L$ affine near $1$ its positive chart maps halfspace traces bijectively: multiply the image margin by $L$, or use the inverse $(p',x')\mapsto c(p',x')/(1-a\cdot p')$ for $L(p)=c+a\cdot p$ with $c>0$. A sampled $d$-fiber scales by $1/L(p)$, giving the reweighting. Varying the base normal of a selected cap by $\eta$ and the intercept by $\eta\cdot p_i$ keeps the mark on its boundary. Its mass as a function of $\eta$ has a minimum at zero, since the old cap attains the depth there. The $C^2$ profile hypothesis gives
$$
\sum_p S_{ijp}\delta_{ip}=0,\qquad H^*_{ij}\succeq0.
$$

Take an arbitrary affine $B$ and independent velocities $c_{ij}\in\operatorname{span}(P-P)$, and set $L_t=1+tA+t^2B/2$. Move the base normal to $a_{ij}+tc_{ij}$ and the intercept to $b_{ij}+tc_{ij}\cdot p_i+s_{ij}(t)$. Impose common raw mass $\tau_i(t)$ in group $i$ and $\sum_j\rho_{ij}s_{ij}(t)=0$. The $C^2$ implicit function theorem applies to these equations at the original thresholds and mass: the homogeneous linearization is $-S_{ij}ds_{ij}-d\tau_i=0$ and $\sum_j\rho_{ij}ds_{ij}=0$, which force every increment to vanish. The moved caps cover the whole $p_i$-plane because their weighted margins sum to zero there. The fixed cheaper covers retain strict slack relative to $\beta M(t)$, where $M(t)=\int L_t^{-d}\,d\mu$.

The threshold on fiber $p$ is $T_{ijp}+s_{ij}(t)-tc_{ij}\cdot\delta_{ip}$. Its first derivative gives
$$
\tau_i'(0)=B_{ij}(A)-S_{ij}s_{ij}'(0)
$$
by the first-order stationary identity for the base normals. Averaging with $\kappa_{ij}$ cancels the threshold derivatives because $\kappa_{ij}S_{ij}$ is proportional to $\rho_{ij}$. Thus $\tau_i'(0)=t_i(A)$ and $s_{ij}'(0)=v_{ij}$. In particular $(\tau_i-\beta M(t))'(0)=g_i(A)$. If all these derivatives were strictly negative, the exact covers would contradict local minimality for small $t>0$. This proves the first-order condition and, by separation, $0\in\operatorname{conv}\{g_i\}$. At a first-order tie any convex combination giving zero is supported on $I_0(A)$, by evaluation at $A$.

The weight derivatives are $-dA$ and $d(d+1)A^2-dB$. In one row of the twice-differentiated common-mass equation, the terms beyond the integral of the weight's second derivative and $-S_{ij}s_{ij}''(0)$ are
$$
2d\sum_p A(p)S_{ijp}(v_{ij}-c_{ij}\cdot\delta_{ip})
  -\sum_p R_{ijp}(v_{ij}-c_{ij}\cdot\delta_{ip})^2.
$$
Average again with $\kappa_{ij}$, canceling the second threshold derivatives. Using $A(p)=A(p_i)+b\cdot\delta_{ip}$, the terms with $A(p_i)$ vanish by stationarity and $\sum_j\kappa_{ij}S_{ij}v_{ij}=0$. Subtracting $\beta M''(0)$ gives
$$
(\tau_i-\beta M(t))''(0)=g_i(B)+N_i(A,c_i),
$$
where
$$
N_i(A,c_i)=d(d+1)\left[\sum_j\kappa_{ij}\int_{H_{ij}}A^2\,d\mu-\beta\int A^2\,d\mu\right]
+\sum_j\kappa_{ij}\left[k_{ij}v_{ij}^2+c_{ij}^{\mathsf T}H^*_{ij}c_{ij}-2c_{ij}\cdot w_{ij}\right].
$$
Every group outside $I_0(A)$ already decreases its raw difference at first order. Simultaneous strict negativity of these second derivatives in the tied groups would therefore make all covers strictly cheaper in normalized mass for small $t>0$. Hence for every $B$ and all the velocities the maximum of $g_i(B)+N_i(A,c_i)$ over $I_0(A)$ is nonnegative.

For $i\in J(A)$, completing the square in each row yields $\min_{c_i}N_i(A,c_i)=n_i(A)$, attained at $c_{ij}=(H^*_{ij})^\dagger w_{ij}$, up to null vectors. For $i\notin J(A)$ within $I_0(A)$, some $w_{ij}$ has nonzero pairing with a null vector. Motion in that vector's signed direction makes $N_i$ arbitrarily negative. The velocities for different groups are independent, and only finite velocities are needed to make a strictly negative comparison. Therefore $J(A)$ is nonempty and $\max_{i\in J(A)}[g_i(B)+n_i(A)]\ge0$ for every $B$.

The affine space $\{(g_i(B)+n_i(A))_{i\in J(A)}:B\}$ thus misses the open negative orthant. Separation yields a nonzero linear functional with nonnegative coordinates, bounded below on that affine space by its supremum on the negative orthant, which is zero. Boundedness below on an affine space forces it to annihilate the direction space. Normalizing its coordinates to sum to one gives the three conclusions for $\pi_i$. For the last assertion, each group outside $E(W)$ satisfies the range conditions only on a proper linear subspace of $W$. A finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot cover $W$, as the product of linear equations vanishing on those subspaces is a nonzero polynomial. Choose $A$ outside the union, or any $A$ if it is empty. Then $I_0(A)$ consists of all groups and $J(A)=E(W)$. The identity for $\pi_i$ proves the stated convex-hull inclusion.
