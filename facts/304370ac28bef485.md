## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(k\ge1\), put \(r=k+1\) and define

\[
P_k:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times[-1,1]^k)\cup\{(2,\mathbf0_k)\}\bigr),
\qquad D:=P_k\cap\mathbb Z^r.
\]

Then \(P_k\) has affine dimension \(r\), and its complete lattice support is exactly

\[
D=(\{0\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^k)
  \cup\{(1,\mathbf0_k),(2,\mathbf0_k)\},
\qquad \operatorname{conv}D=P_k.
\]

Let \(p\) be any log-affine law on \(D\), namely \(p_{\mathbf x}=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}/\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}\) for arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^r\). For each \(\mathbf j\in D\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(D\) with mean \(\mathbf j\). In the notation of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, the exact identifications are \(A=D\), \(m=r\), and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{\mathbf j}\), where \(r_{\mathbf j}\) is its relative-entropy minimizer for the source \(p\); equivalently, \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{D,\mathbf j}\) in its maximum-entropy notation. These rows are independent of \(\boldsymbol\ell\).

Their complete census is as follows. For \(\mathbf j=(0,\mathbf y)\) with \(\mathbf y\in\{-1,0,1\}^k\), its minimal face is

\[
F_{\mathbf j}
=\{(0,\mathbf z):\mathbf z\in[-1,1]^k,
                  \ z_i=y_i\text{ whenever }y_i\in\{-1,1\}\}.
\]

The row \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) fixes those coordinates and gives the coordinates with \(y_i=0\) independent uniform laws on \(\{-1,0,1\}\). If there are \(f\) zero coordinates, its face has dimension \(f\) and its row has \(3^f\) equally weighted atoms. There are exactly \(\binom{k}{f}2^{k-f}\) such roots for each \(0\le f\le k\). The apex \((2,\mathbf0_k)\) has singleton minimal face and Dirac row. The middle root \(\mathbf j=(1,\mathbf0_k)\) is the only interior lattice point, and its minimal face is \(P_k\).

Put \(a_{\mathrm{base}}:=3^k\) and \(Z_*:=2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}\). The middle row is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}((0,\mathbf z))&=Z_*^{-1}
       &&(\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^k),\\
\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}((1,\mathbf0_k))&=\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}/Z_*,\\
\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}((2,\mathbf0_k))&=a_{\mathrm{base}}/Z_*.
\end{aligned}
\]

For this row, write \(\alpha_k:=a_{\mathrm{base}}/Z_*=\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}/(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1)\) and \(\beta_k:=\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}/Z_*=1/(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1)\). These are exactly the proposed layer masses \(\alpha,\beta\), respectively, and are distinct from the fixed-family constants \(\alpha,\beta\) in bf1ff94487296374. The bottom, middle, and apex layers have probabilities \(\alpha_k,\beta_k,\alpha_k\), respectively, and the conditional bottom law is the uniform ternary product law.

For every \(\mathbf j\in D\) and every direction \(\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r\), including directions of zero variance,

\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}
 \bigl[(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j))^4\bigr]
\le9\left(
 \operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}[\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf X]
 \right)^2.
\]

Here \(\kappa=9\) has the same meaning as the directional fourth-moment constant in the product-conversion proof of bf1ff94487296374; no uniform positive lower bound on individual row atoms is required. Bottom rows satisfy the stronger bound with \(\kappa=3\), and Dirac rows have both moments zero.

In contrast, the middle row satisfies the exact identity

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}}
 [\log\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}(\mathbf X)]
=\frac{\alpha_k}{2}k^2(\log3)^2,
\qquad
\frac{k^2(\log3)^2}{6}
\le\frac{\alpha_k}{2}k^2(\log3)^2
\le\frac{k^2(\log3)^2}{4}.
\]

For the admissible uniform source \(p\), this is also the variance of \(\log(\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}(\mathbf X)/p_{\mathbf X})\).

## Proof

Write a point of \(\mathbb R^r\) as \((s,\mathbf z)\). Taking the convex combination of a base point and the apex shows that

\[
P_k=\{(s,\mathbf z):0\le s\le2,
              \ |z_i|\le1-s/2\text{ for every }1\le i\le k\}.
\]

Indeed, at height \(s\), the apex coefficient must be \(s/2\), so the remaining base coefficient is \(1-s/2\). Conversely, if \(s<2\) satisfies the displayed inequalities, \(\mathbf z/(1-s/2)\) is a base point and gives this convex combination; at \(s=2\) the inequalities force \(\mathbf z=\mathbf0_k\). For an integral point, the only possible heights are \(s=0,1,2\). At height zero, all coordinates belong to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). At height one, \(|z_i|\le1/2\) forces every integral coordinate to be zero. At height two there is only the apex. This proves the claimed support and all its layers. Every base-cube vertex and the apex belong to \(D\), so \(\operatorname{conv}D=P_k\). The points \((0,\mathbf0_k)\), \((0,\mathbf e_i)\) for \(1\le i\le k\), and \((2,\mathbf0_k)\) are affinely independent, proving dimension \(r=k+1\). Its affine hull is all of \(\mathbb R^r\), and its intrinsic affine lattice is therefore \(\mathbb Z^r\). The support is not a Cartesian product of its height and remaining-coordinate projections: these contain \(1\) and \(\mathbf e_1\), respectively, but \((1,\mathbf e_1)\notin D\).

The base \(s=0\) is a supporting face. The other defining inequalities can be written \(s+2z_i\le2\) and \(s-2z_i\le2\). At a bottom root \((0,\mathbf y)\), equality in one of these inequalities holds exactly when the corresponding coordinate \(y_i\) is \(1\) or \(-1\). Intersecting the base face with these supporting equalities gives exactly \(F_{\mathbf j}\) in the statement, hence a face containing \(\mathbf j\). In its remaining free coordinates, \(\mathbf j\) has coordinate zero, so \(\mathbf j\) lies in its relative interior. This makes it the minimal face: for any point \(\mathbf x\) of this face, its relative interior property permits a point \(\mathbf w\) in the same face and a number \(0<\lambda<1\) such that \(\mathbf j=\lambda\mathbf x+(1-\lambda)\mathbf w\); any face containing \(\mathbf j\) must then contain \(\mathbf x\). The face dimension and face support are respectively \(f\) and \(3^f\). Choosing its \(f\) zero coordinates and then the signs of all other coordinates gives \(\binom{k}{f}2^{k-f}\) roots. Their total is \(\sum_{f=0}^k\binom{k}{f}2^{k-f}=3^k\).

The apex is the unique maximizer of the height functional \(s\), so its singleton is its minimal face. The middle point \((1,\mathbf0_k)\) satisfies \(0<s<2\) and all coordinate inequalities strictly, so it is interior and has minimal face \(P_k\). The support census already proves that no other lattice point can be interior. This accounts for all \(3^k+2\) roots and their minimal faces. The base-cube vertices remain vertices of \(P_k\), since the base is a face; together with the apex they are all its vertices by its defining convex hull. Thus, for \(k\ge2\), it has \(2^k+1>r+1\) vertices and is not a simplex.

For a law \(\mu\) on \(D\), write \(H(\mu):=-\sum_{\mathbf x:\mu_{\mathbf x}>0}\mu_{\mathbf x}\log\mu_{\mathbf x}\). If its mean is \(\mathbf j\), log-affinity gives the exact identity

\[
D(\mu\|p)
=-H(\mu)-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf j
   +\log\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}
=-H(\mu)-\log p_{\mathbf j}.
\]

Thus minimizing relative entropy is exactly maximizing entropy, independently of the source tilt. Accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 supplies uniqueness and the assertion that every feasible law at a root is supported on its minimal face support. This proves the stated identification with its entropy rows.

We use the elementary inequality \(D(\mu\|q)\ge0\) for a strictly positive law \(q\) on a finite support. For completeness, \(\log t\le t-1\) gives

\[
-D(\mu\|q)
=\sum_{\mathbf x:\mu_{\mathbf x}>0}
       \mu_{\mathbf x}\log\frac{q_{\mathbf x}}{\mu_{\mathbf x}}
\le\sum_{\mathbf x:\mu_{\mathbf x}>0}q_{\mathbf x}-1
\le0.
\]

Equality holds only when \(\mu=q\). For a bottom root, the uniform law on its \(3^f\)-point face support has the required mean, since each free coordinate has mean zero. For any feasible law on that support, its relative entropy to this uniform law is \(\log(3^f)-H(\mu)\). Hence this uniform law uniquely maximizes entropy. Its product description follows from its constant mass \(3^{-f}\) at each choice of the free coordinates. The apex has only its Dirac feasible law.

For the middle root, consider the positive law \(q\) on \(D\) given by \(q_{(s,\mathbf z)}=a_{\mathrm{base}}^{s/2}/Z_*\). There are \(a_{\mathrm{base}}\) bottom points, so its total unnormalized mass is \(a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+a_{\mathrm{base}}=Z_*\). Bottom symmetry gives mean zero in every \(\mathbf z\)-coordinate, and its height mean is \((\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+2a_{\mathrm{base}})/Z_*=1\).

For any law \(\mu\) with mean \((1,\mathbf0_k)\), the affine formula \(\log q_{(s,\mathbf z)}=(s/2)\log a_{\mathrm{base}}-\log Z_*\) yields \(D(\mu\|q)=-H(\mu)+\log Z_*-\tfrac12\log a_{\mathrm{base}}\). Since \(q\) has the required mean, this expression and nonnegativity of relative entropy prove that \(q\) is the unique maximum-entropy row. The point masses and layer probabilities in the statement follow. In particular, \(\alpha_k\ge1/3\), because \(\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}\ge1\), while \(\alpha_k<1/2\).

We next compute the moments of a uniform ternary cube projection. Let \(U_i\) be independent uniform random variables on \(\{-1,0,1\}\), indexed by any subset of the coordinates, and let \(T=\sum_i b_iU_i\). Each \(U_i\) has mean zero, vanishing odd moments, and \(\mathbb E U_i^2=\mathbb E U_i^4=2/3\). Independence therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
v:=\mathbb E T^2&=\frac23\sum_i b_i^2,\\
\mathbb E T^4
&=\frac23\sum_i b_i^4+\frac83\sum_{i<h}b_i^2b_h^2\\
&=3v^2-\frac23\sum_i b_i^4\le3v^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Also \(\mathbb E T=\mathbb E T^3=0\), since the law of \(T\) is symmetric. These formulas include an empty coordinate set and vanishing coefficients.

For a bottom root, the centered height and the centered fixed coordinates vanish identically. Every directional projection \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\) is therefore exactly such a sum over the free coordinates. The preceding formula proves the bound with constant three, including every zero-variance direction. It also covers bottom vertices, whose free coordinate set is empty. The apex row is Dirac, so its centered projections vanish as well.

For the middle row, write \(\mathbf X=(X_1,\mathbf Y)\) and \(\mathbf u=(t,\mathbf b)\). Its centered projection is \(W=t(X_1-1)+\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf Y\). Conditional on the bottom layer, \(W=-t+T\), where \(T\) is the uniform ternary cube projection with all \(k\) coordinates and \(v=(2/3)\sum_{i=1}^k b_i^2\). On the apex, \(W=t\), and on the middle atom, \(W=0\). The bottom and apex layers both have probability \(\alpha_k\), so \(\mathbb E W=0\). Expansion of the second and fourth powers, using the vanishing odd moments of \(T\), gives the full directional formulas

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(W)&=\alpha_k(2t^2+v),\\
\mathbb E W^4
&=\alpha_k\left(2t^4+6t^2v+3v^2
                   -\frac23\sum_{i=1}^k b_i^4\right)\\
&\le\alpha_k(2t^4+6t^2v+3v^2)\\
&\le3\alpha_k(2t^2+v)^2
 =\frac3{\alpha_k}\operatorname{Var}(W)^2
 \le9\operatorname{Var}(W)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The penultimate comparison follows from \(3(2t^2+v)^2-(2t^4+6t^2v+3v^2)=10t^4+6t^2v\ge0\). No step divides by the variance. In particular, if it is zero, \(\alpha_k>0\) forces \(t=0\) and \(v=0\), so \(W=0\) almost surely and the fourth moment is zero. These formulas establish the bound for every direction, with all mixed terms accounted for, and complete the row census and spatial moment assertion.

Finally, the middle row has \(\log\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}(\mathbf X)=(X_1/2)\log a_{\mathrm{base}}-\log Z_*\). Its height mean is one and its height variance is \(2\alpha_k\), since its heights zero and two each have mass \(\alpha_k\). Consequently its self-log-density variance is \((\log a_{\mathrm{base}})^2(2\alpha_k)/4=\alpha_k k^2(\log3)^2/2\). The bounds in the statement follow from \(1/3\le\alpha_k\le1/2\). For the uniform source, \(p_{\mathbf x}=1/(3^k+2)\), so \(\log(\nu_{(1,\mathbf0_k)}(\mathbf X)/p_{\mathbf X})\) differs from the self log density by a constant and has the same variance. This quadratic variance does not affect the independently proved absolute spatial fourth-moment constant.
