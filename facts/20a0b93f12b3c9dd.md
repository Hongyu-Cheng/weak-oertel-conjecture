## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(K,L\subset\mathbb R^n\) be nonempty compact convex unconditional sets. Here unconditional means that \((\epsilon_1x_1,\ldots,\epsilon_nx_n)\) belongs to the set whenever \(x\) does, for every \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\). No full-dimensionality hypothesis is imposed. Then
\[
\#\left(\frac{K+L}{2}\cap\mathbb Z^n\right)
\ge 2^{-n}\sqrt{\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^n)\,\#(L\cap\mathbb Z^n)}.
\]

## Proof

First, any convex unconditional set \(U\subset\mathbb R^n\) has the following coordinate contraction property: if \(x\in U\) and \(|v_i|\le |x_i|\) for every coordinate, then \(v\in U\). For a single coordinate \(i\), the segment joining \(x\) to its reflection in \(x_i=0\) belongs to \(U\) by convexity. It contains every vector obtained by replacing \(x_i\) by a number between \(-|x_i|\) and \(|x_i|\), with the other coordinates unchanged. Performing this operation successively in the \(n\) coordinates proves the property. In particular, a nonempty such set contains the origin. This argument works in any affine dimension and includes boundary points.

Put \(A=K\cap\mathbb Z^n\), \(B=L\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and \(M=(K+L)/2\). These sets are nonempty, and their lattice-point sets are finite by compactness. The set \(M\) is compact and convex. It is unconditional because applying the same coordinate sign flips to a point of \(K\) and a point of \(L\) applies those flips to their midpoint. Consequently \(M\) has the coordinate contraction property proved above.

Define the two finite rounded midpoint sets
\[
F=\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{a+b}{2}\right\rfloor:a\in A,\ b\in B\right\},
\qquad
E=\left\{\left\lceil\frac{a+b}{2}\right\rceil:a\in A,\ b\in B\right\},
\]
where floor and ceiling are applied separately in each coordinate. Theorem 1.2 of Halikias, Klartag, and Slomka, specialized to \(\lambda=1/2\), yields
\[
|A|\,|B|\le |F|\,|E|.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, apply that theorem to \(f=\mathbf1_A\), \(g=\mathbf1_B\), \(h=\mathbf1_F\), and \(k=\mathbf1_E\), all viewed as nonnegative functions on \(\mathbb Z^n\). Its pointwise hypothesis holds with equality when \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\), by the definitions of \(F\) and \(E\). For all other pairs, its left side is zero. All four sums are finite. Thus every hypothesis of the cited theorem is checked.

For integers \(q\), define
\[
u(q)=\begin{cases}q+1,&q<0,\\q,&q\ge0,\end{cases}
\qquad
v(q)=\begin{cases}q,&q\le0,\\q-1,&q>0.\end{cases}
\]
Apply these maps coordinatewise to integer vectors. For every real \(t\),
\[
|u(\lfloor t\rfloor)|\le |t|,
\qquad
|v(\lceil t\rceil)|\le |t|.
\tag{2}
\]
To verify the first inequality, if \(t\ge0\), then \(0\le\lfloor t\rfloor\le t\), and \(u\) fixes that integer. If \(t<0\), write \(q=\lfloor t\rfloor\le-1\). Then \(q\le t<q+1\le0\), so \(|u(q)|=|q+1|\le|t|\). For the second inequality, if \(t\le0\), then \(t\le\lceil t\rceil\le0\), and \(v\) fixes that integer. If \(t>0\), write \(q=\lceil t\rceil\ge1\). Then \(0\le q-1<t\le q\), so \(|v(q)|=q-1\le|t|\).

For every \(q\in F\), choose a pair \(a\in A,b\in B\) with \(q=\lfloor(a+b)/2\rfloor\). The midpoint \((a+b)/2\) belongs to \(M\), so (2) and the coordinate contraction property give \(u(q)\in M\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The same reasoning gives \(v(q)\in M\cap\mathbb Z^n\) for every \(q\in E\).

Every integer has at most two inverse images under \(u\): zero has inverse images \(-1,0\); a positive integer \(z\) has only \(z\); a negative integer \(z\) has only \(z-1\). Under \(v\), zero has inverse images \(0,1\); a positive integer \(z\) has only \(z+1\); a negative integer \(z\) has only \(z\). Therefore the coordinatewise maps each have at most \(2^n\) inverse images for any vector, and hence
\[
|F|\le2^n\#(M\cap\mathbb Z^n),
\qquad
|E|\le2^n\#(M\cap\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1) and (3), and taking nonnegative square roots, proves the stated inequality. No step uses positive Lebesgue volume, a nonempty interior, or a common affine hull for \(K\) and \(L\). Thus the same proof covers all the permitted lower-dimensional cases.

Exact theorem used: for \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) and nonnegative functions \(f,g,h,k\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\), the pointwise inequalities
\[
f(x)g(y)\le h(\lfloor\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\rfloor)
k(\lceil(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y\rceil)
\quad(x,y\in\mathbb Z^n)
\]
imply \((\sum f)(\sum g)\le(\sum h)(\sum k)\). Both integer-part operations are coordinatewise. We use \(\lambda=1/2\) and four finite-set indicators, as checked in the proof. The theorem has no symmetry, convexity, or full-dimensionality assumption on these supports and contains no added cube or other padding.
