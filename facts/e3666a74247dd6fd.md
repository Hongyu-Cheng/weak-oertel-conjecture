## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and retain one common instantiation of the canonical zero-fresh hard branch in accepted facts 6b09aae189415c70 and 6bfe50fed9a5540b. Thus, for

\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]

write

\[
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,\qquad
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\tag{1}
\]

For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put

\[
A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z.
\]

A positive-mass class is good when \(\max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\le A_\xi/4\). The half-strength canonical product reservoir \(r=(r_e)\) is supported on unordered distinct pairs in good classes and is given there by

\[
r_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
\qquad(u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi),
\tag{2}
\]

and it is zero elsewhere. Put

\[
Q:=|r|=\sum_er_e>0,\qquad
d_z(r):=\sum_{e\ni z}r_e\le\frac{a_z}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\), and use the restarted matching, loads, and canonical endpoint notation of accepted fact 6bfe50fed9a5540b:

\[
0\le\omega_e^b\le b_e\le r_e,\qquad
\Omega_b:=\sum_e\omega_e^b>\frac{97}{100}Q,
\qquad G<\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{4}
\]

\[
\beta_z^b:=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e^b,\qquad
E_b:=\{z:\beta_z^b>0\},\qquad
\sum_z\beta_z^b=2\Omega_b,
\tag{5}
\]

with points \(p_z^b\) satisfying

\[
p_z^b\in\mathcal K_z\cap\mathsf M_{\beta_z^b}(C_z),
\qquad
p_z^b+\delta_*D_z\subseteq\mathsf M_{a_z/4}(C_z),
\tag{6}
\]

where

\[
\delta_*:=\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}-1>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The accepted endpoint defect is

\[
\inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\sum_{z\in E_b}\beta_z^b\|p_z^b-a(z)\|_{D_z}
\ge\delta_*(2\Omega_b-G).
\tag{8}
\]

Define the reservoir endpoint set and the reservoir-wide common body by

\[
Z_r:=\{z\in Z:d_z(r)>0\},\qquad
\widehat D:=\bigcap_{z:\,d_z(r)>0}D_z
=\bigcap_{z\in Z_r}D_z.
\tag{9}
\]

Then \(Z_r\) is finite and nonempty, and \(\widehat D\) is a compact full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body. In particular,

\[
\widehat D\subseteq D_z\qquad(z\in Z_r).
\tag{10}
\]

Moreover \(E_b\subseteq Z_r\), and the common-gauge endpoint defect

\[
\widehat{\mathfrak D}
:=
\inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
\sum_{z\in E_b}\beta_z^b
\|p_z^b-a(z)\|_{\widehat D}
\tag{11}
\]

satisfies

\[
\widehat{\mathfrak D}
\ge\delta_*(2\Omega_b-G).
\tag{12}
\]

For \(z\in Z_r\), put

\[
\bar\beta_z:=d_z(r).
\tag{13}
\]

For every good class \(\xi\), let

\[
I_\xi:=\{z\in Z:z\equiv\xi\},\qquad
\bar L_\xi:=\sum_{z\in I_\xi}\bar\beta_z.
\tag{14}
\]

Then every \(\bar\beta_z\) in \(I_\xi\) is positive and

\[
\max_{z\in I_\xi}\bar\beta_z\le\frac{\bar L_\xi}{4}.
\tag{15}
\]

Define a new reservoir-degree product matching \(\widehat s\), distinct from the retained matching denoted \(s\) in accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70, by

\[
\widehat s_{\{u,v\}}
:=\frac{\bar\beta_u\bar\beta_v}{\bar L_\xi}
\qquad(u<v,\ u,v\in I_\xi),
\tag{16}
\]

and set it to zero elsewhere. Write \(s_{uv}:=\widehat s_{\{u,v\}}\) for its positive edge coordinates. Its source degrees and total mass satisfy

\[
d_u(\widehat s)
=\bar\beta_u\left(1-\frac{\bar\beta_u}{\bar L_\xi}\right)
\le\bar\beta_u=d_u(r)\le\frac{a_u}{4},
\tag{17}
\]

\[
|\widehat s|\ge\frac34Q.
\tag{18}
\]

Put

\[
\varepsilon:=\frac{\delta_*}{16},\qquad
q_z:=
\begin{cases}
p_z^b,&z\in E_b,\\
c_z,&z\in Z_r\setminus E_b.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]

Every reservoir endpoint, active or auxiliary, has the legal tube

\[
q_z+\varepsilon\widehat D
\subseteq\mathsf M_{a_z/4}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in Z_r).
\tag{20}
\]

Consequently, for each positive edge \(\{u,v\}\) of \(\widehat s\),

\[
q_u+\varepsilon\widehat D\subseteq\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_u),
\qquad
q_v+\varepsilon\widehat D\subseteq\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_v).
\tag{21}
\]

Since \(u\equiv v\pmod2\), the midpoint

\[
m_{uv}:=\frac{u+v}{2}
\tag{22}
\]

is integral. Define

\[
K_{uv}:=\frac{q_u+q_v}{2}+\varepsilon\widehat D.
\tag{23}
\]

Then the positive-root interpolation of accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e gives the genuine midpoint-fiber tube

\[
K_{uv}\subseteq\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_{m_{uv}}).
\tag{24}
\]

The endpoint witnesses are simultaneously source-feasible in the following exact sense. For each \(z\in Z_r\), choose arbitrarily one point

\[
x_{zv}\in q_z+\varepsilon\widehat D
\]

for every positive incident edge \(\{z,v\}\). There are measurable densities \(f_{zv}\) supported on \(C_z\) such that

\[
0\le\sum_{v:\,s_{zv}>0}f_{zv}\le\mathbf1_{C_z}\quad\text{a.e.},
\qquad
\int f_{zv}=s_{zv},
\qquad
\frac1{s_{zv}}\int x f_{zv}(x)\,dx=x_{zv}.
\tag{25}
\]

Finally, there are vectors \(U_z\in\mathbb R^d\) for \(z\in Z_r\) and symmetric edge vectors \(v_{uv}=v_{vu}\in\mathbb R^d\) on the positive edges of \(\widehat s\) such that

\[
U_z=0\quad(z\in Z_r\setminus E_b),
\qquad
h_{\widehat D}(U_z)\le\beta_z^b\quad(z\in E_b),
\tag{26}
\]

\[
\sum_{z\in Z_r}U_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in Z_r}U_zz^{\mathsf T}=0,
\qquad
-\sum_{z\in E_b}\langle U_z,p_z^b\rangle
=\widehat{\mathfrak D},
\tag{27}
\]

\[
\sum_{v:\,s_{uv}>0}v_{uv}=\frac18U_u,
\qquad
h_{\widehat D}(v_{uv})\le s_{uv},
\tag{28}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{\{u,v\}}v_{uv}=0,
\qquad
\sum_{\{u,v\}}v_{uv}m_{uv}^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{29}
\]

Their total support is strictly negative at the macroscopic scale:

\[
\boxed{
-\sum_{\{u,v\}}h_{K_{uv}}(v_{uv})
>\frac{3\delta_*}{50}Q.}
\tag{30}
\]

All edge sums in (28)-(30) range over the positive edges of \(\widehat s\). The midpoint inclusions (24) are individual inclusions. No simultaneous target-fiber packing of witnesses belonging to different edges is asserted. No positive gauge-load lower bound is asserted; in particular, no bound of the form \(\sum h_{\widehat D}(v_{uv})\ge cQ\) with \(c>0\) is claimed. No \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\) certificate, packet-energy certificate, or terminal closure of the weak Oertel conjecture follows from this theorem.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes \(Z\) finite. Accepted fact 6b09aae189415c70 proves that every \(D_z\) in (1) is a compact origin-symmetric convex body containing the origin in its interior and that the reservoir has the exact formula (2). Since \(Q>0\), the set \(Z_r\) is nonempty. For each \(z\in Z_r\), choose \(\rho_z>0\) such that \(\rho_zB_2^d\subseteq D_z\). Finiteness gives

\[
\left(\min_{z\in Z_r}\rho_z\right)B_2^d
\subseteq\widehat D.
\tag{31}
\]

Thus \(\widehat D\) has nonempty interior. It is compact, convex, and origin-symmetric because it is a nonempty finite intersection of bodies with those properties. Equation (10) is immediate from its definition. If \(z\in E_b\), then some incident \(\omega_e^b\) is positive; (4) gives \(r_e>0\), so \(d_z(r)>0\). Hence \(E_b\subseteq Z_r\).

For origin-symmetric convex bodies \(D\subseteq D'\), their Minkowski gauges satisfy \(\|x\|_D\ge\|x\|_{D'}\). Applying this to \(\widehat D\subseteq D_z\) at every \(z\in E_b\), then taking the infimum over the same affine maps in (8) and (11), proves

\[
\widehat{\mathfrak D}
\ge
\inf_a\sum_{z\in E_b}\beta_z^b
\|p_z^b-a(z)\|_{D_z}
\ge\delta_*(2\Omega_b-G).
\tag{32}
\]

This is precisely why replacing the endpoint bodies by the smaller reservoir-wide body strengthens, rather than weakens, the zero-fresh affine defect.

We now prove the common-norm affine-regression dual from first principles. Set

\[
X:=(\mathbb R^d)^{E_b},\qquad
N((x_z)_z):=\sum_{z\in E_b}\beta_z^b\|x_z\|_{\widehat D},
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal V:=\{(a(z))_{z\in E_b}:a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d
\text{ affine}\}.
\]

All \(\beta_z^b\) are positive, so \(N\) is a norm. The set \(\mathcal V\) is a finite-dimensional linear subspace of \(X\), hence closed. On the quotient \(X/\mathcal V\), define

\[
\|[x]\|_q:=\inf_{v\in\mathcal V}N(x-v).
\tag{33}
\]

This is well defined on cosets, is homogeneous, and satisfies the triangle inequality. If \(\|[x]\|_q=0\), a sequence \(v_k\in\mathcal V\) satisfies \(N(x-v_k)\to0\), so \(v_k\to x\); closedness gives \(x\in\mathcal V\). Thus (33) is a norm. The infimum is also attained: a minimizing sequence may be taken with \(N(x-v_k)\le N(x)+1\), whence

\[
N(v_k)\le N(x)+N(x-v_k)\le2N(x)+1.
\]

It has a convergent subsequence in the finite-dimensional space \(\mathcal V\), and continuity of \(N\) gives a minimizer.

Put \(p:=(p_z^b)_{z\in E_b}\). Then

\[
\|[p]\|_q=\widehat{\mathfrak D}>0
\tag{34}
\]

by (11)-(12). We next give the finite-dimensional extension argument that produces a norming functional on \(X/\mathcal V\). More generally, let \(Y\) be a finite-dimensional real normed space with norm \(\nu\), let \(M\subsetneq Y\) be a subspace, and suppose a linear functional \(f\) on \(M\) satisfies \(f(m)\le\nu(m)\) for all \(m\in M\). Choose \(y_0\notin M\). An extension of the form

\[
F(m+ty_0):=f(m)+tc
\tag{35}
\]

is dominated by \(\nu\) if \(c\) is chosen in the interval

\[
\sup_{m\in M}\bigl(f(m)-\nu(m-y_0)\bigr)
\le c\le
\inf_{m\in M}\bigl(\nu(m+y_0)-f(m)\bigr).
\tag{36}
\]

This interval is nonempty. Indeed, for arbitrary \(m_1,m_2\in M\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
f(m_1)-\nu(m_1-y_0)
&\le \nu(m_2+y_0)-f(m_2)
\end{aligned}
\]

because

\[
f(m_1)+f(m_2)
=f(m_1+m_2)
\le\nu(m_1+m_2)
\le\nu(m_1-y_0)+\nu(m_2+y_0).
\tag{37}
\]

The value \(m=0\) shows that both bounding families contain finite numbers, and (37) bounds every member of the left family by every member of the right family. Hence the supremum and infimum in (36) are finite and occur in the stated order.

For \(t>0\), the inequality \(F(m+ty_0)\le\nu(m+ty_0)\), after division by \(t\), is exactly the upper inequality in (36) with \(m/t\) in place of \(m\). For \(t<0\), division by \(-t\) gives the lower inequality, and \(t=0\) uses the hypothesis on \(f\). Hence (35) is a dominated extension. Repeating this one-dimensional step along a basis of a complement of \(M\) extends \(f\) to all of \(Y\) without changing the domination.

Apply this extension argument to \(Y=X/\mathcal V\), \(\nu=\|\cdot\|_q\), and the one-dimensional subspace spanned by \([p]\). Define there

\[
\ell_0(t[p]):=t\|[p]\|_q.
\]

It satisfies \(\ell_0(y)\le\|y\|_q\), including for negative \(t\), because in fact \(|\ell_0(t[p])|=\|t[p]\|_q\). The preceding construction supplies a linear functional \(\ell\) on \(X/\mathcal V\) such that

\[
|\ell(y)|\le\|y\|_q\quad(y\in X/\mathcal V),
\qquad
\ell([p])=\|[p]\|_q.
\tag{38}
\]

The absolute-value bound follows by applying the domination to both \(y\) and \(-y\). Lift \(\ell\) to \(X\) by

\[
L(x):=\ell([x]).
\tag{39}
\]

Then \(L\) vanishes on \(\mathcal V\), and, because the choice \(v=0\) in (33) gives \(\|[x]\|_q\le N(x)\),

\[
|L(x)|\le N(x)\qquad(x\in X),
\qquad
L(p)=\widehat{\mathfrak D}.
\tag{40}
\]

Every linear functional on \(X\) has unique coordinate vectors \(W_z\in\mathbb R^d\) such that

\[
L((x_z)_z)=\sum_{z\in E_b}\langle W_z,x_z\rangle.
\tag{41}
\]

We verify exactly how the weighted norm gives coordinate caps. For any \(w,x\in\mathbb R^d\), origin symmetry of \(\widehat D\) gives

\[
|\langle w,x\rangle|
\le h_{\widehat D}(w)\|x\|_{\widehat D}.
\tag{42}
\]

Indeed, after dividing by \(\|x\|_{\widehat D}\), the resulting point lies in \(\widehat D\), and symmetry handles the absolute value. If (40) holds, test it on vectors supported in one coordinate \(z\). Taking the supremum over \(x_z\in\widehat D\) gives

\[
h_{\widehat D}(W_z)\le\beta_z^b.
\tag{43}
\]

Conversely, (42) shows that the coordinate inequalities (43) imply \(|L(x)|\le N(x)\). More generally this proves the exact weighted dual-norm identity

\[
\|L\|_{N,*}
=\max_{z\in E_b}\frac{h_{\widehat D}(W_z)}{\beta_z^b}.
\tag{44}
\]

Indeed, if the maximum on the right is \(\lambda\), then (42) gives \(|L(x)|\le\lambda N(x)\), so \(\|L\|_{N,*}\le\lambda\). Conversely, the definition of the dual norm gives \(|\langle W_z,y\rangle|\le\|L\|_{N,*}\beta_z^b\) for every \(y\in\widehat D\) by testing a vector supported only in coordinate \(z\). Taking the supremum over \(y\in\widehat D\) gives \(h_{\widehat D}(W_z)/\beta_z^b\le\|L\|_{N,*}\) for every \(z\), proving the reverse inequality.

We also identify the annihilator without omission. Write an affine map as \(a(z)=Az+c\), where \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and \(c\in\mathbb R^d\). Equation (41) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
L((a(z))_z)
&=\left\langle\sum_zW_z,c\right\rangle
+\sum_z\langle W_z,Az\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]

The second term is the Frobenius pairing of \(A\) with \(\sum_zW_zz^{\mathsf T}\). Since \(c\) and \(A\) are arbitrary, \(L\) vanishes on \(\mathcal V\) if and only if

\[
\sum_{z\in E_b}W_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in E_b}W_zz^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{46}
\]

Define \(U_z:=-W_z\) on \(E_b\). Equations (34), (38), and (40)-(46) give

\[
h_{\widehat D}(U_z)\le\beta_z^b,\qquad
\sum_zU_z=0,\qquad
\sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}=0,
\qquad
-\sum_z\langle U_z,p_z^b\rangle
=\widehat{\mathfrak D}.
\tag{47}
\]

For completeness, these vectors attain the full dual, not merely a lower bound. If \((\widetilde U_z)\) satisfies the two balance equations and the coordinate caps, then for every affine \(a\), (42) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\sum_z\langle\widetilde U_z,p_z^b\rangle
&=-\sum_z\langle\widetilde U_z,p_z^b-a(z)\rangle\\
&\le\sum_z\beta_z^b\|p_z^b-a(z)\|_{\widehat D}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]

Taking the infimum over \(a\) bounds every dual-feasible objective by \(\widehat{\mathfrak D}\), while (47) achieves equality. Thus the distance-to-affine-subspace dual and its attainment have been proved entirely within the packet. Extend the attaining field by \(U_z=0\) on \(Z_r\setminus E_b\). This proves (26)-(27). Put

\[
H:=\sum_{z\in Z_r}h_{\widehat D}(U_z).
\]

The dual caps and (5) give

\[
H\le\sum_{z\in E_b}\beta_z^b=2\Omega_b.
\tag{49}
\]

We next prove the reservoir quarter-cap. Fix a good class \(\xi\), abbreviate \(A:=A_\xi\), and put \(x_z:=a_z/A\) for \(z\in I_\xi\). Then \(0<x_z\le1/4\) and \(\sum_zx_z=1\). The degree formula obtained directly from (2) is

\[
\bar\beta_z
=\frac{A}{4}x_z(1-x_z),
\qquad
\bar L_\xi
=\frac{A}{4}\left(1-\sum_zx_z^2\right).
\tag{50}
\]

In particular, every \(\bar\beta_z\) is positive. Moreover,

\[
\sum_{w\ne z}x_w^2
\le\frac14\sum_{w\ne z}x_w
=\frac{1-x_z}{4},
\]

so

\[
1-\sum_wx_w^2
\ge\frac{3+x_z-4x_z^2}{4}
\ge4x_z(1-x_z).
\tag{51}
\]

The last inequality follows because the difference between its two sides is

\[
\frac34(1-x_z)(1-4x_z)\ge0.
\]

Equations (50)-(51) give \(\bar L_\xi\ge4\bar\beta_z\), proving (15).

For the matching (16), direct summation gives (17), while

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat s|_\xi
&=\frac{1}{2\bar L_\xi}
\left(\bar L_\xi^2-\sum_{z\in I_\xi}\bar\beta_z^2\right)\\
&\ge\frac{1}{2\bar L_\xi}
\left(\bar L_\xi^2-\frac{\bar L_\xi^2}{4}\right)
=\frac38\bar L_\xi,
\end{aligned}
\tag{52}
\]

where (15) implies \(\sum_z\bar\beta_z^2\le(\max_z\bar\beta_z)\bar L_\xi\le\bar L_\xi^2/4\). Every reservoir edge lies in one good class, and each edge is counted twice by the source degrees. Hence

\[
\sum_{\xi\ {\rm good}}\bar L_\xi=2Q.
\tag{53}
\]

Summing (52) proves (18).

We prove the tube assertions. Since

\[
2<(4/3)^3<4,
\]

one has \(0<\delta_*<1\), and therefore \(0<\varepsilon<1\). If \(z\in E_b\), then \(\widehat D\subseteq D_z\), so

\[
q_z+\varepsilon\widehat D
\subseteq p_z^b+\delta_*D_z
\subseteq\mathsf M_{a_z/4}(C_z)
\tag{54}
\]

by (6). If \(z\in Z_r\setminus E_b\), then \(q_z=c_z\), and

\[
q_z+\varepsilon\widehat D
\subseteq c_z+D_z
=\mathcal K_z
=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z).
\]

Multiplying any representing density by \(1/2\) preserves its normalized first moment and lowers its mass from \(a_z/2\) to \(a_z/4\). This proves (20) also at every auxiliary endpoint. Notice that only \(\widehat D\subseteq D_z\), not the common body attached to a different endpoint set, has been used here.

More generally, if \(x\in\mathsf M_m(K)\) and \(0<m'\le m\), multiplying a representing density by \(m'/m\) proves \(x\in\mathsf M_{m'}(K)\). Equations (17) and (20) therefore prove (21). For \(h\in\varepsilon\widehat D\), both \(q_u+h\in\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_u)\) and \(q_v+h\in\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_v)\). Apply the positive-mass generating-set convexity proved in accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e to the two triples

\[
(u,q_u+h,s_{uv}^{1/d}),
\qquad
(v,q_v+h,s_{uv}^{1/d}).
\]

Their midpoint has the same positive root coordinate and gives

\[
\frac{q_u+q_v}{2}+h
\in\mathsf M_{s_{uv}}(C_{(u+v)/2}).
\]

This holds for every \(h\in\varepsilon\widehat D\), proving (24). Integrality of the target follows from same parity.

For simultaneous source feasibility, fix \(z\) and points \(x_{zv}\) as in (25). By (20), choose \(g_{zv}\) with \(0\le g_{zv}\le\mathbf1_{C_z}\), mass \(a_z/4\), and barycenter \(x_{zv}\). Set

\[
f_{zv}:=\frac{4s_{zv}}{a_z}g_{zv}.
\]

Then \(f_{zv}\) has mass \(s_{zv}\) and the same barycenter, and (17) gives

\[
\sum_vf_{zv}
\le\frac{4d_z(\widehat s)}{a_z}\mathbf1_{C_z}
\le\mathbf1_{C_z}.
\tag{55}
\]

This proves (25).

It remains to construct and estimate the stress. For \(z\in E_b\), equations (4)-(5) give

\[
\beta_z^b=\sum_{e\ni z}\omega_e^b
\le\sum_{e\ni z}r_e
=\bar\beta_z.
\tag{56}
\]

For auxiliary endpoints \(U_z=0\), so \(h_{\widehat D}(U_z)=0\le\bar\beta_z\). Thus, on each good parity class, the weights \((\bar\beta_z)_{z\in I_\xi}\), the quarter-cap (15), the common body \(\widehat D\), and the vectors \((U_z)_{z\in I_\xi}\) satisfy every hypothesis of accepted fact 5e659eb8f0568a54. Apply that fact classwise. It gives symmetric vectors \(V_{uv}\) with

\[
\sum_{v\in I_\xi\setminus\{u\}}V_{uv}=2U_u,
\tag{57}
\]

\[
h_{\widehat D}(V_{uv})
\le16\frac{\bar\beta_u\bar\beta_v}{\bar L_\xi}
=16s_{uv},
\tag{58}
\]

and, after summing its classwise total-gauge estimate over the good classes,

\[
\sum_{\{u,v\}}h_{\widehat D}(V_{uv})
\le8H.
\tag{59}
\]

Define, with the exact scaling,

\[
v_{uv}:=\frac1{16}V_{uv}.
\tag{60}
\]

Equations (57)-(58) prove (28), and (59) gives

\[
\sum_{\{u,v\}}h_{\widehat D}(v_{uv})\le\frac H2.
\tag{61}
\]

Each unordered edge is counted twice in the row sums, so (27), (57), and (60) yield

\[
\sum_{\{u,v\}}v_{uv}
=\frac12\sum_u\sum_{v\ne u}v_{uv}
=\frac1{16}\sum_uU_u=0.
\tag{62}
\]

Likewise,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\{u,v\}}v_{uv}m_{uv}^{\mathsf T}
&=\frac12\sum_{\{u,v\}}v_{uv}(u+v)^{\mathsf T}\\
&=\frac12\sum_u
\left(\sum_{v\ne u}v_{uv}\right)u^{\mathsf T}\\
&=\frac1{16}\sum_uU_uu^{\mathsf T}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{63}
\]

This proves the exact balances (29).

Finally, (23) gives

\[
h_{K_{uv}}(v_{uv})
=\left\langle v_{uv},\frac{q_u+q_v}{2}\right\rangle
+\varepsilon h_{\widehat D}(v_{uv}).
\tag{64}
\]

The translation part sums exactly to

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\{u,v\}}
\left\langle v_{uv},\frac{q_u+q_v}{2}\right\rangle
&=\frac12\sum_u
\left\langle\sum_{v\ne u}v_{uv},q_u\right\rangle\\
&=\frac1{16}\sum_u\langle U_u,q_u\rangle
=-\frac1{16}\widehat{\mathfrak D}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{65}
\]

The last equality uses \(q_z=p_z^b\) on \(E_b\), \(U_z=0\) off \(E_b\), and (27). Combining (32), (49), (61), and \(\varepsilon=\delta_*/16\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\{u,v\}}h_{K_{uv}}(v_{uv})
&\le-\frac{\widehat{\mathfrak D}}{16}
+\frac{\varepsilon H}{2}\\
&\le-\frac{\delta_*(2\Omega_b-G)}{16}
+\frac{\delta_*\Omega_b}{16}\\
&=-\frac{\delta_*(\Omega_b-G)}{16}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{66}
\]

By (4),

\[
\Omega_b-G>
\left(\frac{97}{100}-\frac1{100}\right)Q
=\frac{24}{25}Q.
\tag{67}
\]

Negating (66) and using (67) proves

\[
-\sum_{\{u,v\}}h_{K_{uv}}(v_{uv})
>\frac{\delta_*}{16}\frac{24}{25}Q
=\frac{3\delta_*}{50}Q,
\]

which is (30). The construction used source-density feasibility only in (25); positive-root interpolation was applied edge by edge. It therefore supplies none of the explicitly excluded target-packing, gauge-load, off-diagonal-centroid, packet-energy, or terminal interfaces.
