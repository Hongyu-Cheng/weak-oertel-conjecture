## Statement

Let \(r,d\geq1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and for \(z\in\mathbb Z^r\) set
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=|C_z|_d.
\]
Fix \(\epsilon\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r\) such that
\[
M_\epsilon:=\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod 2}a_z>0.
\]
For \(c\in\mathbb Z^r\), define
\[
S_c^\epsilon:=\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2}a_za_{2c-z},
\qquad
b_c^\epsilon:=\frac{S_c^\epsilon}{M_\epsilon}.
\]
Whenever \(a_z>0\), let
\[
g_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx
\]
be the centroid of \(C_z\). For every \(c\) with \(S_c^\epsilon>0\), define
\[
q_c^\epsilon
:=
\frac1{S_c^\epsilon}
\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2}
a_za_{2c-z}\frac{g_z+g_{2c-z}}2;
\]
only indices for which both volumes are positive occur in this sum. For a bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(m>0\), write
\[
\mathsf M_m(K)
:=
\left\{
\frac1m\int_{\mathbb R^d}x f(x)\,dx:
0\leq f\leq\mathbf1_K\ \text{a.e.},\ \int_{\mathbb R^d}f=m
\right\}.
\]
Then, for every \(c\) with \(S_c^\epsilon>0\),
\[
q_c^\epsilon\in\mathsf M_{b_c^\epsilon}(C_c),
\qquad
\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z^r}b_c^\epsilon=M_\epsilon.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\beta_\epsilon
:=
\sum_{c:S_c^\epsilon>0}
b_c^\epsilon\,\delta_{(c,q_c^\epsilon)}
\]
is a finite measure of mass \(M_\epsilon\), carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d)\). If
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^r}|C_z\cap H_z|_d,
\qquad
H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\},
\]
then every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{r+d}\) satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\gamma_d\,\beta_\epsilon(H),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

## Proof

The projection of the compact set \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^r\) is compact and therefore meets \(\mathbb Z^r\) in finitely many points. Thus only finitely many fibers are nonempty, all sums below are finite, and every first moment used below is finite. Apply accepted fact `aca624dd8874e5a5` with its integer dimension \(n=r\), its weights \(w_z=a_z\), and its positive parity class equal to \(\epsilon\). Its normalized parity-midpoint measure has total mass \(M_\epsilon\), and its mass in target fiber \(c\) is exactly \(S_c^\epsilon/M_\epsilon=b_c^\epsilon\). That accepted scalar capacity and bookkeeping result gives
\[
0\leq b_c^\epsilon\leq a_c
\quad\text{for every }c,
\qquad
\sum_cb_c^\epsilon=M_\epsilon.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, only finitely many \(b_c^\epsilon\) are positive. Equation (1) is invoked from the accepted predecessor rather than reproved here.

Fix a target \(c\) with \(S:=S_c^\epsilon>0\), and put
\[
I_c:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^r:z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2,\ a_za_{2c-z}>0\}.
\]
This is a nonempty finite set. For \(z\in I_c\), abbreviate \(w:=2c-z\), and set
\[
p_z:=\frac{g_z+g_w}{2},
\qquad
t_z:=\frac{a_z^{1/d}+a_w^{1/d}}2,
\qquad
\lambda_z:=\frac{a_za_w}{S}.
\tag{2}
\]
The weights \(\lambda_z\) are positive and sum to one. Both endpoint fibers in (2) have positive \(d\)-volume, so they are full-dimensional compact convex bodies and their centroids are defined by the displayed uniform first moments.

Accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` proves, before taking the closure appearing in its statement, that the positive-root set
\[
G:=\{(z,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_z)\}
\]
is convex. The uniform dominated density \(\mathbf1_{C_z}\) shows
\[
(z,g_z,a_z^{1/d})\in G,
\]
and similarly \((w,g_w,a_w^{1/d})\in G\). Their equal-weight convex combination therefore gives the exact lift point
\[
(c,p_z,t_z)\in G.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining all the points (3) with the prescribed collision weights \(\lambda_z\), and using finite convexity of \(G\), yields
\[
\left(c,q_c^\epsilon,\widetilde s_c\right)
=
\sum_{z\in I_c}\lambda_z(c,p_z,t_z)
\in G,
\qquad
\widetilde s_c:=\sum_{z\in I_c}\lambda_z t_z.
\tag{4}
\]
The second coordinate in (4) is exactly \(q_c^\epsilon\), including the two occurrences of a reflected pair when its endpoints are distinct. Hence
\[
q_c^\epsilon\in\mathsf M_{\widetilde b_c}(C_c),
\qquad
\widetilde b_c:=\widetilde s_c^d.
\tag{5}
\]

It remains to compare this root mass with the desired scalar mass. For \(z\in I_c\), let
\[
u_z:=\sqrt{a_za_{2c-z}}>0,
\qquad
U_p:=\sum_{z\in I_c}u_z^p.
\]
Thus \(U_2=S\). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality in (2) gives \(t_z\geq u_z^{1/d}\), and therefore
\[
\widetilde s_c
\geq
\frac{U_{2+1/d}}{S}.
\tag{6}
\]
The reflected-pair power-sum estimate follows directly from Holder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(d+1\) and \((d+1)/d\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_2
&=
\sum_{z\in I_c}
u_z^{1/(d+1)}
\left(u_z^{2+1/d}\right)^{d/(d+1)}\\
&\leq
U_1^{1/(d+1)}
U_{2+1/d}^{d/(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
U_{2+1/d}
\geq
\frac{S^{1+1/d}}{U_1^{1/d}}.
\tag{7}
\]
The reflection \(z\mapsto2c-z\) is a bijection of the parity class \(\epsilon\) onto itself. Extending the sum by its zero terms and applying Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_1
&=
\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2}
\sqrt{a_za_{2c-z}}\\
&\leq
\left(\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2}a_z\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{z\equiv\epsilon\pmod2}a_{2c-z}\right)^{1/2}
=M_\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (6), (7), and (8) yields
\[
\widetilde s_c
\geq
\frac{S^{1/d}}{M_\epsilon^{1/d}}
=
\left(b_c^\epsilon\right)^{1/d},
\qquad
\widetilde b_c\geq b_c^\epsilon.
\tag{9}
\]

By (5), there is a measurable \(f\) with
\[
0\leq f\leq\mathbf1_{C_c},
\qquad
\int f=\widetilde b_c,
\qquad
\int x f(x)\,dx=\widetilde b_c q_c^\epsilon.
\]
Since \(0<b_c^\epsilon\leq\widetilde b_c\), the scaled dominated density
\[
f_0:=\frac{b_c^\epsilon}{\widetilde b_c}f
\]
still satisfies \(0\leq f_0\leq\mathbf1_{C_c}\), has mass \(b_c^\epsilon\), and has first moment \(b_c^\epsilon q_c^\epsilon\). Consequently
\[
q_c^\epsilon\in\mathsf M_{b_c^\epsilon}(C_c).
\tag{10}
\]
This proves the genuinely new first-moment realization.

For every graph atom, (1) gives \(a_c\geq b_c^\epsilon>0\). Thus \(C_c\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, applied to (10), shows both that \(q_c^\epsilon\in C_c\) and that every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(q_c^\epsilon\) satisfies
\[
|C_c\cap J|_d\geq\gamma_d b_c^\epsilon,
\tag{11}
\]
including the case in which \(q_c^\epsilon\) lies on the boundary of \(J\) and, under the convention allowing it, the improper whole-space halfspace. Together with (1), this proves that \(\beta_\epsilon\) is finite, has mass \(M_\epsilon\), and is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^r\times\mathbb R^d)\).

Now let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{r+d}\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace. After reversing its normal if necessary, write
\[
H=\{(z,x):\langle\alpha,z\rangle+\langle v,x\rangle\geq\tau\}
\]
with \((\alpha,v)\ne0\). Its section in fiber \(c\) is
\[
H_c=\{x:\langle v,x\rangle\geq\tau-\langle\alpha,c\rangle\}.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(v\ne0\), (12) is a proper closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^d\). If \(v=0\), it is either all of \(\mathbb R^d\) or empty; whenever \((c,q_c^\epsilon)\in H\), it is the whole space. Thus, for every graph atom lying in \(H\), the section \(H_c\) is a closed halfspace containing \(q_c^\epsilon\), so (11) applies. Summing only over those fibers and retaining the nonnegative contributions of every other fiber gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_C(H)
&=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^r}|C_z\cap H_z|_d\\
&\geq
\sum_{\substack{c:S_c^\epsilon>0\\(c,q_c^\epsilon)\in H}}
|C_c\cap H_c|_d\\
&\geq
\gamma_d
\sum_{\substack{c:S_c^\epsilon>0\\(c,q_c^\epsilon)\in H}}
b_c^\epsilon
=
\gamma_d\beta_\epsilon(H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
This treats both orientations by the choice of normal, all boundary equalities because the halfspaces are closed, and the vertical case \(v=0\) through its whole-or-empty fiber sections. If the whole ambient space is admitted as an improper halfspace, (13) follows from (11) fiber by fiber, or directly from \(a_c\geq b_c^\epsilon\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\); the empty improper halfspace has both sides zero.

Finally, if \(S_c^\epsilon=0\), then \(b_c^\epsilon=0\), no centroid \(q_c^\epsilon\) is required, and no graph atom is placed in that fiber. Empty or nonempty lower-dimensional endpoint fibers have \(a_z=0\), so they never occur in \(I_c\) and their centroids are never used. A positive collision target satisfies \(a_c>0\) by (1), so no zero-volume or lower-dimensional target fiber is passed to `6769574fc1bd9f8d`. These observations cover all zero-volume conventions. The proof establishes only the conditional first-moment realization and the pointwise halfspace domination (13). It does not establish a mixed-depth lower bound for \(\beta_\epsilon\); the graph mixed-depth step and the parity-repacking centerpoint lemma remain unproved.
