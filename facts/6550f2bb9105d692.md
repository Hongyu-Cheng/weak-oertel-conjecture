## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and take the three closed halfplanes
$$
H_0=\{x+y\le3/5\},\qquad H_1=\{3x-y\ge3/5\},\qquad H_2=\{3y-x\ge3/5\}.
$$
They form a minimal positive normal circuit and cover the plane. Their proper chords in $T$ have the common midpoint $(3/10,3/10)$. The cap fractions are $(9/25,12/25,12/25)$ and the pair fractions in order $01,02,12$ are $(3/25,3/25,2/25)$. The triple intersection is the common midpoint. The unweighted multiplicity overlap is $(8/25)|T|$, which is $|T|/75$ below $|T|/3$, although all cap fractions are below $1/2$. If $S_i$ are chord lengths and $u_i$ the inward unit normals, the probability vector $(1/4,3/8,3/8)$ balances $\sum_i\theta_i S_i u_i=0$ and gives the weighted fraction $9/20$.

## Proof

The displayed inward margins satisfy $2L_0+L_1+L_2=0$ and any two of their normals are independent. Thus at least one margin is nonnegative everywhere, and all three can be nonnegative only at their common zero $(3/10,3/10)$.

The boundary lines cut $T$ in chords with endpoint pairs $((3/5,0),(0,3/5))$, $((1/5,0),(2/5,3/5))$, and $((0,1/5),(3/5,2/5))$. Their midpoints coincide. The cap of $H_0$ is the origin triangle with normalized area $(3/5)^2$. The cap of $H_1$ has the chord endpoints and $(1,0)$ as vertices, hence fraction $(4/5)(3/5)$. The last fraction follows by reflection exchanging the coordinate axes. The $01$ pair triangle has vertices $(1/5,0),(3/5,0),(3/10,3/10)$, so its fraction is $(2/5)(3/10)$. Reflection gives the $02$ fraction. The $12$ pair triangle has vertices $(2/5,3/5),(3/5,2/5),(3/10,3/10)$. The absolute determinant of its vectors from the last vertex is $2/25$, giving its fraction. The sum of the cap areas minus $|T|$ is $(8/25)|T|$, and $1/3-8/25=1/75$.

A quarter-turn of each oriented chord displacement gives the vectors $S_i u_i=(-3/5,-3/5),(3/5,-1/5),(-1/5,3/5)$. Their claimed weighted balance and weighted area are direct substitution. More generally, for any three concurrent closed halfplanes whose normals form a minimal positive circuit in the plane, let $e_i$ be the area of points of a planar convex body belonging only to cap $i$, and $d_i$ the area of points belonging to the other two only. The positive relation covers the plane and spanning makes the triple intersection a single point. Thus each point outside null boundaries contributes exactly its captured weights. In particular, for any probability vector $\theta$, the weighted cap area is $\sum_i\theta_i e_i+\sum_i(1-\theta_i)d_i$, including sectors with zero area.
