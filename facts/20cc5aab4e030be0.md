## Statement

Let \(n\geq2\), let \(m_1,\ldots,m_n\) be positive integers, and let \(d\) be an integer with \(d\geq2n^2\). Define
\[
C=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\begin{array}{ll}
 |x_i|\leq m_i,\quad y_i\geq |x_i|/m_i & (1\leq i\leq n),\\
 y_j\geq0 & (n<j\leq d),\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^d y_j\leq d
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z\), and
\[
\mu_C(A)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in A\}.
\]
For \(q\in C_z\), define \(\delta_C(z,q)\) as the infimum of \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap G)\) over proper closed halfspaces \(G\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(q\). For \(p\in S\), let \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mu_C(H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces, and let \(h_{\max}(C)=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p)\). Here \(w_z\) is the fiber volume called \(a_z\) in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 and \(w_z\) in accepted fact bb2b5f9519ae23e8; \(C_z,S,W,\mu_C,\delta_C,h_S\) otherwise have the same meanings and normalizations.

Write
\[
D=\prod_{i=1}^n\{-m_i,-m_i+1,\ldots,m_i\},\qquad
t_z=(|z_1|/m_1,\ldots,|z_n|/m_n),\qquad
T_z=\sum_{i=1}^n t_{z,i},\qquad L_z=d-T_z.
\]
Then \(C\) is a full-dimensional compact rational convex polytope, all its nonempty fibers have the same simplex facet normals, \(D=\{z:w_z>0\}\), and
\[
C_z=(t_z,0_{d-n})+L_z\Delta_d,\qquad
w_z=\frac{L_z^d}{d!}\quad(z\in D),\qquad
\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:u_j\geq0,\ \sum_{j=1}^d u_j\leq1\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\frac{\max_{z\in D}w_z}{\min_{z\in D}w_z}
 =\left(\frac d{d-n}\right)^d\geq e^n,
\]
so the family has no absolute bound on its positive fiber-volume ratios.

For each \(i\), define the exact finite sums
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_i&=
\frac{\sum_{k=-m_i}^{m_i}(|k|/m_i)e^{-|k|/m_i}}
     {\sum_{k=-m_i}^{m_i}e^{-|k|/m_i}},\qquad
\overline L=d-\sum_{i=1}^n\tau_i,
\\
p&=\left(0_n,\,
(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n,0_{d-n})
+\frac{\overline L}{d+1}\mathbf1_d\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
This single point belongs to \(S\) and satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_C(H)&\geq \frac{W}{968}\geq\frac{W}{1000}
\quad\hbox{for every closed halfspace }H\ni p,
\\
\frac{\delta_C(0_n,p_y)}{w_{0_n}}&\leq\frac{64}{n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, if \(n>1024\), then \(p_y\) does not have relative depth \(1/16\) in its own fiber. The displayed finite sums specify the point exactly; no numerical approximation is part of the assertion.

For any body in this family, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{16}(C)&=
\sup_{\substack{\varnothing\ne E\subseteq D,\ q_z\in C_z\ (z\in E)\\
                 \delta_C(z,q_z)\geq w_z/16\ (z\in E)}}
 \frac{2^{n-r_E}\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{s(E,q)+1},
\\
r_E&=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E),\\
s(E,q)&=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{(z,q_z):z\in E\}-r_E.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every nonempty subset and every admissible independent choice of its points is included. For each integer \(n\geq10000\), let \(C_n\) be the special case \(m_1=\cdots=m_n=n\), \(d=2n^2\), and write \(W_n=\mu_{C_n}(\mathbb R^{n+d})\). Then
\[
\frac{W_n}{n+1}\leq\mathcal A_{16}(C_n)
 \leq\frac{2880}{n}W_n.
\]
Thus no absolute \(c>0\) satisfies \(\mathcal A_{16}(C)\geq cW\) throughout the stated fixed-normal simplex class.

For completeness, accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 applies to every admissible selection with the atomic measure \(\sigma=\sum_{z\in E}(w_z/16)\delta_{(z,q_z)}\). Its intrinsic base rank \(\rho_\sigma\) equals \(r_E\), and its residual rank \(r_\sigma\) equals \(s(E,q)\). Consequently
\[
h_{\max}(C)\geq\frac{2^{-n}}{16}\mathcal A_{16}(C).
\]
The upper bound on \(\mathcal A_{16}(C_n)\) is a limitation of this certificate; the explicit point \(p\) above has the asserted constant actual depth in these same bodies.

## Proof

All defining inequalities of \(C\) are rational linear inequalities: replace \(y_i\geq |x_i|/m_i\) by \(m_i y_i\geq x_i\) and \(m_i y_i\geq-x_i\). The inequalities bound \(x\), and they imply \(y_j\geq0\) and \(\sum_j y_j\leq d\), so \(C\) is compact. The point \((0_n,\tfrac12\mathbf1_d)\) satisfies every defining inequality strictly, proving full dimensionality. In the notation of the fixed-normal class, take \(P=\prod_i[-m_i,m_i]\), \(\psi_0=d\), \(\psi_i(x)=-|x_i|/m_i\) for \(1\leq i\leq n\), and \(\psi_j=0\) for \(n<j\leq d\). These functions are continuous, concave, rational piecewise affine, and their sum is at least \(d-n>0\) on \(P\). Translation of each fiber gives the asserted \(C_z\) and \(w_z\). Every point of \(D\) has positive fiber volume, and other lattice fibers are empty. The maximum \(L_z=d\) occurs at \(z=0_n\), and the minimum \(L_z=d-n\) occurs at every corner with \(|z_i|=m_i\). Since \(-\log(1-u)\geq u\) for \(0\leq u<1\), the exact volume ratio is at least \(e^n\).

We first establish elementary moment formulas used in both parts of the proof. If \(U\) is uniform on \(\Delta_d\), set \(N=d+1\) and \(U_0=1-\sum_{j=1}^d U_j\). For nonnegative integers \(k_0,\ldots,k_d\),
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{j=0}^d U_j^{k_j}
 =\frac{d!\prod_{j=0}^d k_j!}{(d+\sum_{j=0}^d k_j)!}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the corresponding unnormalized integral over \(\Delta_d\) is \(\prod_j k_j!/(d+\sum_j k_j)!\). This follows by induction on the number of integrated coordinates: integrate the last coordinate from \(0\) to the remaining slack and rescale it to \([0,1]\), then use \(\int_0^1 t^a(1-t)^b\,dt=a!b!/(a+b+1)!\). The latter identity follows by repeated integration by parts. At all zero exponents the integral is \(1/d!\), so normalization gives (1). In particular,
\[
\mathbb EU_j=\frac1N,\qquad
\mathbb EU_j^2=\frac2{N(N+1)},\qquad
\mathbb EU_jU_k=\frac1{N(N+1)}\quad(j\ne k),
\]
and therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(U)=\frac1{(d+1)(d+2)}
 \left(I_d-\frac{\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_d^{\mathsf T}}{d+1}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

Introduce an auxiliary product probability law, with independent coordinates \(Z_i\) and
\[
\mathbb P(Z_i=k)=
 \frac{e^{-|k|/m_i}}{\mathcal Z_i},\qquad
\mathcal Z_i=\sum_{\ell=-m_i}^{m_i}e^{-|\ell|/m_i},
\qquad -m_i\leq k\leq m_i.
\tag{3}
\]
These auxiliary real weights do not change the rational defining inequalities of \(C\). Put \(R_i=Z_i/m_i\). Symmetry gives \(\mathbb ER_i=0\), \(\mathbb E|R_i|=\tau_i\), and \(\mathbb E[R_i(|R_i|-\tau_i)]=0\).

For the uniform law on \(\{-1,-1+1/m,\ldots,1\}\), summing the first and second powers of \(1,\ldots,m\) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\rm unif}R^2=\frac{m+1}{3m}\geq\frac13,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\rm unif}|R|=\frac{m+1}{2m+1}\geq\frac12,
\]
and direct subtraction gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\rm unif}(|R|)
 =\frac{m+1}{3m}-\left(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}\right)^2
 =\frac1{12}+\frac{4m^2+7m+4}{12m(2m+1)^2}\geq\frac1{12}.
\tag{4}
\]
The probability in (3), divided by the corresponding uniform probability, is at least \(e^{-1}\), because \(\mathcal Z_i\leq2m_i+1\) and \(|k|/m_i\leq1\). Thus expectations of nonnegative functions compare with factor \(e^{-1}\). Applying this to \(R_i^2\) and \(|R_i|\), and minimizing \(\mathbb E(|R_i|-a)^2\) over \(a\in\mathbb R\) for the variance comparison, proves
\[
\sigma_{1,i}^2:=\mathbb ER_i^2\geq\frac1{3e}>\frac19,\qquad
\sigma_{2,i}^2:=\operatorname{Var}(|R_i|)
 \geq\frac1{12e}>\frac1{36},\qquad
\tau_i\geq\frac1{2e}>\frac16.
\tag{5}
\]
Here the elementary inequality \(e<3\) suffices for the strict bounds.

Define independent two-dimensional blocks
\[
\Xi_i=\left(\frac{R_i}{\sigma_{1,i}},
            \frac{|R_i|-\tau_i}{\sigma_{2,i}}\right),
\qquad \Xi=(\Xi_1,\ldots,\Xi_n)\in\mathbb R^{2n}.
\]
They have mean zero and covariance \(I_2\). Since \(|R_i|\leq1\) and \(0\leq\tau_i\leq1\), (5) gives
\[
\|\Xi_i\|_2^2\leq45,\qquad
\mathbb E\|\Xi_i\|_2^2=2,\qquad
\mathbb E\|\Xi_i\|_2^4\leq90.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, for any \(\theta_i\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
\mathbb E\langle\theta_i,\Xi_i\rangle^4
 \leq45\|\theta_i\|_2^2\,
       \mathbb E\langle\theta_i,\Xi_i\rangle^2
 =45\bigl(\mathbb E\langle\theta_i,\Xi_i\rangle^2\bigr)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The first inequality follows pointwise from
\(\langle\theta_i,\Xi_i\rangle^2\leq45\|\theta_i\|_2^2\).

We also compare the true fiber weights with (3), uniformly in all the parameters under consideration. For \(0\leq u\leq1/2\),
\[
-u-u^2\leq\log(1-u)\leq-u.
\tag{8}
\]
For example, the lower bound follows because the derivative of \(\log(1-u)+u+u^2\) is \(u(1-2u)/(1-u)\geq0\); the upper bound follows by differentiating \(\log(1-u)+u\). Since \(T_z\leq n\) and \(d\geq2n^2\), we have \(T_z/d\leq1/2\) and \(T_z^2/d\leq1/2\). Hence
\[
w_z=\frac{d^d}{d!}e^{-T_z}r_z,\qquad
r_z:=e^{T_z}(1-T_z/d)^d,\qquad
e^{-1/2}\leq r_z\leq1.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\mathbb P(Z=z)=e^{-T_z}/\prod_i\mathcal Z_i\), summing (9) and then dividing proves
\[
\frac{w_z}{W}
 =\mathbb P(Z=z)\frac{r_z}{\mathbb Er_Z},\qquad
e^{-1/2}\mathbb P(Z=z)
 \leq\frac{w_z}{W}\leq e^{1/2}\mathbb P(Z=z).
\tag{10}
\]
In particular, for every subset \(E\subseteq D\),
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{W}
 \leq e^{1/2}\mathbb P(Z\in E)
 \leq2\mathbb P(Z\in E).
\tag{11}
\]

We now prove the uniform affine-neighborhood estimate needed for the selector upper bound. For any independent centered blocks satisfying (6) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(\Xi_i)=I_2\), and any affine subspace \(F\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}\) of codimension \(k\geq4608\),
\[
\mathbb P\bigl(\operatorname{dist}(\Xi,F)\leq48\bigr)\leq\frac{720}{k}.
\tag{12}
\]
To prove this, let \(\Pi\) be the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of the direction space of \(F\). Its restriction to \(F\) is constant; call that value \(b\). Then \(b\in\operatorname{im}\Pi\) and
\(V:=\operatorname{dist}(\Xi,F)^2=\|\Pi\Xi-b\|_2^2\).
Write \(\Pi_{ij}\) for the \(2\)-by-\(2\) blocks of \(\Pi\). Independence and zero block means imply
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Xi^{\mathsf T}\Pi\Xi)
 =\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(\Xi_i^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ii}\Xi_i)
   +4\sum_{i<j}\mathbb E(\Xi_i^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ij}\Xi_j)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Here distinct off-diagonal terms have zero covariance: if their index pairs are disjoint use independence, and if they share one index condition on that block and use zero means of the two other blocks. For a diagonal term indexed by \(i\) and an off-diagonal term involving \(i,j\), condition on \(\Xi_i\) and average over the centered independent block \(\Xi_j\); disjoint indices again give independence. Thus diagonal and off-diagonal terms also have zero covariance. These arguments also prove that all off-diagonal means are zero. Isotropy gives
\(\mathbb E(\Xi_i^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ij}\Xi_j)^2=\|\Pi_{ij}\|_{\rm F}^2\).
Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\Xi_i^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ii}\Xi_i)
 \leq\mathbb E(\Xi_i^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ii}\Xi_i)^2
 \leq\|\Pi_{ii}\|_{\rm F}^2\mathbb E\|\Xi_i\|_2^4
 \leq90\|\Pi_{ii}\|_{\rm F}^2.
\]
Consequently (13) is at most \(90\|\Pi\|_{\rm F}^2=90k\), since \(\Pi\) is an orthogonal projection of rank \(k\). The full vector \(\Xi\) has mean zero and covariance \(I_{2n}\), so
\[
\mathbb EV=k+\|b\|_2^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V)
 \leq2\operatorname{Var}(\Xi^{\mathsf T}\Pi\Xi)
     +8\operatorname{Var}(b^{\mathsf T}\Xi)
 \leq180k+8\|b\|_2^2\leq180\mathbb EV.
\]
The variance inequality here follows from
\(\operatorname{Var}(A+B)\leq2\operatorname{Var}(A)+2\operatorname{Var}(B)\); also \(b^{\mathsf T}\Pi\Xi=b^{\mathsf T}\Xi\). Since \(\mathbb EV\geq k\geq4608=2\cdot48^2\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\mathbb P(V\leq48^2)
 \leq\frac{180\mathbb EV}{(\mathbb EV-48^2)^2}
 \leq\frac{720}{\mathbb EV}\leq\frac{720}{k}.
\]
This proves (12) for every orientation and every translation of \(F\).

Specialize next to \(m_i=n\), \(d=2n^2\), \(n\geq10000\). In this part write \(C=C_n\) and \(W=W_n\). The one-coordinate laws (3) are identical, and their largest atom satisfies
\[
p_{\max}=\left(\sum_{\ell=-n}^n e^{-|\ell|/n}\right)^{-1}
 \leq\frac e{2n+1}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, each term in the denominator is at least \(e^{-1}\).

Let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\). Formulas (1)-(2) and \(L_z\leq d\) give, for a uniform point \(Y_z\) in \(C_z\),
\[
c_z=(t_z,0_{d-n})+\frac{L_z}{d+1}\mathbf1_d,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_z)
 =\frac{L_z^2}{(d+1)(d+2)}
  \left(I_d-\frac{\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_d^{\mathsf T}}{d+1}\right)
 \preceq I_d.
\tag{15}
\]
Every admissible \(q_z\) satisfies \(\|q_z-c_z\|_2\leq4\). To see this when \(q_z\ne c_z\), let \(v=(q_z-c_z)/\|q_z-c_z\|_2\). The proper closed halfspace through \(q_z\) with outer normal \(v\) has relative fiber mass at least \(1/16\). Chebyshev's inequality and (15) therefore give
\[
\frac1{16}\leq
 \mathbb P\bigl(\langle v,Y_z-c_z\rangle
                  \geq\|q_z-c_z\|_2\bigr)
 \leq\frac1{\|q_z-c_z\|_2^2}.
\]
The case \(q_z=c_z\) is immediate. Thus this displacement bound covers every point allowed in \(\mathcal A_{16}\).

Let \(q_z^{[n]}\) denote the first \(n\) coordinates of \(q_z\), and set
\[
M=I_n-\frac{\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}}{d+1},\qquad
v_z=M^{-1}\left(q_z^{[n]}-\frac d{d+1}\mathbf1_n\right).
\]
The eigenvalues of \(M\) are \(1\) on \(\mathbf1_n^\perp\) and \(1-n/(d+1)>1/2\) on its span. Thus \(\|M^{-1}\|_{\rm op}\leq2\). The first \(n\) centroid coordinates in (15) equal \(\frac d{d+1}\mathbf1_n+Mt_z\), so
\[
\|v_z-t_z\|_2\leq8.
\tag{16}
\]
The transformation \((z,q)\mapsto(z/n,M^{-1}(q^{[n]}-\frac d{d+1}\mathbf1_n))\) is affine. Hence for any fixed admissible selection on any nonempty \(E\subseteq D\), with \(r=r_E\) and \(s=s(E,q)\),
\[
K_E:=\operatorname{aff}\{(z/n,v_z):z\in E\}
\quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad
\dim K_E\leq r+s.
\tag{17}
\]
Also \(s\geq0\), since projecting the original graph to its base is onto \(E\).

There are three exhaustive cases for this arbitrary selection. If \(r<n\), there are \(r\) base coordinate positions whose projection is injective on \(\operatorname{aff}(E)\). Namely, choose \(r\) independent rows from any \(n\)-by-\(r\) basis matrix for its direction space; for \(r=0\), use the empty set of coordinates. After fixing these coordinates, at most one configuration of the remaining \(n-r\) coordinates lies in \(E\). Under the product law (3), each conditional probability of such a configuration is at most \(p_{\max}^{n-r}\). Averaging over the fixed coordinates proves \(\mathbb P(Z\in E)\leq p_{\max}^{n-r}\). Because \(2p_{\max}<1\), (11) and (14) imply
\[
\frac{2^{n-r}\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{(s+1)W}
 \leq2(2p_{\max})^{n-r}
 \leq4p_{\max}\leq\frac{4e}{2n+1}<\frac6n.
\tag{18}
\]
This includes singleton subsets and every lower base rank.

If \(r=n\) and \(s\geq n/2\), the same normalized expression is at most \(1/(s+1)\leq2/n\), because \(\sum_{z\in E}w_z\leq W\).

Finally suppose \(r=n\) and \(s<n/2\). Define the affine isomorphism
\[
\mathcal T(u,t)=
 \left(\frac{u_1}{\sigma_{1,1}},
       \frac{t_1-\tau_1}{\sigma_{2,1}},\ldots,
       \frac{u_n}{\sigma_{1,n}},
       \frac{t_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_{2,n}}\right)
 \quad((u,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n).
\]
By (5), its linear part has operator norm at most \(6\). It takes \((Z/n,|Z|/n)\) to \(\Xi\). Put \(F_E=\mathcal T(K_E)\). Its codimension \(k\) is at least \(n-s>n/2\), by (17), and (16) implies
\[
Z\in E\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{dist}(\Xi,F_E)\leq48.
\]
For this implication one can use the specific comparison point \(\mathcal T(z/n,v_z)\in F_E\). Since \(k>n/2\geq5000>4608\), (12) gives
\[
\mathbb P(Z\in E)\leq\frac{720}{k}\leq\frac{1440}{n}.
\]
Equation (11) now yields
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{(s+1)W}
 \leq\frac{\sum_{z\in E}w_z}{W}\leq\frac{2880}{n}.
\tag{19}
\]
The entire selection was fixed arbitrarily before forming \(F_E\); estimate (12) holds for every resulting affine subspace. Thus there is no restriction on how the selected points move, and no restriction on the subset. Taking the supremum over the three cases proves the upper bound on \(\mathcal A_{16}(C_n)\).

For the matching lower bound, the Grünbaum fiber inequality recorded in accepted fact bb2b5f9519ae23e8 gives
\(\delta_C(z,c_z)\geq\gamma_d w_z\), where
\(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d>e^{-1}>1/16\).
The strict exponential bound follows from \(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\). Thus the centroid selection on all of \(D\) is admissible. Formula (15) expresses every centroid coordinate affinely in \(t_z\), and the invertibility of \(M\) recovers \(t_z\) from its first \(n\) coordinates. The centroid graph consequently has the same affine dimension as \(\{(z,t_z):z\in D\}\). That dimension is exactly \(2n\): it is at most \(2n\), and the graph contains \((0_n,0_n)\) and \((\pm n e_i,e_i)\), \(1\leq i\leq n\); the pairwise sums and differences of these \(2n\) nonzero vectors span \(\mathbb R^{2n}\). The base has affine dimension \(n\). Hence this selection has \(r_E=n\), \(s(E,c)=n\), and gives \(\mathcal A_{16}(C_n)\geq W_n/(n+1)\). The upper and lower estimates together also show that \(\mathcal A_{16}(C_n)/W_n\) tends to zero.

We turn to the actual-depth assertion, again allowing arbitrary positive integer \(m_i\) and every integer \(d\geq2n^2\) with \(n\geq2\). Let \(Z\) have law (3), let \(U\) be uniform on \(\Delta_d\) independently of \(Z\), and define
\[
Y=(t_Z,0_{d-n})+L_Z U,\qquad
\nu=\operatorname{Law}(Z,Y).
\tag{20}
\]
Conditional on \(Z=z\), \(Y\) is exactly uniform on the actual fiber \(C_z\). Therefore (10) implies domination on every Borel set:
\[
\mu_C/W\geq e^{-1/2}\nu\geq\tfrac12\nu.
\tag{21}
\]
All these measures are supported on \(S\). Symmetry and (1) imply
\(\mathbb E_\nu Z=0_n\) and
\(\mathbb E_\nu Y=(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_n,0_{d-n})+
\frac{\overline L}{d+1}\mathbf1_d=p_y\).
The point \(p\) is in \(S\): its base is \(0_n\), every continuous coordinate is positive, and
\[
\sum_{j=1}^d p_{y,j}
 =\sum_{i=1}^n\tau_i+\frac{d\overline L}{d+1}
 =d-\frac{\overline L}{d+1}<d,
\]
because \(\overline L\geq d-n>0\). This verifies membership in \(C_{0_n}=d\Delta_d\) directly.

We next prove a fourth-moment bound for every centered scalar projection of this particular law \(\nu\). From (7), any centered single-coordinate expression \(A_i=\alpha_i Z_i+\beta_i(t_{Z,i}-\tau_i)\) has \(\mathbb EA_i^4\leq45(\mathbb EA_i^2)^2\): it is the projection of \(\Xi_i\) with coefficient vector \((\alpha_i m_i\sigma_{1,i},\beta_i\sigma_{2,i})\). The \(A_i\) are independent and have zero means, so expansion gives
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_iA_i\right)^4
 =\sum_i\mathbb EA_i^4+
   6\sum_{i<j}\mathbb EA_i^2\,\mathbb EA_j^2
 \leq45\left(\sum_i\mathbb EA_i^2\right)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
The equality holds because every other monomial in the expansion has an index appearing just once and hence has zero expectation. Thus (22) holds for arbitrary signed coefficients.

We also need the fourth moment of any simplex-noise projection
\(V_b=\langle b,U-\mathbf1_d/N\rangle\), \(b\in\mathbb R^d\), \(N=d+1\). Set \(b_0=0\) and \(\beta_j=b_j-N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^d b_\ell\). Then \(\sum_j\beta_j=0\) and \(V_b=\sum_{j=0}^d\beta_j U_j\). Let \(p_k=\sum_{j=0}^d\beta_j^k\). We claim that (1) implies
\[
\mathbb EV_b^2=\frac{p_2}{N(N+1)},\qquad
\mathbb EV_b^4=
 \frac{3p_2^2+6p_4}{N(N+1)(N+2)(N+3)}.
\tag{23}
\]
Here is an explicit expansion. Let
\(h_k=\sum_{\ell_0+\cdots+\ell_d=k}\prod_{j=0}^d\beta_j^{\ell_j}\).
The multinomial expansion and (1) give
\(\mathbb EV_b^k=k!h_k/[N(N+1)\cdots(N+k-1)]\).
The formal power-series identity
\[
\sum_{k\geq0}h_k t^k
 =\prod_{j=0}^d(1-\beta_jt)^{-1}
 =\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{p_k}{k}t^k\right)
\]
follows by multiplying geometric series and by expanding their formal logarithms. Since \(p_1=0\), its coefficients of \(t^2\) and \(t^4\) are respectively \(p_2/2\) and \(p_4/4+p_2^2/8\). This proves (23). Since \(p_4\leq p_2^2\), (23) gives
\[
\mathbb EV_b^4\leq9(\mathbb EV_b^2)^2.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(p_2=0\), all \(\beta_j=0\), so both sides are zero and the same conclusion holds.

Let \(B=L_Z V_b\). Independence of \(U\) and \(Z\), together with \(d-n\leq L_Z\leq d\), implies
\[
\mathbb EB^2=(\mathbb EV_b^2)\mathbb EL_Z^2,\qquad
\mathbb EB^4\leq9(\mathbb EV_b^2)^2\mathbb EL_Z^4
 \leq9\left(\frac d{d-n}\right)^2(\mathbb EB^2)^2.
\tag{25}
\]
For the last inequality, use \(L_Z^4\leq d^2L_Z^2\) and \(\mathbb EL_Z^2\geq(d-n)^2\). As \(n\geq2\) and \(d\geq2n^2\), one has \(n/d\leq1/4\), hence \(d/(d-n)\leq4/3\). Thus (25) gives \(\mathbb EB^4\leq16(\mathbb EB^2)^2\).

Fix any ambient normal \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), and let
\(F=a\cdot Z+b\cdot(Y-p_y)\).
Write \(F=A+B\), with \(A=\mathbb E[F\mid Z]\) and \(B=L_ZV_b\) as above. The conditional centroid formula in (15), valid also for general side lengths, gives the exact expression
\[
A=\sum_{i=1}^n\left[
 a_i Z_i+
 \left(b_i-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^d b_j}{d+1}\right)
 (t_{Z,i}-\tau_i)\right].
\tag{26}
\]
This is covered by (22). Put \(v_A=\mathbb EA^2\), \(v_B=\mathbb EB^2\). Both variables are centered, and \(\mathbb E[B\mid Z]=0\), so \(\mathbb EAB=0\) and \(\mathbb EF^2=v_A+v_B\). By (22), (25), Minkowski's inequality in \(L^4\), and Cauchy-Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathbb EF^4)^{1/4}
 &\leq45^{1/4}\sqrt{v_A}+2\sqrt{v_B},\\
\mathbb EF^4
 &\leq(\sqrt{45}+4)^2(v_A+v_B)^2
 \leq121(\mathbb EF^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The last constant follows from \(\sqrt{45}<7\); the non-strict form includes zero variances. There is no independence assumption between \(A\) and \(B\) here. Minkowski holds for any such variables, and the variance identity uses the displayed conditional mean. Equation (27) controls every ambient direction, including \(b=0\).

For clarity, we prove directly how this directional bound yields depth at the same mean \(p\). If a centered scalar variable \(Q\) has variance \(\sigma^2>0\) and \(\mathbb EQ^4\leq K\sigma^4\), Hölder's inequality gives
\[
\sigma^2=\mathbb E|Q|^2
 \leq(\mathbb E|Q|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Q|^4)^{1/3},
\qquad
\mathbb E|Q|\geq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt K}.
\]
Since \(Q\) is centered,
\(\mathbb EQ_+=\mathbb EQ_-=\frac12\mathbb E|Q|\), where \(Q_+=\max(Q,0)\) and \(Q_-=\max(-Q,0)\). Cauchy-Schwarz implies
\[
\mathbb P(Q>0)\geq
 \frac{(\mathbb EQ_+)^2}{\mathbb EQ_+^2}
 \geq\frac{(\mathbb EQ_+)^2}{\sigma^2}
 \geq\frac1{4K},
\]
and the same argument applies to \(Q<0\). If the variance is zero, \(Q=0\) almost surely.

Any proper closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(p\) contains the parallel halfspace through \(p\) of the form \(\{(x,y):a\cdot x+b\cdot(y-p_y)\geq0\}\), after choosing its orientation. Apply the preceding argument to \(F\) in (27), with \(K=121\). Its zero-variance case gives probability one for this closed halfspace; otherwise its probability is at least \(1/484\). It follows that \(\nu(H)\geq1/484\) for every closed halfspace \(H\ni p\). Combining with (21) proves
\[
\mu_C(H)\geq\frac W2\,\nu(H)\geq\frac W{968}\geq\frac W{1000}.
\]
If the whole space is allowed as an improper halfspace, its mass is \(W\), so it also satisfies the assertion. The point \(p\) was fixed by the finite sums before the arbitrary ambient normal or halfspace was chosen.

It remains to verify the claimed shallowness of this point in its own fiber. Let \(Y_0\) be uniform on \(C_{0_n}=d\Delta_d\), and put \(Q_0=\sum_{i=1}^nY_{0,i}\). The moments (1)-(2) give
\[
\mathbb EQ_0=\frac{dn}{d+1},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Q_0)
 =\frac{d^2n(d+1-n)}{(d+1)^2(d+2)}\leq n.
\tag{28}
\]
Indeed, summing the first \(n\) rows and columns of the covariance in (2), and multiplying by \(d^2\), gives the displayed variance. The bound follows because \(d^2/[(d+1)(d+2)]\leq1\) and \(1-n/(d+1)\leq1\). On the other hand, the definition of \(p\) and (5) imply
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n p_{y,i}-\mathbb EQ_0
 =\left(1-\frac n{d+1}\right)\sum_{i=1}^n\tau_i
 \geq\frac34\cdot\frac n6=\frac n8.
\]
The factor \(3/4\) is valid because \(n/(d+1)\leq1/4\) for the stated parameters. Therefore the proper closed fiber halfspace
\(G=\{y:\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\geq\sum_{i=1}^n p_{y,i}\}\)
contains \(p_y\), and (28) with Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
\frac{\delta_C(0_n,p_y)}{w_{0_n}}
 \leq\mathbb P(Y_0\in G)
 \leq\frac{n}{(n/8)^2}=\frac{64}{n}.
\]
This also proves the strict failure of the \(1/16\) requirement when \(n>1024\).

Finally, we verify the stated selector interpretation with its precise predecessor normalization. For any admissible selection, let
\(\sigma=\sum_{z\in E}(w_z/16)\delta_{(z,q_z)}\).
For a proper ambient halfspace \(H=\{(x,y):a\cdot x+b\cdot y\geq c\}\), every atom in \(H\) has a fiber section containing its selected point. If \(b\ne0\), that section has \(C_z\)-volume at least \(w_z/16\) by admissibility. If \(b=0\), that section is the whole fiber and has volume \(w_z\). Summing over atoms in \(H\) proves \(\sigma(H)\leq\mu_C(H)\); the whole-space case is immediate. The support lies in \(S\), and \(|\sigma|=\sum_{z\in E}w_z/16>0\). The coordinate-free rank identity and dominated-measure theorem of accepted fact be359f8a6c628483 therefore apply with
\[
\rho_\sigma=r_E,\qquad
r_\sigma=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{(z,q_z):z\in E\}-r_E=s(E,q).
\]
They produce, for this selection, a point of \(S\) before all containing halfspaces with depth at least
\(\sum_{z\in E}w_z/[16\,2^{r_E}(s(E,q)+1)]\).
Taking the supremum over selections proves
\(h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-n}\mathcal A_{16}(C)/16\).
