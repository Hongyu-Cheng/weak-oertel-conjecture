## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(\rho\) be a nonzero compactly
supported log-concave finite Borel measure on
\(\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\).  Put
\[
M:=|\rho|,\qquad P(z,x):=z,\qquad \pi:=P_\#\rho.
\]
Fix \(z_0\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1]\).  Suppose that every closed
halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(z_0\) satisfies
\[
\pi(Q)\geq\alpha M.
\]
Then there is a compactly supported log-concave finite Borel measure
\(\sigma\) on \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\) such that
\[
0\leq\sigma\leq\rho,\qquad |\sigma|=\alpha M,\qquad
P\!\left(\frac1{\alpha M}\int_{\mathbb R^{n+d}}v\,d\sigma(v)\right)=z_0.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
p:=\frac1{\alpha M}\int_{\mathbb R^{n+d}}v\,d\sigma(v)=(z_0,x_0),
\]
then every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing
\(p\) satisfies
\[
\rho(H)\geq\frac{\alpha}{e}M.
\]
These conclusions include \(\alpha=1\), points \(z_0\) on the boundary of the
level-\(\alpha\) metronoid, measures supported on proper affine subspaces, and
closed halfspaces whose boundaries carry positive mass.

## Proof

Normalize the base marginal by
\[
\mu:=\frac{\pi}{M}.
\]
It is a compactly supported probability measure.  For \(0<\alpha\leq1\), define
its level-\(\alpha\) metronoid by
\[
\mathcal M_\alpha:=
\left\{
\frac1\alpha\int_{\mathbb R^n}z\,g(z)\,d\mu(z):
0\leq g\leq1\ \mu\text{-a.e.},\
\int_{\mathbb R^n}g\,d\mu=\alpha
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The set of admissible \(g\)'s is weak-star compact in \(L^\infty(\mu)\), and
the functions \(1,z_1,\ldots,z_n\) belong to \(L^1(\mu)\), because \(\mu\) has
compact support.  Hence \(\mathcal M_\alpha\) is compact and convex.

We first prove
\[
z_0\in\mathcal M_\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}\), let
\[
Q_u:=\{z:u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\}.
\]
The hypothesis gives \(\mu(Q_u)\geq\alpha\).  Thus
\[
g_u:=\frac{\alpha}{\mu(Q_u)}\mathbf 1_{Q_u}
\]
is admissible in (1), and
\[
u^\top\left(\frac1\alpha\int z g_u\,d\mu\right)\geq u^\top z_0.
\]
Therefore the support function of \(\mathcal M_\alpha\) satisfies
\[
h_{\mathcal M_\alpha}(u)\geq u^\top z_0
\qquad(u\neq0).
\]
If (2) failed, strict separation of a point from the compact convex set
\(\mathcal M_\alpha\) would give a nonzero \(u\) with the reverse strict
inequality.  This proves (2).  Notice that \(Q_u\) is closed and all of its
boundary mass was retained in this argument.

The case \(\alpha=1\) is immediate but is best separated from the entropy
argument.  Applying the hypothesis to \(Q_u\) and \(Q_{-u}\) shows that both
have full \(\mu\)-mass.  Hence
\[
\mu\{z:u^\top(z-z_0)=0\}=1
\]
for every \(u\).  Taking the coordinate vectors gives \(\mu=\delta_{z_0}\).
Thus the base coordinate is \(z_0\), \(\rho\)-almost everywhere.  Taking
\(\sigma=\rho\) gives all of the selection conclusions.  The final halfspace
bound will be proved below simultaneously with the case \(0<\alpha<1\).

Assume henceforth that \(0<\alpha<1\).  We next construct the selection for
every \(y\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M_\alpha\).  Put
\[
Y(z):=(1,z)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}
\]
and introduce the compact convex zonoid
\[
\mathcal Z:=
\left\{\int g(z)Y(z)\,d\mu(z):0\leq g\leq1\ \mu\text{-a.e.}\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(E:=\operatorname{span}\mathcal Z\), with its Euclidean structure, and let
\[
m:=\int z\,d\mu(z),\qquad t_0:=\alpha(1,m).
\]
We claim that
\[
t_0\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal Z.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, fix \(\theta\in E\setminus\{0\}\) and write
\[
s_\theta(z):=\theta^\top Y(z),\qquad
s_{\theta,+}:=\max\{s_\theta,0\},\qquad
s_{\theta,-}:=\max\{-s_\theta,0\}.
\]
The function \(s_\theta\) is not zero \(\mu\)-almost everywhere.  Otherwise
\(\theta\) would be orthogonal to every vector in (3), hence would belong to
\(E\cap E^\perp=\{0\}\).  Pointwise maximization over \(0\leq g\leq1\) gives
\[
h_{\mathcal Z}(\theta)=\int s_{\theta,+}\,d\mu.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathcal Z}(\theta)-\theta^\top t_0
&=(1-\alpha)\int s_{\theta,+}\,d\mu
  +\alpha\int s_{\theta,-}\,d\mu\\
&>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Strict support-function inequalities in every nonzero direction of
\(\operatorname{span}\mathcal Z\) characterize relative interior, so (5)
proves (4).

The affine slice of \(\mathcal Z\) at first coordinate \(\alpha\) is exactly
\[
\mathcal Z_\alpha:=
\mathcal Z\cap\{t:t_1=\alpha\}
=\{(\alpha,\alpha y):y\in\mathcal M_\alpha\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The slicing hyperplane meets \(\operatorname{ri}\mathcal Z\) at \(t_0\).
For a finite-dimensional convex set and an affine subspace meeting its
relative interior,
\[
\operatorname{ri}(\mathcal Z\cap A)
=\operatorname{ri}\mathcal Z\cap A.
\tag{7}
\]
This follows directly by intersecting a relative open ball in
\(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal Z\) with \(A\), and conversely by extending
relative line segments through one fixed point of
\(A\cap\operatorname{ri}\mathcal Z\).  Applying (7) to (6) shows that, for
every \(y\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M_\alpha\),
\[
t:=\alpha(1,y)\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal Z.
\tag{8}
\]

We now solve the constrained binary-entropy problem and record its
Lagrange multipliers.  On \(E\), define
\[
\Phi(\theta):=
\int\log\!\left(1+\exp(\theta^\top Y(z))\right)\,d\mu(z),
\qquad
F_t(\theta):=\Phi(\theta)-\theta^\top t.
\tag{9}
\]
Compact support makes \(\Phi\) finite and continuously differentiable.
Moreover,
\[
\log(1+e^r)\geq\max\{0,r\}
\]
implies
\[
F_t(\theta)\geq h_{\mathcal Z}(\theta)-\theta^\top t.
\tag{10}
\]
By (8), the continuous function
\[
\theta\longmapsto h_{\mathcal Z}(\theta)-\theta^\top t
\]
is strictly positive on the unit sphere of \(E\).  Its minimum there is some
\(\delta>0\), so (10) gives
\[
F_t(\theta)\geq\delta\|\theta\|.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(F_t\) is coercive and has a minimizer \(\theta_*\in E\).

Differentiating (9) in every direction of \(E\) gives
\[
\int
\frac{\exp(\theta_*^\top Y(z))}
     {1+\exp(\theta_*^\top Y(z))}
Y(z)\,d\mu(z)=t.
\tag{12}
\]
To justify the vector equality rather than only its projection onto \(E\),
observe that both the integral in (12), which belongs to \(\mathcal Z\), and
\(t\) belong to \(E\).  Write \(\theta_*^\top Y(z)=a+b^\top z\), and set
\[
\lambda_y(z):=
\frac{\exp(a+b^\top z)}{1+\exp(a+b^\top z)}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then (12) says exactly
\[
0<\lambda_y(z)<1,\qquad
\int\lambda_y\,d\mu=\alpha,\qquad
\int z\lambda_y(z)\,d\mu(z)=\alpha y.
\tag{14}
\]

For completeness, (13) is the unique constrained entropy maximizer, not merely
a feasible KKT point.  Let
\[
\mathfrak h(q):=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q),\qquad 0\leq q\leq1,
\]
with \(0\log0=0\).  The pointwise identity
\[
\log(1+e^r)
=\max_{0\leq q\leq1}\{\mathfrak h(q)+qr\}
\tag{15}
\]
has the unique maximizer \(q=e^r/(1+e^r)\).  If \(q\) satisfies the two
constraints in (14), integration of (15) with
\(r=\theta_*^\top Y(z)\) gives
\[
\int\mathfrak h(q)\,d\mu
\leq\Phi(\theta_*)-\theta_*^\top t
=\int\mathfrak h(\lambda_y)\,d\mu,
\]
with equality only if \(q=\lambda_y\), \(\mu\)-almost everywhere.  Thus the
constrained maximizer exists and is unique, (13) gives its Lagrange-multiplier
form, and its strict \(0<\lambda_y<1\) behavior is explicit.

The logistic weight is log-concave.  Indeed, for
\(\ell(s)=e^s/(1+e^s)\),
\[
\frac{d^2}{ds^2}\log\ell(s)
=-\frac{e^s}{(1+e^s)^2}<0.
\tag{16}
\]
Hence \(z\mapsto\lambda_y(z)=\ell(a+b^\top z)\) is log-concave.  Define
\[
d\sigma_y(z,x):=\lambda_y(z)\,d\rho(z,x).
\tag{17}
\]
Then \(0\leq\sigma_y\leq\rho\), and (14) gives
\[
|\sigma_y|=M\int\lambda_y\,d\mu=\alpha M,
\qquad
\int z\,d\sigma_y(z,x)=M\int z\lambda_y(z)\,d\mu(z)=\alpha M y.
\tag{18}
\]

The measure in (17) is log-concave even when \(\rho\) is singular in the
ambient space.  The affine-support characterization of log-concave Radon
measures says that, after normalization, \(\rho\) is an affine pushforward of
a measure with a log-concave Lebesgue density.  Pulling (17) back through that
affine map multiplies this density by the log-concave function
\(\lambda_y\circ P\) composed with an affine map.  The product is log-concave,
and its affine pushforward is log-concave.  This also covers a zero-dimensional
affine support.  Compact support of \(\sigma_y\) follows from domination by
\(\rho\).

It remains to reach an arbitrary \(z_0\in\mathcal M_\alpha\), including a
boundary point.  Equation (4), the slice identity (7), and (6) imply
\[
m\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M_\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
For \(k\geq2\), put
\[
y_k:=\left(1-\frac1k\right)z_0+\frac1k m.
\]
A strict convex combination of a point of a convex set with a
relative-interior point is again in the relative interior, so
\(y_k\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M_\alpha\), and \(y_k\to z_0\).  Construct
\(\sigma_k:=\sigma_{y_k}\) by (17).

All \(\sigma_k\) are supported on the one fixed compact set
\(\operatorname{supp}\rho\), and all have mass \(\alpha M\).  Weak sequential
compactness therefore gives a subsequence, not relabeled, and a finite Borel
measure \(\sigma\) such that
\[
\sigma_k\Rightarrow\sigma.
\tag{20}
\]
For every nonnegative continuous function \(\varphi\),
\[
\int\varphi\,d\sigma
=\lim_k\int\varphi\,d\sigma_k
\leq\int\varphi\,d\rho.
\]
Regularity of finite Borel measures implies
\[
0\leq\sigma\leq\rho.
\tag{21}
\]
Testing (20) with \(1\) and with the coordinate functions on the common compact
support, and using (18), yields
\[
|\sigma|=\alpha M,\qquad
\int z\,d\sigma(z,x)=\lim_k\alpha M y_k=\alpha M z_0.
\tag{22}
\]

Log-concavity is also preserved in (20).  Here is a direct verification,
including possible loss of affine dimension.  Let \(A,B\) be compact sets and
\(0<\gamma<1\).  For all but countably many \(\varepsilon>0\), the closed
\(\varepsilon\)-neighborhoods \(A^\varepsilon,B^\varepsilon\) are
\(\sigma\)-continuity sets.  For such an \(\varepsilon\), weak convergence,
log-concavity of \(\sigma_k\), and the closed-set part of the Portmanteau
theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma\bigl((1-\gamma)A^\varepsilon+\gamma B^\varepsilon\bigr)
&\geq
\limsup_k
\sigma_k\bigl((1-\gamma)A^\varepsilon+\gamma B^\varepsilon\bigr)\\
&\geq
\sigma(A^\varepsilon)^{1-\gamma}
\sigma(B^\varepsilon)^\gamma.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Choose admissible \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).  The compact sets on the left of
(23) decrease to \((1-\gamma)A+\gamma B\): the reverse inclusion is clear,
and the other inclusion follows by taking convergent subsequences of the two
compact-set coordinates.  Continuity from above in (23) proves
\[
\sigma((1-\gamma)A+\gamma B)
\geq\sigma(A)^{1-\gamma}\sigma(B)^\gamma.
\tag{24}
\]
This is log-concavity of \(\sigma\).  Thus (21), (22), compact support, and
(24) prove the required selection for every boundary or interior \(z_0\).

Finally define the barycenter
\[
p:=\frac1{\alpha M}\int v\,d\sigma(v)=(z_0,x_0).
\]
Let \(H\) be a proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(p\), say
\[
H=\{v:c^\top v\geq r\},\qquad c\neq0.
\]
The boundary-through-\(p\) halfspace
\[
H':=\{v:c^\top v\geq c^\top p\}
\]
satisfies \(H'\subseteq H\).  Let \(A_\sigma\) be the affine hull of the
support of \(\sigma\).  If \(c^\top v\) is constant on \(A_\sigma\), then
\(\sigma(H')=\alpha M\).  Otherwise, the normalized measure
\(\sigma/(\alpha M)\), expressed in affine coordinates on \(A_\sigma\), has a
log-concave probability density, \(H'\cap A_\sigma\) is a genuine halfspace
through its centroid, and the log-concave Grünbaum inequality gives
\[
\sigma(H')\geq\frac1e|\sigma|=\frac{\alpha}{e}M.
\tag{25}
\]
In the genuine-halfspace case the relative boundary has Lebesgue measure zero;
in the constant case it carries all the mass.  Thus (25) is valid for closed
halfspaces regardless of boundary mass.  A convention allowing the whole
space as a halfspace is trivial.  Using domination and \(H'\subseteq H\),
\[
\rho(H)\geq\sigma(H)\geq\sigma(H')
\geq\frac{\alpha}{e}M.
\]
This proves the consequence and completes the proof.

## External sources

Christer Borell, “Convex measures on locally convex spaces,” Arkiv för
Matematik 12 (1974), 239-252, Theorem 1.1 for \(s=0\),
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02384761, primary PDF:
https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/116/7214-11512_2006_Article_BF02384761.pdf.
The theorem characterizes a log-concave Radon probability measure on Euclidean
space as an affine pushforward of a measure having a log-concave Lebesgue
density.  Applied to the normalization of \(\rho\), it justifies the
affine-support multiplication argument after (18), including proper affine
supports.  Finite compactly supported Borel measures on Euclidean space are
Radon, so its hypotheses apply.

László Lovász and Santosh Vempala, “The Geometry of Logconcave Functions and
Sampling Algorithms,” Random Structures & Algorithms 30 (2007), 307-358,
Lemma 5.12, https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20135, author-hosted primary PDF:
https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~vempala/papers/logcon.pdf.  Lemma 5.12 states
that every halfspace containing the centroid of a log-concave probability
density has mass at least \(1/e\).  It applies in (25) after restricting to
the affine hull of \(\sigma\) and normalizing; the constant-functional case
is handled separately.
