## Statement

In base rank zero take the ordinary unit disk $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ and the invariant energy-only covariance buffer at $\varepsilon=1$. Its unique deepest mark is the origin and its raw depth is $Q=2\pi/3+\sqrt{3}/4$. The three $\Gamma$-unit normals $u_j=2n_j$ for unit vectors at angles $0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3$, with equal weights, give a regular active cap group. Its actual signed boundary-response density vanishes arclength-almost everywhere, while its quadratic Hessian contribution is positive definite and its boundary is a circle. Thus actual criticality and the freely tested regular density condition allow a curved boundary.

## Proof

The disk has $M=\pi$, centroid zero and $\Sigma=I/4$. By d90ba05577427854 the energy at zero is $\pi$, its projective optimizer is zero, and $f(y)=(E_K(y)/M)^{1/3}\le1$ everywhere, including the zero boundary extension. At zero each continuous direction cuts at unit-normal height $-1/2$, giving the stated area by subtracting the circular segment of area $\pi/3-\sqrt{3}/4$ from $\pi$. For $y\ne0$, take the Euclidean unit direction $y/\lVert y\rVert$ and double it to make it $\Gamma$-unit. Its Euclidean threshold $\lVert y\rVert-f(y)/2>-1/2$ gives strictly smaller area. The base-rank-zero value is $M$. This proves the unique deepest mark and the critical cap masses.

All three own traces are proper, with Euclidean chord length $\sqrt{3}$ and coarea $S_j=\sqrt{3}/2$. The weights $\rho_j=1/3$ satisfy the exact source balance since the $n_j$ sum to zero, and $\theta_j=1/3$, $B=2/\sqrt{3}$ and $\delta=\sqrt{3}/2$. The weighted cap margins have zero buffered slack at zero and nonnegative buffered slack throughout the disk because $f\le1$, so this is an actual single-contact covering group. Each circle point except the three common chord endpoints lies outside exactly one of the caps. Indeed the arcs on which $n_j\cdot X<-1/2$ have angle $2\pi/3$ and partition the circle up to endpoints. Hence $W=2/3$ almost everywhere. Also $\sum_j\rho_j(u_j\cdot X)^2=2\lVert X\rVert^2$. On the unit circle the density of cf17d6bf1aab7a2d with group weight one is
$$
\frac23-\frac Q\pi+\frac{\sqrt{3}}2
 \left(\frac1{3\pi}+\frac{2}{2\pi}-\frac{5}{6\pi}\right)=0.
$$
The continuous normals span the plane and have positive weights, giving a positive definite quadratic contribution. The circle is therefore an actual curved calibration of the free convex tests.
