## Statement

Use the simplex, direction and physical measure of the directional chord distribution lemma, and the prescribed-volume quantile $\eta$ of the sharp transverse projection theorem for $B\subseteq T$ of volume $b\in(0,V]$. For $h>d$ put $H=h+1$. Let $U_B(y)$ be the upper endpoint of the $t$ section of $B$ and define
$$
R_e(B,h)=\int_{\pi_e B}\bigl[h+U_B(y)\bigr]\,d\nu(y).
$$
Then
$$
R_e(B,h)\ge b+(H-m)P\eta^{d-1}\ge b+(H-m)\frac{Vd}{m}\eta^{d-1}
\ge Hb/m .
$$
If a set $A$ contains a segment $S$ parallel to $e$ with $t$ endpoints $l<-1$ and $h$, then $|(S+B)\cap\{t\ge0\}|=R_e(B,h)$. In particular, if $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ for a measurable $K$, then $2^d|K\cap\{t\ge0\}|\ge R_e(B,h)$.

## Proof

On $T$, $t=a_0\cdot x\le1$ and $t=-\sum_{i\ne0}a_i\cdot x\ge-d$. Therefore $h+U_B(y)\ge \ell_B(y)+H-m$, where $\ell_B=U_B-L_B$ is the actual length of the section of $B$. Integration gives $R_e(B,h)\ge b+(H-m)\nu(\pi_eB)$, and $H-m>0$. The sharp prescribed-volume projection theorem, with this same $B,e,t,\nu$, gives $\nu(\pi_eB)\ge P\eta^{d-1}$. Since $\sigma\ge1$, $P\ge Vd/m$. Finally $b/V=d\eta^{d-1}-(d-1)\eta^d\le d\eta^{d-1}$ proves the last inequality.

The segment has a single transverse coordinate $y_S$. The section of $S+B$ at $y_S+y$ is exactly $[l+L_B(y),h+U_B(y)]$, by addition of intervals. Its lower endpoint is negative since $l<-1$ and $L_B\le1$, and its upper endpoint is positive since $h>d$ and $U_B\ge-d$. Its positive section length is therefore $h+U_B(y)$. Translation invariance of the physical transverse measure gives the exact swept volume. The inclusion $(S+B)/2\subseteq K$ and dilation by $1/2$, preserving $\{t\ge0\}$ and multiplying $d$-volume by $2^{-d}$, give the last assertion.
