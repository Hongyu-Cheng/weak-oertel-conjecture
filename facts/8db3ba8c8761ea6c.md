## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Retain the constants of accepted fact 075a3f88fa24d326:
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_{\mathrm{lik}}&=4+2\max\left\{16,\frac{131}{27}e^8,65e^3\right\},
&K_{\mathrm{row}}&=20288,\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}&=(8K_{\mathrm{row}})^{-1},
&\beta_{\mathrm{row}}&=\sqrt{8K_{\mathrm{row}}c_{\mathrm{lik}}},\\
g&=\frac{32}{27},
&a_{\mathrm{box}}&=\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}
 \exp\left(-\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{row}}^2}{2\log g}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The assignment's constants \(\alpha,\beta,a\) mean exactly \(\alpha_{\mathrm{row}},\beta_{\mathrm{row}},a_{\mathrm{box}}\), respectively. These retain the row constants rather than using constants from a bounded-support-family theorem. In particular, \(a_{\mathrm{box}}\) is not any of the integer translation coordinates \(a_i\) below.

Fix any integer \(k\ge1\), integers \(m_i\ge1\), and real numbers \(\theta_i\), independently for \(1\le i\le k\). Define \(D_i=\{0,\ldots,m_i-1\}\), \(Z_i=\sum_{x\in D_i}e^{\theta_i x}\), \(p_i(x)=e^{\theta_i x}/Z_i\), \(D=\prod_{i=1}^kD_i\), and \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k p_i\). Set \(R=\#\{i:m_i\ge2\}\) and \(b_{\ge3}=\#\{i:m_i\ge3\}\). Here \(b_{\ge3}\) is exactly the integer denoted \(b\) in the product consequences of 075a3f88fa24d326; it is not a capacity prefactor.

For a probability law \(\pi\) on a nonempty finite support \(A\subset\mathbb R^s\), its actual normalized depth and moments are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)
 &=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^s}
   \sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in A\\
        \mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\ge0}}\pi_{\mathbf x},\\
\mathcal M_q(\pi)
 &=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}\pi_{\mathbf z}\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^q
 \qquad(q=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\]
This depth equals the infimum of \(\pi(A\cap H)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\) containing \(\mathbf z\). A closed cap trace means \(A\cap H\). In dimension zero, the sole point has depth one; full and empty traces are understood as the traces of constant affine inequalities. The quantities \(\tau_P\) and \(\mathcal M_2(P)\) are exactly those used in 075a3f88fa24d326, with no change of the root law. Also define the normalized exponential capacity by \(\mathsf C_\pi(\mathbf z)=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^s}\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}\pi_{\mathbf x}e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)}\); its scalar specialization and \(\mathcal B_2(p_i)=\sum_{j\in D_i}p_i(j)\mathsf C_{p_i}(j)^2\) agree with both accepted predecessors.

The actual original product law satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_2(P)&\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^2\,4^{-R},\\
\mathcal M_3(P)&\ge\mathcal M_2(P)^{3/2}
                 \ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\,8^{-R},\\
\mathcal M_2(P)^{1/2}&\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}\,2^{-R},
&\mathcal M_3(P)^{1/3}&\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}\,2^{-R}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These bounds hold uniformly without any upper bound on the finite \(m_i\), any restriction on the real \(\theta_i\), or any fixed finite family of interval supports.

For the exact capacity normalization and translation, fix integers \(a_i\), write \(\mathbf a=(a_i)_{i=1}^k\), and set \(A=\mathbf a+D\). Let \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\theta_i)_{i=1}^k\), choose \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}>0\), and assign positive weights \(c_{\mathbf z}=b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}\) on \(A\). The symbol \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}\) is exactly the prefactor denoted \(b\) in the assignment's formula \(c_{\mathbf z}=b e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}\), distinguished from the predecessor's factor count. Put \(T=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}c_{\mathbf z}\) and \(\pi_{\mathbf z}=c_{\mathbf z}/T\). Thus \(\pi\) always denotes the original normalized weights, not a capacity-reweighted root law. Define the actual unnormalized cap depth and its capacity-weighted third moment by
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_c(\mathbf z)&=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}
                  \sum_{\mathbf x\in A\cap H}c_{\mathbf x},\\
\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)
  &=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}c_{\mathbf z}h_c(\mathbf z)^3
   =T^4\mathcal M_3(\pi)
   \ge T^4a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\,8^{-R}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The infimum is over closed halfspaces. This \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)\) is the assignment's \(M3capacity\), with actual cap mass rather than exponential capacity inside the cube. The normalized moment bounds above apply to \(\pi\), and \((\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)/T^4)^{1/3}\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}2^{-R}\).

The intrinsic affine dimension and difference-lattice rank of \(A\) are both exactly \(R\). Enumerate \(I=\{i:m_i\ge2\}\) as \(i_1,\ldots,i_R\), put \(D_{\mathrm{red}}=\prod_{t=1}^R D_{i_t}\), and define the integer chart
\(\psi(\mathbf y)=\mathbf a+\sum_{t=1}^R y_t\mathbf e_{i_t}\).
Here \(\mathbf e_i\) is the standard coordinate vector. This chart fixes every singleton coordinate, maps \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) bijectively onto \(A\), and maps \(\mathbb Z^R\) bijectively onto \(\operatorname{aff}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). In particular, \(A\) is complete in its saturated affine lattice: \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). The empty product when \(R=0\) is the single point in \(\mathbb R^0\).

More generally, let \(N\ge R\) be an integer, let \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^N\), and let \(B\in\mathbb Z^{N\times R}\) have column rank \(R\) and satisfy the saturation condition \(B\mathbb Z^R=(B\mathbb R^R)\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Then the injective integer affine chart \(\Phi(\mathbf y)=\mathbf v+B\mathbf y\) maps \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) onto a support \(A'\) of intrinsic rank \(R\), complete in the ambient integer lattice: \(A'=\operatorname{conv}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Push forward the weights by \(c'_{\Phi(\mathbf y)}=c_{\psi(\mathbf y)}\), and set \(\pi'_{\mathbf z}=c'_{\mathbf z}/T\). The entire original law, total capacity \(T\), all closed cap traces and their masses, actual depths, exponential capacities, and moments are preserved under this correspondence.

The exact parameter map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{red}}&=(\theta_{i_t})_{t=1}^R,
&b_{\mathrm{red}}&=b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf a},\\
B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell'&=\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{red}},
&b'&=b_{\mathrm{red}}e^{-\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf v},\\
c'_{\mathbf z}&=b'e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf z}
&& (\mathbf z\in A').
\end{aligned}
\]
A solution \(\boldsymbol\ell'\in\mathbb R^N\) always exists, and any solution with the displayed prefactor gives precisely the pushed-forward weights. Conversely, arbitrary ambient log-affine weights \(b'e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf z}\) pull back to the intrinsic tilt \(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell'\) and prefactor \(b'e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf v}\). Thus every such saturated affine image with any real ambient tilt satisfies the same actual moment inequalities. In particular, the weaker ambient-dimensional inequalities \(\mathcal M_2\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^2 4^{-N}\), \(\mathcal M_3\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-N}\), and \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}\ge T^4a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-N}\) also hold.

## Proof

For every factor with \(m_i\ge3\), accepted fact 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9 applies with its scalar \(m=m_i\), \(\theta=\theta_i\), \(D=D_i\), and \(p_j=p_i(j)\). Its capacities and capacity second moment have exactly the definitions in the statement. Consequently, \(4\mathcal B_2(p_i)\ge4(8/27)=g=32/27>1\), uniformly over all these factors and tilts.

The remaining factors also have the correct normalizations. A singleton has \(\mathsf C_{p_i}(0)=1\) and \(\mathcal B_2(p_i)=1\). For \(D_i=\{0,1\}\), taking the scalar exponential parameter to minus or plus infinity shows that the capacity infimum at the two endpoints is at most \(p_i(0)\) and \(p_i(1)\), respectively. Each defining sum consists of nonnegative terms and contains the endpoint mass, so its infimum cannot be smaller. Thus
\(\mathcal B_2(p_i)=p_i(0)^3+p_i(1)^3=1-3p_i(0)p_i(1)\ge1/4\).
There are \(R\) nonsingleton factors and \(b_{\ge3}\) factors to which the uniform gain applies, so \(\prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i)\ge4^{-R}g^{b_{\ge3}}\).

Apply the unconditional product conversion of accepted fact 075a3f88fa24d326 to this precise product \(P\). Its factor lengths, tilts, normalized root law, \(\mathcal M_2\), \(\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}\), and \(\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\) coincide with those here, and its factor count \(b\) equals \(b_{\ge3}\). It gives
\[
\mathcal M_2(P)\ge
\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^2 e^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt{b_{\ge3}}}
 \prod_i\mathcal B_2(p_i).
\]
The hypotheses of that same accepted fact's conditional gain consumer have now been established with the single absolute \(g=32/27\). That consumer therefore gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
4^R\mathcal M_2(P)
 &\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^2
 e^{b_{\ge3}\log g-2\beta_{\mathrm{row}}\sqrt{b_{\ge3}}}\\
 &\ge\alpha_{\mathrm{row}}^2
 e^{-\beta_{\mathrm{row}}^2/\log g}
 =a_{\mathrm{box}}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The square completion in this consumer holds also when \(b_{\ge3}=0\); there is no minimum interval length beyond the case split already handled. All constants are finite and \(g>1\), so \(0<a_{\mathrm{box}}<1\). In particular the all-singleton case, whose depth and moments are one, satisfies the same bounds.

The third-moment step concerns the original law \(P\). For any probability law \(\pi\) on a finite support, Hölder's inequality with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_2(\pi)
 &=\sum_{\mathbf z}\pi_{\mathbf z}
       \bigl(\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^3\bigr)^{2/3}\\
 &\le\left(\sum_{\mathbf z}\pi_{\mathbf z}
                   \tau_\pi(\mathbf z)^3\right)^{2/3}
       \left(\sum_{\mathbf z}\pi_{\mathbf z}\right)^{1/3}
 =\mathcal M_3(\pi)^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves \(\mathcal M_3(P)\ge\mathcal M_2(P)^{3/2}\), hence the claimed third-moment and root bounds. No change of the target probabilities occurs in this step or in the invocation of the accepted actual-moment conversion.

We verify the geometric and capacity transport in detail. If \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge t\}\) contains \(\mathbf z\), then \(t\le\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\); consequently its cap trace contains the centered trace \(\{\mathbf x\in A:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\ge0\}\). Conversely, every such trace for a nonzero normal is a closed-halfspace trace containing \(\mathbf z\). The zero normal gives all of \(A\), which is also a trace of a closed halfspace containing a finite support in positive ambient dimension, and never decreases the infimum. This proves the asserted equality of actual depth with the centered formula, including constant-normal directions on an affine support and the rank-zero convention.

Translation by \(\mathbf a\) identifies the halfspace traces of \(D\) and \(A\). Direct factorization gives
\[
T=b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf a}
       \prod_{i=1}^k Z_i,
\qquad
\pi_{\mathbf a+\mathbf x}
 =\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{e^{\theta_i x_i}}{Z_i}
 =P(\mathbf x).
\]
Thus it identifies depths and both normalized moments under precisely the original root probabilities. For every cap trace, its unnormalized mass is \(T\) times its normalized mass, so taking the infimum yields \(h_c(\mathbf z)=T\tau_\pi(\mathbf z)\). Multiplying its cube by \(c_{\mathbf z}=T\pi_{\mathbf z}\) and summing proves \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)=T^4\mathcal M_3(\pi)\).

For intrinsic rank, every point of \(A\) has coordinate \(a_i\) when \(m_i=1\), and all differences lie in \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\mathbf e_i:i\in I\}\). The point \(\mathbf a\) belongs to \(A\), as does \(\mathbf a+\mathbf e_i\) for each \(i\in I\). Their differences are independent and generate the integer lattice
\[
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(A-A)
 =\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Z\mathbf e_i
 =\mathbb Z^k\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(A-A).
\]
This proves both ranks equal \(R\), proves saturation, and shows that \(\psi\) maps all intrinsic integer points bijectively onto \(\operatorname{aff}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^k\). The convex hull is the coordinate box \(\prod_i[a_i,a_i+m_i-1]\): each interval is the convex hull of its endpoints, and products of the coordinate endpoint convex coefficients express every point of the box as a convex combination of its vertices. Its integer points are exactly \(A\), including the fixed singleton coordinates.

Deleting singleton coordinates preserves their deterministic probability-one factors. For any ambient affine inequality \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge t\), its pullback by \(\psi\) is
\(\sum_{t'=1}^R u_{i_{t'}}y_{t'}\ge t-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf a\).
Conversely, any intrinsic affine inequality is obtained by choosing these components of \(\mathbf u\) and setting the other components to zero. The full or empty constant traces are harmless and realizable on a finite positive-dimensional support. Thus \(\psi\) identifies all closed cap traces and their masses, including traces containing any specified root. Its pulled-back weights are exactly \(b_{\mathrm{red}}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{red}}\cdot\mathbf y}\), and \(T=b_{\mathrm{red}}\prod_{t=1}^R Z_{i_t}\). The same conclusions hold directly for the sole point when \(R=0\).

For the general chart \(\Phi\), injectivity and the points \(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_R\) in \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) show that \(\operatorname{aff}(A')=\mathbf v+B\mathbb R^R\) and \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}(A'-A')=B\mathbb Z^R\); both ranks equal \(R\). Saturation and the integrality of \(\mathbf v\) give \(\operatorname{aff}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^N=\mathbf v+B\mathbb Z^R\). If \(\mathbf z\in\operatorname{conv}(A')\cap\mathbb Z^N\), affine preservation of convex combinations gives \(\mathbf z=\mathbf v+B\mathbf y\) for some \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathrm{red}})\). Saturation supplies \(\mathbf h\in\mathbb Z^R\) with \(\mathbf z-\mathbf v=B\mathbf h\). Injectivity forces \(\mathbf y=\mathbf h\), and the coordinate box's integer completeness gives \(\mathbf y\in D_{\mathrm{red}}\). Therefore \(\mathbf z\in A'\). The reverse inclusion is immediate, proving ambient integer completeness. This also covers any injective integer affine image of the original box whose induced intrinsic image lattice is saturated, by first using \(\psi\).

An ambient closed affine inequality \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge t\) pulls back through \(\Phi\) to \((B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf y\ge t-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v\). Since \(B\) has column rank \(R\), the map \(B^{\mathsf T}:\mathbb R^N\to\mathbb R^R\) is surjective. Thus every nonconstant intrinsic affine inequality extends to an ambient one; constant inequalities give only full or empty support traces, also obtainable on a finite support or covered by the zero-dimensional convention. This proves equality of the cap-trace families under the bijection, with the same containment relation for every root. Each trace has the same mass because individual weights are pushed forward unchanged. Hence \(T\), \(\pi\), \(h_c\), \(\tau_\pi\), and all their rooted moments are preserved. Exponential capacities are preserved as well: for an intrinsic root \(\mathbf j\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf C_{\pi'}(\Phi(\mathbf j))
 &=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^N}
   \sum_{\mathbf y\in D_{\mathrm{red}}}
   \pi_{\psi(\mathbf y)}
   e^{(B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf j)}\\
 &=\inf_{\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^R}
   \sum_{\mathbf y\in D_{\mathrm{red}}}
   \pi_{\psi(\mathbf y)}e^{\mathbf w\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf j)}
 =\mathsf C_\pi(\psi(\mathbf j)).
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality also uses the same coordinate restriction and extension for \(\psi\). These cap and capacity identities need only injectivity; saturation is what establishes ambient integer completeness.

Surjectivity also supplies an ambient tilt satisfying \(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell'=\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{red}}\). For any such choice, substitution proves the exact prefactor formula:
\[
b'e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\Phi(\mathbf y)}
 =b_{\mathrm{red}}e^{-\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf v}
   e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot(\mathbf v+B\mathbf y)}
 =b_{\mathrm{red}}e^{\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{red}}\cdot\mathbf y}
 =c_{\psi(\mathbf y)}.
\]
Conversely, substitution of \(\mathbf z=\mathbf v+B\mathbf y\) in any given ambient log-affine weights yields intrinsic prefactor \(b'e^{\boldsymbol\ell'\cdot\mathbf v}\) and tilt \(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell'\). Their normalizing sum factorizes into the geometric factors on \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\), so their original law is exactly of the already proved type. When \(R=0\), the equation for the empty tilt is vacuous and the formula still retains the sole weight exactly.

All the actual moment bounds therefore transport to every asserted chart. Finally, \(N\ge R\) implies \(4^{-R}\ge4^{-N}\) and \(8^{-R}\ge8^{-N}\), which proves the weaker ambient-dimensional bounds without changing the coefficient or normalization.
