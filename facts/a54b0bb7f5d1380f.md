## Statement

Let \(r\ge32\) be an integer and set
$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=r^4,&m&=r^3,&h&=k-m,&R&=r^2,\\
b&=2^{20k}k^2,&\beta&=0,&T&=(R+1/2)b.
\end{aligned}
$$
Partition \([k]=\lbrace1,\ldots,k\rbrace\) into a cheap group \(P\) of size \(m\) and an expensive group of size \(h\). For \(i\in P\), put \(B_i=b\); otherwise put \(B_i=2b\). Set \(C_{4,i}=2B_i-1\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_i(0)&=0,&g_i(1)&=1,&g_i(2)&=B_i,&g_i(3)&=C_{4,i},\\
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),\qquad
f_E=\begin{cases}T-E+1,&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}\\
Z_4&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},&
Z_3&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The arguments of \(f_E\) are nonnegative integers. These are exactly the original complete height-tail normalizers of accepted fact `a2136c3725dae630`, with its minimum-cost pool \(P\), minimum cost \(b\), and pool size \(m\) identical to those here. The number \(b\) is even, \(T\) is an integer, and \(\lfloor T/b\rfloor=R=\sqrt{k}\).

For a ternary vector, let \(a\) count its cheap twos and \(q\) its expensive twos. Write \(Z_3^{\rm good}\) for the sum of its original height-tail weights over \(q\ge R/1024\). Then
$$
Z_3^{\rm good}\ge\frac12Z_3,
\qquad
\exp(r/8192)\le\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}\le\exp(38r).
\tag{1}
$$
Consequently \(Z_4/Z_3\to\infty\) along the displayed finite family, even with \(R=\sqrt{k}\).

To state the distinction from the depth moment precisely, put
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_T^{(4)}&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}&=1/Z_4,\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H),\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T^{(4)}}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3,
\end{aligned}
$$
where \(H\) ranges over closed affine halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\) with nonzero normal. Accepted fact `69083720ffaaff7c` gives \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\rm two}8^{-(k+1)}\) with its same absolute \(\kappa_{\rm two}>0\). No locked symbol \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) is redefined here.

## Proof

We retain the original costs \(0\) and \(1\), including their effect on every height interval. All counts below have unit spatial multiplicities and unit weight at each allowed integer height.

First, \(b>2k\). Consider a four-site vector with \(a\) cheap twos, \(j\) cheap threes, \(q\) expensive twos, and \(\ell\) expensive threes. Let \(n_0=k-a-j-q-\ell\) be the number of remaining coordinates, each at site \(0\) or \(1\), and put \(u=a+2j+2q+4\ell\). If \(v\) of the remaining coordinates are ones, its exact energy is \(bu-j-\ell+v\). If \(u\le R\), this is at most \(bR+k<T\), so all \(2^{n_0}\) binary choices are feasible. If \(u\ge R+1\), it is at least \(b(R+1)-k>T\), so none is feasible. Thus the original feasibility condition is exactly \(u\le R\).

For fixed feasible occupancies, summing the complete tails \(T-\Gamma+1\) over those binary choices gives
$$
2^{n_0}\left[b(R+1/2-u)+1+j+\ell-\frac{n_0}{2}\right].
\tag{2}
$$
Indeed, each one contributes one to \(\Gamma\), and the sum of the number of ones over all binary choices is \(n_0 2^{n_0}/2\), also zero when \(n_0=0\). The \(+1\) in (2) retains both integer height endpoints. In particular the exact full four-site count is
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_4={}&\sum_{\substack{a,j,q,\ell\ge0,\ a+j\le m,\ q+\ell\le h\\
a+2j+2q+4\ell\le R}}
\binom{m}{a}\binom{m-a}{j}\binom{h}{q}\binom{h-q}{\ell}
2^{k-a-j-q-\ell}\\
&\quad\cdot\left[b(R+1/2-a-2j-2q-4\ell)+1+j+\ell
-\frac{k-a-j-q-\ell}{2}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Every summation variable in this proof is an integer. With \(j=\ell=0\), define for \(a,q\ge0\), \(a\le m\), \(q\le h\), and \(s:=a+2q\le R\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{a,q}&=b(R+1/2-a-2q)+1-\frac{k-a-q}{2},\\
w_{a,q}&=\binom{m}{a}\binom{h}{q}2^{k-a-q}L_{a,q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Thus \(w_{a,q}\) is the exact original ternary mass of that occupancy stratum and \(Z_3=\sum_{a+2q\le R}w_{a,q}\), with the size bounds understood. In particular \(L_{a,q}\ge b/2+1-k/2>1\). The formula is an exact sum of the original tails; it does not identify sites \(0\) and \(1\).

We next prove the asserted original mass fraction using two injective maps on occupancy pairs. For pairs with \(s<R/2\), the pair \((a+1,q)\) remains feasible. Since \(L_{a+1,q}=L_{a,q}-b+1/2\), the exact ratio is
$$
\frac{w_{a+1,q}}{w_{a,q}}
=\frac{m-a}{2(a+1)}\frac{L_{a,q}-b+1/2}{L_{a,q}}.
\tag{5}
$$
Here \(a+1\le R\), \(m-a\ge m/2\), and
\(L_{a,q}\ge b(R/2+1/2)+1-k/2\ge bR/2+1\ge2b\).
Therefore the two factors in (5) are at least \(r/4\) and \(1/2\), respectively. Since \(r\ge32\), (5) is at least \(r/8\ge4\). Distinct pairs have distinct images, all among the pairs summed in \(Z_3\). It follows that
$$
\sum_{a+2q<R/2}w_{a,q}\le\frac14Z_3.
\tag{6}
$$

Now consider the pairs with \(s\ge R/2\) and \(q<R/1024\). They satisfy \(a=s-2q\ge R/3\), so \(a-1\ge R/4\) because \(R\ge1024\). The pair \((a-2,q+1)\) is feasible with the same \(s\); its number of binary coordinates increases by one, and \(L_{a-2,q+1}=L_{a,q}-1/2\). Consequently
$$
\frac{w_{a-2,q+1}}{w_{a,q}}
=\frac{2a(a-1)}{(m-a+1)(m-a+2)}
\frac{h-q}{q+1}\frac{L_{a,q}-1/2}{L_{a,q}}.
\tag{7}
$$
The inequalities needed for this comparison are
$$
\begin{aligned}
a(a-1)&\ge R^2/12,&
(m-a+1)(m-a+2)&\le4m^2,\\
h-q&\ge k-m-R\ge k/2,&
q+1&\le R/512,&
\frac{L_{a,q}-1/2}{L_{a,q}}&\ge1/2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The bound on \(h-q\) uses \(m/k+R/k=1/r+1/r^2\le1/2\); the bound on \(q+1\) uses \(R\ge1024\). These also show \(q+1\le h\), so the image pair is defined. Since \(Rk=m^2\), the right side of (7) is at least
$$
\frac{2(R^2/12)}{4m^2}\frac{k/2}{R/512}\frac12
=\frac{16}{3}\frac{Rk}{m^2}
=\frac{16}{3}\ge4.
\tag{8}
$$
The second map is injective as well, and its images again occur in \(Z_3\). Hence
$$
\sum_{\substack{a+2q\ge R/2\\q<R/1024}}w_{a,q}\le\frac14Z_3.
\tag{9}
$$
The strata with \(q<R/1024\) are contained in the union of the strata in (6) and (9). Adding those bounds proves \(Z_3^{\rm good}\ge Z_3/2\). This fraction is proved for the exact original mass, with no auxiliary probability law or coefficient asymptotic.

For the normalizer comparison, fix any feasible original ternary occupancy pair \((a,q)\). Inside the four-site support, consider all vectors with exactly \(a\) cheap twos, no expensive threes, and exactly \(q\) sites among cheap threes and expensive twos. If \(j\) of those \(q\) sites are cheap threes, then there are \(q-j\) expensive twos. The number of binary coordinates is still \(k-a-q\), the degree remains \(a+2q\), and the exact height-tail factor is \(L_{a,q}+j\), by (2). The mass \(V_{a,q}\) of this family is therefore
$$
V_{a,q}=\binom{m}{a}2^{k-a-q}
\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom{m-a}{j}\binom{h}{q-j}(L_{a,q}+j),
\tag{10}
$$
where a binomial coefficient is zero if its lower index exceeds its nonnegative upper index. All these vectors are original feasible vectors by the first paragraph of the proof. Summing over the choices of \(j\) and dropping only its nonnegative addition to the tail factor gives the exact finite Vandermonde comparison
$$
V_{a,q}\ge
\binom{m}{a}2^{k-a-q}L_{a,q}\binom{h+m-a}{q}
=D_{a,q}w_{a,q},
\qquad
D_{a,q}:=\frac{\binom{h+m-a}{q}}{\binom{h}{q}}.
\tag{11}
$$
For completeness, the binomial identity in (11) counts \(q\)-element subsets of a disjoint union of sets of sizes \(m-a\) and \(h\), classified by their number \(j\) in the first set. Families in (10) are disjoint as \((a,q)\) varies: their vectors recover \(a\) as the cheap-two count and \(q\) as the sum of the cheap-three and expensive-two counts. Thus \(Z_4\ge\sum_{a+2q\le R}V_{a,q}\).

Always \(D_{a,q}\ge1\). For a good pair, \(q\ge R/1024\), while \(a\le R\) gives \(m-a\ge m-R\ge m/2\). Since \(h\le k\) and \(q\le h\),
$$
D_{a,q}
=\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}\left(1+\frac{m-a}{h-i}\right)
\ge\left(1+\frac1{2r}\right)^q
\ge\exp\left(\frac{q}{4r}\right)
\ge\exp(r/4096).
\tag{12}
$$
The logarithmic inequality used here is \(\log(1+x)\ge x/2\) for \(0\le x\le1\), which follows by integrating \(1/(1+t)\ge1/2\) on \([0,x]\).

Combining (11), (12), and the proved good mass fraction yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_4}{Z_3}
&\ge1+\bigl(\exp(r/4096)-1\bigr)\frac{Z_3^{\rm good}}{Z_3}\\
&\ge\frac{1+\exp(r/4096)}2
\ge\exp(r/8192).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
The last inequality is \((1+y^2)/2\ge y\) with \(y=\exp(r/8192)\). This proves the lower bound for every integer \(r\ge32\), without absorbing a prefactor into an unspecified cutoff.

For the upper bound in (1), apply accepted fact `a2136c3725dae630` to these identical \(g_i,\Gamma,T,\beta,f_E,Z_4,Z_3,b,P,m,R\). Its hypotheses hold because \(B_i\ge2\) and \(C_{4,i}=2B_i-1\). Since \(R^2/m=r\), its original normalizer bound gives \(Z_4/Z_3\le\exp(38r)\).

Finally, the map to accepted fact `69083720ffaaff7c` is \(k_1=m\), \(k_2=h\), \(I_1=P\), \(I_2=[k]\setminus P\), \(B_1=b\), \(B_2=2b\), with its \(C_{4,i},g_i,\Gamma,T,\beta\) identical to ours. Its original \(A_T,Z,p,\tau_p,M_3\) are respectively the present \(A_T^{(4)},Z_4,p,\tau_p,M_3\), with the same ambient halfspaces. Both groups are nonempty, and its theorem includes \(\beta=0\), so its absolute moment bound applies directly. Thus (13) is an obstruction to a uniform comparison with the all-three-site restriction at \(R\le\sqrt{k}\), while the actual full-law moment satisfies the stated absolute bound.
