## Statement

Let \(n,d\geq1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and define
\[
 C_y:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(y,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_y:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y),
 \qquad
 Z_+:=\{y\in\mathbb Z^n:a_y>0\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), put
\[
 \mu_C(H):=
 \sum_{y\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_y:(y,x)\in H\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a positive-volume convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), use the metronoid normalization
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
  f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\ {\rm a.e.},\
  \int_Kf(x)\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(y\in Z_+\), define
\[
 c_y:=\frac1{a_y}\int_{C_y}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 L_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y),
 \qquad
 D_y:=L_y-c_y.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite nonempty label set. Give every
\(\sigma\in\Sigma\) a weight \(\eta_\sigma>0\) and two endpoint
occurrences
\[
 a_\sigma,b_\sigma\in Z_+,
 \qquad
 a_\sigma\equiv b_\sigma\pmod2.
\tag{6}
\]
Occurrences are counted with multiplicity, so a loop
\(a_\sigma=b_\sigma=y\) contributes \(2\eta_\sigma\) at \(y\).
Assume the quarter-volume endpoint cap
\[
 \ell_y:=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\eta_\sigma
 \left(
  \mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma=y\}}+
  \mathbf1_{\{b_\sigma=y\}}
 \right)
 \leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in Z_+).
\tag{7}
\]
For every label, set
\[
 m_\sigma:=\frac{a_\sigma+b_\sigma}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 q_\sigma:=\frac{c_{a_\sigma}+c_{b_\sigma}}2,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
 B_\sigma:=\frac{D_{a_\sigma}+D_{b_\sigma}}2,
 \qquad
 K_\sigma:=q_\sigma+B_\sigma
 =\frac{L_{a_\sigma}+L_{b_\sigma}}2,
 \qquad
 P:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\eta_\sigma.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\Sigma\neq\varnothing\) and its endpoints lie in \(Z_+\),
\(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\neq\varnothing\). Hence
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\ {\rm closed\ halfspace}\\p\in H}}\mu_C(H)
\tag{10}
\]
is a well-defined nonnegative real number. Write
\[
 \mathcal D:=2^n h_{\max}(C).
\tag{11}
\]

Fix a linear subspace \(W\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
 k:=\dim W,
 \qquad
 V:=\mathbb R^d/W,
 \qquad
 \pi:\mathbb R^d\to V,
\tag{12}
\]
and write
\[
 \bar q_\sigma:=\pi(q_\sigma),
 \qquad
 \bar B_\sigma:=\pi(B_\sigma).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(x\in V\), define
\[
 \|x\|_{\bar B_\sigma}
 :=\inf\{t\geq0:x\in t\bar B_\sigma\}.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(V=\{0\}\), this is the zero gauge of the zero-dimensional body
\(\{0\}\). Define
\[
 \Delta_{\rm ref}:=
 \min_{\bar s:\operatorname{aff}\{m_\sigma:\sigma\in\Sigma\}\to V
                 \ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\eta_\sigma
 \left(
  \|\bar q_\sigma-\bar s(m_\sigma)\|_{\bar B_\sigma}
  -\frac12
 \right)_+.
\tag{15}
\]
The minimum in (15) is attained.

Choose any attaining \(\bar s\), and put
\[
 \mathscr G:=
 \left\{
  \sigma\in\Sigma:
  \|\bar q_\sigma-\bar s(m_\sigma)\|_{\bar B_\sigma}\leq1
 \right\},
 \qquad
 P_{\mathscr G}:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr G}\eta_\sigma.
\tag{16}
\]
There are simultaneous points \(p_\sigma\in K_\sigma\),
\(\sigma\in\mathscr G\), such that
\[
 2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr G}
 \eta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,p_\sigma)}
 \preceq_{\rm hs}\mu_C.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(P_{\mathscr G}>0\), the nonzero measure on the left side of (17)
has continuous residual rank at most \(k\) in the exact sense of
accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. If \(P_{\mathscr G}=0\), no residual rank is
assigned to the zero measure. In all cases,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D\geq
 \frac{2\gamma_d}{k+1}
 \bigl(P-2\Delta_{\rm ref}\bigr)_+.
 }
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently, when \(k\leq3\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D\geq
 \frac{\gamma_d}{2}
 \bigl(P-2\Delta_{\rm ref}\bigr)_+.
 }
\tag{19}
\]

Identify \(V^*\) with \(W^\perp\) through
\(\langle\theta,\pi(x)\rangle:=\langle\theta,x\rangle\), and for a
compact convex \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) write
\[
 h_B(\theta):=\max_{z\in B}\langle\theta,z\rangle.
\tag{20}
\]
Then the exact dual of (15) is the attained maximum
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{\rm ref}=\max\quad&
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \left[
  \langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle
  -\frac12h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right]\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&
 \theta_\sigma\in W^\perp,\\
 &h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)\leq\eta_\sigma
 \qquad(\sigma\in\Sigma),\\
 &\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\theta_\sigma=0,\\
 &\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \theta_\sigma m_\sigma^{\mathsf T}=0.
 \end{aligned}
 }
\tag{21}
\]
When \(W=\mathbb R^d\), the unique feasible force family is
\(\theta_\sigma=0\), and both sides of (21) are zero.

Fix any maximizing force family \((\theta_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\).
For \(y\in Z_+\), define the occurrence multiplicity and collapsed
endpoint force
\[
 N_{\sigma,y}:=
 \mathbf1_{\{a_\sigma=y\}}+
 \mathbf1_{\{b_\sigma=y\}},
 \qquad
 U_y:=\frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 N_{\sigma,y}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{22}
\]
Then
\[
 U_y\in W^\perp,
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_y=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in Z_+}U_yy^{\mathsf T}=0,
\tag{23}
\]
and the endpoint support loads obey
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{D_y}(U_y)
 \leq
 \frac12\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 \leq\ell_y
 \leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in Z_+).
 }
\tag{24}
\]

For each label, choose
\[
 d_{\sigma,a}\in D_{a_\sigma},
 \qquad
 d_{\sigma,b}\in D_{b_\sigma}
\tag{25}
\]
with
\[
 \langle\theta_\sigma,d_{\sigma,a}\rangle
 =h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma),
 \qquad
 \langle\theta_\sigma,d_{\sigma,b}\rangle
 =h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma),
\tag{26}
\]
and define
\[
 x_{\sigma,a}:=
 c_{a_\sigma}-\frac12d_{\sigma,a}\in L_{a_\sigma},
 \qquad
 x_{\sigma,b}:=
 c_{b_\sigma}-\frac12d_{\sigma,b}\in L_{b_\sigma},
\tag{27}
\]
\[
 r_\sigma:=
 \frac{x_{\sigma,a}+x_{\sigma,b}}2
 =
 q_\sigma-\frac14(d_{\sigma,a}+d_{\sigma,b})
 \in K_\sigma.
\tag{28}
\]
These actual reflected contacts realize the dual objective:
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{\rm ref}
 =\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \langle\theta_\sigma,r_\sigma\rangle.
 }
\tag{29}
\]
They also satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \eta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,r_\sigma)}
 \preceq_{\rm hs}\mu_C,
 }
\tag{30}
\]
and every \((m_\sigma,r_\sigma)\) lies in
\(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), with all loop and collision
multiplicities retained.

No part of (21)-(30) identifies the reflected contact measure, its
signed stress objective, or the collapsed endpoint forces with
\(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\). No inequality charging \(\Delta_{\rm ref}\)
to an existing terminal capacity, and no proof of the weak Oertel
conjecture, is asserted.

## Proof

We first verify the geometry used by the quotient gauges. Fix
\(y\in Z_+\), abbreviate \(K=C_y\), \(a=a_y\), \(c=c_y\), and define
\[
 \Gamma_y:=
 \int_K(x-c)(x-c)^{\mathsf T}\,dx.
\tag{31}
\]
This matrix is positive definite. Indeed, if \(t\neq0\), the
nonnegative function \(\langle t,x-c\rangle^2\) cannot vanish almost
everywhere on the positive-volume convex set \(K\). For every
sufficiently small \(t\in\mathbb R^d\), the density
\[
 f_t(x):=\frac12+\langle t,x-c\rangle
\tag{32}
\]
lies between zero and one on \(K\), has integral \(a/2\), and has
normalized first moment
\[
 \frac2a\int_Kx f_t(x)\,dx
 =
 c+\frac2a\Gamma_yt.
\tag{33}
\]
Thus \(L_y\) contains a neighborhood of \(c_y\). Its defining density
set is convex and weak-* compact, so \(L_y\) is compact and convex.
If \(f\) represents \(p\in L_y\), the complementary density \(1-f\)
represents \(2c_y-p\). Hence \(D_y=L_y-c_y\) is a full-dimensional
compact convex origin-symmetric body.

It follows that every \(B_\sigma\) is full-dimensional, compact,
convex, and origin-symmetric. Its quotient image \(\bar B_\sigma\)
has nonempty interior relative to \(V\), including the case
\(V=\{0\}\). Therefore the gauge in (14) is a finite continuous norm
when \(V\neq\{0\}\), and it is the zero gauge when \(V=\{0\}\).

Let
\[
 A:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_\sigma:\sigma\in\Sigma\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L:=
 \{(\bar s(m_\sigma))_{\sigma\in\Sigma}:
   \bar s:A\to V\ {\rm affine}\}
 \subseteq V^\Sigma.
\tag{34}
\]
The evaluation space \(\mathcal L\) is a finite-dimensional linear
subspace. When \(V\neq\{0\}\), equivalence of the finitely many quotient
gauges with a fixed norm on \(V\) shows that the objective in (15),
viewed as a function of the evaluation tuple in \(\mathcal L\), tends
to infinity with the norm of that tuple. Hence its sublevel sets are
compact and its minimum is attained. Attainment is immediate when
\(V=\{0\}\).

Fix an attaining \(\bar s\). Every label outside \(\mathscr G\) has
gauge strictly larger than one and therefore contributes strictly more
than \(\eta_\sigma/2\) to (15). Consequently,
\[
 P_{\mathscr G}\geq P-2\Delta_{\rm ref}.
\tag{35}
\]
For \(\sigma\in\mathscr G\), the gauge bound and closedness of
\(\bar B_\sigma\) give \(e_\sigma\in B_\sigma\) such that
\[
 \pi(e_\sigma)=
 \bar q_\sigma-\bar s(m_\sigma).
\tag{36}
\]
Set \(p_\sigma:=q_\sigma-e_\sigma\). Symmetry of \(B_\sigma\) gives
\(p_\sigma\in K_\sigma\). Choose a linear right inverse of \(\pi\) and
use it to lift \(\bar s\) to an affine map
\(s:A\to\mathbb R^d\). Equation (36) yields
\[
 p_\sigma-s(m_\sigma)\in W
 \qquad(\sigma\in\mathscr G).
\tag{37}
\]

The restricted label family still satisfies the cap (7). Accepted
fact `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd` applied to the simultaneous choices \(p_\sigma\)
proves (17), including mixed-integer support and all collision
multiplicities. Suppose first that \(P_{\mathscr G}>0\). Let
\(\rho_{\mathscr G}\) and \(r_{\mathscr G}\) denote the intrinsic base
and continuous residual ranks of the nonzero measure in (17), as
defined in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Equation (37) gives
\[
 r_{\mathscr G}\leq k,
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathscr G}\leq n.
\tag{38}
\]
The dominated-measure residual theorem in `be359f8a6c628483` therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D
 &\geq
 2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathscr G}}
 \frac{P_{\mathscr G}}{r_{\mathscr G}+1}\\
 &\geq
 \frac{2\gamma_d}{k+1}
 \bigl(P-2\Delta_{\rm ref}\bigr)_+.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
If \(P_{\mathscr G}=0\), equation (35) gives
\((P-2\Delta_{\rm ref})_+=0\). The mixed-integer support is nonempty
by (6), so \(\mathcal D\geq0\), and (18) again follows, without
assigning ranks to a zero measure. Equation (19) follows from
\(k+1\leq4\). This proves the complete small-defect branch with the
full \(2\gamma_d\) coefficient from `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd`.

We turn to the exact dual. If \(B\) is a full-dimensional compact
convex origin-symmetric body in a finite-dimensional vector space,
polar gauge duality gives
\[
 \|x\|_B
 =
 \max_{h_B(w)\leq1}\langle w,x\rangle.
\tag{40}
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), positive homogeneity then gives
\[
 \eta\left(\|x\|_B-\frac12\right)_+
 =
 \max_{h_B(\theta)\leq\eta}
 \left[
  \langle\theta,x\rangle-\frac12h_B(\theta)
 \right].
\tag{41}
\]
To see the factor exactly, write a nonzero feasible force as
\(\theta=t w\), where \(h_B(w)=1\) and \(0<t\leq\eta\), maximize over
\(w\), and then maximize the scalar linear function
\(t(\|x\|_B-1/2)\) over \(0\leq t\leq\eta\). Formula (41) is also
valid on the zero vector space, where both sides vanish.

Define the continuous convex function
\[
 F((z_\sigma)_\sigma):=
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \eta_\sigma
 \left(
  \|z_\sigma\|_{\bar B_\sigma}-\frac12
 \right)_+
 \qquad((z_\sigma)_\sigma\in V^\Sigma).
\tag{42}
\]
Equation (41) says that the conjugate of the \(\sigma\)-summand is
\[
 \theta\longmapsto
 \frac12h_{\bar B_\sigma}(\theta)
 +
 I_{\{h_{\bar B_\sigma}(\theta)\leq\eta_\sigma\}}(\theta).
\tag{43}
\]
Finite-dimensional Fenchel duality applied to
\(\min_{z\in\mathcal L}F(\bar q-z)\) has no gap because \(F\) is
finite and continuous. It gives
\[
 \Delta_{\rm ref}
 =
 \max_{\theta\in\mathcal L^\perp}
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
 \left[
  \langle\theta_\sigma,\bar q_\sigma\rangle
  -\frac12h_{\bar B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right],
\tag{44}
\]
under the constraints
\(h_{\bar B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)\leq\eta_\sigma\). The feasible set
is compact because each \(\bar B_\sigma\) has interior in \(V\), so
the maximum is attained. Under \(V^*=W^\perp\),
\[
 \langle\theta_\sigma,\bar q_\sigma\rangle
 =\langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle,
 \qquad
 h_{\bar B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 =h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma).
\tag{45}
\]

A force tuple belongs to \(\mathcal L^\perp\) if and only if
\[
 \sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma m_\sigma^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{46}
\]
Necessity follows by testing constant maps and restrictions of ambient
linear maps. Conversely, every affine map on \(A\) extends to an
affine map on \(\mathbb R^n\), and the two identities in (46)
annihilate every such evaluation tuple. Substitution of (45)-(46)
into (44) proves (21), including the zero-dimensional quotient.

We now audit the endpoint collapse. The identity
\(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}=2\) counts the two endpoint occurrences even for
a loop. Thus
\[
 \sum_yU_y
 =
 \frac12\sum_\sigma
 \left(\sum_yN_{\sigma,y}\right)\theta_\sigma
 =
 \sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma
 =0.
\tag{47}
\]
Likewise, using \(a_\sigma+b_\sigma=2m_\sigma\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_yU_yy^{\mathsf T}
 &=
 \frac12\sum_\sigma
 \theta_\sigma(a_\sigma+b_\sigma)^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=
 \sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma m_\sigma^{\mathsf T}
 =0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Every \(U_y\) lies in \(W^\perp\). Since each \(D_y\) is
origin-symmetric, its support function is nonnegative. If \(y\) is
one endpoint occurrence of label \(\sigma\), then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac12h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 &\leq
 \frac12\left[
  h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
  +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right]\\
 &=
 h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 \leq\eta_\sigma.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{49}
\]
Subadditivity and positive homogeneity of \(h_{D_y}\), followed by
(49) once for every endpoint occurrence, give
\[
 h_{D_y}(U_y)
 \leq
 \frac12\sum_\sigma
 N_{\sigma,y}h_{D_y}(\theta_\sigma)
 \leq
 \sum_\sigma N_{\sigma,y}\eta_\sigma
 =\ell_y.
\tag{50}
\]
Together with (7), this proves (23)-(24), with no lost factor of
\(1/2\).

Finally, compactness of the endpoint bodies gives the support
maximizers in (25)-(26), also when \(\theta_\sigma=0\). Convexity and
origin symmetry imply \(-d/2\in D_y\) for every \(d\in D_y\), proving
the memberships in (27). Moreover,
\[
 \frac{d_{\sigma,a}+d_{\sigma,b}}2\in B_\sigma,
\]
so (28) indeed gives \(r_\sigma\in q_\sigma+B_\sigma=K_\sigma\).
The support function of the Minkowski average is
\[
 h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma)
 =
 \frac12\left[
  h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
  +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right].
\tag{51}
\]
Therefore, label by label,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle\theta_\sigma,r_\sigma\rangle
 &=
 \langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle
 -\frac14\left[
  h_{D_{a_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
  +h_{D_{b_\sigma}}(\theta_\sigma)
 \right]\\
 &=
 \langle\theta_\sigma,q_\sigma\rangle
 -\frac12h_{B_\sigma}(\theta_\sigma).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{52}
\]
Summing (52) and using optimality in (21) proves (29). The complete
label family satisfies the cap (7), and every \(r_\sigma\in K_\sigma\).
Accepted fact `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd` applied to these simultaneous choices proves
(30) and the mixed-integer support assertion, including repeated
endpoints, loops, and coincident contact atoms.

Accepted facts `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd` and `be359f8a6c628483` do not make the measure in
(30) admissible for \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\), and the signed objective
in (29) is not a halfspace-dominated mass. Thus the final limitation
in the statement is required by the proved interface.
