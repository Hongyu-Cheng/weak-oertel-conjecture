## Statement

Use the two-overlap convention of accepted fact d8676f01de073eb0, with the following explicit hypotheses and definitions. Let \(m_0,m_1\) be positive integers, and let \(X\) be partitioned into \(m_0\) pairs of label zero and \(m_1\) pairs of label one. Let \(c_0,c_1\) be distinct atoms of \(X\), with \(c_1\) an endpoint of one label-zero pair and \(c_0\) an endpoint of one label-one pair. Give every atom a positive original weight \(w_x\), retain the original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in X}w_x\) and original probabilities \(p_x=w_x/Z\), and put \(a_a=p_{c_a}\). For each \(a\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), require every label-\(a\) pair to have endpoint-probability product \(a_a^2\). In particular, the two forced pair totals are \(L_0=a_1+a_0^2/a_1\) and \(L_1=a_0+a_1^2/a_0\).

Let \(S_a\) be the total original probability of the endpoints of label-\(a\) pairs, and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=m_0+m_1,& S_0+S_1&=1,\\
\mu_a&=\frac{S_a/a_a+2}{2(m_a+1)},&
A&=K^2\sum_{x\in X}p_x^3,\\
\Delta_a&=4m_a(m_a+1)K^2a_a^3,&
H&=\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{\Delta_a}{\mu_a}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(m_a\) counts all pairs, including the forced pair, and \(K\) is the original occupied parity count. Assume there are distinct labels \(s,b\) with \(1\le m_s\le99\) and \(m_b\ge2^{40}\). Then, with arbitrary positive nonforced pair weights,
$$
1-A\le\frac34H.
$$
If also \(A<1\) and \(A_{\mathrm{full}}:=A+\Delta_0+\Delta_1\le9/8\), define the exact Shapley increments
$$
r_a=\frac12\left[
\log\frac{A+\Delta_a}{A}
+\log\frac{A_{\mathrm{full}}}{A+\Delta_{1-a}}
\right].
$$
Then
$$
-\log A\le\sum_{a=0}^1\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}\,r_a.
$$

## Proof

Write \(t_{a,j}\) for the total original probability of the \(j\)-th label-\(a\) pair, relabeling pairs so that \(t_{a,1}=L_a\). Use the algebraic rescalings
$$
\eta_a=Ka_a,\qquad v_{a,j}=Kt_{a,j},\qquad
\bar v_a=\frac{KS_a}{m_a},\qquad \lambda_a=KL_a.
\tag{1}
$$
These are scaled original probabilities, without any change of law. The pair partition gives \(\sum_{a,j}v_{a,j}=K\), and arithmetic-geometric mean gives \(v_{a,j}\ge2\eta_a\), hence \(2\eta_a\le\bar v_a\). The forced pairs give exactly
$$
\lambda_a=\eta_{1-a}+\frac{\eta_a^2}{\eta_{1-a}}.
\tag{2}
$$

Define \(\psi(v)=v^3-3v+2=(v-1)^2(v+2)\) for \(v\ge0\), and put
$$
\mathcal D=\sum_{a,j}\psi(v_{a,j}),\qquad
\mathcal C_a=3m_a\bar v_a\eta_a^2-
\frac{6m_a(m_a+1)^2\eta_a^4}{m_a\bar v_a+2\eta_a}.
\tag{3}
$$
In particular, \(\mathcal D\ge0\). For any pair of total \(t\) and endpoint product \(a_a^2\), its cubic contribution is \(t^3-3a_a^2t\). Consequently
$$
KA=\sum_{a,j}v_{a,j}^3-3\sum_a m_a\bar v_a\eta_a^2.
$$
Also \(\mu_a=(m_a\bar v_a+2\eta_a)/(2(m_a+1)\eta_a)\), so \(K(3/4)\Delta_a/\mu_a\) is the positive fraction in (3). There are exactly \(K\) pairs and their scaled totals sum to \(K\). Therefore the exact identity is
$$
K\left(A+\frac34H-1\right)=\mathcal D-\mathcal C_0-\mathcal C_1.
\tag{4}
$$

Since \(2\eta_a\le\bar v_a\), the denominator in (3) is at most \((m_a+1)\bar v_a\). Completing a square therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_a
&\le3m_a\bar v_a\eta_a^2-
\frac{6m_a(m_a+1)}{\bar v_a}\eta_a^4\\
&=\frac{3m_a}{8(m_a+1)}\bar v_a^3-
\frac{6m_a(m_a+1)}{\bar v_a}
\left(\eta_a^2-\frac{\bar v_a^2}{4(m_a+1)}\right)^2\\
&\le\frac{3m_a}{8(m_a+1)}\bar v_a^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

Suppose for contradiction that \(A+(3/4)H<1\). By (4),
$$
\mathcal D<\mathcal C_0+\mathcal C_1.
\tag{6}
$$
We first obtain an absolute bound valid under this failure assumption for every pair of positive counts. The function \(\psi\) is convex on the nonnegative half-line, since \(\psi''(v)=6v\). Thus Jensen within each core gives
$$
\mathcal D\ge\sum_a m_a\psi(\bar v_a).
\tag{7}
$$
For every \(v\ge0\), the following polynomial identity proves \(v^3\le2\psi(v)+2\):
$$
2\psi(v)+2-v^3
=v\left(v-\frac32\right)^2+
3\left(v-\frac{11}{8}\right)^2+\frac{21}{64}>0.
$$
Keep the factor \(m_a/(m_a+1)\) in (5). Since \(m_a+1\ge2\) and \(\sum_a m_a/(m_a+1)<2\), equations (5)-(7) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D
&<\frac38\sum_a\frac{m_a}{m_a+1}\bar v_a^3\\
&\le\frac34\sum_a\frac{m_a}{m_a+1}\psi(\bar v_a)
+\frac34\sum_a\frac{m_a}{m_a+1}\\
&<\frac38\mathcal D+\frac32.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence \(\mathcal D<12/5\).

Set \(m=m_s\) and \(M=m_b\). The function \(\psi\) decreases on \([0,1]\) and increases on \([1,\infty)\), because \(\psi'(v)=3(v^2-1)\). From (7), \(\mathcal D<12/5\), and \(\psi(2)=4\), we get \(\bar v_s<2\). The mass identity is \(M\bar v_b+m\bar v_s=M+m\). Positivity of \(\bar v_s\) and \(M\ge2^{40}\) therefore gives \(\bar v_b<1+99/M<101/100\).

Also \(\bar v_b>1/2\). Indeed, otherwise (7), monotonicity on \([0,1]\), and \(M\ge4\) would give \(\mathcal D\ge M\psi(1/2)\ge5/2>12/5\). We have proved
$$
\bar v_s<2,\qquad\frac12<\bar v_b<\frac{101}{100}.
\tag{8}
$$
Both \(\lambda_a\) occur among the actual \(v_{a,j}\), so \(\psi(\lambda_a)\le\mathcal D<12/5\). Since \(\psi(2)=4\) and \(\psi\) increases above one, this forces \(\lambda_s<2\) and \(\lambda_b<2\). By (5) and \(\bar v_s<2\), we also have \(\mathcal C_s<3\). Equations (6) and \(\mathcal D\ge0\) consequently imply \(\mathcal C_b>-3\).

Put \(z=M\eta_b^2/\bar v_b^2\). The first line of (5) yields
$$
\mathcal C_b\le3\bar v_b^3\left[z-2\left(1+\frac1M\right)z^2\right].
\tag{9}
$$
If \(z\ge3\), then \(z-2z^2\le-15\), as follows from \((z-3)(2z+5)\ge0\). Equations (8)-(9) would imply \(\mathcal C_b\le-45\bar v_b^3<-45/8<-3\), a contradiction. Hence \(z<3\). Using (8) now gives
$$
\eta_b^2<\frac{3(101/100)^2}{M}<\frac9M,
\qquad \eta_b<\frac3{\sqrt M}.
\tag{10}
$$
By (2), \(\lambda_s=\eta_b+\eta_s^2/\eta_b\). Together with \(\lambda_s<2\) and (10), this gives \(\eta_s^2<2\eta_b<6/\sqrt M\).

We now sharpen the absolute bound on \(\mathcal D\). Discarding the negative fraction in (3), then using \(m\le99\), \(\bar v_s<2\), and the preceding bound on \(\eta_s^2\), gives
$$
\mathcal C_s<\frac{3564}{\sqrt M}
\le\frac{3564}{2^{20}}<\frac1{100}.
\tag{11}
$$
The last comparison is the exact integer inequality \(356400<1048576\). Also (5) and (8) give
$$
\mathcal C_b<\frac38\left(\frac{101}{100}\right)^3<\frac{39}{100},
\tag{12}
$$
where the second comparison is \(3090903<3120000\) after multiplication by \(8000000\). Equations (6), (11), and (12) imply \(\mathcal D<2/5\).

Since \(\psi(1/2)=5/8\) and \(\psi(3/2)=7/8\), monotonicity and \(\psi(\lambda_a)\le\mathcal D\) now force
$$
\frac12<\lambda_s,\lambda_b<\frac32.
\tag{13}
$$
The forced relations (2) read \(\lambda_b=(\eta_b^2+\eta_s^2)/\eta_s\) and \(\lambda_s=(\eta_b^2+\eta_s^2)/\eta_b\). Direct substitution therefore verifies the exact inversion
$$
\eta_b=\frac{\lambda_b^2\lambda_s}{\lambda_b^2+\lambda_s^2}.
\tag{14}
$$
The numerator in (14) is greater than \(1/8\) and its denominator is less than \(9/2\) by (13). Therefore \(\eta_b>1/36\). On the other hand, (10) and \(M\ge2^{40}\) give \(\eta_b<3/2^{20}<1/36\), with the final comparison equivalent to \(108<1048576\). This contradiction proves \(A+(3/4)H\ge1\), and hence the atomic assertion. Every actual pair total was retained throughout; no equality of nonforced pair totals was assumed.

For the Shapley consequence, \(X\) has exactly \(2K\) atoms. Holder on the original law gives \(\sum_xp_x^3\ge1/(2K)^2\), so \(A\ge1/4\). Pair arithmetic-geometric mean gives \(S_a/a_a\ge2m_a\), hence \(\mu_a\ge1\). The increments \(\Delta_a\) are positive. We can therefore apply the abstract atomic-to-Shapley conversion proved in accepted fact d8676f01de073eb0, equations (15)-(18), with exactly the meanings of \(A,\Delta_a,\mu_a,r_a\) specified above. To exhibit the needed comparison, that conversion gives
$$
r_a\ge\frac{2\Delta_a}{A+A_{\mathrm{full}}},\qquad
\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}\ge\frac{128}{129\mu_a}.
$$
Writing \(\delta=1-A>0\), the atomic inequality and \(A_{\mathrm{full}}\le9/8\) give
$$
\sum_a\frac{256}{256+2\mu_a}r_a
\ge\frac{256H}{129(A+A_{\mathrm{full}})}
\ge\frac{1024\delta}{387(A+9/8)}.
$$
The same accepted conversion proves
$$
(A+9/8)\frac{-\log A}{1-A}
\le\frac{11}{6}\log4<\frac{1024}{387}
\qquad(1/4\le A<1).
$$
Together these inequalities prove the claimed B256 budget, with the original normalizer and occupied \(K\) unchanged.
