## Statement

Use the standard recursive symmetric saturated chain partition of \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) from accepted fact 032423921efad6c8: in dimension zero begin with the one empty chain, and replace each dimension-\(m\) chain \(S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k}\), upon adjoining coordinate \(m+1\), by
$$
S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k},S_{m-k}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace
\quad\text{and}\quad
S_k\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,\ldots,S_{m-k-1}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,
$$
omitting the second chain when empty. Identify sets with their coordinate vectors in the order \(1,\ldots,m\). Let \(D_m\) be the nonterminal chain vertices, write their successors as \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), and define
$$
E_4=\lbrace c+2e_{i_4(c)}:c\in D_4\rbrace,
\qquad
T_4=\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\cap\mathbb Z^4,
\qquad
C_4^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\cap B_4.
$$
Here \(T_4\) is a block integer hull and is not the rooted coordinate tree \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}\) of 032423921efad6c8. For every integer \(Q\ge1\), let \(\nu=4Q\), \(N=\nu+1\), and define the serial objects
$$
\Gamma_{4,Q}=\bigcup_{s=0}^{Q}E_4^s\times B_4^{Q-s},
\qquad
P_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=\operatorname{conv}(\Gamma_{4,Q}),
\qquad
A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}=P_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\cap\mathbb Z^{\nu}.
$$
The superscript \(\mathrm{ser}\) distinguishes these objects from the tree support \(A_{m,Q}\) and its polytope in 032423921efad6c8; their spatial dimension is \(4Q\), not \(\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\). An empty Cartesian power denotes a singleton. Then the exact block and serial completions are
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_4={}&\lbrace
(2,0,0,0),(1,2,0,0),(1,1,2,0),(1,1,1,2),
(2,0,0,1),\phantom{(1,2,0,0)}\\
&\hspace{13mm}(1,2,0,1),(2,0,1,0),(1,0,1,2),
(0,1,2,0),(0,1,1,2)
\rbrace,\\
C_4^{\mathrm{bool}}={}&\lbrace(1,1,1,0),(1,1,1,1)\rbrace,
\qquad T_4=E_4\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_4^{\mathrm{bool}},\\
A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}={}&\bigcup_{s=0}^{Q}T_4^s\times B_4^{Q-s},\\
|A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|={}&
16^Q+10\sum_{s=1}^{Q}12^{s-1}16^{Q-s}
=\frac{7\cdot16^Q-5\cdot12^Q}{2}
<\frac72\,2^{\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For each \(z\) in any nonempty subset \(Z\subseteq A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\), choose a finite nonempty set \(L_z\subset\mathbb R\) of heights. Choose arbitrary original weights \(w_{(z,t)}>0\) for all \(z\in Z\), \(t\in L_z\), and retain exactly the support and normalization
$$
\mathcal I=\lbrace(z,t):z\in Z,\ t\in L_z\rbrace,
\qquad W=\sum_{(z,t)\in\mathcal I}w_{(z,t)},
\qquad p_{(z,t)}=w_{(z,t)}/W.
$$
For every \(\mathbf I\in\mathcal I\), define the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its cubic moment by
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=
\inf\lbrace p(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace},\ \mathbf I\in H\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathcal I}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{1}{128|Z|^3}
\ge\frac{1}{128|A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|^3}
>\frac1{686}\,8^{-N}.
$$
In particular the non-strict uniform bound holds with the explicit absolute constant \(c=1/686\). The heights need not be consecutive, integral, or shared between columns. The conditional height laws need not coincide. Empty columns are represented by omitting their base points from \(Z\). The proof extends the same-column closed-tail estimate appearing in accepted fact cdf4b723cb4dc8ec without importing that fact's common-height-law hypothesis or its tree-support moment conclusion.

## Proof

First compute the standard four-coordinate partition directly from the stated recursion. The partitions in dimensions one, two, and three are, respectively,
$$
\begin{gathered}
(0,1),\\
(00,10,11),\quad(01),\\
(000,100,110,111),\quad(001,101),\quad(010,011).
\end{gathered}
$$
Applying the recursion once more gives the following complete partition in dimension four:
$$
\begin{gathered}
(0000,1000,1100,1110,1111),\qquad
(0001,1001,1101),\\
(0010,1010,1011),\qquad (0011),\qquad
(0100,0110,0111),\qquad(0101).
\end{gathered}
$$
The digit strings in these three displays denote coordinate vectors, not integers. There are ten nonterminal vertices. Replacing the zero coordinate added by each successor with two gives exactly the ten listed members of \(E_4\). This verifies the specific standard partition and the spike coordinates, rather than invoking a result about an arbitrary partition from 032423921efad6c8.

We determine \(C_4^{\mathrm{bool}}\) by rational inclusions and separating inequalities. Number the displayed \(E_4\) vectors in their displayed order as \(v_1,\ldots,v_{10}\). These names are local aliases for those ten four-coordinate vectors. The exact rational identities
$$
(1,1,1,0)=\tfrac14(v_1+v_2+v_3+v_9),
\qquad
(1,1,1,1)=\tfrac18(v_1+v_2+v_3+v_9)+\tfrac12v_4
$$
prove that both proposed Boolean points are in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\).

For the reverse inclusion, every \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\) satisfies the exact linear inequalities
$$
4x_1+4x_2+2x_3+x_4\ge8,
\qquad 2x_1+x_2+3x_3\ge4.
$$
Their validity follows by the following evaluations at every generating vector; convex combinations preserve both inequalities.

| Vector | \(4x_1+4x_2+2x_3+x_4\) | \(2x_1+x_2+3x_3\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(v_1=(2,0,0,0)\) | 8 | 4 |
| \(v_2=(1,2,0,0)\) | 12 | 4 |
| \(v_3=(1,1,2,0)\) | 12 | 9 |
| \(v_4=(1,1,1,2)\) | 12 | 6 |
| \(v_5=(2,0,0,1)\) | 9 | 4 |
| \(v_6=(1,2,0,1)\) | 13 | 4 |
| \(v_7=(2,0,1,0)\) | 10 | 7 |
| \(v_8=(1,0,1,2)\) | 8 | 5 |
| \(v_9=(0,1,2,0)\) | 8 | 7 |
| \(v_{10}=(0,1,1,2)\) | 8 | 4 |

For a Boolean \(x\), if either \(x_1=0\) or \(x_2=0\), the first left side is at most \(4+2+1=7\). Thus the first inequality forces \(x_1=x_2=1\). If also \(x_3=0\), the second left side is three. Hence it forces \(x_3=1\). Only the two witnessed choices for \(x_4\) remain. These two rational linear inequalities are explicit separating certificates for every one of the other fourteen Boolean points: the first excludes every point with \(x_1x_2=0\), and the second excludes the remaining two points with \(x_3=0\). This proves the stated exact Boolean intersection.

To determine the full integer hull, take \(x\in\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\cap\mathbb Z^4\). All its coordinates are in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\). If none is two, \(x\) belongs to the Boolean intersection just determined. Otherwise fix a coordinate \(i\) with \(x_i=2\), and express \(x\) as a convex combination of the ten vectors, discarding zero coefficients. Since every generating coordinate is at most two, every positively weighted vector has its \(i\)-th coordinate equal to two. Each such vector has only Boolean coordinates elsewhere. For each coordinate \(j\ne i\), its integer average lies in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), so all positively weighted vectors agree with that average in coordinate \(j\). They therefore all equal \(x\), and \(x\in E_4\). Conversely all ten generators and both witnessed Boolean points are integer points in the hull. This proves \(T_4=E_4\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_4^{\mathrm{bool}}\) and \(|T_4|=12\). This argument is also the direct single-block realization argument of 032423921efad6c8, applied here to the hull without adjoining all of \(B_4\).

We next prove the serial completion for every \(Q\); no assertion that \(\Gamma_{4,Q}\) is complete is used. Each point of \(P_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\) is in \([0,2]^{4Q}\). Let \(x\) be an integer point in this polytope, with its four-coordinate blocks numbered \(1,\ldots,Q\). If every coordinate is Boolean, then \(x\in B_4^Q\). Otherwise let \(s\) be the largest block number containing a coordinate equal to two, and fix such a coordinate in block \(s\).

Represent \(x\) as a convex combination of generators in \(\Gamma_{4,Q}\) with all coefficients positive. The chosen coordinate can average to two only if every such generator has two in that coordinate. In particular every generator in the representation belongs to a serial level of length at least \(s\), so its first \(s\) blocks all belong to \(E_4\). Averaging each of those blocks gives a point of \(\operatorname{conv}(E_4)\cap\mathbb Z^4=T_4\). By the choice of \(s\), every later block of \(x\) is Boolean. Thus
\(x\in T_4^s\times B_4^{Q-s}\). This proves one inclusion in the proposed union.

For the converse, fix \(s\) and a point of \(T_4^s\times B_4^{Q-s}\). Express each of its first \(s\) blocks as a convex combination of \(E_4\), and keep the Boolean suffix fixed. Taking products of these block coefficients expresses the entire point as a convex combination of \(E_4^s\times B_4^{Q-s}\). This generator set is one of the sets defining \(\Gamma_{4,Q}\). The point is integral and belongs to \(P_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}\), proving the converse inclusion.

For the exact count, the all-Boolean part has \(16^Q\) points. Every other point has a unique largest block number \(s\) containing a two. Its first \(s-1\) blocks can be arbitrary elements of \(T_4\), its block \(s\) must be one of the ten elements of \(E_4\), and its remaining blocks can be arbitrary elements of \(B_4\). The completion already proved shows that all of these choices occur, with no identification between different tuples. Their number is \(10\cdot12^{s-1}16^{Q-s}\). The resulting disjoint count gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
|A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|
&=16^Q+10\sum_{s=1}^{Q}12^{s-1}16^{Q-s}\\
&=16^Q\left[1+\frac{10}{16}\sum_{s=1}^{Q}(3/4)^{s-1}\right]\\
&=16^Q\left[\frac72-\frac52(3/4)^Q\right]
=\frac{7\cdot16^Q-5\cdot12^Q}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular it is strictly less than \((7/2)2^{\nu}\), independently of \(Q\).

It remains to prove the actual cubic-depth assertion with separate column laws. We give the full argument, including the elementary closed-tail estimate used in cdf4b723cb4dc8ec. For any probability law \(q\) on a finite nonempty ordered set \(L\subset\mathbb R\), define
$$
d_q(t)=\min\left\{
\sum_{r\in L:\ r\le t}q_r,
\sum_{r\in L:\ r\ge t}q_r
\right\}\qquad(t\in L).
$$
The heights for which the first closed-tail sum is less than \(1/4\) form an initial segment with total probability less than \(1/4\). Indeed, when this segment is nonempty, its total probability is its first-tail sum at its largest height. The heights for which the second closed-tail sum is less than \(1/4\) form a final segment of total probability less than \(1/4\), by the analogous argument. Outside their union, both closed tails are at least \(1/4\), and this complement has probability at least \(1/2\). Therefore
$$
\sum_{t\in L}q_t d_q(t)^3\ge\frac12\left(\frac14\right)^3=\frac1{128}.
$$
The argument includes singleton laws and arbitrarily large atoms because the two tails are closed.

For every original nonempty column, put
$$
W_z=\sum_{t\in L_z}w_{(z,t)},
\qquad r_z=W_z/W,
\qquad q_{z,t}=w_{(z,t)}/W_z.
$$
Thus \(p_{(z,t)}=r_zq_{z,t}\), every \(q_z\) is a probability law on its own set \(L_z\), and \(\sum_{z\in Z}r_z=1\). This factorization only conditions within each column; it does not assert a common distribution across columns.

Fix an arbitrary original ambient closed halfspace \(H\) containing an original sample \((z,t)\). Write its inequality as \(a\cdot x+\beta u\ge b\). If \(\beta>0\), it contains every original same-column point \((z,r)\) with \(r\ge t\). If \(\beta<0\), it contains every such point with \(r\le t\). If \(\beta=0\), it contains the whole original column, since it contains the sample. Consequently in every case
$$
p(H)\ge r_zd_{q_z}(t),
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\tau_p(z,t)\ge r_zd_{q_z}(t).
$$
This tests every containing halfspace in the full original \(\mathbb R^N\). It neither projects the depth to a smaller ambient space nor discards any original column points in a normalization.

Cubing and sampling with the original law now gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\sum_{z\in Z}\sum_{t\in L_z}
r_zq_{z,t}\,r_z^3d_{q_z}(t)^3\\
&=\sum_{z\in Z}r_z^4
\sum_{t\in L_z}q_{z,t}d_{q_z}(t)^3
\ge\frac1{128}\sum_{z\in Z}r_z^4.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fourth factor of each column mass consists of its original sampling factor and three depth factors. Equivalently, before normalizing, the same estimate reads
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{1}{128W^4}\sum_{z\in Z}W_z^4,
$$
with \(W\) still the sum of every original weight in every original column. Hölder's inequality yields
$$
1=\left(\sum_{z\in Z}r_z\right)^4
\le |Z|^3\sum_{z\in Z}r_z^4.
$$
Combining the last two estimates proves \(M_3(p)\ge1/(128|Z|^3)\). Since \(|Z|\le|A_{4,Q}^{\mathrm{ser}}|<(7/2)2^{\nu}\),
$$
M_3(p)>
\frac{1}{128(7/2)^3}\,8^{-\nu}
=\frac{1}{5488}\,8^{-\nu}
=\frac1{686}\,8^{-(\nu+1)}.
$$
This proves the stated constant for every \(Q\ge1\), with all original heights, all original probabilities, and the full original closed-halfspace depth.
