## Statement

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact and convex, let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ be nonempty, and put $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$. Fix an actual $Y\in S$ and $0<t<1$. Define $L_t=t(S-Y)\cap[-(1-t)(S-Y)]$, $G_t(Y)=\mathcal H_q(L_t)$. Suppose $t=r/m$ with $m=r+s$ and positive coprime integers $r,s$. Write $K_z=\{x:(z,x)\in S\}$. At $Y=(p,x)$, $G_t(p,x)$ is the sum of $\mathcal H_q(tK_z\cap(x-(1-t)K_w))$ over ordered occupied pairs with $rz+sw=mp$. This equation is equivalent to $z=p+s\ell,\ w=p-r\ell$ for $\ell\in\mathbb Z^n$. An ordered pair has integral weighted base precisely if $z\equiv w\pmod m$. For each residue class $c\bmod m$ let $\mu_c$ be the original intrinsic measure restricted to its fibers, $M_c=\mu_c(S)$ and $m_c=\int A\,d\mu_c(A)$. For every nonnegative Borel or bounded signed Borel test $\varphi$,
$$
\int_S \varphi(Y)G_t(Y)\,d\mu(Y)
=[t(1-t)]^q\sum_c\iint\varphi(tA+(1-t)B)\,d\mu_c(A)d\mu_c(B).
$$
In particular the total and vector first moment are $[t(1-t)]^q\sum_c M_c^2$ and $[t(1-t)]^q\sum_c M_cm_c$. If the summand uses the unscaled intersection $K_z\cap((x-(1-t)K_w)/t)$, its pushforward factor is instead $(1-t)^q$.

## Proof

The occupied bases are finite, and the maximum of their fiber dimensions equals $q$. The first scaled endpoint has displacement $t(z-p)$ in its base coordinate. Matching it to the second gives $rz+sw=mp$. For a fixed $p$ distinct solutions have distinct first bases and hence disjoint displacements. In the continuous coordinate translating by $tx$ gives the stated intersection at each displacement. Coprimality in the identity $r(z-p)=-s(w-p)$ yields the parameterization coordinatewise. The weighted base $w+(r/m)(z-w)$ is integral precisely when $z-w\in m\mathbb Z^n$ since $\gcd(r,m)=1$.

Only pairs of $q$-dimensional fibers can have positive $q$-measure. For an integral-output pair of these fibers, convexity places $tK_z+(1-t)K_w$ inside $K_p$. The affine direction of that sum is the sum of the two $q$-dimensional directions, and $\dim K_p\le q$, so both directions coincide with the direction of $K_p$. Use orthonormal coordinates in their common direction, choosing affine origins $x_z,x_w$ and $t x_z+(1-t)x_w$ in the three fibers. In these coordinates the intersection measure is the convolution of the indicators of the scaled sets at the output coordinate. Its support lies in $K_p$. Tonelli and the change of variables from scaled endpoints to original endpoints produce the Jacobian $t^q(1-t)^q$. Sum over the finitely many pairs, grouped by residue class. The same argument for $q=0$ is a count of singleton endpoint pairs, with factor one. The measure identity also gives measurability, for example each intersection-volume summand is the integral of a joint Borel indicator in the common coordinates. Signed bounded tests follow by subtraction of nonnegative tests since the measures are finite.

Take tests $1$ and the bounded coordinate functions on $S$ to compute the moments. For the vector moment both endpoint integrals give $M_cm_c$, multiplied by $t$ and $1-t$. Finally the unscaled summand differs by the factor $t^{-q}$, giving its stated pushforward. At $t=1/2$, $G_t=2^{-q}\mathcal H_q(S\cap(2Y-S))$ and the residue classes are parity classes, with the corresponding factor $2^{-q}$ for the unscaled reflection mass.
