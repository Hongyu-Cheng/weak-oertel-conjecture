## Statement

Let $S$ be the lifted union of a nonempty finite family of positive compact convex $d$-fibers with $d\ge1$, $dm$ the sum of their $d$-volumes, $M=m(S)$ and $C=\operatorname{conv}S$. For each $r\ge d$ and $y\in\operatorname{relint}C$, there is exactly one affine $L$ on $\operatorname{aff}C$ that is strictly positive on $C$ and satisfies
$$
M^{-1}\int_S L^{-r}\,dm=1,\qquad
y=\frac{\int_S wL^{-r-1}\,dm}{\int_S L^{-r-1}\,dm}.
$$
For the single fiber $C=[0,1]$ and $r=2$ the parameter is $L(x)=[y+(1-2y)x]/\sqrt{y(1-y)}$.

## Proof

Translate $y$ to the relative origin and minimize $I(a)=M^{-1}\int_S[1+a\cdot(w-y)]^{-r}\,dm$ over coefficients on the relative direction space giving a strictly positive affine chart on $C$. The chart domain is bounded because $C$ contains a relative ball about $y$. At any boundary coefficient, the nonnegative chart has a zero on $C$, hence a zero on $S$ by affinity and compactness of $S$. On that positive $d$-fiber, 30c3f3ea847fe4b6 gives divergence for the positive approaching charts with exponent $r\ge d$, including the identically zero restriction. Therefore the finite sublevel containing $a=0$ stays away from the boundary, and continuity on the open positive domain gives a minimizer. Strict convexity of $s\mapsto s^{-r}$ gives strict convexity of $I$: two distinct relative affine charts cannot agree almost everywhere on every fiber because their difference would then vanish on every closed fiber and on its affine hull. Differentiating at the unique minimizer is legitimate by uniform chart positivity and yields $\int_S(w-y)[1+a\cdot(w-y)]^{-r-1}\,dm=0$. Scaling the chart by $I(a)^{1/r}$ gives the desired $L$. Conversely write any such $L$ as $L(y)[1+a\cdot(w-y)]$. The tilted-mean identity makes $a$ stationary for $I$, so the strict convexity and the normalization fix $a$ and $L(y)$.

For the interval formula set $A=y$, $B=1-2y$ and $D=A+B=1-y$. The integrals of $(A+Bx)^{-2}$, $(A+Bx)^{-3}$ and $x(A+Bx)^{-3}$ on $[0,1]$ are respectively $1/(AD)$, $(A+D)/(2A^2D^2)$ and $1/(2AD^2)$ by direct substitution, with the constant case $B=0$ given by continuity. Thus the normalized inverse square has mass one and the inverse cube mean is $A/(A+D)=y$.
