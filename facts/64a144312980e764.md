## Statement

Use the triangle frustum, marks, normals and exact near-quantile caps of the preceding lemma, with $\gamma=Q/V=4/9$. For a bounded inward speed $v$ continuous on each closed body facet (traces on adjacent facets may differ), set
$$
D=\int_{\partial K}v\,d\mathcal H^2,\quad
L_i(\beta)=\int_{\partial K\cap H_i(\beta,c_\beta)}v\,d\mathcal H^2,\quad
R_\beta=\frac13\sum_iL_i(\beta)-\gamma D.
$$
The first cap losses below are in raw volume units after subtracting the target-level loss $\gamma D$; the corresponding probability losses divide by $V$. Where the facet speed is used for an actual deformation, assume the first losses on fixed caps and on the whole body are given by these boundary integrals. Write $\Gamma_i=\partial K\cap\{n_i\cdot X=0\}$ and $a_{iF}=\|n_i-(n_i\cdot\nu_F)\nu_F\|$ for each crossed facet $F$ with outer unit normal $\nu_F$, embedding $n_i$ horizontally. Then
$$
R'_0=\frac13\sum_{i,F}\int_{\Gamma_i\cap\operatorname{relint}F}
\frac{v(X,t)(t-t_0)}{a_{iF}}\,d\mathcal H^1.
$$
For $g>0$ the positive normal-recovery weights give fixed-cap response $gR_\beta/\beta$ as $\beta\downarrow0$. This tends to $gR'_0$ when $R_0=0$. The actual convex erosion $K\cap\{t+e\|X\|^2\le1\}$ for $e\ge0$ has $D=(9/2)(1+\varepsilon)^4$ and $R_0=-(2/9)(1+\varepsilon)^4$, so its response tends to $-\infty$.

The actual coarea first moment about $Y$ on the near-quantile section is
$$
M_i(\beta)=\bigl(-2c_\beta v_i,\ 2t_0c_\beta+(2A/3)\beta\bigr),
$$
where $v_i$ is the corresponding triangle vertex. At a fixed $\beta$, when the mark moves at velocity $(\xi,h)$, the normal at bounded velocity $B_i(\beta)$, and the threshold relative to the mark is fixed, the first cap loss is $L_i(\beta)+S_\beta N_i(\beta)\cdot(\xi,h)-B_i(\beta)\cdot M_i(\beta)$. For an actual deformation in this assertion take compact convex bodies $K_e$ with $d_H(K_e,K)=O(|e|)$ in addition to the assumed fixed-cap first losses. If $R_0=0$ and $B_i(\beta)\to B_i(0)$, the level-corrected weighted loss tends to
$$
gR'_0+4gh-\frac{2gA}{9}\sum_i B_{i,t}(0).
$$
With normals and mark fixed, a separate retuning to the moving normalized level $\gamma$ forces threshold velocity $-[L_i(\beta)-\gamma D]/S_\beta$ for each cap.

For $0<a<1/2$, the actual nested homothety $K_e=(1-e)K+e(Y+a e_t)$ has affine-image mark $Y_e=Y+ea e_t$ and $L_i(\beta)-\gamma D=(c_\beta-a\beta)S_\beta$. It has $R_0=0$ and $gR'_0=-4ga$, canceled by the mark term $4ga$. Taking the affine-image thresholds $(1-e)c_\beta$ relative to $Y_e$ makes the normalized response zero at each tilt. The normalized depth at the affine-image mark is unchanged.

## Proof

The unperturbed cut crosses each contributing facet transversely and contains no body edge, as its plane intersects the interior of each triangular height slice, misses all its vertices, and crosses two of its edges. On a crossed facet, use the linear coordinate $s=n_i\cdot X$ with surface coarea factor $1/a_{iF}$. For small $\beta$, the moving boundary is $s+\beta(t-t_0)=c_\beta$ with $c'_0=0$. Integrating along the facet transverse to the section line, the first signed change of the captured surface integral is the boundary integral of $v(t-t_0)/a_{iF}$. To justify differentiation, the changed set on the facet lies in a uniform $O(|\beta|)$ strip, and on lines crossing the section away from its finitely many endpoints the difference quotient converges to the stated integrand by continuity of the facet trace. The strip bound gives domination on the bounded line projection; the endpoints contribute zero length. Facets not crossed have a positive gap. Summing gives the derivative. Normal-recovery weights are $g/(3\beta)$, yielding the fixed-cap limits.

The displayed erosion is convex for $e\ge0$. Its first inward speed is $\|X\|^2$ on the top facet and zero on the other facet interiors. This follows also by integrating the removed vertical thickness at fixed $X$: the thickness divided by $e$ is bounded by $\|X\|^2$, tends to that value for $X$ interior to the top triangle, and tends to zero outside it. The same argument applies in each fixed cap off its top-facet cut line. On $T$, the centroid is zero and the second-moment average of $\|X\|^2$ is $1$ (uniform triangle barycentric coordinates satisfy $\mathbb E\lambda_i^2=1/6$, $\mathbb E\lambda_i\lambda_j=1/12$). Each cap at $\beta=0$ on $T$ is $(2/3)T+v_i/3$ of area $2$. Thus the total integral is $9/2$, and the average of the three cap integrals is $2[(4/9)+(1/9)\sum_i\|v_i\|^2/3]=16/9$. Scaling the top facet by $1+\varepsilon$ multiplies these second-moment integrals by $(1+\varepsilon)^4$. Subtracting $\gamma D$ gives the stated negative $R_0$.

For the section moment, set $r=1+\varepsilon t$, $q=t-t_0$, and $b=c_\beta-\beta q$. At $i=1$ the section in each slice has $X_1=b$ and $-r\le X_2\le r-b$, with raw coarea $dX_2\,dt$. Its length is $2r-b$ and its conditional $X_2$ centroid is $-b/2$. The quantile equation and the two height identities in the preceding lemma give $\int 2rb\,dt=4c_\beta$ and $\int b^2\,dt=8c_\beta$. Therefore the horizontal moments are $(-4c_\beta,2c_\beta)$; the vertical moment is $\int q(2r-b)\,dt=2t_0c_\beta+(2A/3)\beta$. The linear symmetries of determinant modulus one permuting the vertices send this calculation to the other indices and preserve the coarea when the normals transform contragrediently, giving the stated vector.

For a fixed $\beta$, the mark and normal motion increase the cap margin to first order by $B_i(\beta)\cdot(W-Y)-N_i(\beta)\cdot(\xi,h)$. Integrating that affine function on the proper section with the raw coarea gives the change of cap mass on the old body. With a moving body, the difference between the moved and old cap indicators is supported in an $O(|e|)$ strip about the old cutting plane. For convex $K_e$, the support functions of $K$ and $K_e$ differ by at most their Hausdorff distance. The corresponding inner and outer parallel containments confine the symmetric difference to an $O(|e|)$ neighborhood of the old boundary. The cap strip and this boundary neighborhood intersect in volume $O(e^2)$ by transverse facet incidence and finiteness of the facets. Thus the two first changes add. Sign reversal gives the first loss. Summing the terms with weights $g/(3\beta)$ cancels the horizontal mark term by $\sum_i n_i=0$ and the horizontal normal-motion term tends to zero by $c_\beta=O(\beta^2)$. The vertical limits follow from $S_0=4$ and the section moment formula. For separate threshold retuning, increasing the relative threshold at rate $\dot c_i$ adds $S_\beta\dot c_i$ to the loss, so the fixed-mark, fixed-normal mass equation gives the displayed derivative.

The homothety center is interior, since $t_0+a\in(-1,1)$ and $0\in\operatorname{int}T$. Its first speed is $(W-Y-a e_t)\cdot\nu$ on the body boundary and its first total loss is $3V$. Apply the divergence theorem on $K\cap H_i(\beta,c_\beta)$ to $W-Y$ and $e_t$. The section has outward normal $-N_i(\beta)/\|N_i(\beta)\|$ relative to the retained cap; its fluxes are $-c_\beta S_\beta$ and $-\beta S_\beta$. The remaining body-boundary fluxes are therefore $3Q+c_\beta S_\beta$ and $\beta S_\beta$. This gives the exact homothety loss and $R'_0=-4a$. At fixed relative threshold the mark velocity adds $a\beta S_\beta$. The affine-image threshold velocity $-c_\beta$ then adds $-c_\beta S_\beta$, leaving zero level-corrected loss at each tilt. Alternatively, the affine images of the caps and body all scale in volume by $(1-e)^3$, which preserves their probability masses and the normalized depth of the affine-image mark.

For $\beta\ne0$, the selected near-quantile caps all miss $Y$ and their continuous normals sum to $3\beta e_t$. The limiting certificate and its finite response therefore do not give an exact circuit cover of the old source.
