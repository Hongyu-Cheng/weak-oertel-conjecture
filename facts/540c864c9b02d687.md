## Statement

Use the exact canonical quantities of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66: for a convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), let
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 W(C):=\sum_{z:a_z>0}a_z,
\]
let \(Q(C)\) be the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir, let \(G(C):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\), and let
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G(C)+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+2^nh_{\max}(C).
\]

For every \(k\ge402\), let \(C_k\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{2k}\) be the body constructed in accepted fact aa31087efa15757e. Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal A(C_k)\ge G(C_k)\ge\frac{W(C_k)}{64}
 \ge\frac{Q(C_k)}8.}
\tag{1}
\]

For every \(N\ge4\), let \(C^{(N)}\subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^N\) be the body constructed in accepted fact b1466080cb203fb4. Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal A(C^{(N)})\ge G(C^{(N)})
 \ge\frac{W(C^{(N)})}{96}
 \ge\frac{Q(C^{(N)})}{12}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently neither body can satisfy the canonical hard inequality
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac{Q(C)}{100}.
\tag{3}
\]
This exclusion holds independently of the arbitrary legal matching weights in aa31087efa15757e and b1466080cb203fb4; in particular, decreasing their common weight below the displayed quarter-capacity value cannot place either fixed body in the canonical hard branch. No assertion is made that failure of the aligned centroid-extraction condition is repaired for arbitrary legal matchings.

## Proof

We first record the comparison between the upstream reservoir mass and the full mixed mass. In a good parity class \(\xi\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z.
\]
The reservoir formula in accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_\xi
 &=\sum_{\substack{\{u,v\}\subseteq\xi\\u\ne v}}
 \frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}\\
 &=\frac{A_\xi^2-\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2}{8A_\xi}
 \le\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Bad classes contribute zero. Summing (4) over all parity classes proves the universal bound
\[
 Q(C)\le\frac{W(C)}8.
\tag{5}
\]

Consider first \(C_k\) from aa31087efa15757e. Its base dimension is \(n=2\), and its base projection contains the nondegenerate polygon exhibited in that accepted fact. Hence the affine hull of its positive integer-fiber support is all of \(\mathbb R^2\). Apply accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c with the affine slice \(A=\mathbb R^2\). In its notation \(q=n=2\), \(W_A=W(C_k)\), and \(\alpha_2=1/64\). Its global rank-two conclusion therefore gives
\[
 G(C_k)\ge\frac{W(C_k)}{64}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (5) and (6), and using that \(G\) is a nonnegative summand of \(\mathcal A\), proves (1).

We next audit all integer fibers of \(C^{(N)}\), rather than only the endpoint and midpoint fibers used by b1466080cb203fb4. Put
\[
 q_i:=(2i,2i^2)\in\mathbb Z^2,
 \qquad K:=B_2^N,
\]
and define
\[
 \widehat C^{(N)}
 :=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{i=1}^N
 \bigl(\{q_i\}\times(K+3\mathbf e_i)\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^N.
\tag{7}
\]
The defining generators of \(C^{(N)}\) in b1466080cb203fb4 occur at the two heights \(0\) and \(2\), with the same continuous set at both heights. We claim the exact coordinate-ordered identity
\[
 C^{(N)}
 =\{((u,h),x):(u,x)\in\widehat C^{(N)},\ 0\le h\le2\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Every defining generator lies in the right side, so convexity proves one inclusion. Conversely, take \((u,x)\in\widehat C^{(N)}\), choose a convex representation
\[
 (u,x)=\sum_j\lambda_j(q_{i_j},x_j),
 \qquad x_j\in K+3\mathbf e_{i_j},
\]
and let \(h\in[0,2]\). With \(\theta=h/2\),
\[
 ((u,h),x)=\sum_j\lambda_j
 \left((1-\theta)((q_{i_j},0),x_j)
 +\theta((q_{i_j},2),x_j)\right),
\]
which is a convex combination of defining generators of \(C^{(N)}\). This proves (8).

Let
\[
 D:=\{u\in\mathbb Z^2:\operatorname{vol}_N(\widehat C^{(N)}_u)>0\},
 \qquad b_u:=\operatorname{vol}_N(\widehat C^{(N)}_u).
\]
Equation (8) gives the exact positive integer-fiber support and volumes
\[
 I_+(C^{(N)})=D\times\{0,1,2\},
 \qquad a_{(u,h)}=b_u
 \quad(u\in D,\ h\in\{0,1,2\}).
\tag{9}
\]
For completeness, every \(u\) in the base projection of \(\widehat C^{(N)}\) has a positive-volume fiber: a barycentric representation of \(u\) by the \(q_i\) puts a translate of
\(
 \sum_i\lambda_iK=K
\)
inside that fiber. In particular \(q_1,q_2,q_3\in D\), and these three points are noncollinear by the parabola calculation in b1466080cb203fb4.

Take the integral affine plane
\[
 A:=\mathbb R^2\times\{1\}\subsetneq\mathbb R^3.
\tag{10}
\]
By (9), its positive-fiber support has affine hull \(A\), so the intrinsic rank in accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c is exactly \(q=2\). There is no lattice-index issue: \(A\cap\mathbb Z^3=\mathbb Z^2\times\{1\}\), and the three noncollinear integral points \((q_1,1),(q_2,1),(q_3,1)\) belong to the support. Equation (9) also gives the exact slice mass
\[
 W_A=\sum_{u\in D}b_u
 =\frac13W(C^{(N)}).
\tag{11}
\]
Apply the proper-slice global augmentation bound (15) of accepted fact 8aac56c49321466c with \(n=3\), \(q=2\), \(\alpha_2=1/64\), and (11). It gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G(C^{(N)})
 &\ge
 \min\left\{
 \frac{W_A}{64}+\frac{W(C^{(N)})-W_A}{2},
 2^{3-2}\frac{W_A}{64}
 \right\}\\
 &=\min\left\{\frac{65}{192}W(C^{(N)}),
 \frac1{96}W(C^{(N)})\right\}
 =\frac1{96}W(C^{(N)}).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Combining (5) and (12), and again using \(\mathcal A\ge G\), proves (2).

If \(Q(C)>0\), equations (1) and (2) respectively give
\(
 \mathcal A(C)\ge Q(C)/8
\)
and
\(
 \mathcal A(C)\ge Q(C)/12
\),
both strictly larger than \(Q(C)/100\). If \(Q(C)=0\), (3) would require the nonnegative quantity \(\mathcal A(C)\) to be negative. Thus (3) is impossible in every case. The arbitrary counterexample matching and its common weight do not enter \(W\), the canonical reservoir \(Q\), or the complete-core lower bounds, so rescaling that weight does not alter this conclusion.
