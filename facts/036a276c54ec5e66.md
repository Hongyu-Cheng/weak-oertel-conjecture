## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,\qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\tag{1}
\]
For a Borel set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), let
\[
 \mu_C(B):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d
 \{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\},
\tag{1a}
\]
and put
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\ {\rm closed\ halfspace}\\
 p\in H}}
 \mu_C(H).
\tag{1b}
\]
For a positive-volume bounded measurable set \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<m\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), put
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
  0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_Kf=m
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\ {\rm closed\ halfspace}\\x\in J}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J).
\tag{2a}
\]
For \(z\in Z\), define
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,\qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),\qquad
 D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\tag{3}
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}a_z.
\tag{4}
\]
Call \(\xi\) good when \(A_\xi>0\) and
\[
 \max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\leq\frac{A_\xi}{4}.
\tag{5}
\]
Define the half-strength canonical product reservoir in the normalization of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` by
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}:=
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi},
 &u\ne v,\quad u\equiv v\equiv\xi,\quad \xi\text{ good},\\[2mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
and put
\[
 Q:=|r|=\sum_er_e.
\tag{7}
\]
Assume \(Q>0\). For a reservoir edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},\qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2},\qquad
 D_e:=\frac12(D_u+D_v),\qquad
 L_e:=\bar c_e+D_e.
\tag{8}
\]
Let
\[
 R:=R_C(r)
\tag{9}
\]
be the canonical maximum affinely half-metronoid-realizable submatching mass. Choose the load-minimal primal-dual optimizer of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` for \(r\), and retain its dual vectors \(v_e\), loads, support points, and endpoint contacts:
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),\qquad
 \Omega:=\sum_e\omega_e,
\tag{10}
\]
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq r_e,\qquad
 Q-R\leq\Omega\leq Q,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 \ell_e=\frac{x_{e,u}+x_{e,v}}2\in L_e,
 \qquad
 x_{e,u}\in\mathcal K_u,\quad x_{e,v}\in\mathcal K_v.
\tag{12}
\]
Put \(F_+:=\{e:\omega_e>0\}\). For two distinct edges \(e=\{z,a\}\) and \(f=\{z,b\}\) in \(F_+\) sharing their unique common endpoint \(z\), define
\[
 h_{e,z}:=\frac{a-z}{2},\qquad
 h_{f,z}:=\frac{b-z}{2},
\tag{13}
\]
and call \(\{e,f\}\) transverse when these two vectors are linearly independent. Let \(\mathcal T\) be the set of all such transverse unordered pairs, and let \(\vartheta\) maximize
\[
 P:=\sum_{\{e,f\}\in\mathcal T}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
\tag{14}
\]
subject to
\[
 \vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal T}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
 \leq\omega_e
 \quad(e\in F_+).
\tag{15}
\]
For this optimizer define
\[
 \kappa_e:=\sum_{f:\{e,f\}\in\mathcal T}
 \vartheta_{\{e,f\}},\qquad
 \lambda_e^\perp:=\omega_e-\kappa_e,\qquad
 |\lambda^\perp|:=\sum_e\lambda_e^\perp.
\tag{16}
\]
The notation \(\lambda^\perp\) denotes only the residual of the transverse pairing; in particular, it is not the canonical capacity \(R\) and is not the centroid matching denoted by \(\lambda\) elsewhere. Then every connected component of the graph with edge set
\[
 \{e:\lambda_e^\perp>0\}
\tag{17}
\]
is contained in an affine lattice line, and
\[
 \boxed{|\lambda^\perp|\leq G.}
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{\Omega=2P+|\lambda^\perp|.}
\tag{19}
\]

Now impose the canonical hard inequality from accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`. Namely, put
\[
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
\tag{20}
\]
and assume
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then necessarily \(n\geq2\), and
\[
 \boxed{P>\frac{99}{200}Q.}
\tag{22}
\]

For every positive pairing contribution, retain its full label
\[
 \sigma=(z;e,f),\qquad
 \theta_\sigma:=\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}>0,
\tag{23}
\]
where \(e=\{z,a\}\), \(f=\{z,b\}\), and define
\[
 m_\sigma:=\frac{a+b}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 X_\sigma:=\frac{x_{e,a}+x_{f,b}}2.
\tag{24}
\]
The resulting actual labelled transverse atlas is
\[
 \mathscr A_\perp:=
 \{(\sigma,m_\sigma,X_\sigma,\theta_\sigma):
 \theta_\sigma>0\}.
\tag{25}
\]
Every atlas label has the exact full-mass realization and fiber depth
\[
 \boxed{
 X_\sigma\in\mathsf M_{\theta_\sigma}(C_{m_\sigma}),
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)
 \geq\gamma_d\theta_\sigma.}
\tag{26}
\]
Here \(\delta_C\) is the absolute fiber depth used in accepted facts `6769574fc1bd9f8d` and `1c54f5dc74c15059`.

For each loaded incidence \((e,y)\), choose once and for all a half-mass witness \(f_{e,y}\) of its contact \(x_{e,y}\):
\[
 0\leq f_{e,y}\leq1,\qquad
 \int_{C_y}f_{e,y}=\frac{a_y}{2},\qquad
 \frac2{a_y}\int_{C_y}x f_{e,y}(x)\,dx=x_{e,y}.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(\sigma=(z;e,f)\), \(e=\{z,a\}\), \(f=\{z,b\}\), define the two opposite-source densities
\[
 g_{\sigma,a}:=\frac{2\theta_\sigma}{a_a}f_{e,a},
 \qquad
 g_{\sigma,b}:=\frac{2\theta_\sigma}{a_b}f_{f,b}.
\tag{28}
\]
They have mass \(\theta_\sigma\) and barycenters \(x_{e,a}\), respectively \(x_{f,b}\). For every subfamily \(\mathscr Q\subseteq\mathscr A_\perp\) and every physical source fiber \(C_y\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr Q\\
 y\text{ is an opposite source of }\sigma}}
 g_{\sigma,y}\leq\frac12
 \quad\text{pointwise on }C_y.}
\tag{29}
\]
Thus the atlas has the exact interpolation and simultaneous source-feasibility interface of accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059`, with the sharper source bound \(1/2\).

For the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\) in (1a), the single positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_\perp:=
 2\gamma_d\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr A_\perp}
 \theta_\sigma\delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)}}
\tag{30}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), has mass
\[
 |\nu_\perp|=2\gamma_dP,
\tag{31}
\]
and is genuinely halfspace-dominated:
\[
 \boxed{\nu_\perp(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{32}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Let
\[
 A_\perp:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_\sigma:\theta_\sigma>0\},
 \qquad
 \rho_\perp:=\dim A_\perp,
\tag{33}
\]
and define the intrinsic continuous residual rank
\[
 r_\perp:=
 \min_{a:A_\perp\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{X_\sigma-a(m_\sigma):\theta_\sigma>0\}.
\tag{34}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_\perp}
 \frac{P}{r_\perp+1},}
\tag{35}
\]
and the hard inequality gives the exact rank screen
\[
 \boxed{
 r_\perp+1>
 99\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_\perp}.}
\tag{36}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\nu_\perp)
 =\rho_\perp+r_\perp\geq n+6,}
\tag{37}
\]
so in particular \(d\geq6\), and the support contains at least \(n+7\) distinct actual atoms.

Finally, let
\[
 N_\perp:=|\{\sigma:\theta_\sigma>0\}|
\tag{38}
\]
be the number of positive atlas labels, counted before aggregating coincident atoms. Then
\[
 \boxed{N_\perp>99\gamma_d\,2^n.}
\tag{39}
\]
The conclusions (22), (26), (29), and (35)--(39) do not assert that the labels contain a complete compatible full-rank Cartesian grid, that colliding labels may be pooled into an original label, or that the resulting weight array has a macroscopic rank-one minorant. Hence this theorem does not by itself produce the conditional full-rank packet of accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059` and is not a final theorem.

## Proof

For a base \(z\) in a good class \(\xi\), direct summation of (6) gives
\[
 d_z(r):=\sum_{e\ni z}r_e
 =\frac{a_z}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_z}{A_\xi}\right)
 \leq\frac{a_z}{4};
\tag{40a}
\]
on every other class the degree is zero. Thus \(r\) is an off-diagonal same-parity matching satisfying the endpoint hypothesis \(d_z(r)\leq a_z/2\) of accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d`, which validates (8)--(12).

Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes \(Z\), the reservoir support, and the feasible-dimensional linear program (14)--(15) finite. Its feasible region is a nonempty compact polytope, so an optimizer exists. Each variable \(\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\) is charged once to each of its two source edges. Therefore
\[
 \sum_e\kappa_e=2P,
 \qquad
 \Omega=\sum_e\omega_e
 =2P+\sum_e(\omega_e-\kappa_e)
 =2P+|\lambda^\perp|,
\tag{40}
\]
which proves (19).

Suppose two positive-residual edges \(e,f\) share a vertex \(z\) and have linearly independent half-directions there. Increasing \(\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\) by a positive number no larger than
\(
\min\{\lambda_e^\perp,\lambda_f^\perp\}
\)
preserves all constraints and strictly increases the objective. This contradicts optimality. Hence all positive-residual edge directions incident at any vertex are pairwise linearly dependent. Along an edge path, dependence at consecutive common vertices and reversal of an edge direction at its other endpoint show inductively that all edge differences lie in one fixed one-dimensional linear subspace. Every positive-residual component is therefore contained in the affine lattice line through either edge in that component. This proves the component assertion.

We prove (18) at the exact half-strength scale. First suppose
\[
 G<\frac W4.
\tag{41}
\]
If \(n=1\), a line containing a residual edge is all of \(\mathbb R\) and contains physical fiber mass \(W\). Equation (11) of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` would give \(G\geq W/4\), contrary to (41). Thus there is no residual edge in this case and (18) is immediate.

Suppose \(n>1\). If \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) is a line carrying a residual component, put
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in Z\cap L}a_z.
\tag{42}
\]
The line contains two distinct positive-fiber bases, so its intrinsic affine rank is one. Equation (15) of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c`, with \(q=1\) and \(\alpha_1=1/4\), gives
\[
 G\geq
 \min\left\{
 \frac{W_L}{4}+\frac{W-W_L}{2},
 \ 2^{n-3}W_L
 \right\}.
\tag{43}
\]
The first entry is
\(
W/2-W_L/4\geq W/4
\).
Under (41), the second entry must therefore be at most \(G\), and
\[
 W_L\leq2^{3-n}G.
\tag{44}
\]

Fix a good parity class \(\xi\), enumerate its positive-residual components by \(j\), let \(V_{\xi,j}\) be their pairwise disjoint vertex sets, and put
\[
 B_{\xi,j}:=\sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z,
 \qquad
 \Lambda_\xi:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi}}
 \lambda_e^\perp.
\tag{45}
\]
Each \(V_{\xi,j}\) lies on one of the lines in (42), so (44) gives
\[
 B_{\xi,j}\leq2^{3-n}G.
\tag{46}
\]
Using \(\lambda_e^\perp\leq\omega_e\leq r_e\), the exact reservoir formula (6), and \(\sum_jB_{\xi,j}\leq A_\xi\), we obtain
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Lambda_\xi
 &\leq
 \sum_j\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V_{\xi,j}}
 \frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}\\
 &=\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \sum_j\left(
 B_{\xi,j}^2-
 \sum_{z\in V_{\xi,j}}a_z^2
 \right)\\
 &\leq\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \left(\max_jB_{\xi,j}\right)
 \sum_jB_{\xi,j}\\
 &\leq\frac18\max_jB_{\xi,j}
 \leq2^{-n}G.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
The inequality is vacuous for a class with no residual edge. There are at most \(2^n\) parity classes, so summing (47) gives
\[
 |\lambda^\perp|=\sum_\xi\Lambda_\xi\leq G.
\tag{48}
\]

It remains to cover the regime \(G\geq W/4\), independently of the later hard hypothesis. For a good class, direct summation of (6) gives
\[
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq\xi}r_{\{u,v\}}
 =\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \left(A_\xi^2-
 \sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z^2\right)
 \leq\frac{A_\xi}{8}.
\tag{49}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 |\lambda^\perp|\leq\Omega\leq Q
 \leq\frac W8\leq\frac G2\leq G.
\tag{50}
\]
Equations (48) and (50) prove (18) in all cases.

Assume now the hard inequality (21). Its summands are nonnegative, so
\[
 R+G<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{51}
\]
Equations (11), (18), and (19) give
\[
 P=\frac{\Omega-|\lambda^\perp|}{2}
 \geq\frac{Q-R-G}{2}
 >\frac{99}{200}Q.
\tag{52}
\]
Thus \(P>0\), so a transverse pair exists. Such a pair cannot exist in one integer dimension, proving \(n\geq2\) and (22).

We construct the actual atlas using the exact interpolation convention of accepted fact `1c54f5dc74c15059`. All reservoir edges are same-parity, so if \(\sigma=(z;e,f)\), \(e=\{z,a\}\), \(f=\{z,b\}\), then \(a\equiv z\equiv b\pmod2\). Hence \(m_\sigma=(a+b)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\). Feasibility and the reservoir degree bound give
\[
 \theta_\sigma\leq\omega_e\leq r_e
 \leq d_a(r)
 =\frac{a_a}{4}
 \left(1-\frac{a_a}{A_\xi}\right)
 \leq\frac{a_a}{4},
\tag{53}
\]
and symmetrically \(\theta_\sigma\leq a_b/4\).

Scale the fixed half-mass witnesses \(f_{e,a}\), \(f_{f,b}\) by \(2\theta_\sigma/a_a\), respectively \(2\theta_\sigma/a_b\). Equation (53) makes them admissible densities of mass \(\theta_\sigma\), with barycenters \(x_{e,a}\), respectively \(x_{f,b}\). Therefore
\[
 (a,x_{e,a},\theta_\sigma^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (b,x_{f,b},\theta_\sigma^{1/d})
\tag{54}
\]
belong to the positive-root fiber-metronoid generating set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. That accepted fact proves this generating set convex before closure. Taking the midpoint in (54), with the common positive root unchanged, gives
\[
 X_\sigma=\frac{x_{e,a}+x_{f,b}}2
 \in\mathsf M_{\theta_\sigma}
 \left(C_{(a+b)/2}\right).
\tag{55}
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` turns (55) into
\[
 \delta_C(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)
 \geq\gamma_d\theta_\sigma.
\tag{56}
\]
This proves (26).

We next prove the simultaneous source bound. Fix a subfamily
\(\mathscr Q\subseteq\mathscr A_\perp\) and a physical source base \(y\). For a loaded edge \(e=\{z,y\}\), only pairings of \(e\) at its other endpoint \(z\) use \(x_{e,y}\) as an opposite source. Hence (15) gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma=(z;e,f)\in\mathscr Q\\e=\{z,y\}}}
 \theta_\sigma\leq\omega_e.
\tag{57}
\]
Since every witness in (27) is pointwise at most one, grouping the source occurrences first by their edge and applying (57) gives, pointwise on \(C_y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\mathscr Q\\
 y\text{ is an opposite source of }\sigma}}
 g_{\sigma,y}
 &\leq\frac2{a_y}\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_ef_{e,y}\\
 &\leq\frac2{a_y}\sum_{e\ni y}\omega_e
 \leq\frac{2d_y(r)}{a_y}
 \leq\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{58}
\]
This proves (29), including arbitrary subfamilies, repeated physical source fibers, and midpoint collisions.

We now prove domination of the one sum measure (30), rather than adding separate atomwise depth bounds. Fix a closed ambient halfspace \(H\). If
\((m_\sigma,X_\sigma)\in H\), then at least one of
\[
 (a,x_{e,a}),\qquad (b,x_{f,b})
\tag{59}
\]
belongs to \(H\), because their midpoint is \((m_\sigma,X_\sigma)\). Orient this atlas occurrence toward one such source point. For a physical source base \(y\), let \(\mathscr Q_y(H)\) be the occurrences oriented toward \(y\), and put
\[
 F_y:=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr Q_y(H)}g_{\sigma,y},
 \qquad
 T_y:=\int_{C_y}F_y
 =\sum_{\sigma\in\mathscr Q_y(H)}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{60}
\]
Equation (29) gives \(0\leq2F_y\leq1\). If \(T_y>0\), the barycenter of \(F_y\) is a convex combination of the oriented source points in the fiber section \(H_y\), and hence belongs to \(H_y\). The density \(2F_y\) has mass \(2T_y\), the same barycenter, and is admissible in \(C_y\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \geq2\gamma_dT_y
\tag{61}
\]
when \(H_y\) is a proper halfspace. If \(H_y=C_y\), the same inequality follows from \(2T_y\leq a_y\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\). Summing (61) over the pairwise distinct source fibers gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu_\perp(H)
 &=2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\sigma:(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)\in H}\theta_\sigma\\
 &=2\gamma_d\sum_yT_y
 \leq\sum_y\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{62}
\]
This proves (31)--(32). In particular, independently constructed target-fiber densities are never pooled.

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to \(\nu_\perp\). Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (33)--(34), even after coincident atoms are aggregated, and it gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{2\gamma_dP}
 {2^{\rho_\perp}(r_\perp+1)}.
\tag{63}
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) proves (35). Equations (22), (35), and (51) imply
\[
 \frac Q{100}
 >D
 \geq
 2\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_\perp}
 \frac{P}{r_\perp+1}
 >\frac{99\gamma_d}{100}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_\perp}Q}{r_\perp+1},
\tag{64}
\]
which rearranges to (36).

The same accepted fact gives the intrinsic rank identity
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\nu_\perp)
 =\rho_\perp+r_\perp.
\tag{65}
\]
For every integer \(r\geq0\), one has \(r+1\leq2^r\). Also
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 \leq\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}<3,
\tag{66}
\]
so \(\gamma_d>1/3\). From (36),
\[
 2^{\rho_\perp+r_\perp}
 \geq2^{\rho_\perp}(r_\perp+1)
 >99\gamma_d2^n
 >33\,2^n
 >2^{n+5}.
\tag{67}
\]
The exponent on the left is an integer, so (37) follows. Since the support lies in \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), equation (37) also gives \(d\geq6\). Any finite set of affine dimension at least \(n+6\) contains at least \(n+7\) distinct points, proving the stated support count.

It remains to prove the label-count screen. For each positive label \(\sigma\), the singleton measure
\[
 \nu_\sigma:=
 2\gamma_d\theta_\sigma
 \delta_{(m_\sigma,X_\sigma)}
\tag{68}
\]
is a positive submeasure of \(\nu_\perp\), hence is itself halfspace-dominated. Every closed ambient halfspace containing its atom therefore has mixed volume at least \(2\gamma_d\theta_\sigma\). Consequently,
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq2\gamma_d\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 D\geq2^{n+1}\gamma_d\theta_\sigma.
\tag{69}
\]
Using (51), every positive label satisfies
\[
 \theta_\sigma<
 \frac{Q}{100\cdot2^{n+1}\gamma_d}.
\tag{70}
\]
There are \(N_\perp\) such labels and their weights sum to \(P\). Equations (22) and (70) give
\[
 \frac{99}{200}Q<P
 <N_\perp
 \frac{Q}{100\cdot2^{n+1}\gamma_d},
\tag{71}
\]
which is exactly (39).

The atlas may still have arbitrarily many labels over one atom or base, and neither (36), (37), nor (39) supplies the missing Cartesian completion or a rank-one weight minorant. Thus no incompatible local packet energies are added, and the final non-closure qualification is essential.
