## Statement

Let \(L\ge2\) be an integer and \(0<r\le1\). Use the original geometric probabilities
\[
p_j=\frac{r^j}{\sum_{k=0}^{L}r^k},\qquad
b_j=\min\{j,L-j\},\qquad N_j=2b_j+1
\quad(0\le j\le L).
\]
Here \(N_j\) is the canonical oriented reflected count \(k_j=\#(\{0,\ldots,L\}\cap(2j-\{0,\ldots,L\}))\), including zero displacement, and \(\mathsf H_j=N_jp_j\) is the original reflected Hellinger mass. Define
\[
F_L(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{L}p_j^4N_j^3,\qquad
Q_L(j)=\frac{p_j^4N_j^3}{F_L(r)},\qquad s=-\log r.
\]
Then the absolute constants \(\alpha=(8/27)^{3/2}>1/8\) and \(C=92/9\) satisfy
\[
F_L(r)\ge\alpha,\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_L}\frac{s^2b_J(b_J+1)}3\le C.
\tag{1}
\]
At \(r=1\) the score in the second assertion is identically zero. The finite auxiliary law \(Q_L\) is distinct from the infinite law \(Q_s\) in accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`; their rate and generating-function argument agree through \(s=-\log r\) and \(z=r^4=e^{-4s}\).

For \(L=1\), with the same definitions,
\[
F_1(r)=\frac{1+r^4}{(1+r)^4}\ge\frac18,
\qquad
F_1(r)-\frac18
=\frac{(1-r)^2(7r^2+10r+7)}{8(1+r)^4}.
\tag{2}
\]
Equality holds exactly at \(r=1\), and \(b_0=b_1=0\), so the displacement and score-displacement variance are zero for every \(r\).

## Proof

For each admissible displacement \(-b_j\le v\le b_j\), geometricity gives \(\sqrt{p_{j-v}p_{j+v}}=p_j\). Thus the reflected Hellinger mass is exactly \(\mathsf H_j=N_jp_j\).

First prove a finite second reflection-moment estimate. Write
\(F_{2,L}(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{L}p_j^3N_j^2=\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_J^2\).
We will show \(F_{2,L}(r)\ge8/27\) for every \(L\ge2\). This estimate concerns the finite original law, rather than an infinite generating function.

For \(0<q<1\), expand \((2j+1)^2=4j^2+4j+1\) and apply \(q\,d/dq\) to \(1/(1-q)\) up to twice to obtain
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^2q^j
=\frac{1+6q+q^2}{(1-q)^3}.
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(\ell=L+1\) and
\(S_{2,L}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{L}(2\min\{j,L-j\}+1)^2q^j\).
The exact finite formulas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-q)^3S_{2,L}(q)
&=(1-q^{\ell})(1+6q+q^2)
-8\ell q^{(\ell+1)/2}(1-q),&&\ell\text{ odd},\\
(1-q)^3S_{2,L}(q)
&=(1-q^{\ell})(1+6q+q^2)
-4\ell q^{\ell/2}(1-q^2),&&\ell\text{ even}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a derivation valid for every length. When \(L=2m\), extend the right-hand squared count beyond its support as \((4m+1-2j)^2\) for \(j\ge m+1\). Its difference from \((2j+1)^2\) is \(-8\ell(j-m)\), whose series is \(-8\ell q^{m+1}/(1-q)^2\). Beyond the original support, at \(j=L+1+k\), the extended square is \((2k+1)^2\). Subtracting that tail, namely \(q^{\ell}\) times (3), gives the first formula in (4). When \(L=2m+1\), the corresponding extension is \((4m+3-2j)^2\), and its difference at \(j=m+1+k\) is \(-4\ell(2k+1)\). Its series is \(-4\ell q^{m+1}(1+q)/(1-q)^2\). The same tail subtraction gives the second formula. All these series converge absolutely for \(0<q<1\).

Now suppose \(0<r<1\), so \(s>0\), and put \(\xi=s\ell/2\). The original normalizer is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{L}e^{-sj}
=e^{-s(\ell-1)/2}\frac{\sinh\xi}{\sinh(s/2)}.
\tag{5}
\]
For compactness in the following repeated expressions, define
\(\mathcal A_s=\cosh(3s)+3\) and \(\mathcal B_s=1+2\cosh s\).
Substitute \(q=e^{-3s}\) into (4) and divide by the cube of (5). Using
\(\sinh(3s/2)/\sinh(s/2)=\mathcal B_s\), one obtains
\[
F_{2,L}(r)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{4\mathcal A_s\sinh(3\xi)-16\ell\sinh(3s/2)}{8\mathcal B_s^3\sinh^3\xi},&\ell\text{ odd},\\[6pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4\mathcal A_s\sinh(3\xi)-8\ell\sinh(3s)}{8\mathcal B_s^3\sinh^3\xi},&\ell\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Every denominator in (3)--(6) is strictly positive in its stated domain.

The identity \(\sinh(3\xi)=3\sinh\xi+4\sinh^3\xi\) shows that the numerator of (6), minus \((8/27)\) times its denominator, is the sum of
\[
\frac{16}{27}(27\mathcal A_s-4\mathcal B_s^3)\sinh^3\xi
\tag{7}
\]
and a remainder. The first term is nonnegative, since the following exact polynomial identity has only nonnegative terms:
\[
27\mathcal A_s-4\mathcal B_s^3
=27(\cosh s-1)+180(\cosh s-1)^2+76(\cosh s-1)^3.
\tag{8}
\]

The function \(\sinh x/x\) increases for \(x>0\). Indeed, the numerator of its derivative is \(x\cosh x-\sinh x\), which vanishes at zero and has derivative \(x\sinh x>0\). If \(\ell\) is odd, then \(L\ge2\) implies \(\ell\ge3\) and \(\xi\ge3s/2\). Consequently \(\sinh\xi\ge(\ell/3)\sinh(3s/2)\). The remainder in this case satisfies
\[
12\mathcal A_s\sinh\xi-16\ell\sinh(3s/2)
\ge4\ell(\mathcal A_s-4)\sinh(3s/2)\ge0.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(\ell\) is even, then \(L\ge2\) implies \(\ell\ge4\) and \(\xi\ge2s\). Thus \(\sinh\xi\ge(\ell/4)\sinh(2s)\), and this remainder satisfies
\[
12\mathcal A_s\sinh\xi-8\ell\sinh(3s)
\ge\ell\bigl(3\mathcal A_s\sinh(2s)-8\sinh(3s)\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{10}
\]
The last inequality follows from another exact identity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
3\mathcal A_s\sinh(2s)-8\sinh(3s)
={}&2\sinh s\bigl(7(\cosh s-1)+47(\cosh s-1)^2\\
&\hspace{36mm}+48(\cosh s-1)^3+12(\cosh s-1)^4\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
For example, (8) follows from \(\cosh(3s)=4\cosh^3s-3\cosh s\). For (11), use that identity together with \(\sinh(2s)=2\cosh s\sinh s\) and \(\sinh(3s)=(4\cosh^2s-1)\sinh s\), then expand in \(\cosh s-1\). Thus these polynomial certificates require no numerical minimum or unproved sign determination. Equations (7)--(11) prove \(F_{2,L}(r)\ge8/27\) for \(0<r<1\). The original finite sums defining \(p_j\) and \(F_{2,L}\) are continuous at \(r=1\), with a positive normalizer, so the same inequality holds there.

Apply Hölder on the original probability space \(p\), with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\):
\[
F_{2,L}(r)=\mathbb E_p[(\mathsf H_J^3)^{2/3}\,1]
\le\bigl(\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_J^3\bigr)^{2/3}
=F_L(r)^{2/3}.
\tag{12}
\]
This proves the first assertion in (1). The comparison \(\alpha>1/8\) is exact: squaring and clearing positive denominators gives \(32768>19683\).

For the score bound assume first that \(s>0\), and let \(m=\lfloor L/2\rfloor\) and \(z=e^{-4s}=r^4\). Fold the unnormalized law \(Q_L\) onto the index \(b_J\). For \(0\le b\le m\), its folded weights are
\[
w_b=\begin{cases}
(2b+1)^3(z^b+z^{L-b}),&2b<L,\\
(2b+1)^3z^b,&2b=L.
\end{cases}
\]
The original normalizer cancels in \(Q_L\), so these are its exact folded weights. Since \(z\in(0,1)\) and \(L-2b\ge0\), they obey
\((2b+1)^3z^b\le w_b\le2(2b+1)^3z^b\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_L}b_J^2
\le2\frac{\sum_{b=0}^{m}b^2(2b+1)^3e^{-4sb}}
{\sum_{b=0}^{m}(2b+1)^3e^{-4sb}}
=2\mathbb E_{Q_s}[J^2\mid J\le m]
\le2\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(Q_s(j)=(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}/F_3(s)\) is exactly the infinite law of accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`. The last inequality holds because \(J^2\le m^2\) on the conditioning event and \(J^2>m^2\) on its complement; averaging the two conditional expectations can only increase the first one. The accepted predecessor supplies \(\mathbb E_{Q_s}J^2\le23/(3s^2)\). Since every nonnegative integer \(b\) obeys \(b(b+1)\le2b^2\), (13) yields
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_L}\frac{s^2b_J(b_J+1)}3
\le\frac{2s^2}{3}\mathbb E_{Q_L}b_J^2
\le\frac{4s^2}{3}\frac{23}{3s^2}=\frac{92}{9}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(r=1\), \(s=0\) and the finite score is identically zero, as stated.

Finally, when \(L=1\), both counts are one and both \(b_j\) are zero. Direct substitution gives (2), and expansion verifies its factorization. Its denominator and quadratic factor are positive for \(0<r\le1\), so the equality and zero-displacement assertions follow. This also shows exactly why binary intervals cannot have a uniform surplus over 1/8.
