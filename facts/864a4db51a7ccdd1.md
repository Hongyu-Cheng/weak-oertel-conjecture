## Statement

For each integer \(A\ge2\), set \(B=A^2+A-1\), and let
\[
D_{A,B}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(A,0),(0,B)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
\mathbf j_A=(1,A^2-2).
\]
Here \(A,B\) are the side lengths in the assignment. The support called \(A\) in the depth conventions accompanying accepted fact 558e5cac7133593e is \(D_{A,B}\), not the side length \(A\). This family has \(A\to\infty\) and \(B/A=A+1-1/A\to\infty\). Every \(\mathbf j_A\) is a supported integer point strictly inside the triangle.

Let \(\nu_{\mathbf j_A}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(D_{A,B}\) with full vector mean \(\mathbf j_A\), and write its original random vector as \(\mathbf X=(X,Y)\). It is positive at every point of \(D_{A,B}\), and has the form
\[
\nu_{\mathbf j_A}(x,y)
=\frac{\exp(\boldsymbol\theta_A\cdot(x,y))}
 {\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in D_{A,B}}
                 \exp(\boldsymbol\theta_A\cdot(u,v))}
\]
for a unique finite \(\boldsymbol\theta_A\in\mathbb R^2\).

Suppose its original centered law admits an exact finite mixture
\[
\operatorname{Law}_{\nu_{\mathbf j_A}}
       (\mathbf X-\mathbf j_A)
=\sum_{a\in\mathcal E}\lambda_a\gamma_a,
\qquad
\lambda_a>0,\quad \sum_{a\in\mathcal E}\lambda_a=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{\gamma_a}\mathbf V=\mathbf0.
\]
The component law \(\gamma_a\), its centered variable \(\mathbf V\), and the weights \(\lambda_a\) have exactly the meanings of the corresponding objects in the component lemma of 558e5cac7133593e. Every component has the full original vector mean after adding \(\mathbf j_A\); component labels do not merely preserve one projected mean.

If a finite constant \(\kappa\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{\gamma_a}(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf V)^4
\le\kappa\left[\mathbb E_{\gamma_a}
                     (\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf V)^2\right]^2
\quad
(a\in\mathcal E,\ \mathbf u\in\mathbb R^2),
\]
including zero-variance directions, then \(\kappa\ge A/2\). Consequently there is no absolute finite \(\kappa\) with this mixture property for all entropy rows on arbitrary complete right lattice triangles, even when restricted to the displayed family and roots.

## Proof

First compute the complete support, including the floors. Put \(h=A+1\), so \(B=Ah-1\). For each integer \(0\le x\le A\), the highest allowed ordinate is
\[
\left\lfloor B\left(1-\frac{x}{A}\right)\right\rfloor
=\left\lfloor B-hx+\frac{x}{A}\right\rfloor
=\begin{cases}
B-hx,&0\le x\le A-1,\\
0,&x=A.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the original support is exactly
\[
D_{A,B}
=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:
          0\le x\le A-1,\ 0\le y\le B-hx\}
  \cup\{(A,0)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
No change of lattice or omission of intermediate integer points has been made. All three vertices belong to this support, so its convex hull is precisely the original triangle. The root ordinate is \(A^2-2=B-h>0\). The triangle's noncoordinate inequality at the root satisfies \(AB-B-A(B-h)=Ah-B=1>0\). Together with its positive coordinates, this proves that the root is strictly interior. Equation (1) also places it in the original support.

We verify existence and the exact meaning of the entropy row without importing any undeclared row-identification result. Define
\[
F(\boldsymbol\theta)
=\log\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{A,B}}
                 e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j_A)}.
\]
Because \(\mathbf j_A\) is interior to \(\operatorname{conv}(D_{A,B})\), that convex hull contains a Euclidean ball of some radius \(\varepsilon>0\) about the root. Maximizing a linear function over the convex hull is the same as maximizing it over \(D_{A,B}\), so

\(F(\boldsymbol\theta)\ge\max_{\mathbf x\in D_{A,B}}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j_A)\ge\varepsilon\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert\).

Hence \(F\) attains a finite minimum. Its gradient is the centered mean under its positive exponential law, so a minimizing parameter has full mean \(\mathbf j_A\). Its Hessian is that law's covariance matrix. For every nonzero direction the variance is positive: all support masses are positive, and the three vertices are not collinear. Thus \(F\) is strictly convex, and its minimizer \(\boldsymbol\theta_A\) is unique.

For any probability law \(\eta\) on \(D_{A,B}\) with mean \(\mathbf j_A\), direct substitution of the exponential masses gives
\[
D(\eta\Vert\nu_{\mathbf j_A})
=H(\nu_{\mathbf j_A})-H(\eta)\ge0,
\qquad
H(\eta)=-\sum_{\mathbf x}\eta_{\mathbf x}\log\eta_{\mathbf x}.
\]
Zero summands are omitted. The inequality and its equality condition follow from \(-\log t\ge1-t\) applied to \(t=\nu_{\mathbf j_A}(\mathbf x)/\eta(\mathbf x)\) at positive \(\eta\)-masses, and then summed. Equality requires full support and equality at each summand, hence \(\eta=\nu_{\mathbf j_A}\). This proves the asserted unique maximum-entropy interpretation, with the original support and full mean.

The original row can also be specified by exact geometric column balances. Define the affine integer coordinate \(z(x,y)=B-hx-y\). Write \(\boldsymbol\theta_A=(\lambda_x,\beta_{\mathrm v})\), set \(q=e^{-\beta_{\mathrm v}}>0\) and \(t=e^{\lambda_x-h\beta_{\mathrm v}}>0\), and let \(Z\) be the following normalizer. The exact normalization map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{\lambda_x x+\beta_{\mathrm v}y}
  &=q^{-B}t^xq^{z(x,y)},\\
Z
  &=\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^x\sum_{r=0}^{B-hx}q^r+t^Aq^{-1},\\
\nu_{\mathbf j_A}(x,y)
  &=\frac{t^xq^{z(x,y)}}{Z}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
In particular the original exponential normalizer is \(q^{-B}Z\). The variable \(z\) is an affine coordinate, not the locked problem's lattice index or a latent center; \(Z\) is a partition sum, not a random variable. Given \(X=x<A\), the original vertical coordinate \(Y\) has masses proportional to \(q^{-y}=e^{\beta_{\mathrm v}y}\) on the exact interval \(\{0,\ldots,B-hx\}\). At \(x=A\) it is zero.

Since \(z(\mathbf j_A)=0\), the two full mean equations for (2) are exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}(x-1)t^x\sum_{r=0}^{B-hx}q^r
       +(A-1)t^Aq^{-1}&=0,\\
\sum_{x=0}^{A-1}t^x\sum_{r=0}^{B-hx}r q^r
       -t^Aq^{-1}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The preceding existence and uniqueness proof ensures exactly one positive pair \((t,q)\) solving these balances, because the parameter transformation is bijective. No approximate parameters, noninteger conditional means, or projected log-concavity assumptions are used. In particular the exceptional original atom \((A,0)\) has strictly positive mass \(t^Aq^{-1}/Z\).

Now consider any mixture in the statement. Positivity of its weights and exact equality with the original law force every component to be supported on \(D_{A,B}-\mathbf j_A\): mass outside that finite set could not be canceled by other nonnegative components. After translating one component by \(\mathbf j_A\), let \(\rho\) denote its law on \(D_{A,B}\). Its full mean is exactly \(\mathbf j_A\). At least one component has \(\pi:=\rho\{(A,0)\}>0\), because that atom has positive original mass. Fix such a component. Nonnegativity of \(X\) and the horizontal mean equation give \(A\pi\le\mathbb E_\rho X=1\), so \(\pi\le1/A\).

For this component put \(T=z(X,Y)\). Equation (1) shows that \(T=-1\) at the exceptional atom, while \(T\) is a nonnegative integer everywhere else. Also \(\mathbb E_\rho T=0\), since the component has the full root mean. With \(T_+=\max\{T,0\}\), this gives
\[
\mathbb E_\rho T_+=\pi,
\qquad
\Pr_\rho(T>0)\le\pi,
\qquad
\Pr_\rho(T\ne0)\le2\pi\le\frac2A.
\tag{4}
\]
The middle inequality uses the exact integer spacing: \(T_+\ge1\) on \(\{T>0\}\). The chosen direction is precisely a centered original linear projection, since

\(T=-h(X-1)-(Y-(B-h))=\mathbf u\cdot((X,Y)-\mathbf j_A)\), with \(\mathbf u=(-h,-1)\).

Its second moment is positive because \(T=-1\) with probability \(\pi>0\). Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to \(T^2\mathbf1_{\{T\ne0\}}\), and then the proposed component fourth-moment inequality, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\mathbb E_\rho T^2)^2
&\le\Pr_\rho(T\ne0)\,\mathbb E_\rho T^4\\
&\le2\pi\,\kappa(\mathbb E_\rho T^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Canceling the positive squared second moment proves \(\kappa\ge1/(2\pi)\ge A/2\). This calculation applies to every component charging the exceptional atom, not just a specially chosen decomposition. Any proposed absolute \(\kappa\) is therefore contradicted by an integer \(A>2\kappa\) in this one family.

For an exact calibration of the geometric obstruction, the law supported on \((A,0),(0,B),(0,B-1)\) with respective masses \(1/A,(A-2)/A,1/A\) has full mean \(\mathbf j_A\). Its horizontal mean is one and its vertical mean is \(((A-1)B-1)/A=B-h\), because \(B+1=Ah\). All three points are in (1); when \(A=2\) the middle mass is zero. Its \(T\)-values are respectively \(-1,0,1\), so its second and fourth moments both equal \(2/A\), and its directional ratio is exactly \(A/2\). This auxiliary law only calibrates the bound. It is not substituted for the original entropy row, which remains (2) throughout the counterexample.

Finally, in the notation of the conditional-centered-component lemma of accepted fact 558e5cac7133593e, take \(m_i=2\), \(D_i=D_{A_i,B_i}\), and \(\nu_i=\nu_{\mathbf j_{A_i}}\). If the hypothesized absolute component constant existed, that lemma would give mass at least \(1/(4\kappa)\) for every closed halfspace containing the concatenated mean under the original product. The lower bound \(\kappa\ge A_i/2\) shows that this sufficient hypothesis cannot be uniform when the side lengths are independently unbounded. It gives no upper bound on actual product cap mass. The product mean-cap theorem itself remains unresolved by this packet.
