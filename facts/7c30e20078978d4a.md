## Statement

In the sampled-peak setting above, suppose $T>0$ and $N\ge2$. Equality $A=h(T-W)$ holds exactly when the real projection is $[u_0,u_{N-1}]$ and the fibers there are
$$
Q_u=K+\frac{u-u_0}{u_{N-1}-u_0}v
$$
for a fixed positive-volume $s$-body $K$ and vector $v$. For $T>0,N=1$, equality holds exactly when $Q$ consists of its unique sampled fiber. For $T=0$, equality holds exactly when $A=0$.

## Proof

Assume $T>0,N\ge2$ and equality. The positive variation coefficient of the sampled-peak refinement forces all adjacent weights to coincide. Thus all sampled weights are $W>0$. Between the extreme sampled sites Brunn–Minkowski gives actual volume at least $W$. The volume root is concave and continuous in the open interval, so the equality of integrals forces volume $W$ everywhere there. There is no point of $Q$ at a base outside this interval, since its convex hull with the positive-volume fiber at the nearest extreme sampled base would give positive volume outside the interval. Thus the extreme real fibers have exactly the sampled weights.

The Minkowski midpoint of the two extreme compact convex fibers of volume $W$ is contained in the actual midpoint fiber of volume $W$. Equality in Brunn–Minkowski for positive-volume convex bodies makes the two extreme fibers homothetic, hence translates because their volumes agree. At each intermediate base their Minkowski interpolant is therefore a translate of the first fiber. It is contained in the actual fiber with equal volume, which gives equality of the compact convex bodies: a strict additional point would create positive excess volume by convexity and the existence of an interior ball. This proves the asserted shear form. Conversely that form has volume $h(N-1)W$ and sampled weights all $W$, so it gives equality.

When $N=1,T>0$, any point over a different base would make a positive-volume cone with the single positive-volume sampled fiber. Thus $A=0$ is equivalent to the asserted form. When $T=0$, one has $W=0$, and the equality condition is precisely $A=0$.
