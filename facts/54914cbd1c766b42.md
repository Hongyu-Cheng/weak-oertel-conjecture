## Statement

For $1\le w<1+1/\sqrt3$ let $P_w=\{(X,Y):0\le X\le w,\ |Y|\le\min(X,1)\}$. Its area is $T=2w-1$, its centroid is $(g,0)$ with $g=(w^2-1/3)/T$, and its ordinary raw depth at the centroid is
$$\min\{g^2,\ T/2-(2g-w)^2/2\}.$$
For $1\le w\le1+1/\sqrt6$ the first term is a minimum and equals $(4/9)T+(w-1)^3(9w-5)/[9T^2]$. In particular, $K=\operatorname{conv}(([-3/4,3/4]\times[-3/8,3/8])\cup\{(-17/3,0)\})$ has area $k=95/32$ and centroid $g_K=(-2537/1710,0)$, where its ordinary raw depth is $51165409/38338200$. The excesses over $(4/9)k$ and $(k+1)/3$ are $16119/1064950$ and $1788661/153352800$, respectively. When $K$ is an actual middle fiber at zero of a mixed set, the mixed raw depth at $(0,g_K)$ is at least this ordinary depth.

## Proof

Integrating the vertical width $2\min(X,1)$ and its first moment in $X$ gives $T$ and $g$. The range gives $w/2<g<1$ and $0<2g-w=(w-2/3)/T<1$. Translate any containing halfplane to pass through the centroid. Reflection in $Y=0$ and choice of either side reduce the nonhorizontal lines to $X+tY=g$, $t\ge0$. The horizontal line gives $T/2$ on each side. Put $E(t)=|P_w\cap\{X+tY\le g\}|$.

The upper intersection is $(g/(1+t),g/(1+t))$. The other lies on the lower slope for $0\le t\le1-g$, the horizontal lower edge for $1-g\le t\le w-g$, and the right edge for $t\ge w-g$. In the first case the captured triangle has its other vertices at the origin and $(g/(1-t),-g/(1-t))$. In the second case use the origin and the two lower points $(1,-1)$ and $(g+t,-1)$. In the last case use the origin, both lower vertices $(1,-1),(w,-1)$, and the right-edge point $(w,(g-w)/t)$. The triangle and shoelace area formulas give
$$
E(t)=\begin{cases}
g^2/(1-t^2),&0\le t\le1-g,\\
g+(t-1)/2+g^2/[2(1+t)],&1-g\le t\le w-g,\\
T/2+[A t-B]/[2t(1+t)],&t\ge w-g,
\end{cases}
\qquad A=w(2g-w),\quad B=(w-g)^2.
$$
The first two expressions are nondecreasing, with the middle-piece derivative $E'(t)=1/2-g^2/[2(1+t)^2]>0$ on the middle piece. In the last piece the derivative has the sign of $-At^2+2Bt+B$. Since $A+B=g^2$, its unique positive zero is $t_*=(w-g)/(2g-w)>w-g$. The maximum there is $T/2+(2g-w)^2/2$ and the limit at infinity is $T/2$. Thus the infimum of $E$ is $\min(g^2,T/2)$, and the infimum of the complementary area is $T/2-(2g-w)^2/2$. Boundaries are area-null, so the displayed minimum is the closed-halfplane depth. The difference of the latter area and $g^2$ is $(w-1)^2[1-6(w-1)^2]/[6T^2]$. Expanding $g^2-(4/9)T$ gives the claimed surplus on the smaller range.

The map $(s,t)\mapsto((s+17/3)/(59/12),t/(3/8))$ takes the specified $K$ to $P_{77/59}$ with inverse area factor $59/32$. Here $6(18/59)^2<1$, and $g=14306/16815$. Applying the inverse map to $(g,0)$ and multiplying the depth by $59/32$ gives the displayed rational values. Any closed ambient cap containing $(0,g_K)$ restricts to an ordinary containing halfplane in $K$ if its continuous coefficient is nonzero, and otherwise it contains all of $K$.
