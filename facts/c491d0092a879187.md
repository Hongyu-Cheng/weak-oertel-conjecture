## Statement

For every integer $d\ge2$, every $s\ge r>0$, and every $0\le\varepsilon\le r/2$,
$$
(r-2\varepsilon)^d\ge\frac{r^d+s^d-(s+\varepsilon)^d}{2}.
$$
The uniform coefficient $1/2$ is optimal, with equality and a positive bracket at $d=2$, $s=r$, $\varepsilon=r/3$.

## Proof

For fixed $\varepsilon\ge0$, the increment $(s+\varepsilon)^d-s^d$ is nondecreasing in $s$, so the largest right-hand side occurs at $s=r$. After dividing by $r^d$ and setting $t=\varepsilon/r$, it suffices to show
$$
2(1-2t)^d+(1+t)^d\ge2\qquad(0\le t\le1/2).
$$
The left side is convex. With $k=4^{-1/(d-1)}$, its derivative vanishes at $t=(1-k)/(2+k)$, since $1-2t=k(1+t)$ there. This point lies in $(0,1/2)$, and the value there is $3^d/[2(2+k)^{d-1}]$, using $k^{d-1}=1/4$. Convexity of $x^{d-1}$ gives
$$
\left(\frac{2+k}{3}\right)^{d-1}\le\frac{2+k^{d-1}}3=\frac34.
$$
Thus the minimum is at least $2$. For $d=2$, the convexity inequality is an equality and the critical point is $t=1/3$. At $s=r$ the bracket equals $2r^2/9>0$, proving optimality of the coefficient.
