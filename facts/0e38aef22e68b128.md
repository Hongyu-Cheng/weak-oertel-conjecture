## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Retain the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), and
\[
h_S(\mathbf p)=\inf\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):H\text{ is a closed ambient halfspace containing }\mathbf p\}.
\]
For every \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\) and \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\). Use the complete original positive support \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\) and assume \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S)>0\). Thus no original positive-volume fiber is omitted; empty and lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. Suppose that some \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies \(D=\{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I:\mathbf I\in D\}\). Set \(w_{\max}=\max_D w_{\mathbf I}\), \(w_{\min}=\min_D w_{\mathbf I}\), and \(B=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\min})\), with natural logarithms.

Then \(\mathbf J\in D\), and the original depth satisfies
\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf J}}h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y))
\ge\frac{e^{-B}}{12(1+B)}W.
\]
In particular, for every fixed \(0\le\sigma<\log 2\), define \(\beta=\log 2-\sigma\) and
\[
c(\sigma)=\min\left\{\frac1{2e},\frac{e\beta}{12(1+\sigma)}\right\}\in(0,1/e).
\]
For all the bodies above satisfying \(B\le\sigma n\), there exists one \(\mathbf y^\star\in C_{\mathbf J}\), selected before the halfspace, such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \((\mathbf J,\mathbf y^\star)\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c(\sigma)2^{-n}W\). In particular, \(h_{\max}(C):=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)\ge c(\sigma)2^{-n}W\). The coefficient depends only on the fixed \(\sigma\), not on \(n,d,C\), or the fiber shapes.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes \(D\) finite, and \(W>0\) makes it nonempty. Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` applies to this actual complete fiber table, with exactly the volumes and normalization in the statement. In particular, the table is globally extendable, its positive support is lattice-convex, and \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) for \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). Choose any \(\mathbf I\in D\). Its reflection \(2\mathbf J-\mathbf I\) belongs to \(D\), so their midpoint \(\mathbf J\) lies in \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). This also covers the case \(\mathbf I=\mathbf J\).

Use the canonical potential \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\) of `488f0a583886b5fc`. It takes values in \([0,B]\), and its convex-admissibility inequality holds at every integer barycenter of positive sources. Retain that fact's family \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) of nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), its unique positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\), and its logarithmic gap \(G_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). The packing \(P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) maximizes \(\sum_E x_E\) over these supports with \(G_E\le L\), subject to \(x_E\ge0\) and the full-occurrence constraints \(\sum_{E\ni\mathbf I}x_E\le w_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D\). Thus each endpoint of a pair is charged the full pair mass, not half of it.

The reflection \(\mathbf I\mapsto2\mathbf J-\mathbf I\) partitions \(D\) into the singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\) and a family \(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf J}\) of disjoint two-element orbits. For each \(E=\{\mathbf I,2\mathbf J-\mathbf I\}\in\mathcal Q_{\mathbf J}\), the endpoints are distinct, hence affinely independent, and \(\mathbf J\) is their relative-interior midpoint with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\). Therefore \(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\), and
\[
0\le G_E
=\frac{\phi_{\mathbf I}+\phi_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}2-\phi_{\mathbf J}
\le B.
\]
The first inequality is convex-admissibility, and the last follows from \(0\le\phi_{\mathbf J}\) and both endpoint potentials being at most \(B\). The central singleton also belongs to \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) and has gap zero. All these supports are therefore legal at the weak cutoff \(L=B\), including when \(B=0\).

At this cutoff assign \(x_{\{\mathbf J\}}=w_{\mathbf J}\), assign \(x_E=\min_{\mathbf I\in E}w_{\mathbf I}\) to each \(E\in\mathcal Q_{\mathbf J}\), and assign zero to every other support. Disjointness means that every source participates in exactly one selected support. Its charge is no greater than its original capacity, so this is a feasible packing. Each noncentral pair appears twice in the oriented sum over \(D\), while its central term appears once. Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,B;w)
&\ge w_{\mathbf J}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal Q_{\mathbf J}}\min_{\mathbf I\in E}w_{\mathbf I}\\
&=\frac12\left(w_{\mathbf J}+\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\min\{w_{\mathbf I},w_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}\}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{|D|w_{\min}}2
\ge\frac{e^{-B}W}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the last inequality, \(W\le|D|w_{\max}\) and \(w_{\min}=e^{-B}w_{\max}\). Thus the proposed oriented reflected stock estimate is valid without counting either endpoint's capacity more than once.

For clarity, the root and hitting scores consumed next are the same actual-table objects as in the two accepted inputs. The root cost is \(L_E=\log\bigl(w_{\mathbf J}/(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d})^d\bigr)\). The packing \(P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\) uses the preceding full-occurrence program with \(L_E\le L\) in place of \(G_E\le L\). The mass \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\) is the minimum original weight \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) of a subset \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every such root-cost support. The semicolon merely displays the table argument; these are \(P_d(\mathbf J,L)\) and \(m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) in `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, and \(\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=\max_{\mathbf K\in D,L\ge0}m_d(\mathbf K,L;w)/(1+L)\) has its unchanged meaning.

The actual-cost inclusion of `488f0a583886b5fc` gives \(P_d(\mathbf J,B;w)\ge P_{\log}(\mathbf J,B;w)\). The packing-hitting comparison of `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` gives \(m_d(\mathbf J,B;w)\ge P_d(\mathbf J,B;w)\). Its original-depth transfer then gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C)
&\ge\frac{\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)}6
\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,B;w)}{6(1+B)}\\
&\ge\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,B;w)}{6(1+B)}
\ge\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J,B;w)}{6(1+B)}
\ge\frac{e^{-B}W}{12(1+B)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, the pointwise version of that same accepted transfer states \(\max_{\mathbf y\in C_{\mathbf J}}h_S((\mathbf J,\mathbf y))\ge m_d(\mathbf J,B;w)/[6(1+B)]\), with its maximum attained. It proves the stronger fiberwise bound in the statement and selects a single witnessing point before any containing halfspace is chosen.

Suppose now that \(B\le\sigma n\), where \(0\le\sigma<\log 2\), and put \(\beta=\log 2-\sigma>0\). For every \(t>0\), the function \(e^t/t\) has its minimum \(e\) at \(t=1\): its derivative is \(e^t(t-1)/t^2\). Taking \(t=\beta n\) gives \(e^{\beta n}/n\ge e\beta\). Since \(n\ge1\), also \(1+\sigma n\le(1+\sigma)n\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^ne^{-B}}{12(1+B)}
&\ge\frac{e^{\beta n}}{12(1+\sigma n)}
\ge\frac{e^{\beta n}}{12(1+\sigma)n}\\
&\ge\frac{e\beta}{12(1+\sigma)}
\ge c(\sigma)>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
By its explicit definition, \(c(\sigma)\le1/(2e)<1/e\). Combining this scalar bound with the attained fiberwise depth bound proves the uniform class theorem, including \(\sigma=0\).

All sums and capacities above use the complete original table. In particular, every positive-volume boundary fiber is included. Nonempty zero-volume fibers remain in the locked set \(S\) and contribute zero to its mixed volume; they have not been removed from \(S\) or assigned positive capacity. The accepted depth transfer is for exactly this original \(S\), with closed ambient halfspaces and their boundary mass, including vertical halfspaces. There is no change of continuous dimension, volume normalization, or lattice coordinates. If \(D=\{\mathbf J\}\), then \(B=0\), \(\mathcal Q_{\mathbf J}\) is empty, and the central packing has mass \(W\), so every displayed bound still holds. More generally the argument uses only singleton and two-point affine independence, so it applies without alteration when \(\operatorname{conv}D\) has dimension less than \(n\). No symmetry of the actual fiber shapes has been assumed or deduced.
