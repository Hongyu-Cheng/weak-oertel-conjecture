## Statement

Use the attained actual-fiber hinge problem on fixed positive polytopal fibers with $N$ caps, Euclidean unit continuous normals, total volume $M$, and value $v(\tau)$. Let $C=\operatorname{conv}(S)$ be the canonical hull of the measured fibers and put
$$
D_{\rm all}=\{v:(0,v)\in C-C\},\quad R=\max_{v\in D_{\rm all}}\lVert v\rVert,\qquad
S_*=\sum_z\sup_{\lVert u\rVert=1,t}\mathcal H^{d-1}(K_z\cap\{u\cdot X=t\}).
$$
At a regular interior feasible budget write $\Psi_i,c_i,S_i,r_i$ for the actual hinge, cap volume, effective section mass and budget multiplier of a sensitivity-normalized stress. Set $W_i=c_i+r_iS_i$. Then $\Psi_i\le R W_i$, and $(\Psi_i/W_i)u_i\in D_{\rm all}$ if $W_i>0$. Hence, for $A=M/(RS_*)$ and $B=NM^2/S_*$,
$$
v(\tau)\le R[N\tau M-(S_*/M)v'(\tau)],\qquad v'(\tau)+A v(\tau)\le B\tau .
$$
For every compact interior feasible interval $[a,b]$ this implies
$$
e^{Ab}v(b)-e^{Aa}v(a)\le B\int_a^b t e^{At}\,dt .
$$

## Proof

The difference set is compact, symmetric and convex and contains the differences in each real fiber; conversely every one of its elements is a difference of points at the same real base. A positive sampled fiber gives $R>0$, and $0<S_*<\infty$ by comparison with enclosing balls, with section counting measure for $d=1$.

Use d83edebb7d5b8852 for the normalized stress. For cap $i$ its source-side measure is the actual cap volume plus $r_i$ times its consistent section measure. Its contact-side measure assigns $\eta_z\lambda_i$ times the actual saddle law at base $z$ and projects to the fiber point. Both nonnegative measures are supported in $C$. The mass and base Euler equations give common mass $W_i$ and common base first moment. If $W_i>0$, their probability barycenters are in the same real fiber, and the continuous Euler equation gives difference $(\Psi_i/W_i)u_i$. This proves the asserted inclusion and the radius bound, also allowing the radial length of $D_{\rm all}$ in the direction $u_i$ in place of $R$. If $W_i=0$, then $c_i=0$ and $\Psi_i=0$. Since $S_i\le S_*$ and $c_i\le\tau M$, sum the radius estimates and use $M\sum_i r_i=-v'(\tau)$. Rearranging gives the differential inequality with the stated constants.

On each regular interval the derivative of $e^{A\tau}v(\tau)$ is at most $B\tau e^{A\tau}$. Integrate on the regular pieces and take their one-sided limits as in the actual-hinge jump lemma. Every jump of $e^{A\tau}v(\tau)$ is downward, by right continuity and monotonicity of $v$. Summing yields the integrated inequality.
