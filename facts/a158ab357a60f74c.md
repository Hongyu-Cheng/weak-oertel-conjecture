## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and $N\ge0$ be integers. Put $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$ and $V=\operatorname{vol}_d(T)$. Let the complete integer fibers of $S\subset\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d$ be $r_kT$ at $k=-N,\ldots,N$, with $0\le r_{-k}=r_k\le r_0=R$ and $R>0$. Write $\mu$ for the sum of $d$-volume on these fibers, $M=V\sum_k r_k^d$ and $y=(0,Rt,\ldots,Rt)$, where $t=1/(d+1)$. Fix a nonnegative continuous normal $u$ with $\sum_i u_i=1$. After relabeling, put $a=u_1=\max_i u_i$, $b=\max_{j\ge2}u_j$ and $q=2^{-1/d}$. For $\beta\in\mathbb R$ write $H_\beta=\lbrace(z,x):\beta z+u\cdot x\ge Rt\rbrace$. Suppose $a>b$ and $(a-t)/(a-b)\le q$. Put $c=Rt/a$ and $C_u=a^{d-1}/\prod_{j=2}^d(a-u_j)\ge1$.

The infimum over $\beta\in\mathbb R$ is attained at $\beta=0$ and equals
$$
\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\mu(S\cap H_\beta)
=V C_u\sum_{k=-N}^N(r_k-c)_+^d.
$$

## Proof

Normalization gives $a\ge1/d>t$. Since $q<1$, the ratio condition gives $b<t$, and hence $0<c<R$.

For $X$ uniform on $T$ write $X=(W,(1-W)Y)$ with $Y$ uniform on the standard $(d-1)$-simplex and independent of $W$. The change of variables has Jacobian $(1-W)^{d-1}$, so the density of $W$ on $[0,1]$ is $d(1-W)^{d-1}$. With $Z=\sum_{j=2}^d u_jY_j\in[0,b]$, the conditional projection is $aW+(1-W)Z$. Thus at any real threshold $w$, the conditional cap fraction given $Z=z_0$ is
$$
G_{z_0}(w)=\min\left\lbrace 1,\left(\frac{(a-w)_+}{a-z_0}\right)^d\right\rbrace.
$$
In particular it is one at and below $z_0$, and zero at and above $a$. At $w=a$ only $W=1$ can lie in the closed cap, an event of zero probability.

For a reflected pair at $\pm k$ of common radius $r>0$, the two normalized thresholds for the original common tilt are $v-s$ and $v+s$, where $v=Rt/r\ge t$ and $s=|\beta|k/r$. If $v\ge a$, then $G_{z_0}(v)=0$ and the pair has nonnegative mass. Otherwise $z_0\le b<t\le v<a$, and
$$
G_{z_0}(v)\le\left(\frac{a-t}{a-b}\right)^d\le\frac12.
$$
If $v-s\ge z_0$, all three tails agree with the convex function $(a-w)_+^d/(a-z_0)^d$, so $G_{z_0}(v-s)+G_{z_0}(v+s)\ge2G_{z_0}(v)$. If $v-s<z_0$, the first term equals one and gives the same inequality by itself. At $v-s=z_0$ the power equals one. At $v+s=a$ it equals zero. In particular the full/empty plateau, when $s\ge\max\lbrace v-z_0,a-v\rbrace$, has pair fraction one and satisfies the bound, including its endpoints. Averaging over $Z$ and multiplying by $Vr^d$ proves the pair comparison. Every pair attains its zero-shift mass simultaneously at $\beta=0$, and the own fiber at zero is independent of $\beta$. For a positive-radius pair the original halfspace takes one full and one null cap whenever $|\beta|k\ge\max\lbrace Rt,ar-Rt\rbrace$. This pair then contributes exactly $Vr^d$, including the endpoint when a closed cap consists of a highest vertex. Zero-radius fibers are singletons of zero $d$-volume for all tilts. The own fiber and the $N$ reflected pairs exhaust the census.

At zero tilt, $r b\le Rb<Rt$ and $0<Rt$, so every vertex other than $r e_1$ is strictly below the cutting level when $r>0$. If $ar<Rt$ the cap is empty, and if $ar=Rt$ it is a single vertex of zero mass. If $ar>Rt$, the edge fractions from $r e_1$ toward $0$ and toward $r e_j$, respectively, are
$$
\frac{ar-Rt}{ar},\qquad \frac{ar-Rt}{r(a-u_j)}\quad (j\ge2).
$$
Multiplying these fractions by $Vr^d$ gives the volume by the one-vertex simplex determinant formula in accepted fact f75e1f526e17ab44. The resulting volume is $V(ar-Rt)_+^d/[a\prod_{j=2}^d(a-u_j)]=VC_u(r-c)_+^d$, also zero for $r=0$. Each factor $a-u_j$ belongs to $(0,a]$, so $C_u\ge1$, with equality for a coordinate normal. The boundary of a cap with $u\ne0$ has zero $d$-volume in each positive-radius fiber, so the two closed sides have masses adding to $M$. Summing the zero-tilt volumes proves the formula. When $N=0$ the same identity comes from the own fiber alone, and every tilt has the same mass.
