## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^N\) be finite and nonempty, and assume completeness in the precise sense
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
\]
Let \(n=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D)\), and let \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^N\) be arbitrary. The parameter \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is an alias of the log-affine parameter \(\boldsymbol\ell\) of accepted fact ebb695e93a721952. Retain its meanings of \(p_{\mathbf z},R_{\mathbf z},k_{\mathbf z}\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{\mathbf z}&=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\quad(\mathbf z\in D),
& W=Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}w_{\mathbf z},
& p_{\mathbf z}&=w_{\mathbf z}/W,\\
R_{\mathbf z}&=\{\mathbf x\in D:2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\in D\},
& k_{\mathbf z}&=|R_{\mathbf z}|,\\
b_{\mathbf z}&=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z/2}}{W}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x/2},
& F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then every root satisfies \(b_{\mathbf z}\ge k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\), and
\[
F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)
\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}(k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z})^3
\ge\left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2\right)^3
\ge8^{-n}.
\]
The exponent uses intrinsic affine rank \(n\), not ambient dimension \(N\).

More precisely, choose \(\mathbf z_0\in D\), let \(E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D-\mathbf z_0)\), and let the columns of \(U\in\mathbb Z^{N\times n}\) be a lattice basis of the saturated intersection lattice \(E\cap\mathbb Z^N\). The affine chart \(\phi(\mathbf u)=\mathbf z_0+U\mathbf u\) maps \(\mathbb Z^n\) bijectively onto \(\operatorname{aff}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N\). For
\(\widehat D=\phi^{-1}(D)\) and \(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}=U^\mathsf T\boldsymbol\theta\), one has
\[
F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)=F_{\widehat D}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}).
\]
Here \(\widehat D\) is complete in \(\mathbb Z^n\), and all root probabilities and reflected cardinalities are preserved. The bound is sharp: \(D=\{0,1\}^n\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\) give \(F_D=8^{-n}\) for every \(n\ge1\). A rank-zero support is a singleton and gives \(F_D=1\).

## Proof

First consider the case \(N=n\ge1\) in complete integer coordinates. Extend \(w_{\mathbf j}\) by zero for \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus D\), and set \(p_{\mathbf j}=w_{\mathbf j}/W\) at every lattice point. This is the original unnormalized site-weight table, and its total weight is exactly \(W=Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)\), with no conditional normalization. The function
\[
f(\mathbf x)=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}\,
\mathbf1_{\operatorname{conv}(D)}(\mathbf x)
\quad(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^n)
\]
is finite-valued and log-concave. Indeed, between two points of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) the log-concavity inequality is equality, and if either endpoint is outside that set its right side is zero. Completeness makes its lattice restriction precisely \(w\), including every zero. Thus this table is admissible in the sense of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160.

For \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D\) whose midpoint \(\mathbf z\) belongs to \(D\), log-affinity gives
\[
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)}}{W^2}
=p_{\mathbf z}^2.
\]
In particular, for \(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\) this is the reflection identity of accepted fact ebb695e93a721952 with \(\mathbf y=2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\). Reflection permutes \(R_{\mathbf z}\), so arithmetic-geometric mean gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{\mathbf z}
&=\sqrt{p_{\mathbf z}}\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{\mathbf z}}}{2}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}
\left(\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}}+\sqrt{p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x}}\right)\\
&\ge\sqrt{p_{\mathbf z}}\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}}
(p_{\mathbf x}p_{2\mathbf z-\mathbf x})^{1/4}
=k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This includes the central site and all reflected sites; it requires no assumption on the sign or size of the tilt.

Here is the exact map to the Hellinger and parity quantities of accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. Its \(w,W,p\) are precisely the table and normalization just defined. Use its oriented displacement sum, including zero,
\[
H_{\mathbf j}=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}
\sqrt{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}},
\qquad h_{\mathbf j}=H_{\mathbf j}/W.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in D\), the displacement \(\mathbf v\) has a positive summand exactly when \(\mathbf z+\mathbf v\in R_{\mathbf z}\), and every such summand equals \(w_{\mathbf z}\). Consequently
\[
H_{\mathbf z}=k_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z},
\qquad h_{\mathbf z}=k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}.
\]
If \(\mathbf j\notin D\), a positive reflected summand would place \(\mathbf j\) in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), a contradiction, so \(H_{\mathbf j}=0\). The scalar denoted \(R_{\mathbf j}\) in ab57fac07c0d3160 is different from the reflected set denoted \(R_{\mathbf z}\) here. To make that collision explicit, denote only the predecessor's scalar by
\[
R_{\mathbf j}^{\mathrm{mid}}:=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}
p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}.
\]
The exact alias is \(R_{\mathbf j}^{\mathrm{mid}}=R_{\mathbf j}\) of ab57fac07c0d3160, with its original normalization. The log-affine identity gives
\(R_{\mathbf z}^{\mathrm{mid}}=k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2=p_{\mathbf z}h_{\mathbf z}\) on \(D\), and \(R_{\mathbf j}^{\mathrm{mid}}=0\) outside \(D\).

Use exactly that predecessor's parity quantities
\[
\pi_{\mathbf b}=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in D\\\mathbf z\equiv\mathbf b\pmod2}}p_{\mathbf z},
\qquad q=\sum_{\mathbf b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n}\pi_{\mathbf b}^2.
\]
For completeness, the midpoint reindexing is exact here: an ordered pair \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in D^2\) with equal parity has integer midpoint \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\), hence in \(D\). It corresponds bijectively to \((\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\) with \(\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf z}\), with inverse \(\mathbf y=2\mathbf z-\mathbf x\). Every ordered pair is retained, including diagonal pairs. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}^2
&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}R_{\mathbf j}^{\mathrm{mid}}\\
&=\sum_{\substack{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in D^2\\\mathbf x\equiv\mathbf y\pmod2}}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
=\sum_{\mathbf b}\pi_{\mathbf b}^2=q.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\sum_{\mathbf b}\pi_{\mathbf b}=1\), Cauchy-Schwarz over the \(2^n\) parity classes, including empty ones, yields \(q\ge2^{-n}\).

The function \(s\mapsto s^3\) is convex on \([0,\infty)\). Jensen's inequality under the original probability table \(p\), not the conditional midpoint law, now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}(k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z})^3\\
&\ge\left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}k_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\right)^3
=q^3\ge8^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the claimed chain in integer coordinates.

We now justify the saturated intrinsic chart and the exponent for a support in \(\mathbb Z^N\). Let \(E\) and \(\mathbf z_0\) be as in the statement. The group \(E\cap\mathbb Z^N\) is saturated: if \(m\mathbf a\in E\cap\mathbb Z^N\) for \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z^N\) and an integer \(m\ge1\), then \(\mathbf a\in E\cap\mathbb Z^N\). It has an integer group basis. One elementary proof that every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^N\) has such a basis is induction on \(N\): project onto the first coordinate; if the image is zero, use a basis of the kernel, and otherwise the image is \(d\mathbb Z\) for some \(d\ge1\), so a lift of \(d\) together with a basis of the kernel is a basis of the subgroup. The kernel lies in \(\mathbb Z^{N-1}\). An integer group basis is real-linearly independent, because real dependence among integer vectors implies rational dependence by Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb Q\), hence an integer relation. Since \(E\cap\mathbb Z^N\) lies in the \(n\)-dimensional space \(E\) and contains differences from \(D-\mathbf z_0\) spanning \(E\), its basis has exactly \(n\) vectors. These give the asserted matrix \(U\) and bijective chart of the entire intersection lattice, rather than merely a sublattice generated by support differences.

The chart is an affine bijection from \(\mathbb R^n\) onto \(\operatorname{aff}(D)\). Affine maps commute with convex hulls. Thus an integer \(\mathbf u\in\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\) has \(\phi(\mathbf u)\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\), proving
\(\widehat D=\operatorname{conv}(\widehat D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\).
Moreover
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{\phi(\mathbf u)}&=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z_0}
 e^{\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}\cdot\mathbf u},\\
Z_D(\boldsymbol\theta)&=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z_0}
 Z_{\widehat D}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}),\\
p_{\phi(\mathbf u)}&=\widehat p_{\mathbf u}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Hats denote precisely the same definitions on \(\widehat D\) with parameter \(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}\). The identity
\(2\phi(\mathbf u)-\phi(\mathbf v)=\phi(2\mathbf u-\mathbf v)\)
proves
\(R_{\phi(\mathbf u)}=\phi(\widehat R_{\mathbf u})\) and
\(k_{\phi(\mathbf u)}=\widehat k_{\mathbf u}\). Substitution into the half-tilt coefficient cancels the translation factor exactly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{\phi(\mathbf u)}
&=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z_0}
 e^{\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}\cdot\mathbf u/2}
 \sum_{\mathbf v\in\widehat R_{\mathbf u}}
 e^{\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}\cdot\mathbf v/2}}
 {e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z_0}
 Z_{\widehat D}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta})}
=\widehat b_{\mathbf u},\\
F_D(\boldsymbol\theta)
&=\sum_{\mathbf u\in\widehat D}\widehat p_{\mathbf u}\widehat b_{\mathbf u}^3
=F_{\widehat D}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Likewise, at a support center every positive ambient displacement belongs to \(E\cap\mathbb Z^N\) and is uniquely \(U\mathbf v\), so the ambient \(H_{\phi(\mathbf u)}\) is \(e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z_0}\widehat H_{\mathbf u}\), and its normalized value is \(\widehat h_{\mathbf u}\). No lattice-index or volume factor occurs.

The relevant parity quotient is the intrinsic lattice modulo twice itself, with \(2^n\) classes. To check its ambient interpretation, saturation gives
\((E\cap\mathbb Z^N)\cap2\mathbb Z^N=2(E\cap\mathbb Z^N)\): for the nontrivial inclusion, halve an even integer vector in \(E\). Therefore \(\phi(\mathbf u)\) and \(\phi(\mathbf v)\) have the same ambient parity if and only if \(\mathbf u\equiv\mathbf v\pmod2\). Thus the supported ambient parity classes are indexed by intrinsic parity classes, and their collision probability is the chart's \(q\), bounded below by \(2^{-n}\), not merely by \(2^{-N}\). Applying the proved integer-coordinate chain to \(\widehat D\) proves all the intrinsic bounds when \(n\ge1\). If \(n=0\), then \(D=\{\mathbf z_0\}\), and direct substitution gives \(p_{\mathbf z_0}=k_{\mathbf z_0}=b_{\mathbf z_0}=F_D=1=8^0\); the zero-dimensional chart has one empty tuple and the same values.

Finally, \(\operatorname{conv}(\{0,1\}^n)=[0,1]^n\), whose integer points are exactly \(\{0,1\}^n\). At every root of this complete support, reflection forces every coordinate of a reflected site to equal the root coordinate, so \(R_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf z\}\) and \(k_{\mathbf z}=1\). With zero tilt, \(p_{\mathbf z}=b_{\mathbf z}=2^{-n}\), and \(F_D=8^{-n}\), proving sharpness. The proof nowhere uses exposed faces or any halfspace realization. Its lower bound concerns the coefficient \(F_D\) only, so it cannot supply a lower bound for the actual cubic halfspace-depth moment.
