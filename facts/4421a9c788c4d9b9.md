## Statement

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\) be finite, let \(P_D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\) have dimension two, and assume \(D=P_D\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Assume every \((k,l)\in D\) has \(-1\leq k\leq2\), that \(D\) meets both \(k=-1\) and \(k=2\), and that \(\mathbf0=(0,0)\in D\). For \(k\in\{-1,0,1,2\}\), put \(I_k=\{l\in\mathbb Z:(k,l)\in D\}\), allowing an empty section. For \(t,q>0\), define the original weights and their normalization by
\[
W(k,l)=t^kq^l,
\qquad
Z=\sum_{(k,l)\in D}W(k,l),
\qquad
\nu_{\mathbf0}(k,l)=\frac{W(k,l)}{Z}.
\]
The legacy weight \(w_{(k,l)}\) is exactly \(W(k,l)\), with no scaling. Suppose both mean equations hold exactly: \(\sum_{(k,l)\in D}kW(k,l)=0\) and \(\sum_{(k,l)\in D}lW(k,l)=0\).

The row notation is \(\nu_{\mathbf0}=r_{D,\mathbf0}\) of accepted facts `1ed6b6d9eb994c90` and `bf1ff94487296374`: it denotes the unique maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf0\) law. In the positive-law notation of `bf1ff94487296374`, the exact aliases are \(A=D\), \(p=\nu_{\mathbf0}\), and \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\log t,\log q)\); the tilt \(\boldsymbol\lambda\) of `1ed6b6d9eb994c90` is this same vector. The actual depth \(\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf0}}(\mathbf0)\) has precisely the proper closed-halfspace definition in `bf1ff94487296374`.

Define the unnormalized reflected-submeasure mass
\[
Z_{\mathrm{ref}}
=\sum_{k=-1}^{1}\ \sum_{l\in I_k\cap(-I_{-k})}
  \min\{t^kq^l,t^{-k}q^{-l}\}.
\]
Then
\[
Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\geq\frac{Z}{64e^2},
\qquad
\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf0}}(\mathbf0)\geq\frac1{128e^2}.
\]
All lattice coordinates are taken in the saturated affine lattice \(\operatorname{aff}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^2=\mathbb Z^2\), not in a potentially smaller lattice generated by support differences. Every change of coordinates used below has determinant \(1\) or \(-1\).

## Proof

Write \(I_0=\{-a,-a+1,\ldots,b\}\), where \(a,b\) are nonnegative integers, and put \(h=\min\{a,b\}\). Indeed, the real section \(P_D\cap\{k=0\}\) is an interval, completeness includes every integer in that interval, and the origin belongs to the section. In particular \(\{(0,l):-h\leq l\leq h\}\subseteq D\).

We first prove the entropy estimate
\[
Z\leq4e(h+2).
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\) so that the lower integer endpoint of the section after the reflection \((k,l)\mapsto(k,\varepsilon l)\) is \(-h\). This reflection has determinant \(\varepsilon\), fixes the origin, and preserves the exact zero mean. Its new vertical weight parameter is \(q^{\varepsilon}\), while its horizontal parameter remains \(t\).

Let \(\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(x)\) be the lower real endpoint of this reflected polygon at horizontal coordinate \(x\in[-1,2]\). Its horizontal projection is exactly \([-1,2]\), since both extreme sections are met. Convexity makes \(\ell_{\mathrm{low}}\) convex: the segment between two lower-endpoint points lies in the polygon, so the lower endpoint at an intermediate coordinate is no larger than the interpolated endpoints. This function is finite and piecewise affine because the polygon has finitely many edges. Completeness at horizontal coordinate zero gives
\[
-h-1<\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)\leq-h.
\tag{2}
\]
There is a finite supporting slope \(s\) such that \(\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(x)\geq\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)+sx\) for every \(x\in[-1,2]\). To see this directly, for \(u<0<v\), convexity at zero implies
\[
\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(u)-\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)}{u}
\leq
\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(v)-\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)}{v}.
\]
The supremum of the slopes on the left is therefore at most the infimum on the right. Both are finite, since zero is interior to the horizontal projection and the function is piecewise affine. Any slope between them supplies the asserted supporting line, with the inequality on the negative side reversed upon division by \(u<0\).

Choose an integer \(N\) with \(|s-N|\leq1/2\), and use the affine lattice chart
\[
T(k,l)=(k,v),\qquad v=\varepsilon l-Nk,
\qquad
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-N&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
Its determinant is \(\varepsilon\). Its inverse is \((k,v)\mapsto(k,\varepsilon(v+Nk))\), which is integral. Thus this is a primitive unimodular chart of the full saturated lattice, and \(D'=T(D)=\operatorname{conv}(D')\cap\mathbb Z^2\). It cannot introduce or discard lattice points by passing to an unsaturated support-difference lattice.

Every \((k,v)\in D'\) satisfies
\[
v\geq\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)+(s-N)k
\geq\ell_{\mathrm{low}}(0)-1
>-h-2,
\]
since \(|k|\leq2\). Integrality consequently gives \(v\geq-h-1\). The transformed weights are exactly
\[
W'(k,v)=W\bigl(T^{-1}(k,v)\bigr)
=(tq^{\varepsilon N})^k(q^{\varepsilon})^v.
\tag{4}
\]
Their normalizer is the original \(Z\), their weight at the origin is still one, and their normalized law \(\nu'_{\mathbf0}=T_*\nu_{\mathbf0}\) has both means zero. This also states the entire normalization map; no constant factor is suppressed.

Let \(H(\nu)=-\sum\nu\log\nu\). Exact mean zero and the weight formulas give
\[
H(\nu'_{\mathbf0})=H(\nu_{\mathbf0})=\log Z.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the same mean-zero log-affine entropy identity used in `1ed6b6d9eb994c90`, with the aliases stated above. That fact's entropy-row identification agrees with the maximum-entropy notation of `bf1ff94487296374`.

On the integer set \(\{-1,0,1,2\}\times\{-h-1,-h,\ldots\}\), consider the probability law
\[
g_{\mathrm{ref}}(k,v)
=\frac1{4(h+2)}
 \left(\frac{h+1}{h+2}\right)^{v+h+1}.
\tag{6}
\]
The geometric series verifies that its total mass is one. It is strictly positive at every point of \(D'\). Relative entropy against this reference is nonnegative. Explicitly, \(-\log u\geq1-u\) gives
\[
\sum_{(k,v)\in D'}\nu'_{\mathbf0}(k,v)
 \log\frac{\nu'_{\mathbf0}(k,v)}{g_{\mathrm{ref}}(k,v)}
\geq1-\sum_{(k,v)\in D'}g_{\mathrm{ref}}(k,v)\geq0.
\]
Using \(\mathbb E_{\nu'_{\mathbf0}}v=0\), this yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log Z
&\leq\log\bigl(4(h+2)\bigr)
 +(h+1)\log\frac{h+2}{h+1}\\
&\leq\log\bigl(4(h+2)\bigr)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality is \(\log(1+x)\leq x\) with \(x=1/(h+1)\). Exponentiating proves (1). No counts or concavity properties of rounded noncentral sections have been used.

We next use the central section in the original coordinates. Put \(u=|\log q|\). For every integer \(0\leq l\leq h\), the central section contains a point of weight \(e^{ul}\): choose \((0,l)\) when \(q\geq1\), and \((0,-l)\) when \(q<1\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{l=0}^{h}e^{ul}\leq Z,
\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{ref}}\geq\sum_{l=-h}^{h}e^{-u|l|}
\geq\sum_{l=0}^{h}e^{-ul}.
\tag{7}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz on these two reciprocal geometric sequences now gives
\[
(h+1)^2
\leq\left(\sum_{l=0}^{h}e^{ul}\right)
     \left(\sum_{l=0}^{h}e^{-ul}\right)
\leq Z Z_{\mathrm{ref}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (1) and (8) imply, including \(h=0\),
\[
\frac{Z_{\mathrm{ref}}}{Z}
\geq\frac{(h+1)^2}{Z^2}
\geq\frac{(h+1)^2}{16e^2(h+2)^2}
\geq\frac1{64e^2},
\]
because \(h+2\leq2(h+1)\).

Finally define a submeasure on \(D\) by assigning mass \(Z^{-1}\min\{W(\mathbf x),W(-\mathbf x)\}\) to each \(\mathbf x\in D\cap(-D)\), and zero to the other points. It is dominated by \(\nu_{\mathbf0}\), is invariant under negation, and has total mass \(Z_{\mathrm{ref}}/Z\). The equality of this total with the displayed four-strip formula follows because \(D\cap(-D)\) can have horizontal coordinates only \(-1,0,1\).

Every proper closed halfspace containing the origin contains its parallel translate through the origin. For a nonzero normal \(\mathbf a\), the latter is \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x\geq0\}\). On each pair \(\{\mathbf x,-\mathbf x\}\), this closed halfspace contains at least one member; the two submeasure masses are equal. Points on its boundary, including the origin, are retained rather than discarded. Its submeasure mass is therefore at least half the total. Taking the infimum in the actual-depth definition of `bf1ff94487296374` proves \(\tau_{\nu_{\mathbf0}}(\mathbf0)\geq Z_{\mathrm{ref}}/(2Z)\geq1/(128e^2)\), as required.
