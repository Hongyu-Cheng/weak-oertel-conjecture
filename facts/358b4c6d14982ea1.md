## Statement

Let n >= 1, and let A be a finite nonempty subset of \(\mathbb Z^n\). Assume that A is closed under integral midpoints, that each occupied coordinatewise parity class contains at most two atoms, and that p is an original positive probability law on A satisfying \(p_u p_v=p_{(u+v)/2}^2\) for every distinct same-parity pair u,v in A. Equivalently, arbitrary positive original weights \(w_x\) may be supplied, with the single original normalizer \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\) and \(p_x=w_x/Z\). Extend p by zero outside A.

Let \(\mathcal J\) be the set of all midpoints of distinct same-parity pairs in A. For a supported center x put \(R_x=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace\), and assume exactly
\(A=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal J}R_j\).
In particular \(\mathcal J\ne\varnothing\). Write \(H=|\mathcal J|\), let K be the actual number of occupied parity classes, and let \(m_j\ge1\) be the number of unordered nontrivial pairs in \(R_j\), so \(h_j=|R_j|=2m_j+1\). Every hub is included in \(\mathcal J\), even when it is an endpoint in another full core. Define the full endpoint sum \(S_j=\sum_{x\in R_j\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace}p_x\) and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_j&=\frac{S_j/p_j+2}{2(m_j+1)},
&M&=\min_{j\in\mathcal J}\mu_j,
&U&=\max_{j\in\mathcal J}\mu_j,\\
\Delta_j&=4m_j(m_j+1)K^2p_j^3,
&\Delta&=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\Delta_j,
&A_0&=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3,\\
H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},
&b(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x)^2,
&A_{\rm full}&=K^2b(p)=A_0+\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Use the full oriented reflected-row quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)},
&Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,
&q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_{x\in A}q_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Thus nonzero pair orientations occur twice, and the central atom occurs once. In the two-hub notation of accepted fact a87effc3cf58f392, its \(a_a,S_a,m_a,h_a,\mu_a,\Delta_a\) are respectively the present \(p_j,S_j,m_j,h_j,\mu_j,\Delta_j\). The present K is always occupied, never a substitution for the ambient capacity \(2^n\).

Assume \(A_0<1\) and \(U\le2M\), and put \(d=-\log A_0\), \(g=\log A_{\rm full}\), and \(t=\log(2U)\). With the absolute constant \(C=2^{60}\),
$$
g>0,\qquad d\le Ce^{-t}g,\qquad \nu\le Ct^2g.
\tag{3}
$$
More precisely, when \(M\ge256\), the scaled class masses \(\rho_\eta=K\sum_{x\in\eta}p_x\), with eta ranging over the K occupied parity classes, satisfy
$$
\mathcal F:=\sum_\eta(\rho_\eta-1)^2(\rho_\eta+2),
\qquad H\le8\mathcal F<\frac{96K}{13M^2},
\qquad K>\frac{13HM^2}{96}.
\tag{4}
$$
When \(M\ge2^{13}\), one also has
$$
1-A_0\le\frac3{4M^2},\qquad
\Delta>\frac1{2048M},\qquad
g>\frac1{8192M},\qquad
\nu\le2t^2g.
\tag{5}
$$

For the product consequence, let L >= 1, and for each i let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), \(n_i\ge1\), and its original positive law \(p_i=w_i/Z_i\) satisfy all the geometric, exact-equality, and exact-union hypotheses above. The hub number and the occupied count \(H_i,K_i\) may vary arbitrarily. Require \(U_i\le2M_i\) only in active factors, meaning those with \(A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3<1\). Put \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i\) and \(N=\sum_i n_i\). Define the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its moments by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace,\\
M_2(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^2,
&M_3(P)&=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
The one common absolute coefficient \(c=\frac18\exp(-72C^2)>0\) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-2}\ge c4^{-N},\\
M_3(P)&\ge c\prod_iK_i^{-3}\ge c8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
These conclusions include one hub, multiple hubs in a parity cell, overlaps of center and endpoint roles, lower-dimensional supports, and zero row-score variances. Every original atom and normalizer remains in use. No convex completeness assumption is needed for this restricted theorem, and no result is claimed for a law on a strictly larger support by applying this theorem to its core union.

## Proof

First fix one factor. Every two-atom parity class is one pair, whose supported midpoint is a hub. Conversely, every nontrivial supported reflected pair is one of these classes. Distinct two-atom classes have disjoint endpoint sets. Therefore the sets \(R_j\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace\), j in \(\mathcal J\), are pairwise disjoint, even if their atoms include other hubs. A pair cannot contain its own midpoint, because its endpoints are distinct. Let \(\mathcal J_{\rm sing}\) be the atoms in singleton parity classes. The exact-union assumption says precisely that every member of \(\mathcal J_{\rm sing}\) is a hub. Consequently
$$
\sum_jm_j+|\mathcal J_{\rm sing}|=K,
\qquad
\sum_jS_j+\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\rm sing}}p_j=1,
\qquad |A|\le2K.
\tag{8}
$$
All sums over j without another specification range over \(\mathcal J\). The disjointness used here concerns endpoint sets, not the full cores themselves.

Write the weights of the r-th pair centered at j as \(p_je^{u_{j,r}},p_je^{-u_{j,r}}\). Exact equality gives this representation with real scores. Thus
$$
\mu_j=\frac{1+\sum_{r=1}^{m_j}\cosh u_{j,r}}{m_j+1}\ge1,
\qquad
2m_j\mu_jp_j\le S_j=2p_j((m_j+1)\mu_j-1)
\le4m_j\mu_jp_j.
\tag{9}
$$
Exact equality also makes the full geometric law uniform on each hub row, so \(H_p(j)=h_jp_j\) and \(V_j=(2/h_j)\sum_ru_{j,r}^2\). At a nonhub the reflected row is a singleton, so \(H_p(x)=p_x\) and \(V_x=0\). This proves the final identity in (1). Holder for the entire original law and \(|A|\le2K\) give \(A_0\ge1/4\). On the active branch, therefore, \(0<d\le\log4<2\).

We establish the occupied-count full-energy charge. This is the mechanism of accepted fact 530e875c56330666, whose proof is now repeated at occupied K; its ambient-capacity hypothesis is not assumed. The same occupied version for two hubs appears in accepted fact a87effc3cf58f392, and no part of the argument below bounds the number of hubs.

For every atom x let \(m_x\) be its pair count when it is a hub and zero otherwise. Put \(z_x=Kp_x\), \(L_x=(2m_x+1)z_x\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_{\rm row}&=\sum_xz_x(L_x-1)^2,
&G&=\sum_\eta(\rho_\eta-1)^2,\\
\mathcal B&=\sum_xm_xz_x^2,
&\beta&=K\sum_jm_jp_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
Here \(\mathcal F_{\rm row},G,\mathcal B\) are the quantities denoted F,G,B in 530e875c56330666, with the present occupied-cell scaling. The different symbol \(\mathcal F\) in (4) always denotes the cubic class defect. We have \(\mathcal B=K\beta\), \(\sum_xz_x=\sum_\eta\rho_\eta=K\), and exactly K terms in the class sums. Exact pair products give
\(\sum_\eta\rho_\eta^2=\sum_xz_x^2+2\mathcal B=\sum_xz_xL_x\).
Expansion then yields
$$
\mathcal F_{\rm row}+2G
=\sum_xz_xL_x^2-K
=K(A_{\rm full}-1).
\tag{11}
$$

For this paragraph only, call a hub charge-good if \(7/8\le L_x\le9/8\), and charge-bad otherwise. Write \(\mathcal B_{\rm g},\mathcal B_{\rm b}\) for their contributions to \(\mathcal B\), and let \(Z_{\rm g}\) be the sum of \(z_x\) over charge-good hubs. A charge-good hub has \(z_x\le3/8\) and \(m_xz_x^2=z_x(L_x-z_x)/2\le9z_x/16\); hence \(\mathcal B_{\rm g}\le9Z_{\rm g}/16\). A charge-bad hub satisfies \(m_xz_x^2\le z_xL_x/2\). The quotient \(L/[2(L-1)^2]\) is at most 28 for \(0\le L<7/8\) and at most 36 for \(L>9/8\). Indeed it increases up to 7/8 on the first interval; on the second write it as \(1/(2v^2)+1/(2v)\) with \(v=L-1>1/8\). Thus \(\mathcal B_{\rm b}\le36\mathcal F_{\rm row}\).

Cells with \(\rho_\eta<7/8\) contribute at most 56G to \(Z_{\rm g}\), since their total charge-good hub mass is at most \(\rho_\eta\le56(\rho_\eta-1)^2\). If a charge-good hub is in a cell of total at least 7/8, the cell is a pair. Denote this endpoint mass by \(\upsilon\le3/8\), its partner by \(\omega\ge1/2\), and its midpoint mass by \(\xi=z_k\). There is at most one charge-good hub in this cell, because two would have total at most 3/4. The identity \(\upsilon\omega=\xi^2\) holds. If k is charge-good, then \(\xi\le3/8\), and the larger root formula gives
$$
\omega=\frac{\rho_\eta+\sqrt{\rho_\eta^2-4\xi^2}}2
\ge\frac{7+\sqrt{13}}{16}>\frac58,
\qquad \upsilon\le\frac85\xi^2.
\tag{12}
$$
If k is charge-bad, \(\omega\ge1/2\) gives \(\upsilon\le2\xi^2\). Distinct cells are distinct pairs; summing the squares of their midpoint weights counts each hub at most its pair multiplicity. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm g}&\le\frac85\mathcal B_{\rm g}+2\mathcal B_{\rm b}+56G,\\
\mathcal B_{\rm g}&\le\frac9{10}\mathcal B_{\rm g}
+\frac98\mathcal B_{\rm b}+\frac{63}2G,\\
\mathcal B&\le\frac{49}4\mathcal B_{\rm b}+315G
\le441\mathcal F_{\rm row}+315G
\le441(\mathcal F_{\rm row}+2G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
Equations (10)-(11) imply \(A_{\rm full}-1\ge\beta/441>0\), proving \(g>0\). This charge proof includes arbitrary hub endpoint incidences and uses no directed acyclicity.

For later bounded-mean use, suppose \(U\le T\), where \(T\ge1\), and put \(s=\sum_jm_jp_j\). From (8)-(9), the endpoint mass is at most 4Ts, while the singleton mass is at most s because every singleton is a hub with \(m_j\ge1\). Hence \(s\ge1/(5T)\). Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and \(\sum_jm_j\le K\) give
$$
\beta=K\sum_jm_jp_j^2
\ge\frac{K}{\sum_jm_j}\left(\sum_jm_jp_j\right)^2
\ge s^2\ge\frac1{25T^2}.
\tag{14}
$$
Thus \(A_{\rm full}\ge1+1/(11025T^2)\), and \(\log(1+v)\ge v/2\) for \(0\le v\le1\) gives \(g\ge1/(22050T^2)\). This evaluates the logarithm at its displayed lower bound, not at an assumed upper bound for the actual excess.

We next prove the new hub count. Assume activity, comparability, and \(M\ge256\). A pair centered at j with total probability r has endpoint cubic sum \(r^3-3p_j^2r\). Jensen over the \(m_j\) pair totals and (9) give
$$
\sum_{x\in R_j\setminus\lbrace j\rbrace}p_x^3
\ge\frac{S_j^3}{m_j^2}-3p_j^2S_j
\ge\left(1-\frac3{4M^2}\right)\frac{S_j^3}{m_j^2}
\ge\frac{13}{16}\frac{S_j^3}{m_j^2}.
\tag{15}
$$
The last inequality already holds for \(M\ge2\). The endpoint sets are disjoint, and singleton cubic terms are nonnegative. Activity therefore implies
\(\sum_jS_j^3/m_j^2<16/(13K^2)\).
Define \(b_j=Kp_j\) and \(\overline R_j=KS_j/m_j\). By (9) and comparability,
\(2Mb_j\le\overline R_j\le8Mb_j\).
For \(f(r)=(r-1)^2(r+2)=r^3-3r+2\), the exact cellwise cubic identity gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
K(1-A_0)&=3\sum_jm_jb_j^2\overline R_j-\mathcal F,\\
\mathcal F&<3\sum_jm_jb_j^2\overline R_j
\le\frac3{4M^2}\sum_jm_j\overline R_j^3
<\frac{12K}{13M^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
For clarity, the cubic identity for a pair cell centered at j is
\(z_u^3+z_v^3=\rho_\eta^3-3b_j^2\rho_\eta\), whereas a singleton contributes \(\rho_\eta^3\). Also \(\sum_\eta f(\rho_\eta)=\sum_\eta\rho_\eta^3-K\), because there are K occupied cells and their total scaled mass is K. These observations prove the first line of (16), including singleton hubs.

For the rest of the count proof, call a hub row-good when \(1/2\le\overline R_j\le2\), and row-bad otherwise. These terms are separate from the charge terminology used in (12)-(13). The function f is convex on \([0,\infty)\), since \(f''(r)=6r\), and satisfies \(f(r)\ge5/8\) outside \([1/2,2]\). On \([0,1/2]\) it decreases to \(f(1/2)=5/8\); on \([2,\infty)\) it increases from \(f(2)=4\). Let \(\mathcal F_j\) be the sum of \(f(\rho_\eta)\) over the \(m_j\) pair cells centered at j. Jensen gives \(\mathcal F_j\ge m_j f(\overline R_j)\), whence
$$
\sum_{j\text{ row-bad}}m_j
\le\frac85\sum_{j\text{ row-bad}}\mathcal F_j
\le\frac85\mathcal F.
\tag{17}
$$
The second inequality holds because the pair-cell groups partition all pair cells and every singleton defect is nonnegative.

Every row-good hub has \(1/(16M)\le b_j\le1/M\). The number of row-good hubs whose own containing parity cell has total outside \([1/2,2]\) is at most \((16/5)\mathcal F\): there are at most two hubs in a cell, and each such cell contributes at least 5/8 to \(\mathcal F\). This includes every singleton row-good hub, since its cell total is \(b_j\le1/M<1/2\).

Consider any remaining row-good hub j. Its containing cell has total \(\rho_\eta\in[1/2,2]\), so it cannot be a singleton. Its partner has scaled mass \(\omega=\rho_\eta-b_j\ge1/2-1/M\ge3/8\). Let k be this pair's midpoint, which is a hub. Exact equality gives
$$
b_k^2=b_j\omega\ge\frac3{128M},
\qquad
\overline R_k\ge2Mb_k\ge\sqrt{\frac{3M}{32}}>2.
\tag{18}
$$
Thus k is row-bad. Every such j is an endpoint in a pair belonging to a row-bad k, and a row with \(m_k\) pairs contains at most \(2m_k\) such hub endpoints. By (17), these remaining row-good hubs number at most \((16/5)\mathcal F\). Row-bad hubs themselves number at most \(\sum_{\rm bad}m_j\le(8/5)\mathcal F\). Adding these three exhaustive hub categories proves \(H\le8\mathcal F\), and (16) proves (4).

This count needs neither unique hub parity classes nor an ordering of the hubs. If two hubs occupy the same cell, the two-endpoint bounds already count both. A normal-mass cell with a row-good endpoint is charged directly to its row-bad midpoint; no recursive charge is used, so potential cycles cause no repeated-charge assumption. When H = 1 the same argument and all the positive sums remain valid.

We record the atomic deficit with its sharper coefficient. For every \(M\ge2\), (15) before its last inequality bounds each row's endpoint cubic sum below by \((1-3/(4M^2))S_j^3/m_j^2\). Bound each singleton term below by the same factor times its cube. Treat a row endpoint group as having capacity \(m_j\), and a singleton as having capacity one. The capacities sum to K and the masses sum to one by (8). Holder yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0&\ge\left(1-\frac3{4M^2}\right)
K^2\left(\sum_j\frac{S_j^3}{m_j^2}
+\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_{\rm sing}}p_j^3\right)
\ge1-\frac3{4M^2},\\
\delta:=1-A_0&\le\frac3{4M^2},
\qquad d\le\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}
\le\frac{12}{13M^2}<\frac1{M^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
The Holder inequality used is \(\sum_av_a^3/c_a^2\ge(\sum_av_a)^3/(\sum_ac_a)^2\) for positive capacities \(c_a\). The final bound uses \(3/(4M^2)\le3/16\).

Comparability and (8)-(9) give \(1\le(8M+1)s\le9Ms\), so \(s\ge1/(9M)\). A second use of Holder gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&\ge4K^2\sum_j\frac{(m_jp_j)^3}{m_j}
\ge\frac{4K^2s^3}{(\sum_j\sqrt{m_j})^2}
\ge\frac{4Ks^3}{H}\\
&>\frac{13}{17496M}>\frac1{2048M}
\qquad(M\ge256).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$
Here \((\sum_j\sqrt{m_j})^2\le H\sum_jm_j\le HK\), and the strict lower bound uses (4). If \(M\ge2^{13}\), then (19)-(20) imply \(\delta<\Delta/4\), because \(1536/M\le3/16<1/4\). Consequently
$$
A_{\rm full}=1+\Delta-\delta\ge1+\frac34\Delta
>1+\frac3{8192M},
\qquad
g>\frac3{16384M}>\frac1{8192M}.
\tag{21}
$$
The logarithm estimate again follows from \(\log(1+v)\ge v/2\), evaluated at \(v=3/(8192M)<1\). Since \(e^{-t}=1/(2U)\ge1/(4M)\), (19) and (21) imply \(d\le2^{15}e^{-t}g\).

We estimate the full price next. The increasing concavity of \(v\mapsto\operatorname{arcosh}(v)^2\) on \([1,\infty)\) is proved in accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. Applying it to the auxiliary uniform average of 1 and all the \(\cosh u_{j,r}\) gives
$$
V_j\le\frac{2(m_j+1)}{h_j}\operatorname{arcosh}(\mu_j)^2
\le\frac43\log(2\mu_j)^2\le\frac43t^2.
\tag{22}
$$
This is an average of scores only, not a conditional replacement of the original law. Since \(h_j\ge3\), the center probabilities satisfy
$$
\sum_jq_j
=\frac{K^2}{A_{\rm full}}\sum_jh_j^2p_j^3
\le\frac98\frac\Delta{A_{\rm full}}.
\tag{23}
$$
For \(M\ge2^{13}\), the inequality \(A_{\rm full}-1\ge3\Delta/4\) and \(1-a^{-1}\le\log a\) for \(a\ge1\) show \(\sum_jq_j\le(3/2)g\). Equations (22)-(23), and zero variance at every nonhub, prove \(\nu\le2t^2g\). This proves (5) and the scalar estimates in the large-mean range.

If \(1\le M<2^{13}\), then \(U\le T:=2^{14}\). By (14), \(g\ge1/(22050T^2)\), while \(d<2\), \(e^{-t}\ge1/(2T)\), and \(\nu\le(4/3)t^2\) by (22) and \(\sum_jq_j\le1\). Therefore
$$
\frac{d}{e^{-t}g}<88200T^3<2^{59},
\qquad
\frac{\nu}{t^2g}\le29400T^2<2^{43}.
\tag{24}
$$
The division by \(t^2\) is legitimate since \(t\ge\log2>0\). Together with the large-mean bounds, this proves (3) with \(C=2^{60}\).

It remains to prove that this scalar budget composes without a loss per factor. We give the factor-level binning argument of accepted fact 1b91f86ed4c91107 and the selected-reflection argument of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 explicitly, so that the unchanged original laws and the occupied counts are visible throughout.

In each inactive factor select singleton auxiliary rows at every center. For each active factor retain its \(t_i,d_i,g_i,\nu_i\) from (3). For every integer \(\ell\ge0\), form
\(I_\ell=\lbrace i:A_{0,i}<1,\ \ell\le t_i<\ell+1\rbrace\)
and \(G_\ell=\sum_{i\in I_\ell}g_i\). There are finitely many nonempty bins. Select all full rows in every factor of a bin precisely when \(G_\ell\ge4C(\ell+1)^2\); otherwise select singleton rows throughout its factors. If a bin is selected in full, its total price \(T_\ell=\sum_{i\in I_\ell}\nu_i\) obeys
\(T_\ell\le C(\ell+1)^2G_\ell\le G_\ell^2/4\), so \(G_\ell\ge2\sqrt{T_\ell}\).
In a discarded bin its lost logarithmic energy obeys
\(\sum_{i\in I_\ell}d_i\le Ce^{-\ell}G_\ell<4C^2e^{-\ell}(\ell+1)^2\).
Since \(e^{-1}<1/2\) and \(\sum_{\ell\ge0}(\ell+1)^2r^\ell=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\), all discarded deficits sum to at most \(D=48C^2\).

Denote each chosen symmetric row by \(\widehat R_{i,x}\), and define the auxiliary quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(x)&=\sum_{y\in\widehat R_{i,x}}\sqrt{p_{i,y}p_{i,2x-y}},
&\widehat b_i&=\sum_xp_{i,x}\widehat H_i(x)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,x}(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,y}p_{i,2x-y}}}{\widehat H_i(x)},
&\widehat q_{i,x}&=\frac{p_{i,x}\widehat H_i(x)^2}{\widehat b_i},\\
\widehat V_{i,x}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,x}}
\left(\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,y}}{p_{i,2x-y}}\right)^2,
&\widehat\nu_i&=\sum_x\widehat q_{i,x}\widehat V_{i,x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
In a factor selected in full, \(\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i)=g_i\) and \(\widehat\nu_i=\nu_i\). In a factor selected as singletons, \(K_i^2\widehat b_i=A_{0,i}\) and \(\widehat\nu_i=0\). Inactive factors therefore contribute nonnegative logarithmic energy and zero price. The bin estimates give
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_g:=\sum_i\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i)
&\ge\sum_{\ell\text{ selected}}G_\ell-D\\
&\ge2\sum_{\ell\text{ selected}}\sqrt{T_\ell}-D
\ge2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}-D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
The final inequality is \(\sum_\ell\sqrt{T_\ell}\ge\sqrt{\sum_\ell T_\ell}\), also valid for an empty sum.

For a product center \(J=(j_i)\), put \(\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}\), \(\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i)\), and \(\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i}\). Let \(Y_J(X)=\sum_i(1/2)\log(p_{i,x_i}/p_{i,2j_i-x_i})\) and \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}\). Each row involution preserves its geometric law and negates its score; hence the score means vanish. Independence and direct substitution give
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,
\qquad \mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,
\qquad
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}
\quad(X\in\widehat R_J).
\tag{27}
$$
The probability here is exactly \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,x_i}/\prod_iZ_i\).

Every ambient closed halfspace containing J contains at least one member of each reflected pair \(X,2J-X\), and contains J itself. Summing over these disjoint reflection orbits, with the two nonzero orientations and one central atom, yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}
\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
$$
The final inequality uses Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz. At the central orbit its factor one half uses no more than the available central mass. This argument applies to every ambient containing halfspace regardless of the affine dimension of the support.

Under the auxiliary product center law \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), the exact identity \(P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=(\prod_i\widehat b_i)\prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}\) holds, and \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\). Squaring (28), averaging under the original P, and using Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz again give
$$
