## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge 1$, a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb R^n$, and nonempty compact convex sets $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ for $z\in P$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_z})$, and $M=\mu(S)$. Let $h_\mu(z,x)$ be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(z,x)$.

The family is compatible if the fiber of $C=\operatorname{conv}(S)$ at every $z\in P$ is exactly $K_z$. Equivalently, whenever a convex combination of bases in $P$ equals a base $z\in P$, the same Minkowski combination of the corresponding fibers lies in $K_z$. For a lattice application require $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$, so $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$.

Assume compatibility and $M>0$. Put $q_z=\max_{x\in K_z}h_\mu(z,x)$ and $Q=\max_{z\in P}q_z$. At any maximizing mark $x_z$ of a positive-volume fiber $K_z$, the active unit continuous normals
$$
U_z=\{u\in\mathbb S^{d-1}:c_z(u,u\cdot x_z)=q_z\}
$$
have zero in their convex hull, where $c_z(u,t)=\inf_a\mu\{(w,y):a\cdot(w-z)+u\cdot y\ge t\}$. There are $2\le k\le d+1$ actual nonvertical closed caps of mass exactly $q_z$ through $(z,x_z)$ whose unit continuous normals form a minimal positive dependence. The caps cover the whole plane $\{z\}\times\mathbb R^d$. At a null site two actual nonvertical zero-mass caps cover its whole plane. In particular at most $|P|(d+1)$ such caps cover $P\times\mathbb R^d$, each of mass at most $Q$. Caps selected at a site with $q_z<Q$ have strict slack at the common budget $Q$.

## Proof

By the anchored capacity theorem, the depth on a fiber is the minimum of $c_z(u,u\cdot x)$ over unit $u$, the capacities are continuous, and every directional infimum is attained at finite slope. In particular $U_z$ is nonempty and compact. The compatible-fiber positive-depth lemma puts $x_z$ in the interior of $K_z$. Fix $r>0$ with $x_z+rB_d\subset K_z$.

If zero is outside $\operatorname{conv}U_z$, strict separation gives a unit $v$ and $\eta>0$ with $u\cdot v<-\eta$ on $U_z$. The directions satisfying $u\cdot v<-\eta$ form an open neighborhood of $U_z$ on the unit sphere. For such a direction and $0<s\le r/2$, replacing $x_z$ by $x_z+sv$ lowers the threshold by more than $\eta s$. For every slope $a$, the cap mass increases by at least
$$
\eta s\,\kappa_{d-1}(r/2)^{d-1},
$$
where $\kappa_{d-1}$ is the volume of the unit $(d-1)$-ball and $\kappa_0=1$. Indeed the cap grows on each fiber, and the anchor fiber alone gains the slab in its ball with normal coordinates in $[-\eta s,0)$ measured from $x_z$. Each of the slab's sections has radius at least $r/2$ (for $d=1$ use the interval length). The added mass is uniform in $a$ because the slope term vanishes at $z$. Taking the infimum over $a$ retains this uniform increase. On the compact complementary set of directions, if nonempty, continuity and absence of active directions give a positive minimum gap above $q_z$ at $x_z$. Uniform continuity keeps a positive gap there for all sufficiently small $s>0$. Therefore the minimum over the sphere strictly increases at $x_z+sv\in K_z$, a contradiction.

Choose an inclusion-minimal subset of $U_z$ whose convex hull contains zero. By Carathéodory it has at most $d+1$ members. In a convex representation of zero using this subset all coefficients $\lambda_j$ are positive, and no proper subset has a positive dependence. There are at least two unit normals. Choose one attaining slope for each selected $u_j$, with cap threshold $u_j\cdot x_z$ at $z$. These caps pass through the mark and have mass $q_z$. On the entire plane over $z$, the weighted sum of their cap margins is $\sum_j\lambda_j u_j\cdot(x-x_z)=0$. Thus at least one margin is nonnegative at every point of that plane.

If $K_z$ is null, its minimal face in $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ has only null fibers by the compatible-fiber lemma. Take its affine exposer $g$ as in that lemma and any unit $u$. For sufficiently large finite $T>0$, the two caps with margins $Tg(w)+u\cdot y$ and $Tg(w)-u\cdot y$ exclude all original off-face fibers. They have mass zero, and their restrictions over $z$ are opposite closed halfspaces that cover the whole plane. Combining the selections for every site gives the bound because $2\le d+1$. Since $M>0$ there is a positive fiber and $Q>0$. The selected caps over null sites or other sites with $q_z<Q$ are therefore slack as asserted.
