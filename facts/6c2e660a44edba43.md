## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and lattice complete, and let $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be positive compatible compact convex fibers, so their canonical hull $C$ has exactly these fibers at $P$. Write $dm$ for the original mixed volume of total mass $M$, and $E_{C_z}(y)$ for ordinary projective energy at an own-interior mark. Let $L\ge0$ be continuous and concave on $C$ with $\int L\,dm=M$. Put $N=d+1$, $F=E^{1/d}$ and $f=(LE/M)^{1/N}$, with $E=0$ at own boundaries. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and fixed covector norm $\Gamma$, the buffered depth of $(p,y)$ is the infimum of its unchanged pure-base containing cap masses and the masses of all nonvertical caps
$$
a\cdot Z+u\cdot X\ge a\cdot p+u\cdot y-\varepsilon f(p,y)\Gamma(u).
$$
Assume the sampled maximum $Q>0$ is attained and every deepest sampled mark is own-interior with an attaining nonvertical cap that cuts its own fiber properly. For any positive convex combination $w=\sum_i\lambda_iw_i$ of deepest marks with sampled base, $w$ is deepest and $f(w)=\sum_i\lambda_i f(w_i)$. Such a combination cannot use both zero-$L$ and positive-$L$ inputs. For an opposite-status pair $w_0,w_+$,
$$
f((w_0+w_+)/2)\ge \tfrac12 f(w_+)(1+F(w_0)/F(w_+))^{d/N}>\tfrac12 f(w_+).
$$
The deepest bases partition into $Z$ supporting zero-$L$ marks and $J$ supporting positive-$L$ marks. Both satisfy $\operatorname{conv}(Z)\cap P=Z$, $\operatorname{conv}(J)\cap P=J$, with empty sets allowed. Their occupied parities are disjoint. In particular $k(Z)+k(J)=k(Z\cup J)\le k(P)$, where $k$ counts occupied classes modulo $2\mathbb Z^n$.

## Proof

On the own-interior domain $F$ is jointly concave by 5aeb5916857f97a9. It is positive: if the own ball of radius $r$ around the mark lies in its fiber, every admissible denominator $1+b\cdot(x-y)$ has $r\lVert b\rVert\le1$ and is at most $2$ on that ball. The energy infimum is then at least the ball volume times $2^{-(d+1)}$. Convexity of $C$ combines own inballs, so any convex combination of these marks is still own-interior. Weighted Hölder and the two concavity inequalities give concavity of $f=M^{-1/N}L^{1/N}F^{d/N}$ there, including zeros of $L$. For any fixed nonvertical normal, the condition that its buffered cap has mass at least $Q$ requires the convex function $a\cdot p+u\cdot y-\varepsilon\Gamma(u)f(p,y)$ to lie in a lower interval, since cap mass decreases with threshold. For pure-base through-mark caps the same argument uses the affine threshold with no buffer. Thus all cap inequalities of mass at least $Q$ hold at the combination. Its sampled base makes it an eligible mark, so its depth is exactly $Q$.

Choose an attaining cap at this combination, normalize $\Gamma(u)=1$, and write $t$ for its threshold and $t_i$ for the thresholds of the same normal at the deepest inputs. The actual own fiber is full-dimensional and the cut proper, so moving the threshold any sufficiently small positive amount loses positive own volume. Hence any threshold greater than $t$ has mass strictly below $Q$, even if other fibers have plateaus. Each $t_i$ has mass at least $Q$, so $t_i\le t$. Concavity gives $t\le\sum_i\lambda_i t_i\le t$. Since $\varepsilon>0$, equality forces the asserted affinity of $f$ along this combination.

If some $L_i=0$ and some $L_i>0$, apply weighted Hölder using only the positive-$L$ indices $B$:
$$
\sum_i\lambda_i f(w_i)\le
M^{-1/N}(\sum_i\lambda_i L_i)^{1/N}(\sum_{i\in B}\lambda_i F_i)^{d/N}.
$$
The corresponding lower bound for $f(w)$ has the sum of $F_i$ over all indices and is strictly larger because each $F_i>0$. This contradicts affinity. The two-input calculation with equal weights yields the explicit midpoint inequality.

If $L=0$ at an own-interior point, nonnegativity and concavity force it to vanish throughout that fiber: any chosen fiber point can be paired with a point just beyond the given interior point on the same line. Thus $Z$ and $J$ are disjoint. For any sampled base in the convex hull of one class, combine deepest marks from that class. The new mark is deepest, and its affinity of $f$ gives the same status because $F>0$ there. This proves closedness of both classes. A zero and a positive base of equal parity would have a sampled midpoint by lattice completeness, contradicting the mixed-status exclusion.
