## Statement

Let $K_w(p)\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a finite actual family of positive compact convex fibers at fixed bases $w\in P$, parametrized on an open neighborhood of $p_0$ (which can be an open coordinate chart of a feasible equality family). Write $\mu_p$ for the sum of their fiber Lebesgue measures and $M(p)>0$ for its total mass. Fix a positive circuit of actual closed caps $H_j(b)=\{(w,x):v_j\cdot w+u_j\cdot x\ge b\}$ through an old interior mark at base $z$, each with the same old raw mass $\tau_0$. The fixed weights satisfy $\sum_j\rho_j=1$, $\sum_j\rho_ju_j=0$, and $A=\sum_j\rho_jv_j$. Assume $F_j(p,b)=\mu_p(H_j(b))$ and $M(p)$ are $C^2$ near the old data and $S_j=-\partial_bF_j(p_0,b_j)>0$.

The actual common-mass thresholds $b_j(p)$ and mass $\tau_c(p)$ solving $F_j(p,b_j(p))=\tau_c(p)$ and $\sum_j\rho_jb_j(p)=A\cdot z$ are locally unique and $C^2$. The caps cover every continuous plane over $A\cdot w\ge A\cdot z$. At the old parameter in direction $\delta$, put $B_j=\partial_pF_j[\delta]$, $\theta_j=(\rho_j/S_j)/\sum_k(\rho_k/S_k)$, $a=\sum_j\theta_jB_j$ and $v'_j=(B_j-a)/S_j$. Then
$$
D\tau_c[\delta]=a,\qquad Db_j[\delta]=v'_j,\qquad
D^2\tau_c[\delta,\delta]=\sum_j\theta_j\bigl(\partial_{pp}F_j[\delta,\delta]+2\partial_{pb}F_j[\delta]v'_j+\partial_{bb}F_j(v'_j)^2\bigr).
$$
For $f_c=\tau_c/M$ with $f_c(p_0)=\beta$, on $\ker Df_c(p_0)$ one has $D^2 f_c[\delta,\delta]=(D^2\tau_c[\delta,\delta]-\beta D^2M[\delta,\delta])/M(p_0)$.

## Proof

At the old mark, the weighted threshold sum is $A\cdot z$ because the continuous normals balance. The implicit function theorem first inverts each threshold to a $C^2$ function $Q_j(p,\tau)$; its old mass derivative is $-1/S_j$. A second implicit inversion of $\sum_j\rho_j Q_j(p,\tau)=A\cdot z$ is valid since its mass derivative is $-\sum_j\rho_j/S_j<0$. Every cap has the asserted actual mass. The weighted sum of shifted cap margins at any $(w,x)$ is $A\cdot(w-z)$. When it is nonnegative at least one margin is nonnegative, which proves the closed cover.

Differentiate the individual mass equations on the straight chart line $p_0+t\delta$. They give $\tau_c'=B_j-S_jb_j'$. Together with $\sum_j\rho_j b_j'=0$ this yields $a$ and $v'_j$. On differentiating again, $\tau_c''$ is the displayed cap quadratic term minus $S_jb_j''$. Multiply by $\rho_j/S_j$, sum, and use $\sum_j\rho_j b_j''=0$ to obtain the Hessian. In $\tau_c=M f_c$, the cross term $2M'f_c'$ vanishes on the stated kernel. Solving for $f_c''$ gives the normalization.
