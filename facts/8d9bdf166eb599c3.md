## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $D\subset\mathbb R^{d-1}$ be a centrally symmetric convex body with center zero, and fix $0<H\le d-1$. Put $K=\operatorname{conv}(D\times\{-1\},\{(0,H)\})$. Every oblique through-zero section of $K$, meaning neither horizontal nor vertical, has strictly negative mean height with respect to Euclidean section area. The horizontal section has centroid zero. Every vertical section has centroid $(0,(H-(d-1))/d)$. Thus the horizontal section is the only through-zero section whose centroid is zero for $H<d-1$; at $H=d-1$, all vertical sections and the horizontal section have centroid zero.

## Proof

For an oblique plane write $z=-a\cdot X$ with $a\ne0$. Accepted fact 2f58c412150dafaf applies with base $D$ and apex height $H$. In its actual section chart $X=Hb/(H+1-a\cdot b)$, $a\cdot b\le1$. Its pullback section area divided by $\lVert(a,1)\rVert$ is $H^{d-1}(H+1)(H+1-a\cdot b)^{-d}\,db$. Hence the integral of $a\cdot X$ over the section has the sign of
$$
\int_{-\infty}^1 \frac{q f(q)}{(H+1-q)^{d+1}}\,dq,
$$
where $f$ is the density of the pushforward of base volume by $q=a\cdot b$. The density is even, bounded, and compactly supported, and is positive near zero. It is nonincreasing for $q\ge0$: in base dimension one it is constant on a symmetric interval, and otherwise the section at any height between $-q$ and $q$ contains a Minkowski combination of the two sections there. Those two sections have equal volume by symmetry, so Brunn–Minkowski gives the monotonicity.

For $0<R\le1$, the integral of $q/(H+1-q)^{d+1}$ from $-R$ to $R$ is strictly positive by pairing $q$ and $-q$. For $R\ge1$ the integral from $-R$ to $1$ is strictly larger than the integral from $-\infty$ to $1$, since the omitted tail is strictly negative. Substitution $v=H+1-q$ gives the latter integral as
$$
\frac{d-1-H}{d(d-1)H^d}\ge0.
$$
Every nonzero level of $f$ with a positive-length superlevel set yields one such symmetric interval, up to endpoints, before truncation to $q\le1$. Layer-cake and Fubini give strict positivity of the section integral; the signed integrand is integrable on the bounded support because its denominator is at least $H^{d+1}$. Consequently the mean $z=-a\cdot X$ is strictly negative.

The horizontal section is $[H/(H+1)]D$ at height zero. A vertical section is a $(d-1)$-dimensional pyramid over a central symmetric section of $D$ with horizontal centroid zero. Its horizontal coordinate centroid is zero and its height is the average of $H-(H+1)r$ under a density proportional to $r^{d-2}$ on $[0,1]$, giving $(H-(d-1))/d$. This also applies when $d=2$ and the vertical section is an interval.
