## Statement

Let $N$ distinct original caps give an inclusion-minimal cover of a set $S$. Suppose $s\le h$ groups together cover $S$, using only those original caps, with group sizes $k_g\le d+1$. For cap $j$ let $m_j$ be the number of groups containing it. Then
$$
\Delta=h(d+1)-N=(h-s)(d+1)+\sum_g(d+1-k_g)+\sum_j(m_j-1).
$$
For minimal positive continuous-normal circuits of ranks $r_g=k_g-1$, the middle term is $\sum_g(d-r_g)$. At most $\Delta$ original caps are shared and at most $2\Delta$ groups meet any shared cap. Among groups that are minimal positive circuits, at most $\Delta$ have deficient continuous rank. Each group has at least $\max(0,N-(s-1)(d+1))$ private caps, that is, caps occurring only in that group.

If $N>(h-1)(d+1)$, then $s=h$ and every group has at least $d+1-\Delta$ private caps, with at least $h-2\Delta$ groups meeting no shared cap. In particular when $h=2^n$ the criterion $N>hd$ implies this conclusion if $d\ge h-1$. For $n=d=2$, $h=4$, and $N=10,11,12$, the private counts are at least $1,2,3$ respectively. At $12$ the four groups are disjoint triples. A triple that is a minimal positive continuous-normal circuit has full continuous rank. At $11$ at most one cap is shared and at least two groups meet no shared cap. A strict cover with cap masses $<M/9$ of a source of total mass $M>0$ requires at least ten distinct caps; the structural conclusions still require the specified group selection.

## Proof

Inclusion-minimality implies $m_j\ge1$ for each cap, since otherwise the selected groups would yield a proper subcover. The displayed identity follows from $\sum_g k_g=\sum_jm_j$ and hence every term on the right is nonnegative. Each shared cap uses at least one unit of the repetition term. Each deficient circuit group uses at least one unit of the size term, since $k_g=r_g+1$. The number of groups meeting a shared cap is at most $\sum_{j:m_j\ge2}m_j\le2\sum_j(m_j-1)\le2\Delta$. The union of the other $s-1$ groups has at most $(s-1)(d+1)$ original caps, so every original cap outside that union is private to the given group.

If $s<h$, then $N\le s(d+1)\le(h-1)(d+1)$, contradicting the strict threshold. Substituting $s=h$ in the private-count bound gives $d+1-\Delta$. If $d\ge h-1$ then $hd\ge(h-1)(d+1)$, proving the stated sufficient criterion. For $h=4,d=2$, substitute $\Delta=12-N$. At $N=12$ the size and repetition terms both vanish. At $N=11$ the repetition term is at most one and the number of groups meeting repeats is at most two. Finally subadditivity gives $M\le\sum_j\mu(H_j)<NM/9$, so $N>9$ for strict below-target masses. This mass computation does not construct a circuit selection.
