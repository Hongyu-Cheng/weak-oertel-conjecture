## Statement

Let $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$ and $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$. Let $A,B$ be planar convex bodies and $K$ a nondegenerate triangle with $B\subseteq T$ and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Suppose $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and each line $v_i\cdot x=2v_i\cdot y-1$ is proper for $A$. Set $t_i=h_K(-v_i)$, $\tau=\sum_i t_i$, $\lambda_i(x)=(v_i\cdot x+t_i)/\tau$, and $u_i=\lambda_i(y)$. There exist $p\in\mathbb R^2$, $0<\rho<2$ with $B\subseteq-p+\rho K$ and the exact erosion $2K\ominus B=p+(2-\rho)K\supseteq A$, where $2K\ominus B=\{x:x+B\subseteq2K\}$.

Put $c_i=[v_i\cdot p+(\rho-1)t_i]/\tau$ and $d_i=(1+t_i)/\tau$. Then $c_i+d_i<2u_i$ and $\sum_i(c_i+d_i)=\rho+3/\tau$. In the $\rho$-simplex for $B$ the three original inequalities defining $T$ become exactly $S\xi\le c+d$, where $S_{ij}=\lambda_i(P_j)$ on the vertices of $K$. If the three middle caps $\lambda_i\ge u_i$ have the strict distinct-vertex incidence of the clipping polynomial theorem, the actual $B$ preimage lies in $E_\rho$ of that theorem and $|B|/|K|\le|E_\rho|/|K|$ with its exact polynomial upper bound.

## Proof

Use the true barycentric coordinates of $K$ temporarily and write them as $s_i-n_i\cdot x$, so the $n_i$ span the plane, $\sum_i n_i=0$, and $\sum_i s_i=1$. The true facet supports of $K$ in the outward normals $n_i$ are $s_i$. Put $\rho=\sum_i h_B(n_i)>0$ by evaluating strict support inequalities at an interior point of $B$ and summing. The erosion is given exactly by $n_i\cdot x\le2s_i-h_B(n_i)$ for all $i$, since these three true facets define $2K$. The erosion contains $A$ by midpoint inclusion, so an interior point of $A$ satisfies the inequalities strictly. Summing gives $2-\rho>0$. Solve $n_i\cdot p=\rho s_i-h_B(n_i)$: the right sides sum to zero and the normals span with that sole relation. The erosion now has right sides $(2-\rho)s_i+n_i\cdot p$ and is exactly $p+(2-\rho)K$. Also the three supports of $B$ are the defining right sides $\rho s_i-n_i\cdot p$ of $-p+\rho K$, which proves its enclosure.

For the separate aligned outer triangle, the positivity of $\tau$ follows by summing the strict opposite support inequalities at an interior point of $K$. Its displayed $\lambda_i$ are the barycentric coordinates of $\{x:v_i\cdot x\ge-t_i\ \forall i\}$ and $\min_K\lambda_i=0$. On $p+(2-\rho)K$, the minimum of $\lambda_i$ is $c_i$, by evaluating at $p+(2-\rho)x$ for $x\in K$. The proper cut on $A$ implies that some $A$ point has $\lambda_i(x)<2u_i-d_i$, proving $c_i+d_i<2u_i$. Summing the definitions gives $\rho+3/\tau$ by balance.

For $x=-p+\sum_j\xi_jP_j$ with $\xi_j\ge0$, $\sum_j\xi_j=\rho$, we have $v_i\cdot x=-v_i\cdot p+\tau(S\xi)_i-\rho t_i$. Thus $v_i\cdot x\le1$ is exactly $(S\xi)_i\le c_i+d_i$. These are the exact original $T$ inequalities in the enclosing simplex. The actual $B$ preimage is contained in their intersection and therefore in $E_\rho$ by the strict individual comparisons. Translation does not change area and the clipping theorem uses precisely the area normalization of this vertex map. Under its stated strict incidence and $0<\rho<2$ it gives the asserted bound.
