## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge7\), let \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) and use the standard symmetric chain partition of accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c. Equivalently, scan a word from left to right, pairing a one with the last previously unmatched zero whenever one exists. Let \(D_m\) consist of the words with an unmatched zero, let \(i_m(c)\) be the first unmatched-zero position, and put \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) for \(c\in D_m\), where \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector. Define the same block sets as in accepted facts 7538f1751c45475c and 6fee4107100a027d:
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m,\qquad
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m.
$$
For every \(\gamma\in\mathbb R^m\), let
\(p=p_\gamma\) be the positive probability law on \(T_m\) given by
\(p_x=e^{\gamma\cdot x}/Z_{T_m}(\gamma)\), where \(Z_{T_m}(\gamma)=\sum_{z\in T_m}e^{\gamma\cdot z}\), extended by zero off \(T_m\).
Use precisely the oriented reflected quantities of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc:
$$
H_p(x)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^m}\sqrt{p_{x+v}p_{x-v}},\qquad
b(p)=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_xH_p(x)^2,\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_xH_p(x).
$$
Then, for all these \(m\) and \(\gamma\),
$$
4^m b(p_\gamma)\ge1+\frac1{20736}.
$$

## Proof

Fix \(m\ge7\) and \(\gamma\), and set \(N=2^m\). All matching statements used below concern the prescribed standard matching, not an arbitrary symmetric chain partition.

First identify the exact reflection multiplicities needed in the argument. Accepted fact 6fee4107100a027d gives \(T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\), injectivity of \(F_m\), and the following parity description. A spike \(c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) has parity \(c\); different spikes have different parities; and the only possible distinct same-parity pair in \(B_m\cup E_m\) consists of that spike and the Boolean word \(c\). Consequently the distinct same-parity pairs in \(T_m\) are exactly
\(\lbrace c,c+2e_{i_m(c)}\rbrace\) with \(c\in C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\).
Their midpoints \(F_m(c)\) belong to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) by convexity. Define
\(J_m=F_m(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m)\).
Injectivity of \(F_m\) shows that each center in \(J_m\) has exactly one unordered distinct reflected pair. Thus, with the zero displacement counted once and each nonzero pair counted in both orientations,
$$
r_{T_m}(x)=
\begin{cases}
3,&x\in J_m,\\
1,&x\in T_m\setminus J_m.
\end{cases}
$$
In particular every spike has multiplicity one. Log-affinity gives
\(\sqrt{p_{x+v}p_{x-v}}=p_x\) for any supported reflected pair centered at \(x\), so \(H_p(x)=r_{T_m}(x)p_x\).

The matching proof in accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c shows that the unmatched-symbol word has the form \(1^k0^\ell\), that the intervening matched words remain internally matched when unmatched symbols are changed, and that every word with at least three unmatched ones belongs to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). It follows that every word with at least four unmatched ones belongs to \(J_m\). Indeed, change its last unmatched one to zero. The resulting unmatched-symbol word is \(1^{k-1}0^{\ell+1}\), with \(k-1\ge3\). The resulting word lies in \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\cap D_m\), its first unmatched zero is the changed position, and applying \(F_m\) recovers the original word.

We now construct a family \(\mathcal G_m\) of exactly \(N/128\) Boolean targets. Start with any word of length \(m-2\) beginning with five ones; there are exactly \(2^{m-7}\) such words. It has at least five unmatched ones. Immediately before its last unmatched one, insert two additional ones. At that position the matching stack is empty, so the inserted ones are unmatched and leave every old match unchanged. The resulting word \(y\) has at least seven unmatched ones, and its last three unmatched ones occupy consecutive positions \(j_1,j_2,j_3\). This insertion is injective: from the result, find its last unmatched one and delete the two immediately preceding positions. Define \(\mathcal G_m\) to be the image. Hence \(|\mathcal G_m|=2^{m-7}=N/128\), and \(\mathcal G_m\subseteq J_m\).

For each \(y\in\mathcal G_m\), write its unmatched-symbol word as \(1^k0^\ell\), where \(k\ge7\). Use the four-spike certificate from accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c at the last three unmatched-one positions. Explicitly, keep all other coordinates equal to those of \(y\), and replace its entries at \(j_1,j_2,j_3\) by the four source patterns \(000,100,110,010\). Call these sources \(c^{(1)}(y),\ldots,c^{(4)}(y)\), and let \(z^{(h)}(y)=c^{(h)}(y)+2e_{i_m(c^{(h)}(y))}\).
The first unmatched-zero positions in the four sources are respectively \(j_1,j_2,j_3,j_3\), and the four spike patterns are respectively
$$
200,\qquad120,\qquad112,\qquad012.
$$
The corresponding source unmatched-symbol words are
\(1^{k-3}0^{\ell+3}\),
\(1^{k-2}0^{\ell+2}\),
\(1^{k-1}0^{\ell+1}\), and
\(1^{k-3}0^{\ell+1}\).
For the fourth word the \(01\) at \(j_1,j_2\) is matched internally. Thus all four sources are in \(D_m\), and each has at least four unmatched ones. By the preceding paragraph, all four sources belong to \(J_m\). Their spike witnesses satisfy
$$
\frac14\sum_{h=1}^4z^{(h)}(y)=y,\qquad
p_y^4=\prod_{h=1}^4p_{z^{(h)}(y)}.
$$
The probability identity follows directly by exponentiating the displayed affine identity and using the same normalizer on all five points.

Each of the four maps \(y\mapsto z^{(h)}(y)\) is injective on \(\mathcal G_m\). Here are exact recovery procedures. A spike identifies its selected position by its unique coordinate equal to two, and changing that entry to zero recovers its source. For the first map, change the first three unmatched zeros of the source to ones; these are precisely \(j_1,j_2,j_3\). For the second map, change the first two unmatched zeros to ones; these are \(j_2,j_3\). For the third map, change the first unmatched zero to one; it is \(j_3\). For the fourth map, let \(i\) be the position of the spike's two. Since \(j_1,j_2,j_3\) are consecutive and \(i=j_3\), replace the entries at \(i-2,i-1,i\) by \(111\). Each procedure recovers \(y\), proving the four injectivities. The fourth procedure is why consecutiveness was imposed.

The smallest dimension is included: at \(m=7\), the initial word is \(11111\), the target is \(1111111\), and its four witnesses are \(1111200,1111120,1111112,1111012\). Their source unmatched-one counts are respectively \(4,5,6,4\). In particular no recovery procedure requests a nonexistent coordinate at the boundary.

It remains to turn this packing into a uniform weighted gain. For \(u\in B_m\), let \(q_u\) be the total \(p\)-mass in parity class \(u+2\mathbb Z^m\). Accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc applies because \(T_m\) is a complete convex lattice support and \(p\) is positive and log-affine; it gives \(\chi(p)=\sum_{u\in B_m}q_u^2\). Define two nonnegative quantities
$$
\mathcal D=\frac1N\sum_{u\in B_m}(Nq_u-1)^2=N\chi(p)-1,\qquad
\mathcal V=\sum_{x\in T_m}p_x(NH_p(x)-1)^2.
$$
Expanding the second square gives the exact identity
$$
N^2b(p)-1=\mathcal V+2\mathcal D.
$$

Let \(E_m^{<}\) consist of the spikes \(z=c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) whose Boolean sources \(c\) lie in \(J_m\) and which satisfy \(Np_z<4/9\). We claim
$$
\frac{|E_m^{<}|}{N}\le81\mathcal D+\frac{81}{4}\mathcal V.
$$
To prove this, first count the spikes whose source parity satisfies \(Nq_c<8/9\). Each such parity contributes more than \(1/(81N)\) to \(\mathcal D\), and spikes have distinct source parities. There are therefore at most \(81N\mathcal D\) of these spikes. For every remaining spike in \(E_m^{<}\), the exact parity description above gives \(q_c=p_c+p_z\). Thus \(Np_c>4/9\). Its source lies in \(J_m\), so \(H_p(c)=3p_c\), and its contribution to \(\mathcal V\) is bounded below by
$$
p_c(3Np_c-1)^2\ge\frac{4}{9N}\left(\frac13\right)^2=\frac4{81N}.
$$
Different spikes have distinct sources, so at most \((81/4)N\mathcal V\) spikes fall in this remaining case. This proves the claim.

For each fixed \(h\), injectivity of the witness map shows that at most \(|E_m^{<}|\) targets \(y\in\mathcal G_m\) have \(z^{(h)}(y)\in E_m^{<}\). All witness sources are in \(J_m\), so at least \(|\mathcal G_m|-4|E_m^{<}|\) targets have all four witness probabilities at least \(4/(9N)\). For each such target, log-affinity yields \(p_y\ge4/(9N)\). Since \(y\in J_m\), its contribution to \(\mathcal V\) is at least \(4/(81N)\), by the same calculation as for the sources. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V
&\ge\frac4{81}\left(\frac{|\mathcal G_m|}{N}-\frac{4|E_m^{<}|}{N}\right)\\
&\ge\frac4{81}\left(\frac1{128}-4\left(81\mathcal D+\frac{81}{4}\mathcal V\right)\right)\\
&=\frac1{2592}-16\mathcal D-4\mathcal V.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first inequality remains valid when its right-hand side is negative, since \(\mathcal V\ge0\). Rearrangement gives \(5\mathcal V+16\mathcal D\ge1/2592\). Finally,
$$
8\bigl(N^2b(p)-1\bigr)
=8\mathcal V+16\mathcal D
\ge5\mathcal V+16\mathcal D
\ge\frac1{2592}.
$$
Since \(N^2=4^m\), this is the claimed bound with \(\delta=1/20736\). Only the certified Boolean inclusion and explicit four-spike witnesses were used; no description exhausting \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\) was assumed.
