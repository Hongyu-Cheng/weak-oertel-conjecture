## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 A_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
\]
Assume
\[
 W:=\sum_z A_z>0,
 \qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:A_z>0\},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
For \(z\in Z\), let
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{A_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{A_z/2}(C_z),
 \qquad
 D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\tag{1}
\]
The accepted facts imply that every \(D_z\) is an origin-symmetric convex body with \(0\) in its interior.
Here, for a positive-volume bounded measurable set \(K\) and
\(0<m\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1m\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ {\rm a.e.},\quad \int_Kf=m
 \right\}.
\]
For a convex body \(D\) containing \(0\) in its interior, write
\[
 h_D(u):=\max_{x\in D}\langle u,x\rangle,
 \qquad
 \|x\|_D:=\inf\{r>0:x\in rD\}.
\]

Let \(q=(q_e)\) be a nonzero off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching. Thus its positive support \(F:=\{e:q_e>0\}\) consists of unordered pairs \(e=\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z\) with \(z\ne z'\) and \(z\equiv z'\pmod2\), and assume the strong degree bounds
\[
 d_z(q):=\sum_{e\ni z}q_e\leq\gamma_d A_z
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{z+z'}2,
 \qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_z+c_{z'}}2,
 \qquad
 D_e:=\frac12(D_z+D_{z'}),
 \qquad
 L_e:=\bar c_e+D_e
       =\frac12(\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'}).
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 \Delta_C(q):=
 \min_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{e\in F}q_e
 \bigl(\|s(m_e)-\bar c_e\|_{D_e}-1\bigr)_+.
\tag{4}
\]

Choose the load-minimal dual optimizer and its support contacts supplied by accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d. Write its vectors as \(v_e\in\mathbb R^d\) and set
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 \Omega:=\sum_{e\in F}\omega_e.
\tag{5}
\]
Assume the positive-load case \(\Omega>0\). The accepted dual conclusion gives
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq q_e,
 \qquad
 \sum_ev_e=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_ev_em_e^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad
 \Delta_C(q)=-\sum_eh_{L_e}(v_e).
\tag{6}
\]
Let
\[
 F_\omega:=\{e\in F:\omega_e>0\}.
\]
For each \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_\omega\), choose
\[
 \ell_e=\frac{x_{e,z}+x_{e,z'}}2,
 \qquad
 x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z,
 \qquad
 x_{e,z'}\in\mathcal K_{z'},
\tag{7}
\]
such that
\[
 h_{L_e}(v_e)=\langle v_e,\ell_e\rangle.
\tag{8}
\]
No normalized incidence direction and no endpoint contact is assigned to an edge with \(\omega_e=0\).

For \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_\omega\), define
\[
 \lambda_{e,z}:=\frac12h_{D_z}(v_e),
 \qquad
 u_{e,z}:=\frac{v_e}{h_{D_z}(v_e)}.
\tag{9}
\]
These quantities are well-defined and satisfy
\[
 \lambda_{e,z}>0,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{e,z}+\lambda_{e,z'}=\omega_e,
 \qquad
 h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})=1,
 \qquad
 \langle u_{e,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle=1.
\tag{10}
\]
For each loaded endpoint, put
\[
 t_z:=\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}\lambda_{e,z},
 \qquad
 \widetilde p_z:=
 \frac1{t_z}\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}
 \lambda_{e,z}x_{e,z}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_z t_z=\Omega,
 \qquad
 0<t_z\leq\gamma_dA_z\leq\frac{A_z}{2},
\tag{12}
\]
and the pooled selector satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \widetilde p_z\in
 \mathcal K_z\cap\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
 \qquad
 \widetilde p_z+\frac15D_z
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{A_z/4}(C_z).}
\tag{13}
\]

Define
\[
 U_z:=\frac12\sum_{e\ni z}v_e
      =\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}
       \lambda_{e,z}u_{e,z},
 \qquad
 \tau_z:=h_{D_z}(U_z),
 \qquad
 T:=\sum_z\tau_z.
\tag{14}
\]
At an unloaded endpoint, both \(t_z\) and \(U_z\) are zero. One has
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_zh_{\mathcal K_z}(U_z)
 =-\Delta_C(q)-\Omega+T.}
\tag{15}
\]

For \(t_z>0\), define
\[
 \mathcal I_z:=
 t_z-\langle U_z,\widetilde p_z-c_z\rangle,
\tag{16}
\]
and, for distinct loaded edges \(e,f\ni z\), define
\[
 g_{z;e,f}:=
 \langle u_{e,z}-u_{f,z},x_{e,z}-x_{f,z}\rangle.
\tag{17}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal I_z=
 \frac1{t_z}
 \sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\subseteq F_\omega\\e\ne f,\ e\ni z,\ f\ni z}}
 \lambda_{e,z}\lambda_{f,z}g_{z;e,f},}
\tag{18}
\]
where each unordered pair is counted once, and
\[
 0\leq g_{z;e,f}\leq4,
 \qquad
 t_z-\tau_z\leq\mathcal I_z\leq t_z+\tau_z.
\tag{19}
\]
Put
\[
 \mathcal I:=\sum_{z:t_z>0}\mathcal I_z.
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal I\geq\Omega-T,}
\tag{21}
\]
and the strengthened affine-defect inequality is
\[
 \boxed{
 \inf_{s:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{z:t_z>0}t_z
 \|\widetilde p_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}
 \geq\Delta_C(q)+\mathcal I.}
\tag{22}
\]
Equivalently, because \(\Omega>0\), the left side divided by \(\Omega\) is at least
\[
 \frac{\Delta_C(q)}{\Omega}
 +\frac{\mathcal I}{\Omega}.
\tag{23}
\]

Form the line graph on \(F_\omega\): two distinct stress edges are adjacent when they share a base endpoint. Choose nonnegative weights
\[
 \vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\geq0
 \qquad(e\ne f,\ e\cap f\ne\varnothing)
\]
maximizing
\[
 P:=\sum_{\{e,f\}}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}
\tag{24}
\]
subject to
\[
 \sum_{f:f\sim e}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\leq\omega_e
 \qquad(e\in F_\omega).
\tag{25}
\]
Set
\[
 c_e:=\sum_{f:f\sim e}\vartheta_{\{e,f\}},
 \qquad
 r_e:=\omega_e-c_e.
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
 r_e>0,\ r_f>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e\cap f=\varnothing,
\tag{27}
\]
and the exact interaction-to-pairing bound is
\[
 \boxed{P\geq\frac{\mathcal I}{4}.}
\tag{28}
\]

Every adjacent pair of distinct base edges has a unique common endpoint. If
\[
 e=\{z,a\},
 \qquad
 f=\{z,b\},
\]
define
\[
 e\star_z f:=\{a,b\}.
\tag{29}
\]
This is an off-diagonal same-parity edge. Define, with all equal resulting edges aggregated,
\[
 q^{\rm sq}_g:=
 \sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}:\ e\star_zf=g}}
 \vartheta_{\{e,f\}},
 \qquad
 q^{\rm dis}_g:=
 \begin{cases}
 r_g,&g\in F_\omega,\\
 0,&g\notin F_\omega,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 q^\sharp:=q^{\rm sq}+q^{\rm dis}.
\tag{30}
\]
This definition also aggregates a residual contribution with squared contributions when they produce the same edge. The residual support in \(q^{\rm dis}\) is endpoint-disjoint. The new matching is strong and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 |q^{\rm sq}|=P,
 \qquad
 |q^{\rm dis}|=\Omega-2P,
 \qquad
 |q^\sharp|=\Omega-P\geq\frac{\Omega}{2},}
\tag{31}
\]
and, pointwise,
\[
 \boxed{
 d_z(q^\sharp)
 \leq\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}\omega_e
 \leq d_z(q)
 \leq\gamma_dA_z.}
\tag{32}
\]

For every positive edge \(g=\{a,b\}\) of \(q^\sharp\), there are points
\[
 y_{g,a}\in
 \mathcal K_a\cap\mathsf M_{q^\sharp_g}(C_a),
 \qquad
 y_{g,b}\in
 \mathcal K_b\cap\mathsf M_{q^\sharp_g}(C_b)
\tag{33}
\]
such that
\[
 \boxed{
 y_g:=\frac{y_{g,a}+y_{g,b}}2
 \in\mathsf M_{q^\sharp_g}
 \left(C_{(a+b)/2}\right).}
\tag{34}
\]
Let \(\mu_C\) denote mixed-volume measure:
\[
 \mu_C(B):=
 \sum_{z\in Z}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\}
\]
for Borel \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and define
\[
 \delta_C(z,x):=
 \inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\ {\rm proper\ closed\ halfspace}\\
                 x\in J}}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J),
 \qquad
 h_{\max}(C):=
 \sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\ {\rm closed\ halfspace}\\p\in H}}\mu_C(H).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma^\sharp:=
 \frac12\sum_{g=\{a,b\}}q^\sharp_g
 \delta_{((a+b)/2,y_g)}}
\tag{35}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and is
halfspace-dominated by \(\mu_C\): for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\),
\[
 \sigma^\sharp(H)\leq\mu_C(H).
\tag{36}
\]
Its mass satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 |\sigma^\sharp|=\frac{|q^\sharp|}{2}
 \geq\frac{\Omega}{4}.}
\tag{37}
\]

Finally, consider one positive squared contribution
\[
 e=\{z,a\},
 \qquad
 f=\{z,b\},
 \qquad
 \theta:=\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}>0,
\tag{38}
\]
and put
\[
 h_e:=\frac{a-z}{2},
 \qquad
 h_f:=\frac{b-z}{2}.
\tag{39}
\]
Only if \(h_e,h_f\) are linearly independent, define the rank-two integral chart
\[
 \Phi(s,t):=z+sh_e+th_f,
 \qquad(s,t)\in[0,1]^2.
\tag{40}
\]
Its four corner bases are
\[
 z,
 \qquad
 \frac{z+a}{2},
 \qquad
 \frac{z+b}{2},
 \qquad
 \frac{a+b}{2},
\tag{41}
\]
and the four points
\[
 X_{00}:=x_{e,z},
 \qquad
 X_{10}:=\frac{x_{e,z}+x_{e,a}}2,
 \qquad
 X_{01}:=\frac{x_{f,z}+x_{f,b}}2,
 \qquad
 X_{11}:=\frac{x_{e,a}+x_{f,b}}2
\tag{42}
\]
satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 X_{ij}\in\mathsf M_\theta(C_{\Phi(i,j)})
 \qquad(i,j\in\{0,1\}).}
\tag{43}
\]
Every corner therefore has absolute fiber depth at least \(\gamma_d\theta\). Taking both rank-one vertex arrays to be constant with value \((\gamma_d\theta)^{1/(2d)}\), their factor energies are each \(2(\gamma_d\theta)^{1/2}\), so the local packet energy is
\[
 \boxed{E=4\gamma_d\theta.}
\tag{44}
\]
Accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq2^{-2}E=\gamma_d\theta.
\tag{45}
\]
The mixed second difference is
\[
 \boxed{
 X_{00}-X_{10}-X_{01}+X_{11}
 =\frac12(x_{e,z}-x_{f,z}).}
\tag{46}
\]
Thus this rank-two germ is nonseparable when the two common-fiber contacts differ. Even when \(P\) is macroscopic, the paired directions may be collinear, so (44) supplies no macroscopic rank-two packet reservoir without an additional directional extraction theorem.

## Proof

First note that
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}\leq\frac12,
\tag{47}
\]
by the binomial inequality \((1+1/d)^d\geq2\). Hence (2) is stronger than the half-mass degree hypothesis in accepted fact 6496cb3b0fd44f9d.

We first remove the zero-load ambiguity. Because \(D_e\) is an origin-symmetric convex body, \(h_{D_e}\) is a norm on the dual space. Thus \(\omega_e=0\) implies \(v_e=0\). Such an edge contributes nothing to any formula below and is omitted. If \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_\omega\), then \(v_e\ne0\). Since \(0\in\operatorname{int}D_z\cap\operatorname{int}D_{z'}\),
\[
 h_{D_z}(v_e)>0,
 \qquad
 h_{D_{z'}}(v_e)>0.
\tag{48}
\]
This proves that every division in (9) occurs only at a strictly positive endpoint support value.

The support function of a Minkowski average gives
\[
 h_{D_e}(v_e)
 =\frac12\bigl(h_{D_z}(v_e)+h_{D_{z'}}(v_e)\bigr),
\tag{49}
\]
which proves the first three assertions in (10). Moreover,
\[
 h_{L_e}(v_e)
 =\frac12\bigl(h_{\mathcal K_z}(v_e)
                 +h_{\mathcal K_{z'}}(v_e)\bigr).
\]
Equations (7)-(8) express the left side as the average of
\(\langle v_e,x_{e,z}\rangle\) and
\(\langle v_e,x_{e,z'}\rangle\). Each term is at most its corresponding support value, so equality of their averages forces equality separately:
\[
 \langle v_e,x_{e,z}\rangle=h_{\mathcal K_z}(v_e),
 \qquad
 \langle v_e,x_{e,z'}\rangle=h_{\mathcal K_{z'}}(v_e).
\tag{50}
\]
Since \(\mathcal K_z=c_z+D_z\), dividing (50) by the positive value in (48) proves the last assertion in (10).

Summing (49) over the loaded edges proves
\[
 \sum_z t_z=\sum_{e\in F_\omega}\omega_e=\Omega.
\]
Also
\[
 t_z
 \leq\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}\omega_e
 \leq\sum_{e\ni z}q_e
 =d_z(q)
 \leq\gamma_dA_z,
\tag{51}
\]
which proves (12).

We now verify the pooled incidence density. For every loaded incidence \((e,z)\), choose a half-mass witness \(f_{e,z}\) for \(x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z\):
\[
 0\leq f_{e,z}\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_z}f_{e,z}=\frac{A_z}{2},
 \qquad
 \frac2{A_z}\int_{C_z}x f_{e,z}(x)\,dx=x_{e,z}.
\tag{52}
\]
Set
\[
 G_z:=\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni z}}
 \frac{2\lambda_{e,z}}{A_z}f_{e,z}.
\tag{53}
\]
This is one pooled density, not a family whose capacities are added afterward. Pointwise, using (12) and (47),
\[
 0\leq G_z
 \leq\frac{2t_z}{A_z}
 \leq2\gamma_d
 \leq1.
\tag{54}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 \int_{C_z}G_z=t_z,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_z}xG_z(x)\,dx
 =\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}x_{e,z}
 =t_z\widetilde p_z.
\tag{55}
\]
Thus \(\widetilde p_z\in\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)\). It lies in \(\mathcal K_z\) by convexity, because (11) is a convex combination of points of \(\mathcal K_z\). Hence \(\widetilde p_z-c_z\in D_z\), and
\[
 \widetilde p_z+\frac15D_z
 \subseteq c_z+\frac65D_z
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{A_z/4}(C_z),
\tag{56}
\]
where the last inclusion is accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b. This proves (13).

From (6), (49), and \(\mathcal K_z=c_z+D_z\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 -\Delta_C(q)
 &=\sum_eh_{L_e}(v_e)\\
 &=\sum_z\langle U_z,c_z\rangle+\Omega,
\end{aligned}
\tag{57}
\]
and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_zh_{\mathcal K_z}(U_z)
 &=\sum_z\bigl(\langle U_z,c_z\rangle+h_{D_z}(U_z)\bigr)\\
 &=-\Delta_C(q)-\Omega+T.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the exact identity (15).

Subadditivity of support functions and (10) give
\[
 \tau_z=h_{D_z}(U_z)
 \leq\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})
 =t_z.
\tag{58}
\]
For a loaded \(z\), use (10) to write
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal I_z
 &=\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}
   \langle u_{e,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle
   -\left\langle
      \sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}u_{e,z},
      \frac1{t_z}\sum_{f\ni z}
      \lambda_{f,z}(x_{f,z}-c_z)
    \right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]
The weighted covariance identity turns (59) into (18).

For a pair \(e,f\ni z\), equation (10) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_{z;e,f}
 &=\bigl(1-\langle u_{e,z},x_{f,z}-c_z\rangle\bigr)
  +\bigl(1-\langle u_{f,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{60}
\]
Both brackets are nonnegative because \(x_{f,z}-c_z\in D_z\) and
\(h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})=1\), and similarly after interchanging \(e,f\).
Origin symmetry gives every cross pairing at least \(-1\), so each bracket is at most \(2\). This proves \(0\leq g_{z;e,f}\leq4\). Also
\[
 -\tau_z
 \leq\langle U_z,\widetilde p_z-c_z\rangle
 \leq\tau_z
\tag{61}
\]
because \(\widetilde p_z-c_z\in D_z\). Equations (16) and (61) prove the second part of (19). Summing its lower bound and using (12) proves (21).

The two dual moment cancellations in (6) imply
\[
 \sum_zU_z=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{62}
\]
Equations (16), (57), and \(\sum_zt_z=\Omega\) give
\[
 \sum_{z:t_z>0}\langle U_z,\widetilde p_z\rangle
 =-\Delta_C(q)-\mathcal I.
\tag{63}
\]
For every affine \(s\), equation (62) gives
\(\sum_z\langle U_z,s(z)\rangle=0\). Hence
\[
 \sum_{z:t_z>0}\langle-U_z,\widetilde p_z-s(z)\rangle
 =\Delta_C(q)+\mathcal I.
\tag{64}
\]
Since \(D_z\) is origin-symmetric, polar Hölder and (58) give
\[
 \langle-U_z,\widetilde p_z-s(z)\rangle
 \leq\tau_z\|\widetilde p_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}
 \leq t_z\|\widetilde p_z-s(z)\|_{D_z}.
\tag{65}
\]
Summing proves (22), and division by \(\Omega>0\) proves (23).

The line-graph linear program is finite, so an optimizer exists. If two adjacent edges had \(r_e,r_f>0\), increasing
\(\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\) by a sufficiently small positive amount would preserve (25) and increase \(P\), a contradiction. This proves (27). Moreover,
\[
 \sum_ec_e=2P,
 \qquad
 \sum_er_e=\Omega-2P,
 \qquad
 2P\leq\Omega.
\tag{66}
\]

We next prove (28), including all capacity bookkeeping. Distinct base edges share at most one endpoint. For every loaded \(z\) and every unordered pair of distinct loaded edges \(e,f\ni z\), define
\[
 \vartheta^{\rm can}_{\{e,f\}}
 :=\frac{\lambda_{e,z}\lambda_{f,z}}{t_z}.
\tag{67}
\]
There is no division by zero: a loaded incidence has \(\lambda_{e,z}>0\), hence \(t_z>0\). For a fixed loaded edge \(e=\{z,z'\}\), the total canonical capacity used is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{f:f\sim e}\vartheta^{\rm can}_{\{e,f\}}
 &=\sum_{w\in\{z,z'\}}
   \frac{\lambda_{e,w}}{t_w}
   \bigl(t_w-\lambda_{e,w}\bigr)\\
 &\leq\lambda_{e,z}+\lambda_{e,z'}
 =\omega_e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{68}
\]
Thus (67) is feasible for (25). Equations (18)-(19) yield
\[
 \mathcal I
 =\sum_z
   \sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\subseteq F_\omega\\
                   e\ne f,\ e\ni z,\ f\ni z}}
   \vartheta^{\rm can}_{\{e,f\}}g_{z;e,f}
 \leq4\sum_{\{e,f\}}\vartheta^{\rm can}_{\{e,f\}}
 \leq4P,
\tag{69}
\]
which proves \(P\geq\mathcal I/4\).

For a paired pair \(e=\{z,a\}\), \(f=\{z,b\}\), distinctness gives \(a\ne b\), and same parity gives \(a\equiv b\pmod2\). Thus (29) is an eligible off-diagonal edge. Aggregation preserves total mass, so (66) proves the first three equalities in (31), while \(P\leq\Omega/2\) proves its lower bound.

We verify degrees before constructing endpoint witnesses. Fix a base point \(a\). A squared contribution is incident with \(a\) only when exactly one of its two source edges, say \(e\), is incident with \(a\) and the paired edges share the other endpoint of \(e\). Its mass is then charged to the capacity \(c_e\). If a pair shares the endpoint \(a\), its opposite edge does not contain \(a\). Consequently,
\[
 d_a(q^{\rm sq})
 \leq\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni a}}c_e.
\tag{70}
\]
The residual contributions give
\[
 d_a(q^{\rm dis})
 =\sum_{\substack{e\in F_\omega\\e\ni a}}r_e.
\tag{71}
\]
Equal resulting edges, including coincidences between (70) and (71), are merely aggregated and do not alter degree. Adding (70)-(71) gives
\[
 d_a(q^\sharp)
 \leq\sum_{e\ni a}(c_e+r_e)
 =\sum_{e\ni a}\omega_e
 \leq d_a(q),
\]
which proves (32).

We now realize every aggregated edge by pooled endpoint densities. Regard \(q^\sharp\) as the aggregate of elementary contributions. A squared contribution of mass \(\theta=\vartheta_{\{e,f\}}\), with \(e=\{z,a\}\) and \(f=\{z,b\}\), contributes the endpoint contacts
\[
 x_{e,a}\in\mathcal K_a,
 \qquad
 x_{f,b}\in\mathcal K_b
\tag{72}
\]
to the resulting edge \(\{a,b\}\). A residual contribution of mass \(r_e\) contributes the two contacts of \(e\). Every elementary contribution mass \(\eta\) assigned to an endpoint \(a\) obeys
\[
 0<\eta\leq\omega_e\leq q_e
 \leq d_a(q)\leq\gamma_dA_a\leq\frac{A_a}{2}
\tag{73}
\]
for its corresponding source edge \(e\ni a\).

Fix a positive aggregated edge \(g=\{a,b\}\). Index all its elementary contributions by \(\alpha\), write their masses as \(\eta_\alpha>0\), and write their assigned endpoint contacts as
\(\xi_{\alpha,a}\in\mathcal K_a\) and
\(\xi_{\alpha,b}\in\mathcal K_b\). Thus
\[
 \sum_\alpha\eta_\alpha=q^\sharp_g.
\tag{74}
\]
For each endpoint contact choose a half-mass witness
\(f_{\alpha,a}\) as in (52), and define
\[
 F_{g,a}:=\sum_\alpha
 \frac{2\eta_\alpha}{A_a}f_{\alpha,a},
 \qquad
 y_{g,a}:=\frac1{q^\sharp_g}
 \sum_\alpha\eta_\alpha\xi_{\alpha,a}.
\tag{75}
\]
This pools all sources producing \(g\), rather than selecting one source or assuming resulting edges are distinct. By (32) and (47),
\[
 0\leq F_{g,a}
 \leq\frac{2q^\sharp_g}{A_a}
 \leq\frac{2d_a(q^\sharp)}{A_a}
 \leq2\gamma_d\leq1.
\tag{76}
\]
Also
\[
 \int_{C_a}F_{g,a}=q^\sharp_g,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_a}xF_{g,a}(x)\,dx=q^\sharp_gy_{g,a}.
\tag{77}
\]
Thus \(y_{g,a}\in\mathsf M_{q^\sharp_g}(C_a)\), and convexity of \(\mathcal K_a\) gives \(y_{g,a}\in\mathcal K_a\). The same construction at \(b\) proves (33).

Set \(s_g:=(q^\sharp_g)^{1/d}>0\). The positive-root interpolation proved in accepted fact dfd25e72c1f45a4e applies to
\[
 (a,y_{g,a},s_g),
 \qquad
 (b,y_{g,b},s_g).
\]
The proof of that accepted fact places the midpoint directly in its positive-root generating set, not merely in its terminal closure. Because the two roots are equal and positive, it gives
\[
 \left(\frac{a+b}{2},
       \frac{y_{g,a}+y_{g,b}}2,
       s_g\right)
 \quad\text{with}\quad
 y_g\in\mathsf M_{s_g^d}(C_{(a+b)/2})
 =\mathsf M_{q^\sharp_g}(C_{(a+b)/2}).
\tag{78}
\]
The target base is integral because \(a\equiv b\pmod2\), and a metronoid
point belongs to its convex fiber. Thus (78) proves (34) and shows that
every atom in (35) is carried by the mixed-integer body.

It remains to prove halfspace domination without adding fiber-depth lower bounds. Let \(H\) be a closed ambient halfspace. For every atom of \(\sigma^\sharp\) lying in \(H\), the defining affine functional at its midpoint is the average of its values at
\((a,y_{g,a})\) and \((b,y_{g,b})\). Hence at least one endpoint lies in \(H\), including in the boundary case. Orient the atom toward one such endpoint. Let
\[
 R_a:=\frac12
 \sum_{\substack{g=\{a,b\},\ ((a+b)/2,y_g)\in H\\
                         g\ {\rm oriented\ toward}\ a}}
 q^\sharp_g.
\tag{79}
\]
Then
\[
 R_a\leq\frac12d_a(q^\sharp)
 \leq\frac{\gamma_dA_a}{2}.
\tag{80}
\]
If \(R_a=0\), no mass must be paid for in that fiber. If \(R_a>0\), the fiber section \(H_a\) contains at least one endpoint point
\(y_{g,a}\in\mathcal K_a=\mathsf M_{A_a/2}(C_a)\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_a\cap H_a)
 \geq\frac{\gamma_dA_a}{2}
 \geq R_a.
\tag{81}
\]
For a vertical halfspace, a nonempty oriented section is the whole fiber and the same inequality holds. Crucially, (81) is invoked once in fiber \(a\); the depths of several endpoint points are not added. Summing (81) once over the fibers gives
\[
 \mu_C(H)
 \geq\sum_aR_a
 =\sigma^\sharp(H).
\]
This proves (36), and (31) gives (37).
