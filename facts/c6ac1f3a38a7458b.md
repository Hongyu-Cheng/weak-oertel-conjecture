## Statement

Let \(0<\alpha _0\leq1\), and set
\[
 \varepsilon^2:=\frac{\alpha _0}{36}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
 \Delta:=\{(y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb R^2:y_1,y_2\geq0,\ y_1+y_2\leq1\},
 \qquad Q:=[0,1]^2,
\]
and put \(V:=\operatorname{vol}_4(\Delta\times Q)=1/2\).  Define
\[
 B_\varepsilon:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
 \{0\}\times\varepsilon\Delta,\
 \{1\}\times\Delta,\
 \{2\}\times\varepsilon\Delta
 \bigr)\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2
\]
and, with \((s,u_1,u_2)\) as the integer coordinates and
\((y_1,y_2,q_1,q_2)\) as the continuous coordinates, define
\[
 C_{\alpha _0}:=
 \left\{
 ((s,u),(y,q)):
 (s,y)\in B_\varepsilon,\ u\in[0,1]^2,\ q\in Q
 \right\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^4.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a compact full-dimensional convex body.  Its positive-volume
lattice fibers are indexed by
\[
 D:=\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,1\}^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Writing \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_4((C_{\alpha _0})_z)\), one has, for
every \(v\in\{0,1\}^2\),
\[
 (C_{\alpha _0})_{(1,v)}=\Delta\times Q,
 \qquad
 (C_{\alpha _0})_{(0,v)}=(C_{\alpha _0})_{(2,v)}
 =\varepsilon\Delta\times Q,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 w_{(1,v)}=V,
 \qquad
 w_{(0,v)}=w_{(2,v)}=\varepsilon^2V,
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_z w_z=4(1+2\varepsilon^2)V.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(\mu\) be the mixed-volume measure of \(C_{\alpha _0}\), let
\(\mathsf A\mu\) be the full parity-autocorrelation measure of accepted
fact `aca624dd8874e5a5`, and put
\[
 \kappa_z:=\mathsf A\mu(\{z\}\times\mathbb R^4).
\]
Then the positive profile support is exactly \(D\), and
\[
 \kappa_{(0,v)}=\kappa_{(2,v)}=\frac{\varepsilon^2V}{16},
 \qquad
 \kappa_{(1,v)}=\frac{(1+\varepsilon^2)V}{8}.
\tag{6}
\]
For every \(\alpha\in[\alpha _0,1]\), set
\[
 t_z^{(\alpha)}:=\alpha\kappa_z\qquad(z\in D).
\tag{7}
\]
Then
\[
 0<t_z^{(\alpha)}<\frac14w_z\qquad(z\in D),
 \qquad
 \sum_{z\in D}t_z^{(\alpha)}=\alpha\,2^{-3}W.
\tag{8}
\]
The complete set of weighted lattice centerpoints of this profile, at
the rank-three threshold \(2^{-3}\sum_z t_z^{(\alpha)}\), is
\[
 \operatorname{Cent}_{\mathbb Z^3}(t^{(\alpha)})
 =\{(1,v):v\in\{0,1\}^2\}.
\tag{9}
\]
At every one of these four points \(m\), the barycentric metronoid core
from accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672` is empty:
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_{t^{(\alpha)}})
 :=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(D)}
 \sum_{z\in D}\lambda_z
 \mathsf M_{t_z^{(\alpha)}}((C_{\alpha _0})_z)
 =\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(\alpha _0>1\), the interval \([\alpha _0,1]\) is empty, so the
corresponding assertion for a fixed positive lower scale is vacuous.

## Proof

The set \(B_\varepsilon\) is the convex hull of compact convex sets and is
therefore compact and convex.  It contains the tetrahedron with vertices
\[
 (0,0,0),\quad(0,\varepsilon,0),\quad(0,0,\varepsilon),
 \quad(2,0,0),
\]
so it is full-dimensional in \(\mathbb R^3\).  Taking its product with
the full-dimensional box \([0,1]^2\times Q\), followed only by the
coordinate ordering used in (2), proves that \(C_{\alpha _0}\) is a
compact full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^7\).

The projection of \(B_\varepsilon\) onto its first coordinate is
\([0,2]\).  Its sections at the two extreme points are forced to be
\(\varepsilon\Delta\).  Its section at \(s=1\) contains \(\Delta\) by
construction and is contained in \(\Delta\), because every set used in
the convex hull has its last two coordinates in
\(\varepsilon\Delta\subseteq\Delta\).  Thus that section is exactly
\(\Delta\).  The only integer points of \([0,1]^2\) are its four
vertices.  This proves (3)-(4).  Scaling a two-dimensional simplex by
\(\varepsilon\) scales its area by \(\varepsilon^2\), while \(Q\) has
area one.  Equations (5) follow.

We next compute the entire autocorrelation profile.  Fix
\(v\in\{0,1\}^2\).  The even first-coordinate parity class indexed by
\(v\) consists of \((0,v)\) and \((2,v)\), each of mass
\(\varepsilon^2V\), and has total mass \(2\varepsilon^2V\).  In the
notation of `aca624dd8874e5a5`, its normalized midpoint convolution has
base masses
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon^2V}{2},\qquad
 \varepsilon^2V,\qquad
 \frac{\varepsilon^2V}{2}
\tag{11}
\]
at first coordinates \(0,1,2\), respectively.  Indeed, the two diagonal
ordered pairs each contribute
\((\varepsilon^2V)^2/(2\varepsilon^2V)=\varepsilon^2V/2\), and the two
off-diagonal ordered pairs together contribute \(\varepsilon^2V\).
The odd first-coordinate parity class indexed by the same \(v\) consists
only of \((1,v)\), of mass \(V\), so its normalized midpoint convolution
puts mass \(V\) there.  No parity class mixes distinct values of \(v\).
Multiplying the sum of these eight normalized convolutions by the global
factor \(2^{-3}=1/8\) gives exactly (6), and there are no other profile
fibers.  Summing (6) gives
\[
 \sum_z\kappa_z=\frac{(1+2\varepsilon^2)V}{2}=\frac W8.
\tag{12}
\]

For an endpoint fiber and a middle fiber, respectively, the selected
mass-to-capacity ratios are
\[
 \frac{t_{(0,v)}^{(\alpha)}}{w_{(0,v)}}
 =\frac{t_{(2,v)}^{(\alpha)}}{w_{(2,v)}}=\frac\alpha{16},
 \qquad
 \frac{t_{(1,v)}^{(\alpha)}}{w_{(1,v)}}
 =\frac{\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)}8.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(\alpha\leq1\) and
\(\varepsilon^2=\alpha _0/36\leq1/36\), the first ratio is at most
\(1/16\), and the second is at most \(37/288<1/4\).  This proves (8),
including the claimed strict capacity margin.

Put \(T_\alpha:=\sum_z t_z^{(\alpha)}\).  By (8), the centerpoint
threshold is
\[
 2^{-3}T_\alpha
 =\frac{\alpha(1+2\varepsilon^2)V}{16}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(m=(1,v)\), its atom alone has mass
\[
 t_m^{(\alpha)}=\frac{\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)V}{8}
 >\frac{\alpha(1+2\varepsilon^2)V}{16},
\tag{15}
\]
where the difference is \(\alpha V/16\).  Every closed base halfspace
containing \(m\) contains this atom, so all four middle points are
weighted centerpoints.

Conversely, let \(p=(i,v_1,v_2)\in D\) with \(i\in\{0,2\}\).  Define
\[
 \sigma_0:=i-1,\qquad \sigma_j:=2v_j-1\quad(j=1,2),
 \qquad L_p(x):=\sum_{j=0}^2\sigma_jx_j.
\]
The point \(p\) is the unique maximizer of \(L_p\) on the box
\([0,2]\times[0,1]^2\).  Hence the proper closed supporting halfspace
\[
 H_p:=\{x:L_p(x)\geq L_p(p)\}
\]
meets \(D\) only at \(p\).  Its profile mass is
\(\alpha\varepsilon^2V/16\), strictly less than (14), so no endpoint is
a centerpoint.  Any lattice point outside
\(\operatorname{conv}D=[0,2]\times[0,1]^2\) is strictly separable from
that box by a proper closed halfspace containing the point and no profile
mass, so it is not a centerpoint either.  The lattice points inside this
box are exactly the twelve points of \(D\).  This proves (9).

We now give the exact simplex metronoid calculation used to prove (10).
For \(\rho>0\), put \(K_\rho:=\rho\Delta\times Q\), and define
\[
 \ell(y_1,y_2,q_1,q_2):=y_1+y_2.
\]
For \(0<\tau\leq\operatorname{vol}_4(K_\rho)=\rho^2V\), set
\(\beta:=\tau/(\rho^2V)\).  Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \min_{x\in\mathsf M_\tau(K_\rho)}\ell(x)
 =\frac{2\rho}{3}\sqrt\beta.}
\tag{16}
\]
To prove this, observe that for \(0\leq s\leq\rho\),
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_4\{x\in K_\rho:\ell(x)\leq s\}=s^2V.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(f\) is any admissible metronoid density, then
\(0\leq f\leq1\) and \(\int_{K_\rho}f=\beta\rho^2V\).  Layer cake and
(17) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_{K_\rho}\ell f
 &=\int_0^\rho\int_{\{\ell>s\}}f\,dx\,ds\\
 &\geq\int_0^{\rho\sqrt\beta}
       (\beta\rho^2V-s^2V)\,ds
 =\frac23\rho^3V\beta^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
After division by \(\tau=\beta\rho^2V\), this is the lower bound in
(16).  Equality is attained by
\(f=1_{\rho\sqrt\beta\,\Delta\times Q}\).  That set has volume \(\tau\),
and the \(\ell\)-coordinate of its centroid is
\(2\rho\sqrt\beta/3\).  Thus (16) is an exact minimum, not merely a
bound.

Fix a centerpoint \(m=(1,v)\).  The two coefficient vectors
\[
 \lambda^{\mathrm{mid}}_m=1
\tag{19}
\]
and
\[
 \lambda^{\mathrm{end}}_{(0,v)}
 =\lambda^{\mathrm{end}}_{(2,v)}=\frac12
\tag{20}
\]
belong to \(\Lambda_m(D)\).  The corresponding members of the family
whose intersection defines the core are
\[
 A_{\mathrm{mid}}
 :=\mathsf M_{\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)V/8}(\Delta\times Q)
\tag{21}
\]
and
\[
 A_{\mathrm{end}}
 :=\frac12\mathsf M_{\alpha\varepsilon^2V/16}
       (\varepsilon\Delta\times Q)
 +\frac12\mathsf M_{\alpha\varepsilon^2V/16}
       (\varepsilon\Delta\times Q).
\tag{22}
\]
Applying (16) to (21), with
\(\beta=\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)/8\), shows that every
\(x\in A_{\mathrm{mid}}\) satisfies
\[
 \ell(x)\geq
 L_\alpha:=\frac23\sqrt{\frac{\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)}8}.
\tag{23}
\]
Every metronoid point is the barycenter of a probability measure supported
on its convex underlying fiber, so it belongs to that fiber.  Therefore
every point of (22), being an average of two points of the convex set
\(\varepsilon\Delta\times Q\), also belongs to that set.  Hence
\[
 \ell(x)\leq\varepsilon\qquad(x\in A_{\mathrm{end}}).
\tag{24}
\]
The choice (1) and \(\alpha\geq\alpha _0\) give the uniform strict
comparison
\[
 L_\alpha^2
 =\frac{\alpha(1+\varepsilon^2)}{18}
 \geq\frac{\alpha _0(1+\varepsilon^2)}{18}
 >\frac{\alpha _0}{18}
 =2\varepsilon^2
 >\varepsilon^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(A_{\mathrm{mid}}\cap A_{\mathrm{end}}=\varnothing\).  Since both
sets occur among the barycentric Minkowski sums in (10), their full
intersection is empty.  The same representations exist for every
\(v\in\{0,1\}^2\), and (25) holds for every
\(\alpha\in[\alpha _0,1]\).  This proves (10) with the claimed stronger
uniform quantifier.

It remains to record the limitation.  Fix any middle fiber
\(m=(1,v)\), trim the profile to \(E=\{m\}\), and select its full mass
\(V\).  Its full-mass metronoid is the centroid singleton
\[
 \mathsf M_V(\Delta\times Q)
 =\left\{\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac12,\frac12\right)\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The singleton barycentric core is therefore nonempty and its absolute
base halfspace depth is \(V\).  Accepted fact `ae0161dd5d4ea672`, with
\(d=4\) and \(\gamma_4=(4/5)^4=256/625\), gives for the displayed
centroid \(c\)
\[
 h_{C_{\alpha _0}\cap(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R^4)}((m,c))
 \geq\frac{256}{625}V
 =\frac{512}{625(1+2\varepsilon^2)}\,2^{-3}W.
\tag{27}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 V>\frac W8,
\tag{28}
\]
because \(\varepsilon^2\leq1/36<1/2\).  Thus the middle fiber is a
heavy singleton, equivalently a heavy rank-zero primitive slice.  In fact,
from \(\varepsilon^2\leq1/36\), the coefficient in (27) is at least
\[
 \frac{9216}{11875}>\frac12>\frac1e.
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently this body satisfies every locked weak Oertel lower bound with
\(c_0<1/e\).  Equations (13) and (23)-(25) isolate the actual failure:
there is ample capacity in every target fiber, but the singleton-middle
and symmetric-endpoint representations force the selected continuous
points into disjoint \(\ell\)-regions.  Adaptive trimming to the heavy
singleton removes that incompatibility.
