## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(I=\lbrace1,\ldots,n\rbrace\), and let \(E\subseteq I\times I\) contain no pair \((i,i)\). Its underlying simple undirected graph \(G\) has edge \(\lbrace u,v\rbrace\) if at least one of \((u,v),(v,u)\) belongs to \(E\). Suppose every vertex of \(G\) has degree at most four. Both orientations of an edge are allowed, and there is no connectedness assumption. For each \(i\in I\), let \(A_i\) be a nonempty consecutive integer interval contained in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), and suppose
$$
D=\left\lbrace x\in\prod_{i\in I}A_i:
 x_u-x_v\le1\text{ for every }(u,v)\in E\right\rbrace
\ne\varnothing.
$$
For every \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), retain the entire support and original affine law
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in D}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z}\quad(x\in D),\qquad
\tau_p(j)=\inf_{\substack{\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ closed halfspace}\\j\in\mathcal H}}p(\mathcal H),\qquad
M_k(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^k.
$$
Here \(p\) is zero off \(D\), and \(p(\mathcal H)=\sum_{x\in D\cap\mathcal H}p_x\). In accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 these are exactly \(L=1\), \(n_1=n\), \(A_1=D\), \(w_{1,x}=e^{\ell\cdot x}\), \(Z_1=Z\), and \(p_1=p\); its block index is suppressed. For every law used in the proof its symbols \(R_j,H_p,b,\kappa_j,Y_j,V_j,q_j\) retain exactly that fact's meanings. In particular the reflected sum is oriented and includes its central term once. For an unmasked law the notation \(A_E\) and \(\nu(p)=\sum_jq_jV_j\) agrees with accepted fact 668fd1e2877a80d5, with the same fields and original normalization.

Fix any positive function \(C_{\mathrm{ALO}}\) furnished by Theorem 1.8 of Anari, Liu and Oveis Gharan, arXiv:2001.00303v3, as specified in EXTERNAL SOURCES. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_*&=\frac{13}{256},&
A_*&=1+C_{\mathrm{ALO}}(\delta_*),&
a_4&=\frac{10\log3}{3},\\
C_4&=\frac{A_*}{2}a_4^2,&
\varepsilon_4&=\frac{2}{17}\log\frac{1536}{1531},&
c_4&=\frac14\exp\left(-\frac{C_4}{\varepsilon_4}\right)>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(D\) is a complete convex lattice census and
$$
M_2(p)\ge c_4\,4^{-n},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge c_4^{3/2}\,8^{-n}.
$$
The constant is independent of the graph, its number of components, the masks, \(n\), and every real field. No boundary points or actually supported assignments are discarded. If the underlying degree is at most two, one may instead use the entirely elementary coefficient
\(c_2=\frac14e^{-192C_2}\), where \(C_2=4(1+\sqrt2)(\log3)^2\), in both conclusions, replacing \(c_4\) by \(c_2\).

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. Define
$$
Q=\left\lbrace z\in\mathbb R^n:
 \min A_i\le z_i\le\max A_i\ (i\in I),\quad
 z_u-z_v\le1\ ((u,v)\in E)\right\rbrace.
$$
This is a compact convex set, is nonempty because it contains \(D\), and has exactly \(D\) as its integer points: the integer points in each closed coordinate interval are exactly \(A_i\). In addition \(D\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq Q\), so \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). All inequalities are non-strict. This proves completeness even for singleton or lower-dimensional supports.

We first prove the moment estimate when every mask is \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), and write this full support as \(A_E\). It is nonempty, since it contains \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\). For a supported center \(j\), use the canonical row and its displacement translation
$$
R_j=\lbrace x\in A_E:2j-x\in A_E\rbrace,\qquad
\mathcal E_j=\lbrace v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v\in R_j\rbrace.
$$
Affineness gives \(\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}=e^{\ell\cdot j}/Z=p_j\) for every \(v\in\mathcal E_j\). Consequently, with exactly the definitions of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705,
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(j)&=p_j|R_j|,&
\kappa_j(j+v)&=\frac1{|R_j|},&
Y_j(j+v)&=\ell\cdot v,\\
b(p)&=\frac1{Z^3}\sum_{k\in A_E}e^{3\ell\cdot k}|R_k|^2,&
q_j&=\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
 {\sum_{k\in A_E}e^{3\ell\cdot k}|R_k|^2}.&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Every row contains its center, so these denominators are positive. In particular \(q_j=p_jH_p(j)^2/b(p)\) is precisely the full canonical center law, not a replacement for the original law \(p\).

Here is the full row census, proved for the present arbitrary orientations. Set
$$
F(j)=\left\lbrace i\in I:j_i=1,\quad
 j_v\ne0\text{ for all }(i,v)\in E,\quad
 j_u\ne2\text{ for all }(u,i)\in E\right\rbrace.
$$
Then
$$
\mathcal E_j=\left\lbrace v\in\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace^n:
 v_i=0\ (i\notin F(j)),\quad
 |v_u-v_v|\le1\text{ for every edge }\lbrace u,v\rbrace
 \text{ of }G[F(j)]\right\rbrace.
\tag{2}
$$
Indeed the box conditions on \(j\pm v\) force zero displacement at coordinates of \(j\) equal to zero or two, and allow \(-1,0,1\) at coordinates equal to one. For each directed constraint \((u,v)\), its two reflected inequalities are equivalent to
\(|v_u-v_v|\le1-(j_u-j_v)\). If \(j_u-j_v=1\), the center pair is \((1,0)\) or \((2,1)\); its extreme coordinate is already pinned to zero displacement, and the other coordinate is forced to zero as well. These are exactly the exclusions in \(F(j)\). If \(j_u=j_v=1\), the remaining condition is \(|v_u-v_v|\le1\), and it is automatic if either coordinate was already pinned. Equal extreme centers both have zero displacement. If \(j_u-j_v<0\), at least one center is extreme, so the displacement difference has magnitude at most one whereas the right side is at least two. There is no other case because \(j\) is supported. This proves both inclusions in (2). Two opposite directed constraints on the same underlying edge give the same restriction between its free coordinates.

By (1) and (2), the displacement row is uniform on spins \(-1,0,1\) on the simple graph \(G[F(j)]\), with adjacent opposite nonzero spins forbidden. We now establish the required covariance bound. For any finite simple graph \(B\) of maximum degree at most four, form a simple graph \(H\) with vertices \(i^+,i^-\) for each vertex \(i\) of \(B\), edges \(\lbrace i^+,i^-\rbrace\), and, for every edge \(\lbrace i,k\rbrace\) of \(B\), edges \(\lbrace i^+,k^-\rbrace\) and \(\lbrace i^-,k^+\rbrace\). The plus and minus copies give a bipartition. Each copy of \(i\) has degree \(1+\deg_B(i)\le5\).

There is an exact bijection between its independent sets and the allowed spin configurations: a spin \(+1\) selects \(i^+\), a spin \(-1\) selects \(i^-\), and a zero spin selects neither. The edge between the two copies forbids selecting both; the two edges corresponding to a base edge forbid exactly the two opposite nonzero spin pairs. Thus each allowed spin configuration gives an independent set, and every independent set gives one such configuration, with these operations inverse. Uniform spins therefore correspond exactly to the hardcore law of activity \(\lambda=1\) on \(H\), since every independent set has weight \(1^{|S|}=1\). No multiplicity or additional weight is present.

The critical activity used by the external theorem is
\(\lambda_c(\Delta)=(\Delta-1)^{\Delta-1}/(\Delta-2)^\Delta\) for \(\Delta\ge3\). At \(\Delta=5\) it is \(256/243\), and
\((1-\delta_*)\lambda_c(5)=(243/256)(256/243)=1\). Theorem 1.8, with its signed influence convention in Definition 1.1, therefore applies to every nonempty \(H\) constructed here and bounds the largest eigenvalue of its influence matrix by \(C_{\mathrm{ALO}}(\delta_*)\). There are at least two vertices whenever \(B\) is nonempty, so its \(\eta_0\) assertion applies also at the smallest nonempty size.

For clarity, convert that assertion directly into a covariance bound. For a uniform independent set on \(H\), let \(U_a\) be its occupancy indicator at vertex \(a\), let \(K_{ab}=\operatorname{Cov}(U_a,U_b)\), and let \(\mathsf D\) be the diagonal matrix with entries \(\operatorname{Var}(U_a)\). This matrix \(\mathsf D\) is unrelated to the support \(D\) in the statement. Each diagonal entry is positive: both the empty set and the singleton \(\lbrace a\rbrace\) have positive probability. For distinct \(a,b\), if \(s_a=\mathbb P(U_a=1)\), then
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{ab}
&=s_a(1-s_a)\bigl(
 \mathbb P(U_b=1\mid U_a=1)
 -\mathbb P(U_b=1\mid U_a=0)\bigr),\\
\mathsf I+\Psi&=\mathsf D^{-1}K,\qquad
\mathsf D^{-1/2}K\mathsf D^{-1/2}
 =\mathsf D^{1/2}(\mathsf I+\Psi)\mathsf D^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The diagonal identity follows from \(K_{aa}=\mathsf D_{aa}\) and \(\Psi(a,a)=0\). The matrix on the left in the second line is symmetric and positive semidefinite, and the right identity is a similarity. Hence the external eigenvalue bound implies
\(\mathsf D^{-1/2}K\mathsf D^{-1/2}\preceq A_*\mathsf I\), where \(\mathsf I\) denotes the identity matrix. For any real coefficient vector \(\mathbf h=(h_a)_a\), using the Bernoulli variance bound \(\mathsf D_{aa}\le1/4\), we obtain
$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_a h_aU_a\right)
 =\mathbf h^{\mathsf T}K\mathbf h\le A_*\sum_a h_a^2\mathsf D_{aa}
 \le\frac{A_*}{4}\sum_a h_a^2.
\tag{4}
$$
The spin at \(i\) is exactly \(U_{i^+}-U_{i^-}\). Substituting \(h_{i^+}=t_i\), \(h_{i^-}=-t_i\) in (4) proves
$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_i t_iv_i\right)
 \le\frac{A_*}{2}\sum_i t_i^2
\tag{5}
$$
for the uniform allowed spin law on every such \(B\). For an empty graph on zero vertices the sum is identically zero, so (5) also holds without invoking the external theorem. The proof needs neither connectedness nor a mixing-time implication.

Apply (5) with \(B=G[F(j)]\) and \(t_i=\ell_i\). Global sign reversal preserves (2), so the row score has mean zero. By (1),
$$
V_j=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2
 =\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_j}(\ell\cdot(X-j))
 \le\frac{A_*}{2}\sum_{i\in F(j)}\ell_i^2.
\tag{6}
$$
An empty free set has a singleton row and \(V_j=0\), as included in this inequality.

We next bound the free-site probabilities under the full center law \(q\). Fix \(i\in F(j)\), and let \(j^{(0)},j^{(2)}\) be obtained by replacing \(j_i=1\) by zero and two. Both centers are supported. At zero, incoming constraints hold since their other center is not two, and outgoing constraints hold automatically. At two, outgoing constraints hold since their other center is nonzero, and incoming constraints hold automatically. Put
\(N^-(i)=\lbrace u:(u,i)\in E\rbrace\) and
\(N^+(i)=\lbrace v:(i,v)\in E\rbrace\). The free-site criterion gives exactly
$$
F(j^{(0)})=F(j)\setminus(\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^-(i)),\qquad
F(j^{(2)})=F(j)\setminus(\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^+(i)).
\tag{7}
$$
For the first equality, changing the center at \(i\) to zero newly pins precisely its formerly free incoming neighbors, while the incoming-center test at an outgoing neighbor remains satisfied. No previously nonfree coordinate can become free. The second equality follows with outgoing and incoming interchanged, and zero and two interchanged. A neighbor present in both sets is counted once.

Combining (7) with (2) yields the exact zero-section identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{j^{(0)}}
 &=\lbrace v\in\mathcal E_j:v_u=0
     \text{ for every }u\in\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^-(i)\rbrace,\\
\mathcal E_{j^{(2)}}
 &=\lbrace v\in\mathcal E_j:v_u=0
     \text{ for every }u\in\lbrace i\rbrace\cup N^+(i)\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Every adjacency involving a coordinate thus set to zero is automatic; all other adjacencies are exactly those of the new induced free graph. Moreover replacing any selected spin coordinates by zero preserves every allowed spin constraint in (2). Zeroing the indicated coordinates therefore maps the whole old displacement row onto each section in (8), and each fiber contains at most \(3^5\) configurations. Indeed each indicated set contains at most the vertex \(i\) and its four distinct undirected neighbors. Translation does not change cardinality, so
\(|R_j|\le3^5|R_{j^{(0)}}|\) and
\(|R_j|\le3^5|R_{j^{(2)}}|\).

Condition the full center law on every coordinate except \(i\). If its completion with \(J_i=1\) is not supported and free, the conditional free-site probability is zero. Otherwise call this completion \(j\). Both replacement completions just described are supported. Their exact unnormalized center weights from (1) obey
$$
\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
 {e^{3\ell\cdot j^{(0)}}|R_{j^{(0)}}|^2}
 \le3^{10}e^{3\ell_i},\qquad
\frac{e^{3\ell\cdot j}|R_j|^2}
 {e^{3\ell\cdot j^{(2)}}|R_{j^{(2)}}|^2}
 \le3^{10}e^{-3\ell_i}.
\tag{9}
$$
For \(\ell_i\ge0\) compare the conditional probability to the weight of the completion of value two; for \(\ell_i\le0\) compare to the completion of value zero. Combining with the probability bound one and averaging the conditioning gives
\(\mathbb P_q(i\in F(J))\le\min\lbrace1,3^{10}e^{-3|\ell_i|}\rbrace\).
The weights in (9) are all from the original full center law. Their common normalizer cancels, and no completion of positive mass has been removed.

For every \(t\ge0\),
\(t^2\min\lbrace1,3^{10}e^{-3t}\rbrace\le a_4^2\).
To check it, the two terms in the minimum are equal at \(t=a_4\); for \(t\le a_4\) use \(t^2\le a_4^2\). For \(t\ge a_4\), the derivative of \(3^{10}t^2e^{-3t}\) is \(3^{10}e^{-3t}t(2-3t)\le0\), since \(a_4>2/3\). Thus (6), averaged against \(q\), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu(p)=\sum_jq_jV_j
&\le\frac{A_*}{2}\sum_{i\in I}
 \ell_i^2\mathbb P_q(i\in F(J))\\
&\le\frac{A_*}{2}a_4^2n=C_4n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
There is no restriction on the size or sign of any field.

We reproduce the actual-halfspace source inequality from the proof of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 before its incidence-parameter substitution. For any supported \(j\) and any original ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing \(j\), each noncentral reflected pair \(x,2j-x\) has at least one endpoint in \(\mathcal H\). Otherwise a defining linear inequality would strictly exclude their midpoint. The center itself belongs to \(\mathcal H\). Summing over the reflection orbits, with their two orientations and the center counted once, proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{x\in R_j}\min\lbrace p_x,p_{2j-x}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_p(j)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}e^{-|Y_j|}
 \ge\frac{H_p(j)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}|Y_j|}
 \ge\frac{H_p(j)}2e^{-\sqrt{V_j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The factor one-half uses only half of the available central mass, so also covers a singleton row. The equality follows directly from the canonical definitions of \(\kappa_j\) and \(Y_j\); the last two inequalities are Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking the infimum over every containing ambient halfspace, squaring, and summing against the original law gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(p)
&\ge\frac14\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2e^{-2\sqrt{V_j}}
 =\frac{b(p)}4\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sqrt{V_J}}\\
&\ge\frac{b(p)}4e^{-2\mathbb E_q\sqrt{V_J}}
 \ge\frac{b(p)}4e^{-2\sqrt{\nu(p)}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
In particular this bound is for the actual moment, with the original \(p\) and \(Z\) in (1).

Accepted fact dec10c04f9ccf987 applies to this very unmasked support, graph and affine law with degree bound \(\Delta=4\). Its full-reflection estimate is
\(b(p)\ge4^{-n}e^{\varepsilon_4n}\), because its deficit equals
\(-\log(1-5/(48\cdot2^5))=\log(1536/1531)\) and \(1+\Delta^2=17\). Combining with (10) and (12), then completing the square, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(p)&\ge\frac14\,4^{-n}
 \exp\bigl(\varepsilon_4n-2\sqrt{C_4n}\bigr),\\
\varepsilon_4n-2\sqrt{C_4n}
 &=\varepsilon_4\left(\sqrt n-\frac{\sqrt{C_4}}{\varepsilon_4}\right)^2
   -\frac{C_4}{\varepsilon_4}
 \ge-\frac{C_4}{\varepsilon_4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$
This proves the desired unmasked second-moment bound for every positive dimension. For zero remaining coordinates, a support consists of one point and its depth and moments are one. It also satisfies the bound, since \(0<c_4\le1/4\).

We now extend this estimate to all masks in the statement, retaining every supported assignment. We construct a finite disjoint partition by deleting fixed coordinates and splitting binary masks. At any node, after inserting the values already fixed, the exact remaining support has the same form on the induced directed graph: each remaining coordinate has a consecutive mask in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), and the constraints between remaining labels are unchanged. Discard a node only if this support is empty.

Here are the exact updates that justify this assertion inductively. If coordinate \(i\) has singleton mask \(\lbrace a\rbrace\), delete it and remember the fixed value. If \(a=0\), every incoming constraint \((u,i)\) requires \(x_u\le1\), so remove 2 from precisely those remaining incoming-neighbor masks; every outgoing constraint is automatic. If \(a=2\), every outgoing constraint \((i,v)\) requires \(x_v\ge1\), so remove 0 from precisely those remaining outgoing-neighbor masks; every incoming constraint is automatic. If \(a=1\), all incident constraints are automatic for values in \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\). Both orientations, when present, are treated by these same rules. Removing an endpoint preserves the consecutive-interval property unless the mask becomes empty, in which case the fiber is empty. The rules capture every constraint involving the deleted coordinate, so the updated support is exactly its fiber, not an enlargement. Subsequent singleton masks can be deleted by the same rule.

If a remaining coordinate has a binary mask, it is either \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) or \(\lbrace1,2\rbrace\). Partition according to its two values, delete that coordinate in each branch using the preceding updates, and retain exactly the nonempty fibers. The union is the entire parent support, disjointly. If there are no singleton or binary masks, stop. Every step deletes a coordinate, so each branch has length at most \(n\). A nonempty root therefore yields finitely many nonempty leaves whose supports partition \(D\).

At a leaf \(\lambda\), let \(I_\lambda\) be the remaining labels, \(r_\lambda=|I_\lambda|\), and let \(\eta_{\lambda,i}\) be the remembered value for \(i\notin I_\lambda\). All remaining masks are \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\). Thus the leaf is exactly the affine insertion \(\iota_\lambda(A_{E|I_\lambda})\) of an unmasked induced-graph census, of maximum degree at most four. There is also the capacity bound
$$
\sum_\lambda2^{r_\lambda}\le2^n.
\tag{14}
$$
Indeed at a node with \(k\) remaining labels, a terminal leaf contributes \(2^k\). A singleton deletion has at most one nonempty child on \(k-1\) labels, and a binary split has at most two. Induction from the leaves shows that their total contribution is at most \(2^k\) at every node. Empty children contribute zero. This proves (14), also for leaves with no remaining labels.

For each leaf define its conditional law and original marginal mass explicitly by
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_\lambda&=\sum_{y\in A_{E|I_\lambda}}
 e^{\sum_{i\in I_\lambda}\ell_i y_i},&
p_\lambda(y)&=\frac{e^{\sum_{i\in I_\lambda}\ell_i y_i}}{Z_\lambda},\\
\alpha_\lambda&=
 \frac{e^{\sum_{i\notin I_\lambda}\ell_i\eta_{\lambda,i}}Z_\lambda}{Z},&
p_{\iota_\lambda y}&=\alpha_\lambda p_\lambda(y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
For \(r_\lambda=0\), the sum defining \(Z_\lambda\) is the single empty assignment and equals one. Since the leaf supports partition \(D\), all \(\alpha_\lambda>0\) and \(\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda=1\). In particular (15) relates every leaf normalizer to the same original \(Z\); the inherited fields are the original \(\ell_i\).

Let \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be any closed halfspace containing \(\iota_\lambda y\). Its trace under \(\iota_\lambda\) is a containing closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{I_\lambda}\) when the restricted normal is nonzero, and all of that space when the restricted normal vanishes. In both cases its \(p_\lambda\)-mass is at least \(\tau_{p_\lambda}(y)\). The same holds on a singleton leaf, with depth one. Restricting the original halfspace mass to this leaf and using (15) therefore gives
\(\tau_p(\iota_\lambda y)\ge\alpha_\lambda\tau_{p_\lambda}(y)\).
The unmasked bound (13), including its zero-dimensional convention, consequently yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(p)&\ge\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda^3M_2(p_\lambda)
 \ge c_4\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda^3\,4^{-r_\lambda},\\
1=\left(\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda\right)^3
&\le\left(\sum_\lambda\alpha_\lambda^3\,4^{-r_\lambda}\right)
       \left(\sum_\lambda2^{r_\lambda}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$
The last line is Holder with exponents \(3\) and \(3/2\), applied to
\(\alpha_\lambda=(\alpha_\lambda4^{-r_\lambda/3})4^{r_\lambda/3}\).
Equations (14)--(16) prove \(M_2(p)\ge c_4\,4^{-n}\). The coefficient is used once at the terminal unmasked estimates; it is not multiplied along paths of the conditioning tree.

Finally Jensen's inequality under the entire original law proves
\(M_3(p)=\mathbb E_p[(\tau_p(J)^2)^{3/2}]\ge M_2(p)^{3/2}\), giving the stated third-moment bound. Every halfspace in the argument is an original ambient containing halfspace or its exact trace. The proof allows disconnected graphs and isolated vertices throughout. Its size-one case either has a one-vertex free graph, whose doubled graph has two vertices, or an empty free graph; all singleton and fixed-coordinate cases were explicitly included.

For the elementary degree-two coefficient, accepted fact 668fd1e2877a80d5 supplies \(\nu(p)\le C_2n\) for every unmasked underlying degree-at-most-two graph, with exactly the canonical law and rows in (1). Accepted fact dec10c04f9ccf987 at \(\Delta=2\) supplies \(b(p)\ge4^{-n}e^{n/192}\). Thus (12) and the same square completion give \(M_2(p)\ge\frac14e^{-192C_2}4^{-n}=c_2\,4^{-n}\). The zero-dimensional case also satisfies this estimate. Applying precisely the partition and transfer (14)--(16) with \(c_2\) proves the masked degree-two assertion, and Jensen gives its third moment.
