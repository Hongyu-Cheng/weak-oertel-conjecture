## Statement

Let $r,s\ge1$ be integers, $Q\subset\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^s$ compact and convex, and $a+L$ a full-rank lattice coset in $\mathbb R^r$ of covolume $v$. Let $P=\{p\in a+L:Q_p\ne\varnothing\}$ include all nonempty fibers, including those of zero $s$-volume. If $P$ affinely spans $\mathbb R^r$, then
$$
M\ge \frac{v\,s!}{(r+s)!}\,T,\qquad
M=\operatorname{vol}_{r+s}(Q),\qquad T=\sum_{p\in P}\operatorname{vol}_s(Q_p).
$$
The coefficient $s!/(r+s)!$ is sharp, and remains the sharp infimum if all nonempty sampled fibers are required to have positive $s$-volume.

For any finite family of such sets with the same fine covolume $v$, let $T>0$ and $M$ now denote the sums of their sampled masses and ordinary volumes. The total sampled mass of levels whose nonempty sampled bases affinely span $\mathbb R^r$ is at most $(r+s)!M/(s!\,v)$. The remaining levels carry at least $(T-(r+s)!M/(s!\,v))_+$.

## Proof

If $T=0$ the one-set inequality is immediate. Otherwise triangulate $\operatorname{conv}P$ with every point of $P$ a vertex, by the finite lattice hull volume lemma. Each full-dimensional simplex $\Delta$ has volume at least $v/r!$. Write its vertices as $p_0,\ldots,p_r$, with barycentric coordinates $\alpha_i$, and put $b_i=\operatorname{vol}_s(Q_{p_i})$. Convexity gives $\sum_i\alpha_i Q_{p_i}\subseteq Q_{\sum_i\alpha_i p_i}$. For the indices with $\alpha_i>0$ and $b_i>0$, Brunn–Minkowski and homogeneity give a volume lower bound $(\sum_i\alpha_i b_i^{1/s})^s$ for their Minkowski sum. For every other index with $\alpha_i>0$, fix one point in its nonempty fiber and add its weighted translation. If there are no positive-volume summands, the required lower bound is zero. Therefore, by the multinomial expansion for the integer $s\ge1$,
$$
\operatorname{vol}_s(Q_{\sum_i\alpha_i p_i})
\ge\left(\sum_i\alpha_i b_i^{1/s}\right)^s
\ge\sum_i\alpha_i^s b_i.
$$
On $\Delta$ the fraction of its volume with $\alpha_i\ge t$ is $(1-t)^r$ for $0\le t\le1$, by homothety about $p_i$. Its normalized $\alpha_i$ density is $r(1-t)^{r-1}$, and integration by parts gives
$$
\int_\Delta\alpha_i^s\,du=\operatorname{vol}_r(\Delta)\,r\int_0^1 t^s(1-t)^{r-1}\,dt
=\operatorname{vol}_r(\Delta)\,\frac{r!s!}{(r+s)!}.
$$
Integrating the section inequality over the simplices and applying Fubini gives at least $v s!/(r+s)!$ times the sum of all incident vertex volumes. Each sampled base occurs at least once and the volumes are nonnegative. The rest of the base contributes nonnegative volume.

For sharpness let the base be the simplex on a lattice basis and the coset origin. Put an $s$-body $K$ of volume $V>0$ at one vertex, the singleton $\{0\}$ at each other vertex, and take their placed convex hull. The only sampled bases are the $r+1$ vertices. At barycentric coordinate $\alpha_0$ for the first vertex the section is exactly $\alpha_0 K$ by uniqueness of the barycentric weights. Thus $T=V$ and the integral just calculated gives equality. The hull is full-dimensional since its bases span $\mathbb R^r$ and one fiber is full-dimensional. Replace the other vertex fibers by $\varepsilon K$ for $\varepsilon>0$ to make every sampled fiber positive. At each base the fiber is $(\alpha_0+\varepsilon(1-\alpha_0))K$ by convexity of $K$. The mass is then $(1+r\varepsilon^s)V$ and the volume tends to the equality value by dominated convergence as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

For the family sum the proved inequality over precisely the levels with full sampled affine rank. Their ordinary volumes sum to at most $M$, and empty sample sets carry no mass. Subtract from $T$ and use nonnegativity for the complementary bound.
