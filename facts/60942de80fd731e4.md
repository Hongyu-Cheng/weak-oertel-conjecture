## Statement

Let \(N\ge 1\). For \(i=1,\ldots,N\), let \(a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Z\) satisfy \(a_i\le b_i\), and put
\[
D=\prod_{i=1}^N\{a_i,\ldots,b_i\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^N.
\]
Fix \(c>0\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), and give \(D\) the positive log-affine weights
\[
w_{\mathbf j}=c\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf j),
\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}.
\]
For \(s>0\), set \(A_s=\{\mathbf j\in D:w_{\mathbf j}\ge s\}\). For a finite \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^N\) and \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb R^N\), define the unnormalized closed ambient-halfspace depth
\[
d_A(\mathbf y)
=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf y\in H}}|A\cap H|,
\qquad
d_s=d_{A_s},
\]
and define the actual-depth layer functional
\[
L(w)=3\int_0^\infty s^2
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf j)^2\,ds.
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
r=\bigl|\{i:b_i-a_i+1\ge3\}\bigr|,
\qquad
\gamma=\log\frac{32}{27}.
\]
Then the stronger parameter-dependent estimate
\[
4^N\frac{L(w)}{W^3}
\ge
\frac14\left(\frac{32}{27}\right)^r
\exp(-\sqrt{12r})
\ge
\frac14\exp\left(-\frac3\gamma\right)
\tag{2}
\]
holds. In particular, the source-level parameter estimate and its requested dimension-uniform consequence hold:
\[
4^N\frac{L(w)}{W^3}
\ge
\frac14\left(\frac{32}{27}\right)^r
\exp(-\sqrt{24r})
\ge
\kappa_{\square},
\qquad
\kappa_{\square}
=\frac14\exp\left(-\frac6{\log(32/27)}\right)>0.
\tag{3}
\]
The value of the exact expression in (3) is
\[
\kappa_{\square}
=1.150245967728600\ldots\times10^{-16};
\tag{4}
\]
in particular, \(1.49\times10^{-11}\) is not the numerical value of that expression.

For a finite lattice set \(A\), define the oriented, zero-inclusive reflection count
\[
R_A(\mathbf j)
=\bigl|\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^N:
\mathbf j+\mathbf v,\mathbf j-\mathbf v\in A\}\bigr|
\]
and its integrated square
\[
\mathcal R(w)
=3\int_0^\infty s^2
\sum_{\mathbf j\in A_s}R_{A_s}(\mathbf j)^2\,ds.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(L(w)\ge\mathcal R(w)/4\), and the following exact integrated four-endpoint identity holds:
\[
\mathcal R(w)
=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf j\in D,\ \mathbf u,\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^N\\
\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v\in D,\ \mathbf j\pm\mathbf u\in D}}
w_{\mathbf j}^{\,3}
\exp\left(
-3\max\bigl\{|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v|,
|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf u|\bigr\}
\right).
\tag{6}
\]

For the normalized one-coordinate interval \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), \(m\ge1\), and \(0<q\le1\), put
\[
h_m(j)=\min\{j,m-1-j\},
\quad
R_m(j)=2h_m(j)+1,
\quad
W_m(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}q^j,
\quad
Z_m(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}q^{3j}R_m(j)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
With \(z=q^3\) and \(A(z)=1+6z+z^2\), the exact finite-interval energy formula, for \(0<q<1\), is
\[
(1-z)^3Z_m(q)
=A(z)(1-z^m)-
\begin{cases}
4m z^{m/2}(1-z^2),&m\text{ even},\\
8m z^{(m+1)/2}(1-z),&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]
At \(q=1\), all ensuing quotient statements are understood by continuity. The exact zero-displacement and cubic-energy bounds are
\[
\frac{Z_m(q)}{W_m(q)^3}\ge
\begin{cases}
1,&m=1,\\
1/4,&m=2,\\
8/27,&m\ge3.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]
If \(a=-\log q\) and a triple \((J,V,U)\) has probability
\[
\mathbb P(J=j,V=v,U=u)=\frac{q^{3j}}{Z_m(q)},
\qquad
|v|,|u|\le h_m(j),
\tag{10}
\]
then \(V\) and \(U\) are conditionally independent and uniform on the indicated symmetric interval, and
\[
\mathbb E(aV)=\mathbb E(aU)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E(aV)^2\le\frac23,
\qquad
\mathbb E(aU)^2\le\frac23.
\tag{11}
\]
Both second moments in (11) vanish when \(m\le2\) or \(q=1\). In particular, the weaker bounds \(4/3\) for each of the two second moments also hold.

All conclusions are invariant under a common positive rescaling of the weights and under affine unimodular lattice transformations \(\mathbf x\mapsto U\mathbf x+\mathbf b\), where \(U\in\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb Z)\) and \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z^N\). Thus (2)--(3) also hold for every affine-unimodular image of such a box, with the transported log-affine weights.

## Proof

First compare actual depth with reflection depth. The ambient integer dimension \(N\) here is the variable called \(n\) in accepted fact d52c89d30b11ee18, and the present \(R_A(\mathbf j)\) is exactly that fact's oriented, zero-inclusive reflection count \(r_A(\mathbf j)\). That fact proves, for every supported target,
\[
d_A(\mathbf j)\ge\frac{R_A(\mathbf j)+1}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, the nonzero displacements split into antipodal pairs, every closed halfspace containing their midpoint contains at least one endpoint of each pair, and the zero displacement contributes the target. Since \(R_A(\mathbf j)\ge1\) at a supported target, (12) gives
\[
d_A(\mathbf j)^2\ge\frac14R_A(\mathbf j)^2.
\]
Integrating this pointwise inequality in (1) proves
\[
L(w)\ge\frac14\mathcal R(w).
\tag{13}
\]

We next prove the exact identity (6). All relevant sums are finite, so expand the square in (5) and interchange the finite sum and integral. A tuple \((\mathbf j,\mathbf v,\mathbf u)\) contributes at level \(s\) exactly when
\[
\mathbf j,\ \mathbf j+\mathbf v,\ \mathbf j-\mathbf v,
\ \mathbf j+\mathbf u,\ \mathbf j-\mathbf u\in A_s.
\]
Its contribution to the integral is therefore
\[
3\int_0^{m(\mathbf j,\mathbf v,\mathbf u)}s^2\,ds
=m(\mathbf j,\mathbf v,\mathbf u)^3,
\tag{14}
\]
where \(m(\mathbf j,\mathbf v,\mathbf u)\) is the minimum of the five corresponding weights. Log-affinity gives
\[
w_{\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v}
=w_{\mathbf j}e^{\pm\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf j\pm\mathbf u}
=w_{\mathbf j}e^{\pm\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf u}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
m(\mathbf j,\mathbf v,\mathbf u)
=w_{\mathbf j}
\exp\left(-\max\bigl\{
|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v|,
|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf u|
\bigr\}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Substituting (15) into (14) and summing proves (6), including the cases \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\), \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\), and \(\mathbf v=\pm\mathbf u\); orientations in the square are retained exactly once each.

We now normalize the box. Translation, followed independently in each coordinate by reflection about the midpoint of its integer interval when the corresponding tilt is positive, maps closed halfspaces bijectively to closed halfspaces and preserves all counting depths. Multiplying every weight by a common scalar \(\lambda>0\) gives
\[
A_s(\lambda w)=A_{s/\lambda}(w),
\qquad
L(\lambda w)=\lambda^3L(w),
\qquad
W(\lambda w)^3=\lambda^3W(w)^3.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus it is enough to prove the ratio estimate for
\[
D=\prod_{i=1}^N\{0,\ldots,m_i-1\},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf j}=\prod_{i=1}^Nq_i^{j_i},
\qquad
0<q_i\le1.
\tag{17}
\]
Put \(a_i=-\log q_i\ge0\), and use the quantities in (7) with \(m=m_i\) and \(q=q_i\), writing them as \(W_i,Z_i,h_i,R_i\).

For each coordinate \(i\), independently choose a triple \((J_i,V_i,U_i)\) by
\[
\mathbb P(J_i=j,V_i=v,U_i=u)=\frac{q_i^{3j}}{Z_i},
\qquad
|v|,|u|\le h_i(j).
\tag{18}
\]
The normalization is exact because there are \(R_i(j)^2\) choices of \((v,u)\) at a fixed \(j\). Define
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^Na_iV_i,
\qquad
T=\sum_{i=1}^Na_iU_i.
\tag{19}
\]
In (6), the four endpoint conditions factor coordinatewise as
\[
|v_i|,|u_i|\le h_i(j_i),
\]
and \(w_{\mathbf j}^3=\prod_iq_i^{3j_i}\). Moreover,
\[
|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v|=|S|,
\qquad
|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf u|=|T|
\]
after the coordinate reflections in (17). Therefore (6) factors exactly as
\[
\mathcal R(w)
=\left(\prod_{i=1}^NZ_i\right)
\mathbb E\exp\bigl(-3\max\{|S|,|T|\}\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]
This is one global expectation; no coordinatewise halfspace or exponential loss has been inserted.

We record precisely how the accepted one-dimensional estimates enter. Accepted fact 6d7276756d672c31 uses interval endpoint parameter \(L=m-1\), geometric parameter \(t=q\), centered reflection size \(s_j=R_m(j)\), normalized mass \(p_j=q^j/W_m(q)\), and
\[
k_L(t)=\frac{Z_m(q)}{W_m(q)^3}.
\tag{21}
\]
Under this alias map, its exact finite-sum identity is (8), and its reflection-energy bounds are exactly (9). Its triple law is also exactly (10), because
\[
\frac{p_j^3}{k_L(t)}
=\frac{(q^j/W_m(q))^3}{Z_m(q)/W_m(q)^3}
=\frac{q^{3j}}{Z_m(q)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Hence its sharp variance estimate gives (11). This use includes finite truncation, both parities of \(m\), the limit \(q=1\), and reflection of a tilt of either sign.

For clarity, the zero-displacement boundary constants can also be checked directly. If \(m=1\), only \(j=v=u=0\) occurs, so \(Z_m/W_m^3=1\). If \(m=2\), again \(h_m(j)=0\) at both sites, and
\[
4(1+q^3)-(1+q)^3
=3(1-q)^2(1+q)\ge0,
\]
so \(Z_m/W_m^3\ge1/4\). In both cases \(V=U=0\). For \(m\ge3\), (8)--(9) give the strict cubic-energy gain \(8/27=(1/4)(32/27)\). It follows, including all singleton coordinates in the ambient exponent, that
\[
\prod_{i=1}^N\frac{Z_i}{W_i^3}
\ge4^{-N}\left(\frac{32}{27}\right)^r.
\tag{23}
\]
Also \(W=\prod_iW_i\).

Conditional symmetry in (18) centers every \(V_i\) and \(U_i\). Independence across coordinates and (11) therefore give
\[
\mathbb E S^2\le\frac{2r}{3},
\qquad
\mathbb E T^2\le\frac{2r}{3},
\qquad
\mathbb E(S^2+T^2)\le\frac{4r}{3};
\tag{24}
\]
coordinates with at most two sites contribute zero. Since \(x\mapsto e^{-3x}\) is convex on \([0,\infty)\), Jensen's inequality, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E e^{-3\max\{|S|,|T|\}}
&\ge
\exp\bigl(-3\,\mathbb E\max\{|S|,|T|\}\bigr)\\
&\ge
\exp\left(-3\sqrt{\mathbb E(S^2+T^2)}\right)\\
&\ge\exp(-\sqrt{12r}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
The weaker second-moment bound \(8r/3\), obtained by weakening each \(2/3\) in (11) to \(4/3\), similarly gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{-3\max\{|S|,|T|\}}\ge e^{-\sqrt{24r}}.
\tag{26}
\]

Combine (13), (20), (23), and (25), and divide by \(W^3=\prod_iW_i^3\). This proves the first inequality in (2). Completing the square in \(\sqrt r\) gives
\[
\gamma r-\sqrt{12r}
=\gamma\left(\sqrt r-\frac{\sqrt3}{\gamma}\right)^2
-\frac3\gamma
\ge-\frac3\gamma,
\tag{27}
\]
which proves the second inequality in (2). Using (26) instead gives the source-level parameter estimate in (3), and
\[
\gamma r-\sqrt{24r}
=\gamma\left(\sqrt r-\frac{\sqrt6}{\gamma}\right)^2
-\frac6\gamma
\ge-\frac6\gamma,
\tag{28}
\]
which proves its dimension-uniform consequence.

Finally,
\[
\log(32/27)=0.169899036795397\ldots,
\qquad
\frac6{\log(32/27)}=35.3150913222984\ldots,
\]
so direct evaluation of the exact expression in (3) gives (4), not \(1.49\times10^{-11}\). The proof of positivity and every inequality uses the exact expression, not this decimal evaluation.

For affine-unimodular invariance, let \(T(\mathbf x)=U\mathbf x+\mathbf b\) with \(U\in\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathbb Z)\) and \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z^N\). The map \(T\) bijects the lattice and carries closed halfspaces bijectively to closed halfspaces. Transporting a level set and its weights by \(w'_{T\mathbf j}=w_{\mathbf j}\) therefore preserves \(W\), every supported counting depth, and \(L\). The transported weights remain log-affine because
\[
w'_{\mathbf x}
=c\exp\bigl(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot U^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf b)\bigr),
\]
with a harmless new common factor and tilt \(U^{-T}\boldsymbol\ell\). This proves the final invariance assertion and completes the proof.
