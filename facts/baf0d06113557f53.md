## Statement

Use \(D,P,w,W,\phi\) as in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. The values may be arbitrary real convex-admissible potentials; the normalized potential of that fact is \(\phi-\min_D\phi\), and \(w_{\max}=\exp(-\min_D\phi)\). Use \(\boldsymbol\theta(u)\), \(p^u\), and \(\mathcal G(u)\) exactly as in accepted fact 3ee88fc83ab3b533: \(\boldsymbol\theta(u)\) is the unique tilt making the law proportional to \(\exp(-u_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta(u)\cdot\mathbf I)\) have mean zero, and \(\mathcal G(u)=\mathbb E_{p^u}u-u_{\mathbf0}\). Its variation direction denoted \(h\) will be denoted \(\eta\) here; its residual operator \(R\) and covariance \(\Sigma\) keep their meanings. The homogeneous function at the end of a fan deformation is denoted \(h\), and is not that predecessor's variation direction. In the rank-one example, \(I\) is the single coordinate of \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^1\).

First, let \(r\ge1\), let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\) be finite and complete with \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) full dimensional and \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and let a finite convex supporting-fan extension of a convex-admissible potential normalized at zero be
\[
u_0(\mathbf x)=\max_j(\mathbf a_j\cdot\mathbf x+b_j),
\qquad \max_j b_j=0.
\]
For \(0\le t\le1\), put
\[
u_t(\mathbf x)=\max_j\bigl(\mathbf a_j\cdot\mathbf x+(1-t)b_j\bigr),
\qquad h(\mathbf x)=u_1(\mathbf x)=\max_j\mathbf a_j\cdot\mathbf x.
\]
All these functions are finite and convex on \(\mathbb R^r\), \(u_t(\mathbf0)=0\), and \(h\) is positively homogeneous. They are restricted to the same complete \(D\) throughout. On every interval where the affine branch selected at each site is fixed, write \(\eta_{\mathbf I}=\partial_tu_t(\mathbf I)\). At a differentiable positive-temperature branch \(\beta(t)\), with the tilt recomputed for \(\beta(t)u_t|_D\), set \(\boldsymbol\theta_t=\boldsymbol\theta(\beta(t)u_t|_D)\) and write expectations under \(p^{\beta(t)u_t|_D}\). Then
\[
\boldsymbol\theta_t'
=\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb E\!\left[
\mathbf I\bigl(\beta'(t)u_t+\beta(t)\eta\bigr)\right].
\]
Whenever \(\beta(t)\) is stationary for the scalar energy \(\beta\mapsto\mathcal G(\beta u_t|_D)\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal G(\beta(t)u_t|_D)
=\beta(t)\left(\mathbb E\eta-\beta(t)\mathbb E[(Ru_t)(R\eta)]\right).
\]
The covariance expression need not have the sign required for monotonicity.

For an exact counterexample, take \(r=1\), \(P=[-3,3]\), \(D=\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}\), and define globally \(f_t(x)=\max\{|x|,2|x|-2+2t\}\) for \(0\le t\le1\). Put
\[
M(t)=\max_{\beta>0}\mathcal G(\beta f_t|_D).
\]
Here \(M(t)\) is the optimized energy envelope, not the normalization \(M\) mentioned in the realization alias map of 488f0a583886b5fc. This maximum exists for every \(t\). At \(t=0\), there is a unique maximizing \(\beta_*>0\). It is specified exactly as the unique positive solution of \(\mu(\beta)=\beta V(\beta)\), where \(\mu(\beta)\) and \(V(\beta)\) are the mean and variance of the height \(f_0(I)\) under the law proportional to \(\exp(-\beta f_0(I))\) on \(D\). This scalar variance \(V(\beta)\) is not the convex roof denoted \(V\) in the realization alias map of 488f0a583886b5fc. The original convex-admissible potential \(\phi_I=\beta_*f_0(I)\) has original mean zero and energy gap \(M(0)\). For all sufficiently small \(t\ge0\), the maximizer \(\beta(t)\) is unique and differentiable from the right, \(\beta(0)=\beta_*\), and \(M'(0+)<0\).

The endpoint \(f_1(x)=2|x|\) is finite, convex, and positively homogeneous. Every lattice site, including both endpoints and all interior sites, retains strictly positive weight at every finite \(\beta>0\) and every \(t\in[0,1]\).

## Proof

Accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc provides a finite convex piecewise-affine lower roof on all of \(P\), equal to every prescribed site value. Its roof can be written as a maximum of finitely many affine functions on \(P\). Extending that maximum to \(\mathbb R^r\) gives the supporting-fan form in the statement. Subtracting the value at zero makes \(\max_j b_j=0\). This is the exact normalization map to that predecessor; no sign hypothesis away from zero is imposed. Each \(b_j\le0\), so all \(u_t\) are finite maxima of affine functions, and their value at zero is zero. At \(t=1\), every intercept vanishes, giving the claimed positive homogeneity. Nothing changes \(D\).

For the derivative identities, the potential vector passed to accepted fact 3ee88fc83ab3b533 is \(v(t)=\beta(t)u_t|_D\). Its variation direction is \(k=\beta' u_t+\beta\eta\). Although \(v(t)\) itself may have nonzero tilt, its effective potential \(v_{\mathbf I}(t)-\boldsymbol\theta(v(t))\cdot\mathbf I\) is originally centered. Affine invariance of \(\mathcal G\), of the centered law, and of residuals permits the variation formulas of that accepted fact at this effective potential. Its first centering variation is \(\Sigma^{-1}\mathbb E[\mathbf I k]\), proving the displayed formula for \(\boldsymbol\theta_t'\). Its first energy variation is
\[
D\mathcal G(v)[k]=\mathbb E k-k_{\mathbf0}-\mathbb E[(Rv)(Rk)].
\]
Here \(u_t(\mathbf0)=\eta_{\mathbf0}=0\), \(Rv=\beta Ru_t\), and \(R\) is linear in the value direction at the current law. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
D\mathcal G(v)[u_t]
&=\mathbb E u_t-\beta\mathbb E[(Ru_t)^2],\\
D\mathcal G(v)[\eta]
&=\mathbb E\eta-\beta\mathbb E[(Ru_t)(R\eta)].
\end{aligned}
\]
Scalar stationarity sets the first of these to zero. Expanding \(k\) then proves the stated envelope derivative, including the centering and temperature terms. Thus its sign is an additional inequality, not a consequence of having differentiated an uncentered law.

Now verify the complete counterexample and its temperature maximum. The interval \(P\) is full dimensional in \(\mathbb R\), zero is interior, and its complete lattice set is exactly \(D\). The functions \(f_t\) are even and globally convex. Therefore the original weights \(\exp(-\beta f_t(I))\) have mean zero, and the unique centering tilt is zero, for every \(\beta>0\) and every \(t\in[0,1]\). This uses all seven sites. At \(t=0\), their heights are \(f_0(0)=0\), \(f_0(\pm1)=1\), \(f_0(\pm2)=2\), and \(f_0(\pm3)=4\).

For \(0\le t<1/2\), their heights are \(0,1,2+2t,4+2t\) at these respective sites. In particular the right variation at zero is \(\eta_I=2\mathbf1_{\{|I|\ge2\}}\). This is precisely removal of the two negative intercepts in the fan \(f_0(x)=\max\{x,-x,2x-2,-2x-2\}\). The two zero intercepts stay zero, while each intercept \(-2\) is changed to \(-2+2t\). At \(t=1\), the fan becomes \(2|x|\).

Write \(q=\exp(-\beta)\). This auxiliary scalar \(q\in(0,1)\) is not the integer continuous dimension \(q\) in 488f0a583886b5fc. At \(t=0\), introduce the following polynomials only for this scalar calculation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z&=1+2q+2q^2+2q^4,\\
N&=2q+4q^2+8q^4,\\
A&=2q+8q^2+32q^4,\\
T&=2q+16q^2+128q^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The polynomial \(Z\) is exactly \(W\) for potential \(\beta f_0\) and normalization \(w_{\max}=1\). The polynomials \(N,A,T\) are its unnormalized first, second, and third height moments; in particular \(A\) is not the support called \(A=D\) in the realization alias map of 488f0a583886b5fc. These scalar moments are unrelated to the predecessor's barycentric family or any capacity. Thus
\[
\mu=\frac NZ,\qquad
V=\frac AZ-\mu^2,\qquad
\kappa_3=\frac TZ-\frac{3\mu A}{Z}+2\mu^3.
\]
Every law here gives positive mass to at least two different heights, so \(\mu>0\) and \(V>0\). Its energy is \(E_0(\beta)=\beta\mu(\beta)\). Direct differentiation of the finite sum gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu'&=-V,&
V'&=-\kappa_3,\\
E_0'&=\mu-\beta V,&
E_0''&=\beta\kappa_3-2V.
\end{aligned}
\]
These agree with the first and second variations of accepted fact 3ee88fc83ab3b533, since evenness gives \(\mathbb E[I f_0]=0\) and \(Rf_0=f_0-\mu\).

A direct polynomial multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu\kappa_3-2V^2
&=\frac{Z(NT-2A^2)+N^2A}{Z^3}\\
&=\frac{-4q^2-24q^3-56q^4-168q^6+112q^7
-896q^8-864q^9-256q^{10}}{Z^3}<0
\end{aligned}
\]
for \(0<q<1\). For the strict sign, \(-168q^6+112q^7<-56q^6\), and all remaining displayed nonzero terms are negative. At every stationary \(\beta\), one has \(\beta=\mu/V\), so \(E_0''=(\mu\kappa_3-2V^2)/V<0\). Every stationary point is therefore a strict downward crossing of \(E_0'\). There cannot be two such points: between two strict local maxima a continuous differentiable function would attain an interior local minimum, whose derivative would vanish, contradicting the strict negative second derivative at every stationary point. Also \(E_0(\beta)>0\) for \(\beta>0\) and tends to zero as \(\beta\) decreases to zero and as \(\beta\) tends to infinity. Hence it attains an interior maximum, which is its unique stationary point \(\beta_*\) and is strict globally. This proves the exact characterization in the statement without an optimizer as a certificate.

At \(\beta=\log2\), one has \(q=1/2\), and the exact moments give
\[
\mu=\frac{20}{21},\qquad
V=\frac{440}{441},\qquad
E_0'(\log2)=\frac{420-440\log2}{441}>0.
\]
Indeed \(\log2<3/4<21/22\). The first bound follows, for example, by integrating the strictly convex function \(1/x\) over \([1,2]\) below its endpoint chord. Since the unique stationary point is a downward crossing, \(\beta_*>\log2\).

It remains to prove the strict sign of the intercept variation at this exact globally optimized temperature. Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(\beta)
&=\mathbb E[f_0(I)\mid |I|\ge2]-\mu(\beta)\\
&=\frac{2+4q^2}{1+q^2}-\mu(\beta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Differentiating this conditional mean and \(\mu\) gives \(\Delta'=V-4q^2/(1+q^2)^2\). For \(0<q\le1/2\), its numerator after multiplication by the positive denominator \(Z^2(1+q^2)^2\) is exactly
\[
2q+4q^2-8q^3+16q^4+14q^5+56q^6
+44q^7+32q^8+36q^9>0.
\]
The only negative term is absorbed by \(4q^2-8q^3=4q^2(1-2q)\ge0\). Thus \(\Delta\) is strictly increasing as a function of \(\beta\) for \(\beta\ge\log2\). At \(\beta=\log2\), one has \(\Delta=12/5-20/21=152/105\).

The identity
\[
\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(1/3)^{2j+1}}{2j+1},
\]
obtained by integrating the geometric series for \(1/(1-z^2)\) from zero to \(1/3\), gives \(\log2>2/3+2/81=56/81\). Since \(56\cdot152=8512>8505=105\cdot81\), it follows that \((\log2)\Delta(\log2)>1\). The function \(\Delta\) is positive and increasing on this range. Therefore \(\beta\Delta(\beta)>1\) whenever \(\beta\ge\log2\), in particular at \(\beta_*\).

For the right variation \(\eta=2\mathbf1_{\{|I|\ge2\}}\), one has \(\operatorname{Cov}(f_0,\eta)=\mathbb E\eta\cdot\Delta(\beta)\). The exact centered first variation, or direct differentiation of these even finite sums, now yields
\[
\left.\partial_t\mathcal G(\beta f_t|_D)\right|_{t=0+}
=\beta\mathbb E\eta\bigl(1-\beta\Delta(\beta)\bigr)<0
\]
at \(\beta=\beta_*\). No tilt correction is omitted: the tilt is exactly zero throughout. No scalar correction is omitted either: \(\partial_\beta\mathcal G(\beta f_0|_D)=0\) at \(\beta_*\).

To justify differentiating the globally optimized envelope, for \(t\) in a fixed sufficiently small interval \([0,\varepsilon]\), all nonzero heights of \(f_t\) lie in \([1,4+2\varepsilon]\). Hence \(\mathcal G(\beta f_t|_D)\) tends to zero uniformly in \(t\) as \(\beta\) decreases to zero and as \(\beta\) tends to infinity. The latter follows by bounding its positive numerator by \(6\beta(4+2\varepsilon)\exp(-\beta)\), while its denominator is at least one. Consequently its maximizers for small \(t\) stay in one compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\). On that interval the finite-sum energy and its derivatives converge uniformly to those at \(t=0\). The unique strict global maximum \(\beta_*\) forces every maximizing \(\beta\) to converge to \(\beta_*\) as \(t\) decreases to zero. Its strictly negative second derivative permits the implicit function theorem applied to \(\partial_\beta\mathcal G(\beta f_t|_D)=0\), giving one differentiable critical branch near \(\beta_*\). For small \(t\), all global maximizers lie on that unique branch. The envelope derivative is therefore the displayed negative \(\partial_t\) derivative, proving \(M'(0+)<0\).

The original potential \(\phi=\beta_*f_0\) has minimum zero, \(w_{\max}=1\), \(B=4\beta_*\) in the notation of 488f0a583886b5fc, and original mean zero. Its energy is computed using exactly \(\exp(-\phi)/W\). This completes the counterexample to the optimized monotonicity step. The broader statement that a homogeneous potential on the same \(D\) can dominate every centered convex-admissible potential remains unresolved by this packet.
