## Statement

Let integers $m\ge k\ge2$ and $0<p<1$ be given. Let $X,Y,E_+,E_-$ be independent, with $X\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)$, $Y\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1)$, and $E_\pm\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$. Write
$$
q=k-1,\quad c=1-p,\quad b=\frac{q+p}{m},\quad
A=q+(m+1)p,\quad B=m+p+q,\quad
D=qp^2+\bigl[(k+1)m+k^2-k+2\bigr]p+q(m+k+1).
$$
Set $P(p)=\Pr(X+pE_+\ge E_-+bY)$ and $Z=(X-E_-)/(E_++Y)$. Then
$$
f_Z(z)=\frac{m+1}{2^k}
\begin{cases}
(1-z)^{-m-2}, & z<0,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^q 2^j\binom{m+j+1}{j}\frac{z^j}{(1+z)^{m+j+2}}, & z\ge0 .
\end{cases}
$$
The probability and its derivative are
$$
P(p)=\Pr(Z\ge b)+\int_{-p}^b\left(\frac{p+z}{p+b}\right)^m f_Z(z)\,dz,\qquad
P'(p)=\frac{J(p)}{(p+b)^{m+1}},
$$
where $J(p)=\int_{-p}^b(p+z)^{m-1}[q-(m+1)z]f_Z(z)\,dz$. Put
$$
S(t)=\sum_{j=0}^q2^j\binom{m+j+1}{j}(t-p)^jc^{q-j}.
$$
Let $Q$ be the unique polynomial with $(t^mQ(t))'=t^{m-1}[A-(m+k)t]S(t)$, where the prime in this equation is with respect to $t$. Then
$$
J(p)=\frac{(m+1)(A/B)^mQ(A/B)}{2^k c^{k+1}}
     +\frac{(-1)^k p^{m+k}D}{m c^{k+1}(1+p)^2}.
$$

## Proof

Put $T=E_++Y$ and $U=E_+/T$. The change $(E_+,Y)=(tu,t(1-u))$ has Jacobian $t$. Its density factors as the density of $\operatorname{Gamma}(m+1,1)$ times that of $\operatorname{Beta}(1,m)$, with $U$ independent of $(T,X,E_-)$. The event is $Z+(p+b)U\ge b$. Conditional on $Z=z$ its probability is zero for $z<-p$, one for $z\ge b$, and $[(p+z)/(p+b)]^m$ in between by the beta survival formula.

For $W=X-E_-$ the convolution integrand is $(w+v)^qe^{-w-2v}/q!$ for $v\ge\max(0,-w)$. For $w<0$, shifting $v$ by $-w$ gives $2^{-k}e^w$. For $w\ge0$, expanding the power and integrating each term gives $2^{-k}e^{-w}\sum_{j=0}^q (2w)^j/j!$. In $f_Z(z)=\mathbb E[T f_W(Tz)]$, integration of $t^{m+j+1}e^{-(1+z)t}$ on the positive side and $t^{m+1}e^{-(1-z)t}$ on the negative side gives the displayed density. It is continuous at zero. The Leibniz derivative of the probability integral has upper boundary term $b'f_Z(b)$, canceled by the derivative of the tail, and zero lower boundary term. The integrand and its partial derivative are bounded on the moving intervals locally in $p$. The remaining derivative formula uses $m(p+b)-(m+1)(p+z)=q-(m+1)z$.

Write $J_-$ and $J_+$ for the parts below and above zero. In $J_-$ take $t=(p+z)/(1-z)$ with $0\le t\le p$. Its integrand becomes
$$
\frac{m+1}{2^k(1+p)^2}\,t^{m-1}[A+(q-m-1)t],
$$
and hence $J_-=p^mN_0/[2^k m(1+p)^2]$ with $N_0=(m+1)q+(mk+1)p$. In $J_+$ take $t=(p+z)/(1+z)$, with $p\le t\le A/B$. The $j$-th term of the density contributes
$$
\frac{m+1}{2^k c^{j+2}}\,2^j\binom{m+j+1}{j}\,t^{m-1}(t-p)^j[A-(m+k)t].
$$
Thus $J_+=(m+1)[(A/B)^mQ(A/B)-p^mQ(p)]/(2^kc^{k+1})$. Existence and uniqueness of $Q$ follow by dividing each coefficient of $[A-(m+k)t]S(t)$ of degree $\ell$ by $m+\ell$.

For the lower endpoint let $V_0=(m+1)A-m(m+k)p$, $r=-2p$, and $s=c$. Integrating the polynomial primitive from $0$ to $p$ and setting $t=pu$ gives
$$
m(m+1)Q(p)=\sum_{j=0}^q r^js^{q-j}(V_0+jA).
$$
In fact the two beta integrals in the $j$-th term are $\int_0^1u^{m-1}(1-u)^j\,du=(m-1)!j!/(m+j)!$ and $\int_0^1u^m(1-u)^j\,du=m!j!/(m+j+1)!$. Multiplying them by $m(m+1)\binom{m+j+1}{j}$ produces $m+j+1$ and $m$, respectively. The geometric sum is $(s^k-r^k)/(s-r)$ and its $r\partial_r$ derivative is $[rs^k-ksr^k+(k-1)r^{k+1}]/(s-r)^2$. Expanding the two brackets gives
$$
V_0(s-r)+Ar=N_0s,\qquad V_0(s-r)+A[ks-(k-1)r]=D.
$$
Consequently $m(m+1)Q(p)=[N_0 s^{k+1}-D r^k]/(1+p)^2$. In $J_-+J_+$ the term with $N_0$ cancels and $p^m r^k/2^k=(-1)^k p^{m+k}$, as claimed.
