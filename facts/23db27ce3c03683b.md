## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $K$ be a centroid-zero $d$-simplex with barycentric functions $1/(d+1)+l_i(x)$, $1\le i\le d+1$. Let $(K_z)$ be a finite compatible family of positive full-dimensional compact convex fibers with an affine interior section $q(z)$. If for every $z,i$ the cap $l_i(x-q(z))\ge0$ captures exactly the fraction $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ of $K_z$, then $K_z=q(z)+r_zK$ for some $r_z>0$ at every $z$. In particular $q$ is the centroid section.

## Proof

Fix one fiber and translate $q(z)$ to zero. Write $g$ for its centroid. If $l_i(g)>0$, passing from $l_i(x)\ge0$ to $l_i(x)\ge l_i(g)$ loses positive volume: the centroid is an interior point, and its sufficiently small interior neighborhood meets the intervening open slab. The original cap has fraction $\gamma_d$, while classical Grünbaum's centroid inequality, as recorded in the proof of 03bc771bddd39a8a, gives at least $\gamma_d$ for the smaller through-centroid cap. This is a contradiction. Since all $l_i(g)\le0$ and $\sum_i l_i=0$, all are zero; their rank is $d$, so $g=0$.

The caps cover the whole space because their margins sum to zero. Thus each point's ordinary normalized depth is at most $\gamma_d$. Fact 03bc771bddd39a8a gives ordinary maximum depth at least $\gamma_d$, with equality only for a simplex, so the fiber is a simplex. Its $i$th specified cap is an equality centroid cap. The oriented equality case of Grünbaum stated in that fact's proof makes it the apex-side cut of a pyramid with base parallel to the cap. For a simplex this apex is one vertex and the other $d$ vertices lie in the opposite facet. Thus each $l_i$ is positively proportional to an opposite-facet barycentric gradient of this fiber. The $d+1$ distinct canonical directions match its $d+1$ facet gradients bijectively. After relabeling, write $\nabla\mu_i=c_i l_i$ for its barycentric functions, with $c_i>0$. Since $\sum_i\nabla\mu_i=0$ and the sole dependence of the $l_i$ is the constant coefficient vector, all $c_i$ equal a common $c>0$. At the fiber's centroid zero, $\mu_i(0)=1/(d+1)$, so the fiber is $\{x:1/(d+1)+c\,l_i(x)\ge0\text{ for all }i\}=c^{-1}K$. Translating back proves the claim.
