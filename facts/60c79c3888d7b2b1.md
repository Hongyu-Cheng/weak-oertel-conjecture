## Statement

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be a convex body whose only nonempty integer fibers are positive-length intervals at three consecutive bases. Choose the shorter of the two endpoint intervals, choosing either if their lengths agree. For a body $K$ of positive total integer fiber length $W(K)$, let $h_K(p)$ be the infimum of the total integer fiber length in a closed ambient halfspace containing the mixed point $p$. Write $D(K)=\max_{p\in K\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)}h_K(p)/W(K)$.

There is a path of convex bodies from $C$, continuous in the Hausdorff metric, on which $D$ is continuous and nonincreasing. The same three integer bases remain nonempty throughout. The chosen endpoint interval has nonincreasing length, is never globally deepest while its length is positive, and ends as a retained singleton of zero length. The other endpoint keeps its initial positive length, and the middle interval ends with that same length, so the two positive fibers are adjacent and $D$ at the terminal body is $1/4$. One-dimensional length is used throughout. There are at most four nonconstant pieces: an optional Minkowski segment (affine in support functions) followed by convex hulls of the three lifted intervals with endpoints affine in the parameter of each piece.

## Proof

Use a fixed affine map that takes the three bases to $-1,0,1$, reflecting the base if necessary to put the chosen endpoint at $-1$. Subtract the affine function of the base interpolating the original endpoint interval centers. Reflect all heights if necessary to make the initial middle center nonnegative. These maps preserve the mixed lattice, fiber lengths, and closed halfspaces, so we construct the path in these coordinates and pull it back by the fixed inverse. Write the normalized initial fibers as
$$
K_-=[-a/2,a/2],\qquad K_0=[s-U/2,s+U/2],\qquad K_+=[-b/2,b/2],
\qquad 0<a\le b,\quad U>0,\quad s\ge0.
$$
Convexity forces $(K_-+K_+)/2\subseteq K_0$, or
$$
U\ge (a+b)/2,\qquad |s|\le U/2-(a+b)/4.
$$
These inequalities also suffice at $a=0$. The lifted hull projects to $[-1,1]$, has the specified intervals at the two extreme bases, and at base zero the endpoint weights in a convex combination are equal. Thus the contribution from those endpoints is a multiple of a point of $(K_-+K_+)/2$. Combining it with a point of $K_0$ stays in $K_0$. The hull is compact and has interior because it contains the segment of length $b>0$ at base $1$ and a point at a different base. It has exactly the three listed nonempty integer fibers, including the endpoint if it is a singleton.

First take the Minkowski segment from the normalized original body to the lifted hull of its own integer fibers. This hull lies in the original body, so each intermediate body contains the hull and is contained in the original body. The inclusions preserve every integer fiber, all candidate points, every halfspace mass and $D$. The path then uses lifted hulls with the displayed compatible parameters.

For every later body with $a>0$, the hypotheses of accepted fact `c680a307d3f26cec` hold. Its middle raw maximum $F$ and the raw global maximum $\max\{b/2,F\}$ are for actual closed ambient halfspaces. The depth on the left fiber never exceeds $a/2<F$, since $F\ge(U+a)/2$. At fixed $a,b,U$, increase $s$ to $U/2-(a+b)/4$. This stays compatible and $D$ is nonincreasing by the accepted formula, including any competing endpoint. On contact the middle fiber is $[-(a+b)/4,U-(a+b)/4]$. Keep this form and keep $b$ fixed in all remaining pieces. Compatibility now reduces to $U\ge(a+b)/2$. The accepted contact profile gives
$$
F_{\rm contact}=
\begin{cases}
U/2+a,&(a+b)/2\le U\le b,\\
(U+b)/4+a,&b\le U\le b+2a,\\
(U+a)/2,&U\ge b+2a.
\end{cases}
$$
The adjacent expressions agree at the seams. The accepted tie classification gives $F_{\rm contact}\le b/2$ exactly when $U\le b-2a$, with equality precisely at $U=b-2a$. This tie is compatible exactly for $a\le b/5$.

If $F_{\rm contact}\le b/2$, increase $U$ to $b-2a$ at fixed $a$ on contact. Compatibility is preserved, the raw global maximum stays $b/2$, and the total $a+b+U$ increases. Thus $D$ is nonincreasing until the positive tie, including the case that the tie already holds.

If $F_{\rm contact}>b/2$, decrease $a$ on contact at fixed $U$ until the first positive tie or $a=0$. Compatibility is preserved because $(a+b)/2$ decreases. While the middle is deepest, the derivatives with respect to $a$ of $D=F_{\rm contact}/(a+b+U)$ in the three respective branches are
$$
\frac{b+U/2}{(a+b+U)^2},\qquad
\frac{3(b+U)}{4(a+b+U)^2},\qquad
\frac{b}{2(a+b+U)^2}.
$$
All are positive, and continuity at the seams and at a tie gives descent along this whole piece. If $U<b$, the positive tie occurs at $a=(b-U)/2$, which is below the starting $a$ by the initial strict inequality. If $U\ge b$, no positive tie occurs.

From any positive tie, decrease $a$ to zero with $U=b-2a$ and $s=(b-5a)/4$. The condition $a\le b/5$ continues to give compatibility. Both positive deepest fibers have raw maximum $b/2$ throughout the positive part of this piece. The total length is $2b-a$, so $D=b/[2(2b-a)]$ decreases to $1/4$ as $a$ decreases to zero. In this case the terminal lengths are $(0,b,b)$.

If $a=0$ is reached without a positive tie, then $U\ge b$. At this collapse, and for any subsequent $U\ge b$, the fibers are
$$
K_-=\{0\},\qquad K_0=[-b/4,U-b/4],\qquad K_+=[-b/2,b/2].
$$
The point fiber has raw depth zero by the closed halfspace $z\le-1$. For the two positive fibers, the positive support is exactly $\{0,1\}$. The linear functionals $-z$ and $z$ strictly expose $0$ and $1$, respectively, in that positive support. Thus accepted fact `2acc8bc803acf1c0`, (6)–(7), applies with $n=d=1$ to each positive interval, giving its own interval depth at every point and raw maxima $U/2$ and $b/2$. These same calculations at $U=b$ also certify the endpoint of the positive-tie piece. For $U\ge b$ the normalized maximum at $a=0$ is $U/[2(U+b)]$. Its limit from the shrinking positive piece agrees: if $U>b$ the third contact branch applies for all sufficiently small $a$ and tends to $U/2$. If $U=b$ the contact value tends to $b/2$. Now decrease $U$ to $b$, keeping the displayed intervals. The midpoint inclusion remains true since $U\ge b>b/2$. The derivative of $U/[2(U+b)]$ is $b/[2(U+b)^2]>0$, so $D$ decreases along any nonconstant such piece and ends at $1/4$.

All pieces keep $b$ fixed and $U/b\ge(1+a/b)/2\ge1/2$. The ratio $a/b$ is nonincreasing to zero. On the tie piece $U/b=1-2a/b$ and tends to one. On the other route the final adjustment reduces $U/b$ from at least one to one. Thus both surviving lengths stay positive and the total stays bounded away from zero. The normalized formulas are continuous along the contact pieces and agree at each join. On the translation piece continuity is given by the accepted profile.

There are at most two pieces after the translation: increasing $U$ then following the tie, or shrinking $a$ then following the tie or adjusting $U$ after collapse. Together with the initial Minkowski segment this gives at most four nonconstant pieces. The support function of the Minkowski segment is affine in its parameter and continuous uniformly on unit directions, giving Hausdorff continuity. In each later piece the six lifted endpoints (allowing repeats) vary affinely. Convex combinations with the same weights in two such hulls differ by at most the largest change of a corresponding endpoint, giving Hausdorff continuity even when two endpoints merge. The matching endpoint parameters at joins give a single continuous path. A fixed affine inverse preserves Hausdorff continuity and the depth properties.
