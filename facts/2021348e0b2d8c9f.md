## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, and fix any support-minimal balanced negative cap circuit furnished by accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb` in its empty-core branch. Thus the circuit has \(r\in\{2,3\}\) individual cap bodies \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\). For every strictly positive probability vector
\[
 \boldsymbol\vartheta=(\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_r)
 \in\Delta_r^\circ
 :=\left\{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathbb R_{>0}^r:
 \sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i=1\right\},
\]
let \(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta}\) be the positive same-base reweighting from that accepted fact. Put
\[
 K_{i,z}:=(A_i)_z
 \qquad(1\le i\le r,\ z\in\mathbb Z^n)
\]
and define the common lattice support
\[
 Z_\cap:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:K_{i,z}\ne\varnothing
 \text{ for every }1\le i\le r\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a finite set. Accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb` gives
\[
 (B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})_z
 =\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_iK_{i,z}
 \quad(z\in Z_\cap),
 \qquad
 (B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})_z=\varnothing
 \quad(z\notin Z_\cap).
\tag{2}
\]
For a mixed body \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\), write
\[
 W(D):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{area}(D_z).
\]
For nonempty compact convex sets \(K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^2\), including lower-dimensional ones, let \(V_2(K,L)\) denote their mixed area in the normalization
\[
 \operatorname{area}(sK+tL)
 =s^2\operatorname{area}(K)+2stV_2(K,L)
  +t^2\operatorname{area}(L)
 \qquad(s,t\ge0).
\tag{3}
\]
Define the symmetric mixed-area matrix \(\mathbf M\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}\) by
\[
 M_{ij}:=\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}V_2(K_{i,z},K_{j,z}).
\tag{4}
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. For every \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r^\circ\),
\[
 \boxed{W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
 =\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M
  \boldsymbol\vartheta.}
\tag{5}
\]

2. Let
\[
 \Delta_r:=\left\{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathbb R_+^r:
 \sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i=1\right\},
\]
let \(H\in\mathbb R\), and let \(\mathbf 1\) be the all-ones vector in \(\mathbb R^r\). The following are equivalent:
\[
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})\le H
 \quad\text{for every }\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r^\circ;
\tag{6}
\]
\[
 \max_{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r}
 \boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M
 \boldsymbol\vartheta\le H;
\tag{7}
\]
\[
 H\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf M
 \quad\text{is copositive, meaning that}
\quad
 x^{\mathsf T}(H\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf M)x\ge0
 \quad(x\in\mathbb R_+^r).
\tag{8}
\]
The boundary values in (7) are only the continuous common-support extension of (5). In particular, at the \(i\)-th simplex vertex one obtains
\[
 e_i^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M e_i
 =\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z}),
\tag{9}
\]
which need not equal the full cap mass \(W(A_i)\).

3. Put
\[
 a_{i,z}:=\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z}),
 \qquad
 g_i:=\bigl(\sqrt{a_{i,z}}\bigr)_{z\in Z_\cap},
\tag{10}
\]
and define the Gram matrix and mixed-area excess matrix by
\[
 (\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}})_{ij}
 :=\langle g_i,g_j\rangle
 =\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}\sqrt{a_{i,z}a_{j,z}},
 \qquad
 \mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}}:=\mathbf M-\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathbf M=\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}}+\mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}},}
 \qquad
 (\mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}})_{ii}=0,
 \qquad
 (\mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}})_{ij}\ge0\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
 =\left\|\sum_{i=1}^r\vartheta_i g_i\right\|_2^2
 +\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}}
  \boldsymbol\vartheta.
\tag{13}
\]
If
\[
 W_\cap(A_i):=\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z})
\tag{14}
\]
denotes the common-support mass of the \(i\)-th cap, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r^\circ}
 \boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}}
  \boldsymbol\vartheta
 =\max_{\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r}
 \boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}}
  \boldsymbol\vartheta
 =\max_{1\le i\le r}W_\cap(A_i).}
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 W(A_i)-W_\cap(A_i)
 =\sum_{z\notin Z_\cap}\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z})\ge0,
\tag{16}
\]
so strict inequality occurs exactly when some fiber outside \(Z_\cap\) has positive area in \(A_i\). Such a fiber contributes to \(W(A_i)\) and to neither side of (5). Hence the optimized pure Brunn--Minkowski term cannot certify a reweighting heavier than the best common-support cap, and a fortiori cannot certify one heavier than the best full individual cap. For every \(H\in\mathbb R\) and every \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r^\circ\), the exact missing-excess condition is
\[
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})>H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf E_{\mathrm{mix}}
  \boldsymbol\vartheta
 >H-\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}}
  \boldsymbol\vartheta.
\tag{17}
\]

4. The following direct segment bound also holds. Let \(S\subseteq Z_\cap\). Suppose that, for every \(z\in S\) and \(1\le i\le r\), the cap fiber \(K_{i,z}\) contains a segment
\[
 x_{i,z}+[0,d_{i,z}]e_i,
 \qquad d_{i,z}\ge0,
\tag{18}
\]
where \(e_i\in\mathbb R^2\) is a unit vector independent of \(z\). Then every \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r^\circ\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
 \ge
 \sum_{z\in S}\sum_{1\le i<j\le r}
 \vartheta_i\vartheta_j
 |\det(e_i,e_j)|d_{i,z}d_{j,z}.}
\tag{19}
\]
In particular, in the situation of accepted fact `6974f69acff32c8c`, apply its boundary-metronoid construction separately to every index of the same planar circuit. Write \(s_{i,z}\) and \(F_{i,z}\) for the quantities denoted there by \(s_z\) and \(F_z\) when the construction is applied to index \(i\), put
\[
 e_i:=\frac{Jw_i}{\|w_i\|_2},
\]
where \(J\) is rotation by \(+\pi/2\), and let \(d_{i,z}\) be the Euclidean length of \(F_{i,z}\). For every
\[
 S\subseteq\{z:s_{i,z}>0\text{ for every }i\},
\tag{20}
\]
the actual boundary sections \(F_{i,z}\subseteq K_{i,z}\) satisfy (18), so (19) applies. When \(r=2\), positive balance forces \(w_1,w_2\), and hence \(e_1,e_2\), to be parallel, so this particular lower bound is identically zero. No positive uniform lower bound is asserted for \(r=3\).

## Proof

We first record the elementary planar mixed-area expansion used below. For convex polygons \(P_1,\ldots,P_r\), merge their cyclic edge-direction lists, inserting zero-length edges so that all lists use the same directions. The edge vector of \(\sum_i t_iP_i\) in each direction is the corresponding linear combination of the edge vectors of the \(P_i\). After choosing starting vertices compatibly, every vertex of the sum is therefore linear in \((t_1,\ldots,t_r)\). The shoelace formula shows that its area is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial in these coefficients. Its diagonal coefficients are \(\operatorname{area}(P_i)\), and its \(t_it_j\) coefficient is \(2V_2(P_i,P_j)\), where
\[
 V_2(P_i,P_j)
 =\frac12\bigl(\operatorname{area}(P_i+P_j)
 -\operatorname{area}(P_i)-\operatorname{area}(P_j)\bigr).
\]
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{area}\left(\sum_{i=1}^rt_iP_i\right)
 =\sum_{i,j=1}^rt_it_jV_2(P_i,P_j).
\tag{21}
\]
Every nonempty compact convex set in \(\mathbb R^2\), including a segment or a point, is a Hausdorff limit of convex polygons. Minkowski addition and area are continuous under Hausdorff convergence. Approximating all sets simultaneously in (21) proves (3) and (21) for arbitrary nonempty compact convex sets.

For \(z\notin Z_\cap\), equation (2) makes the reweighted fiber empty. For \(z\in Z_\cap\), apply (21) to the sets \(K_{1,z},\ldots,K_{r,z}\), and then sum over the finite set \(Z_\cap\). This gives
\[
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
 =\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}\sum_{i,j=1}^r
 \vartheta_i\vartheta_jV_2(K_{i,z},K_{j,z})
 =\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M
  \boldsymbol\vartheta,
\]
proving (5).

The quadratic form in (5) is continuous on \(\Delta_r\), and \(\Delta_r^\circ\) is dense in \(\Delta_r\). Thus (6) and (7) are equivalent. If \(x\in\mathbb R_+^r\setminus\{0\}\), put
\[
 \boldsymbol\vartheta:=\frac{x}{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x}\in\Delta_r.
\]
Then
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}(H\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf M)x
 =(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x)^2
 \left(H-\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M
 \boldsymbol\vartheta\right).
\tag{22}
\]
The case \(x=0\) is automatic. Hence (7) and (8) are equivalent. Taking \(\boldsymbol\vartheta=e_i\) in the continuous quadratic extension gives (9). Notice that positive reweightings keep exactly the common support even when \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\to e_i\); they do not recover cap fibers outside \(Z_\cap\). This proves the common-support qualification after (9).

For nonempty compact convex \(K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^2\), planar Brunn--Minkowski and (3) give, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 \operatorname{area}(K)+2tV_2(K,L)+t^2\operatorname{area}(L)
 =\operatorname{area}(K+tL)
 \ge\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{area}(K)}
 +t\sqrt{\operatorname{area}(L)}\right)^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 V_2(K,L)\ge
 \sqrt{\operatorname{area}(K)\operatorname{area}(L)}.
\tag{23}
\]
This also covers zero-area sets. Applying (23) fiberwise gives
\[
 M_{ij}\ge (\mathbf G_{\mathrm{Gram}})_{ij}.
\]
For \(i=j\), equation (3) gives
\[
 V_2(K_{i,z},K_{i,z})=\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z}),
\]
so equality holds on the diagonal. This proves (12) and then (13).

For every \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_r\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \left\|\sum_i\vartheta_i g_i\right\|_2
 \le\sum_i\vartheta_i\|g_i\|_2
 \le\max_i\|g_i\|_2.
\tag{24}
\]
The closed-simplex upper bound is attained at any vertex \(e_i\) maximizing \(\|g_i\|_2\), while the same value is approached from \(\Delta_r^\circ\). Since
\[
 \|g_i\|_2^2=W_\cap(A_i),
\]
this proves (15). Equation (16) follows because \(Z_\cap\) is only a subset of the lattice support of \(A_i\). Equations (13) and (15) give the remaining assertions in part 3, including (17).

Finally, under (18), the fiber \(\sum_i\vartheta_iK_{i,z}\) contains a translate of the planar zonotope
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r[0,\vartheta_id_{i,z}e_i].
\]
For vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\in\mathbb R^2\), the exact area formula
\[
 \operatorname{area}\left(\sum_i[0,v_i]\right)
 =\sum_{i<j}|\det(v_i,v_j)|
\tag{25}
\]
follows by induction: adding \([0,v_j]\) increases area by \(\|v_j\|_2\) times the width of the preceding zonotope perpendicular to \(v_j\), and that width is
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\frac{|\det(v_i,v_j)|}{\|v_j\|_2}
\]
when \(v_j\ne0\), with the zero-vector case trivial. Area monotonicity, (25), and summation over \(z\in S\) prove (19).

For the final specialization, accepted fact `6974f69acff32c8c` makes every \(F_{i,z}\) with \(s_{i,z}>0\) a nonempty compact convex subset of the line \(\{x:\langle w_i,x\rangle=\ell_i(z)\}\). It is therefore a point or segment parallel to \(e_i\). Also \(\mathsf M_{s_{i,z}}(C_z)\subseteq C_z\), because each metronoid point is a barycenter of a probability density supported on the convex set \(C_z\). Since every point of \(F_{i,z}\) lies on the cap boundary, \(F_{i,z}\subseteq K_{i,z}\). Thus (18) holds on every set (20), and (19) follows. If \(r=2\), the positive balanced relation between the two nonzero normals makes them antiparallel, proving the last calibration statement.
