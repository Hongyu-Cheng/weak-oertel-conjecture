## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be compact and convex with positive mixed volume $M=\sum_z|K_z|$. Let $\mu$ be normalized mixed volume, and choose an integer median base $z_0$. Split the measure at $z_0$ proportionally so that the two sides, each including its assigned central share, have mass $1/2$. Double those measures to obtain probability marginals $\alpha,\beta$ with $\alpha+\beta=2\mu$. For a legal pair $p=(i,x),q=(j,y)$, put
$$
T(p,q)=\begin{cases}
[(j-z_0)x+(z_0-i)y]/(j-i),&i<j,\\
(x+y)/2,&i=j=z_0 .
\end{cases}
$$
By convexity this is in the actual fiber $K_{z_0}$.

Let $(\sigma_*,y_*)$ maximize closed-halfspace depth jointly over the median couplings and centers, with value $v$. Fix $h,b\in\mathbb R^d$ and let $Q$ translate the continuous coordinate at base $z$ by $h+(z-z_0)b$. Let $\theta\le\sigma_*$ be any subcoupling whose translated marginals are dominated by $\alpha,\beta$. Put $\xi=\sigma_*-\theta$ and let $\Pi_h$ couple the two deficits after subtracting the translated marginals of $\theta$ from $\alpha,\beta$. For each $\varepsilon>0$ some finite unit normals $u_i$ and weights $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, satisfy
$$
\max_{\kappa\in\Pi_h}\int c_\lambda^h\,d\kappa
+\sum_i\lambda_i[\sigma_*\{u_i\cdot(T-y_*)\ge0\}-v]
\le\int c_\lambda^0\,d\xi+\varepsilon,
$$
where $c_\lambda^h=\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf1_{\{u_i\cdot(T-y_*-h)\ge0\}}$. The bracketed old cap slacks are nonnegative. This includes zero residual mass.

## Proof

The two marginal deficits have common mass $1-\theta(\mathrm{total})$, and their coupling set is nonempty, weakly compact and convex by the compact endpoint supports and the closed marginal constraints. In the zero-mass case it contains just the zero measure. The translated retained law plus any residual coupling $\kappa$ is feasible. The weights in $T$ have base mean $z_0$, including the central-central case, so the translated retained midpoint equals its original midpoint plus $h$. Hence in direction $u$ the new cap mass through $y_*+h$ is
$$
b_u+\int c_u^h\,d\kappa,\qquad
b_u=\theta\{u\cdot(T-y_*)\ge0\},\quad
c_u^h=\mathbf1_{\{u\cdot(T-y_*-h)\ge0\}}.
$$
Each such new law has depth at the displaced center at most $v$ by joint optimality. The constraints $b_u+\int c_u^h\,d\kappa\ge v+\varepsilon$ are closed by continuity of $T$ on the clopen base-pair strata and upper semicontinuity of closed-cap masses. Their full intersection is empty. Compactness yields a finite subfamily with empty intersection. Its optimized minimum is attained and is strictly less than $v+\varepsilon$.

For this finite subfamily the downward payoff set consists of vectors lying coordinatewise below the cap values $b_{u_i}+\int c_i^h\,d\kappa$ of some $\kappa\in\Pi_h$. This set is closed by compactness and upper semicontinuity, and convex by affinity. Its largest diagonal point has common coordinate the optimized minimum. A supporting hyperplane there has nonnegative coefficients by downward closure, and they can be normalized to sum to one. For these $\lambda_i$ the largest weighted cap value is at most that diagonal value. Substituting
$b_{u_i}=\sigma_*\{u_i\cdot(T-y_*)\ge0\}-\int c_i^0\,d\xi$
and rearranging gives the inequality. The old cap slacks are nonnegative by the depth at $y_*$. The proof uses closed indicators throughout, so atoms on either cut cause no loss in the aligned retained mass.
