## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be a convex body with $0$ in its interior. Let $a$ be its positive centroid-projective parameter, so $T_a(x)=x/(1+a\cdot x)$ maps $K$ to a body $K'$ of centroid zero. Write $\delta=\min_K(1+a\cdot x)>0$, and let $f$ equal $t^{-d}$ above $\delta$ and its tangent at $\delta$ below. If $g_K$ is the gauge of $K$, then $K_\varepsilon=\{x:g_K(x)+\varepsilon f(1+a\cdot x)\le1\}$ is a nested convex erosion with $0$ in its interior for all small $\varepsilon\ge0$. For every closed halfspace $H$ through $0$,
$$
|K\cap H|-|K_\varepsilon\cap H|=\varepsilon d|K'\cap H|+O(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad |K|-|K_\varepsilon|=\varepsilon D+O(\varepsilon^2),\quad D=d|K'|.
$$
The constants are uniform in $H$. Each closed halfspace containing $0$ loses at least $\varepsilon\Gamma_d D-O(\varepsilon^2)$ with the same uniformity, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. At every regular old boundary point $x$, its first inward normal speed is $h_K(n_K(x))f(1+a\cdot x)$.

## Proof

The positive centroid-projective parameter exists by d90ba05577427854. The tangent extension is positive and convex since its derivatives match at $\delta$ and the inverse-power derivative increases thereafter. Thus the defining sublevel sets are convex, nested inside $K$, and contain $0$ in their interior for small $\varepsilon$ since $\varepsilon f(1)<1$.

Let $\rho(u)$ be the old radial length in a unit direction $u$. Its perturbed radial endpoint satisfies
$$
r_\varepsilon/\rho+\varepsilon f(1+a\cdot r_\varepsilon u)=1.
$$
Indeed the expression on the left is continuous, is below $1$ at the origin and is at least $1$ at the old endpoint. Its derivative in $r$ on the old radial interval is uniformly positive for small $\varepsilon$, since $\rho$ is bounded above and $f'$ is bounded on the compact denominator range. Boundedness of $f$ gives $r_\varepsilon=\rho+O(\varepsilon)$ uniformly. The Lipschitz bound on $f$ then yields $r_\varepsilon=\rho-\varepsilon\rho f(1+a\cdot \rho u)+O(\varepsilon^2)$ uniformly. Raising to the $d$th power and integrating the radial volume element $r^{d-1}dr$ over the hemisphere of $H$ gives the claimed first coefficient. It equals $d|K'\cap H|$ because the image radial length is $\rho/(1+a\cdot \rho u)$. The same argument on the whole sphere gives $D$. In $d=1$ the sphere integral is a sum over the two rays. The quadratic error is uniform by the finite measure of the entire sphere. Grünbaum's centroid bound 6769574fc1bd9f8d applied to $K'$ gives the central lower bound. A containing halfspace contains its parallel central subcap, and the loss is the volume of its intersection with $K\setminus K_\varepsilon$. At a regular boundary point $x$ the first radial displacement projected onto its unit normal is $(x\cdot n_K(x))f(1+a\cdot x)=h_K(n_K(x))f(1+a\cdot x)$, as asserted.
