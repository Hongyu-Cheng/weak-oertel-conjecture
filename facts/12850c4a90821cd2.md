## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(k\ge1\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_k&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times[-1,1]^k)\cup\{(2,\mathbf0)\}\bigr),\\
D_k&=P_k\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}
 =\bigl(\{0\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^k\bigr)
   \cup\{(1,\mathbf0),(2,\mathbf0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are the polytope \(P\) and support \(D\) of the assignment. The support \(A\) in accepted fact \(\texttt{bf1ff94487296374}\) is here \(D_k\); its intrinsic rank is \(k+1\). Use that fact's entropy capacity \(\mathsf C_p\), capacity second moment \(\mathcal B_2(p)\), and mean-constrained entropy minimizer \(r_{\mathbf j}\). Thus \(r_{\mathbf j}\) is the row denoted \(\nu_{\mathbf j}^*\) in the assignment.

For arbitrary \(\tau\in\mathbb R\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k)\in\mathbb R^k\), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
t&=e^\tau,&
A_0&=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+2\cosh\theta_i),&
Z&=A_0+t+t^2,\\
p(x,\mathbf y)&=\frac{e^{\tau x+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}}{Z}
\qquad((x,\mathbf y)\in D_k),\\
a_{\mathrm{base}}&=3^k,&
\tau_*&=\tfrac12\log a_{\mathrm{base}},&
A_{\mathrm{mid}}&=(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1)^2,\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}&=\frac{\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}}
                              {2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1},&
B_0&=\prod_{i=1}^k(9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the accepted fact's source tilt is \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\tau,\boldsymbol\theta)\); \(\tau\) is a scalar, not its depth function \(\tau_p\). The quantities \(A_{\mathrm{mid}}\), \(\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\), and \(\tau_*\) are exactly the assignment's scalar \(A\), \(\alpha\), and \(\tau^*\), respectively; the subscripts distinguish them from the accepted fact's support and conversion constants.

For every root \(\mathbf j\in D_k\), define its likelihood variance and its capacity-second-moment weight by

\[
V_p(\mathbf j)=\operatorname{Var}_{r_{\mathbf j}}
  \left[\log\frac{r_{\mathbf j}(X,\mathbf Y)}{p(X,\mathbf Y)}\right],
\qquad
\omega_p(\mathbf j)=\frac{p(\mathbf j)\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2}
                         {\mathcal B_2(p)}.
\]

Thus \(V_p(\mathbf j)\) is the assignment's row quantity \(V\) at root \(\mathbf j\), and \(\omega_p\) is its root probability law \(\omega\). The logarithm is evaluated only on the positive support of its row. The following formulas hold for every tilt.

For a bottom root \((0,\mathbf y)\), write \(s(\mathbf y)=|\{i:y_i=0\}|\). The row \(r_{(0,\mathbf y)}\) is uniform on the \(3^{s(\mathbf y)}\) bottom points whose coordinates with \(y_i\ne0\) equal \(y_i\). Its other coordinates are independent uniform variables on \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and

\[
\mathsf C_p(0,\mathbf y)=\frac{e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}
                              3^{s(\mathbf y)}}{Z},
\qquad
V_p(0,\mathbf y)=\frac23\sum_{i:y_i=0}\theta_i^2.
\]

The middle row has the following individual atom probabilities, independently of the source tilt:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_{(1,\mathbf0)}(0,\mathbf y)
  &=\frac{1}{2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}}
       &&(\mathbf y\in\{-1,0,1\}^k),\\
r_{(1,\mathbf0)}(1,\mathbf0)
  &=\frac{\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}}
          {2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}},\\
r_{(1,\mathbf0)}(2,\mathbf0)
  &=\frac{a_{\mathrm{base}}}
          {2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Under this row,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}},&
\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)&=\frac{2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}}{3},\\
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y_i)&=0,&
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)&=0\quad(i\ne j).
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mathsf C_p(1,\mathbf0)=\frac{t(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1)}{Z},
\qquad
V_p(1,\mathbf0)=2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}(\tau_*-\tau)^2
             +\frac{2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}}{3}\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i^2.
\]

The apex row is \(r_{(2,\mathbf0)}=\delta_{(2,\mathbf0)}\), so \(\mathsf C_p(2,\mathbf0)=t^2/Z\) and \(V_p(2,\mathbf0)=0\).

In particular, the capacity second moment and middle-row weight are exactly

\[
\mathcal B_2(p)=\frac{B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6}{Z^3},
\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}:=\omega_p(1,\mathbf0)
 =\frac{A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3}{B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6}.
\]

The entire weighted likelihood price has the exact expression

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_k}\omega_p(\mathbf j)V_p(\mathbf j)
&=\frac{B_0}{B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6}
      \sum_{i=1}^k\frac{6\theta_i^2}{9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)}\\
&\quad+\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}
       \left[2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}(\tau_*-\tau)^2
        +\frac{2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}}{3}\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Set \(c_{\mathrm{dec}}=\tfrac12\log(11/9)>0\) and \(d_{\mathrm{shift}}=\tfrac12\log(27/11)>0\), the assignment's constants \(c,d\), respectively. Take the absolute price constant \(K_{\mathrm{price}}\), denoted \(C\) in the assignment, to be

\[
K_{\mathrm{price}}=\frac23+\frac{24}{e^2}
                   +\frac{3d_{\mathrm{shift}}^2}{c_{\mathrm{dec}}e}
                   +\frac{16\sqrt{11}}{3e^2}.
\]

Then, for every \(k\ge1\), every \(\tau\), and every \(\boldsymbol\theta\),

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega_p}V_p
 =\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_k}\omega_p(\mathbf j)V_p(\mathbf j)
 \le K_{\mathrm{price}}k\le K_{\mathrm{price}}(k+1).
\]

Nevertheless the middle row's self-varentropy is exactly
\(\operatorname{Var}_{r_{(1,\mathbf0)}}[\log r_{(1,\mathbf0)}(X,\mathbf Y)]
 =\tfrac12\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}k^2(\log3)^2\).
At \(\tau=0\), \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\), this is also its likelihood variance, while its capacity weight is
\(A_{\mathrm{mid}}/(11^k+A_{\mathrm{mid}}+1)\le9(3/11)^k\).

## Proof

The horizontal section of \(P_k\) at coordinate \(x\in[0,2]\) is \((1-x/2)[-1,1]^k\). This follows directly by expressing a point as a convex combination of a bottom point and the apex. Thus the only integer horizontal coordinates are \(0,1,2\); their integer sections are respectively \(\{-1,0,1\}^k\), \(\{\mathbf0\}\), and \(\{\mathbf0\}\). This proves the displayed description of \(D_k\), its completeness, and its rank \(k+1\). Summing the source numerator over these sections gives \(Z=A_0+t+t^2\), so \(p\) is a strictly positive probability law on this support for every stated tilt.

Apply the entropy-capacity identity, including its boundary-root assertion, from accepted fact \(\texttt{bf1ff94487296374}\). For any probability law \(\nu\) on \(D_k\) with mean \(\mathbf j=(j_x,\mathbf j_y)\), log-affinity gives

\[
D(\nu\|p)=-H(\nu)-\tau j_x
                       -\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j_y+\log Z.
\]

Hence its entropy minimizer is its unique maximum-Shannon-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) law, independently of the source tilt, and
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=e^{H(r_{\mathbf j})+\tau j_x+
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j_y}/Z\).
Below all maximum-entropy claims can be checked by nonnegativity of relative entropy: for probability laws \(q,r\) with \(r\) positive on the support of \(q\), \(D(q\|r)\ge0\), with equality only for \(q=r\). Indeed, on positive atoms of \(q\), \(-\log(r/q)\ge1-r/q\); summing gives \(D(q\|r)\ge1-\sum_{q>0}r\ge0\), and the equality condition follows from the strict equality condition in this logarithm inequality.

Fix a bottom root \((0,\mathbf y)\). Every feasible mean-root law has \(X=0\) almost surely because \(X\ge0\). On the bottom section, if \(y_i=1\) or \(y_i=-1\), the corresponding coordinate must equal that endpoint almost surely. Thus every feasible law is supported on the \(3^{s(\mathbf y)}\) points stated above. The uniform law on this set has mean \((0,\mathbf y)\). For any law on this set, relative entropy with respect to the uniform law is \(s(\mathbf y)\log3-H(\nu)\); nonnegativity proves that the uniform law is the unique maximum-entropy law. It has entropy \(s(\mathbf y)\log3\), proving its capacity formula. On its support the log-likelihood ratio is a constant minus \(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Y\). The free coordinates are independent uniform ternary coordinates, each with variance \(2/3\), and the other coordinates are constant. This proves the bottom variance formula.

For the middle root, define \(r^*\) on all of \(D_k\) with numerator \(e^{\tau_*x}\) and normalizer \(2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}\). Its bottom and apex total masses are both \(\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\), and its middle mass is \(1-2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}=1/(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1)\). Therefore \(\mathbb E_{r^*}X=1\). Its bottom conditional law is uniform on the ternary cube, so \(\mathbb E_{r^*}\mathbf Y=\mathbf0\). Thus \(r^*\) is feasible. Every law \(\nu\) with this mean satisfies

\[
D(\nu\|r^*)=-H(\nu)-\tau_*
                   +\log(2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}).
\]

Nonnegativity, with its equality condition, proves that \(r^*=r_{(1,\mathbf0)}\). It also gives

\[
H(r^*)=\log(2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}})-\tau_*
      =\log(2\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}+1),
\]

which proves the middle capacity formula. The slice probabilities yield
\(\mathbb E_{r^*}X^2=4\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}+(1-2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}})
 =1+2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\).
Only the bottom slice has nonzero \(\mathbf Y\); there its coordinates are independent, centered, and have variance \(2/3\). Thus the stated variances and covariances follow, including \(\mathbb E_{r^*}XY_i=0\). The log-likelihood ratio is

\[
\log\frac{r^*(X,\mathbf Y)}{p(X,\mathbf Y)}
 =(\tau_*-\tau)X-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf Y
   +\log Z-\log(2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}).
\]

Its variance, using these covariances, is precisely the stated \(V_p(1,\mathbf0)\). At the apex, \(X\le2\) and mean \(2\) force \(X=2\) almost surely; the only such support point is the apex. Its entropy is zero, and its likelihood ratio is constant. This proves all row and capacity formulas, including the boundary cases.

For a bottom root, its unnormalized capacity weight is
\(p(0,\mathbf y)\mathsf C_p(0,\mathbf y)^2
 =Z^{-3}e^{3\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}9^{s(\mathbf y)}\).
Summing factors coordinatewise gives the bottom contribution \(Z^{-3}B_0\) to \(\mathcal B_2(p)\). The middle and apex contributions are \(Z^{-3}A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3\) and \(Z^{-3}t^6\). This proves the capacity-second-moment formula and the middle weight. In fact all normalized row weights are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_p(0,\mathbf y)
 &=\frac{e^{3\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}9^{s(\mathbf y)}}
          {B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6},\\
\omega_p(1,\mathbf0)
 &=\frac{A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3}{B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6},\\
\omega_p(2,\mathbf0)
 &=\frac{t^6}{B_0+A_{\mathrm{mid}}t^3+t^6}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Under the bottom weights normalized by their own total, the coordinates are independent and a zero in coordinate \(i\) has probability \(9/(9+2\cosh(3\theta_i))\). Multiplying by the bottom variance and by the bottom total normalized weight gives the exact bottom price stated in the theorem. Adding the middle price and the zero apex price proves the exact formula for the entire price.

We now bound this formula with constants independent of all tilts and of \(k\). First, \(\cosh(3\theta_i)\ge1+(9/2)\theta_i^2\), for example by the nonnegative terms of its power series. Consequently,

\[
\frac{6\theta_i^2}{9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)}
 \le\frac{6\theta_i^2}{11+9\theta_i^2}\le\frac23.
\]

The bottom total normalized weight is at most one, so the entire bottom price is at most \(2k/3\).

To handle the middle weight, set

\[
z=3\tau-\tfrac12\log B_0,
\qquad
b_{\mathrm{tilt}}=\log B_0-k\log11\ge0,
\qquad
\mathcal R=\frac{A_{\mathrm{mid}}}{\sqrt{B_0}}.
\]

The quantity \(b_{\mathrm{tilt}}\) is the assignment's \(b\), not the accepted fact's count of selected factors. The ratio \(\mathcal R\) is exactly the scalar denoted \(R\) in the assignment, not the accepted fact's total product rank. Dividing the numerator and denominator of \(\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\) by \(\sqrt{B_0}\,t^3\) gives

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}=
 \frac{\mathcal R}{e^{-z}+\mathcal R+e^z}
 =\frac{\mathcal R}{2\cosh z+\mathcal R}
 \le\mathcal R e^{-|z|}.
\]

Since \(\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}}\ge1\), one has
\(A_{\mathrm{mid}}\le9a_{\mathrm{base}}\). Using
\(B_0=11^ke^{b_{\mathrm{tilt}}}\) therefore yields

\[
\mathcal R\le9\exp(-c_{\mathrm{dec}}k-b_{\mathrm{tilt}}/2),
\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\le9\exp(-c_{\mathrm{dec}}k-b_{\mathrm{tilt}}/2-|z|).
\]

The definitions also give the exact identity
\(\tau-\tau_*=(z+b_{\mathrm{tilt}}/2-d_{\mathrm{shift}}k)/3\).
The elementary inequality \((u+v+w)^2\le3(u^2+v^2+w^2)\), applied with \(u=z\), \(v=b_{\mathrm{tilt}}/2\), \(w=-d_{\mathrm{shift}}k\), now gives

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}(\tau-\tau_*)^2
 \le3e^{-c_{\mathrm{dec}}k-b_{\mathrm{tilt}}/2-|z|}
       \left(z^2+\frac{b_{\mathrm{tilt}}^2}{4}+d_{\mathrm{shift}}^2k^2\right).
\]

For \(\lambda>0\), differentiation of \(u^2e^{-\lambda u}\) on \(u\ge0\) gives maximum \(4/(\lambda^2e^2)\) at \(u=2/\lambda\). Differentiation of \(u e^{-c_{\mathrm{dec}}u}\) gives maximum \(1/(c_{\mathrm{dec}}e)\). In particular,

\[
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}z^2e^{-|z|}=\frac4{e^2},
\qquad
\sup_{v\ge0}v^2e^{-v/2}=\frac{16}{e^2},
\qquad
k^2e^{-c_{\mathrm{dec}}k}\le\frac{k}{c_{\mathrm{dec}}e}.
\]

All remaining exponential factors in the preceding variance bound are at most one. Hence

\[
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}(\tau-\tau_*)^2
 \le\frac{24}{e^2}+\frac{3d_{\mathrm{shift}}^2}{c_{\mathrm{dec}}e}\,k
 \le\left(\frac{24}{e^2}
             +\frac{3d_{\mathrm{shift}}^2}{c_{\mathrm{dec}}e}\right)k,
\]

where the last step uses \(k\ge1\).

For the transverse source tilt, put
\(b_i=\log((9+2\cosh(3\theta_i))/11)\ge0\), so \(b_{\mathrm{tilt}}=\sum_i b_i\).
The inequality \(9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)\ge e^{3|\theta_i|}\) gives

\[
\theta_i^2e^{-b_i/2}
 =\frac{\sqrt{11}\,\theta_i^2}
         {\sqrt{9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)}}
 \le\sqrt{11}\,\theta_i^2e^{-3|\theta_i|/2}
 \le\frac{16\sqrt{11}}{9e^2}.
\]

In the last step use the same differentiated maximum with \(\lambda=3/2\). Separating each term's factor \(e^{-b_i/2}\), and discarding only factors at most one, proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i^2
&\le9e^{-c_{\mathrm{dec}}k-|z|}\sum_{i=1}^k
        \theta_i^2e^{-b_i/2}
                     \prod_{h\ne i}e^{-b_h/2}\\
&\le\frac{16\sqrt{11}}{e^2}\,k.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally \(2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}<1\). The middle price is therefore at most

\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}V_p(1,\mathbf0)
&\le\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}(\tau-\tau_*)^2
      +\frac13\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i^2\\
&\le\left(\frac{24}{e^2}
                        +\frac{3d_{\mathrm{shift}}^2}{c_{\mathrm{dec}}e}
                        +\frac{16\sqrt{11}}{3e^2}\right)k.
\end{aligned}
\]

Adding the bottom bound proves \(\mathbb E_{\omega_p}V_p\le K_{\mathrm{price}}k\), and hence the asserted linear-rank bound \(K_{\mathrm{price}}(k+1)\).

For the final self-varentropy assertion, \(\log r^*(X,\mathbf Y)=\tau_*X-\log(2a_{\mathrm{base}}+\sqrt{a_{\mathrm{base}}})\). Its variance is \(2\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\tau_*^2=\tfrac12\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}k^2(\log3)^2\). Since \(\alpha_{\mathrm{mid}}\to1/2\), this variance divided by \(k^2\) tends to \((\log3)^2/4>0\). At zero source tilt, \(\log p\) is constant on the whole support, so this equals the middle likelihood variance. Also \(B_0=11^k\) and \(t=1\), giving its displayed capacity weight. The bound \(A_{\mathrm{mid}}\le9\cdot3^k\) and the denominator bound \(11^k+A_{\mathrm{mid}}+1\ge11^k\) prove \(\omega_{\mathrm{mid}}\le9(3/11)^k\). Thus this quadratic row cost is not a counterexample to the averaged bound.
