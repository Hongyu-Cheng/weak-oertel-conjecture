## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, and fix an actual support-minimal balanced negative cap circuit of cardinality three in the notation of accepted facts `2021348e0b2d8c9f` and `6974f69acff32c8c`.  Write its individual caps as \(A_1,A_2,A_3\), put
\[
 K_{i,z}:=(A_i)_z,
 \qquad
 Z_\cap:=\{z:K_{i,z}\ne\varnothing\text{ for }i=1,2,3\},
\tag{1}
\]
and define
\[
 a_{i,z}:=\operatorname{area}(K_{i,z}),
 \qquad
 M_{ij}:=\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}V_2(K_{i,z},K_{j,z}).
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(\mathbf M=(M_{ij})_{i,j=1}^3\).  For every strictly positive probability vector \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_3^\circ\), the corresponding positive same-base reweighting satisfies
\[
 W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})
 =\boldsymbol\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\mathbf M\boldsymbol\vartheta.
\tag{3}
\]

Put
\[
 W:=W(C),
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\]
and suppose that, for some \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\le H:=(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\le\frac{\eta}{6}W.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(1\le i\le3\), define the common-support cap mass and its gap by
\[
 \mu_i:=M_{ii}=\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}a_{i,z},
 \qquad
 \delta_i:=H-\mu_i.
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(\delta_i\ge0\).  For \(i<j\), define the actual fiberwise Gram entry and the canonical mixed-area excess by
\[
 \Gamma_{ij}:=\sum_{z\in Z_\cap}\sqrt{a_{i,z}a_{j,z}},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{ij}:=M_{ij}-\Gamma_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
Define the pair slack
\[
 \beta_{ij}
 :=H-M_{ij}+\sqrt{\delta_i\delta_j}
 =H-\Gamma_{ij}-\mathcal E_{ij}
   +\sqrt{\delta_i\delta_j}.
\tag{7}
\]
When all three \(\beta_{ij}\) are nonnegative, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Xi:= {}&
 \sqrt{\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3}
 +(H-\Gamma_{12}-\mathcal E_{12})\sqrt{\delta_3}
 +(H-\Gamma_{13}-\mathcal E_{13})\sqrt{\delta_2}\\
 &+(H-\Gamma_{23}-\mathcal E_{23})\sqrt{\delta_1}
 +\sqrt{2\beta_{12}\beta_{13}\beta_{23}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Then the following are equivalent.

1. There is \(\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\Delta_3^\circ\) such that
   \[
   W(B_{\boldsymbol\vartheta})>H.
   \tag{9}
   \]

2. The symmetric matrix
   \[
   \mathbf A:=H\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf M
   \tag{10}
   \]
   is not copositive.

3. At least one of the following two alternatives holds:

   (a) for some \(i<j\),
   \[
   \beta_{ij}<0,
   \tag{11}
   \]
   equivalently,
   \[
   \mathcal E_{ij}
   >H+\sqrt{\delta_i\delta_j}-\Gamma_{ij};
   \tag{12}
   \]

   (b) all three \(\beta_{ij}\) are nonnegative and
   \[
   \Xi<0.
   \tag{13}
   \]

If (11) holds, a two-cap mixture on the \(ij\)-edge already has value greater than \(H\), and it can be approximated by vectors in \(\Delta_3^\circ\) while preserving the strict inequality.  If all pair slacks are nonnegative and (13) holds, every noncopositive witness uses all three coordinates, so the witnessing probability vector is already strictly positive.

The excess in (6) is exactly the one defined in accepted fact `2021348e0b2d8c9f`.  In contrast, the different algebraic quantity
\[
 M_{ij}-\sqrt{\mu_i\mu_j}
 =\mathcal E_{ij}
  -\left(\sqrt{\mu_i\mu_j}-\Gamma_{ij}\right)
\tag{14}
\]
has no asserted sign and is not used in the criterion.  No conclusion that (11) or (13) follows from (4) alone is asserted here; deriving one of these exact inequalities from the common-body geometry remains the geometric step.

## Proof

Accepted fact `2021348e0b2d8c9f` gives (1)-(3), the diagonal identity in (5), and the decomposition
\[
 M_{ij}=\Gamma_{ij}+\mathcal E_{ij},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{ij}\ge0
 \quad(i<j).
\tag{15}
\]
The common-support mass \(\mu_i\) is at most the full cap mass \(W(A_i)\).  Every \(A_i\) belongs to the descendant collection used in accepted fact `6974f69acff32c8c`, so (4) gives
\[
 \mu_i\le W(A_i)\le R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\le H.
\tag{16}
\]
This proves \(\delta_i\ge0\).  Equation (14) follows from (5), (6), and (15), and no sign conclusion is drawn from it.

By (3) and density of \(\Delta_3^\circ\) in the closed simplex \(\Delta_3\), (9) holds if and only if there is a nonzero \(x\in\mathbb R_+^3\) with
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}\mathbf A x<0.
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, normalize such a vector by \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x\).  If it has a zero coordinate, strictness in (17) persists after a sufficiently small perturbation toward the simplex interior.  Thus assertions 1 and 2 are equivalent.

We prove the exact three-by-three copositivity criterion used for assertion 3.  Let
\[
 \mathbf Q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 d_1&q_{12}&q_{13}\\
 q_{12}&d_2&q_{23}\\
 q_{13}&q_{23}&d_3
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d_i\ge0,
\tag{18}
\]
and put
\[
 \widehat q_{ij}:=q_{ij}+\sqrt{d_id_j}.
\tag{19}
\]
We claim that \(\mathbf Q\) is copositive if and only if
\[
 \widehat q_{12},\widehat q_{13},\widehat q_{23}\ge0
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 0\le {}&
 \sqrt{d_1d_2d_3}
 +q_{12}\sqrt{d_3}
 +q_{13}\sqrt{d_2}
 +q_{23}\sqrt{d_1}
 +\sqrt{2\widehat q_{12}\widehat q_{13}\widehat q_{23}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

First suppose \(d_1d_2d_3>0\).  Positive diagonal rescaling reduces (18) to a matrix with diagonal entries one and off-diagonal entries
\[
 c_{ij}:=\frac{q_{ij}}{\sqrt{d_id_j}}.
\tag{22}
\]
Its restriction to the \(ij\)-coordinate plane is copositive exactly when \(c_{ij}\ge-1\), which is (20).  On the simplex \(x_1+x_2+x_3=1\), set
\[
 p:=1-c_{12},
 \qquad
 q:=1-c_{13},
 \qquad
 r:=1-c_{23}.
\tag{23}
\]
The rescaled quadratic form is
\[
 1-2F(x),
 \qquad
 F(x):=p x_1x_2+q x_1x_3+r x_2x_3.
\tag{24}
\]
The pair conditions give \(p,q,r\le2\).  Every boundary value of \(F\) is at most \(1/2\).

We also have \(F\le1/2\) throughout the simplex whenever
\[
 p+q+r\le4.
\tag{25}
\]
To see this, order the three products
\(x_1x_2,x_1x_3,x_2x_3\) as \(u_1\ge u_2\ge u_3\ge0\).  Under \(p,q,r\le2\) and (25), the sum of the positive parts of \(p,q,r\) is at most four: if all three are nonnegative this is (25), while if one is negative there are at most two positive entries, each at most two.  Hence
\[
 F(x)\le2u_1+2u_2.
\]
Any two of the three pair products share one coordinate, so
\[
 u_1+u_2=t(1-t)\le\frac14
\]
for that shared coordinate \(t\in[0,1]\).  This proves the claim.

Consequently, a value \(F>1/2\) can occur only when
\[
 p+q+r>4.
\tag{26}
\]
Under (26) and \(p,q,r\le2\), all three numbers are positive and satisfy the strict triangle inequalities.  Put
\[
 D:=2(pq+pr+qr)-(p^2+q^2+r^2)>0
\tag{27}
\]
and
\[
 x_1:=\frac{r(p+q-r)}D,
 \quad
 x_2:=\frac{q(p+r-q)}D,
 \quad
 x_3:=\frac{p(q+r-p)}D.
\tag{28}
\]
These coordinates are strictly positive and sum to one.  Direct substitution gives
\[
 F(x)=\frac{pqr}{D}.
\tag{29}
\]
The identity
\[
 (p+q+r-4)^2
 -2(2-p)(2-q)(2-r)
 =2pqr-D
\tag{30}
\]
shows that the value in (29) is greater than \(1/2\) exactly when
\[
 4-(p+q+r)
 +\sqrt{2(2-p)(2-q)(2-r)}<0.
\tag{31}
\]
Conversely, if the quadratic form has a negative value, then the maximum of \(F\) is greater than \(1/2\).  It cannot lie on the boundary and, by (25), (26) holds.  The interior Lagrange equations have the unique solution (28), so (29)-(31) follow.  Thus the rescaled matrix is copositive exactly when
\[
 1+c_{12}+c_{13}+c_{23}
 +\sqrt{2(1+c_{12})(1+c_{13})(1+c_{23})}
 \ge0.
\tag{32}
\]
Multiplying (32) by \(\sqrt{d_1d_2d_3}\) yields (21), and the three pair conditions become (20).

Now suppose at least one diagonal entry is zero.  If, for example, \(d_1=0\), conditions (20) give
\[
 q_{12}\ge0,
 \qquad
 q_{13}\ge0.
\tag{33}
\]
Every term involving \(x_1\ge0\) is then nonnegative.  The remaining two-coordinate block is copositive exactly when
\[
 q_{23}+\sqrt{d_2d_3}\ge0,
\tag{34}
\]
which is the remaining condition in (20), because
\[
 d_2x_2^2+2q_{23}x_2x_3+d_3x_3^2
 =(\sqrt{d_2}x_2-\sqrt{d_3}x_3)^2
 +2(q_{23}+\sqrt{d_2d_3})x_2x_3.
\tag{35}
\]
In the same case, the left side of (21) is
\[
 q_{12}\sqrt{d_3}
 +q_{13}\sqrt{d_2}
 +\sqrt{2q_{12}q_{13}
 (q_{23}+\sqrt{d_2d_3})},
\tag{36}
\]
which is automatically nonnegative under (20).  The cases \(d_2=0\) or \(d_3=0\), including two or three zero diagonal entries, are identical.  This proves (20)-(21) for all nonnegative diagonal entries.

Apply the criterion to \(\mathbf Q=\mathbf A\).  Equations (5), (7), and (10) give
\[
 d_i=\delta_i,
 \qquad
 q_{ij}=H-M_{ij}
 =H-\Gamma_{ij}-\mathcal E_{ij},
 \qquad
 \widehat q_{ij}=\beta_{ij}.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus failure of a pair condition is exactly (11), equivalently (12).  If all pair conditions hold, failure of (21) is exactly \(\Xi<0\).  This proves the equivalence of assertions 2 and 3.

If a pair condition fails, the two-coordinate restriction of \(\mathbf A\) has a strictly negative value; perturbing its normalized witness into \(\Delta_3^\circ\) preserves strictness.  If all pair conditions hold, every two-coordinate restriction is copositive, so any vector satisfying (17) has all three coordinates positive.  Normalizing it proves the final assertion.
