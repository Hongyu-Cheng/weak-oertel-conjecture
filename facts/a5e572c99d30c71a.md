## Statement

Let $k\ge1$, let $p_i>0$ for $1\le i\le k$, and let $Y_i,Y_i'$ be independent mean-one exponentials, with any finite number of additional independent mean-one exponentials of coefficient zero. Set $V=\sum_{i=1}^k p_i(Y_i-Y_i')$, and let $\mu_j$ denote each coordinate mean under the coarea conditional law at $V=0$. Then $\Pr(V\ge0)=1/2$, and
$$
\prod_j\mu_j\ge\frac14,\qquad \prod_{j\ne r}\mu_j\ge\frac14\quad\text{for every coordinate }r.
$$
Both inequalities are strict for $k\ge2$. For $k=1$, the active means are $1/2$ and the zero-coefficient means are one. Thus the full product and each omission of a zero-coefficient coordinate are $1/4$, and each omission of an active coordinate is $1/2$.

On a uniform $d$-simplex with these coefficients as the values of an affine functional on its $d+1$ vertices, let $b_j$ be the barycentric coordinates of the uniform section centroid at functional value zero. Then $\mu_j=d b_j$.

## Proof

In the active coordinates put $D_i=Y_i-Y_i'$ and $M_i=\min(Y_i,Y_i')$. On each half-plane of a pair the change of variables has Jacobian one, giving density $e^{-2M_i-|D_i|}$ on $M_i\ge0$. Thus $M_i$ is exponential of rate two, independent of $D_i$ with density $e^{-|t|}/2$. The linear conditioning involves only the differences. In the coarea integral one can therefore integrate out the minima as independent rate-two exponentials and integrate the difference density on $\sum_i p_iD_i=0$. Equivalently these formulas follow from the density of the independent differences and linear disintegration in their last coordinate. Simultaneous sign reversal preserves the difference law on that hyperplane and gives
$$
\mu_i=\mu_i'=\frac12+\frac12\mathbb E(|D_i|\mid V=0).
$$
The unconditional law of $V$ is symmetric and continuous, so $\Pr(V\ge0)=1/2$. The zero-coefficient variables retain mean one. For $k=1$ the conditional difference is identically zero, giving all asserted equality values.

Suppose $k\ge2$. The density $g_i$ of $\sum_{j\ne i}p_jD_j$ is continuous, everywhere positive, even, and nonincreasing on the positive half-line. Each scaled Laplace density has these properties. To check the monotonicity under convolution, represent each even nonincreasing density by its centered-interval level sets. The overlap of two such intervals is nonincreasing in the separation between centers, so integrating those overlaps proves the claim. The conditional density of $D_i$ is proportional to $e^{-|t|}g_i(p_it)$. Hence $|D_i|$ has the mean-one exponential law tilted by the positive nonincreasing function $w(t)=g_i(p_it)$. For independent mean-one exponentials $T,T'$,
$$
2\operatorname{Cov}(T,w(T))=\mathbb E[(T-T')(w(T)-w(T'))]<0.
$$
The strict sign follows because $w$ is continuous, positive, nonincreasing and integrable on the half-line, so it is nonconstant and the integrand is negative on a product of intervals of positive probability. Thus the tilted mean is less than one and is positive, giving $1/2<\mu_i=\mu_i'<1$.

On the active orthant section $\sum_i p_i(y_i-y_i')=0$, the unnormalized coarea integral with density $\exp[-a\sum_i(y_i+y_i')]$ is a positive finite constant times $a^{-(2k-1)}$ by dilation, for $a>0$. Differentiating its logarithm at $a=1$ gives $\sum_i(\mu_i+\mu_i')=2k-1$, or $\sum_i(1-\mu_i)=1/2$. Differentiation is valid by exponential decay on the nonnegative orthant. For numbers $\delta_i=1-\mu_i$ the elementary inequality $\prod_i(1-\delta_i)\ge1-\sum_i\delta_i$ follows by successive multiplication, with a strict inequality when two deficits are positive and their total is $1/2$. Squaring gives the bound on the full product. Removing any factor of size at most one preserves the bound.

For the simplex correspondence write each orthant coordinate as $y_j=R u_j$ with $R=\sum_jy_j$ and $\sum_ju_j=1$. In the $d$-dimensional homogeneous hyperplane $V=0$, the affine slice $R=1$ is the barycentric simplex section. At radius $R$ the slice measure scales by $R^{d-1}$ and the linear coarea factor for $R$ is constant. Thus the density on this cone factors into uniform affine slice measure in $u$ and the radial density proportional to $e^{-R}R^{d-1}$. The radial mean is $d$. Affine barycentric coordinates carry this uniform slice to the uniform section measure by a constant Jacobian, so $\mathbb E(y_j\mid V=0)=d\,b_j$. This includes a zero-dimensional section when $d=1$.
