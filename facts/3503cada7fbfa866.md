## Statement

For all $R>0$, $p,q\in[0,1]$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb R$, take $E_{w,\theta}$ and $F_{w,\theta}$ as in the exact two-tent cap menu. Then
$$
J=|[(E_{1,p}+E_{R,q})/2]\cap\{s\ge0\}|+F_{1,p}(\alpha)+F_{R,q}(-\alpha)\ge4/3.
$$
Equality is attained at $R=1,p=q=0,\alpha=0$.

## Proof

Write $Q$ for the midpoint upper cap and $P$ for the two endpoint tails. Independently of the parameters, $Q\ge4/9$: both zero caps have area at least $4/9$ by accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with weight identically one, and their midpoint lies in the middle cap and pays at least $4/9$ by planar Brunn–Minkowski.

### Status reduction

First take $0<p,q<1$. The endpoint sum is piecewise quadratic, continuously differentiable and equal to one outside a bounded interval, so it attains a minimum. Full caps pay at least one and hence are safe. Both caps cannot be empty because their thresholds sum to zero and the upper supports are positive. An empty transition opposite a proper cut has nonzero derivative, and the other nonproper cases have a full cap. Two increasing density pieces give a strictly concave quadratic. A mode against a strictly increasing piece has a strictly concave interval on the side where both densities increase, so if the first derivative is zero there can be no local minimum. Modes against falling pieces, including simultaneous modes, and the two falling pieces are covered by the same-batch falling-branch bound. A mixed quadratic with zero second derivative is either nonconstant affine or has paired mass one, as follows from the quadratic below with equal denominators. A negative second derivative cannot give an interior minimum. It suffices to examine strictly convex mixed stationary points.

Relabel and rescale area-preservingly so the increasing profile has width one and mode fraction $a$, and the falling profile has width $R$ and fall fraction $V/R^2$. Let $D=a-V$, $T=1-R+a-V/R$. If $x$ is the distance of the increasing threshold from its lower support, the falling threshold has distance $x-T/3$ from its upper support, because their thresholds sum to zero. The sum is $1-x^2/a+(x-T/3)^2/V$. At a strict interior minimum $D,T>0$ and the two distances are $aT/(3D)$ and $VT/(3D)$, so $P=1-T^2/(9D)$. The two mode constraints are $T\le3D$ and $T\le3D/R$. The formulas extend to $V=0$ as supporting-empty limits where $D>0$. The midpoint peak has $6H=2T+3(R-1)$. In the flat case $a=V$ the linear coefficient vanishes only for $T=0$, which gives $P=1$.

### Increasing profile no wider than the other

Let $R\ge1$, put $t=V/a\in[0,1)$, and $w=a(1+t)$. Now $T=1-R+a(1-t/R)>0$ requires $R<2$. The stronger mode constraint is automatic, since
$$
T-3a(1-t)/R=1-R+a[1+(2t-3)/R]
\le1-R+\max(0,1-1/R)<0
$$
for $R>1$, and for $R=1$ the difference is $-2a(1-t)$. The roof menu yields $m=(R+1)/2+\operatorname{dist}(w,[1,R^2])/(2R)$ for the total midpoint area. Since $H>0$, write $Q=m-Q_-$, with reflected support parameter $S_-=R+T/3$. The menu gives
$$
Q_-=\begin{cases}
S_-/2-a/4+(S_--a)^2/[4(R^2-at)],&w\le R^2,\\
S_-/(2R)-(1-at/R^2)/4+(S_--R+at/R)^2/(4a),&w\ge R^2.
\end{cases}
$$
For fixed $R,t$, $J=m-Q_-+P$ is concave in $a$ on the cells separated by $w=1,R^2$. Indeed a square of an affine function divided by a positive affine function is convex, as seen by differentiating twice, and the remaining terms are affine. At $T=0$ with positive denominators the value $P=1$ is safe. The other boundaries are $a=1$ and the two roof lines. On $w=1$, substitute $t=(1-a)/a$. The cap uses a square over $4(R^2-1+a)$ and $P$ uses a negative square over $9(2a-1)$, so concavity in $a$ still holds. At $a=1/2$ the value of $T$ is $-(2R-1)(R-1)/(2R)\le0$, and the feasible interval therefore has its ends at $T=0$ or $a=1$. On $w=R^2$, substitute $t=(R^2-a)/a$. The cap denominator is $4a$ and the negative square in $P$ has denominator $9(2a-R^2)$, so again $J$ is concave in $a$. At $a=R^2/2$, $T=(R-1)(R-2)/2\le0$, so the only ends are $T=0$ and $a=1$. At the singular corner $R=1,t\to1$ the loss $T^2/[9a(1-t)]$ tends uniformly to zero: $0<T\le a(1-t)$ for $R\ge1$, since $T-a(1-t)=(R-1)(at/R-1)\le0$.

For $a=1$ put $x=R-1\in[0,1)$, $y=1-t\in(0,1]$. Here $T>0$ means $y>x^2$. Substitution yields the numerator of $J-4/3$ over $36R^2(R^2-t)(1-t)$ for $w\le R^2$,
$$
N_1=(-6x-11)y^3+(3x^2+4x+15)y^2+(6x^5+33x^4+76x^3+66x^2+30x)y-4x^6-8x^5.
$$
Using $y\le1$ on the cubic and $y\ge x^2$ on the linear term gives $(3x^2-2x+4)y^2+6x^7+29x^6+68x^5+66x^4+30x^3>0$ as a lower bound. In the other cell the denominator is $36R^2(1-t)$ and the numerator is
$$
N_2=-4y^3-(4x^2+18x+7)y^2+(-x^4+17x^2+30x+15)y-4x^4.
$$
Here $y\le U=1-2x-x^2$. Writing $N_2=yB(y)-4x^4$, the quadratic $B$ decreases on $y\ge0$ and $B(U)=-x^4+10x^3+48x^2+42x+4\ge4x^2+4$. Thus $N_2\ge4y+4x^2(y-x^2)>0$.

### Wider increasing profile, with $H\ge0$

Now $0<R<1$ and $c=1-R$. The constraints are $0\le a\le1$, $0\le V\le R^2$, $3c/2\le T\le3D$, and $D\ge c/2>0$. Put $w=a+V$. By the cap menu, with the profiles exchanged and the coordinates inversely rescaled to width one when needed, the area is $m=(1+1/R)/2+\operatorname{dist}(w,[R^2,1])/(2R)$. The preceding formulas for $Q_-$ use $V$ in place of $at$, and the split remains at $w=R^2$. These formulas come from the reflected fall order, which compares the first fall fraction $a$ with $R^2-V$. On the lower cell $R^2-V>0$ since otherwise $w\le R^2$ forces $a=0$. On the other cell $a>0$. Thus $J=m-Q_-+P$ is jointly concave on each compact polygonal cell cut by $w=R^2,1$ and is bounded below by its smallest vertex value.

On $a=1$ the numerator is $N_2$ above with $x=R-1\in(-1,0)$ and $1-R^2\le y=1-V\le1$. Its second derivative in $y$ is $-2[12y+4x^2+18x+7]<0$, since the bracket is at least $-8x^2-6x+7>0$. At $V=0$ it is $R[-5R^3+20R^2-17R+6]>0$, with the bracket at least $15R^2-17R+6>0$. At $V=R^2$ it is $R(R-1)[R^4-13R^3-26R^2+14R-6]>0$, since the bracket is at most $-25R^2+14R-6<0$.

On $T=3D$ the first cut is at its mode and the other on its falling piece, covered by the same-batch falling bound. On $H=0$ one has $V=Ra-Rc/2$, $c/2\le a\le(1+R)/2$ and $P=1-c/[4(a+R/2)]$. The positive roof starts at the peak and gives $Q=V(2R+1)/(4R^2)+(1-a)/4$ if $w\ge1$, and $Q=(1-a)(R+2)/(4R)+V/(4R^2)$ otherwise. Thus $Q$ is piecewise affine and $P$ concave in $a$. The lower endpoint $a=c/2$ lies on $T=3D$. At the breakpoint $a=1-R/2$, feasible for $R\ge1/2$, the excess is $(9R^2-8R+3)/(24R)>0$. At $a=(1+R)/2$ the excess is $(18R^2+23R-11)/[24(2R+1)]$ for $R\ge1/2$, and $(-6R^3+11R^2+R+6)/[24R(2R+1)]$ otherwise. The first numerator is increasing from five and the second is positive since $6R^3\le3R^2$ in its range.

The other possible vertices are on $V=0$ or $V=R^2$ and one of the roof lines, outside the edges already checked. This leaves $(a,V)=(R^2,0)$, feasible only for $R\ge1/2$, and $(1-R^2,R^2)$, with $H\ge0$ only for $R\le1/2$. The first excess is $-[5R^4-4R^3+12R^2-22R+5]/(36R^2)>0$, because the bracket is convex and negative at $1/2$ and $1$. The second excess has positive denominator $36R(R-1)(2R^2-1)$ and numerator $6R^6+18R^5-29R^4-15R^3+42R^2-26R+6$. Substituting $z=2R$ gives Bernstein coefficients $(6,23/6,71/30,241/160,83/80,23/32,15/32)$ of degree six. Each basis function $\binom6j z^j(1-z)^{6-j}$ is nonnegative on $[0,1]$. This completes the vertex check.

### Wider increasing profile, with $H\le0$

The inequalities $T\le3D$ and $T\le3c/2$ become $c/2+V(3R-1)/(2R)\le a\le c/2+V/R$. Together with $0\le V\le R^2$ they give the triangle with vertices $E=(c/2,0)$, $F=((1+R)/2,R^2)$ and $G=((1-2R+3R^2)/2,R^2)$. Evaluating at its vertices shows $0<a<1$, $D\ge c^2/2>0$ and $T>0$ throughout. If $R\ge7/9$, then $P\ge1-T/3\ge(1+R)/2$ and $Q\ge4/9$ prove the result. If $R\le2/9$, the larger-to-smaller width ratio is at least $9/2$ and the same-batch wide estimate applies. It remains to treat $2/9\le R\le7/9$.

Set $L=1-a$, $S=1-T/3$ and $w=a+V$. The cap menu yields
$$
Q=\begin{cases}
S/2-L/4+(S-L)^2/(4V),&w\ge1,\\
S/(2R)-V/(4R^2)+(S-V/R)^2/(4L),&w\le1.
\end{cases}
$$
The denominators are positive on their respective closed cells because $L>0$ and $w\ge1$ implies $V\ge L$. In either cell $J$ is affine plus one positive perspective of the form $l(a,V)^2/m(a,V)$ with affine $l,m$, minus the convex perspective $T^2/(9D)$. At any interior point choose a nonzero direction satisfying $d l=(l/m)\,d m$, a single linear homogeneous equation in two variables. On the corresponding line the ratio $l/m$ is constant and the positive perspective becomes affine. On the maximal segment in the compact cell the sum is concave, so its value is at least the smaller endpoint value. This reduces the check to cell boundaries. Edge $EF$ has $H=0$, edge $EG$ has $T=3D$, and both have been handled. The only additional boundaries are $FG$ and the line $w=1$.

On $FG$ put $V=R^2$ and $a=(1-2R+3R^2)/2+3R(1-R)u/2$, $0\le u\le1$. In the $w\ge1$ cell the excess is $A(R,u)/[24(3Ru-R+1)]$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
A={}&18R^3u^3-39R^3u^2+18R^3u+3R^3-36R^2u^3+51R^2u^2+36R^2u-33R^2\\
&+18Ru^3-18Ru^2-24Ru+47R+6u^2-17.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $u\ge U(R)=(1+2R-5R^2)/[3R(1-R)]$. The derivative of $U$ is $-(3R^2-2R+1)/[3R^2(1-R)^2]<0$ and $U(1/2)=1$. Thus the feasible region lies in the three rectangles $[1/2,3/5]\times[5/9,1]$, $[3/5,5/8]\times[19/45,1]$, $[5/8,7/9]\times[0,1]$, using $U(3/5)=5/9$, $U(5/8)=19/45$.
In the $w\le1$ cell the excess is $B(R,u)/[24R(3R+1-3Ru)(3Ru-R+1)]$, with
$$
\begin{aligned}
B={}&-45R^5u^3+129R^5u^2-123R^5u+39R^5-9R^4u^3-48R^4u^2+135R^4u-78R^4\\
&+54R^3u^3-9R^3u^2-111R^3u+60R^3-54R^2u^2+81R^2u-28R^2-6Ru+R+6.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $u\le U(R)$ and $U(7/10)<0$, the feasible region lies in $[2/9,1/2]\times[0,1]$, $[1/2,3/5]\times[0,1]$, $[3/5,7/10]\times[0,5/9]$. The displayed excess denominators are positive on their feasible regions.

On $w=1$ one has $R\ge1/2$ because $a\le c/2+V/R$ gives $1=a+V\le c/2+R+R^2$. Put $D=2a-1>0$. Then
$$
Q=(1-T/3)^2/[2(1-D)],\qquad T=[(1-R)(2R-1)+(R+1)D]/(2R).
$$
The inequalities $T\le3D$ and $T\le3c/2$ give $c(2R-1)/(5R-1)\le D\le c$. Enlarge to this interval and write $D=c[(2R-1)/(5R-1)+(1-(2R-1)/(5R-1))v]$ for $0\le v\le1$. For positive $D$ the excess is $N/[36(1-a)(2a-1)]$, where
$$
N=(6a-5)T^2-6(2a-1)T+24a^2-18a+3=3cC(R,v)/[4(5R-1)^3]
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
C={}&27R^5v^3+234R^5v^2+576R^5v+288R^5-483R^4v^2-984R^4v-208R^4\\
&-54R^3v^3+276R^3v^2+1080R^3v-192R^3+54R^2v^2-648R^2v+240R^2\\
&+27Rv^3-78Rv^2+168Rv-64R-3v^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The polynomial check can include the corner $R=1/2,v=0$, which has $D=0$, since the excess formula is used only for $D>0$.

For the exact sign checks, on each rectangle substitute the affine coordinates $x,y\in[0,1]$ and write the resulting polynomial as $\sum c_{rs}x^r y^s$. Its Bernstein coefficient at $(i,j)$ of degrees $(m,n)$ is
$$
b_{ij}=\sum_{r\le i,\,s\le j} c_{rs}
\frac{\binom ir\binom js}{\binom mr\binom ns}.
$$
This identity follows by expanding the binomial formula for the Bernstein representation of each monomial. Applying the formula to $A$ of degrees $(3,3)$ on its three rectangles gives the respective minimum coefficients $8/81,863692/421875,111/512$. For $B$ of degrees $(5,3)$ on its three rectangles the minima are $5/2,168/125,138693/100000$. For $C$ of degrees $(5,3)$ on $[1/2,7/9]\times[0,1]$ the minimum is zero. Thus all three numerators have the required signs on their domains. This completes the compact negative-peak case.

### Boundary profiles and sharpness

The preceding checks cover a minimizing tilt for every interior pair of tent parameters. Exchange of the endpoints and reversal of the tilt let the wider-to-smaller ratio be used for the wide estimate. The computations cover both signs of $H$ and agree on $H=0$. At a parameter $p$ or $q$ equal to zero or one, approximate it by interior values with the widths and tested tilt fixed. The kite vertices and hence the midpoint polygons vary continuously in Hausdorff distance. All limiting polygons have positive area and their boundaries and the cutting lines have area zero, so all three cap areas converge. The inequality passes to the limit at that tilt. At $R=1,p=q=0,\alpha=0$, the midpoint equals the common triangle and its centroid cut and the two endpoint cuts each have area $4/9$ by the tail formula.
