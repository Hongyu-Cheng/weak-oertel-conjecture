## Statement

Fix \(R>0\), take \(n=2\) and \(d=1\), and put
\[
 v_{00}=(0,0),\qquad v_{10}=(2,0),\qquad
 v_{01}=(0,2),\qquad v_{11}=(2,2),
\]
\[
 c_{00}=c_{10}=c_{01}=0,\qquad c_{11}=1.
\]
For \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 S_{ij}:=\{v_{ij}\}\times[c_{ij}-R,c_{ij}+R]
 \subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R
\]
and
\[
 C:=\operatorname{conv}(S_{00}\cup S_{10}\cup S_{01}\cup S_{11}).
\]
For \(x\in\mathbb R^2\), write \(C_x:=\{t\in\mathbb R:(x,t)\in C\}\). For every interval \(K\subseteq\mathbb R\) of positive length and every \(0<s\leq\operatorname{vol}_1(K)\), use the mass-\(s\) metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_s(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1s\int_K t f(t)\,dt:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\
 \int_K f(t)\,dt=s
 \right\}.
\]

Then \(C\) is a compact full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^3\). Its fibers are empty outside \([0,2]^2\), while for \(x=(x_1,x_2)\in[0,2]^2\) they are exactly
\[
 C_x=
 \left[
 \max\left\{0,\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}-1\right\}-R,
 \min\left\{\frac{x_1}{2},\frac{x_2}{2}\right\}+R
 \right].
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular,
\[
 C_{v_{ij}}=[c_{ij}-R,c_{ij}+R].
 \tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
 \Delta_0:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{00},v_{10},v_{01}\},
 \qquad
 \Delta_1:=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{10},v_{01},v_{11}\},
\]
and define the affine functions
\[
 q_0(x):=0,
 \qquad
 q_1(x):=\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}-1.
\]
With the common positive mass \(s:=2R\), one has
\[
 q_i(x)\in\mathsf M_s(C_x)
 \qquad(x\in\Delta_i,\ i\in\{0,1\}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Hence (3) holds in particular on every relevant lattice fiber, where
\[
 \Delta_0\cap\mathbb Z^2
 =\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,2)\},
\]
\[
 \Delta_1\cap\mathbb Z^2
 =\{(2,0),(1,1),(0,2),(2,1),(1,2),(2,2)\}.
\]
The two affine fills agree on the entire common edge:
\[
 q_0(x)=q_1(x)=0
 \qquad
 \left(x\in\Delta_0\cap\Delta_1
 =\{x\in[0,2]^2:x_1+x_2=2\}\right).
 \tag{4}
\]

Nevertheless, there is no affine function \(q:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 q(v_{ij})\in\mathsf M_s(C_{v_{ij}})
 \qquad(i,j\in\{0,1\}).
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the coefficients
\[
 \lambda_{00}=\lambda_{11}=1,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{10}=\lambda_{01}=-1
\]
form the square affine circuit
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}v_{ij}=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}c_{ij}=1.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus every affine \(q\) annihilates the first two relations in (6), whereas the vertex metronoids in (5) force its values to have circuit sum \(1\).

## Proof

Put \(p_{ij}:=(v_{ij},c_{ij})\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(B:=\{(0,0)\}\times[-R,R]\). Since \(S_{ij}=p_{ij}+B\) and \(B\) is convex,
\[
 C=\operatorname{conv}\{p_{00},p_{10},p_{01},p_{11}\}+B.
 \tag{7}
\]
To check both inclusions in (7), a convex combination of points \(p_{ij}+b_{ij}\), with \(b_{ij}\in B\), equals the corresponding convex combination of the \(p_{ij}\) plus a point of \(B\); conversely, a point \(\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}p_{ij}+b\), with common \(b\in B\), equals \(\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}(p_{ij}+b)\).

The body \(C\) is compact and convex because it is the convex hull of finitely many compact segments. Moreover, the three segments over \(v_{00},v_{10},v_{01}\) have common center height zero, so their convex hull is
\[
 \Delta_0\times[-R,R]\subseteq C.
\]
Because \(R>0\) and \(\Delta_0\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^2\), this subset has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^3\). Hence \(C\) is full-dimensional for every \(R>0\).

We now compute every fiber exactly. Write \(u=x_1/2\) and \(v=x_2/2\). A convex representation of \(x\in[0,2]^2\) by the four base vertices is uniquely parameterized by \(\rho:=\alpha_{11}\):
\[
 \alpha_{11}=\rho,
 \quad
 \alpha_{10}=u-\rho,
 \quad
 \alpha_{01}=v-\rho,
 \quad
 \alpha_{00}=1-u-v+\rho.
 \tag{8}
\]
The four coefficients in (8) are nonnegative exactly when
\[
 \max\{0,u+v-1\}\leq\rho\leq\min\{u,v\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Only \(p_{11}\) has nonzero center height, so the height of the corresponding point in \(\operatorname{conv}\{p_{ij}\}\) is precisely \(\rho\). Equations (7)-(9) therefore give the exact interval (1). The base projection of \(C\) is the square \([0,2]^2\), so fibers outside that square are empty. Substitution of the four vertices in (1) gives (2).

For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), let
\[
 P_i:=\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{v\in\operatorname{vert}(\Delta_i)}
 \{v\}\times[c_v-R,c_v+R]\right).
\]
The vertex heights on \(\Delta_i\) are the values of \(q_i\). Uniqueness of barycentric coordinates in a triangle gives
\[
 P_i=\{(x,t):x\in\Delta_i,\ |t-q_i(x)|\leq R\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
For completeness, if \(x=\sum_v\alpha_vv\), then a convex combination of vertex-segment points has height
\[
 \sum_v\alpha_v(c_v+r_v)=q_i(x)+\sum_v\alpha_vr_v,
 \qquad -R\leq r_v\leq R,
\]
and its final sum ranges over \([q_i(x)-R,q_i(x)+R]\). Conversely, every value in that interval is obtained by taking every \(r_v\) equal to the desired displacement from \(q_i(x)\). This proves (10). Since \(P_i\subseteq C\),
\[
 [q_i(x)-R,q_i(x)+R]\subseteq C_x
 \qquad(x\in\Delta_i).
 \tag{11}
\]

Fix \(x\in\Delta_i\) and let \(f\) be the indicator of the interval in (11), viewed as a function on \(C_x\). It satisfies \(0\leq f\leq1\), has mass \(2R=s\), and has normalized first moment
\[
 \frac1{2R}\int_{q_i(x)-R}^{q_i(x)+R}t\,dt=q_i(x).
\]
This proves (3) for every real base point in each triangle. Directly solving the inequalities defining the two triangles gives the two displayed lists of all their lattice points. Their common edge is the segment \(x_1+x_2=2\), and both affine formulas equal zero there, proving (4) on the entire common face.

It remains to prove the obstruction. By (2), every vertex fiber has length exactly \(s=2R\). If \(f\) is admissible in its mass-\(s\) metronoid, then
\[
 \int_{C_{v_{ij}}}(1-f)=0,
\]
so \(f=1\) almost everywhere. Therefore its normalized barycenter is the interval center, and
\[
 \mathsf M_s(C_{v_{ij}})=\{c_{ij}\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
If an affine \(q\) satisfied (5), equation (12) would force
\[
 q(v_{00})=q(v_{10})=q(v_{01})=0,
 \qquad
 q(v_{11})=1.
 \tag{13}
\]
On the other hand, the first two circuit identities in (6) imply for every affine function that
\[
 q(v_{00})-q(v_{10})-q(v_{01})+q(v_{11})=0.
\]
The forced values (13) make the left-hand side equal to \(1\), a contradiction. Hence (5) is impossible. In particular, the two compatible local affine metronoid fills cannot be restrictions of one global affine fill, despite using the same full-dimensional body, the same positive mass, and agreeing on their entire common edge.
