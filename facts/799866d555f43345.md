## Statement

Let \(k,r\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) with \(N=k+r\ge1\). Let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^N\) be compact and convex, let
\[
D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\ne\varnothing,
\]
and give \(D\) positive log-affine weights
\[
w_{\mathbf z}=c\exp(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z),
\qquad c>0,
\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}w_{\mathbf z}.
\]
For \(s>0\), put \(A_s=\{\mathbf z\in D:w_{\mathbf z}\ge s\}\). For a finite \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^N\), define its unnormalized ambient halfspace depth by
\[
d_A(\mathbf y)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf y\in H}}|A\cap H|,
\]
and define
\[
L(w)=3\int_0^\infty s^2
\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_s}d_{A_s}(\mathbf z)^2\,ds.
\tag{1}
\]

Assume that an affine-unimodular map
\(T(\mathbf z)=U\mathbf z+\mathbf b\), with
\(U\in\operatorname{GL}_N(\mathbb Z)\) and \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb Z^N\),
sends \(D\) into
\(
\{0,1\}^k\times\mathbb Z^r
\)
and that, after this map, every nonempty fiber
\[
D_{\boldsymbol\eta}
=\{\mathbf t\in\mathbb Z^r:(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t)\in D\},
\qquad \boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k,
\tag{2}
\]
is a Cartesian lattice box
\[
D_{\boldsymbol\eta}
=\prod_{i=1}^r\{a_{\boldsymbol\eta,i},\ldots,b_{\boldsymbol\eta,i}\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(a_{\boldsymbol\eta,i},b_{\boldsymbol\eta,i}\in\mathbb Z\) and
\(a_{\boldsymbol\eta,i}\le b_{\boldsymbol\eta,i}\).
For \(r=0\), the empty product in (3) is the singleton of \(\mathbb Z^0\). Let
\[
E=\{\boldsymbol\eta:D_{\boldsymbol\eta}\ne\varnothing\},
\qquad M=|E|,
\qquad 1\le M\le2^k.
\tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
\gamma=\log(32/27),
\qquad
\widehat\kappa_{\square}=\frac14e^{-3/\gamma},
\qquad
\kappa_{\square}=\frac14e^{-6/\gamma}.
\tag{5}
\]

If \(r\ge1\), then
\[
L(w)
\ge
\widehat\kappa_{\square}\,4^{-r}M^{-2}W^3
=
\widehat\kappa_{\square}\frac{4^k}{M^2}4^{-N}W^3
\ge
\widehat\kappa_{\square}4^{-N}W^3.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently the same three inequalities hold with
\(\widehat\kappa_{\square}\) replaced by the source-level box constant
\(\kappa_{\square}\).

If \(r=1\), the sharper estimate is
\[
L(w)
\ge
\frac1{54M^2}W^3
=
\frac{2}{27}\frac{4^k}{M^2}4^{-N}W^3
\ge
\frac{2}{27}4^{-N}W^3.
\tag{7}
\]
For each fixed \(k\ge0\) and each \(1\le M\le2^k\), the coefficient
\[
\frac{2}{27}\frac{4^k}{M^2}
\tag{8}
\]
in front of \(4^{-N}W^3\) is optimal over this class restricted to supports with exactly \(M\) nonempty fibers, in the sense of an infimum over finite admissible tables. In particular, \(2/27\) is optimal for the unrestricted \(r=1\) class, where all \(2^k\) fibers may be nonempty.

If \(r=0\), then
\[
L(w)
\ge M^{-2}W^3
=\frac{4^k}{M^2}4^{-N}W^3
\ge4^{-N}W^3.
\tag{9}
\]
For each fixed \(k\ge1\) and \(1\le M\le2^k\), the coefficient \(4^k/M^2\) in (9) is optimal for supports with exactly \(M\) nonempty labels; hence the universal coefficient \(1\) is optimal when the number of labels is unrestricted. No optimality claim is made for either box constant in (5): accepted fact 60942de80fd731e4 proves those constants as valid uniform lower bounds but does not identify the infimum over boxes.

## Proof

Affine-unimodular maps biject the lattice and map closed halfspaces bijectively to closed halfspaces. If \(T(\mathbf z)=U\mathbf z+\mathbf b\), then \(T(Q)\cap\mathbb Z^N=T(D)\), and the transported weights satisfy
\[
w'_{\mathbf x}=w_{T^{-1}\mathbf x}
=c'\exp\bigl((U^{-T}\boldsymbol\ell)\cdot\mathbf x\bigr)
\]
for some \(c'>0\). Thus \(T\) preserves \(W\), every superlevel-set depth, and \(L(w)\). We may therefore work in the coordinates of (2)--(3).

Write
\(
\boldsymbol\ell=(\boldsymbol\ell_B,\boldsymbol\ell_F)
\)
according to
\(
\mathbb R^N=\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^r
\).
For every \(\boldsymbol\eta\in E\), the restricted weights are
\[
w_{\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t}
=c_{\boldsymbol\eta}
\exp(\boldsymbol\ell_F\cdot\mathbf t),
\qquad
c_{\boldsymbol\eta}
=c\exp(\boldsymbol\ell_B\cdot\boldsymbol\eta)>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the restriction to each nonempty fiber is itself a positive log-affine table on the box (3). Put
\[
W_{\boldsymbol\eta}
=\sum_{\mathbf t\in D_{\boldsymbol\eta}}
w_{\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t},
\qquad
B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}
=\{\mathbf t\in D_{\boldsymbol\eta}:
w_{\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t}\ge s\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(W=\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}W_{\boldsymbol\eta}\).

Assume first \(r\ge1\). Let
\(
d^{(r)}_B(\mathbf t)
\)
denote counting depth in the fiber ambient space \(\mathbb R^r\). Fix \(s>0\),
\(
\boldsymbol\eta\in E
\),
\(
\mathbf t\in B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}
\), and an ambient closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing
\((\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t)\). Its restriction
\[
H_{\boldsymbol\eta}
=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^r:
(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf u)\in H\}
\tag{12}
\]
is a closed halfspace of \(\mathbb R^r\) containing \(\mathbf t\), or it is the whole fiber space. In the latter case its intersection with \(B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}\) also has size at least the fiber depth. Hence in all cases
\[
|A_s\cap H|
\ge |B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}\cap H_{\boldsymbol\eta}|
\ge d^{(r)}_{B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}}(\mathbf t).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) gives
\[
d_{A_s}(\boldsymbol\eta,\mathbf t)
\ge d^{(r)}_{B_{s,\boldsymbol\eta}}(\mathbf t).
\tag{13}
\]
After squaring, summing over all fibers, and integrating the nonnegative finite sums, (13) yields the fiber restriction inequality
\[
L(w)\ge
\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}
L\bigl(w|_{D_{\boldsymbol\eta}}\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]

Accepted fact 60942de80fd731e4 applies to every table in (10), including boxes with singleton coordinate intervals, and gives
\[
L\bigl(w|_{D_{\boldsymbol\eta}}\bigr)
\ge
\widehat\kappa_{\square}4^{-r}
W_{\boldsymbol\eta}^3.
\tag{15}
\]
Hölder's inequality gives
\[
W^3
=\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}W_{\boldsymbol\eta}\right)^3
\le M^2\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}W_{\boldsymbol\eta}^3.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (14)--(16), then using \(M\le2^k\) and \(N=k+r\), proves (6). The same accepted fact also gives the weaker constant \(\kappa_{\square}\), so the stated source-level consequence follows without any new estimate.

When \(r=1\), every nonempty box (3) is a finite integer interval. Accepted fact 3cd1aafbacc5db14 gives the sharp fiber inequality
\[
L\bigl(w|_{D_{\boldsymbol\eta}}\bigr)
\ge\frac1{54}W_{\boldsymbol\eta}^3.
\tag{17}
\]
Equations (14), (16), and (17) prove (7).

We next prove the asserted \(r=1\) optimality with finite complete supports. Fix \(k\ge0\), choose any \(E_0\subseteq\{0,1\}^k\) with \(|E_0|=M\), and, for \(m\ge1\), set
\[
Q_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_0)\times[0,m-1],
\qquad
D_m=E_0\times\{0,\ldots,m-1\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The equality \(D_m=Q_m\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}\) holds: every lattice point in \([0,1]^k\) is Boolean, and a Boolean point outside \(E_0\) cannot lie in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_0)\), because it is an exposed vertex of the Boolean cube. Give \(D_m\) the log-affine weights
\[
w_{\boldsymbol\eta,t}=q^t,
\qquad 0<q\le1,
\tag{19}
\]
which are independent of \(\boldsymbol\eta\).

For a fixed label \(\boldsymbol\eta\), define
\[
\psi_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf u)
=\sum_{i:\eta_i=1}u_i
+\sum_{i:\eta_i=0}(1-u_i).
\tag{20}
\]
This affine function equals \(k\) at \(\boldsymbol\eta\) and is at most \(k-1\) at every other Boolean label. Every superlevel set of (19) is \(E_0\times I_s\) for an integer interval \(I_s\). Given either closed ray in the \(t\)-coordinate through a target \((\boldsymbol\eta,t)\), adding a sufficiently large positive multiple of
\(
\psi_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf u)-k
\)
to its defining affine inequality produces an ambient closed halfspace whose intersection with the superlevel set is exactly that ray inside the selected fiber. Together with (13), this proves equality of ambient depth and one-dimensional fiber depth at every supported target and every level. Therefore
\[
L(w)=M L_{m,q},
\qquad
W=M W_{m,q},
\tag{21}
\]
where \(L_{m,q}\) and \(W_{m,q}\) are the layer functional and mass of one interval table. Accepted fact 3cd1aafbacc5db14 supplies a diagonal sequence of finite pairs \((m,q)\) for which
\(
L_{m,q}/W_{m,q}^3\to1/54
\).
Since \(N=k+1\), (21) gives
\[
4^N\frac{L(w)}{W^3}
=\frac{4^{k+1}}{M^2}
\frac{L_{m,q}}{W_{m,q}^3}
\longrightarrow
\frac{2}{27}\frac{4^k}{M^2}.
\tag{22}
\]
This matches (7), proving (8) and its \(M=2^k\) specialization.

Finally suppose \(r=0\). Each nonempty fiber is one supported point. Whenever that point belongs to \(A_s\), every ambient halfspace containing it meets \(A_s\), so its depth is at least one. Consequently
\[
L(w)
\ge3\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}
\int_0^{w_{\boldsymbol\eta}}s^2\,ds
=\sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in E}w_{\boldsymbol\eta}^3
\ge M^{-2}W^3,
\tag{23}
\]
where the last step is (16). This proves (9). For sharpness, take any \(E_0\subseteq\{0,1\}^k\) with \(|E_0|=M\), let \(Q=\operatorname{conv}(E_0)\), and assign the uniform weights \(w_{\boldsymbol\eta}=1\). As above, \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^k=E_0\), and (20) exposes each point by a closed halfspace. Thus every supported point has depth exactly one, so \(L(w)=M\), \(W=M\), and
\[
4^N\frac{L(w)}{W^3}=\frac{4^k}{M^2}.
\]
This proves the last optimality assertion and completes the proof.
