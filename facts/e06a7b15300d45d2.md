## Statement

Let $J$ be any centrally symmetric planar convex body of area one and center zero, and let $0\ne P\in J$. Set $K_z=P+(1-|z|/4)(J-P)$ for real $|z|\le3$. These are the sections of a convex body with seven actual integer fibers of summed area $M=11/4$. The greatest numerical lower certificate among the own-fiber half-area bounds and the reflected-pair symmetry-center bounds in 5e107e24de91c307 §8 (16), taken at every integer base, is $9/16$, which is below the sharp target $11/18$ by $7/144$. The actual point $(0,P/4)$ has raw mixed depth at least $27/32$.

## Proof

The positive concave radius $r(z)=1-|z|/4$ and nesting of dilations of $J-P$, which contains zero, imply convexity: mixing two fibers gives a copy with radius at most $r$ of the mixed base. Compactness follows from continuity of $r$, and the fibers' positive areas on a base interval give interior. Their centers are $(1-r(z))P$ and the sampled volumes from $-3$ through $3$ are $(1/16,1/4,9/16,1,9/16,1/4,1/16)$.

For the certificate of the same-batch symmetry-center coupling theorem, list the pairs up to reflection of the base. At zero the equal-volume pairs of areas $9/16,1/4,1/16$ pay $\max(0,a_j-1/2)$, so the value is $1/2+1/16=9/16$. At one, pair $(0,2)$ has midpoint volume bound $9/16$ and smaller area $1/4$, paying $1/8$; pair $(-1,3)$ has midpoint volume bound $1/4$ and pays zero. The total is $9/32+1/8=13/32$. At two the sole pair $(1,3)$ has midpoint volume bound $1/4$ and smaller area $1/16$, paying $1/32$, so the total is $1/8+1/32=5/32$. At three the value is $1/32$ with no pair. The own-fiber half-area terms never exceed these respective values. The sum of the areas is $11/4$, and $(4/9)M/2-9/16=7/144$.

For the explicit depth witness, the three fibers at $-1,0,1$ contain the same symmetric body $E=(3/4)J+P/4$ of area $9/16$. At $(0,P/4)$ a through-mark cap with nonzero continuous normal captures from the two endpoint copies of $E$ a combined area $9/16$, since the thresholds are opposite about their symmetry center. It also captures half the middle copy of $E$. A pure-base cap contains the middle copy and at least one endpoint copy. Parallel tightening therefore gives raw depth at least $27/32$.
