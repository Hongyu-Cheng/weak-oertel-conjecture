## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be a nonempty convex set centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf c\), meaning that \(Q=2\mathbf c-Q\). Assume \(D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite and that \(2\mathbf c-D=D\). Let \(r:Q\to(0,\infty)\) be finite and concave, and put \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r(\mathbf z)^d\). Suppose \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and

\[
r(\mathbf c)^d\le(1+\varepsilon)
\left(\frac{r(\mathbf x)+r(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x)}2\right)^d
\qquad(\mathbf x\in Q).
\tag{1}
\]

There exists an affine function \(L:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) supporting \(r\) at \(\mathbf c\), that is, \(L(\mathbf c)=r(\mathbf c)\) and \(L(\mathbf x)\ge r(\mathbf x)>0\) for all \(\mathbf x\in Q\). Every such supporting affine function satisfies

\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\bigl(L(\mathbf z)^d-r(\mathbf z)^d\bigr)
\le\bigl((1+\varepsilon)^2-1\bigr)W
=(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2)W.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the excess is at most \(3\varepsilon W\) for \(0\le\varepsilon\le1\), and at most \((21/10)\varepsilon W\) for \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/10\). Neither \(Q\) nor the affine hull of \(D\) need have full dimension, and the center need not be an integer point. No positive lower bound on individual roots or pointwise multiplicative majorant hypothesis is imposed.

For the actual-body consequence, use precisely the notation of accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\): let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, let \(C_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(D_+=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf z}>0\}\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}w_{\mathbf z}>0\). Suppose there is a base \(Q\) satisfying the preceding assumptions such that \(D_+=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and suppose the actual real root \(r(\mathbf x)=w_{\mathbf x}^{1/d}\) is positive and concave on \(Q\) and satisfies (1). Here the scalar theorem's \(D\) is exactly \(D_+\), and its \(r(\mathbf z)^d\) is exactly the unscaled original fiber volume \(w_{\mathbf z}\). Write \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\), \(\theta_d=1-\gamma_d\), and \([t]_+=\max\{t,0\}\). There exists \(\mathbf j_L\in D_+\) whose actual fiber centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf j_L}\) gives one point \(\mathbf y^\star=(\mathbf j_L,\mathbf c_{\mathbf j_L})\in S\) such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
&:=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y^\star}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\\
&\ge2^{-n}\bigl[\gamma_d-\theta_d(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2)\bigr]_+W,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

where the infimum is over all closed ambient halfspaces. The point is fixed before the halfspace is chosen. For \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/10\), this in particular gives

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge
\frac{121-21e}{100e}\,2^{-n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{4}
\]

The coefficient in (4) lies in \((0,1/e)\). This is a theorem for the displayed conditional class, not a conclusion that all convex bodies satisfy (1).

## Proof

Put \(\rho=r(\mathbf c)>0\) and \(\kappa=(1+\varepsilon)^{-1/d}\), so \(0<\kappa\le1\). First we prove existence of an affine support, including arbitrary relative rank and boundary values.

The midpoint of any opposite pair lies in \(Q\), so \(\mathbf c\in Q\). Let \(V=\operatorname{span}(Q-\mathbf c)\). Then the affine hull of \(Q\) is \(\mathbf c+V\). If \(V=\{\mathbf0\}\), then \(Q=\{\mathbf c\}\) and the constant function \(L=\rho\) is a support. Otherwise choose a basis \(\mathbf v_1,\ldots,\mathbf v_k\) of \(V\) from \(Q-\mathbf c\). Central symmetry puts both \(\mathbf c+\mathbf v_i\) and \(\mathbf c-\mathbf v_i\) in \(Q\). Their convex hull contains a ball about \(\mathbf c\) relative to \(\mathbf c+V\): indeed, the invertible linear map sending the standard basis to the \(\mathbf v_i\) sends the set \(\{\mathbf t:\sum_i|t_i|\le1\}\), which contains a neighborhood of zero, to this centered convex hull. Choose \(\eta>0\) such that \(\mathbf c+\eta B_V\subseteq Q\), where \(B_V\) is the closed unit ball in \(V\).

The function \(r\) is continuous at \(\mathbf c\), as follows directly from its positivity and concavity. For \(\mathbf h\in V\) with \(0<\|\mathbf h\|\le\eta\), put \(\mathbf v=\mathbf h/\|\mathbf h\|\) and \(t=\|\mathbf h\|/\eta\). Interpolation from \(\mathbf c\) to \(\mathbf c+\eta\mathbf v\) gives \(r(\mathbf c+\mathbf h)\ge(1-t)\rho\). Also \(\mathbf c=(1+t)^{-1}(\mathbf c+\mathbf h)+t(1+t)^{-1}(\mathbf c-\eta\mathbf v)\), so positivity at the second point and concavity give \(r(\mathbf c+\mathbf h)\le(1+t)\rho\). These bounds imply continuity at \(\mathbf c\).

Here is an elementary supporting-plane construction that does not assume continuity on the boundary of \(Q\). In the Euclidean space \(V\times\mathbb R\), let \(\mathcal K=\{(\mathbf h,s):\mathbf c+\mathbf h\in Q,\ s\le r(\mathbf c+\mathbf h)\}\).

Concavity makes \(\mathcal K\) convex, and its closure is convex and closed. The point \(P=(\mathbf0,\rho)\) belongs to it, whereas \(P_m=(\mathbf0,\rho+1/m)\) does not belong to its closure for any positive integer \(m\): otherwise a sequence in \(\mathcal K\) approaching \(P_m\) would contradict the continuity at \(\mathbf c\) just proved. Let \(X_m\) be a point of the closure nearest to \(P_m\). It exists because the closed ball of radius \(1/m\) about \(P_m\) contains \(P\); minimizing distance on its intersection with the closure is a minimization on a nonempty compact set, and points outside the ball cannot improve the distance.

Set \(\nu_m=(P_m-X_m)/\|P_m-X_m\|\). For every \(Y\) in the closure, minimizing the squared distance along \(X_m+t(Y-X_m)\), \(0\le t\le1\), gives \(\langle\nu_m,Y-X_m\rangle\le0\). Also \(\|X_m-P\|\le2/m\). Pass to a convergent subsequence of unit normals and write its unit limit as \(\nu=(\mathbf a,b)\in V\times\mathbb R\). For every fixed \(Y\) in the closure the preceding inequality passes to the limit, giving \(\langle\nu,Y-P\rangle\le0\). Taking \(Y=(\mathbf0,\rho-t)\), \(t>0\), shows \(b\ge0\). If \(b=0\), then \(\langle\mathbf a,\mathbf h\rangle\le0\) for every \(\mathbf h\in Q-\mathbf c\). The ball \(\eta B_V\subseteq Q-\mathbf c\) forces \(\mathbf a=\mathbf0\), contradicting that \(\nu\) is a unit vector. Thus \(b>0\). Taking \(Y=(\mathbf h,r(\mathbf c+\mathbf h))\) proves that \(L(\mathbf x)=\rho-\langle\mathbf a,\mathbf x-\mathbf c\rangle/b\) is the required affine support on \(Q\), extended to \(\mathbb R^n\) by the same formula. This includes all points of \(Q\), with no boundary continuity assumption.

Now let \(L\) be any affine support at \(\mathbf c\). Affinity gives \(L(\mathbf x)+L(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x)=2\rho\). Taking positive \(d\)-th roots in (1) gives \(r(\mathbf x)+r(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x)\ge2\kappa\rho\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\le(L(\mathbf x)-r(\mathbf x))+(L(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x)-r(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x))\\
&=2\rho-r(\mathbf x)-r(2\mathbf c-\mathbf x)\\
&\le2(1-\kappa)\rho.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Both deficits are nonnegative, so (5) implies \(0\le L(\mathbf x)-r(\mathbf x)\le2(1-\kappa)\rho\) individually on all of \(Q\). This is an additive bound, not a multiplicative comparison with \(r(\mathbf x)\).

If \(D\) is empty, all sums in (2) vanish and the scalar result is proved. Suppose henceforth \(N=|D|>0\). The reflection \(\mathbf z\mapsto2\mathbf c-\mathbf z\) is a permutation of exactly the original integer sites. Summing its opposite-pair root inequality gives

\[
2\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r(\mathbf z)
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}\bigl(r(\mathbf z)+r(2\mathbf c-\mathbf z)\bigr)
\ge2N\kappa\rho.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, no integer mass at \(\mathbf c\) is needed. If there is a fixed site, it is \(\mathbf c\) and the equality in (6) still counts its root twice, exactly as required. If \(D\) is a singleton, reflection forces that site to be \(\mathbf c\), and the same calculation remains valid.

For \(t\ge0\), set \(u_{\mathbf z}=r(\mathbf z)+t\rho>0\). Equation (6) gives \(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}\ge N(\kappa+t)\rho\). Since \(s\mapsto s^{d-1}\) and \(s\mapsto s\) are nondecreasing on \((0,\infty)\), the finite covariance identity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}^d
-\left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}^{d-1}\right)
 \left(\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}\right)
&=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z,\mathbf z'\in D}
 (u_{\mathbf z}^{d-1}-u_{\mathbf z'}^{d-1})
 (u_{\mathbf z}-u_{\mathbf z'})\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

For \(d=1\), both sides before the inequality in (7) are zero, so this case is included. Dividing positive quantities yields

\[
\frac{\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}^{d-1}}
 {\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}^{d}}
\le\frac{N}{\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}u_{\mathbf z}}
\le\frac{1}{(\kappa+t)\rho}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(F(t)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}(r(\mathbf z)+t\rho)^d\), so \(F(0)=W>0\). Differentiation and (8) give \(F'(t)\le dF(t)/(\kappa+t)\). Thus the derivative of \(F(t)/(\kappa+t)^d\) is nonpositive, and

\[
F(t)\le\left(\frac{\kappa+t}{\kappa}\right)^dW
\qquad(t\ge0).
\tag{9}
\]

Apply this with \(t=2(1-\kappa)\). The pointwise additive estimate following (5), positivity, and (9) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}L(\mathbf z)^d
&\le F(2(1-\kappa))\\
&\le\left(\frac{2-\kappa}{\kappa}\right)^dW\\
&\le\kappa^{-2d}W
=(1+\varepsilon)^2W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last inequality uses \(\kappa(2-\kappa)\le1\), which is equivalent to \((1-\kappa)^2\ge0\). Subtracting \(W\) proves (2), including \(\varepsilon=0\). The stated linear bounds follow from \(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\le3\varepsilon\) for \(\varepsilon\le1\) and \(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\le(21/10)\varepsilon\) for \(\varepsilon\le1/10\). All sums throughout (6)-(10) use one copy of each original integer site, independently of affine rank or the number of sites.

For the actual-body consequence, \(\operatorname{conv}(D_+)\subseteq Q\), so \(\varphi=L|_{\operatorname{conv}(D_+)}\) is positive and convex and satisfies \(\varphi(\mathbf z)^d\ge w_{\mathbf z}\) at every positive integer fiber. These are exactly the hypotheses of the convex-majorant certificate (2) in accepted fact \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\), with the same \(n,d,C,w_{\mathbf z},D_+,W\). Its notation \(h_C\) is our locked \(h_S\); its centroid \(\mathbf c_{\mathbf j}\) has exactly the actual-fiber meaning stated above, distinct from the base symmetry center \(\mathbf c\). The certificate weights are \(q_{\mathbf z}=(w_{\mathbf z}-\theta_dL(\mathbf z)^d)_+\), with total \(Q_L=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}q_{\mathbf z}\). The positive part of a real number is at least the number itself, so (2) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_L
&\ge W-\theta_d\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}L(\mathbf z)^d\\
&=\gamma_dW-\theta_d\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_+}(L(\mathbf z)^d-w_{\mathbf z})\\
&\ge\bigl(\gamma_d-\theta_d(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2)\bigr)W.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Also \(Q_L\ge0\). The accepted certificate selects \(\mathbf j_L\in D_+\) before any halfspace and gives depth at its actual fiber centroid at least \(2^{-n}Q_L\). This proves (3), including zero certificate weight, and retains all boundary and vertical halfspace cases of that accepted certificate.

Finally, for \(\varepsilon\le1/10\), one has \(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\le21/100\). The accepted estimate \(\gamma_d>1/e\) in \(\texttt{0bcc51d8cd216cf3}\) gives

\[
\gamma_d-\frac{21}{100}\theta_d
=\frac{121}{100}\gamma_d-\frac{21}{100}
>\frac{121-21e}{100e}>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Positivity follows from \(e<3\), also established in that accepted fact. Since \(e>1\), the same coefficient is less than \(1/e\). The original sum \(W\) equals \(\mathcal H_d(S)\), so (3) and (12) prove (4).
