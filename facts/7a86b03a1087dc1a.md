## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge1\), integer \(T\ge1\), and real \(\beta>0\), put
\[
A_T=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb Z:
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\le t\le T\right\},\qquad
Z_T(\beta)=\sum_{(x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(x,t)}=\frac{e^{-\beta t}}{Z_T(\beta)}.
\]
Define
\[
\tau_p(x,t)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+1}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\(x,t)\in H}}
\sum_{(y,u)\in A_T\cap H}p_{(y,u)},\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{(x,t)\in A_T}p_{(x,t)}\tau_p(x,t)^3.
\]
These are exactly the normalized depth and moment conventions of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, with arithmetic dimension \(N=n+1\). All logarithms below are natural. Set
\[
\alpha=\frac{3\sqrt3}{16},\qquad
\delta=\log(8\alpha)>0,\qquad
c=\frac{e^{-4096}}{2048},\qquad
N_* =\left\lceil\max\left\{64,\left(\frac{327}{\delta}\right)^2\right\}\right\rceil,
\]
and \(\kappa=\min\{c,\,1/[32((N_*+5)\log2)^3]\}>0\). Then \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa8^{-(n+1)}\). More specifically, whenever \(n\ge64\), \(\beta<\log5\), and \(T>n/32\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge c e^{-327\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\tag{1}
\]
The conclusion also holds for every real cutoff \(T\ge1\), since its original support equals \(A_{\lfloor T\rfloor}\).

## Proof

For \(s>0\), define \(\theta(s)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-s j^2}\). We first establish the theta comparison needed for an exponential surplus. Gaussian periodization gives
\[
\theta(s)=\sqrt{\frac\pi s}\,\theta\left(\frac{\pi^2}{s}\right).
\tag{2}
\]
Here is a derivation. The smooth periodic function \(x\mapsto\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-s(x+j)^2}\) has Fourier coefficient at integer \(m\) equal to
\(\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-s x^2}e^{-2\pi i m x}\,dx\). If
\(I_s(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-s x^2}e^{-i\xi x}\,dx\), differentiation and integration by parts give
\(I_s'(\xi)=-\xi I_s(\xi)/(2s)\). The Gaussian integral gives \(I_s(0)=\sqrt{\pi/s}\), and hence
\(I_s(\xi)=\sqrt{\pi/s}\,e^{-\xi^2/(4s)}\). The periodized series and its derivatives converge uniformly on a period, and its resulting Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable. Evaluating its Fourier series at zero proves (2).

The function \(\theta\) decreases in its argument, directly term by term. Equation (2) therefore shows that \(\sqrt s\,\theta(s)=\sqrt\pi\,\theta(\pi^2/s)\) increases in \(s\). In particular, for \(a\ge1\),
\[
\theta(as)\ge a^{-1/2}\theta(s),\qquad
\theta(s/2)\le\sqrt2\,\theta(s).
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(\nu=4s/3\) and \(H(s)=\theta(\nu)\). Taking \(a=4\) and \(a=4/3\) in (3) gives the uniform factor estimate
\[
\frac{\theta(4s)H(s)^3}{\theta(s)^4}
\ge\frac12\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^3
=\alpha>\frac18.
\tag{4}
\]

We will also need elementary Gaussian moment bounds at all rates. For \(a>0\), let \(q_a(j)=e^{-a j^2}/\theta(a)\). For \(y\ge0\), maximizing \(ye^{-y/2}\) and \(y^2e^{-y/2}\) shows \(y\le e^{y/2}\) and \(y^2\le4e^{y/2}\), using \(e>2\). Thus (3) implies
\[
\mathbb E_{q_a}j^2
\le\frac{\theta(a/2)}{a\theta(a)}\le\frac2a,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{q_a}j^4
\le\frac{4\theta(a/2)}{a^2\theta(a)}\le\frac8{a^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Define the auxiliary root law and displacement law by
\[
Q_s(j)=\frac{e^{-4s j^2}}{\theta(4s)},\qquad
\rho_s(v)=\frac{e^{-\nu v^2}}{H(s)},\qquad j,v\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{6}
\]
Write \(a_s=\mathbb E_{Q_s}j^2\) and \(m_s=\mathbb E_{\rho_s}v^2\). Equation (5) yields
\[
a_s\le\frac1{2s},\quad
\mathbb E_{Q_s}j^4\le\frac1{2s^2},\quad
m_s\le\frac3{2s},\quad
\mathbb E_{\rho_s}v^4\le\frac9{2s^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
The functions \(a_s,m_s\) are continuous for \(s>0\), because their defining series are uniformly dominated by a polynomial times a Gaussian on each compact interval of positive rates. Their sum tends to zero as \(s\to\infty\), also directly from (7).

The infinite quadratic epigraph has normalizer
\[
Z_\infty(s)
=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^n}\sum_{u\ge0}e^{-s(\sum_i x_i^2+u)}
=\frac{\theta(s)^n}{1-e^{-s}}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(s\ge\beta\) and \(\lambda=s-\beta\), the full original finite normalizer obeys
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le e^{\lambda T}Z_\infty(s).
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed every original height is at most \(T\), so \(e^{-\beta t}=e^{-s t}e^{\lambda t}\le e^{\lambda T}e^{-s t}\). This comparison does not change the original law.

We prove (1) first. Assume \(n\ge64\), \(\beta<\log5\), and \(T>n/32\). In particular \(T>1\). Let
\(g(s)=n(a_s+m_s)+48\sqrt n/s+1\). If \(g(\beta)\le T\), choose \(s=\beta\). Otherwise continuity, \(g(\beta)>T\), and \(g(s)\to1<T\) give a choice \(s>\beta\) with \(g(s)=T\). Always \(s\ge\beta\) and \(g(s)\le T\), with equality if \(\lambda>0\). In that latter case, (7) gives
\[
s(T-1)=ns(a_s+m_s)+48\sqrt n\le2n+48\sqrt n.
\]
Since \(T-1\ge n/64\) in this regime, \(s\le128+3072/\sqrt n\le512\). If \(s=\beta\), this upper bound holds because \(\beta<\log5<512\). Thus in all cases
\[
0<s\le512.
\tag{10}
\]

Take independent root coordinates with law \(Q_s\), and retain the good roots
\[
\mathcal G_s=\left\{j\in\mathbb Z^n:
\left|\sum_i j_i^2-na_s\right|\le\frac{4\sqrt n}{s},\quad
\sum_i j_i^2\le\frac{16n}{s}\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
The first excluded event has probability at most \(1/32\) by Chebyshev and the fourth-moment bound in (7). The second has probability at most \(1/32\) by Markov and the bound on \(a_s\). Hence
\(Q_s^{\otimes n}(\mathcal G_s)\ge15/16>1/2\).

Put \(L=\lfloor1/s\rfloor\). For every \(j\in\mathcal G_s\), use the distinct original integer root heights
\[
t(j,w)=\sum_i j_i^2+
\left\lceil nm_s+\frac{8\sqrt n}{s}\right\rceil+w,
\qquad 0\le w\le L.
\tag{12}
\]
Writing \(u=t(j,w)-\sum_i j_i^2\), the ceiling and \(w\le1/s\le\sqrt n/s\) give
\[
nm_s+\frac{8\sqrt n}{s}\le u
\le nm_s+\frac{9\sqrt n}{s}+1.
\tag{13}
\]

Fix a good root \(j\). Take independent \(V_i\) with law \(\rho_s\), and set
\(E=\sum_i V_i^2\) and \(b=2\sum_i j_iV_i\). These are integer valued. The symmetry of \(\rho_s\) gives \(\mathbb E b=0\), and (7), (11) give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(E)\le\frac{9n}{2s^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(b)=4m_s\sum_i j_i^2\le\frac{96n}{s^2}.
\]
Consequently the finite symmetric displacement set
\[
\mathcal V_j=\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:
\left|\sum_i v_i^2-nm_s\right|\le\frac{4\sqrt n}{s},\quad
\left|2\sum_i j_iv_i\right|\le\frac{32\sqrt n}{s}\right\}
\tag{14}
\]
has \(\rho_s^{\otimes n}\)-probability at least
\(1-9/32-3/32=5/8>1/2\). It is invariant under \(v\mapsto-v\).

For \(v\in\mathcal V_j\) and an integer \(l\) with \(|l|\le L\), set \(h=b+l\). The two reflected endpoints are
\((j+v,t+h)\) and \((j-v,t-h)\), where \(t=t(j,w)\). The exact quadratic identities are
\[
\sum_i(j_i+v_i)^2=\sum_i j_i^2+E+b,\qquad
\sum_i(j_i-v_i)^2=\sum_i j_i^2+E-b.
\tag{15}
\]
Both lower epigraph inequalities hold because (13)-(14) imply \(u\ge E+|l|\). The upper cutoff is also retained: \(|h|\le33\sqrt n/s\), and (11)-(13) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
t+|h|
&\le n(a_s+m_s)+\frac{(4+9+33)\sqrt n}{s}+1\\
&=g(s)-\frac{2\sqrt n}{s}\le T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every endpoint belongs to the full original \(A_T\). Equations (13) and (16) also show that each queried root belongs to \(A_T\).

For a fixed root, the map \((v,l)\mapsto(v,h)=(v,2\sum_i j_iv_i+l)\) is injective and commutes with negation. The resulting endpoint set about \((j,t)\) therefore consists of \((2L+1)|\mathcal V_j|\) distinct atoms and is centrally symmetric. Every closed halfspace containing the root contains at least half of these atoms: in each antipodal pair at least one endpoint is captured, and a central endpoint, if present, is captured. Each captured endpoint has its original weight at least
\[
p_{(j,t)}e^{-\beta|h|}\ge p_{(j,t)}e^{-33\sqrt n},
\tag{17}
\]
using \(\beta\le s\). All boundary atoms are included in this argument.

The product law in (6), the lower bound on \(E\) in (14), and the probability bound for \(\mathcal V_j\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal V_j|
&=H(s)^n\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_j}\rho_s^{\otimes n}(v)e^{\nu\sum_i v_i^2}\\
&\ge\frac12 H(s)^n
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}nm_s-\frac{16}{3}\sqrt n\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Combining (17)-(18) with the captured cardinality proves, for every height (12),
\[
\tau_p(j,t)\ge\frac{2L+1}{4}\,p_{(j,t)}H(s)^n
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}nm_s-\frac{115}{3}\sqrt n\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Cube (19), multiply by the original \(p_{(j,t)}\), and sum only over the distinct roots (12). This keeps four powers of the full original normalizer:
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{(2L+1)^3e^{-115\sqrt n}}{64Z_T(\beta)^4}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal G_s}\sum_{w=0}^L
e^{-4\beta t(j,w)}H(s)^{3n}e^{4snm_s}.
\tag{20}
\]
Put \(F(s)=\theta(4s)H(s)^3\). With the auxiliary root law (6), each summand in (20) is exactly
\[
F(s)^n Q_s^{\otimes n}(j)
e^{4\lambda t(j,w)}e^{-4s(u-nm_s)}.
\tag{21}
\]
By (13), the last factor is at least \(e^{-36\sqrt n-4s}\). If \(\lambda>0\), (11) and the lower bound in (13) give
\(t(j,w)\ge n(a_s+m_s)+4\sqrt n/s\). Since \(g(s)=T\), this implies
\[
e^{4\lambda(t(j,w)-T)}
\ge e^{-176\sqrt n-4s}.
\tag{22}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\), (22) holds as well, since its left side is one. Use (8)-(9), the probability at least one half of the good roots, and the \(L+1\) choices of height in (20)-(22). The result is
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{e^{-327\sqrt n-8s}}{128}
(1-e^{-s})^4(2L+1)^3(L+1)
\left(\frac{F(s)}{\theta(s)^4}\right)^n.
\tag{23}
\]
In particular the fourth power of the normalizer was bounded only by (9); neither the original support nor its probabilities were conditionally renormalized.

The vertical factor in (23) is at least \(1/16\). For \(0<s\le1\), use \(1-e^{-s}\ge s/2\), \(L+1\ge1/s\), and \(2L+1\ge L+1\). For \(s\ge1\), use \(1-e^{-s}\ge1-e^{-1}>1/2\) and both counting factors at least one. Equations (4), (10), and (23) prove (1).

We cover two remaining parameter regimes by the original atom at \((0,0)\). Every containing halfspace retains that atom, so \(M_3(p)\ge p_{(0,0)}^4=Z_T(\beta)^{-4}\). Since \(j^2\ge|j|\) for integer \(j\),
\(\theta(\log5)\le\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}5^{-|j|}=3/2\). Equation (8) therefore gives, if \(\beta\ge\log5\),
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le\frac54\left(\frac32\right)^n,
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac45\right)^4\left(\frac{16}{81}\right)^n.
\tag{24}
\]
If \(T\le n/32\), for every \(\beta>0\) the pointwise inequality
\(e^{-\beta t}\le5^{T-t}\) on the original support and (8) give
\[
Z_T(\beta)\le5^T\frac54\left(\frac32\right)^n,
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\left(\frac45\right)^4
\left(\frac{16}{81}\,5^{-1/8}\right)^n.
\tag{25}
\]
The base \(\eta=(16/81)5^{-1/8}\) exceeds \(1/8\):
\((8\eta)^8=(128/81)^8/5>(3/2)^8/5>1\). Thus both (24) and (25) give at least \((4/5)^4 8^{-n}\), which is more than \(c8^{-(n+1)}\) and hence at least the claimed bound.

For \(n\ge N_*\) outside these two regimes, (1) applies and \(327\sqrt n\le\delta n\). Consequently
\(M_3(p)\ge c8^{-n}\ge\kappa8^{-(n+1)}\).

Finally suppose \(1\le n<N_*\). The set
\(Q_T=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\sum_i x_i^2\le t\le T\}\) is compact and convex. Its full lattice support is \(A_T=Q_T\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), and
\(A_T=\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\cap\mathbb Z^{n+1}\): the forward inclusion is immediate, and the reverse follows from \(\operatorname{conv}(A_T)\subseteq Q_T\). The original zero-extended law has the globally log-concave extension
\(e^{-\beta t}\mathbf1_{Q_T}(x,t)/Z_T(\beta)\). Thus the unconditional cubic estimate (8) of accepted fact `3bcbf21f33edf564`, with \(N=n+1\), yields
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{8^{-(n+1)}}{32[(n+5)\log2]^3}
\ge\frac{8^{-(n+1)}}{32[(N_*+5)\log2]^3}
\ge\kappa8^{-(n+1)}.
\]
This proves the conclusion in every dimension and every rate, including every finite upper cutoff.
