## Statement

Let $K$, $u_j$, $\rho_j=1/3$, $Q$ and $\nu_V$ be the centered triangle, covariance-unit normals, weights, covariance quadratic and cone probability of the centered triangle menu. Here $V=9/2$. Use the actual outward uniform kernel $\Psi(s)=\min(1,\max(0,1+s))$, width $h>0$, and tied buffer $\varepsilon f(y)$, with $f(y)=(E_K(y)/V)^{1/3}$ extended by zero at the boundary. Normalize every normal by $\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}=1$, and evaluate the actual cap with threshold $u\cdot y-\varepsilon f(y)$.

For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon,h>0$ the deepest mark is uniquely $0$ and its minimizing normals are exactly $u_0,u_1,u_2$. Their common normalized cap mass is
$$
\beta_{\varepsilon,h}=
\frac{4+2\sqrt2(\varepsilon+h/2)+(\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon h+h^2/3)/2}{9};
$$
their common raw mass is $V\beta_{\varepsilon,h}$. At those actual caps the strip coarea $S$ and width derivative $A$ are common with $S>0$ and $-hS\le A\le0$. In the one-group common-budget response, the positive source-plus-covariance height density per unit $S\varepsilon$ is $(1+t_hQ)\nu_V$, where $t_h=1-A/(S\varepsilon)\in[1,1+h/\varepsilon]$. Along every positive-width choice with $h=o(\varepsilon)$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, the fraction of this height measure captured by each of its own actual smoothed cap profiles, or by its actual closed buffered halfspace, tends to $100/243<4/9$. Thus for all sufficiently small parameters along such a choice the normalized positive density underpays $\Gamma_2$.

## Proof

For a centered vertex-value triple $a,-a(1+s)/2,-a(1-s)/2$ with $a>0$ and $|s|\le1/2$, the norm is $a\sqrt{(3+s^2)/24}$ by the barycentric moments in the centered menu. A cap at outward covariance displacement $q$ still isolates the positive vertex for $0\le q\le1/100$, since the other values are at most $-a/4$ and the displacement in this scale is less than $a/4$. The product of the two intercepted edge ratios gives its normalized area
$$
\frac{4}{9-s^2}\left(1+q\sqrt{(3+s^2)/24}\right)^2.
$$
For each such $q$ this strictly increases with $s^2$. When the triple has just one positive value and $1/2\le |s|\le1$, the undisplaced area is already at least $16/35$ by the same edge ratios at $q=0$. The other sign pattern, including a zero value, can be scaled to $1,b,-1-b$ with $0\le b\le1$, whose complementary vertex triangle has area fraction $(1+b)^2/[(2+b)(1+2b)]\le1/2$. Nonnegative outward displacements only increase either bound. At $s=0$ the fraction is $(4+2\sqrt2q+q^2/2)/9<16/35$ for $q\le1/100$. Thus the exact minimizing directions for every $q$ in this range are the three canonical rays. In unit covariance scale, the uniform ramp is the exact average over thresholds displaced by $q\in[\varepsilon,\varepsilon+h]$ from the centroid plane, since $f(0)=1$. This proves the full minimizing statement for $\varepsilon+h\le1/100$ and averaging $q,q^2$ gives $\beta_{\varepsilon,h}$.

The attained energy theorem d90ba05577427854 gives $f(y)<1$ for every noncentroid interior mark, and the boundary extension gives zero there. For $y\ne0$, at least one of the $u_j\cdot y$ is nonnegative because $\sum_j u_j=0$. The threshold at this mark in that direction is therefore strictly higher than the same cap threshold at $0$. For every displaced cut in the ramp average, the old centroid cut is proper, so increasing its threshold strictly decreases its area, even if the new cut becomes empty. The depth at $y$ is at most this strictly smaller cap value. The old caps at $0$ have a common budget and cover $K$, since their through-centroid versions already cover it.

The affine permutations of the vertices preserve volume, covariance scale, and the centroid, and permute the three caps. Consequently their strip data agree. Write $c=-\varepsilon$, $r_j(X)=(u_j\cdot X-c)/h$ and $\psi=\mathbf1_{(-1,0)}$. Then $S=\int_K\psi(r_j)\,dX/h>0$ since the strip is proper, and $A=\int_K r_j\psi(r_j)\,dX$ lies in $[-hS,0]$. These are the data of the actual uniform cap on the original body.

For clarity, take an admissible signed inward normal speed $v$, write $D=\int_{\partial K}v\,dS$, $J_j=\int_{\partial K}(u_j\cdot X)^2v\,dS$ and $B_j=\int_{\partial K}\Psi(r_j)v\,dS$. At fixed $u_j$ with old unit covariance length, differentiating the centered covariance gives $\dot\Gamma_j=(D-J_j)/(2V)$, since the first moment at the old centroid is zero. At a threshold velocity $\dot c_j$, differentiating the ramp on the fixed domain gives captured-volume loss $S\dot c_j+A\dot\Gamma_j$, with the two kink planes of zero area, and the direct shell loss adds $B_j$. Since $S>0$, each nearby actual cap has a differentiable local inverse threshold for the same raw mass parameter. Choose the common mass so that the $\rho_j$-weighted mean of these thresholds equals the minimum over current marks of the $\rho_j$-weighted mean of their buffered through-mark thresholds. The inverse mean is strictly decreasing, so this equality has a local solution. At every current mark at least one through-mark threshold is at least its selected inverse threshold, giving a cover at the common raw mass. Differentiating this relation uses the $\rho_j$-average of the through-source threshold derivatives. At the old singleton contact this is $-\varepsilon(\dot f(0)+\sum_j\rho_j\dot\Gamma_j)$, by evaluation of the old supporting contact. Equivalently, for these fixed balanced normals and a varying single body the minimized threshold bracket is attained at the centroid because $f_t$ is uniquely maximized there, and $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=0$ cancels the mark term identically. Hence the average of the three loss equations gives common-budget loss
$$
\sum_j\rho_j B_j-\varepsilon S\dot f(0)
+(A-\varepsilon S)\sum_j\rho_j\dot\Gamma_j.
$$
The fixed-mark radial energy shell calculation in 5efc62439ac46cfb applies on an interior neighborhood of $0$ for the signed radial paths with its uniform shell bound. It gives $\dot f(0)=-\int_{\partial K}q_Hv\,dS+D/(2V)$ since $f(0)=1$. Here $q_H=1/(2V)$, from $P=1$ and $E=V$. Thus the positive source coefficient is $e=S\varepsilon$, and the positive covariance coefficient of each $(u_j\cdot X)^2/(2V)$ is $(S\varepsilon-A)/3$. Multiplying just these positive terms by boundary height and dividing by $e$ gives $(1+t_hQ)\nu_V$. This calculation uses the actual one-group response and does not impose a body-minimizing stationarity condition.

As $h=o(\varepsilon)$, the strip bound gives $t_h\to1$. The actual boundary ramp and the actual buffered closed cap indicator each converge $\nu_V$-almost everywhere to the corresponding centroid cap indicator; its proper cutting line meets the old boundary in two points. The triangle is bounded, $Q$ is bounded there, and dominated convergence gives captured mass $4/9+64/81$ and total mass $3$ in the limit. Their quotient is $100/243$.
