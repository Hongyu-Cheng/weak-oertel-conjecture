## Statement

First extend the scalar expressions for fiber volumes to an arbitrary table \((w_z)_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\) of finite nonnegative real numbers with finite support, where \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(W:=\sum_z w_z>0\). The scalar assertion does not assume that the table is realized by a convex body. Assume that whenever \(x,y\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfy \(x\equiv y\pmod 2\) and \(w_xw_y>0\), their integer midpoint has
\(w_{(x+y)/2}^{2}\ge w_xw_y\). In particular, every such midpoint has positive weight. Congruence is coordinatewise, and all logarithms are natural.

For every center \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_j>0\), put \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\) and \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\}\). The dependence of \(\alpha\) on the currently fixed center is suppressed, as in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. For \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), put \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\). For each real \(L\ge0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)&:=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v),
&\mathsf H_j(L)&:=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}},\\
K_j(w)&:=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},
&K(w)&:=\max_{j:w_j>0}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\]

All displacement sums are oriented and include zero. The curvature is finite, nonnegative, and even on \(\mathcal U_j\), and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). Each maximum over \(L\) in this statement, including the Hellinger maximum below, is attained at a member of the finite set \(\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\). The maximum over centers is also attained.

For \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let \(W_b:=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}w_z\), and put \(q:=\sum_b(W_b/W)^2\). Thus \(2^{-n}\le q\le1\). This scalar \(q\) is the parity-collision probability and is distinct from the even thinning family \((q_v)\) in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`. Define \(F_j:=w_j^{-1}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\).

There exists a single positive integer center \(j\), chosen before either cutoff, for which \(F_j\ge qW\) and both

\[
\begin{aligned}
K(w)\ge K_j(w)
&\ge\frac W2\left(1-\sqrt{1-q^2}\right)
\ge\frac{q^2W}{4}
\ge\frac14\,4^{-n}W,\\
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{\mathsf H_j(L)}{1+L}
&\ge\frac{F_j}{3}
\ge\frac{qW}{3}
\ge\frac13\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently, let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body with \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). Now give \(C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)\), and \(h_S(p):=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\) exactly their canonical meanings from accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`; empty and lower-dimensional fibers have zero volume, and \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. When \(W>0\), these actual weights satisfy the scalar hypotheses. Put \(\ell:=-\log(1-e^{-1})\). When \(W>0\), the same center \(j\) selected above supports a point \(p^\star=(j,x^\star)\in S\) satisfying

\[
h_S(p^\star)
\ge\frac{\ell W}{32}\left(1-\sqrt{1-q^2}\right)
\ge\frac{\ell q^2W}{64}
\ge\frac{\ell}{64}\,4^{-n}W.
\]

The point is chosen before the halfspace, so this bound holds for every closed ambient halfspace containing it. The maximizing minimum-weight and Hellinger cutoffs may differ, but their integer center is the same. When \(W=0\), every point of the assumed nonempty set \(S\) has depth at least zero; no \(q\) or positive center is required in that case.

## Proof

Fix first an abstract table satisfying the scalar hypotheses. Its positive support is finite and nonempty. For any positive center \(j\), the set \(\mathcal U_j\) is finite, invariant under \(v\mapsto-v\), and contains zero. Every logarithm defining \(\Delta_j\) has positive finite arguments. The endpoints \(j+v,j-v\) have the same parity, so midpoint logconcavity gives \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\). Its evenness and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\) follow directly. The zero displacement contributes \(w_j\) to both cutoff sums at every \(L\ge0\).

For this fixed center, let \(T_j:=\{\Delta_j(v):v\in\mathcal U_j\}\). This finite nonempty set contains zero. Given any real \(L\ge0\), its largest member \(t\le L\) exists, and both cutoff sums have exactly the same summands at \(L\) and \(t\). Dividing these nonnegative sums by \(1+t\le1+L\) shows that both cutoff ratios are maximized at members of \(T_j\). The finitely many positive centers then give attainment of the maximum over centers. This argument also proves that every cutoff used below is a finite real curvature value. The auxiliary notation \(T_j\) here denotes only this finite set of thresholds.

The parity masses are nonnegative and sum to \(W\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(W^2=(\sum_bW_b)^2\le2^n\sum_bW_b^2\), whereas expansion of the square of their sum gives \(\sum_bW_b^2\le W^2\). Thus \(2^{-n}\le q\le1\).

There is a bijection between ordered positive same-parity endpoint pairs \((x,y)\) and pairs \((j,v)\) with \(w_j>0\) and \(v\in\mathcal U_j\): it sends \((x,y)\) to \(((x+y)/2,(x-y)/2)\), with inverse \((j,v)\mapsto(j+v,j-v)\). Integrality follows from the parity assumption, and midpoint positivity follows from the scalar hypothesis. Conversely, the two endpoints of every such reflected pair are positive and have the same parity. Diagonal endpoint pairs correspond exactly to zero displacements. Consequently,

\[
\sum_{j:w_j>0}w_jF_j
=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in\mathbb Z^n\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}w_xw_y
=\sum_bW_b^2
=qW^2.
\]

The weighted average of the finitely many \(F_j\), with probability weights \(w_j/W\), is therefore \(qW\). Select once and for all a positive center \(j\) with \(F_j\ge qW\). Every remaining scalar quantity in the proof is evaluated at this center.

Put \(\beta(v):=\max\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), and define the discounted minimum-weight mass \(\mathscr D_j:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\alpha(v)e^{-\Delta_j(v)}\). This is an auxiliary scalar, not a halfspace-depth function or a relative entropy. The finite layer-cake identity gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr D_j
&=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\alpha(v)\int_{\Delta_j(v)}^\infty e^{-L}\,dL
=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}B_j^\Delta(L)\,dL\\
&\le K_j(w)\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)\,dL
=2K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\]

There is no endpoint omission at zero curvature: the integral beginning at zero equals one, so the zero displacement contributes exactly \(w_j\) to \(\mathscr D_j\). Interchanging the sum and integral is valid because the sum is finite.

Since \(\alpha(v)\beta(v)=w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\) and \(e^{-\Delta_j(v)}=w_{j+v}w_{j-v}/w_j^2\), each summand in \(F_j\) is \(\sqrt{\alpha(v)e^{-\Delta_j(v)}}\sqrt{\beta(v)}\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives \(F_j^2\le\mathscr D_j\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\beta(v)\). On the other hand, symmetry of \(\mathcal U_j\) gives the exact endpoint identity

\[
\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\bigl(\alpha(v)+\beta(v)\bigr)
=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\bigl(w_{j+v}+w_{j-v}\bigr)
=2\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}w_{j+v}
\le2W.
\]

The last inequality uses injectivity of \(v\mapsto j+v\). Also \(e^{-\Delta_j(v)}\le1\) and \(\alpha(v)\le w_{j+v}\), so \(0\le\mathscr D_j\le\sum_v\alpha(v)\le W\). It follows that

\[
q^2W^2\le F_j^2
\le\mathscr D_j\left(2W-\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\alpha(v)\right)
\le\mathscr D_j(2W-\mathscr D_j).
\]

Equivalently, \((W-\mathscr D_j)^2\le W^2(1-q^2)\). Because \(0\le\mathscr D_j\le W\) and \(0\le q\le1\), taking nonnegative square roots gives \(\mathscr D_j\ge W(1-\sqrt{1-q^2})\). Combining this with \(\mathscr D_j\le2K_j(w)\) proves the first lower bound for \(K_j(w)\). For \(0\le t\le1\), rationalizing gives \(1-\sqrt{1-t}=t/(1+\sqrt{1-t})\ge t/2\). Use \(t=q^2\) and \(q\ge2^{-n}\) to obtain all remaining minimum-weight bounds.

At the same center, the identity \(e^{-\Delta_j(v)/2}=\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}/w_j\) gives a second finite layer-cake calculation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_j
&=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}\sqrt{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}\,e^{-\Delta_j(v)/2}\\
&=\int_0^\infty \tfrac12 e^{-L/2}\mathsf H_j(L)\,dL\\
&\le\left(\max_{L\ge0}\frac{\mathsf H_j(L)}{1+L}\right)
\int_0^\infty \tfrac12 e^{-L/2}(1+L)\,dL\\
&=3\max_{L\ge0}\frac{\mathsf H_j(L)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the constant is \(1+2=3\), obtained from the zeroth and first moments of the displayed exponential density. The zero displacement again contributes exactly \(w_j\). Together with \(F_j\ge qW\), this proves the Hellinger assertion without changing the center.

Now take an actual convex body as in the statement, with \(W>0\). Its compact base projection meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points, so its actual volume table has finite support. To check midpoint positivity before using the accepted curvature statement, take positive same-parity endpoint fibers \(C_x,C_y\). Each is a full-dimensional compact convex set and contains a Euclidean ball of positive radius. Convexity of the common body gives \((C_x+C_y)/2\subseteq C_{(x+y)/2}\). The left side contains a ball of positive radius, namely the midpoint sum of those two balls. Hence the integer midpoint fiber has positive volume. With this positivity established, the midpoint-volume inequality recorded in the proof of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`, using these same actual fibers and volumes, gives

\[
w_{(x+y)/2}^{1/d}
\ge\frac{w_x^{1/d}+w_y^{1/d}}2
\ge(w_xw_y)^{1/(2d)}.
\]

Raising to the power \(2d\) proves the scalar midpoint hypothesis. The second inequality is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for the two positive numbers \(w_x^{1/d},w_y^{1/d}\). Thus the scalar construction applies to every actual table in the statement.

Retain its parity-selected center \(j\), and choose a finite curvature threshold \(L_B\) attaining \(K_j(w)\). Apply the fixed-center even-thinning assertion of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14` with its coefficients \(q_v:=\alpha(v)\mathbf1_{\{v\in\mathcal U_j,\ \Delta_j(v)\le L_B\}}\), setting them to zero outside \(\mathcal U_j\). They are even, obey \(0\le q_v\le\alpha(v)\), and have positive total mass \(M_q=B_j^\Delta(L_B)\ge w_j\) because zero is retained. In that predecessor's notation their average curvature satisfies \(\overline\Delta_{j,q}\le L_B\). All its other geometric objects are those of the same actual body, with unchanged fiber-volume normalization and unchanged oriented displacement convention. The predecessor therefore supplies one point \((j,x^\star)\in S\) with

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((j,x^\star))
&\ge\frac{\ell B_j^\Delta(L_B)}{16(\overline\Delta_{j,q}+\log2)}
\ge\frac{\ell B_j^\Delta(L_B)}{16(1+L_B)}\\
&=\frac{\ell}{16}K_j(w)
\ge\frac{\ell W}{32}\left(1-\sqrt{1-q^2}\right)
\ge\frac{\ell q^2W}{64}
\ge\frac{\ell}{64}\,4^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

The predecessor's pointwise conclusion already includes every closed ambient halfspace containing the chosen point, including vertical and boundary cases. The center was fixed before both scalar cutoffs; \(L_B\) and the point were then fixed before the halfspace. This is the asserted quantifier order. If \(W=0\), every such halfspace has nonnegative mixed-integer volume, so any point of the assumed nonempty mixed set gives the zero lower bound.
