## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(d=2\), and let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body. For \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), set
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_2(C_z),
 \qquad
 Z:=\{z:a_z>0\},
\]
and put
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\]
Assume \(0<G<W/2\). Choose a saturated maximum-matching selected profile and an admissible integral centerpoint as in accepted fact 6974f69acff32c8c. Thus \(t_z\) is extended by zero to \(Z\), and, with
\[
 E:=\{z\in Z:t_z>0\},
 \qquad
 T:=\sum_{z\in E}t_z,
 \qquad
 U:=\{z\in Z:t_z<a_z\},
\]
one has
\[
 0<t_z\leq a_z\quad(z\in E),
 \qquad
 t_z=a_z\quad(z\in Z\setminus U),
\]
\[
 \sum_{z\in U}a_z\leq G,
 \qquad
 W-T\leq G.
\tag{1}
\]
The centerpoint \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z\geq2^{-n}T
\tag{2}
\]
for every proper closed base halfspace \(J\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\).

For \(z\in E\), define
\[
 D_z:=\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z),
\]
and, for
\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,
 \ \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m\right\},
\]
put
\[
 \mathcal Q_\lambda:=\sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zD_z.
\]
Assume the complete core is empty:
\[
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}\mathcal Q_\lambda=\varnothing.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(v\in\mathbb R^2\), let
\[
 \phi_t(v):=
 \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}h_{\mathcal Q_\lambda}(v)
 =\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zh_{D_z}(v).
\tag{4}
\]
Assume that the minimum support cardinality among all families of nonzero directions \(w_i\), positive weights \(\theta_i\), and cardinalities \(r\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^r\theta_iw_i=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^r\theta_i\phi_t(w_i)<0
\tag{5}
\]
is exactly \(r=3\). Fix a corresponding circuit and the exact affine minorants \(\ell_i\) supplied by accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb, and let
\[
 A_i:=C\cap
 \{(z,x):\langle w_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\},
 \qquad 1\leq i\leq3.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\) be the descendant-retention capacity used in accepted fact 6974f69acff32c8c: the supremum of \(W(B)\) over all individual cap bodies and all strictly positive same-base circuit reweightings arising from maximum-matching selected profiles, admissible integral centerpoints with empty complete core, and support-minimal circuit data. Fix \(0<\eta\leq1\) and assume
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\leq\frac{\eta}{6}W.
\tag{7}
\]

Define the full-selected-mass base set
\[
 S:=\{z\in E:t_z=a_z\}=E\setminus U.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(S\) is a base set, not the scalar denoted by \(S\) in equation (4) of accepted fact 6974f69acff32c8c. Then
\[
 m\notin\operatorname{conv}S,
 \qquad
 \frac GW\geq\frac1{2^n+1},
 \qquad
 \eta\leq\frac35.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(n\geq4\). If \(n=4\), then
\[
 \frac6{17}\leq\eta\leq\frac35,
 \qquad
 \frac1{17}\leq\frac GW\leq\frac\eta6.
\tag{10}
\]

## Proof

We first prove \(m\notin\operatorname{conv}S\), using the convention \(\operatorname{conv}\varnothing=\varnothing\). If \(S=\varnothing\), the conclusion is immediate. Hence suppose \(S\neq\varnothing\) and, for a contradiction, that \(m\in\operatorname{conv}S\). There is then a vector \(\lambda^0\in\Lambda_m(E)\) supported on \(S\).

For \(z\in S\), one has \(t_z=a_z\). In the definition of \(\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)\), an admissible density \(f\) satisfies \(0\leq f\leq1\) and
\[
 \int_{C_z}f=a_z=\operatorname{vol}_2(C_z).
\]
Thus \(f=1\) almost everywhere, and
\[
 D_z=\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)=\{c_z\},
\tag{11}
\]
where \(c_z\) is the centroid of \(C_z\). It follows that
\[
 \mathcal Q_{\lambda^0}=\{q\},
 \qquad
 q:=\sum_{z\in S}\lambda_z^0c_z.
\tag{12}
\]

If \(q\) belonged to every \(\mathcal Q_\lambda\), it would belong to the complete core in (3). Therefore some \(\mu\in\Lambda_m(E)\) satisfies \(q\notin\mathcal Q_\mu\). Each \(D_z\), and hence \(\mathcal Q_\mu\), is a nonempty compact convex set by accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb. Strict separation of the point \(q\) from \(\mathcal Q_\mu\) supplies \(v\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}\) such that
\[
 h_{\mathcal Q_\mu}(v)<\langle v,q\rangle.
\tag{13}
\]
Using (4), (12), and (13),
\[
 \phi_t(v)\leq h_{\mathcal Q_\mu}(v)<\langle v,q\rangle,
 \qquad
 \phi_t(-v)\leq h_{\mathcal Q_{\lambda^0}}(-v)
 =-\langle v,q\rangle.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac12\phi_t(v)+\frac12\phi_t(-v)<0,
 \qquad
 \frac12v+\frac12(-v)=0.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(v,-v\), with weights \(1/2,1/2\), form a balanced negative family of support cardinality two. This contradicts the assumption that the minimum cardinality in (5) is three. Hence \(m\notin\operatorname{conv}S\).

This argument does not require \(S\) to have full affine dimension. If \(S\) is a singleton or is otherwise affinely degenerate, \(m\in\operatorname{conv}S\) still supplies \(\lambda^0\), (11) still makes \(\mathcal Q_{\lambda^0}\) a singleton, and strict separation in (13) is valid even if \(\mathcal Q_\mu\) is lower-dimensional. When \(S\) is empty, the asserted nonmembership was immediate, and the halfspace construction below still applies.

Because \(S\) is finite and \(m\notin\operatorname{conv}S\), there is a proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) with \(J\cap S=\varnothing\). Indeed, if \(S\neq\varnothing\), strict separation of \(m\) from the compact convex set \(\operatorname{conv}S\) gives such a halfspace. If \(S=\varnothing\), choose any nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^n\) and take
\[
 J:=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\langle u,x\rangle\geq\langle u,m\rangle\}.
\]
The centerpoint inequality (2), the disjointness \(J\cap S=\varnothing\), and the saturation bounds (1) give, with every inequality in the indicated direction,
\[
 2^{-n}T
 \leq\sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z
 \leq\sum_{z\in E\cap J}a_z
 \leq\sum_{z\in U}a_z
 \leq G.
\tag{16}
\]
Here \(E\cap J\subseteq U\) because a point of \(E\setminus U\) belongs to \(S\). Since \(W-T\leq G\), one also has \(T\geq W-G\). Combining this with (16) yields
\[
 2^{-n}(W-G)\leq G,
\]
and hence
\[
 W\leq(2^n+1)G,
 \qquad
 \frac GW\geq\frac1{2^n+1}.
\tag{17}
\]

In dimension two,
\[
 \gamma_2=\left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac49.
\tag{18}
\]
Accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb gives, for every individual circuit cap in (6),
\[
 W(A_i)\geq\gamma_2T=\frac49T.
\tag{19}
\]
Each \(A_i\) is one of the descendants whose masses are bounded above by \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\), as stated in accepted fact 6974f69acff32c8c. Therefore (7), (19), \(T\geq W-G\), and \(G\leq\eta W/6\) imply
\[
 (1-\eta)W
 \geq R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)
 \geq W(A_i)
 \geq\frac49T
 \geq\frac49(W-G)
 \geq\frac49\left(1-\frac\eta6\right)W.
\tag{20}
\]
After division by \(W>0\), (20) gives
\[
 1-\eta\geq\frac49\left(1-\frac\eta6\right).
\]
Multiplication by \(27\) gives \(27-27\eta\geq12-2\eta\), so \(15\geq25\eta\), and hence
\[
 \eta\leq\frac35.
\tag{21}
\]
In particular, (7) and (21) give
\[
 \frac GW\leq\frac\eta6\leq\frac1{10}.
\tag{22}
\]

Combining (17) and (22) gives
\[
 \frac1{2^n+1}\leq\frac1{10}.
\]
Thus \(2^n+1\geq10\). Since \(n\) is a positive integer, \(n\geq4\).

Finally suppose \(n=4\). Equation (17) becomes
\[
 \frac GW\geq\frac1{17}.
\]
Together with (7), this gives
\[
 \frac1{17}\leq\frac GW\leq\frac\eta6,
\]
so \(\eta\geq6/17\). Combining this with (21) proves (10).
