## Statement

Let \(r,m\) be positive integers, put \(a=6r\), \(b=6r+1\), and \(Q=18r^2\), and define
\[
D=\{(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:
             \textstyle\sum_i x_i+ay_1+by_2\le Q\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)=\frac{e^{\lambda(ay_1+by_2)}}{\mathcal Z}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal Z\) sums the numerator over every original point of \(D\). Let \(F_m(u)=\binom{u+m}{m}\) for integer \(u\ge0\), and let \(n(q)\) count all nonnegative integer solutions of \(ay_1+by_2=q\). Set \(H_0=\sum_{i=1}^m(Q+i)^{-1}\). For occupied endpoints \(0\le q_0\le q_1\le Q\), use exactly the complete-band coefficient of d1c92fee58caca38:
\[
\eta_{q_0,q_1}
=\frac{F_m(Q-q_1)}{\mathcal Z}
       \left(\sum_{q=q_0}^{q_1}n(q)\right)
\begin{cases}
e^{\lambda q_0},&\lambda\ge0,\\
e^{\lambda q_1},&\lambda<0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\eta_* =\max_{q_0,q_1}\eta_{q_0,q_1}.
\]
For every \(\lambda>H_0\),
\[
\eta_*\ge\frac{1}{3000000(m+1)^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, this coefficient does not degenerate as \(r\) tends to infinity.

Using the unchanged original-root third moment \(M_3(p)\), and \(g_0>1\) exactly as in d1c92fee58caca38, the same family at every real \(\lambda\) satisfies
\[
M_3(p)\ge
\frac{g_0^m}{32\,3000000^4(m+1)^8}\,8^{-(m+2)}
\ge
\frac{1}{32\,3000000^4g_0}
       \left(\frac{\log g_0}{8}\right)^8 8^{-(m+2)}.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

Put \(K=3r\), so \(a=2K\), \(b=2K+1\), and \(Q=2K^2\). For a heavy pair of total degree \(s=y_1+y_2\), its height is \(as+y_2\). Degrees above \(K\) are excluded, and degree \(K\) contributes exactly the exposed pair \((K,0)\), of height \(Q\). Every pair of degree at most \(K-1\) is included, since its height is at most \(b(K-1)<Q\). Thus the heavy support is exactly this exposed point together with the complete triangle \(y_1,y_2\ge0\), \(y_1+y_2\le K-1\).

For the triangle, introduce the integer coordinates \(j_1=y_1\), \(j_2=K-1-y_1-y_2\). This is a bijection onto \(j_1,j_2\ge0\), \(j_1+j_2\le K-1\). The original residual light budget is
\[
u=Q-ay_1-by_2=u_{\rm base}+j_1+bj_2,
\qquad u_{\rm base}=K+1.
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(j_2=j\), the occupied budgets form the integer interval
\(u_{\rm base}+bj,\ldots,u_{\rm base}+bj+K-1-j\), of length \(\ell_j=K-j\), where \(0\le j\le K-1\). These intervals are disjoint. Their successive gaps have size at most \(b\), and the smallest distance between points in different intervals is at least \(K+2\). Their union, denoted \(\mathcal U\), has smallest budget \(u_{\rm base}\) and largest budget \(Q\). In particular, the first occupied strip is \(u_{\rm base},\ldots,2K\), namely the residual gaps \(3r+1,\ldots,6r\) in the assignment.

Every heavy point has a different height: within the triangle this follows from the disjoint intervals, and the exposed point has residual budget zero. Consequently the exact original normalizer is
\[
e^{-\lambda Q}\mathcal Z=1+Z_{\triangle},
\qquad
Z_{\triangle}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}F_m(u)e^{-\lambda u}.
\tag{4}
\]
The term one is the exposed point, with its unique zero-budget light point. Each summand in \(Z_{\triangle}\) includes all \(F_m(u)\) light points at that heavy point. No residue or degree is discarded.

We first prove a count estimate for the triangle. For any occupied center \(\widehat u\in\mathcal U\), let
\(A(R)=|\mathcal U\cap[\widehat u-R,\widehat u+R]|\) for \(R>0\). Then, for every real \(t\ge1\),
\[
A(tR)\le128t^2A(R).
\tag{5}
\]
Write \(\widehat u=u_{\rm base}+bj_0+i_0\), with \(0\le i_0\le K-1-j_0\), and put \(\widehat\ell=K-j_0\). A row contributing to \(A(T)\) must satisfy \(|j-j_0|<T/b+1/2\), because both within-row coordinates lie between zero and \(K-1<b/2\). Therefore
\[
A(T)\le(2T/b+3)(\widehat\ell+T/b+1).
\tag{6}
\]
Also the center row alone gives
\(A(R)\ge\min\{\lfloor R\rfloor+1,\widehat\ell\}\ge\frac12\min\{R+1,\widehat\ell\}\).

Suppose first that \(R<b\), and write \(T=tR\). If \(T<K\), only the center row contributes, so
\(A(T)\le\min\{2tR+1,\widehat\ell\}\le2t\min\{R+1,\widehat\ell\}\le4tA(R)\).
If \(T\ge K\), put \(x=T/b\). Since \(K\ge3\), one has \(x\ge3/7\), so (6) gives
\(A(T)\le11x\widehat\ell+44x^2\).
Let \(l=\min\{R+1,\widehat\ell\}\). The inequalities \(R<b\), \(\widehat\ell\le K<b\), and \(t\ge1\) give \(x\widehat\ell\le t^2l\): if \(l=\widehat\ell\), use \(x<t\); if \(l=R+1\), use \(x\widehat\ell<tR\). Also \(x^2<t^2\le t^2l\). Hence \(A(T)\le55t^2l\le110t^2A(R)\).

If \(b\le R<2b\), the whole center row is retained, so \(A(R)\ge\widehat\ell\). Formula (6), with \(T/b<2t\), gives
\(A(T)\le7t(\widehat\ell+3t)\le28t^2\widehat\ell\le28t^2A(R)\).

Finally, suppose \(R\ge2b\), and set \(h=\lfloor R/(2b)\rfloor\). Then \(h\ge1\) and \(h\ge R/(4b)\). All rows with \(|j-j_0|\le h\) lie wholly in the radius-\(R\) window, since \(bh+K-1<R\). For real \(w\ge0\), write
\(C(w)=\sum_{0\le j\le K-1,\,|j-j_0|\le w}(K-j)\).
Thus \(A(R)\ge C(h)\), and every row contributing to \(A(tR)\) has \(|j-j_0|\le6th\).

For any positive integer \(k\), one has \(C(kh)\le k^2C(h)\). To see this, map a noncentral row at distance \(d\) from \(j_0\) to the row on the same side at distance \(\lceil d/k\rceil\), and fix the center row. Each noncentral image has at most \(k\) preimages. On the side \(j<j_0\), the original row length \(\widehat\ell+d\) is at most \(k(\widehat\ell+\lceil d/k\rceil)\); on the side \(j>j_0\), the image row length is at least the original length. All image rows remain between zero and \(K-1\), and have distance at most \(h\). Summing proves the claim. Taking \(k=\lceil6t\rceil\le7t\) gives \(A(tR)\le49t^2C(h)\le49t^2A(R)\). These cases establish (5).

Now extend \(F_m(u)\) to real \(u\ge0\) by \(F_m(u)=\prod_{i=1}^m(u+i)/i\), and put
\(f(u)=F_m(u)e^{-\lambda u}\) and \(H(u)=\sum_{i=1}^m(u+i)^{-1}\). This extension agrees with the original counts at every occupied budget. Its logarithmic derivative is \(H(u)-\lambda\), and \(H\) is strictly decreasing. Let \(\widehat u\) maximize \(f\) on \(\mathcal U\).

If \(\lambda\ge H(u_{\rm base})\), take \(\widehat u=u_{\rm base}\); then \(f\) is nonincreasing on \([u_{\rm base},\infty)\). If \(H(Q)<\lambda<H(u_{\rm base})\), there is a unique continuous maximizer \(u_*\in(u_{\rm base},Q)\), with \(H(u_*)=\lambda\). The sampled maximizer \(\widehat u\) is one of the two occupied budgets bracketing \(u_*\), and hence \(|\widehat u-u_*|\le b\). Since \(b=2u_{\rm base}-1\) and \(\widehat u\ge u_{\rm base}\), this gives \(H(\widehat u)\le3H(u_*)=3\lambda\): when \(\widehat u\ge u_*\), use monotonicity; otherwise use \(u_*\le3\widehat u\) in each reciprocal. Moreover,
\[
u_*\le m/\lambda-1,
\qquad b<2m/\lambda,
\qquad \widehat u<3m/\lambda.
\tag{7}
\]
The first follows from \(\lambda=H(u_*)\le m/(u_*+1)\), and the second from \(\lambda<H(u_{\rm base})\le m/(u_{\rm base}+1)\). In both cases we therefore have \(H(\widehat u)\le3\lambda\).

Take \(R=1/(12\lambda)\). The inequality \(H(\widehat u)\ge1/(\widehat u+1)\) implies \(R\le(\widehat u+1)/4\le\widehat u/2\). Thus, for \(u\ge\widehat u-R\), one has \(H(u)\le2H(\widehat u)\le6\lambda\). Let \(u_-\) and \(u_+\) be the smallest and largest occupied budgets in the radius-\(R\) window. They exist because its center is occupied. Integrating the logarithmic derivative of \(F_m\) gives
\[
F_m(u_-)e^{-\lambda u_+}A(R)
\ge e^{-7/12}f(\widehat u)A(R)
>\frac13f(\widehat u)A(R).
\tag{8}
\]
Here the two losses are at most \(6\lambda R=1/2\) for the light-budget minimum and \(\lambda R=1/12\) for the exponential minimum. The interval with endpoints \(q_0=Q-u_+\) and \(q_1=Q-u_-\) is a complete original band and contains exactly \(A(R)\) heavy points. In particular (8) uses the minimum light budget and exponential on that band, rather than its marginal mass.

It remains to bound the full triangle sum in (4) by (8). For all \(u\ge2m/\lambda\),
\((\log f)'(u)=H(u)-\lambda\le-\lambda/2\).
Put \(u_c=\widehat u+4m/\lambda\). Whenever \(u_c\le Q\), we claim that \(f(u_c)\le f(\widehat u)\). In the case \(\lambda\ge H(u_{\rm base})\), this is monotonicity. In the other case, take the largest occupied budget \(v\le u_c\). The gap bound and (7) give
\(v\ge u_c-b>\widehat u+2m/\lambda\ge2m/\lambda\).
Since \(f(v)\le f(\widehat u)\), and \(f\) decreases between \(v\) and \(u_c\), the claim follows. Consequently every occupied \(u\ge u_c\) satisfies
\[
f(u)\le f(\widehat u)
       \exp\bigl(-\tfrac\lambda2(u-\widehat u-4m/\lambda)\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]
If \(u_c>Q\), there are no such occupied budgets and the same tail assertion is vacuous.

Set \(W=4(m+1)/\lambda=48(m+1)R\). Every occupied budget to the left of \(\widehat u\) lies within distance \(W\): in the first case the center is the smallest budget, and in the second case use (7). All occupied values have \(f(u)\le f(\widehat u)\). For each integer \(j\ge1\), the budgets with distance in \((jW,(j+1)W]\) are therefore to the right, and (9) bounds their weights by \(f(\widehat u)e^{-2j}\), since \(j(m+1)-m\ge j\). Applying (5) to the inner window and all these shells gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\triangle}
&\le f(\widehat u)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-2j}A((j+1)W)\\
&\le128\,[48(m+1)]^2 f(\widehat u)A(R)
       \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)^2e^{-2j}\\
&\le884736(m+1)^2 f(\widehat u)A(R).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
For the last inequality, the sum is \((1+e^{-2})/(1-e^{-2})^3<3\), since \(e>2\) implies \(e^{-2}<1/4\), and \((1+1/4)/(1-1/4)^3=80/27<3\). No support mass is omitted by the windows or shells.

By (4), the exposed-point band has coefficient \(1/(1+Z_{\triangle})\). Equations (8) and (10) give a complete triangle band with coefficient at least
\(Z_{\triangle}/[2654208(m+1)^2(1+Z_{\triangle})]\).
For every \(z\ge0\) and \(B>0\),
\(\max\{1,z/B\}/(1+z)\ge1/(1+B)\), because \(1+z\le(1+B)\max\{1,z/B\}\).
Taking \(B=2654208(m+1)^2\), we obtain
\[
\eta_*\ge\frac{1}{1+2654208(m+1)^2}
\ge\frac{1}{3000000(m+1)^2},
\]
which proves (1) with the unchanged original normalizer.

For completeness, d1c92fee58caca38 already gives \(\eta_*\ge1/[8(m+1)^2]\) for \(\lambda\le H_0\), including negative tilts with their stated minimum-exponential convention. Thus the weaker coefficient in (1) holds at every real tilt for this family. The complete-band moment comparison (13) in the proof of d1c92fee58caca38 applies at every tilt and gives
\(M_3(p)\ge\eta_*^4g_0^m8^{-(m+2)}/32\).
Inserting the coefficient proves the first inequality in (2). Finally, \(e^z\ge z\), applied to \(z=(m+1)\log g_0/8\) and raised to the eighth power, yields
\(g_0^m/(m+1)^8\ge g_0^{-1}(\log g_0/8)^8\), proving its second inequality. All depth and root probabilities are those of the original \(p\), as required by the accepted comparison.
