## Statement

Let \(a,b,Q,m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), \(Q\ge2ab\), and \(m\ge1\). Let \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and let
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+ay_1+by_2\le Q\right\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)=\frac{e^{\lambda(ay_1+by_2)}}{\mathcal Z},
\]
where \(\mathcal Z\) sums the displayed numerator over every point of \(D\). Put \(n(q)=|\{(y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:ay_1+by_2=q\}|\) and \(F_m(u)=\binom{u+m}{m}\) for nonnegative integers \(u\). For integers \(0\le q_0\le q_1\le Q\) with \(n(q_0),n(q_1)>0\), define
\[
N_{q_0,q_1}=\sum_{q=q_0}^{q_1}n(q),
\qquad
\eta_{q_0,q_1}
=\frac{F_m(Q-q_1)N_{q_0,q_1}}{\mathcal Z}
\min\{e^{\lambda q_0},e^{\lambda q_1}\},
\qquad
\eta_* =\max_{q_0,q_1}\eta_{q_0,q_1}.
\]
These are exactly the complete original bands of d1c92fee58caca38, under the specialization \(h_1=a\), \(h_2=b\), \(g_{\rm ar}=1\), and \(L=Q\). Then
\[
0\le\lambda\le\frac{4(m+1)}{Q+1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\eta_*\ge\frac{1}{22059(m+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, (1) holds throughout \(H_0<\lambda<4(m+1)/(Q+1)\), where \(H_0=\sum_{i=1}^m(Q+i)^{-1}\). Combining (1) with d1c92fee58caca38 for negative tilts and 3afed55e42e0b924 for the upper-tilt range gives, for every real \(\lambda\),
\[
\eta_*\ge\frac{e^{-13}}{216(m+1)}.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

Write \(P=ab\) and \(k=m+1\ge2\). The exact arithmetic proved in d1c92fee58caca38 gives \(\lfloor q/P\rfloor\le n(q)\le\lfloor q/P\rfloor+1\) for every nonnegative integer \(q\). Consequently, for all integers \(0\le q\le Q\),
\[
n(q)\le\frac{Q}{P}+1\le\frac{3Q}{2P},
\qquad
q\ge\frac Q2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n(q)\ge\frac{Q}{4P}>0.
\tag{3}
\]
For the lower bound, use \(q/P\ge Q/(2P)\ge1\) and \(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\) for \(x\ge1\). The upper bound uses \(Q/P\ge2\).

First suppose \(0\le\lambda\le4k/(Q+1)\). With the residual coordinate \(u=Q-q\), define
\[
A_u=n(Q-u)F_m(u)e^{-\lambda u}\quad(0\le u\le Q),
\qquad
\mathcal Z_{\rm res}=\sum_{u=0}^{Q}A_u=e^{-\lambda Q}\mathcal Z.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, at each heavy height \(q\) there are exactly \(F_m(Q-q)\) light-coordinate points, so (4) sums every original atom. Thus \(A_u/\mathcal Z_{\rm res}\) is exactly the original probability of residual height \(u\), without a change of normalization or conditioning.

If \(Q<64k\), there are \(Q+1\le64k\) possible residual integers. Some \(u\) has \(A_u/\mathcal Z_{\rm res}\ge1/(64k)\), and it is occupied since its mass is positive. Its singleton band \(q_0=q_1=Q-u\) has coefficient exactly \(A_u/\mathcal Z_{\rm res}\). This proves (1) in this case.

Now suppose \(Q\ge64k\). Put \(R=\lfloor Q/64\rfloor\) and \(t=\lceil Q/8\rceil\). For every integer \(0\le u\le R\), the integer \(v=u+t\) satisfies
\[
v>R,
\qquad
v\le\frac{9Q}{64}+1\le\frac Q2,
\qquad
u+m\le\frac Q{32},
\qquad
t\ge\frac Q8,
\qquad
t\le\frac Q8+1\le\frac{Q+1}{4}.
\tag{5}
\]
The last two upper inequalities hold because \(Q\ge64k\ge128\), and \(m<k\le Q/64\). Applying (3) at \(Q-v\ge Q/2\), and its global upper bound at \(Q-u\), gives \(n(Q-v)\ge n(Q-u)/6\). The product formula for \(F_m\), together with (5), gives
\[
\frac{F_m(v)}{F_m(u)}
=\prod_{i=1}^m\left(1+\frac{t}{u+i}\right)
\ge\left(1+\frac{t}{u+m}\right)^m
\ge5^m,
\qquad
\lambda t\le k.
\tag{6}
\]
It follows directly from (4) that
\[
A_{u+t}\ge\frac{5^m e^{-k}}6 A_u\ge\frac{1}{6e}A_u.
\tag{7}
\]
For the last inequality, \(k=m+1\) and \(e<3<5\) give \(5^m e^{-k}=e^{-1}(5/e)^m\ge e^{-1}\).

The shift \(u\mapsto u+t\) is injective and, by (5), maps all integers \(0,\ldots,R\) into \(R+1,\ldots,Q\). Therefore, with \(A=\sum_{u=0}^R A_u\) and \(B=\sum_{u=R+1}^Q A_u\), summing (7) yields \(B\ge A/(6e)\). Since \(\mathcal Z_{\rm res}=A+B>0\), this proves the original-mass estimate
\[
\frac{B}{\mathcal Z_{\rm res}}\ge\frac{1}{1+6e}.
\tag{8}
\]
Only the comparison of shifted small residuals used dense multiplicities. The mass \(B\) in (8) retains all the other original residues, including any unoccupied heights.

Let \(w=\lfloor Q/(64k)\rfloor\ge1\). Partition the consecutive residual integers \(R+1,\ldots,Q\) into consecutive blocks of \(w\) integers, with the last block possibly shorter. Because \(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\) for \(x\ge1\), the number \(J\) of blocks satisfies
\[
J=\left\lceil\frac{Q-R}{w}\right\rceil
\le\frac Qw+1\le128k+1\le129k.
\tag{9}
\]
By (8), one block has original residual probability at least \(1/[(1+6e)J]\ge1/[129k(1+6e)]\). Let \(u_0,u_1\) be respectively the smallest and largest occupied residuals in this block. They exist because the block has positive mass. Removing its unoccupied endpoints loses no mass or heavy pairs. We have
\[
u_0\ge R+1>\frac Q{64},
\qquad
0\le u_1-u_0\le w-1\le\frac{Q}{64k}.
\tag{10}
\]
For any residual integer \(u_0\le u\le u_1\), \(1+s\le e^s\) and (10) bound the two factors separately:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F_m(u)}{F_m(u_0)}
&=\prod_{i=1}^m\left(1+\frac{u-u_0}{u_0+i}\right)
\le\exp\left(\frac{m(u_1-u_0)}{u_0+1}\right)\le e,\\
\frac{e^{-\lambda u}}{e^{-\lambda u_1}}
&=e^{\lambda(u_1-u)}
\le\exp\left(\frac{4k}{Q+1}\frac{Q}{64k}\right)\le e^{1/16}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
No log-concavity of the multiplicity-weighted residual table is used. Multiplying (11), multiplying by each actual \(n(Q-u)\), and summing gives
\[
\sum_{u=u_0}^{u_1}A_u
\le e^{17/16}F_m(u_0)e^{-\lambda u_1}
\sum_{u=u_0}^{u_1}n(Q-u).
\tag{12}
\]
Take \(q_0=Q-u_1\) and \(q_1=Q-u_0\). These are occupied endpoints; the sum in (12) is exactly \(N_{q_0,q_1}\), including every heavy pair at every intervening height. Since \(\lambda\ge0\), (4) and the original definition of the coefficient give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta_{q_0,q_1}
&=\frac{F_m(u_0)e^{-\lambda u_1}}{\mathcal Z_{\rm res}}
\sum_{u=u_0}^{u_1}n(Q-u)\\
&\ge e^{-17/16}\frac{\sum_{u=u_0}^{u_1}A_u}{\mathcal Z_{\rm res}}
\ge\frac{e^{-17/16}}{129k(1+6e)}
\ge\frac{1}{22059k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(e^{17/16}<e^2<9\), \(1+6e<19\), and \(9\cdot129\cdot19=22059\). The retained band has the same mass as its selected block, because only unoccupied endpoints were removed. This proves (1).

For the all-tilt consequence (2), when \(\lambda\ge4k/(Q+1)\), apply 3afed55e42e0b924 with exactly the same \(a,b,Q,m,\lambda,n,F_m,\mathcal Z\), and complete-band coefficient; its conclusion is \(\eta_*\ge e^{-13}/(216k)\). On \(0\le\lambda\le4k/(Q+1)\), (1) is stronger, since \(216e^{13}>216\cdot2^{13}>22059\). Finally, for \(\lambda<0\), d1c92fee58caca38 under the explicit specialization in the statement gives \(\eta_*\ge(1-e^{-1})^3/(e+1)>1/32\). This exceeds \(e^{-13}/(216k)\), since \(k\ge2\) and \(e^{-13}<1\). These three ranges cover every real tilt and prove (2), with the original minimum-exponential convention in each range.
