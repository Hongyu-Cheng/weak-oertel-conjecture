## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$ and $0<a<1$. Let $H=(1-a)/a$, $r(z)=(H-z)/(H+1)$ and consider the cone $-1\le z\le H$, $|x|\le r(z)$, with planar density $f(x,z)=r(z)^{d-2}/2$. For $s\ge0$, let $P(s)$ be the mass of $z+sx\ge0$, and $C(s),M(s)$ the section integrals of $f,zf$, respectively, with respect to $d\mathcal H^1/\sqrt{1+s^2}$. Put $V_0=(H+1)/d$, $C_0=(1-a)^{d-1}$ and $p=(1-a)^d$. For $s>0$ define
$$
e_s=\frac1{2s}\int_{-s}^{\min(s,1)}(1-ab)^{-d}\,db,\qquad
m_s=\partial_a e_s,\qquad k_s=\frac{(s-1)_+}{2s},\qquad v_s=1-k_s.
$$
At $s=0$ set $(e_0,m_0,k_0)=(1,0,0)$. Thus $e_s=C(s)/C_0$ and $M(s)/C_0=-(1-a)m_s/d$. A centered law is a probability law with average section moment zero.

For $s\ge1$ and $q=1+as$ one has
$$
m_s=\frac{(dq-d+1)q^{-d}-(1-da)(1-a)^{-d}}{2a(d-1)(q-1)}.
$$
When $a\ge1/d$, $m_s>0$ for every positive finite $s$. When $0<a<1/d$, there is a unique positive zero $s_*=(R-1)/a>1$ of $m_s$, where $R>1+a$ solves $(dR-d+1)R^{-d}=(1-da)(1-a)^{-d}$. Then $m_s>0$ for $0<s<s_*$ and $m_s<0$ for $s>s_*$.

For a proper frustum cutoff $T\ge1$, set $U=HT/(H+1+T)$ and let $P_f,C_f,M_f,V$ be the corresponding cap, coarea, moment and total mass. If $a\ge1/d$, or if $a<1/d$ and $T<s_*$, every centered frustum slope law on $[0,\infty]$ is supported on $\{0,\infty\}$ and has $P_f-cC_f=V/2$ on average for $c=(P_f(0)-V/2)/C_f(0)$.

## Proof

The cone energy records from 18e7bdf54588779d apply to the Dirac law at each finite slope. Differentiating its integral on the symmetric interval $[-s,s]$ for $0<s\le1$ gives a positive value, since $b[(1-ab)^{-d-1}-(1+ab)^{-d-1}]>0$ for $0<b\le s$. For $s\ge1$, the integral evaluates to $[(1-a)^{1-d}-q^{1-d}]/[2(d-1)(q-1)]$. Differentiate at fixed $s$, using $a\,\partial_a q=q-1$, to obtain the formula.

The function $F(q)=(dq-d+1)q^{-d}$ is positive for $q>1$ and has derivative $-d(d-1)(q-1)q^{-d-1}<0$. It tends to zero at infinity. At $q=1+a$ the numerator of $m_s$ is positive by the small-slope argument. If $a\ge1/d$ the subtracted constant is nonpositive. Otherwise it is positive, and strict decrease gives the unique root and signs.

For the proper frustum, $U/r(U)=T$, so f4be2679c83c2fd1 gives $M_f(s)=M(s)$ up to $T$ and $M_f(s)=(T/s)M_f(T)$ for finite $s>T$. The normalized moment formula makes $M_f(s)<0$ at every finite positive slope in the stated branches. Thus a centered law is supported on $0,\infty$. At $\infty$ the record is $(V/2,0,0)$, and the displayed choice of $c$ gives the same corrected cap at $0$.
