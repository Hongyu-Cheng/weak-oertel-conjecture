## Statement

For every real \(M>3\), there is a one-dimensional system with target bases \(m\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and two edges at each target with the following properties.

At targets \(m=-1,1\), the edge radius intervals and translated intervals are
\[
D_{m,1}=[-M,M],\qquad D_{m,2}=[-1,1],
\]
and
\[
L_{m,1}=[-2M-2,-2],\qquad L_{m,2}=[-1,1].
\]
At target \(m=0\), use the reflected translated intervals and the same, centrally symmetric radius intervals:
\[
D_{0,1}=[-M,M],\qquad D_{0,2}=[-1,1],
\]
and
\[
L_{0,1}=[2,2M+2]=-L_{1,1},\qquad L_{0,2}=[-1,1]=-L_{1,2}.
\]
For every target set
\[
E_m:=\frac12(D_{m,1}+D_{m,2}),\qquad
Z_m:=\frac12(L_{m,1}+L_{m,2}).
\]
Let
\[
y:=\frac{2}{M+1},\qquad Y_{-1}=Y_1=y,\qquad Y_0=-2y.
\]
Writing \(\lambda_m:=h_{E_m}(Y_m)\), give edge \(i\) at target \(m\) the harmonic-polar capacity \(b_{m,i}:=\lambda_m/2\). Then
\[
(b_{-1,1},b_{-1,2})=(b_{1,1},b_{1,2})=(1/2,1/2),
\qquad
(b_{0,1},b_{0,2})=(1,1).
\]

Capacity-feasible exact splits exist at all three targets. The target forces satisfy
\[
\sum_mY_m=0,
\qquad
\sum_m mY_m=0,
\]
and their total translated support is
\[
\sum_m h_{Z_m}(Y_m)=-\frac{4}{M+1}<0.
\]
Nevertheless, for every family \((v_{m,i})\) satisfying, for each \(m\),
\[
v_{m,1}+v_{m,2}=Y_m,
\qquad
h_{D_{m,i}}(v_{m,i})\leq b_{m,i}\quad(i=1,2),
\]
one has the exact global force and affine-moment balances
\[
\sum_{m,i}v_{m,i}=0,
\qquad
\sum_{m,i}m v_{m,i}=0,
\]
but also
\[
\sum_{m,i}h_{L_{m,i}}(v_{m,i})
\geq \frac{2(M-3)}{M(M+1)}>0.
\]
Thus the corresponding abstract implication to edge-level translated-support negativity is false. The system is not asserted to arise from a canonical hard body and makes no counterexample claim about weak Oertel.

## Proof

For an interval \([a,b]\subset\mathbb R\), its support function is
\[
h_{[a,b]}(u)=
\begin{cases}
bu,&u\geq0,\\
au,&u<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
At every target,
\[
E_m=\left[-\frac{M+1}{2},\frac{M+1}{2}\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
\lambda_{-1}=\lambda_1=h_{E_1}(y)
=\frac{M+1}{2}\frac{2}{M+1}=1,
\]
whereas
\[
\lambda_0=h_{E_0}(-2y)
=\frac{M+1}{2}\frac{4}{M+1}=2.
\]
This proves the stated capacity values. At either outer target, \((v_{m,1},v_{m,2})=(0,y)\) is feasible because \(y<1/2\) for \(M>3\). At the center, \((v_{0,1},v_{0,2})=(0,-2y)\) is feasible because \(2y=4/(M+1)<1\). Hence all capacity-constrained force-split sets are nonempty.

For \(m=-1,1\), direct Minkowski addition gives
\[
Z_m=\left[-\frac{2M+3}{2},-\frac12\right].
\]
At \(m=0\), reflection gives
\[
Z_0=-Z_1=\left[\frac12,\frac{2M+3}{2}\right].
\]
Since \(y>0\) and \(-2y<0\), equation (1) yields
\[
h_{Z_{-1}}(Y_{-1})=h_{Z_1}(Y_1)=-\frac y2=-\frac1{M+1}
\]
and
\[
h_{Z_0}(Y_0)=\frac12(-2y)=-y=-\frac2{M+1}.
\]
Their sum is exactly \(-4/(M+1)\).

The target-force balances are immediate:
\[
Y_{-1}+Y_0+Y_1=y-2y+y=0
\]
and
\[
(-1)Y_{-1}+0Y_0+1Y_1=-y+y=0.
\]
For any edge family recovering every \(Y_m\), summing the three recovery identities, and then their products by \(m\), gives
\[
\sum_{m,i}v_{m,i}=\sum_mY_m=0,
\qquad
\sum_{m,i}m v_{m,i}=\sum_m mY_m=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every feasible family, not only the displayed witnesses, has the required exact force and affine-moment balances.

It remains to prove the positive edge-support lower bound. Fix either outer target and abbreviate its split by \(v_1+v_2=y\). Its capacity conditions are exactly
\[
M|v_1|\leq\frac12,
\qquad
|v_2|\leq\frac12.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
v_2=y-v_1\geq y-\frac1{2M}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
Both possible signs of the first edge force must be checked. If \(v_1<0\), then (1) and (4) give
\[
h_{L_{m,1}}(v_1)+h_{L_{m,2}}(v_2)
=(-2M-2)v_1+v_2
=y-(2M+3)v_1
>y.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(v_1\geq0\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{L_{m,1}}(v_1)+h_{L_{m,2}}(v_2)
&=-2v_1+v_2\\
&=y-3v_1\\
&\geq \frac2{M+1}-\frac3{2M}\\
&=\frac{M-3}{2M(M+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The right side of (6) is positive, and it is smaller than \(y\), so (5) and (6) together prove that each outer target contributes at least
\[
\delta:=\frac{M-3}{2M(M+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]

Now abbreviate a feasible center split by \(w_1+w_2=-2y\). Its capacities are
\[
M|w_1|\leq1,
\qquad
|w_2|\leq1.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
w_2=-2y-w_1\leq-2y+\frac1M
=\frac{1-3M}{M(M+1)}<0.
\tag{9}
\]
Again check both signs of the first edge force. If \(w_1>0\), then
\[
h_{L_{0,1}}(w_1)+h_{L_{0,2}}(w_2)
=(2M+2)w_1-w_2
=2y+(2M+3)w_1
>2y.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(w_1\leq0\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{L_{0,1}}(w_1)+h_{L_{0,2}}(w_2)
&=2w_1-w_2\\
&=2y+3w_1\\
&\geq \frac4{M+1}-\frac3M\\
&=\frac{M-3}{M(M+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The right side of (11) is positive and smaller than \(2y\), so (10) and (11) show that the center contributes at least
\[
2\delta=\frac{M-3}{M(M+1)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Adding (7) for the two outer targets and (12) for the center proves, for every feasible exact edge split,
\[
\sum_{m,i}h_{L_{m,i}}(v_{m,i})
\geq 2\delta+2\delta
=\frac{2(M-3)}{M(M+1)}>0.
\]
This contradicts the proposed abstract inference from negative target translated support to the existence of a negative edge translated support, even with exact force recovery, the stated capacities, and affine-moment balance. Nothing here realizes the interval data as a canonical hard-body system. Accordingly, this is an abstract interface obstruction only, not a canonical hard-body or weak-Oertel counterexample.
