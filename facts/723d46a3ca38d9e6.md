## Statement

For every rational $0<\varepsilon\le1/100$, put $D=53/5$,
$$
A=\operatorname{conv}\{(-10,-10),(3/5+\varepsilon,3/5-\varepsilon),(3/5-\varepsilon,3/5+\varepsilon)\},
\quad B=\{(x,y):x\le1,\ y\le1,\ x+y\ge-1\}.
$$
There is a compact full-dimensional convex body with exact integer fibers $A,K,B$ at $z=-1,0,1$ and $|K|>768$. At $(0,0,0)$ the closed caps $H_1=\{x\ge z\}$, $H_2=\{y\ge z\}$, $H_3=\{-x-y\ge z\}$ have equal mixed mass, proper $A$ traces, zero $B$ area, and middle normals forming a positive circuit. Each normal is a strict local minimizer of cap mass modulo positive rescaling and a global minimizer over all real base coefficients with its continuous normal fixed. Each reflected $A$ section fits in its corresponding actual facet of the same $B$ after a shift of magnitude at most $8\varepsilon$, and the middle chord lengths are independent of $\varepsilon$ with centroid offsets at most $\varepsilon^2$. Yet the normalized sum of missed areas, counting multiplicity, is
$$
\frac{\sum_i|A\setminus (H_i)_{-1}|}{|A|}
=\frac{162}{D^2-\varepsilon^2}+1-(105/106)^2
>\frac{16411}{11236}=\frac54+\frac{1183}{5618}.
$$
At $\varepsilon=1/100$ it equals $6146466463/4208252788$. Moreover these endpoints force $|K|>|A|+|B|$ for every compatible middle fiber.

## Proof

The cap normals in the continuous plane are $u_1=(1,0)$, $u_2=(0,1)$, $u_3=(-1,-1)$, summing to zero and spanning. In $B$ the cuts $u_i\cdot(x,y)\ge1$ capture its supporting edges, hence zero area. The triangle $A$ has area $a=2D\varepsilon$, and all three cuts $u_i\cdot(x,y)\ge-1$ are proper. Intersecting the sides of $A$ with $x=-1$ gives the coordinate interval $[l,h]$ on that line, with
$$
l=-1-\frac{18\varepsilon}{D+\varepsilon},\qquad
h=-1+\frac{18\varepsilon}{D-\varepsilon}.
$$
The $y=-1$ data are identical after exchanging coordinates. Write
$$
s=h-l=\frac{36D\varepsilon}{D^2-\varepsilon^2},\quad
a_0=(l+h)/2=-1+\frac{18\varepsilon^2}{D^2-\varepsilon^2},\quad
b_0=1-2s,\quad \delta=a_0+b_0 .
$$
Since $0<\varepsilon\le1/100$ and $D=53/5$, these give $3\varepsilon<s<4\varepsilon$, $0<a_0+1<\varepsilon^2/5$, and $-8\varepsilon<\delta<0$. On the edge $x=1$ of $B$, use density $1/2$ on $y\in[1-3s,1-s]$ and zero elsewhere. This is an actual edge interval with weighted length $s$ and centroid $(1,b_0)$. The reflected interval $-[l,h]+\delta$ is centered at $b_0$ with half-length $s/2$, strictly inside this edge. Use the exchanged construction for the second edge. The $A$ section on $x+y=1$ has centroid $(1/2,1/2)$ and length $(105\varepsilon/53)\sqrt2$, by linear interpolation from the apex to the opposite edge. It reflects into the $B$ edge $x+y=-1$ of length $3\sqrt2$ and centroid $(-1/2,-1/2)$. Constant density $35\varepsilon/53$ on that edge has the same weighted length and reflected centroid.

Take $r=16$, $c=-s\delta/(2r)$, $p=r+c$, $m=-r+c$. Let $K_0$ have consecutive vertices
$$
(p,0),\ (0,p),\ (-r,r),\ (m,0),\ (0,m),\ (r,-r).
$$
Here $0<c<\varepsilon^2$ and its area by the shoelace formula is $3r^2+c^2>768$. Its two axis chords have length $2r$ and centroids $(0,c),(c,0)$, and the diagonal chord on $x+y=0$ has endpoints $(-r,r),(r,-r)$ and centroid zero. The axis tangential moment cancels by $sa_0+2rc+sb_0=0$. The polygon contains the diamond $|x|+|y|\le r-c$ because it contains the four axial vertices of that diamond. Each midpoint of an $A$ vertex and a $B$ vertex lies in $[-6,2]^2$, hence in that diamond, so $(A+B)/2\subset K_0$.

The cap areas in $A$, by similar triangles at the cuts, and the respective areas in $K_0$, by triangulating along the chords, are
$$
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=a(1-81/(D^2-\varepsilon^2)),\quad
\alpha_3=a(105/106)^2,\qquad
q_1=q_2=|K_0|/2+rc,\quad q_3=|K_0|/2-2rc .
$$
Put $e=\alpha_3-\alpha_1-3rc$ and $b=e/p$. The inequalities $81/(D^2-\varepsilon^2)>0.72$, $1-(105/106)^2<0.02$ and $3rc<48\varepsilon^2$ imply $0<e<22\varepsilon$ and $0<b<2\varepsilon$. Replace the edge between $(p,0)$ and $(0,p)$ by the two segments via $(p/2+b,p/2+b)$ to obtain $K$. The added triangle has area $pb=e$ and its interior lies in $x>0,y>0$. The vertex order is simple since the new triangle lies outside the old first-quadrant edge and the other old edges lie outside the open first quadrant. The successive turns, starting at the new vertex, have determinants
$$
2b(c+r),\quad ((c+r)^2+2b(c-r))/2,\quad c^2+r^2,\quad
(r-c)^2,\quad(r-c)^2,\quad c^2+r^2,\quad((c+r)^2+2b(c-r))/2.
$$
They are all positive, since $c<1/10000$, $b<1/50$ and $r=16$. Thus $K$ is convex, still contains $(A+B)/2$, and has the same three chords as $K_0$. The added triangle increases only the first two cap areas, so all mixed cap areas equal $q_1+e+\alpha_1=q_3+\alpha_3$. The convex hull of the three lifted fibers has these exact integer fibers: the endpoint bases are exposed, the projected interval is $[-1,1]$, and equal endpoint weights in any middle combination give points in $K$. It has interior since the middle body has area and the base interval has length.

Fix a cap and perturb its homogeneous ambient margin $u_i\cdot(x,y)-z$ by $t\ell(z,x,y)$ for $t\downarrow0$, with $\ell$ linear. On its fiber cut line write a point as $w+\sigma u_i/\|u_i\|$ in the continuous coordinates. The perturbed cutting coordinate is
$$
\sigma=-t\,\ell(w)/(\|u_i\|+t\,\ell(0,u_i/\|u_i\|)),
$$
where $w$ is the lifted point of the old cut line. On each proper section in $A,K$ the signed gained area to first order is $t\int\ell(w)\,d\mathcal H^1(w)/\|u_i\|$. On the supporting edge in $B$, which lies on the boundary with the body on the outside of the cap, it is $t\int[\ell(w)]_+\,d\mathcal H^1(w)/\|u_i\|$. These expansions are uniform for unit coefficient perturbations for the fixed polygons. In the swept strip of width $O(t)$ the relevant boundary edges intersect the old cut transversely at the ends of its section. After discarding along the cut intervals of length $O(t)$ at those ends, every normal line has the indicated proper-section or supporting-edge status throughout that strip. Those end intervals contribute at most $O(t^2)$, including when the cut runs through a polygon vertex. Away from the ends, replacing the denominator by $\|u_i\|$ introduces a uniform $O(t^2)$ area error.

The first moment of the proper-section measures plus that of the selected $B$ density is zero as a vector in lifted coordinates. For the axis cuts the base and normal moments cancel by matching the $A,B$ lengths and opposite base and normal coordinates, and the tangential moment is $sa_0+2rc+sb_0=0$. For the diagonal cut the corresponding $A,B$ section centroids, including their base coordinates, are opposite with equal weighted lengths, and the middle centroid is zero. Thus for the selected density $\theta_i$ on the supporting edge $F_i$, the directional first variation of mixed mass is
$$
\frac{1}{\|u_i\|}\int_{F_i}\bigl([\ell(w)]_+-\theta_i(w)\ell(w)\bigr)\,d\mathcal H^1(w).
$$
The integrand is nonnegative everywhere and strictly positive where $\ell\ne0$ on the nondegenerate interval with $0<\theta_i<1$. Its integral can vanish only if $\ell$ vanishes on that entire lifted edge line, which spans the two-dimensional ambient cap plane as vectors from the mark because it lies in $z=1$. Such directions are multiples of the original margin. On the unit circle of coefficient variations orthogonal to that margin, the continuous first variation therefore has a positive minimum. The uniform error proves strict local minimality modulo positive scaling, since nearby projective margins have representatives in this orthogonal affine chart.

Now keep $u_i$ fixed and use margin $u_i\cdot(x,y)+\beta z$. The thresholds in $A,B$ are $\beta,-\beta$, respectively, and the middle trace stays fixed. For $\beta\le-1$ the $A$ mass can only increase relative to $\beta=-1$ and the $B$ mass is zero. For $t=\beta+1\in[0,3]$, the $B$ gain is $3t-t^2/2\ge(3/2)t$: the excluded subtriangle has height fraction $(3-t)/3$, and the area of $B$ is $9/2$. The projected area density of a triangle in any direction is at most twice its area divided by its projection range, since the density is piecewise linear from the projection of a vertex to the next one and vanishes outside the range. In the three unscaled $u_i$ coordinates, the projection ranges of $A$ are $D+\varepsilon,D+\varepsilon,2D$, so its density is at most $4\varepsilon$. Its loss is at most $4\varepsilon t$, which is smaller than the gain for $t>0$. For $\beta\ge2$ the whole area $9/2$ of $B$ is captured, exceeding $a$. This covers every base coefficient.

Finally the missed ratio is $[3a-(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3)]/a$. Substitution yields the stated expression, its strict limit gap, and its rational endpoint value. To verify the forced-heaviness scope, the consecutive vertices of $A+B$ are
$$
(-9,-12),\ (8/5+\varepsilon,-7/5-\varepsilon),\
(8/5+\varepsilon,8/5-\varepsilon),\
(8/5-\varepsilon,8/5+\varepsilon),\
(-7/5-\varepsilon,8/5+\varepsilon),\ (-12,-9).
$$
Each listed point is a vertex sum. Of the three other sums, $(-9,-9)$ lies in the convex hull of the two displayed lower vertices and the two vertices with coordinate sum $16/5$. The two remaining sums are $(8/5-\varepsilon,-7/5+\varepsilon)$ and its coordinate swap. The first lies on the segment between $(8/5+\varepsilon,-7/5-\varepsilon)$ and $(-7/5-\varepsilon,8/5+\varepsilon)$, and the other lies on the same segment by reflection. This verifies the vertex-sum hull directly. The shoelace area is $681/10+(136/5)\varepsilon$, so compatibility forces $|K|-a-|B|\ge|A+B|/4-a-9/2=501/40-(72/5)\varepsilon>0$.
