## Statement

Write a point of \(\mathbb R^{14}\) as \((\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\), where \(\mathbf s,\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^7\). Define the following linear functions:

\[
v_i(\mathbf s)=
\begin{cases}
\frac25s_1+s_2+2s_{i+2},&1\le i\le4,\\
\frac25s_1+s_2,&i=5,\\
s_2+2s_7,&i=6,\\
s_2,&i=7,\\
-2s_1-7s_2-2\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^7s_k,&i=8,
\end{cases}
\qquad
h_i(\mathbf t)=
\begin{cases}
t_i,&1\le i\le7,\\
-\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^7t_k,&i=8.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(1\le i\le8\), put \(f_i(\mathbf s,\mathbf t)=1+v_i(\mathbf s)\) and \(f_{8+i}(\mathbf s,\mathbf t)=1-v_i(\mathbf s)+2h_i(\mathbf t)\). Thus all sixteen functions have the required normalized form \(f_i(\mathbf z)=1+\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf z\), where \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\). Define

\[
P=\left\{(\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\in\mathbb R^{14}:
f_i(\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\ge0\quad(1\le i\le16)\right\},
\qquad D=P\cap\mathbb Z^{14}.
\]

Then \(P\) is a full-dimensional lattice polytope, these sixteen inequalities are its distinct irredundant facet inequalities, and \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^{14}=\{\mathbf0\}\). For every choice of fourteen distinct functions among \(f_1,\ldots,f_{16}\), their floor map is noninjective on \(D\).

## Proof

Use \(\mathbf a=(f_1,\ldots,f_8)\) and \(\mathbf b=(f_9,\ldots,f_{16})\) as aliases for the two blocks of normalized facet slacks. The definitions give \(\sum_{i=1}^8v_i=\sum_{i=1}^8h_i=0\). Therefore the affine map from \((\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\) to \((\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\) takes values in the affine space \(\sum_i a_i=\sum_i b_i=8\).

This map is an affine isomorphism. Indeed, given any pair in that affine space, set \(\mathbf v=\mathbf a-\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf h=(\mathbf a+\mathbf b-2\mathbf1)/2\). Both vectors have coordinate sum zero. The complete inverse is

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_1&=\frac52(v_5-v_7),&
s_2&=v_7,&
s_{i+2}&=\frac{v_i-v_5}{2}\quad(1\le i\le4),&
s_7&=\frac{v_6-v_7}{2},\\
t_i&=h_i\quad(1\le i\le7).
\end{aligned}
\]

The formula for \(v_8\) then follows from \(\sum_i v_i=0\). Consequently \(P\) is affinely isomorphic to

\[
\left\{\mathbf a\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^8:\sum_i a_i=8\right\}
\times
\left\{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^8:\sum_i b_i=8\right\}.
\]

It is bounded and has dimension fourteen. Each equation \(a_i=0\) or \(b_i=0\) has a point at which all fifteen other inequalities are strict, and its intersection with the displayed product has dimension thirteen. Hence all sixteen inequalities define distinct facets. Since they already describe \(P\), these are exactly its facets.

All occurrences of \(\mathbf e_i\) below denote the standard basis vectors of \(\mathbb R^8\). The vertices in slack coordinates are exactly \((\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=(8\mathbf e_i,8\mathbf e_j)\), for \(1\le i,j\le8\). At such a vertex, write \(\mathbf q_i=8\mathbf e_i-\mathbf1\). Then \(\mathbf v=\mathbf q_i\) and \(\mathbf h=4(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_j)-\mathbf1\). The vector \(\mathbf h\) is integral. Every coordinate difference of \(\mathbf q_i\) is a multiple of eight, so the inverse formulas give integral \(s_1,\ldots,s_7\) as well. Thus all sixty-four vertices have integral coordinates in the specified \(\mathbb R^{14}\), proving that \(P\) is a lattice polytope for the standard lattice \(\mathbb Z^{14}\).

The origin has all sixteen slacks equal to one, so it is interior. Suppose now that \((\mathbf s,\mathbf t)\) is an interior lattice point. Each \(h_i\) is integral, and \(a_i+b_i=2+2h_i\) is a strictly positive even integer. Hence \(a_i+b_i\ge2\) for every \(i\). These eight sums total sixteen, forcing \(a_i+b_i=2\) for every \(i\). It follows that \(\mathbf h=\mathbf0\), \(\mathbf a=\mathbf1+\mathbf v\), and \(\mathbf b=\mathbf1-\mathbf v\), so \(|v_i|<1\) for all \(i\).

The formulas for \(v_6,v_7,v_8\) show that these three coordinates are integers, and therefore they are zero. The first five coordinates differ pairwise by even integers. Since they all lie in \((-1,1)\), they must be equal. Their sum is zero, so they also vanish. The inverse formulas now give \(\mathbf s=\mathbf t=\mathbf0\). This argument checks every possible interior lattice point directly.

Consider first a choice that omits two slacks from the first block, say \(f_i,f_j\) with \(i\ne j\). The two distinct lattice vertices with slack pairs \((8\mathbf e_i,8\mathbf e_8)\) and \((8\mathbf e_j,8\mathbf e_8)\) agree on every selected slack, hence on every selected floor. If both omitted slacks belong to the second block, the same conclusion follows from the vertices with slack pairs \((8\mathbf e_8,8\mathbf e_i)\) and \((8\mathbf e_8,8\mathbf e_j)\). This includes every choice that omits both facets from one block, without imposing a linear independence condition.

It remains to omit \(f_i\) and \(f_{8+j}\), where \(1\le i,j\le8\) are arbitrary. Put \(\mathbf c=\sum_{k=1}^5\mathbf e_k\). Define \(\mathbf I_i,\mathbf J_j\in\mathbb R^{14}\) by the following slack pairs, using the explicit affine inverse above:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(\mathbf a(\mathbf I_i),\mathbf b(\mathbf I_i)\bigr)
&=\left(\frac25\mathbf c+4\mathbf e_8+2\mathbf e_i,\,
        \frac85\mathbf c\right),\\
\bigl(\mathbf a(\mathbf J_j),\mathbf b(\mathbf J_j)\bigr)
&=\left(\frac45\mathbf c+4\mathbf e_8,\,
        \frac65\mathbf c+2\mathbf e_j\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Every coordinate is nonnegative, and each of the four displayed vectors has coordinate sum eight. Thus both points lie in \(P\).

They are standard lattice points, as can be checked without a lattice census. Define \(\mathbf g=\frac25\mathbf c-2\mathbf e_8\). Their corresponding \(\mathbf v\) vectors are

\[
\mathbf v(\mathbf I_i)=\mathbf q_8+\mathbf g+2(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_8),
\qquad
\mathbf v(\mathbf J_j)=\mathbf q_8+2\mathbf g.
\]

The inverse sends \(\mathbf q_8\) to \((0,-1,0,0,0,0,0)\) in the \(\mathbf s\) coordinates and sends \(\mathbf g\) to \((1,0,0,0,0,0,0)\). It also sends each \(2(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_8)\) to an integral vector: its seventh coordinate is an integer and all coordinate differences appearing in the inverse are even integers, including the difference multiplied by \(5/2\). Linearity of this inverse therefore proves that both \(\mathbf s\) vectors are integral. Their \(\mathbf h\) vectors are, respectively,

\[
\mathbf h(\mathbf I_i)=\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_8-\mathbf e_6-\mathbf e_7,
\qquad
\mathbf h(\mathbf J_j)=\mathbf e_j+\mathbf e_8-\mathbf e_6-\mathbf e_7.
\]

These are integral and have coordinate sum zero, so both \(\mathbf t\) vectors are integral. Consequently \(\mathbf I_i,\mathbf J_j\in D\). They are distinct because \(\mathbf a(\mathbf I_i)-\mathbf a(\mathbf J_j)=2\mathbf e_i-\frac25\mathbf c\ne\mathbf0\).

For a selected coordinate in the first block, that is, \(1\le k\le8\) with \(k\ne i\), the two entries are \(2/5\) and \(4/5\) when \(k\le5\), are both four when \(k=8\), and are both zero for the other possible values of \(k\). Their floors therefore agree. For a selected coordinate in the second block, that is, \(1\le\ell\le8\) with \(\ell\ne j\), the entries are \(8/5\) and \(6/5\) when \(\ell\le5\), and are both zero otherwise. Their floors again agree. Hence \(\mathbf I_i\) and \(\mathbf J_j\) collide for the choice omitting \(f_i,f_{8+j}\).

Every fourteen-facet choice omits either two facets from one block or one facet from each block. The exhibited lattice points prove noninjectivity in every case, as claimed.
