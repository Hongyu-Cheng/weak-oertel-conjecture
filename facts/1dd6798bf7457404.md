## Statement

For any integers $n,d\ge1$, put $\varepsilon=1/100$ and take the body
$$
C=\{(t,z,x): z\in[0,1]^{n-1},\ x\in[0,1]^d,\ x_1/2-\varepsilon\le t\le1+\varepsilon-x_1/2\}
$$
with $(t,z)$ the integer coordinates. In $C^+=C+[-1,1]e_t$, every original mixed point has normalized closed-halfspace depth at most $2/(51\cdot2^{n-1})<\Gamma_d/2^n$. For any $b\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}$, the new point $(0,b,(1/2,\ldots,1/2))$ has normalized depth at least $13/(51\cdot2^{n-1})>\Gamma_d/2^n$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$.

## Proof

The two $t$ bounds are affine, so the body is convex and full-dimensional. At each binary integer base $z=b$, the original positive fibers are both $S=[0,2\varepsilon]\times[0,1]^{d-1}$ at $t=0,1$. Symmetric extrusion shifts the lower and upper endpoints by $-1$ and $1$ on each actual $t$ line. Substitution of integer $t$ shows that its fibers at $-1,0,1,2$ are $S,U,U,S$, with $U=[0,1]^d$, and no other integer $t$ occurs. The total mass per binary base is $2+4\varepsilon=51/25$.

For a fixed binary base $b$, let $h_b(z)=\sum_{i:b_i=0}z_i+\sum_{i:b_i=1}(1-z_i)$. The affine margin $2\varepsilon-x_1-2h_b(z)$ gives a closed cap containing every old point at $z=b$ and excluding all other binary bases. At $b$ it captures $2\varepsilon$ from each of the four extruded fibers, so its mass is $8\varepsilon=2/25$. Dividing by the full mass proves the old-point bound.

Let $g=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$ and reduce a containing halfspace at $(0,b,g)$ to one whose boundary passes through this point. On the $z=b$ slice its margin is $\alpha t+u\cdot(x-g)$. When $u\ne0$, the own full cube at $t=0$ contributes exactly $1/2$ by reflection. Reflection $x\mapsto2g-x$ takes the small fiber at $t=-1$ into the full cube at $t=1$ and reverses the sign of the margin on paired points. Every point of the small fiber either contributes itself or has its reflection in the captured part of the full fiber. The two contributions therefore total at least $2\varepsilon$. When $u=0$, the own full cube lies in the closed cap, including when its restricted margin vanishes identically, which already gives the stated lower bound. The raw depth is at least $1/2+2\varepsilon=13/25$ in the full product. Since $2\le(1+1/d)^d<e<3$, we have $1/3<\Gamma_d\le1/2$, and the strict target comparisons follow from $2/51<1/6$ and $13/51>1/4$.
