## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), and let \([0,j]=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:0\le x_i\le j_i\text{ for every }i\}\). Let \(\psi:[0,j]\to[0,\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous, convex, and coordinatewise nondecreasing, with \(\psi(0)=0\). Zero coordinates of \(j\) are allowed. Adopt \(e^{-\infty}=0\), and for every finite \(t\ge0\) define
\[
F_t=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ \psi(u)\le t\},
\qquad
\mathcal R(\psi)=\sup_{t\ge0}\frac{e^{-t}|F_t-F_t|}{1+2t}.
\]
The difference set contains each integer difference once, including zero. Then
\[
\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z^n\\0\le u\le j}}e^{-\psi(u)}
\le120\,\mathcal R(\psi).
\tag{1}
\]
The supremum is attained at a member of the finite set \(\{\psi(u):0\le u\le j,\ u\in\mathbb Z^n,\ \psi(u)<\infty\}\), which contains zero.

The finite-set ingredient has independent scope: for every nonempty finite \(E\subset\mathbb Z^n\), the number of coordinatewise parity classes met by \(E\) is at most \((|E-E|/|E|)^3\). Consequently the preceding threshold families satisfy \(|F_t|^4\le |F_{t/2}|\,|F_t-F_t|^3\) for every \(t\ge0\).

For the actual-body consequence, use exactly the normalization of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f. Let \(n,d\ge1\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and set \(C_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}\), \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\), \(W=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0\), and \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero. Assume \(w_z=0\) at every integer \(z\notin\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), and assume \(w_u\ge w_v\) whenever \(0\le u\le v\) are integer vectors, including zero values. Put \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\).

At every fixed \(j\in D\), retain \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j=\{v:\alpha(v)>0\}\), \(\Delta_j(v)=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) on \(\mathcal U_j\), \(B_j^\Delta(L)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j,\,\Delta_j(v)\le L}\alpha(v)\), and \(K_j(w)=\max_{L\ge0}B_j^\Delta(L)/(1+L)\). These symbols, as well as \(h_S(p)=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), have exactly their meanings in 03f071403cdfc69f; the infimum is over closed ambient halfspaces. Displacement sums are oriented and include zero. All logarithms are natural. In this application the local box vector \(j\) is the same prescribed integer center \(j\), and the local variable \(u\) is a forward displacement, so its weight is \(w_{j+u}\).

For every such prescribed \(j\), there is a finite cutoff \(L_j\ge0\) and one \(x_j^\star\in C_j\) such that
\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L_j)}{1+L_j}
\ge\frac1{120}\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z^n\\0\le u\le j}}w_{j+u},
\qquad
h_S((j,x_j^\star))
\ge\frac1{1440}\sum_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z^n\\0\le u\le j}}w_{j+u}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular the first lower bound applies to \(K_j(w)\). The center, cutoff, and continuous point are selected before the ambient halfspace. No assertion is made that one of these forward blocks contains a fixed multiple of \(2^{-n}W\).

## Proof

First establish the finite-set parity bound from elementary cardinality arguments. All sets in this paragraph are auxiliary finite subsets of the additive group \(\mathbb Z^n\); in particular they are distinct from the actual-body objects introduced later. Fix a nonempty \(E\), put \(\kappa=|E-E|/|E|\), and choose a nonempty \(X\subseteq E\) minimizing \(\rho=|X-E|/|X|\) among nonempty subsets of \(E\). Then \(\rho\le\kappa\), and every \(Y\subseteq X\), including the empty set, satisfies \(|Y-E|\ge\rho|Y|\).

For every nonempty finite set \(V\), we claim that \(|X-E+V|\le\rho|X+V|\). Enumerate \(V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_q\}\) and put
\[
X_i=\{x\in X:x+v_i\notin\bigcup_{k<i}(X+v_k)\}.
\]
The newly added points of \(X+v_i\) are precisely \(X_i+v_i\), so \(|X+V|=\sum_i|X_i|\). If \(x\in X\setminus X_i\), then \(x+v_i=x'+v_k\) for some \(x'\in X\) and \(k<i\). Thus every point of \((X\setminus X_i)-E+v_i\) has already appeared in \(\bigcup_{k<i}(X-E+v_k)\). The number of newly added points of \(X-E+v_i\) is consequently at most
\[
|X-E|-|(X\setminus X_i)-E|
\le\rho|X|-\rho|X\setminus X_i|
=\rho|X_i|.
\]
Summing proves the claim. Taking \(V=-E\) gives \(|X-E-E|\le\rho^2|X|\).

For any nonempty finite sets \(U,V,X\), one also has \(|U-V|\,|X|\le|U+X|\,|V+X|\). To see this, choose one representation \(a=u_a-v_a\) for each \(a\in U-V\). The map \((a,x)\mapsto(u_a+x,v_a+x)\) is injective: its output difference recovers \(a\), after which its first coordinate recovers \(x\). Apply this inequality with \(U=-E-E\) and \(V=-E\). The preceding estimates yield
\[
|E-E-E|
\le\frac{|X-E-E|\,|X-E|}{|X|}
\le\rho^3|X|
\le\kappa^3|E|.
\tag{3}
\]
These arguments require no convexity, ordering, or collision assumptions on \(E\).

Write \(2\cdot E=\{2x:x\in E\}\) for the pointwise dilate, to distinguish it from a Minkowski sum. For each parity class \(b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\) met by \(E\), choose \(a_b\in E\) in that class. The set \(a_b-2\cdot E\) has cardinality \(|E|\), lies entirely in parity class \(b\), and is contained in \(E-E-E\). These sets are disjoint for distinct \(b\). If \(m(E)\) is the number of represented classes, (3) gives \(m(E)|E|\le\kappa^3|E|\), proving
\[
m(E)\le\left(\frac{|E-E|}{|E|}\right)^3.
\tag{4}
\]
This bounds distinct parity classes, and does not count multiple representations of a difference as different outputs.

Return to the potential in the statement and put \(N(t)=|F_t|\). Each \(F_t\) is finite, nonempty, and coordinatewise downward closed; it contains zero. For \(u\in F_t\), let \(\lfloor u/2\rfloor\) denote coordinatewise rounding down. Both this point and \(u/2\) belong to \([0,j]\). Monotonicity and convexity at zero imply
\[
\psi(\lfloor u/2\rfloor)
\le\psi(u/2)
\le\tfrac12\psi(u)+\tfrac12\psi(0)
\le t/2.
\tag{5}
\]
In a fixed parity class, the map \(u\mapsto\lfloor u/2\rfloor\) is injective, because its inverse there is \(q\mapsto2q+b\), using the representative \(b\in\{0,1\}^n\) of that class. Equation (5) shows that its image lies in \(F_{t/2}\). Every parity class of \(F_t\) therefore has at most \(N(t/2)\) members. Applying (4) with \(E=F_t\) proves
\[
N(t)
\le N(t/2)\left(\frac{|F_t-F_t|}{N(t)}\right)^3,
\qquad
N(t)^4\le N(t/2)|F_t-F_t|^3.
\tag{6}
\]
Only finite-potential sites are used in (5); infinite values introduce no exceptional arithmetic. A zero coordinate of \(j\) remains zero under every map above.

There are finitely many finite lattice values of \(\psi\), and zero is one of them. The set \(F_t\), and hence its difference set, is constant between consecutive such values. The factor \(e^{-t}/(1+2t)\) strictly decreases on \(t\ge0\). It follows that \(\mathcal R(\psi)\) is finite, is at least one, and is attained at one of the stated thresholds, including the possibility \(t=0\).

Since \(0\in F_{t/2}\), the injection \(u\mapsto u-0\) gives \(N(t/2)\le|F_{t/2}-F_{t/2}|\). Use this in (6), and then use the definition of \(\mathcal R(\psi)\) at both thresholds. Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean gives the last step in
\[
\begin{aligned}
N(t)
&\le |F_{t/2}-F_{t/2}|^{1/4}|F_t-F_t|^{3/4}\\
&\le\mathcal R(\psi)e^{7t/8}(1+t)^{1/4}(1+2t)^{3/4}\\
&\le\mathcal R(\psi)e^{7t/8}\left(1+\tfrac74t\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For each lattice site, including one with infinite potential, \(e^{-\psi(u)}=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\mathbf1_{\{\psi(u)\le t\}}\,dt\). Summing this finite collection of identities and using (7) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{0\le u\le j}e^{-\psi(u)}
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}N(t)\,dt\\
&\le\mathcal R(\psi)\int_0^\infty e^{-t/8}\left(1+\tfrac74t\right)\,dt\\
&=\mathcal R(\psi)\left(8+\tfrac74\cdot64\right)
=120\,\mathcal R(\psi).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The sum in (8), like all sums over \(u\) below, is over integer sites. This proves (1) with no restriction on the number of nonzero coordinates or the finite lengths of their chains.

We next construct the interpolation needed for the actual-body consequence. The positive integer support \(D\) is finite because \(C\) is compact, and is nonempty because \(W>0\). Its assumed monotonicity makes it a downset in the nonnegative integer orthant. Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) and \(r_z=w_z^{1/d}>0\) for \(z\in D\). For \(x\in P\), define
\[
\widehat r(x)=
\max\left\{\sum_{z\in D}\lambda_zr_z:
\lambda_z\ge0,\ \sum_{z\in D}\lambda_z=1,
\ \sum_{z\in D}\lambda_z z=x\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The feasible coefficient set is nonempty and compact, so this maximum exists. Mixing maximizing representations proves concavity of \(\widehat r\). Also \(\min_{z\in D}r_z\le\widehat r(x)\le\max_{z\in D}r_z\), so it is finite and positive on \(P\).

The Brunn-Minkowski fiber argument recorded in accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f applies to arbitrary convex combinations of positive fibers, by iteration of the two-set form stated there. Convexity of the original body gives \(\sum_z\lambda_z C_z\subseteq C_x\), and Brunn-Minkowski gives \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)^{1/d}\ge\sum_z\lambda_zr_z\). Thus \(\widehat r(x)\le\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)^{1/d}\) for every \(x\in P\). In particular an integer point of \(P\) has a positive actual fiber, so \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). At \(z\in D\), the single-site representation and the preceding upper bound give \(\widehat r(z)=r_z\). This establishes exact interpolation of the positive integer data and identifies every integer zero outside \(P\).

To check real monotonicity of this envelope, fix \(y\in P\) and \(0\le x\le y\), without initially assuming \(x\in P\). Realize a maximizing representation in (9) as a random vector \(Z\in D\) with \(\mathbb EZ=y\) and \(\mathbb E r_Z=\widehat r(y)\). Conditional on \(Z\), replace each coordinate \(Z_i\) by a binomial variable with \(Z_i\) trials and success probability \(x_i/y_i\) when \(y_i>0\). If \(y_i=0\), then \(Z_i=0\) almost surely and set the replacement coordinate equal to zero. The conditional draws can be made independently across coordinates. The resulting finite-valued integer vector \(U\) satisfies \(0\le U\le Z\), \(U\in D\), and \(\mathbb EU=x\). Hence \(x\in P\), and monotonicity of the original integer table yields
\[
\widehat r(x)\ge\mathbb E r_U\ge\mathbb E r_Z=\widehat r(y).
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore \(P\) is downward closed in the real nonnegative orthant and \(\widehat r\) is coordinatewise nonincreasing there.

The envelope \(\widehat r\) is upper semicontinuous on \(P\). Indeed, for \(x_k\to x\in P\), choose maximizing coefficient vectors in the compact simplex indexed by \(D\). A subsequence attaining the upper limit of the objective has a further convergent subsequence; its limiting vector represents \(x\). Its objective is at most \(\widehat r(x)\). Define \(\widehat w(x)=\widehat r(x)^d\) on \(P\), and \(\widehat w(x)=0\) outside \(P\). Since \(P\) is closed and the envelope is positive on \(P\), this extension is upper semicontinuous. It is coordinatewise nonincreasing on the nonnegative orthant: (10) covers \(y\in P\), and the comparison is automatic when \(y\notin P\). Concavity and positivity of \(\widehat r\), followed by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, show that \(\widehat w\) is log-concave, with its zero extension on the convex set \(P\). It agrees with the entire actual integer table, including zeros. No assertion about monotonicity of the original body's fiber volumes at noninteger bases has been used.

Fix the prescribed \(j\in D\), and on its forward box define
\[
\psi(u)=-\log\frac{\widehat w(j+u)}{w_j},
\qquad 0\le u\le j,
\tag{11}
\]
where a zero numerator gives \(+\infty\). The exact identity \(\widehat w(j)=w_j\), monotonicity, and log-concavity show that this is a nonnegative, convex, coordinatewise nondecreasing potential with \(\psi(0)=0\). Upper semicontinuity of \(\widehat w\) gives lower semicontinuity of \(\psi\), including at infinite values. Thus all hypotheses of (1) hold, also when \(P\) or the forward box has zero length in some coordinates. Agreement on integer sites gives
\[
\sum_{0\le u\le j}w_{j+u}
=w_j\sum_{0\le u\le j}e^{-\psi(u)}
\le120w_j\mathcal R(\psi).
\tag{12}
\]

For each threshold \(t\ge0\), the resulting \(F_t\) is an integer downset. If \(v=a-b\in F_t-F_t\), then its coordinatewise positive and negative parts satisfy \(v^+\le a\) and \(v^-\le b\). Thus \(v^+,v^-\in F_t\). They have disjoint supports and recover \(v\) uniquely as \(v=v^+-v^-\). In particular \(-j\le v\le j\), and both actual endpoint bases \(j\pm v\) lie in the nonnegative orthant. Their weights satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{j+v}&\ge w_{j+v^+}\ge w_je^{-t},\\
w_{j-v}&\ge w_{j+v^-}\ge w_je^{-t}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Here the first inequalities use monotonicity of the actual integer table; the second use (11) and exact agreement of the envelope at integers. Hence \(v\in\mathcal U_j\), \(\alpha(v)\ge w_je^{-t}\), and the full actual midpoint curvature satisfies \(\Delta_j(v)\le2t\). Its nonnegativity follows from the actual-fiber conclusion of 03f071403cdfc69f. Summing once over each distinct \(v\in F_t-F_t\), with both orientations and zero, gives
\[
B_j^\Delta(2t)\ge w_je^{-t}|F_t-F_t|.
\tag{14}
\]
There is no count of all representing pairs in (14); the uniquely determined disjoint positive and negative parts certify each difference only once.

Choose a maximizing threshold \(t_j\) for \(\mathcal R(\psi)\), whose finite attainment was proved above, and put \(L_j=2t_j\). Equations (12) and (14) give the first assertion of (2). Now apply the fixed-center, fixed-cutoff actual-body statement (3) of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f, with precisely this original \(C\), original table \(w\), prescribed center \(j\), and cutoff \(L_j\). Its hypotheses hold because \(n,d\ge1\), \(C\) is a convex body, \(W>0\), \(w_j>0\), and \(L_j\ge0\). It supplies one \(x_j^\star\in C_j\) such that
\[
h_S((j,x_j^\star))
\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(L_j)}{12(1+L_j)}
\ge\frac1{1440}\sum_{0\le u\le j}w_{j+u}.
\tag{15}
\]
The accepted statement includes every closed ambient halfspace containing this one point, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing full fibers. The envelope was used only to build the potential in (11); no actual fiber shape, weight, curvature, or halfspace mass was replaced. Equation (15) finishes the claimed fixed-center consequence. A global choice of a center with a sufficiently large forward-block mass remains a separate problem.
