## Statement

For every integer \(L\ge1\), define
\(k_s=\lfloor s^2/\log(3/2)\rfloor\) for \(s=0,\ldots,L\), put \(n=k_L\), and set \(N=n+1\). The symbol \(n=N-1\) denotes the number of horizontal coordinates; the registered height remains the last coordinate \(I_N\). Define the polytope

\[
P=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
\begin{array}{l}
0\le y\le L,\\
0\le x_i\le 2+\dfrac{y-t}{L+1}
\quad\text{for }1\le t\le L,\ k_{t-1}<i\le k_t
\end{array}\right\}.
\]

This is a compact full-dimensional rational convex polytope. Let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\), take the registered parameters \(h=0\) and \(\ell=0\), and retain the registered quantities
\(w_I=e^{-I_N^2}\), \(W=\sum_{I\in D}w_I\),
\(w_{\max}=\max_{I\in D}w_I\), and
\(\phi_I=\log(w_{\max}/w_I)\).
For integer \(s\) and \(v\ge0\), let \(W_s(v)\) be the original weight at height \(s\) with \(\phi_I\le v\), and let \(p_s(v)\) count the occupied horizontal residue classes modulo two of that same truncated row. Thus
\(A(w)=\max_{s,v:p_s(v)>0}W_s(v)/p_s(v)\), exactly as in the registered subgoal.

For this family,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A(w)&=1,\\
W&=2^{N-1}\sum_{s=0}^{L}
 e^{-s^2}\left(\frac32\right)^{k_s},\\
\frac{L+1}{3}
&<\frac{W}{2^N A(w)}
\le\frac{L+1}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently no absolute finite constant \(C\) satisfies
\(W\le C2^N A(w)\) over the registered class.
Nevertheless, the original point \(J=(1,\ldots,1,L)\in D\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\ I_N=L}}w_I
\ge\frac{W}{3\cdot2^N}
\]

for every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\). Thus the negative conclusion is restricted to the scalar sufficient assertion.

## Proof

The sequence \(k_s\) is nondecreasing, \(k_0=0\), and \(n=k_L\ge1\). Its blocks \(k_{t-1}<i\le k_t\), for \(1\le t\le L\), partition the horizontal coordinates. All defining inequalities of \(P\) are affine with rational coefficients. For \(0\le y\le L\) and \(1\le t\le L\), their upper bounds satisfy

\[
1<2-\frac{L}{L+1}
\le 2+\frac{y-t}{L+1}
\le 2+\frac{L-1}{L+1}<3.
\]

The polytope is closed and bounded. The point
\((1/2,\ldots,1/2,L/2)\) satisfies every defining inequality strictly, so \(P\) is full-dimensional.

There are no lattice points at heights outside \(\{0,\ldots,L\}\). Fix one of these integer heights \(s\). For a coordinate in block \(t\), the upper bound \(2+(s-t)/(L+1)\) is strictly between one and two if \(s<t\), and belongs to \([2,3)\) if \(s\ge t\). Its allowed integer coordinates are therefore exactly \(\{0,1\}\) in the first case and \(\{0,1,2\}\) in the second case. Since the inequalities impose no other restriction within that row, the complete support is exactly

\[
D=\bigcup_{s=0}^{L}
\left(\{0,1,2\}^{k_s}\times
\{0,1\}^{\,n-k_s}\times\{s\}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, equality at an upper bound retains the new coordinate value two. Formula (1) includes all boundary sites and excludes no convexity-forced lattice point.

All weights in row \(s\) equal \(e^{-s^2}\). Row zero is nonempty and has weight one at every point, while every other occupied height is positive. Hence \(w_{\max}=1\) and \(\phi_I=s^2\) throughout row \(s\). Its full original mass, denoted \(W_s=\sum_{I\in D:I_N=s}w_I\), is

\[
W_s=e^{-s^2}3^{k_s}2^{n-k_s}
=2^n e^{-s^2}\left(\frac32\right)^{k_s}.
\tag{2}
\]

For every occupied height, (1) contains the whole binary cube \(\{0,1\}^n\times\{s\}\). Thus every one of the \(2^n\) horizontal parity classes is occupied. Moreover, a potential cutoff either retains the whole row or retains none of it: for \(0\le s\le L\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
v<s^2&\ \Longrightarrow\ W_s(v)=p_s(v)=0,\\
v\ge s^2&\ \Longrightarrow\
W_s(v)=W_s,\qquad p_s(v)=2^n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Empty heights do not enter the maximum defining \(A(w)\).

The defining floor inequality gives
\(k_s\log(3/2)\le s^2<(k_s+1)\log(3/2)\). Exponentiating its difference with \(s^2\) yields

\[
\frac23<
e^{-s^2}\left(\frac32\right)^{k_s}
\le1
\qquad(0\le s\le L).
\tag{4}
\]

By (2) and (3), the expression in the middle of (4) is exactly every nonempty value \(W_s(v)/p_s(v)\) at height \(s\). At \(s=0\) it equals one, since \(k_0=0\). Consequently \(A(w)=1\). Summing (2), applying (4), and using \(N=n+1\) proves

\[
\frac{2^{N}(L+1)}3
<W
\le\frac{2^{N}(L+1)}2.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the asserted ratio. Given any proposed finite constant \(C\), choose an integer \(L\ge1\) with \(L+1>3C\). Then (5) contradicts \(W\le C2^N A(w)\). This conclusion uses an analytic family, not a finite computational certificate.

It remains to verify the stated distinction from true singleton depth. At height \(L\), one has \(k_L=n\), so the row is exactly \(\{0,1,2\}^n\times\{L\}\), with constant weight \(e^{-L^2}\). It is centrally symmetric about the original point \(J=(1,\ldots,1,L)\). Every closed halfspace containing \(J\) contains at least one point of each pair \(I,2J-I\): if both were in its open complement, convexity of that complement would put their midpoint \(J\) there as well. The center itself is also retained. Therefore every such halfspace contains row mass at least \(W_L/2\).

By (2) and (4), \(W_L>(2/3)2^n\), whereas (5) gives \(W\le(L+1)2^n\). It follows that
\(W_L/2>W/[3(L+1)]\).
Finally \(0<\log(3/2)<1\) implies
\(n=\lfloor L^2/\log(3/2)\rfloor\ge L^2\ge L\), and hence
\(2^N=2^{n+1}\ge L+1\).
Combining these inequalities proves the stated lower bound \(W/(3\cdot2^N)\) for every containing closed halfspace at \(J\).
