## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, $B=-B$ be a full-dimensional convex body and $A\subseteq B$ be a full-dimensional convex body. Fix $c\in A$, and for $0\le t\le1$ take the layered convex hull of $A_t,B,B,-A_t$ at bases $0,1,2,3$, where $A_t=c+(1-t)(A-c)$. These are the exact integer fibers at every stage. With $b=|B|_d$ and $a_t=(1-t)^d|A|_d$, the maximum normalized mixed depth equals $(b/2+a_t)/[2(b+a_t)]$, strictly decreases to $1/4$, and the two endpoint points at $t=1$ have raw depth zero and can be removed without changing the positive mixed measure.

In $d=1$, an actual centrally symmetric array of four consecutive positive interval fibers, with symmetry $(z,x)\mapsto(3-z,-x)$ after centering and relabeling, is carried to this model by a mixed-length-preserving affine shear. Thus with endpoint length $a$ and middle length $b$ it has the same depth formula and a compatible decreasing contraction, obtained by pulling the path back through that shear.

## Proof

Convexity gives $A_t\subseteq A\subseteq B$ and $-A_t\subseteq B$. The layered hull is in $\mathbb R\times B$, so it cannot enlarge either prescribed middle fiber, and the two extreme bases expose exactly $A_t,-A_t$. For $t<1$ they have equal positive volume $a_t$, and the accepted exact four-fiber theorem 265eb94234158b2c applies with $K=B$, $g=0$, and $q=b/2$. The latter follows since symmetry gives $b/2$ at zero and two opposite through-point caps have summed volume $b$ at every point. Hence the raw maximum is $b/2+a_t$ and the total mass is $2(b+a_t)$.

At $t=1$ the endpoints are null. Each middle center has depth at least $b/2$ from its own fiber. At any mark in either middle fiber choose a through-mark ordinary halfspace of $B$ of mass at most $b/2$, which exists by opposite cuts. Tilting it finitely in the base coordinate about the mark's layer excludes the other positive fiber while preserving its own trace, because both bodies are compact and the two bases are distinct. The null endpoint marks have depth zero by their exposing pure-base caps. This proves the same formula at $t=1$, and deleting both null fibers leaves the measure and the raw maximum unchanged. As a function of $a_t$ the normalized maximum has derivative $b/[4(b+a_t)^2]>0$. Since $a_t$ strictly decreases as $t$ increases, the normalized maximum strictly decreases to $1/4$.

For the interval classification let $f$ be the affine function interpolating the centers at bases $1,2$. Central symmetry makes them opposite and gives equal lengths, so $f(3-z)=-f(z)$. Under $(z,x)\mapsto(z,x-f(z))$, the middle fibers become the same centered interval $B$ and the endpoints become a reflected pair $A,-A$. The map is invertible, preserves fiber lengths, and maps closed halfspaces to closed halfspaces. Compatibility of bases $0$ and $2$ at their midpoint gives $(A+B)/2\subseteq B$, which implies $A\subseteq B$ by subtracting support values. Thus the contraction and exact depth formula apply, and the inverse shear gives the stated path for the original array.
