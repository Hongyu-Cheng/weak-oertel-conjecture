## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and $1\le H\le d$ be integers. Put $t=1/(d+1)$ and $\Gamma=(dt)^d$. At the zero-right-index double knot let
$$
a_H=\frac{H+1}{H(d+1)},\qquad \beta_H=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}.
$$
Write $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For each integer $h\ge0$ put $r_h=(1-a_H h)_+$, $q_h=t-\beta_H h$, and $p_h=t+\beta_H(h+1)$. For $r,s>0$ let $E_d(s,r)=d^d s^{d-1}(r-ds)_+$, and let $E_d(s,0)=0$ for every real $s$. The thresholds are positive on positive radii. Define the finite diagonal-envelope deficit and the two normalized sums by
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_d(a_H,\beta_H)&=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0}r_h^d-\sum_{h\ge0}\bigl[E_d(q_h,r_h)+E_d(p_h,r_h)\bigr],\\
W_{d,H}&=\sum_{j\ge0}(1-a_H j)_+^d,\\
K_{d,H}&=\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}(1-j/d)^{d-1}(1-j/H).
\end{aligned}
$$
The normalized left envelope sum is $K_{d,H}$, the right envelope sum vanishes, and $D_d(a_H,\beta_H)/\Gamma=W_{d,H}-K_{d,H}<1/6$.

## Proof

**Normalization.** For the $J=0$ coordinates, $\beta_H\le a_H t$ is equivalent to $H\le d$, and $0<a_H<1$ holds for $H\ge1$ and $d\ge2$. Since $a_H H=(H+1)/(d+1)\le1$ and $1/a_H\le d$, all indices $0\le h<H$ have positive radii, and any positive radius has $h<d$ and hence $q_h=t(1-h/d)>0$. Also $p_h>0$. On positive radii, direct substitution gives
$$
r_h-dq_h=t(1-h/H),\qquad r_h-dp_h=-h(a_H+t).
$$
The right envelope vanishes even at $h=0$, where its bracket is zero. The left envelope vanishes for $h\ge H$ and gives $(1-h/d)^{d-1}(1-h/H)$ after division by $\Gamma=d^d t^d$ when $h<H$. At $H=d$ the radius at $h=H$ is zero, so the zero-radius convention gives the same endpoint value. Thus $D_d(a_H,\beta_H)/\Gamma=W_{d,H}-K_{d,H}$.

**Geometric majorant and $H=1$.** For $0<a\le1$ and any integer $j\ge0$, Bernoulli's inequality gives $(1-aj)_+\le(1-a)^j$, with the right side at $j=0$ understood to be $1$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}(1-aj)_+^d\le \frac1{1-(1-a)^d}.
$$
We use $8/3<e<3$ and $e^2>7$. The exponential series for $e$ through degree three sums to $8/3$, and $m!\ge2^{m-1}$ for $m\ge1$, strictly for $m\ge3$, gives $e<3$. The series for $e^2$ through degree four sums to $7$. We also use
$$
\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}=2\int_0^x\frac{ds}{1-s^2}>2x\qquad(0<x<1).
$$
For $H=1$, $K_{d,1}=1$ and $a_1=2/(d+1)$. The logarithmic inequality at $x=1/d$ gives $q=((d-1)/(d+1))^d<e^{-2}<1/7$. The geometric majorant now gives $W_{d,1}-K_{d,1}\le q/(1-q)<1/6$.

**Bounds for $H\ge2$.** Fix $H\ge2$ and put $c=1+1/H$. For real $z\ge H$ define $Q_H(z)=(1-c/(z+1))^z$. With $x=c/(z+1)\in(0,1/H]$, its logarithmic derivative is
$$
\frac{d}{dz}\log Q_H(z)=\log(1-x)+\frac{x}{1-x}-\frac{x^2}{c(1-x)}
=\frac{x^2}{1-x}\left(A(x)-\frac1c\right),\qquad
A(x)=\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{x^{m-2}}{m(m-1)} .
$$
Here the power series for $\log(1-x)$ gives $A(x)=[x+(1-x)\log(1-x)]/x^2$. When $H\ge3$, bounding its coefficients by $1/2$, strictly for $m\ge3$, gives $A(x)<1/[2(1-x)]\le H/[2(H-1)]\le H/(H+1)=1/c$. When $H=2$, the positive series gives $A(x)\le A(1/2)=2-2\log2<2/3=1/c$, using the logarithmic inequality at $x=1/3$. Thus $Q_H$ decreases and $(1-a_H)^d=Q_H(d)\le Q_H(H)=(1-1/H)^H$. In particular,
$$
W_{d,H}\le\frac1{1-(1-1/H)^H}.
$$

For a fixed integer $j\ge2$ and real $z>j$, set $x=j/z$. The logarithmic derivative of $(1-j/z)^{z-1}$ is
$$
\log(1-x)+\frac{x}{1-x}-\frac{x^2}{j(1-x)}
=\sum_{m\ge2}\left(\frac{m-1}{m}-\frac1j\right)x^m>0.
$$
Every coefficient is nonnegative and those with $m\ge3$ are positive. Hence for $2\le j<H\le d$ the $j$th term of $K_{d,H}$ is at least $(1-j/H)^H$. For $j=1$, $\log(1+y)<y$ for $y>0$ gives $(1+1/(d-1))^{d-1}<e$, so $(1-1/d)^{d-1}>1/e>1/3$. This strict estimate has the positive multiplier $1-1/H$. Including the term at $j=0$ gives
$$
K_{d,H}>1+\frac{H-1}{3H}+\sum_{j=2}^{H-1}(1-j/H)^H .
$$

**The range $2\le H\le9$.** Subtraction gives $W_{d,H}-K_{d,H}<U_H$, where
$$
U_H=\frac{1}{1-(1-1/H)^H}-1-\frac{H-1}{3H}-\sum_{j=2}^{H-1}(1-j/H)^H .
$$

**The range $H\ge10$.** The inequality $\log(1-1/H)<-1/H$ gives $(1-1/H)^H<1/e<3/8$, so $W_{d,H}<8/5$. For each fixed $j\in\{2,3\}$, the logarithmic derivative of $(1-j/z)^z$ for $z>j$, again with $x=j/z$, is $\log(1-x)+x/(1-x)=\sum_{m\ge2}(m-1)x^m/m>0$. Keep the terms with $j=0,1,2,3$ in the lower bound on $K_{d,H}$, use $(H-1)/(3H)\ge3/10$, and evaluate the two fixed-index powers at $H=10$. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{d,H}&>\frac{13}{10}+(4/5)^{10}+(7/10)^{10},\\
W_{d,H}-K_{d,H}&<\frac85-\frac{13}{10}-(4/5)^{10}-(7/10)^{10}
=\frac{1643782927}{10^{10}}
=\frac16-\frac{68651219}{3\cdot10^{10}}<\frac16.
\end{aligned}
$$
The three ranges exhaust $1\le H\le d$.

**Exact arithmetic.** The following uses only rational arithmetic. It reproduces the finite table and checks the constant for $H\ge10$.

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F

for H in range(2, 10):
    q = (1 - F(1, H)) ** H
    U = 1 / (1 - q) - 1 - F(H - 1, 3 * H) - sum(
        (1 - F(j, H)) ** H for j in range(2, H)
    )
    margin = F(1, 6) - U
    assert margin >= 0
    print(H, margin)

large = F(8, 5) - F(13, 10) - F(4, 5) ** 10 - F(7, 10) ** 10
assert large == F(1643782927, 10 ** 10)
assert F(1, 6) - large == F(68651219, 3 * 10 ** 10) > 0
```
