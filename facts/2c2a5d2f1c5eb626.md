## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\). With exactly the mixed-integer volume and closed-halfspace depth of the locked problem, write

\[
C_z=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z:w_z>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

These are the actual-fiber objects and original normalization of accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c. There exists one \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), chosen before any halfspace, such that

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{W}{100000e}.
\tag{1}
\]

When \(W=0\), every \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) satisfies (1). There are no fiber-shape, symmetry, centroid-rank, or projection-size assumptions.

## Proof

For positive-volume compact convex bodies \(K_i\subset\mathbb R^d\), write \(v_i=\operatorname{vol}_d(K_i)\), \(r_i=v_i^{1/d}\), and \(b_i\) for their uniform centroids. Apply the displacement-interpolation theorem specified below to their uniform laws. For every \(t\in[0,1]\), its interpolant \(\nu_t\) has support in \((1-t)K_0+tK_1\), mean \((1-t)b_0+tb_1\), and density \(f_t\) satisfying

\[
\|f_t\|_\infty
\le\bigl((1-t)r_0+tr_1\bigr)^{-d}.
\tag{2}
\]

The coupling marginals give the support and mean. Substituting endpoint density norms \(1/v_i\) into the theorem proves (2), including \(t=0,1\).

As a consequence, whenever two real fibers of \(C\) have positive volume, convexity places \((1-t)C_x+tC_{x'}\) inside \(C_{(1-t)x+tx'}\). The unit mass and density bound in (2) imply

\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{(1-t)x+tx'})^{1/d}
\ge(1-t)\operatorname{vol}_d(C_x)^{1/d}
+t\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{x'})^{1/d}.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular the positive integer support \(D_+\) is an integer interval and the sequence \(r_z=w_z^{1/d}\), \(z\in D_+\), is concave. Compactness makes that support finite. If \(W=0\), choose any point of the assumed nonempty \(S\); nonnegativity of mixed-integer volume proves (1). Henceforth \(W>0\), so \(D_+\ne\varnothing\).

Choose an integer maximum \(z_0\in D_+\), put \(R=r_{z_0}>0\), and define

\[
\delta=\frac1{10000d},\qquad
I=\{z\in D_+:r_z\ge(1-\delta)R\},\qquad
W_I=\sum_{z\in I}w_z.
\tag{4}
\]

The set \(I\) is an integer interval containing \(z_0\), by (3). Write its endpoints as \(z_-,z_+\), and put \(m_-=z_0-z_-\), \(m_+=z_+-z_0\), and \(N=|I|=m_-+m_++1\).

We prove the original-mass estimate

\[
\frac{W_I}{W}\ge\frac{9999}{10000\cdot20001}>\frac1{25000}.
\tag{5}
\]

For either sign \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), write \(m_\sigma=m_-\) or \(m_+\) as appropriate. If that side has any positive integer fiber outside \(I\), then \(z_0+\sigma(m_\sigma+1)\in D_+\) and its radius is strictly less than \((1-\delta)R\). For every further positive integer fiber \(z_0+\sigma k\), \(k\ge m_\sigma+1\), apply (3) to the segment from \(z_0\) to \(z_0+\sigma k\), evaluated at \(z_0+\sigma(m_\sigma+1)\). Rearrangement gives

\[
0<r_{z_0+\sigma k}
\le R+\frac{k}{m_\sigma+1}
\bigl(r_{z_0+\sigma(m_\sigma+1)}-R\bigr)
<R\left(1-\frac{\delta k}{m_\sigma+1}\right).
\tag{6}
\]

For \(u_+=\max\{u,0\}\), the decreasing function \(g_\sigma(x)=(1-\delta x/(m_\sigma+1))_+^d\) obeys

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_\sigma(k)
\le\int_0^{\infty}g_\sigma(x)\,dx
=\frac{m_\sigma+1}{\delta(d+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the mass of all positive fibers on that side outside \(I\) is at most \(R^d(m_\sigma+1)/(\delta(d+1))\). If that side has no such fibers, its mass is zero and the same upper bound holds. The \(N\) fibers in \(I\) each have mass at most \(R^d\). Summing both sides proves

\[
W\le R^d\left(N+\frac{N+1}{\delta(d+1)}\right)
\le NR^d\left(1+\frac2{\delta(d+1)}\right)
\le20001NR^d.
\tag{8}
\]

Bernoulli's inequality gives \((1-\delta)^d\ge1-d\delta=9999/10000\). Therefore \(W_I\ge N(1-\delta)^dR^d\ge(9999/10000)NR^d\). Together with (8), this gives the first inequality in (5). The strict second inequality follows from \(9999\cdot25000>10000\cdot20001\). The calculation includes single-point supports, endpoint maxima, ties for the maximum, and sides without a tail.

Suppose first that \(z_-<z_+\). Retain the actual slab

\[
C'=C\cap([z_-,z_+]\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
S'=C'\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d).
\tag{9}
\]

It is a compact convex body in \(\mathbb R^{1+d}\): the convex hull of interior balls in the two positive endpoint fibers already has nonempty ambient interior. Its integer fibers are exactly the unchanged \(C_z\), \(z\in I\), and its mixed-integer volume is \(W_I\).

For \(z\in I\), let \(b_z\) and \(\Sigma_z\) be the true uniform-volume centroid and covariance of \(C_z\), with exactly their definitions in accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c. Define the single affine map

\[
\ell(z)=\frac{z_+-z}{z_+-z_-}b_{z_-}
+\frac{z-z_-}{z_+-z_-}b_{z_+}.
\tag{10}
\]

Writing \(\ell(z)=Lz+b\) identifies its slope \(L\in\mathbb R^{d\times1}\) and intercept \(b\in\mathbb R^d\) with precisely the affine-approximation objects in that accepted theorem.

For each \(z\in I\), set \(t=(z-z_-)/(z_+-z_-)\) and apply (2) to the two actual endpoint fibers. This gives a probability measure \(\nu_z\) supported in \((1-t)C_{z_-}+tC_{z_+}\subset C_z\), with mean \(\ell(z)\) and density at most \(((1-t)r_{z_-}+tr_{z_+})^{-d}\). Relative to the uniform law \(\mu_z\) on the actual \(C_z\), its likelihood ratio \(q_z=d\nu_z/d\mu_z\) therefore satisfies

\[
0\le q_z\le
\frac{w_z}{((1-t)r_{z_-}+tr_{z_+})^d}
\le(1-\delta)^{-d}
\le\frac{10000}{9999},\qquad
\int q_z\,d\mu_z=1.
\tag{11}
\]

Here the numerator bound is the integer maximum \(w_z\le R^d\), the denominator bound uses both endpoints in \(I\), and the last inequality follows from the same Bernoulli estimate. No maximum over noninteger fibers is used.

Equation (11) gives

\[
\int(q_z-1)^2\,d\mu_z
=\int q_z^2\,d\mu_z-1
\le\frac1{9999}.
\tag{12}
\]

For every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\), the equality of the transported mean to \(\ell(z)\) and Cauchy-Schwarz now imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\beta\cdot(\ell(z)-b_z)|
&=\left|\int(q_z-1)\,\beta\cdot(y-b_z)\,d\mu_z(y)\right|\\
&\le\frac1{\sqrt{9999}}
\left(\int\bigl(\beta\cdot(y-b_z)\bigr)^2\,d\mu_z(y)\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\frac1{\sqrt{9999}}\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

These simultaneous inequalities use each actual fiber's own covariance, with no comparison across different fibers. They also include \(\beta=0\). Since \(9999>144\) and \(e<3\), the fixed error \(\epsilon=1/\sqrt{9999}\) is less than \(1/12<1/(4e)\). All hypotheses of accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c hold for the body \(C'\), with \(n=1\), the original slab weights, its unchanged actual centroids and covariances, and the affine map (10). That theorem gives one fixed actual point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S'\) satisfying

\[
h_{S'}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{W_I}{4e}.
\tag{14}
\]

If instead \(z_-=z_+\), the slab is lower-dimensional, so we handle it separately. Put \(C''=[z_--1/4,z_-+1/4]\times C_{z_-}\), which is an ambient convex body with mixed support \(S''=\{z_-\}\times C_{z_-}\subset S\) and mixed volume \(W_I=w_{z_-}\). Apply accepted fact 711f1969d2e61d1c to \(C''\) with \(n=1\), \(L=0\), \(b=b_{z_-}\), and \(\epsilon=0\). The only positive integer fiber has exactly its original uniform law, centroid, and covariance, so its approximation error is zero. The theorem gives \(\mathbf y^\star=(z_-,b_{z_-})\in S''\) and \(h_{S''}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge W_I/(4e)\). This auxiliary cylinder serves only to apply the accepted theorem to the single original fiber.

In either case the retained mixed support is a measurable subset of the original \(S\). Every closed halfspace containing the chosen fixed point consequently has original mixed volume at least its volume on that retained support, which by (14) or the singleton argument is at least \(W_I/(4e)\). Taking the infimum over precisely the original closed ambient halfspaces and using (5) yields

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{W_I}{4e}
\ge\frac{W}{100000e}.
\]

This proves (1) with one actual point selected before every halfspace. The constant is absolute, independent of \(d\) and \(C\).
