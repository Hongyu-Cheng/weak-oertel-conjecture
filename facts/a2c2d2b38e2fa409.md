## Statement

For every integer \(m\ge1\), use exactly the standard recursive symmetric chain partition and block objects of accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c. Thus \(B_m=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), \(D_m\) is the set of nonterminal words, \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) is their chain successor, and
$$
E_m=\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\rbrace,\qquad
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap B_m,\qquad
T_m=\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\cap\mathbb Z^m.
$$
Here \(e_i\) is the coordinate unit vector. The partition starts with the one empty chain in dimension zero. A chain \(S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k}\) is replaced in the next dimension by
$$
S_k,\ldots,S_{m-k},S_{m-k}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace
$$
and
$$
S_k\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,\ldots,S_{m-k-1}\cup\lbrace m+1\rbrace,
$$
with an empty second chain omitted. For \(v\in B_m\), write
$$
\sigma_k(v)=2\sum_{h=1}^k v_h-k\qquad(0\le k\le m).
$$
Then
$$
C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}
=\left\lbrace b\in B_m:\max_{0\le k\le m}\sigma_k(b)\ge3\right\rbrace.
\tag{1}
$$

Here is a separating functional for every \(b\) excluded by (1). Match zeros and ones in \(b\) by scanning from left to right and pairing each one with the last previously unmatched zero, whenever such a zero exists. Let \(j_1<\cdots<j_p\) be the positions of the unmatched ones, let \(\mathcal J(b)\) be the positions of the matched ones, and let \(\mathcal Z(b)\) be all positions where \(b\) is zero. For \(p\le2\), define the linear functional
$$
L_b(x)
=-2^{p+1}\sum_{h\in\mathcal Z(b)}x_h
+2^p\sum_{h\in\mathcal J(b)}x_h
-\sum_{r=1}^p2^{p-r}x_{j_r}.
\tag{2}
$$
Empty sums equal zero. Every \(z\in E_m\) satisfies
$$
L_b(z)\le L_b(b)-1,
\tag{3}
$$
so (2) strictly separates \(b\) from \(\operatorname{conv}(E_m)\).

In particular, if \(N_m\) denotes the number of words in the right-hand side of (1), then
$$
|C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}|=N_m,\qquad
|T_m|=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}+N_m.
\tag{4}
$$
With out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero, the exact counts are
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{2r}&=2^{2r}-\binom{2r}{r}-2\binom{2r}{r+1} &&(r\ge1),\\
N_{2r+1}&=2^{2r+1}-2\binom{2r+1}{r+1}-\binom{2r+1}{r+2} &&(r\ge0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

## Proof

Accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c proves three matching properties for the specified partition. Its successor changes the first unmatched zero to one; a word is terminal exactly when there is no unmatched zero; and the number of unmatched ones in a word \(b\) equals \(\max_k\sigma_k(b)\). It also proves that the unmatched-symbol word has the form \(1^p0^q\), with internally matched words between consecutive unmatched positions. Changing unmatched symbols preserves those intervening matches. We use these properties with the same coordinate order and matching convention.

We first prove the restriction that makes necessity possible. Suppose \(c\in D_m\), \(c\le b\) coordinate by coordinate, and \(i=i_m(c)\) satisfies \(b_i=1\). Then \(i\) is an unmatched-one position of \(b\).

Indeed, \(i\) is the first unmatched-zero position of \(c\). Immediately before position \(i\) is scanned, its zero stack is empty. Otherwise a zero already on the stack would lie below the zero at \(i\). Since the zero at \(i\) survives to the end, every zero below it would also survive, contradicting that \(i\) is the first unmatched zero. An empty stack at the end of this prefix implies
$$
\sigma_{i-1}(c)\ge\sigma_k(c)\qquad(0\le k<i).
\tag{6}
$$
For clarity, at any scan time the walk height is the number of unmatched ones minus the current zero-stack size. The number of unmatched ones never decreases. When the stack is empty at time \(i-1\), the height there therefore dominates all earlier heights, proving (6).

If the one at \(i\) were matched in \(b\), let \(h<i\) be its paired zero. The interval from \(h\) through \(i\) has equally many zeros and ones. This follows directly from the last-zero stack rule: all entries between an opening zero and its closing one match internally. Consequently
$$
\sigma_{i-1}(b)-\sigma_{h-1}(b)=-1.
$$
Since \(c\le b\), summing the walk increments on this same interval gives
$$
\sigma_{i-1}(c)-\sigma_{h-1}(c)
=\sum_{k=h}^{i-1}(2c_k-1)
\le\sum_{k=h}^{i-1}(2b_k-1)=-1,
$$
contradicting (6). This proves the asserted restriction.

Fix now \(b\) with \(p\le2\) unmatched ones, and put \(s=2^p-1\). Formula (2) gives
$$
L_b(b)=2^p|\mathcal J(b)|-s.
\tag{7}
$$
Take any \(z=c+2e_{i_m(c)}\in E_m\). Every coordinate of \(z\) is nonnegative, exactly one coordinate is two, and all other coordinates are zero or one. We prove (3) by exhausting three cases.

First suppose \(z_h>0\) for at least one \(h\in\mathcal Z(b)\). Then
$$
\sum_{h\in\mathcal Z(b)}z_h\ge1,\qquad
\sum_{h\in\mathcal J(b)}z_h\le|\mathcal J(b)|+1,
$$
and the last sum in (2) is nonpositive after its minus sign. Thus (7) yields
$$
L_b(z)-L_b(b)
\le-2^{p+1}+2^p+s=-1.
\tag{8}
$$

Otherwise \(z_h=0\) at every original zero position. This forces \(c\le b\), and the spike coordinate \(i_m(c)\) is a one position of \(b\). The restriction just proved puts that spike among \(j_1,\ldots,j_p\). In particular \(z_h=c_h\le1\) for every \(h\in\mathcal J(b)\).

In the second case, at least one matched-one coordinate is zero in \(z\). Then
$$
\sum_{h\in\mathcal J(b)}z_h\le|\mathcal J(b)|-1.
$$
Discarding the nonpositive last term of (2) again gives
$$
L_b(z)-L_b(b)\le-2^p+s=-1.
\tag{9}
$$

It remains that \(z\) agrees with \(b\) at all original zero positions and at all matched-one positions. The source \(c\) can therefore differ from \(b\) only at its \(p\) unmatched-one positions. By the intervening-word matching property from 7538f1751c45475c, matching \(c\) reduces to matching a word \(v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^p\) followed by the \(q\) original unmatched zeros. Its first unmatched zero must be among the first \(p\) symbols, since the spike coordinate belongs to \(\lbrace j_1,\ldots,j_p\rbrace\).

For \(p=0\), this is impossible, so this third case has no generator. For \(p=1\), the only possible first symbol of the reduced source is zero, and hence \(z_{j_1}=2\). Formula (2), with all other coordinates fixed, gives
$$
L_b(z)-L_b(b)=-(2-1)=-1.
$$
For \(p=2\), the only reduced source patterns with a surviving zero among the first two positions are \(00\) and \(10\). The pattern \(01\) matches internally, and \(11\) has no zero there. The respective restrictions of \(z\) to \((j_1,j_2)\) are therefore \((2,0)\) and \((1,2)\). In either case
$$
2z_{j_1}+z_{j_2}=4,
$$
whereas the same weighted sum for \(b\) is three. Thus \(L_b(z)-L_b(b)=-1\) in this case as well. This completes all cases and proves (3).

A convex combination of generators also satisfies (3), so no \(b\) with \(p\le2\) belongs to \(C_m^{\mathrm{bool}}\). Since \(p=\max_k\sigma_k(b)\), this proves the missing inclusion in (1). The opposite inclusion, together with explicit four-generator rational certificates for all its words, is exactly the membership result in accepted fact 7538f1751c45475c. Therefore (1) holds for every \(m\ge1\).

Finally, 7538f1751c45475c proves both the formulas (5) for the number of words hitting height three and the disjoint decomposition
$$
T_m=E_m\mathbin{\dot\cup}C_m^{\mathrm{bool}},\qquad
|E_m|=2^m-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}.
$$
Combining these accepted identities with (1) gives (4) and the exact counts claimed.
