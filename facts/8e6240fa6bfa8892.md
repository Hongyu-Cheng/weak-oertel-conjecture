## Statement

Let $n,d\ge1$, let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex, and let $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ be nonempty. Put $q=\dim_{\mathrm H}S$, $\mu=\mathcal H^q|_S$ and $M=\mu(S)>0$. Write $\mu_r$ for the restriction to base parity $r$ and use only $r$ with $M_r=\mu_r(S)>0$. The measure $\pi=\sum_r(\mu_r\otimes\mu_r)/M_r$ has mass $M$, both marginals $\mu$, swap symmetry, and actual integer-base midpoints. Its midpoint pushforward has density $2^q\sum_r\mathcal H^q(S_r\cap(2Y-S_r))/M_r$ relative to $\mu$, where $S_r$ is the parity-$r$ trace.

For any finite cover of $S$ by closed ambient halfspaces $H_i$, assign each ordered pair symmetrically and measurably to a cap containing its midpoint, and refine by the midpoint base $j$. Let $\pi_{ij}$ be the resulting pair submeasures, $\nu_{ij}$ their common marginal and $t_{ij}$ their mass. Then $\sum_{ij}\nu_{ij}=\mu$, each $\nu_{ij}\le\mu$, and $\int(z-j)\,d\nu_{ij}=0$. For $t_{ij}>0$ the centroid $Y_{ij}$ of $\nu_{ij}$ belongs to $S\cap H_i$. For any affine margin $L_i$ defining $H_i$,
$t_{ij}L_i(Y_{ij})=\int L_i((A+B)/2)\,d\pi_{ij}\ge0$. If $\max_i L_i\ge\varepsilon>0$ on $S$, assignment by a largest margin ensures the stronger bound $\varepsilon t_{ij}$.

For any subset $T$ of positive table labels, write $I$ for its cap indices, $U=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i$, and $\pi_T,\nu_T,t_T$ for the corresponding sums. Put $\kappa_T=\pi_T(U\times U)$, $c_i=\mu(H_i)$, and $D_I=\sum_{i\in I}c_i-\mu(U)$. Then
$$
t_T+\kappa_T=2\nu_T(U)\le2\mu(U),\qquad
t_T+\kappa_T+2D_I\le2\sum_{i\in I}c_i.
$$
In particular the single-index aggregates satisfy $t_i+\kappa_i\le2c_i$. Under assignment prioritizing any common endpoint cap, $\sum_i\kappa_i>0$. If $c_i\le B$ for all $i$ and $K$ indices are active, then $M+\sum_i\kappa_i\le2KB$ and $K>M/(2B)$. With $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, $B<\gamma_d M/2^n$ implies $K>2^n/(2\gamma_d)\ge2^n$.

## Proof

The parity-class product divided by $M_r$ has both marginals $\mu_r$. Convexity and the congruence of the two input bases make every midpoint actual. For $q>0$, a positive input fiber has dimension $q$, and the alignment in 01212b33858677e1 applies with the two input measures both $\mu_r$. The same alignment argument applies if $C$ lacks ambient interior: the Minkowski average of two positive fibers has direction equal to the sum of their directions and lies in an actual midpoint fiber of dimension at most $q$. This forces both directions to agree and their midpoint to have exactly that common direction. On the resulting common $q$-direction in each class, use $B=2Y-A$ for each fixed $A$ in the product integral. The Jacobian on the actual midpoint fiber is $2^q$. For fixed $Y$, summing over all ordered base pairs gives the $\mathcal H^q$-mass of exactly those $A\in S_r$ for which $2Y-A\in S_r$. Zero-measure input fibers add nothing to this density almost everywhere. Indeed if one input fiber has dimension less than $q$, its reflection intersection with any fixed $q$-fiber is null. For $q=0$ every nonempty fiber is a point and the substitution counts ordered pairs with determinant factor one. Thus the stated formula holds with no normalization by the sum of squared class masses.

Finite least-index tie-breaking gives a symmetric Borel assignment. For the common-endpoint option take the least cap containing both endpoints whenever possible. Its convexity places the midpoint in the cap too. Each restriction is swap-symmetric, so its midpoint mean equals the common marginal mean. The base of every assigned midpoint is $j$. Its conditional mean lies in the closed convex fiber of $C\cap H_i$ at $j$, proving the centroid claim. Affinity gives the margin integral, and pointwise maximizing selection gives the strict-margin variant.

A pair assigned to a cap in $I$ cannot have both endpoints outside $U$. Otherwise the strict complement of its assigned closed halfspace would contain both endpoints and their midpoint. If the double-capture mass is $\kappa_T$, the two mixed inside-outside statuses have a common mass $s$ by symmetry, so $t_T=\kappa_T+2s$ and $\nu_T(U)=\kappa_T+s$. Domination by $\mu$ proves the exact union identity and both inequalities. The same proof with one cap index proves its aggregate bound. Some cap covers positive mass in a represented parity because the cover is finite and exhaustive. Its within-parity endpoint square has positive $\pi$-mass. Common-endpoint priority assigns every such pair to a cap containing both, so the sum of double-capture terms is positive. Summing over active indices gives the strict count and also $B>0$. The binomial theorem gives $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$, which supplies the last comparison.
