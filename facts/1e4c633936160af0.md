## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty and finite, and give each $p\in P$ a full-dimensional compact convex $d$-body $K_p$, $d\ge1$, measured by summed fiber volume. Fix a real mark $(z,y)$, any norm $\Gamma$ on continuous covectors, and a bounded interval of signed displacements $\delta$. For the caps
$$
a\cdot(p-z)+u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\delta,\qquad \Gamma(u)=1,
$$
there is a bounded set of replacement base covectors $a'$ containing a representative of every cap with the exact mass on each fiber preserved and every proper cut unchanged. Consequently for every fixed $\delta$ the nonvertical cap-mass infimum is attained. The tilt bound is for fixed $(z,y)$ and the stated bounded interval.

## Proof

Put $l_p(u)=\min_{K_p}u\cdot(X-y)$ and $h_p(u)=\max_{K_p}u\cdot(X-y)$. For a full trace in volume require $a'\cdot(p-z)\ge-\delta-l_p(u)$, and for an empty trace in volume require $a'\cdot(p-z)\le-\delta-h_p(u)$. For a proper trace preserve the exact value $a\cdot(p-z)$, using a pair of inequalities. At either support the supporting section has zero $d$-volume because $u\ne0$, so these full and empty conventions preserve the corresponding masses. The original tilt is feasible. The proper scalar lies between the two bounded endpoints, so all right-hand sides are bounded, uniformly in $\delta$ and the compact $\Gamma$-unit sphere.

There are finitely many status patterns and their row vectors are among the fixed vectors $\pm(p-z)$. The minimum-norm argument in the proof of 155691fc3768c06f applies to each fixed matrix: a minimum-norm feasible tilt is zero or is given by $V_I^{\mathsf T}(V_IV_I^{\mathsf T})^{-1}c_I$ on some independent active row set $I$ with right side $c_I$. There are only finitely many such systems. This gives a uniform bound and preserves every prescribed trace mass. Take a minimizing sequence with clamped tilts and pass to a subsequence in the common ball and the $\Gamma$-unit sphere. At the limit all cutting planes remain fiber-volume null because $u\ne0$. Dominated convergence gives attainment.
