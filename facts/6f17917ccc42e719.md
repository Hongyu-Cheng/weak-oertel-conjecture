## Statement

Let $A,B,C\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies, $d\ge1$, with $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$ and volumes $a,b,c$. Suppose $0<m=(a+c-b)/2\le\min(a,c)$. Let $\mathcal P_m$ consist of measures on $A\times C$ with mass $m$ and marginals dominated by Lebesgue volume on their respective factors. For $\pi\in\mathcal P_m$ set $\lambda_\pi=\mathbf1_B\,dx+T_\#\pi$, where $T$ is the midpoint map. The maximum raw closed-halfspace depth of these targets over both $\pi$ and marks $y\in B$ is attained.

At any fixed $y$, put $W(y)=\max_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}\inf_{H\ni y}\lambda_\pi(H)$, using closed halfspaces. For every $\varepsilon>0$ some nonempty finite list of through-$y$ caps $H_i$ has optimum $W_F=\max_\pi\min_i\lambda_\pi(H_i)$ in $[W(y),W(y)+\varepsilon)$. For every such finite list, write $b_i=|B\cap H_i|$ and $c_i=\mathbf1_{H_i}\circ T$. The attained dual is
$$
W_F=\min_{\theta_i\ge0,\ \sum_i\theta_i=1}
\left(\sum_i\theta_i b_i+\max_{\pi\in\mathcal P_m}\int\sum_i\theta_i c_i\,d\pi\right).
$$
For a list approximating $W(y)$ and any minimizing weights, every fixed-center optimizer for $W(y)$ is within $\varepsilon$ of the optimal weighted partial-transport cost, in the same raw units.

## Proof

The class is nonempty by the product of the two volumes each scaled to mass $m$, divided by $m$. It is weakly compact and convex: mass and marginal domination are closed conditions by testing with continuous functions, and the product is compact. At a fixed unit normal $u$, if $\pi_k$ tends weakly to $\pi$ and $y_k\to y$, the moving closed cap pulls back eventually into $\{u\cdot(T-y)\ge-\eta\}$ for every $\eta>0$. By Portmanteau and continuity from above, the limsup is bounded by the pullback mass of $\{u\cdot(T-y)\ge0\}$. The same argument on the baseline $\mathbf1_B\,dx$ bounds its cap mass. The infimum over unit normals is upper semicontinuous and equals depth, since a containing cap contains the parallel through-mark cap. Compactness of $\mathcal P_m\times B$ gives joint attainment.

For any finite list the minimum of its cap masses is also upper semicontinuous, so its optimum is attained. If all finite lists permitted one measure making every listed cap mass at least $W(y)+\varepsilon$, the resulting closed constraints would have the finite intersection property. This would contradict $W(y)$ by compactness. Thus a finite list has $W_F<W(y)+\varepsilon$, and $W_F\ge W(y)$ by restriction.

The downward closure of the finite cap-mass vectors is convex by affinity and closed by weak compactness and upper semicontinuity. The point $(W_F,\ldots,W_F)$ is on its boundary and has a supporting normal with nonnegative coordinates, by downward closure. Normalize the normal to sum one. Its support inequality gives weighted value at most $W_F$, while for any simplex weights a maximizing finite primal measure has weighted value at least $W_F$. The inner weighted maxima are attained by upper semicontinuity. At a fixed-center optimizer every listed cap mass is at least $W(y)$. Its weighted shortfall is thus less than $\varepsilon$, and the constants $b_i$ cancel in this shortfall.
