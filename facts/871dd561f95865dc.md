## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $K$ be a $d$-simplex with centroid $g$ and barycentric functions $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$, and let $P=\{0,1\}^n$ for $n\ge0$, with one base if $n=0$. Write $N=2^n$ and $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For $p\in P$ let $L_p(z)=\sum_\ell[(1-p_\ell)z_\ell+p_\ell(1-z_\ell)]$, the affine function equal to Hamming distance from $p$ on $P$. The closed caps
$$
H_{p,j}=\{\lambda_j(x)-1/(d+1)-L_p(z)\ge0\}
$$
form a regular critical circuit through $(p,g)$ on the measured product $P\times K$. Each has mass $\Gamma_d|K|$, full mixed section centroid $(p,g)$ and coarea $d(d/(d+1))^{d-1}|K|$ for this affine margin. The response profile is $W_p=(d+1)^{-1}\sum_j\mathbf1_{H_{p,j}}$. For $\eta$ the sum of actual fiber boundary $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ marked by the outward unit normal, and nonnegative continuous potentials $\Phi$ convex in $(z,x)$ at each normal with positive $\eta$-integral, the optimal individual efficiency $\int\Phi W_p\,d\eta/\int\Phi\,d\eta$ is $\Gamma_d$. The optimal minimum of these efficiencies over $p$ is $\Gamma_d/N$. Both optima are attained. For one standard triangle, the maximum and the sum of its three positive canonical cap margins have efficiencies $2/5$ and $5/12$, respectively.

## Proof

On every other fiber the margin is strictly negative since $L_p\ge1$ and $\lambda_j\le1$. On the own fiber the cut is proper, with a captured copy of $K$ at linear scale $d/(d+1)$ about vertex $j$. This gives the mass. Differentiating $|K| (1-s)^d$ at $s=1/(d+1)$ gives the stated coarea. The section has one barycentric coordinate fixed at $1/(d+1)$ and the other $d$ permuted by its volume-preserving affine symmetries. Its centroid is $g$. The gradients of all $\lambda_j$ sum to zero, so they positively balance with equal coefficients and the equal coareas give equal response weights. The depth on $[0,1]^n\times K$ is the own ordinary depth on each exposed binary base by 2acc8bc803acf1c0. Its simplex maximum is $\Gamma_d|K|$ and is attained at $g$. In the singleton convention use that same ordinary simplex computation. Thus the displayed circuits attain the global depth. Alternatively their caps cover the own plane because the barycentric coordinates sum to one, and the ordinary centroid lower bound applies in every containing halfspace at $(p,g)$.

On a facet of $K_p$ the outward normal is constant and the restriction of $\Phi$ is convex. By f566248002918e0f applied to its negative, with the source count equal to $(d+1)W_p$, its weighted facet integral is at most $\Gamma_d$ times its facet integral. The normalized facet inequality multiplies by its facet area, and facet intersections are null. For $d=1$ both endpoints have profile value $1/2$. Hence the whole numerator is at most $\Gamma_d\int_{\partial K_p}\Phi\,d\eta$. This bounds the single ratio and summation bounds the minimum over $p$. The hinge $(1-L_p)_+$ is one on $p$ and zero on the other sampled bases. The constant test has equality on each facet by the accepted identity, so this hinge attains $\Gamma_d$. The sum of the hinges is one on all actual fibers, yielding the common ratio $\Gamma_d/N$. For $d\ge2$, equality in each own-fiber inequality requires affine restrictions on each facet, by the accepted equality case. In particular common optimality requires these equalities and equal denominator allocations across bases. Those conditions are sufficient as well.

On a standard triangle edge, use the affine parameter $s\in[0,1]$ between its vertices. The profile is $1/3+(1/3)\mathbf1_{[1/3,2/3]}(s)$ almost everywhere. The maximum positive margin is $\max(s,1-s)-1/3$, whose total and middle-third integrals in this parameter are $5/12,1/12$. The sum of positive margins adds the other positive margin on the middle third and equals $1/3$ there, giving totals $4/9,1/9$. Taking $1/3$ plus a third of the middle-to-total ratio gives $2/5$ and $5/12$. Each edge length cancels in these common ratios.
