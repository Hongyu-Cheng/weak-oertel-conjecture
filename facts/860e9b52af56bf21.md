## Statement

Let $k\ge0$ and $r,s\ge1$ be integers, let $C\subset\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb R^s$ be compact and convex, and sample at $(z,u)\in\mathbb Z^k\times(a+L)$, where $L$ has full rank and covolume $v$ in $\mathbb R^r$. For $z\in\mathbb Z^k$ let $Q_z=\{(u,x):(z,u,x)\in C\}$, $T_z=\sum_{u\in a+L}\operatorname{vol}_s((Q_z)_u)$, and $M_z=\operatorname{vol}_{r+s}(Q_z)$. Put $T=\sum_z T_z>0$ and $M=\sum_z M_z$ over all coarse integer slices. Let $J\ge1$ be any upper bound on the number of coarse levels with $T_z>0$.

Assume for each $1\le t\le r-1$ that every full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R^t\times\mathbb R^s$ with positive sampled mass on $\mathbb Z^t\times\mathbb R^s$ has a sampled point of normalized depth at least $2^{-t}\Gamma_s$, where $\Gamma_s=(s/(s+1))^s$. Then $C$ has a sampled point for which every containing closed halfspace has sampled mass, divided by $T$, at least
$$
\frac{\Gamma_s}{J\,2^{r-1}}\left(1-\frac{r!M}{vT}\right)_+.
$$
In particular $J\le2^k$ and $M/(vT)\le1/(2r!)$ imply normalized depth at least $\Gamma_s/2^{k+r}$. For $k=0$ take $J=1$ to obtain the one-level estimate. For $r=1$ all these conclusions are unconditional, and the point can be chosen as the centroid of an actual largest-volume sampled fiber of $C$.

## Proof

At every level with $T_z>0$, use the sharp hyperplane concentration result on the section-volume function of $Q_z$. Retain its positive-volume sampled fibers on the selected hyperplane and let $R_z$ be their total mass. Then $R_z\ge(T_z-r!M_z/v)_+$. Every selected hyperplane has positive retained mass, since it contains the positive support in the deficient case and contains the greedy points in the full-rank case. At zero-mass levels put $R_z=0$. Thus $\sum_zR_z\ge(T-r!M/v)_+$, with the ordinary volumes of coarse levels without positive samples included in $M$. Choose a positive level with greatest $R_z$, which has $R_z\ge(T-r!M/v)_+/J$. Apply the exact retained-fiber lattice reduction there. The retained affine rank $t$ is at most $r-1$. If $t=0$ use its unconditional continuous conclusion, and if $t>0$ use the assumed bound in dimensions $(t,s)$. The resulting sampled point has raw original depth at least $2^{-t}\Gamma_s R_z\ge 2^{-(r-1)}\Gamma_s R_z$. Dividing by $T$ proves the displayed formula. Under the two additional conditions the positive-part factor is at least $1/2$ and $J\le2^k$ gives the stated sharp constant.

When $r=1$, a hyperplane is one base point. In the full-rank case the greedy choice is a highest-volume fiber, and in the deficient case the unique positive sampled base is automatically highest. Therefore the chosen level with greatest $R_z$ gives a largest-volume fiber of the entire source. Its centroid is the point supplied by the rank-zero argument.
