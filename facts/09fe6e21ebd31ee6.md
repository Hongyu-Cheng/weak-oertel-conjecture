## Statement

Retain the complete canonical setting, objects, and normalizations of accepted facts `49092268f4f67b8e`, `6e6a3b541bc64f05`, `c1d49368c95bedb4`, and `f7f81ebc8d8cef0f`. Thus
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body of positive mixed volume,
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 I_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in I_+}a_z,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).
\tag{2}
\]
The positive whole-label family is \(\Gamma\). Every
\(\sigma\in\Gamma\) has mass \(\theta_\sigma>0\), physical target
\(t(\sigma)\), four-entry tagged occurrence multiset
\[
 \mathcal O_\sigma=
 [y_{\sigma,1},y_{\sigma,2},y_{\sigma,3},y_{\sigma,4}],
 \qquad
 \frac14\sum_{j=1}^4y_{\sigma,j}=t(\sigma),
\tag{3}
\]
and prescribed target barycenter
\[
 c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
 \qquad
 r_\sigma^c:=\frac14\sum_{j=1}^4c_{y_{\sigma,j}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Repeated physical sources in (3) remain separate tags. The labels are the retained labels of the canonical degree-weighted complete-product square family, with their fixed blocker tags and blocker-to-parent ancestry data. In particular,
\[
 R_z^\Gamma
 :=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}
 \theta_\sigma\operatorname{mult}_z(\mathcal O_\sigma)
 \leq a_z,
\tag{5}
\]
and accepted fact `f7f81ebc8d8cef0f`, using positive-root one-body convexity, gives
\[
 r_\sigma^c\in\mathsf M_{\theta_\sigma}(C_{t(\sigma)})
 \qquad(\sigma\in\Gamma).
\tag{6}
\]

Fix a nonempty subfamily \(\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\) and one tagged selector
\[
 o:\Lambda\longrightarrow\{1,2,3,4\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Put
\[
 w_z^{\Lambda,o}:=
 \frac14\sum_{\substack{\sigma\in\Lambda\\
 y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}=z}}\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 M_\Lambda:=\sum_z w_z^{\Lambda,o}
 =\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{8}
\]
For an affine subspace \(L\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
 M_L^{\Lambda,o}:=\sum_{z\in L}w_z^{\Lambda,o},
 \qquad
 D_L:=I_+\cap L,
 \qquad
 q_L:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_L)
\tag{9}
\]
whenever \(M_L^{\Lambda,o}>0\), and put
\[
 \mathsf S_{\Lambda,o}:=
 \sup_{\substack{L:\ M_L^{\Lambda,o}>0\\q_L\leq2}}
 2^{n-q_L}M_L^{\Lambda,o}.
\tag{10}
\]

Define the unit-normalized selected-label target cost by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathsf J_{\Lambda,o}:=
 \inf_{(\lambda_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Lambda}}
 \quad&
 \sum_t\int_{C_t}
 \max\left\{0,
 \max_{\substack{\sigma\in\Lambda\\t(\sigma)=t}}
 \lambda_\sigma(x)\right\}\,dx,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&
 \lambda_\sigma:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R
 \text{ affine},\\
 &\lambda_\sigma(r_\sigma^c)=1,\\
 &\lambda_\sigma(c_{y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}})\geq\frac12
 \qquad(\sigma\in\Lambda).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The feasible set in (11) is nonempty, because the constant family
\(\lambda_\sigma\equiv1\) is feasible. Hence
\[
 0\leq\mathsf J_{\Lambda,o}
 \leq\sum_{t:\Lambda_t\ne\varnothing}a_t<\infty.
\tag{12}
\]
Every physical target collision remains inside the single pointwise maximum in (11).

The selected source profile and every inherited replacement packet remain on the original no-reuse physical ledgers. More precisely,
\[
 0\leq w_z^{\Lambda,o}
 \leq\frac{R_z^\Gamma}{4}
 \leq\frac{a_z}{4},
 \qquad
 f_z^{\Lambda,o}:=
 \frac{w_z^{\Lambda,o}}{a_z}\mathbf1_{C_z}
 \leq\frac14\mathbf1_{C_z},
\tag{13}
\]
and \(f_z^{\Lambda,o}\) has mass \(w_z^{\Lambda,o}\) and barycenter \(c_z\). If \(F_{\sigma,z}\) denotes the labelwise blocker-to-parent replacement density from accepted fact `49092268f4f67b8e`, then restriction to \(\Lambda\) gives
\[
 f_z^{\Lambda,o}(x)
 +\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}F_{\sigma,z}(x)
 \leq\frac{17}{32}
 \quad\text{for every }z\text{ and a.e. }x\in C_z,
\tag{14}
\]
while every label packet retains mass and full first moment
\[
 \sum_z\int_{C_z}F_{\sigma,z}(x)\,dx=\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 \sum_z\int_{C_z}(z,x)F_{\sigma,z}(x)\,dx
 =\theta_\sigma(t(\sigma),r_\sigma^c).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus passing to \(\Lambda\), normalizing affine pieces, or fixing the selector in (7) does not reset any source density, reuse a blocker complement, split a physical target collision, or discard a parent incidence.

Consider the following two uniform assertions over the retained canonical class.

1. Diffuse-active fusion with arbitrary normalized pieces. There is \(C_{\mathrm F}<\infty\) such that, for every affine family
\[
 \ell=(\ell_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Gamma},
 \qquad
 \ell_\sigma(r_\sigma^c)\leq1,
\tag{16}
\]
if
\[
 \Lambda(\ell):=
 \left\{\sigma\in\Gamma:
 \ell_\sigma(r_\sigma^c)\geq\frac12\right\}
\tag{17}
\]
and one chooses, for every \(\sigma\in\Lambda(\ell)\), a tag satisfying
\(\ell_\sigma(c_{y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}})\geq1/2\), then
\[
 \boxed{
 M_{\Lambda(\ell)}
 \leq C_{\mathrm F}
 \left(G+I(\ell)+\mathsf S_{\Lambda(\ell),o}\right).}
\tag{F}
\]

2. Unit-normalized selected-label fusion. There is
\(C_{\mathrm U}<\infty\) such that every nonempty
\(\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\) and every selector (7) satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 M_\Lambda
 \leq C_{\mathrm U}
 \left(G+\mathsf J_{\Lambda,o}+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o}\right).}
\tag{U}
\]

These two assertions are equivalent up to a factor two in their constants:
\[
 \boxed{
 (\mathrm F)\text{ with }C_{\mathrm F}
 \Longrightarrow
 (\mathrm U)\text{ with }C_{\mathrm U}=C_{\mathrm F},}
\tag{18}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 (\mathrm U)\text{ with }C_{\mathrm U}
 \Longrightarrow
 (\mathrm F)\text{ with }C_{\mathrm F}=2C_{\mathrm U}.}
\tag{19}
\]
No instance of (U), and hence no unconditional diffuse-active fusion inequality, is asserted here. Equations (11), (18), and (19) identify the exact smaller theorem still to be proved.

Finally, retain the canonical zero-fresh hard square normalization of accepted facts `6e6a3b541bc64f05` and `f7f81ebc8d8cef0f`:
\[
 Q>0,
 \qquad
 \Theta:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\theta_\sigma
 >\frac{1143}{12800}Q,
 \qquad
 W\geq8Q,
 \qquad
 G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{20}
\]
Then every profile arising in (F) satisfies the exact hard slice screen
\[
 \boxed{\mathsf S_{\Lambda(\ell),o}\leq16G.}
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently, if (U) holds with any finite absolute constant
\(C_{\mathrm U}\), then every normalized family (16) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta
 \leq K_{\mathrm U}
 \bigl(G+A(\ell)+I(\ell)\bigr),
 \qquad
 K_{\mathrm U}:=
 \max\{4,136C_{\mathrm U}\}.}
\tag{22}
\]
For the capped prescribed-barycenter packing capacity
\(\mathsf P_\Gamma\) of accepted fact `49092268f4f67b8e`, its exact dual identity therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta\leq K_{\mathrm U}(G+\mathsf P_\Gamma),
 \qquad
 \max\{G,\mathsf P_\Gamma\}
 >\frac{1143}{25600K_{\mathrm U}}Q.}
\tag{23}
\]
Thus any finite constant in (U) gives an absolute pack-or-core terminal for the square branch. The terminal constant in (23) need not equal \(1/100\).

## Proof

We first verify that the reduction stays on the accepted physical interfaces. Since the selector chooses exactly one tagged occurrence of every label in \(\Lambda\), finite summation gives
\[
 \sum_z w_z^{\Lambda,o}
 =\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\theta_\sigma,
\tag{24}
\]
which is (8). At a fixed physical source \(z\), every selected tag is one of the occurrences counted with its exact multiplicity in \(R_z^\Gamma\). Hence
\[
 4w_z^{\Lambda,o}\leq R_z^\Gamma\leq a_z.
\tag{25}
\]
The constant density in (13) therefore lies below \(1/4\), has the displayed mass, and has barycenter \(c_z\). This proves (13) without combining witnesses from different affine slices.

Accepted facts `6e6a3b541bc64f05` and `49092268f4f67b8e` construct each
\(F_{\sigma,z}\) from the three nonchosen original tags, the literal complement of the chosen blocker density, and every incidence of the blocker ancestry matching. It proves (15) label by label and proves the pointwise bound
\[
 \sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^4
 \frac{\theta_\sigma}{4a_z}
 \mathbf1_{\{y_{\sigma,j}=z\}}\mathbf1_{C_z}
 +\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma}F_{\sigma,z}(x)
 \leq\frac{17}{32}.
\tag{26}
\]
All densities are nonnegative. Delete the labels outside \(\Lambda\), and, for every retained label, delete the three original occurrence packets other than the tag selected by \(o\). The remaining original-occurrence density is exactly \(f_z^{\Lambda,o}\), so (26) gives (14), while (15) is unchanged for every retained replacement packet. The four-tag identities (3)-(4) are exactly the complete-product square identities used in the first-moment equation in (15). Accepted fact `f7f81ebc8d8cef0f` proves (6) from the same four positive-root source packets by one-body convexity. Therefore every source, ancestry, and target packet asserted in (13)-(15) is an actual packet in the same body \(C\); no abstract replacement is introduced in this reduction.

We prove (18). Assume (F) with constant \(C_{\mathrm F}\), and fix
\(\Lambda,o\). Let \((\lambda_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\) be any feasible family in (11). Extend it to all of \(\Gamma\) by
\[
 \ell_\sigma:=
 \begin{cases}
 \lambda_\sigma,&\sigma\in\Lambda,\\
 -1,&\sigma\in\Gamma\setminus\Lambda.
 \end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]
This is normalized as in (16). Its good-label set is exactly \(\Lambda\), because the retained pieces have value one at their prescribed barycenters and every deleted piece has value \(-1\). The fixed tag is legal because feasibility in (11) gives
\[
 \ell_\sigma(c_{y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}})
 =\lambda_\sigma(c_{y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}})
 \geq\frac12.
\tag{28}
\]
The constant negative pieces do not change any pointwise maximum with zero. Hence the target cost \(I(\ell)\) is exactly the objective value of the feasible family in (11). Applying (F) and then taking the infimum over all feasible families proves (U) with
\(C_{\mathrm U}=C_{\mathrm F}\). No attainment of (11) is required.

We prove (19). Assume (U) with constant \(C_{\mathrm U}\), fix a normalized family \(\ell\), and put
\(\Lambda:=\Lambda(\ell)\). If \(\Lambda=\varnothing\), then the active mass is zero and (F) is immediate. Suppose \(\Lambda\ne\varnothing\), and fix any legal selected tag for every good label. Put
\[
 a_\sigma^\ell:=\ell_\sigma(r_\sigma^c)
 \in\left[\frac12,1\right]
 \qquad(\sigma\in\Lambda),
\tag{29}
\]
and define
\[
 \lambda_\sigma(x):=
 \frac{\ell_\sigma(x)}{a_\sigma^\ell}.
\tag{30}
\]
Then
\[
 \lambda_\sigma(r_\sigma^c)=1,
 \qquad
 \lambda_\sigma(c_{y_{\sigma,o(\sigma)}})
 \geq\frac{1/2}{a_\sigma^\ell}
 \geq\frac12,
\tag{31}
\]
so (30) is feasible in (11).

For every target \(t\) and every \(x\in C_t\), if the unit-normalized maximum is positive, choose a label attaining a positive term. Its unscaled value is also positive, and (29) gives
\[
 \lambda_\sigma(x)
 =\frac{\ell_\sigma(x)}{a_\sigma^\ell}
 \leq2\ell_\sigma(x)
 \leq2\max\left\{0,
 \max_{\substack{\tau\in\Gamma\\t(\tau)=t}}
 \ell_\tau(x)\right\}.
\tag{32}
\]
If the unit-normalized maximum is nonpositive, the same inequality after taking a maximum with zero is trivial. Integrating and summing over physical targets gives
\[
 \mathsf J_{\Lambda,o}\leq2I(\ell).
\tag{33}
\]
Apply (U) and (33):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_\Lambda
 &\leq C_{\mathrm U}
 \left(G+\mathsf J_{\Lambda,o}
 +\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o}\right)\\
 &\leq C_{\mathrm U}
 \left(G+2I(\ell)+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o}\right)\\
 &\leq2C_{\mathrm U}
 \left(G+I(\ell)+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
This is (F) with \(C_{\mathrm F}=2C_{\mathrm U}\), proving (19).

It remains to prove the hard-branch consequences. Fix an active profile from (F) and an affine subspace \(L\) contributing to (10). In the notation of accepted fact `c1d49368c95bedb4`, put
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in I_+\cap L}a_z.
\tag{35}
\]
Equation (25), or equivalently the physical slice comparison in that accepted fact, gives
\[
 W_L\geq4M_L^{\Lambda(\ell),o}.
\tag{36}
\]
The support-free rank-at-most-two slice theorem of accepted fact
`8aac56c49321466c` gives
\[
 G\geq\frac1{64}
 \min\left\{W,2^{n-q_L}W_L\right\}.
\tag{37}
\]
The hard normalization (20) implies
\[
 G<\frac Q{100}\leq\frac W{800}<\frac W{64}.
\tag{38}
\]
Therefore the first entry in the minimum in (37) is impossible, and (36)-(37) give
\[
 G\geq\frac1{64}2^{n-q_L}W_L
 \geq\frac1{16}2^{n-q_L}M_L^{\Lambda(\ell),o}.
\tag{39}
\]
Taking the supremum proves (21).

Assume now that (U) holds with finite \(C_{\mathrm U}\), and fix a normalized family \(\ell\). Accepted fact `49092268f4f67b8e` gives
\[
 M_{\Lambda(\ell)}
 \geq\frac{\Theta-2A(\ell)}4.
\tag{40}
\]
If \(A(\ell)\geq\Theta/4\), then
\[
 \Theta\leq4A(\ell).
\tag{41}
\]
Otherwise (40) gives
\[
 M_{\Lambda(\ell)}>\frac\Theta8.
\tag{42}
\]
Use (U), the feasible normalization (30), equation (33), and the hard slice screen (21):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac\Theta8
 &<M_{\Lambda(\ell)}\\
 &\leq C_{\mathrm U}
 \left(G+\mathsf J_{\Lambda(\ell),o}
 +\mathsf S_{\Lambda(\ell),o}\right)\\
 &\leq C_{\mathrm U}\left(17G+2I(\ell)\right)\\
 &\leq17C_{\mathrm U}\left(G+I(\ell)\right).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
Equations (41) and (43) prove (22) with
\(K_{\mathrm U}=\max\{4,136C_{\mathrm U}\}\).

Accepted fact `49092268f4f67b8e` proves the exact target dual identity
\[
 \mathsf P_\Gamma
 =\inf_\ell\{A(\ell)+I(\ell)\}.
\tag{44}
\]
Take a sequence of normalized families whose objectives decrease to the infimum in (44), apply (22), and pass to the limit. This gives
\[
 \Theta\leq K_{\mathrm U}(G+\mathsf P_\Gamma)
 \leq2K_{\mathrm U}\max\{G,\mathsf P_\Gamma\}.
\tag{45}
\]
The square mass lower bound in (20) turns (45) into (23). This is a positive absolute terminal for every finite \(C_{\mathrm U}\), and completes all claimed reductions and hard-branch consequences.
