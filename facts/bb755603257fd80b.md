## Statement

Let $I=[a,b]$, $a<b$, and let $w$ be continuous and nonnegative on $I$ and positive on its interior. Let $A$ be continuous, nonnegative, nonincreasing and concave on $I$, with finite $s_L=A'_+(a)$. There is a continuous nonnegative nonincreasing concave piecewise affine replacement $\widehat A$ preserving $A(a)$ and the three records
$$
\int_I wA,\qquad \int_I vwA,\qquad \int_I wA^2 ,
$$
with $\widehat A'_+(a)\le s_L$. If the cell is internal and $s_R=A'_-(b)$ is finite, one can preserve $A(b)$ and obtain $\widehat A'_-(b)\ge s_R$ using at most eight atoms in the closed-cell curvature representation, including any join atoms at the endpoints. On a terminal cell, with no restriction on the terminal slope, one can use at most seven atoms in the weighted curvature representation and require $\widehat A(b)\ge A(b)$. An atom at the terminal right endpoint represents slack in its completed value.

## Proof

Put $\mu=-A''$ on $(a,b)$ and $\ell(v)=A(a)+s_L(v-a)$. For $v<b$ the integrated slope identity is
$$
A(v)=\ell(v)-\int_{(a,b)}(v-t)_+\,d\mu(t).
$$
Indeed on a compact interval from $a$ to an interior $v$ the drop from $s_L$ is the accumulated curvature, and integrating that drop gives the kernel. In the internal case $\mu$ has mass $N=s_L-s_R$. Use arbitrary nonnegative measures $\eta$ of mass $N$ on the closed cell, keep their first moment $\int t\,d\eta(t)$ fixed, and use the same kernel $(v-t)_+$. This preserves $A(b)$ because $\int(b-t)\,d\eta$ is fixed. Its left slope is $s_L-\eta\{a\}\le s_L$ and its right slope is $s_R+\eta\{b\}\ge s_R$. Both are nonpositive, and the endpoint values and concavity imply nonnegativity. An endpoint atom records a slope drop at a join, with the arm value continuous there.

In the terminal case monotone convergence at $b$ gives the finite weighted curvature $d\nu(t)=(b-t)d\mu(t)$ of mass $N=\ell(b)-A(b)$. Use arbitrary nonnegative measures $\eta$ of mass $N$ on the closed cell, now with kernel
$$
D(v,t)=\frac{(v-t)_+}{b-t}\quad(t<b),\qquad D(v,b)=0\quad(v<b).
$$
Define $A_\eta(v)=\ell(v)-\int D(v,t)\,d\eta(t)$ for $v<b$ and extend by its left limit at $b$. The limit is $A(b)+\eta\{b\}$ by dominated convergence with $0\le D\le1$. Each arm is concave and nonincreasing and its initial slope is at most $s_L$, since each kernel is convex and nondecreasing in $v$. The completed value is nonnegative, as is the whole arm. An atom at $b$ leaves the interior arm unchanged and contributes no integral record. If $N=0$ in either construction, the original arm is affine.

Write
$$
T_i(t)=\int_t^b(v-t)v^i w(v)\,dv\quad(i=0,1).
$$
Fix $\int T_i\,d\eta$ in the internal problem and $\int K_i\,d\eta$ in the terminal problem, where $K_i(t)=T_i(t)/(b-t)$ for $t<b$ and $K_i(b)=0$. These are the records of the original ordinary or weighted curvature, respectively. By Fubini they fix the two linear arm integrals. All kernels used for linear measure constraints are continuous on the closed interval, including $K_i$ by boundedness of $v^i w(v)$. The feasible positive measures form a compact convex set in the weak topology. For each $v<b$ the arm kernel is continuous and bounded in $t$, including at $t=b$. Dominated convergence, using a uniform bound from fixed mass and the bounded kernel, gives continuity of $\eta\mapsto A_\eta$ into $L^2(w\,dv)$. This map is injective for fixed mass. Equality in that norm gives equality of continuous arms on the open cell. Their derivatives recover the curvature there, and their initial slopes recover the left endpoint atom (scaled by $1/(b-a)$ in the terminal case). In the terminal case multiply the recovered interior curvature by $b-t$ to recover the interior part of $\eta$. The remaining mass recovers the right endpoint atom in both cases.

Let $J(\eta)=\int_I wA_\eta^2$. It is continuous and strictly convex on the fixed-mass class, so its maximum on the feasible set occurs at an extreme point and its minimum exists. An extreme measure has at most as many support points as the number of linear records, including mass. Indeed if there are more support points, choose that many disjoint positive-measure neighborhoods. A linear dependence among their record vectors gives a nonzero signed perturbation with bounded density relative to the measure, supported on those neighborhoods. Both sufficiently small signs of that perturbation stay nonnegative and preserve all records. This contradicts extremality. The maximum therefore uses at most four atoms internally and three on a terminal cell.

For the minimum, consider the normalized moment hull of $(t,T_0(t),T_1(t))$ internally or $(K_0(t),K_1(t))$ on a terminal cell. Both hulls are full-dimensional in their coordinate spaces. An affine relation on the first curve, differentiated twice, gives $w(t)(c_0+c_1t)=0$ and then only zero coefficients. On the second, evaluation at $b$ gives zero constant term. Multiplying by $b-t$ and differentiating twice gives the same conclusion. If the prescribed moment is on the boundary, a supporting affine functional gives a nonzero nonnegative function $W$ in the affine span of the kernels, vanishing on the support of every feasible measure. Internally take $H=W$ on $(a,b)$. It either is nonzero affine, with at most one zero on the cell, or satisfies $H''=w(c_0+c_1t)$ with a nonzero affine factor. On a terminal cell take $H=(b-t)W$. Again $H''=w(c_0+c_1t)$ with nonzero affine factor, since otherwise $W$ would be a nonzero constant and could not vanish on a nonempty support.

If the prescribed moment is in the interior, let the value function be the minimum of $J$ on the fiber of each normalized moment in the hull. It is finite and convex by convexity of $J$, and its epigraph is closed by compactness of the fixed-mass class. A supporting hyperplane to the epigraph at this value has nonzero value-coordinate coefficient, since a purely moment-coordinate support would support the moment hull at an interior point. Its subgradient implies that the minimizing measure also minimizes $J$ minus a linear combination of the moments over all positive measures of mass $N$. Write $D(v,t)$ for the arm kernel, unweighted in the internal case. The first-variation function of this penalized objective is
$$
G(t)=-2\int_I D(v,t)w(v)A_\eta(v)\,dv-\text{the corresponding linear combination of moment kernels}.
$$
Testing the direction $N\delta_t-\eta$ gives $G(t)\ge N^{-1}\int G\,d\eta$ for every $t$. The continuous function $W=G-N^{-1}\int G\,d\eta$ is therefore nonnegative and vanishes on the support. In the internal case take $H=W$, and in the terminal case take $H=(b-t)W$. Twice differentiating in the open interval gives $H''=w(-2A_\eta+c_0+c_1t)$ for suitable coefficients. In the terminal case the multiplication removes the denominator from the derivative kernel before differentiating, and the mass term becomes affine.

In each nonaffine contact application, the support condition on the open interval is exactly the one in the two-contact lemma, because $-A_\eta''$ equals the restriction of $\eta$ internally and equals $\eta/(b-t)$ in the terminal case. If necessary translate $(a,b)$ into $(0,\infty)$, preserving the affine load form. The lemma gives at most two interior support points. With the two possible endpoint atoms this gives a minimizing measure with at most four atoms in both problems, including the affine boundary case.

Interpolate between such minimizing and maximizing measures. The linear records stay fixed, and continuity of $J$ gives an interpolation with the original quadratic value. The combined support has at most eight points internally and seven at a terminal cell. The arm is piecewise affine, with no actual slope break induced by a slack atom at the terminal right endpoint. Its endpoint and join assertions follow from the representations above.
