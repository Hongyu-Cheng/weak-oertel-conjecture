## Statement

Let $\mu$ be the original summed $d$-dimensional volume of a finite compact sampled family, $d\ge1$, with total mass $M>0$. Fix a positive-volume compact convex fiber $K$ at one sampled base and identify it with its lift. Among all finite nonnegative log-concave measures $\rho$ supported on this fixed fiber with $\rho(H)\le\mu(H)$ for every ambient closed halfspace $H$, the maximum total mass $L(K)$ is attained and satisfies $|K|\le L(K)\le M$. Here log-concavity is the multiplicative set inequality on compact sets, so measures on proper affine subspaces and the zero measure are allowed.

## Proof

Uniform volume restricted to $K$ is log-concave by the Brunn–Minkowski inequality and is a submeasure of $\mu$. A halfspace containing all sampled fibers gives the upper bound $M$. Finite measures of mass at most $M$ on compact $K$ are weakly sequentially compact, and mass is continuous there by testing against the constant one.

If $\rho_j$ are feasible and $\rho_j$ converges weakly to $\rho$, take $H=\{w:a\cdot w\ge b\}$. For $\delta>0$ let $G_\delta=\{a\cdot w>b-\delta\}$ and $H_\delta=\{a\cdot w\ge b-\delta\}$. Open-set Portmanteau gives $\rho(H)\le\rho(G_\delta)\le\liminf_j\rho_j(G_\delta)\le\mu(H_\delta)$. Send $\delta\downarrow0$ and use continuity from above. This preserves every constraint, including vertical boundaries carrying whole fibers.

For compact test sets $A,B$ in the target fiber plane and $0<t<1$, put $D=(1-t)A+tB$. The convex combination of their open $\delta$-neighborhoods is contained in the closed $\delta$-neighborhood of $D$. The log-concavity inequality for those open sets follows by compact inner approximation and monotonicity. Apply the inequality for $\rho_j$, then use the closed-set upper and open-set lower parts of Portmanteau. The limit mass of the closed $\delta$-neighborhood of $D$ is at least $\rho(A)^{1-t}\rho(B)^t$. Let $\delta\downarrow0$ to obtain log-concavity of $\rho$, allowing lower-dimensional support. A convergent subsequence of a maximizing sequence now gives an admissible maximizer by continuity of mass.
