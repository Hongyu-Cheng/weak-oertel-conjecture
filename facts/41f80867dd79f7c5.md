## Statement

Let the positive interval fibers $[l_i,h_i]$ at consecutive integer bases $0,\ldots,N$ have a convex lower roof and a concave upper roof. A cap boundary $x=y+a(z-p)$ passes through an actual mark $(p,y)$ and misses every sampled endpoint. The proper sections form a consecutive block $l,\ldots,r$ containing $p$. If the mass of the cap $x\ge y+a(z-p)$ is locally minimized in $a$, then $l+r=2p$. Thus the block has odd size, can contain the right endpoint only for $p\ge N/2$, the left endpoint only for $p\le N/2$, and both only for even $N$ and $p=N/2$.

## Proof

Linearly interpolate the lower and upper roofs. The intersection of the two strict inequalities $l(z)<y+a(z-p)<h(z)$ with the closed real interval $[0,N]$ is convex by convexity of $l$ and concavity of $h$. Its integer points are exactly the proper sections, and $p$ belongs because the own mark is in its interval and the boundary misses every endpoint. Thus those integers are consecutive. Near the selected slope all statuses stay fixed. The captured length of a proper section is $h_i-y-a(i-p)$, while a full or empty section has locally constant length. The derivative of total cap length is
$$
-\sum_{i=l}^r(i-p)=-(r-l+1)[(l+r)/2-p].
$$
A local minimum in the unrestricted tilt has derivative zero. The endpoint claims follow from $0\le l\le r\le N$.
