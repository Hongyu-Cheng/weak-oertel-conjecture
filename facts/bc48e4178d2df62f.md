## Statement

Fix any integer \(k\ge8\). Put
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor
\quad(8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
and put \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), \(n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\), and \(N=n+1\). Define \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) for \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). Here \(k\) is the height parameter, \(Q\) is tree depth, \(m\) is the branching number, \(n\) counts spatial coordinates, and \(N\) counts all integer coordinates.

Use any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), with the exact successor objects of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8: \(D_m\) is its set of nonterminal vectors, and \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\) is its injective successor, with \(c_{i_m(c)}=0\). The spatial coordinates are indexed by
$$
\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace^r.
$$
For a node \(v\) of depth less than \(Q\), including the empty root \(\varnothing\), let \(z[v]=(z_{v1},\ldots,z_{vm})\) denote its child-coordinate block. For each Boolean source \(b\), start at the root, continue from \(v\) to \(vi_m(b[v])\) when \(b[v]\in D_m\), and stop on failure or on reaching depth \(Q\). Write \(h(b)\) for its path length and \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\) for its selected coordinates. Retain exactly the spatial and height supports of accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df, with the latter's profile specialized to \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\):
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{m,Q}
&=\left\lbrace z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}:
 b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},
 \ 0\le s\le h(b)\right\rbrace,\\
\widehat A
&=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
 a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad
 t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace .
\end{aligned}
$$
The level of \(z_s(b)\) is \(s\), equivalently its number of coordinates equal to two. Define the disjoint root-block pattern sets
$$
R_0=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m,\qquad
R_+=\left\lbrace c+2e_{i_m(c)}:c\in D_m\right\rbrace.
$$
Let \(f:R_0\cup R_+\longrightarrow(0,\infty)\) be arbitrary. Let \(\lambda=(\lambda_u)_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\in\mathbb R^n\) vanish on all \(m\) root-child coordinates. In the same normalization as accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2, define
$$
\Pi(\lambda)=
\max_{v_0,\ldots,v_Q}
 \sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{v_ri}|,
$$
where \(v_0=\varnothing\) and \(v_{r+1}\) is a child of \(v_r\). Assume \(\Pi(\lambda)\le1\).

At every original index use the weights, full normalizer, and normalized law
$$
w_{(z,t)}=f(z[\varnothing])e^{\lambda\cdot z}2^{-t}
\quad((z,t)\in\widehat A),\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},\qquad
p_{\mathbf I}=\frac{w_{\mathbf I}}W.
$$
All other integer weights are zero. For every \(\mathbf I\in\widehat A\), define its actual ambient halfspace depth by
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)=
\inf\left\lbrace
 \sum_{\mathbf J\in H\cap\widehat A}p_{\mathbf J}:
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf I
\right\rbrace,
\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}
 p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
Then, for every \(k\), every permitted chain partition, every such \(f\), and every such \(\lambda\), including \(\Pi(\lambda)=1\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge 2^{-63}8^{-N}.
$$
Every original spatial point and column height is retained in \(W\) and \(p\), and every containing closed ambient halfspace is allowed. Accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba is used below for its actual neighbor-and-endpoint selection mechanism on this exact support.

## Proof

Write \(P_\lambda=\Pi(\lambda)\) and
$$
\mathcal B=\lbrace u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}:|u|\ge2\rbrace,\qquad
Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}=\prod_{u\in\mathcal B}(1+e^{\theta_u})
$$
for any field \(\theta\) vanishing on the root-child coordinates. Thus \(|\mathcal B|=n-m\), and \(Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}\) sums the Boolean exponential weights on all coordinates outside the root block. The full Boolean normalizer denoted \(Z_\theta\) in accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2 is exactly \(\prod_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}(1+e^{\theta_u})=2^mZ_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}\), since the root-child field is zero. This is the explicit normalization map; only the outside product is used below. The symbols \(A_{m,Q}\), \(Q\), \(m\), \(n\), and \(N\) keep their predecessor meanings.

A positive-level spatial point has root block \(c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) for its unique source block \(c\in D_m\). Conversely, every such root block occurs at level one with every Boolean assignment on \(\mathcal B\). Replacing its unique two by zero recovers \(c\), so the map \(c\mapsto c+2e_{i_m(c)}\) is injective. Consequently
$$
|R_0|=2^m,\qquad 0<|R_+|=|D_m|\le2^m.
$$
This also shows that \(R_0\) and \(R_+\) are disjoint, and that they describe all possible root blocks on \(\widehat A\).

For \(j\in\lbrace0,+\rbrace\), put
$$
F_j=\sum_{r\in R_j}f(r),\qquad
F_{j,4}=\sum_{r\in R_j}f(r)^4.
$$
Convexity of the fourth power, applied to the \(|R_j|\) positive numbers, gives
$$
\frac{F_{j,4}}{F_j^4}\ge |R_j|^{-3}\ge8^{-m}.
$$
The same two-term inequality \((1+x)^4\le8(1+x^4)\) for \(x>0\) gives
$$
\frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}
=\prod_{u\in\mathcal B}
 \frac{1+e^{4\lambda_u}}{(1+e^{\lambda_u})^4}
\ge8^{-(n-m)}.
$$
In particular, the product of these two ratios is at least \(8^{-n}\) for either root-block class.

Split the original support into the two sets
$$
\widehat A_0=\lbrace(z,t)\in\widehat A:\operatorname{level}(z)=0\rbrace,
\qquad
\widehat A_+=\lbrace(z,t)\in\widehat A:\operatorname{level}(z)\ge1\rbrace.
$$
For each class let \(\nu_j=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_j}w_{\mathbf I}\delta_{\mathbf I}\), let \(W_j=\nu_j(\mathbb R^N)>0\), and let \(q_j=\nu_j/W_j\). In defining \(\tau_{q_j}\), use all closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^N\) containing the sampled index, exactly as in the definition of \(\tau_p\). Write
$$
M_3(q_j)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_j}
 q_j(\mathbf I)\tau_{q_j}(\mathbf I)^3.
$$
These auxiliary laws will only estimate the original law; their normalizers will be kept separately until the final transfer.

The zero-level support is the entire Boolean cube, with every original height \(0,\ldots,k\). Since the field vanishes on the root block,
$$
W_0=F_0Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}\sum_{t=0}^{k}2^{-t}\le2F_0Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
Every halfspace containing an atom contains that atom's own mass. Apply this observation just to the Boolean atoms at height zero. Their unnormalized fourth powers sum to \(F_{0,4}Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}\), and therefore
$$
M_3(q_0)\ge
\frac{F_{0,4}Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{W_0^4}
\ge\frac1{16}
 \frac{F_{0,4}}{F_0^4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}
\ge\frac1{16}8^{-n}
=2^{-1}8^{-N}.
$$

We next estimate the positive-level normalizer. Fix \(r\in R_+\), let \(c\in D_m\) be its recovered Boolean source block, and fix \(s\ge1\). The unique-source map sends the Boolean assignments outside the root block whose forward path reaches \(s\) injectively to the level-\(s\) spatial points with root block \(r\). For such an assignment, the exponential multiplier caused by the selected zeros becoming twos is
$$
\exp\left(2\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\lambda_{u_\ell}\right)
\le e^{2P_\lambda}.
$$
Indeed the selected coordinates belong to the child blocks of a rooted path, which can be extended to depth \(Q\), and their absolute field sum is at most \(P_\lambda\). Dropping the forward-success restrictions on the Boolean assignments consequently bounds the spatial weight at that level and label by \(e^{2P_\lambda}f(r)Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}\). Summing every height in every positive-level column proves
$$
W_+\le e^{2P_\lambda}F_+Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}} R_k^+,\qquad
R_k^+=\sum_{s=1}^{Q}\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}.
$$
Let \(R_k=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}\), with exactly the meaning in accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2. Its elementary geometric-tail bound there, or directly the definition of \(b_\ell\), gives
$$
R_k^+\le R_k
\le2+2\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell2^{-\ell}
\le2+\frac{H_k-H_7}{4}
\le\frac{9+\log k}{4}.
$$
Here \(H_j=\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}1/\ell\). Thus the bound on \(W_+\) retains all positive-level columns and uses no restriction on \(f\).

We require a complete-point census with a prescribed root block. Write
$$
\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}.
$$
Accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df give \(|D_m|=|\operatorname{im}F_m|=2^m\eta\) and \(\eta^Q>3/4\). For \(1\le s\le Q\) and \(r\in R_+\), let \(\mathcal C_s(r)\) consist of the level-\(s\) spatial points with root block \(r\) whose canonical inverse reconstruction continues all the way to depth \(Q\). This is the same completeness notion as in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba. We claim that every field \(\theta\) vanishing on the root-child coordinates satisfies
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s(r)}e^{\theta\cdot j}
\ge \eta^{Q-1}e^{-3\Pi(\theta)}Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}
\qquad(1\le s\le Q,\ r\in R_+).
\tag{1}
$$

To prove (1), fix the recovered source block \(c\) and its first selected node \(u_1\). Give the Boolean coordinates in \(\mathcal B\) their independent product law of density \(e^{\theta\cdot b}/Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}}\); the fixed root block contributes no exponential factor. Starting at \(u_1\), require the next \(s-1\) visited blocks to belong to \(D_m\), taking their forward selections, and require the final \(Q-s\) visited blocks to belong to \(\operatorname{im}F_m\), taking their inverse selections. All \(Q-1\) visited blocks are fresh, because their coordinates have successive different depths.

A complete successful transcript fixes exactly its visited patterns at their recursively determined locations and leaves every unused coordinate free. Different transcripts give disjoint cylinder events: at their first difference their earlier patterns determine the same next location, where the two specified patterns differ. Under fair independent bits, every next success has probability \(\eta\), irrespective of its location. The total probability of the successful transcripts is therefore \(\eta^{Q-1}\).

For a coordinate \(u\) and its specified bit \(d\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), the probability ratio between the product law and fair bits is
$$
\frac{2e^{\theta_ud}}{1+e^{\theta_u}}\ge e^{-|\theta_u|}.
$$
The ratio for each complete transcript is thus at least \(e^{-\Pi(\theta)}\): its revealed coordinates are the child blocks on one rooted path, apart from the root block whose field is zero. Unused coordinates integrate to one under both laws. Summing the disjoint cylinder comparisons proves that mixed exploration succeeds with probability at least \(e^{-\Pi(\theta)}\eta^{Q-1}\). This argument is uniform even when the prescribed root block changes the locations of all later blocks.

On success, change the selected zero to two in the fixed root block and in each of the next \(s-1\) forward blocks. The resulting point is in \(\mathcal C_s(r)\); its remaining mixed-exploration blocks are precisely its inverse continuation. Conversely, a point of \(\mathcal C_s(r)\) recovers its unique Boolean source by replacing its twos by zeros, and its forward prefix and inverse suffix recover the successful mixed exploration. This is a bijection. Its exponential weight multiplier is at least \(e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}\), since the absolute field sum on its selected prefix coordinates is at most \(\Pi(\theta)\). Multiplying the preceding success-probability bound by \(Z_\theta^{\mathrm{out}} e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}\) proves (1).

First consider \(k\ge2^{16}\). Put \(L=\log k\), \(u=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\), and \(v=2u\). Then \(u\ge256\), \(v\le k\), and \(L>9\). Define
$$
U_k=\sum_{\ell=8}^{u}b_\ell2^{-\ell},\qquad
\mathcal G_+=
\left\lbrace(j,a_s+1):
 j\in\mathcal C_s(r),\ r\in R_+,\ 8\le a_s\le u
\right\rbrace.
$$
Every indicated height is an original height. The floor and harmonic estimates in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba give \(U_k\ge L/32\). Explicitly,
$$
U_k\ge\frac{H_u-H_7}{8}-\frac1{128}
\ge\frac L{16}-\frac{H_7}{8}-\frac1{128}
\ge\frac L{32},
$$
because \(H_u\ge\log(u+1)>L/2\), \(H_7/8+1/128<1/3\), and \(L/32>1/3\). Also the preceding normalizer bound becomes
$$
W_+\le e^{2P_\lambda}F_+Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}\,\frac L2.
\tag{2}
$$

Fix \(\mathbf I=(j,a_s+1)\in\mathcal G_+\), and put \(r=j[\varnothing]\). The actual-halfspace selection argument of accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba applies to every closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf I\). Its selected points have the forms
$$
(j-e_{u_\ell},a_s)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(j-V_\ell,a_\ell),\ (j+V_\ell,a_\ell),
\qquad
V_\ell=\sum_{q=s+1}^{\ell}e_{u_q},\quad \ell>s,
\tag{3}
$$
where \(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_Q\) is the complete inverse continuation. It selects distinct original indices inside the halfspace with total untilted weight \(2^{-t}\) at least \(L/128\). This is its pointwise neighbor-and-endpoint conclusion, before any division by a normalizer.

For clarity, the selection has no dependence on a weight comparison or on zero-level points. For an ambient halfspace through \(\mathbf I\), if its height coefficient is nonnegative, one of the endpoints in (3) is inside at every entrance height at least \(v\), giving untilted mass at least \(L/32\). If the height coefficient is negative, either the lower neighbors in (3) already give mass at least \(L/128\), or the partial-sum argument in that accepted fact discards at most \(4k2^uL\) of the high-entrance endpoint choices. Their total loss is at most \(4k2^{-u}L\le L/64\), leaving at least \(L/64\). A general containing halfspace contains a parallel one through \(\mathbf I\).

All points in (3) have level \(s\) or a larger level, hence belong to \(\widehat A_+\). More strongly, every changed coordinate has depth at least \(s+1\ge2\). Thus every one of those points preserves the entire root-child block \(r\), not just its selected child. Its changed coordinates lie on the complete rooted path through \(j\), and each changes by one in absolute value. Therefore its spatial field factor is at least \(e^{\lambda\cdot j-P_\lambda}\). The arbitrary multiplier on every selected point is exactly \(f(r)\). Summing the selected weights inside each containing halfspace, with no changes to the remaining original weights, proves
$$
h_{\nu_+}(\mathbf I)
:=W_+\tau_{q_+}(\mathbf I)
\ge f(r)e^{\lambda\cdot j-P_\lambda}\frac L{128}
\qquad(\mathbf I\in\mathcal G_+).
\tag{4}
$$
This bound permits all closed halfspaces of the original ambient space.

We now sum (4) under the entire positive-level law, retaining the fourth power of its own normalizer. Apply (1) to \(\theta=4\lambda\), whose path variation is \(4P_\lambda\). Using \(\sum_{s:8\le a_s\le u}2^{-a_s}=U_k\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q_+)
&=\frac1{W_+^4}
 \sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_+}
 w_{\mathbf I}h_{\nu_+}(\mathbf I)^3\\
&\ge
 \frac{e^{-3P_\lambda}L^3}{128^3W_+^4}
 \sum_{\substack{s:8\le a_s\le u}}2^{-(a_s+1)}
 \sum_{r\in R_+}f(r)^4
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s(r)}e^{4\lambda\cdot j}\\
&\ge
 \frac{\eta^{Q-1}e^{-15P_\lambda}
       F_{+,4}Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}\,U_kL^3}
      {2\cdot128^3W_+^4}\\
&\ge 3\cdot2^{-25}e^{-23P_\lambda}
 \frac{F_{+,4}}{F_+^4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality uses (2), \(U_k\ge L/32\), and \(\eta^{Q-1}\ge\eta^Q>3/4\). The census costs \(12P_\lambda\), the cubed depth comparison costs \(3P_\lambda\), and the fourth power of the normalizer costs \(8P_\lambda\); these explain the exponent \(23P_\lambda\). The root-block and remaining-coordinate fourth-moment inequalities now give
$$
M_3(q_+)\ge3\cdot2^{-25}e^{-23}8^{-n}
=3\cdot2^{-22}e^{-23}8^{-N}
>2^{-60}8^{-N}.
\tag{5}
$$
For the last numerical estimate, \(e<3\), \(3^5<2^8\), and \(3^3<2^5\) imply \(e^{23}<2^{37}\).

It remains to prove the positive-level bound for \(8\le k<2^{16}\). In this range \(\log k<16\) and \(R_k<8\), so
$$
W_+<8e^{2P_\lambda}F_+Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
Since \(b_8=4\), the first entrance height is \(a_1=8\). For every \(r\in R_+\) and every Boolean assignment on \(\mathcal B\), the spatial point with that root block and that assignment has level one, and its original atom at height eight belongs to \(\widehat A_+\). No later forward success is needed. The fourth powers of precisely these unnormalized atom weights sum to
$$
2^{-32}F_{+,4}Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}.
$$
Using each atom's own mass as a lower bound for its conditional halfspace depth gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q_+)
&\ge\frac{2^{-32}F_{+,4}Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{W_+^4}\\
&>2^{-44}e^{-8P_\lambda}
 \frac{F_{+,4}}{F_+^4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{\mathrm{out}}}{(Z_\lambda^{\mathrm{out}})^4}\\
&\ge2^{-44}e^{-8}8^{-n}
=2^{-41}e^{-8}8^{-N}
>2^{-54}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-60}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(e^8<3^8<2^{13}\). Together with (5), this establishes \(M_3(q_+)\ge2^{-60}8^{-N}\) for every \(k\ge8\).

Finally put \(\alpha_j=W_j/W\) for \(j\in\lbrace0,+\rbrace\). These are positive and sum to one. At every index \(\mathbf I\in\widehat A_j\), every original containing halfspace has \(p\)-mass at least \(\alpha_j\) times its \(q_j\)-mass. Taking the infimum over the same family of ambient halfspaces therefore gives
$$
\tau_p(\mathbf I)\ge\alpha_j\tau_{q_j}(\mathbf I).
$$
Since \(p_{\mathbf I}=\alpha_jq_j(\mathbf I)\) on that class, summing over both classes yields the exact transfer inequality
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\alpha_0^4M_3(q_0)+\alpha_+^4M_3(q_+)\\
&\ge2^{-60}8^{-N}(\alpha_0^4+\alpha_+^4)\\
&\ge2^{-63}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step is \((\alpha_0+\alpha_+)^4\le8(\alpha_0^4+\alpha_+^4)\). This returns to the full original normalizer \(W=W_0+W_+\) and its unchanged law \(p\). The argument used no additional restriction on the chain partition, the positive root-block multiplier, or the signs of the remaining field, and every estimate holds on \(P_\lambda=1\).
