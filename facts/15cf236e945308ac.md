## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge1\), set \(d=k\), \(n=6k\), \(T=[0,1]^k\), and, for \(1\le j\le k\) and \(1\le r\le6\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{6(j-1)+r}(\mathbf y)
&=\min\left\{1,\frac{676}{675}y_j\right\},\\
C&=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d:
\mathbf y\in T,\quad
-\ell_i(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i(\mathbf y)
\ \ (1\le i\le n)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These roofs are bounded, nonnegative, continuous, and concave. The set \(C\) is a convex body invariant under each coordinate reflection \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\), and \(C\subseteq[-1,2]^n\times\mathbb R^d\).

Use the original objects \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), \(C_I=\{\mathbf y:(I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)=\sum_I|C_I|\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(\mathbf y)&=\prod_{i=1}^n(\lfloor\ell_i(\mathbf y)\rfloor+1),\\
N_C(\mathbf y)&=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathbf1_{C_I}(\mathbf y),\\
R_C(\mathbf y)&=\sum_{I\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathbf1_{C_I\cap C_{-I}}(\mathbf y).
\end{aligned}
\]
All three densities are zero outside \(T\), and \(|\cdot|\) denotes the original \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue volume. For \(\mathbf t\in[0,\infty)^n\), put \(K_{\mathbf t}=\{\mathbf y\in T:\ell_i(\mathbf y)\ge t_i\text{ for every }i\}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_C&=2^{6k}A,\qquad
W=2^{6k}\left(\frac{739}{676}\right)^k,\\
\max_{\substack{\mathbf t\in[0,\infty)^n\\|K_{\mathbf t}|>0}}
\frac{\left(\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}\sqrt{R_C(\mathbf y)}\,d\mathbf y\right)^2}
{|K_{\mathbf t}|}
&=\left(\frac{729}{676}\right)^k,\\
\frac{\displaystyle
\sup_{\substack{\mathbf t\in[0,\infty)^n\\|K_{\mathbf t}|>0}}
\left(\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}\sqrt{R_C}\right)^2/|K_{\mathbf t}|}
{\displaystyle\int_T A}
&=\left(\frac{729}{739}\right)^k
\longrightarrow0 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently no positive absolute \(c\), independent of \(n,d\) and the roofs, can satisfy the registered threshold-cap square-root comparison.

In contrast with this certificate failure, the same original bodies admit, by accepted fact \(\texttt{d4a3be98ad8645b7}\), one point \(\mathbf y^\star\in T\) for which
\[
\tau_{R_C}(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac{3W}{40\,2^n},
\qquad
h_S((\mathbf0,\mathbf y^\star))\ge\frac{3W}{80\,2^n}.
\]
Here \(\tau_{R_C}\) has exactly the accepted fact's normalization: the infimum of the integral of \(R_C\) over proper closed continuous halfspaces containing its argument. The actual depth \(h_S\) uses every containing closed ambient halfspace, and the point is selected before all such halfspaces.

## Proof

Write \(p=1/676\) and \(\alpha=675/676=1-p\). These are scalar parameters, distinct from the canonical mixed-integer points and densities.

The minimum of the affine function \((676/675)y_j\) and the constant function one is concave and continuous on \(T\). It takes values in \([0,1]\). Its hypograph description, or directly concavity of each roof, shows that the inequalities defining \(C\) specify a convex set. Continuity makes this set closed, and the bound on the roofs makes it bounded. It contains \([0,1]^n\times T=[0,1]^{n+d}\), so it has nonempty interior. Thus \(C\) is a convex body. Each coordinate interval \([-\ell_i(\mathbf y),1+\ell_i(\mathbf y)]\) is preserved by \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\) and lies in \([-1,2]\), proving the stated reflection and support properties. Also \(C_{\mathbf0}=T\).

For any \(j\), all six roofs in its group have floor zero when \(0\le y_j<\alpha\) and floor one when \(\alpha\le y_j\le1\). At a continuous point \(\mathbf y\), a coordinate with floor zero has exactly the integer sites \(0,1\), and a coordinate with floor one has exactly the integer sites \(-1,0,1,2\). The sites whose negatives are also present are respectively \(\{0\}\) and \(\{-1,0,1\}\). All coordinate choices are independent within the defining product section of \(C\). Therefore the full pointwise counts are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(\mathbf y)
&=\prod_{j=1}^k\left(1+63\,\mathbf1_{[\alpha,1]}(y_j)\right),\\
R_C(\mathbf y)
&=\prod_{j=1}^k\left(1+728\,\mathbf1_{[\alpha,1]}(y_j)\right),\\
\sqrt{R_C(\mathbf y)}
&=\prod_{j=1}^k\left(1+26\,\mathbf1_{[\alpha,1]}(y_j)\right),\\
N_C(\mathbf y)&=2^{6k}A(\mathbf y)
\qquad(\mathbf y\in T).
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficients are \(2^6-1=63\), \(3^6-1=728\), and \(3^3-1=26\). These identities include the threshold faces \(y_j=\alpha\), the plateau where the roofs equal the integer one, and all faces of \(T\). In particular, an integer-valued roof is counted with its closed endpoints.

The interval \([\alpha,1]\) has length \(p\). Fubini's theorem now gives \(\int_T A=(1+63p)^k=(739/676)^k\). Integrating the exact identity for \(N_C\) gives the claimed full original mass \(W\).

We next classify every threshold cap. If any \(t_i>1\), the cap is empty. Otherwise, put \(u_j=\max_{1\le r\le6}t_{6(j-1)+r}\), so \(0\le u_j\le1\). For every \(u\in[0,1]\), the condition \(\min\{1,y_j/\alpha\}\ge u\) is exactly \(y_j\ge\alpha u\), including \(u=0\) and \(u=1\). Hence
\[
K_{\mathbf t}=\prod_{j=1}^k[\alpha u_j,1],
\qquad
|K_{\mathbf t}|=\prod_{j=1}^k v_j,
\qquad
v_j=1-\alpha u_j\in[p,1].
\]
Conversely, every vector \((v_1,\ldots,v_k)\in[p,1]^k\) occurs: set the six thresholds in group \(j\) equal to \((1-v_j)/\alpha\). Thus this parametrization covers all positive-volume threshold caps, and every nonempty threshold cap has positive volume.

Each interval \([\alpha u_j,1]\) contains the full high interval \([\alpha,1]\). The exact square-root count and Fubini's theorem therefore yield
\[
\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}\sqrt{R_C}
=\prod_{j=1}^k(v_j+26p),
\qquad
\frac{\left(\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}\sqrt{R_C}\right)^2}{|K_{\mathbf t}|}
=\prod_{j=1}^k\frac{(v_j+26p)^2}{v_j}.
\]
For every \(v\in[p,1]\), the identity \(676p^2=p\) gives the exact scalar calculation
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(v+26p)^2}{v}-\frac{729}{676}
&=v+52p+\frac{p}{v}-(1+53p)\\
&=\frac{(v-1)(v-p)}{v}
\le0 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Equality holds at both endpoints \(v=p\) and \(v=1\). Multiplying these bounds proves that the cap score is at most \((729/676)^k\) for every threshold vector. The zero threshold vector gives \(K_{\mathbf0}=T\) and has all \(v_j=1\), so this upper bound is attained. In fact every choice \(v_j\in\{p,1\}\) attains it.

Dividing this exact maximum by the full original \(\int_T A=W/2^n\) gives \((729/739)^k\). Its base lies strictly between zero and one, so it tends to zero. For any proposed \(c>0\), a sufficiently large integer \(k\) consequently violates the registered comparison. For each \(k\), this calculation remains entirely in that body's original continuous dimension \(d=k\) and uses its complete original integer-fiber counts; there is no auxiliary density, lifted support, or discarded mass.

Finally, accepted fact \(\texttt{d4a3be98ad8645b7}\) is used only for the stated depth comparison. We proved that \(C\) is a convex body, has every required coordinate reflection, lies in \([-1,2]^n\times\mathbb R^d\), has \(C_{\mathbf0}=T\), and has positive original mass \(W\). Its \(R_C,N_C,W,\tau_{R_C}\) and \(h_S\) are exactly the canonical objects used here. Thus all hypotheses of that fact's mixed-integer conclusion hold, and its same-point reflected-depth and actual-depth bounds apply as written. This establishes the claimed distinction between the failed cap certificate and the actual depth of these bodies.
