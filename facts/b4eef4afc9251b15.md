## Statement

Lemma (the minimal rank-four cube Cayley lift is complete-core dominated). Let \(d\geq1\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a \(d\)-dimensional convex body of volume

\[
 a:=\operatorname{vol}_d(K)>0,
\]

and let \(u_\varepsilon\in\mathbb R^d\) be arbitrary vectors indexed by

\[
 \mathcal E:=\{0,1\}^3.
\]

Define

\[
 C_\square:=
 \operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal E}
 \bigl(\{2\varepsilon\}\times(K+u_\varepsilon)\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(z\in\mathbb R^3\), put

\[
 \Lambda_z:=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^{\mathcal E}:
 \sum_{\varepsilon}\lambda_\varepsilon=1,
 \quad
 2\sum_{\varepsilon}\lambda_\varepsilon\varepsilon=z
 \right\},
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
 T_z:=
 \left\{
 \sum_{\varepsilon}\lambda_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon:
 \lambda\in\Lambda_z
 \right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Then \(C_\square\) is a full-dimensional convex body and its exact positive integer-fiber support and exact fibers are

\[
 \boxed{
 \{z\in\mathbb Z^3:\operatorname{vol}_d((C_\square)_z)>0\}
 =\{0,1,2\}^3,
 \qquad
 (C_\square)_z=K+T_z.}
\tag{4}
\]

If at most one coordinate of \(z\in\{0,1,2\}^3\) is odd, then \(T_z\) is a singleton and

\[
 \boxed{a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d((C_\square)_z)=a.}
\tag{5}
\]

Use the half-strength canonical product reservoir of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66. Its good parity classes are exactly the parity-zero class and the three classes whose parity vector has one nonzero coordinate. More explicitly,

\[
 r_{\{v,w\}}=
 \begin{cases}
 a/32,
 &v\ne w,\quad v,w\in\{0,2\}^3,\\[1mm]
 a/16,
 &v\ne w,\quad v\equiv w\pmod2,
 \quad |v\bmod2|=1,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]

where \(|v\bmod2|\) is the number of odd coordinates. Consequently its actual mass is

\[
 \boxed{Q(C_\square)=\frac{7a}{8}
 +3\frac{3a}{8}=2a.}
\tag{7}
\]

Let

\[
 G(C_\square):=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_\square).
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{G(C_\square)\geq8a=4Q(C_\square).}
\tag{8}
\]

With the remaining nonnegative capacities in their accepted normalizations, put

\[
 \mathcal A(C_\square):=
 R+G(C_\square)+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C_\square)
 +M_{\mathrm{off}}(C_\square)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C_\square)
 +2^3h_{\max}(C_\square).
\tag{9}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(C_\square)\geq G(C_\square)
 \geq4Q(C_\square)>\frac{Q(C_\square)}{100}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Thus no body in (1) enters the canonical hard branch. This conclusion is independent of the translations \(u_\varepsilon\), including translations chosen to make the continuous centroid field maximally nonaffine.

For that last calibration, specialize to \(d=8\), \(K=B_2^8\), fix \(L>0\), index the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^8\) by \(\mathcal E\), and put

\[
 u_\varepsilon:=L\mathbf e_\varepsilon.
\tag{11}
\]

The eight vertex-fiber centroids are

\[
 c_{2\varepsilon}=L\mathbf e_\varepsilon,
\tag{12}
\]

and their intrinsic continuous residual rank is exactly four:

\[
 \boxed{
 \min_{s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^8\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{c_{2\varepsilon}-s(2\varepsilon):
 \varepsilon\in\mathcal E\}=4.}
\tag{13}
\]

Moreover, consider any finite occurrence-density system on all eight vertex fibers with the following purely local properties. For every \(\varepsilon\), let \(g_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\geq0\) be finitely many densities on \((C_\square)_{2\varepsilon}\), each of positive mass \(\eta_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\) and barycenter \(x_{\sigma,\varepsilon}\). Put

\[
 F_\varepsilon:=\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma,\varepsilon},
 \qquad
 0<\int F_\varepsilon<\frac a2,
 \qquad
 B_\varepsilon:=\frac12-F_\varepsilon\geq0,
\tag{14}
\]

and let \(\bar q_\varepsilon\) be the barycenter of \(B_\varepsilon\). There do not exist an affine map \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^8\) and a linear subspace \(W\subseteq\mathbb R^8\) with \(\dim W\leq3\) such that simultaneously

\[
 x_{\sigma,\varepsilon}-s(2\varepsilon)\in W
 \quad\text{for every occurrence},
 \qquad
 \bar q_\varepsilon-s(2\varepsilon)\in W
 \quad(\varepsilon\in\mathcal E).
\tag{15}
\]

This last assertion audits the desired chart obstruction at the exact local first-moment interface. It does not assert that an occurrence system in (14) is the canonical transverse atlas: equation (10) proves that no such system can occur under the complete canonical hard hypotheses for this family.

## Proof

Group an arbitrary convex combination in (1) by its vertex label. Its base coordinate and continuous coordinate have the form

\[
 \left(
 2\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon\varepsilon,
 \sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon k_\varepsilon
 +\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon
 \right),
 \qquad
 \lambda\in\mathbb R_+^{\mathcal E},
 \quad
 \sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon=1,
 \quad
 k_\varepsilon\in K.
\tag{16}
\]

Convexity of \(K\) puts \(\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon k_\varepsilon\) in \(K\). Conversely, for any \(k\in K\) and any \(\lambda\), take \(k_\varepsilon=k\) on the positive support of \(\lambda\). Therefore the global representation is exact:

\[
 C_\square=
 \left\{
 \left(2\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon\varepsilon,
 k+\sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon\right):
 \lambda_\varepsilon\geq0,\
 \sum_\varepsilon\lambda_\varepsilon=1,\ k\in K
 \right\}.
\tag{17}
\]

The base projection is \([0,2]^3\). For every \(z\in[0,2]^3\), the product probability distribution on \(\mathcal E\) with coordinate means \(z_i/2\) belongs to \(\Lambda_z\), so \(\Lambda_z\ne\varnothing\). Equation (17) then shows that the fiber contains a translate of \(K\), and hence has positive \(d\)-volume. Outside the base cube the fiber is empty. Taking integer points proves the support in (4), while taking the fiber in (17) proves its exact formula.

Suppose first that every coordinate of \(z\) is even. If \(z_i=0\), every \(\varepsilon\) in the support of a member of \(\Lambda_z\) has \(\varepsilon_i=0\); if \(z_i=2\), all such \(\varepsilon\) have \(\varepsilon_i=1\). Thus \(\Lambda_z\) is a singleton. If exactly one coordinate, say \(i\), is odd, the other two coordinates again fix their two bits. Only two cube vertices remain, and the equation \(2\sum\lambda_\varepsilon\varepsilon_i=1\) forces their weights to be \(1/2,1/2\). Thus \(\Lambda_z\), and therefore \(T_z\), is again a singleton. This proves (5). Three noncoplanar base differences and \(d\) independent vertical differences inside one copy of \(K\) prove that \(C_\square\) has affine dimension \(d+3\); compactness and convexity are immediate from (1).

We compute the reservoir before using any optimizer or atlas. A parity vector \(\xi\in\{0,1\}^3\) with \(k\) odd coordinates has exactly \(2^{3-k}\) representatives in \(\{0,1,2\}^3\). For \(k=0\) these are the eight cube vertices, and for \(k=1\) they are four edge midpoints. Equations (4)-(5) give mass \(a\) at each such point. Hence these four parity classes are good in the definition of accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66: the largest atom is \(a\), while one quarter of the class mass is \(2a\) for \(k=0\) and \(a\) for \(k=1\).

For \(k=2\), a parity class has two positive atoms. Its larger atom has mass at least one half of the class mass, which is strictly larger than one quarter, so the class is bad. For \(k=3\), the class is a positive singleton and is also bad. Thus the list of good classes is exact, even though the six face-center fiber volumes and the central fiber volume may depend on the translations.

In a good class of \(M\) equal atoms of mass \(a\), accepted fact 036a276c54ec5e66 assigns every unordered pair the weight

\[
 \frac{a^2}{4Ma}=\frac{a}{4M},
\]

so the class contributes

\[
 \binom{M}{2}\frac{a}{4M}=\frac{M-1}{8}a.
\tag{18}
\]

Taking \(M=8\) once and \(M=4\) three times proves (6)-(7).

We now exhibit one complete-core profile. Let

\[
 E_0:=\{2\varepsilon:\varepsilon\in\mathcal E\},
 \qquad
 t_z:=a\quad(z\in E_0),
 \qquad
 m:=0.
\tag{19}
\]

The barycentric-representation polytope \(\Lambda_m(E_0)\) is a singleton. Indeed, all coordinates of all points in \(E_0\) are nonnegative, so a convex combination equal to zero puts weight one on the vertex zero. For full selected mass, \(\mathsf M_a((C_\square)_z)\) is the singleton consisting of the fiber centroid. Hence the complete metronoid core at \(m=0\) is nonempty.

Every proper closed base halfspace containing \(m=0\) contains the selected base atom at zero. It therefore contains selected mass at least

\[
 a=2^{-3}\sum_{z\in E_0}t_z.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus (19) is an admissible profile in the exact definition of \(G\) from accepted fact 3d1f63abf09b2abb, of total mass \(8a\). This proves (8). Every term in (9) is nonnegative, so \(\mathcal A(C_\square)\geq G(C_\square)\). Combining this with \(Q(C_\square)=2a\) proves (10). This is the fail-fast point: no downstream capacity or optimizer value is needed to exclude the hard branch.

It remains to audit the rank-four specialization. The unit ball has centroid zero, so (12) follows from the exact vertex fibers. The eight graph points

\[
 (2\varepsilon,L\mathbf e_\varepsilon)
 \quad(\varepsilon\in\mathcal E)
\tag{21}
\]

are affinely independent: the continuous part of any affine dependence is \(L\sum_\varepsilon\alpha_\varepsilon\mathbf e_\varepsilon=0\), which forces every coefficient \(\alpha_\varepsilon\) to vanish. Their affine hull therefore has dimension seven.

For any affine \(s:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^8\), if the residuals in (13) span a space of dimension \(r\), then all graph points (21) lie in an affine space of dimension at most \(3+r\). Hence \(7\leq3+r\), so \(r\geq4\). Conversely, interpolate the four centroid values at the base points \(0,(2,0,0),(0,2,0),(0,0,2)\) by one affine map. The four corresponding residuals vanish, and the remaining four residuals span a space of dimension at most four. Therefore \(r\leq4\), proving (13). This also shows minimality: on any base-spanning family of at most seven points in \(\mathbb R^3\), one affine map can interpolate four affinely independent bases, leaving at most three residual vectors.

Finally fix an occurrence-density system as in (14). Let

\[
 R_\varepsilon:=\int F_\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 b_\varepsilon:=\int B_\varepsilon
 =\frac a2-R_\varepsilon>0.
\]

The defining barycenter identities and \(F_\varepsilon+B_\varepsilon=1/2\) give

\[
 \frac a2c_{2\varepsilon}
 =\sum_\sigma
 \eta_{\sigma,\varepsilon}x_{\sigma,\varepsilon}
 +b_\varepsilon\bar q_\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \sum_\sigma\eta_{\sigma,\varepsilon}+b_\varepsilon
 =\frac a2.
\tag{22}
\]

If (15) held, subtracting \((a/2)s(2\varepsilon)\) from (22) would express \((a/2)(c_{2\varepsilon}-s(2\varepsilon))\) as a linear combination of vectors in \(W\). Therefore

\[
 c_{2\varepsilon}-s(2\varepsilon)\in W
 \quad(\varepsilon\in\mathcal E),
\]

contradicting the exact residual rank four in (13). This proves the local chart obstruction and all assertions.
