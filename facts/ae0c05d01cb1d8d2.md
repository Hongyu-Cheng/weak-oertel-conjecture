## Statement

Let $\xi=(z,1)$ for $z\in\mathbb R^n$ and let each labeled original cap have one nonzero continuous normal $u_i$ and margin $u_i\cdot x+c_i\cdot\xi$. Each group $I_g$ has $d+1$ such normals spanning $\mathbb R^d$ with a strictly positive dependence normalized to sum one. There are unique $Q_g\in\mathbb R^{d\times(n+1)}$ and $f_g\in\mathbb R^{n+1}$ such that $c_i=f_g-Q_g^{\mathsf T}u_i$ for $i\in I_g$. Its caps cover the whole continuous plane at $z$ exactly when $F_g(z)=f_g\cdot\xi\ge0$. Its equal-margin point is $q_g(z)=Q_g\xi$.

Given nearby prescribed $Q'_g,f'_g$, all their nearby original-cap realizations are exactly the solutions of
$$
(Q'_g-Q'_{g_0})^{\mathsf T}u'_i=f'_g-f'_{g_0}
\quad (g\ne g_0,\ i\in I_g),
$$
using one reference group $g_0$ per original and one coefficient vector $c'_i=f'_{g_0}-(Q'_{g_0})^{\mathsf T}u'_i$. The first-order condition is $M_i\dot u_i=b_i$, with $M_i$ stacking $(Q_g-Q_{g_0})^{\mathsf T}$ and $b_i$ stacking $\dot f_g-\dot f_{g_0}-(\dot Q_g-\dot Q_{g_0})^{\mathsf T}u_i$. This is equivalent to $b_i\perp\ker M_i^{\mathsf T}$, with $d-\operatorname{rank}M_i$ free normal components. Exact compatibility along a constant-rank neighborhood gives a nearby solution of the same regularity as the prescribed data. Full row rank gives exact compatibility for every sufficiently small prescribed change.

If all shared originals are fixed and a group has $t\ge1$ shared caps, the allowed sections satisfy $(u_i-u_{i_0})^{\mathsf T}Q'_g=c_{i_0}-c_i$ for the $t-1$ other shared caps, and $f'_g=c_{i_0}+(Q'_g)^{\mathsf T}u_{i_0}$. This gives $d+1-t$ freedoms per section column, with independent nearby private normals. If there is no shared cap, both $Q'_g$ and $f'_g$ are free. If there is at most one shared cap per group, any nearby sections admit a realization with the same assigned cover of a finite set of bases, by allowing a common nonnegative offset increase in the original margins.

## Proof

For each group, the square matrix with rows $(u_i^{\mathsf T},-1)$ is invertible. A null vector $(v,t)$ would have $u_i\cdot v=t$ for all $i$. The positive sum-one dependence gives $t=0$, and spanning gives $v=0$. Solving in each base-affine coefficient gives the representation. The weighted margin sum is $F_g(z)$, so a nonnegative value proves coverage. A negative value makes every cap miss $q_g(z)$. At a zero, the equal-margin point is a common boundary point. The construction uses the displayed raw margins and can change under individual positive scalings.

Subtract the equations for two occurrences to obtain the stated shared system. Solving it and defining $c'_i$ gives all the old incidence relations exactly. The group matrices stay invertible and their normalized dependences stay positive for small changes, by continuity of the unique solution for the normalized dependence. Differentiate the shared equations to get $M_i\dot u_i=b_i$ and
$$
\dot c_i=\dot f_{g_0}-\dot Q_{g_0}^{\mathsf T}u_i-Q_{g_0}^{\mathsf T}\dot u_i.
$$
The cokernel criterion and freedom count are the range and nullity identities. A specified unit-normal gauge adds $u_i\cdot\dot u_i=0$ to the first-order equations. The linearized criterion alone does not ensure that a prescribed path solves the exact equations. For exact consistent data of constant rank, choose a nonzero pivot minor at the old data, fix the other normal coordinates at their old values and solve the independent rows. The other rows hold by consistency and constant rank, and the solution depends on the data with the stated regularity. Full row rank persists and makes the right side arbitrary. In particular one original shared between two groups has an $(n+1)\times d$ matrix, so full freedom for prescribed $(Q,f)$ requires $n+1\le d$.

For the fixed shared originals eliminate $f'_g$ using the cap $i_0$. The resulting $t-1$ row differences are independent by affine independence of all the circuit normals, which follows from the same augmented matrix. Thus the number of freedoms is the dimension claimed. Set each private $c'_i=f'_g-(Q'_g)^{\mathsf T}u'_i$. In the case of at most one shared original per group there is no restriction on the prescribed section. For a finite list of originally covered assignments $(g,p)$, set
$$
\kappa=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{-F'_g(p):(g,p)\text{ assigned}\}\bigr).
$$
Add $\kappa$ to the constant term of every original margin once. This adds $\kappa$ to every $F'_g$ and changes no section or continuous normal. It preserves the exact shared equations and is small with the prescribed perturbation. At originally strict assigned inequalities it is unnecessary locally. At a boundary assignment the necessary one-sided tangent condition is $\dot F_g(p)\ge0$, sufficient if strict, or if $F_g(p)$ follows an affine path with nonnegative slope. A feasible differentiable two-sided path there needs zero derivative. The repair can be merely Lipschitz and enlarges the caps.

For an all-integer cover, fixed $f_g$ preserve every old projected sign. Another sufficient hypothesis is that $\{z:F_g(z)\le0\text{ for all }g\}$ is bounded, nonempty and integer-free. Its recession cone is trivial, hence the maximum of the homogeneous left sides is positive on the unit sphere with a uniform lower bound. This bound persists under small coefficient changes and confines all new common nonpositive points to one fixed ball. Any sequence of newly feasible integer points then has a constant subsequence, yielding an old feasible integer point by coefficient continuity.

If an inclusion-minimal original cover with $N=h(d+1)-1$ caps is covered by $s\le h$ selected groups of size at most $d+1$, every original occurs. For sizes $k_g$ and occurrence counts $m_i$,
$$
1=(h-s)(d+1)+\sum_g(d+1-k_g)+\sum_i(m_i-1)
$$
by counting $\sum_g k_g=\sum_i m_i$. Thus $s=h$. When all groups have full rank, exactly one original occurs twice and all others occur once. If the single defect is a deficient group, the full-rank motion coordinates apply only to the other groups. This count assumes a selection from the original labels.
