## Statement

For a finite probability law \(q\) on \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\), define
\[
 S_{\mathbf j}:=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^r:
 \mathbf j+\mathbf v,\mathbf j-\mathbf v\in E\},
 \qquad r_{\mathbf j}:=|S_{\mathbf j}|,
\]
\[
 \mathcal K(q):=\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}q_{\mathbf j}^{\,3}r_{\mathbf j}^{\,2},
 \qquad
 \mathcal G(q):=\log\!\bigl(4^r\mathcal K(q)\bigr).
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_q\) be the probability law on triples
\((\mathbf J,\mathbf V,\mathbf U)\) given by
\[
 \mathcal R_q(\mathbf J=\mathbf j,\mathbf V=\mathbf v,
 \mathbf U=\mathbf u)
 =\frac{q_{\mathbf j}^{\,3}}{\mathcal K(q)}
 \quad(\mathbf v,\mathbf u\in S_{\mathbf j}).
\]
These are the reflected-displacement objects used in the variance mechanism under examination.
Also define the actual ambient halfspace depth and its second moment by
\[
 \tau_q(\mathbf j):=
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^r\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
 \mathbf j\in H}}q(E\cap H),
 \qquad
 M_2(q):=\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}q_{\mathbf j}\tau_q(\mathbf j)^2.
\]

For every integer \(m\ge2\), put
\[
 D_m=
 \bigl(\{0\}\times\{0,1\}^m\bigr)
 \cup
 \bigl(\{1\}\times\{0,\ldots,m^2\}^m\bigr)
 \subseteq\mathbb Z^{m+1}.
\]
There is a probability law \(p_m\) on \(D_m\), obtained from one affine tilt
\(\boldsymbol\ell_m\in\mathbb R^{m+1}\), such that
\[
 D_m=\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1},
 \qquad
 p_m(\mathbf j)=
 \frac{e^{\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf j}}
 {\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_m}e^{\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf x}},
\]
and
\[
 4^{m+1}\mathcal K(p_m)\longrightarrow8,
 \qquad
 \mathcal G(p_m)\longrightarrow\log8,
\]
while
\[
 \frac1m\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal R_{p_m}}
 \bigl(\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf V\bigr)
 \longrightarrow\frac29.
\]
Consequently, no absolute finite constant \(C\) can satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal R_q}
 (\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf V)
 \le C\bigl(1+\mathcal G(q)\bigr)
\]
for every complete convex log-affine lattice law \(q\).
Here \(\boldsymbol\ell\) denotes the single affine tilt defining \(q\).

Nevertheless, every \(p_m\) satisfies
\[
 4^{m+1}M_2(p_m)\ge\frac{e^{-4}}4
\]
by the heterogeneous exposed-join conclusion of accepted fact
6d7276756d672c31. Thus this sequence satisfies H2 with a uniform positive
constant and is not an H2 counterexample.

## Proof

Put
\[
 a_m:=\frac1m,
 \qquad t_m:=e^{-a_m}.
\]
For \(L\ge0\), define the one-dimensional truncated geometric law
\[
 u_{L,m}(j):=\frac{t_m^j}{Z_{L,m}},
 \qquad
 Z_{L,m}:=\sum_{j=0}^L t_m^j,
 \qquad 0\le j\le L.
\]
Set
\[
 q_m^{(0)}:=u_{1,m}^{\otimes m},
 \qquad
 q_m^{(1)}:=u_{m^2,m}^{\otimes m}.
\]
For later use, write
\[
 d_{L,j}:=\min(j,L-j),
 \qquad
 k_{L,m}:=\sum_{j=0}^L
 u_{L,m}(j)^3(2d_{L,j}+1)^2.
\]
The reflected displacement set at \(j\in\{0,\ldots,L\}\) is exactly
\(\{-d_{L,j},\ldots,d_{L,j}\}\). Product factorization therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal K(q_m^{(0)})=k_{1,m}^m,
 \qquad
 \mathcal K(q_m^{(1)})=k_{m^2,m}^m.
\]
Define the two normalized layer energies
\[
 A_{0,m}:=4^m k_{1,m}^m,
 \qquad
 A_{1,m}:=4^m k_{m^2,m}^m,
\]
and then put
\[
 \lambda_m:=\left(\frac{A_{0,m}}{A_{1,m}}\right)^{1/3},
 \qquad
 \alpha_{0,m}:=\frac1{1+\lambda_m},
 \qquad
 \alpha_{1,m}:=\frac{\lambda_m}{1+\lambda_m}.
\]

We first verify the support and the single common tilt. Let
\(P_m:=\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\). Its first coordinate lies in
\([0,1]\). Hence every lattice point of \(P_m\) has first coordinate \(0\)
or \(1\). A point of a convex combination with first coordinate \(0\) can
use only points of the lower layer, and one with first coordinate \(1\) can
use only points of the upper layer. The corresponding horizontal sections
are respectively
\[
 [0,1]^m
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 [0,m^2]^m.
\]
Their lattice points are precisely the two displayed layers of \(D_m\).
Thus
\[
 P_m\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1}=D_m,
\]
which proves convex completeness.

Let
\[
 B_{0,m}:=Z_{1,m}^m,
 \qquad B_{1,m}:=Z_{m^2,m}^m,
\]
and choose \(s_m\in\mathbb R\) by
\[
 e^{s_m}=\lambda_m\frac{B_{0,m}}{B_{1,m}}.
\]
On \(D_m\), use the single affine tilt
\[
 \boldsymbol\ell_m=(s_m,-a_m,\ldots,-a_m).
\]
The total unnormalized mass of layer \(0\) is \(B_{0,m}\), whereas that of
layer \(1\) is
\(e^{s_m}B_{1,m}=\lambda_mB_{0,m}\). Consequently the normalized common
log-affine law is exactly
\[
 p_m(h,\mathbf x)=
 \alpha_{h,m}q_m^{(h)}(\mathbf x),
 \qquad h\in\{0,1\}.
\]
In particular, the layer masses have not been selected independently of the
common affine weight.

We next compute both layer energies. For the two-point interval,
\(d_{1,0}=d_{1,1}=0\), so
\[
 k_{1,m}=\frac{1+t_m^3}{(1+t_m)^3}
\]
and
\[
 4k_{1,m}
 =1+3\left(\frac{1-t_m}{1+t_m}\right)^2
 =1+3\tanh^2\!\left(\frac1{2m}\right).
\]
It follows that
\[
 0\le\log A_{0,m}
 =m\log\!\left(1+3\tanh^2\!\left(\frac1{2m}\right)\right)
 \le\frac{3}{4m},
\]
and hence
\[
 A_{0,m}\longrightarrow1.
\]

For the long interval, set
\[
 L:=m^2,
 \qquad z_m:=t_m^3=e^{-3/m},
 \qquad M:=\lfloor L/2\rfloor.
\]
Introduce
\[
 D_L(z):=\sum_{j=0}^Lz^j(2d_{L,j}+1)^2
\]
and its infinite counterpart
\[
 D_\infty(z):=\sum_{j\ge0}z^j(2j+1)^2
 =\frac{1+6z+z^2}{(1-z)^3}.
\]
The summands agree for \(0\le j\le M\), and every remaining finite summand
is no larger than the corresponding infinite summand. Therefore
\[
 0\le D_\infty(z_m)-D_L(z_m)
 \le\sum_{j>M}z_m^j(2j+1)^2.
\]
For every fixed nonnegative integer \(d\),
\[
 \sum_{j>M}j^d e^{-3j/m}
 =O\!\left(m^{2d+1}e^{-3m/2}\right).
\]
Indeed, write \(j=M+1+r\), use
\((M+1+r)^d\le2^d((M+1)^d+r^d)\), and note that for \(m\ge3\),
\[
 1-e^{-3/m}\ge\frac1m,
 \qquad
 \sum_{r\ge0}r^dz_m^r\le\frac{d!}{(1-z_m)^{d+1}},
 \qquad
 z_m^{M+1}\le e^{-3m/2}.
\]
The first of these inequalities follows from
\(1-e^{-x}\ge x/(1+x)\) with \(x=3/m\); the second follows by bounding
\(r^d\) by the corresponding rising factorial and summing its generating
function.
Thus
\[
 D_\infty(z_m)-D_L(z_m)
 =O\!\left(m^5e^{-3m/2}\right).
\]
Since
\[
 D_\infty(z_m)\sim\frac8{27}m^3,
\]
we obtain
\[
 \frac{D_L(z_m)}{D_\infty(z_m)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 k_{m^2,m}
 =\frac{D_L(z_m)}{Z_{m^2,m}^3}
 =(1-t_m)^3
 \frac{D_L(z_m)}{(1-t_m^{m^2+1})^3}.
\]
Because \(t_m^{m^2+1}=e^{-m-1/m}\to0\), the preceding ratio and the exact
identity
\[
 (1-t)^3D_\infty(t^3)
 =\frac{1+6t^3+t^6}{(1+t+t^2)^3}
\]
give
\[
 k_{m^2,m}\longrightarrow\frac8{27},
 \qquad
 A_{1,m}^{1/m}=4k_{m^2,m}\longrightarrow\frac{32}{27}.
\]
The exact interval bound in accepted fact 6d7276756d672c31 also gives
\(A_{1,m}\ge(32/27)^m\) for every \(m\ge2\). Together with the bound on
\(A_{0,m}\), this yields
\[
 \frac1m\log\lambda_m
 \longrightarrow-\frac13\log\frac{32}{27},
 \qquad
 \lambda_m\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \alpha_{0,m}\longrightarrow1.
\]

We now compute the reflection energy of the full two-layer law. If
\((h,\mathbf x)\in D_m\) and
\((v_0,\mathbf v)\in S_{(h,\mathbf x)}\), then both
\(h+v_0\) and \(h-v_0\) belong to \(\{0,1\}\), which forces \(v_0=0\).
Thus all reflected displacements remain inside one layer, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal K(p_m)
 &=\alpha_{0,m}^3\mathcal K(q_m^{(0)})
   +\alpha_{1,m}^3\mathcal K(q_m^{(1)}),\\
 4^{m+1}\mathcal K(p_m)
 &=4\bigl(\alpha_{0,m}^3A_{0,m}
   +\alpha_{1,m}^3A_{1,m}\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The definition \(\lambda_m^3=A_{0,m}/A_{1,m}\) gives the exact balance
\[
 \alpha_{0,m}^3A_{0,m}
 =\alpha_{1,m}^3A_{1,m}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 4^{m+1}\mathcal K(p_m)
 =8\alpha_{0,m}^3A_{0,m}\longrightarrow8,
\]
and hence \(\mathcal G(p_m)\to\log8\).

It remains to calculate the variance and justify the long-interval limit.
Under \(\mathcal R_{p_m}\), the probability that the center lies in layer
\(h\) is
\[
 \frac{\alpha_{h,m}^3\mathcal K(q_m^{(h)})}
 {\mathcal K(p_m)}.
\]
The exact energy balance above makes both layer probabilities equal to
\(1/2\). Conditional on a layer, the reflected triple law factors over the
\(m\) coordinates. In layer \(0\), every reflected displacement is zero.
In layer \(1\), the coordinates of \(\mathbf V\) are independent copies of
the \(V\)-marginal for \(u_{m^2,m}\), and each has mean zero by reflection
symmetry.

Let
\[
 \sigma_m^2:=
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal R_{u_{m^2,m}}}(a_mV).
\]
The first coordinate of every reflected displacement is zero, so
\(\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf V=-a_m\sum_{i=1}^mV_i\). The two conditional
means are zero. Independence and the equal layer probabilities therefore give
the exact identity
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal R_{p_m}}
 (\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf V)
 =\frac m2\sigma_m^2.
\]

To evaluate \(\sigma_m^2\), define
\[
 N_L(z):=\sum_{j=0}^L
 z^j(2d_{L,j}+1)^2d_{L,j}(d_{L,j}+1)
\]
and
\[
 N_\infty(z):=\sum_{j\ge0}
 z^j(2j+1)^2j(j+1)
 =\frac{6z(3+10z+3z^2)}{(1-z)^5}.
\]
Conditional on \(J=j\), the displacement \(V\) is uniform on
\(\{-d_{L,j},\ldots,d_{L,j}\}\), whose variance is
\(d_{L,j}(d_{L,j}+1)/3\). Hence
\[
 \sigma_m^2=\frac{a_m^2}{3}
 \frac{N_L(z_m)}{D_L(z_m)}.
\]
As for the denominator, termwise comparison gives
\[
 0\le N_\infty(z_m)-N_L(z_m)
 \le\sum_{j>M}z_m^j(2j+1)^2j(j+1)
 =O\!\left(m^9e^{-3m/2}\right).
\]
Since
\[
 N_\infty(z_m)\sim\frac{32}{81}m^5,
\]
the finite truncation has vanishing relative effect:
\[
 \frac{N_L(z_m)}{N_\infty(z_m)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
It follows that \(\sigma_m^2\) has the same limit as
\[
 \frac{a_m^2}{3}\frac{N_\infty(z_m)}{D_\infty(z_m)}
 =
 \frac{2a_m^2z_m(3+10z_m+3z_m^2)}
 {(1-z_m)^2(1+6z_m+z_m^2)}.
\]
Because \(z_m\to1\) and \(a_m/(1-z_m)\to1/3\), this expression tends to
\(4/9\). Thus
\[
 \sigma_m^2\longrightarrow\frac49
\]
and the exact mixture identity proves
\[
 \frac1m\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal R_{p_m}}
 (\boldsymbol\ell_m\cdot\mathbf V)
 \longrightarrow\frac12\cdot\frac49=\frac29.
\]

The left side of the proposed universal estimate therefore grows linearly in
\(m\), whereas \(1+\mathcal G(p_m)\to1+\log8\). No finite absolute \(C\) can
make that estimate valid.

Finally, view \(D_m\) as the heterogeneous join with exposed label set
\(\{0,1\}=[0,1]\cap\mathbb Z\). Its two conditional supports are Cartesian
products of finite integer intervals, and their conditional laws are
log-affine. The exposed-join theorem in accepted fact 6d7276756d672c31
therefore applies and gives
\[
 4^{m+1}M_2(p_m)\ge e^{-4}/4
\]
for every \(m\). This proves explicitly that the sequence does not violate
H2. Since the construction only refutes an auxiliary variance estimate and
has uniformly positive normalized actual-depth moment, it also supplies no
counterexample to the locked weak Oertel conjecture.
