## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, $n\ge1$, $d\ge0$. Take arbitrary integer generators $v_i\in\mathbb Z^n$ and integer lengths $k_i\ge0$ for $1\le i\le m$. For a linearly independent index set $I$ put $V_I=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{v_i:i\in I\}$, $L_I=V_I\cap\mathbb Z^n$, $m(I)=[L_I:\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Zv_i]$, and let $\pi_I$ be the base quotient by $V_I$ with quotient lattice $\mathbb Z^n/L_I$. The continuous coordinates are fixed. With the empty index term interpreted as $\mu_{n,d}(C)$,
$$
\mu_{n,d}\left(C+\sum_i[0,k_i](v_i,0)\right)
=\sum_{I\ {\rm independent}} m(I)\,\mu_{n-|I|,d}(\pi_I C)\prod_{i\in I} k_i.
$$
All indices equal one for a unimodular partial basis; individually primitive generators may give larger indices.

## Proof

Each saturated $L_I$ gives a free quotient lattice, so the primitive extrusion identity applies in every successive quotient. Add segments one at a time, applying the total-mass identity to the current body. The newly created term projects all earlier segments and multiplies by $k_i$ times the divisibility of the new projected vector in the current quotient lattice. A zero projected vector has no such term. Recursing gives exactly one term per independent subset, with coefficient the product of successive divisibilities in its projection chain.

For an independent $I$ and an additional vector $v$ outside $V_I$, let $c$ be the divisibility of its class in $\mathbb Z^n/L_I$. The rank-one saturation of that class is $L_{I\cup\{v\}}/L_I$, so $[L_{I\cup\{v\}}:L_I+\mathbb Zv]=c$. Since $v$ is independent of $V_I$, the natural map
$$
L_I/\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Zv_i\ \longrightarrow\
(L_I+\mathbb Zv)/\left(\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb Zv_i+\mathbb Zv\right)
$$
is an isomorphism. Index multiplication gives $m(I\cup\{v\})=c\,m(I)$ and hence the formula in every order. For $C=\{0\}$, $n=2,d=0$, $v_1=(1,0),v_2=(1,2)$, it gives $1+k_1+k_2+2k_1k_2$. At unit lengths the extra point besides the four vertices is $(1,1)$. The same arithmetic coefficients arise for the full-dimensional $C=[-1/10,1/10]^2$, since its own lattice count and the two rank-one quotient counts are one.
