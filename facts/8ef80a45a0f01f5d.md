## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, \(A=(a_{ri})\in\mathbb R_{>0}^{2\times n}\), \(R\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2}\), and \(\theta\in\mathbb R_{\le0}^{n}\). Define
\[
D=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{n}:Az\le R\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{z\in D}e^{\theta\cdot z},\qquad
p_j=e^{\theta\cdot j}/Z\quad(j\in D).
\]
For \(j\in D\), let its normalized, closed-halfspace depth be
\[
\tau_p(j)=\inf_{u\in\mathbb R^n}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in D\colon u\cdot(z-j)\ge0}}p_z.
\]
This is also the infimum over all closed halfspaces containing \(j\), since such a halfspace contains the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through \(j\). In particular, this is actual original-law halfspace depth, not exponential capacity. The oriented reflected count, including zero displacement, is \(k_j=\#\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v,j-v\in D\}\), and its original-law reflected Hellinger mass is \(\mathsf H_j=k_jp_j\).

All logarithms are natural. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
K&=8192,&
C_{\mathrm{slab}}&=\frac{2\log64}{1-\sqrt{7/8}},&
L_*&=(8/27)^{3/2},\\
\delta&=\log(8L_*),&
B&=32+\log(48K^4),\\
C_0&=\log16+9C_{\mathrm{slab}}+64+2B,&
C_1&=K\sqrt2\,B+28\sqrt{13},\\
\kappa_*&=\exp\left(-C_0-\frac{C_1^2}{4\delta}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(C_{\mathrm{slab}}\) is exactly the constant in accepted fact `4d7d60d01a9ce8e5`. The constants satisfy \(\delta>0\) and \(0<\kappa_*<1\). For every stipulated datum,
\[
\mathbb E_{j\sim p}\tau_p(j)^3
=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3
\ge \kappa_*8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
Only the one-coordinate geometric reflection-second-moment inequality in `609407d6db81b18b` and the shared coordinate selection theorem `9ec030107c9689ad` are used in addition to the all-real-row slab theorem.

## Proof

The set \(D\) contains zero and is finite because its first positive resource row bounds every coordinate. Thus \(Z>0\). If some \(R_r=0\), positivity forces \(D=\{0\}\), and the depth and moment are one. This proves (1) in that case. Assume both capacities are positive.

First derive the new quartic coordinate inputs. To distinguish these objects from the square-weighted \(F,Q_\rho\) in accepted fact `609407d6db81b18b`, use
\[
G(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}(2m+1)^3z^m
=\frac{1+23z+23z^2+z^3}{(1-z)^4}\quad(0\le z<1),\qquad
Q^{(3)}_s(m)=\frac{(2m+1)^3e^{-4sm}}{G(e^{-4s})}
\quad(s>0).
\tag{2}
\]
The generating-function identity follows by expanding \((2m+1)^3=8m^3+12m^2+6m+1\) and differentiating the geometric series. Write \(\mu_3(s)=\mathbb E_{Q^{(3)}_s}m\), and put \(h=\log3\). We will use
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-e^{-s})^4G(e^{-4s})&\ge L_*&& (s>0),\\
\mu_3(s)&\le8/s,\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q^{(3)}_s}m^2\le13/s^2&& (s>0),\\
\mu_3(s)&\ge1/(20s)&& (0<s\le h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
For the first inequality let \(\rho=e^{-s}\) and \(P_\rho(m)=(1-\rho)\rho^m\). Accepted fact `609407d6db81b18b`, with this exact geometric law, proves
\(\sum_m(2m+1)^2P_\rho(m)^3\ge8/27\).
Set \(X(m)=(2m+1)P_\rho(m)\). Jensen's inequality for \(x\mapsto x^{3/2}\), applied under \(P_\rho\) to \(X^2\), gives
\[
(1-\rho)^4G(\rho^4)=\mathbb E_{P_\rho}X^3
\ge(\mathbb E_{P_\rho}X^2)^{3/2}\ge L_*.
\]
This uses only the predecessor's one-coordinate inequality, not its capacity or simplex conclusion.

For the remaining estimates put \(z=e^{-4s}\). The generating function \(G(zt)/G(z)\) factors as
\[
\frac{1+23zt+23z^2t^2+z^3t^3}{1+23z+23z^2+z^3}
 \left(\frac{1-z}{1-zt}\right)^4.
\]
Thus a coordinate with law \(Q^{(3)}_s\) is \(Y+W\), where \(Y\) is a sum of four independent geometric variables of parameter \(z\), and the independent \(W\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) has masses proportional to \(1,23z,23z^2,z^3\). Hence
\[
\mathbb EY=\frac4{e^{4s}-1}\le\frac1s,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}Y=\frac{4z}{(1-z)^2}\le\frac1{4s^2},\qquad
\mathbb EY^2\le\frac5{4s^2}.
\]
The variance inequality uses \(z/(1-z)^2=[4\sinh^2(2s)]^{-1}\) and \(\sinh(2s)\ge2s\). Also \(\mathbb EW\le72e^{-4s}\) and \(\mathbb EW^2\le124e^{-4s}\). The maxima of \(s e^{-4s}\) and \(s^2e^{-4s}\) are \(1/(4e)\) and \(1/(4e^2)\), respectively. The elementary inequality \(e>8/3\) consequently gives \(\mathbb EW<7/s\) and \(\mathbb EW^2<5/s^2\). Therefore \(\mu_3(s)\le8/s\) and
\(\mathbb E(Y+W)^2\le2\mathbb EY^2+2\mathbb EW^2<13/s^2\).
Finally \(u/(e^u-1)\) decreases on \((0,\infty)\), so for \(s\le h\),
\[
s\mu_3(s)\ge\frac{4s}{e^{4s}-1}
\ge\frac{4\log3}{80}>\frac1{20}.
\]
Here \(\log3>1\) follows from \(e<3\). This proves (3).

We next choose shifted rates while allowing zero multipliers, zero coordinates of \(\theta\), and cutoff ties. Define \(\psi_3(s)=\tfrac14\log G(e^{-4s})\) and \(\phi_3(s)=\max\{0,\psi_3(s)-\psi_3(h)\}\) for \(s>0\), with \(\phi_3(0)=+\infty\). Differentiating the convergent positive exponential series gives
\(\psi_3'(s)=-\mu_3(s)\) and \(\psi_3''(s)=4\operatorname{Var}_{Q^{(3)}_s}(m)\ge0\).
Thus \(\phi_3\) is convex, is zero for \(s\ge h\), diverges at zero, and \(\mu_3\) is nonincreasing. Minimize on \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2\)
\[
\mathcal F_3(\lambda)=\lambda\cdot R+
\sum_{i=1}^n\phi_3\left(\sum_{r=1}^2\lambda_ra_{ri}-\theta_i\right).
\tag{4}
\]
A positive first multiplier gives a finite value. A finite sublevel is bounded because \(\mathcal F_3\ge\lambda\cdot R\) and both capacities are positive. A minimizing subsequence converges in the closed orthant. Divergence at zero excludes a zero coordinate argument at its limit; continuity at the remaining positive arguments proves attainment. At a minimizer put
\[
t_i=\sum_r\lambda_ra_{ri},\qquad
s_i=t_i-\theta_i>0,\qquad
\mu_i=\mu_3(s_i),\qquad \Lambda=\lambda\cdot R.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \(0\le t_i\le s_i\) and \(|\theta_i|\le s_i\). A zero multiplier vector is allowed; the positivity of \(s_i\) still holds.

There is a vector \(x\) satisfying
\[
\begin{gathered}
x_i=\mu_i\ (s_i<h),\qquad x_i=0\ (s_i>h),\qquad
0\le x_i\le\mu_i\ (s_i=h),\\
Ax\le R,\qquad \lambda_r(R_r-a_r\cdot x)=0\quad(r=1,2).
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
Here is a direct justification. Let \(\mathcal X\) be the displayed box with its untied coordinates fixed, let \(\mathcal G=\{R-Ax:x\in\mathcal X\}\), and let \(\mathcal Q\) be the closed cone whose coordinate \(r\) is zero if \(\lambda_r>0\), and nonnegative if \(\lambda_r=0\). If these sets were disjoint, a nearest pair \(g_0,u_0\) would exist at positive distance, because \(\mathcal G\) is compact and \(\mathcal Q\) is closed. Put \(v=u_0-g_0\ne0\). Distance minimization along segments gives \(v\cdot g\le v\cdot g_0\) for every \(g\in\mathcal G\) and \(v\cdot u\ge v\cdot u_0\) for every \(u\in\mathcal Q\). Taking \(u=0,2u_0\) yields \(v\cdot u_0=0\), and hence \(\max_{g\in\mathcal G}v\cdot g\le-\|v\|^2<0\). The cone inequality also implies \(v_r\ge0\) for each zero multiplier, so \(v\) is a feasible direction at \(\lambda\). The directional derivative of (4) in direction \(v\) is exactly \(\max_{x\in\mathcal X}v\cdot(R-Ax)\). For a cutoff tie its contribution is \(\max\{0,-\mu_i\sum_rv_ra_{ri}\}\), and elsewhere it is the ordinary derivative. All arguments are positive, so these derivatives are within the domain. The resulting strictly negative derivative contradicts minimality, proving (6).

Choose an extreme point of the compact nonempty polytope (6). At most two tied coordinates are strictly between zero and \(\mu_i\): otherwise a nonzero perturbation on three such coordinates annihilating both resource rows would preserve every constraint for small perturbations of either sign. Set \(I_0=\{i:x_i=\mu_i\}\) and \(I_* =\{i:0<x_i<\mu_i\}\). Thus
\[
\begin{gathered}
|I_*|\le2,\qquad \Lambda=\sum_i t_ix_i,\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_0}a_{ri}\mu_i\le R_r,\\
s_i\le h\ (i\in I_0),\qquad s_i\ge h\ (i\notin I_0),\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_*}t_ix_i\le16.
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
The equality follows from complementary slackness in (6). The last bound uses \(t_i\le s_i\) and \(s_i\mu_i\le8\) from (3).

Apply accepted fact `9ec030107c9689ad` with its dimension \(n\), row count \(k=2\), selection parameter \(\kappa=K\), and weights \(w_{ri}=a_{ri}\mu_i\) on \(I_0\) and zero outside \(I_0\). Delete the zero columns from its selected set. The resulting \(I\subseteq I_0\), with \(U_r=\sum_{i\in I}a_{ri}\mu_i\), satisfies
\[
|I|\le K\sqrt{2n}+2,\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_0\setminus I}(a_{ri}\mu_i)^2\le U_r^2/K^2\quad(r=1,2).
\tag{8}
\]
If \(I_0\) is empty take \(I=\varnothing\) directly. If \(I_0\) is nonempty, every row on \(I_0\) is strictly positive; the tail inequality rules out \(I=\varnothing\), and both \(U_r\) are positive.

For \(i\in I_0\), put \(s_i'=Ks_i\) on \(I\) and \(s_i'=s_i\) otherwise. Under a product law \(Q\), let \(J_i\) have law \(Q^{(3)}_{s_i'}\) on \(I_0\), and let \(J_i=0\) deterministically outside \(I_0\). Write \(\nu_i=\mathbb E_QJ_i\). Monotonicity and (3) give
\[
\nu_i\le\mu_i\quad(i\in I_0),\qquad
\nu_i\le(160/K)\mu_i\le\mu_i/4\quad(i\in I),\qquad
\mathbb E_QJ_i^2\le13/(s_i')^2\quad(i\in I_0).
\tag{9}
\]
For any nonnegative integer vector \(j\) and any real row \(b\), write
\(\sigma_b(j)^2=\sum_i b_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3\).
This is precisely the variance of \(b\cdot V\) for independent uniform integer displacements \(V_i\in\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\), matching the normalization in `4d7d60d01a9ce8e5`.

If \(I_0\ne\varnothing\), equations (7) and (9) yield
\(\mathbb E_Q(a_r\cdot J)\le R_r-3U_r/4\).
Moreover both \(\operatorname{Var}_Q(a_r\cdot J)\) and \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_{a_r}(J)^2\) are at most
\[
5200\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{(a_{ri}\mu_i)^2}{(s_i'/s_i)^2}
\le\frac{10400U_r^2}{K^2}\le\frac{U_r^2}{48^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed \(\mu_i\ge1/(20s_i)\) on \(I_0\), giving the coefficient \(13\cdot20^2=5200\) for the coordinate second moment. For the displacement variance use \(J_i(J_i+1)\le2J_i^2\); its coefficient is smaller. The selected sum of squared nonnegative weights is at most \(U_r^2\), and the unselected sum is controlled by (8). The final inequality is the exact comparison \(10400\cdot48^2\le8192^2\).

The dual score \(T_Q=t\cdot J\) and the original-score displacement width obey
\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le\Lambda-\mathbb E_QT_Q&\le16+8|I|,\\
\operatorname{Var}_QT_Q&\le13n,\\
\mathbb E_Q\sigma_\theta(J)^2&\le13n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
For the first line, the exact difference is
\(\sum_{i\in I_*}t_ix_i+\sum_{i\in I}t_i(\mu_i-\nu_i)\).
Every uninflated active coordinate has mean \(\mu_i\), and every other dropped coordinate has \(x_i=0\). Equations (3), (7), and \(t_i\le s_i\) give the stated bound. Independence, (9), and \(t_i\le s_i\le s_i'\) give the variance bound. Similarly \(|\theta_i|\le s_i\le s_i'\) and \(J_i(J_i+1)\le2J_i^2\) give
\(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_\theta(J)^2\le(26/3)n\le13n\).
These also cover a zero multiplier vector and zero original-score coordinates.

Let \(E\) be the event on which, for both resource rows,
\[
\begin{gathered}
|a_r\cdot J-\mathbb E_Q(a_r\cdot J)|
\le4\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q(a_r\cdot J)},\qquad
\sigma_{a_r}(J)\le4\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q\sigma_{a_r}(J)^2},\\
T_Q\ge\mathbb E_QT_Q-4\sqrt{13n},\qquad
\sigma_\theta(J)\le4\sqrt{13n}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{12}
\]
The two score conditions occur once each. Chebyshev's inequality for the two resource sums and the dual score, and Markov's inequality for the three squared widths, give a union bound over six exceptional events, each of probability at most \(1/16\). Zero-variance and zero-expectation conditions hold identically. Thus \(Q(E)\ge5/8\ge1/2\). Equations (10) and the mean margin imply, on \(E\),
\[
a_r\cdot J+\sigma_{a_r}(J)\le R_r\quad(r=1,2),\qquad
T_Q\ge\Lambda-D_n,\qquad
\sigma_\theta(J)\le4\sqrt{13n},
\quad D_n=16+8|I|+4\sqrt{13n}.
\tag{13}
\]
For each resource, the allowed rise of its sum plus its width is at most \(8U_r/48=U_r/6\), smaller than the reserved \(3U_r/4\). If \(I_0\) is empty, \(J=0\) and the resource assertions hold identically; (11) still gives the dual assertion. In all cases \(E\) consists of roots in the original \(D\).

Now compare actual halfspaces at a fixed \(j\in E\). Let \(V_i\) be independent uniform integers in \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Apply accepted fact `4d7d60d01a9ce8e5` with its row count \(k=3\) and real rows \(a_1,a_2,\theta\). It gives
\[
\Pr\bigl(|a_1\cdot V|\le\sigma_{a_1}(j),
|a_2\cdot V|\le\sigma_{a_2}(j),
|\theta\cdot V|\le\sigma_\theta(j)\bigr)
\ge e^{-3C_{\mathrm{slab}}}.
\tag{14}
\]
This includes zero widths. Write \(\mathcal V_j\) for the integer displacement set in (14). Its size is at least \(e^{-3C_{\mathrm{slab}}}\prod_i(2j_i+1)\), it is invariant under \(v\mapsto-v\), and (13) ensures \(j\pm v\in D\) for every \(v\in\mathcal V_j\). For any \(u\), at least half of \(\mathcal V_j\) has \(u\cdot v\ge0\). Each corresponding original probability satisfies
\(p_{j+v}=p_je^{\theta\cdot v}\ge p_je^{-\sigma_\theta(j)}\).
The endpoints are distinct as \(v\) varies. Therefore every closed halfspace through \(j\) has original probability at least
\[
\tau_p(j)\ge\tfrac12e^{-3C_{\mathrm{slab}}-\sigma_\theta(j)}
 p_j\prod_i(2j_i+1).
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(k_j\le\prod_i(2j_i+1)\), this also gives the proposed comparison to \(\mathsf H_j=k_jp_j\). The stronger product bound in (15) is what will be summed, and the width is controlled by (13).

The original denominator is bounded without changing the law. Nonnegative resource multipliers give \(t\cdot z\le\Lambda\) for every \(z\in D\), so \(\theta=t-s\) yields
\[
Z\le e^\Lambda\sum_{z\in D}e^{-s\cdot z}
\le e^\Lambda\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}.
\tag{16}
\]
Put \(Z_Q=\prod_{i\in I_0}G(e^{-4s_i'})\), using one for an empty product. On the support of \(Q\), the exact quartic coordinate normalization is
\[
\prod_i(2j_i+1)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}
=Z_QQ(J=j)\exp\left(4\sum_{i\in I_0}(s_i'+\theta_i)j_i\right)
\ge Z_QQ(J=j)e^{4t\cdot j}.
\tag{17}
\]
The inequality uses \(s_i'\ge s_i\), \(s_i+\theta_i=t_i\), and \(j_i\ge0\); coordinates outside \(I_0\) equal zero. Thus the exponent is the original weight \(4\theta\cdot j\), appropriate to a third depth moment under the original root law.

Cube (15), multiply by the original root probability \(p_j\), and sum over \(E\subseteq D\). Use (13), (16), (17), and \(Q(E)\ge1/2\) to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3
&\ge\frac{e^{-9C_{\mathrm{slab}}-12\sqrt{13n}}}{8Z^4}
 \sum_{j\in E}\prod_i(2j_i+1)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}\\
&\ge\frac1{16}e^{-9C_{\mathrm{slab}}-12\sqrt{13n}-4D_n}
 Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4\\
&=\frac1{16}e^{-9C_{\mathrm{slab}}-64-32|I|-28\sqrt{13n}}
 Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The normalization throughout the first line is the actual original \(Z^4\), and the roots are weighted by the actual original \(p_j\). The event is finite because it lies in \(D\).

Every uninflated active coordinate contributes at least \(L_*\) to the product in (18), by (3). Every deterministic coordinate has \(s_i\ge h\), so it contributes at least \((1-e^{-h})^4=16/81>L_*\); the strict comparison follows by squaring and using \(256/6561>512/19683\). For every inflated coordinate,
\[
\frac{G(e^{-4Ks_i})}{G(e^{-4s_i})}\ge\frac1{48K^4}.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed the numerator polynomial \(1+23z+23z^2+z^3\) lies between 1 and 48 on \([0,1]\), and \(1-e^{-Kv}\le K(1-e^{-v})\) for the positive integer \(K\), by the geometric-sum identity. Consequently (18), (19), and (8) give
\[
\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3
\ge e^{-\log16-9C_{\mathrm{slab}}-64-B|I|-28\sqrt{13n}}L_*^n
\ge e^{-C_0-C_1\sqrt n}L_*^n.
\tag{20}
\]
All these steps include an empty random-coordinate set, cutoff ties, and inactive multipliers.

Finally \((8L_*)^2=32768/19683>1\), so \(\delta=\log(8L_*)>0\) and \(L_*^n=8^{-n}e^{\delta n}\). Completing the square gives
\(\delta n-C_1\sqrt n\ge-C_1^2/(4\delta)\).
Equation (20) is therefore at least \(\kappa_*8^{-n}\), proving (1) for every \(n\ge1\). This is a two-resource original-depth theorem and makes no claim to solve the unchanged locked mixed-integer problem.
