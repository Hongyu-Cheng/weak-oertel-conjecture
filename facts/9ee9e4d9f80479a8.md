## Statement

Use the original-weight logarithmic gaps, hitting mass, and closed lifted-cap convention of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Explicitly, for a finite positive support \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), a target \(\mathbf J\in D\), and original weights \(w\), put \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\). A barycentric support consists of a nonempty \(E\subseteq D\) and positive coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) with sum one and \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). Its logarithmic gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). For \(L\ge0\), the number \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) is the minimum original weight \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) of a subset \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every barycentric support of gap at most \(L\). The same value is obtained by using the affinely independent supports, as established in that accepted fact; this equivalence will also be checked directly for the present family.

For each integer \(k\ge1\), set \(N=2^{2k}\), \(n=2N\), and \(A=k\log2\). Let \(\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\) be the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^n\). Define the complete table on \(\mathbb Z^n\) by

\[
w_{\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
1,&\mathbf I=\mathbf0,\\
2^{3k},&\mathbf I=\mathbf e_i,\quad 1\le i\le N,\\
2^{-3k},&\mathbf I=-\mathbf e_i,\quad 1\le i\le N,\\
2^{-k},&\mathbf I=\pm\mathbf e_i,\quad N<i\le2N,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

This table is globally extendable by a finite-valued log-concave function agreeing with every prescribed positive and zero lattice value. Its positive support and convex hull are \(D=\{\mathbf0,\pm\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\pm\mathbf e_n\}\) and \(P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i|x_i|\le1\}\), with \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\). The target \(\mathbf J=\mathbf0\) is interior to \(P\), so its minimal face is the whole \(P\) and its intrinsic lattice is exactly \(\mathbb Z^n\).

On that whole face, let \(p^{\mathbf0}\) be the positive Gibbs law proportional to \(w_{\mathbf I}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\) with mean \(\mathbf0\). This law exists and is unique. With \(Z=1+2N+2N2^{-k}\), it and its centered energy are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
p^{\mathbf0}_{\mathbf0}&=Z^{-1},\qquad
p^{\mathbf0}_{\pm\mathbf e_i}=Z^{-1}
\quad(1\le i\le N),\\
p^{\mathbf0}_{\pm\mathbf e_i}&=2^{-k}Z^{-1}
\quad(N<i\le2N),\\
g_{\mathbf0}&:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p^{\mathbf0}_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}
-\phi_{\mathbf0}
=\frac{2NA2^{-k}}{Z}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(0<g_{\mathbf0}<A2^{-k}<1\), and \(g_{\mathbf0}\to0\). The original closed marginal depth and the exact original logarithmic hitting profile satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\mathbf0}
&:=\min_{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{\mathbf0\}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf I\ge0}}w_{\mathbf I}
=1+2^{-k}+2^k,\\
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
&=
\begin{cases}
1+2^{-k},&0\le L<A,\\
1+2^{-k}+2^k,&L\ge A.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently, for every \(C,c>0\) there is an integer \(k\) for which
\(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C(1+g_{\mathbf0});w)<c\tau_{\mathbf0}\).
All masses in this comparison are computed from the original table displayed above.

## Proof

The convex hull of the listed support is the crosspolytope \(P\). An integer point in \(P\) has sum of absolute integer coordinates at most one, so it is either \(\mathbf0\) or one of the listed \(\pm\mathbf e_i\). This proves completeness. The origin is an interior point, because \(P\) contains a neighborhood of the origin. In particular the minimal face of \(P\) containing the origin is \(P\), the affine span is \(\mathbb R^n\), and the whole intrinsic lattice is \(\mathbb Z^n\).

Define

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\exp\left(3A\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i
-A\sum_{i=N+1}^{2N}|x_i|\right),&x\in P,\\
0,&x\notin P.
\end{cases}
\]

The exponent is concave on \(\mathbb R^n\), being affine minus a positive multiple of a sum of absolute values. For two endpoints in \(P\), their segment lies in \(P\), and concavity of the exponent proves the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). If at least one endpoint is outside \(P\), the right side of that inequality is zero, so the inequality again holds. The function is finite-valued: on \(P\) its exponent is at most \(3A\). Evaluating at the listed integer points gives precisely the displayed positive weights, and completeness gives every required zero value outside them. Thus \(f\) is a global log-concave extension of the complete table.

The original maximum is \(w_{\max}=2^{3k}=e^{3A}\), and hence the normalized original potential values are

\[
\phi_{\mathbf0}=3A,\qquad
\phi_{\mathbf e_i}=0,\quad
\phi_{-\mathbf e_i}=6A\quad(1\le i\le N),\qquad
\phi_{\pm\mathbf e_i}=4A\quad(N<i\le2N).
\]

These are also the restrictions of the convex function
\(x\mapsto3A-3A\sum_{i=1}^N x_i+A\sum_{i=N+1}^{2N}|x_i|\) to \(D\). This checks convex-admissibility directly, without replacing the complete table by a selected subset.

Choose the tilt with \(\theta_i=-3A\) for \(1\le i\le N\) and \(\theta_i=0\) for \(N<i\le2N\). The resulting unnormalized Gibbs weights are one at the origin and at both endpoints of each of the first \(N\) pairs, and \(2^{-k}\) at both endpoints of every remaining pair. Their sum is exactly \(Z\). Opposite endpoints have equal probabilities, so the mean is \(\mathbf0\). Conversely, the \(i\)-th coordinate of the mean of any such Gibbs law is \(p_{\mathbf e_i}-p_{-\mathbf e_i}\), since no other support point has a nonzero \(i\)-th coordinate. A mean-zero Gibbs law must therefore satisfy \(w_{\mathbf e_i}e^{\theta_i}=w_{-\mathbf e_i}e^{-\theta_i}\) separately for every \(i\). This forces \(\theta_i=-3A\) in the first group and \(\theta_i=0\) in the second. The tilt and the positive centered law are consequently unique, on the complete original minimal face.

The two potential values in each first-group pair average to \(3A=\phi_{\mathbf0}\), whereas both values in a second-group pair are \(4A\). Substituting the just-computed probabilities gives \(g_{\mathbf0}=2NA2^{-k}/Z\). Since \(Z>2N\), this is strictly less than \(A2^{-k}\). For \(k\ge1\), one has \(k\le2^k\) and \(\log2<1\), so \(A2^{-k}<1\). Also \(k/2^k\to0\), proving the stated energy limit. This calculation uses the normalized original potential throughout; the tilt changes only the probability law used to evaluate its expectation.

Every closed base halfspace through the origin contains the origin and at least one endpoint of each opposite pair. A pair whose normal coordinate is zero contributes both endpoints, since the halfspace is closed. Therefore every such halfspace has original mass at least
\(1+N2^{-3k}+N2^{-k}=1+2^{-k}+2^k\).
Take a normal with \(b_i=-1\) for \(1\le i\le N\) and \(b_i=1\) for \(N<i\le2N\). The resulting halfspace contains exactly the less expensive endpoint of each first-group pair, exactly one endpoint of each second-group pair, and the origin. It attains that bound, proving the formula for \(\tau_{\mathbf0}\). Allowing closed halfspaces that merely contain the origin instead of having boundary through it gives the same minimum: for a fixed normal, moving the boundary to the origin only decreases the contained set.

We now check all barycentric supports. Extend any barycentric coefficient vector by zero to the whole \(D\). Its mean-zero equations give
\(\lambda_{\mathbf e_i}=\lambda_{-\mathbf e_i}\) for every \(i\). Thus every nonzero coordinate pair occurring in its positive support occurs as a complete opposite pair. If the support contains \(s\) such pairs and omits the origin, its affine span has dimension \(s\) and it has \(2s\) points. Affine independence then requires \(2s\le s+1\), and therefore \(s=1\). If it includes the origin and at least one such pair, its affine span still has dimension \(s\), but it has \(2s+1>s+1\) points and is affinely dependent. The only affinely independent barycentric supports are consequently the singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) and the opposite pairs, the latter with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\).

For an arbitrary barycentric coefficient vector, including dependent supports and those using the origin, the affine term in the original potential cancels at mean zero. Equivalently, substitute the potential values and the coordinate equations to obtain the exact gap identity

\[
G_\lambda
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf0}
=A\sum_{i=N+1}^{2N}
\bigl(\lambda_{\mathbf e_i}+\lambda_{-\mathbf e_i}\bigr).
\]

It follows that every gap lies in \([0,A]\). The singleton and every first-group opposite pair have gap zero; every second-group opposite pair has gap exactly \(A\).

Fix \(0\le L<A\), and let
\(F_k=\{\mathbf0\}\cup\{-\mathbf e_i:1\le i\le N\}\).
Any hitting set must include the origin to hit the singleton and must contain at least one endpoint of each first-group pair to hit their zero-gap supports. Those constraints concern disjoint sites, so its original mass is at least \(1+N2^{-3k}=1+2^{-k}\). The set \(F_k\) has exactly that mass and hits every support of gap at most \(L\), even among arbitrary dependent supports. Indeed, a support using the origin meets \(F_k\). A support omitting the origin and having gap below \(A\) must place some positive coefficient on the first group, by the gap identity and the normalization of its coefficients. The coordinate equations then make it contain a complete first-group opposite pair, whose negative endpoint belongs to \(F_k\). This proves the claimed hitting equality for the whole interval \(0\le L<A\).

If \(L\ge A\), every barycentric support is eligible. In addition to the previous requirements, each second-group pair must be hit. Their disjointness gives the lower bound \(1+N2^{-3k}+N2^{-k}\). It is attained by taking the origin, every first-group negative endpoint, and one endpoint from each second-group pair. Every support using the origin is hit, and every other barycentric support contains a complete opposite pair and is therefore hit. This proves the formula for \(L\ge A\), including the equality cutoff \(L=A\). It also directly verifies, for this family, agreement between the independent-support and arbitrary-support definitions from accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`.

There is an exact closed lifted-cap witness for \(F_k\) at every \(0\le L<A\). In the notation of the closed lifted-cap formula in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, take \(t=1\), \(b_i=-4A\) for \(1\le i\le N\), and \(b_i=0\) otherwise. The atom scores \(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf I+\phi_{\mathbf0}+L-\phi_{\mathbf I}\) are \(L\) at the origin, \(L+A\) at first-group negative endpoints, and \(L-A\) at all other endpoints. Thus the closed cap consists exactly of \(F_k\). At \(L=0\) it retains the equality atom at the origin, and every omitted atom has strictly negative score. At \(L=A\), the formerly omitted equality atoms enter, consistent with treating the upper endpoint separately. This supplies the lower lifted-cap interpretation without discarding any boundary atoms or changing any capacities.

Finally, let arbitrary constants \(C,c>0\) be given. Choose an integer \(k\ge1\) such that \(k\log2>2C\) and \(2^{-k}<c\). Because \(g_{\mathbf0}<1\), the cutoff satisfies \(C(1+g_{\mathbf0})<2C<A\). The established exact profile therefore gives

\[
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C(1+g_{\mathbf0});w)}{\tau_{\mathbf0}}
=\frac{1+2^{-k}}{1+2^{-k}+2^k}
<2^{-k}<c.
\]

For the strict middle inequality, multiply the denominator by \(2^{-k}\): the result is \(1+2^{-k}+2^{-2k}\), which is strictly larger than the numerator. This proves failure for every proposed pair of constants. Equivalently, deleting \(F_k\) removes less than \(c\tau_{\mathbf0}\) of original mass, but every remaining barycentric representation of the origin has gap exactly \(A\). Such representations exist, since every second-group opposite pair remains. Hence the deletion obstruction is an attained exact convex-roof cost, rather than failure of the complement to represent the target.

The matching linear-programming dual certificate is explicit. The affine function \(x\mapsto4A-4A\sum_{i=1}^N x_i\) equals \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\) at every retained site \(\mathbf I\in D\setminus F_k\), and its value at the origin is \(4A\). It is therefore an affine lower bound for the retained potential values with value \(4A\) at the target. A second-group pair gives a barycentric representation with mean potential \(4A\). These matching bounds prove that the retained convex roof at the origin is exactly \(4A\), and subtracting \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=3A\) gives exactly the gap \(A\).

The mechanism concerns the mismatch between original masses and the centered law used in the energy. The first group supplies many zero-gap points to the centered law, while its original cheaper endpoints have negligible total mass. The second group supplies most of the original marginal depth and has gap \(A\), but its centered probability is small. For the distinction from all-target selection, the largest original atom is \(w_{\max}=2^{3k}\). Since \(|D|=2n+1\), the total original mass \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\) is at most \((2n+1)w_{\max}\), and consequently \(2^n w_{\max}/W\ge2^n/(2n+1)\to\infty\). The family therefore does not obstruct an all-target criterion that includes the largest-atom term, and the registered counterexample asserts no disproof of the locked mixed-integer depth statement.
