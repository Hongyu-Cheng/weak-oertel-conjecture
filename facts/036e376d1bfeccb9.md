## Statement

Assume the setting and conclusions of accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42`. Fix \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) for which
\[
K:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf x)\in C\}
\]
has positive \(d\)-dimensional measure. For
\(\mathbf u=(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1}\), with
\(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^d\), and
\(\mathbf y\in K\), put
\[
H_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u)
:=\{(\mathbf z,\mathbf x):
\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z_0)
+\mathbf b^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)\geq0\},
\qquad
M_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u):=\mu(H_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u)).
\]
Thus these are ordinary closed halfspaces whose boundary contains
\((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf y)\). Let \(\mathbf y_0\) maximize
\[
g(\mathbf y):=h_S((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf y))
=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1}}M_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u)
\]
over \(K\), and write \(t:=g(\mathbf y_0)\). Then
\(\mathbf y_0\in\operatorname{int}K\) and \(t>0\).

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), define
\[
\mathcal B_\varepsilon
:=\left\{\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^d:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there is }\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^n
\text{ with }(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\in\mathbb S^{n+d-1},\\
M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\leq t+\varepsilon
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbf0\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\mathcal B_\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
In particular, for every \(\varepsilon,\delta>0\), there are
\(1\leq r\leq d+1\), unit normals
\(\mathbf u_i=(\mathbf a_i,\mathbf b_i)\), and numbers
\(\lambda_i>0\), \(\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1\), such that
\[
M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u_i)\leq t+\varepsilon
\quad(1\leq i\leq r),
\qquad
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\mathbf b_i\right\|_2<\delta.
\]

Moreover, the following exact alternative holds.

(A) For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the preceding caps and weights can be
chosen with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\mathbf b_i=\mathbf0.
\]

(B) If (A) fails, there is a sequence of nonvertical unit normals
\(\mathbf u_j=(\mathbf a_j,\mathbf b_j)\) such that
\[
t\leq M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u_j)\leq t+\frac1j,
\qquad
0<\|\mathbf b_j\|_2<\frac1j.
\]
After passage to a subsequence and reindexing,
\(\mathbf u_j\to(\mathbf a_\ast,\mathbf0)\) with
\(\|\mathbf a_\ast\|_2=1\). Thus the failure of arbitrarily near-active
exact ordinary balance forces near-active ordinary caps to converge to a
vertical normal. Alternative (A) gives exact finite balance, while (B)
gives arbitrarily accurate finite balance with one cap.

## Proof

Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` shows that \(\mu\) is a finite measure
supported on finitely many integer fibers, that boundary-through-point
ordinary halfspaces compute \(h_S\), and that \(h_S\) is upper
semicontinuous. Hence \(g\) is upper semicontinuous on the compact set
\(K\), so a maximizer \(\mathbf y_0\) exists.

We first record a uniform slab bound. Let
\[
Z:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:C_{\mathbf z}\neq\varnothing\},
\qquad
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}.
\]
The set \(Z\) is finite. Choose \(R<\infty\) so that every
\(C_{\mathbf z}\), \(\mathbf z\in Z\), is contained in a Euclidean
\(d\)-ball of radius \(R\). By slicing such a ball orthogonally to a unit
vector and applying Cavalieri's formula, there is a finite constant
\(L=L(d,R,|Z|)\) such that, for every unit
\(\mathbf e\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\), every interval \(I\subseteq\mathbb R\),
and every \(\mathbf z\in Z\),
\[
\mathcal H_d\{\mathbf x\in C_{\mathbf z}:
\mathbf e^\top\mathbf x\in I\}\leq \frac{L}{|Z|}|I|.
\]
For \(d=1\), this is just the fact that the length cut from an interval
by an interval of length \(|I|\) is at most \(|I|\). Fibers of
\(d\)-dimensional measure zero satisfy the inequality trivially. Summing
over \(Z\) gives the corresponding bound \(L|I|\) for mixed measure.

This slab bound has two consequences. First, for every unit
\(\mathbf u=(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\) and every
\(\mathbf y,\mathbf y'\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
|M_{\mathbf y'}(\mathbf u)-M_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u)|
\leq L\|\mathbf y'-\mathbf y\|_2. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), the two halfspaces induce exactly the
same subsets of every fiber. If \(\mathbf b\neq\mathbf0\), put
\(\mathbf e=\mathbf b/\|\mathbf b\|_2\). On any fixed integer fiber,
changing \(\mathbf y\) to \(\mathbf y'\) moves the scalar threshold in
the \(\mathbf e\)-direction by
\(\mathbf e^\top(\mathbf y'-\mathbf y)\). The symmetric difference is
therefore contained in a slab of width at most
\(\|\mathbf y'-\mathbf y\|_2\), and summing the slab bound proves (1).
Thus fixed-normal cap masses are uniformly continuous under motion
inside the chosen fiber, uniformly even as the normal approaches a
vertical one.

Second, every boundary point of \(K\) has mixed halfspace depth zero.
Fix \(\mathbf y\in\partial K\), put
\(\mathbf p=(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf y)\), and choose a unit supporting
normal \(\mathbf e\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\) of \(K\) at \(\mathbf y\), so
\[
\mathbf e^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)\leq0
\qquad(\mathbf x\in K).
\]
For \(\eta>0\), the point
\(\mathbf q_\eta=\mathbf p+\eta(\mathbf0,\mathbf e)\) is outside \(C\).
Let \(\mathbf c_\eta\) be a nearest point of \(C\) to
\(\mathbf q_\eta\), and set
\[
\mathbf u_\eta=(\mathbf a_\eta,\mathbf b_\eta)
:=\frac{\mathbf q_\eta-\mathbf c_\eta}
{\|\mathbf q_\eta-\mathbf c_\eta\|_2}.
\]
The nearest-point inequality gives
\(\mathbf u_\eta^\top\mathbf x
\leq\mathbf u_\eta^\top\mathbf c_\eta\) for every
\(\mathbf x\in C\), while
\(\mathbf u_\eta^\top\mathbf q_\eta>
\mathbf u_\eta^\top\mathbf c_\eta\). Since \(\mathbf p\in C\),
\[
0\leq
\Delta_\eta:=
\mathbf u_\eta^\top(\mathbf c_\eta-\mathbf p)
<
\mathbf u_\eta^\top(\mathbf q_\eta-\mathbf p)
=\eta\,\mathbf b_\eta^\top\mathbf e
\leq\eta\|\mathbf b_\eta\|_2. \tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(\mathbf b_\eta\neq\mathbf0\). Every point of
\(C\cap H_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u_\eta)\) satisfies
\[
0\leq
\mathbf u_\eta^\top((\mathbf z,\mathbf x)-\mathbf p)
\leq\Delta_\eta.
\]
On each integer fiber this is a slab, in the direction
\(\mathbf b_\eta/\|\mathbf b_\eta\|_2\), of width at most
\(\Delta_\eta/\|\mathbf b_\eta\|_2<\eta\) by (2). Hence
\[
M_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf u_\eta)\leq L\eta.
\]
Boundary reduction from the accepted fact now yields
\(h_S((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf y))=0\).

On the other hand, if \(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{int}K\), choose
\(\rho>0\) with \(B_d(\mathbf y,\rho)\subseteq K\). For a unit normal
\((\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\) with \(\mathbf b\neq\mathbf0\), the
\(\mathbf z_0\)-fiber of its boundary-through-\((\mathbf z_0,\mathbf y)\)
cap contains one half of this ball. If \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\), it
contains all of \(K\). Therefore
\[
M_{\mathbf y}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)
\geq\frac12\mathcal H_d(B_d(\mathbf0,\rho))>0
\]
for every unit normal. Thus \(g(\mathbf y)>0\) at every interior point
of \(K\). Since \(K\) has positive \(d\)-volume, it has a nonempty
interior. The maximum \(t\) is consequently positive, and no boundary
point can maximize \(g\). Hence
\(\mathbf y_0\in\operatorname{int}K\).

We next prove the closed-convex-hull assertion. Fix
\(\varepsilon>0\), and suppose for contradiction that
\(\mathbf0\notin
\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\mathcal B_\varepsilon\).
The latter set is a closed bounded convex subset of \(\mathbb R^d\).
Taking its nearest point to \(\mathbf0\) gives a unit vector
\(\mathbf v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\) and a number \(\gamma>0\) such that
\[
\mathbf v^\top\mathbf b\geq\gamma
\qquad(\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_\varepsilon). \tag{3}
\]
Choose \(\rho>0\) with \(B_d(\mathbf y_0,\rho)\subseteq K\), and for
small \(s>0\) put
\(\mathbf y_s=\mathbf y_0-s\mathbf v\). We take
\[
0<s<\min\{\rho/2,\varepsilon/(2L)\}. \tag{4}
\]

If \(\mathbf u=(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\) satisfies
\(M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u)\leq t+\varepsilon\), then
\(\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_\varepsilon\), so (3) holds. On the
\(\mathbf z_0\)-fiber, moving from \(\mathbf y_0\) to
\(\mathbf y_s\) changes the defining inequality from
\[
\mathbf b^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf y_0)\geq0
\quad\text{to}\quad
\mathbf b^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf y_0)
+s\mathbf b^\top\mathbf v\geq0.
\]
Thus the cap expands. Moreover, inside \(B_d(\mathbf y_0,\rho)\) the
added part contains the slab
\[
-s\gamma\leq
\frac{\mathbf b^\top(\mathbf x-\mathbf y_0)}
{\|\mathbf b\|_2}<0.
\]
Here (3) implies \(\mathbf b\neq\mathbf0\), and
\(\mathbf v^\top\mathbf b/\|\mathbf b\|_2\geq\gamma\) because
\(\|\mathbf b\|_2\leq1\). Since \(s\leq\rho/2\), the
\((d-1)\)-dimensional sections of this slab inside the ball have a
uniformly positive lower bound. Consequently there is a constant
\(c=c(d,\rho)>0\), independent of \(\mathbf u\) and \(s\), such that
\[
M_{\mathbf y_s}(\mathbf u)
\geq M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u)+cs\gamma
\geq t+cs\gamma. \tag{5}
\]
For \(d=1\), the added interval has length at least \(s\gamma\), so the
same conclusion holds.

If instead
\(M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u)>t+\varepsilon\), (1) and (4) give
\[
M_{\mathbf y_s}(\mathbf u)
>t+\varepsilon-Ls
>t+\varepsilon/2. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5) and (6), valid for every unit normal, imply
\[
g(\mathbf y_s)\geq
t+\min\{cs\gamma,\varepsilon/2\}>t.
\]
This contradicts the maximality of \(\mathbf y_0\). Therefore
\[
\mathbf0\in
\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\mathcal B_\varepsilon.
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, the only possible nonclosedness relevant to (7)
occurs at vertical normals. Indeed, if
\(\mathbf u_k=(\mathbf a_k,\mathbf b_k)\to
\mathbf u=(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\) with \(\mathbf b\neq\mathbf0\),
then on every positive-volume integer fiber the boundary of
\(H_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u)\) is a \((d-1)\)-dimensional affine
hyperplane and has \(d\)-measure zero. The cap indicators converge
away from that boundary, so dominated convergence on the finitely many
fibers gives
\[
M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u_k)
\longrightarrow M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u). \tag{8}
\]
Mixed measure on a vertical boundary hyperplane is allowed and is
exactly why no continuity at \(\mathbf b=\mathbf0\) is asserted.

Given \(\delta>0\), (7) supplies a point of
\(\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_\varepsilon\) of norm less than
\(\delta\). Any finite convex representation of a point in
\(\mathbb R^d\) can be reduced to at most \(d+1\) terms: if it has more
terms, the represented points are affinely dependent, and perturbing
the weights along a nonzero affine dependence until one weight becomes
zero preserves both their sum and the represented point. Repeating
proves the asserted \(d+1\)-term approximate balance.

It remains to prove the exact alternative. If, for some fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\), the vectors in
\(\mathcal B_\varepsilon\) are bounded away from \(\mathbf0\), then the
corresponding set of unit normals is compact. To see this, take any
convergent sequence of such normals. Its limiting continuous component
is still nonzero, and (8) shows that the limiting cap still has mass at
most \(t+\varepsilon\). Hence \(\mathcal B_\varepsilon\) is compact,
so its convex hull is compact. Equation (7) then yields
\(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{conv}\mathcal B_\varepsilon\), and the
preceding affine-dependence reduction gives an exact balance using at
most \(d+1\) caps. Consequently,
\[
\text{if no exact balance exists at tolerance }\varepsilon,
\quad
\inf_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal B_\varepsilon}\|\mathbf b\|_2=0.
\tag{9}
\]

If (A) holds, there is nothing more to prove. Otherwise choose
\(\varepsilon_0>0\) for which no exact balance exists. Since
\(\mathcal B_\eta\subseteq\mathcal B_{\varepsilon_0}\) whenever
\(0<\eta\leq\varepsilon_0\), no exact balance exists for any such
\(\eta\). Put
\(\eta_j=\min\{\varepsilon_0/2,1/j\}\). By (9), choose a unit normal
\(\mathbf u_j=(\mathbf a_j,\mathbf b_j)\) with
\[
M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u_j)\leq t+\eta_j,
\qquad
\|\mathbf b_j\|_2<1/j.
\]
Each \(\mathbf b_j\) is nonzero, because a zero continuous component
would itself be a one-term exact balance. Also
\(M_{\mathbf y_0}(\mathbf u_j)\geq t\) by the definition of \(t\).
Compactness of the unit sphere gives a convergent subsequence. After
reindexing it, the displayed bounds remain valid with \(1/j\), and its
limit is \((\mathbf a_\ast,\mathbf0)\) with
\(\|\mathbf a_\ast\|_2=1\). This is (B). Finally, in (B), choosing
\(j\) with \(1/j<\min\{\varepsilon,\delta\}\) gives the requested
arbitrarily accurate one-cap balance at tolerance \(\varepsilon\);
in (A), the balance is exact.
