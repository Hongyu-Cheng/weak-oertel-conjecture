## Statement

Take an attained local minimum of the normalized body-tied buffered maximum $\beta=Q/M$ over positive compatible families on a fixed complete base, at fixed positive scale. Alternatively take a local minimum of $\beta_\varepsilon-\tau\log\varepsilon$ with $\tau>0$ and interior scale. Assume the proper deepest-source and pure-base reserve conditions of the finite-cover theorem. Fix a cover from that theorem, including each group's actual mass-$Q$ caps, probabilities $\rho_{ij}$ and finite old contacts $Y_{ia}$, with $\delta_{ia}=\varepsilon f(Y_{ia})$. The local minimum is taken over actual families, in particular along all paths of the signed physical cone theorem. All cap normal copies have independent signed velocities, shared across their own group's contacts. In the interior-scale case both signs of $s=\dot{\log\varepsilon}$ are feasible.

There are nonnegative cap weights $\omega_{ij}$ summing to one, nonnegative contact forces $\kappa_{ia}$, and effective outside-face selectors $\theta^{\rm eff}_{ij}\in[0,1]$, all from one probability mixture of the conservative linear branches. Let $T_{ij}^{\rm eff}$ and $m_{ij}^{\rm eff}$ be the coareas and ambient section moments obtained with these selectors in the conservative branch formula, and $\kappa_i=\sum_a\kappa_{ia}$. For every positive-weight group they obey
$$
\omega_{ij}T_{ij}^{\rm eff}=\rho_{ij}\kappa_i,\qquad
\frac{m_{ij}^{\rm eff}}{T_{ij}^{\rm eff}}
=\frac{\sum_a\kappa_{ia}\bigl[Y_{ia}-\delta_{ia}(0,\nabla\Gamma(u_{ij}))\bigr]}{\kappa_i}.
$$
The same effective selectors define the body capture density $W_z(X)$ on the marked sampled boundaries: it sums the $\omega_{ij}$ times their body selectors, equal to one on strict full and inside-supporting fibers, equal to the old captured-boundary indicator on proper fibers, equal to $\theta_{ij}^{\rm eff}$ on outside supporting faces, and zero elsewhere. In particular $0\le W\le1$. For $e_{ia}=\kappa_{ia}\delta_{ia}$, $e_i=\sum_a e_{ia}$ and $\Delta=\sum_i e_i$, define $A$ by the following nonnegative density with respect to marked surface area $\sigma$:
$$
W_z(X)+\sum_{ia:p_{ia}=z}e_{ia}\left[\frac{P_a(X)^{-N}}{N E_a}+\frac1{dNV_z}\right]
+\frac1{2M}\sum_i e_i\sum_j\rho_{ij}[u_{ij}\cdot(X-g_z)]^2.
$$
The measures $A$ and $B_*\sigma$, where $B_*=\beta+(d+2)\Delta/(2dM)$, obey
$$
\int\Phi\,dA\le\int\Phi\,d(B_*\sigma)
$$
for every jointly continuous finite normal-dependent field convex in the full physical coordinates. Their normal marginals and conditional full physical first moments agree, and a coupling from $A$ to $B_*\sigma$ preserves the continuous normal and has conditional physical mean equal to its full ambient input point. At interior scale the same coefficients satisfy $\Delta=M\tau$. On the normalized compact class of the attainment theorem, at sufficiently small scales, $\Delta$ is bounded above and below by positive constants times $\varepsilon$ and the displayed terms beyond $W$ are uniformly $O(\varepsilon)$.

## Proof

Take the actual cover, contact continuation and conservative equalization. Let $D$ be total first body loss. At the fixed scale the normalized conservative improvement for group $i$, in mass units, is $\ell_i^0-\beta D$. At interior scale it is $\ell_i^0-\beta D+M\tau s$. Indeed $q_i(t)=Q-t\ell_i+o(t)$, $M(t)=M-tD+o(t)$, and the penalized objective has additional variation $-\tau st$. At assigned bases the exact cover bounds depth by the actual $q_i(t)$, and off the deepest bases the old strict gap persists. If all these conservative improvements were strictly positive then the actual objective would strictly decrease for small positive $t$ along the given family, contradicting the local minimum.

Let $\mathcal X$ be the finite disjoint union of the group/contact labels times the products of the outside selector spaces $\{\theta\in L^\infty:0\le\theta\le1\}$ on the supporting faces with surface measure. It is compact Hausdorff in the weak-star product topologies. Every branch at a fixed direction is continuous on $\mathcal X$: each selector pairs with an integrable speed, moment, or constant, and $T_{ij}\ge S_{ij}>0$ with uniform finite upper bound. The branch values here include the common $-\beta D$ and the optional $M\tau s$.

Fix finitely many physical fields and all signed cap normal and optional scale coordinates. Admissible directions in their coefficient space have nonnegative physical coefficients and arbitrary signed coordinates. The image of $\mathcal X$ under the branch coefficient map is compact. If its compact convex hull missed the negative polar of the admissible coefficient cone, strict separation from this closed polar cone would give an admissible direction with a uniform positive lower bound on every branch. Indeed a separating vector with finite supremum on the negative polar lies in the admissible cone by the bipolar identity, and its supremum on that polar is zero. This would strictly improve every group, which was excluded. Hence a finite probability mixture belongs to the polar. On the compact space of probabilities on $\mathcal X$, each physical field inequality and signed-coordinate equality is closed by branch continuity. Finite feasibility just proved gives one probability $\Lambda$ satisfying the entire class at once. Only the finite-dimensional restrictions require finite mixtures.

Write $B_i^\theta=\sum_j\rho_{ij}/T_{ij}$ and integrate over the indicated labels and selector tuple. Define
$$
\kappa_{ia}=\int_{(i,a,\theta)}\frac{1}{B_i^\theta}\,d\Lambda,\qquad
\omega_{ij}=\int_{(i,a,\theta),\,a}\frac{\rho_{ij}}{B_i^\theta T_{ij}}\,d\Lambda.
$$
The $\omega_{ij}$ sum to one. For $\omega_{ij}>0$ define $\theta_{ij}^{\rm eff}$ by averaging $\theta_{ij}$ with the same weights as in $\omega_{ij}$ and dividing by $\omega_{ij}$. The average defines a $[0,1]$ element of $L^\infty$ through its pairings against $L^1$. All cap weights of a positive-weight group are positive. Integrating the branch coarea with its cap weight gives $\omega_{ij}T_{ij}^{\rm eff}=\rho_{ij}\kappa_i$. The same averaging applies to the boundary and moment integrals because they are affine in the selectors. The coefficient of the independent signed $\nu_{ij}$ is
$$
\rho_{ij}\sum_a\kappa_{ia}[Y_{ia}-\delta_{ia}(0,\nabla\Gamma(u_{ij}))]-\omega_{ij}m_{ij}^{\rm eff}.
$$
It must vanish, giving the claimed mean. The coefficient of the signed scale direction is $M\tau-\Delta$, proving its identity where applicable.

On physical directions, substitution of the contact budget into the averaged branch yields $\int (dA/d\sigma-B_*)\Phi\,d\sigma\le0$. The terms come respectively from the same $B_j(v)$ and outside selectors, the own energy and volume derivatives, the pooled covariance derivative, and the two total-mass terms. The effective inside body selector is one and its section selector is zero throughout this construction. Both signs of $\Phi(u,w)=\psi(u)L(w)$ for continuous $\psi$ and affine $L$ give the normal and conditional first moment equalities. Taking $\Phi(u,w)=\psi_+(u)G(w)$ with $\psi_+\ge0$ continuous and $G$ finite convex gives the localized convex-order inequality. Apply 311757ca7529ae19 to the compact marked exposed-face relation of the old actual fibers. Both measures are absolutely continuous with respect to surface area and hence supported on its same Borel regular-normal graph. The resulting coupling has the stated full ambient row means. The actual-fiber hypothesis of bf891d8f55d6abf7 also holds, so the same boundary coupling has active anchored base rows.

In the compact normalized class, every selected cap has a uniform proper coarea lower bound and total coarea upper bound by the old own-ball floor, the covariance bounds and the uniform outer radius. The area of each convex fiber boundary is uniformly bounded under that outer radius, by surface area monotonicity for convex bodies, with the two endpoints in $d=1$. Thus $1/B_i^\theta$ is bounded above and away from zero uniformly. The old source floors and $f\le1$ bound $\delta_{ia}/\varepsilon$ above and away from zero, giving the asserted two-sided $\Delta$ estimate. The denominator, energy and volume floors and bounded covariance integrands bound the extra density terms uniformly by a constant times $\varepsilon$.
