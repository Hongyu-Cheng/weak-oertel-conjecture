## Statement

Put
\[
R:=2100,\qquad d:=10,\qquad
g(z):=|z_1|+|z_2|,\qquad
\mathbf 1:=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^{10},
\]
and define
\[
C^{\mathrm{cen}}
:=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{10}:
2|x_j|+g(z)\leq R\ (1\leq j\leq10)\},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
C^{\mathrm{kink}}
:=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{10}:
0\leq x_j,\ x_j+g(z)\leq R\ (1\leq j\leq10)\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both sets are compact full-dimensional convex bodies.  For either body \(B\),
write
\[
B_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in B\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{10}(B_z).
\]
Their complete positive lattice-fiber profiles coincide:
\[
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}
=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:g(z)\leq2099\},
\qquad
w_z=(2100-g(z))^{10}\quad(z\in D_+),
\tag{3}
\]
and \(w_z=0\) outside \(D_+\).  The total mass is
\[
\begin{aligned}
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z
&=2100^{10}
+4\sum_{k=1}^{2099}k(2100-k)^{10}\\
&=222904975974045919640891634431980350000.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
This profile satisfies
\[
w_z<\frac W4\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2),
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\sum_{z\in(a+L)\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z<\frac W2
\tag{6}
\]
for every coset \(a+L\) of every primitive rank-one sublattice
\(L\leq\mathbb Z^2\), and
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W2
\tag{7}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(0\).  Moreover,
\[
0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}D_+.
\tag{8}
\]

For \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z,\qquad
Q^\circ:=\sum_rW_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2.
\]
Then, with \(e\) denoting the base of the natural logarithm,
\[
Q^\circ>\frac1{16}W^2>
\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{9}
\]

Put
\[
\gamma:=\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^{10},\qquad
q:=(1-\gamma)^{1/10},\qquad
S_0:=2qR.
\]
For \(A,B\geq0\), define
\[
\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B)
:=A^{10}+B^{10}
-
\max_{\substack{0\leq s\leq A,\ 0\leq t\leq B\\s+t\leq S_0}}
(s^{10}+t^{10}),
\]
\[
\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B)
:=\min\{\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B),A^{10},B^{10}\}.
\]
With one representative from every nonzero antipodal pair
\(\{v,-v\}\subseteq\mathbb Z^2\), define
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)
:=
\gamma w_0+
\sum_{v/\pm}
\Psi_{10,S_0}\!\left(w_v^{1/10},w_{-v}^{1/10}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)<\frac W{64}.
\tag{11}
\]

For \(B\in\{C^{\mathrm{cen}},C^{\mathrm{kink}}\}\), \(z\in D_+\), and
\(x\in B_z\), put
\[
\delta_z^B(x)
:=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_{10}(B_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J\},
\]
\[
K_z^B(t):=\{x\in B_z:\delta_z^B(x)\geq t\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Let
\[
P:=\operatorname{conv}D_+
=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:g(z)\leq2099\},
\qquad
D(P):=P\cap\mathbb Z^2=D_+,
\tag{13}
\]
and let \(\mathcal R_P(0)\) be the family of distinct sets
\(D(P)\cap Q\), where \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) is a proper closed
halfspace whose boundary contains \(0\) and no point of
\(D(P)\setminus\{0\}\).  Set
\[
t_z:=\frac{w_z}{2}\qquad(z\in D(P)).
\tag{14}
\]
For every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing
\(0\),
\[
\sum_{z\in D(P)\cap Q}t_z\geq\frac W4.
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathcal R}t_z\geq\frac W4,
\qquad(\mathcal R\in\mathcal R_P(0)).
\]
Nevertheless,
\[
K_z^{C^{\mathrm{cen}}}(t_z)=\{0\}
\qquad(z\in D(P)),
\tag{16}
\]
so the constant map \(s(z)=0\) meets every centered core, whereas
\[
K_z^{C^{\mathrm{kink}}}(t_z)
=\left\{\frac{R-g(z)}2\,\mathbf1\right\}
\qquad(z\in D(P)),
\tag{17}
\]
and no affine map meets every kinked core.

The infeasibility in (17) has an exact three-fiber circuit.  Let
\(e_1=(1,0)\in\mathbb Z^2\) and
\(\varepsilon_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^{10}\), and put
\[
u_{-e_1}:=-\varepsilon_1,\qquad
u_0:=2\varepsilon_1,\qquad
u_{e_1}:=-\varepsilon_1.
\tag{18}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_z u_z=0,\qquad
\sum_z z_1u_z=0,\qquad
\sum_z z_2u_z=0,
\tag{19}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{z\in\{-e_1,0,e_1\}}
\min_{x\in K_z^{C^{\mathrm{kink}}}(t_z)}
\langle u_z,x\rangle
=1>0.
\tag{20}
\]
The scalar mass of its support is
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_{-e_1}+w_0+w_{e_1}
&=2100^{10}+2\cdot2099^{10}\\
&=4988112689911981111116939756858002
<\frac W{40000}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

For completeness, define the anchored value of a finite
\(E\subseteq D_+\) with \(0\in\operatorname{conv}E\), and of an affine map
\(s:\operatorname{aff}(E)\to\mathbb R^{10}\) satisfying
\(s(v)\in B_v\) for \(v\in E\), by
\[
\alpha_0^B(E,s)
:=
\inf_{\substack{Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(E)
\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\0\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{conv}(E)\cap\mathbb Z^2\cap Q}
\delta_z^B(s(z)),
\tag{22}
\]
when \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)>0\).  If
\(\dim\operatorname{aff}(E)=0\), necessarily \(E=\{0\}\), and define
\(\alpha_0^B(E,s):=\delta_0^B(s(0))\).  Put
\(\mathfrak A_0(B):=\sup_{E,s}\alpha_0^B(E,s)\).
For the kinked body, take
\[
a:=350,\qquad
E:=\{(\pm a,0),(0,\pm a)\},\qquad
s(z):=875\,\mathbf1.
\tag{23}
\]
This is an admissible anchored affine certificate and
\[
\alpha_0^{C^{\mathrm{kink}}}(E,s)
\geq
\frac{1750^{10}\bigl(2a(a+1)+2\bigr)}4
>
\frac W{64}.
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently
\[
\mathfrak A_0(C^{\mathrm{kink}})>\frac W{64}.
\tag{25}
\]

## Proof

The function \(g\) is convex.  Each constraint in (1) and (2) is therefore
convex, and both sets are closed.  Their constraints imply
\(g(z)\leq R\) and bound every \(x_j\), so both sets are compact.  All
constraints are strict at \((0,0)\) for (1), and at
\((0,(R/4)\mathbf1)\) for (2); hence both bodies have nonempty interior in
\(\mathbb R^{12}\).

At a real base point \(z\) with \(g(z)\leq R\), their fibers are
\[
(C^{\mathrm{cen}})_z
=\left[-\frac{R-g(z)}2,\frac{R-g(z)}2\right]^{10},
\qquad
(C^{\mathrm{kink}})_z=[0,R-g(z)]^{10}.
\tag{26}
\]
Both cubes have side length \(R-g(z)\).  If \(g(z)=R\), each fiber is a
singleton and has ten-dimensional volume zero; if \(g(z)>R\), it is empty.
Thus the entire shell \(g(z)=R\), including any part retained by a closed
halfspace, contributes zero mixed volume.  This proves (3), including the
zero-volume boundary shell.  The shell
\(\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:g(z)=k\}\) has \(4k\) points for \(k\geq1\), which
gives the finite sum in (4) and its displayed exact integer value.

The maximum fiber mass is \(R^{10}\), and
\[
W-4R^{10}
=222898304021654639240891634431980350000>0.
\tag{27}
\]
This proves (5).

Consider any affine lattice line \(a+\mathbb Zv\), \(v\neq0\), and choose
\(i\in\{1,2\}\) with \(v_i\neq0\).  Its \(i\)-th coordinates are distinct
integers.  Since
\[
w_z\leq (R-|z_i|)_+^{10},
\]
where \(r_+:=\max\{r,0\}\),
its total mass is at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
L
&:=R^{10}+2\sum_{j=1}^{R-1}(R-j)^{10}\\
&=636869506603755599191663828920818500.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The exact gap
\[
W-2L
=221631236960838408442508306774138713000>0
\tag{29}
\]
proves (6), even without primitivity.

The profile is centrally symmetric.  Write a proper closed halfspace
containing \(0\) as \(Q=\{z:\ell(z)\geq b\}\), where \(\ell\neq0\) and
\(b\leq0\).  Then \(Q_0:=\{\ell\geq0\}\subseteq Q\).  The closed
halfspace \(Q_0\) contains \(0\) and at least one point of every antipodal
pair \(\{z,-z\}\); if the pair lies on its boundary, it contains both.
Thus it retains at least \(W/2\), proving (7) with all closed-boundary mass.
The four vertices \((\pm2099,0),(0,\pm2099)\) belong to \(D_+\), while
\(D_+\subseteq\{g\leq2099\}\).  This proves (8) and the equality for \(P\)
in (13).

Cauchy--Schwarz over the four parity classes and the pointwise bound
\(w_z\leq R^{10}\) give
\[
\sum_rW_r^2\geq\frac{W^2}{4},
\qquad
\sum_z w_z^2\leq R^{10}W.
\tag{30}
\]
The exact comparison
\[
3W-16R^{10}
=668688240112572637322674903295941050000>0
\tag{31}
\]
gives \(R^{10}<3W/16\).  Hence
\[
Q^\circ
\geq\frac{W^2}{4}-R^{10}W
>\frac{W^2}{16}.
\]
Since \(e>0\), (9) follows.

We next prove (11) directly from (10).  The exact positive integer
\[
11^{10}21^{10}-10^{10}21^{10}-20^{10}11^{10}
=235117529492562122801
\tag{32}
\]
is equivalent to \(q>20/21\), so
\[
S_0=4200q>4000.
\tag{33}
\]
For a nonzero \(v\) with \(g(v)=k\), symmetry gives equal endpoint roots
\[
A=B=(R-k)_+.
\]
If \(k\geq100\), then \(A+B\leq4000<S_0\), so \((A,B)\) is feasible in
the maximum defining \(\Phi_{10,S_0}\).  Therefore
\(\Phi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,B)=0\), including every zero-volume
shell \(k\geq R\).

If \(1\leq k\leq99\), the pair
\[
(s,t)=(2100-k,1900+k)
\]
satisfies \(0\leq s,t\leq A\) and \(s+t=4000<S_0\).  Consequently
\[
\Psi_{10,S_0}(A,A)
\leq(2100-k)^{10}-(1900+k)^{10}.
\tag{34}
\]
There are \(2k\) antipodal pairs on shell \(k\).  It follows that
\[
\mathcal E^\Psi_{10}(w;0)\leq\frac{N_U}{11^{10}},
\]
where the exact integer evaluation is
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_U
&:=10^{10}R^{10}
+11^{10}\sum_{k=1}^{99}
2k\bigl((2100-k)^{10}-(1900+k)^{10}\bigr)\\
&=89339560114004193481855207707159995310060000000.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Finally,
\[
11^{10}W-64N_U
=63849160218264190758598471232906091394750350000>0,
\tag{36}
\]
which proves (11).

We now determine the cores in (16) and (17), including all halfspace
boundaries.  Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}\) be a cube of side
\(\ell>0\) and center \(c\).  Given any proper closed halfspace containing
\(c\), translate its boundary parallel toward \(c\).  The resulting
boundary-through-\(c\) halfspace is contained in the original one.  Central
reflection about \(c\) exchanges its two open sides, and its boundary
hyperplane has ten-dimensional volume zero.  Thus every such halfspace
contains at least \(\ell^{10}/2\) of \(K\).  A coordinate halfspace through
\(c\) attains equality, so
\[
\delta_K(c)=\frac{\ell^{10}}2.
\tag{37}
\]
If \(x\neq c\), choose \(j\) with \(x_j\neq c_j\) and take the closed
coordinate halfspace from \(x_j\) toward the nearer face on that side of
\(c\).  It contains \(x\) and cuts a slab of width strictly less than
\(\ell/2\), hence of volume strictly less than \(\ell^{10}/2\).  Therefore
\[
\{x\in K:\delta_K(x)\geq\ell^{10}/2\}=\{c\}.
\tag{38}
\]

Every fiber indexed by \(D(P)=D_+\) has positive side length.  Applying
(38) to (26) proves (16) and (17).  Applying the same antipodal argument
as for (7) to \(t_z=w_z/2\) gives
\[
\sum_{z\in D(P)\cap Q}t_z
=\frac12\sum_{z\in D(P)\cap Q}w_z
\geq\frac W4,
\]
which proves (15), again retaining all boundary points.  The constant map
\(0\) meets (16).  The polygon \(P\) is also admissible for the kinked
body before imposing the cores: since every fiber over \(P\) has side at
least \(1\), the constant affine map \(\mathbf1/2\) lies in every such
fiber.

For (18), direct calculation gives
\[
-\varepsilon_1+2\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_1=0,
\]
\[
(-1)(-\varepsilon_1)+(1)(-\varepsilon_1)=0,
\]
and every selected base point has second coordinate zero.  This proves
(19).  The singleton core centers at \(\pm e_1\) equal
\((R-1)\mathbf1/2\), while the center at \(0\) equals \(R\mathbf1/2\).
Hence the strict separation value is exactly
\[
-\frac{R-1}{2}+R-\frac{R-1}{2}=1,
\]
which proves (20).  Indeed, the moment equations make
\(\sum_z\langle u_z,s(z)\rangle=0\) for every affine map \(s\), whereas
the prescribed singleton values make that sum \(1\); thus no affine graph
meets all the kinked cores.

The equality in (21) is direct, and its strict comparison follows from
\[
\begin{aligned}
W-40000\bigl(R^{10}+2(R-1)^{10}\bigr)
=23380468377566675196214044157660270000>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
This completes the scalar-profile indistinguishability and tiny-circuit
claims.

It remains to prove the rescue (24).  For \(a,E,s\) in (23),
\[
\operatorname{conv}E=\{z:g(z)\leq a\}.
\]
Every kinked fiber over this diamond contains the common cube
\[
[0,R-a]^{10}=[0,1750]^{10},
\]
whose center is \(s(z)=875\mathbf1\).  Thus \(s\) is affine and feasible.
For every proper closed continuous halfspace containing \(s(z)\), its
intersection with the full fiber contains its intersection with this
inner cube.  The closed-boundary argument proving (37) therefore gives
\[
\delta_z^{C^{\mathrm{kink}}}(s(z))
\geq\frac{1750^{10}}2
\tag{40}
\]
at every lattice point of the diamond.

The diamond has
\[
N=1+4\sum_{k=1}^ak=1+2a(a+1)=245701
\tag{41}
\]
lattice points.  Every proper closed base halfspace containing \(0\)
contains the anchor and at least one member of each of the
\((N-1)/2\) nonzero antipodal pairs, including both members when a pair is
on the boundary.  It therefore contains at least
\((N+1)/2=122851\) lattice points.  Combining this with (40) and taking
the infimum in (22) proves the first inequality in (24).  Finally, put
\[
M:=1750^{10}\bigl(2a(a+1)+2\bigr)
=66189511899755477905273437500000000000.
\]
The exact gap
\[
16M-W
=836127214422041726843483365568019650000>0
\tag{42}
\]
gives \(M/4>W/64\), proving (24) and (25).  Hence the kinked body supplies
the asserted scalar no-go but not an anchored-capacity counterexample.
