## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Put $S=(\lbrace-1\rbrace\times A)\cup(\lbrace0\rbrace\times K)\cup(\lbrace1\rbrace\times B)$, measured by $d$-dimensional Hausdorff measure $\mu$ on $S$. Fix $y\in K$ and let $Q$ be the infimum of $\mu(S\cap H)$ over all closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(0,y)$.

Suppose $H_\alpha=\lbrace(z,x):u\cdot(x-y)+\alpha z\ge0\rbrace$, with $u\ne0$, attains $Q$. Let $U,q,V$ be the respective $d$-volumes of its traces on $A,K,B$. Each whole endpoint volume is at least $U+V$. There is a finite $\beta$ for which $H_\beta$ captures equal endpoint volumes $w$ and the same middle volume $q$, with $U+V\le2w\le2q$. In particular, $Q\le3q$.

If $U^{1/d}+V^{1/d}=2q^{1/d}$, then $Q=3q$ and $U+V=2q$. For $d>1$ this gives $U=V=q$. If also $q>0$, both endpoint cuts are proper, meaning their boundary hyperplanes meet the interiors of their respective fibers.

## Proof

Each fiber is an isometric embedding of a $d$-body, so the three disjoint fibers contribute their ordinary volumes and $Q=U+q+V$. For real $\beta$ let $H_\beta$ be given by the same formula as $H_\alpha$ with coefficient $\beta$. Its three traces have thresholds $\beta,0,-\beta$ for $u\cdot(x-y)$. Each such cap contains $(0,y)$. Choose $L>\max_{x\in A\cup B}|u\cdot(x-y)|$. At $\beta=-L$ it captures all of $A$ and strictly misses $B$, while at $\beta=L$ it strictly misses $A$ and captures all of $B$. Thus $Q\le q+|A|$ and $Q\le q+|B|$, proving the whole-endpoint floors. This comparison includes original full traces, empty traces and nonempty traces of zero $d$-volume, such as supporting faces.

The captured endpoint volumes vary continuously over $[-L,L]$, since the moving halfspace indicators converge pointwise except on a hyperplane of $d$-volume zero and the fibers have finite volume. The endpoint volume difference has opposite signs at the two finite extremes, so it vanishes at some $\beta$ with both captures of volume $w$. If $w=0$, then $w\le q$. If $w>0$, the two captured sets are compact convex bodies. Their Minkowski midpoint lies in $K$ by compatibility and in the middle trace because the endpoint thresholds average to zero. Ordinary Brunn–Minkowski gives $w\le q$. Since $H_\beta$ is a containing ambient cap, $Q\le q+2w$ and $U+V\le 2w$.

Under the root equality, convexity of $t\mapsto t^d$ on $[0,\infty)$ gives $U+V\ge2q$. Hence $U+V=2q$ and $Q=3q$. For $d>1$, strict convexity gives $U=V=q$. If $q>0$, each endpoint has captured volume $q$ and strict-complement volume at least $q$, by the floors. The cutting plane then meets the interior of each body. If $q=0$, all three captures have volume zero, and the proof has made no use of Brunn–Minkowski on their possibly lower-dimensional traces.

In $d=1$, the individual endpoint volumes can differ even in exact equality. Take $A=K=B=[-1,1]$, $y=0$, $u=1$ and $\alpha=1/2$. Central symmetry gives depth at least half the total mass, and this cap takes exactly half with volumes $(1/2,1,3/2)$. In the same example $\alpha=2$ also attains depth, with volumes $(0,1,2)$ and one full and one empty endpoint.
