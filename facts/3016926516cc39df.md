## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted fact `9ecd32c422b8d963`. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body,
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
and the canonical hard inequality holds. In particular,
\[
 D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(y\in E_{1/20}\), let \(\tau_y>0\) and \(p_y\in\mathsf M_{\tau_y}(C_y)\) be its accepted pooled endpoint weight and point. Put
\[
 T_{1/20}:=\sum_{y\in E_{1/20}}\tau_y.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `9ecd32c422b8d963` gives
\[
 T_{1/20}>\frac{89}{100}Q,
 \qquad
 \tau_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 \frac{3}{320}a_y\leq\tau_y\leq\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in E_{1/20}),
\tag{5}
\]
and the single measure
\[
 \nu_{1/20}:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{y\in E_{1/20}}
 \tau_y\delta_{(y,p_y)}
\tag{6}
\]
is halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure \(\mu_C\).

Put
\[
 \alpha:=\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{7}
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\) and integer \(k\geq0\), define the half-open dyadic bin
\[
 B_{\xi,k}:=
 \left\{
 y\in E_{1/20}:
 y\equiv\xi\pmod2,\quad
 2^{-(k+1)}\alpha\leq\tau_y<2^{-k}\alpha
 \right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Within every nonempty bin, partition all but at most one of its elements into unordered pairs. Let \(\mathcal M_2\) be the union of all pairs produced in this way, and let \(U_2\) be the set of all unpaired singleton endpoints. Then all pairs in \(\mathcal M_2\) are globally endpoint-disjoint, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{y\in U_2}\tau_y<\frac Q{200}.}
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently, the total endpoint weight covered by the pairs satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal M_2}(\tau_u+\tau_v)
 >\frac{177}{200}Q.}
\tag{10}
\]

For \(e=\{u,v\}\in\mathcal M_2\), put
\[
 t_e:=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\},
 \qquad
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 q_e:=\frac{p_u+p_v}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\), and
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\max\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}}
 {\min\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}}<2,}
\tag{12}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}t_e>
 \frac{59}{200}Q.}
\tag{13}
\]
Every endpoint \(y\in e\) also satisfies the pair-scale physical-volume bounds
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{3}{640}a_y<t_e\leq\frac{a_y}{4},}
\tag{14}
\]
and hence, for \(e=\{u,v\}\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \max\left\{\frac{a_u}{a_v},\frac{a_v}{a_u}\right\}
 <\frac{160}{3}.}
\tag{15}
\]

Every pair produces an actual full-mass metronoid point in its midpoint fiber:
\[
 \boxed{
 q_e\in\mathsf M_{t_e}(C_{m_e})}
 \qquad(e\in\mathcal M_2).
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, \((m_e,q_e)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

The single positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_2:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}
 t_e\delta_{(m_e,q_e)}}
\tag{17}
\]
has mass
\[
 \boxed{
 |\sigma_2|=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}t_e
 >\frac{59\gamma_d}{200}Q,}
\tag{18}
\]
and is genuinely halfspace-dominated:
\[
 \boxed{\sigma_2(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{19}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\).

Define
\[
 A_2:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:e\in\mathcal M_2\},
 \qquad
 \rho_2:=\dim A_2,
\tag{20}
\]
and
\[
 r_2:=
 \min_{a:A_2\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{q_e-a(m_e):e\in\mathcal M_2\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_2}
 \frac{\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}t_e}{r_2+1}
 >
 \frac{59\gamma_d}{200}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_2}Q}{r_2+1},}
\tag{22}
\]
\[
 \boxed{
 r_2+1>
 \frac{59\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{n-\rho_2},}
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{(m_e,q_e):e\in\mathcal M_2\}
 =\rho_2+r_2\geq n+4.}
\tag{24}
\]

The theorem does not assert that different pairs have distinct midpoints or distinct midpoint atoms. It does not put the midpoint atoms in one common full-rank affine or lattice-polysimplex chart, and it does not place a macroscopic subfamily at one common midpoint. Although every individual pair is a reflected pair about its own midpoint, the theorem does not authorize adding the corresponding fixed-midpoint \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) scores over different midpoints. It proves neither an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality nor the locked final theorem.

## Proof

Equation (4) says that every endpoint weight belongs to the interval \((0,\alpha)\). The half-open intervals
\[
 [2^{-(k+1)}\alpha,2^{-k}\alpha),
 \qquad k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},
\tag{25}
\]
are pairwise disjoint and partition \((0,\alpha)\). Thus every endpoint belongs to exactly one bin in (8). The endpoint set is finite, so only finitely many bins are nonempty. Pairing within each finite bin leaves at most one singleton, and the union \(\mathcal M_2\) is globally endpoint-disjoint because every endpoint occurs in exactly one bin and in at most one pair.

Fix one parity class \(\xi\). If \(B_{\xi,k}\) leaves an unmatched singleton, its weight is strictly less than \(2^{-k}\alpha\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{\substack{y\in U_2\\y\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
 \tau_y
 <
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\alpha
 =2\alpha.
\tag{26}
\]
There are \(2^n\) parity classes. Equations (7) and (26) give
\[
 \sum_{y\in U_2}\tau_y
 <2^n\cdot2\alpha
 =\frac Q{200},
\tag{27}
\]
which proves (9). Subtracting (9) from the strict lower bound in (4) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal M_2}(\tau_u+\tau_v)
 &=T_{1/20}-\sum_{y\in U_2}\tau_y\\
 &>\frac{89}{100}Q-\frac1{200}Q
 =\frac{177}{200}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
This proves (10).

The two endpoints of a pair lie in one parity class, so \(m_e\in\mathbb Z^n\). They also lie in one bin. If \(t_e\) is their smaller weight, the half-open bin bounds give
\[
 t_e\leq\max\{\tau_u,\tau_v\}<2t_e.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (12), and
\[
 \tau_u+\tau_v<3t_e.
\tag{30}
\]
Summing (30) over \(\mathcal M_2\) and using (10) proves (13).

Equation (5) is equivalent to
\[
 4\tau_y\leq a_y\leq\frac{320}{3}\tau_y
 \qquad(y\in E_{1/20}).
\tag{31}
\]
For an endpoint \(y\) of \(e\), equation (29) gives
\[
 t_e\leq\tau_y<2t_e.
\tag{32}
\]
Combining (31)--(32),
\[
 4t_e\leq a_y<\frac{640}{3}t_e.
\tag{33}
\]
Rearrangement proves (14). Applying (33) to both endpoints of one pair proves (15).

We prove the metronoid membership. Since \(p_y\in\mathsf M_{\tau_y}(C_y)\), choose a representing density \(f_y\) with
\[
 0\leq f_y\leq1,\qquad
 \int_{C_y}f_y=\tau_y,\qquad
 \frac1{\tau_y}\int_{C_y}x f_y(x)\,dx=p_y.
\tag{34}
\]
For \(e=\{u,v\}\), scale \(f_u\) and \(f_v\) by \(t_e/\tau_u\) and \(t_e/\tau_v\), respectively. The two scaled densities are admissible, have mass \(t_e\), and retain barycenters \(p_u,p_v\). Thus
\[
 (u,p_u,t_e^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (v,p_v,t_e^{1/d})
\tag{35}
\]
belong to the positive-root fiber-metronoid generating set of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e`. That accepted fact proves this generating set convex before closure. Taking the midpoint of the two elements in (35), with their common positive root unchanged, gives (16).

We next prove domination of the one global measure (17). Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace. If \((m_e,q_e)\in H\), then at least one of \((u,p_u)\) and \((v,p_v)\) lies in \(H\), because the first point is their midpoint. Orient every such pair toward one endpoint point lying in \(H\). The pairs in \(\mathcal M_2\) are globally endpoint-disjoint, so all selected endpoints are distinct. Also \(t_e\leq\tau_y\) at either endpoint \(y\) of \(e\). Hence the accepted domination of (6) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_2(H)
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal M_2\\(m_e,q_e)\in H}}t_e\\
 &\leq
 \gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{y\ {\rm selected}\\(y,p_y)\in H}}\tau_y\\
 &\leq\nu_{1/20}(H)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
This proves (19), including coincident midpoint atoms. Equation (18) follows directly from (13) and the definition of \(\sigma_2\).

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to the nonzero measure \(\sigma_2\). Its intrinsic ranks are exactly (20)--(21), even if coincident atoms are aggregated, and that theorem gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_d\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}t_e}
 {2^{\rho_2}(r_2+1)}.
\tag{37}
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) and using (13) proves (22). Equations (2) and (22) imply
\[
 \frac Q{100}>
 \frac{59\gamma_d}{200}
 \frac{2^{n-\rho_2}Q}{r_2+1},
\tag{38}
\]
which rearranges to (23).

The intrinsic-rank identity in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives the equality in (24). Since \(r_2+1\leq2^{r_2}\), equation (23) gives
\[
 2^{\rho_2+r_2}
 \geq2^{\rho_2}(r_2+1)
 >
 \frac{59\gamma_d}{2}\,2^n.
\tag{39}
\]
Accepted fact `9ecd32c422b8d963` proves \(\gamma_d>4/11\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{59\gamma_d}{2}>
 \frac{118}{11}>8,
\tag{40}
\]
so (39) is strictly larger than \(2^{n+3}\). The exponent \(\rho_2+r_2\) is an integer, hence it is at least \(n+4\). This proves (24) and completes the proof.
