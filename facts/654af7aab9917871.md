## Statement

Retain exactly the support \(D_M^{\mathrm{trap}}\) of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`. For each integer \(M\ge2\), define a new original root law \(\widetilde p^{(M)}\) on that same support by

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_M^{\mathrm{trap}}
&=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(0,M^2),(1,0),(1,M)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2,\\
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\theta}_M
&=\left(\log\frac{M^2+1}{M+1},0\right),
\qquad \widetilde Z_M=2(M^2+1),\\
\widetilde p^{(M)}_{(0,j)}
&=\frac1{2(M^2+1)}\quad(0\le j\le M^2),\\
\widetilde p^{(M)}_{(1,j)}
&=\frac1{2(M+1)}\quad(0\le j\le M).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\widetilde p^{(M)}_{\mathbf I}=e^{\widetilde{\boldsymbol\theta}_M\cdot\mathbf I}/\widetilde Z_M\) on the complete full-rank support. These are the original laws in this packet, not conditional laws and not the different laws \(p^{(M)}\) in the accepted fact. The exact alias map is identity on \(D_M^{\mathrm{trap}}\), with that fact's tilt \((2\log M,0)\) and normalizer \(Z_M=M^3+2M^2+1\) replaced by the displayed new tilt and \(\widetilde Z_M\). All probability calculations below concern \(\widetilde p^{(M)}\).

Extend these laws by zero to \(\mathbb Z^2\). Use precisely the canonical normalization of `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`, now with root law \(p=\widetilde p^{(M)}\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\pi^{(M)}_{\mathbf b}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\mathbf b\pmod2}\widetilde p^{(M)}_{\mathbf I},\\
\widetilde q^{\mathrm{can},(M)}_{\mathbf J}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^2}
\frac{\widetilde p^{(M)}_{\mathbf I}
\widetilde p^{(M)}_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}}
{\widetilde\pi^{(M)}_{\mathbf I\bmod2}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

All four parity masses are positive. Equivalently, choose a parity class with its original mass, choose two independent endpoints conditional on that class, and take their midpoint. This is not global conditioning of two independent original endpoints on parity agreement.

The maximum atom is \(1/[2(M+1)]\to0\). For every sequence of invertible real affine maps \(F_M(\mathbf x)=B_M\mathbf x+\mathbf a_M\), every subsequential weak probability limit of \((F_M)_\#\widetilde p^{(M)}\) has an atom of mass at least \(1/2\). For any such convergent subsequence, after a further subsequence there exist \(\mathbf a,\mathbf c,\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^2\) such that the root and canonical laws converge respectively to

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu
&=\frac12\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf a+U\mathbf v)
  +\frac12\delta_{\mathbf c},\\
\nu
&=\frac12\operatorname{Law}\left(\mathbf a+
                  \frac{U+U'}2\mathbf v\right)
  +\frac12\delta_{\mathbf c},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(U,U'\) are independent uniform variables on \([0,1]\). All these limits have finite fourth moments. If \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\), the root limit has no continuous component; if \(\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0\), it has both a continuous segment component and an atom. In neither case can it have a density with respect to \(\operatorname{Leb}^a\otimes\#_{\mathbb Z^b}\) on \(\mathbb R^a\times\mathbb Z^b\), for any integers \(a\ge1\), \(b\ge0\), \(a+b\le2\), even after an affine identification of the limiting support. In particular it cannot have the required globally log-concave density. Therefore no affine normalization and subsequence meet the proposed joint compactness/operator assertion.

## Proof

The support description can be checked directly. The trapezoid has integer first coordinate only zero or one. Its corresponding vertical fibers are exactly the integer intervals \(0\le j\le M^2\) and \(0\le j\le M\). Its four displayed vertices belong to these fibers, so their convex hull is exactly the trapezoid and the set of its integer points is exactly \(D_M^{\mathrm{trap}}\). This proves completeness. The support contains \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\); hence it has real affine rank two and its difference lattice is exactly \(\mathbb Z^2\), not a proper sublattice. This also verifies independently the support geometry inherited from `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`.

The exponential weights are one on the zero fiber and \((M^2+1)/(M+1)\) on the one fiber. Their sum is exactly \(2(M^2+1)\), giving the stated positive log-affine table. Each fiber has original mass exactly \(1/2\), with its original conditional law uniform. Since \(M^2+1>M+1\), the displayed maximum atom is exact. For comparison with the original atomic normalization in the accepted fact, one also has the exact identity \(64\sum_{\mathbf I}(\widetilde p^{(M)}_{\mathbf I})^4=4((M^2+1)^{-3}+(M+1)^{-3})\to0\). This is an atomic fourth-power sum, not a spatial fourth moment. No prelimit spatial moment assumption or whitening is used.

Fix any sequence of invertible affine maps and any subsequence on which the normalized root laws converge weakly to a probability measure. In the following argument restrict to that subsequence. Write \(\mathbf h_M=B_M\mathbf e_2\), \(\mathbf c_M=F_M(1,0)\), and \(\mathbf v_M=M^2\mathbf h_M\). Here \(\mathbf a_M=F_M(0,0)\) is already the translation in \(F_M\). The two normalized fibers are precisely

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left\{\mathbf a_M+j\mathbf h_M:0\le j\le M^2\right\},\\
&\left\{\mathbf c_M+j\mathbf h_M:0\le j\le M\right\},
\end{aligned}
\]

with uniform conditional laws and original masses \(1/2\) each.

Weak convergence to a probability measure supplies a fixed ball of radius \(R>0\) containing more than \(3/4\) of the normalized mass for all sufficiently large terms. One can obtain this without any moment claim: choose a bounded continuous cutoff supported in a sufficiently large ball whose integral against the limiting probability is greater than \(3/4\), and apply weak convergence. Because each fiber has total mass \(1/2\), more than half the sites of each fiber lie in this ball.

For the long fiber, any two sites lying in the ball have index difference at most \(2R/\|\mathbf h_M\|\). The number of its sites in the ball is therefore at most \(2R/\|\mathbf h_M\|+1\). Invertibility gives \(\mathbf h_M\ne\mathbf0\). Comparing this count with \((M^2+1)/2\) gives

\[
\|\mathbf h_M\|(M^2-1)\le4R,
\qquad
\|\mathbf v_M\|\le\frac{4RM^2}{M^2-1},
\qquad
M\|\mathbf h_M\|\le\frac{4RM}{M^2-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]

In particular, the long fiber's diameter is uniformly bounded and the short fiber's diameter tends to zero. Each fiber has a site inside the fixed ball. The long-fiber diameter bound therefore bounds its initial point \(\mathbf a_M\), and the short-fiber diameter bound bounds its initial point \(\mathbf c_M\). The vectors \(\mathbf a_M,\mathbf c_M,\mathbf v_M\) are all bounded. Passing to a further subsequence, let them converge respectively to \(\mathbf a,\mathbf c,\mathbf v\).

The long-fiber conditional law is the uniform grid law on \(\mathbf a_M+(j/M^2)\mathbf v_M\), \(0\le j\le M^2\). Riemann sums against any bounded continuous function converge to its integral on \(\mathbf a+u\mathbf v\), \(0\le u\le1\). This remains valid if \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\). The short-fiber conditional law converges to \(\delta_{\mathbf c}\), since its diameter tends to zero and its initial point converges to \(\mathbf c\). Combining the two original masses proves the stated formula for \(\mu\). Since the original subsequence already had a weak limit, this formula describes that same limit, not a different possible limit.

Thus every weak probability limit under every affine normalization has \(\mu(\{\mathbf c\})\ge1/2\). The formula also proves compact support and hence finite fourth moments. Every singleton has zero measure under \(\operatorname{Leb}^a\otimes\#_{\mathbb Z^b}\) when \(a\ge1\). Any probability having a density relative to that reference measure is therefore nonatomic. Affine identifications or further affine projections cannot remove the positive mass of an atom. This excludes all the demanded reference measures, including lower-dimensional ones, without needing to examine log-concavity. It proves the failure for all choices of normalization and subsequence.

For completeness, track the affine support group and the canonical operator. Before normalization the support's affine lattice is \(\mathbb Z^2\), and the difference lattice is also \(\mathbb Z^2\). After applying \(F_M\), these become the coset \(\Gamma_M=\mathbf a_M+B_M\mathbb Z^2\) and the group \(B_M\mathbb Z^2\), respectively. The four transported parity classes are exactly \(\mathbf a_M+B_M(\mathbf b+2\mathbb Z^2)\), \(\mathbf b\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\). They are cosets modulo \(2B_M\mathbb Z^2\), regardless of whether \(B_M\) is integral. For any source endpoints of the same parity, \(F_M((\mathbf X+\mathbf Y)/2)=(F_M(\mathbf X)+F_M(\mathbf Y))/2\in\Gamma_M\). Consequently the pushforward of the source canonical law is precisely canonical midpoint sampling on the transported lattice and its transported parity classes. No fixed ambient \(\mathbb Z^2\) is asserted after normalization.

On this support the first-coordinate parity determines the actual fiber. Canonical endpoint sampling therefore never pairs endpoints from different fibers. The mass of class \((0,r)\) is one half the fraction of integers of parity \(r\) in \(\{0,\ldots,M^2\}\); the mass of \((1,r)\) is one half the corresponding fraction in \(\{0,\ldots,M\}\). Since even and odd counts in an integer interval differ by at most one, every parity mass differs from \(1/4\) by at most \(1/[4(M+1)]\), and all four masses tend to \(1/4\). Conditional on \((0,r)\), the two endpoint fractions \(j/M^2\) are independent uniform variables on the even or odd grid in \([0,1]\). Each grid has mesh \(2/M^2\), so its uniform law converges to uniform measure on \([0,1]\). Two-dimensional Riemann sums show that their normalized midpoint law converges to \(\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf a+(U+U')\mathbf v/2)\). Conditional on either class \((1,r)\), both normalized endpoints converge to \(\mathbf c\), so their midpoint converges to \(\mathbf c\). Summing with the original parity masses proves the displayed formula for \(\nu\). Thus the fine second-coordinate parity disappears within each long fiber, but the distinction between the original fibers survives in the sampling rule unless kept as a discrete coordinate.

Two concrete normalizations illustrate the operator issue without replacing the preceding all-normalizations obstruction. With \(F_M(x,y)=(x,y/M^2)\), the transported lattices are \(\mathbb Z\times M^{-2}\mathbb Z\). The limit is mass \(1/2\) uniformly on \(\{0\}\times[0,1]\) and mass \(1/2\) at \((1,0)\). Its ambient limiting group is \(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R\), with coset translation zero. Retaining the discrete first-coordinate parity does give the computed conditional midpoint law. Nevertheless the row at first coordinate one is a point mass, so the limiting law has no density relative to counting times Lebesgue measure.

With \(F_M(x,y)=(x/M,y/M^2)\), the root limit on the vertical line is \(\frac12\operatorname{Law}(U\mathbf e_2)+\frac12\delta_{\mathbf0}\), while the canonical limit is \(\frac12\operatorname{Law}((U+U')\mathbf e_2/2)+\frac12\delta_{\mathbf0}\). The transported lattices are \(M^{-1}\mathbb Z\times M^{-2}\mathbb Z\); the two original first-coordinate parity classes collapse onto the same limiting continuous support. The ordinary midpoint operator on that one-dimensional continuous root limit has atom at zero of mass exactly \(1/4\), since both endpoints must choose its atom and every other endpoint combination is nonatomic. The actual canonical limit has atom at zero of mass \(1/2\). Thus discarding the collapsed coarse parity label fails operator compatibility as well. The universal failure was already proved by the atom that survives every tight normalization.

Finally, apply the width-one separation of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` to the new original law. Its hypotheses hold with \(\omega=1\), no conditioning or change of root law. With exactly that fact's \(\kappa>0\) and total-variation convention,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{TV}(\widetilde p^{(M)},
                 \widetilde q^{\mathrm{can},(M)})
&\ge\left[\kappa-\frac3{M+1}\right]_+,\\
\liminf_{M\to\infty}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\widetilde q^{\mathrm{can},(M)}
                    \|\widetilde p^{(M)})
&\ge2\kappa^2>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Accordingly this sequence cannot be used as a near-stationarity counterexample. It identifies a false compactness step in the unrestricted proposed theorem: positive-mass fibers need not have comparable continuous scales under one affine map, even in the plane and even for complete globally log-affine tables. The missing requirement cannot be obtained from diffuseness alone. No thin-shell estimate, higher-rank empty-cell argument, or mixed-integer depth conclusion is used or claimed.
