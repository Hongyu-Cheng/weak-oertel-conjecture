## Statement

Let \(b:\mathbb Z\to[0,\infty)\) have finite, nonempty, consecutive positive support and satisfy \(b_k^2\ge b_{k-1}b_{k+1}\) for all \(k\). Equivalently, these are finite lattice restrictions of nonnegative log-concave functions on \(\mathbb R\). Put \(W:=\sum_k b_k>0\) and \(M:=\max_k b_k\).

For every integer \(j\), put \(H_j:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}\sqrt{b_{j+v}b_{j-v}}\), and put \(H_*:=\max_j H_j\) and \(g:=\log(2H_*/W)\). For \(b_j>0\), let \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v:b_{j+v}b_{j-v}>0\}\). On this finite set define

\[
 Q_j(v):=\frac{\sqrt{b_{j+v}b_{j-v}}}{H_j},\qquad
 \Delta_j(v):=\log\frac{b_j^2}{b_{j+v}b_{j-v}},\qquad
 T_j(v):=\frac12\log\frac{b_{j+v}}{b_{j-v}},\qquad
 \rho_j:=1-\frac{b_j}{H_j}.
\]

All logarithms are natural. The law \(Q_j\) includes displacement zero. Its curvature \(\Delta_j\) is nonnegative and even, and \(T_j\) is odd.

There is an absolute constant \(g_0\in(0,1/100]\), independent of the support and all factor values, with the following properties.

1. One has \(0\le g\le\log2\). If \(g=0\), the positive support consists of two adjacent integers, both of weight \(W/2\). Conversely, these factors have \(g=0\).

2. If \(0<g\le g_0\), two adjacent integers \(a,a+1\), carrying the two largest weights, satisfy
   \(\sum_{k\notin\{a,a+1\}}b_k\le4g^2W\).
   Every integer \(j\) maximizing \(H_j\) belongs to \(\{a,a+1\}\) and satisfies \(\rho_j\le5g\). In particular,
   \((1-2M/W)_+\le4g^2\).

3. For every \(j\) with \(b_j>0\), the mean of \(T_j\) under \(Q_j\) is zero. If \(0<\rho_j<1\), the following explicit moment bounds hold:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j
&\le
\frac{\rho_j(2-\rho_j)}{1-\rho_j}
 \log\frac{2-\rho_j}{\rho_j}
\le2,\\
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)
&\le
\rho_j\left[
\operatorname{arcosh}\left(1+\frac{W/H_j-1}{\rho_j}\right)
\right]^2
\le2(W/H_j-1).
\end{aligned}
\]

   When \(\rho_j=0\), both moments are zero. Consequently, whenever \(H_j\ge W/2\), both \(\mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j\) and \(\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)\) are at most 2.

4. Set \(C:=16+g_0^{-2}\). For every maximizing integer center \(j\), one has

\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-2M/W)_+&\le Cg^2,\\
\mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j&\le Cg\log(e/g),\\
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)&\le Cg[\log(e/g)]^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

   The last two right sides are defined to be zero at \(g=0\).

The constant \(g_0\) is proved to exist by compactness below; a numerical lower bound for it is not asserted. Thus all constants in these estimates are absolute, although the proof does not assign a numerical value to \(C\).

## Proof

Multiplying all weights by the same positive scalar does not change \(g\), \(Q_j\), \(\Delta_j\), \(T_j\), or \(\rho_j\). Integer translations and reflection of the index preserve every assertion. Whenever convenient below we normalize \(W=1\) and translate a maximizing atom to index zero.

The assumed discrete log-concavity gives a log-concave extension by linear interpolation of \(\log b_k\) between consecutive positive integers, followed by extension by zero outside their convex hull. The same construction works for summable sequences with infinite consecutive support, which will occur only as limits in the proof.

Apply the integer-median conclusion of accepted fact 97622625586363a7 to this extension, with its lattice values \(a_k=b_k\) and the same \(W\). Its raw reflection sum is \(S_j=\sum_v\min(b_{j+v},b_{j-v})\). We do not use its symbol for the sum of positive-displacement minima; that symbol is distinct from the present maximum atom \(M\). At every integer weighted median \(m\), that fact gives \(b_m>0\) and \(S_m\ge W/2\). Since \(H_m\ge S_m\), one has \(H_*\ge W/2\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(H_j\le W\) for every \(j\). Therefore \(0\le g\le\log2\). Log-concavity also shows that \(H_j>0\) implies \(b_j>0\), so every maximizing center is eligible for \(Q_j\).

We first characterize equality in \(H_*\ge W/2\), allowing finite or infinite summable log-concave sequences. Normalize \(W=1\), suppose \(\sup_jH_j=1/2\), and choose an integer weighted median \(m\). Then \(H_m=S_m=1/2\). Each term in \(H_m-S_m\) is nonnegative. Thus \(b_{m-v}=b_{m+v}\) whenever both weights are positive.

Write \(u=b_m\), \(L=\sum_{v\ge1}b_{m-v}\), and \(R=\sum_{v\ge1}b_{m+v}\). Because the positive support is an interval, all unmatched positive terms outside its reflection overlap occur on at most one side. The equality of weights on the overlap gives \(S_m=u+2\min(L,R)\). Consequently \(|L-R|=1-S_m=1/2\). The median inequalities \(L,R\le1/2\), together with \(1=u+L+R\), imply \(|L-R|\le u\). Also \(u\le H_m=1/2\). Hence \(u=1/2\) and \(\min(L,R)=0\). After reflection and translation, the support begins at zero, \(b_0=1/2\), and \(\sum_{k\ge1}b_k=1/2\).

Divide these weights by \(b_0\), writing them temporarily as \(c_0=1\), \(c_k\ge0\) for \(k\ge1\), and \(\sum_{k\ge1}c_k=1\). All \(c_k\le1\), since any larger atom would have \(H_k>1\) in this normalization. Put \(t=c_1\). If \(t=1\), all later terms vanish, giving exactly two equal adjacent atoms. Otherwise \(0<t<1\). Log-concavity bounds \(c_k\le t^k\), so \(1\le t/(1-t)\) and \(t\ge1/2\). The tail has at least two positive terms; put \(s=c_2>0\). Its successive ratios are nonincreasing and at most \(s/t\le t<1\), whence
   \(1=\sum_{k\ge1}c_k\le t/(1-s/t)\).
Rearranging gives \(s\ge t(1-t)\). At center one,
   \(H_1\ge t+2\sqrt{s}\ge t+2\sqrt{t(1-t)}>1\).
The strict inequality follows by squaring \(2\sqrt{t(1-t)}>1-t\), which is equivalent to \(5t>1\) when \(t<1\). This contradicts the normalized supremum 1. The equality characterization follows. Its converse follows by direct inspection of a two-point support.

We next establish the qualitative compactness statement needed to turn equality into a quantitative estimate:

For every fixed \(\eta>0\), there exists \(\varepsilon_\eta>0\) such that every finite log-concave sequence of mass 1 with \(H_*\le1/2+\varepsilon_\eta\) has two adjacent atoms whose sum is greater than \(1-\eta\).

Here is a proof including the possibility of increasingly diffuse sequences. Suppose this assertion fails. There are finite log-concave sequences \(b^{(r)}\) of mass 1, with \(H_*^{(r)}\to1/2\), such that no two adjacent atoms have sum greater than \(1-\eta\). Translate a mode of each sequence to zero and write \(p_r=b^{(r)}_0=\max_k b^{(r)}_k\). Passing to a subsequence, \(p_r\to p\in[0,1]\).

Consider first \(p>0\). For all sufficiently large \(r\), there is a fixed \(p_{\min}>0\) with \(p_r\ge p_{\min}\). Choose an integer \(K\ge2/p_{\min}\). Unimodality on each side of a mode and total mass 1 give \(b^{(r)}_{\pm K}\le1/(K+1)\le p_{\min}/2\). If such an endpoint is zero, all farther terms on that side are zero. Otherwise log-concavity between zero and an index \(k\) with \(|k|\ge K\) gives
   \(b^{(r)}_k\le p_r(b^{(r)}_{\operatorname{sgn}(k)K}/p_r)^{|k|/K}\le2^{-|k|/K}\).
This is a summable bound, uniform in \(r\). A diagonal subsequence converges at every integer to a sequence \(b\). The uniform tail bound implies convergence in \(\ell^1(\mathbb Z)\), so \(\sum_kb_k=1\) and \(b_0=p>0\). Log-concavity and the absence of interior zeros pass to the limit: between any two positive limiting endpoints, the log-concavity interpolation inequality supplies a positive lower bound at every intervening integer.

For any two nonnegative sequences \(b,c\) of mass 1, let \(a_k=\sqrt{b_k}\) and \(d_k=\sqrt{c_k}\). Reflection about an integer center is an isometry of \(\ell^2\), so

\[
 \sup_j|H_j(b)-H_j(c)|
 \le2\|a-d\|_2
 \le2\|b-c\|_1^{1/2}.
\]

The last inequality uses \((\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y})^2\le|x-y|\). Thus the limiting sequence satisfies \(\sup_jH_j(b)=1/2\). The equality characterization just proved makes \(b\) two equal adjacent atoms. Convergence in \(\ell^1\) then gives adjacent mass tending to 1 in \(b^{(r)}\), a contradiction.

It remains to rule out \(p=0\). Use mesh \(p_r\) and define the step density \(f_r(x)=b^{(r)}_k/p_r\) for \(x\in[p_rk,p_r(k+1))\). It has integral 1 and maximum 1. Let \(\widetilde f_r\) be the function whose logarithm interpolates the values \(b^{(r)}_k/p_r\) linearly between successive positive grid values, and which is zero outside the interval from the first positive grid point to the last. Eventually there are at least two positive weights, since a singleton would have \(p_r=1\). The function \(\widetilde f_r\) is log-concave, has maximum \(\widetilde f_r(0)=1\), and is nondecreasing on \((-\infty,0]\) and nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\).

On each grid interval, \(\widetilde f_r\) lies between its two endpoint values. If the positive support of \(b^{(r)}\) is \([A_r,B_r]\cap\mathbb Z\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|f_r-\widetilde f_r\|_{L^1}
 &\le\sum_{k=A_r}^{B_r-1}
       |b^{(r)}_{k+1}-b^{(r)}_k|+b^{(r)}_{B_r}
 \le2p_r,\\
 \int_{\mathbb R}\widetilde f_r
 &\le
 \sum_{k=A_r}^{B_r-1}
   \frac{b^{(r)}_k+b^{(r)}_{k+1}}2
 \le1.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the second inequality, the exponential of a linear function lies below its endpoint chord. The first uses the total variation of a unimodal sequence with peak \(p_r\), namely \(2p_r-b^{(r)}_{A_r}-b^{(r)}_{B_r}\). In particular \(\int\widetilde f_r\to1\).

These interpolated densities have uniform integrable tails. Monotonicity and \(\int\widetilde f_r\le1\) imply \(\widetilde f_r(2)\le1/2\) and \(\widetilde f_r(-2)\le1/2\). Log-concavity with the value 1 at zero therefore gives
   \(\widetilde f_r(x)\le2^{-|x|/2}\) for \(|x|\ge2\);
if the value at the corresponding endpoint is zero, the same assertion follows from its interval support. On \([-2,2]\) the bound 1 applies.

For clarity, the elementary compactness of these densities does not require any entropy theorem. On each half-line they are monotone and bounded by 1. Diagonal extraction at rational points and monotone sandwiching yield a subsequence converging at every continuity point of a monotone limiting function on each half-line, hence almost everywhere. This limit has a log-concave representative. To see the last assertion, pass the log-concavity inequalities to any triple of convergence points. Inside the interval where the limit is positive, the logarithms on this dense set have concave secant slopes and are locally bounded between a finite lower bound and zero. On each smaller compact interval the secant inequalities bound their slopes by the slopes to two fixed exterior positive points. They therefore extend continuously and concavely across the exceptional points. Exponentiating this extension and setting it to zero outside its positive interval gives the claimed representative. Endpoint values do not affect the integrals. The uniform integrable bound above and dominated convergence now give a log-concave density \(f\) with \(\int f=1\) and \(\widetilde f_r\to f\) in \(L^1\). Consequently \(f_r\to f\) in \(L^1\) as well.

For a nonnegative integrable function \(f\), write
   \(K_f(x):=\int_{\mathbb R}\sqrt{f(z)f(2x-z)}\,dz\).
The same \(L^2\) argument used for discrete reflections shows, for densities of integral 1,
   \(\sup_x|K_f(x)-K_h(x)|\le2\|f-h\|_{L^1}^{1/2}\).
Moreover \(K_f\) is continuous in \(x\), since translations are continuous in \(L^2\) for \(\sqrt f\). This latter elementary fact follows first for finite linear combinations of interval indicators, and then for all \(L^2\) functions by approximation and the isometry of translation.

At the grid centers there is an exact identity
   \(K_{f_r}(p_r(j+1/2))=H_j(b^{(r)})\).
Indeed, reflection about \(p_r(j+1/2)\) sends the cell with index \(k\) to the cell with index \(2j-k\), up to endpoints of measure zero. Given any real \(x\), choose \(j_r\) with \(p_r(j_r+1/2)\to x\). Uniform convergence of the kernels and continuity of \(K_f\) imply \(K_f(x)\le1/2\).

We show directly that no log-concave density \(f\) of integral 1 can have this property. Choose a real median and translate it to zero. Since a density has a continuous distribution function, its two half-line masses are exactly \(1/2\). Put
   \(P:=\int_0^\infty\min(f(-v),f(v))\,dv\).
The continuous version of the elementary raw-reflection shell argument gives \(P\ge1/4\), as follows. Partition the positive quadrant into \(p\ge q\) and \(q>p\), and put \(r=\min(p,q)\). Tonelli's theorem gives

\[
 \frac14
 =
 \int_0^\infty
 \left[
    \int_r^\infty f(-p)f(r)\,dp
    +\int_r^\infty f(-r)f(q)\,dq
 \right]\,dr.
\]

For a fixed \(r\), if \(f(-r)\le f(r)\), the inward-pair inequality from log-concavity gives \(f(-p)f(r)\le f(-r)f(2r-p)\) for \(p\ge r\). The first integral is then at most \(f(-r)\int_{-\infty}^r f\), and the second is \(f(-r)\int_r^\infty f\). Their sum is at most \(f(-r)\). If \(f(r)<f(-r)\), keep the first integral and use \(f(-r)f(q)\le f(r)f(q-2r)\) in the second; their sum is at most \(f(r)\). The inward-pair inequality itself follows by multiplying the two log-concavity inequalities for two points with the same sum lying between two fixed endpoints. It remains valid when an endpoint value is zero. Integrating the shell bounds proves \(1/4\le P\).

It follows that \(K_f(0)\ge2P\ge1/2\). If \(K_f(0)=1/2\), both inequalities are equalities, and \(f(-v)=f(v)\) for almost every \(v>0\) where both values are positive. The positive support is an interval containing the median in its interior. Outside the reflected overlap, all unmatched positive mass can occur on at most one side. The two half-line masses are equal, so this unmatched mass must be zero. Therefore \(f\) is symmetric about zero almost everywhere, giving \(K_f(0)=\int f=1\), a contradiction. Thus \(K_f(0)>1/2\). This rules out the diffuse case and completes the qualitative compactness statement.

Choose an absolute \(\varepsilon_0\in(0,1/100]\) sufficiently small that this compactness statement, with \(\eta=1/100\), holds whenever \(H_*\le1/2+\varepsilon_0\). We now prove explicit local bounds. Let \(W=1\), write \(\varepsilon=H_*-1/2\le\varepsilon_0\), and choose adjacent sites carrying the two largest weights. Such a choice is always possible for a unimodal sequence: after choosing an endpoint of a modal plateau, or the unique mode, the largest remaining value is attained at an adjacent site on one of the two monotone sides. In the present situation their sum exceeds \(99/100\), since it is at least the adjacent sum furnished by compactness.

By translation and reflection write these two weights as \(b_0=A\ge b_1=B\), and put \(t=\sum_{k\notin\{0,1\}}b_k=1-A-B<1/100\). Since \(A\le H_*=1/2+\varepsilon\le51/100\), one has \(A\ge B\ge48/100\). Put \(x=b_{-1}\) and \(y=b_2\), taking zero when outside the positive support. Log-concavity bounds the left and right outside tails by the geometric sums starting with ratios \(x/A\) and \(y/B\). Since \(x,y\le t\le1/100\), those ratios are at most \(1/48\). Thus
   \(t\le x/(1-x/A)+y/(1-y/B)\le(48/47)(x+y)\).
These bounds include zero tails.

The two available centers satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
 1+2\varepsilon
 =2H_*
 &\ge H_0+H_1\\
 &\ge A+B+2\sqrt{Bx}+2\sqrt{Ay}\\
 &\ge1-t+2\sqrt{B(x+y)}
 \ge1-t+2\sqrt{47t/100}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(0\le t\le1/100\), one has \(t\le(1/10)\sqrt t\) and \(2\sqrt{47/100}-1/10\ge1\). The last display therefore gives \(2\varepsilon\ge\sqrt t\), or \(t\le4\varepsilon^2\). Also \((1-2A)_+\le t\).

Every maximizing center belongs to \(\{0,1\}\). To verify this, decompose the vector \((\sqrt{b_k})_k=c+d\), where \(c\) is supported on \(\{0,1\}\) and \(d\) on its complement. For an integer center \(j\notin\{0,1\}\), reflection about \(j\) pairs no two indices of \(\{0,1\}\) with each other, because the mixed pair has midpoint \(1/2\). Hence the \(c,c\) inner product is zero. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the other terms by
   \(H_j\le2\sqrt{(1-t)t}+t\le21/100<1/2\).
Such a center cannot maximize \(H\). For a maximizing center in \(\{0,1\}\), its weight is at least \(B\ge1/2-\varepsilon-t\), so
   \(\rho_j\le(2\varepsilon+t)/(1/2+\varepsilon)\le4\varepsilon+2t\le5\varepsilon\).
The last inequality follows from \(t\le4\varepsilon^2\) and \(\varepsilon\le1/100\).

Set \(g_0:=\min\{1/100,\log(1+2\varepsilon_0)\}\). If \(g\le g_0\), then \(\varepsilon=(e^g-1)/2\le\varepsilon_0\). Moreover \(\varepsilon\le\log(1+2\varepsilon)=g\) for \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/2\), since the derivative of \(\log(1+2\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\) is nonnegative on that interval. Therefore \(t\le4g^2\) and \(\rho_j\le5g\), as asserted. The equality case \(g=0\) was already treated separately.

We finish by proving the moment bounds, without using compactness for them. Fix any positive center \(j\), and let
   \(r_v=\sqrt{b_{j+v}b_{j-v}}/b_j=\exp(-\Delta_j(v)/2)\)
on its symmetric positive support. For nonnegative integer \(v\), this is a nonincreasing log-concave sequence with \(r_0=1\). Define \(r_v=0\) beyond its positive support and put \(R=\sum_{v\ge1}r_v\). Then \(H_j/b_j=1+2R\) and \(\rho_j=2R/(1+2R)\). If \(R=0\), the law is concentrated at zero and all the assertions are immediate. Suppose \(R>0\).

We need an elementary entropy comparison for this one-sided sequence. Put \(\theta=R/(1+R)\) and \(s_v=\theta^v\), \(v\ge0\). The sums of \(r_v\) and \(s_v\) are both \(1+R\), and both begin at 1 and are nonincreasing. Log-concavity shows that \(r_v/s_v\) crosses the level 1 at most once after index zero, from above to below: on the positive support, \(\log(r_v/s_v)/v\) is nonincreasing in positive \(v\), because \(\log(r_v/s_v)\) is concave and has value zero at index zero. Beyond the finite support, \(r_v-s_v<0\). Equality of the total sums rules out an initial negative difference followed only by negative differences. Therefore
   \(\sum_{v=0}^m r_v\ge\sum_{v=0}^m s_v\)
for every nonnegative integer \(m\). Before the crossing, each prefix difference is a sum of nonnegative terms; after the crossing, it is the negative of the remaining nonpositive tail difference.

For a nonincreasing summable sequence \(z_v\), the sum \(\sum_v(z_v-u)_+\), for \(u>0\), is the maximum over prefixes of \(\sum_{v=0}^m z_v-(m+1)u\), with the empty prefix also permitted. The preceding prefix inequality consequently gives
   \(\sum_v(r_v-u)_+\ge\sum_v(s_v-u)_+\)
for \(0<u<1\). This implies
   \(\sum_v r_v\log r_v\ge\sum_v s_v\log s_v\).
For an explicit justification, use the identity
   \(z\log z=\int_0^1[(z-u)_+-z(1-u)]\,du/u\)
for \(0\le z\le1\), with \(0\log0=0\). The linear terms cancel because the total sums agree. The remaining integrand difference is nonnegative. The identity may be summed and integrated here: its integrand for each individual \(z\) is nonpositive, the \(r\) sequence has finite support, and \(\sum_v(-s_v\log s_v)<\infty\). Thus Tonelli's theorem for the negative integrands and finiteness justify the subtraction.

It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j
 &=\frac{4}{1+2R}\sum_{v\ge1}r_v(-\log r_v)\\
 &\le\frac{4R(1+R)}{1+2R}\log(1+1/R)\\
 &=\frac{\rho_j(2-\rho_j)}{1-\rho_j}
       \log\frac{2-\rho_j}{\rho_j}.
\end{aligned}
\]

To bound this by 2, the convexity of \(x\mapsto1/x\) on \([R,R+1]\) gives the trapezoidal bound
   \(\log(1+1/R)=\int_R^{R+1}dx/x\le(1/R+1/(R+1))/2\).
Substitution gives exactly 2.

Evenness of \(Q_j\) and oddness of \(T_j\) give \(\mathbb E_{Q_j}T_j=0\). Direct cancellation of the square roots gives

\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_j}\cosh T_j
 =\frac1{2H_j}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}
      (b_{j+v}+b_{j-v})
 =\frac1{H_j}\sum_{v\in\mathcal U_j}b_{j+v}
 \le\frac W{H_j}.
\]

Since \(t^2\le2(\cosh t-1)\), this already proves
   \(\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)\le2(W/H_j-1)\).
For the refined estimate, the function \(u\mapsto[\operatorname{arcosh}u]^2\) is increasing and concave on \([1,\infty)\). Indeed, with \(u=\cosh t\), its derivative is \(2t/\sinh t\), decreasing in \(t\ge0\) because \(\sinh t-t\cosh t\le0\); the inequality follows by differentiating that expression. Condition on the event \(v\ne0\), which has probability \(\rho_j\). The preceding hyperbolic-cosine identity bounds its conditional mean by \(1+(W/H_j-1)/\rho_j\). Jensen's inequality proves the stated arcosh estimate.

It remains only to express these exact bounds in terms of \(g\). At any maximizing center, \(H_j\ge W/2\). For \(0<\rho_j\le1/2\), the curvature bound gives
   \(\mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j\le3\rho_j\log(2/\rho_j)\).
Also \(\operatorname{arcosh}u\le\log(2u)\) for \(u\ge1\), so the refined tilt bound gives
   \(\operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)\le\rho_j[\log(4/\rho_j)]^2\)
when \(H_j\ge W/2\).

If \(0<g\le g_0\), then \(\rho_j\le5g\le1/20\). The functions \(x\log(2/x)\) and \(x[\log(4/x)]^2\) are increasing on \((0,1/20]\), as is seen by differentiation. Hence

\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_j}\Delta_j
 \le15g\log(e/g),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{Q_j}(T_j)
 \le5g[\log(e/g)]^2.
\]

If \(g>g_0\), the atom deficit is at most 1 and each moment is at most 2. Since \(g<1\) and \(\log(e/g)\ge1\), these bounds are dominated by the three asserted expressions with \(C=16+g_0^{-2}\): one has \(Cg^2\ge1\), and \(Cg\ge2\) because \(g_0\le1/100\). At \(g=0\), the equality characterization makes each maximizing law concentrate at displacement zero. This proves all statements.
