## Statement

Let $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge2$, be a full-dimensional simplex of volume $V$ and centroid $g$. Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, and let $\rho_d$ be the reflected simplex profile minimum. Let $A\subseteq T$ be compact convex with $0<a=|A|\le\rho_dV$. The convex hull of $\lbrace-1\rbrace\times A$, $\lbrace0\rbrace\times T$, and $\lbrace1\rbrace\times T$ is full dimensional and has exactly these integer fibers. In the sum of $d$-dimensional volumes on those fibers, using all closed ambient halfspaces, the raw maximum over the mixed-integer points in the hull is $\Gamma_d V+a$, attained only at $(0,g)$. Its normalized maximum for $\alpha=a/V$ is $(\Gamma_d+\alpha)/(2+\alpha)$, with right derivative $(2-\Gamma_d)/4$ at $\alpha=0$. The raw right derivative with respect to $a$ is one.

## Proof

All continuous coordinates of the hull lie in $T$, so the presence of the full middle generator gives exactly $T$ at base zero. The endpoints are extreme projection levels and therefore have precisely their original fibers. No other integer base lies in $[-1,1]$. The hull is full dimensional because it contains $\lbrace0\rbrace\times T$ and a point at base one. Every fiber over $[-1,1]$ also contains $A$, since each of the three generating fibers contains $A$. In particular every point in the interior of $A$ lies in the continuous interior of every real fiber.

For any closed halfspace containing $(0,g)$ it suffices to bound the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through $(0,g)$ and which is contained in the original one. If the continuous normal is zero, the through-point cap includes the whole middle fiber and at least one endpoint, of total mass at least $V+a\ge\Gamma_d V+a$. Otherwise normalize its continuous normal to a unit vector $u$ and write the cap as
$$
u\cdot(x-g)+\beta z\ge0.
$$
Use the reflected profile notation $b=u\cdot g$, $\ell=\min_T u\cdot x$, $f(u)=|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge b\rbrace|/V$, and $R(u)=|T\cap\lbrace u\cdot x\ge2b-\ell\rbrace|/V$. The $+1$ threshold is $c=b-\beta$ and the $-1$ threshold is $2b-c$. If $c\le2b-\ell$, the $+1$ trace has mass at least $VR(u)$. If $c>2b-\ell$, the $-1$ trace contains all of $A$, since $2b-c<\ell$. At equality of $c$ with $2b-\ell$ the $-1$ trace also contains all of $A$ by closedness. Hence the sum of the two actual endpoint contributions is at least $\min\lbrace a,VR(u)\rbrace$. The mass of the single ambient cap is therefore at least
$$
V[f(u)+\min\lbrace\alpha,R(u)\rbrace]\ \ge\ V(\Gamma_d+\alpha).
$$
Indeed, if $R(u)\ge\alpha$, use $f(u)\ge\Gamma_d$; otherwise use $f(u)+R(u)\ge\Gamma_d+\rho_d$ and $\alpha\le\rho_d$. The reflected profile lemma applies because the two large fibers are the same full-dimensional simplex and the continuous normal has unit length.

Let $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$ be the barycentric functions on $T$ and $m=d+1$. The $2m$ closed ambient caps
$$
P_i=\lbrace\lambda_i(x)-2z\ge1/m\rbrace,\qquad
Q_i=\lbrace\lambda_i(x)+2(z-1)\ge1/m\rbrace
$$
cover the actual source: at base zero the $P_i$ cover $T$ since $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, at base one the $Q_i$ do the same, and all the $P_i$ contain the entire $-1$ fiber. Since $0\le\lambda_i\le1$ on $A$ and $T$, every $P_i$ excludes the $+1$ fiber, while every $Q_i$ excludes the other two. Each canonical own cap has mass $\Gamma_dV$ by homothety. Thus the $P_i$ have mass $\Gamma_dV+a$ and the $Q_i$ have mass $\Gamma_dV$, giving the global upper bound.

For a middle point $(0,y)$ with $y\ne g$, some $\lambda_i(y)>1/m$. Raise the threshold of $P_i$ to $\lambda_i(y)\le1$; the endpoint traces stay full at $-1$ and empty at $+1$, so the cap has mass $V(1-\lambda_i(y))^d+a<\Gamma_dV+a$. At any point over $+1$ a covering $Q_i$ has mass $\Gamma_dV<\Gamma_dV+a$. At any point over $-1$ the vertical cap $z\le-1$ has mass $a<\Gamma_dV+a$. Thus the unique maximizer is $(0,g)$.

The exact total is $2V+a$. Without the added endpoint the maximum is $\Gamma_d V$, since each simplex centroid gives at least its own ordinary centroid mass (or a whole fiber for a pure base normal), and a finite barycentric cap cover isolating each fiber gives the matching upper bound. Consequently the displayed formulas extend to that baseline, and
$$
\frac{\Gamma_d+\alpha}{2+\alpha}
=\frac{\Gamma_d}{2}+\frac{2-\Gamma_d}{4}\alpha+O(\alpha^2).
$$
The derivative of $\Gamma_d V+a$ with respect to $a$ is one, regardless of how the contained subsets vary.
