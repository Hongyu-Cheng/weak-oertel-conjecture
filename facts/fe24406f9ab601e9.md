## Statement

Let $S$ be a tetrahedron with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_3$, and let $H_i=\{\lambda_i\ge1/4\}$ be its four canonical centroid caps. Let $R$ be any finite or infinite union in $S$ of strict traces $a\lambda_0+b\lambda_1>c\lambda_2+d\lambda_3$ with $a,b\ge0$, $a+b>0$, $c,d>0$. Assume $R\subseteq\{\lambda_2+\lambda_3\le1/4\}$. The pushforward of ordinary volume on $R$ to $t=\lambda_1/(\lambda_0+\lambda_1)$ has a convex density on $[0,1]$, and
$$
\sum_{i=0}^3|R\cap H_i|\le\frac32|R|.
$$
For $|R|>0$ this is strictly below $(27/16)|R|$, by at least $(3/16)|R|$.

## Proof

The claim is trivial for an empty union. On the tube containing $R$, write
$$
(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=\frac{(1-t,t,y,z)}{1+y+z},\qquad y,z\ge0,\quad 0\le t\le1.
$$
The volume Jacobian in the chart $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ is $(1+y+z)^{-4}$: the $t$ derivative of $\lambda_1$ is $(1+y+z)^{-1}$, while the determinant of $(y,z)\mapsto(\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ is $(1+y+z)^{-3}$. Physical volume differs by a fixed positive constant. Each trace is $cy+dz<a+(b-a)t$. Set $y=r\cos\alpha$, $z=r\sin\alpha$, $k=\cos\alpha+\sin\alpha$, $0\le\alpha\le\pi/2$. The union at fixed $t,\alpha$ fills the radial interval up to
$$
R_\alpha(t)=\sup_{\rm traces}\frac{a+(b-a)t}{c\cos\alpha+d\sin\alpha},
$$
with radial endpoints irrelevant to volume. This nonnegative function is convex in $t$, and jointly measurable because the supremum is lower semicontinuous. The tube hypothesis gives $k R_\alpha(t)\le1/3$ since $\lambda_2+\lambda_3=kr/(1+kr)$. On this range the increasing function $W_k(r)=\int_0^r u(1+ku)^{-4}\,du$ is convex, because $W_k''(r)=(1-3kr)/(1+kr)^5\ge0$. Thus $\int_0^{\pi/2}W_k(R_\alpha(t))\,d\alpha$ is a convex nonnegative volume density in $t$, up to the fixed positive Jacobian constant.

For any such density $f$, the function $g(t)=f(t)+f(1-t)$ is convex and symmetric and hence nonincreasing on $[0,1/2]$. Therefore $\int_{1/4}^{1/2}g\le\int_0^{1/4}g$, equivalently the middle half of $[0,1]$ carries at most half the mass. Inside the tube, $H_2,H_3$ meet $R$ only on volume-null boundaries. Also $H_0$ implies $t\le3/4$, while $H_1$ implies $t\ge1/4$. The cap multiplicity is thus at most $1+\mathbf1_{[1/4,3/4]}(t)$ almost everywhere in $R$. Integrating against the density gives the claim and $27/16-3/2=3/16$.
