## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer, put \(n=N-1\), and let \(a<b\) be real numbers. For \(1\le i\le n\), let \(l_i,u_i:[a,b]\to\mathbb R\) be continuous functions such that \(l_i\) is convex, \(u_i\) is concave, \(l_i(t)\le u_i(t)\) on \([a,b]\), and \(l_i(t)<u_i(t)\) on \((a,b)\). Define

\[
P=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
a\le t\le b,\quad l_i(t)\le x_i\le u_i(t)\text{ for }1\le i\le n\right\},
\qquad D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N,
\]

and assume \(D\ne\varnothing\). For any \(h\in\mathbb R\), assign each original point \(I\in D\) the weight \(w_I=e^{-(I_N-h)^2}\), and put \(W=\sum_{I\in D}w_I\).

There exist an integer height \(s^\star\in[a,b]\) and an original point \(J\in D\) with \(J_N=s^\star\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s^\star}}w_I
\ge \frac{W}{160\cdot2^N}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(n=N-1\) counts the horizontal coordinates. For \(n=0\), the empty product is one and the product of zero horizontal coordinate sets is the single empty tuple. Thus the statement includes \(N=1\). All lattice points on the boundaries are included, and integer heights with empty sections are allowed.

## Proof

Write \(L_i(t)=u_i(t)-l_i(t)\). Each \(L_i\) is continuous and concave, is nonnegative on \([a,b]\), and is positive on \((a,b)\). The set \(P\) is bounded because all its coordinate bounds are continuous on the compact interval \([a,b]\). Hence \(D\) is finite and \(W>0\).

Let \(\mathcal T=[a,b]\cap\mathbb Z\), and let \(E\subseteq\mathcal T\) be the set of occupied integer heights. The exact original support at every \(s\in\mathcal T\) is

\[
\{I\in D:I_N=s\}
=\prod_{i=1}^n
\{z\in\mathbb Z:\lceil l_i(s)\rceil\le z\le\lfloor u_i(s)\rfloor\}
\times\{s\}.
\tag{2}
\]

There are no occupied heights outside \(\mathcal T\), and \(D\ne\varnothing\) implies that \(E\) is a nonempty finite set. Formula (2) retains both endpoints whenever they are integers. A zero-length coordinate interval gives a singleton when its common endpoint is an integer and an empty coordinate set otherwise.

For \(s\in E\), define
\(p_i(s)=\lceil l_i(s)\rceil\) and
\(n_i(s)=\lfloor u_i(s)\rfloor-p_i(s)+1\ge1\).
Put \(w_s=e^{-(s-h)^2}\) and \(W_s=w_s\prod_{i=1}^n n_i(s)\). Thus \(W=\sum_{s\in E}W_s\). Define
\(r_i(s)=\lfloor(n_i(s)-1)/2\rfloor\) and \(o_i(s)=2r_i(s)+1\). The original integer core, its original integer center, and its total original weight are

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_s&=\prod_{i=1}^n\{p_i(s),p_i(s)+1,\ldots,p_i(s)+2r_i(s)\}\times\{s\},\\
J_s&=(p_1(s)+r_1(s),\ldots,p_n(s)+r_n(s),s),\\
R_s&=w_s\prod_{i=1}^n o_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The core is contained in the original row because \(o_i(s)\le n_i(s)\). It is centrally symmetric about \(J_s\), and \(J_s\in D\). Any closed halfspace containing \(J_s\) contains at least one member of each two-point pair \(I,2J_s-I\) in \(C_s\): otherwise both members, and hence their midpoint, would belong to the open complementary halfspace. The fixed point \(J_s\) is also contained. Since all points of this core have equal original weight, every such halfspace satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s}}w_I\ge \frac{R_s}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

This includes a singleton core. It remains to bound \(W\) by \(160\cdot2^n\max_{s\in E}R_s\).

We first record two coordinate estimates, with the height \(s\in E\) suppressed in their notation. If \(n_i\le12\), then

\[
n_i\le2o_i^{3/4}.
\tag{5}
\]

For odd \(n_i\), one has \(o_i=n_i\le11<16\), which gives (5). For even \(n_i=2j\le12\), one has \(o_i=2j-1\), and (5) is equivalent to \(j^4\le(2j-1)^3\). At \(j=1\) there is equality. On the real interval \([2,6]\),
\(\frac{d}{dr}\frac{r^4}{(2r-1)^3}
=\frac{2r^3(r-2)}{(2r-1)^4}\ge0\).
Consequently, for the remaining integers \(2\le j\le6\),
\(j^4/(2j-1)^3\le6^4/11^3=1296/1331<1\).
This proves (5) by exact inequalities.

The floor and ceiling bounds give \(L_i-1\le n_i\le L_i+1\), while the definition of \(o_i\) gives \(o_i\ge n_i-1\). Therefore, whenever \(L_i\ge6\),

\[
n_i\le\frac76L_i,
\qquad
\frac23L_i\le L_i-2\le n_i-1\le o_i.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, \(L_i\le(3/2)o_i\). Interpolating the first estimate in (6) with this last estimate gives

\[
n_i\le \frac76 L_i
\le \frac76\left(\frac32\right)^{3/4}o_i^{3/4}L_i^{1/4}
\qquad(L_i\ge6).
\tag{7}
\]

We next prove the Gaussian comparison needed for heights with gaps. Let \([p,q]\) be a compact real interval, let \(F\) be a positive continuous function on it, and suppose that \(\log F(t)+t^2\) is concave. For every nonempty set \(E'\subseteq[p,q]\cap\mathbb Z\),

\[
\sum_{s\in E'}F(s)^{1/4}
\le10\max_{s\in E'}F(s)^{1/4}.
\tag{8}
\]

The singleton-interval case is immediate. In the other case, set \(g=\log F\) and choose a real maximizer \(\xi\in[p,q]\) of \(g\). Concavity of \(g(t)+t^2\) means that

\[
g((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)
\ge (1-\lambda)g(x)+\lambda g(y)
+\lambda(1-\lambda)(x-y)^2
\quad(0\le\lambda\le1).
\tag{9}
\]

For \(\xi<x<y\), put \(\lambda=(x-\xi)/(y-\xi)\). Apply (9) to \(\xi,y\), and use \(g(\xi)\ge g(x)\). Rearranging gives

\[
g(x)-g(y)\ge(1-\lambda)(y-\xi)^2
=(y-x)(y-\xi)\ge(y-x)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

The same inequality for \(x=\xi<y\) follows by continuity as \(x\) decreases to \(\xi\) from within \((\xi,y)\). Applying this argument to \(g(-t)\) on \([-q,-p]\), for which \(g(-t)+t^2\) is also concave, proves the reflected estimate on the left of \(\xi\). Thus moving a distance \(\delta\) farther from \(\xi\) along either side decreases \(g\) by at least \(\delta^2\), measured from any nearer point on that same side. This reasoning allows a maximizer at either endpoint and uses no derivatives of \(F\).

Split \(E'\) into its points at least \(\xi\) and its points less than \(\xi\). On a nonempty right side, list the points in increasing order \(s_0<s_1<\cdots\); the point \(s_0\) is the nearest occupied point on that side. Since these are distinct integers, \(s_j-s_0\ge j\). Inequality (10) gives
\(F(s_j)^{1/4}\le F(s_0)^{1/4}e^{-j^2/4}\).
On a nonempty left side, listing its points in decreasing order gives the same bound from its nearest occupied point by the reflected estimate. Moreover,

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-j^2/4}
\le\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-j/4}
=\frac1{1-e^{-1/4}}<5,
\tag{11}
\]

because \(j^2\ge j\) for these integers and \(e^{1/4}>1+1/4=5/4\). Each side therefore contributes at most five times its nearest occupied value of \(F^{1/4}\), which is at most the maximum over \(E'\). Empty sides contribute zero. This proves (8), including arbitrary missing heights; the value \(F(\xi)\) does not replace the sampled maximum in (8).

We now construct the width patterns. Let \(\tau=(a+b)/2\), set \(M_i=\max_{t\in[a,b]}L_i(t)\), and let \(G=\{i:M_i\ge12\}\). For every \(i\in G\) one has

\[
L_i(\tau)\ge \frac{M_i}{2}\ge6.
\tag{12}
\]

To verify the first inequality, choose \(t_i\in[a,b]\) with \(L_i(t_i)=M_i\). If \(t_i\le\tau\), concavity on the segment from \(t_i\) to \(b\), together with \(L_i(b)\ge0\), yields
\(L_i(\tau)\ge\frac{b-\tau}{b-t_i}M_i\ge M_i/2\).
If \(t_i>\tau\), concavity on the segment from \(a\) to \(t_i\), together with \(L_i(a)\ge0\), yields
\(L_i(\tau)\ge\frac{\tau-a}{t_i-a}M_i\ge M_i/2\).
Both denominators are positive in their respective cases, and these arguments also cover a maximizer at an endpoint.

For \(i\in G\), the set \(\{t\in[a,b]:L_i(t)\ge6\}\) is a closed interval containing \(\tau\), by continuity, concavity, and (12). Define the wide-coordinate set at every integer height \(s\in\mathcal T\) by
\(A_s=\{i\in G:L_i(s)\ge6\}\).
As \(s\) increases through heights \(s\le\tau\), these sets are nested increasing: if a superlevel interval contains \(s\) and \(\tau\), it contains every intervening height. As \(s\) increases through heights \(s>\tau\), these sets are nested decreasing, by the corresponding interval property on the right.

Partition each of the two disjoint integer intervals
\(\{s\in\mathcal T:s\le\tau\}\) and
\(\{s\in\mathcal T:s>\tau\}\)
into its maximal nonempty consecutive integer intervals on which \(A_s\) is constant. Empty sides produce no intervals. Denote the resulting partition of \(\mathcal T\) by \(\mathcal B\). For \(B\in\mathcal B\), write \(A_B\) for its fixed wide-coordinate set and \(k_B=|A_B|\). Within either nested chain, different successive sets have different cardinalities. Consequently, if \(b_k\) is the number of blocks with \(k_B=k\), then

\[
b_k\le2\qquad(0\le k\le n).
\tag{13}
\]

The split assigns an integer midpoint to the left side only. The argument counts the actual integer blocks, allows coincident threshold events and equality rows, and requires no bound on the number of corners of the \(L_i\).

At an occupied height \(s\in B\), every coordinate outside \(A_B\) has \(n_i(s)\le12\). Indeed, if \(i\notin G\), then \(L_i(s)\le M_i<12\), so the integer \(n_i(s)\le L_i(s)+1<13\) is at most twelve. If \(i\in G\setminus A_B\), then \(L_i(s)<6\), so the same argument gives \(n_i(s)\le6\). Thus (5) applies to all coordinates outside \(A_B\), and (6)--(7) apply to all coordinates in \(A_B\).

For a block \(B\) with integer endpoints \(p=\min B\), \(q=\max B\), define

\[
F_B(t)=e^{-(t-h)^2}\prod_{i\in A_B}L_i(t)
\qquad(p\le t\le q).
\tag{14}
\]

Every factor with \(i\in A_B\) is at least six throughout \([p,q]\), because it is at least six at both endpoints and is concave. Thus \(F_B\) is positive and continuous. A positive concave function has concave logarithm: for \(0\le\lambda\le1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log L_i((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)
&\ge\log\bigl((1-\lambda)L_i(x)+\lambda L_i(y)\bigr)\\
&\ge(1-\lambda)\log L_i(x)+\lambda\log L_i(y),
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

where the second inequality is concavity of the scalar logarithm. It follows that
\(\log F_B(t)+t^2=2ht-h^2+\sum_{i\in A_B}\log L_i(t)\)
is concave on \([p,q]\). The case \(p=q\) causes no difficulty.

Put \(R_{\max}=\max_{s\in E}R_s>0\). Consider a block with \(E\cap B\ne\varnothing\), and write \(k=k_B\). Multiplying (5) for its \(n-k\) other coordinates and (7) for its \(k\) wide coordinates, with the Gaussian factor kept unchanged, gives for every \(s\in E\cap B\)

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_s
&\le2^{n-k}\left(\frac76\right)^k
\left(\frac32\right)^{3k/4}
R_s^{3/4}F_B(s)^{1/4},\\
F_B(s)
&\le\left(\frac32\right)^kR_s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The Gaussian factor on the first right-hand side is exactly \(w_s^{3/4}w_s^{1/4}=w_s\). For the second inequality, use \(L_i(s)\le(3/2)o_i(s)\) on wide coordinates and \(o_i(s)\ge1\) on all other coordinates.

Apply (8) to the arbitrary occupied subset \(E\cap B\), and then use (16). This yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s\in E\cap B}W_s
&\le
2^{n-k}\left(\frac76\right)^k
\left(\frac32\right)^{3k/4}
R_{\max}^{3/4}
\sum_{s\in E\cap B}F_B(s)^{1/4}\\
&\le
10\cdot2^{n-k}\left(\frac76\right)^k
\left(\frac32\right)^{3k/4}
R_{\max}^{3/4}
\left(\left(\frac32\right)^kR_{\max}\right)^{1/4}\\
&=10\cdot2^n\left(\frac78\right)^kR_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

A block with \(E\cap B=\varnothing\) contributes zero, so it satisfies the same upper bound without defining a maximum on an empty set. Summing over the exact partition and applying (13), we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&\le10\cdot2^nR_{\max}
\sum_{k=0}^n b_k\left(\frac78\right)^k\\
&\le20\cdot2^nR_{\max}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac78\right)^k
=160\cdot2^nR_{\max}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Choose \(s^\star\in E\) maximizing \(R_s\), and set \(J=J_{s^\star}\). Combining (4) and (18), for every closed halfspace containing this original point,

\[
\sum_{\substack{I\in D\cap H\\I_N=s^\star}}w_I
\ge\frac{R_{\max}}2
\ge\frac{W}{320\cdot2^n}
=\frac{W}{160\cdot2^N}.
\]

All steps remain valid for \(n=0\): every product is empty, \(E=\mathcal T\), the cores are singleton points, and at most two blocks occur, each with \(k=0\). Empty integer rows and zero-width boundary coordinates were handled by (2), while (3) constructs every center directly in the original integer row. No change of the ambient lattice or of the original weights is used. This proves (1).
