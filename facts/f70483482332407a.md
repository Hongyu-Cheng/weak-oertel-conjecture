## Statement

In $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ take the lifted convex hull of centered intervals at $0,1,2,3$ of lengths $(a,b,b,a)$, with $0<a<b/2$. The raw maximum mixed depth is $Q=b/2+a$ and the normalized maximum is $\beta=1/4+a/[4(a+b)]$. Any convex extension preserving the old fibers has no point at a new integer base. A preserving extension to base $-r<0$ or $3+r$ must have $r\le a/(b-a)$.

For $a=1,b=10$, the four open caps $\pm x-6(z-1)>-1/4$ and $\pm x+6(z-2)>-1/4$ each have raw mass $25/4<13/2$. Their closed complements form a full-dimensional diamond with projection $[25/24,47/24]$ and attained group contacts $(25/24,0)$ and $(47/24,0)$. The two groups cover every integer plane. The exact raw depth superlevel at $13/2$ is nonempty, avoids the mixed lattice and is contained in the diamond.

Replacing both endpoint lengths by $(1-t)a$ for $0\le t\le1$ gives compatible actual sources throughout, ending with two point endpoint fibers. The normalized maximum strictly decreases to $1/4$ according to the same formula with $(1-t)a$ in place of $a$.

## Proof

The upper roof obtained by linear interpolation of the interval radii is concave, and the lower roof is convex. Thus the hull realizes all four intervals, including when $a=0$. Write $F_v(s)=\min(v,\max(0,v/2-s))$ for the length above level $s$ in a centered interval of length $v$, including $F_0=0$. An upward through-cap at $(1,0)$ with threshold $x=s(z-1)$ has mass $b/2+F_b(s)+F_a(-s)+F_a(2s)$. For $s\ge0$, $F_b(s)\ge F_a(s)$ and $F_a(-s)+F_a(s)=a$. For $s<0$, $F_a(2s)\ge F_a(s)$ gives the same lower bound $b/2+a$. Reflection in $x$ covers the opposite orientation. A pure-base through-cap at $(1,0)$ contains its entire length-$b$ fiber, and on the left side also the length-$a$ fiber; the right cap contains both length-$b$ fibers. Thus pure-base masses are at least $b/2+a$ for $0\le a\le b/2$. Any containing cap can be tightened to a through-cap without increasing mass.

For $s>b$, each of the two caps $\pm x\ge s(z-1)$ has mass $b/2+a$, and their union covers every integer plane $z\le1$. Their base reflections about $3/2$ cover all planes $z\ge2$ with the same masses. This gives the global upper bound, matching the lower bound at $(1,0)$ and its reflection. Divide by $2(a+b)$ for the normalized formula.

If an extension contains a point at base $-r$, its segment combinations with the length-$b$ interval at $1$ produce at base $0$ an interval of length $rb/(r+1)$. Preservation forces $r\le a/(b-a)<1$. Reflect to handle the other side. Every new integer base has $r\ge1$ on one side.

In the numerical specialization, each listed cap captures a full length-$1$ endpoint interval and length $5+1/4$ in one middle interval, with all other traces empty. Their closed complements give $|x|\le6(z-1)-1/4$ and $|x|\le6(2-z)-1/4$, which show the projection, the nonempty interior, and the two contact points. Each pair of continuous normals is opposite, and averaging its margins gives the corresponding projected-facet inequality. Strict violation of a projected facet gives coverage of the whole continuous plane at that base. Central symmetry of the mixed measure about $(3/2,0)$ gives raw depth $11$ there: any containing halfspace includes a through-halfspace of mass at least $11$, and a nonvertical through-halfspace has mass exactly $11$ by its null boundary. The actual mixed maximum is $6<13/2$. Any point in one of the four open caps lies in a parallel closed cap of mass at most $25/4$, so the exact depth superlevel is in their common closed complement.

Finally the roof argument and depth computation hold for each $a_t=(1-t)a\ge0$. The derivative of $(b/2+a_t)/[2(b+a_t)]$ with respect to $a_t$ is $b/[4(b+a_t)^2]>0$. Hence contraction strictly decreases normalized depth to $1/4$.
