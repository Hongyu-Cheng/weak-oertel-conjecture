## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body such that every nonempty vertical real fiber is a Euclidean ball: for every \(x\) in the projection of \(C\),

\[
C_x:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(x,y)\in C\}=c(x)+r(x)B_2^d,
\qquad r(x)\ge0.
\]

No affineness assumption is imposed on \(c\). Set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\), assume \(S\ne\varnothing\), and use mixed-integer volume and halfspace depth exactly as in the locked problem. There exists one actual point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), fixed before all halfspaces, satisfying

\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge\frac1{512}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{1}
\]

Thus this class satisfies the locked \(n=1\) inequality with the absolute constant \(c_0=1/256\in(0,1/e)\). The proof uses accepted theorem 711f1969d2e61d1c with its actual-fiber centroids and covariances, unchanged original volume weights, and an explicit normalization map below.

## Proof

Write \(I\subset\mathbb R\) for the projection of \(C\). It is a compact interval. Convexity gives, for \(x_0,x_1\in I\), \(t\in[0,1]\), and \(x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1\),

\[
(1-t)C_{x_0}+tC_{x_1}\subseteq C_{x_t},
\qquad
\|c(x_t)-(1-t)c(x_0)-tc(x_1)\|_2
+(1-t)r(x_0)+tr(x_1)\le r(x_t).
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, the Minkowski sum on the left is the ball with center \((1-t)c(x_0)+tc(x_1)\) and radius \((1-t)r(x_0)+tr(x_1)\). A ball with center \(u\) and radius \(s\ge0\) is contained in a ball with center \(v\) and radius \(r\ge0\) only if \(\|u-v\|_2+s\le r\), by taking the farthest point of the first ball from \(v\). The same statement includes radius-zero balls. This proves (2), and in particular \(r\) is concave on \(I\).

Use the actual-fiber notation from 711f1969d2e61d1c:

\[
w_z=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
D_+=\{z\in\mathbb Z:w_z>0\},\qquad
W=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S).
\]

If \(W=0\), any point of the nonempty \(S\) proves (1). Assume \(W>0\). Let \(\kappa_d=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_2^d)\) and put \(r_z=r(z)\), so \(w_z=\kappa_d r_z^d\) for \(z\in D_+\). Compactness makes \(D_+\) finite. It is a nonempty consecutive interval of integers: between two positive radii, concavity gives positive radius at every intermediate integer. The restriction \(z\mapsto r_z\) is a positive concave sequence on this interval.

Set \(R=\max_{z\in D_+}r_z>0\), choose \(k\in D_+\) with \(r_k=R\), and set \(\delta=1/(24d)\). Define

\[
J=\{z\in D_+:r_z\ge(1-\delta)R\}
=[a_-,a_+]\cap\mathbb Z,
\qquad
m=|J|,\qquad W_J=\sum_{z\in J}w_z.
\]

Here \(J\) is a nonempty consecutive integer interval and \(k\in J\). We first prove the original-volume estimate

\[
\frac{W_J}{W}\ge\frac{23}{1176}>\frac1{64}.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(t\in[0,1]\), let \(N(t)=|\{z\in D_+:r_z\ge tR\}|\). We claim

\[
N(t)\le m\left(1+\frac{2(1-t)}\delta\right).
\tag{4}
\]

If there are no elements of \(D_+\) left of \(a_-\), that side contributes nothing outside \(J\). Otherwise \(a_--1\in D_+\) and \(r_{a_--1}<(1-\delta)R\). Put \(s=k-a_-+1\le m\). For every \(z\in D_+\) with \(z\le a_--1\), concavity on the segment from \(z\) to \(k\), evaluated at \(a_--1=k-s\), gives

\[
r_{a_--1}\ge\frac{s}{k-z}r_z+
\left(1-\frac{s}{k-z}\right)R,
\qquad
R-r_z\ge\frac{k-z}{s}(R-r_{a_--1})
>\frac{\delta R(k-z)}s.
\]

Consequently \(r_z\ge tR\) implies \(k-z<(1-t)s/\delta\le(1-t)m/\delta\). These distances are distinct positive integers, so the number of such left-side elements is at most \((1-t)m/\delta\). The right side is identical: if it is nonempty outside \(J\), use \(a_++1\in D_+\), \(r_{a_++1}<(1-\delta)R\), and \(s=a_++1-k\le m\), obtaining \(z-k<(1-t)m/\delta\). At most \(m\) elements lie in \(J\), proving (4).

Integrating the finite sum of the identities \(r_z^d=R^d\int_0^{r_z/R}d\,t^{d-1}\,dt\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac W{\kappa_d R^d}
&=\int_0^1d\,t^{d-1}N(t)\,dt\\
&\le m\left(1+\frac2{\delta(d+1)}\right)
=m\left(1+\frac{48d}{d+1}\right)\le49m.
\end{aligned}
\]

On the other hand, Bernoulli's inequality yields \((1-\delta)^d\ge1-d\delta=23/24\), and therefore \(W_J\ge\kappa_d mR^d(1-\delta)^d\ge(23/24)\kappa_d mR^d\). This proves the first inequality in (3); the second follows from \(23\cdot64=1472>1176\). These estimates use the largest integer radius only, so a larger radius at a noninteger point causes no difficulty.

If \(m=1\), take the actual point \(\mathbf y^\star=(k,c(k))\in S\). Every ambient closed halfspace containing it retains at least half of the ball \(C_k\). For a nonzero continuous normal this follows from central symmetry of the ball and a parallel boundary moved through its center; for a zero continuous normal the entire chosen fiber is retained. Thus \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge w_k/2=W_J/2>W/128\), which proves (1) in this case.

Assume \(m\ge2\), so \(a_-<a_+\). Let \(L\in\mathbb R^{d\times1}\) and \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) define the affine endpoint chord

\[
Lx+v=
\frac{a_+-x}{a_+-a_-}c(a_-)
+\frac{x-a_-}{a_+-a_-}c(a_+).
\]

For each \(z\in J\), apply (2) to the two endpoints and use \(r_z\le R\) and \(r_{a_-},r_{a_+}\ge(1-\delta)R\). This gives

\[
\|c(z)-Lz-v\|_2
\le r_z-
\frac{a_+-z}{a_+-a_-}r_{a_-}
-\frac{z-a_-}{a_+-a_-}r_{a_+}
\le\delta R.
\tag{5}
\]

The true centroid of \(C_z\) is \(c(z)\), and its uniform-volume covariance is \(\Sigma_z=r_z^2 I_d/(d+2)\). For completeness, central symmetry gives the centroid, and rotational symmetry makes covariance a scalar multiple of \(I_d\). For a uniform point in the radius-\(r_z\) ball, the radius distribution has cumulative distribution \((s/r_z)^d\) on \(0\le s\le r_z\); integrating its second moment gives \(\mathbb E\|Y-c(z)\|_2^2=dr_z^2/(d+2)\). Taking the trace proves the covariance formula.

The approximation error in (5), measured in this fiber's own standard-deviation units, satisfies

\[
\frac{\delta R\sqrt{d+2}}{r_z}
\le\frac{\sqrt{d+2}}{24d-1}
\le\frac{\sqrt3}{23}<\frac1{12}.
\tag{6}
\]

The middle inequality follows from \(d+2\le3d^2\) and \(24d-1\ge23d\) for integer \(d\ge1\); the strict last inequality follows from \(432<529\). Hence Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for every \(z\in J\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R^d\),

\[
|\beta\cdot(c(z)-Lz-v)|
\le\frac1{12}\frac{r_z\|\beta\|_2}{\sqrt{d+2}}
=\frac1{12}\sqrt{\beta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_z\beta}.
\tag{7}
\]

Now define the slab body \(C'=C\cap([a_-,a_+]\times\mathbb R^d)\), and let \(S'=C'\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)\). This is a convex body, not merely a lower-dimensional slice: (2) shows that it contains the sheared cylinder \(\{(x,Lx+v+u):a_-\le x\le a_+,\ \|u\|_2\le\min(r_{a_-},r_{a_+})\}\), whose radius is positive and whose base interval has positive length. Its positive-volume integer fibers are exactly those indexed by \(J\), unchanged from \(C\). Consequently \(\mathcal H_d(S')=W_J\), with precisely their original weights \(w_z\).

Apply accepted theorem 711f1969d2e61d1c to \(C'\) with \(n=1\), its \(D_+\) identified with \(J\), its centroid \(b_z\) identified with \(c(z)\), its covariance identified with the \(\Sigma_z\) computed above, its matrix \(L\) equal to this endpoint-chord matrix, its affine translation \(b\) identified with \(v\), and its \(\epsilon=1/12\). Thus there is no change of meaning or normalization in the predecessor's objects. Inequality (7) is exactly its approximation hypothesis. Also \(1/12<1/(4e)\), since \(e<3\). The predecessor gives one fixed actual point \(\mathbf y^\star\in S'\) such that, with its \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d>1/e\),

\[
h_{S'}(\mathbf y^\star)
\ge\frac{\gamma_d-1/12}{2}W_J
>\frac18W_J.
\tag{8}
\]

Here \(\gamma_d-1/12>1/3-1/12=1/4\). Because \(S'\subseteq S\), every halfspace containing this same point has at least as much original mixed volume in \(S\) as in \(S'\). Taking infima, (3) and (8) yield \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge h_{S'}(\mathbf y^\star)>W_J/8>W/512\). This proves (1) for \(W>0\), completing all cases. Finally \(1/256\in(0,1/e)\) follows again from \(e<3\).
