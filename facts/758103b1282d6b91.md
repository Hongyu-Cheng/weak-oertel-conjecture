## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{1+d}\) be a convex body, and put
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d,
\qquad
S:=C\cap\Lambda,
\qquad
T:=\mathcal H_d(S)>0.
\]
For \(\mathbf y\in S\), let \(h_S(\mathbf y)\) be the halfspace depth from the locked problem. Define the normalized centerpoint depth by
\[
\mathcal F(S):=\frac1T\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y).
\]
The maximum exists by accepted fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\). With
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
one has
\[
\mathcal F(S)
\geq
\frac12\left[
\gamma_d-(1-\gamma_d)\log\frac1{1-\gamma_d}
\right].
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently, there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{1+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\geq
\frac12\left[
\gamma_d-(1-\gamma_d)\log\frac1{1-\gamma_d}
\right]T.
\tag{2}
\]

The universal-constant consequence of (1) is
\[
\mathcal F(S)\geq
\frac12\left[
\frac1e-\left(1-\frac1e\right)\log\frac e{e-1}
\right]
=0.038970725974\ldots.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, for
\[
c_0:=
\frac1e-\left(1-\frac1e\right)\log\frac e{e-1}
=0.077941451949\ldots\in(0,1/e),
\]
the point in (2) satisfies
\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\geq c_0\,2^{-1}T.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(d=2\), (1) gives
\[
\mathcal F(S)
\geq
\frac12\left[
\frac49-\frac59\log\frac95
\right]
=0.058948148638\ldots.
\tag{5}
\]

## Proof

For each \(i\in\mathbb Z\), define the compact convex fiber and its volume by
\[
A_i:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(i,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_i:=\operatorname{vol}_d(A_i),
\]
with \(a_i=0\) when \(A_i=\varnothing\). The projection of \(C\) onto its first coordinate is compact, so only finitely many \(A_i\) are nonempty and
\[
T=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}a_i.
\tag{6}
\]

We first record the sequence structure that will be used twice. Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^{1+d}\) be any compact convex set and let
\[
D_i:=\{\mathbf x:(i,\mathbf x)\in D\},
\qquad
s_i:=\operatorname{vol}_d(D_i).
\]
If \(s_r,s_t>0\) and \(r<i<t\), convexity gives
\[
\frac{t-i}{t-r}D_r+\frac{i-r}{t-r}D_t
\subseteq D_i.
\]
The Brunn-Minkowski inequality therefore gives \(s_i>0\). Hence the positive support of \((s_i)\) is an interval of integers. For three consecutive indices with \(s_{i-1}s_{i+1}>0\), convexity and Brunn-Minkowski give
\[
s_i^{1/d}
\geq
\frac{s_{i-1}^{1/d}+s_{i+1}^{1/d}}2
\geq
(s_{i-1}s_{i+1})^{1/(2d)}.
\]
If either neighboring term is zero, the resulting inequality is automatic. Thus
\[
s_i^2\geq s_{i-1}s_{i+1}
\qquad(i\in\mathbb Z),
\tag{7}
\]
so \((s_i)\) is log-concave in the standard discrete sense. On its positive support, the ratios \(s_i/s_{i-1}\) are nonincreasing by (7). They can cross \(1\) at most once, so \((s_i)\) is unimodal, with possible plateaus. This proves both log-concavity and unimodality for every sequence of fiber volumes considered below. In particular, it proves these properties for the full-fiber sequence \((a_i)\).

Choose \(j\) to be the first index in the positive support of \((a_i)\) for which
\[
\sum_{i\leq j}a_i\geq\frac T2.
\]
Then \(a_j>0\), and minimality within the positive support gives
\[
\sum_{i<j}a_i<\frac T2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_{i\leq j}a_i\geq\frac T2,
\qquad
\sum_{i\geq j}a_i>\frac T2.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(j\) is a weighted median supported on a positive-volume fiber. Since \(A_j\) is a \(d\)-dimensional compact convex set, its centroid
\[
\mathbf c_j:=\frac1{a_j}\int_{A_j}\mathbf x\,d\mathbf x
\]
belongs to \(A_j\). Set
\[
\mathbf y^\star:=(j,\mathbf c_j)\in S.
\tag{9}
\]

Accepted fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\) says that, even when mixed-integer measure charges a boundary hyperplane, it is enough to consider closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(\mathbf y^\star\). Fix such a halfspace and write it as
\[
H_0:=
\left\{
(z,\mathbf x):
\ell(z,\mathbf x):=
\alpha(z-j)+\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf x-\mathbf c_j\rangle
\geq0
\right\},
\tag{10}
\]
where \((\alpha,\mathbf v)\neq(0,\mathbf0)\).

First suppose that \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\), the vertical, fiber-separating case. Then \(\alpha\neq0\), and \(H_0\) contains exactly all integer fibers with \(i\geq j\) or exactly all integer fibers with \(i\leq j\), including the entire boundary fiber \(i=j\). The boundary can have mixed-integer mass \(a_j>0\), and (8) counts it on the closed side. Hence
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)\geq\frac T2.
\tag{11}
\]

Now suppose that \(\mathbf v\neq\mathbf0\), the nonvertical case, and define the opposite closed halfspace and its fiber sections by
\[
\overline H_0:=\{\ell\leq0\},
\qquad
B_i:=\{\mathbf x\in A_i:\ell(i,\mathbf x)\leq0\},
\qquad
b_i:=\operatorname{vol}_d(B_i).
\tag{12}
\]
The set \(C\cap\overline H_0\) is compact and convex. Applying the sequence argument above to this set proves that the bad-fiber sequence \((b_i)\) has interval positive support, is log-concave, and is unimodal.

Because \(\mathbf v\neq\mathbf0\), for every integer \(i\) the set
\[
\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:\ell(i,\mathbf x)=0\}
\]
is a genuine affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^d\) and has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Thus the two closed fiber sides overlap only in a null set, and
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}(a_i-b_i).
\tag{13}
\]
This is the precise boundary-mass distinction from (11): vertical boundaries may carry an entire positive-mass fiber, while nonvertical boundaries have zero mixed-integer mass because they have zero \(d\)-volume in every one of the finitely many fibers.

On the median fiber, \(H_0\) induces the closed halfspace
\[
\{\mathbf x:\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf x-\mathbf c_j\rangle\geq0\}
\]
through the centroid of \(A_j\). Grünbaum's inequality in dimension \(d\) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d\{\mathbf x\in A_j:
\langle\mathbf v,\mathbf x-\mathbf c_j\rangle\geq0\}
\geq\gamma_da_j.
\]
The cutting hyperplane has zero \(d\)-volume, so, with
\[
\theta:=1-\gamma_d\in(0,1),
\tag{14}
\]
we obtain
\[
b_j\leq\theta a_j.
\tag{15}
\]

Unimodality of \((b_i)\) implies that there is a sign
\(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\) such that
\[
b_{j+\sigma k}\leq b_j
\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed, if \(b_j>0\), choose a mode of \((b_i)\). If a mode lies at or to the left of \(j\), the sequence is nonincreasing to the right and one takes \(\sigma=+1\); if a mode lies at or to the right of \(j\), the sequence is nondecreasing up to \(j\) and one takes \(\sigma=-1\). If \(b_j=0\), interval positive support prevents positive terms on both sides of \(j\), so one of the two rays is identically zero and (16) again holds.

For every \(k\geq0\), (15), (16), and \(0\leq b_{j+\sigma k}\leq a_{j+\sigma k}\) give
\[
a_{j+\sigma k}-b_{j+\sigma k}
\geq
\bigl(a_{j+\sigma k}-\theta a_j\bigr)_+.
\tag{17}
\]
Define
\[
u_k:=\frac{a_{j+\sigma k}}{a_j}
\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{18}
\]
The sequence \((u_k)\) is nonnegative and summable, \(u_0=1\), its positive support is an initial interval of nonnegative integers, and it is log-concave by (7). Therefore all hypotheses of accepted fact \(772688f4504b1b5b\) hold. Invoking that fact with the exact parameter \(\theta=1-\gamma_d\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\geq0}
\bigl(a_{j+\sigma k}-\theta a_j\bigr)_+
&=
a_j\sum_{k\geq0}(u_k-\theta)_+\\
&\geq
\left(1-\theta-\theta\log\frac1\theta\right)
\sum_{k\geq0}a_{j+\sigma k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Both rays from \(j\) have full-fiber mass at least \(T/2\) by (8). Combining (13), (17), and (19) therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_0)
&\geq
\sum_{k\geq0}
\bigl(a_{j+\sigma k}-\theta a_j\bigr)_+\\
&\geq
\frac12\left(1-\theta-\theta\log\frac1\theta\right)T\\
&=
\frac12\left[
\gamma_d-(1-\gamma_d)\log\frac1{1-\gamma_d}
\right]T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
The coefficient \(1-\theta-\theta\log(1/\theta)\) lies in \((0,1)\), so the vertical estimate (11) also implies (20). Thus (20) holds for every boundary-through-\(\mathbf y^\star\) closed halfspace. For an arbitrary closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\), fact \(978e8555ec5bac42\) supplies a boundary-through-\(\mathbf y^\star\) closed halfspace \(H_0\subseteq H\). Monotonicity of mixed-integer measure then extends (20) to \(H\). This proves (1) and (2).

It remains to verify the two stated consequences. Put
\[
q(\gamma):=
\gamma-(1-\gamma)\log\frac1{1-\gamma}
=\gamma+(1-\gamma)\log(1-\gamma).
\]
For \(0<\gamma<1\),
\[
q'(\gamma)=-\log(1-\gamma)>0.
\tag{21}
\]
Also
\[
\gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>\frac1e.
\]
Hence \(q(\gamma_d)\geq q(1/e)\), which proves (3). Since \(q\) is strictly increasing and \(q(0)=0\), while the second term subtracted in the definition of \(q(1/e)\) is positive, one has
\[
0<c_0=q(1/e)<1/e.
\]
This proves (4). Finally, when \(d=2\), \(\gamma_2=4/9\); direct substitution in (1) gives (5).

No part of the argument treats more than the two rays available when the integer dimension is \(n=1\). In particular, the unimodality step (16) does not supply an analogue in \(\mathbb Z^n\) for \(n>1\). The theorem therefore closes only \(n=1\), not the locked all-\(n\) statement.

## External sources

Brunn-Minkowski theorem: R. J. Gardner, "The Brunn-Minkowski Inequality," Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002), Theorem 4.1, p. 362, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2, https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2. Exact form used: for nonempty bounded measurable \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<\lambda<1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-\lambda)A+\lambda B)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_d(A)^{1/d}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_d(B)^{1/d}.
\]
Applicability: every section used above is compact, its Minkowski combination is compact, and the only nontrivial applications have positive-volume endpoint sections. The same statement and its fiber-concavity application are recorded in the local Cheng-Basu source manuscript at lines 298-320.

Grünbaum inequality: Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and Section 3, pp. 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257. Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(\mathfrak c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(L\) containing \(\mathfrak c(K)\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap L)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K).
\]
Applicability: \(A_j\) has positive \(d\)-volume and is therefore a \(d\)-dimensional convex body, and the halfspace induced by (10) on its fiber contains its centroid. This exact formulation is also recorded as Theorem 2.2 in the local Cheng-Basu source manuscript at lines 123-130.
