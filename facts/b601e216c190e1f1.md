## Statement

Let $K\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be compact and convex, with positive original mixed length $M$ on at most three positive integer interval fibers. For an actual mixed point $p=(j,t)$ put $R(p)=\mu(K\cap(2p-K))$, with $\mu$ summed integer-fiber length. Some $p$ has $R(p)\ge M/2$, so its raw closed-halfspace depth is at least $M/4$. For three positive fibers $A,B,C$ at $-1,0,1$, of lengths $a,b,c$, the midpoint $t$ of $B$ satisfies
$$
R(0,t)\ge b+2\min(a,c,(a+c-b)_+),\qquad
R_{\max}\ge\max\{a,c,b+2\min(a,c,(a+c-b)_+)\}.
$$
If every fiber has mass strictly below $M/2$, then $R(0,t)>M/2$.

For $0<\varepsilon<1$, the actual three-fiber body with $[0,1],[0,1],[0,\varepsilon]$ at $-1,0,1$ has $R_{\max}=1+2\varepsilon$ and $M=2+\varepsilon$. In $n$ integer coordinates and one continuous coordinate, take its product with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ in the added integer coordinates. The product has $3\cdot 2^{n-1}$ positive fibers, $R_{\max}=1+2\varepsilon$, $M=2^{n-1}(2+\varepsilon)$, and every fiber is strictly below $M/2^n$. Its maximum retained fraction tends to $2^{-n}$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

## Proof

For a reflected intersection about $(j,t)$ the fiber at $z$ is $K_z\cap(2t-K_{2j-z})$. Its summed length is invariant under the reflection, which swaps the two closed sides of a hyperplane through the center. Their masses sum to at least the retained mass, so each captures at least half of it. A containing halfspace can be tightened through the center and then captures at least that much in the original measure. Empty or zero-length fibers contribute no length. Positive fibers have consecutive indices by convexity.

For the three-fiber case write $A=[l_A,l_A+a]$, $C=[l_C,l_C+c]$, $B=[l_B,l_B+b]$. Put $s=2t-l_A-l_C$ for $t=l_B+b/2$. Midpoint inclusion $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$ gives $a+c-b\le s\le b$ by the two endpoint inequalities. The overlap length of $A$ with $2t-C$ is
$$
[\min(a,s)-\max(0,s-c)]_+=[\min(a,c,s,a+c-s)]_+.
$$
If $a+c\le b$, it is nonnegative; otherwise both $s$ and $a+c-s$ are at least $a+c-b>0$. Thus the overlap is at least $\min(a,c,(a+c-b)_+)$. Reflection gives the same contribution from the other endpoint, and the middle interval reflects into itself. Each extreme midpoint retains its own full interval. If any fiber has length at least $M/2$, its midpoint suffices, including the case of only one or two positive fibers. Otherwise $x=a+c-b>0$ by nonheaviness of the middle, and the two endpoint nonheavy inequalities give $x<2a$ and $x<2c$. The middle bound gives $R(0,t)\ge b+x=a+c>M/2$.

For the sharp family the convex hull has lower boundary zero and upper boundary equal to one on $[-1,0]$ and $1-(1-\varepsilon)z$ on $[0,1]$. This concave upper boundary gives exactly the listed fibers, with no other integer bases. At the middle base self-overlap is at most one and each endpoint overlap at most $\varepsilon$. All three bounds are attained simultaneously at $t=1/2$, since $2t-[0,\varepsilon]=[1-\varepsilon,1]\subseteq[0,1]$. At the extremes only the own fiber contributes positive mass, so the exact overall maximum is $1+2\varepsilon$. The other $n-1$ integer coordinates in the product each lie in $\{0,1\}$. Reflection in an integer center in any such coordinate keeps only the slice at that center, since the opposite coordinate maps to $-1$ or $2$. Thus the retained maximum is unchanged while the total mass is multiplied by $2^{n-1}$. The largest fiber length is one, strictly below $(2+\varepsilon)/2=M/2^n$. The stated limit follows from the positive three-fiber family, with the vanishing fiber carrying zero length in the limit.
