## Statement

For every $d\ge3$, there are full-dimensional compact convex bodies $A^{(d)},B^{(d)},K^{(d)}=(A^{(d)}+B^{(d)})/2$ with three proper common through-interior halfspaces forming a rank-two minimal positive circuit. All captured-side $d$-volume root comparisons are equalities, yet $A^{(d)},B^{(d)}$ are not positively homothetic and whole-body Brunn–Minkowski is strict. They are also the actual fibers of one complete convex mixed family at bases $-1,0,1$, in the order $A^{(d)},K^{(d)},B^{(d)}$.

## Proof

Use the planar nongluing construction with its $A,B,K,H_i$ and $(\lambda_i,v_i)$. Write $p=(x,y)$ and set $m=d-2$. Adjoin $t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^m$ with $\sum_jt_j\le\phi_D(p)$ to each planar $D=A,B,K$, where
$$
\phi_A=19-11x-y,\qquad \phi_B=14-11x-y,\qquad \phi_K=33/2-11x-y.
$$
These are bounded convex bodies. At $p=0$, the vector with all $t_j=1/m$ gives an interior point of all three by their strict inequalities. Lift the three planar cuts by ignoring $t$. They remain proper, through that point, and cover the full space with the same rank-two minimal dependence.

Put $\rho_i=(1+\lambda_i)/2$. With $w=(11,1)$ the three maps satisfy $14-w\cdot v_i=19\lambda_i$, namely $57/4=19(3/4)$, $323/16=19(17/16)$ and $19=19$. It follows that
$$
\phi_B(\lambda_i p+v_i)=\lambda_i\phi_A(p),\qquad
\phi_K(\rho_i p+v_i/2)=\rho_i\phi_A(p).
$$
These identities preserve nonnegative roof status in both directions. Extending the planar maps by $t\mapsto\lambda_i t$ and $t\mapsto\rho_i t$, respectively, gives exact positive homotheties of the endpoint and midpoint caps. In particular each lifted $K$ cap is the midpoint of the corresponding lifted $A,B$ caps and has captured-side volume-root equality. The full Minkowski midpoint lies inside $K^{(d)}$ by the planar inclusion and the affine identity for averaging the full roofs. Conversely the cap cover and midpoint cap equalities put each point of $K^{(d)}$ in the full midpoint.

The planar projections are clipped by the corresponding nonnegative roof, since $t=0$ is feasible exactly there. For $A^{(d)}$ the upper-right planar vertex $(2,-1)$ is cut off and replaced by $(9/5,-4/5)$ on $x+y=1$, while the bottom-right vertex $(2,-3)$ lies on the roof-zero line. For $B^{(d)}$ the analogous replacement is $(27/20,-17/20)$ on $x+y=1/2$, with bottom-right vertex $(3/2,-5/2)$ on the roof-zero line. The other vertices have positive roofs. In the two projections the bottom edges both have vector $(4,-2)$, while the next edges have vectors $(-1/5,11/5)$ and $(-3/20,33/20)$ with ratio $3/4$. Each direction occurs only once in its oriented polygon boundary, so there is no positive homothety of the projections or the full bodies. The classical full-dimensional Brunn–Minkowski equality case gives strict whole-body inequality. The hull over bases $-1,1$ has exactly their Minkowski midpoint at base zero and no additional integer bases.
