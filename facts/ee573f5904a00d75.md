## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, $n$ a unit vector, $h=h_K(n)$ and $F=K\cap\{x:n\cdot x=h\}$. Assume $\mathcal H^{d-1}(F)=0$ and write $K_s=K\cap\{x:n\cdot x\le h-s\}$ for small $s\ge0$. Then $|K\setminus K_s|=o(s)$, which bounds the absolute difference of the two volumes in any common measurable cap, uniformly even if the cap moves. For each fixed $B<\infty$, uniformly for $\lVert v\rVert\le B$ and $0\le\sigma\le B$,
$$
h_{K_{\varepsilon\sigma}}(n+\varepsilon v)
=h+\varepsilon\big(h_F(v)-\sigma\big)+o(\varepsilon).
$$
For finitely many such fixed directions, cuts of depths $\varepsilon\sigma_i$ with bounded nonnegative $\sigma_i$ lose $o(\varepsilon)$ total volume. Each cut's indexed perturbed-normal support improves over the original body by at least $\varepsilon\sigma_i+o(\varepsilon)$.

## Proof

Translate the parallel slices at height $h-s$ into the supporting plane at $h$. Compactness of $K$ places them in any prescribed relative neighborhood of $F$ for sufficiently small $s$, since otherwise a sequence of slice points would converge to a point of $F$ outside that neighborhood. Their $(d-1)$-volumes therefore tend to zero by outer regularity. Integrating those areas proves the volume estimate, and intersecting the lost set with a cap cannot increase its volume.

Fix $q\in\operatorname{int}K$, put $\Delta=h-n\cdot q>0$, and choose $x\in F$ maximizing $v\cdot x$. The point $(1-\varepsilon\sigma/\Delta)x+(\varepsilon\sigma/\Delta)q$ is in $K_{\varepsilon\sigma}$. Its support evaluation is $h-\varepsilon\sigma+\varepsilon h_F(v)+O(\varepsilon^2)$, uniformly in the parameter ranges. Conversely a maximizer $x_\varepsilon$ of $n+\varepsilon v$ on the truncated body satisfies $0\le h-n\cdot x_\varepsilon\le C\varepsilon$ for one constant $C$ by this lower bound and boundedness of $K$. Its distance to $F$ tends uniformly to zero by compactness. Hence $v\cdot x_\varepsilon\le h_F(v)+o(1)$ uniformly, and the truncation gives $n\cdot x_\varepsilon\le h-\varepsilon\sigma$. This proves the expansion, including $\sigma=0$. Subtracting that case gives the improvement for one cut. A further $o(\varepsilon)$ change of the normal changes the value by $o(\varepsilon)$ by boundedness. For finitely many cuts their removed sets have union of volume at most the sum of their lost volumes, and further intersections only decrease each support value.
