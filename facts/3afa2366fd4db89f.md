## Statement

Let \(a,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^a\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W(C):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^a}w_z.
\]
Empty fibers have volume zero.  For \((z,x)\in C\), define
\[
\delta_C(z,x):=
\inf_{\substack{J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
x\in J}}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J),
\]
and set \(\delta_C(z,x):=0\) when \((z,x)\notin C\).  For an affine map
\(s:\mathbb R^a\to\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
Q_C(s):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^a}\delta_C(z,s(z)),\qquad
\mathcal Q_C:=\sup_{s\ {\rm affine}}Q_C(s),\qquad
M_{\rm vol}(C):=\max_{z\in\mathbb Z^a}w_z.
\]
If \((z_0,s(z_0))\in C\), define the pointwise anchored affine objective
\[
D_C(s;z_0):=
\inf_{\substack{Q\subseteq\mathbb R^a\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
z_0\in Q}}
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^a}\delta_C(z,s(z)).
\]
Also put
\[
S_C:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^a\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
For a measurable \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^a\times\mathbb R^d\), use the
mixed measure
\[
\mathcal H_d(A):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^a}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x:(z,x)\in A\},
\]
and, for \(p\in S_C\), define
\[
h_{S_C}(p):=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{a+d}\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
p\in H}}
\mathcal H_d(S_C\cap H).
\]

For every \(L\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}\), regard
\[
\widetilde C_L:=
\{(z,t,x):(z,x)\in C,\ 0\leq t\leq L\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^{a+1}\times\mathbb R^d
\]
as a body with \(t\) as an additional integer coordinate.  Then
\[
W(\widetilde C_L)=(L+1)W(C),\qquad
M_{\rm vol}(\widetilde C_L)=M_{\rm vol}(C),\qquad
\mathcal Q_{\widetilde C_L}=(L+1)\mathcal Q_C.
\tag{1}
\]
The last identity permits arbitrary affine maps depending on \(t\).

There is an explicit family to which (1) applies already with \(d=1\).
For every \(m\in\mathbb Z_{\geq2}\), put
\[
V_m:=\{0,1\}^m.
\]
Fix a bijection \(\kappa:V_m\to\{0,\ldots,2^m-1\}\) and a transcendental
number \(\tau>1\), and set \(c_v:=\tau^{\kappa(v)}\).  For every
\(T\subseteq V_m\) of cardinality \(m+2\), fix a nonzero integer affine
dependence
\[
\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T=0,\qquad
\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T v=0.
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
r_m:=
\frac12
\min_{\substack{T\subseteq V_m\\|T|=m+2}}
\frac{\left|\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T c_v\right|}
{\sum_{v\in T}|\lambda_v^T|}
>0,
\qquad
I_v:=[c_v-r_m,c_v+r_m],
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
C_m^0:=
\operatorname{conv}\!
\left(\bigcup_{v\in V_m}\{v\}\times I_v\right)
\subseteq\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a full-dimensional convex body, and its nonempty integer fibers are
exactly
\[
(C_m^0)_v=I_v\qquad(v\in V_m).
\tag{5}
\]
Writing \(S_m^0:=C_m^0\cap(\mathbb Z^m\times\mathbb R)\), one has
\[
W(C_m^0)=2^{m+1}r_m,\qquad
M_{\rm vol}(C_m^0)=2r_m,\qquad
\mathcal Q_{C_m^0}=(m+1)r_m.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, for every \(v\in V_m\) and \(x\in I_v\),
\[
h_{S_m^0}(v,x)
=
\min\{x-(c_v-r_m),(c_v+r_m)-x\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\max_{p\in S_m^0}h_{S_m^0}(p)
=r_m
=\frac12\,2^{-m}W(C_m^0).
\tag{8}
\]

The corresponding pointwise anchored affine functional is also exact.  Define
\[
\mathfrak D_{\rm aff}(C_m^0):=
\sup_{\substack{s\ {\rm affine},\ z_0\in\mathbb Z^m\\
(z_0,s(z_0))\in C_m^0}}
D_{C_m^0}(s;z_0).
\]
Then
\[
\mathfrak D_{\rm aff}(C_m^0)=r_m.
\tag{9}
\]

Now tensor (4):
\[
C_{m,L}:=
\{(z,t,x):(z,x)\in C_m^0,\ 0\leq t\leq L\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\times\mathbb R,
\qquad N:=m+1.
\tag{10}
\]
Write
\[
S_{m,L}:=C_{m,L}\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R).
\]
The body \(C_{m,L}\) is full-dimensional, and its positive integer fibers are
the intervals \(I_v\) indexed by
\((v,k)\in V_m\times\{0,\ldots,L\}\), and
\[
W(C_{m,L})=2^{m+1}r_m(L+1),\qquad
M_{\rm vol}(C_{m,L})=2r_m,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_{C_{m,L}}=(m+1)r_m(L+1).
\tag{11}
\]
It follows that
\[
\frac{\mathcal Q_{C_{m,L}}+2^N M_{\rm vol}(C_{m,L})}
{W(C_{m,L})}
=
\frac{m+1}{2^{m+1}}+\frac2{L+1}.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, for every \(c>0\), some \(m\geq2\) and then some \(L\geq1\)
make the ratio in (12) smaller than \(c\).  Hence there is no universal
\(c>0\) for which
\[
\mathcal Q_C+2^N M_{\rm vol}(C)\geq cW(C)
\tag{13}
\]
holds for every full-dimensional mixed-integer convex body with \(N\)
integer coordinates, even when \(d=1\).

The same conclusion holds for every preassigned finite-heavy-fiber repair,
with the following quantifier order.  Let
\((K_N)_{N\geq1}\) be any sequence of nonnegative integers, fixed
independently of the bodies and of \(L\).  Order all integer-fiber volumes as
\(w_{(1)}\geq w_{(2)}\geq\cdots\), padding the list by zeros.  Then
\[
\forall c>0\ \forall (K_N)_{N\geq1}
\exists m\geq2\ \exists L\geq1,\quad N=m+1,
\tag{14}
\]
such that the full-dimensional \(d=1\) body \(C_{m,L}\) satisfies
\[
\mathcal Q_{C_{m,L}}
+2^N\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}w_{(j)}
<cW(C_{m,L}).
\tag{15}
\]
More precisely, \(m\) can first be chosen using only \(c\); after \(N=m+1\)
and therefore the finite number \(K_N\) are fixed, \(L\) is chosen last.

Finally, the same tensor family has a primitive-column target-scale rescue.
For every \(v\in V_m\), the affine line
\[
A_v:=\{(v,t):t\in\mathbb R\}\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\tag{16}
\]
is a primitive lattice subspace of codimension \(m=N-1\), and its mixed mass
inside \(C_{m,L}\) is
\[
W_{A_v}=2r_m(L+1)=2^{-m}W(C_{m,L}).
\tag{17}
\]
Let \(k_\star:=\lfloor L/2\rfloor\) and
\(p_\star:=(v,k_\star,c_v)\).  The constant affine map
\(\widetilde s\equiv c_v\) satisfies the exact anchored identity
\[
D_{C_{m,L}}(\widetilde s;(v,k_\star))
=
r_m\left(\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor+1\right),
\tag{18}
\]
where \(D\) uses the \(N\)-dimensional integer base.  Directly in the original
mixed measure,
\[
h_{S_{m,L}}(p_\star)
\geq
r_m\left(\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor+1\right)
\geq
\frac12\,2^{-N}W(C_{m,L}).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (12) and (15) obstruct only the proposed scalar affine-plus-finitely-many-
fibers closures.  They are not counterexamples to weak Oertel.

## Proof

For every \(T\) in (2), the vectors \((1,v)\in\mathbb Z^{m+1}\),
\(v\in T\), are linearly dependent, so a nonzero integer dependence exists.
The exponents \(\kappa(v)\) are distinct.  Therefore
\(\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T\tau^{\kappa(v)}\) is the value at the
transcendental number \(\tau\) of a nonzero polynomial with integer
coefficients, and hence it is nonzero.  There are finitely many sets \(T\);
every denominator in (3) is positive.  Thus \(r_m>0\).

The set in (4) is the convex hull of finitely many compact segments, hence is
compact and convex.  Its projection onto \(\mathbb R^m\) is \([0,1]^m\).
The \(m+2\) points
\[
(0,c_0-r_m),\quad (0,c_0+r_m),\quad
(e_i,c_{e_i})\quad(1\leq i\leq m)
\]
are affinely independent: after subtracting the first point, projection onto
\(\mathbb R^m\) forces all coefficients of the \(e_i\)-differences to vanish,
and the remaining vertical difference is nonzero.  Hence \(C_m^0\) is
full-dimensional.

Every \(v\in V_m\) is an extreme point of \([0,1]^m\).  If a convex
combination of the generating segments in (4) has base coordinate \(v\),
extremality forces every generator of positive weight to have base coordinate
\(v\).  Its continuous coordinate therefore lies in \(I_v\).  The reverse
inclusion is part of the construction, proving (5).  Since
\([0,1]^m\cap\mathbb Z^m=V_m\), there are no other integer fibers.
Every fiber has length \(2r_m\), proving the first two identities in (6).

We next compute the affine objective.  Suppose an affine
\(s:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R\) meets \(I_v\) for every \(v\) in some
\(T\subseteq V_m\) of cardinality \(m+2\).  Affinity and (2) give
\[
\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T s(v)=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T c_v\right|
&=
\left|\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T(c_v-s(v))\right|\\
&\leq r_m\sum_{v\in T}|\lambda_v^T|
\leq\frac12\left|\sum_{v\in T}\lambda_v^T c_v\right|,
\end{aligned}
\]
which is impossible because the last absolute value is positive.  Thus an
affine graph meets at most \(m+1\) integer fibers.  For \(x\in I_v\),
\[
\delta_{C_m^0}(v,x)
=\min\{x-(c_v-r_m),(c_v+r_m)-x\}\leq r_m.
\tag{20}
\]
It follows that \(Q_{C_m^0}(s)\leq(m+1)r_m\).  Conversely, the points
\(0,e_1,\ldots,e_m\) are affinely independent, so there is an affine map
\(s_0\) with
\[
s_0(0)=c_0,\qquad s_0(e_i)=c_{e_i}\quad(1\leq i\leq m).
\]
These \(m+1\) fibers each contribute \(r_m\), so
\(Q_{C_m^0}(s_0)\geq(m+1)r_m\).  This proves the last identity in (6).

We prove the exact mixed-depth formula (7).  Fix \(p=(v,x)\) with
\(x\in I_v\), and let \(H\) be any ambient closed halfspace containing
\(p\).  If the coefficient of the continuous coordinate in a defining
inequality for \(H\) is zero, then \(H\) contains the entire \(v\)-fiber and
contributes \(2r_m\).  If that coefficient is nonzero, the section of \(H\)
in the \(v\)-fiber is a closed ray containing \(x\), so its intersection with
\(I_v\) has length at least the right-hand side of (20).  Hence
\[
h_{S_m^0}(v,x)\geq\delta_{C_m^0}(v,x).
\tag{21}
\]

For the reverse inequality, define
\[
\sigma_v:=(2v_1-1,\ldots,2v_m-1).
\]
For \(u\in V_m\setminus\{v\}\),
\[
\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)=-\operatorname{dist}_{\rm H}(u,v)\leq-1.
\tag{22}
\]
Write \(L_u:=c_u-r_m\) and \(U_u:=c_u+r_m\).  If
\(U_v-x\leq x-L_v\), choose \(R>0\) so large that
\[
R\bigl(-\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)\bigr)>U_u-x
\qquad(u\neq v),
\]
and use
\[
H^+:=\{(u,y):R\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)+y-x\geq0\}.
\tag{23}
\]
On the \(v\)-fiber this cuts out \([x,U_v]\), of length \(U_v-x\);
by (22) and the choice of \(R\), it misses every other integer fiber.
If \(x-L_v\leq U_v-x\), choose \(R\) so large that
\[
R\bigl(-\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)\bigr)>x-L_u
\qquad(u\neq v),
\]
and use
\[
H^-:=\{(u,y):R\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)+x-y\geq0\}.
\tag{24}
\]
This cuts out \([L_v,x]\) in the \(v\)-fiber and misses every other integer
fiber.  The formulas remain valid at the interval endpoints, where a
singleton has length zero.  Thus (21) is an equality, proving (7) and (8).

For (9), the exposing base halfspace
\[
Q_v:=\{u\in\mathbb R^m:\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)\geq0\}
\tag{25}
\]
contains \(v\) and no other point of \(V_m\), by (22).  Therefore, for every
feasible pair \((s,z_0)\), necessarily \(z_0\in V_m\), and
\[
D_{C_m^0}(s;z_0)
\leq\delta_{C_m^0}(z_0,s(z_0))\leq r_m.
\]
Conversely, fix \(v\in V_m\) and take the constant affine map \(s\equiv c_v\).
Every base halfspace containing \(v\) contains its contribution \(r_m\), so
\(D_{C_m^0}(s;v)\geq r_m\).  The halfspace (25) gives the reverse inequality.
This proves (9) without using any predecessor fact.

We now prove the tensor identities (1), including the quantification over
affine maps that depend on the new coordinate.  The integer fiber of
\(\widetilde C_L\) at \((z,k)\), \(0\leq k\leq L\), is exactly \(C_z\);
there are no other positive fibers.  This immediately proves the first two
identities in (1).  An arbitrary affine map has the form
\[
\widetilde s(z,t)=Az+bt+c.
\]
For each \(k\in\{0,\ldots,L\}\), its restriction
\[
s_k(z):=Az+bk+c
\]
is an affine map on \(\mathbb R^a\).  Since the tensor fiber at \((z,k)\)
is \(C_z\),
\[
Q_{\widetilde C_L}(\widetilde s)
=\sum_{k=0}^L Q_C(s_k)
\leq(L+1)\mathcal Q_C.
\tag{26}
\]
Conversely, for every affine \(s:\mathbb R^a\to\mathbb R^d\), the
\(t\)-independent affine map \(\widetilde s(z,t):=s(z)\) satisfies
\[
Q_{\widetilde C_L}(\widetilde s)=(L+1)Q_C(s).
\]
Taking the supremum over \(s\) gives the reverse inequality in (26), proving
the third identity in (1).  The product of the interiors of \(C_m^0\) and
\([0,L]\), with coordinates reordered, is nonempty, so \(C_{m,L}\) is
full-dimensional.  Applying (1) to (6) proves (11), and direct division proves
(12).

We verify the heavy-fiber quantifiers.  All positive fibers of \(C_{m,L}\)
have volume \(2r_m\), so for every nonnegative integer \(K_N\),
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}w_{(j)}\leq2r_mK_N.
\]
Using \(N=m+1\) and (11),
\[
\frac{\mathcal Q_{C_{m,L}}
+2^N\sum_{j=1}^{K_N}w_{(j)}}{W(C_{m,L})}
\leq
\frac{m+1}{2^{m+1}}+\frac{2K_N}{L+1}.
\tag{27}
\]
Given \(c>0\) and the entire sequence \((K_N)\), first choose \(m\geq2\)
so that \((m+1)/2^{m+1}<c/2\).  This fixes \(N=m+1\) and the finite integer
\(K_N\).  Only now choose \(L\) so that \(2K_N/(L+1)<c/2\).  Equation (27)
then gives (15).  Taking \(K_N=1\), or directly using (12), proves the
single-heavy-fiber no-go (13).

It remains to prove the primitive-column calibration.  The integer points of
\(A_v\) are exactly \(\{v\}\times\mathbb Z\).  Equivalently, the homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z^N\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^m,\qquad (z,t)\longmapsto z
\]
is onto and has the direction lattice of \(A_v\) as its kernel after
translation.  Thus \(A_v\) is primitive and has codimension \(m\).  Its
\(L+1\) fibers each have length \(2r_m\), which proves (17).

For the exact anchored identity, take \(\widetilde s\equiv c_v\).  At every
\((v,k)\), \(0\leq k\leq L\), its fiber contribution is \(r_m\).  The
restriction to \(A_v\) of any base halfspace containing
\((v,k_\star)\) is either all of \(A_v\) or a one-dimensional closed
halfspace containing \(k_\star\).  It therefore contains at least
\[
\min\{k_\star+1,L-k_\star+1\}
=\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor+1
\tag{28}
\]
of the integer points \(0,\ldots,L\) on this column.  All other graph weights
are nonnegative, so (28) is a lower bound for the anchored objective.

For the matching upper bound, take \(R>L\).  The two proper base halfspaces
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q^+&:=\{(u,t):R\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)+t-k_\star\geq0\},\\
Q^-&:=\{(u,t):R\sigma_v^\mathsf T(u-v)+k_\star-t\geq0\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
contain \((v,k_\star)\).  By (22) and \(R>L\), each excludes every supported
point \((u,k)\) with \(u\neq v\).  On the \(v\)-column, \(Q^+\) contains
exactly \(k_\star,\ldots,L\), while \(Q^-\) contains exactly
\(0,\ldots,k_\star\).  Taking the smaller sum in (29) proves equality in
(18).

Finally, let \(H\) be any ambient closed halfspace containing
\(p_\star=(v,k_\star,c_v)\).  Pulling \(H\) back along the affine center line
\[
t\longmapsto(v,t,c_v)
\]
gives all of \(\mathbb R\) or a closed one-dimensional halfspace containing
\(k_\star\).  Hence it contains the centers of at least the number of column
fibers in (28).  In each such fiber, the section of \(H\) either contains the
whole interval or is a closed ray containing its center \(c_v\); in both
cases it captures length at least \(r_m\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal H_1(S_{m,L}\cap H)
\geq r_m\left(\left\lfloor\frac L2\right\rfloor+1\right).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\), using
\(\lfloor L/2\rfloor+1\geq(L+1)/2\), and substituting (11) and \(N=m+1\)
gives (19).  This completes all claimed exact computations and no-go
quantifiers.
