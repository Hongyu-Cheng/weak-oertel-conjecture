## Statement

A planar convex body is a compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^2\) with nonempty interior, and \(|E|=\mathcal L^2(E)\) denotes planar Lebesgue area. Let \(\eta>0\), let \(m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(G_1,\ldots,G_m\) be a finite cover of a planar convex body \(K\), where each \(G_i\) is an open halfplane, the empty set, or the whole plane. Assume \(|K\cap G_i|\le\eta\) for every \(i\). There are indices \(j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), possibly equal, such that, with \(G=G_j\), \(G'=G_k\), \(\sigma=|K\setminus(G\cup G')|\), and \(\tau=|K\cap G\cap G'|\),

\[
\sigma^2\le\eta\tau,
\qquad
|K|\le\frac{9\eta}{4}-\frac{(\sigma-\eta/2)^2}{\eta}
\le\frac{9\eta}{4}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the ambient application, let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) be arbitrary, let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, and use the locked set \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2\) and \(S=C\cap\Lambda\). For every \(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^n\), define \(A_{\mathbf t}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in C\}\); empty sections are permitted. For integer \(\mathbf z\), put \(a_{\mathbf z}=|A_{\mathbf z}|\). For Borel ambient sets \(E\), use the actual mixed area

\[
\mu(E)=\mathcal H_2(S\cap E)
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\bigl|\{\mathbf x\in A_{\mathbf z}:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in E\}\bigr|,
\qquad
W=\mu(\mathbb R^{n+2})=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_{\mathbf z}>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose \(\eta>0\) and \(H_1,\ldots,H_m\) are open ambient halfspaces satisfying \(\mu(H_i)<\eta\) for every \(i\), and covering every point of every positive-area integer fiber:

\[
\bigcup_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\\a_{\mathbf z}>0}}
\bigl(\{\mathbf z\}\times A_{\mathbf z}\bigr)
\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^m H_i.
\tag{3}
\]

Then \(a_{\mathbf z}\le9\eta/4\) for every integer \(\mathbf z\). For every chosen \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\), including targets whose original fiber has zero area or is empty, there are two original covering members \(H,H'\), possibly equal, with the following properties. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=C\setminus(H\cup H'),&
K_{\mathbf t}&=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in R\}
\quad(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^n),\\
\sigma&=|K_{\mathbf J}|,&
\tau&=\bigl|\{\mathbf x\in A_{\mathbf J}:
(\mathbf J,\mathbf x)\in H\cap H'\}\bigr|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The set \(R\) is compact and convex, possibly empty, and all its sections \(K_{\mathbf t}\) are compact and convex, possibly empty. The selection satisfies \(\tau\ge\sigma^2/\eta\) and
\(0\le |K_{\mathbf z}|\le a_{\mathbf z}\le9\eta/4\) for every integer \(\mathbf z\). Its exact total residual identity and ensuing bounds are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_2(R\cap\Lambda)
&=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}|K_{\mathbf z}|\\
&=W-\mu(H)-\mu(H')+\mu(H\cap H')\\
&>W-2\eta+\mu(H\cap H')\\
&\ge W-2\eta+\tau
\ge W-2\eta+\frac{\sigma^2}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, keeping all the other fibers in their original positions,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\\\mathbf z\ne\mathbf J}}
|K_{\mathbf z}|
&>W-2\eta+\tau-\sigma\\
&\ge W-2\eta+\frac{\sigma^2}{\eta}-\sigma\\
&=W-\frac{9\eta}{4}+\frac{(\sigma-\eta/2)^2}{\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Every original lattice barycentric combination of these residual sections is retained. Precisely, take any integer \(r\ge1\), indices \(\mathbf z_1,\ldots,\mathbf z_r\in\mathbb Z^n\), and real coefficients \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\ge0\) with \(\sum_{\ell=1}^r\lambda_\ell=1\). With \(\mathbf t=\sum_{\ell=1}^r\lambda_\ell\mathbf z_\ell\), one has

\[
\left\{\sum_{\ell:\lambda_\ell>0}\lambda_\ell\mathbf x_\ell:
\mathbf x_\ell\in K_{\mathbf z_\ell}
\text{ for every }\ell\text{ with }\lambda_\ell>0\right\}
\subseteq K_{\mathbf t}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus if \(\mathbf t\) is integral, the right side is the actual residual integer fiber at that index, and if \(\mathbf t=\mathbf J\), its area is \(\sigma\). Zero coefficients are omitted as displayed; if a positively weighted section is empty, the set on the left is empty. Repeated indices are allowed.

The cover hypothesis (3) holds whenever \(h_S(\mathbf y)<\eta\) for every point of the positive-area integer fibers, where \(h_S(\mathbf y)\) is the infimum of \(\mu(L)\) over all closed ambient halfspaces \(L\) containing \(\mathbf y\). In particular, failure of the locked depth threshold at every point of \(S\) implies this hypothesis. All statements use the original planar area and mixed area, without normalizing \(W\) to one.

## Proof

We first prove the finite planar Helly fact needed for the cover reduction. In any finite family of convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^2\) with empty total intersection, choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily \(F_1,\ldots,F_r\) with empty intersection. If \(r\ge4\), minimality supplies points \(\mathbf x_i\in\bigcap_{j\ne i}F_j\) for \(i=1,2,3,4\). The four vectors \((\mathbf x_i,1)\in\mathbb R^3\) are linearly dependent. Consequently there are real numbers \(b_i\), not all zero, with \(\sum_i b_i\mathbf x_i=\mathbf0\) and \(\sum_i b_i=0\). There are both positive and negative coefficients. Dividing the positive and negative parts by their common positive sum gives a point \(\mathbf y\) that is a convex combination of the \(\mathbf x_i\) with \(b_i>0\), and also a convex combination of those with \(b_i<0\). For every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\), at least one of these two disjoint index sets avoids \(j\). Every point in that combination belongs to \(F_j\), so convexity implies \(\mathbf y\in F_j\). This contradicts the empty intersection. Hence \(r\le3\).

Now consider the planar cover in the statement. If two members cover \(K\), allowing repetition, select them. Then \(\sigma=0\), and \(\sigma^2\le\eta\tau\) follows from \(\tau\ge0\). This includes a member equal to the whole plane, a single member containing \(K\), and any covering pair of identical members.

Otherwise no two members, even with repetition, cover \(K\). Apply the just-proved Helly fact to the convex sets \(K\) and \(\mathbb R^2\setminus G_i\), \(1\le i\le m\). Their total intersection is empty by the cover hypothesis. An inclusion-minimal empty subfamily of at most three sets cannot contain \(K\), since then at most two \(G_i\) would cover \(K\). Nor can it consist of at most two complements, since the corresponding at most two \(G_i\) would cover the whole plane. Thus it consists of three complements. None of the selected \(G_i\) can be empty, because its complement would be redundant; none can be the whole plane, by the already treated case. After relabeling, \(G_1,G_2,G_3\) are proper open halfplanes whose union is the whole plane, while no two of them cover \(K\) or the whole plane.

We establish the geometry of this minimal triple in the original Euclidean plane. Its three boundary lines cannot all be parallel. Indeed, in a coordinate perpendicular to parallel lines, each member is a left or right open ray. A finite union of these rays that covers the real line is already the union of a largest left ray and a largest right ray, contradicting minimality. Thus, after relabeling, the first two boundary lines are nonparallel. Use invertible affine coordinates \(u_1,u_2\) in which \(G_1=\{u_1<0\}\) and \(G_2=\{u_2<0\}\). The third halfplane contains the entire quadrant \(u_1,u_2\ge0\). Writing it as \(a u_1+b u_2>c\), containment of the origin gives \(c<0\), and containment of the two unbounded coordinate rays gives \(a,b\ge0\). If \(a=0\), then \(G_2\cup G_3\) covers the plane; if \(b=0\), then \(G_1\cup G_3\) does so. Therefore \(a,b>0\). Dividing by \(-c\) and relabeling these positive coefficients, the three halfplanes have the coordinate descriptions
\(u_1<0\), \(u_2<0\), and \(a u_1+b u_2>-1\).

The point with coordinates \((-1/(4a),-1/(4b))\) belongs to all three open halfplanes; denote the corresponding point in the original plane by \(\mathbf p\). Before unit normalization, the outward normals are \(\nabla u_1\), \(\nabla u_2\), and \(-a\nabla u_1-b\nabla u_2\). The first two are linearly independent, and these three vectors have a positive linear dependence. Their unit normalizations \(\mathbf q_1,\mathbf q_2,\mathbf q_3\) therefore span \(\mathbb R^2\) and satisfy \(\sum_i\alpha_i\mathbf q_i=\mathbf0\) for some \(\alpha_i>0\). After dividing by \(\sum_i\alpha_i\), this is a convex combination with positive coefficients. Their triangle is nondegenerate: if they were affinely collinear, its affine line would contain \(\mathbf0\), contradicting linear independence of \(\mathbf q_1,\mathbf q_2\). Hence \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf q_1,\mathbf q_2,\mathbf q_3\}\). The affine coordinates have only classified the halfplanes; areas and unit normals in the rest of the proof remain those of the original plane.

Put \(Q_i=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf q_i\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)\le0\}\). Since \(\mathbf p\) lies strictly in \(G_i\), one has the exact containment \(Q_i\subset G_i\). We next prove \(\mathbf p\in\operatorname{int}K\).

For the supporting-line fact used here, if a point \(\mathbf p\) is outside a compact convex set \(K\), choose a nearest point \(\mathbf v\in K\). Minimizing squared distance along the segment from \(\mathbf v\) to any \(\mathbf x\in K\) gives \((\mathbf p-\mathbf v)\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf v)\le0\), and thus \((\mathbf p-\mathbf v)\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)<0\). If \(\mathbf p\in\partial K\), choose points outside \(K\) tending to \(\mathbf p\), apply the preceding argument, and normalize the resulting vectors to length one. The nearest points tend to \(\mathbf p\); a convergent subsequence of the unit vectors yields a nonzero vector \(\mathbf n\) with \(\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)\le0\) for all \(\mathbf x\in K\). This proves that whenever \(\mathbf p\notin\operatorname{int}K\), such a nonzero \(\mathbf n\) exists.

Every vector \(\mathbf n\in\mathbb R^2\) is a nonnegative combination of at most two of the \(\mathbf q_i\). To see this directly, write it in the basis \(\mathbf q_1,\mathbf q_2\), initially assigning coefficient zero to \(\mathbf q_3\). Add the smallest nonnegative multiple of the positive relation \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)\) that makes all three coefficients nonnegative. Such a multiple exists, and at least one coefficient is then zero. For \(\mathbf n\ne\mathbf0\), at least one of the remaining coefficients is positive.

If \(\mathbf p\notin\operatorname{int}K\), apply this representation to the supporting vector \(\mathbf n\), using indices \(j,k\), with a zero coefficient allowed if only one index is needed. Choose \(\mathbf x\in K\setminus(G_j\cup G_k)\), which exists because no pair covers \(K\). Since \(Q_j\subset G_j\) and \(Q_k\subset G_k\), both \(\mathbf q_j\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)\) and \(\mathbf q_k\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)\) are strictly positive. Their nonnegative combination representing \(\mathbf n\), with at least one positive coefficient, gives \(\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf p)>0\), a contradiction. Thus \(\mathbf p\in\operatorname{int}K\).

We now use exactly the positive-fan auxiliary assertion of accepted fact 2a8c2bc6d7992c5e. Its \(K,\mathbf p,\mathbf q_i,Q_i\) are the same objects just constructed, in the same Euclidean normalization. Its areas are
\(c_i=|K\cap Q_i|\),
\(s_i=|K\setminus(Q_j\cup Q_k)|\), and
\(D_i=|K\cap Q_j\cap Q_k|\) for \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\).
Thus the supplied manuscript's fan areas \(\sigma_i,\tau_i\) are respectively these \(s_i,D_i\). The current \(\eta\) is the cover's area bound; the predecessor's temporary scalar also called \(\eta\) in its separate three-slice proof is not used. All the hypotheses of its fan assertion have been checked: \(K\) is a planar convex body, \(\mathbf p\) is interior, the normals are unit vectors, and the origin is interior to their convex hull. That assertion gives positive \(s_i,D_i\) and an index \(i\) such that \(3s_i^2\le(c_1+c_2+c_3)D_i\). Since \(Q_\ell\subset G_\ell\), each \(c_\ell\le\eta\), so \(s_i^2\le\eta D_i\).

Select the corresponding original members \(G_j,G_k\), not their auxiliary \(Q_j,Q_k\). Enlargement from the \(Q\)'s to the \(G\)'s decreases the uncovered set and increases the overlap set. Consequently \(\sigma\le s_i\) and \(\tau\ge D_i\), proving \(\sigma^2\le\eta\tau\). This completes the selection in both cases.

For every selected pair, measurable inclusion-exclusion, valid also for repeated members, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|K|
&=|K\cap G|+|K\cap G'|-\tau+\sigma\\
&\le 2\eta+\sigma-\frac{\sigma^2}{\eta}\\
&=\frac{9\eta}{4}-\frac{(\sigma-\eta/2)^2}{\eta}
\le\frac{9\eta}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Division by \(\eta\) is legitimate because \(\eta>0\). No boundary deletion has been used in this identity. This proves (1), including empty traces, whole traces, and repeated covering members.

We turn to the ambient assertion. Compactness of \(C\) implies that its projection to \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded, so only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty. Every nonempty \(A_{\mathbf z}\) is compact and convex. If its area is positive, it contains three noncollinear points and hence their triangle, so it has nonempty interior and is a planar convex body. Formula (2) is exactly the locked mixed-area formula; the measure is finite. Its \(\mu\) has the same actual mixed-area meaning as in accepted fact 2a8c2bc6d7992c5e, and \(W\) is the quantity denoted \(T\) there when specializing to that fact's three-fiber situation.

For an integer \(\mathbf z\) and each covering index \(i\), let
\(G_{i,\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in H_i\}\).
An open ambient halfspace has a representation
\(\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf t+\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf x<\beta_i\}\)
with \((\mathbf a_i,\mathbf b_i)\ne\mathbf0\). If \(\mathbf b_i\ne\mathbf0\), its trace \(G_{i,\mathbf z}\) is an open planar halfplane. If \(\mathbf b_i=\mathbf0\), the trace is the whole plane when \(\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf z<\beta_i\), and is empty when \(\mathbf a_i\cdot\mathbf z\ge\beta_i\). In particular, equality gives an empty open trace, even if the ambient boundary contains an entire fiber.

For each positive-area fiber, (3) makes these traces a finite cover, and
\(|A_{\mathbf z}\cap G_{i,\mathbf z}|\le\mu(H_i)<\eta\).
Applying (1) with the non-strict bound \(\eta\) gives \(a_{\mathbf z}\le9\eta/4\). The same bound is automatic for zero-area or empty fibers.

Fix any \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^n\). If \(a_{\mathbf J}>0\), apply the selection part of (1) to \(A_{\mathbf J}\) and its traces, and take the original ambient members \(H_j,H_k\) with those selected indices. The planar argument returns original traces, so no translation, shrinking, rotation, or replacement of the ambient halfspaces occurs. Definitions (4) then give exactly the selected \(\sigma,\tau\), proving \(\tau\ge\sigma^2/\eta\). If \(a_{\mathbf J}=0\), take any covering member twice. Such a member exists because \(W>0\) and (3) covers a nonempty positive-area fiber. Both \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\) are zero, and the same inequality holds. This also handles empty target fibers.

The identity \(R=C\cap(\mathbb R^{n+2}\setminus H)\cap(\mathbb R^{n+2}\setminus H')\) expresses \(R\) as the intersection of \(C\) with two closed halfspaces. It is therefore compact and convex, with the permitted possibility of emptiness. Its sections have the stated properties, and \(K_{\mathbf z}\subseteq A_{\mathbf z}\) gives the area upper bounds. Inclusion-exclusion for the finite measure \(\mu\), together with \(\mu(C)=W\), yields the exact identity
\(\mu(R)=W-\mu(H)-\mu(H')+\mu(H\cap H')\).
Since \(R\subseteq C\), formula (2) identifies \(\mu(R)\) with \(\mathcal H_2(R\cap\Lambda)=\sum_{\mathbf z}|K_{\mathbf z}|\).
The two inequalities \(\mu(H)<\eta\) and \(\mu(H')<\eta\) give the strict step in (5), also when \(H=H'\). The contribution of the target fiber to \(\mu(H\cap H')\) is exactly \(\tau\), so this overlap mass is at least \(\tau\). The selected inequality gives its final lower bound by \(\sigma^2/\eta\). These facts prove (5). Subtracting \(|K_{\mathbf J}|=\sigma\) and completing the square gives (6).

These mass identities hold on the exact Borel sets, including every retained boundary. In particular, no assertion that an ambient boundary has zero mixed area has been made; fibers lying on a selected boundary stay on its closed complementary side. Repetition of a selected halfspace preserves inclusion-exclusion, since its intersection with itself has its full mass.

To prove (7), discard the zero coefficients. If a remaining section is empty, the asserted inclusion has empty left side. Otherwise choose any \(\mathbf x_\ell\in K_{\mathbf z_\ell}\) for every remaining index. Each \((\mathbf z_\ell,\mathbf x_\ell)\) lies in the same convex set \(R\). Their convex combination is
\((\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell\mathbf z_\ell,\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell\mathbf x_\ell)\in R\),
which is exactly the claimed membership in \(K_{\mathbf t}\). This proof allows arbitrary real barycentric coefficients, repeated indices, and every original pair of ambient cuts, without relocating any fiber.

Finally, suppose every point of the positive-area integer fibers has \(h_S(\mathbf y)<\eta\). Their union is compact, being a finite union of compact fibers. For each such \(\mathbf y\), the strict infimum inequality gives a closed ambient halfspace \(L\ni\mathbf y\) with \(\mu(L)<\eta\). Write
\(L=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf t+\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf x\le c\}\)
with nonzero normal. The open halfspaces
\(L_\varepsilon=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf t+\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf x<c+\varepsilon\}\), \(\varepsilon>0\), contain \(\mathbf y\) and decrease to \(L\) as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Continuity from above for the finite measure \(\mu\) gives \(\mu(L_\varepsilon)\to\mu(L)<\eta\). Choose one with mass less than \(\eta\) for each point. Compactness supplies a finite subcover, proving (3). This argument includes mass on the original boundary because the decreasing limit is the closed halfspace \(L\) itself. It completes the bridge from pointwise low depth to the residual ledger for arbitrary \(n\) and \(d=2\).
