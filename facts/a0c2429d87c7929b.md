## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the exact canonical hard \(I\)-dominant objects of accepted facts `7ec63df101c30343` and `d7b1da2c485d6f04`.  Thus

\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 I\ge O,
\]

the \(I\)-bad opposite-edge family is \(\mathcal E_I\), the exceptional family is \(\mathcal B\), and

\[
 J:=\{e\in\mathcal E_I\setminus\mathcal B:s_e>0\},
 \qquad
 \chi_e:=\min\{\omega_e,s_e/16\}\quad(e\in J),
 \qquad
 X:=\sum_{e\in J}\chi_e>\frac Q{160}.
\tag{1}
\]

Put \(\chi_e=0\) for \(e\notin J\), and write \(m_e\) for the physical midpoint base of \(e\).  For an affine subspace \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), define

\[
 X(A):=\sum_{e\in J:\,m_e\in A}\chi_e,
\tag{2}
\]

and let

\[
 q_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(Z\cap A),
 \qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)>0\},
\tag{3}
\]

when \(Z\cap A\ne\varnothing\).  If \(Z\cap A=\varnothing\), then \(X(A)=0\).  If \(q_A\le2\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 X(A)\le
 \begin{cases}
  G/32,&q_A=0,\\[1mm]
  G/4,&q_A=1,\\[1mm]
  8G,&q_A=2.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently every affine line \(\ell\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{X(\ell)<\frac25X,
 \qquad X-X(\ell)>\frac35X.}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, if

\[
 A_\chi:=\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:\chi_e>0\},
 \qquad \rho_\chi:=\dim A_\chi,
\tag{6}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{\rho_\chi\ge2.}
\tag{7}
\]

Assume now that \(\rho_\chi=2\).  Retain from accepted fact `d7b1da2c485d6f04` the residual ranks

\[
 r_\ell,\quad r_F,\quad r_*,\quad r_y,\quad r_a^\dagger,
 \quad\text{and}\quad r_{\bar c},
\tag{8}
\]

where \(a:A_\chi\to\mathbb R^d\) is arbitrary and \(r_a^\dagger\) is formed from any permitted choice \(q_e^a\in\{\ell_e,F_e^\flat\}\) with

\[
 \|q_e^a-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}\ge\frac14.
\]

In this rank-two case put

\[
 \widehat Z_a:=
 \frac{2^{n-2}}{r_{\bar c}+1}
 +32\gamma_d
 \max_{r\in\{r_\ell,r_F,r_*,r_y,r_a^\dagger\}}
 \frac{2^{n-2}}{r+1}.
\tag{9}
\]

Then, for every such \(a\) and every permitted selection,

\[
 \boxed{\widehat Z_a<\frac{971}{2816}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 r+1>
 \frac{90112}{971}\,\gamma_d\,2^{n-2}
 \quad
 (r\in\{r_\ell,r_F,r_*,r_y,r_a^\dagger\}),}
\tag{11}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\bar c}+1>
 \frac{2816}{971}\,2^{n-2}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, failure of any one of (11)--(12) is already terminal: it forces \(\mathcal A(C)\ge Q/100\).  The lemma does not assert a full-rank star or convert the continuous ranks in (11)--(12) into integral chart rank.

## Proof

For \(e\in J\), accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` gives

\[
 t_e\ge\frac18s_e,
\tag{13}
\]

because \(J\cap\mathcal B=\varnothing\).  By the definition of \(\chi_e\),

\[
 \chi_e\le\frac1{16}s_e\le\frac12t_e.
\tag{14}
\]

Let \(\Theta_I(A)\) denote the total accepted \(I\)-triangle target mass whose physical midpoint lies in \(A\), in the exact normalization of accepted fact `07889c9b4686d8b4`.  Summing (14) over the edges with midpoint in \(A\) gives

\[
 X(A)\le\frac12\Theta_I(A).
\tag{15}
\]

Accepted fact `07889c9b4686d8b4` gives, according as \(q_A=0,1,2\),

\[
 \Theta_I(A)\le\frac G{16},
 \qquad
 \Theta_I(A)\le\frac G2,
 \qquad
 \Theta_I(A)\le16G.
\tag{16}
\]

Equations (15)--(16) prove (4).  Every affine line has \(q_A\le1\), so the hard inequality and (1), (4) give

\[
 X(\ell)\le\frac G4<\frac Q{400}<\frac25X.
\tag{17}
\]

This proves (5).  If \(\rho_\chi\le1\), all \(\chi\)-positive midpoints lie on one affine line, contradicting (5).  Hence (7) holds.

Suppose \(\rho_\chi=2\).  Applying (4) to \(A=A_\chi\) gives

\[
 X\le8G.
\tag{18}
\]

Introduce the dimensionless quantities

\[
 r_0:=\frac RQ,
 \qquad
 g_0:=\frac GQ,
 \qquad
 \zeta:=\frac XQ,
 \qquad
 s:=r_0+g_0.
\tag{19}
\]

The correlated overlap estimate in accepted fact `d7b1da2c485d6f04` is

\[
 \zeta\ge
 \frac{11}{512}
 -\frac{195}{128}r_0
 -\frac{307}{4096}g_0.
\tag{20}
\]

Equation (18) is

\[
 \zeta\le8g_0.
\tag{21}
\]

The same accepted fact gives the exact single-use hard ledger

\[
 r_0+g_0+4(1-r_0)x_{\rm src}
 +\zeta\widehat Z_a<\frac1{100},
\tag{22}
\]

where \(x_{\rm src}\ge0\).  Since \(r_0<1/100<1\), dropping the nonnegative source term in (22) yields

\[
 s+\zeta\widehat Z_a<\frac1{100}.
\tag{23}
\]

For compactness put

\[
 a_0:=\frac{11}{512},
 \qquad
 b_0:=\frac{195}{128},
 \qquad
 c_0:=\frac{307}{4096},
 \qquad
 k:=8-b_0+c_0=\frac{26835}{4096}.
\tag{24}
\]

Then (20) reads

\[
 \zeta\ge a_0-b_0s+(b_0-c_0)g_0.
\tag{25}
\]

Combining (21) and (25) gives

\[
 g_0\ge\frac{a_0-b_0s}{k}.
\tag{26}
\]

The numerator is positive because \(s<1/100\) by (23) and

\[
 a_0-\frac{b_0}{100}=\frac1{160}>0.
\]

Since \(g_0\le s\), equation (26) also gives

\[
 s\ge s_0:=\frac{a_0}{b_0+k}
 =\frac{a_0}{8+c_0}
 =\frac{88}{33075}.
\tag{27}
\]

Substituting (26) in (25) and using \(k+b_0-c_0=8\) gives the sharper lower bound

\[
 \zeta\ge\frac{8(a_0-b_0s)}{k}.
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (23) and (28) now imply

\[
 \widehat Z_a
 <\frac{k(1/100-s)}{8(a_0-b_0s)}.
\tag{29}
\]

The right side of (29) is strictly decreasing for \(s<1/100\), because its derivative has the sign of

\[
 \frac{b_0}{100}-a_0=-\frac1{160}<0.
\]

Using (27), its maximum is therefore attained at \(s=s_0\).  Since \(a_0-b_0s_0=ks_0\),

\[
 \widehat Z_a
 <\frac{1/100-s_0}{8s_0}
 =\frac{971}{2816}.
\tag{30}
\]

This proves (10).

Every term in (9) is nonnegative.  Hence, for each
\(r\in\{r_\ell,r_F,r_*,r_y,r_a^\dagger\}\), equations (9)--(10) give

\[
 32\gamma_d\frac{2^{n-2}}{r+1}<\frac{971}{2816},
\]

which is exactly (11).  The first term in (9) similarly gives

\[
 \frac{2^{n-2}}{r_{\bar c}+1}<\frac{971}{2816},
\]

which is (12).  Every estimate used the actual \(I\)-triangle masses, the actual contact submatching, and the accepted single-use capacity ledger.  No source occurrence, physical target fiber, or packet score was added twice.
