## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, $d\ge2$, and let $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$ be compact convex with positive mixed $d$-volume $\mu$. Assume every supporting plane of every positive integer fiber meets that fiber in a set of zero $\mathcal H^{d-1}$ measure. For any two distinct maximizers $y_i=(p_i,x_i)$ and $y_j=(p_j,x_j)$ of raw closed ambient halfspace depth over $C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$, there is a $\delta_{ij}>0$ such that every minimizing through-cap at $y_i$ with margin $L(z,x)=a\cdot(z-p_i)+u\cdot(x-x_i)$, $\|u\|=1$, satisfies $L(y_j)\le-\delta_{ij}$. In particular all these caps miss a fixed positive-radius continuous ball centered at $x_j$ inside its own fiber. When there are at least two maximizers one radius works for every ordered pair in this fixed body.

## Proof

The actual positive fibers are a finite compatible family since they come from $C$. Fact 286b2220d4bcff4f gives strict exclusion, positivity and own-fiber interior at the maximizing marks, and finiteness of the maximizing set. In particular every minimizing through-cap at $y_i$ has negative margin at $y_j$. Suppose the supremum is zero and take a sequence of these caps whose margins at $y_j$ tend to zero. Write its thresholds as $t_k(z)=u_k\cdot x_i-a_k\cdot(z-p_i)$. Apply fact 0a73ba4a9c7b24d1 on the finite positive base set with a common ball containing all fibers. The realizing finite cap has unit continuous direction $u=\lim u_k$, exact limiting thresholds at the finite-limit sites, and mixed mass equal to the limiting minimum mass. At $p_i$ the limiting threshold equals $u\cdot x_i$ by the through-point equality. At $p_j$ it equals $u\cdot x_j$, since $u_k\cdot x_j-t_k(p_j)\to0$. Both sites belong to the positive set by own-fiber interior, so the realizing cap passes through both points exactly. The strict exclusion in 286b2220d4bcff4f forbids this. Hence some $\delta_{ij}>0$ works. Changing only the continuous coordinate at $p_j$ by norm at most $\delta_{ij}/2$ leaves the margin negative. Choose the radius also smaller than the distance of $x_j$ to its own fiber boundary. Finitely many ordered pairs allow taking the minimum of these radii.
