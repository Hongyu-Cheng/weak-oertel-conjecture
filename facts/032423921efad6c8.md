## Statement

For a positive integer \(n\) and a nonempty finite set \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\), define \(r_A(j):=|\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v\in A,\ j-v\in A\}|\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\). Displacements are oriented; zero is counted exactly once at centers in \(A\). Call \(A\) lattice-convex if \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^n\).

First, the following convex-realization lemma holds independently of the tree construction. For every integer \(m\ge1\), if \(B_m:=\{0,1\}^m\) and \(E\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^m\) consists of points having exactly one coordinate equal to \(2\), then \(\operatorname{conv}(B_m\cup E)\cap\mathbb Z^m=B_m\cup E\). No restriction is imposed on the choice of \(E\), and \(E\) may be empty.

For the tree construction let \(m,Q\ge1\) be integers, put \([m]:=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and define
\[
\mathcal T_{m,Q}:=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{Q}[m]^\ell,
\qquad n_{m,Q}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{Q}m^\ell,
\qquad \eta_m:=1-2^{-m}\binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}.
\]
The integer \(Q\) denotes tree depth, replacing the supplied construction's parameter \(q\); it is unrelated to parity-collision parameters. The symbol \(d\) remains reserved for continuous fiber dimension. Coordinates of \(\mathbb R^{n_{m,Q}}\) are indexed by the nonempty words in \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}\). The empty word \(\varnothing\) is the root. For \(u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}\cup\{\varnothing\}\) with \(|u|<Q\), write \(x[u]:=(x_{u1},\ldots,x_{um})\) for the block of child coordinates, where \(|u|\) is word length.

Choose any partition of \(2^{[m]}\), identified with \(\{0,1\}^m\), into symmetric saturated chains. Such a chain has sets of each cardinality from \(k\) through \(m-k\), with every successive set obtained by adding one element; the existence of the partition is proved below. Let \(D_m\) be the Boolean vectors that are not terminal in their chains, and let \(F_m:D_m\to\{0,1\}^m\) be the chain successor. This is an injective partial map with
\[
|D_m|=2^m\eta_m,
\qquad F_m(b)=b+e_{i_m(b)},
\qquad b_{i_m(b)}=0,
\qquad \mathbf1\in\operatorname{im}F_m.
\]
Here \(e_i\) is a coordinate unit vector in the indicated Boolean block. For a tree coordinate \(u\), \(e_u\) denotes the unit vector of the full tree-coordinate space.

Given \(b\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\), start at \(u_0=\varnothing\). Whenever \(b[u_{\ell-1}]\in D_m\), select the child \(u_\ell:=u_{\ell-1}i_m(b[u_{\ell-1}])\). Stop at the first block outside \(D_m\), or on reaching depth \(Q\), and let \(h(b)\) be the resulting path length. For \(0\le k\le h(b)\), put
\[
z_k(b):=b+2\sum_{\ell=1}^k e_{u_\ell},
\qquad
A_{m,Q}:=\{z_k(b):b\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}},\ 0\le k\le h(b)\},
\qquad P_{m,Q}:=\operatorname{conv}(A_{m,Q}).
\]
Every selected source coordinate is zero, so these points are ternary. The level of \(z_k(b)\) is \(k\), equivalently its number of coordinates equal to \(2\). Then \(P_{m,Q}\subseteq[0,2]^{n_{m,Q}}\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope, and \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}=A_{m,Q}\).

The points of level \(k\) number exactly \(2^{n_{m,Q}}\eta_m^k\), for \(0\le k\le Q\). Each parity class has the unique Boolean source \(b\) and consists precisely of \(z_0(b),\ldots,z_{h(b)}(b)\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|A_{m,Q}|}{2^{n_{m,Q}}}&=\sum_{k=0}^{Q}\eta_m^k,\\
|\{b:h(b)=k\}|&=2^{n_{m,Q}}\eta_m^k(1-\eta_m)
&& (0\le k<Q),\\
|\{b:h(b)=Q\}|&=2^{n_{m,Q}}\eta_m^Q.
\end{aligned}
\]

The following reconstruction describes every center and every reflected pair. Fix \(j\in A_{m,Q}\) of level \(s\). Its coordinates equal to \(2\) form an initial rooted path \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\). Starting from \(u_s\), or from the root if \(s=0\), extend this path while the current node has depth less than \(Q\): if the Boolean block \(j[u]\in\operatorname{im}F_m\), take its unique predecessor \(c:=F_m^{-1}(j[u])\) and the next child \(u\,i_m(c)\); otherwise stop. Let \(h_-(j)\) be the number of successful additional steps. For every \(t\in\{s+1,\ldots,s+h_-(j)\}\), define
\[
b^{(t)}:=j-2\sum_{\ell=1}^s e_{u_\ell}
-\sum_{\ell=s+1}^{t}e_{u_\ell}.
\]
The vector \(b^{(t)}\) is Boolean, its forward path has length at least \(t\), and the unique unordered distinct pair with center \(j\) and upper endpoint level \(t\) is \(\{z_s(b^{(t)}),z_t(b^{(t)})\}\). These are all unordered distinct pairs centered at \(j\). Thus every nonzero reflected pair has endpoint levels \(s<t\), with at most one unordered pair for each \(t\), and
\[
r_{A_{m,Q}}(j)=1+2h_-(j)\le 1+2(Q-s),
\qquad
r_{A_{m,Q}}(\mathbf1)=\max_j r_{A_{m,Q}}(j)=2Q+1.
\]
The all-ones center has exactly one unordered pair for every upper endpoint level \(t=1,\ldots,Q\). Every integer center outside \(A_{m,Q}\) has reflection count zero. The single-block chain construction is the case \(Q=1\), with \(|A_{m,1}|=2^{m+1}-\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) and maximum count three.

For the sequence \(m_Q:=2(Q+1)^4\), write \(n_Q:=n_{m_Q,Q}\), \(A_Q:=A_{m_Q,Q}\), and \(\tau_Q:=|A_Q|/2^{n_Q}\). Then
\[
Q+\tfrac12<\tau_Q<Q+1,
\qquad \max_j r_{A_Q}(j)=2Q+1,
\qquad \frac{\max_j r_{A_Q}(j)}{\tau_Q}\longrightarrow2.
\]
In particular, for every real \(p>1\) and every \(a>0\), some \(A_Q\) satisfies \(\max_jr_{A_Q}(j)<a\tau_Q^p\). More generally, if \(g:[1,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) satisfies \(g(t)\to\infty\) as \(t\to\infty\), no inequality \(\max_jr_A(j)\ge(|A|/2^n)g(|A|/2^n)\) holds uniformly for all full-dimensional convex ternary lattice sets with \(|A|/2^n\ge1\). Conversely, every nonempty finite lattice-convex \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies \(\max_jr_A(j)\ge |A|/2^n\). Thus exponent one works with coefficient one, and every exponent greater than one fails with every positive coefficient.

There is also an exact actual-body obstruction. On \(P_{m,Q}\), define
\[
\rho(x):=\min\left\{1,\ \min_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}
\left[1-\frac{|u|}{|u|+1}(x_u-1)\right]\right\},
\qquad
C_{m,Q}:=\{(x,y):x\in P_{m,Q},\ 0\le y\le\rho(x)\}.
\]
The set \(C_{m,Q}\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_{m,Q}}\times\mathbb R\) is a full-dimensional compact convex polytope with continuous dimension \(d=1\). For every integer \(z\), let \(C_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C_{m,Q}\}\), and use the actual fiber volumes \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_1(C_z)\), with zero for empty fibers. They are exactly
\[
w_z=\begin{cases}1/(s+1),&z\in A_{m,Q}\text{ has level }s,\\0,&z\notin A_{m,Q}.
\end{cases}
\]
The table is globally extendable: there exists a finite-valued \(f:\mathbb R^{n_{m,Q}}\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing with \(w_z\) at every integer point, including zeros, and satisfying \(f((1-t)x+tx')\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(x')^t\) for all \(x,x'\) and \(0<t<1\), with a zero endpoint making the right-hand side zero. In fact, take \(f=\rho\) on \(P_{m,Q}\) and zero outside. Every Boolean lattice point has weight one.

Put \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}}w_z\). For every fixed center \(j\) with \(w_j>0\), define \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\), \(\mathcal U_j:=\{v\in\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}:\alpha(v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(w_j^2/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\) for \(v\in\mathcal U_j\). All logarithms are natural. Define
\[
B_j^\Delta(L):=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
\quad(L\ge0),
\qquad
K(w):=\max_{\substack{j:w_j>0\\L\ge0}}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}.
\]
These sums are oriented and include zero. The curvatures are nonnegative, and the maximum is attained. For a center \(j\) of level \(s\), every nonzero pair with endpoint levels \(s,t\) has \(\alpha(v)=1/(t+1)\) and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log((t+1)/(s+1))\). Hence, with the reconstruction length defined above,
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_j^\Delta(L)
&=\frac1{s+1}
+2\sum_{\substack{s<t\le s+h_-(j)\\t+1\le(s+1)e^L}}\frac1{t+1}
\le1+2L,\\
K(w)&\le2,
\qquad \frac{W}{2^{n_{m,Q}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{Q}\frac{\eta_m^k}{k+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(m=m_Q\), denote this actual table by \(w^{[Q]}\) and its total volume by \(W_Q\). With \(H_{Q+1}:=\sum_{k=0}^{Q}1/(k+1)\), one has
\[
\frac{W_Q}{2^{n_Q}}>H_{Q+1}-\frac1{Q+1}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{2^{n_Q}K(w^{[Q]})}{W_Q}
<\frac2{H_{Q+1}-1/(Q+1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore no absolute \(c>0\) gives \(K(w)\ge c\,2^{-n}W\) for every globally extendable ternary table, or even for every actual interval-fiber table of a full-dimensional convex polytope. For \(m\ge2\) the tables here are not coordinatewise nonincreasing and cannot have the form \(w_z=b(\{u:z_u=2\})\) throughout the ternary cube. Their full Boolean plateau therefore does not put them in either of those restricted classes.

## Proof

For the general convex-realization lemma, put \(P:=\operatorname{conv}(B_m\cup E)\subseteq[0,2]^m\), and let \(x\in P\cap\mathbb Z^m\). If \(x\) has no coordinate equal to \(2\), then \(x\in B_m\). Otherwise choose \(i\) with \(x_i=2\), and represent \(x\) as a convex combination of points of \(B_m\cup E\), retaining only positive coefficients. Since every generating coordinate is at most \(2\), every positively weighted point has coordinate \(i\) equal to \(2\). These points belong to \(E\), and their other coordinates lie in \(\{0,1\}\). Their integer average in any other coordinate is therefore either \(0\) or \(1\), and all positively weighted points must agree with that average. Thus every positively weighted point equals \(x\), proving \(x\in E\). The opposite inclusion is immediate.

We next construct the symmetric saturated chain partition. In dimension zero use the one chain consisting of the empty set. Suppose a chain in dimension \(m\) is \(S_k\subset S_{k+1}\subset\cdots\subset S_{m-k}\), with \(|S_h|=h\). Its two copies after adjoining element \(m+1\) are partitioned into the chains
\[
\begin{aligned}
&S_k,\ S_{k+1},\ldots,S_{m-k},\ S_{m-k}\cup\{m+1\},\\
&S_k\cup\{m+1\},\ S_{k+1}\cup\{m+1\},\ldots,
S_{m-k-1}\cup\{m+1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Omit the second chain when empty. The first has ranks from \(k\) to \((m+1)-k\); the second, when present, has ranks from \(k+1\) to \((m+1)-(k+1)\). Both are saturated and symmetric. They are disjoint and contain exactly the two copies of the old chain. Applying this operation to all old chains proves the asserted partition by induction.

Every symmetric chain contains exactly one set of size \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\). Thus there are \(\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) chains. Deleting the one terminal vertex in each leaves \(D_m\) with the asserted size. The successor increases exactly one zero coordinate to one. It is injective because each noninitial chain vertex has exactly one predecessor, and different chains are disjoint. The chain containing \([m]\) has ranks from \(0\) through \(m\), so \(\mathbf1\) has a predecessor whenever \(m\ge1\). This proves every asserted property of \(F_m\).

The path construction is well-defined because each successful step moves to a strictly deeper block, and each selected source coordinate is zero. Each generator \(z_k(b)\) has exactly \(k\) coordinates equal to \(2\), forming an initial rooted path, and all other coordinates are Boolean. Its reduction modulo two is exactly \(b\). In particular all Boolean points occur at level zero, and \(P_{m,Q}\) contains \([0,1]^{n_{m,Q}}\). The hull is a compact, full-dimensional polytope contained in the ternary box.

To prove its lattice-convexity, let \(x\in P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}\), and take a convex representation of \(x\) by generators with positive coefficients. The point \(x\) is ternary. If it has no coordinate equal to \(2\), it belongs to the Boolean cube and hence to \(A_{m,Q}\). Otherwise, for every coordinate \(u\) with \(x_u=2\), every positively weighted generator also has coordinate \(u\) equal to \(2\).

The set of these coordinates is an initial rooted path. Indeed, it is contained in the path of each positively weighted generator, so it contains no incomparable nodes. If it contains a node, every generator has coordinate \(2\) at each ancestor of that node, and therefore \(x\) also has coordinate \(2\) at every such ancestor. Write this initial path as \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\).

In the block of children of \(u_{\ell-1}\), every generator has value \(2\) at the same selected child \(u_\ell\). It has only Boolean values at the other children, since its coordinates equal to \(2\) form a path. The averages at those other children are integers, so all positively weighted generators agree there. Hence their common source block is exactly \(x[u_{\ell-1}]\) with its selected \(2\) replaced by \(0\). This block belongs to \(D_m\) and its successor selects \(u_\ell\), because the assertion holds for every generating source.

Let \(b\) be obtained from \(x\) by replacing all its coordinates equal to \(2\) by zero. It is Boolean. The preceding argument shows successively that its first \(s\) forward selections are \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\). Coordinates in all other blocks impose no condition on stopping at level \(s\). Consequently \(x=z_s(b)\in A_{m,Q}\), proving \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}=A_{m,Q}\).

For the level census, reduction modulo two recovers the source \(b\), and within a source the level recovers the point. To have \(h(b)\ge k\), choose an ordered list of \(k\) local patterns in \(D_m\). These patterns determine the successive visited blocks and assign their entries. Distinct parent nodes have disjoint child-coordinate blocks, so exactly \(mk\) coordinates have been assigned. Every other Boolean coordinate is unrestricted, giving \(2^{n_{m,Q}-mk}\) choices. Conversely, any source with \(h(b)\ge k\) recovers exactly this ordered list and the remaining entries. The count is therefore
\[
|\{b:h(b)\ge k\}|=|D_m|^k2^{n_{m,Q}-mk}
=2^{n_{m,Q}}\eta_m^k.
\]
This equals the number of level-\(k\) points. Summing gives the total cardinality. Each of the \(2^{n_{m,Q}}\) parity classes contains precisely the levels belonging to its source \(b\), so its size is \(h(b)+1\). Taking differences of the displayed counts at \(k\) and \(k+1\), and retaining the last count at \(Q\), proves the full parity-class census.

Consider now an unordered distinct pair with an integer midpoint. Two integer points have an integer midpoint if and only if they have the same parity. Thus the pair must be \(\{z_a(b),z_t(b)\}\) for one source \(b\) and two levels \(a<t\le h(b)\). Its midpoint is
\[
b+2\sum_{\ell=1}^a e_{u_\ell}
+\sum_{\ell=a+1}^t e_{u_\ell}.
\]
Its coordinates equal to \(2\) are exactly the first \(a\) selected nodes, so its level is \(a\). It belongs to \(A_{m,Q}\) by lattice-convexity. Therefore, for a fixed center \(j\) of level \(s\), the smaller endpoint level must be \(s\).

The first \(s\) selected nodes are determined by the coordinates of \(j\) equal to \(2\). At the next visited block, only one coordinate has changed from source value zero to midpoint value one. Hence the midpoint block equals \(F_m\) of the source block. If the midpoint block is not in the image of \(F_m\), no pair with a higher endpoint level exists. If it is in the image, injectivity recovers the source block uniquely and determines the next selected child. Repeating this argument determines every continuation step through the higher endpoint level \(t\). Outside the first \(s\) nodes and the reconstructed continuation, the source coordinates equal those of \(j\). Thus a pair, if present, has exactly the source \(b^{(t)}\) stated above, proving uniqueness and the necessity of all the inverse steps.

Conversely, suppose inverse reconstruction succeeds through level \(t\). Every reconstructed midpoint block is Boolean: all coordinates of \(j\) equal to \(2\) are in the first \(s\) nodes. The predecessor of such a block changes exactly its selected coordinate from one to zero. Therefore \(b^{(t)}\) is Boolean. Its first \(s\) forward selections remain valid because the modified continuation blocks are disjoint from the first \(s\) visited blocks. In each later visited block its source pattern is the reconstructed predecessor, so its next forward selection is exactly the child specified by inverse reconstruction. Thus \(h(b^{(t)})\ge t\).

The points \(z_s(b^{(t)})\) and \(z_t(b^{(t)})\) are consequently in \(A_{m,Q}\); the midpoint formula gives their average as \(j\). They are distinct and have the required levels. This proves sufficiency, the complete reconstruction, and \(r_{A_{m,Q}}(j)=1+2h_-(j)\), including the single zero displacement. At \(j=\mathbf1\), every encountered block equals the all-ones Boolean block, which always has a predecessor. Reconstruction therefore succeeds for all \(Q\) steps, proving one pair per level and the exact maximum \(2Q+1\). A center outside \(A_{m,Q}\) cannot be the integer midpoint of two points of the convex set \(P_{m,Q}\), so its reflection count is zero.

The lower bound with exponent one holds for every finite lattice-convex \(A\). Let \(N_\epsilon\) be the number of its points in parity class \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\). There is a bijection between ordered same-parity pairs \((x,y)\in A^2\) and the pairs \((j,v)\) counted by \(\sum_{j\in A}r_A(j)\): take \(j=(x+y)/2\) and \(v=(x-y)/2\). Lattice-convexity ensures that the integer midpoint lies in \(A\). This includes diagonal pairs and zero displacements. Consequently,
\[
\sum_{j\in A}r_A(j)=\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^n}N_\epsilon^2
\ge 2^{-n}\left(\sum_\epsilon N_\epsilon\right)^2
=2^{-n}|A|^2.
\]
The inequality follows by expanding \(\sum_\epsilon(N_\epsilon-|A|/2^n)^2\ge0\). Averaging over \(j\in A\) proves \(\max_jr_A(j)\ge|A|/2^n\).

To analyze the tree sequence, use the elementary central-binomial identity and estimate, valid for every integer \(r\ge1\),
\[
\frac{\binom{2r}{r}}{4^r}
=\prod_{h=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac1{2h}\right)
\le\exp\left(-\frac12\sum_{h=1}^{r}\frac1h\right)
\le\frac1{\sqrt{r+1}}.
\]
The first inequality uses \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) for \(0<u<1\), and the second uses \(\sum_{h=1}^r1/h\ge\int_1^{r+1}du/u=\log(r+1)\). For \(m_Q=2(Q+1)^4\), this yields \(0<\delta_Q:=1-\eta_{m_Q}<1/(Q+1)^2\). Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_Q
&=\sum_{k=0}^{Q}(1-\delta_Q)^k
\ge\sum_{k=0}^{Q}(1-k\delta_Q)\\
&=(Q+1)-\frac{Q(Q+1)}2\delta_Q
>Q+\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(0<\eta_{m_Q}<1\) and \(Q\ge1\), also \(\tau_Q<Q+1\). These bounds and the exact maximum give \(\max_jr_{A_Q}(j)/\tau_Q\to2\). For any fixed \(p>1\),
\[
\frac{\max_jr_{A_Q}(j)}{\tau_Q^p}
<2\left(Q+\frac12\right)^{1-p}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves the failure with every positive coefficient. Also \(\tau_Q\to\infty\) and \(\max_jr_{A_Q}(j)/\tau_Q<2\), so any \(g\) tending to infinity eventually violates the asserted multiplicative improvement along this same sequence. No Cartesian amplification or unproved counting hypothesis is used.

It remains to verify the actual-body obstruction. The function \(\rho\) is the minimum of finitely many affine functions on \(P_{m,Q}\); it is continuous and concave. Because \(0\le x_u\le2\) and \(1\le|u|\le Q\), every displayed affine function is at least \(1/(Q+1)\), and thus \(1/(Q+1)\le\rho(x)\le1\). For a lattice point of level \(s\), the coordinates equal to \(2\) occur at depths \(1,\ldots,s\). A coordinate equal to \(0\) or \(1\) contributes an affine value at least one; a coordinate equal to \(2\) at depth \(\ell\) contributes \(1/(\ell+1)\). Hence \(\rho(z)=1/(s+1)\), including \(\rho(z)=1\) at level zero.

The conditions defining \(C_{m,Q}\) are the polytope conditions for \(x\in P_{m,Q}\), the lower bound \(y\ge0\), and the finitely many affine upper bounds that define \(\rho\). They make \(C_{m,Q}\) a closed bounded convex polytope. All its coefficients can be taken rational, since \(P_{m,Q}\) is the convex hull of integer points and the displayed upper bounds are rational. For \(x\in[0,1]^{n_{m,Q}}\), all those affine values are at least one, so \(\rho(x)=1\). Therefore \(C_{m,Q}\) contains \([0,1]^{n_{m,Q}}\times[0,1]\) and is full-dimensional. The integer base support is exactly \(P_{m,Q}\cap\mathbb Z^{n_{m,Q}}=A_{m,Q}\), and its fibers there are the intervals \([0,\rho(z)]\). This proves the asserted actual-volume table without a limiting realization argument.

For global log-concavity, if \(x,x'\in P_{m,Q}\) and \(0<t<1\), concavity and the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
\rho((1-t)x+tx')\ge(1-t)\rho(x)+t\rho(x')
\ge\rho(x)^{1-t}\rho(x')^t.
\]
If an endpoint is outside \(P_{m,Q}\), the corresponding zero extension makes the right-hand side zero, and the inequality follows from nonnegativity. Thus the stated finite-valued global extension agrees with every lattice value, including all zeros.

Fix a positive center \(j\) of level \(s\). The exact pair reconstruction shows that each nonzero pair has one endpoint at level \(s\) and the other at level \(t>s\), with precisely one unordered pair for every \(s<t\le s+h_-(j)\). Its endpoint volumes are \(1/(s+1)\) and \(1/(t+1)\), whereas the center volume is \(1/(s+1)\). Direct substitution gives \(\alpha(v)=1/(t+1)\) and \(\Delta_j(v)=\log((t+1)/(s+1))\ge0\). The zero displacement has \(\alpha(0)=1/(s+1)\) and \(\Delta_j(0)=0\). Including both nonzero orientations proves the asserted exact formula for \(B_j^\Delta(L)\).

If the sum in that formula is nonempty, let \(T\) be its largest index. Since \(u\mapsto1/u\) is decreasing, each summand \(1/(t+1)\) is at most its integral over \([t,t+1]\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{t=s+1}^{T}\frac1{t+1}
\le\int_{s+1}^{T+1}\frac{du}{u}
=\log\frac{T+1}{s+1}\le L.
\]
If the sum is empty its value is zero, so the same bound holds. Since \(1/(s+1)\le1\), this proves \(B_j^\Delta(L)\le1+2L\) for every center and every \(L\ge0\), and hence \(K(w)\le2\). There are only finitely many positive centers and reflected pairs. For each center the cutoff numerator changes only at its finitely many nonnegative curvature values, including zero, and the denominator increases between them. A maximum is therefore attained at one of these finitely many thresholds, as stated.

The exact level count and the actual volume at each level give \(W/2^{n_{m,Q}}=\sum_{k=0}^{Q}\eta_m^k/(k+1)\). For \(m=m_Q\), the same Bernoulli bound yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{W_Q}{2^{n_Q}}
&\ge\sum_{k=0}^{Q}\frac{1-k\delta_Q}{k+1}\\
&=H_{Q+1}-\delta_Q\bigl((Q+1)-H_{Q+1}\bigr)
>H_{Q+1}-\frac1{Q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last quantity tends to infinity, since \(H_{Q+1}\ge\log(Q+2)\). Combining it with \(K(w^{[Q]})\le2\) proves the claimed normalized score limit and refutes every positive uniform scalar coefficient.

Finally, for \(m\ge2\), consider the first two edges of the symmetric chain containing the empty set: \(\varnothing\to\{i_1\}\to\{i_1,i_2\}\), where \(i_1\ne i_2\). Take the global Boolean source with root-block pattern \(e_{i_1}\) and all other coordinates zero. Its level-one point \(x\) has coordinate \(1\) at the root child \(i_1\), coordinate \(2\) at the root child \(i_2\), and zeros elsewhere; hence \(w_x=1/2\). Obtain \(y\) from \(x\) by changing its root-child coordinate \(i_1\) from \(1\) to \(0\). Its Boolean parity source is the all-zero vector, whose first selected root child is \(i_1\), not \(i_2\). Since \(y\) has level one, it cannot belong to \(A_{m,Q}\), and \(w_y=0\). Thus \(y\le x\) but \(w_y<w_x\), and \(x,y\) have exactly the same set of coordinates equal to \(2\) but different weights. This proves both asserted failures of the restricted hypotheses. The counterexample concerns the uniform scalar lower bound alone; its small score supplies no upper bound on the halfspace depth of the actual bodies.
