## Statement

Let \(s,d\) be nonnegative integers, put \(n=s+d\ge1\) and \(K=2^n\), and assume \(s^2\le n\). Let \(C_0\subseteq\mathbb Z^s\) contain zero and satisfy
$$
\operatorname{conv}(C_0)\cap\mathbb Z^s=C_0,\qquad
\operatorname{conv}(C_0\cup(-C_0))\cap\mathbb Z^s=C_0\cup(-C_0).
\tag{1}
$$
Assume that \(C_0\) contains at most one representative of each coordinatewise parity class. In particular it is finite, \(|C_0|\le2^s\), and \(C_0\cap(-C_0)=\lbrace0\rbrace\). Set
$$
B=C_0\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^d,\qquad
A=B\cup(-B),\qquad Q=\operatorname{conv}(A).
$$
The zero-dimensional lattice and zero-dimensional cube each mean their singleton zero set. Thus both \(s=0\) and \(d=0\) are included when the other hypotheses hold.

Then \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\), and every distinct supported pair with an integral midpoint is antipodal. For an arbitrary vector \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), retain the entire original affine law
$$
Z=\sum_{x\in A}e^{\ell\cdot x},\qquad
p_x=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot x}}{Z}\quad(x\in A),
$$
extended by zero off \(A\). An additive constant in an affine field cancels from this law. Use exactly the canonical full reflected quantities of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,
&\kappa_j(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_p(j)},\\
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}},
&V_j&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}Y_j^2,\\
q_j&=\frac{p_jH_p(j)^2}{b(p)},
&\nu&=\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
Both nonzero orientations occur in a row sum, and the central atom occurs once. Define the atom quantity \(A_0=K^2\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3\) and \(g=\log(K^2b(p))\). All logarithms are natural. With the single absolute constant \(C=2^{5000}\),
$$
A_0<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g>0\quad\hbox{and}\quad0\le\nu\le Cg.
\tag{3}
$$
There is no bound on any seed coordinate in this assertion.

There is also the following unconditional heterogeneous-product consequence. For any \(L\ge1\), choose \(L\) seeds, dimensions, and fields satisfying the preceding geometric hypotheses, with respective indices \(i\), and put
$$
\begin{aligned}
n_i&=s_i+d_i,& A_i&=(C_{0,i}\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{d_i})
 \cup-(C_{0,i}\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{d_i}),\\
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}e^{\ell_i\cdot x},&
p_{i,x}&=\frac{e^{\ell_i\cdot x}}{Z_i},\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i,
\qquad c_* =\frac14e^{-C}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each \(n_i\ge1\) and \(s_i^2\le n_i\); the fields and dimensions can differ arbitrarily between factors. Define the original ambient closed-halfspace depth by
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):
\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace.
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^2\ge c_*4^{-N},\qquad
M_3(P):=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^3\ge c_*^{3/2}8^{-N}.
\tag{4}
$$
No factor in (4) has an activity restriction. The product support is its complete convex lattice census.

The class in (3)--(4) includes the seed in dimension \(s=3\)
$$
C_0=\lbrace0,e_1,e_2,e_3,(1,1,3),(1,-1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)\rbrace.
\tag{5}
$$
For every \(d\ge6\), its suspension satisfies \(s^2\le n\), occupies every parity class, and is active at the uniform field. For every integer \(m\ge1\) and every \(B'\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) containing zero, no injective affine map from \(\mathbb R^{3+d}\) to \(\mathbb R^m\) maps that suspended support onto \(B'\cup(-B')\). Here \(m\) has the Boolean target-dimension meaning used in a91f98e9427d1a45, and \(B'\) distinguishes that target subset from the suspension representatives \(B\).

## Proof

We first establish the geometry, including the endpoints. Since the convex hull of a finite Cartesian product is the product of its convex hulls, every point of \(Q\) can be written
$$
\lambda(u,t)-(1-\lambda)(u',t'),\qquad
0\le\lambda\le1,\quad
u,u'\in\operatorname{conv}(C_0),\quad t,t'\in[0,1]^d.
\tag{6}
$$
For \(0<\lambda<1\), every free coordinate is strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\). If the point in (6) is integral, all its free coordinates are zero. Its seed part belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(C_0\cup(-C_0))\), so (1) puts it in \(C_0\cup(-C_0)\). The entire point therefore belongs to \(A\). At \(\lambda=1\), integrality puts the seed part in \(C_0\) by (1) and the free part in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^d\); at \(\lambda=0\), the corresponding conclusion is membership in \(-B\). This proves \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n=A\). The same reasoning applies when either block has dimension zero.

Parity injectivity of \(C_0\) and of the Boolean cube gives parity injectivity of \(B\). It also implies \(B\cap(-B)=\lbrace0\rbrace\): a common point has free part zero, and its seed part and its negative are two members of \(C_0\) with the same parity. Consequently the zero parity class of \(A\) is just zero, and each of its other represented parity classes is precisely \(\lbrace b,-b\rbrace\) for a unique nonzero \(b\in B\). Distinct points have an integral midpoint exactly when they have the same parity. Every nontrivial such pair is therefore antipodal, with midpoint zero. In particular
$$
R_0=A,\qquad R_j=\lbrace j\rbrace\quad(j\ne0).
\tag{7}
$$

Write \(m_0=|C_0|\), \(M=|B|=m_0 2^d\), and \(h=|A|=2M-1\). The symbol \(h\) retains its support-cardinality meaning from a6c872b23ec15ebd; \(s\), not \(h\), is the seed dimension. Here \(M\le K\). Define \(Z_3=\sum_{x\in A}e^{3\ell\cdot x}\). Direct substitution in the canonical definitions (2) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(0)&=h/Z,&H_p(j)&=p_j\quad(j\ne0),\\
A_0&=K^2Z_3/Z^3,&K^2b(p)&=K^2(Z_3-1+h^2)/Z^3,\\
\kappa_0(x)&=1/h,&Y_0(x)&=\ell\cdot x,\\
V_0&=\frac2h\sum_{b\in B}(\ell\cdot b)^2,&
q_0&=\frac{h^2}{Z_3-1+h^2},\qquad \nu=q_0V_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Every other row has zero score and variance. These are the same identities as the one-center calculation in a6c872b23ec15ebd, now established directly for this support. In particular \(Z\), \(p\), \(b\), \(A_0\), and \(\nu\) have not been renormalized or replaced.

Assume henceforth in the one-factor argument that \(A_0<1\). Hölder gives \(\sum_xp_x^3\ge h^{-2}\), so
$$
h>K,\qquad M>K/2,\qquad M>1.
\tag{9}
$$
For the uniform average over the representative set \(B\), define
$$
c_b=\cosh(\ell\cdot b),\qquad
\mu=\mathbb E_Bc_b,\qquad
\mathcal V=\mathbb E_B(c_b-\mu)^2.
$$
This uniform average is only an auxiliary finite average. The original law remains \(p\). Since zero is counted just once in \(A\), the exact identities are
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=2M\mu-1,\\
Z_3&=8M\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-6M\mu-1,\\
\mathbb E_Bc_b^3-\mu^3
&=\mathbb E_B[(c_b-\mu)^2(c_b+2\mu)]\ge2\mu\mathcal V.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
Here \(\mu\ge1\), and the second identity uses \(\cosh(3t)=4\cosh^3t-3\cosh t\). Substitute (10) into \(K^2Z_3<Z^3\), expand, and divide by \(8K^2M\mu\). This proves the exact active algebra used in a6c872b23ec15ebd without a Boolean assumption on the seed:
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\mathcal V+\left(1-\frac{M^2}{K^2}\right)\mu^2
&<\frac34+\frac1{8M\mu}-\frac{3M\mu}{2K^2}
 +\frac3{4K^2}-\frac1{8K^2M\mu}\\
&\le\frac34+\frac18+\frac3{16}
=\frac{17}{16}<2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
The last inequality uses \(K\ge2\), \(M\mu\ge1\), and discards negative terms. Since \(M\le K\), the two terms on the left are nonnegative. In particular \(\mathcal V<1\).

We now prove the new seed/free-fiber estimate
$$
\mu\le8d+3\sqrt{m_0}+8.
\tag{12}
$$
Let \(\eta\in\mathbb R^d\) be the free-coordinate block of \(\ell\). If \(\mu<\max\lbrace8,2\sqrt{m_0}\rbrace\), then \(\mu<8+2\sqrt{m_0}\), which already implies (12). Otherwise \(\mu\ge8\) and \(\mu\ge2\sqrt{m_0}\). Put \(z=\operatorname{arcosh}\mu\), \(\delta=4/\mu\), and
$$
G=\lbrace b\in B:|c_b-\mu|\le2\rbrace.
$$
Since \(\sum_{b\in B}(c_b-\mu)^2=M\mathcal V<M\), one has \(|G|>3M/4\). The derivative of \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) is at most \(2/\mu\) on \([\mu-2,\mu+2]\) when \(\mu\ge8\). Thus
$$
\bigl||\ell\cdot b|-z\bigr|\le\delta\quad(b\in G).
\tag{13}
$$
For each free coordinate \(i\), flipping that bit permutes \(B\) itself. Two subsets of \(B\) of cardinality greater than \(M/2\) intersect, so some pair differing only in bit \(i\) has both endpoints in \(G\). Their field values each lie within \(\delta\) of either \(z\) or \(-z\), and their difference is \(\eta_i\) up to sign. Therefore there is an integer \(k_i\in\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\) such that
$$
|\eta_i-2zk_i|\le2\delta=8/\mu.
\tag{14}
$$
The complete zero-seed fiber \(\lbrace0\rbrace\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^d\) has \(2^d\) points. The total squared deviation in (11) is less than \(M=m_0 2^d\), so at least one \(t\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^d\) satisfies
$$
|\cosh(\eta\cdot t)-\mu|<\sqrt{m_0}.
\tag{15}
$$
On \([\mu-\sqrt{m_0},\mu+\sqrt{m_0}]\subseteq[\mu/2,3\mu/2]\), the derivative of \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) is at most \(3/\mu\), because \(\mu\ge8\). Hence for some \(\varepsilon\in\lbrace-1,1\rbrace\),
$$
|\eta\cdot t-\varepsilon z|\le3\sqrt{m_0}/\mu.
\tag{16}
$$
On the other hand (14), summed over the coordinates with \(t_i=1\), gives
$$
|\eta\cdot t-2zk|\le8d/\mu,
\qquad k=\sum_i k_i t_i\in\mathbb Z.
$$
The integer \(2k-\varepsilon\) is odd and nonzero. Combining this observation with (16) yields
$$
\mu z\le8d+3\sqrt{m_0}.
$$
Since \(z=\operatorname{arcosh}\mu>1\) for \(\mu\ge8\), this proves (12). If \(d=0\), the fiber is a singleton, the sums in (14) are empty, and the same odd-integer argument applies with \(k=0\). If \(s=0\), then \(m_0=1\), and every step also applies.

Because \(m_0\le2^s\), \(d\le n\), and \(n\ge1\), (12) gives
$$
2\mu\le16d+6\,2^{s/2}+16\le38n\,2^{s/2}.
$$
For real \(u\ge1\), \(\log u\le\sqrt u\): the minimum of \(\sqrt u-\log u\) occurs at \(u=4\) and is \(2-\log4>0\). Also \(\log38<4\) and \(\log2<1\). Using \(s\le\sqrt n\), we obtain
$$
\log(2\mu)\le\log38+\log n+\frac{s}{2}\log2
\le6\sqrt n.
\tag{17}
$$

The function \(u\mapsto\operatorname{arcosh}(u)^2\) is increasing and concave on \([1,\infty)\), as established in 445a9e668ee1a705. Jensen on the auxiliary uniform average, followed by (8) and (17), gives
$$
0\le\nu\le V_0
=\frac{2M}{h}\mathbb E_B(\ell\cdot b)^2
\le2\operatorname{arcosh}(\mu)^2
\le2\log^2(2\mu)\le72n.
\tag{18}
$$
Moreover \(h>K\), \(Z<2K\mu\), and \(Z_3-1\ge0\). Formula (8) therefore implies
$$
g>n\log2-3\log(2\mu)\ge n\log2-18\sqrt n.
\tag{19}
$$
For \(n\ge4096\), one has \(18/\sqrt n\le9/32<1/3\le(\log2)/2\). The last inequality follows from \(\log2=\int_1^2u^{-1}du\ge2/3\), by convexity of \(u\mapsto u^{-1}\). Thus (18)--(19) imply
$$
g>\frac{n\log2}{2},\qquad \frac\nu g<\frac{144}{\log2}\le216.
\tag{20}
$$

We next prove a uniform positive gap that also covers \(M=K\); it uses no finite classification of seeds. Let \(\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)\), with the canonical meaning from 445a9e668ee1a705. Completeness and affinity give the exact ordered-pair identity
$$
\chi(p)
=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
 p_{(x+y)/2}\sqrt{p_xp_y}
=\sum_{\rho\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}p(\rho+2\mathbb Z^n)^2.
\tag{21}
$$
Indeed every same-parity pair has a supported integral midpoint, and \(p_{(x+y)/2}=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\) under the original affine law. The zero parity mass is \(p_0=1/Z\le1/h\), since \(Z\ge h\). Each of the other \(M-1\) nonempty parity classes has mass \(2\cosh(\ell\cdot b)/Z\ge2p_0\). Cauchy--Schwarz on these classes gives
$$
\chi(p)\ge p_0^2+\frac{(1-p_0)^2}{M-1}
\ge\frac{4M-3}{(2M-1)^2}
\ge\frac{4K-3}{(2K-1)^2}.
\tag{22}
$$
For the middle inequality, the function \(u^2+(1-u)^2/(M-1)\) is decreasing on \(0\le u<1/M\), and \(p_0\le1/(2M-1)<1/M\). For the last one, \((4M-3)/(2M-1)^2\), viewed as a function of real \(M\ge1\), has derivative \(-8(M-1)/(2M-1)^3\le0\). Activity gave \(M>1\), so all these expressions are defined.

Jensen under the original \(p\) gives \(b(p)\ge\chi(p)^2\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
g&\ge2\log\left(1+\frac{K-1}{(2K-1)^2}\right)\\
&\ge\frac{K-1}{(2K-1)^2}\ge\frac1{8K}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$
Here the fraction inside the logarithm is in \([0,1]\), where \(2\log(1+u)\ge u\); the final inequality follows from \(8K(K-1)\ge(2K-1)^2\) for \(K\ge2\). In particular (23) does not require a missing parity class.

For \(1\le n<4096\), (18) and (23) give
$$
\frac\nu g\le576n2^n
<576\cdot4096\cdot2^{4096}<2^{4118}<2^{5000}.
$$
Together with (20), this proves (3) in every dimension and for all seed coordinates.

We now derive (4), retaining the original product probabilities and all their normalizers. This is the symmetric-row comparison of 445a9e668ee1a705, with the active/inactive auxiliary-row selection in a6c872b23ec15ebd; the following argument verifies it directly for the present class. Put \(K_i=2^{n_i}\) and \(A_{0,i}=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3\). For each active factor \(A_{0,i}<1\), select its full rows \(\widehat R_{i,j}=R_{i,j}\). For every other factor select \(\widehat R_{i,j}=\lbrace j\rbrace\). Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat H_i(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_{i,j}}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat b_i&=\sum_jp_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2,\\
\widehat\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{\widehat H_i(j)},
&\widehat q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}\widehat H_i(j)^2}{\widehat b_i},\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},
&\widehat V_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
\widehat\nu_i&=\sum_j\widehat q_{i,j}\widehat V_{i,j},
&g_i&=\log(K_i^2\widehat b_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$
For active factors, (3) and (23) give \(g_i>0\) and \(\widehat\nu_i\le Cg_i\). For inactive factors, \(\widehat H_i(j)=p_{i,j}\), \(\widehat\nu_i=0\), and \(g_i=\log A_{0,i}\ge0\). Thus, writing \(S_g=\sum_i g_i\),
$$
S_g\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\widehat\nu_i\le CS_g.
\tag{25}
$$
Only auxiliary rows were selected. The original probability remains exactly \(P_X=\prod_i e^{\ell_i\cdot x_i}/\prod_iZ_i\) at every supported \(X=(x_i)\).

For a supported center \(J=(j_i)\), put
$$
\widehat R_J=\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i},\qquad
\widehat H_P(J)=\prod_i\widehat H_i(j_i),\qquad
\widehat\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\widehat\kappa_{i,j_i},
$$
and let \(Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(x_i)\) and \(\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat V_{i,j_i}\). Reflection in each row preserves its auxiliary law and negates its score. Consequently independence and direct substitution give
$$
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,\qquad
P_X=\widehat H_P(J)\widehat\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}.
\tag{26}
$$
Every ambient closed halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing \(J\) contains at least one member of each distinct reflected pair \(X,2J-X\) and also contains \(J\). Summing over these orbits, counting both nonzero orientations and taking only half of the available central mass, proves
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in\widehat R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{\widehat H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The equality is the likelihood identity (26); the last inequality is Jensen followed by Cauchy--Schwarz. This lower bound holds for the infimum defining the original \(\tau_P(J)\).

Under the auxiliary product center law \(\widehat q=\bigotimes_i\widehat q_i\), one has \(\mathbb E_{\widehat q}\sigma_J^2=\sum_i\widehat\nu_i\) and the exact factorization
$$
P_J\widehat H_P(J)^2=\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \prod_i\widehat q_{i,j_i}.
$$
Squaring (27), averaging over the entire original \(P\), and using Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz again yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \mathbb E_{\widehat q}e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i\widehat b_i\right)
 \exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\widehat\nu_i}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since \(\prod_iK_i^2=4^N\), (25) gives
$$
4^NM_2(P)\ge\frac14\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{CS_g}\right)
\ge\frac14e^{-C}=c_*.
$$
The last inequality is \((\sqrt{S_g}-\sqrt C)^2\ge0\). Jensen under \(P\) gives \(M_3(P)\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the remaining bound in (4). Each factor is complete by (6), and the convex hull of the product support is the product of the factor convex hulls. Its intersection with \(\mathbb Z^N\) is therefore exactly the product support.

Finally we verify the strict non-Boolean example (5) with a finite hand certificate. Write a seed-space point as \((x,y,z)\). Every member of \(C_0\cup(-C_0)\) in (5) satisfies
$$
\begin{gathered}
|x|\le1,\quad |y|\le1,\quad |z|\le3,\quad |x+y-z|\le1,\\
|3x+3y-z|\le3,\quad |3x-2z|\le3,\quad |3y-2z|\le3.
\end{gathered}
\tag{28}
$$
These inequalities consequently contain its convex hull. For integral points, there are only the following possibilities with \(z\ge0\):
$$
\begin{array}{c|l}
z& (x,y)\\ \hline
0&(-1,0),(-1,1),(0,-1),(0,0),(0,1),(1,-1),(1,0)\\
1&(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\\
2&\text{none}\\
3&(1,1).
\end{array}
\tag{29}
$$
Here is a direct exhaustive check. At \(z=0\), the constraints reduce to \(|x|,|y|\le1\) and \(|x+y|\le1\), giving the seven listed pairs. At \(z=1\), the last two inequalities in (28) force \(x,y\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), while \(|3x+3y-1|\le3\) forces \(x+y\le1\), giving the three listed pairs. At \(z=2\), the last two force \(x=y=1\), which violates \(|3x+3y-2|\le3\). At \(z=3\), they force \(x=y=1\), which satisfies every inequality. Central symmetry supplies the negative-height slices. The resulting fifteen points are exactly \(C_0\cup(-C_0)\); all are in the convex hull by definition. This proves its complete census by hand, without relying on a numerical polyhedral census.

The functional \((x,y,z)\mapsto3x+2y+z\) is strictly positive on all seven nonzero members of \(C_0\). Thus no negative nonzero member belongs to \(\operatorname{conv}(C_0)\). The just proved symmetric census now implies \(\operatorname{conv}(C_0)\cap\mathbb Z^3=C_0\). Its eight parity residues are precisely all of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^3\), with no repetitions. Hence both hypotheses in (1) and parity injectivity hold.

For its suspension, \(M=8\cdot2^d=2^{3+d}=K\), so every parity class is represented. At the uniform field \(p_x=1/(2K-1)\), and \(A_0=K^2/(2K-1)^2<1\). When \(d\ge6\), \(s^2=9\le3+d=n\), as required.

Let \(v=(1,1,3)\), with the same meaning as in a91f98e9427d1a45. For every suspension, the supported nonzero point \((e_3,0)\) has the strict convex representation
$$
(e_3,0)=\frac13(v,0)+\frac13(-e_1,0)+\frac13(-e_2,0).
\tag{30}
$$
All three points on the right are distinct supported points different from \((e_3,0)\). Every nonzero point of an antipodal Boolean-subset support is exposed, as proved in a91f98e9427d1a45. To recall its elementary reason, for nonzero \(c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) with \(I=\lbrace i:c_i=1\rbrace\), the functional
$$
y\longmapsto m\sum_{i\in I}y_i-\sum_{i\notin I}y_i
$$
uniquely maximizes at \(c\) on the positive cube, with value \(m|I|\), and has maximum at most \(m-|I|<m|I|\) on the negative cube. Its negative exposes \(-c\). This applies to every subset of the two cubes containing the specified point.

Suppose an injective affine map \(T\) took the suspended \(A\) onto \(B'\cup(-B')\). Both finite supports are centrally symmetric about zero, so summing \(T(x)\) over all \(x\in A\) gives \(|A|T(0)=0\), and therefore \(T(0)=0\). Injectivity makes \(T(e_3,0)\) nonzero. Applying \(T\) to (30) expresses it as a strict convex combination of three distinct other supported image points. No functional can uniquely maximize there, contradicting the exposed-point property. This establishes the last assertion and completes the theorem.
