## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge8\), use the complete fixed canonical lift of 604889294a17d7df in its log-affine specialization. Explicitly, let
$$
b_\ell=0\ (1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\ (8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}b_h,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\quad(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell),\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,\quad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\quad N=n+1.
$$
Choose any symmetric saturated-chain partition \(\mathcal C\) of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\). Its nonterminal set and injective successor are \(D_m\) and \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\). The spatial coordinates are indexed by
\(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\). For a Boolean source \(b\), follow its successive forward child choices while its visited blocks belong to \(D_m\), obtaining a path \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and put
$$
z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r},\qquad
A_{m,Q}=\lbrace z_s(b):0\le s\le h(b)\rbrace,
$$
where the union over all Boolean sources is understood. Retain exactly the original support
$$
\widehat A_k=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
Here \(\operatorname{lev}(z)\) is the number of coordinates equal to two. These are the tree and complete lift of 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df, with every original height atom present.

Let \(\theta\) be arbitrary on the whole root child block \([m]\), and zero elsewhere. Let \(\lambda\) vanish on that block and obey exactly the normalization of dbbd63823b5f4870:
$$
\Pi(\lambda)=\max_{\substack{u_0=\varnothing,\ u_r\in[m]^r\\u_r\text{ child of }u_{r-1}\ (1\le r\le Q)}}
\sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{u_ri}|\le1.
$$
For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), set \(\gamma=\theta+\lambda\) and use the full original law
$$
w_J=e^{\gamma\cdot z_J+\beta t_J},\qquad
Z=\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}w_J,\qquad p_J=w_J/Z.
$$

Use precisely the mixed-row convention of 8ab782832d1c7da2. At each center \(J\), retain its separately fixed singleton and pairwise disjoint pairs or triples of distinct other supported points with arithmetic mean \(J\). For a source in a cycle \(\mathcal O\), define
$$
g_J(I)=\left(\prod_{I'\in\mathcal O}p_{I'}\right)^{1/|\mathcal O|},\quad
H_J=\sum_{I\in R_J}g_J(I),\quad
\kappa_J(I)=g_J(I)/H_J,\quad
Y_J(I)=\log\frac{p_I}{g_J(I)},
$$
$$
V_J=\sum_{I\in R_J}\kappa_J(I)Y_J(I)^2,\quad
b_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jp_JH_J^2,\quad
q_J=p_JH_J^2/b_{\mathrm m},\quad
\nu_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jq_JV_J.
$$
These are its superscript \((\mathrm m)\) objects, with that superscript suppressed. Set
\(A_0=4^N\sum_Jp_J^3\) and \(B=4^Nb_{\mathrm m}\).

There is a finite absolute constant \(C\ge4\), independent of \(k,\mathcal C,\theta,\lambda,\beta\), such that every active law \(A_0<1\) admits such rows with
$$
B\ge1,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm m}\le C\log B.
\tag{1}
$$
In fact the construction below satisfies (1) for every law in this class, whether active or not. For \(k\ge64\) its rows satisfy the stronger uniform bounds
$$
B\ge\frac{400}{361},\qquad \nu_{\mathrm m}\le4.
\tag{2}
$$

Consequently let \(L\ge1\), choose arbitrary heterogeneous factors of this class, and let \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}p_i\), with \(\mathcal N=\sum_iN_i\). Define the actual original ambient-halfspace depth and moments by
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf\lbrace P(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{\mathcal N}\text{ a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace,
\qquad M_r(P)=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^r.
$$
Then the same \(C\) gives
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac{e^{-3C}}9\,4^{-\mathcal N},\qquad
M_r(P)\ge\left(\frac{e^{-3C/2}}3\right)^r2^{-r\mathcal N}\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{3}
$$
Every denominator in these assertions is the full original normalizer of its factor.

## Proof

Put
$$
\delta=2^{-m}\binom m{m/2},\qquad \eta=1-\delta.
$$
Accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 gives \(0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2}\), \(|D_m|=2^m\eta\), and the injective source-to-level parametrization. A useful additional count follows directly from its chains. For each label \(i\), there are at most \(2^m\delta\) edges of the successor which add coordinate \(i\), because a saturated chain adds that coordinate at most once. The same bound holds for inverse edges removing it. Also at most two vertices per chain lack two successive predecessors. All these assertions are uniform over the allowed \(\mathcal C\).

We first prove (2). Throughout this part \(k\ge64\), and write \(x=e^\beta\). For \(0\le t\le k\), let
$$
q_t=Q_t,\qquad d_t=\min(t,k-t),\qquad r_t=1+2d_t,
$$
where \(q_t=0\) for \(t<8\). Define the scalar sums
$$
H_0(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^t,\qquad
U_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}q_tx^t,\qquad
T_{\mathrm v}(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}r_t^2x^{3t}.
\tag{4}
$$
Put \(c_0=1\), and \(c_r=1+3\cdot2^{r-1}\) for integers \(r\ge1\), and set
$$
T_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^{3t}\sum_{s=1}^{q_t}c_{q_t-s}^2.
\tag{5}
$$
An empty inner sum is zero. In particular \(T_*>0\).

At every Boolean spatial center \((j,t)\), use all vertical reflection pairs in its column and the target singleton. This row has \(r_t\) sources. At a positive-level center \((j,t)\) of level \(s\), put \(r=q_t-s\). If \(r=0\), use its singleton. If \(r\ge1\), test the same two-predecessor and two inverse-continuation conditions as in the local construction (4)-(5) of abbc24783c13e0f6, below the existing level-\(s\) prefix. On success use all its \(2^{r-1}\) disjoint triples, lifted to this same actual height \(t\), and its singleton; on failure use just its singleton.

The cited local construction verifies membership, distinctness, avoidance of the target, and disjointness of all sources. Its verification uses only symmetric saturated-chain properties, so applies to every \(\mathcal C\) here. Its sources have spatial levels \(s+r,s+r,s\). Because \(s+r=q_t\), all are present at height \(t\). The positive-level rows preserve the whole existing prefix, in particular the entire root block. A successful row has \(c_r\) sources. Rows at different centers need not be disjoint, as required by the convention of 8ab782832d1c7da2.

We record a uniform adaptive estimate for the remainder field. Let \(\mu_{a\lambda}\) be the Boolean product law on all nonroot coordinates with parameter \(a\lambda\), for \(a\ge0\). Start at any specified root child. A fresh queried block at a node \(v\) has block budget
\(A_v=\sum_i|\lambda_{vi}|\le1\). Its product-law density relative to the fair block law is between \(e^{-aA_v}\) and \(e^{aA_v}\). Consequently a forward or inverse successor selects a specified label with probability at most \(e^a\delta\); a one-predecessor or one-successor test fails with probability at most \(e^a\delta\); and a two-predecessor test fails with probability at most \(2e^a\delta\).

These conditional assertions remain valid at each adaptive query: its location is determined by already exposed blocks, and its coordinates are fresh independent bits. In particular, for one forward path, stopped either at failure or at the desired number of steps, let \(S\) be the sum of \(|\lambda_u|\) at the successfully selected coordinates. Conditional on the history before querying \(v\), its expected next contribution is at most \(e^a\delta A_v\). The sum of \(A_v\) along every realized queried path is at most one by the whole-child-block definition of \(\Pi\). Summing the conditional expectations therefore proves
$$
0\le S\le1,\qquad \mathbb E_{\mu_{a\lambda}}S\le e^a\delta.
\tag{6}
$$
This is an expectation over the original product bits, before imposing future success conditions. It does not condition the path budget on success or replace the original law by a label normalizer.

For a fixed positive-level target level \(s\) and fixed root successor label, its source requires \(s-1\) forward steps below that root label. The spatial spike multiplier is \(e^{2\sum\lambda_u}\). Since \(|e^{2v}-1|\le2e^2|v|\) for \(|v|\le1\), (6) with \(a=1\) gives
$$
\mathbb E_{\mu_\lambda}\left[e^{2\sum\lambda_u}
\mathbf1_{\lbrace\text{forward prefix succeeds}\rbrace}\right]
\le1+2e^3\delta.
\tag{7}
$$

For the cubic weighted census use \(\mu_{3\lambda}\). If \(r=q_t-s\ge1\), requiring the forward prefix and the triple test uses \(s-1\) forward tests, one two-predecessor test, and \(2(r-1)\) one-predecessor tests, all in fresh blocks. Their total failure probability is at most
\((s+2r-1)e^3\delta\le2Qe^3\delta\).
For \(r=0\), requiring just the forward prefix has the same upper bound. The cubic spike multiplier is \(e^{6\sum\lambda_u}\). Apply (6) with \(a=3\) and \(|e^{6v}-1|\le6e^6|v|\) to obtain, for the corresponding success event \(\mathcal G\),
$$
\mathbb E_{\mu_{3\lambda}}
\left[e^{6\sum\lambda_u}\mathbf1_{\mathcal G}\right]
\ge1-2Qe^3\delta-6e^9\delta.
\tag{8}
$$
The forward sum in (8) is defined along its stopped forward path on all outcomes, so the error estimate uses no conditioning on \(\mathcal G\).

Here all errors are smaller than \(\varepsilon=1/100\). Indeed \(Q\ge b_{64}=2^{55}\). Using \(e<3\) and \(\delta<Q^{-2}\), the error in (8) is bounded by
\(54/Q+118098/Q^2<1/100\), and that in (7) by \(54/Q^2<1/100\). Thus the bounds (7)-(8) hold with \(1+\varepsilon\) and \(1-\varepsilon\), uniformly over every root label, level, height, partition, and field under consideration.

We now retain the root weights exactly. Define
$$
\mathfrak B(\theta)=2^{-m}\sum_{b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}e^{\theta\cdot b},\qquad
\mathfrak E(\theta)=2^{-m}\sum_{b\in D_m}
e^{\theta\cdot(b+2e_{i_m(b)})},
$$
$$
Z_{\mathrm{out}}(\lambda)=\prod_{u:\,|u|\ge2}(1+e^{\lambda_u}).
\tag{9}
$$
These are auxiliary finite sums, not normalizers defining a new original law. Every positive-level spatial point has a unique root source \(b\in D_m\), the root spike label \(i_m(b)\), and the original remaining Boolean source. At level \(s\), precisely the next \(s-1\) forward steps are required. Thus (7), summed over all original levels and heights, gives the full-normalizer bound
$$
Z\le2^mZ_{\mathrm{out}}(\lambda)
\left[\mathfrak B(\theta)H_0(x)
+(1+\varepsilon)\mathfrak E(\theta)U_*(x)\right].
\tag{10}
$$
The Boolean contribution in (10) is exact. No part of a root class or height column is removed from its denominator.

Let \(m_J=|R_J|\) for the selected rows. All cycle means are \(J\). The affine identities (9) of 8ab782832d1c7da2 therefore give
$$
g_J(I)=p_J,\quad H_J=m_Jp_J,\quad
V_J=\frac1{m_J}\sum_{I\in R_J}
\bigl(\gamma\cdot(z_I-z_J)+\beta(t_I-t_J)\bigr)^2,
\quad b_{\mathrm m}=Z^{-3}\sum_Jm_J^2w_J^3.
\tag{11}
$$
For each fixed root source, level, and height, on the success event in (8) the row size is \(c_{q_t-s}\); for \(r=0\) it is one. The source-to-level parametrization is injective, so summing (8) gives
$$
\sum_Jm_J^2w_J^3
\ge2^mZ_{\mathrm{out}}(3\lambda)
\left[\mathfrak B(3\theta)T_{\mathrm v}(x)
+(1-\varepsilon)\mathfrak E(3\theta)T_*(x)\right].
\tag{12}
$$
For the Boolean rows this again is an equality. Equations (10)-(12) account for all omitted or failed positive-level classes by bounding their contribution to the full denominator in (10), while retaining a proved subset for the lower energy estimate (12).

For positive numbers, \((\sum_{j=1}^{h}y_j)^3\le h^2\sum_jy_j^3\). Apply this to the \(2^m\) Boolean root weights and the \(|D_m|\le2^m\) root spike weights. Also multiply \((1+y)^3\le4(1+y^3)\) over all nonroot coordinates. This gives
$$
\mathfrak B(3\theta)\ge\mathfrak B(\theta)^3,\quad
\mathfrak E(3\theta)\ge\mathfrak E(\theta)^3,\quad
4^{n-m}\frac{Z_{\mathrm{out}}(3\lambda)}{Z_{\mathrm{out}}(\lambda)^3}\ge1.
\tag{13}
$$
There is no bound on the coefficients of \(\theta\) in this step. By (10)-(13),
$$
B\ge4\,
\frac{\mathfrak B(\theta)^3T_{\mathrm v}(x)
+(1-\varepsilon)\mathfrak E(\theta)^3T_*(x)}
{\left[\mathfrak B(\theta)H_0(x)
+(1+\varepsilon)\mathfrak E(\theta)U_*(x)\right]^3}.
\tag{14}
$$
For \(a,b,u,v>0\), Hölder gives
\((a+b)^3\le(a^3u+b^3v)(u^{-1/2}+v^{-1/2})^2\).
Use it in (14), after absorbing the height sums into \(a,b\). Define
$$
v_*(x)=\frac{H_0(x)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{T_{\mathrm v}(x)}},\qquad
e_*(x)=\frac{U_*(x)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{T_*(x)}}.
$$
Then
$$
B\ge\frac4{\left[v_*(x)+
\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}e_*(x)\right]^2}
\ge\frac4{\left[v_*(x)+\frac{21}{20}e_*(x)\right]^2}.
\tag{15}
$$
The last inequality follows by squaring: \((101/100)^3/(99/100)<(21/20)^2\).

It remains to check two scalar ranges. If \(0<x\le1/16\), then \(H_0\le16/15\) and \(T_{\mathrm v}\ge1\), so \(v_*<10/9\). Since \(b_\ell\le2^\ell/64\) for \(\ell\ge8\),
$$
U_*(x)=\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell\sum_{t=\ell}^{k}x^t
\le\frac{4x^8}{(1-x)(1-2x)}<5x^8.
$$
At height eight \(q_8=4\), and
\(c_3^2+c_2^2+c_1^2+c_0^2=13^2+7^2+4^2+1=235\).
Consequently \(T_*\ge235x^{24}\) and
$$
e_*\le\sqrt{125/235}=5/\sqrt{47}<3/4,
\qquad
v_*+\frac{21}{20}e_*<\frac{10}{9}+\frac{63}{80}<\frac{19}{10}.
\tag{16}
$$

If \(x\ge1/16\), the one-dimensional interval estimate (4)-(6) of 6d7276756d672c31 applies exactly to the geometric law on heights \(0,\ldots,k\): its \(k_L\) is \(T_{\mathrm v}/H_0^3\), with \(L=k\). Thus
$$
v_*\le\sqrt{27/8}<15/8.
\tag{17}
$$
Moreover \(U_*\le Q(k+1)\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace\), and the level-one target term at top height gives
\(T_*\ge c_{Q-1}^2x^{3k}\), where \(c_{Q-1}\ge3\cdot2^{Q-2}\).
The parameter estimates proved in f1fcf9518bf577b3 give \(Q\ge12k\) for \(k\ge32\). Hence
$$
e_*\le\frac43[Q(k+1)]^{3/2}2^{6k-Q}
\le\frac43(Q+1)^3\,2^{-Q/2}.
\tag{18}
$$
For integers \(q\ge64\), \((q+1)^6\le2^q\), as proved in f1fcf9518bf577b3. Taking \(q=\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\) yields
\((Q+1)^3\le8\cdot2^{Q/4}\). Substitution in (18), using \(Q\ge2^{55}\), gives
$$
e_*\le\frac{32}{3}\,2^{-Q/4}<\frac1{100}.
$$
Together with (17), this implies
\(v_*+(21/20)e_*<15/8+21/2000<19/10\).
Equations (15)-(18) prove \(B\ge400/361\) for every \(x>0\).

We next check the variance, using the same rows. At positive-level rows all heights and all root coordinates are unchanged. The triple table in abbc24783c13e0f6 changes only coordinates on two inverse descendant paths, by at most one in absolute value at each coordinate. Therefore every source score has absolute value at most \(2\Pi(\lambda)\le2\), and \(V_J\le4\). Singleton rows have zero variance.

In a Boolean row the source score is \(\beta u\), with \(u\) uniform on \(\lbrace-d_t,\ldots,d_t\rbrace\). Its variance is \(\beta^2d_t(d_t+1)/3\). Conditional on the center being Boolean under the energy law \(q_J\), the height distribution is exactly proportional to \(r_t^2x^{3t}\); all root and other spatial factors cancel. The scalar variance estimate (15)-(22) of 6d7276756d672c31, with \(L=k\), bounds this conditional average by \(2/3\), for either sign of \(\beta\). The overall \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\) is a mixture of this average and the positive-level averages bounded by four. Thus \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\le4\), completing (2).

We now treat \(8\le k<64\), including all noncompact spatial and height endpoints. Fix one such \(k\) and one allowed \(\mathcal C\). Let \(D=\widehat A_k\), a finite full-dimensional complete lattice support by 604889294a17d7df. At every center take its full reflected row
$$
R_J^{\mathrm f}=\lbrace I\in D:2J-I\in D\rbrace,\qquad \mathsf r_J=|R_J^{\mathrm f}|.
$$
This is an admissible singleton and disjoint-pair row under 8ab782832d1c7da2. We consider the entire family of positive affine laws \(p_J=e^{\ell\cdot J}/\sum_Ie^{\ell\cdot I}\), \(\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), which includes every law in the statement for this fixed support. For these rows,
$$
B_{\mathrm f}(p)=4^N\sum_J\mathsf r_J^2p_J^3,
\quad
\nu_{\mathrm f}(p)=
\frac{\sum_J\mathsf r_Jp_J^3
\sum_{I\in R_J^{\mathrm f}}\left(\log(p_I/p_J)\right)^2}
{\sum_J\mathsf r_J^2p_J^3}.
\tag{19}
$$

For every interior affine law, \(B_{\mathrm f}>1\). Indeed affineness gives \(p_Ip_{2J-I}=p_J^2\). The bijection from ordered same-parity endpoints to their midpoint and reflected displacement, using completeness of \(D\), gives
$$
\chi(p):=\sum_J\mathsf r_Jp_J^2
=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^N}p(c+2\mathbb Z^N)^2\ge2^{-N}.
\tag{20}
$$
At any Boolean spatial point \(j\), the full rows at heights zero, one, and two have respectively one, three, and five sources, all vertical. To see this, the endpoint heights in such a pair sum to at most four, whereas every positive spatial level enters at height at least eight. Thus both endpoint bases are Boolean; their Boolean midpoint \(j\) forces them to equal \(j\). Conversely all those vertical sources are present. Consequently the corresponding values of \(H_J=\mathsf r_Jp_J\) are
\(e^{\gamma\cdot j}(1,3x,5x^2)/Z\), and cannot all agree. Strict Jensen under the positive original law, followed by (20), gives
\(b_{\mathrm f}>\chi(p)^2\ge4^{-N}\).

Let \(\overline{\mathcal P}\) be the closure in the finite probability simplex of this entire affine family. Every full-support point of this closure is again an affine law: its logarithmic ratios on an integer affine basis of \(D\) determine convergent finite parameters. We prove the required behavior at every boundary law \(p^*\in\overline{\mathcal P}\) with proper support \(S\subsetneq D\). Take a convergent sequence of affine laws with parameters \(\ell_j\), and choose \(J_0\in S\). Set
\(V=\operatorname{span}\lbrace J-J_0:J\in S\rbrace\).
For a basis chosen among these integer differences,
\(\ell_j\cdot(J-J_0)=\log(p^{(j)}_J/p^{(j)}_{J_0})\) converges. Hence the orthogonal projections of \(\ell_j\) onto \(V\) are bounded. For each \(I\notin S\) the same logarithmic ratio tends to minus infinity. It follows that the projections onto \(V^\perp\) have strictly negative scalar product with every \(I-J_0\), for all sufficiently large \(j\), and zero scalar product with every \(J-J_0\), \(J\in S\). In particular \(V\ne\mathbb R^N\), and one such projection exposes precisely \(S\) as a proper face of \(\operatorname{conv}D\).

The space \(V^\perp\) is defined by integer linear equations. Its rational points are dense, so its exposing vector can be replaced by a rational vector preserving the finitely many strict inequalities. Multiplying by a common denominator and dividing by the gcd gives a nonzero primitive integral normal \(a\), with
\(a\cdot J=c\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(J\in S\). At least one coordinate of \(a\) is odd. Therefore \(S\) occupies at most \(2^{N-1}\) ambient parity classes, since all its points obey \(a\cdot J\equiv c\pmod2\). This argument uses full-dimensionality of the canonical \(D\); it does not assert a parity reduction for the original support before taking a proper boundary limit.

The affine pair identities and the polynomial identity (20) persist in the limit. Jensen under \(p^*\), now allowing zero entries, therefore gives
$$
B_{\mathrm f}(p^*)\ge
4^N\chi(p^*)^2\ge
4^N(2^{-(N-1)})^2=4.
\tag{21}
$$

Finally \(\nu_{\mathrm f}\) in (19) extends continuously to this whole compact closure. For a center with positive limiting probability, each endpoint of an original reflected pair also has positive limiting probability, by \(p_Ip_{2J-I}=p_J^2\), so its logarithms are continuous. At any positive affine law the same pair identity and the upper bound on probabilities imply
$$
p_J^2\le p_I\le1,\qquad
|\log(p_I/p_J)|\le-\log p_J.
$$
The absolute value of the numerator contribution of a center tending to zero is thus bounded by
\(\mathsf r_J^2p_J^3(\log p_J)^2\), which tends to zero. There are only finitely many centers. The denominator in (19) remains positive on the simplex, since it is at least \(\sum_Jp_J^3\ge|D|^{-2}\). These observations prove continuity and boundedness of the extended variance, without bounding the affine parameters.

By the interior strict inequality and (21), the continuous \(\log B_{\mathrm f}\) is positive everywhere on \(\overline{\mathcal P}\). It has a positive minimum; hence \(\nu_{\mathrm f}/\log B_{\mathrm f}\) has a finite maximum. For each fixed \(k\) there are finitely many allowed chain partitions of its fixed finite Boolean cube. There are only the 56 integers \(8\le k<64\). Taking the maximum of their finitely many maxima and \(4/\log(400/361)\) and 4 defines one finite absolute constant \(C\). This proves (1), in fact for all the laws in the statement. No bounded-field approximation or omitted endpoint is used.

For the heterogeneous product choose these rows in each active factor and singleton rows in each inactive factor. Inactive factors have \(B_i=A_{0,i}\ge1\) and \(\nu_i=0\). Set \(G=\sum_i\log B_i\ge0\). Then \(\sum_i\nu_i\le CG\), and the mixed product comparison (5)-(7) of 8ab782832d1c7da2 gives
$$
4^{\mathcal N}M_2(P)\ge\frac19
\exp\left(G-2\sqrt{3\sum_i\nu_i}\right)
\ge\frac19e^{G-2\sqrt{3CG}}\ge\frac19e^{-3C}.
$$
The last inequality completes the square in \(\sqrt G\). Jensen under the original product law gives \(M_r(P)\ge M_2(P)^{r/2}\) for \(r\ge2\). This is (3), with every original factor normalizer retained.
