## Statement

Fix $d\ge2$. Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)\subset\mathbb R^d$ have volume $V$ and centroid $g$, and put $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/(d+1)$ let $E_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(0,g+\varepsilon e_1,\ldots,g+\varepsilon e_d)$ and $a=|E_\varepsilon|$. For the ordinary law of density $2\,1_T+1_{E_\varepsilon}$, its maximal raw halfspace depth and maximal normalized halfspace depth are respectively
$$
2\Gamma_d V+\frac{a}{d+1}+o(a),\qquad
\Gamma_d+\frac{a}{2V}\left(\frac1{d+1}-\Gamma_d\right)+o(a)
\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
$$
The normalized coefficient is strictly negative. This law is the projection onto $\mathbb R^d$ of the mixed measure on the actual fibers $E_\varepsilon,T,T$ over $-1,0,1$. The mixed raw maximum has right derivative one with respect to $a$, whereas the projected raw maximum has right derivative $1/(d+1)$.

## Proof

Put $m=d+1$, $h=d/m$, and let $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$ be the barycentric coordinates of $T$ with vertex zero indexed by zero. Each nonzero vertex of $E_\varepsilon$ has nonnegative coordinates summing to $h+\varepsilon<1$, so $E_\varepsilon\subseteq T$. Its volume ratio is $a/V=\varepsilon^{d-1}(\varepsilon+h)$: the matrix of its nonzero vertices is $\varepsilon I+g\mathbf1^\top$, with eigenvalues $\varepsilon$ on the sum-zero subspace and $\varepsilon+h$ on the span of $\mathbf1$. In particular $a$ is strictly increasing in $\varepsilon$ and tends to zero. The exact three-fiber census and its mixed depth theorem apply with $A=E_\varepsilon$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ because $\rho_d>0$. The projection therefore has the stated density, and the mixed raw maximum is $\Gamma_dV+a$.

Write $\nu_\varepsilon$ for the uniform probability on $E_\varepsilon$. On one probability space choose a uniform barycentric tuple $(L_0,\ldots,L_d)$ and put $X_\varepsilon=R g+\varepsilon(L_1,\ldots,L_d)$, with $R=\sum_{i=1}^d L_i$. Then $X_\varepsilon$ has law $\nu_\varepsilon$ and tends almost surely to $Rg$ with $R<1$ almost surely. For $y_\varepsilon\to g$ and unit $u_\varepsilon$ tending to the canonical normal $\nabla\lambda_i/\lVert\nabla\lambda_i\rVert$, the indicators of $\lbrace u_\varepsilon\cdot(X_\varepsilon-y_\varepsilon)\ge0\rbrace$ converge almost surely: at the limit, $\lambda_0(Rg)>1/m$ and each $\lambda_i(Rg)<1/m$ for $i\ge1$. Thus the added cap probabilities converge to $c_0=1$ and $c_i=0$ for $i\ge1$, respectively. Write $\bar c=1/m$ for their average.

The unperturbed ordinary maximum for $2\,1_T$ is $2\Gamma_d V$ at $g$, by the centroid cap bound and the homothetic canonical caps from the reflected profile lemma. It is unique: at any other point of $T$ some $\lambda_i>1/m$, and the cap at that level has strictly smaller mass; off the interior there is a supporting or separating cap of zero mass. The perturbed law also has a maximizing point: its cap mass is jointly continuous in the point and unit normal by hyperplane nullity, so its minimum over the compact sphere is continuous, and the maximum can be taken on compact $T$. At any maximizer $y_\varepsilon$, the maximal raw value $D_\varepsilon$ obeys
$$
2\Gamma_dV\le D_\varepsilon\le 2V(1-\lambda_i(y_\varepsilon))^d+a
\qquad(0\le i\le d).
$$
The lower bound uses $g$ and nonnegativity of the addition. The upper bound uses the $i$th canonical cap at $y_\varepsilon$. Taking $d$th roots for small $a$ gives $\lambda_i(y_\varepsilon)\le1/m+O(a)$ uniformly in maximizers. Since the coordinates sum to one, each coordinate also has lower bound $1/m-O(a)$, so $y_\varepsilon=g+O(a)$.

Set $\kappa=dVh^{d-1}$. At these maximizers the $i$th canonical cap mass equals
$$
2\Gamma_dV-2\kappa(\lambda_i(y_\varepsilon)-1/m)+a c_i+o(a)
$$
by the exact homothetic fraction and the moving-cap probability convergence above. The maximum is at most the average of these masses; the coordinate deviations cancel, yielding $D_\varepsilon\le 2\Gamma_dV+a\bar c+o(a)$.

For the lower bound choose the point $y_\varepsilon^*$ with barycentric coordinates $1/m+a(c_i-\bar c)/(2\kappa)$. They sum to one and are positive for small $a$. If the lower bound $D_\varepsilon\ge 2\Gamma_dV+a\bar c-o(a)$ failed, along some sequence there would be through-$y_\varepsilon^*$ caps with masses at least a fixed positive multiple of $a$ below $2\Gamma_dV+a\bar c$. Pass to a convergent subsequence of their unit normals. The limit must be a canonical normal: continuity of the $T$ cap, nonnegativity of the addition, and $y_\varepsilon^*\to g$ force the limit's $T$ cap mass to be at most $\Gamma_dV$. The reflected profile lemma applies to this $T$ and gives exactly the canonical equality directions.

In a neighborhood of the $i$th canonical normal and $g$, the cut has one strictly positive vertex margin $u\cdot(v_i-y)$ and strictly negative margins at all $v_j$ with $j\ne i$, where $v_j$ are the vertices of $T$. These strict signs persist throughout a neighborhood. The $T$ cap mass is the smooth function
$$
F(y,u)=V\,\frac{[u\cdot(v_i-y)]^d}{\prod_{j\ne i} u\cdot(v_i-v_j)}
$$
by multiplying the edge intercept fractions. At the canonical normal its derivative in $y$ at $g$ is $-\kappa$ times the linear part of $\lambda_i$, by the canonical homothetic formula. Continuity of this derivative gives, along the convergent subsequence,
$$
F(y_\varepsilon^*,u_\varepsilon)
=F(g,u_\varepsilon)-\kappa(\lambda_i(y_\varepsilon^*)-1/m)+o(a).
$$
This expansion uses $y_\varepsilon^*-g=O(a)$ and requires no rate for convergence of $u_\varepsilon$. The centroid term is at least $\Gamma_dV$ at every $u_\varepsilon$, and the added cap pays $a c_i+o(a)$ by the same almost-sure convergence. The full cap therefore has mass at least $2\Gamma_dV-a(c_i-\bar c)+a c_i+o(a)=2\Gamma_dV+a\bar c+o(a)$, contradicting the alleged loss. This proves the matching lower bound.

Dividing by the exact mass $2V+a$ gives the normalized expansion. For $d\ge2$ the $m$ canonical caps cover $T$ and two of them overlap in a nonempty relatively open set of positive volume, where both corresponding barycentric coordinates exceed $1/m$. Thus $m\Gamma_d>1$, proving the strict negative sign. The two first variations concern different halfspace classes.

The exact mixed maximum invoked above ranges over the three actual integer fibers.
