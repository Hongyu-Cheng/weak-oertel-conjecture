## Statement

Let \(s>0\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) satisfy \(|\theta|<s\). Put \(a_+=s-\theta\), \(a_-=s+\theta\), and define the original scalar probability law by
\[
C(s,\theta)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{\theta j-s|j|}
=1+\frac1{e^{a_+}-1}+\frac1{e^{a_-}-1},\qquad
p_{s,\theta}(j)=\frac{e^{\theta j-s|j|}}{C(s,\theta)}.
\tag{1}
\]
At \(\theta=0\), this is exactly \(q_s\) in accepted fact `f0e6fc20211dbdc2`, whose scalar normalizer is \(c_s=\coth(s/2)\). The new kernel below differs from that fact's displacement kernel: its excess rate is \(2s\), replacing \(4s/3\). In particular its tail constant is \(\coth s\), replacing \(\coth(2s/3)\).

Define the new auxiliary quantities
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_s^{(2)}(j)&=2|j|+\coth s,\\
F^{(2)}(s,\theta)&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 e^{4\theta j-4s|j|}H_s^{(2)}(j)^3,\\
Q_{s,\theta}^{(2)}(j)&=
 \frac{e^{4\theta j-4s|j|}H_s^{(2)}(j)^3}{F^{(2)}(s,\theta)},\\
e_j(v)&=(|v|-|j|)_+,\qquad
b_j(v)=\frac{|j+v|-|j-v|}{2},\\
K_s(v\mid j)&=\frac{e^{-2s e_j(v)}}{H_s^{(2)}(j)}
 \quad(v\in\mathbb Z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Both \(Q_{s,\theta}^{(2)}\) and \(K_s(\cdot\mid j)\) are probability laws. They are auxiliary laws, not replacements or conditional renormalizations of (1). Set \(\alpha=(29/108)^{3/2}>1/8\). Then
\[
\frac{F^{(2)}(s,\theta)}{C(s,\theta)^4}
=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}p_{s,\theta}(j)^4H_s^{(2)}(j)^3
\ge\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]

For \(J\sim Q_{s,\theta}^{(2)}\), and conditionally \(V\sim K_s(\cdot\mid J)\), define \(D(J)=s|J|-\theta J\) and \(m_s(j)=\mathbb E[e_j(V)\mid J=j]\). The following bounds hold with the same original normalization (1):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E D(J)^2&\le16,&
\mathbb E J^2&\le\frac{16}{(s-|\theta|)^2},\\
0\le m_s(j)&\le\frac1{2s},&
\mathbb E[e_j(V)^2\mid J=j]&\le\frac1{2s^2},\\
\mathbb E[b_J(V)^2]&\le\frac{16}{(s-|\theta|)^2},&
\mathbb E V^2&\le\frac{33}{(s-|\theta|)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
For every \(\beta\) with \(|\theta|\le\beta\le s\), the full score displacement \(\ell_{\beta,j}(v)=\theta v-\beta b_j(v)\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_{\beta,J}(V)\mid J]=0,\qquad
\mathbb E\ell_{\beta,J}(V)^2\le33.
\tag{5}
\]
The unscaled vertical second moment in (4) is allowed to deteriorate at the one-sided limit; the full score bound in (5) does not deteriorate.

More explicitly, take any \(n\ge1\), a common \(s>0\), and \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\) with \(|\theta_i|<s\). Under independent copies of the corresponding root and conditional displacement laws, the fourth-power scalar factors multiply to at least \(\alpha^n\). If \(\max_i|\theta_i|\le\beta\le s\), then
\[
\mathbb E\left[\sum_{i=1}^n
 (\theta_iV_i-\beta b_{J_i}(V_i))\right]^2\le33n,
\quad
\mathbb E\left[\left.\left(\sum_{i=1}^n
 (e_{J_i}(V_i)-m_s(J_i))\right)^2\right|\mathbf J\right]
\le\frac{n}{2s^2}.
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

All root series converge because \(a_+,a_->0\), and all displacement series converge because \(s>0\). Summing the positive and negative geometric tails proves (1).

First, the geometry and the full score are exact. Write \(k=|j|\). One has \(b_j(v)=\operatorname{sgn}(j)\operatorname{sgn}(v)\min\{k,|v|\}\), with \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0\), and
\[
|j+v|=|j|+e_j(v)+b_j(v),\qquad
|j-v|=|j|+e_j(v)-b_j(v).
\tag{7}
\]
For \(j,v\ge0\), split into \(v\le j\) and \(v\ge j\): the sum of the two endpoint magnitudes is \(2\max\{j,v\}\), and their difference is \(2\min\{j,v\}\). Negating either argument leaves the sum unchanged and changes the sign of the difference. This proves the formula for \(b_j(v)\) and both identities (7), including zero arguments. In particular \(b_j(v)\) is an integer, is odd in \(v\), and has magnitude at most \(k\).

Put \(q=e^{-2s}\) and \(c=\coth s=(1+q)/(1-q)\). There are \(2k+1\) displacement values of excess zero, and two values of each positive integer excess. Their weight sum is \(2k+1+2q/(1-q)=2k+c=H_s^{(2)}(j)\), proving the normalization of \(K_s\). Differentiating \(\sum_{r\ge1}q^r=q/(1-q)\) once and twice gives
\[
m_s(j)=\frac{2q}{(1-q)^2H_s^{(2)}(j)},\qquad
\mathbb E[e_j(V)^2\mid j]
=\frac{2q(1+q)}{(1-q)^3H_s^{(2)}(j)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Since \(H_s^{(2)}(j)\ge(1+q)/(1-q)\), the first expression is at most \(1/\sinh(2s)\le1/(2s)\). The second is at most \(2q/(1-q)^2=1/(2\sinh^2s)\le1/(2s^2)\). This proves the conditional bounds in (4).

For \(|\theta|\le\beta\le s\), the exact formula for \(b_j(v)\) gives
\[
|\theta v-\beta b_j(v)|\le D(j)+s e_j(v).
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, when \(|v|\le k\), its left side is \((\beta-\theta\operatorname{sgn}(j))|v|\le(s-\theta\operatorname{sgn}(j))k=D(j)\). When \(|v|>k\), write \(v=\operatorname{sgn}(v)(k+e_j(v))\); then the score is \(\operatorname{sgn}(v)[(\theta-\beta\operatorname{sgn}(j))k+\theta e_j(v)]\), whose magnitude is at most the right side of (9). The zero-root case follows directly. The kernel is even in \(v\) and the score is odd, so its conditional mean is zero. Its conditional second moment, using (8) and \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), is at most \(2D(j)^2+1\).

Next we prove the uniform fourth-power factor by an exact rational certificate. Under the original scalar law (1), put
\[
\mathcal L_2(s,\theta)=
\mathbb E_{p_{s,\theta}}[(p_{s,\theta}(J)H_s^{(2)}(J))^2].
\tag{10}
\]
We prove \(\mathcal L_2(s,\theta)\ge29/108\). Write \(a=e^{-a_+}\), \(b=e^{-a_-}\), \(X=a/(1-a)\), \(Y=b/(1-b)\). The exact normalizations become \(C=1+X+Y\) and \(c=1+2XY/C\), because \(ab=e^{-2s}\). For \(0<z<1\), expansion of \((2k+c)^2\) and two differentiations of the geometric series prove
\[
G_{2,c}(z):=\sum_{k\ge0}(2k+c)^2z^k
=\frac{c^2}{1-z}+\frac{4cz}{(1-z)^2}
+\frac{4z(1+z)}{(1-z)^3}.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently \(\mathcal L_2=[G_{2,c}(a^3)+G_{2,c}(b^3)-c^2]/C^3\). The subtraction counts the zero root once.

Here is a finite polynomial certificate for its lower bound. Define \(U(z)=3z^2+3z+1\), \(\eta=X+Y\), \(\xi=XY\), and \(\Delta=(X-Y)^2\). Substitution into (11), clearing the positive denominator, and expansion give the identity
\[
108C^4U(X)^3U(Y)^3
\left(\mathcal L_2-\frac{29}{108}\right)
=\sum_{r=0}^{16}\eta^{\,r\bmod2}T_r(\Delta,\xi),
\tag{12}
\]
where the polynomials are given in the following table.

For clarity, (12) is a polynomial identity rather than a numerical or sign assumption. It follows by using \(1-a^3=U(X)/(1+X)^3\) and \(1-b^3=U(Y)/(1+Y)^3\) in (11). After clearing the displayed denominator, group the resulting polynomial by total degree \(r\) in \(X,Y\). A symmetric homogeneous polynomial of odd degree has a factor \(\eta\). Express each remaining even-degree homogeneous polynomial in \(\eta^2\) and \(\xi\), and substitute \(\eta^2=\Delta+4\xi\); the resulting component is exactly the listed \(T_r\). This finite expansion proves (12) and gives a direct coefficient-by-coefficient way to check every row. Since \(\eta,\xi,\Delta\ge0\), every term in its right side is nonnegative. Its denominator is positive. Thus \(\mathcal L_2\ge29/108\).

Hölder under the original probability law (1) now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_2
&=\mathbb E_{p_{s,\theta}}
 [((p_{s,\theta}(J)H_s^{(2)}(J))^3)^{2/3}]\\
&\le\left(\mathbb E_{p_{s,\theta}}
 [(p_{s,\theta}(J)H_s^{(2)}(J))^3]\right)^{2/3}
=\left(\frac{F^{(2)}(s,\theta)}{C(s,\theta)^4}\right)^{2/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The Hölder exponents are \(3/2\) and 3. All these moments are finite by their geometric tails. Equations (12)-(13) prove (3). The strict surplus is exact: \(\alpha^2=29^3/108^3>1/64\) is equivalent to \(24389>19683\).

We finish the root and displacement moment bounds. For any \(r>0\) and \(c\ge1\), let the one-sided auxiliary law be
\[
R_{r,c}(k)=\frac{(2k+c)^3e^{-4rk}}{F_{r,c}},\qquad
F_{r,c}=\sum_{h\ge0}(2h+c)^3e^{-4rh},\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]
The exact alias \(R_{r,1}=Q_r\) is the cubic-weight one-sided law of accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`; its rate parameter there is \(s=r\). That fact proves \(\mathbb E_{R_{r,1}}k^2<8/r^2\). The relative weight \(w(k)=((2k+c)/(2k+1))^3\) is nonincreasing in \(k\): its inner ratio is \(1+(c-1)/(2k+1)\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Cov}_{R_{r,1}}(k^2,w(k))\le0\). Indeed an independent copy gives twice this covariance as the expectation of \((k^2-h^2)(w(k)-w(h))\), which is nonpositive pointwise. Normalizing the relative weight proves
\(\mathbb E_{R_{r,c}}k^2\le\mathbb E_{R_{r,1}}k^2\le8/r^2\).

Use (14) with \(r=a_+\) and \(r=a_-\), and the present \(c=\coth s\). Denote their normalizers by \(F_+\) and \(F_-\). Then \(F^{(2)}=F_++F_--c^3\), where the single zero-root term is \(c^3\), so \(F^{(2)}\ge c^3\) and \(F_++F_-\le2F^{(2)}\). The zero root contributes no second moment, and (14) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{s,\theta}^{(2)}}D(J)^2
\le\frac{8F_++8F_-}{F^{(2)}}\le16.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(D(j)\ge(s-|\theta|)|j|\), this also gives the root second moment in (4). The bound for \(b\) follows from \(|b_j(v)|\le|j|\). Finally \(|v|=\min\{|j|,|v|\}+e_j(v)\le|j|+e_j(v)\), so (8) and (15) give
\(\mathbb EV^2\le2\mathbb EJ^2+1/s^2\le33/(s-|\theta|)^2\). Combining (9) with (15) gives \(\mathbb E\ell_{\beta,J}(V)^2\le2\cdot16+1=33\), completing (4)-(5).

For independent coordinates, the source sum is exactly
\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \left(\prod_i p_{s,\theta_i}(j_i)\right)^4
 \prod_i H_s^{(2)}(j_i)^3
=\prod_i\frac{F^{(2)}(s,\theta_i)}{C(s,\theta_i)^4}
\ge\alpha^n.
\tag{16}
\]
The scores have zero conditional mean and are independent, so their second moments add; this proves the first part of (6). Conditional excesses are independent and centered after subtracting their respective \(m_s(J_i)\); their variances are at most the conditional second moments in (8), proving the other part of (6).

The source-counting role of the changed kernel can also be checked without any depth claim. For a fixed root \(\mathbf j\), write \(E(\mathbf v)=\sum_i e_{j_i}(v_i)\) and \(m(\mathbf j)=\sum_i m_s(j_i)\). If a displacement set \(\mathcal V\) has conditional kernel probability at least \(\gamma>0\) and \(E(\mathbf v)\ge m(\mathbf j)-R\) throughout it, then its exact cardinality satisfies
\[
\#\mathcal V
=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal V}
 \mathbb P(\mathbf V=\mathbf v\mid\mathbf j)
 \prod_iH_s^{(2)}(j_i)e^{2sE(\mathbf v)}
\ge\gamma\prod_iH_s^{(2)}(j_i)e^{2s(m(\mathbf j)-R)}.
\tag{17}
\]
For an original infinite-cone weight proportional to \(e^{\sum_i\theta_i j_i-st}\), a height \(t=\|\mathbf j\|_1+u\) contributes \(e^{-4su}\) to its fourth-power source after factoring the root weights in (16). Cubing the exponential factor in (17) produces \(e^{6s(m(\mathbf j)-R)}\). Their product is exactly \(e^{2sm(\mathbf j)-4s(u-m(\mathbf j))-6sR}\), and its excess-mean term \(2sm(\mathbf j)\) is nonnegative. The infinite-cone original normalizer is explicitly \(\prod_i C(s,\theta_i)/(1-e^{-s})\), by the slack sum \(t=\|\mathbf j\|_1+u\), \(u\ge0\); it has not been changed in this calculation. Thus using rate \(2s\), rather than \(4s/3\), does not introduce a loss proportional to \(n\) through excess cancellation. This identity is only the source factor comparison; feasibility and finite-cutoff choices remain separate requirements.
