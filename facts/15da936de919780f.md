## Statement

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), \(a\le b\), and let \(w=(w_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) satisfy \(w_j>0\) on the complete interval \(D=\{a,\ldots,b\}\), \(w_j=0\) off \(D\), and \(w_j^2\ge w_{j-1}w_{j+1}\) for every \(j\in\mathbb Z\). Use the canonical total weight \(W=\sum_{j\in D}w_j\), maximum weight \(w_{\max}=\max_{j\in D}w_j\), and potential \(\phi_j=\log(w_{\max}/w_j)\). All logarithms are natural. These are exactly the objects of accepted facts `488f0a583886b5fc` and `8660ffca617b9702`, with scalar \(j,J\) the \(n=1\) aliases of lattice indices \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\). Write \(p_j=w_j/W\), so \(p\) is the normalized table probability and \(p_{\max}=w_{\max}/W\).

If this is an actual body's one-dimensional integer-fiber profile, its sources are the fibers \(C_j=\{y:(j,y)\in C\}\) with their restricted \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measures, \(w_j=\mathcal L^d(C_j)\), and \(W=\mathcal H_d(S)\). The normalized source probability is \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap\cdot)/W\); its normalized halfspace depth is \(h_S(\cdot)/W\), with \(S\) and \(h_S\) as in the locked problem. No change to these source measures or depth normalization is made here.

Define \(R_J=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}\sqrt{w_{J+v}w_{J-v}}\) for \(J\in D\), and \(R=\max_{J\in D}R_J\). Thus \(R_J\) and \(R\) are exactly \(\mathcal R_J(w)\) and \(R_{1/2}(w)\) of `8660ffca617b9702`; the displacement sums are oriented and include zero. Assume

\[
\frac{w_{\max}}{W}<\frac12,
\qquad
0<\delta:=\frac{R}{W}-\frac12<\frac1{27000}.
\]

At any center \(J\in D\), let \(\mathcal U_J=\{v\in\mathbb Z:J+v,J-v\in D\}\), exactly the positive reflected-displacement set of `488f0a583886b5fc`. On this complete symmetric interval define the auxiliary displacement probability and energies by

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_J(v)&=\frac{\sqrt{w_{J+v}w_{J-v}}}{R_J},\\
G_J(v)&=\frac{\phi_{J+v}+\phi_{J-v}}2-\phi_J,\\
A_J(v)&=\frac{\phi_{J+v}-\phi_{J-v}}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The symbol \(A_J\) is potential imbalance, not the positive support \(D\) or a source measure. For \(v\ne0\), the reflected support \(\{J+v,J-v\}\), with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\), has precisely canonical logarithmic cost \(G_J(v)=G_E\) from `488f0a583886b5fc`; at \(v=0\) its singleton cost is zero. Its legal component capacity is \(\alpha(v)=\min\{w_{J+v},w_{J-v}\}\), with the suppressed center in the predecessor's \(\alpha\) equal to \(J\). The probability \(Q_J\) is auxiliary and does not replace the full-occurrence capacities of that predecessor.

Denote the assignment's requested absolute constant by \(C_\ast\), reserving \(C\) for a convex body. For every \(J\in D\) satisfying \(R_J=R\), one has, with \(C_\ast=1000\),

\[
\phi_J\le C_\ast\delta^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_J}\!\left[G_J+A_J^2\right]
\le C_\ast\delta\left[1+\log\frac1\delta\right]^2.
\]

Here expectations are the finite sums over \(\mathcal U_J\). In particular a maximizing center with both bounds exists.

## Proof

Normalization by \(W\) preserves log-concavity and all potentials, and gives \(R_J/W=\sum_v\sqrt{p_{J+v}p_{J-v}}\). Apply the small-surplus conclusion of accepted fact `8660ffca617b9702`. There are adjacent sites \(\ell,\ell+1\in D\) such that \(p_\ell,p_{\ell+1}>2/5\) and, writing \(\theta=\sum_{j\notin\{\ell,\ell+1\}}p_j\), one has \(\theta\le100\delta^2\). Since each central atom is at most \(p_{\max}<1/2\) and their sum is \(1-\theta\), each satisfies \(p_\ell,p_{\ell+1}\ge1/2-\theta\).

First every maximizing center belongs to \(\{\ell,\ell+1\}\). For any \(j\in D\), Cauchy-Schwarz on the positive displacements gives

\[
\frac{R_j}{W}
=p_j+2\sum_{v\ge1}\sqrt{p_{j-v}p_{j+v}}
\le p_j+2\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i<j}p_i\right)
\left(\sum_{i>j}p_i\right)}.
\]

If \(j<\ell\), both \(p_j\) and the left-tail sum are at most \(\theta\). If \(j>\ell+1\), both \(p_j\) and the right-tail sum are at most \(\theta\). In either case the other tail sum is at most one, whence \(R_j/W\le\theta+2\sqrt\theta\le100\delta^2+20\delta\). Since \(\delta<1/27000<1/100\), this is less than \(21/100<1/2\), whereas \(R/W=1/2+\delta>1/2\). This proves the claimed localization of every maximizer.

Fix any maximizing \(J\). Thus \(p_J>2/5\) and \(p_J\ge1/2-\theta\). Using \(p_{\max}<1/2\) and \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x\ge0\),

\[
0\le\phi_J
=\log\frac{p_{\max}}{p_J}
\le\frac{p_{\max}-p_J}{p_J}
\le\frac{5\theta}{2}
\le250\delta^2.
\]

In particular \(\phi_J<1\). Also
\(1-Q_J(0)=(1/2+\delta-p_J)/(1/2+\delta)\le2\delta+2\theta\le3\delta\), where the last inequality uses \(\delta<1/200\).

Completeness of the support gives \(\mathcal U_J=\{-m,\ldots,m\}\), where \(m=\min\{J-a,b-J\}\). Necessarily \(m\ge1\): if \(m=0\), then \(R_J/W=p_J\le p_{\max}<1/2\), contradicting maximality. This deals with possible table endpoints and excludes a singleton reflected interval under the present hypotheses. All subsequent arguments also apply when \(m=1\).

Taking logarithms of log-concavity shows that the successive differences of \(\phi\) on \(D\) are nondecreasing. Consequently \(G_J\) is even and discretely convex on \(\mathcal U_J\), with \(G_J(0)=0\). Indeed, for \(1\le k<m\), its second difference is

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_J(k+1)-2G_J(k)+G_J(k-1)
={}&\frac12\bigl(\phi_{J+k+1}-2\phi_{J+k}+\phi_{J+k-1}\bigr)\\
&+\frac12\bigl(\phi_{J-k-1}-2\phi_{J-k}+\phi_{J-k+1}\bigr)
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The midpoint inequality for the convex interpolation of \(\phi\) gives \(G_J(v)\ge0\). Summing the nondecreasing successive differences of \(G_J\) from zero yields \(G_J(k)\ge kG_J(1)\) for every \(1\le k\le m\).

Direct substitution of \(w_j=w_{\max}e^{-\phi_j}\) gives the exact identities

\[
\sqrt{w_{J+v}w_{J-v}}=w_Je^{-G_J(v)},
\qquad
Q_J(v)=Q_J(0)e^{-G_J(v)},
\qquad
\alpha(v)=w_Je^{-G_J(v)-|A_J(v)|}.
\]

Thus \(Q_J\) is a symmetric log-concave probability on its complete displacement interval, while the legal capacities retain the factor \(e^{-|A_J(v)|}\). The pair-cost assertion in the statement follows from the definition \(G_E=\sum_{i\in E}\lambda_{E,i}\phi_i-\phi_J\) in accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`; the singleton has \(G_E=0\). These identities do not assign geometric-mean mass to a legal component.

Put \(\eta=e^{-G_J(1)}>0\). The exact reservoir sum and the central mass bound give

\[
\begin{aligned}
2p_J\sum_{k=1}^m e^{-G_J(k)}
&=\frac12+\delta-p_J\le\delta+\theta,\\
\eta
&\le\frac{\delta+\theta}{2p_J}
\le\frac54(\delta+100\delta^2)
\le2\delta<\frac1{16}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the penultimate inequality it suffices that \(\delta<1/200\), because then \((5/4)(1+100\delta)<15/8<2\). Since \(\log2=\int_1^2 dx/x\ge1/2\), it follows that \(G_J(1)=\log(1/\eta)>\log16\ge2\). Therefore \(G_J(k)\ge kG_J(1)>2\) at every nonzero displacement.

We now bound the energy second moment without any upper bound on slopes or support length. The function \(x\mapsto x^2e^{-x}\) is nonincreasing for \(x\ge2\), as its derivative is \(e^{-x}x(2-x)\). Hence the lower energy bound \(G_J(k)\ge kG_J(1)\) implies

\[
e^{-G_J(k)}G_J(k)^2
\le e^{-kG_J(1)}\bigl(kG_J(1)\bigr)^2
=\eta^k k^2\left[\log\frac1\eta\right]^2.
\]

Using \(Q_J(0)\le1\), evenness, and extension of this nonnegative sum to all positive integers,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_J}[G_J^2]
&=2Q_J(0)\sum_{k=1}^m e^{-G_J(k)}G_J(k)^2\\
&\le2\left[\log\frac1\eta\right]^2\sum_{k=1}^\infty k^2\eta^k\\
&=2\left[\log\frac1\eta\right]^2\frac{\eta(1+\eta)}{(1-\eta)^3}
\le24\eta\left[\log\frac1\eta\right]^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The series identity follows by applying \(\eta\,d/d\eta\) twice to \(\sum_{k\ge0}\eta^k=(1-\eta)^{-1}\), valid for \(0<\eta<1\). The final inequality uses \(\eta<1/16<1/2\), so \((1+\eta)/(1-\eta)^3\le12\).

The function \(\eta\mapsto\eta[\log(1/\eta)]^2\) is nondecreasing on \((0,1/16]\): its derivative is \(\log(1/\eta)(\log(1/\eta)-2)\ge0\) there. Since \(\eta\le2\delta<1/16\), we conclude

\[
\mathbb E_{Q_J}[G_J^2]
\le48\delta\left[\log\frac1{2\delta}\right]^2
\le48\delta\left[\log\frac1\delta\right]^2.
\]

This comparison also handles arbitrarily large individual energy increments: increasing an energy above \(kG_J(1)\) only decreases its contribution to the second moment. Extending the sum to infinity handles every finite tail length uniformly.

Finally all potentials are nonnegative, so
\(|A_J(v)|\le(\phi_{J+v}+\phi_{J-v})/2=G_J(v)+\phi_J\).
At every nonzero displacement, \(G_J(v)>2\) and \(\phi_J<1\), giving \(|A_J(v)|\le(3/2)G_J(v)\) and \(G_J(v)\le G_J(v)^2\). At zero, both \(G_J(0)\) and \(A_J(0)\) are exactly zero. Thus the pointwise bound \(G_J+A_J^2\le4G_J^2\) holds everywhere on \(\mathcal U_J\), and

\[
\mathbb E_{Q_J}[G_J+A_J^2]
\le192\delta\left[\log\frac1\delta\right]^2
\le1000\delta\left[1+\log\frac1\delta\right]^2.
\]

Together with \(\phi_J\le250\delta^2\), this proves both requested estimates with \(C_\ast=1000\), for every maximizer. Existence follows from finiteness and nonemptiness of \(D\). Every support truncation and both orientations were retained throughout, and no product packing result was used.
