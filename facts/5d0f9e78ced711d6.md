## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex body. Retain the locked mixed-integer set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For every \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\), define \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}}\), and \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\). Put \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\) and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I}w_{\mathbf I}>0\). Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have weight zero. The finite set \(D\) includes every lattice point of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\), including its boundary. There is no assumption on the affine rank of \(D\). For \(\mathbf I\in D\), let \(U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}/w_{\mathbf I}\). All logarithms are natural.

For a target \(\mathbf J\in D\), a finite feasible coupled scheme has components indexed by \(k\). Each component consists of a nonempty set \(E_k\subseteq D\) of distinct source indices, numbers \(\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}>0\), a mass \(x_k\ge0\), and a joint probability law \(\Pi_k\) on \(\prod_{\mathbf I\in E_k}C_{\mathbf I}\), satisfying

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J,
\qquad
(\Pi_k)_{\mathbf I}=U_{\mathbf I},
\qquad
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\le w_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D).
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, the finite measure \(x_k\Pi_k\) has marginal exactly \(x_kU_{\mathbf I}\) at each source occurrence. Every occurrence is charged the full scalar \(x_k\), independently of its positive barycentric coefficient. Capacities are shared across all components, including components with identical supports. This is exactly the capacity and uniform-marginal convention of accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`; its separate central mass is included here as a singleton component \(E_k=\{\mathbf J\}\) with coefficient one. No bound on the support sizes and no affine-independence requirement are imposed.

Let \(P_k\) be the law of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E_k}\lambda_{k,\mathbf I}\mathbf X_{\mathbf I}\) under \(\Pi_k\), let \(M=\sum_kx_k>0\), and set \(P=M^{-1}\sum_kx_kP_k\). Require that this final probability law \(P\) have a bounded measurable Lebesgue density. Convexity supports it in \(C_{\mathbf J}\). Define

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(C)
=\sup_{\substack{\text{finite feasible coupled schemes at }\mathbf J\\
M>0,\;P\text{ has a bounded measurable density}}}
\frac{M}{1+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})},
\qquad
\mathcal E(C)=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(C).
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|U_{\mathbf J})=\int q\log(qw_{\mathbf J})\,d\mathcal L^d\) for the output density \(q\), with \(0\log0=0\). It is finite under the stated hypothesis. Only the entropy of the final mixture occurs in (2), and the component couplings may be arbitrary.

Normalize \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\) on \(D\), with zero outside \(D\). Use exactly the quantities of accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`:

\[
I(\mathbf J)=-\log\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)},
\qquad
I_*=\min_{\mathbf J\in D}I(\mathbf J),
\qquad
E(p)=e^{-I_*}.
\]

The source's unnormalized exponential capacities are therefore precisely

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)
&:=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}
=W e^{-I(\mathbf J)},\\
\mathfrak C_*(w)&:=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)
=W E(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The notation \(E(p)\) retains its accepted meaning; \(\mathcal E(C)\) is the different actual-mixture score (2). The variational capacity formula and the actual-depth tensor rate in accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df` are existing inputs, not new conclusions of this packet.

Write \(C^{\times m}\) for the product of \(m\ge1\) copies of \(C\), regrouped into \(nm\) integer and \(dm\) continuous coordinates. Its full lattice-volume table is \(w^{\otimes m}_{(\mathbf I_1,\ldots,\mathbf I_m)}=\prod_{a=1}^m w_{\mathbf I_a}\), its positive support is exactly \(D^m\), and its mixed volume, measured with \(\mathcal H_{dm}\), is \(W^m\). The definitions (1)-(2) on this product use dimension \(dm\), its entire positive support, and all its actual uniform product-fiber laws.

For every positive target, \(w_{\mathbf J}\le\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(C)\le\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)\). For the tensor powers,

\[
\mathcal E(C^{\times m})\le\mathfrak C_*(w)^m
\quad(m\ge1),
\qquad
\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathcal E(C^{\times m})^{1/m}
=\mathfrak C_*(w)=W E(p)\ge2^{-n}W.
\tag{4}
\]

More explicitly, fix \(\mathbf J\in D\) and a rational probability vector \(\lambda=(\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) with mean \(\mathbf J\). Zero entries of \(\lambda\) are allowed. Set
\(L_{\log}(\lambda)=\log w_{\mathbf J}-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}\ge0\).
For every integer \(m\ge1\) such that all \(m\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) are integers, put
\(b_m=\exp(m\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I})\).
There is a feasible scheme at \(\mathbf J^{\times m}=(\mathbf J,\ldots,\mathbf J)\) whose mass and final output law satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_m&\ge\frac1m
\binom{m}{(m\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}}b_m,\\
\frac{dP_m}{dU_{\mathbf J}^{\otimes m}}
&\le\frac{m^{dm}w_{\mathbf J}^m}{b_m}
\quad\text{a.e.},\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_m\|U_{\mathbf J}^{\otimes m})
&\le dm\log m+mL_{\log}(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\mathcal E(C^{\times m})\ge
\frac{\displaystyle
\binom{m}{(m\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}}
\exp\!\left(m\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}\right)}
{\displaystyle m\bigl(1+dm\log m+mL_{\log}(\lambda)\bigr)}.
\tag{6}
\]

Equality \(\mathfrak C_*(w)=2^{-n}W\) holds if and only if \(D\) consists of exactly \(2^n\) vertices of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\), that convex hull has no other lattice points, and every \(w_{\mathbf I}\) equals \(W/2^n\). In that case \(\mathcal E(C^{\times m})=2^{-nm}W^m\) for every \(m\ge1\). For every fixed body \(C\), including the strict-capacity case, there is an integer \(m_0(C)\ge1\) such that

\[
\mathcal E(C^{\times m})\ge2^{-nm}W^m
\qquad(m\ge m_0(C)).
\tag{7}
\]

Finally, for each integer \(r\ge1\), define

\[
P_r=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r,-\mathbf1\}
\subset\mathbb R^r,
\qquad
C_r=P_r\times[0,1],
\qquad
A_r=\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_r,-\mathbf1\}.
\]

The complete integer support of \(P_r\) is \(A_r\), and every fiber of \(C_r\) over \(A_r\) has volume one. Thus \(W(C_r)=r+2\), \(\mathfrak C_*(w)=r+2\), and \(\mathcal E(C_r)=2\). More precisely, if a target \(\boldsymbol{\jmath}\in A_r^m\) has exactly \(k\) zero blocks, where \(0\le k\le m\), then

\[
\mathcal E_{\boldsymbol{\jmath}}(C_r^{\times m})
=1+\frac{(r+2)^k-1}{r+1}.
\qquad
\mathcal E(C_r^{\times m})
=1+\frac{(r+2)^m-1}{r+1}.
\tag{8}
\]

Both upper bounds control every feasible scheme and every admissible coupling; the displayed values are attained with zero final output entropy. Consequently no absolute constant \(c>0\) satisfies \(\mathcal E(C)\ge c\mathfrak C_*(w)\) for all such bodies. There are also no absolute \(A>0\) and polynomial \(Q(m,n,d)\), positive on positive integer inputs, for which

\[
\mathcal E(C^{\times m})\le Q(m,n,d)[A\mathcal E(C)]^m
\quad\text{for every }n,d,C,m.
\tag{9}
\]

The family \(C_r\) satisfies \(\mathcal E(C_r)\ge2^{-r}W(C_r)\), so (8)-(9) do not disprove the requested universal one-copy dyadic lower bound. No assertion that a universal threshold in (7) is impossible is made.

## Proof

Every positive-volume fiber is a compact convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\), and compactness of the base projection makes \(D\) finite. To check the complete-support issue directly, if \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), write \(\mathbf J=\sum_{a=1}^s t_a\mathbf I_a\) with all \(t_a>0\), \(\sum_a t_a=1\), and \(\mathbf I_a\in D\). Choose any \(\mathbf y_a\in C_{\mathbf I_a}\) for \(a\ge2\). Convexity gives
\(t_1C_{\mathbf I_1}+\sum_{a=2}^s t_a\mathbf y_a\subseteq C_{\mathbf J}\).
Its volume is \(t_1^d w_{\mathbf I_1}>0\), so \(\mathbf J\in D\). This argument also works when \(s=1\), at boundary targets, and in any affine rank.

Accepted fact `b563a1eb51a20cd2` proves global log-concavity of the entire actual real fiber-volume function, with all zeros retained. Its positive integer support is the present \(D\), denoted \(A\) there. Applying its finite convex-combination inequality gives
\(\log w_{\mathbf J}\ge\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}\)
for every probability vector of mean \(\mathbf J\). This proves the nonnegativity of \(L_{\log}(\lambda)\), without assumptions on the affine rank or on interiority of \(\mathbf J\).

For any scheme, convexity puts every output in its target fiber. The measures \(x_k\Pi_k\) have the stated exact marginals, and (1) gives simultaneously

\[
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}(x_k\Pi_k)_{\mathbf I}
=\left(\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\right)U_{\mathbf I}
\le w_{\mathbf I}U_{\mathbf I}=\mu_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{10}
\]

Thus these are the full measure capacities used in accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`. Summing the scalar capacities over \(D\) gives \(\sum_k|E_k|x_k\le W\), hence \(M\le W\). If \(P\) has a bounded density, its density ratio \(g=dP/dU_{\mathbf J}\) is bounded, and \(\int g\log g\,dU_{\mathbf J}\) is finite. The inequality \(t\log t\ge t-1\) for \(t\ge0\) gives its nonnegativity. The central singleton with mass \(w_{\mathbf J}\) has output \(U_{\mathbf J}\), proving \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(C)\ge w_{\mathbf J}>0\).

The accepted variational formula in `01c991c9f33e83df`, under the exact normalization (3), reads

\[
\log\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)
=\max_{\substack{\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\;\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\\
\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J}}
\left[H(\lambda)+\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}\right],
\qquad
H(\lambda)=-\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log\lambda_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, the bracket is \(\log W-\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log(\lambda_{\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\). The accepted entropy minimum is \(I(\mathbf J)\), giving (11) and (3). The accepted proof handles a boundary target by its minimal face \(F_{\mathbf J}\) of \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\): every feasible distribution is supported on \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\), and it constructs an optimizing distribution positive at every point of \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). The construction works in the relative affine span and suppresses points outside that face by an exposing normal. In particular it makes no assertion that the full exponential infimum is attained by a finite ambient exponent. These are the boundary and rank conventions used below.

We first prove the upper bound for arbitrary couplings. Fix \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n\) and a scheme at \(\mathbf J\). Every component contains at least one source with \(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0\), because the positive weighted average of these scores is zero. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
M
&\le\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0}}
\sum_{k:\mathbf I\in E_k}x_k\\
&\le\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\ge0}}w_{\mathbf I}
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}
e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

This includes \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\). Taking the infimum proves \(M\le\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)\). Since the denominator in (2) is at least one, it proves the claimed upper bound for \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\), independently of the supports, coefficients, or couplings.

Product fibers and their volumes factor exactly, including zero-volume factors, so the product assertions before (4) follow by finite summation and product Lebesgue measure. At a positive product target \((\mathbf J_1,\ldots,\mathbf J_m)\), the exponential sum factorizes into \(m\) factors with independent exponent vectors. Its infimum equals \(\prod_{a=1}^m\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J_a}(w)\): one inequality follows by bounding every factor below by its infimum, and the other by choosing each exponent within an arbitrarily small error of its own infimum. Each infimum is positive because its target term is \(w_{\mathbf J_a}>0\). This argument includes boundary infima that are not attained. Maximizing (12) on \(D^m\) proves the upper bound in (4). Nonempty zero-volume fibers outside \(D^m\) have zero mixed-volume contribution and are not sources or targets of (2).

We next construct the schemes in (5). For a rational \(\lambda\) and an allowed \(m\), let \(\mathcal T_m(\lambda)\) be the set of words \((\mathbf I_1,\ldots,\mathbf I_m)\in D^m\) in which \(\mathbf I\) occurs exactly \(m\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) times. Its size is the multinomial coefficient in (5). Every such word has product-fiber volume \(b_m\).

Partition \(\mathcal T_m(\lambda)\) into orbits under cyclic rotation of its \(m\) coordinate blocks. If an orbit \(E\) has \(s\) distinct words, then \(1\le s\le m\) and each distinct word occurs exactly \(m/s\) times among all \(m\) rotations of one of them. Averaging all rotations gives, in each coordinate block, the empirical mean \(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). The uniform average over the distinct words in \(E\) therefore equals \(\mathbf J^{\times m}\), including for periodic words.

Use one component with support \(E\), equal positive coefficients \(1/s\), and mass exactly \(b_m\). The orbits are disjoint. Thus each word in the type class is used in exactly one component and is charged exactly its full capacity \(b_m\), and every other source is unused. The number of orbits is at least \(|\mathcal T_m(\lambda)|/m\), giving the mass bound in (5). Arity may vary from one orbit to another and may grow with \(m\); no support reduction is invoked.

For this component choose its \(s\) source random vectors independently, each with its exact uniform law on its product fiber in \(\mathbb R^{dm}\). A source vector has density bounded by \(1/b_m\). Scaling it by \(1/s\) gives density bounded by \(s^{dm}/b_m\), because the continuous dimension is \(dm\). If a random vector with density \(f\) is independent of a probability law \(\nu\), their sum has density \(f*\nu\) and \(|(f*\nu)(\mathbf y)|\le\|f\|_\infty\) almost everywhere, directly by integration. Applying this to the remaining scaled source vectors bounds the component output density by \(s^{dm}/b_m\le m^{dm}/b_m\). When \(s=1\), the component is already a uniform source law, so the same bound holds without a convolution.

All component outputs lie in \(C_{\mathbf J}^{\times m}\). Their common density bound passes directly to their normalized mass mixture, giving a bounded measurable final density at most \(m^{dm}/b_m\). The target volume is \(w_{\mathbf J}^m\), so its density ratio is bounded by the second line of (5). This is a bound on the final mixture itself. Integrating its logarithm against that final probability law gives

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_m\|U_{\mathbf J}^{\otimes m})
\le\log\frac{m^{dm}w_{\mathbf J}^m}{b_m}
=dm\log m+mL_{\log}(\lambda).
\tag{13}
\]

The ratio bound is at least one, also evident from \(L_{\log}(\lambda)\ge0\). This proves all of (5); division of its mass lower bound by one plus its entropy upper bound gives (6). At \(m=1\), an integral type of mean \(\mathbf J\) is the singleton type at \(\mathbf J\), and the construction and formula remain valid. If \(D\) itself is a singleton, the general upper bound and the central singleton give \(\mathcal E(C^{\times m})=W^m\) for every \(m\).

We give the all-integer limit argument explicitly. Fix any rational feasible \(\lambda\), and choose an integer \(q\ge1\) for which \(q\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) is integral for every \(\mathbf I\). For each \(m\ge1\), set \(a_m=q\lfloor m/q\rfloor\), \(t_m=m-a_m\), and

\[
\lambda^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}
=\frac{a_m}{m}\lambda_{\mathbf I}
+\frac{t_m}{m}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf I=\mathbf J\}}.
\tag{14}
\]

These are feasible probability vectors of mean \(\mathbf J\); all \(m\lambda^{(m)}_{\mathbf I}\) are integers. This amounts to padding the type of length \(a_m\) with \(t_m<q\) copies of the target. It also covers \(a_m=0\). We have \(\lambda^{(m)}\to\lambda\) and \(L_{\log}(\lambda^{(m)})=(a_m/m)L_{\log}(\lambda)\), so these gaps stay bounded.

For completeness, monotonicity of \(\log x\) on \([1,N]\) gives \(\log(N!)=N\log N-N+O(\log(N+1))\) for integers \(N\ge1\), by bounding the sum of logarithms between its integral and that integral plus \(\log N\). The same bound with an absolute constant includes \(N=0\), using \(0!=1\) and \(0\log0=0\). Subtracting this bound for the finitely many type counts from the bound for \(m!\) yields, uniformly over all types on the fixed finite alphabet \(D\),

\[
\log\binom{m}{(m\lambda^{(m)}_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}}
=mH(\lambda^{(m)})+O(|D|\log(m+1)).
\tag{15}
\]

Apply (6) to \(\lambda^{(m)}\). The logarithm of its denominator, divided by \(m\), tends to zero because \(d\) is fixed and \(L_{\log}(\lambda^{(m)})\) is bounded. Entropy is continuous on the finite probability simplex with the zero convention. Taking logarithms, dividing by \(m\), and using (15) gives

\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log\mathcal E(C^{\times m})
\ge H(\lambda)+\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{16}
\]

Rational feasible barycentric distributions are dense even at boundary targets and in smaller affine rank. Here is an elementary justification. Their feasible set is the compact set \(\{\lambda\ge0:B\lambda=b\}\), where the columns of the integer matrix \(B\) are \((1,\mathbf I)\) and \(b=(1,\mathbf J)\). If the columns on the positive support of a feasible vector are linearly dependent, a nonzero null vector supported there has both signs, since its coordinates sum to zero. Move in either direction along it until a coordinate first becomes zero. The original vector is a convex combination of these two feasible endpoint vectors, each with smaller support. Iterating expresses any feasible vector as a convex combination of feasible vectors whose positive-support columns are linearly independent. Such a vector is rational, because its supported coordinates solve a full-column-rank rational linear system. Approximating the finitely many convex-combination coefficients by nonnegative rational numbers summing to one proves the density assertion. It includes a feasible set consisting of the single vertex distribution.

Continuity of the objective in (11) consequently lets the right side of (16), for rational \(\lambda\), approach \(\log\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)\). Maximize over the finitely many targets and combine with the upper bound in (4). This proves the limit in (4). The bound \(\mathfrak C_*(w)\ge2^{-n}W\) is already implied by accepted fact `01c991c9f33e83df`. We give a different parity argument next in order to prove its full equality classification.

Normalize to \(p=w/W\). Consider the nonnegative arrays \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), with both indices in \(D\), satisfying

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
\quad(\mathbf J\in D).
\tag{17}
\]

The diagonal array is feasible, and all coordinates lie in compact intervals \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\), so this feasible set is nonempty and compact. Write \(q_{\mathbf J}=\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), and minimize \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\|\mathbf J\|^2\) on it.

At a minimizer, suppose two distinct positive rows \(\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2\) have the same coordinatewise parity. Their midpoint \(\mathbf K=(\mathbf J_1+\mathbf J_2)/2\) belongs to \(D\) by its complete lattice support. Choose \(0<\varepsilon\le\min(q_{\mathbf J_1},q_{\mathbf J_2})\), remove the array row \(\varepsilon r_{\mathbf J_a,\mathbf I}/q_{\mathbf J_a}\) from each of those two rows, and add both removed rows to row \(\mathbf K\). Column sums are unchanged. The removed rows have masses \(\varepsilon\) and means \(\mathbf J_1,\mathbf J_2\), so their sum has mass \(2\varepsilon\) and mean \(\mathbf K\). Thus (17) and nonnegativity are preserved. The objective decreases by
\(\varepsilon(\|\mathbf J_1\|^2+\|\mathbf J_2\|^2-2\|\mathbf K\|^2)=\varepsilon\|\mathbf J_1-\mathbf J_2\|^2/2>0\), a contradiction. There are therefore at most \(2^n\) positive rows.

For each positive row, let \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}=r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/q_{\mathbf J}\). It is a probability vector of mean \(\mathbf J\), and on its support \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\le p_{\mathbf I}\) gives \(p_{\mathbf I}/\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge q_{\mathbf J}\). Formula (11) now gives

\[
\frac{\mathfrak C_*(w)}W
\ge\frac{\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)}W
\ge\exp\!\left(\sum_{\mathbf I:\lambda_{\mathbf I}>0}\lambda_{\mathbf I}
\log\frac{p_{\mathbf I}}{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\right)
\ge q_{\mathbf J}.
\tag{18}
\]

The row masses sum to one, so some positive row has mass at least \(2^{-n}\). This reproves the sharp capacity bound using complete lattice support and entropy.

Suppose now \(\mathfrak C_*(w)/W=2^{-n}\). Every positive row mass is at most \(2^{-n}\) by (18), and there are at most \(2^n\) such rows. Hence exactly \(2^n\) rows are positive and all their masses equal \(2^{-n}\). Equality holds throughout (18) for every positive row. Each term \(\log(p_{\mathbf I}/\lambda_{\mathbf I})\) on its support is at least \(\log q_{\mathbf J}\); equality of their weighted average forces equality term by term. Thus
\(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=q_{\mathbf J}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\)
whenever that row uses \(\mathbf I\). Since each positive column has sum \(p_{\mathbf I}\), it is assigned entirely to one row. The positive-row supports consequently form a partition \((A_{\mathbf J})\) of \(D\).

Equality in the middle step of (18) also makes each row distribution an optimizer in (11). Its support is exactly \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\), where \(F_{\mathbf J}\) is the target's minimal face. To justify the full-support conclusion without presuming uniqueness, the accepted face construction after (11) supplies a feasible distribution positive at every site of \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\), and all feasible distributions vanish off that face. If an optimizer omitted a site there, mixing it with that positive distribution by a small amount \(t>0\) would increase its entropy objective: the formerly zero coordinates contribute a positive constant times \(-t\log t\), up to an \(O(t)\) term, whereas the change from its positive coordinates and the linear weight term is \(O(t)\). This is positive for sufficiently small \(t\), a contradiction. Therefore
\(A_{\mathbf J}=D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\)
for every positive row.

Two sites of the same parity cannot lie in different members of this partition. Their integer midpoint belongs to \(D\), hence to some \(A_{\mathbf K}=D\cap F_{\mathbf K}\). A face that contains the midpoint of two points of the convex hull contains both endpoints: this follows by applying its supporting linear functional and using equality in the average of two inequalities. Both sites would therefore be in \(A_{\mathbf K}\), contrary to their distinct partition members. Thus different members use disjoint parity classes. There are \(2^n\) nonempty members and only \(2^n\) parities, so every member uses exactly one parity.

Each member \(D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\) is closed under integer midpoints: the face is convex and every integer point in the hull lies in \(D\). A finite set with this property, whose sites all have one parity, is a singleton. Otherwise choose a closest pair of distinct sites; its integer midpoint belongs to the set and is strictly closer to either endpoint. It follows that every \(A_{\mathbf J}\) is a singleton. Its row mean is \(\mathbf J\), so it must be \(\{\mathbf J\}\). Since \(F_{\mathbf J}=\operatorname{conv}(D\cap F_{\mathbf J})\), this minimal face is a singleton and \(\mathbf J\) is a vertex. There are exactly \(2^n\) such sites, every \(p_{\mathbf J}=q_{\mathbf J}=2^{-n}\), and complete support excludes any further lattice points of their hull. This proves necessity, with no full-affine-rank assumption.

Conversely, under the stated vertex and equal-weight conditions, every probability vector of mean \(\mathbf J\in D\) is the singleton vector because \(\mathbf J\) is a vertex. Formula (11) gives \(\mathfrak C_{\mathbf J}(w)=w_{\mathbf J}=W/2^n\), proving capacity equality. At every product target each coordinate block is a vertex, forcing every positive-coefficient source word to equal that target. Hence every component is a singleton, its total mass is at most \((W/2^n)^m\), and central mass attains that value with zero entropy. This proves the asserted exact entropy score for all \(m\) in the equality case. If instead \(\mathfrak C_*(w)>2^{-n}W\), the limit (4) is strictly larger than \(2^{-n}W\), so for all sufficiently large integers \(m\) the \(m\)-th root of the entropy score exceeds \(2^{-n}W\). This proves (7) with a threshold allowed to depend on the fixed body.

It remains to verify the exact family and its all-target upper bounds. The vertices of \(P_r\) are affinely independent: if \(\sum_{i=1}^r a_i(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf1)=\mathbf0\), then each coordinate gives \(a_i+\sum_j a_j=0\); summing these identities gives \((r+1)\sum_j a_j=0\), and then every \(a_i=0\). Thus \(P_r\) is full-dimensional and \(C_r\) is a convex body with continuous dimension one. Their vertices sum to zero, so the origin lies in the simplex.

Every point \(\mathbf x\in P_r\) has a representation \(x_i=\alpha_i-\alpha_0\), where \(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_r\ge0\) and \(\sum_{i=0}^r\alpha_i=1\). If \(\alpha_0=0\) and \(\mathbf x\) is integral, its coordinates are nonnegative integers summing to one, so \(\mathbf x=\mathbf e_i\) for some \(i\). If \(\alpha_0=1\), then \(\mathbf x=-\mathbf1\). If \(0<\alpha_0<1\), every integral coordinate satisfies \(x_i\ge0\), because \(x_i\ge-\alpha_0>-1\). Also \(\sum_i x_i=1-(r+1)\alpha_0<1\), so integrality forces every coordinate to vanish. The origin is indeed attained by \(\alpha_i=1/(r+1)\) for all \(i\). This proves \(P_r\cap\mathbb Z^r=A_r\), with every listed fiber exactly \([0,1]\) and all others empty.

The uniform distribution on \(A_r\) has mean zero. For every \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r\), the inequality \(e^u\ge1+u\) gives \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in A_r}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}\ge r+2\), with equality at \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\). Hence \(\mathfrak C_{\mathbf0}(w)=r+2\). Every capacity is at most \(W=r+2\) by choosing zero exponent, proving \(\mathfrak C_*(w)=r+2\).

First take the all-zero target in \(A_r^m\). Let the cyclic permutation of the \(r+1\) nonzero vertices fix zero, and act by that same permutation simultaneously on every block of a word. Every nonzero word has an orbit of exactly \(r+1\) distinct words, since at least one of its blocks is a nonzero vertex on a full cycle. In each block the orbit average is zero: a zero label stays zero, and a nonzero label runs through all vertices whose sum is zero. These disjoint orbits give feasible components with equal coefficients \(1/(r+1)\) and mass one. Add the central singleton of mass one. Every source word is used once at its exact unit capacity, giving total mass \(1+((r+2)^m-1)/(r+1)\). All source and target fibers are the same cube \([0,1]^m\). In each component, take every source vector to be the same random vector uniformly distributed on that cube. Each marginal is exact and each barycentric output is that same random vector. The final mixture is uniform, so its entropy is zero. This proves the lower bound in (8) at that target.

For the matching upper bound, take any positive-coefficient component at the all-zero target that contains a nonzero source word. In a block where one of its source labels is nonzero, let \(a_0\) be the total barycentric coefficient of source words having label \(-\mathbf1\), and \(a_i\) that of words having label \(\mathbf e_i\). The block's mean-zero condition is \(a_i-a_0=0\) for every \(i\). At least one of these numbers is positive, hence all \(r+1\) are positive. Thus the component contains at least \(r+1\) distinct nonzero source words, one for each of these labels. Summing full-occurrence charges over the \((r+2)^m-1\) nonzero source capacities shows that the total mass of all such components is at most \(((r+2)^m-1)/(r+1)\). Components containing no nonzero word are central singletons, whose combined mass is at most one. This proves the same upper bound on \(M\), even if other components also charge the central source. Nonnegative entropy gives the upper bound on (2) for every coupling.

For a general target \(\boldsymbol{\jmath}\in A_r^m\), every block of its target that is a vertex forces that block to be fixed at that vertex in all source words of every component. This follows from the defining extreme-point property and the positivity of all coefficients. If exactly \(k\) blocks are zero, there are exactly \((r+2)^k\) possible source words after fixing the vertex blocks. The preceding charge argument applied to the zero blocks gives \(M\le1+((r+2)^k-1)/(r+1)\), including the case \(k=0\), when only the target word is allowed. Acting cyclically only in those zero blocks supplies matching disjoint orbits. Keep the vertex blocks fixed and use the same common-uniform couplings on the actual full-dimensional fibers \([0,1]^m\). The outputs remain uniform on that cube. This proves the first formula of (8) at every target, and its maximum occurs at \(k=m\). Setting \(m=1\) gives \(\mathcal E(C_r)=2\).

Consequently \(\mathcal E(C_r)/\mathfrak C_*(w)=2/(r+2)\to0\), proving the first impossibility claim. If (9) held, fix \(r\), put \(n=r,d=1,C=C_r\), and take \(m\)-th roots. For a fixed polynomial positive on the given inputs, \(Q(m,r,1)^{1/m}\to1\). Formula (8) gives a left-hand limit \(r+2\), while the right-hand limit is \(2A\). Thus \(r+2\le2A\) for every \(r\), a contradiction. Finally \(r+2\le2^{r+1}\) for all \(r\ge1\): it holds at \(r=1\), and doubling the inequality at \(r\) dominates the next left-hand side \(r+3\). Hence \(2=\mathcal E(C_r)\ge2^{-r}(r+2)\), as asserted.
