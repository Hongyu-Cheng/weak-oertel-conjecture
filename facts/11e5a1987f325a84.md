## Statement

Let $b:(0,\bar\varepsilon]\to\mathbb R$ be lower semicontinuous and satisfy $b_*+c\varepsilon\le b(\varepsilon)\le b_*+C\varepsilon$ with $0<c\le C<\infty$. For all sufficiently small $\tau>0$, $b(\varepsilon)-\tau\log\varepsilon$ attains its minimum, and every minimizer is interior and satisfies
$$
\frac{\tau}{eC}\le\varepsilon_\tau\le\frac{2\tau}{c}\log(2C/c).
$$
The same bounds and interior conclusion hold for an attained minimum over joint choices with fixed-scale infima $b(\varepsilon)$, without a separate lower-semicontinuity assumption on $b$.

## Proof

For fixed $\tau>0$ the objective diverges at zero and is lower semicontinuous, so it attains its infimum on a compact subinterval. For small $\tau$ the trial $\tau/C$ is in the interval and gives an infimum at most $b_*+\tau[1+\log C-\log\tau]$. At a minimizing scale write $s=\varepsilon_\tau/\tau$ and compare to the lower value bound to obtain $cs-\log s\le1+\log C$. Dropping $cs$ gives $s\ge1/(eC)$. The tangent bound $\log s\le (c/2)s+\log(2/c)-1$ gives $s\le (2/c)\log(2C/c)$. For small $\tau$ this upper bound times $\tau$ is strictly less than $\bar\varepsilon$. For an already attained joint minimum the same trial upper bound holds by taking fixed-scale infima, and its attained value is bounded below by the slice lower bound, so the same comparison proves the estimates.
