## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
For every bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(m>0\), use the canonical metronoid normalization
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1m\int_{\mathbb R^d}x f(x)\,dx:
  0\leq f\leq\mathbf 1_K\ \text{a.e.},\quad
  \int_{\mathbb R^d}f=m
 \right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(\varepsilon\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), let
\[
 \varnothing\neq I\subseteq
 \{z\in\mathbb Z^n:z\equiv\varepsilon\pmod2\}
\]
be finite, and suppose that
\[
 b_z>0,
 \qquad q_z\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)
 \qquad(z\in I).
\tag{2}
\]
Extend \(b_z\) by zero to \(\mathbb Z^n\setminus I\), and put
\[
 M:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}b_z>0,
 \qquad
 S_c^\circ:=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\z\ne c}}
 b_zb_{2c-z},
 \qquad
 b_c^\circ:=\frac{S_c^\circ}{M}
 \quad(c\in\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
Whenever \(S_c^\circ>0\), define
\[
 q_c^\circ:=\frac1{S_c^\circ}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\z\ne c,\ b_zb_{2c-z}>0}}
 b_zb_{2c-z}\frac{q_z+q_{2c-z}}2.
\tag{4}
\]
Every summand in (4) has \(z,2c-z\in I\), so all continuous points in (4) are defined.  Then
\[
 q_c^\circ\in\mathsf M_{b_c^\circ}(C_c)
 \qquad(S_c^\circ>0).
\tag{5}
\]

Set
\[
 B^\circ:=\sum_{c\in\mathbb Z^n}b_c^\circ.
\]
The exact total-mass and full first-moment identities are
\[
 B^\circ
 =M-\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
 \sum_{c\in\mathbb Z^n}b_c^\circ c
 =\sum_{z\in I}b_zz-\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2z,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{c:S_c^\circ>0}b_c^\circ q_c^\circ
 =\sum_{z\in I}b_zq_z-\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2q_z.
\tag{8}
\]
Equivalently, (7)--(8) are the two coordinates of
\[
 \sum_{c:S_c^\circ>0}b_c^\circ(c,q_c^\circ)
 =\sum_{z\in I}b_z(z,q_z)
  -\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2(z,q_z).
\tag{9}
\]

For the exact conditional interpretation, define
\[
 \pi_z:=\frac{b_z}{M}\quad(z\in I),
 \qquad
 s_2:=\sum_{z\in I}\pi_z^2,
 \qquad
 p:=1-s_2.
\tag{10}
\]
Let \(X,Y\) be independent with common law
\(\mathbb P(X=z)=\pi_z\), and put
\[
 \mathcal E:=\{X\ne Y\},
 \qquad
 U:=\frac{X+Y}{2},
 \qquad
 V:=\frac{X-Y}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]
The one-parity hypothesis makes \(U\in\mathbb Z^n\) surely.  One has
\[
 B^\circ=Mp.
\tag{12}
\]
If \(|I|=1\), then \(p=B^\circ=0\), all \(S_c^\circ\) vanish, no \(q_c^\circ\) is defined, and every assertion below requiring conditioning is void.  If \(|I|\geq2\), then \(p>0\), and the probability law
\[
 \nu^\circ:=\sum_{c:S_c^\circ>0}
 \frac{b_c^\circ}{Mp}\,\delta_c
\tag{13}
\]
is exactly
\[
 \nu^\circ=\mathcal L(U\mid\mathcal E).
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover, with \(Q:I\to\mathbb R^d\) denoting the input field
\(Q(z):=q_z\),
\[
 q_c^\circ
 =\mathbb E\left[
   \frac{Q(X)+Q(Y)}2
   \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal E,\ U=c
  \right]
 \qquad(S_c^\circ>0).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus the normalized output is the law of the conditional base midpoint together with its realized conditional continuous barycenter, not the uncollapsed joint law of the continuous midpoint.

Define the finite atomic measure
\[
 \beta^\circ:=
 \sum_{c:S_c^\circ>0}b_c^\circ\delta_{(c,q_c^\circ)},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{16}
\]
For a closed ambient halfspace
\(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
 H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\},
 \qquad
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H_z|_d.
\tag{17}
\]
Then every closed ambient halfspace satisfies
\[
 \mu_C(H)\geq\gamma_d\,\beta^\circ(H).
\tag{18}
\]
This includes atoms on the boundary, vertical ambient halfspaces, and the whole space if it is admitted as a degenerate halfspace; an admitted empty degenerate halfspace has both selected-side terms equal to zero.

Assume now that
\[
 \delta:=\max_{z\in I}\pi_z\leq\frac14.
\tag{19}
\]
Then \(p\geq3/4\), so the conditional law is nonzero.  Put
\[
 \bar z:=\mathbb E X,
 \qquad
 \Sigma:=\operatorname{Cov}(X).
\tag{20}
\]
Under conditioning on \(\mathcal E\), the exact means are
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal E}U
 =\bar z-\frac{\sum_{z\in I}\pi_z^2(z-\bar z)}p,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal E}V=0.
\tag{21}
\]
The realized output base law (13) has covariance
\[
 \frac5{36}\Sigma
 \preceq\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(U)
 \preceq\frac23\Sigma,
\tag{22}
\]
while
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(V)=\frac{\Sigma}{2p},
 \qquad
 \frac12\Sigma
 \preceq\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(V)
 \preceq\frac23\Sigma,
\tag{23}
\]
and the centered cross covariance is
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(U,V)=0.
\tag{24}
\]
No nonsingularity is assumed, and
\[
 \ker\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(U)
 =\ker\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal E}(V)
 =\ker\Sigma,
\tag{25}
\]
so both conditional covariance ranks equal \(\operatorname{rank}\Sigma\).

More generally, let \(\mathcal H\) be a real Hilbert space and let
\(f:I\to\mathcal H\).  Define
\[
 F:=\frac{f(X)+f(Y)}2,
 \qquad
 g(c):=\mathbb E_{\mathcal E}[F\mid U=c]
 \quad(S_c^\circ>0),
\tag{26}
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_\pi(f):=
 \inf_a\mathbb E\|f(X)-a(X)\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\tag{27}
\]
where \(a:\operatorname{aff}(I)\to\mathcal H\) is affine, and
\[
 \mathcal R_\pi(f):=
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal E}\|F-g(U)\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal D_{\nu^\circ}(g):=
 \inf_a\mathbb E_{\mathcal E}\|g(U)-a(U)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{28}
\]
In the last infimum, affine maps may be taken on the same ambient affine space; restriction or affine extension does not change its value.  Both infima are attained, and
\[
 \mathcal D_{\nu^\circ}(g)+\mathcal R_\pi(f)
 =\inf_a\mathbb E_{\mathcal E}\|F-a(U)\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\tag{29}
\]
\[
 \left(\frac12-\delta\right)\mathcal D_\pi(f)
 \leq
 \mathcal D_{\nu^\circ}(g)+\mathcal R_\pi(f)
 \leq
 \frac{\mathcal D_\pi(f)}{2(1-\delta)},
\tag{30}
\]
and
\[
 \max\{\mathcal D_{\nu^\circ}(g),\mathcal R_\pi(f)\}
 \geq
 \left(\frac14-\frac\delta2\right)\mathcal D_\pi(f).
\tag{31}
\]
For \(\mathcal H=\mathbb R^d\) and \(f=Q\), equation (15) says precisely that
\(g(c)=q_c^\circ\).  Consequently the first term in (29)--(31) is the affine defect of the actual metronoid-realized output field in (5), while the second is the exact conditional within-midpoint residual.

All assertions above concern one fixed input parity class.  They neither pair inputs of different parity, whose midpoint need not be integral, nor authorize adding masses produced by separate applications at a common target.  In fact unscaled cross-parity pooling fails even for this diagonal-deleted operation.  Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be any convex body of volume \(a>0\), let \(g_K\) be its centroid, and take
\[
 C=[-1,3]\times K\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d.
\tag{32}
\]
Apply the construction separately to the even input class
\(I_0=\{0,2\}\) and the odd input class \(I_1=\{-1,3\}\), with
\(b_z=a\) and \(q_z=g_K\) at all four inputs.  Each application produces mass exactly \(a\) at target base \(1\), with continuous point \(g_K\).  Their unscaled merge would require
\[
 g_K\in\mathsf M_{2a}(C_1)=\mathsf M_{2a}(K),
\tag{33}
\]
but \(\mathsf M_{2a}(K)=\varnothing\) because every admissible density bounded by \(\mathbf1_K\) has mass at most \(a\).  This is the off-diagonal analogue of the capacity warning in accepted fact `fc828b039898bc41`; no broader impossibility for scaled pooling is asserted.

## Proof

All sums are finite.  If \(b_zb_w>0\), then \(z,w\in I\), hence
\(z\equiv w\pmod2\) and \((z+w)/2\in\mathbb Z^n\).  Thus the ordered off-diagonal pairs used below always have integer midpoints.

Fix \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(S_c^\circ>0\), and set
\[
 J_c^\circ:=
 \{z\in\mathbb Z^n:z\ne c,\ b_zb_{2c-z}>0\},
 \qquad
 w_z:=2c-z,
 \qquad
 \lambda_z:=\frac{b_zb_{w_z}}{S_c^\circ}.
\tag{34}
\]
Then \(J_c^\circ\ne\varnothing\), all \(\lambda_z>0\), and
\(\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}\lambda_z=1\).  The involution
\(z\mapsto w_z\) maps \(J_c^\circ\) bijectively to itself and has no fixed point there: \(w_z=z\) would give \(z=c\), which has been deleted.

Accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` proves, in its positive-root argument, the convexity of
\[
 G:=\{(y,x,s):s>0,\ x\in\mathsf M_{s^d}(C_y)\}.
\tag{35}
\]
The closure in that accepted statement is needed only to adjoin zero-root boundary points; every point used here has positive root.  By (2),
\[
 (z,q_z,b_z^{1/d}),
 \qquad
 (w_z,q_{w_z},b_{w_z}^{1/d})
 \in G.
\]
Their midpoint lies in \(G\), and then the finite convex combination with weights \(\lambda_z\) gives
\[
 (c,q_c^\circ,r_c)\in G,
\tag{36}
\]
where
\[
 r_c:=\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}\lambda_z
 \frac{b_z^{1/d}+b_{w_z}^{1/d}}2.
\tag{37}
\]
Hence
\[
 q_c^\circ\in\mathsf M_{r_c^d}(C_c).
\tag{38}
\]

Because \(J_c^\circ\) and the weights \(b_zb_{w_z}\) are invariant under the fixed-point-free involution, the two root terms in (37) have equal sums.  Therefore
\[
 r_c
 =\frac1{S_c^\circ}
 \sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}.
\tag{39}
\]
Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \((d+1)/d\) and \(d+1\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_c^\circ
 &=\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}
 \bigl(b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\bigr)^{d/(d+1)}
 b_{w_z}^{1/(d+1)}\\
 &\leq
 \left(\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\right)^{d/(d+1)}
 \left(\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}b_{w_z}\right)^{1/(d+1)}\\
 &\leq
 \left(\sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}\right)^{d/(d+1)}
 M^{1/(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{z\in J_c^\circ}b_z^{1+1/d}b_{w_z}
 \geq(S_c^\circ)^{1+1/d}M^{-1/d},
\]
and (39) yields
\[
 r_c\geq\left(\frac{S_c^\circ}{M}\right)^{1/d},
 \qquad
 r_c^d\geq b_c^\circ.
\tag{41}
\]
Choose a representing density \(h_c\) for (38):
\[
 0\leq h_c\leq\mathbf1_{C_c},
 \qquad
 \int h_c=r_c^d,
 \qquad
 \frac1{r_c^d}\int xh_c(x)\,dx=q_c^\circ.
\]
Then
\[
 f_c:=\frac{b_c^\circ}{r_c^d}h_c
\]
is bounded by \(\mathbf1_{C_c}\), has mass exactly \(b_c^\circ\), and has normalized first moment \(q_c^\circ\).  This proves (5).  If \(S_c^\circ=0\), then \(b_c^\circ=0\); no output point is defined or needed, so no zero-mass metronoid is invoked.

We next enumerate ordered pairs.  The correspondence
\[
 (z,w)\in I^2,\ z\ne w
 \longleftrightarrow
 \left(c=\frac{z+w}2,z\right)
\tag{42}
\]
is a bijection from ordered off-diagonal input pairs to all nonzero summands in the family \((S_c^\circ)_c\).  In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B^\circ
 &=\frac1M\sum_{\substack{z,w\in I\\z\ne w}}b_zb_w\\
 &=\frac1M\left(M^2-\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (6), and division by \(M^2\) also proves (12).  The same ordered-pair enumeration gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_cb_c^\circ c
 &=\frac1{2M}
 \sum_{\substack{z,w\in I\\z\ne w}}b_zb_w(z+w)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in I}b_zz-\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2z,
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
because the corresponding sum over all ordered pairs is
\(2M\sum_zb_zz\), while the diagonal contribution before division by \(2M\) is \(2\sum_zb_z^2z\).  Likewise, using (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{c:S_c^\circ>0}b_c^\circ q_c^\circ
 &=\frac1{2M}
 \sum_{\substack{z,w\in I\\z\ne w}}
 b_zb_w(q_z+q_w)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in I}b_zq_z-\frac1M\sum_{z\in I}b_z^2q_z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
This proves (7)--(9), with the diagonal subtraction and the ordered-pair normalization explicit.

If \(|I|=1\), there are no ordered off-diagonal pairs, so the singleton assertions following (12) are immediate.  Suppose \(|I|\geq2\).  Since every \(\pi_z>0\), two distinct support points are drawn with positive probability, so \(p>0\).  For every \(c\in\mathbb Z^n\), independence and (42) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P(U=c,\mathcal E)
 &=\frac1{M^2}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\z\ne c}}
 b_zb_{2c-z}
 =\frac{S_c^\circ}{M^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]
Since \(\mathbb P(\mathcal E)=p\), equations (3), (12), and (45) give
\[
 \mathbb P(U=c\mid\mathcal E)
 =\frac{S_c^\circ}{M^2p}
 =\frac{b_c^\circ}{Mp},
\]
which proves (14).  On a positive output, conditioning further on \(U=c\) assigns to the ordered pair
\((z,2c-z)\) the probability
\(b_zb_{2c-z}/S_c^\circ\).  Taking the conditional expectation of the continuous midpoint is therefore exactly (4), proving (15).  Equations (7)--(8) are also the corresponding unnormalized conditional first moments multiplied by \(Mp\).

We prove (18).  Let
\[
 A_H:=\{c:S_c^\circ>0,\ (c,q_c^\circ)\in H\}.
\tag{46}
\]
For each \(c\in A_H\), membership (5) has positive mass \(b_c^\circ\).  Hence \(|C_c|_d\geq b_c^\circ>0\), so the compact convex fiber \(C_c\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.

First let \(H\) be a proper ambient halfspace, written as
\[
 H=\{(y,x):\langle u,y\rangle+\langle v,x\rangle\geq t\},
 \qquad (u,v)\ne(0,0).
\tag{47}
\]
If \(v\ne0\), then
\[
 H_c=\{x:\langle v,x\rangle\geq t-\langle u,c\rangle\}
\]
is a proper closed fiber halfspace containing \(q_c^\circ\), including when that point lies on its boundary.  The metronoid--Grünbaum containment in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 |C_c\cap H_c|_d\geq\gamma_db_c^\circ.
\tag{48}
\]
If \(v=0\), then \(H_c\) is either empty or all of \(\mathbb R^d\).  It is all of \(\mathbb R^d\) for \(c\in A_H\), and thus
\[
 |C_c\cap H_c|_d=|C_c|_d
 \geq b_c^\circ\geq\gamma_db_c^\circ.
\tag{49}
\]
If the whole space is admitted as a degenerate halfspace, (49) applies to every selected target.  If the empty set is admitted, then \(A_H=\varnothing\) and \(\beta^\circ(H)=0\).  Summing (48) or (49) over the pairwise distinct selected fibers gives in every case
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\geq\sum_{c\in A_H}|C_c\cap H_c|_d\\
 &\geq\gamma_d\sum_{c\in A_H}b_c^\circ
 =\gamma_d\beta^\circ(H),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (18), including every stated boundary and degenerate case.

We now compose with the abstract conditioned-product theorem.  Under (19), the probability law \(\pi\) on the finite affine set \(I\) has maximum atom \(\delta\leq1/4\).  Apply accepted fact `ca2d152bc11360bc` with its probability law \(\mu\) equal to \(\pi\), its event \(E\) equal to \(\mathcal E\), and its midpoint and half-difference variables \(M,S\) renamed here as \(U,V\), respectively.  The definitions of \(s_2,p,\bar z,\Sigma\) agree exactly with (10) and (20).  Equations (21)--(25) are therefore precisely the mean, covariance, cross-covariance, kernel, and rank conclusions of that accepted fact.  By (14), the midpoint covariance in those conclusions is the covariance of the realized output base law \(\nu^\circ\), not an auxiliary law.

For the Hilbert-valued part, use the same substitution and identify the accepted fact's \(\mathcal D_\mu,\mathcal R_\mu,\mathcal D_\nu\) with the explicitly defined \(\mathcal D_\pi,\mathcal R_\pi,\mathcal D_{\nu^\circ}\) in (27)--(28).  Its conditional total-variance identity and two-sided bounds give (29)--(31), including attainment and the affine-extension convention.  For \(f=Q\), the conditional-expectation definition of \(g\) is (15), so \(g(c)=q_c^\circ\).  Membership (5) is exactly the missing realization not supplied by `ca2d152bc11360bc`, and no assertion of simultaneous affine localization is being added.

Finally, verify the cross-parity calibration.  In (32), every relevant fiber is \(K\), and \(g_K\in\mathsf M_a(K)\) is represented by \(\mathbf1_K\).  In either class \(I_0\) or \(I_1\), the total input mass is \(M=2a\).  The only off-diagonal ordered pairs are the two orientations of the pair of distinct inputs; both have midpoint base \(1\).  Thus
\[
 S_1^\circ=2a^2,
 \qquad
 b_1^\circ=\frac{2a^2}{2a}=a,
 \qquad
 q_1^\circ=g_K.
\]
So the two separate applications each use the full fiber capacity \(a\) at the same target.  Their unscaled sum has requested mass \(2a>|K|_d\), proving (33) and the stated prohibition.  The related unconditioned warning and its normalization are recorded in accepted fact `fc828b039898bc41`; neither warning rules out a separately proved scaled pooling operation.
