## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}P\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite nonempty, let the source fibers be compact with positive $d$-volumes and total mass $M$, and suppose an attained fixed-count minimum of the total hinge over nonvertical unit-normal closed cap covers of every plane over $P$ has normals in a common $r$-dimensional subspace $V$, $r\ge1$. Its cap masses obey $c_i\le\tau M$. Some subfamily of at most $2^n(r+1)$ caps covers all those planes, and every positive-hinge cap belongs to every covering subfamily. Consequently there are at most $2^n(r+1)$ positive-source-mass caps and $\tau\ge1/[2^n(r+1)]$. In particular $r=1$ is impossible if $\tau<\gamma_d/2^n$.

## Proof

Project the continuous coordinate onto $V$, where all cap memberships are determined. Write $h=2^n(r+1)$. If no subfamily of at most $h$ covers, choose a point over $P$ with all margins strictly negative for each nonempty such index subset. These finitely many witnesses fit in one compact cube in $V$, and one $\epsilon>0$ is smaller than every absolute negative margin at its associated witness. On $D=\operatorname{conv}(P)$ times that cube, the closed convex tests $\lbrace L_i\le-\epsilon\rbrace $ have every subfamily of at most $h$ meeting its integer trace. Its integer bases are exactly $P$ with the same-parity midpoint property, and its continuous fiber dimension is $r$. Fact 151fa708661bd0e3, with its occupied parity count at most $2^n$, forces a common point on the trace, contradicting coverage. If $n=0$ the same argument uses ordinary Helly on the cube. Pulling back by projection gives the whole-space subcover.

Any positive-hinge cap omitted from a covering subfamily could be made source-empty with the same unit normal and a sufficiently large finite offset, preserving coverage and budgets and strictly lowering the objective. Hence every positive-hinge cap occurs in the subcover. Since the cap planes are null in $d$-volume, positive hinge is equivalent to positive cap mass. Coverage gives $M\le\sum_{i:c_i>0}c_i\le h\tau M$ even if some zero-mass caps are essential for coverage. Finally $2\gamma_d\le1$ by $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$, so $r=1$ contradicts the strict budget in the statement.
