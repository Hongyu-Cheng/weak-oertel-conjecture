## Statement

On $T=[0,1]$ put $g=1/2$. For $0<t<1/4$, the additions $\nu_t=\delta_g$ and $\widetilde\nu_t=\delta_{g+\sqrt t}$ have the same weak limit $\delta_g$. The exact maximum ordinary raw closed-halfspace depths of $1_T\,dx+t\nu_t$ and $1_T\,dx+t\widetilde\nu_t$ are respectively $1/2+t$ at $g$ and $(1+t)/2$ at $(1+t)/2$. Their right derivatives at zero are $1$ and $1/2$.

## Proof

Depth on the line is the minimum of the masses of the two closed rays through the point. Outside $[0,1]$ there is a containing ray of mass zero. For the atom at $g$, both rays through $g$ have mass $1/2+t$. At points below $g$ the left ray has mass below $1/2$, and above $g$ the right ray does.

For the moving atom $a_t=g+\sqrt t\in(0,1)$, points $y<a_t$ in $[0,1]$ give ray masses $y$ and $1-y+t$. Their minimum is at most their mean $(1+t)/2$, attained at $y=(1+t)/2<a_t$. At or above $a_t$ the right ray has mass at most $1-a_t+t=1/2-\sqrt t+t<(1+t)/2$. These comparisons give the exact maxima and their derivatives. Both atoms converge to $g$.
