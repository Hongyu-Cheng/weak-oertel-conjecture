## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(v_0,\ldots,v_d)$ be a $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, in its affine hull, and let $y\in T$ have barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$. Consider the relative volume of an oriented closed halfspace cap through $y$ as a function of its oriented normal. Call the cut proper when some vertex is strictly included and some vertex is strictly excluded. At a stationary proper cap, if two strictly included vertices $i,j$ have $\lambda_i,\lambda_j\le1/d$, there is a feasible variation with strictly negative second derivative. If two strictly excluded vertices satisfy the same coordinate bound, there is a feasible variation with strictly positive second derivative. The conclusions permit zero margins at other vertices. Under $\max_i\lambda_i\le1/d$, every proper local minimum strictly includes exactly one vertex, and any stationary proper cap with at least two strictly included and two strictly excluded vertices has both signs of second variation.

For a stationary proper cap with at least three nonzero vertex margins, all $\lambda_i$ are positive and every zero vertex margin satisfies $\lambda_i=1/d$.

## Proof

Represent the oriented through-$y$ cap by the vertex values $c=(c_0,\ldots,c_d)$ of its affine margin, with $\lambda\cdot c=0$ and the included halfspace given by nonnegative margin. Affine interpolation identifies the nonzero feasible vectors with the oriented normals up to positive rescaling. Stationarity is equivalently vanishing first derivative on $\lambda\cdot w=0$, and local minimality in the normal implies local minimality in this parametrization.

Let $G=\sum_i E_i$ for independent unit exponentials and $U_i=E_i/G$. The change of variables to $(G,U)$ has Jacobian $G^d$. It gives $U$ uniform in barycentric coordinates, independent of the shape-$d+1$ unit-scale gamma variable $G$. Thus the cap volume is $P(c)=\Pr(V\ge0)$ for $V=\sum_i c_iE_i=G L$ and $L=\sum_i c_i U_i$.

First assume at least two positive vertex values and at least one negative. At zero the density $f_L$ is continuous and bounded nearby. Indeed, it is the section volume divided by the constant gradient norm and $|T|$. Properness makes the zero level intersect the interior and prevents it from coinciding with a facet. After translating the parallel sections to a fixed hyperplane, their indicators converge almost everywhere there, with a common bounded support. The same section formula gives a global bound on the density. The density of $V$ is therefore $f(v)=\mathbb E[f_L(v/G)/G]$ near zero by independence and dominated convergence at zero, and $f(0)=f_L(0)/d$ since $\mathbb E[1/G]=1/d$. Shifting all coefficients by $s$ replaces $L$ by $L+s$, so the sum of the first derivatives of $P$ is $f_L(0)=d f(0)$. Stationarity makes the gradient a multiple of $\lambda$ and consequently
$$
P_i=d f(0)\lambda_i .
$$
The signed exponential derivative formulas give $P_i>0$ and $P_i=f(0)$ at a zero margin. Thus all $\lambda_i>0$ and every zero margin has $\lambda_i=1/d$.

For a chosen positive pair $i,j$, use the signed exponential derivative lemma with $\phi(a)=f_{V+aE'}(0)/f(0)$ and set $\psi=1/\phi$. This is the reciprocal transform in the half-line lemma with $h(t)=f(-t)/f(0)$. Its hypotheses hold by positivity, continuity and log-concavity of $f$. Write $q_i=\phi(c_i)=d\lambda_i$ and $q_j=\phi(c_j)=d\lambda_j$. Both are positive and at most one, so $\psi'(c_i),\psi'(c_j)>0$. The feasible direction $w$ with $w_i=q_j$, $w_j=-q_i$ and all other entries zero preserves the strict signs and zero margins for sufficiently small steps. It is not a radial rescaling, because a negative coefficient outside the pair remains fixed and nonzero. The signed exponential Hessian formulas give
$$
\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}P(c+tw)\right|_{t=0}
=2 f(0) q_i^2 q_j^2
\left(D_{ij}\psi-\psi'(c_i)-\psi'(c_j)\right),
$$
where $D_{ij}\psi$ is the divided difference at $c_i,c_j$, or the derivative if they are equal. By convexity the divided difference is at most the derivative at the larger argument. The bracket is strictly negative also at equality, because the derivative at the smaller argument is strictly positive. The first variation vanishes, so this excludes a local minimum.

For the opposite orientation, $P(-c)=1-P(c)$ since a proper hyperplane is volume-null. The linear map $c\mapsto-c$ preserves feasibility and sends stationary caps to stationary caps. Applying the same negative-variation proof to a strictly excluded pair of the original cap gives a positive variation for the original cap. Every proper cut with at least three nonzero margins has at least two positive margins in one of these orientations, so the conclusions about $\lambda_i$ and zero margins apply to either orientation. With all $\lambda_i\le1/d$, properness supplies at least one strictly included vertex, and any additional one precludes local minimality.
