## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and set
\[
\Lambda:=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad S:=C\cap\Lambda.
\]
For a measurable \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
\mu(E):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(E_z),
\qquad
E_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in E\}.
\]
For \(p=(m,x)\in S\), define
\[
A_p:=C\cap(2p-C),\qquad J_C(p):=\mu(A_p),
\]
and let
\[
h_S(p):=\inf\{\mu(C\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), define
\[
s_C(z,x):=
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(C_z\cap(2x-C_z)\bigr).
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1.  For every \(p\in S\),
\[
h_S(p)\geq\frac12J_C(p)
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
h_S(p)\geq
2^{-n-1}\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}\bigl(C\cap(2p-C)\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]

2.  The function
\[
(z,x)\longmapsto s_C(z,x)^{1/d}
\tag{3}
\]
is concave on all of \(C\).  Its value is zero whenever the corresponding
reflected-fiber intersection has zero \(d\)-volume, including on
lower-dimensional fibers.

3.  Let \((z_i,x_i)\in C\) for \(i=1,\ldots,r\), where \(r\geq1\), and let
\(\alpha_i\geq0\) satisfy \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\).  If
\[
m:=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i z_i\in\mathbb Z^n,\qquad
x:=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i x_i,
\]
then \((m,x)\in S\) and
\[
h_S((m,x))
\geq
\frac12\left(
\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i s_C(z_i,x_i)^{1/d}
\right)^d.
\tag{4}
\]

The sampling statement used in (2) is the following.  If
\(q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^q\) is a nonempty compact
centrally symmetric convex set whose center belongs to \(\mathbb Z^q\), then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_q(K)\leq2^q|K\cap\mathbb Z^q|.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, if \(f:\mathbb R^q\to[0,\infty]\) is measurable and, for every
\(t>0\), the superlevel set
\[
K_t:=\{u\in\mathbb R^q:f(u)\geq t\}
\]
is either empty or a compact centrally symmetric convex set, with all
nonempty \(K_t\) having one common center in \(\mathbb Z^q\), then, with
extended integrals allowed,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^q}f(u)\,du
\leq
2^q\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^q}f(k).
\tag{6}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the sampling statement self-containedly.  Translating by the
lattice center preserves both sides of (5), so suppose that \(K=-K\) and
that its center is \(0\).  Put
\[
N:=|K\cap\mathbb Z^q|,\qquad E:=\tfrac12K.
\]
The number \(N\) is finite because \(K\) is compact.  Let
\(Q=[0,1)^q\), and for \(u\in Q\) define the integer-valued multiplicity
\[
M(u):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^q}\mathbf 1_E(u+k).
\]
Only finitely many summands can be nonzero, uniformly for \(u\in Q\), because
\(E\) is bounded.  The half-open cells \(Q+k\) tile \(\mathbb R^q\), so
\[
\int_QM(u)\,du
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^q}\int_Q\mathbf 1_E(u+k)\,du
=\operatorname{vol}_q(E).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(\operatorname{vol}_q(E)>N\), then (7) gives a point \(u\in Q\) for
which \(M(u)\geq N+1\).  Thus there are distinct
\(k_0,\ldots,k_N\in\mathbb Z^q\) such that
\(u+k_j\in E\).  The \(N+1\) vectors
\[
0,\quad k_1-k_0,\quad\ldots,\quad k_N-k_0
\tag{8}
\]
are distinct lattice points.  They all belong to
\[
E-E=\tfrac12K-\tfrac12K
=\tfrac12(K+K)=K.
\tag{9}
\]
Here the second equality uses \(K=-K\), and the last uses convexity of
\(K\).  This contradicts the definition of \(N\).  Hence
\(\operatorname{vol}_q(E)\leq N\), and scaling gives (5).  This argument
also covers lower-dimensional \(K\), for which the left side is zero.

For the layer-cake consequence, apply (5) to each nonempty \(K_t\).  Empty
levels satisfy the same inequality trivially.  Tonelli's theorem and the
pointwise identity
\[
\int_0^\infty\mathbf 1_{\{f(u)\geq t\}}\,dt=f(u)
\tag{10}
\]
give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^q}f(u)\,du
&=\int_0^\infty\operatorname{vol}_q(K_t)\,dt\\
&\leq
2^q\int_0^\infty|K_t\cap\mathbb Z^q|\,dt\\
&=2^q\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^q}f(k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
No finiteness assumption is needed for (11), because every integrand is
nonnegative and both sides may take the value \(+\infty\).

We now prove (1).  Fix \(p=(m,x)\in S\).  The reflection
\[
R_p(z,y):=(2m-z,2x-y)
\tag{12}
\]
maps \(\Lambda\) bijectively onto itself, preserves \(d\)-dimensional volume
on every continuous fiber, and leaves \(A_p\) invariant.  Hence it preserves
the mixed measure restricted to \(A_p\).

Let \(H\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(p\), and move its
boundary parallel toward \(p\) until the boundary passes through \(p\).
The resulting closed halfspace \(H_0\) satisfies
\[
p\in\partial H_0,\qquad H_0\subseteq H.
\tag{13}
\]
The reflected halfspace \(R_p(H_0)\) is the opposite closed halfspace.
They cover \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and their intersection is their common
boundary.  Reflection invariance and inclusion-exclusion therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mu(A_p\cap H_0)
&=\mu(A_p\cap H_0)+\mu(A_p\cap R_p(H_0))\\
&=\mu(A_p)+\mu(A_p\cap\partial H_0)
\geq J_C(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Thus positive mixed mass on the boundary is retained, rather than discarded.
Since \(A_p\subseteq C\), (13)-(14) imply
\(\mu(C\cap H)\geq J_C(p)/2\).  If the admitted halfspace is the whole
space, the same conclusion follows directly from
\(\mu(C)\geq\mu(A_p)\).  Taking the infimum over all closed halfspaces
containing \(p\) proves (1).

To obtain (2), abbreviate \(A:=A_p\), and define on \(\mathbb R^n\)
\[
f(q):=\operatorname{vol}_d(A_q),
\tag{15}
\]
where an empty fiber has volume zero.  This function is upper
semicontinuous.  Indeed, if \(q_j\to q\), compactness of \(A\) implies that
for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
A_{q_j}\subseteq A_q+\varepsilon\overline B_2^d
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }j,
\tag{16}
\]
with the empty-fiber case handled by closedness of the compact base
projection.  Otherwise one could choose \(y_j\in A_{q_j}\) outside the
right side, pass to a convergent subsequence in the compact continuous
projection of \(A\), and obtain a limit in \(A_q\), a contradiction.
Taking volumes in (16) and then letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), using
continuity from above for compact parallel sets, gives
\[
\limsup_{j\to\infty}f(q_j)\leq f(q).
\tag{17}
\]

For \(q_0,q_1\) in the base projection of \(A\), convexity gives
\[
(1-\lambda)A_{q_0}+\lambda A_{q_1}
\subseteq A_{(1-\lambda)q_0+\lambda q_1}.
\tag{18}
\]
The \(d\)-dimensional Brunn-Minkowski inequality, including its
zero-volume cases, shows from (18) that \(f^{1/d}\) is concave on the base
projection.  It follows that every positive superlevel set of \(f\) is
convex.  By (17) and compactness of the base projection, each such superlevel
set is compact.  Since \(A\) is centrally symmetric about \(p=(m,x)\), the
map \(y\mapsto2x-y\) is a volume-preserving bijection from \(A_q\) to
\(A_{2m-q}\).  Hence
\[
f(q)=f(2m-q),
\tag{19}
\]
so all positive superlevel sets are centrally symmetric about the common
lattice point \(m\).

Apply (6), with \(q=n\), to the function (15).  Fubini's theorem, the
description of the fibers of \(A_p\), and (1) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}_{n+d}(A_p)
&=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(q)\,dq\\
&\leq2^n\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}f(z)\\
&=2^n\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(
C_z\cap(2x-C_{2m-z})
\bigr)\\
&=2^nJ_C(p)
\leq2^{n+1}h_S(p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
This proves (2), including the case in which \(A_p\) or some of its fibers
is lower-dimensional.

We next prove assertion 2.  For \(i=0,1\), take
\(p_i=(z_i,x_i)\in C\) and put
\[
K_i:=C_{z_i}\cap(2x_i-C_{z_i}).
\tag{21}
\]
Each \(K_i\) is a nonempty compact convex set because it contains \(x_i\).
For \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), set
\[
z_\lambda:=(1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1,\qquad
x_\lambda:=(1-\lambda)x_0+\lambda x_1.
\]
If \(y_i\in K_i\), then both \(y_i\) and \(2x_i-y_i\) belong to
\(C_{z_i}\).  Convexity of \(C\) therefore gives
\[
(1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1\in C_{z_\lambda}
\tag{22}
\]
and
\[
2x_\lambda-\bigl((1-\lambda)y_0+\lambda y_1\bigr)
=(1-\lambda)(2x_0-y_0)+\lambda(2x_1-y_1)
\in C_{z_\lambda}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (22)-(23) prove the required Minkowski containment
\[
(1-\lambda)K_0+\lambda K_1
\subseteq
C_{z_\lambda}\cap(2x_\lambda-C_{z_\lambda}).
\tag{24}
\]
Applying Brunn-Minkowski to the nonempty compact sets \(K_0,K_1\) and then
using (24) yields
\[
s_C(z_\lambda,x_\lambda)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)s_C(z_0,x_0)^{1/d}
+\lambda s_C(z_1,x_1)^{1/d}.
\tag{25}
\]
Brunn-Minkowski permits either input to have zero \(d\)-volume, so (25)
holds on the whole of \(C\), including lower-dimensional source or midpoint
fibers.  This proves assertion 2.

Finally, let the points and coefficients be as in assertion 3.  Convexity of
\(C\) gives
\[
(m,x)=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i(z_i,x_i)\in C.
\tag{26}
\]
Since \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), this proves \((m,x)\in S\).  Finite Jensen
applied to (3) gives
\[
s_C(m,x)^{1/d}
\geq\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i s_C(z_i,x_i)^{1/d}.
\tag{27}
\]
The summand of \(J_C((m,x))\) indexed by the central fiber \(z=m\) is
exactly
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(C_m\cap(2x-C_m)\bigr)
=s_C(m,x).
\tag{28}
\]
All other summands are nonnegative, so (1), (27), and (28) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((m,x))
&\geq\tfrac12J_C((m,x))\\
&\geq\tfrac12s_C(m,x)\\
&\geq
\frac12\left(
\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i s_C(z_i,x_i)^{1/d}
\right)^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (4).  In particular, the argument uses one central-fiber term of
the ambient symmetric kernel; it does not identify local symmetric overlap
with fiber Tukey depth.

## External sources

The only external theorem used is the \(d\)-dimensional Brunn-Minkowski
inequality in the following exact form: for nonempty compact measurable
sets \(E,F\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-\lambda)E+\lambda F)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_d(E)^{1/d}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_d(F)^{1/d}.
\]
Zero-volume inputs are allowed.  See R. J. Gardner, “The Brunn-Minkowski
Inequality,” Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 39 (2002),
Theorem 4.1, pp. 362-363,
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2.  It applies in (18) and (24)
because all source fibers and reflected-fiber intersections there are
nonempty compact subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\); monotonicity of Lebesgue measure
then transfers the estimate through the displayed containments.  The
sampling estimate and its layer-cake consequence were proved above and use
no external result.
