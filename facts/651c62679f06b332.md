## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(v\ge0\), and \(T\ge0\) be integers and let \(\beta\ge0\) be a finite real number. For \(i\in[k]\), let \(B_i\ge2\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) be integers and put \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,C_{4,i})\) on \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\). The remaining \(v\) coordinates have \(g_i=(0,1)\) on \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace\). Put \(N=k+v+1\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm full}&=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^v,
&\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^{k+v}g_i(x_i),\\
f_E&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{t=E}^T e^{-\beta t},&0\le E\le T,\\0,&E>T,\end{cases}
&Z_a&=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}
\quad(a\in\lbrace{\rm full},{\rm sel}\rbrace).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Here \(E\) is a nonnegative integer. Choose disjoint \(P,J\subseteq[k]\), with \(m=|P|\ge1\), and an integer \(b\ge2\) such that \(B_i\le b\) on \(P\) and \(B_i\ge b\) on \(J\). In (1), the selective domain and its occupation statistics are
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm sel}&=\lbrace\mathbf y\in D_{\rm full}:y_i\ne3\text{ for every }i\in J\rbrace,\\
Q_{P,J}(\mathbf y)&=|\lbrace i\in P\cup J:y_i\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace\rbrace|,
\qquad A_P(\mathbf y)=|\lbrace i\in P:y_i=1\rbrace|,\\
F_{P,J}(\mathbf y)&=\frac{Q_{P,J}(\mathbf y)(Q_{P,J}(\mathbf y)+A_P(\mathbf y)/b)}m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
For \(a\in\lbrace{\rm full},{\rm sel}\rbrace\), retain every integer height atom in
\(\mathcal A_a=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in D_a,\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace\), with original probability \(p^a_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z_a\). Every spatial site has multiplicity one. For a probability law \(p\) in \(\mathbb R^N\), let \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) be the infimum of \(p(H)\) over closed affine halfspaces with nonzero ambient normal containing \(\mathbf z\), and let \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\). All full and selective depths use this same ambient halfspace family. These \(g_i,\Gamma,f_E,Z_a,P,J,m,b,Q_{P,J},A_P,F_{P,J},N,p^a\) are exactly the corresponding objects of accepted 3ecc2f69fbd9c9ca.

Define the untruncated selective Gibbs occupation probabilities and their sum by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_i&=\frac{e^{-\beta B_i}+e^{-\beta C_{4,i}}}
 {1+e^{-\beta}+e^{-\beta B_i}+e^{-\beta C_{4,i}}}
 &&(i\in P),\\
\rho_i&=\frac{e^{-\beta B_i}}{1+e^{-\beta}+e^{-\beta B_i}}
 &&(i\in J),
\qquad \mu_{\rm occ}=\sum_{i\in P\cup J}\rho_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Thus the pool retains four sites while the deleted tail has three sites. This \(\mu_{\rm occ}\) is exactly the \(\mu\) of accepted cf74a7e79555e26b and is not a scalar saddle mean. If a finite real \(a\ge0\) satisfies \(\mu_{\rm occ}^2/m\le a^2\) and \(\mu_{\rm occ}/b\le a\), then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z_{\rm full}}{Z_{\rm sel}}&\le K(a),
&M_3(p^{\rm full})&\ge K(a)^{-4}M_3(p^{\rm sel}),\\
K(a)&=\sqrt{\frac e{e-1}}\,
\exp\!\left(64e^{260}a^2+a(e^{64}-1)\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
The coefficient is exactly the one in cf74a7e79555e26b and is independent of the cap.

There is also the complementary deterministic alternative. Put \(\eta=\min_{i\in P\cup J}B_i\ge2\). For every finite real \(D_0\ge0\),
$$
\frac{T^2}{\eta^2m}+\frac{T}{\eta b}\le D_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{Z_{\rm full}}{Z_{\rm sel}}\le e^{64D_0},
\qquad
M_3(p^{\rm full})\ge e^{-256D_0}M_3(p^{\rm sel}).
\tag{5}
$$

The following calibration uses the exact three-cost family of accepted cc3b72590e88e9a9. For every integer \(\ell\ge2400\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_0&=\ell^2,&m_1&=\left\lceil e^{2\ell/3}\ell^2\right\rceil,
&m_2&=\left\lceil e^{4\ell/3}\ell^2\right\rceil,\\
k&=m_0+m_1+m_2,&M&=2^{20k}k^2,
&B^{(0)}&=10M,\qquad B^{(1)}=\ell M,\qquad B^{(2)}=2\ell M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Let \(I_0,I_1,I_2\) be the respective coordinate groups of sizes \(m_0,m_1,m_2\). The group values \(B^{(j)}\) mean that the canonical coordinate costs satisfy \(B_i=B^{(j)}\) on \(I_j\); put \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,2B_i-1)\), with unit spatial multiplicities.

To specify the reference cap without changing its normalization, for every rate \(s\ge0\), coordinate \(i\), and \(\lambda\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{i,\lambda}&=\lbrace h\in\mathbb Z:\lambda+h,\lambda-h\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\rbrace,\\
e_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]-g_i(\lambda),\\
b_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)-g_i(\lambda-h)],\\
K^{\rm ref}_{s,i}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}}
 e^{-(2s/3)[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]},\\
H^{\rm ref}_{s,i}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}}e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)},\\
P_i^{\rm ref}(s)&=\sum_{\lambda=0}^3K^{\rm ref}_{s,i}(\lambda)^3,
\qquad Z_i^{\rm ref}(s)=\sum_{x=0}^3e^{-s g_i(x)},\\
\delta_i^{\rm ref}(s)&=\log\frac{8P_i^{\rm ref}(s)}{Z_i^{\rm ref}(s)^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Let the scalar root \(\mathsf J_i\) have probabilities \(K^{\rm ref}_{s,i}(\lambda)^3/P_i^{\rm ref}(s)\); conditionally on \(\mathsf J_i=\lambda\), let \(\mathsf V_i\) have probabilities \(e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)}/H^{\rm ref}_{s,i}(\lambda)\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{{\rm ref},i}(s)&=\mathbb E[g_i(\mathsf J_i)+e_{i,\mathsf J_i}(\mathsf V_i)],\\
R_{{\rm ref},i}(s)&=s^2\left(1+\mathbb E[
g_i(\mathsf J_i)^2+e_{i,\mathsf J_i}(\mathsf V_i)^2+b_{i,\mathsf J_i}(\mathsf V_i)^2]\right),\\
\mu_{\rm ref}(s)&=\sum_i\mu_{{\rm ref},i}(s),
\qquad V_{\rm ref}(s)=\sum_iR_{{\rm ref},i}(s),\\
L_{\rm ref}(s)&=40\sqrt{\sum_i\left(1+\mathbb E[
g_i(\mathsf J_i)^2+e_{i,\mathsf J_i}(\mathsf V_i)^2+b_{i,\mathsf J_i}(\mathsf V_i)^2]\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
In particular \(L_{\rm ref}(s)=40\sqrt{V_{\rm ref}(s)}/s\) for \(s>0\). The exact aliases to cc3b72590e88e9a9 are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&(K^{\rm ref}_{s,i},H^{\rm ref}_{s,i},P_i^{\rm ref},Z_i^{\rm ref},\delta_i^{\rm ref},\mathsf J_i,\mathsf V_i,\mu_{{\rm ref},i},R_{{\rm ref},i},\mu_{\rm ref},V_{\rm ref},L_{\rm ref})\\
&\qquad=(K_{s,i},H_{s,i},P_i,Z_i,\delta_i,J_i,V_i,\mu_i,R_i,\mu,V_s,L).
\end{aligned}
$$
Its \(I_{i,\lambda},e_{i,\lambda},b_{i,\lambda}\) are unchanged, and its \(\sigma^b_{s,i}(\lambda)\) is exactly \(\mathbb E[b_{i,\lambda}(\mathsf V_i)^2\mid\mathsf J_i=\lambda]\). Thus no energy moment is being replaced by an occupation probability.

Set
\(s_g=\log(1+19/(20M))\) and
\(T_\ell=\lceil\mu_{\rm ref}(s_g)+L_{\rm ref}(s_g)\rceil+20M+1\).
This is exactly the cap denoted \(T\) in cc3b72590e88e9a9. Its special original tilt is denoted here by \(\beta_{\rm ref}\): it is the unique positive solution of

For every finite original tilt \(\beta\ge0\), every integer cap \(0\le U\le T_\ell\), and every integer number \(v\ge0\) of binary companions, form the full original law (1) with the costs (6), cap \(U\) in place of \(T\), and that \(\beta\). If \(\kappa_{\rm two}>0\) is exactly the coefficient specified in (3) of accepted 69083720ffaaff7c, then this law satisfies

\tag{9}
$$
Here \(T_\ell\) is a fixed reference cap, whereas \(U\) and \(\beta\) are the arbitrary original cap and tilt. The arithmetic dimension \(N=k+v+1\) does not redefine the locked mixed-integer dimensions or mixed set.

## Proof

All displayed original normalizers are positive: the zero spatial vector at height zero has weight one. They are finite because the supports are finite. The spatial marginal of \(p^{\rm sel}\) is exactly \(f_{\Gamma(\mathbf y)}/Z_{\rm sel}\), obtained by summing every height in its original fiber.

For the occupation branch, apply accepted cf74a7e79555e26b with its pool and deleted-tail index sets equal to the present \(P,J\), its \(m,b,T,\beta,B_i,C_{4,i}\) unchanged, and its core consisting of all coordinates outside \(P\cup J\), including the binary companions. Give each such core vector spatial weight one and its original additive energy. This core has a zero-energy site. The coordinate site sets on \(P\) are the full four-site sets, those on \(J\) are their three-site restrictions, and all remaining original sites occur exactly once in that core. Its \(\Gamma,f_E,Z_{\rm sel},A_P,Q\) are therefore precisely (1)-(2), and its untruncated \(\rho_i,\mu\) are (3), with \(\mu=\mu_{\rm occ}\). Consequently that accepted fact gives
\(\mathbb E_{\rm sel}\exp(64\min\lbrace F_{P,J},Q_{P,J}\rbrace)\le K(a)\)
under the two assumptions in (4). The original cap and tilt have not changed. Applying (4) and (6) of accepted 3ecc2f69fbd9c9ca, with the identical objects already mapped in the statement, proves both conclusions in (4).

For the deterministic branch, every feasible selective vector has \(\Gamma(\mathbf y)\le T\). Each coordinate counted by \(Q_{P,J}\) costs at least \(\eta\), including a retained pool three because \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\ge B_i\). All other costs are nonnegative. Hence \(Q_{P,J}\le T/\eta\). Also \(A_P\le m\), so pointwise on the selective support,
$$
F_{P,J}\le\frac{Q_{P,J}^2}{m}+\frac{Q_{P,J}}b
\le\frac{T^2}{\eta^2m}+\frac{T}{\eta b}\le D_0.
\tag{10}
$$
It follows that the same original expectation of \(\exp(64\min\lbrace F_{P,J},Q_{P,J}\rbrace)\) is at most \(e^{64D_0}\). The two conclusions in (5) follow from the same accepted exchange and moment transfer.

In both branches the fourth power has its original measure meaning. Indeed, with \(\delta=Z_{\rm sel}/Z_{\rm full}\), the selective atoms are a subset of the full atoms in the same \(\mathbb R^N\), and \(p^{\rm full}_{\mathbf z}=\delta p^{\rm sel}_{\mathbf z}\) at each selective atom. Thus every closed ambient halfspace has full mass at least \(\delta\) times its selective mass, and \(\tau_{p^{\rm full}}(\mathbf z)\ge\delta\tau_{p^{\rm sel}}(\mathbf z)\) there. Restricting the original sampling sum to these atoms gives \(M_3(p^{\rm full})\ge\delta^4M_3(p^{\rm sel})\). This also directly verifies that the normalizers and halfspaces in (4)-(5) have not been replaced.

We next prove the finite reference-cap estimate required for (9). All scalar calculations in this paragraph concern the fixed \(k\) four-site coordinates in (6), with the reference law (7)-(8), not the binary companions or the original Gibbs law. From (6), \(M\ge100\), \(M\ge k\), \(k/M\le1\), and \(k/M^2\le1\). The ceiling bounds give
\(m_j\le2\ell^2e^{2j\ell/3}\) for \(j=1,2\), because the quantity inside each ceiling is at least one.

Put \(c=Ms_g\). The inequalities \(x/(1+x)\le\log(1+x)<x\), for \(x>0\), give
\(c\ge(19/20)/(1+19/(20M))>9/10\) and \(c<19/20<1\); the strict lower comparison follows already from \(M\ge100\). In particular \(0<s_g<1<12\log2\).

We use the individual-coordinate assertions (13)-(14) of accepted 69083720ffaaff7c. For each present \(i\in I_j\), their scalar \(g_i,B_j,C_{4,i},s,K,H,P,Z,J_i,V_i,\mu_i,R_i\) map to the present \(g_i,B^{(j)},2B^{(j)}-1,s_g,K^{\rm ref},H^{\rm ref},P^{\rm ref},Z^{\rm ref},\mathsf J_i,\mathsf V_i,\mu_{{\rm ref},i},R_{{\rm ref},i}\). Their conditional excess and imbalance are exactly the displayed \(e_{i,\lambda},b_{i,\lambda}\). These accepted assertions each hold for one arbitrary admissible four-site cost, so their individual use here imposes no two-group hypothesis on the three-group product. In particular they give
\(R_{{\rm ref},i}(s_g)<256\) and \(s_g\mu_{{\rm ref},i}(s_g)<32\). For the latter estimate, their conditional mean \(\alpha_{s,i}(\lambda)=g_i(\lambda)+\mathbb E[e_{i,\lambda}(\mathsf V_i)\mid\mathsf J_i=\lambda]\) has mean \(\mu_{{\rm ref},i}\), and their (13) gives
\(s_g\mu_{{\rm ref},i}\le\sqrt{s_g^2\mathbb E\alpha_{s_g,i}(\mathsf J_i)^2}\le\sqrt{2R_{{\rm ref},i}}<\sqrt{512}<32\).
Thus the cheap group contributes less than \(36\ell^2\) to \(\mu_{\rm ref}(s_g)/M\) and less than \(256\ell^2\) to \(V_{\rm ref}(s_g)\), since \(32/c<320/9<36\).

For \(j=1,2\), write \(q_j=e^{-2s_gB^{(j)}/3}\). We have \(s_gB^{(j)}=j\ell c>24\), since \(\ell\ge2400\). The sharper individual assertions (14) therefore apply and give
$$

 =2\ell^2e^{j\ell/15},\\

\qquad

\quad(i\in I_j).

\tag{11}
$$
Combining these with the cheap-group estimates, and using \(s_g<1/M\) and \(c<1\), gives the explicit bounds
$$

 +128\ell^2(m_1q_1+4m_2q_2)\\

For the last mean comparison, \(36\ell^2+1\le37\ell^2\le21\ell^3\) for \(\ell\ge2\). For the last radius comparison, \(256\ell^2+2\le258\ell^4\le320\ell^4e^{2\ell/15}\) for \(\ell\ge1\). These justify the constants without asymptotic estimates.

\(L_{\rm ref}(s_g)/M=40\sqrt{V_{\rm ref}(s_g)}/c\le(16000/9)\ell^2e^{\ell/15}\le1800\ell^2e^{\ell/15}\).

For the exchange in the calibration, choose \(P=I_1\), \(J=I_2\), \(m=m_1\), and \(b=\eta=\ell M\), leaving the cheap and middle groups as four-site coordinates. The eligibility inequalities hold because the pool costs equal \(b\) and the deleted-tail costs equal \(2b\). The reference cap obeys

The two complete supports are then literally the same subset of \(\mathbb R^{k+v+1}\); their normalizers, probabilities, closed ambient halfspace masses, depths, and moments are equal. \(M_3(p^{\rm sel})\ge(\kappa_{\rm two}/52488)8^{-((m_0+m_1)+(m_2+v)+1)}\).

The proof uses no hypothesis about the original probability of any good-cutoff set and assigns no such set mass one.
