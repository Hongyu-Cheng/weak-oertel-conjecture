## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ be compact and convex, with positive total mixed length $M$ and true mixed centroid having integer base. Translate its centroid to $(0,0)$, and write $w_i>0$ for its positive integer fiber widths, $Q=\sum_iw_i^2$, $m=\max_iw_i$. The least mass over closed ambient halfspaces containing the centroid is attained by a finite nonvertical cap. One may choose a minimizing cap which either wholly captures no positive fiber, or has no endpoint coincidences on the positive fibers, has proper cuts on precisely $\{-h,\ldots,h\}$ for some $h\ge0$, and has homogeneous outside tails with at least one wholly captured tail. Moreover any containing cap with no wholly captured positive fiber has mass at least $Q/(2m)>M/3$. The singleton positive-fiber case has depth $M/2$.

## Proof

Zero-width fibers make no contribution, so use the hull of the positive integer fibers. They are consecutive by convexity, and this replacement leaves all positive measured fibers and the centroid unchanged. With just one positive fiber the midpoint of that interval has depth $M/2$. Otherwise the hull is full dimensional and the mixed centroid is interior: a nonnegative supporting affine function vanishing there would integrate to zero, hence vanish on every positive interval and at least two distinct bases, making it identically zero.

A containing halfspace can be translated inward to pass through the centroid. In one continuous orientation the nonvertical through-centroid cap has form $x+ai\ge0$ for finite $a$, with width $w_i$ and upper endpoint $r_i$ at base $i$. Its length is the continuous piecewise affine function $\sum_i \min\{w_i,\max(0,r_i+ai)\}$. The anchor $i=0$ is strictly proper since the centroid is interior. For sufficiently large positive or negative $a$ the mass is constant, equal to $r_0+\sum_{i>0}w_i$ or $r_0+\sum_{i<0}w_i$, respectively. These plateaus cost strictly less than the corresponding closed vertical cap, because the latter captures $w_0>r_0$. Applying the same reasoning after reflection gives finite attainment in either orientation.

The line $x=-ai$ through the interior point meets the hull in one segment, whose points strictly between its ends are interior. Thus the properly cut indices form one block $[-p,q]\cap\mathbb Z$ containing zero, and any endpoint coincidences on either side can occur only at the adjacent indices $-A$ and $B$, with $A=p+1$, $B=q+1$. Past a section endpoint the cap status is homogeneous on that side: a switch between upper and lower exclusion of the line would force an additional intersection by convexity. Denote an upper endpoint coincidence (zero capture) by U and a lower endpoint coincidence (full capture) by L. Put $\Delta=B-A$ and $S=\sum_{i=-p}^q i=\Delta(A+B-1)/2$. Every proper cut has slope $i$, so the left and right derivatives in $a$ at a cusp are as follows, with a dash denoting no coincidence:

| Left | Right | Left derivative | Right derivative | Allowed $\Delta$ at a local minimum |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| – | – | $S$ | $S$ | $0$ |
| U | – | $S-A$ | $S$ | $0,1$ |
| L | – | $S$ | $S-A$ | none |
| – | U | $S$ | $S+B$ | $0,-1$ |
| – | L | $S+B$ | $S$ | none |
| U | U | $S-A$ | $S+B$ | $-1,0,1$ |
| U | L | $S-A+B$ | $S$ | $0$ |
| L | U | $S$ | $S-A+B$ | $0$ |
| L | L | $S+B$ | $S-A$ | none |

These derivatives follow from the clipped-affine formula: U enters the block when its margin increases; L enters when its margin decreases. The local-minimum condition is left derivative $\le0\le$ right derivative. For $\Delta\ge2$, $S>A$, and for $\Delta\le-2$, $S<-B$. At $\Delta=1$ one has $S=A$ and at $\Delta=-1$ one has $S=-B$. These facts give the table, using that $S$ and $\Delta$ have the same sign away from zero.

Except for U/U at $\Delta=0$, each allowed coincidence case has a flat side that gives a symmetric block after a sufficiently small tilt. For U on the left alone with $\Delta=0$, increase $a$ to leave that index excluded; with $\Delta=1$, decrease $a$ to admit it and obtain the symmetric block. Reflect for U on the right alone. For U/U with $\Delta=1$ decrease $a$ to admit only the left index, and for $\Delta=-1$ increase $a$ to admit only the right. In a mixed U/L case both derivatives are zero; choose the direction keeping U excluded and L captured. With no coincidences the block is already symmetric. Since there are finitely many breakpoints, no other index changes status in these small moves; any already captured fiber remains captured. The U/U case with $\Delta=0$ has both line-section ends at upper endpoints and therefore both outer tails excluded, so it already belongs to the no-captured alternative. This proves the angular statement.

For the quantitative assertion, translate inward through the centroid and shear or reflect so a nonvertical tested cap is $x\ge0$. It still captures no full positive fiber if the original cap did not. A vertical cap through the centroid would capture the entire central positive fiber, so it need not be considered in this branch. The continuous centroid equation gives $\sum_iw_ir_i=Q/2$. The cap length is $F=\sum_i(r_i)_+$, and $mF\ge\sum_iw_i(r_i)_+\ge Q/2$.

For completeness, the strict width estimate follows from the following one-sided quadratic bound. For a concave real sequence $x_0,\ldots,x_s$ with $x_0=1$, the minimum of $\sum_i(x_i^2-2x_i/3)$ is $(s+1)/(3(2s+1))$ at $v_i=1-2i/(2s+1)$. For $s\ge1$, write $e=x-v$ and
$$
e_i=(i/s)e_s+\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}(2e_t-e_{t-1}-e_{t+1})G_t(i),\qquad
G_t(i)=\min(i,t)-it/s .
$$
This identity follows by matching endpoint values and second differences. For $b_i=v_i-1/3$, the direct sums are $\sum_i b_i i=0$ and $\sum_i b_iG_t(i)=t(s-t)(s+1-t)/(3(2s+1))\ge0$. The concavity coefficients in the representation are nonnegative, so the difference of quadratic objectives at $x$ and $v$ is $\sum_i e_i^2+2\sum_i b_ie_i\ge0$. Summing the quadratic at $v$ gives the minimum above, including $s=0$ directly. Apply this bound to both sequences of widths divided by $m$, running in either direction from a maximum at index $j$, and subtract the duplicated peak contribution $1/3$. If $l,r$ are the extreme positive indices, this gives
$$
Q-\frac23mM\ge \frac{m^2}{6}\left(\frac1{2(j-l)+1}+\frac1{2(r-j)+1}\right)>0.
$$
Combining with $F\ge Q/(2m)$ proves the strict no-full-fiber bound.
