## Statement

Let \(s\ge1\) be an integer, let \(Q\subset\mathbb R^s\) be an origin-symmetric compact convex body, and let \(F:\mathbb R^s\to[0,\infty)\) be finite, even, and convex, with \(F(0)=0\). Put \(D=Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\), and for \(L\ge0\) define

\[
Z_F(0;L)=\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\F(z)\le L}}e^{-F(z)},
\qquad
M=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{Z_F(0;L)}{1+L}.
\]

The maximum exists. For every \(v\in\mathbb R^s\) and every \(c\in\{0,1\}^s\),

\[
\sum_{y\in Q\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^s)}e^{-F(y-v)}\le22M.
\tag{1}
\]

No assumption is made on the affine rank of the occupied parity class or of its shifted cost sublevels. Consequently,

\[
\sup_{v\in\mathbb R^s}\sum_{y\in D}e^{-F(y-v)}
\le22\,2^s\max_{L\ge0}
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\F(z)\le L}}e^{-F(z)}}{1+L}.
\tag{2}
\]

## Proof

All constructions below are derived here. The sole external mathematical input is the finite-sumset dimension inequality specified in EXTERNAL SOURCES.

The finite set \(D\) contains the origin. The numerator defining \(M\) changes only at the finitely many costs \(F(z)\), includes equality at each such cost, and is constant after the largest cost. Its denominator increases between consecutive distinct costs. Therefore the maximum is attained at one of these costs, and \(M\ge1\).

Write \(N_0(u)=|\{z\in D:F(z)\le u\}|\) for \(u\ge0\). Since every weight in this cutoff is at least \(e^{-u}\),

\[
N_0(u)\le e^u Z_F(0;u)\le e^u(1+u)M.
\tag{3}
\]

Also, for every \(T\ge0\), termwise integration of the finite sum gives

\[
\int_0^T e^{-t}N_0(t)\,dt
=Z_F(0;T)-e^{-T}N_0(T)
\le Z_F(0;T)\le(1+T)M.
\tag{4}
\]

When \(F(z)=T\), its two contributions on the right of the equality cancel; thus (4) is valid with the stated closed cutoffs.

Fix \(v\) and \(c\). If \(A_c=D\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^s)\) is empty, (1) is immediate. Otherwise choose \(a_c\in A_c\) minimizing the shifted cost, and put \(m_c=F(a_c-v)\). For \(t\ge m_c\), define

\[
A_c(t)=\{y\in A_c:F(y-v)\le t\},
\qquad
N_c(t)=|A_c(t)|,
\qquad
r_c(t)=\dim\operatorname{aff} A_c(t).
\]

These sets are nonempty for \(t\ge m_c\); their rank is the rank of the actual occupied shifted sublevel in the chosen parity class. Extend \(N_c(t)=0\) to \(0\le t<m_c\). The layer identity gives

\[
\sum_{y\in A_c}e^{-F(y-v)}
=\int_{m_c}^{\infty}e^{-t}N_c(t)\,dt.
\tag{5}
\]

We first prove three counting bounds. For every \(t\ge m_c\), they are

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_c(t)&\le N_0\!\left(\frac{t+m_c}{2}\right),\\
N_c(t)&\le\frac{2}{r_c(t)+2}\,N_0(t),\\
N_c(t)&\le2^{r_c(t)}N_0(t/2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

For the first bound, send \(y\in A_c(t)\) to \(z=(y-a_c)/2\). This map is injective and integral because \(y\) and \(a_c\) have the same parity. Symmetry and convexity give \(z\in Q\). Evenness and convexity give

\[
F(z)=F\!\left(\frac{(y-v)-(a_c-v)}2\right)
\le\frac{F(y-v)+F(a_c-v)}2
\le\frac{t+m_c}{2}.
\]

For the remaining two bounds, fix this \(t\), choose any \(a\in A_c(t)\), and set \(B=(A_c(t)-a)/2\subset\mathbb Z^s\). Thus \(B\) contains zero, has cardinality \(N_c(t)\), and spans a real vector space of dimension \(r_c(t)\). Its difference set is \(B-B=(A_c(t)-A_c(t))/2\). Every element of this difference set belongs to \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) and has central cost at most \(t\), by the same midpoint calculation. Hence \(N_0(t)\ge|B-B|\).

Suppose first that \(r_c(t)\ge1\). Identify the real span of \(B\) linearly with \(\mathbb R^{r_c(t)}\), and apply the finite-sumset dimension inequality to the images of \(B\) and \(-B\). They have equal cardinalities, and each has full affine dimension \(r_c(t)\), so the dimension hypothesis holds. We obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0(t)&\ge |B-B|\\
&\ge (r_c(t)+1)N_c(t)
       -\frac{r_c(t)(r_c(t)+1)}2\\
&\ge\frac{r_c(t)+2}{2}\,N_c(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The last step uses \(N_c(t)\ge r_c(t)+1\), which follows from the affine dimension. When \(r_c(t)=0\), the set \(A_c(t)\) is a singleton, so \(N_c(t)=1\le N_0(t)\); (7) holds in that case as well. Rearranging proves the second bound in (6).

For the third bound, let \(\Gamma\) be the additive subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^s\) generated by \(B\). It is free abelian of rank \(r_c(t)\), so \(\Gamma/2\Gamma\) has \(2^{r_c(t)}\) elements. This elementary lattice assertion can be seen by induction on the ambient number of coordinates: projection of a subgroup to the first coordinate is either zero, or \(h\mathbb Z\) for some \(h>0\); in the latter case a group element with first coordinate \(h\), together with a basis for the kernel of that projection, gives a direct-sum basis. The number of resulting basis vectors is the dimension of the real span.

Partition \(B\) according to its residue classes modulo \(2\Gamma\). In each nonempty class choose \(b_0\) and send \(b\) in that class to \(z=(b-b_0)/2\). The image lies in \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb Z^s\), and the map is injective on the class. With \(y=a+2b\) and \(y_0=a+2b_0\), both in \(A_c(t)\), it is also \(z=(y-y_0)/4\). Symmetry and convexity of \(Q\), including \(0\in Q\), give \(z\in Q\). Applying convexity to the three terms with coefficients \(1/4,1/4,1/2\), and using evenness, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(z)
&=F\!\left(\tfrac14(y-v)+\tfrac14(v-y_0)+\tfrac12\,0\right)\\
&\le\tfrac14 F(y-v)+\tfrac14F(y_0-v)+\tfrac12F(0)
\le t/2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each residue class therefore has at most \(N_0(t/2)\) members. There are at most \(2^{r_c(t)}\) classes, which proves the third bound in (6). This argument also applies when the rank is zero.

We now bound (5) by splitting its levels into the interval \([m_c,2m_c]\) and the tail \([2m_c,\infty)\). The shared endpoint has measure zero in the layer integral. All counting functions themselves continue to include equality.

On the levels in the first interval with \(r_c(t)\ge m_c/3\), the second bound in (6) gives \(N_c(t)\le6N_0(t)/(m_c+6)\). By (4), their total contribution is at most

\[
\frac{6}{m_c+6}\int_0^{2m_c}e^{-t}N_0(t)\,dt
\le\frac{6(1+2m_c)}{m_c+6}M
\le12M.
\tag{8}
\]

On the remaining levels in \([m_c,2m_c]\), the inequality \(r_c(t)<m_c/3\) and \(\log2<3/4\) imply \(2^{r_c(t)}\le e^{m_c/4}\). Using the third bound in (6) and then (3), their contribution is at most

\[
\begin{aligned}
e^{m_c/4}\int_{m_c}^{\infty}e^{-t}N_0(t/2)\,dt
&\le e^{m_c/4}M
  \int_{m_c}^{\infty}(1+t/2)e^{-t/2}\,dt\\
&=(m_c+4)e^{-m_c/4}M
\le4M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

The integration domain was enlarged only after replacing the integrand by a nonnegative upper bound valid at every selected low-rank level. For the final inequality, use \(e^{m_c/4}\ge1+m_c/4\). The elementary numerical inequality \(\log2<3/4\) follows, for example, from \(e^{3/4}\ge1+3/4+(3/4)^2/2=65/32>2\).

Finally the first bound in (6), followed by (3), bounds the tail by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{2m_c}^{\infty}e^{-t}N_c(t)\,dt
&\le M\int_{2m_c}^{\infty}
 \left(1+\frac{t+m_c}{2}\right)e^{-(t-m_c)/2}\,dt\\
&=(3m_c+4)e^{-m_c/2}M
\le6M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Here \(e^{m_c/2}\ge1+m_c/2\) and \(3m_c+4\le6(1+m_c/2)\). The estimates are valid for \(m_c=0\) as well: the first interval then has zero length, and (10) bounds the whole integral.

Combining (5) and (8)--(10) yields \((12+4+6)M=22M\), proving (1). The argument retained the complete finite supports and every cost boundary throughout, and used only the rank of \(A_c(t)\). Summing (1) over the \(2^s\) parity classes, which partition \(D\), proves (2) for each real shift. The right side is independent of the shift, so it also bounds the supremum.

## External sources

Imre Z. Ruzsa, [Sum of sets in several dimensions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01302969), Combinatorica 14 (1994), 485--490, DOI 10.1007/BF01302969. The bound is stated in the publisher's abstract: if nonempty finite sets \(U,V\subset\mathbb R^r\) satisfy \(|U|\le|V|\) and no affine hyperplane contains both a translate of \(U\) and a translate of \(V\), then \(|U+V|\ge |V|+r|U|-r(r+1)/2\). In (7), \(U\) and \(V\) are the linear images of \(B\) and \(-B\) in their actual \(r_c(t)\)-dimensional span. Both have full affine dimension and cardinality \(N_c(t)\), so all hypotheses hold and the resulting sumset has cardinality \(|B-B|\). The rank-zero case is proved separately. No asymptotic or sufficiently-large-cardinality version of a difference-set theorem is used.
