## Statement

At one actual deepest mark take a finite menu of nonvertical depth-attaining closed caps through the mark, regular in the full affine parameters, with continuous normals $u_j$ and positive total coareas $S_j$. Let $b_j=S_ju_j$ and let $\eta$ be the sum of the actual fiber surface measures marked by their continuous outward unit normals. The possible normalized response weights of balanced sublists are
$$
\mathcal T=\{\theta\ge0:\textstyle\sum_j\theta_j=1,\ \sum_j\theta_j b_j=0\}.
$$
If this set is nonempty, all its extreme points have support of size at most $d+1$. For a normalized nonnegative continuous potential $\Phi$, convex in the physical variables for each fixed normal and with $\int\Phi\,d\eta=1$, put $A_j=\int\Phi\mathbf1_{H_j}\,d\eta$. Then
$$
\sup_\Phi\max_{\theta\in\mathcal T}\sum_j\theta_j A_j
=\max_{\theta\in\operatorname{ext}\mathcal T}\sup_\Phi\sum_j\theta_j A_j,
\qquad
\max_{\theta\in\mathcal T}\sum_j\theta_j A_j
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R^d}\max_j(A_j-v\cdot b_j).
$$

## Proof

Geometric weights $\rho_j$ balance $u_j$ and give response weights proportional to $\rho_j/S_j$. Conversely choose $\rho_j=\theta_jS_j$ and discard zero rows. The resulting geometric balance makes the weighted margin identically zero on the mark's continuous plane, so the positive rows cover that plane. Positive rescaling of a defining margin multiplies $u_j$ by the scale and divides $S_j$ by it, so $b_j$ is unchanged.

The feasible set is a closed subset of the finite probability simplex. At an extreme point the columns $(1,b_j)$ of its positive support are linearly independent, since otherwise a nonzero dependence supported there allows small perturbations with either sign. Thus its support has size at most their rank, which is at most $d+1$. Linear maximization on the polytope occurs at an extreme point. There are finitely many extreme points, and taking a supremum over $\Phi$ commutes with a finite maximum. For fixed $\Phi$, the equality-constrained finite linear program in $\theta$ has dual minimizing $t$ subject to $t+v\cdot b_j\ge A_j$ for every $j$. Its nonempty compact primal has finite value, so finite linear-program duality gives equality. Eliminate $t$ to obtain the displayed formula. This argument does not require a full-rank constraint matrix or attainment of the potential supremum.
