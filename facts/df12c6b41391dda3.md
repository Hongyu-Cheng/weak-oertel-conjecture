## Statement

Let $C=\{(z,x):-1/4\le z\le9/4,\ |x|\le(1+2z)/2\}$ and sample $C_{s,t}=\{(sz+t,x):(z,x)\in C\}$ on $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R$, with $s>0$. Write $\beta(s,t)$ for its global maximum normalized mixed length depth over all closed ambient halfspaces. In the chamber $-1<t-s/4<0$, $2<t+9s/4<3$, its minimum is $5/18$ precisely on $t=(s-1)/2$, $10/11<s<14/11$. At $(1,0)$ this minimum is strict along either coordinate axis separately. The loss contact at $(10/11,-1/22)$ still has value $5/18$ with base $2$ retained, and the two-sample-side limit is $3/8$. The entrance point $(14/11,3/22)$ has four positive fibers and value $13/32$. A continuous parameter path starting in the open ridge and staying at depth at most $5/18$ cannot change its number of positive sampled fibers.

## Proof

In the chamber the integer sites are $0,1,2$, with centered lengths $(q,q+2,q+4)/s$, where $q=s-2t>0$. In the exact three-interval formula take endpoint lengths $a=q$, $b=q+4$, middle length $U=q+2$ and shift zero, ignoring the common length factor $1/s$ in the ratio. Its compatible widths satisfy $U=(a+b)/2$, and its midpoint raw maximum is $U/2+a$. Thus
$$
\beta(s,t)=\frac{\max(q+4,3q+2)}{6(q+2)}.
$$
The branch derivatives in $q$ are $-1/[3(q+2)^2]$ for $q<1$ and $2/[3(q+2)^2]$ for $q>1$. Substituting $q=1$ into the chamber inequalities gives the stated interval in $s$ and the unique value of $t$ at each $s$. Each coordinate line through $(1,0)$ has a unique point with $q=1$.

At loss contact the upper projection endpoint is exactly $2$, hence the closed body retains the positive last interval, and the three-fiber formula still applies. Near this point on the two-sample side, both sites are exposed. Their best depths equal half their lengths, using the shorter side of the own interval cut and a finite tilt isolating it. The limiting two lengths are proportional to $(1,3)$, giving $3/8$.

At entrance the upper projection endpoint is $3$, so four centered lengths proportional to $(1,3,5,7)$ are present. The outer sites have raw maxima $1/2$ and $7/2$ in the rescaled units. At base $1$, retaining the first three fibers gives raw depth at least $5/2$ at the midpoint by the exact three-interval formula, and the lower cap $x\le -(5/2)(z-1)$ and its height reflection attain that mass and cover the entire base-$1$ line. At base $2$ use the centered lower cap $x\le v(z-2)$. For $v\ge0$ the contributions from bases $1$ and $3$ sum to at least $5$, directly from clipping $3/2-v$ and $7/2+v$ to their lengths. For $-1/4\le v\le0$ their sum is $5$. For $v\le-1/4$ the base-$0$ interval is full and the paired endpoint contributions are at least $3$: their unclipped lengths sum to $5$, so if a term saturates at its full length the sum is at least $3$, and otherwise clipping at zero can only increase the sum. In each case the own half length $5/2$ gives at least $13/2$ in total. At $v=-7/2$ the cap attains $13/2$. Its reflection gives the reverse orientation at the midpoint and covers the rest of base $2$ together with the first cap. Pure base caps through the midpoint contain the whole own fiber and have at least the corresponding steep oblique mass. Thus the actual maximum is $(13/2)/16=13/32$.

While a path is inside the chamber its prescribed upper bound forces $q=1$. Its first possible chamber exits are the two contacts just computed. Entrance violates the bound at the contact. In a neighborhood of loss contact the lower projection endpoint stays strictly between $-1$ and $0$, and the upper projection endpoint is close to $2$. In that neighborhood a path staying at the bound has the same three samples and still has $q=1$ by the three-fiber formula, because the two-sample side exceeds the bound by continuity of its explicit ratio. Hence the preimage of the ridge including loss contact is open and closed relative to any such path's parameter domain, since the omitted entrance contact is forbidden, so it cannot change sample count.
