## Statement

Retain the complete canonical instance and notation of accepted fact 48031b9fbd29cb13. In particular,

\[
M=\sum_{t\in T}b_t>\frac{381}{1600}Q,
\qquad
q_t+\frac65K\subseteq\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t),
\qquad
0<b_t\leq\frac{a_t}{4},
\tag{1}
\]

and the actual proper closed halfspace through \(p=(m,a(m))\) is

\[
H=\left\{(z,y):
\langle u,z-m\rangle+\langle v,y-a(m)\rangle\geq0
\right\},
\qquad
v\ne0,
\tag{2}
\]

with

\[
\mu_C(H)<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
\qquad
\gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{3}
\]

For every \(t\in T\), including targets outside the particular \(J_H\) of that fact, put

\[
\tau_t:=\langle v,a(m)\rangle-\langle u,t-m\rangle,
\qquad
H_t:=\{y:\langle v,y\rangle\geq\tau_t\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Define the global hit and miss families

\[
\mathcal I_H^\circ
:=\{t\in T:H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\ne\varnothing\},
\qquad
\mathcal R_H^\circ:=T\setminus\mathcal I_H^\circ,
\tag{5}
\]

and their masses

\[
I_H^\circ:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H^\circ}b_t,
\qquad
R_H^\circ:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ}b_t.
\tag{6}
\]

Then

\[
I_H^\circ<\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d2^n},
\qquad
R_H^\circ>
\left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac{1}{100\gamma_d2^n}\right)Q
>\frac{359}{1600}Q.
\tag{7}
\]

Moreover, every closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing the original \(m\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\cap J}b_t
\geq2^{-n}M-I_H^\circ.
\tag{8}
\]

The global miss measure has its own exact lattice centerpoint: there is

\[
m^\circ\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}\mathcal R_H^\circ
\tag{9}
\]

such that every closed base halfspace \(J\ni m^\circ\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\cap J}b_t
\geq2^{-n}R_H^\circ,
\tag{10}
\]

and \(C_{m^\circ}\ne\varnothing\).

Every \(t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\) has the strict common-normal separation and actual-cap bound

\[
h_{\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)}(v)<\tau_t,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)<b_t.
\tag{11}
\]

The global small-cap family

\[
\mathcal S_H^\circ
:=\left\{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ:
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)<\frac{b_t}{10}
\right\}
\tag{12}
\]

satisfies

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathcal S_H^\circ}b_t
>R_H^\circ-\frac{Q}{10\,2^n}
>\frac{279}{1600}Q.
\tag{13}
\]

Here is the quotient-affine terminal screen. Let \(E\subseteq\mathcal R_H^\circ\) be nonempty, put

\[
B_E:=\sum_{t\in E}b_t,
\qquad
\rho_E:=\dim\operatorname{aff}E,
\tag{14}
\]

and suppose that there are an affine map

\[
s:\operatorname{aff}E\longrightarrow\mathbb R^d
\tag{15}
\]

and simultaneous choices

\[
x_t\in q_t+\frac65K
\qquad(t\in E)
\tag{16}
\]

such that

\[
x_t-s(t)\in\operatorname{span}\{v\}
\qquad(t\in E).
\tag{17}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq
\frac{\gamma_d B_E}{2^{\rho_E+1}}.}
\tag{18}
\]

Consequently, in the canonical hard instance,

\[
\boxed{
B_E<\frac14R_H^\circ}
\tag{19}
\]

for every quotient-affine selectable family (14)-(17). In particular, a future proof that some such \(E\) has \(B_E\geq R_H^\circ/4\) would complete the dominant branch without any further cap estimate. No such family is asserted here.

The hypothesis (17) is not a consequence of one common normal, affine thresholds, common tubes, or arbitrarily small common-normal caps alone. Precisely, let

\[
\widetilde C:=
\left\{(z,x,y)\in\mathbb R^3:
-1\leq z\leq1,
-1\leq x\leq0,
2z^2-2\leq y\leq2
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]

For \(t\in\widetilde T:=\{-1,0,1\}\), put

\[
w_t:=4-2t^2,
\qquad
\widetilde q_t:=\left(-\frac12,t^2\right),
\qquad
\widetilde b_t:=\frac14,
\qquad
\widetilde K:=\left[-\frac1{12},\frac1{12}\right]^2.
\tag{21}
\]

Then \(\widetilde C\) is a full-dimensional convex body,
\(\operatorname{vol}_2(\widetilde C_t)=w_t\), and

\[
0<\widetilde b_t\leq\frac14\operatorname{vol}_2(\widetilde C_t),
\qquad
\widetilde q_t+\frac65\widetilde K
\subseteq\mathsf M_{\widetilde b_t}(\widetilde C_t).
\tag{22}
\]

The point \(0\) is a \(2^{-1}\)-centerpoint of the equal base weights
\(\widetilde b_t\). With

\[
\varepsilon:=\frac1{200},
\qquad
\widetilde H:=\{(z,x,y):x\geq-\varepsilon\},
\qquad
\widetilde v:=(1,0),
\tag{23}
\]

the boundary of \(\widetilde H\) contains
\((0,-\varepsilon,0)\in\widetilde C\), and for every
\(t\in\widetilde T\),

\[
\widetilde H_t\cap
\mathsf M_{\widetilde b_t}(\widetilde C_t)=\varnothing,
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_2(\widetilde C_t\cap\widetilde H_t)
<\frac{\widetilde b_t}{10}.
\tag{24}
\]

Nevertheless there are no affine \(s:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2\) and choices
\(\widetilde x_t\in\widetilde q_t+(6/5)\widetilde K\) for which

\[
\widetilde x_t-s(t)\in\operatorname{span}\{\widetilde v\}
\qquad(t=-1,0,1).
\tag{25}
\]

More sharply, after quotienting by \(\operatorname{span}\{\widetilde v\}\), the least uniform distance of the three projected tube intervals from an affine function is exactly \(2/5\). Truncating any fiber by a halfspace parallel to \(\widetilde H\), taking the centroid of either nonempty truncated part, or reflecting across any hyperplane parallel to \(\partial\widetilde H\) preserves the projected values \(t^2\). Thus affine minimax, common-normal truncation, and reflection do not establish (17) without an additional canonical capacity argument. This calibration is not asserted to satisfy the canonical hard branch and is not a counterexample to (18), (19), or the broader common-normal absorption statement.

## Proof

Fix the actual halfspace (2). If \(t\in\mathcal I_H^\circ\), choose

\[
r_t\in H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t).
\]

Because \(v\ne0\), \(H_t\) is a proper closed halfspace in
\(\mathbb R^d\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives

\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)\geq\gamma_db_t.
\tag{26}
\]

The targets are distinct integer fibers of the same actual ambient halfspace, so summing (26), once per fiber, and using (3) yields

\[
\gamma_d I_H^\circ
\leq\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H^\circ}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)
\leq\mu_C(H)
<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n}.
\tag{27}
\]

This proves the hit bound in (7). Subtracting it from (1) gives the first strict lower bound for \(R_H^\circ\). Accepted fact 48031b9fbd29cb13 proves the uniform numerical estimate \(\gamma_d>4/11\). Since \(n\geq1\),

\[
\frac{1}{100\gamma_d2^n}<\frac{11}{800}
=\frac{22}{1600}.
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (1), (7), and (28) give

\[
R_H^\circ>\left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac{22}{1600}\right)Q
=\frac{359}{1600}Q,
\]

which completes (7).

For any closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\), the original weighted lattice-centerpoint property in accepted fact 48031b9fbd29cb13 gives

\[
\sum_{t\in T\cap J}b_t\geq2^{-n}M.
\]

Deleting all global hits can remove at most \(I_H^\circ\), independently of \(J\). This proves (8).

The positive bound in (7) makes the measure

\[
\beta^\circ:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ}b_t\delta_t
\]

nonzero. Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c with continuous dimension zero. It supplies \(m^\circ\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfying (10). If \(m^\circ\notin\operatorname{conv}\mathcal R_H^\circ\), strict separation from that finite convex hull would give a closed base halfspace containing \(m^\circ\) with zero \(\beta^\circ\)-mass, contradicting (10). This proves (9).

By (1) and \(0\in K\), every \(q_t\) belongs to
\(\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives
\(q_t\in C_t\). Choose a convex representation of \(m^\circ\) by points of
\(\mathcal R_H^\circ\). Convexity of \(C\) applied to the corresponding points
\((t,q_t)\) gives a point of \(C_{m^\circ}\), proving
\(C_{m^\circ}\ne\varnothing\).

If \(t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\), compactness of
\(\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\), its disjointness from the closed upper halfspace
\(H_t\), and \(v\ne0\) give

\[
h_{\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)}(v)<\tau_t.
\]

If instead \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)\geq b_t\), the density

\[
\frac{b_t}{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)}
\mathbf1_{C_t\cap H_t}
\]

would have mass \(b_t\), be bounded by one, and have its normalized barycenter in the convex halfspace \(H_t\). This would put a point in
\(H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\), a contradiction. This proves (11).

For every \(t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\setminus\mathcal S_H^\circ\),

\[
b_t\leq10\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t).
\]

Summing only over these distinct fibers of the actual \(H\) gives

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H^\circ\setminus\mathcal S_H^\circ}b_t
\leq10\mu_C(H)<\frac{Q}{10\,2^n}.
\tag{29}
\]

Subtracting (29) from \(R_H^\circ\) proves the first inequality in (13). Equations (7), \(2^n\geq2\), and
\(Q/(10\,2^n)\leq Q/20=80Q/1600\) prove the second:

\[
\sum_{t\in\mathcal S_H^\circ}b_t
>\left(\frac{359}{1600}-\frac{80}{1600}\right)Q
=\frac{279}{1600}Q.
\]

We prove the quotient-affine screen. Under (15)-(17), each selected point
\(x_t\) belongs to \(\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\) by (1). Accepted fact
6769574fc1bd9f8d therefore gives its absolute fiber depth

\[
\delta_C(t,x_t)\geq\gamma_db_t.
\tag{30}
\]

The residual vectors \(x_t-s(t)\) span a space of dimension at most one by (17). Apply accepted fact ae8bb1ec375ace78 to the points \(x_t\), the weights \(\gamma_db_t\), their intrinsic base affine hull of dimension
\(\rho_E\), and the affine map \(s\). Its residual-selector bound gives

\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_dB_E}{2^{\rho_E}(1+1)},
\]

which is (18). This use creates one atomic selector measure with one atom in each selected physical fiber; it neither treats a metronoid tube as a physical density nor adds separately dominated ambient measures.

Suppose \(B_E\geq R_H^\circ/4\). Since \(\rho_E\leq n\), (18), (7), and \(\gamma_d>4/11\) imply

\[
2^nh_{\max}(C)
\geq\frac{\gamma_dR_H^\circ}{8}
>\frac{4}{11}\frac{359}{1600}\frac18Q
=\frac{359}{35200}Q
>\frac{Q}{100},
\tag{31}
\]

where the last comparison is \(359>352\). This contradicts the hard ledger in accepted fact 48031b9fbd29cb13. Hence (19) holds.

It remains to verify the calibration. The set (20) is compact and has nonempty interior. It is convex because it is the intersection of the slabs
\(-1\leq z\leq1\), \(-1\leq x\leq0\), \(y\leq2\), and the epigraph
\(y\geq2z^2-2\) of a convex function. Its fiber at \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}\) is the rectangle

\[
\widetilde C_t=[-1,0]\times[2t^2-2,2],
\]

whose second side has length \(w_t=4-2t^2\in\{2,4\}\), whose area is \(w_t\), and whose centroid is \(\widetilde q_t=(-1/2,t^2)\). This proves the geometric assertions preceding (22) and the quarter-volume bound there.

We prove the tube inclusion in (22) by explicit simultaneous admissible densities. Fix

\[
h=(h_1,h_2)\in\left[-\frac1{10},\frac1{10}\right]^2
=\frac65\widetilde K.
\]

On \(\widetilde C_t\), use centered coordinates

\[
r:=x+\frac12\in\left[-\frac12,\frac12\right],
\qquad
\sigma:=y-t^2\in\left[-\frac{w_t}{2},\frac{w_t}{2}\right]
\]

and define

\[
f_{t,h}(r,\sigma)
:=\frac{\widetilde b_t}{w_t}
+\frac{12\widetilde b_t h_1}{w_t}r
+\frac{12\widetilde b_t h_2}{w_t^3}\sigma.
\tag{32}
\]

When \(w_t=2\), the absolute value of the two perturbation terms is at most

\[
\frac34|h_1|+\frac38|h_2|
\leq\frac9{80}<\frac18
=\frac{\widetilde b_t}{w_t}.
\]

When \(w_t=4\), it is at most

\[
\frac38|h_1|+\frac3{32}|h_2|
\leq\frac3{64}<\frac1{16}
=\frac{\widetilde b_t}{w_t}.
\]

Thus \(0<f_{t,h}<2\widetilde b_t/w_t\leq1/4<1\). Symmetry of the centered rectangle gives

\[
\int_{\widetilde C_t}f_{t,h}=\widetilde b_t.
\]

Using

\[
\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}r^2\,dr=\frac1{12},
\qquad
\int_{-w_t/2}^{w_t/2}\sigma^2\,d\sigma=\frac{w_t^3}{12},
\]

a direct first-moment calculation gives the normalized barycenter

\[
\frac1{\widetilde b_t}
\int_{\widetilde C_t}(x,y)f_{t,h}(x,y)\,dx\,dy
=\widetilde q_t+h.
\]

Therefore every point of
\(\widetilde q_t+(6/5)\widetilde K\) belongs to
\(\mathsf M_{\widetilde b_t}(\widetilde C_t)\), proving (22).

For the normal \(\widetilde v=(1,0)\), the top cap of the rectangle
\(\widetilde C_t\) having area \(\widetilde b_t\) is

\[
\left[-\frac{\widetilde b_t}{w_t},0\right]
\times[2t^2-2,2].
\]

The elementary bathtub comparison, equivalently the support-function computation in accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, gives

\[
h_{\mathsf M_{\widetilde b_t}(\widetilde C_t)}(\widetilde v)
=-\frac{\widetilde b_t}{2w_t}
\leq-\frac1{32}
<-\frac1{200}.
\tag{33}
\]

Hence every metronoid is disjoint from
\(\widetilde H_t=\{x\geq-1/200\}\). Also

\[
\operatorname{vol}_2(\widetilde C_t\cap\widetilde H_t)
=\frac{w_t}{200}
\leq\frac1{50}
<\frac1{40}
=\frac{\widetilde b_t}{10}.
\]

This proves (24). Any closed halfline in the base containing zero contains at least two of the three equal atoms, hence mass \(2\widetilde b_t\geq(3\widetilde b_t)/2\); this verifies the stated lattice-centerpoint property.

Quotienting by \(\operatorname{span}\{\widetilde v\}\) retains only the \(y\)-coordinate. The three projected tube intervals are

\[
[t^2-1/10,t^2+1/10]
\qquad(t=-1,0,1).
\tag{34}
\]

If an affine function \(g(t)=\alpha t+\beta\) met all three intervals, then

\[
\beta=g(0)\leq\frac1{10},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{g(-1)+g(1)}2\geq\frac9{10},
\]

a contradiction. This proves (25). More generally, if the maximum distance of \(g(t)\) from the intervals (34) is \(\eta\), then

\[
|g(t)-t^2|\leq\eta+\frac1{10}
\qquad(t=-1,0,1).
\]

The second difference of an affine function vanishes, so

\[
2
\leq|g(-1)-1|+|g(1)-1|+2|g(0)|
\leq4\left(\eta+\frac1{10}\right).
\]

Thus \(\eta\geq2/5\), while \(g\equiv1/2\) attains equality. Finally, every fiber rectangle is a Cartesian product in the \(x\) and \(y\) coordinates. A nonempty truncation by \(x\geq c\) or \(x\leq c\) changes only its \(x\)-interval, so the truncated centroid retains \(y\)-coordinate \(t^2\). Reflection across \(x=c\) also leaves the \(y\)-coordinate unchanged. The same nonaffine second difference therefore survives all the claimed common-normal truncations and reflections. This completes the calibration and the proof.
