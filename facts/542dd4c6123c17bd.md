## Statement

Use the actual-fiber normalization of accepted fact `5bc145747671c668`. Subscripts distinguish the present family; for \(n=1\), the objects \(N_1,V_1,\rho_1,W_1,\widetilde W_1,m_1\) below are respectively that fact's \(N,v,\rho,W,\widetilde W,m\). Thus \(V_n\) is a continuous smoothed marginal, and every original mixed-integer volume is computed from \(N_n\) or the original fibers.

For every integer \(n\ge1\), put \(r=29/10\), \(\delta_n=(r/3)^n=(29/30)^n\), and \(R_n=1+\delta_n\). With continuous dimension \(d=1\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_n&=\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
       0\le y\le R_n,\ |x_i|\le R_n-y\ (1\le i\le n)\},\\
S_n&=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R),\qquad
\widetilde C_n=C_n+([-1/2,1/2]^n\times\{0\}).
\end{aligned}
\]

\(C_n\) and \(\widetilde C_n\) are full-dimensional convex bodies. For every \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\), the complete original fiber \((C_n)_{\mathbf z}=\{y:(\mathbf z,y)\in C_n\}\) and its length \(w_{n,\mathbf z}\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\mathbf z &(C_n)_{\mathbf z}&w_{n,\mathbf z}\\ \hline
\mathbf0_n &[0,R_n]&R_n\\
\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n\setminus\{\mathbf0_n\}&[0,\delta_n]&\delta_n\\
\mathbf z\notin\{-1,0,1\}^n&\varnothing&0.
\end{array}
\]

Let \(N_n(y)\) be the number of lattice points in the horizontal section of \(C_n\), and let \(V_n(y)\) be the ordinary \(n\)-dimensional volume of the horizontal section of \(\widetilde C_n\). Put \(A_n=2R_n+1\). Their exact formulas, including all section boundaries, are

\[
N_n(y)=
\begin{cases}
3^n,&0\le y\le\delta_n,\\
1,&\delta_n<y\le R_n,\\
0,&y\notin[0,R_n],
\end{cases}
\qquad
V_n(y)=
\begin{cases}
(A_n-2y)^n,&0\le y\le R_n,\\
0,&y\notin[0,R_n].
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently the original and smoothed total volumes and the continuous coordinate of the smoothed centroid are

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_n&=\mathcal H_1(S_n)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{n,\mathbf z}
      =\int N_n(y)\,dy=1+r^n,\\
\widetilde W_n&=\operatorname{vol}_{n+1}(\widetilde C_n)
      =\int V_n(y)\,dy=\frac{A_n^{n+1}-1}{2(n+1)},\\
m_n&=\frac{\int yV_n(y)\,dy}{\widetilde W_n}
      =\frac{A_n}{2(n+2)}
       -\frac{(n+1)(A_n-1)}{2(n+2)(A_n^{n+1}-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The full smoothed centroid is the fixed actual point \(\mathbf y_n^\star=(\mathbf0_n,m_n)\in S_n\). It satisfies

\[
\frac{R_n}{n+2}\le m_n<\frac{A_n}{2(n+2)},
\qquad m_n\sim\frac{3}{2n},
\qquad \delta_n<m_n<R_n\quad(n\ge240).
\]

Define the proper closed halfspace \(H_n=\{(\mathbf x,y):y\ge m_n\}\), which contains \(\mathbf y_n^\star\), and put \(B_n=A_n-2m_n\). The normalized original and smoothed cap masses are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n&=\frac{\mathcal H_1(S_n\cap H_n)}{W_n}
     =\frac{R_n-m_n+(3^n-1)\max\{\delta_n-m_n,0\}}{1+r^n},\\
Q_n&=\frac{\operatorname{vol}_{n+1}(\widetilde C_n\cap H_n)}{\widetilde W_n}
     =\frac{B_n^{n+1}-1}{A_n^{n+1}-1}>\frac14.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every \(n\ge240\), \(P_n=(R_n-m_n)/(1+r^n)\), and

\[
0<\frac{P_n}{2^{-n}Q_n}<8(20/29)^n\longrightarrow0.
\]

In particular, for every absolute \(c>0\) there is a member of this family with \(P_n<c2^{-n}Q_n\). Moreover,
\(h_{S_n}(\mathbf y_n^\star)/(2^{-n}W_n)\to0\), so this particular centroid selector cannot establish any absolute positive constant in the locked target. Nevertheless the actual point
\(\mathbf p_n=(\mathbf0_n,t_n)\), where \(t_n=W_n/(2\cdot3^n)\in(0,\delta_n)\), satisfies \(h_{S_n}(\mathbf p_n)\ge W_n/4\) for every \(n\ge1\).

## Proof

The inequalities defining \(C_n\) are affine inequalities after writing each absolute-value condition as two inequalities. They define a compact convex set, and \((\mathbf0_n,R_n/2)\) satisfies them strictly, so the set has nonempty interior. The Minkowski sum defining \(\widetilde C_n\) is compact, convex, and contains \(C_n\); it is also full-dimensional. Since \(0<\delta_n<1\), we have \(1<R_n<2\). For an original integer fiber, the inequalities are exactly \(0\le y\le R_n-\|\mathbf z\|_\infty\). The integer values of \(\|\mathbf z\|_\infty\) are zero, one, or at least two. This proves the complete fiber table, including its closed endpoints. In particular every nonempty original fiber has positive length, and the positive-fiber index set is exactly \(\{-1,0,1\}^n\).

At each \(y\in[0,R_n]\), the original horizontal section is the closed cube with coordinate radius \(\rho_n(y)=R_n-y\). It contains exactly \((2\lfloor\rho_n(y)\rfloor+1)^n\) lattice points. The radius is at least one precisely when \(0\le y\le\delta_n\), and it is less than one otherwise. Thus \(N_n(\delta_n)=3^n\) and \(N_n(R_n)=1\), as specified. Adding the closed cube \([-1/2,1/2]^n\) enlarges each coordinate radius by \(1/2\), so its horizontal ordinary volume is \((2\rho_n(y)+1)^n=(A_n-2y)^n\), including \(V_n(R_n)=1\). Outside this interval both sections are empty.

For every measurable \(E\subseteq\mathbb R\), summing the finite original fibers gives \(\mathcal H_1(S_n\cap(\mathbb R^n\times E))=\int_E N_n(y)\,dy\). Ordinary Fubini gives \(\operatorname{vol}_{n+1}(\widetilde C_n\cap(\mathbb R^n\times E))=\int_E V_n(y)\,dy\). These are the actual-volume conventions in `5bc145747671c668`, with no probability reweighting of an original fiber. The original fiber sum is \(R_n+(3^n-1)\delta_n=1+3^n\delta_n=1+r^n\). Integrating \((A_n-2y)^n\) from zero to \(R_n\), using \(A_n-2R_n=1\), proves the formula for \(\widetilde W_n\).

Reflection of each base coordinate preserves \(\widetilde C_n\), so every base coordinate of its ordinary centroid is zero. The substitution \(u=A_n-2y\) gives

\[
\int_0^{R_n}yV_n(y)\,dy
 =\frac14\left(
 \frac{A_n(A_n^{n+1}-1)}{n+1}
 -\frac{A_n^{n+2}-1}{n+2}\right).
\]

Dividing by \(\widetilde W_n\) and using \(A_n^{n+2}-1=A_n(A_n^{n+1}-1)+(A_n-1)\) proves the displayed exact value of \(m_n\). The marginal is positive throughout \([0,R_n]\), so its mean is strictly between zero and \(R_n\). The zero original fiber is \([0,R_n]\), which proves actual membership \(\mathbf y_n^\star\in S_n\), before the halfspace is chosen.

The identity \((A_n^{n+1}-1)/(A_n-1)=\sum_{j=0}^n A_n^j\ge n+1\) bounds the correction in \(m_n\) above by \(1/(2(n+2))\). It follows that \(m_n\ge(A_n-1)/(2(n+2))=R_n/(n+2)\). The correction is strictly positive, giving the stated upper bound. Since \(A_n\to3\) and \(A_n\ge3\), multiplying the correction by \(n\) bounds it above by \(2n(n+1)/((n+2)(3^{n+1}-1))\to0\). Hence \(nm_n\to3/2\).

To certify the explicit range where the original cap has only the zero fiber, the binomial theorem gives \((30/29)^{30}>1+30/29>2\). Therefore \(\delta_{240}<2^{-8}=1/256\), and \(242\delta_{240}<242/256<1\). For \(n\ge240\), the ratio of successive terms of \((n+2)\delta_n\) is \((29/30)(n+3)/(n+2)<1\). Thus \(\delta_n<1/(n+2)\le m_n\) throughout this range. Together with \(m_n<R_n\), this proves the claimed position of the centroid.

Integrating \(N_n\) above \(m_n\) gives the exact formula for \(P_n\) in both possible positions relative to \(\delta_n\). In particular, for \(n\ge240\), all nonzero lattice fibers lie strictly below the cutting level and the original cap length is exactly \(R_n-m_n\). Integrating \(V_n\) above the same level gives \((B_n^{n+1}-1)/(2(n+1))\), proving the exact formula for \(Q_n\).

The centroid formula also gives

\[
B_n=\frac{n+1}{n+2}\left(A_n+\frac{A_n-1}{A_n^{n+1}-1}\right)
 \ge\frac{n+1}{n+2}A_n.
\]

For \(k=n+1\ge2\), the binomial theorem and \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\) show
\((1+1/k)^k\le\sum_{j=0}^k1/j!<3\). Consequently \((k/(k+1))^k>1/3\). Since \(A_n^k>9\), the cap formula yields

\[
Q_n>\frac{A_n^k/3-1}{A_n^k-1}\ge\frac14.
\]

For \(n\ge240\), use \(R_n-m_n<2\), \(W_n>r^n\), and \(Q_n>1/4\) to obtain
\(P_n/(2^{-n}Q_n)<8(2/r)^n=8(20/29)^n\to0\). Also \(H_n\) contains the actual point \(\mathbf y_n^\star\), so by the locked depth definition \(h_{S_n}(\mathbf y_n^\star)\le W_nP_n\). This gives \(h_{S_n}(\mathbf y_n^\star)/(2^{-n}W_n)<2(20/29)^n\to0\). These conclusions refute only the fixed smoothed-centroid selector.

Finally, \(r^n>1\) implies \(0<t_n=(1+r^n)/(2\cdot3^n)<\delta_n\), so \(\mathbf p_n\in S_n\). Since \(N_n=3^n\) on \([0,t_n]\), the original mixed-integer volume on either side of \(y=t_n\) is exactly \(W_n/2\); the boundary has zero mixed-integer volume. For arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\alpha\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), let
\(H_{\boldsymbol\alpha,\beta}=\{(\mathbf x,y):\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf x+\beta(y-t_n)\ge0\}\). For every original fiber point, we have

\[
\mathbf1_{\{\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf z+\beta(y-t_n)\ge0\}}
+\mathbf1_{\{-\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf z+\beta(y-t_n)\ge0\}}
\ge\mathbf1_{\{\beta(y-t_n)\ge0\}}.
\]

The original fibers at \(\mathbf z\) and \(-\mathbf z\) coincide. Reindexing their sum shows that the two halfspaces on the left have equal original mixed-integer volumes. The original volume on the right is \(W_n/2\) if \(\beta\ne0\), and \(W_n\) if \(\beta=0\). Integration therefore gives \(\mathcal H_1(S_n\cap H_{\boldsymbol\alpha,\beta})\ge W_n/4\). Every proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf p_n\) contains the parallel halfspace whose boundary passes through \(\mathbf p_n\); the whole space also satisfies the bound. Taking the infimum proves \(h_{S_n}(\mathbf p_n)\ge W_n/4\). Thus this family provides no disproof of the locked existential statement.
