## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, $n\ge0$, and let the positive-volume $d$-dimensional fibers $K_{z,\varepsilon}$, $z\in P$, be those of an actual nested convex deformation with fixed measured sites $P$. Assume each fiber satisfies the homothet and almost-everywhere radial speed hypotheses of the physical retained-mass derivative theorem, with old fiber $K_z$ and inward speed $v_z$. Let
$$
M_\varepsilon=\sum_{z\in P}|K_{z,\varepsilon}|,\qquad
M=M_0,\qquad D_{\rm mix}=\sum_{z\in P}\int_{\partial K_z}v_z\,dS .
$$
Fix $p\in P$ and $y\in\operatorname{int}K_p$ and prescribe $y_\varepsilon=y+\varepsilon w+o(\varepsilon)$ in that fiber. Let $U_p$ be the old optimizing hinge set for $T=T_{K_p}(y)$ and put $\tau=T/M$. The right derivative is
$$
\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{0+}
\frac{T_{K_{p,\varepsilon}}(y_\varepsilon)}{M_\varepsilon}
=\frac1M\min_{u\in U_p}\left\{
\tau D_{\rm mix}
-\int_{\partial K_p}[1+u\cdot(x-y)]_+v_p(x)\,dS(x)-T\,u\cdot w
\right\}. \tag{6}
$$
It is nonnegative exactly when every row in braces is nonnegative. Off the centroid there is a single row. At $y=g_{K_p}$ the criterion is exactly
$$
\tau D_{\rm mix}-D_p-p_{K_p-y}(-B_p-|K_p|w)\ge0,\qquad
D_p=\int_{\partial K_p}v_p\,dS,\quad
B_p=\int_{\partial K_p}(x-y)v_p\,dS .
\tag{7}
$$
When $K_p$ is a polytope and $y=g_{K_p}$, the minimum can be taken at the finitely many vertices of $U_p=\{u:1+u\cdot(a-y)\ge0\text{ for every vertex }a\text{ of }K_p\}$. These are linear response rows when the actual physical speeds and prescribed velocity depend linearly on deformation parameters.

One actual setting for such averaging is the single-baseline case of accepted fact 84cd8a85417b0d7a. Use one fixed continuous-in-normal, affine-in-base support baseline satisfying its majorization hypotheses; average the nonnegative convex cut fields on that baseline before constructing the body. For a Borel probability average, assume a nonempty compact metric field parameter space and joint continuity of the fields, and average prescribed velocities integrable in that parameter. The response in (6) is then the average of individual responses off the centroid; at the centroid it is the minimum of the averaged rows.

For a conditional use of (6), define depth as the infimum of the physical mixed masses of closed ambient halfspaces containing the mark, and deepest points as the maximum-depth points on $\bigcup_{p\in P}\{p\}\times K_{p,\varepsilon}$. For this maximum, define $T_L(q)$ at boundary marks by the same mass and moment constraints; it is zero there since a supporting hyperplane through $q$ is Lebesgue-null and every feasible density with barycenter $q$ must be concentrated on that hyperplane. Suppose the secondary maximum $S(\varepsilon)$ of $T_{K_{p,\varepsilon}}(y)/M_\varepsilon$ among deepest points exists for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon\ge0$. Assume a fixed nonempty finite list of paths $(p_j,y_j(\varepsilon))$, each deepest for all such $\varepsilon$, with $p_j\in P$, $y_j(0)\in\operatorname{int}K_{p_j}$ and $y_j(\varepsilon)=y_j(0)+\varepsilon w_j+o(\varepsilon)$. Assume every secondary maximizer for small $\varepsilon>0$ lies on a listed path. Let $\tau_j=T_{K_{p_j}}(y_j(0))/M$ and $b_j$ be the derivative (6) on path $j$. Then
$$
S(\varepsilon)=\tau_*+\varepsilon\max_{j:\tau_j=\tau_*}b_j+o(\varepsilon),
\qquad \tau_*=\max_j\tau_j .
\tag{8}
$$
This gives the finite right derivative at zero if and only if $\tau_*=S(0)$, equivalently if at least one listed path begins at an old secondary maximizer. Without this equality, (8) is the right-hand expansion about the branch limit.

## Proof

The constant-weight radial formula on the finitely many fibers gives $M_\varepsilon=M-\varepsilon D_{\rm mix}+o(\varepsilon)$. The physical retained-mass derivative theorem on the marked fiber and the quotient rule give (6). The attained minimum and $1/M>0$ give the all-row criterion; the centroid gauge gives (7). At the centroid the hinge is nonnegative throughout $K_p$, so each row is affine in $u$. Its admissible set can be described by the old polytope vertices, is bounded because $y$ is interior, and is compact. Thus its affine minimum occurs at a vertex. At fixed $u$, the response is linear in the speeds and $w$.

For the averaging application use all the hypotheses of 84cd8a85417b0d7a and take its index set to be a singleton with baseline $\ell(s,z)$ at unit normal $s$. The fixed-source baseline in the source note is $\ell(s,z)=h_{K_p}(s)+A(s)\cdot(z-p)$ for a continuous slope $A$ satisfying $\ell(s,z)\ge h_{K_z}(s)$ on $P$. The accepted lemma gives speed $\mathbf1_{\{\ell(n_z(x),z)=h_{K_z}(n_z(x))\}}V(n_z(x),z,x)$ at a regular old boundary point with unit normal $n_z(x)$, for each cut field $V$. The average field is continuous by joint continuity on a compact domain and is still nonnegative and convex in the physical variables, so the lemma also gives an actual compatible erosion for this field. The fixed indicator multiplies the individual fields and the average, making the resulting physical speed the average of the individual speeds. Each row therefore averages by linearity of integration and of the prescribed velocities. At a noncentroid the singleton minimum commutes with this average; at a centroid it gives the minimum of the averaged rows. This does not linearize a general maximum over several varying contact fields.

Each listed path has expansion $\tau_j+\varepsilon b_j+o(\varepsilon)$ by (6). All listed paths are deepest, and the list contains a new secondary maximizer, so the maximum of the listed values is $S(\varepsilon)$ for small positive $\varepsilon$. The paths with $\tau_j<\tau_*$ lose by a fixed positive gap for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Among the ties the largest slope gives (8), since the maximum of the absolute values of finitely many $o(\varepsilon)$ errors is $o(\varepsilon)$. At zero the listed points are old deepest, so $\tau_*\le S(0)$. If strict, (8) gives a downward jump from $S(0)$ and no finite right derivative there. Equality gives the displayed linear coefficient. Cover neutrality alone does not give these branches, an exact primary-extremizer path in an outer variational problem, or an exact higher-order secondary tie for tertiary selection.
