## Statement

Write $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Let $P$ be a nonempty finite real base and suppose every actual positive compatible $d$-fiber family on every real base $Q$ with fewer sites than $P$ has maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth at least $\gamma_d/h(Q)$, with $h$ the Helly number for convex-set traces. If a positive compatible family on $P$ has maximum normalized depth strictly below $\gamma_d/h(P)$, it admits a positive compatible polytopal approximation on $P$ with the same strict inequality. For any such polytopal approximation, its projective orbit under positive affine denominators attains its infimum at a positive denominator. Its minimizing image is still polytopal and satisfies the necessary two-jet infeasibility condition of the exact polytopal projective descent theorem.

## Proof

For each old $K_p$ take convex hulls of finite inner nets with mesh tending to zero, and take the convex hull of all their lifted points. Its actual slice over $p$ lies between the inner net hull and $K_p$ by original compatibility. Thus its actual slices converge in Hausdorff distance, and they are positive for all sufficiently fine nets by support comparison with an inner ball. They are polytopes, being slices of a polytope. The maximum normalized depth converges: use 43ed87d91b76249c along a subsequence of maximizing marks converging at one fixed base for the upper limit, and approximate an old maximizing mark in the new corresponding fibers for the lower limit. The maxima exist by the same fixed-base depth continuity in the mark and compactness. Hence a fine net preserves the strict gap.

Fix one such polytopal family. Translate coordinates so that $0$ is in the relative interior of the base hull. By the projective law in 6b80e85ff6937910 the orbit depth $D(L)$ is the normalized maximum on the fixed candidate set using the positive fiber weights $L(p)^{-d}$. Multiplying $L$ by a positive scalar leaves $D(L)$ unchanged. Impose $\sum_{p\in P}L(p)=|P|$. The nonnegative closure of the affine evaluation arrays with this normalization is compact: it is an intersection of a finite-dimensional closed linear space and a bounded closed simplex, and mixing each nonnegative array with the constant array $1$ approximates it by positive ones. On the positive domain the normalized finite fiber coefficients vary continuously. Thus the measures on the fixed sampled set converge in total variation there, and every cap probability, its infimum at a mark, and its maximum over marks change by at most this total variation difference.

If a sequence of positive arrays tends to a boundary array, its zero set $F$ is a nonempty proper exposed-face trace of $P$. Because every original $V_p>0$, the normalized weights concentrate on $F$: the total unnormalized mass there diverges, and the mass outside stays bounded. For each member of the sequence, the normalized restricted weighted depth on $F$ is the unweighted geometric depth of its projective image on the transformed base $F_L$. Use the same full chart and restrict it to the affine span of the exposed face. The family is compatible there since the map preserves convex hulls and is injective, and it has fewer sites than $P$. The finite convex traces of $F$ correspond bijectively to those of $F_L$: a subset is a convex trace exactly when its convex hull acquires no extra sites, and the chart maps each convex hull to the hull of the image points. Thus $h(F_L)=h(F)$. Every convex trace on the exposed face is also a trace on $P$ via its convex hull, giving $h(F)\le h(P)$. The smaller-base hypothesis therefore bounds the depth of this restricted weighted family below by $\gamma_d/h(F)$. At a mark in $F$, each containing ambient cap retains at least its own $F$ contribution. Intrinsic caps on the face plane extend to ambient caps, so the maximum for the whole normalized law is at least the $F$-mass fraction times the restricted depth. Taking lower limits yields at least $\gamma_d/h(P)$ along every such boundary sequence.

A minimizing sequence has limit points in the compact closure. Its value is at most $D(1)<\gamma_d/h(P)$, so the boundary estimate forces a positive limit point. Continuity gives an attained positive denominator $L_*$. Projective maps in the positive chart send polytopes to polytopes by the convex-hull identity. Every sufficiently small positive affine projective change of the attained image stays in the orbit: composing with denominator $a+b\cdot p'$ in image base coordinates $p'=p/L_*(p)$ gives the original affine denominator $aL_*(p)+b\cdot p$, positive on $P$. The attained image is thus locally projectively minimal. The exact polytopal descent theorem applies there and excludes the feasible two-jets in its statement.
