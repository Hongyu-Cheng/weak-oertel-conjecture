## Statement

Let $A,C\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies with $0<a=|A|\le |C|$ and let $u\ne0$. For a body $E$ put $E_u(t)=|E\cap\{u\cdot x\ge t\}|$. Then
$$
K_u(t)=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}[A_u(t+v)+C_u(t-v)]
$$
is the Lipschitz continuous closed survival function of an atomless finite measure $\kappa_u$ of mass $a$, carried by the interval between the averages of the two projection minima and maxima. For any measure $0\le\sigma\le\mathbf1_C\,dx$ of mass $a$ and any coupling of $\mathbf1_A\,dx$ and $\sigma$, let $\eta$ be its midpoint pushforward and $g_\eta$ its centroid. Then $\eta\{u\cdot x\ge t\}\le K_u(t)$ at every real threshold, and $\int s\,d\kappa_u(s)\ge a\,u\cdot g_\eta$, where $g_\eta=(g_A+g_\sigma)/2$.

If a middle body $B$ of volume $b>0$ contains $(A+C)/2$, place the bodies at integer levels $-1,0,1$ and use their actual mixed measure $\mu$. Write $V_u=B_u+K_u$, let $h(u)$ be the mean of its scalar law of mass $b+a$, and put $y=(b g_B+a g_\eta)/(b+a)$. Then $y\in B$, $h(u)\ge u\cdot y$, and for every closed cap $H=\{u\cdot x+v z\ge t_0\}$ containing $(0,y)$,
$$
V_u(h(u))\le V_u(u\cdot y)\le \mu(H).
$$
All assertions scale by a common positive volume normalization on the fibers.

## Proof

Write $l_A,l_C$ and $r_A,r_C$ for the respective projection minima and maxima. The cap functions are nonincreasing and Lipschitz: a slab of projection width $\delta$ has volume at most $\delta/\lVert u\rVert$ times an upper bound on the volumes of the orthogonal sections of the bounded body. For $d=1$ one can use section bound one. The sum has a common Lipschitz bound for every $v$, and the finite infimum preserves it and monotonicity. A finite sufficiently large tilt always allows the smaller body to be full and the other empty, proving $0\le K_u\le a$. For $t\le(l_A+l_C)/2$ the two thresholds sum to at most $l_A+l_C$, so at least one cap is full and the sum is at least $a$. For $t\ge(r_A+r_C)/2$ choose thresholds at or above their respective maxima, so both cap volumes are zero (including the supporting planes). Thus $a-K_u$ is a continuous distribution function of total mass $a$ and the associated finite measure is atomless and has the claimed closed survival.

For every $v$, a midpoint with $u$-projection at least $t$ has first endpoint projection at least $t+v$ or second endpoint projection at least $t-v$. Integrate this indicator inequality against the coupling and apply the second marginal bound to obtain $\eta\{u\cdot x\ge t\}\le A_u(t+v)+C_u(t-v)$. Infimizing gives the asserted domination, including any atoms of $\eta$. The two scalar laws have the same mass $a$ and bounded support. On a common support interval $[L,U]$, Fubini gives $\int s\,d\nu=La+\int_L^U\nu([t,\infty))\,dt$, yielding the first-moment comparison. Affinity of the midpoint gives the claimed $g_\eta$.

The law of $V_u$ adds the projection of volume on $B$ to $\kappa_u$. Its mass is $b+a$, and its first moment is at least $b\,u\cdot g_B+a\,u\cdot g_\eta$. Convexity and support give $g_\eta\in(A+C)/2\subseteq B$ and therefore $y\in B$. The actual mass of $H$ includes $B_u(t_0)$ and its two endpoint cap costs at the tilt $v$, so $V_u(t_0)\le\mu(H)$. Since $h(u)\ge u\cdot y\ge t_0$ and $V_u$ is nonincreasing, the two asserted inequalities follow.
