## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
\[
\mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\). For \(0<s\leq a_z\), put
\[
\mathsf M_s(C_z):=
\left\{
\frac1s\int_{C_z}x\rho(x)\,dx:
0\leq\rho\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
\int_{C_z}\rho=s
\right\}.
\]
For every nonempty compact convex \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), use
\[
h_K(w):=\max_{x\in K}\langle w,x\rangle,
\qquad
F_K(w):=\{x\in K:\langle w,x\rangle=h_K(w)\}.
\]

Retain one common canonical zero-fresh self-saturated instance of accepted facts `6bfe50fed9a5540b` and `cb96640c07ffd5fa`. Its finite nonempty positive label set is \(I\). For each \(j\in I\), write \(z_j^-\) and \(z_j^+\) for the two endpoint occurrences denoted by \(u_j\) and \(v_j\), respectively, in accepted fact `cb96640c07ffd5fa`. They are positive-volume, off-diagonal, same-parity lattice fibers, and
\[
m_j:=\frac{z_j^-+z_j^+}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]
Put
\[
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z,
\]
where \(c_z\) is the centroid of \(C_z\), and set
\[
D_j:=\frac12(D_{z_j^-}+D_{z_j^+}),
\qquad
L_j:=\frac12(\mathcal K_{z_j^-}+\mathcal K_{z_j^+}).
\]
Every \(D_j\) is an origin-symmetric convex body with \(0\) in its interior. There is a number \(\vartheta_j>0\) such that
\[
L_j\subseteq\mathsf M_{\vartheta_j}(C_{m_j}).
\tag{1}
\]

The instance has positive self-saturated loads \(q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}>0\), total load
\[
\Omega_b:=\sum_{j\in I}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
>\frac{97}{100}Q,
\tag{2}
\]
endpoint and physical-target budgets
\[
\sum_{j\in I}
q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{z_j^-=z\}}+\mathbf1_{\{z_j^+=z\}}\bigr)
\leq\frac{a_z}{4}
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n),
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\sum_{j\in I:m_j=m}
\frac{q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\vartheta_j}\leq1
\quad(m\in\mathbb Z^n),
\tag{4}
\]
and a reduced gauge-load-minimal stress
\[
v_j^b=q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}u_j,
\qquad
h_{D_j}(u_j)=1,
\tag{5}
\]
such that
\[
\sum_jq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}u_j=0,
\qquad
\sum_jq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}u_jm_j^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
With
\[
\delta_*:=\frac{3\log(4/3)}{\log2}-1>0,
\qquad
\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}
:=-\sum_jq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}h_{L_j}(u_j),
\]
one has
\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}>
\frac{97}{100}\delta_*Q,
\qquad
\frac{\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\Omega_b}
>\frac{95}{97}\delta_*.
\tag{7}
\]

Then there are a finite nonempty set \(\mathscr A\) and nonnegative numbers \(q_i^\alpha\), for \(\alpha\in\mathscr A\) and \(i\in I\), with the following properties. For every \(i\in I\),
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr A}q_i^\alpha
=q_i^{\mathrm{sat}}.}
\tag{8}
\]
For every \(\alpha\), put
\[
I_\alpha:=\{i\in I:q_i^\alpha>0\},
\qquad
\Omega_\alpha:=\sum_iq_i^\alpha,
\qquad
\Delta_\alpha:=-\sum_iq_i^\alpha h_{L_i}(u_i).
\tag{9}
\]
Then \(\Omega_\alpha>0\), \(\Delta_\alpha>0\), and
\[
\sum_iq_i^\alpha u_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_iq_i^\alpha u_im_i^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{10}
\]
More precisely, if
\[
\Xi_i:=\bigl(u_i,\operatorname{vec}(u_im_i^{\mathsf T})\bigr)
\in\mathbb R^{d(n+1)},
\tag{11}
\]
then \(I_\alpha\) is the support of a support-minimal positive linear dependence among the lifted columns \((\Xi_i)_{i\in I}\). This is the meaning of circuit here; no circuit property of the lattice points \((m_i)\) alone is asserted. In particular,
\[
1\leq |I_\alpha|\leq d(n+1)+1.
\tag{12}
\]
The circuit masses and defects add exactly:
\[
\boxed{
\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha=\Omega_b,
\qquad
\sum_\alpha\Delta_\alpha=\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}.}
\tag{13}
\]

Fix, once for all circuits, arbitrary simultaneous contacts
\[
\ell_i\in F_{L_i}(u_i),
\qquad
p_{ij}\in F_{L_j}(u_i)
\quad(i,j\in I),
\qquad
p_{ii}=\ell_i.
\tag{14}
\]
For every \(\alpha\), extend
\[
\pi_i^\alpha:=\frac{q_i^\alpha}{\Omega_\alpha}
\quad(i\in I),
\]
so \(\pi_i^\alpha=0\) off \(I_\alpha\), and define
\[
\Psi_\alpha:=
\sum_jq_j^\alpha\sum_i\pi_i^\alpha h_{L_j}(u_i),
\qquad
t_\alpha:=\frac{\Delta_\alpha}
{\Delta_\alpha+\Psi_\alpha},
\tag{15}
\]
\[
a_{ij}^\alpha
:=(1-t_\alpha)q_i^\alpha\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}
+t_\alpha q_i^\alpha\pi_j^\alpha.
\tag{16}
\]
Then \(\Psi_\alpha\geq0\), \(0<t_\alpha\leq1\), and
\[
\sum_ja_{ij}^\alpha=q_i^\alpha,
\qquad
\sum_ia_{ij}^\alpha=q_j^\alpha,
\tag{17}
\]
\[
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha u_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha u_im_j^{\mathsf T}=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha h_{L_j}(u_i)=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal O_\alpha:=
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha
\bigl(h_{L_j}(u_i)-\langle u_i,\ell_j\rangle\bigr),
\]
\[
\mathcal T_\alpha:=
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha
\langle u_i,\ell_j-\ell_i\rangle.
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_\alpha=(1-t_\alpha)\Delta_\alpha\geq0,
\qquad
\mathcal T_\alpha=t_\alpha\Delta_\alpha>0,
\qquad
\mathcal O_\alpha+\mathcal T_\alpha=\Delta_\alpha.}
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently, for
\[
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}}:=\sum_\alpha\mathcal O_\alpha,
\qquad
\mathcal T_{\mathrm{circ}}:=\sum_\alpha\mathcal T_\alpha,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}}+\mathcal T_{\mathrm{circ}}
=\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}},
\qquad
\max\{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}},\mathcal T_{\mathrm{circ}}\}
>\frac{97}{200}\delta_*Q.}
\tag{21}
\]

The circuit contact measure
\[
\nu_{\mathrm{circ}}:=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr A}\sum_{i,j\in I}
a_{ij}^\alpha\delta_{(m_j,p_{ij})}
\tag{22}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies
\[
\boxed{2\gamma_d\nu_{\mathrm{circ}}
\preceq_{\mathrm{hs}}\mu_C,}
\qquad
\boxed{|\nu_{\mathrm{circ}}|=\Omega_b>
\frac{97}{100}Q.}
\tag{23}
\]
This is one domination statement for all positive-weight circuit labels. Zero coefficients \(a_{ij}^\alpha\), coincident endpoint labels, coincident physical targets, and coincident contact atoms create no extra charge.

For every \(\alpha,j,i,k\), put
\[
b_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}:=
\frac{t_\alpha q_j^\alpha}{2}\pi_i^\alpha\pi_k^\alpha.
\tag{24}
\]
Let
\[
E_{\mathrm{circ}}:=\left\{m\in\mathbb Z^n:
\eta_m:=\sum_{j\in I:m_j=m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
=\sum_\alpha\sum_{j\in I:m_j=m}q_j^\alpha>0\right\}.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(m\in E_{\mathrm{circ}}\), define
\[
c_{\alpha j}:=\sum_i\pi_i^\alpha p_{ij},
\qquad
c_m^{\mathrm{circ}}:=\frac1{\eta_m}
\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^\alpha c_{\alpha j},
\tag{26}
\]
\[
g_{\alpha,ik}^{(j,m)}:=
\frac{b_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}}{\eta_m}(p_{ij}-p_{kj}),
\tag{27}
\]
\[
\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{circ}}:=
c_m^{\mathrm{circ}}
+\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}\sum_{i,k}
[-g_{\alpha,ik}^{(j,m)},g_{\alpha,ik}^{(j,m)}],
\tag{28}
\]
and the full pooled body
\[
\mathcal P_m:=\frac1{\eta_m}
\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}L_j.
\tag{29}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{circ}}
\subseteq\mathsf M_{\eta_m/2}(C_m),}
\tag{30}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{circ}}
\subseteq\mathcal P_m
\subseteq\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m).}
\tag{31}
\]
The body \(\mathcal P_m\) in (29) is exactly the full pooled body \(\mathcal P_m^{\mathrm{sw}}\) of accepted fact `2d4c7439acb19da3`, not a circuit-dependent subbody. Hence every affine-hit, mixed-depth, and residual-rank conclusion of that accepted fact for these bodies remains available. Physical targets outside \(E_{\mathrm{circ}}\) are unused and no normalized center, generator, zonotope, or metronoid assertion is assigned to them. A block with \(q_j^\alpha=0\) contributes zero to (24), (26), and (28), and it consumes no density.

Every generator-normal term below is nonnegative, and the stationary energy is preserved exactly:
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m\in E_{\mathrm{circ}}}\eta_m
\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}\sum_{i,k}
\left\langle u_i-u_k,g_{\alpha,ik}^{(j,m)}\right\rangle
=\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}}.}
\tag{32}
\]

Finally, put
\[
r_*:=\frac{\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\Omega_b},
\qquad
\mathscr G:=\left\{\alpha\in\mathscr A:
\Delta_\alpha\geq\frac{r_*}{2}\Omega_\alpha\right\}.
\tag{33}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G}\Delta_\alpha
>\frac12\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}},
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_\alpha}{\Omega_\alpha}
>\frac{95}{194}\delta_*
\quad(\alpha\in\mathscr G).}
\tag{34}
\]
If
\[
\mathscr G_{\mathcal O}:=
\{\alpha\in\mathscr G:\mathcal O_\alpha\geq\mathcal T_\alpha\},
\qquad
\mathscr G_{\mathcal T}:=\mathscr G\setminus\mathscr G_{\mathcal O},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\max\left\{
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G_{\mathcal O}}\mathcal O_\alpha,
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G_{\mathcal T}}\mathcal T_\alpha
\right\}
>\frac18\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}
>\frac{97}{800}\delta_*Q.}
\tag{35}
\]

The conclusions above do not convert \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}}\), \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{circ}}\), or the good-circuit subfamily into any accepted hard-ledger capacity. In particular, no terminal absorption inequality, arbitrary-separator realization, or closure of the zero-fresh branch is asserted.

## Proof

Accepted fact `cb96640c07ffd5fa` constructs the self-saturated capacity \(q^{\mathrm{sat}}\), proves that \(v^b\) remains optimal for that reduced capacity, and proves that every optimizer \(\widetilde v\) for the reduced capacity saturates every coordinate:
\[
h_{D_i}(\widetilde v_i)=q_i^{\mathrm{sat}}
\quad(i\in I).
\tag{36}
\]
Thus every reduced optimizer has total gauge load \(\sum_iq_i^{\mathrm{sat}}=\Omega_b\). In particular, \(v^b\) is gauge-load-minimal for the reduced capacity, not merely for the unreduced capacity. This verifies the load-minimality hypothesis needed below.

Apply the same-ray circuit decomposition in accepted fact `6496cb3b0fd44f9d` to the matching capacity \(q^{\mathrm{sat}}\) and optimizer \(v^b\). It gives nonnegative coefficients \(q_i^\alpha\) such that
\[
v_i^b=\sum_\alpha q_i^\alpha u_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_\alpha q_i^\alpha=q_i^{\mathrm{sat}}
\quad(i\in I).
\tag{37}
\]
Every component has both moment cancellations and is supported on an extreme ray of the cone
\[
\left\{(x_i)_{i\in I}:x_i\geq0,
\ \sum_ix_i\Xi_i=0\right\}.
\tag{38}
\]
Therefore its support is a support-minimal positive dependence among the lifted columns in (11) and has size at most \(d(n+1)+1\). The same accepted fact proves strict negativity of every component support score. This gives (9)-(12), including \(\Delta_\alpha>0\). Summing (8) over \(i\) proves the mass identity in (13), while multiplying (8) by \(-h_{L_i}(u_i)\), summing, and using finite interchange proves the defect identity in (13).

Fix the contacts in (14). For each \(\alpha\), apply accepted fact `487ffbb5b46832e9` on the positive support \(I_\alpha\) with the data map
\[
\sigma_i=q_i^\alpha,
\qquad
\mathbf m_i=m_i,
\qquad
K_i=L_i,
\qquad
\mathbf v_i=u_i,
\qquad
\mathbf q_i=\ell_i.
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (9)-(10) verify its strict objective and two moment hypotheses. Extending its coefficients by zero off \(I_\alpha^2\) gives exactly (15)-(20). Summing (20) over \(\alpha\) and using (13) gives the first identity in (21). The second follows from
\[
\max\{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{circ}},\mathcal T_{\mathrm{circ}}\}
\geq\frac12\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}
>\frac{97}{200}\delta_*Q.
\]

We next prove (23), including the positive-weight requirement in accepted fact `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd`. Use only triples \((\alpha,i,j)\) for which \(a_{ij}^\alpha>0\) as labels, give such a label weight \(a_{ij}^\alpha\), give it the two endpoint occurrences \(z_j^-,z_j^+\), and select its point \(p_{ij}\in L_j\). At any original endpoint fiber \(z\), the total occurrence-counted label load is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\alpha,i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{z_j^-=z\}}+\mathbf1_{\{z_j^+=z\}}\bigr)
&=\sum_j
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{z_j^-=z\}}+\mathbf1_{\{z_j^+=z\}}\bigr)
\sum_\alpha\sum_i a_{ij}^\alpha\\
&=\sum_j
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{z_j^-=z\}}+\mathbf1_{\{z_j^+=z\}}\bigr)
\sum_\alpha q_j^\alpha\\
&=\sum_jq_j^{\mathrm{sat}}
\bigl(\mathbf1_{\{z_j^-=z\}}+\mathbf1_{\{z_j^+=z\}}\bigr)
\leq\frac{a_z}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{40}
\]
Here the column marginal in (17), the coordinatewise identity (8), and occurrence counting are all essential. Accepted fact `ff5bc0ba02cc86cd` applies once to this entire positive-weight family. It proves the support assertion and the domination in (23), including all collisions. Zero coefficients were not presented as labels. Finally,
\[
|\nu_{\mathrm{circ}}|
=\sum_{\alpha,i,j}a_{ij}^\alpha
=\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha
=\Omega_b,
\]
which proves the mass assertion in (23).

Fix \(m\in E_{\mathrm{circ}}\). We prove the simultaneous density assertion (30). For every \(i,j\in I\) with \(m_j=m\), membership (1) and (14) provide a representing density
\[
\rho_{ij}:C_m\longrightarrow[0,1]
\]
of mass \(\vartheta_j\) and barycenter \(p_{ij}\). If \(q_j^\alpha>0\), set
\[
f_{\alpha,ij}:=
\frac{q_j^\alpha}{\vartheta_j}\pi_i^\alpha\rho_{ij},
\qquad
F_{\alpha j}:=\sum_i f_{\alpha,ij}.
\tag{41}
\]
If \(q_j^\alpha=0\), set all these densities to zero and make no positive-mass product-box assertion for that block. For a positive block,
\[
\int f_{\alpha,ij}=q_j^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha,
\qquad
\frac1{q_j^\alpha\pi_i^\alpha}
\int x f_{\alpha,ij}(x)\,dx=p_{ij}
\tag{42}
\]
whenever \(\pi_i^\alpha>0\), and
\[
0\leq F_{\alpha j}
\leq\frac{q_j^\alpha}{\vartheta_j}
\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{43}
\]
Consequently, by (4) and (8),
\[
\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}F_{\alpha j}
\leq
\sum_{j:m_j=m}
\frac{\sum_\alpha q_j^\alpha}{\vartheta_j}
=\sum_{j:m_j=m}
\frac{q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\vartheta_j}
\leq1
\quad\text{a.e. on }C_m.
\tag{44}
\]

For a fixed positive block \((\alpha,j)\), apply the explicit product-box density of accepted fact `c0f08e8df1893e54` to two identical copies of the positive members of \((f_{\alpha,ij})_i\), with both total masses and the parameter \(\beta\) equal to \(q_j^\alpha\). For independent
\[
\theta_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)}\in[-t_\alpha/2,t_\alpha/2],
\qquad
\lambda_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)}:=\frac12+\theta_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)},
\]
its density is
\[
h_{\alpha j}^{\boldsymbol\theta}
:=\sum_{i,k}
\left[
(1-\lambda_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)})\frac{\pi_k^\alpha}{2}
f_{\alpha,ij}
+\lambda_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)}\frac{\pi_i^\alpha}{2}
f_{\alpha,kj}
\right].
\tag{45}
\]
The explicit bounds in that accepted fact give, and direct summation confirms,
\[
0\leq h_{\alpha j}^{\boldsymbol\theta}
\leq F_{\alpha j},
\qquad
\int h_{\alpha j}^{\boldsymbol\theta}=\frac{q_j^\alpha}{2},
\tag{46}
\]
with normalized first moment
\[
c_{\alpha j}
+\sum_{i,k}\pi_i^\alpha\pi_k^\alpha
\theta_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)}(p_{kj}-p_{ij}).
\tag{47}
\]
Indices with \(\pi_i^\alpha=0\) have zero density and zero coefficient, so (45)-(47) agree with the sums over all of \(I\).

Sum (45) over all positive blocks \((\alpha,j)\) with \(m_j=m\). Equations (44) and (46) give one aggregate density between zero and one. Its mass is
\[
\frac12\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^\alpha
=\frac{\eta_m}{2}.
\tag{48}
\]
Its normalized first moments, as all parameters vary independently, are
\[
c_m^{\mathrm{circ}}
+\frac1{\eta_m}
\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^\alpha
\sum_{i,k}\pi_i^\alpha\pi_k^\alpha
\theta_{ik}^{(\alpha,j)}(p_{kj}-p_{ij}).
\tag{49}
\]
Because each parameter interval is \([-t_\alpha/2,t_\alpha/2]\), the set in (49) is exactly the symmetric-segment sum in (28), with harmless reversal of each generator orientation. This proves (30) by one common physical-target density budget. Equation (48) also proves \(0<\eta_m\leq a_m\).

We prove the first inclusion in (31) by exact coefficient collection. Let \(x\in\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{circ}}\). There are numbers \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}\in[-1,1]\) such that
\[
\eta_mx
=\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^\alpha c_{\alpha j}
+\sum_\alpha\sum_{j:m_j=m}\sum_{i,k}
\varepsilon_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}b_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}
(p_{ij}-p_{kj}).
\tag{50}
\]
For each block and \(\ell\in I\), collect the coefficient of \(p_{\ell j}\):
\[
\zeta_{\alpha,\ell j}
:=q_j^\alpha\pi_\ell^\alpha
+\sum_k\varepsilon_{\alpha,\ell k}^{(j)}
b_{\alpha,\ell k}^{(j)}
-\sum_i\varepsilon_{\alpha,i\ell}^{(j)}
b_{\alpha,i\ell}^{(j)}.
\tag{51}
\]
The exact ordered-pair coefficients give
\[
\sum_kb_{\alpha,\ell k}^{(j)}
+\sum_ib_{\alpha,i\ell}^{(j)}
=t_\alpha q_j^\alpha\pi_\ell^\alpha.
\tag{52}
\]
Hence
\[
\zeta_{\alpha,\ell j}
\geq(1-t_\alpha)q_j^\alpha\pi_\ell^\alpha\geq0,
\qquad
\sum_\ell\zeta_{\alpha,\ell j}=q_j^\alpha.
\tag{53}
\]
For \(q_j^\alpha>0\), the corresponding block in (50) is therefore \(q_j^\alpha\) times a convex combination of the points \(p_{\ell j}\in L_j\), hence lies in \(q_j^\alpha L_j\); a zero block contributes nothing. Summing first over \(\alpha\) for each fixed \(j\), convexity of \(L_j\) and (8) give
\[
\sum_\alpha q_j^\alpha L_j
=q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}L_j.
\tag{54}
\]
Summing (54) over all labels at \(m\) proves
\[
\eta_m\mathcal Z_m^{\mathrm{circ}}
\subseteq\sum_{j:m_j=m}q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}L_j,
\]
which is the first inclusion in (31).

For the second inclusion in (31), apply accepted fact `43849354cffec2b9` to Lebesgue measure on \(C_m\), the bodies in (1), weights \(q_j^{\mathrm{sat}}\), and the exact budget (4). It gives
\[
\mathcal P_m\subseteq\mathsf M_{\eta_m}(C_m).
\]
The definition (29) agrees term by term with the full pooled body in accepted fact `2d4c7439acb19da3`; therefore the previously accepted screens for that body apply without any change of normalization.

For the energy identity, apply the pairization conclusion of accepted fact `487ffbb5b46832e9` within every circuit. If \(\mathcal O_{\alpha,j}\) is the target-\(j\) contribution to \(\mathcal O_\alpha\), it gives
\[
\mathcal O_{\alpha,j}
=\sum_{i,k}b_{\alpha,ik}^{(j)}
\langle u_i-u_k,p_{ij}-p_{kj}\rangle,
\tag{55}
\]
and every summand is nonnegative. Multiplying (27) by \(\eta_m\), summing (55) first over labels at \(m\), then over \(m\) and \(\alpha\), proves (32) exactly.

It remains to prove the good-circuit estimates. By (7),
\[
r_*=\frac{\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}}{\Omega_b}
>\frac{95}{97}\delta_*.
\tag{56}
\]
For \(\mathscr B:=\mathscr A\setminus\mathscr G\), the strict complement of (33) and (13) give
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr B}\Delta_\alpha
<\frac{r_*}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr B}\Omega_\alpha
\leq\frac{r_*}{2}\Omega_b
=\frac12\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\tag{57}
\]
This proves the first part of (34), and the second follows from (33) and (56). By (20),
\[
\max\{\mathcal O_\alpha,\mathcal T_\alpha\}
\geq\frac12\Delta_\alpha.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G_{\mathcal O}}\mathcal O_\alpha
+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G_{\mathcal T}}\mathcal T_\alpha
=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr G}
\max\{\mathcal O_\alpha,\mathcal T_\alpha\}
>\frac14\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\tag{58}
\]
At least one of the two nonnegative sums in (58) is greater than \(\Delta^{\mathrm{sat}}/8\), and (7) then proves (35). None of these estimates identifies either energy with a hard-ledger capacity, so the stated non-absorption limitation remains.
