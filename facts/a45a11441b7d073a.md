## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be real, and let $(w_i)$ be a nonzero nonnegative sequence on a finite consecutive integer interval, with $(w_i^{1/d})$ concave there and $\sum_i i w_i=0$. Put $M=\sum_i w_i$, $m=\max_i w_i$, $r_d=(2/(d+2))^{(d+1)/d}$ and $\kappa_d=1/(3+d r_d)$. For every integer $h\ge0$,
$$
\min\left\{\sum_{i>h}w_i,\ \sum_{i<-h}w_i\right\}
\ge\kappa_d\bigl[M-(2h+1)m\bigr].
$$
The constants satisfy $1/5<\kappa_d\le9/31<3/10$, with $\kappa_1=9/31$ and $\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_d=1/5$.

Let $d$ be a positive integer and $C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ a convex body of positive mixed $d$-volume $M$ whose mixed-volume centroid has integer base coordinate $j$. Write $w_i=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_i)$ and $m=\max_{i\in\mathbb Z} w_i$. With $\kappa_d$ as above, each of $z>j+h$ and $z<j-h$ has mixed $d$-volume in $C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^d)$ at least $\kappa_d[M-(2h+1)m]$ for every integer $h\ge0$.

## Proof

Since $0<2/(d+2)<1$, we have $d r_d<2d/(d+2)<2$. By convexity of $v\mapsto v^d$ on the positive axis and its tangent at $v=1$, $(3/2)^d\ge1+d/2$. Hence $(2/(d+2))^{1/d}\ge2/3$ and $d r_d\ge4d/(3(d+2))\ge4/9$. These bounds give $1/5<\kappa_d\le9/31<3/10$, with equality $\kappa_d=9/31$ at $d=1$. Also $d r_d=(2d/(d+2))\exp(-\log((d+2)/2)/d)\to2$.

Remove zero endpoint terms, preserving mass, mean and root concavity. Concavity and nonnegativity of the roots imply that the remaining positive support is consecutive: any index between two positive terms lies above their positive interpolated root. For a singleton the mean forces its index to be zero, and both inequalities are immediate since $M=m$. Otherwise write the positive support as $[\ell,r]\cap\mathbb Z$ with $\ell<0<r$, by positivity and the zero mean.

It suffices to prove the right-tail bound, since reflection preserves all hypotheses. Put $T=\sum_{i>h} w_i$ and $k=2h+1$. Suppose $T<\kappa_d(M-km)$, so $M>km$. If $T=0$, the capped integer first-moment bound for all masses gives $0\le hM-M^2/(2m)+M/2$, implying $M\le km$. Thus $T>0$. Put $t=T/M$, $\rho=m/M$ and $x=k\rho$. Then $0<t<\kappa_d(1-x)<3/10$, $0<\rho\le x<1$, and $x<1-t/\kappa_d$. Since $r\ge h+1$ and $\ell<0$, $s=h+1$ lies in the positive support.

Set $\beta=1/(d+1)$ and $F_j=\sum_{i=j}^r w_i$ for $\ell\le j\le r$. By the actual suffix concavity theorem, applied to these positive consecutive masses with concave $d$th roots, the decrements $F_j^\beta-F_{j+1}^\beta$ are nondecreasing for $\ell\le j<r$. If $s<r$ and the decrements from $s$ onward are at least $c>0$, summing them gives $F_{s+v}\le(T^\beta-vc)_+^{d+1}$ whenever $s+v\le r$. This bound also holds for $s+v>r$ if we put $F_{s+v}=0$ there for summation. Define $E=\sum_{i>h}(i-h)w_i$. Summing suffixes counts each term $w_i$ exactly $i-h$ times. Bounding the sum after $v=0$ by the integral of the decreasing nonnegative envelope gives
$$
E=\sum_{v\ge0}F_{s+v}\le T+\int_0^\infty (T^\beta-vc)_+^{d+1}\,dv
=T+\frac{T^{1+\beta}}{(d+2)c}.
$$
Here $a_+=\max(a,0)$. If $s=r$, then $E=T$ directly, without using a decrement to an artificial suffix.

Apply the capped integer first-moment bound to the mass $M-T$ on $i\le h$. The first moment on $i>h$ is $hT+E$. The zero mean gives
$$
0\le hM-\frac{(M-T)^2}{2m}+\frac{M-T}{2}+E,
\qquad
x\ge (1-t)^2-\rho t-\frac{2m}{M^2}(E-T).
$$

Choose an index $p$ at which $w_p=m$. First suppose $p\le s$. For $s<r$, the decrement at $p$ is an actual one, and by concavity of $u^\beta$ it is at least $\beta F_p^{\beta-1}m\ge\beta M^{\beta-1}m$. Monotonicity of the actual decrements allows this as $c$ from $s$ onward. Therefore
$$
E-T\le\frac{d+1}{d+2}\frac{M^{1-\beta}T^{1+\beta}}m,
\qquad
x\ge1-3t+t\left(t-2\frac{d+1}{d+2}t^\beta\right),
$$
where the moment bound also holds when $E=T$, and the bound on $x$ uses $\rho\le1$. The function $v\mapsto v-2(d+1)v^\beta/(d+2)$ on $v\ge0$ has its minimum at $r_d$. Its derivative on $v>0$ is $1-2v^{\beta-1}/(d+2)$, strictly increasing and vanishing at $r_d$. At this point $2(d+1)r_d^\beta/(d+2)=(d+1)r_d$, so the minimum equals $-d r_d$. Thus $x\ge1-(3+d r_d)t=1-t/\kappa_d$, a contradiction.

Next suppose $p>s$. For $0\le i\le h$, the root at $s$ is at least the root at $i$ by concavity between $i$ and the maximum at $p$. Consequently $(h+1)w_s\ge F_0-T$. The positive normalized masses $w_i/M$ have concave $d$th roots and integer mean zero, so the closed-tail theorem gives $F_0>M((d+1)/(d+2))^{d+1}>M/3$. The last inequality follows from $\log(1+1/(d+1))<1/(d+1)$ and $e<3$, the latter following from $k!\ge 2^{k-1}$ for $k\ge2$, strictly at $k=3$, in the exponential series. In this case $s<r$, so the decrement at $s$ is at least $\beta T^{\beta-1}w_s$ by concavity of $u^\beta$. Use this as $c$ and substitute $(h+1)w_s\ge F_0-T>M/3-T>0$ to obtain
$$
E-T\le\frac{d+1}{d+2}\frac{T^2}{w_s}\le\frac{(h+1)T^2}{M/3-T}.
$$
The common normalized moment bound, $2(h+1)\rho=x+\rho$ and $\rho\le x$ give
$$
\begin{aligned}
x &\ge (1-t)^2-\rho t-\frac{(x+\rho)t^2}{1/3-t}
   \ge (1-t)^2-x t-\frac{2xt^2}{1/3-t},\\
x &\ge \frac{(1-t)^2}{1+t+2t^2/(1/3-t)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0<t<3/10$ the denominator is positive, and
$$
(1-t)^2-\left(1-\frac{10t}3\right)\left(1+t+\frac{2t^2}{1/3-t}\right)
=\frac{t}{3(1/3-t)}\left[7\left(t-\frac4{21}\right)^2+\frac5{63}\right]>0.
$$
Thus $x>1-10t/3$, whereas the assumed failure gives $x<1-t/\kappa_d<1-10t/3$. This also is a contradiction. The left-tail bound follows by reflecting the indices.

For the geometric consequence, the Minkowski interpolation of two fibers lies in the fiber at the interpolated base by convexity of $C$. By Brunn–Minkowski, as in the geometric proof of the closed-tail theorem, the positive integer fiber masses are consecutive and their $d$th roots are concave. The support is finite by compactness. Translating the integer base mean $j$ to zero gives $\sum_i (i-j)w_i=0$. The two scalar tail sums after translation are exactly the original mixed volumes in the two stated outer regions.
