## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Let \(k\ge1\) and \(T\ge0\) be integers. For each \(1\le i\le k\), let
$$
g_i:\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\cup\lbrace +\infty\rbrace
$$
be even, with \(g_i(0)=0\), and suppose its finite-value support \(I_i\) is an integer interval containing zero. Its finite forward differences are assumed nondecreasing, and \(g_i(j)\ge|j|\) at every finite site. Supports may be finite, infinite, or singletons. Let \(f_0,\ldots,f_T\) be positive, nonincreasing, and logconcave:
$$
f_t^2\ge f_{t-1}f_{t+1}\qquad(1\le t<T).
$$
Set \(f_t=0\) for integers \(t>T\). Define the complete original table by
$$
\Gamma(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x_i),\qquad
a_{\mathbf x}=
\begin{cases}
f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)},&\Gamma(\mathbf x)\le T,\\
0,&\Gamma(\mathbf x)>T\text{ or }\Gamma(\mathbf x)=+\infty.
\end{cases}
$$
Use the exact finite-table notation and energy of accepted `0cd230a98ae241c3`:
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_a&=\lbrace \mathbf x:a_{\mathbf x}>0\rbrace,&
M_a&=\sum_{\mathbf x}a_{\mathbf x},&a_{\max}&=\max_{\mathbf x}a_{\mathbf x},\\
A^a_\omega&=\lbrace \mathbf x:a_{\mathbf x}\ge\omega\rbrace,&
r^a_\omega(\mathbf q)&=|A^a_\omega\cap(2\mathbf q-A^a_\omega)|,\\
E(a)&=\sum_{\mathbf q\in D_a}
\max_{0<\omega\le a_{\mathbf q}}\omega^4r^a_\omega(\mathbf q)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the exact alias to the archived research lead is \(E(a)=\mathscr E(a)\), without normalization of weights, mass, levels, or roots. Then
$$
E(a)\ge2^{-96}\,8^{-k}M_a^4.
\tag{1}
$$
Every root maximum is attained at a positive attained table value, including all ties. The assertion includes finite profile caps, hard coordinate endpoints, singleton coordinates, complete singleton support, and unbounded coordinate supports. The locked symbols \(n,d,C,W,h_S,c_0\), and the canonical maximum potential \(B\), retain their original meanings and are not auxiliary objects in this theorem.

## Proof

At every positive site, \(\Gamma(\mathbf x)\ge\sum_i|x_i|\). Hence \(D_a\) is finite, contains \(\mathbf0\), and has positive finite mass. Nonincrease of the profile gives \(a_{\max}=f_0\). If \(0<\omega\le a_{\mathbf q}\), take the smallest attained positive table value \(\widehat\omega\) at least \(\omega\). Then
$$
\omega\le\widehat\omega\le a_{\mathbf q},\qquad
A^a_{\widehat\omega}=A^a_\omega,
$$
so replacing \(\omega\) by \(\widehat\omega\) weakly increases the energy summand. This proves finite attainment with weak levels and ties. If \(D_a=\lbrace \mathbf0\rbrace\), then \(E(a)=f_0^4=M_a^4\), proving (1). This includes \(T=0\). We may therefore assume \(T\ge1\).

We first establish the small-dimension input internally. Linearly interpolate each \(g_i\) between its adjacent finite integer sites and extend by infinity outside their real interval. Denote this extension by \(\widetilde g_i\); for singleton support its finite domain is just \(\lbrace 0\rbrace\). The nondecreasing finite differences give nondecreasing slopes of the interpolant. Each secant slope is an average of these slopes, so successive secant slopes are ordered, which is the convexity inequality. Extension by infinity preserves convexity because the finite domain is an interval. Thus \(\widetilde g_i\) is convex, nonnegative, zero at zero, and agrees with \(g_i\) at integer sites. This argument also works on unbounded intervals, since any real segment involved meets only finitely many unit intervals.

For \(0<\omega\le f_0\), put
$$
t_\omega=\max\lbrace t\in\lbrace 0,\ldots,T\rbrace:f_t\ge\omega\rbrace.
$$
The exact weak level is \(A^a_\omega=\lbrace \mathbf x:\Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t_\omega\rbrace\), including tied profile values. If two sites lie in this level and their midpoint is integral, interval support and convexity of the extensions give
$$
\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\mathbf x+\mathbf y}{2}\right)
\le\frac{\Gamma(\mathbf x)+\Gamma(\mathbf y)}2\le t_\omega.
$$
Every positive level is therefore integer-midpoint-closed. Levels above \(f_0\) are empty. The table satisfies all finite-table hypotheses of accepted `0cd230a98ae241c3`, with unchanged \(k,a,D_a,M_a,a_{\max},A^a_\omega,r^a_\omega,E(a)\). In its notation,
$$
\chi(a)=\frac1{M_a}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}
a_{\mathbf x}\log\frac{f_0}{a_{\mathbf x}},\qquad
E(a)\ge\frac{512}{19683(1+\chi(a))^3}\,8^{-k}M_a^4.
\tag{2}
$$
Only this accepted finite-table conclusion is imported here.

To bound the spread, let \(\phi\) be the piecewise linear interpolation of \(-\log(f_t/f_0)\) on \([0,T]\). Its successive slopes are \(\log(f_t/f_{t+1})\), which are nonnegative by nonincrease and nondecreasing by logconcavity. Hence \(\phi\) is convex and nondecreasing, with \(\phi(0)=0\). Normalize only an auxiliary table by \(u_{\mathbf x}=a_{\mathbf x}/f_0\), and put \(M_u=M_a/f_0\). On \(D_a\),
$$
u_{\mathbf x}=e^{-\phi(\Gamma(\mathbf x))}.
$$
Round toward zero in each coordinate by
$$
F(\mathbf x)_i=\operatorname{sgn}(x_i)\lfloor|x_i|/2\rfloor,
\qquad \operatorname{sgn}(0)=0.
$$
For \(x_i\ne0\), this site is \(\lambda x_i\) for \(0\le\lambda\le1/2\), and is supported because \(I_i\) contains the segment of integer sites between zero and \(x_i\). Convexity and \(g_i(0)=0\) give
$$
g_i(F(\mathbf x)_i)\le\lambda g_i(x_i)\le\tfrac12g_i(x_i).
$$
The same inequality holds for \(x_i=0\). Thus \(F(D_a)\subseteq D_a\), \(\Gamma(F(\mathbf x))\le\Gamma(\mathbf x)/2\), and
$$
\phi(\Gamma(F(\mathbf x)))
\le\phi(\Gamma(\mathbf x)/2)
\le\tfrac12\phi(\Gamma(\mathbf x)),\qquad
\sqrt{u_{\mathbf x}}\le u_{F(\mathbf x)}.
\tag{3}
$$
All profile arguments in this display belong to \([0,T]\). In one coordinate, zero has preimages \(-1,0,1\), a positive integer \(j\) has preimages \(2j,2j+1\), and a negative integer \(j\) has preimages \(2j,2j-1\). Consequently each vector has at most \(3^k\) preimages, even after restricting the domain to \(D_a\). Summing (3) yields
$$
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}\sqrt{u_{\mathbf x}}
\le3^k M_u.
$$
Under the full original probability law \(a_{\mathbf x}/M_a=u_{\mathbf x}/M_u\), Jensen's inequality for the exponential now gives
$$
e^{\chi(a)/2}
\le\frac1{M_u}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}
u_{\mathbf x}e^{\frac12\log(1/u_{\mathbf x})}
=\frac1{M_u}\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}\sqrt{u_{\mathbf x}}
\le3^k.
$$
Thus, without importing any spread assertion from the archived lead,
$$
\chi(a)\le2k\log3,\qquad
\frac{E(a)}{M_a^4}
\ge\frac{512}{19683(1+2k\log3)^3}\,8^{-k}.
\tag{4}
$$

We next derive the large-dimension mechanism from the scalar accepted fact. For \(s>0\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i(s)&=\sum_{j\in I_i}e^{-s g_i(j)},\\
K_{i,s}(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{2s}{3}\bigl(g_i(j+v)+g_i(j-v)\bigr)\right],\\
\mathcal P_i(s)&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}K_{i,s}(j)^3,\qquad
Z(s)=\prod_iZ_i(s),\qquad
\mathcal P(s)=\prod_i\mathcal P_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
A summand with an infinite endpoint is zero. At every \(u>0\),
$$
1\le Z_i(u)\le1+2\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-uj}
=\frac{1+e^{-u}}{1-e^{-u}}<\infty.
\tag{6}
$$
Hence accepted `1dc18246b40e64c7` applies to each coordinate at every positive rate. The precise aliases to that fact are
$$
g\mapsto g_i,\quad I\mapsto I_i,\quad
Z_s\mapsto Z_i(s),\quad K_s\mapsto K_{i,s},\quad
\mathcal P_s\mapsto\mathcal P_i(s).
$$
Its rate is unchanged, including the kernel coefficient \(2s/3\). In particular,
$$
1\le\mathcal P_i(s)<\infty,\qquad
\frac{\mathcal P(s)}{Z(s)^4}\ge6^{-k}.
\tag{7}
$$
For supported roots and finite endpoints, use the further exact aliases
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{i,j}&=\lbrace v:j+v,j-v\in I_i\rbrace,\\
e_{i,j}(v)&=\frac{g_i(j+v)+g_i(j-v)}2-g_i(j),\\
b_{i,j}(v)&=\frac{g_i(j+v)-g_i(j-v)}2,\\
H_{i,s}(j)&=\sum_{v\in I_{i,j}}e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,j}(v)},\qquad
Q_{i,s}(j)=\frac{K_{i,s}(j)^3}{\mathcal P_i(s)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
Thus \(I_j,e_j,b_j,H_s\) of the accepted fact become \(I_{i,j},e_{i,j},b_{i,j},H_{i,s}\), respectively. Its root variable is called \(J_i\) and its displacement \(V_i\) here. Their laws are exactly
$$
\mathbb P(J_i=j)=Q_{i,s}(j),\qquad
\mathbb P(V_i=v\mid J_i=j)
=\frac{e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,j}(v)}}{H_{i,s}(j)}\quad(v\in I_{i,j}).
\tag{9}
$$
No profile tilt or modified normalizer is used. The accepted fact gives \(e_{i,j}\ge0\),
$$
1\le H_{i,s}(j)<\infty,\qquad
K_{i,s}(j)=e^{-(4s/3)g_i(j)}H_{i,s}(j)>0\quad(j\in I_i),
\tag{10}
$$
and zero kernel off \(I_i\). With \(R_{\mathrm{aux}}=1024\), its moment bound gives
$$
\mathbb E\!\left[g_i(J_i)^2+e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2\right]
\le\frac{4608}{e^2s^2}<\frac{R_{\mathrm{aux}}}{s^2}.
\tag{11}
$$
Indeed \(e^2>1+2+2=5\), and \(4608/5<1024\). These are the only scalar mathematical conclusions imported from `1dc18246b40e64c7`.

Every supported root has positive probability, so (11) ensures finite conditional second moments at every such root. The domain \(I_{i,j}\) is invariant under negation of \(v\); its excess is even and its imbalance is odd. The conditional law (9) is even, so the conditional mean of the integrable imbalance is zero. Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{i,s}(j)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,j}(V_i)\mid J_i=j],\\
v^e_{i,s}(j)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,j}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=j],\qquad
v^b_{i,s}(j)=\mathbb E[b_{i,j}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=j],\\
\beta_{i,s}(j)&=g_i(j)+m_{i,s}(j),\\
\mu_i(s)&=\mathbb E_{Q_{i,s}}\beta_{i,s}(J_i),\qquad
\mu(s)=\sum_i\mu_i(s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
Conditional Cauchy-Schwarz and (11) imply
$$
\mathbb E_{Q_{i,s}}\beta_{i,s}(J_i)^2
\le\frac{2R_{\mathrm{aux}}}{s^2},\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_{i,s}}[v^e_{i,s}(J_i)+v^b_{i,s}(J_i)]
\le\frac{R_{\mathrm{aux}}}{s^2}.
\tag{13}
$$
In particular every mean in (12) is finite.

We justify the rate derivative needed to select a cutoff. On the fixed countable tuple domain
$$
\mathcal T_i=\lbrace (j,v_1,v_2,v_3):j\in I_i,\ v_\ell\in I_{i,j}\ (1\le\ell\le3)\rbrace,
$$
define the finite nonnegative energy
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_i(j,v_1,v_2,v_3)
&=\frac23\sum_{\ell=1}^3[g_i(j+v_\ell)+g_i(j-v_\ell)]\\
&=4g_i(j)+\frac43\sum_{\ell=1}^3e_{i,j}(v_\ell).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
Expansion of the kernel cube by nonnegative summation gives
$$
\mathcal P_i(s)=\sum_{\mathcal T_i}e^{-s\Xi_i}.
\tag{15}
$$
The normalized tuple weights have root marginal \(Q_{i,s}\). By the second line of (14), their three conditional displacements are independent and each has exactly (9). Thus their mean energy is \(4\mu_i(s)\).

For any compact positive rate interval \([s_0,s_1]\), the differentiated summands satisfy
$$
\Xi_i e^{-s\Xi_i}
\le\frac2{e s_0}e^{-(s_0/2)\Xi_i}\qquad(s\in[s_0,s_1]),
$$
because the maximum of \(x e^{-s_0x/2}\) for \(x\ge0\) is \(2/(e s_0)\). The sum of the dominating terms is \(2\mathcal P_i(s_0/2)/(e s_0)<\infty\). The original terms are dominated by their summable values at \(s_0\). The mean value theorem applies the derivative bound to difference quotients, proving termwise differentiation; the same domination proves continuity of the derivative. It follows from (15) and the exact tuple mean that
$$
\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathcal P_i(s)=-4\mu_i(s),\qquad
\frac14\frac{d}{ds}\log\mathcal P(s)=-\mu(s),
\tag{16}
$$
and \(\mu\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\). This uses the full auxiliary tuple domains independently of the original cap \(T\).

Choose the absolute auxiliary constants
$$
B_{\mathrm{aux}}=128,\qquad D_{\mathrm{aux}}=5B_{\mathrm{aux}}=640,
\qquad s_{\max}=12\log2,\qquad N_{\mathrm{aux}}=2^{28}.
$$
The constant \(B_{\mathrm{aux}}\) has no connection to the canonical maximum potential \(B\). For \(s>0\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(s)&=\max_{0\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z}
\left(\log\frac{f_t}{f_0}+st\right),\\
\mathcal J(s)&=\left\lbrace t\in\lbrace 0,\ldots,T\rbrace:
\log\frac{f_t}{f_0}+st=\mathcal F(s)\right\rbrace,\\
\theta(s)&=\mu(s)+\frac{D_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
The original table is not normalized. For every positive site,
$$
a_{\mathbf x}\le f_0e^{\mathcal F(s)}e^{-s\Gamma(\mathbf x)}.
$$
Summing its complete mass and enlarging to \(\prod_iI_i\) gives, by nonnegative product summation,
$$
M_a\le f_0e^{\mathcal F(s)}Z(s)
=f_t e^{st}Z(s)\qquad(t\in\mathcal J(s)).
\tag{18}
$$

Minimize the continuous function
$$
\Psi(s)=\mathcal F(s)+\tfrac14\log\mathcal P(s)
-D_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}\log s
\tag{19}
$$
on \(0<s\le s_{\max}\). The zero profile index gives \(\mathcal F(s)\ge0\), and the all-zero tuple gives \(\mathcal P(s)\ge1\). Hence \(\Psi(s)\to+\infty\) as \(s\downarrow0\). Excluding a small interval near zero on which \(\Psi(s)>\Psi(s_{\max})\), and minimizing on the remaining compact interval, shows that a positive minimizing rate exists.

Write \(t_-(s)=\min\mathcal J(s)\) and \(t_+(s)=\max\mathcal J(s)\). A maximum of finitely many affine functions has left derivative the smallest active slope and right derivative the largest active slope: inactive lines have positive gaps at the given point and cannot win at sufficiently small increments; among active lines the smallest slope wins to the left and the largest to the right. Equations (16) and (19) therefore give
$$
\Psi'_-(s)=t_-(s)-\theta(s),\qquad
\Psi'_+(s)=t_+(s)-\theta(s).
$$
At an interior minimum these derivatives are respectively nonpositive and nonnegative. Thus \(t_-\le\theta\le t_+\). The successive differences of
$$
t\longmapsto\log(f_t/f_0)+st
$$
are nonincreasing by profile logconcavity. Its linear interpolation is concave, so between two maximizing indices it lies at least on their constant chord and hence equals the maximum. Every intervening integer index is active. Consequently, at an interior minimum,
$$
t_\star=\lceil\theta(s)\rceil\in\mathcal J(s),\qquad
\theta(s)\le t_\star\le\theta(s)+1.
\tag{20}
$$
At a minimum at \(s=s_{\max}\), the left derivative is nonpositive, since \(\Psi(s_{\max})-\Psi(s_{\max}-h)\le0\) for small \(h>0\). We may then choose
$$
t_\star=t_-(s_{\max})\in\mathcal J(s_{\max}),\qquad
t_\star\le\theta(s_{\max}).
\tag{21}
$$
These two alternatives exhaust the minimizing rates and include every possible tie and finite cap.

Consider the interior alternative. Let \(Q_s\) be the product of the root laws \(Q_{i,s}\). Define its set of good roots by
$$
\mathcal G_s=\left\lbrace \mathbf j\in\prod_iI_i:
\left|\sum_i\beta_{i,s}(j_i)-\mu(s)\right|
\le\frac{B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s,\quad
\sum_i[v^e_{i,s}(j_i)+v^b_{i,s}(j_i)]
\le\frac{B_{\mathrm{aux}}^2 k}{s^2}\right\rbrace.
\tag{22}
$$
Independence and (13) bound the variance of the first sum by \(2R_{\mathrm{aux}}k/s^2\); the mean of the second sum is at most \(R_{\mathrm{aux}}k/s^2\). Chebyshev, Markov, and a union bound imply
$$
Q_s(\mathcal G_s)\ge1-\frac{3R_{\mathrm{aux}}}{B_{\mathrm{aux}}^2}
=\frac{13}{16}>\frac12.
\tag{23}
$$
The means \(m_{i,s}\) are nonnegative, so every good root has
$$
\Gamma(\mathbf j)\le\sum_i\beta_{i,s}(j_i)
\le\mu(s)+\frac{B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s.
$$
The domination of \(\Gamma\) over the integer \(\ell_1\) norm also makes \(\mathcal G_s\) finite.

Fix such a root and sample independent coordinate displacements with (9), conditional on it. Put
$$
m_s(\mathbf j)=\sum_i m_{i,s}(j_i),\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)=\sum_i e_{i,j_i}(v_i),\qquad
b_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)=\sum_i b_{i,j_i}(v_i).
$$
Their latter two conditional means are \(m_s(\mathbf j)\) and zero. Independence across coordinates bounds the sum of their variances by the second sum in (22). Thus Chebyshev on each sum and addition of failure probabilities show that
$$
\mathcal V_{\mathbf j}=\left\lbrace \mathbf v\in\prod_iI_{i,j_i}:
|\varepsilon_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)-m_s(\mathbf j)|
\le\frac{2B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s,\quad

\tag{24}
$$
has conditional probability at least \(3/4\), and hence at least \(1/2\). Indeed the total failure bound is at most
$$
\frac{B_{\mathrm{aux}}^2k/s^2}{4B_{\mathrm{aux}}^2k/s^2}=\frac14.
$$
No independence between excess and imbalance within one coordinate is required.

The endpoint identities are exact:
$$
\Gamma(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v)
=\Gamma(\mathbf j)+\varepsilon_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)\pm b_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v).
$$
On (22) and (24), these give

\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v)
&\le\Gamma(\mathbf j)+m_s(\mathbf j)
+\frac{4B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s\\
&\le\mu(s)+\frac{5B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}s

\tag{25}

The root itself also has \(\Gamma(\mathbf j)\le t_\star\). Nonincrease of the profile places both endpoints and the root in the actual positive level \(A^a_{f_{t_\star}}\). Therefore \(f_{t_\star}\le a_{\mathbf j}\) is a legal threshold for that root's original full maximum. Ties can only enlarge the level.

The injective map \(\mathbf v\mapsto\mathbf j+\mathbf v\) maps (24) into the reflected level at \(\mathbf j\). Consequently
$$
r^a_{f_{t_\star}}(\mathbf j)\ge|\mathcal V_{\mathbf j}|.
$$
This set is finite because its image lies in the finite original level. Its conditional point probabilities are
$$
p_s(\mathbf v\mid\mathbf j)
=\frac{e^{-(4s/3)\varepsilon_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)}}{\prod_i H_{i,s}(j_i)}.

Reciprocal density, the lower excess bound in (24), and its probability at least \(1/2\) yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal V_{\mathbf j}|
&=\left(\prod_iH_{i,s}(j_i)\right)
\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal V_{\mathbf j}}
p_s(\mathbf v\mid\mathbf j)e^{(4s/3)\varepsilon_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)}\\
&\ge\frac12\left(\prod_iH_{i,s}(j_i)\right)

The factorization (10) and the product root law give
$$
\prod_iH_{i,s}(j_i)^3
=e^{4s\Gamma(\mathbf j)}\mathcal P(s)Q_s(\mathbf j).
$$
Using the one allowed threshold at each good root, cubing (26), and then applying the lower bound in (22) and the mass bound (23), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(a)
&\ge f_{t_\star}^4\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathcal G_s}

&\ge\frac18f_{t_\star}^4\mathcal P(s)

\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
Other roots contribute nonnegatively. If this profile value is not attained by the table, the finite-attainment argument at the start gives an equally good or better attained threshold at each used root.

Divide (27) by the fourth power of (18) at the active index. Using (7), (20), and \(s\le s_{\max}\), we find

\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(a)}{M_a^4}
&\ge\frac1{16}e^{-4s(t_\star-\mu(s))-12B_{\mathrm{aux}}\sqrt{k}}

Here \(4D_{\mathrm{aux}}+12B_{\mathrm{aux}}=4096\), and
$$

$$
The rounding loss is at most one integer energy unit, independently of the coordinate cost scales.

It remains to handle the endpoint alternative (21). We prove a high-rate estimate for the same mean used in (16). Convexity of \(\widetilde g_i\) gives
$$
g_i(j+v)+g_i(j-v)\ge2g_i(j)
$$
for admissible endpoints. Evenness also makes \(v-j=-(j-v)\) supported. The midpoint of \(j+v\) and \(v-j\) is \(v\), so \(v\in I_i\) and convexity gives
$$
g_i(j+v)+g_i(j-v)\ge2g_i(v).
$$
Taking half the sum of these two lower bounds in each endpoint pair proves from (14) that
$$
\Xi_i(j,v_1,v_2,v_3)
\ge2g_i(j)+\frac23\sum_{\ell=1}^3g_i(v_\ell).
$$
The tuple domain is contained in \(I_i^4\), so enlarging a nonnegative sum gives for every \(u>0\)
$$
\mathcal P_i(u)\le Z_i(2u)Z_i(2u/3)^3.
\tag{29}
$$
At \(s=s_{\max}=12\log2\), (6) implies
$$
Z_i(s)\le\frac{4097}{4095},\qquad
Z_i(s/3)\le\frac{17}{15}.
$$
In particular (29) at rate \(s/2\) yields

\mathcal P_i(s/2)\le Z_i(s)Z_i(s/3)^3
\le(17/15)^4<2,\qquad
\log Z_i(s)<\frac1{16}.
\tag{30}
$$
The strict power bound follows from \(17^4=83521<101250=2\cdot15^4\). For the logarithm, \(Z_i(s)\le1+2/4095\), and \(\log(1+x)<x\) at \(x>0\), with \(2/4095<1/16\).

The unique tuple with \(\Xi_i=0\) is the all-zero tuple. Indeed each nonnegative endpoint potential in (14) must then vanish, and \(g_i(j)\ge|j|\) makes both endpoints zero in every pair, forcing the root and all displacements to be zero. The elementary maximum of \(x e^{-x/2}\) for \(x\ge0\) is \(2/e\). Apply
$$
x e^{-x}\le(2/e)e^{-x/2}
$$
only to the positive-energy tuples. Equations (15), (16), and (30) give
$$
\begin{aligned}
