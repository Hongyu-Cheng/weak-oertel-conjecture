## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(v_0,\ldots,v_d)$ be a $d$-simplex, $d\ge1$, and let $y\in T$ have barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ satisfying $\max_i\lambda_i\le1/d$. The normalized ordinary halfspace depth, using $d$-volume and closed halfspaces in the affine hull, is
$$
\inf_{H\ni y}\frac{|T\cap H|}{|T|}=\min_i d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j .
$$
If $y$ is interior, every minimizing halfspace has boundary through $y$. Its unique strictly included vertex is $v_i$ for an index attaining $\max_j\lambda_j$, and its boundary meets each edge $[v_i,v_j]$, $j\ne i$, at $(1-d\lambda_j)v_i+d\lambda_j v_j$. Each such index gives a minimizing halfspace. If $y$ is on the boundary of $T$, the depth is zero and the minimizing halfspaces are the outward supporting halfspaces through $y$.

## Proof

Work in the affine hull of $T$. For an interior $y$, tightening a containing halfspace to $y$ strictly decreases its volume if $y$ was strictly inside that halfspace: the removed strip contains an open subset of a ball about $y$ lying in $T$. The volume of the cap through $y$ is continuous in its unit normal by dominated convergence, since its boundary is volume-null. Compactness of the unit sphere gives attainment. For $d\ge2$ every through-$y$ cap is proper at an interior point, and the stationary simplex cap theorem shows that every minimizer strictly includes just one vertex. For $d=1$ every through-interior-point cap already has this form.

For a cap strictly including only $v_i$, write its intercepts as $(1-t_j)v_i+t_jv_j$ with $0<t_j\le1$. The closed cap is the simplex on $v_i$ and the $d$ intercepts, and its defining inequality is $\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j(x)/t_j\le1$, as in accepted fact f75e1f526e17ab44. That fact gives the unique optimizing parameters $t_j=\min(1,\mu_i\lambda_j)$ with $\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j/t_j=1$ and relative volume $\prod_{j\ne i}t_j$. Here $\mu_i=d$ solves the water-level equation, including ties at one. At least one intercept parameter is below one since $\lambda_i>0$. Thus this is a proper cap with exactly one strictly included vertex. Its volume is $d^d\prod_{j\ne i}\lambda_j$. Taking the minimum over $i$ proves the formula and classifies all minimizers, since at an interior point the product is minimized by omitting a largest coordinate.

If $y$ belongs to the boundary of $T$, any facet through $y$ provides an outward supporting halfspace with zero $d$-volume. Conversely, a halfspace of zero cap volume cannot meet the interior of $T$, even on its boundary, since a ball about an interior point would supply positive volume. Density of the interior in $T$ then puts all of $T$ on the closed opposite side. Since $y$ belongs to the given halfspace, its boundary passes through $y$. The product minimum is zero as well, because some $\lambda_k=0$ and there is an index $i\ne k$. In the central region a boundary point must have exactly one zero coordinate and all others $1/d$, so this also covers the facet centroid and all lower-dimensional supporting traces through it. For $d=1$ the central constraint is automatic and the formula includes the endpoints.
