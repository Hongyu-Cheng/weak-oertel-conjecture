## Statement

For each integer $d\ge1$, let $T=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i\le1\}$, $V=1/d!$, $g=(1,\ldots,1)/(d+1)$, $a=10^{-d}$, $r=11/20$ and $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. The hull of the lifts of $A=T/10$, $B=T$ and $C=T$ at bases $-1,0,1$ has exactly these three positive integer fibers, none as large as half of their total mixed volume $M=(2+a)V$. Its mass-$aV$ midpoint measure $\eta=(a/r^d)\mathbf1_{rT}\,dx$ attains the sharp density bound $|A|/|(A+C)/2|$ for uniform endpoint marginals and is the unique midpoint measure satisfying that bound. The centroid of $\mathbf1_B\,dx+[(1+a)/a]\eta$, placed at base zero, is the actual point $(0,hg)$ with $h=(31+11a)/(40+20a)$, independent of the endpoint coupling. The closed halfspace $H=\{(z,x):\sum_i x_i+z\le dh/(d+1)\}$ through this point has mixed mass $V(\Gamma h^d+a)\le(39/40)\Gamma V<\Gamma M/2$. For its base-zero trace $H_0$, the auxiliary cost $|B\cap H_0|+\eta(H_0)$ is at most this same mixed mass.

## Proof

The Minkowski average of the endpoint fibers is $rT\subset T$. Grouping the two endpoint terms in any hull point at base zero therefore gives the exact middle fiber. The integer bases are precisely $-1,0,1$ and the outer bases are extreme. The endpoint planes with their full-dimensional fibers give ambient interior. Their volumes are $aV,V,V$, all strictly below $(2+a)V/2$.

Dilation by ten sends uniform probability on $A$ to uniform probability on $C$, and the midpoint under this coupling is uniform on $rT$. The endpoint root-volume average raised to the $d$th power is $r^dV$, exactly the volume of $rT$. Every midpoint measure of mass $aV$ from these marginals is supported on $rT$. A density bounded above by $a/r^d$ must saturate the bound almost everywhere, since its integral must equal the integral of the bound. The centroid of a midpoint is the average of the endpoint centroids, which is $rg$ for every coupling. The weighted measure has mass $M$ and centroid $[1+(1+a)r]g/(2+a)=hg$. In particular $0<h\le31/40$ and $hg\in T$.

Let $c=d/(d+1)$ and $q=ch$. On the left the cap threshold is $q+1>1/10$ and on the right it is $q-1<0$. The cap includes all of $A$, excludes all of $C$ and cuts out $qT$ from the middle. Its mass divided by $\Gamma V$ is $h^d+a/\Gamma$. Since $c\ge1/2$, this is at most $(31/40)^d+(1/5)^d\le39/40$ for every $d\ge1$. The target divided by $\Gamma V$ is $1+a/2>1$. Finally $\eta(H_0)\le aV$, so the auxiliary cost is bounded by $|qT|+aV$, exactly the actual cap mass.
