## Statement

Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be finite and nonempty. Fix \(c\in\mathbb R\) and \(\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), and assign the strictly positive log-affine weights
\[
w_x=e^{c+\ell\cdot x}\qquad(x\in D).
\]
Put \(W=\sum_{x\in D}w_x\), \(p_x=w_x/W\), and, for \(x\in D\), define the unnormalized and normalized closed-halfspace depths by
\[
\tau_w(x)=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed halfspace}\\x\in H}}
\sum_{y\in D\cap H}w_y,
\qquad
\tau_p(x)=\frac{\tau_w(x)}W.
\]
For a set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^N\), write \(w(B)=\sum_{y\in D\cap B}w_y\).

Suppose that \(D\) is the disjoint union of \(K\ge1\) nonempty chains. For each chain there are an integer \(q\ge0\), a point \(x_0\), and linearly independent vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_q\) such that its points, in order, are
\[
x_s=x_0+\sum_{i=1}^s v_i\qquad(0\le s\le q).
\]
Assume that every prefix has its full negative cube completion in \(D\):
\[
x_s-\sum_{i\in E}v_i\in D
\quad\text{for every }0\le s\le q
\text{ and every }E\subseteq\{s+1,\ldots,q\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then, for every real \(r>0\) and \(X\sim p\),
\[
\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)^r]
\ge \frac25(128K)^{-r}.
\tag{2}
\]
The completion cubes may overlap each other, within one chain or across different chains; only the displayed target chains are required to partition \(D\).

For the rooted-tree consequence, use the support \(A_{m,Q}\) and successor map \(F_m\) of accepted fact `c2b7164e5a46e1b3`, for arbitrary integers \(m,Q\ge1\). Its integer dimension \(n_{\mathrm{tree}}=\sum_{j=1}^Qm^j\) is denoted by \(n\) here; this is only an alias for the same quantity. For any \(\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), set
\[
p_z=\frac{e^{\ell\cdot z}}
{\sum_{x\in A_{m,Q}}e^{\ell\cdot x}}
\qquad(z\in A_{m,Q}),
\]
and let \(\tau_p\) be depth with respect to all closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^n\). Then, for every real \(r>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)^r]
\ge \frac25\,128^{-r}2^{-rn}.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
2^n\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)]\ge\frac1{320},
\qquad
4^n\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)^2]\ge\frac1{40960}.
\tag{4}
\]
The constants are independent of \(m,Q,n\), the chain lengths, and \(\ell\).

## Proof

We first prove a one-chain estimate. Fix one of the chains, write
\[
a_s=w_{x_s},\qquad M=\sum_{s=0}^q a_s,
\]
and set \(\delta=1/128\) and \(\rho=\sqrt2\). Call an index \(s\) bad if \(\tau_w(x_s)<\delta M\). Every closed halfspace containing \(x_s\) contains the atom at \(x_s\), including when \(x_s\) is on its boundary. Hence \(a_s\le\tau_w(x_s)\), so every bad index satisfies
\[
a_s<\delta M.
\tag{5}
\]

Choose \(k\in\{0,\ldots,q\}\) so that
\[
\sum_{t=k+1}^q a_t<\delta M
\le\sum_{t=k}^q a_t.
\tag{6}
\]
Such a \(k\) exists because the tail after \(q\) is zero and the tail beginning at zero is \(M\). The total weight of bad indices at or after \(k\) is less than \(2\delta M\): the tail after \(k\) has weight less than \(\delta M\), and the additional point \(x_k\), if bad, has weight less than \(\delta M\) by (5).

For the bad indices before \(k\), use the disjoint bins
\[
L_b<a_s\le U_b,
\qquad
U_b=\delta M\rho^{-b},
\qquad
L_b=U_b/\rho,
\qquad b=0,1,2,\ldots.
\tag{7}
\]
They contain every positive number strictly below \(\delta M\). We claim that bin \(b\) contains at most
\[
R_b=16+2b
\tag{8}
\]
bad indices before \(k\).

Suppose otherwise. The earliest of \(R_b+1\) such indices has at least \(R_b\) later indices in the same bin before \(k\). Thus there are indices
\[
s=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_R<k,
\qquad R=R_b,
\tag{9}
\]
all in bin \(b\), with \(s\) bad. By badness there is a closed halfspace \(H\) containing \(x_s\) whose total \(D\)-weight is strictly less than \(\delta M\). It cannot contain every chain point \(x_t\) with \(t\ge k\), since those points have total weight at least \(\delta M\) by (6). Choose \(t\ge k\) with \(x_t\notin H\), put \(t_{R+1}=t\), and define
\[
d_i=x_{t_i}-x_{t_{i-1}}
=\sum_{j=t_{i-1}+1}^{t_i}v_j
\qquad(1\le i\le R+1).
\tag{10}
\]

Consider the \((R+1)\)-dimensional affine cube
\[
\mathcal C=
\left\{x_s-\sum_{i\in I}d_i:
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,R+1\}\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
For each \(I\), the sum in (11) is the sum of the original increments \(v_j\) over a union of disjoint index blocks contained in \(\{s+1,\ldots,t\}\). Thus (1), applied at the prefix \(s\), puts every vertex of \(\mathcal C\) in \(D\). The vectors \(d_i\) are linearly independent: in a relation \(\sum_i\lambda_i d_i=0\), every original vector \(v_j\) in the \(i\)-th block has coefficient \(\lambda_i\), so linear independence of the \(v_j\) forces every \(\lambda_i=0\). Consequently the \(2^{R+1}\) vertices in (11) are distinct.

The center of this cube is
\[
x_s-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{R+1}d_i
=x_s-\frac12(x_t-x_s)
=\frac{3x_s-x_t}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Write the closed halfspace as \(H=\{x:L(x)\ge0\}\) for a nonconstant affine functional \(L\). Since \(x_s\in H\) and \(x_t\notin H\),
\[
L\!\left(\frac{3x_s-x_t}{2}\right)
=\frac{3L(x_s)-L(x_t)}2>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Pair the vertices of \(\mathcal C\) antipodally about its center. The two values of \(L\) in each pair sum to twice the positive value in (13), so at least one vertex of every pair lies in the closed halfspace. This retains all equality vertices and proves that \(H\) contains at least \(2^R\) distinct vertices of \(\mathcal C\).

Log-affinity gives, for every \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,R+1\}\),
\[
w_{x_s-\sum_{i\in I}d_i}
=a_s\prod_{i\in I}e^{-\ell\cdot d_i}
=a_s\prod_{i\in I}\frac{a_{t_{i-1}}}{a_{t_i}}.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(1\le i\le R\), both endpoint weights in the ratio lie in bin \(b\), so
\[
\frac{a_{t_{i-1}}}{a_{t_i}}>\frac{L_b}{U_b}=\frac1\rho.
\]
For the final ratio, \(a_{t_R}>L_b\) and \(a_t\le M\), so \(a_{t_R}/a_t>L_b/M\). These lower bounds remain valid when their factors are omitted from (14), because \(1\ge1/\rho\) and \(1\ge L_b/M\). Together with \(a_s>L_b\), every cube vertex therefore has weight strictly greater than
\[
\frac{L_b^2}{M\rho^R}.
\tag{15}
\]
The \(2^R\) vertices forced into \(H\) now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
w(H)
&>2^R\frac{L_b^2}{M\rho^R}\\
&=\delta^2M\rho^{-2b-2}
\left(\frac2\rho\right)^{16+2b}\\
&=\delta^2M\rho^{14}
=\delta M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Here \(2/\rho=\rho\), \(\rho^{14}=128\), and \(\delta=1/128\). This contradicts the choice of \(H\), and proves the bin-count claim (8).

The total weight of all bad indices on this chain is consequently less than
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\delta M+\sum_{b=0}^\infty R_bU_b
&=\delta M\left(
2+\frac{16}{1-1/\rho}
+\frac{2/\rho}{(1-1/\rho)^2}
\right)\\
&=\delta M(42+22\sqrt2)\\
&<\frac{75}{128}M
<\frac35M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The penultimate strict inequality follows from \(\sqrt2<3/2\), and the last from \(375<384\). Thus the complementary good indices have total weight greater than \(2M/5\). We have proved the one-chain estimate
\[
\sum_{\substack{0\le s\le q\\
\tau_w(x_s)\ge M/128}}a_s\ge\frac25M.
\tag{18}
\]
The proof includes \(q=0\): then the only atom itself already gives depth at least its chain mass.

For every real \(r>0\), (18) gives on a chain of mass \(M\)
\[
\sum_{s=0}^q a_s\tau_w(x_s)^r
\ge\frac25\,128^{-r}M^{r+1}.
\tag{19}
\]
Let the masses of the \(K\) disjoint target chains be \(M_1,\ldots,M_K\). Their sum is exactly \(W\), even though their completion cubes may overlap. Summing (19) adds each target contribution \(w_x\tau_w(x)^r\) exactly once and does not add completion-cube masses from different targets. Since \(r+1>1\), convexity gives
\[
\sum_{\alpha=1}^K M_\alpha^{r+1}
\ge K^{-r}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^K M_\alpha\right)^{r+1}
=K^{-r}W^{r+1}.
\tag{20}
\]
Finally,
\[
\mathbb E_p[\tau_p(X)^r]
=\frac1{W^{r+1}}\sum_{x\in D}w_x\tau_w(x)^r.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (19)--(21) prove (2).

It remains to check the rooted-tree hypotheses exactly. We use the canonical construction and notation of accepted fact `c2b7164e5a46e1b3`, with its \(n_{\mathrm{tree}}\) renamed \(n\) as stated above. For a Boolean tree vector \(\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^n\), begin at the root and invert \(F_m\) on each successively visited child block of \(\mathbf j\), stopping when the current block is not in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\) or depth \(Q\) is reached. Since \(F_m\) is injective, every predecessor is unique. If the selected coordinates are
\[
u_1,\ldots,u_{h_-(\mathbf j)},
\]
then they are distinct tree coordinates, their depths are respectively \(1,\ldots,h_-(\mathbf j)\), and \(\mathbf j_{u_i}=1\), because \(F_m\) changes its selected coordinate from zero to one. In the notation of the accepted fact, define
\[
z^+_{\mathbf j,s}=\mathbf j+\sum_{i=1}^s \mathbf e_{u_i}
\qquad(0\le s\le h_-(\mathbf j)).
\tag{22}
\]

We verify both directions of the exact partition. For fixed \(\mathbf j,s\), the vector
\[
\beta_{\mathbf j,s}=\mathbf j-\sum_{i=1}^s \mathbf e_{u_i}
\]
is Boolean. At each of the first \(s\) visited child blocks it is precisely the unique predecessor under \(F_m\) of the corresponding block of \(\mathbf j\); changes at other visited depths lie in different child blocks. Its forward successor path therefore follows \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\). The canonical tree construction gives
\[
z_s(\beta_{\mathbf j,s})
=\beta_{\mathbf j,s}+2\sum_{i=1}^s \mathbf e_{u_i}
=z^+_{\mathbf j,s}\in A_{m,Q}.
\tag{23}
\]
Conversely, take any canonical point \(z_s(\beta)\in A_{m,Q}\), and replace each of its \(s\) coordinates equal to two by one. This produces a Boolean vector \(\mathbf j\). At every visited block, the block of \(\mathbf j\) is \(F_m\) of the corresponding block of \(\beta\), so inverse reconstruction from \(\mathbf j\) recovers the same selected path through depth \(s\), and (22) recovers the original point. The vector \(\mathbf j\) is uniquely determined by replacing twos by ones, and \(s\) is uniquely determined by the number of twos. Hence
\[
A_{m,Q}
=\bigsqcup_{\mathbf j\in\{0,1\}^n}
\{z^+_{\mathbf j,s}:0\le s\le h_-(\mathbf j)\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus there are exactly \(K=2^n\) disjoint target chains, including chains of length zero.

Fix one such chain, a prefix \(s\), and a set \(E\subseteq\{s+1,\ldots,h_-(\mathbf j)\}\). Put
\[
\beta_{\mathbf j,s,E}
=\mathbf j-\sum_{i=1}^s \mathbf e_{u_i}-\sum_{i\in E}\mathbf e_{u_i}.
\tag{25}
\]
This is Boolean because the \(u_i\) are distinct and every selected coordinate of \(\mathbf j\) is one. The changes indexed by \(E\) occur at depths strictly greater than \(s\), hence in child blocks different from the first \(s\) visited blocks. Exactly as in (23), the forward path of \(\beta_{\mathbf j,s,E}\) follows \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\). Therefore
\[
z_s(\beta_{\mathbf j,s,E})
=z^+_{\mathbf j,s}-\sum_{i\in E}\mathbf e_{u_i}
\in A_{m,Q}.
\tag{26}
\]
Under the general theorem, the exact notation map is \(D=A_{m,Q}\), \(x_s=z^+_{\mathbf j,s}\), and \(v_i=\mathbf e_{u_i}\) on the chain rooted at \(\mathbf j\). Thus (26) is precisely the completion condition (1). The increments are distinct standard basis vectors and hence linearly independent. Equations (24) and (26) verify every hypothesis of the general theorem. Applying (2) with \(K=2^n\) gives (3); substituting \(r=1\) and \(r=2\) gives (4). Since the proof used only log-affinity, no bound on \(\ell\) or on any weight ratio is required.

The last conclusion is deliberately unlifted. In a height lift, the completion points available at a tree prefix are horizontal points at one common height. If a closed ambient halfspace contains \((x_s,h_s)\) but excludes a later point \((x_t,h_t)\) because of its height coefficient, this does not imply that it contains the center \(((3x_s-x_t)/2,h_s)\) of the same-height horizontal completion cube. The affine point forced by the contain/exclude comparison has height \((3h_s-h_t)/2\), where no completion cube is known. Thus the antipodal half-cube step (12)--(16) does not apply, and neither the theorem nor its rooted-tree application proves a bound for arbitrary joint horizontal and height tilts on lifted supports.
