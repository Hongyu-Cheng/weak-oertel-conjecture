## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body, $d\ge1$, with volume $V$, raw ordinary halfspace depth $f$, peak $A=\max_Kf>0$ and levels $W_s=\{x\in K:f(x)\ge s\}$. For every measurable $H$, $\int_0^A|W_s\cap H|\,ds=\int_{K\cap H} f(x)\,dx$. For every $0\le s\le A$ and any maximizer $g_*$,
$$
g_*+[1-(s/A)^{1/d}](K-g_*)\subseteq W_s,\qquad
V[1-(s/A)^{1/d}]^d\le |W_s|\le V-2s .
$$
Thus $VA/\binom{2d}{d}\le\int_K f\le V^2/4$. Both bounds are exact for intervals. The lower factor is also attained for the larger class of potentials with nonnegative concave $d$th root and zero boundary values. Any probability mixture of the uniform laws on $W_s$ for $0\le s<A$ that equals uniform volume on $K$ is concentrated at $s=0$. The same holds if $s=A$ is allowed with its law defined as the Dirac mass at the unique deepest point. In particular, for triangle fibers the layer-cake total is $(7/54)\sum_z|K_z|^2$.

## Proof

Use 4e868f774de77608 on the product $[0,1]\times K$ to obtain concavity of $f^{1/d}$. The same compact-sphere cap formula and dominated convergence for a fixed fiber give continuity of $f$ on $K$. It is zero on the boundary by a supporting cap and positive in the interior by a centered ball. The maximum therefore exists and is positive. For the peak-law assertion, the ordinary-depth uniqueness argument at the start of 9e6b5c9eeaab615d applies directly to $K$ (equivalently, take all bodies there equal to $K$ and $s=t=1/2$).

Fubini applied to $\mathbf1_{\{s\le f(x)\}}$ gives the layer-cake identity. Root concavity along the segment from $g_*$ to any $x\in K$ gives the lower inclusion and volume bound. Fix a unit direction and let $Y$ be the projection of a uniform point of $K$. Its tail $T(t)=\Pr(Y\ge t)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing on its support interval. Hyperplanes have zero volume, and each intermediate nonempty open slab meets the interior, proving these properties. Hence $T(Y)$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. At a point of $W_s$, the two opposing through-point caps both have volume at least $s$, so $T(Y)$ is in $[s/V,1-s/V]$. In particular $A\le V/2$, and the length of that interval gives the upper volume bound. Integration gives $V^2/4$ as the upper moment bound; for the lower one use $t=(s/A)^{1/d}$ to obtain $VA\int_0^1 d t^{d-1}(1-t)^d\,dt=VA/\binom{2d}{d}$. On an interval, $f$ is the nearer endpoint distance, so $|W_s|=V-2s$ and both integral bounds agree. For the larger potential class, take $f(x)=A(1-p(x-g_*))^d$, with $p$ the convex gauge of $K-g_*$ for an interior $g_*$. Its root is concave, and all its levels are exactly the homothetic copies in the lower inclusion. The sum over triangle fibers follows by applying triangle-exact-inner-level-volume-and-mean to each triangle with its own area.

For a probability law $\eta$ of levels in $[0,A)$, the mixed measure has density $G(x)=\int_{[0,f(x)]}|W_s|^{-1}\,d\eta(s)$ on $K$, by Tonelli. Every $|W_s|$ is positive for $s<A$ by the lower bound, and these volumes are uniformly bounded below for $s$ in a fixed interval $[0,r]$ with $r<A$. For each small $\rho>0$ the set of interior points with $f(x)<\rho$ has positive volume: continuity at any boundary point gives a neighborhood with this inequality, and a segment to any interior ball gives positive volume there. If $G=1/V$ almost everywhere, choose such interior points from that equality set with depths tending to zero. The boundedness for $s\le r$ and continuity from above for $\eta$ give $1/V=\eta(\{0\})/V$. An allowed atom at the peak would instead give positive mass to a singleton, impossible under uniform volume. Thus every uniform mixture is concentrated at zero.
