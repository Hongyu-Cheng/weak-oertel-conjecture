## Statement

For a mixed-integer convex body \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), write
\[
 C_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 D_+(C):=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W(C):=\sum_z a_z.
\]
For nonempty \(I\subseteq D_+(C)\), let \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)\) be the affine residual rank from accepted fact 428d46805148687c, and define
\[
 \mathcal L_n(C)
 :=
 \sup_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq D_+(C)}
 \frac{\gamma_d\sum_{z\in I}a_z}{r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)+1},
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{1}
\]
For a proper primitive affine lattice subspace \(A\subsetneq\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
 W_A(C):=\sum_{z\in A\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P_n(C)
 :=
 \sup_A 2^{\operatorname{codim}A}W_A(C).
\tag{2}
\]
For an admissible factorized lattice-polysimplex packet \(\Pi\) from accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5, let \(q\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) be its total rank and let
\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 :=
 \prod_{i=1}^k
 \left(\sum_{u_i\in\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i}}g_i(u_i)^d\right),
 \qquad
 q=\sum_{i=1}^k r_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Use the exact ambient normalization from accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12,
\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi):=2^{n-q}\mathcal E(\Pi),
\tag{4}
\]
and set
\[
 \mathcal F_{n,<n}(C)
 :=\sup_{\Pi:q<n}\operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F_{n,n}(C)
 :=\sup_{\Pi:q=n}\mathcal E(\Pi),
\tag{5}
\]
where the supremum of an empty family is zero. Let
\(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\) be the exact complete metronoid-core capacity defined in accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12.

There is an explicit sequence, indexed by every integer \(n\ge2\), of full-dimensional convex bodies
\[
 C_n\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n}
\]
with \(d_n\to\infty\) and positive mixed masses \(W_n:=W(C_n)\), such that
\[
 \frac{\mathcal L_n(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathcal P_n(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathcal F_{n,<n}(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathcal F_{n,n}(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The estimates in (6) are simultaneous over every nonempty fiber subset, every proper primitive affine lattice subspace, every packet factorization \(q=r_1+\cdots+r_k\), every integral injective embedding, and every admissible collection of factor functions and compatible continuous corner points.

Nevertheless,
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow1,
\tag{7}
\]
and accepted fact 107b9182522633f9 gives a point \(p_n\in C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n})\) satisfying
\[
 h_{C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n})}(p_n)
 \ge
 \frac{\gamma_{d_n}}2\,2^{-n}W_n
 >
 \frac1{2e}\,2^{-n}W_n.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, no absolute \(\eta>0\) can force one of the three scores
\(\mathcal L_n(C)\), \(\mathcal P_n(C)\), or \(\mathcal F_{n,n}(C)\) to be at least \(\eta W(C)\). Even adjoining all proper-rank factorized packets does not repair this terminal system. Equation (8) shows that this conclusion is only a no-go for that certificate system, not a disproof of the locked weak Oertel statement.

## Proof

Fix an integer \(n\ge2\). Put
\[
 R_n:=2^{n^3},
 \qquad
 B_n:=R_nB_2^n,
 \qquad
 D_n:=B_n\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 N_n:=|D_n|,
 \qquad
 d_n:=N_n,
 \qquad
 \delta_n:=\frac1{4n}.
\tag{9}
\]
The continuous coordinates of the body will be indexed by \(D_n\).

1. Construction of one common convex body.

Set
\[
 b_n:=2R_n+1,
 \qquad
 \ell_n(x):=\sum_{j=1}^n b_n^{j-1}x_j,
 \qquad
 H_n:=2R_n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_n^j.
\tag{10}
\]
The functional \(\ell_n\) is injective on \(D_n\). Indeed, if \(z,z'\in D_n\) are distinct and \(j\) is the largest index for which \(h_j:=z_j-z'_j\ne0\), then
\[
 |h_j|b_n^{j-1}\ge b_n^{j-1},
 \qquad
 \left|\sum_{i<j}h_i b_n^{i-1}\right|
 \le(b_n-1)\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}b_n^i
 =b_n^{j-1}-1.
\]
Thus \(\ell_n(z)-\ell_n(z')\ne0\). Also, for \(x\in B_n\) and \(w\in D_n\), every coordinate difference has absolute value at most \(2R_n\), and hence
\[
 |\ell_n(x)-\ell_n(w)|\le H_n.
\tag{11}
\]

Define
\[
 \varepsilon_n:=\frac{\delta_n}{N_nH_n},
 \qquad
 \psi_w(x):=1-\varepsilon_n|\ell_n(x)-\ell_n(w)|
 \quad(w\in D_n),
\tag{12}
\]
and, with \(y=(y_w)_{w\in D_n}\in\mathbb R^{N_n}\), let
\[
 C_n
 :=
 \left\{
 (x,y):
 \begin{array}{l}
 x\in B_n,\\
 y_w\ge-\psi_w(x)\quad(w\in D_n),\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{w\in D_n}y_w\le1
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Each \(\psi_w\) is concave, so \(-\psi_w\) is convex and each inequality \(y_w\ge-\psi_w(x)\) defines a convex epigraph. The remaining constraints are convex. Thus \(C_n\) is convex. It is closed. The lower bounds on all coordinates \(y_w\), together with their upper bound on the sum, bound every \(y_w\) above as well, so \(C_n\) is compact. From (11)-(12),
\[
 \psi_w(x)\ge1-\frac{\delta_n}{N_n}>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Consequently \((0,0)\) satisfies all fiber inequalities and the sum inequality strictly, while \(0\in\operatorname{int}B_n\). Hence \(C_n\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{n+N_n}\) and is a full-dimensional convex body.

2. Exact fibers, near-uniform volumes, and independent centroid labels.

For \(x\in B_n\), define
\[
 S_n(x)
 :=1+\sum_{w\in D_n}\psi_w(x)
 =N_n+1-\varepsilon_n
 \sum_{w\in D_n}|\ell_n(x)-\ell_n(w)|.
\tag{15}
\]
Let
\[
 \Delta_{N_n}
 :=\left\{u\in\mathbb R^{N_n}:u_w\ge0,\ \sum_wu_w\le1\right\}.
\]
The coordinate change \(u_w=y_w+\psi_w(x)\) gives the exact identity
\[
 (C_n)_x=-\boldsymbol\psi(x)+S_n(x)\Delta_{N_n}.
\tag{16}
\]
By (11)-(12),
\[
 N_n+1-\delta_n\le S_n(x)\le N_n+1.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus every real fiber over \(B_n\), and in particular every lattice fiber over \(D_n\), is an \(N_n\)-dimensional simplex. There are no other lattice fibers. For \(z\in D_n\), write
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{N_n}((C_n)_z)
 =\frac{S_n(z)^{N_n}}{N_n!}.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(a_n^-:=\min_za_z\), \(a_n^+:=\max_za_z\), and
\(\Theta_n:=a_n^+/a_n^-\), then
\[
 1\le\Theta_n
 \le
 \left(\frac{N_n+1}{N_n+1-\delta_n}\right)^{N_n}
 \le e^{\delta_n}.
\tag{19}
\]
For the last inequality, use
\(-\log(1-t)\le t/(1-t)\) with \(t=\delta_n/(N_n+1)\), and note that
\(N_n\delta_n/(N_n+1-\delta_n)\le\delta_n\). Hence
\[
 \Theta_n\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 N_na_n^-\le W_n\le N_na_n^+.
\tag{20}
\]

For \(z,w\in D_n\), set
\[
 d_{z,w}:=|\ell_n(z)-\ell_n(w)|,
 \qquad
 q_z:=\sum_{w\in D_n}d_{z,w}.
\tag{21}
\]
The centroid of the simplex (16) is
\[
 c_z
 =-\boldsymbol\psi(z)+\frac{S_n(z)}{N_n+1}\mathbf1
 =\varepsilon_n
 \left(d_{z,w}-\frac{q_z}{N_n+1}\right)_{w\in D_n}.
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(N_n\ge3\), since \(0,e_1,-e_1\in D_n\). These \(N_n\) centroid vectors are linearly independent. To prove this, order the distinct real numbers \(\ell_n(z)\) increasingly and let
\[
 \mathsf D_{ij}:=|\ell_n(z_i)-\ell_n(z_j)|.
\]
The distance matrix \(\mathsf D\) is invertible. Indeed, if
\(\mathsf Dv=0\), then
\[
 f(t):=\sum_jv_j|t-\ell_n(z_j)|
\]
vanishes at every consecutive node. It is affine between consecutive nodes, so its slope is zero on every such interval. Consecutive slope differences force all interior coefficients \(v_2,\ldots,v_{N_n-1}\) to vanish. The first slope equation gives \(v_1=v_{N_n}\), and \(f(\ell_n(z_1))=0\) then gives \(v_{N_n}=0\). Thus \(v=0\). This also covers \(N_n=2\), with the empty interior-coefficient step omitted.

Let
\[
 \mathsf M:=I-\frac1{N_n+1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It has eigenvalue \(1\) on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) and eigenvalue \(1/(N_n+1)\) on \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\), so it is invertible. The matrix whose rows are the \(c_z^{\mathsf T}\) is
\[
 \varepsilon_n\mathsf D\mathsf M.
\tag{23}
\]
It is invertible, proving the asserted exact independence. The perturbation tends to zero metrically, but its algebraic rank remains \(N_n\).
Indeed, \(d_{z,w}\le H_n\) and \(q_z\le N_nH_n\) imply
\[
 |(c_z)_w|
 \le2\varepsilon_nH_n
 =\frac{2\delta_n}{N_n},
 \qquad
 \|c_z\|_2\le\frac{2\delta_n}{\sqrt{N_n}}\longrightarrow0.
\]

3. Uniform residual-rank bound.

Fix nonempty \(I\subseteq D_n\), and put \(k:=|I|\). By (23), the \(k\times N_n\) matrix of the selected centroid rows has rank \(k\). For any affine map \(s(x)=Ax+b:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^{N_n}\), the \(k\times N_n\) matrix of the rows \(s(z)^{\mathsf T}\), \(z\in I\), has rank at most \(n+1\). The rank inequality therefore gives
\[
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{c_z-s(z):z\in I\}
 \ge\max\{0,k-n-1\}.
\]
Taking the minimum over \(s\), exactly as in accepted fact 428d46805148687c, yields
\[
 r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)\ge\max\{0,k-n-1\}.
\tag{24}
\]
For every \(k\ge1\),
\[
 \frac{k}{r_{\mathrm{aff}}(I)+1}
 \le
 \frac{k}{\max\{1,k-n\}}
 \le n+1.
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(\sum_{z\in I}a_z\le ka_n^+\), equations (1), (20), and (25) imply
\[
 \frac{\mathcal L_n(C_n)}{W_n}
 \le
 \Theta_n\frac{n+1}{N_n}.
\tag{26}
\]

4. Every proper primitive slice is negligible.

Let \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be any affine \(q\)-plane with \(0\le q<n\) that contains lattice points. Distinct lattice points in \(A\) have Euclidean distance at least one. Relative \(q\)-balls of radius \(1/2\) centered at the points of \(A\cap D_n\) therefore have disjoint interiors. If \(a_0\) is the orthogonal projection of \(0\) onto \(A\), then every point of \(A\cap B_n\) has relative distance at most \(R_n\) from \(a_0\). All the radius-\(1/2\) balls lie in the relative ball of radius \(R_n+1/2\), and hence
\[
 |A\cap D_n|\le(2R_n+1)^q.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(q=0\), this has the intended meaning \(|A\cap D_n|\le1\).

Write
\[
 \kappa_n:=\operatorname{vol}_n(B_2^n),
 \qquad
 \rho_n:=\frac{\sqrt n}{2}.
\]
Coordinatewise rounding shows that the unit cubes centered at \(D_n\) cover
\((R_n-\rho_n)B_2^n\). Their interiors are disjoint, so
\[
 N_n\ge\kappa_n(R_n-\rho_n)^n.
\tag{28}
\]
The cube \([-1/\sqrt n,1/\sqrt n]^n\) lies in \(B_2^n\), whence
\(\kappa_n\ge(2/\sqrt n)^n\). Since \(R_n\ge\sqrt n\), (28) gives
\[
 N_n\ge\left(\frac{R_n}{\sqrt n}\right)^n.
\tag{29}
\]
This also shows \(N_n\to\infty\), so (26) tends to zero.

For an affine \(q\)-plane \(A\), equations (20) and (27) give
\[
 \frac{2^{n-q}W_A(C_n)}{W_n}
 \le
 \Theta_n\frac{2^{n-q}(2R_n+1)^q}{N_n}.
\tag{30}
\]
Because \((2R_n+1)/2>1\), the right side is largest at \(q=n-1\). Therefore every proper primitive affine lattice subspace satisfies
\[
 \frac{2^{\operatorname{codim}A}W_A(C_n)}{W_n}
 \le
 \Theta_n\frac{2(2R_n+1)^{n-1}}{N_n}
 \le
 \Theta_n\frac{2\cdot3^{n-1}n^{n/2}}{R_n}.
\tag{31}
\]
The last expression tends to zero for \(R_n=2^{n^3}\). Thus
\[
 \frac{\mathcal P_n(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{32}
\]

5. Uniform geometry and lattice count for every full-rank product of simplices.

We need two elementary estimates. First, let
\[
 n=r_1+\cdots+r_k,
 \qquad r_i\ge1,
\]
and suppose \(Q\subseteq RB_2^n\) is an invertible affine image of a product of simplices of dimensions \(r_1,\ldots,r_k\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_n(Q)
 \le
 \frac{2^n}{n^{n/2}}R^n.
\tag{33}
\]
To prove this, replace each factor by a regular simplex
\(\Sigma_{r_i}\) centered at zero whose vertices have norm one. Its volume is
\[
 v_{r_i}
 =
 \frac{(r_i+1)^{(r_i+1)/2}}{r_i!\,r_i^{r_i/2}},
\tag{34}
\]
as follows by taking the Gram determinant of the \(r_i\) edge vectors from one vertex. Write
\[
 Q=b+A\prod_i\Sigma_{r_i}.
\]
If \(V_i\) is uniform on the vertices of \(\Sigma_{r_i}\), independently over \(i\), then
\[
 \mathbb EV_i=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E V_iV_i^{\mathsf T}=\frac1{r_i}I_{r_i}.
\]
Every product vertex belongs to \(RB_2^n\). Thus, with
\[
 D:=\operatorname{diag}
 \left(\frac1{r_1}I_{r_1},\ldots,\frac1{r_k}I_{r_k}\right),
\]
one has
\[
 R^2\ge\mathbb E\|b+AV\|_2^2
 =\|b\|_2^2+\operatorname{tr}(ADA^{\mathsf T})
 \ge\operatorname{tr}(ADA^{\mathsf T}).
\]
Put \(\widetilde A:=AD^{1/2}\). The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for its squared singular values gives
\[
 |\det\widetilde A|
 \le\left(\frac{\|\widetilde A\|_{\mathrm F}^2}{n}\right)^{n/2}
 \le\frac{R^n}{n^{n/2}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_n(Q)
 \le
 \frac{R^n}{n^{n/2}}
 \prod_i\frac{(r_i+1)^{(r_i+1)/2}}{r_i!}.
\tag{35}
\]
For \(r=1\), the last one-factor expression equals \(2\). For \(r\ge2\),
\[
 (r!)^2
 =\prod_{j=1}^rj(r+1-j)\ge r^r,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \frac{(r+1)^{(r+1)/2}}{r!}
 \le\sqrt{e(r+1)}\le2^r.
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to \(e(r+1)\le4^r\). It holds at \(r=2\), and persists for all larger \(r\) because the right side is multiplied by \(4\), while the left side is multiplied by less than \(2\). Multiplying over the factors proves (33).

Define
\[
 \alpha_n:=\frac{2^n}{\kappa_n n^{n/2}}.
\tag{36}
\]
For \(n=2m\),
\[
 \kappa_n=\frac{\pi^m}{m!},
 \qquad
 \alpha_n
 =\frac{2^m m!}{\pi^m m^m}
 \le\left(\frac2\pi\right)^m.
\tag{37}
\]
For \(n=2m+1\),
\[
 \kappa_n=\frac{2^{m+1}\pi^m}{(2m+1)!!},
 \qquad
 \alpha_n
 =\frac{2^m(2m+1)!!}{\pi^m(2m+1)^{m+1/2}}
 \le
 \sqrt{2m+1}\left(\frac2\pi\right)^m.
\]
Thus \(\alpha_n\to0\) through both parities. Consequently (33) gives
 \[
 \operatorname{vol}_n(Q)\le\alpha_n\kappa_nR^n,
 \qquad
 \alpha_n\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{38}
 \]

Second, every full-dimensional convex \(Q\subseteq RB_2^n\) satisfies
\[
 |Q\cap\mathbb Z^n|
 \le
 \operatorname{vol}_n(Q)
 +\kappa_n\bigl((R+\rho_n)^n-R^n\bigr).
\tag{39}
\]
Indeed, the half-open unit cubes centered at \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) are disjoint and lie in \(Q+\rho_nB_2^n\), so their number is at most the volume of that parallel body. We prove the required parallel-volume estimate directly. Surface area is monotone under inclusion of convex bodies. To see this, fix a unit vector \(u\). The area formula for the orthogonal projection onto \(u^\perp\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K|u^\perp)
 =
 \frac12\int_{\partial K}|\langle u,\nu_K(x)\rangle|\,
 d\mathcal H_{n-1}(x),
\]
because almost every point in the interior of the projection has exactly two boundary preimages. Averaging over \(u\in S^{n-1}\), the inner spherical integral is independent of the unit normal \(\nu_K(x)\). Hence the surface area of \(K\) is a positive dimensional constant times the average \((n-1)\)-volume of its orthogonal projections. Inclusion preserves every projected inclusion, proving surface-area monotonicity.

Now let \(K\subseteq RB_2^n\). For every \(s\ge0\),
\[
 K+sB_2^n\subseteq(R+s)B_2^n.
\]
The coarea formula applied to the distance from \(K\), whose gradient has norm one almost everywhere outside \(K\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_n(K+tB_2^n)-\operatorname{vol}_n(K)
 =
 \int_0^t \mathcal H_{n-1}(\partial(K+sB_2^n))\,ds.
\]
Surface-area monotonicity therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{vol}_n(K+tB_2^n)-\operatorname{vol}_n(K)
 &\le
 \int_0^t n\kappa_n(R+s)^{n-1}\,ds\\
 &=\kappa_n((R+t)^n-R^n).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking \(K=Q\) and \(t=\rho_n\) proves (39).

Combining (28), (38), and (39), every affine product of simplices
\(Q\subseteq R_nB_2^n\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{|Q\cap\mathbb Z^n|}{N_n}
 \le
 \beta_n
 :=
 \frac{
 \alpha_nR_n^n+(R_n+\rho_n)^n-R_n^n
 }{(R_n-\rho_n)^n}.
\tag{40}
\]
Put \(x_n:=\rho_n/R_n\). For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\(nx_n\le1/4\). Then
\[
 (1-x_n)^n\ge1-nx_n\ge\frac34,
 \qquad
 (1+x_n)^n-1\le e^{nx_n}-1\le2nx_n.
\]
Equations (37)-(40) give
\[
 \beta_n
 \le2\alpha_n+4nx_n
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{41}
\]

6. Uniform control of every factorized packet.

Fix an arbitrary full-rank admissible packet \(\Pi\) for \(C_n\), with an arbitrary partition
\(n=r_1+\cdots+r_k\), arbitrary lattice simplices \(\Delta_i\), arbitrary integral full-rank embedding \(\Phi\), and arbitrary admissible corner data. Put
\[
 Q_\Pi:=\Phi\left(\prod_i\Delta_i\right).
\]
Every product vertex is the base point of one of the packet's selected corner points and hence belongs to the base projection \(B_n\). Since \(B_n\) is convex and the product of simplices is the convex hull of its product vertices,
\[
 Q_\Pi\subseteq B_n.
\tag{42}
\]
Accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5 constructs a multiaffine selector \(x(u)\) and gives, for every product lattice point \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\),
\[
 \delta_{C_n}(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \ge\prod_i g_i(u_i)^{N_n}.
\tag{43}
\]
Absolute fiber depth never exceeds the total volume of its compact fiber, so
\[
 \prod_i g_i(u_i)^{N_n}\le a_{\Phi(u)}\le a_n^+.
\tag{44}
\]
The full-rank integral map \(\Phi\) is injective. Expanding the energy and using (44),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 &=\sum_{u\in\prod_i(\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i})}
 \prod_i g_i(u_i)^{N_n}\\
 &\le a_n^+
 \left|\Phi\left(\prod_i(\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i})\right)\right|\\
 &\le a_n^+|Q_\Pi\cap\mathbb Z^n|.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]
This estimate does not use a saturated image lattice and does not depend on the selected continuous corners or factor functions beyond packet admissibility. Equations (20), (40), and (45) yield
\[
 \frac{\mathcal E(\Pi)}{W_n}
 \le\Theta_n\beta_n.
\]
Taking the supremum over every full-rank packet gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal F_{n,n}(C_n)}{W_n}
 \le\Theta_n\beta_n
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{46}
\]

For every proper-rank packet, accepted fact fbeb0a501b268d12 proves the exact packet-specific domination
\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi)
 \le\mathcal P_n(C_n).
\tag{47}
\]
This covers every \(1\le q<n\), every rank partition of \(q\), and every nonsaturated integral embedding. Equations (32) and (47) therefore give
\[
 \frac{\mathcal F_{n,<n}(C_n)}{W_n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{48}
\]
Together, (26), (32), (46), and (48) prove all four limits in (6).

7. Exact covariance-polar complete-core calibration.

It remains to show why these same bodies satisfy weak Oertel at the correct dyadic scale. We calculate the covariance-polar displacement of the common point \(0\in(C_n)_z\).

For brevity, write \(N:=N_n\), \(S_z:=S_n(z)\), \(\varepsilon:=\varepsilon_n\), and let \(J:=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\). Direct integration on the standard simplex gives
\[
 \Sigma_{\Delta_N}
 =\frac{(N+1)I-J}{(N+1)^2(N+2)},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_{\Delta_N}^{-1}
 =(N+1)(N+2)(I+J).
\tag{49}
\]
For example, these identities follow from
\[
 \mathbb Eu_i=\frac1{N+1},\quad
 \mathbb Eu_i^2=\frac2{(N+1)(N+2)},\quad
 \mathbb Eu_iu_j=\frac1{(N+1)(N+2)}\quad(i\ne j).
\]
By (16), the covariance matrix of \((C_n)_z\) is
\[
 \Sigma_z=S_z^2\Sigma_{\Delta_N}.
\tag{50}
\]
Let \(d_z:=(d_{z,w})_{w\in D_n}\). Equation (22) gives
\[
 \mathbf1^{\mathsf T}c_z=\frac{\varepsilon q_z}{N+1},
 \qquad
 (I+J)c_z=\varepsilon d_z.
\tag{51}
\]
For the displacement \(r_z:=-c_z\), accepted fact 107b9182522633f9 uses the affine score
\[
 h_z(y):=\langle\Sigma_z^{-1}r_z,y-c_z\rangle.
\]
Writing \(y=-\boldsymbol\psi(z)+S_zu\), \(u\in\Delta_N\), and using (49)-(51), the maximum absolute value of this affine function is attained at a vertex of \(\Delta_N\) and equals
\[

\tag{52}
\]

Since \(0\le d_{z,w}\le H_n\), one has \(q_z\le NH_n\). Also,

 \le
