## Statement

Let $L\subset\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge1$, be a full-dimensional polyhedron with at least one irredundant facet, and let $A\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be a convex set. Suppose $\operatorname{int}L\cap A\cap\mathbb Z^n=\varnothing$ and each facet $F_i$ has a chosen witness $p_i\in\operatorname{relint}(F_i)\cap A\cap\mathbb Z^n$. Then each $p_i$ is a vertex of $W=\operatorname{conv}\{p_i\}$, and $W\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{p_i\}$. The witnesses have distinct coordinate parities, so there are at most $2^n$ of them, and equality gives one in every class. This applies to a lattice-free $L$ by taking $A=\mathbb R^n$, and requires no boundedness of $L$.

## Proof

In an irredundant facet description of $L$, each witness is tight in its own inequality and strictly satisfies every other one. Hence its own facet functional uniquely maximizes at that witness among the listed points, and each is a vertex of $W$. Any convex combination involving at least two distinct witnesses with positive weights strictly satisfies each facet inequality, because at most one of those witnesses can be tight at any given facet. Thus it lies in $\operatorname{int}L$ and, by convexity, in $A$. The exclusion hypothesis rules out its being integral. This leaves exactly the chosen witnesses as lattice points of $W$. Two witnesses of the same parity would have an integral midpoint of this forbidden type, proving the parity count and its equality case.
