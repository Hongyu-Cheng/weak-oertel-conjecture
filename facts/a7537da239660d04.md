## Statement

For each integer \(r\ge1\), take the original complete weighted-simplex support \(D_r\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{3r+1}\), its exact minimal face \(F_r\), and its unique maximum-entropy law \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) at the original full mean \(\mathbf j=\mathbf1_{3r+1}\) from accepted fact \(637094343d0ed42a\). All these objects and the parameters \(a_r,b_r,t_r\) have exactly that predecessor's meanings and normalizations. Write \(\mathbf R_r=(X_1,\ldots,X_r,Z_1,\ldots,Z_r,U_1,\ldots,U_r,Y)\) for a random vector with this law. Thus its complete face layers are \(Y=0\), with each \(X_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\); \(Y=1\), with each \(X_i\in\{1,2\}\); and \(Y=2\), with each \(X_i=1\). In every layer \(Z_i=3-Y-X_i\) and \(U_i=2X_i-Y\).

Let \(b\ge1\) be an integer, let \(r_1,\ldots,r_b\ge1\) be arbitrary integers, and sample the full vectors \(\mathbf R_{r_1},\ldots,\mathbf R_{r_b}\) independently. Here \(b\) is a product block count, and is distinct from each predecessor parameter \(b_{r_s}\). Put \(\mathbf R=(\mathbf R_{r_1},\ldots,\mathbf R_{r_b})\), with original ambient mean \(\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}}=(\mathbf1_{3r_1+1},\ldots,\mathbf1_{3r_b+1})\). Then for every real original ambient normal \(\boldsymbol\theta\),
\[
\min\left\{\Pr\bigl(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf R-\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}})\ge0\bigr),\Pr\bigl(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf R-\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}})\le0\bigr)\right\}
\ge \frac1{196608\cdot20736}.
\]
Consequently every closed halfspace in the original product ambient space that contains \(\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}}\) has product-law mass at least \(1/(196608\cdot20736)\). The constant is independent of the number of blocks, all block sizes, and the normal. For one block the stronger lower bound \(1/(65536\cdot20736)\) holds. In each case, if the reduced \(X_i\)-coefficients defined in the proof all vanish, both closed mean caps have probability at least \(1/2\).

## Proof

We first establish a uniform scalar decomposition for one arbitrary block and one arbitrary original ambient normal. Fix \(r\ge1\). Within this block abbreviate \(a_r,t_r\) to \(a,t\); the block parameter \(b_r\) remains unchanged. Accepted fact \(637094343d0ed42a\) gives \(1/2<a<2/3\), the exact layer probabilities \(\Pr(Y=0)=\Pr(Y=2)=1/(2+t)\) and \(\Pr(Y=1)=t/(2+t)\), and every original mean equal to one. It also gives the independent conditional \(X_i\)-laws: at \(Y=0\), each mass at \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) is \(a^k/U(a)\), where \(U(a)=1+a+a^2+a^3\); at \(Y=1\), each mass at \(k\in\{1,2\}\) is proportional to \(a^k\); and at \(Y=2\), every \(X_i=1\).

Write these conditional means as \(m_0=m_0(a)\), \(m_1=(1+2a)/(1+a)\), and \(m_2=1\), with \(m_0(a)\) retaining its definition in \(637094343d0ed42a\). Put \(q:=m_1-1=a/(1+a)\). Thus \(1/3<q<2/5\), and the predecessor's exact mean equation gives \(m_0-1=-tq\). Furthermore the predecessor proves that \(\rho(a)^2=a(1+a)/(1+a^2)\) is increasing on this interval. Hence
\[
0<t=\rho(a)^r\le(9/10)^r<1,
\qquad
\rho(a)^2<\rho(2/3)^2=10/13<81/100.
\]
In particular the outer-layer probability \(1/(2+t)\) is at least \(1/3\).

Let the original normal have coefficients \((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r,\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_r,\upsilon_1,\ldots,\upsilon_r,\eta)\) in the original \((\mathbf x,\mathbf z,\mathbf u,y)\) coordinates. Define the reduced coefficients \(\alpha_i:=\xi_i-\zeta_i+2\upsilon_i\), \(\beta:=\eta-\sum_i\zeta_i-\sum_i\upsilon_i\), and put \(A:=\sum_i\alpha_i\), \(S_2:=\sum_i\alpha_i^2\). These are just scalar coefficients of the original normal, not new ambient coordinates. Using the exact face equations and subtracting the original full mean gives
\[
\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf R_r-\mathbf1_{3r+1})
=\sum_{i=1}^r\alpha_i(X_i-1)+\beta(Y-1).
\]
Indeed, substituting \(Z_i=3-Y-X_i\) and \(U_i=2X_i-Y\) into the uncentered projection gives \(3\sum_i\zeta_i+\sum_i\alpha_iX_i+\beta Y\); its expectation is \(3\sum_i\zeta_i+A+\beta\), the original normal applied to \(\mathbf1_{3r+1}\).

The scalar symbols \(W,V,N\) below are used in exactly the roles of the scalar random variables in accepted fact \(b0f9a49859be646c\); \(V\) is not an original vector coordinate. Define \(N:=\sum_i\alpha_i(X_i-m_Y)\). Define latent functions \(W=W(Y)\) and \(V=V(Y)\) by
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
y&0&1&2\\ \hline
W(y)&-Atq/2-\beta&0&Atq/2+\beta\\
V(y)&-Atq/2&Aq&-Atq/2.
\end{array}
\]
At each of the three values of \(Y\), these definitions give \(W+V=A(m_Y-1)+\beta(Y-1)\). Thus the centered original projection equals \(W+V+N\). Conditional centering gives \(\mathbb E[N\mid Y]=0\). The involution \(y\mapsto2-y\) preserves the exact latent law because the two outer layer probabilities agree; \(W\) is odd and \(V\) is even under this involution. The exact probabilities also give \(\mathbb EV=[2(-Atq/2)+tAq]/(2+t)=0\). There is no bound or regularity requirement on \(\beta\), or on the odd scalar \(W\).

We now verify the two uniform fourth-moment hypotheses. Let \(v_y:=\operatorname{Var}(X_1\mid Y=y)\), the common coordinate conditional variance. Conditional independence yields \(\mathbb E[N^2\mid Y=y]=v_yS_2\). For any law on \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), the variance identity \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=\sum_{k<\ell}\Pr(X=k)\Pr(X=\ell)(k-\ell)^2\) follows by expanding \(\frac12\mathbb E(X-X')^2\) for an independent copy \(X'\). The pair \((0,3)\) therefore gives, for the exact outer-layer coordinate law,
\[
v_0\ge\frac{9a^3}{U(a)^2}\ge\frac18,
\qquad
U(a)\le U(2/3)=65/27<3,
\qquad a^3\ge1/8.
\]
Writing \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb EN^2\), the outer-layer probability consequently implies \(\sigma^2\ge S_2/24\).

Every conditional coordinate has support in an interval of length at most three. If its interval midpoint is \(c\), then its conditional variance is at most \(\mathbb E[(X_1-c)^2\mid Y=y]\le9/4\), since centering at its mean minimizes the second moment. Thus \(0\le v_y\le9/4\). Also \(|X_i-m_y|\le3\), so \(\mathbb E[(X_i-m_y)^4\mid Y=y]\le9v_y\). Conditional independence and centering give the fourth-power expansion
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[N^4\mid Y=y]
&=\sum_i\alpha_i^4\mathbb E[(X_i-m_y)^4\mid Y=y]
+6v_y^2\sum_{i<j}\alpha_i^2\alpha_j^2\\
&\le\bigl(9v_y+3v_y^2\bigr)S_2^2
\le\frac{567}{16}S_2^2
\le36S_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\sum_i\alpha_i^4\le S_2^2\) and \(2\sum_{i<j}\alpha_i^2\alpha_j^2\le S_2^2\). At \(Y=2\) both sides of the expansion vanish, so it also covers the deterministic layer. Averaging gives \(\mathbb EN^4\le36S_2^2\).

For the even drift, its exact three masses give
\[
\mathbb EV^4
=A^4q^4\frac{t+t^4/8}{2+t}
\le\frac9{625}\,tA^4
\le\frac9{625}\,r^2(9/10)^rS_2^2.
\]
The first inequality uses \(t^4\le t\), \(2+t\ge2\), and \(q^4\le(2/5)^4=16/625\). The second uses Cauchy--Schwarz in the form \(A^2\le rS_2\). Each nonnegative term \(r^2(9/10)^r\) is at most their sum over positive integers. Differentiating the convergent geometric series twice, or directly multiplying the resulting series by \((1-z)^3\), gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}k^2z^k=z(1+z)/(1-z)^3\) for \(0<z<1\). At \(z=9/10\) this sum is exactly \(1710\). Consequently \(\mathbb EV^4\le(15390/625)S_2^2\le36S_2^2\).

If \(S_2>0\), the lower bound on \(\sigma^2\) now implies
\[
\mathbb EN^4\le20736\sigma^4,
\qquad
\mathbb EV^4\le20736\sigma^4,
\qquad 20736=36\cdot24^2.
\]
If \(S_2=0\), every \(\alpha_i=0\), so \(A=N=V=0\) and \(\sigma=0\); the same inequalities hold. We have therefore checked all hypotheses of \(b0f9a49859be646c\) for every original normal, with the one common constant \(K=20736\). Its scalar conclusion gives both closed caps at least \(1/(65536\cdot20736)\), and gives at least \(1/2\) when \(S_2=0\).

Finally consider the stated product. Decompose its arbitrary original normal into its \(b\) block normals and apply the preceding construction separately in block \(s\), using the actual parameter \(a_{r_s}\) and actual original layer probabilities. The independent full vectors make the experiments \((Y_s,N_s)\) independent. Each row has an odd \(W_s\), an even centered \(V_s\), conditional noise mean zero, and the two moment bounds with the same \(K=20736\). The total original centered projection is exactly \(\sum_s(W_s+V_s+N_s)\). The sum conclusion of accepted fact \(b0f9a49859be646c\) therefore gives the claimed two-sided bound \(1/(196608\cdot20736)\), uniformly over all \(b,r_1,\ldots,r_b\) and normals. If every block's reduced \(X_i\)-coefficients vanish, every noise variance is zero and the stronger \(1/2\) conclusion of that sum theorem applies.

For a nontrivial closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}}\), choose its orientation as \(\{\mathbf v:\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf v\ge h\}\). Containment of the mean gives \(h\le\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}}\). The closed mean cap \(\{\mathbf v:\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf j_{\mathrm{prod}})\ge0\}\) is therefore a subset of that halfspace, proving its required mass bound. A halfspace equal to the whole ambient space has mass one. All arguments used the original full mean and original ambient normal, and retained every atom of the exact complete face law.
