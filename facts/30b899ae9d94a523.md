## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with $d\ge1$, positive mixed-integer $d$-volume, and $F$ integer fibers of positive $d$-volume. Its maximum normalized halfspace depth is at least $\Gamma_d/F$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Hence a strict counterexample to $1/(e2^n)$ requires $F/2^n>e\Gamma_d>1+1/[2(d+1)]$. If such a strict counterexample has $n=52$ and $F\le2^{52}+32764$, then necessarily $d\ge68727866368$. This obstruction is for the specific $1/e$ target and is not a lower bound for the sharp-counterexample total dimension.

## Proof

# A support-count obstruction to the specific 1/e bound

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body,
with $d\ge1$, and suppose its mixed-integer volume $W$ is positive.
Let $F$ be the number of integer fibers with positive $d$-volume.
Write $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Then the maximum normalized halfspace
depth of its mixed-integer set is at least $\Gamma_d/F$.
Consequently a strict counterexample to $1/(e2^n)$ requires

$$
\frac{F}{2^n}>e\Gamma_d>1+\frac{1}{2(d+1)}.
$$

In particular, any such counterexample on the support of the existing
$n=52$ construction requires $d\ge68,727,866,368$, regardless of its
fiber shapes or volumes. This bound concerns the specific $1/e$ target;
it does not exclude smaller-dimensional counterexamples to
$\Gamma_d/2^n$ or settle the repository's weaker universal-constant target.

## Proof

There are finitely many integer fibers because $C$ is compact. Let $M>0$
be the largest fiber volume. Its fiber is a full-dimensional convex body
in its continuous affine space. At its ordinary centroid, every
containing ambient closed halfspace restricts either to a containing
continuous halfspace or to the whole fiber. The ordinary centroid
Grünbaum inequality therefore gives retained volume at least
$\Gamma_dM$. Other fiber contributions are nonnegative. Since
$W\le FM$, the normalized depth at that actual mixed-integer point is
at least $\Gamma_dM/W\ge\Gamma_d/F$.

If every point has depth strictly below $1/(e2^n)$, this lower bound
implies $F/2^n>e\Gamma_d$. Also,

$$
\log(e\Gamma_d)
=1-d\log(1+1/d)
=\int_0^1\frac{t}{d+t}\,dt
>\frac{1}{2(d+1)}.
$$

Using $\exp(x)>1+x$ for $x>0$ proves the second strict inequality.

The existing support has all $2^{52}$ positive Boolean bases, including
zero, and $4(2^{13}-1)=32764$ additional negative Boolean bases in four
disjoint blocks. Thus $F\le2^{52}+32764$, even if some fibers are assigned
zero volume. The necessary inequality gives

$$
d+1>\frac{2^{51}}{32764}=\frac{2^{49}}{8191}
=68,727,866,368+\frac{1024}{8191}.
$$

Because $d$ is an integer, $d\ge68,727,866,368$. The ordinary centroid
argument applies to every containing closed ambient halfspace, including
ones parallel to or containing the whole maximal fiber. Lower-dimensional
fibers contribute zero $d$-volume and do not weaken it.

The geometric inequality used here is [Grünbaum, *Partitions of mass-distributions
and of convex bodies by hyperplanes*, Section 3](https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf).
The [earlier independent audit](../research/dimension-reduction-2026-11-07/verify-current.md) checks its application
to the existing construction and the exact support arithmetic.
