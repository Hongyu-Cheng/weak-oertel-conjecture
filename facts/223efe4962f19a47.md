## Statement

Let \(r\geq1\) be an integer, let \(K\subset\mathbb{R}^r\) be a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone, and let \(a\in\operatorname{int}(K^*)\), where \(K^*=\{b:b\cdot x\geq0\text{ for every }x\in K\}\). Set

\[
Z=\int_K e^{-a\cdot x}\,dx,
\qquad
f(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-a\cdot x}\mathbf{1}_K(x),
\qquad
R_f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^r}\min\{f(x+y),f(x-y)\}\,dy.
\]

There is an absolute constant \(c_{\mathrm{cone}}>0\) such that

\[
8^r\int_{\mathbb{R}^r} f(x)R_f(x)^3\,dx\geq c_{\mathrm{cone}}
\]

for every such \(r,K,a\). Thus the infimum in the task is positive. For \(r=1\), the normalized moment is exactly \(16/35\).

Here is a quantitative cross-section comparison, including its normalization. For \(r\geq2\), put \(m=r-1\). Choose invertible linear coordinates \(x=(v,s)\in\mathbb{R}^m\times\mathbb{R}\) with \(s=a\cdot x\), and center the height-one cross-section to write

\[
K=\{(su,s):s>0,\ u\in B\}\cup\{0\},
\qquad
0\in\operatorname{int}(B),
\]

where \(B\subset\mathbb{R}^m\) is a convex body. The displayed \(K,f,R_f,Z\) in these coordinates mean their linear pushforwards, as specified in the proof; the target moment is unchanged. Write \(|\cdot|\) for Lebesgue volume in the relevant dimension and define the normalized reflection volume and its normalized cubic moment by

\[
\Theta_B(u)=\frac{|B\cap(2u-B)|}{|B|},
\qquad
J_B=\frac{8^m}{|B|}\int_B\Theta_B(u)^3\,du.
\]

Then the explicit comparison is

\[
8^r\int f(x)R_f(x)^3\,dx
\geq
16(1-e^{-1})^3e^{-4}
\frac{(4m)!}{4^{4m}(m!)^4}\,J_B.
\]

There are absolute constants \(b_{\mathrm{GM}}>0\) and \(A_{\mathrm{GM}}\geq1\), coming from the published concentration theorem stated under EXTERNAL SOURCES, such that \(J_B\geq e^{2b_{\mathrm{GM}}\sqrt m}/(27A_{\mathrm{GM}}^2)\). Consequently, uniformly over cones of dimension \(r=m+1\geq2\),

\[
8^r\int f(x)R_f(x)^3\,dx
\geq
\frac{32(1-e^{-1})^3e^{-17/2}}{27A_{\mathrm{GM}}^2}
\frac{e^{2b_{\mathrm{GM}}\sqrt m}}{m^{3/2}}.
\]

The root measure in every displayed cone moment is exactly \(f(x)\,dx\); it is not Lebesgue measure on a truncation, a layer-mixing measure, or an escort density.

## Proof

An invertible linear map \(L\) sends the density to \(f_L(z)=|\det L|^{-1}f(L^{-1}z)\). Substituting \(y=Lh\) in the reflection integral gives \(R_{f_L}(Lx)=R_f(x)\). Hence

\[
\int f_L(z)R_{f_L}(z)^3\,dz
=\int f(x)R_f(x)^3\,dx.
\]

The pushed-forward cone is \(LK\), the pushed-forward coefficient is \(L^{-\mathsf T}a\), and the pushed-forward normalizing integral is \(|\det L|Z\). This proves the normalization map used above.

Because \(a\in\operatorname{int}(K^*)\), some \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfies \(a\cdot x\geq\varepsilon\|x\|_2\) for all \(x\in K\): for nonzero \(x\in K\), use \(a-\varepsilon x/\|x\|_2\in K^*\). In particular \(a\cdot x>0\) for nonzero \(x\in K\). The slice \(a\cdot x=1\) is therefore compact, and it has nonempty relative interior because \(K\) has nonempty interior. After taking linear coordinates with last coordinate \(s=a\cdot x\), it gives a convex body \(B\subset\mathbb{R}^m\). The shear

\[
(v,s)\longmapsto
\left(v-\frac{s}{|B|}\int_Bu\,du,\ s\right)
\]

centers this slice. Its centroid is in its interior: if the centroid were on a supporting hyperplane, a nonnegative affine supporting functional, positive on a nonempty open subset of \(B\), would have zero integral, a contradiction. Thus the centered slice contains \(0\) in its interior.

An additional linear map on \(v\) can make the uniform law on \(B\) isotropic. Indeed, its covariance matrix is positive definite since \(B\) has interior, so applying its inverse square root gives mean zero and covariance the identity. All these maps preserve the target integral by the preceding calculation. They also preserve \(\Theta_B\) and \(J_B\) under their corresponding changes of variables. We may therefore use an isotropic \(B\) when proving the bound on \(J_B\).

For \(r\geq2\), the coordinates \(x=(su,s)\) have Jacobian \(s^m\). Integration by parts gives \(\int_0^\infty e^{-s}s^m\,ds=m!\), and consequently

\[
Z=|B|m!,
\qquad
f(su,s)\,dx
=\frac{e^{-s}s^m}{m!}\,ds\,\frac{du}{|B|}.
\]

This is the exact independent product of the radial \(\operatorname{Gamma}(r,1)\) law and the uniform law on the cross-section. In particular, the radial shape parameter is \(r=m+1\), not \(r+1\).

For completeness, the truncated-cone foothold and its layer-cake relation have the following exact factors. Put \(T_\ell=K\cap\{s\leq\ell\}\) for \(\ell>0\). Then

\[
|T_\ell|=\frac{|B|\ell^r}{r},
\qquad
\frac{|T_\ell|}{Z}=\frac{\ell^r}{r!}.
\]

The midpoint of any two points of \(T_1\) belongs to \(T_1\). Changing variables \(z=2x\) in the convolution of its indicator therefore gives

\[
\frac{1}{|T_1|^2}\int_{T_1}|T_1\cap(2x-T_1)|\,dx
=2^{-r}.
\]

This is precisely the reflection first moment under the uniform density on \(T_1\). Tonelli's theorem applied to \(e^{-q}=\int_q^\infty e^{-\ell}\,d\ell\) gives the layer-cake formula

\[
R_f(x)=\frac1Z\int_0^\infty e^{-\ell}|T_\ell\cap(2x-T_\ell)|\,d\ell.
\]

If \(\Theta_{T_\ell}(x)=|T_\ell\cap(2x-T_\ell)|/|T_\ell|\), then the layer-mixing density is \(w_{\mathrm{layer}}(\ell)=e^{-\ell}\ell^r/r!\), and

\[
R_f(x)=\int_0^\infty w_{\mathrm{layer}}(\ell)\Theta_{T_\ell}(x)\,d\ell,
\qquad
f(x)=\int_0^\infty w_{\mathrm{layer}}(\ell)
\frac{\mathbf{1}_{T_\ell}(x)}{|T_\ell|}\,d\ell.
\]

The layer-mixing law has shape parameter \(r+1\). It is distinct from the original root law of shape parameter \(r\). We next use the exact cross-section form of the same overlap instead of discarding the cubic geometry by averaging these layers separately.

Fix \(s>0\) and \(u\in B\), and write the reflection variable as \(y=(z,t)\). The two support constraints are \(su+z\in(s+t)B\) and \(su-z\in(s-t)B\). On this set the minimum of the two densities is \(Z^{-1}e^{-s-|t|}\). Translating \(z\mapsto su+z\) therefore yields the exact identity

\[
R_f(su,s)=\frac{e^{-s}}{|B|m!}
\int_{-s}^{s}e^{-|t|}
\bigl|(s+t)B\cap\bigl(2su-(s-t)B\bigr)\bigr|\,dt.
\]

For \(s>1\) and \(|t|\leq1\), the inclusion \(0\in B\) implies \((s-1)B\subseteq(s+t)B\) and \((s-1)B\subseteq(s-t)B\). Hence the intersection in this identity contains \((s-1)B\cap(2su-(s-1)B)\), whose volume is

\[
(s-1)^m|B|\,\Theta_B\!\left(\frac{su}{s-1}\right).
\]

Integrating just over \(|t|\leq1\) gives

\[
R_f(su,s)\geq
\frac{2(1-e^{-1})}{m!}
e^{-s}(s-1)^m\,
\Theta_B\!\left(\frac{su}{s-1}\right).
\]

This inclusion does not compare the original density with another density. Its dimension-dependent dilation factors will be integrated exactly.

Convexity shows that \(\Theta_B(v)=0\) for \(v\notin B\), since a nonempty intersection expresses \(v\) as the midpoint of two points of \(B\). Also \((1-1/s)B\subset B\) for \(s>1\). Thus the substitution \(u=(1-1/s)v\) gives

\[
\frac1{|B|}\int_B
\Theta_B\!\left(\frac{su}{s-1}\right)^3\,du
=\left(\frac{s-1}{s}\right)^m
\frac1{|B|}\int_B\Theta_B(v)^3\,dv.
\]

Cubing the overlap lower bound and integrating with the exact original root measure now gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int f(x)R_f(x)^3\,dx
&\geq
\frac{8(1-e^{-1})^3}{(m!)^4}
\left(\frac1{|B|}\int_B\Theta_B(v)^3\,dv\right)
\int_1^\infty e^{-4s}(s-1)^{4m}\,ds\\
&=
\frac{8(1-e^{-1})^3e^{-4}(4m)!}{4^{4m+1}(m!)^4}
\left(\frac1{|B|}\int_B\Theta_B(v)^3\,dv\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality follows by \(s=1+t\), followed by \(q=4t\) and the same factorial integral used above. Multiplying by \(8^{m+1}\) and using the stated definition of \(J_B\) proves the cross-section comparison with factor \(16(1-e^{-1})^3e^{-4}\). In particular, the radial shift costs only \(e^{-4}\), rather than a constant raised to the dimension.

It remains to prove the required dimension-uniform growth of \(J_B\). Let \(\mu_B\) be the uniform probability measure on an isotropic copy of \(B\). For independent \(X_1,X_2\) with law \(\mu_B\), set \(Y=(X_1+X_2)/2\). Direct convolution and the Jacobian \(2^m\) show that the density of \(Y\), relative to \(\mu_B\), is

\[
g_B(u)=2^m\Theta_B(u),
\qquad
\int_B g_B(u)\,d\mu_B(u)=1,
\qquad
J_B=\int_B g_B(u)^3\,d\mu_B(u).
\]

The support of this law lies in \(B\), again by convexity. This also proves the cross-section reflection first-moment identity \(\int_B\Theta_B\,d\mu_B=2^{-m}\).

Use the Guédon-Milman concentration theorem quoted under EXTERNAL SOURCES, with its constants denoted \(c_{\mathrm{GM}}>0\) and \(A_{\mathrm{GM}}\geq1\). Its hypotheses hold here: \(\mu_B\) has a log-concave density because \(B\) is convex, and it has mean zero and covariance the identity by the affine normalization. Put

\[
\delta=1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\qquad
b_{\mathrm{GM}}=c_{\mathrm{GM}}\delta^3,
\qquad
E_m=\{u:\|u\|_2\leq\sqrt{3m/4}\}.
\]

Since \(0<\delta<1\), the theorem gives \(\mu_B(E_m)\leq A_{\mathrm{GM}}e^{-b_{\mathrm{GM}}\sqrt m}\). On the other hand, independence, mean zero, and isotropy imply \(\mathbb{E}\|Y\|_2^2=m/2\). Integrating this nonnegative random variable on the complement of \(E_m\) gives \(\mathbb{P}(Y\notin E_m)\leq2/3\), so \(\mathbb{P}(Y\in E_m)\geq1/3\). Hölder's inequality consequently yields

\[
\frac13
\leq\int_{E_m}g_B\,d\mu_B
\leq J_B^{1/3}\mu_B(E_m)^{2/3},
\qquad
J_B\geq\frac{e^{2b_{\mathrm{GM}}\sqrt m}}{27A_{\mathrm{GM}}^2}.
\]

Finally, the needed factorial estimate can be proved without an asymptotic approximation. Concavity of \(\log x\), applied on each interval between consecutive integers, implies for \(m\geq1\)

\[
\log(m!)\leq\int_1^m\log x\,dx+\frac12\log m,
\qquad
m!\leq e\sqrt m\left(\frac me\right)^m.
\]

For any integer \(k\geq1\), use \(k!=\int_0^\infty t^ke^{-t}\,dt\) on the interval \(t=k+u\), \(0\leq u\leq\sqrt k\). The elementary inequality \(\log(1+z)\geq z-z^2/2\) for \(z\geq0\) follows by differentiation. It gives

\[
k!\geq e^{-1/2}\sqrt k\left(\frac ke\right)^k.
\]

Taking \(k=4m\) and combining these two estimates proves

\[
\frac{(4m)!}{4^{4m}(m!)^4}
\geq2e^{-9/2}m^{-3/2}.
\]

Together with the cross-section comparison and the bound on \(J_B\), this proves the claimed lower bound for \(r\geq2\), which tends to infinity as \(r\to\infty\).

For \(r=1\), linear normalization makes \(K=[0,\infty)\), \(a=1\), and \(f(s)=e^{-s}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(s)\). The support condition is \(|y|\leq s\), giving \(R_f(s)=2e^{-s}(1-e^{-s})\) for \(s\geq0\). Substitution \(t=e^{-s}\) and expansion of \((1-t)^3\) give

\[
8\int f(s)R_f(s)^3\,ds
=64\int_0^1 t^3(1-t)^3\,dt
=\frac{16}{35}.
\]

For \(t>0\), differentiation shows that \(e^{2b_{\mathrm{GM}}t}/t^3\) has minimum \((2eb_{\mathrm{GM}}/3)^3\). Applying this with \(t=\sqrt m\), one possible absolute constant in the theorem is

\[
c_{\mathrm{cone}}=
\min\left\{
\frac{16}{35},
\frac{32(1-e^{-1})^3e^{-17/2}}{27A_{\mathrm{GM}}^2}
\left(\frac{2eb_{\mathrm{GM}}}{3}\right)^3
\right\}>0.
\]

Tonelli's theorem and changes of variables above apply to nonnegative integrands. The target moment is finite as well: \(R_f(x)\leq\int f(x+y)\,dy=1\). This completes the proof.

## External sources

Olivier Guédon and Emanuel Milman, “Interpolating Thin-Shell and Sharp Large-Deviation Estimates For Isotropic Log-Concave Measures,” arXiv:1011.0943v3, Theorem 1.1 and its stated specialization to the identity map and arbitrary isotropic log-concave densities. Primary-source abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0943v3 . Primary-source manuscript: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.0943v3 . TeX source: https://arxiv.org/src/1011.0943v3 . The manuscript's abstract explicitly states the following specialized result, also obtained immediately after Theorem 1.1: there exist universal positive constants \(c_{\mathrm{GM}},A_{\mathrm{GM}}\) such that, for every integer \(m\geq1\), every random vector \(X\in\mathbb{R}^m\) with log-concave density, \(\mathbb{E}X=0\), and \(\mathbb{E}(X\otimes X)=I_m\), and every \(t\geq0\),

\[
\mathbb{P}\!\left(
\left|\|X\|_2-\sqrt m\right|\geq t\sqrt m
\right)
\leq A_{\mathrm{GM}}
\exp\!\left(-c_{\mathrm{GM}}\sqrt m\min\{t^3,t\}\right).
\]

Increasing \(A_{\mathrm{GM}}\) to at least \(1\) preserves this statement. The application uses the uniform random vector on an affine-isotropic convex body \(B\), whose density is log-concave and whose mean and covariance satisfy exactly these hypotheses, and \(t=1-\sqrt3/2\in(0,1)\). No other external theorem or accepted project fact is used as an auxiliary input.
