## Statement

For an integer $m\ge3$, let $W$ be uniform on the simplex $\Delta_m=\{x\in\mathbb R^m:x_j\ge0,\ \sum_jx_j=1\}$, let $J$ be uniform on its vertex indices, and let $B$ have an absolutely continuous probability density $w$ on $[0,1]$. Assume the three variables are independent. Then $(1-B)W+Be_J$ is uniform on $\Delta_m$ if and only if $w(b)=m(1-b)^{m-1}$ almost everywhere. If an atom at zero is allowed, any mixture of this beta law and the unit mass at zero is stationary. For $m=2$ the constant step $B=1/2$ is also stationary.

## Proof

The beta-step merging lemma proves sufficiency. For necessity, set $G(u)=\int_0^u w(b)/(1-b)^{m-1}\,db$ for $0\le u<1$. Conditioning on $B=b$ and $J=j$ gives density $(1-b)^{-(m-1)}\mathbf 1_{\{x_j\ge b\}}$ relative to uniform probability. Integrating and averaging yields density $m^{-1}\sum_jG(x_j)$ almost everywhere. Thus stationarity gives $\sum_jG(x_j)=m$ almost everywhere. The function $G$ is continuous on $[0,1)$, so the identity holds at every interior point and extends to each boundary tuple with all coordinates less than $1$ by continuity.

For $x,y>0$ with $x+y<1$, compare the tuples $(x,y,1-x-y,0,\ldots,0)$ and $(x+y,1-x-y,0,\ldots,0)$. Since $G(0)=0$, the comparison gives $G(x)+G(y)=G(x+y)$. This holds also when either variable is zero. The centroid gives $G(1/m)=1$. Additivity gives $G(1/(mn))=1/n$ for every positive integer $n$, and $G(k/(mn))=k/n$ for $0\le k<mn$. Continuity now gives $G(u)=m u$ on $[0,1)$. Differentiate on compact subintervals of $[0,1)$ to obtain the claimed density almost everywhere.

The step $0$ leaves the uniform law unchanged, so mixtures with its unit mass are stationary by linearity. In the two-vertex case the two half-length images at step $1/2$ partition the interval up to a null midpoint, giving uniformity directly.
