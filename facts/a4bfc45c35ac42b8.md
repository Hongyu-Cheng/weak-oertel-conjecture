## Statement

For $L\ge0$, let $C_L$ be the convex hull of four unit intervals at binary bases $00,10,01,11$, with fibers $[0,1]$ except the fiber $[L,L+1]$ at $11$. Its mixed mass is $M=4$ and its maximum normalized ambient closed-halfspace depth is $1/8$. The real fiber over $(s,t)\in[0,1]^2$ is
$$
[L\max(0,s+t-1),\,1+L\min(s,t)].
$$
Thus $D_{\rm int}=[-1,1]$ and $D_{\rm all}=[-W,W]$ with $W=1+L/2$. At cap count eight and cap budget $1/2$ the exact width-normalized hinge minima divided by $M$ are $1/4$ for $D_{\rm int}$ and $1/(4W)$ for $D_{\rm all}$. In particular there is no positive dimension-only lower bound for the latter normalized minimum at the sharp depth budget, even at $n=2,d=1$.

## Proof

The four binary bases are extreme in the projection, so these are exactly the displayed integer fibers. In any convex combination over base $(s,t)$, the weight at $11$ ranges over $[\max(0,s+t-1),\min(s,t)]$ by solving for the remaining three nonnegative weights. For fixed weights the sum of the four weighted unit intervals is a unit interval with left endpoint $L$ times that weight. Varying it gives the claimed fiber. The width is $1+L\min(s,t,1-s,1-t)$, with maximum $W$ at the square's center. The two difference bodies follow.

A closed ambient halfspace containing an integer-fiber midpoint contains at least half of that interval. Conversely for any point in any integer interval one can cut through it toward the nearer endpoint and subtract a sufficiently large multiple of the affine binary distance from its base to exclude every other fiber. Its mass is at most $1/2$. Therefore the maximum normalized depth is exactly $1/8$.

Any nonvertical cap containing an integer midpoint at budget $1/2$ has exactly the own-fiber half-interval and zero mass on the other fibers. For both signs to cover a neighborhood of the midpoint they must both occur there, since the other finitely many caps miss a neighborhood of it. These required caps are all distinct across fibers. At count eight they exhaust the family, and a full cover of this type is realized by the same binary-distance tilts. Each unit-normal hinge integral equals $\int_0^{1/2}t\,dt=1/8$. The integer width representatives keep these margins, whereas the real width representatives divide them by $W$.
