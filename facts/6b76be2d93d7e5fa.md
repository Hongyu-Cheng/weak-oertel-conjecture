## Statement

Use the complete support, original weights, logarithmic gaps, and full-occurrence hitting conventions of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Thus \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\), \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/w_{\mathbf I})\), and a barycentric support at \(\mathbf J\in D\) has positive coefficients of sum one and barycenter \(\mathbf J\). Its logarithmic gap is \(G_\lambda=\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}\). The quantity \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) is the minimum original weight of a set meeting all such supports with \(G_\lambda\le L\), equivalently all eligible affinely independent supports. Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\mathbf J}
&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\text{ a closed halfspace}\\\mathbf J\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\cap H}w_{\mathbf I},
&\tau_*&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D}\tau_{\mathbf J},\\
T&=W2^{-n},
&R&=\tau_*/T,\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every integer \(k\ge1\) and every \(\sigma\in\{1,k\}\), let \(n=4k+1\), write the base coordinates as \((t,x)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{4k}\), and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=\left\{(t,x):-1\le t\le1,\quad
\frac{t-1}{2}\le x_i\le\frac{t+1}{2}\quad(1\le i\le4k)\right\},\\
D&=\{(1,b):b\in\{0,1\}^{4k}\}
\cup\{\mathbf0\}
\cup\{(-1,-b):b\in\{0,1\}^{4k}\},\\
w_{(1,b)}&=1,\qquad
w_{\mathbf0}=2^{-k},\qquad
w_{(-1,-b)}=\sigma2^{-4k},\qquad
w_{\mathbf I}=0\quad(\mathbf I\notin D),\\
\ell_{k,\sigma}&=k\log2-\frac12\log\sigma>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), and \(P\) has full affine dimension. The displayed table is globally extendable and log-concave, including its prescribed zero values. Its normalized potential is zero on the upper layer, \(k\log2\) at the origin, and \(4k\log2-\log\sigma\) on the lower layer.

The exact original marginal depths are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{(1,b)}&=1,&
\tau_{(-1,-b)}&=\sigma2^{-4k},&
\tau_{\mathbf0}&=\sigma+2^{-k}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, the origin is the unique maximizer over the entire positive integer support. The exact mass and reserve are

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=2^{4k}+\sigma+2^{-k},
&\tau_*&=\sigma+2^{-k},\\
T&=\frac{2^{4k}+\sigma+2^{-k}}{2^{4k+1}},
&R&=\frac{2^{4k+1}(\sigma+2^{-k})}{2^{4k}+\sigma+2^{-k}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For every \(L\ge0\), including the cutoff equality, the complete hitting profiles are

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;w)
&=\begin{cases}
2^{-k},&0\le L<\ell_{k,\sigma},\\
\sigma+2^{-k},&L\ge\ell_{k,\sigma},
\end{cases}\\
m_{\log}((1,b),L;w)&=1,\qquad
m_{\log}((-1,-b),L;w)=\sigma2^{-4k},\\
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
&=\max\left\{1,\frac{\sigma+2^{-k}}{1+\ell_{k,\sigma}}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(\sigma=1\), one has \(1<R<3\), \(\ell_{k,1}=k\log2\), and \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=1\). Consequently, for every \(C_1,c_1>0\), a finite member satisfies
\(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C_1(1+\log R)^2;w)<c_1\tau_*\), and there is no other marginal maximizer to choose.

For \(\sigma=k\), one has \(1<R<3k\) and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\tau_*}{k}&\longrightarrow1,
&\frac{R}{2k}&\longrightarrow1,
&\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)&\longrightarrow\frac1{\log2},\\
\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)(1+\log R)^2}{\tau_*}
&\le\frac{3}{\log2}\frac{(1+\log(3k))^2}{k}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus no absolute \(c_2>0\) can satisfy
\(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge c_2\tau_*/(1+\log R)^2\) for every table in the registered class. In both regimes \(1/2<T<1\) and \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge1>T\).

## Proof

Fix \(k\ge1\) and \(\sigma\in\{1,k\}\). The elementary inequality \(k\le2^k\) follows by induction. It gives \(\log\sigma\le\log k\le k\log2\), so \(\ell_{k,\sigma}\ge(k\log2)/2>0\) and \(0<\sigma2^{-4k}\le2^{-3k}<1\). All listed sites therefore have positive weight, and the maximum weight is one.

We first verify the whole support. The affine change of real coordinates \(u_i=x_i-t/2\) identifies \(P\) with the product \([-1,1]\times[-1/2,1/2]^{4k}\). In particular \(P\) is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope. Its vertices have \(t=1\) and \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\), or \(t=-1\) and \(x_i\in\{-1,0\}\). All those vertices belong to \(D\), and the origin belongs to \(P\). Thus \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). This real coordinate change is used only to describe the polytope, not to replace the integer lattice.

In the original coordinates, every integer point of \(P\) has \(t\in\{-1,0,1\}\). At \(t=1\), each integer coordinate \(x_i\) belongs to \([0,1]\); at \(t=-1\), each belongs to \([-1,0]\); and at \(t=0\), each belongs to \([-1/2,1/2]\), forcing \(x=\mathbf0\). Conversely every listed site satisfies the defining inequalities. Hence \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\), with no omitted boundary or additional positive lattice sites.

For a zero-preserving global log-concave extension, define the convex function on \(\mathbb R^n\)

\[
V(t,x)=\max\left\{
k\log2(1-t),\quad
k\log2+(\log\sigma-3k\log2)t
\right\}.
\]

This is the maximum of two affine functions and is independent of \(x\). At \(t=1\) the first expression is zero and the second is \(\log\sigma-2k\log2<0\). At \(t=0\) both are \(k\log2\). At \(t=-1\) the expressions are \(2k\log2\) and \(4k\log2-\log\sigma\), with the second larger. Therefore \(e^{-V}\) gives exactly the stated lattice weights on \(D\). Moreover \(V\ge0\) throughout \(P\), because its first expression is nonnegative there.

Set \(f=e^{-V}\) on \(P\) and \(f=0\) outside \(P\). When both endpoints lie in \(P\), convexity of \(V\) and \(P\) proves the log-concavity inequality for \(f\). If either endpoint is outside \(P\), the required right side is zero. The function is finite-valued and agrees with the table at every lattice point, including every zero. Its restriction supplies exactly the normalized potential \(\phi\) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`; in particular no tilted weights or alternative potential normalization are involved.

We next compute the original marginal depths at every possible center. The sites other than the origin form \(2^{4k}\) disjoint antipodal pairs \(\{(1,b),(-1,-b)\}\). Any closed halfspace containing the origin contains at least one endpoint of each pair, and also contains the origin. Since the smaller weight in every pair is \(\sigma2^{-4k}\), its mass is at least \(2^{-k}+2^{4k}\sigma2^{-4k}=\sigma+2^{-k}\). This argument includes equality atoms: a pair on the boundary contributes both endpoints. The closed halfspace \(\{(t,x):t\le0\}\) contains exactly the origin and the entire lower layer, so it attains this bound.

Every outer site is an exposed vertex. To verify this explicitly, fix \(b\in\{0,1\}^{4k}\) and put

\[
F_b(t,x)=(4k+1)t+\sum_{i=1}^{4k}(2b_i-1)x_i.
\]

At fixed \(t\), each nonzero coefficient forces its unique maximizing coordinate to be \((t+1)/2\) when \(b_i=1\), and \((t-1)/2\) when \(b_i=0\). Substitution gives the section maximum \((2k+1+\sum_i b_i)t+2k\), whose coefficient of \(t\) is positive. Thus \(F_b\) has its unique maximum on \(P\) at \((1,b)\). The identity \(P=-P\) shows that \(F_b(-t,-x)\) uniquely exposes \((-1,-b)\).

The closed halfspace at the maximum of either exposing functional contains exactly the corresponding site of \(D\). Since every containing halfspace must contain that site's own atom, its marginal depth equals its own weight. This proves both outer-depth formulas. They are strictly below \(\sigma+2^{-k}\), because \(\sigma\ge1\). The lattice census exhausts \(D\), so the origin is the unique original marginal maximizer. Summing the two layer masses and the central mass proves the formulas for \(W,T,R\).

It remains to check every eligible barycentric support, including arbitrary dependent supports. Take any barycentric law at the origin and extend its coefficients by zero to all of \(D\). Let \(\lambda_{\mathbf0}\) be its coefficient at the origin. The first coordinate of its barycenter is zero, so the total coefficient on each outer layer is \((1-\lambda_{\mathbf0})/2\). The exact gap is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_\lambda
&=\frac{1-\lambda_{\mathbf0}}2
(4k\log2-\log\sigma)
+\lambda_{\mathbf0}k\log2-k\log2\\
&=(1-\lambda_{\mathbf0})\ell_{k,\sigma}.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular every support avoiding the origin has gap exactly \(\ell_{k,\sigma}\), and every barycentric support has gap at most \(\ell_{k,\sigma}\). The origin singleton has gap zero and forces every hitting set to contain the origin. If \(0\le L<\ell_{k,\sigma}\), every eligible support contains the origin, so that singleton is itself a hitting set of mass \(2^{-k}\). This proves the first branch of the profile without excluding dependent supports.

At \(L\ge\ell_{k,\sigma}\), all supports are eligible. In addition to the origin singleton, each antipodal pair is an affinely independent barycentric support with coefficients \(1/2,1/2\) and gap exactly \(\ell_{k,\sigma}\). The pairs are mutually disjoint and disjoint from the origin. Every hitting set therefore has mass at least \(2^{-k}+2^{4k}\sigma2^{-4k}=\sigma+2^{-k}\). The set consisting of the origin and the whole lower layer has this mass and hits every barycentric support: a support avoiding the origin has positive total coefficient on the lower layer by its first-coordinate equation. This proves the second branch, including \(L=\ell_{k,\sigma}\). The lower bounds use only affinely independent supports and the upper bounds hit all supports, so the two conventions agree directly.

At an exposed outer vertex, applying its exposing functional to any barycentric representation shows that every positive source coefficient must be on that same vertex. Its only support is consequently the singleton, with gap zero. The displayed outer hitting profiles follow. At the origin the ratio below the threshold is at most \(2^{-k}\), attained at zero, and at or above the threshold it is maximized at the threshold, with value \((\sigma+2^{-k})/(1+\ell_{k,\sigma})\). Upper-layer vertices contribute exactly one and lower-layer vertices contribute less than one. This proves the exact global score formula.

For the reserve bounds, write \(s=\sigma+2^{-k}\) solely in this paragraph. One has \(s>1\) and \(s\le k+1/2<2^{4k}\); the final inequality follows from \(2^{4k}\ge16k\), which also follows by induction. The formulas are \(T=(2^{4k}+s)/2^{4k+1}\) and \(R=2^{4k+1}s/(2^{4k}+s)\). They give \(1/2<T<1\) and \(R>1\). The latter also follows directly from \((2^{4k+1}-1)s>2^{4k}\). Further, \(R<2s\), yielding \(R<3\) when \(\sigma=1\), and \(R<3k\) when \(\sigma=k\).

Now take \(\sigma=1\). Since \(2^{-k}\le1/2<\log2\le k\log2\), the global score formula gives \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)=1\). Given \(C_1,c_1>0\), choose a finite integer \(k\) such that \(k\log2>C_1(1+\log3)^2\) and \(2^{-k}<c_1\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1(1+\log R)^2&<\ell_{k,1},\\
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C_1(1+\log R)^2;w)}{\tau_*}
&=\frac{2^{-k}}{1+2^{-k}}<c_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The origin is the only marginal maximizer, so a choice among tied deepest sites cannot rescue the proposed conclusion.

Finally take \(\sigma=k\). We already proved \(\ell_{k,k}\ge(k\log2)/2\). Since \(k+2^{-k}\le3k/2\), the exact score is at most \(3/\log2\). Together with \(R<3k\) and \(\tau_*\ge k\), this gives

\[
\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)(1+\log R)^2}{\tau_*}
\le\frac{3}{\log2}\frac{(1+\log(3k))^2}{k}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus for every \(c_2>0\) some finite member violates the proposed global reserve bound. The additional limits in the statement follow from the exact formulas: \(2^{-k}/k\to0\), \((k+2^{-k})/2^{4k}\to0\), and \((\log k)/k\to0\) give \(\tau_*/k\to1\), \(R/(2k)\to1\), and \((k+2^{-k})/(1+\ell_{k,k})\to1/\log2>1\). Hence the maximum defining the global score has that same limit.

Every conclusion used the original atom weights and the complete positive lattice support. All containing halfspaces were closed, both layers and all boundary atoms were retained in their appropriate counts, and the profile jump includes its equality cutoff. Since an upper singleton always gives \(\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)\ge1>T\), this family preserves a positive logarithmic score at the lattice-centerpoint scale even while both reserve comparisons fail.
