## Statement

Let $K=\{(x_1,x_2,z):0\le z\le1,\ |x_i|\le z,\ i=1,2\}$, $V=|K|=4/3$, and $y=(0,0,c)$ with $c=19/25$. Let $f_K(y)$ be ordinary raw depth over closed halfspaces and $p_{KK}(y)$ the infimum of the sum of two raw cap volumes for one nonzero normal $u$ and thresholds $u\cdot y\pm t$, $t\in\mathbb R$. Then $f_K(y)=6859V/15625$, attained by $z\le c$, and $p_{KK}(y)\le13428V/15625$. This certificate is below $2f_K(y)$ by $58V/3125$. For every $0<\varepsilon\le1/1000$ the proper upward cuts at $1-\varepsilon$ and $13/25+\varepsilon$ have total cost at most $2f_K(y)-389V/25000$.

## Proof

The uniform law on $K$ is $(ZU,ZU',Z)$ with $U,U'$ independent uniform on $[-1,1]$ and independent $Z$ of density $3z^2$ on $[0,1]$, by the Jacobian $z^2$. A through-$y$ normal with zero vertical component gives half the volume by simultaneous transverse reflection. For positive vertical coefficient, divide the coefficient out and write the cap as $Z(1+W)\ge c$ with $W$ a linear combination of $U,U'$. The conditional captured probability is $\phi(w)=0$ for $1+w\le c$ and $1-c^3/(1+w)^3$ otherwise. A negative vertical coefficient gives the complementary probability of a positive-coefficient cap because each cutting plane is null.

Write $A\ge B\ge0$ for the absolute coefficients in $W$. If $B=0$ its law is uniform on $[-A,A]$, with a point mass when $A=0$. If $B>0$, the convolution of the two interval densities is the mixture of uniforms on $[-v,v]$ with density $v/(2AB)$ for $v\in[A-B,A+B]$. Indeed the mixing mass is one, and integrating $1/(2v)$ against that density for $v\ge |w|$ gives the convolution density $(A+B-\max(A-B,|w|))_+/(4AB)$.

The uniform average $Q(v)$ of $\phi$ over $[-v,v]$, with $Q(0)=1-c^3$, is
$$
Q(v)=\begin{cases}
1-c^3/(1-v^2)^2,&0\le v\le1-c,\\
[1+v-3c/2+c^3/(2(1+v)^2)]/(2v),&v\ge1-c.
\end{cases}
$$
The first expression decreases to $Q(6/25)$, with $Q(6/25)-c^3=1002251/15015625>0$. In the second range, $15625\cdot 4v(1+v)^2[Q(v)-c^3]=3814v^3+3253v^2-4936v+2484$. The quadratic part is positive because its leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is $-7957712$. Thus each positive-coefficient cap has normalized volume at least $c^3$.

For the opposite orientation set $\psi=1-\phi$. When $0\le w\le1-c$, convexity of $x^{-3}$ gives $\psi(w)+\psi(-w)\ge2c^3$. For $w>1-c$ one has $\psi(-w)=1>2c^3$. Symmetry of $W$ proves the same lower bound for the normalized complementary cap. The purely horizontal case has $1/2>c^3$. Every containing cap contains its through-point translate. The cut $z\le c$ attains $c^3V$ by the distribution of $Z$.

The two upward thresholds $1$ and $13/25$ average to $c$ and have total normalized cost $1-(13/25)^3=13428/15625$. The first cut has zero volume. For the proper cuts the normalized cost is $C(\varepsilon)=2-(1-\varepsilon)^3-(13/25+\varepsilon)^3$, and $C'(\varepsilon)\le3$ on the stated interval. Hence $2c^3-C(\varepsilon)\ge58/3125-3/1000=389/25000$. The two endpoint cuts can be taken from the one lifted halfspace $z-c+t j\ge0$ at bases $j=-1,1$. Its lifted convex hull also contains the middle copy $K$, and the certificate counts the endpoint volumes.
