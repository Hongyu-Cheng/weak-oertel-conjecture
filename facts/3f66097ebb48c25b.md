## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be the convex body in the canonical hard-branch and dyadic setting of accepted facts `036a276c54ec5e66`, `9c47a4888e8a2947`, and `32aedd8da549416a`, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
\[
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C).
\tag{1}
\]
Retain the upstream actual transverse atlas of accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66`. For each pooled physical endpoint \(y\), let \(\mathcal O(y)\) be the finite set of positive atlas occurrences having \(y\) as an opposite source. For \(\alpha\in\mathcal O(y)\), write \(\theta_\alpha>0\) for its atlas weight, \(x_{\alpha,y}\in\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)\) for its actual endpoint contact, and \(g_{\alpha,y}\) for its fixed source density. Thus
\[
 \int_{C_y}g_{\alpha,y}=\theta_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \frac1{\theta_\alpha}\int_{C_y}xg_{\alpha,y}(x)\,dx=x_{\alpha,y},
\tag{2}
\]
and, for every subfamily of occurrences and every physical source fiber, the corresponding source densities have pointwise sum at most \(1/2\). Retain also the pooled weights from accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947`:
\[
 \tau_y=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal O(y)}\theta_\alpha>0.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(\mathcal M_2\) be the globally endpoint-disjoint same-parity dyadic matching of accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947`, with weights
\[
 t_e=\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}\in\mathcal M_2).
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(v=(v_e)_{e\in\mathcal M_2}\) be any dual-feasible stress in that accepted normalization, and put
\[
 \omega_e:=h_{D_e}(v_e),
 \qquad
 F_+(v):=\{e\in\mathcal M_2:\omega_e>0\},
 \qquad
 T_*:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal M_2}\omega_e.
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
 0\leq\omega_e\leq t_e\leq\min\{\tau_u,\tau_w\},
 \qquad
 T_*=\sum_y\sigma_y,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\sigma_y\) are the endpoint stress loads of accepted fact `9c47a4888e8a2947`. The all-zero stress is allowed. All further assertions involving ranks or ratios are conditional on \(T_*>0\).

For every \(e=\{u,w\}\in F_+(v)\), there is a nonnegative occurrence transport array
\[
 \lambda^e=(\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta})_{
   \alpha\in\mathcal O(u),\ \beta\in\mathcal O(w)}
\tag{7}
\]
satisfying
\[
 \sum_{\beta\in\mathcal O(w)}\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}
 \leq\theta_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal O(u)}\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}
 \leq\theta_\beta,
 \qquad
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}=\omega_e.
\tag{8}
\]
For every positive transport cell retain the full cross-occurrence label
\[
 \ell=(e,\alpha,\beta),
 \qquad
 m_\ell:=m_e:=\frac{u+w}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 X_\ell:=\frac{x_{\alpha,u}+x_{\beta,w}}2.
\tag{9}
\]
Then every cell has the actual full-mass target realization
\[
 \boxed{
 X_\ell\in\mathsf M_{\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}}(C_{m_e}),
 \qquad
 \delta_C(m_e,X_\ell)
 \geq\gamma_d\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}.}
\tag{10}
\]

The arrays in (7)--(8) may be chosen so that the following exact concentration property holds. Define
\[
 \theta_y^{\max}:=
 \max_{\alpha\in\mathcal O(y)}\theta_\alpha,
\tag{11}
\]
\[
 b_e:=\min\{\omega_e,\theta_u^{\max},\theta_w^{\max}\}
 \qquad(e=\{u,w\}\in F_+(v)),
\tag{12}
\]
and, for every distinct positive-stress midpoint base,
\[
 \mathcal M(v):=\{m_e:e\in F_+(v)\},
 \qquad
 b_m:=\max_{\substack{e\in F_+(v)\\m_e=m}}b_e.
\tag{13}
\]
There is one simultaneous choice of the transports such that the largest positive cell over a fixed edge \(e\) has weight exactly \(b_e\), and hence the largest positive cross-occurrence label over a fixed physical midpoint base \(m\) has weight exactly \(b_m\). Moreover, these values are optimal under (8): no source-feasible transport family can have a cell over \(e\) larger than \(b_e\), or a cell over \(m\) larger than \(b_m\).

Fix such a transport family. Let \(\mathscr L(v)\) be its set of positive cross-occurrence labels, counted with labels before any atom aggregation, and define the single positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v):=
 2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\ell=(e,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathscr L(v)}
 \lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}\,
 \delta_{(m_e,X_\ell)}.}
\tag{14}
\]
It is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), has exact mass
\[
 \boxed{|\nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)|=2\gamma_dT_*,}
\tag{15}
\]
and is genuinely halfspace-dominated by the mixed-volume measure:
\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{16}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). This remains true with repeated physical source endpoints in the upstream atlas, repeated use of one occurrence contact in several transport cells, collisions among different dyadic midpoint bases, and coincident continuous target atoms. No independently produced target-fiber densities are added in proving (16).

Let \(P_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)\) be the support of (14) after coincident atoms are aggregated, put
\[
 A_{\mathrm{occ}}:=
 \operatorname{aff}\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}
 P_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)
 =\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M(v),
 \qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{occ}}:=\dim A_{\mathrm{occ}},
\tag{17}
\]
and define
\[
 r_{\mathrm{occ}}:=
 \min_{a:A_{\mathrm{occ}}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{X-a(m):(m,X)\in P_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq
 2\gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{T_*}{r_{\mathrm{occ}}+1}.}
\tag{19}
\]
In the canonical hard branch, where \(D<Q/100\), this forces
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathrm{occ}}+1>
 200\gamma_d\frac{T_*}{Q}
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}.}
\tag{20}
\]
For the gauge-load-minimal optimizer used in accepted fact `32aedd8da549416a`, one has
\[
 T_*>
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)Q,
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\mathrm{occ}}+1>
 (59\gamma_d-2)2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}.}
\tag{21}
\]

There is also one legal residual selector that retains cross-occurrence labels without adding incompatible selectors. Define the exact retained occurrence fraction
\[
 B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v):=
 \sum_{m\in\mathcal M(v)}b_m,
 \qquad
 \eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v):=
 \frac{B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)}{T_*}\in(0,1].
\tag{22}
\]
For every \(m\in\mathcal M(v)\), select one positive label \(\ell_m\) of weight \(b_m\). Put
\[
 r_\sharp:=
 \min_{a:A_{\mathrm{occ}}\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{X_{\ell_m}-a(m):m\in\mathcal M(v)\}.
\tag{23}
\]
Then \(r_\sharp\leq r_{\mathrm{occ}}\), and the points \(X_{\ell_m}\) with the single family of weights \(\gamma_db_m\) form one legal residual selector. Therefore, in the normalization of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`,
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d\,2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)}{r_\sharp+1}
 =
 \gamma_d\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{T_*}{r_\sharp+1}.}
\tag{24}
\]
The numerator \(B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)\) is exactly the most transport mass that the source-feasible architecture can retain when it is required to use at most one cross-occurrence label over each distinct physical midpoint base. Thus (12)--(13) separate the two losses exactly: \(b_e<\omega_e\) is occurrence fragmentation at the endpoints of \(e\), while replacing \(\sum_{e:m_e=m}b_e\) by \(\max_{e:m_e=m}b_e\) is midpoint congestion.

Finally retain the stress-frame score \(\mathfrak F_*(v)\) in the normalization of accepted fact `f1e56bd72bbfe076`. Equation (24) gives the following sufficient condition for the desired high-support stress-frame inequality:
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{4\gamma_d\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}}
 {r_\sharp+1}
 +\frac{8\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*}
 \geq1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T_*\leq4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+8\mathfrak F_*(v).}
\tag{25}
\]
Consequently, failure of that stress-frame inequality forces the exact strict obstruction
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{4\gamma_d\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)
 2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}}
 {r_\sharp+1}
 +\frac{8\mathfrak F_*(v)}{T_*}<1.}
\tag{26}
\]
The theorem does not bound \(\eta_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)\) away from zero or \(r_\sharp\) from above. It therefore does not itself prove the high-support stress-frame inequality or the weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

Fix \(e=\{u,w\}\in F_+(v)\). Equations (3), (4), and (6) give
\[
 0<\omega_e\leq\tau_u=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal O(u)}\theta_\alpha,
 \qquad
 0<\omega_e\leq\tau_w=\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal O(w)}\theta_\beta.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus a transport satisfying (8) exists. For example,
\[
 \lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}:=
 \omega_e
 \frac{\theta_\alpha}{\tau_u}
 \frac{\theta_\beta}{\tau_w}
\tag{28}
\]
has row sum \(\omega_e\theta_\alpha/\tau_u\leq\theta_\alpha\), column sum \(\omega_e\theta_\beta/\tau_w\leq\theta_\beta\), and total mass \(\omega_e\).

We prove the sharper concentration assertion. Choose \(\alpha_0\in\mathcal O(u)\) and \(\beta_0\in\mathcal O(w)\) attaining the maxima in (11), put \(b=b_e\), and assign mass \(b\) to the cell \((\alpha_0,\beta_0)\). The residual row and column capacities have total masses \(\tau_u-b\) and \(\tau_w-b\), both at least \(\omega_e-b\). If \(r:=\omega_e-b>0\), let \(A:=\tau_u-b\), \(B:=\tau_w-b\), let \(a_\alpha\) and \(c_\beta\) be the residual capacities, and set
\[
 \lambda'_{\alpha\beta}:=
 r\frac{a_\alpha}{A}\frac{c_\beta}{B}.
\tag{29}
\]
The row sums in (29) are at most \(a_\alpha\), the column sums are at most \(c_\beta\), and the total is \(r\). Adding the distinguished cell produces (8). If \(r=0\), the distinguished cell alone is the required transport. If \(r>0\), then \(b<\omega_e\), so at least one of the distinguished row and column capacities is exhausted by the mass \(b\); hence (29) adds no further mass to that cell. Every feasible cell is at most the total \(\omega_e\), its row capacity, and its column capacity. Hence it is at most \(b_e\), while the construction attains \(b_e\). Since different dyadic edges have disjoint source endpoints, these transports can be chosen independently and simultaneously. Taking a maximum over the edges sharing a physical midpoint proves (13), including its optimality assertion.

For a positive cell \(\ell=(e,\alpha,\beta)\), define source densities
\[
 G_{\ell,u}:=
 \frac{\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}}{\theta_\alpha}
 g_{\alpha,u},
 \qquad
 G_{\ell,w}:=
 \frac{\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}}{\theta_\beta}
 g_{\beta,w}.
\tag{30}
\]
Each is admissible, has mass \(\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}\), and has barycenter \(x_{\alpha,u}\), respectively \(x_{\beta,w}\). The endpoints \(u,w\) have the same parity, so \(m_e\) in (9) is integral. Apply the positive-root fiber-metronoid convexity of accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` to the two equal-root triples represented by (30). Their midpoint gives the first assertion of (10). The metronoid-depth theorem of accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives the second assertion.

We next prove the simultaneous source budget used for global domination. Fix a physical dyadic endpoint \(y\). Endpoint-disjointness gives one incident edge \(e(y)\). Suppose first that \(y=u\) in the notation above. Summing (30) first over the opposite occurrence \(\beta\), and then over \(\alpha\), gives pointwise on \(C_u\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}G_{(e,\alpha,\beta),u}
 &=\sum_\alpha
 \frac{\sum_\beta\lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}}
 {\theta_\alpha}g_{\alpha,u}\\
 &\leq\sum_\alpha g_{\alpha,u}
 \leq\frac12.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
The same proof applies when \(y=w\). Any subfamily of transport cells obeys the same upper bound. This calculation is exactly where repeated uses of one upstream occurrence and repeated physical atlas endpoints are charged: all reuse of \(g_{\alpha,y}\) is limited by the corresponding row or column capacity, and the dyadic matching never charges one physical source through two different edges.

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace. If \((m_e,X_\ell)\in H\), then at least one of
\[
 (u,x_{\alpha,u}),
 \qquad
 (w,x_{\beta,w})
\tag{32}
\]
belongs to \(H\), because their midpoint is \((m_e,X_\ell)\). Orient the labeled transport cell toward one such source point. For each physical source \(y\), let \(\mathscr Q_y(H)\) be the cells oriented toward it, and put
\[
 F_y:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathscr Q_y(H)}G_{\ell,y},
 \qquad
 M_y:=\int_{C_y}F_y.
\tag{33}
\]
Equation (31) gives \(0\leq2F_y\leq1\). If \(M_y>0\), the barycenter of \(F_y\) is a convex combination of source points lying in the fiber section \(H_y\), so it also lies in \(H_y\). The density \(2F_y\) is admissible, has mass \(2M_y\), and has the same barycenter. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \geq2\gamma_dM_y
\tag{34}
\]
when \(H_y\) is a proper halfspace. If \(H_y=\mathbb R^d\), the same estimate follows from \(2M_y\leq a_y\) and \(\gamma_d\leq1\); an empty section contains no oriented source point. Summing (34) over the distinct physical source fibers gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)(H)
 &=2\gamma_d
 \sum_{\ell:(m_\ell,X_\ell)\in H}
 \lambda_\ell\\
 &=2\gamma_d\sum_yM_y\\
 &\leq\sum_y\operatorname{vol}_d(C_y\cap H_y)
 \leq\mu_C(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
This proves (16) without adding target-fiber densities. Coincident target atoms merely aggregate positive summands in the measure, while different edges with one common midpoint remain separately labeled throughout the orientation. Equations (8) and (5) give
\[
 |\nu_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)|
 =2\gamma_d\sum_e\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
 \lambda^e_{\alpha\beta}
 =2\gamma_d\sum_e\omega_e
 =2\gamma_dT_*,
\tag{36}
\]
which proves (15).

Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to (14). Its base projection is exactly \(\mathcal M(v)\), and its intrinsic ranks are (17)--(18), irrespective of atom collisions. It gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \frac{2\gamma_dT_*}
 {2^{\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}(r_{\mathrm{occ}}+1)}.
\tag{37}
\]
Multiplying by \(2^n\) proves (19). Combining (19) with \(D<Q/100\) proves (20). Accepted fact `32aedd8da549416a` gives the displayed lower bound on \(T_*\) for the canonical gauge-load-minimal optimizer. Substitution into (20) gives
\[
 200\gamma_d\frac{T_*}{Q}
 >200\gamma_d
 \left(\frac{59}{200}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)
 =59\gamma_d-2,
\]
which proves (21).

For each \(m\in\mathcal M(v)\), the concentration construction supplies a positive cross-occurrence label \(\ell_m\) with weight \(b_m\). Equation (10) gives
\[
 0<\gamma_db_m
 \leq\delta_C(m,X_{\ell_m}).
\tag{38}
\]
The bases in \(\mathcal M(v)\) are pairwise distinct by definition, so (38) is one legal residual selector, not a sum of capacities from different selectors. The definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) in accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \geq
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{\mathrm{occ}}}
 \frac{\sum_mb_m}{r_\sharp+1},
\tag{39}
\]
which is (24). The selected support is a subset of \(P_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)\) and has the same base affine hull. Restricting an affine map attaining (18) to the selected atoms shows \(r_\sharp\leq r_{\mathrm{occ}}\).

It remains only to verify the exact obstruction interpretation. For any source-feasible transport, a cell over \(e=\{u,w\}\) is at most \(\omega_e\), \(\theta_u^{\max}\), and \(\theta_w^{\max}\), hence at most \(b_e\). At a fixed physical midpoint base, a one-label selector can retain at most the largest cell among all colliding edges, hence at most \(b_m\). The concentration construction attains all \(b_e\) simultaneously, so \(B_{\mathrm{occ}}(v)=\sum_mb_m\) is the exact optimum for this architecture. This proves the assertions following (24).

Finally, multiply (24) by four and add \(8\mathfrak F_*(v)\), where accepted fact `f1e56bd72bbfe076` supplies the stated frame normalization. The left side is at most \(4R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+8\mathfrak F_*(v)\). Condition (25) therefore implies the high-support stress-frame inequality. Its contrapositive is (26). No bound proved here excludes the joint possibility that occurrence weights are highly fragmented, many dyadic edges collide at the same midpoint, and the selected continuous points have high residual rank. Hence no stronger conclusion is claimed.
