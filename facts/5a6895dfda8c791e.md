## Statement

Let $K$ be a planar triangle or quadrilateral containing $0$ in its interior. Let three balanced spanning normals define through-source caps each of area strictly less than $|K|/2$. Use the canonical bridge triangle $T$ at the same six boundary-ray endpoints. A sector's core is the triangle spanned by its two ray endpoints and $0$. The unique sectors belong to exactly one cap in their interiors, and the double sectors belong to exactly two.

If an open unique sector contains no vertex of $K$, its intersection with $K$ is its core. If all three open unique sectors contain vertices and no open double sector does, the double sectors of $K$ are cores and $\Psi(K)\le4$, strictly if $K\ne T$. Here for a body $B$ with these ray endpoints,
$$
f_i(B)=\frac{2\sqrt{|B|Q_i(B)}}{\sigma_i\sum_{s=1}^3 Q_s(B)/\sigma_s},
\qquad
\Psi(B)=\sum_i f_i(B)^2+\prod_i f_i(B),
$$
where $Q_i(B)$ is its cap area and $\sigma_i$ is its cutting chord length divided by the norm of its normal.

In the remaining case $K$ is a quadrilateral with exactly one vertex in each open unique sector and one in a single open double sector. The three caps of $T$ strictly isolate its distinct vertices. The unique sector opposite the enlarged double sector is unchanged, and the other two unique sectors have strictly positive reciprocal losses $\ell_i,\ell_j$, where $\ell_i=1/(2X_i(K))-1/(2X_i(T))$ for the unique-sector areas $X_i$. Put $g=1/(2Y(T))-1/(2Y(K))$ for the enlarged double-sector areas $Y$. In the triangle's reciprocal sector matrix let $\lambda_i,\lambda_j$ be the positive scales of the two rank-one unique blocks, written as $\lambda_i(\beta_i,-\alpha_i)^\top(\beta_i,-\alpha_i)$ with positive kernel $(\alpha_i,\beta_i)$ normalized by $\alpha_i+\beta_i=1$, and likewise for $j$. Let $\theta_i,\theta_j$ be the entries of these two kernels at the endpoints of the enlarged double sector. Then
$$
g\left(\frac{\theta_i^2}{\ell_i}+\frac{\theta_j^2}{\ell_j}+\frac1{\lambda_i}+\frac1{\lambda_j}\right)=1.
$$

## Proof

The polar angles of the boundary of a convex polygon containing $0$ in its interior parametrize its boundary in cyclic order. Between consecutive ray endpoints any boundary vertex in the relative interior of that arc lies in the corresponding open sector. In the absence of such a vertex the arc is the chord between the endpoints. This proves the core assertion, even when an endpoint is a vertex.

Assume all three open unique sectors contain vertices. If the open double sectors contain none, their areas agree with their cores in $T$. The sector comparison gives the other changes as nonnegative unique losses, with the cutting chords fixed. In the ordinary triangle cubic inequality use $T$ with its interior source and the same normals, which allows weak incidences. Then the pure unique loss inequality gives $\Psi(K)\le4$. It is strict when a loss is positive. If no loss is positive, the corresponding unique pieces of $K$ and $T$ have equal area and the former is contained in the latter. Two nested compact convex sets of equal positive area coincide: any omitted point would yield an omitted positive-area set by moving toward a small interior disk of the larger set. Their double pieces already coincide, so $K=T$.

Otherwise vertex counting puts exactly one vertex in each open unique sector and one in one open double sector. A sector with one open vertex has strictly positive excess over its core. The two bridges bordering the unique sector opposite the enlarged double sector are both core boundary chords of $K$. Their lines are also sides of $T$. The boundary edges of $K$ containing the two chords meet at the sole vertex of that unique sector, so that vertex and the entire unique piece agree with $T$'s. For each unique sector the sector inclusion places its open vertex of $K$, strictly beyond its core chord, in $T$. The comparison triangle has one vertex in each closed unique cone. If a given vertex lay on a cutting ray, that sector's boundary arc would have no open vertex and the sector would be its core. Hence all of $T$'s vertices lie in their open unique cones, proving strict isolation. The positive double gain and the zero-loss test for the reciprocal sector matrix now force strictly positive losses in both adjacent unique sectors.

Each ray endpoint of $K$ in this case lies in the relative interior of an edge. Index its six endpoints cyclically by $P_s$ and write $D_s=\det(P_s,P_{s+1})$. Use the reciprocal sector criterion with the actual vertex $u_sP_s+v_sP_{s+1}$ in each noncore sector, and $u_s=v_s=1/2$ in each core sector. On a positive-excess sector put $a_s=u_s+v_s-1$, $r_s=a_s/(D_su_s)$ and $s_s=a_s/(D_sv_s)$. Put both charges equal to zero on core sectors. The straight turns at all six ray endpoints make the ray budget equalities $b_s=r_{s-1}+s_s$ by the determinant formula of the criterion. Its edge coefficient is $h_s=r_s s_s/(r_s+s_s)$ on a positive edge, and zero on a core edge.

The positive edges form two paths on the six ray nodes because exactly two double edges are cores. Choose a positive vector $w$ along those paths with $w_{s+1}/w_s=s_s/r_s$ on every positive edge. Then $h_s(w_s+w_{s+1})/w_s=s_s$ and $h_{s-1}(w_{s-1}+w_s)/w_s=r_{s-1}$, with zero terms on core edges. The budget equalities give $Lw=0$ for the reciprocal matrix $L$. Its block on the four changed nodes is $C-g aa^\top$, where $C$ is the positive definite sum of the two independently updated unique blocks and $a$ indicates the two endpoints of the changed double edge. The restriction of $w$ to that block is nonzero. Thus $I-g(C^{-1/2}a)(C^{-1/2}a)^\top$ is singular, which forces $g\,a^\top C^{-1}a=1$. The inverse diagonals in the two-corner frontier formula are exactly the four displayed terms.
