## Statement

Let $K_0=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and $K_1=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)K_0$, and let $C$ be the convex hull of their lifts at integer bases zero and one. With original mixed volume and residual-covariance normalization of cap normals, the minimum feasible normalized cap budget for six closed caps is $2/9$. It is attained. Every sharp-budget six-cap cover consists of the three canonical cap types at each triangle centroid, with zero mass on its other fiber. All Fritz–John stresses at this budget have zero objective multiplier, even if a cap supports its other fiber. The obstruction also applies to the six-cap covers required to cover the whole continuous space at each of the two sampled bases.

## Proof

The projection is $[0,1]$, and its endpoints are exposed, so the only integer fibers are exactly the two prescribed positive triangles. The hull is full-dimensional. Both triangles have area $1/2$, giving total mixed mass one. Their centroids are $g_0=(1/3,1/3)$ and $g_1=(2/3,1/6)$. Regression on the binary base fits both means exactly. The covariance formula $\Sigma_{K}=\sum_j(v_j-g)(v_j-g)^\top/12$ for a uniform triangle follows by expanding its barycentric second moments, $\mathbb E[\lambda_j^2]=1/6$ and $\mathbb E[\lambda_j\lambda_k]=1/12$ for $j\ne k$. Thus the residual covariance is the average
$$
\Sigma=\tfrac12\big(\tfrac1{144}\begin{pmatrix}8&-4\\-4&8\end{pmatrix}
+\tfrac1{144}\begin{pmatrix}32&-4\\-4&2\end{pmatrix}\big)
=\tfrac1{144}\begin{pmatrix}20&-4\\-4&5\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A cap containing a centroid has mass at least $(2/3)^2(1/2)=2/9$ by baee8ddaa588aef0. At equality its cutting plane goes through the centroid, since shifting a strictly containing plane would remove a slab of positive area. The simplex equality case forces one of the three canonical types, and equality spends all its mass on this fiber. It cannot also contain the other centroid. Any cap missing the first centroid misses a neighborhood of it in its fiber. To cover all points of that neighborhood all three canonical types must occur, since a small move increasing the missing barycentric coordinate and decreasing the other two avoids every other type. The same holds on the other fiber. The six caps therefore exhaust the local types. Each canonical margin can be tilted in the base to exclude the other fiber strictly, since the other triangle is compact and the bases differ. This gives attainment, and each local family covers its entire continuous plane. Every feasible sharp-budget six-cap cover has the same total hinge, because the normalization fixes each own margin and the other trace contributes no hinge. Hence the compact-source optimization has an attained minimum at that budget.

For a positive coverage multiplier, a saddle contact on each fiber satisfies all cap margins $\le0$. For its three own types this forces each barycentric coordinate to be $1/3$, so the only contact is the centroid. Every other fiber cap strictly misses that centroid, since otherwise it would have positive cap mass there. Any positive contact weight for an individual cap is therefore on its own centroid only.

Use the mass and base balances and the covariance-normalized continuous equation from the residual-covariance cap-balance lemma. For a cap based at zero, the actual cap base moment and contact base moment vanish, so the base balance forces $r_i S_{i,1}=0$ for its effective section mass on the other fiber. For a cap based at one, subtract the base balance from the mass balance to get $r_i S_{i,0}=0$. Since the same nonnegative effective section measure gives all the moments, a zero multiplied mass forces zero multiplied continuous moment on that fiber, including supporting coincidences. The proper own section has centroid $g_z$ and is taken with constant coarea density. Subtracting $g_z$ times the mass balance from the continuous balance consequently gives
$$
\alpha_0 c(g_{\rm cap}-g_z)=\alpha_0\Psi_i\Sigma u_i .
$$
For the cap of $K_0$ toward $(1,0)$ its barycentric gradient is $(1,0)$ and $u_i=(6/\sqrt5,0)$. Its mass is $c=2/9$ and its centroid displacement is $(2/9,-1/9)$ by homothety at scale $2/3$ toward the apex. Its hinge is $\Psi_i=c\,u_i\cdot(g_{\rm cap}-g_0)=8/(27\sqrt5)$. The two moment vectors are $(4/81,-2/81)$ and $(4/81,-4/405)$, respectively, with second-coordinate difference $-2/135$. Thus $\alpha_0=0$ in every such stress.

The actual-source and whole-space coverage constraints agree locally in the six-cap parameters. At a sharp cover and a fixed base, the three own continuous normals are affinely independent and their convex hull contains zero in its interior. This follows from the rank-two simplex gradients and their strictly positive dependence, which persists under positive individual normalization. The properties persist under small changes of the normals. The equal-margin equations for the three own caps have a unique solution near the centroid because the two differences of the normals are linearly independent. This point remains in the interior of the own triangle. All other cap margins are strictly smaller there, by their strict negative gap at the original centroid.

At the equal-margin point the maximum of all six margins equals the common own margin. A positive probability combination of the three own normals is zero, so at any other continuous point the same combination of the three margins equals that common value. The maximum is therefore at least the common value everywhere. This point minimizes the maximum on the whole continuous plane and on the own triangle. The two coverage margin functions are identical on this neighborhood, including changes of thresholds and base tilts. Thus their local contact and budget equations give the same obstruction for whole-space coverage.
