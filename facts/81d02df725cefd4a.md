## Statement

\[

## Proof

Global log-concavity gives \(w_j^2\ge w_{j+v}w_{j-v}\) at every integer midpoint. Whenever the endpoint product is positive, \(w_j>0\). Hence \(\Delta_j(v)\ge0\) on its stated domain; zero belongs to each \(\mathcal U_j\) and has curvature zero. At a fixed positive center the cutoff sum changes only at the finitely many values \(\Delta_j(v)\). For any \(L\ge0\), replace \(L\) by the largest such value not exceeding \(L\); the numerator is unchanged and the denominator cannot increase. This proves cutoff attainment, and the finite support proves attainment of the remaining maxima. A center outside the positive support has no positive reflected endpoint pair, so its \(S_j\) and \(H_j\) are zero. Since \(v\mapsto j+v\) is injective and \(\alpha_j(v)\le w_{j+v}\), we also have \(M(w)\le K(w)\le S_*(w)\le W\). The scalar part of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f, applied with precisely this midpoint hypothesis and cutoff normalization, supplies

\[
K(w)\ge\frac{W}{24(n+1)2^n}.
\tag{5}
\]

The padding set is given by finitely many rational affine inequalities. It is bounded and closed. On \(-1\le t\le1\), \(a_r(t)\ge1-1/r^2>0\), because \(r\ge32\). At \((t,x,Y)=(0,0,0)\) all defining inequalities are strict, which proves full dimension. Convexity follows directly from those affine inequalities. At an integer base, \(t\) must be \(-1,0,1\). For \(t=1\) the coordinate intervals are \([0,1]\), for \(t=0\) they are \([-1/2,1/2]\), and for \(t=-1\) they are \([-1,0]\). This proves exactly the three asserted layers, with no additional nonempty integer fibers. Their continuous fibers are cubes of side length \(a_r(t)\), giving the stated rational positive volumes.

On the convex set \(P_r\), the function \(\log F_r=D\log a_r(t)\) is concave. Indeed, \(a_r\) is positive and affine there, and the logarithm is concave. For two endpoints in \(P_r\), their segment remains in \(P_r\), so this proves the log-concavity inequality. If either endpoint is outside \(P_r\), the right side of that inequality for the zero extension is zero and the inequality follows from nonnegativity. Thus \(F_r\) is globally log-concave and finite-valued, including at the boundary and outside the support. Multiplying its inequality by the one for \(f\) proves the global log-concavity of \(f'\) when the endpoint products are positive; if either endpoint product is zero, the required inequality is immediate. Its values at all lattice tuples, including those with an original zero factor or a padding zero factor, are exactly \(w'\). Its positive support is \(A\times A_u\), which is finite.

The elementary inequality \(\log(1-s)\le-s\), for \(0<s<1\), gives \(\epsilon_r\le e^{-r}<1/4\) and \(\eta_r\le e^{-r/2}\le1\). Also \(\eta_r/2^r\le2^{-r}\le1/4\). Therefore \(2^r\le Z_r\le(3/2)2^r\). Finite summation gives \(W'=WZ_r\), so the asserted interval for \(\theta_r\) and the normalized mass identity follow. Since \(A_u\) has \(2^{r+1}+1\) elements, multiplying support cardinalities proves the normalized support formula. The largest padding weight is one, attained on every upper base, and all other padding weights are smaller. This proves \(M(w')=M(w)\).

We next classify every padding center and every positive reflected displacement. A positive endpoint product forces its integer midpoint into \(P_r\cap\mathbb Z^{r+1}=A_u\), either by convexity or by the proved extension. At an upper center \((1,b)\), both endpoint \(t\)-coordinates must equal one. In each remaining coordinate the two Boolean endpoint values have average \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\), so they both equal \(b_i\). The only displacement is zero. At a lower center the same reasoning applies to the coordinate set \(\{-1,0\}\), again giving only zero. At the middle center \(0=(0,0)\), either both endpoint \(t\)-coordinates are zero, in which case both bases are zero, or the endpoints are \((1,b)\) and \((-1,-b)\). Thus its displacements are precisely zero and both orientations of \((1,b)\), for every \(b\in\{0,1\}^r\). It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{(1,b)}(u)&=H_{(1,b)}(u)=1,
&S_{(-1,-b)}(u)&=H_{(-1,-b)}(u)=\epsilon_r,\\
S_0(u)&=\eta_r+2^{r+1}\epsilon_r,
&H_0(u)&=\eta_r+2^{r+1}\sqrt{\epsilon_r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

All other padding centers have zero reflected sums. Every padding zero displacement has curvature zero. All nonzero positive padding displacements have center zero and the common curvature

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_r
&=\log\frac{\eta_r^2}{\epsilon_r}
=-r^3\log\left(1-\frac1{(2r^2-1)^2}\right),\\
0\le\delta_r
&\le\frac{r^3}{(2r^2-1)^2-1}
=\frac r{4(r^2-1)}
\le\frac1{3r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Here \(-\log(1-s)\le s/(1-s)\) follows by integrating \(1/(1-s)\) from zero to \(s\), and the final inequality holds for \(r\ge2\). At any positive product center \((j,\xi)\), the positive displacement set is exactly \(\mathcal U_j\times\mathcal U_\xi(u)\), and the product formula for weights gives \(\Delta'_{(j,\xi)}(v,\zeta)=\Delta_j(v)+\Delta^u_\xi(\zeta)\). This proves the uniform curvature bound in (1), including all zero displacements.

The middle-layer mass is uniformly small in the dimension of the original table. Set \(c_r=\eta_r+2^{r+1}\epsilon_r\). Since \(e>8/3\), as follows already by keeping the first four terms of its exponential series and observing the remaining positive terms, both \(e^{-1/2}<3/4\) and \(2/e<3/4\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_r
&\le e^{-r/2}+2(2/e)^r
<3(3/4)^r
<3^{-4n-3}
\le\frac1{288(n+1)2^n},\\
c_rW&\le\frac{W}{288(n+1)2^n}\le\frac{K(w)}{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The second strict inequality uses \(r=16(n+1)\) and \((3/4)^4=81/256<1/3\). For the final nonstrict inequality in the first line, \(n+1\le2^n\), and \(288\cdot4^n\le27\cdot81^n=3^{4n+3}\) for all \(n\ge1\): it holds already at \(n=1\) and the ratio of right to left increases with \(n\). The second line uses precisely (5). This is the uniform accepted bound needed for the middle-layer comparison.

For clarity, write \(B'^{\Delta}_{(j,\xi)}(L)\) for the product cutoff sum. At an upper padding center the classification and zero padding curvature give \(B'^{\Delta}_{(j,(1,b))}(L)=B_j^\Delta(L)\). At a lower padding center they give \(B'^{\Delta}_{(j,(-1,-b))}(L)=\epsilon_r B_j^\Delta(L)\). These identities hold for every \(j\in A\) and every \(L\ge0\), including cutoffs equal to a curvature value. At the middle padding center, the exact formula is

\[
\begin{aligned}
B'^{\Delta}_{(j,0)}(L)
={}&\eta_r B_j^\Delta(L)\\
&+2^r\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)+\delta_r\le L}}
\left[
\min\{w_{j+v},\epsilon_r w_{j-v}\}
+\min\{\epsilon_r w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

The second sum is empty when \(L<\delta_r\). The two minima are the two padding orientations; the original displacement sum remains oriented as required. Bound them respectively by \(\epsilon_r w_{j-v}\) and \(\epsilon_r w_{j+v}\), and remove the cross-pair cutoff. Each original endpoint sum over \(\mathcal U_j\) is at most \(W\). Thus, for every real \(L\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
B'^{\Delta}_{(j,0)}(L)&\le\eta_r B_j^\Delta(L)+2^{r+1}\epsilon_r W,\\
\frac{B'^{\Delta}_{(j,0)}(L)}{1+L}
&\le\eta_r K(w)+2^{r+1}\epsilon_r W
\le c_rW\le\frac{K(w)}{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

In the second line we used \(K(w)\le W\). Thus every upper padding center reproduces the original cutoff values, every lower one scales them by \(\epsilon_r\le1\), and every middle one is bounded by \(K(w)/12\). These are all positive product centers. A product center outside \(A\times A_u\) has no positive reflected pair, so cannot add any cutoff contribution; no curvature at a zero-weight center is evaluated. Taking the attained maxima proves \(K(w')=K(w)\).

The same exact enumeration without cutoffs gives \(S_{(j,(1,b))}(w')=S_j(w)\), \(S_{(j,(-1,-b))}(w')=\epsilon_r S_j(w)\), and \(S_{(j,0)}(w')\le\eta_r S_j(w)+2^{r+1}\epsilon_r W\le c_rW\). By (8) and \(K(w)\le S_*(w)\), every middle value is at most \(S_*(w)/12\). Outside the positive support all values are zero. The upper layer therefore gives, and no other center exceeds, \(S_*(w')=S_*(w)\).

We derive the Hellinger assertions directly from finite sums. First, let \(W_b=\sum_{z\equiv b\pmod2}w_z\), for the \(2^n\) parity classes. The map \((j,v)\mapsto(j+v,j-v)\) bijects integer center-displacement pairs with ordered same-parity endpoint pairs. The midpoint inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz on the parity masses give

\[
\frac{W^2}{2^n}
\le\sum_bW_b^2
=\sum_{j,v}w_{j+v}w_{j-v}
\le\sum_jw_jH_j(w)
\le WH_*(w).
\tag{11}
\]

Thus \(G(w)\ge0\), with exactly the normalization in accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160. Separating the two displacement sums in the product gives \(H_{(j,\xi)}(w')=H_j(w)H_\xi(u)\). Since the maxima of the nonnegative factors are attained independently, \(H_*(w')=H_*(w)H_*(u)\). Consequently

\[
G(w')=G(w)+\log\frac{2^{r+1}H_*(u)}{Z_r}.
\tag{12}
\]

By (6), \(H_*(u)\ge2^{r+1}\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\), and \(Z_r\le(3/2)2^r\). The elementary integral inequality \(\log(1-s)\ge-s/(1-s)\) gives \(\log\epsilon_r\ge-r-r/(r^2-1)\). It follows that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{2^{r+1}H_*(u)}{Z_r}
&\ge\log\left(\frac83\,2^r\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\right)\\
&\ge r(\log2-1/2)+\log(8/3)-\frac r{2(r^2-1)}
\ge\frac r6.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

To check the last constants, \(\log2\ge2/3\) follows by integrating, or differentiating, \(\log x-2(x-1)/(x+1)\) for \(x\ge1\); its derivative is \((x-1)^2/[x(x+1)^2]\ge0\). Also \(\log(8/3)\ge\log2\ge2/3\), while \(r/[2(r^2-1)]\le1/3\) for \(r\ge2\). Combining (11)--(13) yields \(G(w')\ge r/6=(8/51)n'>n'/8\). The product identities used here have all been proved, rather than imported from a tensor assertion in the supplied transcript.

We now prove the actual-depth comparison. The coordinate regrouping is an ambient coordinate permutation, so it preserves closed halfspaces and the relevant product fiber volumes. At an integer base \((z,\xi)\), the fiber of \(C'\) is exactly \(C_z\times(B_r)_\xi\). Fubini's theorem gives its \((d+D)\)-dimensional volume \(w_zu_\xi\), including zero when the original fiber is empty or has zero \(d\)-dimensional volume. Hence \(W'=WZ_r\). If \(W=0\), every halfspace intersection in each body has mixed volume zero. Both mixed sets are nonempty, and both maximum depths are zero. This proves (2) in that case without defining a normalized table or a positive-center score.

Suppose \(W>0\). Accepted fact ab57fac07c0d3160 supplies the global admissibility of the actual table, retaining every empty and lower-dimensional fiber as a zero. Accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f supplies attainment of \(h_{\max}\) and the bound \(h_{\max}(C)\ge K(w)/12\). Any point of the product mixed set can be written as \((p,\xi,Y_0)\) before regrouping, where \(p\in S\), \(\xi\in A_u\), and \(Y_0\in(B_r)_\xi\). This description includes points whose original fiber has zero volume.

If \(\xi=0\) is the middle padding base, the closed ambient halfspace \(t\le0\) contains this point and has mixed volume \(W(\eta_r+2^r\epsilon_r)\le c_rW\le K(w)/12\le h_{\max}(C)\). Its volume calculation sums the entire middle and lower padding layers; it uses no positive-volume assumption on the original fiber of \(p\).

For an upper or lower padding base, we give an explicit isolating halfspace at every candidate. For an upper base \(\xi=(1,b)\), put \(\ell_{+,b}(t,x)=(r+1)t+\sum_{i=1}^r(2b_i-1)x_i\). Its value at \((1,b)\) exceeds its value at any different upper base by the positive Hamming distance of their Boolean vectors. Its value there is \(r+1+\sum_i b_i\ge r+1\), exceeding the middle value zero. At a lower base \((-1,-c)\), the difference from its value at \((1,b)\) is \(2(r+1)+\sum_i(2b_i-1)(b_i+c_i)\ge r+2\). Thus it strictly exposes \(\xi\) among all integer padding bases, with gap at least one. The padding support is centrally symmetric, so \(\ell_{-,b}=-\ell_{+,b}\) exposes \((-1,-b)\) with the same gap. Denote the appropriate functional for the selected base by \(\ell_\xi\).

Fix any original proper closed halfspace through \(p\), written \(H_a=\{X:a\cdot(X-p)\ge0\}\) with \(a\ne0\), where \(X\in\mathbb R^{n+d}\) contains all original coordinates. Choose \(R>\max_{X\in C}a\cdot(X-p)\), which is finite by compactness. In the product use

\[
\left\{(X,\zeta,Y):
a\cdot(X-p)+R[\ell_\xi(\zeta)-\ell_\xi(\xi)]\ge0\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]

This is a proper closed halfspace containing the candidate, regardless of \(Y_0\). At any other integer padding base its left side is strictly negative for every \(X\in C\), because the exposure gap is at least one. At the selected base it is precisely the original halfspace condition, independent of \(Y\). Its mixed volume is therefore \(u_\xi\mathcal H_d(S\cap H_a)\). A containing original halfspace contains its parallel translate through \(p\), so the infimum over these \(H_a\) equals \(h_S(p)\), without any need for an attaining halfspace. Taking that infimum in the constructed halfspaces proves that the product candidate's depth is at most \(u_\xi h_S(p)\le h_{\max}(C)\). This argument remains valid when \(p\) belongs to a zero-volume original fiber: it only uses \(p\in C\), compactness, and the same fiber-volume product identity. Along with the middle case, it bounds the depth of every product point and proves \(h_{\max}(C')\le h_{\max}(C)\).
