## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
C\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^d
\]
be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
\quad(z\in\mathbb Z^2).
\]
Fix \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^2\) with \(w_{z_0}>0\). For \(x\in C_z\), define
\[
\delta_z(x)
:=
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d
\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(t\geq0\), put
\[
K_z(t):=\{x\in C_z:\delta_z(x)\geq t\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let
\[
D_+:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}.
\]
For \(q\in\{0,1,2\}\), let \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)\) be the family of all
\(q\)-dimensional lattice polytopes \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) such that
\[
z_0\in P,\qquad
\operatorname{vert}(P)\subseteq D_+,
\tag{3}
\]
and for which there exists an affine map
\[
s:\operatorname{aff}(P)\longrightarrow\mathbb R^d
\quad\text{satisfying}\quad
s(v)\in C_v\quad(v\in\operatorname{vert}(P)).
\tag{4}
\]
Write
\[
D(P):=P\cap\mathbb Z^2
\]
and let \(\Theta_P\) be the nonempty set of affine maps satisfying (4).
The family \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)\) is allowed to be empty. In rank zero it is
never empty and consists exactly of \(P=\{z_0\}\).

For \(q=0\), define
\[
\mathcal R_P(z_0):=\{\{z_0\}\}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(q=1\), let \(\mathcal R_P(z_0)\) consist of the two distinct subsets
of \(D(P)\) cut out by the two closed rays in
\(\operatorname{aff}(P)\) with endpoint \(z_0\). For \(q=2\), let
\(\mathcal R_P(z_0)\) consist of the distinct subsets
\[
D(P)\cap Q,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(Q\subseteq\operatorname{aff}(P)=\mathbb R^2\) is a proper closed
halfspace whose boundary contains \(z_0\) and no point of
\(D(P)\setminus\{z_0\}\). This is a finite nonempty row family.

For \(R\in\mathcal R_P(z_0)\) and \(z\in D(P)\), put
\[
A_P(R,z):=\mathbf 1_{\{z\in R\}}.
\tag{7}
\]
Then the anchored affine-certificate capacity from the registered subgoal
has the exact finite max-min representation
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A_{z_0}(C)
=
\max_{\substack{q\in\{0,1,2\}\\
P\in\mathcal P_q(z_0)}}
\ \max_{s\in\Theta_P}
\ \min_{R\in\mathcal R_P(z_0)}
\sum_{z\in R}\delta_z(s(z)).}
\tag{8}
\]
Ranks for which \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)=\varnothing\) contribute no term to
the outer maximum. The rank-zero term is
\[
\max_{x\in C_{z_0}}\delta_{z_0}(x).
\tag{9}
\]
Every maximum in (8) is attained.

Let \(\mathcal A(P)\) be the vector space of all affine maps from
\(\operatorname{aff}(P)\) to \(\mathbb R^d\). For
\(z\in D(P)\) and \(t_z\geq0\), define
\[
\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)
:=
\{s\in\mathcal A(P):s(z)\in K_z(t_z)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
For every \(a\geq0\), the following are equivalent:
\[
\mathfrak A_{z_0}(C)\geq a;
\tag{11}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\text{there exist }q\in\{0,1,2\},\
P\in\mathcal P_q(z_0),\text{ and }
t=(t_z)_{z\in D(P)}\in[0,\infty)^{D(P)}
\text{ such that}\\[1mm]
A_Pt\geq a\mathbf1
\quad\text{and}\quad
\displaystyle\bigcap_{z\in D(P)}\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)
\neq\varnothing .
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
Here the vector inequality has one row for every
\(R\in\mathcal R_P(z_0)\). In rank zero it is the single inequality
\(t_{z_0}\geq a\).

Consequently, for every \(a>0\), strict small capacity has the exact
universal form
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A_{z_0}(C)<a
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{array}{l}
\text{for every }q,\ P\in\mathcal P_q(z_0),\text{ and }t\geq0
\text{ satisfying }A_Pt\geq a\mathbf1,\\[1mm]
\displaystyle\bigcap_{z\in D(P)}\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)
=\varnothing .
\end{array}}
\tag{13}
\]
The universal assertion is vacuous for a rank with
\(\mathcal P_q(z_0)=\varnothing\).

There is an exact finite affine-circuit dual for the empty intersection
in (13). Fix affine coordinates
\[
\xi_z=(\xi_{z,1},\ldots,\xi_{z,q})\in\mathbb R^q
\qquad(z\in D(P)).
\tag{14}
\]
For every fixed \(P,t\), the intersection in (13) is empty if and only if
at least one of the following holds:

1. There is \(z\in D(P)\) with
\[
K_z(t_z)=\varnothing.
\tag{15}
\]

2. Every \(K_z(t_z)\) is nonempty, and there are a set
\[
I\subseteq D(P),\qquad
|I|\leq d(q+1)+1,
\tag{16}
\]
and vectors \(u_z\in\mathbb R^d\), \(z\in I\), not all zero, such that
\[
\sum_{z\in I}u_z=0,
\qquad
\sum_{z\in I}\xi_{z,j}u_z=0
\quad(j=1,\ldots,q),
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{z\in I}
\min_{x\in K_z(t_z)}\langle u_z,x\rangle
>0.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus the Helly support bounds are \(2d+1\) in rank one and \(3d+1\) in
rank two. In rank zero, alternative 2 is impossible for nonempty cores,
and (13) reduces to the empty-core condition
\[
K_{z_0}(a)=\varnothing,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\max_{x\in C_{z_0}}\delta_{z_0}(x)<a.
\tag{19}
\]

Finally, for a fixed \(P\), let \(\Delta_P\) be the probability simplex
on \(\mathcal R_P(z_0)\) and define
\[
c_z(\lambda)
:=
\sum_{\substack{R\in\mathcal R_P(z_0)\\z\in R}}\lambda_R.
\tag{20}
\]
The exact selector/halfspace order in the \(P\)-term of (8) is
\[
\max_{s\in\Theta_P}
\min_{\lambda\in\Delta_P}
\sum_{z\in D(P)}c_z(\lambda)\delta_z(s(z)).
\tag{21}
\]
Denote the displayed sum by \(F_P(s,\lambda)\). It always obeys the weak
minimax inequality
\[
\max_s\min_\lambda F_P(s,\lambda)
\leq
\min_\lambda\max_s F_P(s,\lambda).
\tag{22}
\]
No equality or reversal is asserted. In dimensions \(d>1\), the
unrooted absolute depth used in \(F_P\) need not be concave in the selected
fiber point, even though accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` proves concavity
after taking the \(d\)-th root.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that its base projection is compact, so
\(D_+\) is finite. Hence each \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)\) is finite: every one
of its polytopes has its vertex set in the fixed finite set \(D_+\).

Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` applies to (1). It proves that every
\(K_z(t)\) in (2) is compact and convex, possibly empty, and that
\(\delta_z\) is upper semicontinuous on \(C_z\). It also covers
lower-dimensional fibers: then their \(d\)-volume and
their absolute depths are zero, while \(K_z(0)=C_z\).

We first reduce the registered certificates to lattice polytopes. Let
\((E,s)\) be any admissible pair in the registered definition, and put
\[
P:=\operatorname{conv}(E),\qquad q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(E).
\]
Every vertex of \(P\) belongs to \(E\), so it lies in \(D_+\).
The value of the certificate depends only on \(P\), \(D(P)\), and the
restriction of the same affine map \(s\). Thus replacing \(E\) by
\(\operatorname{vert}(P)\) changes neither its anchored value nor its
rank. Conversely, if \(P\) satisfies (3)-(4), take
\(E=\operatorname{vert}(P)\). For any \(z\in P\), write
\[
z=\sum_{v\in\operatorname{vert}(P)}\lambda_vv,
\qquad
\lambda_v\geq0,\qquad
\sum_v\lambda_v=1.
\]
Affineness gives
\[
(z,s(z))
=
\sum_v\lambda_v(v,s(v)),
\]
which belongs to \(C\) by convexity. Hence \(s(z)\in C_z\) throughout
\(P\), and \((E,s)\) is an admissible registered certificate. This proves
the exact correspondence with the polytopes in \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)\).
If no compatible affine map exists for a proposed polytope, it belongs to
no \(\mathcal P_q(z_0)\) and corresponds to no registered certificate.

For \(P\in\mathcal P_q(z_0)\), choose \(q+1\) affinely independent
vertices. Evaluation at those vertices identifies \(\mathcal A(P)\) with
\(\mathbb R^{d(q+1)}\). The values of a map in \(\Theta_P\) at the chosen
vertices lie in a product of compact fibers. Feasibility at every
additional vertex is a closed condition on those values. Therefore
\(\Theta_P\) is compact.

We next prove the halfspace-row reduction, including closed boundaries.
Let \(q>0\), let nonnegative weights \(b_z\) be assigned to \(D(P)\), and
let
\[
Q=\{y\in\operatorname{aff}(P):\ell(y)\geq\beta\}
\]
be a proper closed halfspace containing \(z_0\), where \(\ell\) is
nonconstant. Since \(\beta\leq\ell(z_0)\), the boundary-through-anchor
halfspace
\[
Q_0:=\{y:\ell(y)\geq\ell(z_0)\}
\]
satisfies \(Q_0\subseteq Q\). Thus
\[
\sum_{z\in D(P)\cap Q_0}b_z
\leq
\sum_{z\in D(P)\cap Q}b_z.
\tag{23}
\]
In rank one the two possible boundary-through-anchor sides give exactly
the two ray rows in the statement, including the case in which \(z_0\) is
an endpoint of \(P\).

Suppose \(q=2\) and the boundary line of \(Q_0\) contains a point of
\(D(P)\setminus\{z_0\}\). Rotate the oriented boundary by a sufficiently
small positive or negative angle around \(z_0\). No point originally off
the boundary crosses it. The nonanchor points originally on the boundary
lie on its two open rays from \(z_0\); after a generic small rotation, the
points on one ray lie in the chosen closed side and the points on the
other ray lie outside it. In either rotational direction, the newly
included former-boundary points form a subset of the points included by
the original closed boundary. Therefore each adjacent generic row has
weight no greater than the original row. There are only finitely many
directions from \(z_0\) to \(D(P)\setminus\{z_0\}\), so the generic
directions form finitely many angular chambers, and the row is constant
inside each chamber. It follows that the infimum over all proper closed
base halfspaces containing \(z_0\) is the minimum over the finite family
\(\mathcal R_P(z_0)\) in (6). This proves the row reduction without
discarding the anchor or any closed-boundary mass.

Apply the preceding reduction with
\[
b_z=\delta_z(s(z)).
\]
The correspondence between registered certificates and
\((P,s)\), followed by the finite row minimum, proves (8), except for
attainment. For a fixed \(P\), each row score is a finite sum of
upper-semicontinuous functions of \(s\). A finite minimum of
upper-semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous. It therefore
attains its maximum on compact \(\Theta_P\). There are finitely many
admissible polytopes, and the rank-zero polytope \(\{z_0\}\) is always
present. Hence the outer maximum is a genuine nonempty finite maximum.
This completes (8), its attainment assertion, and the rank-zero identity
(9).

We now prove the threshold-allocation equivalence. Suppose (11) holds.
By attainment in (8), choose a maximizing certificate \(P,s\) whose
anchored value is at least \(a\), and set
\[
t_z:=\delta_z(s(z))
\qquad(z\in D(P)).
\]
Then \(s\in\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)\) for every \(z\), and every row score is
at least \(a\). Hence (12) holds.

Conversely, suppose (12) holds and choose
\[
s\in\bigcap_{z\in D(P)}\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z).
\]
Every core is contained in its fiber, so \(s(z)\in C_z\) for every
\(z\in D(P)\), in particular at every vertex. Thus \(s\in\Theta_P\).
Moreover,
\[
\delta_z(s(z))\geq t_z.
\]
For each row \(R\),
\[
\sum_{z\in R}\delta_z(s(z))
\geq
\sum_{z\in R}t_z
\geq a.
\]
Equation (8) gives (11). This proves (11)-(12). Since the maximum in (8)
is attained, negating the equivalence at a fixed \(a>0\) gives exactly
(13), including the vacuous ranks with no admissible polytopes.

It remains to prove the affine-circuit dual. Fix \(P,t\), and first suppose
some \(K_z(t_z)\) is empty. Then \(\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)\) is empty, so the
full intersection is empty and alternative 1 holds.

Now suppose every core is nonempty. Each set
\(\mathcal S_{P,z}(t_z)\) is a closed convex subset of the
\(m\)-dimensional parameter space
\[
\mathcal A(P)\cong\mathbb R^m,\qquad m=d(q+1).
\]
If their full intersection is empty, the finite Helly property gives a
set \(I\subseteq D(P)\) with
\[
|I|\leq m+1
\]
whose intersection is already empty. For completeness, the finite Helly
property follows from Radon's affine-dependence argument. If a minimal
empty family had \(k\geq m+2\) members, choose a point \(p_i\) in the
intersection of all members except the \(i\)-th. Any \(m+2\) of the
points have an affine dependence
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i p_i=0,\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i=0,
\]
with both positive and negative coefficients. Normalizing the positive
and negative parts gives a common point of the convex hulls of two
disjoint groups. For each member of the minimal family, one of those two
groups contains no exceptional point for that member. If the member's
index was not among the chosen \(m+2\), both groups have that property.
Thus the common point belongs to every member, a contradiction. Hence
\(k\leq m+1\).

For this \(I\), put
\[
\mathcal K_I(t):=\prod_{z\in I}K_z(t_z)
\subseteq(\mathbb R^d)^I
\]
and
\[
\mathcal L_I(P):=
\{(B\xi_z+b)_{z\in I}:
B\in\mathbb R^{d\times q},\ b\in\mathbb R^d\}.
\tag{24}
\]
The first set is nonempty compact convex and the second is a closed linear
subspace. The selected affine-core constraints have empty intersection
exactly when
\[
\mathcal K_I(t)\cap\mathcal L_I(P)=\varnothing.
\tag{25}
\]

Choose \(k^\ast\in\mathcal K_I(t)\) minimizing the Euclidean distance to
\(\mathcal L_I(P)\), let \(\ell^\ast\) be its orthogonal projection onto
\(\mathcal L_I(P)\), and put
\[
u:=k^\ast-\ell^\ast=(u_z)_{z\in I}.
\]
Compactness gives the minimizing \(k^\ast\), and (25) gives \(u\neq0\).
Orthogonality to every vector in \(\mathcal L_I(P)\) is equivalent to the
moment equations (17): pairing \(u\) first with arbitrary constant maps
and then with each arbitrary linear coefficient of \(B\) gives those
equations, and the equations conversely annihilate every vector in
\(\mathcal L_I(P)\).

The first-order condition for minimizing the squared distance gives
\[
\langle u,k-k^\ast\rangle\geq0
\qquad(k\in\mathcal K_I(t)).
\]
Since \(u\perp\ell^\ast\),
\[
\langle u,k^\ast\rangle
=
\langle u,u+\ell^\ast\rangle
=\|u\|_2^2>0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\min_{k\in\mathcal K_I(t)}\langle u,k\rangle
>0.
\]
The minimum over the product separates by coordinates, so this is exactly
(18).

Conversely, if (17)-(18) hold, every vector in
\(\mathcal L_I(P)\) pairs to zero with \(u\), while every vector in
\(\mathcal K_I(t)\) pairs strictly positively. The two sets are disjoint,
so the selected core constraints, and hence the full intersection, are
empty. This proves the two dual alternatives and their support bound.

In rank zero, \(\mathcal A(P)=\mathbb R^d\) and the evaluation subspace in
(24) is all of \(\mathbb R^d\). It meets every nonempty core. Thus only
the empty-core alternative can occur. Since the sole allocation row is
\(t_{z_0}\geq a\), the universal condition in (13) is equivalent to
\(K_{z_0}(a)=\varnothing\), proving (19).

Finally, for fixed \(P,s\), the minimum of the finitely many row scores is
unchanged if it is taken over probability distributions on the rows,
because a linear function on a probability simplex attains its minimum at
a vertex. This proves (21). The elementary inequality
\[
\max_s\min_\lambda F_P(s,\lambda)
\leq
\min_\lambda\max_s F_P(s,\lambda)
\]
proves (22).

To verify the final warning exactly, take the unit disk
\(B\subseteq\mathbb R^2\). Its absolute depth at the origin is
\(\pi/2\), while its depth at \((1,0)\) is zero. At their midpoint, the
closed cap \(B\cap\{x_1\geq1/2\}\) has area
\[
\int_{1/2}^{1}2\sqrt{1-r^2}\,dr
=
\frac{\pi}{3}-\frac{\sqrt3}{4}
<
\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
The strict inequality follows, for example, from
\(\pi<4<3\sqrt3\).
Hence the unrooted depth at the midpoint is strictly below the average of
the endpoint depths, so it is not concave. This does not prove strictness
in (22); it proves only that the concavity needed for a free minimax
reversal is unavailable. The exact characterization remains
(11)-(18), with the selector chosen before its worst base halfspace.
