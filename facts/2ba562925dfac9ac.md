## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the positive-load hypotheses and canonical notation of accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70` and `c9858b28a86fc01c`. Thus \(Q>0\),
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
 \qquad D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D,
\tag{1}
\]
and the load-minimal optimizer supplies endpoint loads \(\beta_z\), points \(p_z^\flat\), and an origin-symmetric full-dimensional convex body \(D_*\) satisfying
\[
 p_z^\flat+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z)
 \qquad(\beta_z>0).
\tag{2}
\]
On the retained parity classes, let
\[
 s_{\{u,v\}}=\frac{\beta_u\beta_v}{L_\xi},
 \qquad d_z(s):=\sum_{e\ni z}s_e\le\beta_z,
\tag{3}
\]
and, for a positive edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad F_e^\flat:=\frac{p_u^\flat+p_v^\flat}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
For every positive retained label \((\xi,c)\), retain the canonical point \(q_{\xi,c}^\circ\), write \(\ell(e):=(\xi,m_e)\) for the label of \(e\subseteq\xi\), and let
\[
 \mathcal G:=
 \left\{e:s_e>0,
 \ \|F_e^\flat-q_{\ell(e)}^\circ\|_{D_*}\le\frac14\right\}
\tag{5}
\]
be the canonical good-edge family.

Fix an affine map \(a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\), and let \(w=(w_e)_{e\in\mathcal G}\) be arbitrary weights satisfying
\[
 0\le w_e\le s_e,
 \qquad
 w_e>0\Longrightarrow
 \|q_{\ell(e)}^\circ-a(m_e)\|_{D_*}\le\frac14.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
 B_{w,a}:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal G}w_e.
\tag{7}
\]
Then the finite positive measure
\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_{w,a}:=
 \gamma_d\sum_{e\in\mathcal G}w_e
 \delta_{(m_e,a(m_e))}}
\tag{8}
\]
is carried by \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\tau_{w,a}(H)\le\mu_C(H)}
\tag{9}
\]
for every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\). This includes coincident atoms coming from different edges or parity labels.

If \(B_{w,a}>0\), put
\[
 \rho_{w,a}:=
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{m_e:w_e>0\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The continuous residual rank of \(\tau_{w,a}\) is zero, and
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\ge
 \frac{\gamma_dB_{w,a}}{2^{\rho_{w,a}}},
 \qquad
 D\ge
 \gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{w,a}}B_{w,a}.}
\tag{11}
\]
The same affine-tube subfamily is visible to the realizable-submatching capacity:
\[
 \boxed{R\ge\frac1{16}B_{w,a}.}
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, using the two distinct summands \(R\) and \(D\) exactly once,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal A(C)\ge
 \left(\frac1{16}+\gamma_d2^{n-\rho_{w,a}}\right)B_{w,a}.}
\tag{13}
\]
When \(B_{w,a}=0\), equations (8)--(9) hold with the zero measure and the rank-dependent assertions (10)--(13) are void except for their trivial zero-mass numerical content.

Now specialize to the full good-edge weights. In the notation of accepted fact `c9858b28a86fc01c`, put
\[
 W^g_{\xi,c}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal G, e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
 \qquad
 B_g:=\sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c},
\tag{14}
\]
and, for every affine \(a\), define its good-label affine-tube mass by
\[
 B(a):=
 \sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}\le1/4\}}.
\tag{15}
\]
Assume the exact hard inequality and the \(O\)-only side condition
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad I\le\frac Q4.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(B(a)>0\), let \(\rho(a)\) be the affine rank of the physical target bases occurring with positive weight in (15). Then
\[
 \boxed{
 B(a)<
 \frac{Q}{100\bigl(1/16+\gamma_d2^{n-\rho(a)}\bigr)}.}
\tag{17}
\]
For every affine \(a\), including \(B(a)=0\), the dimension-free consequence is
\[
 \boxed{
 B(a)<\frac{Q}{100(1/16+\gamma_d)}
 <\frac{272}{11700}Q.}
\tag{18}
\]

Define the unscaled good-output affine defect
\[
 O_g:=
 \inf_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{O_g>\frac{4759}{11700}Q.}
\tag{20}
\]
Let \(O\) be the canonical labelled-output defect of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`, and let \(O_\triangle\) be the actual triangle-backed conditioned-output defect of accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`. Then
\[
 \boxed{
 O\ge O_g>\frac{4759}{11700}Q,
 \qquad
 O_\triangle>\frac{2321}{46800}Q.}
\tag{21}
\]

Finally, suppose affine maps \(a_1,\ldots,a_K\) have the property that every positive good label \((\xi,c)\) lies in at least one of their affine \(D_*/4\)-tubes:
\[
 W^g_{\xi,c}>0
 \Longrightarrow
 \min_{1\le j\le K}
 \|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a_j(c)\|_{D_*}\le\frac14.
\tag{22}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{K\ge19.}
\tag{23}
\]
Equation (23) is only a necessary consequence of the hard branch. It neither asserts that a finite affine-tube cover with any bounded cardinality exists nor converts the forced tube complexity into a terminal capacity.

## Proof

Fix \(a\) and \(w\) satisfying (6). For an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) with \(w_e>0\), put
\[
 k_e:=a(m_e)-F_e^\flat.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) and the triangle inequality in the common norm give
\[
 k_e=
 \bigl(a(m_e)-q_{\ell(e)}^\circ\bigr)
 +\bigl(q_{\ell(e)}^\circ-F_e^\flat\bigr)
 \in\frac14D_*+\frac14D_*
 =\frac12D_*.
\tag{25}
\]
Therefore (2) supplies the two shifted source memberships
\[
 x_{e,u}:=p_u^\flat+k_e
 \in\mathsf M_{\beta_u}(C_u),
 \qquad
 x_{e,v}:=p_v^\flat+k_e
 \in\mathsf M_{\beta_v}(C_v).
\tag{26}
\]
Their graph midpoint is exactly the affine-graph atom:
\[
 \frac12\bigl((u,x_{e,u})+(v,x_{e,v})\bigr)
 =\left(m_e,F_e^\flat+k_e\right)
 =(m_e,a(m_e)).
\tag{27}
\]
The two endpoint points lie in their source fibers, so convexity of \(C\) and the same-parity integrality of \(m_e\) prove that every atom in (8) belongs to \(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a closed ambient halfspace. For every edge whose atom (27) lies in \(H\) and whose weight is positive, at least one of the two shifted endpoints in (27) lies in \(H\). Orient the edge toward one such endpoint, choosing arbitrarily if both lie in \(H\). Let \(b_z(H)\) be the total \(w\)-weight oriented toward source \(z\). Then
\[
 b_z(H)\le\sum_{e\ni z}w_e
 \le\sum_{e\ni z}s_e
 =d_z(s)\le\beta_z.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(b_z(H)>0\), the fiber section \(H_z\) contains a point of \(\mathsf M_{\beta_z}(C_z)\). The metronoid halfspace-depth theorem in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
 \ge\gamma_d\beta_z
 \ge\gamma_db_z(H)
\tag{29}
\]
when \(H_z\) is proper. If \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), the same conclusion follows from \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\ge\beta_z\ge b_z(H)\) and \(\gamma_d\le1\). If \(H_z=\varnothing\), no shifted endpoint over \(z\) lies in \(H\), so \(b_z(H)=0\). These cases include vertical ambient halfspaces and boundary atoms. Summing (29) over the physical source fibers gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\ge\gamma_d\sum_zb_z(H)\\
 &=\gamma_d
 \sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal G\\(m_e,a(m_e))\in H}}w_e
 =\tau_{w,a}(H).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (9), including all atom collisions: edge weights are summed at the source-degree level in (28), not pooled as target-fiber densities.

Suppose \(B_{w,a}>0\). The mass of \(\tau_{w,a}\) is \(\gamma_dB_{w,a}\), its intrinsic base rank is (10), and the affine map \(a\) itself makes every continuous residual zero. Hence its continuous residual rank is exactly zero. Apply the dominated-measure residual theorem of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483` to (9). It gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \ge\frac{\gamma_dB_{w,a}}{2^{\rho_{w,a}}}.
\]
Multiplication by \(2^n\) proves (11).

For every edge with \(w_e>0\), equation (25) also says
\[
 a(m_e)\in F_e^\flat+\frac12D_*.
\]
Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives
\[
 F_e^\flat+\frac12D_*\subseteq L_e,
 \qquad \frac1{16}s\le r
\tag{31}
\]
coordinatewise. Thus the edge weights \(w_e/16\) form a positive submatching of the canonical reservoir \(r\), and the single affine map \(a\) hits \(L_e\) on every edge of its support. By the exact realizable-submatching definition of \(R=R_C(r)\) in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`,
\[
 R\ge\sum_e\frac{w_e}{16}
 =\frac{B_{w,a}}{16}.
\tag{32}
\]
This proves (12). In (13), the \(D\)-summand is bounded only once, by (11), and the separate \(R\)-summand is bounded only once, by (12). No lower bounds from two choices of \(a\), from two submeasures, or from two residual-rank calculations are added inside either capacity.

Assume now (16), fix an affine \(a\), and set \(w_e=s_e\) precisely when \(e\in\mathcal G\) and its label is counted by (15), with \(w_e=0\) otherwise. Then \(B_{w,a}=B(a)\). If this mass is positive, its base rank is \(\rho(a)\), so (13) and (16) prove (17). Since \(\rho(a)\le n\), every positive \(B(a)\) also satisfies
\[
 B(a)<\frac{Q}{100(1/16+\gamma_d)}.
\tag{33}
\]
The same inequality is trivial if \(B(a)=0\). Accepted fact `c9858b28a86fc01c` proves the strict rational estimate \(\gamma_d>25/68\). Therefore
\[
 \gamma_d+\frac1{16}
 >\frac{25}{68}+\frac1{16}
 =\frac{117}{272},
\]
and (33) proves (18).

For every affine \(a\), the two threshold conditions in (15) and (19) are complementary, with equality belonging to (15). Hence
\[
 \sum_{\xi,c}W^g_{\xi,c}
 \mathbf1_{\{\|q_{\xi,c}^\circ-a(c)\|_{D_*}>1/4\}}
 =B_g-B(a),
\]
and consequently
\[
 O_g=B_g-\sup_aB(a).
\tag{34}
\]
Accepted fact `c9858b28a86fc01c` gives \(B_g>43Q/100=5031Q/11700\). Equation (18) gives \(\sup_aB(a)\le272Q/11700\). Thus
\[
 O_g>
 \frac{5031-272}{11700}Q
 =\frac{4759}{11700}Q,
\]
which proves (20). Notice that the strict conclusion uses the strict lower bound on \(B_g\); no attainment of the supremum in (34) is required.

Labelwise one has \(0\le W^g_{\xi,c}\le S_{\xi,c}\). Therefore, for every affine \(a\), the bad-label sum defining the canonical defect \(O\) is at least the corresponding sum defining \(O_g\). Taking infima gives
\[
 O\ge O_g>\frac{4759}{11700}Q>\frac Q4.
\tag{35}
\]
The \(O\)-branch theorem `7ec63df101c30343` is therefore applicable and gives the exact actual-output inequality
\[
 O_\triangle\ge\frac18(O-G).
\tag{36}
\]
All summands in (1) are nonnegative, so (16) gives \(G<Q/100=117Q/11700\). Equations (35)--(36) yield
\[
 O_\triangle>
 \frac18\left(\frac{4759-117}{11700}\right)Q
 =\frac{2321}{46800}Q.
\]
This proves (21) using the actual triangle-backed weights and conditioned-output points of accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`; no formal output measure replaces them.

Finally, suppose (22) holds. For each \(j\), equation (18) bounds the total full good-label mass in the \(j\)-th tube by a quantity strictly smaller than \(272Q/11700\). Since the tubes cover every positive good label, subadditivity gives
\[
 B_g
 \le\sum_{j=1}^K B(a_j)
 <K\frac{272}{11700}Q.
\tag{37}
\]
If \(K\le18\), the right side is at most \(4896Q/11700\), whereas \(B_g>5031Q/11700\). This contradiction proves (23). Overlaps only increase the sum in (37), so no disjoint assignment or hidden label-pooling assumption is used.
