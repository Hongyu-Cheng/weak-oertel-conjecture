## Statement

Let $(v_1,v_2,v_3)=((1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1))$, $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1,\ i=1,2,3\}$, and $b=|T|=9/2$. For every planar convex body $A$, writing $h_A$ for its support function,
$$
|(A+T)/2|=\frac{|A|+b+3\sum_j h_A(v_j)}4.
$$
If $(A+T)/2\subseteq K$ for a planar convex body $K$, the same expression lower-bounds $|K|$. For every $i$ such that $R_i=A\cap\{v_i\cdot x\le-1\}$ has positive area, writing $q_i=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge0\}|$,
$$
|K|-q_i\ge\frac{|R_i|+b+3\sum_j h_{R_i}(v_j)}4.
$$

## Proof

Let $S=\sum_i h_A(v_i)$. Translation of $A$ leaves both the area and $S$ unchanged because $\sum_i v_i=0$, so assume $0\in A$. The mixed area polynomial of 2021348e0b2d8c9f, equation (3), gives $|T+tA|=b+2tV_2(T,A)+t^2|A|$ for $t\ge0$. We compute the right derivative at zero.

Write $h_i=h_A(v_i)$. The outer triangle $\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1+th_i\}$ contains $T+tA$. It is a translate of $(1+tS/3)T$, since the three offsets $t(h_i-S/3)$ sum to zero and hence are the three $v_i$-coordinates of a common translation vector. Its area is $b+3tS+O(t^2)$.

For each $i$ choose $a_i\in A$ with $v_i\cdot a_i=h_i$ and put $n_i=v_i/\lVert v_i\rVert$. The portion of the $i$-th facet of $T$ with $v_j\cdot x\le1-Ct$ for $j\ne i$ has length $3\lVert v_i\rVert-O(t)$ as $t\downarrow0$. Choose a fixed $C>0$ large enough that for every $0\le\lambda\le th_i/\lVert v_i\rVert$ and every $j\ne i$, both $v_j\cdot(\lambda n_i)$ and $v_j\cdot(\lambda n_i-ta_i)$ are at most $Ct$. The strips $x+\lambda n_i$ from these shortened facets then lie in $T+t a_i$, because subtracting $ta_i$ satisfies all three constraints of $T$. For $\lambda>0$ they are outside $T$ only in the $i$-th constraint. They are therefore disjoint from $T$ and from one another there. Since $T\subseteq T+tA$, their total area adds $3tS-O(t^2)$ to the lower bound. The derivative of the area is thus $3S$. Setting $t=1$ and scaling by $1/4$ proves the area identity.

For $x\in R_i$ and $y\in T$, the midpoint has $v_i\cdot(x+y)/2\le0$ and is in $K$. The cutting line has zero planar area, so this complementary middle cap has area $|K|-q_i$. Apply the area identity to $R_i$ to conclude.
