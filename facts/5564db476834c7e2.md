## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be nonempty and finite with positive compact convex sampled $d$-fibers $K_z$, $d\ge1$, and let $B=\operatorname{conv}(P)$. Suppose a nonempty compact index space carries jointly continuous uniformly bounded coefficients for affine roofs $q_i(u,z)=b_i(u)-a_i(u)\cdot z$, for unit $u$. Assume $q_i(u,z)\ge h_{K_z}(u)$ and $\min_i q_i(u,z)=h_{K_z}(u)$ at every sampled $z$ and unit $u$. For affine $\ell(z,X)$ use all cuts $u\cdot X+t b_i(u)\ell(z,X)\le q_i(u,z)$ with $z\in B$. For sufficiently small signed $t$ these define compact convex sets with projection $B$ and nonempty interior relative to $\operatorname{aff}(B)\times\mathbb R^d$, positive actual fibers over exactly $P$ and uniform $O(|t|)$ radial change at the sampled sites. At a regular old fiber-boundary point with normal $n$, the inward first speed for the path $t\downarrow0$ or $-t$ with $t\downarrow0$ is $\max_{i:q_i(n,z)=h_{K_z}(n)}(\pm b_i(n)\ell(z,X))$ respectively.

Each of these speeds is a bounded pointwise almost-everywhere limit on the original marked boundary of globally finite jointly continuous signed fields convex in $(z,X)$ at fixed $u$. The convergence passes all pairings with finite measures concentrated on the regular marked boundary, in particular every integrable stress density with respect to its summed original surface measure.

## Proof

For fixed $z$ and any $X\ne0$ in a new fiber, test a cut at $u=X/|X|$. The roofs are uniformly bounded on $B$, and $b_i(u)\ell(z,X)$ is bounded in absolute value by $A(1+|X|)$ uniformly in $i,u,z$. Thus $(1-|t|A)|X|$ is bounded uniformly. On the resulting bounded region and on the old fibers all perturbing affine functions have a common bound $D$. Completeness yields the old support inequalities with error at most $|t|D$ at sampled bases. Hence the new fiber there lies in the outer parallel body of radius $|t|D$ about the old one. Conversely the old homothet of ratio $1-|t|D/r_z$ about an inball center $o_z$ of radius $r_z>0$ has slack at least $|t|D$ in every old cut and stays inside. These two comparisons give positive sampled fibers and uniform $O(|t|)$ radial displacement. The intersection is closed and convex since all cuts are affine and $B$ is closed. Retaining the sampled sites and staying over $B$ give the full original projection and its exact lattice census. The positive sampled fibers and the spanning sampled bases give full affine dimension in $\operatorname{aff}(B)\times\mathbb R^d$.

The joint set of coefficient triples $(u,a_i(u),b_i(u))$ is a compact subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, so convergent subsequences of tight coefficient triples suffice in the following argument. For each sign write the new endpoint at a fixed regular old point $X$ as $X_t=o_z+(1-s_t)(X-o_z)$ for a positive parameter $t$. Old active cuts at normal $n$ give $s_t n\cdot(X-o_z)\ge t(\pm b_i(n)\ell(z,X_t))$. Conversely each new endpoint has a tight cut by compactness of the parameter and normal set, since a positive common slack would allow radial extension. The old slack of such a cut is $O(t)$, and any limit of tight cuts is active at the old point. Uniqueness of the regular normal forces the limiting $u$ to be $n$. Dropping the nonnegative old slack in the tight equation gives the matching upper bound on the upper limit of $s_t/t$, with denominator $n\cdot(X-o_z)\ge r_z$. This gives the stated inward normal speed after multiplying by the denominator.

At each sign take the fields $\max_i[\pm b_i(u)\ell(z,X)-R(q_i(u,z)-u\cdot X)]$. These are globally finite continuous and convex in $(z,X)$ at each fixed normal by compactness and continuity of the indexed affine functions. On the original regular marked boundary every slack is nonnegative and some slack is zero. The fields decrease with $R$, uniformly bounded from above by the largest score and from below by the smallest score there. For maximizing indices along $R\to\infty$, the slacks tend to zero by those bounds, and compactness gives a limit index with zero slack. Continuity then identifies the limit with the largest active score. Dominated convergence on the fixed old regular marked boundary gives the stated pairings. In dimension one use counting measure on the two endpoints.
