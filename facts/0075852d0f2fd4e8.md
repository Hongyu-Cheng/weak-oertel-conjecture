## Statement

Let \(k\ge0\) be an integer, let \(N=k+1\), and put \(Q=2^k\). Let \(D\subseteq\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\) be finite and nonempty, with
$$
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
\qquad
\#\lbrace a:x_a=2\rbrace\le2\quad(x\in D).
\tag{1}
$$
Set \(A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N\). For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\), define the original affine probability law by
$$
w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x),\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad
p_{(x,0)}=p_x=w_x/Z.
\tag{2}
$$
For \(z\in A\) and \(c\in D\), use exactly the original quantities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{u\in A\cap H}p_u,
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
&T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_cs_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Thus the reflection sum is ordered and includes its diagonal once. The halfspaces may be required to have a nonzero normal. Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N},\qquad
T(p)\ge2^{-40}8^{-N}.
\tag{4}
$$
No Boolean point is assumed to belong to \(D\), and no assumption is made on its affine rank.

## Proof

Completeness implies that every integral midpoint \(c=(x+y)/2\) of two points of \(D\) belongs to \(D\). Equation (2) gives the exact identity \(p_c^2=p_xp_y\) at every such midpoint.

For a fixed center \(c\), distinct unordered reflected pairs \(\lbrace x,y\rbrace\), with \(x\ne y\) and \(x+y=2c\), have disjoint endpoints: the partner of an endpoint \(x\) is uniquely \(2c-x\). None of these endpoints equals \(c\). Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains an endpoint of each pair. Indeed, if the halfspace is written as \(\lbrace u:v\cdot u\ge a\rbrace\), exclusion of both endpoints would give \(v\cdot(c,0)<a\), a contradiction. Consequently, if \(R(c)\) is the sum of \(\min(p_x,p_y)\) over any collection of distinct reflected pairs counted in both orders, then
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+\tfrac12R(c),\qquad s_p(c)\ge R(c).
\tag{5}
$$
This applies to halfspaces whose boundaries contain the center and does not use full affine rank. Restricting to nonzero normals does not change this argument. Such containing halfspaces exist, for example \(\lbrace u:u_N\ge0\rbrace\).

In particular, \(\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c\), while the diagonal in (3) gives \(s_p(c)\ge p_c\). Hence, putting \(S_4=\sum_{x\in D}p_x^4\), we have
$$
M_3(p)\ge S_4,\qquad T(p)\ge S_4.
\tag{6}
$$

We next prove a local bound using the entire original probability band. For \(t>0\), set \(\mathcal V_t=\lbrace x\in D:t/2<p_x\le t\rbrace\), and define
$$
\mathcal M(t)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal V_t}p_c\tau_p((c,0))^3,
\qquad
\mathcal T(t)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal V_t}p_cs_p(c)^3.
\tag{7}
$$
Let \(W\subseteq\mathcal V_t\) be any actual subset with \(n=|W|\ge2Q\). Define its deduplicated actual midpoint image by
$$
C(W)=\left\lbrace\frac{x+y}{2}:x,y\in W,\ x\ne y,\ x\equiv y\pmod2\right\rbrace,
\qquad r=|C(W)|.
\tag{8}
$$
Writing \(n_b\) for the number of points of \(W\) in parity class \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\), the number \(v\) of ordered distinct same-parity pairs obeys
$$
v=\sum_b n_b(n_b-1)
\ge\frac{n^2}{Q}-n
\ge\frac{n^2}{2Q}>0.
\tag{9}
$$
Here the first inequality is Cauchy's inequality, and the second uses \(n\ge2Q\). Thus \(r>0\). Every point of \(C(W)\) is integral, belongs to \(D\), and satisfies \(t/2<p_c\le t\), by \(p_c^2=p_xp_y\). In particular \(C(W)\subseteq\mathcal V_t\).

For \(c\in C(W)\), let \(a_c\) be the number of ordered pairs in (8) having midpoint \(c\), and let \(R_W(c)\) sum \(\min(p_x,p_y)\) over those same ordered pairs. Then \(\sum_c a_c=v\) and \(R_W(c)\ge(t/2)a_c\). Hölder's inequality gives \(\sum_c a_c^3\ge v^3/r^2\). Applying (5) at these actual centers, all of which lie in the full band (7), proves the common bound
$$
\begin{aligned}
\min\bigl(\mathcal M(t),\mathcal T(t)\bigr)
&\ge\frac18\sum_{c\in C(W)}p_cR_W(c)^3\\
&\ge\frac{t^4}{128}\sum_{c\in C(W)}a_c^3
\ge\frac{t^4v^3}{128r^2}
\ge\frac{2^{-10}t^4n^6}{Q^3r^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
The factor \(1/8\) is only needed for \(\mathcal M(t)\); using it for both moments is valid.

The max-two hypothesis forces every point of \(C(W)\) to have at most one coordinate equal to two. To see this, suppose a midpoint of a distinct same-parity pair had two coordinates equal to two. Both endpoints must equal two at those coordinates, since all coordinates lie in \([0,2]\). Their max-two budgets force every other coordinate to be binary. Equal parity then forces equality at every other coordinate as well, contradicting distinctness of the endpoints.

If \(r<2Q\), (10) and \(n\ge2Q\ge Q\) imply
$$
\min\bigl(\mathcal M(t),\mathcal T(t)\bigr)
\ge\frac{2^{-12}t^4n^6}{Q^5}
\ge\frac{2^{-12}t^4n^{9/2}}{Q^{7/2}}.
\tag{11}
$$
If \(r\ge2Q\), apply the same construction to the actual subset \(C(W)\subseteq\mathcal V_t\). Its actual midpoint image \(B=C(C(W))\) consists entirely of Boolean points. Indeed, a midpoint with a coordinate equal to two would force both endpoints to have their only possible two at that coordinate. All their remaining coordinates are binary, so equal parity would force the endpoints to coincide. Thus \(B\subseteq D\cap\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\) and \(|B|\le Q\). By (9) applied to \(C(W)\), this second image is nonempty. By the affine identity it remains in the same original band \(\mathcal V_t\). Therefore (10), now with endpoint cardinality \(r\) and image cardinality \(|B|\), yields
$$
\min\bigl(\mathcal M(t),\mathcal T(t)\bigr)
\ge\frac{2^{-10}t^4r^6}{Q^3|B|^2}
\ge\frac{2^{-10}t^4r^6}{Q^5}.
\tag{12}
$$
The left side in (10) and (12) is the same quantity, defined using all points of the original band. Denote their respective rightmost expressions by \(F\) and \(G\). It is at least \(\max(F,G)\), hence at least
$$
F^{3/4}G^{1/4}
=\frac{2^{-10}t^4n^{9/2}}{Q^{7/2}}
\ge\frac{2^{-12}t^4n^{9/2}}{Q^{7/2}}.
\tag{13}
$$
This uses a maximum, not a sum, so any overlap between the first and second images causes no double counting. Combining (11) and (13), we have proved for every such \(W\) the local estimate
$$
\min\bigl(\mathcal M(t),\mathcal T(t)\bigr)
\ge2^{-12}Q^{-7/2}t^4|W|^{9/2}.
\tag{14}
$$

We now sum this estimate over disjoint bands. If \(S_4\ge2^{-40}Q^{-3}\), (6) immediately gives the stronger common lower bound \(2^{-37}8^{-N}\), since \(Q^{-3}=8\,8^{-N}\). We may therefore assume \(S_4<2^{-40}Q^{-3}\).

Set \(t_0=2^{-10}/Q\), \(t_j=t_02^{-j}\) for \(j\ge0\), and
$$
V_j=\mathcal V_{t_j},\qquad n_j=|V_j|,\qquad
m_j=\sum_{x\in V_j}p_x,\qquad
\mathcal J=\lbrace j\ge0:n_j\ge2Q\rbrace.
\tag{15}
$$
These \(V_j\) are bands of all points of \(D\), including its Boolean and one-two points; they are not bands restricted to double points. The mass of points above \(t_0\) is bounded by
$$
\sum_{\substack{x\in D\\p_x>t_0}}p_x
\le t_0^{-3}S_4<2^{-10}.
\tag{16}
$$
Every remaining point lies in exactly one of the half-open bands (15). The total mass in bands with \(n_j<2Q\) is at most
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}2Qt_j=4Qt_0=2^{-8}.
\tag{17}
$$
It follows that
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j
>1-2^{-10}-2^{-8}=\frac{1019}{1024}>\frac12.
\tag{18}
$$
All probabilities are positive and \(D\) is finite, so only finitely many bands are nonempty. In particular \(\mathcal J\) is finite and nonempty in the present case.

Apply (14) with \(W=V_j\). Since \(m_j\le t_jn_j\), and since the full bands \(V_j\) are disjoint, both original moments satisfy
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p),\ T(p)
&\ge\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\min\bigl(\mathcal M(t_j),\mathcal T(t_j)\bigr)\\
&\ge2^{-12}Q^{-7/2}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^{9/2}t_j^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
There is no reuse of a center across these bands, including at dyadic boundaries, because all restrictions in (7) use the same half-open probability convention.

Hölder's inequality with exponents \(9/2\) and \(9/7\), applied to
\(m_j=(m_j^{9/2}t_j^{-1/2})^{2/9}t_j^{1/9}\), gives
$$
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j\right)^{9/2}
\le
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^{9/2}t_j^{-1/2}\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}t_j^{1/7}\right)^{7/2}.
\tag{20}
$$
The geometric sum satisfies
$$
\sum_{j\ge0}t_j^{1/7}
=\frac{t_0^{1/7}}{1-2^{-1/7}}
<16t_0^{1/7}.
\tag{21}
$$
For the strict numerical inequality, Bernoulli's inequality gives
\((15/16)^7\ge1-7/16=9/16>1/2\), so \(2^{-1/7}<15/16\). Equations (18), (20), and (21) imply
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}m_j^{9/2}t_j^{-1/2}
>2^{-37/2}t_0^{-1/2}
>2^{-19}t_0^{-1/2}.
\tag{22}
$$
Substituting \(t_0^{-1/2}=2^5Q^{1/2}\) in (19) therefore gives, in this remaining case,
$$
M_3(p),\ T(p)
>2^{-26}Q^{-3}=2^{-23}8^{-N}.
\tag{23}
$$
The atomic case and (23) both imply (4). The proof treats every affine field and affine rank, uses only actual points forced by completeness, and does not require a nonempty Boolean part in advance.
