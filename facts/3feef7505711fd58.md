## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Put $S=(\lbrace-1\rbrace\times A)\cup(\lbrace0\rbrace\times K)\cup(\lbrace1\rbrace\times B)$, measured by $d$-dimensional Hausdorff measure $\mu$ on $S$. Fix $y\in K$ and let $Q$ be the infimum of $\mu(S\cap H)$ over all closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(0,y)$.

Suppose $H_\alpha=\lbrace(z,x):u\cdot(x-y)+\alpha z\ge0\rbrace$, with $u\ne0$, attains $Q$. Let $U,q,V$ be the respective $d$-volumes of its traces on $A,K,B$. Write $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$ and $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. Suppose $q\ge\Gamma|K|$ and $U^{1/d}+V^{1/d}\ge2(1-\eta)q^{1/d}$ for some $0\le\eta\le1$. Set $D=2(1-\eta)^d$ and $\rho=(D\Gamma)^{1/d}$. Then the normalized depth at $(0,y)$ satisfies

$$
\frac{Q}{M}\ge\frac{(1+D)\Gamma}{1+\rho^d+(2-\rho)^d}.
$$

If $\eta\le1/[2(d+1)]$, then $Q/M>8\Gamma/15>\Gamma/2$.

## Proof

Apply the whole-endpoint floors of the finite-tilt lemma to the given three full-dimensional compatible bodies and the given actual depth-attaining cap. This gives $|A|,|B|\ge U+V$. Convexity of $t\mapsto t^d$ for $t\ge0$, also valid for zero captured volume and $d=1$, gives $U+V\ge2[(U^{1/d}+V^{1/d})/2]^d\ge Dq$. Thus $Q=q+U+V\ge(1+D)\Gamma|K|$ and $|A|,|B|\ge D\Gamma|K|$.

Set $a=|A|^{1/d}/|K|^{1/d}$ and $b=|B|^{1/d}/|K|^{1/d}$. Ordinary Brunn–Minkowski on the whole endpoint bodies and their midpoint inclusion give $a+b\le2$, while the floors give $a,b\ge\rho$. The binomial theorem gives $(1+1/d)^d\ge2$, so $\Gamma\le1/2$ and $0\le\rho\le1$. Increase one root to saturate the sum and maximize the convex function $a^d+(2-a)^d$ over $[\rho,2-\rho]$. Its maximum is $\rho^d+(2-\rho)^d$ at an endpoint, including a constant function when $d=1$ and a singleton when $\rho=1$. It follows that $M\le[1+\rho^d+(2-\rho)^d]|K|$. Dividing the raw depth lower bound by this upper bound gives the displayed ratio.

For the strict estimate, assume $\eta\le1/[2(d+1)]$. Bernoulli's inequality gives $D\ge2(1-d\eta)>1$. Put $c_0=1-\log 2\in(0,1)$. Since $\log(1+1/d)<1/d$, one has $\Gamma>e^{-1}$. Therefore $\rho=(2\Gamma)^{1/d}(1-\eta)\ge(1-c_0/d)(1-\eta)\ge1-c_0/d-\eta$ and

$$
(2-\rho)^d\le e^{d(1-\rho)}\le e^{c_0+d\eta}<e^{3/2}/2<9/4.
$$

For the last comparison, the exponential series gives $e<8/3+(1/24)/(1-1/5)=87/32$ by bounding the tail from term $1/4!$ by a geometric series, strictly from the term $1/6!$ onward. The exact comparison $(87/32)^3=658503/32768<81/4$ proves $e^{3/2}<9/2$. Finally $\rho^d=D\Gamma\le D/2$, so the normalized-depth bound divided by $\Gamma$ exceeds $(1+D)/(13/4+D/2)$. This function is strictly increasing for $D\ge0$ since the derivative has positive numerator $11/4$. Its value at $D=1$ is $8/15$, proving the strict margin.
