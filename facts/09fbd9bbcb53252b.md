## Statement

For $N\ge2$, let $X=(X_1,\ldots,X_N)$ be uniform on the standard simplex $\{x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\}$ in its affine hyperplane. For every $c\in[0,1]$,
$$
\Pr(X_i\le c\text{ for every }i)\le [1-(1-c)^{N-1}]^N.
$$
For a centroid-zero $d$-simplex $T$, set $\rho_d=|T\cap(-T)|/|T|$. Then $\rho_d<1/3$ for every $d\ge5$. The exact values for $1\le d\le7$ are $1,2/3,1/2,46/125,22/81,3364/16807,151/1024$ in that order.

## Proof

Conditional on $X_i=x$ for almost every $x\in[0,1)$, the remaining coordinates have the law of $(1-x)Y$, where $Y$ is uniform on the $(N-1)$-coordinate simplex. This follows from Fubini in the affine hyperplane and affine scaling of each conditional section, including its singleton section in the case $N=2$. For a subset $I$ of the other indices, $g(x)=\Pr(X_j\le c\text{ for every }j\in I\mid X_i=x)$ is nondecreasing because the thresholds in $Y$ are $c/(1-x)$. With $f(x)=1_{\{x\le c\}}$ and an independent copy $X_i'$ of $X_i$,
$$
2\operatorname{Cov}(f(X_i),g(X_i))
=\mathbb E[(f(X_i)-f(X_i'))(g(X_i)-g(X_i'))]\le0.
$$
Thus the joint event with indices $\{i\}\cup I$ has probability at most the product of the event for $i$ and the event for $I$. Iterate over smaller index sets under the same original law. Each marginal tail above $c$ has probability $(1-c)^{N-1}$, since subtracting $c$ from the one coordinate leaves a simplex of linear ratio $1-c$. This proves the first inequality.

Write $T=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le1\ \forall i\}$ with the $d+1$ facet covectors normalized to have sum zero. Such a representation follows from the barycentric coordinates of $T$, which have common value $1/(d+1)$ at zero. In those coordinates $-T$ imposes $X_i\le 2/(d+1)$ for every $i$. Thus $\rho_d$ is the left side of the probability bound with $N=d+1$ and $c=2/N$. For real $x>2$, the derivative of $(x-1)\log(1-2/x)$ is $-\log y+(y-1/y)/2$ for $y=x/(x-2)>1$. As a function of $y$ this vanishes at one and has derivative $(y-1)^2/(2y^2)$, so it is positive. For $N\ge9$ this gives $(1-2/N)^{N-1}\ge(1-2/4)^3=1/8$, and hence $\rho_d\le(7/8)^N\le(7/8)^9<1/3$ since $3\cdot7^9=121060821<8^9=134217728$.

For the remaining finite values use the exact support erosion formula with $A=K=-T$, $B=T\cap(-T)$. All three are convex bodies. The midpoint inclusion follows from $B\subseteq -T$ and the actual opposite supports of both $A$ and $K$ are one. All its barycentric thresholds are $2/(d+1)$. It gives
$$
\rho_d=\frac{1}{(d+1)^d}
 \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}(-1)^j\binom{d+1}{j}(d+1-2j)^d .
$$
Substitution for $d=1,\ldots,7$ gives the listed values, in particular values below $1/3$ for $d=5,6,7$.
