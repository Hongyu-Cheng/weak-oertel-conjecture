## Statement

Let $m\ge0$, $1=r_0\ge r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_m\ge0$, with nondecreasing outward drops $r_j-r_{j+1}$, and set $r_{-j}=r_j$, $b=r_m$, and $M=\sum_{j=-m}^{m}r_j^2$.

For $1/2<a<2/3$, set
$$
\begin{gathered}
c=1/(3a),\quad \rho=1/[3(1-a)],\quad A=a/(2a-1),\quad
L=(1-a)^2/[(2a-1)(2-3a)],\quad B=(2a-1)/(2-3a),\\
k(r)=A(r-c)_+^2-L(r-\rho)_+^2,\qquad
\lambda=(18a^3-24a^2+8a-1)/[27a^2(a-1)],\\
E(b)=
\begin{cases}
(1-b)^2/6 & b\ge \rho,\\
(1-\rho)^2/2-(1-\rho)^3/[3(1-b)] & b<\rho ,
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
$$
where $x_+=\max(x,0)$.

For $m\ge1$, including a constant radius leg and the endpoint cases $b=0,1$,
$$
\sum_{j=-m}^m k(r_j)\ \ge\
\lambda[M-b^2-(1-b)^2/3]+k(b)-2BE(b).
$$

## Proof

Interpolate the radii linearly on $[0,m]$, writing the interpolant $r(z)$ and its drops $\delta_i=r_i-r_{i+1}$. Direct integration gives
$$
3\int_0^1 k(v)\,dv=A(1-c)^3-L(1-\rho)^3=\lambda.
$$
The ratio $k(v)/v^2$ is zero up to $c$, increases on $[c,\rho]$ by its formula $A(1-c/v)^2$, and is nondecreasing on $[\rho,1]$ by the identity
$$
k(v)/v^2=\tfrac12[1-(2/(3v)-a)^2/(a(2-3a))].
$$
In the last interval $2/(3v)-a>0$, and the formulas agree at the joins. Thus $f(v)=k(v)-\lambda v^2$ has nonnegative upper-tail integrals on $[0,1]$: it has total integral zero and can change sign only from negative to positive. Also $f(1)\ge0$. A nonconstant cell contributes to $\int_0^m f(r(z))\,dz$ with density $1/\delta_i$ on $(r_{i+1},r_i)$. Because the nonzero drops are nondecreasing outward, that density, extended by zero below $b$, is a nonnegative nondecreasing step function on $[0,1]$, hence a nonnegative linear combination of upper-interval indicators. Initial constant cells contribute only at radius $1$. It follows that $\int_0^m k(r(z))\,dz\ge\lambda\int_0^m r(z)^2\,dz$.

Set $H(v)=(v-\rho)_+^2$. Since $A-L+B=0$, the $C^1$ function $k+BH$ is convex, with second derivative $2A$ on $(c,\rho)$ and zero elsewhere. On a unit cell whose radius runs from $u$ down to $w<u$, let $\mathcal E(G;u,w)$ be the trapezoid estimate minus the exact integral of $G(r(z))$ over that cell. Convexity gives $\mathcal E(k;u,w)\ge -B\mathcal E(H;u,w)$. Twice integrating by parts gives
$$
\mathcal E(H;u,w)=\frac1{2(u-w)}\int_w^u (s-w)(u-s)H''(s)\,ds .
$$
The kernel $(s-w)(u-s)/(u-w)$ increases when $w$ decreases or $u$ increases, by its respective partial derivatives $-(u-s)^2/(u-w)^2$ and $(s-w)^2/(u-w)^2$. The radius intervals of the nonconstant cells have disjoint interiors. Since $H''\ge0$, summing their errors gives at most $\mathcal E(H;1,b)=E(b)$ for $b<1$, by integrating the clipped square. Constant cells have error zero, and $E(1)=0$.

The full sampled sum is twice the trapezoid sum plus the terminal value $k(b)$. The exact square error on each cell is $\delta_i^2/6$, so
$$
M=2\int_0^m r(z)^2\,dz+b^2+\frac13\sum_i\delta_i^2.
$$
Here $\sum_i\delta_i^2\le(1-b)^2$. Also $\lambda>0$, because $\lambda-2/9=(2a-1)(6a^2-6a+1)/[27a^2(a-1)]>0$ on the stated interval. Combining the inequalities gives the claim.
