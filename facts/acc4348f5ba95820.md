## Statement

For a convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) with positive total integer-fiber volume, use the objects of accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`: \(C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\}\), \(w_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z})\), \(W:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}>0\), and \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). For \(w_{\mathbf j}>0\), write \(\alpha_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v):=\min(w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v})\), \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}:=\{\mathbf v:\alpha_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)>0\}\), and \(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v):=\log(w_{\mathbf j}^2/(w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}))\) on \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}\). The subscripted \(\alpha_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)\) is exactly that predecessor's \(\alpha(v)\) with its center \(j=\mathbf j\); the bold letters only indicate vectors. Set \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L):=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}:\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)\le L}\alpha_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf v)\) and \(K(w):=\max_{\mathbf j:w_{\mathbf j}>0,\,L\ge0}B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)/(1+L)\). Displacements are oriented and include zero, exactly as in that accepted fact.

Define the multivariate Hellinger reflection quantities by \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(w):=\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}\), \(\mathsf H_*(w):=\max_{\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^n}\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(w)\), and \(\mathsf G(w):=\log(2^n\mathsf H_*(w)/W)\). Here \(\mathsf G\) is the global gain from the proposed coupled-profile freezing argument. It is distinct, when \(n>1\), from the one-dimensional normalization \(g=\log(2H_*/W)\) in accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e`. In dimension one, \(\mathsf H_j\) and \(\mathsf H_*\) specialize to that fact's \(H_j,H_*\), and \(\mathsf G=g\). All logarithms below are natural.

(i) Let \(P:=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(6,8,35)\}\subset\mathbb R^3\), and let \(A:=P\cap\mathbb Z^3\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
A=\{&(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(6,8,35),\\
    &(1,1,3),(1,1,4),(2,3,11),(3,4,17)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each parity class in \(\mathbb Z^3/2\mathbb Z^3\) occurs exactly once in \(A\), and no four points of \(A\) are coplanar. For every integer \(m\ge1\), the single convex body \(C_m:=P^m\times[0,1]\subset\mathbb R^{3m}\times\mathbb R\) has integer dimension \(n=3m\), continuous dimension \(d=1\), and weights \(w_{\mathbf z}=\mathbf1_{A^m}(\mathbf z)\). With \(W_m\) denoting its \(W\), it satisfies \(W_m=8^m\), \(\mathsf H_*(w)=K(w)=1=2^{-3m}W_m\), and \(\mathsf G(w)=0\).

For every nonzero \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^{3m}\) and every \(s\in\mathbb R\),

\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{3m}\\\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z=s}}w_{\mathbf z}
\le\frac38 W_m.
\]

The constant \(3/8\) is attained by a hyperplane with a primitive integer normal. The union of any two affine hyperplanes of the base has total weight at most \(3W_m/4\), including two adjacent integer level hyperplanes with a common primitive normal. In particular, no estimate asserting the existence of a hyperplane of weight at least \(\frac12(1-c\mathsf G(w)^2)W\), for all sufficiently small nonnegative global gains and any fixed finite \(c\ge0\), can hold for all actual convex-body profiles. More generally, replacing \(c\mathsf G^2\) by any nonnegative error function whose value at zero is zero still gives a false assertion.

(ii) For \(0<t<1\), let \(b^{(t)}_0=b^{(t)}_1=1\), \(b^{(t)}_2=t^2\), and \(b^{(t)}_k=0\) otherwise. This is a finite log-concave sequence with consecutive positive support. Apply the definitions of accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e` to this sequence, appending a subscript \(t\) to \(W,M,H_j,H_*,g,Q_j,\Delta_j,T_j,\rho_j\). In particular, \(M_t\) is the largest atom, \(Q_{j,t}\) is the Hellinger displacement law, \(\Delta_{j,t}\) is its curvature, and \(T_{j,t}\) is its signed tilt. These are exact aliases of that accepted fact's objects, with \(b=b^{(t)}\); no relative entropy or midpoint probability law is being renamed.

The unique maximizing integer center is \(j=1\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_t&=2+t^2,\qquad M_t=1,\qquad H_{*,t}=1+2t,\\
g_t&=\log\frac{2+4t}{2+t^2},\qquad \rho_{1,t}=\frac{2t}{1+2t},\\
\mathbb E_{Q_{1,t}}\Delta_{1,t}&=\frac{4t}{1+2t}\log(1/t),\\
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{1,t}}(T_{1,t})&=\frac{2t}{1+2t}[\log(1/t)]^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The maximal-atom deficit \( (1-2M_t/W_t)_+\) and the smallest outside-mass fraction \(\min_{a\in\mathbb Z}W_t^{-1}\sum_{k\notin\{a,a+1\}}b^{(t)}_k\) both equal \(t^2/(2+t^2)\). The exact asymptotic constants are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{(1-2M_t/W_t)_+}{g_t^2}
&=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\min_{a\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{k\notin\{a,a+1\}}b^{(t)}_k}{W_tg_t^2}=\frac18,\\
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\mathbb E_{Q_{1,t}}\Delta_{1,t}}{g_t\log(1/g_t)}&=2,\\
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{1,t}}(T_{1,t})}{g_t[\log(1/g_t)]^2}&=1.

\]

The same limits hold if \(\log(1/g_t)\) is replaced by \(\log(e/g_t)\). Consequently the corresponding uniform upper bounds in the accepted one-dimensional theorem cannot be improved to \(o(g^2)\), \(o(g\log(e/g))\), or \(o(g[\log(e/g)]^2)\), respectively. Moreover, the conditional arcosh bound in that theorem is exactly attained for every \(0<t<1\):

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{1,t}}(T_{1,t})
=\rho_{1,t}\left[\operatorname{arcosh}\left(1+\frac{W_t/H_{1,t}-1}{\rho_{1,t}}\right)\right]^2.

## Proof

We first verify the geometry and all finite arithmetic in (i). The barycentric coefficients of \((x,y,z)\) with respect to the four listed vertices of \(P\) are \(1-x-y+13z/35\), \(x-6z/35\), \(y-8z/35\), and \(z/35\). They sum to one. Thus membership in \(P\) is equivalent to the four inequalities \(z\ge0\), \(35x\ge6z\), \(35y\ge8z\), and \(35(x+y)\le35+13z\). The lower bounds on \(x+y\) and its upper bound imply \(14z\le35+13z\), hence \(z\le35\). The determinant of the three nonzero vertices is \(35\ne0\), so \(P\) is full-dimensional.

At height \(z=0\), the integer solutions are \((0,0,0),(1,0,0),(0,1,0)\). At height \(z=35\), the lower bounds \(x\ge6,y\ge8\) and upper bound \(x+y\le14\) give only \((6,8,35)\). For an integer height \(1\le z\le34\), the second and third barycentric coefficients are nonnegative and have sum at most \(1-z/35<1\). Therefore a lattice point, if present, must have \(x=\lceil6z/35\rceil\) and \(y=\lceil8z/35\rceil\). Conversely this forced pair belongs to \(P\) precisely when \(s_z:=35+13z-35\lceil6z/35\rceil-35\lceil8z/35\rceil\ge0\). This temporary integer slack is used only for the exhaustive membership certificate below.

As alternative direct ways to reproduce the two certificates, one may enumerate the bounding box \(0\le x\le6,0\le y\le8,0\le z\le35\) against the four barycentric inequalities, and compute each determinant as that of the four rows \((1,p_i),(1,p_j),(1,p_k),(1,p_l)\). Now \(C_1=P\times[0,1]\) is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior. Its fiber at a base point is \([0,1]\) on \(P\) and empty outside \(P\), so its integer weights are exactly \(\mathbf1_A\). If both \(\mathbf j+\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf j-\mathbf v\) belong to \(A\), their difference is \(2\mathbf v\), so they have the same parity. Parity injectivity forces the endpoints to coincide, and hence \(\mathbf v=\mathbf0\). It follows that \(\mathsf H_{\mathbf j}(w)=1\) for \(\mathbf j\in A\) and zero otherwise. At any positive center, \(\mathcal U_{\mathbf j}=\{\mathbf0\}\), \(\alpha_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf0)=1\), and \(\Delta_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf0)=0\). Therefore \(B_{\mathbf j}^{\Delta}(L)=1\) for every \(L\ge0\), and \(K(w)=1\), attained at \(L=0\). There are eight unit weights, giving \(W=8\) and \(\mathsf G(w)=\log(8/8)=0\).

Noncoplanarity implies that every affine hyperplane in \(\mathbb R^3\) contains at most three points of \(A\), for any real nonzero normal. Thus every such hyperplane has weight at most three. The plane \(z=0\) contains exactly \(p_0,p_1,p_2\), so the upper bound is attained by the primitive integer normal \((0,0,1)\).

For general \(m\ge1\), the product \(P^m\) is a full-dimensional compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R^{3m}\); consequently \(C_m=P^m\times[0,1]\) is an actual convex body with \(d=1\). Its integer base support is \(A^m\), and each weight is one. The coordinatewise product of the parity bijections for \(A\) is a parity bijection for \(A^m\). The same reflected-endpoint argument therefore gives \(\mathsf H_*(w)=K(w)=1\), while \(W_m=8^m=2^{3m}\), proving all global gain identities.

To prove the hyperplane estimate for this product, decompose a nonzero normal into blocks \(\mathbf a=(\mathbf a^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf a^{(m)})\), with \(\mathbf a^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^3\), and choose a block \(r\) with \(\mathbf a^{(r)}\ne0\). Fix the other \(m-1\) supported blocks. The equation \(\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z=s\) then imposes one proper affine-plane equation on the remaining block \(\mathbf z^{(r)}\in A\). It has at most three solutions by the three-dimensional certificate. There are \(8^{m-1}\) choices of the fixed blocks, so the hyperplane has at most \(3\cdot8^{m-1}=(3/8)W_m\) supported points. The plane requiring the third coordinate of the first block to vanish attains this bound and has a primitive integer normal. Subadditivity bounds the weight of a union of two hyperplanes by \(3W_m/4\); disjointness is not needed.

The base hyperplane weight is exactly the mixed volume of its lift \(S\cap(\{\mathbf z:\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}\mathbf z=s\}\times\mathbb R)\), because the retained fibers all have length one. In particular these are actual losses when freezing the base to one affine slice. Since the upper bound is \(3W_m/8<W_m/2\) while the global gain is exactly zero, every asserted single-slice lower bound described in (i) fails. Two adjacent integer hyperplanes are a special case of the union bound. Finally, \(K(w)=2^{-3m}W_m\) is equality at the desired sufficient scale, so these calculations supply no counterexample to that sufficient inequality or to the locked target.

We next prove the distinct one-dimensional calibration (ii). The sequence has positive support \(\{0,1,2\}\). The only nontrivial discrete log-concavity inequality is at index one and reads \(1\ge t^2\). The inequalities at indices zero and two have a zero factor on the right, as do all inequalities outside the support. Alternatively, interpolate the logarithms with slope zero on \([0,1]\) and slope \(2\log t<0\) on \([1,2]\), then extend by zero as a weight outside \([0,2]\). This is a log-concave extension and verifies the hypotheses of accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e` directly.

The reflection sums, from their definitions in that accepted fact, are \(H_{0,t}=1\), \(H_{1,t}=1+2t\), \(H_{2,t}=t^2\), and zero at all other integer centers. Since \(0<t<1\), the unique maximizing center is one. Also \(W_t=2+t^2\) and \(M_t=1\), giving the stated formula for \(g_t\). It is positive because \(4t>t^2\). The two largest atoms are exactly the adjacent sites zero and one. No other adjacent pair has combined weight two, and the outside fraction for this pair is \(t^2/(2+t^2)\). This is also the maximal-atom deficit, because \(1-2/(2+t^2)=t^2/(2+t^2)>0\).

At center one, the positive displacements are precisely \(-1,0,1\). Their probabilities are \(Q_{1,t}(0)=1/(1+2t)\) and \(Q_{1,t}(-1)=Q_{1,t}(1)=t/(1+2t)\). Their curvatures are \(\Delta_{1,t}(0)=0\) and \(\Delta_{1,t}(-1)=\Delta_{1,t}(1)=2\log(1/t)\). Their tilts are \(T_{1,t}(0)=0\), \(T_{1,t}(1)=\log t\), and \(T_{1,t}(-1)=-\log t\). Thus the mean tilt is zero, \(\rho_{1,t}=2t/(1+2t)\), and direct summation gives both displayed moment formulas. The curvature expectation here is the mean of the scalar second difference, not the relative entropy \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}\) appearing in accepted fact `280b299bc1e87c14`.

For the asymptotics, the formula \(g_t=\log(1+2t)-\log(1+t^2/2)\) extends continuously to zero at \(t=0\). Differentiation at zero gives \(\lim_{t\downarrow0}g_t/t=2\). Hence \(\log(1/g_t)=\log(1/t)-\log(g_t/t)\), with the second term tending to the finite number \(\log2\), while \(\log(1/t)\to\infty\). It follows that \(\log(1/t)/\log(1/g_t)\to1\). Substitution into the already proved exact formulas gives, with positive denominators for all sufficiently small \(t\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t^2}{(2+t^2)g_t^2}&\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2\cdot2^2}=\frac18,\\
\frac{4t\log(1/t)}{(1+2t)g_t\log(1/g_t)}&\longrightarrow\frac42=2,\\
\frac{2t[\log(1/t)]^2}{(1+2t)g_t[\log(1/g_t)]^2}&\longrightarrow\frac22=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Adding one to \(\log(1/g_t)\) does not change these limits. Since all three limiting ratios are strictly positive, a uniform upper bound by the respective smaller little-o order would fail along this family. In particular the same construction certifies the outside-mass order even when the adjacent pair is chosen optimally, and the two moment orders at the unique maximizing center.

Finally, all denominators below are positive for \(0<t<1\), and exact simplification gives

\(1+(W_t/H_{1,t}-1)/\rho_{1,t}=1+(1-t)^2/(2t)=(1+t^2)/(2t)=\cosh(\log(1/t))\).

Because \(\log(1/t)>0\), applying \(\operatorname{arcosh}\) yields precisely \(\log(1/t)\). Multiplying its square by \(\rho_{1,t}\) gives the exact variance already calculated, proving equality in the conditional arcosh estimate of accepted fact `b85cce72a986b40e`. Equivalently, the reflected overlap contains all three positive atoms and the absolute tilt is constant conditional on a nonzero displacement. This completes the two stated obstructions.
