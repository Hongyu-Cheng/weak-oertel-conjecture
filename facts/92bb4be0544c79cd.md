## Statement

Put $p_1=(1,0)$, $p_2=(0,1)$ and $p_3=(-1,-1)$. At $p_j$ put $[-1,0]$, at $-p_j$ put $[0,1]$, and at zero put $[H/2-B,H/2+B]$, $B\ge2$, $0<H<1$. These are exactly the seven integer fibers of their canonical hull. Use the unit ramp $\Psi(q)=(q+1)_+-q_+$ at smoothed width $h=H/\sigma$ for the actual pooled within-fiber standard deviation $\sigma$. In continuous orientations $u=\pm1$, the nonvertical ramp argument at $(p,y)$ is $(a\cdot(z-p)+u(t-y))/H$; retain ordinary closed pure-base caps. The unique deepest mark is $(0,H/2)$, with raw depth $Q=B+3+H/2$ and mass $M=2B+6$. It has actual opposite attaining continuous orientations. For the untilted positive cap, the limiting records as $H\downarrow0$ have $q=-1/2$, $\tau=1/2$, $\chi=1$ on all six outer supports, with equality in every simultaneous full-normal Bregman test and every transition quadratic. Nevertheless the original complementary measures $\mu_{\rm out}=\tfrac12\sum_j\delta_{p_j}$ and $\mu_{\rm in}=\tfrac12\sum_j\delta_{-p_j}$ have equal mass and first moment and neither dominates the other in convex order. Thus there is no ordinary martingale coupling in either direction for these measures, nor for the full original support measures or the selector measures.

## Proof

The hexagon of the six outer bases is defined by $|z_1|\le1$, $|z_2|\le1$, $|z_1-z_2|\le1$. Its six listed vertices are exposed and the seven displayed lattice points are all its integer points. Every combination of outer fibers at zero stays in $[-1,1]$ inside the given middle fiber, so the lifted hull has the prescribed sections.

At $(0,H/2)$ pair $(p_j,t)$ with $(-p_j,-t)$. Their positive-orientation arguments sum to $-1$ for every tilt, and $\Psi(q)+\Psi(-1-q)=1$. The outer capture is exactly three. For the negative orientation the sum is $1$, so monotonicity gives capture at least three. A sufficiently large generic finite tilt saturates one fiber of each pair and excludes the other, realizing equality. The centered middle interval captures $B+H/2$ in either direction by integrating the ramp on the interval of width $H$ inside the body. Every pure-base cap through zero captures the whole middle interval and at least one vertex from each opposite pair, so its mass is at least $2B+3$. This proves the stated depth and actual opposite menu directions. For every other middle mark, use the orientation facing its displacement from $H/2$ and a large finite tilt isolating one fiber of each pair. The middle capture decreases strictly from the centered value for small displacement because the transition lies inside the interval there, and remains monotone for all larger displacements. Each outer vertex has an exposing pure-base cap of mass one. This proves uniqueness.

For the positive untilted cap the outer endpoint $t=0$ has roof argument $-1/2$ at every width. The outside selector is the ramp value, and the inside omission its complement, both $1/2$. The transition footprint is one. In the limit the supporting points are reflected through $(0,0)$ and every common normal displacement changes sign under reflection. For $J'=\Psi$, differentiating $J(q+s)-J(q)-\Psi(q)s$ in $s$ shows its invariance under $(q,s)\mapsto(-1-q,-s)$ by the complement identity and the zero at $s=0$. At $q=-1/2$ this makes each paired cost equal for every simultaneous test. The quadratic footprints likewise agree. The unrestricted squared and absolute incidence comparisons likewise have equality, since each uses the same even function of paired opposite displacements. These are the original supporting measures on the six bases, with complementary factor $1/2$ in each. They have equal mass $3/2$ and zero first moment since $\sum_j p_j=0$. The convex tests $(z_1+z_2-1)_+$ and $(-z_1-z_2-1)_+$ have integrals $(0,1/2)$ and $(1/2,0)$, respectively, in the order outside, inside. Thus neither convex order is possible. A martingale would force the corresponding convex order by conditional Jensen. The full original measures are obtained by multiplying both by two, and the selector layers equal the same half-selected measures. The contradiction holds for each pair.
