## Statement

For integers $n,d\ge2$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, take the three-fiber source with $K_0=A$, $K_1=P/2$ and $K_2=B$ from the preceding ordinary and paired simplex-container obstruction. Set $F=\max_j|K_j|$ and $Q=(F/V)^{1/d}T$ in its notation. At bases $(j,0)$ use $K_j$, and at $(j,1)$ use $Q$, for $j=0,1,2$. Form the hull and take its product with $[0,1]^{n-2}$ in extra integer coordinates. These are exactly the $6\cdot2^{n-2}$ positive actual fibers at the binary layers. If $W$ is total mixed $d$-volume then each has volume strictly below $W/2^n$. Both deficit sums are at most $2^{n-2}\Gamma\varepsilon^dV$, and convexifying any enclosing simplex containers at the same bases adds at least $2^{n-2}\varepsilon V/(16d^2)$ mixed $d$-volume.

## Proof

The two-coordinate projection is $[0,2]\times[0,1]$. At the two extreme values of the second base coordinate, convex combinations use only that layer, giving the original three-fiber body below and $[0,2]\times Q$ above. Both layer bodies have interior relative to their parallel $(d+1)$-planes, so their hull has ambient interior. Taking the binary product gives the stated census and preserves full dimension. Before padding the mixed volume is $3F+|A|+|P/2|+|B|>4F$ because all three summands from the lower layer are positive and one equals $F$. After padding the total is multiplied by $2^{n-2}$ and every fiber still has volume at most $F$, proving the strict claim.

The upper fibers are simplices with both deficits zero by the preceding proof. The lower three fibers in each binary layer have the preceding deficit estimate. Every integer layer in the additional coordinates is an exposed cube vertex. Fixing it and then fixing the second base coordinate at zero forces the hull of replacement containers to stay in that lower layer, where the preceding arbitrary-container lower bound applies. Sum over the $2^{n-2}$ lower layers and use nonnegativity of upper-layer excess.
