## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies at $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, volumes $0<a\le b$ for the endpoints and $|K|=1$. Put $M=1+a+b$, $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, and assume every whole fiber mass is at most $M/2$. For $0<Q\le\gamma_d M/2$, let
$
F_Q(q)=(a-Q+q)^{1/d}+b^{1/d}-2(1-q)^{1/d},\qquad \max(0,Q-a)\le q\le1.
$
There is a unique root $h(Q)$ strictly inside this interval, strictly increasing in $Q$ in the stated range. For every nonvertical ambient cap of raw mass $Q$ that attains the mixed depth at its middle source $(0,y)$, the middle mass is at most $\min(Q,h(Q))$.

## Proof

Whole-body Brunn–Minkowski gives $a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}\le2$, so $a\le1$, while $b\le1+a$ by nonheaviness. Hence $Q\le\gamma_d(1+a)\le2\gamma_d<1$. The function $F_Q$ is continuous and strictly increasing in $q$, and is positive at $q=1$. If $Q<a$, then $F_Q(0)<a^{1/d}+b^{1/d}-2\le0$. Otherwise the left endpoint $q_0=Q-a$ satisfies $1-q_0\ge(1-\gamma_d)(1+a)\ge(1-\gamma_d)b>b/2$, so $F_Q(q_0)<b^{1/d}-2(b/2)^{1/d}<0$, using $d>1$. Thus the root exists and is interior. If $Q'>Q$ and the old root lies in the new domain, $F_{Q'}(h(Q))<F_Q(h(Q))=0$; otherwise the new left endpoint is already greater than $h(Q)$. Both cases give $h(Q')>h(Q)$.

Write $U,q,V$ for the masses on $A,K,B$ of the given critical cap. By the endpoint floors of 03c59c70402b66a2, $s=U+V=Q-q\le a$. If $s=a$, then $q=Q-a<h(Q)$ from the left-endpoint bound. If $s<a$, both strict endpoint residuals have positive volumes $a-U,b-V$. Their midpoints lie on the strict excluded side of the same original cap in $K$. Apply ordinary Brunn–Minkowski to positive-volume compact subcuts at margins at most $-\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon>0$, then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ to get
$
(a-U)^{1/d}+(b-V)^{1/d}\le2(1-q)^{1/d}.
$
At fixed $s$, the left-hand side as a function of $U\in[0,s]$, $V=s-U$, is concave, so its minimum is at an endpoint. The decrement $t^{1/d}-(t-s)^{1/d}$ is nonincreasing for $t\ge s$, as follows from concavity of the root. Thus the smaller value comes from $U=s,V=0$. This gives $F_Q(q)\le0$ and proves $q\le h(Q)$. The inequality $q\le Q$ follows by nonnegativity of the other captures.
