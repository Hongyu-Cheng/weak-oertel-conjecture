## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, $m=d+1$, $b=1/m$, $h=d/m$, and $\Gamma=h^d$. On a $(d-1)$-simplex with barycentrics $s_1,\ldots,s_d$, let $P$ be uniform probability, $N(s)=\#\{i:s_i\ge b\}$ and $E_d(\psi)=\int(N-m\Gamma)\psi\,dP$. For $0<c<1$ set $E(c)=E_d(\min(s_1,c))$. Write $I_L(c)=0$ for $c<0$ and otherwise
$$
I_L(c)=\frac{cL^{d-1}}{d-1}-\frac{L^d-(L-c)_+^d}{d(d-1)}.
$$
Then $E(c)>0$ and
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(c)&=m\Gamma(d-1)I_1(c)-(d-1)I_h(c-b)-(d-1)^2I_h(c),\\
E(c)&=\left(h^{d-1}-\frac1d\right)(1-c)^d-\frac{d-1}{d}(h-c)_+^d\qquad(c\ge b).
\end{aligned}
$$
For $0<c<b$ one has $E''(c)\ge(h^{d-2}/m)(d-1)(1-c)^{d-2}$, with $E(0)=E'(0)=0$.

Let
$$
k_d=\min\left\{\frac{h^{d-2}}m,\ d h^{d-1}-1-(d-1)((d-1)/d)^d\right\}>0.
$$
For each continuous nonnegative concave $\phi$ on $[0,1]$, write $\mu=-\phi''$ on $(0,1)$ in the distributional sense. Define the affine function
$$
\ell(x)=(1-x)\phi(0)+x\phi(1)
 +(1-x)\int_{(0,b]}c\,d\mu(c)+x\int_{(b,1)}(1-c)\,d\mu(c).
$$
Then $\ell\ge\phi$, $\int(\ell(s_1)-\phi(s_1))\,dP\le E_d(\phi(s_1))/k_d$, and the deficit is zero exactly when $\phi$ is affine.

In the standard facet coordinates $\mathcal B=\{z\in\mathbb R^{d-1}:z_j\ge0,\ \sum_jz_j\le1\}$, let $K_\varepsilon$ be the body with $\sum_jz_j-1\le r\le\varepsilon\phi(z_1)$ over $\mathcal B$. For $\ell(x)=A+Bx$, let $S_\varepsilon$ have the same lower bound and upper bound $\varepsilon\ell(z_1)$ over all $z_j\ge0$. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, $S_\varepsilon$ is an enclosing simplex of volume $(1+\varepsilon A)^d/[d!(1-\varepsilon B)]$, and
$$
1-\frac{|K_\varepsilon|}{|S_\varepsilon|}
=\varepsilon d\int(\ell(s_1)-\phi(s_1))\,dP+O_\phi(\varepsilon^2)
\le\frac{\varepsilon d}{k_d}E_d(\phi(s_1))+O_\phi(\varepsilon^2).
$$
If $\phi$ is fixed and nonaffine, the lower limit of the ratio of the normalized maximum ordinary halfspace depth excess $D(K_\varepsilon)-\Gamma$ to this missing fraction is at least $k_d/m$. For each $0<a<k_d/m$ and all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, this excess is at least $a$ times the missing fraction, and hence at least $a$ times the infimum of the closed-halfspace discrepancies to uniform probability laws on full-dimensional simplices.

## Proof

The cap probability of each facet coordinate $s_i$ at threshold $b$ is $h^{d-1}$ by simplex scaling. Thus $\int N\,dP=dh^{d-1}=m\Gamma$. Symmetry and $\sum_i s_i=1$ give $\int Ns_i\,dP=(\int N\,dP)/d$, so $E_d$ annihilates affine functions.

The marginal $X=s_1$ has density $(d-1)(1-x)^{d-2}$ on $0<x<1$, by slicing the standard simplex. Given $X=x$, the other coordinates are uniform on a simplex scaled by $1-x$. Thus the density of $X$ multiplied by the conditional mean of $N-m\Gamma$ is
$$
(d-1)(1-x)^{d-2}(\mathbf1_{\{x\ge b\}}-m\Gamma)
 +(d-1)^2(h-x)_+^{d-2},
$$
where the last power for $d=2$ denotes $\mathbf1_{\{x<h\}}$. Indeed for $d=2$ the other coordinate is deterministically $1-x$, and the difference at $x=h$ is immaterial. Since $E_d$ annihilates constants and $\min(x,c)=c-(c-x)_+$, integration of this density against $-(c-x)_+$ gives the first formula for $E(c)$. Here $I_L(c)=\int_0^{\min(c,L)}(c-x)(L-x)^{d-2}dx$ for $c\ge0$ and $L>0$, as follows by integrating the power. For $c\ge b$ substitution shows that the coefficient of $c$ cancels since $m\Gamma=dh^{d-1}$, and the constant term cancels since $h+b=1$. The surviving powers give the second formula.

For $0<c<b$, differentiation under the one-dimensional integral gives
$$
E''(c)=(d-1)(1-c)^{d-2}
\left[d h^{d-1}-(d-1)\left(\frac{h-c}{1-c}\right)^{d-2}\right].
$$
Since $(h-c)/(1-c)\le h$ and $dh-(d-1)=1/m$, the claimed bound follows. The zero initial values come from the same integral, so $E(c)>0$ on $(0,b]$. For $c\ge b$ the ratio $E(c)/(1-c)^d$ is nondecreasing because $(h-c)_+/(1-c)$ is nonincreasing. Positivity holds through $c<1$. In particular the second constant in the minimum defining $k_d$ equals $d E(b)/(1-b)^d>0$.

For $c\le b$ the constant $c$ majorizes the hinge $\min(x,c)$ with mean error $(d-1)I_1(c)$, a function with zero value and derivative at zero and second derivative $(d-1)(1-c)^{d-2}$. The bound on $E''$ therefore bounds $E(c)$ from below by the first constant in $k_d$ times this error. For $c>b$ the function $x$ majorizes the hinge with mean error $\mathbb E(X-c)_+=(1-c)^d/d$, by the marginal density. Monotonicity of $E(c)/(1-c)^d$ yields the bound by the second constant times the error.

To integrate the hinge bounds, set $G_c(x)=\min(x,c)-cx$. The Green representation is
$$
\phi(x)=(1-x)\phi(0)+x\phi(1)+\int_{(0,1)}G_c(x)\,d\mu(c).
$$
On any compact subinterval $[\alpha,\beta]\subset(0,1)$ the analogous formula uses the endpoint interpolation there and the kernel $(\min(x,c)-\alpha)(\beta-\max(x,c))/(\beta-\alpha)$ for $x,c\in[\alpha,\beta]$, extended by zero outside in $c$. The integral is finite by local finiteness of $\mu$. The kernel vanishes at the endpoints and has negative distributional second derivative $\delta_c$ in $x$ for interior $c$, so the difference of the two sides has zero distributional second derivative and is affine with zero endpoint values. For fixed interior $x$, as the intervals increase to $(0,1)$, the nonnegative kernels increase to $G_c(x)$ (each factor expression increases as $\alpha$ decreases or $\beta$ increases). Endpoint continuity and monotone convergence give the formula. In particular using $x=b$, the integrals of $c$ on $(0,b]$ and $1-c$ on $(b,1)$ against $\mu$ are finite. Replacing $G_c$ by $c(1-x)$ or $(1-c)x$ according as $c\le b$ or $c>b$ gives the displayed affine majorant. The signed deficit may be integrated in $c$ because $N-m\Gamma$ is bounded and the nonnegative Green mixture is integrable. Tonelli applies to the nonnegative majorant errors. Since the deficit annihilates affine functions, this gives $E_d(\phi(s_1))=\int E(c)\,d\mu(c)$ and the claimed error bound. Strict positivity of every interior hinge deficit makes this zero exactly when $\mu=0$, equivalently $\phi$ is affine.

In the last part put $q=r-\sum_jz_j+1$. In $(z,q)$ coordinates the enclosing simplex has $z,q\ge0$ and $q+(1-\varepsilon B)z_1+\sum_{j=2}^{d-1}z_j\le1+\varepsilon A$, with positive coefficients for small $\varepsilon$. Containment follows from $\ell\ge\phi$. Rescaling coordinates gives the exact volume and integration in $r$ gives $|K_\varepsilon|=(1+\varepsilon d\int\phi\,dP)/d!$. Since $\int\ell\,dP=A+B/d$, division gives the missing-fraction expansion. Apply a8cbe3efd17354fb to the fixed facet roof $\psi(z)=\phi(z_1)$ to get the all-normal depth expansion $D(K_\varepsilon)=\Gamma+\varepsilon(d/m)E_d(\phi(s_1))+o(\varepsilon)$. This part of that theorem concerns $K_\varepsilon$ and is independent of the majorant used here. For nonaffine $\phi$ the mean majorant error is positive, since it is nonnegative and continuous and $X$ has full support. The ratio tends to $E_d(\phi(s_1))/(m\int(\ell-\phi)\,dP)\ge k_d/m$. For the nested bodies the total variation of their uniform laws is the missing fraction, by integrating the positive difference of their densities on the smaller body. The closed-halfspace discrepancy is no larger, giving the last assertion.
