## Statement

Let
$$
P=\mathbb Z^3\cap\{(x,y,z):x,y,z\ge0,\ x+z\ge1,\ x+2y+z\le5,\ 2x+y+z\le4,\ 3x+2z\le6\}.
$$
This is a connected hollow thirteen-site lattice support. For every rational Jensen function $g$ on $P$, its integral graph $T_g=\{(p,g(p)):p\in P,\ g(p)\in\mathbb Z\}$ has relative Helly number at most eight. The empty graph has Helly number zero.

## Proof

The inequalities imply $0\le x\le2$, $0\le y\le5/2$, $0\le z\le3$. Substituting the integers in those ranges gives the following census, with each point lying on at least one defining plane. Thus each anchored representation uses a supporting plane of dimension at most two. The displayed column $g_0$ will be used for the Jensen feature space.

| $i$ | $p_i$ | $g_0(p_i)$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $(0,0,1)$ | 0 |
| 1 | $(0,0,2)$ | 0 |
| 2 | $(0,0,3)$ | 0 |
| 3 | $(0,1,1)$ | 3 |
| 4 | $(0,1,2)$ | 3 |
| 5 | $(0,1,3)$ | 3 |
| 6 | $(0,2,1)$ | 6 |
| 7 | $(1,0,0)$ | 0 |
| 8 | $(1,0,1)$ | 0 |
| 9 | $(1,1,0)$ | 0 |
| 10 | $(1,1,1)$ | 1 |
| 11 | $(1,2,0)$ | 0 |
| 12 | $(2,0,0)$ | 0 |

The following table lists every positive representation of a target from two or three other affinely independent sites. The order of coefficients is the order of source indices.

| target | sources | coefficients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0, 2 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 3 | 0, 6 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 4 | 2, 6 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 4 | 3, 5 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 4 | 0, 5, 6 | $1/4,1/2,1/4$ |
| 4 | 1, 5, 6 | $1/3,1/3,1/3$ |
| 8 | 1, 12 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 8 | 0, 2, 12 | $1/4,1/4,1/2$ |
| 8 | 2, 7, 12 | $1/3,1/3,1/3$ |
| 9 | 7, 11 | $1/2,1/2$ |
| 10 | 5, 11, 12 | $1/3,1/3,1/3$ |

To reproduce the table, for each two- or three-subset $J$ form the matrix with columns $(1,p_j)$, $j\in J$. Use its first nonzero square row minor of order $|J|$, in lexicographic row order, skipping a subset when there is none. For each $i\notin J$, solve that minor against the corresponding coordinates of $(1,p_i)$ by Cramer's rule. Retain exactly the strictly positive solutions that satisfy all four rows. This gives the table using integer determinants and rational comparisons. Every nontrivial anchored representation is a convex combination of those with affinely independent supports after excluding the target as a source and renormalizing the remaining coefficients. This follows by taking the extreme points of the coefficient polytope. Each support lies in a plane, so at most three sources suffice. Consequently the table determines the full Jensen space. The hypergraph joining the target and sources of each row is connected. Every row preserves $g_0$. The rows determine all values from the five indexed $0,2,6,11,12$: first determine $1,3,4$ using their first midpoint rows, then $5$ using the other midpoint for $4$, then $8$ from $1,12$, $7$ from the row for $8$ with sources $2,7,12$, and $9,10$ from their rows. The five functions $1,x,y,z,g_0$ are independent because $p_0,p_2,p_7,p_{11}$ form an affine basis with $g_0=0$ there, and $g_0(p_3)=3$. They are therefore a basis of the Jensen space.

A nine-subset is in convex position if and only if it contains none of the table's target-and-source rows. Necessity follows from the displayed convex representations. Sufficiency follows because a redundant point would have a representation supported on at most three other points in its supporting plane, by taking an extreme coefficient vector. To verify the subset enumeration, encode each table row by the set of all indices in it, then for every four-subset $D$ of $\{0,\ldots,12\}$ retain its complement exactly if $D$ meets every row set. This checks all $\binom{13}{4}=715$ complements and yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\{1,2,3,5,6,7,8,9,10\},\qquad \{1,2,3,5,6,7,8,10,11\},\\
&\{1,2,3,5,6,7,9,10,12\},\qquad \{1,2,3,5,6,8,9,10,11\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
All four contain the indices $1,2,3,5,6$ and omit $4$. In each one the pairs $(2,6)$ and $(3,5)$ give two disjoint midpoint representations of $p_4$. For every Jensen function, its lift is the midpoint of both pairs. With $A_i=(1,p_i,g_0(p_i))$ one also has $A_4=A_3+A_2-A_1$ by the census, so $g(p_4)=g(p_3)+g(p_2)-g(p_1)$ for every Jensen function. Thus when the nine heights are integral the lift of $p_4$ lies in $T_g$ and is in their hull without being one of the nine lifts.

For completeness, every nine points of $T_g$ whose bases fail convex position fail convex position themselves, since Jensen affinity lifts the redundant point's anchored representation. If their bases are in convex position they are one of the four nine-subsets just tested, whose hulls contain the additional point over $p_4$. Hence there is no nine-point empty vertex set in $T_g$. Any subset of an empty vertex set is again an empty vertex set, because any deleted vertex lies outside the smaller hull. The finite-set witness lemma of accepted fact bee3cc8e50a0dcea now gives $h(T_g)\le8$ for every nonempty $T_g$.
