## Statement

Let $d\ge 2$ and let $K\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body of volume $V$ and centroid zero. The only positive-volume integer fibers of a convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ are $A=a+rK$, $B=b+RK$, and $C=c+sK$ at bases $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $r,R,s>0$ and $(A+C)/2\subseteq B$. Write $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$ and $M=V(r^d+R^d+s^d)$. Raw mass means the sum of the $d$-dimensional volumes of the traces on the actual integer fibers. Label the endpoints so that $r\le s$, and assume $s<R$ and $R^d<r^d+s^d$. For every closed ambient halfspace $H$ containing $(0,b)$, its raw mass satisfies
$$
\mu(H)\ge V\left[\gamma_dR^d+\frac{(r-2(R-s))^d}{2}\right]
\ge \frac{\gamma_d M}{2}+\frac{1-2\gamma_d}{4}V(r^d+s^d-R^d)
>\frac{\gamma_d M}{2}.
$$

## Proof

The shear $(z,x)\mapsto(z,x-b-z(c-b))$ preserves all integer bases, all fiber volumes and all closed ambient halfspaces. It sends the middle centroid to $(0,0)$ and gives $B=RK$, $C=sK$, and $A=a'+rK$ with $a'=a+c-2b$. Since $K$ is convex, midpoint compatibility becomes $a'+(r+s)K\subseteq2RK$. Comparing widths gives $2R\ge r+s$. Subtracting the support functions of $(r+s)K$ from those of $2RK$ in the support inequalities gives
$$
a'\in(2R-r-s)K.
$$
This includes $a'=0$ when $2R=r+s$.

Put $\varepsilon=R-s$. We have $2\varepsilon<r$: otherwise, since $s\ge r$ and $d\ge2$,
$$
R^d-s^d\ge(s+r/2)^d-s^d
\ge(3r/2)^d-r^d>r^d,
$$
contradicting the strict upper bound on $R^d$. The copy $A_0=a'+(r-2\varepsilon)K$ lies in $A$ because $0\in K$. It also lies in $sK$, since
$$
(2R-r-s)K+(r-2\varepsilon)K=sK.
$$
In particular $|A_0|=V(r-2\varepsilon)^d$, with the original offset $a'$ retained.

It suffices to bound each closed halfspace through $(0,0)$. Any containing halfspace contains the parallel one through that point. With nonzero continuous normal, write the halfspace as $u\cdot x+tz\ge0$, where $u\ne0$. Its thresholds at the three bases are $t,0,-t$. Apply the measurable-subset conclusion of 7ad3d72ee6061cf3 with convex body $sK$ (centroid zero) and subset $A_0$. Its three terms are the cap masses on $A_0,sK,sK$, respectively, with the two copies of $sK$ at the middle and larger endpoint. They sum to at least $\gamma_d s^dV+|A_0|/2$. The additional mass from the actual middle $RK$ in that very halfspace is exactly
$$
|RK\cap\{u\cdot x\ge0\}|-|sK\cap\{u\cdot x\ge0\}|
=(R^d-s^d)|K\cap\{u\cdot x\ge0\}|
\ge \gamma_d(R^d-s^d)V.
$$
The last inequality is the full-volume centroid case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d. Adding gives the first stated bound. A purely base-normal through-point halfspace contains the whole middle and at least one whole endpoint, so its mass is at least $V(R^d+r^d)$, which also gives the bound. Fibers of zero volume contribute nothing.

Finally the scalar trimming lemma gives $(r-2\varepsilon)^d\ge(r^d+s^d-R^d)/2$. Inserting this estimate and subtracting $\gamma_dM/2$ gives at least $(1-2\gamma_d)V(r^d+s^d-R^d)/4$. This is positive: the bracket is positive by hypothesis, and $\gamma_d<1/2$ by the binomial theorem for $d\ge2$.
