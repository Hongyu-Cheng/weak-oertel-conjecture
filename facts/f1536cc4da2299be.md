## Statement

All logarithms are natural, and entropy on any finite set is \(H(q)=-\sum_xq_x\log q_x\), with \(0\log0=0\).

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf x})_{\mathbf x\in A}\) satisfy \(p_{\mathbf x}>0\) and \(\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}=1\). Assume that its original mean \(\mathbf J_0=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x\) belongs to \(\mathbb Z^n\). Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}A\), \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}A\), and \(U=\operatorname{relint}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), with relative interior in \(\operatorname{aff}A\).

Then \(U\) is finite and contains \(\mathbf J_0\). There are a probability law \(\nu=(\nu_{\mathbf u})_{\mathbf u\in U}\) and nonnegative numbers \(a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}\), for \(\mathbf u\in U\) and \(\mathbf x\in A\), satisfying

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}
&=p_{\mathbf x}
&&(\mathbf x\in A),\\
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}
&=\nu_{\mathbf u},
&
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}\mathbf x
&=\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u
&&(\mathbf u\in U),\\
H(\nu)&\ge H(p)-r\log4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=\mathbf J_0\). For every finite real-valued convex function \(F:P\to\mathbb R\), this same coupling satisfies \(\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}F(\mathbf u)\le\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}F(\mathbf x)\). No completeness assumption on \(A\) is required for (1), and \(\nu\) may vanish at some sites of \(U\). When \(n=r\) and \(\mathbf J_0=0\), this is the full-affine-rank, mean-zero assertion on \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\).

Suppose additionally that \(A=D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is the complete positive support of \(p\), extended by zero off \(D\), and that this full table is globally log-concave in the extension sense of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Explicitly, there is a finite-valued \(f:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\) agreeing with \(p\) at every lattice point, including all its zeros, such that \(f((1-t)x+ty)\ge f(x)^{1-t}f(y)^t\) for \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(0<t<1\), with zero on the right when either endpoint value is zero. Then \(U\subseteq D\), and the same \(\nu,a\) satisfy

\[
0\le
\operatorname{KL}(\nu\|p)
:=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in U\\\nu_{\mathbf u}>0}}
\nu_{\mathbf u}\log\frac{\nu_{\mathbf u}}{p_{\mathbf u}}
\le r\log4,
\tag{2}
\]

where \(\nu\) is extended by zero off \(U\).

For the capacity assertion, let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be any finite nonempty complete support, set \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\) and \(r=\dim\operatorname{aff}D\), and fix \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Let \(\psi=(\psi_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in D}\) be finite real convex-admissible site values: for every \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0\) with \(\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\mathbf K=\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\), require \(\psi_{\mathbf K}\le\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}\). Put \(U=\operatorname{relint}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). With the prescribed center \(\mathbf J\) fixed, define the unnormalized capacities

\[
C_\psi(E)
:=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}
e^{-\psi_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}
\qquad(E=D\ \text{or}\ E=U).
\tag{3}
\]

Then \(e^{-\psi_{\mathbf J}}\le C_\psi(E)<\infty\) for both supports, and

\[
C_\psi(D)\le4^r C_\psi(U).
\tag{4}
\]

The infima in (3) are optimized independently. There is no assumption on the mean of either un-tilted weight table, on the affine rank of \(U\), or on attainment of the inner infimum. All lattice intersections use the whole affine lattice inherited from \(\mathbb Z^n\). Rank zero is included: both supports are the singleton center, (1) and (2) have zero loss, and (4) is equality.

## Proof

First assume \(r\ge1\), \(n=r\), and \(\mathbf J_0=0\), so \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\) has full affine rank and \(p\) has mean zero. Positivity of every \(p_{\mathbf x}\) implies \(0\in\operatorname{int}P\). Indeed, if zero were a boundary point, a nonzero supporting linear functional nonnegative on \(P\) would have positive coefficients averaging its values on \(A\) to zero. It would therefore vanish at every point of \(A\), contrary to full affine rank. Thus \(U=\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r\) is finite, nonempty, and contains zero.

Suppose first that every \(p_{\mathbf x}\) is rational. Let \(m\ge2\) tend to infinity through multiples of a common denominator. Use exactly the type objects of accepted facts 9c50e23fa3b45d45 and a65d7bf2c6e74ad1, with their dimension \(d\) equal to \(r\), their support label \(a\) written as \(\mathbf x\in A\), and their \(A,P,p\) unchanged. Thus \(\mathcal T_m(p)\subseteq A^m\) is the set of distinct words having exactly \(mp_{\mathbf x}\) copies of each \(\mathbf x\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_m&=\operatorname{conv}\mathcal T_m(p),\\
V_m&=\left\{(\mathbf x_1,\ldots,\mathbf x_m)\in(\mathbb R^r)^m:
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbf x_i=0\right\},\\
L_m&=V_m\cap(\mathbb Z^r)^m,
&
\mathcal I_m&=\operatorname{relint}(Q_m)\cap L_m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Here relative interior is in \(V_m\). Accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45 proves \(\operatorname{aff}Q_m=V_m\), \(\dim V_m=r(m-1)\), and the exact chart \(\Phi_m(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1})=(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_{m-1},-\sum_{i<m}\mathbf y_i)\), mapping the whole \(\mathbb Z^{r(m-1)}\) bijectively onto \(L_m\). It also proves that every block of a point of \(\mathcal I_m\) belongs to \(U\). Accepted fact a65d7bf2c6e74ad1 supplies the sharper count

\[
|\mathcal T_m(p)|\le4^{r(m-1)}|\mathcal I_m|.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, \(\mathcal I_m\) is a finite nonempty subset of \(U^m\), and every one of its tuples has block sum zero.

Fix \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf z_1,\ldots,\mathbf z_m)\in\mathcal I_m\). Since \(\mathbf z\in Q_m\), choose \(\lambda_\omega\ge0\), indexed by the finite set \(\omega\in\mathcal T_m(p)\), with \(\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega=1\) and \(\mathbf z_i=\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega\omega_i\) for every position \(i\). Define its empirical law and a finite coupling by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u}
&=\frac1m\bigl|\{i:\mathbf z_i=\mathbf u\}\bigr|,\\
a^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}
&=\frac1m\sum_{\{i:\mathbf z_i=\mathbf u\}}
\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal T_m(p)}
\lambda_\omega\mathbf1_{\{\omega_i=\mathbf x\}}
\qquad(\mathbf u\in U,\ \mathbf x\in A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The row sum in (7) is \(\nu^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u}\). Every word has its prescribed multiplicities, so the column sums and row moments are exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}a^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}
&=\frac1m\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega
\bigl|\{i:\omega_i=\mathbf x\}\bigr|
=p_{\mathbf x},\\
\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}a^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}\mathbf x
&=\frac1m\sum_{\{i:\mathbf z_i=\mathbf u\}}\sum_\omega\lambda_\omega\omega_i
=\frac1m\sum_{\{i:\mathbf z_i=\mathbf u\}}\mathbf z_i
=\nu^{(\mathbf z)}_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

Thus every interior tuple already supplies a coupling with the original columns, without any modification of \(p\).

There are at most \((m+1)^{|U|}\) empirical laws of words in \(U^m\), since each site count is an integer between zero and \(m\). If \(\eta\) is one such empirical law, each word of that type has probability \(e^{-mH(\eta)}\) under the product probability law \(\eta^{\otimes m}\). Only sites with \(\eta_{\mathbf u}>0\) occur in that product. Summing these equal probabilities over its type class gives at most one, so the number of words of that type is at most \(e^{mH(\eta)}\).

Choose \(\mathbf z^{(m)}\in\mathcal I_m\) maximizing the entropy of its empirical law, and write \(\nu^{(m)}=\nu^{(\mathbf z^{(m)})}\) and \(a^{(m)}=a^{(\mathbf z^{(m)})}\). Such a choice exists because \(\mathcal I_m\) is finite and nonempty. Partitioning \(\mathcal I_m\) by empirical laws and using (6) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal I_m|
&\le(m+1)^{|U|}e^{mH(\nu^{(m)})},\\
H(\nu^{(m)})
&\ge\frac{\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|}{m}
-r\left(1-\frac1m\right)\log4
-\frac{|U|\log(m+1)}m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

The exact multinomial count is \(|\mathcal T_m(p)|=m!/\prod_{\mathbf x\in A}(mp_{\mathbf x})!\). Accepted fact 9c50e23fa3b45d45 establishes the elementary factorial estimate \(\log(k!)=k\log k-k+O(\log(k+1))\), hence \(m^{-1}\log|\mathcal T_m(p)|\to H(p)\) along the permitted multiples. Therefore (9) gives \(\liminf_mH(\nu^{(m)})\ge H(p)-r\log4\).

Each \(a^{(m)}\) lies in the fixed finite cube \([0,1]^{U\times A}\). Pass to a subsequence converging entrywise to \(a\), and let \(\nu_{\mathbf u}=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}\). Nonnegativity, the column equations, and the row-moment equations (8) pass to the limit, since they involve only finitely many coordinates. The column sums show that \(\sum_{\mathbf u}\nu_{\mathbf u}=1\), and the row sums converge: \(\nu^{(m)}\to\nu\) along this subsequence. The function \(-t\log t\), extended by zero at \(t=0\), is continuous on \([0,1]\). Hence entropy is continuous on this finite simplex, and (9) yields \(H(\nu)\ge H(p)-r\log4\). This proves (1) for rational \(p\), including an actual limiting array.

For a real strictly positive centered law \(p\) on the same \(A\), the integer matrix whose columns are \((1,\mathbf x)\), \(\mathbf x\in A\), has rank \(r+1\). Choose \(r+1\) independent columns. Approximate the probabilities at all other columns by rational numbers and solve the normalization and mean-zero equations for the selected coordinates. The selected square integer matrix has rational inverse, so the resulting laws \(p^{(k)}\) are rational and have mean exactly zero and total mass exactly one. They converge to \(p\), and all coordinates are positive for sufficiently close approximations. If no other columns remain, the same inverse proves that \(p\) itself is rational.

For each such \(p^{(k)}\), the result just proved gives an array \(a^{(k)}\) on the same finite set \(U\times A\), with row law \(\nu^{(k)}\) and \(H(\nu^{(k)})\ge H(p^{(k)})-r\log4\). A convergent subsequence of these arrays exists in \([0,1]^{U\times A}\). Its limit has column law \(p\), the prescribed row moments, and row law \(\nu=\lim_k\nu^{(k)}\). Continuity of entropy on both fixed finite simplices passes the displayed lower bound to \(\nu,p\). The full-rank mean-zero case is proved for all real positive laws.

Now return to \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) of arbitrary affine rank with original integer mean \(\mathbf J_0\). A strictly positive convex combination of all sites lies in the relative interior of their convex hull: a supporting affine functional nonconstant on the affine hull, nonnegative on \(P\), and zero at that combination would have to vanish at every site, a contradiction. Thus \(\mathbf J_0\in\operatorname{relint}P\).

Use the intrinsic-lattice notation of accepted fact b8b036fb81da12b7:
\(E_{\mathrm{aff}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(A-\mathbf J_0)\) and \(\Gamma=E_{\mathrm{aff}}\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Its lattice-basis argument applies here without completeness, since \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) is spanned by integer vectors. Explicitly, \(\Gamma\) is a discrete subgroup containing \(r\) linearly independent integer vectors spanning \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\). Every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\) has an integer basis, obtained by projecting onto a coordinate, splitting off an element at the positive generator of the image when the image is nonzero, and applying induction to the kernel. Integer rank agrees with real rank for integer vectors, by rational matrix elimination. Consequently an integer basis of the whole \(\Gamma\) has \(r\) elements and defines a linear isomorphism \(\iota:\mathbb R^r\to E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) with \(\iota(\mathbb Z^r)=\Gamma\).

If \(r=0\), then \(A=U=\{\mathbf J_0\}\). Set \(p_{\mathbf J_0}=\nu_{\mathbf J_0}=a_{\mathbf J_0,\mathbf J_0}=1\). All assertions in (1), including the convex-function conclusion, follow immediately. Assume henceforth that \(r\ge1\).

Define \(A_0=\iota^{-1}(A-\mathbf J_0)\) and \(P_0=\operatorname{conv}A_0\), and transfer the probabilities by \(p^0_{\mathbf x}=p_{\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf x)}\). These are the chart objects \(D_0,P_0\) of b8b036fb81da12b7 when \(A=D\) is complete; \(A_0\) allows an incomplete support. The map \(\mathbf x\mapsto\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf x)\) bijects the whole \(\mathbb Z^r\) with \(\operatorname{aff}P\cap\mathbb Z^n=\mathbf J_0+\Gamma\), not merely with the group generated by support differences. It takes \(P_0\) to \(P\), and therefore takes \(\operatorname{int}(P_0)\cap\mathbb Z^r\) bijectively to \(U\).

The law \(p^0\) is strictly positive and centered, and \(A_0\) has full affine rank \(r\). Apply the already proved mean-zero case and push both coupling indices through this affine bijection. Its row moments transform by
\(\sum_{\mathbf x}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}(\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf x))
=\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf J_0+\iota(\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u)
=\nu_{\mathbf u}(\mathbf J_0+\iota(\mathbf u))\).
Thus the pushed array satisfies (1) in the original coordinates. Entropy is unchanged by these finite bijections, and there is no lattice-index or determinant factor.

Summing the row moments of (1) and then using its column sums gives \(\sum_{\mathbf u}\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u=\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\mathbf x=\mathbf J_0\). If \(\nu_{\mathbf u}>0\), the numbers \(a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}/\nu_{\mathbf u}\) are nonnegative, sum to one, and have barycenter \(\mathbf u\). Convexity of any finite real-valued \(F\) therefore gives
\(\nu_{\mathbf u}F(\mathbf u)\le\sum_{\mathbf x}a_{\mathbf u,\mathbf x}F(\mathbf x)\).
If \(\nu_{\mathbf u}=0\), nonnegativity forces its entire row to vanish, so the same inequality holds as \(0\le0\). Summing over rows proves the claimed convex-function comparison for this same coupling.

For the relative-entropy assertion, use accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc with exactly \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=p_{\max}:=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}\), and its canonical potential
\(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\).
That fact supplies the finite convex roof \(\widehat\phi\) on all of \(P\), equal to \(\phi_{\mathbf I}\) at every site of \(D\). Its convex function \(\widehat\phi-\log p_{\max}\) has site values \(-\log p_{\mathbf I}\). Because \(U\subseteq D\), the convex-function comparison already proved gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}(-\log p_{\mathbf u})
&\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p_{\mathbf I}(-\log p_{\mathbf I})
=H(p),\\
\operatorname{KL}(\nu\|p)
&=\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}(-\log p_{\mathbf u})-H(\nu)
\le H(p)-H(\nu)
\le r\log4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

All \(p_{\mathbf u}\) in this sum are strictly positive. Zero entries of \(\nu\) contribute zero, and no logarithm of a zero table value outside \(D\) is used. Nonnegativity follows from Jensen's inequality for the logarithm:
\(\sum_{\nu_{\mathbf u}>0}\nu_{\mathbf u}\log(p_{\mathbf u}/\nu_{\mathbf u})
\le\log\sum_{\nu_{\mathbf u}>0}p_{\mathbf u}\le0\).
This proves (2).

It remains to prove (4) under its separate capacity hypotheses. Completeness and \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) imply \(\mathbf J\in U\subseteq D\). The term at \(\mathbf J\) in every exponential sum is \(e^{-\psi_{\mathbf J}}>0\). Choosing zero tilt also gives a finite upper bound, so both capacities have the asserted positivity and finiteness. If \(r=0\), then \(D=U=\{\mathbf J\}\) and both capacities equal \(e^{-\psi_{\mathbf J}}\), proving (4).

For \(r\ge1\), the full intrinsic chart just used, now centered at \(\mathbf J\), has \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D-\mathbf J)\), \(\Gamma=E_{\mathrm{aff}}\cap\mathbb Z^n\), \(D_0=\iota^{-1}(D-\mathbf J)\), and \(P_0=\operatorname{conv}D_0\). For comparison with the centered-capacity convention of b8b036fb81da12b7, its normalized potential is
\(\psi^0_{\mathbf v}=\psi_{\mathbf J+\iota(\mathbf v)}-\psi_{\mathbf J}\), so \(\psi^0_0=0\). Its \(C_{\psi^0}(1)\) equals \(e^{\psi_{\mathbf J}}C_\psi(D)\) in (3). Indeed, an ambient tilt induces the linear functional \(\mathbf v\mapsto\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\iota(\mathbf v)\), and every linear functional on \(\mathbb R^r\) arises this way. The common additive potential shift multiplies both capacities in (3) by the same factor. This identifies the normalization exactly, while leaving the inner optimization independent.

The convex-roof construction of 488f0a583886b5fc applies to the normalized site values \(\psi-\min_D\psi\) on this complete \(D\). Adding the minimum back gives a finite convex function \(\widehat\psi\) on \(P\) equal to \(\psi_{\mathbf I}\) at every \(\mathbf I\in D\). Equivalently, its value at \(x\in P\) is the minimum of \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}\) over nonnegative coefficients of sum one and barycenter \(x\). These feasible sets are nonempty compact subsets of a finite simplex, so the minimum is attained and finite; combining minimizers proves convexity, while admissibility and singleton representations give agreement at every site.

Write \(Z_\psi(E,\boldsymbol\theta)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}e^{-\psi_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}\). The outer sum depends only on the orthogonal projection of \(\boldsymbol\theta\) to \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\). Since \(\mathbf J\) is in the relative interior, \(P-\mathbf J\) contains a closed ball of some radius \(\rho>0\) about zero in \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\). For \(\boldsymbol\theta\in E_{\mathrm{aff}}\), this gives

\[
Z_\psi(D,\boldsymbol\theta)
\ge e^{-\max_D\psi+
\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}
\ge e^{-\max_D\psi+\rho\|\boldsymbol\theta\|}.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus the continuous outer sum is coercive on \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) and attains its infimum at some \(\boldsymbol\theta_*\) there. Differentiation in that subspace gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{-\psi_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=0\):
the vector lies in \(E_{\mathrm{aff}}\) and is orthogonal to every vector of that subspace. Consequently the full-support probability law

\[
p^*_{\mathbf I}
=\frac{e^{-\psi_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\theta_*\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}}{C_\psi(D)}
\quad(\mathbf I\in D)
\qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p^*_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J,
\quad
H(p^*)-\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p^*_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}
=\log C_\psi(D).
\tag{12}
\]

Apply (1) to \(A=D\) and this \(p^*\), whose original mean in that application is precisely \(\mathbf J_0=\mathbf J\). It supplies a law \(\nu\) on \(U\), an explicit finite coupling, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u&=\mathbf J,\\
H(\nu)&\ge H(p^*)-r\log4,\\
\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}\psi_{\mathbf u}
&\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}p^*_{\mathbf I}\psi_{\mathbf I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The last line uses the same coupling and the convex function \(\widehat\psi\). Combining (12) and (13) gives
\(H(\nu)-\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}\psi_{\mathbf u}\ge\log C_\psi(D)-r\log4\).

For every inner tilt \(\boldsymbol\theta'\in\mathbb R^n\), the mean identity in (13) and Jensen's inequality for the logarithm yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\nu)-\sum_{\mathbf u\in U}\nu_{\mathbf u}\psi_{\mathbf u}
&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in U\\\nu_{\mathbf u}>0}}
\nu_{\mathbf u}
\log\frac{e^{-\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta'\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf J)}}{\nu_{\mathbf u}}\\
&\le
\log\left(\sum_{\substack{\mathbf u\in U\\\nu_{\mathbf u}>0}}
e^{-\psi_{\mathbf u}+\boldsymbol\theta'\cdot(\mathbf u-\mathbf J)}\right)\\
&\le\log Z_\psi(U,\boldsymbol\theta').
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Hence \(Z_\psi(U,\boldsymbol\theta')\ge4^{-r}C_\psi(D)\) for every \(\boldsymbol\theta'\). Taking its infimum proves \(C_\psi(U)\ge4^{-r}C_\psi(D)\), which is (4). This step requires neither an arithmetic-mean condition on \(U\) nor an inner minimizing tilt, and it remains valid when \(U\) has smaller affine rank than \(D\).
