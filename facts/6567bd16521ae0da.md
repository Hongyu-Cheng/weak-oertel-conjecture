## Statement

There exist compatible full-dimensional polygons $A,K,B$ at bases $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, a common-support face $F_K$ parametrized by $s\in[0,1]$, and three ambient cap cuts proper on $A,K$ and supporting on $B$, such that the midpoint face $I_0=(F_A+F_B)/2$ corresponds to $[899/1000,901/1000]$. The middle face signatures in increasing $s$ are $\{2\},\{1,2\},\{1\}$, with switches at $L=2579/10200$ and $Z=7781/10200$. The corresponding $A$ face has signature $\{1\}$ throughout.

For the functional tests assign weights $p_1=p_2=7/50,\ p_3=18/25$ and comparison ratio $r=21/100$. They obey $\max_i p_i<3/4$, every pair sum is above $1/4$, and $r<2/9$. Let $f(s)$ be the assigned middle face load minus $r$ and put $c=9/10$. Then every finite concave $G$ on $[0,1]$ satisfies
$$
\int_0^1 G(s)f(s)\,ds\ge (7/5000)G(c).
$$
In particular every full-face unit tent with an interior apex has strictly positive cost, while an inner tent supported on $I_0$ has negative cost. The $A$ tent cost in the same face-parameter units is $C_A=-7/50000$. The combined cost $C_A+\frac12\int Gf$ of a concave repair with $G(c)\ge1$ is at least $7/12500$, attained by $G=1$. Allowing any interior apex $c'\in I_0$ and requiring only $G\ge0$ on $I_0$ and $G(c')\ge1$ gives cost strictly above $21/100000$, its exact infimum. A middle-only concave profile $G\ge0$ on $I_0$ has nonnegative cost. The physical arclength costs for the realized polygons multiply all the stated costs by $\sqrt2$. The prescribed $p_i,r$ are data of the functional implication under test.

## Proof

The loads of the two singleton portions are $7/50$ and that of the middle portion is $14/50$. Thus $f=-7/100$ outside $[L,Z]$ and $f=7/100$ inside. The differences $Z-L=51/100$ and $Z+L=259/255$ give $\int f=7/5000$ and $\int sf=63/50000=(7/5000)c$. With $m=7/5000$, the signed measure $f(s)\,ds-m\delta_c$ has zero mass and first moment. Its positive part lies on $[L,Z]$ and its negative part lies outside, including the atom at $c>Z$. The affine secant of $G$ through $L,Z$ lies below $G$ on $[L,Z]$ and above it outside. Subtracting that secant in the signed integral leaves a nonnegative integral and proves the claimed inequality. Every full-face unit tent is positive at $c$, and $f$ is negative on all of $I_0$.

In the parameter units for $F_K$ the $A$ face has twice the length of $I_0$, so the integral of its unit tent is $|I_0|$. Its constant load minus $r$ is $-7/100$, giving $C_A=-7/50000$ regardless of the apex. If $G(c)\ge1$, the inequality gives combined cost at least $-7/50000+7/10000=7/12500$, with equality for the constant roof. For an arbitrary interior apex $c'$, write $a_0=899/1000$, $b_0=901/1000$. If $c'\ge c$, concavity between $a_0$ and $c'$ gives $G(c)\ge(c-a_0)/(c'-a_0)>1/2$. If $c'\le c$, concavity between $c'$ and $b_0$ gives $G(c)\ge(b_0-c)/(b_0-c')>1/2$. This gives the strict lower bound $-7/50000+7/20000=21/100000$. The affine line through $(a_0,0),(c',1)$ in the first case and through $(b_0,0),(c',1)$ in the second is nonnegative on $I_0$, attains the interpolation bound, and integrates to $mG(c)$. Sending $c'$ to the nearer endpoint proves the exact infimum. The same integral inequality gives nonnegative cost whenever $G\ge0$ on $I_0$.

For the physical incidence choose $v_1=(1,0)$, $v_2=(0,1)$, $v_3=(-1,-1)$, middle mark zero and middle cuts $v_i\cdot x\ge0$. Let $X(s)=(s-L,Z-s)$, $P=(-4/5,4/5)$, $F_K=X([0,1])$, and $F_A=\{2X(s)-P:s\in I_0\}$. Let $B$ be the hull of
$$
P,\quad (-11/10,1),\quad (-6/5,1),\quad (-3/5,-2/5),\quad
(-2/5,-3/5),\quad (1,-6/5),\quad(1,-11/10).
$$
All points satisfy $x_1\le1,\ x_2\le1,\ x_1+x_2\ge-1$, and two distinct listed points attain each bound, so each $h_B(v_i)=1$ with a positive-length support face. The unique maximizer of $x_1+x_2$ in $B$ is $P$, whose sum is zero while every other listed point has negative sum. Let $A$ be the hull of $F_A$ and $(-11/10,3/2),(-11/10,-11/10)$. The sum on $F_A$ is $2(Z-L)=51/50$, strictly larger than either additional vertex sum. For $s\in I_0$ its points have $x_1>-1$ and $x_2<-1$, so the old cap tests $v_i\cdot x\ge-1$ have only signature $\{1\}$ there. Each cutting line is proper on $A$, since the coordinate extrema straddle $-1$ and the sums straddle $1$. Define $K$ as the hull of $F_K$, $(A+B)/2$ and $[-1/10,1/10]^2$. Its $(1,1)$-exposed face is precisely $F_K$, since the midpoint body has exposed face $(F_A+P)/2=X(I_0)$ and the small square has strictly lower support. The square gives interiority of the zero mark and proper middle cuts. Along $X(s)$ the first margin changes sign at $L$, the second at $Z$, and the third is $-(Z-L)<0$. The slopes of $X(s)$ and $2X(s)-P$ have norms $\sqrt2$ and $2\sqrt2$ respectively, giving the common arclength multiplier. The three-site hull realizes exactly these fibers by the inclusion of the midpoint body in $K$.

The constant and affine equality roofs also give actual compatible polygon variations for each fixed interior apex: move the $A$ apex outward in the common unit support direction, and replace the middle edge by the corresponding affine height times half the displacement. At the old midpoint face the height is nonnegative and at the new seed point it is at least half the displacement. All other vertices are strictly below that face at the limit, and the new seed retains strict slack in the other middle facet inequalities since the original apex was in the relative interior of $F_A$. The signed strip gives the corresponding first-order arclength integral. The construction thus realizes the face incidence and compatible geometry; a deduction from actual criticality would require the section-derived weights and the genuine budget ratio in addition to the numerical and incidence data used here.
