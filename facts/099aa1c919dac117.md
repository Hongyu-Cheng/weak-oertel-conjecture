## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $U$ be uniform on $\Delta_d=\{u\in\mathbb R_+^{d+1}:\sum_j u_j=1\}$, and for $x\ge0$ put $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot U\le t)$ and $\phi_d(x)=\inf_{t>0:F_x(t)>0} t/F_x(t)^{1/d}$. Let $X$ be a local maximizer of $H(X)=\sum_i c_i\phi_d(X_i)$ over nonnegative column-stochastic $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ matrices, where $X_i$ is row $i$ and every $c_i>0$. If one row is strictly positive, every other nonzero row has at most one zero entry.

## Proof

The claim is automatic in $d=1$, where any nonzero row has at most one zero. Suppose $d\ge2$ and some nonzero row $i$ has $r$ zeros with $2\le r\le d$. Its capacity is zero by the fixed-support boundary calculation in the final proof paragraph of fact c80576b633835713. Keep one zero and raise the other $r-1$ zeros to $\epsilon$, subtracting $\epsilon$ in the same columns from a strictly positive row $k$. For small positive $\epsilon$ this preserves feasibility. The new row $i$ has one zero, so its capacity is exactly
$$
\left(\prod_{j:X_{ij}>0}X_{ij}\right)^{1/d}\epsilon^{(r-1)/d}
$$
by the one-zero formula in the same accepted proof.

Capacity is coordinatewise nondecreasing by cap inclusion and positively homogeneous by rescaling thresholds. If $a=\min_j X_{kj}>0$, the modified funding row is coordinatewise at least $(1-\epsilon/a)X_k$, so its capacity loss is at most $(\epsilon/a)\phi_d(X_k)$. The gain times $c_i>0$ dominates this linear loss times $c_k$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon$, since $(r-1)/d<1$. This contradicts local maximality.
