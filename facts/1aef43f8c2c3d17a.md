## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex and let $\mu$ be a probability supported on its integer fibers. At an integer median $z_0$, let $L,R,w$ be the strict left, strict right, and central masses, with $L,R\le1/2$ and $w<1/2$. Form the probability half-marginals $\alpha,\beta$ by taking twice the strict left and right measures, respectively, and filling the deficits proportionally from the central measure $\mu_0$ so that $\alpha+\beta=2\mu$. Map each legal pair to the intersection of its segment with the median fiber, using its midpoint for a central-central pair. The target laws of probability couplings with zero central-central mass are exactly $2\mu_0+T_\#\rho$, where $T$ is the median segment map on strict-cross pairs and $\rho$ is any measure of mass $1-2w$ whose first and second marginals are dominated by the strict parts of $\alpha,\beta$.

In particular, suppose the source is summed Lebesgue volume on three consecutive fibers $A,B,C_+$ at $-1,0,1$ with volumes $a,b,c$ and total $M=a+b+c$, where $a,c\le M/2$ and $b<M/2$. With $m=(a+c-b)/2$, the unnormalized targets are precisely $\mathbf1_B\,dx+T_\#\pi$, with $T(x,x')=(x+x')/2$, $|\pi|=m$, $\pi_X\le\mathbf1_A\,dx$, and $\pi_Y\le\mathbf1_{C_+}\,dx$. Each has mass $b+m=M/2$ and is supported on $B$.

## Proof

Write $r=1-2w$. Since $0<r\le 2L,2R$, the strict-cross class is nonempty by scaling the two strict marginals to mass $r$ and taking their product divided by $r$. After using any such $\rho$, the remaining strict-left mass is $2L-r=1-2R$, equal to the mass of the right marginal at $z_0$. The remaining strict-right mass is $2R-r=1-2L$, equal to the left marginal's mass at $z_0$. Couple these two residual blocks separately by products of equal-mass marginals divided by their common mass, omitting a block of zero mass. This produces a full legal coupling with no central-central block. Each of the two new blocks maps to its central endpoint, so their images sum to $2\mu_0$. Conversely, every full coupling avoiding the central-central block must use all the central marginals in these two blocks, leaving exactly $r$ in the strict-cross block. This proves both directions of the description.

In the three-fiber case multiply all target and strict-cross measures by $M/2$. The middle component becomes uniform volume on $B$, the strict marginals become the stated endpoint volumes, and the strict-cross mass becomes $m$. The segment intersection at the middle site is the midpoint and $(A+C_+)/2\subseteq B$ by convexity.
