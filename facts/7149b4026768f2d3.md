## Statement

Let $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$ and $V=|T|=1/2$. Suppose the positive integer fibers of an actual convex body are $r_kT$ at the bases $k$ in a nonempty finite integer interval, and let $\mu$ sum their ordinary planar areas. Assume that the radii form a concave sequence. Fix a base $j$ and put $q=r_j/3$, $z_k=k-j$, and $t_k=(1+\rho)q-\beta z_k$. Consider the shared caps
$$
H_{\rho,\beta}=\{(k,x):x_1+\rho x_2+\beta(k-j)\ge (1+\rho)q\},
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1,\quad \beta\in\mathbb R .
$$
Here the halfspace margin is ambient, with the integer base replaced by a real variable away from the source fibers. The absolute closed-halfspace depth at $(j,q,q)$ divided by $V$ is the minimum of $F(\rho,\beta)=\mu(H_{\rho,\beta})/V$. Clipping $\beta$ to $[-B_*,B_*]$ preserves $F$, where $B_*=1+\max_{k\ne j}(2q+r_k)/|z_k|$ or $B_*=1$ for a singleton. The exact coupled identities and tilt condition are given below.

If all $r_k$ are rational, this minimum can be found by enumerating a finite arrangement of rational trace lines and solving univariate polynomial equations of degree at most six, with exact real-algebraic sign and value comparisons. The minimum and one realizing pair $(\rho,\beta)$ lie in a common real number field of degree at most six over $\mathbb Q$.

For the actual three-fiber chain of radii $(1/2,3/2,3/4)$ with the middle anchor, there is a strict interior local minimum of the coupled objective at $\beta=-1/8$ and $\rho=1-\sqrt{102}/12$. Its value is $(\sqrt{102}-2)/8<81/80$.

## Proof

The nonnegative-normal reduction in 6f616779219fd76c applies with $d=2$ to these actual fibers. Its touching affine majorant holds by actual convexity, or by a supporting line to the piecewise affine concave interpolant at $j$. Swap the continuous coordinates if necessary, preserving every fiber and its centroid. For its normalized $w_1\ge w_2\ge0$, divide the margin by $w_1>0$, so $\rho=w_2/w_1$ and the new tilt is $\beta=a/w_1$. The reduction covers even closed purely base-normal caps. Each normalized cap is an actual containing cap, so the minima agree in the same ordinary area measure.

For $0<\rho<1$, the vertex heights on $rT$ are $0,\rho r,r$. If $0<t<\rho r$, the complementary triangle has axis intercepts $t,t/\rho$ and area divided by $V$ equal to $t^2/\rho$. If $\rho r<t<r$, the retained top triangle has adjacent edge fractions $(r-t)/r$ and $(r-t)/[(1-\rho)r]$, giving $(r-t)^2/(1-\rho)$. With the full and empty regimes added, the four values are
$$
g(r,t;\rho)=
\begin{cases}
r^2,&t\le0,\\
r^2-t^2/\rho,&0\le t\le\rho r,\\
(r-t)^2/(1-\rho),&\rho r\le t\le r,\\
0,&t\ge r.
\end{cases}
$$
The formulas agree at shared endpoints. Equivalently, with $s_+=\max(s,0)$,
$$
F(\rho,\beta)=\sum_k\left(
\frac{(r_k-t_k)_+^2}{1-\rho}
-\frac{(\rho r_k-t_k)_+^2}{\rho(1-\rho)}
+\frac{(-t_k)_+^2}{\rho}\right).
$$
Indeed, substituting the four threshold ranges yields $g$ term by term. The positive supports of $r_k-t_k$ and $\rho r_k-t_k$ are integer intervals by concavity, and the second is contained in the first. At $\rho=0$ the proper cap value is $(r-t)^2$ for $0\le t\le r$, by a coordinate cut. At $\rho=1$ it is $r^2-t^2$ on that interval, by a sum-coordinate cut. Both endpoints again have full and empty values outside the proper range.

If $\beta\ge B_*$, every fiber with $z_k>0$ is full and every fiber with $z_k<0$ is empty, since $0\le x_1+\rho x_2\le r_k$ and $q\le(1+\rho)q\le2q$. At $\beta\le -B_*$ the roles reverse. The own fiber is independent of tilt. Thus clipping preserves all cap masses. Joint continuity on the rectangle follows from dominated convergence on each of the finitely many fibers, since the continuous normal $(1,\rho)$ is nonzero and its cutting line is area-null. In particular the minimum is attained.

Partition the rectangle by the lines $t_k=0,\ t_k=\rho r_k,\ t_k=r_k$. These include the own-fiber line $\rho=1/2$. In a cell with $0<\rho<1$, let $F_0,L,H$ denote its full, low proper, and high proper fibers, respectively. Empty fibers contribute zero. The exact numerator is
$$
N=\rho(1-\rho)\sum_{k\in F_0\cup L}r_k^2
 -(1-\rho)\sum_{k\in L}[(1+\rho)q-\beta z_k]^2
 +\rho\sum_{k\in H}[r_k-(1+\rho)q+\beta z_k]^2 .
$$
It is $A(\rho)\beta^2+B(\rho)\beta+C(\rho)$, with degrees at most $1,2,3$ respectively. For $L_i=\sum_{k\in L}z_k^i$, $H_i=\sum_{k\in H}z_k^i$, and $H_{zr}=\sum_{k\in H}z_kr_k$, direct expansion gives
$$
A=\rho(L_2+H_2)-L_2,\qquad
B=2q(1-\rho^2)L_1+2\rho H_{zr}-2q\rho(1+\rho)H_1 .
$$
All index sets arise from the same $(\rho,\beta)$. Thus $F_{\beta\beta}=2A/[\rho(1-\rho)]$. At an interior local minimum $A\ge0$. If $A>0$ then $\beta=-B/(2A)$ and $\rho>L_2/(L_2+H_2)$, with strictly positive denominator. If $A=0$, stationarity forces $B=0$ and the whole vertical slice of the cell has that same value. By continuity its endpoint on a trace or rectangle edge does too.

For rational radii the rectangle and all lines are rational. In an interior cell at $A>0$, substitute the stationary tilt to get $U/W=(4AC-B^2)/[4A\rho(1-\rho)]$. Here $\deg U\le4,\ \deg W\le3$, and $W>0$. At an interior minimum the derivative vanishes, so $U'W-UW'=0$, of degree at most six. If nonzero, isolate its real roots and retain those with $0<\rho<1$, $A>0$ and stationary tilt in the cell interior. Both the tilt and the objective are rational functions of such a root. If the derivative polynomial is identically zero, the rational function is a rational constant. Where the stationary graph meets the open cell with $A>0$, it has an open interval of feasible $\rho$, so a rational sample supplies the same value. Whether that interval exists can be tested by isolating zeros of the numerator polynomials in the substituted cell inequalities and testing rational points between them, with $A>0$ included.

For a nonvertical edge in $0<\rho<1$, substitution of its rational affine tilt into $N$ leaves a polynomial of degree at most three over $\rho(1-\rho)$. Boundary branch formulas agree. Its derivative numerator has degree at most four, with constant edges handled by one rational sample. On a vertical edge with $0<\rho<1$, $\rho$ is rational and the objective is quadratic or lower in $\beta$. On $\rho=0,1$, partitioned at trace endpoints, the explicit boundary formulas also give rational quadratics or lower. Thus stationary points on those edges can be taken rational, including constant edges. All vertices are rational. This finite list covers the value of every global minimizer, even when there is a continuum of minimizing points. Select the least feasible value by exact algebraic comparisons. A root of an individual equation and its rational-function coordinates and value lie in the same field, so comparing candidates does not enlarge the realizing field degree.

For the displayed chain, concavity follows from $3/2>(1/2+3/4)/2$ and its concave linear interpolation gives the actual fibers. In the cell with all three high proper, summing the three squares and differentiating in $\beta$ gives $\beta=-1/8$, strictly positive tilt curvature $4/(1-\rho)$, and reduced function
$$
f(\rho)=\frac{33/32-(5/4)\rho+(3/4)\rho^2}{1-\rho}
        =\frac{17}{32(1-\rho)}+\frac12-\frac34\rho .
$$
Its stationary point satisfies $(1-\rho)^2=17/24$ and is $\rho_*=1-\sqrt{102}/12\in(0,1/4)$, with $f''(\rho)=17/[16(1-\rho)^3]>0$ and $f(\rho_*)=(\sqrt{102}-2)/8$. At the left, middle and right fiber, the respective thresholds are $3/8+\rho_*/2,\ 1/2+\rho_*/2,\ 5/8+\rho_*/2$. The condition $0<\rho_*<1/4$ places each strictly above $\rho_*r_k$ and strictly below $r_k$. Hence the point is strictly interior to the high proper cell and minimizes strictly locally in both variables. Finally $102<(101/10)^2$ gives the stated improvement over $81/80$.
