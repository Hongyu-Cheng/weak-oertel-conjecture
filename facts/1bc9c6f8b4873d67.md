## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(N\ge2\), define
\[
D_N=(\{0,1\}\times\{0\})
       \cup(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\times\{1\}),
\qquad
p_{\beta,b}(x,t)
=\frac{e^{\beta x+bt}}{Z_2(\beta)+e^b Z_N(\beta)},
\]
where \(Z_L(\beta)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}e^{\beta j}\) for \(L\ge2\). The support is complete in \(\mathbb Z^2\) and has affine rank two. Retain the actual closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\) and moment \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\) from accepted fact `c3ede657c2f01110`. Its parameter \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is exactly \((\beta,b)\) here; its ambient-dimension symbol \(N\) is two here, whereas the present \(N\) is the upper-row cardinality.

For the scalar geometric law \(\pi_{\beta,L}(j)=e^{\beta j}/Z_L(\beta)\) on \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\), write \(A_L(\beta)=M_3(\pi_{\beta,L})\). This is the same actual-depth moment, now in scalar dimension one; \(\pi_{\beta,2}\) and \(\pi_{\beta,N}\) are exactly the conditional laws of the lower and upper rows. Set \(q_t=p_{\beta,b}(\{(x,t)\in D_N\})\). The following exact reduction holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p_{\beta,b})
&=q_0^4 A_2(\beta)+q_1^4 A_N(\beta),\\
\min_{b\in\mathbb R}M_3(p_{\beta,b})
&=\bigl(A_2(\beta)^{-1/3}+A_N(\beta)^{-1/3}\bigr)^{-3},\\
\frac{q_1}{q_0}
&=\left(\frac{A_2(\beta)}{A_N(\beta)}\right)^{1/3}
\quad\text{at this unique minimizing row-mass choice.}
\end{aligned}
\]

For every \(N\) a finite global minimizer \((\beta_N,b_N)\in\mathbb R^2\) exists. For any choice of one such minimizer for each \(N\), as \(N\to\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_N&\longrightarrow0,&
b_N&\longrightarrow-\infty,\\
A_N(\beta_N)&\longrightarrow\frac1{32},&
\min_{\beta,b}M_3(p_{\beta,b})
&\longrightarrow(2+32^{1/3})^{-3},\\
q_0&\longrightarrow\frac1{1+4^{1/3}},&
q_1&\longrightarrow\frac{4^{1/3}}{1+4^{1/3}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular \(|b_N|\to\infty\). Every such minimizer also has the unique minimizing traces and the mean and covariance stationarity conditions asserted in `c3ede657c2f01110`.

Let \(\mathbf X\sim p_{\beta_N,b_N}\) and let its original score be \(\beta_N X_1+b_N X_2\). The infimum of the lengths of real intervals containing at least \(3/4\) of this score law tends to infinity.

## Proof

The convex hull of \(D_N\) is the trapezoid
\(\{(x,t):0\le t\le1,\ 0\le x\le1+(N-2)t\}\).
An integer point in it has height zero or one and therefore belongs to the specified row at that height. This proves completeness. The support differences \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) prove affine rank two.

First prove exact actual-depth decoupling. Each of the two rows is an exposed face. A closed halfspace containing a target \((j,t)\) restricts to that row as a closed half-line containing \(j\), or as the whole row. Its original mass is therefore at least \(q_t\tau_{\pi_{\beta,L_t}}(j)\), where \(L_0=2\) and \(L_1=N\). Conversely, a minimizing scalar half-line can be extended to an ambient closed halfspace excluding the entire other row: add a sufficiently large multiple of the exposing functional of the target row. Finiteness of the other row permits this while leaving the target-row trace unchanged. Hence
\(\tau_{p_{\beta,b}}(j,t)=q_t\tau_{\pi_{\beta,L_t}}(j)\).
The original probability of this target is \(q_t\pi_{\beta,L_t}(j)\). Summing its probability times its cubed depth proves the fourth-power row-mass formula in the statement.

At fixed \(\beta\), the row odds are \(q_1/q_0=e^b Z_N(\beta)/Z_2(\beta)\), so finite \(b\) parametrizes every \(q_1\in(0,1)\). The function \(A_2(\beta)q_0^4+A_N(\beta)q_1^4\), with \(q_0+q_1=1\), is strictly convex. Its derivative vanishes exactly when \(A_2(\beta)q_0^3=A_N(\beta)q_1^3\). Thus
\[
q_0=\frac{A_2(\beta)^{-1/3}}{A_2(\beta)^{-1/3}+A_N(\beta)^{-1/3}},
\qquad
q_1=\frac{A_N(\beta)^{-1/3}}{A_2(\beta)^{-1/3}+A_N(\beta)^{-1/3}},
\]
and substitution proves both the reduced minimum and the stated row odds.

We next need a scalar bound that uses actual depths and no cap enumeration. Let \(p_0,\ldots,p_{L-1}\) be any positive scalar law, and put \(s_j=\sum_{k=0}^j p_k\), with \(s_{-1}=0\). Its depth at \(j\) is \(\min\{s_j,1-s_{j-1}\}\). For every \(u\in[s_{j-1},s_j]\), this depth is at least \(\min\{u,1-u\}\), because each of its two defining tails is at least that quantity. Multiplication by the cell length \(p_j\) and summation give
\[
M_3(p)
\ge\int_0^1\min\{u,1-u\}^3\,du
=2\int_0^{1/2}u^3\,du
=\frac1{32}.
\]
In particular \(A_N(\beta)\ge1/32\) for every \(N,\beta\).

At zero tilt the scalar law is uniform, so its depth at \(j\) is \(\min\{j+1,N-j\}/N\). The exact finite sum is
\[
A_N(0)=\frac1{N^4}
\left(2\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}k^3
 +\mathbf 1_{\{N\text{ odd}\}}\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)^3\right).
\]
Using \(\sum_{k=1}^h k^3=h^2(h+1)^2/4\) shows \(A_N(0)\to1/32\). This is an exact scalar sum; the zero-tilt law is used only for comparison and is not asserted to be a minimizer.

The lower row has two endpoint targets, whose depths are their respective atom masses. With \(\xi=e^\beta\), therefore,
\(A_2(\beta)=(1+\xi^4)/(1+\xi)^4\).
Its derivative with respect to \(\xi\) is \(4(\xi^3-1)/(1+\xi)^5\). Consequently \(A_2(\beta)\ge1/8\), with equality only at \(\beta=0\). Reflection of the scalar interval gives \(A_2(-\beta)=A_2(\beta)\), and it is strictly increasing for positive \(\beta\).

Apply part (a) of accepted fact `c3ede657c2f01110` to the finite support \(D_N\). It gives a finite global minimizer for every \(N\). Every global minimizer must first minimize over \(b\) at its fixed \(\beta\). The reduced formula then says that \(\beta_N\) globally maximizes
\(A_2(\beta)^{-1/3}+A_N(\beta)^{-1/3}\).
The two scalar bounds and comparison with \(\beta=0\) give
\[
2+A_N(0)^{-1/3}
\le A_2(\beta_N)^{-1/3}+A_N(\beta_N)^{-1/3}
\le2+32^{1/3}.
\]
The two outer quantities have the same limit. Moreover the two summands in the middle are separately at most \(2\) and \(32^{1/3}\). Their separate nonnegative deficits therefore tend to zero. It follows that \(A_2(\beta_N)\to1/8\) and \(A_N(\beta_N)\to1/32\). Strict monotonicity and reflection of \(A_2\) imply \(\beta_N\to0\): for every \(\varepsilon>0\), any \(|\beta_N|\ge\varepsilon\) would give \(A_2(\beta_N)\ge A_2(\varepsilon)>1/8\). The reduced minimum and optimal row odds now give all the moment and row-mass limits in the statement.

The optimized height parameter satisfies the exact identity
\[
b_N=\frac13\log\frac{A_2(\beta_N)}{A_N(\beta_N)}
       +\log Z_2(\beta_N)-\log Z_N(\beta_N).
\]
For each fixed integer \(L\ge2\) and all \(N\ge L\),
\(Z_N(\beta_N)\ge\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}e^{\beta_N j}\to L\).
Since \(L\) is arbitrary, \(Z_N(\beta_N)\to\infty\). The first two terms in the displayed identity have finite limits, proving \(b_N\to-\infty\).

These are finite global, hence local, minima of exactly the exponential family and actual moment in `c3ede657c2f01110`. Its parts (b) and (c) therefore supply unique minimizing traces, their locally fixed analytic branches, the conditional mean identity, and the positive semidefinite covariance difference at every chosen minimizer. The argument has not substituted a tied comparison law or an unchecked stationary root for a true minimum.

Finally prove the stronger original-score claim. The largest upper-row conditional atom is
\[
\max_j\pi_{\beta_N,N}(j)
=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-|\beta_N|k}\right)^{-1}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, for each fixed \(L\), the denominator is eventually at least its first \(L\) terms, whose sum tends to \(L\). The largest upper-row original score consequently obeys
\[
\exp\!\left(b_N+\max\{0,\beta_N(N-1)\}\right)
=\frac{q_1}{q_0}Z_2(\beta_N)
          \max_j\pi_{\beta_N,N}(j)
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus this largest score tends to minus infinity, whereas the smaller of the two lower-row scores is \(\min\{0,\beta_N\}\to0\). The gap between all upper-row scores and all lower-row scores tends to infinity. Both row masses are eventually strictly below \(3/4\): their limits are as stated, and \(4^{1/3}<3\) implies that the larger limit \(4^{1/3}/(1+4^{1/3})\) is below \(3/4\). Any real interval shorter than this gap meets at most one row of scores and therefore carries less than \(3/4\) of the original mass. The claimed minimum interval width tends to infinity.
