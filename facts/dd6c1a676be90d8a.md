## Statement

In a nondegenerate tetrahedron orient a strict quadrilateral cut toward vertices $0,1$. Its diagonal point has homogeneous coordinates $(x,y,z,w)>0$. Write $S=x+y+z+w$, $P=x+y$, $R=z+w$, $a=xy$, $b=zw$, $k=a-b$, and $D=k^2+S(aR+bP)$. Set
$$
(D_0,D_1,D_2,D_3)=((x+z)(x+w),(y+z)(y+w),(x+z)(y+z),(x+w)(y+w))
$$
and
$$
(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)
=(x[S(2x+R)-D_0],y[S(2y+R)-D_1],z[S(P+2z)-D_2],w[S(P+2w)-D_3]).
$$
The section centroid has coordinates $\lambda_i=N_i/(3SD_i)>0$. Centrality, meaning every $\lambda_i\le1/3$, is equivalent to nonnegativity of $bS-kx,bS-ky,aS+kz,aS+kw$. After sorting $x\le y,z\le w$, the possible middle pairs of $\lambda$ are $\{0,1\},\{2,3\},\{1,2\},\{0,3\}$, allowing ties and breaking tied positions consistently with the within-pair orders. For a selected middle pair $\{j,k'\}$ put $B_{jk'}=12N_jN_{k'}-3S(D_jN_{k'}+D_{k'}N_j)$ and $c=4N_jN_{k'}/B_{jk'}$. On the central domain $B_{jk'}>0$. Let $F$ be the cap volume fraction and let $s$ be the section area divided by the tetrahedral volume times the projection of the ordinary centroid minus the section centroid onto the oriented unit normal. Then $27/64\le F-cs\le37/64$ is equivalent to the two homogeneous degree-ten expressions
$$
(64b(PS-a+b)-27D)B_{jk'}-192S(bP-aR)N_jN_{k'}\ge0,
$$
$$
(37D-64b(PS-a+b))B_{jk'}+192S(bP-aR)N_jN_{k'}\ge0.
$$
The order constraints can be imposed by comparing $N_iD_l$ with $N_lD_i$. In particular the negative pair $\{2,3\}$ is a middle pair in the chosen within-pair order exactly when $N_2(x+w)\ge N_0(y+z)$ and $N_1(x+w)\ge N_3(y+z)$.

Under the normalization $S=1$, the aggregate domain is exactly $0<P<1$, $0<a\le P^2/4$, $0<b\le R^2/4$. Define
$$
U=P(b+k^2)-2k(a+b),\quad V=b^2-bkP+k^2a,\quad
W=R(a+k^2)+2k(a+b),\quad Z=a^2+akR+k^2b.
$$
Centrality is equivalent to $2b-kP\ge0$, $V\ge0$, $2a+kR\ge0$, $Z\ge0$. Within this domain, the unordered negative pair is a middle pair exactly when
$$
(W-U)W+2(V-Z)D\le0,\qquad
(W-U)^2Z+(W-U)(V-Z)W+(V-Z)^2D\ge0.
$$
These conditions include all tied orderings. When that pair is selected, put $N=D-W+Z$ and $B=2D-3W+4Z$. Then $B>0$, $c=(4/3)N/B$ and
$$
F-cs=\frac{b(P-a+b)B-N(bP-aR)}{DB}.
$$
The positive within-pair case follows by reversing the split. These equivalences express the correction condition as polynomial signs on an exact domain.

## Proof

Apply tetrahedral-quadrilateral-section-calculus (1462fa9ce6ced54e) to the positive diagonal point $(x,y,z,w)/S$. Its cut is oriented by heights $(1/x,1/y,-1/z,-1/w)$ up to a positive common factor. That fact gives $3\lambda_0=x[1/(x+z)+1/(x+w)-1/S]$ and the corresponding formulas by exchanges, proving the numerator identities. The section centroid lies in the interior of the tetrahedron, so every $N_i>0$. For example, $D_0(1-3\lambda_0)=b-kx/S$ by expansion, and the other deficits follow by exchanges. The same accepted cap and moment formulas, after homogenizing, give $F=b(PS-a+b)/D$ and $s=3S(bP-aR)/(4D)$.

On the central domain use within-pair ordering (238906fb08a2b14c) after normalizing by $S$, which preserves the order of the diagonal coordinates. The six interleavings of the two ordered pairs have only the four listed middle pairs. For the negative middle pair it suffices and is necessary to have $\lambda_0\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3\le\lambda_1$; cancellation of positive common factors gives the stated inequalities. To verify denominator positivity, write $\delta_i=1-3\lambda_i\ge0$ with sum one. If $A$ is the largest deficit and $B_0$ the smallest, then $A<1$ and the two middle deficits satisfy
$$
\sum_{i\in\{j,k'\}}\frac{\delta_i}{1-\delta_i}
\le \frac{1-A-B_0}{1-A}\le1.
$$
Thus $E=12-1/\lambda_j-1/\lambda_{k'}=3[2-\sum_{i\in\{j,k'\}}\delta_i/(1-\delta_i)]\ge3$. Since $B_{jk'}=N_jN_{k'}E$, the correction $4/E$ is as stated. Multiply each inequality by $64DB_{jk'}>0$ to obtain the exact degree-ten tests.

For $S=1$, each pair is the positive root multiset of its quadratic with the prescribed sum and product. This gives precisely the stated aggregate domain. The first two deficit numerators are $b-kx,b-ky$, with sum $2b-kP$ and product $V$. The other two have sum $2a+kR$ and product $Z$. Two real numbers are nonnegative exactly when their sum and product are nonnegative. Also $D_0D_1=D_2D_3=D$, $D_0+D_1=P-2k$, and $xD_1+yD_0=2a-kP$. Hence the positive deficits have sum $U/D$ and product $V/D$, and the negative deficits have sum $W/D$ and product $Z/D$ by exchange of the pairs. In particular $U+W=D$.

The negative pair is middle exactly when both its deficits lie between the two positive-pair deficits. The quadratic $q(t)=t^2-(U/D)t+V/D$ is nonpositive precisely on that closed interval. At each negative-pair deficit it is $[(W-U)t+V-Z]/D$, by reduction modulo the negative-pair quadratic. Its values there are both nonpositive exactly when their sum is nonpositive and product is nonnegative. Clearing positive powers of $D$ gives the two claimed inequalities. A repeated root gives the same test, including the case of an interval consisting of a single point.

Finally for the negative middle pair $(1-\delta_2)(1-\delta_3)=N/D$ and $(1-\delta_2)+(1-\delta_3)=(2D-W)/D$. Substituting in $E$ gives $E=3B/N$, with $N>0$, so $B>0$. Inserting $4/E$ and the normalized cap and moment in $F-cs$ gives the displayed fraction. Pair reversal sends $(F,s)$ to $(1-F,-s)$ and leaves the selected correction value unchanged.
