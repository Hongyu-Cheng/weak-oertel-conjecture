## Statement

Use the integer bases of the full-rank cube stress example in odd dimension $d\ge3$. Keep the central cube $[-1,1]^d$ and put $r_j[-1,1]^d$ at the outer bases, where $r_j>0$. The maximum normalized depth of this measured finite union is
$$
\frac{\max\{r_{\max}^d/2,\ 1/2+r_{\min}^d\}}{1+\sum_j r_j^d}.
$$
Compatibility with the given central cube requires mean outer radius at most one. For outer radii of mean one, the affine interpolant of the outer radii gives an actual convex body with these integer fibers, with the central radius unchanged. In particular, the path $(1-s,1+s,1,\ldots,1)$, $0<s\le1/(4d)$, strictly decreases the actual maximum from $3/[2(d+2)]$.

## Proof

Use the complete integer census, exposing base forms, and balanced outer sign maps of the full-rank cube stress example. With $V=2^d$, the total mass is $V(1+\sum_jr_j^d)$. For a through-cap at the central origin, its central mass is at least $V/2$, including pure-base caps. The common smaller cube of radius $r_{\min}$ belongs to every outer cube. Applying the zero-sum coupling of the original example to these smaller cubes proves an additional captured mass of at least $Vr_{\min}^d$. Conversely at any central point choose a coordinate cap of mass at most $V/2$ and tilt to take only a smallest outer cube among the outer sites. The compact cubes and the same strict exposure gaps make this possible with a finite coefficient. Each outer site's maximum equals its own maximum $Vr_j^d/2$: any containing cap pays at least its own mass, and a sufficiently large exposing tilt isolates it. These bounds prove the formula for arbitrary positive radii on the measured finite union.

The Minkowski average of the outer cubes is the cube of their mean radius. It lies in the central fiber of every compatible family since zero is the mean of the outer vertices. Thus the mean outer radius must be at most one. If the radii have mean one, their affine interpolant $r(z)$ on the base simplex has value one at zero, since zero is the mean of its outer vertices. It is positive throughout the simplex, so the shared inequalities $\pm x_k\le r(z)$ and the unchanged base simplex define the stated actual convex body. For the specified path, convexity gives $1+\sum_j r_j^d\ge d+2$. The central numerator $1/2+(1-s)^d$ is strictly below $3/2$, and the outer numerator is at most $e^{1/4}/2<2/3<3/2$, where $e^{1/4}<4/3$ follows by integrating $1/(1-t)>1$ on $(0,1/4)$. Thus the maximum strictly decreases.
