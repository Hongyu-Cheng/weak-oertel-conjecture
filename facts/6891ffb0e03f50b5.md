## Statement

Let the open interiors of finitely many ambient halfspaces cover $C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ for a compact convex set $C$, with $d\ge1$. A region exclusive to a cap consists of points in its open interior and in no other open cap. Each integer fiber meets at most $d+1$ different exclusive regions, regardless of their volumes.

## Proof

If $d+2$ distinct regions meet a fiber, choose one point from each, with continuous coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_{d+2}$. An affine dependence gives coefficients of sum zero, of both signs, and with $\sum_i c_i x_i=0$. After normalizing the positive and negative coefficients by their common sum, the convex hulls of these two disjoint groups share a point. By convexity this point is in the same fiber of $C$. For any cap, one group has no point of its exclusive color, so all points in that group lie in the closed complement of that cap's open interior. Its convex hull, and hence the common point, also misses that cap. This contradicts open coverage.
