## Statement

Let $g_0,\ldots,g_{m-1}$ be a nonnegative, nonzero concave sequence on consecutive indices, with $m\ge1$. Concavity means that its successive differences are nonincreasing. Put $H=\max_i g_i$ and choose any index $j$ with $g_j=H$. For every real $p\ge2$,
$$
(p+1)\sum_i g_i^p-pH\sum_i g_i^{p-1}
\ge \frac{H^p}{2}\left(\frac1{2j+1}+\frac1{2(m-1-j)+1}\right).
$$
The bound allows zero endpoints, a peak at either endpoint, and an arbitrary choice of peak in a plateau.

## Proof

Normalize the maximum to one. Consider a concave arm $u_0=1,u_1,\ldots,u_h$ oriented outwards from a peak. It is nonnegative and nonincreasing. If $h\ge1$, the fixed-length part of 4d628efe7673ce2d (5) applies to this arm and gives a nonnegative affine arm $v_i=1+\eta i$ with the same square sum. The square sum is strictly increasing in the admissible slope, and the original square sum is at most $h+1$, so $\eta\le0$. The fixed-length proof of (5) shows that the differences $u_i^2-v_i^2$ have a nonnegative prefix and a nonpositive suffix. Their sum is zero. On indices where these differences are nonzero the denominators $u_i+v_i$ are positive and nonincreasing. Dividing by these denominators gives $\sum_i(u_i-v_i)\le0$. Indeed, when the differences are nonzero, replacing each denominator by the one at the last positive difference weakly increases every resulting quotient, and their modified sum is zero.

Thus $3\sum_i u_i^2-2\sum_i u_i$ is at least the corresponding affine expression. The latter is
$$
h+1+4\eta\sum_{i=0}^h i+3\eta^2\sum_{i=0}^h i^2.
$$
Its minimum is $(h+1)/(2h+1)$ at $\eta=-2/(2h+1)$, whose endpoint is $1/(2h+1)>0$. When $h=0$ the arm contributes $1$, in agreement with the same minimum formula. Apply this to both outward arms of $g$ and subtract the duplicate peak term $1$. This proves the normalized quadratic bound with coefficient $\kappa=\frac12[(2j+1)^{-1}+(2(m-1-j)+1)^{-1}]$.

For $p>2$, write $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For each $0<t<1$, the indices where $g_i>t$ are consecutive because concavity puts every intermediate value above the chord between any two such indices. On this block $g_i-t$ is concave, has maximum $1-t$, and has the same chosen peak with weakly shorter arms. The quadratic bound on the block therefore has coefficient at least $\kappa$, since both reciprocal denominators weakly decrease as the arms shorten. Append zeros to the sums after applying the bound. This gives
$$
3\sum_i(g_i-t)_+^2-2(1-t)\sum_i(g_i-t)_+\ge\kappa(1-t)^2.
$$
Multiply by $t^{p-3}$ and integrate from $0$ to $1$. The finite sum and the bounded factors multiplying the integrable weight justify termwise integration. For each $x\in[0,1]$, using $1-t=(1-x)+(x-t)$ on $0<t<x$ and integrating powers gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1 t^{p-3}\bigl[3(x-t)_+^2-2(1-t)(x-t)_+\bigr]\,dt
 &=\frac{2[(p+1)x^p-px^{p-1}]}{(p-2)(p-1)p},\\
\int_0^1 t^{p-3}(1-t)^2\,dt&=\frac{2}{(p-2)(p-1)p}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The first identity includes $x=0$, when both sides vanish. Cancel the common factor and restore the original maximum by homogeneity of degree $p$. This proves the stated inequality.
