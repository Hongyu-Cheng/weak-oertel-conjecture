## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body and let finitely many open halfspaces $U_i=\{(z,x):u_i\cdot z+v_i\cdot x>b_i\}$ cover $C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$. Write $K_z=C_z$ and $Q_z=\{x:v_i\cdot x\le b_i-u_i\cdot z\text{ for all }i\}$. For an affine map $c:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d$ and $s\ge1$, let $C_s$ be the image of $C$ under $(z,x)\mapsto(z,c(z)+s(x-c(z)))$. The same caps cover $C_s\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ for every $s\ge1$ exactly when
$$
\bigl(K_z+\operatorname{cone}(K_z-c(z))\bigr)\cap Q_z=\varnothing
$$
at every nonempty integer fiber, including fibers of zero $d$-volume. Here the cone consists of the nonnegative multiples of points of $K_z-c(z)$. If $c(z)$ is in the relative interior of $K_z$, the obstruction is exactly $Q_z\cap\operatorname{aff}K_z\ne\varnothing$. In particular, at an interior center of a full-dimensional fiber, indefinite coverage at that fiber is equivalent to $Q_z=\varnothing$.

For any nonempty polytope $K\subset\mathbb R^d$, nonempty polyhedron $Q$ disjoint from $K$, and $c\in\mathbb R^d$, the dilates $c+s(K-c)$, $s\ge1$, all miss $Q$ exactly when some $\xi\ne0$ satisfies
$$
\xi\cdot c\ge h_K(\xi),\qquad \inf_{q\in Q}\xi\cdot q>h_K(\xi).
$$
The separating condition can fail for a general compact convex $K$ whose every finite dilate misses $Q$.

## Proof

The ambient dilation is invertible affine and fixes the base. In one fiber,
$$
\bigcup_{s\ge1}\bigl(c+s(K-c)\bigr)=K+\operatorname{cone}(K-c).
$$
Indeed $c+s(x-c)=x+(s-1)(x-c)$. Conversely $x+t(y-c)=c+(1+t)(k-c)$ for $k=(x+ty)/(1+t)\in K$. Thus a fixed cap cover persists precisely when this union misses the complementary section in every fiber. With $c\in\operatorname{relint}K$ the dilates of a relative ball about $c$ exhaust $\operatorname{aff}K$ and all dilates stay in that affine space.

In the polytopal case the separating inequalities suffice, since a dilate has support $s h_K(\xi)+(1-s)\xi\cdot c\le h_K(\xi)$. For necessity write $K=\{x:Bx\le d_0\}$ and $Q=\{x:Ex\le f\}$. A hit at a finite $s\ge1$ is equivalent to feasibility of
$$
Ex\le f,\qquad Bx-s(d_0-Bc)\le Bc,\qquad -s\le-1.
$$
Indeed the second inequality tests $c+(x-c)/s\in K$. If infeasible, the finite-dimensional Farkas alternative gives row multipliers $\lambda,\eta\ge0$ and a scalar $\theta\ge0$ with $\lambda E+\eta B=0$, $\eta(Bc-d_0)=\theta$, and $\lambda f+\eta Bc-\theta=\lambda f+\eta d_0<0$. Put $\xi=\eta B=-\lambda E$ as a linear functional. Then $h_K(\xi)\le\eta d_0\le\xi\cdot c$, and $\inf_Q\xi\cdot q\ge-\lambda f>\eta d_0$. The inequalities and nonemptiness of both sets exclude $\xi=0$.

For failure take the closed unit disk centered at $(1,0)$, $c=(0,0)$, and $Q=\{(0,1)\}$. Its dilate has center $(s,0)$ and radius $s$, so the sole point of $Q$ is outside. However $h_K(\xi)=\xi_1+\|\xi\|$ and $\xi\cdot c=0$, so a nonzero candidate $\xi$ must be $(-a,0)$ for $a>0$. It has value zero on $Q$, which equals $h_K(\xi)$. Indeed $x=(x_1,x_2)$ belongs to a finite dilate precisely when $x_1^2+x_2^2\le2s x_1$ for some $s\ge1$, so the union contains exactly the open right halfplane and the origin. Its closure would incorrectly obstruct this dilation.
