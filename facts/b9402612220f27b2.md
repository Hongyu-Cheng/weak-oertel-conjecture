## Statement

The count and gamma normalization is that of accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`: its active vector \(\mathbf N_I\), active mean \(\mathbf j_I^+\), positive weights \((q_i)_{i\in I}\), and partition function \(\mathcal Z\) are respectively \(\mathbf N\), \(\mathbf j\), \(\mathbf q\), and \(\mathcal Z_k\) below, with \(I=\{1,\ldots,2k\}\). Its gamma coefficients \(b_i\) will be \(a_i+u_i\). The support below is not the complete-composition support \(\mathcal E\) of that fact.

For each integer \(k\ge2\), partition \(I\) into \(A_k=\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and \(B_k=\{k+1,\ldots,2k\}\), and define
\[
J_k:=\left\{\mathbf N\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2k}:
  \sum_{i\in I}N_i=2k,\quad
  k-1\le\sum_{i\in A_k}N_i\le k+1\right\}.
\]
Let \(q_i=1\) for every \(i\), let \(\mathcal Z_k=|J_k|\), and let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf N)=1/\mathcal Z_k\) for \(\mathbf N\in J_k\). Then \(J_k\) satisfies symmetric exchange, every coordinate has minimum zero and is nondeterministic, and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is the integer-mean maximum-entropy row with \(\mathbf j=\mathbf1\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}J_k\). Its affine hull has dimension \(2k-1\).

Define \(a_i=1\) on \(A_k\) and \(a_i=0\) on \(B_k\), and define the full constant-gauge space by \(G_{J_k}:=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^{2k}:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf N\text{ is constant on }J_k\}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{J_k}&=\{c\mathbf1:c\in\mathbb R\},\\
Q(\mathbf a)&:=\inf_{\mathbf u\in G_{J_k}}
  \sum_{i\in I}(a_i+u_i)^2(j_i+1)=k,\\
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N)
  &=\frac{4k^2}{6k^2+k-1},\\
\frac{Q(\mathbf a)}{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N)}
  &=\frac{6k^2+k-1}{4k}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently no absolute finite constant satisfies the proposed gamma-noise comparison for all such rows.

## Proof

Fix \(k\ge2\). The set \(J_k\) is finite, consists of nonnegative integer compositions of \(m=2k\), and contains \(\mathbf1\).

First verify symmetric exchange directly, writing \(\mathbf e_i\) for the \(i\)-th standard coordinate vector. Take \(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in J_k\) and an index \(i\) with \(x_i>y_i\). Let \(D\) be the block \(A_k\) or \(B_k\) containing \(i\). Both block sums of every point of \(J_k\) lie in \([k-1,k+1]\), since the total is \(2k\). If some \(h\in D\) has \(x_h<y_h\), choose that \(h\). The two vectors \(\mathbf x-\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_h\) and \(\mathbf y+\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_h\) remain nonnegative, preserve both block sums, and belong to \(J_k\).

Otherwise \(x_h\ge y_h\) for every \(h\in D\), with strict inequality at \(i\), so \(\sum_{h\in D}x_h>\sum_{h\in D}y_h\). Equal total sums imply there is an index \(h\) in the other block with \(x_h<y_h\). Choose it. The strict integer inequality between the \(D\)-sums gives \(\sum_{h\in D}x_h\ge k\) and \(\sum_{h\in D}y_h\le k\). Therefore decreasing the first sum by one and increasing the second by one leaves both in \([k-1,k+1]\). The complementary block sums also remain in that interval because the totals are fixed. Nonnegativity follows from \(x_i\ge1\) and \(y_h\ge1\). Thus the same two exchanged vectors belong to \(J_k\), proving the required symmetric exchange property in all cases.

For any distinct indices \(i,h\), the points \(\mathbf1\pm(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_h)\) belong to \(J_k\): all coordinates are nonnegative, the total is unchanged, and the first block sum is either \(k\), \(k-1\), or \(k+1\). In particular, every coordinate attains zero and two, so no deterministic coordinate is present and every required coordinate-minimum translation is the identity. Taking \(h=2k\), the \(2k-1\) independent directions \(\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_{2k}\) span the fixed-sum hyperplane. The convex hull of the displayed positive and negative translates contains a relative neighborhood of \(\mathbf1\) in that hyperplane. Hence the affine hull has dimension \(2k-1\), \(\mathbf1\) is in its relative interior, and its minimal face is the entire polytope.

The uniform law is invariant under permutations within either block and under swapping the two blocks. These symmetries act transitively on all \(2k\) coordinates. Thus every coordinate has the same expectation; the fixed total \(2k\) makes that expectation one. This proves \(\mathbb E\mathbf N=\mathbf j=\mathbf1\), with positive probability on every point of \(J_k\). The uniform distribution maximizes entropy on a finite set, and it is feasible for this mean, so it is also the unique maximum-entropy law with this mean. Indeed, Jensen's inequality for \(t\log t\), with \(0\log0=0\), gives for any probability mass function \(p\) on \(J_k\)
\[
\log\mathcal Z_k+\sum_{\mathbf N\in J_k}p(\mathbf N)\log p(\mathbf N)
 =\frac1{\mathcal Z_k}\sum_{\mathbf N\in J_k}
  \bigl(\mathcal Z_kp(\mathbf N)\bigr)\log\bigl(\mathcal Z_kp(\mathbf N)\bigr)
 \ge0,
\]
with equality only for the uniform law.

If \(\mathbf u\in G_{J_k}\), comparison of \(\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf1+\mathbf e_i-\mathbf e_h\) gives \(u_i=u_h\) for every pair \(i,h\). Conversely, any common coefficient is constant on a fixed-sum support. Therefore \(G_{J_k}=\mathbb R\mathbf1\), with no additional block or component gauges. Since \(j_i+1=2\), direct minimization gives
\[
\sum_{i\in I}(a_i+c)^2(j_i+1)
 =2k\bigl((1+c)^2+c^2\bigr)
 =4k(c+1/2)^2+k.
\]
Its infimum is \(k\), attained at \(c=-1/2\).

It remains to compute the projection distribution exactly. Set \(Y:=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf N-\mathbf j)=\sum_{i\in A_k}N_i-k\); thus \(Y\in\{-1,0,1\}\). The number of nonnegative integer compositions of an integer \(s\ge0\) into \(k\) coordinates is \(\binom{s+k-1}{k-1}\), by placing \(k-1\) separators among \(s+k-1\) positions. Consequently the counts for \(Y=0\), \(Y=-1\), and \(Y=1\) are respectively
\[
\binom{2k-1}{k-1}^{\!2},\qquad
\binom{2k-2}{k-1}\binom{2k}{k-1},\qquad
\binom{2k-2}{k-1}\binom{2k}{k-1}.
\]
The ratio of either outer count to the central count is
\(2k^2/((2k-1)(k+1))\), obtained by cancelling the factorials. Normalizing these three exact counts therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(Y=0)&=\frac{(2k-1)(k+1)}{6k^2+k-1},\\
\Pr(Y=-1)=\Pr(Y=1)&=\frac{2k^2}{6k^2+k-1},\\
\mathbb EY=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=\frac{4k^2}{6k^2+k-1}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Variance is unaffected by centering, so this is the stated variance of \(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf N\). Combining it with \(Q(\mathbf a)=k\) yields the stated divergent ratio. All parameters, probabilities, means, affine-rank assertions, and exchanges have been checked exactly.

Finally, this ratio is precisely the noise obstruction for the conditional gamma construction in accepted fact `cfb2a3f2d408f191`, not a diagonal covariance estimate. Given \(\mathbf N\), take conditionally independent \(T_i\) with density \(e^{-t}t^{N_i}/N_i!\) on \(t>0\). As in that fact, \(\mathbb E[T_i\mid\mathbf N]=N_i+1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(T_i\mid\mathbf N)=N_i+1\). For \(\mathbf u\in G_{J_k}\), put \(b_i=a_i+u_i\) and \(\widetilde Y_{\mathbf u}:=\sum_i b_i(T_i-j_i-1)\). The gauge term vanishes in the centered count projection, so \(\mathbb E[\widetilde Y_{\mathbf u}\mid\mathbf N]=Y\). Conditional independence and total variance give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y_{\mathbf u})
 =\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\sum_{i\in I}(a_i+u_i)^2(j_i+1).
\]
Taking the infimum over every allowed gauge leaves the noise term \(Q(\mathbf a)=k\), whereas \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)<1\). This proves the comparison fails even after the stipulated support reductions and all affine-constant gauges.
