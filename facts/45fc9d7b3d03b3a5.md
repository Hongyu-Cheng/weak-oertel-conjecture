## Statement

Let $Z$ have a finitely supported integer-valued probability law of mean $\theta\in(0,1)$. Among dominated sublaws of mass $\theta$, let $U$ be the largest first moment. The top selection, filling the largest sites first with at most one partially retained site, attains $U$. Then $U\ge\theta$, with equality exactly when $\min\operatorname{supp}Z=0$ or $\max\operatorname{supp}Z=1$. The constant density $\theta$ gives first moment $\theta^2$. If the support extends below zero and above one, a density realizing both mass and first moment $\theta$ can be chosen uniformly bounded away from zero and one on the support.

## Proof

A maximizer exists by compactness of the finite mass box. If it uses positive mass at a smaller site while a larger site is not full, moving a positive amount from smaller to larger increases the objective. This characterizes the top selection. Let $p=\Pr(Z\ge1)$.

If $p\ge\theta$, the top selection is supported on sites at least one, so $U\ge\theta$. It uses positive mass above one whenever such a site is present. In this case equality is equivalent to maximum support one. If $p<\theta$, the complement is the bottom $1-\theta$ mass supported on sites at most zero. Its moment is nonpositive, so $U\ge\theta$ by subtracting that moment from $\mathbb E Z=\theta$. The bottom selection contains positive mass below zero whenever such a site is present, so equality in this case is equivalent to minimum support zero. These conclusions give the stated union of cases: if the support minimum is zero then $\theta=\mathbb E Z\ge p$, and equality $p=\theta$ forces all positive sites to be one. If the support maximum is one then $\theta\le p$.

The constant density has mass $\theta$ and first moment $\theta^2$. If $U>\theta$, combine this density with the top density using coefficient $(\theta-\theta^2)/(U-\theta^2)$ on the top density. The coefficient lies strictly between zero and one, giving the requested moments and strict uniform bounds.
