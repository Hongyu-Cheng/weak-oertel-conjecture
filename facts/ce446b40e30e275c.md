## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\) be a compact full-dimensional convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\mathcal H_d(C_z).
\]
Write
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z>0,\qquad
D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^2:w_z>0\}.
\]
For every Borel set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}\), define
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}
\mathcal H_d\bigl(\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\bigr),
\]
and, for \(p\in S\), define
\[
h_S(p):=\inf\{\mu(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
Assume
\[
w_z<\frac W4\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^2).
\]
Let \(z_0\in D\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{z\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}w_z\geq\frac W4
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(z_0\), and assume
\[
z_0\in\partial\operatorname{conv}D.
\]
Let \(c_0\) be the centroid of the \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(C_{z_0}\). Then
\[
h_S((z_0,c_0))>\frac W{64}.
\]
Moreover, the restriction of \(h_S\) to \(\{z_0\}\times C_{z_0}\) attains its maximum, and every maximizer \((z_0,y_0)\) satisfies
\[
h_S((z_0,y_0))>\frac W{64}.
\]

## Proof

The projection of \(C\) to \(\mathbb R^2\) is compact, so it meets \(\mathbb Z^2\) in only finitely many points. In particular, \(D\) is finite and all sums below are finite.

Choose a supporting line \(\ell\) of \(\operatorname{conv}D\) through \(z_0\). Thus there is a nonzero linear functional \(\eta:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) such that
\[
\ell=z_0+\ker\eta,\qquad
\eta(z-z_0)\geq0\quad(z\in D).
\]
The outward closed halfspace
\[
Q_0:=\{z:-\eta(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
is proper, contains \(z_0\), and meets \(D\) exactly in \(D\cap\ell\). The marginal hypothesis therefore gives
\[
\sum_{z\in D\cap\ell}w_z\geq\frac W4.
\tag{1}
\]
Since \(w_{z_0}<W/4\), there is a point \(z_1\in D\cap\ell\) distinct from \(z_0\). Hence \(\ell\) has rational direction. If \(v\in\mathbb Z^2\) is the primitive integer vector parallel to \(z_1-z_0\), then
\[
\ell\cap\mathbb Z^2=z_0+\mathbb Z v.
\tag{2}
\]

Both closed rays in (2) have mass at least \(W/4\), and this follows from finite tilts rather than a limiting argument. Choose a linear functional \(\rho:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\) with \(\rho(v)=1\). For each \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), choose \(\varepsilon_\sigma>0\) so small that
\[
\varepsilon_\sigma|\rho(z-z_0)|<\eta(z-z_0)
\qquad(z\in D\setminus\ell);
\tag{3}
\]
if \(D\setminus\ell=\varnothing\), any positive \(\varepsilon_\sigma\) works. The proper closed halfspace
\[
Q_\sigma:=
\{z:-\eta(z-z_0)+\varepsilon_\sigma\sigma\rho(z-z_0)\geq0\}
\]
contains \(z_0\). By (3), it contains no point of \(D\setminus\ell\), while on \(\ell\) it contains exactly the lattice points \(z_0+kv\) with \(\sigma k\geq0\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{k\geq0}w_{z_0+kv}\geq\frac W4,\qquad
\sum_{k\leq0}w_{z_0+kv}\geq\frac W4.
\tag{4}
\]
The rays are closed, so the anchor fiber is retained in both estimates. Since \(w_{z_0}<W/4\), each ray also contains a positive-mass fiber other than the anchor.

Consider the affine space and its relative slice
\[
E:=(z_0+\mathbb Rv)\times\mathbb R^d,\qquad
K:=C\cap E.
\]
Identify \(E\) with \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) by \((t,x)\mapsto(z_0+tv,x)\), and put
\[
A_k:=C_{z_0+kv},\qquad a_k:=\mathcal H_d(A_k)
\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{5}
\]
The anchor section \(A_0\) has positive \(d\)-volume, so it contains \(d+1\) affinely independent points in \(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d\). By (4) and \(a_0<W/4\), \(K\) also contains a point in a positive-mass fiber with nonzero first coordinate. Thus \(\operatorname{aff}K=E\), and \(K\) is a compact convex body of relative dimension \(d+1\). This verifies relative full dimensionality even if some nonanchor fibers of \(K\) are nonempty but have zero \(d\)-volume.

The centroid \(c_0\) belongs to \(A_0\): otherwise a separating linear functional would have value at its average strictly outside the range of its values on \(A_0\). Hence \(p_0:=(z_0,c_0)\) belongs to \(S\). By accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42`, it is enough to bound closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(p_0\); translating the boundary of any containing halfspace toward \(p_0\) only decreases the halfspace.

Let \(H\) be such an ambient closed halfspace. Its restriction to \(E\) is all of \(E\), or, in the coordinates above, is the proper relative halfspace
\[
\{(t,x):\alpha t+b^\top(x-c_0)\geq0\}
\tag{6}
\]
for some \((\alpha,b)\neq(0,0)\). If the restriction is all of \(E\), its mass on the rank-one slice is at least either ray mass in (4), hence at least \(W/4\). If (6) is proper and \(b=0\), then it contains one entire closed lattice ray, including \(k=0\), and again has slice mass at least \(W/4\). These cases include every base-only, or vertical, restriction and retain any mixed mass on the anchor boundary.

It remains to treat an ordinary restriction, so assume \(b\neq0\). Define the opposite cap
\[
B:=K\cap\{(t,x):\alpha t+b^\top(x-c_0)\leq0\},
\]
and its integer-fiber sections and volumes
\[
B_k:=\{x\in A_k:\alpha k+b^\top(x-c_0)\leq0\},
\qquad b_k:=\mathcal H_d(B_k).
\tag{7}
\]
Both \(K\) and \(B\) are convex. Consequently,
\[
\frac12(A_{k-1}+A_{k+1})\subseteq A_k,\qquad
\frac12(B_{k-1}+B_{k+1})\subseteq B_k.
\tag{8}
\]
The Brunn--Minkowski inequality applied to (8), followed by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, yields
\[
a_k^2\geq a_{k-1}a_{k+1},\qquad
b_k^2\geq b_{k-1}b_{k+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
If an outer term in either inequality has zero \(d\)-volume, the corresponding assertion is immediate. If two sections at indices \(i<j\) have positive \(d\)-volume, convexity and Brunn--Minkowski applied with parameter \((k-i)/(j-i)\) show that every section at an integer \(i<k<j\) also has positive \(d\)-volume. Thus each positive support is an interval of integers. Compactness makes both sequences finitely supported. In particular, \((a_k)\) and \((b_k)\) are nonnegative summable log-concave sequences in the standard discrete sense. Nonempty lower-dimensional boundary fibers simply contribute zero terms and cause no exception.

Set
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
\theta_d:=1-\gamma_d.
\tag{10}
\]
The cutting hyperplane in every fiber has zero \(\mathcal H_d\)-measure because \(b\neq0\). Grünbaum's centroid inequality applied in the anchor fiber to the side in (6) gives
\[
a_0-b_0\geq\gamma_da_0,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
b_0\leq\theta_da_0.
\tag{11}
\]
Applying the same theorem to the opposite side also gives \(b_0>0\).

Log-concavity at index zero gives \(b_0^2\geq b_{-1}b_1\), so at least one of \(b_{-1},b_1\) is at most \(b_0\). Choose \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\) accordingly. On the positive support, successive ratios of a log-concave sequence are nonincreasing. It follows along the chosen ray, with zeros handled by interval support, that
\[
b_{\sigma k}\leq b_0\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{12}
\]
Since the boundary in (6) has zero \(d\)-volume in every fiber, the mass contributed by its side on fiber \(\sigma k\) equals \(a_{\sigma k}-b_{\sigma k}\). Equations (11)-(12) therefore imply
\[
a_{\sigma k}-b_{\sigma k}
\geq (a_{\sigma k}-\theta_da_0)_+
\qquad(k\geq0).
\tag{13}
\]

Apply accepted fact `772688f4504b1b5b` to
\[
u_k:=\frac{a_{\sigma k}}{a_0}\qquad(k\geq0)
\]
with threshold \(\theta_d\). Its hypotheses hold by (9), interval support, finite support, and \(u_0=1\). Combining that fact with (4) and (13) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu\bigl(H\cap((z_0+\mathbb Zv)\times\mathbb R^d)\bigr)
&\geq
\sum_{k\geq0}(a_{\sigma k}-\theta_da_0)_+\\
&\geq
q_d\sum_{k\geq0}a_{\sigma k}\\
&\geq q_d\,\frac W4,
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
q_d:=
\gamma_d-(1-\gamma_d)\log\frac1{1-\gamma_d}.
\tag{15}
\]

The constant in (15) is strictly greater than \(1/16\), uniformly in \(d\). Indeed,
\[
d\log\left(1+\frac1d\right)<1,
\]
so \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\). For \(0<\gamma<1\), put
\[
q(\gamma):=\gamma+(1-\gamma)\log(1-\gamma).
\]
Then \(q(0)=0\) and \(q'(\gamma)=-\log(1-\gamma)>0\), so
\[
q_d=q(\gamma_d)>q(e^{-1}).
\]
Moreover, \(-\log(1-s)>s\) for \(0<s<1\), and hence
\[
q(e^{-1})
=\int_0^{e^{-1}}-\log(1-s)\,ds
>\frac1{2e^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Finally,
\[
e=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{j!}
\leq\frac52+\frac16\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}4^{-m}
=\frac{49}{18}
<\frac{11}{4},
\]
so \(e^2<121/16<8\). Thus (16) gives
\[
q_d>\frac1{2e^2}>\frac1{16}.
\tag{17}
\]

In the all-of-\(E\) and \(b=0\) cases, the slice mass is at least \(W/4\), which is also at least \(q_dW/4\) because \(0<q_d<1\). In the ordinary case, (14) gives the latter bound. The line-slice mixed mass is a submass of the ambient mixed mass, because it sums only the fibers indexed by \(z_0+\mathbb Zv\). Therefore every ambient closed halfspace whose boundary contains \(p_0\) has mass at least
\[
q_d\frac W4>\frac W{64}.
\]
The boundary reduction in accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` now yields
\[
h_S((z_0,c_0))\geq q_d\frac W4>\frac W{64}.
\tag{18}
\]

Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` also states that \(h_S\) is upper semicontinuous on \(S\). The set \(\{z_0\}\times C_{z_0}\) is compact, so the restriction of \(h_S\) to this fiber attains its maximum. Every maximizer \((z_0,y_0)\) satisfies
\[
h_S((z_0,y_0))
\geq h_S((z_0,c_0))
>\frac W{64},
\]
which proves the asserted consequence.

Exact statement used: if \(0<\lambda<1\), \(X,Y\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) are nonempty bounded measurable sets, and \((1-\lambda)X+\lambda Y\) is measurable, then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-\lambda)X+\lambda Y)^{1/d}
\geq
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_d(X)^{1/d}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_d(Y)^{1/d}.
\]
Applicability: whenever both neighboring sections in (8) have positive volume, they are compact convex subsets of \(\mathbb R^d\), their midpoint Minkowski sum is compact, and (8) contains that sum in the middle section. When an outer volume is zero, (9) is immediate. The same check applies to both \(A_k\) and the convex opposite-cap sections \(B_k\).
