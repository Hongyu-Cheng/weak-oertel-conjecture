## Statement

Let $k\ge0$, $m\ge1$, and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^k$ be finite and nonempty and span $\mathbb R^k$ affinely. For every $\ell$ let $(K_{\ell,z})_{z\in P}$ be a compatible family of convex $m$-bodies in $\mathbb R^m$: for every anchored representation $z=\sum_a\lambda_a z_a$ in $P$, with nonnegative coefficients summing to one, $\sum_a\lambda_a K_{\ell,z_a}\subseteq K_{\ell,z}$. Put $M_\ell=\sum_z |K_{\ell,z}|_m$ and assume $|K_{\ell,z}|_m/M_\ell\to p_z>0$ for each $z$. Let $P_1,\ldots,P_s$ be the dependence components generated by the nontrivial anchored representations, let $L(P)$ be the scalar space preserving all anchored representations, and write $t=\dim L(P)-k-1$ and $D=sm+t$.

There are full-dimensional convex bodies $B_\ell\subset\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^D$ with exactly the integer bases $P$, whose integer fibers lie in fixed affine $m$-planes and are uniformly bounded there. The lifted mixed trace has intrinsic $\mathcal H_m$-mass one, with masses $|K_{\ell,z}|_m/M_\ell$. A global affine map $F_\ell$ fixing the base maps it onto the original placed family and pushes its intrinsic measure to the original sum of fiber volumes divided by $M_\ell$. On the fixed plane of a lifted fiber over $P_i$, in isometric coordinates, its continuous map has the form $x\mapsto A_{\ell,i}x+b_{\ell,z}$ with $|\det A_{\ell,i}|=M_\ell$. At every lifted point the original normalized depth at its image is at least its lifted depth. At a point in component $i$ the lifted depth equals the component mass times its depth for the normalized component law, and is continuous within each fiber. Along a subsequence all lifted fibers converge in Hausdorff distance to positive $m$-bodies in their planes; their hull is a full-dimensional limit lift with exactly those integer fibers, and maximum normalized lifted depth converges to its maximum normalized depth. Finally $m\le D\le (m+1)2^k-k-1$. All depths use every containing closed ambient halfspace.

## Proof

The anchored representation relations stay within components. Hence the indicators of the components belong to $L(P)$, and the graph $(z,G(z))$, formed by adjoining the $t$ functions that complete $1,z_1,\ldots,z_k$ to a basis of $L(P)$, is affinely equivalent to the canonical graph of ba0f3db1bf4f62c6. Its affine dimension is $\dim L(P)-1$ and it preserves anchored representations, closed traces and relative Helly number. A simplex on that many independent affine directions in the graph has $\dim L(P)$ facets whose total intersection is empty and whose intersection after omitting any one facet contains the opposite vertex. This gives $h(P)\ge\dim L(P)$ (use one empty test when the dimension is zero). By 3d5f56b513c21fc4 the components have disjoint occupied base parities and $h(P)\le 2^k$; both bounds are immediate for the singleton at $k=0$. Thus $s\le2^k$ and the claimed bounds on $D$ follow.

First rescale all the original fibers by $M_\ell^{-1/m}$. The resulting volumes lie in $[\eta,1]$ for a single $\eta>0$, by positivity of the limits and the finitely many positive initial volumes. Apply aa4df3d20ca63976 within each dependence component to these rescaled families. Its determinant-one continuous normalizations bound all diameters in that component uniformly. Take their linear parts, ignoring the common translations that do not affect the diameters. The combined maps from the original bodies are invertible linear maps $T_{\ell,i}$ with $|\det T_{\ell,i}|=1/M_\ell$. The family $Q_{\ell,z}=T_{\ell,i}K_{\ell,z}$ on $z\in P_i$ remains compatible because each representation is inside one component. Apply 89563f7ef7a4762f to the whole compatible $Q$ family with this common diameter bound. It produces a representation-preserving vector array $g_{\ell,z}$ and bounded residual bodies $R_{\ell,z}=Q_{\ell,z}-g_{\ell,z}$ that remain compatible and have masses $|K_{\ell,z}|_m/M_\ell$.

Use the continuous space $(\mathbb R^m)^s\times\mathbb R^t$. For $z\in P_i$ place the residual fiber as $\{(E_i x,G(z)):x\in R_{\ell,z}\}$, where $E_i$ is the isometric inclusion in block $i$. Let $B_\ell$ be the convex hull of all these placed fibers over their original bases. At each anchored representation the graph coordinates combine exactly, and all source blocks coincide with the target block, where the residual inclusion holds. Consequently the placed fibers are exactly the sections of the hull at sites in $P$, by ba0f3db1bf4f62c6, and completeness of $P$ excludes all other integer sections. The hull directions include each full $m$-block because each component contains a positive-volume fiber. Modulo the sum of the blocks, the graph in the base and remaining $t$ coordinates spans affine dimension $k+t$. Thus the hull has full dimension $k+D$. Each placement is isometric on its $m$-plane. Finite additivity of $\mathcal H_m$ on the disjoint fibers gives the claimed masses.

On $P_i$ put $h_{\ell,z}=T_{\ell,i}^{-1}g_{\ell,z}$. Every relation stays inside a component, so this vector array still preserves all representations. The basis $(1,z,G(z))$ gives an affine map $h_\ell(z,\tau)$ with values $h_{\ell,z}$ at $\tau=G(z)$. Define
$$
F_\ell(z,X_1,\ldots,X_s,\tau)
=\left(z,\ \sum_i T_{\ell,i}^{-1}X_i+h_\ell(z,\tau)\right).
$$
On a fiber this sends its residual coordinate $x$ to $T_{\ell,i}^{-1}(x+g_{\ell,z})$, exactly recovering $K_{\ell,z}$. The determinant of the inverse on the $m$-block has absolute value $M_\ell$, so the pushforward of the lifted measure is the original sum divided by $M_\ell$. Any original containing closed halfspace pulls back to a containing closed halfspace or the whole space, establishing the pointwise depth inequality.

For a point in component $i$, every ambient containing cut restricts to a containing cut on its affine span or to the whole span. Conversely extend the affine functional of any containing restricted cut and subtract $T(1-I_i)$, where $I_i$ is the affine extension of the component indicator in $(z,\tau)$. This leaves the chosen closed trace unchanged and strictly excludes all other components for a sufficiently large finite $T$, by boundedness of the finitely many other fibers. The whole component can be isolated by $I_i\ge1/2$. This proves that the pointwise depth equals the mass of the component times its normalized component depth, including boundary mass. The same argument applies to the ordinary canonical residual placement $(z,G(z),x)$ of 89563f7ef7a4762f. On each component the latter placement and the present block placement are affinely equivalent by the block isometry and the coordinate permutation, preserving the intrinsic fiber masses. Thus the two lifted depth functions agree at corresponding points. The within-fiber continuity and maximum-depth convergence in 89563f7ef7a4762f therefore apply to this placement as well.

For the subsequence, compactness of bounded families of nonempty compact convex sets in the Hausdorff metric gives simultaneous convergence of the residual bodies. Their indicators converge almost everywhere in the common ball: outside the limit they are eventually zero, in the interior support-function convergence puts the point in every sufficiently late body, and the boundary of a full-dimensional convex limit has volume zero (a lower-dimensional limit itself has volume zero). Hence their limiting volumes are $p_z>0$. Compatibility passes to limits by choosing converging representatives of the source points in every anchored combination. The same exact-fiber and full-dimension arguments apply to the limiting placed hull. The canonical residual depth convergence just invoked now gives the claimed convergence of maxima.
