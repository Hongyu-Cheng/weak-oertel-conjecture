## Statement

For each integer $q\ge1$ there is $\sigma_q\in(0,1/2]$ such that if $A,B$ are convex bodies in the same $q$-dimensional Euclidean space with
$$
1-\sigma_q\le |A|,|B|\le1,\qquad |(A+B)/2|\le1+\sigma_q,
$$
then some translate $B+u$ satisfies $|A\cap(B+u)|\ge3/4$. All volumes are $q$-dimensional.

## Proof

Otherwise take counterexample pairs $A_j,B_j$ with the volume tolerance tending to zero. Their midpoint bodies $D_j=(A_j+B_j)/2$ have volume tending to one by Brunn–Minkowski and the stipulated upper bound. In each $D_j$ choose a maximum-volume inscribed simplex, which exists by compactness of the tuples of vertices and has positive volume. Send this simplex to $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_q)$ by an affine map with linear part $L_j$. Replacing a simplex vertex by any point of the normalized $D_j$ shows that the absolute value of each barycentric coordinate is at most one. In particular $T\subset D'_j\subset[-1,1]^q$. Consequently the determinant factors $|\det L_j|=|D'_j|/|D_j|$ stay between positive constants.

Translate $L_j A_j,L_j B_j$ separately so both contain zero, calling the results $A'_j,B'_j$. The widths of either input are bounded by those of $2D'_j$, since widths add under Minkowski addition. Thus these translated inputs are in a common bounded box. By compactness of bounded nonempty convex compact sets in the Hausdorff metric, pass to Hausdorff limits of both inputs and to a limit $\ell>0$ of the determinant factors. Volume is continuous for compact convex sets in this metric. To see this, the outer parallel neighborhoods of the limit give an upper bound tending to its volume. For a full-dimensional limit $K$ containing a ball of radius $r>0$ about an interior point, each homothetic copy of ratio $1-\delta$ about that point lies in all sufficiently close sets, since its support functions have a margin at least $\delta r$ in every unit direction. This yields the lower bound, and for a lower-dimensional limit the upper bound suffices.

The two limits both have volume $\ell$ and their midpoint has volume $\ell$, since Minkowski addition is continuous in Hausdorff distance. Brunn–Minkowski equality makes the limits translates. Translate the second sequence by that fixed displacement to align the limits. The same inner homothetic copies lie in both sequences eventually, so their intersection volumes tend to $\ell$. Undoing the input translations and $L_j$ gives relative translates of the original pairs with overlap tending to one. This contradicts the failure of the $3/4$ bound.
