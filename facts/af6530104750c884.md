## Statement

For any positive integer $N$, take $C_N=\{(t,x):0\le x\le1,\ 0\le t\le N\sqrt{x}\}$ in one integer and one continuous coordinate. The added unit-extrusion measure $\rho_N=\mu|_{C_N+[0,1]e_t}-\mu|_{C_N}$ has mass one. Every point of its actual added set and its closure has raw depth under $\rho_N$ at most $(4\sqrt{2N}+2)/N$, while the quotient interval has maximum raw depth $1/2$. For every $n,d\ge1$, products give a unit extrusion with maximum normalized roof depth strictly below half the maximum normalized depth of its quotient law, by taking $N=4096$.

## Proof

Concavity of the square root makes $C_N$ convex. By the exact Borel roof law, the added points lie on levels $t=j$ with $(j-1)^2/N^2\le x<j^2/N^2$ for $1\le j\le N$, and at the extra point $(N+1,1)$ of zero mass. The $j$ intervals have lengths $(2j-1)/N^2<2/N$ and total mass one. At a point $(j_0,x_0)$ of any closed interval from these levels, take the closed cap
$$
x-\frac{2j_0}{N^2}t\le x_0-\frac{2j_0^2}{N^2}.
$$
To capture positive length at level $j$ requires $(j-1)^2\le N^2 x_0+2j_0(j-j_0)\le j_0^2+2j_0(j-j_0)$, or $(j-j_0-1)^2\le2j_0\le2N$. There are at most $2\sqrt{2N}+1$ such indices. At the extra point use $\{t\ge N+1\}$ of zero roof mass. Thus the bound holds on the entire actual roof and its closure. At $N=4096$, $2N<91^2$ gives a bound below $366/4096=183/2048<1/4$.

For dimensions $n,d\ge1$, take the product body with $[0,1]^{n-1}$ in integer-base directions and $[0,1]^{d-1}$ in continuous directions. Each binary integer base carries the same roof law and a quotient unit $d$-cube of mass one. A finite base tilt isolates any chosen binary vertex without altering the cap on its fiber: for a vertex $b$, $(2b-\mathbf1)\cdot(z-b)\le-1$ at every other binary vertex, and the untilted margin is bounded on the compact support. Consequently the roof raw upper bound is unchanged. The quotient has raw maximum $1/2$: the center of an individual cube gives at least that by symmetry, and a coordinate cut at each candidate, tilted to isolate its base, has at most that mass. Both total masses are $2^{n-1}$, yielding the normalized strict comparison.
