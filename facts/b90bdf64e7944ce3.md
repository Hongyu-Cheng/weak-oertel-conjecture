## Statement

Use precisely the scalar and prefix normalization of accepted predecessor 30375b78b4633f77, restated here for the quantities in this theorem. Let \(k\) be a nonnegative integer, let \(T\) be a nonnegative integer and \(\beta\ge0\) a finite real number, and, when \(k>0\), let \(2\le B_1\le\cdots\le B_k\) and \(C_{4,i}\ge2B_i-1\) be integers. Set \(g_i=(0,1,B_i,C_{4,i})\) on \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\). For \(0\le j\le k\), use the prefix alphabet \(D_i^{(j)}=\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\) for \(i\le j\), and \(D_i^{(j)}=\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) for \(i>j\). For every allowed root \(\lambda\), put
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}&=\lbrace h\in\mathbb Z:\lambda+h,\lambda-h\in D_i^{(j)}\rbrace,\\
e_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]-g_i(\lambda),\\
b_{i,\lambda}(h)&=\tfrac12[g_i(\lambda+h)-g_i(\lambda-h)],\\
K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}}
 e^{-(2s/3)[g_i(\lambda+h)+g_i(\lambda-h)]},\\
H_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\sum_{h\in I_{i,\lambda}^{(j)}}e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)},\\
Z_i^{(j)}(s)&=\sum_{x\in D_i^{(j)}}e^{-s g_i(x)},\qquad
P_i^{(j)}(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in D_i^{(j)}}K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
These definitions apply for every finite \(s\ge0\). Let \(J_i\) have probabilities \(K_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)^3/P_i^{(j)}(s)\); conditionally on \(J_i=\lambda\), let \(V_i\) have probabilities \(e^{-(4s/3)e_{i,\lambda}(h)}/H_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)\). In particular, \(\sigma^b\) below is the conditional imbalance second moment, with no change to the predecessor convention:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar e_{s,i}^{(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,\lambda}(V_i)\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\sigma_{s,i}^{e,(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[e_{i,\lambda}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\sigma_{s,i}^{b,(j)}(\lambda)&=\mathbb E[b_{i,\lambda}(V_i)^2\mid J_i=\lambda],\\
\mu_i^{(j)}(s)&=\mathbb E[g_i(J_i)+\bar e_{s,i}^{(j)}(J_i)],\\
R_i^{(j)}(s)&=s^2\left(1+\mathbb E\left[g_i(J_i)^2+
 \sigma_{s,i}^{e,(j)}(J_i)+\sigma_{s,i}^{b,(j)}(J_i)\right]\right),\\
V_s^{(j)}&=\sum_{i=1}^kR_i^{(j)}(s),\qquad
\mu^{(j)}(s)=\sum_{i=1}^k\mu_i^{(j)}(s),\\
L_j(s)&=40\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^k\left(1+\mathbb E\left[g_i(J_i)^2+
 \sigma_{s,i}^{e,(j)}(J_i)+\sigma_{s,i}^{b,(j)}(J_i)\right]\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed conditional-expectation expression for \(R_i^{(j)}\) equals the predecessor's expression involving \(e_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2+b_{i,J_i}(V_i)^2\) by conditioning. Interpret \(R_i^{(j)}(s)/s^2\) at \(s=0\) as the displayed factor multiplying \(s^2\). Thus \(L_j\) is continuous at zero and equals \(40\sqrt{V_s^{(j)}}/s\) for \(s>0\).

Let
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_t&=\sum_{u=t}^T e^{-\beta u}\quad(0\le t\le T),\qquad s_{\max}=12\log2,\\
\mathcal F(s)&=\max_{0\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z}[\log(f_t/f_0)+st],\\
\mathcal J(s)&=\lbrace t:\log(f_t/f_0)+st=\mathcal F(s)\rbrace,\\
\Psi_j(s)&=\mathcal F(s)+\tfrac14\sum_{i=1}^k\log P_i^{(j)}(s)
              -\int_0^sL_j(y)\,dy\qquad(0\le s\le s_{\max}).
\end{aligned}
$$
If the initial cap is real and nonnegative, use its integer part, exactly as in 30375b78b4633f77.

For every coordinate and prefix, the extended \(R_i^{(j)}(s)/s^2\) and \(\mu_i^{(j)}(s)\) have strictly negative derivatives at every finite \(s\ge0\), where the derivative at zero may be read as the right derivative. In particular both quantities are strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\).

If \(k\ge1\), then \(L_j'(s)<0\) and \((\mu^{(j)})'(s)<0\) at every finite \(s\ge0\). Each \(\Psi_j\) is strictly convex on \([0,s_{\max}]\) and has a unique global minimizer in \((0,s_{\max}]\). For an arbitrary prescribed rate \(a\in[0,s_{\max}]\), the necessary and sufficient certificates that it is this unique minimizer are
$$
\begin{cases}
\max\mathcal J(0)\ge\mu^{(j)}(0)+L_j(0),&a=0,\\
\min\mathcal J(a)\le\mu^{(j)}(a)+L_j(a)\le\max\mathcal J(a),
 &0<a<s_{\max},\\
\min\mathcal J(s_{\max})\le\mu^{(j)}(s_{\max})+L_j(s_{\max}),
 &a=s_{\max}.
\end{cases}
$$
The first certificate never holds when \(k\ge1\). These certificates include all ties among the active heights. If \(T=0\) and \(k\ge1\), the unique minimizing rate is \(s_{\max}\).

If \(k=0\), the strict convexity and uniqueness claims are false: \(\Psi_0=\mathcal F\). For \(T\ge1\), set
$$
\theta_T=\min_{1\le t\le T}\frac{\log(f_0/f_t)}{t}>0.
$$
Its full minimizing set on \([0,s_{\max}]\) is \([0,\min(s_{\max},\theta_T)]\). For \(T=0\), every rate in \([0,s_{\max}]\) minimizes it. Thus the condition \(k\ge1\) is necessary for the strict theorem. No independence assertion about any original epigraph law is made. The locked \(n,d,C,\Lambda,S,W,c_0\), and arithmetic \(N\) are not changed or reused.

## Proof

Fix a prefix and one of its coordinates. Only within the scalar calculation, suppress its index \(i\) and prefix superscript \((j)\): \(g,B,C_4,P,R,\mu\), and the root and displacement laws mean exactly \(g_i,B_i,C_{4,i},P_i^{(j)},R_i^{(j)},\mu_i^{(j)}\), and the laws just defined. This is the identical normalization of accepted predecessor 30375b78b4633f77. We prove the new assertions directly from those definitions.

Expand the cube of each root kernel by ordered triples of displacements \(\mathbf h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\in I_\lambda^3\). Define the energy and averaged moment contribution of this tuple by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Xi(\lambda,\mathbf h)&=4g(\lambda)+\tfrac43\sum_{q=1}^3e_\lambda(h_q),\\
A(\lambda,\mathbf h)&=g(\lambda)^2+\tfrac13\sum_{q=1}^3
   [e_\lambda(h_q)^2+b_\lambda(h_q)^2].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
The factorization
$$
K_s(\lambda)=e^{-(4s/3)g(\lambda)}H_s(\lambda)
$$
gives the exact finite expansion
$$
P(s)=\sum_\lambda\ \sum_{\mathbf h\in I_\lambda^3}e^{-s\Xi(\lambda,\mathbf h)}.
\tag{2}
$$
Give these tuples probabilities \(e^{-s\Xi}/P(s)\), and write \(\mathbb E_s\) for this auxiliary tuple expectation. Its root marginal is exactly \(K_s(\lambda)^3/P(s)\). Conditionally on the root, its three displacement slots are independent and each has precisely the displayed law of \(V_i\). Consequently, averaging the three identical slot expectations proves
$$
\frac{R(s)}{s^2}=1+\mathbb E_s A,
\qquad
\mathbb E_s\Xi=4\mathbb E[g(J_i)+\bar e_s(J_i)]=4\mu(s),
\tag{3}
$$
with the stipulated continuous meaning for the first equality at zero. This is an algebraic auxiliary expansion of one kernel, with no claim about the original complete law.

For a four-site coordinate the endpoint roots \(0,3\) allow only displacement zero. Each interior root \(\lambda=1,2\) allows \(0,1,-1\). For these interior roots define
$$
e_\lambda=\tfrac12[g(\lambda+1)+g(\lambda-1)]-g(\lambda),
\qquad
b_\lambda=\tfrac12[g(\lambda+1)-g(\lambda-1)].
$$
Then \(e_\lambda(\pm1)=e_\lambda\), \(b_\lambda(\pm1)=\pm b_\lambda\), and both variables vanish at displacement zero. If exactly \(r\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\) of the tuple's slots are nonzero, (1) becomes
$$
\Xi_{\lambda,r}=4g(\lambda)+\tfrac43e_\lambda r,
\qquad
A_{\lambda,r}=g(\lambda)^2+\tfrac r3(e_\lambda^2+b_\lambda^2).
\tag{4}
$$
There are \(\binom3r2^r\) such tuples. Thus (2) has exactly \(1+27+27+1=56\) terms before collecting equal energies, or the same formula with the indicated positive multiplicities after collecting by \((\lambda,r)\). The endpoint pairs are
$$
(\Xi_0,A_0)=(0,0),\qquad (\Xi_3,A_3)=(4C_4,C_4^2).
\tag{5}
$$
For the interior roots,
$$
e_1=B/2-1\ge0,\quad b_1=B/2,
\qquad
e_2=(1+C_4)/2-B\ge0,\quad b_2=(C_4-1)/2.
\tag{6}
$$
Within either root, both quantities in (4) are nondecreasing as \(r\) increases. If \(e_\lambda=0\), all its energies coincide; any differences between its moment contributions then have zero product with the energy difference. Such degeneracies therefore do not create a negative pair.

The ranges across roots are also ordered. At root \(1\), the smallest and largest energies are \(4\) and \(2B\), and
$$
1\le A_{1,r}\le1+e_1^2+b_1^2=B^2/2-B+2\le B^2.
\tag{7}
$$
The last inequality follows from \(B^2/2+B-2>0\) for \(B\ge2\). At root \(2\), the smallest and largest energies are \(4B\) and \(2(C_4+1)\), and
$$
B^2\le A_{2,r}\le B^2+e_2^2+b_2^2<C_4^2.
\tag{8}
$$
To verify the last inequality for every allowed fourth cost, the exact difference is
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_4^2-B^2-e_2^2-b_2^2
 &=\tfrac12C_4^2+BC_4-2B^2+B-\tfrac12\\
 &=\tfrac12[C_4-(2B-1)](C_4+4B-1)+2B(B-1)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$
Here the first product is nonnegative and \(2B(B-1)>0\). Finally \(2B<4B\) and \(2(C_4+1)<4C_4\), since \(B\ge2\) and \(C_4\ge3\). Equations (5), (7), and (8) therefore show that in the order root \(0\), root \(1\) with increasing \(r\), root \(2\) with increasing \(r\), root \(3\), both energies and moment contributions are nondecreasing. For any two raw tuples, or any two terms collected by \((\lambda,r)\), it follows that
$$
(A-A')(\Xi-\Xi')\ge0.
\tag{10}
$$
The pair consisting of root \(0\) and the root-\(1\), \(r=0\) tuple has \((A,\Xi)=(0,0)\) and \((A',\Xi')=(1,4)\), so its product in (10) is \(4>0\).

For a binary coordinate the only displacements are zero, so (2) has the two terms with pairs \((A,\Xi)=(0,0)\) and \((1,4)\). Thus (10) and the same strict pair hold there as well; in particular \(P(s)=1+e^{-4s}\), \(\mu(s)=e^{-4s}/(1+e^{-4s})\), and \(R(s)/s^2=1+\mu(s)\), exactly in the canonical normalization.

For either alphabet all tuple weights are strictly positive at every finite \(s\ge0\). Differentiating a finite exponential sum gives, for any tuple function independent of \(s\),
$$
\frac d{ds}\mathbb E_s A
 =-\mathbb E_s[A\Xi]+\mathbb E_sA\,\mathbb E_s\Xi
 =-\operatorname{Cov}_s(A,\Xi).
\tag{11}
$$
If the tuple probabilities are \(\omega_a\), the exact pair formula for this covariance is
$$
\operatorname{Cov}_s(A,\Xi)
 =\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}\omega_a\omega_b(A_a-A_b)(\Xi_a-\Xi_b)>0.
\tag{12}
$$
Every summand is nonnegative by (10), and the strict pair identified above has positive weights. For example that unordered pair alone contributes \(4\omega_0\omega_{1,0}>0\) in the four-site case. This proves from (3) and (11) that the extended \(R(s)/s^2\) has strictly negative derivative. All finite sums involved extend analytically through zero with a positive denominator, so the derivative statement includes its continuous value there.

Likewise, (2) and (3) give
$$
\tfrac14(\log P)'(s)=-\mu(s),
\qquad
\mu'(s)=-\tfrac14\operatorname{Var}_s(\Xi)<0.
\tag{13}
$$
The variance is strictly positive because root \(0\) and root \(1\) with all displacements zero have distinct energies \(0,4\) and positive probabilities. This argument includes both alphabets and every degeneracy in (6).

Restore all indices. If \(k\ge1\), the sum inside the square root defining \(L_j\) is at least \(k>0\). Its summands are exactly the extended \(R_i^{(j)}(s)/s^2\), each of which has negative derivative. Hence
$$
L_j'(s)=
\frac{20\sum_{i=1}^k\dfrac d{ds}\bigl(R_i^{(j)}(s)/s^2\bigr)}
 {\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^kR_i^{(j)}(s)/s^2}}<0,
\qquad
(\mu^{(j)})'(s)<0,
\tag{14}
$$
again using the continuous quotient at zero. In particular \(L_j\) is smooth on the finite rate interval, not merely continuous.

The finite maximum \(\mathcal F\) of affine functions is continuous and convex. The remaining, smooth part of \(\Psi_j\) has derivative \(-\mu^{(j)}-L_j\) by (13) and second derivative
$$
-(\mu^{(j)})'(s)-L_j'(s)>0.
\tag{15}
$$
It is therefore strictly convex on the entire interval. For completeness, a continuously differentiable function with strictly increasing derivative is strictly convex: on two consecutive nonempty subintervals, integration makes the average derivative on the left strictly smaller than that on the right, which is the strict secant-slope inequality. This applies here by (15). Adding the convex function \(\mathcal F\) preserves the strict inequality defining strict convexity. Thus \(\Psi_j\) is strictly convex even where \(\mathcal F\) has corners. Compactness gives a minimizer; strict convexity precludes two distinct minimizers.

We now justify the certificates without presupposing that local optimality is global. A finite maximum of affine functions has right derivative equal to the largest active slope and left derivative equal to the smallest active slope. Indeed, at a fixed rate every inactive line has a strictly positive gap below the maximum; because there are finitely many lines, sufficiently small perturbations discard all of them. For positive perturbations the active lines with largest slope give the maximum, and for negative perturbations those with smallest slope do so. Consequently the existing one-sided derivatives of the now-proved convex objective are
$$
\Psi'_{j,-}(a)=\min\mathcal J(a)-\mu^{(j)}(a)-L_j(a),
\qquad
\Psi'_{j,+}(a)=\max\mathcal J(a)-\mu^{(j)}(a)-L_j(a),
\tag{16}
$$
where only the inward derivative is used at an endpoint.

For a convex function, at an interior point the inequalities \(\Psi'_-\le0\le\Psi'_+\) are necessary and sufficient for a global minimum. To see sufficiency directly, every secant from that point to a point on its right has slope at least the right derivative and is therefore nonnegative; every secant from a point on its left to that point has slope at most the left derivative and is therefore nonpositive. Both comparisons give at least the value at the proposed minimum. Necessity follows by taking limits of these secants. At the left endpoint only the nonnegative right derivative is required, and at the right endpoint only the nonpositive left derivative is required, by the same secant argument. Substituting (16) proves all three displayed certificates. The minimum is unique by the strict convexity already established, so these are global uniqueness certificates at any prescribed rate, including a profile corner.

At zero the unique active height is \(0\): if \(t>0\), then \(f_t<f_0\), whereas the line for \(t=0\) has value zero. This remains true for \(T=0\), when there is only that line. Since \(L_j(0)\ge40\sqrt{k}>0\) and \(\mu^{(j)}(0)\ge0\), (16) gives
$$
\Psi'_{j,+}(0)=-\mu^{(j)}(0)-L_j(0)<0.
$$
The unique minimizing rate thus lies in \((0,s_{\max}]\). If \(T=0\), then \(\mathcal F=0\) everywhere and \(\Psi_j'=-\mu^{(j)}-L_j<0\), so that rate is \(s_{\max}\).

Finally, if \(k=0\), the empty sums give \(L_0=\mu^{(0)}=0\) and \(\Psi_0=\mathcal F\). The line for \(t=0\) shows \(\mathcal F\ge0\). For \(T\ge1\), all other lines are nonpositive exactly when
$$
s\le\frac{\log(f_0/f_t)}t\quad\text{for every }1\le t\le T,
$$
which is exactly \(s\le\theta_T\). The finite minimum defining \(\theta_T\) is strictly positive. Above it at least one line is positive. Intersecting with the rate domain proves the stated full minimizing interval. For \(T=0\), \(\mathcal F=0\) on the whole domain. This handles the only empty-coordinate exception and completes the proof.
