## Statement

Let $K,L\subset\mathbb R^m$ be convex bodies, $m\ge1$, and suppose $|L|\ge a|K|$ and $|K+L|\le D|K|$ for $a,D>0$. Write $\kappa_j$ for unit $j$-ball volume, $\kappa_0=1$, and put
$$
R=m(m+2),\quad H=\frac{D\kappa_m R^m}{\kappa_{m-1}},\quad
\rho=\frac{a\kappa_m}{(m+1)2^m H^{m-1}},\quad c=\frac{\rho}{R}.
$$
There are $p\in K$ and $q\in L$ such that $c(K-p)\subseteq L-q\subseteq H(K-p)$. Consequently $c(K-K)\subseteq L-L\subseteq H(K-K)$, and all directional widths satisfy the corresponding comparison. If pairs obeying these same hypotheses converge in Hausdorff distance to nonempty compact convex sets $K_*,L_*$ in a common bounded region, then $\operatorname{span}(K_*-K_*)=\operatorname{span}(L_*-L_*)$.

## Proof

Apply the ball sandwich of 77d36834ea2179e7 in dimension $m$ with base dimension $m$ and continuous dimension zero, using only its ordinary affine map. Write $K'=T(K-p)$ and $L'=T(L-p)$ for the two images under that same affine map. Since $B_2^m\subseteq K'\subseteq R B_2^m$, the center $p$ is in $K$, and volume covariance gives $|L'|\ge a\kappa_m$ and $|K'+L'|\le D\kappa_m R^m$. In the sum the common translation changes the image only by a translation and hence does not affect volume. If $[b_0,b_1]\subseteq L'$ is a longest segment, the capsule $B_2^m+[b_0,b_1]$ is inside $K'+L'$. Slicing along that segment gives the volume of the cylinder plus the two hemispheres, namely $\kappa_{m-1}|b_1-b_0|+\kappa_m$, also for $m=1$. Hence $\operatorname{diam} L'\le H$.

Let $\Delta$ be a maximum-volume inscribed simplex in $L'$. The simplex-to-cube portion of 03bc771bddd39a8a, applied in dimension $m$, gives $|\Delta|\ge |L'|/(2^m m!)\ge a\kappa_m/(2^m m!)$. Each facet area is at most $H^{m-1}/(m-1)!$ by Hadamard's determinant bound on its edge vectors, with counting area one when $m=1$. Each altitude is therefore at least $a\kappa_m/(2^m H^{m-1})$. The simplex centroid $q'$ has distance at least $\rho$ to every facet plane, since its distance is the corresponding altitude divided by $m+1$. Thus $\rho B_2^m\subseteq L'-q'\subseteq H B_2^m$, the upper bound following from $q'\in L'$ and the diameter bound. Combining the two sandwiches gives $(\rho/R)K'\subseteq L'-q'\subseteq H K'$. Pull back with $q=p+T^{-1}q'$.

Subtract the inclusions from themselves and take support functions to get the difference-body and width bounds. Under Hausdorff convergence, support functions converge uniformly on the unit sphere and so do widths. The fixed bounds imply that the limiting zero-width directions coincide. For any nonempty compact convex set these directions form the orthogonal complement of the span of its difference body, proving the direction-space identity also in dimension zero or in positive lower dimension.
