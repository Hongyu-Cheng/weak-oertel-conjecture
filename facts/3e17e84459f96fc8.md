## Statement

At bases $-1,0,1,2$ take the intervals $[-5/4,-21/20]$, $[-1,1]$, $[-3/4,3/4]$, $[-7/40,7/40]$, respectively, and linearly interpolate their endpoints to obtain a planar convex body. In the pure uniform outward smoothing with physical width $w=h\sqrt\Sigma$ satisfying $0<w<1/20$, the complete deepest set is $\{(0,-1/10),(1,0)\}$, both with raw depth $11/10+w/2$. Every active cap has zero outside strip coarea. Both parities are represented by the deepest bases, but every positive raw balanced cap menu at base zero uses a cap with outside mass zero and deficit $1/5$ relative to the pure-base outside reserve. The two orientation classes in such a menu each have total response weight $1/2$.

## Proof

Successive lower endpoint increments are $1/4,1/4,23/40$ and upper increments $41/20,-1/4,-23/40$. Their convex and concave interpolants define a convex body with the stated fibers, of lengths $1/5,2,3/2,7/20$. The pooled variance is positive. By normal scaling every nonvertical through-mark cap at $(p,y)$ has margin $a(z-p)\pm(x-y)$ for a real $a$. If the own threshold and its outward strip are in the own interval, the own mass is the ordinary cap length plus $w/2$ by integration of the uniform ramp. This applies to both signs at both displayed marks.

At $(0,-1/10)$ the positive orientation with $a=-9/10$ misses all outside fibers, since the maximal outside margins are at most $-1/20$. Its own mass is $11/10+w/2$, which is the lower bound for every cap of that sign. The negative orientation has own mass $9/10+w/2$. If $a\le19/20$, the minimum left-fiber margin is $19/20-a\ge0$ and the whole left fiber is captured. If $a>19/20$, the minimum margin at base one is $a-17/20>0$, so the whole length $3/2$ is captured. A sufficiently negative tilt captures only the left fiber outside, attaining the minimum outside mass $1/5$. Equality therefore forces saturated outside integrands for both signs.

At $(1,0)$ both signs have own mass $3/4+w/2$. For $a\ge7/40$ the entire right fiber is captured. For $a<7/40$ the ordinary mass at base zero alone is $\min(2,1-a)>33/40$, and outward smoothing can only increase it. A sufficiently positive tilt captures the right fiber and misses both left fibers, giving the exact minimum outside mass $7/20$. All equality tilts again have saturated outside integrands. Here and at base zero, any fiber with smoothed mass zero has integrand zero throughout by continuity, so its strip coarea vanishes.

The minimum pure-base cap masses at zero and one are $11/5$ and $37/20$, strictly above the common value $11/10+w/2$. At each middle mark freeze one minimizing tilt of each sign. Moving the continuous mark upwards strictly reduces the own positive-orientation mass from its proper value, and moving downwards strictly reduces the own negative-orientation mass. This follows from strict decrease for the initial threshold displacement and monotonicity for larger displacements. In each case all outside masses can only decrease. Thus these are the unique maximizers on their two fibers. At the exposed endpoint bases the pure-base cap isolating the respective whole fiber gives an upper bound of at most $7/20$, so the displayed deepest set is complete.

The least pure-base outside mass at zero is $\min(1/5,3/2+7/20)=1/5$. Every positive-orientation active cap there has outside mass zero. After covariance-unit normalization the two possible continuous normals are opposite, so a positive raw balanced menu assigns equal total raw weight to the two orientations. Its strip coarea is the same for every active cap at that mark: only the own interval contributes and each proper full strip has physical length $w$. The response weights are the raw weights divided by that same coarea and normalized. Each sign therefore has total response weight $1/2$, and in particular a positive orientation with deficit $1/5$ occurs.
