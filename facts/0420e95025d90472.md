## Statement

For a planar convex body $K$ containing $0$ in its interior and three nonzero spanning normals $v_1+v_2+v_3=0$, write
$$
k=|K|,\qquad Q_i=|K\cap\{v_i\cdot x\ge0\}|,\qquad
\sigma_i=\mathcal H^1(K\cap v_i^\perp)/\lVert v_i\rVert .
$$
Set $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$, $f_i=2\sqrt{kQ_i}/(\sigma_i M)$ and $\Phi_i=f_i+(f_j+f_l)^2/4$ where $\{i,j,l\}=\{1,2,3\}$. A triangle has strict distinct-vertex incidence if cap $i$ contains its vertex $P_i$ in its open halfplane and excludes both other vertices strictly.


Fix a strict distinct-vertex triangle $T$ containing $0$ in its interior, and keep the mark and three normals fixed. There is a Hausdorff neighborhood of $T$ in which every convex body $K$ contains the mark in its interior and satisfies $\Phi_i(K)\le2$ for all three rows. Every nontriangular $K$ there satisfies all three inequalities strictly.

For a row tight at $T$, apply the support-preserving six-sector replacement to $K$, and let $P$ be its polygon and $T_0$ the triangle of the three auxiliaries in the unique-cap sectors. For sufficiently close $K$, one has
$$
\Phi_i(P)\le\Phi_i(T_0)-c_i|P\setminus T_0|\le2-c_i|P\setminus T_0|
$$
with $c_i>0$ depending on the fixed configuration.

## Proof

**Continuity and replacement.** Choose $r>0$ with $r\mathbb B\subset T$, where $\mathbb B$ is the unit disk. For Hausdorff distance $\epsilon<r$, comparison of support functions gives $(1-\epsilon/r)T\subseteq K\subseteq(1+\epsilon/r)T$. This keeps the source interior and proves convergence of all six ray endpoints, six sector areas, caps and coareas by intersecting the inclusions with the same rays, sectors, halfplanes or cutting lines. Each limiting ray endpoint is in the relative interior of a triangle edge by strict incidence. Any supporting linear form at a moving ray endpoint normalized to take value one there has norm at most $2/r$ for small $\epsilon$. Every subsequential limit supports $T$ at the limiting endpoint and so equals its unique edge support. Thus all chosen forms converge regardless of their choice.

Apply fact 4b408a905c45b287 to the six cutting rays and those chosen supports. The rays have gaps strictly less than $\pi$ because the three distinct cutting lines pass through the source. Its constructed polygon $P$ has exactly the sector areas and ray endpoints of $K$ and retains the chosen supports. Therefore it has the same $k,Q_i,\sigma_i$. The construction in each sector with successive endpoints $A,B$ uses an auxiliary with positive coordinates $(u,v)$ relative to $A,B$, in the two supporting halfplanes, with $u+v=2|K\cap\operatorname{cone}(A,B)|/|\det(A,B)|$.

In a sector containing one old vertex, its limiting wedge in those coordinates is $u,v\ge0$, $u+p v\le1$, $q u+v\le1$ with $p,q<1$. The old vertex $W$ has both coordinates positive and makes the two inequalities equalities, giving $(1-pq)W_1=1-p>0$ and $(1-pq)W_2=1-q>0$. The wedge is exactly the triangle sector because its four vertices $0,A,B,W$ belong to $T$. The positive combination of its two inequalities with weights $W_2,W_1$ bounds $u+v$ by $W_1+W_2$, with equality only at $W$. Each auxiliary coordinate sum converges to that value by the area condition and the nonzero limiting endpoint determinant. The coordinates are bounded and every subsequential limit meets the wedge constraints. Hence the auxiliary converges to the old vertex. In a sector belonging to two caps the limiting sector is the triangle with endpoints $0,A,B$, so the coordinate sum tends to one and every limit auxiliary lies in the bridge segment $[A,B]$ in the interior of one old edge. These assertions use the opposite cap's strict negativity on each old edge and the balanced sum to identify the two-ray bridge.

**Ear geometry.** The three unique-sector auxiliaries are exposed vertices of $P$ for small $\epsilon$. A fixed linear form strictly exposing an old vertex has a positive gap there over the other vertices and the three compact bridge segments, and this gap persists. Their triangle $T_0$ converges to $T$ and is strict distinct-vertex with the same interior source. Each remaining construction point of $P$ is near a compact interior bridge of one edge of $T_0$. Its two barycentric coordinates belonging to that edge are bounded below by some fixed $c>0$ and its remaining coordinate is at least $-\delta$ with $\delta\to0$. No point of $P$ has two negative barycentric coordinates: if a convex combination had coordinates $i,j$ negative, the total weights $a_i,a_j$ on the two opposite edge groups would have to satisfy $ca_j<\delta a_i$ and $ca_i<\delta a_j$. All omitted contributions are nonnegative. These strict inequalities are impossible for $\delta<c$. The exterior of $T_0$ in $P$ thus splits into three ears with disjoint interiors. Clipping the hull outside one edge leaves the construction points beyond that edge and intersections with its base line. If the ear is nonempty, a point of its base-line intersection outside the base segment would have another negative barycentric coordinate. Mixing it with a sufficiently small positive weight on a point beyond the edge would give two negative coordinates, a contradiction. Thus those intersections lie in the base segment. The closed ear is therefore the hull of the base and those exterior points.

For an edge $[A,B]$ use coordinates $A+s(B-A)+h w$ with $w$ the outward quarter-turn of $B-A$ divided by $|B-A|^2$, giving absolute Jacobian one. The ear is $0\le s\le1$, $0\le h\le h_e(s)$ for a nonnegative concave polygonal roof with zero endpoints. Every nontrivial knot belongs to a fixed $[\kappa,1-\kappa]$ with $\kappa>0$ by the compact bridge locations. If $H_e=\max h_e$ and $E_e=\int_0^1h_e$, then $H_e/2\le E_e\le H_e$ by the tent below its maximum and $\operatorname{Lip}(h_e)\le H_e/\kappa$ by the end slopes. All heights tend uniformly to zero.

**Linear first variation and uniform clipping error.** Let $D$ be the seven raw quantities. At an edge each of its two incident cap margins has form $\Delta(s-s_0)+b h$, with $\Delta\ne0$ and $0<s_0<1$ uniformly away from the endpoints near $T$. The cap belonging to the opposite vertex remains strictly absent. Define $L_e(h_e)$ by adding the area integral to $k$, integrating the roof over the captured base subinterval for each $Q_j$, and using $h_e(s_0)/|\Delta|$ for each crossing coarea. The actual area differs from this ideal value by at most a constant times $H_e^2$ because the two indicators differ only within $|s-s_0|\le |b/\Delta|H_e$. The chord extension has height $h_*$ determined by
$$
h_*=h_e(s_0-bh_*/\Delta).
$$
The right side has Lipschitz constant at most $|b/\Delta|H_e/\kappa<1/2$ on $[0,H_e]$ for small ears. Its difference from $h_*$ has opposite weak signs at the endpoints and is strictly decreasing. Its unique zero differs from $h_e(s_0)$ by $O(H_e^2)$. The coarea increment is $h_*/|\Delta|$, since the change from $(s,h)$ to $(\Delta(s-s_0)+bh,h)$ has Jacobian magnitude $|\Delta|$ in the unit-Jacobian edge coordinates. Extensions from different ears have disjoint relative interiors and add. This proof includes crossings at roof knots. Put $E=\sum_e E_e$ and $\delta_0=\max_e H_e$. Since $H_e^2\le2\delta_0 E_e$ and $L_e$ has size $O(E_e)$, one obtains, uniformly,
$$
D(P)-D(T_0)=\sum_e L_e(h_e)+O(\delta_0 E),\qquad
\lVert D(P)-D(T_0)\rVert=O(E).
$$

For a unit-height tent $T_t(s)=\min(s/t,(1-s)/(1-t))$ with $0<t<1$, the functional $\mathcal L_e=\nabla\Phi_i(D(T))\cdot L_e$ gives the actual derivative of an outward ear at $T$ with peak $t$ and unit area Jacobian. The clipping estimates above apply to the roof $\varepsilon T_t$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. In any other transverse edge direction the area Jacobian just multiplies the same area, cap and coarea increments by a positive factor. This follows by using that transverse direction in the identical clipping calculation, with its determinant multiplying the integrals and $h_*/|\Delta|$.

Fix a tight row of $T$ and relabel it as row $1$. First use the accepted signed-row inequality a19fb2ae234afb2a for all nearby strict triangles. Its $f_i$ equals the actual row variable by the corner formula $Q_i/\sigma_i$ equal to half the positive vertex height. The source-centered linear map with the first two normals as its rows is invertible and makes the three normals canonical. This preserves the row values because $k,Q_i$ and $\sigma_i$ all scale by the absolute determinant, the last from the coarea change of variables with the normal threshold unchanged. The canonical tight-triangle parametrization therefore applies, with a possible exchange of caps $2,3$. The opposite-edge and adjacent-edge first-variation lemmas then give $\mathcal L_e(T_t)<0$ on the closed double-signature subinterval of each edge. Those deformations have fixed base points and positive outward area Jacobians. This sign is independent of the transverse direction by the preceding calculation.

For the unique-signature parts, first note that $\mathcal L_e(1-s)=\mathcal L_e(s)=0$. To see the first, move $A$ a small amount in the direction $A-C$ along the other incident edge, where $C$ is the opposite vertex. In coordinates with transverse vector $A-C$, the edge line has profile $\varepsilon(1-s)$ and the other two edge lines stay fixed. This is a smooth two-sided variation of strict triangles through a tight maximum, so its row derivative vanishes. The clipping formulas for the signed affine strip give precisely the affine profile functional times its nonzero Jacobian. Indeed the cut crossings stay in the interiors, and the first area and coarea increments follow from the base-step and height estimates for that signed strip. Moving $B$ along $B-C$ similarly gives the second identity. Let $0<\theta<\psi<1$ be the two cut crossings along the edge, and let $j$ denote the cap of $A$. For $0<t\le\theta$, the profile $T_t-(1-s)/(1-t)$ vanishes off $[0,t]$ and at both cut crossings. Its integral is $-t/[2(1-t)]$, and only cap $j$ shares this area increment with $k$. Thus
$$
\mathcal L_e(T_t)=-\alpha_{ij}(T)\frac{t}{2(1-t)}<0
$$
by the positive fixed-section loss coefficient. Reflection gives $-\alpha_{il}(T)(1-t)/(2t)<0$ for $\psi\le t<1$, where cap $l$ belongs to $B$. Both formulas apply at their crossing endpoints as the difference vanishes at the crossing. This proves the sign for every fixed interior peak, with both seams included.

**Finite margin and strictness.** The functional on a unit tent depends continuously on the triangle and $t$ on $[\kappa,1-\kappa]$, directly from the linear integrals and point evaluations with nonzero crossing slopes. Its negative therefore has a positive minimum across the three edges at $T$ on this compact peak interval. For nearby $T_0$ its values are uniformly at most $-c_i$ for some $c_i>0$. At each knot $t_r$ of a roof use coefficient $a_r=t_r(1-t_r)$ times its downward slope jump. The positive sum $\sum_r a_r T_{t_r}$ has the same slope jumps and zero endpoints as the roof, hence equals it, with $\sum_r a_r=2E_e$ by integration. Taylor's formula for the smooth raw-data functional near positive data gives
$$
\Phi_i(P)-\Phi_i(T_0)
=\sum_e \nabla\Phi_i(D(T_0))\cdot L_e(h_e)+O(\delta_0 E)
\le -2c_i E+O(\delta_0 E),
$$
using $E^2\le3\delta_0 E$. Shrinking the neighborhood gives the stated margin, since $\Phi_i(T_0)\le2$ by the strict triangle theorem. If $E=0$, the replacement is $T_0$. Its retained ray endpoints lie in its edge interiors and each retained support must then equal that triangle edge line. The same supports bound $K$ inside $T_0$, and their areas agree. Strict separation from any omitted point of a full-dimensional convex body would give a missing cap of positive area, so $K=T_0$. Thus all tight rows are strict for nontriangles. The rows originally strict remain strict by raw-data continuity and there are only three rows.
