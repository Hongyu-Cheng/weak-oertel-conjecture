## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, \(a_i>0\), \(R\ge0\), and \(\theta_i\le0\) for \(1\le i\le n\). Define the complete original support and its original probabilities by
\[
D=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot z\le R\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{z\in D}e^{\theta\cdot z},\qquad
p_z=e^{\theta\cdot z}/Z.
\]
For \(j\in D\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of \(p(H\cap D)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(j\). Use the canonical oriented reflected count and original reflected Hellinger mass
\(k_j=\#(D\cap(2j-D))\) and \(\mathsf H_j=k_jp_j\), including zero displacement. Define \(M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p_j\tau_p(j)^3\).

The following constants are absolute, with natural logarithms:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=(8/27)^{3/2},&\gamma&=2048,&
b&=20+\log(48\gamma^4),\\
c&=e^{-(20+b)}/128,&B&=\gamma b+144,&
\delta&=\log(8\alpha)>0,\\
N&=\left\lceil\max\{1,(B/\delta)^2\}\right\rceil,&
\kappa_0&=\min\{c,1/(64\cdot8^N)\}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then, for every stated input,
\[
M_3(p)\ge c\,e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n,
\qquad M_3(p)\ge\kappa_0\,8^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
The support, weights, and normalizer \(Z\) in these inequalities are never replaced by a selected or conditioned law. The auxiliary resource model does not change any hypotheses of the locked mixed-integer problem. The present result is its registered original atomic depth-moment input, not a final theorem for the locked problem.

The only accepted input used is the geometric coordinate inequality in accepted fact `609407d6db81b18b`: with its generating function aliased here as \(F_2(q)=(1+6q+q^2)/(1-q)^3\), one has \((1-e^{-s})^3F_2(e^{-3s})\ge8/27\) for every \(s>0\). All third-power coordinate estimates, resource selection, and depth comparisons below are proved directly.

## Proof

The set \(D\) is finite and contains zero. If \(R=0\), positivity of the resource coefficients gives \(D=\{0\}\) and \(M_3(p)=1\). Both assertions of (1) hold because \(c<1\), \(0<\alpha<1\), and \(\kappa_0<1\). Assume \(R>0\) until the small-dimension argument at the end.

1. Third-power coordinate law and its exact estimates.

For \(0<q<1\), the generating function is
\[
F_3(q)=\sum_{u\ge0}(2u+1)^3q^u
=\frac{1+23q+23q^2+q^3}{(1-q)^4}.
\tag{2}
\]
This follows by expanding the numerator times \((1-q)^{-4}\), or by differentiating the geometric series three times. For \(s>0\), define the new auxiliary coordinate law
\(Q_s(u)=(2u+1)^3e^{-4su}/F_3(e^{-4s})\), \(u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), and put \(\mu(s)=\mathbb E_{Q_s}u\). This \(Q_s\) is a third-power coordinate law, not the second-power coordinate law of the accepted predecessor.

Write \(q=e^{-4s}\). Under \(Q_s\), the index \(u\) has the law of \(K+G\), where \(K\) and \(G\) are independent, \(K\) has probabilities proportional to \((1,23q,23q^2,q^3)\) on \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), and \(G\) is the sum of four independent geometric variables with probabilities \((1-q)q^v\), \(v\ge0\). Their probability generating functions multiply to \(F_3(qt)/F_3(q)\), proving this exact representation. It gives
\[
\mathbb E K^2\le124q,\qquad
\mathbb E G^2=\frac{4q+16q^2}{(1-q)^2}.
\]
For \(t=4s>0\), one has \(t^2e^{-t}\le4/e^2<1\) and
\(q/(1-q)^2=[2\sinh(t/2)]^{-2}\le t^{-2}\). The latter inequality follows from \(2\sinh(t/2)\ge t\). Thus
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_s}u^2
\le2\mathbb E K^2+2\mathbb E G^2
\le\frac{248+40}{t^2}=\frac{18}{s^2},
\qquad \mu(s)\le\frac5s.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(h=\log3\) and \(0<s\le h\), convexity of the exponential gives
\(e^{4s}-1\le(s/h)(e^{4h}-1)=80s/h\). Since \(h>1\), the geometric part of the representation yields
\[
\mu(s)\ge\frac4{e^{4s}-1}\ge\frac h{20s}\ge\frac1{20s}
\quad(0<s\le h).
\tag{4}
\]
Differentiating the positive exponential series gives \(\mu'(s)=-4\operatorname{Var}_{Q_s}(u)\le0\).

The coordinate factor for a third moment satisfies
\[
(1-e^{-s})^4F_3(e^{-4s})\ge\alpha\quad(s>0).
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, under the ordinary geometric law \(P_s(u)=(1-e^{-s})e^{-su}\), the random variable \((2u+1)P_s(u)\) has second moment \((1-e^{-s})^3F_2(e^{-3s})\) and third moment \((1-e^{-s})^4F_3(e^{-4s})\). The monotonicity of probability-space \(L^r\) norms bounds its third moment below by the three-halves power of its second moment. The displayed coordinate inequality from accepted fact `609407d6db81b18b` proves (5).

2. Truncated resource dual and a small coordinate selection.

Put \(\psi(s)=\tfrac14\log F_3(e^{-4s})\), \(\phi(s)=\max\{0,\psi(s)-\psi(h)\}\) for \(s>0\), and \(\phi(0)=+\infty\). The identities \(\psi'=-\mu\) and \(\psi''=4\operatorname{Var}_{Q_s}(u)\ge0\) show that \(\phi\) is convex, vanishes for \(s\ge h\), and diverges at zero. Minimize
\[
\lambda R+\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(\lambda a_i-\theta_i)
\quad\text{over }\lambda\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
A positive \(\lambda\) gives a finite value. Finite sublevels are bounded because \(R>0\) and every \(\phi\ge0\). A minimizing subsequence therefore converges; divergence at zero excludes zero coordinate arguments at its limit, and continuity at positive arguments proves attainment. Fix a minimizer, and put
\(t_i=\lambda a_i\), \(s_i=t_i-\theta_i>0\), \(\mu_i=\mu(s_i)\), and \(\Lambda=\lambda R\). Since \(\theta_i\le0\), one has \(0\le t_i\le s_i\).

There is a vector \(x\) such that \(x_i=\mu_i\) when \(s_i<h\), \(x_i=0\) when \(s_i>h\), and \(0\le x_i\le\mu_i\) when \(s_i=h\), with
\[
a\cdot x\le R,\qquad \lambda(R-a\cdot x)=0,
\qquad \#\{i:0<x_i<\mu_i\}\le1.
\tag{7}
\]
To check every boundary case, if \(\lambda>0\), the left and right derivatives in (6) imply
\(\sum_{s_i<h}a_i\mu_i\le R\le\sum_{s_i\le h}a_i\mu_i\). Fill the tied coordinates successively to obtain equality, with at most one partially filled coordinate. If \(\lambda=0\), the right derivative is nonnegative, so \(\sum_{s_i<h}a_i\mu_i\le R\); set every tied coordinate to zero. No partially filled coordinate is needed. This also verifies complementary slackness and handles all ties.

Let \(I_0=\{i:x_i=\mu_i\}\). Then \(s_i\le h\) on \(I_0\), and \(s_i\ge h\) off \(I_0\). Moreover \(\Lambda=\sum_i t_ix_i\), the sum \(\sum_{i\in I_0}a_i\mu_i\) is at most \(R\), and the possible partially filled coordinate contributes at most 5 to \(\sum_i t_ix_i\), by (3) and \(t_i\le s_i\).

If \(I_0\ne\varnothing\), choose the \(m=\min\{|I_0|,\lceil\gamma\sqrt n\rceil\}\) largest values of \(a_i\mu_i\) in \(I_0\), and call their index set \(I\). Put \(U=\sum_{i\in I}a_i\mu_i>0\). If \(m<|I_0|\), every remaining value is at most \(U/m\), and therefore
\[
|I|\le\gamma\sqrt n+1,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_0\setminus I}(a_i\mu_i)^2\le U^2/\gamma^2.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(m=|I_0|\), the tail sum is zero, proving the same assertion. If \(I_0=\varnothing\), set \(I=\varnothing\) and \(U=0\).

3. An auxiliary set of roots with resource and score margins.

For \(i\in I_0\), set \(s_i'=\gamma s_i\) on \(I\) and \(s_i'=s_i\) elsewhere. Under a product law \(Q\), let \(J_i\) have law \(Q_{s_i'}\) for \(i\in I_0\), and set \(J_i=0\) deterministically off \(I_0\). Monotonicity and (3)-(4) give
\[
\mathbb E_QJ_i\le\mu_i\ (i\in I_0),\qquad
\mathbb E_QJ_i\le\frac{100}{\gamma}\mu_i\le\frac14\mu_i\ (i\in I),
\qquad \mathbb E_QJ_i^2\le18/(s_i')^2.
\tag{9}
\]
For a nonnegative integer root \(j\), define the two displacement variances
\[
\sigma_a(j)^2=\sum_i a_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3,
\qquad
\sigma_\theta(j)^2=\sum_i\theta_i^2j_i(j_i+1)/3.
\tag{10}
\]
These are the exact variances of \(a\cdot V\) and \(\theta\cdot V\) for independent uniform integers \(V_i\in\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\).

When \(I_0\ne\varnothing\), (7) and (9) yield
\(\mathbb E_Q(a\cdot J)\le R-3U/4\). Using \(s_i\le h\), (4), and the integer inequality \(J_i(J_i+1)\le2J_i^2\), both \(\operatorname{Var}_Q(a\cdot J)\) and \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_a(J)^2\) are at most
\[
7200\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{(a_i\mu_i)^2}{(s_i'/s_i)^2}
\le14400U^2/\gamma^2\le U^2/16^2.
\tag{11}
\]
The selected-coordinate square sum is at most \(U^2/\gamma^2\), and the unselected sum is bounded by (8). The last inequality is the exact comparison \(14400\cdot16^2=3686400\le4194304=2048^2\).

Chebyshev for \(a\cdot J\) and Markov for \(\sigma_a(J)^2\) show that, with probability at least \(7/8\),
\(a\cdot J\le\mathbb E_Q(a\cdot J)+4\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q(a\cdot J)}\) and \(\sigma_a(J)\le4\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q\sigma_a(J)^2}\). On this event,
\(a\cdot J+2\sigma_a(J)\le R-3U/4+4U/16+8U/16\le R\). A zero variance or zero second moment makes its condition automatic. If \(I_0=\varnothing\), \(J=0\) and the same resource condition holds surely.

For every active coordinate, \(|\theta_i|\le s_i\le s_i'\). Therefore (9)-(10) give \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_\theta(J)^2\le12n\le18n\). Markov's inequality implies \(\sigma_\theta(J)\le4\sqrt{18n}\) with probability at least \(15/16\). Consequently the set
\[
G=\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:a\cdot j+2\sigma_a(j)\le R,
\ \sigma_\theta(j)\le4\sqrt{18n}\}
\]
has \(Q(G)\ge13/16\ge1/2\) and is contained in the original \(D\).

The relevant dual score is \(T=t\cdot J\), not \(s\cdot J\). From (7)-(9),
\[
0\le\Lambda-\mathbb E_QT\le5(1+|I|).
\tag{12}
\]
Only the at most one partially filled coordinate and the inflated coordinates contribute to this difference; each contributes at most \(t_i\mu_i\le5\). This also holds for \(\lambda=0\), when \(T=\Lambda=0\).

For \(u\ge0\), let \(\ell(u)=\log\mathbb E_Qe^{-u(T-\mathbb E_QT)}\). The tilted coordinate rates are \(s_i'+ut_i/4>0\). By (3), independence, and \(t_i\le s_i\le s_i'\), one has \(\ell''(u)\le18n\). All these derivatives exist at the positive rates. Since \(\ell(0)=\ell'(0)=0\), integration gives \(\ell(u)\le9nu^2\). Restriction to \(G\) and conditional Jensen give
\(\ell(u)\ge\log Q(G)+u(\mathbb E_QT-\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G])\). Minimize \((-\log Q(G))/u+9nu\) over \(u>0\), using \(-\log Q(G)\le\log2<1\); when \(Q(G)=1\), take \(u\downarrow0\). Together with (12), this proves
\[
\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G]\ge\Lambda-5(1+|I|)-6\sqrt n.
\tag{13}
\]

4. The score slab gives original halfspace mass.

Fix \(j\in G\), and take independent uniform displacements as in (10). Each of the two conditions \(|a\cdot V|\le2\sigma_a(j)\) and \(|\theta\cdot V|\le2\sigma_\theta(j)\) fails with probability at most \(1/4\), with a zero variance handled identically. Their intersection thus contains at least half of the \(\prod_i(2j_i+1)\) displacements. It is symmetric under \(v\mapsto-v\). Every displacement in it satisfies \(j\pm v\in D\), because the coordinates are nonnegative and the resource margin defining \(G\) applies.

For any closed halfspace containing \(j\), at least one of \(j+v,j-v\) belongs to it. Symmetry therefore puts at least half of these allowed endpoints in that halfspace. Each such endpoint has its original probability at least
\(p_j e^{-2\sigma_\theta(j)}\ge p_j e^{-8\sqrt{18n}}\ge p_j e^{-40\sqrt n}\), where \(64\cdot18<40^2\). Taking the infimum over all containing halfspaces proves
\[
\tau_p(j)\ge\frac14e^{-40\sqrt n}p_j\prod_i(2j_i+1)
\quad(j\in G).
\tag{14}
\]
This is a score-restricted box comparison, and does not assert \(\tau_p(j)\ge\mathsf H_j/2\).

5. Original fourth-power normalization and the exponential surplus.

For \(z\in D\), \(t\cdot z\le\Lambda\), and \(\theta_i=t_i-s_i\). Thus the actual normalizer satisfies
\[
Z\le e^\Lambda\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}.
\tag{15}
\]
Put \(Z_Q=\prod_{i\in I_0}F_3(e^{-4s_i'})\), with the empty product equal to one. On the support of \(Q\), every other coordinate is zero, so its exact product normalization gives
\[
\prod_i(2j_i+1)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}
=Z_QQ(J=j)e^{4\sum_{i\in I_0}(s_i'+\theta_i)j_i}
\ge Z_QQ(J=j)e^{4t\cdot j}.
\tag{16}
\]
Cube (14), multiply by the original \(p_j\), and sum over \(G\). When using (16), discard any nonnegative summands outside the support of \(Q\). Equations (13), (15)-(16), and conditional Jensen yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)
&\ge\frac{e^{-120\sqrt n}}{64Z^4}
\sum_{j\in G}\prod_i(2j_i+1)^3e^{4\theta\cdot j}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-120\sqrt n}}{64}
Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4 Q(G)
e^{4(\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G]-\Lambda)}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-20}}{128}
e^{-20|I|-144\sqrt n}Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The denominator here is the original \(Z^4\) throughout. The conditional exponential expectation is finite because \(G\subseteq D\) is finite.

An uninflated coordinate in \(I_0\) contributes at least \(\alpha\) to the last product, by (5). A deterministic coordinate contributes \((1-e^{-s_i})^4\ge(1-e^{-h})^4=16/81\ge\alpha\); the last comparison follows on squaring from \(768\ge512\). For an inflated coordinate,
\[
\frac{F_3(e^{-4\gamma s_i})}{F_3(e^{-4s_i})}
\ge\frac1{48\gamma^4}.
\tag{18}
\]
The numerator polynomial in (2) lies between 1 and 48 on \([0,1]\), and \(1-q^\gamma\le\gamma(1-q)\) for the positive integer \(\gamma\), by the geometric-sum identity. These two observations prove (18). Applying the factors to (17), followed by (8), gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{e^{-20}}{128}
e^{-b|I|-144\sqrt n}\alpha^n
\ge c\,e^{-B\sqrt n}\alpha^n.
\tag{19}
\]
All empty-active-set cases are included, since their auxiliary law is \(J=0\) and every product identity above remains exact.

Finally \(\alpha>1/8\), because \(\alpha^2=512/19683>1/64\) is equivalent to \(32768>19683\). Thus \(\delta>0\), and for \(n\ge N\) one has \(B\sqrt n\le\delta n\). Equation (19) proves \(M_3(p)\ge c8^{-n}\) in that range.

6. A universal finite-dimension estimate.

We prove the remaining estimate directly, without a compactness assumption. For a fixed root \(j\), write \(E_j=D\cap(2j-D)\). For every \(x\in E_j\), log-affinity gives \(\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=p_j\). If a closed halfspace \(H\) contains \(j\), at least one point of each reflected pair is in \(H\). Writing \(A=E_j\cap H\), reflection and Cauchy-Schwarz therefore give
\[
\mathsf H_j=\sum_{x\in E_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}
\le2\sum_{x\in A}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}
\le2\sqrt{p(A)p(2j-A)}\le2\sqrt{p(H\cap D)}.
\]
Taking the infimum yields \(\tau_p(j)\ge\mathsf H_j^2/4\).

Partition \(D\) by its original coordinatewise parity, and denote the masses by \(\pi_r\), \(r\in\{0,1\}^n\). Whenever \(x,y\in D\) have the same parity, their integer midpoint \(j=(x+y)/2\) belongs to \(D\), by nonnegativity and the weak resource inequality. Conversely every oriented reflected pair is such a pair. Since \(p_j^2=p_xp_y\), this proves the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j=\sum_r\pi_r^2\ge2^{-n},
\qquad \mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^2\ge4^{-n}.
\]
Probability-space Jensen now gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^6
\ge\frac1{64}(\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^2)^3
\ge\frac1{64}64^{-n}.
\tag{20}
\]
For \(1\le n<N\), the ratio of this last bound to \(8^{-n}\) is \(8^{-n}/64\ge1/(64\cdot8^N)\ge\kappa_0\). For \(n\ge N\), (19) gives the same conclusion since \(\kappa_0\le c\). The zero-capacity case was already checked. This proves both assertions of (1), including zero scores, tied rates, inactive or zero dual multiplier, and deterministic auxiliary coordinates, always with the original probabilities.
