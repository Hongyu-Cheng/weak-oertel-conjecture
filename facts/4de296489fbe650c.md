## Statement

Retain the common actual canonical hard \(n=3\), affine-target-rank-two instance and notation of accepted facts 2992eea9e109d58a and 2b09f2b516439517. Thus
\[
 p:=|q^*|=\sum_{t\in T_*}x_t>\frac{553}{51200}Q,
 \qquad
 A:=\operatorname{aff}(T_*),
 \qquad
 \dim A=2,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
 G<\frac Q{100},
 \qquad
 x_t<\frac{64}{553}p\quad(t\in T_*),
 \qquad
 |T_*|\geq9,
\tag{2}
\]
and, with the common full-dimensional compact origin-symmetric body \(D_0\subseteq\mathbb R^d\),
\[
 L_t:=q_t+D_0\subseteq\mathsf M_{y}(C_t)
 \qquad(0<y\leq x_t,\ t\in T_*).
\tag{3}
\]
For an affine map \(a:A\to\mathbb R^d\), put
\[
 H_x(a):=\sum_{\substack{t\in T_*\\a(t)\in L_t}}x_t,
 \qquad
 \mathsf H_x:=\max_{a:A\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}H_x(a),
 \qquad
 Y_0:=\frac{256}{553}p.
\tag{4}
\]
The maximum in (4) is attained. If
\[
 \mathsf H_x\geq Y_0,
\tag{5}
\]
then there are a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq T_*\), masses \(0<y_t\leq x_t\), and an integral relative \(1/4\)-center
\[
 m\in A\cap\mathbb Z^3\cap\operatorname{conv}E
\tag{6}
\]
such that
\[
 \sum_{t\in E}y_t=Y_0,
 \qquad
 \rho_{D_0}(E,m)\leq1.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently condition 2 of accepted fact 2992eea9e109d58a applies and gives
\[
 G\geq\frac{512}{553}p>\frac Q{100}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, the strict hard branch (2) forces
\[
 \boxed{\mathsf H_x<\frac{256}{553}p.}
\tag{9}
\]

The failure (9) has the following exact selected-load dual form. Write \(h_{D_0}\) for the support function, equivalently the dual norm of the Minkowski norm of \(D_0\), and define
\[
 \Delta_x:=\min_{a:A\to\mathbb R^d\ {\rm affine}}
 \sum_{t\in T_*}x_t\operatorname{dist}_{D_0}(a(t),L_t).
\tag{10}
\]
Then the minimum is attained and
\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_x=
 \max\left\{
 -\sum_{t\in T_*}h_{L_t}(\eta_t):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \eta_t\in(\mathbb R^d)^*\quad(t\in T_*),\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{t\in T_*}\eta_t=0,\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{t\in T_*}t\otimes\eta_t=0,\\
 h_{D_0}(\eta_t)\leq x_t\quad(t\in T_*)
 \end{array}
 \right\}.}
\tag{11}
\]
The maximum is attained. Under (9), one has \(\Delta_x>0\). Fix a primal optimizer \(a_*\) in (10) and a dual optimizer \(\eta^x=(\eta_t^x)_{t\in T_*}\) in (11), and put
\[
 \omega_t^x:=h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x),
 \qquad
 \Omega_x:=\sum_{t\in T_*}\omega_t^x,
 \qquad
 J_x:=\{t\in T_*:\omega_t^x>0\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Then
\[
 0\leq\omega_t^x\leq x_t,
 \qquad
 \omega_t^x=x_t\quad\text{whenever }a_*(t)\notin L_t,
\tag{13}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \Omega_x\geq p-H_x(a_*)\geq p-\mathsf H_x
 >\frac{297}{553}p.}
\tag{14}
\]
There are support contacts \(h_t^x\in D_0\) and actual target points
\[
 u_t^x:=q_t+h_t^x\in L_t\subseteq\mathsf M_{\omega_t^x}(C_t)
 \qquad(t\in J_x)
\tag{15}
\]
such that
\[
 \eta_t^x(h_t^x)=h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x)=\omega_t^x,
 \qquad
 \eta_t^x(u_t^x)=h_{L_t}(\eta_t^x),
\tag{16}
\]
while the exact target force and target-base moment equations are
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{t\in J_x}\eta_t^x=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t\in J_x}t\otimes\eta_t^x=0.}
\tag{17}
\]
Moreover the diffuse cap in (2) and the actual off-diagonal selected-edge structure retained in accepted facts 2c7ef442c71a1564 and 4aa2d3090ea613b8 imply
\[
 \boxed{|J_x|\geq5,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(J_x)=2.}
\tag{18}
\]
No assertion is made that (14)-(18) alone already imply (5). They identify the remaining minimal diffuse-tube theorem: absorb one actual selected-tube balanced contact stress of load \(>297p/553\) and full planar base rank into either an affine-hit subfamily of load at least \(256p/553\) or a direct \(Q/100\) terminal.

## Proof

We first prove the affine-hit closure. Since \(T_*\) is finite, the collection of subsets \(U\subseteq T_*\) for which there is an affine map \(a:A\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfying \(a(t)\in L_t\) for all \(t\in U\) is finite and nonempty. Maximizing \(\sum_{t\in U}x_t\) over this collection and choosing a witnessing affine map proves attainment in (4).

Assume (5), and fix a witnessing hit set \(U\subseteq T_*\) and affine map \(a\). List the points of \(U\), retain their full capacities successively, and truncate the last positive capacity when the accumulated mass first reaches \(Y_0\). Omitting unused points gives a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq U\) and masses
\[
 0<y_t\leq x_t\quad(t\in E),
 \qquad
 \sum_{t\in E}y_t=Y_0.
\tag{19}
\]
The planar lattice-center assertion in accepted fact 2992eea9e109d58a supplies an integral relative \(1/4\)-center \(m\) for this nonzero profile, proving (6).

For each \(t\in E\), write
\[
 a(t)=q_t+d_t,
 \qquad
 d_t\in D_0.
\tag{20}
\]
If \(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E)\), affineness of \(a\), (20), and convexity of \(D_0\) give
\[
 a(m)-\sum_{t\in E}\lambda_tq_t
 =\sum_{t\in E}\lambda_td_t\in D_0.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the one point \(a(m)\) lies within \(D_0\)-distance one of every barycentric target-center sum at \(m\). By the definition in accepted fact 2992eea9e109d58a, (21) is exactly \(\rho_{D_0}(E,m)\leq1\), proving (7). That accepted fact now gives (8). This contradicts (2), so (9) follows.

We next prove the exact dual statement. Let
\[
 \mathcal A:=\{(a(t))_{t\in T_*}:a:A\to\mathbb R^d\text{ affine}\}
 \subseteq(\mathbb R^d)^{T_*},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L:=\prod_{t\in T_*}L_t.
\tag{22}
\]
The evaluation set \(\mathcal A\) is a linear subspace and \(\mathcal L\) is compact and convex. On \((\mathbb R^d)^{T_*}\) put
\[
 \mathcal N_x((v_t)_t):=\sum_{t\in T_*}x_t\|v_t\|_{D_0}.
\tag{23}
\]
Then (10) is the distance
\[
 \Delta_x=\min_{y\in\mathcal A,\ \ell\in\mathcal L}
 \mathcal N_x(y-\ell).
\tag{24}
\]
Distance from the compact set \(\mathcal L\) to the closed finite-dimensional subspace \(\mathcal A\) is attained, so the minimum exists.

The dual norm of (23) is
\[
 \mathcal N_x^*((\eta_t)_t)
 =\max_{t\in T_*}\frac{h_{D_0}(\eta_t)}{x_t}.
\tag{25}
\]
The annihilator \(\mathcal A^\perp\) consists exactly of the covector families satisfying the two moment equations in (11): testing constant affine maps gives \(\sum_t\eta_t=0\), and testing all linear parts gives \(\sum_tt\otimes\eta_t=0\); the converse is direct substitution. Separating \(\mathcal A-\mathcal L\) from the open \(\mathcal N_x\)-ball of radius \(\Delta_x\), exactly as for distance from a compact convex set to a subspace, gives
\[
 \Delta_x
 =\max_{\substack{\eta\in\mathcal A^\perp\\\mathcal N_x^*(\eta)\leq1}}
 \left(-h_{\mathcal L}(\eta)\right).
\tag{26}
\]
Here \(h_{\mathcal L}(\eta)=\sum_th_{L_t}(\eta_t)\), and (25) turns dual feasibility into \(h_{D_0}(\eta_t)\leq x_t\). This proves (11). The feasible dual set is compact because \(D_0\) contains a neighborhood of the origin, so its maximum is attained.

If \(\Delta_x=0\), equation (24) gives an affine map hitting every \(L_t\), and then \(\mathsf H_x=p>Y_0\), contrary to (9). Thus \(\Delta_x>0\).

Fix primal and dual optimizers \(a_*,\eta^x\). For each target choose a nearest point \(\ell_t^*\in L_t\) to \(a_*(t)\). Dual feasibility and the polar inequality give, target by target,
\[
 \eta_t^x(a_*(t))-h_{L_t}(\eta_t^x)
 \leq
 h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x)
 \operatorname{dist}_{D_0}(a_*(t),L_t)
 \leq
 x_t\operatorname{dist}_{D_0}(a_*(t),L_t).
\tag{27}
\]
Because \(\eta^x\in\mathcal A^\perp\), one has
\[
 \sum_t\eta_t^x(a_*(t))=0.
\tag{28}
\]
Summing (27), using (28), and comparing the equal primal and dual optimum values shows that equality holds in (27) for every \(t\). If \(a_*(t)\notin L_t\), its distance is positive, so equality in the second inequality of (27) forces
\[
 h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x)=x_t.
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (13). The missed set of \(a_*\) has \(x\)-mass \(p-H_x(a_*)\), and all its coordinates are saturated by (29). Summing gives (14), where the final strict inequality is (9).

For \(t\in J_x\), compactness of \(D_0\) supplies \(h_t^x\in D_0\) with
\[
 \eta_t^x(h_t^x)=h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x)=\omega_t^x.
\tag{30}
\]
The support function of a translate satisfies
\[
 h_{L_t}(\eta_t^x)
 =\eta_t^x(q_t)+h_{D_0}(\eta_t^x)
 =\eta_t^x(q_t+h_t^x),
\tag{31}
\]
which proves the support assertions in (15)-(16). Since \(0<\omega_t^x\leq x_t\), the actual nested tube inclusion (3), with \(y=\omega_t^x\), proves \(u_t^x\in\mathsf M_{\omega_t^x}(C_t)\). The two equations in (17) are precisely dual membership in \(\mathcal A^\perp\), with zero-load terms deleted.

It remains to prove (18). Equations (2), (12), and (14) give
\[
 \frac{297}{553}p
 <\Omega_x
 =\sum_{t\in J_x}\omega_t^x
 \leq\sum_{t\in J_x}x_t
 <|J_x|\frac{64}{553}p.
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(p>0\), this yields \(|J_x|>297/64>4\), hence \(|J_x|\geq5\).

For the affine-rank assertion, use the actual selected edges of \(q^*\) retained in accepted fact 2c7ef442c71a1564. For every edge \(e\) with physical target \(m_e=t\), define
\[
 \widetilde q_e:=\frac{\omega_t^x}{x_t}q_e^*.
\tag{33}
\]
Every \(x_t\) is positive on \(T_*\), so (33) is defined, and \(0\leq\omega_t^x/x_t\leq1\). Thus
\[
 0\leq\widetilde q_e\leq q_e^*\leq\bar q_e^{\ne},
 \qquad
 \sum_{e:m_e=t}\widetilde q_e=\omega_t^x,
 \qquad
 |\widetilde q|=\Omega_x.
\tag{34}
\]
Its target support is exactly \(J_x\). If \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(J_x)\leq1\), accepted fact 4aa2d3090ea613b8 applied to the actual subfamily (33) gives
\[
 G\geq2|\widetilde q|=2\Omega_x
 >\frac{594}{553}p
 >\frac{594}{51200}Q
 >\frac Q{100},
\tag{35}
\]
where (1) supplies the penultimate strict inequality and
\[
 \frac{594}{51200}-\frac1{100}
 =\frac{41}{25600}>0.
\tag{36}
\]
This contradicts (2). Hence \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(J_x)\geq2\). Since \(J_x\subseteq T_*\subseteq A\) and \(\dim A=2\), equality follows. This proves (18) and every assertion.
