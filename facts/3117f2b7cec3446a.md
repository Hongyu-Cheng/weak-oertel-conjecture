## Statement

For every \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), write continuous coordinates as \((t,u)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{d-1}\), with \(u\) absent when \(d=1\), and put
\[
 q:=2^n,\qquad N:=3^n,\qquad T:=3^{n/2},\qquad
 L:=q+\frac{q-1}{T},\qquad U:=[-1,1]^{d-1}.
\]
Here \(U\subset\mathbb R^0\) is the singleton when \(d=1\), and its \(0\)-dimensional volume is \(1\). Define
\[
 \widetilde C_{n,L}:=
 \operatorname{conv}\!\left(
 [-1,1]^n\times[-L,0]\;\cup\;\{(\mathbf{0},T)\}
 \right)\subset\mathbb R^{n+1},
 \qquad
 C:=\widetilde C_{n,L}\times U\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}.
\]
For Borel \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), let
\[
 \mu(E):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{(t,u):(z,t,u)\in C\cap E\},
 \qquad W:=\mu(C).
\]
Set
\[
 \ell(z,t,u):=-t,\qquad z_0:=\mathbf{0},\qquad
 A:=C\cap\{t\geq-1\}=C\cap\{\ell\leq1\},
 \qquad p:=(\mathbf{0},0,\mathbf{0}).
\]
Then \(C\) is a full-dimensional convex body, and
\[
 \mu(A)=2^{-n}W,
 \qquad
 \frac1{\mu(A)}\int_A(z,t,u)\,d\mu=p.
\]
Moreover, \(p\in A\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\). In particular, the base coordinate of the mixed centroid is the lattice point \(z_0\).

At mass \(a:=\mu(A)=2^{-n}W\), consider the following problem over \(\mu\)-measurable densities \(g\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{minimize}\quad &\int_C\ell\,g\,d\mu,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 &0\leq g\leq1\quad\mu\text{-almost everywhere},\\
 &\int_Cg\,d\mu=a,\\
 &\int_C(z-z_0)g\,d\mu=\mathbf{0}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{P}
\]
The unique optimizer of \((P)\), modulo mixed-null sets, is \(g=\mathbf{1}_A\). It has the finite linked KKT representation
\[
 A=C\cap
 \{\ell+\lambda\cdot(z-z_0)\leq\eta\},
 \qquad \lambda=\mathbf{0},\quad\eta=1.
\]
The threshold hyperplane has zero mixed measure:
\[
 \mu\{(z,t,u)\in C:t=-1\}=0.
\]
Finally, the linked closed halfspace
\[
 H:=\{t\geq0\}=\{\ell\leq\ell(p)\}
\]
passes through \(p\) and satisfies the exact identity
\[
 \frac{\mu(A\cap H)}{\mu(A)}
 =\frac1{3^{n/2}+1}.
\]
Thus the ratio tends to zero for arbitrarily large integer dimension \(n\). If \(r\) denotes the affine rank of the positive-mass base support of \(A\), then \(r=n\) and
\[
 (r+1)\frac{\mu(A\cap H)}{\mu(A)}
 =\frac{n+1}{3^{n/2}+1}\longrightarrow0.
\]

## Proof

First determine every lattice fiber. Let \(B:=[-1,1]^n\times[-L,0]\). Every point of \(\widetilde C_{n,L}=\operatorname{conv}(B\cup\{(\mathbf{0},T)\})\) has a representation
\[
 (z,t)=(1-\theta)(y,s)+\theta(\mathbf{0},T),
 \qquad
 0\leq\theta\leq1,\quad y\in[-1,1]^n,\quad s\in[-L,0].
 \tag{1}
\]
For fixed \(z\in[-1,1]^n\), equation (1) implies
\(
 \theta\leq1-\lVert z\rVert_\infty
\)
and hence
\(
 -L\leq t\leq T(1-\lVert z\rVert_\infty).
\)
The lower endpoint is attained in the prism at \((z,-L)\). The upper endpoint is attained by taking
\(
 \theta=1-\lVert z\rVert_\infty
\)
and \(s=0\), with \(y=z/\lVert z\rVert_\infty\) when \(z\neq\mathbf{0}\), and by taking the apex when \(z=\mathbf{0}\). Since a fiber of a convex set is an interval, this proves
\[
 (\widetilde C_{n,L})_z
 =[-L,T(1-\lVert z\rVert_\infty)]
 \qquad(z\in[-1,1]^n).
 \tag{2}
\]
The base projection of \(C\) is \([-1,1]^n\). Therefore its nonempty lattice fibers are exactly those over
\[
 D:=\{-1,0,1\}^n,
 \qquad |D|=N.
\]
Every nonzero \(z\in D\) has \(\lVert z\rVert_\infty=1\). Thus (2) gives all the lattice fibers:
\[
 C_{\mathbf{0}}=[-L,T]\times U,
 \qquad
 C_z=[-L,0]\times U\quad(z\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}),
 \qquad
 C_z=\varnothing\quad(z\notin D).
 \tag{3}
\]
The body is compact and convex. It is full dimensional because it contains the full-dimensional box
\(
 [-1,1]^n\times[-L,0]\times U
\)
and \(L>0\).

Put \(V:=\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(U)=2^{d-1}\), with \(V=1\) when \(d=1\). From (3),
\[
 W
 =V\bigl((L+T)+(N-1)L\bigr)
 =V(T+NL).
 \tag{4}
\]
Since \(q\geq2\), one has \(L>1\). Intersecting every fiber in (3) with \(t\geq-1\) gives
\[
 A_{\mathbf{0}}=[-1,T]\times U,
 \qquad
 A_z=[-1,0]\times U\quad(z\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}),
 \qquad
 A_z=\varnothing\quad(z\notin D).
 \tag{5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 a:=\mu(A)
 =V\bigl((T+1)+(N-1)\bigr)
 =V(N+T).
 \tag{6}
\]
The identities \(N=T^2\) and \(L=q+(q-1)/T\) yield
\[
 T+NL
 =T+Nq+\frac{N(q-1)}T
 =T+Nq+T(q-1)
 =q(N+T).
 \tag{7}
\]
Equations (4), (6), and (7) prove
\(
 W=qV(N+T)=2^na
\), hence \(a=2^{-n}W\) exactly.

We next compute every coordinate of the mixed centroid of \(A\). For each base coordinate \(j\), the three values \(-1,0,1\) occur equally often in \(D\), so
\[
 \sum_{z\in D}z=\mathbf{0}.
\]
All noncentral fibers in (5) have the same volume \(V\), while the central fiber has base coordinate \(\mathbf{0}\). Therefore
\[
 \int_A z\,d\mu
 =V\sum_{z\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}z
 =\mathbf{0}.
 \tag{8}
\]
For the \(t\)-coordinate, the central and noncentral contributions are respectively
\[
 V\int_{-1}^Tt\,dt
 =\frac V2(T^2-1)
 =\frac V2(N-1)
\]
and
\[
 (N-1)V\int_{-1}^0t\,dt
 =-\frac V2(N-1).
\]
They cancel, so
\[
 \int_A t\,d\mu=0.
 \tag{9}
\]
For each coordinate \(u_k\), symmetry of \(U\) gives
\(
 \int_Uu_k\,du=0
\), and hence
\[
 \int_Au\,d\mu=\mathbf{0}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (8), (9), and (10), divided by the positive mass \(a\), prove that the mixed centroid is \(p=(\mathbf{0},0,\mathbf{0})\). Also, the positive-mass base support in (5) is \(D\), which contains \(\mathbf{0},e_1,\ldots,e_n\). Its affine rank is exactly \(n\).
Since \(0\in[-1,T]\) and \(\mathbf{0}\in U\), equation (5) also gives \(p\in A\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).

It remains to prove optimality and uniqueness without importing a purification theorem. The density \(g_A:=\mathbf{1}_A\) satisfies the mass constraint by (6) and the base-moment constraint by (8), so it is feasible for \((P)\). Let \(g\) be any other feasible density. Equal masses imply
\[
 \int_C\ell(g-g_A)\,d\mu
 =\int_C(\ell-1)(g-g_A)\,d\mu.
 \tag{11}
\]
On \(A\cap\{t>-1\}\), one has \(\ell-1=-t-1<0\) and \(g-g_A=g-1\leq0\). On \(C\setminus A=C\cap\{t<-1\}\), one has \(\ell-1>0\) and \(g-g_A=g\geq0\). Thus the integrand on the right of (11) is nonnegative everywhere except for irrelevant choices of representatives, and
\[
 \int_C\ell g\,d\mu\geq\int_C\ell g_A\,d\mu.
 \tag{12}
\]
This already proves optimality among all equal-mass densities, even before the base-moment constraint is imposed.

There are only \(N\) nonempty lattice fibers. In each such fiber, the threshold set \(t=-1\) is a \((d-1)\)-dimensional affine slice and has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Hence
\[
 \mu(C\cap\{t=-1\})=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
If equality holds in (12), then the nonnegative integrand in (11) vanishes almost everywhere. Away from the null set (13), its first factor is strict. It follows that \(g=1\) almost everywhere on \(A\) and \(g=0\) almost everywhere on \(C\setminus A\). Thus \(g=g_A\) mixed-almost everywhere. This proves the asserted uniqueness, including the threshold issue.

For completeness, the corresponding finite KKT certificate is explicit. With \(\lambda=\mathbf{0}\) and \(\eta=1\), the adjusted score is
\[
 \ell+\lambda\cdot(z-z_0)-\eta=-t-1.
\]
With the mass constraint written as \(a-\int_Cg\,d\mu=0\), the Lagrangian is
\[
 \mathcal L(g,\lambda,\eta)
 =\int_C\bigl(\ell+\lambda\cdot(z-z_0)-\eta\bigr)g\,d\mu+\eta a.
\]
The adjusted score is negative on \(A\cap\{t>-1\}\), positive on \(C\setminus A\), and zero only on the mixed-null threshold (13). Pointwise minimization of this Lagrangian over \(0\leq g\leq1\) therefore selects \(g_A\), while (6) and (8) give primal feasibility. Both multipliers are finite, and the cap is exactly
\(
 C\cap\{\ell+\lambda\cdot(z-z_0)\leq\eta\}=A
\).

Finally,
\[
 H=\{t\geq0\}=\{\ell\leq0\}=\{\ell\leq\ell(p)\}
\]
is a closed halfspace through \(p\), linked to the same objective functional \(\ell=-t\), whose continuous normal is nonzero. In the central fiber, \(A\cap H\) has continuous section \([0,T]\times U\), of volume \(VT\). In every noncentral fiber, the intersection is \(\{0\}\times U\), which has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Hence
\[
 \mu(A\cap H)=VT.
 \tag{14}
\]
Using (6) and \(N=T^2\),
\[
 \frac{\mu(A\cap H)}{\mu(A)}
 =\frac{VT}{V(N+T)}
 =\frac{T}{T^2+T}
 =\frac1{T+1}
 =\frac1{3^{n/2}+1}.
\]
This tends to zero exponentially, and multiplying by \(r+1=n+1\) still gives a quantity tending to zero.

This is a counterexample only to a linked-cap transfer bound of the stated type. It is not a counterexample to the locked weak Oertel statement: the displayed ratio is measured inside the selected cap, and
\[
 \frac{1/(3^{n/2}+1)}{2^{-n}}
 =\frac{2^n}{3^{n/2}+1}
 \sim\left(\frac2{\sqrt3}\right)^n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
