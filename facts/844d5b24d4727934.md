## Statement

There is a compact convex body in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$ with three positive integer fibers occupying $k=2$ parities and forming one component in the hypergraph that joins each distinct same-parity pair to its midpoint. Its mixed source has an almost-everywhere partition into $r=4$ original closed caps of masses $(1,1,6,2)$, and one of these caps contains an entire positive fiber. Thus $r=2k>2c(P)$ and $r>2(k-1)$ despite $|P|>k$, where $c(P)$ counts midpoint components.

## Proof

Let $C$ be the convex hull of the lifted intervals $C_{-1}=[-1,1]$, $C_0=[-1,5]$, $C_1=[5,7]$. These are exactly its integer fibers. At the endpoint bases this follows from extremality, and at zero every combination from the endpoints uses equal total weights on them, with midpoint interval $[2,4]\subset C_0$. Any additional weight from the middle interval stays in it by convexity.

Use the four caps $x\ge6(z+1)$, $x\le-2(z+1)$, $x\ge1-z$, and $x\le1+3z$, in that order. At $-1$ only the first two traces are nonempty, namely $[0,1]$ and $[-1,0]$. At $0$ only the third and fourth are nonempty, namely $[1,5]$ and $[-1,1]$. At $1$ only the third is nonempty and is the whole interval $[5,7]$. The cap masses are thus $(1,1,6,2)$, and the sole overlaps are the two cut endpoints, of zero length. The pair $-1,1$ and its midpoint $0$ give a hyperedge containing all three bases. Hence $c(P)=1$, $k=2$ and $r=4$, as claimed.
