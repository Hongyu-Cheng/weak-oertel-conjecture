## Statement

Let \(d\geq1\), let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of volume \(a>0\), and let \(0<m<a\). Define the selected-mass metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_m(K):=
 \left\{
  \frac1m\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
  f\in L^\infty(K),\quad 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
  \int_K f\,dx=m
 \right\}.
\]
For every \(u\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), choose \(s_u\) so that the top cap
\[
 A_u:=K\cap\{x:\langle u,x\rangle\geq s_u\}
\]
has volume \(m\), and put
\[
 c_u:=\frac1m\int_{A_u}x\,dx.
\]
Then
\[
 \{y\in\mathsf M_m(K):\langle u,y\rangle=h_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(u)\}
 =\{c_u\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, with the outward normal cone convention
\[
 N_L(y):=\{v\in\mathbb R^d:\langle v,x-y\rangle\leq0\ \text{for every }x\in L\},
\]
one has
\[
 N_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(c_u)=\{\alpha u:\alpha\geq0\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every nonzero direction has a unique metronoid support contact, and that contact has a unique nonzero outward-normal ray. Here \(A_u\) is a cap of the original body \(K\), while \(c_u\) is its centroid and a boundary point of the metronoid. No assertion is made that \(c_u\in\partial K\) or that \(u\in N_K(c_u)\). The restriction \(m<a\) is essential for (2): when \(m=a\), the metronoid is the singleton consisting of the centroid of \(K\).

Now assume all the hypotheses, dual optimizer, and support-contact choices in equations (1)--(20) of accepted fact 3f9592d2fb367884, with exactly its notation and normalization. In particular, \(\Omega>0\), \(F_\omega=\{e:\omega_e>0\}\), and every loaded incidence \((e,z)\) has
\[
 \lambda_{e,z}>0,\qquad
 u_{e,z}=\frac{v_e}{h_{D_z}(v_e)},\qquad
 h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})=1,\qquad
 x_{e,z}\in\mathcal K_z=\mathsf M_{A_z/2}(C_z),
\]
\[
 \langle u_{e,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle=1,
 \qquad
 t_z=\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z},
 \qquad
 U_z=\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}u_{e,z},
 \qquad
 \tau_z=h_{D_z}(U_z),
\]
where incidence sums in this statement run over \(e\in F_\omega\). Suppose that its same-body interaction vanishes:
\[
 \mathcal I=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Then the following conclusions hold.

1. For every loaded endpoint \(z\), there are a point \(x_z\in\mathcal K_z\) and a nonzero vector \(u_z\) with \(h_{D_z}(u_z)=1\) such that
\[
 x_{e,z}=x_z,
 \qquad
 u_{e,z}=u_z
 \qquad(e\in F_\omega,\ e\ni z).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \widetilde p_z=x_z,
 \qquad
 U_z=t_zu_z,
 \qquad
 \tau_z=t_z.
\tag{5}
\]

2. The endpoint-collapse load equals the total stress load:
\[
 T=\sum_z\tau_z=\sum_zt_z=\Omega.
\tag{6}
\]
The normalized endpoint directions satisfy the exact dual balances
\[
 \sum_{z:t_z>0}t_zu_z=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{z:t_z>0}t_zu_zz^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{7}
\]

3. Let \(\mathscr G_\omega\) be the graph with edge set \(F_\omega\) and vertex set the loaded endpoints. For each connected component \(\Gamma\) of \(\mathscr G_\omega\), there is a nonzero vector \(w_\Gamma\), and there are positive scalars \(\alpha_z\) for \(z\in V(\Gamma)\) and \(\beta_e\) for \(e\in E(\Gamma)\), such that
\[
 u_z=\alpha_z w_\Gamma,
 \qquad
 v_e=\beta_e w_\Gamma.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
 B_\Gamma:=\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\beta_e.
\]
Then
\[
 B_\Gamma
 =\sum_{z\in V(\Gamma)}t_z\alpha_z>0,
\tag{9}
\]
and the two definitions
\[
 \bar m_\Gamma
 :=\frac1{B_\Gamma}\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\beta_em_e
 =\frac1{B_\Gamma}\sum_{z\in V(\Gamma)}t_z\alpha_zz
\tag{10}
\]
agree. The component rays obey
\[
 \sum_\Gamma B_\Gamma w_\Gamma=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_\Gamma B_\Gamma w_\Gamma\bar m_\Gamma^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, the positive-load case with \(\mathcal I=0\) has at least two loaded connected components. The conclusion aligns directions only within a loaded component; it does not identify contacts belonging to different fibers or produce a capacity estimate.

## Proof

We first prove (1)--(2) directly from the exact equality case in the bathtub comparison. Fix \(u\ne0\), write \(\phi_u(x):=\langle u,x\rangle\), and put
\[
 \underline s_u:=\min_{x\in K}\phi_u(x),
 \qquad
 \overline s_u:=\max_{x\in K}\phi_u(x).
\]
The function
\[
 F_u(s):=\bigl|K\cap\{x:\phi_u(x)\geq s\}\bigr|_d
\]
is continuous: indicators converge pointwise away from the level hyperplane, which has \(d\)-dimensional measure zero, so dominated convergence applies. Since \(F_u(\underline s_u)=a>m\) and \(F_u(\overline s_u)=0<m\), there is \(s_u\in(\underline s_u,\overline s_u)\) with \(F_u(s_u)=m\). Thus both sides of the cutting hyperplane meet the interior of \(K\), and \(A_u\) is a full-dimensional convex body of volume \(m\).

Let \(f\) be any admissible density in the definition of \(\mathsf M_m(K)\). Equality of the two masses gives the exact identity
\[
 \int_{A_u}\phi_u(x)\,dx-
 \int_K\phi_u(x)f(x)\,dx
 =\int_K(\phi_u(x)-s_u)
       \bigl(\mathbf1_{A_u}(x)-f(x)\bigr)\,dx.
\tag{12}
\]
The integrand on the right is nonnegative pointwise. On \(\{\phi_u>s_u\}\), its vanishing forces \(f=1\), and on \(\{\phi_u<s_u\}\), its vanishing forces \(f=0\). The omitted level hyperplane has measure zero. It follows that equality in (12) holds if and only if
\[
 f=\mathbf1_{A_u}\quad\text{a.e. on }K.
\tag{13}
\]
The indicator of \(A_u\) is admissible, so (12) gives the support value in direction \(u\). If a metronoid point attains that support value, any density representing it has equality in (12), hence equals \(\mathbf1_{A_u}\) almost everywhere by (13), and its barycenter is \(c_u\). This proves (1).

It remains to prove the normal-ray assertion rather than merely contact uniqueness. Suppose \(v\ne0\) also belongs to \(N_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(c_u)\). Applying (1) in direction \(v\) shows that \(c_u\) is the unique support point in direction \(v\). Choose an admissible density representing \(c_u\). Equality in the bathtub comparison for both \(u\) and \(v\) forces this one density to equal both cap indicators, so
\[
 \mathbf1_{A_u}=\mathbf1_{A_v}\quad\text{a.e. on }K.
\tag{14}
\]
We justify carefully what (14) says about the cutting directions. Put \(O:=\operatorname{int}K\). Each cutting hyperplane meets \(O\), because its threshold lies strictly between the corresponding minimum and maximum on \(K\): take a point of \(O\); if it is not already on the hyperplane, join it to an extremizer on the opposite side, and use that every non-endpoint of this segment is interior. If two oriented affine halfspaces whose boundary hyperplanes meet \(O\) have indicators agreeing almost everywhere on \(O\), then the halfspaces are identical. Indeed, a point of \(O\) lying strictly inside the first halfspace but not strictly inside the second has a small ball, or a half-ball when it lies on the second boundary, on which the indicators differ on a set of positive measure. Thus their open sides agree in \(O\). Their relative boundaries in \(O\) agree as well; an open \((d-1)\)-dimensional disk in one boundary is therefore contained in the other, so the two affine hyperplanes coincide. Equality of the open sides fixes the same orientation. Applying this observation to (14) gives
\[
 v=\alpha u\quad\text{for some }\alpha>0.
\tag{15}
\]
Conversely every nonnegative multiple of \(u\) is plainly in \(N_{\mathsf M_m(K)}(c_u)\). This proves (2). Notice that the argument concerns the normal cone of the metronoid at the centroid of an original-body cap. It never treats that centroid as a boundary point of the original body.

We now prove the zero-interaction conclusions. All identities in the remainder use the objects and normalizations of accepted fact 3f9592d2fb367884. At a loaded endpoint \(z\), that fact gives
\[
 \mathcal I_z
 =\frac1{t_z}
   \sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\subseteq F_\omega\\
                   e\ne f,\ e\ni z,\ f\ni z}}
   \lambda_{e,z}\lambda_{f,z}g_{z;e,f},
 \qquad
 \mathcal I=\sum_{z:t_z>0}\mathcal I_z,
\tag{16}
\]
where every coefficient is positive and every \(g_{z;e,f}\) is nonnegative. Hence (3) implies
\[
 g_{z;e,f}=0
\tag{17}
\]
for every pair of distinct loaded edges sharing \(z\). The exact gap decomposition in that accepted fact is
\[
 g_{z;e,f}
 =\bigl(1-\langle u_{e,z},x_{f,z}-c_z\rangle\bigr)
  +\bigl(1-\langle u_{f,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle\bigr),
\tag{18}
\]
and both brackets are nonnegative. Equations (17)--(18) therefore give
\[
 \langle u_{e,z},x_{f,z}-c_z\rangle=1,
 \qquad
 \langle u_{f,z},x_{e,z}-c_z\rangle=1.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(\mathcal K_z=c_z+D_z\) and \(h_{D_z}(u_{e,z})=1\), the first equality in (19) says that \(x_{f,z}\) is a support point of the half-metronoid \(\mathcal K_z=\mathsf M_{A_z/2}(C_z)\) in direction \(u_{e,z}\). The prescribed point \(x_{e,z}\) is another such support point. Here \(C_z\), not \(\mathcal K_z\), is the original convex body; positive \(A_z\) makes \(C_z\) full-dimensional, and \(0<A_z/2<A_z\). Applying (1) to that original body and selected mass gives
\[
 x_{e,z}=x_{f,z}.
\tag{20}
\]
The common point is then supported by both nonzero directions \(u_{e,z}\) and \(u_{f,z}\). Equation (2) puts those directions on the same positive ray. Their common normalization by \(h_{D_z}=1\) makes the positive scalar equal to one, so
\[
 u_{e,z}=u_{f,z}.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(z\) has only one loaded incidence, (20)--(21) are vacuous and its unique contact and direction define \(x_z,u_z\). Otherwise they hold for every pair. This proves (4).

The weighted definitions now immediately give
\[
 \widetilde p_z
 =\frac1{t_z}\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}x_z=x_z,
 \qquad
 U_z=\sum_{e\ni z}\lambda_{e,z}u_z=t_zu_z.
\tag{22}
\]
Positive homogeneity and \(h_{D_z}(u_z)=1\) give \(\tau_z=t_z\), proving (5). The accepted load identity \(\sum_zt_z=\Omega\) then proves (6).

The two exact dual cancellations in the accepted fact yield
\[
 \sum_zU_z=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_zU_zz^{\mathsf T}=0.
\tag{23}
\]
Unloaded endpoints have \(U_z=0\). Substituting \(U_z=t_zu_z\) into (23) proves (7).

Finally, take an edge \(e=\{z,z'\}\in F_\omega\). The endpoint definitions give
\[
 v_e=h_{D_z}(v_e)u_z
     =h_{D_{z'}}(v_e)u_{z'},
\tag{24}
\]
and both support values in (24) are strictly positive. Thus the endpoint directions across every loaded edge lie on one positive ray. Propagating along paths proves the ray assertion in each connected component. Choose any nonzero representative \(w_\Gamma\) of that ray. This gives the positive and uniquely determined coefficients in (8).

The accepted identity \(U_z=\frac12\sum_{e\ni z}v_e\), together with (5) and (8), gives the scalar relation
\[
 t_z\alpha_z
 =\frac12\sum_{\substack{e\in E(\Gamma)\\e\ni z}}\beta_e.
\tag{25}
\]
Summing (25) over \(z\in V(\Gamma)\) counts every component edge twice and proves (9). Multiplying (25) by \(z\), summing over the component, and using \(m_e=(z+z')/2\) gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in V(\Gamma)}t_z\alpha_zz
 =\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\beta_em_e,
\tag{26}
\]
which proves (10). Grouping the two equations in (7) by connected components and using (8)--(10) proves (11). If there were only one component, the first equation in (11) would read \(B_\Gamma w_\Gamma=0\), contradicting \(B_\Gamma>0\) and \(w_\Gamma\ne0\). Hence at least two loaded components are necessary. This completes the proof without asserting any terminal mass or capacity consequence.
