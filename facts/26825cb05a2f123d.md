## Statement

For planar convex bodies $A\subseteq C$ centrally symmetric about zero, form the opposed flow with fixed midpoint $B$. Put $a_s=|A_s|$, $c_s=|C_s|$, $a=a_0$, $c=c_0$, $W=|B|$, and $M_s=a_s+c_s+W$. On $0\le s\le1/2$, the maximum normalized mixed closed-halfspace depth over all actual points is
$$
D_s=\frac{\max(c_s/2,\ W/2+a_s)}{M_s}>\frac14.
$$
If $A\subsetneq C$, the first normalized branch strictly decreases and the second strictly increases. There is a unique minimum in $(0,1/2)$ exactly when $c>W+2a$. It occurs at $c_s=W+2a_s$ and has value $(W+2a_s)/(2(2W+3a_s))$. The crossing polynomial is $c-2a-W+(4a-2q-2c)s+\Delta s^2$, with $q=V(A,C)$ and $\Delta=2q-a-c$. When $A\subsetneq C$ and $c\le W+2a$, the minimum on this half-interval is at $s=0$. If $A=C$, depth is constant. In particular the three homothetic fibers $((1-\theta)K,K,(1+\theta)K)$ for centrally symmetric planar $K$ and $0\le\theta<1$ have depth $\max((1+\theta)^2/2,1/2+(1-\theta)^2)/(3+2\theta^2)$.

## Proof

By the opposed-flow mass lemma the three fibers are exactly $A_s,B,C_s$ at $-1,0,1$. Their supports are affine in $s$ and $h_A\le h_C$, so $A_s\subseteq B\subseteq C_s$ for $0\le s\le 1/2$. All three are symmetric about zero.

Every cap containing $(1,0)$ pays at least $c_s/2$ in that fiber by symmetry, including caps containing its whole plane. Every nonvertical cap containing $(0,0)$ contains its parallel cap through that point. On the latter, the middle trace has area $W/2$ and the endpoint cutoffs are opposite. The trace in $C_s$ contains the same trace in a copy of $A_s$, and the two opposite-cutoff upper traces on $A_s$ have areas summing to $a_s$ by $x\mapsto -x$ and the zero area of a line. A vertical cap containing $(0,0)$ includes the middle fiber and at least one endpoint fiber, with mass at least $W+a_s$. The two selected marks thus certify raw depth at least $\max(c_s/2,W/2+a_s)$.

For the matching upper bound, choose any unit $u$ and $L>\max_{x\in C_s}|u\cdot x|$. The two caps $-Lz\pm u\cdot x\ge0$ each capture the smaller endpoint, half the middle, and none of the larger endpoint, and together contain all the middle points. The two caps $L(z-1)\pm u\cdot x\ge0$ isolate the upper endpoint and contain all its points between them, with mass $c_s/2$ each. The two caps $-L(z+1)\pm u\cdot x\ge0$ do the same at the smaller endpoint, with mass $a_s/2$ each. This covers the whole mixed set, so the maximum equals the lower certificate, after division by $M_s$.

If $A\subsetneq C$, their supports differ in some direction. Hence $A_s$ strictly increases and $C_s$ strictly decreases by inclusion as $s$ increases, even on the full parameter interval. Strict inclusion of convex bodies of positive area strictly increases area. The branches $c_s/(2M_s)$ and $(a_s+W/2)/M_s$ are consequently strictly decreasing and increasing, respectively. More precisely their derivatives have numerators $[c_s'(a_s+W)-c_s a_s']/2$ and $a_s'(c_s+W/2)-c_s'(a_s+W/2)$, over $M_s^2$. These have strict signs at interior parameters: by ordinary Brunn–Minkowski, $\sqrt{a_s}$ is concave on $[0,1]$ and strictly increasing there, so its derivative at every interior parameter is bounded below by a strictly positive later secant. The sign for $c_s'$ follows from $c_s=a_{1-s}$. The branch difference has the sign of $c_s-W-2a_s$, which by the opposed-flow area polynomials is $c-2a-W+(4a-2q-2c)s+\Delta s^2$. At $s=1/2$ it is negative because $a_s=c_s=W>0$. This proves the exact crossing criterion, the unique strict interior minimum when the crossing is interior, and the boundary minimum otherwise. At the crossing the quotient is $(W+2a_s)/(2(2W+3a_s))$. If $c_s\ge W+2a_s$, the endpoint branch exceeds $1/4$ since $c_s>W+a_s$. Otherwise the middle branch exceeds $1/4$ since $c_s<W+3a_s$. When $A=C$ both endpoint areas are constant, so the depth is constant.

For the homothetic display, take $A=(1-\theta)K$, $C=(1+\theta)K$ at $s=0$ and cancel the common factor $|K|$. For the explicit flow starting from $A=K/2,C=3K/2$, the parameter in this formula is $\theta=1/2-s$. The crossing equation is $\theta^2-6\theta+2=0$, so the strict minimum is at $s=\sqrt7-5/2\in(0,1/4)$ with normalized depth $19/62-2\sqrt7/217>1/4$. For $K=[-1,1]^2$, the six caps above work with $L=10(1-2s)$ on $[0,1/4]$ for any unit $u$: their continuous margins have magnitude at most $(3/2)\sqrt2<5$, while $L\ge5$. At the crossing the two critical budgets have opposite derivative signs, and the smaller-endpoint budget is strictly slack.

For a nonhomothetic instance take $A=[-1,1]\times[-1/2,1/2]$ and $C=[-2,2]\times[-3,3]$. Their full width and height multiply to give $a_s=2+12s+10s^2$, $c_s=24-32s+10s^2$ and $W=21/2$. The crossing polynomial is $19/2-56s-10s^2$, whose positive root is $(\sqrt{879}-28)/10\in(0,1/2)$. The source with $s$ reflected to $1-s$ has the same depth by reversing the base sign.
