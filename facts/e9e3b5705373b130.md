## Statement

In the planar three-cap aggregation, let the positive integer fibers be partitioned into ownership groups $P_i$. Each group has three actual closed ambient caps $H_{i,j}$ of mixed mass at most $B$, with restrictions on each owned fiber that cover its whole plane and whose normals span that plane with a strictly positive dependence. For each group let $L_i$ be the sum over owned fibers of the minimum of the three local cap areas, and let $X_i$ be the sum of the three cap masses outside the owned fibers, counted with multiplicity. The unconditional inequality $\sum_i X_i\ge (3/4)\sum_i(B-L_i)$ fails even for a single group whose caps are all active at the same globally deepest point of a full-dimensional mixed-integer convex body.

Specifically, take $C=\{(z,x,y):-1\le z\le1,\ |x-z/2|\le1,\ |y|\le1\}$ in $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2$. The actual integer fibers are $K_z=[z/2-1,z/2+1]\times[-1,1]$ at $z=-1,0,1$. Their total mixed area is $M=12$, and the origin attains the global maximum raw depth $6$, or normalized depth $1/2$. The one group owns every fiber and uses $H_1=\{x\ge0\}$, $H_2=\{y\ge0\}$ and $H_3=\{x+y\le0\}$. Each has mixed mass $B=6$, and all three traces are positive proper cuts on every owned fiber. In this group $X=0$, $L=33/8$, and $X-(3/4)(B-L)=-45/32$. The actual local overlap surpluses above the coefficient $3/4$ sum to $93/32$.

## Proof

The invertible linear map $(z,s,y)\mapsto(z,s+z/2,y)$ sends $[-1,1]^3$ onto $C$, so $C$ is a compact full-dimensional convex body. Its projection on the base axis is $[-1,1]$, whose integer points are exactly $-1,0,1$. At each of them the defining inequalities give exactly the rectangle in the statement, of area $4$. Write $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)$ and $\mu(E)=\mathcal H_2(S\cap E)$ for ambient measurable $E$. Then $\mu$ has total mass $M=12$ and is invariant under reflection through $o=(0,0,0)\in S$.

If $H$ is a closed halfspace with boundary through $o$, then $H$ and its reflection $-H$ have equal mass and cover $S$, so $\mu(H)\ge6$. This also applies if the boundary contains a full fiber. A closed halfspace containing $o$ includes its parallel translate whose boundary passes through $o$, giving the same lower bound. Conversely, for any $w=(z_0,x_0,y_0)\in S$, the closed halfspaces $x\ge x_0$ and $x\le x_0$ both contain $w$, and their common boundary has zero area on each fiber. Their mixed masses sum to $12$, so the depth at $w$ is at most $6$. Thus the raw maximum equals $6$ at $o$.

The cap margins $x,y,-x-y$ sum to zero at each ambient point, so the three caps cover the ambient space and hence the whole plane over every integer base. Their unit continuous normals are $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ and $(-1,-1)/\sqrt2$. They are pairwise independent and their combination with coefficients $1,1,\sqrt2$ is zero. Hence they form a minimal positive rank-two circuit. All three boundaries pass through $o$ and have zero mixed area because each intersects any fiber plane in a line. Reflection therefore gives each cap mass $6$, making each an exact depth minimizer at $o$.

Let $A_j(z)=|K_z\cap H_j|$ denote the area of the corresponding fiber trace, where the intersection uses the ambient cap at base $z$. The areas are
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
z&A_1(z)&A_2(z)&A_3(z)\\ \hline
-1&1&2&23/8\\
0&2&2&2\\
1&3&2&9/8
\end{array}
$$
Indeed, the first cap cuts a rectangle of area $2+z$, and the second bisects each rectangle. For the third cap at $z=-1$, the vertical segment has length $2$ for $-3/2\le x\le-1$ and length $1-x$ for $-1\le x\le1/2$. Its area is $1+\int_{-1}^{1/2}(1-x)\,dx=23/8$. Reflection sends the first rectangle onto the last and sends the third cap to its complementary halfspace with null common boundary, yielding $4-23/8=9/8$. The centered middle rectangle is bisected. Every entry is strictly between zero and $4$, and every column sums to $6$.

Since this single group owns every fiber, $X=0$. The sum of the three row minima is $L=1+2+9/8=33/8$, so $\Delta=B-L=15/8$ and $X-(3/4)\Delta=-45/32$. This directly violates the proposed universal sign with the exact maximum depth as the common budget. For $r=3/4$, define the local surplus $E_z=\sum_j A_j(z)-|K_z|-r\min_j A_j(z)$. The table gives
$$
(E_{-1},E_0,E_1)=(9/8,1/2,41/32),\qquad \sum_z E_z=93/32.
$$
The identity obtained by summing all three cap masses is therefore
$$
(3-r)B=M+X-r\Delta+\sum_z E_z
=12-45/32+93/32=27/2.
$$
The local surplus exceeds the dispersion charge by $3/2$ in this example.

The counterexample concerns a universal sign for the chosen cover. The normalized maximum $1/2$ exceeds the sharp target $2^{-1}(2/3)^2=2/9$ for $(n,d)=(1,2)$, and hence also the weak target $c_0/2$ for $0<c_0<1/e$. For this same body an alternative critical triple is given by the margins $x-z/2$, $y$, and $-x+z/2-y$. These sum to zero and bisect every owned fiber through its own center. Thus that triple has $L=B=6$ and zero credit.
