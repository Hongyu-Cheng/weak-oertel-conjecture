## Statement

Let $(p_k)$ be a probability sequence positive exactly on a finite consecutive integer interval $[\ell,r]\cap\mathbb Z$, with $p_k^2\ge p_{k-1}p_{k+1}$ at its interior indices. If $m=\sum_k k p_k$ is an integer, both $\sum_{k\ge m}p_k$ and $\sum_{k\le m}p_k$ are strictly greater than $1/e$. The infimum of either closed tail over all such sequences is $1/e$.

## Proof

A singleton has both tails one. Otherwise set $F_k=\sum_{j\ge k}p_j$ on $[\ell,r]\cap\mathbb Z$. The positive ratios $p_{k+1}/p_k$ are nonincreasing. For each fixed $j\ge0$, $p_{k+j}/p_k$ is a product of $j$ successive ratios, nonincreasing in $k$, and becomes zero when $k+j>r$. Summation shows that $F_k/p_k$ is nonincreasing. Consequently $F_{k+1}/F_k=1-p_k/F_k$ is nonincreasing on $\ell\le k<r$, so the piecewise affine interpolation of $\log F_k$ on $[\ell,r]$ is concave. Jensen at the integer mean yields
$$
\log F_m\ge\sum_{k=\ell}^r p_k\log F_k
=\sum_{k=\ell}^r(F_k-F_{k+1})\log F_k
>\int_0^1\log x\,dx=-1.
$$
Here $F_{r+1}=0$ is used only in the sum and integral. The partition intervals all have positive length and $\log$ is strictly increasing, so the upper-endpoint sum is strict even with the integrable singularity at zero. Reflection proves the other tail.

For sharpness, for each integer $D\ge1$ use the finite integer-mean sequences of fact 4d24a4fbd205befd whose upper closed tails approach $((D+1)/(D+2))^{D+1}$. Their positive $D$th roots are concave, which implies log-concavity by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and raising to power $2D$. These sequences are therefore admissible here. Letting $D$ increase gives upper-tail infimum at most $1/e$, and reflection gives the lower-tail infimum.
