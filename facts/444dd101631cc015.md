## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and assume the exact canonical hard-branch hypotheses and notation of accepted facts `6b09aae189415c70` and `7ec63df101c30343`. In particular, put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
\]
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),\qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C),
\]
and let \(Q>0\) be the mass of the half-strength canonical product reservoir. Use the canonical capacity
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D
\]
and assume
\[
 \mathcal A(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{1}
\]
The accepted hard-branch conclusions include
\[
 n\ge2,\qquad G<\frac Q{100},\qquad Q\le\frac W8.
\tag{2}
\]

For every retained parity label \((\xi,c)\), use
\[
 S_{\xi,c}:=\sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_e,
 \qquad
 q_{\xi,c}^{\circ}:=
 \frac1{S_{\xi,c}}
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}s_eF_e^\flat
 \quad(S_{\xi,c}>0).
\tag{3}
\]
Let \(t_e\) be the actual opposite-edge masses of the canonical transverse-triangle construction, and put
\[
 T_{\xi,c}:=
 \sum_{\substack{e\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}t_e,
 \qquad
 P_\triangle:=\sum_{\xi,c}T_{\xi,c}=\sum_et_e.
\tag{4}
\]
Accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` gives
\[
 0\le T_{\xi,c}\le\frac12S_{\xi,c},
 \qquad
 P_\triangle>\frac{8397}{51200}Q,
\tag{5}
\]
where a zero label is omitted, while accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives, for every positive label,
\[
 q_{\xi,c}^{\circ}+\frac12D_*
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c).
\tag{6}
\]

For every physical target
\[
 c\in\mathcal T:=\left\{c\in\mathbb Z^n:
 T_c:=\sum_\xi T_{\xi,c}>0\right\},
\tag{7}
\]
choose a retained parity label \(\xi(c)\) maximizing \(T_{\xi,c}\), and put
\[
 \widehat T_c:=T_{\xi(c),c},
 \qquad q_c:=q_{\xi(c),c}^{\circ}.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
 \rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal T),
\qquad
 r:=\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal T)\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}\{q_c-a(c):c\in\mathcal T\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 \ge\frac{4\gamma_d}{2^\rho(r+1)}P_\triangle,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A(C)
 \ge\frac{8\gamma_d}{2^\rho(r+1)}P_\triangle
 >\frac{8397\gamma_d}{6400\,2^\rho(r+1)}Q.}
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently every canonical hard branch satisfies
\[
 \boxed{2^\rho(r+1)>48.}
\tag{11}
\]

The full triangle-output masses also satisfy the following collision-safe slice bounds. Every retained label obeys
\[
 \boxed{T_{\xi,c}\le\frac{a_c}{16}.}
\tag{12}
\]
For every set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
 P_\triangle(E):=
 \sum_{c\in E\cap\mathbb Z^n}\sum_\xi T_{\xi,c}.
\]
For an affine subspace \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^n\), put
\[
 D_A:=Z\cap A,\qquad W_A:=\sum_{z\in D_A}a_z.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(D_A=\varnothing\), then \(P_\triangle(A)=0\). If \(D_A\ne\varnothing\) and
\[
 q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_A)\le2,
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 P_\triangle(A)\le
 \begin{cases}
 G/16,&q=0,\\
 G/2,&q=1,\\
 16G,&q=2.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal T|\ge263,\qquad \rho\ge3,}
\tag{15}
\]
every affine line \(\ell\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 P_\triangle(\mathbb R^n\setminus\ell)
 >\frac{8141}{51200}Q,}
\tag{16}
\]
and every affine subspace \(A\) whose occupied-fiber rank is at most two satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 P_\triangle(\mathbb R^n\setminus A)
 >\frac{41}{10240}Q.}
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (11) and (15), the remaining rank escapes include
\[
 \rho=3\Longrightarrow r\ge6,
 \qquad
 \rho=4\Longrightarrow r\ge3,
 \qquad
 \rho=5\Longrightarrow r\ge1.
\tag{18}
\]
All assertions use the full mass \(P_\triangle\). In the O-branch, accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` additionally gives \(O_\triangle>3Q/100\), but no implication from that labelled affine defect to (10), or to a complete O-branch contradiction, is asserted here.

## Proof

We first construct the heaviest-label selector. At a fixed physical target there are at most \(2^n\) parity labels. Therefore (7)--(8) give
\[
 \widehat T_c\ge2^{-n}T_c,
 \qquad
 \sum_{c\in\mathcal T}\widehat T_c
 \ge2^{-n}P_\triangle.
\tag{19}
\]
Only one point has been chosen in each physical target fiber.

Fix \(c\in\mathcal T\), abbreviate \(\xi=\xi(c)\), and choose a representing density for the center membership supplied by (6):
\[
 q_c\in\mathsf M_{2S_{\xi,c}}(C_c).
\]
Equation (5) gives \(4\widehat T_c\le2S_{\xi,c}\). Multiplying the representing density by
\[
 \frac{4\widehat T_c}{2S_{\xi,c}}\le1
\]
preserves its normalized barycenter and proves
\[
 q_c\in\mathsf M_{4\widehat T_c}(C_c).
\tag{20}
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` applied to (20) shows that every proper closed halfspace of the fiber \(C_c\) containing \(q_c\) cuts volume at least
\[
 w_c:=4\gamma_d\widehat T_c.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the distinct physical bases \(c\in\mathcal T\), the points \(q_c\), and the positive weights \(w_c\) are an admissible family in the exact residual-selector capacity of accepted fact `be359f8a6c628483`. Using an affine map attaining (9), and then (19), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)
 &\ge2^{n-\rho}\frac{\sum_cw_c}{r+1}\\
 &=2^{n-\rho}\frac{4\gamma_d\sum_c\widehat T_c}{r+1}\\
 &\ge\frac{4\gamma_d}{2^\rho(r+1)}P_\triangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The same accepted fact gives \(h_{\max}(C)\ge2^{-n}R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\), so
\[
 D\ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).
\tag{23}
\]
In the definition of \(\mathcal A(C)\), \(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)\) and \(D\) are distinct nonnegative summands. Hence
\[
 \mathcal A(C)\ge R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+D
 \ge2R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C).
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (5), (22), and (24) prove every inequality in (10).

We audit the rational constant in (11). The binomial theorem gives
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d<
 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}=e,
\]
so \(\gamma_d>1/e\). Moreover,
\[
 e=\sum_{k=0}^{5}\frac1{k!}+\sum_{k=6}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <\frac{163}{60}+\frac1{720}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac1{7^j}
 =\frac{11743}{4320}<\frac{68}{25},
\tag{25}
\]
where the final comparison is
\(11743\cdot25=293575<293760=4320\cdot68\). Thus
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac{25}{68}.
\tag{26}
\]
Combining the strict hard inequality (1) with (10), and dividing by \(Q>0\), yields
\[
 2^\rho(r+1)>\frac{8397\gamma_d}{64}
 >\frac{8397\cdot25}{64\cdot68}
 =\frac{209925}{4352}>48,
\tag{27}
\]
because \(48\cdot4352=208896<209925\). This proves (11), including the strict endpoint.

We next prove the label and slice bounds. Fix a positive retained label \((\xi,c)\). For any edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) in this label, \(u+v=2c\), and convexity gives
\[
 \frac12(C_u+C_v)\subseteq C_c.
\]
Brunn--Minkowski therefore gives
\[
 \sqrt{a_ua_v}\le a_c.
\tag{28}
\]
Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives \(\beta_z\le a_z/4\). Consequently
\[
 \sqrt{\beta_u\beta_v}\le\frac{a_c}{4},
\]
and the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \beta_u\beta_v
 \le\frac{a_c}{8}(\beta_u+\beta_v).
\tag{29}
\]
For fixed \(c\), the reflected edges \(\{u,2c-u\}\) form a graph-theoretic matching, so within the fixed retained class
\[
 \sum_{\substack{e=\{u,v\}\subseteq\xi\\m_e=c}}
 (\beta_u+\beta_v)\le L_\xi,
\tag{30}
\]
where \(L_\xi=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}\beta_z\). Since
\(s_{\{u,v\}}=\beta_u\beta_v/L_\xi\), equations (29)--(30) imply
\[
 S_{\xi,c}\le\frac{a_c}{8}.
\tag{31}
\]
Equation (5) now proves (12). In particular every positive triangle target has \(a_c>0\), so it belongs to \(Z\).

There are at most \(2^n\) parity labels at one physical target. Summing (12) over the labels and over an affine slice gives
\[
 P_\triangle(A)\le2^{n-4}W_A.
\tag{32}
\]
This also proves the zero assertion when \(D_A=\varnothing\).

Assume now that \(D_A\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
 B:=\operatorname{aff}(D_A),\qquad q:=\dim B\le2,
\qquad
 \alpha_0:=1,\quad\alpha_1:=\frac14,\quad\alpha_2:=\frac1{64}.
\tag{33}
\]
The affine hull \(B\) is proper. Indeed, if \(B=\mathbb R^n\), then \(q=n\); since \(n\ge2\) and \(q\le2\), this forces \(q=n=2\). The full-rank clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` would then give \(G\ge W/64\), contradicting
\[
 G<\frac Q{100}\le\frac W{800}.
\tag{34}
\]

The proper-slice clause of accepted fact `8aac56c49321466c` gives
\[
 G\ge
 \min\left\{
 \alpha_qW_A+\frac{W-W_A}{2},
 \ 2^{n-q}\alpha_qW_A
 \right\}.
\tag{35}
\]
For \(q=0,1,2\), respectively, the first entry in (35) is at least
\[
 \frac W2,\qquad\frac W4,\qquad\frac W{64}.
\tag{36}
\]
Each lower bound is strictly larger than \(G\) by (34). Hence the second entry in (35) is at most \(G\), and
\[
 W_A\le\frac{2^{q-n}}{\alpha_q}G.
\tag{37}
\]
Combining (32) and (37) gives
\[
 P_\triangle(A)
 \le\frac{2^{q-4}}{\alpha_q}G,
\tag{38}
\]
whose three coefficients are exactly \(1/16,1/2,16\). This proves (14).

For a single physical target, (14) and (1) give
\[
 T_c\le\frac G{16}<\frac Q{1600}.
\tag{39}
\]
If \(N:=|\mathcal T|\), equations (5) and (39) imply
\[
 N>\frac{(8397/51200)Q}{Q/1600}
 =\frac{8397}{32}=262+\frac{13}{32}.
\tag{40}
\]
Thus the integer \(N\) is at least 263.

If \(\rho\le2\), apply (14) to \(A=\operatorname{aff}(\mathcal T)\). It would give
\[
 P_\triangle\le16G<\frac{4Q}{25}
 =\frac{8192}{51200}Q,
\]
contradicting (5). Hence \(\rho\ge3\), proving (15).

For an affine line \(\ell\), its occupied-fiber rank is at most one, so (14), (1), and (5) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_\triangle(\mathbb R^n\setminus\ell)
 &=P_\triangle-P_\triangle(\ell)\\
 &>\left(\frac{8397}{51200}-\frac1{200}\right)Q
 =\frac{8141}{51200}Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
For any affine subspace \(A\) of occupied-fiber rank at most two, the same calculation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_\triangle(\mathbb R^n\setminus A)
 &>\left(\frac{8397}{51200}-\frac{16}{100}\right)Q\\
 &=\frac{205}{51200}Q
 =\frac{41}{10240}Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
This proves (16)--(17). Finally, (18) follows by inserting \(\rho=3,4,5\) into the strict integer inequality (11). No output densities belonging to different parity labels were added anywhere in the proof. The heaviest-label argument always retained one point per physical target, while the slice argument used only the separate labelwise mass estimate (12). This completes the proof without asserting the missing O-defect aggregation step.
