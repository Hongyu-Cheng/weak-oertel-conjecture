## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\}.
\]
Let
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in D}a_z>0,qquad M:=2^{-n}W,
 \qquad r:=\dim\operatorname{aff}D.
\]
For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), define the target-mass allocation polytope
\[
 B_m:=\left\{b\in\mathbb R^D:
 0\leq b_z\leq a_z,\quad
 \sum_{z\in D}b_z=M,\quad
 \sum_{z\in D}b_z z=Mm
 \right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(D\) is finite and lattice-convex,
\[
 \operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D,
\tag{2}
\]
and at least one \(B_m\) is nonempty.

Call \(A\in\mathbb S_{++}^n\) circuit-generic for \(D\) if, for every affine circuit \(\{z_1,\ldots,z_s\}\subseteq D\) and its nonzero affine dependence
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^s\alpha_i=0,qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^s\alpha_i z_i=0,
\]
one has
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^s\alpha_i z_i^{\mathsf T}Az_i\neq0.
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(A_0\in\mathbb S_{++}^n\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is a circuit-generic \(A\in\mathbb S_{++}^n\) with \(\lVert A-A_0\rVert_F<\varepsilon\).

Fix such an \(A\). For every \(m\) with \(B_m\neq\varnothing\), the problem
\[
 \min_{b\in B_m}V_A(m,b),
 \qquad
 V_A(m,b):=\frac1M\sum_{z\in D}b_z(z-m)^{\mathsf T}A(z-m),
\tag{4}
\]
has a unique minimizer \(b^{(m)}\). In particular, a global minimizer over all feasible integral \(m\) exists. For every fixed-base minimizer \((m,b)=(m,b^{(m)})\), there are \(c\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(R^2\geq0\) such that, with
\[
 \mathcal E:=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:(x-c)^{\mathsf T}A(x-c)\leq R^2\},
\tag{5}
\]
one has, for every \(z\in D\),
\[
 (z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c)<R^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_z=a_z,
\tag{6}
\]
\[
 (z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c)>R^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_z=0.
\tag{7}
\]
The boundary set
\[
 F:=D\cap\partial\mathcal E
 =\{z\in D:(z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c)=R^2\}
\tag{8}
\]
is affinely independent. Thus
\[
 |F|\leq r+1,
\tag{9}
\]
including the possibilities \(F=\varnothing\) and \(r=0\). Every fractional site \(0<b_z<a_z\) belongs to \(F\).

Put
\[
 I:=D\cap\operatorname{int}\mathcal E,qquad
 B:=M-\sum_{z\in I}a_z.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(B=\sum_{z\in F}b_z\geq0\). If \(B=0\), all boundary masses vanish. If \(B>0\), then
\[
 y_F:=\frac{Mm-\sum_{z\in I}a_z z}{B}\in\operatorname{conv}F,
\tag{11}
\]
and the boundary masses are exactly
\[
 b_z=B\lambda_z(y_F)\qquad(z\in F),
\tag{12}
\]
where \((\lambda_z(y_F))_{z\in F}\) are the unique barycentric coordinates of \(y_F\) in the affinely independent set \(F\). In particular, if \(F=\varnothing\), then necessarily \(B=0\), and (6)-(7) determine the allocation completely.

The disputed strict-interiority assertion is valid. Namely, if
\[
 V_A(m,b)>0,
\]
then
\[
 (m-c)^{\mathsf T}A(m-c)<R^2,qquad
 m\in D,qquad b_m=a_m<M.
\tag{13}
\]

For \(0<t\leq a_z\), let \(\mathsf M_t(C_z)\) be the mass-\(t\) metronoid of the fiber, as in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, and let \(g_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\). The exact set of continuous coordinates of barycenters of dominated selected submeasures having base allocation \(b\) is
\[
 \mathcal X(m,b)
 =\frac1M\left(
 \sum_{z\in I}a_zg_z
 +\sum_{\substack{z\in F\\ b_z>0}}
 b_z\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)
 \right).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus every continuous selector degree of freedom is confined to at most \(r+1\) affinely independent boundary fibers. Formula (14) is structural only and makes no depth claim about a point \((m,x)\) with \(x\in\mathcal X(m,b)\).

Finally, define the mixed halfspace-depth optimum
\[
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}
 \inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\},
\tag{15}
\]
and put \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\). If \(F\neq\varnothing\) and \(q:=\dim\operatorname{aff}F=|F|-1\), then
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq \gamma_d2^{-q}\sum_{z\in F}a_z.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
 W_{\leq}:=\sum_{z\in D\cap\mathcal E}a_z,qquad
 W_{>}:=\sum_{z\in D\setminus\mathcal E}a_z,
\]
one has, also when \(F=\varnothing\),
\[
 W_{\leq}\leq M+\frac{2^r}{\gamma_d}h_{\max}(C),
\qquad
 W_{>}\geq W-M-\frac{2^r}{\gamma_d}h_{\max}(C).
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, if \(h_{\max}(C)\leq\eta\,2^{-n}W\), then
\[
 \frac{W_{>}}W
 \geq1-2^{-n}-\frac{2^{r-n}}{\gamma_d}\eta.
\tag{18}
\]

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes its base projection bounded, so \(D\) is finite. To prove (2), let
\[
 z=\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i z_i\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad \lambda_i>0,\quad \sum_i\lambda_i=1.
\]
Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
 \sum_i\lambda_iC_{z_i}\subseteq C_z.
\]
Every \(C_{z_i}\) has positive \(d\)-volume and hence contains a nonempty open ball. Their positive weighted Minkowski sum also contains a nonempty open ball, so \(a_z>0\) and \(z\in D\). This proves lattice convexity.

Accepted fact `725defc391f9d55d`, applied with \(\tau=2^{-n}\), supplies coefficients \(0\leq\lambda_z\leq1\) and an integral \(m\) such that
\[
 \sum_z\lambda_z a_z=M,
 \qquad
 \sum_z\lambda_z a_z z=Mm.
\]
Thus \(b_z=\lambda_z a_z\) belongs to \(B_m\), proving nonemptiness of the target-mass allocation class. Any feasible \(m\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\) by (2), so there are only finitely many feasible \(m\). Each \(B_m\) is a compact polytope, and (4) is continuous. Hence fixed-base and global minima exist.

We next prove the existence of generic perturbations. Let \(\mathcal C=\{z_1,\ldots,z_s\}\subseteq D\) be an affine circuit, and choose its circuit coefficients \(\alpha_i\). We claim that
\[
 S_{\mathcal C}:=\sum_i\alpha_i z_i z_i^{\mathsf T}\neq0.
\tag{19}
\]
Orient and normalize the dependence so that its positive and negative parts are probability distributions. They have disjoint supports, all their weights are positive, and they have the same barycenter \(x\). If (19) failed, their second-moment matrices, and hence their covariance matrices, would coincide. For a finite probability distribution with positive weights, the range of its covariance is exactly the direction space of the affine hull of its support: indeed, its quadratic form vanishes on a vector precisely when that vector is orthogonal to every support displacement from the barycenter. The positive and negative supports would therefore have the same affine direction space \(L\).

Every proper subset of a circuit is affinely independent. Hence the affine dimensions of the two sign supports are respectively one less than their cardinalities, while the affine dimension of the whole circuit is \(s-2\), the sum of those two dimensions. Equality of both direction spaces with \(L\) would instead put the whole circuit in \(x+L\). If \(\dim L>0\), its affine dimension would then be at most \(\dim L\), strictly less than twice \(\dim L=s-2\). If \(\dim L=0\), both sign supports would be singletons, so two distinct points would form an affine circuit, which is impossible. This proves (19).

Condition (3) is the nonvanishing condition
\[
 \langle A,S_{\mathcal C}\rangle_F\neq0.
\]
There are finitely many circuits, and by (19) each forbidden equality is a proper hyperplane in \(\mathbb S^n\). Their finite union has empty interior. Since \(\mathbb S_{++}^n\) is open, every sufficiently small Frobenius ball about \(A_0\) inside that cone meets the complement of all forbidden hyperplanes. This proves the perturbation assertion. If \(D\) has no circuit, the condition is vacuous.

Fix a feasible \(m\). Multiplying (4) by \(M\), the fixed-base problem is the finite linear program
\[
 \min\left\{
 \sum_{z\in D}b_zc_z:
 \sum_zb_z=M,
 \sum_zb_z(z-m)=0,
 0\leq b_z\leq a_z
 \right\},
 \qquad c_z:=(z-m)^{\mathsf T}A(z-m).
\tag{20}
\]
Its dual is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{\tau,v,(s_z)}\quad&M\tau-\sum_za_zs_z,\\
 \text{subject to}\quad&
 \tau+v^{\mathsf T}(z-m)-s_z\leq c_z\quad(z\in D),\\
 &s_z\geq0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Both optima are attained and equal by finite-dimensional linear programming duality. Choose an optimal dual solution and set
\[
 \rho_z:=c_z-\tau-v^{\mathsf T}(z-m)+s_z\geq0,
 \qquad
 \Theta(z):=\tau+v^{\mathsf T}(z-m)-c_z=s_z-\rho_z.
\tag{22}
\]
For any primal optimum \(b\), the exact primal-dual gap is
\[
 \sum_zb_zc_z-\left(M\tau-\sum_za_zs_z\right)
 =\sum_z b_z\rho_z+\sum_z(a_z-b_z)s_z.
\tag{23}
\]
Every summand on the right is nonnegative, and the gap is zero. Therefore
\[
 b_z\rho_z=0,qquad (a_z-b_z)s_z=0
\tag{24}
\]
for every \(z\). If \(\Theta(z)>0\), then \(s_z>0\), so (24) gives \(b_z=a_z\). If \(\Theta(z)<0\), then \(\rho_z>0\), so (24) gives \(b_z=0\). If \(0<b_z<a_z\), both complementary variables vanish, so \(\Theta(z)=0\).

Complete the square by putting
\[
 c:=m+\frac12A^{-1}v,
 \qquad
 R^2:=\tau+\frac14v^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}v.
\tag{25}
\]
Then
\[
 \Theta(z)=R^2-(z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c).
\tag{26}
\]
Since \(M>0\), some \(b_z>0\). For such a site, (24) gives \(\rho_z=0\), hence \(\Theta(z)=s_z\geq0\). Equation (26) then implies \(R^2\geq0\). Equations (6)-(8), as well as the assertion about fractional sites, now follow from (22)-(26).

Every \(z\in F\) satisfies an equation
\[
 z^{\mathsf T}Az+\ell^{\mathsf T}z=\kappa
\tag{27}
\]
for fixed \(\ell\) and \(\kappa\). If \(F\) were affinely dependent, it would contain an affine circuit. Multiplying (27) by the circuit coefficients and summing would cancel the affine terms and give
\[
 \sum_i\alpha_i z_i^{\mathsf T}Az_i=0,
\]
contrary to circuit-genericity. Hence \(F\) is affinely independent and (9) follows from \(F\subseteq\operatorname{aff}D\).

The same optimal dual solution can be paired with every primal optimum. Thus (6) forces every primal optimum to equal the capacity vector on \(I\), (7) forces it to vanish off \(I\cup F\), and two primal optima can differ only on \(F\). Their difference \((d_z)_{z\in F}\) obeys
\[
 \sum_{z\in F}d_z=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{z\in F}d_zz=0.
\]
Affine independence of \(F\) forces every \(d_z=0\). The fixed-base optimum is therefore unique. The mass and moment equations, after subtracting the saturated interior, give
\[
 \sum_{z\in F}b_z=B,
 \qquad
 \sum_{z\in F}b_zz=Mm-\sum_{z\in I}a_zz.
\tag{28}
\]
If \(B=0\), nonnegativity gives \(b_z=0\) on \(F\). If \(B>0\), division by \(B\) makes \((b_z/B)_{z\in F}\) a convex representation of (11). Its uniqueness is exactly (12). If \(F=\varnothing\), (28) forces \(B=0\). This proves every boundary and equality case claimed.

We now prove (13). Put \(\lambda_z=b_z/M\). These weights are positive exactly on the selected support and have barycenter \(m\). For every selected \(z\), (24) gives \(\Theta(z)=s_z\geq0\), so \((z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c)\leq R^2\). The quadratic barycenter identity is
\[
 \sum_z\lambda_z(z-c)^{\mathsf T}A(z-c)
 =(m-c)^{\mathsf T}A(m-c)+V_A(m,b).
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 (m-c)^{\mathsf T}A(m-c)
 \leq R^2-V_A(m,b)<R^2.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(m\) is strictly inside the full-dimensional ellipsoid, not merely in its relative interior. Feasibility and (2) give \(m\in D\), while (6) gives \(b_m=a_m\). If \(a_m\geq M\), the allocation concentrated with mass \(M\) at \(m\) belongs to \(B_m\) and has objective zero, contradicting positive optimal variance. Hence \(a_m<M\), completing (13).

For the selector formula, accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` proves that every fiber metronoid in (14) is nonempty, compact, convex, and contained in its fiber. At full mass it is the singleton \(\mathsf M_{a_z}(C_z)=\{g_z\}\). Conversely, the definition of a metronoid says exactly that choosing \(q_z\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)\) is equivalent to choosing a density between zero and one on \(C_z\), of mass \(b_z\), with normalized barycenter \(q_z\). Choices in distinct fibers are independent. Using (6)-(7) and summing their first moments proves the equality (14), including full or zero boundary masses.

It remains to prove the capacity bounds. Suppose first that \(F=\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\}\neq\varnothing\), where affine independence gives \(q=|F|-1\leq r\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d`, at full fiber mass, gives a centroid \(g_{v_i}\in C_{v_i}\) with absolute fiber depth at least \(\gamma_da_{v_i}\).

If \(q=0\), every closed ambient halfspace containing \((v_0,g_{v_0})\) receives from that fiber at least \(\gamma_da_{v_0}\); a vertical section containing the point contains the whole fiber, and a nonvertical section is a closed fiber halfspace containing the centroid. Hence
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_da_{v_0}.
\tag{31}
\]
If \(q\geq1\), apply accepted fact `5b6523682250e7a5` with one block, the standard lattice simplex
\[
 \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_q\}\subseteq\mathbb R^q,
\]
base map
\[
 \Phi(t)=v_0+\sum_{i=1}^qt_i(v_i-v_0),
\]
and vertex roots \((\gamma_da_{v_i})^{1/d}\). The differences \(v_i-v_0\) are linearly independent, and the only lattice points of \(\Delta\) are its vertices. The general product-simplex bound in that fact therefore gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq2^{-q}\sum_{i=0}^q\gamma_da_{v_i},
\]
which is (16). This also agrees with (31) when \(q=0\).

The strict-interior capacities are all selected, so
\[
 \sum_{z\in I}a_z\leq M.
\tag{32}
\]
When \(F\neq\varnothing\), (16) and \(q\leq r\) give
\[
 \sum_{z\in F}a_z\leq\frac{2^r}{\gamma_d}h_{\max}(C).
\tag{33}
\]
For empty \(F\), (33) is trivial. Adding (32) and (33) proves the first inequality in (17); subtracting from \(W\) proves the second. Substitution of \(M=2^{-n}W\) and the hypothesis in (18) proves (18).
