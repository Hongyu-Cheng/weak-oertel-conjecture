## Statement

Let $1/16\le a\le3/16$ and let $C(a)$ be the canonical convex hull of centered intervals at bases $-1,0,1$ of lengths $a,1,1$. Let $dm$ be original mixed length, $M=a+2$, and $\Sigma=(a^3+2)/(12M)$ the volume-weighted within-fiber variance. For $0<\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$ consider the arithmetic energy buffer of physical shift $\varepsilon\sqrt{\Sigma}\sqrt{LE/M}$ for unit continuous normals and unchanged pure-base caps. Among all continuous nonnegative concave $L$ on $C(a)$ with $\int L\,dm=M$, the unique minimizer of the maximum raw buffered depth is $L_a(z,x)=M(1+z)/3$. For this profile the unique deepest mark is $(0,0)$ and the raw maximum is $Q_\varepsilon(a)=a+1/2+\varepsilon D(a)$, where $D(a)=\sqrt{(a^3+2)/(36M)}$. The optimized normalized value $\beta_\varepsilon(a)=Q_\varepsilon(a)/M$ satisfies
$$
\beta_\varepsilon'(a)=\frac{3}{2M^2}\left[1+\varepsilon D(a)\left(\frac{a^2M}{a^3+2}-1\right)\right]
\ge \frac{3}{2M^2}(1-\varepsilon/6)>0.
$$
The actual nested path $C(a-t)$ for $t\ge0$ while $a-t$ stays in range holds the middle and right sampled fibers fixed and strictly lowers the optimized normalized value for $t>0$. All proper closed-minor quotas remain strict. In particular the whole-zero-fiber example at $a=1/8$ is excluded from joint body minimality for this objective.

## Proof

The compatible centered roof is $-1\le z\le1$, $2|x|\le1$, $2|x|\le1+(1-a)z$. The sampled intervals therefore have the stated lengths and $(0,0)$ is ambient interior. The variance formula follows by integrating $x^2$ over the three intervals and dividing by $M$. All singleton masses are less than $M/2$, and the two consecutive pairs have mass less than $M$; these are the nonempty proper closed subsets, with one and two occupied parities respectively.

Write $Y=(0,0)$. At $Y$ a finite affine supporting majorant of $L$ has the form $\ell+bz+cx$ with $\ell=L(Y)$. On the left endpoints its nonnegativity gives $b\le \ell-a|c|/2$. Its mixed mean is $\ell+(1-a)b/M$. Hence $1\le 3\ell/M$, with equality only for $b=\ell,c=0$ and equality of $L$ and the majorant almost everywhere on each sampled fiber. Continuity gives equality on the full sampled intervals; every point of $C(a)$ is a convex combination of these points, so concavity and the majorant extend it to all of $C(a)$. The profile $L_a$ is feasible and has $\ell=M/3$.

For a centered interval of length $v$, its projective energy at height $y$ is $v(1-4y^2/v^2)$. For an interior mark this follows by integrating $[1+b(x-y)]^{-2}$ over the interval and maximizing the denominator product $(1+b(-v/2-y))(1+b(v/2-y))$ in its admissible range. The product is a concave quadratic, maximized at $b=-y/(v^2/4-y^2)$ with both factors positive; the energy at either endpoint is zero. In particular the shift at $Y$ for $L_a$ is $\delta_*=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Sigma/3}=\varepsilon D(a)$.

For either continuous orientation at $Y$ and any shift $0\le\delta<1/2$, the own cap length is $1/2+\delta$. The endpoint thresholds have mean $-\delta$. With $g$ as in the interval clipping proof of c680a307d3f26cec, the endpoint infimum is
$$
\min\{a,\max(0,(a+1)/2+2\delta)\}=a.
$$
The proof there applies to thresholds of any prescribed mean, independently of the own interval; steep finite tilts fill the left endpoint and miss the right. A pure-base cap at $Y$ has at least $1+a$, so the center depth is exactly $a+1/2+\delta$. At any other middle mark $y$, use the orientation towards the nearer end and the same finite isolation of the left endpoint. Its depth is at most $a+1/2-|y|+\delta_*\sqrt{1-4y^2}$, strictly below the value at $Y$. At the left base a finite outward tilt bounds its maximum by $a/2$ because $L_a=0$ there. At the right base an outward tilt and the smaller continuous cap bound its maximum by $1/2+\varepsilon\sqrt{2\Sigma/3}$. Since $\Sigma\le1/12$ and $\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$, this is strictly below the value at $Y$ for $a\ge1/16$.

Any other feasible profile has $L(Y)>M/3$ and thus strictly larger shift at $Y$. Depth at a fixed mark is nondecreasing in its shift for every cap, with pure-base masses fixed. Apply the exact center formula to a shift strictly between $\delta_*$ and the new shift and smaller than $1/2$. This gives a strict increase, proving optimality over the full class.

Replace $a$ by $a-t$ in the roof inequalities. On $z\le0$ the right-hand roof can only decrease, and on $z\ge0$ it remains redundant to $2|x|\le1$. The sets are nested and only the left sampled interval shrinks. Removed sampled mass is supported on $L_a=0$, so the renormalized restriction is $(M-t)L_a/M=L_{a-t}$. The variance is recomputed on the new original sampled lengths. Differentiating the displayed optimized value gives the derivative, whose lower bound uses $D(a)\le1/6$ and $a^2M/(a^3+2)\ge0$. Since $a$ decreases along the path, the normalized value decreases strictly.
