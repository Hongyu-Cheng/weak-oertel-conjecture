## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Fix an integer \(M\ge1\), and put
$$
T_0(M)=2\log(2048M^6),\qquad C=512M^6,\qquad
c_M=\frac14\exp(-48C^2)>0.
$$
Let \(L\ge1\) and \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers. For each \(i\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, with positive weights \(w_{i,x}\). Assume each coordinatewise parity class contains at most \(M\) atoms of \(A_i\), and every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\) satisfies
$$
j=(x+y)/2\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^2=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
$$
Assume that for each factor there is a finite list \(T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,\ell_i}\), possibly empty, such that
$$
T_{i,1}\ge T_0(M),\qquad T_{i,b+1}\ge4T_{i,b}\quad(1\le b<\ell_i),
$$
when the indicated indices exist, and every distinct same-parity unequal-weight pair satisfies
$$
\left|\frac12\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\in\bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},3T_{i,b}/2].
$$
An empty list therefore requires that there be no unequal-weight same-parity pair. Bands containing no scores, equal-weight pairs, and factors with no unequal-weight pairs are allowed.

Keep the original normalizers, laws, and occupied parity counts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i,\\
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,&
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i.
\end{aligned}
$$
Extend these laws by zero off their supports. For every supported product center \(\mathbf j\), use the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its original-law moments:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,\\
M_r(P)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\qquad(r=2,3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_M\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_M^{3/2}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-3}.
$$
These constants are independent of all dimensions, original weights, occupied parity counts, band counts and bases, and \(L\). No full-dimensionality assumption is needed.

## Proof

We retain the original-law and reflected-row notation of accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705, 5e2dee70e6a96862, and 8a51b83b22717cf1. In particular, \(K_i\) is the actual occupied parity count, not the ambient capacity. We reproduce the scalar and product arguments so that no full-row result is applied to a selected row without justification.

Fix one factor and suppress its index. For every original center \(j\in A\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
Y_j(x)&=\tfrac12\log(p_x/p_{2j-x})\quad(x\in R_j),\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j),\\
b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)^2,&
F&=K^2b(p),\\
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace x\in R_j:w_x=w_j\rbrace,&
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},\\
\widehat\chi(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j),&
E&=K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
Thus \(E,F\) and the hats have exactly their meanings in 5e2dee70e6a96862 and 8a51b83b22717cf1; the full quantities and score agree with 445a9e668ee1a705. Every full or selected row below includes both orientations of each retained noncentral reflection orbit, and the central atom once.

The endpoints \(x,2j-x\) of a full row have the same parity. The midpoint hypothesis, or the trivial central identity, implies \(p_xp_{2j-x}=p_j^2\). Consequently every geometric row summand is \(p_j\). Equal endpoint weights are equivalent to both being equal to \(w_j\); hence \(\widehat R_j\) consists exactly of the zero-score orbits. It is symmetric about \(j\), contains \(j\), and has positive geometric mass.

Write \(\mathcal C_\eta\) for an occupied parity class in \(A\), and set
$$
\mu_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x,\qquad
R_\eta=K\mu_\eta,\qquad \rho_x=Kp_x,\qquad
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}=\sum_{\eta\text{ occupied}}(R_\eta-1)^2.
\tag{2}
$$
These are the same scaled masses and unnormalized defect as in the two accepted score-product facts. Since there are \(K\) occupied classes and \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), one has \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}=K^2\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2-K\).

The map \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) bijects full oriented row entries with ordered same-parity pairs, including the diagonal. Midpoint closure gives its inverse. Each contribution to \(\chi(p)\) is the product of the endpoint probabilities. Thus
$$
\chi(p)=\sum_\eta\mu_\eta^2\ge K^{-1},\qquad
F\ge K^2\chi(p)^2\ge1.
\tag{3}
$$
The first energy comparison is Jensen under the original law \(p\). The central row entries, \(|A|\le MK\), and Hölder give
$$
E\ge K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_j^3
\ge K^2/|A|^2\ge M^{-2}.
\tag{4}
$$

Let \(\mathscr Q_{\ne}\) be the set of all unordered distinct same-parity pairs with unequal weights. For \(e=\lbrace x,y\rbrace\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\), write \(j_e=(x+y)/2\) and \(s_e=\tfrac12|\log(p_x/p_y)|>0\). Define
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}}\rho_{j_e}^2.
\tag{5}
$$
This is the all-mixed-pair loss of 5e2dee70e6a96862, with its full multiplicities, and the same loss as 8a51b83b22717cf1 when occupancy is at most two. Removing both orientations of every mixed pair from (3) gives
$$
K\widehat\chi(p)=1+\frac{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}}{K},
\qquad E\ge\bigl(K\widehat\chi(p)\bigr)^2.
\tag{6}
$$
If there is no mixed pair, then \(E=F\ge1\). This handles every \(M=1\) factor. We therefore need a scalar label only when \(E<1\); assume this henceforth. Then \(0<K\widehat\chi(p)<1\), so \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\), and a nonempty band list is available.

Call an occupied class balanced when \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). In every balanced class fix one largest atom \(z_\eta\), breaking ties by lexicographic order. Form a star pair \(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) for each atom \(x\in\mathcal C_\eta\) with \(\rho_x<\rho_{z_\eta}\). Let \(\mathscr S\) be the resulting set of unordered pairs and put
$$
B_* =\sum_{e\in\mathscr S}\rho_{j_e}^2,\qquad
B_b=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr S\\s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]}}\rho_{j_e}^2.
\tag{7}
$$
The bands are disjoint, so \(B_* =\sum_bB_b\). The new \(B_*\) sums only largest-atom star pairs. It is not the all-balanced-pair sum \(\mathcal B_2\) used in 5e2dee70e6a96862 and 8a51b83b22717cf1.

We first prove the comparison that permits this change of sum. Orient any mixed pair in a balanced class so \(\rho_x<\rho_y\). Its smaller endpoint satisfies \(\rho_x<\rho_{z_\eta}\), and hence determines a star pair. Exact midpoint equality gives
\(\rho_{(x+y)/2}^2=\rho_x\rho_y\le\rho_x\rho_{z_\eta}=\rho_{(x+z_\eta)/2}^2\).
For a fixed smaller endpoint \(x\), there are at most \(M-1\) larger endpoints \(y\). Therefore
$$
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\\\text{source class balanced}}}\rho_{j_e}^2
\le M B_*.
\tag{8}
$$
This counts all unequal-weight pairs, including pairs whose larger endpoint is a different atom tied for the maximum. There is no star at an equal maximum, and none is needed because such an atom cannot be the strictly smaller endpoint of a mixed pair involving another maximum.

For an unbalanced source class, a mixed pair oriented with \(\rho_x<\rho_y\) satisfies
\(\rho_x/\rho_y=e^{-2s_e}\le e^{-2T_1}\). Hence \(\rho_{j_e}^2\le e^{-2T_1}R_\eta^2\). There are at most \(M(M-1)/2\) unordered pairs in a class, and an unbalanced class satisfies \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\). Combining this with (8) yields
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}
\le2M B_*+4M^2e^{-2T_1}\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}.
\tag{9}
$$
The threshold gives \(4M^2e^{-2T_1}\le4M^2/(2048M^6)^4\le1/2\). Together with \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}\), (9) implies
$$
B_*>0,\qquad \mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<4M B_*,\qquad
\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\le2M B_*.
\tag{10}
$$
Indeed \(B_*=0\) would force \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}<\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}/2\), impossible for a nonnegative defect. Put \(d=-\log E>0\). Equations (4), (6), and (10) then give, with \(a_{\mathrm{eq}}=K\widehat\chi(p)\in(0,1)\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-E&\le1-a_{\mathrm{eq}}^2\le2(1-a_{\mathrm{eq}})
=2(\Lambda_{\mathrm{loss}}-\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}})/K
\le4M B_*/K,\\
d&\le(1-E)/E\le M^2(1-E)\le4M^3 B_*/K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$

Next we prove that a star midpoint cannot have a balanced own parity class. Every balanced-class maximum satisfies
$$
1/(2M)\le \rho_{z_\eta}\le2.
\tag{12}
$$
Take a star \(e=\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) in band \(b\), and put \(j=j_e\). The exact geometric mean gives
\(\rho_j=\rho_{z_\eta}e^{-s_e}\le2e^{-T_b}\).
The thresholds satisfy
$$
T_b\ge T_0(M)>4\log(4M)>\log(4M),
\tag{13}
$$
because \(T_0(M)-4\log(4M)=14\log2+8\log M>0\).
If the class of \(j\) were balanced, its chosen maximum \(z_{\eta(j)}\) would have mass at least \(1/(2M)>\rho_j\). Thus \(\lbrace j,z_{\eta(j)}\rbrace\) would be a distinct same-parity mixed pair, whose score is
$$
s'=\frac{s_e}{2}+\frac12\log\frac{\rho_{z_{\eta(j)}}}{\rho_{z_\eta}}.
$$
Both maximum masses satisfy (12). With \(c=\tfrac12\log(4M)\), this places \(s'\) in
$$
[T_b/2-c,\,3T_b/4+c]\subset(3T_b/8,\,T_b).
\tag{14}
$$
The strict lower inclusion follows from \(T_b>4\log(4M)=8c\); the upper inclusion follows from the weaker \(T_b>4c\). Any earlier band has upper endpoint at most \(3T_b/8\), since its base is at most \(T_b/4\). The current and later bands start at or above \(T_b\). Thus the positive score \(s'\) is in none of the allowed bands, a contradiction. When \(b=1\), there are simply no earlier bands. Closed band endpoints cause no exception because (14) is strict.

For each band let \(J_b\) be the set of its distinct star midpoints. By (14), every class meeting \(J_b\) is unbalanced, and each such class contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\). There are therefore at most \(4\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) such classes and at most \(4M\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) distinct atoms in \(J_b\). Each of these atoms has mass at most \(2e^{-T_b}\), so (10) gives
$$
\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j
\le8M e^{-T_b}\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}
\le32M^2e^{-T_b}B_*.
\tag{15}
$$
Only distinct target classes and distinct target atoms are counted in this estimate. Source pairs sharing a midpoint have not been charged separately to its class defect. A target class may also be a source class; (9) and (15) are separate bounds using the same total defect, and are not added as disjoint defect charges.

Define a whole-factor row selection for band \(b\) by
$$
R_{b,j}=\widehat R_j\cup
\lbrace x\in R_j:|Y_j(x)|\in[T_b,3T_b/2]\rbrace
\qquad(j\in A).
\tag{16}
$$
This retains all zero-score orbits and all mixed orbits in this band, including those with unbalanced source classes. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{b,j}}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
F_b&=K^2\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_b(j)^2,&G_b&=F_b-E,\\
\kappa_{b,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_b(j)},&
q_{b,j}&=\frac{K^2p_jH_b(j)^2}{F_b},\\
V_{b,j}&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{b,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_b&=\sum_{j\in A}q_{b,j}V_{b,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
$$
Each row contains its central atom, so its mass and \(F_b\) are positive and these auxiliary laws are defined. They leave \(p\), \(Z\), and all original centers unchanged.

Let \(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}=|\widehat R_j|\), let \(r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) count all mixed unordered pairs in band \(b\) centered at \(j\), and let \(r_{b,j}\) count only those belonging to \(\mathscr S\). At a fixed center, distinct unordered pairs are disjoint reflection orbits: a given endpoint has a unique reflected mate. Thus
\(|R_{b,j}|=h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) and \(r_{b,j}\le r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\).
Since every retained summand equals \(p_j\), the exact selected-row expansion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_b&=\frac1K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
\left((h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})^2-h_{\mathrm{eq},j}^2\right)\\
&\ge\frac4K\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3,
\qquad B_b=\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}\rho_j^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
In particular \(G_b\ge0\). If \(B_b>0\), then \(J_b\) is nonempty and its mass sum is positive. Cauchy--Schwarz on the distinct centers, using the entries \(r_{b,j}\rho_j^{3/2}\) and \(\rho_j^{1/2}\), together with (15) and (18), gives
$$
B_b^2\le\left(\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}^2\rho_j^3\right)
\left(\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\right),\qquad
G_b\ge\frac{e^{T_b}B_b^2}{8M^2K B_*}>0.
\tag{19}
$$
The pair multiplicities are in \(r_{b,j}\), including its square in the gain. The estimate does not assert that selected rows form a midpoint-closed support.

We now select one band. The separation gives \(T_b\ge T_1+3T_1(b-1)\): each successive increment is at least \(3T_1\). Put \(q=e^{-T_1/4}\). Then \(q\le(2048M^6)^{-1/2}<1/2\), and
$$
\sum_b e^{-T_b/4}\le\sum_{b=1}^{\infty}q^{1+3(b-1)}
=\frac{q}{1-q^3}<\frac47<1.
\tag{20}
$$
Since \(B_* >0\) and \(\sum_bB_b=B_*\), there is a band satisfying
$$
B_b\ge e^{-T_b/4}B_*>0.
\tag{21}
$$
Otherwise summing the strict reverse inequalities would contradict (20). Empty bands cannot satisfy (21), so they do not affect the choice.

For this band put \(\theta=T_b/2\), \(F_*=F_b\), \(G_*=G_b\), and \(\nu_*=\nu_b\). The full-row quantity \(F\) from (1) is not redefined. Equations (11), (19), and (21) imply
$$
G_*\ge\frac{e^{T_b/2}B_*}{8M^2K},\qquad
d\le32M^5e^{-\theta}G_*.
\tag{22}
$$
There are at most \(K(M-1)\le MK\) star pairs, and each star in band \(b\) has squared midpoint mass at most \(4e^{-2T_b}\). Hence \(B_b\le4MK e^{-2T_b}\). Combining this with (11) and (21) yields the separate absolute deficit estimate
$$
d\le4M^3 e^{T_b/4}B_b/K
\le16M^4e^{-7T_b/4}
=16M^4e^{-7\theta/2}.
\tag{23}
$$

We prove \(F_*>1\) and the two label estimates needed for product selection. Here \(\theta\ge\log(2048M^6)\). If \(0<G_*\le1\), then (22) gives \(d\le G_*/(64M)\le G_*/64\). As \(1-E\le-\log E=d\),
\(F_*=E+G_*\ge1+63G_*/64\ge1+G_*/2>1\).
Consequently \(g=\log F_*\ge G_*/4\), by integrating \((1+u)^{-1}\ge1/2\) on \([0,G_*/2]\). Equation (22) gives
$$
d\le128M^5e^{-\theta}g\le C e^{-\theta}g.
\tag{24}
$$
If \(G_*\ge1\), (4) instead gives \(F_*\ge1+M^{-2}>1\) and
\(g\ge\log(1+M^{-2})\ge1/(M^2+1)\ge1/(2M^2)\).
Using (23),
$$
d\le16M^4e^{-7\theta/2}
\le32M^6e^{-7\theta/2}g
\le C e^{-\theta}g.
\tag{25}
$$
The last comparison uses \(\theta\ge0\) and \(32M^6\le C\). This covers the shared boundary \(G_*=1\) as well. In all cases
$$
F_*>1,\qquad d\le C e^{-\theta}g\le g/4,
\tag{26}
$$
because \(C e^{-\theta}\le512M^6/(2048M^6)=1/4\).

For the selected score price, every nonzero score has magnitude at most \(3T_b/2=3\theta\). The row law in (17) is uniform, and its nonzero scores occur in opposite pairs. Therefore its exact energy-weighted second moment is
$$
F_*\nu_*=
\frac2K\sum_{j\in A}\rho_j^3
(h_{\mathrm{eq},j}+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_{\ne}\\j_e=j,\ s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]}}s_e^2.
\tag{27}
$$
For \(h\ge1\) and \(r\ge0\),
\(2r(h+2r)\le(h+2r)^2-h^2\), since their difference is \(2hr\ge0\).
Apply this rowwise to (27) and (18). It gives \(F_*\nu_*\le9\theta^2G_*\). Also \(G_*/F_*=1-E/F_*\le\log(F_*/E)=g+d\), by \(1-u\le-\log u\) for \(u>0\). Thus (26) proves
$$
\nu_*\le9\theta^2(g+d)
\le\frac{45}{4}\theta^2g\le12\theta^2g\le C\theta^2g.
\tag{28}
$$
This is a direct computation under the original probabilities at all centers, not an application of a bounded-score theorem to a selected table.

Return to all factors. If \(E_i\ge1\), choose all its same-weight rows. Their scaled logarithmic energy is nonnegative and their score price is zero. Every other factor has the label \((d_i,g_i,\theta_i)\) just proved, with two available selections: all same-weight rows, of scaled energy \(e^{-d_i}\) and price zero; or the one selected band together with all same-weight rows, of scaled energy \(e^{g_i}\) and price at most \(C\theta_i^2g_i\).

We use the binning mechanism of 8a51b83b22717cf1 and 5e2dee70e6a96862, with the following explicit map. The occupied counts \(K_i\) are unchanged. The label scale is the present \(\theta_i=T_{i,b_i}/2\), and the label constant is \(C=512M^6\). Retaining a label here chooses its selected-band row system, in place of the full-row system of those predecessors. Equations (26) and (28) prove precisely the needed label bounds for this new choice. There is no additional bounded-score group.

For each integer \(m\ge0\), let \(I_m\) be the set of labels with \(\theta_i\in[m,m+1)\), and put \(G_m=\sum_{i\in I_m}g_i\). Retain every label in this bin if \(G_m\ge4C(m+1)^2\); otherwise choose the same-weight rows for the whole bin. There are finitely many nonempty bins. For a retained bin put \(W_m=C(m+1)^2G_m\). Its total price is at most \(W_m\), and the retention inequality gives \(G_m\ge2\sqrt{W_m}\). For a discarded bin, its total deficit satisfies
$$
\sum_{i\in I_m}d_i\le Ce^{-m}G_m
<4C^2e^{-m}(m+1)^2.
\tag{29}
$$
Empty bins contribute zero. The numerical series obeys
$$
\sum_{m\ge0}e^{-m}(m+1)^2
\le\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m}(m+1)^2=12,
\tag{30}
$$
where the final equality follows by differentiating the geometric series twice, or from \(\sum_{m\ge0}(m+1)^2r^m=(1+r)/(1-r)^3\). Hence the total discarded deficit is at most \(48C^2\).

Denote the final chosen row at center \(j\in A_i\) by \(R_{i,j}^*\), and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_i^*(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}},&
b_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2,\\
\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_i^*(j)},&
q_{i,j}^*&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_i^*(j)^2}{b_i^*},\\
V_{i,j}^*&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}^2,&
\nu_i^*&=\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}^*V_{i,j}^*,\qquad
S_g=\sum_i\log(K_i^2b_i^*).
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
$$
Here the score is the original score restricted to the chosen row. These are the selected-row quantities of the two score-product predecessors, now with the selected-band option specified above. Since each row contains its original center, all denominators are positive and every original center remains in (31). The bin estimates give
$$
S_g\ge\sum_{m\text{ retained}}G_m-48C^2,
\qquad
\sum_i\nu_i^*\le\sum_{m\text{ retained}}W_m.
\tag{32}
$$
For finitely many nonnegative numbers, \(\sqrt{\sum_mW_m}\le\sum_m\sqrt{W_m}\). Using the retained-bin bounds in (32) yields
$$
S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\ge-48C^2.
\tag{33}
$$
This includes the cases of no labels, no retained bins, and no mixed pairs in any factor.

It remains to relate these quantities to actual original-product halfspaces. We give the argument using the reflection convention of 445a9e668ee1a705. Each selected row is symmetric about its center. Reflection preserves its geometric law and negates its score, so \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}^*}Y_{i,j}=0\). For every selected endpoint the exact likelihood identity is
$$
p_{i,x}=H_i^*(j)\kappa_{i,j}^*(x)e^{Y_{i,j}(x)}=w_{i,x}/Z_i.
\tag{34}
$$
In particular the selected energy retains the full original normalization:
$$
b_i^*=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}^*}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{35}
$$
For every supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), form
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}^*&=\prod_iR_{i,j_i}^*,&
H_P^*(\mathbf j)&=\prod_iH_i^*(j_i),&
\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*&=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}^*,\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(X)&=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i),&
\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2&=\sum_iV_{i,j_i}^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{36}
$$
Finite geometric-mean sums show that \(H_P^*(\mathbf j)\) is the geometric mass of this product row. Independence and the zero score means imply \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2\). Multiplying (34) gives
\(P_X=H_P^*(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*(X)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(X)}\), with the original \(P_X=\prod_iw_{i,X_i}/\prod_iZ_i\).

Any closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace X,2\mathbf j-X\rbrace\). Indeed, writing the halfspace as an affine inequality, strict exclusion of both endpoints would strictly exclude their midpoint. It also contains the central atom. Simultaneous reflection preserves the selected product row and its geometric law, and negates \(Y_{\mathbf j}\). Summing the smaller original mass in every noncentral orbit, using half the available central mass, and then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_{\mathbf j}^*}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2\mathbf j-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}^*}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P^*(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral pair twice before the factor \(1/2\), and the central atom once. Thus boundary atoms, singleton rows, zero variance, and lower-dimensional supports are all included.

Let \(q^*=\bigotimes_iq_i^*\). By (31) and (36),
$$
P_{\mathbf j}H_P^*(\mathbf j)^2
=\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\prod_iq_{i,j_i}^*,\qquad
\mathbb E_{q^*}\sigma_{\mathbf j}^2=\sum_i\nu_i^*.
$$
Square (37) and sum under the original \(P\). Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under the auxiliary center law then show
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)\mathbb E_{q^*}e^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^*\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_iK_i^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(S_g-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^*}\right)
\ge c_M\prod_iK_i^{-2},
\end{aligned}
$$
using (33). Finally Jensen under the same original law gives
\(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\), proving the stated third-moment bound.

Only selected reflection masses and auxiliary laws factor in this proof. No tensorization identity for actual halfspace depths or their moments has been asserted. Every original probability, its normalizer, and every center remains unchanged throughout.
