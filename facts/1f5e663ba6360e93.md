## Statement

Let $I_{-1}=I_1=[-1/2,1/2]$, $I_0=[-11/20,19/20]$, and $J=[-1/2,1/2]$. Take the convex hull of the lifted rectangles $I_j\times J$ at integer bases $j=-1,0,1$, then pad the base by $[0,1]^{n-1}$ for any $n\ge1$. The actual integer fibers are the stated rectangles in every binary copy, with total area $M=2^{n-1}(7/2)$ and $k=2^n$ occupied parities. Every fiber has area strictly less than $M/k$.

For any choice of marks in these fibers whose graph contains the midpoint of every pair of marks on congruent bases modulo two, the sum of their raw ordinary own depths is at most $2^{n-1}(31/20)<(4/9)M$. The midpoint-closed interval construction obtained by omitting $J$ at $n=1$ has exact maximum total own depth $31/20<7/4$. In contrast, the rectangle source has an actual point of raw mixed depth at least $33/40>7/9=(4/9)M/k$ in each binary copy. The corresponding point in the interval source has raw mixed depth exactly $33/20$.

## Proof

The hull projects to $[-1,1]$. At either endpoint the lifted fiber is extreme. At base zero any convex combination with nonzero endpoint contribution has equal endpoint weights, so that contribution, after dividing by its total coefficient, lies in the endpoint Minkowski midpoint $I_{-1}\times J$, a subset of $I_0\times J$. This proves the exact fiber list, and the same argument applies with $J$ omitted. Padding adds precisely the $2^{n-1}$ binary copies. The original rectangle areas are $1,3/2,1$, since $J$ has length one, so $M/k=7/4$.

In each binary copy, write $t_j$ for the first coordinate of the mark at $j$. Its endpoint marks have congruent bases, and midpoint closure requires $2t_0=t_{-1}+t_1$. Put $e_{-1}=t_{-1}$, $e_0=t_0-1/5$ and $e_1=t_1$. The sum of their absolute values is at least $1/5$, since $|2e_0-e_{-1}-e_1|=2/5$. Each own depth is at most the smaller cap area among the two coordinate cuts through the mark. These upper bounds are $1/2-|e_{-1}|$, $3/4-|e_0|$, and $1/2-|e_1|$. Their sum is at most $7/4-1/5=31/20$, which is $1/180$ below $(4/9)(7/2)=14/9$. Summing over the binary copies proves the bound in every integer dimension. Any smaller certified charges satisfy the same bound. For the interval source the smaller cap length is the actual own depth, and all three $t_j=0$ attain $31/20$.

For the actual-depth comparison on the interval source, apply c680a307d3f26cec with endpoint lengths $a=b=1$, middle length $U=3/2$ and middle shift $s=1/5$. These parameters satisfy the realizability inequalities. Its middle-depth formula at $t=1/10$ yields both orientation minima $33/20$, and the same theorem includes pure base normals. Now take the point with that base and height in a fixed binary copy and added continuous coordinate zero. In any closed ambient cap containing the point, restrict the binary coordinates to its copy and slice by the added coordinate $q\in J$. On the half of $J$ where the term from the added coordinate is nonnegative, the remaining cap in the interval variables contains $(0,1/10)$. If the remaining normal vanishes, that slice contains the entire interval source, which also suffices. Integration over this half of $J$ gives raw mass at least $33/40$. If the added coefficient vanishes, all of $J$ contributes. The bound uses one fixed copy, so it survives padding.
