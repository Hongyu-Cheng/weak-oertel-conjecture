## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let $B$ be the unit ball of volume $V$ in $\mathbb R^d$, and use summed $d$-volume on the integer fibers of $C=[0,1]^2\times B$. The mixed maximum raw depth is $V/2$. The full-dimensional ambient raw level-$V$ depth region projects onto the medial diamond of the square. Its projection meets both boundary lines of the maximal lattice-free split $0\le z_2\le1$.

At the lower contact $Y=((1/2,0),0)$ and its reflection at the upper boundary, there are mass-$V$ supporting cap groups with only null $(d-1)$-dimensional sections on all sampled fibers. Each group is a minimal positive circuit of rank one, and there is also such a pair of groups of full continuous rank $d$. The bases over which the group unions cover the whole continuous plane are exactly the lower and upper exteriors of the split, respectively. In particular together they cover the entire mixed support. A pure-base halfspace preserving even one group's sampled coverage has mass at least $2V$. Thus a two-group bound charging each null-section group one cap budget would give $4V\le2V$, which is false.

## Proof

All four integer bases are exposed vertices. At each, a sufficiently large multiple of an exposing base linear functional isolates a chosen own-fiber cap through a prescribed mark. Conversely any ambient cap through that mark pays at least its own-fiber depth, so the mixed maximum is the ordinary maximum $V/2$ of a centered unit ball.

Let $D$ denote the ambient depth region at level $V$. A point outside $C$ has a mass-zero separating cap. Depth superlevels are convex because a halfspace containing a convex combination contains at least one of the participating points. Reflection in $x=0$ preserves $D$, so a base in its projection also has $(z,0)\in D$. Each edge midpoint at $x=0$ has depth at least $V$ by the centrally symmetric pair of adjacent balls. Conversely at any $z\in[0,1]^2$ outside the diamond there is a binary vertex $v$ whose affine Hamming distance $q_v(z)$ is less than $1/2$. With $s=q_v(z)$ the through-cap
$$
u\cdot x\ge \frac{q_v(z')-s}{1-s},\qquad \|u\|=1,
$$
at $(z,0)$ takes strictly less than $V$ at $v$, only null or empty traces at the other bases, and proves the projection claim.

For full dimension consider three binary bases and their barycenter $g$. For a nonvertical cap through $(g,x)$ with unit continuous normal $u$, the three thresholds $b_i$ on those balls satisfy $\sum_i b_i=3u\cdot x$. If any pair has sum at most zero, the two ball tails sum to at least $V$ by symmetry. Otherwise $\sum_i b_i>0$, and every $b_i<\sum_i b_i$ because the other pair has positive sum. Choose $\delta>0$ so small that a unit-ball cap at threshold $3\delta$ has volume greater than $V/3$. For $\|x\|<\delta$ all three tails then sum to more than $V$ in this second case. A pure-base cap through $g$ contains at least one of the three bases and its full ball. Tightening any containing cap to the marked point proves that $D$ contains a continuous ball over $g$. Together with the edge midpoints at continuous coordinate zero this gives full ambient dimension. The open strip has no lattice point. Any convex enlargement containing a point beyond either bounding line makes a lattice point on that line interior, since that lattice point is a strict convex combination of the added point and some point in the original open strip. Hence the split is maximal lattice-free.

At $Y$ take the two margins $\pm[u\cdot x-2(z_1-1/2)]-M z_2$ with $\|u\|=1$ and $M>2$. Each captures one whole bottom ball, at most a tangency point of the other bottom ball, and none of the top balls. Raising its threshold slightly loses positive volume, so a point of $D$ cannot have strictly positive margin. Both margins vanish at $Y$, and the sum is $-2M z_2$. For any base their two margins can be made equal by varying $u\cdot x$; hence the set of bases at which the union covers the whole continuous plane is exactly $\{z_2\le0\}$.

For rank $d$ use $u_i=e_i$, $a_i=-2$ for $1\le i\le d$, and $u_{d+1}=-\sum_i e_i$, $a_{d+1}=2d$. Take the $d+1$ margins
$$
u_j\cdot x+a_j(z_1-1/2)-M z_2,\qquad M\ge4d+4.
$$
The first $d$ capture the whole ball at $(0,0)$, at most a tangent point at $(1,0)$, and no top points. The last captures the whole ball at $(1,0)$ strictly and misses all other balls, since $d>\sqrt d$ for $d\ge2$. All their fiber sections are empty or supporting singletons, hence null in dimension $d-1$. The normals have the unique relation proportional to their positive sum, and both the continuous and $z_1$ coefficients sum to zero. For any $z$, set all coordinates of $x$ to $2(z_1-1/2)$ to make all margins equal to $-M z_2$. Together with their sum this gives the same exact set of whole-plane coverage bases.

For support of the rank-$d$ caps, the diamond gives $|z_1-1/2|\le z_2$ on $D$. For every nonzero $w$ the margin $w\cdot x-2\|w\|z_2$ cuts the two bottom balls in halves and excludes the top balls. Its mass is $V$, and an inward threshold increase loses positive bottom volume. Thus $w\cdot x\le2\|w\|z_2$ on $D$. Substituting $w=u_j$ bounds each displayed rank-$d$ margin by $(2\|u_j\|+|a_j|-M)z_2\le0$ there, while all vanish at $Y$.

Reflect across $z_2=1/2$ for the upper group. A pure-base replacement that retains either group's sampled coverage must contain both of its assigned bases, so it pays at least $2V$. The reflected pair covers the sampled set, whose mass is $4V$.
