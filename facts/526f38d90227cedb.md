## Statement

For a positive probability law \(p\) on a finite nonempty support \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\), define its actual closed-halfspace depth and third moment by \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(H)\) and \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\), where the infimum is over proper closed halfspaces and \(p(H)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in A\cap H}p_{\mathbf x}\). Every boundary atom is included. For each occupied level \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), write \(A_j=\{y:(j,y)\in A\}\), \(\pi_j=p(\{j\}\times A_j)\), and \(p_j(y)=p(j,y)/\pi_j\). Conditional depths are computed in the residual ambient space, with the same closed-halfspace convention.

Take \(m=1\), \(A=\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,1)\}\), and the original probabilities \(p_{\mathbf z}=1/3\) for every \(\mathbf z\in A\). This support is complete, meaning \(A=\operatorname{conv}(A)\cap\mathbb Z^2\), and \(p_{\mathbf z}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}/\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}\) with the finite affine parameter \(\boldsymbol\theta=(0,0)\). Set \(\kappa=1/4\). Then every slice satisfies \(M_3(p_j)=1\ge\kappa8^{-m}=1/32\), but
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p)-\sum_{j=0}^2\pi_j^4M_3(p_j)&=0,\\
\kappa8^{-m}\left(\frac18-\sum_{j=0}^2\pi_j^4\right)_+
&=\frac{19}{6912}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the proposed universal compensation inequality fails under all its stated hypotheses.

## Proof

Write \(\mathbf a=(0,0)\), \(\mathbf b=(1,0)\), and \(\mathbf c=(2,1)\). These points are affinely independent, since the determinant of \((\mathbf b-\mathbf a,\mathbf c-\mathbf a)\) is one. Their convex hull is exactly
\[
\operatorname{conv}(A)
=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:y\ge0,\ x-2y\ge0,\ 1-x+y\ge0\}.
\]
Indeed, the three expressions \(1-x+y\), \(x-2y\), and \(y\) are the barycentric coefficients at \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\), respectively: they sum to one and reconstruct \((x,y)\). On this triangle \(0\le x\le2\). At integer \(x=0\), the inequalities force \(y=0\). At integer \(x=1\), they give \(0\le y\le1/2\), so the only integer value is \(y=0\). At integer \(x=2\), they force \(y=1\). Therefore the entire lattice intersection is exactly \(A\), proving completeness. The constant parameter \(\boldsymbol\theta=(0,0)\) gives exactly the stated original probabilities.

The following table exhausts every possible halfspace trace on \(A\). Each listed inequality defines a proper closed halfspace, and substitution of the three points gives the indicated trace, including all boundary atoms.

| Trace on \(A\) | Closed halfspace inequality |
| --- | --- |
| \(\varnothing\) | \(y\ge2\) |
| \(\{\mathbf a\}\) | \(y-x\ge0\) |
| \(\{\mathbf b\}\) | \(x-2y\ge1\) |
| \(\{\mathbf c\}\) | \(y\ge1\) |
| \(\{\mathbf a,\mathbf b\}\) | \(y\le0\) |
| \(\{\mathbf a,\mathbf c\}\) | \(x-2y\le0\) |
| \(\{\mathbf b,\mathbf c\}\) | \(x-y\ge1\) |
| \(A\) | \(y\ge0\) |

There are precisely eight subsets of a three-point support, so no trace is omitted. For any root \(\mathbf z\in A\), every containing closed halfspace includes its original atom of mass \(1/3\). The singleton trace in the table attains this lower bound. Hence \(\tau_p(\mathbf a)=\tau_p(\mathbf b)=\tau_p(\mathbf c)=1/3\), and direct summation with the original root probabilities gives \(M_3(p)=3(1/3)(1/3)^3=1/27\).

The residual slice supports are \(A_0=A_1=\{0\}\) and \(A_2=\{1\}\). Consequently \(\pi_0=\pi_1=\pi_2=1/3\), and each \(p_j\) is a unit point mass. Every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R\) containing that point has conditional mass one; therefore its conditional depth and \(M_3(p_j)\) are both one. All slice hypotheses hold because \(0<\kappa=1/4\le1/4\) and \(1\ge(1/4)8^{-1}=1/32\).

Finally, \(\sum_{j=0}^2\pi_j^4M_3(p_j)=\sum_{j=0}^2\pi_j^4=3(1/3)^4=1/27\), so the exact surplus is zero. The positive-part argument is \(1/8-1/27=19/216>0\), and multiplication by \(\kappa8^{-m}=1/32\) gives \(19/6912\). This is a strict failure of the stated inequality. All depths, conditional laws, and moments above use the original finite probabilities and all containing closed halfspaces.
