## Statement

Set $A=(2,-1), B=(-1,2), C=(-1,-1)$, $T=\operatorname{conv}(A,B,C)$, $V=9/2$, $\sigma=1/100$, $D_\sigma=(1-\sigma)A+\sigma T$, and $v=V\sigma^2$. Take the actual fibers $T$ at base zero and $D_\sigma$ at both bases $\pm1$. For the buffer-free outward uniform ramp of width $0<h\le1/100000$ in the pooled within-fiber covariance norm $\Gamma$, there is a unique deepest mark $Y=(0,y)$, with raw maximum $2\le Q\le2+\delta$, $|y_i|\le2\delta$, $\delta=v+4h\le49/100000$. Every active cap has continuous normal $u$ which, after scaling to have maximum vertex value $2$, has other vertex values $-1\pm s$ with $|s|<1/4$. In the cone at $A$, each active cap captures exactly one entire endpoint fiber and has zero outside ramp mass on the other. In the cones at $B,C$, it has zero endpoint ramp mass and its physical positive halfspaces exclude both endpoint interiors. All endpoint strip coareas vanish. Every positively balanced active menu uses all three cones, so no such menu has a common interior target on either exposed endpoint fiber.

## Proof

Since $D_\sigma\subset T$ and the endpoints agree, the lifted convex hull has the three stated actual integer fibers. Its pooled within-fiber covariance is $t_\sigma\Sigma_T$, where
$$
t_\sigma=\frac{1+2\sigma^4}{1+2\sigma^2}\le1,\qquad
\Sigma_T=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/4\\-1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
These entries follow by averaging the three vertex outer products with coefficient $1/12$ for the uniform centroid-zero triangle; the endpoint covariances scale by $\sigma^2$. In each unit continuous direction the extra strip width is at most $h\sqrt3/2$, so the extra own area of $T$ is at most $\operatorname{diam}(T)h\sqrt3/2<4h$, and that of $D_\sigma$ is less than $4\sigma h$.

At base zero, a sufficiently large finite tilt fully captures one endpoint and misses the other, with unchanged own mass, giving an upper bound of ordinary triangle depth plus $\delta$. The ordinary maximum is $2$, and centroid Grünbaum gives depth at least $2$ at the middle centroid, even after adding endpoint and ramp masses. At an endpoint use a finite tilt isolating that base with any chosen own direction; its depth is at most $2\sigma^2+4\sigma h<2$. Thus deepestness is in the middle and $2\le Q\le2+\delta$. A deepest mark there has ordinary depth at least $2-\delta$. The three normals $e_1,e_2,-e_1-e_2$ yield areas $V(2-u\cdot y)^2/9$ when $u\cdot y\ge-1$ (the cut isolates the top vertex). Therefore each of the three values is at most $2-\sqrt{4-2\delta}\le\delta$; values below $-1$ already meet this bound. Summing the three values gives $|y_i|\le2\delta$.

The center region given by these bounds has a fixed own inball and proper own strips for every direction. Pure-base caps there have mass at least $V+v>Q$. With $\Gamma(u)=1$, clip the scalar tilt to a fixed interval beyond which both endpoint ramp traces are saturated. Continuity then gives a deepest mark and a finite active nonvertical cap. The deepest superlevel within base zero is convex, since for fixed tilt and normal each ramp tail is nonincreasing in $u\cdot y$. If two marks were deepest, take a minimizing cap at their midpoint. The threshold is the average of the thresholds at the two marks. At any strictly greater threshold its mass decreases, since its own strip is proper at the midpoint. Thus both marks must have the same threshold for this cap and it minimizes at both. To see that this forces the same mark, use the unnormalized normal parameters $(a,u)$: a minimizing pair is an unconstrained local minimum for $u\ne0$ because the objective is homogeneous of degree zero under positive scaling. Write $r=[a(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)]/(h\Gamma(u))$ and integrate the ramp $\min(1,\max(0,1+r))$. Its kink planes are null in each fiber. Differentiation in $u$ at the minimum gives
$$
y=\frac{\int_{-1<r<0}(X-hr\nabla\Gamma(u))\,d\mu}{\int_{-1<r<0}d\mu}.
$$
The denominator is positive from the own strip. Equal thresholds for the same cap give the same right side. Hence the deepest mark is unique.

For a direction scaled to maximum vertex value $2$, the other values are $-1\pm s$. At zero the cap area is $18/(9-s^2)$ for $|s|\le1$. For $|s|\ge1$, the complementary vertex cap has fraction $(|s|+1)^2/[2|s|(|s|+3)]\le1/2$, giving area at least $9/4$ on the positive side. Thus outside the three cones $|s|<1/4$, the ordinary cap at zero has area at least $2+2/143$. Moving to $y$ costs at most $\operatorname{diam}(T)|y|\le12\delta$, and ramp and outside masses are nonnegative. Since $13\delta<2/143$, such a cap cannot be active.

Represent the three cones by $U_A=(1+s/3,2s/3)$, $U_B=(2s/3,1+s/3)$, $U_C=(-1+s/3,-1-s/3)$. For $u$ in $B$ or $C$, $\max_{D_\sigma}u\cdot(X-y)\le-(3/4)(1-\sigma)+2\sigma+4\delta<-7/10$. At tilt zero both endpoint ramps vanish, and the ramp width in these representatives is less than $2h$. Since tilt does not change own mass, every active cap in these cones has zero outside ramp mass. This excludes positive margins on endpoint interiors by continuity and positive fiber area. For $u$ in $A$, $\min_{D_\sigma}u\cdot(X-y)\ge2(1-\sigma)-(5/4)\sigma-4\delta>19/10$. At every tilt one endpoint has nonnegative base term and is captured fully. Large tilt achieves exactly this outside mass $v$; every active cap in $A$ must achieve it. All these outside ramp traces have zero strip measure, hence no outside response coarea.

Any two cones lie in an open halfspace, as witnessed respectively for $A,B$, $A,C$, $B,C$ by the vectors $(1,1),(1,-2),(-2,1)$ with all dot products at least $3/4$. Therefore a positive balance needs all three. Balanced active menus do exist: with compact clipped parameters, the mark derivatives of active caps are strictly negative scalar multiples of their $u$'s, by the proper own strips. If zero were outside the convex hull of these gradients, separation, continuity and compactness would give a direction increasing every near-active cap mass to first order and preserving the gap for the other caps, contradicting deepestness. Carathéodory gives a finite balance, and rescaling each vector by its positive strip factor transfers between quantile and response balances. Every such menu contains a $B$ cap and a $C$ cap, each of which excludes both endpoint interiors. The endpoints are the only exposed vertices of the complete base hull.
