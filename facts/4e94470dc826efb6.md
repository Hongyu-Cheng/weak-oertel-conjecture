## Statement

For every integer \(n\geq36000\), set \(m=n\) and \(d=n\), and define the actual body and locked mixed set by

\[
\begin{aligned}
C&=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n:
 |x_i|\leq m,\quad |x_i|/m\leq y_i\leq2\quad(1\leq i\leq n)\},\\
S&=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^n),\qquad
D_+=\{-m,-m+1,\ldots,m\}^n.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(z\in D_+\), put \(t_{z,i}=|z_i|/m\). Write \(C_z=\{y:(z,y)\in C\}\), let \(c_z\) be its actual centroid, and let \(w_z=\operatorname{vol}_n(C_z)\). Then \(C\) is a full-dimensional compact rational convex polytope, its complete positive integer support is \(D_+\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_z&=\prod_{i=1}^n[t_{z,i},2]
     =c_z+T_z[-1/2,1/2]^n,\\
c_{z,i}&=1+t_{z,i}/2,\qquad
T_z=\operatorname{diag}(2-t_{z,1},\ldots,2-t_{z,n}),\\
w_z&=\prod_{i=1}^n(2-t_{z,i}),\qquad
W:=\mathcal H_n(S)=\sum_{z\in D_+}w_z=(3m+1)^n,\qquad
w_{\max}=2^n.
\end{aligned}
\]

The normalized original base law is \(p_z=w_z/W\). Its coordinates are independent, each having probability \((2-|k|/m)/(3m+1)\) at \(k\in\{-m,\ldots,m\}\). The volume weights \(w_z,W\) agree with those of accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3`; \(w_z\) is the centroid-specialization weight called \(a_z\) in accepted fact `ae8bb1ec375ace78`, not the arbitrary selector weight in that fact. For \(j\in D_+\), the actual marginal depth is
\(\tau_p(j)=\inf_{Q\ni j}\sum_{z\in D_+\cap Q}p_z=\tau(j)/W\), where the infimum is over proper closed base halfspaces and \(\tau\) is the original-weight marginal depth of `0bcc51d8cd216cf3`. No marginal law is replaced by an auxiliary root or logarithmic law.

For every real affine map \(\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\), define
\(E_\ell=\{z\in D_+:\|T_z^{-1}(\ell(z)-c_z)\|_2\leq1/\sqrt{48}\}\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}:=\frac{2^nw_{\max}}W
 &=\left(\frac4{3n+1}\right)^n\leq\frac1{100},\\
\sup_{\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \frac1W\sum_{z\in E_\ell}w_z
 &\leq\frac{360}{n}\leq\frac1{100}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(n=m=d=36000\) is an exact witness for the feasibility test. In the residual-rank notation of `ae8bb1ec375ace78`, this family has \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_+)=n\) and \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(D_+)=n\).

Set
\(\eta=(m+1)(4m-1)/[3m(3m+1)]\) and \(y^\star=(0_n,(1+\eta/2)\mathbf1_n)\). This point belongs to \(S\), and every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing it satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_n(S\cap H)\geq\frac W{126},
\qquad h_S(y^\star)\geq\frac W{126}.
\tag{2}
\]

The affine-fit certificate being tested is itself valid: for every affine \(\ell\), there exists \(y_\ell\in S\), fixed before the containing halfspace, such that \(h_S(y_\ell)\geq(3/32)2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E_\ell}w_z\). Thus (1) limits the amount supplied by this affine-fit certificate, whereas (2) concerns actual depth. No disproof of the locked conjecture is asserted.

The body in accepted fact `20cc5aab4e030be0` is a different simplex-fiber body, with continuous dimension at least \(2n^2\). Its scalar quantity called \(T_z\) is not the matrix \(T_z\) here. There is no identification of these shapes, dimensions, or probability laws. The new moment and projection estimates required for the box construction are proved below.

## Proof

Replace \(|x_i|/m\leq y_i\) by the two rational linear inequalities \(x_i\leq my_i\) and \(-x_i\leq my_i\). The remaining inequalities are rational linear as well, so their intersection is convex and rational polyhedral. They bound \(x\) and imply \(0\leq y_i\leq2\), proving compactness. The point \((0_n,\mathbf1_n)\) satisfies all defining inequalities strictly, proving full dimension. Directly taking fibers gives the displayed boxes, their centroids, and their invertible shape matrices; every width is between 1 and 2. Outside \(D_+\) integer fibers are empty. For a single coordinate,
\(\sum_{k=-m}^m(2-|k|/m)=2+2\sum_{k=1}^m(2-k/m)=3m+1\).
Multiplication gives \(W=(3m+1)^n\), the stated product law, and \(w_{\max}=w_{0_n}=2^n\).

We first prove exact moment bounds for that original law. Let \(K\) have its one-coordinate probability \((2-|k|/m)/(3m+1)\), and put \(R=K/m\), \(t=|R|\). Symmetry gives \(\mathbb ER=0\) and \(\mathbb E[R(t-\eta)]=0\). The identities \(\sum_{k=1}^mk=m(m+1)/2\), \(\sum_{k=1}^mk^2=m(m+1)(2m+1)/6\), and \(\sum_{k=1}^mk^3=m^2(m+1)^2/4\) follow by induction, comparing the expressions at \(m\) and \(m-1\). Using them in the finite sums gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb Et&=\frac{2\sum_{k=1}^m(k/m)(2-k/m)}{3m+1}
 =\eta=\frac{(m+1)(4m-1)}{3m(3m+1)},\\
\sigma_R^2:=\mathbb ER^2=\mathbb Et^2
 &=\frac{2\sum_{k=1}^m(k/m)^2(2-k/m)}{3m+1}
 =\frac{(m+1)(5m+1)}{6m(3m+1)},\\
\sigma_t^2:=\operatorname{Var}(t)&=\sigma_R^2-\eta^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For clarity, we verify dimension-independent lower bounds without approximating these rational expressions. Write \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{unif}}\) for uniform expectation on \(\{-1,-1+1/m,\ldots,1\}\). Each original probability divided by the corresponding uniform probability is
\((2-t)(2m+1)/(3m+1)\geq2/3\).
The same power sums give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{unif}}R^2&=\frac{m+1}{3m}\geq\frac13,\\
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{unif}}(t)
 &=\frac{m+1}{3m}-\left(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}\right)^2
 =\frac1{12}+\frac{4m^2+7m+4}{12m(2m+1)^2}\geq\frac1{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\sigma_R^2\geq2/9\). For each real \(a\), the nonnegative-function comparison gives \(\mathbb E(t-a)^2\geq(2/3)\mathbb E_{\mathrm{unif}}(t-a)^2\). Minimizing both sides over \(a\) gives \(\sigma_t^2\geq1/18\). Use the positive square roots \(\sigma_R,\sigma_t\). Since \(0\leq t\leq1\), also \(0\leq\eta\leq1\).

Let \(Z\) have the original product law \(p\), and set \(R_i=Z_i/m\) and \(t_i=|R_i|\). Define the new, independent two-dimensional blocks
\(\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}=(R_i/\sigma_R,(t_i-\eta)/\sigma_t)\), and concatenate them in that order to obtain \(\Xi^{\mathrm{box}}\in\mathbb R^{2n}\). Equation (3) and symmetry show that each block has mean zero and covariance \(I_2\). The preceding lower bounds imply

\[
\|\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}\|_2^2\leq\frac92+18=\frac{45}2,
\qquad
\mathbb E\|\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}\|_2^2=2,
\qquad
\mathbb E\|\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}\|_2^4\leq45.
\tag{4}
\]

These are bounds for the original linear-weight law, not the exponential auxiliary law used for the simplex example in `20cc5aab4e030be0`.

We reprove the projection-variance argument underlying equation (12) of accepted fact `20cc5aab4e030be0`, with the new bound 45 in (4). For any affine subspace \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}\) of codimension \(k\geq12\), let \(\Pi\) be orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal complement of its direction space and let \(b_{\mathcal F}=\Pi v\) for any \(v\in\mathcal F\). This is independent of the chosen \(v\). With \(\mathcal V=\operatorname{dist}(\Xi^{\mathrm{box}},\mathcal F)^2\),
\(\mathcal V=\|\Pi\Xi^{\mathrm{box}}-b_{\mathcal F}\|_2^2\) and \(\mathbb E\mathcal V=k+\|b_{\mathcal F}\|_2^2\).

Write \(\Pi_{ij}\) for the two-by-two blocks of \(\Pi\). Expanding its quadratic form, independence and zero block means give

\[
\operatorname{Var}\bigl((\Xi^{\mathrm{box}})^{\mathsf T}\Pi\Xi^{\mathrm{box}}\bigr)
=\sum_i\operatorname{Var}\bigl((\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}})^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{ii}\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}\bigr)
 +4\sum_{i<j}\|\Pi_{ij}\|_{\mathrm F}^2
\leq45k.
\tag{5}
\]

Here the diagonal summands are independent. Distinct off-diagonal summands have zero covariance: for disjoint pairs use independence, and for pairs sharing one block condition on that block and average the two other centered blocks. A diagonal summand and an off-diagonal summand also have zero covariance, by conditioning on the shared block if any. Isotropy and independence give the off-diagonal second moment \(\|\Pi_{ij}\|_{\mathrm F}^2\). Each diagonal variance is at most \(\|\Pi_{ii}\|_{\mathrm F}^2\mathbb E\|\Xi_i^{\mathrm{box}}\|_2^4\leq45\|\Pi_{ii}\|_{\mathrm F}^2\). Finally,
\(\|\Pi\|_{\mathrm F}^2=\sum_i\|\Pi_{ii}\|_{\mathrm F}^2+2\sum_{i<j}\|\Pi_{ij}\|_{\mathrm F}^2=k\), which proves the last inequality in (5).

The full vector is isotropic and \(b_{\mathcal F}\in\operatorname{im}\Pi\). The inequality \(\operatorname{Var}(A+B)\leq2\operatorname{Var}(A)+2\operatorname{Var}(B)\), obtained by applying \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\) to centered variables, now yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal V)&\leq90k+8\|b_{\mathcal F}\|_2^2
 \leq90\mathbb E\mathcal V,\\
\mathbb P\bigl(\operatorname{dist}(\Xi^{\mathrm{box}},\mathcal F)\leq\sqrt6\bigr)
&\leq\frac{90\mathbb E\mathcal V}{(\mathbb E\mathcal V-6)^2}
 \leq\frac{360}{\mathbb E\mathcal V}\leq\frac{360}{k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The probability inequality is Chebyshev's inequality, which here follows from Markov's inequality applied to \((\mathcal V-\mathbb E\mathcal V)^2\). Its next inequality uses \(\mathbb E\mathcal V\geq k\geq12\). The constant 90 in (6) is twice our independently proved fourth-norm constant 45 in (4); it is not imported from the predecessor's different block law. Both the orientation and the translation of \(\mathcal F\) were arbitrary.

Fix an arbitrary affine \(\ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\), and form the affine subspace

\[
\mathcal F_\ell=\left\{
 \left(\frac{r_i}{\sigma_R},
 \frac{2\ell_i(mr)-2-\eta}{\sigma_t}\right)_{i=1}^n:
 r\in\mathbb R^n\right\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{2n}.
\]

Its first coordinate in each block recovers \(r_i\), so it has dimension exactly \(n\) and codimension \(n\), regardless of \(\ell\). If \(z\in E_\ell\), then \(\|T_z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2\) gives
\(\|\ell(z)-c_z\|_2\leq2/\sqrt{48}\), and hence
\(\|(2\ell(z)-2\mathbf1_n)-t_z\|_2\leq4/\sqrt{48}=1/\sqrt3\).
Compare \(\Xi^{\mathrm{box}}\) at \(Z=z\) with the point of \(\mathcal F_\ell\) indexed by \(r=z/m\). Their first block coordinates agree, and the squared distance in their second coordinates is at most \(\sigma_t^{-2}/3\leq6\). Thus
\(Z\in E_\ell\) implies \(\operatorname{dist}(\Xi^{\mathrm{box}},\mathcal F_\ell)\leq\sqrt6\).
Apply (6) with \(k=n\) to obtain
\(W^{-1}\sum_{E_\ell}w_z=\mathbb P(Z\in E_\ell)\leq360/n\).
The bound holds for each arbitrary real affine map, with no bound on its coefficients, so it proves the supremum assertion of (1). The largest-atom calculation gives \(\rho_{\mathrm{atom}}=(4/(3n+1))^n\leq2^{-n}\leq2^{-7}<1/100\) for the stated \(n\). This proves both feasibility inequalities.

The base support has affine dimension \(n\). The centroid graph contains \((0_n,\mathbf1_n)\) and \((\pm m e_i,\mathbf1_n+e_i/2)\) for \(1\leq i\leq n\), where \(e_i\) is the ith standard basis vector. Relative to \((0_n,\mathbf1_n)\), the sums and differences of the two vectors for each \(i\) are \((0_n,e_i)\) and \((2m e_i,0_n)\). They span \(\mathbb R^{2n}\); consequently the centroid graph has affine dimension \(2n\). Applying the residual-rank identity (16) of accepted fact `ae8bb1ec375ace78`, with its \(a_z=w_z\), gives \(r_{\mathrm{aff}}(D_+)=2n-n=n\). This is not the rank-one centroid geometry of a family changing in only one continuous direction.

We next prove (2) for the same original measure. Independently of \(Z\), take independent \(U_i\) uniform on \([-1/2,1/2]\), and set
\(Y_i=1+t_i/2+(2-t_i)U_i\).
Given \(Z=z\), this is uniform volume on the actual box \(C_z\). Therefore, on every measurable ambient set \(A\),
\(\mathbb P((Z,Y)\in A)=\mathcal H_n(S\cap A)/W\).
The pairs \((R_i,Y_i)\) are independent across \(i\). Their means are \((0,1+\eta/2)\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,Y_i)&=0,\\
\sigma_Y^2:=\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)
 &=\frac{\mathbb E(2-t)^2}{12}+\frac{\sigma_t^2}{4}
 =\frac{4-4\eta+\sigma_R^2}{12}
   +\frac{\sigma_R^2-\eta^2}{4}\geq\frac1{12}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, symmetry gives \(\mathbb E[R_i(1+t_i/2)]=0\), and the conditional noise is centered. Direct integration gives \(\mathbb EU_i^2=1/12\); conditional variances and the variance of the conditional mean give (7). Its lower bound follows from \(2-t\geq1\). This also specifies the variance exactly in terms of the rational moments (3); use its positive square root \(\sigma_Y\).

Define centered isotropic blocks
\(\Upsilon_i=(R_i/\sigma_R,(Y_i-1-\eta/2)/\sigma_Y)\).
Since \(0\leq Y_i\leq2\) and \(1\leq1+\eta/2\leq3/2\), the continuous centered coordinate has absolute value at most \(3/2\). Equations (3) and (7) give the exact bound
\(\|\Upsilon_i\|_2^2\leq9/2+12(3/2)^2=63/2\).
Put \(\kappa=63/2\). For every real two-dimensional coefficient vector \(v\), pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz and isotropy imply
\(\mathbb E\langle v,\Upsilon_i\rangle^4\leq\kappa\|v\|_2^2\mathbb E\langle v,\Upsilon_i\rangle^2=\kappa(\mathbb E\langle v,\Upsilon_i\rangle^2)^2\).

For any ambient normal \((a,b)\), write its centered projection as
\(F=a\cdot Z+b\cdot(Y-(1+\eta/2)\mathbf1_n)=\sum_i F_i\), where \(F_i=a_imR_i+b_i(Y_i-1-\eta/2)\).
Each \(F_i\) is a projection of \(\Upsilon_i\) with coefficient vector \((a_im\sigma_R,b_i\sigma_Y)\). The variables \(F_i\) are independent and centered. With \(v_i=\mathbb EF_i^2\), expansion of the fourth power gives

\[
\mathbb EF^4=\sum_i\mathbb EF_i^4+6\sum_{i<j}v_iv_j
 \leq\kappa\left(\sum_i v_i\right)^2
 =\kappa(\mathbb EF^2)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

Every other term in the expansion contains an index appearing once and has zero expectation. The inequality uses \(\kappa\geq3\). Independence thus preserves this constant for all ambient projections.

For completeness, if a centered scalar variable \(Q\) has variance \(\sigma^2>0\) and fourth moment at most \(\kappa\sigma^4\), Hölder's inequality with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\) applied to \(|Q|^{2/3}|Q|^{4/3}\) yields
\(\sigma^2\leq(\mathbb E|Q|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|Q|^4)^{1/3}\), hence \(\mathbb E|Q|\geq\sigma/\sqrt\kappa\).
Centering gives \(\mathbb EQ_+=\mathbb EQ_-=\mathbb E|Q|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz, applied to \(Q_+\mathbf1_{Q>0}\), gives
\(\mathbb P(Q>0)\geq(\mathbb EQ_+)^2/\mathbb EQ_+^2\geq1/(4\kappa)\); likewise \(\mathbb P(Q<0)\geq1/(4\kappa)\). If the variance is zero, \(Q=0\) almost surely and its closed nonnegative side has probability one.

The point \(y^\star\) belongs to \(S\), since its integer base is zero and its continuous coordinates lie in \([1,3/2]\subseteq C_{0_n}=[0,2]^n\). It is the mean of \((Z,Y)\). Every proper closed ambient halfspace containing it contains a parallel closed halfspace through this mean of the form \(F\geq0\). Apply the preceding scalar argument to (8). That halfspace has probability at least \(1/(4\kappa)=1/126\). Multiplying by \(W\) proves (2), including vertical halfspaces and the zero-variance case. The whole space, if admitted as a halfspace, has mass \(W\). This establishes the bound at the same point fixed before all halfspaces.

Finally, we verify the affine-fit certificate rather than using the unaccepted lead as a premise. For independent uniform \(U_i\) on \([-1/2,1/2]\), direct integration gives \(\mathbb EU_i^2=1/12\) and \(\mathbb EU_i^4=1/80\). For any nonzero vector \(b\), set \(Q=b\cdot U\) and \(\sigma^2=\|b\|_2^2/12\). Independence and symmetry give

\[
\mathbb EQ^4=\frac1{80}\sum_i b_i^4+
 \frac1{24}\sum_{i<j}b_i^2b_j^2\leq3\sigma^4.
\]

Splitting the second moment at \(|Q|=\sigma/2\), then using Cauchy-Schwarz, gives
\(\sigma^2\leq\sigma^2/4+(\mathbb EQ^4\,\mathbb P(|Q|\geq\sigma/2))^{1/2}\).
Consequently \(\mathbb P(|Q|\geq\sigma/2)\geq3/16\). Symmetry makes its two disjoint tails equiprobable, so \(\mathbb P(Q\geq\sigma/2)\geq3/32\).

Fix \(z\in E_\ell\). The fitted normalized displacement has every coordinate of absolute value at most \(1/\sqrt{48}<1/2\), so \(\ell(z)\in C_z\). For any nonzero fiber normal \(v\), let \(b=T_z^{\mathsf T}v\ne0\). Then
\(v\cdot(\ell(z)-c_z)\leq\|b\|_2/\sqrt{48}=\sigma/2\).
The halfspace through \(\ell(z)\) with normal \(v\), evaluated on uniform points \(c_z+T_zU\), therefore has volume at least \((3/32)w_z\). Every containing closed fiber halfspace contains such a parallel halfspace through the fitted point.

If \(E_\ell\ne\varnothing\), apply the finite weighted lattice centerpoint assertion recorded in accepted fact `0bcc51d8cd216cf3` to \(E_\ell\) with the original weights \(w_z\). It gives \(j\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\) such that every containing proper closed base halfspace has weight at least \(2^{-n}\sum_{E_\ell}w_z\). In particular, \(j\in D_+\) and the original-law depth obeys \(\tau_p(j)\geq2^{-n}W^{-1}\sum_{E_\ell}w_z\). Convexity of \(C\) and affinity of \(\ell\) give \(y_\ell=(j,\ell(j))\in S\). For any closed ambient halfspace containing this point, shrink it to a parallel halfspace through the point and write it as
\(a\cdot(x-j)+v\cdot(y-\ell(j))\geq0\).
The affine function \(g(x)=a\cdot(x-j)+v\cdot(\ell(x)-\ell(j))\) vanishes at \(j\). Its nonnegative set is either a proper closed base halfspace containing \(j\) or, when \(g\) is identically zero, all of \(\mathbb R^n\). It thus captures at least the stated centerpoint weight from \(E_\ell\). On every captured fiber the ambient section contains \(\ell(z)\); its volume is at least \((3/32)w_z\) by the proved cap bound when \(v\ne0\), and is the entire fiber when \(v=0\). Summing proves
\(h_S(y_\ell)\geq(3/32)2^{-n}\sum_{E_\ell}w_z\).
If \(E_\ell\) is empty, any point of \(S\) supplies the automatic zero bound. This proves the certificate with its original weights and all boundary cases, and completes the separation between certificate fitting and actual depth.
