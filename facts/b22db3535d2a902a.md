## Statement

Fix a finite nonempty real base $P$ and $d\ge1$. Suppose the compact convex bodies $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ have positive volume and are the exact sampled fibers of $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$. Use the sum of their $d$-volumes as the mixed measure, with total $M$. Write $q_p$ for the maximum raw ordinary closed-halfspace depth over $K_p$, $Q=\max_{p\in P}q_p$ and $\beta_0=Q/M$. Let $F_p$ be the smallest face of $\operatorname{conv}(P)$ containing $p$ and let $I=\{p:q_p=Q\}$. If $u\in P\setminus\bigcup_{p\in I}F_p$, replace just the input at $u$ by $K_u+t B_d$ for $t>0$, where $B_d$ is the Euclidean unit ball, and form the hull $C_t$ of these inputs. Its actual fibers satisfy $K_z\subseteq K_z(t)\subseteq K_z+tB_d$ for all $z\in P$, and their total mass $M_t$ is strictly greater than $M$. For all sufficiently small $t>0$, $Q(C_t)=Q$ and $\beta_0(C_t)=Q/M_t<\beta_0(C)$.

Consequently the faces $F_p$ for $p\in I$ cover $P$ at every local minimum of $\beta_0$ among all actual positive compatible families on the fixed base, in the product Hausdorff topology. If $P$ contains a site in $\operatorname{relint}(\operatorname{conv}(P))$, then at least one deepest base at such a minimum belongs to this relative interior.

## Proof

A point of $K_z(t)$ is a convex combination of input points with projected combination $z$. Group by base, and replace the point from the enlarged input at $u$ by one in $K_u$ at distance at most $t$. Actuality puts the resulting combination in $K_z$, and the displacement of the combination is at most $t$. The lower inclusion follows from $C\subset C_t$. The sampled fibers of $C_t$ have $C_t$ as their canonical hull because they contain all the new inputs and themselves lie in $C_t$. The base hull is unchanged. The input $K_u+t B_d$ has strictly greater volume than $K_u$, because the radius-$t$ ball about a supporting point contains an open ball on the exterior side of its supporting hyperplane. All other sampled volumes are nondecreasing, so $M_t>M$.

For $p\in I$ every convex combination projecting into $F_p$ uses only bases in $F_p$. Since $u\notin F_p$, the entire restriction of $C_t$ over $F_p$ is unchanged. The exact face isolation lemma applies to both families and gives identical raw depths at all marks over $p$. For $p\notin I$, the positive-body stability fact 43ed87d91b76249c gives $q_p(C_t)\to q_p(C)$. Approximate a maximizing mark by feasible marks to bound the lower limit. For the upper limit, take a convergent subsequence of maximizing marks in the bounded moving fibers. The maxima exist by continuity on each compact fiber, also from that fact. By finiteness, all the strict gaps at $p\notin I$ persist for sufficiently small $t$. Thus the raw maximum stays $Q$. It is positive because a ball inside one positive fiber contributes at least half its volume to every cap containing its center. This proves the strict normalized descent. Its arbitrarily small Hausdorff size excludes an uncovered site at a local minimum. A proper face cannot contain a relative-interior site, giving the last conclusion.
