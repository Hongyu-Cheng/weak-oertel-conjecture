## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, $d\ge0$, $N=n+d$, and let $C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a compact convex body with nonempty interior. There is an invertible affine map $F(z,x)=(Az+t,Bz+Tx+c)$, with $A,T$ invertible on their respective spaces, such that
$$
B_2^N\subseteq K=F(C)\subseteq R B_2^N,\qquad R=N(N+2).
$$
For a Borel set $E$ put $\mu(E)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|(C\cap E)_z|_d$ and $\nu(E)=|\det A|\sum_{\lambda\in A\mathbb Z^n+t}|(K\cap E)_\lambda|_d$. Then $\nu(F(E))=|\det A|\,|\det T|\,\mu(E)=|\det DF|\,\mu(E)$. The map takes the original sampled source bijectively to $K\cap((A\mathbb Z^n+t)\times\mathbb R^d)$ and preserves normalized depth whenever the total mass is positive. For $d=0$, use counting measure on $\mathbb R^0$ and $|\det T|=1$.

## Proof

Apply the maximum-inscribed-simplex normalization of 03bc771bddd39a8a in dimension $N$. It sends the simplex to $\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_N)$ and the body between this simplex and $[-1,1]^N$. The standard simplex centroid has all coordinates $1/(N+1)$. Its distances to the coordinate facets are $1/(N+1)$ and its distance to the remaining facet is $r_N=1/((N+1)\sqrt N)$. Translate the centroid to zero and dilate by $1/r_N$. The resulting body contains the unit ball and lies in the radius-$N(N+2)$ ball because each coordinate before dilation has absolute value at most $(N+2)/(N+1)$.

Let $L_0$ denote the linear part of the map so far. Postcompose with an orthogonal map taking $L_0(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d)$ onto $\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d$. The two balls are unchanged. The composed linear part preserves the vertical subspace, and its restrictions to that subspace and to the quotient are invertible. Thus it has the stated block form. The base lattice maps to $A\mathbb Z^n+t$, and the fiber map at each $z$ has linear part $T$ and translation $Bz+c$. Each fiber volume is multiplied by $|\det T|$. Multiplication by the transformed lattice covolume $|\det A|$ gives the claimed identity, including the counting convention. Affine bijectivity takes the closed halfspaces through or containing a mark bijectively to the corresponding halfspaces at its image. The common positive determinant cancels from each mass ratio.
