## Statement

Let $X=\sum_{i=1}^k p_i E_i$ and $Y=\sum_{j=1}^\ell b_j F_j$, with $k,\ell\ge1$, positive coefficients, and all variables independent mean-one exponentials. For the $k\times k$ matrix $T$ with $T_{ii}=-1/p_i$, $T_{i,i+1}=1/p_i$ for $i<k$, and other entries zero,
$$
\Pr(X\ge Y)=e_1^{\mathsf T}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(I-b_jT)^{-1}\right]\mathbf1.
$$
For rational inputs start with $v=\mathbf1$ and, for each $b_j$, update $v_i\leftarrow(p_i v_i+b_j v_{i+1})/(p_i+b_j)$ in descending order $i=k,\ldots,1$, setting $v_{k+1}=0$. The resulting $v_1$ gives the probability, including repeated $p_i$ or $b_j$.

## Proof

Let $q_i(t)$ be the survival of the sum of the phases starting at $i$, using $>$, and set $q_{k+1}(t)=0$. Conditioning on the waiting time of phase $i$ gives $q_i(t)=e^{-t/p_i}+p_i^{-1}\int_0^t e^{-(t-s)/p_i}q_{i+1}(s)\,ds$. Therefore $q_i'=(q_{i+1}-q_i)/p_i$ with $q_i(0)=1$, and $q(t)=e^{tT}\mathbf1$. The eigenvalues of $T$ are negative, so the integral against the density of $b_jF_j$ converges, and integrating the derivative of $e^{(T-I/b_j)t}$ gives $\mathbb E[e^{b_jF_jT}]=(I-b_jT)^{-1}$. Independence and $e^{(t+s)T}=e^{tT}e^{sT}$ give the formula by conditioning on $Y$; $X$ has no atoms. The factors commute as rational functions of $T$. In each solve, the $i$th equation is $(1+b_j/p_i)v_i^{\rm new}-(b_j/p_i)v_{i+1}^{\rm new}=v_i^{\rm old}$, exactly the descending update.
