## Statement

Let \(N\ge1\) be an integer and put \(n=N-1\). Let \(A<B\) be real, and let \(R,T:[A,B]\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous concave functions that are positive on \((A,B)\). For \(n\ge1\), fix \(a_i\ge0\) and \(b_i,c_i>0\), for \(1\le i\le n\), and define

\[
P:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R:
A\le t\le B,\quad x_i\ge0,\quad
b\cdot x\le R(t),\quad c\cdot x\le T(t)\right\}.
\]

For \(n=0\), define \(P=[A,B]\), and use the empty-coordinate convention. Assume that the complete support \(D:=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\) is nonempty. For any \(\lambda>0\) and \(h\in\mathbb R\), define \(w_{(x,s)}:=\exp(-\lambda(s-h)^2-a\cdot x)\) on \(D\), with \(a\cdot x=0\) when \(n=0\), and put \(W:=\sum_{(x,s)\in D}w_{(x,s)}\).

There are integers \(p\le q\) and one original point \(J=(j,k)\in D\), with \(p\le k\le q\), such that \(\lambda(q-p)^2\le16\) and every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(J\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{(x,s)\in D\cap H\\p\le s\le q}}
w_{(x,s)}
\ge e^{-500}\,2^{-N}W.
\]

The band is allowed to be a singleton. The sum uses the complete original support in the band and retains all equality atoms. No integrality of \(A,B\), rationality of the resource coefficients, or restriction on \(\lambda\) beyond positivity is imposed.

## Proof

Write \(\mathcal I:=[A,B]\cap\mathbb Z\). Each integer height in \(\mathcal I\) contains the horizontal zero vector, and these are exactly the occupied heights. Thus \(\mathcal I\ne\varnothing\). The interval and all horizontal coordinates are bounded, because \(x_i\le \max_{[A,B]}R/b_i\) when \(n\ge1\). Consequently \(D\) is finite and \(0<W<\infty\).

We first extract three scalar estimates from accepted fact c847c935ecb28857. For fixed nonnegative budgets \(\rho,\tau\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{\rho,\tau}
&:=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le\rho,\ c\cdot z\le\tau\},\\
F(\rho,\tau)&:=\sum_{z\in K_{\rho,\tau}}e^{-a\cdot z}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For a fixed pair of budgets, let \(\widetilde w_z=e^{-a\cdot z}\) on \(K_{\rho,\tau}\) and zero elsewhere, and let
\(S_j^{\rho,\tau}:=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{\widetilde w_{j+v},\widetilde w_{j-v}\}\).
The sum is oriented and includes zero. In the notation of c847c935ecb28857, its scalar support \(A\) is \(K_{\rho,\tau}\), its fixed \(R,T\) are \(\rho,\tau\), and its \(\widetilde W,\widetilde S_j\) are \(F(\rho,\tau),S_j^{\rho,\tau}\). Its horizontal \(n,a,b,c\) are unchanged. Its resource multipliers \((\lambda,\mu)\) are denoted \((\beta,\gamma)\) here, so they are distinct from the present height curvature \(\lambda\).

For \(s>0\), define \(f(s):=\max_{m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}\{\log(2m+1)-sm\}\), and set \(f(0)=+\infty\). For \(n\ge1\), define the auxiliary profile

\[
\Psi(\rho,\tau):=
\inf_{\beta,\gamma\ge0}
\left\{\beta\rho+\gamma\tau+
\sum_{i=1}^n f(a_i+\beta b_i+\gamma c_i)\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(n=0\), set \(F(\rho,\tau)=1\), let \(K_{\rho,\tau}\) contain the unique empty vector, and set \(\Psi(\rho,\tau)=0\). Put \(\kappa_n:=e^{-16\sqrt n-10}\) and \(\eta_n:=2n+2\log3\). We claim that for every \(\rho,\tau\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\rho,\tau)&\le(3/2)^n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)},\\
\text{some }j\in K_{\rho,\tau}\text{ satisfies }\quad
S_j^{\rho,\tau}&\ge\kappa_n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)},\\
\Psi(t\rho,t\tau)&\le\Psi(\rho,\tau)+(t-1)\eta_n
\qquad(t\ge1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

Here \(\Psi\) is finite, nonnegative, concave, and nondecreasing in both budgets.

We verify all these assertions, including the estimates beyond the coarse statement of the accepted fact. The terms defining \(f\) include zero, so \(f\ge0\). Taking \(\beta\) sufficiently large and \(\gamma=0\) makes all arguments at least \(\log3\), and then \(f=0\). Indeed, \(2m+1\le3^m\) for nonnegative integers \(m\), by induction, so \(m=0\) maximizes when \(s\ge\log3\). Thus (1) is finite and nonnegative. It is concave as an infimum of affine functions of \((\rho,\tau)\), and is nondecreasing because all their coefficients \(\beta,\gamma\) are nonnegative.

For \(n\ge1\) and \(\rho,\tau>0\), the minimizer construction in Equations (2)--(4) of c847c935ecb28857 applies to exactly (1). It gives a finite minimizer \((\beta,\gamma)\), positive \(s_i:=a_i+\beta b_i+\gamma c_i\), coordinate maximizing integers \(m_i\), and dual slack

\[
0\le\beta(\rho-b\cdot m)+\gamma(\tau-c\cdot m)\le2\log3,
\qquad s_i m_i\le2.
\tag{3}
\]

The coordinate inequality in Equation (1) of that accepted proof gives
\(2(1-e^{-s_i})(2m_i+1)e^{-s_i m_i}\ge4/3\).
For every \(z\in K_{\rho,\tau}\), the resource inequalities imply
\(e^{-a\cdot z}\le e^{\beta\rho+\gamma\tau}\prod_i e^{-s_i z_i}\).
Summing the resulting geometric majorant and using the coordinate inequality proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\rho,\tau)
&\le e^{\beta\rho+\gamma\tau}\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}\\
&\le(3/2)^n e^{\beta\rho+\gamma\tau}
\prod_i(2m_i+1)e^{-s_i m_i}
=(3/2)^n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The accepted construction in Equations (5)--(9) of c847c935ecb28857 gives an integer \(j\in K_{\rho,\tau}\) with

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_j^{\rho,\tau}
&\ge\frac14\,7^{-(6\sqrt n+2)}
e^{-a\cdot m-2\sqrt{8n/3}}\prod_i(2m_i+1)\\
&\ge\frac14
\exp\!\left(-2\sqrt{8n/3}-2\log3
-(6\sqrt n+2)\log7\right)e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)}
\ge\kappa_n e^{\Psi(\rho,\tau)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

For the second inequality, subtract the slack in (3) from the exponent in (1). For the last, use \(2\sqrt{8/3}\le4\) and \(\log3,\log4,\log7<2\). These estimates cost at most \(16\sqrt n+10\).

The same minimizing multipliers satisfy

\[
\beta\rho+\gamma\tau
\le\sum_i(\beta b_i+\gamma c_i)m_i+2\log3
\le\sum_i s_i m_i+2\log3
\le\eta_n.
\tag{6}
\]

Evaluating the infimum for \(t\rho,t\tau\) at these multipliers proves the third inequality of (2).

If \(\rho=0\), strict positivity of \(b\) gives \(K_{\rho,\tau}=\{0\}\). In (1), take \(\gamma=0\) and \(\beta\) large enough that every argument is at least \(\log3\); the attained value is zero and \(\beta\rho+\gamma\tau=0\). Thus \(F=1\), \(\Psi=0\), \(S_0^{\rho,\tau}=1\), and all parts of (2) follow, including the scaling inequality. The case \(\tau=0\) is identical with the roles exchanged. For \(n=0\), all the assertions are immediate from the definitions. This completes the verification of (2) on its full domain.

One consequence of the scaling inequality that will also be used is

\[
\Psi((1-\varepsilon)\rho,(1-\varepsilon)\tau)
\ge\Psi(\rho,\tau)-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\eta_n
\qquad(0<\varepsilon<1).
\tag{7}
\]

Apply the third part of (2) to the smaller budgets with \(t=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\). This proof uses no derivative or rounding of the height coordinate.

For real \(t\in[A,B]\), put \(\psi(t):=\Psi(R(t),T(t))\). The monotonicity and concavity just proved, together with the concavity of the two budgets, imply that \(\psi\) is concave. For \(s\in\mathcal I\), define

\[
g_s:=e^{-\lambda(s-h)^2},\qquad
U_s:=g_s e^{\psi(s)},\qquad
Z:=\sum_{s\in\mathcal I}U_s,\qquad
M:=\max_{s\in\mathcal I}U_s.
\]

The first part of (2) gives

\[
W=\sum_{s\in\mathcal I}g_sF(R(s),T(s))
\le(3/2)^n Z.
\tag{8}
\]

The sampled sequence \(U_s\) is positive and log-concave. More precisely, for any same-parity integers \(s,t\in\mathcal I\), its integer midpoint satisfies

\[
\log U_{(s+t)/2}
\ge\frac{\log U_s+\log U_t}{2}
+\frac{\lambda(s-t)^2}{4}.
\tag{9}
\]

Consider the near-maximum set
\(\mathcal E:=\{s\in\mathcal I:U_s\ge M/e\}\).
It is a nonempty consecutive interval of integers by log-concavity. Denote its first and last heights by \(s_-,s_+\), and put \(L:=s_+-s_-+1\). We will use

\[
Z\le3LM.
\tag{10}
\]

To prove this, the mass in \(\mathcal E\) is at most \(LM\). Choose a maximizing height \(s_\star\in\mathcal E\). If there is a right tail, its first height \(s_++1\) has log weight strictly below \(\log M-1\), and lies at distance at most \(L\) from \(s_\star\). The secant slope between these two heights is therefore at most \(-1/L\). Concavity makes every subsequent increment no larger. Since \(L\ge1\), it follows, for each right-tail height \(s_++r\), \(r\ge1\), that
\(U_{s_++r}\le M e^{-r/L}\).
Its total mass is at most
\(M/(e^{1/L}-1)\le LM\).
The left tail is bounded in the same way, and a missing tail contributes zero. This proves (10).

There is also a bound on the original width of this interval whenever \(L\ge3\):

\[
\lambda(s_+-s_-)^2\le16.
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, (9) shows that any two same-parity heights in \(\mathcal E\) have distance at most \(2/\sqrt\lambda\), because both endpoint log weights are at least \(\log M-1\) and the midpoint log weight is at most \(\log M\). Put \(d=s_+-s_-=L-1\ge2\). If the endpoints have the same parity, use both; otherwise use \(s_-\) and \(s_+-1\). Their distance is at least \(d-1\ge d/2\), which gives (11). Every interval contained in \([s_-,s_+]\) therefore satisfies the same width bound.

Set \(\delta:=1/(8\sqrt N)\), so \(0<\delta\le1/8\). Suppose first that \(L\le64/\delta\). Choose a maximizing height \(s_\star\), and apply (2) to the budgets \(R(s_\star),T(s_\star)\), including the zero-budget cases. This gives \(j\) with original row reflection sum at least \(\kappa_n U_{s_\star}=\kappa_n M\). Choose \(J=(j,s_\star)\) and \(p=q=s_\star\).

For completeness, we will use the following reflection comparison both here and below. Extend a finite nonnegative weight on the chosen band by zero to all of \(\mathbb Z^N\), and write
\(S_J^{\mathrm{band}}:=\sum_{V\in\mathbb Z^N}\min\{w_{J+V},w_{J-V}\}\).
If a closed halfspace \(H\) contains \(J\), at least one of \(J+V,J-V\) lies in \(H\) for every \(V\). Otherwise their midpoint would satisfy the strict opposite halfspace inequality. Pairing the summands at \(V\) and \(-V\), with zero displacement retained, gives

\[
2\sum_{I\in D\cap H\cap\mathrm{band}}w_I
\ge S_J^{\mathrm{band}}.
\tag{12}
\]

In the present singleton case, (8), (10), and (12) therefore give

\[
\frac{2^N}{W}
\sum_{\substack{(x,s)\in D\cap H\\s=s_\star}}w_{(x,s)}
\ge\frac{\kappa_n}{3L}\left(\frac43\right)^n
\ge\frac{\delta\kappa_n}{192}\left(\frac43\right)^n.
\tag{13}
\]

The band width is zero.

It remains to consider \(L>64/\delta\). Put

\[
k:=\left\lfloor\frac{s_-+s_+}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
r:=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta L}{16}\right\rfloor,\qquad
p:=k-r,\qquad q:=k+r.
\]

Here \(\delta L/16>4\), and hence
\(r\ge\delta L/32\) and \(2r+1\ge\delta L/16\).
The distance from \(k\) to each of \(s_-,s_+\) is at least \((L-2)/2\). Since \(r\le\delta L/16\), \(\delta\le1/8\), and \(L>64/\delta\), the entire band lies in \([s_-,s_+]\). It follows from (11) that \(\lambda(q-p)^2\le16\).

The distance from \(k\) to each of the real endpoints \(A,B\) is also at least \((L-2)/2\). In particular \(k\in(A,B)\), so \(R(k),T(k)>0\). Nonnegative concavity gives, for every real \(t\in[k-r,k+r]\),

\[
(1-\delta)R(k)\le R(t)\le(1+\delta)R(k),
\qquad
(1-\delta)T(k)\le T(t)\le(1+\delta)T(k).
\tag{14}
\]

Here is the explicit endpoint estimate. For \(0\le u\le r\), the chord from \(k\) to \(B\) gives
\(R(k+u)\ge(1-u/(B-k))R(k)\).
Expressing \(k\) as a convex combination of \(A\) and \(k+u\) gives
\(R(k+u)\le(1+u/(k-A))R(k)\).
The negative-displacement inequalities exchange the endpoints, and the same proof applies to \(T\). Every ratio in these estimates is at most
\(2r/(L-2)\le\delta L/(8(L-2))\le\delta\), proving (14). Thus these bounds use the original real endpoints and the original integer center.

Let \(\rho:=(1-\delta)R(k)\), \(\tau:=(1-\delta)T(k)\). The full horizontal support \(K_{\rho,\tau}\) is contained in every original row of the band. Choose \(j\in K_{\rho,\tau}\) from (2). Equation (7) gives

\[
S_j^{\rho,\tau}
\ge\kappa_n
\exp\!\left(\psi(k)-\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\eta_n\right).
\tag{15}
\]

For every integer \(s\) in the band, (14), monotonicity, and the scaling inequality in (2) give \(\psi(s)\le\psi(k)+\delta\eta_n\). Also \(s\in\mathcal E\), so \(U_s\ge M/e\). Consequently the original Gaussian height weight satisfies

\[
g_s=U_s e^{-\psi(s)}
\ge M\exp(-1-\psi(k)-\delta\eta_n).
\tag{16}
\]

This comparison accounts for arbitrary \(h\), including a height interval far from the Gaussian center.

Choose \(J=(j,k)\). For each integer \(-r\le u\le r\), and each horizontal displacement \(v\) with \(j\pm v\in K_{\rho,\tau}\), both original endpoints \((j+v,k+u)\) and \((j-v,k-u)\) belong to \(D\) and the chosen band. Their minimum original weight is at least the lower bound in (16) times
\(\min\{e^{-a\cdot(j+v)},e^{-a\cdot(j-v)}\}\).
Summing these oriented original pairs, including zero, and using (15) proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_J^{\mathrm{band}}
&\ge(2r+1)\kappa_n M
\exp\!\left(-1-
\left(\delta+\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\right)\eta_n\right)\\
&\ge\frac{\delta L}{16}\kappa_n M
\exp(-1-3\delta\eta_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The final inequality uses \(\delta\le1/8\). All other points and reflected pairs of the band remain available; (17) is only a lower bound on its full reflection sum.

Combining (8), (10), (12), and (17), every closed halfspace containing this one \(J\) has

\[
\frac{2^N}{W}
\sum_{\substack{(x,s)\in D\cap H\\p\le s\le q}}w_{(x,s)}
\ge\frac{\delta\kappa_n}{48}\left(\frac43\right)^n
\exp(-1-3\delta\eta_n).
\tag{18}
\]

Both (13) and (18) are bounded below by
\(\delta\kappa_n(4/3)^n\exp(-1-3\delta\eta_n)/192\).
This quantity has a positive absolute lower bound. Explicitly, \(\eta_n\le4N\), so \(3\delta\eta_n\le(3/2)\sqrt N\). Also
\(\log(4/3)\ge1/4\), \(\log1536<8\), and \(\log\sqrt N\le\sqrt N\). Using \(n=N-1\) and \(\sqrt n\le\sqrt N\), the logarithm of the common lower bound is at least

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac N4-\frac14-16\sqrt N-\frac32\sqrt N-\sqrt N-19
&\ge\frac N4-20\sqrt N-20\\
&=\left(\frac{\sqrt N}{2}-20\right)^2-420
\ge-420.
\end{aligned}
\]

For example, \(\log1536<8\) follows from \(e^2>7\), since \(e^8>7^4>1536\). Thus the common lower bound is at least \(e^{-420}\), and in particular at least \(e^{-500}\).

The empty-coordinate case \(n=0\) was included in (2) and all subsequent height estimates. The small-interval case includes singleton rows caused by zero endpoint budgets, and the large-interval case has a strictly interior center. In both cases the band and the original center are chosen before the halfspace. This proves all the stated quantifiers.
