## Statement

Let \(m\ge2\), \(h\ge1\), and \(L\ge1\) be integers with \(2h\le L<3h\). On the original support
\[
E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\},\qquad
R(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{\exp(\beta\sum_i x_i+\theta y)}{\sum_{(\mathbf z,v)\in E_{L,h,m}}\exp(\beta\sum_i z_i+\theta v)},
\]
where \(\beta<0\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb R\) are finite. Suppose the full mean is \((j\mathbf1,1)\), with integer \(j\). Let \((\mathbf X,Y)\) have law \(R\), and let \(\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^m\) satisfy \(\sum_i b_i=0\). Then, for \(N=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf X\),
\[
\mathbb E_R N^4\le3\cdot10^9(\mathbb E_R N^2)^2.
\]
This includes the zero direction and the case \(L-2h=0\). All expectations and height probabilities in the inequality use the original law \(R\).

## Proof

Use \(X_\Sigma=\sum_iX_i\) and \(\mu_y=\mathbb E_R[X_\Sigma\mid Y=y]\), exactly as in accepted fact 43cb56218b18ec82, and put \(B_y=L-hy\), as in accepted fact 9ba4a3963a51edd3. The law \(R\) is the same original source law as in those facts; their different letters \(R\) and \(p\) for its probability mass are aliases. Only the uniform-composition identities from 9ba4a3963a51edd3 are used below, and those identities are stated and proved for every nonnegative total, independently of the sign of the source tilt.

The support contains an atom with \(x_1>0\), and every atom has positive mass. Therefore \(j=\mathbb E_R X_1>0\), so the integer \(j\) is at least one. Write \(p_y=\Pr_R(Y=y)\). The height support is exactly \(\{0,1,2\}\), and \(\mathbb E_R Y=1\) implies \(p_0=p_2\). Accepted fact 43cb56218b18ec82 proves that the original transverse partition function \(Z_m(B,u)\) is log-concave in the nonnegative integer budget \(B\), for every real \(u\). Applying that result to the equally spaced budgets \(B_2,B_1,B_0\) gives
\[
Z_m(B_0,\beta)Z_m(B_2,\beta)\le Z_m(B_1,\beta)^2,\qquad
p_0p_2\le p_1^2.
\]
Consequently \(p_1\ge1/3\).

We first prove the budget comparison needed for the total means. Put \(r=e^\beta\in(0,1)\), matching the normalization of \(r\) in 43cb56218b18ec82. For each nonnegative integer \(B\), let \(S_B\) have probabilities proportional to
\(w_s=\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}r^s\), \(0\le s\le B\), and write \(\nu_B=\mathbb E S_B\). Thus \(\nu_{B_y}=\mu_y\). These are precisely the original conditional total laws. Increasing the budget only adds larger values with the same positive weights, so \(\nu_B\) is nondecreasing: explicitly,
\[
\nu_{B+1}=\frac{\bigl(\sum_{s=0}^Bw_s\bigr)\nu_B+(B+1)w_{B+1}}{\sum_{s=0}^{B+1}w_s}\ge\nu_B.
\]

In addition, \(\nu_B/B\) is nonincreasing for \(B\ge1\). To prove this, begin with an auxiliary variable \(U\) having density \(m u^{m-1}\) on \([0,1]\), and, conditionally on \(U=u\), a binomial variable with \(B\) trials and success probability \(u\). Its unconditional mass at \(s\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m\binom Bs\int_0^1u^{s+m-1}(1-u)^{B-s}\,du
&=\frac{mB!(s+m-1)!}{s!(B+m)!}\\
&=\frac{\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}}{\binom{B+m}{m}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The integral identity follows by repeated integration by parts. Hence this mixture gives the budget total law at \(r=1\). Multiplying the joint law by \(r^s\) and normalizing gives exactly \(S_B\). After this tilt, its auxiliary variable, denoted \(U_B\), has density proportional to \(u^{m-1}(1-(1-r)u)^B\), and, given \(U_B=u\), the total is binomial with success probability
\(g(u)=ru/(1-(1-r)u)\). Therefore \(\nu_B/B=\mathbb E g(U_B)\) for \(B\ge1\).

The density of \(U_{B+1}\) is obtained from that of \(U_B\) by multiplication by the decreasing positive function \(t(u)=1-(1-r)u\) and normalization. The function \(g\) is increasing, since \(g'(u)=r/(1-(1-r)u)^2>0\). For independent copies \(U_B,U_B'\),
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(g(U_B),t(U_B))
=\mathbb E\bigl[(g(U_B)-g(U_B'))(t(U_B)-t(U_B'))\bigr]\le0.
\]
It follows that
\(\mathbb E g(U_{B+1})=\mathbb E[g(U_B)t(U_B)]/\mathbb E t(U_B)\le\mathbb E g(U_B)\), proving the asserted mean-per-budget monotonicity.

Here \(B_1\ge h\ge1\) and \(B_0\le2B_1\), the latter inequality being equivalent to \(L\ge2h\). Thus
\[
\mu_0\le\frac{B_0}{B_1}\mu_1\le2\mu_1,\qquad
mj=\mathbb E_R X_\Sigma\le\mu_0.
\]
In particular \(\mu_1\ge mj/2\ge m/2\) and \(\mu_0\ge m\). This comparison is sufficient; a stronger concavity assertion for \(\nu_B\) is not needed.

We next establish a raw-moment estimate for the totals. Let \(T\) be any nonnegative integer variable with positive masses \(q_0,\ldots,q_M\) and nonincreasing successive mass ratios. Its survival function \(F(k)=\Pr(T\ge k)\) satisfies \(F(0)=1\) and has nonincreasing successive ratios before it becomes zero. Indeed,
\(F(k)/q_k=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M-k}q_{k+\ell}/q_k\) decreases with \(k\): each term decreases by the successive-ratio assumption, and the next sum has one fewer nonnegative term. Hence \(F(k+1)/F(k)=1-q_k/F(k)\) decreases. Products of these ratios imply \(F(k+\ell)\le F(k)F(\ell)\); if a tail has become zero, this inequality is immediate.

Let \(\nu=\mathbb ET\) and \(t_*=\lceil2(\nu+1)\rceil\le3(\nu+1)\). Markov's inequality gives \(F(t_*)\le1/2\), so \(F(nt_*)\le2^{-n}\). On the event \(nt_*\le T<(n+1)t_*\), integrality gives \((T+1)^4\le((n+1)t_*)^4\). Consequently
\[
\mathbb E(T+1)^4\le t_*^4\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)^4 2^{-n}
=300t_*^4\le24300(\nu+1)^4.
\]
The sum equals 300 by differentiating the geometric series four times.

For \(S_B\), the successive weight ratio is \(w_{s+1}/w_s=r(s+m)/(s+1)\), which is nonincreasing. Thus the preceding estimate applies, including \(B=0\). Every original conditional total is stochastically at most \(S_{B_0}\), since it is the same positive weighted law truncated to a smaller prefix. For each \(1\le k\le4\), the power-mean inequality therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_R(X_\Sigma/m)^k
&\le24300^{k/4}\bigl((\mu_0+1)/m\bigr)^k\\
&\le24300\bigl((\mu_0+1)/m\bigr)^4\\
&\le24300\cdot81(\mu_1/m)^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second inequality uses \((\mu_0+1)/m\ge1\); the last uses \(\mu_0+1\le2\mu_1+1\le3\mu_1\), because \(\mu_1\ge m/2\ge1\).

Finally put \(Q=\sum_i b_i^2\) and \(Q_4=\sum_i b_i^4\), with exactly the meanings used in the composition identities of 9ba4a3963a51edd3. Given \((Y,X_\Sigma)=(y,s)\), the vector \(\mathbf X\) is uniform on the complete compositions \(\mathbf C_s\) of \(s\) into \(m\) parts. The uniform-composition normalization is identical to that of this predecessor: each vector has probability \(1/\binom{s+m-1}{m-1}\). Its conditional mean in the direction \(\mathbf b\) is zero. The second-moment identity and fourth-moment bound in that accepted fact give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_R N^2&=\frac{Q}{m(m+1)}\mathbb E_R[X_\Sigma(X_\Sigma+m)],\\
\mathbb E_R N^4&\le Q^2\left(9\mathbb E_R(X_\Sigma/m)^4+18\mathbb E_R(X_\Sigma/m)^3
 +10\mathbb E_R(X_\Sigma/m)^2+\mathbb E_R(X_\Sigma/m)\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the fourth bound uses \(Q_4\le Q^2\), exactly as in that fact; no source-law estimate for positive \(\beta\) is imported. The raw-moment estimate just proved yields
\(\mathbb E_R N^4\le38\cdot24300\cdot81\,Q^2(\mu_1/m)^4=74795400\,Q^2(\mu_1/m)^4\).
On the other hand, the original middle mass \(p_1\ge1/3\) and Jensen's inequality give
\[
\mathbb E_R N^2\ge\frac{Q\mu_1^2}{3m(m+1)}\ge\frac Q6(\mu_1/m)^2,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(m/(m+1)\ge1/2\). Combining these two estimates proves
\[
\mathbb E_R N^4\le74795400\cdot36(\mathbb E_R N^2)^2
=2692634400(\mathbb E_R N^2)^2
\le3\cdot10^9(\mathbb E_R N^2)^2.
\]
If \(Q=0\), then every coefficient is zero and the same conclusion is immediate. Every averaging step used the original conditional laws and original height probabilities, completing the proof.
