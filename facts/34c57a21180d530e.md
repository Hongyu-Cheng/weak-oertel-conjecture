## Statement

Let a planar convex body have exactly three positive integer interval fibers at consecutive bases. Assume the true centroid for summed original mixed length has integer base. Its normalized closed-halfspace depth is at least $(\sqrt3-1)/2$, sharply. More precisely, translate the bases to $-1,0,1$ and shear both endpoint centers to zero, reflecting heights if needed. The endpoints then have the same length $a>0$ and the middle has length $b\ge a$ and center $c\in[0,(b-a)/2]$. With $M=b+2a$ and centroid height $t=bc/M$, the raw depth at $(0,t)$ is
$$
\min\{b/2+2ac/M+2(a/2-t)_+,\ M/2-2ac/M\}.
$$
Equality in the universal bound is attained by the actual lifted convex hull of $[0,1],[0,1+\sqrt3],[0,1]$ at $-1,0,1$, with true centroid $(0,1)$.

## Proof

The positive base masses put their mean strictly between the endpoints, so an integer mean is the middle base and forces equal endpoint lengths. An affine shear in the base can set the two endpoint centers to zero. A height reflection makes the middle center nonnegative. These maps preserve the lattice of bases, the mixed length and the cap class. The middle contains the endpoint midpoint interval $[-a/2,a/2]$, giving $b\ge a$ and $0\le c\le(b-a)/2$. The actual centroid height is $t=bc/M$. Any additional zero-length fibers have zero measure in every cap and do not affect the centroid. The hull of the three positive fibers has these exact slices by midpoint compatibility and hence the same measure; use this hull when applying c680a307d3f26cec.

In the accepted pointwise formula take both endpoint lengths equal to $a$, the middle length $b$ and shift $c$. Then $g(t)=2(a/2-t)_+$ and $g(-t)=a$ for $t\ge0$. Since $c-t=2ac/M$, its upward and downward raw minima are respectively the two expressions in the statement. That formula minimizes over all finite tilts including their constant tails; the pure-base caps have mass $b+a$ and do not lower the minimum.

Write $r=b/a$. The second branch divided by $M$ is at least $1/2-(r-1)/(r+2)^2\ge5/12>(\sqrt3-1)/2$, because $(r-1)/(r+2)^2$ is maximized at $r=4$ over $r\ge1$. The first branch decreases in $c$ up to $bc/M=a/2$ and increases thereafter. Its minimum over compatible $c$ is attained at $\min((b-a)/2,aM/(2b))$. The two upper limits cross at $r_0=1+\sqrt3$. The resulting first-branch normalized minima are
$$
\frac12-\frac{(r-1)^2}{(r+2)^2}\quad(1\le r\le r_0),\qquad
\frac{r^2+2}{2r(r+2)}\quad(r\ge r_0).
$$
The first is decreasing for $r>1$, and the derivative of the second is $(r^2-2r-2)/(r^2(r+2)^2)$, nonnegative for $r\ge r_0$. Their common minimum is $(\sqrt3-1)/2$.

In the equality hull the midpoint of the endpoint intervals is $[0,1]$ inside the middle interval, so the positive slices are exactly the three given and the projection is $[-1,1]$. Symmetry gives base mean zero, and the continuous mean is $(2+(1+\sqrt3)^2)/(2(3+\sqrt3))=1$. The cap $x\ge1$ captures length $\sqrt3$ from the middle and zero length from the endpoints, giving fraction $\sqrt3/(3+\sqrt3)=(\sqrt3-1)/2$.
