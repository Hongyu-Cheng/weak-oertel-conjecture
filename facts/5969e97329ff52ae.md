## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $A\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be compact and convex, and put $P=A\cap\mathbb Z^n$. Let the full-dimensional polyhedron $L=\{z:a_r\cdot z\le\beta_r\}$ have $2^n$ nonzero rows, with $\operatorname{int}L\cap P=\varnothing$ and one witness $p_r\in P$ on each row that strictly satisfies all other rows. Index the witnesses by their parities $r\bmod 2$. For $z\in\mathbb Z^n$ write $[z]=z\bmod2$ and $R(z)=(z+p_{[z]})/2$.

Every $y\in\mathbb Z^n$ has exactly the $2^n$ distinct preimages $\{2y-p_r\}_r$, hence at most $2^n$ in $P$, and $R(P)\subseteq P$. Every full-lattice orbit terminates at a fixed witness. If $z_0=z$, $z_{j+1}=R(z_j)$, and $z_k=p_s$, then
$$
z-p_s=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}2^j(p_s-p_{[z_j]}).
$$
Thus the basin of $p_s$ lies in $p_s$ plus the nonnegative integer semigroup generated by the $p_s-p_r$. On that basin $a_s\cdot z\ge\beta_s$, with equality only at $p_s$. In particular $L\cap\mathbb Z^n=\{p_r\}_r$. On $P$ the potential $V(z)=\max_r(a_r\cdot z-\beta_r)$ is nonnegative, vanishes exactly at the witnesses and satisfies $V(R(z))\le V(z)/2$. If $P$ has a nonroot and $\delta$ is its least strictly positive potential, an orbit from a nonroot $z\in P$ takes at most $1+\lceil\log_2(V(z)/\delta)\rceil$ steps to terminate.

## Proof

At a witness, perturbing across its own nonzero row with all other strict inequalities maintained shows that its row is irredundant and the witness lies in its facet's relative interior. Apply accepted fact 095440dfdf5f6eab with its convex domain equal to $A$. The hull $W$ of the witnesses has exactly the witnesses as integer points, and their parities exhaust the $2^n$ classes. The formula for $R$ preserves integrality by parity and preserves $A$ for an input in $A$ by convexity. For a preimage of parity $r$, solving $R(z)=y$ gives $z=2y-p_r$, which itself has parity $r$. This proves the inverse census.

Every witness is fixed. From any full-lattice starting point $z$, the orbit stays in the compact hull of $\{z\}\cup W$, which has only finitely many lattice points. For a nearest $q\in W$ to $z_j$, the midpoint $(q+p_{[z_j]})/2$ lies in $W$. Hence $\operatorname{dist}(z_{j+1},W)\le\operatorname{dist}(z_j,W)/2$. The positive distances to $W$ among the finitely many possible states have a positive minimum if there are any. The orbit must therefore reach $W\cap\mathbb Z^n$ and stay at its witness.

Substitute $z_j=2z_{j+1}-p_{[z_j]}$ repeatedly and subtract $p_s$ to obtain the displayed formula. Each summand has nonnegative margin under $a_s$, and the margin of $p_s-p_r$ is strictly positive for $r\ne s$. If $z\ne p_s$, at least one digit differs from $s$ by the same formula. This proves the exterior inequality and shows that the only lattice points satisfying every row of $L$ are its witnesses. Each witness satisfies all rows and is tight in one, so $V$ has the stated signs. For each $r$, the slack margin of $p_{[z]}$ in row $r$ is at most zero, hence $a_r\cdot R(z)-\beta_r\le(a_r\cdot z-\beta_r)/2$. Taking the maximum proves contraction. After the displayed number of steps the potential is strictly below $\delta$, so it is zero.
