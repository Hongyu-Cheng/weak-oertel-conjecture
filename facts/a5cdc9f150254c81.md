## Statement

For every positive integer \(N\), let
\[
C_N:=\{(z,x)\in\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^N:
|z_i|\le x_i\le2\text{ for }1\le i\le N\}.
\]
Use exactly the positive fibers, half-mass metronoids, off-diagonal same-parity matching capacities \(d_z(q)\le a_z/2\), edge-specific affine realizability, and quantities \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}\) and \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}\) of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5, with \(n=d=N\). Then
\[
W=4^N,\qquad
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_N)
=\frac{4^N-2^N}{4},\qquad
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_N)}
{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_N)}=0.
\]
Consequently no absolute \(\lambda>0\) satisfies the universal comparison \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C)\ge\lambda\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C)\) for all positive-mass mixed convex bodies \(C\).

An explicit uniform upper bound is as follows. Put
\[
B_N:=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(5,N)}\binom Nj,\qquad
J_N:=\left\lfloor4+\frac43\sqrt N\right\rfloor,\qquad
F_N:=(1+4\sqrt N)^{128\sqrt N}
\sum_{j=0}^{\min(J_N,N)}\binom Nj2^j,
\qquad
\beta:=\frac34\,4^{1/16}<1.
\]
For every \(N\ge2\),
\[
\frac{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{aff}}(C_N)}
{\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_N)}
\le
\frac{2^N}{2^N-1}
\left(
\frac1{8\sqrt N}
+B_N\beta^N
+F_N2^{-N/16}
\right).
\]
Here \(\log F_N=O(\sqrt N\log N)=o(N)\), so every term on the right tends to zero.

## Proof

The body \(C_N\) is the Cartesian product of \(N\) copies of the full-dimensional compact triangle \(\{(t,u):|t|\le u\le2\}\). It is therefore a convex body in \(\mathbb R^{2N}\).

We first compute all actual positive fibers. An integer fiber has positive \(N\)-dimensional volume exactly when \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^N\); a coordinate \(|z_i|=2\) makes the volume zero, and a coordinate \(|z_i|>2\) makes the fiber empty. For a positive fiber put \(v:=|z|\in\{0,1\}^N\), with coordinatewise absolute value, and \(k:=\sum_i v_i\). Then
\[
(C_N)_z=\prod_{i=1}^N[v_i,2],\qquad
a_z=2^{N-k},\qquad
c_z=c_v:=\mathbf1+\frac v2.
\]
The vector \(v\) is also the parity vector of \(z\). Its parity class consists of all \(2^k\) independent sign choices on its support, and every fiber in this class is the same box. Thus every parity class has total fiber volume \(2^k2^{N-k}=2^N\), and there are \(2^N\) classes. This proves \(W=4^N\).

The class \(v=0\) is a singleton and has no eligible off-diagonal edge. In every other class, the sum of endpoint capacities is \(2^N/2\), so its free matching mass is at most \(2^N/4\). This upper bound is attained by the unordered pairs \(\{z,-z\}\), with weight \(q_{\{z,-z\}}=a_z/2\). Each vertex is used exactly once and its degree is exactly \(a_z/2\); these pairs are distinct-endpoint same-parity pairs. Hence \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_N)=(2^N-1)2^N/4\), consistently with the exact free-capacity formula in accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5.

We next obtain a dimension-independent Euclidean outer bound for every actual half-mass metronoid. Denote by \(\mathcal K_v\) the common half-mass metronoid of the fibers in parity class \(v\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d makes it compact and convex. Let \(X\) be uniformly distributed on its fiber box, with mean \(c_v\). Every \(y\in\mathcal K_v\) can be written as \(y=2\mathbb E[Xf(X)]\), where \(0\le f\le1\) and \(\mathbb E f=1/2\). The coordinates of \(X\) are independent uniforms on intervals of lengths \(2-v_i\), so
\[
\operatorname{Var}(u\cdot X)
=\sum_i\frac{(2-v_i)^2}{12}u_i^2
\le\frac13\|u\|_2^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(f)\le\frac14.
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz gives
\[
|u\cdot(y-c_v)|
=2|\mathbb E[(u\cdot(X-c_v))(f-1/2)]|
\le\frac1{\sqrt3}\|u\|_2.
\]
Taking \(u=y-c_v\) proves
\(\mathcal K_v\subseteq c_v+R\mathbb B_2^N\), where \(R:=1/\sqrt3\) and \(\mathbb B_2^N\) is the closed Euclidean unit ball.

Fix any affinely realizable matching \(q\), with affine map \(s(m)=Am+b\), where \(A\) is an arbitrary real \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrix and \(b\in\mathbb R^N\). There are no restrictions on pair correlations or on the witnesses of different edges. For a positive edge in parity class \(v\), its midpoint is \(m=(z+z')/2\in\{-1,0,1\}^N\), with \(\operatorname{supp}(m)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(v)\). The endpoint metronoids are identical, and convexity gives \((\mathcal K_z+\mathcal K_{z'})/2=\mathcal K_v\). Therefore the actual edge-specific witness identity implies
\(\|Am+b-c_v\|_2\le R\).

For this fixed \(A,b\), define the outer set of compatible midpoints
\[
T_v:=\{m\in\{-1,0,1\}^N:
\operatorname{supp}(m)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(v),\
\|Am+b-c_v\|_2\le R\}.
\]
It may include midpoints that do not correspond to any eligible edge; using this larger set only enlarges our upper bounds. Every positive edge of \(q\) in class \(v\) has its midpoint in \(T_v\).

Here is the full endpoint ledger. For a fixed \(m\in T_v\) with \(j\) nonzero coordinates, any endpoint \(z\) of an edge with midpoint \(m\) must satisfy \(z_i=m_i\) in those \(j\) coordinates. In the other \(k-j\) coordinates of the parity support it has at most two sign choices. Thus at most \(2^{k-j}\) vertices can be endpoints of such edges. If \(E_v\) is the union of vertices incident to positive edges of \(q\) in class \(v\), then
\[
\frac{\#E_v}{2^k}
\le\min\left\{1,\sum_{m\in T_v}2^{-|\operatorname{supp}(m)|}\right\}.
\]
Write \(Q_v\) for the matching mass in this class. All its vertices have capacity \(a_z/2=2^{N-k}/2\). Summing the original degree inequalities, and counting each edge twice, gives
\[
2Q_v\le\#E_v\,\frac{2^{N-k}}2,
\qquad
Q_v\le\frac{2^N}{4}\,
\min\left\{1,\sum_{m\in T_v}2^{-|\operatorname{supp}(m)|}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a capacity bound for every admissible fractional matching, rather than an estimate only for one pairing law or one source graph.

We now split the matrix \(A\) according to its singular values at \(\varepsilon:=N^{-1/4}\). Let \(h\) be the number of singular values strictly below \(\varepsilon\), counting zero singular values. Let \(P_{\mathrm{weak}}\) be the orthogonal projection onto the associated left singular subspace; it has rank \(h\), and \(\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}A\|_{2\to2}\le\varepsilon\). If \(T_v\ne\varnothing\), then some \(m\in T_v\) has \(\|m\|_2\le\sqrt N\), and hence
\[
\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}(c_v-b)\|_2
\le R+\varepsilon\sqrt N
\le2N^{1/4}.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that \(h\ge128\sqrt N\). Choose \(v\) uniformly from \(\{0,1\}^N\), so \(c_v=(5/4)\mathbf1+Y\), where the coordinates of \(Y\) are independent and equal to \(1/4\) or \(-1/4\) with equal probabilities. Put \(t:=b-(5/4)\mathbf1\) and \(\Xi:=\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}(Y-t)\|_2^2\). Direct expansion gives
\[
\mathbb E\Xi=\frac h{16}+\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}t\|_2^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\Xi)
=\frac1{64}\sum_{i<j}(P_{\mathrm{weak}})_{ij}^2
+\frac14\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}t\|_2^2
\le\frac h{128}+\frac14\|P_{\mathrm{weak}}t\|_2^2
\le\frac14\mathbb E\Xi.
\tag{3}
\]
For completeness, the diagonal terms of \(Y^{\mathsf T}P_{\mathrm{weak}}Y\) are constant. Its distinct off-diagonal products are pairwise uncorrelated, and each has second moment \(1/256\), giving the first variance term. The quadratic and linear terms are uncorrelated by the symmetry of the independent signs. Finally \(\sum_{i<j}(P_{\mathrm{weak}})_{ij}^2\le\operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathrm{weak}}^2)/2=h/2\). Thus (3) uses no distributional assumption on matching endpoints.

Since \(\mathbb E\Xi\ge h/16\ge8\sqrt N\), Chebyshev's inequality implies
\[
\mathbb P(\Xi\le4\sqrt N)
\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\Xi)}{(\mathbb E\Xi/2)^2}
\le\frac1{\mathbb E\Xi}
\le\frac{16}{h}
\le\frac1{8\sqrt N}.
\]
By (2), every parity class with \(T_v\ne\varnothing\) belongs to this event. Consequently at most \(2^N/(8\sqrt N)\) classes can carry positive edges. Applying (1) gives
\[
|q|\le\frac{2^N}{4}\,\frac{2^N}{8\sqrt N}.
\tag{4}
\]

It remains to treat \(h<128\sqrt N\). Let \(P_{\mathrm{strong}}\) be the orthogonal projection onto the right singular subspace associated to singular values at least \(\varepsilon\); its rank is \(N-h\). For each parity vector \(v\), invert those singular values on the corresponding left component of \(c_v-b\), obtaining a vector \(m_v^0\) in the range of \(P_{\mathrm{strong}}\). Explicitly, if \(A=U\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i)V^{\mathsf T}\) is a singular value decomposition, then
\(m_v^0=\sum_{i:\sigma_i\ge\varepsilon}\sigma_i^{-1}\langle U_i,c_v-b\rangle V_i\).
Every \(m\in T_v\) satisfies
\[
\|P_{\mathrm{strong}}m-m_v^0\|_2\le\frac R\varepsilon
=\frac1{\sqrt3}N^{1/4}=:K.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(T_v\) lies within distance \(K\) of the affine plane \(L_v:=m_v^0+\ker P_{\mathrm{strong}}\), whose dimension is \(h\).

We prove the required bound on the number of ternary points in this tube. If \(T_v\) is empty, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose \(m_*\in T_v\), and let \(\pi\) be orthogonal projection onto \(L_v\). All projected points \(\pi(m)\), \(m\in T_v\), lie in the ball of radius \(2\sqrt N\) about \(\pi(m_*)\) within \(L_v\), since
\(\|\pi(m)-\pi(m_*)\|_2\le\|m-m_*\|_2\le2\sqrt N\).
That ball admits a radius-one net with at most \((1+4\sqrt N)^h\) points. Indeed, choose a maximal set whose distinct points have distance greater than one. The balls of radius \(1/2\) about its points are disjoint and lie in the ball of radius \(2\sqrt N+1/2\), so comparison of \(h\)-dimensional volumes gives this bound; maximality makes it a radius-one net. For \(h=0\) a single point is a net and the same bound is one.

Assign each \(m\in T_v\) to one net point at distance at most one from \(\pi(m)\). In any nonempty cell choose one ternary representative \(m'\). Two points in that cell have projection difference of norm at most two. Their normal residuals \(m-\pi(m)\) and \(m'-\pi(m')\) have norm at most \(K\) by (5). The projection difference and normal difference are orthogonal, so
\[
\|m-m'\|_2^2\le4+4K^2
=4+\frac43\sqrt N.
\]
Since all coordinates are integers, each coordinate in which \(m\) differs from \(m'\) contributes at least one to this squared distance. There are at most \(J_N\) changed coordinates, with at most two alternative ternary values for each. A cell therefore has at most \(\sum_{j=0}^{\min(J_N,N)}\binom Nj2^j\) points. Combining the cells and \(h<128\sqrt N\) proves
\[
\#T_v\le F_N\quad\text{for every }v.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\(\log F_N\le128\sqrt N\log(1+4\sqrt N)+\log(N+1)+J_N\log(2N)=O(\sqrt N\log N)\).

We finally split midpoints by their number of nonzero coordinates. First fix any ternary vector \(m\), with no restriction on its support. There are at most \(B_N\) parity vectors \(v\) for which \(\|Am+b-c_v\|_2\le R\). To see this, if one such vector \(v_0\) exists, every other satisfies \(\|c_v-c_{v_0}\|_2\le2R\). But \(\|c_v-c_{v_0}\|_2^2\) is one quarter of their Hamming distance. That distance is at most \(16R^2=16/3\), hence at most five. This gives the stated bound by counting Hamming neighbors of \(v_0\).

The total number of ternary vectors having at most \(N/16\) nonzero coordinates satisfies
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor N/16\rfloor}\binom Nj2^j
\le4^{N/16}\sum_{j=0}^N\binom Nj(2/4)^j
=4^{N/16}(3/2)^N.
\]
Therefore the number of parity classes whose \(T_v\) contains at least one such small-support midpoint is at most \(B_N4^{N/16}(3/2)^N\). Their fraction among the \(2^N\) classes is at most \(B_N\beta^N\). The strict inequality \(\beta<1\) follows, for example, from \(\beta^{16}=3^{16}/4^{15}<1\).

In every remaining parity class, all vectors of \(T_v\) have more than \(N/16\) nonzero coordinates. Equation (6), followed by the endpoint ledger (1), bounds the fraction of available endpoint signs used in that class by \(F_N2^{-N/16}\). Classes with small-support midpoints have fraction at most one. Summing (1) over all parity classes yields
\[
|q|\le\frac{2^N}{4}\,2^N
\left(B_N\beta^N+F_N2^{-N/16}\right).
\tag{7}
\]

Every matrix \(A\) belongs to one of the two cases (4) and (7). Adding their nonnegative upper-bound expressions gives a uniform bound valid for every affinely realizable \(q\), every affine map, and every choice of edge-specific witnesses. Taking the supremum over \(q\) and dividing by \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{free}}(C_N)=(2^N-1)2^N/4\) proves the explicit estimate in the statement. Since \(B_N\) is polynomial in \(N\), \(\beta<1\), and \(\log F_N=o(N)\), this proves the capacity ratio tends to zero and disproves the universal comparison.

To verify explicitly that the locked Weak Oertel target is not disproved, the largest fiber, at \(z=0\), is \([0,2]^N\) with volume \(2^N\) and centroid \(\mathbf1\). Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d with mass equal to the entire fiber volume gives centroid halfspace depth at least \(\gamma_N2^N\), where \(\gamma_N=(N/(N+1))^N>1/e\). Every ambient closed halfspace containing \((0,\mathbf1)\) has a fiber section containing that centroid, so its mixed volume is at least \(\gamma_N2^N=\gamma_N2^{-N}W\). Thus this point already meets the locked target for every \(c_0<1/e\).
