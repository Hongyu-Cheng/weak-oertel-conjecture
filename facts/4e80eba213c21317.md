## Statement

For any integer $N\ge2$ and positive $d$-body $K$, let $C=[-N,N]\times K$ with one integer coordinate, let $\mu$ be normalized mixed volume, let $Q(w)=|w|^2$, and let $y=(0,g_K)$ for the volume centroid $g_K$. The convex-order contraction has unique optimizer $\delta_y$, the finite affine obstacle $h(w)=2y\cdot w-|y|^2$ attains the dual, and the row from $y$ sends its whole mass to $\mu$ in one affine contact set. Yet the full original mixed volume cannot be realized by the own fiber and finitely many off-own equal-mass endpoint pairs with physical midpoint laws at $0$ whose sum is dominated by the own volume.

## Proof

The full mixed probability has mean $y$ by base reflection and by equality of its fibers. Every primal feasible law has this mean by testing both signs of each linear functional. Jensen for the strictly convex $Q$ gives the unique minimum at $\delta_y$, which is feasible by Jensen for all convex tests and $y\in C$. The identity $Q(w)-h(w)=|w-y|^2$ gives the obstacle, and $\int h\,d\mu=Q(y)$ attains the value by weak duality. A martingale from $\delta_y$ has a single row equal to $\mu$, with mean $y$. Taking the affine majorant $\ell=h$ gives the whole ambient space as its contact set.

In unnormalized mixed volume let $b=|K|>0$. The mass off the own fiber is $2Nb$. Exhausting it by equal-mass pairs forces $2\sum_jm_j=2Nb$. Each midpoint pushforward preserves the mass $m_j$ of its coupling. A summed midpoint law dominated by the own volume has mass at most $b$, contradicting $\sum_jm_j=Nb>b$.
