## Statement

Let \(a,b,Q,m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) satisfy \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), \(Q\ge2ab\), and \(m\ge1\). Let \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\lambda\ge4(m+1)/(Q+1)\). On the full support
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:\sum_{i=1}^m x_i+ay_1+by_2\le Q\right\},
\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y_1,y_2)=\frac{e^{\lambda(ay_1+by_2)}}{\mathcal Z},
\]
where \(\mathcal Z\) is the sum of the displayed numerator over every point of \(D\), put
\[
n(q)=\left|\{(y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:ay_1+by_2=q\}\right|,
\qquad
F_m(u)=\binom{u+m}{m}\quad(u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}).
\]
For integers \(0\le q_0\le q_1\le Q\) with occupied endpoints, define the complete-band count and coefficient
\[
N_{q_0,q_1}=\sum_{q=q_0}^{q_1}n(q),
\qquad
\eta_{q_0,q_1}=\frac{F_m(Q-q_1)N_{q_0,q_1}e^{\lambda q_0}}{\mathcal Z}.
\]
These are exactly the original normal-band quantities of d1c92fee58caca38, specialized by \(h_1=a\), \(h_2=b\), \(g_{\rm ar}=1\), and \(L=Q\). Then some complete band satisfies
\[
\lceil Q/2\rceil\le q_0\le q_1\le Q,
\qquad
\eta_{q_0,q_1}\ge\frac{e^{-13}}{216(m+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, the maximum \(\eta_*\) over all admissible complete bands has this lower bound, with absolute constants \(c=e^{-13}/216\) and \(A=1\).

More explicitly, let \(E_{q_0,q_1}=\{(y_1,y_2)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:q_0\le ay_1+by_2\le q_1\}\) and let \(\nu\) be uniform on \(\Delta_{Q-q_1,m}\times E_{q_0,q_1}\), where \(\Delta_{T,m}=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i x_i\le T\}\). Extended by zero off that product, it satisfies \(p\ge\eta_{q_0,q_1}\nu\) pointwise with the original normalization \(\mathcal Z\).

## Proof

Put \(P=ab\), \(k=m+1\), \(K=\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\), and \(r=e^{-\lambda}\in(0,1)\). The exact arithmetic of d1c92fee58caca38 gives, for every integer \(q\ge0\),
\[
\lfloor q/P\rfloor\le n(q)\le\lfloor q/P\rfloor+1.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(\lceil Q/2\rceil\le q\le Q\), we have \(q/P\ge Q/(2P)\ge1\). The elementary inequality \(\lfloor t\rfloor\ge t/2\) for \(t\ge1\) therefore gives \(n(q)\ge Q/(4P)>0\). At every \(0\le q\le Q\), (2) gives \(n(q)\le Q/P+1\le3Q/(2P)\), because \(Q/P\ge2\).

At heavy height \(q\), the number of light-coordinate points is \(F_m(Q-q)\). Indeed, adjoining the nonnegative integer slack makes those points the compositions of \(Q-q\) into \(k=m+1\) parts. Hence, with the residual coordinate \(u=Q-q\), the full original normalizer is
\[
\mathcal Z
=\sum_{q=0}^Q n(q)F_m(Q-q)e^{\lambda q}
=e^{\lambda Q}\sum_{u=0}^Q n(Q-u)F_m(u)r^u.
\tag{3}
\]
Introduce the positive comparison sum
\(S_\infty=\sum_{u=0}^\infty F_m(u)r^u=(1-r)^{-k}\).
To verify this identity and the probability calculations below, take \(k\) independent nonnegative geometric variables, each with mass \((1-r)r^v\) at \(v\ge0\), and denote their sum by \(\mathsf U\). Counting compositions shows
\[
\mathbb P(\mathsf U=u)=\frac{F_m(u)r^u}{S_\infty},
\qquad
\mu:=\mathbb E\mathsf U=\frac{kr}{1-r}=\frac{k}{e^\lambda-1}\le\frac{k}{\lambda}\le\frac{Q+1}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]
The inequality \(e^\lambda-1\ge\lambda\) was used here. These geometric variables supply only a comparison law; the original source probabilities in (3) are not changed.

For any integer interval \([u_0,u_1]\subseteq[0,K]\), choose \(q_0=Q-u_1\), \(q_1=Q-u_0\). Every height of this band is occupied by the lower bound following (2). Its full heavy-pair count is at least \((u_1-u_0+1)Q/(4P)\). On the other hand, (3) and the global upper bound following (2) give \(e^{-\lambda Q}\mathcal Z\le[3Q/(2P)]S_\infty\). Consequently
\[
\eta_{q_0,q_1}
\ge\frac16\,
\frac{(u_1-u_0+1)F_m(u_0)r^{u_1}}{S_\infty}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus it remains to select an interval inside \([0,K]\) with a large coefficient on the right. Markov's inequality and (4) give
\[
\mathbb P(\mathsf U>K)
\le\frac{\mu}{K+1}
\le\frac{Q+1}{4(K+1)}\le\frac12.
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality holds for both even and odd \(Q\).

First suppose \(\lambda\ge1/4\). Then \(\mu\le4k\). Put \(R=\min\{K,16k\}\), an integer. If \(R=K\), (6) applies. If \(R=16k\), Markov's inequality gives \(\mathbb P(\mathsf U>R)\le4k/(16k+1)<1/4\). Therefore \(\mathbb P(0\le\mathsf U\le R)\ge1/2\) in either case. There are at most \(16k+1\le17k\) integers in this interval, so some \(u\in[0,R]\) has \(\mathbb P(\mathsf U=u)\ge1/(34k)\). Taking the singleton interval \(u_0=u_1=u\) in (5) yields
\(\eta_{Q-u,Q-u}\ge1/(204k)\ge e^{-13}/(216k)\).

Now suppose \(0<\lambda<1/4\). We first exclude an initial part of the comparison law. The elementary bound \(e<3<(4/3)^4\) implies \(e^{1/4}<4/3\) and hence \(r>3/4\). Independence of the geometric variables gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{-3\lambda\mathsf U}
=\left(\frac{1-r}{1-r^4}\right)^k
=\frac{1}{(1+r+r^2+r^3)^k}.
\]
Set \(T=\lceil k/(12\lambda)\rceil\). Since \(\mathsf U\) is integer valued, \(\{\mathsf U<T\}=\{\mathsf U<k/(12\lambda)\}\). Exponential Markov's inequality, with the displayed Laplace transform, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\mathsf U<T)
&\le\left(\frac{e^{1/4}}{1+r+r^2+r^3}\right)^k\\
&<\left(\frac{4/3}{1+3/4+9/16+27/64}\right)^k
=\left(\frac{256}{525}\right)^k
<2^{-k}\le\frac14,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
where \(k=m+1\ge2\).

In this case put \(R=\min\{K,\lfloor4k/\lambda\rfloor\}\). If the minimum is \(K\), (6) applies. Otherwise \(R+1>4k/\lambda\), and (4) and Markov's inequality give \(\mathbb P(\mathsf U>R)<1/4\). Thus in either case \(\mathbb P(\mathsf U>R)\le1/2\). Together with (7), this proves
\(\mathbb P(T\le\mathsf U\le R)\ge1/4\), and in particular \(T\le R\).

Let \(w=\lfloor1/\lambda\rfloor\ge4\). Partition the consecutive integers \(T,\ldots,R\) into consecutive blocks of \(w\) integers, except that the last block may be shorter. Since \(w\ge1/(2\lambda)\), the number \(J\) of blocks satisfies
\[
J=\left\lceil\frac{R-T+1}{w}\right\rceil
\le\frac{4k/\lambda+1}{w}+1
\le8k+2\lambda+1\le8k+2\le9k.
\tag{8}
\]
Some block \([u_0,u_1]\) therefore has comparison probability at least \(1/(4J)\ge1/(36k)\). This block lies in \([0,K]\), has \(u_0\ge T\ge k/(12\lambda)\), and has \(u_1-u_0<w\le1/\lambda\).

For every integer \(u\) in that block, the product formula for \(F_m\) and \(1+t\le e^t\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{F_m(u)}{F_m(u_0)}
&=\prod_{i=1}^m\left(1+\frac{u-u_0}{u_0+i}\right)\\
&\le\exp\left(\frac{m(u_1-u_0)}{u_0+1}\right)
\le e^{12m/k}\le e^{12},
\qquad
\frac{r^u}{r^{u_1}}=e^{\lambda(u_1-u)}\le e.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing these inequalities over the whole block gives
\[
\frac{(u_1-u_0+1)F_m(u_0)r^{u_1}}{S_\infty}
\ge e^{-13}\mathbb P(u_0\le\mathsf U\le u_1)
\ge\frac{e^{-13}}{36k}.
\tag{9}
\]
Substituting (9) into (5) proves (1) also in the small-tilt case. The residual interval has upper endpoint at most \(K\), so the corresponding normal band always has \(q_0\ge Q-K=\lceil Q/2\rceil\), as required.

Finally, \(\Delta_{Q-q_1,m}\times E_{q_0,q_1}\subseteq D\), because every heavy point of the band has height at most \(q_1\). The uniform product law \(\nu\) gives each of its atoms mass \(1/[F_m(Q-q_1)N_{q_0,q_1}]\). Every such atom has original source mass at least \(e^{\lambda q_0}/\mathcal Z\), since \(\lambda>0\). Multiplying proves \(p\ge\eta_{q_0,q_1}\nu\) pointwise. Thus the coefficient uses the minimum exponential over the entire selected band and the full original partition, with all heavy pairs and all residues retained.
