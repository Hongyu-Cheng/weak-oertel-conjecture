## Statement

For $P=\{0,1\}^2$, $d\ge1$, and a full-dimensional $d$-simplex $K$, the hull of the placed fibers $K_{j,z}=jz_1z_2e_1+K$ has exactly those integer fibers and maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth $\gamma_d/4$ for every $j\ge1$, where $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Subtracting the best ordinary affine fit of the representatives $jz_1z_2e_1+x_0$ for any fixed $x_0\in K$ and contracting by the residual array norm yields four limiting graph atoms of equal weight, with maximum normalized atomic depth $1/4$.

## Proof

Every $z$ is a vertex of $[0,1]^2$ and hence an exposed base. A representation of a vertex uses only that vertex, proving the exact sections. The hull has full dimension since it projects onto a two-dimensional square and contains a positive $d$-volume fiber. By fact 2acc8bc803acf1c0 each fiber point has mixed raw depth equal to its own simplex depth, whose maximum is $\gamma_d|K|$. The total mixed volume is $4|K|$.

On the four-site array the function $f(z)=(z_1-1/2)(z_2-1/2)$ is orthogonal to $1,z_1,z_2$, and $z_1z_2-f(z)$ is affine. Thus the residual array is $jf(z)e_1$ and its Frobenius norm is $j/2$. The transformed fiber is $2f(z)e_1+(2/j)(K-x_0)$, which shrinks to the singleton $2f(z)e_1$. The mass of each fiber remains $1/4$, so the pushforwards converge weakly to the four equal atoms. Every halfspace containing a graph point contains its atom, and an exposing base halfspace isolates that point, so its atomic depth is exactly $1/4$.
