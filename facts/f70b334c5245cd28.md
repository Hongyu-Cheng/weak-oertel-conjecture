## Source

- [595eea8dbe136178](../candidates/595eea8dbe136178.json), 595eea8dbe136178:02 Exact failure of automatic mass-budget preservation, trapezoid.

## Statement

On the actual interval fibers $K_0=[0,1]$ and $K_1=[0,1/2]$, the supporting pair $a_0(z)=0$, $b_0(z)=1-z/2$ gives the two-cap strip cover $x+z/2-1/2\ge0$ or $1/2-x\ge0$. Its cap masses are each $1=2M/3$, where $M=3/2$. The same source admits the two-cap horizontal cover at $x=3/8$ with masses $3/4=M/2$ each. Thus a valid choice in the supporting-strip operation can exceed the individual budget $M/2$ even when that budget is feasible.

## Proof

The compact convex trapezoid $0\le z\le1$, $0\le x\le1-z/2$ has precisely the two stated integer fibers. Its lower and upper endpoint functions are $a_0,b_0$ and $W=1$. Its supporting width is at most $W$ at both bases. The first strip cap covers $[1/2,1]$ at zero and all of $[0,1/2]$ at one. The second covers $[0,1/2]$ at both bases. Thus each cap has length $1$ and together they cover $S$. The horizontal lower cap $x\le3/8$ captures $3/8$ at each base; the upper cap $x\ge3/8$ captures $5/8$ and $1/8$. Each has mass $3/4$ and their union is $S$. The comparison concerns a particular valid output of the supporting-strip operation and does not assert that all alternative bounded-hinge covers must fail.
