## Statement

Use the entropy-row and closed-halfspace conventions of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, with the exact aliases \(A=D\), \(m=n=r\), \(p=\nu\), and \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\lambda\). The maximum-entropy row \(r_{D,\mathbf j}\) means its \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) with \(E=D\). In particular,
\[
\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)
=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^r\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\
                    \mathbf j\in H}}\nu(H).
\]

For every odd integer \(r\ge9\), set \(k=r-1\), \(L=3^{k/2}\), and \(a=3^k=L^2\). Write points of \(\mathbb R^r\) as \((t,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^k\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times[-1,1]^k)
                         \cup\{(L,\mathbf0)\}\bigr),\\
D&=P\cap\mathbb Z^r,\qquad \mathbf j=(1,\mathbf0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(P\) is a polytope, not the product law denoted \(P\) in the accepted fact. There is a unique positive real number \(q\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{t=2}^{L}(t-1)q^t=L^2,
\qquad 1<q<16^{1/L}.
\tag{1}
\]
Set \(Q=\sum_{t=1}^{L}q^t\), \(Z=a+Q\), and define the law on \(D\) by \(\nu_{(t,\mathbf y)}=q^t/Z\). Then \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^r\) has full intrinsic rank \(r\), and \(\nu\) is positive on every point of \(D\). It is log-affine with \(\boldsymbol\lambda=(\log q,\mathbf0)\), its mean is exactly the integral interior point \(\mathbf j\in D\), and \(r_{D,\mathbf j}=\nu\).

Its actual prescribed-mean depth satisfies
\[
\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)=\frac QZ,
\qquad
\frac1{L+1}\le\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)
\le\frac{16}{L+16}
\le16\,3^{-(r-1)/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently there are no absolute constants \(c>0\) and finite \(A\ge0\) such that every complete full-rank support \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\) and every positive log-affine law on \(D\) with mean \(\mathbf j\in D\) satisfy \(\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)\ge c/(r+1)^A\). The proposed bound with \(c=1\), \(A=1\) already fails at \(r=11\).

In contrast, the same law has
\[
\tau_\nu((0,\mathbf0))=\frac{a+1}{2Z}\longrightarrow\frac12
\qquad(r\to\infty\text{ through odd integers}).
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

The slice of \(P\) at height \(t\in[0,L]\) is \((1-t/L)[-1,1]^k\). At height zero its lattice points are all vectors in \(\{-1,0,1\}^k\). At an integer height \(1\le t\le L\), each remaining coordinate has absolute value at most \(1-t/L<1\), so its only possible integer value is zero. Thus
\[
D=(\{0\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^k)
  \cup\{(t,\mathbf0):1\le t\le L\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The base contains exactly \(3^k=a=L^2\) points. Every vertex of \(P\) is integral and belongs to \(D\), so \(P=\operatorname{conv}(D)\), proving completeness. Its base has dimension \(k\), and its apex lies outside the base hyperplane, so its rank is \(k+1=r\). Since \(0<1<L\) and zero is interior to \((1-1/L)[-1,1]^k\), the point \(\mathbf j=(1,\mathbf0)\) is interior to \(P\), as well as integral and in the support.

For existence and bounds on the exact tilt, let \(F(q)=\sum_{t=2}^{L}(t-1)q^t\) for \(q\ge0\). This polynomial is continuous, strictly increasing on \(q>0\), vanishes at zero, and tends to infinity. Furthermore \(F(1)=L(L-1)/2<L^2\). The integer \(L\) is odd. At \(\bar q=16^{1/L}\), use the terms with \((L+1)/2\le t\le L\), for which \(\bar q^t\ge4\), to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(\bar q)
&\ge4\sum_{t=(L+1)/2}^{L}(t-1)
 =4\frac{3(L^2-1)}8\\
&=\frac32(L^2-1)>L^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last inequality holds for \(L\ge3\), whereas here \(L\ge81\). This proves the unique root and both inequalities in (1). In particular every positive-height weight \(q^t\) lies strictly between one and sixteen, and \(L<Q<16L\). All base weights equal one. Equation (4) therefore shows that \(Z=a+Q\) is the correct normalizer and that the law is positive on the entire complete support.

The remaining coordinate means vanish: each base vector occurs together with its negative and has the same weight, while every positive-height point has remaining coordinates zero. Equation (1) gives the first-coordinate mean exactly, since
\[
\mathbb E_\nu t
=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{L}tq^t}{a+Q}
=\frac{Q+\sum_{t=2}^{L}(t-1)q^t}{a+Q}
=1.
\]
Thus the full mean is \(\mathbf j\). To verify the entropy-row identification directly, for any other law \(\rho\) on \(D\) with this same mean, let \(H(\rho)=-\sum_{\mathbf x}\rho_{\mathbf x}\log\rho_{\mathbf x}\), omitting zero summands. Log-affinity and the exact mean give
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\rho\|\nu)
&=-H(\rho)-\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf j+\log Z\\
&=H(\nu)-H(\rho)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Nonnegativity, with equality only when \(\rho=\nu\), follows from strict convexity of \(u\log u\), applied with positive weights \(\nu_{\mathbf x}\) and arguments \(\rho_{\mathbf x}/\nu_{\mathbf x}\). Hence \(\nu\) is the unique maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) law. This is precisely the row \(r_{D,\mathbf j}\) of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Also, any two finite log-affine tilts with this mean must give that same law; their difference then annihilates every support difference. Full rank makes their tilts identical. The tilt specified in (1) is therefore not merely an assumed balancing tilt.

We next compute the actual closed-halfspace depth. Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains its parallel translate with boundary through \(\mathbf j\). Such a translated proper halfspace has the form
\[
\{(t,\mathbf y):\alpha(t-1)+\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\},
\qquad (\alpha,\mathbf u)\ne(0,\mathbf0).
\tag{5}
\]
If \(\alpha>0\), it contains every positive-height point in (4), including the mean point \((1,\mathbf0)\), and thus has mass at least \(Q/Z\). If \(\alpha\le0\), its condition on the base is \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf y\ge\alpha\). It includes the zero base vector and at least one vector from every nonzero pair \(\{\mathbf y,-\mathbf y\}\), because one member has nonnegative projection and \(\alpha\le0\). It consequently contains at least \((a+1)/2\) base points and has mass at least \((a+1)/(2Z)\). But
\[
Q<16L<\frac{L^2+1}{2}=\frac{a+1}{2},
\]
where the second inequality follows from \(L\ge81\). Thus every halfspace in (5) has mass at least \(Q/Z\). The proper closed halfspace \(\{(t,\mathbf y):t\ge1\}\) contains exactly the positive-height chain in (4). It contains the mean point itself and attains mass \(Q/Z\). This proves the exact equality in (2); in particular no atom at the prescribed mean is dropped by using an open cut.

The function \(x\mapsto x/(a+x)\) is increasing on \(x\ge0\). Applying \(L<Q<16L\), and then \(a=L^2\), proves the bounds in (2). For any fixed \(c>0\) and finite \(A\ge0\),
\[
(r+1)^A\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)
\le16(r+1)^A3^{-(r-1)/2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves failure of every asserted polynomial lower bound. At \(r=11\), one has \(L=243\), and the exact bound \(\tau_\nu(\mathbf j)<16/259<1/12=1/(r+1)\) disproves the sharp test without numerical approximation to \(q\).

Finally, every proper halfspace through \((0,\mathbf0)\) includes at least \((a+1)/2\) base points by the same pairing argument, now with base threshold zero. Thus its depth is at least \((a+1)/(2Z)\). Choose \(\mathbf u=(1,3,\ldots,3^{k-1})\). For \(\mathbf y\in\{-1,0,1\}^k\), its projection \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf y\) vanishes only at zero: the largest nonzero term in absolute value exceeds the sum of all preceding absolute values. The proper closed halfspace \(\{(t,\mathbf y):-t+\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf y\ge0\}\) excludes every positive-height point and includes exactly one member of every nonzero base pair, as well as zero. It attains mass \((a+1)/(2Z)\), proving (3). Since \(Q/a<16/L\to0\), that depth tends to \(1/2\). Therefore the exhibited failure is a failure of the prescribed-integer-mean polynomial certificate, not of the existential-center conclusion of the locked problem.
