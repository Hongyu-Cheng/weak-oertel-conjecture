## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^2$ be compact and convex with original integer-fiber length measure $\mu$ of positive total mass $M$. Its positive fibers are $K_i=[l_i,u_i]$, with $w_i=u_i-l_i$ on a consecutive finite integer index set $P$. Fix $j\in P$. Let $T_j$ be the maximum mass of any dominated source of base centroid $j$, equivalently the maximum of $\sum_i m_i$ over $0\le m_i\le w_i$ with $\sum_i(i-j)m_i=0$. Let $c_j(T_j)$ be the midpoint of the interval of continuous centroids of all such maximal-mass sources.

The maximizing allocation $m^*$ is unique. If the original base centroid is $j$, it keeps all fibers. If the base centroid is larger than $j$, it keeps all fibers through $j$, fills the fibers to the right in order of increasing index until the moment is balanced, and discards the remaining right fibers. There is at most one partially filled terminal fiber. If the original base centroid is smaller than $j$, the reflected description holds. In the convex hull of the retained full intervals and the centered subinterval of the partial terminal fiber, if present, the positive integer fibers are exactly these retained intervals. This hull has mixed length $T_j$ and true mixed centroid
$$
\left(j,c_j(T_j)\right),\qquad
c_j(T_j)=\frac{1}{T_j}\sum_i m_i^*\frac{l_i+u_i}{2}.
$$
The original raw closed-halfspace depth at this point exceeds $T_j/3$ when the hull has more than one positive fiber, and is at least $T_j/2$ when it has one. In particular $T_j\ge 3M/4$ implies a positive-mass path point of original raw depth at least $M/4$.

## Proof

An allocation in the feasible box is realized by taking subintervals of the assigned lengths, so this is the correct maximization for dominated sources. It has positive maximum since the full own fiber alone is feasible. If the original base centroid is $j$, every full fiber is feasible and the only way to reach total mass $M$ is to fill all of them. Otherwise reflect the base if necessary so the original base centroid exceeds $j$. The total moment available on the right then strictly exceeds the absolute moment of the full left side. Retain all $i\le j$ and let $k>j$ be the first right index whose cumulative full moment reaches that left moment, taking the first right index when the left moment is zero. Keep all $j<i<k$, fill just enough at $k$ for balance, and keep none to its right. Exact saturation at $k$ is allowed, as is an empty first right fiber when the left moment is zero.

For any feasible row its total mass is $\sum_i(1-(i-j)/(k-j))m_i$ since its centered base moment vanishes. The coefficient is positive for $i<k$, zero at $k$, negative for $i>k$. Our row saturates every nonzero term at its maximal value, proving optimality. Equality forces those same coordinates, and base balance fixes $m_k$ as $k-j>0$. This proves uniqueness, including an extreme anchor.

Every maximal source therefore has the same allocation. On a fiber the extreme first moments are $l_i m_i^*+(m_i^*)^2/2$ and $u_i m_i^*-(m_i^*)^2/2$, by filling the bottom or top. The integral of the coordinate minus the fill threshold against the signed density difference proves extremality in each case. Both global extremes are attained by the corresponding fills on all fibers, and mixtures attain intermediate values. The midpoint formula follows by averaging the extrema and dividing by $T_j$.

The retained positive bases are consecutive. In their lifted convex hull every full interval is unchanged since it is included and the hull lies inside $C$. The partially retained interval, if present, is at an extreme base of the hull, so its fiber is precisely the chosen centered subinterval. The base projection of the hull contains no further integer bases outside that consecutive retained range. Thus its mixed measure is the unweighted sum of the retained interval lengths, with the displayed centroid by base balance and the centering. Possible original length-zero fibers have no effect on these measures. If the hull has more than one positive fiber it is a planar convex body with integer-base mixed centroid, so 9c1d072121e84553 applied with mass $T_j$ gives strict raw depth $>T_j/3$ in its own mixed measure. If there is one positive interval, that measure gives depth $T_j/2$ at its midpoint. The original measure dominates the hull's mixed measure on every closed halfspace. The final capacity implication follows in either case.
