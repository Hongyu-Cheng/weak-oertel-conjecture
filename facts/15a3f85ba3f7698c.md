## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty with $\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n=P$. For $z\in P$ let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be full-dimensional compact convex bodies, $d\ge1$, satisfying $\sum_p\alpha_p K_p\subseteq K_z$ whenever $\alpha$ is a convex coefficient vector with $\sum_p\alpha_p p=z$. Assume an affine $q:\operatorname{aff}P\to\mathbb R^d$ satisfies $q(z)\in\operatorname{int}K_z$ for every $z$. Then there are arbitrarily close compatible positive homothets $L_z=q(z)+s_z(K_z-q(z))$ preserving $W=\sum_z|K_z|$ exactly. They can be chosen so that all representations using only other bases have a uniform strictly positive support gap. There is an open product Hausdorff neighborhood of these fibers in which each family is realized by its lifted convex hull, with exactly those integer fibers and no others. Its hull is full-dimensional in $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$.

In detail, after centering by $q$, choose $0<m\le h_{K_z}(u)\le M$ for all unit $u,z$. Peel successive vertex layers of the convex hull of the remaining bases, writing $\ell(z)$ for their indices starting at zero and $L=\max_z\ell(z)$. If an excluded-target representation exists, let $\alpha_*$ be the smallest positive coordinate of any extreme point of the corresponding finite collection of coefficient polytopes, and set
$$
\delta=\min(1/2,\alpha_*m/M),\quad b_z=1-\delta^{\ell(z)},\quad
\kappa=(1-\delta)\delta^L,\quad
s_z=\lambda(1+\varepsilon b_z),\quad
\lambda=\left(\frac{W}{\sum_z(1+\varepsilon b_z)^d|K_z|}\right)^{1/d}.
$$
For $\varepsilon>0$ the uniform gap is at least $g=\lambda\varepsilon m\kappa$ and independent perturbations of size $<\eta$ suffice for $0<\eta<\min(g/4,\lambda m/2)$. If all bases are vertices, take $L_z=K_z$ and any sufficiently small positive radius for the neighborhood.

For every $u\ne0$, the through-section cap $H_u=\{(z,x):u\cdot(x-q(z))\ge0\}$ retains its fraction in each fiber. If that fraction is constant over the bases, its summed cap budget is exactly preserved. This holds simultaneously for every direction with constant original fraction, in particular for all directions if the fibers are centrally symmetric about $q(z)$.

## Proof

Apply the affine shear $x\mapsto x-q(z)$, which preserves fiber volumes, compatibility, and the fiberwise Hausdorff distances. With zero interior to every centered fiber, the bounds $m,M$ exist by finiteness and compactness of the unit sphere. For a nonvertex $z$, its excluded-target representation polytope is nonempty and compact; it has finitely many extreme points. Every representation of $z$ by other points uses at least one earlier layer, since otherwise $z$ would not be a vertex of the remaining hull when it was removed.

Fix an extreme representation of $z$ and a unit normal $u$. Set $H=\sum_p\alpha_p h_{K_p}(u)\in[m,M]$. The probability weights $\alpha_p h_{K_p}(u)/H$ give at least $\delta$ to one earlier-layer point. If $\ell=\ell(z)\ge1$, the weighted mean of $b_p$ is therefore at most
$$\delta(1-\delta^{\ell-1})+(1-\delta)(1-\delta^L)=b_z-\kappa.$$
Consequently
$$
b_z h_{K_z}(u)-\sum_p\alpha_p b_p h_{K_p}(u)
\ge b_z(h_{K_z}(u)-H)+m\kappa.
$$
The original compatibility gives $h_{K_z}(u)-H\ge0$. Thus the unnormalized expansion $(1+\varepsilon b_z)K_z$ has support gap at least $\varepsilon m\kappa$ for each extreme representation, uniformly in $u$. Linearity in $\alpha$ extends the same inequality to every excluded-target representation. The common factor $\lambda$ preserves these inequalities with gap $g$, restores exactly $W$, and tends to one as $\varepsilon\to0$. This proves the approximation and the gap.

Any perturbed fiber within $\eta$ has support functions differing by less than $\eta$ and still contains a positive ball, since the centered $L_z$ contains the radius $\lambda m$ ball. The difference between target support and the weighted source support remains at least $g-2\eta>0$. A representation using its target with weight $\theta<1$ reduces to an excluded-target one by dividing the other weights by $1-\theta$; weight one is tautological. Thus the perturbed family satisfies all compatibility inclusions. Grouping coefficients of any convex combination of lifted points by base proves that the convex hull has exactly the prescribed fibers; lattice completeness excludes other integer bases. Its affine hull is $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$ because there is a full-dimensional fiber over each base. If all bases are vertices there are no excluded-target constraints and the same hull proof applies without any expansion.

Finally $H_u$ is invariant within each fiber under positive dilation about $q(z)$. Its cap volume and that fiber's volume both multiply by $s_z^d$, preserving their ratio. If the original fraction is the same $p_u$ at every base, its new summed volume is $p_u W$, exactly its original volume. Every such direction has this identity simultaneously. For centrally symmetric fibers each $p_u=1/2$, since each cap boundary has zero fiber volume. Two opposite such cap budgets sum to $W$, so maintaining or reducing both budgets cannot increase total mass.
