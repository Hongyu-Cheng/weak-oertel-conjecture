## Statement

Let $A,B$ be planar convex bodies, let $v_1,v_2,v_3$ be nonzero spanning normals with $\sum_i v_i=0$, and fix $y\in\mathbb R^2$. Use planar area $|\cdot|$. Set $h_i=h_B(v_i)-v_i\cdot y$, where $h_B(v)=\max_{x\in B}v\cdot x$, and put $W=\sum_i h_i$, $H_i^A=\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge-h_i\}$, $U_i=|A\cap H_i^A|$, and $a=|A|$.

Fix an index $i$ whose $A$ cut is proper, meaning that its cutting line meets $\operatorname{int}A$. Suppose the residual corner $R_i=A\cap\bigcap_{j\ne i}\{x:v_j\cdot(x-y)<-h_j\}$ is nonempty. Define $T_i=|A\cap\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge W-h_i\}|$ and $\delta_i=(a-U_j-U_k)_+$ for the other indices $j,k$, where $(s)_+=\max(0,s)$. Write $\sigma_{A,i}=\mathcal H^1(A\cap\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)=-h_i\})/\lVert v_i\rVert$. Then $W>0$, $0<T_i<U_i$, $T_i\ge\delta_i$, and
$$
\sigma_{A,i}\le \frac{2}{W}\bigl(U_i-\sqrt{U_iT_i}\bigr)
\le \frac{2}{W}\bigl(U_i-\sqrt{U_i\delta_i}\bigr),\qquad
\sigma_{A,i}<\frac{2U_i}{W}.
$$

## Proof

For an interior point of $B$, each support gap is strictly positive. Their sum is $W$ by balance, so $W>0$. Each point of $R_i$ satisfies $v_i\cdot(x-y)>h_j+h_k=W-h_i$. The corner has positive area, since a point in it can be moved toward an interior point of $A$ while preserving both strict inequalities, and the resulting interior point has a small ball in the corner. Thus $T_i\ge |R_i|>0$, and the complement of the union of the other two $A$ caps has area at least $a-U_j-U_k$, giving $T_i\ge\delta_i$. Properness gives a positive-area slab just above the original line, below the level shifted by $W$, so $T_i<U_i$.

Let $F(t)=|A\cap\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge -h_i+t\}|$ for $0\le t\le W$. Convexity of $A$ gives Minkowski containment for the interpolated caps, so planar Brunn–Minkowski gives concavity of $\sqrt{F(t)}$. The parallel section lengths are continuous near the proper level, by concavity of the section length at interior levels, and Fubini gives $F'(0)=-\sigma_{A,i}$. The tangent to $\sqrt F$ at zero therefore gives
$$
\sqrt{T_i}\le \sqrt{U_i}-\frac{W\sigma_{A,i}}{2\sqrt{U_i}}.
$$
Rearrange and use $T_i\ge\delta_i$ for the two upper bounds. The last bound is strict because $T_i>0$, even when $\delta_i=0$.
