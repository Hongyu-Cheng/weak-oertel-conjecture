## Statement

Under the hypotheses of the joint rate-coordinate defect lemma, retain its actual subsequence and $\widehat a,\widehat b$. For every globally finite nonnegative convex physical test $f$, every continuous normal weight $\psi\ge0$, and every finite nonnegative convex nondecreasing $g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$,
$$
\int\psi f g(r)\,d\widehat a+\int\psi f r g(r)\,d\widehat b
\le\beta\int\psi f g(r)\,d\widehat b .
$$
In particular this holds for $g(r)=r^m$, $m\ge1$ integer, and $g=1$ recovers the projected reserve. For $0\le t\le\beta$, put $a_t=\pi_*((r-t)_+\widehat a)$ and $b_t=\pi_*((r-t)_+\widehat b)$. Then
$$
\int\psi f\,da_t+\int\psi f(r-t)_+^2\,d\widehat b
\le(\beta-t)\int\psi f\,db_t .
$$

## Proof

For fixed $\lambda>0$ the function $k(s)=s_+g(\lambda s_+)$ is nonnegative, nondecreasing and convex on the line. To see convexity, represent $g$ on the half-line by its affine supports at positive arguments. They have nonnegative slopes and approximate the endpoint value by continuity. On the nonnegative line $t g(\lambda t)$ is therefore a supremum of quadratics with nonnegative quadratic coefficients, and is nondecreasing because both factors are nonnegative and nondecreasing. Composition with $s_+$ gives the claim. As a finite convex nondecreasing function, $k$ is a supremum of affine supports with nonnegative slopes. Thus $t k(\log t-T)$ for $t>0$ is a supremum of convex functions of the form $m t\log t+(b-mT)t$ with $m\ge0$. Its perspective $a k(\log(a/b)-T)$ is jointly convex for $a,b>0$. It is nondecreasing in $a$ by nonnegativity and monotonicity of $k$, and nonincreasing in $b$ by monotonicity of $k$.

Assume first that $f$ is bounded below by a positive constant by adding a positive constant to the desired test. Fix $T>\sup\log f$ on the common compact physical range, and write $c=T-\log f>0$ there. The perspective with $\lambda=\lambda_j$, $a=f(w)$ and $b=L_j(w)$ is convex on the positive neighborhood by the two monotonicities, convexity of $f$ and concavity of $L_j$. Its nonnegative globally finite extension, as in the floor lemma, is a permitted test for that same separator. On the sampled boundary the test is $f v_jg(\lambda_j v_j)$ where $s_j=(\ell_j)_+$ and $v_j=(s_j-c)_+$. The numbers $r_j=\lambda_js_j$ lie in a common compact interval, on which $g$ is Lipschitz by finite convexity and monotonicity on the larger half-line. With $c$ bounded and $s_j-v_j=\min(s_j,c)$, we have
$$
v_jg(\lambda_jv_j)=s_jg(r_j)-\min(s_j,c)g(0)+E_j,\qquad
|E_j|\le 2\,\operatorname{Lip}(g)\lambda_j c s_j .
$$
Indeed the differences are $v_j[g(\lambda_j v_j)-g(r_j)]$ and $-(s_j-v_j)[g(r_j)-g(0)]$. Their bound vanishes in integral because the first log masses are uniformly bounded.

In the $W_j-\beta_j$ term, the first term on the right passes by joint weak convergence of the rate lift. The truncated correction $\min(s_j,c)$ is continuous as a function of $L_j,w$ after assigning $c(w)$ at nonpositive inputs, so it passes by the ordinary boundary and selector weak limits. Together their limit is
$$
g(0)\int\psi(W-\beta)f(\ell-c)_+\,d\eta
+\int\psi f g(r)\,d(\widehat a-\beta\widehat b).
$$
The separator's logarithmic term equals $\int\psi f r_jv_jg(\lambda_jv_j)\,d\eta_j$, since $v_j>0$ implies $\ell_j>0$. The difference from $\int\psi f r_js_jg(r_j)\,d\eta_j$ tends to zero: multiply the above expansion by $r_j$, use its uniform bound for $E_j$, and use $r_j=\lambda_js_j$ for the bounded truncated correction. The latter integral passes to $\int\psi f r g(r)\,d\widehat b$ by the rate lift, with zero ordinary part. The remainder term is bounded by a constant times $\lambda_j\int s_j\,d\eta_j$ and tends to zero.

Now let $T\to\infty$ after taking the subsequential limit. The ordinary term tends to zero by domination by $\ell_+$, and adding then removing a positive constant from $f$ gives the result for every nonnegative $f$. Taking $g(r)=(r-t)_+$ and using $r(r-t)_+=t(r-t)_++(r-t)_+^2$ proves the tail inequality. The tests control the actual residual integrals. They do not give a separate pointwise bound on the load at every rate.
