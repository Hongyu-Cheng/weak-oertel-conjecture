## Statement

Fix finitely many bases $P$. Suppose $K_z^j$ are positive convex $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$, converging in Hausdorff distance to positive $K_z$, with uniform outer radius $R$, inradii and lower volumes. Denote their lifted sampled unions by $S_j,S$, canonical hulls by $C_j,C$, and their mixed volumes by $dm_j,dm$ of masses $M_j,M$. Suppose $L_j$ is a positive finite minimum of affine planes on $C_j$, $\int\log L_j\,dm_j=0$, and $\max_{S_j}L_j\le L_*$ for $L_*\ge1$. Take finitely many source labels $i$ at fixed bases $p_i$ with $Y_{ij}=(p_i,y_{ij})\to Y_i$, uniform own source balls $B(y_{ij},r_s)\subset K_{p_i}^j$, and $L_{ij}=L_j(Y_{ij})\ge l_*>0$. Suppose there are probabilities $\rho_{ij}$ on $S_j$ and $\sigma_{ij}>0$ summing to one with
$$
\int w\,d\rho_{ij}=Y_{ij},\qquad \int L_j\,d\rho_{ij}=L_{ij},\qquad
\sum_i(\sigma_{ij}/L_{ij})\rho_{ij}=dm_j/(M_jL_j).
$$
There are affine majorants $A_{ij}$ touching $L_j$ at $Y_{ij}$ on the sampled fibers, with uniformly bounded full coefficients on $\operatorname{aff}P\times\mathbb R^d$, such that $L_j=\min_i A_{ij}$ on $S_j$. Along a subsequence these planes converge coefficientwise to $A_i$, and the sampled representatives converge uniformly on a common ambient compact set to $L=\min_i A_i$. Each $A_i$ is nonnegative on $C$, $L(Y_i)=\lim_j L_{ij}\ge l_*$, and $L$ is positive on every sampled fiber interior with $\int_S\log L\,dm=0$. Its sampled zero set is a mixed-volume-null subset of the sampled boundaries, contained in a finite union of their exposed zero faces.

## Proof

Choose any affine plane of $L_j$ active at $Y_{ij}$, with continuous slope $c_{ij}$. On the own source ball the plane is positive, which gives $|c_{ij}|\le L_{ij}/r_s\le L_*/r_s$ by evaluating it along the opposite slope direction. For this slope, choose an affine majorant of the form $A_{ij}(z,X)=L_{ij}+c_{ij}\cdot(X-y_{ij})+b_{ij}\cdot(z-p_i)$. The constraints are
$$
b_{ij}\cdot(z-p_i)\ge \max_{K_z^j}(L_j(z,X)-c_{ij}\cdot X)-L_{ij}+c_{ij}\cdot y_{ij}\qquad(z\in P).
$$
They are feasible by the original plane, and the right sides are uniformly bounded. Choose the minimum-norm feasible base vector in the space parallel to $\operatorname{aff}P$. It lies in the span of its active constraint rows: any nonzero orthogonal component can be decreased a little while preserving all tight constraints and the finitely many strict ones, reducing the norm. An independent subset of the active rows then determines the vector in its span by a fixed full-row-rank system, including the empty subset for the zero vector. Only finitely many matrices occur over all $i,j$. Their inverses on the row spans, and the bounded data, give the uniform bound. The intercepts are bounded by the source values and coordinates.

By the barycenter and $L$-moment of $\rho_{ij}$, $\int(A_{ij}-L_j)\,d\rho_{ij}=0$. The integrand is nonnegative on $S_j$, so equality holds on the row. The minimum of the chosen majorants therefore equals $L_j$ almost everywhere for the positive mixture, hence for $dm_j$, and by continuity throughout the closed sampled fibers. After subselection the finitely many bounded coefficient lists converge. The finite minimum converges uniformly on every compact range, and the limiting planes are nonnegative on $C$ by nonnegativity on the converging sampled fibers and affinity. Source values pass by uniform convergence.

Put $m_{zj}=\max_{K_z^j}L_j$ and $V_{zj}=|K_z^j|$. The zero log integral gives $\sum_z V_{zj}\log m_{zj}\ge0$. Since $m_{zj}\le L_*$, with a uniform lower volume and upper total volume, each $m_{zj}$ has a fixed positive lower bound $m_*$. On each sampled fiber the segment from a maximum through $X$ to the opposite boundary gives
$$
L_j(z,X)\ge m_*\operatorname{dist}(X,\partial K_z^j)/(2R).
$$
This also holds at a maximizing $X$ and extends to the boundary. For a common inball radius $r_b$, the homothety with ratio $1-t/r_b$ about an inball center is at distance at least $t$ from the boundary, so the layer of distance at most $t$ has volume $O(t)$ uniformly. Integrating the logarithmic lower bound on that layer gives an $O(t(1+|\log t|))$ bound on the integral of $|\log L_j|$ there; this follows by integrating the distribution bound $\operatorname{vol}\{\operatorname{dist}<e^{-s}\}=O(e^{-s})$ for $s>|\log t|$ and bounding the remaining part by $O(t|\log t|)$. The positive log part is uniformly bounded by $\log L_*$. Positive Hausdorff convergence gives eventual containment of each limiting compact inner core in the current fiber, and the complement of a fixed limiting core lies in a current collar of comparable width for sufficiently small Hausdorff error, by uniform support convergence and the support-distance formula on each fiber. Thus $L$ is positive on each sampled interior, the log integrals converge by uniform convergence on inner cores and the uniform collar estimate, and their limit is zero. Finally the sampled zero set of the finite minimum is the union of the zero sets of its nonnegative affine planes. No plane can vanish identically on a sampled fiber because $L>0$ on its interior. Each nonempty zero set is therefore an exposed boundary face.
