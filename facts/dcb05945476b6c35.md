## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite nonempty set of distinct real bases and let $[l_j,r_j]$ be a bounded nonempty interval at each $j\in P$. Use the sum of lengths on the placed fibers as the measure. At any point $(k,x)$ of the family, its raw closed-halfspace depth is the minimum over both vertical through-point caps and the two orientations of each line $y=x+a(z-k)$ with slope $a=(e-x)/(j-k)$ for every endpoint $e$ at a base $j\ne k$, together with the two constant tail values in slope of each orientation. With no other base, the two nonvertical masses are constant in slope. Zero-width intervals have zero mass.

## Proof

Each containing halfspace can be moved parallel inward to its through-point position. The vertical cases have captured mass equal to the sum of the fiber lengths at bases $j\le k$ or $j\ge k$. In the above-line orientation, the contribution of fiber $j$ is
$\max(0,r_j-\max(l_j,x+a(j-k)))$.
It is continuous and affine in $a$ between the specified breakpoints, and is constant on each unbounded slope interval since every nonanchored fiber is saturated or empty there. The contribution of the anchoring fiber is constant. The below-line orientation is the total mass minus the above-line mass because the line meets each interval in a length-null set. A continuous piecewise affine function with constant tails has both extremes at breakpoints or on its tails. If there are no breakpoints, the function is constant. This proves the evaluation rule.
