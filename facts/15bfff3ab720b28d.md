## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact and convex with nonempty mixed trace $S$, and suppose each nonempty integer fiber is a singleton. Let $M=|S|$, let $N\ge1$ bases of $S$ belong to one parity class, and put $\Delta=M-2N+1$. Then $\Delta\ge0$. If $\Delta\le1/4$, then $\Delta=0$. For $N=1$, $S$ is a singleton. For $N\ge2$, the class consists of the even sites of a consecutive chain of $2N-1$ integer positions in a primitive line lattice, all these positions exhaust $S$, and their singleton heights are affine along the chain. The middle mark has normalized closed-halfspace counting depth at least $N/(2N-1)>1/2$.

## Proof

Triangulate the convex hull of the selected $N$ bases with exactly those bases as vertices. For example triangulate the hull vertices by pulling, then insert the remaining bases by stellar subdivision of their containing minimal faces and stars. The graph is connected and has at least $N-1$ edges. Its edge midpoints are integer by parity and have nonempty actual fibers by convexity. They are distinct from vertices and one another because relative interiors of distinct simplices in a triangulation are disjoint. Thus $M\ge N+(N-1)$ and the integer deficit is nonnegative. If the affine rank is at least two, a full simplex contributes a triangle to the connected graph, which then has at least $N$ edges. This implies $\Delta\ge1$ and is incompatible with $\Delta\le1/4$.

At equality, rank zero gives $N=M=1$. In rank one let the extremes be $p_0,p_0+2Lv$ for a primitive integer $v$. Convexity provides a point at every intervening integer base on the segment joining the lifted extremes. The intervening even bases are all in the selected class, so $L=N-1$. Since $M=2N-1$, these positions exhaust the trace, and the singleton assumption forces their points to be exactly the affine interpolation along the lifted segment. Every closed halfspace containing the middle mark contains that mark and at least one point of each opposite pair. This gives the claimed counting bound.
