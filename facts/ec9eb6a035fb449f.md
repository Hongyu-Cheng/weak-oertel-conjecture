## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $d\ge1$, $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty, and $K_p\subset\mathbb R^d$ be convex bodies for $p\in P$. Fix $q\in P$. Assume the strict compatibility condition at $q$: for every extreme point of the polytope $E_q$ of convex representations of $q$ using $P\setminus\{q\}$, the associated Minkowski combination of the $K_p$ is compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K_q$. This condition is vacuous if $E_q$ is empty. For any unit $u$ whose support face in $K_q$ is $\{v\}$, there exists $a\in\mathbb R^n$ such that $a\cdot(p-q)+h_{K_p}(u)-h_{K_q}(u)<0$ for every $p\ne q$. Thus $(q,v)$ is the unique maximizer of $(z,x)\mapsto a\cdot z+u\cdot x$ on $C=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{p\in P}(\{p\}\times K_p)$. In particular, if the own boundary is $C^1$ and strictly convex, each own boundary point has a unique unit supporting normal and is exposed in $C$.

## Proof

If $P=\{q\}$, take $a=0$. Otherwise put $s_p=h_{K_p}(u)$ and form $X=\operatorname{conv}\{(p-q,s_p-s_q):p\in P\setminus\{q\}\}$. A point of $X$ with first coordinate zero has last coordinate $\sum_{p\ne q}\lambda_p s_p-s_q$ for some $\lambda\in E_q$. At every extreme $\lambda$ this is strictly negative by compact containment and additivity of the support function under Minkowski sums. By linearity it remains strictly negative for every $\lambda\in E_q$. Thus $X$ misses the closed ray $R=\{0\}\times[0,\infty)$.

Strict separation of the compact convex $X$ and the closed convex $R$ gives $(a_0,c)$ with $\sup_{x\in X}(a_0,c)\cdot x<\inf_{r\in R}(a_0,c)\cdot r$. Finiteness of the latter infimum gives $c\ge0$, and the infimum then equals zero. If $c=0$, increase it to a sufficiently small positive value, preserving the strict inequalities at the finitely many generating vectors of $X$. Divide by $c$ and put $a=a_0/c$. All labeled fibers other than $q$ now have strictly smaller support value for the displayed functional than $\{q\}\times K_q$. In the latter fiber the unique maximizing point is $(q,v)$. A convex combination can attain that maximal value only by combining maximizers, so $(q,v)$ also uniquely maximizes over $C$. On a $C^1$ strictly convex boundary, smoothness supplies uniqueness of the unit supporting normal and strict convexity makes its support face a singleton.
