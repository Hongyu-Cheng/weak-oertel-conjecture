## Statement

Let a proper plane in a nondegenerate tetrahedron cut off a closed cap of relative three-dimensional volume $F$, and let $\lambda$ be the barycentric centroid of the actual section for normalized area. If the split is strict $2+2$, then for a specified strictly captured vertex $r$, the sole centroid restriction $\lambda_s\le1/3$ at the other captured vertex $s$ implies $F>27\prod_{j\ne r}\lambda_j$. In particular, for any proper incidence with all $\lambda_j\le1/3$, each strictly captured vertex $r$ satisfies $F\ge27\prod_{j\ne r}\lambda_j$. If the cap has exactly one strictly captured vertex, its product formula is equality without a centroid restriction. If the complementary cap has exactly one strictly captured vertex $s$, then for a strictly captured vertex $r$ of the original cap the difference equals $1-27\lambda_j\lambda_k(1-\lambda_j-\lambda_k)\ge0$ with $\{j,k\}$ the other two labels, again without a centroid restriction.

## Proof

In the strict quadrilateral case label the captured vertices $0,1$ and take $r=0$. Let $x,y,z,w>0$ be homogeneous diagonal coordinates and put $T=x+y+z+w$, $R=z+w$, $b=zw$. Apply accepted tetrahedral quadrilateral section calculus to $(x,y,z,w)/T$; its bijection uses the same plane since rescaling all four reciprocal coordinates rescales the signed heights by a common positive factor. Thus the actual area centroid and cap volume are
$$
\begin{aligned}
3\lambda_1&=y[(y+z)^{-1}+(y+w)^{-1}-T^{-1}],\\
3\lambda_2&=z[(x+z)^{-1}+(y+z)^{-1}-T^{-1}],\\
3\lambda_3&=w[(x+w)^{-1}+(y+w)^{-1}-T^{-1}],\\
F&=\frac{b[(x+y)T-xy+b]}{(x+z)(x+w)(y+z)(y+w)} .
\end{aligned}
$$
Let $c=b(T+y)-xy^2$, $M=R(x+y)+x^2+xy+y^2$, and
$$
E=R^3+2R^2x+3R^2y+Rx^2+5Rxy+3Ry^2+2x^2y+2xy^2+y^3 .
$$
Bringing the displayed rational expressions to a common denominator gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1-3\lambda_1)T(y+z)(y+w)&=c,\\
F-27\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3
&=\frac{b(cM+bE+b^2y)}{T^3(y+z)^2(y+w)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are polynomial identities after clearing the positive denominators, using $R=z+w$ and $b=zw$. The assumption gives $c\ge0$ and $bE>0$, proving strictness, including repeated vertex heights. Interchanging $x,y$ proves the other captured-vertex version.

Every other proper incidence has exactly one strictly captured vertex on at least one side: each side has a vertex of its strict sign and a tetrahedron has four vertices. On a side with just one strictly captured vertex $r$, the cap is the tetrahedron with edge parameters $e_j$ on the three incident edges, allowing $e_j=1$ at a boundary vertex. The section triangle has barycentric coordinates averaging to $\lambda_j=e_j/3$ for $j\ne r$. The edge determinant therefore gives $F=\prod_{j\ne r}e_j=27\prod_{j\ne r}\lambda_j$. If the complementary side has just one strictly captured vertex $s$, this formula and zero ambient volume of the section give $F=1-27\lambda_r\lambda_j\lambda_k$. Subtracting the requested product gives $1-27\lambda_j\lambda_k(1-\lambda_j-\lambda_k)$, nonnegative by AM–GM on the three nonnegative factors of sum one. With all coordinates at most $1/3$, either the strict quadrilateral inequality or these incidence formulas apply to every strictly captured $r$.
