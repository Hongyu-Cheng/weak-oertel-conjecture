## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the canonical notation and normalizations of accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70`:
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\tag{1}
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
 \qquad
 D:=2^nh_{\max}(C).
\tag{2}
\]
For each parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 A_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z,
\tag{3}
\]
and call \(\xi\) good when
\(\max_{z\equiv\xi}a_z\leq A_\xi/4\). The half-strength canonical product reservoir is
\[
 r_{\{u,v\}}:=\frac{a_ua_v}{4A_\xi}
 \quad
 (u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\equiv\xi,\ \xi\text{ good}),
\tag{4}
\]
and is zero on all other unordered pairs. Put
\[
 Q:=\sum_er_e>0,
 \qquad
 m_e:=\frac{u+v}{2},
 \qquad
 \bar c_e:=\frac{c_u+c_v}{2}
 \quad(e=\{u,v\}),
\tag{5}
\]
where \(c_z\) is the centroid of \(C_z\), and put
\[
 \bar\nu_r:=\sum_er_e\delta_{(m_e,\bar c_e)}.
\tag{6}
\]
Use the exact canonical capacities of `6b09aae189415c70` and assume the hard inequality
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=R+G+R_{\mathrm{sel}}(C)+M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 +R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)+D<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{7}
\]

For every physical midpoint \(t\) hit by the reservoir, define
\[
 E_t:=\{e:r_e>0,\ m_e=t\},
\tag{8}
\]
choose one edge \(e_t\in E_t\) of maximum reservoir weight, and put
\[
 L:=\sum_{t:E_t\ne\varnothing}r_{e_t},
\tag{9}
\]
\[
 \sigma_\circ
 :=\sum_{t:E_t\ne\varnothing}
 \sum_{e\in E_t\setminus\{e_t\}}
 r_e\delta_{(t,\bar c_e)}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{L<\frac{803}{1200}Q,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{|\sigma_\circ|=Q-L>\frac{397}{1200}Q.}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, \(\sigma_\circ\ne0\), it is a positive submeasure of \(\bar\nu_r\), and every one of its edge occurrences is supported at a physical midpoint hit by at least two positive reservoir edges.

Define its intrinsic ranks by
\[
 \rho_\circ
 :=\dim\operatorname{aff}
 \{t:E_t\setminus\{e_t\}\ne\varnothing\},
\tag{12}
\]
\[
 r_\circ
 :=\min_{a:\operatorname{aff}\{t:E_t\setminus\{e_t\}\ne\varnothing\}
 \to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
 \dim\operatorname{span}
 \{\bar c_e-a(t):e\in E_t\setminus\{e_t\}\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 r_\circ+1>\frac{397}{6}\,2^{n-\rho_\circ}.}
\tag{14}
\]

For a nonempty remnant cluster
\(J_t:=E_t\setminus\{e_t\}\), put
\[
 S_t^\circ:=\sum_{e\in J_t}r_e,
 \qquad
 k_t:=\dim\operatorname{aff}\{\bar c_e:e\in J_t\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Every such cluster satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 k_t+1>400\gamma_{2d}2^n\frac{S_t^\circ}{Q},
 \qquad
 \gamma_{2d}:=\left(\frac{2d}{2d+1}\right)^{2d}.}
\tag{16}
\]

Finally, fix any physical midpoint \(t\) and source parity class \(\xi\), and put
\[
 E_{\xi,t}
 :=\{e=\{u,v\}\in E_t:u\equiv v\equiv\xi\},
 \qquad
 S_{\xi,t}:=\sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}r_e.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(S_{\xi,t}>0\), define
\[
 q_{\xi,t}
 :=\frac1{S_{\xi,t}}
 \sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}r_e\bar c_e.
\tag{18}
\]
Then there is one measurable density \(g_{\xi,t}\) on the actual physical fiber \(C_t\) such that
\[
 \boxed{
 0\leq g_{\xi,t}\leq1\ \text{ a.e.},
 \qquad
 \int_{C_t}g_{\xi,t}=8S_{\xi,t},
 \qquad
 \frac1{8S_{\xi,t}}
 \int_{C_t}xg_{\xi,t}(x)\,dx=q_{\xi,t}.}
\tag{19}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \boxed{
 q_{\xi,t}\in\mathsf M_{8S_{\xi,t}}(C_t),
 \qquad
 8S_{\xi,t}\leq a_t.}
\tag{20}
\]
The construction in (19) accounts for every edge of \(E_{\xi,t}\) with its full canonical weight \(r_e\); its reflected endpoint pairs are disjoint. No assertion is made that the densities \(g_{\xi,t}\) for different parity classes over the same physical target can be summed pointwise. No cross-parity aggregation, hard-ledger rank-to-volume charge, complete-core lower bound, or terminal conclusion follows from (11)--(20) alone.

## Proof

Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives
\[
 Q\geq\frac3{32}(W-4G).
\tag{21}
\]
Since \(G\) is a nonnegative summand of \(\mathcal A(C)\), equation (7) gives \(G<Q/100\). Rearranging (21) therefore yields
\[
 W\leq\frac{32}{3}Q+4G
 <\left(\frac{32}{3}+\frac1{25}\right)Q
 =\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{22}
\]

Fix a physical midpoint \(t\) and its selected leader
\(e_t=\{u,v\}\). Its endpoints lie in one good parity class \(\xi\), so
\[
 a_u\leq\frac{A_\xi}{4},
 \qquad
 a_v\leq\frac{A_\xi}{4},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{a_ua_v}\leq\frac{A_\xi}{4}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (4) and (23) give
\[
 16r_{e_t}
 =\frac{4a_ua_v}{A_\xi}
 =\sqrt{a_ua_v}\,\frac{4\sqrt{a_ua_v}}{A_\xi}
 \leq\sqrt{a_ua_v}.
\tag{24}
\]
The actual midpoint-fiber Brunn--Minkowski estimate in accepted fact `17cb2e32be8479b1` gives
\[
 a_t\geq\sqrt{a_ua_v}.
\tag{25}
\]
Consequently \(16r_{e_t}\leq a_t\). Sum this inequality once over the distinct physical targets, not once per edge. Equations (9) and (22) give
\[
 16L\leq\sum_{t:E_t\ne\varnothing}a_t
 \leq W
 <\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{26}
\]
This proves the first inequality in (11). Every positive reservoir edge belongs to exactly one set \(E_t\), so deleting exactly one leader from every nonempty \(E_t\) gives
\[
 |\sigma_\circ|
 =\sum_t\sum_{e\in E_t\setminus\{e_t\}}r_e
 =Q-L
 >\left(1-\frac{803}{1200}\right)Q
 =\frac{397}{1200}Q.
\tag{27}
\]
This proves the second inequality in (11), including strictness. If an edge remains at \(t\), the deleted leader is a distinct positive edge at the same physical midpoint. Hence the support assertion after (11) also follows.

Accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` proves for every nonzero positive submeasure \(\sigma\leq\bar\nu_r\) that
\[
 M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 \geq2^{n-\rho_\sigma}\frac{|\sigma|}{r_\sigma+1},
 \qquad
 D\geq M_{\mathrm{off}}(C).
\tag{28}
\]
Apply (28) to \(\sigma_\circ\), whose ranks are exactly (12)--(13). Since \(M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)\) and \(D\) are two distinct nonnegative summands of \(\mathcal A(C)\),
\[
 \mathcal A(C)
 \geq2M_{\mathrm{off}}(C)
 \geq2^{n-\rho_\circ+1}
 \frac{|\sigma_\circ|}{r_\circ+1}.
\tag{29}
\]
Combine (7), (11), and (29), cancel \(Q>0\), and rearrange:
\[
 r_\circ+1
 >200\,2^{n-\rho_\circ}\frac{|\sigma_\circ|}{Q}
 >200\cdot\frac{397}{1200}\,2^{n-\rho_\circ}
 =\frac{397}{6}\,2^{n-\rho_\circ}.
\tag{30}
\]
This proves (14).

For a nonempty \(J_t\), accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives the exact reflected-cloud lower bound
\[
 R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)
 \geq4\gamma_{2d}2^n
 \frac{S_t^\circ}{k_t+1}.
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)\) is a nonnegative summand of \(\mathcal A(C)\), equation (7) gives
\(R_{\mathrm{cloud}}(C)<Q/100\). Combining this with (31) and rearranging proves (16).

It remains to construct the fixed-color density. Fix \((\xi,t)\) with \(S_{\xi,t}>0\). For
\(e=\{u,v\}\in E_{\xi,t}\), accepted fact `32aedd8da549416a` defines
\[
 \beta_e
 :=\left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2\right)^d
\tag{32}
\]
and proves
\[
 \bar c_e\in\mathsf M_{\beta_e}(C_t),
 \qquad
 \beta_e\geq\sqrt{a_ua_v}.
\tag{33}
\]
Choose a representing density \(f_e\) on \(C_t\), so
\[
 0\leq f_e\leq1,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_t}f_e=\beta_e,
 \qquad
 \int_{C_t}xf_e(x)\,dx=\beta_e\bar c_e.
\tag{34}
\]
Equations (4) and (33) give
\[
 \frac{r_e}{\beta_e}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt{a_ua_v}}{4A_\xi}.
\tag{35}
\]

At a fixed midpoint \(t\), reflection sends an endpoint \(u\) to the unique endpoint \(2t-u\). Therefore distinct unordered edges in \(E_{\xi,t}\) have disjoint endpoint sets. Arithmetic--geometric mean and this disjointness give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}\frac{r_e}{\beta_e}
 &\leq\frac1{4A_\xi}
 \sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E_{\xi,t}}\sqrt{a_ua_v}\\
 &\leq\frac1{8A_\xi}
 \sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E_{\xi,t}}(a_u+a_v)\\
 &\leq\frac1{8A_\xi}\sum_{z\equiv\xi}a_z
 =\frac18.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
This is the exact source accounting: each reflected source appears in at most one summand, and every selected edge keeps its full canonical weight.

Define
\[
 g_{\xi,t}
 :=8\sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}
 \frac{r_e}{\beta_e}f_e.
\tag{37}
\]
Equations (34) and (36) give \(0\leq g_{\xi,t}\leq1\) almost everywhere. They also give
\[
 \int_{C_t}g_{\xi,t}
 =8\sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}r_e
 =8S_{\xi,t},
\tag{38}
\]
and
\[
 \int_{C_t}xg_{\xi,t}(x)\,dx
 =8\sum_{e\in E_{\xi,t}}r_e\bar c_e
 =8S_{\xi,t}q_{\xi,t}.
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (37)--(39) prove (19), and the definition of the metronoid proves (20). The proof constructs only one density for one fixed \((\xi,t)\); equation (36) is not a bound on the sum over different parity classes. This proves the stated scope limitation and completes every asserted conclusion.
