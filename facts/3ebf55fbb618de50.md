## Statement

Take the actual fibers $K_0=K=[-1,1]^2$ and $K_{-1}=K_1=D=K\cap\{|x_1|+|x_2|\le3/2\}$ at bases $-1,0,1$. Their masses are $4,7/2,7/2$, total $M=11$, and every nonempty proper closed minor $U=P\cap\operatorname{conv}U$ has mass less than $M k(U)/k(P)$, where $k$ counts occupied parities. The unique unbuffered deepest mark is $(0,0)$, with raw depth $11/2$.

Fix $0<\varepsilon\le1/10$. In the covariance-buffered model, the nonvertical cap through $(p,y)$ has margin
$$
a(z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon\Gamma(u)\left(\frac{\ell_p E_{K_p}(y)}{M}\right)^{1/3},
$$
where $\Gamma(u)^2=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u$ for the pooled within-fiber covariance; retain closed pure-base caps. Among positive sampled-concave arrays $\ell$ with $\sum_z w_z\log\ell_z=0$, the unique fixed-body minimizer of maximal buffered depth is $\ell\equiv1$. At this optimum the deepest mark is again uniquely $(0,0)$, with strict pure-base gap. With $\sigma=\delta_0$, its one-source tight original-volume row is $(7/22,4/11,7/22)$. Its Minkowski body is $B_0=K\cap\{|x_1|+|x_2|\le37/22\}$, with $|K|-|B_0|=49/242$, but its scalar support slack is zero at every regular source normal, including with any bounded weighting of source surface measure. Each vertical face shrinks from length $2$ to $15/11$.

## Proof

The endpoints agree and lie in $K$; every convex combination at base zero lies in $K$, and the lifted hull has exactly the specified integer fibers. The three singleton quotas are strict since their masses are below $11/2$; the adjacent pairs have two parities and mass $15/2<11$, and the endpoint pair is not closed. At $\ell=1$ the one-source row is forced by its column marginal and has both required moments by symmetry. On the common fan of the axis and diagonal normals, the Minkowski average $(4K+7D)/11$ has axis support $1$ and unnormalized diagonal support $37/22$. The supports agree with the asserted clipped square on all cones of that fan by linearity there, proving equality. Four corner triangles of leg $7/22$ give the deficit $49/242$, and the clipped vertical length is $2(37/22-1)=15/11$. Every regular normal of $K$ is an axis normal with zero support slack.

Full central symmetry gives mass $11/2$ for each nonvertical cap through $(0,0)$ before buffering. The smallest closed pure-base cap at base zero has mass $15/2$; each endpoint admits an isolating pure-base cap of mass $7/2$. For a nonzero mark $y$ at base zero take $u=y$ and zero tilt: raising the threshold from zero to $u\cdot y>0$ removes a positive slab of $K$. This gives strict uniqueness at zero width.

For $\ell=1$, the moments $\int_K x_1^2=4/3$ and $\int_D x_1^2=47/48$ follow by integrating the square and subtracting the four triangles. Reflection and interchange symmetry give $\Sigma=(79/264)I$. Compact energy gives $E_K(0)=4$ and $E_K(y)<4$ at every other mark, with the boundary value zero. For any unit $u$ the displacement at zero is $\delta=\varepsilon(4/11)^{1/3}\sqrt{79/264}<1/10$. The zero-tilt axis cap has mass $11/2+6\delta<15/2$, since all three section lengths are $2$ on this strip. A minimizing nonvertical cap at the center exists: restrict $|u|=1$ and clip the scalar tilt outside a fixed compact interval where the endpoints are already saturated. At a nonzero $y$, choose its reflected copy in the continuous coordinates if needed so that $u\cdot y\ge0$. The smaller energy and the nonnegative threshold increase make the new threshold strictly higher than at the center, removing positive own area from a proper trace of the square. This proves unique deepestness with the buffer, since the center's value is at least its unbuffered $11/2$ and the endpoints have their pure-base upper bound.

Write $(a,b,c)$ for another normalized concave array. Then
$$
0=\tfrac7{22}(\log a+\log c)+\tfrac4{11}\log b
\le\tfrac7{11}\log[(a+c)/2]+\tfrac4{11}\log b
\le\log b.
$$
Equality requires $a=b=c=1$. If $b>1$, at the middle center the displacement in every unit direction becomes $b^{1/3}\delta$. The gain in every nonvertical cap is uniformly positive over unit directions and all base tilts: use the square's unit inball and the strip of width $\min\{(b^{1/3}-1)\delta,1/10\}>0$ adjacent to the old proper plane, on which the own section has a fixed positive lower bound. Pure-base caps remain at least $15/2$, strictly above the old minimum, so the new depth at zero strictly exceeds the old maximal value. This proves the claimed unique parameter optimum.
