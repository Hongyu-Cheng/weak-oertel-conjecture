## Statement

Let $C=[0,1]^2\times[0,1]$ and let $Q$ be the tetrahedron with vertices $(0,0,-1),(1,0,2),(0,1,2),(1,1,-1)$. The four open complements of the facet inequalities of $Q$ are an inclusion-minimal strict cover of the mixed trace of $C$. Each closed cap has normalized mixed length $1/4$. No affine map $c:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$, with arbitrary real centers, preserves that fixed open cover after all continuous dilation factors $s\ge1$.

## Proof

The barycentric functions of the listed vertices are
$$
\frac{5-3z_1-3z_2-x}{6},\qquad
\frac{3z_1-3z_2+x+1}{6},\qquad
\frac{-3z_1+3z_2+x+1}{6},\qquad
\frac{3z_1+3z_2-x-1}{6}.
$$
Their sum is one and their evaluations on the four vertices give the four coordinate vectors. Thus the vertices are affinely independent and nonnegativity of the functions describes $Q$. Number the functions $0,1,2,3$. At the four integer bases $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$, substitution at $x=0$ and $x=1$ gives exactly one strictly negative function, with indices $3,2,1,0$ respectively. The others are strictly positive, and these signs persist for every $x\in[0,1]$ by affinity in $x$. These are all the sampled sites. Each cap therefore has mixed length one and each is necessary. Each base is an extreme point of the projected square, so the corresponding fiber of $Q$ consists only of its listed vertex height, $-1$ on the diagonal and $2$ off the diagonal.

The dilate of $[0,1]$ about $c$ is $[(1-s)c,(1-s)c+s]$. If $c>0$, its lower endpoint reaches $-1$ at $s=1+1/c$. If $c\le0$, its lower endpoint stays at least zero. Thus avoidance of $-1$ for all finite scales is exactly $c\le0$. Likewise its upper endpoint reaches $2$ at $s=1+1/(1-c)$ if $c<1$, and stays at most one if $c\ge1$. Thus the two diagonal centers must be at most zero, while the two others must be at least one. Affinity implies that the sums on the diagonals coincide, which is impossible.
