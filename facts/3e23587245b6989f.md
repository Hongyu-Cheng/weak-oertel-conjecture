## Statement

Fix integers \(m,Q\ge 1\).  Use the rooted-tree objects of accepted fact `032423921efad6c8`: the tree-coordinate set
\[
\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r,
\qquad
n=n_{m,Q}=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,
\]
a symmetric saturated-chain partition of \(\{0,1\}^m\), its injective partial successor \(F_m\), and the complete convex lattice support \(A_{m,Q}\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\).  Put
\[
\eta=\eta_m=1-2^{-m}\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},
\qquad
A_s=\{z\in A_{m,Q}:\operatorname{lev}(z)=s\}.
\]
Thus \(|A_s|=2^n\eta^s\).  Here \(\operatorname{lev}\) is the function called `level` in accepted fact `604889294a17d7df`.

Choose an integer \(T\ge0\), integers
\(0=a_0\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_Q\le T\), and arbitrary positive real numbers \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_T\).  Let
\[
N=n+1,
\qquad
D=\{(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The integer \(N\), including the height coordinate, is the total integer dimension.  Thus the integer dimension denoted by \(n\) in the lifted part of accepted fact `c2b7164e5a46e1b3` is our \(N\); our \(D\) is its support in (10), with its scale \(\omega=1\), and is the support \(\widehat A\) of `604889294a17d7df` under that fact's special entrance schedule and \(T=k\).

Give \(D\) weights
\[
w_{(z,t)}=\rho_t,
\qquad
W=\sum_{J\in D}w_J,
\qquad
p_J=\frac{w_J}{W}.
\]
For \(J\in D\), define the full closed-halfspace depths
\[
\tau_w(J)=\inf_{H\ni J}\sum_{I\in D\cap H}w_I,
\qquad
\tau_p(J)=\frac{\tau_w(J)}{W},
\]
where the infimum is over all closed halfspaces of \(\mathbb R^N\).  Then
\[
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p(J)
\ge \frac8{91}\,2^{-N},
\qquad
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p(J)^2
\ge \frac1{48}\,4^{-N}.
\tag{1}
\]
No monotonicity or log-concavity of the positive profile is assumed.

For the canonical reflection obstruction, specialize to each integer \(k\ge8\) by taking
\[
T=k,
\qquad
\rho_t=2^{-t},
\qquad
\mathfrak b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
Q=Q_k=\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}\mathfrak b_\ell,
\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,
\]
and taking \(a_s=\ell\) when
\(\sum_{r=8}^{\ell-1}\mathfrak b_r<s\le\sum_{r=8}^{\ell}\mathfrak b_r\), with \(a_0=0\).  The symbol \(\mathfrak b_\ell\) is exactly the entrance multiplicity \(b_\ell\) of `604889294a17d7df`; it is renamed only to avoid collision with Boolean tree vectors.  Write \(p^{(k)},W_k,N_k,Q_k\) for the resulting objects and \(\nu_k=W_k/2^{N_k}\).  Although `604889294a17d7df` gives reflected-pair mass at most one at every center, the actual full depths satisfy
\[
2^{N_k}\sum_Jp^{(k)}_J\tau_{p^{(k)}}(J)
\ge \frac{2^{Q_k-2k}}{8\nu_k^2}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
4^{N_k}\sum_Jp^{(k)}_J\tau_{p^{(k)}}(J)^2
\ge \frac{2^{2Q_k-3k}}{16\nu_k^3}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{3}
\]

There are two further conclusions for actual convex-body realizations when
\[
m=2(Q+1)^4.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(d\ge1\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and let
\(C_I=\{y:(I,y)\in C\}\).  Assume its positive-volume integer fibers are exactly those indexed by \(D\), and
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{(z,t)})=\rho_t
\quad ((z,t)\in D).
\tag{5}
\]
Put \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^N\times\mathbb R^d)\), so that \(\mathcal H_d(S)=W\).  For the locked problem's depth,
\[
\max_{q\in S}h_S(q)\ge \frac3{280}\,2^{-N}W.
\tag{6}
\]
This conclusion permits arbitrary shapes and locations of the fibers consistent with convexity and (5).

If, in addition,
\[
\rho_{t+1}\le\frac12\rho_t\qquad(0\le t<T),
\tag{7}
\]
put
\[
\beta_0=1,
\qquad
\beta_s=1+2^{s-1}\quad(1\le s\le Q),
\qquad
\Xi=\max_{0\le s\le Q}\beta_s\rho_{a_s}.
\]
There are a Boolean center \(j\in A_0\), a maximizing level \(s\), and a zero-cost packing at \((j,a_s)\) consisting of the target singleton and, when \(s\ge1\), \(2^{s-1}\) pairwise source-disjoint triples.  Each triple has three distinct indices, coefficients \(1/3\), and barycenter \((j,a_s)\); every component has both logarithmic cost and \(d\)-root-volume cost zero in the conventions of `072548cdd7a2b880` and `c2b7164e5a46e1b3`.  The packing has total component mass
\[
\Xi\ge\frac37\,2^{-N}W.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(c_{j,a_s}\) is the centroid of the target fiber, then the single point
\(q=(j,a_s,c_{j,a_s})\), fixed before the halfspace, satisfies
\[
h_S(q)\ge\gamma_d\Xi
\ge\frac{3\gamma_d}{7}\,2^{-N}W,
\qquad
\gamma_d=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\ge e^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]

All four conclusions concern weights constant across the tree coordinates at each fixed height.  They do not prove the corresponding assertions for weights \(e^{\ell\cdot z}\rho_t\) with nonconstant \(\ell\), or for arbitrary joint tilts \(e^{\ell\cdot z+\beta t}\).

## Proof

Accepted fact `032423921efad6c8` gives the following properties that will be used without reproving them.  A Boolean vector \(b\) on the tree coordinates determines a forward path \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and
\[
z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}
\quad(0\le s\le h(b))
\]
are precisely the points of \(A_{m,Q}\).  The support is complete convex, its level-\(s\) census is \(2^n\eta^s\), and \(F_m\) is injective, with both its domain and image occupying the fraction \(\eta\) of the Boolean block.  Accepted fact `c2b7164e5a46e1b3` gives the exact lifted-support identity \(D=\widehat P\cap\mathbb Z^N\), including boundary lattice points.  Consequently, with
\[
V_s=\sum_{t=a_s}^{T}\rho_t,
\qquad
V=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\eta^sV_s,
\qquad
M_0=2^n,
\]
one has
\[
W=M_0V=2^{N-1}V.
\tag{10}
\]
The symbol \(M_0\) is only the number of Boolean centers and is neither the maximum atom \(M(w)\) of `604889294a17d7df` nor a scheme mass.

We first count the centers used below.  Let
\[
\alpha=2^{-m}|\operatorname{im}(F_m^2)|,
\]
the fraction of Boolean blocks possessing at least two predecessors in their symmetric chain.  For \(m\ge2\),
\[
\alpha\ge\eta^3,
\qquad
\frac{\eta^2}{\alpha}\le\frac{25}{16}.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, a symmetric-chain partition has
\(2^m(1-\eta)=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\) chains, and at most the first two members of each chain lack two predecessors.  Hence \(\alpha\ge2\eta-1\).  For \(m=2\), directly \(\eta=1/2\) and \(\alpha=1/4\), which gives (11).  For \(m\ge3\), the central-binomial ratio is at most \(3/8\), so \(\eta\ge5/8\).  The ratio bound follows from the elementary recurrences
\[
2^{-(2r+1)}\binom{2r+1}{r}
=\frac{2r+1}{2r+2}\,2^{-2r}\binom{2r}{r},
\]
and
\[
2^{-(2r+2)}\binom{2r+2}{r+1}
=2^{-(2r+1)}\binom{2r+1}{r}.
\]
On \([5/8,1]\),
\[
(2\eta-1)-\eta^3=(1-\eta)(\eta^2+\eta-1)\ge0,
\]
and \(\eta^2/(2\eta-1)\) is decreasing, with value \(25/16\) at \(5/8\).  This proves (11).

For \(1\le s\le Q\), define \(G_s\subseteq A_0=\{0,1\}^n\) as follows.  For \(j\in A_0\), require its root block \(c=j[\varnothing]\) to have two predecessors, and write
\[
F_m^{-1}(c)=c-e_{v_1},
\qquad
F_m^{-2}(c)=c-e_{v_1}-e_{u_1},
\qquad u_1\ne v_1.
\tag{12}
\]
Starting in the two distinct root subtrees \(u_1\) and \(v_1\), repeatedly require the current child block of \(j\) to lie in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\), take its unique predecessor, and descend along the coordinate removed by that predecessor.  Require both inverse paths
\[
u_1,\ldots,u_s,
\qquad
v_1,\ldots,v_s
\]
to reach depth \(s\).  The sets \(G_s\) are nested, the paths at smaller depths are prefixes of those at larger depths, and
\[
|G_s|=M_0\alpha\eta^{2s-2}.
\tag{13}
\]
This is an exact count.  The root requirement has fraction \(\alpha\).  After it, there are \(2s-2\) predecessor queries.  Each query examines a previously unexposed Boolean block, since the two paths lie in disjoint root subtrees and blocks along a path have different parent nodes.  Conditional on all previous blocks and on the adaptively selected query location, the new block is uniform and lies in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\) with fraction \(\eta\).  Multiplication gives (13).

Every coordinate \(u_r,v_r\) selected this way has value one in \(j\).  Fix \(j\in G_s\).  For each
\(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_2,\ldots,\varepsilon_s)\in\{0,1\}^{s-1}\), define three vectors, leaving all unlisted coordinates equal to those of \(j\), by
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{coordinate}&x_{s,\varepsilon}&y_{s,\varepsilon}&z_{s,\varepsilon}\\ \hline
u_1&2&1&0\\
v_1&0&2&1\\
u_r,\ 2\le r\le s&2&1-\varepsilon_r&\varepsilon_r\\
v_r,\ 2\le r\le s&1-\varepsilon_r&2&\varepsilon_r
\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]
with the evident empty choice of \(\varepsilon\) when \(s=1\).  Coordinatewise,
\[
x_{s,\varepsilon}+y_{s,\varepsilon}+z_{s,\varepsilon}=3j.
\tag{15}
\]

These vectors belong to \(A_{m,Q}\), with respective levels \(s,s,0\).  To verify the first assertion, replace the twos of \(x_{s,\varepsilon}\) by zeros.  Its root block is \(F_m^{-2}(c)\), whose successor first selects \(u_1\).  At each later block on the \(u\)-path, it is exactly the predecessor of the corresponding block of \(j\), so its forward path starts \(u_1,\ldots,u_s\).  Alterations in the \(v_1\)-subtree cannot affect this path.  Thus \(x_{s,\varepsilon}=z_s(b)\) for its Boolean reduction \(b\).  The same argument for \(y_{s,\varepsilon}\), beginning with \(F_m^{-1}(c)\), gives the \(v\)-path.  The third vector is Boolean and hence is in \(A_0\).

For fixed \(s\), all \(3\cdot2^{s-1}\) sources in (14) are distinct.  The first two source families have twos on different paths, the third has none, and within each family the entries recover \(\varepsilon\).  Thus (14) supplies \(2^{s-1}\) pairwise source-disjoint triples, all avoiding \(j\).

There is also a simultaneous choice disjoint across every depth.  Retain the sole triple at \(s=1\); for each \(s\ge2\), retain precisely those triples with \(\varepsilon_s=0\).  Put
\[
L_1=1,
\qquad
L_s=2^{s-2}\quad(s\ge2).
\tag{16}
\]
Non-Boolean sources from different depths have different levels.  A retained Boolean source at depth \(s\ge2\) has zeros at both \(u_s,v_s\), whereas every Boolean source retained at an earlier depth leaves those two coordinates equal to their value one in \(j\).  This also separates the depth-one Boolean source from every later one.  The within-depth argument already separates sources at equal depth, and every source avoids \(j\).  Hence all retained sources, at every available depth for the fixed center, are pairwise distinct.  This proves the required cross-level source disjointness.

For \(0\le s\le Q\) and \(0\le t\le T\), define the inclusive height depth
\[
d_s(t)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\min\left\{\sum_{h=a_s}^{t}\rho_h,
             \sum_{h=t}^{T}\rho_h\right\},&t\ge a_s,\\[6pt]
0,&t<a_s.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]
For a Boolean center \(j\), the central column is present at every height.  Consider any closed halfspace containing \((j,t)\).  Moving a proper halfspace's boundary parallel to itself until it passes through \((j,t)\) can only decrease captured mass, so write the resulting inequality as
\[
u\cdot(x-j)+b(h-t)\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]
In each retained level-\(s\) triple, (15) makes at least one source \(z\) satisfy \(u\cdot(z-j)\ge0\).  If \(b>0\), its column contributes the upper inclusive tail from \(t\); if \(b<0\), it contributes the lower inclusive tail; and if \(b=0\), it contributes the entire available tail.  Each contribution is at least \(d_s(t)\).  The two level-\(s\) sources enter at \(a_s\), while the Boolean source enters at \(a_0=0\le a_s\), and every one has weight \(\rho_h\) at height \(h\).  The cross-level disjointness just proved, together with the separate central column, therefore gives the pointwise bound
\[
\tau_w((j,t))
\ge d_0(t)+\sum_{s=1}^{Q}\mathbf1_{G_s}(j)L_s d_s(t).
\tag{19}
\]
This proof includes \(b=0\), height atoms lying on the boundary, and both choices of closed vertical ray.

We record the height-atom moment bound.  For every \(r\ge1\),
\[
\sum_{t=0}^{T}\rho_t d_s(t)^r
\ge\frac{V_s^{r+1}}{2^r(r+1)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Normalize the tail beginning at \(a_s\) to a probability distribution, let \(U\) be uniform on \((0,1)\), and let \(X\) be its left-continuous quantile.  On the atom selected by \(X\), the inclusive lower cumulative mass is at least \(U\), and the inclusive upper cumulative mass is at least \(1-U\).  Thus its normalized closed-ray depth is at least \(\min\{U,1-U\}\), including when \(U\) lies inside an atom.  Integration gives
\[
\int_0^1\min\{u,1-u\}^r\,du=\frac1{2^r(r+1)},
\]
which proves (20).  This is why no atom may be discarded or assigned to only one side of a closed height boundary.

For \(m\ge2\), use \((\sum_iq_i)^r\ge\sum_iq_i^r\) in (19), sum first over heights against \(\rho_t\), and then over Boolean centers.  Non-Boolean targets contribute nonnegatively and may be omitted.  Equations (13), (16), and (20) give
\[
\sum_{J\in D}w_J\tau_w(J)^r
\ge
\frac{M_0}{2^r(r+1)}
\left[V_0^{r+1}
+\sum_{s=1}^{Q}\alpha\eta^{2s-2}L_s^rV_s^{r+1}\right].
\tag{21}
\]
Put \(\chi_0^{(r)}=1\) and
\(\chi_s^{(r)}=\alpha\eta^{2s-2}L_s^r\) for \(s\ge1\).  Hölder's inequality yields
\[
V^{r+1}
\le
\left(\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\chi_s^{(r)}V_s^{r+1}\right)B_r^r,
\tag{22}
\]
where
\[
B_r
=1+\left(\frac{\eta^2}{\alpha}\right)^{1/r}
\sum_{s=1}^{Q}\frac{\eta^{s(r-1)/r}}{L_s}.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, (22) is Hölder applied to
\(\eta^sV_s=(\chi_s^{(r)})^{1/(r+1)}V_s\,\eta^s(\chi_s^{(r)})^{-1/(r+1)}\); substitution of \(\chi_s^{(r)}\) gives exactly (23).

Since \(\sum_{s\ge1}L_s^{-1}=3\), (11) gives, for \(r\ge2\),
\[
B_r
\le1+3\left(\frac{\eta^{r+1}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/r}
\le1+3\eta^{(r-2)/r}\le4.
\tag{24}
\]
For \(r=1\), it gives
\[
B_1\le1+3\frac{\eta^2}{\alpha}\le\frac{91}{16}.
\tag{25}
\]
Dividing (21) by \(W^{r+1}=(M_0V)^{r+1}\), using (22) and \(M_0=2^n\), gives
\[
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p(J)^r
\ge\frac1{(r+1)B_r^r}\,2^{-rN}.
\tag{26}
\]
At \(r=1\), equations (25) and (26) give the first inequality in (1); at \(r=2\), equations (24) and (26) give the second.

It remains to cover \(m=1\), for which \(\eta=1/2\) and no two-predecessor center is needed.  Since the entrances are nondecreasing, \(V_s\le V_0\), and
\[
V=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}2^{-s}V_s\le2V_0.
\]
Keeping only the central-column contribution in (21), whose derivation does not use \(\alpha\), gives
\[
\sum_{J\in D}p_J\tau_p(J)^r
\ge\frac1{r+1}\left(\frac{V_0}{V}\right)^{r+1}2^{-rN}
\ge\frac1{(r+1)2^{r+1}}\,2^{-rN}.
\]
For \(r=1\) this coefficient is \(1/8>8/91\), and for \(r=2\) it is \(1/24>1/48\).  This completes the proof of (1).

We next prove (2) and (3).  In the canonical specialization, accepted fact `604889294a17d7df` proves
\[
\max_J\sum_v\min\{w_{J+v},w_{J-v}\}=1,
\qquad
\nu_k\le1+\frac{H_k-H_7}{8}.
\tag{27}
\]
It also identifies this as the log-affine reflection obstruction.  Put \(\delta=1-\eta\).  By the central-binomial estimate in `032423921efad6c8`, the choice \(m=2(Q+1)^4\) gives
\(0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2}\).  Since at most the first two points in each symmetric chain lack two predecessors,
\(\alpha\ge1-2\delta\).  Therefore (13) at depth \(Q\), Bernoulli's inequality, and \(2Q/(Q+1)^2\le1/2\) give
\[
\frac{|G_Q|}{2^n}
=\alpha\eta^{2Q-2}
\ge(1-2\delta)(1-\delta)^{2Q-2}
\ge1-2Q\delta
\ge\frac12.
\tag{28}
\]

At one fixed depth no last-bit restriction is needed, so every \(j\in G_Q\) has the full \(2^{Q-1}\) source-disjoint triples from (14).  At the top height \(k\), every one of their source columns is present and \(d_Q(k)=\rho_k=2^{-k}\).  Hence
\[
\tau_w((j,k))\ge2^{Q-1}2^{-k}=2^{Q-k-1}.
\tag{29}
\]
Using only the at least \(2^{n-1}\) targets in (28), each of weight \(2^{-k}\), and using \(W=2^N\nu_k\), we get
\[
2^N\sum_Jp_J\tau_p(J)
\ge\frac{2^{Q-2k}}{8\nu_k^2},
\]
and
\[
4^N\sum_Jp_J\tau_p(J)^2
\ge\frac{2^{2Q-3k}}{16\nu_k^3}.
\]
Finally \(Q\ge\lfloor2^k/(8k)\rfloor\), while (27) is logarithmic in \(k\).  Both displayed lower bounds tend to infinity, proving (2) and (3).

We now turn to actual fibers under (4) and (5).  Equation (28), with the same proof for arbitrary \(Q\), supplies a nonempty set
\[
G=G_Q\subseteq A_0,
\qquad |G|\ge2^{n-1},
\tag{30}
\]
whose two inverse paths reach every depth.  Fix \(j\in G\).  For each \(s\ge1\), use all \(2^{s-1}\) triples in (14); only within-level disjointness is needed in the next comparison.

Fix one such triple \(\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\), a height \(t\ge a_s\), and write
\(K_0=C_{(j,t)}\) and \(K_r=C_{(v_r,t)}\).  These are \(d\)-dimensional convex bodies of the same positive volume \(\rho_t\).  Convexity of \(C\) and the index barycenter give
\[
\frac13(K_1+K_2+K_3)\subseteq K_0.
\tag{31}
\]
Put \(K_{12}=(K_1+K_2)/2\).  Brunn-Minkowski first gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{12})^{1/d}\ge\rho_t^{1/d}\), and then gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(\frac23K_{12}+\frac13K_3\right)^{1/d}
\ge\frac23\operatorname{vol}_d(K_{12})^{1/d}
+\frac13\rho_t^{1/d}
\ge\rho_t^{1/d}.
\]
The set on the left is the set in (31), so inclusion in \(K_0\) gives the reverse volume bound.  Both inequalities in this chain are therefore equalities.  The equality case applied first to \(K_1,K_2\), and then to \(K_{12},K_3\), shows that \(K_1,K_2,K_3\) are homothetic.  Their equal positive volumes make the homothety ratios one, so they are translates.  The set in (31) is a full-dimensional compact convex subset of \(K_0\) having the same volume; a strict convex inclusion would add a cone of positive volume, so equality holds in (31).  Consequently \(K_0\) is a translate of the other three as well.  Write \(K_r=K_0+q_r(t)\).  Then equality in (31) says
\(K_0+(q_1(t)+q_2(t)+q_3(t))/3=K_0\); compactness forces the translation vector to vanish.  Thus
\[
K_r=K_0+q_r(t),
\qquad
q_1(t)+q_2(t)+q_3(t)=0.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(d=1\), the same conclusion is immediate because the four positive-volume compact convex sets are intervals of the same length.  This verifies all hypotheses needed for the equality and translation step; no common fiber shape was assumed.

Let \(H\) be any closed ambient halfspace, with linear part \((a,b,u)\) on tree, height, and continuous coordinates.  At the fixed height \(t\), let \(K_r\cap H\) abbreviate the subset of \(K_r\) whose point in the integer fiber \((v_r,t)\), or \((j,t)\) for \(r=0\), lies in \(H\).  Define
\[
\Delta_r=a\cdot(v_r-j)+u\cdot q_r(t).
\]
Equations (15) and (32) give \(\sum_r\Delta_r=0\), so some \(\Delta_r\ge0\).  Translating every point of \(K_0\cap H\) by \(q_r(t)\), while changing its tree index from \(j\) to \(v_r\), moves the halfspace's linear functional by \(\Delta_r\ge0\).  It therefore injects \(K_0\cap H\) into \(K_r\cap H\) without changing volume.  Hence
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{3}\operatorname{vol}_d(K_r\cap H)
\ge\operatorname{vol}_d(K_0\cap H).
\tag{33}
\]
The selected source may depend on \(t\), but different integer heights are disjoint summands of mixed-integer measure.

For \(s\ge1\), sum (33) over the \(2^{s-1}\) source-disjoint triples and all heights \(t\ge a_s\), and include the target column itself.  For \(s=0\), keep only that column.  With the \(\beta_s\) from the statement, this proves, for every closed ambient halfspace,
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
\ge\beta_s
\sum_{t=a_s}^{T}\operatorname{vol}_d
\{y\in C_{(j,t)}:(j,t,y)\in H\}.
\tag{34}
\]

Consider the compact convex section
\[
\mathcal C_{j,s}=\{(t,y):(j,t,y)\in C,\ t\ge a_s\}
\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d.
\]
If \(a_s<T\), its positive-volume fibers at the two distinct integer heights \(a_s\) and \(a_s+1\) make it full-dimensional.  Apply accepted fact `072548cdd7a2b880` to this section with integer dimension one.  Its total mixed volume is \(V_s\), so the dimension-one specialization of that fact gives a point \(q_s\) for which every containing closed halfspace in the section has mixed volume at least
\[
\frac{V_s}{20(1+1)2^1}=\frac{V_s}{80}.
\tag{35}
\]
The restriction of an ambient halfspace to the affine section \(j\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d\) is a closed halfspace or the whole section, so (35) applies to the right side of (34).  If \(a_s=T\), take instead the centroid of the sole positive fiber.  Grünbaum's inequality gives at least \(\gamma_dV_s\ge V_s/e>V_s/80\) in every containing fiber halfspace, so (35) remains valid.  This also covers \(T=0\) and preserves boundary atoms.

Equations (34) and (35) imply
\[
\max_{q\in S}h_S(q)
\ge\frac1{80}\max_{0\le s\le Q}\beta_sV_s.
\tag{36}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
V
\le\left(\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\frac{\eta^s}{\beta_s}\right)
\max_s\beta_sV_s,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\frac{\eta^s}{\beta_s}
\le1+\frac12+\frac13+\sum_{s=3}^{\infty}2^{1-s}
=\frac73.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus the right side of (36) is at least \(3V/560\).  By (10), \(V=2\cdot2^{-N}W\), which proves (6).

Finally assume (7).  Every tail obeys \(V_s\le2\rho_{a_s}\).  Therefore (37) and the definition of \(\Xi\) give
\[
V\le2\Xi\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\frac{\eta^s}{\beta_s}
\le\frac{14}{3}\Xi,
\]
which, together with (10), proves (8).

Choose a maximizing level \(s\).  At the index \(J=(j,a_s)\), assign component mass \(\rho_{a_s}\) to the singleton \(\{J\}\) and, when \(s\ge1\), to each lifted triple
\[
\{(x_{s,\varepsilon},a_s),(y_{s,\varepsilon},a_s),(z_{s,\varepsilon},a_s)\}
\]
from the full family in (14).  Their sources are pairwise distinct and avoid \(J\), so every full source-charge constraint is met with equality or zero.  Each triple is affinely independent: on the root coordinates \((u_1,v_1)\), two of its displacement vectors from \(j\) are \((1,-1)\) and \((0,1)\).  All indices in every component have weight \(w_J=\rho_{a_s}\).  Hence, for its barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_I\), both costs are exactly
\[
\log w_J-\sum_I\lambda_I\log w_I=0
\]
and
\[
\log\frac{w_J}{\left(\sum_I\lambda_Iw_I^{1/d}\right)^d}=0.
\]
The total component mass is \(\beta_s\rho_{a_s}=\Xi\).  This proves the asserted zero-cost packing without using any linked artifact or reported finite check.

Let \(c_{j,a_s}\) be the centroid of \(C_J\).  From (32), the centroids of the three source fibers in each triple are
\(c_{j,a_s}+q_1(a_s),c_{j,a_s}+q_2(a_s),c_{j,a_s}+q_3(a_s)\).  Their full ambient points, including their integer indices, average to
\((j,a_s,c_{j,a_s})\).  Therefore every closed halfspace containing this target centroid contains at least one source-fiber centroid from each triple.  Grünbaum's inequality captures at least \(\gamma_d\rho_{a_s}\) in that source fiber, and it captures the same amount in the target fiber itself.  Since all these integer fibers are distinct, their contributions add to
\[
h_S((j,a_s,c_{j,a_s}))
\ge\gamma_d\beta_s\rho_{a_s}
=\gamma_d\Xi.
\]
Together with (8), this is (9).  The elementary inequality \((1+1/d)^d\le e\) gives \(\gamma_d\ge e^{-1}\).

The moment proof used uniform Boolean-center mass and equal source weights at each fixed height, while the actual-fiber proof used the same equal-volume condition in (31).  A horizontal factor changes those weights and removes both inputs.  Nothing above supplies a replacement estimate for a nonconstant horizontal tilt or an arbitrary joint tilt, proving the stated boundary of the theorem.

## External sources

The Brunn-Minkowski theorem and its equality case are used in (31)-(32): for positive-volume convex bodies \(K,L\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<\lambda<1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d((1-\lambda)K+\lambda L)^{1/d}
\ge(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_d(K)^{1/d}
+\lambda\operatorname{vol}_d(L)^{1/d},
\]
with equality exactly when \(K,L\) are homothetic; equal volumes then make them translates.  A primary source for the general sum-set theorem and corrected equality conditions is R. Henstock and A. M. Macbeath, “On the Measure of Sum-Sets. I. The Theorems of Brunn, Minkowski, and Lusternik,” Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society (1953), https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-3.1.182.  It applies because every fiber in (31) is compact, convex, and has the same positive \(d\)-volume.  The one-dimensional case is verified directly in the proof.

Grünbaum's centroid inequality is used in (35) and (9): if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body with centroid \(c_K\), every closed halfspace containing \(c_K\) contains at least \((d/(d+1))^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\).  The primary source is B. Grünbaum, “Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes,” Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10 (1960), 1257-1261, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257.  It applies only to the positive-volume target or source fibers whose centroids are explicitly shown to lie in the relevant restricted halfspace.
