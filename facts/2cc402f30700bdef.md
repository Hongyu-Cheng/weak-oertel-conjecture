## Statement

Let $\Xi$ be uniform on the probability tetrahedron and set $\phi_3(x)=\inf_{t>0,F_x(t)>0}t/F_x(t)^{1/3}$ for $F_x(t)=\Pr(x\cdot\Xi\le t)$. Take $0<p<1/3$, $q=(1-p)/(1+3p)$, $s=p+q$, and $c_1=(p,q,-1,-1)$, $c_2=(q,p,-1,-1)$, $c_3=(-s,-s,2,2)$. Put $T=(4s-1)/3$, $U_0=(3s+2)/s$, $\lambda=3s/(8s^2+7s+6)$. Define $x_i=\lambda(T\mathbf 1-c_i)$ for $i=1,2$, $x_3=\lambda(U_0\mathbf1-c_3)$ and $x_4=0$. The first three rows are positive and independent, and the matrix is column-stochastic. The minimizing thresholds are uniquely $\lambda T,\lambda T,\lambda U_0$, with fractions $P_1=P_2=(4s-1)/[4(s+2)]$ and $P_3=4(3s+2)/(s+2)^3$. Weights $P_i^{1/3}$ on these rows and $1/10$ on the zero row give objective one and a nonpositive first variation in every feasible direction.

For $z=(0,0,1,-1)$, the unscaled weighted optimized curvatures are $-1/6,-1/6,s(6-s)/[6(s+2)^2]$. The first two Hessians are nonpositive with exactly their row-radial kernels, and the third has one positive direction. The exact least unscaled second-order cost of funding $+z$ in row 3 from rows 1 and 2 is $1/12$, attained with $-z/2$ in each. The actual feasible funded path has second derivative $(3s-2)(2-s)/[12\lambda(s+2)^2]>0$, which is $3211/306180$ at $p=1/5$.

## Proof

Let $P(c)=\Pr(c\cdot\Xi\ge0)$ and let $f(c)$ be the density at zero of $\sum_j c_j E_j$, for independent unit exponentials $E_j$. Normalizing the exponentials by their sum gives $\Xi$. For raw signed heights $(p,q,-r,-w)$ with $p,q,r,w>0$, the raw cap formula of 1462fa9ce6ced54e combines to
$$
P(p,q,-r,-w)=
\frac{p^2q^2+pq(p+q)(r+w)+(p^2+pq+q^2)rw}{(p+r)(p+w)(q+r)(q+w)}.
$$
Its normalized coarea is $3f(c)$. In fact $f(c)=\mathbb E[1/R]$ times the uniform-simplex projection density at zero, where the independent radius $R=\sum_j E_j$ is Gamma$(4,1)$ and $\mathbb E[1/R]=1/3$.

Here $pq=(1-s)/3$ and $2/3<s<1$. Fact 6ad992fcbb68c716 identifies the three sections with common actual centroid $b=(1/(s+2),1/(s+2),s/[2(s+2)],s/[2(s+2)])$. For $c_1,c_2$, put $u=(1+p)^{-1}$ and $v=(1+q)^{-1}$. Integrating the survival function of an Erlang$(2,1)$ negative sum against the positive sum gives $P=1-uv(3-u-v)$ and $f=uv(2-u-v)$. The relation on $pq$ yields $P_1=P_2=(4s-1)/[4(s+2)]$ and $f_1=f_2=3/[4(s+2)]$. For $c_3$, put $a=U_0-2$ and $h=U_0+s$. Fact 1f0ffae097710a37 on the row with lower pair $a$ and upper pair $h$ gives $a/h=1/(s+1)$, normalized cut parameter $2/(s+2)$, minimizing threshold $U_0$, and $P_3=4(3s+2)/(s+2)^3$. The same conclusion about the threshold follows from $f_3=4s/(s+2)^3$ by the beta density and $3f_3=\partial_t F$. In particular $T=P_1/f_1$ and $U_0=P_3/f_3$.

The candidate thresholds solve $t\,\partial_t F=3F$ in the unscaled rows. Concavity of $F^{1/3}$ on the support makes the tangent line there an upper bound, equal to $P_i^{1/3}$ at threshold $t$ and with slope $P_i^{1/3}/t$. Equivalently $F(\tau)^{1/3}\le P_i^{1/3}\tau/t$ for all $\tau$ in the support. For larger $\tau$ the CDF is one and the same bound persists. Fact d2ba4e3cbd6eb730 gives uniqueness. Positivity of the first two rows follows from $T-s/2=(5s-2)/6>0$ and $(T-s/2)^2-(q-p)^2/4=4(1-s)^2/9>0$. For the third, $U_0>2$. The margins sum to zero, and $\lambda(2T+U_0)=1$, so the columns sum to one. The first two row difference spans $(1,-1,0,0)$. The third row is pair-constant and nonconstant, and the sum of all rows is $\mathbf1$. Thus the active span is $\{v:v_3=v_4\}$. The weighted gradient of each positive row is $b$ by d2ba4e3cbd6eb730. The objective is $\lambda(2T+U_0)=1$. For any feasible first-order direction with zero-row velocity $v\ge0$, its first variation is $-b\cdot v+\phi_3(v)/10$. Positive homogeneity gives the directional derivative of the zero row and $\phi_3(v)\le \max_j v_j$, by choosing the full cap. Since $\min_j b_j>1/8$, this derivative is strictly negative for $v\ne0$ and zero otherwise.

Write $H=\nabla^2P$ in the signed raw coordinates. Set $e=(1,-1,0,0)$ and $z=(0,0,1,-1)$. At $c_1$,
$$
H[e,e]=\frac{27(3s-2)}{8(s+2)^2},\qquad H[z,z]=\frac{3}{8(s+2)},\qquad H[e,z]=0.
$$
To verify the first value, at fixed $s$ and $r=w=1$ the cap is $1-(1+2s+3v)/(1+s+v)^2$ with $v=pq$. At $v=(1-s)/3$ its first derivative in $v$ vanishes and its second derivative is $3/(1+s+v)^3$. Along $e$, $v'=q-p$ and $(q-p)^2=(3s-2)(s+2)/3$. For the second value set $r,w=1\mp\varepsilon$, so $rw=1-\varepsilon^2$. The coefficient of $\varepsilon^2$ in the raw formula is
$$
\frac{P[(1+p)^2+(1+q)^2]-(s^2-pq)}{(1+s+pq)^2}.
$$
Substitution of $pq=(1-s)/3$ gives $3/[16(s+2)]$. The zero mixed term follows from exchange of the last two coordinates. The three vectors $e,z,c_1$ span the tangent space $b^\perp$, since the last two entries of $c_1$ agree and are nonzero, and $c_1$ is not in the span of $e,z$. Homogeneity gives $Hc_1=-\nabla P$ and $\nabla P=3f_1 b$, so the restriction is positive semidefinite with exactly its radial kernel. In threshold-one units, the Schur expression of 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9 writes the capacity quadratic form as a negative multiple of this restriction plus a square, inside the same negative prefactor, in the decomposition $v+s_0\mathbf1$, $b\cdot v=0$. If $v=k c$ for the threshold-one margin $c$, its square vanishes exactly at $s_0=-k$, since $\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Hc=-3P$. Thus the only capacity kernel is the row-radial line. The same proof works after exchanging the first two coordinates for row 2.

Along $z$, exchange symmetry gives $\nabla P\cdot z=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Hz=0$. In raw units, the weight $P^{1/3}$ times the optimized second derivative is therefore $-H[z,z]/(3f)$ by 8bdcdcd6e2fa69d9, rescaling its threshold-one expression by the actual unscaled threshold $P/f$. For rows 1 and 2 the result is $-1/6$. In row 3 the split vector $z$ belongs to the lower pair. Fact 1f0ffae097710a37 with $h-a=s+2$ and $u_*=2/(s+2)$ gives the cap second derivative $2(1-u_*)^2(1-4u_*)/(s+2)^2$, so the weighted optimized second derivative is $s(6-s)/[6(s+2)^2]$. Since $1/(s+1)\in(1/2,3/5)$, that fact gives precisely one positive eigenvalue, in the $z$ direction. In particular the active span does not contain this direction.

For arbitrary funding velocities in rows 1 and 2 whose sum is $-z$, their antisymmetric parts are $-\theta z$ and $-(1-\theta)z$. The symmetric parts are Hessian-orthogonal to these and have nonpositive second derivative in their own rows. Thus the optimal unscaled funding cost is $\min_{\theta\in\mathbb R}[\theta^2+(1-\theta)^2]/6=1/12$, attained at equal funding. The surplus is $(3s-2)(2-s)/[12(s+2)^2]>0$. Degree-one homogeneity scales every Hessian by $1/\lambda$ at the actual rows. The path with velocities $(-z/2,-z/2,z,0)$ therefore preserves column sums and positivity for both signs near zero and has zero first derivative and strictly positive second derivative equal to the stated surplus divided by $\lambda$. There are also feasible directions with strictly negative first derivative into the zero row. At $p=1/5,q=1/2$, one has $s=7/10$, $\lambda=35/247$ and the unscaled surplus is $13/8748$, giving $3211/306180$ in actual units.
