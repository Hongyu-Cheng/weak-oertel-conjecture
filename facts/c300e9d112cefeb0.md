## Proof

For any finite cost vector, the diagonal array is feasible, and every feasible coordinate lies in \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). Thus the feasible set and each minimizing face are nonempty compact convex sets. Each summand \(-t\log(t/p_{\mathbf I})\) is continuous and strictly concave on its closed interval, including at zero. Hence \(H\) has a unique maximizer on the entire minimizing face. This is the same finite-dimensional existence and uniqueness argument as in 57c57a5fe0d4d1cc; it does not require a generalization of that fact's row log-concavity conclusions. Summing the row moment equations also gives \(\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\mathbf J=\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\). Adding an affine function to a cost therefore changes its objective by a fixed constant and leaves the full minimizing face unchanged.

We first prove the interpolation criterion. Necessity follows from strict Jensen inequality for a nontrivial positive convex combination. For sufficiency let \(f\in\mathscr C_D^{\mathrm{str}}\) and put

\[
\vartheta_E=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I\|^2-\|\mathbf J\|^2
=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf I-\mathbf J\|^2>0
\]

for each nonsingleton member of the indicated finite families. If at least one such member exists, choose

\[
\epsilon=\frac12\min_{\mathbf J,\,E\ne\{\mathbf J\}}
\frac{\delta_E(f)}{\vartheta_E}>0;
\]

otherwise choose \(\epsilon=1\). Set \(g_{\mathbf I}=f_{\mathbf I}-\epsilon\|\mathbf I\|^2\). Every nonsingleton independent representation has \(\delta_E(g)>0\), and every singleton has zero gap. Every arbitrary representation of a lattice site has mean \(g\)-value at least \(g_{\mathbf J}\): minimize its mean \(g\)-value and then take a minimizer with smallest positive support. The support-reduction argument in the proof of 488f0a583886b5fc makes that support affinely independent, so it is one of the tested representations. This argument also covers the case with no nonsingleton representations.

Apply the lower-roof construction of 488f0a583886b5fc with its arbitrary roof values \(r_{\mathbf I}\) equal to the present \(g_{\mathbf I}\). Denote this roof by \(\mathcal R\), to distinguish it from the canonical potential roof \(\widehat\phi\). The preceding admissibility inequalities and the singleton representation give \(\mathcal R(\mathbf I)=g_{\mathbf I}\). That accepted construction proves that \(\mathcal R\) is the maximum of finitely many affine functions on \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), including when \(P\) has lower affine rank. Extend these affine functions from \(\operatorname{aff}P\) to \(\mathbb R^n\), for example by composing with the affine orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{aff}P\). Their finite maximum \(\overline{\mathcal R}\) is a finite convex function on all of \(\mathbb R^n\) agreeing with \(\mathcal R\) on \(P\). Consequently

\[
F(x)=\overline{\mathcal R}(x)+\epsilon\|x\|^2
\]

is globally strongly convex and satisfies \(F(\mathbf I)=f_{\mathbf I}\) at every prescribed site. This is an exact extension, not an approximation of the values or a perturbation of the selected source capacities.

For the Boolean factorization, an integral last-coordinate vector in the convex hull of the displayed support belongs to \(\{0,1\}^k\). We next give an explicit extension for part 2. For \(0\le h<M\), define the strictly convex quadratic cost

\[

\[

All the data in this maximum are integral, so \(L\) may in particular be taken to be a positive integer. For \(\boldsymbol\zeta\in\mathbb R^k\), set

\[

=\max_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k}
\left[\mathcal Q_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}(x,y)+\|\boldsymbol\zeta\|^2
+L\sum_{i=1}^k(2\eta_i-1)(\zeta_i-\eta_i)\right].
\]

This is finite everywhere. Every term is a positive-definite quadratic plus an affine function on \(\mathbb R^{k+2}\). Because there are finitely many terms, one positive multiple of \(\|(x,y,\boldsymbol\zeta)\|^2\) can be subtracted from each while retaining convexity. Their maximum after that subtraction remains convex. Hence \(F\) is globally strongly convex.

At a site \((\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\theta)\in D\), the penalty in the term indexed by \(\boldsymbol\eta\) is minus \(L\) times the Hamming distance between \(\boldsymbol\eta\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta\). For a different label that distance is at least one, while the difference of the two \(\mathcal Q\)-values is at most \(L-1\). The label \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is therefore the unique maximizing term there, and

\[
