## Statement

For every integer \(\ell\ge2\), let
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\ell&=\left\lbrace a\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^\ell:
\#\lbrace i:a_i=2\rbrace=1\right\rbrace,\\
S_\ell&=E_\ell\cup
\left\lbrace a\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^\ell:
\sum_{i=1}^\ell a_i\ge2\right\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then \(S_\ell=\operatorname{conv}(S_\ell)\cap\mathbb Z^\ell\). Let \(f\ge0\) be any integer, put
$$
D=S_\ell\times\lbrace0,1\rbrace^f,
\qquad N=\ell+f+1,
\qquad A=D\times\lbrace0\rbrace\subseteq\mathbb R^N.
$$
The support \(D\) is also complete: \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^{\ell+f}\). Choose arbitrary activities
$$
r_i\in[1/2,2]\quad(1\le i\le\ell),
\qquad t_h>0\quad(1\le h\le f).
$$
For \(x=(a,u)\in D\), use the original affine weights and their original normalization
$$
w_x=\prod_{i=1}^\ell r_i^{a_i}\prod_{h=1}^f t_h^{u_h},
\qquad Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,
\qquad p_{(x,0)}=\frac{w_x}{Z}.
$$
These are precisely \(w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x)\) with
\(\beta=(-\log r_1,\ldots,-\log r_\ell,-\log t_1,\ldots,-\log t_f)\).
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) solely as an abbreviation, and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(z)&=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed affine halfspace}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{y\in A\cap H}p_y,\\
M_3(p)&=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3,\\
s_p(c)&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y)
\quad(c\in D),\\
T(p)&=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
\end{aligned}
$$
The reflection sum counts ordered pairs, including the diagonal once. The halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal. With
$$
c_*=\frac{1}{8192\cdot17^6}>2^{-38},
$$
one has, for all the choices above,
$$
M_3(p)\ge c_*\ell^2 8^{-N},
\qquad
T(p)\ge 8c_*\ell^2 8^{-N}.
$$
In particular both requested bounds hold with the same absolute constant \(c=c_*\), or with \(c=2^{-38}\). Empty products are one, and \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^0\) is a singleton.

## Proof

First, \(S_\ell\) is the set of lattice points in \(\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\). Every point of \(E_\ell\) has coordinates in \([0,2]\) and coordinate sum at least two. These properties persist under convex combinations. If a point \(a\in\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\) has \(a_i=2\), every member of \(E_\ell\) occurring with positive coefficient in a convex representation of \(a\) must also have its \(i\)-th coordinate equal to two. All other coordinates of those members lie in \([0,1]\), so \(a_j\le1\) for every \(j\ne i\). Consequently any lattice point of \(\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\) either has exactly one coordinate equal to two and all others binary, or is Boolean with coordinate sum at least two. It belongs to \(S_\ell\).

Conversely, take a Boolean \(a\) with at least two ones, and choose distinct indices \(i,j\) at which \(a_i=a_j=1\). If \(e_i\) denotes the \(i\)-th unit vector, then
$$
a=\frac12(a+e_i-e_j)+\frac12(a-e_i+e_j),
\qquad a+e_i-e_j,\ a-e_i+e_j\in E_\ell.
$$
Thus \(S_\ell\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell)\), and since \(E_\ell\subseteq S_\ell\),
$$
\operatorname{conv}(S_\ell)=\operatorname{conv}(E_\ell),
\qquad
\operatorname{conv}(S_\ell)\cap\mathbb Z^\ell=S_\ell.
$$
For finite nonempty sets \(U,V\), one has
\(\operatorname{conv}(U\times V)=\operatorname{conv}(U)\times\operatorname{conv}(V)\): one inclusion follows by projection, and the other follows by multiplying the coefficients of convex representations in the two factors. Applying this identity to \(S_\ell\) and the Boolean cube gives
$$
\operatorname{conv}(D)=\operatorname{conv}(S_\ell)\times[0,1]^f,
$$
whose lattice points are exactly \(D\). This proves completeness also when \(f=0\).

We next record a halfspace estimate valid for every finite support with positive probabilities, without any product assumption. Fix \(c\in D\). The non-diagonal reflected pairs about \(c\), viewed as unordered pairs, have disjoint endpoints: an endpoint \(x\) has the unique partner \(2c-x\). Their endpoints differ from \(c\). Every closed affine halfspace containing \((c,0)\) contains at least one endpoint of each pair. Indeed, if both endpoints were outside a halfspace \(\lbrace z:v\cdot z\ge b\rbrace\), their average \((c,0)\) would also be outside. The center atom and these disjoint pairs therefore give
$$
\tau_p((c,0))\ge p_c+
\sum_{\substack{\lbrace x,y\rbrace\subseteq D,\ x\ne y\\x+y=2c}}
\min(p_x,p_y)
=\frac{s_p(c)+p_c}{2}\ge\frac{s_p(c)}2.
$$
Here the sum in the middle is over unordered two-element pairs. Cubing and summing with the original probabilities proves
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac18T(p).
$$
This argument takes place in \(\mathbb R^N\) and applies to all containing closed halfspaces, including those whose boundary contains the center.

To bound the reflection quantity, define the original leaf marginal and the Boolean-factor probabilities by
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_a^{\mathrm L}&=\prod_{i=1}^\ell r_i^{a_i},
&Z_{\mathrm L}&=\sum_{a\in S_\ell}w_a^{\mathrm L},
&\lambda_a&=\frac{w_a^{\mathrm L}}{Z_{\mathrm L}},\\
b_u&=\prod_{h=1}^f\frac{t_h^{u_h}}{1+t_h}
&&\left(u\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^f\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The product support gives the exact identities
$$
Z=Z_{\mathrm L}\prod_{h=1}^f(1+t_h),
\qquad p_{(a,u)}=\lambda_a b_u,
\qquad \sum_u p_{(a,u)}=\lambda_a.
$$
Thus \(\lambda\) is the marginal of the original law, with no change to the original weights. Write
$$
s_{\mathrm L}(c)=\sum_{\substack{a,a'\in S_\ell\\a+a'=2c}}
\min(\lambda_a,\lambda_{a'}),
\qquad
T_{\mathrm L}=\sum_{c\in S_\ell}\lambda_c s_{\mathrm L}(c)^3.
$$

For every Boolean vector \(c\), write \(X(c)=\sum_i c_i\). Consider a Boolean center \(c\in S_\ell\). For every ordered pair of distinct indices \((i,j)\) among its one-indices, the points
$$
a=c+e_i-e_j,\qquad a'=c-e_i+e_j
$$
are reflected members of \(E_\ell\). Their ordered pairs are distinct as \((i,j)\) varies, because \(a-c=e_i-e_j\) determines \((i,j)\). Moreover
$$
\lambda_a=\lambda_c\frac{r_i}{r_j},
\qquad
\lambda_{a'}=\lambda_c\frac{r_j}{r_i},
\qquad
\min(\lambda_a,\lambda_{a'})\ge\frac{\lambda_c}{4},
$$
where the last inequality uses \(r_i/r_j\in[1/4,4]\). There are \(X(c)(X(c)-1)\) such ordered pairs. Hence
$$
s_{\mathrm L}(c)\ge\frac{X(c)(X(c)-1)}4\lambda_c,
$$
and consequently
$$
T_{\mathrm L}\ge\frac{1}{64Z_{\mathrm L}^4}
\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^\ell}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^\ell r_i^{4c_i}\right)
\bigl[X(c)(X(c)-1)\bigr]^3.
$$
In this last sum, Boolean vectors of weights zero or one contribute zero, so adding them does not require them to belong to \(S_\ell\).

Put
$$
B_1=\prod_{i=1}^\ell(1+r_i),
\qquad B_4=\prod_{i=1}^\ell(1+r_i^4).
$$
The spike sets with distinct two-indices are disjoint, as is the Boolean part of \(S_\ell\). Summing their weights gives the exact leaf partition function
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm L}
&=\sum_{i=1}^\ell r_i^2\prod_{j\ne i}(1+r_j)
+B_1-1-\sum_{i=1}^\ell r_i\\
&=B_1\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^\ell\frac{r_i^2}{1+r_i}\right)
-1-\sum_{i=1}^\ell r_i\\
&\le B_1\left(1+\frac{4\ell}{3}\right)
\le2\ell B_1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed \(r^2/(1+r)\) is increasing for \(r>0\), with value \(4/3\) at \(r=2\), and \(1+4\ell/3\le2\ell\) for \(\ell\ge2\).

To evaluate the finite sum above, let \(C_1,\ldots,C_\ell\) be independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities
$$
q_i=\frac{r_i^4}{1+r_i^4}\ge\frac1{17},
\qquad X=\sum_{i=1}^\ell C_i.
$$
Their product law gives exactly
$$
\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^\ell}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^\ell r_i^{4c_i}\right)
\bigl[X(c)(X(c)-1)\bigr]^3
=B_4\,\mathbb E\bigl[(X(X-1))^3\bigr].
$$
Since \(X(X-1)\ge0\), convexity of the cube on the nonnegative reals yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\bigl[(X(X-1))^3\bigr]
&\ge\bigl(\mathbb E[X(X-1)]\bigr)^3\\
&=\left(\sum_{i\ne j}q_iq_j\right)^3
\ge\frac{\ell^3(\ell-1)^3}{17^6}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For every \(r>0\), convexity of the fourth power gives
$$
\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)^4\le\frac{1+r^4}{2},
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\frac{1+r^4}{(1+r)^4}\ge\frac18.
$$
Combining all these estimates, with the original \(Z_{\mathrm L}\), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm L}
&\ge\frac{B_4}{64Z_{\mathrm L}^4}
\frac{\ell^3(\ell-1)^3}{17^6}\\
&\ge\frac{B_4}{B_1^4}
\frac{(\ell-1)^3}{1024\ell\,17^6}\\
&\ge\frac{\ell^2}{8192\cdot17^6}\,8^{-\ell}
=c_*\ell^2 8^{-\ell}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last line uses \(\ell-1\ge\ell/2\), valid also at \(\ell=2\).

It remains to pass to the free Boolean coordinates. For any nonnegative numbers \(\rho,\rho',\sigma,\sigma'\), both \(\rho\sigma\) and \(\rho'\sigma'\) are at least \(\min(\rho,\rho')\min(\sigma,\sigma')\). Thus
$$
\min(\rho\sigma,\rho'\sigma')\ge\min(\rho,\rho')\min(\sigma,\sigma').
$$
Summing this inequality over independently reflected ordered pairs in two product factors gives the product lower bound for their reflection sums. For the present Boolean factor there is in fact equality: if \(u,u',v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^f\) satisfy \(u+u'=2v\), each coordinate forces \(u_h=u'_h=v_h\), so \(u=u'=v\). Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_p((c,v))
&=\sum_{\substack{a,a'\in S_\ell\\a+a'=2c}}
\min(\lambda_a b_v,\lambda_{a'}b_v)
=b_v s_{\mathrm L}(c),\\
T(p)&=T_{\mathrm L}\sum_{v\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^f}b_v^4
=T_{\mathrm L}\prod_{h=1}^f\frac{1+t_h^4}{(1+t_h)^4}
\ge T_{\mathrm L}8^{-f}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here each Boolean coordinate has only its diagonal reflected pair, and the same fourth-power inequality applies to every arbitrary positive \(t_h\). Consequently, since \(N=\ell+f+1\),
$$
T(p)\ge c_*\ell^2 8^{-(\ell+f)}
=8c_*\ell^2 8^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac18T(p)\ge c_*\ell^2 8^{-N}.
$$
Finally \(17^6=24137569<2^{25}\) implies \(8192\cdot17^6<2^{38}\), proving \(c_*>2^{-38}\). No factorization of halfspace depths has been used.
