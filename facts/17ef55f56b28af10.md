## Statement

Consider the compact convex polygon $C=\{(z,x):-1\le z\le1,\ L(z)\le x\le U(z)\}$, with $L(z)=-11/20+|z|/20$ and $U(z)=19/20-9|z|/20$. Use summed interval length $\mu$ on its integer fibers. Their total mass is $M=7/2$ and the largest fiber length is $3/2<M/2$, where two base parities are occupied.

The point $y=(0,1/10)$ attains the global maximum normalized mixed depth $33/70$. Its actual depth-attaining caps $H_+=\{x\ge1/10\}$ and $H_-=\{x-2z\le1/10\}$ have mass $33/20$ each. The first cuts all three fibers properly. The second is strictly empty on the left, proper in the middle and strictly full on the right. With continuous normals normalized to $+1$ and $-1$, their section counts are respectively $3$ and $1$, and both full ambient section centroids are $y$. Their normalized inverse-section weights are $(1/4,3/4)$, which yield cap multipliers $(\lambda_+,\lambda_-)=(35/66,35/22)$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i\mu(H_i)=M$.

Lift the original polygon to the convex hypograph $0\le r\le f_z(x)$, where $f_z(x)=\min\{x-L(z),U(z)-x\}$ is the own raw interval depth. Its uniform summed integer fiber area $\widehat\mu$ has total $\widehat M=17/16$. The vertical lifts $\widehat H_\pm$ of the original caps, with their inequalities independent of $r$, have areas $409/800$ and $369/800$, giving different normalized budgets $409/850$ and $369/850$. Their original cap multipliers give $\sum_i\lambda_i\widehat\mu(\widehat H_i)=2653/2640=\widehat M-19/330$. These identities hold independently of the selected lift height over $y$.

## Proof

The lower endpoint $L$ is convex and the upper endpoint $U$ is concave, so $C$ is compact and convex with interior. Its only integer fibers are $[-1/2,1/2]$ at $z=\pm1$ and $[-11/20,19/20]$ at $z=0$. This gives the mass and parity census. Apply c680a307d3f26cec with endpoint lengths $a=b=1$, middle length $3/2$ and middle center $s=1/5$. Its compatibility inequalities hold since $3/2\ge1$ and $1/5\le1/4$. Its second row applies since $\alpha=0$ and $\beta=1$. This gives the middle maximizer $x_*=1/10$ and raw depth $33/20$, exceeding the endpoint maxima $1/2$. Dividing by $M$ gives $33/70$.

For $H_+$ the traces have lengths $2/5,17/20,2/5$ at the three bases. Its three section points are $(-1,1/10),(0,1/10),(1,1/10)$ and have mean $y$. For $H_-$ the endpoint thresholds are $-19/10$ and $21/10$, strictly outside the left and right interval respectively. The trace lengths are $0,13/20,1$ and the unique section point is $y$. Thus both caps through $y$ attain its depth and have the stated full section centroids. Their continuous normals have length one, and ordinary coarea section masses are the section counts. Normalizing their inverse counts $1/3$ and $1$ gives $(1/4,3/4)$. Dividing these weights by the common normalized depth $33/70$ gives $(35/66,35/22)$ and summing the resulting original volume budget gives $M$.

The two terms in $f_z(x)$ are concave in $(z,x)$, hence their minimum is concave and its hypograph on $C$ is compact and convex. The height over an interval of length $l$ is a tent of area $l^2/4$, so $\widehat M=1/4+9/16+1/4=17/16$. The lower part of the middle tent up to $x=1/10$ is before its peak at $x=1/5$ and has area $(13/20)^2/2=169/800$. Each endpoint upper trace of $H_+$ gives area $(2/5)^2/2=2/25$. The sole full endpoint of $H_-$ gives area $1/4$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\mu(\widehat H_+) &=9/16-169/800+2(2/25)=409/800,\\
\widehat\mu(\widehat H_-) &=169/800+1/4=369/800.
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing by $\widehat M$ gives the stated fractions. The original multipliers give $(35/66)(409/800)+(35/22)(369/800)=2653/2640$, whereas $\widehat M=2805/2640$. Since the cap inequalities have no $r$ term, the lift height of $y$ cannot change any of these quantities.
