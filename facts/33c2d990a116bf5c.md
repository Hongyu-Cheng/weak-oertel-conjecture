## Statement

Let $B$ be a $d$-simplex with $d\ge1$, $N=d+1$, and barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$. For $0\le r\le N$, the largest subset of $B$ satisfying the $r$ specified bounds $\lambda_i\le1/N$ is $P_{N,r}=B\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^r\{\lambda_i\le1/N\}$, with
$$
 \frac{|P_{N,r}|}{|B|}=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j\binom{r}{j}(1-j/N)^d.
$$
If $k=N-r\ge1$, this is a full-dimensional convex body with exactly $k$ coordinates whose supremum strictly exceeds $1/N$, and
$$
 \frac{|P_{N,r}|}{|B|}
 =\frac{d!}{(k-1)!}\int_{[0,1/N]^r}\!\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\right)^{k-1}\,dx .
$$
The empty-coordinate integral has its usual zero-dimensional convention. For $r=N$ the feasible set is just the centroid.

## Proof

The imposed ceilings give the containment directly. Use inclusion-exclusion on the $r$ strict violations. For a specified $j$ of them, subtract $1/N$ from those coordinates; the translated barycentric simplex has total remaining mass $1-j/N$ and hence relative $d$-volume $(1-j/N)^d$. Replacing strict inequalities by weak ones affects only null hyperplanes. If $j=N$ the volume is zero because $d\ge1$. This proves the sum formula.

When $r<N$, omit one unconstrained coordinate as the dependent coordinate. For each $x\in[0,1/N]^r$, the $k-1$ independent unconstrained coordinates fill a coordinate simplex of volume $(1-\sum_i x_i)^{k-1}/(k-1)!$. The whole box is allowed since $r/N<1$, and division by the coordinate volume $1/d!$ of $B$ gives the integral; the affine Jacobian cancels. Taking all constrained coordinates strictly between zero and $1/N$ and distributing the remaining positive mass over the unconstrained coordinates gives an interior point. Each constrained coordinate reaches $1/N$ by putting the remaining mass in an unconstrained coordinate. Each unconstrained coordinate reaches one at its vertex. Finally, for $r=N$ the sum-one condition forces all coordinates equal to $1/N$.
