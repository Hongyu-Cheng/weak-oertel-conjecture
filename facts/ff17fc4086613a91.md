## Statement

Let $K_0,K_1,K_2\subset\mathbb R$ be compact positive-length intervals with $(K_0+K_2)/2\subseteq K_1$. At a point $x$ of $K_z$ let $g(z,x)$ be its distance to the nearer endpoint of $K_z$. For a closed ambient halfspace $H\subset\mathbb R^2$, put $\nu(H)=\sum_{z=0}^2\sup\{g(z,x):x\in K_z,\ (z,x)\in H\}$, with each empty term zero. Let $A_z=|K_z|/2$ and $T=(A_0+A_1+A_2)/2$. There is an actual point whose every closed containing halfspace has capacity at least $T$. If some $A_z\ge T$, its midpoint works.

Otherwise shear the endpoint midpoints to zero and reflect the continuous coordinate if needed, so that $K_0=[-A_0,A_0]$, $K_2=[-A_2,A_2]$ and $K_1=[-L,U]$ with $L\le U$. Write $s=A_0+A_2$ and $m=\min(A_0,A_2)$. Then $(1,x_*)$ works, with $x_*=\max(0,T-m-L)$. For any $x\in K_1$ the exact through-point nonvertical upper- and lower-cap minima in these coordinates are
$$
\min(A_1,U-x)+\min(m,(s-2x)_+),\qquad
\min(A_1,L+x)+\min(m,(s+2x)_+),
$$
where $(v)_+=\max(v,0)$.

## Proof

An affine shear in $x$ translates each fiber and preserves halfspaces and within-fiber distances. The common reflection also preserves them. In the stated coordinates the midpoint inclusion gives $L,U\ge s/2$, and $A_1=(L+U)/2$. If $A_z\ge T$, any cap containing its midpoint has that contribution alone.

For an upper cap with threshold $r$ on a centered interval $[-a,a]$, the capacity is $\min(a,(a-r)_+)$. A threshold at or below zero retains the peak; above zero the depth is decreasing and attains its maximum on a nonempty trace at its threshold. For a line through $(1,x)$ the two endpoint thresholds sum to $2x$, and every such pair is realizable by a finite slope. If a summand is full, their total capacity is at least $m$. If neither is full, the positive-part inequality gives at least $(s-2x)_+$. The total can attain $m$ by taking the smaller endpoint full and the other empty with sufficiently separated thresholds. When $0<s-2x<m$, split $s-2x$ into two nonnegative residuals bounded by the respective $A_0,A_2$ and subtract each residual from its endpoint radius to get the two thresholds. When $s-2x\le0$, take both thresholds at least their endpoint radii. This proves the endpoint upper minimum $\min(m,(s-2x)_+)$. Reflection gives the endpoint lower minimum $\min(m,(s+2x)_+)$. The middle capacities are $\min(A_1,U-x)$ and $\min(A_1,L+x)$ by the same tent calculation. These are independent of the line slope, proving both displayed formulas.

Assume all $A_z<T$. The two endpoint inequalities give $A_1>s-2m$, and the middle inequality gives $A_1<s$. Thus $T-m<A_1$ and $0<T-A_1<m$. Put $c=(U-L)/2$. Since $L+c=A_1$, the definition gives $0\le x_*\le c$. Also $L\ge s/2$ gives $T-m-L\le A_1/2-m\le T/2$, so $x_*\le T/2$. The lower endpoint term is $m$ because $s+2x_*\ge s\ge m$. The middle lower term is at least $T-m$ by $L+x_*\ge T-m$ and $A_1>T-m$. The upper middle term is $A_1$ because $x_*\le c$. Its endpoint term is at least $T-A_1$ since $s-2x_*\ge s-T=T-A_1$ and $m>T-A_1$. Both oriented minima are at least $T$.

A vertical through-point halfspace contains the middle and at least one endpoint fiber and has capacity at least $A_1+m>T$. Every containing halfspace may be translated to pass through the point, which only decreases capacity. This proves the bound for all containing halfspaces.
