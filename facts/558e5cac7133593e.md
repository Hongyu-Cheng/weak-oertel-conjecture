## Statement

Use the actual closed-halfspace depth and entropy capacity of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374, with their unchanged normalizations:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\substack{H\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\\mathbf j\in H}}p(H),\\
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
&:=\inf_{\mathbf u}\sum_{\mathbf x\in A}p_{\mathbf x}
                 e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(A\) is the finite support of the positive probability law \(p\), and \(\mathbf j\in A\). The notation \(\tau\) always denotes actual depth under the indicated original law, not an entropy capacity or a depth under a component or center law.

First, the following conditional-centered-component lemma holds. Let \(b\ge1\), let \(m_i\ge1\), and let \(\nu_i\) be probability laws on finite nonempty supports \(D_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{m_i}\), positive on those supports, with means \(\mathbf j_i\in D_i\). Suppose that, for a common \(\kappa\ge3\), the centered law of each row has a finite mixture representation
\[
\operatorname{Law}_{\nu_i}(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal E_i}\lambda_{i,a}\gamma_{i,a},
\qquad
\lambda_{i,a}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{a\in\mathcal E_i}\lambda_{i,a}=1,
\]
where every \(\gamma_{i,a}\) is a finitely supported vector law with mean \(\mathbf0\), and for every \(\mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^{m_i}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}(\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf V)^4
 \le\kappa\left[\mathbb E_{\gamma_{i,a}}
                         (\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf V)^2\right]^2.
\]
The inequality includes zero-variance projections without division by their variance. For \(Q:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^b\nu_i\) and the fixed mean \(J:=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_b)\), every proper closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{\sum_i m_i}\) containing \(J\) satisfies \(Q(H)\ge1/(4\kappa)\). Thus \(\tau_Q(J)\ge1/(4\kappa)\).

For the kite corollary, let \(L\ge2\) be any even integer and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_L^{\mathrm{kite}}
 &:=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,L),(1,-L),(3,0)\},\\
D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}
 &:=\{(0,0),(3,0)\}
 \cup\{(1,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ -L\le y\le L\}\\
 &\qquad\cup\{(2,y):y\in\mathbb Z,\ -L/2\le y\le L/2\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(q_L>0\) be the unique solution of \((L+1)q_L^2+2q_L^3=1\), and set
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}
 &:=1+(2L+1)q_L+(L+1)q_L^2+q_L^3,\\
\nu_{L,(x,y)}^{\mathrm{kite}}
 &:=\frac{q_L^x}{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}\qquad((x,y)\in D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}),\\
\boldsymbol\theta_L&:=(\log q_L,0),\qquad \mathbf j:=(1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are complete full-rank lattice supports, and the displayed laws are positive log-affine laws with exact integral mean \(\mathbf j\). For every \(b\ge1\) and every independently chosen even integers \(L_1,\ldots,L_b\ge2\), define the original product law and its fixed mean by \(Q:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^b\nu_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}}\) and \(J:=(\mathbf j,\ldots,\mathbf j)\in\prod_i D_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}}\). Then
\[
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{2b}\text{ a proper closed halfspace}\\J\in H}}
 Q(H)=\tau_Q(J)\ge\frac1{24},
\]
and, equivalently, for all \(\mathbf u_i\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
Q\left(\sum_{i=1}^b\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j)\ge0\right)
 \ge\frac1{24}.
\]
The last inequality also holds when all block normals vanish. Each tilt is precisely \(\boldsymbol\theta_{L_i}\), with \(q_i:=q_{L_i}\); no common size or common tilt is required. Arbitrary other tilts on the kite supports are not asserted to have this same mean or cap bound.

The exact aliases to bf1ff94487296374 are \(A=D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), \(p=\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), \(m=n=r=2\), and \(\boldsymbol\ell=\boldsymbol\theta_L\) for a single row, and \(A=\prod_iD_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}}\), \(p=Q\), \(m=n=r=2b\), and \(\mathbf j=J\) for its depth and capacity definitions. Our \(Q\) is the product denoted \(P\) in that fact. Moreover, if \(p^{\boldsymbol\ell}\) denotes any positive log-affine reference law on \(D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), its entropy-minimizing mean-\(\mathbf j\) law \(r_{\mathbf j}\) in that fact is exactly \(\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\). Thus the kite row is also the maximum-entropy mean-\(\mathbf j\) row, not a law of latent centers. The cap conclusion is about \(Q\), not about a product of the arbitrary reference laws \(p^{\boldsymbol\ell}\).

## Proof

We first prove the conditional-centered-component lemma. Realize each mixture by sampling a label \(K_i\in\mathcal E_i\) with probabilities \(\lambda_{i,a}\), then sampling \(\mathbf V_i\) with law \(\gamma_{i,K_i}\) and setting \(\mathbf X_i=\mathbf j_i+\mathbf V_i\). Make these experiments independent across \(i\). By the mixture identities, the resulting law of \((\mathbf X_1,\ldots,\mathbf X_b)\) is exactly \(Q\). Conditional on any label pattern \(K_i=a_i\) of positive probability, the vectors remain independent, and each has mean zero in centered coordinates.

Fix arbitrary block normals \(\mathbf u_i\). For this label pattern put \(T_i:=\mathbf u_i\cdot\mathbf V_i\), \(v_i:=\mathbb E[T_i^2\mid K_i=a_i]\), and \(T:=\sum_iT_i\). Until we average over labels, all expectations and probabilities are conditional on this fixed pattern. Independence and centering give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb ET&=0,\qquad \mathbb ET^2=\sum_iv_i,\\
\mathbb ET^4
 &=\sum_i\mathbb ET_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h\\
 &\le\kappa\sum_iv_i^2+6\sum_{i<h}v_iv_h
 \le\kappa\left(\sum_iv_i\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, every other term in the fourth-power expansion has at least one independently centered factor to the first power, and the last inequality uses \(6\le2\kappa\).

If \(\mathbb ET^2=0\), then \(T=0\) almost surely, so the closed event \(T\ge0\) has conditional probability one. Otherwise set \(\sigma^2:=\mathbb ET^2>0\). Hölder with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), applied to \(|T|^{2/3}|T|^{4/3}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sigma^2
 &\le(\mathbb E|T|)^{2/3}(\mathbb E|T|^4)^{1/3},\\
\mathbb E|T|
 &\ge\frac{\sigma^3}{(\mathbb ET^4)^{1/2}}
 \ge\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt\kappa}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Write \(T_+:=\max\{T,0\}\). Since \(\mathbb ET=0\), \(\mathbb ET_+=\mathbb E|T|/2\). Cauchy--Schwarz and \(T_+^2\le T^2\) therefore yield
\[
\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt\kappa}
 \le\mathbb ET_+
 \le(\mathbb ET_+^2)^{1/2}\Pr(T\ge0)^{1/2}
 \le\sigma\Pr(T\ge0)^{1/2}.
\]
Consequently every positive-probability label pattern has \(\Pr(T\ge0\mid K_1,\ldots,K_b)\ge1/(4\kappa)\). Averaging over the patterns proves \(Q(\sum_i\mathbf u_i\cdot(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf j_i)\ge0)\ge1/(4\kappa)\), with no loss depending on label probabilities or the number of factors. This includes any pattern on which the sum is identically zero.

A proper closed halfspace containing the already fixed \(J\) has the form \(H=\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\ge c\}\), where \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\) and \(c\le\mathbf u\cdot J\). Partition \(\mathbf u\) into the block normals \(\mathbf u_i\). Its boundary-through-\(J\) translate \(\{\mathbf x:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-J)\ge0\}\) is contained in \(H\), so the preceding bound gives \(Q(H)\ge1/(4\kappa)\). Taking the infimum proves the lemma in the actual-depth convention of bf1ff94487296374. No capacity-to-depth inference or change of measure has been used.

We now verify the kite geometry and the original law. The intersection of the inequalities \(0\le x\le3\), \(|y|\le Lx\), and \(|y|\le L(3-x)/2\) has exactly the four stated vertices and hence equals \(P_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\). Its vertical sections are
\[
\{y:(x,y)\in P_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\}
 =\begin{cases}
 [-Lx,Lx],&0\le x\le1,\\
 [-L(3-x)/2,L(3-x)/2],&1\le x\le3.
 \end{cases}
\]
At integral \(x=0,1,2,3\), these are precisely the columns in \(D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), and there are no other integral \(x\)-coordinates in the polygon. Since \(L\) is even, the third column has \(L+1\) points. Thus \(D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}=P_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\cap\mathbb Z^2\). All vertices belong to the support, so \(\operatorname{conv}(D_L^{\mathrm{kite}})=P_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), establishing completeness. The support contains \((0,0),(1,0),(1,1)\); its differences generate \(\mathbb Z^2\), and its affine rank is two. The point \(\mathbf j=(1,0)\) satisfies the polygon's defining inequalities strictly, so it is an integral interior support point.

The function \(t\mapsto(L+1)t^2+2t^3\) is continuous, strictly increasing on \(t>0\), is zero at zero, and has value \(L+3>1\) at one. It follows that its equation with right side one has exactly one positive solution \(q_L\in(0,1)\). Rearranging gives
\[
q_L^2=\frac1{L+1+2q_L},
\qquad
\frac1{\sqrt{L+3}}<q_L<\frac1{\sqrt{L+1}}.
\]
The normalizer \(Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\) counts, respectively, one point at \(x=0\), \(2L+1\) points at \(x=1\), \(L+1\) points at \(x=2\), and one point at \(x=3\). Hence the law is positive and normalized, and \(q_L^x=\exp(\boldsymbol\theta_L\cdot(x,y))\) proves log-affinity. Each column is vertically symmetric, so \(\mathbb E_{\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}Y=0\). Its horizontal mean is exactly one because
\[
Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\,
 \mathbb E_{\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}(X-1)
 =-1+(L+1)q_L^2+2q_L^3=0.
\]
Here \((X,Y)\) is the original planar vector, so its centered vector is \((X-1,Y)\).

For completeness, the product support is also complete and full-rank: \(\operatorname{conv}(\prod_iD_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}})=\prod_iP_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}}\), since products of individual convex-combination coefficients express every point of the right side as a convex combination of product-support points. Intersecting with \(\mathbb Z^{2b}\) then gives \(\prod_iD_{L_i}^{\mathrm{kite}}\). The product tilt is the concatenation of the \(\boldsymbol\theta_{L_i}\), so its log-affinity has exactly the accepted normalization. Its mean \(J\) was fixed by the exact balancing equations before any cap normal was chosen.

For the entropy-row alias, let \(\eta\) be any probability law on \(D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), and write its entropy as \(H(\eta):=-\sum_{\mathbf z}\eta_{\mathbf z}\log\eta_{\mathbf z}\), with zero summands omitted. Since \(\log\nu_{L,(x,y)}^{\mathrm{kite}}=x\log q_L-\log Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}})&=\log Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}-\log q_L,\\
D(\eta\|\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}})
 &=\log Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}-\log q_L-H(\eta)
 =H(\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}})-H(\eta)\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, nonnegativity follows by applying \(\log t\le t-1\) to \(t=\nu_{L,\mathbf z}^{\mathrm{kite}}/\eta_{\mathbf z}\) on the positive support of \(\eta\) and summing: \(D(\eta\|\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}})\ge1-\sum_{\eta_{\mathbf z}>0}\nu_{L,\mathbf z}^{\mathrm{kite}}\ge0\). Equality requires full support and equality at every summand, hence \(\eta=\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\). This proves the unique maximum-entropy assertion. For an arbitrary log-affine reference law \(p^{\boldsymbol\ell}_{\mathbf z}=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}/\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\),
\[
D(\eta\|p^{\boldsymbol\ell})
 =-H(\eta)-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf j
   +\log\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}.
\]
Thus its unique entropy minimizer at \(\mathbf j\) is \(\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), exactly the row \(r_{\mathbf j}\) from bf1ff94487296374. In particular, for the actual original law \(p=\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\), that fact's entropy formula gives \(\mathsf C_{\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}(\mathbf j)=1\). Likewise \(\mathsf C_Q(J)=1\) because \(Q\) is its own mean-\(J\) entropy minimizer. These capacity identities do not by themselves imply the claimed actual-depth lower bound.

The exact original column probabilities, with the subscript signs referring to \(X-1\), are
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{-,L}&:=\frac1{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}},&
p_{0,L}&:=\frac{(2L+1)q_L}{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}},\\
p_{+,L}&:=\frac{(L+1)q_L^2}{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}},&
\varepsilon_L&:=\frac{q_L^3}{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}},\\
p_{-,L}&=p_{+,L}+2\varepsilon_L.&
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality is exactly the balancing equation, divided by \(Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\). Each column's vertical coordinate is uniform on its displayed integer interval, because the weight depends only on \(x\).

For an integer \(h\ge0\), let \(U_h\) be uniform on \(\{-h,\ldots,h\}\), and let \(\delta_{\mathbf v}\) denote the point mass at \(\mathbf v\). Define three centered vector component laws, whose roman labels are labels rather than supports:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{L,\mathrm M}&:=\operatorname{Law}(0,U_L),\\
\gamma_{L,\mathrm B}&:=\tfrac12\delta_{(-1,0)}
                         +\tfrac12\operatorname{Law}(1,U_{L/2}),\\
\gamma_{L,\mathrm A}&:=\tfrac23\delta_{(-1,0)}
                         +\tfrac13\delta_{(2,0)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their means are all exactly \(\mathbf0\): \(U_h\) has mean zero by symmetry, the two horizontal values in component \(\mathrm B\) have equal weights, and component \(\mathrm A\) has horizontal mean \(-2/3+2/3=0\). Their mixture weights are \(p_{0,L}\), \(2p_{+,L}\), and \(3\varepsilon_L\), respectively. These weights are positive and sum to one, since
\[
p_{0,L}+2p_{+,L}+3\varepsilon_L
 =p_{0,L}+p_{+,L}+\varepsilon_L+p_{-,L}=1.
\]
This is an exact mixture identity for the full original centered vector:
\[
\operatorname{Law}_{\nu_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}(X-1,Y)
 =p_{0,L}\gamma_{L,\mathrm M}
   +2p_{+,L}\gamma_{L,\mathrm B}
   +3\varepsilon_L\gamma_{L,\mathrm A}.
\]
To check every atom, the mixture assigns mass \(p_{0,L}/(2L+1)=q_L/Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\) to each \((0,y)\), mass \(p_{+,L}/(L+1)=q_L^2/Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}\) to each \((1,y)\), mass \(p_{+,L}+2\varepsilon_L=p_{-,L}\) to \((-1,0)\), and mass \(\varepsilon_L\) to \((2,0)\). It assigns no other mass. Adding \(\mathbf j\) to these coordinates yields precisely all the original atoms with their original probabilities.

We verify the uniform directional fourth-moment bound on these components. Symmetry gives \(\mathbb EU_h=\mathbb EU_h^3=0\). The power-sum identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=1}^hr^2&=\frac{h(h+1)(2h+1)}6,\\
\sum_{r=1}^hr^4&=\frac{h(h+1)(2h+1)(3h^2+3h-1)}{30}
\end{aligned}
\]
follow by induction: both right sides vanish at \(h=0\), and their differences between \(h\) and \(h-1\) are respectively \(h^2\) and \(h^4\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EU_h^2&=\frac{h(h+1)}3,\\
\mathbb EU_h^4&=\frac{h(h+1)(3h^2+3h-1)}{15},\\
3(\mathbb EU_h^2)^2-\mathbb EU_h^4
 &=\frac{h(h+1)(2h^2+2h+1)}{15}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
These formulas also hold for \(h=0\), where \(U_0=0\) almost surely.

Fix any planar normal \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\). Under component \(\mathrm M\), its projection is \(vU_L\), so its fourth moment is at most three times the square of its second moment, and therefore at most six times that square. If \(v=0\), both moments vanish.

For component \(\mathrm B\), set \(\sigma_{L/2}^2:=\mathbb EU_{L/2}^2\). Its projection is \(-u\) with probability \(1/2\), and \(u+vU_{L/2}\) with probability \(1/2\). Using the zero first and third moments gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\gamma_{L,\mathrm B}}(uV_1+vV_2)^2
 &=u^2+\tfrac12v^2\sigma_{L/2}^2,\\
\mathbb E_{\gamma_{L,\mathrm B}}(uV_1+vV_2)^4
 &=u^4+3u^2v^2\sigma_{L/2}^2
                +\tfrac12v^4\mathbb EU_{L/2}^4\\
 &\le u^4+3u^2v^2\sigma_{L/2}^2
                +\tfrac32v^4(\sigma_{L/2}^2)^2\\
 &\le6\left(u^2+\tfrac12v^2\sigma_{L/2}^2\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The difference in the last inequality is \(5u^4+3u^2v^2\sigma_{L/2}^2\ge0\). Since \(L/2\ge1\), the second moment vanishes exactly when \(u=v=0\); then the fourth moment is zero as well.

For component \(\mathrm A\), the projection has values \(-u\) and \(2u\) with probabilities \(2/3\) and \(1/3\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\gamma_{L,\mathrm A}}(uV_1+vV_2)^2&=2u^2,\\
\mathbb E_{\gamma_{L,\mathrm A}}(uV_1+vV_2)^4&=6u^4
 =\tfrac32(2u^2)^2\le6(2u^2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
When \(u=0\), its projection is identically zero, independently of \(v\). Thus every component satisfies the lemma's inequality with the same \(\kappa=6\), for all directions and all even \(L\ge2\), including every zero-variance case.

For independently chosen \(L_i\), sample the three component labels independently, with the exact weights just proved, and then sample their centered component vectors independently conditional on the labels. This produces the original product \(Q\), not a replacement product. For each label pattern and every choice of block normals, the lemma's fourth expansion and scalar argument give conditional closed nonnegative-side probability at least \(1/24\). An identically zero conditional sum gives probability one. Averaging proves the displayed directional bound under \(Q\), and the halfspace translation in the lemma proves \(\tau_Q(J)\ge1/24\).

The absence of an unconditional fourth-moment hypothesis is substantive. For the original horizontal projection \(T_L:=X-1\), let \(s_L:=\Pr(T_L\ne0)\). The balancing equation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}&=(2L+1)q_L+2-q_L^3,\\
s_L&=\frac{2-q_L^3}{Z_L^{\mathrm{kite}}}
 <\frac{2\sqrt{L+3}}{2L+1}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Its second moment is positive. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to \(T_L^2\mathbf1_{\{T_L\ne0\}}\) gives \((\mathbb ET_L^2)^2\le s_L\mathbb ET_L^4\). Hence its fourth-to-second-moment-square ratio is at least \(1/s_L\), and is unbounded as even \(L\to\infty\), despite the product cap bound.

An explicit horizontal symmetric-noise comparison can be checked without assuming any unverified obstruction. Represent \(T_L=W_L+N_L\) by the four branches
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{branch probability}&W_L&N_L\\ \hline
p_{0,L}&0&0\\
2p_{+,L}&0&\text{equiprobable }-1,1\\
2\varepsilon_L&1/2&\text{equiprobable }-3/2,3/2\\
\varepsilon_L&-1&0
\end{array}
\]
with the noise conditionally symmetric given the branch. The masses sum to one, and the output probabilities at \(-1,0,1,2\) are respectively \(p_{+,L}+2\varepsilon_L=p_{-,L}\), \(p_{0,L}\), \(p_{+,L}\), and \(\varepsilon_L\). Thus the representation is exact. Its center satisfies
\[
\mathbb EW_L=0,\qquad
\mathbb EW_L^2=\tfrac32\varepsilon_L,\qquad
\mathbb EW_L^4=\tfrac98\varepsilon_L,\qquad
\frac{\mathbb EW_L^4}{(\mathbb EW_L^2)^2}
 =\frac1{2\varepsilon_L}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
because \(0<\varepsilon_L\le s_L\to0\). This comparison is not used in the cap proof and makes no claim about every possible symmetric-noise representation. The successful mixture instead combines the last two horizontal branches into the mean-zero component \(\mathrm A\); conditioning on that whole component preserves the original mean. Every cap estimate above uses the closed event and includes its zero atoms.
