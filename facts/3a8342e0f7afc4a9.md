## Statement

Let $Y_i$ be independent unit-rate exponentials and $V=\sum_i c_iY_i$ with at least one strictly positive and one strictly negative coefficient. Write $f$ for its density and $\mu_i$ for the mean under its actual coarea conditional law at $V=0$. Put $p=\max c_i$, $r=-\min c_i$, $I=(-1/r,1/p)$, $\beta_i(t)=1-tc_i$, $Z(t)=\prod_i\beta_i(t)$, and $c_i(t)=c_i/\beta_i(t)$ for $t\in I$. Let $F_t$ be the nonnegative-side probability and $\mu_i(t)$ the section means for $c_i(t)$ with unit-rate coordinates. Set $A(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{tv}f(v)\,dv$, $B(t)=\int_{-\infty}^0e^{tv}f(v)\,dv$, $G(t)=A(t)B(t)/(A(t)+B(t))$ and $H(t)=1/G(t)$. For $t\in I$ the new density is $f_t(v)=Z(t)e^{tv}f(v)$ and
$$
 \mu_i(t)=\beta_i(t)\mu_i,\qquad A(t)+B(t)=1/Z(t),\qquad
 F_t=Z(t)A(t),\qquad F_t(1-F_t)=Z(t)G(t).
$$
Consequently the ratio of the full product of the $\mu_i(t)$ to $F_t(1-F_t)$ equals $\prod_i\mu_i/G(t)$, and the ratio with one index $j$ omitted equals $\prod_{i\ne j}\mu_i/[\beta_j(t)G(t)]$. The function $H$ is convex on $I$. For a selected largest positive coefficient $p$, the function $(1-pt)G(t)$ is nondecreasing and then nonincreasing, allowing a monotonic whole interval, flat portions or an endpoint supremum.

## Proof

Both the sum of positive terms and the sum of absolute values of the negative terms have bounded densities that are positive on the open positive half-line. Convolution with the reflected negative sum gives a positive continuous density on $\mathbb R$, by translation continuity in $L^1$ for one density and the boundedness of the other. The section density and first moments are finite with positive denominator: eliminate a positive coordinate using the linear form and integrate its exponential density on the other coordinates on the open straddling domain. The extra linear coordinate factor in a moment remains integrable under exponential domination.

The moment generating function on $I$ is $\prod_i(1-tc_i)^{-1}$. Multiplying the joint density by $Z(t)e^{tV}$ produces independent exponential variables of rates $\beta_i(t)$. The tilt factor is constant on the zero section, so the conditional law on that section before rescaling is unchanged. Under the rescaling $Y_i'=\beta_i(t)Y_i$, the form is still $V=\sum_i c_i(t)Y_i'$. The linear transformation carries the coarea conditional law to that for the new coefficients because disintegration with respect to the same coordinate $V$ changes the raw coarea by the same constant in numerator and denominator. Equivalently, eliminating any nonzero coordinate with $V$ fixed gives the same density relation, including at $V=0$. This yields the means and density formulas. The cap and quotient identities follow by integration and multiplication of the rates. A coefficient zero has rate one and section mean one throughout.

On compact subintervals of $I$ the half-transforms may be differentiated repeatedly using a slightly larger subinterval for exponential domination. Convolution of the signed one-sided log-concave densities preserves log-concavity by Prékopa's theorem. The normalized positive half-density $e^{tv}f(v)/A(t)$ and the normalized reflected negative half-density $e^{-tv}f(-v)/B(t)$ on $v\ge0$ are positive log-concave. By 506e246f680da4b5 each has variance at most its squared mean. Direct differentiation gives $(1/A)''=[(\mathbb E_t V)^2-\operatorname{Var}_t(V)]/A\ge0$ for the positive half-density, and the reflected argument gives $(1/B)''\ge0$. Thus $H=1/A+1/B$ is convex. The derivative of $H/(1-pt)$ has numerator $N=(1-pt)H'+pH$, with $N'=(1-pt)H''\ge0$. Its sign can change only from negative to positive, including a possible zero interval. Taking the reciprocal proves the stated monotonicity of $(1-pt)G(t)$.
