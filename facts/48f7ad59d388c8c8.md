## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and let \(\pi:\mathbb R^{n+d}\to\mathbb R^n\) be projection onto the first \(n\) coordinates. Write
\[
\mathbb B_\infty^n:=\{\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n:\|\mathbf v\|_\infty\le 1\}.
\]
Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, set \(K:=\pi(C)\), and suppose that
\[
\mathbf z_0+k\mathbb B_\infty^n\subseteq K
\]
for some \(\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(k>\tfrac12\). Set \(\epsilon:=1/(2k)\). For a convex body \(D\subset\mathbb R^m\), denote its centroid by
\[
\mathfrak c(D):=\frac{1}{\mathcal H_m(D)}\int_D\mathbf y\,d\mathbf y.
\]
Then there exists \(\mathbf w\in C\) such that
\[
C':=(1-\epsilon)C+\epsilon\mathbf w
\]
is a convex body and satisfies:

1. \(C'\subseteq C\) and
\[
\mathcal H_{n+d}(C')=(1-\epsilon)^{n+d}\mathcal H_{n+d}(C).
\]
2. \(\pi(\mathfrak c(C'))\in\mathbb Z^n\). Consequently,
\[
\mathfrak c(C')\in C'\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\subseteq C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]
3. \(\pi(C')\) contains a translate of
\[
\left(k-\tfrac12\right)\mathbb B_\infty^n.
\]

## Proof

Put \(\rho:=1-\epsilon\). Since \(k>\tfrac12\), one has \(0<\epsilon<1\) and hence \(\rho>0\). Let
\[
Q:=[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^n=\tfrac12\mathbb B_\infty^n
\]
and write \(\mathfrak c(C)=(\mathbf z_C,\mathbf x_C)\).

We first choose the projected coordinate of \(\mathbf w\). Since \(\epsilon k=\tfrac12\), the box assumption gives
\[
\epsilon\mathbf z_0+Q
=\epsilon(\mathbf z_0+k\mathbb B_\infty^n)
\subseteq\epsilon K.
\]
After translating this inclusion,
\[
\mathbf a+Q\subseteq \rho\mathbf z_C+\epsilon K,
\qquad
\mathbf a:=\rho\mathbf z_C+\epsilon\mathbf z_0.
\]
Every translate of \(Q\) contains an integer vector. Indeed, define
\[
z_i^\star:=\left\lfloor a_i+\tfrac12\right\rfloor
\quad (i=1,\ldots,n).
\]
Then \(|z_i^\star-a_i|\le\tfrac12\), so \(\mathbf z^\star:=(z_1^\star,\ldots,z_n^\star)\in(\mathbf a+Q)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). It follows that \(\mathbf z^\star\in\rho\mathbf z_C+\epsilon K\). Thus there is \(\mathbf u\in K\) such that
\[
\rho\mathbf z_C+\epsilon\mathbf u=\mathbf z^\star.
\]
Because \(K=\pi(C)\), choose \(\mathbf w\in C\) with \(\pi(\mathbf w)=\mathbf u\).

Define the affine map \(T:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^m\) by
\[
T(\mathbf y):=\rho\mathbf y+\epsilon\mathbf w.
\]
Then \(C'=T(C)\). The linear part of \(T\) is \(\rho I_m\), which is invertible because \(\rho>0\). Hence \(T\) maps the compact convex set \(C\) to a compact convex set and maps the nonempty interior of \(C\) to a nonempty open set contained in \(C'\). Therefore \(C'\) is full-dimensional and is a convex body.

For every \(\mathbf y\in C\), the point \(T(\mathbf y)=\rho\mathbf y+\epsilon\mathbf w\) is a convex combination of \(\mathbf y\) and \(\mathbf w\). Convexity of \(C\) therefore gives \(T(\mathbf y)\in C\), proving \(C'\subseteq C\).

The determinant of the linear part of \(T\) is \(\rho^m\). The change of variables \(\mathbf v=T(\mathbf y)\) consequently yields
\[
\mathcal H_m(C')
=\int_C \rho^m\,d\mathbf y
=\rho^m\mathcal H_m(C)
=(1-\epsilon)^{n+d}\mathcal H_{n+d}(C),
\]
which is the asserted exact volume scaling.

The same change of variables proves affine equivariance of the centroid in this case:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak c(C')
&=\frac{1}{\rho^m\mathcal H_m(C)}
  \int_C T(\mathbf y)\rho^m\,d\mathbf y\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal H_m(C)}
  \int_C(\rho\mathbf y+\epsilon\mathbf w)\,d\mathbf y\\
&=\rho\mathfrak c(C)+\epsilon\mathbf w.
\end{aligned}
\]
Projecting onto the first \(n\) coordinates and using the choice of \(\mathbf u\) gives
\[
\pi(\mathfrak c(C'))
=\rho\mathbf z_C+\epsilon\mathbf u
=\mathbf z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n.
\]
The centroid of a compact convex set belongs to that set: its defining integral is a limit of finite convex combinations of points of the set, and the set is convex and closed. Thus \(\mathfrak c(C')\in C'\); together with \(C'\subseteq C\), this also proves the consequence in item 2.

Finally, linearity of \(\pi\) gives
\[
\pi(C')=\rho K+\epsilon\mathbf u.
\]
Since \(\mathbf z_0+k\mathbb B_\infty^n\subseteq K\),
\[
\bigl(\rho\mathbf z_0+\epsilon\mathbf u\bigr)
+\rho k\mathbb B_\infty^n
\subseteq \rho K+\epsilon\mathbf u
=\pi(C').
\]
Because
\[
\rho k=(1-\epsilon)k=k-\tfrac12,
\]
the projection \(\pi(C')\) contains a translate of \((k-\tfrac12)\mathbb B_\infty^n\), as required.
