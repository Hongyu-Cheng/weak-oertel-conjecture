## Statement

Let $d\ge2$, let convex bodies $K_j\subset\mathbb R^d$ converge in Hausdorff distance to a convex body $K$, and let $M_j\to M>0$, $\lambda_j>0$ with $\lambda_j\to0$. On $K_j$ let $L_j=\min_{1\le i\le k}A_{ij}>0$ have a fixed finite affine presentation, with coefficient limits $A_i$ and limiting minimum $L$. Suppose $\int_{K_j}(M_jL_j)^{-1}\,dX\le C_{\rm inv}$. Put $r_j=\lambda_j(-\log L_j)_+$. Partition volume into geometric active cells $C_{ij}=K_j\cap\bigcap_l\{A_{ij}\le A_{lj}\}$, counting identical planes once and discarding volume-null cells. Pass to a subsequence with fixed retained labels and, for every retained cell, put $b_{ij}=\max_{C_{ij}}A_{ij}$ and $R_i=\lim_j\lambda_j(-\log b_{ij})_+$. For the finite-cell assertion suppose the rates are uniformly bounded, so these limits are finite after selection.

For any common weak limit of the cell measures $(M_jA_{ij})^{-1}\mathbf1_{C_{ij}}\,dX\,\delta_{r_j(X)}$, there is no mass on $0<r<R_i$. Its restriction to $r>R_i$ is $\gamma_i(r)(dX)\,dr$ with a decreasing, right-continuous version in measure order. The conditionals are locally bounded in mass on $r>R_i$, are supported on $K\cap\{A_i=L=0\}$, and for almost every such $r$ and all compact $U,V$ of positive conditional mass satisfy
$$
\gamma_i(r)(\theta U+(1-\theta)V)^{1/(d-1)}
\ge\theta\gamma_i(r)(U)^{1/(d-1)}+(1-\theta)\gamma_i(r)(V)^{1/(d-1)}
\qquad(0<\theta<1).
$$
In particular they are log-concave and their nonzero supports are convex and nested. The slicing conclusion leaves open mass at $R_i$. A constant limiting plane contributes no positive-rate mass.

For a single affine $L_j$ whose limit has nonzero gradient and zero face $F=K\cap\{L=0\}\ne\varnothing$, the same conclusions hold on all $r>0$ without a uniform upper rate bound, for every joint weak limit locally on that range. The projected prelimit slice masses at $r\ge r_0>0$ are at most $2^d C_{\rm inv}/r_0$ for all sufficiently large $j$. The same local slice argument applies to a single geometric cell with convergent nonzero limiting gradient and anchors of uniformly positive height.

## Proof

Since interior points of $K$ eventually lie in $K_j$, Fatou's lemma gives $\int_K1/L\le MC_{\rm inv}$, interpreting the reciprocal as infinite at zeros. If a nonnegative affine $A_i$ vanishes at an interior point of $K$, it is identically zero there. Because $0\le L\le A_i$ on $K$, this contradicts reciprocal integrability. Hence all $A_i$ and $L$ are positive in $\operatorname{int}K$, and any constant $A_i$ is strictly positive. Uniform coefficient convergence then excludes positive rates for such a cell. Distinct prelimit affine planes tie only on volume-null sets, so the cell restrictions have total inverse mass at most $C_{\rm inv}$.

Fix a remaining cell and suppress its label. Write $c_j$ for the affine gradient and $m=d-1$. Rotate its level planes to coordinates in the fixed hyperplane perpendicular to the limiting gradient, translating by the perpendicular foot of the zero plane. The rotations may be chosen to tend to the identity, and the translations converge because the gradient limit is nonzero. Let $D_j(s)$ be the compact convex tangential slice at affine level $s$. For a cell anchor with tangential coordinate $u_j$ and height $b_j$, convexity gives
$$
D_j(t)\supset u_j+\frac{b_j-t}{b_j-s}(D_j(s)-u_j)
\qquad(0<s<t<b_j).
$$
Thus $\widetilde D_j(s)=u_j+\frac{b_j}{b_j-s}(D_j(s)-u_j)$ increases with $s<b_j$. This uses only membership of the anchor in the cell. At rate $r>0$, $s_j(r)=e^{-r/\lambda_j}$, and linear coarea and $|ds|/s=dr/\lambda_j$ give the exact slice measure $\mathbf1_{D_j(s_j(r))}\,dy/(M_j\lambda_j|c_j|)$. Write $\widetilde\gamma_j(r)$ for the same scaled Lebesgue measure on $\widetilde D_j(s_j(r))$.

When $0<r<R_i$ no level at that rate lies in the cell, uniformly on compact subintervals for large $j$. On any compact rate interval strictly above $R_i$, $s_j(r)/b_j\to0$ uniformly, since $\lambda_j(-\log b_j)_+\to R_i$. The projected measures decrease with $r$ there, and the projection approaches the identity uniformly on the bounded slices, with Jacobian tending to one. For $R_i<q<r_0$ the Jacobian is at most $2^m$ on $[q,r_0]$ for large $j$. Integration on that interval gives
$$
\widetilde\gamma_j(r)(\mathbb R^m)\le\frac{2^m C_{\rm inv}}{r_0-q}
\qquad(r\ge r_0).
$$
For one affine plane use any fixed $o\in\operatorname{int}K$ as anchor, so $b_j\to L(o)>0$ and $R_i=0$, and take $q=r_0/2$. A cell anchor with uniformly positive height works by the same argument.

Apply monotone Helly selection on compact subintervals above the threshold to the integrals against a countable uniformly dense family of nonnegative continuous functions on a common compact tangential set. A diagonal subsequence gives convergence simultaneously off a countable set of jumps. The mass bound and positivity extend the limit to finite measures on the tangential set. Monotonicity gives a right-continuous version by taking weak right limits. The projection error and the Jacobian correction both tend to zero there. Transporting to physical coordinates and applying dominated convergence in $r$ on compact subintervals identifies the asserted disintegration for the given joint limit. Hausdorff convergence and $A_{ij}=L_j=s_j(r)\to0$ place it on $K\cap\{A_i=L=0\}$.

Each projected slice is convex, so the ordinary $m$-dimensional Brunn–Minkowski inequality applied to the intersections of the two test sets with that slice gives the displayed inequality for its scaled Lebesgue measure. For weak closure, take open $\epsilon$-neighborhoods of the two compact positive-mass input sets and the closed $2\epsilon$-neighborhood of their Minkowski combination as target. The Brunn–Minkowski inequality also applies to the input intersections with the neighborhoods by inner approximation by compact sets. The open lower and closed upper Portmanteau bounds yield the inequality with the enlarged target and the limiting input masses. Shrink $\epsilon$ to zero. Weighted arithmetic-geometric mean implies log-concavity on positive-mass compact sets, and the zero-mass case has a zero right side. Neighborhoods of support points then prove convexity of nonzero supports. The order of the measures gives nesting. See [Gardner, The Brunn–Minkowski inequality, Theorem 4.1](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/jaw/COURSES/520s/523/HO.523.20/Gardner.BullAMS.02.pdf).
