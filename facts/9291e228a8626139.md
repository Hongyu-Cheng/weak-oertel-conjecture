## Statement

Let $\mu$ be normalized mixed length on $([-1,1]\times[-1,1])\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$, and let $g(z)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}\operatorname{depth}_\mu(z,x)$ use closed ambient halfspaces, also for real $z$. Then $g(-1/2)=g(-1/4)=1/3$ and $g(0)=1/2$. For every strictly increasing $\phi$ defined on $[1/3,1/2]$, the values of $\phi\circ g$ at these bases violate the midpoint concavity inequality. The same values hold if the optimization at a base is restricted to its actual real section.

## Proof

Halfspace-depth superlevels are convex because a containing halfspace at a convex combination contains at least one of the combined points of positive weight. Reflection in the continuous coordinate preserves $\mu$, so averaging $(z,x)$ and $(z,-x)$ shows that the optimum is at $x=0$. At the origin all containing caps capture at least half the mass by central symmetry, with equality for $x\ge0$. At $(-1/2,0)$ the two length-two fibers at $-1,0$ are centrally symmetric about the mark and supply at least two units of length to each containing cap. The pure-base cap $z\le-1/2$ captures exactly those two units from the single fiber at $-1$. Dividing by the total six gives $g(-1/2)=1/3$. By convexity of the depth superlevel at $1/3$, the point $(-1/4,0)$ has depth at least $1/3$, and $z\le-1/4$ again gives equality. The midpoint of $-1/2$ and $0$ is $-1/4$, where $\phi(1/3)<(\phi(1/3)+\phi(1/2))/2$. The optimizing marks all belong to their real sections.
