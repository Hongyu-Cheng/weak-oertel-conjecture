## Statement

For integers $j\ge2$ let $C_j=[1/j,1+1/j]\times[0,1]$ and $C=[0,1]\times[0,1]$. Although $C_j\to C$ in ambient Hausdorff distance, the maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth over $C_j\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$ is $1/2$ for every $j$, whereas the corresponding maximum over $C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R)$ is $1/4$. Each normalization uses the total mixed length of its own body.

## Proof

Translation by $(1/j,0)$ takes $C$ to $C_j$. The sampled bases of $C_j$ are exactly $\{1\}$, and those of $C$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$. Every sampled interval has length one and is strictly exposed among the positive-volume bases, including the singleton case. By the exposed-fiber depth identity (7) of 2acc8bc803acf1c0, raw mixed depth on each such fiber equals its intrinsic interval depth, whose maximum is $1/2$ by the one-simplex case of (12). The total mixed lengths are respectively one and two. The loss of continuity occurs at the newly sampled full interval at base zero.
