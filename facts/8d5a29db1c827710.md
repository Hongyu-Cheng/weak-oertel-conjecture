## Statement

For every real M >= 4, define
\[
r=\frac{1}{2M\log M},\qquad
a=-\log\left(1-\frac{1-r}{M}\right),\qquad
b=\log\frac{M}{r}=2\log M+\log(2\log M),
\]
and define Lebesgue densities on the real line by
\[
g(x)=e^{-x}\mathbf1_{[0,\infty)}(x),\qquad
f_M(x)=M e^{-x}\bigl(\mathbf1_{[0,a]}(x)+\mathbf1_{[b,\infty)}(x)\bigr).
\]
Here g and f_M are respectively the reference probability density g and the original probability density f of accepted fact c141f5a75f9e90d4; the exact alias is f=f_M, with no change of normalization. The parameter r is the original probability mass of the tail interval, not a dimension or centroid rank.

The reference density g is log-concave, f_M is a probability density with finite first absolute moment, and f_M <= M g everywhere. Its own mean is
\[
m_M:=\int_{\mathbb R}x f_M(x)\,dx
=1-(M-1+r)a+r b.
\]
It satisfies a < m_M < b, and consequently
\[
\int_{m_M}^{\infty}f_M(x)\,dx
=r=\frac{1}{2M\log M}.
\]
Moreover, as M tends to infinity,
\[
M a\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac b{\log M}\longrightarrow2,\qquad
M m_M\longrightarrow\frac32,
\qquad
\int_{m_M}^{\infty}g(x)\,dx=e^{-m_M}\longrightarrow1.
\]
In particular, the original cap has the requested upper order with the fixed constant C=1/2, while the reference cap at the same threshold has a different asymptotic size.

## Proof

Since M >= 4, we have log M > 1 and 0 < r < 1/8. Put u=(1-r)/M, so 0 < u < 1/4. The elementary integral bounds
\[
u\le-\log(1-u)=\int_0^u\frac{dt}{1-t}
\le\frac{u}{1-u}
\]
give
\[
0<a\le\frac{1-r}{M-1+r}\le\frac{1}{M-1}\le\frac13<1.
\]
Also b > 2 log M > 2. Hence 0 < a < b and the two intervals in f_M are disjoint. Convexity of [0,infinity) and affinity of log g=-x on this support prove log-concavity of g, including its zero extension outside the support. Direct integration gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx=1,\qquad
M\int_0^a e^{-x}\,dx=M(1-e^{-a})=1-r,
\qquad
M\int_b^\infty e^{-x}\,dx=M e^{-b}=r.
\]
Thus f_M has total mass one, is nonnegative, and satisfies f_M <= M g. Its first absolute moment is finite because it is supported on [0,infinity) and is bounded by M g. Since
\[
\int_0^a x e^{-x}\,dx=1-(a+1)e^{-a},\qquad
\int_b^\infty x e^{-x}\,dx=(b+1)e^{-b},
\]
its mean equals
\[
\begin{aligned}
m_M
&=M[1-(a+1)e^{-a}]+r(b+1)\\
&=1-Ma e^{-a}+r b
=1-(M-1+r)a+r b.
\end{aligned}
\]

To prove m_M > a, use the sharper logarithmic bound
\[
-\log(1-u)-u=\int_0^u\frac{t}{1-t}\,dt
\le\frac{u^2}{2(1-u)}.
\]
It yields
\[
M a\le1-r+\frac{(1-r)^2}{2(M-1+r)}
\le1-r+\frac{1}{2(M-1)}.
\]
Because a < 1 and r > 0, this implies
\[
(M+r)a-1
\le\frac{1}{2(M-1)}-r(1-a)
<\frac{1}{2(M-1)}.
\]
On the other hand, b > 2 log M gives r b > 1/M, and M > 2 gives 1/M > 1/[2(M-1)]. Therefore
\[
m_M-a=1-(M+r)a+r b>0.
\]
For the other strict inequality, the exact mean formula gives m_M <= 1+r b, since (M-1+r)a is positive. The bounds r < 1/8 and b > 2 give
\[
m_M<1+\frac b8<b,
\]
where the last inequality follows from 1 < 7b/8. This establishes a < m_M < b for every stated M, without an asymptotic qualification. The interval (a,b) carries no f_M mass, so the original right cap through m_M consists exactly of the tail and has mass M e^{-b}=r. Endpoints are immaterial because both laws have densities.

For the asserted limits, r tends to zero and u tends to zero. The bounds u <= a <= u/(1-u) show
\[
1-r\le M a\le\frac{1-r}{1-u},
\]
so M a tends to one and a tends to zero. The displayed exact formula for b and the elementary limit log(2 log M)/log M -> 0 show b/log M -> 2. Changing variables x=a t in the early first moment gives
\[
M\left(M\int_0^a x e^{-x}\,dx\right)
=(M a)^2\int_0^1 t e^{-a t}\,dt\longrightarrow\frac12,
\]
because e^{-a t} tends uniformly to one on [0,1]. The tail first moment satisfies
\[
M r(b+1)=\frac{b+1}{2\log M}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Adding these two first moments proves M m_M -> 3/2. In particular m_M tends to zero and is positive, so the reference right cap is exactly e^{-m_M} and tends to one.

Accepted fact c141f5a75f9e90d4 bounds the reference g cap at the original f mean, with the alias f=f_M stated above; that reference cap is indeed e^{-m_M} here. The original cap is instead exactly r. Since M r=1/(2 log M) tends to zero, for every positive constant c the inequality integral from m_M to infinity of f_M >= c/M fails for all sufficiently large M. This proves the claimed adversarial calibration.
