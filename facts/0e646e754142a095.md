## Statement

Let $\mu=\sum_z\mu_z$ be a finite positive measure on finitely many compact fibers at distinct bases $z$, keeping only positive masses $w_z$, with lifted fiber barycenters $g_z$ and global barycenter $g$. Fix an anchor $y_*=(z_*,x_*)$ at a positive fiber and finitely many closed caps $H_j=\{w:a_j\cdot(w-y_*)\ge0\}$ with $a_j=(v_j,u_j)$. Suppose that every cap has the same mass $D>0$ and zero mass on every other fiber. Let $\lambda_j\ge0$, not all zero, satisfy $\sum_j\lambda_j u_j=0$. Define the raw flux $J_j=\sum_z (a_j\cdot(g_z-g))\mu_z(H_j)$. Then $\sum_j\lambda_j J_j\ge0$, with equality exactly when there is no other positive fiber. For a regular nonvertical cut of an ordinary mixed-volume family, the coarea section mass is $S_j=\sum_z\mathcal H_{d-1}(K_z\cap\partial H_j(z))/\lVert u_j\rVert$; continuous center-gradient balancing coefficients $\theta_j$ give raw coefficients $\lambda_j=\theta_jS_j$ (which must be nonzero as a vector to apply the conclusion).

Replacing zero outside mass by strict exteriority of every outside fiber barycenter fails for finite measures. At bases $0,1$ take $\mu_0=\frac34\delta_1+\frac14\delta_{-100}$ and $\mu_1=\frac14\delta_3+\frac34\delta_{-1}$ on compact interval fibers, and the anchor $(0,0)$. For the two closed caps with margins $x-z/2$ and $-x-z/2$, the common mass is $1$ and the outside barycenter $(1,0)$ has both margins $-1/2$, but the raw fluxes are $-95/16$ and $-99/16$, summing to $-97/8$ with weights $(1,1)$.

## Proof

By the displayed finite-sum definition and isolation, $J_j=D a_j\cdot(g_{z_*}-g)$. The definition agrees with the finite closed-tail flux of 18ac4869642d018e; no tree representation is needed. Put $a=\sum_j\lambda_j a_j$. Its continuous component is zero, so $a\cdot(g_{z_*}-y_*)=0$. In each other positive fiber the closed cap has zero mass, so $a_j\cdot(w-y_*)<0$ almost everywhere there for each $j$. Hence $a\cdot(g_z-y_*)<0$ for every such $z$, since at least one $\lambda_j>0$. Averaging over the fibers gives $D a\cdot(g_{z_*}-g)>0$ if another fiber is positive, and gives zero otherwise.

For the coarea remark, varying the continuous anchor by $\xi$ shifts each scalar cut threshold by $u_j\cdot\xi$. At regular cuts, with the summed slice density continuous at the threshold, integration along $u_j/\lVert u_j\rVert$ gives the derivative $-S_j u_j\cdot\xi$. Thus $\sum_j\theta_jS_j u_j=0$ is exactly the required balance for the stated raw weights.

In the example the cap fractions at $0,1$ are $(3/4,1/4)$ and $(1/4,3/4)$, respectively; no atom is on a cap plane. The two scalar margin mean pairs are $(-97/4,-1/2)$ and $(97/4,-1/2)$. For unit fiber masses the global scalar mean is the average, so with mean pair $(m_0,m_1)$ and cap fractions $(P_0,P_1)$ the flux is $(m_0-m_1)(P_0-P_1)/2$. This gives respectively $-95/16$ and $-99/16$. The continuous normals are $1,-1$, and the two halfspaces strictly exclude the outside barycenter.
