## Statement

Let a finite compatible family of positive compact convex fibers have total summed fiber-volume $M$ and maximum normalized closed-halfspace depth $\beta$, and let $C$ be its lifted hull. For an exposing affine functional $\ell$ put $h=\max_C\ell$, $F=C\cap\{\ell=h\}$ and $C_t=C\cap\{\ell\le h-t\}$. Assume $F$ contains no whole measured fiber, and the removed mixed mass $\varepsilon_t$ is positive for small $t>0$.

Select one critical circuit at each deepest base. If all selected cap boundaries avoid $F$, use the constant binary incidence $I_j$ of $F$ in the cap. A strict binary-incidence score greater than $\beta$ at every selected circuit, with weights $\rho_j/S_j^-$ for inside vertices and $\rho_j/S_j^+$ for outside ones, implies strict descent along $C_t$. Here the score is the total inside weight divided by the sum of inside and outside weights. At an unrestricted fiberwise Hausdorff local minimum, for every exposed point $v$ in the measured family there is a deepest base at which every critical circuit whose boundaries avoid $v$ has score at most $\beta$. A circuit family that is empty satisfies the universal statement.

For positive polytopal fibers and $d\ge2$, the conclusion at a local minimum applies to all critical circuits at some deepest base, for each exposed vertex $v$ and each chosen exposing functional, if the score inequality is replaced by $\Phi_{C_p}(\iota)\ge0$ from the two-slope response and the incidences on cap boundaries are the actual tangent-cone fractions. For a general convex exposed point in $d\ge2$, any fixed finite selected menu satisfies the slab hypothesis, and the two-slope response applies on each sequence of positive trims along which its incidence vector converges. At a fixed-complexity local minimum the same necessary assertions require admissibility of the corresponding supporting-plane trims.

## Proof

Each measured fiber contains an interior point with strictly lower $\ell$ than $h$: this is automatic when its maximum is below $h$, and otherwise follows from the assumption that $F$ contains no whole fiber and the affinity of $\ell$. Any fixed strict homothet of that fiber toward such an interior point is retained for small $t$. Thus the actual sections stay positive and converge fiberwise in Hausdorff distance. Any fixed interior anchor ball of sufficiently small radius survives, since the center has a strict gap. The deleted sets approach $F$ by compactness of $C$, and their masses tend to zero because $\ell=h$ has zero volume in every measured fiber that meets it. The face is connected, so a cap boundary avoiding it leaves it entirely on one strict side. Compactness gives a uniform sign gap on $F$. The same sign is eventually shared by the whole deleted set, even after a threshold motion $O(\varepsilon_t)$. The binary-incidence descent criterion of the localized-removal quantile theorem therefore applies.

For an exposed measured point $v$, the gap on every other fiber is strict and the exposing continuous part is nonzero on its own fiber. Consequently only the own fiber is trimmed and the removed mass is positive. If the avoiding-circuit protection failed, choose a circuit of score strictly above $\beta$ at each deepest base. This is a finite selection and the common trim gives strict descent, contrary to local minimality. This proves the stated order of quantifiers. It is enough for the contradiction that the trim path belongs to the class of competitors.

For a polytopal vertex use the one-vertex case of the simultaneous trim lemma. Its tangent-cone fractions give exact deleted incidences and its $o(\varepsilon_t)$ estimate applies in $d\ge2$, including when some caps pass through the vertex. Negating protection again provides one strict circuit at each deepest base, so the two-slope theorem gives the same contradiction. For a nonpolytopal exposed point the one affected fiber's removed portion has diameter tending to zero by compactness and unique exposure. A width-$O(\varepsilon_t)$ slab meets it in volume at most its width times a constant times the $(d-1)$-st power of that diameter, hence in $o(\varepsilon_t)$ for $d\ge2$. The finite incidence vector takes values in a compact cube and has subsequential limits. On every sequence where that vector converges, the two-slope theorem supplies the stated response. The diameter argument for vanishing interaction requires $d\ge2$.
