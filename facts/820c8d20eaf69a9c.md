## Statement

For the three-fiber source with endpoint simplex $K$ and middle triangle $B$ in the nonsymmetric cap-flat rounding lemma, every product Hausdorff neighborhood of its fibers and every neighborhood of its canonical three cap planes $l_i(x,y)=0$ contain an actual compatible family on bases $-1,0,1$ and a whole-space-covering group of three caps with total mass $>5/2$ and every selected cap mass $<5/4$.

## Proof

Use the lemma's unnormalized rounding with $E$ and the caps $H_i(a)$. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, vary just the fiber at $1$ from $K+\varepsilon E$ to $Q(W)+\varepsilon E$, where the columns of $W$ are the labeled vertices of the new triangle in the old barycentric coordinates. Each column sums to one, and $W=I$ is the old simplex. Let $V_\varepsilon(W)$ be the change of this fiber's volume from its rounded baseline. Let $F_{\varepsilon i}(W,a)$ be its mass in the trace $\lambda_i(x,y)=l_i(x,y)+1/3\ge a_i$. The exact global cap increment from $\widehat M_\varepsilon/2$ is
$$
C_{\varepsilon i}(W,a)=F_{\varepsilon i}(W,a)-F_{\varepsilon i}(I,a).
$$
Indeed the other two fibers are unchanged and the full rounded family at $I$ has that budget for every $a$. In particular all pure threshold derivatives of $C_{\varepsilon i}(I,a)$ vanish.

A fixed invertible linear map sends $E$ to the Euclidean unit disk. It takes $Q(W)$ to a labeled affine perturbation of the transformed triangle, and each cap to the transformed halfplane with the same signed vertex margins. On a small neighborhood of $(I,a^*)$, $a^*=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, exactly the vertex labeled $i$ is inside the $i$th cap with strict uniform margins. Thus b6faa5c5f97bd079 makes both the transformed total and retained areas polynomial in the small radius with smooth parameter coefficients. Dividing by the fixed determinant gives the same regularity for $V_\varepsilon$ and $C_\varepsilon$, including a joint smooth extension across zero radius for the implicit equation.

At zero radius the moved fiber is the centered simplex of area $V=1/2$, with trace $\lambda_i\ge a_i$ and $C_{0i}=V(P_i(W,a)-P_i(I,a))$, where $P_i=\det W\,(W_{ii}-a_i)^2/\prod_{j\ne i}(W_{ii}-W_{ij})$. These are exactly the functions used in the matrix calculation of af5faac906b2cdf7 for $d=2$, $m=3$, $\Gamma=4/9$. We use the calculation on these functions. It requires the moving baseline identity just proved here. For a zero-column-sum velocity $U$, the physical cap derivatives at $(I,a^*)$ are $V f_i(U)$, with $f_i(U)=\Gamma(\operatorname{tr}U+(U\mathbf1)_i)$. The proof there gives the right inverse $\mathcal R r=S(r/V)$, where $S(y)_{ii}=y_i/(3\Gamma)$ and $S(y)_{ij}=y_i/(3\Gamma)-(\sum_\ell y_\ell)/(6\Gamma)$ for $i\ne j$. For the elementary matrix $U_0$ with $(U_0)_{01}=1,(U_0)_{11}=-1$ and all other entries zero, put $U=U_0-S(f(U_0))$ and $\omega=e_1-e_0$. The accepted calculation gives $f(U)=0$ and the vanishing gradient of
$$
L(W,a)=V_0(W)-(3\Gamma)^{-1}\sum_i C_{0i}(W,a)
$$
at $(I,a^*)$ on this parameter space. Its Hessian divided by $V$ on $(U,\omega)$ is $-1/6+2(1/2)=5/6$, including the mixed block twice and the zero pure threshold block.

Set $W(t,r)=I+tU+\mathcal Rr$ and $a(t)=a^*+t\omega$. The derivative in $r$ of $C_0(W(t,r),a(t))$ at zero is the identity. The implicit function theorem gives a joint smooth solution $r(t,\varepsilon)$ with $C_\varepsilon(W(t,r(t,\varepsilon)),a(t))=0$. It has $r(0,\varepsilon)=0$ because $C_\varepsilon(I,a^*)=0$, and $r_t(0,0)=0$ because the first cap derivative along $(U,\omega)$ vanishes. The function $J_\varepsilon(t)=V_\varepsilon(W(t,r(t,\varepsilon)))$ is zero at $t=0$. At $\varepsilon=0$ it equals $L$ along the implicit curve, whose initial tangent is $(U,\omega)$. Hence $J_0''(0)=V(5/6)=5/12$. For all sufficiently small fixed positive $\varepsilon$ we have $J_\varepsilon''(0)>0$. The symmetric difference $J_\varepsilon(t)+J_\varepsilon(-t)$ is then strictly positive for small $t>0$, so one of the two signs has strict volume gain at exactly the baseline budgets. The rounded support margin is positive at this fixed radius, and these times can be arbitrarily small, so the perturbation retains compatibility. The $r$ derivative stays invertible at the chosen time. Changing the cap increment target from zero to a sufficiently small negative common number by the implicit function theorem lowers every cap budget while keeping the strict volume gain and compatibility.

Apply the common dilation $\alpha_\varepsilon$ from the rounding lemma to the new family and its halfspaces. It restores the baseline mass and budget to $5/2$ and $5/4$ respectively, preserving the strict inequalities. The three transformed margins sum to zero because $\sum_i a_i=1$, so the new group covers the whole space. Taking the rounding small, then the motion and cap targets sufficiently small, gives the stated joint neighborhood conclusion.
