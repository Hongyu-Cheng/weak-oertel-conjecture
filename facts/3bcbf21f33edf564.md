## Statement

All logarithms are natural.  Let \(N\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\) be finite and nonempty, and suppose
\[
D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^N.
\]
Let \(p=(p_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in D}\) be a positive probability law.  Extend it by zero to \(\mathbb Z^N\), and assume that this zero-extended table is the restriction to \(\mathbb Z^N\) of a globally log-concave function on \(\mathbb R^N\).  No product structure or full-dimensionality of \(D\) is assumed.  Define
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
:=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf z\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
M_q(p):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q.
\]
For \(\mathbf c\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^N\), put
\[
D_{\mathbf c}:=\{\mathbf z\in D:\mathbf z\equiv\mathbf c\pmod2\},
\qquad
\alpha_{\mathbf c}:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D_{\mathbf c}}p_{\mathbf z},
\]
and let \(b\) be the number of occupied classes, namely those with
\(\alpha_{\mathbf c}>0\).  Also put
\(\beta:=\sum_{\mathbf c}\alpha_{\mathbf c}^2\), where only occupied classes are summed.

Then the first moment satisfies the parity-resolved estimate
\[
M_1(p)
\geq
\frac14\sum_{\mathbf c}
\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf c}^2}{\log(16/\alpha_{\mathbf c})}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2,
\tag{1}
\]
and hence both
\[
M_1(p)
\geq
\frac{1}{4b\log(16b)}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2
\tag{2}
\]
and the adaptive estimate
\[
M_1(p)
\geq
\frac{\beta}{4\log(16/\beta)}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Use exactly the accepted erosion notation
\[
t_{\mathbf z}:=\tau_p(\mathbf z),
\qquad
r_s(\mathbf z):=\min\{p_{\mathbf z},(t_{\mathbf z}-s)_+\},
\qquad
R_s:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}r_s(\mathbf z),
\qquad
G(s):=\int_s^\infty R_u\,du.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
H_b:=2b\log(16b).
\tag{5}
\]
At every erosion level,
\[
G(s)\geq\frac{R_s^2}{2H_b},
\qquad
G(s)\geq\frac{(1-H_bs)_+^2}{2H_b}.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, for every real \(q\geq1\),
\[
\boxed{
M_q(p)
\geq
\frac{H_b^{-q}}{q+1}
+\frac q{q+1}\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^{q+1}.}
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(b\leq2^N\), the cubic specialization is
\[
M_3(p)
\geq
\frac{8^{-N}}{32[(N+4)\log2]^3}
+\frac34\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}^4.
\tag{8}
\]

Now let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, let
\(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\ne\varnothing\), and put
\(W:=\mathcal H_d(S)\).  Then there exists a point
\(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), selected before the arbitrary containing closed halfspace, such that
\[
h_S(\mathbf y^\star)
\geq
\frac{1}{24\sqrt{15}(\log2)(n+5)}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular the same conclusion holds with the smaller coefficient
\[
\frac{1}{96(\log2)(n+5)}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{10}
\]
The latter is exactly the direct cubic composition of (8).

## Proof

We first establish the energy inequality used in (1).

**Kernel lemma.**  Let \(\eta\) be a Borel probability measure on
\(\mathbb R\) with finite exponential moments at \(1\) and \(-1\).  Put
\[
K:=\left(\int e^s\,d\eta(s)\right)
   \left(\int e^{-s}\,d\eta(s)\right),
\qquad
J:=\iint e^{-|s-t|}\,d\eta(s)d\eta(t).
\]
Then
\[
J\geq\frac{1}{4\log(4\sqrt K)}.
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed, translation of \(\eta\) leaves \(J\) and \(K\) unchanged.  Translate so that the two exponential moments both equal \(\sqrt K\).  Then
\(\int\cosh s\,d\eta(s)=\sqrt K\).  For every compactly supported absolutely continuous function \(g\), define
\[
F(t):=\int_{s\geq t}e^{-(s-t)}\,d\eta(s).
\]
Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
2\int_{\mathbb R}F(t)^2\,dt
=\iint e^{-|s-u|}\,d\eta(s)d\eta(u)=J.
\]
Also
\[
g(s)=\int_{-\infty}^s e^{-(s-t)}(g'(t)+g(t))\,dt.
\]
After integrating this identity against \(\eta\), Fubini and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\left(\int g\,d\eta\right)^2
\leq \frac J2\int_{\mathbb R}(g'+g)^2
=\frac J2\int_{\mathbb R}(g'^2+g^2),
\tag{12}
\]
because \(\int 2gg'=0\).

Set \(a:=\log(4\sqrt K)\) and
\[
g(s):=\left(1-\frac{\cosh s}{\cosh a}\right)_+.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(K\geq1\), so \(a>0\).  Since
\(\cosh a\geq2\sqrt K\),
\[
\int g\,d\eta
\geq1-\frac{\sqrt K}{\cosh a}\geq\frac12.
\]
A direct even integration on \([-a,a]\) gives
\[
\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}(g'^2+g^2)\,ds=a-\tanh a\leq a.
\]
Substitution in (12) proves (11).

We apply the lemma separately to the occupied parity classes.  Global log-concavity and completeness imply that, for
\(\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D_{\mathbf c}\), the integer midpoint belongs to \(D\) and
\[
p_{(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2}\geq\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}}.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\mathbf c}}p_{\mathbf x}^{3/2}\right)
\left(\sum_{\mathbf y\in D_{\mathbf c}}p_{\mathbf y}^{1/2}\right)
&=\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D_{\mathbf c}}
p_{\mathbf x}\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}}\\
&\leq\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\mathbf c}}p_{\mathbf x}
\sum_{\mathbf y\in D_{\mathbf c}}p_{(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2}\\
&\leq\alpha_{\mathbf c}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
For the last inequality, for each fixed \(\mathbf x\) the map
\(\mathbf y\mapsto(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)/2\) is injective into \(D\), so its inner sum is at most the total probability one.

Let \(\mathbf X\) have conditional law
\(p_{\mathbf x}/\alpha_{\mathbf c}\) on \(D_{\mathbf c}\), and apply (11) to
\(\frac12\log p_{\mathbf X}\).  By (14), the corresponding \(K\) is at most \(1/\alpha_{\mathbf c}\).  Since the right side of (11) decreases with \(K\), this gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathbf c}
&:=\sum_{\mathbf x,\mathbf y\in D_{\mathbf c}}
p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}
\exp\left(-\frac12|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|\right)\\
&\geq
\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf c}^2}
{2\log(16/\alpha_{\mathbf c})}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

For \(\mathbf z\in D\), extend \(p\) by zero and define the oriented reflection energy
\[
S_p(\mathbf z):=
\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^N}
\min\{p_{\mathbf z+\mathbf v},p_{\mathbf z-\mathbf v}\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z\) contains the central atom and at least one endpoint of each nonzero antipodal pair.  Since the two orientations of each nonzero pair both occur in (16),
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)\geq\frac{S_p(\mathbf z)+p_{\mathbf z}}2.
\tag{17}
\]
Under the bijection
\((\mathbf z,\mathbf v)\leftrightarrow
(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=(\mathbf z+\mathbf v,\mathbf z-\mathbf v)\), the endpoint pair \((\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\) ranges over all ordered same-parity pairs.  For such a pair, (13) and
\[
\min\{p_{\mathbf x},p_{\mathbf y}\}
=\sqrt{p_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf y}}
\exp\left(-\frac12|\log p_{\mathbf x}-\log p_{\mathbf y}|\right)
\]
give
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z}p_{\mathbf z}S_p(\mathbf z)
\geq\sum_{\mathbf c}A_{\mathbf c}.
\tag{18}
\]
Multiplying (17) by \(p_{\mathbf z}\), summing, and using (15) proves (1).

For (2), the function
\[
f(x):=\frac{x^2}{\log(16/x)}
\]
is convex on \((0,1]\): if \(L=\log(16/x)\), then
\[
f''(x)=\frac2L+\frac3{L^2}+\frac2{L^3}>0.
\]
Jensen over the \(b\) positive parity masses gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf c}f(\alpha_{\mathbf c})\geq1/[b\log(16b)]\), proving (2).

For (3), use the probability weights
\(\rho_{\mathbf c}:=\alpha_{\mathbf c}^2/\beta\).  Jensen for the convex function \(x\mapsto1/x\) yields
\[
\sum_{\mathbf c}
\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf c}^2}{\log(16/\alpha_{\mathbf c})}
\geq
\frac{\beta}
{\sum_{\mathbf c}\rho_{\mathbf c}\log(16/\alpha_{\mathbf c})}.
\tag{19}
\]
Moreover, nonnegativity of relative entropy of \(\rho\) with respect to
\(\alpha\) says
\[
0\leq\sum_{\mathbf c}\rho_{\mathbf c}
\log\frac{\rho_{\mathbf c}}{\alpha_{\mathbf c}}
=\sum_{\mathbf c}\rho_{\mathbf c}\log\alpha_{\mathbf c}-\log\beta.
\]
Thus
\(\sum\rho_{\mathbf c}\log(1/\alpha_{\mathbf c})\leq\log(1/\beta)\).  Inserting this in (19) and then in (1) proves (3).

We next apply this new first-moment estimate throughout depth erosion.  We use only the unconditional erosion conclusions of accepted fact `01a4100542206b94`, not its additional midpoint-dominance theorem.  In the exact notation (4), that accepted fact supplies all of the following interface: \(t_{\mathbf z}\geq p_{\mathbf z}\), hence \(R_0=1\); for every closed halfspace \(H\),
\[
r_s(H)\leq(p(H)-s)_+;
\tag{20}
\]
the positive support \(A_s=\{\mathbf z:t_{\mathbf z}>s\}\) is complete; every nonzero \(r_s\) admits a globally log-concave extension, including for lower-dimensional \(A_s\); and
\[
G(s)=
\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)(t_{\mathbf z}-s)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2.
\tag{21}
\]
It also supplies a depth-minimizing closed halfspace for every original atom.  Applying (20) to such a minimizing halfspace shows, for every
\(\mathbf z\in A_s\),
\[
\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)\leq t_{\mathbf z}-s,
\tag{22}
\]
where \(\tau_{r_s}\) is unnormalized closed-halfspace depth.

Fix \(s\) with \(R_s>0\), and normalize the surviving table by
\(\widetilde p_s(\mathbf z):=r_s(\mathbf z)/R_s\) on \(A_s\).  Its support occupies some number \(b_s\leq b\) of parity classes.  Scaling of halfspace mass gives
\(\tau_{\widetilde p_s}=\tau_{r_s}/R_s\).  Applying (2) to this normalized globally log-concave table, and using that
\(x\mapsto x\log(16x)\) is increasing for \(x\geq1\), gives the exact rescaled inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)
&=R_s^2M_1(\widetilde p_s)\\
&\geq
\frac{R_s^2}{4b_s\log(16b_s)}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2\\
&\geq
\frac{R_s^2}{4b\log(16b)}
+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
This is the normalization step: the depth contributes one factor \(R_s\), the sampling mass contributes the other, and the atomic term rescales exactly to \(\frac12\sum r_s^2\).

Combining (21), (22), and (23) proves the first inequality in (6):
\[
G(s)
\geq\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)\tau_{r_s}(\mathbf z)
-\frac12\sum_{\mathbf z}r_s(\mathbf z)^2
\geq\frac{R_s^2}{4b\log(16b)}
=\frac{R_s^2}{2H_b}.
\tag{24}
\]
It is automatic when \(R_s=0\).

Because the table is finite, \(R_s\) is continuous and piecewise affine,
\(G\) is continuously differentiable, and \(G'=-R_s\).  From (24), wherever \(G>0\),
\[
\frac d{ds}\sqrt{G(s)}
=-\frac{R_s}{2\sqrt{G(s)}}
\geq-\sqrt{\frac{H_b}{2}}.
\tag{25}
\]
At zero, \(R_0=1\) and (24) give
\(G(0)\geq1/(2H_b)\).  Integrating (25) shows
\[
\sqrt{G(s)}\geq\frac{1-H_bs}{\sqrt{2H_b}}
\qquad(0\leq s<1/H_b).
\]
The right side is positive, so \(G\) cannot first vanish before \(1/H_b\); hence the integration is valid throughout that interval.  Squaring there and using nonnegativity afterwards proves the second inequality in (6).

To recover all moments, sample \(\mathbf Z\) with law \(p\), and conditional on \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf z\), let \(Y\) be uniform on
\([t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z},t_{\mathbf z}]\).  This interval is nonnegative because \(t_{\mathbf z}\geq p_{\mathbf z}\).  Directly from (4),
\[
\mathbb P(Y>s)=R_s.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(q=1\), (6) gives
\(\mathbb EY=G(0)\geq1/(2H_b)\).  For \(q>1\), Tonelli and (6) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EY^q
&=q(q-1)\int_0^\infty s^{q-2}G(s)\,ds\\
&\geq
\frac{q(q-1)}{2H_b}
\int_0^{1/H_b}s^{q-2}(1-H_bs)^2\,ds\\
&=\frac{H_b^{-q}}{q+1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
where
\(\int_0^1u^{q-2}(1-u)^2du=2/[(q-1)q(q+1)]\).

The atomic correction is retained exactly.  For \(t\geq p>0\) and
\(q\geq1\), put
\[
F_q(x):=x^q-\frac{x^{q+1}-(x-1)^{q+1}}{q+1}
\qquad(x\geq1).
\]
Then \(F_q(1)=q/(q+1)\), while
\[
F_q'(x)
=q\left(x^{q-1}-\int_{x-1}^xu^{q-1}\,du\right)\geq0.
\]
Consequently
\[
pt^q-\int_{t-p}^tu^q\,du
\geq\frac q{q+1}p^{q+1}.
\tag{28}
\]
Conditional uniformity gives
\(\mathbb EY^q=\sum_{\mathbf z}\int_{t_{\mathbf z}-p_{\mathbf z}}^{t_{\mathbf z}}u^qdu\).  Sum (28) and insert (27), including the separately proved \(q=1\) case, to obtain (7).

Since \(b\leq2^N\),
\[
H_b=2b\log(16b)
\leq2^{N+1}(N+4)\log2.
\tag{29}
\]
Putting \(q=3\) in (7) and using (29) gives (8).

It remains to compose the arithmetic theorem with the mixed-depth transfer.  Fix any real \(q>2\).  A log-affine probability law on a complete support
\(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^N\), as used in the dimension-local hypothesis of accepted fact `c5f6533ba1c11991`, has the globally log-concave extension
\(x\mapsto e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot x}\mathbf1_Q(x)\), up to its normalizing constant.  Thus (7) and (29), after dropping the nonnegative atomic term, prove that accepted hypothesis \((\mathrm H_{q,N}(\kappa_N(q)))\) holds with
\[
\kappa_N(q)
:=\frac{1}{(q+1)2^q[(N+4)\log2]^q},
\tag{30}
\]
because
\[
M_q(p)
\geq\frac{1}{q+1}
\left[2^{N+1}(N+4)\log2\right]^{-q}
=\kappa_N(q)2^{-qN}.
\]

We now begin the mixed-body composition.  If \(W=0\), both (9) and (10) are automatic for every point of the assumed nonempty \(S\).  Hence assume \(W>0\).  We record the exact epigraph notation to prevent a collision between potential and score.  For the actual positive fiber weights
\(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\), let
\(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\),
\(w_{\max}=\max_Dw_{\mathbf I}\), and
\[
\phi_{\mathbf I}:=\log\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\mathbf I}},
\qquad
B:=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}\phi_{\mathbf I}.
\tag{31}
\]
Here \(B\) is the maximum potential and is not a hitting score.  For every center \(\mathbf J\), every affinely independent
\(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and its positive barycentric coefficients \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\), put
\[
G_E:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}.
\]
Let \(m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) be the minimum full original weight
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}\) over sets \(F\subseteq D\) meeting every such \(E\) with \(G_E\leq L\), including the singleton at \(\mathbf J\) and every equality case.  The score is
\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
:=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\geq0}
\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\tag{32}
\]

Apply accepted fact `c5f6533ba1c11991` with arithmetic dimension
\(N=n+1\), coefficient (30), original mass \(W\), maximum potential (31), and score (32).  Its epigraph conclusion and actual-body consumer give
\[
\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)
\geq
\frac{\kappa_{n+1}(q)^{1/q}}{2C_q^{1/q}}\,2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)
\geq\frac16\mathcal B_{\log}^{\mathrm{hit}}(w),
\tag{33}
\]
where
\[
C_q:=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)^q\,dL.
\]
Since
\[
\kappa_{n+1}(q)^{1/q}
=\frac{1}{2(q+1)^{1/q}(n+5)\log2},
\]
(33) becomes
\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)
\geq
\frac{1}{24[(q+1)C_q]^{1/q}(n+5)\log2}
\,2^{-n}W
\qquad(q>2).
\tag{34}
\]

For \(q=3\), repeated elementary integration by parts gives
\(C_3=16\).  Equation (34) then has denominator
\(24(4\cdot16)^{1/3}=96\), proving (10).

For the stronger direct composition, let \(q\downarrow2\).  On
\(2<q\leq3\), the integrand defining \(C_q\) is dominated by
\(e^{-L}(1+L)^3\), so dominated convergence gives
\[
C_q\longrightarrow C_2
=\int_0^\infty e^{-L}(1+L)^2\,dL
=1+2+2=5.
\tag{35}
\]
Continuity of the remaining elementary factors therefore gives
\[
[(q+1)C_q]^{1/q}\longrightarrow\sqrt{15}.
\tag{36}
\]
Since (34) holds for every \(q>2\), its left side is at least the limit of the right sides, giving the numerical lower bound in (9).

Finally, the point realizing (9) exists.  Define the finite mixed-volume Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^{n+d}\) by
\[
\mu(A):=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\mathcal L^d\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in A\cap C\}.
\]
The set \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) is compact.  For a unit vector \(u\), the function
\[
F_u(\mathbf y):=\mu\{\mathbf x:u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)\geq0\}
\]
is upper semicontinuous: if \(\mathbf y_k\to\mathbf y\), then for every
\(\delta>0\), eventually
\(F_u(\mathbf y_k)\leq
\mu\{\mathbf x:u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)\geq-\delta\}\), and continuity from above as \(\delta\downarrow0\) gives
\(\limsup_kF_u(\mathbf y_k)\leq F_u(\mathbf y)\).  Translating the boundary of any containing halfspace to the queried point shows
\[
h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{\|u\|=1}F_u(\mathbf y).
\]
An infimum of upper semicontinuous functions is upper semicontinuous, so
\(h_S\) attains its maximum on compact \(S\).  Choose a maximizer after taking the scalar limit (36); it satisfies (9) simultaneously for every containing closed halfspace.
