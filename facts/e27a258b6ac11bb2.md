## Statement

Let $0<U,V,F<1$, $L=U+V-1>0$ and $0\le r\le F(1-F)$. Set $A=((U-F)^2+r)/U^2$ and $B=((V-1+F)^2+r)/V^2$. Then
$$
\bigl((1-V)\sqrt A+(1-U)\sqrt B\bigr)^2>L^2(A+B-1).
$$

## Proof

The gap is concave as a function of $r$. Its only nonaffine term is $2(1-U)(1-V)\sqrt{AB}$, and the second derivative of $\sqrt{(a+br)(c+dr)}$ where both factors are positive is $-(ad-bc)^2/(4[(a+br)(c+dr)]^{3/2})$. Continuity gives concavity through a zero endpoint. It suffices to prove positivity at both endpoints.

At $r=F(1-F)$ write $Z=(1-V)^2A+(1-U)^2B-L^2(A+B-1)$. Substituting $A=1-2F/U+F/U^2$ and $B=1-2(1-F)/V+(1-F)/V^2$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z&=-\frac{2(U-1)(V-1)(FU-FV+UV-U)}{UV},\\
Z^2-4(1-U)^2(1-V)^2AB
&=-\frac{4F(1-F)(1-U)^2(1-V)^2L^2}{U^2V^2}<0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $Z+2(1-U)(1-V)\sqrt{AB}>0$.

At $r=0$ write $z_1=(U-F)/U$, $z_2=(V-1+F)/V$. Their weighted sum $Uz_1+Vz_2=L$ is positive. If both are nonnegative, then $1=U(1-z_1)+V(1-z_2)<2-z_1-z_2$ since $U,V<1$, $z_1,z_2\le1$, and the nonnegative summands are not both zero. Hence $z_1^2+z_2^2<1$, proving the desired strict inequality. Otherwise exchange $U,V$ and replace $F$ by $1-F$ if necessary so that $z_1=-t<0$ and $z_2=b>0$. Then $L/V\le b\le1$ and $t=(Vb-L)/U$. The affine expression $(1-V)t+(1-U)b-Lt$ is $(1-U)L/V>0$ at $b=L/V$ and $2(1-U)(1-V)/U>0$ at $b=1$. Thus the left square root exceeds $Lt$, and $t^2+b^2-1\le t^2$ proves the desired strict inequality. Concavity now proves it for the entire interval.
