## Statement

For $d\ge1$ put $\gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$ and $\kappa=(1-\gamma)^{1/d}$. For every $a,b>0$, there are homothetic $d$-simplex endpoints of volume roots $a,b$, and their exact Minkowski midpoint $K$ of root $(a+b)/2$, with one common closed cut capturing all of the first endpoint and none of the second, with the two zero-volume endpoint sections empty. The same cut takes a canonical centroid cap of fraction $\gamma$ on $K$. The mean of the endpoint residual roots minus the residual root at $K$ is $(b-\kappa(a+b))/2$, positive exactly when $b/(a+b)>\kappa$. At fixed $b=1$ as $a\downarrow0$, it tends to $(1-\kappa)/2>0$ although the fully captured endpoint volume $a^d$ tends to zero. Thus no universal local correction depending only on that volume and tending to zero with it bounds this residual-root loss even on the whole-body equality slice with a canonical source-centroid cut.

## Proof

Choose a unit-volume simplex $T$ with one vertex at height zero and the opposite facet at height one for a linear coordinate $t$. The centroid has height $q_*=d/(d+1)$ and the prefix at height $q\in[0,1]$ is a homothetic simplex of volume $q^d$. Choose $q_A>1$ large enough that $q_B=((a+b)q_*-a q_A)/b<0$. If $e_t$ is a vector of height one, set $A=a(T-q_A e_t)$ and $B=b(T-q_B e_t)$. Their midpoint is $K=((a+b)/2)(T-q_* e_t)$. For the common cut $t\le0$, the first endpoint lies strictly inside the captured side and the second lies strictly outside. The cut takes exactly a canonical vertex cap through the centroid of $K$, of fraction $q_*^d=\gamma$. These are actual integer fibers at $-1,0,1$ by taking the hull of the two endpoint lifts. The residual roots of the endpoints are $0,b$, and the source residual root is $\kappa(a+b)/2$. Taking their difference gives the formula and the claimed limit.
