## Statement

For a symmetric matrix $K\succeq0$, set $b=B^{\mathsf T}v$ and $q(w)=v^{\mathsf T}Av+2b^{\mathsf T}w+w^{\mathsf T}Kw$. The infimum of $q$ is finite exactly when $b\in\operatorname{ran}K$. Then it equals $v^{\mathsf T}Av-b^{\mathsf T}K^\dagger b$ with minimizers $-K^\dagger b+\ker K$, where $\dagger$ denotes the Moore–Penrose inverse. This need not give the Hessian of a truly optimized cap or of a general optimized smooth function.

If $K\succ0$ and $Rw=d(v)$ is feasible, the minimum of the same quadratic under that constraint is
$$
v^{\mathsf T}Av-b^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}b+e^{\mathsf T}(RK^{-1}R^{\mathsf T})^\dagger e,
\qquad e=d(v)+RK^{-1}b.
$$
The constraint rows may be redundant.

## Proof

Decompose $w$ and $b$ orthogonally into $\operatorname{ran}K$ and $\ker K$. If the kernel component of $b$ is nonzero, send the kernel component of $w$ along its negative ray; the objective tends to $-\infty$. Otherwise complete the square on $\operatorname{ran}K$, where $K$ is positive definite, to obtain the stated value and affine family of minimizers.

For the distinction from an optimized value, take $\Phi(h,\theta)=\theta^4+h\theta^2$. At $(0,0)$ all three Hessian blocks vanish, and $\theta=0$ is an isolated normal minimum at $h=0$. Minimization over $\theta$ is equivalent to minimization of $s^2+hs$ over $s=\theta^2\ge0$. The value is zero for $h\ge0$ and $-h^2/4$ for $h<0$, with minimizing $\theta$ of size $\sqrt{-h/2}$ in the latter case. The optimized value has no second derivative at zero.

For $K\succ0$, substitute $p=K^{1/2}(w+K^{-1}b)$. The objective is the unconstrained minimum plus $\lVert p\rVert^2$, and the constraint is $Mp=e$ with $M=RK^{-1/2}$. Feasibility gives $e\in\operatorname{ran}M=\operatorname{ran}(MM^{\mathsf T})$. The minimum-norm solution is $p_0=M^{\mathsf T}(MM^{\mathsf T})^\dagger e$: it solves the equation and is perpendicular to $\ker M$, so adding any nonzero kernel vector increases its squared norm. Its squared norm is $e^{\mathsf T}(MM^{\mathsf T})^\dagger e$, as claimed.
