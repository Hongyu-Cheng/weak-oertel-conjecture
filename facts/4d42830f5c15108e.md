## Statement

For a nonempty finite complete convex lattice support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\), put \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\), so \(P\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\). For \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^N\), use the canonical original law \(p_{\mathbf z}=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}/W\), where \(W=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\). Its actual normalized closed-halfspace depth is \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)=\min_{H\ni\mathbf z}p(D\cap H)\), and \(M_q(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^q\). The minimum is over all containing closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\). Define
\[
G_k(p)=\mathbb P\!\left(\frac{\mathbf X_1+\cdots+\mathbf X_k}{k}\in\mathbb Z^N\right),
\qquad \mathbf X_1,\ldots,\mathbf X_k\text{ independent with law }p.
\]
Every integral average in this definition belongs to \(D\) by completeness and convexity; all such centers are retained.

For each \(k\in\{4,8\}\), there is no finite constant \(C_k\), independent of \(N,D,\boldsymbol\ell\), such that \(G_k(p)\le C_k M_{k-1}(p)\) for all these laws. Explicitly, for integers \(m\ge1\), set \(K=2^m\), \(N=m+1\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_m&=\left\{(t,\mathbf x):-1\le t\le1,\quad
\frac{t-1}{2}\le x_i\le\frac{t+1}{2}\ (1\le i\le m)\right\},\\
D_m&=(\{1\}\times\{0,1\}^m)\cup\{\mathbf0\}
\cup(\{-1\}\times\{0,-1\}^m),\\
B&=K^{(k-2)/(2k-1)},\qquad
\boldsymbol\ell=(\log B,0,\ldots,0),\qquad
W=1+K(B+B^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Use \(D=D_m\), \(P=P_m\), and \(p_{(t,\mathbf x)}=B^t/W\). Then, as \(m\to\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{(k-1)N}M_{k-1}(p)&\longrightarrow2^k,\\
\frac{G_4(p)}{M_3(p)}&\sim3K^{\log_2 6-15/7}
\quad(k=4),\\
\frac{G_8(p)}{M_7(p)}&\sim35K^{\log_2 70-21/5}
\quad(k=8).
\end{aligned}
\]
Both displayed ratio exponents are positive. In particular \(2^{(k-1)N}G_k(p)\to\infty\), although the corresponding normalized actual moment stays bounded. This does not give a counterexample to a positive dimension-uniform lower bound for \(2^{(k-1)N}M_{k-1}\).

## Proof

The support family is the one in accepted fact `80ecb88cb7667aab`, with its Boolean dimension \(m\), arithmetic dimension \(N=m+1\), and \(K=2^m\) unchanged. Its partition function \(Z\), for the general weights \(B^t\) appearing in that fact's proof, is exactly the present \(W\). Its original-coordinate parameter is the present \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\log B,0,\ldots,0)\). Here \(B\) is the explicitly displayed parameter, rather than that fact's special critical parameter \(B_K\); no assertion of local minimality is needed.

We verify the complete support and every actual depth directly. The invertible real transformation \((t,\mathbf x)\mapsto(t,2\mathbf x-t\mathbf1)\) maps \(P_m\) onto \([-1,1]^{m+1}\). Its vertices correspond precisely to the nonzero points displayed in \(D_m\), so \(P_m=\operatorname{conv}D_m\) is full-dimensional. An integer point of \(P_m\) has \(t=-1,0,1\). At these three heights, its integer sections are respectively \(\{0,-1\}^m\), \(\{\mathbf0\}\), and \(\{0,1\}^m\). Thus \(P_m\cap\mathbb Z^{m+1}=D_m\), without any missing lattice point.

Each nonzero supported point is an exposed vertex of \(P_m\). A supporting closed halfspace through that vertex can therefore have precisely that singleton as its support trace. Every containing closed halfspace must retain the vertex's own atom. Consequently \(\tau_p(1,\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=B/W\) and \(\tau_p(-1,-\boldsymbol\varepsilon)=B^{-1}/W\) for all \(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m\).

The \(K\) nonzero antipodal pairs are \(\{(1,\boldsymbol\varepsilon),(-1,-\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\}\). Every closed halfspace containing the origin contains at least one endpoint of each pair. It also contains the atom at the origin. Since \(B\ge1\), its mass is at least \((1+K/B)/W\). The closed halfspace \(\{t\le0\}\) attains this value, including the center atom. Hence \(\tau_p(\mathbf0)=(1+K/B)/W\). These are actual minimum depths over all containing closed halfspaces, not a reflected or counting substitute. Summing over all centers gives the exact moment formula
\[
M_{k-1}(p)
=\frac{K(B^k+B^{-k})+(1+K/B)^{k-1}}{W^k}.
\tag{1}
\]

We next count every ordered tuple with integral average. Its average belongs to \(D_m\), and the average of its first coordinates must be \(-1,0,1\). If this average is \(1\), all \(k\) observations are in the top layer. Each Boolean coordinate has integer average only if its \(k\) bits are all identical. Thus these tuples are exactly the \(K\) top diagonal tuples, each of original probability \(B^k/W^k\). The bottom case supplies the \(K\) bottom diagonal tuples of probability \(B^{-k}/W^k\).

An average with first coordinate zero must be the origin, because that is the only supported integer point at height zero. Suppose such a tuple has \(a\) top words, \(a\) bottom words, and \(k-2a\) origins, where \(0\le a\le k/2\). There are \(k!/(a!^2(k-2a)!)\) choices of their ordered positions. In one Boolean coordinate, the number of choices with zero sum is
\(\sum_{j=0}^a\binom aj^2=\binom{2a}a\): the top bits and the absolute values of the bottom bits must have the same number of ones. This identity follows by choosing \(a\) objects from two groups of \(a\) and conditioning on the number chosen from the first group. The \(m\) coordinates are independent counting choices, so their number is \(\binom{2a}a^m\). The product of the original weights of every such tuple is exactly \(B^aB^{-a}/W^k=W^{-k}\). Therefore the all-center tuple probability is exactly
\[
G_k(p)
=\frac{K(B^k+B^{-k})+
\displaystyle\sum_{a=0}^{k/2}
\frac{k!}{a!^2(k-2a)!}\binom{2a}a^m}{W^k}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular the zero-center polynomial in (2) is \(1+12K+6\cdot6^m\) for \(k=4\), and \(1+56K+420\cdot6^m+560\cdot20^m+70\cdot70^m\) for \(k=8\). No tuple has been reweighted, transported, or omitted from (2).

Our choice of \(B\) gives the exact equality \(B^{2k-1}=K^{k-2}\). Since \(0<(k-2)/(2k-1)<1\), both \(B\) and \(K/B\) tend to infinity, and \(W/(KB)=1+B^{-2}+(KB)^{-1}\to1\). The central and top leading terms of (1) are balanced exactly:
\[
\left(\frac KB\right)^{k-1}=KB^k,
\qquad
\frac{K(B^k+B^{-k})+(1+K/B)^{k-1}}{KB^k}\longrightarrow2.
\]
It follows that \(M_{k-1}(p)\sim2K^{1-k}\). Since \(2^{(k-1)N}=2^{k-1}K^{k-1}\), this proves the first limit in the statement.

Put \(c_k=\binom{k}{k/2}\), so \(c_4=6\) and \(c_8=70\). The largest exponential counting term in the finite sum (2) is \(c_k^{m+1}\); every term with \(a<k/2\) has a strictly smaller base \(\binom{2a}a<c_k\). The vertex numerator has size \(KB^k=K^{1+k(k-2)/(2k-1)}\). For \(k=4\), the exponent difference is \(\log_2 6-15/7>0\), because \(6^7=279936>32768=2^{15}\). For \(k=8\), it is \(\log_2 70-21/5>0\), because \(70^5=1680700000>2097152=2^{21}\). Hence in both cases the zero-center term strictly dominates the vertex terms, and (2) gives
\[
G_k(p)\sim\frac{c_k^{m+1}}{(KB)^k},
\qquad
\frac{G_k(p)}{M_{k-1}(p)}
\sim\frac{c_k}{2}
K^{\log_2 c_k-1-k(k-2)/(2k-1)}.
\]
Substituting \(k=4,8\) gives the two asserted divergent ratios. Thus for every proposed finite \(C_k\), one member of the complete-support sequence violates \(G_k(p)\le C_kM_{k-1}(p)\). The weights can even be rational along a subsequence: take \(m\) divisible by \(7\) for \(k=4\), or by \(5\) for \(k=8\), so \(B\) is an integer power of two.

The contradiction concerns the attempted consumer of the entire original integral-average reservoir. It forces that consumer to lose an unbounded fraction of that reservoir along this sequence. Because the reservoir's excess over \(2^{-(k-1)N}\) is itself unbounded, a compensation using that excess could still recover the desired scale. Equations (1)-(2) establish neither a failure nor a proof of such compensation, and neither a failure nor a proof of the requested fixed-moment theorem.
