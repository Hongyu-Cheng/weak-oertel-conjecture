## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, and $J\subset\mathbb R^d$ a full-dimensional compact convex set with $0\in\operatorname{int}J$. Write $K_z=\{x:(z,x)\in C\}$. On a convex base domain let $a$ be an affine map with $a(z)\in K_z$ everywhere. Then $r(z)=\max\{t\ge0:a(z)+tJ\subset K_z\}$ exists and is concave on that domain.

## Proof

For fixed $z$, radius zero is feasible, and the radii form a closed set by closedness of $K_z$. They are bounded because $J$ contains a ball of positive radius about zero and $C$ is bounded. For any two bases $z,w$ and $\lambda\in[0,1]$, convexity of $C$ gives
$$
(1-\lambda)(a(z)+r(z)J)+\lambda(a(w)+r(w)J)
\subseteq K_{(1-\lambda)z+\lambda w}.
$$
The set on the left equals $a((1-\lambda)z+\lambda w)+((1-\lambda)r(z)+\lambda r(w))J$ by affineness and convexity of $J$, including when the radius is zero. Therefore the corresponding interpolated radius is feasible, proving concavity.
