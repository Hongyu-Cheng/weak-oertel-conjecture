## Statement

Let $C$ be the convex hull of the lifts of $K_-=[-21/80,-13/80]$, $K_0=[-1/2,1/2]$ and $K_+=[-59/80,5/16]$ at integer bases $-1,0,1$. These are its exact integer fibers, with mixed length $M=43/20$. For the base marginal, $j=0$ is the unique integer point of raw closed-halfspace depth at least $M/2$, and its marginal depth is $D_0=11/10$.

For $t>0$, let $M_t(0)$ consist of the continuous centroids of all submeasures dominated by the mixed-length measure, of mass $t$ and with base centroid zero. The feasible masses are $0<t\le6/5$. With $A=t(1-t)/2$ and $I_t=[\max(0,(t-1)/2),\min(1/10,t/2)]$, put
$$
F_+(t)=\max_{\ell\in I_t}\{(2t-17/20)\ell-3\ell^2\},\qquad
F_-(t)=\max_{\ell\in I_t}\{2t\ell-3\ell^2\}.
$$
Then $M_t(0)=[-(A+F_-(t))/t,(A+F_+(t))/t]$. Its interval centroid $y(t)$ equals
$$
\begin{cases}
-t/6,&0<t\le3/10,\\
-1/10+3/(200t),&3/10\le t\le17/40,\\
t/6-29/120+433/(9600t),&17/40\le t\le29/40,\\
-17/(400t),&29/40\le t\le6/5.
\end{cases}
$$
For every $y\in[-17/80,0]$, including every $y(t)$, the raw mixed depth at $(0,y)$ is $3/5+y$. Consequently $y(t)$ fails the sharp budget $M/4$ exactly for $2/5<t<(43+3\sqrt{13})/80$, with equality at the two endpoints. In particular at the prescribed $t=D_0/2=11/20$, the interval is $[-163/440,619/2640]$ and its centroid is $-359/5280$. The closed ambient cap $x\le -359/5280-(707/1056)z$ attains this point's raw depth $2809/5280$, below $M/4$ by $29/5280$.

## Proof

The two endpoint intervals have common center $s=-17/80$ and lengths $1/10,21/20$; the middle length is one. Their Minkowski midpoint is $[-1/2,3/40]\subset K_0$. At base zero the coefficients on the two endpoints agree, so the hull has exact middle fiber $K_0$; at either extreme base only the corresponding interval contributes. The projection is $[-1,1]$ and a segment at one base and a point at another span a triangle of positive area. Thus this is an actual convex body, and its length measure is the sum of the three interval measures. The lesser of the two closed marginal rays through each base gives the marginal depth. The three depths are $1/10,11/10,21/20$, and the depth at every other integer is zero. Their comparisons with $M/2=43/40$ give the unique eligible base and prescribed mass.

A balanced source has equal endpoint masses $\ell$ and middle mass $t-2\ell$. The interval length constraints are precisely $\ell\in I_t$, which is nonempty exactly for $0<t\le6/5$. Each such allocation is attainable by interval submeasures. On an interval of length $w$ and center $g$, a dominated submeasure of mass $m$ has first moment between $mg-m(w-m)/2$ and $mg+m(w-m)/2$. For the upper extremum fill the top interval of length $m$. Subtracting its density from any competing density gives a signed density of zero mass, nonnegative below the fill threshold and nonpositive above it. Integrating the coordinate minus that threshold against the difference is nonpositive. Reflection gives the lower extremum.

For a fixed $\ell$, summing those bounds gives a moment interval centered at $2s\ell=-17\ell/40$ with radius
$$
\tfrac12\{t(1-t)+(4t-17/20)\ell-6\ell^2\}.
$$
Maximizing the upper endpoint and the negative of the lower endpoint gives $A+F_+(t)$ and $A+F_-(t)$, respectively. They are attained by compactness of $I_t$ and the fill construction. The fixed-mass balanced dominated submeasures form a convex set, so all moments between these extrema are attained. Division by $t$ proves the interval formula. The interval has positive length for every feasible $t$: when $0<t<1$, the allocation $\ell=0$ allows a proper middle fill; when $t=1$, choose $\ell=1/20$; and when $1<t\le6/5$, choose $\ell=1/10$, a positive proper fill in $K_+$. Its centroid is its midpoint.

For $F_-$ the optimal $\ell$ is $t/3$ up to $t=3/10$ and $1/10$ thereafter. For $F_+$ it is zero up to $17/40$, $(2t-17/20)/6$ up to $29/40$, and $1/10$ thereafter. These follow by clipping the stationary points to $I_t$. In particular the lower endpoint of $I_t$ is positive only for $t>1$, when the two optimizers are already $1/10$. Substitution in $y(t)=(F_+(t)-F_-(t))/(2t)$ proves the four branches. Also $0\le F_--F_+\le(17/20)\max I_t\le17t/40$, so $y(t)\in[-17/80,0]\subset K_0$.

To compute the actual ambient depth use fact c680a307d3f26cec, with lengths $a=1/10$, $b=21/20$, $U=1$. Translate the continuous coordinate by $17/80$. This preserves all lengths, the mixed lattice and ambient halfspaces, centers the endpoints at zero, and gives middle center $s'=17/80=U/2-(a+b)/4$, satisfying the compatibility conditions with equality. A point $(0,y)$ becomes $(0,x')$ with $x'=y+17/80$. When $y\in[-17/80,0]$, $0\le x'\le17/80$ and $(a+b)/2\mp2x'\ge 3/20>a$, so both terms $g(x'),g(-x')$ of the accepted formula equal $1/10$. The two nonvertical orientation minima are $3/5-y$ and $3/5+y$. The two vertical through-base masses are $11/10$ and $41/20$, both larger. Thus the raw depth is $3/5+y$.

The budget $M/4$ is met if and only if $y(t)\ge-1/16$. The first branch gives $y(t)\ge-1/20$. The second branch violates the budget exactly for $t>2/5$. In the third branch the violation is equivalent to $1600t^2-1720t+433<0$, with roots $(43\pm3\sqrt{13})/80$. Only the larger root is in this branch: the smaller is below $17/40$ and the larger is strictly between $17/40$ and $29/40$. The fourth branch gives $y(t)\ge-17/290>-1/16$. This proves the exact range and its endpoint equalities.

At $t=11/20$ one has $\ell_+=1/24$, $\ell_-=1/10$, $F_+=1/192$, $F_-=2/25$, and $A=99/800$. These give the stated interval and midpoint. For the displayed closed cap the thresholds at $-1,0,1$ are $397/660$, $-359/5280$, and $-59/80$. Its left trace is full, its right trace is a singleton of length zero, and its middle length is $1/2-359/5280$. The sum is $2809/5280$, compared with $M/4=2838/5280$. For comparison the same exact formula gives depth $7/12$ at the selected $t=1/10$ and $3/5$ at $(0,0)$, each above $M/4$.
