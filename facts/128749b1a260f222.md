## Statement

Let \(m,h,L\ge1\) be integers with \(K:=\lfloor L/h\rfloor\ge2\), and let \(\beta,\theta\in\mathbb R\) be finite. On the original support
\[
 E_{L,h,m}=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+1}:
                         \sum_{i=1}^m x_i+hy\le L\right\},
 \qquad
 R(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{\exp(\beta\sum_i x_i+\theta y)}
     {\sum_{(\mathbf z,v)\in E_{L,h,m}}
                            \exp(\beta\sum_i z_i+\theta v)},
\]
suppose the full mean is \((j\mathbf1,1)\), where \(j\ge1\) is an integer. Write \(X_\Sigma=\sum_iX_i\), \(p_k=\Pr_R(Y=k)\), \(B_k=L-hk\), \(\mu_k=\mathbb E_R[X_\Sigma\mid Y=k]\), and \(v_1=\operatorname{Var}_R(X_\Sigma\mid Y=1)\). Put \(s=\mu_1-\mu_0\) and
\[
 V=\mu_Y-mj-s(Y-1),
 \qquad
 R_k=\mu_k+(k-1)\mu_0-k\mu_1
                    \quad(0\le k\le K).
\]
Then \(v_1\ge1/20\), \(V=R_Y-\mathbb E_RR_Y\), and
\[
                  \mathbb E_RV^4\le2^{55}v_1^2.
\]
The bound holds for every \(\beta\ne0\). It also holds at \(\beta=0\), where \(V=0\) identically. The notation \(R\), \(E_{L,h,m}\), \(X_\Sigma\), \(\mu_k\), \(v_1\), and the partition function below has exactly the same meaning as in accepted fact `43cb56218b18ec82`, with its height restriction removed. The subscripted quantity \(R_k\) is a drift curvature, not a probability mass. The auxiliary scalar variable called \(V\) in the scalar estimate in the proof of that predecessor is called \(W\) here; the present \(V\) always denotes the displayed centered drift.

## Proof

For a nonnegative integer \(B\) and real \(u\), use the original transverse partition function
\[
 Z_m(B,u)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\\
                                       \sum_i x_i\le B}}
                                       \exp(u\sum_i x_i).
\]
There are \(\binom{a+m-1}{m-1}\) transverse vectors with total \(a\): insert \(m-1\) separators among \(a\) units, allowing empty parts. Consequently the conditional total in a slice of budget \(B\), at transverse tilt \(u\), has weights
\(w_a(u)=\binom{a+m-1}{m-1}e^{ua}\), for \(0\le a\le B\), and normalization \(Z_m(B,u)\). Its successive ratios \(w_{a+1}(u)/w_a(u)=e^u(a+m)/(a+1)\) are nonincreasing. Thus every conditional total law is log-concave with contiguous positive integer support. Every height \(0,\ldots,K\) also has positive source probability.

First establish the variance lower bound for all heights. Put \(b=B_1=L-h\) and \(r=e^\beta\). The source slack \(L-X_\Sigma-hY\) is nonnegative and is strictly positive at the positive-probability origin. Its expectation is therefore a positive integer,
\(b-mj\ge1\). In particular \(b\ge m+1\). Since \(L\ge2h\), also \(L=b+h\le2b\), so every original slice budget is at most \(2b\). Let \(q_a=w_a(\beta)/Z_m(b,\beta)\) and \(q_*:=\max_{0\le a\le b}q_a\).

If \(r<1\), the weights on all transverse vectors, normalized by \((1-r)^m\), give independent geometric coordinates of mean \(r/(1-r)\). Their total mean is \(mr/(1-r)\). Conditioning that total to be at most any budget cannot increase its mean, because every value on that event is no greater than every value on its complement. Each original slice is exactly such a conditioning. Averaging its mean over the original heights gives \(mj\le mr/(1-r)\), so \(r\ge j/(j+1)\ge1/2\).

Every possible maximum atom of the middle-slice law then has an adjacent atom of at least half its mass. At \(a=0\) the next-atom ratio is \(mr\ge1/2\). At \(a=b\) the previous-atom ratio is \(b/[r(b+m-1)]\ge b/(b+m-1)\ge1/2\). For \(1\le a\le b-1\), the product of its two adjacent ratios is
\[
 \frac{q_{a-1}q_{a+1}}{q_a^2}
       =\frac{a(a+m)}{(a+1)(a+m-1)}\ge\frac12,
\]
because twice the numerator minus the denominator is \(a^2+m(a-1)+1\ge0\). At least one adjacent ratio is at least \(1/\sqrt2\), hence at least \(1/2\). The total mass is therefore at least \(3q_*/2\), proving \(q_*\le2/3\).

If \(r\ge1\), the weights are nondecreasing, so \(q_*=q_b\). Suppose \(q_b>9/10\). Its previous-atom ratio would satisfy \(\eta:=b/[r(b+m-1)]=q_{b-1}/q_b<1/9\). For any positive original budget \(B\le L\le2b\), monotonicity of \(B/(B+m-1)\) gives
\[
 \frac{B}{r(B+m-1)}
       \le\frac{2b}{r(2b+m-1)}
       \le2\eta<\frac29.
\]
The monotonicity includes \(m=1\), when the ratio without \(r\) is constant. In that slice, let \(H_d=w_{B-d}(\beta)/w_B(\beta)\), for \(0\le d\le B\). Then \(H_0=1\) and \(H_d/H_{d-1}=(B-d+1)/[r(B-d+m)]\le B/[r(B+m-1)]<2/9\). Thus \(H_d\le(2/9)^d\), and the conditional expected slack satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_R[B-X_\Sigma\mid B]
       =\frac{\sum_{d=0}^BdH_d}{\sum_{d=0}^BH_d}
       \le\sum_{d\ge0}d(2/9)^d
       =\frac{18}{49}<1.
\]
For budget zero the slack is zero. Averaging over all original heights contradicts \(b-mj\ge1\). Hence \(q_*\le9/10\) for \(r\ge1\) as well. For an independent copy \(S'\) of the middle-slice total \(S\), integrality now gives
\[
 2v_1=\mathbb E(S-S')^2
      \ge\Pr(S\ne S')
      =1-\sum_aq_a^2\ge1-q_*\ge\frac1{10}.
\]
This proves \(v_1\ge1/20\). Write \(\sigma=\sqrt{v_1}\).

We use the following scalar estimate established in the proof of accepted fact `43cb56218b18ec82`: if \(W\) is a finite log-concave integer law with contiguous positive support, mean \(\mu\), and variance \(\sigma^2\ge1/20\), then its exponential tilt by any \(t\) satisfying \(|t|\sigma\le1/32\) has variance at most \(16384\sigma^2\). This scalar assertion in that proof is established before its application to the three-height partitions and has no height or source-mean hypothesis. In particular it applies to the middle-slice total just considered. Changing its transverse tilt from \(\beta\) to \(\beta+t\) is precisely the scalar exponential tilt by \(t\). If \(v_{B_1}(u)\) denotes the variance at budget \(B_1\) and tilt \(u\), we obtain
\[
 v_{B_1}(\beta+t)\le16384v_1
                    \quad\text{for }|t|\le\frac1{32\sigma}.
\]

We next prove the needed partition curvature and height-tail statements directly on the original budgets. For every real \(u\), the sequence \(B\mapsto Z_m(B,u)\) is log-concave. Indeed, for \(B\ge1\), the nonincreasing weight ratios give \(w_{B+1}(u)w_{a-1}(u)\le w_B(u)w_a(u)\) for \(1\le a\le B\). Summing these inequalities yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w_{B+1}(u)Z_m(B-1,u)
     &\le w_B(u)\sum_{a=1}^Bw_a(u)
       <w_B(u)Z_m(B,u),\\
 Z_m(B,u)^2-Z_m(B-1,u)Z_m(B+1,u)
     &=w_B(u)Z_m(B,u)-w_{B+1}(u)Z_m(B-1,u)>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence the successive differences of \(\log Z_m(B,u)\) are nonincreasing. Comparing sums of \(h\) consecutive differences proves that \(a_k(u):=\log Z_m(B_k,u)\), for \(0\le k\le K\), is a concave sequence in \(k\). Its successive differences therefore satisfy \(a_k(u)-a_0(u)\le k[a_1(u)-a_0(u)]\) for \(k\ge2\). Consequently
\[
 D_k(u):=k\log Z_m(B_1,u)
             -(k-1)\log Z_m(B_0,u)-\log Z_m(B_k,u)\ge0
                     \quad(2\le k\le K,\ u\in\mathbb R).
\]
For any budget \(B\), differentiation of the positive finite sums gives \((\log Z_m(B,u))'=\mu_B(u)\) and \((\log Z_m(B,u))''=v_B(u)\), where these are the conditional total mean and variance at that budget and tilt. Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_k'(\beta)&=-R_k,\\
 D_k''(u)&=k v_{B_1}(u)-(k-1)v_{B_0}(u)-v_{B_k}(u)
                                      \le k v_{B_1}(u).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Throughout \(|t|\le1/(32\sigma)\), the scalar tilt estimate bounds the last expression at \(u=\beta+t\) by \(16384k\sigma^2\). If \(D_k'(\beta)\ne0\), choose \(t=-\operatorname{sgn}(D_k'(\beta))/(32\sigma)\). Taylor's formula with its integral remainder, applied along the segment between \(\beta\) and \(\beta+t\), gives
\[
 0\le D_k(\beta+t)
       \le D_k(\beta)-\frac{|D_k'(\beta)|}{32\sigma}
                     +\frac{16384k\sigma^2t^2}{2}
       =D_k(\beta)-\frac{|R_k|}{32\sigma}+8k.
\]
The same conclusion is immediate if the derivative vanishes. Writing \(D_k=D_k(\beta)\), we have proved
\( |R_k|\le32\sigma(D_k+8k)\) for every \(2\le k\le K\).

The original height masses are
\(p_k=e^{\theta k}Z_m(B_k,\beta)/\sum_{a=0}^Ke^{\theta a}Z_m(B_a,\beta)\).
The source mean \(\mathbb E_RY=1\) gives the exact identity \(p_0=\sum_{k=2}^K(k-1)p_k\), so \(p_0\ge(k-1)p_k\) for each \(k\ge2\). Cancellation of the height tilt and normalization yields the exact partition ratio
\[
 \frac{p_0^{k-1}p_k}{p_1^k}=e^{-D_k}.
\]
All masses are positive. Taking the positive \(k\)-th root after using \(p_0\ge(k-1)p_k\) gives
\[
 p_k\le p_1(k-1)^{-(k-1)/k}e^{-D_k/k}
                        \le e^{-D_k/k}.
\]
Here \(k\) divides \(D_k\) inside the exponential. This exponent is the one required for all-height absorption.

For \(d\ge0\), the logarithmic derivative of \((d+8)^4e^{-d/2}\) is \(4/(d+8)-1/2\le0\). Therefore \((d+8)^4e^{-d/2}\le8^4\). Using the preceding probability bound on one square-root factor of \(p_k\), and putting \(d=D_k/k\), proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_kR_k^4
    &\le32^4v_1^2\,p_k(D_k+8k)^4\\
    &\le32^4v_1^2\sqrt{p_k}\,e^{-D_k/(2k)}(D_k+8k)^4\\
    &\le32^4\,8^4 k^4\sqrt{p_k}\,v_1^2
      =2^{32}k^4\sqrt{p_k}\,v_1^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

It remains to sum the height factor uniformly. The partition concavity just proved and the geometric multiplier \(e^{\theta k}\) make \(p_k\) a log-concave law on its contiguous positive support \(\{0,\ldots,K\}\). Extend its masses by zero, and put \(Q(k)=\Pr_R(Y\ge k)\). This survival sequence is log-concave: for \(1\le k\le K\), nonincreasing mass ratios give \(p_kp_a\ge p_{k-1}p_{a+1}\) for every \(a\ge k\). Summing proves
\[
 Q(k)^2-Q(k-1)Q(k+1)
       =p_kQ(k)-p_{k-1}Q(k+1)\ge0.
\]
At indices outside this range the survival inequalities follow from the constant-one or constant-zero tails. Since \(Q(0)=1\) and \(\mathbb E_RY=1\), Markov's inequality gives \(Q(2)\le1/2\); it is positive because \(K\ge2\). The successive differences of \(\log Q\) are nonincreasing as long as the tails are positive. Their first \(k\) average is consequently no greater than their first two average for every \(k\ge2\). Hence \(Q(k)\le Q(2)^{k/2}\le2^{-k/2}\), with the same inequality immediate for a zero tail. In particular
\(\sqrt{p_k}\le2^{-k/4}\) for \(k\ge2\).

The exact inequality \(2(6^4)>7^4\), namely \(2592>2401\), gives \(2^{-1/4}<6/7\). Also \(k^4\le k(k+1)(k+2)(k+3)=24\binom{k+3}{4}\) for \(k\ge1\). The generating identity \(\sum_{k\ge1}\binom{k+3}{4}z^k=z/(1-z)^5\), for \(0<z<1\), follows by multiplying five geometric series and counting their coefficient of degree \(k-1\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{k=2}^K k^4\sqrt{p_k}
    \le\sum_{k\ge1}k^4(6/7)^k
    \le24\frac{6/7}{(1-6/7)^5}
    <24\cdot7^5=403368<2^{19}.
\]
Since \(R_0=R_1=0\), the absorbed estimate now yields
\(\mathbb E_RR_Y^4\le2^{51}v_1^2\).

Finally \(R_k=\mu_k-\mu_0-ks\). The original two mean identities \(\mathbb E_R\mu_Y=mj\) and \(\mathbb E_RY=1\) give \(\mathbb E_RR_Y=mj-\mu_0-s=mj-\mu_1\). It follows directly that \(V=R_Y-\mathbb E_RR_Y\). Convexity of the fourth power gives \(|\mathbb E_RR_Y|^4\le\mathbb E_RR_Y^4\), and \(|a-b|^4\le8(|a|^4+|b|^4)\) follows by applying that same convexity to the midpoint. Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_RV^4
      \le8\left(\mathbb E_RR_Y^4+|\mathbb E_RR_Y|^4\right)
      \le16\mathbb E_RR_Y^4
      \le2^{55}v_1^2.
\]
All steps preceding this last inequality in fact allow \(\beta=0\). Its drift can additionally be identified exactly. At zero transverse tilt, adjoin the slack as a \((m+1)\)-st coordinate: conditional on budget \(B\), the resulting vector is uniform on the complete compositions of \(B\) into \(m+1\) parts. Permutation symmetry gives each coordinate mean \(B/(m+1)\), so \(\mu_k=m(L-hk)/(m+1)\) is affine in \(k\). Consequently \(R_k=0\) at every height, and \(V=0\) identically. This proves the zero-tilt assertion as well.
