## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), and let \(p=(p_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n}\) be a nonnegative, finitely supported probability table with nonempty positive support \(D\). Assume that the complete table, including all zeros, has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension \(u:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty)\): for \(0<t<1\), \(u((1-t)x+ty)\ge u(x)^{1-t}u(y)^t\), where a zero endpoint gives a zero right-hand side. Put \(P=\operatorname{conv}D\). No assumption on \(\dim P\) is made. Use the normalization of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc with \(w=p\) and \(W=1\), and set \(p_{\max}=\max_D p_{\mathbf I}\) and \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(p_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D\).

Use the feasible arrays, row masses, and entropies of accepted fact 464815b935df9890, with \(\mathcal F=\mathcal F(p)\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F
&=\left\{r\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{D\times D}:
\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D),\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0
\quad(\mathbf J\in D)\right\},\\
q_{\mathbf J}
&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I},\qquad
H(r)=-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}},\\
S(q)&=-\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}q_{\mathbf J}\log q_{\mathbf J},\qquad
G(r)=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J}),\qquad
I(r)=S(q)-H(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(0\log0=0\), and \(S\) denotes Shannon entropy for any finite probability law. For finite probability tables \(a,b\) on the same index set, write \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(a\|b)=\sum_{a_e>0}a_e\log(a_e/b_e)\), with value \(+\infty\) if a positive \(a_e\) has \(b_e=0\). The notation \(r^H\) below denotes the maximizer of \(H\) on all of \(\mathcal F\). It does not denote the variance-first entropy selector.

In the assigned archived response, \(\mathcal A(r)=H(r)\), \(\mathcal G(r)=G(r)\), \(\mathcal I(r)=I(r)\), and \(\mathsf H(q)=S(q)\). Its potential \(-\log p_{\mathbf I}\) equals \(\phi_{\mathbf I}-\log p_{\max}\); this constant shift leaves every energy difference and barycentric logarithmic cost unchanged. Its optimizer \(r^\star\) is \(r^H\) here, and its normalizing sum \(S_{\mathbf I}\) is denoted \(N(\mathbf I)\) below to distinguish it from Shannon entropy.

The maximum exists and is unique. One has \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). For \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(F_{\mathbf J}\) be the minimal face of \(P\) containing \(\mathbf J\), including \(P\) itself as a face, and put \(D_{\mathbf J}=D\cap F_{\mathbf J}\). Then

\[
r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}.
\]

In particular \(r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}>0\), \(q^H_{\mathbf J}>0\) for every \(\mathbf J\in D\), and every row has its prescribed mean \(\mathbf J\). There exist finite vectors \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\in\mathbb R^n\) such that, with the positive table \(f\) on \(D\) defined below,

\[
\begin{aligned}
N(\mathbf I)
&=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf K\in D\\\mathbf I\in F_{\mathbf K}}}
\exp\!\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)\bigr)
\ge1,\qquad
f_{\mathbf I}=\frac{p_{\mathbf I}}{N(\mathbf I)},\\
r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
&=
\begin{cases}
f_{\mathbf I}\exp\!\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\bigr),
&\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J},\\
0,&\mathbf I\notin D_{\mathbf J}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

The values \(\log N\) are convex-admissible on \(D\): if a finite positive coefficient vector \((\lambda_{\mathbf I})_{\mathbf I\in E}\) sums to one, \(E\subseteq D\), and \(\mathbf z=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\) is integral, then \(\mathbf z\in D\) and \(\log N(\mathbf z)\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log N(\mathbf I)\). For each row, the assignment-share potential
\(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=-\log(r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\) on \(D_{\mathbf J}\) is nonnegative and convex-admissible there. It has a finite convex extension to \(F_{\mathbf J}\). The complete tables \(f\), \(r^H_{\mathbf J,\cdot}\), and \(r^H_{\mathbf J,\cdot}/q^H_{\mathbf J}\), extended by zero at every other lattice point, have finite-valued globally log-concave extensions.

The target table has the explicit globally log-concave extension given by exponential capacity:

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathbf x}(f)
=\inf_{\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}f_{\mathbf I}
\exp\!\bigl(\mathbf t\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf x)\bigr),
\qquad
q^H_{\mathbf J}=\mathcal C_{\mathbf J}(f)\quad(\mathbf J\in D).
\]

This capacity is finite and strictly positive on all of \(P\), zero on \(\mathbb R^n\setminus P\), and globally log-concave. Thus it matches every lattice zero of the target law as well as every positive entry, including for boundary targets and lower affine rank.

For every \(r\in\mathcal F\), the following identities are finite and exact:

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r^H)&=H(r^H)-H(r),\\
G(r)+I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)&=S(p)-H(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\pi_{\mathbf b}=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D:\,\mathbf I\bmod2=\mathbf b}p_{\mathbf I}\) for \(\mathbf b\in\{0,1\}^n\), with zero parity masses retained, and define
\(r^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}p_{2\mathbf J-\mathbf I}/\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}\) for \(\mathbf J,\mathbf I\in D\), using the original zero extension of \(p\). Its denominators are positive. This array belongs to \(\mathcal F\), and the unrestricted optimizer satisfies the four-term parity budget

\[
G(r^H)+I(r^H)
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q^H\|p)
+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r^{\mathrm{par}}\|r^H)
=S(\pi)\le n\log2.
\]

Every term on the left is nonnegative. In particular
\(G(r^H)\le n\log2-S(q^H)+H(r^H)\), with coefficient one. This coefficient is optimal among strictly positive universal coefficients for this selector: for every \(0<c<1\), a complete actual-body table satisfies
\(G(r^H)>c(n\log2-S(q^H)+H(r^H))\).

For every nonempty \(E\subseteq D_{\mathbf J}\) and every positive coefficient vector summing to one with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\), the scale-invariant logarithmic costs of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc obey

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\le G_\lambda(p)
&:=\log p_{\mathbf J}-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}\\
&\le
G_\lambda(r^H_{\mathbf J,\cdot})
:=\log r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf J}
-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
=G_\lambda(r^H_{\mathbf J,\cdot}/q^H_{\mathbf J}).
\end{aligned}
\]

No affine-independence assumption on \(E\) is needed.

Finally, for \(n=1\), an integer \(m\ge2\), and the uniform complete table \(p_j=1/m\) on \(D=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), one has
\(\max_j q^H_j\le 2\log2/\log m\), hence \(\max_j q^H_j\to0\). These tables are the normalized integer fiber-volume tables of the actual convex bodies \(C_m=[0,m-1]\times[0,1]\subset\mathbb R^2\). Consequently there is no absolute \(a>0\) such that \(\max_{\mathbf J}q^H_{\mathbf J}\ge a\,2^{-n}\) for all the tables in this statement, even when restricted to actual convex-body profiles.

## Proof

First, repeated application of global log-concavity to any finite convex combination of points of \(D\) makes its extension strictly positive at that combination. Therefore any lattice point of \(P\) belongs to \(D\), so \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). At a lattice barycenter \(\mathbf z=\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\), the same inequality gives
\(\log p_{\mathbf z}\ge\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}\), equivalently convex-admissibility of \(\phi\). These are also the complete-table implications in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Allowing all integer targets would not enlarge the feasible set: a positive row has its mean in \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). Thus the displayed \(\mathcal F\) is the full original barycentric feasible set.

The diagonal array \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\mathbf1_{\{\mathbf J=\mathbf I\}}\) is feasible. All coordinates of every feasible array lie in \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\); hence \(\mathcal F\) is nonempty and compact. The continuous function \(H\) attains its maximum. For fixed \(p_{\mathbf I}>0\), \(-t\log(t/p_{\mathbf I})\) is strictly concave on \([0,p_{\mathbf I}]\). On the positive interval this follows from its second derivative \(-1/t\), and the strict inequality for a segment with a zero endpoint follows directly from \(-t\log t\). Summing over coordinates proves strict concavity in the entire array and uniqueness of \(r^H\).

We describe all feasible entries, without an interiority assumption on \(P\). A polytope's minimal face \(F_{\mathbf J}\) is exposed relative to its affine hull: one can sum outward normals of the finitely many relative facets containing \(\mathbf J\). Extending these normals to \(\mathbb R^n\) gives a vector \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf J}\) with
\(\mathbf v_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=0\) on \(F_{\mathbf J}\) and strictly negative value on \(P\setminus F_{\mathbf J}\). When \(F_{\mathbf J}=P\), take \(\mathbf v_{\mathbf J}=\mathbf0\). For any feasible row its moment equation gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\mathbf v_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=0\). Every summand is nonpositive, so all its positive entries lie in \(D_{\mathbf J}\).

Conversely, \(\mathbf J\) lies in \(\operatorname{relint}F_{\mathbf J}\), and \(D_{\mathbf J}\) generates this face. There is a probability vector positive on every point of \(D_{\mathbf J}\) with mean \(\mathbf J\). To see this explicitly, let \(\mathbf m\) be the average of all points of \(D_{\mathbf J}\). For sufficiently small \(0<\delta<1\), the point \((\mathbf J-\delta\mathbf m)/(1-\delta)\) is in \(F_{\mathbf J}\), by relative interiority; this is also true when the face is a singleton. Express that point as a convex combination of \(D_{\mathbf J}\), then mix its coefficients with weight \(\delta\) on the uniform distribution of \(D_{\mathbf J}\). The resulting coefficients \(\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) are all positive and have mean \(\mathbf J\).

Starting with the diagonal array, choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that \(\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathbf I}<p_{\mathbf I}\) for every \(\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}\). Subtract \(\varepsilon\lambda_{\mathbf I}\) from entry \((\mathbf I,\mathbf I)\) and add it to entry \((\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\). Column sums are preserved. Removed diagonal masses have zero row moment, while the added row has moment
\(\varepsilon\sum\lambda_{\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\). At \(\mathbf I=\mathbf J\) the removal and addition cancel. This is a feasible array positive at every allowed entry of row \(\mathbf J\). Average these constructions over all \(\mathbf J\) to obtain a feasible \(r^\circ\) positive on precisely
\(\mathcal T=\{(\mathbf J,\mathbf I):\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}\}\).

The maximizer is positive on all of \(\mathcal T\). If it vanished at an allowed coordinate, the entropy change along \((1-t)r^H+tr^\circ\), as \(t\downarrow0\), would be \(-t\log t\) times the positive sum of \(r^\circ\) over the formerly zero coordinates, plus \(O(t)\). The formerly positive coordinates contribute only \(O(t)\) by differentiability. The total change would therefore be positive for sufficiently small \(t\), a contradiction. Every feasible array vanishes outside \(\mathcal T\), so this also proves the asserted exact support.

Finite entropy multipliers exist on that exact support. Every array \(h\) supported on \(\mathcal T\) with zero column sums and zero row moments permits perturbations \(r^H\pm th\) for all sufficiently small \(t>0\). Stationarity of \(H\) therefore annihilates the common kernel of those equality constraints. The orthogonal complement of a finite matrix's kernel is its row space, whether or not its equations are redundant. Consequently finite scalars \(a_{\mathbf I}\) and finite vectors \(\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\) exist with

\[
-\log\frac{r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}-1
=a_{\mathbf I}+\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)
\qquad((\mathbf J,\mathbf I)\in\mathcal T).
\]

Set \(\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}=-\boldsymbol\gamma_{\mathbf J}\). Exponentiating and summing in column \(\mathbf I\) shows that \(e^{-1-a_{\mathbf I}}N(\mathbf I)=1\). This proves the common exponential formula. The summand \(\mathbf K=\mathbf I\) belongs to \(N(\mathbf I)\) and equals one, so \(N(\mathbf I)\ge1\). No multiplier uniqueness, full-dimensional constraint system, or positive entry outside \(\mathcal T\) has been assumed.

Fix a positive barycentric combination \(\mathbf z=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\in D\). If \(\mathbf z\in F_{\mathbf K}\), the face property forces every used \(\mathbf I\) into \(F_{\mathbf K}\). Put \(A_{\mathbf z}=\{\mathbf K\in D:\mathbf z\in F_{\mathbf K}\}\). Weighted Hölder's inequality on this nonempty common set gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
N(\mathbf z)
&=\sum_{\mathbf K\in A_{\mathbf z}}
\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}
\left(e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)}\right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}\\
&\le
\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf K\in A_{\mathbf z}}
e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf K}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf K)}\right)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}
\le\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}N(\mathbf I)^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For completeness, the first inequality follows by normalizing each positive family over \(A_{\mathbf z}\) by its sum, applying weighted arithmetic-geometric mean to the normalized entries at each \(\mathbf K\), and summing. The last inequality uses the proved inclusion of \(A_{\mathbf z}\) in every source's active set. Thus differing active sets at boundary sources cause no problem. Taking logarithms proves the convex-admissibility of \(\log N\).

The formula for each row gives
\(\psi_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf I)=\log N(\mathbf I)-\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\).
It is nonnegative because \(r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\le p_{\mathbf I}\), and subtracting this affine function preserves convex-admissibility. A lattice barycenter of \(D_{\mathbf J}\) lies in \(F_{\mathbf J}\cap\mathbb Z^n=D_{\mathbf J}\), so these inequalities account for every possible lattice barycenter in the row support.

The values \(-\log f_{\mathbf I}=-\log p_{\mathbf I}+\log N(\mathbf I)\) are convex-admissible on \(D\). Define their lower convex roof on \(P\) by

\[
\widehat g(x)=
\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\lambda_{\mathbf I}(-\log f_{\mathbf I}):
\lambda_{\mathbf I}\ge0,\quad \sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}=1,\quad
\sum_{\mathbf I}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I=x\right\}.
\]

Every feasible coefficient set is nonempty and compact, so this roof is finite and attained. Combining two minimizers proves convexity. Convex-admissibility at lattice barycenters, together with the singleton representation, proves
\(\widehat g(\mathbf I)=-\log f_{\mathbf I}\) on \(D\). Therefore \(\widetilde f=e^{-\widehat g}\) on \(P\), set equal to zero outside \(P\), is finite-valued and globally log-concave. It agrees with \(f\) at every lattice point because \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\). This is the convex-roof construction in accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc applied to the common table \(f\).

For a row, the function
\(\widetilde f(x)e^{\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(x-\mathbf J)}\mathbf1_{F_{\mathbf J}}(x)\)
is finite-valued and globally log-concave: its positive domain is the convex face \(F_{\mathbf J}\), and its logarithm there is concave. It has exactly the specified complete row lattice values. Dividing by \(q^H_{\mathbf J}>0\) gives the normalized row extension, and its mean is \(\mathbf J\) by the original moment equation. The same lower-roof construction on \(F_{\mathbf J}=\operatorname{conv}D_{\mathbf J}\), using the values \(\psi_{\mathbf J}\), gives its stated finite convex extension. All constructions take place in the affine hull actually present and require no transverse thickening or removal of zero lattice entries.

We next prove the capacity assertion, including positivity and boundary values. For any \(\mathbf x\in P\), choose coefficients of a convex representation \(\mathbf x=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\mathbf I\), with all used coefficients positive. For every \(\mathbf t\), weighted arithmetic-geometric mean yields

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}f_{\mathbf I}e^{\mathbf t\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf x)}
\ge\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}
f_{\mathbf I}e^{\mathbf t\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf x)}
\ge\prod_{\mathbf I\in E}f_{\mathbf I}^{\lambda_{\mathbf I}}>0.
\]

The lower bound is independent of \(\mathbf t\), and taking \(\mathbf t=\mathbf0\) gives the finite upper bound \(\sum_D f_{\mathbf I}\). Thus capacity is positive and finite on every point of \(P\). If \(\mathbf x\notin P\), strict separation from the compact convex polytope supplies a vector \(\mathbf v\) with \(\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf x)<0\) for all \(\mathbf I\in D\). Taking \(\mathbf t=a\mathbf v\) and \(a\to+\infty\) proves \(\mathcal C_{\mathbf x}(f)=0\).

At a target \(\mathbf J\), consider the face-restricted exponential sum
\(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf J}}f_{\mathbf I}e^{\mathbf t\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)}\).
Its gradient at \(\mathbf t=\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\) is the zero row moment, and the sum is convex. Thus that point is a global minimum, of value \(q^H_{\mathbf J}\). This minimum assertion also follows termwise from \(e^s\ge1+s\) and the row moment equation. Every full exponential sum is at least its face-restricted part and hence at least \(q^H_{\mathbf J}\). Conversely, along
\(\mathbf t=\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}+a\mathbf v_{\mathbf J}\), the terms in the face remain unchanged and every term outside the face tends to zero. The limit is \(q^H_{\mathbf J}\). When \(F_{\mathbf J}=P\), the restricted and full sums coincide directly. These two inequalities establish
\(\mathcal C_{\mathbf J}(f)=q^H_{\mathbf J}\), without asserting finite full-support tilt attainment at a boundary target.

For \(\mathbf x\in P\), positivity allows one to take logarithms of the infimum:

\[
\log\mathcal C_{\mathbf x}(f)
=\inf_{\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^n}
\left[
\log\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}f_{\mathbf I}e^{\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf I}\right)
-\mathbf t\cdot\mathbf x
\right].
\]

This is an infimum of affine functions of \(\mathbf x\), hence concave on \(P\). Combined with the zero value outside the convex set \(P\), it proves global log-concavity of capacity. The exact lattice identity on \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) then proves all claimed target-table values and zeros, including rank zero.

The exponential representation also proves the exact KL projection identity. On every allowed entry,
\(\log(r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})
=\boldsymbol\beta_{\mathbf J}\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)-\log N(\mathbf I)\).
Summing this formula against any \(r\in\mathcal F\) removes the moment term and fixes the remaining term by the column sums. In particular,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
&=-\sum_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I}\log N(\mathbf I)
=-H(r^H),\\
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(r\|r^H)
&=\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}
\log\frac{r^H_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}}{p_{\mathbf I}}
=H(r^H)-H(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Only entries with positive \(r\) are used in these sums. They are all in the exact positive support of \(r^H\), so the relative entropy is finite.

Accepted fact 464815b935df9890 applies with exactly the present \(p,D,\phi,\mathcal F(p),H,I,G,S\). It supplies the parity array's feasibility and positive denominators, \(H(r^{\mathrm{par}})=S(p)-S(\pi)\), the bound \(S(\pi)\le n\log2\), and nonnegativity of \(G(r)\), \(I(r)\), and relative entropy under these complete-table hypotheses. No weaker midpoint-only variant of that fact is being used.

For arbitrary \(r\in\mathcal F\), the same entropy cancellation from that fact, before specializing to its parity array, reads

\[
\begin{aligned}
G(r)+I(r)+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\|p)
&=\left(S(p)+\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}\right)
+\bigl(S(q)-H(r)\bigr)\\
&\qquad{}-S(q)-\sum_{\mathbf J}q_{\mathbf J}\log p_{\mathbf J}\\
&=S(p)-H(r).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the expression for \(G(r)\) uses only the column sums and cancellation of the constant \(\log p_{\max}\), so it applies to every feasible array, including zero rows. Apply this identity to \(r^H\) and the new KL projection identity to the accepted parity array. Adding gives
\(S(p)-H(r^{\mathrm{par}})=S(\pi)\), which is exactly the four-term budget. Its four left-hand terms are nonnegative, so discarding the two KL terms proves the stated coefficient-one control. For every \(0<c<1\), the rational actual-body family in the all-array sharpness assertion of accepted fact 464815b935df9890 forces \(G(r)>c(n\log2-S(q)+H(r))\) for every feasible \(r\). Applying it to the present feasible \(r^H\) proves the stated optimality among strictly positive coefficients without repeating that family's proof.

For a barycentric component at \(\mathbf J\) in \(D_{\mathbf J}\), the row formula and the mean equation for its coefficients give

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_\lambda(r^H_{\mathbf J,\cdot})
&=\log p_{\mathbf J}
-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log p_{\mathbf I}
+\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{\mathbf I}\log N(\mathbf I)
-\log N(\mathbf J).
\end{aligned}
\]

The final two terms have nonnegative sum by convex-admissibility of \(\log N\). The first cost is nonnegative by global log-concavity of \(p\). Multiplying a table by a positive constant adds the same constant to all its logarithms, which cancels because the coefficients sum to one. This proves both the original-cost comparison and its equality under row normalization, with precisely the scale-invariant logarithmic-cost convention of accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc.

For the final obstruction, the uniform table on \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) has the globally log-concave extension \(m^{-1}\mathbf1_{[0,m-1]}\), and \(\phi=0\), so \(G(r^H)=0\). Its parity law has at most two classes. The four-term budget gives \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q^H\|p)\le\log2\).
For a fixed site \(j\), put \(t=q^H_j\). Since all rows are positive and \(m\ge2\), \(0<t<1\). Grouping this site against the remaining sites, and using nonnegativity of the relative entropy of their conditional law against the uniform law on \(m-1\) sites, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q^H\|p)
&\ge t\log(mt)+(1-t)\log\frac{1-t}{1-1/m}\\
&=t\log m+t\log t+(1-t)\log(1-t)
-(1-t)\log(1-1/m)\\
&\ge t\log m-\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last line uses the binary entropy bound \(-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)\le\log2\) and \(\log(1-1/m)<0\). Combining the upper and lower bounds proves \(t\le2\log2/\log m\) at every site. Each rectangle \(C_m\) is a full-dimensional compact convex body for \(m\ge2\); its integer fibers are exactly \(\{j\}\times[0,1]\) for \(j=0,\ldots,m-1\), each of one-dimensional volume one, with empty fibers at every other integer. Its total mixed-integer volume is \(m\), so its normalized table is the one just analyzed. With \(n=1\) fixed, \(2^{-n}=1/2\) remains fixed while the largest assigned row mass tends to zero. This proves the stated obstruction. It concerns budgets bounded by a chosen row's assigned mass and does not give a counterexample to the locked conjecture.
