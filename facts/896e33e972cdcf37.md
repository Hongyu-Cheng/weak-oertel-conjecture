## Statement

$$
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\quad(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell),\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,\quad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\quad N=n+1.
$$
Use any symmetric saturated-chain partition \(\mathcal C\) of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), its nonterminal set \(D_m\), and its injective successor
\(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\). These are exactly the objects of 032423921efad6c8. Spatial coordinates are indexed by
\(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\).
For a Boolean source \(b\), start at the root and follow the child selected by \(i_m(b[u])\) whenever the current child block \(b[u]\) belongs to \(D_m\), stopping at its first failure or depth \(Q\). Write the resulting path as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and set
$$
z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r},\qquad
A_{m,Q}=\bigcup_b\lbrace z_s(b):0\le s\le h(b)\rbrace,
$$
$$
\widehat A_k=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
Here \(\operatorname{lev}(z)\) is the number of coordinates equal to two. This is the complete fixed canonical lift of 604889294a17d7df; every original height atom is retained.

Choose any tree node \(v\) with depth \(0\le d<Q\). Let \(\theta\) be an arbitrary real field supported on the whole child block \(\lbrace vi:1\le i\le m\rbrace\), and let \(\lambda\) vanish on that block and satisfy the whole-child-block normalization of dbbd63823b5f4870:

\sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{u_ri}|\le1.
$$
For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), let \(\gamma=\theta+\lambda\), and keep the full original law
$$
w_J=e^{\gamma\cdot z_J+\beta t_J},\qquad
Z=\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}w_J,\qquad p_J=w_J/Z.

Use exactly the mixed rows of 8ab782832d1c7da2: each row consists of its separately fixed center singleton and pairwise disjoint pairs or triples of distinct other supported points, each with arithmetic mean the center. Rows at different centers may overlap. If \(\mathcal O\) is the chosen cycle containing \(I\), define

g_J(I)=\left(\prod_{I'\in\mathcal O}p_{I'}\right)^{1/|\mathcal O|},\quad

Y_J(I)=\log\frac{p_I}{g_J(I)},\quad

b_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jp_JH_J^2,\quad
q_J=p_JH_J^2/b_{\mathrm m},\quad
\nu_{\mathrm m}=\sum_Jq_JV_J,
\qquad A_0=4^N\sum_Jp_J^3,\quad B=4^Nb_{\mathrm m}.
$$
These are precisely the superscript \((\mathrm m)\) objects of 8ab782832d1c7da2, with that superscript suppressed, rather than the full reflected-row objects.

There is one finite absolute constant \(C\ge4\), independent of every parameter just specified, such that every active law \(A_0<1\) admits these rows with
$$
B\ge1,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm m}\le C\log B.
\tag{1}
$$
Indeed the construction proves this for all laws in this class, and, when \(k\ge64\), gives
$$

Consequently, choose any positive integer \(L\), arbitrary heterogeneous factors of this class (including different \(k,\mathcal C,v,\theta,\lambda,\beta\)), and their original product law \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}p_i\) in dimension \(\mathcal N=\sum_iN_i\). ## Proof

Write
$$
\delta=2^{-m}\binom m{m/2},\qquad \eta=1-\delta.
$$
Accepted fact 032423921efad6c8 gives \(0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2}\), successor injectivity, and the injective Boolean-source parametrization of every level. The number of chains is \(2^m\delta\). For any label \(i\), put
$$
\mathcal L_i=\lbrace c\in D_m:i_m(c)=i\rbrace.
$$
Every saturated chain uses label \(i\) at most once, so
$$
1\le|\mathcal L_i|\le2^m\delta.
\tag{4}
$$
For the lower inequality, the chain containing the empty set runs through every cardinality up to the full set, and uses each label once. The corresponding inverse-edge label count has the same upper bound. At most two vertices per chain lack two successive predecessors. These statements use only the defining properties of \(\mathcal C\).

Depth \(d=0\) is exactly the theorem of 1af079babe686d6e. Its lower bound \(400/361\) in the large-\(k\) range is stronger than (2). We prove the new case \(d\ge1\), first for \(k\ge64\).

Set \(x=e^\beta\). For \(0\le t\le k\), define
$$
q_t=Q_t,\qquad d_t=\min(t,k-t),\qquad r_t=1+2d_t,
$$
and let \(c_0=1\), \(c_r=1+3\cdot2^{r-1}\) for integers \(r\ge1\). Use the scalar sums
$$
H_0(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^t,\qquad
U_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}q_tx^t,\qquad
T_{\mathrm v}(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}r_t^2x^{3t},
$$
$$
T_*(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^{3t}\sum_{s=1}^{q_t}c_{q_t-s}^2,
$$
$$
U_d(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}(q_t-d)_+x^t,\qquad
T_d(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}x^{3t}\sum_{s=d+1}^{q_t}c_{q_t-s}^2.
\tag{5}
$$
Empty sums are zero. Since \(d<Q=q_k\), both \(U_d\) and \(T_d\) are positive. The subscript in \(U_d,T_d\) refers to the chosen node depth, whereas \(d_t\) is only the height reflection radius. The first four sums are exactly those in equations (4)-(5) of 1af079babe686d6e.

We record explicitly the bounded-field estimates needed below. For \(a\ge0\), let \(\mu_{a\lambda}\) be the Boolean product law with parameter \(a\lambda\). At a fresh child block \(u\), set \(A_u=\sum_i|\lambda_{ui}|\). Its density relative to the fair block law is between \(e^{-aA_u}\) and \(e^{aA_u}\). Consequently a prescribed successor or inverse-successor label has probability at most \(e^a\delta\). A one-predecessor or forward-successor test fails with probability at most \(e^a\delta\), and a two-predecessor test with probability at most \(2e^a\delta\).

All these bounds remain valid conditionally at adaptive fresh queries, since the location of the next block is determined before its independent bits are exposed. If \(S\) is the sum of \(|\lambda_u|\) at selected coordinates on any such stopped forward path, then
$$
0\le S\le1,\qquad \mathbb E_{\mu_{a\lambda}}S\le e^a\delta.
\tag{6}
$$
To check the second bound with a random stopping rule, conditional on each query the expected contribution is at most \(e^a\delta A_u\); the sum of the queried block budgets on every realized path is at most \(\Pi(\lambda)\le1\). Sum these conditional expectations. This argument also applies to paths starting below a fixed prefix, and to paths stopped on first reaching \(v\). In the latter application the block at \(v\) is never queried. Thus no assertion conditions a remaining block budget on a future success event. These are also the adaptive estimates proved in (6)-(8) of 1af079babe686d6e, here including a possible root query with bounded field.

The elementary estimates
$$
|e^{2u}-1|\le2e^2|u|,\qquad |e^{6u}-1|\le6e^6|u|\qquad(|u|\le1)
\tag{7}
$$
will be used with (6). Fix \(\varepsilon=1/100\). The parameter estimates in f1fcf9518bf577b3 give \(Q\ge12k\) for \(k\ge32\), and the definition gives \(Q\ge b_{64}=2^{55}\) for \(k\ge64\). In particular
$$
2e^3\delta<\varepsilon,\qquad
2Qe^3\delta+7e^9\delta<\varepsilon.
\tag{8}
$$
Indeed, using \(e<3\) and \(\delta<Q^{-2}\), the second left side is smaller than \(54/Q+137781/Q^2<1/100\); the first is smaller than \(54/Q^2\).

Write the chosen node as \(v=v_1\cdots v_d\), and its ancestors as \(v^{(h)}=v_1\cdots v_h\), with \(v^{(0)}=\varnothing\). We use the following rows. At every Boolean spatial center \((j,t)\), take its singleton and all vertical reflection pairs in its column; its size is \(r_t\). At a positive-level center \((j,t)\) of level \(s\), call the center an avoidance center if its first prefix label is different from \(v_1\). Call it an entry center if \(s\ge d+1\) and its prefix passes through \(v\). These two classes are disjoint. At either kind of center, put \(r=q_t-s\). If \(r=0\), use the singleton. If \(r\ge1\), apply the local two-predecessor and two inverse-path test of equations (4)-(5) of abbc24783c13e0f6 below its existing level-\(s\) prefix. On success, take the singleton and all its \(2^{r-1}\) triples at this same actual height \(t\); the row size is \(c_r\). On failure take the singleton. At every other positive-level center take only its singleton.

The cited local construction verifies distinctness, disjointness, the center-mean identity, and membership of every source. Its hypotheses and verification use only symmetric saturated chains, so apply to every \(\mathcal C\). Its source levels are \(s+r,s+r,s\), and \(s+r=q_t\), so every source is present at the original height \(t\). Every avoidance row lies below a prefix whose first root child differs from \(v_1\); hence it never changes the block at \(v\). Every entry row preserves its entire existing prefix, including that block, since \(s>d\). Boolean rows change only height. Thus every selected row preserves the whole \(\theta\)-block.

Here is the exact entry factor. Let \(\mathcal O\) be the set of all spatial coordinates outside the block at \(v\), and set
$$
P_a=\prod_{u\in\mathcal O}(1+e^{a\lambda_u}),\qquad
F_a=e^{2a\sum_{h=1}^{d}\lambda_{v^{(h)}}}
\prod_{h=0}^{d-1}
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathcal L_{v_{h+1}}}e^{a\lambda[v^{(h)}]\cdot c}}
{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+e^{a\lambda_{v^{(h)}i}})}.
\tag{9}
$$
The notation \(\lambda[u]\) denotes the vector of coefficients on the child block of \(u\). In particular \(P_a\) is an auxiliary Boolean product sum, not an original-law normalizer. The product in \(F_a\) is exactly the \(\mu_{a\lambda}\)-probability that the first \(d\) successor choices follow the prescribed route to \(v\). Its exponential prefactor includes every \(\lambda\)-spike on these \(d\) ancestor steps. No bound on \(F_a\), and no smallness assumption on the resulting weighted entry mass, is made.

Define the exact strong-block sums
$$
\mathfrak B(\theta)=2^{-m}\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m}e^{\theta\cdot c},\qquad
\mathfrak E(\theta)=2^{-m}\sum_{c\in D_m}e^{\theta\cdot(c+2e_{i_m(c)})}.
\tag{10}
$$
These are the sums of 1af079babe686d6e, now on the block at \(v\).

We first bound the full original normalizer. Every positive-level point has a unique source and prefix. If that prefix has not traversed an edge out of \(v\), its strong block is Boolean. For levels \(s\le d\), this includes all sources; the prefix does not query that block. For \(s>d\), the nonentry sources are precisely those successful prefixes not following the route to \(v\), an event independent of the strong-block bits. In either case, expose a forward path stopped before querying \(v\). The sum over strong-block bits factors as \(2^m\mathfrak B(\theta)\). By (6)-(8) with \(a=1\), the remaining successful-prefix spike expectation is at most \(1+\varepsilon\). The Boolean level has exactly the spatial sum \(2^mP_1\mathfrak B(\theta)\).

For an entry point of level \(s\ge d+1\), the first \(d\) ancestor blocks give exactly \(F_1\). The block at \(v\) gives exactly \(2^m\mathfrak E(\theta)\), including the arbitrary strong spike, and chooses a child. Conditional on any such choice, the next \(s-d-1\) forward steps use fresh blocks. Their spike expectation, with success indicator retained, is at most \(1+\varepsilon\) by the same stopped-path bound. Summing these two exhaustive classes over every allowed level and every allowed actual height therefore gives
$$
Z\le2^mP_1\left[
\mathfrak B(\theta)\bigl(H_0+(1+\varepsilon)U_*\bigr)
+(1+\varepsilon)F_1\mathfrak E(\theta)U_d
\right].
\tag{11}
$$
The nonentry upper bound uses all \(q_t\) positive levels, even when some of their sources entered, which only increases it. The entry term is retained separately with its exact ancestor factor and all \(q_t-d\) eligible levels. Thus (11) bounds the entire original \(Z\), including the positive-level centers where only singleton rows were chosen.

Let \(m_J=|R_J|\). By the affine identities (9) of 8ab782832d1c7da2,
$$
g_J(I)=p_J,\quad H_J=m_Jp_J,\quad
b_{\mathrm m}=Z^{-3}\sum_Jm_J^2w_J^3,
$$
$$
V_J=\frac1{m_J}\sum_{I\in R_J}
\bigl(\gamma\cdot(z_I-z_J)+\beta(t_I-t_J)\bigr)^2.
\tag{12}
$$
We now lower-bound the numerator in (12) on disjoint classes of original centers.

The Boolean centers give exactly
\(2^mP_3\mathfrak B(3\theta)T_{\mathrm v}\). Fix a positive level \(s\le q_t\), and consider the avoidance centers. Before excluding the root label \(v_1\), the forward prefix and the local triple test use at most \(s+2r\le2Q\) units of one-predecessor or successor failure probability, where a two-predecessor test is counted twice. For \(r=0\) only the prefix is required. Under \(\mu_{3\lambda}\), their failure probability is at most \(2Qe^3\delta\), and their cubic prefix spike multiplier is \(e^{6\sum\lambda_u}\). Equations (6)-(7) show that its expectation differs from one by at most \(6e^9\delta\), before imposing any success event. Removing also the event that the first root label is \(v_1\) costs at most \(e^6e^3\delta=e^9\delta\). Consequently the cubic expectation on successful avoidance centers is at least
$$
1-2Qe^3\delta-7e^9\delta\ge1-\varepsilon.
\tag{13}
$$
On this event all queried blocks and selected coordinates are outside the root branch containing \(v\), so neither its success indicator nor its spike multiplier depends on the strong-block bits. Their sum therefore factors exactly as \(2^m\mathfrak B(3\theta)\). This justifies applying (13) with the unrestricted strong field; it is not a density comparison for that field.

For an entry center of level \(s\ge d+1\), retain every ancestor cylinder in (9) and every strong-block source in (10). Their exact cubic factor is \(F_3\mathfrak E(3\theta)\). Conditional on these data, the remaining forward prefix and local triple test use fresh descendant blocks. Their weighted cubic expectation is at least \(1-\varepsilon\) by (6)-(8), without the extra root-label exclusion and uniformly over the chosen strong successor label. Hence, by source injectivity and disjointness of entry and avoidance center classes,
$$
\sum_Jm_J^2w_J^3
\ge2^mP_3\left[
\mathfrak B(3\theta)\bigl(T_{\mathrm v}+(1-\varepsilon)T_*\bigr)
+(1-\varepsilon)F_3\mathfrak E(3\theta)T_d
\right].
\tag{14}
$$
When \(r=0\), its contribution is exactly that of the selected singleton \(c_0=1\); no fictitious triple is counted at the final level. Failed rows and all other centers have nonnegative energy and were simply omitted from this lower bound, never from (11).

We next prove the entry energy gain with its original weights. Set
$$
\mathcal D=4^{n-m}P_3/P_1^3.
$$
The coordinate inequality \((1+y)^3\le4(1+y^3)\) gives \(\mathcal D\ge1\). On an ancestor block, cancellation of its product denominator in \(\mathcal D F_3/F_1^3\) leaves exactly
$$
4^m\frac{\sum_{c\in\mathcal L_{v_{h+1}}}e^{3\lambda[v^{(h)}]\cdot c}}
{\left(\sum_{c\in\mathcal L_{v_{h+1}}}e^{\lambda[v^{(h)}]\cdot c}\right)^3}
\ge\frac{4^m}{|\mathcal L_{v_{h+1}}|^2}\ge\delta^{-2}.
$$
Here the first inequality is the finite cubic Hölder inequality. The fixed ancestor spike prefactors in \(F_3/F_1^3\) cancel exactly. The \(d\) ancestor blocks are disjoint, and every remaining coordinate factor in \(\mathcal D\) is at least one. Therefore
$$
\mathcal D\ge1,\qquad
\mathcal D F_3\ge\delta^{-2d}F_1^3.
\tag{15}
$$
This is an exact finite-sum inequality, valid regardless of how strongly the original entry mass was magnified. Likewise the cubic Hölder inequality on the \(2^m\) Boolean weights, and on the at most \(2^m\) strong successor weights, gives
$$
\mathfrak B(3\theta)\ge\mathfrak B(\theta)^3,
\qquad \mathfrak E(3\theta)\ge\mathfrak E(\theta)^3.
\tag{16}
$$

Put
$$
H_*=H_0+(1+\varepsilon)U_*,\qquad
\widetilde T_*=T_{\mathrm v}+(1-\varepsilon)T_*.
$$
Substituting (11), (14)-(16) into (12), and using \(N=n+1\), proves
$$
B\ge4\,
\frac{\mathfrak B(\theta)^3\widetilde T_*
+(1-\varepsilon)\delta^{-2d}(F_1\mathfrak E(\theta))^3T_d}
{\left[\mathfrak B(\theta)H_*+(1+\varepsilon)F_1\mathfrak E(\theta)U_d\right]^3}.
\tag{17}
$$
For \(a,b,u,w>0\), Hölder gives
\((a+b)^3\le(a^3u+b^3w)(u^{-1/2}+w^{-1/2})^2\).
Apply it in (17), retaining \(F_1\mathfrak E(\theta)\) rather than bounding it. With
$$
v_*(x)=H_0(x)^{3/2}/\sqrt{T_{\mathrm v}(x)},\quad
e_*(x)=U_*(x)^{3/2}/\sqrt{T_*(x)},\quad
e_d(x)=U_d(x)^{3/2}/\sqrt{T_d(x)},
$$
we get
$$
B\ge\frac4{
\left[H_*^{3/2}/\sqrt{\widetilde T_*}
+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}\delta^d e_d\right]^2}.
\tag{18}
$$
A second use of the same Hölder inequality, on the two terms of \(H_*\), yields
$$
\frac{H_*^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\widetilde T_*}}
\le v_*+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}e_*
\le v_*+\frac{21}{20}e_*.
\tag{19}
$$
The numerical bound follows by squaring:
\((101/100)^3/(99/100)<(21/20)^2\).
Equations (16)-(18) of 1af079babe686d6e establish, for these exact scalar sums, every \(k\ge64\), and every \(x>0\),
$$
v_*(x)+\frac{21}{20}e_*(x)<\frac{19}{10}.
\tag{20}
$$
Its proof splits at \(x=1/16\): below that value it uses \(T_*\ge235x^{24}\) and the exact entrance-height upper sum, while above it uses the complete interval energy bound and the exponentially large top-height term. In particular (20) has no activity restriction or lower bound on \(\beta\).

For completeness, the new entry scalar has the elementary bound
$$
e_d(x)\le k+1\qquad(x>0).
\tag{21}
$$
Indeed \(c_r\ge2r+1\) for every \(r\ge0\): this is immediate for \(r=0,1\), and for \(r\ge1\) follows inductively from \(c_{r+1}=2c_r-1\). For every integer \(h\ge1\),
$$
\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}c_r^2\ge
\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}(2r+1)^2
=\frac{h(4h^2-1)}3\ge h^3.
$$
Apply this at \(h=(q_t-d)_+\). Thus
\(T_d\ge\sum_{t=0}^{k}((q_t-d)_+x^t)^3\), and cubic Hölder on the \(k+1\) height terms gives (21). This estimate includes every height and every entry level, even when \(v\) is just above the final level.

Since \(d\ge1\), \(\delta^d\le\delta\), and the parameter estimates above give \(k+1\le Q\). Hence
$$
\delta^de_d\le\delta(k+1)<1/Q\le2^{-55}.
$$
Combining (18)-(21), the denominator capacity is smaller than
\(19/10+(21/20)2^{-55}<39/20\). This proves
\(B\ge4/(39/20)^2=1600/1521\), uniformly in depth and in all the fields.

We verify variance for the very same rows. At each nontrivial positive-level row, every source has the same height and the same \(\theta\)-block as the center. The coordinate table in abbc24783c13e0f6 changes only coordinates on two inverse descendant paths, by at most one in absolute value per coordinate. Their total absolute \(\lambda\)-score is at most \(2\Pi(\lambda)\le2\). Equation (12) therefore gives \(V_J\le4\), and singleton rows have variance zero.

At a Boolean row the score is \(\beta u\), uniformly over \(u=-d_t,\ldots,d_t\), so its variance is \(\beta^2d_t(d_t+1)/3\). Conditional on Boolean centers under the energy law \(q_J\), its height distribution is exactly proportional to \(r_t^2x^{3t}\); all spatial sums, including the unrestricted \(\theta\)-sum, cancel. The exact interval variance bound in (15)-(22) of 6d7276756d672c31, with interval length \(k\), bounds this conditional average by \(2/3\), for either sign of \(\beta\). The total \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\) is a mixture of this average and positive-level averages at most four, so \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\le4\). This proves (2).

It remains to cover the finitely many \(8\le k<64\) without restricting the strong field, the remainder field, or \(\beta\). The finite-support argument (19)-(21) in 1af079babe686d6e proves the following stronger fact for each fixed such \(k\) and \(\mathcal C\): the full reflected rows on \(D=\widehat A_k\) satisfy \(B_{\mathrm f}>1\) for every positive globally affine law on this entire support, and \(\nu_{\mathrm f}/\log B_{\mathrm f}\) is bounded on the closure of the entire affine family. We recall why this is the entire family, so its application here does not require a root-block reduction.

Let \(\mathsf r_J\) be the full reflection count. The affine pair identities and completeness of \(D\) give
\(\chi=\sum_J\mathsf r_Jp_J^2=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^N}p(c+2\mathbb Z^N)^2\ge2^{-N}\).
Strict Jensen gives \(B_{\mathrm f}>4^N\chi^2\ge1\) at every positive affine law: at a fixed Boolean spatial point, the full vertical rows at heights zero, one, two have sizes one, three, five, so their values \(\mathsf r_Jp_J\), proportional to \(1,3x,5x^2\), cannot all agree. Every proper support of a boundary affine law is an exposed face, hence lies in an integral affine hyperplane with primitive normal and uses at most \(2^{N-1}\) parity classes. The same polynomial identities give \(B_{\mathrm f}\ge4\) there. Finally the affine pair identity implies \(|\log(p_I/p_J)|\le-\log p_J\) on every reflected row. Thus each term approaching a zero center is bounded by \(\mathsf r_J^2p_J^3(\log p_J)^2\to0\), proving continuous extension and boundedness of the variance on that compact closure. These are precisely the fixed-support arguments proved in the cited accepted fact; none assumes where the affine field is supported.

There are finitely many allowed partitions for each fixed \(k\), and finitely many \(k\) in this range. Their price constants therefore have a finite absolute maximum. Take \(C\) to be the maximum of that number, the root-block constant from 1af079babe686d6e, \(4/\log(1600/1521)\), and four. Equations (1)-(2) follow for every law in the statement, in particular every active law.

For the heterogeneous product, choose the just-proved rows in each active factor and singleton rows in each inactive factor. The inactive factor has \(B_i=A_{0,i}\ge1\) and \(\nu_i=0\). Hence \(G=\sum_i\log B_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i\nu_i\le CG\). The mixed comparison (5)-(7) of 8ab782832d1c7da2, with the explicit mixed-object aliases in the statement, gives
$$
4^{\mathcal N}M_2(P)\ge\frac19
\exp\left(G-2\sqrt{3\sum_i\nu_i}\right)
\ge\frac19e^{G-2\sqrt{3CG}}\ge\frac19e^{-3C}.
$$
The last step completes the square in \(\sqrt G\). Jensen under the original product law gives \(M_r(P)\ge M_2(P)^{r/2}\) for all real \(r\ge2\), proving (3). All mass decompositions above used the original \(Z\), and this final comparison uses the original product of those laws.
