## Statement

Let \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\) be a nonempty finite complete lattice support, where \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) is convex, and let \(p\) be a probability law on \(D\). Define \(\nu_0=p\) and, for \(r\geq0\),
\[
 \nu_{r+1}(z)=\sum_{x,y\in D:\ x+y=2z}\nu_r(x)\nu_r(y),
 \qquad
 \beta_r(p)=\sum_{z\in D}\nu_r(z).
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
 \beta_{r+1}(p)\geq2^{-N}\beta_r(p)^2,
 \qquad
 \beta_j(p)\geq2^{-N(2^j-1)}
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(j\geq0\). Here \(\beta_j\) is the probability that, in the balanced dyadic tree on \(2^j\) independent samples, every internal midpoint is integral; it is not merely the probability that the final average is integral.

If, in addition, \(p\) is positive and log-affine on \(D\), so that
\(p_x=Z^{-1}e^{\langle\theta,x\rangle}\) for some
\(\theta\in\mathbb R^N\), let \(b_{D,j}(z)\) be the number of ordered
\(2^j\)-tuples of leaves in \(D\) whose fixed balanced dyadic tree has every
internal node integral and has root \(z\). Then the exact center grouping is
\[
 \nu_j(z)=b_{D,j}(z)p_z^{\,2^j},
 \qquad
 \beta_j(p)=\sum_{z\in D}b_{D,j}(z)p_z^{\,2^j}.
\tag{2a}
\]
In particular, the four-sample hierarchical mass
\(\mathcal R_4(p):=\beta_2(p)\) satisfies
\(\mathcal R_4(p)=\sum_{z\in D}b_{D,2}(z)p_z^4\).

For a finite positive weighted support, write
\[
 \tau_w(x)=\inf_{H\ni x}\sum_{y\in D\cap H}w_y,
 \qquad W=\sum_{y\in D}w_y,
 \qquad p_y=\frac{w_y}{W},
\tag{3}
\]
where the infimum is over ambient closed halfspaces, and put
\[
 \tau_p(x)=\frac{\tau_w(x)}W,
 \qquad
 M_q(p)=\sum_{x\in D}p_x\tau_p(x)^q.
\tag{4}
\]
On the complete ternary box \(D=\{0,1,2\}^N\), take \(w_x=\prod_{i=1}^Nt_i^{x_i}\), where \(0<t_i<1\), and set \(\alpha_i=-\log t_i\). If \(I(x)=\{i:x_i=1\}\), then the exact ambient halfspace depth is
\[
 \tau_w(x)=w_x\frac{T_{I(x)}+1}{2},
 \qquad
 T_I=\sum_{v\in\{-1,0,1\}^{I}}
       \exp\!\left(-\left|\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_iv_i\right|\right).
\tag{5}
\]

Fix any \(j\geq1\), let \(m=2^j\) and \(q=m-1\). There is no finite constant \(A_j\), independent of the dimension, for which
\[
 \beta_j(p)\leq A_jM_q(p)
\tag{6}
\]
holds for all complete finite log-affine laws. More precisely, for every \(A>0\) there are finite \(N\), the complete box \(D=\{0,1,2\}^N\), and positive rational weights \(w_x=\prod_{\ell=1}^Nt_\ell^{x_\ell}\) such that \(\beta_j(p)>A M_q(p)\).

These witnesses are not counterexamples at the matching depth-moment scale. They can be chosen with every \(t_\ell\leq1/4\), and then
\[
 2^{qN}M_q(p)
 \geq
 \left(\frac{2^q}{(21/16)^{q+1}}\right)^N,
\tag{7}
\]
whose base is greater than one for every \(q=2^j-1\). In particular, when \(j=2\),
\[
 8^NM_3(p)\geq
 \left(\frac{524288}{194481}\right)^N.
\tag{8}
\]

## Proof

First consider (1). For each parity vector \(c\in\{0,1\}^N\), let \(D_c=D\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^N)\). If \(x,y\in D\) have the same parity, then \((x+y)/2\) is integral and belongs to \(P\) by convexity, hence belongs to \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^N\). Therefore summing the recurrence over its output gives the exact identity
\[
 \beta_{r+1}(p)
 =\sum_{c\in\{0,1\}^N}\nu_r(D_c)^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\sum_c\nu_r(D_c)=\beta_r(p)\) give the first inequality in (2). Since \(\beta_0=1\), induction with \(e_0=0\) and \(e_{r+1}=2e_r+1\) gives \(e_r=2^r-1\) and the second inequality. The same recurrence shows inductively that \(\nu_r(z)\) is the total probability of all labeled balanced dyadic trees on \(2^r\) leaves whose internal midpoints are integral and whose root is \(z\). Thus the interpretation following (2), including all intermediate-node integrality, is exact.

Expanding the recurrence also shows that \(\nu_j(z)\) is the sum of
\(\prod_{\ell=1}^{2^j}p_{x_\ell}\) over precisely the ordered leaf tuples
counted by \(b_{D,j}(z)\). Every such tuple has root equal to its leaf average,
so \(\sum_{\ell=1}^{2^j}x_\ell=2^jz\). Under the stated log-affine law, each
summand therefore equals
\[
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{2^j}p_{x_\ell}
 =Z^{-2^j}\exp\!\left\langle\theta,\sum_{\ell=1}^{2^j}x_\ell\right\rangle
 =Z^{-2^j}e^{\langle\theta,2^jz\rangle}
 =p_z^{\,2^j}.
\]
There are exactly \(b_{D,j}(z)\) summands, proving both identities in (2a)
and their \(j=2\) specialization.

We next prove (5). Fix \(x\in D\), write \(I=I(x)\), and consider the face
\[
 F_x=\{y\in D:y_i=x_i\text{ whenever }i\notin I\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Its displacements from \(x\) are exactly \(v\in\{-1,0,1\}^I\). Every closed halfspace containing \(x\) contains at least one of \(x+v,x-v\), because \(x\) is their midpoint. Moreover,
\[
 w_{x+v}=w_xe^{-\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_iv_i},
 \qquad
 w_{x-v}=w_xe^{\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_iv_i}.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently every such halfspace has mass at least the central mass \(w_x\) plus the lighter mass in each nonzero antipodal pair. This is
\[
 w_x\left(1+\frac12\sum_{0\ne v\in\{-1,0,1\}^I}
 e^{-|\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_iv_i|}\right)
 =w_x\frac{T_I+1}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]

This lower bound is attained by an ambient halfspace. If \(I\ne\varnothing\), choose \(b\in\mathbb R^I\) arbitrarily close to \((\alpha_i)_{i\in I}\) so that \(b\cdot v\ne0\) for every nonzero \(v\in\{-1,0,1\}^I\), while \(\operatorname{sgn}(b\cdot v)=\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha\cdot v)\) whenever \(\alpha\cdot v\ne0\). Such a choice exists because there are only finitely many strict sign conditions and finitely many hyperplanes to avoid. Thus \(b\cdot v\geq0\) selects exactly the lighter endpoint from every unequal-weight antipodal pair and exactly one endpoint from every equal-weight nonzero pair. For \(y\in D\), define
\[
 g_x(y)=-\sum_{i:x_i=0}y_i+\sum_{i:x_i=2}(y_i-2).
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(g_x=0\) on \(F_x\) and \(g_x\leq-1\) on \(D\setminus F_x\). Extend \(b\) by zero outside \(I\), and choose \(K>\max_{y\in D}|b\cdot(y-x)|\). The closed halfspace
\[
 \{y\in\mathbb R^N:b\cdot(y-x)+K g_x(y)\geq0\}
\tag{14}
\]
contains \(x\), excludes every point of \(D\setminus F_x\), and on \(F_x\) makes exactly the selection just described. If \(I=\varnothing\), the same construction with \(b=0\) isolates the vertex \(x\). Hence (12) is attained and (5) follows, including all equal-energy pairs.

We now construct the obstruction. Fix \(j\), hence \(m=2^j\) and \(q=m-1\), and define
\[
 \lambda=\frac1{2\,3^q}.
\tag{15}
\]
For an arbitrary integer \(k\geq1\), choose distinct primes \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\). For a positive integer \(L\), put
\[
 t_i=P_i^{-L},
 \qquad
 n_i=\left\lfloor\lambda P_i^{mL}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 N=\sum_{i=1}^kn_i,
\tag{16}
\]
and use \(n_i\) coordinates with tilt \(t_i\). For all sufficiently large \(L\), every \(n_i\) is positive. The resulting weights on the full box are positive and rational.

Let
\[
 c_s=\#\{v\in\{-1,0,1\}^s:\textstyle\sum_rv_r=0\}
 =\sum_{h=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\binom{s}{2h}\binom{2h}{h},
 \qquad c_0=c_1=1.
\tag{17}
\]
If a point has \(s_i\) middle coordinates in group \(i\), its quantity \(T_I\) in (5) depends only on \(\mathbf s=(s_1,\ldots,s_k)\); call it \(T_{\mathbf s}(L)\). For a displacement of these middle coordinates, let \(r_i\) be the sum of its entries in group \(i\). Its energy is \(L\sum_i(\log P_i)r_i\). Unique prime factorization, applied to the rational identity \(\prod_iP_i^{r_i}=1\), gives
\[
 \sum_i(\log P_i)r_i=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 r_i=0\text{ for every }i.
\tag{18}
\]
For each fixed \(\mathbf s\), the zero-energy terms therefore number \(\prod_i c_{s_i}\), while every one of the finitely many nonzero-energy terms tends to zero exponentially in \(L\). Hence
\[
 T_{\mathbf s}(L)\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^kc_{s_i}.
\tag{19}
\]

Let \(W=\prod_i(1+t_i+t_i^2)^{n_i}\). From (4) and (5), grouping points according to their middle-coordinate counts gives the exact expression
\[
 \mathcal A_{k,L}:=W^mM_q(p)
 =\sum_{0\leq s_i\leq n_i}
 \left[\prod_{i=1}^k
 \binom{n_i}{s_i}(1+t_i^{2m})^{n_i-s_i}t_i^{ms_i}\right]
 \left(\frac{T_{\mathbf s}(L)+1}{2}\right)^q.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, a middle coordinate contributes \(t_i^m\) to \(w_x^m\), while summing the two endpoint choices contributes \(1+t_i^{2m}\).

For fixed \(\mathbf s\), one has \(n_it_i^m\to\lambda\), so
\[
 \binom{n_i}{s_i}t_i^{ms_i}\longrightarrow\frac{\lambda^{s_i}}{s_i!},
 \qquad
 (1+t_i^{2m})^{n_i-s_i}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{21}
\]
To justify passage of the limit through the growing finite sum, extend its summand by zero to all \(\mathbf s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k\). Since \(n_it_i^m\leq\lambda\),
\[
 \binom{n_i}{s_i}t_i^{ms_i}\leq\frac{\lambda^{s_i}}{s_i!},
 \qquad
 \prod_i(1+t_i^{2m})^{n_i}
 \leq \exp\!\left(\sum_in_it_i^{2m}\right)\leq e^{k\lambda}.
\tag{22}
\]
Also \(1\leq T_{\mathbf s}(L)\leq3^{\sum_i s_i}\), and therefore \(((T_{\mathbf s}(L)+1)/2)^q\leq3^{q\sum_i s_i}\). The summands in (20) are consequently dominated by the \(L\)-independent summable array
\[
 e^{k\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{(3^q\lambda)^{s_i}}{s_i!}.
\tag{23}
\]
Dominated convergence on the counting measure of \(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k\) is thus applicable.

Define
\[
 F_a(\lambda)=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}c_s^a\frac{\lambda^s}{s!}
 \quad(0\leq a\leq q),
 \qquad F_0(\lambda)=e^\lambda.
\tag{24}
\]
Expanding \(((\prod_i c_{s_i})+1)^q\) after applying (19)--(23) gives
\[
 \mathcal A_{k,L}\longrightarrow
 \mathcal A_k
 =2^{-q}\sum_{a=0}^q\binom qaF_a(\lambda)^k
 \leq F_q(\lambda)^k,
\tag{25}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(c_s\geq1\), hence \(F_a\leq F_q\) for \(0\leq a\leq q\).

It remains to calculate the dyadic functional. Begin in one coordinate with the unnormalized weights \((1,t,t^2)\), and apply the midpoint recurrence in (1) without the global normalization. At level \(r\) the three weights are
\[
 \bigl(1,\gamma_rt^{2^r},t^{2^{r+1}}\bigr),
 \qquad \gamma_0=1,
 \qquad \gamma_{r+1}=\gamma_r^2+2.
\tag{26}
\]
This follows by induction: the two ordered endpoint pairs contribute \(2t^{2^{r+1}}\) to the new middle weight, and the old middle paired with itself contributes \(\gamma_r^2t^{2^{r+1}}\). Midpoint convolution tensorizes over coordinates. More explicitly, if \(\mu_r^{(t_\ell)}\) denotes the one-coordinate triple in (26), then induction in (1) gives
\[
 \nu_r(z)=W^{-2^r}\prod_{\ell=1}^N\mu_r^{(t_\ell)}(z_\ell).
\]
Thus level \(j\) is homogeneous of degree \(m=2^j\) in the initial weights, and
\[
 W^m\beta_j(p)
 =\prod_{i=1}^k(1+\gamma_jt_i^m+t_i^{2m})^{n_i}
 \longrightarrow e^{\gamma_jk\lambda}.
\tag{27}
\]
For completeness, the limit follows by taking logarithms: if \(u_i=\gamma_jt_i^m+t_i^{2m}\), then \(n_iu_i\to\gamma_j\lambda\) and \(n_iu_i^2\to0\), so \(n_i\log(1+u_i)\to\gamma_j\lambda\).

We finally bound \(F_q\). For \(s\geq1\), choosing the first \(s-1\) entries of a zero-sum ternary vector determines its last entry if that entry is allowed, so \(c_s\leq3^{s-1}\). Hence
\[
 F_q(\lambda)
 \leq1+\frac{e^{3^q\lambda}-1}{3^q}
 =1+\frac{e^{1/2}-1}{3^q}
 <1+\frac1{3^q}=1+2\lambda.
\tag{28}
\]
The strict inequality \(e^{1/2}<2\) follows from \(e=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}1/r!<1+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}2^{1-r}=3<4\). The recurrence in (26) gives \(\gamma_j\geq3\) for \(j\geq1\). Since the normalizing factor \(W^m=W^{q+1}\) is common to (20) and (27), (25), (27), \(e^u>1+u\) for \(u>0\), and (28) yield
\[
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\beta_j(p)}{M_q(p)}
 =\frac{e^{\gamma_jk\lambda}}{\mathcal A_k}
 \geq\left(\frac{e^{\gamma_j\lambda}}{F_q(\lambda)}\right)^k
 >\left(\frac{1+\gamma_j\lambda}{1+2\lambda}\right)^k.
\tag{29}
\]
The last base is strictly greater than one. Given any finite \(A>0\), choose a finite \(k\) for which that base to the \(k\)-th power exceeds \(A\). The limit in (29) is then strictly greater than \(A\), so the definition of convergence supplies a sufficiently large finite integer \(L\) for which \(\beta_j(p)/M_q(p)>A\). Equations (16) then give a finite dimension and positive rational weights. This proves the asserted failure of every dimension-free comparison, without relying on any numerical or external computation.

To verify the calibration, observe that the all-zero vertex has weight one and can be isolated by the halfspace \(\{x:\sum_i x_i\leq0\}\). Thus \(\tau_w(0)=1\), and its single contribution gives
\[
 M_q(p)\geq W^{-(q+1)}.
\tag{30}
\]
When \(L\geq2\), every prime is at least two, so every tilt in (16) is at most \(1/4\), and
\[
 W=\prod_{\ell=1}^N(1+t_\ell+t_\ell^2)
 \leq(21/16)^N.
\tag{31}
\]
Combining (30) and (31) gives (7). At \(q=1\) its base is \(2/(21/16)^2>1\), and increasing \(q\) multiplies the base by \(32/21>1\), so the base is greater than one for every \(q\geq1\). Substitution of \(q=3\) gives (8). Therefore the family obstructs the proposed bridge while retaining exponentially positive slack in the corresponding normalized moment.
