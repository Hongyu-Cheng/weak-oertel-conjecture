## Statement

On $[-1,1]$ the lengths of the slabs $[-2,-1]$ and $[-1,0]$ below the concave roof $r(x)=-x^2$ are $1$ and $1-x^2$. There is no convex vertical body having those respective lengths as its upward cap lengths above the fixed levels $-2$ and $-1$, even if only prescribed at $x=-1,0,1$. This persists under a common affine vertical shear or translation of the body and both cutting planes. The same two selectors are realized by the actual sampled convex body
$C_\delta=\{(z,y): -1\le z\le1,\ -2\le y\le-\delta z^2\}$ with $0<\delta\le1$, using outward uniform width $\delta$ and cap thresholds $-\delta$ and $0$.

## Proof

The two slab lengths follow by intersecting with $(-\infty,-x^2]$. At either outside column, the upward lengths $(1,0)$ require its vertical interval to contain $-2$ and end at $-1$. Their midpoint lies at height $-2$ in the middle column. At the middle column the required lengths $(1,1)$ force positive length above $-1$ and zero length between $-2$ and $-1$, contradicting the presence of height $-2$ and convexity of the interval. A simultaneous affine shear is invertible, preserves each vertical length, and pulls the two planes back to the fixed levels.

The actual sampled base of $C_\delta$ is $\{-1,0,1\}$, with positive interval fibers and a concave upper roof. The strips for thresholds $-\delta$ and $0$ are $[-2\delta,-\delta]$ and $[-\delta,0]$, above the fixed lower plane. Their occupied lengths divided by $\delta$ are $1$ and $1-z^2$, respectively, including both endpoint fibers.
