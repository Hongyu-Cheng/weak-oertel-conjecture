## Statement

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite set in an affine lattice, equal to the lattice points of $\operatorname{conv}P$, and let $d\ge1$. At each $z\in P$ let $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, with $\sum_z\lambda_zK_z\subseteq K_p$ whenever $\sum_z\lambda_z z=p\in P$ is a convex representation. Write $C$ for the canonical convex hull of the lifted fibers, $C_z$ for its fiber over $z$, and $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathbf1_{K_z}\,dX)$ for the measured fibers. Put $M=\sum_z|K_z|$, let $g_z$ be the centroid of $K_z$, and define
$$
N=d+1,\qquad
\Sigma=\frac1M\sum_z\int_{K_z}(X-g_z)(X-g_z)^{\mathsf T}\,dX,\qquad
\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u},\qquad
f(z,x)=\left(\frac{E_{C_z}(x)}{M}\right)^{1/N}.
$$
Here $E_K(y)$ is the minimum of $\int_K[1+a\cdot(X-y)]^{-N}\,dX$ over parameters with positive denominator on $K$ when $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, and is zero on the own boundary. At a sampled mark $(p,y)$, the raw buffered depth $b_\varepsilon(p,y)$ is the infimum of $\mu$-masses of the closed caps
$$
a\cdot(Z-p)+u\cdot(X-y)\ge-\varepsilon f(p,y)\Gamma(u),\qquad u\ne0,
$$
and of the closed pure-base caps containing $p$ (with value $M$ in base rank zero). Write $\beta_\varepsilon(K)=\max_{p\in P,\ y\in K_p}b_\varepsilon(p,y)/M$. The case $\varepsilon=0$ gives ordinary normalized closed-halfspace depth.

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and suppose every old buffered maximizing mark has a proper shifted own section in every nonvertical direction, meaning the shifted plane cuts its own fiber interior. Let $Q=M\beta_\varepsilon$ and $I$ be the sampled bases at which $Q$ is attained. Then $I$ is the full lattice trace of $\operatorname{conv}I$. There are at most $k(I)\le k(P)$ cap groups, with parity counted in the inherited affine lattice of $P$. Each group $i$ is associated with a face $F_i$ of $\operatorname{conv}I$ and an old maximizing source over a sampled base in $\operatorname{relint}F_i$. The sets $J_i=I\cap F_i$ cover $I$. Each group consists of at most $d+1$ nonvertical caps $\{N_{ij}\cdot(Z,X)\ge c_{ij}\}$ of actual mass $Q$, with $N_{ij}=(a_{ij},u_{ij})$, $\Gamma(u_{ij})=1$, and weights $\rho_{ij}>0$ summing to one. They are proper on the group source's own fiber and their thresholds are right upper $Q$-quantiles. Define $P_i(z,x)=\sum_j\rho_{ij}N_{ij}\cdot(z,x)$ and $h_i=\sum_j\rho_{ij}c_{ij}$. Then
$$
P_i(z,x)-h_i-\varepsilon f(z,x)\ge0\qquad(z\in F_i,\ x\in C_z).
$$
For every real $z\in F_i$ the zero is a unique own-interior mark $y_z$, every selected buffered quantile constraint is an equality there, and the marks are affine on $F_i$. The bodies $C_z-y_z$ and the positive energy $E_{C_z}(y_z)=E_i$ are constant on that face, so $f(z,y_z)=(E_i/M)^{1/N}$.

## Proof

Each positive fiber contributes positive variance in every nonzero continuous direction, hence $\Sigma$ is positive definite. At this fixed old family apply the continuous change $X'=\Sigma^{-1/2}X$; the covariance in the new coordinates is the identity. The determinant law of [d90ba05577427854](d90ba05577427854.md), and the transformation of total mass and within-fiber covariance, preserve $f$ and the correspondence of caps, with all masses multiplying by the same factor. In the new coordinates the buffer is Euclidean, with shape $E_{C'_z}(x')^{1/N}$ and scale $\varepsilon (M')^{-1/N}$. The pure-base caps also correspond. At each maximizing source, properness for all directions implies own-interiority, since at a boundary mark the energy is zero and a supporting direction is available. For an interior mark the minimum over unit $u'$ of the lower supporting gap $u'\cdot y'-\min_{C'_p}u'\cdot X'$ is its distance to its own boundary: this is the maximum radius of a centered ball contained in the fiber by the support inequalities. The minimum is attained, so properness gives the strict distance inequality required by [c5ce541ffa55a29c](c5ce541ffa55a29c.md), [09a0ec46efc18691](09a0ec46efc18691.md), and [78f0698dc6ca4224](78f0698dc6ca4224.md).

By [0b2fbdaf8d3134a0](0b2fbdaf8d3134a0.md), the $Q'$-depth region in the new coordinates is convex for this energy power, and agrees with sampled depth at the old sampled bases. Its projection contains $\operatorname{conv}I$. Each lattice point there lies in $P$ and has a point in the $Q'$-depth region, so lies in $I$. Choose every inclusion-maximal face of $\operatorname{conv}I$ having a sampled point in its relative interior. Such faces cover $I$ since every point lies in the relative interior of its minimal face. Choose a sampled relative-interior point as a representative of each chosen face. If representatives of two distinct chosen faces have the same inherited parity, their midpoint lies in $I$. Any face containing their midpoint contains both endpoints and thus their minimal faces. The minimal face at the midpoint would be a larger occupied face, a contradiction. This gives the group count including the one-point case.

For each representative apply the finite active-cap balance theorem in the whitened coordinates at one of its maximizing marks. Its strict properness gives positive weights, at most $d+1$ actual mass-$Q'$ caps with right-quantile thresholds, and a supporting own gradient with the specified balance. The fixed-face theorem applies to $E^{1/N}$ by energy inclusion monotonicity and positive homogeneity, and identifies the weighted buffered margin over the representative's face with the full-tangent gap there. The fixed-face rigidity theorem then gives exactly the stated zero locus and whole-fiber equalities. Pull back the caps: unit continuous normals in the whitened system become $\Gamma$-unit in the old system. Thresholds, margins, and weights transport by substitution of the coordinates. The shared energy rescales by the common determinant, preserving equality within the group.
