## Statement

Use the binary simplex and positive shell fiber setting of the aggregate credit theorem. Put $b=1/(d+1)$, $h=1-b$, $a_p=|A_p|$, and $E=\sum_p a_p$. For each fixed core vertex $q$, flip coordinates to make it zero and use $p$ for the transformed outside bases. For a barycentric coordinate $\lambda_i$, write $U_{p,i}=\max_{x\in A_p}\lambda_i(x)$. Define
$$
\Omega_{q,i}=\min_Z \sum_{p\notin Z}a_p,
\qquad
\eta_j\ge h\ (1\le j\le n),\quad \eta\cdot p\ge U_{p,i}-b\ (p\in Z),
$$
where the minimum is over subsets of the positive outside bases for which the displayed system has a solution $\eta\in\mathbb R^n$. That system is feasible exactly when every $\gamma_j,\mu_p\ge0$ with $\sum_j\gamma_j e_j+\sum_{p\in Z}\mu_pp=0$ satisfies $h\sum_j\gamma_j+\sum_{p\in Z}\mu_p(U_{p,i}-b)\le0$. The raw depth at $(q,g)$ is at most $\Gamma_d V+\min_i\Omega_{q,i}$.

Let $\chi_q(a)$ be the minimum weight $\sum_{p\in F}a_p$ over discarded outside subsets $F$ such that $\eta_j\ge1$ for all $j$ and $\eta\cdot p\ge1$ for each $p\notin F$ are feasible. This is equivalent to strict exposure of $q$ after discarding $F$, and feasibility is equivalent to absence of a nonzero nonnegative dependence of the row vectors. Then $E_q\le\chi_q(a)\le E$, and $\chi_q$ is homogeneous, continuous and piecewise linear in the nonnegative masses. Every point of the core fiber at $q$ has raw depth at most $\Gamma_d V+\chi_q(a)$. If $E\le\rho_d V$, the maximum over all actual mixed-integer points lies between $\Gamma_d V+\max_q E_q$ and $\Gamma_d V+\max_q\chi_q(a)$. Along a fixed finite support with $a_p(t)=t\omega_p+o(t)$, the same bracket bounds the lower and upper right difference quotients of the raw maximum by $\max_q E_q(\omega)$ from below and $\max_q\chi_q(\omega)$ from above, respectively.

## Proof

For any feasible directional system, the cap $\lambda_i(x)-\eta\cdot z\ge b$ has own mass $\Gamma_d V$. At any other core vertex $\eta\cdot z\ge h$, and the continuous threshold is at least $1$. On a fiber $p\in Z$ its threshold is at least $U_{p,i}$. Each such restriction has zero volume even at equality because it is supporting or empty. The remaining outside mass is at most the cost in the minimum. Both types of systems permit the empty retained outside subset, so the minima exist. Farkas's alternative for a linear system with free variables gives the displayed certificate.

The base-only test is strict exposure because the nonzero binary vertices include the $e_j$, and any strictly positive finite gap can be scaled to one. The alternative with unit right sides gives the stated dependence criterion. Each owned $p$ is opposite a binary vertex across zero, so both cannot have positive $\eta$ evaluation. It must be discarded, giving the lower bound $E_q$. Discarding all extra bases gives the upper bound $E$. The feasible subsets are independent of the masses. The minimum of their finite linear cost functions is continuous, homogeneous, and piecewise linear. At any $y$ in the core fiber choose $\lambda_i(y)\ge b$ and use $\lambda_i(x)-2\eta\cdot z\ge\lambda_i(y)$ for a minimizing feasible subset. Every retained other base is excluded since $\lambda_i\le1$ and its base evaluation is at least one. The own mass is at most $\Gamma_d V$ and the discarded mass at most $\chi_q(a)$.

If $E\le\rho_d V$, the aggregate credit theorem gives the lower bound at each core centroid. An outside positive shell base has a coordinate $-1$ or $2$, so its outward vertical cap excludes the core and has mass at most $E<\Gamma_d V$. A null integer fiber cannot lie over the convex hull of the positive bases. Any representation by positive bases would put a positive-volume Minkowski combination in that fiber. Strict base separation then gives every point on a null fiber depth zero. These cases prove the bracket on the actual source. The finite linear minima obey $\chi_q(a(t))=t\chi_q(\omega)+o(t)$, uniformly over the finite set of core vertices, and the same expansion holds for the linear $E_q$.
