## Statement

Use the definitions and conclusions of accepted fact
`edd7db9fe417122f`.  In dimension \(d=2\), put
\[
\gamma_2:=\left(\frac23\right)^2=\frac49,
\qquad
\theta_2:=1-\gamma_2=\frac59,
\qquad
\chi_2:=\frac49-\frac59\log\frac95,
\qquad
\delta_3:=2^{-3}=\frac18.
\]
For a convex body with fiber masses \(a_{\mathbf z}\), total mass \(W\),
and a weighted lattice centerpoint \(\mathbf m\), let
\(\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)\) be the angular deficit from that accepted
fact.  The proposed alternatives are
\[
\tag{A}
a_{\mathbf m}\geq
\frac{\chi_2}{2\gamma_2}\,\delta_3W
\]
and
\[
\tag{B}
a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
\leq
(\gamma_2-\chi_2)a_{\mathbf m}
+\frac{\chi_2}{2}\,\delta_3W.
\]

For every integer \(R\geq5\), set
\[
G_R:=\{-R,\ldots,R\}^2,
\qquad
N_R:=(2R+1)^2,
\qquad
s:=\frac1{\sqrt{10}},
\qquad
\beta:=1-s,
\]
and define, with base coordinates
\((\mathbf p,t)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\) and continuous
coordinates \(\mathbf x=(x_1,x_2)\in\mathbb R^2\),
\[
C_R:=
\left\{(\mathbf p,t;\mathbf x):
\mathbf p\in[-R,R]^2,
\ x_1,x_2\geq0,
\ x_i+\beta|t|\leq1\ (i=1,2)
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(C_R\subseteq\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^2\) is a compact
full-dimensional convex body.  Its positive-volume lattice-fiber support
is
\[
D_+=G_R\times\{-1,0,1\},
\tag{2}
\]
with masses
\[
a_{(\mathbf p,0)}=1,
\qquad
a_{(\mathbf p,\pm1)}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
W=\frac65N_R,
\qquad
\delta_3W=\frac3{20}N_R.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(M_R\) be the set of all \(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^3\) such that
every proper closed base halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\) has fiber
mass at least \(\delta_3W\).  This set is nonempty by accepted fact
`edd7db9fe417122f`.  Every \(\mathbf m\in M_R\) has the form
\(\mathbf m=(\mathbf i,0)\) with \(\mathbf i\in G_R\), and hence
\(a_{\mathbf m}=1\).  For every such \(\mathbf m\),
\[
a_{\mathbf m}
<\frac{\chi_2}{2\gamma_2}\,\delta_3W
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
>(\gamma_2-\chi_2)a_{\mathbf m}
+\frac{\chi_2}{2}\,\delta_3W.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus every weighted lattice centerpoint violates both (A) and (B).
The upper-layer rays alone satisfy
\[
a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
\geq\frac{\chi_2N_R}{10},
\tag{6}
\]
and their one-step mass is exactly
\[
\frac{N_R}{10}
=\frac23\,\delta_3W
=\frac1{12}W.
\tag{7}
\]
Each of these rays attains equality in the mass-sensitive bound
\(a_{\mathbf m}\Delta_{\mathbf v}
\leq\chi_2\sum_{k\geq1}a_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v}\).

Now use the covariance sets and covariance Minkowski cores of accepted
fact `107b9182522633f9`.  For every \(\mathbf m\in M_R\), the core made
from the full positive support is empty:
\[
\mathcal I_{\mathbf m}(D_+)=\varnothing.
\tag{8}
\]
In contrast, for the middle-layer subset
\[
E_0:=G_R\times\{0\},
\tag{9}
\]
the same \(\mathbf m\) is an \(E_0\)-weighted centerpoint at threshold
\(2^{-3}\), its covariance core is nonempty, and in fact
\[
\mathcal I_{\mathbf m}(E_0)
=\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)
+\left\{\mathbf r\in\mathbb R^2:
|r_1|+|r_2|\leq\frac16\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]
Every \(\mathbf x\) in (10), including the middle-fiber centroid, obeys
\[
h_{S_R}(\mathbf m,\mathbf x)
\geq\frac{N_R}{36}
=\frac5{27}\,\delta_3W
>\frac{\chi_2}{2}\,\delta_3W,
\qquad
S_R:=C_R\cap(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb R^2).
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently this family disproves the proposed anchor-choice
dichotomy, not the weak Oertel statement.

## Proof

Write
\[
\tau:=\log\frac95.
\]
The exact constants above satisfy
\[
\gamma_2-\chi_2=\frac59\tau.
\tag{12}
\]
We will use both
\[
\frac49<\tau<\frac35.
\tag{13}
\]
For the lower bound, the integral formula for the logarithm gives
\[
\tau=\int_1^{9/5}\frac{du}{u}
>\int_1^{9/5}\frac59\,du
=\frac49.
\]
For the upper bound,
\[
e^{3/5}
>1+\frac35+\frac12\left(\frac35\right)^2
+\frac16\left(\frac35\right)^3
=\frac{227}{125}
>\frac95.
\]
Taking logarithms proves the other half of (13).  Therefore
\[
\chi_2>\frac19,
\qquad
\gamma_2-\chi_2<\frac13,
\qquad
\chi_2<\frac{16}{81}.
\tag{14}
\]

We first verify the body and its fibers.  The set in (1) is the
intersection of the box constraint on \(\mathbf p\), the affine
halfspaces \(x_i\geq0\), and the sublevel sets
\(x_i+\beta|t|\leq1\) of convex functions.  Hence it is convex and
closed.  Its constraints imply
\(|t|\leq1/\beta\) and \(0\leq x_i\leq1\), so it is bounded.  Choosing
\(\mathbf p\) in the interior of its box, \(t\) close to zero, and
\(0<x_i<1-\beta|t|\) shows that it has nonempty interior.  Thus it is a
compact full-dimensional convex body.

At a base point \((\mathbf p,t)\), its continuous fiber is
\[
[0,r(t)]^2,
\qquad
r(t):=1-\beta|t|,
\tag{15}
\]
whenever \(r(t)\geq0\), and it is empty otherwise.  In particular,
\[
r(0)=1,
\qquad
r(\pm1)=s,
\qquad
r(\pm2)=2s-1<0.
\]
For integral \(t\), this proves (2) and (3).  It also records explicitly
that all nonempty fibers are homothetic squares generated by the one
common concave side-length profile \(r(t)\).

We next locate every weighted lattice centerpoint.  Accepted fact
`edd7db9fe417122f` proves that every point satisfying the weighted
centerpoint inequalities has positive anchor-fiber mass.  Thus every
\(\mathbf m\in M_R\) lies in \(D_+\).  If
\(\mathbf m=(\mathbf i,1)\), then the proper closed base halfspace
\(t\geq1\) contains \(\mathbf m\) but has mass
\[
\frac{N_R}{10}<\frac{3N_R}{20}=\delta_3W,
\]
which contradicts the defining centerpoint inequality.  The halfspace
\(t\leq-1\) gives the same contradiction for a lower-layer point.
Therefore every \(\mathbf m\in M_R\) lies in the middle layer, as
claimed, and \(a_{\mathbf m}=1\).

Fix any \(\mathbf m=(\mathbf i,0)\in M_R\).  For every upper-layer
point \(\mathbf z=(\mathbf j,1)\), put
\[
\mathbf v:=\mathbf z-\mathbf m=(\mathbf j-\mathbf i,1).
\]
The last coordinate of \(\mathbf v\) is one, so \(\mathbf v\) is
primitive, and distinct upper-layer points give distinct directed rays.
Along this ray, the normalized mass sequence from accepted fact
`edd7db9fe417122f` is
\[
u_{\mathbf v,0}=1,
\qquad
u_{\mathbf v,1}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
u_{\mathbf v,k}=0\quad(k\geq2).
\]
It follows exactly that
\[
T_{\mathbf v}=\frac1{10},
\qquad
X_{\mathbf v}=0,
\qquad
\Delta_{\mathbf v}=\frac{\chi_2}{10},
\tag{16}
\]
because \(1/10<\theta_2=5/9\).  Moreover,
\[
a_{\mathbf m}\Delta_{\mathbf v}
=\frac{\chi_2}{10}
=\chi_2a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}
=\chi_2\sum_{k\geq1}a_{\mathbf m+k\mathbf v},
\tag{17}
\]
which proves the asserted saturation.

Take \(\mathbf q=(0,0,1)\) in the definition of
\(\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)\).  Every upper ray in (16) has
\(\mathbf q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v=1\).  Every selected horizontal ray
has first-step mass \(a_{\mathbf m+\mathbf v}=a_{\mathbf m}=1\), so the
zero-deficit implication in accepted fact `edd7db9fe417122f` gives
\(\Delta_{\mathbf v}=0\) on every such ray.  Lower-layer rays have
\(\mathbf q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v=-1\) and are not selected.  Hence the
sum for this \(\mathbf q\) is exactly
\[
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathscr R_{\mathbf m}\\
\mathbf q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v\geq0}}
\Delta_{\mathbf v}
=\frac{\chi_2N_R}{10}.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (17)-(18) prove (6)-(7).

We now disprove both alternatives with exact constants.  By (3),
\[
\frac{\chi_2}{2\gamma_2}\,\delta_3W
=\frac{27\chi_2N_R}{160}
>\frac{3N_R}{160}
\geq\frac{363}{160}
>1=a_{\mathbf m},
\tag{19}
\]
where (14) and \(N_R\geq121\) were used.  This proves (4), so (A)
fails.  On the other hand, (18) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)
-\left((\gamma_2-\chi_2)a_{\mathbf m}
+\frac{\chi_2}{2}\delta_3W\right)\\
&\qquad\geq
\frac{\chi_2N_R}{10}
-(\gamma_2-\chi_2)-\frac{3\chi_2N_R}{40}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{\chi_2N_R}{40}-(\gamma_2-\chi_2)\\
&\qquad>
\frac{N_R}{360}-\frac13
=\frac{N_R-120}{360}
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
This proves (5), so (B) also fails.  The argument used only properties
shared by all \(\mathbf m\in M_R\), and therefore covers the full
centerpoint quantifier.

For clarity, accepted fact `edd7db9fe417122f` gives the primitive-ray
certificate with entries
\[
\gamma_2a_{\mathbf m}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
\chi_2\delta_3W+(\gamma_2-\chi_2)a_{\mathbf m}
-a_{\mathbf m}\mathfrak D(\mathbf m).
\]
Failure of (A) makes the first entry strictly smaller than
\((\chi_2/2)\delta_3W\), while failure of (B) makes the second entry
strictly smaller than that same target.  The two alternatives depend
only on \(a_{\mathbf m}\), \(\mathfrak D(\mathbf m)\), and \(W\), so
changing the continuous point inside the anchor fiber cannot repair the
dichotomy.

It remains to compare the covariance cores.  For \(\rho>0\), write
\(K_\rho:=[0,\rho]^2\).  Its centroid and covariance matrix are
\[
\mathbf c_\rho=\left(\frac\rho2,\frac\rho2\right),
\qquad
\Sigma_\rho=\frac{\rho^2}{12}I_2.
\]
The covariance-polar definition in accepted fact
`107b9182522633f9` therefore gives
\[
\mathcal E(K_\rho)
=\left\{\mathbf r\in\mathbb R^2:
|r_1|+|r_2|\leq\frac\rho6\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Put
\[
\mathcal A_\rho:=\mathbf c_\rho+\mathcal E(K_\rho).
\]
For the functional \(\ell(\mathbf x):=x_1+x_2\),
\[
\min_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal A_1}\ell(\mathbf x)=\frac56,
\qquad
\max_{\mathbf x\in\mathcal A_s}\ell(\mathbf x)=\frac{7s}{6}.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(s=1/\sqrt{10}<5/7\), equations (22) imply
\[
\mathcal A_1\cap\mathcal A_s=\varnothing.
\tag{23}
\]

Fix again \(\mathbf m=(\mathbf i,0)\in M_R\).  The barycentric polytope
\(\Lambda_{\mathbf m}(D_+)\) from accepted fact
`107b9182522633f9` contains the point mass at \(\mathbf m\).  Its
corresponding Minkowski set is \(\mathcal A_1\).  It also contains the
barycentric representation assigning weight \(1/2\) to each of
\((\mathbf i,1)\) and \((\mathbf i,-1)\).  Its corresponding Minkowski
set is
\[
\frac12\mathcal A_s+\frac12\mathcal A_s=\mathcal A_s.
\]
The full-support core is the intersection over all such representations,
so (23) gives
\[
\mathcal I_{\mathbf m}(D_+)
\subseteq\mathcal A_1\cap\mathcal A_s
=\varnothing.
\]
This proves (8) for every weighted lattice centerpoint.

Finally take the subset \(E_0\) from (9).  Its total mass is
\(W_{E_0}=N_R\).  Let \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^3\) be any proper closed
base halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\), and put
\[
k:=\#(E_0\cap J).
\]
If the trace of \(J\) on the plane \(t=0\) is all of that plane, then
\(k=N_R\).  Otherwise that trace is a proper closed halfspace
\(L\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(\mathbf i\).  Its vertical
extension \(\widetilde J:=L\times\mathbb R\) is a proper closed base
halfspace containing \(\mathbf m\).  Each horizontal lattice location
in \(L\cap G_R\) contributes total mass \(1+1/10+1/10=6/5\), so the
full weighted-centerpoint property of \(\mathbf m\) gives
\[
\frac65k
=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\widetilde J\cap\mathbb Z^3}a_{\mathbf z}
\geq\delta_3W
=\frac{3N_R}{20}.
\]
Thus \(k\geq N_R/8=2^{-3}W_{E_0}\) in every case.  Since
\(\mathbf m\in E_0\), it also lies in \(\operatorname{conv}E_0\), so
all subset-centerpoint hypotheses of accepted fact
`107b9182522633f9` hold.

Every fiber indexed by \(E_0\) equals \(K_1\).  Convexity of
\(\mathcal A_1\) therefore gives, for every
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbf m}(E_0)\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E_0}\lambda_{\mathbf z}\mathcal A_1
=\mathcal A_1.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal I_{\mathbf m}(E_0)=\mathcal A_1\), which is (10).
The depth conclusion of accepted fact `107b9182522633f9` now yields,
for every \(\mathbf x\in\mathcal A_1\),
\[
h_{S_R}(\mathbf m,\mathbf x)
\geq\frac{\gamma_2}{2}\,2^{-3}W_{E_0}
=\frac{N_R}{36}
=\frac5{27}\,\delta_3W.
\tag{24}
\]
By (14),
\[
\frac{\chi_2}{2}<\frac8{81}<\frac{15}{81}=\frac5{27},
\]
which proves the strict comparison in (11).  In particular,
\((1/2,1/2)\in\mathcal A_1\), so the ordinary centroid of every
qualifying middle fiber has the bound (24).  The family therefore has a
uniform positive depth ratio at the same \(2^{-3}W\) scale.  It blocks
the proposed primitive-ray anchor-choice route, but supplies no
counterexample to the weak Oertel conjecture.
