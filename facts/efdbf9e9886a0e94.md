## Statement

Retain the canonical hard-branch stopped set \(A_\star\), weights \(w_y\), mass function \(W(A):=\sum_{y\in A}w_y\), fiber bodies \(C_y\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), fiber volumes \(a_y\), and fiber centroids \(c_y\) from accepted facts d01de728d8b5e33c and d06d919de3083960. Thus \(A_\star\) is a nonempty finite subset of \(\mathbb Z^n\), every \(y\in A_\star\) satisfies \(0<w_y\le a_y/4\), and, with

\[
\rho_\star:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_\star),
\]

one has

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}W(A_\star)>\frac{11}{320}Q.
\tag{1}
\]

For each \(y\in A_\star\), use the metronoid normalization retained in accepted fact 10f1feebe06e22d0 and put

\[
D_y:=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)-c_y.
\tag{2}
\]

By accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b, \(D_y\) is an origin-symmetric convex body. Write \(\|\cdot\|_{D_y}\) for its Minkowski functional. For every nonempty \(A\subseteq A_\star\), put

\[
L:=\operatorname{aff}(A),
\qquad
q:=\dim L,
\]

and define

\[
\Delta_w(A):=
\inf_{s:L\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\sum_{y\in A}w_y
\bigl(\|s(y)-c_y\|_{D_y}-1\bigr)_+.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(q<n\), then the relative complete metronoid-core capacity satisfies

\[
G_L^{\mathrm{rel}}
\ge
\bigl(W(A)-5\Delta_w(A)\bigr)_+.
\tag{4}
\]

If \(q=n\), then \(L=\mathbb R^n\), and the global complete metronoid-core capacity satisfies

\[
G
\ge
\bigl(W(A)-5\Delta_w(A)\bigr)_+.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(L_\star:=\operatorname{aff}(A_\star)\). Suppose the applicable convex-filling threshold fails, meaning

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}G_{L_\star}^{\mathrm{rel}}<\frac{Q}{100}
\quad\text{if }\rho_\star<n,
\tag{6a}
\]

and

\[
G<\frac{Q}{100}
\quad\text{if }\rho_\star=n.
\tag{6b}
\]

Then

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}\Delta_w(A_\star)
>\frac{39}{8000}Q.
\tag{7}
\]

Moreover, every affine map \(s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d\) satisfies

\[
\sum_{\substack{y\in A_\star:\\
s(y)\notin c_y+\frac65D_y}}
w_y
>
\frac{39}{55}W(A_\star).
\tag{8}
\]

Thus every affine map misses strictly more than \(39/55\) of the \(w\)-mass even after each half-metronoid \(c_y+D_y=\mathsf M_{a_y/2}(C_y)\) is expanded about \(c_y\) by the factor \(6/5\).

## Proof

We first record the metronoid monotonicity with its required direction. Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of volume \(a>0\), and let \(0<m'\le m\le a\). If \(x\in\mathsf M_m(K)\), choose a measurable \(f:K\to[0,1]\) such that

\[
\int_K f=m,
\qquad
x=\frac1m\int_K zf(z)\,dz.
\]

Then \(f':=(m'/m)f\) takes values in \([0,1]\), has integral \(m'\), and has the same normalized barycenter:

\[
\frac1{m'}\int_K zf'(z)\,dz
=\frac1m\int_K zf(z)\,dz
=x.
\]

Therefore

\[
0<m'\le m\le a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathsf M_m(K)\subseteq\mathsf M_{m'}(K).
\tag{9}
\]

This is the decreasing-mass, expanding-metronoid direction.

Fix a nonempty \(A\subseteq A_\star\), and retain \(L=\operatorname{aff}(A)\) and \(q=\dim L\). Accepted fact d01de728d8b5e33c gives \(0<w_y\le a_y/4\) for every \(y\in A\). Accepted fact 11e347c295e9430b gives

\[
c_y+\frac65D_y
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{a_y/4}(C_y).
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (9), the inequality \(w_y\le a_y/4\), and (10) gives

\[
c_y+\frac65D_y
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{a_y/4}(C_y)
\subseteq
\mathsf M_{w_y}(C_y).
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(s:L\to\mathbb R^d\) be affine, and define

\[
E_s:=
\left\{
y\in A:
\|s(y)-c_y\|_{D_y}\le\frac65
\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]

For every \(y\in E_s\), equation (11) yields

\[
s(y)\in\mathsf M_{w_y}(C_y).
\tag{13}
\]

The affine hull \(L\) of the integer set \(A\) is a primitive affine lattice subspace. Indeed, after fixing \(y_0\in A\), its direction space \(V\) is spanned by the integer vectors \(y-y_0\), so \(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\) spans \(V\); it is saturated because \(kz\in V\) with \(k\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) implies \(z\in V\). If \(E_s\ne\varnothing\), apply accepted fact 10f1feebe06e22d0 on \(L\), with selected fiber set \(E_s\), masses \(t_y=w_y\), and the restriction of \(s\). That fact explicitly allows \(E_s\) to span a proper affine subspace of \(L\). It gives

\[
G_L^{\mathrm{rel}}\ge W(E_s)
\quad\text{when }q<n,
\tag{14a}
\]

and, since \(q=n\) implies \(L=\mathbb R^n\), it gives

\[
G\ge W(E_s)
\quad\text{when }q=n.
\tag{14b}
\]

If \(E_s=\varnothing\), the selector lemma is not invoked. Both capacities are suprema of nonnegative admissible masses, so the applicable capacity is at least \(0=W(E_s)\). Hence (14a) or (14b), as applicable, holds for every affine \(s\), including the empty-\(E_s\) case.

For \(y\in A\), put

\[
e_y(s):=
\bigl(\|s(y)-c_y\|_{D_y}-1\bigr)_+,
\qquad
F(s):=\sum_{y\in A}w_ye_y(s).
\tag{15}
\]

Every \(y\in A\setminus E_s\) satisfies \(\|s(y)-c_y\|_{D_y}>6/5\), and hence \(e_y(s)>1/5\). Thus, if \(A\setminus E_s\ne\varnothing\),

\[
W(A\setminus E_s)
<5\sum_{y\in A\setminus E_s}w_ye_y(s)
\le5F(s).
\tag{16}
\]

If \(A\setminus E_s=\varnothing\), one instead has \(W(A\setminus E_s)=0\le5F(s)\). Therefore in all cases

\[
W(E_s)
=W(A)-W(A\setminus E_s)
\ge W(A)-5F(s).
\tag{17}
\]

The applicable inequality in (14), together with nonnegativity of the capacity, now gives

\[
G_L^{\mathrm{rel}}
\ge\bigl(W(A)-5F(s)\bigr)_+
\quad(q<n),
\tag{18a}
\]

and

\[
G
\ge\bigl(W(A)-5F(s)\bigr)_+
\quad(q=n).
\tag{18b}
\]

Letting \(s\) approach the infimum in (3) proves (4) and (5). Explicitly, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) choose \(s_\varepsilon\) with \(F(s_\varepsilon)<\Delta_w(A)+\varepsilon\), apply (18a) or (18b), and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This argument does not assume that the infimum is attained.

We now specialize to \(A=A_\star\). To keep the proper-rank and full-rank capacities distinct, define only for the remainder of the proof

\[
\mathcal G_\star:=
\begin{cases}
G_{L_\star}^{\mathrm{rel}},&\rho_\star<n,\\
G,&\rho_\star=n.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]

Since \(2^{n-\rho_\star}=1\) in the second case, assumptions (6a)-(6b) say uniformly that

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}\mathcal G_\star<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{20}
\]

Equations (4)-(5) imply, in the applicable case,

\[
\mathcal G_\star
\ge
W(A_\star)-5\Delta_w(A_\star),
\tag{21}
\]

because the positive part is at least its argument. Combining (1), (20), and (21) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
2^{n-\rho_\star}\Delta_w(A_\star)
&>
\frac15\left(
2^{n-\rho_\star}W(A_\star)-\frac Q{100}
\right)\\
&>
\frac15\left(\frac{11}{320}-\frac1{100}\right)Q
=\frac{39}{8000}Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

This proves (7), including both strict inequalities.

Finally, fix any affine \(s:L_\star\to\mathbb R^d\), and let \(E_s\) be defined by (12) with \(A=A_\star\). Equations (14a)-(14b) and (20) give

\[
2^{n-\rho_\star}W(E_s)<\frac Q{100},
\tag{23}
\]

where this also holds when \(E_s=\varnothing\). Dividing (23) by the strict positive lower bound (1) gives

\[
\frac{W(E_s)}{W(A_\star)}
<
\frac{1/100}{11/320}
=\frac{16}{55}.
\tag{24}
\]

Because \(D_y\) is closed and contains the origin in its interior, the gauge condition in (12) is equivalent to \(s(y)\in c_y+(6/5)D_y\). Consequently,

\[
\sum_{\substack{y\in A_\star:\\
s(y)\notin c_y+\frac65D_y}}w_y
=W(A_\star)-W(E_s)
>\frac{39}{55}W(A_\star),
\]

which is (8).
