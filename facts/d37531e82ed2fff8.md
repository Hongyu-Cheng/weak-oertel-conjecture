## Statement

Let $T$ be a full-dimensional $d$-simplex, $d\ge2$, of volume $V$ and centroid $g$, and put $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$. In two integer dimensions place $T$ at the four binary bases and the same body of volume $ta$ at both $(-1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$, where $a>0$ and the body is contained in $T$. If the added body is $g+r_t(T-g)$ with $r_t^dV=ta$, the exact local raw mixed closed-halfspace depth maximum at base $q=(0,0)$ is $\Gamma V+2\Gamma ta$. If instead the added body is a homothet of a fixed full-dimensional centrally symmetric body about $g$ with the same volume, the exact local maximum is $\Gamma V+ta$ whenever that homothet is contained in $T$. Both constructions are possible for all sufficiently small $t>0$, have the same masses, and the normalized laws of both additions converge to $\delta_g$. The two local right derivatives in $t$ are different.

## Proof

Both constructions have the exact six designated fibers. The base triangle is $\{z:z_1\le1,\ z_2\le1,\ z_1+z_2\ge0\}$, whose integer rows have respectively one, two and three points. The two added bases are exposed and all continuous generators lie in $T$, proving the census.

For the homothetic simplex take $r=r_t$ and $a'=ta$ in fact dc21348dcd815541, where the local statement is valid for $0<r\le1$. That fact gives the depth $\Gamma V+2\Gamma a'$ at $(q,g)$, with the matching canonical caps covering the whole $q$ fiber. This proves its exact local maximum.

For the symmetric body, any nonvertical through-cap at $(q,g)$ uses opposite shifts on the two identical added bodies. Symmetry and hyperplane nullity make their combined mass exactly $ta$. The own trace pays at least $\Gamma V$ by fact 53fe12b742a7608d. A pure-base through-cap contains all of the own $T$ and at least one addition, giving mass at least $V+ta$. This bounds all containing halfspaces by parallel tightening. Put $h=d/(d+1)$ and $b=1/(d+1)$. The caps $\lambda_i(x)-h(z_1+z_2)\ge b$, as $i$ ranges over the barycentric coordinates, cover the $q$ fiber. Each takes its canonical centroid cap of $T$ of mass $\Gamma V$, half of each symmetric addition, and no positive mass from any other core fiber because its threshold there is at least one. Their common mass gives the upper bound.

The added bodies in both constructions shrink about $g$, giving the stated point limits. The local coefficients are $2\Gamma a$ and $a$, distinct since $\Gamma<1/2$ for $d\ge2$.
