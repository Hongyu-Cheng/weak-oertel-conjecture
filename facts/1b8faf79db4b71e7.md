## Statement

For $n\ge0$, $d\ge1$, a $d$-simplex $T$ with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$, and $0<c<1/(d+1)$, let $N=2^n$ and $S=\{0,1\}^n\times T$. For every base vertex $v$, write $\ell_v(z)=\sum_{j:v_j=0}z_j+\sum_{j:v_j=1}(1-z_j)$ on the base cube. The $N(d+1)$ fixed caps $\lambda_i(x)-2\ell_v(z)\ge c$ form an inclusion-minimal strict cover of $S$, each with normalized mass $(1-c)^d/N$. Moving any one exposed product vertex by any nonzero continuous vector, holding all other vertices and caps fixed, gives a strictly positive right derivative of the largest normalized cap mass. Simultaneously expanding all fibers about the simplex centroid by factor $1+t$ instead gives each fixed cap derivative $-d(1-c)^{d-1}(1/(d+1)-c)/N$. The cover and its strict exclusive witnesses persist locally in both constructions.

## Proof

At any other binary vertex $\ell_v\ge1$, and on $T$ all $\lambda_i\le1$, so a cap labeled by $v$ captures only its own fiber. There it is a homothetic simplex cap with fraction $q=(1-c)^d$. Since the coordinates sum to one, their maximum exceeds $c$ at every point of $T$. A point in the relative interior close to vertex $i$ has $\lambda_i>c$ and all other coordinates strictly below $c$, giving the exclusive witness for each cap. For a small continuous vertex motion the lattice projection stays the cube. A convex combination over an extreme base vertex can only use that same base, so precisely one integer fiber moves. An affine map from the old fiber to the moved fiber, and the compactness of the old fibers, preserve the strict coverage margin, isolated traces and strict witnesses. The same continuity argument applies to the simultaneous expansion.

Move vertex $j$ in one fiber by $t h$ with $h\ne0$. The affine transport is $X\mapsto X+t\lambda_j(X)h$. Write $r_i=\nabla\lambda_i\cdot h$, so $\sum_i r_i=0$ and some $r_i>0$ because the simplex coordinate map is injective. The moved fiber has volume $V(1+tr_j)$ by the determinant lemma. Let $Q_i(t)$ be the conditional capture fraction on this fiber. The transported cap variable is $\lambda_i(X)+t r_i\lambda_j(X)$. For $i=j$ the fraction is $(1-c/(1+tr_i))^d$. For $i\ne j$ the affine variable has value one at vertex $i$, value $t r_i$ at vertex $j$, and value zero at all other vertices. For small $|t|$ only vertex $i$ is above $c$, and the new corner simplex has edge ratios $1-c$ on $d-1$ incident edges and $(1-c)/(1-tr_i)$ toward vertex $j$. Thus its fraction is $q/(1-tr_i)$. It follows that $Q_i'(0)=D_{ij}r_i$, with $D_{jj}=dc(1-c)^{d-1}$ and $D_{ij}=q$ for $i\ne j$.

The total mass is $V(N+tr_j)$. Therefore a moved-fiber cap has normalized derivative $D_{ij}r_i/N+q(N-1)r_j/N^2$, and an unmoved-fiber cap has derivative $-qr_j/N^2$. When $N=1$ a positive $r_i$ makes the first derivative positive. When $N\ge2$ and $r_j<0$ every unmoved-fiber cap has positive derivative. When $N\ge2$ and $r_j\ge0$, a positive $r_i$ gives a positive moved-fiber derivative. All caps initially tie, so the right derivative of their maximum is the largest of their derivatives. The calculation also applies with $-h$.

For the common homothety about the centroid, the old-coordinate threshold becomes $c(t)=1/(d+1)+(c-1/(d+1))/(1+t)$ and the normalized mass is $(1-c(t))^d/N$ because the common fiber volume cancels. Differentiation gives the claimed negative derivative. At $n=1,d=2,c=1/4$, the cover has six masses $9/32$, the own and other $D$ coefficients are $3/8$ and $9/16$, and the coordinated derivative is $-1/16$.
