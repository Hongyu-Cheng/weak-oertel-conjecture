## Proof

The interval family, all-direction formula, unique mark at $\ell=1$, and stated regular caps are from c1db52b8b22d210e. At the endpoint bases the buffered depth is at most two by pure base isolation. The middle formula depends only on $c=\ell_0$. The same fact proves strict increase of its maximum from $c=1$ to any $c>1$ since each $c\ge1$ occurs as the midpoint of a geometrically normalized positive affine multiplier and the middle is always the maximizing base. In the larger domain, concavity and log normalization give
$
0=\tfrac27\log\ell_-+\tfrac37\log\ell_0+\tfrac27\log\ell_+
\le\tfrac47\log\big((\ell_-+\ell_+)/2\big)+\tfrac37\log\ell_0
\le\log\ell_0.
$
Equality forces $\ell_-=\ell_0=\ell_+=1$. Thus the claimed enlarged-domain optimum is actual.

Write $q=\sqrt{43/84}$ and $f=(E/7)^{1/2}$ at the middle mark. Then $\delta=\varepsilon q f$. The interval formula gives $E=(35+4\delta-4\delta^2)/12$ and $a=(B-A)/(2AB)$ for the left and right endpoint distances $A=y+1$, $B=2-y$. This is the displayed $a$, and differentiating the interval energy gives $\partial_y f=fa$. For covariance-normalized cap normals $u_\pm=\pm1/q$ the upper cap has three proper coareas $q$, and the lower cap has one proper coarea $q$ at zero. Their exact buffered normal balance $\rho_+u_++\rho_-u_-=\varepsilon fa$, $\rho_++\rho_-=1$, gives $\rho_\pm=(1\pm\delta a)/2>0$. Equivalently these are the weights balancing the two opposite marked-depth derivatives after division by their coarea masses. With $B_0=\rho_+/(3q)+\rho_-/q$, set $\theta_+=\rho_+/(3q B_0)$, $\theta_-=\rho_-/(q B_0)$ and $\Delta=\varepsilon f/B_0$. These yield the formulas in the statement and $3\theta_+-\theta_-=\Delta a$.

To compute the regular selected-row first response, let $w_z$ be the outward endpoint speed under an interval variation and hold the old physical cap directions fixed. Their covariance normalization changes the additive cap margin by the derivative of $\varepsilon(E/M)^{1/2}(u_\pm^2\Sigma)^{1/2}$, where $\Sigma=q^2$ at the old data and $u_\pm^2\Sigma=1$. This derivative is
$$
\frac{\varepsilon f}{2}\left(\frac{\dot E}{E}-\frac{\dot M}{M}+\frac{\dot\Sigma}{\Sigma}\right).
$$
Each proper trace varies by its coarea times this margin change, in addition to the boundary integral of the old cap indicator against $w_z$. The saturated traces are strict. The sum of the two coareas with their weights is $\theta_+(3q)+\theta_-q=1/B_0$, so the additive contribution to the selected common raw response is the displayed logarithmic derivative multiplied by $\Delta/(\varepsilon f)$. A moving mark adds a term proportional to $-\sum_\pm\rho_\pm u_\pm+\varepsilon f a$, which vanishes by the stated balance. At fixed mark the interval formula for the optimal coefficient is smooth in the endpoints, and stationarity of the denominator integral in that coefficient gives $\dot E=\int_{\partial K_0}L^{-2}w_0\,dS$. Also
$$
\frac{\dot\Sigma}{\Sigma}
=\frac1M\sum_z\int_{\partial K_z}
\left(\frac{(X-g_z)^2}{\Sigma}-1\right)w_z\,dS,
$$
because the derivative of each $g_z$ multiplies a zero centered first moment. Finally, multiplying the derivative of normalized depth by $M$ subtracts $Q\dot M/M$. Combining the two mass terms from the logarithmic derivative with this subtraction gives exactly the regular one-group density at $\ell=1$
$
\Xi_z(X)=\theta_+\mathbf1_{H_+}(z,X)+\theta_-\mathbf1_{H_-}(z,X)-Q/7
+\mathbf1_{z=0}\frac{\Delta L(X)^{-2}}{2E}
+\frac{\Delta}{14}\sum_{\pm}\rho_\pm[u_\pm(X-g_z)]^2
-\frac{\Delta}{7},
$
where $Q=13/4+3\delta/2$, $g_z=(0,1/2,0)_z$, and $L(X)=1+a(X-y)$ on the middle interval. The upper indicator has endpoint normal flux $+1$ on each fiber. The lower indicator has flux $-1$ only on the middle because the outer fibers are strictly full or empty. The projective term has flux $-\Delta a$ at the middle by integration of its derivative and d90ba05577427854. Constants and centered squares have zero endpoint normal flux. Consequently the endpoints have force $\theta_+$ and the middle has $\theta_+-\theta_--\Delta a=-2\theta_+$.

The radial response can be computed without assuming the family is a joint minimizer. In the weighted sum of the radial cap fluxes, the raw volume terms give $Q$ since $\theta_++\theta_-=1$, and the remaining cut contributions are $\Delta[a(y-g_0)-1]-\sum_z g_zF_z$. To see this, the upper and lower cut heights at the middle are $y-\delta,y+\delta$, and all upper cut heights are identical. Sum their signed centered cut heights with weights $\theta_+,\theta_-$. Use $3\theta_+-\theta_-=\Delta a$ and $\delta(3\theta_++\theta_-)=\Delta$ from their definitions. The energy term contributes $\Delta[1/2-a(y-g_0)]$ by one-dimensional integration by parts and the escort identities. The centered square term contributes $3\Delta/2$ using $\sum_z\int_{K_z}(X-g_z)^2\,dX=7q^2$, and the two constants give $-Q-\Delta$. These scalars cancel, leaving $-\sum_zg_zF_z=\theta_+$. An inward common homothety with retained factor $r\in(0,1)$ gives endpoint interval $[-r,r]$ and a middle interval with left endpoint $1/2-3r/2>-r$. The middle no longer contains the endpoint midpoint interval.

At $\ell=1$ the normalized base weights are as displayed, and their barycenter is zero, which gives the stated base martingale. The projective boundary cone mean is $y$ and the ordinary cone means are $g_z$ by the centered cone-lift lemma. The target mean is $3/14$, giving the defect $\Delta(3/14-y)$ and its strict sign.
