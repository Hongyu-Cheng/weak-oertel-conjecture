## Statement

Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a nonempty compact convex set, let
\(A:=\operatorname{aff}K\), and let \(\lambda_A\) be Lebesgue measure on
the Euclidean affine space \(A\).  Thus, when \(K\) is a convex body in the
usual full-dimensional sense, \(A=\mathbb R^d\) and \(\lambda_A\) is the
usual \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure.  Suppose that

\[
 f_1,\ldots,f_N:K\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
\]

are measurable and satisfy

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N f_j(x)\leq1
 \quad\text{for (\lambda_A)-almost every }x\in K.
\tag{1}
\]

Write

\[
 s_j:=\int_K f_j\,d\lambda_A,
 \qquad
 m_j:=\int_K x f_j(x)\,d\lambda_A(x).
\tag{2}
\]

Then there are measurable functions \(g_1,\ldots,g_N:K\to[0,1]\)
such that

\[
 \int_Kg_j\,d\lambda_A=s_j,
 \qquad
 \int_Kxg_j(x)\,d\lambda_A(x)=m_j
 \quad(1\leq j\leq N),
\tag{3}
\]

and, after choosing the representatives constructed below,

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Ng_j(x)\leq1
 \quad\text{for every }x\in A.
\tag{4}
\]

Here every \(g_j\) is extended by zero on \(A\setminus K\).  For every
\(j\) with \(s_j>0\), this extension is log-concave on \(A\), in the
pointwise sense

\[
 g_j((1-t)x+ty)
 \geq g_j(x)^{1-t}g_j(y)^t
 \quad(x,y\in A,\ 0<t<1),
\tag{5}
\]

with the convention \(0^a=0\) for \(a>0\).  If \(s_j=0\), then necessarily
\(m_j=0\), and the conclusion uses \(g_j\equiv0\).  In particular, no
centroid is assigned to a zero-mass color; an expression \(m_j=s_jp_j\)
places no restriction on \(p_j\) when \(s_j=0\).

If \(K\) is lower-dimensional and the original convention instead uses
ambient \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure, then all feasible masses are
zero and the assertion is immediate.  The relative-measure formulation
above is the nontrivial lower-dimensional version.

## Proof

Let

\[
 J:=\{j:s_j>0\}.
\tag{6}
\]

For \(j\notin J\), nonnegativity and (2) imply \(f_j=0\) almost everywhere
and \(m_j=0\).  Remove these colors and relabel the remaining \(q:=|J|\)
colors as \(1,\ldots,q\).  The case \(q=0\) is finished by taking every
\(g_j=0\).  We henceforth assume \(q\geq1\).

Put \(r:=\dim A\).  If \(r=0\), then \(K\) is a singleton.  With relative
Lebesgue measure interpreted as unit mass at that point, the numbers
\(f_j\) themselves already give the conclusion.  Assume \(r\geq1\).

Define the pointwise simplex

\[
 \Delta_q:=
 \left\{p=(p_1,\ldots,p_q)\in[0,1]^q:
 \sum_{j=1}^qp_j\leq1\right\},
 \qquad
 p_0:=1-\sum_{j=1}^qp_j,
\tag{7}
\]

and the weak-star feasible set

\[
 \mathcal U:=
 \left\{u=(u_1,\ldots,u_q)\in L^\infty(K,\lambda_A)^q:
 u(x)\in\Delta_q\text{ almost everywhere}\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

For \(u\in\mathcal U\), let

\[
 Tu:=
 \left(
 \int_Ku_j\,d\lambda_A,
 \int_Kxu_j(x)\,d\lambda_A(x)
 \right)_{j=1}^q
 \in E:=(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^d)^q,
\tag{9}
\]

and set

\[
 \mathcal M:=T(\mathcal U)\subseteq E.
\tag{10}
\]

The set \(\mathcal U\) is weak-star compact.  Indeed, it is contained in
the unit ball of \(L^\infty(K)^q\), and it is weak-star closed: positivity
of every coordinate and nonnegativity of \(1-\sum_ju_j\) are characterized
by integration against nonnegative \(L^1\) test functions.  Banach-Alaoglu
therefore applies.  Since \(L^1(K,\lambda_A)\) is separable, this compactness
is sequential on the bounded set under consideration.  The map \(T\) is
weak-star continuous because \(1\) and every coordinate of \(x\) belong to
\(L^1(K,\lambda_A)\).  Consequently \(\mathcal M\) is a nonempty compact
convex subset of the finite-dimensional space \(E\).  Notice that all
relative interiors below are taken in \(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M\),
not in the possibly larger ambient space \(E\).

For \(p\in\Delta_q\), define multinomial entropy by

\[
 \eta(p):=-\sum_{j=0}^qp_j\log p_j,
 \qquad 0\log0:=0,
\tag{11}
\]

and put

\[
 \mathcal H(u):=\int_K\eta(u(x))\,d\lambda_A(x).
\tag{12}
\]

The functional \(\mathcal H\) is weak-star upper semicontinuous on
\(\mathcal U\).  Here is a direct verification.  The convex function
\(\varphi:=-\eta\) has the conjugate representation

\[
 \varphi(p)=
 \sup_{a\in\mathbb R^q}
 \left\{
 \langle a,p\rangle-
 \log\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^qe^{a_j}\right)
 \right\}.
\tag{13}
\]

The supremum may be restricted to \(a\in\mathbb Q^q\).  Enumerate those
rational vectors and take the increasing maxima of the first \(k\) affine
minorants in (13).  For fixed \(k\), the integral of that pointwise maximum
is the supremum over measurable partitions of \(K\) into \(k\) pieces of
the integrals of the selected affine minorants.  Each such integral is a
weak-star continuous affine functional of \(u\), because its coefficient
is a bounded simple \(L^1\) function.  Monotone convergence in \(k\) now
shows that

\[
 -\mathcal H(u)=\int_K\varphi(u(x))\,d\lambda_A(x)
\tag{14}
\]

is a supremum of weak-star continuous affine functionals.  It is therefore
weak-star lower semicontinuous, proving the claim about \(\mathcal H\).

For \(y\in\mathcal M\), the fiber

\[
 \mathcal U_y:=\{u\in\mathcal U:Tu=y\}
\tag{15}
\]

is nonempty and weak-star compact.  Thus (12) attains its maximum on every
fiber.  Since \(\eta\) is strictly concave on \(\Delta_q\), this maximizer
is unique up to equality almost everywhere.  Define its value by

\[
 V(y):=\max_{u\in\mathcal U_y}\mathcal H(u).
\tag{16}
\]

The finite-valued function \(V\) is concave on \(\mathcal M\): convexly
combining two maximizing families gives a feasible family for the combined
moment vector, and concavity of \(\eta\) gives the required inequality.

We next solve every relative-interior fiber.  Fix
\(y\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M\).  A finite concave function on a
finite-dimensional convex set has a supergradient at each relative-interior
point.  Hence there is a linear functional \(\ell\) on
\(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M-\operatorname{aff}\mathcal M\) such that

\[
 V(z)\leq V(y)+\ell(z-y)
 \quad(z\in\mathcal M).
\tag{17}
\]

Extend \(\ell\) linearly to \(E\).  Such an extension is always possible in
finite dimension.  Writing the extension color by color, choose
\(c_j\in\mathbb R\) and \(b_j\in\mathbb R^d\) so that

\[
 \ell(Tu)=
 \int_K\sum_{j=1}^q
 \bigl(c_j+\langle b_j,x\rangle\bigr)u_j(x)
 \,d\lambda_A(x).
\tag{18}
\]

The multipliers need not be unique when \(A\) or \(\mathcal M\) is
lower-dimensional.  Existence of one extension is all that is required.

Let \(u^y\) be the maximizer in (16).  For arbitrary \(u\in\mathcal U\),
(17) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal H(u)-\ell(Tu)
 &\leq V(Tu)-\ell(Tu)\\
 &\leq V(y)-\ell(y)\\
 &=\mathcal H(u^y)-\ell(Tu^y).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Thus \(u^y\) maximizes the unconstrained integral functional on the left of
(19) over all of \(\mathcal U\).  At a fixed \(x\in K\), the strictly
concave pointwise objective is

\[
 \eta(p)-\sum_{j=1}^q
 \bigl(c_j+\langle b_j,x\rangle\bigr)p_j,
 \qquad p\in\Delta_q.
\tag{20}
\]

Its unique maximizer is the interior point

\[
 u_j^y(x)=
 \frac{
 \exp\bigl(a_j+\langle\beta_j,x\rangle\bigr)
 }{
 1+\sum_{k=1}^q
 \exp\bigl(a_k+\langle\beta_k,x\rangle\bigr)
 },
 \qquad
 a_j:=-c_j,
 \quad
 \beta_j:=-b_j.
\tag{21}
\]

Indeed, the missing-color coordinate is

\[
 u_0^y(x)=
 \frac1{
 1+\sum_{k=1}^q
 \exp\bigl(a_k+\langle\beta_k,x\rangle\bigr)
 },
\tag{22}
\]

and the stationarity equations are

\[
 \log\frac{u_0^y(x)}{u_j^y(x)}
 =c_j+\langle b_j,x\rangle.
\tag{23}
\]

The pointwise maximizer in (21) is measurable and belongs to
\(\mathcal U\).  It maximizes the integrand in (20) at every point.  Hence
the nonnegative pointwise gap between it and any other family integrates
to zero only when the two families agree almost everywhere.  This proves
both (21) and \(Tu^y=y\); it also supplies the multiplier existence rather
than merely assuming Euler-Lagrange equations.

For every \(j\), let

\[
 \Psi(x):=
 \log\left(
 1+\sum_{k=1}^q
 e^{a_k+\langle\beta_k,x\rangle}
 \right).
\tag{24}
\]

The log-sum-exp function \(\Psi\) is convex on \(A\).  Therefore

\[
 \log u_j^y(x)
 =a_j+\langle\beta_j,x\rangle-\Psi(x)
\tag{25}
\]

is concave on \(K\).  Since \(K\) is convex, extending \(u_j^y\) by zero
outside \(K\) preserves log-concavity.  Equations (21)-(22) also give the
strict relative-interior capacity inequality

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^qu_j^y(x)=1-u_0^y(x)<1
 \quad(x\in K).
\tag{26}
\]

It remains to treat an arbitrary, possibly boundary, target moment vector.
Let

\[
 y_*:=((s_j,m_j))_{j=1}^q\in\mathcal M
\tag{27}
\]

be the vector supplied by the original jointly feasible family.  Choose
\(y^\circ\in\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M\), which exists for every nonempty
finite-dimensional convex set, and let

\[
 y_n:=\left(1-\frac1n\right)y_*+\frac1n y^\circ
 \quad(n\geq2).
\tag{28}
\]

Every \(y_n\) belongs to \(\operatorname{ri}\mathcal M\).  To see this
directly, take a relative ball of radius \(\rho>0\) about \(y^\circ\) inside
\(\mathcal M\).  Its convex combination with \(y_*\) in the proportions
shown in (28) is a relative ball of radius \(\rho/n\) about \(y_n\) inside
\(\mathcal M\).

Let \(u^{(n)}\in\mathcal U\) be the multinomial-logistic family (21) with
moment vector \(y_n\).  By sequential weak-star compactness and finiteness
of the color set, pass to one common subsequence such that

\[
 u_j^{(n)}\mathrel{\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}}\bar g_j
 \quad\text{in }L^\infty(K,\lambda_A)
 \quad(1\leq j\leq q).
\tag{29}
\]

Weak-star closedness of \(\mathcal U\) gives

\[
 \bar g_j\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^q\bar g_j\leq1
 \quad\text{almost everywhere on }K.
\tag{30}
\]

Weak-star continuity of \(T\), together with \(y_n\to y_*\), gives

\[
 \int_K\bar g_j\,d\lambda_A=s_j,
 \qquad
 \int_Kx\bar g_j(x)\,d\lambda_A(x)=m_j.
\tag{31}
\]

This step is why the limit is taken in the weak-star topology of
\(L^\infty\), rather than only as an unspecified weak limit of measures:
it retains densities bounded by the same common capacity and cannot create
a singular component on a lower-dimensional face.

We now prove and apply the closure statement needed to choose simultaneous
pointwise representatives.  Extend every \(u_j^{(n)}\) and \(\bar g_j\) by
zero to \(A\), and write

\[
 d\mu_{j,n}:=u_j^{(n)}\,d\lambda_A,
 \qquad
 d\mu_j:=\bar g_j\,d\lambda_A.
\tag{32}
\]

Then \(\mu_{j,n}\) converges weakly to \(\mu_j\), because (29) applies in
particular to every continuous test function on the compact set \(K\).
Each \(\mu_{j,n}\) has a log-concave density.  We record why its weak limit
has a log-concave density representative.

First, if \(C,D\subseteq A\) are compact and \(0<t<1\), the
Prekopa-Leindler inequality applied to the log-concave density gives

\[
 \mu_{j,n}((1-t)C+tD)
 \geq
 \mu_{j,n}(C)^{1-t}\mu_{j,n}(D)^t.
\tag{33}
\]

Let \(G,H\subseteq A\) be open.  By inner regularity, choose compact
continuity sets \(C\subseteq G\) and \(D\subseteq H\) whose \(\mu_j\)-masses
are arbitrarily close to those of \(G\) and \(H\).  Weak convergence gives
\(\mu_{j,n}(C)\to\mu_j(C)\) and
\(\mu_{j,n}(D)\to\mu_j(D)\).  Since
\((1-t)C+tD\) is closed, the closed-set part of the Portmanteau theorem and
(33) yield

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_j((1-t)G+tH)
 &\geq \mu_j((1-t)C+tD)\\
 &\geq\limsup_n\mu_{j,n}((1-t)C+tD)\\
 &\geq\mu_j(C)^{1-t}\mu_j(D)^t.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]

Approximating the two open-set masses proves

\[
 \mu_j((1-t)G+tH)
 \geq\mu_j(G)^{1-t}\mu_j(H)^t.
\tag{35}
\]

For \(x\in A\) and \(\rho>0\), let \(B_A(x,\rho)\) be the open Euclidean
ball in \(A\).  Its \(\lambda_A\)-volume \(v_\rho\) is independent of its
center.  Put

\[
 Q_{j,\rho}(x):=
 \frac{\mu_j(B_A(x,\rho))}{v_\rho}.
\tag{36}
\]

Because

\[
 (1-t)B_A(x,\rho)+tB_A(y,\rho)
 =B_A((1-t)x+ty,\rho),
\tag{37}
\]

equation (35) says exactly that \(Q_{j,\rho}\) is log-concave on \(A\).
Fix one sequence \(\rho_k\downarrow0\), common to all colors, and define

\[
 g_j(x):=\liminf_{k\to\infty}Q_{j,\rho_k}(x).
\tag{38}
\]

The pointwise lower limit of nonnegative log-concave functions is
log-concave.  Indeed, applying log-concavity before taking the lower limit
and using

\[
 \liminf_k(a_k^{1-t}b_k^t)
 \geq(\liminf_ka_k)^{1-t}(\liminf_kb_k)^t
\tag{39}
\]

gives (5).  By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,
\(g_j=\bar g_j\) almost everywhere.  Thus (31) also holds for \(g_j\), and
each \(g_j\) is supported on \(K\): if \(x\notin K\), compactness and
closedness of \(K\) make all sufficiently small balls in (36) disjoint
from \(K\).

The same representatives preserve the common capacity everywhere, not
merely almost everywhere.  Extending (30) by zero outside \(K\), for every
\(x\in A\) and every \(k\) one has

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^qQ_{j,\rho_k}(x)
 =\frac1{v_{\rho_k}}
 \int_{B_A(x,\rho_k)}\sum_{j=1}^q\bar g_j\,d\lambda_A
 \leq1.
\tag{40}
\]

Since the sum is finite, (38) and the elementary lower-limit inequality
give

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^qg_j(x)
 =\sum_{j=1}^q\liminf_kQ_{j,\rho_k}(x)
 \leq\liminf_k\sum_{j=1}^qQ_{j,\rho_k}(x)
 \leq1.
\tag{41}
\]

Every retained color has \(\int_Kg_j=s_j>0\), so it is a nonzero
log-concave density.  Finally restore each discarded zero-mass color as
\(g_j\equiv0\).  Equations (3)-(5) and all stated conventions follow.
