## Statement

Let h,M be positive integers with M>=h, and let t>0. Put
\[
D=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}:0\le i\le M,\ hj\le i\},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{(i,j)\in D}\exp(t(j-i/h)),
\qquad
p_{(i,j)}=Z^{-1}\exp(t(j-i/h))
\]
on D, with p zero elsewhere. Define the original parity masses and canonical law by
\[
\pi_{\mathbf b}=\sum_{\mathbf I\equiv\mathbf b\pmod2}p_{\mathbf I},
\qquad
q^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbf K}
=\sum_{\mathbf I:\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}>0}
\frac{p_{\mathbf I}p_{2\mathbf K-\mathbf I}}{\pi_{\mathbf I\bmod2}}.
\]
This is exactly the original-mass canonical operator of accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`, without conditioning an independent original pair globally on a parity match. Let
\[
\delta=\operatorname{TV}(p,q^{\mathrm{can}})
=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf I}|p_{\mathbf I}-q^{\mathrm{can}}_{\mathbf I}|,
\qquad m=\max_{\mathbf I}p_{\mathbf I},
\qquad N=\lfloor M/h\rfloor.
\]
Write \([u]_+=\max\{u,0\}\). Let \(\kappa\in(0,1]\) be precisely the absolute one-dimensional separation constant appearing in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d`. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta&\ge\frac{[\kappa-3(N+1)m]_+}{1+4N^2}
&&\text{if }1\le N\le3,\\
\delta&\ge\frac{2}{225}(1-e^{-t})^2
&&\text{if }N\ge4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, for all the parameters above,
\[
\delta\ge
\min\left\{\frac{[\kappa-12m]_+}{37},\frac{2}{225}(1-e^{-t})^2\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently every sequence \((h_k,M_k,t_k)\) in this family with \(m_k\to0\) and \(t_k\to\infty\) satisfies
\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\operatorname{TV}(p_k,q_k^{\mathrm{can}})
\ge\min\{\kappa/37,2/225\}>0.
\tag{3}
\]
There is no additional condition on \(h_k/t_k\) or \(M_k/h_k\). No integral-mean condition is imposed.

## Proof

Use the unimodular coordinate map
\[
(R,J)=(i-hj,j),\qquad (i,j)=(R+hJ,J).
\]
It carries D bijectively to
\[
E=\{(R,J)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{2}:R+hJ\le M\}.
\]
The original root law in these coordinates is its pushforward
\[
P_{(R,J)}=p_{(R+hJ,J)}=Z^{-1}a^R,
\qquad a=e^{-t/h}\in(0,1).
\]
This coordinate change is not conditioning or replacement of the root law. In particular,
\[
Z=\sum_{J=0}^{N}\sum_{R=0}^{M-hJ}a^R,
\qquad m=Z^{-1},
\tag{4}
\]
because every weight is at most one and the sites \((R,J)=(0,J)\), \(0\le J\le N\), have weight one. Thus the stated largest-atom condition is the original largest-atom condition exactly.

Both D and E are complete lattice supports. Indeed each is the set of lattice points of its displayed convex triangle; its convex hull lies in that triangle, so intersecting its convex hull with the lattice gives the same support. They have affine rank two because M>=h>=1. The map above has determinant one and is an integer lattice bijection. Modulo two it carries the original parity label to
\[
(R\bmod2,J\bmod2)=(i-hj\bmod2,j\bmod2)
\]
bijectively. It commutes with midpoint formation, so it carries the original canonical law to the canonical law Q of P, with the transformed original parity masses
\[
\Pi_{c,d}=\sum_{R\equiv c,\ J\equiv d\pmod2}P_{(R,J)}.
\]
Total variation is unchanged. The exact aliases to the log-affine root law and its canonical law in accepted fact `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` are \((D,p,\boldsymbol\theta,q^{\mathrm{can}})=(E,P,(-t/h,0),Q)\), with the same Z and largest atom m. To distinguish the coordinate J from the midpoint-vector notation of that predecessor, write the canonical midpoint as \(\mathbf W=(\mathbf X+\mathbf Y)/2\).

If N<=3, swap the coordinates so that J is the first coordinate. This is another unimodular lattice bijection, and the support has first-coordinate range \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\). The bounded-width estimate in `b09fd2c6d7d7ab1d` applies with \(\omega=N\), giving the first line of (1).

Suppose henceforth N>=4. The canonical sampling is as follows: select \(\mathbf B=(c,d)\) with probability \(\Pi_{c,d}\); conditional on this label select \(\mathbf X,\mathbf Y\) independently with the law P conditioned on that full parity class; then average them. Completeness puts the average in E. Both endpoint marginal laws are the original P. For the second coordinate, conditional independence gives the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E_P[J^2]-\mathbb E_Q[J^2]
=\frac12\sum_{c,d}\Pi_{c,d}
\operatorname{Var}(X_J\mid\mathbf B=(c,d)).
\tag{5}
\]
This is also the second-coordinate covariance decrement of the declared predecessor: the common mean is preserved. Since every endpoint and midpoint has second coordinate between zero and N, the function \((R,J)\mapsto J^2/N^2\) takes values in [0,1]. For any two finite probability laws, the expectation difference of a function taking values in [0,1] has absolute value at most their total variation, by summing its values against the positive and negative parts of the signed difference. Therefore (5) implies
\[
\delta\ge\frac{1}{2N^2}\sum_{c,d}\Pi_{c,d}
\operatorname{Var}(X_J\mid\mathbf B=(c,d)).
\tag{6}
\]

We bound each variance without assuming a lower bound for its parity mass. For \(c\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
A_c(L)=\sum_{\substack{0\le R\le L\\R\equiv c\pmod2}}a^R,
\qquad A_c(\infty)=\frac{a^c}{1-a^2}.
\]
If the finite sum is empty it is zero. For L>=h>=1 it is nonempty for both values of c, and its geometric-series formula is
\[
A_c(L)=A_c(\infty)
\left(1-a^{2(\lfloor(L-c)/2\rfloor+1)}\right).
\]
The exponent in this formula is at least L for each c. Consequently, with \(s=1-e^{-t}=1-a^h\),
\[
sA_c(\infty)\le A_c(L)\le A_c(\infty)
\qquad(L\ge h).
\tag{7}
\]
For the nonfinal levels \(0\le J\le N-1\), their upper bound \(L_J=M-hJ\) is at least h, so (7) applies. For the final level J=N, its sum is still at most \(A_c(\infty)\), whether or not that R parity is occupied.

Fix c,d, and let
\[
B_d=\{j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}:j\equiv d\pmod2\},
\qquad n_d=|B_d|.
\]
Because N>=4 and every nonfinal level contains both R parities, all four full parity classes are occupied. The conditional second-coordinate probabilities are exactly
\[
\Pr(X_J=j\mid\mathbf B=(c,d))
=\frac{A_c(M-hj)}{
\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{0\le u\le N\\u\equiv d\pmod2}}A_c(M-hu)}
\qquad(j\equiv d\pmod2).
\tag{8}
\]
There are \(n_d\) nonfinal levels and at most one final level in the denominator. Equations (7)-(8) therefore give
\[
\Pr(X_J=j\mid\mathbf B=(c,d))\ge\frac{s}{n_d+1}
\qquad(j\in B_d).
\tag{9}
\]
This is a conditional probability calculation within the original full parity class; its class-selection weight in (6) remains \(\Pi_{c,d}\), not a squared or renormalized parity mass.

Write n=n_d. The set \(B_d\) is an arithmetic progression of n integer sites with spacing two. The uniform law on it has variance \((n^2-1)/3\), as follows directly from the finite sums of the first n integers and their squares. The pairwise formula for variance and (9), retaining only pairs in \(B_d\), imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X_J\mid\mathbf B=(c,d))
&=\frac12\sum_{u,v}
\Pr(X_J=u\mid\mathbf B)\Pr(X_J=v\mid\mathbf B)(u-v)^2\\
&\ge\frac{s^2}{2(n+1)^2}\sum_{u,v\in B_d}(u-v)^2\\
&=s^2\frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2}\frac{n^2-1}{3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
For N>=4, both \(n_d\) are at least two and at least \(2N/5\). For even N the latter follows from \(n_d=N/2\); for odd \(N=2l+1\ge5\), the smaller count is l>=2 and \(l\ge2(2l+1)/5\). Also, for n>=2,
\[
\frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2}\ge\frac49,
\qquad n^2-1\ge\frac34n^2.
\]
Thus (10) yields, for every full parity class,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X_J\mid\mathbf B=(c,d))
\ge\frac{s^2n_d^2}{9}
\ge\frac{4s^2N^2}{225}.
\]
The original parity masses sum to one. Substitution into (6) proves
\(\delta\ge2s^2/225\), which is the second line of (1).

For N<=3, \(3(N+1)\le12\) and \(1+4N^2\le37\), so the first line of (1) implies \(\delta\ge[\kappa-12m]_+/37\). For N>=4, its second line holds. In either case \(\delta\) is at least the minimum in (2). Along any sequence with the two assumed limits, that minimum converges to the positive constant in (3). This proves the uniform asymptotic separation even when the two parameter regimes alternate. All calculations, including (4), (5), and the parity masses in (6), concern the original root law or its bijective pushforward.
