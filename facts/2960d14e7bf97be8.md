## Statement

For an integer \(r\ge1\), put
\[
T_r:=\left\{\mathbf y\in\mathbb R_+^r:\sum_{s=1}^r y_s\le1\right\}.
\]
For every integer \(k\ge1\), set
\[
r=r_k:=k^2,
\qquad
M=M_k:=64(r+1)^2,
\qquad
T:=T_r^k.
\tag{1}
\]
Write a point of \(T\) as \(\mathbf y=(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_k)\), with \(\mathbf y_i=(y_{i,1},\ldots,y_{i,r})\in T_r\), and put
\[
X_i(\mathbf y_i):=r y_{i,1}.
\]
Define the convex body
\[
C_k:=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{kr}:
\mathbf y\in T,\quad 0\le x_i\le1+M X_i(\mathbf y_i)
\text{ for }1\le i\le k\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the integer dimension is \(n=k\), the continuous dimension is \(d=kr=k^3\), every minus roof is zero, and the plus roofs \(M r y_{i,1}\) are affine. Let
\[
S_k:=C_k\cap(\mathbb Z^k\times\mathbb R^{k^3}),
\qquad
W_k:=\mathcal H_{k^3}(S_k).
\]
For \(\mathbf p\in S_k\), write
\[
h_{S_k}(\mathbf p):=
\inf\left\{\mathcal H_{k^3}(S_k\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+k^3}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf p\in H\right\}.
\]
For \(\mathbf J\in\mathbb Z^k\), define its reflected-count density by
\[
R_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf y)
:=\#\left\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^k:
(\mathbf I,\mathbf y),(2\mathbf J-\mathbf I,\mathbf y)\in C_k\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

The simultaneous roof-threshold caps in this statement are exactly the sets
\[
K_{\mathbf t}:=\left\{\mathbf y\in T:
X_i(\mathbf y_i)\ge t_i\text{ for every }i\right\}
=\prod_{i=1}^k\left\{\mathbf y_i\in T_r:X_i(\mathbf y_i)\ge t_i\right\},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(0\le t_i<r\); these and only these thresholds have positive \(k^3\)-dimensional volume after thresholds below zero are replaced by zero. For \(0<\alpha<1\), set
\[
\mathcal Q_{\alpha,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)
:=
\frac{\left(\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}R_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf y)^\alpha\,d\mathbf y\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{|K_{\mathbf t}|^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}},
\qquad
\Psi_{\alpha,k}
:=\sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf t}
\frac{\mathcal Q_{\alpha,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)}{2^{-k}W_k}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then, for every fixed \(0<\alpha<1\),
\[
\Psi_{\alpha,k}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(k\to\infty),
\tag{6}
\]
and the convergence is eventually exponential in \(k\).

More strongly, call \(\gamma:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)\) a valid dimension-uniform fractional-depth coefficient if, for every \(0<\alpha<1\), every integer \(D\ge1\), every convex body \(B\subset\mathbb R^D\), and every nonnegative integrable \(\rho\) vanishing almost everywhere outside \(B\),
\[
\max_{\mathbf y\in B}\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)
\ge
\gamma(\alpha)
\frac{\left(\int_B\rho^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{|B|^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}},
\tag{7}
\]
where \(\tau_\rho(\mathbf y)\) is the infimum of \(\int_H\rho\) over proper closed halfspaces \(H\subset\mathbb R^D\) containing \(\mathbf y\). For every such \(\gamma\),
\[
\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\ \sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf t}
\frac{\gamma(\alpha)\mathcal Q_{\alpha,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)}
{2^{-k}W_k}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]

Nevertheless, for every \(k\) there are one lattice center \(\mathbf J_k^\star\in\mathbb Z^k\), one nonzero compactly supported log-concave density \(q_k\) on \(\mathbb R^{k^3}\), and its barycenter \(\mathbf y_k^\star\), such that
\[
0\le q_k\le R_{\mathbf J_k^\star},
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}q_k\subseteq
\{\mathbf y:(\mathbf J_k^\star,\mathbf y)\in C_k\},
\qquad
\int q_k\ge2^{-k}W_k,
\tag{9}
\]
and the fixed mixed point \(\mathbf p_k^\star=(\mathbf J_k^\star,\mathbf y_k^\star)\in S_k\) satisfies
\[
h_{S_k}(\mathbf p_k^\star)
\ge\frac1{2e}\int q_k
\ge\frac1{2e}\,2^{-k}W_k.
\tag{10}
\]
The point in (10) is selected before the containing ambient halfspace, and \(W_k\) is the original mixed-integer mass of the body (2), with no discarded mass.

## Proof

The set \(T_r\) is a compact full-dimensional simplex, and (2) is cut out over \(T_r^k\) by affine inequalities. It is compact and convex and contains a product of nonempty open sets in its ambient space. Thus \(C_k\) is indeed a convex body of the stated dimensions. Its integer section is finite at every continuous point and contains \(\{0,1\}^k\), so all displayed mixed masses are finite and positive.

First record a scalar strict gap. Let \(E\) have the exponential distribution of mean one and, for \(j,s,t\ge0\), define
\[
u_j(s):=2\min\{j,(s-j)_+\},
\qquad
F_\alpha(j,t):=e^{-t}
\left(\mathbb E\,u_j(t+E)^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha},
\qquad
\beta_\alpha:=\sup_{j,t\ge0}F_\alpha(j,t).
\tag{11}
\]
We claim that
\[
0<\beta_\alpha<\frac12
\qquad(0<\alpha<1).
\tag{12}
\]
Because the \(L^p\) power mean is nondecreasing in \(p\), memorylessness gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\alpha(j,t)
&\le e^{-t}\mathbb E u_j(t+E)\\
&=\mathbb E\!\left[u_j(E)\mathbf1_{\{E\ge t\}}\right]\\
&\le\mathbb E u_j(E)
=2(e^{-j}-e^{-2j})
\le\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The final equality follows by integrating separately over \([j,2j]\) and \([2j,\infty)\). The last inequality is an equality only at \(j=\log2\). At this value of \(j\), if \(t>j\), the second inequality in (13) is strict because it deletes a positive interval on which \(u_j>0\). If \(t\le j\), the random variable \(u_j(t+E)\) is not almost surely constant, so the first inequality in (13) is strict for \(\alpha<1\). Hence every individual \(F_\alpha(j,t)\) is strictly below \(1/2\).

It remains to exclude an escaping maximizing sequence. The elementary pointwise bounds \(u_j(s)\le2j\) and \(u_j(s)\le s\), together with (13), give
\[
F_\alpha(j,t)\le2j,
\qquad
F_\alpha(j,t)\le2e^{-j},
\qquad
F_\alpha(j,t)\le(t+1)e^{-t}.
\tag{14}
\]
The first bound excludes \(j\downarrow0\), the second excludes \(j\to\infty\), and the third excludes \(t\to\infty\). Since \(F_\alpha\) is continuous and is positive, for example at \((j,t)=(1,0)\), its supremum is attained at finite \(j>0,t\ge0\). The pointwise strictness above proves (12).

We next pass to the exact finite-dimensional scalar model. If \(\mathbf Y\) is uniform on \(T_r\) and \(X=rY_1\), then, for \(0\le t<r\),
\[
\mathbb P(X\ge t)=\left(1-\frac tr\right)^r,
\qquad
m_r:=\mathbb EX=\frac r{r+1},
\tag{15}
\]
and, for \(s\ge0\),
\[
\mathbb P(X-t\ge s\mid X\ge t)
=\left(1-\frac{s}{r-t}\right)_+^r
\le e^{-s},
\qquad
\mathbb P(X\ge t)\le e^{-t}.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(X-t\), conditional on \(X\ge t\), is stochastically dominated by \(E\). Since \(u_j\) is nondecreasing in its scalar argument, (11) and (16) yield, uniformly in \(r,j,t\),
\[
\mathbb P(X\ge t)
\left(\mathbb E[u_j(X)^\alpha\mid X\ge t]\right)^{1/\alpha}
\le\beta_\alpha.
\tag{17}
\]

For a scalar fiber \(\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor Mx\rfloor+1\}\), let \(R_{J,M}(x)\) be the number of its integer sites whose reflection about \(J\) remains in the same fiber. Direct endpoint counting gives
\[
R_{J,M}(x)=
\begin{cases}
2\min\{J,\lfloor Mx\rfloor+1-J\}+1,
&J\ge0\text{ and }J\le\lfloor Mx\rfloor+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
\]
For all \(x\ge0\) and \(J\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\),
\[
0\le \frac1M R_{J,M}(x)-u_{J/M}(x)\le\frac3M,
\tag{19}
\]
while \(R_{J,M}\equiv0\) for \(J<0\), so such centers never contribute. To verify (19), put \(L=\lfloor Mx\rfloor+1\). If \(J>L\), then \(J/M>x\), so both sides are zero. If \(J\le L\), then
\[
\frac1M R_{J,M}(x)
=2\min\left\{\frac JM,\frac{L-J}{M}\right\}+\frac1M.
\]
The second entry of this minimum lies between \((x-J/M)_+\) and \((x-J/M)_++1/M\); applying the one-Lipschitz map \(v\mapsto\min\{J/M,v\}\) proves (19).

For \(0<\alpha<1\), define the scalar probability-normalized score
\[
Q_{r,M,\alpha}(J,t)
:=
\frac{\left(\mathbb E[R_{J,M}(X)^\alpha
\mathbf1_{\{X\ge t\}}]\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{\mathbb P(X\ge t)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}}.
\tag{20}
\]
Equivalently, this equals \(\mathbb P(X\ge t)\) times the conditional \(L^\alpha\) power mean of \(R_{J,M}(X)\). From (19), \((a+b)^\alpha\le a^\alpha+b^\alpha\), and (17),
\[
\left(\frac{Q_{r,M,\alpha}(J,t)}M\right)^\alpha
\le\beta_\alpha^\alpha+\left(\frac3M\right)^\alpha
\tag{21}
\]
for every \(J\in\mathbb Z\) and \(0\le t<r\). For \(J<0\) this is immediate from \(R_{J,M}=0\). For \(J\ge0\), after conditioning on \(X\ge t\), the contribution of \(u_{J/M}\) is at most \(\beta_\alpha^\alpha\) by (17), while the constant rounding contribution is multiplied by \(\mathbb P(X\ge t)^\alpha\le1\).

We now tensorize (21) in the actual body. At \(\mathbf y\in T\), the integer section of (2) is exactly
\[
\prod_{i=1}^k\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor MX_i(\mathbf y_i)\rfloor+1\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently, with \(N_M(x):=\lfloor Mx\rfloor+2\),
\[
R_{\mathbf J}(\mathbf y)=\prod_{i=1}^kR_{J_i,M}(X_i),
\qquad
W_k=\left(|T_r|\,\mathbb E N_M(X)\right)^k.
\tag{23}
\]
The product formula (4) shows that both the integral and the volume normalization in (5) factor exactly:
\[
\mathcal Q_{\alpha,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)
=|T_r|^k\prod_{i=1}^kQ_{r,M,\alpha}(J_i,t_i),
\qquad
\frac{\mathcal Q_{\alpha,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)}{2^{-k}W_k}
=\prod_{i=1}^k
\frac{2Q_{r,M,\alpha}(J_i,t_i)}{\mathbb E N_M(X)},
\]
Also,
\[
\mathbb E N_M(X)\ge M\mathbb EX=M m_r.
\tag{24}
\]
Therefore (21)--(24) give
\[
\Psi_{\alpha,k}
\le
\left[
\frac{2\left(\beta_\alpha^\alpha+(3/M_k)^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{m_{r_k}}
\right]^k.
\tag{25}
\]
For fixed \(\alpha<1\), the bracket converges to \(2\beta_\alpha<1\) by (1), (12), and \(m_r\to1\). This proves (6), including eventual exponential decay. Notice that the optimization includes the cap equal to a candidate center's whole fiber: when \(J_i\ge1\), the condition \((J_i,\mathbf y_i)\in C_k\) is \(X_i\ge(J_i-1)/M\), while for \(J_i=0\) no threshold beyond \(X_i\ge0\) is required.

We next prove the adaptive-exponent statement. Define the endpoint scores by
\[
\mathcal Q_{1,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t):=\int_{K_{\mathbf t}}R_{\mathbf J},
\qquad
\Psi_{1,k}:=\sup_{\mathbf J,\mathbf t}
\frac{\mathcal Q_{1,k}(\mathbf J,\mathbf t)}{2^{-k}W_k}.
\]
The scalar proof above remains valid at \(\alpha=1\), with
\[
\beta_1:=\sup_{j,t\ge0}e^{-t}\mathbb E u_j(t+E)=\frac12.
\]
Thus
\[
\Psi_{1,k}
\le
\left[\frac{1+6/M_k}{m_{r_k}}\right]^k
\le
\exp\left(\frac{6k}{M_k}+\frac{k}{r_k}\right)
=1+o(1).
\tag{26}
\]

Every valid \(\gamma\) in (7) satisfies
\[
0\le\gamma(\alpha)\le\frac12,
\qquad
\lim_{\alpha\uparrow1}\gamma(\alpha)=0.
\tag{27}
\]
The upper bound \(1/2\) follows by applying (7) to the uniform probability density on an interval. To prove the limit, fix an integer \(D\ge1\), a \(D\)-simplex \(B\) with vertices \(\mathbf v_0,\ldots,\mathbf v_D\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1/(D+1)\). Let \(B_i=\mathbf v_i+\varepsilon(B-\mathbf v_i)\), and let \(\rho\) place probability \(1/(D+1)\) uniformly on each \(B_i\). The corner simplexes are disjoint up to null boundaries. At every \(\mathbf y\in B\), some barycentric coordinate \(\lambda_i(\mathbf y)\) is at least \(1/(D+1)>\varepsilon\). The closed halfspace \(\{\lambda_i\ge\lambda_i(\mathbf y)\}\) contains \(\mathbf y\), excludes every \(B_j\) with \(j\ne i\), and therefore has \(\rho\)-mass at most \(1/(D+1)\). Hence \(\max_B\tau_\rho\le1/(D+1)\). On the other hand, direct integration gives
\[
\frac{\left(\int_B\rho^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}}
{|B|^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}}
=\left[(D+1)\varepsilon^D\right]^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}
\longrightarrow1
\qquad(\alpha\uparrow1).
\tag{28}
\]
Therefore \(\limsup_{\alpha\uparrow1}\gamma(\alpha)\le1/(D+1)\). Letting \(D\to\infty\) proves (27).

For each fixed nonzero density on a positive-volume cap, its \(L^\alpha\) probability power mean is nondecreasing in \(\alpha\). Therefore \(\Psi_{\alpha,k}\) is nondecreasing in \(\alpha\). Given \(\epsilon>0\), use (27) to choose \(\delta\in(0,1)\) such that \(\gamma(\alpha)\le\epsilon\) for \(1-\delta\le\alpha<1\). Then
\[
\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\gamma(\alpha)\Psi_{\alpha,k}
\le
\max\left\{
\frac12\Psi_{1-\delta,k},
\epsilon\Psi_{1,k}
\right\}.
\tag{29}
\]
The first term tends to zero by (6), and the second has limit superior at most \(\epsilon\) by (26). Since \(\epsilon\) is arbitrary, (29) proves (8).

It remains to construct the nonuniform reference and prove the actual-depth calibration. For one block, define
\[
G_r(j):=\mathbb E u_j(X)
=2\int_j^{2j}\left(1-\frac sr\right)_+^r ds,
\qquad
j_r:=\frac{r(2^{1/r}-1)}{2\,2^{1/r}-1}.
\tag{30}
\]
where the integral identity follows from
\(u_j(s)=2[(s-j)_+-(s-2j)_+]\) and tail integration. Put
\(a_r=2^{1/r}\). Then \(0<2j_r<r\),
\[
1-\frac{j_r}{r}=\frac{a_r}{2a_r-1},
\qquad
1-\frac{2j_r}{r}=\frac1{2a_r-1}.
\]
Direct integration, using \(a_r^r=2\), gives
\[
G_r(j_r)=m_r\frac{2}{(2\,2^{1/r}-1)^r}.
\tag{31}
\]
We need a precise gain over \(m_r/2\). Put \(a=(\log2)/r\) and \(z=1-e^{-a}\). Since \(0<a<1\),
\[
z\ge\frac a2\ge\frac1{4r},
\qquad
(2e^a-1)^r=4(1-z^2)^r.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac2{(2e^a-1)^r}
=\frac12(1-z^2)^{-r}
\ge\frac12+\frac r2z^2
\ge\frac12+\frac1{32r}.
\]
Multiplying by \(m_r=r/(r+1)\) in (31) yields
\[
G_r(j_r)
\ge\frac{m_r}{2}+\frac1{32(r+1)}.
\tag{32}
\]

Choose an integer \(J_0\) nearest to \(M j_r\), and on \(T_r\) define
\[
q_0(\mathbf y):=M u_{J_0/M}(X(\mathbf y))
=2\left[\min\{J_0,MX(\mathbf y)-J_0\}\right]_+.
\tag{33}
\]
The function \(j\mapsto u_j(s)\) is \(2\)-Lipschitz for every fixed \(s\), so nearest-integer rounding and (32) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E q_0
&\ge M G_r(j_r)-1\\
&\ge\frac{M m_r}{2}+\frac{M}{32(r+1)}-1\\
&\ge\frac{M m_r}{2}+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Here the last inequality follows from \(M=64(r+1)^2\). Since \(N_M(X)=\lfloor MX\rfloor+2\le MX+2\),
\[
\mathbb E q_0\ge\frac12\mathbb E N_M(X).
\tag{35}
\]

On the convex set where it is positive, \(q_0\) is the minimum of a positive constant and a positive affine function, hence is concave. A nonnegative concave function on a convex support is log-concave by the weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, and extending it by zero preserves log-concavity. Equation (19) also gives the exact pointwise minorization
\[
0\le q_0(\mathbf y)\le R_{J_0,M}(X(\mathbf y)).
\tag{36}
\]
For the product body set
\[
\mathbf J_k^\star:=(J_0,\ldots,J_0),
\qquad
q_k(\mathbf y_1,\ldots,\mathbf y_k):=\prod_{i=1}^kq_0(\mathbf y_i).
\tag{37}
\]
Products of log-concave functions on independent blocks are log-concave. Equations (23), (35), and (36) prove
\[
0\le q_k\le R_{\mathbf J_k^\star},
\qquad
\int_Tq_k
=\left(|T_r|\mathbb E q_0\right)^k
\ge2^{-k}W_k.
\tag{38}
\]
Moreover, positivity of a factor in (33) implies that \(J_0\) belongs to the corresponding integer interval in (22); the same remains true on the closure of the positive set. Thus \(\operatorname{supp}q_k\) lies in the continuous fiber over \(\mathbf J_k^\star\).

Let \(\mathbf y_k^\star\) be the barycenter of the probability density \(q_k/\int q_k\). It belongs to the compact convex support of \(q_k\), so \(\mathbf p_k^\star=(\mathbf J_k^\star,\mathbf y_k^\star)\in S_k\). The log-concave centroid halfspace theorem recorded in accepted fact `5bd39b316ef3c194` applies to this normalized, compactly supported log-concave density and gives
\[
\tau_{q_k}(\mathbf y_k^\star)
\ge e^{-1}\int q_k.
\tag{39}
\]

We finish by proving the fixed-center mixed-depth transfer directly. For each \(\mathbf z\in T\), let
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}:=
\left\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^k:
(\mathbf I,\mathbf z),(2\mathbf J_k^\star-\mathbf I,\mathbf z)\in C_k\right\}.
\]
This finite set has cardinality \(R_{\mathbf J_k^\star}(\mathbf z)\) and is invariant under \(\mathbf I\mapsto2\mathbf J_k^\star-\mathbf I\). Consider any proper closed ambient halfspace containing \(\mathbf p_k^\star\). It contains a halfspace through \(\mathbf p_k^\star\) of the form
\[
H_0:=\left\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf z):
\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf J_k^\star)
+\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y_k^\star)\le0\right\},
\qquad
(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\ne(\mathbf0,\mathbf0).
\tag{40}
\]
Whenever \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y_k^\star)\le0\), pairing each \(\mathbf I\in\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}\) with \(2\mathbf J_k^\star-\mathbf I\) shows that at least half of \(\mathcal P_{\mathbf z}\) satisfies \(\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf I-\mathbf J_k^\star)\le0\). Every such site belongs to \(H_0\). If \(\mathbf b\ne0\), integration over this continuous halfspace, followed by (38), gives
\[
\mathcal H_{k^3}(S_k\cap H_0)
\ge\frac12
\int_{\{\mathbf z:\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf y_k^\star)\le0\}}
R_{\mathbf J_k^\star}(\mathbf z)\,d\mathbf z
\ge\frac12\tau_{q_k}(\mathbf y_k^\star).
\tag{41}
\]
If \(\mathbf b=0\), the same pairing applies at every \(\mathbf z\) and gives the same final bound with \(\frac12\int R_{\mathbf J_k^\star}\ge\frac12\int q_k\). Whole-space halfspaces, if included in the convention, are immediate because \(R_{\mathbf J_k^\star}\) is bounded pointwise by the original integer-section count. Taking the infimum over all containing ambient halfspaces and using (38), (39), and (41) proves (10). This completes both the uniform-cap obstruction and the nonuniform-reference calibration on the same original bodies.
