## Statement

Let k>=1 be an integer. Let D be a finite set satisfying
$$
\lbrace 0,1\rbrace ^k\subseteq D\subseteq\lbrace 0,1,2\rbrace ^k,
\qquad D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k,
$$
and suppose every point of D has at most two coordinates equal to two. No paritywise-star assumption is imposed. For every \(\beta\in\mathbb R^k\) satisfying \(r_i:=\exp(-\beta_i)>16\) for all i, define the original affine weights and probabilities by
$$
A=D\times\lbrace 0\rbrace \subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1},\qquad
w_x=\exp(-\beta\cdot x)=\prod_i r_i^{x_i},\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in D}w_x,\qquad p_{(x,0)}=w_x/Z.
$$
Write \(p_x=p_{(x,0)}\) only as an abbreviation. Define
$$
\tau_p(z)=\inf_{\substack{H\text{ closed affine halfspace of }\mathbb R^{k+1}\\z\in H}}
\sum_{v\in A\cap H}p_v,
\qquad M_3(p)=\sum_{z\in A}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
$$
Halfspaces may be required to have nonzero normal; allowing the whole space leaves the infimum unchanged. Define the unchanged full ordered-reflection quantities
$$
s_p(c)=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in D\\x+y=2c}}\min(p_x,p_y),
\qquad T(p)=\sum_{c\in D}p_c s_p(c)^3.
$$
The reflection sum counts ordered pairs, including its diagonal once.

Let \(D_1=\lbrace x\in D:\#\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace \le1\rbrace \) be the actual one-step core, and set \(\alpha=p(D_1)\). For every nonempty coordinate block I and every actual pattern \(u\in\pi_I(D)\) containing a two, let
$$
F_u=\lbrace x\in D:x_I=u\rbrace ,\qquad
p_I(u)=p(F_u),\qquad
D_u=\lbrace x_{I^c}:x\in F_u\rbrace .
$$
Let \(q_u\) be the number of actually occupied parity classes of \(D_u\) in \(\mathbb Z^{I^c}\); in particular \(q_u=1\) if I contains all coordinates. Put
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}=
\max_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\lbrace 1,\ldots,k\rbrace }
8^k\sum_{\substack{u\in\pi_I(D)\\u\text{ contains a two}}}
\frac{p_I(u)^4}{q_u^3}.
$$
Then
$$
1\le 2^{14}\alpha+2^{10}P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4},
\qquad \alpha+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge2^{-44},
$$
and both original cubics satisfy
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-66}8^{-(k+1)},
\qquad T(p)\ge2^{-66}8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
Thus these conclusions apply, in particular, to every paritywise-star support in the registered subgoal with all activities greater than sixteen. The only accepted result used is the one-step occupied-parity assertion of f27ee487069e27e7, with exactly the conditional normalizations described below.

## Proof

Set
$$
B=\sum_{b\in\lbrace 0,1\rbrace ^k}w_b=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+r_i),
\qquad W_1=\sum_{x\in D_1}w_x.
$$
All Boolean points are actual points, so \(B\le W_1\), and \(W_1/Z=\alpha\). Simultaneously relabel coordinates, the field, and every coordinate block so that
\(r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_k>16\). This permutation preserves the probabilities, actual residual parity counts, and the maximum defining \(P_{\mathrm{par}}\). It is also an invertible affine permutation of the ambient space, so it preserves the depth and reflection quantities. The ordered indices in the next argument refer to this relabeling only.

For x having at least one two, write
$$
S(x)=\lbrace i:x_i=0\rbrace ,\qquad U(x)=\lbrace i:x_i=2\rbrace .
$$
Call x balanced if
$$
|U(x)\cap\lbrace 1,\ldots,j\rbrace |\le |S(x)\cap\lbrace 1,\ldots,j\rbrace |
\quad\text{for every }1\le j\le k.
$$
Otherwise call it unbalanced. These labels apply only in this proof and do not change any original probability. Let \(G\subseteq D\setminus\lbrace 0,1\rbrace ^k\) be the balanced points, and for \(x\in G\) put \(g_x=w_x/B\). The original normalization gives the exact identity and inequality
$$
p_x=(B/Z)g_x\le\alpha g_x. \tag{1}
$$

For a balanced point, each index in U(x) can be matched to a distinct earlier index in S(x). Indeed, scan the indices in order, recording unused zero indices, and at each two match it to an unused earlier zero. The balanced prefix condition guarantees that a zero is available. For every matched pair \(s<i\), the ordering gives \(r_s\ge r_i\). If x has \(t\in\lbrace 1,2\rbrace \) twos, then
$$
\frac{w_x}{\prod_i r_i}
=\frac{\prod_{i\in U(x)}r_i}{\prod_{s\in S(x)}r_s}
\le \prod_{s\in S(x)\text{ unmatched}}r_s^{-1}
\le16^{t-|S(x)|}.
$$
Since \(B\ge\prod_i r_i\), this proves
$$
g_x\le16^{t-|S(x)|}. \tag{2}
$$
This argument handles one-two and two-two points separately through the same matching and makes no claim that a modified point belongs to D.

Here is a uniform truncated cap, with \(C=10^4\) and \(\theta=2^{-k}\):
$$
\sum_{x\in G}\min(g_x,\theta)\le C. \tag{3}
$$
To prove it, put \(\rho=5/(4\sqrt2)<9/10\). For each nonnegative g, \(\min(g,\theta)\le\sqrt{g\theta}\). For points with exactly one two, choose its index in k ways and enlarge the allowed zero sets to all subsets of the remaining k-1 indices. Inequality (2) gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{x\in G\\|U(x)|=1}}\min(g_x,\theta)
&\le4k2^{-k/2}\sum_{S\subseteq\lbrace 1,\ldots,k-1\rbrace }4^{-|S|}\\
&=4k2^{-k/2}(5/4)^{k-1}
=2\sqrt2\,k\rho^{k-1}<300.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequality follows from
\(k\rho^{k-1}\le\sum_{n\ge1}n(9/10)^{n-1}=100\).
For points with exactly two twos, when \(k\ge2\), choose their indices in \(\binom k2\) ways and enlarge the zero sets to all subsets of the other k-2 indices. The same inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{x\in G\\|U(x)|=2}}\min(g_x,\theta)
&\le16\binom k2 2^{-k/2}(5/4)^{k-2}\\
&=4k(k-1)\rho^{k-2}\le8000,
\end{aligned}
$$
because
\(k(k-1)\rho^{k-2}\le\sum_{n\ge2}n(n-1)(9/10)^{n-2}=2000\).
There are no two-two points if k=1. These two estimates prove (3), including that case.

Split G into the points with \(g_x\le\theta\) and those with \(g_x>\theta\). By (1) and (3), the first part has original mass at most \(C\alpha\). Every point of the second part contributes \(\theta\) to (3), so this part has at most \(C2^k\) points. The full coordinate block in the definition of \(P_{\mathrm{par}}\) has \(q_x=1\) and therefore gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge8^k\sum_{x\in D\setminus\lbrace 0,1\rbrace ^k}p_x^4.
$$
Hölder on the second part of G now yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(G)
&\le C\alpha+(C2^k)^{3/4}
\left(\sum_{x\in G:g_x>\theta}p_x^4\right)^{1/4}\\
&\le C\alpha+C^{3/4}P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
$$

We next cover all unbalanced points by actual prefix sections. For such a point let j be the first prefix length at which the number of twos exceeds the number of zeros. The jth coordinate is necessarily two. Since there are at most two twos in the entire point, its prefix has one of precisely these forms:

1. The jth coordinate is the first two. There can then be no zero before j, so the prefix is \((1,\ldots,1,2)\).
2. The jth coordinate is the second two. Immediately before j there must be exactly one zero and one two. The zero must occur before the first two, because otherwise that earlier two would already have produced a positive prefix excess. Thus there are indices \(a<b<j\) at which the prefix entries are respectively zero and two, its jth entry is two, and all other prefix entries are one.

Conversely every prefix just described has its first positive excess at j, irrespective of its later completion. At each length j the number of possible prefixes is consequently
$$
N_j=1+\binom{j-1}{2},
$$
where the binomial term is zero for j=1,2. For every such prefix u that actually occurs, use the coordinate block \(I_j=\lbrace 1,\ldots,j\rbrace \) and its original section mass \(\lambda_u=p_{I_j}(u)\). It is a permitted section because u contains a two. Its actual residual parity count satisfies \(1\le q_u\le2^{k-j}\). Thus its one summand in the original score proves
$$
P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge8^k\lambda_u^4/q_u^3\ge8^j\lambda_u^4,
\qquad \lambda_u\le P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}8^{-j/4}. \tag{5}
$$
Every unbalanced point belongs to the section for its first positive prefix. Sections so obtained are disjoint: an element of a section with first positive prefix j cannot also have a different first positive prefix. Nonactual prefixes are omitted, rather than assigned any residual parity count. Summing (5) over the actual prefixes therefore gives, with \(a=8^{-1/4}<3/5\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
p(D\setminus(\lbrace 0,1\rbrace ^k\cup G))
&\le P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}\sum_{j=1}^k N_j a^j\\
&\le P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}
\left(\frac{a}{1-a}+\frac{a^3}{(1-a)^3}\right)\\
&<5P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}.
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
$$
The displayed generating function follows by summing \(\sum_{j\ge1}a^j\) and \(\sum_{j\ge3}\binom{j-1}{2}a^j\); at a=3/5 its value is \(3/2+27/8=39/8<5\).

All Boolean points lie in the actual core, so their original mass is at most \(\alpha\). Equations (4) and (6), and the partition into Boolean, balanced, and unbalanced points, imply
$$
1\le(C+1)\alpha+(C^{3/4}+5)P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}
=10001\alpha+1005P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}
\le2^{14}\alpha+2^{10}P_{\mathrm{par}}^{1/4}. \tag{7}
$$
No completeness or star hypothesis has been used in deriving (7). Either \(\alpha\ge2^{-15}\), or (7) implies \(P_{\mathrm{par}}>2^{-44}\). In the first branch \(\alpha+P_{\mathrm{par}}\ge2^{-15}\ge2^{-44}\); the second branch gives the same claimed bridge.

For completeness we prove both original cubic consumers, with all conditional fourth powers, ambient halfspaces, and actual parity counts explicit. These are the only uses of the accepted one-step assertion f27ee487069e27e7.

First fix a nonempty block I and an actual u containing a two. Let \(J=I^c\), \(s=|J|\), and \(\lambda=p_I(u)>0\). The residual support \(D_u\) is finite and nonempty. It is complete: if \(y\in\operatorname{conv}(D_u)\cap\mathbb Z^J\), then \((u,y)\in\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\), and hence \(y\in D_u\). It has at most one two, since u already contains at least one of the at most two twos. No Boolean residual point is assumed present.

In the residual ambient space \(\mathbb R^{s+1}\), define
$$
A_u=D_u\times\lbrace 0\rbrace ,\qquad
Z_u=\sum_{y\in D_u}\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y),\qquad
\sigma_{(y,0)}=\exp(-\beta_J\cdot y)/Z_u,
$$
and write \(\sigma_y=\sigma_{(y,0)}\). Place the coordinates in their original order when writing \((u,y)\), and let
\(\iota(y,v)=((u,y),v)\). Exact factorization of the original weights gives
$$
\lambda=\exp(-\beta_I\cdot u)Z_u/Z,
\qquad p_{\iota(y,0)}=\lambda\sigma_{(y,0)}. \tag{8}
$$
Define \(\tau_\sigma,M_3(\sigma),s_\sigma,T(\sigma)\) by exactly the same containing-closed-halfspace and full ordered-reflection definitions as in the statement, using \(A_u,D_u\) and this conditional affine law.

An original containing halfspace with nonzero normal has the form
$$
H=\lbrace (x,v):\ell\cdot x+tv\ge b\rbrace .
$$
Its pullback under \(\iota\) is
\(\lbrace (y,v):\ell_J\cdot y+tv\ge b-\ell_I\cdot u\rbrace \).
If \((\ell_J,t)\ne0\), this is a containing halfspace in the residual ambient space. If \((\ell_J,t)=0\), containment of \(\iota(y,0)\) implies \(b-\ell_I\cdot u\le0\); the pullback is the whole residual space and its conditional mass is one. Conversely every residual halfspace with nonzero normal extends to an original one by assigning zero coefficients on I. Thus the conditional halfspace infima agree under this affine embedding, with the whole-space case harmless. In particular, every original containing halfspace has original mass at least its section mass, so (8) proves
$$
\tau_p(\iota(y,0))\ge\lambda\tau_\sigma((y,0)),
\qquad
\sum_{x\in F_u}p_x\tau_p((x,0))^3\ge\lambda^4M_3(\sigma). \tag{9}
$$
For each center \((u,c)\in F_u\), ordered original reflected pairs whose two endpoints remain in the section are exactly the pairs \(((u,y),(u,y'))\) with \(y+y'=2c\). Their contribution to the full original reflection sum is \(\lambda s_\sigma(c)\), by (8). This includes the diagonal once, and the full reflection sum can only be larger. Hence
$$
\sum_{c\in D_u}p_{(u,c)}s_p((u,c))^3\ge\lambda^4T(\sigma). \tag{10}
$$

If s>=1 and \(D_u\) is not a singleton, apply the one-step occupied-parity assertion of f27ee487069e27e7 with dimension s, complete support \(D_u\), residual field \(\beta_J\), normalization \(Z_u\), original conditional probabilities \(\sigma\), and actual occupied parity count \(q_u\). It gives
\(M_3(\sigma),T(\sigma)\ge1/(512q_u^3)\), including every lower affine rank. If \(D_u\) is a singleton, every containing-halfspace mass is one, its only reflected pair is its diagonal with probability one, and both conditional cubics equal one. This includes s=0, with its unique vector in \(\mathbb R^0\) and \(q_u=1\). Thus the same lower bound holds in all cases.

For a fixed I, distinct pattern sections have disjoint centers. Sum (9) and (10) over its actual patterns containing a two, then take the maximum over I. This proves
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{P_{\mathrm{par}}}{512\cdot8^k}. \tag{11}
$$

Second, the actual core \(D_1\) is complete. An integer point in its convex hull is ternary. If two coordinates were both two, every positively weighted generator of that convex combination would have those same two coordinates equal to two, contrary to membership in \(D_1\). The integer point therefore has at most one two, and membership in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^k=D\) puts it in \(D_1\). Since it contains the full Boolean cube, it occupies exactly \(2^k\) parity classes. Its conditional probabilities are
$$
\widehat p_{(x,0)}=w_x/W_1=p_{(x,0)}/\alpha,
\qquad x\in D_1,
$$
with the same field and original ambient embedding. For every core center, any original containing halfspace has at least \(\alpha\) times its core conditional mass. Restricting the full ordered reflection sum to pairs with both endpoints in the actual core gives exactly \(\alpha\) times the corresponding conditional reflection sum. Multiplying by the center probability contributes one further factor \(\alpha\). The original cubic contributions from core centers are therefore at least \(\alpha^4M_3(\widehat p)\) and \(\alpha^4T(\widehat p)\), respectively. Apply the one-step assertion of f27ee487069e27e7 to \(D_1\), field \(\beta\), normalization \(W_1\), and its actual parity count \(2^k\). It gives
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge\frac{\alpha^4}{512\cdot8^k}. \tag{12}
$$

If \(\alpha\ge2^{-15}\), (12) gives
$$
M_3(p),T(p)\ge2^{-69}8^{-k}=2^{-66}8^{-(k+1)}.
$$
If \(\alpha<2^{-15}\), (7) gives \(P_{\mathrm{par}}>2^{-44}\), and (11) gives both original cubics greater than \(2^{-53}8^{-k}\), which is stronger than the same claimed bound. Every support, probability, depth, and reflection sum in these final inequalities is an original one. No paritywise-star property was used, which proves the asserted strengthening as well as the registered all-high-activity star case.
