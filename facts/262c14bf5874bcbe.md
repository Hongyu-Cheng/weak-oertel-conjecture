## Statement

For each fixed $n,d\ge1$, the best universal coefficient in
$$
G((s+t)/2)\ge 2^{-n}\sum_{r:G_r(t)>0}\Phi_d(G_r(s),G_r(t))
$$
is $2^{-n}$, even for full-dimensional simplices that are the convex hull of their actual mixed sets, with every parity surviving at $t$ and the same oblique functional $f=z_1+x_1$. More precisely, for integers $L\ge1$ take $N=2L+n+1$ and $C_N=\{z,x\ge0:\sum_i z_i+2N\sum_j x_j\le N\}$, with $s=0,t=2L$. Put $D=n+d$ and $\Omega_L=\sum_r\Phi_d(G_r(0),G_r(2L))$. Then
$$
2^{-n}\le\frac{G(L)}{\Omega_L}\le
2^{-n}\left(1+\frac{3n+1}{2L+n+1}\right)^D.
$$
For $0<\epsilon\le1$ and $L\ge D(3n+1)/\epsilon$ the upper bound is at most $2^{-n}(1+\epsilon)$. Also the targetwise collision quantity $Q$ of the preceding estimate satisfies $Q/\Omega_L\longrightarrow2^{-n}$ for any of the permitted lexicographic orders.

## Proof

The simplex vertices have integral base, and the fiber at a nonnegative integer $z$ with $\sum_i z_i\le N$ is the standard $d$-simplex scaled by $(N-\sum_i z_i)/(2N)$. On the body $0\le x_1\le1/2$, so each integral threshold $q$ of the oblique $f$ retains precisely the fibers with $z_1\ge q$. For $b>0$ put $U_b=(1/d!)\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z_+^n}(b-\sum_i z_i)_+^d$. On the unit cube $z+[0,1]^n$, $(b-\sum_i y_i)_+^d\le(b-\sum_i z_i)_+^d\le(b+n-\sum_i y_i)_+^d$. The integral over the orthant of $(B-\sum_i y_i)_+^d$ is $d!B^{n+d}/(n+d)!$, by iterated integration. Thus $b^D/D!\le U_b\le(b+n)^D/D!$. Translating $z_1$ at each threshold gives
$$
(G(0),G(L),G(2L))=(2N)^{-d}(U_N,U_{L+n+1},U_{n+1}).
$$
For each residue $r\in\{0,1\}^n$, the base $2Le_1+r$ has coordinate sum at most $N-1$ and belongs to the $t$-tail, so all classes survive. Because $\Omega_L\ge2^{-d}G(0)$, the cube bounds give
$$
G(L)/\Omega_L\le 2^d\big((L+2n+1)/(2L+n+1)\big)^D
=2^{-n}\big(1+(3n+1)/(2L+n+1)\big)^D .
$$
The collision lemma gives $2^{-n}\Omega_L\le Q\le G(L)$, proving both lower bounds and limits. With $x=(3n+1)/(2L+n+1)$ the stated condition on $L$ gives $Dx\le\epsilon/2\le1/2$. The binomial bound $(1+x)^D\le\sum_{j\ge0}(Dx)^j\le1+2Dx$ proves the finite accuracy bound.
