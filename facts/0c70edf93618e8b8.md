## Statement

Let $K_{z,l}$ be actual positive compatible $d$-fibers on a fixed finite complete base, converging fiberwise in Hausdorff distance to positive fibers. Let $C_l$ be their canonical convex hulls and $C$ their limit. Take finitely many nonvertical caps with normalized normals and thresholds $(a_{j,l},u_{j,l},c_{j,l})$ converging to limits with $u_j\ne0$, and widths $h_l\downarrow0$. Fix a positive-area own facet and a compact convex patch in its relative interior. For every limiting cap supporting on this facet, define the near-facet selector to be its ramp capture at outside support and its ramp omission at inside support. On the moving facet-side roof these selectors all have the form
$$
\tau_{j,l}(w)=\Psi(\alpha_{j,l}r_l(w)+\ell_{j,l}(w)),\qquad
\alpha_{j,l}>0,
$$
where $r_l$ is the same concave roof divided by $h_l$ for all caps and each $\ell_{j,l}$ is affine. The CDF is either the uniform outward ramp or a continuous outward CDF strictly increasing on $(-1,0)$ and symmetric in the sense $1-\Psi(q)=\Psi(-1-q)$.

If the facet lies in one nonvertical supporting plane of $C$, choose one compact convex patch inside the relative interior of each positive-area sampled own facet on that plane. The single common roof is jointly concave on the convex hull $G$ of all patches in the plane, including those at boundary sampled bases. For each cap whose limiting plane supports on any of those patches, its near-facet formula uses that same joint roof and affine corrections in the full coordinates of the plane, wherever it has the corresponding supporting orientation. For the uniform ramp, each resulting individual selector is the height length of the convex lift in $G\times[0,1]$ cut out by $t\le1+\alpha_{j,l}r_l+\ell_{j,l}$. On compact patches it is concave on its positive set. After a common subsequence, the individual sampled traces of these bounded convex lifts converge in Hausdorff distance when nonempty, and their height lengths converge strongly in $L^1$. The limiting traces satisfy every anchored Minkowski inclusion among the retained sampled traces in $G$. For the other symmetric CDF the individual selectors are quasiconcave and locally precompact in $L^1$.

On simultaneous unsaturated values, $\Psi^{-1}(\tau_{j,l})/\alpha_{j,l}-\Psi^{-1}(\tau_{k,l})/\alpha_{k,l}$ is affine in the full facet coordinates. At finite width equal normalized continuous normals order captures by their basewise offsets. For exactly opposite normalized continuous normals, the two scaled ramp arguments on a given fiber satisfy $q_j+q_k=s_z$ constant. Then $W_j+W_k\ge1$ if $s_z\ge-1$, $W_j+W_k\le1$ if $s_z\le-1$, with equality for $s_z=-1$. In particular a complementary pair with $s_z=0$ has outside selector at least its inside selector on their common facet. These orders pass to limits along persisting finite-width relations. Equality or opposition of the limiting normals alone imposes no such scaled relation.

## Proof

For an own facet use the transverse unit direction $e$ of its outer normal and its supporting plane as height zero. On a compact inner patch, every vertical line contains a point a uniformly positive distance inside the limiting fiber. To see this uniformly, take convex combinations of the whole facet with a fixed interior ball, using a small fixed positive weight on the ball. The shifted and contracted facet projections cover the inner patch for sufficiently small weight. The resulting inner points contain a uniform ball about a point on each line, and thus belong to the moving bodies for large $l$ by positive Hausdorff convergence. The upper endpoints on these lines form a concave, upper semicontinuous roof $s_l$. The same inward-point argument at arbitrarily small fixed heights and convergence of supports show $s_l\to0$ uniformly on the patch. On smaller inner patches its tangential slopes tend uniformly to zero where defined, by concavity and the uniform bound on a larger patch. Its graph surface densities there tend to one. This identifies the selector computed on the roof with the corresponding facet-side boundary capture or omission. In dimension one the facet is a point and the graph claim uses endpoint counting.

For a nonvertical ambient supporting plane, parameterize the plane by base $z$ and continuous tangential coordinates and take $e$ to be its outward continuous unit direction. Positive Hausdorff convergence at every sampled own patch supplies inward points on its vertical lines, including sampled bases on the boundary of the projected facet. The transverse projection of $C_l$ then contains the convex hull $G$ of the patches. Its upper graph is a single concave, upper semicontinuous function on $G$. Its restriction to each sampled patch is the actual own roof because the sampled fibers are the sections of $C_l$. The uniform upper bound tending to zero on $G$ follows from convergence of the hulls and their support in the ambient plane normal, whose dot product with $e$ is positive. The uniform lower bound tending to zero follows from concavity and the lower bounds on the finitely many original patches. This also treats a hull of lower affine base rank.

On the graph the cap argument is $u_{j,l}\cdot e\,s_l/h_l$ plus an affine function of the physical coordinates in the plane. At outside support $u_j\cdot e>0$, and the selector is the ramp itself. At inside support $u_j\cdot e<0$, and reflection expresses the omission as the ramp with positive coefficient $-u_{j,l}\cdot e$ and reflected affine term. All caps use $r_l=s_l/h_l$. The argument requires only a convergent unscaled roof, so scaled coefficients and corrections may diverge. When a cap is supporting only on a subset of the sampled patches, its affine expression still defines its lifted height on $G$, with the selector interpretation at the patches where its limiting support applies.

For the uniform ramp the lift is closed and convex by upper semicontinuity and concavity of $1+\alpha_{j,l}r_l+\ell_{j,l}$. The vertical length is its positive part truncated at one, with possible height-zero fibers irrelevant to the length. On its positive convex domain the minimum with the constant one remains concave. Consider the bounded trace in the patch and height coordinates, or the trace over each of the finitely many sampled bases of $G$. On a common subsequence, each trace is either always empty or converges in Hausdorff distance as a nonempty compact convex set. For a positive-volume limit the indicators converge off its null boundary by convexity and Hausdorff convergence. For a lower-dimensional limit the volumes tend to zero by containment in shrinking neighborhoods. Fubini therefore gives strong $L^1$ convergence of the length functions, with empty traces giving zero length. The limit is represented by the corresponding convex lift whenever that trace is nonempty. All volumes and convergence at a sampled base use its own tangential-and-height coordinates. Each trace at a sampled base is an actual slice of the same convex lift at that index $l$. For a combination of nonempty limiting traces with a retained sampled target, approximate the input points in their traces at index $l$, take their weighted combination in the target trace by convexity, and then pass to the limit. The target trace cannot be eventually empty in this case. This proves all anchored inclusions within $G$. A diagonal extraction handles an exhaustion of local inner patches. For the other CDF, superlevel sets at rational levels in $(0,1)$ are bounded compact convex sets. Extract Hausdorff limits for the nonempty ones and use the same indicator convergence. Step approximations in the level variable with uniform error tending to zero then make the original selectors Cauchy in $L^1$. Their limits have quasiconcave representatives by the nested convex level sets.

On unsaturated pieces invert the ramp and divide by $\alpha_{j,l}$, then subtract to cancel the common $r_l$. For equal normalized continuous normals the two original arguments differ only by a basewise constant, and monotonicity gives the order. For opposite normals compare $q_k=s_z-q_j$ to $-1-q_j$ and use $1-\Psi(q_j)=\Psi(-1-q_j)$. A complementary pair has $s_z=0$ and its outside capture dominates its inside omission. Pointwise inequalities of this kind persist under simultaneous $L^1$ convergence on the same measured patch or by passing inequalities of measures on a common weakly converging boundary bundle. Limiting normal coincidence without an $h_l$-scale comparison does not justify an order. Each lift is cap dependent; the construction only uses that cap's own truncation of the shared roof.
