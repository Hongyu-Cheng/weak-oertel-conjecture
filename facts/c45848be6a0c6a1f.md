## Statement

For $d\ge1$ and integer $s\ge2$, write $Q=[-1/2,1/2]^d$, $m=2s^d$, and take the convex body
$$
C=[0,m-1]\times\operatorname{conv}\big((\{0\}\times Q)\cup(\{1\}\times Q/s)\big)
$$
with two integer coordinates $(j,h)$. It has mixed mass $M=2(s^d+1)$. For any $\theta\in(0,1)$ put $y=de_1$, $A=4(d+1)/[\theta(1-\theta)]$, $u_i=e_i$ for $1\le i\le d$, and $u_0=-\sum_{i=1}^d e_i$. The $d+1$ margins
$$
L_i(j,h,x)=u_i\cdot(x-hy/\theta)+A(h-\theta)
$$
give closed caps with a rank-$d$ minimal positive circuit of continuous normals, common contact $((m-1)/2,\theta;y)$ outside $C$, and exact common mixed mass $T=2$. Every individual fiber has volume below $T$, and $M/T=s^d+1$ is unbounded.

The contact base equals the mean base of all source fibers for equal coefficients when $\theta=1/2$, or for actual volume coefficients when $\theta=1/(s^d+1)$. In both cases the maximum coefficient tends to zero. For volume coefficients $t_z$, with source volumes $w_z$, one has $\sum_z t_zw_z\to1$ and $(\sum_z t_z^2)^{-1}\to\infty$. The Minkowski interpolant excludes $y$. The respective lifted probabilities on independent uniform fibers are $1,0,1/2$ for cap indices $0,1,i\ge2$, while each corresponding independent source union has probability one. If $s^d+1>4/\gamma_d$, all original cap fractions are below $\gamma_d/4$, and the entire bottom layer remains uncovered.

## Proof

The integer fibers are exactly the $m$ copies of $Q$ at $h=0$ and the $m$ copies of $Q/s$ at $h=1$. The actual real-height section is $[(1-h)+h/s]Q$ since the endpoint cubes are homothetic. These facts give the total mass and show that $y$ is exterior, since its first coordinate is at least one whereas the whole body has continuous coordinates in $[-1/2,1/2]$. The normals have rank $d$ and their only dependence is their positive sum. At $h=\theta$, $x=y$, all margins vanish. At every bottom point the margins are bounded above by
$$
d/2-A\theta=d/2-4(d+1)/(1-\theta)<0.
$$
At every top point they are bounded below by
$$
-d/(2s)-d/\theta+A(1-\theta)
=-d/(2s)+(3d+4)/\theta>0.
$$
These bounds use $|u_i\cdot y|\le d$ and the cube supports. Thus each cap takes all top mass $m/s^d=2$ and no bottom mass. The weighted sum of the margins with equal positive weights is $A(h-\theta)$, and choosing $x=hy/\theta$ makes them all equal. Accordingly the set of bases over which the group covers the entire plane is precisely $h\ge\theta$.

Equal coefficients are $1/(2m)$ and yield mean height $1/2$. Volume coefficients are $1/M$ on the bottom and $s^{-d}/M$ on the top, with height $ms^{-d}/M=1/(s^d+1)$. Both give mean first coordinate $(m-1)/2$. Their interpolant is $[(1-\theta)+\theta/s]Q$ by homothety and excludes $y$. For the volume coefficients, $\sum_z t_zw_z=(1+s^{-2d})/(1+s^{-d})$ and $(\sum_z t_z^2)^{-1}=m(1+s^{-d})^2/(1+s^{-2d})$, proving the limits.

For either choice, take independent uniform source points $X_z$ and let $\overline X=\sum_z t_zX_z\in Q$. Summing the original margins cancels the height terms and gives $u_i\cdot(\overline X-y)$. For $u_0$ this is at least $d/2>0$, and for $u_1$ it is at most $1/2-d<0$. For $i\ge2$ the coordinate sum has a symmetric atomless law and gives probability $1/2$. The independent source union has probability one in each case because every top source is entirely in every cap. Subtracting the lifted probabilities gives respective defects $0,1,1/2$. Finally $T/M=1/(s^d+1)$, giving the stated target comparison even though all bottom fibers are missed.
