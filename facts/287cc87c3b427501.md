## Statement

Fix an integer $d\ge2$, and let $D$ denote ordinary normalized maximal halfspace depth as in accepted fact `03bc771bddd39a8a`. Write $N=d+1$, $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, $J=\lceil N/2\rceil$, and $\alpha=(\Gamma+J/N)/2>\Gamma$. There is $\theta_d>0$, depending only on $d$, such that the following holds for every finite nonempty index set $I$.

Let $B,A_j,C_j\subset\mathbb R^d$, $j\in I$, be compact full-dimensional convex bodies satisfying
$$\frac{A_j+C_j}{2}\subset B,\qquad D(B),D(C_j)\le\Gamma(1+\theta_d),\qquad\frac1{1+\theta_d}\le\frac{|C_j|}{|B|}\le1+\theta_d,$$
and $0<e=\sum_{j\in I}|A_j|\le\theta_d|B|$. Then there is $\mathbf y\in\operatorname{int}B$ such that, for every nonconstant affine functional $f$ and every choice of real offsets $(\beta_j)_{j\in I}$,
$$|B\cap\{f\ge f(\mathbf y)\}|+\sum_{j\in I}\left(|A_j\cap\{f\ge f(\mathbf y)+\beta_j\}|+|C_j\cap\{f\ge f(\mathbf y)-\beta_j\}|\right)\ge\Gamma|B|+\alpha e.$$
One can choose $\theta_d$ also to satisfy $\Gamma+\alpha\theta_d<1$. Thus a cut that contains the whole middle source $B$ automatically has the same lower bound. The pair copies in this statement are separate sources; an application to actual fibers must verify their distinctness.

## Proof

All constants below are chosen using only $d$. We specify an order that is independent of $|I|$. Write $\kappa=J/N-\Gamma>0$ and $\eta=\kappa/2$, so $\alpha=J/N-\eta=\Gamma+\eta$. Positivity follows from $J/N\ge1/2>\Gamma$; the strict inequality for $d\ge2$ follows by expanding $(1+1/d)^d>2$.

**Uniform normalized class.** Apply one invertible affine map to every source, taking a maximum-volume inscribed simplex of $B$ to $T=\operatorname{conv}(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d)$. All volumes scale by one common positive factor, and all asserted cap inequalities and midpoint inclusions are preserved. By accepted fact `03bc771bddd39a8a`, $T\subset B\subset[-1,1]^d$, and as the upper parameter $\theta\downarrow0$ this entire normalized class tends uniformly to $T$. Put $v=|T|$ and $\mathbf g=\operatorname{centroid}(T)$.

For each pair choose $\mathbf p_j\in A_j$ and write $A'_j=A_j-\mathbf p_j$, $C'_j=C_j+\mathbf p_j$. Then $\mathbf0\in A'_j$, $C'_j\subset2B$, and $A'_j\subset2(B-B)$: for any $\mathbf c\in C'_j$, both $\mathbf c/2$ and $(\mathbf a+\mathbf c)/2$ belong to $B$. Every normalized source in a pair lies in the ball of radius $R=4\sqrt d$.

These translations preserve the target cap inequality. Indeed, for $f(\mathbf x)=c+\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x$ they replace the two offsets by $\beta_j-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf p_j$ and its negative. Since each offset is arbitrary, this is a threshold identity, not a claim that all translations form one ambient shear. They also preserve each midpoint inclusion and each ordinary depth.

Every bounded $C'_j$ has volume tending uniformly to $v$ and ordinary depth tending to $\Gamma$ as $\theta\downarrow0$. Fact `03bc771bddd39a8a` therefore gives $s(C'_j)/v\to1$ uniformly. This remains true for an arbitrary finite number of pairs: a failure of uniformity would select one offending partner at each parameter value, contradicting the single-body compactness result.

Let $\lambda_i$, $0\le i\le d$, be the barycentric functions of $T$, $\mathbf n_i=\nabla\lambda_i$, and $b=1/N$. There is an outer homothetic simplex $T^+=\mathbf g+(1+t)(T-\mathbf g)\supset B$, with $t\ge0$ tending uniformly to zero. To see this, choose $r_0>0$ with $\mathbf g+r_0\mathbb B^d\subset T$ and put $t=d_H(B,T)/r_0$; the support inequalities give the inclusion. Let $q=N-J+1$, $\delta_0=N^{-N/(2q)}-b>0$, and $\delta=\delta_0/2$. For all sufficiently small $\theta$, each $C'_j$ has an inscribed simplex $S_j$ such that
$$\frac{|S_j|}{|T^+|}\ge\left(\frac{b+\delta}{b+\delta_0}\right)^q.$$
For every $\mathbf p\in A'_j$, the simplex $(\mathbf p+S_j)/2$ is contained in $T^+$. Its barycentric matrix has determinant $2^{-d}|S_j|/|T^+|$. Fact `724e9b2e1a3ec0bf` shows that at least $J$ of its row maxima are at least $b+\delta$. Since the barycentric functions of $T^+$ are $b+(\lambda_i-b)/(1+t)$ and $t\ge0$, this implies at least $J$ inequalities
$$\lambda_i(\mathbf p)+h_{C'_j}(\lambda_i)\ge2b+2\delta.$$

**Fixed credits and every tilt.** Define measurable sets and credits, before choosing the center,
$$A'_{j,i}=\{\mathbf p\in A'_j:\lambda_i(\mathbf p)+h_{C'_j}(\lambda_i)\ge2b+\delta\},\quad c_{j,i}=|A'_{j,i}|,\quad c_i=\sum_jc_{j,i},\quad\bar c=\frac1N\sum_i c_i.$$
Integrating the preceding pointwise multiplicity gives $0\le c_i\le e$ and $\bar c\ge Je/N$. No fixed set of good coordinates over an entire $A'_j$ is required.

For $f_i=\lambda_i+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x$, $\|\mathbf a\|\le\varepsilon$, and a center $\mathbf y\in B$ with $\max_i(\lambda_i(\mathbf y)-b)_+\le\sigma$, set $r_j=(|A_j|/|C_j|)^{1/d}$. Because $r_j^d\le\theta(1+\theta)$, sufficiently small $\theta$ makes all $r_j\le1$. Fact `67ecf7db6692fe65` gives, for every real $\beta$, endpoint credit at least
$$|A'_j\cap\{f_i\ge2f_i(\mathbf y)-h_{C'_j}(f_i)+w_{C'_j}(f_i)r_j\}|.$$
The midpoint inclusion and $B\subset T^+$ imply $w_{C'_j}(\lambda_i)\le2(1+t)\le3$ after decreasing $\theta$. In the radius-$R$ frame, for each $\mathbf p\in A'_{j,i}$, its margin above this comparison threshold is at least
$$\delta-2\sigma-3r_j-4R\varepsilon-2R\varepsilon r_j\ge\delta-2\sigma-3r_j-6R\varepsilon.$$
This uses $f_i(\mathbf p)+h_{C'_j}(f_i)\ge2b+\delta-2R\varepsilon$, $f_i(\mathbf y)\le b+\sigma+R\varepsilon$, and $w_{C'_j}(f_i)\le3+2R\varepsilon$. If $\sigma\le\delta/8$, $r_j\le\delta/12$, and $\varepsilon\le\delta/(24R)$, the margin is at least $\delta/4$. Thus those same fixed credits $c_{j,i}$ are paid for every allowed nearby direction, center, and real tilt.

**One center and an error proportional to this star's mass.** Let $\mathbf g_B$ be the true centroid, and set $\mathbf y=\mathbf g_B+\mathbf v_B$, where
$$\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf v_B=\frac{c_i-\bar c}{N\Gamma|B|}\quad(0\le i\le d).$$
The right-hand sides sum to zero. The only linear dependence among the barycentric gradients is their sum being zero, so the displacement exists uniquely. In standard coordinates its entries for $i=1,\ldots,d$ are the displayed right-hand sides. Consequently
$$\|\mathbf v_B\|\le K_d e/|B|,\qquad K_d=\sqrt d/(N\Gamma).$$
Choose $\zeta>0$ with $\zeta K_d(\sqrt d+1)\le\eta/2$. By fact `d4e455a28be6cde4`, choose positive $\varepsilon,h$ small enough that the proper-section density estimate
$$|S_B(f_i,f_i(\mathbf g_B)+s)-N\Gamma|B||\le\zeta|B|\qquad(|s|\le h)$$
holds whenever $B$ is sufficiently close to $T$. Decrease $\varepsilon$ further so that
$$\varepsilon\le\min\{1/2,\delta/(24R),\eta/(2\sqrt d)\}.$$
Shrinking the direction neighborhood preserves the density estimate. Now decrease $\theta$ until $B$ is in that neighborhood, $\mathbf y\in\operatorname{int}T$, $\sigma\le\delta/8$, every $r_j\le\delta/12$, and $|f_i(\mathbf y)-f_i(\mathbf g_B)|\le h$. These conditions follow uniformly from $\mathbf g_B\to\mathbf g$, $e/|B|\le\theta$, and the displacement bound.

Put $\Delta=(\mathbf n_i+\mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf v_B$. Fubini's coarea identity, with the signed integral when $\Delta<0$, bounds the error in replacing the middle-cap loss by $N\Gamma|B|\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf v_B$ by
$$\zeta|B||\Delta|+N\Gamma|B|\,|\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf v_B|\le\eta e.$$
Here $\|\mathbf n_i\|\le\sqrt d$, the bound for $\mathbf v_B$, and the chosen $\zeta,\varepsilon$ give at most $\eta e/2$ for each term. Exact Grünbaum at $\mathbf g_B$ therefore yields
$$|B\cap\{f_i\ge f_i(\mathbf y)\}|\ge\Gamma|B|-(c_i-\bar c)-\eta e.$$
Adding the fixed endpoint credits $c_i$ proves the required lower bound $\Gamma|B|+\bar c-\eta e\ge\Gamma|B|+\alpha e$. In particular, no additive Hausdorff error independent of $e$ appears.

**All other orientations and choice of constants.** A unit normal within $\varepsilon/\sqrt d$ of $\mathbf n_i/\|\mathbf n_i\|$ is covered: multiply it by $\|\mathbf n_i\|$ and write it as $\mathbf n_i+\mathbf a$, with $\|\mathbf a\|\le\varepsilon$. Positive rescaling and additive constants do not change the cap inequalities, and rescaled offsets remain arbitrary. For the fixed simplex at its centroid, equality directions in Grünbaum are exactly these oriented facet normals, by its pyramid equality characterization. On the compact complement of the open neighborhoods there is a fixed normalized cap gap $G>0$, unless that complement is empty. Uniform cap-volume convergence under $B\to T$ and $\mathbf y\to\mathbf g$ then gives at least $(\Gamma+G/2)|B|$ from $B$ alone. Decrease $\theta$ until $\alpha\theta\le G/2$. Finally require $\Gamma+\alpha\theta<1$, so a whole-$B$ cut also suffices.

There are finitely many smallness requirements, all depending only on $d$; select a positive $\theta_d$ satisfying all of them. The number of pairs enters only through sums of their masses, and the sole accumulated error is $\eta\sum_j|A_j|$. Reversing the threshold translations and common affine normalization proves the statement for the original sources.
