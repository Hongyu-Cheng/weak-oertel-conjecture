## Statement

Retain the canonical cross labels and aligned-rectangle notation of accepted fact `e0dcff96f9b90a96`, the recomputed-complement chart of accepted fact `de023bd405d38571`, and the maximum-chart three-charge reduction of accepted fact `084a2e888b32fd2d`.

Let \(G=(\mathcal E,\Sigma)\) be any finite loopless multigraph. Its vertices are called missed edges, and every multiedge \(\sigma\in\Sigma\) is oriented from \(E_\sigma^-\) to \(E_\sigma^+\). Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space. At the two incidences of every \(\sigma\), prescribe
\[
 U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^-},\ U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^+}\in V,
 \qquad
 c_\sigma:=U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^+}-U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^-}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(p\in V^{\mathcal E}\), write
\[
 (\delta p)_\sigma:=p_{E_\sigma^+}-p_{E_\sigma^-}.
\tag{2}
\]
Then the following are equivalent.

\(\mathrm{(i)}\) The difference cochain is a gradient: \(c=\delta p\) for some \(p\in V^{\mathcal E}\).

\(\mathrm{(ii)}\) For every closed walk
\[
 E_0\xleftrightarrow{\sigma_1}E_1
 \xleftrightarrow{\sigma_2}\cdots
 \xleftrightarrow{\sigma_k}E_k=E_0,
\]
one has
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^k\varepsilon_i c_{\sigma_i}=0,
\tag{3}
\]
where \(\varepsilon_i=1\) if the walk traverses \(\sigma_i\) in its chosen orientation and \(\varepsilon_i=-1\) otherwise.

\(\mathrm{(iii)}\) There are missed-edge potentials \(p_E\in V\) and label offsets \(a_\sigma\in V\) such that, at both incidences of every label,
\[
 \boxed{U_{\sigma,E}=p_E+a_\sigma.}
\tag{4}
\]

For any factorization (4), the incidence lifts are vertex-only, meaning that there are \(q_E\in V\) with
\[
 U_{\sigma,E}=q_E
 \quad\text{for every incidence }(\sigma,E),
\tag{5}
\]
if and only if the offsets \(a_\sigma\) are constant on the labels in each connected component of \(G\). In that event the componentwise constant can be absorbed into \(p\). If base vectors \(u_E\) are also prescribed in a real vector space \(B\), a vertex-only family \(q_E\) has one linear realization
\[
 q_E=L u_E
 \qquad(E\in\mathcal E)
\tag{6}
\]
for some linear \(L:\operatorname{span}\{u_E\}\to V\) if and only if
\[
 \sum_E\lambda_Eu_E=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_E\lambda_Eq_E=0
\tag{7}
\]
for every scalar family \((\lambda_E)\).

These distinctions apply to the canonical aligned lifts as follows. Globally orient every missed edge

The sign in (10) is necessary: the corresponding oriented base displacement is always \(u_E\), even when two labels use opposite endpoints of \(E\).

Let \(W\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), let \(\pi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d/W\) be the quotient, and apply (1) to

## Proof

Assume first that \(c=\delta p\). For every label \(\sigma\), define
\[
 a_\sigma
 :=U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^-}-p_{E_\sigma^-}.
\tag{27}
\]
Equations (1)-(2) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^+}-p_{E_\sigma^+}
 &=U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^-}+c_\sigma
   -p_{E_\sigma^-}-(\delta p)_\sigma\\
 &=U_{\sigma,E_\sigma^-}-p_{E_\sigma^-}
 =a_\sigma.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Thus (4) holds at both incidences. Conversely, subtracting the two identities in (4) cancels \(a_\sigma\) and gives \(c_\sigma=(\delta p)_\sigma\). This proves the equivalence of conditions \(\mathrm{(i)}\) and \(\mathrm{(iii)}\).

Every gradient telescopes around every closed walk, proving that \(\mathrm{(i)}\) implies \(\mathrm{(ii)}\). Conversely, assume (3). In each connected component choose a root \(E_0\) and put \(p_{E_0}=0\). For any vertex \(E\), choose a walk from \(E_0\) to \(E\) and define \(p_E\) as the signed sum of the \(c_\sigma\) along that walk. Equation (3), applied to one path followed by the reverse of another, makes this definition path-independent. Extending a root-to-\(E_\sigma^-\) path by \(\sigma\) gives
\[
 p_{E_\sigma^+}-p_{E_\sigma^-}=c_\sigma.
\]
Hence \(c=\delta p\), proving all three assertions equivalent.

Suppose (4) holds. If two labels \(\sigma,\tau\) meet at \(E\), equation (5) forces
\[
 p_E+a_\sigma=q_E=p_E+a_\tau,
\]
so \(a_\sigma=a_\tau\). The line graph of the labels in every connected component with at least two labels is connected, hence all its offsets are equal. The assertion is vacuous for a one-label component. Conversely, if the offsets have a componentwise common value \(a\), define \(q_E:=p_E+a\) in that component. This gives (5).

If (6) holds, every relation among the \(u_E\) is taken by \(L\) to the corresponding relation among the \(q_E\), proving (7). Conversely, assume (7). Define
\[
 L\left(\sum_E\lambda_Eu_E\right)
 :=\sum_E\lambda_Eq_E.
\tag{29}
\]
Condition (7) says exactly that (29) is independent of the representation of its input. It is plainly linear and satisfies (6). This proves the affine-realization criterion.

We next verify the canonical sign and the chart implication. If \(\sigma\) uses \(y_E^-\), switching to its partner changes the base by \(u_E\). If it uses \(y_E^+\), switching changes the base by \(-u_E\), and multiplication by \(\epsilon_{\sigma,E}=-1\) restores \(u_E\). This proves the sign assertion after (10). If all aligned rectangle corners have residual in \(W\), then for the two corners differing only by the switch across \(E\), affinity of \(s(x)=Lx+b\) gives, in the quotient,
\[
 \pi\left(
 \epsilon_{\sigma,E}
 \frac{q_{y^\star}^\star-x_{\sigma,y}}2
 \right)
 =\pi\left(
 L\epsilon_{\sigma,E}\frac{y^\star-y}{2}
 \right)
 =\pi(Lu_E).
\tag{30}
\]
This is (12), which is vertex-only and linearly realized. The already proved implications give gradient exactness and (4).

For the calibration, symmetry of \(K\) gives
\[
 \int_Kr_j\,dr=0,
 \qquad
 \int_Kr_i^2\,dr=\frac{16}{3}.
\tag{31}
\]
Equations (16) and (31) prove the mass and barycenter assertions in (17). Moreover,
\[
 0\leq
 \sum_{i=1}^4\widetilde g_{\sigma_i,a}(r)
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{16}
 \left(4+\sum_{i=1}^4r_i\right)
 \leq\frac\varepsilon2<\frac12,
\tag{32}
\]
and the same holds at \(b\). Thus the simultaneous pointwise source ledger is valid.

At \(a^\star,b^\star\) there is no original incidence density. The unused half-density is the constant \(1/2\), of mass \(8\), and the cross demand from the opposite partner is \(4\varepsilon\). The partner-fill formulas of `e0dcff96f9b90a96` therefore give the constant pooled density \(\varepsilon/4\), and each of the four label-level partner densities is the constant \(\varepsilon/16\). Its mass is \(\varepsilon\) and its barycenter is zero. Equations (10) and (17) now give (18), and (19) follows.

At \(a\), and identically at \(b\), the complement mass is
\[
 \int_K\left(
 \frac12-\sum_{i=1}^4\widetilde g_{\sigma_i,a}
 \right)
 =8-4\varepsilon.
\tag{33}
\]
The first moment of the half-density on the centrally symmetric cube is zero, while the total first moment of the four occurrence densities is
\[
 \frac\varepsilon3(1,1,1,1).
\tag{34}
\]
Subtracting (34) and dividing by (33) proves (20).

Write
\[
 \alpha:=\frac{\varepsilon}{3(8-4\varepsilon)}>0.
\]
The four difference vectors from the last point in (21) to the first four are
\[
 \frac13e_i+\alpha(1,1,1,1)
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq4).
\tag{35}
\]
Their matrix is \(\frac13I+\alpha\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\). It has eigenvalue \(1/3\) on the three-dimensional subspace orthogonal to \(\mathbf1\), and eigenvalue \(1/3+4\alpha>0\) on \(\mathbb R\mathbf1\). Hence (35) is linearly independent and the five points in (21) are affinely independent. If a chart \((s,W)\) existed, then at the single base \(a\), subtracting any two of its five residual inclusions would put all vectors in (35) in \(W\), forcing \(\dim W\geq4\). This proves the chart failure. With only \(k\leq3\) occurrence points and one complement point there are at most four points, whose affine dimension is at most three, proving the asserted one-source minimality.

For (22), finite-dimensional orthogonal-complement linear algebra gives
\[
 (\operatorname{im}\delta)^\perp=\ker\delta^\ast,
\]
and hence the displayed orthogonal direct sum. In the parallel-edge example, the contributions of (23) cancel at both vertices, so \(\delta^\ast h=0\). Every gradient takes the same value on two parallel edges with the same orientation, while the two values in (23) differ. Equivalently, traversing \(\sigma_1\) forward and \(\sigma_2\) backward gives the nonzero circulation \(r-(-r)=2r\). One loopless edge has no cycle, so this two-vertex, two-edge configuration is minimal among loopless multigraphs.

For the filled triangle, the definition of the simplicial coboundary is the oriented sum around its boundary, which gives (25). The rank and scaling assertions are immediate because every displayed vector is a scalar multiple of \(r\). A nondegenerate simplicial two-cell has at least three vertices and three boundary edges, so this is the smallest simplicial example.

Finally, each aligned rectangle in `e0dcff96f9b90a96` has four corners generated by the two incidences of one label and satisfies its parallelogram identity internally. Its four boundary segments are not four cross labels joining missed-edge vertices of \(G\). Hence those rectangles do not define a boundary map from two-cells to the label-edge space \(V^\Sigma\), proving the assertion following (25).

The complement-centroid equivalence (26) is exactly the first assertion of `084a2e888b32fd2d`, inherited from the first-moment identity in `de023bd405d38571`. The cube fibers in the calibration have centroid zero, and the affine hull of the four occurrence points is the hyperplane with coordinate sum \(1/3\), which does not contain zero or the complement point in (20). Thus this missing direction is invisible in \(c\) and is of the centroid type isolated by `084a2e888b32fd2d`. That accepted fact defines its midpoint and center charges using the distinct upstream edges \(e(\sigma),f(\sigma)\), while `e0dcff96f9b90a96` defines the vertices of the present graph from \(e_2(a(\sigma)),e_2(b(\sigma))\). Since no cited identity maps one edge system to the other, the Hodge bookkeeping proved here supplies no absorption of those charges. This completes the proof.
