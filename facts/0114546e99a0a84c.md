## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(k\ge1\), define the geometric pyramid and its complete lattice support by
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_k
&:=\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\{0\}\times[-1,1]^k)
                 \cup\{(2,\mathbf0)\}\bigr),\\
D_k
&:=P_k\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}
 =\bigl(\{0\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^k\bigr)
       \cup\{(1,\mathbf0),(2,\mathbf0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the assignment's \(P,D\) mean \(P_k,D_k\). The geometric \(P_k\) is not the product probability law denoted by \(P\) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. The support \(A\) in that fact is specialized to \(D_k\), its ambient dimension and intrinsic rank are both \(k+1\), and its tilt is \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\tau,\boldsymbol\theta)\). Here \(\tau\) is a source height tilt, not that fact's depth function \(\tau_p\).

For arbitrary \(\tau\in\mathbb R\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k)\in\mathbb R^k\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}
t&:=e^\tau,
&A_0&:=\prod_{i=1}^k(1+2\cosh\theta_i),
&Z&:=A_0+t+t^2,\\
p_{(x,\mathbf y)}&:=\frac{e^{\tau x+\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf y}}{Z}
\quad((x,\mathbf y)\in D_k),
&B_0&:=\prod_{i=1}^k(9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)),
&A_k&:=(2\,3^{k/2}+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The assignment's scalar coefficient \(A\) means \(A_k\); it is distinct from the support variable \(A\) in bf1ff94487296374. With exactly the capacity normalization of that fact, define
\[
\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)
 :=\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^{k+1}}
       \sum_{\mathbf x\in D_k}p_{\mathbf x}
          e^{\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)},
\qquad
\mathcal B_2(p)
 :=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_k}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2.
\]
In particular, the assignment's \(B2\) is \(\mathcal B_2(p)\), without rescaling. The exact formula and a uniform preliminary bound are
\[
\mathcal B_2(p)=\frac{B_0+A_k t^3+t^6}{(A_0+t+t^2)^3}
\ge\frac19\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k.
\]

Here is one fixed explicit constant for the exponential improvement. Set
\(k_0:=\left\lceil2\log(9/4)/\log(32/27)\right\rceil=10\),
\(g_0:=(32/27)^{1/4}\), and \(\mathscr A:=\{D_1,\ldots,D_9\}\). For \(A\in\mathscr A\), let \(M_A:=|A|\), \(r_A:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A)\), and, for every nonempty \(B\subseteq A\), let \(F_A(B)\) be the minimal face of \(\operatorname{conv}A\) containing \(B\), and \(\mathbf b_B:=|B|^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf x\in B}\mathbf x\). Specify the positive vertex representations in (11) of bf1ff94487296374 by maximizing their smallest coefficient. Equivalently, its resulting \(\lambda_A\) has the finite formula
\[
\lambda_A:=\min_{\varnothing\ne B\subseteq A}
\max_{\substack{
  \lambda_{\mathbf v}\ge0\ (\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B))\\
  \sum_{\mathbf v}\lambda_{\mathbf v}=1,\quad
  \sum_{\mathbf v}\lambda_{\mathbf v}\mathbf v=\mathbf b_B}}
\min_{\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)}\lambda_{\mathbf v}.
\]
The sums in this formula run over \(\operatorname{vert}F_A(B)\). The maximum is attained, is positive, and its value is independent of which maximizing representation is chosen. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta
&:=\min_{A\in\mathscr A}
       \frac{\exp[-\log(4M_A)/\lambda_A]}{M_A},\\
g_{\mathrm{small}}
&:=\min\left\{\frac{27}{16},
        \min_{A\in\mathscr A}
          \left(1+\frac{4^{r_A}\eta^3}{4}\right)\right\},\\
g&:=\min\{g_0,g_{\mathrm{small}}^{1/k_0}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\eta\) is exactly (12) of bf1ff94487296374 for the specified family and representations, and \(g_{\mathrm{small}}\) is exactly its constant called \(g\) in (14). All members of this family satisfy \(|A|>2^{r_A}\), so none is excluded from the inner minimum. These are finite explicit formulas on nine fixed supports, independent of the source tilts and of the variable \(k\). They give \(g>1\), and for every \(k\ge1\), \(\tau\in\mathbb R\), and \(\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^k\),
\[
\mathcal B_2(p)\ge g^{k+1}4^{-(k+1)}.
\]

## Proof

First verify the geometry and completeness. The pyramid is
\(P_k=\{(x,\mathbf y):0\le x\le2,\ |y_i|\le1-x/2\text{ for every }i\}\).
At the three possible integral heights \(0,1,2\), its integral horizontal coordinates are respectively \(\{-1,0,1\}^k,\{\mathbf0\},\{\mathbf0\}\). This proves the displayed description of \(D_k\). Its convex hull is \(P_k\), because it contains every base vertex and the apex. The base has dimension \(k\), and the apex lies outside its affine hull, so the intrinsic rank is \(r=k+1\). Thus \(D_k\) is complete and full-dimensional. Its cardinality is \(3^k+2>2^{k+1}\): for \(k=1\) it is \(5>4\), and for \(k\ge2\), \(3^k\ge9\,3^{k-2}>8\,2^{k-2}=2^{k+1}\).

We derive the entropy rows directly. For a probability law \(\nu\), write \(H(\nu):=-\sum_{\mathbf x:\nu_{\mathbf x}>0}\nu_{\mathbf x}\log\nu_{\mathbf x}\). Let \(r_{D_k,\mathbf j}\) denote the maximum-entropy law on \(D_k\) with mean \(\mathbf j\); this is precisely the law \(r_{E,\mathbf j}\) in bf1ff94487296374 with \(E=D_k\). For every law \(\nu\) on \(D_k\) with mean \(\mathbf j\), log-affinity gives \(D(\nu\|p)=-H(\nu)-\log p_{\mathbf j}\). Consequently the entropy-capacity identity (2) of bf1ff94487296374, including its boundary case, gives
\(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=p_{\mathbf j}\exp H(r_{D_k,\mathbf j})\).

Consider a bottom target \(\mathbf j=(0,\mathbf z)\), where \(\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^k\), and let \(s(\mathbf z):=|\{i:z_i=0\}|\). Since the height is nonnegative, any law with this mean is supported at height zero. If \(z_i=1\) or \(-1\), its \(i\)-th horizontal coordinate is forced to equal \(z_i\), since all bottom coordinates lie in \([-1,1]\). Its support therefore has at most \(3^{s(\mathbf z)}\) points. The uniform law on the bottom points with these forced coordinates is feasible: at each remaining coordinate it is uniform on \(\{-1,0,1\}\), with mean zero. It attains entropy \(s(\mathbf z)\log3\), the maximal entropy on that many points, and is the unique maximizing law. Hence the bottom capacities and their total contribution are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf C_p((0,\mathbf z))
 &=\frac{3^{s(\mathbf z)}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Z},\\
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1\}^k}
 p_{(0,\mathbf z)}\mathsf C_p((0,\mathbf z))^2
 &=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf z}9^{s(\mathbf z)}
                       e^{3\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}}{Z^3}
 =\frac{B_0}{Z^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the middle target \((1,\mathbf0)\), use the auxiliary entropy-row height tilt \(\tau^\star:=\tfrac12k\log3\), with zero horizontal tilt, and normalization \(Z^\star:=2\,3^k+3^{k/2}\). The law \(\nu^\star_{(x,\mathbf y)}:=e^{\tau^\star x}/Z^\star\) on \(D_k\) has total bottom mass \(3^k/Z^\star\), middle mass \(3^{k/2}/Z^\star\), and apex mass \(3^k/Z^\star\). Its height mean is one because the bottom and apex masses agree. Its horizontal mean is zero by the sign symmetry of the bottom cube. For every mean-\((1,\mathbf0)\) law \(\nu\), nonnegativity of relative entropy yields
\(0\le D(\nu\|\nu^\star)=-H(\nu)-\tau^\star+\log Z^\star\).
Equality holds precisely at \(\nu=\nu^\star\). Thus \(\nu^\star=r_{D_k,(1,\mathbf0)}\), and its entropy is \(\log Z^\star-\tau^\star=\log(2\,3^{k/2}+1)\). The middle capacity is therefore \(t(2\,3^{k/2}+1)/Z\), contributing \(A_k t^3/Z^3\).

At the apex, a law with height mean two is forced to be \(\delta_{(2,\mathbf0)}\). Its entropy is zero, its capacity is \(t^2/Z\), and its contribution is \(t^6/Z^3\). Summing these three contributions proves the exact formula for \(\mathcal B_2(p)\).

For each coordinate \(i\), specialize accepted fact 4735bc2a8a6d5fa9 to interval length \(m=3\) and tilt \(\theta_i\). Translate the coordinate \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\) to \(j=y+1\in\{0,1,2\}\). Its geometric probabilities are exactly
\(\widehat p^{(i)}_j:=e^{\theta_i(j-1)}/(1+2\cosh\theta_i)=e^{\theta_i j}/\sum_{a=0}^2e^{\theta_i a}\),
so \(\widehat p^{(i)}\) is that fact's law \(p\) in this specialization. Its reflected cardinalities \(k_j\) are \((1,3,1)\); the central reflected mass is \(3\widehat p^{(i)}_1\), and the two endpoint masses are \(\widehat p^{(i)}_0,\widehat p^{(i)}_2\). Its proxy \(S(m,\theta)\), with exactly that fact's normalization, therefore gives
\[
\frac{9+2\cosh(3\theta_i)}{(1+2\cosh\theta_i)^3}
 = (\widehat p^{(i)}_0)^3+9(\widehat p^{(i)}_1)^3
      +(\widehat p^{(i)}_2)^3
 = S(3,\theta_i)\ge\frac8{27}.
\]
Multiplying over the coordinates gives \(B_0\ge(8/27)^kA_0^3\). Since \(A_k\ge1\) and \((8/27)^k\le1\), the exact three-layer formula implies
\[
\mathcal B_2(p)
 \ge\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k
       \frac{A_0^3+t^3+t^6}{(A_0+t+t^2)^3}
 \ge\frac19\left(\frac8{27}\right)^k.
\]
The last inequality follows from \((a+b+c)^3\le9(a^3+b^3+c^3)\) for nonnegative \(a,b,c\), applied to \((a,b,c)=(A_0,t,t^2)\). In particular,
\(4^{k+1}\mathcal B_2(p)\ge(4/9)(32/27)^k\).

The value \(k_0=10\) is verified without approximate logarithms: the exact integer comparisons
\(16\,32^9=562949953421312<617673396283947=81\,27^9\)
and
\(81\,27^{10}=16677181699666569<18014398509481984=16\,32^{10}\)
give \((32/27)^9<(9/4)^2<(32/27)^{10}\). For every \(k\ge k_0\),
\[
\log\frac{(4/9)(32/27)^k}{g_0^{k+1}}
 =\frac{3k-1}{4}\log\frac{32}{27}-\log\frac94
 \ge\frac k2\log\frac{32}{27}-\log\frac94
 \ge0.
\]
Thus \(4^{k+1}\mathcal B_2(p)\ge g_0^{k+1}\) in every large rank.

It remains to obtain one strict capacity gain uniform over all tilts of the finite family \(\mathscr A\). First, for every nonempty \(B\subseteq A\), its average \(\mathbf b_B\) lies in the relative interior of \(F_A(B)\). Indeed, a supporting functional tight at that average is tight at every point of \(B\); a proper face of \(F_A(B)\) containing the average would consequently contain \(B\), contradicting minimality. As used in (11) and (44)--(45) of bf1ff94487296374, there is then a vertex representation positive at every vertex. The compact set of all vertex representations has a maximizer of its smallest coefficient, and that maximum is positive. Taking the finite minimum over \(B\) proves \(\lambda_A>0\). Taking the finite minimum over the nine supports proves \(\eta>0\), and hence \(g_{\mathrm{small}}>1\).

Fix \(A=D_k\in\mathscr A\), put \(r=r_A=k+1\), and fix an arbitrary tilt \(\boldsymbol\ell=(\tau,\boldsymbol\theta)\). In the face-conditioning argument (44)--(46) of bf1ff94487296374, the scalar called \(m\) there is \(m_{\boldsymbol\ell}:=\max_{\mathbf x\in A}\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x\) here. Set \(L:=\log(4M_A)\),
\(B:=\{\mathbf x\in A:\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x\ge m_{\boldsymbol\ell}-L\}\),
\(F:=F_A(B)\), and \(E:=A\cap F\). Use exactly the accepted face mass and conditional law \(q:=p(E)\), \(\pi:=p(\,\cdot\mid E)\), not the tensor-power parameter called \(q\) elsewhere in that fact. For completeness, the partition function is at least \(e^{m_{\boldsymbol\ell}}\), whereas the total numerator outside \(B\) is at most \(M_A e^{m_{\boldsymbol\ell}-L}\), so \(q\ge p(B)\ge3/4\). In a chosen vertex representation of \(\mathbf b_B\),
\[
\sum_{\mathbf v\in\operatorname{vert}F}
 \lambda^{A,B}_{\mathbf v}
       (m_{\boldsymbol\ell}-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v)
 =m_{\boldsymbol\ell}-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf b_B
 \le L.
\]
Each summand is nonnegative, and every coefficient is at least \(\lambda_A\). Hence every vertex of \(F\), and by convexity every point of \(E\), has score at least \(m_{\boldsymbol\ell}-L/\lambda_A\). The conditional normalizer is at most \(M_A e^{m_{\boldsymbol\ell}}\), proving \(\pi_{\mathbf x}\ge e^{-L/\lambda_A}/M_A\ge\eta\) on \(E\). These are the quantities and bounds in (46) of bf1ff94487296374. Also, \(E=F\cap\mathbb Z^{k+1}\), so it is complete in its intrinsic affine lattice, and \(\pi\) is log-affine there.

We require a capacity comparison on the original law, rather than that fact's actual-moment coefficient. For every \(\mathbf j\in E\), every mean-\(\mathbf j\) probability law on \(A\) is supported on \(E\). If \(F\) is proper, an exposing functional has its maximum at every point of \(F\) and a strictly smaller value at every point of \(A\setminus E\); its expectation at the specified mean forces zero mass outside \(E\). For the full face the assertion is automatic. On this common feasible set, \(p_{\mathbf x}=q\pi_{\mathbf x}\) gives
\(D(\nu\|p)=D(\nu\|\pi)-\log q\).
Applying the entropy-capacity identity (2) of bf1ff94487296374 to both laws proves the exact identity \(\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)=q\mathsf C_\pi(\mathbf j)\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal B_2(p)
 \ge\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}p_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_p(\mathbf j)^2
 =q^3\sum_{\mathbf j\in E}\pi_{\mathbf j}\mathsf C_\pi(\mathbf j)^2
 =q^3\mathcal B_2(\pi).
\]
The factor \(q^3\) consists of one target-probability factor and two capacity factors.

If \(F=\operatorname{conv}A\), then \(q=1\), \(\pi=p\), every atom is at least \(\eta\), and \(|A|>2^r\). The quantitative strictness (7) of bf1ff94487296374 yields
\(4^r\mathcal B_2(p)\ge1+4^r\eta^3/4\ge g_{\mathrm{small}}\).
If \(F\) is proper, put \(s:=\dim F\le r-1\). Its complete conditional support satisfies the parity capacity bound (5) of bf1ff94487296374, including \(s=0\), and consequently
\[
4^r\mathcal B_2(p)
 \ge4^r q^3\mathcal B_2(\pi)
 \ge4^{r-s}\left(\frac34\right)^3
 \ge\frac{27}{16}\ge g_{\mathrm{small}}.
\]
These are the capacity gains underlying (50)--(51) of that fact, now transferred to the original capacity by the exact face identity. No actual-depth conversion is applied.

For \(1\le k<k_0\), the rank \(k+1\) is at most \(k_0\), so
\(g^{k+1}\le g_{\mathrm{small}}^{(k+1)/k_0}\le g_{\mathrm{small}}\le4^{k+1}\mathcal B_2(p)\).
For \(k\ge k_0\), the large-rank estimate gives
\(g^{k+1}\le g_0^{k+1}\le4^{k+1}\mathcal B_2(p)\).
Finally, \(g_0>1\) and \(g_{\mathrm{small}}>1\), hence their defining minimum gives \(g>1\). This proves the claimed single exponential-in-rank capacity surplus for all ranks and all source tilts.
