## Statement

Use the convex inverse threshold model, and let $\ell$ be a positive sampled-concave array with concave roof $\widehat\ell$ on $B$ agreeing at all sampled bases. Let $Q$ be the maximum of $b_{\ell_p}(p,y)$ over all sampled marks and $I$ its set of maximizing bases. The projected real depth-$Q$ region for buffer parameter $\widehat\ell(z)$ is $\{z\in B:T_Q(z)\le\widehat\ell(z)\}$, which is convex with sampled trace $I$. If $b_{\rm base}(p)<Q$ then $T_Q(p)=\infty$. If $b_{\rm base}(p)>Q$, then $\ell_p\le T_Q(p)$ with equality exactly for $p\in I$. In that case equality is positive. If $b_{\rm base}(p)=Q$, then $p\in I$ exactly when $\ell_p\ge T_Q(p)$, allowing strict inequality. The convex set $\{z\in B:b_{\rm base}(z)>Q,\ T_Q(z)<\widehat\ell(z)\}$ contains no sampled base.

## Proof

At a fixed sampled base and fixed $l$, the depth is upper semicontinuous in the mark: it is the infimum of continuous oblique tail masses, with the constant atomic depth as one more competitor. It therefore has a maximum $g_p(l)$ on the compact own fiber. It is positive because the center of a ball in the own fiber has at least half that ball in every oblique buffered cap, and the whole ball in every pure-base containing cap. The convex inverse threshold theorem gives the projected real region, the sampled trace, and the infinite-threshold and equality-stratum classifications. We prove strict increase below the atomic ceiling to distinguish proper contacts.

At any own-interior mark of depth strictly below $b_{\rm base}(p)$, an oblique minimizing cap exists. For a minimizing sequence normalize $\|u_j\|=1$ and take $u_j\to u$. After taking a subsequence, all finitely many values $a_j\cdot(q-p)$ converge in the extended line. Let $V$ be the span of the rows $q-p$ with finite limits. Orthogonal projections of $a_j$ onto $V$ converge to $a_*$, since their coordinates against a basis of those rows converge. Replace $a_j$ by $a_*+(a_j-\operatorname{proj}_V a_j)$, and use $u$ in place of $u_j$. For all sufficiently large $j$ the rows with infinite limits are saturated on their prescribed full or empty sides, since the continuous terms are bounded. The remaining thresholds now equal the limiting thresholds exactly. Their fiber masses converge by continuity of the oblique tails, so a single such replacement realizes the infimum.

Such a cap cannot capture the entire own fiber. In fact, if it did, choose one missed point at every base with any missed point. Their base hull cannot contain $p$, since a convex combination at base $p$ would belong to the actual own fiber while being on the strictly missed side. A closed base halfspace containing $p$ can therefore avoid all missed bases by strict separation (or take any containing halfspace if none are missed). Its volume is at least $b_{\rm base}(p)$, and the oblique cap fully captures all these fibers, a contradiction. The interior mark is in the shifted cap with nonnegative buffer, and its own trace contains positive interior volume. The trace therefore cuts the interior. These arguments also apply in base rank zero, where a full own trace is already full mass.

If $0\le l_1<l_2$ and $g_p(l_2)<b_{\rm base}(p)$, take a maximizing mark at $l_1$. Its positive depth implies it is interior by the boundary argument of the inverse threshold theorem. At that same mark the $l_2$ depth is at least the $l_1$ depth, by monotonicity, and at most $g_p(l_2)$. An attaining $l_2$ oblique cap thus has proper own trace. Evaluating this same direction at $l_1$ raises its threshold strictly because $\varepsilon>0$, $\Gamma(u)>0$ and the energy is positive. This removes positive own volume, without increasing any other fiber's mass. Hence $g_p(l_1)<g_p(l_2)$.

If $b_{\rm base}(p)>Q$ and $\ell_p>T_Q(p)$, take $l_1=T_Q(p)$ and $l_2=\ell_p$. Then $g_p(l_1)\ge Q$ by threshold attainment, and $g_p(l_2)\le Q$ by the definition of the sampled maximum. This contradicts strict increase. Thus $\ell_p\le T_Q(p)$ there, and the projected trace criterion gives equality exactly at deepest bases in that stratum. Positivity follows from $\ell_p>0$. Finally the strict atomic superlevel is convex: every closed base halfspace containing a convex combination contains at least one participating base and hence has mass at least their minimum depth. The strict inequality $T_Q<\widehat\ell$ also defines a convex region, by convexity of $T_Q$ and concavity of the roof. Their intersection excludes sampled bases by the proper-contact inequality.
