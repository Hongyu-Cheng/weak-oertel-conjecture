## Statement

Let \(L\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). For each \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), let \(n_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, and assign positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) to its points. Throughout, retain the entire original laws
$$
Z_i=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},\qquad
p_{i,x}=w_{i,x}/Z_i,\qquad P=\bigotimes_i p_i,\qquad N=\sum_i n_i.
$$
For a finite law \(p\), define its actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and moments by
$$
\tau_p(j)=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,
\qquad M_r(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^r\quad(r>0).
$$
These are exactly the depth and moment objects of accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705 and e997599529e51c8f.

At every supported center \(j\in A_i\), choose a row \(R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}\subseteq A_i\) partitioned into \(\lbrace j\rbrace\), any number of distinct two-point sets with midpoint \(j\), and any number of distinct three-point sets with arithmetic mean \(j\). All these sets are pairwise disjoint, and none of the pairs or triples contains \(j\). Empty families are allowed. Let \(T_{i,j}\) fix \(j\), swap each pair, and cycle each triple in either orientation. Write \(\lambda_{i,j}(x)\in\lbrace1,2,3\rbrace\) for the length of the cycle containing \(x\). Thus \(T_{i,j}^6\) is the identity, and \(j\) is its unique fixed point in this row. Rows at different centers may overlap.

The following are NEW mixed-row quantities. The role map from 445a9e668ee1a705 is
$$
(R_{i,j},H_{p_i}(j),b(p_i),\kappa_{i,j},Y_{i,j},V_{i,j},q_{i,j},\sum_jq_{i,j}V_{i,j})
\longmapsto
(R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j),b_i^{(\mathrm m)},\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},V^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},q^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}).
$$
This is not an equality with the canonical full reflected row unless the mixed row consists of all available reflection pairs and its center. The role map from e997599529e51c8f replaces each of its \(R^{(3)},H^{(3)},b^{(3)},\kappa^{(3)},Y^{(3)},V^{(3)},q^{(3)},\nu^{(3)}\) by the corresponding superscript \((\mathrm m)\) object below, and replaces its \(g_{i,j}\) by \(g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}\). Its \(T_{i,j}\) maps to the new mixed permutation just defined. These maps are equalities when there are no pairs and the same triple cycles are chosen. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)&=\left(\prod_{a=0}^5p_{i,T_{i,j}^ax}\right)^{1/6}
=\left(\prod_{a=0}^{\lambda_{i,j}(x)-1}p_{i,T_{i,j}^ax}\right)^{1/\lambda_{i,j}(x)},\\
H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)&=\sum_{x\in R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x),
&\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)&=g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)/H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j),\\
Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)&=\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)},
&V^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}&=\mathbb E_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}(Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j})^2,\\
b_i^{(\mathrm m)}&=\sum_jp_{i,j}H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)^2,
&q^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}&=p_{i,j}H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)^2/b_i^{(\mathrm m)},\\
\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}&=\sum_jq^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}V^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For \(s=2,3\), also define the actually used cycle-type components
$$
V^{(\mathrm m,s)}_{i,j}=\mathbb E_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}
\left[(Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j})^2\mathbf1_{\lbrace\lambda_{i,j}(X)=s\rbrace}\right],
\qquad \nu_i^{(\mathrm m,s)}=\sum_jq^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}V^{(\mathrm m,s)}_{i,j}.
\tag{2}
$$
These are components of the mixed row, not independent choices of pair or triple rows. In particular \(V^{(\mathrm m)}=V^{(\mathrm m,2)}+V^{(\mathrm m,3)}\) and \(\nu^{(\mathrm m)}=\nu^{(\mathrm m,2)}+\nu^{(\mathrm m,3)}\). The original-law identities are
$$
\begin{gathered}
0<p_{i,j}\le H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)\le\sum_{x\in R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}p_{i,x}\le1,
\qquad b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\ge\sum_xp_{i,x}^3>0,\\
\mathbb E_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}X=j,
\qquad \mathbb E_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}=0,\\
\mathbb E_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}\log\frac{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(X)}{p_{i,X}}
=-\log H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}\left(\log\frac{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(X)}{p_{i,X}}\right)=V^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j},\\
b_i^{(\mathrm m)}=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_jw_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}\left(\prod_{a=0}^5w_{i,T_{i,j}^ax}\right)^{1/6}\right)^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
$$

For a product target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), set
$$
h_J=\prod_iH_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j_i),\qquad
\sigma_{s,J}^2=\sum_iV^{(\mathrm m,s)}_{i,j_i}\quad(s=2,3),\qquad
\sigma_J^2=\sigma_{2,J}^2+\sigma_{3,J}^2.
$$
The diagonal row permutation has orbit lengths in \(\lbrace1,2,3,6\rbrace\), with arithmetic mean \(J\) in every orbit and unique singleton orbit \(\lbrace J\rbrace\). Every containing closed halfspace captures at least one point of each orbit of length two or three and at least two points of each orbit of length six. If \(\Xi_{2,J}\) denotes the sum of \(\min_{X\in\mathcal O}P_X\) over its length-two orbits, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(J)&\ge\frac{h_J}{3}\exp(-\sigma_{2,J}-\sqrt2\,\sigma_{3,J})
+\frac23P_J+\frac13\Xi_{2,J}\\
&\ge\frac{h_J}{3}\exp(-\sqrt3\,\sigma_J)+\frac23P_J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
In particular, with \(\mathcal V_s=\sum_i\nu_i^{(\mathrm m,s)}\) and \(\mathcal V=\mathcal V_2+\mathcal V_3=\sum_i\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)
\exp\bigl(-2(\sqrt{\mathcal V_2}+\sqrt{2\mathcal V_3})\bigr)\\
&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)\exp(-2\sqrt{3\mathcal V}),\\
M_r(P)&\ge3^{-r}\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)^{r/2}
\exp\bigl(-r(\sqrt{\mathcal V_2}+\sqrt{2\mathcal V_3})\bigr)\quad(r\ge2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$
Here is the conditional price implication. Fix \(C\ge0\), put \(K_i=2^{n_i}\), and call factor \(i\) active when \(A_{0,i}:=K_i^2\sum_xp_{i,x}^3<1\). Suppose each active factor has mixed rows satisfying
$$
B_i:=K_i^2b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\ge1,\qquad
\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}\le C\log B_i.
\tag{6}
$$
Choose singleton rows in inactive factors. Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac19e^{-3C}4^{-N},\qquad
M_r(P)\ge\left(\frac{e^{-3C/2}}3\right)^r2^{-rN}\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{7}
$$
Thus, if one common \(C\) and certificates (6) existed for every active positive globally affine law on every support \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\ne\varnothing\), with \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) compact and convex, the original-law moment hypothesis \(M_r(p)\ge a^r2^{-rn}\) would hold for each fixed \(r\ge2\), with \(a=e^{-3C/2}/3\), and likewise for their heterogeneous products. Existence of those certificates is an additional premise.

Sharper specializations are retained. If there are no pairs, all mixed objects equal the corresponding triple objects of e997599529e51c8f under the explicit map above; (5) reduces to its coefficient \(1/9\) and loss \(2\sqrt{2\mathcal V}\), and (6) gives coefficient \((1/9)e^{-2C}\). If there are no triples, the pair argument gives
$$
\tau_P(J)\ge\frac{h_J}{2}e^{-\sigma_J}+\frac12P_J,\qquad
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{\mathcal V}},
\tag{8}
$$
and (6) gives coefficient \((1/4)e^{-C}\). For full reflected rows these are the exact canonical objects of 445a9e668ee1a705, and the main coefficients \(1/2\) and \(1/4\) retain its sharper reflection comparison. For selected reflection pairs, (8) follows directly from the proof below; no identification with a full canonical row is made. In both specializations the \(r\)-moment bound follows by raising the stated \(M_2\) lower bound to power \(r/2\).

For globally affine weights \(w_{i,x}=\exp(c_i+\ell_i\cdot x)\), write \(m_{i,j}=|R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}|\). Every mixed cycle has mean \(j\), so
$$
\begin{gathered}
g^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)=p_{i,j},\qquad H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)=m_{i,j}p_{i,j},\qquad
\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)=1/m_{i,j},\qquad
Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)=\ell_i\cdot(x-j),\\
V^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}=\frac1{m_{i,j}}\sum_{x\in R^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}}(\ell_i\cdot(x-j))^2,\qquad
b_i^{(\mathrm m)}=\sum_jm_{i,j}^2p_{i,j}^3.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
$$
No equality of the original probabilities inside a cycle is assumed.

Finally, the complete interval \(A=[-1,1]\cap\mathbb Z=\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\), with uniform law, is active but admits only singleton rows under the triple-only rules of e997599529e51c8f. Its triple-row quantities satisfy

Consequently no universal triple-only active certificate using exactly that fixed-target row convention can satisfy even the requirement \(B\ge1\). Across \(L\) such factors the normalized energy of factorwise triple rows is exactly \((4/9)^L\). This is a row-energy statement, not an actual-depth upper bound. A ternary kernel whose nontrivial cycle contains the target is a different convention and is not excluded by this argument.

The mixed construction retains the pair \(\lbrace-1,1\rbrace\) at center zero and singleton rows at the two endpoints. It gives

V^{(\mathrm m)}_j=\nu^{(\mathrm m)}=0.

For the original uniform product law \(P\) on \(A^L\), a concrete strengthened version of (8) is

In particular it is at least \((1/4)(11/27)^L\), using the original product law throughout. Within the stated row convention, retaining this centered pair is necessary to achieve \(B\ge1\) on the interval. Neither a universal mixed-row price nor the general Oertel statement is asserted.

## Proof

First fix one factor and center, abbreviating its mixed geometric weight by \(g\). It is constant on every cycle. Its score sums to zero on that cycle, and the arithmetic mean of its source points is the center. The center singleton has \(g(j)=p_j\) and score zero. Summing cycle by cycle proves the two expectations in (3), as well as zero score mean restricted to either nontrivial cycle type. Arithmetic-geometric mean on a cycle of length \(s\) gives \(s g\le\sum p_x\); summing proves the bounds for \(H\) and hence the lower bound for \(b\). Direct substitution yields
$$
\log\frac{\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x)}{p_{i,x}}
=-\log H_i^{(\mathrm m)}(j)-Y^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j}(x).
$$
The zero score mean proves the stated mean and centered variance of this logarithm. Replacing every \(p\) by \(w/Z_i\) proves the last identity of (3). Thus all auxiliary laws are well-defined, and none renormalizes the original law on a selected row.

Fix a product target \(J\), write \(R_J=\prod_iR^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j_i}\), and let \(\mathcal T_J\) apply \(T_{i,j_i}\) in each coordinate. Put \(\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\kappa^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j_i}\). An orbit has length the least common multiple of the occupied coordinate cycle lengths, hence length one, two, three, or six. Each coordinate cycles equally often around its local cycle, proving the orbit mean is \(J\). The only length-one orbit is \(\lbrace J\rbrace\). Each product source belongs to one orbit, so all counts below respect its full source capacity.

For \(X\in R_J\) and \(a=0,\ldots,5\), define
$$
S_a(X)=\sum_iY^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j_i}(T_{i,j_i}^aX_i),\qquad
m(X)=-\min_{0\le a<6}S_a(X).
$$
Their sum is zero, so \(m\ge0\); \(m\) is constant on an orbit. Geometric weights and \(\kappa_J\) are invariant under \(\mathcal T_J\), and the exact likelihood identity is
$$
P_{\mathcal T_J^aX}=h_J\kappa_J(X)e^{S_a(X)}.
\tag{13}
$$
For \(s=2,3,6\), let \(\Xi_{s,J}\) sum the original minimum probability over the length-\(s\) orbits. It follows exactly that
$$
h_J\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-m}=P_J+2\Xi_{2,J}+3\Xi_{3,J}+6\Xi_{6,J}.
\tag{14}
$$

A halfspace containing the arithmetic mean of a finite orbit contains at least one point of it. For a length-six orbit there is a stronger count. Its points, after subtracting \(J\), are all six vectors \(\pm\mathbf u+\mathbf v_k\), \(k=0,1,2\), where \(\mathbf u\) contains the centered pair coordinates and \(\mathbf v_k\) contains the centered triple coordinates. The Chinese remainder correspondence between parity and residue modulo three gives all six combinations, and \(\mathbf v_0+\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2=0\). An affine functional defining the containing halfspace therefore has values \(c\pm\alpha+\beta_k\), with \(c\ge0\) and \(\beta_0+\beta_1+\beta_2=0\). It suffices to count nonnegative values when \(c=0\), and to replace \(\alpha\) by \(|\alpha|\). If some \(\beta_k\ge|\alpha|\), that phase supplies two nonnegative values. Otherwise all \(\beta_k<|\alpha|\). If at most one of them were at least \(-|\alpha|\), the other two would be strictly smaller than \(-|\alpha|\), giving \(\sum_k\beta_k<-|\alpha|\le0\), a contradiction. Thus two phases supply nonnegative \(\beta_k+|\alpha|\). In every case at least two distinct orbit points are captured, with boundary points included.

Consequently every containing halfspace satisfies the following exact comparison of the counting expressions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)&\ge P_J+\Xi_{2,J}+\Xi_{3,J}+2\Xi_{6,J}\\
&=\frac{h_J}{3}\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-m}+\frac23P_J+\frac13\Xi_{2,J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
This is a lower comparison, not a claim of equality with the halfspace mass.

The score has the same two-period/three-period decomposition even when the occupied cycle type depends on \(X_i\). Namely, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
U(X)&=\sum_iY^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j_i}(X_i)\mathbf1_{\lbrace\lambda_{i,j_i}(X_i)=2\rbrace},\\
B_k(X)&=\sum_iY^{(\mathrm m)}_{i,j_i}(T_{i,j_i}^kX_i)\mathbf1_{\lbrace\lambda_{i,j_i}(X_i)=3\rbrace}\quad(k=0,1,2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(B_0+B_1+B_2=0\), and \(S_a=(-1)^aU+B_{a\bmod3}\). Thus the six residues give exactly
$$
m=|U|-\min_kB_k.
\tag{16}
$$
Each summand of \(U\) and of each \(B_k\) has mean zero: each selected local cycle has constant geometric weight and zero score sum. Rotations preserve that cycle type and its geometric law. Independence of the factor kernels therefore gives
$$
\mathbb E U^2=\sigma_{2,J}^2,\qquad
\mathbb E B_k^2=\sigma_{3,J}^2\quad(k=0,1,2).
\tag{17}
$$
No conditioning on a chosen cycle type was imposed. If three numbers sum to zero and their minimum is \(-v\), the other two sum to \(v\) and have squared sum at least \(v^2/2\). Hence \(v^2\le(2/3)\sum_kB_k^2\). Cauchy-Schwarz, (16), and (17) imply
$$
\mathbb E m\le\sigma_{2,J}+\sqrt2\,\sigma_{3,J}\le\sqrt3\,\sigma_J.
\tag{18}
$$
Jensen gives \(\mathbb E e^{-m}\ge e^{-\mathbb E m}\). Substituting in (15) and taking the infimum proves (4).

Now use the product center law \(q^{(\mathrm m)}=\bigotimes_iq_i^{(\mathrm m)}\), only after averaging depth against the original \(P\). Its exact normalizations are
$$
P_Jh_J^2=\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)q_J^{(\mathrm m)},
\qquad \mathbb E_{q^{(\mathrm m)}}\sigma_{s,J}^2=\mathcal V_s.
$$
Discard the nonnegative corrections in (4), square, and average. Jensen followed by Cauchy-Schwarz gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)
\mathbb E_{q^{(\mathrm m)}}e^{-2(\sigma_{2,J}+\sqrt2\,\sigma_{3,J})}\\
&\ge\frac19\left(\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}\right)e^{-2(\sqrt{\mathcal V_2}+\sqrt{2\mathcal V_3})}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since \(\sqrt{\mathcal V_2}+\sqrt{2\mathcal V_3}\le\sqrt{3\mathcal V}\), this proves both \(M_2\) bounds in (5). Jensen under the original \(P\) gives \(M_r(P)\ge M_2(P)^{r/2}\) for real \(r\ge2\). Distinct target rows were never added inside one halfspace estimate, so overlaps between different targets impose no extra capacity condition.

For (7), inactive singleton rows have \(b_i^{(\mathrm m)}=\sum_jp_{i,j}^3\), \(B_i=A_{0,i}\ge1\), and \(\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}=0\). Together with (6), writing \(G=\sum_i\log B_i\), this gives \(G\ge0\), \(\mathcal V\le CG\), and \(4^N\prod_i b_i^{(\mathrm m)}=e^G\). Therefore
$$
4^NM_2(P)\ge\frac19e^{G-2\sqrt{3CG}}\ge\frac19e^{-3C},
$$
because \(G-2\sqrt{3CG}=(\sqrt G-\sqrt{3C})^2-3C\). This includes \(C=0\); raising to power \(r/2\) proves the other claim. With no pairs, the explicit alias identifies all objects with e997599529e51c8f, or the preceding calculation uses \(\mathcal V_2=0\) and completes the square with \(2C\). With no triples, all noncentral orbits have length two, and the exact analogue of (15) is \(P(\mathcal H)\ge(h_J/2)\mathbb E e^{-|U|}+P_J/2\). Equation (17) and Jensen prove (8), and the price square then uses \(C\). For canonical full reflected rows the geometric means and scores are respectively \(\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\) and \((1/2)\log(p_x/p_{2j-x})\), verifying the equality of every alias with 445a9e668ee1a705. These arguments preserve the sharper pure mechanisms rather than substituting the weaker mixed constants.

For affine weights, the average of \(c_i+\ell_i\cdot x\) over each local cycle is \(c_i+\ell_i\cdot j\). Its geometric probability is therefore \(p_{i,j}\), using the same full \(Z_i\). All identities (9) follow by substitution in (1).

It remains to verify the interval calibration. At center zero a triple of three distinct points in \(\lbrace-1,0,1\rbrace\) would have to be the entire support, which contains the separately fixed center and is prohibited. At either endpoint, all other points lie strictly on one side, so no nontrivial triple can have that endpoint as mean; the endpoints are vertices. Thus every admissible triple-only row is a singleton. Its \(H^{(3)}\) is \(1/3\), its \(b^{(3)}\) is \(3(1/3)(1/3)^2=1/9\), and its atom activity is \(A_0=4\cdot3(1/3)^3=4/9\). This proves (10) and the universal triple-only obstruction. Tensorizing these factorwise row energies gives exactly \(4^L(1/9)^L=(4/9)^L\), with no inequality for actual depth in the upper direction.

The mixed rows described in (11) are also the full canonical reflected rows on this interval. All probabilities are equal, so all their scores vanish. Directly, \(b^{(\mathrm m)}=(1/3)\cdot1^2+2(1/3)(1/3)^2=11/27\), proving (11). For a product target with \(t\) zero coordinates, \(P_J=3^{-L}\) and \(h_J=3^{-(L-t)}\). The exact pair count gives \(\tau_P(J)\ge(h_J+3^{-L})/2\). Under the original uniform product law, \(\mathbb E_Ph_J^2=(11/27)^L\) and \(\mathbb E_Ph_J=(5/9)^L\). Squaring this pointwise lower bound and averaging yields
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\left[(11/27)^L+2\cdot3^{-L}(5/9)^L+3^{-2L}\right],
$$
which is (12). This proves the comparison, its conditional consequences, and the necessity calibration without a universal row-selection or price assertion.
