## Statement

Let \(D\subset\mathbb Z^2\) be finite, nonempty, of affine rank two, and complete in the sense \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^2\). Assume its lattice width is two. Let \(p_{\mathbf x}=e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf x}/\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf z}\) be a positive log-affine probability law with finite real tilt and integral vector mean \(\mathbf j\in\mathbb Z^2\).

An integral translation by \(\mathbf j\), followed by a unimodular coordinate change, puts the mean at \((0,0)\). If \(T\) is the coordinate matrix, the new tilt is \((\theta_1,\beta)=(T^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\theta\), and the new exponential normalizer is \(e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf j}\) times the original one. Each transformed atom keeps its original probability. Thereafter \(D,p\) denote the transformed support and law, and the support has the form
\[
 D=\bigcup_{s\in\{-1,0,1\}}\{s\}\times I_s,
 \qquad I_s=[L_s,U_s]\cap\mathbb Z,
\]
where all three intervals are nonempty and their endpoints are integers. In these coordinates \(0\in I_0\), the law is proportional to \(e^{\theta_1s+\beta y}\), and completeness implies
\[
 \frac{L_-+L_+}{2}\ge L_0-\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac{U_-+U_+}{2}\le U_0+\frac12.
\]
Conversely, these two endpoint inequalities imply completeness for any three nonempty integer intervals on the three indicated levels. This converse does not assert affine rank two or that the minimum width over other directions is two.

The center and product conclusions below also hold directly for any three nonempty integer intervals satisfying the displayed endpoint constraints, provided their support has affine rank two and the displayed positive log-affine row has vector mean zero. No assumption on the minimum width in other directions is needed for these conclusions.

Put \(A_s=\sum_{y\in I_s}e^{\beta y}\). The exact horizontal mean equation and normalizer are
\[
 e^{-\theta_1}A_-=e^{\theta_1}A_+=\sqrt{A_-A_+},
 \qquad Z=A_0+2\sqrt{A_-A_+},
 \qquad \lambda=\frac{2\sqrt{A_-A_+}}{Z}.
\]
Let \(Y_-,Y_+,Y_0\) have the respective probability masses \(e^{\beta y}/A_s\) on \(I_s\), and make \(Y_-\) and \(Y_+\) independent. Define the scalar center law, distinguished from the locked problem's convex body \(C\), by
\[
 \operatorname{Law}(C_{\mathrm{cen}})
 =\lambda\operatorname{Law}\left(\frac{Y_-+Y_+}{2}\right)
  +(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Law}(Y_0).
\]
Its mean is exactly zero. The following single absolute constant suffices for every such support and tilt:
\[
 K_{\mathrm{cen}}=2^{18}(2400e^3)^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^4
 \le K_{\mathrm{cen}}(\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^2)^2.
\]
No log-concavity of this mixture is assumed or asserted.

For every \(k\ge1\), choose these original planar rows independently, with arbitrary supports and tilts satisfying the stated hypotheses. In their individual normalized coordinates write their laws as \(p_i\), their centers as \(C_{\mathrm{cen},i}\), and \(Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k p_i\). For all real block normals \((a_i,b_i)\) and all \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^k b_i C_{\mathrm{cen},i}\ge-t\right)
   &\ge\frac{1}{4K_{\mathrm{cen}}},\\
 Q\left\{\sum_{i=1}^k(a_i S_i+b_i Y_i)\ge-t\right\}
   &\ge\frac{1}{8K_{\mathrm{cen}}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
These statements include zero normals. Therefore the original product's actual closed-halfspace depth at its integral mean is at least \(1/(8K_{\mathrm{cen}})\), also before undoing the coordinate changes.

The exact depth alias to 558e5cac7133593e is its unchanged \(\tau_p(\mathbf j)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf j}p(H)\), with proper closed halfspaces. For a single normalized original row its support \(A\), law \(p\), and mean \(\mathbf j\) are respectively the present \(D,p,(0,0)\). For the original product they are \(\prod_iD_i,Q,\mathbf0\in\mathbb R^{2k}\). When using that fact's conditional-centered-component lemma on the scalar centers, take its number of blocks to be \(k\), its block dimensions \(m_i=1\), its laws \(\nu_i=\operatorname{Law}(C_{\mathrm{cen},i})\), and its means \(j_i=0\). Its component set is a singleton, its component weight is one, and its component law is the scalar center law itself. The centers are not identified with the original planar rows. \(K_{\mathrm{cen}}\) is a new absolute constant, not a predecessor's row constant. No entropy capacity is used or identified with depth.

## Proof

Choose a primitive integer linear functional attaining lattice width two. Its minimum and maximum on the support are integers \(r,r+2\). Positivity of the law at these two extreme levels puts its mean strictly between them. Its mean is integral, so the mean level is \(r+1\). Subtracting the integral vector mean therefore makes the levels \(-1,0,1\). A primitive functional can be completed to a unimodular integer coordinate matrix by Bezout's identity. Applying this matrix preserves the integer lattice, completeness, log-affinity, full affine rank, and halfspace probabilities, while transforming the mean to zero. The support at each integer level is a nonempty integer interval, by convexity and completeness; the middle level contains zero because the mean is a convex combination of support points and hence belongs to the polygon, and completeness includes every integer point of that polygon. This proves the coordinate and support assertions.

For completeness, the convex hull of three prescribed interval columns has sections \(I_-\) and \(I_+\), viewed as real intervals, at its extreme levels. Its real section at level zero is
\[
 \left[
  \min\left\{L_0,\frac{L_-+L_+}{2}\right\},
  \max\left\{U_0,\frac{U_-+U_+}{2}\right\}
 \right].
\]
Indeed, every convex combination giving level zero uses equal total coefficients on the two outer levels. Their contribution is a scalar multiple of the midpoint interval, and the remaining contribution comes from the middle interval. Conversely, both these intervals lie in that section. The integer points of this section are exactly \(I_0\) if and only if its lower endpoint is strictly greater than \(L_0-1\) and its upper endpoint is strictly less than \(U_0+1\). The outer midpoint endpoints are half-integers, so these strict inequalities are equivalent to the two asserted half-unit inequalities. No other integer first coordinates occur in the polygon. This proves both directions of the completeness assertion.

The horizontal mean is \((-e^{-\theta_1}A_-+e^{\theta_1}A_+)/Z\). Setting it to zero gives the balancing identity, the stated normalizer, and the branch probabilities \(\lambda/2,1-\lambda,\lambda/2\). The original vertical mean is
\[
 (1-\lambda)\mathbb EY_0
  +\frac{\lambda}{2}(\mathbb EY_-+\mathbb EY_+)
  =\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}=0.
\]
Thus all the weights used below belong to the original normalized row, not a replacement law.

Write \(w_s=U_s-L_s\ge0\). The endpoint inequalities give \(w_-+w_+\le2w_0+2\). Reflection in the second coordinate negates the tilt and center without changing the centered moments, so it suffices to analyze \(\beta\ge0\). For \(\beta>0\), put \(q=e^{-\beta}\) and \(B_w=\sum_{r=0}^w q^r\). Let \(G_s=U_s-Y_s\); its masses are \(q^r/B_{w_s}\) on \(0\le r\le w_s\). Set
\[
 U_{\mathrm{av}}=\frac{U_-+U_+}{2},
 \qquad t_*=U_0-U_{\mathrm{av}}\ge-\frac12.
\]
On the outer branch the center is \(U_0-t_*-(G_-+G_+)/2\), and on the middle branch it is \(U_0-G_0\). Here \(t_*\) is distinct from the nonnegative halfspace-offset parameter \(t\) in the statement. For every nonnegative integer \(r\), a truncated geometric deficit has
\[
 \Pr(G_s\ge r)=q^r\frac{B_{w_s-r}}{B_{w_s}}\le q^r
 \quad(1\le r\le w_s),
 \qquad \mathbb EG_s\le\frac{q}{1-q}.
\]
The formula is zero beyond its support. Consequently
\[
 0=\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}
 \ge U_0-\lambda t_*-\frac{q}{1-q}.
\]

We first prove the uniform tilt bound \(\beta<\log16\). If the center were identically zero, positivity of both branch weights would force \(I_0=\{0\}\) and \(Y_-+Y_+=0\) almost surely. Independence and positive masses on the two intervals then force both outer intervals to be singletons. The three support points would be collinear, contradicting affine rank two. Thus the centered center law is nonconstant and has positive values as well as negative values.

Suppose now \(\beta\ge\log16\), so \(q\le1/16\). We have \(U_0\ge0\). If \(U_0=0\), a positive center must come from the outer branch. Its maximum \(U_{\mathrm{av}}\) is then a positive half-integer at most \(1/2\), hence \(U_{\mathrm{av}}=1/2\). Since \(B_{w_s}\ge1\) and \(B_{w_0}\le(1-q)^{-1}\), the exact odds satisfy
\[
 \frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}
  =2e^{-\beta t_*}\frac{\sqrt{B_{w_-}B_{w_+}}}{B_{w_0}}
  \ge2(1-q)e^{\beta/2}\ge\frac{15}{2}.
\]
Thus \(\lambda\ge15/17\), and the preceding mean inequality gives \(0\ge15/34-1/15>0\), a contradiction.

If \(U_0\ge1\) and \(t_*\le0\), the mean inequality similarly gives \(0\ge1-1/15>0\). If \(U_0\ge1\) and \(t_*>0\), then \(t_*\ge1/2\), since it is a half-integer. The bounds \(B_{w_s}\le(1-q)^{-1}\) and \(B_{w_0}\ge1\) imply
\[
 \lambda t_*
  \le\frac{2t_*e^{-\beta t_*}}{1-q}
  \le\frac{e^{-\beta/2}}{1-q}\le\frac4{15}.
\]
The middle inequality uses that \(u e^{-\beta u}\) decreases for \(u\ge1/2\) when \(\beta\ge\log16>2\). The mean inequality now gives \(0\ge1-4/15-1/15>0\). All cases contradict the mean. Reflection proves \( |\beta|<\log16\) in the original coordinates as well.

Retain \(0\le\beta<\log16\), defining \(q=1\) and \(B_w=w+1\) if \(\beta=0\). Since \(w_s\le2w_0+2\) for each outer interval, grouping a sum of length \(2w_0+3\) into at most three successive blocks of length \(w_0+1\) gives \(B_{w_s}\le3B_{w_0}\). Therefore
\[
 \frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}
  \le6e^{-\beta t_*}\le6e^{\beta/2}\le24,
 \qquad 1-\lambda\ge\frac1{25}.
\]
This is a uniform lower bound on the original middle-branch weight.

We record an elementary variance estimate. For \(w\ge1\), a deficit \(G\) with masses proportional to \(e^{-\beta r}\) on \(0\le r\le w\), and \(0\le\beta\le\log16\), satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Var}G\ge\frac{1}{96e^3}
       \min\{w^2,\beta^{-2}\},
\]
where at \(\beta=0\) the minimum means \(w^2\). If \(\beta w\le1\), every mass is at least \(e^{-1}/(w+1)\). The independent-copy formula \(\operatorname{Var}G=\frac12\mathbb E(G-G')^2\) then bounds the variance below by \(e^{-2}\) times the variance \(w(w+2)/12\) of the uniform law on \(0,\ldots,w\). This proves the displayed estimate in that regime.

If \(\beta w>1\) and \(0<\beta\le1\), let \(N=\lfloor1/\beta\rfloor\). Then \(1\le N\le w\), \(N\ge1/(2\beta)\), and \(q^N\ge e^{-1}\). The conditional law given \(G\le N\) has variance at least \(N^2/(12e^2)\), by the same independent-copy comparison. Moreover,
\[
 \Pr(G\le N)=\frac{B_N}{B_w}
  \ge(N+1)e^{-1}(1-e^{-\beta})\ge\frac1{2e},
\]
using \(1-e^{-\beta}\ge\beta/2\) for \(0\le\beta\le1\) and \(\beta(N+1)\ge1\). Total variance is at least this probability times the conditional variance, which proves the desired lower bound \(1/(96e^3\beta^2)\). If \(\beta>1\), the probabilities at zero and one are at least \(1-q\) and \(q(1-q)\), respectively. The two corresponding independent-copy terms imply \(\operatorname{Var}G\ge q(1-q)^2\ge1/64\), since \(q\ge1/16\) and \(1-q\ge1/2\). This is also at least \(1/(96e^3\beta^2)\). These arguments prove the variance estimate in every case.

Assume first \(w_0\ge1\). Mixture variance and the middle-weight bound imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}C_{\mathrm{cen}}
  \ge(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Var}Y_0
  \ge\frac{1}{2400e^3}\min\{w_0^2,\beta^{-2}\}.
\]
The same convention is used at \(\beta=0\). The mean is zero, so this variance is \(\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^2\). To control the fourth moment, set \(M_4=\mathbb E|C_{\mathrm{cen}}-U_0|^4\). Centering and Hölder give \( |U_0|^4\le M_4\). The pointwise inequality \( |x+y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) consequently yields \(\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^4\le16M_4\).

If \(\beta w_0\le1\), the center support is contained in \([L_0-1/2,U_0+1/2]\). Hence \( |C_{\mathrm{cen}}-U_0|\le w_0+1/2\le3w_0/2\), and
\[
 \mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^4\le81w_0^4.
\]
Combined with the preceding variance estimate, this proves the claimed fourth-moment inequality in the weak-tilt regime with a constant no larger than \(81(2400e^3)^2\).

If \(\beta w_0>1\), the tail bound for a deficit gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb EG_s^4
  &\le4\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^3e^{-\beta r}\\
  &\le4e^\beta\int_1^\infty u^3e^{-\beta u}\,du
   \le\frac{384}{\beta^4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first inequality follows by summing the increments \(r^4-(r-1)^4\le4r^3\) against the tail probabilities. For the second, integrate on each cell \([r,r+1]\); its integrand is at least \(r^3e^{-\beta(r+1)}\). The last inequality uses \(e^\beta\le16\) and \(\int_0^\infty u^3e^{-\beta u}\,du=6/\beta^4\), obtained by integration by parts. Convexity gives \(\mathbb E[((G_-+G_+)/2)^4]\le384/\beta^4\) as well.

For \(t_*\ge0\), the odds bound proved above yields
\[
 \lambda t_*^4\le6t_*^4e^{-\beta t_*}
   \le\frac{1536}{\beta^4},
\]
since the maximum of \(u^4e^{-u}\) on \(u\ge0\) is \(4^4e^{-4}\le256\). For \(-1/2\le t_*<0\), we instead have \(\lambda t_*^4\le1/16\le1536/\beta^4\), using \(\beta<\log16<3\). The original branch mixture and the fourth-power inequality therefore give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_4
  &\le(1-\lambda)\frac{384}{\beta^4}
    +8\lambda t_*^4
    +8\lambda\frac{384}{\beta^4}
   \le\frac{15744}{\beta^4},\\
 \mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^4
  &\le\frac{251904}{\beta^4}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(251904<2^{18}\), the variance lower bound proves the asserted inequality with \(K_{\mathrm{cen}}\) also in the strong-tilt regime.

It remains to handle \(w_0=0\), without dividing by \(w_0\). Here \(I_0=\{0\}\), and the center support is contained in \(\{-1/2,0,1/2\}\). The nonconstancy argument above and mean zero force both nonzero values to occur. Thus \(L_-+L_+=-1\), \(U_-+U_+=1\), and \(w_-+w_+=2\). Reflection of each outer interval about its own midpoint sends an outer pair with sum \(r\) to one with sum \(-r\). The numbers of pairs with sums \(1\) and \(-1\) are equal and positive; indeed both are endpoint pairs and unique. The outer sum law has masses proportional to these pair counts times \(e^{\beta r}\). Its mean is therefore zero only when \(\beta=0\), and the middle point contributes no mean. At this tilt the outer pairs are uniform. Their number is \((w_-+1)(w_++1)\le4\), so their probability of a nonzero sum is at least \(1/2\). Also \(A_0=1\) and \(A_-A_+\ge1\), giving \(\lambda\ge2/3\). If \(\rho=\Pr(C_{\mathrm{cen}}\ne0)\), then \(\rho\ge1/3\), \(\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^2=\rho/4\), and \(\mathbb E C_{\mathrm{cen}}^4=\rho/16\). Their ratio is \(1/\rho\le3\le K_{\mathrm{cen}}\). This finishes the uniform center moment proof.

Each center has zero in its positive support, because the middle interval contains zero and its branch weight is positive. Apply the conditional-centered-component lemma of 558e5cac7133593e with the alias map in the statement: give each scalar center its single component, namely its own mean-zero law, and take \(\kappa=K_{\mathrm{cen}}\ge3\). The moment hypothesis for an arbitrary scalar direction follows by multiplying the proved inequality by its coefficient's fourth power. The lemma yields the stated product center-cap bound for all nonzero concatenated normals. Zero normals give probability one. A halfspace with offset \(t\ge0\) contains its boundary-through-mean translate, so the same bound holds for every stated \(t\).

Finally, an exact realization of the original planar row uses the middle branch with probability \(1-\lambda\), returning \((0,Y_0)\). On the outer branch, with probability \(\lambda\), sample the independent pair \(Y_-,Y_+\) and an independent equiprobable sign \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), and return
\[
 (S,Y)=\left(\epsilon,
       \frac{Y_-+Y_+}{2}+\epsilon\frac{Y_+-Y_-}{2}\right).
\]
For each outer sign its branch probability is \(\lambda/2=\sqrt{A_-A_+}/Z\), exactly its original column probability, and the selected vertical coordinate has its original geometric law. Thus the realization agrees at every original atom. In a direction \((a,b)\) the projection is \(bC_{\mathrm{cen}}\) plus zero noise on the middle branch, or plus \(\epsilon(a+b(Y_+-Y_-)/2)\) on the outer branch.

Make these realizations independent for the \(k\) blocks. Condition on all branch labels and all sampled vertical coordinates, but not on the outer signs. The sum of the remaining noises is symmetric about zero, since all outer signs are independent and equiprobable. Its conditional probability of being nonnegative is at least \(1/2\), including any zero atom. On every such latent realization for which \(\sum_i b_iC_{\mathrm{cen},i}\ge0\), adding a nonnegative noise sum leaves the original projection sum nonnegative. Averaging gives
\[
 Q\left\{\sum_i(a_iS_i+b_iY_i)\ge0\right\}
  \ge\frac12\Pr\left(\sum_i b_iC_{\mathrm{cen},i}\ge0\right)
  \ge\frac1{8K_{\mathrm{cen}}}.
\]
This conditions on the whole noise sum and spends the factor \(1/2\) only once. Enlarging to offset \(t\ge0\) proves the full product assertion. Every proper closed halfspace containing the original concatenated mean contains its parallel translate through that mean. Taking their infimum gives the actual-depth bound in the stated predecessor normalization. Invertible affine coordinate changes preserve this assertion. No log-concavity, component localization, change of measure, or entropy-to-depth conversion has entered the center argument.
