## Statement

Let $P(a)=\{(x,y):-3x+y\le a_1,\ -x-2y\le a_2,\ x\le a_3,\ x+y\le a_4\}$ have all four edges of positive length in the indicated cyclic order. Write $p_{ij}$ for the vertex of two consecutive edges. Suppose $A=P(a)$ and $B=P(b)$ are the endpoints of an actual positive planar three-fiber family at $-1,0,1$ with middle body $K\supseteq(A+B)/2$. At a fixed middle mark suppose two raw-depth-minimizing caps have proper endpoint cuts and each satisfies the captured-side area-root equality with their actual middle trace. In both endpoint polygons, the cap with continuous normal $(1,0)$ strictly captures exactly $p_{23},p_{34}$ and cuts the two adjacent edges in their interiors. The cap with continuous normal $(-1,-1)$ strictly captures exactly $p_{12},p_{23}$ and cuts the adjacent edges in their interiors. The two integer coefficients may differ. Then $B=A+v$ with $v=(\delta_3,\delta_4-\delta_3)$, where $\delta=b-a$. If $K=(A+B)/2$, then $K=A+v/2$.

## Proof

For each cap, use its own actual integer coefficient. Its proper endpoints have positive captured areas, so exact root equality gives positive middle capture. Fact 2965bef6996cdba5 applies to these three actual compatible fibers and the fixed-mark depth minimizer, and gives translated endpoint caps. The first cap contains the entire original right edge, which has outer normal $(1,0)$. The artificial edge has the opposite normal, so translation of the caps matches the two original right edges. The second cap contains the entire original edge with outer normal $(-1,-2)$, again different from the artificial edge's normal. That pair of original edges also has equal lengths.

The right edge length is $(a_2+2a_4-a_3)/2$, from the intersections at $x=a_3$ with the second and fourth lines. The second edge joins a point of abscissa $-(2a_1+a_2)/7$ to a point of abscissa $a_3$ and is parallel to $(2,-1)$. Its length is a fixed positive multiple of $a_3+(2a_1+a_2)/7$. Therefore $\delta_2+2\delta_4-\delta_3=0$ and $2\delta_1+\delta_2+7\delta_3=0$. Substituting $v_x=\delta_3$, $v_y=\delta_4-\delta_3$ gives
$$
\delta=(-3v_x+v_y,-v_x-2v_y,v_x,v_x+v_y).
$$
These are precisely the support changes for translation by $v$, and every translation lies in this kernel. The equality for the Minkowski midpoint follows by convexity.
