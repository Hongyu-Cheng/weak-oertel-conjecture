## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the specialization \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df and the tree of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Thus \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), \(b_\ell=\lfloor2^\ell/(8\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}b_j\), \(Q=Q_k\), \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), \(n=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}m^j\), and \(N=n+1\). Set \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). In particular, \(k\) is the height parameter, \(Q\) is tree depth, \(m\) is the branching number, and \(n\) counts spatial coordinates.

Choose any symmetric saturated chain partition of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), and retain its nonterminal set \(D_m\), injective successor \(F_m(c)=c+e_{i_m(c)}\), coordinate tree \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}\), forward paths, and spatial support \(A_{m,Q}\). For a Boolean source \(b\), write its forward selected coordinates as \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and put \(z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{j=1}^{s}e_{u_j}\) for \(0\le s\le h(b)\). Retain every original height in every original column:
$$
\widehat A=\left\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
 a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z\right\rbrace.
$$
Here \(\operatorname{level}(z)\) is the number of spatial coordinates equal to two.

Fix any distinguished spatial coordinate \(u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}\), any function \(f:\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\to(0,\infty)\), and any field \(\lambda\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(\lambda_u=0\) and \(\Pi(\lambda)\le1\). The path variation has exactly the normalization of accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2:
$$
\Pi(\lambda)=\max_{v_0,\ldots,v_Q}
 \sum_{j=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{v_ji}|,
$$
where \(v_0=\varnothing\) and \(v_{j+1}\) is a child of \(v_j\). Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{(z,t)}&=f(z_u)e^{\lambda\cdot z}2^{-t}
 &&((z,t)\in\widehat A),\\
W&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I},
&p_{\mathbf I}&=w_{\mathbf I}/W,\\
\tau_p(\mathbf I)&=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf I}p(H\cap\widehat A),
&M_3(p)&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)^3,
\end{aligned}
$$
where the infimum ranges over all closed ambient halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\). All weights outside \(\widehat A\) are zero. Then
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-62}8^{-N}.
$$
This uses the full original support, columns, normalizer, and sampling law. In particular, the estimate has no dependence on \(f(0),f(1),f(2)\).

## Proof

Write \(\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}\) and \(\delta=1-\eta\). From accepted facts 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df, \(Q\ge4\), \(0<\delta<(Q+1)^{-2}\), and the spatial source is uniquely recovered by reduction modulo two. All uses of the tree and height support below have exactly the parameters in the statement.

For each local direction \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,m\rbrace\), define
\(\vartheta_i=2^{-m}|\lbrace c\in D_m:i_m(c)=i\rbrace|\).
There are \(\binom m{m/2}=2^m\delta\) symmetric chains. A saturated chain adds each particular direction at most once, so \(\vartheta_i\le\delta\). Moreover, the chain containing the empty set runs from the empty set to the full set, so every direction occurs at least once and \(\vartheta_i>0\). Injectivity of \(F_m\) also shows that exactly \(2^m\vartheta_i\) patterns in \(\operatorname{im}F_m\) have inverse-predecessor direction \(i\).

Separate the multiplier from the other weights by defining \(v_{(z,t)}=e^{\lambda\cdot z}2^{-t}\). For \(r\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), let \(\widehat A_r=\lbrace(z,t)\in\widehat A:z_u=r\rbrace\), let \(\nu_r=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_r}v_{\mathbf I}\delta_{\mathbf I}\), and let \(W_r=\nu_r(\mathbb R^N)\). All three \(W_r\) are positive: Boolean sources give the slices zero and one, and the positivity of every \(\vartheta_i\) gives a forward prefix reaching \(u\), hence a point of slice two. Let \(p^{[r]}=\nu_r/W_r\), and define its depth using all the same ambient closed halfspaces. Its unnormalized depth is \(h_{\nu_r}(\mathbf I)=W_r\tau_{p^{[r]}}(\mathbf I)\).

We first prove bounds on slices zero and one. For any field \(\xi\in\mathbb R^n\) with \(\xi_u=0\), set \(Z_\xi^{(u)}=\prod_{v\ne u}(1+e^{\xi_v})\), and give the Boolean sources with \(b_u=r\) the product probability law
\(\mu_{\xi,r}(b)=e^{\xi\cdot b}/Z_\xi^{(u)}\), for \(r\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\).
For each \(s\in\lbrace0,\ldots,Q\rbrace\), run the mixed exploration of accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2: use a forward \(D_m\) block for the first \(s\) steps, and an inverse \(\operatorname{im}F_m\) block for the remaining steps. In addition to requiring all \(Q\) block tests to succeed, forbid selection of the distinguished coordinate \(u\) at every step. Call this event \(E_{s,r}\).

Under \(\mu_{0,r}\), each next block is fresh conditional on a successful transcript. Every block other than the child block containing \(u\) is still uniform on its full Boolean cube. Its block-test failure probability is \(\delta\). The block containing \(u\), if visited, is uniform subject only to its specified bit \(b_u=r\). Each of \(D_m^c\) and \((\operatorname{im}F_m)^c\) contains \(2^m\delta\) patterns, so its conditional block-test failure probability is at most \(2\delta\). The forbidden direction has at most \(2^m\delta\) patterns, for either the forward or inverse rule by the preceding direction count, so its conditional probability is at most \(2\delta\). This exceptional block can be visited only once. Summing the first-failure probabilities therefore gives
$$
\mu_{0,r}(E_{s,r})\ge1-(Q+3)\delta
>1-\frac{Q+3}{(Q+1)^2}\ge\frac{18}{25}>\frac12.
$$
The displayed weak inequality uses \(Q\ge4\); equivalently, \((Q+3)/(Q+1)^2\le7/25\). This argument also covers a path that never visits the exceptional block.

A complete successful transcript fixes the patterns in exactly \(Q\) disjoint child blocks on a rooted path. Apart from the already fixed bit \(b_u=r\), it imposes no condition on other coordinates. Its probability under \(\mu_{\xi,r}\), divided by its probability under \(\mu_{0,r}\), is a product over the revealed coordinates other than \(u\). For each revealed coordinate \(v\), its factor is
\(2e^{\xi_v b_v}/(1+e^{\xi_v})\ge e^{-|\xi_v|}\).
The transcript ratio is consequently at least \(e^{-\Pi(\xi)}\). Unrevealed bits integrate to one, including the fixed bit if its block is not visited. The successful transcript cylinders are disjoint: their first different revealed pattern occurs at the same node, determined by their earlier common patterns. Summing their probabilities proves
\(\mu_{\xi,r}(E_{s,r})\ge\tfrac12 e^{-\Pi(\xi)}\).

Let \(\mathcal C_s^{[r]}\) be the image of \(E_{s,r}\) under \(b\mapsto z_s(b)\). This is a bijection onto the level-\(s\) complete points whose forward-prefix/inverse-suffix path avoids \(u\) and whose \(u\)-coordinate is \(r\). Indeed, the forward prefix changes only its selected zeros to twos, so it preserves coordinate \(u\). All suffix blocks stay Boolean and are precisely the inverse blocks of the accepted reconstruction. Conversely, replacing a complete point's twos by zeros recovers its Boolean source and then its unique mixed exploration. The spatial weight multiplier in this map is at least \(e^{-2\Pi(\xi)}\), because its selected prefix coordinates are contained in the visited path's blocks. Hence the new restricted census is
$$
\sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s^{[r]}}e^{\xi\cdot j}
\ge\frac12e^{-3\Pi(\xi)}Z_\xi^{(u)}
\qquad(0\le s\le Q,\quad r\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace).
$$
No equality of the fields at different blocks was used.

Every level-\(s\) point in slice \(r\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\) has a unique Boolean source with \(b_u=r\), and its forward prefix avoids \(u\). Its weight multiplier relative to that source is at most \(e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}\). The full slice normalizer thus satisfies
$$
W_r\le e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}Z_\lambda^{(u)}R_k,
\qquad
R_k=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}
\le\frac{9+\log k}{4}.
$$
The last height-sum estimate is the one in accepted fact b9989d13ba245ea2; it follows directly by bounding each geometric height tail by twice its first term and using \(b_\ell2^{-\ell}\le1/(8\ell)\).

Suppose now that \(k\ge2^{16}\). Put \(g=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\). The height estimates in accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba give \(\log k>9\),
\(U_k:=\sum_{\ell=8}^{g}b_\ell2^{-\ell}\ge(\log k)/32\), and \(R_k\le(\log k)/2\). For each slice \(r\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace\), sample only the original points
\(\mathcal G_r=\lbrace(j,a_s+1):j\in\mathcal C_s^{[r]},\ 8\le a_s\le g\rbrace\).

We use the actual-halfspace selection in the proof of accepted fact d9c4b095a13caaba, recording why it remains inside this slice. At a complete level-\(s\) point \(j\), write its inverse continuation as \(u_{s+1},\ldots,u_Q\), and put \(V_\ell=\sum_{j'=s+1}^{\ell}e_{u_{j'}}\). For every containing closed halfspace at \((j,a_s+1)\), that selection produces distinct original indices of total untilted weight at least \((\log k)/128\), drawn from
$$
(j-e_{u_\ell},a_s),\qquad
(j-V_\ell,a_\ell),\qquad
(j+V_\ell,a_\ell)
\quad(s<\ell\le Q).
$$
This is its lower-neighbor and reconstructed-endpoint argument: for a nonnegative height coefficient one endpoint per sufficiently high level is included; for a negative height coefficient either enough lower neighbors are included or the partial-sum argument includes enough reconstructed endpoints. It applies to every containing closed halfspace, by taking a parallel halfspace through the sampled point first. Every selected spatial displacement changes only coordinates of the inverse continuation, each by one. Since the complete path defining \(\mathcal C_s^{[r]}\) avoids \(u\), every selected index stays in \(\widehat A_r\). Its spatial exponential weight is at least \(e^{\lambda\cdot j-\Pi(\lambda)}\), since the changed coordinates lie in the blocks of that rooted path. The accepted selection consequently proves for the restricted measure itself that
$$
h_{\nu_r}(j,a_s+1)
\ge e^{\lambda\cdot j-\Pi(\lambda)}\frac{\log k}{128}
\qquad((j,a_s+1)\in\mathcal G_r).
$$
This reasoning permits halfspaces whose restriction to the slice has constant value as well; all the selected indices are then included whenever that value is nonnegative.

Apply the restricted census at \(\xi=4\lambda\), so \(\Pi(4\lambda)=4\Pi(\lambda)\). Summing the preceding unnormalized depth bound under the slice's actual sampling weights, and retaining its fourth normalizer, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p^{[r]})
&=W_r^{-4}\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A_r}
 v_{\mathbf I}h_{\nu_r}(\mathbf I)^3\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-3\Pi(\lambda)}(\log k)^3}{128^3W_r^4}
 \sum_{s:\,8\le a_s\le g}2^{-(a_s+1)}
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal C_s^{[r]}}e^{4\lambda\cdot j}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-15\Pi(\lambda)}Z_{4\lambda}^{(u)}U_k(\log k)^3}
 {4\cdot128^3W_r^4}\\
&\ge2^{-24}e^{-23\Pi(\lambda)}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{(u)}}{(Z_\lambda^{(u)})^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the last step we used \(U_k\ge(\log k)/32\) and
\(W_r\le e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}Z_\lambda^{(u)}(\log k)/2\).
Convexity of the fourth power gives \((1+x)^4\le8(1+x^4)\) for \(x>0\). Multiplying this inequality over the \(n-1\) coordinates other than \(u\) yields
\(Z_{4\lambda}^{(u)}/(Z_\lambda^{(u)})^4\ge8^{-(n-1)}\).
Since \(\Pi(\lambda)\le1\), \(N=n+1\), and \(e^{23}<3^{23}<2^{37}\), it follows that
$$
M_3(p^{[r]})\ge2^{-18}e^{-23}8^{-N}
>2^{-55}8^{-N}
\qquad(r\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace,\ k\ge2^{16}).
$$
For the numerical inequality, \(3^5<2^8\) and \(3^3<2^5\) imply \(3^{23}<2^{37}\).

If instead \(8\le k<2^{16}\), then \(R_k<8\). The Boolean points with \(b_u=r\) at height zero are actual slice atoms. A containing closed halfspace includes the atom itself, so their fourth powers alone, together with the same full-slice normalizer bound, give
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p^{[r]})
&\ge\frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{(u)}}{W_r^4}
\ge e^{-8\Pi(\lambda)}R_k^{-4}
 \frac{Z_{4\lambda}^{(u)}}{(Z_\lambda^{(u)})^4}\\
&>e^{-8}2^{-6}8^{-N}
>2^{-19}8^{-N}
\ge2^{-55}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(e^8<3^8<2^{13}\). This completes both height ranges for slices zero and one.

For slice two, write the distinguished word as \(u=i_1\cdots i_{\ell_u}\), where \(1\le\ell_u\le Q\). There are no slice-two points below level \(\ell_u\). A point at level \(\ell_u\) is in slice two exactly when its first \(\ell_u\) forward selections follow this fixed word. The corresponding prescribed blocks are disjoint; in its \(j\)-th block the permissible patterns number \(2^m\vartheta_{i_j}\). All other Boolean bits are unrestricted, so the anchor set
\(\mathcal B_u=\lbrace z\in A_{m,Q}:\operatorname{level}(z)=\ell_u,\ z_u=2\rbrace\)
has positive cardinality
$$
\mathfrak m_u=2^n\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_u}\vartheta_{i_j}
\le2^n\delta^{\ell_u}.
$$
Define \(L_u=\sum_{z\in\mathcal B_u}e^{\lambda\cdot z}>0\).
For any level \(s\ge\ell_u\), a source producing a slice-two level-\(s\) point also produces its unique anchor \(z_{\ell_u}(b)\in\mathcal B_u\). This map from the level-\(s\) source set into \(\mathcal B_u\) is injective, because the anchor recovers the Boolean source by parity. The additional spikes after level \(\ell_u\) multiply its spatial weight by at most \(e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}\). Thus the spatial weight sum of slice two at any level \(s\ge\ell_u\) is at most \(e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}L_u\). Every associated height tail is at most \(2^{1-a_{\ell_u}}\), since the entrance heights are nondecreasing. Therefore the full normalizer of slice two obeys
$$
W_2\le2e^{2\Pi(\lambda)}L_u(Q-\ell_u+1)2^{-a_{\ell_u}}.
$$
Use only the actual anchor atoms \((z,a_{\ell_u})\), for \(z\in\mathcal B_u\). Their self-mass lower-bounds their unnormalized depth. The elementary fourth-power inequality
\((\sum_{j=1}^{h}x_j)^4\le h^3\sum_{j=1}^{h}x_j^4\)
for nonnegative \(x_j\) consequently gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3(p^{[2]})
&\ge W_2^{-4}2^{-4a_{\ell_u}}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal B_u}e^{4\lambda\cdot z}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-8\Pi(\lambda)}}
 {16\mathfrak m_u^3(Q-\ell_u+1)^4}\\
&\ge\frac{e^{-8}}{16}\,8^{-n}
 \frac{\delta^{-3\ell_u}}{(Q-\ell_u+1)^4}
\ge\frac{e^{-8}}{16}\,8^{-n}
>2^{-14}8^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the penultimate inequality,
\(\delta^{-3\ell_u}>(Q+1)^{6\ell_u}\ge(Q+1)^6>Q^4\ge(Q-\ell_u+1)^4\).
The last inequality again uses \(e^8<2^{13}\) and \(8^{-n}=8\,8^{-N}\). This slice bound holds for every \(k\ge8\), without a large-height restriction.

We finally transfer these conditional estimates to the full original law, including the arbitrary multiplier. Put \(\alpha_r=f(r)W_r/W\). Then \(\alpha_r>0\), \(\sum_{r=0}^{2}\alpha_r=1\), and \(p=\sum_{r=0}^{2}\alpha_rp^{[r]}\). For an original index \(\mathbf I\in\widehat A_r\), every containing closed halfspace has \(p\)-mass at least \(\alpha_r\) times its \(p^{[r]}\)-mass. Taking infima gives \(\tau_p(\mathbf I)\ge\alpha_r\tau_{p^{[r]}}(\mathbf I)\), while \(p_{\mathbf I}=\alpha_rp^{[r]}_{\mathbf I}\). Therefore
$$
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{r=0}^{2}\alpha_r^4M_3(p^{[r]}).
$$
Every conditional moment has been proved at least \(2^{-55}8^{-N}\). Some \(\alpha_r\ge1/3\), so
\(M_3(p)\ge(2^{-55}/81)8^{-N}>2^{-62}8^{-N}\).
The multiplier cancels exactly within each slice, and the fourth-power transfer explicitly accounts for its entire effect on the original normalization and sampling law. All support points and all original heights were retained throughout the definitions, and every depth bound allows all containing closed ambient halfspaces.
