## Statement

Use the strictly compatible actual positive mixed fibers over a finite complete base set of ec5e140eb107957e. Write $\beta$ for maximum normalized depth and $I$ for its deepest bases. At each $p\in I$ fix one finite positive circuit of actual critical closed caps $H_{pj}$ through a deepest mark, with unit continuous normals, positive circuit weights $\rho_{pj}$, and equal positive left and right raw threshold slopes $S_{pj}$ for each cap. Put $\kappa_{pj}=(\rho_{pj}/S_{pj})/\sum_\ell(\rho_{p\ell}/S_{p\ell})$. Assume local minimality among actual positive fiber families over the same bases. Let $\Omega$ consist of the measured globally exposed points whose own fiber has a unique unit supporting normal and singleton support face. There is one probability vector $\pi$ on $I$ for which
$$
\sum_p\pi_p\sum_j\kappa_{pj}\mathbf1_{H_{pj}}(w)=\beta
$$
for every $w\in\Omega$ off the selected cap boundaries.

On a $C^1$ patch of an integer fiber boundary contained in $\Omega$, consider a point where the selected cap boundaries passing through it have one common unoriented restriction $\{f=0\}$ to that fiber, transverse to the patch. Write $s_{pj}=1$ or $-1$ according as a cap of this crossing class has restricted margin a positive or negative multiple of $f$. For each crossing $c$ put $J_{pc}=\sum_{j\text{ in }c}\kappa_{pj}s_{pj}$. The same $\pi$ satisfies $\sum_p\pi_pJ_{pc}=0$ at every such crossing. For any finite list of crossings this condition is equivalent to the absence of real coefficients $\lambda_c$ with $\sum_cJ_{pc}\lambda_c>0$ for all $p$. In particular, if unidirectional crossing classes collectively meet each deepest-base circuit, local minimality is impossible.

## Proof

For each finite set of distinct samples, the exposing and own-support hypotheses are precisely those of the signed local mass construction used in ec5e140eb107957e. Each sample admits an outward displacement along its own unit supporting normal. The fixed circuit and two-sided slope assumptions give the accepted equality dual on this entire finite set, because the local minimum is in the same fiberwise Hausdorff topology and the strict slack covers used in that proof account for all nondeep bases. For each individual sample the displayed equation cuts out a closed subset of the finite-dimensional probability simplex. The finite-set result gives the finite intersection property. Compactness gives one $\pi$ for all samples.

At a crossing, take a $C^1$ curve in the patch through the crossing point whose tangent has positive $f$-derivative; transversality gives such a tangent and a local chart realizes it. For sufficiently small positive and negative curve parameters, every cap outside the crossing class has unchanged incidence by its strict margin, and every cap in the class changes its incidence by $s_{pj}$ from the negative to the positive side. The points are off all selected boundaries, even if distinct ambient cap planes happen to restrict to the same fiber plane. Subtract their two incidence equations with the common $\pi$. This gives the stated jump equality. For a finite list, the equations say exactly that zero belongs to the convex hull of the rows $(J_{pc})_c$. Strict separation gives the stated equivalent certificate. In a unidirectional class choose the orientation with all signs positive. Every contributing row then has a strictly positive coefficient and all other coefficients are zero, forcing every contributing base to have $\pi_p=0$. A list meeting every circuit contradicts the probability normalization. With both orientations present the jump equality instead equates the positively and negatively oriented weighted sums. Cancellation can occur, for example for two opposite caps through a ball center with equal weights and slopes, whose score is $1/2$ off their common boundary.
