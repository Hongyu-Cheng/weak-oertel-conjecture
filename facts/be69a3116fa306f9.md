## Statement

Let
\[
R:=2100,\qquad d:=10,
\]
and let
\[
C:=
\left\{
(z,x)\in\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{10}:
\|z\|_\infty\leq R,\quad
0\leq x_j\leq R-\|z\|_\infty\ (1\leq j\leq10)
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\), put
\[
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{10}(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}w_z,
\]
and, for \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), put
\[
W_r:=\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z.
\tag{2}
\]
Define the ordered diagonal-deleted same-parity midpoint profile by
\[
\eta_c:=
\frac14
\sum_{r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2}
\frac1{W_r}
\sum_{\substack{
z,z'\in\mathbb Z^2\\
z\equiv z'\equiv r\pmod2,\ z\ne z'\\
z+z'=2c}}
w_zw_{z'}
\qquad(c\in\mathbb Z^2).
\tag{3}
\]
Then all four \(W_r\) are positive, and, with
\[
a:=R-1=2099,\qquad
D:=[-a,a]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\tag{4}
\]
the exact support is
\[
\operatorname{supp}\eta
=
D\setminus\{(\varepsilon_1a,\varepsilon_2a):
\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\in\{-1,1\}\}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\eta)=\mathbb R^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover:

1. For every \(c\in\mathbb Z^2\),
   \[
   0\leq\eta_c\leq w_c.
   \tag{7}
   \]

2. Every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) containing \(0\)
   satisfies
   \[
   \sum_{c\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}\eta_c>\frac W{64}.
   \tag{8}
   \]

3. With
   \[
   Q^\circ:=\sum_rW_r^2-\sum_z w_z^2,
   \tag{9}
   \]
   one has
   \[
   Q^\circ>\frac1{16}W^2>
   \frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
   \tag{10}
   \]

4. There is no affine map
   \[
   s:\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{supp}\eta)\longrightarrow
   \mathbb R^{10}
   \tag{11}
   \]
   such that, for every \(c\in\operatorname{supp}\eta\),
   \[
   s(c)\in C_c,\qquad
   \delta_C(c,s(c))\geq\eta_c,
   \tag{12}
   \]
   where
   \[
   \delta_C(c,x):=
   \inf\left\{
   \operatorname{vol}_{10}(C_c\cap J):
   J\subseteq\mathbb R^{10}
   \text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
   \right\}.
   \tag{13}
   \]

Thus \(C\) is a counterexample to the registered
diagonal-deleted midpoint-profile affine-localization assertion, but not
to the locked target or to the maximization over all anchored affine
certificates.

## Proof

Accepted fact `8d62da177a11cd1f` gives the exact positive-volume fiber
profile
\[
C_z=[0,R-\|z\|_\infty]^{10}
\quad(\|z\|_\infty\leq R),
\tag{14}
\]
\[
w_z=(R-\|z\|_\infty)^{10}>0\quad(z\in D),
\qquad
w_z=0\quad(z\notin D).
\tag{15}
\]
For each \(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\), the point in
\(\{0,1\}^2\) representing \(r\) belongs to \(D\) and has positive weight.
Hence every \(W_r\) in (2) is positive, so every normalization in (3) is
defined.

We first fix the ordered-pair convention and determine the support.  If
\(z\equiv z'\pmod2\), then \((z+z')/2\in\mathbb Z^2\).  Conversely, for
fixed \(c\in\mathbb Z^2\), every \(z\in\mathbb Z^2\) has reflected partner
\[
z':=2c-z\equiv-z\equiv z\pmod2.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the inner sums in (3) count every ordered pair \((z,z')\) of
distinct positive-weight points exactly once, at its unique midpoint
\(c=(z+z')/2\).  In particular, \((z,z')\) and \((z',z)\) are two
contributions when \(z\ne z'\).

Every positive contribution has \(z,z'\in D\), so its midpoint lies in
\(\operatorname{conv}D=[-a,a]^2\).  Hence
\(\operatorname{supp}\eta\subseteq D\).  If
\(c=(c_1,c_2)\in D\) and \(|c_1|<a\), then
\[
z=c+(1,0),\qquad z'=c-(1,0)
\tag{17}
\]
are distinct points of \(D\), have the same parity, and have midpoint
\(c\).  If \(|c_1|=a\) but \(c\) is not a corner of \(D\), then
\(|c_2|<a\), and the same conclusion follows from
\[
z=c+(0,1),\qquad z'=c-(0,1).
\tag{18}
\]
All four endpoint weights in (17) or (18) are positive, so
\(\eta_c>0\).

Finally, let \(c=(\varepsilon_1a,\varepsilon_2a)\) be a corner.  If
\(c=(z+z')/2\) with \(z,z'\in D\), then in each coordinate the average
of two numbers in \([-a,a]\) equals the extreme value
\(\varepsilon_i a\).  Both numbers must therefore equal
\(\varepsilon_i a\).  Hence \(z=z'=c\), which is deleted in (3).
Thus \(\eta_c=0\) at every corner.  This proves (5).  The support contains
\(0,\pm ae_1,\pm ae_2\), so it affinely spans \(\mathbb R^2\), proving
(6).

We next record the exact numerical estimates used below.  Put
\[
m:=\max_z w_z=w_0=R^{10}=2100R^9,
\qquad
N:=|D|=(2R-1)^2=4199^2=17631601.
\tag{19}
\]
The shell \(\{z:\|z\|_\infty=k\}\) has \(8k\) lattice points for
\(k\geq1\), so (15) gives the exact identity
\[
W=R^{10}+8\sum_{k=1}^{R-1}k(R-k)^{10}.
\tag{20}
\]
Let
\[
q:=\left\lfloor\frac{R-1}{10}\right\rfloor=209.
\tag{21}
\]
For \(1\leq k\leq q\), Bernoulli's inequality gives
\[
(R-k)^{10}
=R^{10}\left(1-\frac kR\right)^{10}
\geq R^{10}\left(1-\frac{10k}{R}\right).
\tag{22}
\]
Keeping only these shells in (20) and evaluating
\(\sum_{k=1}^qk\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^qk^2\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&\geq
8R^{10}\sum_{k=1}^q
k\left(1-\frac{10k}{R}\right)\\
&=
\frac43R^9q(q+1)\bigl(3R-10(2q+1)\bigr)\\
&=
123477200\,R^9.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
The following three exact coefficient comparisons will be used:
\[
123477200-\frac{32}{7}\,2100
=123467600>0,
\tag{24}
\]
\[
3\cdot123477200-16\cdot2100
=370398000>0,
\tag{25}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
(123477200-2\cdot2100)-4(4199)^2
&=123473000-70526404\\
&=52946596>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (19) and (23)-(26) imply, respectively,
\[
W>\frac{32}{7}m,\qquad
\frac mW<\frac3{16},\qquad
W-2m>4NR^9.
\tag{27}
\]

We now prove the pointwise domination.  Let \(\kappa_c\) be the full
normalized same-parity midpoint-fiber mass from accepted fact
`aca624dd8874e5a5`, specialized to \(n=2\).  In the full ordered sum, a
diagonal pair with midpoint \(c\) must be the unique pair \((c,c)\).
Therefore
\[
\kappa_c
=
\eta_c+\frac14\,\frac{w_c^2}{W_{c\bmod2}}.
\tag{28}
\]
The cited fact gives \(0\leq\kappa_c\leq w_c\) for every \(c\), including
zero-volume and lower-dimensional fibers.  Equation (28) now gives
\[
0\leq\eta_c\leq\kappa_c\leq w_c,
\tag{29}
\]
which proves (7).

Summing (3) over all midpoint fibers counts every ordered distinct pair
exactly once.  Consequently the exact total-mass identity is
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\eta|
:=\sum_c\eta_c
&=
\frac14\sum_r
\frac{W_r^2-\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z^2}{W_r}\\
&=
\frac W4-
\frac14\sum_r
\frac{\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z^2}{W_r}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Since \(0\leq w_z\leq m\),
\[
\sum_{z\equiv r\pmod2}w_z^2\leq mW_r
\tag{31}
\]
for each of the four positive parity classes.  Equations (30), (31), and
the first inequality in (27) yield
\[
|\eta|\geq\frac W4-m>\frac W{32}.
\tag{32}
\]

The reflection of every ordered pair \((z,z')\) to
\((-z,-z')\), together with \(w_{-z}=w_z\), proves
\[
\eta_c=\eta_{-c}.
\tag{33}
\]
Let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a proper closed halfspace containing
\(0\).  Write
\[
Q=\{x:u^\top x\geq\beta\},
\qquad u\ne0,\quad\beta\leq0,
\]
and translate its boundary to the anchor:
\[
Q_0:=\{x:u^\top x\geq0\}\subseteq Q.
\tag{34}
\]
By (33), \(\eta(Q_0)=\eta(-Q_0)\).  Because both halfspaces are closed,
their union is all of \(\mathbb R^2\) and their intersection is the
boundary line.  Hence
\[
2\eta(Q_0)
=\eta(Q_0)+\eta(-Q_0)
=|\eta|+\eta(\partial Q_0)
\geq|\eta|.
\tag{35}
\]
No boundary lattice mass is discarded.  Combining (32), (34), and (35)
gives
\[
\sum_{c\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2}\eta_c
\geq\eta(Q_0)
\geq\frac{|\eta|}{2}
>\frac W{64},
\tag{36}
\]
which proves (8).

For the unnormalized ordered off-diagonal mass, Cauchy-Schwarz over the
four parity classes and \(w_z^2\leq mw_z\) give
\[
\sum_rW_r^2\geq\frac{W^2}{4},
\qquad
\sum_z w_z^2\leq mW.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus the second inequality in (27) yields
\[
Q^\circ
\geq\frac{W^2}{4}-mW
>\left(\frac14-\frac3{16}\right)W^2
=\frac1{16}W^2.
\tag{38}
\]
Since \(e>0\),
\[
\frac1{16}W^2
=\frac{16}{256}W^2
>\frac{16-e}{256}W^2.
\tag{39}
\]
This proves both strict comparisons in (10).

It remains to prove the affine incompatibility.  At midpoint \(0\), the
ordered pairs in (3) are exactly \((z,-z)\); the distinctness condition
deletes only \(z=0\).  Write
\[
D_r:=\{z\in D:z\equiv r\pmod2\},
\qquad
N_r:=|D_r|,
\tag{40}
\]
and let \(00\) denote the parity class of \(0\).  Using
\(w_{-z}=w_z\) and \(w_0=m\), the ordered normalization gives the exact
identity
\[
4\eta_0
=
\frac{\sum_{z\in D_{00}\setminus\{0\}}w_z^2}{W_{00}}
+
\sum_{r\ne00}\frac{\sum_{z\in D_r}w_z^2}{W_r}.
\tag{41}
\]
Here both \(z\) and \(-z\) occur in the sums when \(z\ne0\), exactly as
required for ordered pairs.

Every \(D_r\) is nonempty, and \(D_{00}\setminus\{0\}\) is nonempty.
Cauchy-Schwarz within each displayed finite set, followed by
\(N_r\leq N\) and \(N_{00}-1\leq N\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
4\eta_0
&\geq
\frac1N\left[
\frac{(W_{00}-m)^2}{W_{00}}
+\sum_{r\ne00}W_r
\right]\\
&=
\frac1N\left(
W-2m+\frac{m^2}{W_{00}}
\right)
>
\frac{W-2m}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
The strictness follows from \(m^2/W_{00}>0\).  The last inequality in
(27) therefore yields
\[
\eta_0>\frac{W-2m}{4N}>R^9.
\tag{43}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that an affine map \(s\) as in (11)-(12)
exists.  By (5), both axial points \(ae_1\) and \(-ae_1\) belong to
\(\operatorname{supp}\eta\).  Their fibers are exactly
\[
C_{ae_1}=C_{-ae_1}=[0,1]^{10}.
\tag{44}
\]
Feasibility in (12) and affineness give
\[
s(0)=\frac{s(ae_1)+s(-ae_1)}2\in[0,1]^{10}.
\tag{45}
\]
The central fiber is \(C_0=[0,R]^{10}\).  The proper closed fiber
halfspace
\[
J:=\{x\in\mathbb R^{10}:x_1\leq s_1(0)\}
\tag{46}
\]
contains \(s(0)\), and its intersection with the central cube has the
exact volume
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{10}(C_0\cap J)
=s_1(0)R^9\leq R^9.
\tag{47}
\]
The formula remains valid when \(s_1(0)=0\), because the closed boundary
has ten-dimensional volume zero.  Hence (13), (43), and (47) imply
\[
\delta_C(0,s(0))
\leq R^9
<\eta_0,
\tag{48}
\]
contradicting (12) at the support point \(0\).  No affine graph satisfying
all the listed depth-core constraints exists.  This proves claim 4 and
completes the counterexample.
