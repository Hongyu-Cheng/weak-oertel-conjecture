## Statement

Let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^2$ be convex bodies at the only integer bases $-1,0,1$, respectively, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Use planar area on each fiber. Fix $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and nonzero spanning vectors $v_1,v_2,v_3$ with $\sum_i v_i=0$. With $h_i=\max_{x\in B}v_i\cdot x$, assume that each closed ambient cap
$$
v_i\cdot(x-y)+(v_i\cdot y-h_i)z\ge0
$$
attains the actual mixed halfspace depth $Q$ at $(0,y)$ and that its cuts on $A$ and $K$ are proper, meaning that the cutting lines meet the interiors. Its trace on $B$ is a supporting face, so $Q=U_i+q_i$, where $U_i$ is the cap area on $A$ and $q_i=|K\cap\{x:v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}|$. Let $a=|A|$, $k=|K|$, $b=|B|$, $M=a+k+b$, and $D=|\det(v_1,v_2)|$. Write $a_i,k_i$ for the lengths of the respective $A,K$ chords divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, and set $s_i=k_i+2a_i$, $\lambda_i=1/s_i$, $\Lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i$, $p_i=\lambda_i/\Lambda$, and $t_i=a_i/s_i$.

For distinct $i,j,\ell$ satisfying $p_\ell>p_i+3p_j$ and $p_\ell>p_j+3p_i$, put $p_*=\min(p_i,p_j)$ and
$$
T_i=\frac{2-\sqrt{6p_i/(p_\ell-3p_j)-2}}6,\qquad
T_j=\frac{2-\sqrt{6p_j/(p_\ell-3p_i)-2}}6,\qquad
X=\frac{4p_\ell(p_\ell-3p_*)T_iT_j}{p_ip_j}.
$$

Assume $Q\le 2M/9$ and $b\le a+k$. Then
$$
\frac{a}{k}>\frac{X}{9+8X},\qquad
\frac{a}{b}>\frac{X}{9(1+X)}.
$$
For $(p_i,p_j,p_\ell)=(7/50,7/50,18/25)$ these give $a/b>1/16$.

If also $a<b/32$, every weight vector in the stated weight domain satisfies $X<9/23$. In particular the open subset of the positive probability simplex specified by $p_\ell<3/4$, $p_\ell>p_i+3p_j$, $p_\ell>p_j+3p_i$, and $X>9/23$ is excluded, as is the level $X=9/23$ in that domain. Equivalently the open condition is $(23/279)X>1/31$. This open subset includes the following regions and their permutations.

- $13/100<p_i,p_j<3/20$.
- $p_i=p_j=(1-P)/2$ and $7/10\le P=p_\ell<3/4$.
- $p_i,p_j\le2/9$ and $11/15\le p_\ell<3/4$.

The point $(7/50,7/50,18/25)$ is in the first region. The boundary value $p_\ell=3/4$ is impossible for the proper cuts of the statement.

## Proof

Apply the section-scale theorem to these same bodies, supporting traces and depth-attaining caps. Its assumptions are the common setup of the statement. Choose the sector indexed by the smaller of $p_i,p_j$, so the area of that sector times $\Lambda^2$ is less than $C=D/[8p_\ell(p_\ell-3p_*)]$. Write $L=DT_iT_j/(2p_ip_j)$, so $a\Lambda^2>L>0$ and $L/C=X$. Since $q_r\le Q$ on every middle cap, the chosen sector has area at least
$$
k-2Q\ge k-\frac{4(a+k+b)}9\ge\frac{k-8a}9.
$$
This inequality applies also when $k-8a\le0$. It yields $k\Lambda^2<8a\Lambda^2+9C$. Dividing by $a\Lambda^2$ gives $k/a<8+9/X$, hence $a/k>X/(9+8X)$. Using $b\le a+k$ also gives $b/a<9+9/X$, proving the bound on $a/b$.

Under the small-$A$ condition, $32a<b\le a+k$ gives $a/k<1/31$, and $q_r\le Q\le4(a+k)/9<(128/279)k$. The scale ratio with $\epsilon=23/279$ gives $a/k>(23/279)X$. Either this estimate or the lower bound on $a/b$ yields $X<9/23$. At $X=9/23$ the two rational lower bounds are $1/31$ and $1/32$, respectively, so equality in the open threshold also contradicts the small-$A$ assumption. The root arguments are in $(0,4)$ throughout the stated domain, and all displayed denominators are positive. The region specified by the strict inequalities is therefore open in the simplex.

At the displayed weight vector, $T_i=T_j=(1-1/\sqrt5)/3$ and $X=96(3-\sqrt5)/49$. With the small-$A$ cap ceiling the scales read
$$
\frac{a\Lambda^2}{D}>\frac{1500-500\sqrt5}{441},\qquad
\frac{k\Lambda^2}{D}<\frac{3875}{552},\qquad
\frac{a}{k}>\frac{736(3-\sqrt5)}{4557}>\frac1{31}.
$$
The last comparison follows from $\sqrt5<5/2$ and $4557=31\cdot147$. Independently of the small-$A$ condition, $\sqrt5<9/4$ gives $X>72/49>9/7$, and $X/[9(1+X)]>1/16$ holds exactly when $X>9/7$.

In the rectangle, $7/10<p_\ell<37/50$ and $p_\ell-3p_i,p_\ell-3p_j>1/4$. Each ratio in the roots is less than $(3/20)/(1/4)=3/5$, giving $T_i,T_j>3/25$ because $\sqrt{8/5}<32/25$. These estimates put the rectangle in the required weight domain and give
$$
X>4\frac{(7/40)(3/25)^2}{9/400}=\frac{56}{125}>\frac9{23}.
$$

On the symmetric line for $2/3<P<3/4$, substitution gives
$$
X=\frac{8P(5P-3)}{9(1-P)^2}
 \left(1-\sqrt{\frac{3-4P}{5P-3}}\right)^2.
$$
The factors increase with $P$: the derivative of $(5P-3)/(1-P)^2$ is $(5P-1)/(1-P)^3>0$ and the derivative of $(3-4P)/(5P-3)$ is $-3/(5P-3)^2<0$. At $P=7/10$, use $\sqrt{2/5}<19/30$ to get $X>(280/81)(11/30)^2=1694/3645>9/23$.

In the strip, write $P=p_\ell$ and $p_i=1-P-p_j$. The denominator of $(1-P-p_j)/(P-3p_j)$ is positive. The partial derivative in $p_j$ has numerator $3-4P>0$, and that in $P$ has numerator $4p_j-1<0$. The ratio is at most its value at $P=11/15,\ p_j=2/9$, which is $2/3$. This also bounds the ratio with $i,j$ interchanged and ensures the strict weight conditions. Hence $T_i,T_j>2/21$ by $\sqrt2<10/7$. With $P-3p_*\ge(5P-3)/2\ge1/3$ and $p_ip_j\le(1-P)^2/4\le4/225$, we get
$$
X>4\frac{(11/15)(1/3)(2/21)^2}{4/225}=\frac{220}{441}>\frac9{23}.
$$
The strict $p_\ell<3/4$ bound follows from the same section-scale theorem.
