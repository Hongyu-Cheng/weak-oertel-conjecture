## Statement

Let $n\ge0$, $d\ge1$ and $N\ge1$ be integers. Fix affinely independent bases $p_1,\ldots,p_N\in\mathbb R^n$ and coefficients $\lambda_i>0$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Put $j=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i$. For an array $\mathcal A=(A_i)_{i=1}^N$ of full-dimensional compact convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$, let $D_{\mathcal A}=\sum_i\lambda_i A_i$. Define the raw source depth
$$
p_{\mathcal A}(y)=\inf_{H\ni(j,y)}\sum_i |\{x\in A_i:(p_i,x)\in H\}|,
\qquad y\in D_{\mathcal A},
$$
where $H$ ranges over closed ambient halfspaces and $|\cdot|$ denotes ordinary $d$-volume.

For another such array $\mathcal B=(B_i)$ and $t\in[0,1]$, set $\mathcal K=((1-t)A_i+tB_i)_{i=1}^N$. For every $y\in D_{\mathcal A}$ and $z\in D_{\mathcal B}$,
$$
p_{\mathcal K}((1-t)y+tz)^{1/d}
\ge (1-t)p_{\mathcal A}(y)^{1/d}+t p_{\mathcal B}(z)^{1/d}.
$$
The fixed-base maximum $M(\mathcal A)=\max_{y\in D_{\mathcal A}}p_{\mathcal A}(y)$ is positive and attained, and
$$
M(\mathcal K)^{1/d}\ge (1-t)M(\mathcal A)^{1/d}+tM(\mathcal B)^{1/d}.
$$
Both inequalities have homogeneous versions with any nonnegative coefficients $\alpha,\beta$ of positive sum in place of $1-t,t$, using componentwise Minkowski combinations and omitting terms with zero coefficient.

## Proof

**Quantile comparison.** Fix a unit vector $u$. For any of the full-dimensional bodies $A$, the cap-volume function $F_A(h)=|A\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge h\}|$ is continuous by dominated convergence, since hyperplanes have zero $d$-volume. It is strictly decreasing from $|A|$ to $0$ between the minimum and maximum of $u\cdot A$, since every open slab strictly between these extrema meets the interior. Denote the inverse threshold with volume $r^d$ by $q_A(r)$ for $0\le r\le |A|^{1/d}$. Its endpoints are the maximum support value at zero and the minimum support value at full mass.

Write $K=(1-t)A+tB$. For $r\in[0,|A|^{1/d}]$ and $s\in[0,|B|^{1/d}]$, volume Brunn–Minkowski gives $(1-t)r+ts\le |K|^{1/d}$. The Minkowski combination of the two quantile caps lies in the $K$-cap at threshold $(1-t)q_A(r)+tq_B(s)$. Both quantile caps are nonempty compact convex sets, including a supporting face at zero mass and the whole body at full mass. Brunn–Minkowski for nonempty compact sets therefore gives volume root at least $(1-t)r+ts$ for the containing $K$-cap. For a positive root this gives
$$
q_K((1-t)r+ts)\ge (1-t)q_A(r)+tq_B(s).
\tag{1}
$$
If the root is zero and $0<t<1$, then $r=s=0$ and (1) is equality by additivity of support values. The cases $t=0,1$ are identities. In particular the zero-cap case uses a supporting face and does not invert a threshold strictly above the support.

**Directional costs and endpoints.** For any array $\mathcal A$, translate each containing halfspace parallel to itself to pass through $(j,y)$, decreasing its mass. With continuous normal $u$, the resulting thresholds $h_i$ are exactly those satisfying $\sum_i\lambda_i h_i=u\cdot y$. Indeed the cap margin is $a\cdot(p_i-j)+u\cdot x-u\cdot y$. Conversely, affine independence allows an affine form on the base to take the values $u\cdot y-h_i$ at $p_i$. The weighted average condition gives value zero at $j$, so the form is $a\cdot(p-j)$.

For fixed $u$, put $R_*=\min_i|A_i|^{1/d}$, $\Omega=\{\xi\in[0,\infty)^N:\sum_i\xi_i^d=1\}$, and define
$$
Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)=\min_{\xi\in\Omega}\sum_i\lambda_i q_{A_i}(R\xi_i),
\qquad 0\le R\le R_*.
$$
The minimum exists by compactness, the arguments lie in their quantile domains, and the function is continuous. It is strictly decreasing, since evaluating at a minimizer for the smaller $R$ gives a strictly smaller value at the larger $R$ from at least one positive $\xi_i$ with positive $\lambda_i$. At $R=0$ it is the support value $h_{D_{\mathcal A}}(u)=\sum_i\lambda_i\max_{A_i}u\cdot x$.

Let $C_{\mathcal A,u}(s)$ be the infimum of $\sum_i F_{A_i}(h_i)$ subject to $\sum_i\lambda_i h_i=s$. For every $0<R\le R_*$,
$$
C_{\mathcal A,u}(s)<R^d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s>Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R).
\tag{2}
$$
If $s>Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)$, raise all thresholds from a minimizing quantile allocation by the same strictly positive amount $s-Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)$. This strictly reduces at least one positive cap volume, including when that cap initially has full volume, since its quantile threshold is then the minimum support. All other cap volumes weakly decrease. Conversely, choose thresholds of total volume less than $R^d$. Every individual volume $v_i$ is strictly below $|A_i|$. Increase all $v_i$ by the same positive increment $(R^d-\sum_i v_i)/N$. The new volumes sum to $R^d$ and hence still lie in the individual domains. Their quantile thresholds are strictly below the original thresholds, including when an original cap has zero mass or a new cap has full mass. Averaging the new thresholds gives a value strictly below $s$, and their root allocations are feasible in $Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)$. This proves (2) with the full-source endpoint included.

For $N\ge2$, one can set all thresholds except the smallest source's above their maximum support, then take them high enough that the chosen remaining threshold is below its minimum support while keeping weighted average $s$. For $N=1$ the sole cap is always bounded by the full source volume. Thus $C_{\mathcal A,u}\le R_*^d$ in either case. For $s\ge Q_{\mathcal A,u}(0)$, the supporting thresholds raised by the common amount $s-Q_{\mathcal A,u}(0)$ yield zero mass. For $s\le Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R_*)$, (2) gives cost $R_*^d$. At $s=Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)$ between these levels, a minimizing allocation achieves mass $R^d$, and (2) gives the matching lower bound. This proves the directional inverse profile including both plateaus.

A cap with purely base normal through $j$ captures at least one whole source, since $j$ is their weighted average. Its mass is at least $R_*^d$. Consequently $p_{\mathcal A}(y)\le R_*^d$, and for $0<R\le R_*$ this depth is at least $R^d$ precisely when $u\cdot y\le Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R)$ for every unit $u$. When $R=0$ the latter inequalities hold for every $y\in D_{\mathcal A}$ by the support identity. In particular, they hold for all $u$ at the choice $R=p_{\mathcal A}(y)^{1/d}$, including both endpoints of its range.

**Interpolation.** Put $R_A=p_{\mathcal A}(y)^{1/d}$, $R_B=p_{\mathcal B}(z)^{1/d}$ and $R=(1-t)R_A+tR_B$. Each of $R_A,R_B$ is at most the minimum component volume root in its own array. Componentwise volume Brunn–Minkowski therefore gives $R\le\min_i|K_i|^{1/d}$. Apply (1) with the same allocation $\xi\in\Omega$ to each component and then average. For every $u$ and $\xi$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i\lambda_i q_{K_i}(R\xi_i)
&\ge (1-t)\sum_i\lambda_i q_{A_i}(R_A\xi_i)
   +t\sum_i\lambda_i q_{B_i}(R_B\xi_i)\\
&\ge (1-t)Q_{\mathcal A,u}(R_A)+tQ_{\mathcal B,u}(R_B)\\
&\ge u\cdot((1-t)y+tz).
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the minimum over $\xi$ gives the depth criterion for $\mathcal K$. The target lies in $D_{\mathcal K}=(1-t)D_{\mathcal A}+tD_{\mathcal B}$. When $R>0$, (2) and the pure-base bound give depth at least $R^d$. When $R=0$, nonnegativity gives the conclusion. A zero endpoint depth with positive $R$ is already covered by the supporting thresholds for that array.

**Attainment and scaling.** At fixed base $j$, moving the mark by a continuous vector $h$ changes all thresholds for a unit continuous normal by at most $\lVert h\rVert$. Enclosing each body in a fixed ball bounds the volume in every slab of thickness $\lVert h\rVert$ by a constant times that thickness, uniformly in the normal, threshold and base tilt. Pure-base through-point cap masses do not change. Taking the infimum gives a Lipschitz depth function, so it attains its maximum on the compact set $D_{\mathcal A}$. At a weighted combination of points in the interior of every source body, each containing ambient halfspace contains at least one of those actual source points. The body at that base contains a ball about its point. The cap therefore captures at least half of that ball, giving a positive lower bound by taking the smallest of the finitely many ball volumes. At a boundary point of $D_{\mathcal A}$, a supporting direction gives a cap of zero mass by the $R=0$ profile. Applying the pointwise interpolation inequality at maximizers proves the inequality for $M$.

For $c>0$, $(p,x)\mapsto(p,cx)$ bijects the halfspaces used at $y$ and $cy$ and multiplies all masses by $c^d$. Combining this identity with the interpolation parameter $\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$ gives the homogeneous versions. If either coefficient is zero, this is the single-array scaling identity.
