## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ be an integer, $0\le r<1$, and let $G$ be the CDF of the probability density $f(t)=dt^{d-1}/(1-r^d)$ on $[r,1]$. Its mean is $m=\frac{d}{d+1}\frac{1-r^{d+1}}{1-r^d}$. With $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, one has $r\le2m-1<1$ and
$$
G(2m-1)\le1-2\gamma_d.
$$

## Proof

Pairing the density at points equidistant from the interval midpoint gives $m\ge(1+r)/2$, because $f$ is increasing. The formula for $m$ is direct integration. Set $T=((2m-1)^d-r^d)/(1-r^d)=G(2m-1)$.

For $d=2$, substitution gives $T=1/9-8r^3/[9(1+r)^3]\le1/9=1-2\gamma_2$. For $d=3$, let $A=1+r+r^2$ and $N=A+3r^3$. Then $2m-1=N/(2A)$, and $T\le5/32=1-2\gamma_3$ is equivalent to $(5+27r^3)A^3-4N^3\ge0$. Expansion factors the latter as
$$
(1-r)(1+4r+10r^2+8r^3+23r^4+80r^5+90r^6+108r^7+81r^8),
$$
which is nonnegative on the stated interval.

For $d\ge4$ we first show, for $0<\alpha<1$, $0\le R<1$ and $a=(1+5R)/6$, that
$$
\frac{2}{1-R}\int_R^1 u^\alpha\,du\le 1+a^\alpha.
$$
For $0<v\le1$ the difference $-\log v-(1-v)(7+5v)/(2(1+5v))$ has derivative $(v-1)(5v-1)(5v-2)/(2v(1+5v)^2)$. Its minima can only occur at $v=1$ or $1/5$, since it diverges at zero and the critical point $2/5$ is a local maximum. The values are respectively $0$ and $\log 5-8/5>0$. The last sign follows by integrating the geometric series for $1/(1-x^2)$ from $0$ to $2/3$ and keeping its first four terms, which gives $2\sum_{j=0}^3(2/3)^{2j+1}/(2j+1)-8/5=4/15309>0$.

Substitute $v=(R+t)/(1+t)$ for any $t>0$ in the resulting logarithm bound. Because $a+t=(1+t)(1+5v)/6$ and $1-v=(1-R)/(1+t)$, this gives
$$
\frac{1}{1+t}+\frac{1}{a+t}\le\frac{2}{1-R}\int_R^1\frac{du}{u+t}.
$$
Subtract each side multiplied by $t$ from $2$ to obtain
$$
\frac{2}{1-R}\int_R^1\frac{u}{u+t}\,du\le\frac{1}{1+t}+\frac{a}{a+t}.
$$
Integrate against $C_\alpha t^{\alpha-1}\,dt$ on $(0,\infty)$, where $C_\alpha^{-1}=\int_0^\infty v^{\alpha-1}/(1+v)\,dv$. For $u>0$, scaling $t=uv$ gives $C_\alpha\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}u/(u+t)\,dt=u^\alpha$, and at $u=0$ the integral vanishes. The integrands are nonnegative and the resulting integrals are finite. This proves the displayed power bound.

Now use $R=r^d$ and $\alpha=1/d$. The change of variable $u=t^d$ in the mean gives $m=(1-R)^{-1}\int_R^1u^{1/d}\,du$. Hence $0\le 2m-1\le[(1+5R)/6]^{1/d}$, so $T\le1/6$. The function $-d\log(1+1/d)$ decreases in positive real $d$, by $\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)$ for $x>0$. Consequently $\gamma_d\le\gamma_4=256/625$ for $d\ge4$, and $1-2\gamma_d\ge113/625>1/6$.
