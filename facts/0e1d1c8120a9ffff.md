## Statement

All logarithms are natural. For every integer \(M\ge1\), there is a constant \(c_M>0\) with the following property. Let \(L\ge1\), let \(n_1,\ldots,n_L\ge1\) be integers, and let \(A_i\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty. Give every atom \(x\in A_i\) a strictly positive weight \(w_{i,x}\). Suppose every coordinatewise parity class of every \(A_i\) has at most \(M\) atoms, and every distinct same-parity pair \(x,y\in A_i\) satisfies
$$
j=(x+y)/2\in A_i,\qquad w_{i,j}^{\,2}=w_{i,x}w_{i,y}.
\tag{1}
$$
For each \(i\), suppose there is a finite list of positive bases \(T_{i,1},\ldots,T_{i,\ell_i}\), possibly empty, with \(T_{i,b+1}\ge4T_{i,b}\) whenever both indices exist. Require every nonzero score of a distinct same-parity pair to satisfy
$$
\frac12\left|\log\frac{w_{i,x}}{w_{i,y}}\right|
\in[0,1]\ \cup\ \bigcup_{b=1}^{\ell_i}[T_{i,b},3T_{i,b}/2].
\tag{2}
$$
There is no positive lower bound on the first base. Empty bands, empty lists, equal-weight pairs, singleton classes, lower-dimensional supports, and arbitrary numbers of pairs with the same midpoint are allowed.

Keep the entire original supports, normalizers, probabilities, occupied parity counts, and product:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_i&=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},&p_{i,x}&=w_{i,x}/Z_i,&
K_i&=\bigl|\lbrace x+2\mathbb Z^{n_i}:x\in A_i\rbrace\bigr|,\\
P&=\bigotimes_{i=1}^Lp_i,&N&=\sum_{i=1}^Ln_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
Extend each law by zero outside its support. At every original product center \(\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i\), define the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth and its original-law moments by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)&=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }\mathbf j\rbrace,\\
M_r(P)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\prod_iA_i}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^r
\qquad(r=2,3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
Then
$$
M_2(P)\ge c_M\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-2},\qquad
M_3(P)\ge c_M^{3/2}\prod_{i=1}^LK_i^{-3}.
\tag{5}
$$
The same coefficient is paid once for the whole product. It depends only on \(M\), and is independent of factor count, dimensions, original weights, occupied parity counts, band counts, maximum scores, total band width, and first positive band bases. The proof constructs it explicitly from finitely many positive minima on compact sets and verifies every such minimum and every local comparison needed at its possible zeros.

## Proof

The original-law and moment conventions are those of accepted facts 31c9b0b9219d18d9, 0539ec4ffa895fed, 149d1105f63274b9, and d727395b991f2895. Here \(K\) always counts occupied parity classes. The scaled mass \(\rho_x\) below is \(t_x\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9, and \(D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\) is its \(\mathcal D\). Its full-row multiplier is replaced by a selected multiplier here; its full-row theorem is not applied to the selection. The quantities \(E_c,V_c,\chi_c,b_c,\nu_c,h_j\) have the selected-row definitions of 149d1105f63274b9 and d727395b991f2895, with the new selection specified below. The new coefficient \(C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)\) is not the occupancy-four coefficient \(C_c(U)\) of those facts. The present \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\) equals \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{un}}\) of 0539ec4ffa895fed, and the later star sum \(B\) is the restriction of that fact's \(B_*\) to high stars. Our \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}\) sums all omitted pairs, so it need not equal the high-only loss \(\Lambda_H\) of d727395b991f2895. All estimates affected by these changes are proved here. In particular, no extension of the false five-mass scalar inequality identified in 149d1105f63274b9 is used.

Fix one factor and suppress its index. Let \(\mathcal P(A)\) be its occupied representatives in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\), let \(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\), and put \(m_\eta=|\mathcal C_\eta|\). Let \(\mathscr Q\) be all unordered pairs of distinct atoms from a common parity class. For \(e=\lbrace x,y\rbrace\in\mathscr Q\), set \(j_e=(x+y)/2\) and \(s_e=\frac12|\log(p_x/p_y)|\). Equation (1) also holds for the probabilities, since the normalizer cancels. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_x&=Kp_x,&R_\eta&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\rho_x,&
D_{\mathrm{un}}&=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal P(A)}(R_\eta-1)^2,\\
R_j&=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace,&
Y_j(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_x}{p_{2j-x}}\quad(x\in R_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
In particular, \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\). The class cardinality \(m_\eta\), the selected clique cardinality \(k_\eta\) below, and the number of pairs centered at a point are different quantities.

We first establish the new core estimate, without assuming the spectral hypothesis (2). Fix any \(U\ge0\). In every original class choose a nonempty subset \(A_\eta^c\subseteq\mathcal C_\eta\) containing a largest original atom of that class, and suppose every pair of distinct atoms in \(A_\eta^c\) has score at most \(U\). Put \(k_\eta=|A_\eta^c|\). Retain exactly all pairs within these cliques as \(\mathscr Q_c\); every other original pair is omitted. At every original center define
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{c,j}&=\lbrace j\rbrace\ \cup
\bigcup_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}e,&
r_{c,j}&=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_c:j_e=j\rbrace|,&h_j&=1+2r_{c,j},\\
H_c(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{c,j}}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}=h_jp_j,&
\chi_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j),&b_c&=\sum_jp_jH_c(j)^2,\\
E_c&=K^2b_c,&V_c&=\sum_jp_j(KH_c(j)-1)^2,\\
\kappa_{c,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_c(j)}\quad(x\in R_{c,j}),&
q_{c,j}&=\frac{p_jH_c(j)^2}{b_c},\\
v_c(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{c,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_c&=\sum_jq_{c,j}v_c(j),&
\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}&=2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q\setminus\mathscr Q_c}\rho_{j_e}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$
Every sum over centers is over all of \(A\). Different pairs at a fixed center have disjoint endpoints, because each endpoint has the unique reflected mate \(2j-x\); none contains its center. Exact midpoint products make every geometric-mean summand equal to \(p_j\), including the central summand. Thus the displayed formula for \(H_c\) holds, the selected row law is uniform, and all its denominators are positive. The positive odd multiplier \(h_j\) can be arbitrarily large.

The original class-square expansion, counting every original pair once before its factor two, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
K^2\chi_c
&=\sum_jh_j\rho_j^2
=\sum_j\rho_j^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_c}\rho_{j_e}^2\\
&=\sum_\eta R_\eta^2-\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}
=K+D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}},\\
K\chi_c&=1+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}}{K},\\
E_c&=1+V_c+\frac{2D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}-\frac{2\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}}{K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
The last line follows by expanding \(V_c=E_c-2K\chi_c+1\). Shared midpoints do not identify different summands in \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}\). Jensen under the original law gives \(E_c\ge(K\chi_c)^2\), and \(\chi_c>0\). Moreover, retaining all centers gives
$$
E_c=K^2\sum_jh_j^2p_j^3
\ge K^2\sum_jp_j^3
\ge\frac{K^2}{|A|^2}\ge M^{-2}.
\tag{9}
$$
Here \(\sum_jp_j^3\ge|A|^{-2}\) is Jensen for the uniform law on the \(|A|\) original atoms, and \(|A|\le MK\).

We prove that a finite constant \(C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)\ge1\) satisfies
$$
E_c\nu_c\le C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)
\left(V_c+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}\right)
\le C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)
\left(E_c-1+\frac{2\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}}{K}\right).
\tag{10}
$$
The second inequality follows from (8), since the two brackets differ by \(D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\ge0\). Their larger bracket is \(V_c+2D_{\mathrm{un}}/K\ge0\), regardless of whether \(E_c\) is below one. If \(M=1\), no pairs exist. If \(U=0\), every selected pair score is zero. In either case \(\nu_c=0\), and (10) holds with \(C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)=1\). For the construction below assume \(M\ge2\) and \(U>0\).

Put \(\mathsf B=\binom M2U^2\) and \(a=(2M e^{2U})^{-1}\). For each original class define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi_x&=\rho_x(h_x\rho_x-1)^2,\\
F_\eta&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}h_x-\bigl(m_\eta+k_\eta(k_\eta-1)\bigr),\\
f_\eta&=(R_\eta-1)^2+\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}\Psi_x,\\
S_\eta&=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\e\subseteq\mathcal C_\eta}}s_e^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
Thus \(0\le S_\eta\le\mathsf B\). The excess \(F_\eta\) changes the full-class benchmark \(m_\eta^2\) of 31c9b0b9219d18d9 to the actual clique benchmark in (11). The exact global counting identity is
$$
\sum_xh_x=|A|+2|\mathscr Q_c|
=\sum_\eta\bigl(m_\eta+k_\eta(k_\eta-1)\bigr),
\qquad \sum_\eta F_\eta=0.
\tag{12}
$$
Each retained pair contributes two at its midpoint, independently of its source class. When \(k_\eta=1\), one has \(F_\eta\ge0\), because every original multiplier is at least one.

Call a class balanced when \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). Its largest scaled mass is at least \(1/(2M)\). That atom lies in \(A_\eta^c\), so every selected clique mass is at least \(a\), by the pair-score bound \(U\). Every mass in the class is at most two. Ignored masses need no positive lower bound. For \(1\le k\le m\le M\), define the nonempty compact set
$$
\mathcal K_{m,k}=
\left\lbrace(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\in[a,2]^k\times[0,2]^{m-k}:
\frac12\le\sum_{i=1}^m t_i\le2\right\rbrace.
\tag{13}
$$
This is a closed subset of a compact box, and the vector with its first \(k\) coordinates equal to \(1/k\) and its others zero belongs to it. Indeed \(a\le1/(2M)\le1/k\le1\). Every actual balanced class lies in this set after its selected atoms are enumerated first. The zero values permitted in the ignored coordinates are essential to the argument.

For an odd positive integer vector \(\mathbf h=(h_1,\ldots,h_m)\), define on (13)
$$
f_{\mathbf h}(\mathbf t)=
\left(\sum_{i=1}^m t_i-1\right)^2
+\sum_{i=1}^m t_i(h_it_i-1)^2.
\tag{14}
$$
It is continuous and nonnegative. At any zero, every selected coordinate is positive and equals \(1/h_i\), while an ignored coordinate is either zero or \(1/h_i\). Set \(Q=\sum_{i=1}^k1/h_i\) only for this zero analysis. Then \(0<Q\le1\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$$
\sum_{i=1}^m h_i
\ge\sum_{i=1}^k h_i+(m-k)
\ge\frac{k^2}{Q}+(m-k)
\ge k^2+(m-k)=m+k(k-1).
\tag{15}
$$

For negative excess, the multiplier vector satisfies \(\sum_i h_i<m+k(k-1)\le M^2\). There are only finitely many such vectors over all \(m,k\), and (15) excludes every zero on (13), including its boundary. Each such polynomial therefore has a strictly positive minimum on its nonempty compact set. Let \(\delta_->0\) be the minimum of \(1/4\) and these finitely many positive minima; use \(\delta_-=1/4\) if this finite family is empty. For an actual unbalanced class, \(f_\eta\ge(R_\eta-1)^2\ge1/4\). Thus every negative-excess class satisfies \(f_\eta\ge\delta_-\). Also \(-F_\eta\le M^2\), so
$$
\sum_{F_\eta<0}(-F_\eta)
\le\frac{M^2}{\delta_-}\sum_{F_\eta<0}f_\eta.
\tag{16}
$$

For zero excess, \(\sum_i h_i=m+k(k-1)\), so the possible multiplier vectors again form a finite family. If (14) vanishes, equality holds throughout (15). Hence \(Q=1\), every ignored multiplier is one, and equality in Cauchy--Schwarz makes all selected multipliers equal. Their sum is \(k^2\), so each is \(k\); their masses are each \(1/k\). Their total mass is already one, so every ignored mass is zero. Conversely, these multipliers and masses give a zero whenever \(k\) is odd. The parity condition is necessary because all multipliers are odd. This is the entire zero set for zero excess on (13).

For \(k=1\) there are no selected pairs, so \(S_\eta=0\) without an estimate. For each zero-excess vector with \(k\ge2\) that is not the just-described canonical vector, take the whole compact set (13). For the canonical vector, take its closed subset where
\(\max_{1\le i\le k}|t_i-1/k|\ge1/(2k)\).
Discard empty subsets. None of these compact sets contains a zero of its corresponding polynomial. Let \(\delta_0>0\) be the minimum of one and all their strictly positive polynomial minima, with \(\delta_0=1\) if the family is empty. This construction is finite and depends only on \(M,U\). For an actual class in any such set, \(S_\eta\le(\mathsf B/\delta_0)f_\eta\).

It remains to prove a quadratic estimate near each possible zero; compactness alone would not provide it. There the selected multipliers equal \(k\), and \(|t_i-1/k|<1/(2k)\) for all \(i\le k\), so \(t_i>1/(2k)\). Put \(b_i=t_i-1/k\) in this calculation. On intervals between selected masses the derivative of \(\log t\) is at most \(2k\), while
\(t_i(kt_i-1)^2=k^2t_i b_i^2\ge(k/2)b_i^2\).
For every selected pair \(i,j\), therefore,
$$
\frac14\left(\log\frac{t_i}{t_j}\right)^2
\le k^2(t_i-t_j)^2
\le2k^2(b_i^2+b_j^2)
\le4k\bigl(t_i(kt_i-1)^2+t_j(kt_j-1)^2\bigr).
\tag{17}
$$
Summing over the clique uses each selected summand exactly \(k-1\) times. No logarithm of an ignored mass occurs, so its possible zero value causes no exception. This proves \(S_\eta\le4k(k-1)\sum_{x\in A_\eta^c}\Psi_x\) in this neighborhood. For a zero-excess unbalanced class, \(S_\eta\le4\mathsf B f_\eta\). Consequently
$$
L_0=\max\left\lbrace4\mathsf B,\frac{\mathsf B}{\delta_0},4M(M-1)\right\rbrace
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad S_\eta\le L_0 f_\eta\quad(F_\eta=0).
\tag{18}
$$

For nonzero integer excess, \(|F_\eta|\ge1\) implies \(S_\eta\le\mathsf B|F_\eta|\). Combining this with conservation (12) and (16), and then with (18), gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{F_\eta\ne0}S_\eta
&\le2\mathsf B\sum_{F_\eta<0}(-F_\eta)
\le\frac{2\mathsf B M^2}{\delta_-}\sum_{F_\eta<0}f_\eta,\\
L_*&=\max\left\lbrace L_0,\frac{2\mathsf B M^2}{\delta_-}\right\rbrace,\\
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q_c}s_e^2
&\le L_*\sum_\eta f_\eta
=L_*\left(D_{\mathrm{un}}+KV_c\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$
Here \(\sum_x\Psi_x=KV_c\) follows directly from (7). Positive excess is paid globally by negative excess; no pointwise source-class inequality for arbitrary odd multipliers has been claimed.

Uniformity of the selected geometric row law yields
$$
v_c(j)=\frac2{h_j}\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}s_e^2,
\qquad
E_c\nu_c=\frac2K\sum_jh_j\rho_j^3
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr Q_c\\j_e=j}}s_e^2.
\tag{20}
$$
For a center with \(h_j\rho_j\notin[1/2,2]\), its whole numerator before division by \(K\) is at most
$$
2U^2h_j\rho_j^3r_{c,j}
=U^2h_j(h_j-1)\rho_j^3
\le U^2\rho_j(h_j\rho_j)^2
\le4U^2\Psi_j.
\tag{21}
$$
The last inequality uses \(v^2\le4(v-1)^2\) for positive \(v\) outside \([1/2,2]\). This charges a whole center once, irrespective of its pair multiplicity. At any remaining center,
\(h_j\rho_j^3=(h_j\rho_j)^3/h_j^2\le8\),
so each pair contributes at most \(16s_e^2\) to that numerator. Equations (19)--(21) prove
$$
E_c\nu_c
\le4U^2V_c+16L_*\left(V_c+\frac{D_{\mathrm{un}}}{K}\right).
\tag{22}
$$
Thus define \(C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)=1+4U^2+16L_*\) when \(M\ge2,U>0\), and use the earlier value one otherwise. This finite constant proves (10). An atom can be both a center and an endpoint elsewhere; the sum of the two displayed coefficients in (22) accounts for both uses. There is no restriction on midpoint multiplicity. This completes the new core estimate within the present theorem.

We now choose the cliques from the spectrum (2), setting
\(U=96M\) and \(C=C_{\mathrm{core}}(M,U)\ge1\).
Call a pair low if its score is zero, is at most one, or belongs to a listed band whose base is strictly less than \(64M\). Call every other pair high, and denote these two sets by \(\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{low}}\) and \(\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{high}}\). The listed bands are disjoint because their bases increase by a factor of at least four. The definitions agree even where a small band overlaps \([0,1]\). Every low score is at most \(U\), and every high pair lies in a unique band with base at least \(64M\). In particular a band whose base equals \(64M\) is high, including its closed endpoints.

In each original class choose a largest atom \(z_\eta\), breaking ties lexicographically, and put
$$
A_\eta^c=\lbrace z_\eta\rbrace\ \cup
\lbrace x\in\mathcal C_\eta\setminus\lbrace z_\eta\rbrace:
\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{low}}\rbrace.
\tag{23}
$$
The core \(\mathscr Q_c\) retains every pair within these subsets and no other pair; it need not retain every low pair. If no actual high pair exists, every subset in (23) is its entire class, so the core is full and all scores are at most \(U\). Then \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}=0\), and (8), (10) give \(E_c\ge1\) and
\(\nu_c\le C(E_c-1)/E_c\le C\log E_c\).
Such factors will require no high-band label. This includes \(M=1\) and an empty list.

Suppose an actual high pair exists. Disregard high bands containing no pair, and let \(T_{\min}\ge64M\) be the smallest remaining high base. Every low score is strictly smaller than \(T_{\min}\): scores at most one satisfy this directly, and the upper endpoint of any earlier listed band is at most \(3T_{\min}/8\). For atoms in (23), write their nonnegative scores from the maximum as \(s_{zx}=\frac12\log(\rho_{z_\eta}/\rho_x)\), including \(s_{zz}=0\). For any two such atoms, their mutual score is \(|s_{zx}-s_{zy}|\), hence is at most the larger of these low star scores. It is at most \(U\) and strictly below \(T_{\min}\). The allowed spectrum therefore makes this mutual pair low. Thus (23) is indeed a clique satisfying the core estimate. Every high pair is omitted, but there may be additional omitted low or zero-score pairs.

Every outside atom has \(s_{zx}\ge T_{\min}\), while every inside atom has \(s_{zx}<T_{\min}\). Consequently every outside mass is strictly smaller than every inside mass. An omitted pair has at least one outside endpoint. Orient it so that \(\rho_x\le\rho_y\), breaking an equality arbitrarily. Its smaller endpoint \(x\) is therefore outside, even when the pair itself has score zero. Its star \(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\) is high, and exact midpoint products give
$$
\rho_{(x+y)/2}^2=\rho_x\rho_y
\le\rho_x\rho_{z_\eta}
=\rho_{(x+z_\eta)/2}^2.
\tag{24}
$$

In every balanced original class take all its high stars \(\lbrace z_\eta,x\rbrace\), forming \(\mathscr S_H\). For each actual high band define
$$
B=\sum_{e\in\mathscr S_H}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\qquad
B_b=\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathscr S_H\\s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]}}\rho_{j_e}^2,
\qquad B=\sum_bB_b,
\qquad\beta=B/K.
\tag{25}
$$
For a fixed smaller endpoint in (24) there are at most \(M-1\) omitted pairs charged to its star. This counts all balanced omitted pairs, with their multiplicities, including ties and zero-score pairs. Their total squared midpoint mass is at most \(MB\).

In an unbalanced class the same smaller endpoint is outside, so
\(\rho_x\le\rho_{z_\eta}e^{-2T_{\min}}\) and \(\rho_y\le\rho_{z_\eta}\).
Thus every omitted pair, whatever its own score, has squared midpoint mass at most \(e^{-2T_{\min}}R_\eta^2\). There are at most \(\binom M2\) pairs, and unbalanced masses satisfy \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\). Including the factor two in the loss yields
$$
\Lambda_{\mathrm{om}}\le2MB+\epsilon D_{\mathrm{un}},
\qquad \epsilon=4M^2e^{-2T_{\min}}\le\frac12.
\tag{26}
$$
For the last inequality, the elementary induction \(M\le2^{M-1}\) and \(e>2\) give
\(4M^2e^{-128M}<2^{2M-128M}=2^{-126M}<1/2\).
No high-score bound has been applied to an omitted pair merely because it was omitted; the outside-endpoint property is what proves (26).

Every midpoint of a balanced high star has an unbalanced own parity class. Indeed its largest endpoint has mass \(\alpha\in[1/(2M),2]\). For a star in band \(b\) with score \(s\in[T_b,3T_b/2]\), its midpoint \(j\) has mass \(\rho_j=\alpha e^{-s}\le2e^{-T_b}<1/(2M)\). The strict inequality follows from \(T_b\ge64M>\log(4M)\). If the own class of \(j\) were balanced, its largest atom would have mass \(\alpha'\in[1/(2M),2]\), and would differ from \(j\). Their positive pair score would be
$$
s'=\frac{s}{2}+\frac12\log\frac{\alpha'}{\alpha}
\in[T_b/2-\tfrac12\log(4M),\,3T_b/4+\tfrac12\log(4M)]
\subset(3T_b/8,T_b).
\tag{27}
$$
The inclusions are strict since \(T_b\ge64M>4\log(4M)\); the latter follows from \(\log(4M)\le4M-1\). The lower endpoint \(3T_b/8\) is greater than one. Every earlier listed band ends at or below \(3T_b/8\), and the current and later bands start at or above \(T_b\). Thus (27) contradicts (2), including when a band is empty or its endpoints are attained by other scores.

Let \(J_b\) be the set of distinct midpoints of the balanced high stars in band \(b\), and let \(r_{b,j}\) count those stars at a fixed midpoint. Each class meeting \(J_b\) is unbalanced and contributes at least \(1/4\) to \(D_{\mathrm{un}}\). There are at most \(4D_{\mathrm{un}}\) such classes, hence at most \(4M D_{\mathrm{un}}\) distinct target atoms. Since their masses are at most \(2e^{-T_b}\),
$$
\sum_{j\in J_b}\rho_j\le8M e^{-T_b}D_{\mathrm{un}},
\qquad B_b=\sum_{j\in J_b}r_{b,j}\rho_j^2.
\tag{28}
$$
The first sum counts distinct atoms, whereas the second retains all star multiplicities. The loss bound (26) and the target bound (28) are separate inequalities in the same total defect, not a claim that their source and target classes are disjoint.

For each high band define an alternative at every original center by adding the entire original high band to its core:
\(R_{b,j}=R_{c,j}\cup\bigcup\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q_{\mathrm{high}}:j_e=j,\ s_e\in[T_b,3T_b/2]\rbrace\).
This adjoins pairs from balanced and unbalanced source classes alike. Omitted low pairs remain omitted. Let \(r_{\mathrm{all},b,j}\) count these added pairs. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=\sum_{x\in R_{b,j}}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},&
F_b&=K^2\sum_jp_jH_b(j)^2,&G_b&=F_b-E_c,\\
\kappa_{b,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}}{H_b(j)},&
q_{b,j}&=\frac{K^2p_jH_b(j)^2}{F_b},\\
v_b(j)&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{b,j}}Y_j^2,&
\nu_b&=\sum_jq_{b,j}v_b(j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
All centers and probabilities are original. The added band is disjoint from the core, and reflection orbits at a fixed center are disjoint. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_b(j)&=(h_j+2r_{\mathrm{all},b,j})p_j,\\
