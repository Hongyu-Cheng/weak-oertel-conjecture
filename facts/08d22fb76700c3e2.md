## Statement

Let \(m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let \(a,b,Q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) satisfy \(\gcd(a,b)=1\) and \(Q\ge2ab\), let \(c_1,\ldots,c_m\) be positive real numbers, let \(\theta_i\in\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\theta_i\le0\) for every \(1\le i\le m\), and let \(\lambda\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\). The requested unit-cost case is the specialization \(c_i=1\) for every light coordinate. All coordinates and resource parameters below are auxiliary and are not identified with the locked problem's \(n,d,C,S\), or \(h_S\). Empty sums and the unique vector in \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^0\) have their usual meanings. Define the original support, original normalizer, and original law by
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf z,x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m+2}:\sum_{i=1}^m c_i z_i+ax+by\le Q\right\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{(\mathbf z,x,y)\in D}e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i+\lambda(ax+by)},\qquad
p(\mathbf z,x,y)=\frac{e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i+\lambda(ax+by)}}{Z}.
\]
For \(R\ge0\), let
\[
Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:\sum_i c_i z_i\le R}}e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i},\qquad
Q_R(\mathbf z)=\frac{e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i}}{Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)}
\quad\text{when }\sum_i c_i z_i\le R,
\]
and extend \(Q_R\) by zero outside that light support. There exist integers \(0\le q_0\le q_1\le Q\), with both endpoint heights represented by nonnegative integer heavy coordinates, such that, for the complete heavy band
\[
E=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:q_0\le ax+by\le q_1\},\qquad
N=|E|,\qquad R=Q-q_1,
\]
the coefficient \(\eta=N Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda q_0}/Z\) satisfies
\[
\eta\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{300(m+1)^2},\qquad
p\ge\eta\bigl(Q_R\otimes\operatorname{Uniform}(E)\bigr)
\quad\text{pointwise}.
\]
The product is extended by zero outside its product support, and \(Z\) sums all original atoms of \(D\). In particular, no conditioning on represented heights, residue classes, positive heights, or a dense subinterval is made. The result includes \(m=0\), zero light scores, \(\lambda=0\), zero common budget, and exact weak resource boundaries.

## Proof

All resource coefficients are positive, so the original support and every light support occurring above are finite and contain the origin. Consequently \(Z>0\), and \(Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)>0\) for every \(R\ge0\).

Write \(P=ab\), \(k=m+1\), and let \(n(q)\) denote the number of nonnegative integer pairs \((x,y)\) with \(ax+by=q\). This is a multiplicity function, unrelated to the locked problem's integer dimension. We first prove, for every integer \(q\ge0\),
\[
\left\lfloor\frac qP\right\rfloor\le n(q)\le\left\lfloor\frac qP\right\rfloor+1.
\tag{1}
\]
There is a unique \(x_0\in\{0,\ldots,b-1\}\) with \(ax_0\equiv q\pmod b\), because \(a\) and \(b\) are coprime. Put \(y_0=(q-ax_0)/b\), an integer. All integer solutions with nonnegative \(x\) are \(x=x_0+jb\), \(y=y_0-ja\), with an integer \(j\ge0\). Thus \(n(q)=\max\{0,\lfloor y_0/a\rfloor+1\}\). If \(q=tP+r\), where \(t\ge0\) and \(0\le r<P\), then \(y_0/a=t+r/P-x_0/b\), so \(\lfloor y_0/a\rfloor\) is either \(t-1\) or \(t\). This proves (1), including \(b=1\) and \(q=0\).

The dense-range assumption implies the following two bounds:
\[
0\le q\le Q\Longrightarrow n(q)\le\frac{3Q}{2P},\qquad
\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\le q\le Q\Longrightarrow n(q)\ge\frac{Q}{4P}>0.
\tag{2}
\]
For the first, (1) gives \(n(q)\le Q/P+1\le3Q/(2P)\). For the second, integer \(P\) gives \(\lfloor\lfloor Q/2\rfloor/P\rfloor=\lfloor Q/(2P)\rfloor\). Hence (1) gives \(n(q)\ge\lfloor Q/(2P)\rfloor\ge Q/(4P)\), using \(Q/(2P)\ge1\) and \(\lfloor t\rfloor\ge t/2\) for \(t\ge1\). In particular, if \(q'\ge\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\) and \(0\le q\le Q\), then \(n(q')\ge n(q)/6\).

For integer residual budgets \(0\le t\le Q\), put \(B_t=Z_{\mathrm{light}}(t)\) and
\[
A_t=n(Q-t)B_t e^{-\lambda t},\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{res}}=\sum_{t=0}^Q A_t=e^{-\lambda Q}Z.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, at heavy height \(q\), the total light weight is exactly \(Z_{\mathrm{light}}(Q-q)\), and there are exactly \(n(q)\) heavy pairs. Therefore (3) is the full original normalizer in residual coordinates. Values with \(n(Q-t)=0\) remain in this table, with zero mass; in particular no removal or renormalization has occurred. The numbers \(B_t\) are positive and nondecreasing.

We need an elementary light-growth estimate. For integers \(1\le u\le v\le Q\), put \(d=\lceil v/u\rceil\). Map every vector \(\mathbf z\) feasible at light budget \(v\) to \(\widehat{\mathbf z}=(\lfloor z_i/d\rfloor)_i\). Then \(\sum_i c_i\widehat z_i\le v/d\le u\), and each image has at most \(d^m\) preimages. Also \(z_i\ge\widehat z_i\) and \(\theta_i\le0\) imply \(e^{\sum_i\theta_i z_i}\le e^{\sum_i\theta_i\widehat z_i}\). Summing yields
\[
B_v\le\lceil v/u\rceil^m B_u.
\tag{4}
\]
This proof uses only the positivity of the real costs \(c_i\) and the signs of the light scores. For \(m=0\), it is the identity \(B_v=B_u=1\). In particular, \(B_t\le2^m B_{\lceil t/2\rceil}\) for every integer \(2\le t\le Q\).

We next give a band-selection observation that keeps the actual multiplicities. Suppose \(1\le u\le v\le Q\), and suppose \(\sum_{t=u}^v A_t\ge\alpha Z_{\mathrm{res}}\) for some \(\alpha>0\). Define \(G=\log(B_v/B_u)\) and \(W=\lambda(v-u)\). Partition the consecutive integers \(u,\ldots,v\) according to the pair
\[
\left(\left\lfloor\log(B_t/B_u)\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\lambda(t-u)\right\rfloor\right).
\]
Both entries are nondecreasing in \(t\). Each distinct pair therefore occupies a complete consecutive interval, and each change increases the sum of the entries by at least one. Thus the number \(J\) of partition pieces satisfies \(J\le1+\lfloor G\rfloor+\lfloor W\rfloor\le1+G+W\). On a piece \(r,\ldots,s\), one has \(B_t\le e B_r\) and \(e^{-\lambda t}\le e e^{-\lambda s}\) for every index in the piece. Consequently
\[
\sum_{t=r}^s A_t\le e^2 B_r e^{-\lambda s}\sum_{t=r}^s n(Q-t).
\tag{5}
\]
Some piece has original mass at least \(\alpha Z_{\mathrm{res}}/J\), and hence positive mass. Trim only its unoccupied endpoints, obtaining the smallest and largest occupied indices \(r'\) and \(s'\). Its mass and its heavy-pair count are unchanged, while \(B_{r'}e^{-\lambda s'}\ge B_r e^{-\lambda s}\). The complete heavy band with \(q_0=Q-s'\) and \(q_1=Q-r'\) has occupied endpoints, has count \(N=\sum_{t=r'}^{s'}n(Q-t)\), and has coefficient
\[
\eta=\frac{N B_{r'}e^{-\lambda s'}}{Z_{\mathrm{res}}}\ge\frac{e^{-2}\alpha}{J}.
\tag{6}
\]
No variation bound on \(n(Q-t)\) inside a partition piece was used.

If \(Q\le3\), some residual index has original mass at least \(1/(Q+1)\ge1/4\). Its singleton heavy-height band has exactly that coefficient by (3), so the theorem follows. Assume henceforth \(Q\ge4\).

We first treat \(\lambda=0\), together with the case \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(Q<4s_0\), where \(s_0=\lceil1/\lambda\rceil\) is used only in the latter case. Put \(d=\lfloor Q/4\rfloor\ge1\). For \(0\le t<d\), the shifted index \(t+d\) is at most \(2d-1\le Q/2\). Thus (2), monotonicity of \(B_t\), and (3) give
\[
A_{t+d}\ge\frac{e^{-\lambda d}}6 A_t\ge\frac{e^{-2}}6 A_t.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\lambda=0\) the last inequality is immediate; otherwise \(\lambda d\le\lambda Q/4<\lambda s_0<2\). The shift is injective and its image lies in \(d,\ldots,Q\). Hence the mass of \(d,\ldots,Q\), divided by \(Z_{\mathrm{res}}\), is at least \(1/(1+6e^2)\ge e^{-2}/7\).

Writing \(Q=4d+r\) with \(0\le r\le3\) shows \(\lceil Q/d\rceil\le7\). Therefore (4) gives \(\log(B_Q/B_d)\le m\log7\le2m\). Also \(\lambda Q=0\) in the zero-tilt case, and \(\lambda Q<4\lambda s_0<8\) in the other case. The partition of \(d,\ldots,Q\) consequently has at most \(2m+9\le9k\) pieces. Applying (6) with \(\alpha=e^{-2}/7\) gives a complete band with
\[
\eta\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{63k}\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{300k^2}.
\tag{8}
\]

For the remaining positive-tilt cases, define the even integer
\[
T=2\left\lceil\frac{m\log2+\log168+2}{\lambda}\right\rceil\ge2.
\tag{9}
\]
For every \(T\le t\le Q\), put \(j=\lceil t/2\rceil\). Then \(Q-j\ge\lfloor Q/2\rfloor\). Applying (2), (4), and (3) gives
\[
A_t\le6\cdot2^m e^{-\lambda\lfloor t/2\rfloor}A_j
\le6\cdot2^m e^{-\lambda T/2}A_j.
\]
Each target \(j\) has at most two preimages, and every target belongs to the original residual range. Consequently the tail, interpreted as empty when \(T>Q\), satisfies
\[
\sum_{t=T}^Q A_t\le12\cdot2^m e^{-\lambda T/2}Z_{\mathrm{res}}
\le\frac{e^{-2}}{14}Z_{\mathrm{res}}.
\tag{10}
\]
This comparison uses only the original normalizer, with no comparison probability law.

If \(\lambda\ge1\), (9), \(\log2<1\), and \(\log168<6\) give \(T\le2m+18\le18k\). By (10), the interval \(0,\ldots,\min(Q,T-1)\) has mass at least \(13Z_{\mathrm{res}}/14\) and contains at most \(T\) indices. Some occupied singleton therefore gives
\(\eta\ge13/(252k)\ge1/(20k)\ge e^{-4}/(300k^2)\).

It remains to handle \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(Q\ge4s_0\), where \(s_0=\lceil1/\lambda\rceil\). For \(0\le t<s_0\), the shifted residual \(t+s_0\) is at most \(2s_0-1\le Q/2\). Since \(\lambda s_0<2\), the same calculation as (7) gives \(A_{t+s_0}\ge(e^{-2}/6)A_t\). The injective shift implies that the mass of \(s_0,\ldots,Q\) is at least \((e^{-2}/7)Z_{\mathrm{res}}\). After removing the tail (10), the complete interval
\(u=s_0,\ldots,v=\min(Q,T-1)\)
has mass at least \((e^{-2}/14)Z_{\mathrm{res}}\), so it is nonempty. Also \(T\ge2s_0\), since the numerator inside the ceiling in (9) exceeds one.

Equation (9) and \(\lambda<1\) give \(\lambda T\le2m+18\). Since \(s_0\ge1/\lambda\), we have \(v/u<T/s_0\le\lambda T\le2m+18\). Thus (4) implies
\[
\log(B_v/B_u)\le m\log(2m+18)\le m\log(18k)\le m(m+3),\qquad
\lambda(v-u)\le2m+18,
\]
where \(\log18<3\) and \(\log k\le k-1=m\) were used. The partition has at most \(m^2+5m+19\le19k^2\) pieces. Applying (6) yields
\[
\eta\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{266k^2}\ge\frac{e^{-4}}{300k^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
The elementary numerical logarithm bounds used above follow, for example, from \(e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3\): this gives \(e^2>7\), \(e^3>18\), and \(e^6>168\). Equations (8), (10)-(11), and the singleton cases exhaust all possible \(Q\) and \(\lambda\).

Finally, for any selected band, every point of the product support obeys \(\sum_i c_i z_i\le R=Q-q_1\) and \(ax+by\le q_1\), so it belongs to the original support \(D\). At each such point the exact ratio of its original probability to its product probability is
\[
\frac{p(\mathbf z,x,y)}{Q_R(\mathbf z)\operatorname{Uniform}(E)(x,y)}
=\frac{N Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda(ax+by)}}{Z}
\ge\frac{N Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda q_0}}{Z}=\eta.
\]
The claimed comparison follows everywhere after extending the product by zero. All resource inequalities throughout the proof are weak, so real light-cost boundaries and zero common budget require no further convention. This proves both the unit-cost target and the stated positive-real-light-cost extension.
