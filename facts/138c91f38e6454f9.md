## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty. Let the compact convex fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ of positive $d$-dimensional volume be the exact sampled fibers of their canonical convex hull $C$. The marked actual support graph consists of $(u,z,X)$ with $u$ a unit continuous normal, $z\in P$ and $X\in F_{K_z}(u)$. Suppose a finite law $\nu(ds,du)P_{s,u}(dt)$ has both $(u,s)$ and $(u,t)$ on the marked actual support graph, and its conditional target mean is $s$ with target marginal $\eta$. Here $s,t$ include the base and continuous coordinates. There is a marked pair law with source $\nu$, each endpoint marginal $\eta$, common normal and common endpoint parity, such that its midpoint $b$ is on the actual support graph and $\mathbb E[b\mid s,u]=s$. Its conditional midpoint mean after fixing the original parity need not equal $s$.

The same support conclusion holds for any prescribed marked pair law $\tau$ with common normal, equal parity within each pair, endpoints and source on the graph, and the same conditional midpoint mean. For its endpoint marginals $\gamma_\pm$, let $\eta=(\gamma_-+\gamma_+)/2$ and $\rho=b_\#\tau$, retaining the common normal in the marginals. For positive continuous concave $L$ on $C$,
$$
S=\int\log L\,d(\nu-\eta)
 =\int\log L\,d(\nu-\rho)+\Delta,\qquad
 \Delta=\int\log L\,d(\rho-\eta).
$$
Both terms on the right are nonnegative. The symmetric construction has identical endpoint laws within each normal and parity, and their diagonal coupling has midpoint law $\eta$, dominates the old midpoint in convex order on each such class, and has zero tuple-discount loss. Thus if $\eta\le\omega$ this diagonal uses one copy of $\omega$. If $\gamma_\pm\le\eta$ and their average is $\eta$, they are both equal to $\eta$.

For a proper same-normal and same-parity binary branch $\tau_b$ with endpoint marginals $\gamma_\pm$ of mass $m_b>0$ and midpoint law $\rho_b$ on the actual support graph, suppose $\gamma_-+\gamma_+\le\eta$ and $\nu=\eta-\gamma_--\gamma_++2\rho_b$. Then $\nu$ is the source of a physical normal-preserving martingale to $\eta$, and $S=2\Delta_b$ with $\Delta_b=\int\log L\,d(\rho_b-(\gamma_-+\gamma_+)/2)$. If a repaired branch with the same separate endpoint marginals on each normal/parity class and midpoint convex order above the old branch is supplied, and $L$ is finite piecewise affine, then its error $E_b=b_\#[(1-a^d)\tau_{b,\mathrm{new}}]$, with $a=(L(t_-)+L(t_+))/(2L(b))$, obeys
$$
E_b(\mathrm{total})\le m_b[1-\exp(-d S/(2m_b))].
$$
Replacing the branch in the rebuilt source gives error measure $2E_b$. If the repaired branch has discounted law $b_\#(a^d\tau_{b,\mathrm{new}})\le 2^n\omega$ for a common capacity $\omega$, the rebuilt branch has ceiling $2^{n+1}\omega$ on its discounted part, separate from the unchanged diagonal residual.

## Proof

Disintegrate at $(s,u)$. For each parity $r$ let $p_r$ be its probability in $P_{s,u}$ and $P_r$ its normalized restriction if $p_r>0$. The finite sum of $\nu(ds,du)p_rP_r(dt_-)P_r(dt_+)$ is Borel, omitting zero terms. Each endpoint marginal is $\eta$ by summation over $r$. Its conditional midpoint mean is $\sum_r p_r\int t\,dP_r=s$. For a prescribed pair, the conditional average endpoint law is also a probability with physical mean $s$. The parity-conditioned mean can differ from the source. Take the top endpoints of the constant intervals $[0,1]$ at bases $0,1,2,3$, with normal $u=1$. From the top at base $1$ send weights $2/3$ and $1/3$ to the tops at $0$ and $3$. The combined mean is the source, whereas the two parity-conditioned means are the two target points themselves.

Fix a source row with these properties. By the finite support roof lemma, the normal height roof $h$ is a minimum of finitely many affine planes and agrees with the support height at each sampled base. If $B$ is any plane active at the source base, the nonnegative endpoint quantities $B(z_\pm)-h(z_\pm)$ have average conditional expectation zero. Indeed, affine $B$ respects the base mean and $h(z_\pm)=u\cdot X_\pm$ respects the continuous mean. Both endpoint gaps are therefore zero almost surely. This argument applies rowwise to the finitely many planes for a fixed normal, with the asserted conclusion measured by the Borel condition of actual support at the midpoint. The contact cell of $B$ is convex. Along the segment between the endpoints the roof is that plane, so the midpoint has the target support height. Equal parity makes its base integral, the base belongs to $P$ by completeness, and compatibility puts its continuous coordinate in the exact sampled fiber. A midpoint can be a singular boundary point; only membership in its marked exposed face is used here.

Concavity of $\log L$ gives the nonnegative first term by conditional Jensen at the source. Pointwise midpoint Jensen gives the nonnegative second term because the endpoint integrals have exactly the two prescribed marginals. Adding gives the identity. For the symmetric construction each normal/parity endpoint law is identical. The diagonal therefore keeps both marginals, and a convex test of the old midpoint is bounded by the mean of its endpoint test values. The diagonal has $a=1$ for every positive $L$, and the original martingale restores the source. If both endpoints are dominated by their average, the two nonnegative deficit measures sum to zero.

For the proper branch put the residual $\eta-\gamma_--\gamma_+$ on the diagonal and send twice the midpoint law to its two endpoints by choosing each endpoint with probability one half, after disintegrating the pair law at the marked midpoint. The conditional mean equals the midpoint. This gives exactly the specified source and target, and substitution of the source identity gives $S=2\Delta_b$. In a repaired branch the logarithmic Jensen gap $\Delta_{b,\mathrm{new}}$ is bounded by $\Delta_b$ because the separate endpoint integrals are fixed and the midpoint integrals of $-\log L$ increase in convex order. This is a globally finite convex test after extending each negative logarithm of an affine denominator by its tangent below $\min_C L$ and taking the finite maximum. Since the arithmetic mean of two positive numbers is at least their geometric mean, the pointwise $\delta=\log L(b)-(\log L(t_-)+\log L(t_+))/2$ satisfies $-\log a\le\delta$. Jensen for the concave function $x\mapsto1-e^{-dx}$ on the normalized branch then bounds $E_b(\mathrm{total})$ by $m_b[1-\exp(-d\Delta_{b,\mathrm{new}}/m_b)]$. Substitution gives the claimed bound. The rebuilt source replaces $2\rho_b$ by $2\rho_{b,\mathrm{new}}$, which doubles both its error and its discounted branch law.
