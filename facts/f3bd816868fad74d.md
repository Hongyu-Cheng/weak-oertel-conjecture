## Statement

Let N>=1, let P be a compact convex subset of R^N, and let D=P intersect Z^N be finite and nonempty. Let ell in R^N, w_z=exp(ell dot z), W=sum_{z in D}w_z, and p_z=w_z/W. The canonical actual closed-halfspace depth and moment are
\[
\tau_p(z)=\min_{H\ni z}p(D\cap H),\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_{z\in D}p_z\tau_p(z)^3.
\]
Here and throughout, every H in a depth definition is a containing closed halfspace in R^N. Fix an arbitrary assignment of such halfspaces H_z to the sites z in D, and define its actual ordered event and quartic polynomial by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T_H&=\{(z,x_1,x_2,x_3)\in D^4:x_i\in H_z\ (1\le i\le3)\},\\
\Phi_H(w)&=\sum_{(z,x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\mathcal T_H}
w_z w_{x_1}w_{x_2}w_{x_3}
=\sum_{z\in D}w_z\left(\sum_{x\in D\cap H_z}w_x\right)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the actual original caps and original weights. In particular Phi_H(w)>=W^4 M_3(p), with equality for a minimizing assignment; the proposed theorem must lower-bound Phi_H for every assignment.

For 0<=t<=w_max:=max_D w_z, set
\[
D_t=\{z\in D:w_z\ge t\},\qquad n(t)=|D_t|,
\]
and use the actual restricted cap count
\[
\Psi_H(t)=\sum_{z\in D_t}|D_t\cap H_z|^3
=|\mathcal T_H\cap D_t^4|.
\]
For t=0, D_0 means D. Every nonempty D_t is a complete convex lattice support. No cap is reflected or replaced, and equality sites belong to D_t and the closed caps.

A common-threshold ledger is a nonnegative finite measure mu on [0,w_max], with cumulative charge F_mu(a)=mu([0,a]). It is called coefficient-admissible if each actual ordered event tuple is charged no more than its original monomial capacity:
\[
F_\mu\!\left(\min\{w_z,w_{x_1},w_{x_2},w_{x_3}\}\right)
\le w_z w_{x_1}w_{x_2}w_{x_3}
\quad\text{for every }(z,x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\mathcal T_H.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a global ledger over the entire actual event polynomial. Under (1), the certificate starts with Phi_H(w)>=integral Psi_H(t) dmu(t). Its next step is the sharp ambient-dimension uniform estimate from accepted fact 01a4100542206b94, at exponent 3, including that fact's atomic term. The resulting certified number is
\[
\mathscr C_\mu(D,w)
=\frac14\,8^{-N}\int n(t)^4\,d\mu(t)
+\frac34\int n(t)\,d\mu(t).
\tag{2}
\]
The p, D, N, tau_p, and M_3 normalizations in that predecessor agree exactly with those here; its moment exponent is specialized to 3. Its uniform law at a nonempty level is the auxiliary uniform law u_t(z)=1/n(t) on D_t, rather than a change to the original p.

Define the auxiliary integral and original atomic quartic sum
\[
\mathcal J(D,w)=\int_0^{w_{\max}}4t^3 n(t)^4\,dt,
\qquad
\mathcal A(w)=\sum_{z\in D}w_z^4.
\]
For every D, ell, and actual cap assignment above, the exact optimization over all coefficient-admissible common-threshold ledgers is
\[
\sup_\mu\mathscr C_\mu(D,w)
=\frac14\,8^{-N}\mathcal J(D,w)+\frac34\mathcal A(w).
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover there is a sequence, indexed by N>=1, of finite full-dimensional complete supports D_N=P_N intersect Z^N, original log-affine weights w^{(N)}, and explicit containing closed caps, such that
\[
\frac{8^N}{W_N^4}\sup_\mu\mathscr C_\mu(D_N,w^{(N)})
\le\frac94\lambda_N\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\lambda_N=\frac{(4N)!}{4^{4N}(N!)^4}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
The weights in this sequence are rational, strictly positive, and are never rescaled in the coefficient ledger. Consequently no absolute kappa>0 can be obtained for Phi_H(w)>=kappa 8^{-N} W^4 by this mechanism, even with free optimization of the threshold measure and the atomic improvement in (2).

## Proof

First verify the actual polynomial ledger and optimize it globally. Since H_z contains z, every diagonal tuple (z,z,z,z) belongs to T_H. Substituting it in (1) gives the necessary constraints
\[
F_\mu(w_z)\le w_z^4\qquad(z\in D).
\tag{5}
\]
They are also sufficient. For any actual event tuple, let a be the minimum of its four site weights. That minimum is w_y for one of its sites y. Thus (5) gives F_mu(a)<=a^4, while each of its four weights is at least a, so a^4<=w_z w_{x_1}w_{x_2}w_{x_3}. This proves (1). The equivalence between (1) and (5) uses the original diagonal event capacities, not a surrogate probability or a transport congestion assumption.

For a fixed tuple in T_H, it is counted in Psi_H(t) exactly when all its four sites belong to D_t, or equivalently 0<=t<=a. Finite nonnegative summation therefore gives the identity
\[
\int\Psi_H(t)\,d\mu(t)
=\sum_{(z,x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\mathcal T_H}
F_\mu\!\left(\min\{w_z,w_{x_1},w_{x_2},w_{x_3}\}\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (1) and (6) prove Phi_H(w)>=integral Psi_H dmu. Every original event tuple is present in (6), including repetitions and boundary tuples, and is individually charged at most its original product weight.

For t>0, the equality
\[
D_t=\left(P\cap\{x:\ell\cdot x\ge\log t\}\right)\cap\mathbb Z^N
\]
proves completeness and convexity of the level support. D_0=D is complete as well. At a nonempty level, each H_z for z in D_t is an actual containing closed halfspace. Applying accepted fact 01a4100542206b94 to the uniform law u_t, with its q=3, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Psi_H(t)}{n(t)^4}
&=\sum_{z\in D_t}\frac1{n(t)}
\left(\frac{|D_t\cap H_z|}{n(t)}\right)^3\\
&\ge M_3(u_t)
\ge\frac14\,8^{-N}+\frac34\sum_{z\in D_t}u_t(z)^4
=\frac14\,8^{-N}+\frac{3}{4n(t)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The same inequality after multiplication by n(t)^4 holds with both sides zero at an empty level. Integrating (7) in (6) gives precisely (2) as a certified lower bound for Phi_H(w). The predecessor requires no full-dimensionality, so small level supports pose no extra hypothesis.

List the distinct site weights in increasing order as 0<a_1<...<a_J=w_max, and put a_0=0. Let n_j be the number of site weights at least a_j, and set n_{J+1}=0. For t in (a_{j-1},a_j], n(t)=n_j. An atom of mu at zero is included in the first interval's cumulative charge. For either b=1 or b=4, exact finite summation by parts yields
\[
\int n(t)^b\,d\mu(t)
=\sum_{j=1}^J(n_j^b-n_{j+1}^b)F_\mu(a_j).
\tag{8}
\]
Every coefficient in this expression is nonnegative. By (5), it is at most the same expression with F_mu(a_j) replaced by a_j^4. The measure
\[
d\mu_*(t)=4t^3\,dt\quad(0\le t\le w_{\max})
\tag{9}
\]
has cumulative charge F_mu_*(a)=a^4, so it is coefficient-admissible and attains this upper bound simultaneously for b=1 and b=4. The b=4 maximum is J(D,w). The b=1 maximum is
\[
\int_0^{w_{\max}}4t^3 n(t)\,dt
=\sum_{z\in D}\int_0^{w_z}4t^3\,dt
=\mathcal A(w).
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)-(10) prove (3). Thus the optimal threshold density in this mechanism is established by a complete original-coefficient ledger, rather than being guessed or calibrated on examples.

We now give finite complete-support witnesses for failure of the resulting scale. For a fixed N>=1, an integer L>=1, and a rational r in (0,1), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{N,L}&=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:x_i\ge0\ (1\le i\le N),\ \sum_i x_i\le L\},\\
D_{N,L}&=P_{N,L}\cap\mathbb Z^N,\\
\ell_r&=(\log r)\mathbf1_N,\qquad
w_z=r^{\sum_i z_i},\\
H_z&=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\sum_i x_i\ge\sum_i z_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
P_{N,L} is the full-dimensional simplex with vertices 0,L e_1,...,L e_N. These vertices are integer sites, so P_{N,L}=conv(D_{N,L}); completeness is exact. All displayed H_z are closed halfspaces containing z. All their boundary sites are retained. Every weight is positive and rational. The original mass is
\[
W_{N,L}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^L\binom{k+N-1}{N-1}r^k.
\tag{12}
\]
The weak-composition counts used here follow, for example, by choosing N-1 separator positions among k+N-1 positions. Summing over sums at most k gives
\[
b_N(k):=|D_{N,k}|=\binom{k+N}{N}.
\tag{13}
\]
For t in (r^{k+1},r^k] with 0<=k<L, the weight-superlevel support of (11) is D_{N,k}. For 0<=t<=r^L it is D_{N,L}. Consequently (9) gives the exact finite formula
\[
\mathcal J_{N,L}(r)
=(1-r^4)\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}r^{4k}b_N(k)^4
+r^{4L}b_N(L)^4,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{N,L}(r)=W_{N,L}(r^4).
\tag{14}
\]
This calculation includes the complete support and all its weight levels. It has no probabilistic midpoint event or lattice-average filter.

For fixed N and r in (0,1), let L tend to infinity. Products of N convergent geometric sums give
\[
W_{N,\infty}(r)=(1-r)^{-N},\qquad
\mathcal A_{N,\infty}(r)=(1-r^4)^{-N}.
\tag{15}
\]
Because b_N(k)^4 is a polynomial of degree 4N, the last term in (14) tends to zero and the series converges absolutely. Thus
\[
\mathcal J_{N,L}(r)\longrightarrow
\mathcal J_{N,\infty}(r):=(1-r^4)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{4k}\binom{k+N}{N}^4.
\tag{16}
\]
These infinite sums only establish limits of the explicitly finite statistics (12)-(14). The ultimate witnesses will have finite L.

We next evaluate the required dense-weight limit exactly. The polynomial b_N(k)^4 has degree 4N and leading coefficient (N!)^{-4}. For a nonnegative integer j, the identity
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k}{j}s^k
=\frac{s^j}{(1-s)^{j+1}}\qquad(0<s<1)
\tag{17}
\]
follows by differentiating the geometric series j times and multiplying by s^j/j!. Expand b_N(k)^4 in the falling-factorial polynomial basis, whose highest-degree element is (k)_{4N}=(4N)! binom(k,4N). Its coefficient is (N!)^{-4}, because the falling factorial is monic. Applying (17), all lower-degree basis terms have a pole of order at most 4N in the sum in (16), while the highest-degree term has a pole of order 4N+1. Multiplication by 1-s and then by (1-r)^{4N}, with s=r^4, therefore yields
\[
\lim_{r\uparrow1}
\frac{\mathcal J_{N,\infty}(r)}{W_{N,\infty}(r)^4}
=\frac{(4N)!}{(N!)^4}
\lim_{r\uparrow1}\left(\frac{1-r}{1-r^4}\right)^{4N}
=\lambda_N.
\tag{18}
\]
Here 1-r^4=(1-r)(1+r+r^2+r^3). Equation (17) also shows that the convergent series in (16) is a rational function of r with rational coefficients. The limiting argument thus uses an explicitly computable exact rational expression, not numerical quadrature.

For the same original laws, the atomic term has the exact limit
\[
\frac{8^N\mathcal A_{N,\infty}(r)}{W_{N,\infty}(r)^4}
=\left(\frac{8(1-r)^3}{1+r+r^2+r^3}\right)^N
\longrightarrow0\quad(r\uparrow1).
\tag{19}
\]
Thus an atomic fallback does not repair the common-threshold loss on these witnesses.

For completeness, (18)-(19) specify an exact choice of finite witnesses. For each N, choose an integer m_N>=2 so large that r_N=1-1/m_N satisfies
\[
\frac{\mathcal J_{N,\infty}(r_N)}{W_{N,\infty}(r_N)^4}
\le2\lambda_N,
\qquad
\frac{8^N\mathcal A_{N,\infty}(r_N)}{W_{N,\infty}(r_N)^4}
\le\lambda_N.
\tag{20}
\]
Such an integer exists by (18)-(19); it may be defined as the least integer satisfying these exact inequalities. Their expressions are rational by (15)-(17). Then choose a finite integer L_N>=1 so large that
\[
\frac{\mathcal J_{N,L_N}(r_N)}{W_{N,L_N}(r_N)^4}
\le3\lambda_N,
\qquad
\frac{8^N\mathcal A_{N,L_N}(r_N)}{W_{N,L_N}(r_N)^4}
\le2\lambda_N.
\tag{21}
\]
Existence follows from (12)-(16) and the strict slack between (20) and (21). Again, L_N can be the least positive integer satisfying the finite rational inequalities (21). Set P_N=P_{N,L_N}, D_N=D_{N,L_N}, W_N=W_{N,L_N}(r_N), and use the original weights and actual caps in (11) with r=r_N. Equations (3) and (21) now give
\[
\frac{8^N}{W_N^4}\sup_\mu\mathscr C_\mu(D_N,w^{(N)})
=\frac14\frac{\mathcal J_{N,L_N}(r_N)}{W_N^4}
+\frac34\frac{8^N\mathcal A_{N,L_N}(r_N)}{W_N^4}
\le\frac94\lambda_N.
\tag{22}
\]

Finally lambda_N tends to zero by an elementary exact ratio calculation, without an asymptotic factorial theorem. Put lambda_0=1. For j>=1,
\[
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_{j-1}}
=\prod_{h=1}^3\left(1-\frac{h}{4j}\right).
\]
Using log(1-u)<=-u for 0<u<1 gives
\[
\lambda_N\le
\exp\left(-\frac32\sum_{j=1}^N\frac1j\right)
\le(N+1)^{-3/2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]
The second inequality follows by comparison with the integral of 1/x from 1 to N+1. Equations (22)-(23) prove (4). Given any absolute kappa>0, choosing N so large that (9/4)lambda_N<kappa makes every coefficient-admissible certificate in the selected class smaller than kappa 8^{-N} W_N^4.

The failure is the global step that would convert the optimized scalar common-threshold budget J(D,w) into an absolute multiple of W^4. Formula (3) proves that no alternative threshold measure within this exact coefficient ledger can fix it. The actual polynomial Phi_H(w) may exceed the certificate by a large factor; its dimension-uniform lower bound is not disproved here. Closing the original target requires information about unequal weight levels or an additional actual-cap coefficient mechanism beyond the optimized common-threshold estimate.
