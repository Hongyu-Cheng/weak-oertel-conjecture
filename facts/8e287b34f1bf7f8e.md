## Statement

Use the notation and the exact family \(p_k\) of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc. Thus, for every integer \(k\ge3\),
\[
m=5k,\quad K=2^m,\quad B=k2^k,\quad R=B^2,\quad L=2R+1,
\quad r=\lceil\log_2k\rceil,\quad s=2^r-1,\quad N_k=m+r+2,
\]
\[
Z=1+K(B+B^{-1}),
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{Z}{sZ+B^2},
\]
the three-layer core law \(\pi=\pi_{m,B}\) has origin probability \(1/Z\), the interval law \(\lambda_R\) is uniform on \(\{-R,\ldots,R\}\), and the conditional law in each of the \(s\) ordinary Boolean fibers of \(p_k\) is
\(q=\pi\otimes\lambda_R\), with fiber probability \(\alpha\). Write
\[
c_R=M_2(\lambda_R)
=\frac{(R+1)^2+R(R+1)(2R+1)/3}{(2R+1)^3},
\qquad
\gamma_R=B_2(\lambda_R).
\]
Accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc gives
\[
\frac13\le\gamma_R\le1,
\qquad
\alpha\sim\frac1s,
\qquad
Z\sim KB,
\qquad
B_2(p_k)\sim\frac{4\gamma_R}{s^2KB^3},
\tag{1}
\]
as well as
\[
\frac{B_2(p_k)}{M_2(p_k)}=2^{(2/5+o(1))N_k},
\qquad
4^{N_k}M_2(p_k)\longrightarrow\frac43,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
2^{N_k}\max_{\mathbf j}p_{k,\mathbf j}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\max\{p_k(F):F\text{ is a proper face of }\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}p_k)\}
\le\frac12+o(1).
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})
\ge
s^2\alpha^6\frac{K^4c_R^2}{Z^6}
\ge
s^2\alpha^6\frac{K^4}{144Z^6},
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})\le B_2(p_k^{\otimes2})=B_2(p_k)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac1{2304}
\le
\liminf_{k\to\infty}
\frac{M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})}{B_2(p_k)^2}
\le
\limsup_{k\to\infty}
\frac{M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})}{B_2(p_k)^2}
\le1,
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
\frac{M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})}{M_2(p_k)^2}
=2^{(4/5+o(1))N_k}.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, if \(F(N)=2^{o(N)}\), the inequality
\[
M_2(p^{\otimes2})\le F(N)M_2(p)^2
\tag{8}
\]
cannot hold for all positive log-affine laws on complete convex lattice supports in \(\mathbb Z^N\), even after restricting to families satisfying the negligible-atom and proper-face conditions in (3) and with \(4^NM_2(p)\) bounded above and below by positive constants. Thus a two-copy lower bound cannot be converted to the missing one-copy H2 bound through any universal comparison of the form (8) with subexponential loss. Equation (2) also shows that this family remains at the H2 scale, so (7) does not disprove H2.

## Proof

All definitions, construction facts, one-copy asymptotics, and product identities cited below are those proved in accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc.

First consider \(\pi^{\otimes2}\) at its double origin. For every ordered pair
\((\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\in\{0,1\}^m\times\{0,1\}^m\), define
\[
\mathbf z_{\mathbf u,\mathbf v}
=\bigl((1,\mathbf u),(-1,-\mathbf v)\bigr).
\]
Its antipode is
\[
-\mathbf z_{\mathbf u,\mathbf v}
=\bigl((-1,-\mathbf u),(1,\mathbf v)\bigr).
\]
These are \(K^2\) pairwise disjoint antipodal pairs. Each endpoint has probability
\[
\frac{B}{Z}\frac{B^{-1}}{Z}=\frac1{Z^2}
\]
under \(\pi^{\otimes2}\). Every closed halfspace containing the double origin contains at least one endpoint of each pair. Therefore
\[
\tau_{\pi^{\otimes2}}(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\ge\frac{K^2}{Z^2}.
\]
The target itself has probability \(1/Z^2\), so its single contribution to the actual second moment gives
\[
M_2(\pi^{\otimes2})
\ge
\frac1{Z^2}\left(\frac{K^2}{Z^2}\right)^2
=\frac{K^4}{Z^6}.
\tag{9}
\]
This proves the required double-origin reflected-pair lower bound using actual closed-halfspace depth.

The accepted exact interval formula includes the endpoint targets \(h=\pm R\), whose depths are \(1/L\), and all interior targets. It also gives the useful uniform inequality directly:
\[
c_R-\frac1{12}
=\frac{12R^2+22R+11}{12(2R+1)^3}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, (10) follows by subtracting \((2R+1)^3/12\) from the numerator defining \(c_R\); thus no interval endpoint is discarded.

Actual depth moments are supermultiplicative under Cartesian products by accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc. After permuting product coordinates,
\(q^{\otimes2}=\pi^{\otimes2}\otimes\lambda_R^{\otimes2}\). Hence (9), supermultiplicativity twice, and (10) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_2(q^{\otimes2})
&\ge M_2(\pi^{\otimes2})M_2(\lambda_R^{\otimes2})\\
&\ge M_2(\pi^{\otimes2})M_2(\lambda_R)^2\\
&\ge\frac{K^4c_R^2}{Z^6}
\ge\frac{K^4}{144Z^6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Now retain only targets for which both Boolean base coordinates lie in ordinary fibers. There are \(s^2\) ordered pairs of such fibers. Each product fiber has total probability \(\alpha^2\) and conditional law \(q^{\otimes2}\). Restricting any global closed halfspace containing a target to that product fiber gives a conditional closed halfspace containing the same target. Thus the global target depth is at least \(\alpha^2\) times its conditional depth. The target probability contributes one further factor \(\alpha^2\), so each ordinary product fiber contributes at least
\[
(\alpha^2)^3M_2(q^{\otimes2})=\alpha^6M_2(q^{\otimes2}).
\]
Summing the \(s^2\) nonnegative contributions and using (11) proves (4), including all six powers of the ordinary-fiber normalization.

For every finite law \(p\), accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc proves the pointwise inequality
\(\tau_p\le C_p\), hence \(M_2(p)\le B_2(p)\), and exact capacity tensorization
\(B_2(p^{\otimes2})=B_2(p)^2\). Applying these facts to \(p_k\) proves (5).

Divide the second lower bound in (4) by \(B_2(p_k)^2\). Using the three asymptotics in (1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{s^2\alpha^6K^4}{144Z^6B_2(p_k)^2}
&=
\frac{1+o(1)}{144s^4K^2B^6}
\left/
\frac{16\gamma_R^2(1+o(1))}{s^4K^2B^6}
\right.\\
&=\frac{1+o(1)}{2304\gamma_R^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Because \(1/3\le\gamma_R\le1\), equation (12) yields the lower bound in (6). Equation (5) yields the upper bound in (6) for every \(k\).

Finally, factor the amplification ratio as
\[
\frac{M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})}{M_2(p_k)^2}
=
\frac{M_2(p_k^{\otimes2})}{B_2(p_k)^2}
\left(\frac{B_2(p_k)}{M_2(p_k)}\right)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
By (6), the first factor in (13) is bounded between positive absolute constants and one for all sufficiently large \(k\), so its base-two logarithm is \(o(N_k)\). By (2), the second factor is
\(2^{(4/5+o(1))N_k}\). This proves (7). It also proves the precise limitation (8), since any \(F(N)=2^{o(N)}\) is eventually exponentially smaller than the ratio in (7). The retained properties (2)--(3) show simultaneously that this obstruction persists without large atoms or a heavy proper face and that its one-copy actual moment remains at the H2 scale.
