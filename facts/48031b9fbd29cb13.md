## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
 C_z:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(z,y)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 \mu_C(H):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in C_z:(z,y)\in H\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), put
\[
 h_C(p):=
 \inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \mu_C(H),
 \qquad
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)}h_C(p),
\tag{2}
\]
and set
\[
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\tag{3}
\]
For a positive-volume convex body \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and
\(0<s\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(E)\), use the canonical metronoid
\[
 \mathsf M_s(E):=
 \left\{
 \frac1s\int_E y g(y)\,dy:
 0\leq g\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad \int_Eg(y)\,dy=s
 \right\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Retain one common canonical instance of the degree-weighted dominant branch of accepted fact `d5d5de35c3f25af9` and the hard ledger of accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343`, with the same canonical \(Q>0\). Use the exact aliases
\[
 T:=\{t:\alpha_{d,t}>0\},
 \qquad b_t:=b_{d,t},
 \qquad q_t:=q_{d,t},
 \qquad K:=K_d,
\tag{5}
\]
and put
\[
 M:=\sum_{t\in T}b_t=M_{\mathrm{dom}}^d.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(T\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) is finite and nonempty, \(K\) is a full-dimensional compact origin-symmetric convex body, and
\[
 0<b_t\leq\frac{a_t}{4},
 \qquad
 q_t+\frac65K\subseteq\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)
 \quad(t\in T),
\tag{7}
\]
\[
 M\geq\frac{P_{\mathrm{tar}}}{2}
 >\frac{381}{1600}Q.
\tag{8}
\]
The hard ledger gives
\[
 2^n h_{\max}(C)<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{9}
\]

Define the finite measure
\[
 \beta:=\sum_{t\in T}b_t\delta_t
\tag{10}
\]
on \(\mathbb Z^n\). Accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` supplies a point
\(m\in\mathbb Z^n\) such that
\[
 \beta(J)\geq 2^{-n}M
\tag{11}
\]
for every closed base halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(m\), with the same conclusion for the whole space if it is admitted as an improper halfspace. Every such \(m\) lies in \(\operatorname{conv}T\), and \(C_m\neq\varnothing\).

Let
\[
 a(z)=Az+c
\tag{12}
\]
be any affine map satisfying \(a(m)\in C_m\), and put
\[
 p:=(m,a(m)).
\tag{13}
\]
Then the following dichotomy holds.

First, it may be that
\[
 h_C(p)\geq\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
\tag{14}
\]
which already contradicts the hard ledger (9). Otherwise there is a proper closed ambient halfspace whose boundary contains \(p\),
\[
 H=
 \left\{(z,y):
 \langle u,z-m\rangle+
 \langle v,y-a(m)\rangle\geq0
 \right\},
\tag{15}
\]
such that
\[
 \mu_C(H)<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
 \qquad v\neq0.
\tag{16}
\]
In fact, under (9) this second alternative holds for every affine map (12) satisfying \(a(m)\in C_m\).

For the actual halfspace \(H\) in (15), define
\[
 \ell_H:=u+A^{\mathsf T}v,
 \qquad
 \delta_t:=\langle\ell_H,t-m\rangle,
 \qquad
 J_H:=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\langle\ell_H,z-m\rangle\geq0\},
\tag{17}
\]
and let its fiber halfspace be
\[
 H_t:=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:(t,y)\in H\}
 =\left\{y:
 \langle v,y\rangle
 \geq\langle v,a(t)\rangle-\delta_t
 \right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(J_H\) is the closed base halfspace induced by this particular ambient halfspace and contains \(m\); it is not independent data paired with a freely chosen \(v\). Put
\[
 B_H:=\sum_{t\in T\cap J_H}b_t,
\tag{19}
\]
\[
 \mathcal I_H:=
 \{t\in T\cap J_H:
 H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\neq\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_H:=(T\cap J_H)\setminus\mathcal I_H.
\tag{20}
\]
Then
\[
 B_H\geq2^{-n}M
 >\frac{381}{1600\,2^n}Q,
\tag{21}
\]
\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H}b_t
 <\frac{Q}{100\gamma_d\,2^n},
 \qquad
 \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H}b_t}{B_H}
 <\frac{16}{381\gamma_d}<\frac18,
\tag{22}
\]
and hence
\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H}b_t
 >\left(1-\frac{16}{381\gamma_d}\right)B_H
 >\frac78B_H
 >\frac{2667}{12800\,2^n}Q.
\tag{23}
\]
Every missed target \(t\in\mathcal R_H\) has \(\delta_t\geq0\) and satisfies the strict common-normal separation
\[
 h_{\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)}(v)
 <\langle v,a(t)\rangle-\delta_t,
\tag{24}
\]
where \(h_L(v):=\max_{y\in L}\langle v,y\rangle\) for a compact convex set \(L\). Its actual fiber cap also satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)<b_t.
\tag{25}
\]

Define, only inside the missed reservoir,
\[
 \mathcal S_H:=
 \left\{t\in\mathcal R_H:
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)<\frac{b_t}{10}
 \right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H}b_t
 \leq10\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)
 <\frac{Q}{10\,2^n},
\tag{27}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal S_H}b_t
 >\frac{1387}{12800\,2^n}Q.}
\tag{28}
\]
In particular, the complementary estimate in (27) ranges only over missed targets; it makes no assertion about hit targets or targets outside \(J_H\).

Finally, write
\[
 R_H:=\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H}b_t.
\tag{29}
\]
If a future common-normal absorption theorem gave
\[
 h_{\max}(C)\geq\kappa R_H,
\tag{30}
\]
then the coarse bound (23) would give
\[
 2^nh_{\max}(C)>
 \kappa\frac{2667}{12800}Q.
\tag{31}
\]
Thus a dimension-free coefficient chosen strictly above
\[
 \boxed{\frac{128}{2667}}
\tag{32}
\]
is the required safe full-miss coefficient for a direct contradiction to (9); \(128/2667\) is the sharp boundary in the coarse arithmetic.

If the exact hit estimate in (22) is retained instead, then
\[
 R_H>
 \left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac{1}{100\gamma_d}\right)
 \frac{Q}{2^n},
\tag{33}
\]
so the corresponding exact safe condition is
\[
 \kappa>
 \frac{16\gamma_d}{381\gamma_d-16}.
\tag{34}
\]
Under the convention \(\kappa=c\gamma_d\), this is
\[
 c>\frac{16}{381\gamma_d-16}.
\tag{35}
\]
In particular, \(c=1/8\), equivalently \(\kappa=\gamma_d/8\), would require
\[
 \gamma_d>\frac{48}{127},
\tag{36}
\]
which fails already at \(d=20\). Therefore retaining the exact hit ratio does not make a \(\gamma_d/8\) full-miss absorption bound sufficient uniformly in \(d\). No absorption estimate (30) is asserted here.

## Proof

Accepted fact `d5d5de35c3f25af9`, under the aliases (5), gives (7) and
\[
 M=M_{\mathrm{dom}}^d\geq\frac{P_{\mathrm{tar}}}{2}
 >\frac{381}{1600}Q,
\]
which is (8). In the same canonical hard instance, accepted fact `7ec63df101c30343` defines a nonnegative total capacity having \(2^nh_{\max}(C)\) as one summand and assumes that total capacity is less than \(Q/100\). This proves (9).

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the nonzero finite measure \(\beta\) on \(\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^0\). Its mixed-integer centerpoint conclusion is exactly (11). If \(m\notin\operatorname{conv}T\), strict separation of \(m\) from the finite convex hull \(\operatorname{conv}T\) would give a closed base halfspace containing \(m\) and disjoint from \(T\), contradicting (11). Hence \(m\in\operatorname{conv}T\).

Because \(0\in K\), (7) gives
\[
 q_t\in\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t).
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\(\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\subseteq C_t\), so \((t,q_t)\in C\) for every \(t\in T\). Choose convex coefficients \(\lambda_t\geq0\) with
\(\sum_t\lambda_t=1\) and \(m=\sum_t\lambda_tt\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
 \left(m,\sum_t\lambda_tq_t\right)\in C.
\]
Thus \(C_m\neq\varnothing\), and at least the constant affine map with value \(\sum_t\lambda_tq_t\) is admissible in (12). The theorem concerns every admissible affine map, not only this constant one.

Fix such an affine map and its point \(p\). Alternatives (14) and
\(h_C(p)<Q/(100\,2^n)\) are exhaustive. In the second case, the definition of the infimum supplies a closed halfspace \(H_0\ni p\) with
\[
 \mu_C(H_0)<\frac{Q}{100\,2^n}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})
 \geq\sum_{t\in T}a_t
 \geq4M
 >\frac{381}{400}Q
 >\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
\]
so \(H_0\) is proper. Write it as
\(H_0=\{x:\langle N,x\rangle\geq r\}\), where \(N\neq0\) and
\(r\leq\langle N,p\rangle\). Replacing \(r\) by
\(\langle N,p\rangle\) produces a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq H_0\) whose boundary contains \(p\), so it retains the same strict mass upper bound. Splitting \(N=(u,v)\) gives (15) and the first assertion in (16).

We now prove that \(v\neq0\). If \(v=0\), then \(u\neq0\), and every entire fiber \(C_t\) with
\(\langle u,t-m\rangle\geq0\) lies in \(H\). In this case (17) has
\(\ell_H=u\), and (11), (7), and (8) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &\geq\sum_{t\in T\cap J_H}a_t
 \geq4\sum_{t\in T\cap J_H}b_t
 \geq4\,2^{-n}M\\
 &>\frac{381}{400\,2^n}Q
 >\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
 \end{aligned}
\]
contradicting (16). Hence \(v\neq0\).

For \(z=t\), affine identity (12) rewrites the defining expression of \(H\) as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\langle u,t-m\rangle+
 \langle v,y-a(m)\rangle\\
 &\quad=
 \langle u+A^{\mathsf T}v,t-m\rangle+
 \langle v,y-a(t)\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]
This proves (17)-(18). The set \(J_H\) contains \(m\), even when
\(\ell_H=0\) and it is the whole space. Applying (11) to this induced base halfspace proves the first inequality in (21), and (8) proves its strict second inequality.

Fix \(t\in\mathcal I_H\). There is a point
\[
 r_t\in H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t).
\]
The nonzero normal \(v\) makes \(H_t\) a proper closed halfspace in
\(\mathbb R^d\). The metronoid-Grünbaum sandwich in accepted fact
`6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)\geq\gamma_db_t.
\]
Summing over the distinct integer fibers in \(\mathcal I_H\) and using (16) gives
\[
 \gamma_d\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H}b_t
 \leq\mu_C(H)
 <\frac{Q}{100\,2^n},
\]
which proves the first inequality in (22). Dividing it by (21) gives
\[
 \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H}b_t}{B_H}
 <\frac{16}{381\gamma_d}.
\tag{38}
\]

For completeness, all numerical strictness in the last comparison of (22) is uniform in \(d\). Since
\(\log(1+1/d)<1/d\), one has \(\gamma_d>e^{-1}\). Also
\[
 e
 =1+1+\frac12+\frac16+\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}4^{-j}
 =\frac{49}{18}<\frac{11}{4}.
\]
Consequently \(\gamma_d>4/11\), and
\[
 \frac{16}{381\gamma_d}
 <\frac{44}{381}<\frac18,
\]
where the last comparison is \(352<381\). This completes (22). Subtracting the hit mass from \(B_H\) proves the first two strict inequalities in (23), and then (21) gives
\[
 \frac78\frac{381}{1600\,2^n}Q
 =\frac{2667}{12800\,2^n}Q,
\]
which proves the last one.

Fix a missed target \(t\in\mathcal R_H\). Since it lies in \(J_H\),
\(\delta_t\geq0\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` says that
\(\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\) is compact. Its disjointness from the closed upper halfspace (18) therefore implies
\[
 \max_{y\in\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)}\langle v,y\rangle
 <\langle v,a(t)\rangle-\delta_t,
\]
which is (24).

If \(\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)\geq b_t\), then
\[
 g_t:=
 \frac{b_t}{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)}
 \mathbf1_{C_t\cap H_t}
\]
is an admissible density of mass \(b_t\). Its normalized barycenter belongs to the convex halfspace \(H_t\), and hence belongs to
\(H_t\cap\mathsf M_{b_t}(C_t)\), a contradiction. This proves (25).

For every \(t\in\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H\), definition (26) gives
\[
 b_t\leq10\operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t).
\]
Summing this inequality only over \(\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H\), then using that these are distinct fibers of the same actual ambient halfspace, gives
\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H}b_t
 \leq10\sum_{t\in\mathcal R_H\setminus\mathcal S_H}
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_t\cap H_t)
 \leq10\mu_C(H)
 <\frac{Q}{10\,2^n}.
\]
This proves (27), without including hit targets in the complementary sum. Subtracting (27) from (23) yields
\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal S_H}b_t
 >\left(\frac{2667}{12800}-\frac1{10}\right)
 \frac{Q}{2^n}
 =\frac{1387}{12800\,2^n}Q,
\]
which proves (28).

It remains to verify the absorption arithmetic and the claimed failure of
\(\gamma_d/8\). Equation (31) follows immediately from (23) and (30), and
\[
 \frac{1/100}{2667/12800}=\frac{128}{2667},
\]
which proves (32). Using the first inequality in (22) before replacing its ratio by \(1/8\) gives
\[
 R_H=B_H-\sum_{t\in\mathcal I_H}b_t
 >\left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)
 \frac{Q}{2^n},
\]
which is (33). The coefficient in parentheses is positive by
\(\gamma_d>4/11\). Solving the direct contradiction inequality
\[
 \kappa\left(\frac{381}{1600}-\frac1{100\gamma_d}\right)>\frac1{100}
\]
gives (34), and substituting \(\kappa=c\gamma_d\) gives (35). For
\(c=1/8\), (35) is equivalent to
\(381\gamma_d-16>128\), namely (36).

Finally, the binomial theorem gives the exact estimate
\[
 \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^{20}
 >1+1+\frac{190}{400}+\frac{1140}{8000}
 +\frac{4845}{160000}
 =\frac{84729}{32000}
 >\frac{127}{48}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \gamma_{20}=\left(\frac{20}{21}\right)^{20}<\frac{48}{127}.
\]
Thus even the exact retained hit estimate does not allow the proposed
\(\gamma_d/8\) coefficient to cross the hard threshold in all dimensions. The theorem stops at the common-normal miss and small-cap certificates.
