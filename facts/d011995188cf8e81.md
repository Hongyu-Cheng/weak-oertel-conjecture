## Statement

For $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(-10,3),(2,-6),(5,10),(-2,9)\}$ at source $0$ with normals $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$, the data $k=|K|$, $Q_i$ cap areas, $\sigma_i$ coarea chord lengths and $M=\sum_iQ_i/\sigma_i$ satisfy
$$
k=253/2,\quad Q=(369/7,793/8,1012/19),\quad
\sigma=(193/14,73/8,164/19),\quad M=12048360/577649.
$$
They give $(M\prod_i\sigma_i)^2-16kQ_1Q_2Q_3=-891738498096/17689<0$. Thus the triangle coarea scale inequality fails for ordinary quadrilaterals. In this example $Q_2/k=793/1012>1/2$.

## Proof

The four vertices in the given cyclic order have consecutive edge-turn determinants $(219,109,34,144)$. Their successive pair determinants are $(54,50,65,84)$, so the origin lies strictly inside the four oriented edge halfplanes. For convexity one can also check that both nonincident vertices lie strictly to the left of every directed edge, since their oriented determinants with that edge are the two corresponding adjacent turn determinants. The shoelace sum gives $k=253/2$.

The clipped cap polygons, in counterclockwise order, are as follows.

- Cap $x\ge0$ has vertices $(0,-9/2),(2,-6),(5,10),(0,65/7)$.
- Cap $y\ge0$ has vertices $(-10,3),(-6,0),(25/8,0),(5,10),(-2,9)$.
- Cap $x+y\le0$ has vertices $(-10,3),(2,-6),(50/19,-50/19),(-6,6)$.

Each new vertex is obtained by linear interpolation on the crossed edge. Shoelace gives the three asserted areas. For the first two sections the coarea length is the coordinate difference of the two intersections. On $x+y=0$ the Euclidean chord length is $\sqrt2$ times the horizontal difference, and dividing by $\sqrt2$ again gives the coordinate difference. These calculations give the asserted $\sigma$, so adding $Q_i/\sigma_i$ gives $M$, and substituting gives the stated exact square gap. Since both sides of the proposed coarea inequality are positive, the negative square gap refutes it.
