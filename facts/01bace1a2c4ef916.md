## Statement

For every integer \(R\geq2\), there is a full-dimensional convex body
\[
 C_R\subseteq\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^{d_R},
 \qquad
 d_R=N_R:=|RB_2^2\cap\mathbb Z^2|,
\tag{1}
\]
with the following property.  Form the depth hypograph \(B_R\), its lifted
mixed support
\[
 X_R:=B_R\cap(\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R),
\]
and the actual projected hit ranges \(\mathcal A_H\) of accepted fact
4bd2e6d22487dfef.  There are top lifted points
\[
 Q_z=(z,g_R(z))
 \qquad
 (z\in D_R:=RB_2^2\cap\mathbb Z^2)
\tag{2}
\]
such that, for every subset \(J\subseteq D_R\), a proper closed ambient
halfspace \(H_J\subseteq\mathbb R^{2+d_R}\) satisfies
\[
 Q_z\in\mathcal A_{H_J}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 z\in J.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus these \(N_R\) true top hit points are shattered.

Put
\[
 F_J:=X_R\setminus\mathcal A_{H_J}.
\tag{4}
\]
For every finite collection \(J_1,\ldots,J_s\subseteq D_R\),
\[
 \bigcap_{i=1}^sF_{J_i}\neq\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigcup_{i=1}^sJ_i\neq D_R.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently:

1. The miss family \(\{X_R\setminus\mathcal A_H:H\text{ an ambient
   halfspace}\}\) has Helly number at least \(N_R\).  These Helly numbers
   tend to infinity although the integer dimension is always \(2\).

2. Fix any integer \(h\geq2\).  For all sufficiently large \(R\), there is
   a finite indexed subfamily of the miss family for which at least
   \(3/8\) of all \(h\)-element subfamilies intersect, while the largest
   subfamily with a common point has relative size at most
   \[
   \frac{\lceil N_R^{\,1-1/h}\rceil}{N_R}\longrightarrow0.
   \tag{6}
   \]
   Hence no fractional-Helly theorem of any fixed order \(h\) holds
   uniformly for these miss systems.  The order \(h=1\) fails as well.

3. There do not exist fixed integers \(k,r\), even with \(n=2\) fixed,
   such that every such system admits one common map
   \(\Psi_C:X_C\to\mathbb R^k\) under which every predicate
   \(q\notin\mathcal A_H\) is a Boolean combination of at most \(r\)
   affine halfspace predicates.  Equivalently, a common auxiliary lift
   representing all misses cannot have both dimension and halfspace
   complexity bounded independently of the continuous dimension.

All three conclusions concern the miss/complement family used by the
indicated proof templates.  They neither disprove a projected-hit
centerpoint statement nor produce a counterexample to weak Oertel.

## Proof

1. A fixed-\(n=2\) free-radius specialization of the accepted construction.

Fix \(R\geq2\), put
\[
 D_R:=RB_2^2\cap\mathbb Z^2,
 \qquad N:=|D_R|,
 \qquad b:=2R+1,
\]
and define
\[
 \ell(x_1,x_2):=x_1+bx_2,
 \qquad
 H_R:=2R(1+b),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon:=\frac1{8NH_R}.
\tag{7}
\]
The functional \(\ell\) is injective on \(D_R\).  Indeed, if
\(z,z'\in D_R\) and \(z_2-z'_2\neq0\), then
\[
 |b(z_2-z'_2)+(z_1-z'_1)|
 \geq b-2R=1,
\]
while the case \(z_2=z'_2\) is immediate.  Also,
\[
 |\ell(x)-\ell(w)|\leq H_R
 \qquad
 (x\in RB_2^2,\ w\in D_R).
\tag{8}
\]

For \(w\in D_R\), set
\[
 \psi_w(x):=1-\varepsilon|\ell(x)-\ell(w)|
\]
and, with \(y=(y_w)_{w\in D_R}\in\mathbb R^N\), define
\[
 C_R:=
 \left\{
 (x,y):
 \begin{array}{l}
 x\in RB_2^2,\\
 y_w\geq-\psi_w(x)\quad(w\in D_R),\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{w\in D_R}y_w\leq1
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is the construction mechanism of accepted fact
3316a7b28eca6a19 with \(n\) frozen at \(2\) and the radius left free.
Each \(\psi_w\) is concave, so every lower-fiber constraint in (9) is a
convex epigraph constraint.  The base is compact, the coordinate lower
bounds together with the sum upper bound bound every \(y_w\), and hence
\(C_R\) is compact and convex.  By (8),
\[
 \psi_w(x)\geq1-\frac1{8N}>0.
\]
Thus \((0,0)\) satisfies all inequalities strictly and \(0\) is interior
to the base disk.  Therefore \(C_R\) is full-dimensional in
\(\mathbb R^{2+N}\).

For \(x\in RB_2^2\), put
\[
 S(x):=1+\sum_{w\in D_R}\psi_w(x)
 =N+1-\varepsilon\sum_{w\in D_R}|\ell(x)-\ell(w)|.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
 N+1-\frac18\leq S(x)\leq N+1.
\tag{11}
\]
Writing
\[
 \Delta_N:=\{u\in\mathbb R^N:u_w\geq0,\ \sum_wu_w\leq1\},
\]
the change of variables \(y_w+\psi_w(x)=S(x)u_w\) gives
\[
 (C_R)_x=-\boldsymbol\psi(x)+S(x)\Delta_N.
\tag{12}
\]
In particular, every lattice fiber, and every real fiber over the base
disk, is an \(N\)-dimensional simplex.

For \(z,w\in D_R\), write
\[
 d_{z,w}:=|\ell(z)-\ell(w)|,
 \qquad
 s_z:=\sum_{w\in D_R}d_{z,w}.
\]
The centroid of (12) is
\[
 c_z
 =\varepsilon
 \left(d_{z,w}-\frac{s_z}{N+1}\right)_{w\in D_R}.
\tag{13}
\]
These \(N\) centroid vectors are linearly independent.  To verify the
accepted construction's distance-matrix calculation here, order the
distinct nodes \(t_i=\ell(z_i)\) and let
\[
 \mathsf D_{ij}:=|t_i-t_j|.
\]
If \(\mathsf Dv=0\), the piecewise-affine function
\[
 \varphi(t):=\sum_jv_j|t-t_j|
\]
vanishes at every node.  It is therefore identically zero on every
interval between consecutive nodes.  Its consecutive slope jumps give
\(v_2=\cdots=v_{N-1}=0\).  Its slope on the first interval then gives
\(v_1=v_N\), and \(\varphi(t_1)=v_N(t_N-t_1)=0\) gives \(v_N=0\).
Thus \(\mathsf D\) is invertible.  Moreover,
\[
 \mathsf M:=I-\frac1{N+1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}
\]
has eigenvalue \(1\) on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) and \(1/(N+1)\) on
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\).  The matrix whose rows are
\(c_z^{\mathsf T}\) is \(\varepsilon\mathsf D\mathsf M\), so it is
invertible.

2. The true top sections are singletons.

Let
\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_N((C_R)_z),
 \qquad
 \gamma_N:=\left(\frac{N}{N+1}\right)^N.
\]
Consider any \(N\)-simplex \(K\) of volume \(a\), and let
\(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_N\) be its barycentric coordinates.  For
\(y\in K\), choose \(i\) with
\(\lambda_i(y)=\max_j\lambda_j(y)\).  The proper closed halfspace
\[
 \{v:\lambda_i(v)\geq\lambda_i(y)\}
\]
contains \(y\), and its intersection with \(K\) is a homothetic vertex
cap of volume
\[
 (1-\lambda_i(y))^Na.
\]
Since \(\max_i\lambda_i(y)\geq1/(N+1)\), the fiber depth satisfies
\[
 \delta_K(y)\leq\gamma_Na.
\tag{14}
\]
Equality in (14) is possible only if all barycentric coordinates equal
\(1/(N+1)\), that is, only at the centroid.

Apply this to every lattice fiber of \(C_R\).  If
\[
 M_z:=\max_{y\in(C_R)_z}\delta_{C_R}(z,y),
\]
then (14) gives \(M_z\leq\gamma_Na_z\).  Assertion 1 of accepted fact
4bd2e6d22487dfef gives the reverse aggregate inequality
\[
 \sum_{z\in D_R}M_z
 \geq
 \gamma_N\sum_{z\in D_R}a_z.
\tag{15}
\]
All summands in (15) are bounded above termwise by the corresponding
right-hand summand.  Hence
\[
 M_z=\gamma_Na_z
 \quad\text{for every }z\in D_R.
\tag{16}
\]
The equality condition in (14) shows that \(c_z\) is the unique maximizer.

Let \(g_R\), \(B_R\), and \(\mathcal A_H\) be the objects in accepted fact
4bd2e6d22487dfef for \(C_R\).  Equations (2) and (16) mean
\[
 Q_z=\left(z,(\gamma_Na_z)^{1/N}\right).
\tag{17}
\]
The depth section at this top point is exactly
\[
 \{y\in(C_R)_z:
 \delta_{C_R}(z,y)\geq g_R(z)^N\}
 =\{c_z\}.
\tag{18}
\]

3. True ambient halfspaces shatter the top points.

Put \(p_z:=(z,c_z)\in\mathbb R^{2+N}\).  The points \(p_z\) are affinely
independent: any affine dependence among them gives a linear dependence
among their continuous components \(c_z\), and the latter are linearly
independent by (13).

Fix \(J\subseteq D_R\).  Affine independence permits an affine functional
\(L_J\) satisfying
\[
 L_J(p_z)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&z\in J,\\
 -1,&z\notin J.
 \end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
We may take \(L_J\) nonconstant.  This is automatic when both labels
occur.  If all labels agree, the affine hull of the \(N\) points has
dimension \(N-1< N+2\), so a nonzero affine functional vanishing on that
hull can be added to the constant interpolant.  Therefore
\[
 H_J:=\{(x,y):L_J(x,y)\geq0\}
\tag{20}
\]
is a genuine proper closed ambient halfspace.

By the definition of the actual hit range and by (18),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_z\in\mathcal A_{H_J}
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 \text{some }y\in(C_R)_z\cap(H_J)_z
 \text{ has }\delta_{C_R}(z,y)\geq g_R(z)^N\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 (z,c_z)\in H_J\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow z\in J.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
This proves the exact shattering assertion (3), with strict top
incidences \(L_J(p_z)=\pm1\).

4. The exact miss-set intersection rule and ordinary Helly number.

Every hit range is vertically downward closed: if \((z,u)\in\mathcal A_H\)
is witnessed by \(y\), then the same \(y\) witnesses
\((z,v)\in\mathcal A_H\) for every \(0\leq v\leq u\).  In particular, if
\(Q_z\in\mathcal A_{H_J}\), that range contains the entire lifted fiber
\[
 \{z\}\times[0,g_R(z)].
\tag{22}
\]

If \(\bigcup_iJ_i\neq D_R\), choose
\(z\notin\bigcup_iJ_i\).  Equation (21) gives
\(Q_z\notin\mathcal A_{H_{J_i}}\) for every \(i\), so
\(Q_z\in\bigcap_iF_{J_i}\).  Conversely, if
\(\bigcup_iJ_i=D_R\), then for every lattice fiber some \(J_i\) contains
its base point.  Equations (21)-(22) show that the corresponding hit range
contains that whole lifted fiber.  The hit ranges therefore cover \(X_R\),
and the complements have empty intersection.  This proves (5).

Now take the \(N\) sets \(F_{\{z\}}\), \(z\in D_R\).  Their total
intersection is empty, while every \(N-1\) of them intersect by (5).
Thus the miss family has Helly number at least \(N=N_R\).  Since
\(N_R\to\infty\) as \(R\to\infty\), no Helly bound depending only on the
fixed integer dimension exists.

5. Failure of every fixed fractional-Helly order.

Fix \(h\geq2\), take \(R\) sufficiently large, and set
\[
 N:=N_R,
 \qquad
 r:=\left\lceil N^{\,1-1/h}\right\rceil.
\]
For every \(r\)-subset \(T\subseteq D_R\), put
\[
 G_T:=F_{D_R\setminus T}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equation (5) gives, for every finite collection,
\[
 \bigcap_{i=1}^sG_{T_i}\neq\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigcap_{i=1}^sT_i\neq\varnothing.
\tag{24}
\]

Choose \(T_1,\ldots,T_h\) independently and uniformly among the
\(r\)-subsets of \(D_R\), and let
\[
 Z:=|T_1\cap\cdots\cap T_h|,
 \qquad
 \mu:=\mathbb E Z
 =N\left(\frac rN\right)^h
 =\frac{r^h}{N^{h-1}}\geq1.
\]
For two distinct elements of \(D_R\), the probability that both lie in
all \(h\) sets is
\[
 \left(\frac{r(r-1)}{N(N-1)}\right)^h.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E Z^2
 =
 \mu+
 N(N-1)
 \left(\frac{r(r-1)}{N(N-1)}\right)^h
 \leq\mu+\mu^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The second-moment inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P(Z>0)
 \geq\frac{(\mathbb EZ)^2}{\mathbb EZ^2}
 \geq\frac12.
\tag{26}
\]

To express this with ordinary distinct indexed members, let
\[
 M:=\binom Nr,\qquad L:=4h(h-1),
\]
and take \(L\) labelled copies of every \(G_T\).  If \(h\) indexed members
are first sampled independently with replacement, their types are the
independent \(T_i\) above.  The probability that an exact indexed member
is repeated is at most
\[
 \frac{\binom h2}{LM}\leq\frac18.
\]
Conditioning on no repeated indexed member gives the uniform distribution
on ordered \(h\)-tuples of distinct indexed members.  Equations
(24)-(26) therefore imply that at least \(3/8\) of the \(h\)-element
subfamilies have nonempty intersection.

On the other hand, if any indexed subfamily has a common point, (24)
implies that all its associated \(T\)'s contain one common
\(z\in D_R\).  Hence it has at most
\[
 L\binom{N-1}{r-1}
\]
members out of the total \(L\binom Nr\), a relative fraction \(r/N\).
This bound is attained by taking every indexed \(T\) containing a fixed
\(z\), since all corresponding \(G_T\)'s contain \(Q_z\).  Because
\[
 \frac rN\longrightarrow0,
\]
the fixed intersecting \(h\)-tuple fraction \(3/8\) forces no positive
uniform fraction of the family to have a common point.  This disproves a
fractional-Helly theorem of order \(h\).  Since \(h\) was arbitrary, every
fixed order fails.  For \(h=1\), take the sets \(G_{\{z\}}\): each is
nonempty, while the largest common-point subfamily has relative size
\(1/N\).

6. No bounded-dimensional bounded-halfspace-complexity common lift.

Suppose, to the contrary, that fixed integers \(k,r\) have the asserted
representation property.  Apply the common map \(\Psi_{C_R}\) to the
\(N\) top points \(Q_z\).  A single affine halfspace predicate on \(N\)
points in \(\mathbb R^k\) has only polynomially many incidence patterns
in \(N\).  One elementary bound that also counts boundary patterns is
\[
 P_k(N):=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}2^j\binom Nj.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed, regard the \(k+1\) affine coefficients as a parameter point.  The
\(N\) evaluations define an arrangement of \(N\) hyperplanes in parameter
space, and the predicate is constant on every relatively open face.
The standard induction obtained by inserting one hyperplane gives at
most the number of faces in (27).

Consequently, at most
\[
 2^{2^r}(1+P_k(N))^r
\tag{28}
\]
incidence patterns on the top points can be produced by Boolean
combinations of at most \(r\) halfspace predicates.  The factor
\(2^{2^r}\) counts all Boolean functions on at most \(r\) signs, and the
remaining factor counts the predicate patterns.  For fixed \(k,r\), (28)
is polynomial in \(N\).

But (21) realizes all \(2^N\) hit patterns on the top points, and hence
all \(2^N\) miss patterns as well.  For sufficiently large \(R\),
\(2^N\) exceeds (28), a contradiction.  This proves the bounded-lift
no-go.

The construction only exposes unrestricted incidence complexity at true
top depth.  It does not show that every lifted point has a low-mass
containing hit range, and it does not provide a shallow mixed-integer
point.  Thus it is a counterexample to the three indicated proof
templates, not to a projected-hit centerpoint assertion or to weak
Oertel.
