## Statement

Let $D_4=\operatorname{conv}\{(-4,-4),(4,0),(0,4)\}$ and $P=D_4\cap\mathbb Z^2$. For any nonempty proper subset $T\subset P$ satisfying $P\cap\operatorname{conv}(T)=T$, let $k(T)$ count its occupied parity classes modulo $2\mathbb Z^2$. Then
$$
\frac{k(T)}4-\frac{|T|}{31}\ge \frac1{124}.
$$
For a product $P\times K$ with every fiber of the same positive area, $|T|/31$ is exactly the fraction of the mixed area over $T$.

## Proof

For first coordinate $x=-4,\ldots,4$, the second coordinate in $P$ ranges from $\lceil(x-4)/2\rceil$ to $\min(4-x,2x+4)$. Counting gives $31$ sites and parity counts $10,7,7,7$, with the even-even class of size ten. All three outer vertices are even-even. Since $\operatorname{conv}(T)\subseteq D_4$, the assumed closure makes $T$ all lattice points of its own convex hull.

If $T$ contains two distinct sites, the primitive step between them lies in their segment, and that step has at least one odd coordinate. Thus $k(T)=1$ implies $|T|=1$. If $T$ is two-dimensional, choose a triangle of least positive area on its sites. If the determinant of its two edge vectors $v,w$ has absolute value greater than one, the half-open parallelogram on $v,w$ contains a lattice point other than the origin. Indeed, its lattice points represent the cosets of $\mathbb Zv+\mathbb Zw$ in $\mathbb Z^2$, whose index is the absolute determinant. Write such a point as $p=\alpha v+\beta w$ with $\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)$. Either $p$ or $v+w-p$ lies in the triangle with vertices $0,v,w$ and is distinct from its vertices. After translation to the chosen triangle this lattice point belongs to $T$ by closure. Subdivision gives a smaller positive-area triangle on $T$, a contradiction. Hence the chosen triangle is unimodular and its vertices have distinct parity classes because its two edge vectors are independent modulo two. Thus $k(T)=2$ implies collinearity. A primitive step along that line has a nonzero integer coordinate, which can take at most nine values inside $[-4,4]$ along the lattice line. This bounds $|T|$ by nine.

If $k(T)=3$ and the even-even class is omitted, $|T|\le21$. If one of the seven-site classes is omitted, at least one even-even outer vertex must also be omitted. Retaining all three outer vertices would give $\operatorname{conv}(T)=D_4$ and then $T=P$. Hence $|T|\le23$. For $k(T)=4$ properness gives $|T|\le30$. The four lower bounds on the respective gaps are $27/124$, $13/62$, $1/124$, and $1/31$.
