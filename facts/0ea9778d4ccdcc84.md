## Statement

Use the actual closed-halfspace depth \(\tau_p\) of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374 and the actual third moment \(\mathcal M_3\) of accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c. Explicitly, for a strictly positive probability law \(p\) on a nonempty finite \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\), with \(m\ge1\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf x)
 &=\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^m\ \text{a proper closed halfspace}\\
                   \mathbf x\in H}}
       \sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf z}
  =\inf_{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^m}
       \sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in D\\
                  \mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf x)\ge0}}p_{\mathbf z},\\
\mathcal M_3(p)
 &=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The assignment's \(M3(p)\) means exactly \(\mathcal M_3(p)\), with no change of root probabilities. On an intrinsic rank-zero support, the sole point has depth one. Constant affine inequalities give the full or empty intrinsic trace; a containing trace in rank zero is necessarily full. All boundaries are included.

Let \(D=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^s D_i\), where \(s\ge1\) and each \(D_i\) is finite and nonempty. Set \(\lambda_i=p(D_i)>0\), \(p_i=p(\,\cdot\mid D_i)\), and \(r_i=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_i)\). For integer supports this is also the rank of the intrinsic affine lattice. The depths and moments of \(p_i\) may be computed either in the ambient space or in an affine chart of its affine hull, with the rank-zero convention above. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(\mathbf x)&\ge\lambda_i\tau_{p_i}(\mathbf x)
                    &&(\mathbf x\in D_i),\\
\mathcal M_3(p)&\ge\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i^4\mathcal M_3(p_i).
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}>0\) be coefficients such that
\(\mathcal M_3(p_i)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i}\) for every \(i\). Here \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}\) and the common coefficient \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}\) below are exactly the assignment's \(\kappa_i\) and \(\kappa\), respectively; they are not the likelihood/atom constant \(\kappa\) in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. The coefficient-sensitive bound is
\[
\mathcal M_3(p)
 \ge\sum_{i=1}^s\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i}\lambda_i^4
 \ge\left(\sum_{i=1}^s
          \kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}^{-1/3}2^{r_i}\right)^{-3}.
\]
The numerical minimum of the middle expression over all positive \((\lambda_i)_{i=1}^s\) with \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\) is exactly the right-hand side. Its unique minimizing allocation is
\[
\lambda_i
 =\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}^{-1/3}2^{r_i}}
        {\sum_{j=1}^s\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},j}^{-1/3}2^{r_j}}.
\]
This is equality for the numerical allocation problem, not a claim that the actual-depth bound is sharp.

In particular, if every piece satisfies
\(\mathcal M_3(p_i)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-r_i}\) for a common \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}>0\), then
\(\mathcal M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}/(\sum_i2^{r_i})^3\). This applies, in particular, whenever \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}\le\min_i\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}\).
If \(n\ge1\) and \(\sum_i2^{r_i}\le B_{\mathrm{bud}}2^n\), then
\(\mathcal M_3(p)\ge(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}/B_{\mathrm{bud}}^3)8^{-n}\).
The symbol \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}>0\) is the assignment's rank-budget prefactor \(B\), distinguished from chart matrices. If both \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{bud}}\) are absolute constants, so is the resulting coefficient, independently of the number of pieces. No separation or parallelism assumption is needed for these pooling conclusions.

For the capacity normalization, let \(c_{\mathbf z}>0\) be weights on \(D\), let \(T=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}c_{\mathbf z}\), and take \(p_{\mathbf z}=c_{\mathbf z}/T\). In the notation of accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_c(\mathbf x)
 &=\inf_{\substack{H\ \text{a proper closed halfspace}\\\mathbf x\in H}}
       \sum_{\mathbf z\in D\cap H}c_{\mathbf z}
  =T\tau_p(\mathbf x),\\
\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)
 &=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}c_{\mathbf x}h_c(\mathbf x)^3
  =T^4\mathcal M_3(p).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every displayed normalized bound multiplies by \(T^4\) for this capacity third moment. The depth inside its cube is actual cap mass, not exponential capacity; the root weight remains the original \(c_{\mathbf x}\).

Here is a primitive-slice application. Let \(n\ge1\), let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite, nonempty, and complete, meaning \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and let
\(p_{\mathbf z}=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}/\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\)
for an arbitrary \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^n\). Fix \(0\le k\le n\), set \(r=n-k\), and let \(Q:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^k\) be a surjective integer linear map. Parallel primitive codimension-\(k\) slices here mean its affine fibers \(Q\mathbf z=\mathbf b\). For \(k=0\), the target is the one-point group \(\mathbb Z^0\). Let
\(\mathcal I=Q(D)\), \(N=|\mathcal I|\), \(D_{\mathbf b}=D\cap Q^{-1}(\mathbf b)\), and \(\pi_{\mathbf b}=p(\,\cdot\mid D_{\mathbf b})\).

Each \(D_{\mathbf b}\) has a complete support in a saturated rank-\(r\) integer affine chart, and its conditional law is log-affine in that chart. Its actual affine rank may be smaller than \(r\). If \(N\le2^k\) and
\(\mathcal M_3(\pi_{\mathbf b})\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-r}\) for every \(\mathbf b\in\mathcal I\), then
\(\mathcal M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-n}\), regardless of the positive slice masses \(\lambda_{\mathbf b}=p(D_{\mathbf b})\).

For \(r=1\), the conditional moment hypothesis is unconditional with the absolute coefficient \(a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\), where \(a_{\mathrm{box}}\) is exactly the constant in accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c. More explicitly, any complete finite \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) coverable by at most \(2^{n-1}\) parallel primitive lattice lines, with any positive ambient log-affine law \(p\), satisfies
\[
\mathcal M_3(p)
 \ge\frac{a_{\mathrm{box}}^3}
          {\left(\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal I}2^{r_{\mathbf b}}\right)^3}
 \ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\,8^{-n},
\qquad
r_{\mathbf b}=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_{\mathbf b})\in\{0,1\}.
\]
For positive log-affine weights \(c_{\mathbf z}=b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z}\), with \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}>0\) and total capacity \(T\), it follows that
\(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)\ge T^4a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-n}\).
Line lengths, line translations, and slice masses need not agree, and no affine dependence of line starting points on their quotient labels is assumed. The same normalized line conclusion holds for any positive law having geometric conditional interval laws on those lines, even with independently chosen conditional tilts and slice masses.

The integer chart for the primitive quotient is unimodular in the full ambient integer dimension \(n\), so no lattice-index factor occurs. It can be extended by the identity on the continuous coordinates without changing the continuous dimension \(d\). The conclusions use ambient \(n\) even when \(D\) is lower-dimensional. They do not assert an improved line-count threshold with \(n\) replaced by \(\dim\operatorname{aff}(D)\). For \(n=1\), the line assertion means \(k=0\) and one line. No assertion is made that every complete support admits one of the stated partitions or has the required conditional moments.

## Proof

First verify the cap and chart conventions. A proper closed halfspace has the form \(H=\{\mathbf z:\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge t\}\), with \(\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0\). If it contains \(\mathbf x\), then \(t\le\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x\), so its trace contains the centered closed trace
\(\{\mathbf z\in D:\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf x)\ge0\}\).
Conversely, a centered trace with nonzero normal is itself the trace of a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf x\). The zero normal gives the full support and value one, which cannot decrease the infimum. A full finite-support trace is also realized by a proper ambient closed halfspace. This proves the centered formula and its agreement with the actual depth in accepted facts bf1ff94487296374 and 8db3ba8c8761ea6c, including supports in proper affine subspaces.

For a piece of rank \(r_i>0\), choose an affine chart
\(\Phi_i(\mathbf y)=\mathbf v_i+B_i\mathbf y\) onto \(\operatorname{aff}(D_i)\), where \(B_i\) has \(r_i\) independent columns. Write \(\widetilde D_i=\Phi_i^{-1}(D_i)\) and define the chart law by the exact alias
\(\widetilde p_i(\mathbf y)=p_i(\Phi_i(\mathbf y))\); thus \(p_i\) is its pushforward under \(\Phi_i\).
An ambient inequality \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z\ge t\) pulls back to
\((B_i^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf y\ge t-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v_i\).
If it contains the root and \(B_i^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u=\mathbf0\), it contains the whole affine hull. Otherwise it is a genuine intrinsic proper closed halfspace containing the intrinsic root. Conversely, \(B_i^{\mathsf T}:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^{r_i}\) is surjective, so every intrinsic proper closed halfspace extends to an ambient proper closed halfspace with precisely the same trace. Thus, at first use of intrinsic depths, the exact normalization map is
\[
\tau_{\widetilde p_i}(\mathbf y)
 =\tau_{p_i}(\Phi_i(\mathbf y)),
\qquad
\mathcal M_3(\widetilde p_i)=\mathcal M_3(p_i).
\]
All individual probabilities are unchanged. For an integer piece one may choose \(\mathbf v_i\in D_i\) and the columns of \(B_i\) as a basis of
\(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D_i-D_i)\cap\mathbb Z^m\), giving the saturated intrinsic lattice chart described in accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Saturation is not needed for cap transport. If \(r_i=0\), the piece is a singleton, every containing cap includes its unique point, and its conditional depth and moment are both one.

Now let \(\mathbf x\in D_i\) and let \(H\) be any proper ambient closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf x\). Its trace on \(D_i\) is, by the preceding argument, either a genuine intrinsic containing cap or the whole piece. Therefore \(p(D\cap H)\ge p(D_i\cap H)=\lambda_i p_i(D_i\cap H)\ge\lambda_i\tau_{p_i}(\mathbf x)\).
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves the depth inequality. Cubing it and using the exact original root probabilities \(p_{\mathbf x}=\lambda_i p_i(\mathbf x)\), then summing over the disjoint pieces, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_3(p)
 &=\sum_{i=1}^s\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_i}
       \lambda_i p_i(\mathbf x)\tau_p(\mathbf x)^3\\
 &\ge\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i^4
       \sum_{\mathbf x\in D_i}p_i(\mathbf x)
                            \tau_{p_i}(\mathbf x)^3
  =\sum_{i=1}^s\lambda_i^4\mathcal M_3(p_i).
\end{aligned}
\]
There is one mass factor from the root and three from the cubed depth. The same containing cap is used throughout; none of the pieces must be separated from the others.

Apply Hölder's inequality with conjugate exponents \(4\) and \(4/3\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
1
 &=\sum_{i=1}^s
   \left[(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i})^{1/4}\lambda_i\right]
   (\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i})^{-1/4}\\
 &\le
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^s\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i}\lambda_i^4\right)^{1/4}
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^s
        (\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i})^{-1/3}\right)^{3/4}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \((8^{-r_i})^{-1/3}=2^{r_i}\), taking fourth powers and rearranging proves the coefficient-sensitive bound. Hölder has equality precisely when the positive quantities
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i}\lambda_i^4\) are proportional to
\((\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}8^{-r_i})^{-1/3}\). Equivalently, \(\lambda_i\) is proportional to
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},i}^{-1/3}2^{r_i}\). Normalization gives the stated allocation, which is positive and attains the numerical bound. Its uniqueness also follows from strict convexity of the sum of positive fourth-power terms. The case \(s=1\) has the sole allocation \(\lambda_1=1\). Neither this equality condition nor strict convexity establishes equality in the earlier geometric or conditional-moment inequalities.

Taking all coefficients equal to \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}\) proves the common-coefficient formula. The rank-budget hypothesis then gives
\[
\mathcal M_3(p)
 \ge\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}}{(\sum_i2^{r_i})^3}
 \ge\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}}{B_{\mathrm{bud}}^3\,2^{3n}}
 =\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}}{B_{\mathrm{bud}}^3}\,8^{-n}.
\]
For unnormalized weights, each cap mass equals \(T\) times its \(p\)-mass. Taking infima gives \(h_c=T\tau_p\); multiplying the cube by \(c_{\mathbf x}=Tp_{\mathbf x}\) and summing proves \(\mathcal M_3^{\mathrm{cap}}(c)=T^4\mathcal M_3(p)\), exactly the normalization of accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c.

We next construct the primitive-slice chart without an index loss. Let \(L=\ker Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\). This is a saturated rank-\(r\) lattice: \(Q\) has real rank \(k\), its real kernel has dimension \(r\) and an integer basis of its real span, and if an integer multiple of an integer vector lies in \(L\), then that vector lies in \(L\). Choose a lattice basis as columns of \(B\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r}\). Consequently,
\(B\mathbb Z^r=(B\mathbb R^r)\cap\mathbb Z^n=L\).
Surjectivity of \(Q\) supplies an integer matrix \(W\in\mathbb Z^{n\times k}\) with \(QW=I_k\). The matrix \(U=(W\ B)\) is unimodular. Indeed, any \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n\) has the unique expression \(\mathbf z=W(Q\mathbf z)+B\mathbf y\), with \(\mathbf y\in\mathbb Z^r\),
because the difference \(\mathbf z-W(Q\mathbf z)\) lies in \(L\). Applying \(Q\) determines the first coordinates, and independence of the columns of \(B\) determines the remaining coordinates. Thus \(U\) is an integer bijection of \(\mathbb Z^n\), its inverse is integer, and its determinant is \(1\) or \(-1\). This includes empty blocks when \(k=0\) or \(r=0\).

For each \(\mathbf b\in\mathcal I\), let
\(\Phi_{\mathbf b}(\mathbf y)=W\mathbf b+B\mathbf y\) and
\(E_{\mathbf b}=\Phi_{\mathbf b}^{-1}(D_{\mathbf b})\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\).
This chart maps \(\mathbb Z^r\) bijectively onto the integer points of the affine slice. If
\(\mathbf y\in\operatorname{conv}(E_{\mathbf b})\cap\mathbb Z^r\), then
\(\Phi_{\mathbf b}(\mathbf y)\in\operatorname{conv}(D_{\mathbf b})\cap\mathbb Z^n\subseteq\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n=D\).
Its quotient label is \(\mathbf b\), so it lies in \(D_{\mathbf b}\), proving
\(E_{\mathbf b}=\operatorname{conv}(E_{\mathbf b})\cap\mathbb Z^r\).
This proof does not require \(E_{\mathbf b}\) to be full-dimensional. Substituting the chart into the original weights cancels the slice prefactor and yields the exact conditional alias
\[
\pi_{\mathbf b}(\Phi_{\mathbf b}(\mathbf y))
 =\frac{e^{(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell)\cdot\mathbf y}}
        {\sum_{\mathbf h\in E_{\mathbf b}}
              e^{(B^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\ell)\cdot\mathbf h}}.
\]
The normal map is \(B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf u\), as in the earlier cap transport, so these are the same actual conditional depths and moments, not a new root law.

Let \(r_{\mathbf b}=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_{\mathbf b})\le r\). Under the stated slice moment hypothesis, apply the coefficient-sensitive theorem with intrinsic ranks \(r_{\mathbf b}\) and coefficients
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},\mathbf b}=\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{r_{\mathbf b}-r}\).
These are positive and satisfy
\(\kappa_{\mathrm{mom},\mathbf b}8^{-r_{\mathbf b}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-r}\).
The denominator simplifies exactly:
\[
\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal I}
 \kappa_{\mathrm{mom},\mathbf b}^{-1/3}2^{r_{\mathbf b}}
 =\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}^{-1/3}N2^r.
\]
Hence
\(\mathcal M_3(p)\ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}/(N^3 8^r)
 \ge\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-(k+r)}
 =\kappa_{\mathrm{mom}}8^{-n}\).
This treats \(r\) only as a chart-rank budget and does not confuse it with the actual rank of a small slice. It holds for every positive allocation of slice masses.

For the line case, let \(\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n\) be the common primitive direction, meaning that the greatest common divisor of its coordinates is one. Integer Euclidean row operations, consisting of coordinate swaps, sign changes, and addition of an integer multiple of one coordinate to another, transform \(\mathbf v\) to a standard basis vector. Each operation is unimodular, so reversing them and placing that basis vector last gives a unimodular \(U\) whose last column is \(\mathbf v\). The first \(n-1\) rows of \(U^{-1}\) define a surjective quotient \(Q:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^{n-1}\) with kernel \(\mathbb Z\mathbf v\). Every affine line parallel to \(\mathbf v\) and meeting \(\mathbb Z^n\) is a real fiber of this \(Q\), with integer quotient label. Distinct lines give distinct labels. Thus any cover by at most \(2^{n-1}\) such lines gives \(N\le2^{n-1}\) nonempty slices.

In this chart, a nonempty finite complete \(E_{\mathbf b}\subseteq\mathbb Z\) is an interval
\(\{s_{\mathbf b},\ldots,s_{\mathbf b}+m_{\mathbf b}-1\}\), with \(m_{\mathbf b}\ge1\). Put
\(\mathbf z_{0,\mathbf b}=W\mathbf b+s_{\mathbf b}\mathbf v\).
The conditional law on the chart
\(j\mapsto\mathbf z_{0,\mathbf b}+j\mathbf v\), \(0\le j<m_{\mathbf b}\), is exactly
\[
\pi_{\mathbf b}(\mathbf z_{0,\mathbf b}+j\mathbf v)
 =\frac{e^{\theta_{\mathrm{line}}j}}
        {\sum_{h=0}^{m_{\mathbf b}-1}e^{\theta_{\mathrm{line}}h}},
\qquad
\theta_{\mathrm{line}}=\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf v.
\]
Apply accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c to one factor, instantiating its scalar length \(m_1\) as \(m_{\mathbf b}\) and scalar tilt \(\theta_1\) as \(\theta_{\mathrm{line}}\). Its original law \(P\) has the exact alias \(P(j)=\pi_{\mathbf b}(\mathbf z_{0,\mathbf b}+j\mathbf v)\) for \(0\le j<m_{\mathbf b}\). The displayed affine pushforward and normal map therefore identify its actual third moment with \(\mathcal M_3(\pi_{\mathbf b})\), without changing any root probability.

If \(m_{\mathbf b}\ge2\), that accepted theorem has \(R=1\) and gives
\(\mathcal M_3(\pi_{\mathbf b})\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3/8\), uniformly for every finite length and every real tilt. If \(m_{\mathbf b}=1\), its actual depth and moment equal one. Accepted fact 8db3ba8c8761ea6c has \(0<a_{\mathrm{box}}<1\), so the singleton satisfies the stronger rank-zero bound
\(\mathcal M_3(\pi_{\mathbf b})\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\).
Consequently every line piece satisfies
\(\mathcal M_3(\pi_{\mathbf b})\ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3 8^{-r_{\mathbf b}}\), where \(r_{\mathbf b}\) is one for a nonsingleton and zero for a singleton. Applying the common-coefficient pooling theorem yields
\[
\mathcal M_3(p)
 \ge\frac{a_{\mathrm{box}}^3}
          {(\sum_{\mathbf b\in\mathcal I}2^{r_{\mathbf b}})^3}
 \ge\frac{a_{\mathrm{box}}^3}{(2N)^3}
 \ge a_{\mathrm{box}}^3\,8^{-n}.
\]
Every line is treated separately by the one-factor accepted theorem; no product law on the set of lines is invoked. The same reasoning works for independently tilted geometric conditional laws because the accepted scalar bound is uniform in the tilt, and the pooling theorem allows arbitrary original slice masses. Multiplication by \(T^4\) gives the capacity assertion for the original log-affine source weights, whose line prefactors are precisely \(b_{\mathrm{cap}}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf z_{0,\mathbf b}}\).

The support class includes supports that are not affine products. For example, in ambient dimension two,
\(D=(\{0\}\times\{0,1\})\cup(\{1\}\times\{0,1,2\})\)
has convex hull \(\{(x,y):0\le x\le1,\ 0\le y\le1+x\}\). Its integer points are exactly the five displayed points, so it is complete and is covered by two primitive vertical lines. It has affine rank two and cardinality five. A Cartesian product of two or more nonempty positive-dimensional finite factors has composite cardinality, so no injective affine image of such a product is this \(D\); in particular it is not an affine interval box. The proof therefore supplies more than an ambient affine-product specialization.

Finally, \(\mathbf z\mapsto U^{-1}\mathbf z\) acts in the full ambient dimension \(n\) and is an exact bijection on \(\mathbb Z^n\); it preserves closed cap traces and the original individual masses. Its extension
\((\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\mapsto(U^{-1}\mathbf z,\mathbf y)\)
maps \(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) bijectively onto itself and leaves each continuous fiber coordinate unchanged. Thus it preserves the \(d\)-dimensional measure of each fiber and their summed mixed-integer volume, with the same \(n\) and \(d\) and no index factor. For \(n=1\), a primitive direction is \(1\) or \(-1\), the quotient has rank zero, and the single complete line support is an interval or a singleton; the preceding proof applies verbatim. Lower-dimensional overall supports require no change: each actual piece rank is still zero or one, and the assumed line-count bound and the final normalization remain ambient-\(n\) statements. No unrestricted partition-existence claim follows.
