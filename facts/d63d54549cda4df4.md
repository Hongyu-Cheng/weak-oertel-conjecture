## Statement

Fix an integer \(k\ge8\). Use exactly the specialization \(\rho_t=2^{-t}\) of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, with the rooted-tree objects of accepted fact 032423921efad6c8. Explicitly, put \(b_\ell=0\) for \(1\le\ell<8\), \(b_\ell=\lfloor2^\ell/(8\ell)\rfloor\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_\ell=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}b_r\), \(Q=Q_k\), \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), \(n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r\), and \(N=n+1\). Put \(a_0=0\) and \(a_s=\ell\) when \(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell\). For any partition of the Boolean cube \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) into symmetric saturated chains, let \(A_{m,Q}\) be its exact rooted-tree lattice support from 032423921efad6c8, and let
$$
\widehat A
=\left\lbrace
(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\quad
a_{\operatorname{level}(z)}\le t\le k,\quad t\in\mathbb Z
\right\rbrace
$$
be the complete original lattice support from 604889294a17d7df.

For every real \(\gamma\) satisfying \(|\gamma|\ge1/(mQ)\), define \(w_{(z,t)}=\exp(\gamma\sum_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}z_u)2^{-t}\) on \(\widehat A\), \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}w_{\mathbf I}\), and \(p_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}/W\). Then
$$
S_4:=\sum_{\mathbf I\in\widehat A}p_{\mathbf I}^{\,4}
\ \ge\ 8^{-N}.
$$

## Proof

All support, successor, path, and entrance-height notation has exactly the meaning specified in 032423921efad6c8 and 604889294a17d7df. In particular, a source \(b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\) has a forward path of length \(h(b)\), and its level-\(s\) point is \(z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}\) for \(0\le s\le h(b)\). Every selected source coordinate is zero. Consequently, if \(|b|:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}b_u\) and \(\sigma(z):=\sum_{u\in\mathcal T_{m,Q}}z_u\), then \(\sigma(z_s(b))=|b|+2s\). The source is recovered by reducing the point modulo two, so the map \(b\mapsto z_s(b)\) is injective on the sources with \(h(b)\ge s\). These accepted facts also ensure that every Boolean source occurs at level zero, every level-\(s\) point has precisely the allowed original integer heights \(a_s,\ldots,k\), and \(a_s\le k\).

We first record finite parameter estimates. The quantity \(2^\ell/(8\ell)\) equals \(4\) at \(\ell=8\) and is increasing for integers \(\ell\ge8\), since its successive ratio is \(2\ell/(\ell+1)>1\). Thus \(b_\ell\ge4\) for \(8\le\ell\le k\), so \(Q\ge4(k-7)\), \(Q\ge4\), and
$$
k\le Q/4+7\le2Q.
$$
Because \(n\ge m^Q\ge m^4\) and \(m=2(Q+1)^4\), we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{n}{3m^2Q^2}
&\ge\frac{m^2}{3Q^2}
=\frac{4(Q+1)^8}{3Q^2}
\ge\frac43Q^6
\ge16Q^2,\\
\frac n3
&\ge\frac{m^2}{3}
\ge\frac43Q^8
\ge32Q^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last inequalities use respectively \(Q^4\ge12\) and \(Q^6\ge24\), both valid for \(Q\ge4\). Write \(\varepsilon=1/(mQ)\). Then \(0<\varepsilon\le1/8<\log2<1\). The two logarithmic inequalities follow from integrating \(1/x\) over \([1,2]\).

Put \(a=e^\gamma>0\) and \(\gamma_+=\max(\gamma,0)\). Summing the exact original heights and using the source parametrization gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
W
&=\sum_{s=0}^{Q}
\left(\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}2^{-t}\right)
a^{2s}\sum_{\substack{b\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{\mathcal T_{m,Q}}\\h(b)\ge s}}a^{|b|}\\
&\le2(Q+1)e^{2Q\gamma_+}(1+a)^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Indeed, each height sum is at most \(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}2^{-t}=2\), each source sum is at most the unrestricted Boolean product sum \((1+a)^n\), and \(a^{2s}\le e^{2Q\gamma_+}\). This bound retains all original atoms in its normalizer.

Every \((b,0)\) with Boolean \(b\) is in \(\widehat A\). Its weight is \(a^{|b|}\), so its fourth-power contribution before normalization sums to \((1+a^4)^n\). It follows that
$$
8^N S_4
\ge
\frac{\mathcal R(\gamma)^n e^{-8Q\gamma_+}}{2(Q+1)^4},
\qquad
\mathcal R(x):=\frac{8(1+e^{4x})}{(1+e^x)^4}.
$$

Here is the scalar estimate needed for this bound. Direct algebra gives
$$
\mathcal R(x)
=1+6\left(\frac{e^x-1}{e^x+1}\right)^2
+\left(\frac{e^x-1}{e^x+1}\right)^4.
$$
The function \(\mathcal R\) is even and nondecreasing in \(|x|\), since \((e^x-1)/(e^x+1)\) is nonnegative and increasing for \(x\ge0\). For \(0\le x\le\log2\), its displayed fraction lies in \([0,1/3]\) and is at least \(x/3\), because \(e^x-1\ge x\) and \(e^x+1\le3\). Thus
$$
\mathcal R(x)-1\ge\frac23x^2,
\qquad
1\le\mathcal R(x)\le1+\frac69+\frac1{81}
=\frac{136}{81}<2.
$$
For \(y\ge1\), integration gives \(\log y\ge(y-1)/y\). Applying this to \(\mathcal R(x)\) proves
$$
\log\mathcal R(x)\ge x^2/3
\qquad(0\le x\le\log2).
$$
Therefore every \(|\gamma|\ge\varepsilon\), including arbitrarily negative \(\gamma\), satisfies \(\log\mathcal R(\gamma)\ge\log\mathcal R(\varepsilon)\ge\varepsilon^2/3\).

Suppose first that \(\gamma\le\log2\) and \(|\gamma|\ge\varepsilon\). Then \(\gamma_+\le\log2<1\), and the preceding normalizer and scalar estimates give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(8^N S_4)
&\ge\frac{n}{3m^2Q^2}-8Q-\log2-4\log(Q+1)\\
&\ge16Q^2-12Q-1
>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
We used \(\log(Q+1)\le Q\), \(\log2<1\), and \(Q\ge4\). This proves the claim throughout this range.

For the remaining range \(a=e^\gamma\ge2\), we prove a more suitable normalizer bound. There are \(m^s\) possible rooted paths of length \(s\). For any one such path, every source producing that path has its \(s\) selected coordinates equal to zero. After dropping all its other path-selection constraints, the sum of \(a^{|b|}\) over its sources is at most \((1+a)^{n-s}\). The path is determined by its source, so these source classes are disjoint; the same estimate also holds at \(s=0\), with one empty path. It follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
W
&\le2(1+a)^n
\sum_{s=0}^{Q}\left(\frac{ma^2}{1+a}\right)^s\\
&\le2(Q+1)(1+a)^n
\left(\frac{ma^2}{1+a}\right)^Q.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final inequality holds because \(a^2/(1+a)\ge a/2\ge1\) and hence \(ma^2/(1+a)\ge1\).

We next construct the original atom used with this normalizer. The successor of 032423921efad6c8 has \(\mathbf1\) in its image. Let \(c=F_m^{-1}(\mathbf1)\). Since a successor adds exactly one formerly zero coordinate, \(c\) is the all-ones block with exactly one coordinate, say \(i\), replaced by zero, and \(i_m(c)=i\). Let \(u_r\) be the word consisting of \(r\) repetitions of \(i\), for \(1\le r\le Q\). Choose a global Boolean source \(b^\star\) whose coordinates at \(u_1,\ldots,u_Q\) are zero and whose other coordinates are all one. At every visited node \(u_{r-1}\), including the root when \(r=1\), its child block equals \(c\). Its forward path is therefore exactly \(u_1,\ldots,u_Q\), and \(h(b^\star)=Q\).

Consequently \(z^\star=z_Q(b^\star)\) is an original level-\(Q\) point of \(A_{m,Q}\), with \(\sigma(z^\star)=n-Q+2Q=n+Q\). Since \(a_Q\le k\), the index \(\mathbf I^\star=(z^\star,k)\) belongs to the original support \(\widehat A\) and has its exact original weight \(w_{\mathbf I^\star}=a^{n+Q}2^{-k}\). In particular, no enlargement or change of support is used. Its normalized mass satisfies
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathbf I^\star}
&\ge
2^{-k-1}(Q+1)^{-1}m^{-Q}
\left(\frac{a}{1+a}\right)^{n-Q}\\
&\ge
2^{-k-1}(Q+1)^{-1}m^{-Q}
\left(\frac23\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here \(n\ge Q\), \(a/(1+a)\ge2/3\), and \((2/3)^{n-Q}\ge(2/3)^n\).

Since \(S_4\ge p_{\mathbf I^\star}^{\,4}\), this gives
$$
\log(8^N S_4)
\ge
n\log\frac{128}{81}
-(4k+1)\log2-4\log(Q+1)-4Q\log m.
$$
For completeness, \(128/81>3/2\) and \(\log(3/2)>1/3\), the latter by integrating \(1/x\) over \([1,3/2]\). Also \(\log m=\log2+4\log(Q+1)\le1+4Q\le5Q\). Using the proved bounds \(k\le2Q\) and \(n/3\ge32Q^2\), we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log(8^N S_4)
&>\frac n3-20Q^2-12Q-1\\
&\ge12Q^2-12Q-1
>0.
\end{aligned}
$$
This covers every \(\gamma\ge\log2\), and together the two ranges prove \(S_4\ge8^{-N}\) for every required field and every allowed chain partition.
