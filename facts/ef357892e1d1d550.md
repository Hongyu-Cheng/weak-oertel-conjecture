## Statement

Let $C=[-3,3]\times[-1,1]$ have mixed measure equal to the sum of lengths on its seven integer fibers. In the positive projective chart $(z,x)=r(1,s)$, let $R(s)=\min(3,1/|s|)$, interpreted as $3$ at $s=0$, and $f_+(s)=\sum_{1\le k\le R(s)}k$. There is no compact planar convex set containing the origin whose ordinary angular area density in this chart is $f_+(s)$ almost everywhere.

Separately, no convex gauge on the positive chart can interpolate the reciprocal angular radii $1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt6,1/\sqrt{12}$ at $s=1,1/2,1/3$, respectively. For the polynomial interpolation $F_1(R)=R(R+1)/2$ of the arithmetic sum, the candidate reciprocal radius on $1/3<s<1$ is $s/\sqrt{1+s}$ and is strictly concave there.

## Proof

The true mixed centroid is zero by symmetry. At height $k>0$ the length element pulls back under $x=ks$ to $k\,ds$ on $|s|\le1/k$, giving the stated $f_+$. If an auxiliary convex set containing zero has radial length $\widehat R(s)$, the Jacobian of $(r,s)\mapsto(r,rs)$ is $r$, so its angular area density is $\int_0^{\widehat R(s)} r\,dr=\widehat R(s)^2/2$. Hence almost-everywhere matching would require its gauge at $(1,s)$ to equal $1/\sqrt{2f_+(s)}$ almost everywhere wherever $f_+(s)>0$. This is $1/\sqrt2$ on $(1/2,1)$ and $1/\sqrt6$ on $(1/3,1/2)$, almost everywhere. The restricted convex gauge is finite at slopes from full-measure subsets of both intervals. Its convex domain therefore contains their open span, including a neighborhood of $1/2$. It is continuous there. Its two unequal one-sided values contradict continuity.

For the interpolation claim, closed arithmetic radii at the three indicated slopes are the integers $1,2,3$. The corresponding reciprocal angular radii are the values in the statement. Since $1/2=(1/4)1+(3/4)(1/3)$, convexity would require
$$
\frac1{\sqrt6}\le\frac1{4\sqrt2}+\frac3{4\sqrt{12}}.
$$
The strict reverse is equivalent to $4\sqrt2>\sqrt6+3$, or after squaring to $17>6\sqrt6$. The last inequality follows by squaring, since $289>216$ and both sides are positive. In the same chart the original radius is $1/s$ for $1/3<s<1$. Thus the polynomial rule gives reciprocal angular radius $1/\sqrt{R(R+1)}=s/\sqrt{1+s}$ with second derivative $-(1+s/4)/(1+s)^{5/2}<0$. Neither test uses an interpolation constraint at isolated slopes to infer failure of almost-everywhere matching.
