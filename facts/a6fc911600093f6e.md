## Statement

For positive planar fibers with pure uniform outward smoothing at fixed positive width, suppose an actual source admits a probability law on actual active caps for which the coarea-weighted continuous normal has finite mean zero. The supremum of individual marked boundary capture efficiency over balanced active menus and nonnegative continuous physical fields convex in the physical point is at least $1/3$. Over this attained class, allowing arbitrary integer dimension, the universal floor is exactly $1/3$.

## Proof

The integrable response vector of a cap is its continuous normal times its threshold coarea, with zero vector for a pure-base cap if such caps are included in the menu. Let $A$ be the actual range of response vectors of the given balanced law. Its mean lies in $\operatorname{conv}A$, even if this hull is not closed. First it lies in the closed hull by separation and integration. If the mean is in its relative interior, it is also in the hull (the relative interior of a finite-dimensional convex set agrees with that of its closure). Otherwise take a supporting affine hyperplane in its affine span through the mean. Since the law is on one closed side and its mean is on the hyperplane, it is concentrated on the hyperplane. Repeat in that smaller affine span. This proves hull membership with actual vectors. Planar Carathéodory now supplies at most three actual active caps whose response vectors balance, with probability weights. One has weight at least $1/3$.

For that cap let $m$ be its affine through-source margin. Its positive-part field $[m]_+$ is convex and its support is paid at least the cap's weight whenever it has positive boundary integral. A strict positive margin in a measured fiber gives such a boundary patch, since the affine maximum is reached on the boundary and any relatively open boundary patch has positive length. For a supporting cap (or if strict positive margin gives no test), use $[m+\eta]_+$, $\eta>0$. It has positive boundary integral: the cap passes through its mark, so the affine maximum on that fiber is at least zero, attained on the boundary. For a nonvertical cap with actual width $H>0$, its ramp is at least $1-\eta/H$ on this positive support, and the other ramps are nonnegative. Thus the supremum is at least the cap's weight by $\eta\downarrow0$. For a pure-base cap the sampled margins take finitely many values, so choose $\eta$ smaller than all nonzero negative gaps; every measured fiber in the positive support is then fully paid. This gives the same conclusion.

The family of w31-axis-guard-capacity has actual balanced menus and its best-menu supremum tends to $1/3$ for fixed positive $w$ and increasing $L$. Hence no larger universal floor is possible.
