## Statement

Assume the complete triangular-homothetic saturated three-circuit setting and notation of accepted facts 05e841287760f20f, 6ce65197bbcae680, d33ef001609a8cc8, and 8af542f1e42c0f7b.  In particular, let
\[
 L_0:=\sum_{z\in B_0}L_z,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),
\tag{1}
\]
let \(M\) be the reduced common-slack objective
\[
 M:=
 \sup_{\rho\in\Delta_3}
 \sup_{\substack{B'\subseteq B_0,\ 0<\tau_z\leq a_z\\
 q^\rho(z)\in\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z)}}
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z,
\tag{2}
\]
and let \(\pi\), \(\Pi\), \(\mu\),
\[
 h_z:=d_z(\mu)\leq a_z,
 \qquad
 P:=\sum_e\mu_e,
\tag{3}
\]
be the maximum loss-capacity matching and its physically trimmed submatching from accepted fact 8af542f1e42c0f7b.  Write
\[
 D:=2^n h_{\max}(C).
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{D\geq\frac49M,}
 \qquad
 \boxed{D\geq\frac23P,}
\tag{5}
\]
and consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 D\geq\frac4{39}
 \left(L_0-\frac23G\right)^+.}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, the second inequality in (5) is the exact physical-source single-capacity bound
\[
 \boxed{P\leq\frac32D.}
\tag{7}
\]

Suppose in addition that, for some \(\eta\in(0,1]\), the hard-descendant hypotheses in accepted fact d33ef001609a8cc8 hold,
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\leq\frac\eta6W.
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq\frac{148}{1053}\,\eta\,2^{-n}W.}
\tag{9}
\]
No assertion of the affine-amplification property (17)-(19) of accepted fact 8af542f1e42c0f7b, of \(P\leq2G/3+U_0\), or of \(\Pi\leq G+U_0\) is part of this lemma.

## Proof

We first charge the already affine common-slack profiles.  Fix any feasible profile in (2), and put
\[
 T:=\sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(T=0\), there is nothing to prove.  Apply the \(d=0\) specialization of accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to the finite base measure
\[
 \sum_{z\in B'}\tau_z\delta_z.
\]
It supplies an integral point \(m\) whose every proper closed base halfspace has profile mass at least \(2^{-n}T\).  Positive depth forces
\(m\in\operatorname{conv}B'\).  For every
\(\lambda\in\Lambda_m(B')\), affinity of \(q^\rho\) gives
\[
 q^\rho(m)
 =\sum_{z\in B'}\lambda_zq^\rho(z)
 \in
 \sum_{z\in B'}
 \lambda_z\mathsf M_{\tau_z}(C_z).
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the selected-mass complete core is nonempty.  The arbitrary-selected-mass nonempty-core conclusion of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb, in continuous dimension two, gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq
 \left(\frac23\right)^2 2^{-n}T
 =\frac49\,2^{-n}T.
\tag{12}
\]
Taking the supremum over all feasible profiles proves the first inequality in (5).  Attainment of the supremum is unnecessary: apply (12) to profiles with masses tending to \(M\).

We next charge the trimmed matching directly to one physical measure.  Extend \(h_z\) by zero off its finite support and define a finite Borel measure \(\sigma\) on
\(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2\) by
\[
 \sigma(A):=
 \sum_{z:h_z>0}
 \frac{h_z}{a_z}
 \mathcal H_2
 \bigl(\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\}\bigr)
\tag{13}
\]
for every Borel set \(A\).  The physical capacities in (3) give
\[
 0\leq\frac{h_z}{a_z}\leq1,
\]
so \(\sigma\) is pointwise dominated by the mixed-volume measure of \(C\).  Double-counting the endpoints of \(\mu\) gives
\[
 \sigma(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2)
 =\sum_zh_z
 =2\sum_e\mu_e
 =2P.
\tag{14}
\]
This is one source density on each physical fiber; neither the colored matching \(\nu\) nor any midpoint density is used.

If \(P=0\), the second inequality in (5) is immediate.  Suppose \(P>0\).  Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c to \(\sigma\) on
\(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2\).  It gives a point
\(y\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\ni y\) satisfies
\[
 \sigma(H)
 \geq\frac{2P}{2^n(2+1)}
 =\frac{2P}{3\cdot2^n}.
\tag{15}
\]
The point lies in \(C\).  Otherwise strict separation of \(y\) from the compact convex body \(C\) would give a closed halfspace containing \(y\) and disjoint from \(C\), whose \(\sigma\)-mass is zero, contradicting (15).  Hence
\(y\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2)\).  Domination of \(\sigma\) by mixed volume now gives
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq h_{C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^2)}(y)
 \geq\frac{2P}{3\cdot2^n},
\tag{16}
\]
which is the second inequality in (5), and equivalently (7).  This argument preserves every physical source once because the density on \(C_z\) is exactly \(h_z/a_z\leq1\).

Accepted fact 05e841287760f20f gives
\[
 L_0\leq3M+2\Pi,
\tag{17}
\]
while accepted fact 8af542f1e42c0f7b gives
\[
 \Pi-P\leq\frac G3.
\tag{18}
\]
Therefore
\[
 L_0-\frac23G
 \leq3M+2P.
\tag{19}
\]
Equations (4)-(5) imply
\[
 M\leq\frac94D,
 \qquad
 P\leq\frac32D.
\tag{20}
\]
Substituting (20) into (19) gives
\[
 L_0-\frac23G
 \leq
 \frac{27}{4}D+3D
 =\frac{39}{4}D.
\tag{21}
\]
If the left side is nonpositive, (6) follows from \(D\geq0\); otherwise rearranging (21) proves (6).

It remains to derive the hard-branch constant.  Put
\[
 L_U:=L_B-L_0,
 \qquad
 U_0:=\sum_{u\in U}a_u.
\tag{22}
\]
Accepted fact d33ef001609a8cc8 gives, under (8),
\[
 L_B\geq3\eta W-8G,
 \qquad
 U_0\leq G,
\tag{23}
\]
and accepted fact 6ce65197bbcae680 gives
\[
 L_U\leq\frac{10}{9}U_0.
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently
\[
 L_0
 =L_B-L_U
 \geq3\eta W-8G-\frac{10}{9}U_0
 \geq3\eta W-\frac{82}{9}G,
\tag{25}
\]
and hence
\[
 L_0-\frac23G
 \geq3\eta W-\frac{88}{9}G
 \geq
 \left(3-\frac{88}{54}\right)\eta W
 =\frac{37}{27}\eta W.
\tag{26}
\]
The last quantity is positive.  Applying (6) and (4) yields
\[
 2^nh_{\max}(C)
 =D
 \geq
 \frac4{39}\frac{37}{27}\eta W
 =\frac{148}{1053}\eta W,
\tag{27}
\]
which is (9).  At no point were midpoint fibers pooled, separate values of \(G\) added, or the rainbow affine-amplification property used.
