## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Let \(\mu\) be a finite nonnegative Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^d\) with finite first moment, and put \(M:=\mu(\mathbb R^d)\). For \(0<t\leq M\), define its mass-\(t\) metronoid by
\[
 \mathsf M_t(\mu):=
 \left\{
  \frac1t\int_{\mathbb R^d}x f(x)\,d\mu(x):
  f\text{ measurable},\ 0\leq f\leq1\ \mu\text{-a.e.},\
  \int_{\mathbb R^d}f\,d\mu=t
 \right\}.
\]
Let \(I\) be a finite index set. For each \(i\in I\), let \(0<\theta_i\leq M\) and let \(L_i\) be a nonempty subset of \(\mathsf M_{\theta_i}(\mu)\). Let \(w_i\geq0\), and assume
\[
 W:=\sum_{i\in I}w_i>0,
 \qquad
 \kappa:=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{w_i}{\theta_i}\leq1.
\]
Then \(0<W\leq M\) and
\[
 \frac1W\sum_{i\in I}w_iL_i
 \subseteq \mathsf M_W(\mu),
\]
where the left side is the full weighted Minkowski set
\[
 \frac1W\sum_{i\in I}w_iL_i
 :=
 \left\{
  \frac1W\sum_{i\in I}w_i y_i:
  y_i\in L_i\text{ for every }i\in I
 \right\}.
\]
In particular, indices of weight zero impose no restriction on, and make no contribution to, the pooled point.

## Proof

Fix an arbitrary independent selection \(y_i\in L_i\) for every \(i\in I\). Such a selection exists because \(I\) is finite and every \(L_i\) is nonempty. By \(y_i\in\mathsf M_{\theta_i}(\mu)\), for each \(i\) there is a measurable function \(f_i\) such that
\[
 0\leq f_i\leq1\quad\mu\text{-a.e.},
 \qquad
 \int f_i\,d\mu=\theta_i,
 \qquad
 \int x f_i(x)\,d\mu(x)=\theta_i y_i.
\]
The vector integrals are finite because \(0\leq f_i\leq1\) and \(\mu\) has finite first moment. Since every \(\theta_i\) is positive, the coefficients \(c_i:=w_i/\theta_i\) are well-defined, including when \(w_i=0\). Define one pooled density
\[
 g:=\sum_{i\in I}c_i f_i.
\]
The sum is finite. Pointwise almost everywhere,
\[
 0\leq g
 \leq\sum_{i\in I}c_i
 =\kappa
 \leq1.
\]
Its mass and first moment are exactly
\[
 \int g\,d\mu
 =\sum_{i\in I}\frac{w_i}{\theta_i}\int f_i\,d\mu
 =\sum_{i\in I}w_i
 =W
\]
and
\[
 \int xg(x)\,d\mu(x)
 =\sum_{i\in I}\frac{w_i}{\theta_i}
   \int xf_i(x)\,d\mu(x)
 =\sum_{i\in I}w_i y_i.
\]
Because \(0\leq g\leq1\), its mass also satisfies \(W=\int g\,d\mu\leq M\); by hypothesis \(W>0\). Hence \(W\) is an admissible metronoid mass, and the defining density \(g\) proves
\[
 \frac1W\sum_{i\in I}w_i y_i
 =\frac1W\int xg(x)\,d\mu(x)
 \in\mathsf M_W(\mu).
\]
Since the tuple \((y_i)_{i\in I}\) was arbitrary, the asserted full Minkowski-set inclusion follows.

If \(w_i=0\), then \(c_i=0\), so both \(c_if_i\) and \(w_iy_i\) vanish; no division by \(w_i\) occurs. The assumption \(W>0\) excludes the all-zero family and also forces \(M>0\). The argument permits every boundary value \(\theta_i=M\), and it permits \(W=M\); in the latter case the same density \(g\) is admissible at full mass. Thus all stated mass boundaries and zero-weight indices are covered.
