## Statement

Let $P\subset\mathbb R^n$ be finite and nonempty and let $d\ge1$. Let $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$ be the affine barycentric coordinates of a nondegenerate $d$-simplex. For each $z\in P$, let $K_z=\{x:\lambda_i(x)\ge b_i(z)\ \forall i\}$, where $1-\sum_i b_i(z)>0$. Assume that these placed fibers are the actual sampled sections of their convex hull. Let $F_i(z)=K_z\cap\{\lambda_i=b_i(z)\}$, let $n_i$ be its outer unit normal, and put $\eta_i=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes\mathcal H^{d-1}|_{F_i(z)}$ in the physical variables $(z,x)$. For $d=1$ use counting measure on the endpoints. Let $\eta$ be the sum of these measures with their respective normal marks. For any bounded nonnegative Borel profile $W$ on this bundle, define
$$
\mathcal E(W)=\sup_\Phi \frac{\int \Phi W\,d\eta}{\int\Phi\,d\eta},
$$
over continuous nonnegative fields whose restriction at each fixed normal is a finite convex function of $(z,x)$ and whose denominator is positive.

For every $c\ge0$, $\mathcal E(W)\le c$ if and only if, for each $i$, there is a physical martingale with source $\mu_i=(W-c)_+\eta_i$ and second marginal at most $\nu_i=(c-W)_+\eta_i$, with $W$ restricted to mark $n_i$. The transport uses the actual measures, including any closed-cap boundary values in $W$.

Write $G_i=\operatorname{conv}\{(z,b_i(z)):z\in P\}$. For almost every source row on $F_i(p)$, all its target lifted bases lie in the minimal face of $G_i$ containing $(p,b_i(p))$. If $F$ is any nonempty face of $G_i$ and $E_F$ is the union of the actual $i$-facets whose lifted bases lie in $F$, then $\mathcal E(W)\le c$ implies
$$
\mu_i(E_F)\le\nu_i(E_F),\qquad \int_{E_F}W\,d\eta_i\le c\eta_i(E_F).
$$
The used target mass is subject to one global $\nu_i$ ceiling, including where source faces share subfaces. If every lifted base is a vertex of $G_i$, transport at mark $n_i$ preserves the base. If an affine $a$ satisfies $b_i(z)\ge a(z)$ with equality precisely on the bases in $F$, then for some $\delta>0$ an admissible potential equals $\delta$ on $E_F$ and zero on the rest of the marked bundle.

## Proof

First, for finite nonnegative compactly supported Borel measures $\mu,\nu$ on $\mathbb R^D$, the inequalities $\int\phi\,d\mu\le\int\phi\,d\nu$ for every finite continuous nonnegative convex $\phi$ are equivalent to a martingale from $\mu$ into a submeasure of $\nu$. One implication follows from conditional Jensen and domination. For the converse let $r=\nu(\mathbb R^D)-\mu(\mathbb R^D)\ge0$ by the constant test. If $r=0$, for every finite continuous convex $f$ the test $\max(0,f+C)$ agrees with $f+C$ on both compact supports when $C$ is sufficiently large. The masses agree, so cancellation gives $\mu\preceq_{\rm cx}\nu$, and ac53fe19fef9ec25 applies directly.

If $r>0$, put $b=(\int y\,d\nu-\int x\,d\mu)/r$ and let $\widehat\mu=\mu+r\delta_b$. Its mass and first moment agree with those of $\nu$. Given any finite continuous convex $f$, take a supporting affine function $a$ at $b$, which exists because $f$ is finite on all of $\mathbb R^D$. Then $f-a$ is a nonnegative convex test with zero value at $b$. Its integral over $\widehat\mu$ equals that over $\mu$ and is at most the integral over $\nu$. The affine moments cancel, giving $\widehat\mu\preceq_{\rm cx}\nu$. By ac53fe19fef9ec25 there is a martingale $\widehat\pi$ with these exact marginals. Multiply $\widehat\pi(dx,dy)$ by $(d\mu/d\widehat\mu)(x)\in[0,1]$. The first marginal is $\mu$, the second is at most $\nu$, and every bounded Borel row-moment test still gives zero by absorbing the density into the test. This includes collisions of $b$ with original source mass and $\mu=0$.

At mark $n_i$, choose a nonnegative continuous normal selector that is one at $n_i$ and zero at the other finitely many normals. Multiply by a nonnegative continuous convex function $\phi(z,x)$ to see that $\mathcal E(W)\le c$ implies $\int\phi(W-c)\,d\eta_i\le0$. A nonnegative test of denominator zero has zero numerator since $W$ is bounded. Conversely, add these inequalities for the restrictions of each admissible $\Phi$ to the marks. This is exactly the preceding comparison for $\mu_i,\nu_i$. Their supports are compact, so the first part applies and retains both physical coordinates.

Disintegrate a feasible transport over its source. For a row starting at $(p,x)$ on the $i$-facet, let $t_z$ denote its output base probabilities. The physical mean and the facet equation of the targets give
$$
\sum_z t_z z=p,\qquad \sum_z t_z b_i(z)=\lambda_i(x)=b_i(p).
$$
These identities hold for almost every source row. The lifted point $(p,b_i(p))$ is therefore a positive combination of exactly the lifted bases with $t_z>0$. Every face containing that point contains each positive term, so the targets lie in its minimal face. A source in $E_F$ can only send to $E_F$, and hence $\mu_i(E_F)\le\nu_i(E_F)$ by target domination. The equivalent integral quota follows by subtracting the excesses. In particular each target's minimal face is contained in that of its source and capacity on a common subface is counted only in the one target marginal. If the source lift is a vertex the only possible target lift is that vertex, which determines the base.

For the claimed hinge choose $\delta>0$ less than every positive $b_i(z)-a(z)$ on bases outside $F$ (any $\delta>0$ works if there are no such bases). With the normal selector $\psi_i$ used above, set $\Phi(n,z,x)=\psi_i(n)[\delta+a(z)-\lambda_i(x)]_+$. Its values on the actual bundle have the required form. It is finite, continuous and convex at each fixed normal, and the corresponding ratio detects the quota on $E_F$.
