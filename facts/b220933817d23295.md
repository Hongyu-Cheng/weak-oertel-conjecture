## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and set
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
C_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in C\},
\qquad
w_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}).
\]
Let
\[
D_+:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_{\mathbf z}>0\},
\]
assume \(D_+\neq\varnothing\), and suppose that the affine map
\[
q(\mathbf z):=A\mathbf z+\mathbf b
\]
satisfies \(q(\mathbf z)\in C_{\mathbf z}\) for every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\). For \(\mathbf z\in D_+\), define
\[
\delta_{\mathbf z}:=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\ \text{is a proper closed halfspace and }q(\mathbf z)\in J
\right\},
\]
and put \(\delta_{\mathbf z}=0\) for \(\mathbf z\notin D_+\). If
\[
\Delta:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}\delta_{\mathbf z}>0,
\]
then there exists \(\mathbf z^\star\in D_+\) such that
\[
\mathbf y^\star:=(\mathbf z^\star,q(\mathbf z^\star))\in S
\]
and every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\!\left(
C_{\mathbf z}\cap
\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in H\}
\right)
\geq 2^{-n}\Delta.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
h_S(\mathbf z^\star,q(\mathbf z^\star))\geq 2^{-n}\Delta.
\]
Consequently, if for some \(\beta>0\),
\[
\Delta\geq\beta\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z},
\]
then
\[
h_S(\mathbf z^\star,q(\mathbf z^\star))
\geq
\beta 2^{-n}\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_{\mathbf z}.
\]

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) implies that its projection to \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded. Hence only finitely many integer fibers are nonempty, so \(D_+\) is finite and all sums below are finite. For every \(\mathbf z\in D_+\), one has
\[
0\leq\delta_{\mathbf z}\leq w_{\mathbf z}.
\]
Indeed, the first inequality is immediate, and compactness of \(C_{\mathbf z}\) gives a proper closed halfspace containing all of \(C_{\mathbf z}\), whose intersection with \(C_{\mathbf z}\) has volume \(w_{\mathbf z}\).

Put
\[
E:=\{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n:\delta_{\mathbf z}>0\}.
\]
The assumption \(\Delta>0\) makes \(E\) a nonempty finite subset of \(D_+\). The weighted lattice-centerpoint argument proved inside accepted fact `bb2b5f9519ae23e8` applies to an arbitrary finite lattice support with arbitrary positive real weights. Applied to the support \(E\) and weights \(\delta_{\mathbf z}\), it gives a point
\[
\mathbf z^\star\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}(E)
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(\mathbf z^\star\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E\cap Q}\delta_{\mathbf z}
\geq 2^{-n}\Delta.
\tag{1}
\]

We verify that the selected integer point lies in the positive-mass selector domain. Write
\[
\mathbf z^\star=\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i\mathbf z_i,
\qquad
\mathbf z_i\in E,\quad
\lambda_i>0,\quad
\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=1.
\]
Fix \(i_0\), and for each \(i\neq i_0\) choose \(\mathbf x_i\in C_{\mathbf z_i}\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\lambda_{i_0}C_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}
+
\sum_{i\neq i_0}\lambda_i\mathbf x_i
\subseteq C_{\mathbf z^\star}.
\]
The set on the left is a translate of a positive homothetic copy of \(C_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}\), so
\[
w_{\mathbf z^\star}
\geq
\lambda_{i_0}^{\,d}w_{\mathbf z_{i_0}}>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathbf z^\star\in D_+\). The selector hypothesis now gives \(q(\mathbf z^\star)\in C_{\mathbf z^\star}\), and hence \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\).

Let \(H\) be a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\). First suppose that \(H\) has a nonzero normal. Translating its boundary toward \(\mathbf y^\star\) produces the contained boundary-through-\(\mathbf y^\star\) halfspace
\[
H_0:=
\left\{
(\mathbf z,\mathbf x):
\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+
\mathbf c^\top(\mathbf x-q(\mathbf z^\star))
\geq0
\right\}
\subseteq H,
\tag{2}
\]
where \((\mathbf a,\mathbf c)\neq(\mathbf0,\mathbf0)\). Define
\[
\mathbf r:=\mathbf a+A^\top\mathbf c,\qquad
Q:=
\left\{
\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n:
\mathbf r^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)\geq0
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(\mathbf r\neq\mathbf0\), then \(Q\) is a proper closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf z^\star\), and (1) applies. If \(\mathbf r=\mathbf0\), then \(Q=\mathbb R^n\), so
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E\cap Q}\delta_{\mathbf z}
=\Delta\geq2^{-n}\Delta.
\tag{4}
\]

For every \(\mathbf z\in E\cap Q\), affineness of \(q\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+
\mathbf c^\top(q(\mathbf z)-q(\mathbf z^\star))\\
&\qquad=
(\mathbf a+A^\top\mathbf c)^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
=
\mathbf r^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
\geq0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore the fiber section
\[
J_{\mathbf z}:=
\left\{
\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:
\mathbf a^\top(\mathbf z-\mathbf z^\star)
+
\mathbf c^\top(\mathbf x-q(\mathbf z^\star))
\geq0
\right\}
\tag{6}
\]
contains \(q(\mathbf z)\).

If \(\mathbf c\neq\mathbf0\), then \(J_{\mathbf z}\) is a proper closed halfspace, so the definition of \(\delta_{\mathbf z}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap J_{\mathbf z})
\geq\delta_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{7}
\]
This includes the degenerate-pullback case \(\mathbf r=\mathbf0\): because the ambient normal in (2) is nonzero, \(\mathbf r=\mathbf0\) forces \(\mathbf c\neq\mathbf0\), and (5) then says that every selector point lies on the corresponding fiber boundary.

If \(\mathbf c=\mathbf0\), then the ambient halfspace is vertical. For every \(\mathbf z\in Q\), (6) is all of \(\mathbb R^d\), and hence
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap J_{\mathbf z})
=w_{\mathbf z}\geq\delta_{\mathbf z}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(\mathbf a\neq\mathbf0\), so \(\mathbf r=\mathbf a\neq\mathbf0\) and \(Q\) is proper.

Summing only over \(E\cap Q\), using (1) when \(\mathbf r\neq\mathbf0\) and (4) when \(\mathbf r=\mathbf0\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap (H_0)_{\mathbf z})
&\geq
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E\cap Q}
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{\mathbf z}\cap J_{\mathbf z})\\
&\geq
\sum_{\mathbf z\in E\cap Q}\delta_{\mathbf z}\\
&\geq2^{-n}\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(H_0\subseteq H\), the same bound holds for \(H\).

It remains only the degenerate ambient representation with zero normal. A closed halfspace with zero normal that contains \(\mathbf y^\star\) is the whole space. Its mixed volume is
\[
\sum_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}
\geq
\sum_{\mathbf z}\delta_{\mathbf z}
=\Delta
\geq2^{-n}\Delta.
\]
Thus every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) has mixed volume at least \(2^{-n}\Delta\), proving the main assertion. The final claim follows immediately from \(\Delta\geq\beta\sum_{\mathbf z}w_{\mathbf z}\).
