## Statement

For \(q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let

\[
 \Delta_q:=
 \left\{x\in\mathbb R^q:
 x_\nu\geq0\ (1\leq\nu\leq q),\quad
 \sum_{\nu=1}^q x_\nu\leq1\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For \(0<r<1\), define

\[
 K_{q,r}:=
 \left\{(t,x):
 -1\leq t\leq1,\quad
 x\in\bigl(1-(1-r)|t|\bigr)\Delta_q\right\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^q,
\tag{2}
\]

and, for \(k\geq2\), define

\[
 C_{k,q,r}:=K_{q,r}^{\,k}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{kq}.
\tag{3}
\]

For a positive-volume lattice fiber \(C_z\), write

\[
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_{kq}(C_z),\qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^k}a_z,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C_{k,q,r}),
\tag{4}
\]

where \(G\) is the complete metronoid-core capacity of accepted fact
ae0161dd5d4ea672.

For a \(kq\)-dimensional convex fiber \(K\) and
\(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_{kq}(K)\), use the metronoid normalization

\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{\frac1t\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq1\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \int_K f(x)\,dx=t\right\}.
\]

For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^k\) and
\(m\in\mathbb Z^k\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), put

\[
 \Lambda_m(E):=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\quad
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=m\right\}.
\]

Use the exact descendant collection of accepted fact
f23ff6dd60fbbc37, as follows. A free matching \(p=(p_e)\) is supported on
unordered distinct same-parity lattice pairs and satisfies

\[
 d_z(p):=\sum_{e\ni z}p_e\leq\frac{a_z}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]

For every maximum such matching, put \(t_z=2d_z(p)\), omit the zero
masses, and let \(m\in\mathbb Z^k\) be any admissible center, meaning that
every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni m\) contains at least
\(2^{-k}\sum_z t_z\) of the selected mass. Whenever

\[
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_m(E_p)}
 \sum_{z\in E_p}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
 =\varnothing,
\tag{6}
\]

include every individual exact-minorant cap and every strictly positive
same-base reweighting supplied by every support-minimal balanced negative
circuit of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb. Explicitly, if its data are
\((w_i,\theta_i,\ell_i)_{i=1}^s\), the individual caps are

\[
 A_i=C_{k,q,r}\cap
 \{(z,x):\langle w_i,x\rangle\geq\ell_i(z)\},
\]

and every strictly positive probability vector
\(\vartheta=(\vartheta_1,\ldots,\vartheta_s)\) contributes the same-base
body with fibers

\[
 (B_\vartheta)_z=\sum_{i=1}^s\vartheta_i(A_i)_z.
\]

Denote this collection by \(\mathscr D(C_{k,q,r})\), and set

\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C_{k,q,r})
 :=\sup_{B\in\mathscr D(C_{k,q,r})}W(B),\qquad
 W(B):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^k}\operatorname{vol}_{kq}(B_z),
\tag{7}
\]

with the empty-supremum convention from the accepted fact. Formula (7) is
the definition used below; forming this collection does not require a
small-\(G\) hypothesis.

There are absolute constants \(A<\infty\) and \(\beta>0\) such that, for
every \(k\geq2\), \(q\geq1\), and \(0<r<1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 1-\frac{R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C_{k,q,r})}{W(C_{k,q,r})}
 \leq
 A\left(
 \frac{G(C_{k,q,r})}{W(C_{k,q,r})}

\tag{8}
\]

When \(q=1\), one has \(\Delta_1=[0,1]\). For the parameters

\[
 C_{k,1,r_L}=C_L^{\,k}.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus (8) applies in particular to every Cartesian power of every initial

## Proof

Put

\[
 v_q:=\operatorname{vol}_q(\Delta_q)=\frac1{q!},\qquad
 c_q:=\operatorname{centroid}(\Delta_q),\qquad
 b_q:=\left\langle(1,\ldots,1),c_q\right\rangle
 =\frac q{q+1}.
\tag{10}
\]

The function \(t\mapsto1-(1-r)|t|\) is positive and concave on
\([-1,1]\). Thus \(K_{q,r}\), and hence its Cartesian power
\(C_{k,q,r}\), is a compact full-dimensional convex body.

We first identify the fibers and construct one maximum matching and one
legal cap explicitly. For \(z\in\{-1,0,1\}^k\), let

\[
 s(z):=|\{j:z_j\neq0\}|.
\]

The positive-volume lattice support is exactly
\(Z=\{-1,0,1\}^k\), and

\[
 (C_{k,q,r})_z
 =\prod_{j=1}^k
 \begin{cases}
 \Delta_q,&z_j=0,\\
 r\Delta_q,&z_j=\pm1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 a_z=v_q^k r^{q s(z)}.
\tag{11}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 W=v_q^k(1+2r^q)^k.
\tag{12}
\]

Every parity class is determined by the support of \(z\). The central
class is the singleton \(\{0\}\). In every other class, choose one
representative from each antipodal pair \(\{z,-z\}\) and set

\[
 p_{\{z,-z\}}:=\frac{a_z}{2}.
\tag{13}
\]

All other edge weights are zero. Every noncentral vertex then has degree
\(a_z/2\), while the central vertex has degree zero. Thus every available
endpoint capacity in each noncentral parity class is saturated. The mass
of any matching in a parity class is at most half the sum of its endpoint
capacities, so saturation attains that upper bound. No edge is available
in the central singleton class. Hence (13) is a maximum free matching. Its
selected profile is

\[
 t_z=a_z\quad(z\neq0),\qquad t_0=0.
\tag{14}
\]

In particular,

\[
 4|p|=\sum_z t_z=W-a_0,\qquad a_0=v_q^k.
\]

The selected weights are centrally symmetric. Every closed base
halfspace containing \(0\) contains at least one member of every antipodal
pair and hence at least half of the selected mass. Since
\(1/2\geq2^{-k}\), the point \(m=0\) is admissible for (14).

Let \(u=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb R^q\) and

\[
 w:=(u,\ldots,u)\in\mathbb R^{kq}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(z\neq0\), the full-mass metronoid is the centroid singleton. Its
support values in the two directions are

\[
 h_z(w)=b_q\bigl(k-(1-r)s(z)\bigr),\qquad
 h_z(-w)=-b_q\bigl(k-(1-r)s(z)\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]

In the notation of accepted fact 07009c5047a9f7bb, minimizing over
\(\Lambda_0(Z\setminus\{0\})\) gives

\[
 \phi_t(w)=krb_q.
\tag{17}
\]

Indeed, every value in the first part of (16) is at least \(krb_q\), and
equality is attained by the representation assigning weight \(1/2\) to
each point of an antipodal pair with all \(k\) coordinates nonzero.
Similarly,

\[
 \phi_t(-w)=-(k-1+r)b_q,
\tag{18}
\]

because the smallest value in the second part of (16) occurs when
\(s(z)=1\), and an antipodal pair of that type again represents \(0\).
Consequently,

\[
 \frac12\phi_t(w)+\frac12\phi_t(-w)
 =-\frac{(k-1)(1-r)b_q}{2}<0.
\tag{19}
\]

The two nonzero directions \(w,-w\), with weights \(1/2,1/2\), form a
balanced negative family. It is support-minimal because no positive
balanced family of nonzero directions can have one member. The constant
affine functions

\[
 \ell_+(z):=krb_q,\qquad
 \ell_-(z):=-(k-1+r)b_q
\tag{20}
\]

are minorants of the respective support values in (16), and (17)-(18)
show that they are exact at \(m=0\). Thus (19)-(20) are legal circuit data
for (14), prove that the core (6) is empty, and make the individual cap

\[
 A_+:=C_{k,q,r}\cap
 \left\{(z,x):
 \sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{\nu=1}^q x_{j,\nu}\geq krb_q\right\}
\tag{21}
\]

an actual member of \(\mathscr D(C_{k,q,r})\). This verifies matching,
circuit, minorant, and individual-cap legality directly. Also
\(krb_q<k\), so a base point near \(0\) and \(k\) interior simplex points
whose coordinate sums lie strictly between \(rb_q\) and \(1\) give a
strict interior point of (21). Thus this legal cap is full-dimensional.

We next prove a uniform concentration bound for this cap. Normalize the
mixed measure of one block to a probability measure. Its random scale
\(S\in\{1,r\}\) has law

\[
 \mathbb P(S=1)=\frac1{1+2r^q},\qquad
 \mathbb P(S=r)=\frac{2r^q}{1+2r^q}.
\tag{22}
\]

Conditional on the scale, let \(X^{(q)}\) be uniform on \(\Delta_q\), and
put

\[
 V:=\sum_{\nu=1}^qX^{(q)}_\nu,\qquad T:=SV.
\]

Scaling the simplex gives

\[
 \mathbb P(V\leq v)=v^q\quad(0\leq v\leq1),\qquad
 \mathbb EV=b_q.
\tag{23}
\]

Writing \(\delta:=1-r\), the mean gap above the threshold in (21) is

\[
 \mu-rb_q
 :=\mathbb ET-rb_q
 =b_q\frac{\delta}{1+2r^q}
 \geq\frac{\delta}{6}.
\tag{24}
\]

The following exponential-moment estimate is uniform in \(q\) and \(r\).
Put

\[
 X:=q(1-V),\qquad \xi:=q\delta.
\]

Equation (23) gives, for \(0\leq y\leq q\),

\[
 \mathbb P(X\geq y)
 =\left(1-\frac yq\right)^q\leq e^{-y},
\tag{25}
\]

and the probability is zero for \(y>q\). Hence

\[
 \mathbb E e^{X/2}
 =1+\frac12\int_0^\infty e^{y/2}\mathbb P(X>y)\,dy
 \leq2,\qquad
 \mathbb EX\leq1.
\tag{26}
\]

Since

\[
 0\leq q(1-T)=q(1-S)+SX\leq\xi+X,
\]

the centered variable

\[
 Z:=q\bigl((1-T)-\mathbb E(1-T)\bigr)
\]

satisfies

\[
 |Z|\leq2\xi+X+1.
\tag{27}
\]

With \(K:=8(1+\xi)\), equations (26)-(27), followed by Jensen's
inequality, give

\[
 \mathbb E\exp\left(\frac{|Z|}{K}\right)
 \leq e^{1/4}\mathbb E e^{X/8}
 \leq e^{1/4}\bigl(\mathbb Ee^{X/2}\bigr)^{1/4}<2.
\tag{28}
\]

For completeness, (28) implies a Bernstein bound without any unstated
parameter dependence. It gives
\(\mathbb E|Z|^m\leq2m!K^m\) for every \(m\geq2\). Since
\(\mathbb EZ=0\), the power series yields

\[
 \mathbb Ee^{\lambda Z}\leq e^{3\lambda^2K^2}
 \qquad(|\lambda|\leq(4K)^{-1}).
\tag{29}
\]

Applying Chernoff's argument to independent copies and choosing
\(\lambda=\min\{t/(6kK^2),(4K)^{-1}\}\) gives an absolute \(c>0\) such
that

\[
 \mathbb P\left(\sum_{j=1}^k Z_j\geq t\right)
 \leq
 \exp\left[-c\min\left\{\frac{t^2}{kK^2},\frac tK\right\}\right].
\tag{30}
\]

If \(\sum_jT_j<krb_q\), then (24) gives

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^kZ_j
 =q\sum_{j=1}^k(\mu-T_j)
 >kq(\mu-rb_q)\geq\frac{k\xi}{6}.
\]

Because \(K=8(1+\xi)\), equation (30) implies, for an absolute
\(c_1>0\),

\[
 \mathbb P\left(\sum_{j=1}^kT_j<krb_q\right)
 \leq
 \exp\left[-c_1k\min\{\xi^2,1\}\right].
\tag{31}
\]

The normalized mixed measure on the Cartesian product is exactly the law
of the independent blocks above. Thus (21) and (31) give

\[
 1-\frac{W(A_+)}W
 \leq
 \exp\left[-c_1k\min\{q^2(1-r)^2,1\}\right].
\tag{32}
\]

Since \(A_+\in\mathscr D(C_{k,q,r})\),

\[
 \boxed{
 1-\frac{R_{\mathrm{desc}}}{W}
 \leq
 \exp\left[-c_1k\min\{q^2(1-r)^2,1\}\right].}
\tag{33}
\]

We now prove the repaired type-class lemma.

**Permutation-invariant Hamming lemma.** There are absolute constants
\(c_2\in(0,1]\) and \(C_2<\infty\) with the following property. For every
\(m\geq1\) and every \(\eta\in[0,1/4]\), the uniform probability measure
\(\mu_m\) on \(\{0,1\}^m\) has a submeasure \(\nu\leq\mu_m\) such that

\[
 \nu(\{0,1\}^m)\geq c_2e^{-C_2m\eta^2},
\tag{34}
\]

the density \(d\nu/d\mu_m\) depends only on Hamming weight, and, for every
coordinate \(i\),

\[
 \nu\{\varepsilon:\varepsilon_i=1\}
 =\left(\frac12-\eta\right)\nu(\{0,1\}^m).
\tag{35}
\]

To prove the lemma, let

\[
 P_j:=2^{-m}\binom mj
 =\mu_m\{\varepsilon:|\varepsilon|=j\}.
\]

First fix an integer \(h\) satisfying
\(\lvert h-m(1/2-\eta)\rvert\leq1\). For all sufficiently large \(m\), put
\(L=\lfloor\sqrt m/32\rfloor\). For \(1\leq s\leq L\), set

\[
 b_s:=\min\{P_{h-s},P_{h+s}\}.
\]

On the full layer \(|\varepsilon|=h-s\), take the constant density
\(b_s/P_{h-s}\); on the full layer \(|\varepsilon|=h+s\), take the
constant density \(b_s/P_{h+s}\); and on the full central layer
\(|\varepsilon|=h\), take density one. The chosen layers are disjoint and
all densities are at most one. The resulting submeasure \(\nu_h\) is
permutation-invariant, has mass

\[
 M_h=P_h+2\sum_{s=1}^L b_s,
\tag{36}
\]

and has total Hamming first moment exactly

\[
 \int|\varepsilon|\,d\nu_h
 =hP_h+\sum_{s=1}^Lb_s\bigl((h-s)+(h+s)\bigr)
 =hM_h.
\tag{37}
\]

We record the mass estimate. The binomial mode has mass at least
\(c/\sqrt m\): Chebyshev's inequality places at least \(3/4\) of the
binomial mass in \(O(\sqrt m)\) layers around \(m/2\), and the mode is the
largest layer. The exact consecutive-layer ratios, summed from the mode,
give

\[
 P_j\geq\frac c{\sqrt m}
 \exp\left[-C\frac{(j-m/2)^2}{m}\right]
 \quad\text{whenever }|j-m/2|\leq m/3.
\tag{38}
\]

For example, (38) follows by taking logarithms of the ratios
\(P_{j+1}/P_j=(m-j)/(j+1)\) and using
\(\log(1-x)\geq-2x\) in this range. If \(h\) is either the floor or the
ceiling of \(m(1/2-\eta)\), then, for all sufficiently large \(m\), all
indices \(h\pm s\), \(0\leq s\leq L\), are valid and satisfy

\[
 \frac{(h\pm s-m/2)^2}{m}\leq C(m\eta^2+1).
\tag{39}
\]

Equations (36), (38), and (39), together with
\(L+1\geq c\sqrt m\), imply

\[
 M_h\geq c e^{-Cm\eta^2}.
\tag{40}
\]

The finitely many smaller values of \(m\) are handled by taking the full
single layers at the floor and ceiling; their masses are bounded below by
an absolute positive constant after the finite threshold is fixed. This
only changes the constants in (40).

Now put \(\theta=m(1/2-\eta)\). If \(\theta\) is an integer, use
\(\nu_\theta\). Otherwise let \(h_0=\lfloor\theta\rfloor\),
\(h_1=\lceil\theta\rceil\), and write \(a=\theta-h_0\in(0,1)\). Construct
\(\nu_{h_0}\) and \(\nu_{h_1}\) above. Scale each down to the common mass

\[
 M:=\min\{M_{h_0},M_{h_1}\}
\]

and call the scaled measures
\(\bar\nu_{h_0},\bar\nu_{h_1}\). Then

\[
 \nu:=(1-a)\bar\nu_{h_0}+a\bar\nu_{h_1}
\tag{41}
\]

still has density at most one, is permutation-invariant, has mass \(M\),
and has total Hamming first moment

\[
 ((1-a)h_0+ah_1)M=\theta M.
\tag{42}
\]

Permutation invariance makes the \(m\) coordinate marginals equal. Their
sum is (42), so each coordinate marginal is exactly
\(\theta M/m=(1/2-\eta)M\). Equations (40)-(42) prove (34)-(35). This
floor/ceiling interpolation also applies to the finite-\(m\) single-layer
construction and completes the lemma.

We apply the lemma to the critical window. Put

\[
 \rho_q:=2^{-1/q},\qquad
 \alpha_0:=\frac{\log2}{4},
\tag{43}
\]

and suppose

\[
 q(1-r)\leq\alpha_0.
\tag{44}
\]

The inner simplex \(I:=\rho_q\Delta_q\) and the shell
\(O:=\Delta_q\setminus I\) each have volume \(v_q/2\). Their centroids are

\[
 \rho_qc_q
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (2-\rho_q)c_q,
\tag{45}
\]

respectively. The second identity follows by subtracting the first moment
of the half-volume inner simplex from the first moment of \(\Delta_q\).

Since \(\rho_q=e^{-(\log2)/q}\) and
\(1-e^{-x}\geq x/2\) for \(0\leq x\leq\log2\),

\[
 1-\rho_q\geq\frac{\log2}{2q}.
\tag{46}
\]

Define

\[
 \eta:=\frac{1-r}{2(1-\rho_q)},\qquad
 p:=\frac12-\eta.
\tag{47}
\]

Equations (44) and (46) give \(0\leq\eta\leq1/4\). Moreover,

\[
 (1-p)\rho_qc_q+p(2-\rho_q)c_q
 =\bigl(\rho_q+2p(1-\rho_q)\bigr)c_q
 =rc_q.
\tag{48}
\]

Fix a lattice fiber \(C_z\), and let

\[
 m(z):=|\{j:z_j=0\}|
\]

be the number of its unscaled simplex factors. We treat the case
\(m(z)=0\) separately below. Suppose first that \(m(z)\geq1\). In every
unscaled factor split \(\Delta_q\) into \(I\) and \(O\), and encode a
product cell by \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^{m(z)}\), with
\(\varepsilon_i=1\) denoting the outer shell. All \(2^{m(z)}\) cells have
equal volume. On each full product cell, use the constant density supplied
by the Hamming lemma. Use density one in every scaled factor. This gives a
submeasure of the fiber with relative mass \(M_{m(z)}\) satisfying

\[
 M_{m(z)}\geq c_2e^{-C_2m(z)\eta^2}.
\tag{49}
\]

The density is constant on every full Hamming layer. In particular it is
permutation-invariant, and (35) says that the outer-shell marginal of each
individual unscaled factor is exactly \(p\), not merely that the total
mean number of outer shells is \(m(z)p\). Equations (45) and (48)
therefore show that the selected barycenter in every unscaled factor is
exactly \(rc_q\). Every scaled factor already has centroid \(rc_q\).

By (46),

\[
 \eta\leq\frac{q(1-r)}{\log2}.
\]

After enlarging the absolute constant, (49) gives, uniformly over all
fibers with \(m(z)\geq1\),

\[
 M_{m(z)}\geq
 c_2\exp\left[-C_3kq^2(1-r)^2\right].
\tag{50}
\]

Set

\[
 \tau:=c_2\exp\left[-C_3kq^2(1-r)^2\right],
\tag{51}
\]

with \(c_2\leq1\). In every fiber with \(m(z)\geq1\), multiply the density
just constructed by \(\tau/M_{m(z)}\leq1\). This preserves every
normalized barycenter and makes the selected relative mass exactly
\(\tau\).

If \(m(z)=0\), there is no Hamming cube and no type-class lemma to apply.
All \(k\) factors of the fiber are scaled simplices and already have
barycenter \(rc_q\). On this fiber use the constant density \(\tau\).
Thus the zero-unscaled-factor case also has relative mass \(\tau\) and the
same barycenter.

Consequently, for every \(z\in Z\), there is a density bounded by one on
\(C_z\), of mass

\[
 t_z:=\tau a_z>0,
\tag{52}
\]

whose normalized barycenter is the common vector

\[
 c_*:=(rc_q,\ldots,rc_q)\in\mathbb R^{kq}.
\tag{53}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 c_*\in\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\tag{54}
\]

The selected base weights in (52) are centrally symmetric. Every proper
closed base halfspace containing \(0\) therefore contains at least half
of their total mass, which is at least the required \(2^{-k}\) fraction.
For every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_0(Z)\), equation (54) gives

\[
 c_*=\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_zc_*
 \in\sum_{z\in Z}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\]

Thus \(c_*\) belongs to the complete core for every barycentric
representation of \(0\). The tuple (52)-(54) is admissible in the capacity
defining \(G\), with total mass \(\tau W\). Hence (51) proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac GW\geq
 c_2\exp\left[-C_3kq^2(1-r)^2\right]}
 \qquad\text{when }q(1-r)\leq\alpha_0.
\tag{55}
\]

This is only a certified feasible complete-core mass. No assertion that
the supremum defining \(G\) is attained or has been evaluated is used.

It remains to eliminate \(k,q,r\). Put

\[
 g:=\frac GW,\qquad
 L:=1-\frac{R_{\mathrm{desc}}}{W},\qquad
 \xi:=q(1-r).
\tag{56}
\]

The full-mass centroid singleton in any positive fiber gives \(G>0\),
while the fiber-capacity constraints in the definition give \(G\leq W\).
Every body in \(\mathscr D(C_{k,q,r})\) is contained in \(C_{k,q,r}\).
Thus \(0<g\leq1\) and \(0\leq L\leq1\).

First suppose \(\xi\leq\alpha_0\). Since \(\alpha_0<1\), equations (33)
and (55) give

\[
 L\leq e^{-c_1k\xi^2},\qquad
 g\geq c_2e^{-C_3k\xi^2}.
\]

With

\[
 \beta_1:=\frac{c_1}{C_3}>0,
\]

it follows that

\[
 L\leq c_2^{-\beta_1}g^{\beta_1}.
\tag{57}
\]

Now suppose \(\xi>\alpha_0\). If \(g\geq1/2\), then the trivial bound
\(L\leq1\) already has the required Hölder form. Assume \(g<1/2\). The
central fiber has mass

\[
 a_0=v_q^k.
\]

Apply the singleton affine-subspace lower bound from accepted fact
3d1f63abf09b2abb to \(A=\{0\}\). Its relative complete-core capacity is
\(a_0\), realized by the full-mass centroid singleton, so that accepted
bound gives

\[
 G\geq\min\left\{\frac{W+a_0}{2},\,2^ka_0\right\}.
\tag{58}
\]

The first entry in (58) is strictly larger than \(W/2>G\). Therefore the
second entry is at most \(G\), and (12) gives

\[
 g\geq\frac{2^ka_0}{W}
 =\left(\frac2{1+2r^q}\right)^k.
\tag{59}
\]

Since \(\log r\leq-(1-r)\),

\[
 r^q\leq e^{-q(1-r)}<e^{-\alpha_0}.
\]

Put

\[
 b_0:=\frac2{1+2e^{-\alpha_0}}\in(0,1).
\tag{60}
\]

Equations (59)-(60) give \(g\geq b_0^k\). On the other hand, (33) and
\(\xi>\alpha_0\) give

\[
 L\leq e^{-c_1\alpha_0^2k}.
\]

Define

\[
 \beta_0:=\frac{c_1\alpha_0^2}{\log(1/b_0)}>0.
\]

Then

\[
 L\leq b_0^{\beta_0k}\leq g^{\beta_0}.
\tag{61}
\]

Finally let

\[
 \beta:=\min\{\beta_0,\beta_1\}>0,\qquad
 A:=\max\{c_2^{-\beta_1},2^\beta\}.
\]

Because \(0<g\leq1\), lowering an exponent only enlarges \(g\) to that
power. Equation (57) handles the critical window, (61) handles the
large-\(\xi\) window with \(g<1/2\), and, when \(g\geq1/2\),
\(1\leq2^\beta g^\beta\). These three cases prove (8) with absolute
constants.

For clarity about (9), accepted fact 5967de7b4fedb5fd studies adaptive
iterations of half-mass balanced-cap descendants of the single interval
block \(C_L=K_{1,r_L}\). It does not itself make a claim about Cartesian
powers or about the one-step capacity (7). The present theorem says that
the \(k\)-fold powers of those initial blocks, and more generally all the
simplex-block powers (3), satisfy the Hölder retention estimate (8).
