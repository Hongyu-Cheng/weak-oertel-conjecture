## Statement

Let \(n,d\ge1\) be integers and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Retain exactly the actual-body notation of accepted facts 03f071403cdfc69f and d887d95075dfcc06:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_z&=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},&
w_z&=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\\
S&=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),&
W&=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\mathcal H_d(S)>0,\\
T&=2^{-n}W,&
h_S(p)&=\inf_{H\ni p}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. Empty and lower-dimensional fibers have zero volume.

Suppose there are positive real numbers \(b_1,\ldots,b_n\) and a finite-valued nonincreasing function \(g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with \(g(0)>0\), such that
\[
w_z=
\begin{cases}
g(b\cdot z),&z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n,\\
0,&z\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n,
\end{cases}
\qquad b\cdot z=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i z_i.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume global log-concavity with the zero convention: for \(r,s\ge0\) and \(0<\theta<1\),
\(g((1-\theta)r+\theta s)\ge g(r)^{1-\theta}g(s)^\theta\), where a positive power of zero is zero. No continuity assumption on \(g\) is imposed. Equation (1) specifies the entire integer table, including its zeros. Its positive support \(D=\{z:w_z>0\}\) is finite by compactness of \(C\).

For each \(j\in D\) and finite \(t\ge0\), define
\[
F_j(t)=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ w_{j+u}\ge e^{-t}w_j\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Coordinate inequalities are componentwise. This family is exactly the local set \(F_t\) in the actual-body part of d887d95075dfcc06, at its fixed center \(j\): at integer forward displacements its potential is \(-\log(w_{j+u}/w_j)\), with value \(+\infty\) when \(w_{j+u}=0\). The notation \(F_j(t)\) denotes this set-valued family, not the unparameterized all-pair scalar \(F_j\) discussed in 03f071403cdfc69f.

At every fixed positive center retain the same actual, oriented quantities as in both accepted facts:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha(v)&=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\},&
\mathcal U_j&=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:\alpha(v)>0\},\\
\Delta_j(v)&=\log\frac{w_j^2}{w_{j+v}w_{j-v}}
&& (v\in\mathcal U_j),\\
B_j^\Delta(L)&=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal U_j\\\Delta_j(v)\le L}}\alpha(v)
&& (L\ge0),\\
K_j(w)&=\max_{L\ge0}\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L},&
K(w)&=\max_{j\in D}K_j(w).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The center dependence of \(\alpha\) is suppressed exactly as in those predecessors. Every displacement sum includes zero and both orientations. A difference set contains each distinct difference once.

Then the following maximum is attained and satisfies
\[
\max_{\substack{j\in D\\t\ge0}}
\frac{w_j e^{-t}|F_j(t)-F_j(t)|}{1+2t}
\ge e^{-33}T,
\qquad K(w)\ge e^{-33}T.
\tag{4}
\]
There are a center \(j\in D\), a finite threshold \(t\ge0\), and one \(x^\star\in C_j\), all selected before the ambient halfspace, such that
\[
h_S((j,x^\star))
\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(2t)}{12(1+2t)}
\ge\frac{e^{-33}}{12}T
=\frac{e^{-33}}{12\,2^n}\mathcal H_d(S).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus (5) holds simultaneously for every closed ambient halfspace containing this one point, including vertical halfspaces and boundaries containing whole fibers. All actual fiber shapes satisfying the hypotheses are included.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. First prove an auxiliary counting estimate, independently of the actual body. Let \(b_1,\ldots,b_n>0\) be arbitrary real numbers and let \(R\ge0\) be any finite real number. In this auxiliary argument put
\[
\mathcal D=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le R\},
\qquad
A_j=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ b\cdot(j+u)\le R\}
\quad(j\in\mathcal D).
\tag{6}
\]
Define the numerical coefficient
\(Q_n=\tfrac12(11/10)^n\exp(-7/3-(2\sqrt2/3)\sqrt n)\).
We will prove that some \(j\in\mathcal D\) satisfies
\[
|A_j-A_j|\ge Q_n\,2^{-n}|\mathcal D|.
\tag{7}
\]
The auxiliary set \(\mathcal D\) will later be instantiated as one threshold prefix of the actual table; the vectors \(u\) in \(A_j\) are forward displacements.

For a vector \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(v_i^+=\max(v_i,0)\) and \(v_i^-=\max(-v_i,0)\). At a fixed \(j\in\mathcal D\) put \(r=R-b\cdot j\). The exact description of its difference set is
\[
A_j-A_j
=\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:|v_i|\le j_i\text{ for all }i,\
b\cdot v^+\le r,\ b\cdot v^-\le r\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, if \(v=u-a\) for \(u,a\in A_j\), then \(v^+\le u\) and \(v^-\le a\), which proves all the conditions on the right. Conversely, those conditions put both \(v^+\) and \(v^-\) in \(A_j\), and \(v=v^+-v^-\). This proves (8) for distinct differences, regardless of how many other representing pairs they have.

For \(s>0\), set \(\mathcal M(s)=\max_{k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}}(2k+1)e^{-sk}\); the maximum exists because this sequence tends to zero. The needed one-coordinate estimate is
\[
2(1-e^{-3s/4})\mathcal M(s)>\frac{11}{10}
\qquad(s>0).
\tag{9}
\]
Here are elementary bounds on the constants. The exponential series gives \(e>8/3\). Its terms through \(1/4!\) sum to \(65/24\); its tail beginning at \(1/5!\) is less than the geometric sum \((1/120)/(1-1/6)=1/100\). Thus
\(e<1631/600<30/11<11/4\).
Consequently \(3/e>11/10\), \(e^{2/3}<2\), and \(e^{1/2}>8/5\): the latter two follow from \((11/4)^2<8\) and \(8/3>(8/5)^2\).

Suppose \(0<s\le2/3\). Choose an integer \(k\) nearest to \(1/s-1/2\), and write \(\delta=k-(1/s-1/2)\). Then \(k\ge1\), \(|\delta|\le1/2\), and
\(\mathcal M(s)\ge(2/s)e^{s/2-1}(1+s\delta)e^{-s\delta}\).
For \(|x|<1\), integrating \(-u/(1+u)\) from zero to \(x\) gives
\(\log(1+x)-x\ge-x^2/[2(1-|x|)]\).
For \(a>0\), pairing the integrands at \(q\) and \(1-q\) and applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\((1-e^{-a})/a=\int_0^1e^{-aq}\,dq\ge e^{-a/2}\).
Apply these inequalities with \(x=s\delta\) and \(a=3s/4\). They yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
2(1-e^{-3s/4})\mathcal M(s)
&\ge\frac3e
\exp\left(\frac s8-\frac{s^2}{8(1-s/2)}\right)\\
&\ge\frac3e>\frac{11}{10}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The exponent is nonnegative because \(s\le2/3\).

For \(2/3\le s\le\log3\), use the integer \(k=1\). The logarithm of \(6e^{-s}(1-e^{-3s/4})\) is concave: its second derivative is
\(-(9/16)e^{3s/4}/(e^{3s/4}-1)^2<0\).
It is therefore bounded below by the smaller of its two endpoint values. At \(s=2/3\), the expression before taking the logarithm exceeds
\(6(1/2)(3/8)=9/8>11/10\).
At \(s=\log3\), it equals \(2(1-3^{-3/4})>11/10\); the last inequality is equivalent to \(160000<177147\) after raising the positive quantities \(3^{-3/4}\) and \(9/20\) to the fourth power. The interval is nonempty since \(e^{2/3}<2<3\).
For \(s\ge\log3\), use \(k=0\) to get the same lower bound \(2(1-3^{-3/4})>11/10\). This proves (9), including all transition values. Every maximizing integer has the same value \(\mathcal M(s)\), so the estimate applies to either maximizer at a tie.

The maximizing integers have an exact description. The successive ratios
\(e^{-s}(2k+3)/(2k+1)\) strictly decrease with \(k\). Thus zero is maximizing exactly when \(e^{-s}\le1/3\), and \(k\ge1\) is maximizing exactly when
\[
\frac{2k-1}{2k+1}\le e^{-s}\le\frac{2k+1}{2k+3}.
\tag{11}
\]
At \(e^{-s}=(2k-1)/(2k+1)\), the only maximizers are \(k-1,k\); elsewhere the maximizer is unique. For every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
k\log\frac{2k+1}{2k-1}
&=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{2}{1-(q/k)^2}\,dq\\
&\le\int_0^{1/2}\frac{2}{1-q^2}\,dq
=\log3<\frac43.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The last strict inequality follows from
\(e^{4/3}>1+4/3+(4/3)^2/2=29/9>3\).

Let \(V\) be uniform on the integers \(-k,\ldots,k\), with \(k\ge0\). Summing the positive values and their squares gives
\[
\mathbb E V^+=\frac{k(k+1)}{2(2k+1)}\le\frac k3,
\qquad
\eta_k:=\operatorname{Var}(V^+)
=\frac{k(k+1)}6-\frac{k^2(k+1)^2}{4(2k+1)^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
For the mean bound, the case \(k=0\) is immediate, and for \(k\ge1\) it is equivalent to \(3(k+1)\le2(2k+1)\). In particular \(\eta_0=0\).
The variance formula can also be written
\(\eta_k=5(2k+1)^2/192-1/96-1/[64(2k+1)^2]\).
Subtracting this expression at \(k-1\) gives
\[
0<\eta_k-\eta_{k-1}
=\frac{5k}{24}+\frac{k}{8(4k^2-1)^2}
\le\frac{2k}{9}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{14}
\]
For the upper bound, \((4k^2-1)^2\ge9\) gives
\(5/24+1/[8(4k^2-1)^2]\le5/24+1/72=2/9\).
Summing (14) yields
\(\eta_k\le k(k+1)/9\le2k^2/9\) for \(k\ge1\).

For \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), set
\[
\sigma_+(j)^2=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i^2\eta_{j_i},
\qquad
\Phi(j)=\frac43 b\cdot j+2\sigma_+(j).
\tag{15}
\]
Here \(\sigma_+(j)\) is the nonnegative square root. Its square is the variance of the positive-part sum. Equation (14) shows that \(\Phi\) increases when any coordinate increases.

We next select a finite \(\lambda>0\) and a vector \(j\) for which each \(j_i\) maximizes \((2k+1)e^{-(4/3)\lambda b_i k}\), and
\[
\Phi(j)\le R,
\qquad \lambda(R-\Phi(j))<\frac73.
\tag{16}
\]
For every \(\lambda>0\), initially choose the smallest maximizing integer in each coordinate. By (11), these integers are nonincreasing with \(\lambda\), and each tends to infinity as \(\lambda\) decreases to zero. To justify the latter assertion directly, any fixed nonnegative integer upper bound \(N\) on a maximizing coordinate would imply
\(e^{-(4/3)\lambda b_i}\le(2N+1)/(2N+3)<1\), which fails for sufficiently small \(\lambda\).
Thus choose a finite \(\delta>0\) for which the resulting vector has \(\Phi>R\). Choose a finite \(\lambda_+>\delta\) large enough that
\(e^{-(4/3)\lambda_+b_i}<1/3\) for every \(i\). Its maximizing vector is zero and has \(\Phi=0\le R\).

There are only finitely many coordinate decrements between these parameters: each coordinate starts at a finite integer and only decreases. A decrement from \(k\) to \(k-1\) occurs at
\(e^{-(4/3)\lambda b_i}=(2k-1)/(2k+1)\).
At a parameter shared by several coordinates, perform their decrements one at a time in any order. Both choices in each changing coordinate are maximizing at this same parameter, so every intermediate vector still consists entirely of maximizing integers. Since \(\Phi\) decreases at each decrement, there is a first decrement across which its value passes from greater than \(R\) to at most \(R\).
Let \(\lambda\) be this parameter and \(j\) the vector after that decrement. The preceding vector is \(j+e_i\) for some coordinate \(i\), where \(e_i\) is its unit vector and \(k=j_i+1\ge1\).

At this decrement, (12) gives
\(\lambda b_i k=(3/4)k\log((2k+1)/(2k-1))<1\).
Using (14) and \(\sqrt{x+y}-\sqrt x\le\sqrt y\) for \(x,y\ge0\), obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda\bigl(\Phi(j+e_i)-\Phi(j)\bigr)
&\le\lambda b_i\left(\frac43+2\sqrt{\frac{2k}{9}}\right)\\
&<\frac4{3k}+\frac{2\sqrt2}{3\sqrt k}\\
&\le\frac{4+2\sqrt2}{3}<\frac73.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Since \(\Phi(j)\le R<\Phi(j+e_i)\), this proves (16). In particular \(b\cdot j\le R\), so the selected vector belongs to \(\mathcal D\). No positivity assumption on \(R\) entered this construction. When \(R=0\), (16) and positive \(b_i\) force \(j=0\); the variance-zero case below applies.

For any positive selected coordinate \(j_i\), its maximizing condition in (11), together with (12), also gives \(\lambda b_i j_i<1\). Hence
\[
\lambda^2\sigma_+(j)^2
\le\frac29\sum_{i:j_i>0}(\lambda b_i j_i)^2
\le\frac{2n}{9}.
\tag{18}
\]
This bound also holds when all coordinates vanish.

Fix this selected center. Let \(V_i\), independently for different coordinates, be uniform on \(-j_i,\ldots,j_i\). Put \(P_+=\sum_i b_iV_i^+\), \(P_-=\sum_i b_iV_i^-\), and
\(\mu=\sum_i b_i j_i(j_i+1)/[2(2j_i+1)]\).
Reflection gives \(V_i^-\) the same law as \(V_i^+\). Thus the two sums each have mean \(\mu\le b\cdot j/3\) and variance \(\sigma_+(j)^2\), by (13) and independence across coordinates. The variables \(P_+\) and \(P_-\) need not be independent. From (16),
\[
R-b\cdot j\ge\frac13 b\cdot j+2\sigma_+(j)
\ge\mu+2\sigma_+(j).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(\sigma_+(j)>0\), for either sum \(P\) the second-moment bound gives
\(\mathbb P\{P>\mu+2\sigma_+(j)\}\le
\mathbb E(P-\mu)^2/[4\sigma_+(j)^2]=1/4\).
A union bound and (19) therefore imply
\(\mathbb P\{P_+\le R-b\cdot j,\ P_-\le R-b\cdot j\}\ge1/2\).
If \(\sigma_+(j)=0\), (14) and positive \(b_i\) force \(j=0\); both sums are identically zero and the same event has probability one.

The random vector \(V=(V_1,\ldots,V_n)\) is uniform on a box of exactly \(\prod_i(2j_i+1)\) distinct integer vectors. By (8), the event just bounded is precisely membership in \(A_j-A_j\). Consequently
\[
|A_j-A_j|\ge\frac12\prod_i(2j_i+1).
\tag{20}
\]
This argument controls the positive and negative weighted sums separately and counts each resulting displacement only once.

Positive coefficients and a finite budget make \(\mathcal D\) finite and nonempty. For \(z\in\mathcal D\), one has \(1\le e^{\lambda R}e^{-\lambda b\cdot z}\). Summing and extending the nonnegative sum to the full integer orthant gives
\[
|\mathcal D|
\le e^{\lambda R}\prod_i(1-e^{-\lambda b_i})^{-1}.
\tag{21}
\]
At each selected coordinate use (9) with \(s=(4/3)\lambda b_i\) and the fact that \(j_i\) is maximizing. Thus
\(2(1-e^{-\lambda b_i})(2j_i+1)e^{-(4/3)\lambda b_i j_i}>11/10\).
Together with (16), (18), (20), and (21), this gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|A_j-A_j|}{2^{-n}|\mathcal D|}
&\ge\frac12 e^{-\lambda(R-(4/3)b\cdot j)}
\prod_i\left[2(1-e^{-\lambda b_i})(2j_i+1)
e^{-(4/3)\lambda b_i j_i}\right]\\
&\ge\frac12\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^n
\exp\left(-\frac73-2\lambda\sigma_+(j)\right)\\
&\ge\frac12\left(\frac{11}{10}\right)^n
\exp\left(-\frac73-\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\sqrt n\right)
=Q_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
In the second line, \(R-(4/3)b\cdot j=R-\Phi(j)+2\sigma_+(j)\).
This proves (7) for all positive real \(b_i\) and every \(R\ge0\), including arbitrarily small budgets, zero budget, and simultaneous coordinate ties.

Return to the original actual table. Equation (1) and monotonicity of \(g\) give \(w_u\ge w_v\) whenever \(0\le u\le v\) are integer vectors, including zeros. Its maximum is \(M:=g(0)=w_0>0\). For a positive site \(z\), coordinatewise rounding down gives \(b\cdot\lfloor z/2\rfloor\le(b\cdot z)/2\). Monotonicity followed by log-concavity between zero and \(b\cdot z\) yields
\[
w_{\lfloor z/2\rfloor}
=g(b\cdot\lfloor z/2\rfloor)
\ge g((b\cdot z)/2)
\ge\sqrt{Mw_z}.
\tag{23}
\]
Each integer image under \(z\mapsto\lfloor z/2\rfloor\) has at most \(2^n\) preimages in the nonnegative orthant, namely \(2u+\varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n\). The positive support is finite, so summation gives
\[
\sum_{z\in D}\sqrt{Mw_z}\le2^n W.
\tag{24}
\]
Only original weights occur in this estimate.

Choose the integer \(J=\lceil2(n+1)\log2\rceil\). Since \(0<\log2<1\), it satisfies \(1\le J\le2(n+1)\). At a site with \(0<w_z\le Me^{-J}\),
\(w_z\le e^{-J/2}\sqrt{Mw_z}\).
Therefore (24) implies
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in D\\w_z\le Me^{-J}}}w_z
\le e^{-J/2}2^n W\le\frac W2.
\tag{25}
\]
The \(J\) bands
\(E_k=\{z\in D:Me^{-(k+1)}<w_z\le Me^{-k}\}\),
for \(k=0,\ldots,J-1\), form a disjoint partition of all sites with \(w_z>Me^{-J}\). The strict lower and weak upper endpoints assign every boundary weight exactly once. By (25), one band has mass at least \(W/(2J)\). Fix such a \(k\) and set \(t=k+1\), so \(1\le t\le J\).

Define the actual threshold prefix
\(D_t=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z\ge Me^{-t}\}\).
It is finite, nonempty, contained in the nonnegative orthant, and contains the selected band. Each weight in that band is at most \(eMe^{-t}\). Hence
\[
Me^{-t}|D_t|\ge\frac{W}{2eJ}.
\tag{26}
\]
There are no zero-weight sites in \(D_t\).

Put \(R=\max_{z\in D_t}b\cdot z\), which is a finite attained nonnegative real number. Then
\[
D_t=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:b\cdot z\le R\}.
\tag{27}
\]
One inclusion follows from the definition of \(R\). For the other, choose \(z_0\in D_t\) with \(b\cdot z_0=R\). For every nonnegative integer \(z\) with \(b\cdot z\le R\), monotonicity gives
\(w_z=g(b\cdot z)\ge g(R)=w_{z_0}\ge Me^{-t}\).
This proves (27) without continuity of \(g\), rationality of the \(b_i\), or an assumption that a real superlevel endpoint belongs to the support. If \(R=0\), positive \(b_i\) make \(D_t=\{0\}\), a case already included in (7).

Apply (7) to the knapsack (27), using exactly these coefficients and this attained radius. It supplies \(j\in D_t\) such that, with
\(A_j=\{u\in\mathbb Z^n:0\le u\le j,\ j+u\in D_t\}\),
one has \(|A_j-A_j|\ge Q_n2^{-n}|D_t|\).
Set \(\tau=\log(w_j/(Me^{-t}))\). Since \(Me^{-t}\le w_j\le M\), this is finite and satisfies \(0\le\tau\le t\le J\). The exact threshold identity \(w_j e^{-\tau}=Me^{-t}\) gives
\(A_j=F_j(\tau)\), including the sites on the threshold boundary. Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{w_j e^{-\tau}|F_j(\tau)-F_j(\tau)|}{1+2\tau}
&=\frac{Me^{-t}|A_j-A_j|}{1+2\tau}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_n\,2^{-n}Me^{-t}|D_t|}{1+2J}\\
&\ge\frac{Q_n}{2eJ(1+2J)}T\\
&\ge\frac{Q_n}{20e(n+1)^2}T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
For the last denominator bound, \(J\le2(n+1)\) and
\(1+2J\le1+4(n+1)\le5(n+1)\).

We check the absolute constant using exact elementary estimates. Integration gives
\(\log(11/10)>1/11\). For real \(x\ge1\), the derivative of
\(\sqrt x-\log(x+1)\) equals
\((\sqrt x-1)^2/[2\sqrt x(x+1)]\ge0\); its value at \(x=1\) is positive because \(\log2<1\). Thus \(\log(n+1)\le\sqrt n\).
Also \(2\sqrt2/3<1\), and the already established \(e>8/3\) implies \(e^4>40\), so \(\log40<4\).
Using the exact value of \(Q_n\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{Q_n}{20e(n+1)^2}
&=n\log\frac{11}{10}
-\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\sqrt n
-2\log(n+1)-\frac{10}{3}-\log40\\
&>\frac n{11}-3\sqrt n-\frac{22}{3}\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\frac n{11}}-\frac{3\sqrt{11}}2\right)^2
-\frac{385}{12}
>-33.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed, \(385/12<33\). Equations (28) and (29) prove the corner lower bound in (4) with the stated \(e^{-33}\).

For completeness, the maximum in (4) exists. At a fixed \(j\in D\), the box of integer \(u\) with \(0\le u\le j\) is finite. Its finite thresholds
\(\log(w_j/w_{j+u})\), for \(w_{j+u}>0\), form a finite set of nonnegative numbers containing zero. The set \(F_j(t)\) can change only at these thresholds, and its value at any finite \(t\) agrees with its value at the largest such threshold at most \(t\). The factor \(e^{-t}/(1+2t)\) decreases with \(t\). Therefore the maximum at this center is attained at a finite threshold. There are finitely many positive centers. Zero weights never enter any \(F_j(t)\) at finite \(t\), and zero coordinates of \(j\) cause no exception to this argument.

Finally apply the two declared accepted consumers to the original body. The actual table is supported in the nonnegative orthant and is coordinatewise nonincreasing there, as already checked. Thus all actual-body hypotheses of d887d95075dfcc06 hold. Its local set \(F_t\) at any fixed center is exactly our \(F_j(t)\), because the envelope in that accepted fact agrees with the entire integer table. Equation (14) of that fact consequently yields, with precisely the actual \(\alpha\), \(\Delta_j\), and \(B_j^\Delta\),
\[
B_j^\Delta(2s)\ge w_j e^{-s}|F_j(s)-F_j(s)|
\qquad(s\ge0).
\tag{30}
\]
In this application the downset property ensures that each difference has its positive and negative parts in the same \(F_j(s)\). The accepted inequality therefore counts distinct oriented displacements once, including zero; it does not count their representing pairs. Its endpoint weights and curvatures are those of the original table, irrespective of the auxiliary uniform count in (7).

Use (30) at the selected \(j\) and \(s=\tau\) from (28). Then
\(B_j^\Delta(2\tau)/(1+2\tau)\ge e^{-33}T\), and hence
\(K(w)\ge K_j(w)\ge e^{-33}T\).
The cutoff \(L=2\tau\) is finite and nonnegative, \(w_j>0\), and \(C\) is the original convex body with \(W>0\). The fixed-center, fixed-cutoff statement (3) of accepted fact 03f071403cdfc69f therefore supplies one \(x^\star\in C_j\) such that
\[
h_S((j,x^\star))
\ge\frac{B_j^\Delta(2\tau)}{12(1+2\tau)}
\ge\frac{e^{-33}}{12}T.
\tag{31}
\]
The selected center and cutoff depend only on the actual table; the continuous point is then supplied before any halfspace is chosen. The accepted statement covers every closed ambient halfspace, including the boundary cases specified in (5). This proves (5), with \(t=\tau\), and completes the theorem.
