## Statement

Let \(k\ge1\), \(T\ge0\), and \(B\ge2\) be integers, and let \(\beta\ge0\) be real. On \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\), define \(g_B(0)=0\), \(g_B(1)=1\), \(g_B(2)=B\), and \(g_B(3)=2B\). Put
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{i=1}^k g_B(x_i),\\
A_T&=\lbrace(\mathbf x,t):\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace^k,
\ t\in\mathbb Z,\ \Gamma(\mathbf x)\le t\le T\rbrace,\\
Z&=\sum_{(\mathbf x,t)\in A_T}e^{-\beta t},\qquad
p_{(\mathbf x,t)}=e^{-\beta t}/Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$
For each original atom \(\mathbf z\in A_T\), let \(\tau_p(\mathbf z)\) be the infimum of \(p(H)\) over all closed affine halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{k+1}\) containing \(\mathbf z\), and put \(M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in A_T}p_{\mathbf z}\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3\). Then, with the explicit absolute constant \(\kappa=\exp(-2^{80})\),
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{2}
$$
Every normalizer in this statement is the complete original \(Z\) in (1). The locked symbols \(n,d,C,W,c_0\) retain their locked meanings and are not auxiliary quantities in this theorem. The conclusion includes \(T=0\), \(\beta=0\), singleton height intervals, and all equality atoms on energy, height, and halfspace boundaries.

## Proof

The support is finite and contains \((\mathbf0,0)\), so \(0<Z<\infty\). The linear interpolation of \(g_B\) has slopes \(1,B-1,B\), which are nonnegative and nondecreasing. Thus its epigraph on the spatial box is convex and its lattice points at heights at most \(T\) are exactly (1). If \(T=0\), the support is a singleton and \(M_3(p)=1\). In the rest of the proof \(T\ge1\).

Write \(r=e^{-\beta}\), and define \(f_E=\sum_{t=E}^T r^t\) for integers \(0\le E\le T\), and \(f_E=0\) for integers \(E>T\). Let \(a_{\mathbf x}=f_{\Gamma(\mathbf x)}\) on the original spatial box and zero elsewhere. Its total mass is exactly \(M_a=\sum_{\mathbf x}a_{\mathbf x}=Z\). The finite-table notation imported from accepted `0cd230a98ae241c3` has the literal alias map
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_a&=\lbrace\mathbf x:a_{\mathbf x}>0\rbrace,&
a_{\max}&=f_0,&
A^a_\omega&=\lbrace\mathbf x:a_{\mathbf x}\ge\omega\rbrace,\\
r^a_\omega(\mathbf j)&=|A^a_\omega\cap(2\mathbf j-A^a_\omega)|,&
E(a)&=\sum_{\mathbf j\in D_a}\max_{0<\omega\le a_{\mathbf j}}
\omega^4 r^a_\omega(\mathbf j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The symbol \(E(a)\) is only this spatial level energy; the proof will not assert a uniform lower bound for it in branches using the three-site moment theorem.

The profile is strictly decreasing on its positive domain, since \(f_E-f_{E+1}=r^E>0\). For \(1\le E<T\), direct multiplication gives \(f_E^2-f_{E-1}f_{E+1}=r^{E+T}>0\), including when \(r=1\). Hence it is logconcave. Its weak spatial levels are precisely the energy sublevels of \(\Gamma\). Convexity of the interpolation of \(g_B\) implies that each such level is integer-midpoint-closed. All finite-table hypotheses of `0cd230a98ae241c3` therefore hold, with exactly (3) and \(M_a=Z\). That fact gives
$$
E(a)\ge\frac{512}{19683(1+\chi(a))^3}\,8^{-k}Z^4,
\qquad
\chi(a)=\frac1Z\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_a}a_{\mathbf x}
\log\frac{f_0}{a_{\mathbf x}}.
\tag{4}
$$

We also use the original-height reflection consumer established in accepted `dbcf5be1e541e0cf`. To make its applicability independent of the fixed potential in that fact, we give the precise scalar input and root argument. Its scalar calculation (45)-(46) says that a normalized geometric law \(q_j=r^j/\sum_{i=0}^m r^i\), on \(\lbrace0,\ldots,m\rbrace\), satisfies
$$
\sum_{j=0}^m q_j
\left(\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\min(q_{j+h},q_{j-h})\right)^3
\ge\frac1{448}.
\tag{5}
$$
This assertion includes \(m=0\) and \(r=1\), and does not involve any spatial potential. For any original spatial root \(\mathbf j\) and integer \(E\) with \(\Gamma(\mathbf j)\le E\le T\), let \(R\) count its oriented spatial displacements with both endpoint energies at most \(E\). For \(E\le t\le T\) and \(|h|\le\min(t-E,T-t)\), the original atoms \((\mathbf j+\mathbf v,t+h)\) and \((\mathbf j-\mathbf v,t-h)\) are feasible and have midpoint \((\mathbf j,t)\). Negation of \((\mathbf v,h)\) pairs distinct atoms, except for the zero displacement. Every containing closed halfspace includes an endpoint of each pair. The smaller original endpoint mass is \(r^{t+|h|}/Z\). Charging each pair once and half the fixed root mass gives depth at least \(R\sum_h r^{t+|h|}/(2Z)\). Apply (5) with the exact translation \(m=T-E\), \(j=t-E\), and normalized masses \(q^{(E)}_t=r^t/f_E\). Cubing the depth bound and multiplying by the original sampled mass \(r^t/Z\) gives
$$
\sum_{t=E}^T p_{(\mathbf j,t)}\tau_p(\mathbf j,t)^3
\ge\frac{R^3 f_E^4}{3584 Z^4}.
\tag{6}
$$
This is the same consumer as in `dbcf5be1e541e0cf`, with its height profile and original root probabilities literally identified with the present ones; only the spatial displacement set is the present energy sublevel. Taking one attaining level from (3) at each root, and summing over distinct original sampled roots, proves
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac{E(a)}{3584Z^4}.
\tag{7}
$$
Attaining levels exist by the attained-value assertion of `0cd230a98ae241c3`. No levels at one sampled root are added independently.

We first dispose of bounded dimension. Let \(\phi\) be the linear interpolation of \(-\log(f_E/f_0)\) on \([0,T]\). It is convex, nondecreasing, and zero at zero. Rounding every spatial coordinate down after dividing it by two defines \(F(\mathbf x)_i=\lfloor x_i/2\rfloor\). One has \(g_B(F(\mathbf x)_i)\le g_B(x_i)/2\), so \(\phi(\Gamma(F(\mathbf x)))\le\phi(\Gamma(\mathbf x))/2\). Each image has at most \(2^k\) preimages. Consequently, for \(u_{\mathbf x}=a_{\mathbf x}/f_0\), summation followed by Jensen under the original spatial law gives
$$
e^{\chi(a)/2}
\le\frac{\sum_{\mathbf x}\sqrt{u_{\mathbf x}}}{\sum_{\mathbf x}u_{\mathbf x}}
\le2^k\le3^k,
\qquad \chi(a)\le2k\log3.
\tag{8}
$$
Put \(N=2^{1024}\). If \(1\le k<N\), then \(1+2k\log3<1+3k<2^{1026}\), while \(19683<2^{15}\). Equations (4) and (7) imply
$$
M_3(p)\ge2^{-3093}\,8^{-(k+1)}
\ge e^{-2^{80}}\,8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{9}
$$
Here and below the deliberately coarse constants are absolute. We now assume \(k\ge N\).

Two original-mass transfers will handle low occupation of expensive sites. Let \(Z_{\rm bin}\) and \(Z_{\le2}\) be the unnormalized masses obtained by summing (1) over spatial sites in \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k\) and \(\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace^k\), respectively. These are portions of the original mass, so they are not substituted for \(Z\) in any original probability.

For the binary portion, use (6) with zero spatial displacement and \(E=\Gamma(\mathbf j)=|\mathbf j|\). Hölder on its at most \(2^k\) roots gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{3584Z^4}
\sum_{\mathbf j\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k} f_{|\mathbf j|}^4
\ge\frac{8^{-k}}{3584}\left(\frac{Z_{\rm bin}}Z\right)^4.
\tag{10}
$$
Writing \(F_k(U)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^k}\sum_{t=|\mathbf x|}^{U}r^t\), with value zero for \(U<0\), expansion by the numbers \(m_2,m_3\) of coordinates at sites 2 and 3 gives exactly
$$
Z=\sum_{m_2,m_3\ge0\atop m_2+m_3\le k}
\frac{k!}{m_2!m_3!(k-m_2-m_3)!}\,
r^{B(m_2+2m_3)}
F_{k-m_2-m_3}\bigl(T-B(m_2+2m_3)\bigr).
\tag{11}
$$
The inner binary sum is at most \(F_k(T)=Z_{\rm bin}\). Therefore
$$
Z\le Z_{\rm bin}(1+e^{-\beta B}+e^{-2\beta B})^k.
\tag{12}
$$
If \(\beta B\ge\log k\), (12) gives \(Z/Z_{\rm bin}\le e^2\), and (10) proves (2), with a much larger coefficient than claimed. Henceforth assume
$$
\beta B<\log k.
\tag{13}
$$

For the three-site portion we prove a different comparison, valid without any condition on \(\beta\). Put \(R_T=\lfloor T/B\rfloor\), and suppose \(R_T<k\). Partition the original spatial mass by \(m\), the number of coordinates equal to 3, and denote the resulting mass by \(Z_m\). For an original vector in this part, let \(m_2\) be its number of coordinates equal to 2 and put \(r_x=m_2+2m\le R_T\). Choose a set of \(m\) coordinates currently equal to 0 or 1. Change every original 3 into 2, and change these selected low coordinates into 2. The resulting vector \(\mathbf y\) is three-site, has exactly \(r_x\) coordinates equal to 2, and has energy
\(\Gamma(\mathbf y)=\Gamma(\mathbf x)-|\lbrace\text{selected coordinates originally equal to 1}\rbrace|\le\Gamma(\mathbf x)\).
Thus \(a_{\mathbf x}\le a_{\mathbf y}\). Distinct selected coordinate sets give distinct \(\mathbf y\). Their number is
$$
\binom{k-r_x+m}{m}\ge\frac{(k-R_T)^m}{m!}.
\tag{14}
$$
Conversely, if a three-site vector \(\mathbf y\) has \(r_y\le R_T\) coordinates equal to 2, a preimage with \(m\) original 3's is specified by choosing \(m\) of these coordinates as original 3's, choosing another \(m\) as the low partners, and specifying their original 0 or 1 values. Its number of preimages is at most
$$
\binom{r_y}{m}\binom{r_y-m}{m}2^m
\le\frac{2^mR_T^{2m}}{(m!)^2}.
\tag{15}
$$
Summing \(a_{\mathbf x}\le a_{\mathbf y}\) over these edges, using (14)-(15), and then summing \(m\), proves the original-mass inequality
$$
Z_m\le\frac1{m!}\left(\frac{2R_T^2}{k-R_T}\right)^m Z_{\le2},
\qquad
\frac Z{Z_{\le2}}\le\exp\left(\frac{2R_T^2}{k-R_T}\right).
\tag{16}
$$
This double count couples different positive supports; there is no separate fourth-power comparison within one support block.

Accepted `cd9532b1d59c5977` applies to the probability law on the three-site portion with the exact alias map \(B_i=B\), identical \(k,T,\beta\), identical spatial coordinates and ambient halfspaces, and its normalizer equal to \(Z_{\le2}\). Its coefficient is \(\kappa_3=1/2939328\). For any original atom in this portion, every containing halfspace has full original mass at least \((Z_{\le2}/Z)\) times its mass under that normalized three-site law. The original sampled mass at the same atom has the same factor. Summing only these original atoms therefore gives
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa_3\left(\frac{Z_{\le2}}Z\right)^4
8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{17}
$$
This is a comparison of the original depths, not a replacement of the original law.

We now construct the reflected saddle. For \(s\ge0\), put \(u=s\), \(v=sB\), and, when convenient, \(\xi=e^{-u}\), \(\eta=e^{-v}\), \(q=e^{-2v/3}\). Extend \(g_B\) by infinity outside its spatial domain and define
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_j&=\lbrace h\in\mathbb Z:j+h,j-h\in\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\rbrace,\\
K_s(j)&=\sum_{h\in I_j}
\exp\left[-\frac{2s}{3}\bigl(g_B(j+h)+g_B(j-h)\bigr)\right],\\
Z_g(s)&=1+\xi+\eta+\eta^2,
\qquad P_g(s)=\sum_{j=0}^3K_s(j)^3,\\
\delta(s)&=\log\frac{8P_g(s)}{Z_g(s)^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
The symbol \(Z_g\) is a scalar auxiliary mass, not the original \(Z\). The scalar quantities \(I_j,K_s,P_g\) have exactly the reflected-kernel normalization used in `dbcf5be1e541e0cf`, with \(g\) there replaced here by the explicitly displayed \(g_B\); every estimate for this new potential is proved below. Direct evaluation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(K_s(0),K_s(1),K_s(2),K_s(3))
&=(1,\xi^{4/3}+2\eta^{2/3},\eta^{4/3}(1+2\xi^{2/3}),\eta^{8/3}),\\
P_g(s)&=1+(\xi^{4/3}+2\eta^{2/3})^3
+\eta^4(1+2\xi^{2/3})^3+\eta^8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
$$

We need three scalar surplus estimates. First, \(\delta(s)\ge0\) for all \(s\ge0\). To prove this, set \(w=(1,\xi,\eta,\eta^2)\) and \(C_j=\sum_{x+y=2j}w_xw_y\), with both endpoints in \(\lbrace0,1,2,3\rbrace\). The condition \(\eta\le \xi^2\) makes this sequence logconcave. Thus \((w_xw_y)^{1/3}\le w_j^{2/3}\) at every such midpoint, so
$$
\frac{Z_g(s)^2}{2}
\le(w_0+w_2)^2+(w_1+w_3)^2
=\sum_jC_j
\le\sum_jw_j^{2/3}K_s(j)
\le Z_g(s)^{2/3}P_g(s)^{1/3}.
\tag{20}
$$
The first step is Cauchy-Schwarz on the two parity classes, and the last is Hölder. Cubing proves the assertion.

For the compact phase \(0\le u\le1\), \(2u\le v\le24\), we claim the explicit improvement
$$
\delta(s)\ge\varepsilon,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{3^{-48}}{1024}>2^{-106}.
\tag{21}
$$
Indeed, the difference between the middle two sums in (20) is exactly
$$
D=2\eta^{2/3}(\xi^{2/3}-\eta^{1/3})
+2\eta^2(\xi^{2/3}-\xi)\ge0.
\tag{22}
$$
Here \(\eta\ge e^{-24}\) and \(\xi\ge e^{-1}>1/3\). If \(\xi\le31/32\), the identity \(1-\xi^{1/3}=(1-\xi)/(1+\xi^{1/3}+\xi^{2/3})\) shows that the second term in (22) is at least \(e^{-48}/144\). If \(\xi>31/32\) but \(\eta\le(31/32)\xi^2\), the same identity applied to \(31/32\) makes the first term at least \(e^{-16}/96\), hence at least \(e^{-48}/144\). In either case, (20) with this additional gap gives
\(8P_g/Z_g^4\ge(1+2D/Z_g^2)^3\ge1+3D/8\), because \(Z_g\le4\). Since \(e^{-48}/144<1\), monotonicity of the logarithm and \(\log(1+x)\ge x/2\) for \(0\le x\le1\) give \(\delta\ge e^{-48}/768>\varepsilon\).

In the remaining compact subcase, \(\xi>31/32>24/25\) and \(\eta>(31/32)\xi^2>(31/32)^3>9/10\). Formula (19) gives \(K_s(1)\ge \xi^2+2\eta\ge27/10\) and \(K_s(2)\ge \eta^2(1+2\xi)\ge23/10\). Consequently \(P_g\ge1+(27/10)^3+(23/10)^3=657/20\), whereas \(Z_g^4\le256\). Thus \(\delta\ge\log(657/640)\ge17/657>\varepsilon\), proving (21). The elementary bounds \(2<e<3\) used here follow from the exponential series.

For the near-binary phase \(0<u\le1\) and \(v\ge24\), we claim
$$
\delta(s)\ge\frac{7u^2}{512}+\frac q{16}
\ge\frac{u^2+q}{1024}.
\tag{23}
$$
Let \(P_0=1+\xi^4\) and \(Z_0=1+\xi\). The identity
\(8(1+\xi^4)-(1+\xi)^4=(1-\xi)^2(7\xi^2+10\xi+7)\), together with \(1-e^{-u}\ge u/2\), gives \(8P_0/Z_0^4\ge1+7u^2/64\). Also \(8P_0/Z_0^4\le8\), so its logarithm is at least \(7u^2/512\). From (19), \(P_g\ge P_0+6\xi^{8/3}q\), hence \(P_g/P_0\ge1+(3/16)q\). Here \(e^{8/3}<16\), which follows from \(\log2>2/3\). Therefore \(\log(P_g/P_0)\ge3q/32\). Finally \(4\log(Z_g/Z_0)\le8\eta\le q/32\), since \(v\ge24\) and \(e^8>256\). Subtraction proves (23).

For the high-rate phase \(u=s\ge1\), convexity gives \(\eta\le \xi^2\), while \(\xi=e^{-s}<3/8\). Thus \(Z_g\le1+3/8+9/64+81/4096<25/16\). Since \((25/16)^4<6\) and \(P_g\ge1\),
$$
\delta(s)>\log(4/3)>1/4.
\tag{24}
$$

We next establish a scale-sensitive concentration bound. Define at one scalar root
$$
e_j(h)=\frac{g_B(j+h)+g_B(j-h)}2-g_B(j),\qquad
b_j(h)=\frac{g_B(j+h)-g_B(j-h)}2,
\tag{25}
$$
and set \(H_s(j)=\sum_{h\in I_j}e^{-(4s/3)e_j(h)}\). Give \(J\) the law \(Q_s(j)=K_s(j)^3/P_g(s)\), and, conditionally on \(J=j\), give \(V\) the law \(e^{-(4s/3)e_j(h)}/H_s(j)\) on \(I_j\). These are only auxiliary counting laws. The root/displacement notation in this paragraph is the exact normalization of `dbcf5be1e541e0cf`; the present excesses and imbalances are defined by the present potential (25). In particular \(e_j\ge0\), and conditional symmetry gives \(\mathbb E[b_j(V)\mid J=j]=0\).

Put \(\mathcal R_s=u^2+128v^2q\). We claim
$$
s^2\mathbb E\bigl[g_B(J)^2+e_J(V)^2+b_J(V)^2\bigr]
\le\mathcal R_s\qquad(s>0).
\tag{26}
$$
At root 1, the scaled self cost is \(u\), and at each nonzero displacement the scaled excess and imbalance are \(v/2-u\) and \(\pm v/2\). Their joint auxiliary probability is \(2q(\xi^{4/3}+2q)^2/P_g\le18q\). Their squared excess-plus-imbalance is at most \(v^2/2\), since \(v\ge2u\). Root 2 has probability at most \(27\eta^4\); its scaled values are \(g_B(2)s=v\), with nonzero excess \(u/2\) and imbalance \(\pm(v-u/2)\). The sum of their three squares is at most \(3v^2\). Root 3 has probability at most \(\eta^8\), scaled cost \(2v\), and zero displacements. Therefore the left side of (26) is at most \(u^2+9v^2q+81v^2\eta^4+4v^2\eta^8\le u^2+94v^2q\), which proves (26).

Let \(m_s(j)=\mathbb E[e_j(V)\mid J=j]\), \(v_s^e(j)=\mathbb E[e_j(V)^2\mid J=j]\), \(v_s^b(j)=\mathbb E[b_j(V)^2\mid J=j]\), and \(\alpha_s(j)=g_B(j)+m_s(j)\). Put \(\mu(s)=k\mathbb E_{Q_s}\alpha_s(J)\). Conditional Cauchy-Schwarz and (26) give
$$
s^2\mathbb E_{Q_s}\alpha_s(J)^2\le2\mathcal R_s,
\qquad
s^2\mathbb E_{Q_s}[v_s^e(J)+v_s^b(J)]\le\mathcal R_s.
\tag{27}
$$
Expanding each kernel cube over \((j,h_1,h_2,h_3)\) gives a fixed domain of \(56\) tuples with energy
\(\Xi=4g_B(j)+(4/3)\sum_{\ell=1}^3 e_j(h_\ell)\). Differentiating its finite exponential sum proves
$$
\frac14\frac{d}{ds}\log(P_g(s)^k)=-\mu(s).
\tag{28}
$$
This identity also defines the continuous extension of \(\mu\) at \(s=0\).

Let \(s_{\max}=12\log2\). For \(0\le s\le s_{\max}\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(s)&=\max_{0\le t\le T,\ t\in\mathbb Z}
\left(\log\frac{f_t}{f_0}+st\right),\\
\mathcal J(s)&=\lbrace t:\log(f_t/f_0)+st=\mathcal F(s)\rbrace,\\
L(s)&=40\sqrt{k}\sqrt{1+128B^2e^{-2sB/3}},\\
\Psi(s)&=\mathcal F(s)+\tfrac14\log(P_g(s)^k)-\int_0^sL(y)\,dy.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$
For \(s>0\), \(L(s)=40\sqrt{k\mathcal R_s}/s\). All functions in (29) are continuous on the displayed compact interval. Since \(f_t<f_0\) at every \(t>0\), the right derivative of \(\mathcal F\) at zero is zero. Equations (28)-(29) give \(\Psi'_+(0)=-\mu(0)-L(0)<0\). Thus a minimizing rate exists and is positive.

For any \(s>0\) and \(t\in\mathcal J(s)\), the defining maximum gives the original-mass upper bound
$$
Z\le f_0e^{\mathcal F(s)}Z_g(s)^k
=f_t e^{st}Z_g(s)^k.
\tag{30}
$$
At an interior minimum, the left and right derivatives of \(\mathcal F\) are its smallest and largest active integer indices. Logconcavity of \(f_t\) makes all indices between them active. Using (28), we may therefore select an active integer \(t_\star\) satisfying
$$
\mu(s)+L(s)\le t_\star\le\mu(s)+L(s)+1.
\tag{31}
$$
At the endpoint minimum \(s=s_{\max}\), select its smallest active index; the nonpositive left derivative gives instead \(t_\star\le\mu(s)+L(s)\).

For an interior minimum put \(V_s=k\mathcal R_s\). Sample spatial roots independently with product law \(Q_s^{\otimes k}\). Call a root \(\mathbf j\) good when
$$
\left|s\sum_i\alpha_s(j_i)-s\mu(s)\right|\le8\sqrt{V_s},
\qquad
s^2\sum_i[v_s^e(j_i)+v_s^b(j_i)]\le64V_s.
\tag{32}
$$
Independence, (27), Chebyshev, Markov, and a union bound give good-root probability at least \(1-3/64>1/2\). At a fixed good root, sample coordinate displacements independently with the conditional laws above. Another Chebyshev union bound shows that the displacement event
$$
|s(\textstyle\sum_i e_{j_i}(V_i)-\sum_i m_s(j_i))|\le16\sqrt{V_s},
\qquad
|s\textstyle\sum_i b_{j_i}(V_i)|\le16\sqrt{V_s}
\tag{33}
$$
has probability at least \(3/4\), hence at least \(1/2\). Its total failure bound is at most \(64V_s/(256V_s)=1/4\).

The exact endpoint energies are \(\Gamma(\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v)=\Gamma(\mathbf j)+\sum_i e_{j_i}(v_i)\pm\sum_i b_{j_i}(v_i)\). By (31)-(33), they are at most \(t_\star\). The root is feasible at that threshold as well, because all excesses are nonnegative. Reciprocal conditional density on (33), using its lower excess bound, shows that the number of these legal displacements is at least
$$
\frac12\left(\prod_i H_s(j_i)\right)
\exp\left(\frac{4s}{3}\sum_i m_s(j_i)-\frac{64}{3}\sqrt{V_s}\right).
\tag{34}
$$
The identity \(\prod_iH_s(j_i)^3=e^{4s\Gamma(\mathbf j)}P_g(s)^k Q_s^{\otimes k}(\mathbf j)\), followed by cubing (34), summing good roots, and using the lower side of (32), proves
$$
E(a)\ge\frac1{16}f_{t_\star}^4P_g(s)^k
\exp(4s\mu(s)-96\sqrt{V_s}).
\tag{35}
$$
The threshold is a legal weak level even if its height is not an attained spatial energy. Dividing by (30) to the fourth power, and using (31), gives the key estimate
$$
\frac{E(a)}{Z^4}
\ge\frac1{16}\exp\bigl(k\delta(s)-256\sqrt{V_s}-4s\bigr)\,8^{-k}.
\tag{36}
$$
All root and endpoint atoms used in this derivation belong to the original finite cap.

We handle the endpoint minimum before using (36). Since \(s_{\max}<12\) and \(\sup_{v\ge0}v^2e^{-2v/3}=9/e^2<9\), throughout the minimization interval
$$
\mathcal R_s<144+128\cdot9<2^{11}.
\tag{37}
$$
At \(s=s_{\max}\), convexity and \(B\ge2\) give \(Z_g\le1+2^{-12}+2^{-24}+2^{-48}\), so \(\log Z_g<1/16\). In the 56-tuple expansion every nonzero tuple has \(\Xi\ge4\), and exactly one tuple has zero energy. Thus \(P_g(s/2)-1\le55e^{-2s}=55\cdot2^{-24}<1\). The elementary inequality \((s\Xi)e^{-s\Xi}\le(2/e)e^{-s\Xi/2}\), together with (28), gives \(s\mu(s)<k/4\). Therefore the endpoint active index and (30) imply
$$
\log\frac Z{f_0}
\le st_\star+k\log Z_g
\le\frac5{16}k+40\sqrt{V_s}
\le\frac5{16}k+2^{11}\sqrt{k}.
\tag{38}
$$
The original spatial root zero has the legal self-reflection at level \(f_0\), so \(E(a)\ge f_0^4\). Since \(k\ge N\), (38) gives \(E(a)/Z^4\ge e^{-3k/2}\ge8^{-k}\), using \(\log8>3/2\). Equations (7) and (2) follow in this endpoint case.

Consider now an interior minimum. If \(s\ge1\), (24), (37), and \(k\ge N\) imply \(k\delta(s)\ge256\sqrt{V_s}+4s\). If \(s\le1\) and \(sB\le24\), the same follows from (21), (37), \(\varepsilon>2^{-106}\), and \(\sqrt{k}\ge2^{512}\). In either case (36) gives \(E(a)/Z^4\ge8^{-k}/16\), which proves (2) through (7). The same conclusion holds at any near-binary interior minimum satisfying
$$
k\delta(s)\ge256\sqrt{V_s}+4s.
\tag{39}
$$

It remains to handle a near-binary interior minimum at which (39) fails. Thus \(0<s\le1\), \(v=sB\ge24\). Put \(U=ku^2\), \(Q=kq\), and \(G=U+Q\), so \(V_s=U+128v^2Q\). By (23), failure of (39), and \(\sqrt{V_s}\ge s\),
$$
G<2^{19}\sqrt{U+128v^2Q},
\qquad
G<2^{46}v^2.
\tag{40}
$$
For the second inequality, square the first, use \(U,Q\le G\), divide by \(G>0\), and note \(1+128v^2\le129v^2<2^8v^2\). Consequently
$$
\frac{k}{B}=\frac{\sqrt{k}\sqrt U}{v}<2^{23}\sqrt{k},
\qquad
Q=\sqrt{k}\sqrt Q\,e^{-v/3}<2^{25}\sqrt{k},
\qquad
\frac{\sqrt{V_s}}v<2^{24}\sqrt{k}.
\tag{41}
$$
The middle inequality uses \(\sqrt Q<2^{23}v\) from (40) and \(v e^{-v/3}\le3/e<2\). For the last, use \(U/v^2<2^{46}\), \(Q\le k\), and \(\sqrt{x+y}\le\sqrt x+\sqrt y\).

The auxiliary mean obeys a simple uniform bound
$$
\frac{\mu(s)}B\le\frac{k}{B}+64Q.
\tag{42}
$$
Indeed root 1 contributes at most \(1/B\) to its cost divided by \(B\), plus at most \(9q\) in expected excess divided by \(B\), by the same joint probability used in (26). Root 2 has probability at most \(27\eta^4\) and \(\alpha_s(2)/B\le5/4\); root 3 contributes at most \(2\eta^8\). These terms are bounded by \(64q\) in total. Combining (31), (41), and (42), and using \(B\ge2\), gives
$$
\frac{t_\star}{B}
\le\frac{k}{B}+64Q+40\frac{\sqrt{V_s}}v+\frac1B
<H\sqrt{k},\qquad H=2^{34}.
\tag{43}
$$

If \(T/B\le2H\sqrt{k}\), then \(R_T\le2^{35}\sqrt{k}\le k/2\), since \(k\ge N\). Formula (16) gives \(Z/Z_{\le2}\le\exp(2^{72})\). Therefore (17) proves
$$
M_3(p)\ge\kappa_3 e^{-2^{74}}8^{-(k+1)}
\ge e^{-2^{80}}8^{-(k+1)}.
\tag{44}
$$
For the last inequality, \(2939328<2^{22}<e^{32}\), so \(\kappa_3>e^{-32}\), and \(2^{74}+32<2^{80}\).

Finally suppose \(T/B>2H\sqrt{k}\). Equation (43) implies \(t_\star<T/2<T\). Because \(t_\star\) is active, its forward neighboring index gives
$$
s\le\log\frac{f_{t_\star}}{f_{t_\star+1}}
\le\beta+\frac1{T-t_\star}
\le\beta+\frac2T.
\tag{45}
$$
To verify the middle inequality including \(\beta=0\), put \(L=T-t_\star\ge1\). The tail ratio equals \(e^\beta(\sum_{j=0}^{L}r^j)/(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^j)\). Its latter factor is \(1+r^L/\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}r^j\le1+1/L\), giving (45). By (13), with \(T/B>2H\sqrt{k}>2\), this yields
$$
v=sB\le\beta B+\frac{2B}T<\log k+1,
\qquad
Q=ke^{-2v/3}>e^{-2/3}k^{1/3}.
\tag{46}
$$
On the other hand (40) gives \(Q<2^{46}(\log k+1)^2\). These inequalities contradict \(k\ge N\). For an explicit verification, write \(L=\log k\ge512\). The function \(e^{L/3}/(L+1)^2\) is increasing for \(L>5\). At \(L=512\), using \(e>2\), \(e^{2/3}<4\), and \(513^2<2^{20}\), one obtains
\(e^{-2/3}e^{512/3}/513^2>2^{148}>2^{46}\).
This proves that the final remaining case is empty.

All cases have now been covered. The small-rank branch used (4) with the complete original mass. The binary and three-site branches compared original atom and halfspace masses with their explicit original mass portions and paid their fourth-power ratios. The saddle branches used only legal original roots, levels, endpoints, and heights, with the full \(Z^4\) in (7), (30), and (36). Every interval and level was defined by weak inequalities, the reflection argument used closed halfspaces, and scalar singleton intervals were included in (5). Hence no boundary atom, cap case, or original normalizer was lost. This proves (2).
