## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer and let $K_j\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact full-dimensional convex bodies converging in Hausdorff distance to a full-dimensional convex body $K$. Suppose $\mathbf u_j\to\mathbf u\ne\mathbf0$ and $s_j\to s$, where $\min_K\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x<s<\max_K\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x$. Then
$$\frac{\mathcal H_{d-1}(K_j\cap\{\mathbf u_j\cdot\mathbf x=s_j\})}{\|\mathbf u_j\|}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal H_{d-1}(K\cap\{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x=s\})}{\|\mathbf u\|}.$$
For $d=1$, $\mathcal H_0$ counts the single point. Convergence is uniform when $(\mathbf u,s)$ ranges over a compact family of nonzero-normal proper planes of $K$, with parameters perturbed uniformly by $o(1)$.

In particular, let $T=\operatorname{conv}(\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d)$, $N=d+1$, $b=1/N$, $\Gamma=(d/(d+1))^d$, and let $\lambda_i$ be its barycentric functions. If $B\to T$ through convex bodies and $\mathbf g_B$ is the true centroid, then for each $\zeta>0$ there are $\varepsilon,h>0$ and a Hausdorff neighborhood of $T$ such that, for all these $B$, every $i$, every $\|\mathbf a\|\le\varepsilon$, and $|t|\le h$, with $f_i(\mathbf x)=\lambda_i(\mathbf x)+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf x$,
$$|S_B(f_i,f_i(\mathbf g_B)+t)-N\Gamma|B||\le\zeta|B|.$$
Here $S_B(f,s)=\mathcal H_{d-1}(B\cap\{f=s\})/\|\nabla f\|$. All planes in this conclusion are proper and all gradients are nonzero.

## Proof

Identify the moving hyperplanes with the limiting hyperplane by Euclidean isometries and translations tending to the identity. Any convergent sequence of section points has limit in the limiting section, which proves the upper Hausdorff inclusion. Because the limiting plane is proper, it contains a point $\mathbf x_0\in\operatorname{int}K$. For any other point $\mathbf x$ of the limiting section, $(1-t)\mathbf x+t\mathbf x_0$, $0<t\le1$, is an interior point of $K$ still on that plane. A compact interior neighborhood is eventually inside $K_j$: its support-function margin is strictly positive and dominates the Hausdorff error. Slightly adjusting this interior point to the moving plane therefore yields a section point of $K_j$ converging to it. Letting $t\downarrow0$, and using compactness of the limiting section, proves the lower Hausdorff inclusion.

The identified sections thus converge as convex subsets of the fixed $(d-1)$-dimensional plane; their $(d-1)$-volume converges. This follows from indicator convergence off their relative boundary, since a proper section of a full-dimensional convex body has nonempty relative interior. In dimension one a proper section is a singleton, of $\mathcal H_0$ measure one. Division by the convergent nonzero gradient norm proves the assertion.

Uniformity on a compact family follows by contradiction: choose violating parameters, pass to a convergent subsequence within the compact family, and apply the just-proved assertion. Compactness also gives a positive lower bound on normal lengths and a positive properness margin.

For the stated simplex, its cap at barycentric level $s\in(0,1)$ has volume $|T|(1-s)^d$. Hence its coarea density at $s=b$ is
$$d|T|(1-b)^{d-1}=N\Gamma|T|.$$
The preceding continuity result, with $K_j=T$, also gives continuity in nearby normal and level parameters on the fixed simplex. Choose $\varepsilon,h$ small enough that these densities differ from $N\Gamma|T|$ by less than $(\zeta/4)|T|$, all planes are proper, and all normals are nonzero. Then take $B$ sufficiently close to $T$. Its volume and centroid converge to those of $T$ by bounded integration. Compact uniform section continuity and a further decrease of the Hausdorff neighborhood give error at most $\zeta|B|$ after replacing $|T|$ by $|B|$. This establishes the claimed quantifier order. No differentiability of the boundary and no approximation rate relative to any endpoint mass is assumed.
