## Statement

For every $k\ge1$, $\alpha\in(0,1)$, and symmetric real $k\times k$ matrix $A$, there are convex polytopes $K\subset\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}K$ and section $t=0$ centered at $0$, whose angular cap fraction $p$ is analytic near $(0,1)$ and satisfies $p(0,1)=\alpha$ and $\operatorname{Hess}_{\mathbb S^k}p(0,1)=2^k A/(3|K|)$ in horizontal tangent coordinates. The factor $2^k/(3|K|)$ can be arbitrarily large while $\alpha,A$ are fixed. In particular every Hessian signature occurs at each prescribed fraction.

## Proof

First take arbitrary $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ and positive heights $\ell_-,\ell_+$ so that all widths $1+c_it$ stay positive on $[-\ell_-,\ell_+]$. The linear inequalities $-\ell_-\le t\le\ell_+$ and $|z_i|\le1+c_it$ define a compact convex polytope with $0$ interior. Every horizontal section is centered. Write $J(t)=\prod_i(1+c_it)$, $C=\sum_i c_i$, and $V=|K|$. The volume is $2^k\int_{-\ell_-}^{\ell_+}J(t)\,dt$ and the horizontal cap fraction is the ratio of the integral over $[0,\ell_+]$ to the integral over $[-\ell_-,\ell_+]$.

Set $z_i=(1+c_it)v_i$ with $v\in[-1,1]^k$. The cut $t+h\cdot z\ge0$ is $t(1+\sum_i h_i c_i v_i)\ge-\sum_i h_i v_i$. For small $h$ the denominator is positive and the resulting threshold stays strictly within the height interval uniformly in $v$. Write $L=\sum_i h_i v_i$ and $B=\sum_i h_i c_i v_i$. The threshold is $\tau=-L/(1+B)=-L+LB+O(\lVert h\rVert^3)$. The exact cap mass is the integral over the cube of $\int_\tau^{\ell_+}J(t)\,dt$. Because $J$ is polynomial and the power series for $(1+B)^{-1}$ converges uniformly in the cube for $h$ near zero, this cap mass is analytic there. Its difference from the horizontal cap mass is the integral of $L-LB-(C/2)L^2$ up to $O(\lVert h\rVert^3)$. The linear and mixed moments vanish by symmetry, and the integral of $v_i^2$ over the cube is $2^k/3$. The critical point therefore has spherical Hessian
$$
-\frac{2^k}{3V}\operatorname{diag}(C+2c_1,\ldots,C+2c_k)
$$
in the same tangent coordinates, using the chart $h\mapsto(h,1)/\sqrt{1+\lVert h\rVert^2}$.

For the eigenvalues $a_i$ of $A$, choose $c_i=-a_i/2+\operatorname{tr}(A)/(2(k+2))$. Then $C=-\operatorname{tr}(A)/(k+2)$ and $C+2c_i=-a_i$. Rotate the horizontal coordinates by an orthogonal eigenbasis to obtain the claimed matrix. Choose $L_0>0$ small enough that the widths stay positive on $[-L_0,L_0]$. Take $\ell_->0$ sufficiently small that $\alpha/(1-\alpha)$ times $\int_{-\ell_-}^0J(t)\,dt$ is less than $\int_0^{L_0}J(t)\,dt$. The function $\ell\mapsto\int_0^\ell J(t)\,dt$ increases continuously and strictly. There is a unique $\ell_+\in(0,L_0)$ with its value equal to that scaled lower integral, yielding the exact fraction $\alpha$. As $\ell_-\downarrow0$, the lower integral, $\ell_+$, and $V$ all tend to zero. This gives the unbounded factor.

For example, in $k=2$ take $\ell_-=1/4$ and $\ell_+=1/100$. All widths below stay positive. The integrals of $J$ have antiderivative $t+(c_1+c_2)t^2/2+c_1c_2t^3/3$. The resulting data are
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
(c_1,c_2)&V&p(0,1)&3V\operatorname{Hess}_{\mathbb S^2}p(0,1)\\ \hline
(1,1)&304213/375000&30301/608426&-16I_2\\
(-1,-1)&491413/375000&29701/982826&16I_2\\
(1,-1)&382187/375000&29999/764374&\operatorname{diag}(-8,8)
\end{array}
$$
Each fraction is below $1/10<27/64$. The three stationary directions are a strict angular maximum, a strict angular minimum, and a saddle, respectively.
