## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and let nonempty compact convex sets $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be the actual fibers of their lifted convex hull at $z\in P$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$ and $\mu$ for the sum of their $d$-volumes, with $M=\mu(S)>0$. Suppose the maximum normalized closed ambient halfspace depth on $S$ is $\beta>0$, and a given finite cover of $S$ consists of closed ambient halfspaces with nonzero continuous normal and masses at most $\beta M$. At every deepest mark $Y$, each cap of that cover containing $Y$ has mass $\beta M$ and boundary through $Y$. Their continuous normals contain zero in their convex hull. In particular an inclusion-minimal subfamily witnessing this inclusion is a positive circuit of at most $d+1$ caps in the given cover.

## Proof

Let $Y=(z,x)$ have normalized depth $\beta$. The actual fibers are compatible by hypothesis, including null fibers and arbitrary base rank, so 55a57b1cf55d0c87 implies $x\in\operatorname{int}K_z$. Every member of the cover containing $Y$ has mass at least $\beta M$ by definition and at most $\beta M$ by hypothesis. If one contained $Y$ strictly, increasing its threshold to the level at $Y$ would remove a slab of positive $d$-volume inside a ball about $x$ in $K_z$, because its continuous normal is nonzero. The resulting closed cap still contains $Y$ and would have mass below $\beta M$, a contradiction.

If zero were outside the convex hull of the continuous normals of these caps, strict separation would yield a direction $v$ whose scalar product with each is negative, using normals oriented toward the caps. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, $(z,x+\varepsilon v)$ lies in $S$ and strictly outside every one of those caps. Every other cap already excludes $Y$ strictly and still does so after a sufficiently small move, since the cover is finite. This contradicts coverage. Choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily with zero in its convex hull. Carathéodory's theorem bounds its size by $d+1$, and minimality makes each coefficient in a convex representation of zero positive with no positively dependent proper subfamily. Thus it is a positive circuit.
