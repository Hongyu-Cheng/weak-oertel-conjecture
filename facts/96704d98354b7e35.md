## Statement

Let $D$ be a compact convex horizontal body in $(z,X)$ with finite sampled integer bases $P$ and positive compact $(d-1)$-dimensional fibers $B_z$, where $d\ge1$ and $|B_z|=1$ for the singleton in dimension one. Let $H(z)=\min_r a_r(z)>0$ on the projection of $D$, for finitely many affine $a_r$, and put $C=\{(z,X,y):(z,X)\in D,\ |y|\le H(z)\}$. For affine $b_r\ge0$ on that projection, replace $H$ by $H_t=\min_r(a_r-tb_r)$. Set $h_z=\max_{r:a_r(z)=H(z)}b_r(z)$ and $D_0=2\sum_z h_z|B_z|>0$. For all sufficiently small $t\ge0$ this is an actual nested convex deformation with unchanged sampled bases and with original mixed mass loss $tD_0$.

On each moving fiber $h_z>0$, suppose each cap in a finite probability menu has nonzero $y$ coefficient and cuts at a graph $y=q(z,X)$ strictly between the two roofs for every $X\in B_z$. Each cap loses exactly $tD_0/2$ for sufficiently small $t$. Let $W$ be the probability-weighted original boundary load, and $dS$ the sum of fiber boundary surface measures, counting the two endpoints for $d=1$. For $\beta<1/2$, some globally finite continuous nonnegative convex $\Phi$ satisfies $\int(W-\beta)\Phi\,dS>0$.

More generally suppose $W$ is the average of a probability law of actual cap selectors. For its proper part the same strict crossing is required for law-almost every row and $|B_z|$-almost every $X$ on every moving fiber. Allow also whole-fiber rows of total weight $\eta_A$, each with the same captured bit on both roofs, and write $A(z)$ for their average captured weight at $z$. The limiting roof response is $\sum_z h_z|B_z|(1-\eta_A+2A(z)-2\beta)$. The same finite-test exclusion follows for $\beta<1/2$ if $\sum_z h_z|B_z|A(z)\ge(\eta_A/2)\sum_z h_z|B_z|$. This law statement uses no uniform crossing margin.

## Proof

The minimum of the perturbed affine planes is concave. Uniform positive height persists by compactness and boundedness of the finitely many $b_r$, and nonnegativity of all $b_r$ gives nesting. At each sampled $z$, the finitely many inactive planes have positive gaps, so for small $t$ only an old active plane can minimize. Thus $H_t(z)=H(z)-th_z$ simultaneously at every sampled site. The mass loss is the summed volume of the two strips of height $th_z$. In the finite cap menu compactness and finiteness give a positive common crossing margin on the moving fibers. Each cap captures exactly one of the removed roof strips over each $X$ and loses half the total loss.

Define
$$
\Phi_R=\max\left(0,\max_r[b_r(z)-R(a_r(z)-y)],\max_r[b_r(z)-R(a_r(z)+y)]\right).
$$
Each $\Phi_R$ is globally finite continuous and convex. The gaps are nonnegative on the old body. As $R$ increases the fields decrease boundedly on the old measured boundary to the speed $v=h_z$ on both horizontal roof faces and zero away from them. Roof and side intersections are surface-null, and dimension one has only the two endpoints. Dominated convergence gives $\int(W-\beta)\Phi_R\,dS\to\int(W-\beta)v\,dS$.

For any properly crossing row the selectors at the two roof points sum to one. For the law version the strict inequalities identify these selectors almost everywhere on the relevant product by Tonelli. The whole-fiber part contributes $2A(z)$ to the paired sum. Therefore the limit integral equals the stated response, reducing to $(1/2-\beta)D_0$ when $\eta_A=0$. The atomic credit hypothesis bounds the general response from below by the same positive quantity. A single finite $R$ has a positive integral. This last argument concerns the old boundary only, so it requires no uniform margin in the cap law and makes no exact finite-time loss claim for that law.
