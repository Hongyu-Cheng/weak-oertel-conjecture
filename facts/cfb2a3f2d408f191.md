## Statement

For integers \(r,m\ge1\), set
\[
D_{m,r}:=\left\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{r}:\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_i\le m\right\}.
\]
For each \(\mathbf j\in D_{m,r}\), let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the unique maximum-entropy probability law on \(D_{m,r}\) with mean \(\mathbf j\). The exact alias to accepted fact `bf1ff94487296374` is \(E=A=D_{m,r}\) and \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=r_{E,\mathbf j}\). The predecessor's ambient dimension \(m\) is the present \(r\); the present \(m\) denotes only simplex dilation. For any strictly positive log-affine source law on \(D_{m,r}\), \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) is also the relative-entropy minimizer denoted \(r_{\mathbf j}\) in that fact.

Then, for every \(\mathbf a\in\mathbb R^r\) and \(\mathbf X\sim\nu_{\mathbf j}\),
\[
\mathbb E\bigl[\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\bigr]^4
\le45648\left(\operatorname{Var}\bigl[\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\bigr]\right)^2.
\]
This includes zero-variance directions and deterministic vertex rows. Consequently, every closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^r\) containing \(\mathbf j\) satisfies \(\nu_{\mathbf j}(H)\ge1/182592\).

## Proof

Augment the random lattice point and its mean by the slack coordinate:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf N&:=\left(X_1,\ldots,X_r,m-\sum_{i=1}^rX_i\right),\\
\mathbf j^+&:=\left(j_1,\ldots,j_r,m-\sum_{i=1}^rj_i\right),
& I&:=\{i:j_i^+>0\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\sum_iN_i=\sum_i j_i^+=m\). Since the coordinates are nonnegative, \(j_i^+=0\) forces \(N_i=0\) almost surely. On every active coordinate \(i\in I\), integrality gives \(j_i^+\ge1\). The positive support of the row is exactly the complete composition set
\[
\mathcal E:=\left\{(n_i)_{i\in I}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{I}:\sum_{i\in I}n_i=m\right\}.
\]
Indeed, the augmented simplex face containing the mean in its relative interior is defined precisely by the inactive coordinates being zero. The minimal-face and positivity assertions of `bf1ff94487296374` apply to this face. Its multiplier formula (18), applied to a uniform source law, gives positive numbers \(q_i\), \(i\in I\), such that the law of the active counts is
\[
\Pr(\mathbf N_I=\mathbf n)
=\frac{\prod_{i\in I}q_i^{n_i}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad
\mathcal Z:=\sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathcal E}\prod_{i\in I}q_i^{n_i}.
\]
This is the complete-composition law, without multinomial coefficients. Multiplying all \(q_i\) by the same positive number does not change it. The identity (47) of that accepted fact proves the stated independence of the row from the log-affine source tilt.

Extend the given direction by \(a_{r+1}:=0\), and define, for \(i\in I\),
\[
b_i:=a_i-\frac1m\sum_{h\in I}a_hj_h^+.
\]
Subtracting this common coefficient does not change the centered projection, because the total count is fixed. Accordingly, with \(Y:=\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf X-\mathbf j)\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I}b_ij_i^+=0,
\qquad
Y=\sum_{i\in I}b_i(N_i-j_i^+).
\]

Construct auxiliary continuous variables \(T_i\), \(i\in I\), conditionally independently given \(\mathbf N_I\), with conditional density
\(e^{-t_i}t_i^{N_i}/N_i!\) on \(t_i>0\). Integration by parts gives \(\int_0^\infty t^k e^{-t}\,dt=k!\) for every nonnegative integer \(k\). Hence these are probability densities, and
\[
\mathbb E[T_i\mid\mathbf N_I]=N_i+1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(T_i\mid\mathbf N_I)=N_i+1.
\]
Summing the joint density over the composition counts and using the multinomial expansion yields the marginal density
\[
f_{\mathbf T}(\mathbf t)
=\frac{e^{-\sum_{i\in I}t_i}}{m!\mathcal Z}
   \left(\sum_{i\in I}q_it_i\right)^m
   \mathbf1_{\{t_i>0\text{ for all }i\in I\}}.
\]
This is log-concave. On the positive orthant its logarithm is an affine function plus \(m\log(\sum_iq_it_i)\), which is concave; its positive support is convex, so extension by zero preserves log-concavity.

The same joint density gives an additional, exact representation. Conditional on \(\mathbf T=\mathbf t\), the count vector is multinomial with \(m\) trials and probabilities \(q_it_i/(\sum_hq_ht_h)\):
\[
\Pr(\mathbf N_I=\mathbf n\mid\mathbf T=\mathbf t)
=\frac{m!}{\prod_{i\in I}n_i!}
  \prod_{i\in I}\left(\frac{q_it_i}{\sum_{h\in I}q_ht_h}\right)^{n_i}.
\]
Define the random probability vector \(\mathbf W\) by \(W_i=q_iT_i/(\sum_hq_hT_h)\). In particular, \(m\mathbb EW_i=j_i^+\). The law of total covariance, together with the elementary multinomial mean and covariance formulas, gives the full matrix identity
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf N_I)
=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf j_I^+)
 -\frac{\mathbf j_I^+(\mathbf j_I^+)^{\mathsf T}}{m}
 +m(m-1)\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf W).
\]
For completeness, the conditional covariance is \(m\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf W)-m\mathbf W\mathbf W^{\mathsf T}\), while the covariance of the conditional mean is \(m^2\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf W)\); adding their expectations gives the displayed identity. Its last term is positive semidefinite, including when \(m=1\), where it vanishes. Evaluating in the direction \(\mathbf b\) and using \(\sum_i b_ij_i^+=0\) therefore proves
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y)
=\sum_{i\in I}b_i^2j_i^+
 +m(m-1)\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{i\in I}b_iW_i\right)
\ge\sum_{i\in I}b_i^2j_i^+.
\]
This comparison is not inferred from coordinate variance bounds.

Now set \(\widetilde Y:=\sum_{i\in I}b_i(T_i-j_i^+-1)\). The conditional gamma moments imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\widetilde Y\mid\mathbf N_I]&=Y,\\
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)
&=\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\sum_{i\in I}b_i^2(j_i^++1)\\
&\le\operatorname{Var}(Y)+2\sum_{i\in I}b_i^2j_i^+
\le3\operatorname{Var}(Y).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first equality in the variance calculation uses conditional independence of the \(T_i\); the first inequality uses \(j_i^+\ge1\). Jensen's inequality applied conditionally to the convex function \(t\mapsto t^4\) also gives \(\mathbb EY^4\le\mathbb E\widetilde Y^4\).

We justify the remaining log-concavity of the scalar marginal directly, rather than invoking an external marginal theorem. The following one-coordinate integration argument is sufficient. Suppose a finite nonnegative log-concave function \(h(\mathbf z,t)\) has finite integrals in \(t\). Fix \(\mathbf z_0,\mathbf z_1\) and \(0<\theta<1\), and write
\(F(t)=h(\mathbf z_0,t)\), \(G(t)=h(\mathbf z_1,t)\), and \(H(t)=h((1-\theta)\mathbf z_0+\theta\mathbf z_1,t)\). If either \(F\) or \(G\) has integral zero, the desired integral log-concavity inequality is immediate. Otherwise put \(A_0=\int F\), \(A_1=\int G\), \(f=F/A_0\), and \(g=G/A_1\). These normalized one-dimensional log-concave functions are continuous and strictly positive on the interiors of their positive intervals. Their cumulative distributions thus have inverse functions \(x(u),y(u)\), for \(0<u<1\), with derivatives \(x'(u)=1/f(x(u))\) and \(y'(u)=1/g(y(u))\).

The function \(z(u)=(1-\theta)x(u)+\theta y(u)\) is strictly increasing. Log-concavity of \(h\), change of variables on its image, and weighted arithmetic-geometric mean give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}H(t)\,dt
&\ge\int_0^1 H(z(u))z'(u)\,du\\
&\ge A_0^{1-\theta}A_1^\theta
 \int_0^1 f(x(u))^{1-\theta}g(y(u))^\theta
 \left(\frac{1-\theta}{f(x(u))}+\frac{\theta}{g(y(u))}\right)du\\
&\ge A_0^{1-\theta}A_1^\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]
The changes of variables can first be taken on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\) and then extended by letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), so no assertion about derivatives at support endpoints is needed. Continuity inside the positive intervals follows from the secant-slope inequalities for their finite concave logarithms. This proves that integrating one coordinate preserves log-concavity.

Apply this argument successively after an orthogonal change of coordinates to \(f_{\mathbf T}\). All needed sectional integrals are finite: on its orthant \(f_{\mathbf T}\) is bounded by a finite multiple of \((1+\|\mathbf t\|)^m e^{-\|\mathbf t\|}\), and orthogonal changes preserve the Euclidean norm. The same integrability property holds for every intermediate coordinate marginal. Thus every nonzero linear projection of \(\mathbf T\) has a log-concave density. Translating a projection preserves this property, so \(\widetilde Y\) has a log-concave density whenever \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\).

If \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=0\), then \(Y=0\) almost surely and the required fourth-moment inequality is automatic. Otherwise the covariance comparison implies \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), and the scaled scalar conclusion of accepted fact `8bb84543f325a261` applies to \(\widetilde Y\). Explicitly, its scalar random variable \(X\) is here \(\widetilde Y\), its mean \(\mu\) is zero, and its variance \(\sigma^2\) is \(\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb EY^4
\le\mathbb E\widetilde Y^4
\le5072\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde Y)\bigr)^2
\le5072\cdot9\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(Y)\bigr)^2
=45648\bigl(\operatorname{Var}(Y)\bigr)^2.
\]
This proves the asserted absolute bound, for all faces as well as the full simplex.

To obtain the halfspace consequence, take a centered projection \(Y\) defining a halfspace through \(\mathbf j\). For positive variance, interpolation between its first, second, and fourth absolute moments gives \(\mathbb E|Y|\ge\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(Y)/45648}\). Since \(\mathbb EY=0\), \(\mathbb EY_+=\mathbb E|Y|/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz then yields
\[
\Pr(Y\ge0)
\ge\frac{(\mathbb EY_+)^2}{\mathbb EY^2}
\ge\frac1{4\cdot45648}
=\frac1{182592}.
\]
For zero variance the probability is one. A closed halfspace containing the mean contains its parallel translate through the mean, so the same lower bound holds for every such halfspace. Finally, for independent centered projections from any finite collection of these rows, expansion of the fourth moment gives \(\sum_i\mathbb EY_i^4+6\sum_{i<h}\mathbb EY_i^2\mathbb EY_h^2\). Because \(45648\ge3\), this is at most \(45648(\sum_i\mathbb EY_i^2)^2\). Hence the same absolute constant is valid in the product fourth-moment step (25) of `bf1ff94487296374`, without a lower bound on individual row atoms.
