## Statement

Even with an actual nested convex-cut erosion, exact neutrality of an old critical cover and zero first derivative of the normalized retained mass on a prescribed path through the old deepest point do not imply that the path remains deepest. For any $n\ge0$, take $[-1/4,1/4]^n\times[-1,1]$ with only measured base $0$ and continuous dimension $1$, and impose the cuts $x+\varepsilon\le1$ and $-x+\varepsilon\le1$. For $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ the old covering halfspaces $x\ge0$ and $x\le0$ each still have half the mixed mass. The mark $(0,\varepsilon^2)$ has zero first derivative of normalized retained mass at zero, but its raw depth is $1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2$, strictly smaller than the new deepest depth $1-\varepsilon$.

## Proof

The cuts yield the only measured fiber $[-1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$. The two fixed halfspaces cover the ambient space and each contains half the symmetric fiber, so their normalized masses are constant and at zero they are both critical at the midpoint. Any closed ambient halfspace containing the mark restricts to a containing halfline or the whole fiber on the measured base. Both tails starting at the mark are realized by ambient halfspaces. The raw depth is therefore the smaller tail length, which at $x=\varepsilon^2$ is $1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2$ and at the deepest midpoint is $1-\varepsilon$. The dimension-one formula in efa4558174ce60cf applies because $\varepsilon^2<1-\varepsilon$ and gives $T=2(1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)$. The normalized retained ratio is $1-\varepsilon^2/(1-\varepsilon)$ and its right derivative at zero is zero. Thus the path fails to be deepest at each indicated positive parameter.
