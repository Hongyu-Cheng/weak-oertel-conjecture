## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty. In fixed barycentric affine coordinates $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_d$ on $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, prescribe the simplex $K_z=\{x:\lambda_i(x)\ge b_i(z)\ \forall i\}$ at each $z\in P$, with $r_z=1-\sum_i b_i(z)>0$. These are precisely the integer fibers of the hull of their lifts if and only if
$$
b_i(p)\le \sum_z t_z b_i(z)
$$
for each $p,i$ and every extreme representation $t$ with $t_z\ge0$, $\sum_z t_z=1$, $\sum_z t_z z=p$ and $t_p=0$. If every row involving a specified coordinate $b_i(p)$ is strict, changing only that coordinate is feasible on a two-sided interval. Its velocity is constant on its facet $F$ and zero on the remaining facets almost everywhere.

Let $M$ be the original mixed mass and $Q=\beta M$ its maximum raw depth. Suppose finitely many assigned witness groups have finite nonempty menus of circuits of old critical caps of mass $Q$. Each circuit uses unit continuous normals $u_j$ and positive coefficients $\rho_j$ with $\sum_j\rho_j u_j=0$. Its old positively weighted margin is nonnegative on its assigned base planes. Assume each cap has positive total proper coarea $S_j$ and no positive-surface-measure supporting coincidence on any fiber. For strict global conclusions assume the assigned planes and finite persistent slack covers at the remaining bases exhaust the measured bases. Define each circuit's weights $\theta_j=(\rho_j/S_j)/\sum_h(\rho_h/S_h)$ and $W=\sum_j\theta_j\mathbf1_{H_j}$. Write $e_{ic}=|F|_{d-1}^{-1}\int_F W\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}$ for circuit $c$ in group $i$.

Under signed outward speed $r$ on $F$, the derivative of the circuit's common equalized budget $q_{ic}$ with unchanged weighted threshold budget satisfies
$$
q_{ic}'(0)-\beta M'(0)=|F|_{d-1}r(e_{ic}-\beta).
$$
An outward sign gives a strict first-order cover improvement in every group if and only if each group has some $e_{ic}<\beta$. The inward sign has this property if and only if each group has some $e_{ic}>\beta$. If both tests fail, some group has $\min_c e_{ic}\ge\beta$ and some group has $\max_c e_{ic}\le\beta$. For a single group, both failures force $e_{ic}=\beta$ for every circuit. With the stated cover assumptions a strict first-order improvement gives strict descent of the actual maximum normalized depth.

## Proof

The map from barycentric coordinates identifies each simplex with its offset vector plus $r_z$ times the standard simplex of nonnegative coordinates summing to one. Thus a Minkowski combination with weights $t$ has the same facet normals and offsets $\sum_z t_z b_i(z)$. Its inclusion in $K_p$ is equivalent to the displayed inequalities. Their linearity in $t$ extends them from extreme representations to all representations with $t_p=0$. For a row with $0<t_p<1$ remove its self weight, divide the other weights by $1-t_p$, and use convexity of $K_p$. The row with $t_p=1$ is automatic. These inclusions are necessary for actual convex fibers, and sufficient because any lifted hull point at base $p$ uses such a representation. The original lifts supply the reverse inclusion and lattice completeness rules out any further integer base. There are finitely many rows, so the strict rows and positive radii persist on a small interval when one coordinate changes. Its defining plane translates parallel with outward speed $-b_i'(p)/\lVert\nabla\lambda_i\rVert$. The other planes stay fixed and their intersections with it are surface-null.

Let $A=|F|_{d-1}$. The whole-fiber volume derivative is $Ar$. At threshold displacement $ts$ in one of the old caps, the cap mass expansion is
$$
Q+t\left(r\int_F\mathbf1_{H_j}\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}-S_js\right)+o(t)
$$
for bounded $s$, uniformly on compact $s$-intervals. To see the body term, take the signed slab between the two positions of the moved facet, retaining all the other facet inequalities. Integration along lines normal to that facet, followed by division by $t$, yields the limit of its signed thickness $r$ over $F$. The cap indicator tends to its old value at every point of the relative interior of $F$ off the old cutting plane. Those exclusions are null by the no-coincidence hypothesis. The projected slab is uniformly bounded and the thickness divided by $t$ is bounded by $|r|$, giving dominated convergence also along any bounded sequence of threshold speeds. In dimension one the slab is an interval at the moved endpoint and the facet has counting measure one. The old-body term follows by coarea. Proper parallel section areas are continuous at their threshold, by interpolating the old section with an interior point on either side and using compactness for the upper limit. A strictly saturated trace has derivative zero. A supporting trace of zero surface area has section areas tending to zero, by containment of all subsequential limits in the null support face after translating the cutting plane. Thus the old-body coefficient is exactly $-S_js$.

A proper section persists in every row because the old body has an interior ball crossing that row's plane. The nearby cap tail has a continuous, strictly decreasing inverse near $Q$. At mass $Q+tx$, the expansion brackets its threshold displacement divided by $t$ by arbitrarily small fixed increments about $(r\int_F\mathbf1_{H_j}-x)/S_j$. The weighted threshold sum is strictly decreasing in the common mass. Bracketing its zero displacement on both sides shows that the common budget derivative is $\sum_j\theta_j r\int_F\mathbf1_{H_j}$. This applies to either signed path and the formula is linear in $r$. The common threshold shifts preserve the assigned-plane cover, since the positive weighted margin stays nonnegative there. Subtracting $\beta Ar$ proves the response identity. The two strict menu tests and their negations follow from the sign of each row and the finite choices in each group. Persistent slack covers on all remaining bases and the finitely many strict first-order rows give the actual global normalized descent. Failure of the strict tests is a statement about these first-order certificates.
