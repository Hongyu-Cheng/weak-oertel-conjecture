## Statement

Let $K$ be a nondegenerate planar triangle with $y\in\operatorname{int}K$, and let nonzero spanning normals $v_1,v_2,v_3$ satisfy $\sum_i v_i=0$. For their caps through $y$ write $q_i=|K\cap\lbrace v_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\rbrace|/|K|$ and $\sigma_i=\mathcal H^1(K\cap\lbrace v_i\cdot(x-y)=0\rbrace)/\lVert v_i\rVert$. If $q_i\le128/279$ for all $i$ and $\sum_i q_i\le125/93$, then
$$
\min_i\frac{1/\sigma_i}{\sum_j 1/\sigma_j}>\frac{223}{1000}.
$$
In particular, the same bound holds under the individual cap ceiling and the sorted profile $q_{\min}+q_{\mathrm{mid}}+q_{\max}/4\le1$.

## Proof

Apply b08bdaf82b84ef41 with weights $\omega_i=1$ and cap ceiling $c=128/279<2\sqrt3-3$. The triangle is nondegenerate, the source is interior, and the normals span the plane and balance, so its conclusion gives distinct strict corner caps. The strict triangle cap-floor lemma now yields $q_i>20/49$.

Use the signed-row theorem for these same actual caps. It supplies positive $f_i$ with $\sum_i f_i\sqrt{q_i}=2$ and $f_i+(f_j+f_k)^2/4\le2$ for every $i$, and identifies each reciprocal-chord weight with $(f_i/\sqrt{q_i})/\sum_\ell(f_\ell/\sqrt{q_\ell})$. Together with the individual floor and ceiling and the given cap-sum bound, these are precisely the hypotheses of the scalar reciprocal-chord lemma. Its strict conclusion proves the bound. For the stated sorted profile, $\sum_i q_i\le1+3q_{\max}/4\le125/93$, so it implies the cap-sum premise.
