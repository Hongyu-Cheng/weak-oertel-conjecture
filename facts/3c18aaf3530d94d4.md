## Statement

Let $A>0,h>0$, and let $\Psi$ be a nondecreasing CDF equal to zero below $-1$ and one above $0$. Set $J(q)=\int_{-\infty}^q\Psi(s)\,ds$, $\kappa=\eta/h$, and $W=\Psi(\kappa)$. For sufficiently large $H$ so that the remote endpoints remain saturated near $t=0$, define the actual slab masses
$$
F_{\rm in}(t)=A\int_{tv}^{H}\Psi((x+\eta-tc)/h)\,dx,\qquad
F_{\rm out}(t)=A\int_{-H}^{-tv}\Psi((x+\eta-tc)/h)\,dx.
$$
At continuity of $\Psi$ at $\kappa$ their first losses are $-F_{\rm in}'(0)=A[vW+c(1-W)]$ and $-F_{\rm out}'(0)=A(v+c)W$. The relevant first formula also holds when its primitive argument is stationary ($v=c$ or $v=-c$, respectively), regardless of continuity. At differentiability of $\Psi$ at $\kappa$, the signed second-order loss coefficients (with denominator $t^2/2$ after removing the linear term) are respectively $+(A/h)\Psi'(\kappa)(v-c)^2$ and $-(A/h)\Psi'(\kappa)(v+c)^2$. These are classical second derivatives of the losses if $\Psi$ is also continuous near $\kappa$.

Without any regularity assumption, write $D_J(k,s)=J(k+s)+J(k-s)-2J(k)\ge0$. The exact centered losses $2F(0)-F(t)-F(-t)$ are respectively $Ah D_J(\kappa,t(v-c)/h)$ and $-Ah D_J(\kappa,t(v+c)/h)$. For bounded $\kappa$ and fixed sufficiently small $t$ as $h\downarrow0$, these tend to $A|t||v-c|$ and $-A|t||v+c|$. For the uniform outward ramp the signed second coefficients above use $\Psi'(\kappa)=1$ for $-1<\kappa<0$ and zero strictly outside; at $\kappa=-1,0$ the finite-difference identity remains exact, with the two one-sided responses given by the primitive.

## Proof

Substitute the affine argument in the integrals. The inner lower endpoint gives $-AhJ(\kappa+t(v-c)/h)$ and its saturated upper endpoint contributes an affine function of slope $-Ac$. The outer upper endpoint gives $AhJ(\kappa-t(v+c)/h)$ and its saturated lower endpoint contributes zero. At continuity $J'(\kappa)=W$, which gives both linear formulas by differentiation. When the relevant speed vanishes its primitive term is constant. Without continuity an actual two-sided first derivative may fail: for $\Psi=\mathbf1_{\{q\ge0\}}$, $\eta=c=0$, $v=1$, the inner mass is $A(H-\max(t,0))$.

Differentiability of $\Psi$ at $\kappa$ gives $J(\kappa+s)=J(\kappa)+Ws+\Psi'(\kappa)s^2/2+o(s^2)$ by integrating the pointwise first expansion. It gives the second-order expansion even when $J'$ is undefined at nearby jumps. If $\Psi$ is continuous on a neighborhood, the chain rule there and the derivative of $\Psi$ at $\kappa$ give the ordinary second derivatives at zero as well.

For centered losses the affine terms cancel exactly, and $J$ is convex by monotonicity. Since $J(q)-q_+$ is uniformly bounded, $hD_J(\kappa,tL/h)\to|tL|$ for bounded $\kappa$, including $L=0$ and fixed remote endpoints that are saturated uniformly for small $h$. This proves the sharp hinge limits. For the uniform ramp and small $s$, the exact primitives at the two kink offsets are $J(-1+s)=(s_+)^2/2$ and $J(s)=1/2+s+(s_-)^2/2$, with $s_-=\max(0,-s)$. Substitution gives both one-sided second coefficients; in both cases the centered $D_J$ is $s^2/2$ near zero. In general this centered response is not an ordinary two-sided Hessian at the kink.
