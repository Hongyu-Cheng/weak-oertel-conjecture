## Statement

There is a compact full-dimensional body in $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R$ of mixed length $M=72$ with a base $j=(1,0)$ of raw marginal depth exactly $M/4=18$, for which the balanced mass-$M/4$ moment interval has centroid $y=3241/432$. The point $(1,0,y)$ has a containing closed ambient cap of raw mass $3671/432<\gamma_1 M/4=9$. The maximum-marginal-depth base of the same body is $(1,1)$, with raw marginal depth $450/11$.

## Proof

Apply the extreme-layer preservation lemma to the planar counterexample with original mixed length $22$ and mark $1$, using continuous dilation $s=25/11$ in the new layer $r=1$. The exact fibers at $z=0,1,2$ are $[0,4],[2,16],[7,11]$ in layer zero and $[0,100/11],[50/11,400/11],[175/11,25]$ in layer one. Thus the row masses are $22,50$ and the new prescribed mass is $18$. The preserved balanced interval is $[19/3,1873/216]$. The halfspace $z-1-2r\ge0$ through $(1,0)$ contains precisely row-zero bases $1,2$; restriction to row zero gives the matching lower marginal depth $18$.

Use the closed ambient cap
$$
x\ge3241/432+(1513/432)(z-1)+40r.
$$
It passes through $(1,0,y)$ and is the planar certificate on layer zero, including its length-zero boundary trace. Its smallest threshold in layer one is $44$, greater than the largest fiber coordinate $400/11$. Therefore its total length is exactly $3671/432$, strictly below $9$.

At every sampled base, its marginal depth is its depth within its own layer by restriction and a finite tilt excluding the other layer. In the two rows, respectively, the three depths at $z=0,1,2$ are $(4,18,4)$ and $(100/11,450/11,100/11)$, by the two containing rays in each row. Other integer bases have depth zero because they lie outside the projected rectangle. Hence $(1,1)$ is the unique maximizing base. For this middle upper base, for example, $z-1+2(r-1)\ge0$ isolates the right marginal ray in layer one.
