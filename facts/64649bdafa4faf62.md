## Statement

Let $A=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\}$, $B=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(7/5,0),(0,1/100)\}$ and $K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(6/5,0),(7/10,1/2),(0,193/350)\}$. Then $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$, their areas are $a=1/2$, $b=7/1000$, $k=493/1000$ with $a=k+b$, and the ordinary maximum raw depth of $A$ is $(4/9)a$. There is no $x\in\mathbb R^2$ such that $2x-B\subseteq A$.

## Proof

Each triangle has the area given by half the product of its axis intercepts. The four points of $K$ are in strict convex cyclic order as listed, and the shoelace formula gives area $\tfrac12[(6/5)(1/2)+(7/10)(193/350)]=493/1000$. Summing endpoint vertices and taking the convex hull gives
$$
(A+B)/2=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(6/5,0),(7/10,1/2),(0,101/200)\}.
$$
Indeed the Minkowski sum of the two triangles is the hull of their nine vertex sums. At scale one half, the additional points lie on the coordinate edges or below the polygon's two upper edges. In particular the only off-axis nonvertex sum is $(1/2,1/200)$, which lies in the triangle with vertices $(0,0),(6/5,0),(0,101/200)$. The first three displayed vertices belong to $K$, and the last is on its vertical edge because $101/200<193/350$. The lifted hull therefore has the prescribed integer fibers, since the two endpoint weights must agree at base zero. The simplex case of 1a16e89734914fc8 §6 gives the exact ordinary maximum $(4/9)a$. Reflection and translation preserve horizontal width, so $2x-B$ has width $7/5$ for every $x$, larger than the width one of $A$. Containment would force the reverse width inequality.
