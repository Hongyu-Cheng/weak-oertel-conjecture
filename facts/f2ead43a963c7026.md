## Statement

Let \(P\subset\mathbb R^n\) be finite and nonempty, \(n\ge0\), and \(d\ge1\). For every \(z\in P\), let the convex bodies \(K_z^{(j)}\subset\mathbb R^d\) converge in Hausdorff distance to a convex body \(K_z^{(0)}\). Let \(M_j\) be their total \(d\)-volume, \(g_z^{(j)}\) their fiber centroids, and define the within-fiber covariance
$$
\Sigma_j=\frac1{M_j}\sum_{z\in P}\int_{K_z^{(j)}}(x-g_z^{(j)})(x-g_z^{(j)})^{\mathsf T}\,dx.
$$
Let \(\ell_j\ge0\) be continuous on the lifted union of the \(j\)-th fibers. Write \(\delta_j=\max_z d_H(K_z^{(j)},K_z^{(0)})\), and assume
$$
\sup_{\substack{p\in P,\ y_j\in K_p^{(j)},\ y\in K_p^{(0)}\\
\lVert y_j-y\rVert\le\delta_j}}\lvert\ell_j(p,y_j)-\ell_0(p,y)\rvert\longrightarrow0.
$$
Set \(f_j(p,y)=(\ell_j(p,y)E_{K_p^{(j)}}(y)/M_j)^{1/(d+1)}\), using the boundary value zero for the projective energy. For \(\epsilon_j\ge0\) with \(\epsilon_j\to\epsilon_0\), let \(b_j(p,y)\) be the infimum of the \(j\)-th summed fiber masses in the closed caps
$$
a\cdot(z-p)+u\cdot(x-y)+\epsilon_j f_j(p,y)\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_j u}\ge0,
\qquad (a,u)\ne(0,0).
$$
Then \(b_j(p,y_j)\to b_0(p,y)\) uniformly on the displayed correspondences. Each \(b_j\) attains a maximum on its lifted union, and both the maxima and the maxima divided by \(M_j\) converge. The comparison includes pure-base caps and arbitrary oblique base tilts. When \(P\) is the complete positive integer support of compatible actual fibers, the maxima are their actual mixed buffered maxima.

## Proof

The fibers eventually lie in a common ball. Hausdorff convergence to a convex body implies almost-everywhere convergence of its indicator, since off the boundary each interior point eventually belongs to the moving bodies and each exterior point eventually lies outside. Interior inclusion follows from uniform support-function convergence and a positive interior ball. Dominated convergence gives convergence of the volumes and all first and second moments. It also gives
$$
V_j=\sum_{z\in P}|K_z^{(j)}\mathbin{\triangle}K_z^{(0)}|\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus \(M_j\to M_0>0\) and \(\Sigma_j\to\Sigma_0\). Each covariance is positive definite because each full-dimensional fiber has positive variance in every nonzero continuous direction. The norms \(\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_j u}\) converge uniformly on the Euclidean unit sphere.

The marked-energy theorem for positive Hausdorff limits gives uniform convergence of the energies on the displayed correspondences. Otherwise choose a violating sequence of pairs. A subsequence has the same base and both marks converging to one point in its limiting body. Applying joint marked continuity to the moving and fixed bodies gives a contradiction. The assumption on \(\ell_j\), boundedness on these correspondences, and continuity of the positive root imply uniform convergence of \(f_j\) there. It follows that
$$
\Delta_j=\sup_{\substack{p,\ y_j,\ y\ \text{as above}\\\lVert u\rVert=1}}
\left|\epsilon_j f_j(p,y_j)\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_j u}
-\epsilon_0 f_0(p,y)\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_0u}\right|+\delta_j
\longrightarrow0 .
$$

If \(u\ne0\), divide all coefficients by \(\lVert u\rVert\). At each measured base the threshold difference between corresponding caps is at most \(\Delta_j\), since the source base is the same and the term \(a\cdot(z-p)\) cancels. Replacing the bodies at the same threshold changes the total cap mass by at most \(V_j\). Moving the continuous thresholds changes it by at most \(L\Delta_j\), where \(L\) is a common bound on the sum of maximal unit-normal fiber sections in the limiting family. Such a bound exists by containment in a ball, with unit zero-dimensional section mass when \(d=1\). Pure-base caps select exactly the same bases and cost at most \(V_j\). Taking the infimum over the same entire direction set gives the bound \(V_j+L\Delta_j\) for the difference of the two depths, uniformly on the correspondences.

At fixed \(j\), the same slab estimate, continuity of \(f_j\) on each fiber, and cancellation of the base tilt show that \(b_j(p,\cdot)\) is continuous on \(K_p^{(j)}\). Its maximum is attained on the finite union. Every mark in either family has a corresponding mark in the other within \(\delta_j\) at the same base. Comparing the maxima in both directions using the uniform bound proves convergence, and \(M_j\to M_0>0\) gives the normalized assertion.
