## Statement

Fix an integer \(J\ge1\) and a real number \(T\ge0\). Define the finite constant

$$
C(J,T)=
\begin{cases}
0,&T=0,\\
4T^2+512J\bigl(T^2+e^{6T}\bigr),&T>0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
c=\frac14e^{-C(J,T)},
\qquad
\gamma=\frac18e^{-3C(J,T)/2}=c^{3/2}.
$$

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and assign positive weights \(w_x\) to all \(x\in A\). Suppose that at most three distinct weight values occur. For every pair of distinct coordinatewise same-parity atoms \(u,v\in A\), require

$$
\frac{u+v}{2}\in A,
\qquad
w_{(u+v)/2}^{\,2}=w_uw_v,
\qquad
\left|\frac12\log\frac{w_v}{w_u}\right|\le T.
\tag{1}
$$

At most \(J\) occupied coordinatewise parity classes may contain different weight values. There is no restriction on the cardinalities of these classes, on the other occupied classes, or on the number of pairs with a given midpoint.

Retain the original normalization \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\) and law \(p_x=w_x/Z\), extended by zero off \(A\). The canonical full-row notation, with exactly the meanings in 31c9b0b9219d18d9, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P(A)&=\lbrace\eta\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n:
A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\ne\varnothing\rbrace,
&K&=|\mathcal P(A)|,\\
R_x&=\lbrace y\in A:2x-y\in A\rbrace,
&H_p(x)&=\sum_{y\in R_x}\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x)^2,
&\chi(p)&=\sum_{x\in A}p_xH_p(x),\\
\kappa_x(y)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_yp_{2x-y}}}{H_p(x)}\quad(y\in R_x),
&Y_x(y)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_y}{p_{2x-y}},\\
V_x&=\mathbb E_{\kappa_x}Y_x^2,
&q_x&=\frac{p_xH_p(x)^2}{b(p)},
\qquad \nu=\sum_{x\in A}q_xV_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Both orientations of every noncentral reflected pair belong to \(R_x\), and the central atom occurs once. Then

$$
K^2b(p)\ge1,
\qquad
\nu\le C(J,T)\log\bigl(K^2b(p)\bigr).
\tag{3}
$$

For the product conclusion, let \(L\ge1\) be an integer and take arbitrary factors \((A_\ell,w_\ell)\), in integer dimensions \(n_\ell\ge1\), satisfying these hypotheses with the same fixed \(J,T\). Different factors may have different supports, occupancies, dimensions, and weight values. Put

$$
Z_\ell=\sum_{x\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,x},
\qquad
p_{\ell,x}=w_{\ell,x}/Z_\ell,
\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L p_\ell,
\qquad
N=\sum_{\ell=1}^L n_\ell.
\tag{4}
$$

For a supported point \(\mathbf j\) of this original product law, let \(\tau_P(\mathbf j)\) be the infimum of \(P(H)\) over all ambient closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) containing \(\mathbf j\), and put \(M_k(P)=\sum_{\mathbf j}P_{\mathbf j}\tau_P(\mathbf j)^k\). Then

$$
M_2(P)\ge c\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2},
\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\gamma\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3}.
\tag{5}
$$

In particular, the single common coefficient \(\gamma\) gives \(M_2(P)\ge\gamma4^{-N}\) and \(M_3(P)\ge\gamma8^{-N}\). Singleton and lower-dimensional supports are included.

## Proof

We first derive the full-row identities and then prove the new price. This independently establishes their applicability without any occupancy bound. The notation agrees with 31c9b0b9219d18d9; no conclusion of its fixed-occupancy theorem is invoked outside that theorem's hypotheses. We also derive the extreme-level order bound underlying e7a5a5229669c789, since here the scaled weights need not be reciprocals of odd integers.

Fix one factor. Let \(\mathscr Q\) be the set of unordered pairs of distinct same-parity atoms. Choose either endpoint order for every \(e\in\mathscr Q\), and write

$$
j_e=\frac{u_e+v_e}{2},
\qquad
z_e=\frac12\log\frac{p_{v_e}}{p_{u_e}},
\qquad
\mathcal J=\lbrace j_e:e\in\mathscr Q\rbrace,
\qquad
m_x=|\lbrace e\in\mathscr Q:j_e=x\rbrace|,
\qquad
h_x=2m_x+1,
\qquad
t_x=Kp_x.
\tag{6}
$$

Every noncentral reflection orbit in \(R_x\) is exactly a pair in \(\mathscr Q\) with midpoint \(x\): its endpoints have the same parity because their sum is \(2x\). Different such orbits at one center use disjoint endpoints. The remaining orbit is the singleton \(\lbrace x\rbrace\). By (1), every geometric-mean summand in that row is \(p_x\), including its central summand. Thus \(|R_x|=h_x\), \(H_p(x)=h_xp_x\), and \(\kappa_x\) is uniform. In particular, \(b(p)>0\).

For \(\eta\in\mathcal P(A)\), define the occupied class and its probability and scaled masses by \(\mathcal C_\eta=A\cap(\eta+2\mathbb Z^n)\), \(r_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}p_x\), and \(R_\eta=Kr_\eta=\sum_{x\in\mathcal C_\eta}t_x\). Put \(d_\eta=(R_\eta-1)^2\). Expanding the parity squares and then using midpoint products gives

$$
\sum_\eta r_\eta^2
=\sum_xp_x^2+2\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}p_{u_e}p_{v_e}
=\sum_xh_xp_x^2
=\chi(p).
\tag{7}
$$

Retain the canonical energies

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathcal J}&=K^2b(p),
&\mathcal D&=K\sum_\eta r_\eta^2-1
=\frac1K\sum_\eta d_\eta\ge0,\\
\Psi_x&=t_x(h_xt_x-1)^2,
&\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}&=\sum_xp_x(KH_p(x)-1)^2
=\frac1K\sum_x\Psi_x,\\
\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}&=\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$

Indeed, \(\sum_\eta R_\eta=K\), so expansion gives the displayed formula and nonnegativity for \(\mathcal D\). Expanding \(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}\) and applying (7) yields

$$
\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}=A_{\mathcal J}-2K\chi(p)+1,
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}=1+\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D
=1+\mathcal E_{\mathcal J}\ge1.
\tag{9}
$$

Uniformity of the full row gives its score zero at \(x\) and scores \(z_e,-z_e\) at the two endpoints of every pair with midpoint \(x\). Consequently

$$
V_x=\frac2{h_x}\sum_{e:j_e=x}z_e^2,
\qquad
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
=K^2\sum_xp_xH_p(x)^2V_x
=\frac1K\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}2h_{j_e}t_{j_e}^3z_e^2.
\tag{10}
$$

If no pair has a nonzero score, this identity proves \(\nu=0\), and (3) follows from (9). This includes \(\mathscr Q=\varnothing\) and \(T=0\), with the asserted choice \(C(J,0)=0\). It also includes all laws with fewer than three levels: a pair of unequal endpoint weights would, by (1), require its strictly intermediate geometric mean as a third distinct level.

Suppose henceforth that some pair has nonzero score, so \(T>0\). Its midpoint weight is strictly between its two unequal endpoint weights. Since the entire support has at most three levels, there are exactly three, the pair's endpoints are the maximum and minimum levels, and its midpoint is the middle level. Let \(\mathrm H,\mathrm B,\mathrm L\) be the sets of atoms at the maximum, middle, and minimum levels, respectively, and let \(t_{\mathrm H}>t_{\mathrm B}>t_{\mathrm L}>0\) be their common scaled atom masses. Their exact relation is

$$
t_{\mathrm B}^{\,2}=t_{\mathrm H}t_{\mathrm L},
\qquad
\frac{t_{\mathrm H}}{t_{\mathrm B}}
=\frac{t_{\mathrm B}}{t_{\mathrm L}}=e^s,
\qquad
0<s=\frac12\log\frac{t_{\mathrm H}}{t_{\mathrm L}}\le T.
\tag{11}
$$

An \(\mathrm H\)-\(\mathrm B\) same-parity pair would require a supported weight strictly between those two adjacent levels; an \(\mathrm B\)-\(\mathrm L\) pair would do the same between the other two adjacent levels. Both are impossible. Therefore every heterogeneous occupied class consists only of \(\mathrm H\) and \(\mathrm L\) atoms, with both levels present. All pairs with nonzero score are precisely its \(\mathrm H\)-\(\mathrm L\) pairs, their midpoints belong to \(\mathrm B\), and their squared scores are \(s^2\). No global affine representation of the weights is needed for this conclusion.

We will bound the sum on the right of (10) before its division by \(K\). First take a center \(x\) with \(h_xt_x>2\). The number of its nonzero-score pairs is at most \(m_x=(h_x-1)/2\). Since every score is bounded by \(T\), its entire contribution obeys

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e:j_e=x}2h_xt_x^3z_e^2
&\le T^2h_x(h_x-1)t_x^3\\
&\le T^2t_x(h_xt_x)^2
\le4T^2t_x(h_xt_x-1)^2
=4T^2\Psi_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$

Here \(a^2\le4(a-1)^2\) for \(a>2\). This charges each such center once, regardless of midpoint multiplicity.

Every remaining nonzero-score pair has a \(\mathrm B\) midpoint with \(h_xt_{\mathrm B}\le2\), so its coefficient satisfies \(2h_xt_{\mathrm B}^3\le4t_{\mathrm B}^2\). For each heterogeneous class set

$$
U_\eta=|\mathcal C_\eta\cap\mathrm H|t_{\mathrm H},
\qquad
W_\eta=|\mathcal C_\eta\cap\mathrm L|t_{\mathrm L},
\qquad
R_\eta=U_\eta+W_\eta.
\tag{13}
$$

There are exactly \(|\mathcal C_\eta\cap\mathrm H|\,|\mathcal C_\eta\cap\mathrm L|\) nonzero-score pairs in this class. By (11), its remaining contribution is at most \(4s^2U_\eta W_\eta\). This overcounts any pairs already charged in (12), and hence is an upper bound.

Call this class balanced when \(R_\eta\in[1/2,2]\). For an unbalanced class, \(U_\eta W_\eta\le R_\eta^2/4\), and \(R_\eta^2\le4(R_\eta-1)^2\): for \(R_\eta<1/2\) one has \(R_\eta\le1-R_\eta\), and for \(R_\eta>2\) one has \(R_\eta\le2(R_\eta-1)\). Therefore the total remaining contribution from unbalanced classes is at most

$$
\sum_{\substack{\eta\text{ heterogeneous}\\R_\eta\notin[1/2,2]}}
4s^2U_\eta W_\eta
\le4T^2\sum_\eta d_\eta.
\tag{14}
$$

For a balanced class, \(U_\eta W_\eta\le R_\eta^2/4\le1\) and \(U_\eta W_\eta\le2U_\eta\). Let \(N_{\mathrm H}=|\mathrm H|\) and let \(S_{\mathrm H}=N_{\mathrm H}t_{\mathrm H}\) be the scaled mass of the entire maximum level. The heterogeneous classes are disjoint and number at most \(J\); thus

$$
\sum_{\substack{\eta\text{ heterogeneous}\\R_\eta\in[1/2,2]}}
U_\eta W_\eta
\le\min\lbrace J,2S_{\mathrm H}\rbrace.
\tag{15}
$$

We next pay the balanced contribution using actual spatial rows. At a center in an extreme weight level, every endpoint in its full row belongs to the same level. For the maximum level, both endpoint weights are at most the central weight and their product equals its square, forcing both to equal it. For the minimum level the inequalities reverse and give the same conclusion.

Choose a real linear functional taking distinct values on all atoms of \(A\); the excluded functionals form finitely many proper hyperplanes. Order the atoms of any extreme level of cardinality \(N_0\) by this functional. At its atom of rank \(r\), every noncentral row pair has exactly one lower-ranked and one higher-ranked endpoint, because their functional values average to the central value. Different reflected pairs use disjoint endpoints. Thus the actual full row satisfies

$$
h_x\le2\min\lbrace r-1,N_0-r\rbrace+1.
\tag{16}
$$

In particular the first atom of each nonempty extreme level has \(h_x=1\). This is the spatial bound from e7a5a5229669c789, here obtained directly for these rows.

First suppose \(t_{\mathrm H}\le1/4\), and set \(L_0=\lceil1/(4t_{\mathrm H})\rceil\). Each of the first \(\min\lbrace N_{\mathrm H},L_0\rbrace\) ranks of \(\mathrm H\) has, by (16),

$$
h_xt_{\mathrm H}
\le(2r-1)t_{\mathrm H}
\le(2L_0-1)t_{\mathrm H}
\le\frac12+t_{\mathrm H}
\le\frac34.
\tag{17}
$$

The ceiling inequality used here is \(L_0\le1/(4t_{\mathrm H})+1\), so it includes integral ceiling arguments and the endpoint \(t_{\mathrm H}=1/4\). Each such atom has \(\Psi_x\ge t_{\mathrm H}/16\), and \(L_0t_{\mathrm H}\ge1/4\). Consequently

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in A}\Psi_x
&\ge\frac1{16}\min\lbrace N_{\mathrm H}t_{\mathrm H},L_0t_{\mathrm H}\rbrace\\
&\ge\frac1{16}\min\lbrace S_{\mathrm H},1/4\rbrace
\ge\frac1{64}\min\lbrace S_{\mathrm H},1\rbrace.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$

This also covers \(N_{\mathrm H}=1\). Since \(J\ge1\), one has \(\min\lbrace J,2S_{\mathrm H}\rbrace\le2J\min\lbrace S_{\mathrm H},1\rbrace\): use the second argument of the minimum if \(S_{\mathrm H}\le1\), and the first otherwise. Equations (15) and (18) now show that the balanced contribution is at most

$$
4s^2\min\lbrace J,2S_{\mathrm H}\rbrace
\le8JT^2\min\lbrace S_{\mathrm H},1\rbrace
\le512JT^2\sum_x\Psi_x.
\tag{19}
$$

Now suppose \(t_{\mathrm H}>1/4\). Put \(\rho=e^{-2T}/4\). Equation (11) gives \(t_{\mathrm L}=t_{\mathrm H}e^{-2s}\ge\rho\), and also \(t_{\mathrm H}\ge\rho\). Choose the first atom of \(\mathrm H\) and the first atom of \(\mathrm L\) in the strict ordering above. They are distinct atoms and both have \(h_x=1\) by (16). It follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_x\Psi_x
&\ge t_{\mathrm H}(t_{\mathrm H}-1)^2
+t_{\mathrm L}(t_{\mathrm L}-1)^2\\
&\ge\rho\bigl((t_{\mathrm H}-1)^2+(t_{\mathrm L}-1)^2\bigr)
\ge\frac\rho2(t_{\mathrm H}-t_{\mathrm L})^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
$$

Moreover,

$$
2s=\log t_{\mathrm H}-\log t_{\mathrm L}
=\int_{t_{\mathrm L}}^{t_{\mathrm H}}\frac{du}{u}
\le\frac{t_{\mathrm H}-t_{\mathrm L}}{t_{\mathrm L}},
\qquad
t_{\mathrm H}-t_{\mathrm L}\ge2\rho s.
\tag{21}
$$

Combining (20) and (21) gives \(\sum_x\Psi_x\ge2\rho^3s^2\). By (15), the balanced contribution is therefore at most

$$
4Js^2
\le2J\rho^{-3}\sum_x\Psi_x
=128Je^{6T}\sum_x\Psi_x.
\tag{22}
$$

This comparison retains the factor \(s^2\) when the three levels approach equality; it does not require any positive lower bound on their separation.

From (19) and (22), in either case the balanced contribution is at most \(512J(T^2+e^{6T})\sum_x\Psi_x\). Adding (12) and (14), with each large center charged once and each class charged once, yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in\mathscr Q}2h_{j_e}t_{j_e}^3z_e^2
&\le C(J,T)\sum_x\Psi_x+4T^2\sum_\eta d_\eta,\\
A_{\mathcal J}\nu
&\le C(J,T)\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+4T^2\mathcal D
\le C(J,T)\bigl(\mathcal V_{\mathcal J}+2\mathcal D\bigr)
=C(J,T)(A_{\mathcal J}-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
$$

For the second line use (8), (10), \(\mathcal D\ge0\), and \(C(J,T)\ge4T^2\). The extreme-level atoms used to pay balanced classes can also be endpoints or centers elsewhere; their energies occur in the single sum in (23), with the displayed combined coefficient. No energy has been charged separately for every incident pair. Dividing by \(A_{\mathcal J}\ge1\) and using \((a-1)/a\le\log a\) for \(a\ge1\) proves (3). If \(A_{\mathcal J}=1\), (23) directly gives \(\nu=0\).

We finish by deriving the original-product conclusion. This reproduces the full-row consumer used in 31c9b0b9219d18d9, rather than applying that theorem's occupancy-dependent conclusion. For a supported product center \(\mathbf j=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\) and row point \(\mathbf x=(x_1,\ldots,x_L)\), the full row and its geometric law factor exactly:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathbf j}&=\prod_{\ell=1}^L R_{\ell,j_\ell},
&H_P(\mathbf j)&=\prod_{\ell=1}^L H_{p_\ell}(j_\ell),
&\kappa_{\mathbf j}&=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L\kappa_{\ell,j_\ell},\\
Y_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L Y_{\ell,j_\ell}(x_\ell),
&\sigma_{\mathbf j}^{2}&=\sum_{\ell=1}^L V_{\ell,j_\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
$$

Here \(\sigma_{\mathbf j}\) is the nonnegative square root. Reflection preserves each factor row law and negates its score. Each factor score therefore has mean zero, including singleton rows. Independence in (24) gives \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}}Y_{\mathbf j}^2=\sigma_{\mathbf j}^{2}\). Directly from the definitions, on the full product row,

$$
P_{\mathbf x}
=H_P(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)e^{Y_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)},
\qquad
P_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}
=H_P(\mathbf j)\kappa_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)e^{-Y_{\mathbf j}(\mathbf x)}.
\tag{25}
$$

These are likelihood identities for the original law \(P\) of (4).

Every closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf j\) contains at least one endpoint of every noncentral reflection orbit \(\lbrace\mathbf x,2\mathbf j-\mathbf x\rbrace\), because excluding both strictly would exclude their midpoint. It also contains the central atom \(\mathbf j\). The orbits are disjoint. Counting both orientations of each noncentral orbit and half of the available central mass gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_P(\mathbf j)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{\mathbf x\in R_{\mathbf j}}
\min\lbrace P_{\mathbf x},P_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf x}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(\mathbf j)}2
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}}e^{-|Y_{\mathbf j}|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P(\mathbf j)}2
e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_{\mathbf j}}|Y_{\mathbf j}|}
\ge\frac{H_P(\mathbf j)}2e^{-\sigma_{\mathbf j}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$

The last line uses Jensen for the convex exponential and then Cauchy--Schwarz. It remains valid for halfspaces whose boundary contains row points and for lower-dimensional supports.

Let \(q=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^L q_\ell\), where each \(q_\ell\) is the auxiliary center law in (2). The exact factorization is

$$
P_{\mathbf j}H_P(\mathbf j)^2
=\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)\right)q_{\mathbf j},
\qquad
\mathbb E_q\sigma_{\mathbf j}^{2}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\nu_\ell.
\tag{27}
$$

In particular the reflected factor energy, in the original unnormalized weights, is

$$
b(p_\ell)=\frac1{Z_\ell^3}
\sum_{j\in A_\ell}w_{\ell,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{\ell,j}}
\sqrt{w_{\ell,x}w_{\ell,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{28}
$$

Squaring (26), summing under the original law \(P\), and then applying Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz under \(q\) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)\right)
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_{\mathbf j}}\\
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\mathbb E_q\sigma_{\mathbf j}^{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L b(p_\ell)\right)
\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_{\ell=1}^L\nu_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
$$

No original law has been conditioned or replaced; \(q\) is used only through the exact relation (27). Set \(s_{\log}=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\log(K_\ell^2b(p_\ell))\ge0\). Applying (3) separately to the factors gives \(\sum_\ell\nu_\ell\le C(J,T)s_{\log}\). Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2}\right)
\exp\left(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C(J,T)s_{\log}}\right)\\
&\ge\frac14e^{-C(J,T)}\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
$$

For the last inequality, \(s_{\log}-2\sqrt{C(J,T)s_{\log}}=(\sqrt{s_{\log}}-\sqrt{C(J,T)})^2-C(J,T)\ge-C(J,T)\), also when \(C(J,T)=0\). Thus the coefficient is paid once for the whole product. Finally, Jensen under the same original probability \(P\) gives

$$
M_3(P)
=\mathbb E_P\bigl(\tau_P(\mathbf j)^2\bigr)^{3/2}
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}
\ge\frac18e^{-3C(J,T)/2}\prod_{\ell=1}^L K_\ell^{-3}.
\tag{31}
$$

This proves (5). Since \(K_\ell\le2^{n_\ell}\) and \(0<\gamma=c^{3/2}\le c\), both ambient estimates follow with the common coefficient \(\gamma\). All constants depend only on \(J,T\).
