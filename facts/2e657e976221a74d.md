## Statement

Let $K$ be a nondegenerate planar triangle, with an interior point $y$ and three caps cut by lines through $y$ that each strictly capture a different vertex and strictly exclude the other two. Let $q_i$ be the ordinary area fractions of the caps, indexed by the captured vertices. If $q_i\le128/279$ for all $i$, then each barycentric coordinate of $y$ is less than $5/14$, and each $q_i>20/49$.

## Proof

Write $\alpha_i$ for the barycentric coordinate of $y$ at vertex $P_i$. For the cap at $P_i$, let $r,u\in(0,1)$ be the intercept fractions along the edges from $P_i$ to $P_j,P_k$. Since the cut has edge intercepts at those fractions, the through-source condition is $\alpha_j/r+\alpha_k/u=1$ and $q_i=ru$. In particular $q_i\ge4\alpha_j\alpha_k$ by arithmetic–geometric mean.

If $\alpha_j\ge1/2$, the same formula gives $q_i=\alpha_k r^2/(r-\alpha_j)$ for $\alpha_j<r<1$. The derivative of $r^2/(r-\alpha_j)$ is $r(r-2\alpha_j)/(r-\alpha_j)^2\le0$, so $q_i\ge\alpha_k/(1-\alpha_j)$. Applying this to the two caps at vertices other than $P_j$ makes their fractions sum to at least one, contrary to $q_i\le128/279<1/2$. Thus $z=\max_i\alpha_i<1/2$.

Of the other two coordinates, at least one is at least $(1-z)/2$. Using the cap whose excluded vertices carry those two specified coordinates gives $2z(1-z)\le128/279$. The function $2z(1-z)$ is increasing up to $1/2$ and has value $45/98>128/279$ at $5/14$. Thus $z<5/14$. For distinct $j,k$, $\alpha_j+\alpha_k>9/14$ and $(5/14-\alpha_j)(5/14-\alpha_k)>0$. Expanding the product gives $\alpha_j\alpha_k>5/49$, so $q_i\ge4\alpha_j\alpha_k>20/49$ for each $i$.
