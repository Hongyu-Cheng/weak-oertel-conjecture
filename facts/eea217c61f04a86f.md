## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ and let $A,K,B$ be convex bodies in $\mathbb R^d$ of volumes $a,k,b$, with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$ and $a=k+b$. Use the actual lifted hull at bases $-1,0,1$, with the sum of fiber volumes and closed ambient halfspaces. Write $\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, suppose the ordinary maximum raw depth of $A$ equals $\gamma_da$, and let $D$ be the maximum raw mixed depth on the middle fiber. If $D<\gamma_da$, then for every $0<\Delta<\min\{2b,2(\gamma_da-D)/\gamma_d\}$ and every $a_0\in A$, replace $A$ by $a_0+(1-\Delta/a)^{1/d}(A-a_0)$. The resulting actual three-fiber body has total mass $2a-\Delta$, all three volumes less than half that mass, and every mixed point has depth strictly below $\gamma_d(a-\Delta/2)$. If the original bodies are rational polytopes, the contraction can be chosen rational.

## Proof

The lifted hull has the prescribed fibers. An endpoint uses only its own base in any convex representation. At base zero the two endpoint weights agree, so their combined term lies in their Minkowski midpoint, which is contained in $K$. Two distinct bases with full-dimensional fibers give a full-dimensional hull. The same construction applies when $A$ is replaced by a positive-volume subset. The attainment argument in 03f071403cdfc69f gives upper semicontinuity of depth for the finite summed fiber measure, so its restriction to the compact middle fiber attains $D$.

Brunn–Minkowski and arithmetic-geometric mean give $k\ge[(a^{1/d}+b^{1/d})/2]^d\ge\sqrt{ab}$. Thus $k^2\ge b(k+b)$, yielding $b/k\le(\sqrt5-1)/2<2/3$. In particular $2b<a$. The ordinary maximum depth of $B$ is at most $b/2$ by the two opposite through-point cuts, which share only a null hyperplane. Hence it is less than $k/3<\gamma_d k$. The latter inequality follows from $(1+1/d)^d\le\sum_{j=0}^d1/j!<3$, using $j!\ge2^{j-1}$ for $j\ge2$ and the finite geometric sum.

Set $r=(1-\Delta/a)^{1/d}\in(0,1)$. The contracted endpoint $A_r=a_0+r(A-a_0)$ is contained in $A$, so midpoint compatibility persists. Its volume is $a-\Delta$, and the new total mass is $2a-\Delta$. Every closed-cap mass at any middle point weakly decreases, so the new middle maximum is at most $D<\gamma_d(a-\Delta/2)$. Both endpoints are exposed at bases $-1$ and $1$. By 1a16e89734914fc8 §6 their mixed depths are pointwise equal to their ordinary depths, since a sufficiently large base tilt isolates any prescribed ordinary cut. Homothety scales all ordinary cap masses by $r^d$. Thus the new $A_r$ maximum is $\gamma_d(a-\Delta)<\gamma_d(a-\Delta/2)$. The unchanged $B$ maximum is less than $\gamma_d k<\gamma_d(a-\Delta/2)$, where the last strict inequality uses $\Delta<2b$.

The half-total is $a-\Delta/2$. It strictly exceeds $a-\Delta$ and $k=a-b$, and $b<k$. Finally, for rational polytopes choose a rational point $a_0$ of $A$ and a rational $r<1$ sufficiently close to one. The decrement $a(1-r^d)$ then meets the prescribed strict bounds.
