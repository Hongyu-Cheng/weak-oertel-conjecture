## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of positive mixed volume, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
\mu_C(B):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in B\},
\qquad
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
and
\[
h_S(y):=\inf_{H\ni y}\mu_C(H),
\qquad
h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{y\in S}h_S(y),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\]
where the infimum is over ambient closed halfspaces. For every positive-volume fiber, let
\[
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx,
\qquad
\mathcal K_z:=\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z),
\qquad
D_z:=\mathcal K_z-c_z.
\]

Retain one common instantiation of the canonical zero-fresh target system of accepted fact `6bfe50fed9a5540b`, including its half-strength reservoir \(r=(r_e)\) and total reservoir mass \(Q:=\sum_e r_e>0\). Its first-generation reservoir edges \(e=\{u,v\}\) have physical targets \(m_e=(u+v)/2\), positive-root masses
\[
\vartheta_e:=2^{-(d+1)}
\left(a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}\right)^d,
\]
and scaled missed loads \(\alpha_e\). For every physical target \(m\), put
\[
t_m:=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\alpha_e.
\]
The predecessor conclusions used here are
\[
\sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}\leq1
\quad(t_m>0),
\qquad
0\leq\lambda_m\leq t_m,
\qquad
\Lambda:=\sum_m\lambda_m>\frac{49}{50}Q,
\tag{1}
\]
and, whenever \(\lambda_m>0\), its target support point \(z_m\) satisfies
\[
z_m\in\mathsf M_{\lambda_m}(C_m).
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(\mathcal M:=\{m\in\mathbb Z^n:t_m>0\}\), and set \(t_m=\lambda_m=0\) away from this finite target family. The symbol \(z_m\) in (2) is the predecessor's continuous target point, not a lattice-fiber index. The symbols \(a_m,c_m,\mathcal K_m,D_m\) below always denote the canonical data of the actual physical fiber \(C_m\).

Then, for every \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\),
\[
\boxed{0\leq\lambda_m\leq t_m\leq\frac{a_m}{2}.}
\tag{3}
\]

For \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), define
\[
B_\xi:=\sum_{\substack{m\in\mathcal M\\m\equiv\xi}}\lambda_m,
\qquad
\beta_\xi:=\max_{\substack{m\in\mathcal M\\m\equiv\xi}}\lambda_m,
\qquad
U_\xi:=(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+,
\qquad
U:=\sum_\xi U_\xi,
\tag{4}
\]
with \(\beta_\xi=0\) for an empty class. There is a finite nonnegative matching \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\) on unordered off-diagonal same-parity pairs \(f=\{m,m'\}\) such that, with
\[
d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}}):=\sum_{f\ni m}s_f^{\mathrm{tar}},
\qquad
u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\lambda_m-d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}}),
\]
one has
\[
0\leq d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\leq\lambda_m,
\qquad
\sum_{m\equiv\xi}u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}=U_\xi,
\tag{5}
\]
and the exact identities
\[
\boxed{
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})=\Lambda-U,
\qquad
|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|:=\sum_fs_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
=\frac{\Lambda-U}{2}.}
\tag{6}
\]
If \(U_\xi>0\), there is a unique \(m_\xi\equiv\xi\) with \(\lambda_{m_\xi}=\beta_\xi\), and the matching may be chosen so that
\[
u_{m_\xi}^{\mathrm{tar}}=U_\xi,
\qquad
u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}=0
\quad(m\equiv\xi,\ m\ne m_\xi).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(U_\xi=0\), all loads in that class are matched exactly.
Moreover, \(U_\xi\) is the minimum possible total unmatched load in class \(\xi\) among all off-diagonal matchings whose degrees are bounded by \((\lambda_m)_{m\equiv\xi}\). Thus (5)-(7) give the exact minimum-unmatched parity formula, not only one feasible rematching.

The unmatched mass gives the depth alternative
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\max_\xi U_\xi
\geq\gamma_d 2^{-n}U.}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, if \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|=0\), then \(U=\Lambda\) and (8) is an immediate positive depth certificate. If \(U=0\), then \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|=\Lambda/2>0\), so every target load is matched and the construction below applies.

Suppose henceforth that \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|>0\), and let
\[
\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\{f:s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}>0\}.
\]
For \(f=\{m,m'\}\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}\), define the second-generation midpoint and the uniform-fiber centroid midpoint by
\[
\widehat m_f:=\frac{m+m'}2\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad
\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\frac{c_m+c_{m'}}2.
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(c_m,c_{m'}\) are the centroids of the uniform measures on the full fibers \(C_m,C_{m'}\); they are not the predecessor target support points \(z_m,z_{m'}\). Put
\[
T_f(x,x'):=\left(\widehat m_f,\frac{x+x'}2\right),
\]
\[
\nu_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
:=(T_f)_\#\left(
\frac{s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}}{a_ma_{m'}}
\mathcal H_d|_{C_m}\otimes\mathcal H_d|_{C_{m'}}
\right),
\qquad
\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}
\nu_f^{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\) is source-feasible in the exact sense
\[
d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\leq\frac{a_m}{2}
\quad\text{for every source fiber }m,
\tag{11}
\]
and accepted fact `68a56094b7617ea9` applies without rescaling. Consequently,
\[
|\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}|=|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}\subseteq S,
\qquad
\boxed{2\nu^{\mathrm{tar}}(H)\leq\mu_C(H)}
\tag{12}
\]
for every ambient closed halfspace \(H\). Each normalized component
\(\nu_f^{\mathrm{tar}}/s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\) has a log-concave density on its target fiber and continuous centroid \(\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\).

If the uniform-fiber centroid midpoints have a common affine chart, namely if there are \(A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^d\) such that
\[
A\widehat m_f+b=\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
\quad(f\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}),
\tag{13}
\]
then accepted fact `68a56094b7617ea9` gives a point \(y\in S\) with
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)\geq h_S(y)
\geq\frac{2}{e}\,2^{-n}|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|
=\frac1e\,2^{-n}(\Lambda-U).}
\tag{14}
\]

For the approximate-affine alternative, put
\[
D_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\frac12(D_m+D_{m'}),
\qquad
L_f^{\mathrm{tar}}:=\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}+D_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
=\frac12(\mathcal K_m+\mathcal K_{m'}),
\tag{15}
\]
and use exactly the affine-defect normalization of accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b`:
\[
\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
:=\min_{a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^d\ \mathrm{affine}}
\sum_{f\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}
\left(
\|a(\widehat m_f)-\bar c_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\|_{D_f^{\mathrm{tar}}}-1
\right)_+.
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}
\left(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|-5\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\right)_+.}
\tag{17}
\]
In particular, if
\(\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\leq|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|/10\), then
\[
\boxed{
h_{\max}(C)
\geq\frac{\gamma_d}{4}\,2^{-n}|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|
=\frac{\gamma_d}{8}\,2^{-n}(\Lambda-U).}
\tag{18}
\]

Finally, define the source and midpoint base laws of this genuine descendant by
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{src}}
:=\frac1{2|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\delta_m,
\qquad
\pi_{\mathrm{mid}}
:=\frac1{|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_{f\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}
s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\delta_{\widehat m_f}.
\tag{19}
\]
They are probability measures with the same mean
\[
\bar m
:=\frac1{2|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})m
=\frac1{|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_{f\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}
s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\widehat m_f.
\tag{20}
\]
Writing
\[
V_{\mathrm{src}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
:=\frac1{2|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\|m-\bar m\|_2^2,
\]
\[
V_{\mathrm{mid}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
:=\frac1{|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_{f=\{m,m'\}\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}
s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\|\widehat m_f-\bar m\|_2^2,
\]
one has the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
V_{\mathrm{src}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
-V_{\mathrm{mid}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
=\frac1{4|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_{f=\{m,m'\}\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{tar}}}
s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\|m-m'\|_2^2
\geq1.}
\tag{21}
\]
This is a variance decrease for the source law and the actual midpoint descendant law in (19). It does not assert that the descendant law is already another canonical matching state of comparable mass. In particular, (17) leaves the large-affine-defect case open, and (21) is not a rank-descent or final-closure statement.

## Proof

We first prove (3). Fix a target \(m\) with \(t_m>0\), and let \(e=\{u,v\}\) be any first-generation edge with \(m_e=m\) and \(\alpha_e>0\). Convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
\frac12(C_u+C_v)\subseteq C_m.
\tag{22}
\]
The exact Brunn-Minkowski inequality recorded and audited in accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b`, applied to the positive-volume convex fibers in (22), gives
\[
a_m^{1/d}
\geq\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2.
\tag{23}
\]
Therefore
\[
\vartheta_e
=\frac12\left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2\right)^d
\leq\frac{a_m}{2}.
\tag{24}
\]
Using (1),
\[
t_m
=\sum_{e:m_e=m}\vartheta_e
\frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}
\leq\frac{a_m}{2}
\sum_{e:m_e=m}\frac{\alpha_e}{\vartheta_e}
\leq\frac{a_m}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Accepted fact `6bfe50fed9a5540b` gives \(0\leq\lambda_m\leq t_m\). If \(t_m=0\), the declared extension gives \(\lambda_m=0\), and (3) is immediate. This proves (3), including the zero-load boundary.

We next construct the matching and prove the exact unmatched formula. Work in one parity class \(\xi\). If \(U_\xi=0\), set
\[
u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}:=0,
\qquad
r_m:=\lambda_m
\quad(m\equiv\xi).
\tag{26}
\]
Then \(\max_m r_m=\beta_\xi\leq B_\xi/2\). If \(U_\xi>0\), then \(\beta_\xi>B_\xi/2\), so the maximizer \(m_\xi\) is unique. Set
\[
u_{m_\xi}^{\mathrm{tar}}:=U_\xi=2\beta_\xi-B_\xi,
\qquad
u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}:=0\quad(m\ne m_\xi),
\]
\[
r_{m_\xi}:=B_\xi-\beta_\xi,
\qquad
r_m:=\lambda_m\quad(m\ne m_\xi).
\tag{27}
\]
In both cases
\[
r_m=\lambda_m-u_m^{\mathrm{tar}}\geq0,
\qquad
R_\xi:=\sum_{m\equiv\xi}r_m=B_\xi-U_\xi,
\qquad
\max_{m\equiv\xi}r_m\leq\frac{R_\xi}{2}.
\tag{28}
\]
For (27), the heavy value equals \(B_\xi-\beta_\xi=R_\xi/2\), and every other value is at most the sum \(B_\xi-\beta_\xi\) of all nonheavy values. This also covers a singleton class, when \(R_\xi=0\).

It remains to realize the prescribed degrees \((r_m)\) without diagonal edges. If \(R_\xi=0\), use no edge in this class. If \(R_\xi>0\), partition a circle of circumference \(R_\xi\) into half-open consecutive arcs \(I_m\) of lengths \(r_m\), omitting zero-length arcs, and let \(\tau(x):=x+R_\xi/2\pmod{R_\xi}\). Since every \(r_m\leq R_\xi/2\), almost no point of \(I_m\) has its image under \(\tau\) in the same arc. For distinct \(m,m'\), define
\[
s_{\{m,m'\}}^{\mathrm{tar}}
:=\operatorname{Leb}\{x\in I_m:\tau(x)\in I_{m'}\}.
\tag{29}
\]
The right side is symmetric in \(m,m'\), because \(\tau\) is a measure-preserving involution. The arcs partition the circle up to endpoints, so
\[
\sum_{m'\ne m}s_{\{m,m'\}}^{\mathrm{tar}}
=\operatorname{Leb}(I_m)=r_m.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus (29) gives an off-diagonal matching with the prescribed degrees. Combining the independently constructed matchings over all parity classes proves (5) and (7). Summing degrees gives
\[
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
=\sum_\xi(B_\xi-U_\xi)=\Lambda-U.
\tag{31}
\]
Every unordered edge contributes twice to the left side, which proves (6).

For completeness, this unmatched total is minimal. If \(B_\xi=0\), every admissible degree is zero and the assertion is immediate. Suppose \(B_\xi>0\). Consider any off-diagonal matching in class \(\xi\), let its total edge weight be \(L_\xi\), and choose a vertex carrying load \(\beta_\xi\). Trivially \(L_\xi\leq B_\xi/2\). Every edge has at least one endpoint other than this chosen vertex. The sum of all matched degrees outside the chosen vertex is therefore at least \(L_\xi\), while its available load is at most \(B_\xi-\beta_\xi\). Hence
\[
L_\xi\leq B_\xi-\beta_\xi.
\tag{32}
\]
Therefore
\[
2L_\xi
\leq2\min\left\{\frac{B_\xi}{2},B_\xi-\beta_\xi\right\}
=B_\xi-(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+
=B_\xi-U_\xi.
\tag{33}
\]
Every such matching leaves at least \(B_\xi-2L_\xi\geq U_\xi\) unmatched, while (29)-(30) attain equality. This proves the asserted minimum-unmatched formula.

We prove the unmatched-depth alternative. Suppose \(U_\xi>0\). Then \(\lambda_{m_\xi}=\beta_\xi>0\), so (2) gives
\[
z_{m_\xi}\in\mathsf M_{\lambda_{m_\xi}}(C_{m_\xi}).
\]
Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` says that every closed halfspace in the continuous fiber containing \(z_{m_\xi}\) has \(C_{m_\xi}\)-volume at least \(\gamma_d\lambda_{m_\xi}\). If an ambient closed halfspace \(H\) contains \((m_\xi,z_{m_\xi})\), its section in the fiber \(m_\xi\) is such a closed halfspace, possibly the whole fiber. Hence
\[
\mu_C(H)
\geq\gamma_d\lambda_{m_\xi}
\geq\gamma_dU_\xi.
\tag{34}
\]
This proves \(h_{\max}(C)\geq\gamma_d\max_\xi U_\xi\). There are \(2^n\) parity classes, so \(\max_\xi U_\xi\geq2^{-n}U\) when \(U>0\). When \(U=0\), the second inequality in (8) is the trivial nonnegativity bound. Thus (8) holds in every case. Equation (6) also shows that \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|=0\) is equivalent to \(U=\Lambda\). Since \(\Lambda>0\), this zero-matching boundary is already covered by (8). If \(U=0\), (6) gives \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|=\Lambda/2>0\), as claimed.

Assume now that \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|>0\). A positive edge \(f=\{m,m'\}\) has positive degree at both endpoints, hence \(\lambda_m,\lambda_{m'}>0\). By (3), both endpoint fibers have positive volume. Moreover,
\[
d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
\leq\lambda_m
\leq\frac{a_m}{2}.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus every hypothesis of accepted fact `68a56094b7617ea9` holds for the matching \(s^{\mathrm{tar}}\), with its predecessor source symbols \(u,v,m_e,s_e\) replaced respectively by the present physical targets \(m,m'\), second-generation midpoint \(\widehat m_f\), and weight \(s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\). That accepted theorem gives exactly (10)-(12), including support in \(S\), simultaneous domination for every ambient closed halfspace, component log-concavity, and component centroid \((c_m+c_{m'})/2\). Under (13), its affine-centroid conclusion gives
\[
h_{\max}(C)
\geq\frac{2}{e}\,2^{-n}|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|.
\]
Substitution of (6) proves (14). Notice that the chart condition uses centroids of the uniform source-fiber measures, exactly as required by `68a56094b7617ea9`; no target contact point \(z_m\) is substituted for \(c_m\).

For the approximate-affine estimate, accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b` gives \(\gamma_d\leq1/2\). Define the rescaled matching
\[
\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}}:=2\gamma_d s^{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{36}
\]
Equation (35) gives
\[
d_m(\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}})
=2\gamma_d d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
\leq\gamma_da_m,
\tag{37}
\]
so the matching hypothesis of accepted fact `11e347c295e9430b` is satisfied. The bodies and midpoint symbols in (15)-(16) are precisely that fact's \(D_e,L_e,m_e\) after the alias map just stated. Since the defect objective is linear in all matching weights,
\[
|\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}}|
=2\gamma_d|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|,
\qquad
\Delta_C(\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}})
=2\gamma_d\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}}).
\tag{38}
\]
Applying the quarter-mass estimate of `11e347c295e9430b` gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\max}(C)
&\geq\frac14\,2^{-n}
\left(
|\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}}|
-5\Delta_C(\widetilde s^{\mathrm{tar}})
\right)_+\\
&=\frac{\gamma_d}{2}\,2^{-n}
\left(
|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|-5\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})
\right)_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
This is (17), with both the matching weight and the affine defect scaled by the same factor. If \(\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\leq|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|/10\), the positive part in (39) is at least \(|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|/2\). Equation (6) then gives (18).

It remains to prove the variance identity. Each edge \(f=\{m,m'\}\) contributes its weight once at each source endpoint and once at its midpoint, so
\[
\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})m
=\sum_{f=\{m,m'\}}s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}(m+m')
=2\sum_fs_f^{\mathrm{tar}}\widehat m_f.
\tag{40}
\]
Together with \(\sum_m d_m(s^{\mathrm{tar}})=2|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|\), this proves (19)-(20). For every edge, the Euclidean parallelogram identity gives
\[
\frac12\left(
\|m-\bar m\|_2^2+\|m'-\bar m\|_2^2
\right)
=\|\widehat m_f-\bar m\|_2^2
+\frac14\|m-m'\|_2^2.
\tag{41}
\]
Multiply (41) by \(s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}/|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|\) and sum over \(f\). The left side is \(V_{\mathrm{src}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\), and the first term on the right is \(V_{\mathrm{mid}}(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\). This proves the equality in (21). Every edge is off-diagonal and same-parity, so
\[
m-m'\in2\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{0\},
\qquad
\|m-m'\|_2^2\geq4.
\tag{42}
\]
The remaining term in (21) is therefore at least
\[
\frac1{4|s^{\mathrm{tar}}|}
\sum_f4s_f^{\mathrm{tar}}=1.
\]
This proves the exact variance identity and decrease. No step above turns a large value of \(\Delta_C(s^{\mathrm{tar}})\) into depth, compares affine residual ranks, or supplies a further canonical matching state, so the stated limitations follow.
