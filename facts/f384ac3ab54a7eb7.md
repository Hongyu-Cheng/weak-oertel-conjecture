## Statement

For a compact convex set \(E\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\), a Borel set \(B\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), and \(p\in S_E:=E\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), write
\[
 \mu_E(B):=
 \sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}\mathbf 1_{E\cap B}(z,x)\,dx,
 \qquad
 h_E(p):=
 \inf_{\substack{H\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\p\in H}}
 \mu_E(H),
 \qquad
 h_{\max}(E):=\sup_{p\in S_E}h_E(p).
\tag{1}
\]
When \(d=0\), Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb R^0=\{0\}\) is the unit point mass. Allowing the whole space among the halfspaces in (1) does not change the infimum.

Let \(n,s\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\), and let
\[
 C\subseteq
 \mathbb R^n_z\times\mathbb R^k_y\times\mathbb R^s_v
\]
be a compact convex set. For \((z,y)\in\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^k\), set
\[
 K_{z,y}:=\{v\in\mathbb R^s:(z,y,v)\in C\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a nonempty compact convex \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) and \(q\in K\), define its absolute \(s\)-dimensional halfspace depth by
\[
 \delta_K(q):=
 \inf_{\substack{L\subseteq\mathbb R^s\text{ proper closed halfspace}\\q\in L}}
 |K\cap L|_s.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(\Omega\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^k\) be Borel, and let
\[
 q:\Omega\to\mathbb R^s,
 \qquad
 w:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
\]
be Borel maps such that, for every \((z,y)\in\Omega\),
\[
 q(z,y)\in K_{z,y},
 \qquad
 \delta_{K_{z,y}}(q(z,y))\geq w(z,y).
\tag{4}
\]
With \(\Omega_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in\Omega\}\), put
\[
 T:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\int_{\Omega_z}w(z,y)\,dy,
 \qquad
 D_w:=\left\{z:\int_{\Omega_z}w(z,y)\,dy>0\right\},
 \qquad
 \rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_w),
\tag{5}
\]
and assume \(T>0\). Let \(A:\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R^s\) be affine, and let \(V\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) be a linear subspace of dimension \(r\in\{0,\ldots,s\}\), such that
\[
 q(z,y)-A(z,y)\in V
 \quad\text{whenever }(z,y)\in\Omega\text{ and }w(z,y)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Then \(D_w\) is a nonempty finite set, \(0<T<\infty\), and there exists
\[
 p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{k+s})
\]
such that every proper closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+k+s}\) containing \(p\) satisfies
\[
 \mu_C(H)\geq\frac{T}{2^\rho(k+r+1)}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{h_{\max}(C)\geq\frac{T}{2^\rho(k+r+1)}}.
\tag{8}
\]
This includes \(\rho=0\), \(r=0\), and \(k=0\), with the zero-dimensional conventions stated above. Since \(\rho\leq n\), (8) also gives \(h_{\max}(C)\geq T/[2^n(k+r+1)]\).

For the following consequences, if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) is an \(s\)-dimensional convex body and \(0<t\leq |K|_s\), define
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_Kv f(v)\,dv:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf\,dv=t
 \right\},
 \qquad
 \gamma_s:=\left(\frac{s}{s+1}\right)^s.
\tag{9}
\]

Metronoid corollary. Suppose \(\Omega\) is a Borel family on which \(K_{z,y}\) has positive \(s\)-volume, \(t:\Omega\to(0,\infty)\) and \(q:\Omega\to\mathbb R^s\) are Borel,
\[
 0<t(z,y)\leq |K_{z,y}|_s,
 \qquad
 q(z,y)\in\mathsf M_{t(z,y)}(K_{z,y}),
 \qquad
 q(z,y)-A(z,y)\in V,
\tag{10}
\]
where \(A\) is affine and \(\dim V=r\). If
\[
 T_t:=\sum_z\int_{\Omega_z}t(z,y)\,dy>0,
 \quad
 D_t:=\left\{z:\int_{\Omega_z}t(z,y)\,dy>0\right\},
 \quad
 \rho_t:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_t),
\tag{11}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_sT_t}{2^{\rho_t}(k+r+1)}.
 }
\tag{12}
\]

Covariance-tube corollary. On a Borel family \(\Omega\) of positive-volume sections, put
\[
 a(z,y):=|K_{z,y}|_s,
 \quad
 c(z,y):=\frac1{a(z,y)}\int_{K_{z,y}}v\,dv,
 \quad
 \Sigma_{z,y}:=
 \frac1{a(z,y)}\int_{K_{z,y}}(v-c(z,y))(v-c(z,y))^{\mathsf T}\,dv,
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal E_{z,y}:=
 \left\{e\in\mathbb R^s:
 \max_{v\in K_{z,y}}
 \left|\left\langle\Sigma_{z,y}^{-1}e,v-c(z,y)\right\rangle\right|
 \leq1
 \right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Each \(\Sigma_{z,y}\) is positive definite. Suppose there is a Borel selection \(q:\Omega\to\mathbb R^s\), an affine \(A\), and an \(r\)-dimensional \(V\) such that
\[
 q(z,y)\in
 \bigl(c(z,y)+\mathcal E_{z,y}\bigr)
 \cap\bigl(A(z,y)+V\bigr)
 \qquad((z,y)\in\Omega).
\tag{15}
\]
If
\[
 W_\Omega:=\sum_z\int_{\Omega_z}a(z,y)\,dy>0,
 \quad
 D_\Omega:=\left\{z:\int_{\Omega_z}a(z,y)\,dy>0\right\},
 \quad
 \rho_\Omega:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_\Omega),
\tag{16}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_sW_\Omega}{2^{\rho_\Omega+1}(k+r+1)}.
 }
\tag{17}
\]

Spectral-tail corollary. Retain the data \(\Omega,a,c,\mathcal E,W_\Omega\) from (13), (14), and (16). Let \(A:\mathbb R^{n+k}\to\mathbb R^s\) be affine and let \(\sigma:\Omega\to(0,\infty)\) be Borel with
\[
 \sigma(z,y)B_2^s\subseteq\mathcal E_{z,y}
 \qquad((z,y)\in\Omega).
\tag{18}
\]
For a fixed \(r\)-dimensional subspace \(V\subseteq\mathbb R^s\), set
\[
 e(z,y):=c(z,y)-A(z,y),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_V:=
 \frac1{W_\Omega}
 \sum_z\int_{\Omega_z}
 a(z,y)
 \frac{\|\Pi_{V^\perp}e(z,y)\|_2^2}{\sigma(z,y)^2}\,dy.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(\varepsilon_V<1\), define
\[
 \Omega_V:=
 \left\{(z,y)\in\Omega:
 \|\Pi_{V^\perp}e(z,y)\|_2\leq\sigma(z,y)
 \right\},
 \quad
 D_V:=\left\{z:\int_{(\Omega_V)_z}a(z,y)\,dy>0\right\},
 \quad
 \rho_V:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(D_V).
\tag{20}
\]
Then \(D_V\neq\varnothing\) and
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_s(1-\varepsilon_V)W_\Omega}
 {2^{\rho_V+1}(k+r+1)}.
 }
\tag{21}
\]

In particular, suppose the following positive-semidefinite matrix has finite trace:
\[
 R_A:=
 \frac1{W_\Omega}
 \sum_z\int_{\Omega_z}
 \frac{a(z,y)}{\sigma(z,y)^2}
 e(z,y)e(z,y)^{\mathsf T}\,dy.
\tag{22}
\]
Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_s\geq0\). For \(r\in\{0,\ldots,s\}\), let \(V_r\) be the span of the first \(r\) basis vectors, with \(V_0=\{0\}\), and define \(\rho_r\) from (20) using \(V_r\). Whenever
\[
 \tau_r:=\sum_{j=r+1}^s\lambda_j<1,
\tag{23}
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_s(1-\tau_r)W_\Omega}
 {2^{\rho_r+1}(k+r+1)}.
 }
\tag{24}
\]
The sum in (23) is zero when \(r=s\).

Affine-centered homothetic-bundle corollary. Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^k\) be a convex body, let \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) be an \(s\)-dimensional convex body with centroid \(0\), let \(A:\mathbb R^{n+k}\to\mathbb R^s\) be affine, and let \(\varphi:D\to[0,\infty)\) be continuous and concave. Assume \(\varphi(x_0)>0\) for some \(x_0\in\operatorname{int}D\), and define
\[
 C_\varphi:=
 \left\{
 (z,y,A(z,y)+\varphi(z,y)u):
 (z,y)\in D,\ u\in U
 \right\}.
\tag{25}
\]
Then \(C_\varphi\) is a convex body. Put \(D_z:=\{y:(z,y)\in D\}\) and
\[
 W_\varphi:=
 |U|_s\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\int_{D_z}\varphi(z,y)^s\,dy.
\tag{26}
\]
Assume \(W_\varphi>0\), and set
\[
 I_\varphi:=
 \left\{z:\int_{D_z}\varphi(z,y)^s\,dy>0\right\},
 \qquad
 \rho_\varphi:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(I_\varphi).
\tag{27}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C_\varphi)\geq
 \frac{\gamma_sW_\varphi}{2^{\rho_\varphi}(k+1)}.
 }
\tag{28}
\]

Product-inheritance corollary. Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^k\) and \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^s\) be convex bodies, write \(c_U\) and \(V_U>0\) for the centroid and volume of \(U\), and assume \(S_D\neq\varnothing\). Then, for every \(p_D\in S_D\),
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{D\times U}(p_D,c_U)
 \geq\gamma_sV_Uh_D(p_D).
 }
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(D\times U)
 \geq\gamma_sV_Uh_{\max}(D).
 }
\tag{30}
\]

## Proof

We first prove the quotient-residual theorem. For every \((z,y)\in\Omega\), compactness of \(K_{z,y}\) and \(q(z,y)\in K_{z,y}\) give
\[
 w(z,y)\leq\delta_{K_{z,y}}(q(z,y))\leq |K_{z,y}|_s.
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, for any nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^s\), the proper closed halfspace
\[
 \{v:\langle u,v\rangle\leq\max_{x\in K_{z,y}}\langle u,x\rangle\}
\]
contains all of \(K_{z,y}\), hence also \(q(z,y)\). The projection of compact \(C\) onto its \(z\)-coordinates meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in only finitely many points. Tonelli's theorem and (31) therefore give
\[
 T\leq
 \sum_z\int_{\mathbb R^k}|K_{z,y}|_s\,dy
 =\mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+k+s})<\infty.
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(T>0\), \(D_w\) is nonempty, and it is finite by the preceding projection argument.

Choose \(z_0\in D_w\), and put
\[
 E_0:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(D_w-z_0),
 \qquad
 \Gamma:=E_0\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{33}
\]
The space \(E_0\) has dimension \(\rho\) and is spanned by integer vectors. Thus \(\Gamma\) is a rank-\(\rho\) lattice in \(E_0\). Choose a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_\rho\) of \(\Gamma\), and define the affine isomorphism
\[
 \bar\chi:\mathbb R^\rho\to\operatorname{aff}(D_w),
 \qquad
 \bar\chi(t):=z_0+\sum_{i=1}^\rho t_i u_i.
\tag{34}
\]
Its restriction \(\chi:=\bar\chi|_{\mathbb Z^\rho}\) is a bijection
\[
 \chi:\mathbb Z^\rho\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{aff}(D_w)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{35}
\]
because an integer point \(z\) lies in \(z_0+E_0\) exactly when \(z-z_0\in\Gamma\). When \(\rho=0\), (34) and (35) are the unique maps from \(\mathbb R^0\) and \(\mathbb Z^0\) to the singleton \(\{z_0\}\).

Choose a linear isomorphism \(L:\mathbb R^r\to V\), using the unique zero-dimensional isomorphism when \(r=0\). On the Borel set
\[
 P:=\{(z,y)\in\Omega:z\in D_w,\ w(z,y)>0\},
\]
define
\[
 \xi(z,y):=L^{-1}(q(z,y)-A(z,y)).
\tag{36}
\]
This map is Borel by (6). For every Borel \(B\subseteq\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^{k+r}\), define
\[
 \eta(B):=
 \sum_{z\in D_w}\int_{P_z}
 w(z,y)
 \mathbf1_B\bigl(\chi^{-1}(z),y,\xi(z,y)\bigr)\,dy.
\tag{37}
\]
This is a finite Borel measure supported on \(\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^{k+r}\). Indices outside \(D_w\) have zero integrated \(w\)-mass, and points where \(w=0\) contribute nothing, so
\[
 \eta(\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^{k+r})=T.
\tag{38}
\]

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to \(\eta\), with integer dimension \(\rho\) and continuous dimension \(k+r\). It gives
\[
 \widehat p=(j_*,y_*,\xi_*)
 \in\mathbb Z^\rho\times\mathbb R^{k+r}
\tag{39}
\]
such that every closed halfspace in \(\mathbb R^{\rho+k+r}\) containing \(\widehat p\), including the whole space if that convention is used, has \(\eta\)-mass at least
\[
 \alpha:=\frac{T}{2^\rho(k+r+1)}.
\tag{40}
\]

Define the affine map
\[
 F:\mathbb R^\rho\times\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^r
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^s,
 \qquad
 F(j,y,\xi):=
 \bigl(\bar\chi(j),y,A(\bar\chi(j),y)+L\xi\bigr),
\tag{41}
\]
and put \(p:=F(\widehat p)\). Every point used in the pushforward formula (37) satisfies
\[
 F(\chi^{-1}(z),y,\xi(z,y))=(z,y,q(z,y))\in C.
\tag{42}
\]
We claim that \(p\in C\). If not, strict separation of \(p\) from the nonempty compact convex set \(C\) would give a proper closed halfspace \(H_0\) containing \(p\) and disjoint from \(C\). The affine preimage \(F^{-1}(H_0)\) contains \(\widehat p\) and is either a proper closed halfspace or the whole domain. It has \(\eta\)-mass zero by (37) and (42), contradicting (40). Hence \(p\in C\). Its first coordinate is \(\chi(j_*)\in\mathbb Z^n\), so
\[
 p\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{k+s}).
\tag{43}
\]

Let \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+k+s}\) be a proper closed halfspace containing \(p\). Its affine preimage \(\widehat H:=F^{-1}(H)\) contains \(\widehat p\). The preimage cannot be empty; it is either a proper closed halfspace or the whole space. Thus (40) gives
\[
 \eta(\widehat H)\geq\alpha.
\tag{44}
\]
For fixed \((z,y)\), write
\[
 H_{z,y}:=\{v:(z,y,v)\in H\}.
\tag{45}
\]
If a graph point in (42) belongs to \(H\), then \(H_{z,y}\) contains \(q(z,y)\). If the coefficient of \(v\) in a defining affine inequality for \(H\) is nonzero, \(H_{z,y}\) is a proper closed halfspace and (4) gives
\[
 |K_{z,y}\cap H_{z,y}|_s\geq w(z,y).
\tag{46}
\]
If that coefficient is zero, a nonempty section \(H_{z,y}\) is the whole \(\mathbb R^s\), and (46) follows instead from (31). Thus (46) holds in every graph-point case, including a whole pulled-back fiber halfspace. All relevant sets and integrands are Borel. Tonelli's theorem, (37), and (44) now yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_C(H)
 &=\sum_z\int_{\mathbb R^k}|K_{z,y}\cap H_{z,y}|_s\,dy\\
 &\geq
 \sum_{z\in D_w}\int_{P_z}
 \mathbf1_H(z,y,q(z,y))w(z,y)\,dy\\
 &=\eta(F^{-1}(H))
 \geq\alpha.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
This proves (7) and (8). The constructions above explicitly cover \(\rho=0\), \(r=0\), and \(k=0\). Finally, \(D_w\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) implies \(\rho\leq n\), proving the stated weaker denominator \(2^n(k+r+1)\).

For the metronoid corollary, accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` states that
\[
 q(z,y)\in\mathsf M_{t(z,y)}(K_{z,y})
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 q(z,y)\in K_{z,y},
 \qquad
 \delta_{K_{z,y}}(q(z,y))\geq\gamma_st(z,y).
\tag{48}
\]
Apply (8) with \(w=\gamma_st\). Then \(T=\gamma_sT_t\), \(D_w=D_t\), and \(\rho=\rho_t\), which proves (12).

We next prove the covariance-tube corollary. The maps in (13) are Borel on the positive-volume set. Indeed, \(\mathbf1_C(z,y,v)\) is Borel and supported in a bounded set, so parameterized integration in \(v\) shows that the volume, first-moment, and second-moment entries are Borel; division by the positive Borel function \(a\) preserves Borel measurability. Accepted fact `107b9182522633f9`, applied separately to each \(K_{z,y}\), says that \(\Sigma_{z,y}\) is positive definite and
\[
 c(z,y)+\mathcal E_{z,y}
 \subseteq\mathsf M_{a(z,y)/2}(K_{z,y}).
\tag{49}
\]
Therefore the selection in (15) satisfies the metronoid hypotheses with \(t=a/2\) and the same affine map and residual space. In (11), \(T_t=W_\Omega/2\), \(D_t=D_\Omega\), and \(\rho_t=\rho_\Omega\). Substitution in (12) proves (17).

For the fixed-space spectral statement, set
\[
 R(z,y):=\frac{\|\Pi_{V^\perp}e(z,y)\|_2}{\sigma(z,y)}.
\]
The set \(\Omega_V=\{R\leq1\}\) is Borel. Since \(R>1\) on its complement, (19) gives the weighted Markov estimate
\[
 \sum_z\int_{(\Omega\setminus\Omega_V)_z}a(z,y)\,dy
 \leq
 \sum_z\int_{\Omega_z}a(z,y)R(z,y)^2\,dy
 =\varepsilon_VW_\Omega.
\tag{50}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 W_V:=\sum_z\int_{(\Omega_V)_z}a(z,y)\,dy
 \geq(1-\varepsilon_V)W_\Omega>0,
\tag{51}
\]
so \(D_V\neq\varnothing\). On \(\Omega_V\), define the Borel selection
\[
 q_V(z,y):=A(z,y)+\Pi_Ve(z,y).
\tag{52}
\]
It satisfies
\[
 q_V-A=\Pi_Ve\in V,
 \qquad
 q_V-c=-\Pi_{V^\perp}e
 \in\sigma B_2^s
 \subseteq\mathcal E_{z,y}.
\tag{53}
\]
Apply (17) to \(\Omega_V\), then use (51), to obtain
\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 \geq\frac{\gamma_sW_V}{2^{\rho_V+1}(k+r+1)}
 \geq
 \frac{\gamma_s(1-\varepsilon_V)W_\Omega}
 {2^{\rho_V+1}(k+r+1)}.
\]
This is (21).

For (24), finite trace in (22) makes all matrix entries integrable and \(R_A\) positive semidefinite. For every subspace \(V\), linearity of trace gives
\[
 \varepsilon_V
 =\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{V^\perp}R_A).
\tag{54}
\]
If \(V=V_r\) is spanned by eigenvectors for the \(r\) largest eigenvalues, the right-hand side of (54) is exactly \(\sum_{j>r}\lambda_j=\tau_r\). Applying (21) proves (24), including \(r=0\) and \(r=s\).

We prove the homothetic-bundle corollary. The centroid \(0\) of the convex body \(U\) lies in \(\operatorname{int}U\). Indeed, otherwise separation of \(0\) from the open convex set \(\operatorname{int}U\) would give a nonzero linear functional that is nonnegative on \(U\) and strictly positive on a nonempty open subset of \(U\). Its integral over \(U\) would be positive, contradicting that the centroid is \(0\). The map
\[
 (x,u)\longmapsto(x,A(x)+\varphi(x)u)
\]
is continuous on compact \(D\times U\), so \(C_\varphi\) is compact. To prove convexity, take \(x_0,x_1\in D\), \(u_0,u_1\in U\), and \(0\leq\theta\leq1\). Put
\[
 R:=(1-\theta)\varphi(x_0)+\theta\varphi(x_1).
\]
If \(R>0\), then
\[
 u':=\frac{(1-\theta)\varphi(x_0)u_0+\theta\varphi(x_1)u_1}{R}\in U;
\]
if \(R=0\), take \(u'=0\in U\). Concavity gives \(R\leq\varphi((1-\theta)x_0+\theta x_1)\). Since \(0\in U\), scaling a point of \(U\) by a number in \([0,1]\) leaves it in \(U\). It follows that the convex combination of the two chosen points lies in \(C_\varphi\). Thus \(C_\varphi\) is convex.

There is a neighborhood \(N\subseteq\operatorname{int}D\) of the stipulated \(x_0\) and a number \(b>0\) such that \(\varphi\geq b\) on \(N\). Since \(0\in\operatorname{int}U\), the fibers over \(N\) contain a fixed open neighborhood of their affine centers. After the affine shear \((x,v)\mapsto(x,A(x)+v)\), this gives a nonempty open subset of \(C_\varphi\). Hence \(C_\varphi\) is a convex body.

For \((z,y)\in D\) with \(\varphi(z,y)>0\), the conditional section in the \(v\)-coordinates is
\[
 A(z,y)+\varphi(z,y)U.
\]
Its volume and centroid are, respectively,
\[
 a(z,y)=|U|_s\varphi(z,y)^s,
 \qquad
 c(z,y)=A(z,y).
\tag{55}
\]
The constant density \(1\) shows that \(A(z,y)\in\mathsf M_{a(z,y)}(K_{z,y})\). Apply the metronoid corollary on the Borel set where \((z,y)\in D\) and \(\varphi(z,y)>0\), with \(t=a\), \(V=\{0\}\), and \(r=0\). Equations (26) and (27) give \(T_t=W_\varphi\), \(D_t=I_\varphi\), and \(\rho_t=\rho_\varphi\). Formula (12) becomes (28).

Finally, fix \(p_D\in S_D\) and a proper closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+k+s}\) containing \((p_D,c_U)\). Define the affine pullback
\[
 G:=\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+k}:(x,c_U)\in H\}.
\tag{56}
\]
It contains \(p_D\), so it is either a proper closed halfspace or the whole \(\mathbb R^{n+k}\); it cannot be empty. If it is proper, \(\mu_D(G)\geq h_D(p_D)\) by definition. If it is the whole space, the same inequality holds because \(h_D(p_D)\leq\mu_D(\mathbb R^{n+k})\).

The centroid \(c_U\) is represented in \(\mathsf M_{V_U}(U)\) by the constant density \(1\). Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` therefore gives
\[
 |U\cap L|_s\geq\gamma_sV_U
\tag{57}
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(L\) containing \(c_U\). For each \(x=(z,y)\in D\cap G\), the fiber section
\[
 H_x:=\{u:(x,u)\in H\}
\]
contains \(c_U\). It is either a proper closed halfspace, in which case (57) applies, or the whole \(\mathbb R^s\), in which case its intersection with \(U\) has volume \(V_U\geq\gamma_sV_U\). Tonelli's theorem now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_{D\times U}(H)
 &=\sum_z\int_{D_z}|U\cap H_{z,y}|_s\,dy\\
 &\geq\gamma_sV_U\mu_D(G)
 \geq\gamma_sV_Uh_D(p_D).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{58}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(H\) proves (29). Taking the supremum over \(p_D\in S_D\) proves (30).
