## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and, for \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), put
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\]
Assume \(W>0\), and put \(Z:=\{z:a_z>0\}\). Let \(S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\), let
\[
\mu_C(A):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in A\},
\]
and define
\[
h_{\max}(C):=
\sup_{y\in S}\inf\{\mu_C(J):J\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in J\}.
\]
Set
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\qquad
b_z:=\gamma_da_z,
\qquad
H:=\frac{2^nh_{\max}(C)}W.
\tag{1}
\]

Let
\[
\mathcal E:=\bigl\{\{z,z'\}\subseteq Z:
z\ne z',\ z\equiv z'\pmod 2\bigr\}.
\]
For a nonnegative family \(s=(s_e)_{e\in\mathcal E}\), write
\[
d_z(s):=\sum_{e\ni z}s_e,
\qquad
|s|:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}s_e.
\tag{2}
\]
This is the degree and mass notation of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5; in the present statement the distinguished endpoint-capacity vector is \(b_z=\gamma_da_z\), rather than the half-fiber vector \(a_z/2\) used in the definition there. Call \(q=(q_e)_{e\in\mathcal E}\) an exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching if there are \(u_z\ge0\) such that
\[
b_z=u_z+d_z(q)
\tag{3}
\]
and, in each parity class, the sum of the \(u_z\)'s is the least total unmatched capacity possible among all nonnegative families on the off-diagonal edges of that class. Equivalently, with
\[
B_\xi:=\sum_{z\equiv\xi}b_z,
\qquad
\beta_\xi:=\max_{z\equiv\xi}b_z,
\qquad
U_\xi:=(2\beta_\xi-B_\xi)_+,
\tag{4}
\]
where all three quantities are zero for an empty parity class, exactness means
\[
\sum_{z\equiv\xi}u_z=U_\xi
\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n.
\tag{5}
\]
The equivalence and existence of an exact matching in this normalization are included in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483.

There exist nonnegative families \(p=(p_e)_{e\in\mathcal E}\) and \(r=(r_e)_{e\in\mathcal E}\) and an exact minimum-unmatched centroid matching \(q\) such that
\[
q=p+r,
\tag{6}
\]
and the following conclusions hold:

1. \(p\) is graph-theoretic: every \(z\in Z\) is incident with at most one edge \(e\) satisfying \(p_e>0\).

2. If \(\mathcal U:=\sum_{z\in Z}u_z=\sum_\xi U_\xi\), then
\[
|r|\le HW,
\qquad
\mathcal U\le HW.
\tag{7}
\]

3. The main matching satisfies
\[
|p|\ge \frac{\gamma_d}{2}W-\frac32HW.
\tag{8}
\]

4. Its total endpoint-capacity deficit satisfies
\[
\sum_{z\in Z}\bigl(\gamma_da_z-d_z(p)\bigr)\le3HW.
\tag{9}
\]

5. Its paired fiber volumes satisfy
\[
\gamma_d
\sum_{e=\{z,z'\}\in\operatorname{supp}p}|a_z-a_{z'}|
\le3HW.
\tag{10}
\]

All edges of \(p,r,q\) are genuinely off-diagonal and join points in the same parity class. Moreover, each of \(p,r,q\) is also an off-diagonal fractional same-parity matching in the half-fiber normalization of accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5.

## Proof

Compactness of \(C\) makes \(Z\) finite. We first give the complete construction in one nonempty parity class. List its positive capacities in nonincreasing order as
\[
b_1\ge b_2\ge\cdots\ge b_m>0,
\qquad
B:=\sum_{i=1}^m b_i,
\qquad
\beta:=b_1.
\tag{11}
\]

Suppose first that \(\beta>B/2\). Set \(p=0\) in this class and define
\[
r_{\{1,i\}}:=b_i\quad(2\le i\le m),
\qquad
r_e:=0\quad\text{on all other edges}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every vertex except vertex \(1\) is saturated, vertex \(1\) has degree \(B-\beta\), and the unmatched weights are
\[
u_1=\beta-(B-\beta)=2\beta-B,
\qquad
u_i=0\quad(i\ge2).
\tag{13}
\]
This also covers \(m=1\): then (12) is empty and \(u_1=\beta\). Every edge in (12) has two distinct endpoints in the fixed parity class. The residual mass and unmatched mass obey
\[
|r|=B-\beta\le\beta,
\qquad
U=2\beta-B\le\beta.
\tag{14}
\]
The unmatched amount in (13) is minimal. Indeed, for any off-diagonal matching \(s\) in this class, write \(E_0\) for the mass on edges not incident with vertex \(1\). The total degree at all vertices other than \(1\) is \(d_1(s)+2E_0\le B-\beta\), and also \(d_1(s)\le B-\beta\). Therefore
\[
|s|=d_1(s)+E_0
=\frac{d_1(s)+(d_1(s)+2E_0)}2
\le B-\beta.
\]
Consequently its total unmatched mass is \(B-2|s|\ge2\beta-B\), with equality in (12). This agrees with the exact formula in accepted fact be359f8a6c628483.

Now suppose that \(\beta\le B/2\). Define an initial graph-theoretic matching \(p^0\) by pairing consecutive indices:
\[
p^0_{\{2j-1,2j\}}:=b_{2j}
\qquad(1\le j\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor),
\tag{15}
\]
with all other edge weights zero. Its total residual degree is
\[
R_0:=\sum_{i=1}^m\bigl(b_i-d_i(p^0)\bigr)
=(b_1-b_2)+(b_3-b_4)+\cdots,
\tag{16}
\]
with the final \(b_m\) included when \(m\) is odd. Monotonicity of (11) gives \(R_0\le\beta\). More explicitly, if \(m=2k\), then
\[
R_0=b_1-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(b_{2j}-b_{2j+1})-b_{2k}\le b_1,
\]
whereas if \(m=2k+1\), then
\[
R_0=b_1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}(b_{2j}-b_{2j+1})\le b_1.
\]

For \(t\in[0,1]\), put \(p^t:=t p^0\). Its total residual degree is
\[
R(t):=\sum_i\bigl(b_i-d_i(p^t)\bigr)
=B-2t|p^0|.
\tag{17}
\]
This is continuous, \(R(0)=B\ge2\beta\), and \(R(1)=R_0\le\beta\le2\beta\). Hence there is \(t_*\in[0,1]\) with \(R(t_*)=2\beta\). Fix \(p:=p^{t_*}\), and define the prescribed residual degrees
\[
\rho_i:=b_i-d_i(p).
\tag{18}
\]
The construction (15) and \(0\le t_*\le1\) give
\[
0\le\rho_i\le b_i\le\beta,
\qquad
\sum_i\rho_i=2\beta.
\tag{19}
\]
The support of \(p\) is contained in the disjoint consecutive pairs in (15), so \(p\) is graph-theoretic.

We next realize the prescribed degrees (19) by a loopless fractional matching, including all boundary cases. Take a circle of circumference \(2\beta\), partition it, up to null endpoints, into consecutive half-open arcs \(I_i\) of lengths \(\rho_i\), and let
\[
\tau(x):=x+\beta\pmod{2\beta}
\]
be the half-circle translation. For distinct \(i,j\), set
\[
r_{\{i,j\}}:=\operatorname{len}\bigl(I_i\cap\tau(I_j)\bigr).
\tag{20}
\]
The involution and length preservation of \(\tau\) imply
\[
\operatorname{len}(I_i\cap\tau(I_j))
=\operatorname{len}(\tau(I_i)\cap I_j)
=\operatorname{len}(I_j\cap\tau(I_i)),
\]
so (20) is symmetric. Because every \(I_i\) has length at most \(\beta\), the arcs \(I_i\) and \(\tau(I_i)\) have intersection of length zero. Thus the omitted diagonal weight is zero and the construction has no loops. Since the family \((\tau(I_j))_j\) partitions the circle up to a null set,
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}r_{\{i,j\}}
=\operatorname{len}(I_i)=\rho_i.
\tag{21}
\]
Every edge in (20) therefore joins two distinct vertices in the fixed parity class, \(r\) has exactly the prescribed degrees, and
\[
|r|=\frac12\sum_i\rho_i=\beta.
\tag{22}
\]
This is the prescribed-degree version of the circle construction used in accepted facts 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5 and be359f8a6c628483.

By (18) and (21), \(q:=p+r\) saturates every capacity \(b_i\), so all unmatched weights are zero. Since unmatched mass is nonnegative, this is minimum-unmatched. Thus in the case \(\beta\le B/2\),
\[
|r|=\beta,
\qquad
U=0\le\beta.
\tag{23}
\]
Combining (12)-(14) and (15)-(23), every nonempty parity class admits a decomposition \(q=p+r\) such that \(p\) is graph-theoretic, \(q\) is exact minimum-unmatched, and
\[
|r_\xi|\le\beta_\xi,
\qquad
U_\xi\le\beta_\xi.
\tag{24}
\]
On an empty class take all quantities to be zero. Since the edge sets of different parity classes are disjoint, their unions give global nonnegative families \(p,r,q\) satisfying (3), (5), and (6), and preserve the graph-theoretic property of \(p\).

We now control the sum of the class maxima. Put
\[
A_*:=\max_{z\in Z}a_z
\]
and choose \(z_*\in Z\) with \(a_{z_*}=A_*\). Apply accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d to the \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(C_{z_*}\) with \(m=A_*\). Its full-mass metronoid contains its centroid \(c_{z_*}\), and that fact gives depth at least \(\gamma_dA_*\) in every continuous halfspace containing \(c_{z_*}\). If an ambient closed halfspace contains \((z_*,c_{z_*})\), its section in the \(z_*\)-fiber is either such a continuous halfspace or the whole fiber. Hence
\[
h_{\max}(C)\ge\gamma_dA_*.
\tag{25}
\]
There are \(2^n\) parity classes and every \(\beta_\xi\le\gamma_dA_*\). Equations (1), (24), and (25) therefore give
\[
|r|=\sum_\xi|r_\xi|
\le\sum_\xi\beta_\xi
\le2^n\gamma_dA_*
\le2^nh_{\max}(C)=HW,
\tag{26}
\]
and identically
\[
\mathcal U=\sum_\xi U_\xi\le HW.
\tag{27}
\]
This proves (7).

Summing (3) over \(Z\), using \(\sum_zd_z(q)=2|q|\), gives the exact identity
\[
|q|=\frac12(\gamma_dW-\mathcal U).
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(q=p+r\), equations (26)-(28) yield
\[
|p|=|q|-|r|
\ge\frac{\gamma_dW-HW}{2}-HW
=\frac{\gamma_d}{2}W-\frac32HW,
\]
which is (8). Pointwise (3) and (6) give
\[
\gamma_da_z-d_z(p)=u_z+d_z(r).
\]
Summing this identity and using (26)-(27) proves
\[
\sum_z\bigl(\gamma_da_z-d_z(p)\bigr)
=\mathcal U+2|r|\le3HW,
\]
which is (9).

For every \(e=\{z,z'\}\in\operatorname{supp}p\), graph-theoreticity gives
\[
d_z(p)=d_{z'}(p)=p_e.
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_d|a_z-a_{z'}|
&=|b_z-b_{z'}|\\
&=|(b_z-p_e)-(b_{z'}-p_e)|\\
&\le (b_z-d_z(p))+(b_{z'}-d_{z'}(p)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Distinct edges of \(\operatorname{supp}p\) have disjoint endpoint sets. Summing (29) over those edges and applying (9) proves (10).

Finally, all constructed edges lie in \(\mathcal E\), so they are off-diagonal and same-parity. Also
\[
\gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}\le\frac12,
\tag{30}
\]
because the binomial expansion gives \((1+1/d)^d\ge2\). Equations (3) and (6) imply
\[
d_z(p)\le b_z,
\qquad
d_z(r)\le b_z,
\qquad
d_z(q)\le b_z\le\frac{a_z}{2}.
\]
Thus \(p,r,q\) all satisfy the half-fiber degree bounds in accepted fact 8c92d1d2b1a1b7f5, completing every asserted property.
