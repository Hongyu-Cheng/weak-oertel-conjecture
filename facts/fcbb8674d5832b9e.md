## Statement

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer, $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, $V=1/d!$, and $u_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i u_i=1$. Put $F_r(s)=|rT\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge s\}|/V$ and $t=1/(d+1)$. For $k>0$ and $0\le\beta<kt$, set
$$
f_-(h)=F_{1-kh}(t-\beta h),\qquad f_+(h)=F_{1-kh}(t+\beta(h+1))\qquad(0\le h\le1/k)
$$
and extend both by zero for $h\ge1/k$. Each is nonnegative and convex on $[0,\infty)$. For either function $f$, the sum over nonnegative integers satisfies
$$
\sum_{h\ge0}f(h)\ge \int_0^\infty f(h)\,dh+f(0)/2,\qquad \sum_{h\ge0}f(h)\ge f(0).
$$
If $k\le1$ it follows that $\sum_{h\ge0}f(h)\ge(1-k)\int_0^\infty f(h)\,dh+(1+k)f(0)/2$.

Let $\Theta$ be uniform on the opposite facet $\operatorname{conv}(e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, with $\Theta=e_1$ if $d=1$, and put $U=u\cdot\Theta$. For $s>0$, $F_r(s)=\mathbb E[(r^d-(s/U)^d)_+]$, taking the integrand to be zero at $U=0$. Write $c=\beta/k$ and $B=t+\beta$. If $c>0$, then $\int_0^\infty f_\pm(h)\,dh=\mathbb E[J_\pm(U)]/[k(d+1)]$, where $J_-(U)=0$ for $U\le t$ and $J_+(U)=0$ for $U\le B$. On their respective remaining domains,
$$
J_-(U)=1-\frac{t^{d+1}}{cU^d}+\frac{(t-c)^{d+1}}{c(U-c)^d},\qquad
J_+(U)=1+\frac{B^{d+1}}{cU^d}-\frac{(B+c)^{d+1}}{c(U+c)^d}.
$$
If $c=0$ the same integral formula uses $J_-=J_+=0$ for $U\le t$ and $J_-=J_+=1-(d+1)(t/U)^d+d(t/U)^{d+1}$ for $U>t$.

## Proof

Apply the radial identity of accepted fact 9cef204c64b1bff8 with this simplex and normal. For $s>0$ it writes $F_r(s)=d\int_0^r w^{d-1}\mathbb P(wU\ge s)\,dw$. Tonelli and integration on $[s/U,r]$ when $s/U<r$ give the claimed positive-part expression, also for an atom in the law of $U$ and for $r=0$.

In the radius coordinate $r=1-kh\in[0,1]$ the thresholds become $s_-(r)=t-c+cr$ and $s_+(r)=B+c-cr$. Both are positive. For fixed $U>0$, the integrand is active exactly when $rU>s_\pm(r)$. The left activation requires $U>t$ to occur on $[0,1]$, and then its root is $r_0=(t-c)/(U-c)$. The right activation requires $U>B$ and has root $r_0=(B+c)/(U+c)$. On either nonempty active interval $U>c$. For $d\ge2$ the second derivative of $r^d-(s_\pm(r)/U)^d$ there is
$$
d(d-1)\left[r^{d-2}-\frac{c^2 s_\pm(r)^{d-2}}{U^d}\right]
\ge d(d-1)r^{d-2}(1-c^2/U^2)>0.
$$
At the root its right derivative is $d r_0^{d-1}(1-c/U)$ for the left cap and $d r_0^{d-1}(1+c/U)$ for the right cap. Both are positive, so the derivative increases from the zero piece at that root. For $d=1$ the active part is affine with the same positive derivative. Thus every integrand is convex, including identically zero cases. Taking the expectation and composing with the affine map $r=1-kh$ preserves convexity. Since $U\le1$ and both thresholds are bounded below by $t-c>0$, both functions vanish for all sufficiently small radii, so extension by zero at $h=1/k$ preserves convexity.

On every unit interval the integral of a convex function is at most the average of its endpoint values. Summing yields the first lattice inequality. The summand at zero gives the second. For $k\le1$, weights $1-k$ and $k$ give the claimed combination.

For the integral identities change variables to $r$ and use Tonelli, which gives $k^{-1}$ times the expected integral of the active radial difference on $[r_0,1]$. For $c>0$, antiderivatives for $(d+1)(r^d-(s_\pm(r)/U)^d)$ are
$$
r^{d+1}-s_-(r)^{d+1}/(cU^d),\qquad r^{d+1}+s_+(r)^{d+1}/(cU^d),
$$
respectively. At $r_0$, substitution of $s_\pm(r_0)=Ur_0$ gives $-(t-c)^{d+1}/[c(U-c)^d]$ and $(B+c)^{d+1}/[c(U+c)^d]$, respectively. The upper endpoint gives the remaining terms of $J_\pm$. At either inactive cutoff the integral is zero. If $c=0$, the active interval exists exactly when $U>t$ and direct integration of $r^d-(t/U)^d$ between $t/U$ and one gives the displayed $J_\pm/(d+1)$.
