## Statement

Let actual compatible intervals $[r_i-w_i,r_i]$ have positive widths on consecutive integer indices containing zero, and satisfy $\sum_i iw_i=0$ and $\sum_iw_ir_i=Q/2$, where $M=\sum_iw_i$ and $Q=\sum_iw_i^2$. Suppose the proper block of $x\ge0$ is $I=\{-h,\ldots,h\}$ of size $k=2h+1$, and the wholly captured exterior fibers have total width $T$. For each interior index $j$ put
$$
c_j=-\Delta^2r_j,\quad d_j=-\Delta^2w_j,\quad
\kappa_j=\sum_iw_i(i-j)_+-M(-j)_+,\quad
\tau_j=\sum_{i\in I}(i-j)_+-k(-j)_+.
$$
Then $0\le c_j\le d_j$, both deficits are nonnegative, and the mixed cap length is
$$
F=T+\frac{kQ}{2M}+\sum_j c_j\left[\frac{k}{M}\kappa_j-\tau_j\right]
\ \ge\ T+\frac{kQ}{2M}+\sum_j d_j\min\left\{0,\frac{k}{M}\kappa_j-\tau_j\right\}.
$$
If the width probability law $(w_i/M)_i$ dominates the uniform probability on $I$ in convex order, then $F\ge T+kQ/(2M)$. It suffices that $w_i/M\le1/k$ for each $i\in I$.

## Proof

The upper endpoints are concave and the lower endpoints convex, giving $0\le c_j\le d_j$. The two deficits are nonnegative by Jensen for the hinge function, since the width law and the uniform law on $I$ both have mean zero. There is a slope $b$ with
$$r_i=r_0+bi-\sum_j c_j[(i-j)_+-(-j)_+].$$
The expression has the right second differences and value at zero, so its difference from $r$ is a linear term, absorbed in $b$. The width-weighted sum and the centroid condition give $r_0=Q/(2M)+M^{-1}\sum_j c_j\kappa_j$. The unweighted sum over $I$ also cancels the linear term and gives $kr_0-\sum_jc_j\tau_j$. Since each proper fiber contributes $r_i$ and all others contribute either zero or their full width, adding $T$ gives the identity. Bounding each term separately by its minimum over $0\le c_j\le d_j$ gives the stated lower bound without asserting simultaneous feasibility of these minima.

Convex-order dominance applied to the hinges makes all coefficients nonnegative. For the sufficient condition with $h>0$, the difference of the two probability laws is nonpositive on $I$ and nonnegative outside, with zero total mass and first moment. Subtract the affine secant of any convex test function through $-h,h$. The residual is nonpositive inside and nonnegative outside, so its integral against the signed difference is nonnegative. For $h=0$ the uniform law is a point mass at zero, and Jensen gives the dominance.
