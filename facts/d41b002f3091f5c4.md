## Statement

Retain all hypotheses, objects, and notation of accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` and its accepted predecessor `036a276c54ec5e66`. Thus \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) is a convex body, and
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\]
\[
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z>0,
 \qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\rm core}(C).
\tag{1}
\]
The half-strength canonical product reservoir has mass \(Q>0\), and the canonical hard inequality holds:
\[
 \mathcal A(C):=
 R+G+R_{\rm sel}(C)+M_{\rm off}(C)
 +R_{\rm cloud}(C)+2^nh_{\max}(C)
 <\frac Q{100}.
\tag{2}
\]

Let \(E_\perp\) be the set of distinct physical opposite-endpoint bases in the lossless endpoint collapse. For every \(y\in E_\perp\), let \(\tau_y>0\) be its pooled endpoint weight. Put
\[
 T_E:=\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_y.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` gives
\[
 0<\tau_y\le\frac{a_y}{4}
 \qquad(y\in E_\perp),
\tag{4}
\]
\[
 T_E=2P>\frac{99}{100}Q,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(P\) is the mass of the maximum transverse pairing. Then the pairing-capacity constraints also give
\[
 \boxed{T_E\le Q.}
\tag{6}
\]

Every endpoint atom obeys the stronger strict bound
\[
 \boxed{
 \tau_y<\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}
 \qquad(y\in E_\perp).}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{|E_\perp|>396\,2^n,}
\tag{8}
\]
and some parity class contains at least \(397\) distinct bases in \(E_\perp\).

For \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\), define the finite reflected endpoint-pair family
\[
 \mathcal P_m:=
 \left\{
  \{u,v\}\subseteq E_\perp:
  u\ne v,\ u+v=2m
 \right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
When \(\mathcal P_m\ne\varnothing\), put
\[
 S_m:=
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal P_m}
 \sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v},
 \qquad
 M_m:=
 \max_{\{u,v\}\in\mathcal P_m}
 \sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then there is an integer midpoint \(m\) such that
\[
 \boxed{S_m>73M_m.}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal P_m|\ge74.}
\tag{12}
\]
The pairs in \(\mathcal P_m\) are pairwise endpoint-disjoint.

Equations (7)--(12) make no assertion that the reflected ratios \(\tau_u/\tau_v\) are bounded, that the reflected directions span \(\mathbb R^n\) or have full rank in one parity class, or that one same-parity affine basis retains a fixed fraction of the endpoint mass. They imply neither an endpoint-frame aggregation inequality nor the locked final theorem.

## Proof

All six summands in (2) are nonnegative, so
\[
 G<\frac Q{100}.
\tag{13}
\]
The exact reservoir formula in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` gives, after summing over the good parity classes,
\[
 Q\le\frac W8.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(W\ge8Q>800G\), and in particular \(0<G<W/2\). The singleton complete-core bound in accepted fact `f3f94a04a28f5468` therefore applies and gives
\[
 a_z\le2^{-n}G
 \qquad(z\in Z).
\tag{15}
\]
Combining (4), (13), and (15) proves
\[
 \tau_y\le\frac{G}{4\,2^n}
 <\frac{Q}{400\,2^n},
\]
which is (7).

We next verify the upper bound in (6), rather than using it implicitly. Accepted fact `036a276c54ec5e66` gives
\[
 \Omega=2P+|\lambda^\perp|,
 \qquad
 0\le |\lambda^\perp|,
 \qquad
 \Omega\le Q.
\tag{16}
\]
Since accepted fact `3100bf8082ca09d5` gives \(T_E=2P\), equation (16) proves (6). Equations (5) and (7) now imply
\[
 \frac{99}{100}Q<T_E
 <|E_\perp|\frac{Q}{400\,2^n}.
\tag{17}
\]
Cancelling \(Q>0\) proves (8). There are exactly \(2^n\) parity classes. Hence one class contains strictly more than \(396\) endpoint bases, and its integer cardinality is at least \(397\).

It remains to prove the reflected-collision conclusion. Let
\[
 \tau_{\max}:=\max_{y\in E_\perp}\tau_y.
\tag{18}
\]
For every parity class \(\xi\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
 B_\xi:=
 \sum_{\substack{y\in E_\perp\\y\equiv\xi\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
 \sqrt{\tau_y}.
\tag{19}
\]
Define the total geometric-mean weight of all unordered distinct same-parity endpoint pairs by
\[
 S:=
 \sum_{\substack{\{u,v\}\subseteq E_\perp\\
                  u\ne v,\ u\equiv v\ ({\rm mod}\ 2)}}
 \sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}.
\tag{20}
\]
Expanding the squares in (19) gives the exact identity
\[
 S=\frac12\left(\sum_\xi B_\xi^2-T_E\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz over all \(2^n\) parity classes gives
\[
 \sum_\xi B_\xi^2
 \ge2^{-n}\left(\sum_{y\in E_\perp}\sqrt{\tau_y}\right)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(0<\tau_y\le\tau_{\max}\),
\[
 \sqrt{\tau_y}\ge\frac{\tau_y}{\sqrt{\tau_{\max}}}.
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (21)--(23) yield
\[
 S\ge
 \frac12\left(
  2^{-n}\frac{T_E^2}{\tau_{\max}}-T_E
 \right).
\tag{24}
\]
By (7), \(\tau_{\max}<Q/(400\,2^n)\). Therefore
\[
 S>
 \frac12\left(400\frac{T_E^2}{Q}-T_E\right).
\tag{25}
\]
For the positive quadratic term use the strict lower bound in (5), while for the subtracted term use the safe upper bound (6). This gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S
 &>\frac12\left(
  400\left(\frac{99}{100}\right)^2Q-Q
 \right)\\
 &=\frac{39104}{200}Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Every pair in (20) has an integer midpoint because its endpoints have the same parity, so the families (9) partition the pairs counted by \(S\). Hence
\[
 S=\sum_mS_m,
\tag{27}
\]
where empty families contribute zero.

Fix \(m\) with \(\mathcal P_m\ne\varnothing\), and take \(\{u,v\}\in\mathcal P_m\). Let \(c_u,c_v\) be the centroids of the positive-volume fibers \(C_u,C_v\). The full-mass densities show
\[
 c_u\in\mathsf M_{a_u}(C_u),
 \qquad
 c_v\in\mathsf M_{a_v}(C_v).
\tag{28}
\]
The positive-root interpolation proved in accepted fact `dfd25e72c1f45a4e` therefore supplies a metronoid point in the midpoint fiber \(C_m\) of mass
\[
 b_{u,v}:=
 \left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2\right)^d.
\tag{29}
\]
In particular, \(a_m\ge b_{u,v}\). The arithmetic--geometric mean inequality and (4) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a_m
 &\ge
 \left(\frac{a_u^{1/d}+a_v^{1/d}}2\right)^d
 \ge\sqrt{a_ua_v}\\
 &\ge4\sqrt{\tau_u\tau_v}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Taking the maximum over \(\mathcal P_m\), then summing over the distinct integer midpoint fibers, proves
\[
 M_m\le\frac{a_m}{4},
 \qquad
 \sum_mM_m\le\frac W4.
\tag{31}
\]

The canonical reservoir lower bound in accepted fact `6b09aae189415c70` is
\[
 Q\ge\frac3{32}(W-4G).
\tag{32}
\]
Using (13) and rearranging (32),
\[
 W\le\frac{32}{3}Q+4G
 <\left(\frac{32}{3}+\frac1{25}\right)Q
 =\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (26), (27), (31), and (33) give
\[
 \frac{\sum_mS_m}{\sum_mM_m}
 \ge\frac{4S}{W}
 >\frac{117312}{1606}
 >73.
\tag{34}
\]
The denominator is positive because (8) gives at least two endpoints in one parity class. Therefore some midpoint with \(M_m>0\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{S_m}{M_m}>73,
\]
which is (11).

Every summand in \(S_m\) is at most \(M_m\). Consequently
\[
 S_m\le|\mathcal P_m|M_m.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (11) and (35) give \(|\mathcal P_m|>73\), hence the integer bound (12). Finally, reflection about \(m\) is an involution: once one endpoint \(u\) is fixed, its partner must be \(2m-u\). Thus two distinct unordered pairs in \(\mathcal P_m\) cannot share an endpoint. This proves pairwise endpoint-disjointness and completes the theorem.
