## Statement

Let \(p,q\ge1\), put \(r=p+q\), and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^r\) be a full-dimensional lattice polytope affinely isomorphic to the Cartesian product of a \(p\)-simplex and a \(q\)-simplex. Assume \(\operatorname{int}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^r=\{\mathbf0\}\). Retain the complete support \(D=P\cap\mathbb Z^r\), including all boundary lattice points, and define \(B(P)=\inf_{\boldsymbol\theta\in\mathbb R^r}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\exp(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I)\). Then \(B(P)<4^r\).

More precisely, let \(f_0,\ldots,f_p\) and \(g_0,\ldots,g_q\) be the two blocks of affine facet slack functions, normalized to equal one at \(\mathbf0\). There are positive coefficients \(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_p\) and \(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_q\), namely the barycentric coordinates of the two components of \(\mathbf0\) in their respective factors, such that

\[
\sum_{i=0}^p\beta_i f_i(\mathbf x)=1,
\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^q\gamma_j g_j(\mathbf x)=1
\qquad(\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^r).
\]

Label a maximal coefficient in each block by \(0\), so that \(\beta_0\ge\beta_i\) for every \(i\) and \(\gamma_0\ge\gamma_j\) for every \(j\). The explicit vector

\[
\boldsymbol\theta
=-(\log 2)\left(\sum_{i=1}^p\nabla f_i+\sum_{j=1}^q\nabla g_j\right)
\]

satisfies \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\exp(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I)<4^r\). No splitting of the standard lattice along the two geometric factors is assumed.

## Proof

Choose an affine isomorphism that sends \(P\) to the product of the two simplices. Pull back each factor's barycentric coordinate functions to \(\mathbb R^r\), and divide each function by its positive value at \(\mathbf0\). This gives the stated \(f_i,g_j\). Their denominators are the coefficients \(\beta_i,\gamma_j\). The barycentric sum identities give the two displayed relations. Moreover, \(P\) is exactly the set where all \(f_i,g_j\) are nonnegative, and its interior is exactly the set where they are all positive. Each normalized affine function has constant term one. In particular, for every \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\in\mathbb R^r\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_i\bigl(\pm(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\bigr)
&=1\pm\bigl(f_i(\mathbf I)-f_i(\mathbf J)\bigr),\\
g_j\bigl(\pm(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)\bigr)
&=1\pm\bigl(g_j(\mathbf I)-g_j(\mathbf J)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Omit \(f_0,g_0\) and define the remaining \(r\) bin coordinates of a point \(\mathbf I\in D\) by

\[
\mathbf k(\mathbf I)
=\bigl(\lfloor f_1(\mathbf I)\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor f_p(\mathbf I)\rfloor,
        \lfloor g_1(\mathbf I)\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor g_q(\mathbf I)\rfloor\bigr)
\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r.
\]

For \(1\le i\le p\) and \(1\le j\le q\), write \(u_i(\mathbf I)=f_i(\mathbf I)-\lfloor f_i(\mathbf I)\rfloor\) and \(v_j(\mathbf I)=g_j(\mathbf I)-\lfloor g_j(\mathbf I)\rfloor\). These fractional parts lie in \([0,1)\).

Fix a nonempty bin fiber \(D_{\mathbf k}=\{\mathbf I\in D:\mathbf k(\mathbf I)=\mathbf k\}\), and let its cardinality be \(m\). Define on this fiber

\[
\rho(\mathbf I)=\sum_{i=1}^p\frac{\beta_i}{\beta_0}u_i(\mathbf I),
\qquad
\omega(\mathbf I)=\sum_{j=1}^q\frac{\gamma_j}{\gamma_0}v_j(\mathbf I).
\]

Both quantities are nonnegative. Since the selected bin coordinates are fixed, the two barycentric sum identities imply, for \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\in D_{\mathbf k}\),

\[
f_0(\mathbf I)-f_0(\mathbf J)=-\bigl(\rho(\mathbf I)-\rho(\mathbf J)\bigr),
\qquad
g_0(\mathbf I)-g_0(\mathbf J)=-\bigl(\omega(\mathbf I)-\omega(\mathbf J)\bigr).
\]

If \(\mathbf I\ne\mathbf J\) belong to this fiber, every selected slack difference has absolute value strictly less than one. The displayed affine formulas show that all selected slacks of both \(\mathbf I-\mathbf J\) and \(\mathbf J-\mathbf I\) are positive. Both difference vectors are nonzero standard lattice points, so neither can lie in \(\operatorname{int}(P)\). Consequently at least one omitted slack difference is at most \(-1\), and at least one omitted slack difference is at least \(1\). There are exactly two omitted slacks. Thus either

\[
\rho(\mathbf I)-\rho(\mathbf J)\ge1,
\qquad
\omega(\mathbf I)-\omega(\mathbf J)\le-1,
\]

or both inequalities hold with \(\mathbf I,\mathbf J\) interchanged. This conclusion includes equality at an omitted facet; the argument only excludes strictly interior nonzero lattice points.

In particular, the \(\rho\)-values in the fiber are distinct. Enumerate its points as \(\mathbf I_1,\ldots,\mathbf I_m\) in increasing order of \(\rho\). Consecutive \(\rho\)-values increase by at least one, while consecutive \(\omega\)-values decrease by at least one. Nonnegativity of the first \(\rho\)-value and of the last \(\omega\)-value therefore gives

\[
\rho(\mathbf I_\ell)\ge\ell-1,
\qquad
\omega(\mathbf I_\ell)\ge m-\ell
\qquad(1\le\ell\le m).
\]

The maximal choices of \(\beta_0\) and \(\gamma_0\) ensure that every coefficient in the definitions of \(\rho,\omega\) is at most one. Hence every point of this fiber satisfies

\[
\sum_{i=1}^p u_i(\mathbf I_\ell)+\sum_{j=1}^q v_j(\mathbf I_\ell)
\ge\rho(\mathbf I_\ell)+\omega(\mathbf I_\ell)
\ge m-1.
\]

It follows that the entire fiber has the weighted bound

\[
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf k}}
2^{-\sum_{i=1}^p u_i(\mathbf I)-\sum_{j=1}^q v_j(\mathbf I)}
\le m\,2^{-(m-1)}\le1.
\]

The last inequality holds for every positive integer \(m\), since \(2^{m-1}=(1+1)^{m-1}\ge1+(m-1)=m\). It also covers a singleton fiber.

For the stated vector \(\boldsymbol\theta\), affineness and the normalization at \(\mathbf0\) yield \(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I=(\log2)(r-\sum_{i=1}^p f_i(\mathbf I)-\sum_{j=1}^q g_j(\mathbf I))\). Regrouping the finite support by its bin fibers, and applying the weighted bound to each fiber, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I}
&=2^r\sum_{\mathbf k:D_{\mathbf k}\ne\varnothing}
2^{-\sum_{a=1}^r k_a}
\sum_{\mathbf I\in D_{\mathbf k}}
2^{-\sum_{i=1}^p u_i(\mathbf I)-\sum_{j=1}^q v_j(\mathbf I)}\\
&\le2^r\sum_{\mathbf k:D_{\mathbf k}\ne\varnothing}2^{-\sum_{a=1}^r k_a}\\
&<2^r\sum_{\mathbf k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^r}2^{-\sum_{a=1}^r k_a}
=2^r\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\right)^r
=4^r.
\end{aligned}
\]

The strict inequality holds because there are only finitely many occupied fibers, whereas the full nonnegative integer grid is infinite and every summand is positive. Taking the infimum over all real tilts proves \(B(P)<4^r\).

All support points, including those on facets, were included. The only lattice operation used was that the difference of two standard lattice points is again a standard lattice point. In particular, no factorwise lattice decomposition, vertex-index bound, or count of contributing cosets enters the proof.
