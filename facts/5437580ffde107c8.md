## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and retain the locked notation \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d\) and \(S=C\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\). For \(\mathbf y\in S\), its depth is \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces. For every \(\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\), put \(C_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf u\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf I,\mathbf u)\in C\}\) and \(w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d(C_{\mathbf I})\). Set \(D=\{\mathbf I:w_{\mathbf I}>0\}\) and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal H_d(S)\).

When \(W>0\), assume that for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) there are integers \(a_i\le b_i\) and a scalar table \(w^i=(w^i_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}\) that is positive on \(D_i=\{a_i,\ldots,b_i\}\), zero at every other integer, and satisfies \((w^i_t)^2\ge w^i_{t-1}w^i_{t+1}\) for every \(t\in\mathbb Z\). Assume the entire original fiber-volume table is exactly

\[
w_{\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^n w^i_{I_i},&\mathbf I\in\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^n D_i,\\
0,&\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^n D_i.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, \(D=\prod_iD_i\). A zero-volume fiber outside \(D\) may be empty or nonempty and lower-dimensional. The displayed condition prescribes its volume, not its emptiness. Beyond convexity of \(C\) and these complete table assumptions, there are no restrictions on the shapes, centroids, or translations of the fibers, and \(C\) need not be a Cartesian product. Singleton intervals are permitted. When \(W=0\), impose no factorization hypothesis.

Use the explicit constants from accepted fact `9cffeaea85f6fe61`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_\ast&=64000,& M_\ast&=(16\cdot27000)^2,\\
c&=\frac{e^{-8800K_\ast}}{2(1+8M_\ast)}
\left(\frac{e^{-8}}2\right)^{M_\ast},&
c_0&=\min\left\{\frac c6,\frac1{2e}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(c_0\in(0,1/e)\), and there exists \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) containing \(\mathbf y^\star\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W.
\]

The coefficient has no dependence on \(n\), \(d\), the factor lengths, or \(C\). The conclusion also holds if the complete separability condition is satisfied after an affine transformation

\[
\mathscr T(\mathbf x,\mathbf u)
=(\mathbf U\mathbf x+\mathbf z_0,
  \mathbf u+\mathbf A\mathbf x+\mathbf t_0),
\qquad
\mathbf U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z),\quad
\mathbf z_0\in\mathbb Z^n,\quad
\mathbf A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n},\quad
\mathbf t_0\in\mathbb R^d.
\]

This last assertion is conditional on the existence of the indicated chart; it does not assert that every actual table has a separable representation.

## Proof

If \(W=0\), choose any \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\), which exists by assumption. Every containing-halfspace mixed volume is nonnegative and hence at least \(c_0 2^{-n}W=0\). This case requires no positive-support optimization, potential, root, or centroid. We henceforth assume \(W>0\) and the complete factorization in the statement.

We first fix the original measures and the notation map for the three predecessors. Let \(\mu_{\mathbf I}=\mathcal L^d|_{C_{\mathbf I}}\) at every integer base. For an ambient Borel set, \(\mu_C\) is the sum of these measures embedded in their original fibers. Thus, for every ambient halfspace \(H\), with \(H_{\mathbf I}=\{\mathbf u:(\mathbf I,\mathbf u)\in H\}\),

\[
\mu_C(H)=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}\mu_{\mathbf I}(H_{\mathbf I})
=\mathcal H_d(S\cap H),
\qquad \mu_C(\mathbb R^{n+d})=W.
\]

These are exactly \(C_{\mathbf I},\mu_{\mathbf I},w_{\mathbf I},D,W,\mu_C\) of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, and \(C_{\mathbf I},w_{\mathbf I},D,W\) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. The equality with Hausdorff measure uses the locked fiber normalization: \(\mathbf u\mapsto(\mathbf I,\mathbf u)\) is an isometry, and its \(d\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure agrees with \(\mathcal L^d\). Fibers outside \(D\) have zero measure even when nonempty. Compactness of the base projection gives only finitely many nonempty integer fibers, so all these sums are finite. No source measure is normalized to probability or replaced by a measure on another body.

In accepted fact `9cffeaea85f6fe61`, take \(N=n\), the same \(D_i,w^i,D,w,W\), and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_i&=\sum_{t\in D_i}w^i_t,&
w_{\max,i}&=\max_{t\in D_i}w^i_t,&
\phi^i_t&=\log\frac{w_{\max,i}}{w^i_t},\\
W&=\prod_{i=1}^n W_i,&
w_{\max}&=\max_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}
=\prod_{i=1}^n w_{\max,i},&
\phi_{\mathbf I}&=\log\frac{w_{\max}}{w_{\mathbf I}}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\phi^i_{I_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The maxima and sums factor because the entire positive support is the full finite product and every factor is positive there. The \(W_i\) are scalar profile totals; the actual source capacities are still the original \(w_{\mathbf I}\). The potential \(\phi\) and maximum \(w_{\max}\) are exactly those of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. All logarithms are natural, and no potential is evaluated at a zero-volume base. The integer dimension \(N\) of the packing theorem is the original \(n\), while \(d\) remains the original continuous dimension.

For clarity, we specify the scores being composed. At \(\mathbf J\in D\), let \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) be the finite family of nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) with \(\mathbf J\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\), and let \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}>0\) be the unique coefficients with \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}=1\) and \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\mathbf I=\mathbf J\). The singleton \(\{\mathbf J\}\) is included. Define the original roots \(a_{\mathbf I}=w_{\mathbf I}^{1/d}\) on \(D\) and the two costs and cutoff families by

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_E&=\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}-\phi_{\mathbf J},&
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)&=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:G_E\le L\},\\
L_E&=\log\frac{w_{\mathbf J}}
{\left(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}a_{\mathbf I}\right)^d},&
\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)&=\{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}:L_E\le L\},
\qquad L\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The indexed \(a_{\mathbf I}\) are volume roots, not the scalar interval endpoints \(a_i\). These root costs and families are exactly those of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`; the logarithmic costs and families are exactly those of accepted facts `488f0a583886b5fc` and `9cffeaea85f6fe61`. There is no factor of \(d\) or \(2\) added to either cutoff.

Use the full-occurrence programs

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\max\left\{\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D)\right\},\\
P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\max\left\{\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)}x_E:
x_E\ge0,\quad
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le w_{\mathbf I}\quad(\mathbf I\in D)\right\},\\
m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)
&=\min\left\{\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}:
F\subseteq D,\quad F\cap E\ne\varnothing
\quad(E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L))\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The semicolon only displays the table argument, so \(P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)=P_d(\mathbf J,L)\) and \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)=m_d(\mathbf J,L)\) of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`. Every participating source is charged the whole mass \(x_E\), not \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}x_E\), and all components share the same original capacities. With these mass units, the optimized scores are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_{\log}(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L},\\
\mathcal B_d(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L},&
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)&=\max_{\mathbf J\in D,\ L\ge0}
\frac{m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)}{1+L}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are the canonical scores, not normalized by \(W\). Their ratios to \(W\) would be the normalized scores. In particular, the complete profile hypotheses are exactly those of accepted fact `9cffeaea85f6fe61`, which gives
\(\mathcal B_{\log}(w)\ge c2^{-n}W\) with the explicit \(c\) in the statement. It also supplies global extendability of this entire table, including its zeros. No realization of the table by a substitute body is used.

Accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc` gives \(P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w)\) at every original center and every cutoff. Its applicability here is to the actual body \(C\), its actual continuous dimension \(d\), and the actual capacities just fixed. The cost comparison behind it has the exact normalization

\[
0\le L_E
\le\log w_{\mathbf J}-\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\log w_{\mathbf I}
=G_E.
\]

Nonnegativity of the actual root cost is part of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893`, and the upper bound follows from weighted arithmetic-geometric mean applied to the positive roots. Consequently \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}^{\log}(L)\subseteq\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\), with unchanged capacities and the same weak cutoff. Every logarithmic packing is therefore a root packing of the same mass.

Accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` gives \(m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\ge P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)\). This comparison uses no arity loss: for any feasible root packing and any hitting set \(F\), full-occurrence charging gives

\[
\sum_{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)}x_E
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}
\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}(L)\\\mathbf I\in E}}x_E
\le\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}w_{\mathbf I}.
\]

Taking the maximum over packings and minimum over hitting sets proves the stated direction. Thus, pointwise and then after optimization,

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_d(\mathbf J,L;w)&\ge P_d(\mathbf J,L;w)
\ge P_{\log}(\mathbf J,L;w),\\
\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)&\ge\mathcal B_d(w)
\ge\mathcal B_{\log}(w)\ge c2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

We now obtain an original geometric witness. Attainment of \(\mathcal B_{\log}\) is part of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`; choose \(\mathbf J^\star\in D\) and \(L^\star\ge0\) with \(P_{\log}(\mathbf J^\star,L^\star;w)/(1+L^\star)=\mathcal B_{\log}(w)\). Apply the fiberwise conclusion of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` at this same center and cutoff. It gives an attained maximum at some \(\mathbf u^\star\in C_{\mathbf J^\star}\), with

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((\mathbf J^\star,\mathbf u^\star))
&=\max_{\mathbf u\in C_{\mathbf J^\star}}h_S((\mathbf J^\star,\mathbf u))\\
&\ge\frac{m_d(\mathbf J^\star,L^\star;w)}{6(1+L^\star)}\\
&\ge\frac{P_{\log}(\mathbf J^\star,L^\star;w)}{6(1+L^\star)}
=\frac{\mathcal B_{\log}(w)}6\\
&\ge\frac c6\,2^{-n}W
\ge c_0\,2^{-n}W.
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently, with the canonical \(h_{\max}(C)=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)\), the global conclusion of that same accepted fact is \(h_{\max}(C)\ge\mathcal B_d^{\mathrm{hit}}(w)/6\), with an attained original mixed point. The fiberwise application above additionally identifies a witnessing original fiber from the logarithmic packing center. Define \(\mathbf y^\star=(\mathbf J^\star,\mathbf u^\star)\in S\). All choices have now been made, independently of a containing halfspace. For every closed ambient \(H\) containing this point, the definition of \(h_S\) and the original-measure identity give \(\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)=\mu_C(H)\ge h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge c_0 2^{-n}W\). This includes vertical halfspaces and every boundary equality. The point need not be the original fiber centroid, and its construction does not translate or reshape any fiber.

All factors have been used in the original \(n\) integer coordinates. Accepted fact `9cffeaea85f6fe61` explicitly includes singleton intervals, and the actual-body point conclusion of accepted fact `7fa5a28fe1ac6893` does not require full affine rank of \(D\). Affine independence and relative interior in \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf J}\) already apply within the possibly lower-dimensional positive support. In rank zero, \(D=\{\mathbf J\}\), the sole component is its zero-cost singleton, and \(P_d(\mathbf J,0;w)=m_d(\mathbf J,0;w)=W\); the same fiberwise conclusion applies. No integer coordinate is discarded, \(N=n\) and \(2^{-n}\) are unchanged, and no lattice-index, affine-chart, or Jacobian multiplier is introduced. All other original fibers retain their prescribed zero capacity throughout.

For the optional chart assertion, \(\mathscr T\) is an invertible affine map of the ambient space and maps \(\Lambda\) bijectively onto itself, because \(\mathbf U\) is unimodular and \(\mathbf z_0\) is integral. Write \(\widetilde C=\mathscr T(C)\) and \(\widetilde S=\mathscr T(S)=\widetilde C\cap\Lambda\), and use \(\widetilde w,\widetilde W,\mu_{\widetilde C}\) for the corresponding fiber-volume table, total weight, and mixed-volume measure of \(\widetilde C\). At every integer base, its exact fiber and volume are

\[
\widetilde C_{\mathbf U\mathbf I+\mathbf z_0}
=C_{\mathbf I}+\mathbf A\mathbf I+\mathbf t_0,
\qquad
\widetilde w_{\mathbf U\mathbf I+\mathbf z_0}=w_{\mathbf I},
\qquad \widetilde W=W.
\]

Thus this map merely reindexes the complete table and translates each continuous fiber, preserving both positive and zero volumes. Fiberwise translation invariance gives \(\mu_{\widetilde C}=(\mathscr T)_\#\mu_C\). Apply the proved result to \(\widetilde C\), assuming its entire table satisfies the stated product condition, and pull its witness back by \(\mathscr T^{-1}\). Every original containing closed ambient halfspace is mapped to a containing closed ambient halfspace of the image body, and the corresponding mixed volumes agree by the measure identity. The original point therefore satisfies the same bound, with the same \(n,d,W,c_0\). There is no vertical volume Jacobian, since the continuous part of the map on each fixed fiber is a translation.

Finally, the displayed \(c\) is a positive absolute real number, and \(0<c_0\le1/(2e)<1/e\). Neither constant depends on a dimension, a factor length, a fiber shape, or a choice of chart. The factorization hypothesis remains a restriction on the complete original table, so this conclusion does not cover nonseparable tables.
