## Statement

Let $P\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be finite, with capacities $a_z>0$ for $z\in P$. Fix a mass $m\ge0$ and a vector $g\in\mathbb R^n$, and assume
$$
Q=\{b:0\le b_z\le a_z,\ \sum_{z\in P}b_z=m,\ \sum_{z\in P}z b_z=mg\}
$$
is nonempty. A point of $Q$ is extreme exactly when the columns $(1,z)$ at its partially filled sites $0<b_z<a_z$ are linearly independent. Every linear cost $\sum_z c_zb_z$ has an extreme minimizer, and this minimizer has at most $\operatorname{rank}((1,z)_{z\in P})\le n+1$ partially filled sites.

There are a scalar $\lambda$ and vector $v\in\mathbb R^n$ such that the minimum value is
$$
\lambda m+v\cdot(mg)+\sum_{z\in P}a_z\min(0,c_z-\lambda-v\cdot z).
$$
For this pair a feasible $b$ is optimal exactly when $b_z=a_z$ at each strict negative reduced cost $c_z-\lambda-v\cdot z$ and $b_z=0$ at each strict positive one. If $c_z=\|z-g\|_2^2$ and $m>0$, the reduced cost is the squared distance from $g+v/2$ minus a nonnegative squared radius. Every optimizer thus fills the strict interior sites of the corresponding closed ball, leaves its exterior sites empty, and can be fractional only on its sphere.

## Proof

If the partially filled columns at $b$ are dependent, choose a nonzero vector $h$ supported on their sites with $\sum_z h_z(1,z)=0$. Both $b+\epsilon h$ and $b-\epsilon h$ lie in $Q$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, so $b$ is not extreme. Conversely, if $b$ is a midpoint of distinct points of $Q$, they agree with $b$ at its zero and full sites. Their difference is a nonzero dependence on the partially filled columns. A linear cost attains its minimum by compactness. Choose a minimizer with the fewest partially filled sites. Any such dependence $h$ has zero cost by the two-sided feasible perturbation. Moving along it until the first box constraint binds produces a minimizer with fewer partial sites. Thus the chosen minimizer is extreme.

For the multiplier assertion, fix a minimizer $b^*$ and put $Ah=\sum_z h_z(1,z)$. Consider the cone generated by the range of $A^{\mathsf T}$, the coordinate vectors $e_z$ at zero sites of $b^*$, and the vectors $-e_z$ at full sites. This cone is closed: use both signs of a basis for the range to get a finite generator list, then eliminate dependent generators in each nonnegative representation until the remaining generators are independent. The cone is a finite union of closed cones generated by independent lists. If the cost vector $c$ were outside the cone, separation would give $h$ with $c\cdot h<0$ and nonnegative inner product with every vector in the cone. Then $Ah=0$, $h_z\ge0$ at zero sites, and $h_z\le0$ at full sites. Thus $b^*+\epsilon h$ would improve the cost for small positive $\epsilon$. Hence $c$ belongs to the cone and can be written $c_z=\lambda+v\cdot z+\rho_z$, where $\rho_z\ge0$ at zero sites, $\rho_z\le0$ at full sites, and $\rho_z=0$ at partially filled sites of $b^*$.

For every feasible $b$,
$$
\sum_z c_zb_z
=\lambda m+v\cdot(mg)+\sum_z\rho_z b_z
\ \ge\ \lambda m+v\cdot(mg)+\sum_z a_z\min(0,\rho_z).
$$
Termwise equality holds exactly under the stated sign rule, and it holds at $b^*$. This proves the common-multiplier assertion, including boundary allocations. For the quadratic cost, completing the square gives $\rho_z=\|z-(g+v/2)\|_2^2-(\lambda+v\cdot g+\|v\|_2^2/4)$. If $m>0$, some optimal $b_z>0$, hence some $\rho_z\le0$. The displayed squared radius is nonnegative.

The rank bound counts only partial sites. For example one can take arbitrarily many collinear sites of unit capacity and prescribe their full total mass and mean. Every site is full while the column rank stays two. The lemma constrains only base moments and does not assert feasibility of additional continuous moments.
