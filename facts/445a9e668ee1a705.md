## Statement

Let L be a positive integer. For each i in \(\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\), let \(n_i\ge1\) be an integer, let \(A_i\subset\mathbb Z^{n_i}\) be finite and nonempty, and choose arbitrary positive weights \(w_{i,x}\) for \(x\in A_i\). Define the original laws and normalizers by
$$
Z_i=\sum_{x\in A_i}w_{i,x},\qquad
p_{i,x}=\frac{w_{i,x}}{Z_i},\qquad
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i,\qquad N=\sum_{i=1}^L n_i.
\tag{1}
$$
Every law is extended by zero off its support. In particular,
$$
P_{(x_1,\ldots,x_L)}=\frac{\prod_i w_{i,x_i}}{\prod_i Z_i}.
$$
For each supported center j define
$$
R_{i,j}=\lbrace x\in A_i:2j-x\in A_i\rbrace,\qquad
r_i=\max_{j\in A_i}|R_{i,j}|,
$$
$$
D_i=\max_{x\in A_i}\left|\lbrace j\in A_i:j\ne x,\ x\in R_{i,j}\rbrace\right|,
\qquad
\Gamma_i=\operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(r_i^2(D_i+1)\bigr).
\tag{2}
$$
Rows may have arbitrary odd cardinalities and may overlap without restriction other than the measured incidence \(D_i\).

Retain exactly the actual depth and reflected quantities of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc: for a law p on \(\mathbb Z^n\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau_p(j)&=\inf\lbrace p(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^n
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }j\rbrace,\\
H_p(j)&=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^n}\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}},\\
b(p)&=\sum_jp_jH_p(j)^2,\qquad
\chi(p)=\sum_jp_jH_p(j),\\
M_2(p)&=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^2,\qquad
M_3(p)=\sum_jp_j\tau_p(j)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$
The sum for H is oriented: both nonzero orientations occur and zero occurs once. Retain also exactly the geometric-row notation of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{i,j}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,2j-x}}}{H_{p_i}(j)}
\quad(x\in R_{i,j}),\\
Y_{i,j}(x)&=\frac12\log\frac{p_{i,x}}{p_{i,2j-x}},\qquad
V_{i,j}=\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}^2,\\
q_{i,j}&=\frac{p_{i,j}H_{p_i}(j)^2}{b(p_i)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$
These auxiliary laws do not replace any original law in (1) or (3). The definitions in (4) are used here for arbitrary rows, with no matching hypothesis. Then
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}X=j,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}Y_{i,j}=0,
\tag{5}
$$
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_{i,j}}\log\frac{\kappa_{i,j}(X)}{p_{i,X}}
=-\log H_{p_i}(j),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\kappa_{i,j}}\left(\log\frac{\kappa_{i,j}(X)}{p_{i,X}}\right)=V_{i,j},
\tag{6}
$$
and the incidence price satisfies
$$
\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}V_{i,j}
\le\Gamma_i^2
\le\bigl(\log2+2\log r_i+\log(D_i+1)\bigr)^2.
\tag{7}
$$
In particular the standard deviation bound is logarithmic in the support-complexity parameters.

For the original product law and all ambient containing closed halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^N\),
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^L\Gamma_i^2}\right)
\prod_{i=1}^L b(p_i),
\tag{8}
$$
$$
M_3(P)\ge\frac18\exp\left(-3\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^L\Gamma_i^2}\right)
\prod_{i=1}^L b(p_i)^{3/2}.
\tag{9}
$$
The reflected factors retain the exact original normalization
$$
b(p_i)=\frac1{Z_i^3}\sum_{j\in A_i}w_{i,j}
\left(\sum_{x\in R_{i,j}}\sqrt{w_{i,x}w_{i,2j-x}}\right)^2.
\tag{10}
$$
All assertions include singleton and lower-dimensional supports.

Suppose additionally that each \(A_i=Q_i\cap\mathbb Z^{n_i}\), where \(Q_i\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_i}\) is convex, and that p_i is midpoint-log-concave in the following precise sense:
$$
p_{i,(x+y)/2}\ge\sqrt{p_{i,x}p_{i,y}}
\quad\text{whenever }x,y\in A_i\text{ and }(x+y)/2\in\mathbb Z^{n_i}.
\tag{11}
$$
For \(c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n_i}\), write \(p_i(c+2\mathbb Z^{n_i})\) for its original parity-class mass. Then
$$
\chi(p_i)\ge\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n_i}}
p_i(c+2\mathbb Z^{n_i})^2\ge2^{-n_i},
\qquad b(p_i)\ge4^{-n_i}.
\tag{12}
$$
Consequently (8)--(9) give
$$
4^N M_2(P)\ge\frac14\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\Gamma_i^2}\right),
\qquad
8^N M_3(P)\ge\frac18\exp\left(-3\sqrt{\sum_i\Gamma_i^2}\right).
\tag{13}
$$
In particular, fix numerical bounds \(r_0\ge1\) and \(D_0\ge0\), and put
\(\Gamma_0=\operatorname{arcosh}(r_0^2(D_0+1))\).
Every one-support law p satisfying (11), completeness, \(r\le r_0\), and \(D\le D_0\), in any ambient dimension n, obeys the absolute-coefficient bounds
$$
M_2(p)\ge\frac14e^{-2\Gamma_0}4^{-n},\qquad
M_3(p)\ge\frac18e^{-3\Gamma_0}8^{-n}.
\tag{14}
$$
The constants in (14) are independent of n, the support, and the weights. For independent products the exact loss is (8)--(9); in particular it is an absolute coefficient if the total price \(\sum_i\Gamma_i^2\) is absolutely bounded.

## Proof

First fix one law p and suppress its block index. Thus A is finite and nonempty, p is positive on A, and R_j,r,D are given by (2). Set
$$
S_j=\sum_{x\in R_j}p_x.
$$
The point j belongs to R_j. The involution \(x\mapsto2j-x\) preserves R_j, so
$$
0<p_j\le H_p(j)=\sum_{x\in R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}\le S_j.
\tag{15}
$$
The upper inequality follows by summing \(\sqrt{uv}\le(u+v)/2\) over the entire row. In particular
$$
b(p)\ge\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3>0,
\tag{16}
$$
so q in (4) is a probability law. Factoring each probability as w/Z proves (10).

The same row involution preserves \(\kappa_j\), reverses the sign of Y_j, and pairs X with 2j-X. This proves both assertions in (5), including singleton rows. Direct substitution gives the exact likelihood identity
$$
\log\frac{\kappa_j(x)}{p_x}=-\log H_p(j)-Y_j(x).
\tag{17}
$$
Thus (6) follows from the zero score mean. These calculations prove the canonical geometric-row identities without imposing the restricted support assumptions of accepted fact 4d36c889dd0a9ba2.

There is also an exact row identity with the original row mass:
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_p(j)\mathbb E_{\kappa_j}\cosh Y_j
&=\frac12\sum_{x\in R_j}(p_x+p_{2j-x})\\
&=S_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
$$
Sample J with law q and then X conditionally with law \(\kappa_J\), and let \(T=Y_J(X)\). Averaging (18), using (15), gives
$$
\mathbb E\cosh T
=\frac1{b(p)}\sum_{j\in A}p_jH_p(j)S_j
\le\frac1{b(p)}\sum_{j\in A}S_j^3.
\tag{19}
$$
For every row, Holder's inequality gives
$$
S_j^3\le |R_j|^2\sum_{x\in R_j}p_x^3
\le r^2\sum_{x\in R_j}p_x^3.
\tag{20}
$$
Each atom x occurs in its own row R_x and in at most D further supported rows, exactly as counted in (2). No disjointness is used. Summing (20), and then using (16), proves
$$
\sum_{j\in A}S_j^3
\le r^2(D+1)\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3
\le r^2(D+1)b(p).
\tag{21}
$$
Hence \(\mathbb E\cosh T\le r^2(D+1)\).

For completeness, the scalar concavity used in accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc applies here with this incidence bound. The function
\(f(u)=\operatorname{arcosh}(u)^2\) is increasing and concave for \(u\ge1\). Indeed, for \(u=\cosh t\), \(t>0\),
$$
f'(u)=\frac{2t}{\sinh t}>0,\qquad
f''(u)=\frac{2(\sinh t-t\cosh t)}{\sinh^3t}\le0.
$$
The final sign holds because \(\sinh t-t\cosh t\) is zero at t=0 and has derivative \(-t\sinh t\le0\); continuity extends concavity and monotonicity to u=1. Since \(f(\cosh T)=T^2\), Jensen's inequality and (19)--(21) imply
$$
\sum_{j\in A}q_jV_j=\mathbb ET^2
\le f(\mathbb E\cosh T)
\le\operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(r^2(D+1)\bigr)^2.
\tag{22}
$$
Finally, for every \(u\ge1\),
\(\operatorname{arcosh}u=\log(u+\sqrt{u^2-1})\le\log(2u)\).
This proves (7), including r=1,D=0, where the sharper price in (22) is zero.

Now consider the product law in (1) and a supported target \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\). Its full reflected row and reflected mass satisfy
$$
R_J=\prod_iR_{i,j_i},\qquad
H_P(J)=\prod_iH_{p_i}(j_i),\qquad
\kappa_J=\bigotimes_i\kappa_{i,j_i}.
\tag{23}
$$
The middle formula is also the reflection tensorization of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc; all three identities follow directly by factoring the finite geometric-mean sums. Define the product score and its conditional standard deviation by
$$
Y_J(X)=\sum_iY_{i,j_i}(X_i),\qquad
\sigma_J=\left(\sum_iV_{i,j_i}\right)^{1/2}.
$$
By conditional independence and (5),
$$
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}Y_J^2=\sigma_J^2,
\qquad P_X=H_P(J)\kappa_J(X)e^{Y_J(X)}.
\tag{24}
$$
The last identity is exact: in each block its right side reduces to \(p_{i,X_i}=w_{i,X_i}/Z_i\).

Let \(\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\) be any closed halfspace containing J. In each distinct reflected pair X,2J-X, at least one endpoint belongs to \(\mathcal H\). Indeed, a linear inequality strictly excluding both endpoints would strictly exclude their midpoint J. The central atom J belongs to \(\mathcal H\) as well. Summing over these disjoint reflection orbits gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathcal H)
&\ge\frac12\sum_{X\in R_J}\min\lbrace P_X,P_{2J-X}\rbrace\\
&=\frac{H_P(J)}2\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}e^{-|Y_J|}\\
&\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\mathbb E_{\kappa_J}|Y_J|}
\ge\frac{H_P(J)}2e^{-\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
$$
The oriented sum counts each noncentral orbit twice before its factor 1/2, and uses only half of the available central mass. Reflection preserves \(\kappa_J\) and negates Y_J, so (24) proves the equality in (25). The final two inequalities use Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz. Taking the infimum over every ambient containing closed halfspace proves (25) with \(P(\mathcal H)\) replaced by \(\tau_P(J)\). This argument neither assumes full-dimensional support nor discards a zero variance.

Use the auxiliary product center law \(q=\bigotimes_iq_i\). Squaring (25) and summing against the original law P gives the exact factorization
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\sum_JP_JH_P(J)^2e^{-2\sigma_J}\\
&=\frac14\left(\prod_i b(p_i)\right)\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
$$
Here \(P_JH_P(J)^2=(\prod_i b(p_i))\prod_iq_{i,j_i}\), so the original moment and all original normalizers have been preserved. By (22) in each block,
$$
\mathbb E_q\sigma_J^2
=\sum_i\sum_{j\in A_i}q_{i,j}V_{i,j}\le\sum_i\Gamma_i^2.
$$
Another application of Jensen and Cauchy--Schwarz yields
$$
\mathbb E_qe^{-2\sigma_J}
\ge e^{-2\mathbb E_q\sigma_J}
\ge\exp\left(-2\sqrt{\sum_i\Gamma_i^2}\right).
$$
This proves (8). Under the original probability P, Jensen for \(u\mapsto u^{3/2}\) gives
$$
M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}
\ge M_2(P)^{3/2},
$$
which proves (9).

To establish the convex corollary, fix one block and suppress its index again. If x,y in A have the same coordinatewise parity, their midpoint j=(x+y)/2 is an integer point of Q by convexity, and therefore belongs to A by completeness. The correspondence
$$
(j,v)\longleftrightarrow(x,y)=(j+v,j-v)
$$
is a bijection from supported reflected triples to ordered same-parity pairs in A. Assumption (11) consequently implies
$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi(p)
&=\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}
p_{(x+y)/2}\sqrt{p_xp_y}\\
&\ge\sum_{\substack{x,y\in A\\x\equiv y\pmod2}}p_xp_y
=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n}p(c+2\mathbb Z^n)^2
\ge2^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
$$
The final inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz for at most \(2^n\) nonzero parity masses summing to one. Jensen under the original law gives
$$
b(p)=\mathbb E_pH_p^2\ge(\mathbb E_pH_p)^2=\chi(p)^2\ge4^{-n}.
$$
This proves (12) without restricting p to log-affine laws. Substituting into (8)--(9) proves (13). Taking L=1 and using monotonicity of arcosh proves (14).

Finally, verify the relation to the accepted conversion obstructions. In the three-layer core \(D_d\) of accepted fact ce8cccfdbee6f1cc, put K=2^d as there. Central symmetry gives
$$
R_{\mathbf0}=D_d,\qquad |R_{\mathbf0}|=2K+1.
$$
Every nonzero point v is an exposed vertex, so its supported reflected row is the singleton \(R_v=\lbrace v\rbrace\): a nontrivial pair with midpoint v would contradict extremality. Thus, in the support-complexity notation (2),
$$
r(D_d)=2K+1,\qquad D(D_d)=1.
\tag{28}
$$
Indeed each nonzero atom occurs exactly in its own row and the origin row, and the origin occurs only in its own row. The specialization d=5h, B=2^h in that accepted fact has \(M_2/b\to0\); (28) shows precisely why a fixed incidence bound alone cannot give a universal conversion. Its maximum row cardinality diverges.

The stronger family A_k of the same accepted fact also has unbounded maximum row cardinality. Using its parameters m,K,R, choose any ordinary nonzero Boolean base vertex u. Reflection about \((u,0,\mathbf0,0)\) retains its entire ordinary fiber
\(\lbrace u\rbrace\times D_m\times\lbrace-R,\ldots,R\rbrace\).
That one row already has \((2K+1)(2R+1)\) points, tending to infinity. Hence neither accepted large-row family satisfies the fixed-r premise of (14). Their proxy-to-actual separation remains compatible with (8)--(14) and does not refute the original depth conjecture.
