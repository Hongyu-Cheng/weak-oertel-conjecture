## Statement

Let a nonempty finite list have records $(P_i,C_i,M_i)$ with $C_i>0$. Fix $V>0$ and $0<\gamma\le1/2$. A centered mixture is a probability vector $\nu$ with $\sum_i\nu_i M_i=0$; write $\bar P,\bar C$ for its averages. There are real $c,\lambda_L,\lambda_U$ such that $\gamma V+\lambda_LM_i\le P_i-cC_i\le(1-\gamma)V+\lambda_UM_i$ for all $i$ exactly when the intervals
$$
\left[\frac{\bar P-(1-\gamma)V}{\bar C},\frac{\bar P-\gamma V}{\bar C}\right]
$$
have a common point. It suffices to use all singletons of zero moment and all two-point mixtures of strictly opposite moments with their unique positive weights giving moment zero. If there is no centered mixture the intersection condition is vacuous.

## Proof

Averaging gives necessity. For sufficiency fix a common $c$. Each zero $M_i$ imposes exactly the singleton condition. For the lower inequality, each $M_i>0$ bounds $\lambda_L$ from above and each $M_i<0$ bounds it from below. Comparing one lower to one upper bound is exactly the lower inequality averaged against the two-point mixture canceling those moments. Thus every such comparison holds. A finite list of bounds with either sign absent is feasible as well. For the upper inequality, apply the same argument to $-M_i$ and $(1-\gamma)V-P_i+cC_i$, giving $\lambda_U$ independently.

The polytope of centered probabilities has extreme points with support of size at most two, since on three or more positive coordinates there is a nonzero two-sided perturbation preserving both linear equalities. A two-point extreme support has strictly opposite moments; if both moments vanish the vector decomposes into singletons. Both interval endpoints of a convex combination of centered laws are averages of their corresponding endpoints with the positive weights reweighted by their $\bar C$ values. Thus checking extreme laws suffices. For an arbitrary index set containing both a positive and a negative moment, the implication from a fixed common $c$ and all pair/singleton tests to finite multipliers also holds: one fixed index of each sign gives finite bounds for each multiplier, and the pairwise comparisons order the supremum of lower bounds below the infimum of upper bounds. In the absence of one sign, this last finiteness argument is unavailable for an infinite index set.
