## Statement

Let $K$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$. Suppose two compact convex paths satisfy $h_{K_r(\varepsilon)}=h_K+\varepsilon\phi_r+o(\varepsilon)$ uniformly on the unit sphere, with $\phi_r$ continuous, for $r=1,2$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. For fixed $t\in[0,1]$ the right derivative of the volume of $(1-t)K_1(\varepsilon)+tK_2(\varepsilon)$ at zero is
$$
(1-t)\int\phi_1\,dS_K+t\int\phi_2\,dS_K,
$$
the same combination of the individual volume derivatives. Here $S_K$ is the surface area measure of $K$.

## Proof

The support function of $L_\varepsilon=(1-t)K_1(\varepsilon)+tK_2(\varepsilon)$ is the same convex combination of the branch supports. Thus its uniform derivative is $\phi=(1-t)\phi_1+t\phi_2$. It suffices to prove the general whole-volume formula for a single path $L_\varepsilon$ with that derivative. In dimension one the length is $h_{L_\varepsilon}(1)+h_{L_\varepsilon}(-1)$ and the surface area measure of the interval has unit mass at each sign, giving the formula.

In higher dimensions, denote mixed volume by $V$ and mixed area measure by $S$. Use the mixed-volume representation $V(A,B_1,\ldots,B_{d-1})=d^{-1}\int h_A\,dS(B_1,\ldots,B_{d-1})$. Telescoping the $d$ slots from $K$ to $L_\varepsilon$ gives
$$
|L_\varepsilon|-|K|=\frac1d\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}
\int (h_{L_\varepsilon}-h_K)\,dS(L_\varepsilon[i],K[d-1-i]).
$$
Uniform support convergence implies Hausdorff convergence to $K$. Hence the mixed area measures converge weakly to $S_K$ by their continuity under Hausdorff convergence. In particular their total masses are bounded. The uniformly convergent quotients $(h_{L_\varepsilon}-h_K)/\varepsilon$ can therefore be replaced in each integral by the continuous limit $\phi$ at an error tending to zero. Weak convergence then gives the limit $\int\phi\,dS_K$ for each of the $d$ terms after division by $\varepsilon$. The factor $1/d$ cancels. Applying the same argument to each branch proves the exact linear combination.

The mixed-volume representation and continuity used here are the standard mixed-area-measure results in [Schneider, Convex Bodies, second expanded edition, §5.1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/convex-bodies-the-brunnminkowski-theory/400F6173EE613859F144E9598DDD8BDF).
