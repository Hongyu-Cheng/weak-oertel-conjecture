## Statement

Let $n\ge1,d\ge0,D=n+d$ and let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body. Work in fixed unimodular integer-base coordinates and use the actual projected mixed volumes $A_I(C)=\mu_{n-|I|,d}(\pi_I C)$. Put $M=\mu_{n,d}(C)>0$, $V=\operatorname{vol}_D(C)$, $E=\sum_{I\ne\varnothing} A_I(C)$, $V_i=\operatorname{vol}_{D-1}(\pi_{\{i\}}C)$, and $\Gamma_j=(j/(j+1))^j$ for $j\ge1$, $\Gamma_0=1$.

For the ordinary centroid $c$ and any real displacement $y=c+(\delta,0)$, every containing closed halfspace $H$ has ordinary cap volume at least $\Gamma_DV-\sum_i|\delta_i|V_i$, and $V_i\le\sum_{I\ni i}A_I(C)$. In particular, round the integer coordinates of $c$ independently to nearest integers with deterministic ties, leaving the other coordinates fixed. At the resulting $y$ every containing closed halfspace has raw mixed mass at least
$$
\Gamma_DV-E-\sum_i|\delta_i|V_i\ \ge\
B_\delta:=\Gamma_D M-\sum_{I\ne\varnothing}
(\Gamma_D+1+\sum_{i\in I}|\delta_i|)A_I(C).
$$
If $B_\delta>0$, this $y$ belongs to the actual mixed set $S$ and to its convex hull. Let $B$ be the same expression with $\sum_{i\in I}|\delta_i|$ replaced by $|I|/2$. If $B\ge2^{-n}\Gamma_d M$, the rounded point attains at least $2^{-n}\Gamma_d$ normalized depth. In all cases the maximum normalized depth on $S$ is at least
$$
\max\{\Gamma_d W/M,\ \max(0,B)/M\},
\qquad W=\max_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z).
$$

## Proof

For a closed cap through $y$, start with the parallel cap through $c$. At full volume, accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives its ordinary mass at least $\Gamma_D V$. Move the boundary through the coordinate displacements one at a time. In direction $e_i$ the cap is unchanged if its normal coefficient is zero. Otherwise on each line parallel to $e_i$ the cutoff in the $i$-th coordinate shifts by exactly $\delta_i$, with captured length loss at most $|\delta_i|$. Integrating on the ordinary projection bounds this loss by $|\delta_i|V_i$. A containing cap has at least the mass of its parallel through-$y$ cap. Apply the discrepancy lemma to $\pi_{\{i\}}C$ with its remaining integer coordinates to bound $V_i$ by $\sum_{I\ni i}A_I(C)$. With no remaining integer coordinates the comparison is equality.

The discrepancy lemma on the whole body gives $V\ge M-E$. On the containing cap it gives mixed mass at least ordinary volume minus $E$. Substitute these estimates and group the displacement costs to obtain $B_\delta$. Strict separation of $y$ from $C$, or from the compact hull of $S$, would give a containing closed halfspace of zero sampled mass. Thus positivity proves the two membership conclusions, and $|\delta_i|\le1/2$ gives $B_\delta\ge B$.

Independently, choose a centroid of a largest positive-volume fiber. For $d\ge1$, any containing ambient cap either restricts to a containing closed halfspace on that fiber or contains it all. The same accepted full-volume Grünbaum case gives at least $\Gamma_d W$ from the fiber. For $d=0$, the selected point itself contributes $W=1$. The rounded bound contributes if positive, and otherwise its nonnegative part is zero. Divide by $M$.
