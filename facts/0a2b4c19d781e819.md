## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,\qquad
\pi_z:=\frac{w_z}{W}.
\]
Let
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
and for \(p\in S\) define
\[
h_S(p):=
\inf\left\{
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\operatorname{vol}_d\{x\in C_z:(z,x)\in H\}:
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H
\right\}.
\]
Put \(D:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\). For \(z\in D\), define
\[
M(z):=
\max_{x\in C_z}
\inf\{\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in J\},
\]
and set \(M(z):=0\) for \(z\notin D\). On a nonempty lower-dimensional fiber this convention agrees with the displayed maximum; on an empty fiber it makes the otherwise undefined expression harmless.

Let \(z_0\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(\tau\in(0,1]\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\u^\top(z-z_0)\geq0}}\pi_z\geq\tau
\qquad
\text{for every }u\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\},
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal A_\tau(z_0):=
\left\{
(\alpha_z):
\alpha_z\geq0,\quad
\sum_z\alpha_z=1,\quad
\sum_z\alpha_zz=z_0,\quad
\alpha_z\leq\frac{\pi_z}{\tau}
\right\}.
\]
Then:

1. \(\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\neq\varnothing\).

2. For every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\), there are maximizers \(x_z\in C_z\) for all \(z\) with \(\alpha_z>0\) such that
\[
x_\alpha:=\sum_z\alpha_zx_z,\qquad
p_\alpha:=(z_0,x_\alpha)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
and
\[
h_S(p_\alpha)
\geq
\min\left\{
\tau W,\
\left(\sum_z\alpha_zM(z)^{1/d}\right)^d
\right\}.
\]

3. With
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha):=
\left(\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\right)^{-d},
\]
the same point satisfies
\[
h_S(p_\alpha)
\geq
\frac{\gamma_d}{N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)}\,\tau W.
\]

The last bound can be optimized over \(\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\), but no absolute upper bound on the optimized \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) follows.

## Proof

The projection of compact \(C\) onto \(\mathbb R^n\) is bounded, so it meets \(\mathbb Z^n\) in finitely many points. In particular, \(D\) is finite. If \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\) and \(z\notin D\), then
\[
0\leq\alpha_z\leq\frac{\pi_z}{\tau}=0,
\]
so \(\alpha_z=0\). Thus every sum involving \(\alpha\) is actually a finite sum over \(D\), and no maximizer on an empty or zero-volume fiber is ever selected. Accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` additionally proves that the displayed maximum is attained on every nonempty fiber and is zero on every nonempty lower-dimensional fiber. This justifies both the convention and every maximum used below.

Accepted fact `725defc391f9d55d` gives coefficients \((\eta_z)\) satisfying
\[
0\leq\eta_z\leq\pi_z,\qquad
\sum_z\eta_z=\tau,\qquad
\sum_z\eta_zz=\tau z_0.
\]
Setting \(\alpha_z:=\eta_z/\tau\) proves
\[
\alpha_z\geq0,\qquad
\sum_z\alpha_z=1,\qquad
\sum_z\alpha_zz=z_0,\qquad
\alpha_z\leq\frac{\pi_z}{\tau}.
\]
Hence \(\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\neq\varnothing\), proving assertion 1.

Fix an arbitrary \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_\tau(z_0)\), and let
\[
I:=\{z:\alpha_z>0\}.
\]
Then \(I\) is a nonempty finite subset of \(D\). For each \(z\in I\), use accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` to choose \(x_z\in C_z\) with fiber depth \(M(z)\). Since
\[
p_\alpha
=\sum_{z\in I}\alpha_z(z,x_z),
\]
convexity of \(C\), the equality \(\sum_z\alpha_z=1\), and the balance \(\sum_z\alpha_zz=z_0\) give
\[
p_\alpha=(z_0,x_\alpha)\in C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d).
\]

Write \(\delta_C(z,x)\) for the absolute fiber depth appearing in the definition of \(M(z)\). The joint root-concavity in accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, applied finitely many times, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_C(z_0,x_\alpha)^{1/d}
&\geq\sum_{z\in I}\alpha_z\delta_C(z,x_z)^{1/d}\\
&=\sum_z\alpha_zM(z)^{1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` permits the ambient halfspace-depth infimum at \(p_\alpha\) to be restricted to proper closed halfspaces whose boundary contains \(p_\alpha\), without assuming that the boundary has zero mixed-integer mass. Such a halfspace has the form
\[
H_{a,b}:=
\left\{(z,x):
a^\top(z-z_0)+b^\top(x-x_\alpha)\geq0
\right\}
\tag{2}
\]
for some \((a,b)\neq(0,0)\).

If \(b=0\), then \(a\neq0\), and the whole fiber over every \(z\) with \(a^\top(z-z_0)\geq0\) lies in \(H_{a,0}\). The marginal hypothesis, with its closed-boundary convention, therefore gives
\[
\mathcal H_d\!\left(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H_{a,0}\right)
=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\a^\top(z-z_0)\geq0}}w_z
\geq\tau W.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(b\neq0\), the part of \(H_{a,b}\) in the anchor fiber \(z=z_0\) is the closed halfspace
\[
J_b:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:b^\top(x-x_\alpha)\geq0\},
\]
whose boundary contains \(x_\alpha\). Nonnegativity of all other fiber contributions and the definition of fiber depth give, using (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_d\!\left(C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\cap H_{a,b}\right)
&\geq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_{z_0}\cap J_b)\\
&\geq\delta_C(z_0,x_\alpha)\\
&\geq
\left(\sum_z\alpha_zM(z)^{1/d}\right)^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3) and (4) show that every proper boundary-through-\(p_\alpha\) halfspace has mass at least the minimum in assertion 2. Accepted fact `978e8555ec5bac42` then transfers the same conclusion to every proper closed halfspace containing \(p_\alpha\): moving its boundary toward \(p_\alpha\) produces a contained boundary-through-\(p_\alpha\) halfspace, so positive boundary mass can only help. If the convention for “closed halfspace” also includes the whole space, its mass is \(W\geq\tau W\), hence it satisfies the same minimum bound. This proves assertion 2, including the vertical, nonvertical, boundary-mass, and whole-space cases.

For each \(z\in D\), the positive-volume compact convex set \(C_z\) is a full-dimensional convex body in \(\mathbb R^d\). Let \(c_z\) be its centroid. Grünbaum's centroid-halfspace inequality gives
\[
\delta_C(z,c_z)\geq\gamma_dw_z.
\]
By maximality of \(M(z)\),
\[
M(z)\geq\gamma_dw_z
\qquad(z\in D).
\tag{5}
\]
For \(z\notin D\), both sides of (5) are zero under the stated convention. On the other hand, the capacity constraint has the direction
\[
\alpha_z\leq\frac{\pi_z}{\tau}
=\frac{w_z}{\tau W}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w_z\geq\tau W\alpha_z.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (5) and (6), taking nonnegative \(d\)-th roots, multiplying by \(\alpha_z\), and summing gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_z\alpha_zM(z)^{1/d}
&\geq
\gamma_d^{1/d}(\tau W)^{1/d}
\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\left(\sum_z\alpha_zM(z)^{1/d}\right)^d
\geq
\gamma_d\tau W
\left(\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\right)^d
=\frac{\gamma_d}{N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)}\,\tau W.
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to check that the right side of (7) does not exceed the vertical term. Since \(\alpha\) is a finitely supported probability vector,
\[
0<\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}\leq\sum_z\alpha_z=1.
\]
Thus \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)\geq1\). Also \(0<\gamma_d<1\), and hence
\[
\frac{\gamma_d}{N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)}\,\tau W\leq\tau W.
\tag{8}
\]
Both entries in the minimum of assertion 2 are at least the quantity in (8), by (7) and (8). Assertion 3 follows.

This is not a reformulation of the accepted metronoid facts. Accepted fact `725defc391f9d55d` supplies nonemptiness and a barycenter formed from fiber centroids, but no depth bound for that barycenter. Accepted fact `ecdcb5a24b2eac2c` uses a convolution at the centroid barycenter and, for \(\eta_z=\tau\alpha_z\), retains only a minimum-coefficient bound of the form
\[
\frac{\tau W}{e}\min_{\alpha_z>0}\alpha_z.
\]
The present point uses fiber-depth maximizers and retains the root sum over the full coefficient vector. Accepted fact `a02e76f89b3b8062` instead optimizes the minimum original atom weight over sparse convex representations and can give a different, stronger bound in regimes with heavy atoms; it does not prove the every-\(\alpha\) root-depth inequality above.

Finally, the product-body calibration in accepted fact `a02e76f89b3b8062` shows that the remaining effective-number loss is real for this method. Let
\[
D_R=\{-R,\ldots,R\}^n,\qquad N_R=(2R+1)^n,\qquad
C_R=[-R,R]^n\times K,
\]
where \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a full-dimensional centrally symmetric convex body. The fiber marginal is uniform on \(D_R\), \(z_0=0\) has marginal depth at least \(1/2\), and hence the hypotheses hold with \(\tau=2^{-n}\). Every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_{2^{-n}}(0)\) obeys
\[
\alpha_z\leq\frac{2^n}{N_R}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_z\alpha_z^{1+1/d}
\leq
\left(\frac{2^n}{N_R}\right)^{1/d}\sum_z\alpha_z
=\left(\frac{2^n}{N_R}\right)^{1/d},
\qquad
N_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha)\geq\frac{N_R}{2^n}.
\]
For fixed \(n\), this diverges as \(R\to\infty\), even after optimizing over feasible \(\alpha\). The same bodies have normalized mixed depth \(1/2\) at \((0,0)\), so this does not refute the registered conditional bound or the locked target. It identifies exactly why the \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) consequence alone cannot close that target.
