## Statement

Let $C=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(3,0),(3,3),(2,10),(0,2)\}$ and let $C^{[k]}=\{(z,x_1,\ldots,x_k):(z,x_i)\in C\text{ for every }i\}$. Its integer fibers are products of the original intervals of lengths $l_z=(2,6,10,3)$ for $z=0,1,2,3$. For any sequence $a_k\in\mathbb R$, put on these products uniform conditional laws and weights proportional to $\exp(a_kz)l_z^k$. In this law there are mixed points of normalized closed-halfspace depth at least $\beta_k=1/[2(k+1)]$. For all sufficiently large $k$, depending on the tilt sequence, every such mixed point has average projection $(z,k^{-1}\sum_i x_i)$ of normalized depth strictly below $1/4$ for the original mixed length on $C$. When $a_k=0$ they eventually all have base $2$ and the average height tends uniformly to $5$. After symmetrizing any such center over all continuous-coordinate permutations, taking any coordinate gives the same failed projection.

## Proof

The original source has lower graph zero and the concave roof $2+4z$ on $[0,2]$, $24-7z$ on $[2,3]$. Thus its sampled intervals are $[0,l_z]$ with total length $21$ and midpoint heights $m_z=(1,3,5,3/2)$. The power is a convex body: it is an intersection of linear preimages, bounded, and repeating the continuous coordinate of any original interior point gives an interior point of the power. Each weighted power law is a finite Borel probability supported on $\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^k$. By 6d6fcf377e68c79c it has a lattice point of depth at least $\beta_k$. A point outside the compact convex power has a containing cap missing it by separation, so this point is in the mixed source. Averaging keeps its height in its original interval.

The following caps through the four original midpoint marks, in increasing base order, have respective raw masses $1,5,5,3/2$:
$$
x\ge11z+1,\qquad x\ge11(z-1)+3,\qquad x\ge9-2z,\qquad x\ge-11(z-3)+3/2.
$$
At any height within $1/8$ of the corresponding $m_z$, shift that cap's threshold by the height difference $\delta$. For bases $0,1,3$ only the own interval changes its length in the cap, by at most $|\delta|$. At base two the cap mass becomes $5-\delta+(-\delta)_+$, since only bases two and three can contribute positive length. In all cases the mass remains strictly below $21/4$.

For a uniform variable $U$ in any interval $[0,l_z]$, the centered variable $U-m_z$ has mean zero and lies in $[-5,5]$. Convexity of the exponential gives $\mathbb E e^{s(U-m_z)}\le\cosh(5s)\le e^{25s^2/2}$, the latter by integrating $\tanh t\le t$ for $t\ge0$ and using evenness. Independence and the exponential Markov inequality with $s=\varepsilon/25$ bound each one-sided deviation of the average by $\exp(-k\varepsilon^2/50)$. In the untilted law the mass outside base two is at most $3(3/5)^k$. Each point at a different base has a pure base cap excluding two, so eventually no such point has depth $\beta_k$. For any point at base two with average height at least $\varepsilon$ from $5$, the corresponding average-coordinate cap has mass at most $\exp(-k\varepsilon^2/50)+3(3/5)^k$. This is eventually less than $\beta_k$, uniformly over all eligible points.

For arbitrary tilts, argue by contradiction along an infinite sequence of eligible centers whose projections have original depth at least $1/4$. Pass to a subsequence on which $a_k/k$ tends to an extended-real value. For finite limit $b$, the limiting logarithmic weight per coordinate is $\log l_z+bz$. The strict inequalities $6^2>2\cdot10$ and $10^2>6\cdot3$ make its forward differences strictly decreasing, so the maximizers form a singleton or two adjacent integers $J$. Each remaining index has a strictly positive limiting exponent gap to any fixed index in $J$, giving exponentially small total probability outside $J$. For limit $+\infty$ or $-\infty$, the same gap bound holds for the singleton $J=\{3\}$ or $\{0\}$, respectively.

Let $P_J$ be the segment or singleton spanned by the marks $(z,m_z)$ for $z\in J$. For any projected point at distance at least $\varepsilon>0$ from $P_J$, take the closed halfplane through it with unit normal from its nearest point in $P_J$ toward it, on the side away from $P_J$. Its margin over every mark in $J$ is at least $\varepsilon$. At a fixed base in $J$ the continuous component of this normal has magnitude at most one, so entering this halfplane forces the corresponding one-sided deviation in average height of at least $\varepsilon$; if the continuous component vanishes it captures none of that fiber. Pulling the halfplane back under averaging gives a containing halfspace on the power with at most $\exp(-k\varepsilon^2/50)$ mass from $J$ and exponentially small mass from outside $J$. It cannot contain a center of depth at least $\beta_k$ for large $k$. Thus the projections approach $P_J$. Their bases are integers in the fixed set $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Since the ends of $P_J$ have equal or adjacent integer bases, any accumulation point is one of those midpoint marks. Therefore the projections eventually lie within the bad midpoint neighborhoods at their own base, a contradiction.

Permuting the $k$ coordinates preserves the law and the depth of the mark. Every containing closed halfspace at the average of the permuted marks contains at least one of them, since a strict affine inequality holding at each would also hold at their average. The symmetrized mark therefore still has depth at least $\beta_k$ and has all continuous coordinates equal to the old average, proving the last assertion.
