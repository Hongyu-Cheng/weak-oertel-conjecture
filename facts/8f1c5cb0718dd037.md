## Statement

For every integer \(j\geq2\), put
\[
n_j:=4j,
\qquad
T_j:=3^j,
\qquad
P_j:=[-1,1]^{n_j},
\]
and define the compact full-dimensional convex body
\[
C_j:=\operatorname{conv}\!\left(
P_j\times[-1,0]\;\cup\;\{(\mathbf0,T_j)\}
\right)
\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_j}\times\mathbb R.
\]
For \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{n_j}\), write
\[
(C_j)_{\mathbf z}:=
\{t\in\mathbb R:(\mathbf z,t)\in C_j\},
\qquad
a_{\mathbf z}:=\operatorname{vol}_1((C_j)_{\mathbf z}),
\]
where an empty fiber has volume zero. Let
\[
S_j:=C_j\cap(\mathbb Z^{n_j}\times\mathbb R),
\qquad
W_j:=\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{n_j}}a_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
M_j:=2^{-n_j}W_j.
\]
A target-mass base state is a pair \((\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\), where
\(\mathbf m\in\mathbb Z^{n_j}\) and
\(\mathbf b=(b_{\mathbf z})_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{n_j}}\), satisfying
\[
0\leq b_{\mathbf z}\leq a_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}=M_j,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}\mathbf z=M_j\mathbf m.
\]
Its integral-base variance is
\[
V_j(\mathbf m,\mathbf b)
:=
\frac1{M_j}\sum_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}
\lVert\mathbf z-\mathbf m\rVert_2^2.
\]

For an interval \(I\subseteq\mathbb R\) of finite positive length and
\(0<b\leq\operatorname{vol}_1(I)\), define its mass-\(b\) metronoid by
\[
\mathsf M_b(I)
:=
\left\{
\frac1b\int_I t f(t)\,dt:
f:I\to[0,1]\text{ is measurable and }
\int_I f(t)\,dt=b
\right\}.
\]
Given a target-mass state, a compatible continuous selector is any family
\[
q_{\mathbf z}\in\mathsf M_{b_{\mathbf z}}((C_j)_{\mathbf z})
\qquad(b_{\mathbf z}>0).
\]
For such a family put
\[
x(q):=\frac1{M_j}\sum_{b_{\mathbf z}>0}b_{\mathbf z}q_{\mathbf z},
\qquad
y(q):=(\mathbf m,x(q)).
\]
For measurable \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_j}\times\mathbb R\), let
\[
\mu_j(A):=
\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{n_j}}
\operatorname{vol}_1\{t:(\mathbf z,t)\in A\},
\]
and for \(y\in S_j\) define
\[
h_{S_j}(y):=
\inf\{\mu_j(S_j\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_j+1}
\text{ is a closed halfspace and }y\in H\}.
\]

Then the following assertions hold with all the indicated quantifiers.

Assertion 1. For every \(j\geq2\), target-mass states exist and \(V_j\) attains a global minimum over all of them.

Assertion 2. For every \(j\geq2\) and every globally minimizing target-mass state
\((\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\),
\[
\mathbf m=\mathbf0,
\qquad
b_{\mathbf0}=a_{\mathbf0}=T_j+1,
\qquad
V_j(\mathbf m,\mathbf b)>0.
\]

Assertion 3. For every \(j\geq2\), every globally minimizing target-mass state
\((\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\), and every compatible continuous selector
\((q_{\mathbf z})_{b_{\mathbf z}>0}\), one has \(y(q)\in S_j\) and
\[
h_{S_j}(y(q))\leq T_j.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sup_{\substack{
(\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\text{ globally minimizes }V_j\\
q_{\mathbf z}\in\mathsf M_{b_{\mathbf z}}((C_j)_{\mathbf z})
\text{ for every }b_{\mathbf z}>0}}
\frac{h_{S_j}(y(q))}{M_j}
\leq
\frac{T_j}{M_j}
=
\frac{48^j}{81^j+3^j}
\leq
\left(\frac{16}{27}\right)^j
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Equivalently, for every \(\eta>0\) there is \(j\geq2\) such that every global minimizer has positive variance and every compatible selector for every such minimizer satisfies
\[
h_{S_j}(y(q))<\eta M_j.
\]

## Proof

Fix \(j\geq2\), and abbreviate
\(n=n_j\), \(T=T_j\), \(C=C_j\), \(M=M_j\), and \(W=W_j\).

First compute all fibers exactly. The projection of \(C\) onto its first \(n\) coordinates is \(P_j\). Let \(\mathbf y\in P_j\) and put
\(r=\lVert\mathbf y\rVert_\infty\). Every point of \(C\) can be written as
\[
(\mathbf y,t)
=
\lambda(\mathbf p,s)+(1-\lambda)(\mathbf0,T)
\]
with \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), \(\mathbf p\in P_j\), and
\(-1\leq s\leq0\). Hence \(r\leq\lambda\) and
\[
t=\lambda s+(1-\lambda)T
\leq(1-\lambda)T
\leq(1-r)T.
\]
Also \(t\geq-\lambda+(1-\lambda)T\geq-1\). The lower endpoint \((\mathbf y,-1)\) belongs to the slab. If \(r=0\), the proposed upper endpoint is the apex. If \(r>0\), then
\[
(\mathbf y,T(1-r))
=
r(\mathbf y/r,0)+(1-r)(\mathbf0,T),
\]
so the proposed upper endpoint also belongs to \(C\). A fiber of a convex set is convex. Therefore
\[
C_{\mathbf y}=[-1,T(1-\lVert\mathbf y\rVert_\infty)]
\qquad(\mathbf y\in P_j).
\tag{1}
\]

It follows that the lattice support is
\[
D:=\{\mathbf z:a_{\mathbf z}>0\}=\{-1,0,1\}^n,
\]
and
\[
C_{\mathbf0}=[-1,T],
\qquad
a_{\mathbf0}=T+1,
\tag{2}
\]
whereas
\[
C_{\mathbf z}=[-1,0],
\qquad
a_{\mathbf z}=1
\quad(\mathbf z\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus
\[
W=3^n+T=81^j+3^j,
\qquad
M=\frac{81^j+3^j}{16^j}.
\tag{4}
\]
The slab has nonempty interior, so \(C\) is full-dimensional. Compactness and convexity follow directly from its definition.

The numerical inequalities needed below are
\[
\frac MT
=
\left(\frac{27}{16}\right)^j+\left(\frac1{16}\right)^j
>2,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
\frac M{T^2}
=
\left(\frac9{16}\right)^j+\left(\frac1{48}\right)^j
\leq
\left(\frac9{16}\right)^2+\left(\frac1{48}\right)^2
=\frac{730}{2304}<\frac13.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\[
R:=M-(T+1)>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Moreover, \(M<W\), so
\[
R<3^n-1.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
b_{\mathbf0}:=T+1,
\qquad
b_{\mathbf z}:=\frac{R}{3^n-1}
\quad(\mathbf z\in D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}),
\]
and set all other \(b_{\mathbf z}\) equal to zero. Equations (7) and (8) give the capacity bounds. The set
\(D\setminus\{\mathbf0\}\) is centrally symmetric, so its vector sum is zero. Hence this allocation, together with
\(\mathbf m=\mathbf0\), is a target-mass state.

These base states are exactly the fiber-mass data relevant to dominated selected submeasures. Indeed, for every feasible \(\mathbf b\), the density \(b_{\mathbf z}/a_{\mathbf z}\) on each positive-volume fiber realizes mass \(b_{\mathbf z}\), remains between zero and one, and has the prescribed integral base barycenter. Conversely, every dominated selected submeasure of mass \(M\) whose normalized base barycenter is integral gives fiber masses satisfying the displayed base-state constraints, and its base variance is \(V_j(\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\).

For any target-mass state, \(\mathbf m\) is an integral point in the convex hull of \(D\). Thus
\[
\mathbf m\in[-1,1]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n=D.
\tag{9}
\]
There are only finitely many possible \(\mathbf m\). For each fixed
\(\mathbf m\), the feasible allocations form a closed subset of the compact box
\(\prod_{\mathbf z\in D}[0,a_{\mathbf z}]\), and \(V_j(\mathbf m,\cdot)\) is continuous there. It therefore attains a minimum on each nonempty fixed-\(\mathbf m\) feasible set. Taking the least of these finitely many attained values gives a global minimizer. This proves assertion 1.

We next prove that no global minimizer can have a nonzero integral barycenter. Let \((\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\) be any target-mass state with
\(\mathbf m\neq\mathbf0\). If \(m_i=1\), then
\[
0=M(1-m_i)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}b_{\mathbf z}(1-z_i).
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, so \(b_{\mathbf z}>0\) implies
\(z_i=1\). Similarly, if \(m_i=-1\), then
\(b_{\mathbf z}>0\) implies \(z_i=-1\). Consequently,
\[
b_{\mathbf z}>0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf z-\mathbf m\in D.
\tag{10}
\]
Also every \(\mathbf z\) in the selected support is nonzero, because some coordinate of \(\mathbf m\) equals \(1\) or \(-1\), and that coordinate of \(\mathbf z\) must equal the same value.

Translate the selected allocation by \(-\mathbf m\): for
\(\mathbf y\in D\), let
\[
\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}
:=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf z\in D\\
\mathbf z-\mathbf m=\mathbf y}}b_{\mathbf z}.
\]
The translation is injective, every positively loaded source is noncentral and has capacity one, and every noncentral target has capacity one. The central target has capacity \(T+1\). Therefore
\[
0\leq\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}\leq a_{\mathbf y}
\quad(\mathbf y\in D),
\qquad
\widetilde b_{\mathbf0}\leq1<T+1.
\tag{11}
\]
The mass and first moment are
\[
\sum_{\mathbf y}\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}=M,
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf y}\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}\mathbf y
=
\sum_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}(\mathbf z-\mathbf m)
=\mathbf0,
\tag{12}
\]
and its unnormalized quadratic cost equals that of the original state:
\[
\sum_{\mathbf y}\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}\lVert\mathbf y\rVert_2^2
=
\sum_{\mathbf z}b_{\mathbf z}
\lVert\mathbf z-\mathbf m\rVert_2^2.
\tag{13}
\]

Put \(A=M-\widetilde b_{\mathbf0}>0\). Choose
\[
0<s<\min\{A,T+1-\widetilde b_{\mathbf0}\},
\]
and define
\[
b'_{\mathbf0}:=\widetilde b_{\mathbf0}+s,
\qquad
b'_{\mathbf y}:=\left(1-\frac{s}{A}\right)
\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}
\quad(\mathbf y\neq\mathbf0).
\tag{14}
\]
This preserves total mass and zero first moment and respects every capacity. Since \(A>0\), some noncentral mass is present, so the cost in (13) is positive. The new unnormalized cost is
\[
\sum_{\mathbf y}b'_{\mathbf y}\lVert\mathbf y\rVert_2^2
=
\left(1-\frac{s}{A}\right)
\sum_{\mathbf y}\widetilde b_{\mathbf y}\lVert\mathbf y\rVert_2^2,
\]
which is strictly smaller. Thus the original state cannot be globally minimizing. Every global minimizer has
\[
\mathbf m=\mathbf0.
\tag{15}
\]

Let now \((\mathbf0,\mathbf b)\) be a target-mass state with
\(b_{\mathbf0}<T+1\). Its noncentral mass \(A=M-b_{\mathbf0}\) is positive, and its noncentral first moment is zero. Applying exactly the modification (14), now directly to \(\mathbf b\), preserves feasibility and strictly decreases the quadratic cost. Hence every global minimizer satisfies
\[
b_{\mathbf0}=T+1.
\tag{16}
\]
Its noncentral mass is then \(R>0\). Every noncentral lattice point has positive squared norm, so
\[
M V_j(\mathbf0,\mathbf b)
=
\sum_{\mathbf z\neq\mathbf0}b_{\mathbf z}
\lVert\mathbf z\rVert_2^2
>0.
\tag{17}
\]
This proves assertion 2 for every global minimizer.

Fix an arbitrary global minimizer. For every \(0<b\leq\operatorname{vol}_1(I)\), the constant density \(f=b/\operatorname{vol}_1(I)\) shows that \(\mathsf M_b(I)\) is nonempty. Thus compatible selector families exist. Fix an arbitrary one. A normalized average of points of an interval lies in that interval, so
\(q_{\mathbf z}\in C_{\mathbf z}\) whenever \(b_{\mathbf z}>0\). Convexity of \(C\), the mass constraint, and the first-moment constraint give
\[
y(q)
=
\sum_{b_{\mathbf z}>0}
\frac{b_{\mathbf z}}M(\mathbf z,q_{\mathbf z})
\in C.
\]
Its base coordinate is the integral vector \(\mathbf0\), so \(y(q)\in S_j\).
Moreover, the witnessing density in the definition of each \(q_{\mathbf z}\), combined independently across the finitely many selected fibers, produces a dominated submeasure of total mass \(M\) whose normalized barycenter is exactly \(y(q)\).

At the central fiber, (16) selects the full interval mass. If
\(f:[-1,T]\to[0,1]\) has integral \(T+1\), then the nonnegative function
\(1-f\) has integral zero, hence \(f=1\) almost everywhere. Thus the central metronoid is the singleton containing the interval centroid:
\[
q_{\mathbf0}
=
\frac1{T+1}\int_{-1}^{T}t\,dt
=
\frac{T-1}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]
Every noncentral selected fiber is \([-1,0]\), so
\[
-1\leq q_{\mathbf z}\leq0
\qquad(\mathbf z\neq\mathbf0,\ b_{\mathbf z}>0).
\tag{19}
\]
Since the noncentral selected mass is \(R\), equations (18) and (19) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
Mx(q)
&=(T+1)\frac{T-1}{2}
+\sum_{\mathbf z\neq\mathbf0}b_{\mathbf z}q_{\mathbf z}\\
&\geq\frac{T^2-1}{2}-R
>\frac{T^2-1}{2}-\frac{T^2}{3}
=\frac{T^2-3}{6}>0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
where \(R<M<T^2/3\) follows from (6). Hence \(x(q)>0\) for every compatible selector of every global minimizer.

Consider the closed horizontal halfspace
\[
H_q:=\{(\mathbf z,t)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:t\geq x(q)\}.
\]
It contains \(y(q)\) on its boundary. By (3) and \(x(q)>0\), no noncentral lattice fiber contributes to \(S_j\cap H_q\). The central fiber contributes length
\(T-x(q)\), which is nonnegative because \(y(q)\in C_{\mathbf0}\), and at most \(T\). Therefore
\[
h_{S_j}(y(q))
\leq\mu_j(S_j\cap H_q)
=T-x(q)
\leq T.
\tag{21}
\]
This bound used no property of the minimizer or selector beyond the universally quantified properties already proved, so it holds for all of them.

Finally, (4) gives
\[
\frac{T_j}{M_j}
=
\frac{3^j16^j}{81^j+3^j}
=
\frac{48^j}{81^j+3^j}
\leq
\left(\frac{16}{27}\right)^j
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Combining this with (17) and (21) proves assertion 3 and the stated
\(\forall\eta>0\,\exists j\,\forall(\mathbf m,\mathbf b)\,\forall q\)
no-go conclusion. The construction controls only points generated by globally minimum-variance target-mass states. It makes no assertion about the supremum of \(h_{S_j}(y)\) over all \(y\in S_j\), so it does not disprove the weak Oertel conjecture.
