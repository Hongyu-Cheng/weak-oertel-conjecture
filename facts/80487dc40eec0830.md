## Statement

There are actual compatible three-fiber families in continuous dimension two whose independently normalized weak probability laws cannot be represented by equal-volume full-dimensional convex lifts compatible under the endpoint midpoint, in any common lift dimension with a fixed linear projection to the limit line. Their relative-mass laws obtained by one common normalization do admit compatible two-dimensional ball lifts.

## Proof

For $\varepsilon>0$ place the rectangles $[0,1]\times[-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2]$ at $-1,1$ and $[0,2]\times[-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2]$ at $0$, and take their placed convex hull. The endpoints are exposed in the base. At zero the endpoint weights in any convex combination are equal, so the hull trace stays in the middle rectangle, which already contains the endpoint midpoint. There are exactly these three integer fibers with areas $\varepsilon,2\varepsilon,\varepsilon$.

The separate uniform probabilities tend weakly to the densities $\mathbf1_{[0,1]},\frac12\mathbf1_{[0,2]},\mathbf1_{[0,1]}$ on the horizontal line. Their unit-volume centered ball lifts in dimension two have heights $1,1/2,1$ respectively, and the midpoint of the two outer lifts is not in the middle lift. More generally, if equal positive volumes of full-dimensional convex bodies $A_-,A_0,A_+$ and $(A_-+A_+)/2\subseteq A_0$ are given, Brunn–Minkowski forces equality of volumes and makes the endpoint bodies translates by the equality case. The midpoint body has the same volume as $A_0$ and equals it: strict containment of one full-dimensional compact convex body in another gives positive volume difference by taking a small interior region toward an excluded point. The three bodies therefore have projections of the same length under any common linear map to the line. This contradicts the required lengths $1,2,1$.

With common normalizer $4\varepsilon$, the limiting densities are instead $\frac14\mathbf1_{[0,1]},\frac14\mathbf1_{[0,2]},\frac14\mathbf1_{[0,1]}$. Their centered rectangle lifts of height $1/4$ have areas $1/4,1/2,1/4$ and satisfy the midpoint inclusion exactly.
