## Statement

Let \(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\[
\Delta_d:=\left\{y\in\mathbb R^d:y_i\ge 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^d y_i\le 1\right\},
\]
and let
\[
\lambda_0(y):=1-\sum_{i=1}^d y_i,\qquad
\lambda_i(y):=y_i\quad(1\le i\le d)
\]
be its barycentric coordinates. For \(x\in\Delta_d\), define
\[
\delta_{\Delta_d}(x):=
\inf\left\{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in H\right\}.
\]
For every nonempty proper subset \(I\subsetneq\{0,\ldots,d\}\), set
\[
k:=|I|,\qquad
L_I(y):=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_i(y),\qquad
s_I(x):=L_I(x).
\]
Thus \(1\le k\le d\) and \(0\le s_I(x)\le 1\). Define, for \(0\le s\le 1\),
\[
F_{d,k}(s):=
\frac{d!}{(k-1)!(d-k)!}
\int_0^s t^{k-1}(1-t)^{d-k}\,dt.
\]
Then
\[
F_{d,k}(s)
=\sum_{j=k}^{d}\binom dj s^j(1-s)^{d-j}.
\]
The two sets
\[
H_I^-(x):=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:L_I(y)\le s_I(x)\},
\qquad
H_I^+(x):=\{y\in\mathbb R^d:L_I(y)\ge s_I(x)\}
\]
are proper closed halfspaces whose common boundary contains \(x\), and their exact relative cap volumes are
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap H_I^-(x))}
{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)}
=F_{d,k}(s_I(x)),
\]
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap H_I^+(x))}
{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)}
=1-F_{d,k}(s_I(x)).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\delta_{\Delta_d}(x)}{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)}
\le
\min_{\varnothing\ne I\subsetneq\{0,\ldots,d\}}
\min\left\{
F_{d,|I|}(s_I(x)),
1-F_{d,|I|}(s_I(x))
\right\}.
\]

More generally, let \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a full-dimensional simplex, let \(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_d\) be its barycentric coordinates, and define \(\delta_T\) by replacing \(\Delta_d\) with \(T\) above. For \(x\in T\) and every nonempty proper \(I\subsetneq\{0,\ldots,d\}\), put
\[
s_I^T(x):=\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i(x).
\]
Then the proper closed halfspaces
\[
\left\{y:\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i(y)\le s_I^T(x)\right\},
\qquad
\left\{y:\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i(y)\ge s_I^T(x)\right\}
\]
have relative \(T\)-cap volumes \(F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x))\) and \(1-F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x))\), respectively, and
\[
\frac{\delta_T(x)}{\operatorname{vol}_d(T)}
\le
\min_{\varnothing\ne I\subsetneq\{0,\ldots,d\}}
\min\left\{
F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x)),
1-F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x))
\right\}.
\]

## Proof

For \(m\ge 0\) and \(r\ge 0\), let
\[
V_m(r):=
\operatorname{vol}_m\left\{w\in\mathbb R^m:
w_i\ge 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^m w_i\le r\right\},
\]
with the zero-dimensional convention \(V_0(r)=1\). Induction and Fubini's theorem give, for \(m\ge 1\),
\[
V_m(r)
=\int_0^r V_{m-1}(r-z)\,dz
=\int_0^r\frac{(r-z)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\,dz
=\frac{r^m}{m!}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)=V_d(1)=\frac1{d!}.
\]

Fix \(I\) and write \(k=|I|\). A permutation of the \(d+1\) vertices of \(\Delta_d\) extends uniquely to an invertible affine map from \(\Delta_d\) onto itself. Such a map preserves \(d\)-dimensional volume because its constant volume-scaling factor is the ratio of the volume of its image \(\Delta_d\) to the volume of its domain \(\Delta_d\), hence equals \(1\). It also permutes the barycentric coordinates. Since a vertex permutation can send \(I\) to \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\), it is enough to compute the cap volume for
\[
L(y)=y_1+\cdots+y_k.
\]
This reduction applies without change when \(0\in I\); the vertex carrying \(\lambda_0\) is simply one of the permuted vertices.

Write \(y=(u,v)\), where \(u=(y_1,\ldots,y_k)\) and \(v=(y_{k+1},\ldots,y_d)\). For fixed \(u_i\ge 0\) with \(t:=\sum_{i=1}^k u_i\le 1\), the admissible \(v\)'s form a \((d-k)\)-dimensional simplex of volume
\[
V_{d-k}(1-t)=\frac{(1-t)^{d-k}}{(d-k)!}.
\]
This includes \(k=d\), for which \(v\) is absent and the displayed factor is \(V_0(1-t)=1\). Therefore, for \(0\le s\le 1\),
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in\Delta_d:L(y)\le s\}
=
\int_{\substack{u_i\ge 0\\ \sum_i u_i\le s}}
\frac{(1-\sum_i u_i)^{d-k}}{(d-k)!}\,du.
\]
Apart from the null set \(t=0\), use the coordinates
\[
u_i=tq_i\quad(1\le i<k),\qquad
u_k=t\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}q_i\right),
\]
where \(0<t\le s\) and \(q\in\Delta_{k-1}\). The absolute value of the \(k\)-dimensional Jacobian of \((t,q)\mapsto u\) is \(t^{k-1}\). For \(k=1\), there are no \(q\)-coordinates and the same assertion reads \(du=dt\). Since
\(\operatorname{vol}_{k-1}(\Delta_{k-1})=1/(k-1)!\), the preceding integral is
\[
\frac1{(k-1)!(d-k)!}
\int_0^s t^{k-1}(1-t)^{d-k}\,dt.
\]
Dividing by \(\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)=1/d!\) proves
\[
\frac{\operatorname{vol}_d\{y\in\Delta_d:L(y)\le s\}}
{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)}
=
\frac{d!}{(k-1)!(d-k)!}
\int_0^s t^{k-1}(1-t)^{d-k}\,dt
=F_{d,k}(s).
\]
The vertex-permutation reduction proves this formula for every \(I\), including every subset containing the barycentric coordinate \(\lambda_0\).

To prove the polynomial identity, set
\[
B_{d,k}(s):=\sum_{j=k}^{d}\binom dj s^j(1-s)^{d-j}.
\]
Using
\[
j\binom dj=d\binom{d-1}{j-1},
\qquad
(d-j)\binom dj=d\binom{d-1}{j},
\]
and differentiating on \(0<s<1\), the two resulting sums telescope:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{d,k}'(s)
&=
d\sum_{j=k}^{d}\binom{d-1}{j-1}
s^{j-1}(1-s)^{d-j}\\
&\quad
-d\sum_{j=k}^{d-1}\binom{d-1}{j}
s^j(1-s)^{d-j-1}\\
&=
d\binom{d-1}{k-1}s^{k-1}(1-s)^{d-k}\\
&=
\frac{d!}{(k-1)!(d-k)!}
s^{k-1}(1-s)^{d-k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also \(B_{d,k}(0)=0=F_{d,k}(0)\). Hence the fundamental theorem of calculus and continuity at the endpoints give
\[
B_{d,k}(s)=F_{d,k}(s)\qquad(0\le s\le 1).
\]

For every nonempty proper \(I\), the affine function \(L_I\) equals \(1\) at each simplex vertex indexed by \(I\) and equals \(0\) at each vertex indexed by \(I^c\). Both kinds of vertices exist, so \(L_I\) is nonconstant. It follows that \(H_I^-(x)\) and \(H_I^+(x)\) are proper closed halfspaces in the ambient space \(\mathbb R^d\). Their common boundary is the hyperplane \(L_I=s_I(x)\), which contains \(x\) and has \(d\)-dimensional volume zero. Thus the closed boundary contributes no volume, and
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap H_I^+(x))
=
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)
-
\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d\cap H_I^-(x)).
\]
Together with the first cap calculation, this proves that the two exact relative volumes are \(F_{d,k}(s_I(x))\) and \(1-F_{d,k}(s_I(x))\).

The endpoint cases do not change properness. If \(s_I(x)=0\), then
\[
\Delta_d\cap H_I^-(x)
=\operatorname{conv}\{v_j:j\in I^c\},
\qquad
\Delta_d\cap H_I^+(x)=\Delta_d,
\]
where \(v_0=0\) and \(v_i=e_i\). The first set is a face of dimension \(d-k<d\), so its \(d\)-volume is zero. Hence the relative volumes are \(F_{d,k}(0)=0\) and \(1\). If \(s_I(x)=1\), then
\[
\Delta_d\cap H_I^-(x)=\Delta_d,
\qquad
\Delta_d\cap H_I^+(x)
=\operatorname{conv}\{v_i:i\in I\}.
\]
The second set is a face of dimension \(k-1<d\), so the relative volumes are \(F_{d,k}(1)=1\) and \(0\). In both cases, the two ambient halfspaces remain proper and closed and contain \(x\) on their common boundary.

Complementary subsets give the same two cuts in reverse order. Indeed,
\[
L_{I^c}=1-L_I,\qquad
s_{I^c}(x)=1-s_I(x),
\]
so
\[
H_{I^c}^-(x)=H_I^+(x),\qquad
H_{I^c}^+(x)=H_I^-(x).
\]
Since \(|I^c|=d+1-k\), the exact cap formulas also give the consistent beta-symmetry identity
\[
F_{d,d+1-k}(1-s)=1-F_{d,k}(s)
\qquad(0\le s\le 1).
\]

When \(d=1\), necessarily \(k=1\), and
\[
F_{1,1}(s)=\int_0^s1\,dt=s.
\]
Writing \(\Delta_1=[0,1]\) and \(x=r\), the subset \(I=\{1\}\) gives the halfspaces
\[
H_{\{1\}}^-(r)=(-\infty,r],\qquad
H_{\{1\}}^+(r)=[r,\infty),
\]
whose intersections with \([0,1]\) have lengths \(r\) and \(1-r\). The subset \(I=\{0\}\), for which \(\lambda_0(y)=1-y\), swaps these two halfspaces and these two lengths. This remains exact at \(r=0\) and \(r=1\), with one cap of length zero and the other of length one.

For every \(I\), both \(H_I^-(x)\) and \(H_I^+(x)\) are admissible in the infimum defining \(\delta_{\Delta_d}(x)\). Therefore
\[
\frac{\delta_{\Delta_d}(x)}{\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d)}
\le
\min\left\{
F_{d,|I|}(s_I(x)),
1-F_{d,|I|}(s_I(x))
\right\}.
\]
Taking the minimum over all nonempty proper \(I\) proves the standard-simplex depth bound.

Finally, let \(T=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_d\}\) be full-dimensional and let
\[
A(z):=v_0+\sum_{i=1}^d z_i(v_i-v_0).
\]
Then \(A\) is an invertible affine map from \(\Delta_d\) onto \(T\), and its barycentric-coordinate correspondence is
\[
\mu_i(Az)=\lambda_i(z)\qquad(0\le i\le d).
\]
It maps each of the two standard-simplex subset halfspaces onto the corresponding subset halfspace for \(T\), preserving closedness, properness, and the boundary point. If \(M\) is the linear part of \(A\), all \(d\)-dimensional volumes are multiplied by \(|\det M|\). Hence the two relative cap volumes for \(T\) are unchanged and equal to \(F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x))\) and \(1-F_{d,|I|}(s_I^T(x))\).

Moreover, inverse image under \(A\) is a bijection between proper closed halfspaces containing \(x=Az\) and proper closed halfspaces containing \(z\). Therefore
\[
\delta_T(Az)=|\det M|\,\delta_{\Delta_d}(z),
\qquad
\operatorname{vol}_d(T)=|\det M|\,\operatorname{vol}_d(\Delta_d).
\]
The normalized depth is affine-invariant, and the asserted bound for \(T\) follows.
