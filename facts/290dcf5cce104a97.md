## Statement

For every integer \(k\geq 1\), define

\[
\Omega_k=\{0,1\}^k\cup\{0,-1\}^k,
\qquad
K_k=\operatorname{conv}(\Omega_k).
\]

Let \(b,k_1,\ldots,k_b\) be positive integers, put

\[
N=\sum_{r=1}^b k_r,
\qquad
Q=\prod_{r=1}^b K_{k_r},
\qquad
D=\prod_{r=1}^b\Omega_{k_r},
\]

and choose arbitrary vectors \(\boldsymbol\ell^{(r)}\in\mathbb R^{k_r}\). Set

\[
w_{\mathbf x}=\exp\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^b
\boldsymbol\ell^{(r)}\cdot\mathbf x^{(r)}\right),
\qquad
W=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}w_{\mathbf x},
\qquad
p_{\mathbf x}=\frac{w_{\mathbf x}}W.
\]

For \(\mathbf x\in D\), let

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf x)=
\inf_{\substack{H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ a closed halfspace}\\
\mathbf x\in H}}
\sum_{\mathbf y\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf y}.
\]

Then \(Q\) is a compact full-dimensional convex polytope and \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\). For all block dimensions and all tilts,

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq \frac1{16}\,4^{-N}.
\tag{1}
\]

If every \(k_r\geq8\), then

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq \frac14\,4^{-N}.
\tag{2}
\]

If every \(k_r\geq16\), then

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x\in D}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq 4^{-N}.
\tag{3}
\]

These statements include an arbitrary additive constant in the affine exponent, since it cancels from \(p\). They also include the convention \(e^{-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf x}\): the exact parameter map is \(\boldsymbol\ell=-\boldsymbol\beta\). More generally, on an integral translate \(\mathbf a+D\), the change of variables \(\mathbf x=\mathbf I-\mathbf a\) sends weights \(e^{-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf I}\) to the common factor \(e^{-\boldsymbol\beta\cdot\mathbf a}\) times \(e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\), with \(\boldsymbol\ell=-\boldsymbol\beta\). The common factor cancels after normalization, and \(H\mapsto H-\mathbf a\) preserves closed halfspaces and depth.

There is a sharper separation on the exact example in accepted fact `001a95dc6616c6b9`. For \(k\geq2\), that fact has \(N_k=k^2\), \(t=k^{-1/4}\), \(k\) blocks of \(\Omega_k\), the integral translate by \(\mathbf{1}_{N_k}\), and block exponent \(e^{-\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf I^{(r)}}\), where \(\boldsymbol\theta=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\). Under the preceding map, \(\mathbf x=\mathbf I-\mathbf{1}_{N_k}\) and \(\boldsymbol\ell^{(r)}=-\boldsymbol\theta\); moreover \(\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\mathbf{1}_k=0\), so even the common translation factor equals one. For its normalized law,

\[
0\leq 2^{N_k}\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)
\leq
3e^{5/2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi(3+\cosh 2)}8}\,k^{-1/4}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{4}
\]

where \(4\) is the accepted first-moment conclusion of `001a95dc6616c6b9`, while the same law satisfies the independently proved bound

\[
4^{N_k}\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq
2^{k^2-k-6}\exp\!\left(-\frac54\sqrt{k}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5}
\]

Finally, the preceding positive theorem has a precise reflection-only limitation. For a positive probability law \(p\) on a finite \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and \(\mathbf j\in E\), extend every probability law \(\nu\) on \(E\) by zero outside \(E\), and define

\[
\mathcal R_p(\mathbf j)=
\sup_{\substack{\nu\text{ a probability law on }E\\
\nu(2\mathbf j-\mathbf x)=\nu(\mathbf x)\ \forall\mathbf x}}
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x\in E}\frac{\nu(\mathbf x)^2}{p_{\mathbf x}}\right)^{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]

The log-affine specialization \(w_{(\mathbf z,h)}=2^{-h}\) in accepted fact `604889294a17d7df` gives a sequence of complete convex lattice supports \(E_k\subseteq\mathbb Z^{n_k}\). The exact notation map from that fact is \(E_k=\widehat A\), \(n_k=N\), \(h=t\) for its final height coordinate, and \(W_k=W=\sum_{\mathbf x\in E_k}w_{\mathbf x}\). With \(p=w/W_k\), define

\[
S_{\mathbf j}(w)=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^{n_k}\\
\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v\in E_k}}
\min\{w_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v},w_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}\},
\]

the sum being oriented and including \(\mathbf v=\mathbf{0}\), one has, with \(H_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k1/\ell\),

\[
\max_{\mathbf j}S_{\mathbf j}(w)=1,
\qquad
\frac{W_k}{2^{n_k}}\sim\frac{H_k}{8}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
4^{n_k}\sum_{\mathbf j\in E_k}p_{\mathbf j}
\mathcal R_p(\mathbf j)^2
\leq
\frac4{(W_k/2^{n_k})^2}
\sim\frac{256}{H_k^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus even the optimal centrally symmetric comparison of the form \(6\) cannot prove a uniform second-moment bound on arbitrary complete supports. Equation \(8\) is only a limitation of that lower-bound method; it is not an upper bound on actual halfspace depth.

## Proof

First we verify lattice completeness and fix the halfspace boundary convention. Since the two cubes in \(\Omega_k\) have convex hulls \([0,1]^k\) and \([-1,0]^k\), every \(\mathbf x\in K_k\) can be written

\[
\mathbf x=\lambda\mathbf u-(1-\lambda)\mathbf v,
\qquad
0\leq\lambda\leq1,
\qquad
\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in[0,1]^k.
\]

Thus \(-(1-\lambda)\leq x_i\leq\lambda\) for every \(i\). If \(0<\lambda<1\), an integral \(\mathbf x\) must be the origin. If \(\lambda=1\), every integral coordinate is zero or one; if \(\lambda=0\), every integral coordinate is zero or minus one. Hence \(K_k\cap\mathbb Z^k=\Omega_k\), and taking Cartesian products gives \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^N=D\). Each \(K_k\) is compact and contains \(\pm\mathbf e_i\), so \(Q\) is compact and full-dimensional.

Every proper closed halfspace can be written \(H=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf y\geq c\}\) with \(\mathbf a\neq\mathbf{0}\). If \(\mathbf j\in H\), then the closed halfspace

\[
H_{\mathbf j}=\{\mathbf y:\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf j)\geq0\}
\]

has boundary through \(\mathbf j\) and satisfies \(H_{\mathbf j}\subseteq H\). The whole-space case is immediate. We may therefore prove every depth lower bound for halfspaces whose boundary contains the target. Closedness ensures that the target and every zero-valued boundary point are retained.

We next prove \(1\). If \(p\) is positive on a finite set and \(\nu\) is a probability law on that set which is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf j\), put

\[
R_p(\nu)=
\left(\sum_{\mathbf x}\frac{\nu(\mathbf x)^2}{p_{\mathbf x}}\right)^{-1}.
\]

Every boundary-through-\(\mathbf j\) closed halfspace \(H\) has \(\nu(H)\geq1/2\), because reflection about \(\mathbf j\) exchanges its two open sides and leaves its boundary fixed. Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
\frac12\leq\nu(H)
\leq
\left(p(H)\sum_{\mathbf x}\frac{\nu(\mathbf x)^2}{p_{\mathbf x}}\right)^{1/2},
\]

so

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf j)\geq\frac14R_p(\nu).
\tag{9}
\]

For a product \(p=\bigotimes_{r=1}^b p_r\), choose at each block center \(\mathbf j_r\) a centrally symmetric \(\nu_{r,\mathbf j_r}\). Their product is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf j=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_b)\), and direct factorization gives

\[
R_p\!\left(\bigotimes_r\nu_{r,\mathbf j_r}\right)
=\prod_rR_{p_r}(\nu_{r,\mathbf j_r}).
\]

Consequently, \(9\), squaring, and independence yield

\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}\tau_p(\mathbf j)^2
\geq\frac1{16}
\prod_{r=1}^b
\sum_{\mathbf j_r}p_r(\mathbf j_r)
R_{p_r}(\nu_{r,\mathbf j_r})^2.
\tag{10}
\]

The factor \(1/16\) occurs once in \(10\), independently of the number of blocks.

Fix one block \(\Omega_k\), write \(Z(\boldsymbol\ell)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\), \(M=2^k\), and \(L=2M-1=|\Omega_k|\). At a nonzero center choose \(\nu_{\mathbf j}=\delta_{\mathbf j}\); at the origin choose the uniform law on \(\Omega_k\). Both choices are centrally symmetric about their centers. For \(\mathbf j\neq\mathbf{0}\), \(R_p(\nu_{\mathbf j})=p_{\mathbf j}\). Since \(\Omega_k=-\Omega_k\), \(Z(-\boldsymbol\ell)=Z(\boldsymbol\ell)\), and at the origin

\[
R_p(\nu_{\mathbf{0}})
=\left(\frac{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)}{L^2}
\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}e^{-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\right)^{-1}
=\frac{L^2}{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^2}.
\]

It follows that

\[
\sum_{\mathbf j}p_{\mathbf j}R_p(\nu_{\mathbf j})^2
=
\frac{Z(3\boldsymbol\ell)-1}{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^3}
+\frac{L^4}{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^5}.
\tag{11}
\]

We claim that for every \(k\geq1\) and every \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^k\),

\[
\frac{Z(3\boldsymbol\ell)-1}{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^3}
+\frac{(2^{k+1}-1)^4}{Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^5}
\geq4^{-k}.
\tag{12}
\]

For \(k\geq4\), put \(t_i=\ell_i/2\) for \(1\leq i\leq k\), \(t_0=\sum_{i=1}^kt_i\), and

\[
C=\prod_{i=0}^k\cosh t_i,
\qquad
F=\prod_{i=0}^k(1+3\tanh^2t_i).
\]

Adding the positive and negative Boolean-cube partition functions, whose common origin was counted twice, gives

\[
Z(\boldsymbol\ell)=2MC-1,
\qquad
Z(3\boldsymbol\ell)-1=2MC^3F-2.
\tag{13}
\]

The second identity uses \(\cosh(3t)=\cosh^3t(1+3\tanh^2t)\). Let \(\mathsf A\) and \(\mathsf B\) be the two terms on the left of \(12\), respectively, multiplied by \(M^2\). Then

\[
\mathsf A\geq\frac F4,
\qquad
\mathsf B\geq
\frac{M}{2C^5}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)^4.
\tag{14}
\]

For the first inequality, clearing the positive denominator leaves

\[
F(12M^2C^2-6MC+1)-8M^2
\geq4M^2-6M+1>0.
\]

The second follows from \(Z(\boldsymbol\ell)\leq2MC\).

We also have

\[
F\geq4-\frac3{C^2}.
\tag{15}
\]

Indeed, with \(a_i=\operatorname{sech}^2t_i\in[0,1]\), the identity

\[
(4-3a)(4-3b)-(4-3ab)=12(1-a)(1-b)\geq0
\]

iterates to \(\prod_i(4-3a_i)\geq4-3\prod_i a_i\), which is \(15\).

If \(F\geq4\), \(14\) gives \(\mathsf A\geq1\). Suppose \(F<4\), put \(q_i=\tanh^2t_i\), and let \(q_*=\max_iq_i\). Expansion of \(F\) gives \(\sum_iq_i<1\). Moreover,

\[
q_*\leq\frac{k}{k+1}.
\tag{16}
\]

To prove \(16\), choose \(j\) with \(T=|t_j|=\max_i|t_i|\). The signed relation \(t_0-\sum_{i=1}^kt_i=0\) implies \(T\leq\sum_{i\neq j}|t_i|\), whichever index \(j\) is maximal. If \(T>0\), monotonicity of \(s/\tanh s\) on \(s>0\), followed by Cauchy-Schwarz, gives

\[
T\leq\frac{T}{\tanh T}\sum_{i\neq j}\sqrt{q_i}
\leq\frac{T}{\tanh T}\sqrt{k\sum_{i\neq j}q_i}.
\]

The monotonicity follows from \(\sinh s\cosh s\geq s\). Hence \(q_*\leq k\sum_{i\neq j}q_i\leq k(1-q_*)\), proving \(16\); the case \(T=0\) is immediate.

We now prove

\[
C^3\leq\frac{2M}{3}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)^4.
\tag{17}
\]

For \(4\leq k\leq6\), expansion of the factors other than a maximizing one and \(16\) give

\[
F\geq(1+3q_*)\left(1+\frac{3q_*}{k}\right).
\]

If \(q_*>2/3\), the right side is strictly greater than \(3(1+2/k)\geq4\), a contradiction. Thus every \(q_i\leq2/3\). Since \((1-q_i)(1+3q_i)\geq1\), we have \(C^2\leq F<4\) and \(C^3<8\). Bernoulli's inequality and \(M\geq16\) give

\[
\frac{2M}{3}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)^4
\geq\frac{2M-4}{3}\geq\frac{28}{3}>8,
\]

so \(17\) holds.

For \(k\geq7\), convexity of \(-\log(1-q)\) on \([0,k/(k+1)]\), together with \(16\), gives

\[
\log C^2
=\sum_i-\log(1-q_i)
\leq\frac{k+1}{k}\log(k+1)\sum_iq_i
\leq\frac{k+1}{k}\log(k+1).
\]

Therefore

\[
C^3\leq(k+1)^{3(k+1)/(2k)}
\leq(k+1)^2
\leq\frac{2^{k+1}-4}{3}.
\]

The last inequality holds at \(k=7\) and is preserved by induction. Bernoulli's inequality again gives

\[
\frac{2M}{3}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)^4
\geq\frac{2M-4}{3}=\frac{2^{k+1}-4}{3},
\]

proving \(17\). Combining \(14\), \(15\), and \(17\) yields

\[
\mathsf A+\mathsf B
\geq1-\frac3{4C^2}
+\frac{M}{2C^5}\left(1-\frac1{2M}\right)^4
\geq1.
\]

This proves \(12\) for \(k\geq4\).

For \(k=1,2,3\), put \(M=2^k\) and \(m=M-1\). The nonzero points of \(\Omega_k\) form \(m\) antipodal pairs indexed by nonempty \(E\subseteq[k]\). Let

\[
u_E=\cosh(\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf{1}_E),
\qquad
u=\frac1m\sum_{E\neq\varnothing}u_E\geq1.
\]

Then \(Z(\boldsymbol\ell)=1+2mu\), and convexity of \(4u^3-3u\) on \([1,\infty)\) gives

\[
Z(3\boldsymbol\ell)-1
=2\sum_{E\neq\varnothing}(4u_E^3-3u_E)
\geq2m(4u^3-3u).
\]

After multiplying \(12\) by \(M^2Z(\boldsymbol\ell)^5\), it is enough to prove \(F_M(u)\geq0\) for \(u\geq1\), where

\[
F_M(u)=M^2\left[2m(4u^3-3u)(1+2mu)^2+(2M-1)^4\right]-(1+2mu)^5.
\]

Writing \(v=u-1\geq0\), direct exact expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_2(1+v)
={}&153-66v+680v^2+1008v^3+528v^4+96v^5,\\
F_4(1+v)
={}&26313-21630v+9000v^2+40848v^3+28368v^4+6048v^5.
\end{aligned}
\]

The quadratics \(153-66v+680v^2\) and \(26313-21630v+9000v^2\) are positive on \(\mathbb R\), since their discriminants are \(-411804\) and \(-479411100\), respectively, and all omitted terms are nonnegative for \(v\geq0\). For \(M=8\),

\[
F_8(1+v)=2682225-1353030v-633304v^2
+728784v^3+731472v^4+164640v^5.
\]

If \(0\leq v\leq1\), this is at least \(2682225-1353030-633304=695891>0\). If \(v\geq1\), write \(v=1+s\), where \(s\geq0\); then

\[
F_8(2+s)=2320787+3315802s+7588280s^2
+5301072s^3+1554672s^4+164640s^5>0.
\]

Thus \(12\) holds for all positive \(k\). Substituting \(12\) into every factor of \(10\) proves \(1\), including all block sizes \(1,2,3\).

We now prove the improved bound \(2\). Work first with unnormalized one-block weights \(w(\mathbf x)=e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\) and define

\[
W=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}w(\mathbf x),
\qquad
V=\sum_{\substack{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k\\\mathbf x\neq\mathbf{0}}}w(\mathbf x)^3,
\qquad
T=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}\min\{w(\mathbf x),w(-\mathbf x)\}
=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}e^{-|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x|}.
\]

For the product, denote these quantities by \(W_r,V_r,T_r\), and let \(\tau_w=W\tau_p\) be unnormalized depth. Fix a product center \(\mathbf j\). Freeze each nonzero block of \(\mathbf j\), and let every zero block vary over its whole \(\Omega_{k_r}\). This subset is centrally symmetric about \(\mathbf j\). Pairing each point with its reflection, with the center included once and every boundary endpoint retained, gives

\[
\tau_w(\mathbf j)\geq\frac12
\prod_{\mathbf j^{(r)}\neq\mathbf{0}}w_r(\mathbf j^{(r)})
\prod_{\mathbf j^{(r)}=\mathbf{0}}T_r.
\tag{18}
\]

Indeed, if \(\Gamma_{\mathbf j}\) is this symmetric subset, then

\[
\sum_{\mathbf y\in\Gamma_{\mathbf j}}
\min\{w_{\mathbf y},w_{2\mathbf j-\mathbf y}\}
\geq
\prod_{\mathbf j^{(r)}\neq\mathbf{0}}w_r(\mathbf j^{(r)})
\prod_{\mathbf j^{(r)}=\mathbf{0}}T_r,
\]

because for the varying blocks one uses

\[
\min\!\left\{\prod_r a_r,\prod_r b_r\right\}
\geq\prod_r\min\{a_r,b_r\};
\]

the oriented reflected sum includes the center, and a closed halfspace contains at least one endpoint of every antipodal pair. If this oriented sum is \(S_{\Gamma}\), the halfspace mass on \(\Gamma_{\mathbf j}\) is at least \((S_{\Gamma}+w_{\mathbf j})/2\geq S_{\Gamma}/2\), which proves \(18\). If there are no zero blocks, \(18\) merely uses half of the target atom and remains valid. Squaring \(18\), multiplying by \(w_{\mathbf j}\), and summing independently over all block centers gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf j\in D}w_{\mathbf j}\tau_w(\mathbf j)^2
\geq\frac14\prod_{r=1}^b(V_r+T_r^2).
\tag{19}
\]

Again the factor \(1/4\) is paid only once.

We prove that for every \(k\geq8\),

\[
4^k(V+T^2)\geq W^3.
\tag{20}
\]

Put \(m=k+1\), \(B=2^m\), and define

\[
a_i=\ell_i\quad(1\leq i\leq k),
\qquad
a_m=-\sum_{i=1}^k\ell_i.
\]

Thus \(\sum_{i=1}^ma_i=0\). The exact cube-lift normalization map is

\[
\pi(\boldsymbol\varepsilon)
=(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_m,\ldots,
\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon_m),
\qquad
\boldsymbol\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^m.
\tag{21}
\]

It maps onto \(\Omega_k\), every nonzero image has one preimage, and the origin has the two preimages \(\mathbf{0}\) and \(\mathbf{1}\). Moreover \(e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\pi(\boldsymbol\varepsilon)}=e^{\sum_i a_i\varepsilon_i}\). Define

\[
c=\prod_{i=1}^m\cosh(a_i/2),
\qquad
G=\prod_{i=1}^m(1+3\tanh^2(a_i/2)).
\]

The cube map and the triple-angle identity give the exact formulas

\[
W=Bc-1,
\qquad
V=Bc^3G-2.
\tag{22}
\]

If independent uniform binary variables \(\varepsilon_i\) are used in \(21\) and \(X=\sum_i a_i\varepsilon_i\), then \(\mathbb E e^X=\mathbb E e^{-X}=c\). Cauchy-Schwarz and \(e^{|X|}\leq e^X+e^{-X}\) give

\[
\mathbb E e^{-|X|}\geq\frac1{\mathbb E e^{|X|}}\geq\frac1{2c}.
\]

The cube sum counts the origin twice, so

\[
T\geq\frac{B}{2c}-1.
\tag{23}
\]

We need two estimates when \(G<4\). First,

\[
4-G\leq\frac3{c^2}.
\tag{24}
\]

Indeed, if \(s_i=\sinh^2(a_i/2)\), then \(c^2=\prod_i(1+s_i)\) and \(c^2G=\prod_i(1+4s_i)\). Comparing the coefficients of every squarefree monomial gives

\[
\prod_i(1+4s_i)\geq4\prod_i(1+s_i)-3,
\]

which is \(24\).

Second, when \(G<4\),

\[
c^2\leq\frac54k.
\tag{25}
\]

Put \(u_i=\tanh(a_i/2)\). Concavity of \(q\mapsto\log(1+3q)\) on \([0,1]\) puts it above its endpoint chord, so \(\log(1+3q)\geq q\log4\). Hence \(G<4\) implies \(\sum_i u_i^2<1\). If every \(u_i^2\leq1/2\), then

\[
\log c^2=\sum_i-\log(1-u_i^2)
\leq2\log2\sum_i u_i^2<2\log2,
\]

so \(c^2<4\leq5k/4\).

Otherwise relabel so that \(v=u_1^2>1/2\), and put \(s=\sum_{i>1}u_i^2\). Expansion of \(G\) gives

\[
(1+3v)(1+3s)<4,
\qquad
s<\frac{1-v}{1+3v}.
\tag{26}
\]

The zero-sum relation is \(\sum_i\operatorname{arctanh}u_i=0\). The elementary inequalities

\[
z^2\leq(\operatorname{arctanh}z)^2
\leq\frac{z^2}{1-z^2}
\qquad(0\leq z<1)
\]

follow by differentiation. Cauchy-Schwarz, followed by \(1-u_i^2\geq1-s\), gives

\[
v
\leq k\sum_{i>1}(\operatorname{arctanh}u_i)^2
\leq k\frac{s}{1-s}
<\frac{k(1-v)}{4v}.
\tag{27}
\]

Also \(\prod_{i>1}(1-u_i^2)\geq1-s\). Combining \(26\) and \(27\),

\[
c^2
\leq\frac1{(1-v)(1-s)}
<\frac{k(1+3v)}{16v^3}
\leq\frac54k,
\]

because \((1+3v)/v^3\) decreases and has value \(20\) at \(v=1/2\). This proves \(25\).

For every \(k\geq8\),

\[
B=2^{k+1}\geq16\left(\frac54k\right)^{3/2}.
\tag{28}
\]

At \(k=8\), \(28\) is \(512\geq160\sqrt{10}\), whose square is the exact inequality \(262144\geq256000\). Thereafter the left side doubles, while the right side is multiplied by \(((k+1)/k)^{3/2}<2\).

If \(G<4\), \(25\) and \(28\) give \(B\geq16c^3\). Since \(c\geq1\), \(23\) yields

\[
T\geq\frac{7B}{16c}.
\]

Therefore, by \(24\),

\[
T^2\geq\frac{49B^2}{256c^2}
\geq\frac{49}{16}Bc
\geq3Bc
\geq(4-G)Bc^3.
\tag{29}
\]

Equations \(22\) and \(29\) imply \(V+T^2\geq4Bc^3-2\). The same conclusion is immediate from \(22\) when \(G\geq4\). Since \(4^k=B^2/4\) and \(y=Bc\geq B\geq2\),

\[
4^k(V+T^2)
\geq y^3-\frac{B^2}{2}
\geq(y-1)^3=W^3;
\]

the second inequality is equivalent to \(3y^2-3y+1\geq B^2/2\), and
\[
3y^2-3y+1-\frac{B^2}{2}
\geq\frac52B^2-3B+1>0
\]
for \(y\geq B\geq2\). This proves \(20\). Inserting \(20\) into \(19\), using \(W=\prod_rW_r\), and dividing by \(W^3\) proves \(2\).

We next prove \(3\). Fix one block with \(k\geq16\), put \(M=2^k\), and retain \(Z(\boldsymbol\ell)\), \(C\), and \(F\) from \(13\). Let \(Z_3=Z(3\boldsymbol\ell)\). The exact identities are

\[
W+1=2MC,
\qquad
Z_3+1=2MC^3F
=\frac{(W+1)^3}{4M^2}F.
\tag{30}
\]

If \(F\geq4\), every closed halfspace containing a support point contains that atom, so

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}^3
=\frac{Z_3}{W^3}.
\]

Equation \(30\) gives

\[
Z_3\geq\frac{(W+1)^3}{M^2}-1\geq\frac{W^3}{M^2}.
\]

For the last inequality, \(W=2MC-1\geq M\), so \(3W^2+3W+1\geq M^2\). Thus the desired one-block bound follows in this case.

Suppose \(F<4\). Apply \(25\) to the \(k+1\) zero-sum numbers

-2t_0,2t_1,\ldots,2t_k.

Their \(c\) and \(G\) in \(25\) are exactly the present \(C\) and \(F\), because only signs were changed. Hence

\[
C^2\leq\frac54k.
\tag{31}

At the origin define the oriented reflected mass, including zero once,

\[
S_0=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}
\min\{e^{\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x},e^{-\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x}\}
=\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}e^{-|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x|}.
\]

A boundary-through-origin closed halfspace contains the origin and at least one endpoint of each nonzero antipodal pair, including either endpoint when both lie on the boundary. Its unnormalized mass is therefore at least \((S_0+1)/2\geq S_0/2\). Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
S_0\sum_{\mathbf x\in\Omega_k}e^{|\boldsymbol\ell\cdot\mathbf x|}
\geq(2M-1)^2.
\]

The second factor is at most \(Z(\boldsymbol\ell)+Z(-\boldsymbol\ell)=2W\), and \(W\leq2MC\). Also \((2M-1)^2=4M(M-1)+1\). Therefore

\[
S_0\geq\frac{(2M-1)^2}{2W}\geq\frac{M-1}{C}.
\tag{32}
\]

The origin has probability \(1/W\), so its contribution alone, using \(31\) and \(32\), is

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq\frac{S_0^2}{4W^3}
\geq\frac{(M-1)^2}{32M^3C^5}.
\tag{33}
\]

For every \(k\geq16\),

\[
\frac{(2^k-1)^2}{2^k}
\geq\frac78\,2^k
\geq32\left(\frac{5k}{4}\right)^{5/2}.
\tag{34}
\]

The first inequality follows from \((1-1/M)^2\geq(15/16)^2>7/8\). At \(k=16\), the second is \(57344>12800\sqrt{20}\); squaring gives \(3288334336>3276800000\). It persists because \(2^k/k^{5/2}\) is increasing for integer \(k\geq16\): its successive ratio is \(2(k/(k+1))^{5/2}>2(16/17)^3>1\). Combining \(31\), \(33\), and \(34\) gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2\geq M^{-2}=4^{-k}.
\tag{35}
\]

It remains to tensorize \(35\) without losing a constant. For arbitrary finite block laws \(p_r\), a boundary-through-\(\mathbf x\) halfspace has the form

\[
\sum_r\mathbf a_r\cdot(\mathbf y^{(r)}-\mathbf x^{(r)})\geq0.
\]

For each \(r\), let \(E_r=\{\mathbf y^{(r)}:\mathbf a_r\cdot(\mathbf y^{(r)}-\mathbf x^{(r)})\geq0\}\); if \(\mathbf a_r=\mathbf{0}\), take the whole block space. Then \(\prod_rE_r\) lies in the original halfspace, and independence gives

\[
\tau_{\otimes_rp_r}(\mathbf x)
\geq\prod_r\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf x^{(r)}).
\tag{36}
\]

All sets in \(36\) are closed, and zero block normals cause no loss. Squaring, averaging, and applying \(35\) blockwise gives

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq\prod_r\sum_{\mathbf x^{(r)}}p_r(\mathbf x^{(r)})
\tau_{p_r}(\mathbf x^{(r)})^2
\geq\prod_r4^{-k_r}=4^{-N},

which proves \(3\).

We prove the separation \(5\). The first-moment identity in accepted fact `001a95dc6616c6b9` uses unnormalized weights and depths: if its notation is \(A=\sum w_{\mathbf I}\tau_w(\mathbf I)\), then under \(p=w/W\), \(A/W^2=\sum p_{\mathbf I}\tau_p(\mathbf I)\). For its second moment, work in centered coordinates. Put \(M=2^k\) and

\qquad
\tau_p(\mathbf{0})\geq\frac{S}{2W}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq\frac{L^4}{16W^5}.
\tag{37}

The elementary inequality \(\cosh u\leq e^{u^2/2}\) follows by integrating \(\tanh u\leq u\), and therefore

\[
Z=2M\cosh^2(t/2)-1\leq2M e^{t^2/4}.

Using \(t=k^{-1/4}\) in \(37\) yields

\[
4^{k^2}\sum_{\mathbf x}p_{\mathbf x}\tau_p(\mathbf x)^2
\geq
\frac1{16}2^{k^2-k}
\left(1-2^{-k-1}\right)^{4k}
e^{-5\sqrt{k}/4}.
\tag{38}
\]

For \(k\geq2\), \(2^k\geq2k\), and Bernoulli's inequality gives

\[
\left(1-2^{-k-1}\right)^{4k}
\geq\left(1-\frac{k}{2^{k+1}}\right)^4
\geq\left(\frac34\right)^4
\geq\frac14.

Substitution in \(38\) proves \(5\), whose right side diverges because its logarithm is \((k^2-k-6)\log2-(5/4)\sqrt{k}\).

It remains to prove the reflection limitation \(8\). For a fixed \(\mathbf j\), a centrally symmetric law \(\nu\) decomposes into its mass at \(\mathbf j\) and equal masses on every available unordered antipodal pair \(\{\mathbf j+\mathbf v,\mathbf j-\mathbf v\}\). If \(q\) is the total \(\nu\)-mass assigned to a noncentral pair whose \(p\)-masses are \(p_+,p_-\), that pair contributes

\[
\frac{q^2}{4}\left(\frac1{p_+}+\frac1{p_-}\right)
\]

to the quadratic denominator in \(6\). The central orbit with mass \(q_0\) contributes \(q_0^2/p_{\mathbf j}\). For positive numbers \(h_i\), Cauchy-Schwarz shows

\[
\min_{\substack{q_i\geq0\\\sum_iq_i=1}}
\sum_i\frac{q_i^2}{h_i}=\frac1{\sum_i h_i},
\]

with equality at \(q_i=h_i/\sum h_i\). Applying this to all antipodal orbits gives the exact formula

\[
\mathcal R_p(\mathbf j)=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf v\in\mathbb Z^n\\
\mathbf j\pm\mathbf v\in E}}
\frac{2p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}}
{p_{\mathbf j+\mathbf v}+p_{\mathbf j-\mathbf v}},
\tag{39}
\]

where the sum is oriented and includes \(\mathbf v=\mathbf{0}\). The central term is \(p_{\mathbf j}\), while the two orientations of each noncentral pair sum to \(4p_+p_-/(p_++p_-)\), exactly as in the orbit calculation.

Since \(2ab/(a+b)\leq2\min\{a,b\}\), \(39\) implies, for \(p=w/W\),

\[
\mathcal R_p(\mathbf j)\leq\frac{2S_{\mathbf j}(w)}W.
\tag{40}
\]

Accepted fact `604889294a17d7df` supplies the actual complete supports and the two exact asymptotics in \(7\). Thus \(40\) gives

\]

which is \(8\). By \(9\), \(\mathcal R_p(\mathbf j)/4\) is a lower bound for actual depth. Its failure at the normalized second-moment scale supplies no upper bound for actual depth, so the asserted limitation is exactly reflection-only.
