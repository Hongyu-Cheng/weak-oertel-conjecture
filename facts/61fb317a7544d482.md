## Statement

Let $L$ be a full-dimensional compact convex body in $\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, with centroid $g$, and let $B\subseteq L$ be compact and convex. For $0<\eta<1$, if $|L\setminus B|<\Gamma_d\eta^d|L|$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, then $(1-\eta)L+\eta g\subseteq B$. In particular in the actual inner-endpoint triangle theorem it suffices to require $|T\setminus B|<|T|/324$ or more generally $|T\setminus B|<(4/9)\eta_*^2|T|$, with its remaining hypotheses retained.

## Proof

Any $x=(1-\eta)p+\eta g$ with $p\in L$ is the centroid of $L_x=(1-\eta)p+\eta L$. Convexity places $L_x$ inside $L$, and it has volume $\eta^d|L|$. If $x$ is outside nonempty $B$, strict separation gives a closed halfspace through $x$ disjoint from $B$. If $B$ is empty the volume hypothesis already fails. In the nonempty case the full-mass case of 6769574fc1bd9f8d gives at least $\Gamma_d\eta^d|L|$ in that halfspace from $L_x$, hence from $L\setminus B$, a contradiction. The core conclusion follows. In dimension two the canonical triangle has centroid zero, and $(4/9)(1/12)^2=1/324$ with $1/12<\eta_*$. For either threshold the core condition of the actual inner-endpoint triangle theorem now holds at the corresponding parameter.
