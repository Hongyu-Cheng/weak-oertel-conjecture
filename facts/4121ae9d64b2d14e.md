## Statement

Let \(n,k\ge1\) be integers, let \(A=(a_{ri})\in\mathbb R_{>0}^{k\times n}\), let \(R\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^k\), and let \(\theta\in\mathbb R_{\le0}^n\). Write \(a_r\) for row \(r\), and define
\[
D=\{z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n:Az\le R\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{z\in D}e^{\theta\cdot z},\qquad
p_j=\frac{e^{\theta\cdot j}}{Z}\quad(j\in D).
\]
Use the oriented reflected count, including zero displacement, and the original-law reflected Hellinger mass
\[
k_j=\#\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:j+v,j-v\in D\}
=\#(D\cap(2j-D)),\qquad
\mathsf H_j=k_jp_j.
\]
The count has the meaning of the count in accepted facts `3aa03555ebd91f71` and `41d185e7405de421`; the uniform normalization there is replaced here by the displayed original log-affine normalization. Log-affinity gives \(\sqrt{p_{j+v}p_{j-v}}=p_j\) for every feasible reflected pair, so this is exactly its reflected Hellinger mass.

All logarithms are natural. The following constants are absolute:
The constant \(d_0\) is exactly the constant denoted \(d=\log(8/7)\) in accepted fact `41d185e7405de421`; it is distinct from the continuous dimension \(d\) in the locked problem.
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa&=4096,& d_0&=\log(8/7),& \delta&=\log(32/27),\\
C_*&=\frac{2}{1-\sqrt{7/8}},& \tau&=C_*(8+\log6),&
b&=6+\log(8\kappa^3),\\
M&=(\kappa+1)b+2\tau+d_0+6+3\sqrt{14d_0},&
\epsilon&=\min\{1,(\delta/(2M))^2\},&g&=e^{\delta/2}=\sqrt{32/27}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Whenever \(1\le k\le\epsilon n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{j\sim p}\mathsf H_j^2
=\frac{\sum_{j\in D}k_j^2e^{3\theta\cdot j}}{Z^3}
\ge g^n4^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the requested absolute prefactor can be \(c=1\). This includes zero capacities, zero coordinates of \(\theta\), inactive multipliers, a zero multiplier vector, tied rates, and deterministic auxiliary coordinates.

The coordinate law used in the proof has the exact normalization of accepted facts `609407d6db81b18b` and `3aa03555ebd91f71`:
\[
F(z)=\frac{1+6z+z^2}{(1-z)^3},\qquad
Q_{e^{-s}}(u)=\frac{(2u+1)^2e^{-3su}}{F(e^{-3s})}
\quad(u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}),\qquad
\mu(s)=\mathbb E_{Q_{e^{-s}}}u.
\]
Here \(u\) is a coordinate index. The resource count remains \(k\). We use the predecessor estimates
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-e^{-s})^3F(e^{-3s})&\ge8/27&& (s>0),\\
\mu(s)&\le2/s,\qquad
\mathbb E_{Q_{e^{-s}}}u^2\le7/s^2&& (s>0),\\
\mu(s)&\ge1/(10s)&& (0<s\le h),\qquad h=\log3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The first estimate is proved in `609407d6db81b18b`; the other estimates are proved in `3aa03555ebd91f71`. No multi-resource tilted conclusion is assumed from them.

## Proof

The set \(D\) contains zero and is finite, because its first positive row bounds every coordinate. In particular \(Z>0\). If some \(R_r=0\), that positive row forces \(D=\{0\}\), and the left side of (1) is one for every \(\theta\). Since \(1<g<4\), (1) holds in this case. Assume henceforth that all \(R_r>0\).

First we prove a fixed-width displacement bound using exactly the two accepted coupling facts. For every \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), let \(V_i\) be independent uniform integers in \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\), and put
\[
\sigma_r(j)^2=\sum_i a_{ri}^2j_i(j_i+1)/3
=\operatorname{Var}(a_r\cdot V).
\]
We claim
\[
\Pr\{|a_r\cdot V|\le\sigma_r(j)\text{ for all }r\}
\ge e^{-\tau k}.
\tag{3}
\]
Accepted fact `7f35aa79638b5745`, applied with its integer parameter equal to each present \(j_i\), supplies a symmetric coupling \(K_{j_i}\) on \(\{0,\ldots,j_i\}^2\) with identical marginals \(q_{j_i}\), with
\(q_{j_i}(x)\le2/(j_i+1)\), with \(K_{j_i}(x,y)\ge\tfrac18q_{j_i}(x)q_{j_i}(y)\), and with \(X_i-Y_i\) uniform on \(\{-j_i,\ldots,j_i\}\). Take these pairs independently and write \(q=\bigotimes_i q_{j_i}\) for the marginal law of \(X\). Thus \(X-Y\) has exactly the law of \(V\).

The marginal upper bound gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_q(X_i)
\le\mathbb E_q(X_i-j_i/2)^2
\le\frac2{j_i+1}\sum_{x=0}^{j_i}(x-j_i/2)^2
=\frac{j_i(j_i+2)}6
\le\frac{j_i(j_i+1)}3.
\]
Independence consequently gives \(\operatorname{Var}_q(a_r\cdot X)\le\sigma_r(j)^2\). If \(\sigma_r(j)=0\), then \(a_r\cdot X\) and \(a_r\cdot Y\) are the same constant almost surely and the associated condition in (3) holds automatically. If every \(\sigma_r(j)=0\), (3) follows at once. Otherwise, for each row of positive variance define
\[
L_r=\left\lfloor
\frac{a_r\cdot X-\mathbb E_q(a_r\cdot X)}{\sigma_r(j)}
\right\rfloor.
\]
The inequality \(\lfloor u\rfloor^2\le2u^2+2\) implies \(\mathbb E_qL_r^2\le4\). Summing over these rows and using Markov's inequality shows that the integer vectors of bin labels with \(\sum_r L_r^2\le8k\) carry \(q\)-probability at least \(1/2\). If the number of these rows is \(q_0\le k\), the number of such labels is at most
\[
e^{8k}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z^{q_0}}e^{-\sum_r\ell_r^2}
=e^{8k}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-m^2}\right)^{q_0}
\le(3e^8)^k.
\]
For the last inequality, \(\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-m^2}\le1+2\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-m}=1+2/(e-1)<3\), using \(e>2\). Therefore one bin event \(E\) satisfies \(q(E)\ge[2(3e^8)^k]^{-1}\). Any two points in the same bin have \(|a_r\cdot(X-Y)|<\sigma_r(j)\) on every positive-variance row, and the zero-variance rows already hold automatically.

Apply accepted fact `58a99d8c7edb912b` to the product coupling just constructed with its \(\beta=1/8\). Its exponent \(C_\beta=2/(1-\sqrt{1-\beta})\) is the present \(C_*\), so
\[
\Pr(X\in E,Y\in E)\ge q(E)^{C_*}
\ge[2(3e^8)^k]^{-C_*}
\ge(6e^8)^{-C_*k}=e^{-\tau k},
\]
where the penultimate inequality uses \(k\ge1\). The coordinate spaces are finite and nonempty, the marginals are identical, and each coupling is symmetric and has the asserted minorization, including \(j_i=0\). These are all the hypotheses of that accepted fact. This proves (3).

Next we reconstruct the truncated-potential mechanism of accepted fact `41d185e7405de421` with the required shifted rates. Set
\(\psi(s)=\tfrac13\log F(e^{-3s})\),
\(\phi(s)=\max\{0,\psi(s)-\psi(h)\}\) for \(s>0\), and \(\phi(0)=+\infty\). Differentiating the positive exponential series defining \(F(e^{-3s})\) gives \(\psi'(s)=-\mu(s)\) and \(\psi''(s)=3\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{e^{-s}}}(u)\ge0\). Thus \(\phi\) is convex, vanishes for \(s\ge h\), and diverges at zero. In particular \(\mu\) is nonincreasing.

Minimize on \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}^k\) the function
\[
\mathcal F(\lambda)=\lambda\cdot R+
\sum_{i=1}^n\phi\left(\sum_{r=1}^k\lambda_ra_{ri}-\theta_i\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Every argument in (4) is nonnegative. A positive first-coordinate multiplier gives a finite value. A finite sublevel is bounded because \(\mathcal F(\lambda)\ge\lambda\cdot R\) and every capacity is positive. A minimizing subsequence has a limit in the closed orthant. The divergence of \(\phi\) excludes a zero coordinate argument at that limit, and continuity at the remaining positive arguments proves attainment. Fix a minimizer and define
\[
t_i=\sum_r\lambda_ra_{ri},\qquad
s_i=t_i-\theta_i>0,\qquad
\mu_i=\mu(s_i),\qquad
\Lambda=\lambda\cdot R.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(\theta_i\le0\), these rates satisfy \(0\le t_i\le s_i\). The multiplier vector may be zero. If it is zero, the proved positivity of every \(s_i=-\theta_i\) implies every \(\theta_i<0\). If any \(\theta_i=0\), the minimizer cannot be the zero vector. Thus no auxiliary law at a zero rate is used.

There exists a vector \(x\) with
\[
x_i=\mu_i\ (s_i<h),\qquad x_i=0\ (s_i>h),\qquad
0\le x_i\le\mu_i\ (s_i=h),
\]
such that
\[
Ax\le R,\qquad
\lambda_r(R_r-a_r\cdot x)=0\quad(1\le r\le k).
\tag{6}
\]
Here is a justification including all zero multipliers and ties. Let \(\mathcal X\) be the displayed box with fixed untied coordinates, let \(G_0=\{R-Ax:x\in\mathcal X\}\), and let \(\mathcal Q\) be the closed cone whose coordinate \(r\) is zero if \(\lambda_r>0\) and nonnegative if \(\lambda_r=0\). Suppose \(G_0\cap\mathcal Q=\varnothing\). A nearest pair \(g_0,u_0\) exists at positive distance, since \(G_0\) is compact and \(\mathcal Q\) is closed. Put \(v=u_0-g_0\ne0\). Distance minimization along segments gives
\(v\cdot g\le v\cdot g_0\) for all \(g\in G_0\) and \(v\cdot u\ge v\cdot u_0\) for all \(u\in\mathcal Q\). Setting \(u=0,2u_0\) gives \(v\cdot u_0=0\), whence
\(\max_{g\in G_0}v\cdot g\le-\|v\|^2<0\).
The inequality on the cone also gives \(v_r\ge0\) whenever \(\lambda_r=0\), so \(v\) is a feasible direction at the minimizer.

The directional derivative of (4) along \(v\) is exactly \(\max_{x\in\mathcal X}v\cdot(R-Ax)\). For a tied coordinate it is \(\max\{0,-\mu_i\sum_r v_ra_{ri}\}\); for an untied coordinate it is the ordinary derivative. The shift \(-\theta_i\) is constant and changes no directional derivative. All current arguments are positive, so these derivatives are taken within their domain. The derivative is strictly negative by the separation just proved, contradicting minimality. Therefore \(G_0\cap\mathcal Q\ne\varnothing\), proving (6), also when \(\lambda=0\).

Choose an extreme point of the nonempty compact polytope consisting of (6) and the tie box. At most \(k\) tied coordinates can satisfy \(0<x_i<\mu_i\): otherwise a nonzero vector supported on \(k+1\) such coordinates and annihilated by all rows of \(A\) permits small perturbations in both directions within the box, preserving all resource sums, contrary to extremality. Put
\(I_0=\{i:x_i=\mu_i\}\) and \(I_*=\{i:0<x_i<\mu_i\}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|I_*|&\le k,&
\Lambda&=\sum_i t_ix_i,&
\sum_{i\in I_0}a_{ri}\mu_i&\le R_r,\\
s_i&\le h\quad(i\in I_0),&
s_i&\ge h\quad(i\notin I_0),&
\sum_{i\in I_*}t_ix_i&\le2k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The equality is complementary slackness from (6), with \(t_i\), rather than \(s_i\), as the dual score. The last inequality follows from \(t_i\le s_i\) and (2). The untied dropped coordinates have \(x_i=0\). This construction requires no assumption about the full vector of means \((\mu_i)_i\).

Apply accepted fact 9ec030107c9689ad with its coordinate dimension and row count equal to the present \(n,k\), with its parameter equal to the present \(\kappa=4096\), and with the nonnegative row entries
\(w_{ri}=a_{ri}\mu_i\) for \(i\in I_0\) and \(w_{ri}=0\) otherwise. It supplies a shared set of coordinates. Delete any indices outside \(I_0\) from that set; all row masses and tail energies are unchanged by deleting these zero columns. Denote the resulting set by \(I\subseteq I_0\). Define \(U_r=\sum_{i\in I}a_{ri}\mu_i\). The exact conclusion of that accepted fact is
\[
\begin{aligned}
|I|&\le\kappa\sqrt{nk}+k,\\
\sum_{i\in I_0\setminus I}(a_{ri}\mu_i)^2
&\le U_r^2/\kappa^2\qquad(1\le r\le k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This application requires no normalization of the rows. If \(I_0=\varnothing\), choose \(I=\varnothing\) directly; every \(w_r\) is zero and (8) holds. If \(I_0\ne\varnothing\), every row on \(I_0\) is strictly positive. In that case \(I\) is nonempty, because an empty selection would contradict the tail inequality in (8), and consequently every \(U_r>0\).

For \(i\in I_0\), put \(s_i'=\kappa s_i\) when \(i\in I\), and \(s_i'=s_i\) otherwise. Under an auxiliary independent-coordinate law \(Q\), let \(J_i\) have law \(Q_{e^{-s_i'}}\) for \(i\in I_0\), and set \(J_i=0\) deterministically for \(i\notin I_0\). Write \(\nu_i=\mathbb E_QJ_i\) on \(I_0\). Monotonicity of \(\mu\) and (2) give
\[
\nu_i\le\mu_i\quad(i\in I_0),\qquad
\nu_i\le\frac{20}{\kappa}\mu_i\le\frac14\mu_i\quad(i\in I),\qquad
\mathbb E_QJ_i^2\le\frac7{(s_i')^2}\quad(i\in I_0).
\tag{9}
\]

Suppose first \(I_0\ne\varnothing\). The means satisfy
\[
\mathbb E_Q(a_r\cdot J)
\le\sum_{i\in I_0}a_{ri}\mu_i-\frac34U_r
\le R_r-\frac34U_r.
\tag{10}
\]
Both \(\operatorname{Var}_Q(a_r\cdot J)\) and \(\mathbb E_Q\sigma_r(J)^2\) are at most
\[
700\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{(a_{ri}\mu_i)^2}{(s_i'/s_i)^2}
\le\frac{1400U_r^2}{\kappa^2}
\le\frac{U_r^2}{48^2}.
\tag{11}
\]
To check each estimate, \(\mu_i\ge1/(10s_i)\) holds on \(I_0\), so (9) bounds the coordinate variance term by \(700(a_{ri}\mu_i)^2/(s_i'/s_i)^2\). For the squared displacement variance use the integer inequality \(J_i(J_i+1)\le2J_i^2\); its coefficient \(1400/3\) is no larger than 700. The sum over \(I\) is at most \(U_r^2/\kappa^2\), because those coordinates have inflated rates and the sum of squared nonnegative weights is at most the square of their sum. The remaining sum is at most \(U_r^2/\kappa^2\) by the tail inequality in (8). Finally \(1400\cdot48^2\le4096^2=\kappa^2\).

Define \(G_r=\{a_r\cdot J+\sigma_r(J)\le R_r\}\) and \(G=\bigcap_rG_r\). Chebyshev's inequality for the resource sum and Markov's inequality for \(\sigma_r(J)^2\) show that, with probability at least \(7/8\),
\[
a_r\cdot J\le\mathbb E_Q(a_r\cdot J)+4\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_Q(a_r\cdot J)},\qquad
\sigma_r(J)\le4\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q\sigma_r(J)^2}.
\]
Each of the two exceptional probabilities is at most \(1/16\); a zero variance or zero squared expectation makes its condition hold identically. By (10) and (11), on this event the increase above the mean plus the allowed displacement width is at most \(8U_r/48=U_r/6\), smaller than the margin \(3U_r/4\). Hence \(Q(G_r)\ge7/8\). If \(I_0=\varnothing\), the law is \(J=0\), and every \(G_r\) holds identically.

Every \(G_r\) is coordinatewise decreasing, because the row coefficients are positive and \(j_i(j_i+1)\) increases on the nonnegative integers. Independent-coordinate laws positively associate such events. For completeness, decreasing bounded functions \(f,g\) of a single variable satisfy
\(\operatorname{Cov}(f(X),g(X))=\tfrac12\mathbb E[(f(X)-f(X'))(g(X)-g(X'))]\ge0\), where \(X'\) is an independent copy. In a product of variables, condition on all but one variable. The conditional covariance is nonnegative, and the two conditional means remain decreasing functions of the other coordinates. The conditional covariance identity and induction prove the same statement for a product law. Applying it successively to the event indicators and their decreasing intersections gives
\[
Q(G)\ge(7/8)^k=e^{-d_0k},\qquad G\subseteq D.
\tag{12}
\]
The inclusion is for integer roots in the support of \(Q\).

We now bound the conditioning loss of the correct dual score. Put \(T=t\cdot J\) and \(M_Q=\mathbb E_QT\). From (7), (9), and \(0\le t_i\le s_i\),
\[
0\le\Lambda-M_Q
=\sum_{i\in I_*}t_ix_i+\sum_{i\in I}t_i(\mu_i-\nu_i)
\le2k+2|I|.
\tag{13}
\]
Every other term vanishes: unshrunk active coordinates have mean \(\mu_i\), and the remaining dropped coordinates have \(x_i=0\). In particular (13) holds when \(\lambda=0\), in which case \(t=0\), \(\Lambda=0\), and \(T=0\).

For \(u\ge0\), let \(\ell(u)=\log\mathbb E_Qe^{-u(T-M_Q)}\). Its second derivative is the variance of \(T\) under the corresponding exponential tilt. On \(I_0\), that tilted coordinate law is exactly \(Q_{e^{-(s_i'+ut_i/3)}}\). The rates are positive because \(s_i'>0\) and \(t_i\ge0\); zero values of \(t_i\) simply leave a coordinate law unchanged. By independence and (2),
\[
\ell''(u)
=\sum_{i\in I_0}t_i^2\operatorname{Var}_{Q_{e^{-(s_i'+ut_i/3)}}}(J_i)
\le\sum_{i\in I_0}\frac{7t_i^2}{(s_i'+ut_i/3)^2}
\le7n.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(t_i\le s_i\le s_i'\). All derivatives exist by the positive exponential rates. Since \(\ell(0)=\ell'(0)=0\), integration gives \(\ell(u)\le7nu^2/2\).

Put \(D_G=-\log Q(G)\le d_0k\). Restriction of the expectation defining \(\ell(u)\) to \(G\), followed by conditional Jensen, gives
\(\ell(u)\ge-D_G+u(M_Q-\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G])\). Thus for \(u>0\), the mean loss is at most \(D_G/u+7nu/2\). Minimizing when \(D_G>0\), and letting \(u\downarrow0\) when \(D_G=0\), proves
\[
\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G]
\ge\Lambda-2k-2|I|-\sqrt{14d_0nk}.
\tag{15}
\]
This reasoning also covers the constant score \(T=0\).

For a root \(j\in G\), apply the fixed-width estimate (3) to its independent uniform displacement coordinates. Such displacements have \(j\pm V\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\). Whenever all the slab conditions hold,
\(a_r\cdot(j\pm V)\le a_r\cdot j+|a_r\cdot V|\le a_r\cdot j+\sigma_r(j)\le R_r\) for every row. Both endpoints therefore lie in the original \(D\), so its actual count satisfies
\[
k_j\ge e^{-\tau k}\prod_i(2j_i+1)\quad(j\in G).
\tag{16}
\]

The nonnegative multipliers give \(t\cdot z\le\Lambda\) for every \(z\in D\). Since \(\theta_i=t_i-s_i\), the actual original denominator obeys
\[
Z=\sum_{z\in D}e^{\theta\cdot z}
\le e^\Lambda\sum_{z\in D}e^{-s\cdot z}
\le e^\Lambda\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1}.
\tag{17}
\]
Put \(Z_Q=\prod_{i\in I_0}F(e^{-3s_i'})\), with empty products equal to one. On the support of \(Q\), every coordinate outside \(I_0\) is zero. The exact product normalization gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\prod_i(2j_i+1)^2e^{3\theta\cdot j}
&=Z_QQ(J=j)\exp\left(3\sum_{i\in I_0}(s_i'+\theta_i)j_i\right)\\
&\ge Z_QQ(J=j)e^{3t\cdot j},
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
because \(s_i'+\theta_i\ge s_i+\theta_i=t_i\) and \(j_i\ge0\). This is the reason the dual score in (13)--(15) is \(t\cdot J\), rather than the shifted-rate score \(s\cdot J\).

Square (16), multiply by \(e^{3\theta\cdot j}\), sum over \(G\subseteq D\), and use (17) and (18). Jensen applied to \(e^{3T}\) conditional on \(G\), followed by (12) and (15), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sum_{j\in D}k_j^2e^{3\theta\cdot j}}{Z^3}
&\ge e^{-2\tau k}Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^3
\,Q(G)e^{3(\mathbb E_Q[T\mid G]-\Lambda)}\\
&\ge\exp\left[-(2\tau+d_0+6)k-6|I|-3\sqrt{14d_0nk}\right]
Z_Q\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The event \(G\) is contained in the finite set \(D\), so this conditional exponential expectation is finite even if an unrestricted positive exponential moment of \(T\) would diverge. The denominator in (19) has remained the original \(Z^3\) throughout.

For every \(i\in I_0\setminus I\), the corresponding coordinate factor in (19) is at least \(8/27\), by (2). For \(i\notin I_0\), it is \((1-e^{-s_i})^3\ge(1-e^{-h})^3=8/27\), by (7). This includes every deterministic coordinate, including dropped fractional tie coordinates. For \(i\in I\),
\[
\frac{F(e^{-3\kappa s_i})}{F(e^{-3s_i})}\ge\frac1{8\kappa^3}.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed \(1+6z+z^2\) lies between 1 and 8 for \(0\le z\le1\), and the geometric-sum identity for the positive integer \(\kappa\) gives \(1-e^{-\kappa v}\le\kappa(1-e^{-v})\) for \(v>0\). Applying these observations with \(v=3s_i\) proves (20). Therefore (19) becomes
\[
\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^2
\ge\exp\left[-b|I|-(2\tau+d_0+6)k-3\sqrt{14d_0nk}\right]
\left(\frac8{27}\right)^n.
\tag{21}
\]
All estimates also apply when \(I_0=\varnothing\), with the auxiliary law concentrated at zero and all active-coordinate products empty.

By (8), the entire exponential price in (21) is at most
\[
(\kappa b+3\sqrt{14d_0})\sqrt{nk}
+(b+2\tau+d_0+6)k.
\]
When \(k\le n\), one has \(k\le\sqrt{nk}\), so this price is at most \(M\sqrt{nk}\), with exactly the constant \(M\) in the statement. In particular the proof gives
\(\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^2\ge e^{-M\sqrt{nk}}(8/27)^n\) for positive capacities and \(k\le n\). If \(k\le\epsilon n\), then \(\epsilon\le1\) ensures \(k\le n\), and the price is at most \(M\sqrt\epsilon\,n\le\delta n/2\). Since \(8/27=e^\delta/4\), this is exactly
\(\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^2\ge e^{\delta n/2}4^{-n}=g^n4^{-n}\), proving (1). The zero-capacity case was established at the start. The result is a reflection-moment theorem on the stated resource supports, with no claim of a final proof of the locked mixed-integer theorem.
