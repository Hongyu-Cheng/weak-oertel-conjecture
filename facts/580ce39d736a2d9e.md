## Statement

Let $P$ be a fixed finite complete lattice base, $K_z$ positive compatible convex $d$-fibers, $d\ge1$, $\mu$ summed fiber volume, $M=\sum_z|K_z|$, and $\Gamma(u)=\sqrt{u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u}$ for the mass-normalized pooled within-fiber covariance. Let $f$ be the body-tied profile of 32418f8d50e792af, $\varepsilon\ge0$, $h>0$, $\Psi(s)=\min(1,\max(0,1+s))$, $\psi=\mathbf1_{(-1,0)}$. For $N=(a,u)$ with $u\ne0$, define $T_h(N,c)=\int\Psi((N\cdot w-c)/(h\Gamma(u)))\,d\mu$. Use threshold $c=N\cdot Y-\varepsilon f(Y)\Gamma(u)$ at a sampled mark $Y=(p,y)$, and retain ordinary closed pure-base caps. Write $\beta_{\varepsilon,h}$ for the normalized maximum of the through-mark infimum, with the closed ordinary or buffered cap at $h=0$.

For every cap $T_h(N,c)=h^{-1}\int_0^h\mu\{N\cdot w\ge c-s\Gamma(u)\}\,ds$, and globally $\beta_{\varepsilon,h}\ge\beta_{\varepsilon,0}\ge\beta_{0,0}$. For positive families in a fixed containing ball with $M=1$, the raw pointwise excess over width zero is at most $C h$, uniformly over all marks and tilts. The raw depth is continuous under simultaneous positive-fiber Hausdorff and feasible mark convergence at fixed $\varepsilon,h$, and so is its normalized maximum.

Assume additionally a strict ordinary trial $\beta_{0,0}<b$ and the invariant compact normalized original-depth sublevel at $b$ with positive volume, inradius and covariance floors, as in 4bf4936eff4d698d. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon\ge0$ and $h>0$, the unrestricted infimum over the fixed complete compatible family class is attained. Each deepest mark has a uniform own interior ball, and each closed pure-base cap there has a uniform positive reserve over the raw maximum. For each such mark the infimum is attained by a nonvertical cap. After $\Gamma(u)=1$, there is one compact domain of base tilts and continuous normals containing a replacement for every nonvertical through-mark cap on the normalized class. Replacement preserves the full ramp profile and strip data on every old fiber. At every deepest mark the active representatives form a nonempty compact set, with $0<S_{\min}\le S=\int\psi((N\cdot w-c)/h)\,d\mu/h\le S_{\max}$ and $-hS\le A=\int[(N\cdot w-c)/h]\psi((N\cdot w-c)/h)\,d\mu\le0$, uniformly for the indicated parameters.

## Proof

Averaging the upper halfspaces at $c-s\Gamma(u)$ gives exactly the ramp, including both endpoints, by Fubini. These halfspaces contain the zero-width trace, and the nonnegative buffer only enlarges the ordinary cap. Normalize a nonvertical normal to $|u|=1$. All fiber parallel sections in the radius-$R$ ball have area at most $\omega_{d-1}R^{d-1}$, with counting in dimension one. For a point of such a fiber, only an extra slab of Euclidean width $h\Gamma(u)$ can contribute relative to the width-zero cap. The covariance is bounded above on the common ball, proving the uniform comparison. For continuity, in the Fubini representation compare the cap masses at the same $a,u,s$ on two families. Changing the domain costs at most the sum of symmetric differences, tending to zero by positive Hausdorff convergence. On the common containing ball, the remaining threshold displacement is bounded uniformly in $a,u,s$ by mark motion and convergence of $f,\Sigma$ from 32418f8d50e792af. The same slab estimate applies to this displacement. The closed pure-base traces are fixed. Taking infima preserves the uniform comparison and Hausdorff approximation of each compact fiber gives convergence of the maxima. These arguments also give the continuity at fixed width zero needed for the original sublevel.

By 32418f8d50e792af affine normalization preserves the normalized problem, also for the ramp since its halfspace offsets each transform by the same covariance norm. The strict trial stays below $b$ for small $\varepsilon,h$, because 4bf4936eff4d698d bounds the added buffer and the ramp adds at most $Ch$. Global outward dominance puts all sufficiently far terms of a minimizing sequence in the ordinary sublevel, so a normalized limit attains the unrestricted infimum. The buffer and ramp together change the ordinary raw depth by at most $C'(\varepsilon+h)$ markwise. The ordinary maximum is at least $q_0=\gamma_d/|P|$ at mass one, ordinary depth vanishes on own boundaries and is uniformly Lipschitz in the continuous mark by the proof of 4bf4936eff4d698d. Thus any smoothed deepest mark has ordinary depth at least $q_0-C'(\varepsilon+h)$ and a uniform own ball. At a marked base the same predecessor's pure-base reserve over ordinary mark depth is $V_p/2$, so at each smoothed deepest mark it is at least $v_0/4$ for the smoothed maximum. The two slab planes on the own fiber lie at Euclidean distances at most $(\varepsilon+h)\sqrt{\lambda_+}$ from the through-mark plane, hence the entire slab has uniformly proper sections of the own ball for sufficiently small parameters.

For clipping take $B$ larger than the uniform supremum of $|u\cdot(X-y)+\varepsilon f(Y)|+h$ when $\Gamma(u)=1$ and $X$ lies in any sampled fiber. For a tilt $a$, preserve the values $a\cdot(z-p)$ in $[-B,B]$ exactly, and constrain values above $B$ or below $-B$ to stay in the corresponding closed outer ray. The system has the old tilt as a feasible point. Its minimum-Euclidean-norm point belongs to the span of the active rows: small motions in either direction perpendicular to all active rows remain feasible. On that span the point is determined by a linearly independent set of rows, with bounded right-hand sides and with rows from the finite list $\{\pm(z-p):p,z\in P\}$. There are only finitely many independent row matrices, so their solutions in their row spans are uniformly bounded. On clipped fibers both old and replacement margins are strictly saturated, preserving $\Psi$ pointwise and the zero strip integrands. On every other fiber the full margin is unchanged. This preserves the strip moments, the response at a fixed source under fixed-normal body differentiation, and every integral of the old boundary ramp against an inward speed. One may choose the clipping bound uniformly in a small positive Hausdorff neighborhood of each fixed family and all nearby marks, using the same compact domain with any fixed equivalent normalization for the continuous normals. This last choice follows by clipping after rescaling to that fixed normal sphere and using the corresponding uniform bounds on $\Gamma$.

The clipped parameter domain and cap mass continuity give attainment and compactness, and the reserve eliminates pure-base active caps. The kink planes are null on every positive fiber for $u\ne0$. Differentiation by dominated convergence gives $T_h$ continuously differentiable in $(a,u,c)$ for $u\ne0$ at fixed body, with threshold derivative $-S$ in $\Gamma$-unit normalization. On the own ball, averaging proper sections over the strip gives a uniform positive lower bound for $S$ after division by $h$ because $1/|u|\ge\sqrt{\lambda_-}$. The common outer radius similarly gives the upper bound using $1/|u|\le\sqrt{\lambda_+}$. The strip argument is in $(-1,0)$ wherever $\psi$ is nonzero, giving the stated bounds on $A$.
