## Statement

For a centrally symmetric convex body $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}D$, write $h_D(u)=\max_{v\in D}u\cdot v$. For nonvertical affine margins $L_i(z,x)=a_i\cdot z+u_i\cdot x-b_i$, define $\Psi_D=\sum_i\int_S(L_i)_+\,d\mu/h_D(u_i)$ on the finite positive-volume integer fibers with summed volume $\mu$ and mass $M$. Each margin may be positively rescaled to $h_D(u_i)=1$ without changing its contribution or its cap. Let $K_z$ be the fibers over the finite set $P$ of integer bases carrying positive volume, and set $D_{\rm int}=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{z\in P}(K_z-K_z)$. For fibers of a convex body $C$, put $D_{\rm all}=\{v:(0,v)\in C-C\}$. Both bodies are compact, convex, centrally symmetric and full-dimensional, and $D_{\rm int}\subseteq D_{\rm all}$. Under the map $(z,x)\mapsto(Uz+k,Ax+Bz+b)$ with $U$ integral unimodular, $k$ integral and $A$ invertible, each of these two bodies transforms to $AD$. For either choice of body, the ratio $\Psi_D/M$ and the normalized cap masses are invariant.

## Proof

The hinge and support function are positively homogeneous, and positive rescaling leaves the halfspace unchanged. The second difference body consists precisely of continuous differences of two points of $C$ with equal real base. It is the identification of the compact convex symmetric slice $(C-C)\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb R^d)$ with $\mathbb R^d$. The first body is the convex hull of finitely many compact symmetric convex sets. Every difference used in the first body is in the second, and each body contains a neighborhood of zero from any positive-volume fiber.

Under the specified map, same-base continuous differences transform by $A$, and the inverse map has the corresponding property. The integer-base map is bijective on the lattice. Expressing the original margin in new coordinates gives $u_i'=A^{-T}u_i$, $a_i'=U^{-T}(a_i-B^Tu_i')$ and $b_i'=b_i+u_i'\cdot b+a_i'\cdot k$. The margin has the same value pointwise and $h_{AD}(u_i')=h_D(u_i)$. Each fiber volume, cap mass and hinge numerator is multiplied by $|\det A|$.
