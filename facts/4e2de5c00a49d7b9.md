## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\), let \(Q\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be convex, and suppose \(A=Q\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite and nonempty. Assume that each coordinatewise parity class in \(\mathbb Z^n/2\mathbb Z^n\) contains at most two points of \(A\). Let the positive raw weights be given by one global affine law \(w_x=\exp(c+\boldsymbol\ell\cdot x)\), with \(c\in\mathbb R\), \(\boldsymbol\ell\in\mathbb R^n\), and let \(Z=\sum_{x\in A}w_x\), \(p_x=w_x/Z\). Extend \(p\) by zero off \(A\). Suppose every two distinct values of \(\log w_x\) differ by at least \(\log64\). There is no bound on the number of these values.

Use exactly the ambient parity capacity and atomic normalization of accepted fact 011efeb45c7cb9c7: \(K=2^n\) and \(A_0=4^n\sum_{x\in A}p_x^3\). Then
$$
A_0\ge1
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{some exact weight level has original mass at least }
\frac{3961}{4095}>\frac34.
\tag{1}
$$
If the law is uniform on \(A\), its single weight level has mass one, including when the ambient affine field is nonzero but constant on \(A\).

For \(j\in A\), retain the full-row notation \(R_j=\lbrace x\in A:2j-x\in A\rbrace\) and the score \(Y_j(x)=\tfrac12\log(p_x/p_{2j-x})\) of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705. Define the selected row and its original reflected quantities by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat R_j&=\lbrace x\in R_j:w_x=w_j\rbrace,\\
\widehat H_p(j)&=\sum_{x\in\widehat R_j}\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}},
&\widehat b(p)&=\sum_{j\in A}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$
These hats denote selected-row analogues, as in accepted fact 011efeb45c7cb9c7; the unhatted full-row quantities of 445a9e668ee1a705 are not redefined. Each \(\widehat R_j\) contains \(j\), is symmetric about \(j\), and has \(p_x=p_j=p_{2j-x}\) for every retained \(x\). Thus its score is identically zero. More explicitly, with \(\widehat\kappa_j(x)=\sqrt{p_xp_{2j-x}}/\widehat H_p(j)\) on \(\widehat R_j\) and \(\widehat q_j=p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2/\widehat b(p)\), its selected price is
$$
\widehat\nu(p):=
\sum_{j\in A}\widehat q_j\mathbb E_{\widehat\kappa_j}Y_j^2=0,
\qquad
\widehat b(p)\ge4^{-n}.
\tag{3}
$$
Every original probability and every central atom remain in (2)-(3); only noncentral reflected summands are selected.

The following more precise intrinsic estimates include all lower-rank cases. Write \(h=\dim(\operatorname{aff}A)\). If the law is uniform, then \(\widehat b(p)\ge4^{-h}\). If the law is nonuniform and \(A_0<1\), choose a level \(A_*\) supplied by (1), write \(\alpha_*=\sum_{x\in A_*}p_x\) and \(h_*=\dim(\operatorname{aff}A_*)\). Then \(h_*\le h-1\le n-1\) and
$$
\sum_{j\in A_*}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2
\ge \alpha_*^3 4^{-h_*}
\ge4\alpha_*^3 4^{-n}
\ge\frac{27}{16}4^{-n}.
\tag{4}
$$
The rank-zero uniform case gives \(\widehat b(p)=1\).

Now let \(L\ge1\), and for each \(i\in\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace\) let \(p_i\) be any law satisfying the preceding hypotheses in its own ambient dimension \(n_i\ge1\). The factors may have different supports, ranks, weights, and numbers of levels; uniform factors are allowed. Set \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i\) and \(N=\sum_i n_i\), retaining precisely the original product law and normalizers. In the actual closed-halfspace definitions of 445a9e668ee1a705, namely
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf_{\substack{\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ closed halfspace}\\J\in\mathcal H}}P(\mathcal H),
\qquad M_s(P)=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^s,
\tag{5}
$$
one has
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac14\prod_{i=1}^L\widehat b(p_i)
\ge\frac14 4^{-N},
\qquad
M_3(P)\ge\frac18 8^{-N}.
\tag{6}
$$
Equivalently, the same absolute base coefficient \(1/2\) gives \(M_s(P)\ge(1/2)^s2^{-sN}\) for \(s=2,3\). These are actual ambient-halfspace moments, with no assumption that actual depths or moments tensorize.

## Proof

We first record the intrinsic parity facts needed for both the level count and the reflection estimate. Let \(B\subseteq A\) be a nonempty exact weight level, or let \(B=A\) when the law is uniform. Put \(b_0\in B\), \(V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(B-b_0)\), \(h_B=\dim V\), and \(\mathcal L=V\cap\mathbb Z^n\). These are local intrinsic affine-lattice objects; \(h_B\) is not an ambient dimension replacement in the normalization \(K=2^n\).

Because \(V\) is spanned by integer vectors, \(\mathcal L\) has rank \(h_B\). Here is an elementary justification of the group facts being used. Every subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^n\) is free abelian: induct on \(n\), project onto one coordinate, use the induction hypothesis for the kernel, and, if the image is nonzero, lift a generator of its cyclic image to split off one free generator. The rank of an integer matrix equals its rational rank and its real rank, by its nonzero minors. A basis of \(\mathcal L\) therefore has at most \(h_B\) elements, whereas the integer spanning vectors in \(B-b_0\) give at least \(h_B\) independent elements. Consequently \(\mathcal L/2\mathcal L\) has exactly \(2^{h_B}\) elements.

For \(x,y\in b_0+\mathcal L\), ambient same-parity congruence is equivalent to \(x-y\in2\mathcal L\). One direction is immediate. For the other, if \(x-y=2z\) with \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\), then \(z=(x-y)/2\in V\), so \(z\in\mathcal L\). Thus the ambient occupancy bound transfers to the intrinsic parity classes, and
$$
|B|\le2\cdot2^{h_B}.
\tag{7}
$$
This reasoning uses the full lattice \(V\cap\mathbb Z^n\), not just the subgroup generated by the support differences.

The set \(B\) is complete in its intrinsic affine lattice: every point of \(\operatorname{conv}(B)\cap\mathbb Z^n\) lies in \(Q\), hence in \(A\), and has the same affine log-weight as \(B\). It therefore lies in \(B\). In particular, if \(x,y\in B\) have the same intrinsic parity, their integer midpoint belongs to \(B\).

If the law is nonuniform on \(A\), the affine log-weight is nonconstant on \(\operatorname{aff}A\). Each exact level is contained in a proper hyperplane section of that affine hull. Hence \(h_B\le h-1\le n-1\), and (7) gives \(|B|\le2^n=K\). This proves the required level count even when the original support has lower affine rank or the field has irrational coefficients.

We next prove (1) for the nonuniform case with \(A_0<1\). List the distinct original raw weight values as \(v_1>v_2>\cdots>v_m>0\), so \(v_k=w_x\) at every atom in level \(k\). Let \(N_k\) be its number of atoms, and put
$$
\alpha_k=\frac{N_kv_k}{Z},
\qquad
\rho_k=\frac{Kv_k}{Z}.
\tag{8}
$$
Thus \(\alpha_k\) is the original level mass, \(\sum_k\alpha_k=1\), and \(\rho_k=Kp_x\) for any atom in that level. In particular \(Z\) is the original full normalizer. The symbol \(\rho_k\) is a dimensionless level ratio and is distinct from the full-row cardinality parameter \(r\) of 445a9e668ee1a705.

The spectral gap gives \(v_{k+1}\le v_k/64\), hence \(\rho_{k+1}\le\rho_k/64\), for every adjacent pair of existing levels. The count \(N_k\le K\), just proved, and the atomic identity give
$$
\alpha_k=\frac{N_k}{K}\rho_k\le\rho_k,
\qquad
A_0=\sum_{k=1}^m\alpha_k\rho_k^2<1,
\qquad
\alpha_k<\rho_k^{-2}.
\tag{9}
$$
These bounds retain the ambient \(K\), independently of how many parity classes are occupied.

Suppose first that no \(\rho_k\) belongs to \((1/2,2)\). Among levels with \(\rho_k\ge2\), start at the smallest such ratio. Each preceding ratio is at least 64 times the next. By the last bound in (9), their total mass is at most \(1/(4(1-64^{-2}))\). Among levels with \(\rho_k\le1/2\), start at the largest such ratio. Each succeeding ratio is at most \(1/64\) times the preceding one. By the first bound in (9), their total mass is at most \(1/(2(1-64^{-1}))\). Each estimate also holds when the corresponding set of levels is empty, with its actual mass then zero. Therefore
$$
1=\sum_k\alpha_k
\le\frac1{4(1-64^{-2})}+\frac1{2(1-64^{-1})}
=\frac{3104}{4095}<1,
\tag{10}
$$
which is impossible. There is a level \(k_*\) with \(1/2<\rho_{k_*}<2\); there is at most one because distinct ratios are separated by at least a factor of 64.

For the levels strictly above \(k_*\), the geometric bound from (9) is \(1/(4095\rho_{k_*}^2)\). For those strictly below it, it is \(\rho_{k_*}/63\). Empty tails again contribute zero. Since \(1/2<\rho_{k_*}<2\),
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\ne k_*}\alpha_k
&\le\frac1{4095\rho_{k_*}^2}
+\frac{\rho_{k_*}}{63}\\
&\le\frac4{4095}+\frac2{63}
=\frac{134}{4095}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
$$
Thus \(\alpha_{k_*}\ge3961/4095>3/4\). Only upper bounds by infinite geometric series were used: (10)-(11) hold for every finite endpoint, missing intermediate weight values, arbitrary adjacent ratios at most \(1/64\), and the cases in which no level lies above or below the indicated ratio. If the original law is uniform, the one level has mass one without this argument. This proves (1) in all cases.

We now estimate the selected reflected energy without changing any atom weights. Global affinity implies \(w_xw_{2j-x}=w_j^2\) for every full reflected pair. Thus if \(x\in\widehat R_j\), then \(w_x=w_j=w_{2j-x}\); this proves the symmetry and zero-score assertions in (2)-(3). In particular \(\widehat H_p(j)=p_j|\widehat R_j|\ge p_j\). All selected quantities are well defined and positive because the central atom \(j\) belongs to its own row.

Fix one level \(B\) as above, let \(M=|B|\), and let \(t\) denote the common original probability of an atom in \(B\). Then \(\alpha=Mt\) is its original mass. For \(j\in B\), put \(k_j=|\lbrace x\in B:2j-x\in B\rbrace|\); this is exactly \(|\widehat R_j|\). By completeness, the correspondence \((j,x)\mapsto(x,2j-x)\) is a bijection between these oriented row entries and the ordered same-parity pairs of \(B\). If \(M_c\) denotes the count in intrinsic parity class \(c\in\mathcal L/2\mathcal L\), then two applications of Cauchy--Schwarz give
$$
\sum_{j\in B}k_j=\sum_cM_c^2\ge\frac{M^2}{2^{h_B}},
\qquad
\sum_{j\in B}k_j^2\ge\frac1M\left(\sum_{j\in B}k_j\right)^2
\ge M^3 4^{-h_B}.
\tag{12}
$$
This includes every diagonal ordered pair and every central row term once. Using the original probability \(t\), rather than a substituted conditional law, yields
$$
\sum_{j\in B}p_j\widehat H_p(j)^2
=t^3\sum_{j\in B}k_j^2
\ge\alpha^3 4^{-h_B}.
\tag{13}
$$
If the law is uniform, apply (13) to \(B=A\), \(\alpha=1\), \(h_B=h\), to obtain \(\widehat b(p)\ge4^{-h}\ge4^{-n}\). If \(h=0\), the support is a singleton and its selected energy is exactly one. If the law is nonuniform and \(A_0\ge1\), the central terms alone give \(\widehat b(p)\ge\sum_jp_j^3\ge4^{-n}\). In the remaining case choose the level in (1); then \(h_B\le n-1\), and (13) gives (4), since \(4(3/4)^3=27/16\). All other centers still contribute their original nonnegative terms, including every central atom. This proves \(\widehat b(p)\ge4^{-n}\) and all of (3)-(4).

Finally consider the arbitrary heterogeneous product in (5). For \(J=(j_1,\ldots,j_L)\), use the selected product row \(\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}\). It is symmetric about \(J\), contains its central atom, and every retained point \(X\) has its original probability \(P_X=P_J\). Its selected reflected mass therefore factors as
$$
\widehat H_P(J)
=\sum_{X\in\prod_i\widehat R_{i,j_i}}\sqrt{P_XP_{2J-X}}
=\prod_{i=1}^L\widehat H_{p_i}(j_i).
\tag{14}
$$
Every closed ambient halfspace \(\mathcal H\) containing \(J\) contains at least one endpoint of each noncentral reflection pair \(X,2J-X\). Indeed, if a linear inequality excluded both endpoints strictly, it would exclude their midpoint. The central point \(J\) also belongs to \(\mathcal H\). Since paired endpoint probabilities are equal, summing their original masses gives \(P(\mathcal H)\ge\widehat H_P(J)/2\). Taking the infimum over all such halfspaces, including all boundary atoms, gives the same lower bound for \(\tau_P(J)\). No full-dimensionality is used.

Square this pointwise bound, sum against the original product center law, and use only the factorization of finite sums:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2(P)
&\ge\frac14\sum_JP_J\widehat H_P(J)^2\\
&=\frac14\prod_{i=1}^L
\left(\sum_{j_i\in A_i}p_{i,j_i}\widehat H_{p_i}(j_i)^2\right)
=\frac14\prod_{i=1}^L\widehat b(p_i)
\ge\frac14 4^{-N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$
Jensen's inequality for \(u\mapsto u^{3/2}\) on the same original probability space gives \(M_3(P)=\mathbb E_P(\tau_P(J)^2)^{3/2}\ge M_2(P)^{3/2}\ge\tfrac18 8^{-N}\). This proves (6). The original law, its normalizers, all centers, and all halfspaces remain unchanged throughout; only the selected reflected masses in (14)-(15) tensorize.
