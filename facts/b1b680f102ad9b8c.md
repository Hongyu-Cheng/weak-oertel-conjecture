## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body of positive mixed $d$-volume $M$. Fix a linear subspace $V\subset\mathbb R^d$ of dimension $1\le r<d$, write $W=V^\perp$, $m=d-r$, and decompose $x=v+w$. Let $B$ be the projection of $C$ to $\mathbb R^n\times V$. For $(z,v)\in B$ let $f(z,v)$ be the $m$-volume of its $W$-slice, and put $R=(f/\kappa_m)^{1/m}$, where $\kappa_m$ is the unit ball volume in $W$.

The set $C^*=\{(z,v+w):(z,v)\in B,\ |w|\le R(z,v)\}$ is a convex body with projection $B$, including all zero-radius slices. The radius is concave and upper semicontinuous on $B$. The pushforwards of both mixed measures to $\mathbb Z^n\times V$ equal $\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\delta_z\otimes f(z,v)\,dv$, with $f$ extended by zero outside $B$. Thus both bodies have the same nonempty and positive-volume integer-fiber index sets and all integrable Borel tests of $(z,v)$ agree. In particular all closed cap masses, hinges, and integer and projected continuous cap moments for affine margins $L=a\cdot z+u\cdot v-b$ with $u\in V$ agree, including after any parameter perturbations within this family. A fixed family of these margins covers exactly the same specified whole continuous base planes before and after the replacement.

For any nonvertical proper cut $L=0$ of a positive integer fiber, its section measure is $\int_{\{v\in V:L(z,v)=0\}} f(z,v)\,d\mathcal H^{r-1}(v)$ in both bodies. The corresponding integer and $V$-projected section first moments agree, and the $W$-component of the section first moment on $C^*$ is zero. A cap derivative using unnormalized $u$ has the same coarea factor $1/|u|$. The reduced marginal has $f^{1/m}$ concave on $B$, and the original cap budget $\Gamma_d M/2^n$, where $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, is preserved with $d=r+m$.

## Proof

For each $p=(z,v)\in B$ denote the nonempty compact convex $W$-slice of $C$ by $D_p$. Convexity gives $(1-t)D_p+tD_q\subset D_{(1-t)p+tq}$. Brunn–Minkowski gives $R((1-t)p+tq)\ge(1-t)R(p)+tR(q)$. This also holds when both slices have zero volume by nonnegativity. When exactly one has zero volume, retaining one point of it yields the needed translate of a homothetic copy of the other slice.

For $p_j\to p$, closedness of $C$ gives $\limsup_j\mathbf1_{D_{p_j}}(w)\le\mathbf1_{D_p}(w)$ for each $w$. All slices lie in a common bounded ball, so reverse Fatou yields upper semicontinuity of $f$, hence of $R$. This makes $C^*$ closed, and it is bounded. The triangle inequality and radius concavity give convexity. An interior ball of $C$ gives a positive uniform lower bound for $R$ over a sufficiently small open projected ball. Hence $C^*$ has interior. Over every $p\in B$, including $f(p)=0$, the replacement slice is nonempty.

At a fixed integer base, orthogonal Fubini identifies the integral of every integrable Borel test of $(z,v)$ with its integral against $f(z,v)\,dv$. Summing over the finitely many bases proves the pushforward and the per-base mass equalities. Nonempty bases coincide by projection. The specified cap integrands only depend on $(z,v)$, so the equalities apply to their indicators, positive parts and projected moments. Coverage over whole base planes depends only on the margins, which have not changed. In the hyperplane $L=0$, the continuous directions split orthogonally into its $(r-1)$-dimensional affine cut in $V$ and the $m$-dimensional space $W$. Fubini there proves the section formulas and their first-moment versions, with $\mathcal H^0$ when $r=1$. In $C^*$ reflection $w\mapsto-w$ cancels the perpendicular moment. The coarea factor for $u\cdot v$ is $1/|u|$ in either body. The original budget is a function of the unchanged mass and dimension $d$.
