## Statement

Let \(I\) be a nonempty finite index set, let \(D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d\) be a compact full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body, and write \(h_D(x):=\sup_{y\in D}\langle x,y\rangle\). Let \(\bar\beta_i>0\) for \(i\in I\), set \(L:=\sum_{i\in I}\bar\beta_i\), and suppose
\[
\max_{i\in I}\frac{\bar\beta_i}{L}\le \frac14.
\]
For arbitrary vectors \(U_i\in\mathbb{R}^d\) satisfying \(h_D(U_i)\le \bar\beta_i\) for every \(i\in I\), there exist vectors \(V_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^d\), defined for distinct \(i,j\in I\), such that \(V_{ij}=V_{ji}\),
\[
\sum_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}V_{ij}=2U_i\qquad(i\in I),
\]
\[
h_D(V_{ij})\le 16\frac{\bar\beta_i\bar\beta_j}{L}\qquad(i\ne j),
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq I}h_D(V_{ij})\le 8\sum_{i\in I}h_D(U_i),
\]
where the last sum has one term for each unordered two-element subset of \(I\).

## Proof

Origin symmetry, compactness, convexity, and full dimensionality imply that \(h_D\) is a norm: it is symmetric, positively homogeneous, and subadditive, and it is positive away from the origin because \(0\) is an interior point of \(D\).

Normalize the weights by
\[
p_i:=\frac{\bar\beta_i}{L},\qquad q_i:=1-2p_i.
\]
Then \(p_i>0\), \(\sum_i p_i=1\), and \(q_i\ge 1/2\). In particular,
\[
1=\sum_i p_i\le \frac{|I|}{4},
\]
so the hypotheses are inconsistent when \(|I|<4\), including the singleton case. Thus there is no missing low-cardinality construction. The boundary value \(p_i=1/4\) causes no singularity, since then \(q_i=1/2\). If \(|I|=4\), necessarily every \(p_i=1/4\), and the same formulas below apply.

Set
\[
u_i:=h_D(U_i),\qquad H:=\sum_i u_i,
\]
and define
\[
S:=\sum_i\frac{p_i}{q_i},\qquad
A:=\frac{2\sum_i U_i/q_i}{1+S},\qquad
A_i:=\frac{2U_i-p_iA}{q_i}.
\]
All denominators are positive. From the definition of \(A\),
\[
2\sum_i\frac{U_i}{q_i}=(1+S)A,
\]
and hence
\[
\sum_i A_i
=2\sum_i\frac{U_i}{q_i}-A\sum_i\frac{p_i}{q_i}
=(1+S)A-SA=A.
\]
For distinct \(i,j\), define
\[
V_{ij}:=p_jA_i+p_iA_j.
\]
This is symmetric in \(i,j\). Using \(\sum_jp_j=1\) and \(\sum_jA_j=A\), for every \(i\) we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\ne i}V_{ij}
&=(1-p_i)A_i+p_i(A-A_i)\\
&=(1-2p_i)A_i+p_iA\\
&=q_iA_i+p_iA=2U_i.
\end{aligned}
\]

It remains to prove the two norm estimates. Since \(q_i\ge 1/2\), while \(q_i\le1\) gives \(p_i/q_i\ge p_i\),
\[
1+S\ge1+\sum_i p_i=2.
\]
Therefore the triangle inequality and homogeneity give
\[
h_D(A)
\le \frac{2\sum_i u_i/q_i}{1+S}
\le \frac{4H}{2}=2H.
\]
Consequently,
\[
h_D(A_i)
\le\frac{2u_i+p_i h_D(A)}{q_i}
\le 4u_i+4p_iH.
\]
For distinct \(i,j\), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_D(V_{ij})
&\le p_jh_D(A_i)+p_ih_D(A_j)\\
&\le4(p_ju_i+p_iu_j)+8p_ip_jH.
\end{aligned}
\]
The assumptions give \(u_i\le\bar\beta_i=Lp_i\) and \(H\le\sum_i\bar\beta_i=L\). Hence
\[
4(p_ju_i+p_iu_j)\le8Lp_ip_j,
\qquad
8p_ip_jH\le8Lp_ip_j,
\]
so
\[
h_D(V_{ij})\le16Lp_ip_j
=16\frac{\bar\beta_i\bar\beta_j}{L}.
\]

Finally, sum the preceding pre-capacity estimate over unordered pairs. Each \(u_i\) appears with coefficient \(4\sum_{j\ne i}p_j=4(1-p_i)\), and
\[
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq I}p_ip_j
=\frac12\left(1-\sum_i p_i^2\right).
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq I}h_D(V_{ij})
&\le4\sum_i u_i(1-p_i)
+4H\left(1-\sum_i p_i^2\right)\\
&\le4H+4H=8H
=8\sum_i h_D(U_i).
\end{aligned}
\]

If all \(U_i=0\), then \(H=0\) and the displayed construction gives \(A=A_i=V_{ij}=0\), so all conclusions hold. Conversely, \(H=0\) forces all \(U_i=0\) because \(h_D\) is a norm. Individual zero vectors also cause no problem, since the construction never divides by \(u_i\). This completes the proof.
