## Statement

For every integer \(k\ge8\), use exactly the canonical log-affine specialization of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df to construct the support, before choosing any new field. Thus
$$
b_\ell=0\quad(1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\quad(8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}b_h,\qquad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\quad(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell),\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad
n=\sum_{h=1}^{Q}m^h,\qquad N=n+1,
$$
$$
\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2},\qquad
\widehat A_k=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
Allow any partition \(\Pi\) of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) into symmetric saturated chains, and use its injective successor in the construction. Every assertion below is quantified over every such choice. Here \(A_{m,Q}\), its level function \(\operatorname{lev}\), the symmetric-chain successor, and all tree coordinates are precisely those of accepted fact 604889294a17d7df with this choice \(\Pi\). The notation \(\widehat A_k\) is its \(\widehat A\), with dependence on \(k\) displayed and dependence on \(\Pi\) suppressed. The same suppression applies to \(Z_k\) and the one-factor law until the finite-parameter argument below. Its scalar height variable is \(t\), and its dimensions \(n,N\) and branching factor \(m\) are unchanged. In accepted fact 3e23587245b6989f these are the objects obtained with \(T=k\) and its canonical entrance schedule; its \(D\) is \(\widehat A_k\).

For any real \(\beta\), assign the entire support the new positive law
$$
w_{(z,t)}=e^{\beta t},\qquad
Z_k(\beta)=\sum_{(z,t)\in\widehat A_k}e^{\beta t},\qquad
p_{(z,t)}=\frac{e^{\beta t}}{Z_k(\beta)}.
$$
In particular, changing \(\beta\) does not change the support, entrance multiplicities, or tree parameters. At each center choose mixed rows exactly as in accepted fact 8ab782832d1c7da2: the separately fixed target singleton and pairwise disjoint two-point or three-point cycles with that target as arithmetic mean. Write \(H^{(\mathrm m)},b^{(\mathrm m)},\kappa^{(\mathrm m)},Y^{(\mathrm m)},V^{(\mathrm m)},q^{(\mathrm m)},\nu^{(\mathrm m)}\) for precisely its one-factor quantities. Abbreviate \(b_{\mathrm m}=b^{(\mathrm m)}\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm m}=\nu^{(\mathrm m)}\), and put
$$
A_0=4^N\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}p_J^3,\qquad
B=4^N b_{\mathrm m}.
$$
There is one finite numerical constant \(C\ge1\), independent of \(k\), \(\beta\), and the chosen partition \(\Pi\), such that whenever \(A_0<1\), mixed rows can be chosen with
$$
B\ge1,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm m}\le C\log B.
\tag{1}
$$
They can additionally be chosen so that the used triple-cycle variance component \(\nu^{(\mathrm m,3)}\) of accepted fact 8ab782832d1c7da2 is zero. For \(k\ge16\), every selected row score is zero and the construction has \(B\ge9/2\).

Consequently, let \(L\ge1\), choose arbitrary \(k_i\ge8\), arbitrary allowed partitions \(\Pi_i\) for their respective Boolean cubes, and arbitrary real \(\beta_i\), and let \(p_i\) be the laws just defined on their entire respective canonical supports. Set
$$
P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^L p_i,\qquad
\mathcal N=\sum_{i=1}^L N_{k_i}.
$$
For the actual ambient closed-halfspace depth
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf\lbrace P(\mathcal H):\mathcal H\subseteq\mathbb R^{\mathcal N}
\text{ is a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace
$$
and \(M_r(P)=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^r\), one has
$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac{e^{-C}}9\,4^{-\mathcal N},\qquad
M_r(P)\ge\left(\frac{e^{-C/2}}3\right)^r2^{-r\mathcal N}\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{2}
$$
These are the depth and moment conventions of accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705 and 8ab782832d1c7da2. They retain every original factor normalizer. The assertions concern only these canonical supports and zero spatial fields.

## Proof

Fix \(k\) and an arbitrary allowed partition \(\Pi\), and write \(x=e^\beta>0\). Set
$$
S_t=\sum_{s=0}^{Q_t}\eta^s,\qquad
T_k(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}S_tx^t,
\qquad
U_k(x)=\sum_{t=0}^{k}S_tx^{3t}.
\tag{3}
$$
The canonical level census in accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, also recorded in accepted fact 3e23587245b6989f, is \(|A_s|=2^n\eta^s\). Thus
$$
Z_k(\beta)=2^nT_k(x),\qquad
T_k(x)=\sum_{s=0}^Q\eta^s\sum_{t=a_s}^{k}x^t,\qquad
A_0=\frac{4U_k(x)}{T_k(x)^3}.
\tag{4}
$$
All sums use the full original support. The quantities \(T_k,U_k\) are scalar height sums, not probability laws or replacement normalizers.

First, activity forces the uniform lower bound
$$
A_0<1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x>\frac14.
\tag{5}
$$
Indeed, if \(0<x\le1/4\), then \(\eta^s\le1\), the entrance schedule, and the geometric series give
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_k(x)
&\le\frac1{1-x}\left(1+\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell x^\ell\right)\\
&\le\frac43\left(1+\frac1{64}\sum_{\ell=8}^{\infty}(2x)^\ell\right)
\le\frac43\left(1+\frac1{8192}\right)<\frac32.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Here \(b_\ell\le2^\ell/(8\ell)\le2^\ell/64\), and \(\sum_{\ell=8}^{\infty}2^{-\ell}=1/128\). Since \(U_k(x)\ge S_0=1\), (4) implies \(A_0>4/(3/2)^3=32/27>1\), proving (5).

We next construct zero-score rows for \(k\ge16\). The depth-\(Q\) construction in accepted fact 3e23587245b6989f gives a set \(G_Q\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\) of Boolean centers, with
$$
|G_Q|\ge2^{n-1},
\tag{7}
$$
such that each \(j\in G_Q\) has \(2^{Q-1}\) triples of distinct spatial source points, mutually disjoint over the entire family and all avoiding \(j\). Every triple has average \(j\); its source levels are \(Q,Q,0\). These are precisely its fixed-depth triples (14) and center set in (28), specialized to the canonical \(m=2(Q+1)^4\). For clarity, its count follows from the fraction \(\alpha\) of root blocks with two predecessors: \(\alpha\ge1-2\delta\), where \(\delta=1-\eta<(Q+1)^{-2}\). The two inverse paths then query \(2Q-2\) distinct fresh blocks, so the center fraction is \(\alpha\eta^{2Q-2}\ge1-2Q\delta\ge1/2\). This is the same count and the same triple family of that accepted fact; no new uniformity assumption is imposed.

At each target \((j,k)\) with \(j\in G_Q\), retain its target singleton and lift all these triples to height \(k\). At every other target choose the singleton row. All sources are present because every entrance height is at most \(k\). Within a selected row the sources remain distinct, avoid the target, and have the target as their arithmetic mean. Thus these are admissible mixed rows. Their nontrivial rows have the common cardinality
$$
c_Q=1+3\cdot2^{Q-1}.
\tag{8}
$$
Every source in a nontrivial row has the same actual height as its target, hence has exactly the same original probability as that target. Its mixed geometric mean is therefore \(p_{(j,k)}\) and its score is zero. Singleton rows also have score zero, so
$$
\nu_{\mathrm m}=\nu^{(\mathrm m,3)}=0.
\tag{9}
$$
The affine identity (9) of accepted fact 8ab782832d1c7da2 is, in the present notation, \(H^{(\mathrm m)}(J)=|R_J^{(\mathrm m)}|p_J\) and \(b_{\mathrm m}=\sum_J|R_J^{(\mathrm m)}|^2p_J^3\). Using only the targets in (7), (4), and (8) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
B
&\ge4^{n+1}2^{n-1}c_Q^2\left(\frac{x^k}{2^nT_k(x)}\right)^3
=\frac{2c_Q^2x^{3k}}{T_k(x)^3}\\
&\ge\frac92\,\frac{4^Qx^{3k}}{T_k(x)^3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
For every \(x>0\), the unchanged full normalizer satisfies
$$
T_k(x)\le(Q+1)(k+1)\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace.
\tag{11}
$$
Indeed, in its middle expression in (4) there are at most \((Q+1)(k+1)\) nonnegative terms, each at most \(\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace\). In the active regime (5), it follows that
$$
\frac{x^{3k}}{T_k(x)^3}
\ge\frac{4^{-3k}}{(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3},\qquad
B\ge\frac92\,\frac{4^{Q-3k}}{(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3}.
\tag{12}
$$
This bound treats both \(x\le1\) and \(x\ge1\): the ratio of \(x^{3k}\) to \(\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace^3\) is respectively \(x^{3k}\) and one.

The last expression in (12) is at least \(9/2\) for every \(k\ge16\). To check this with elementary bounds, \(2^k/(8k)\ge2\) implies
$$
Q\ge b_k\ge\frac{2^k}{16k}.
$$
For \(k\ge16\), one has \(2^k\ge96k^2\): it holds at sixteen, and the ratio \(2^k/k^2\) increases thereafter because \(2k^2>(k+1)^2\). Hence \(Q\ge6k\ge96\). Also
$$
(q+1)^6\le2^q\qquad\text{for every integer }q\ge64.
\tag{13}
$$
At \(q=64\), use \(65^6<128^6=2^{42}<2^{64}\). For the induction step, \(((q+2)/(q+1))^6\le(66/65)^6<(10/9)^6<2\), proving (13). Since \(k+1\le Q+1\) and \(Q-3k\ge Q/2\),
$$
4^{Q-3k}\ge2^Q\ge(Q+1)^6\ge(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3.
\tag{14}
$$
Equations (9), (12), and (14) prove (1) for all \(k\ge16\), with zero price and with any nonnegative \(C\).

It remains to find one finite price for all supports with \(8\le k\le15\), uniformly over their partition choices. For a positive integer \(m\), let \(\mathcal P_m\) be the set of all partitions of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\) into symmetric saturated chains. This set is finite: its elements are particular partitions of a finite set, together with the unique increasing order within each saturated chain. Write \(m(k)=2(Q_k+1)^4\) for the branching factor prescribed at the integer parameter \(k\). Thus the pairs \((k,\Pi)\) with \(8\le k\le15\) and \(\Pi\in\mathcal P_{m(k)}\) form a finite set, although there can be more than one support for each \(k\). Fix one such pair. On its support choose the full reflected row
$$
R_J=\lbrace I\in\widehat A_k:2J-I\in\widehat A_k\rbrace
\tag{15}
$$
at every target. It partitions into the target singleton and all distinct reflection pairs. In this part, every mixed object is exactly the canonical full-row object \(H_p,b(p),\kappa,Y,V,q,\nu(p)\) of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705, under the pure-pair identification proved in accepted fact 8ab782832d1c7da2. In particular, \(b_{\mathrm m}=b(p)\), \(\nu_{\mathrm m}=\nu(p)\), and \(\nu^{(\mathrm m,3)}=0\). Displaying the partition dependence, write \(B_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)=4^Nb(p)\) and \(\nu_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)=\nu(p)\).

We first prove
$$
B_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)>1\qquad(x>0).
\tag{16}
$$
The support is complete convex by accepted fact 604889294a17d7df, and its positive law is globally affine on that support. For an ordered same-parity pair \(I_1,I_2\), the integer midpoint \(J=(I_1+I_2)/2\) belongs to the support, and affineness gives \(p_J^2=p_{I_1}p_{I_2}\). Consequently the parity identity underlying (12) of accepted fact 445a9e668ee1a705 reads
$$
\chi(p):=\sum_Jp_JH_p(J)
=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^N}p(c+2\mathbb Z^N)^2
\ge2^{-N}.
\tag{17}
$$
The identity is the bijection between reflected pairs about integer centers and ordered same-parity pairs; its summands satisfy \(p_J\sqrt{p_{I_1}p_{I_2}}=p_{I_1}p_{I_2}\). The inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz for the parity masses.

Moreover \(H_p(J)\) is not constant over the support. Fix any Boolean tree point \(j\). At heights \(t=0,1,2\), its full reflected rows have respectively one, three, and five points. Their vertical reflections are all present because \(k\ge8\). A reflection with different spatial bases would require a source of positive tree level, whose entrance height is at least eight. Both source heights are nonnegative and sum to \(2t\le4\), which makes that impossible. Here we use the canonical reflection description in accepted fact 604889294a17d7df: a distinct reflected spatial pair has one endpoint of level strictly above its center level; in particular one endpoint has positive level. Thus
$$
H_p((j,0))=\frac1{Z_k(\beta)},\qquad
H_p((j,1))=\frac{3x}{Z_k(\beta)},\qquad
H_p((j,2))=\frac{5x^2}{Z_k(\beta)}.
\tag{18}
$$
These three numbers cannot be equal: the first equality would give \(x=1/3\), whereas the third numerator would then be \(5/9\ne1\). All targets have positive original probability. Therefore strict variance positivity, or strict Jensen under the original law, gives
$$
b(p)=\sum_Jp_JH_p(J)^2>
\left(\sum_Jp_JH_p(J)\right)^2\ge4^{-N}.
$$
This proves (16).

We now establish the endpoint behavior needed for a bounded price, rather than merely asserting compactness on an unbounded field range. For a fixed support let \(t(J)\) denote a target's height, and define the fixed finite integers
$$
\mathsf r_J=|R_J|,\qquad
\mathsf e_J=\sum_{I\in R_J}(t(I)-t(J))^2.
$$
The full-row affine formulas of accepted facts 445a9e668ee1a705 and 8ab782832d1c7da2 give the exact expressions
$$
B_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)=\frac{4^N}{Z_k(\log x)^3}
\sum_J\mathsf r_J^2x^{3t(J)},
\tag{19}
$$
$$
\nu_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)
=(\log x)^2\,
\frac{\sum_J\mathsf r_J\mathsf e_Jx^{3t(J)}}
{\sum_J\mathsf r_J^2x^{3t(J)}}.
\tag{20}
$$
Indeed \(H_p(J)=\mathsf r_Jp_J\), the row law is uniform, and its score at \(I\) is \((\log x)(t(I)-t(J))\). These formulas also prove continuity for every \(x>0\).

As \(x\to\infty\), the original law converges to the uniform law \(p_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{top}}\) on all supported points at height \(k\). Its support is a complete convex lattice slice. Every top-center reflected pair has both source heights equal to \(k\), since their heights are at most \(k\) and average to \(k\). The expression (19), or continuity of the finite reflected sums, therefore yields
$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}B_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)
=4^Nb(p_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{top}})\ge4.
\tag{21}
$$
To verify the last inequality directly, only \(2^{N-1}\) height-compatible parity classes can be occupied at this slice. Its same-parity midpoints remain in the slice by completeness and convexity. The uniform law is affine there, so (17) applied to the slice gives \(\chi(p_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{top}})\ge2^{-(N-1)}\). Jensen then gives \(b(p_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{top}})\ge4^{-(N-1)}\), proving (21). Full-row quantities for this limiting law can equivalently be computed on its positive slice support, so no positivity assumption on the disappearing lower-height atoms is needed.

For every top center \(J\), the preceding height observation gives \(\mathsf e_J=0\). Let \(M_{k,\Pi}\) be the number of supported top-height points and let
$$
D_{k,\Pi}=\sum_{J:t(J)<k}\mathsf r_J\mathsf e_J<\infty.
$$
For \(x\ge1\), the numerator in the fraction of (20) is at most \(D_{k,\Pi}x^{3k-3}\), while its denominator is at least \(M_{k,\Pi}x^{3k}\), since each top row contains its target. Consequently
$$
0\le\nu_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)
\le\frac{D_{k,\Pi}}{M_{k,\Pi}}(\log x)^2x^{-3}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{22}
$$
The constants here depend on the one fixed pair \((k,\Pi)\). The set of all such pairs in this part is finite.

By (16) and (19)-(20), the function
$$
F_{k,\Pi}(x)=\frac{\nu_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)}{\log B_{k,\Pi}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)}
$$
is continuous for \(x>0\). By (21)-(22), it tends to zero at positive infinity. It is therefore bounded on \([1/4,\infty)\): choose a finite upper cutoff beyond which its absolute value is at most one, and use continuity on the remaining compact interval. Define the numerical constant
$$
C=\max\left\lbrace1,
\max_{8\le k\le15}\ \max_{\Pi\in\mathcal P_{m(k)}}\ \sup_{x\ge1/4}F_{k,\Pi}(x)\right\rbrace<\infty.
\tag{23}
$$
This is an absolute constant: each of the eight integers \(k\) prescribes one finite Boolean cube by the displayed numerical schedule, and \(\mathcal P_{m(k)}\) ranges over every allowed partition of that cube. For each pair the supremum is finite by (16) and (19)-(22), and (23) takes the maximum over finitely many such pairs. Thus it is independent of the later chosen \(k\), field, partition, or number of product factors. Equations (5), (16), and (23) prove (1) for all \(8\le k\le15\) and every allowed partition. Together with the partition-independent zero-price construction for \(k\ge16\), they complete the one-factor certificate theorem.

Finally consider the heterogeneous product in the statement. In each active factor choose the rows just proved; in an inactive factor choose singleton rows. An inactive factor then has \(B_i=A_{0,i}\ge1\) and \(\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}=0\), precisely with its original law and normalizer. Every factor has \(\nu_i^{(\mathrm m,3)}=0\), and with
$$
G=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\log B_i\ge0
$$
one has \(\sum_i\nu_i^{(\mathrm m,2)}=\sum_i\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}\le CG\). Apply the first inequality (5) of accepted fact 8ab782832d1c7da2, with its total integer dimension denoted here by \(\mathcal N\). Its triple variance term vanishes, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{\mathcal N}M_2(P)
&\ge\frac19\exp\left(G-2\sqrt{\sum_i\nu_i^{(\mathrm m)}}\right)\\
&\ge\frac19e^{G-2\sqrt{CG}}
\ge\frac19e^{-C}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final inequality is \(G-2\sqrt{CG}=(\sqrt G-\sqrt C)^2-C\). The mixed-product theorem applies even though some factors use pairs and others use triples; it requires the used components' variances, which are exactly those just computed. Finally Jensen under the original product law gives \(M_r(P)\ge M_2(P)^{r/2}\) for every real \(r\ge2\), proving (2). This uses no assertion about a nonzero spatial field or a different convex support.
