## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex, $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ nonempty, $q=\dim_H S$, $\mu=\mathcal H_q|_S$, and $P$ the bases of the positive $\mathcal H_q$-measure fibers $K_z$, with $w_z=\mathcal H_q(K_z)$, $M=\sum_{z\in P}w_z$ and $m=2^n$. For a finite list of original closed ambient halfspaces $H_i$ and a nonempty group $I$ of their indices, write $U_I=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i$, $e_I(z)=\mathcal H_q(K_z\setminus (U_I)_z)$, and $B_I=\mu(U_I)$.

Let $\mathcal G$ be a nonempty finite family of groups. Let $L$ be the sum of the largest $\min(m,|\mathcal G|)$ union masses $B_I$, $W=\max_z w_z$ and $F(t)=\sum_{z\in P}(w_z-t)_+$. If $0<L<M$, let $t_*\in(0,W)$ be the unique root of $F(t_*)=L$. If $L=0$, put $t_*=W$. Then some $z_*\in P$ satisfies $e_I(z_*)\ge t_*$ for every $I\in\mathcal G$. The same guarantee holds with $L$ replaced by $m B_r<M$ whenever $B_I\le B_r$ for all groups in the menu, for example when they have size at most $r$ and this bound holds for each such group. For any finite almost-everywhere cover, some group of at most $q+1$ original caps has union mass at least $M/m$.

## Proof

The function $F$ is continuous, goes from $M$ to zero on $[0,W]$, and is strictly decreasing there because a term with $w_z=W$ strictly decreases up to $W$. This gives the claimed root for $0<L<M$. If $L=0$, every union has zero mass on each positive fiber, so a fiber of mass $W$ satisfies the claim. Otherwise suppose the common threshold fails, and set $t=V(\mathcal G)<t_*$ in pk-w36-menu-joint-remainder-max-min, with all divisors one. Select at most $m$ groups with the same value $t$. At each base one of the selected groups has residual $\le t$, so the sum of their captured masses in that fiber is at least $(w_z-t)_+$. Summing over the fibers gives
$$
L\ \ge\ \sum_{I\in\mathcal J} B_I\ \ge\ F(t)\ >\ F(t_*)=L ,
$$
a contradiction. This uses each group's actual union mass. Any upper bound $mB_r<M$ for $L$ gives an equal or weaker threshold by monotonicity of $F$, including the zero case.

For the cover assertion, a finite closed union covering a positive compact convex fiber almost everywhere covers it entirely. The intersection of the strict complements with its relative interior is empty. Apply ordinary Helly in its affine $q$-plane to retain at most $q+1$ caps covering its relative interior, hence the whole fiber, with the singleton case immediate. The proof of 0f6ca72852c286b2 selects at most $m$ of these original lists with every positive fiber covered by one list. Their unions cover all measured mass, so their summed union masses are at least $M$; one has mass at least $M/m$.
