## Statement

Let $X$ be uniform on $T=\operatorname{conv}(0,e_1,e_2)$, put $Z=(X_1+X_2)/2-1/3$, and let $\xi$ be independent and equiprobable on $\{-1/6,1/6\}$. Write $G(v)=\Pr(Z+\xi\ge v)$ and $s=1/3$. Then
$$
(G(0),G(s),G(-s))=(4/9,0,17/18),\qquad
G(0)+G(s)+\lambda G(-s)-(4/9+\lambda)=-\lambda/18.
$$
For every $0<\lambda\le7/9$ the unperturbed triangle satisfies the weighted bound for every shift and direction. For every fixed $\lambda>0$ the noisy obstruction persists with three proper noisy tails after a sufficiently small reduction in the two noise magnitudes, at shifts twice the new magnitude. At $\lambda=7/9$ it also persists for absolutely continuous independent symmetric noise.

## Proof

For $F(v)=\Pr(Z\ge v)$ put $q=1/3+v$. Similar triangles give $F(v)=1$ if $q\le0$, $1-4q^2$ if $0\le q\le1/2$, and zero if $q\ge1/2$. The section at any cut level is area-null, including at the support endpoint. The dimensional weighted-tail theorem at $d=2$ gives the starting inequality at $\lambda=7/9$. For smaller positive $\lambda$, it follows since $1-F(-s)\ge0$.

For $t=1/6$, independence gives $G(v)=[F(v-t)+F(v+t)]/2$. The six values of $F$ at $t,-t,s+t,s-t,-s+t,-s-t$ are $0,8/9,0,0,8/9,1$. Substitution gives the stated probability deficit. The unaveraged deficit is $\lambda/9$. Also $F(t)+F(-t)=8/9$, so the paired-tail bound is sharp at the original noise shift.

For $0<\varepsilon<1/18$, take $t_\varepsilon=1/6-\varepsilon$ and $s_\varepsilon=2t_\varepsilon$, with noise equiprobable on $\{\pm t_\varepsilon\}$. The two inner component tails are
$$
A=F(t_\varepsilon)=4\varepsilon-4\varepsilon^2,\qquad
B=F(-t_\varepsilon)=8/9-(4/3)\varepsilon-4\varepsilon^2.
$$
Both are proper. The other components use $F(3t_\varepsilon)=0$ and $F(-3t_\varepsilon)=1$, with both cuts strictly outside the support. The three noisy tails are $(A+B)/2,A/2,(1+B)/2$, each in $(0,1)$. Their unaveraged signed gap is
$$
2A+(1+\lambda)B+\lambda-2(4/9+\lambda)
=-\lambda/9+(20-4\lambda)\varepsilon/3-(12+4\lambda)\varepsilon^2.
$$
Its value at zero is negative, proving persistence for sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$. At $\lambda=7/9,\varepsilon=1/100$, the tails are $(10291/22500,99/5000,42191/45000)$, and the averaged deficit is $3203/202500$.

For absolutely continuous noise use $\xi+\eta$ with the original $\xi$ and an independent $\eta$ uniform on $[-1/1000,1/1000]$. The resulting law is symmetric and absolutely continuous. The derivative of the proper branch of $F$ has magnitude at most $4$, and the clipped formula is continuous, so every noisy tail changes by at most $4/1000$. At $\lambda=7/9$ the weighted score changes by at most $(4/1000)(2+7/9)=1/90$. The original deficit at $s=1/3$ is $7/162$, leaving a deficit of at least $13/405$.
