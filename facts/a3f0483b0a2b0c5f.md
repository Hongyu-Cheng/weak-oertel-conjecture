## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and let \(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n\in[0,1]\). For each \(i\), define \(b_i:\mathbb Z\to[0,1]\) by \(b_i(0)=b_i(1)=1\), \(b_i(2)=\varepsilon_i\), and \(b_i(t)=0\) for \(t\notin\{0,1,2\}\). Set \(w_z:=\prod_{i=1}^n b_i(z_i)\), \(W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z=\prod_{i=1}^n(2+\varepsilon_i)\), and \(j:=(1,\ldots,1)\).

For \(v\in\mathbb Z^n\), let \(\alpha(v):=\min\{w_{j+v},w_{j-v}\}\). On the finite set of positive pairs, meaning those \(v\) with \(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}>0\), define \(\Delta_j(v):=\log(1/(w_{j+v}w_{j-v}))\), where \(\log\) denotes the natural logarithm. For \(L\ge0\), let \(B_j^\Delta(L)\) be the sum of \(\alpha(v)\) over these positive pairs with \(\Delta_j(v)\le L\). Then there exists \(L\ge0\) such that

\[
\frac{B_j^\Delta(L)}{1+L}\ge e^{-500}\,2^{-n}W.
\]

The constant is independent of \(n\) and all the parameters, including parameters equal to zero or one. Consequently the same bound holds with the left side replaced by its maximum over \(L\ge0\).

## Proof

Write \(T_I:=\prod_{i\in I}(1+\varepsilon_i/2)\) for any coordinate set \(I\), with empty product equal to one. In particular, \(T_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}=2^{-n}W\). A positive pair necessarily has \(v_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and \(\varepsilon_i=0\) forces \(v_i=0\). On positive pairs the defining products give

\[
\alpha(v)=\min\left\{\prod_{i:v_i=1}\varepsilon_i,\prod_{i:v_i=-1}\varepsilon_i\right\},
\qquad
\Delta_j(v)=\sum_{i:v_i\ne0}\log(1/\varepsilon_i).
\]

In particular \(\alpha(0)=1\), \(\Delta_j(0)=0\), and every cost is nonnegative. Coordinates with \(\varepsilon_i=0\) contribute the factor one to the target product, so they will always be fixed at zero in the chosen vectors.

For \(s\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), put \(I_s:=\{i:2^{-(s+1)}<\varepsilon_i\le2^{-s}\}\). These sets partition the positive parameters. Only finitely many are nonempty, even though no upper bound on their indices is assumed.

Consider a coordinate block \(I\subseteq I_s\) of size \(k\). Let \(\alpha_I\) and \(\Delta_I\) denote the quantities \(\alpha\) and \(\Delta_j\) with their defining products and sums restricted to coordinates in \(I\). On this block retain the zero vector and, for each ordered pair of distinct indices \(p,q\in I\), the vector having coordinate \(+1\) at \(p\), coordinate \(-1\) at \(q\), and zero elsewhere. These are \(1+k(k-1)\) distinct vectors, all positive pairs. Denote their set by \(\mathcal V_I\) and their total local mass by \(Z_I:=\sum_{u\in\mathcal V_I}\alpha_I(u)\). A nonzero chosen vector has mass \(\min(\varepsilon_p,\varepsilon_q)\) and cost \(\log(1/(\varepsilon_p\varepsilon_q))\). Therefore

\[
Z_I\ge1+k(k-1)2^{-(s+1)},
\qquad
\Delta_I(u)\le2(s+1)\log2\quad(u\in\mathcal V_I),
\qquad
T_I\le(1+2^{-(s+1)})^k.
\]

For disjoint blocks, choose their vectors independently and fix every remaining coordinate at zero. The resulting vector is a positive pair, its cost is the sum of the block costs, and its mass is at least the product of the block masses. The last assertion follows from the elementary inequality \(\min(\prod_r a_r,\prod_r b_r)\ge\prod_r\min(a_r,b_r)\) for nonnegative numbers \(a_r,b_r\). Distinct tuples of block vectors give distinct global vectors. Thus, if the sum of valid block cost bounds is \(L\), then \(B_j^\Delta(L)\ge\prod_I Z_I\), where the product runs over the chosen blocks.

We now specify which blocks to choose. For \(0\le s\le3\), partition \(I_s\) into \(m_s\) full blocks of size eight and fewer than eight remaining coordinates. On every full block the preceding bounds imply \(Z_I\ge(9/8)T_I\). To verify this without numerical approximations, set \(t=2^{s+1}\). The required inequality is \(8(t+56)t^7\ge9(t+1)^8\). For \(t=2,4,8,16\), respectively, the left side minus the right side is \(343\), \(4{,}348{,}695\), \(686{,}321{,}335\), and \(91{,}837{,}005{,}687\), all positive. Every chosen vector in such a block has cost less than six, because \(2(s+1)\log2\le8\log2<6\). Here \(\log2<3/4\) follows, for example, by integrating the strict chord bound for the convex function \(1/t\) on \([1,2]\).

If \(m_s\ge64\), retain all these full blocks and put \(L_s:=6m_s\); otherwise retain no block from this scale and put \(L_s:=0\). Discard the remaining coordinates in either case. The number of discarded coordinates at each of these four scales is less than \(512\). Moreover, whenever the blocks are retained, \((9/8)^{m_s}\ge1+6m_s=1+L_s\). Indeed \(9^6=531441>524288=2\cdot8^6\), so \((9/8)^{64}>(9/8)^{60}>2^{10}>385=1+6\cdot64\). The inequality extends from an integer \(m\ge64\) to \(m+1\), since \((9/8)(1+6m)-(1+6(m+1))=(6m-47)/8>0\).

For \(s\ge4\), take block size \(k_s:=2^{\lceil s/2\rceil+1}\), and partition \(I_s\) into \(m_s\) full blocks of this size and fewer than \(k_s\) remaining coordinates. Because \(k_s2^{-(s+1)}=2^{-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\le1/4\) and \(k_s^2 2^{-(s+1)}\ge2\), every full block satisfies \(Z_I\ge1+2-1/4=11/4\). Also

\[
T_I\le\exp\bigl(k_s2^{-(s+1)}\bigr)\le e^{1/4}<\frac43,
\qquad
\frac{Z_I}{T_I}>\frac{33}{16}>2.
\]

The first inequality uses \(\log(1+x)\le x\). The bound \(e^{1/4}<4/3\) follows by comparing the exponential series with the geometric series. Every chosen vector in such a block has cost at most \(2(s+1)\), since \(\log2<1\).

If \(m_s\ge3s\), retain all these full blocks and put \(L_s:=2m_s(s+1)\); otherwise retain no block from this scale and put \(L_s:=0\). Again discard the remaining coordinates. The number of discarded coordinates at this scale is less than \(3s k_s\). For every retained scale, \(2^{m_s}\ge1+2m_s(s+1)=1+L_s\). To check the starting value \(m=3s\), use \(2^s\ge s+1\) to obtain \(2^{3s}\ge(s+1)^3\ge1+6s(s+1)\) for \(s\ge4\); the difference in the second inequality is \(s(s^2-3s-3)>0\). The bound then extends by induction on \(m\), because \(1+2(m+1)(s+1)\le2(1+2m(s+1))\) for \(m\ge1\).

Let \(R\) be the union of all retained blocks, and let \(D\) be the complementary coordinate set, including the zero parameters. The discarded target product has a uniform bound. At scales \(0\) through \(3\), use fewer than \(512\) discarded coordinates per scale and \(\log(1+\varepsilon_i/2)\le\varepsilon_i/2\le2^{-(s+1)}\). Their total contribution to \(\log T_D\) is at most \(512\sum_{s=0}^3 2^{-(s+1)}=480\). At scales \(s\ge4\), the contribution is at most

\[
\sum_{s=4}^{\infty}3s k_s2^{-(s+1)}
=3\sum_{s=4}^{\infty}s2^{-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}
=3\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}(4t+1)2^{-t}
=\frac{39}{2}.
\]

For the last identity, \(\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}t2^{-t}=3/2\) and \(\sum_{t=2}^{\infty}2^{-t}=1/2\), as follows from the geometric series and its derivative. Zero parameters add nothing. Consequently \(\log T_D\le480+39/2=999/2<500\), and hence \(T_R=T_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}/T_D\ge e^{-999/2}2^{-n}W\).

Finally set \(L:=\sum_{s\ge0}L_s\), which is finite. Every independently selected tuple of vectors from the retained blocks has cost at most \(L\). The block mass inequalities and the amplification inequalities at each retained scale give

\[
B_j^\Delta(L)
\ge\prod_{I\text{ retained}}Z_I
\ge T_R\prod_{s\ge0}(1+L_s)
\ge T_R\left(1+\sum_{s\ge0}L_s\right)
\ge e^{-999/2}2^{-n}W(1+L).
\]

All products and sums here have only finitely many nontrivial terms. The inequality \(\prod_s(1+L_s)\ge1+\sum_s L_s\) holds because every \(L_s\) is nonnegative. If there are no retained blocks, the tuple is the zero vector, the empty product is one, and \(L=0\), so the same argument applies. Thus the claimed bound follows, with the slightly stronger constant \(e^{-999/2}\) also valid.

The construction places \(\varepsilon_i=1\) in scale zero and fixes coordinates with \(\varepsilon_i=0\) at zero, so both endpoint cases have already been included. Finally, the positive-pair set is finite and nonempty. Its finitely many costs include zero; between consecutive costs the numerator \(B_j^\Delta(L)\) is constant and the denominator increases. After the largest cost the ratio decreases as well. Thus its maximum is attained at one of these costs, which justifies the maximum formulation.
