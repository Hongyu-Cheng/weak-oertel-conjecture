## Statement

Let $d\ge1$. Let finitely many positive polytopal fibers $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ be given by shared ambient planes $n_f\cdot x+a_f\cdot z\le b_f$ with $|n_f|=1$, and let $H_j=\{u_j\cdot x+v_j\cdot z\ge b_j\}$ with $|u_j|=1$. Use measurable nonnegative facet ownership weights $\omega_f$ summing to one over owners, and nonnegative cap allocations $\alpha_{jf}$ summing over owners to one on strictly captured old boundary and to zero on strictly missed old boundary. A proper cut gives ordinary section measure $\nu_{jz}$; full, empty and lower-dimensional noncoincident traces give zero. On an inside supporting coincidence let $\sum_f\alpha_{jf}=1-\sigma$ and $\nu_{jz}=\sigma\mathcal H_{d-1}$ on that facet, and on an outside coincidence let $\sum_f\alpha_{jf}=\sigma$ with the same section allocation, where $0\le\sigma\le1$.

For multipliers $\lambda_j\ge0$, let $dW_f=(\omega_f-\sum_j\lambda_j\alpha_{jf})\,d\mathcal H_{d-1}$ over all integer traces of body plane $f$. Assume $\int dW_f=\int z\,dW_f=\int x\,dW_f=0$. Group the positively stressed caps so that each group $i$ has a common full effective-section centroid $(\bar z_i,\bar x_i)$, masses $S_j=\sum_z\nu_{jz}(\mathbb R^d)>0$ and $\lambda_j S_j=\rho_j t_i$ with $\sum_{j\in i}\rho_j(b_j-v_j\cdot\bar z_i)=0$. Put $m=\sum_z\int_{K_z}x\,dx$, $m_j=\sum_z\int_{K_z\cap(H_j)_z}x\,dx$, $B_f=\int z\otimes x\,dW_f$, and $C_j=\sum_z\int(z-\bar z_i)\otimes(x-\bar x_i)\,d\nu_{jz}$. Then
$$
(d+1)\left(m-\sum_j\lambda_jm_j\right)
=-\sum_f a_f\cdot B_f-\sum_j\lambda_jv_j\cdot C_j,
$$
where each contraction is in the base coordinate. For $n=0$ the right side is zero. If also every positively stressed cap has volume fraction $\beta$ and $\sum_j\lambda_j=1/\beta$, then the ordinary body centroid equals the convex combination of its cap centroids with weights $\beta\lambda_j$.

## Proof

Fix $h\in\mathbb R^d$ and use the vector field $x(h\cdot x)$ with divergence $(d+1)(h\cdot x)$. The normal component on an old facet is shared by all its owners. The divergence theorem therefore expresses $(d+1)h\cdot m$ as the sum of its old boundary fluxes with weights $\omega_f$. For cap $j$, write $t_j(z)=b_j-v_j\cdot z$. A proper cut has outward normal $-u_j$, so the flux on its new facet is $-t_j(z)\int (h\cdot x)\,d\nu_{jz}$. The same subtraction from allocated old flux holds on the two coincidence branches. If the body is inside the cap then $n_f=-u_j$, and the $\sigma$ portion removed from old flux is exactly $-t_j(z)\int(h\cdot x)\,d\nu_{jz}$. If the body is outside, $n_f=u_j$, and the allocated old flux is exactly $t_j(z)\int(h\cdot x)\,d\nu_{jz}$, canceled by the section term. Thus the sum of allocated old fluxes for each cap equals $(d+1)h\cdot m_j+\sum_z t_j(z)\int(h\cdot x)\,d\nu_{jz}$, including the zero-volume case.

Subtract the weighted cap identities from the body identity. On body plane $f$, use $n_f\cdot x=b_f-a_f\cdot z$ and $\int x\,dW_f=0$ to express the residual old flux as $-h\cdot\sum_f a_f\cdot B_f$. By the common full centroid, $\sum_z\int z\otimes x\,d\nu_{jz}=S_j\bar z_i\otimes\bar x_i+C_j$, so the remaining section contribution as a vector is $S_j(b_j-v_j\cdot\bar z_i)\bar x_i-v_j\cdot C_j$. The assumed equations cancel its first term in each weighted group. As this holds for every $h$, the stated identity follows. In dimension one the same flux proof uses the endpoint divergence theorem and counting measure on sections. For $n=0$ divide the first-moment equality by the body volume to obtain the asserted centroid combination.
