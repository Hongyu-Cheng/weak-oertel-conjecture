## Statement

For $d\ge2$ let $A,K,R$ be positive-volume fibers of an actual mixed-integer convex body at distinct integer endpoints and their integer midpoint. For one closed cap write $p$ for its middle captured fraction, and $f_A,f_K,f_R$ for the $d$th roots of strict residual volumes. If $f_K<(f_A+f_R)/2$, exactly one endpoint has nonempty strict residual. Calling that endpoint $R$, one has $|R|>2^d(1-p)|K|$. At each fixed $p\in(0,1)$ this factor is sharp as an infimum without a noheavy hypothesis, and $p=0$ cannot give a failure.

If $p\le\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$, then $|R|>|A|+|K|$ and $|R|>\lambda_d |K|$, where $\lambda_d=2^d(1-\gamma_d)>2^{d-1}$. On a finite set of positive fibers choose at most one such low-capture failure edge from each midpoint to its unique surviving endpoint. The underlying undirected graph is a forest and all directed paths of length $h\ge1$ satisfy $\lambda_d^h |C_{\rm start}|<|C_{\rm end}|\le\max_z|C_z|$.

## Proof

The actual fiber midpoint inclusion and the common affine margin give residual midpoint inclusion for two nonempty compact stricter-margin endpoint cuts. Brunn–Minkowski and continuity from below rule out a failure when both endpoint strict residuals are nonempty. If both are empty, failure is also impossible. On a full-dimensional fiber a nonempty strict residual has positive volume, by mixing a strict point with an interior point sufficiently close to it. Thus in a failure there is exactly one surviving endpoint. Its root is at least $f_R>2f_K=2(1-p)^{1/d}|K|^{1/d}$, proving the strict factor. If $p=0$ the full fiber midpoint inclusion already gives $f_A+f_R\le |A|^{1/d}+|R|^{1/d}\le2|K|^{1/d}$.

For $p\le\gamma_d<1/2$, normalize $|K|=1$ and put $b=|R|^{1/d}$ and $a=|A|^{1/d}$. Then $b>2(1-p)^{1/d}\ge2^{1-1/d}\ge\sqrt2>5/4$. Also $a\le2-b$ by Brunn–Minkowski, so $b<2$. The function $x^d-(2-x)^d$ is increasing on $[1,2]$, and its value at $5/4$ is at least $(5/4)^2-(3/4)^2=1$. Therefore $b^d>1+(2-b)^d\ge1+a^d$. The binomial bound $\gamma_d<1/2$ gives the stated uniform factor $\lambda_d$.

To see sharpness of the factor for fixed $p\in(0,1)$, take a volume-one convex body $T$ and use $(2-b)T,T,bT$ at $-1,0,1$ with $2(1-p)^{1/d}<b<2$. The three sections are exact since the midpoint of the endpoints is $T$. The middle cap can have any prescribed fraction by continuity of its volume as a nonzero-normal threshold varies. A sufficiently large tilt fixing that threshold captures the whole smaller-base-side fiber $(2-b)T$ and strictly misses $bT$. Its deficit is positive and $b^d\downarrow 2^d(1-p)$ as $b\downarrow2(1-p)^{1/d}$.

Volume grows by a factor strictly larger than $\lambda_d>1$ on each chosen directed edge, so no directed cycle exists. An undirected cycle would require one outgoing edge at each of its vertices, since it has as many edges as vertices and each vertex has at most one outgoing edge. It would then be a directed cycle, a contradiction. Iteration along a directed path yields the volume bound.
