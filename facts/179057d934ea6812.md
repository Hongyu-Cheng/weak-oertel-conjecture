## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a convex body with $0$ interior and actual cutting-line endpoints $(A,0),(-a,0),(0,B),(0,-b),(\rho,-\rho),(-\tau,\tau)$, all scalar radii positive. Reflect $K\cap\{x\le0,y\le0\}$ by $(u,v)=(-x,-y)$. The reflected sector lies in the nonnegative-quadrant envelope defined by
$$
(\rho-b)u+\rho v\le\rho b,\qquad
\tau u+(\tau-a)v\le\tau a.
$$
This particular envelope is bounded exactly when $D=\rho a+\tau b-ab>0$. In that case its vertices, allowing repetitions, are $0,(a,0),P_*,(0,b)$ with
$$
P_*=\frac1D\big(\rho[ab+\tau(a-b)],\ \tau[ab+\rho(b-a)]\big).
$$
Consequently the sector has area at most
$$
E=\frac{ab(a\tau+b\rho)-\rho\tau(a-b)^2}{2D}.
$$
For the other two unique sectors of $x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le0$, the same formulas use $(a,b,\rho,\tau)=(\rho,A,B,b)$ and $(B,\tau,a,A)$ respectively.

## Proof

Join $(-u,-v)$ in the sector to $(\rho,-\rho)$. Its crossing with $x=0$ has $y=-\rho(u+v)/(\rho+u)$, which is at least $-b$ by actuality of the endpoint. The first inequality follows. Joining instead to $(-\tau,\tau)$ gives the crossing $x=-\tau(u+v)/(\tau+v)$ on $y=0$, at least $-a$, which gives the second inequality. These are actual segment constraints in the same body. Applying them to the actual negative-axis endpoints gives $\rho(a-b)\le ab$ and $\tau(b-a)\le ab$. In particular both axis endpoints of the envelope are feasible and the axis triangle lies in it.

Scale coordinates by $(U,V)=(u/a,v/b)$. The inequalities become $pU+V\le1$ and $U+qV\le1$, with $p=(\rho-b)a/(\rho b)\le1$, $q=(\tau-a)b/(\tau a)\le1$, and $1-pq=D/(\rho\tau)$. If $p,q\ge0$, the two inequalities bound both coordinates. If exactly one is negative, the inequality with both coefficients nonnegative bounds the coordinate with coefficient one, and the other inequality bounds the remaining coordinate. The same reasoning covers a zero coefficient. When $p,q<0$, a nonzero recession direction $(U,V)\ge0$ must have $U,V>0$ and satisfy $1/(-q)\le V/U\le -p$. Such a direction exists exactly when $pq\ge1$. If $p,q$ are both nonnegative then $pq=1$ could only occur at $p=q=1$, excluded because their equality conditions would require $a>b$ and $b>a$ simultaneously. If this closed polyhedron were unbounded, normalizing an unbounded sequence in it would produce a nonzero nonnegative recession direction. These cases prove the boundedness criterion, including equality at $D=0$.

When $D>0$, Cramer's rule gives the displayed intersection of the two lines and both coordinates are nonnegative by the endpoint inequalities. Each coordinate axis has exactly its given intercept as the maximal feasible point, and the line intersection is the only possible additional vertex. The area is $(a(P_*)_y+b(P_*)_x)/2$, giving the stated fraction. The changes of frame $(x,y)\mapsto(y,-x-y)$ and $(x,y)\mapsto(-x-y,x)$ both have determinant one and give the two stated parameter substitutions.

As an immediate consequence, if each cap area is at most $c|K|$ with $c<1/2$, then each unique area is at least $(1-2c)|K|$ by taking the complement of the union of the other two caps. Hence any finite envelope $E_i$ gives $|K|\le E_i/(1-2c)$, which is $279 E_i/23$ at $c=128/279$. In this strict half-cap case the bridge triangle of [1c59eb04985632f3](../facts/1c59eb04985632f3.md) gives another way to see that all three envelopes are bounded. For the negative quadrant its third bridge is $x/A+y/B\le1$, automatically valid there, so the other two bridges give exactly this envelope. The other sectors follow by cycling coordinates.
