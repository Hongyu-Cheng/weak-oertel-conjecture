## Statement

For every integer \(n\ge2\), set \(d_n=n^4\) and \(L_n=d_n^2\). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_n&=[-L_n,L_n]^n,\qquad B_{d_n}=[-1,1]^{d_n},\\
r_n(x)&=1-\frac{\|x\|_\infty}{L_n}\quad(x\in P_n),\\
C_n&=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n}:\|x\|_\infty/L_n+\|y\|_\infty\le1\},\\
S_n&=C_n\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^{d_n}),\qquad
w_{n,z}=\mathcal H_{d_n}(\{y:(z,y)\in C_n\})=2^{d_n}r_n(z)^{d_n},\\
W_n&=\mathcal H_{d_n}(S_n)=\sum_{z\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n}w_{n,z}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(C_n\) is a convex body and its actual fiber at \(x\in P_n\) is \(r_n(x)B_{d_n}\). Its integer peak is \(R_{\mathrm{peak},n}=\max_{z\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n}r_n(z)=1\). The symbol \(L_n\) is the base cube radius, distinct from the peak: in accepted fact b295de5f23afdbe8, the symbols \(P,r,d,R,W\) in the proposed all-dimensional extension correspond respectively to \(P_n,r_n,d_n,R_{\mathrm{peak},n},W_n/2^{d_n}\). The constant factor \(2^{d_n}=\operatorname{vol}_{d_n}(B_{d_n})\) converts its profile weights to the actual original fiber volumes and changes none of the fractions below.

Put \(\delta_n=1/(10000d_n)\) and \(T_n=\{z\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n:r_n(z)\ge(1-\delta_n)R_{\mathrm{peak},n}\}\). Then
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in T_n}w_{n,z}}{W_n}\le9\,16^{-n}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every fixed integer \(\omega\ge0\), define the maximum original slab-mass fraction
\[
\sigma_n(\omega)=\sup_{\substack{u\in\mathbb Z^n\setminus\{0\}\text{ primitive}\\m\in\mathbb Z}}
\frac{\sum_{\substack{z\in P_n\cap\mathbb Z^n\\m\le u\cdot z\le m+\omega}}w_{n,z}}{W_n}.
\]
All displayed boundaries are included. The bound is uniform in the slab direction and location:
\[
\sigma_n(\omega)\le\left(\frac{14}{n}\right)^n+\frac{\omega+1}{2n^4+1}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular both fractions tend to zero as \(n\to\infty\), for every fixed \(\omega\). Equivalently, for every \(c>0\), every \(\eta>0\), and every integer \(\omega\ge0\), there exist integers \(n,d\ge1\) and an actual convex body \(C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\) of this form such that its original top-radius core has mass less than \(c\mathcal H_d(S)\), and every primitive integer width-\(\omega\) slab has original mass less than \(\eta\mathcal H_d(S)\). Consequently no positive \(n\)-independent constants \(c,\eta\) and fixed integer width \(\omega\) give the proposed either/or alternative for all \(n,d,C\).

Finally, with the closed-halfspace depth of the locked problem, the legal point \(\mathbf y_n^\star=(0_n,0_{d_n})\in S_n\) satisfies
\[
h_{S_n}(\mathbf y_n^\star)=W_n/2.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

Fix \(n\ge2\), and abbreviate \(d=d_n=n^4\), \(L=L_n=d^2\), and \(A=W_n/2^d\). These aliases do not change the original weights. The function \(\|x\|_\infty/L+\|y\|_\infty\) is convex and continuous, so \(C_n\) is closed and convex. Its defining inequality bounds both components, making it compact. A neighborhood of the origin satisfies the strict inequality, so it has nonempty interior. Solving the defining inequality at a fixed \(x\) gives the stated fiber \(r_n(x)[-1,1]^d\) on \(P_n\), and no fiber off \(P_n\). The function \(r_n\) is nonnegative and concave on \(P_n\), with peak one at the integer point zero. Its fiber at zero has volume \(2^d\), so \(W_n>0\).

All the discrete counts can be given exactly. For an integer \(0\le k\le L\), the cube \(\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\|z\|_\infty\le k\}\) has \((2k+1)^n\) points. The shell at \(k=0\) has one point, and the shell at every \(1\le k\le L\) has \((2k+1)^n-(2k-1)^n\) points. Hence the original normalized total is the exact finite sum
\[
A=1+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\bigl((2k+1)^n-(2k-1)^n\bigr)(1-k/L)^d.
\tag{4}
\]
The term at \(k=L\) is zero, consistently with the boundary fiber of zero volume.

We will use the elementary inequality \((1-s)^d\ge e^{-2ds}\) for \(0\le s\le1/2\). Indeed,
\(-\log(1-s)=\int_0^s(1-v)^{-1}\,dv\le2s\). Also \(e<3\): the exponential series gives
\(e=1+1+\sum_{j\ge2}1/j!<1+1+\sum_{j\ge2}2^{-(j-1)}=3\), since \(j!\ge2^{j-1}\) for \(j\ge2\) with a strict inequality for \(j\ge3\). No numerical approximation is used below.

The top core is exactly the integer cube of radius \(\lfloor d/10000\rfloor\), because its defining inequality is \(\|z\|_\infty\le\delta_nL=d/10000\). Every normalized fiber weight is at most one. On the other hand, every point in the integer cube of radius \(d\) has normalized weight at least \((1-1/d)^d\). Thus
\[
\frac{\sum_{z\in T_n}w_{n,z}}{W_n}
\le\frac{(2\lfloor d/10000\rfloor+1)^n}{(2d+1)^n(1-1/d)^d}
\le e^2\left(\frac{2d/10000+1}{2d+1}\right)^n.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(d=n^4\ge16\),
\[
\frac{2d/10000+1}{2d+1}
\le\frac1{10000}+\frac1{2d}
\le\frac1{10000}+\frac1{32}
<\frac1{16}.
\]
Using \(e^2<9\) in (5) proves (1).

To bound every slab simultaneously, use the exact layer-cake representation rather than replacing the discrete measure by a continuous limit. For \(0\le t\le1\), the superlevel \(\{x\in P_n:r_n(x)\ge1-t\}\) is the actual cube of radius \(Lt\), whose integer-point count is
\(N(t)=(2\lfloor Lt\rfloor+1)^n\). For any nonzero \(u\in\mathbb Z^n\) and any integer \(m\), let \(N_{u,m}(t)\) be the number of those points also satisfying \(m\le u\cdot z\le m+\omega\), and let \(A_{u,m}\) be the original mass in this slab divided by \(2^d\). For each original point,
\(r_n(z)^d=d\int_{1-r_n(z)}^1(1-t)^{d-1}\,dt\). Summing finitely many such identities gives
\[
A=d\int_0^1(1-t)^{d-1}N(t)\,dt,\qquad
A_{u,m}=d\int_0^1(1-t)^{d-1}N_{u,m}(t)\,dt.
\tag{6}
\]
Non-strict superlevels and slab boundaries are retained throughout; a single integration endpoint contributes no extra mass.

For each integer cube radius \(k\), choose any coordinate \(i\) with \(u_i\ne0\). Fixing the other \(n-1\) integer coordinates leaves \(2k+1\) possible values of each fixed coordinate and restricts \(z_i\) to a closed real interval of length \(\omega/|u_i|\). Such an interval contains at most \(\lfloor\omega/|u_i|\rfloor+1\le\omega+1\) integers. Therefore
\[
\#\{z\in[-k,k]^n\cap\mathbb Z^n:m\le u\cdot z\le m+\omega\}
\le(\omega+1)(2k+1)^{n-1}.
\tag{7}
\]
This estimate holds for every nonzero integer direction, so in particular uniformly for every primitive direction.

Split the second integral in (6) at \(t=1/d\). On \([0,1/d]\), use \(N_{u,m}(t)\le N(t)\le(2d+1)^n\). Since \(d\int_0^{1/d}(1-t)^{d-1}\,dt\le1\), the resulting early contribution is at most \((2d+1)^n\). On \([1/d,1]\), the integer radius \(k=\lfloor Lt\rfloor\) is at least \(d\), so (7) gives
\(N_{u,m}(t)\le(\omega+1)N(t)/(2d+1)\). Consequently
\[
\frac{A_{u,m}}A
\le\frac{(2d+1)^n}{A}+\frac{\omega+1}{2d+1}.
\tag{8}
\]

For the remaining early-contribution ratio, the integer cube of radius \(nd\) is contained in \(P_n\), since \(n\le d\). Its normalized weights are all at least \((1-n/d)^d\), giving
\(A\ge(2nd+1)^n(1-n/d)^d\). Here \(n/d=1/n^3\le1/8\), so the elementary logarithm inequality above yields \((1-n/d)^d\ge e^{-2n}\). Using \(2d+1\le3d\) and \(2nd+1\ge2nd\), we obtain
\[
\frac{(2d+1)^n}{A}
\le e^{2n}\left(\frac{2d+1}{2nd+1}\right)^n
\le\left(\frac{3e^2}{2n}\right)^n
<\left(\frac{27}{2n}\right)^n
\le\left(\frac{14}{n}\right)^n.
\tag{9}
\]
Substitution in (8) proves (2) for every direction and endpoint before taking the supremum. The factors \(2^d\) cancel exactly, so this bound is for the original fiber volumes, not an altered measure. For \(n\ge28\), \((14/n)^n\le2^{-n}\), while \((\omega+1)/(2n^4+1)\to0\) for each fixed \(\omega\). Together with (1), this proves the stated vanishing and the quantifier-correct failure of every choice of fixed \(c,\eta,\omega\). Accepted fact b295de5f23afdbe8 applies when the base dimension is two; the counterexample here concerns only extending its constants to all base dimensions, with the explicit normalization map given in the statement.

Finally, reflection \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)\) preserves \(C_n\), its mixed-integer support, and its original mixed-integer measure. A proper closed halfspace \(H\) containing the origin can be written \(H=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\ge b\}\), with nonzero normal and \(b\le0\). It contains the centered halfspace \(H_0=\{(x,y):\alpha\cdot x+\beta\cdot y\ge0\}\). The reflection of \(H_0\) is its opposite centered closed halfspace, has the same original mass, and together these two halfspaces cover \(S_n\). Thus each has mass at least \(W_n/2\), and so does \(H\). This proves \(h_{S_n}(0_n,0_d)\ge W_n/2\), including normals whose boundary contains entire integer fibers. For the reverse bound choose the particular halfspace \(\{(x,y):y_1\ge0\}\). Every positive-volume fiber is a symmetric \(d\)-cube, whose boundary \(y_1=0\) has \(d\)-dimensional measure zero and whose intersection with this halfspace has exactly half its volume. Zero-volume boundary fibers also obey the same equality. Summing gives exactly \(W_n/2\), which proves (3). The origin is a legal point because it lies in the full-dimensional integer fiber at zero. Hence these bodies satisfy a substantially stronger actual-depth conclusion than the locked target requires.
