## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let
\(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, put
\[
P:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}C,
\qquad
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
\]
and, for \((z,x)\in C\), define
\[
\delta_C(z,x):=
\inf\left\{
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J):
J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a proper closed halfspace and }x\in J
\right\}.
\]
Set
\[
g(z):=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}
\quad(z\in P),
\qquad
B:=\{(z,u):z\in P,\ 0\leq u\leq g(z)\}.
\]
On \(B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), define the finite measure
\(\eta\) by
\[
\eta(E):=
\sum_{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}
\int_0^{g(z)}
\mathbf 1_E(z,u)\,d\,u^{d-1}\,du
\]
for every Borel set \(E\subseteq B\).

For a closed halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\), write
\[
H_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in H\}
\]
and define
\[
\mathcal A_H:=
\left\{
(z,u)\in B:
\text{there is }x\in C_z\cap H_z
\text{ with }\delta_C(z,x)\geq u^d
\right\}.
\]
For \(z\notin P\), adopt the harmless convention
\[
\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x):=0.
\]
Then:

1. The sets \(B\) and \(\mathcal A_H\) are compact and convex, and
\[
|\eta|
=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)
\geq
\gamma_d
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]

2. Every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) satisfies
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\leq
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
This includes vertical halfspaces and the whole space.

3. For \(q\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)\), define
\[
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q):=
\inf\left\{
\eta(\mathcal A_H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }
q\in\mathcal A_H
\right\}.
\]
Let \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\).  If \(q=(m,u)\) and
\(x\in C_m\) satisfies \(\delta_C(m,x)\geq u^d\), then
\[
h_S((m,x))\geq D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q).
\]

No unconditional lower bound on
\(\sup_{q\in B\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R)}D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q)\)
is asserted.

## Proof

Allowing the whole space among the fiber halfspaces in the definition of
\(\delta_C\) would not change its value: the whole-space cap has total
fiber volume, whereas every proper cap is contained in the fiber.  Thus
accepted fact `4e868f774de77608` applies to the present definition.  In
particular,
\[
f(z,x):=\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}
\]
is jointly concave and upper semicontinuous on \(C\), each fiber maximum
defining \(g\) is attained, and \(g\) is concave and upper semicontinuous
on the compact convex set \(P\).

First consider \(B\).  If \((z_i,u_i)\in B\) for \(i=0,1\) and
\(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), concavity of \(g\) gives
\[
(1-\lambda)u_0+\lambda u_1
\leq
(1-\lambda)g(z_0)+\lambda g(z_1)
\leq
g((1-\lambda)z_0+\lambda z_1).
\]
The convex combination also has nonnegative last coordinate, so \(B\) is
convex.  It is bounded because \(P\) is compact and \(g\) is bounded.
If \((z_k,u_k)\in B\) converges to \((z,u)\), then \(z\in P\),
\(u\geq0\), and upper semicontinuity gives
\[
u\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}g(z_k)\leq g(z).
\]
Thus \(B\) is closed and hence compact.

Define the lifted hypograph
\[
\mathcal L:=
\left\{
(z,x,u)\in\mathbb R^{n+d+1}:
(z,x)\in C,\ 0\leq u\leq f(z,x)
\right\}.
\]
Joint concavity of \(f\) makes \(\mathcal L\) convex.  It is closed:
if \((z_k,x_k,u_k)\in\mathcal L\) converges to \((z,x,u)\), then
\((z,x)\in C\), \(u\geq0\), and upper semicontinuity gives
\[
u\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}f(z_k,x_k)\leq f(z,x).
\]
It is bounded because \(C\) is compact and \(f\) is bounded, so
\(\mathcal L\) is compact.  If
\[
\widehat H:=\{(z,x,u):(z,x)\in H\},
\]
then direct unwinding of the definitions gives
\[
\mathcal A_H
=
\operatorname{proj}_{z,u}\bigl(\mathcal L\cap\widehat H\bigr).
\]
The intersection is compact and convex, and a linear projection preserves
convexity and sends compact sets to compact sets.  Therefore every
\(\mathcal A_H\) is compact and convex.  This proof also covers the whole
space and any vertical halfspace.

Since \(P\) is compact, \(P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) is finite.  For each such
integer \(z\),
\[
\int_0^{g(z)}d\,u^{d-1}\,du
=g(z)^d
=\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x).
\]
It follows that \(\eta\) is finite and
\[
|\eta|
=
\sum_{z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n}
\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)
=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x),
\]
where the last equality uses the stated zero convention off \(P\).

Write \(w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\).  If \(w_z=0\), then
\(\max_x\delta_C(z,x)=0\) by accepted fact `4e868f774de77608`, so the
desired lower bound for that fiber is automatic.  If \(w_z>0\), the
compact convex fiber \(C_z\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body.  Let
\(c_z\) be its centroid.  Grünbaum's centroid halfspace theorem gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap J)\geq\gamma_dw_z
\]
for every proper closed halfspace \(J\) containing \(c_z\).  Therefore
\[
\max_{x\in C_z}\delta_C(z,x)
\geq\delta_C(z,c_z)
\geq\gamma_dw_z.
\]
Summing this inequality over the finitely many integer fibers proves the
mass lower bound in assertion 1, including all zero-volume and
lower-dimensional fibers.

We now prove assertion 2 fiber by fiber.  Fix
\(z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\) and put
\[
D_z:=C_z\cap H_z.
\]
The slice \(H_z\) is either empty, all of \(\mathbb R^d\), or a proper
closed halfspace.  If \(D_z=\varnothing\), then
\(\mathcal A_H\cap(\{z\}\times\mathbb R)=\varnothing\), so its
\(\eta\)-mass is zero.

Suppose that \(D_z\neq\varnothing\).  It is compact.  Because
\(\delta_C=f^d\) is upper semicontinuous, it attains on \(D_z\) the
maximum
\[
T_z:=\max_{x\in D_z}\delta_C(z,x).
\]
The set of hit levels in this integer fiber is exactly
\[
\left\{
u\in[0,g(z)]:
\text{there is }x\in D_z\text{ with }\delta_C(z,x)\geq u^d
\right\}
=
[0,T_z^{1/d}].
\]
The inclusion from right to left uses a maximizer of \(T_z\); the reverse
inclusion follows directly from the definition of \(T_z\).  This remains
true when \(T_z=0\), in which case the interval is the singleton
\(\{0\}\).  Consequently, the exact hit mass in this fiber is
\[
\int_0^{T_z^{1/d}}d\,u^{d-1}\,du=T_z.
\]

For every \(x\in D_z\), if \(H_z\) is a proper closed halfspace, it is one
of the competitors in the definition of \(\delta_C(z,x)\), and hence
\[
\delta_C(z,x)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
If \(H_z=\mathbb R^d\), the same inequality follows from
\(\delta_C(z,x)\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\).  Taking the maximum over
\(x\in D_z\) gives in both cases
\[
T_z\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
Thus, also in the empty case,
\[
\int_0^{g(z)}
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal A_H}(z,u)\,d\,u^{d-1}\,du
\leq
\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
Summing this literal fiberwise inequality over
\(z\in P\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and observing that both sides vanish for
integer \(z\notin P\), proves
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\leq
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z).
\]
No boundary mass was discarded: all caps and hit ranges used above are
closed, and the maximum \(T_z\) is attained.

Finally, let \(q=(m,u)\) and \(x\in C_m\) satisfy
\(\delta_C(m,x)\geq u^d\).  For every closed ambient halfspace \(H\)
containing \((m,x)\), one has \(x\in C_m\cap H_m\), so this same \(x\)
witnesses \(q\in\mathcal A_H\).  By the definition of the infimum
\(D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q)\),
\[
\eta(\mathcal A_H)\geq D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q).
\]
Assertion 2, followed by the definition of mixed-integer volume, gives
\[
\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)
=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H_z)
\geq
\eta(\mathcal A_H)
\geq
D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q).
\]
This holds for every closed halfspace containing \((m,x)\), including the
whole space.  Taking the infimum over precisely those halfspaces yields
\[
h_S((m,x))\geq D_{\mathrm{hit}}(q),
\]
which proves assertion 3 and checks both infimum directions.

## External sources

Branko Grünbaum, "Partitions of Mass-Distributions and of Convex Bodies by Hyperplanes," Pacific Journal of Mathematics 10(4) (1960), 1257-1261, Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 3, pages 1259-1260, https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1960.10.1257, primary-source PDF https://msp.org/pjm/1960/10-4/pjm-v10-n4-p18-p.pdf.  Exact form used: if \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body with centroid \(c(K)\), then every closed halfspace \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) containing \(c(K)\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{vol}_d(K\cap J)
\geq
\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(K).
\]
Applicability: the theorem is invoked only for fibers \(C_z\) with positive \(d\)-volume; each such fiber is a \(d\)-dimensional convex body, \(c_z\) is its centroid, and every competitor in the definition of \(\delta_C(z,c_z)\) is a proper closed halfspace containing that centroid.  Fibers of zero \(d\)-volume are handled separately without invoking the theorem.
