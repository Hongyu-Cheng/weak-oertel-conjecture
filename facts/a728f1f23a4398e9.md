## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write

\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),
 \qquad
 W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\tag{1}
\]

Put

\[
 S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]

and, for \(p\in S\), define

\[
 h_S(p):=
 \inf\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\},
 \qquad
 h_{\max}(C):=\sup_{p\in S}h_S(p).
\tag{2}
\]

Define the absolute constant

\[
 c_{\mathrm{sp}}
 :=\frac{33}{5120e\,2^{312}313^{311}}.
\tag{3}
\]

Assume \(S\ne\varnothing\), \(W>0\), and that \(C\) is a counterexample at the least positive base dimension \(n\) for this fixed constant, in the exact sense used by accepted fact 1e3269a0e9b5695a: for every \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\), every \(e'\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and every convex body \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R^{e'}\) with nonempty mixed-integer section and positive total mixed mass \(W_D\), some mixed-integer point of \(D\) has halfspace depth at least \(c_{\mathrm{sp}}2^{-k}W_D\), whereas

\[
 h_S(p)<c_{\mathrm{sp}}2^{-n}W
 \qquad(p\in S).
\tag{4}
\]

Retain all canonical hard-branch hypotheses, objects, and normalizations of accepted facts d06d919de3083960 and 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b. In particular, let \(Q>0\) be the canonical half-strength product-reservoir mass. The hard ledger gives

\[
 W<\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{5}
\]

Let \(A_\star\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be the finite canonical stopped set, with weights \(w_y>0\), and put

\[
 W_\star:=\sum_{y\in A_\star}w_y,
 \qquad
 \rho:=\dim\operatorname{aff}(A_\star)\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]

The stopped-set conclusions retained from accepted fact d06d919de3083960, together with the stopped-fiber normalization retained in accepted fact 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b, are

\[
 4w_y\le a_y
 \qquad(y\in A_\star),
\tag{7}
\]

\[
 2^{n-\rho}W_\star>\frac{11}{320}Q,
\tag{8}
\]

and

\[
 B\subseteq A_\star,
 \quad
 \dim\operatorname{aff}(B)<\rho
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{y\in B}w_y<\frac12W_\star,
\tag{9}
\]

where the empty set has weight zero and affine dimension \(-1\).

Assume that \(A_\star\) lies in one parity class: for some \(\xi\in\{0,1\}^n\),

\[
 A_\star\subseteq\xi+2\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{10}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\rho\le311,}
\tag{11}
\]

and the fixed-rank conclusion of accepted fact 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b gives

\[
 \boxed{
 h_{\max}(C)>
 c_{\mathrm{sp}}2^{-n}W.}
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently, (4) and (10) cannot hold simultaneously. Thus no least-base-dimension counterexample satisfying the retained canonical hard-branch hypotheses can have its canonical stopped set contained in one parity class.

## Proof

First note from (3) that

\[
 0<c_{\mathrm{sp}}<\frac1e,
\tag{13}
\]

because \(33<5120\,2^{312}313^{311}\). Hence the fixed constant in the least-base-dimension hypothesis lies in the range required by accepted fact 1e3269a0e9b5695a.

Let

\[
 L:=\operatorname{aff}(A_\star),
 \qquad
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z.
\tag{14}
\]

Conditions (6)-(10) are exactly the hypotheses of accepted fact 75798cede16b89c5 with \(A=A_\star\), \(W_A=W_\star\), and affine rank \(\rho\). Therefore every off-diagonal midpoint of two points of \(A_\star\) is integral and belongs to \(L\), and that accepted fact gives the strict amplification

\[
 W_L>\rho W_\star.
\tag{15}
\]

We next put (14) into the primitive-lattice form required by accepted fact 1e3269a0e9b5695a. Choose \(y_0\in A_\star\) and set

\[
 \Gamma:=(L-y_0)\cap\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{16}
\]

The differences from \(y_0\) of an affine basis of \(A_\star\) are \(\rho\) real-linearly independent integer vectors spanning \(L-y_0\). Hence \(\Gamma\) has rank \(\rho\) and

\[
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Gamma=L-y_0.
\tag{17}
\]

By its definition in (16),

\[
 \Gamma=(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Gamma)\cap\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{18}
\]

so \(\Gamma\) is saturated and \(L\) is a primitive affine lattice subspace of rank \(\rho\).

If \(\rho<n\), the least-base-dimension hypothesis and (13) allow us to apply accepted fact 1e3269a0e9b5695a to \(L\), giving

\[
 W_L<2^{\rho-n}W.
\tag{19}
\]

If \(\rho=n\), then \(L=\mathbb R^n\), so instead

\[
 W_L=W=2^{\rho-n}W.
\tag{20}
\]

Combining either (19) or (20) with the strict inequality (15) yields, in both cases,

\[
 \rho\,2^{n-\rho}W_\star<W.
\tag{21}
\]

Since \(\rho\ge1\), multiplying (8) by \(\rho\) and using (21) and (5) gives

\[
 \frac{11}{320}\rho Q
 <\rho\,2^{n-\rho}W_\star
 <W
 <\frac{803}{75}Q.
\tag{22}
\]

All quantities divided by below are positive. Cancelling \(Q\) in (22),

\[
 \rho
 <\frac{320}{11}\frac{803}{75}
 =\frac{256960}{825}
 <312,
\tag{23}
\]

where the last inequality is the exact integer comparison

\[
 256960<312\cdot825=257400.
\]

The affine rank \(\rho\) is an integer, so (23) proves (11).

It remains to compute the fixed-rank constant exactly. For every integer \(R_0\ge3\), accepted fact 6bbc2d8b43ec6b3b defines

\[
 c_{R_0}:=
 \max\left\{
 \frac1{eR_0 2^{R_0}(R_0+1)^{R_0}},
 \frac1{e2^{R_0+1}(R_0+2)^{R_0}}
 \right\}
\tag{24}
\]

and proves

\[
 \rho\le R_0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 h_{\max}(C)>
 \frac{33c_{R_0}}{5120}\,2^{-n}W.
\tag{25}
\]

At \(R_0=311\), the ratio of the first entry in the maximum (24) to the second is

\[
 \frac2{311}\left(\frac{313}{312}\right)^{311}.
\tag{26}
\]

The elementary inequality \(\log(1+x)<x\) for \(x>0\) gives

\[
 \left(\frac{313}{312}\right)^{311}
 =\left(1+\frac1{312}\right)^{311}
 <e^{311/312}<e.
\tag{27}
\]

Also \(e<3\): from \(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\), with strict inequality for some \(k\),

\[
 e=1+1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{2^{k-1}}=3.
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (26)-(28) show that the ratio in (26) is strictly less than \(6/311<1\). Thus the second entry in (24) is the larger one and

\[
 c_{311}=\frac1{e\,2^{312}313^{311}}.
\tag{29}
\]

Applying (25) with \(R_0=311\), using (11) and then (29), gives

\[
 h_{\max}(C)
 >\frac{33}{5120e\,2^{312}313^{311}}\,2^{-n}W
 =c_{\mathrm{sp}}2^{-n}W,
\]

which is (12).

Finally, (4) implies \(h_{\max}(C)\le c_{\mathrm{sp}}2^{-n}W\), whereas (12) is strict in the opposite direction. This contradiction excludes (10) only within the stated least-base-dimension canonical hard-branch scope, as claimed.
