## Statement

All logarithms are natural. Use the actual closed-halfspace depth and maximum-entropy row conventions of accepted fact bf1ff94487296374. Its exact aliases here are \(A=D_m\), \(p=p_m\), and \(\mathbf j=\mathbf j_m=\mathbf0_n\); its ambient dimension variable \(m\) is replaced by the present \(n=m+1\). Thus \(r_{D_m,\mathbf j_m}\) means its maximum-entropy probability law on the support \(E=D_m\) with mean \(\mathbf j_m\). The objects \(\mathcal P_m,D_m,p_m\) below are a new plateau family.

For every integer \(h\ge3\), put \(m=2h\), \(n=m+1\), \(B_m=10^m\), \(A_m=2B_m+1\), and \(s_m=(3/5)^h\). Write points as \((t,\mathbf y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^m\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_m
&=\operatorname{conv}\left(
 [-3,-1]\times[-2,2]^m\ \cup\ \{(A_m,\mathbf0_m)\}\right),\\
D_m
&=\mathcal P_m\cap\mathbb Z^n\\
&=\left(\{-3,-2,-1\}\times\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^m\right)
 \cup\left(\{0,\ldots,B_m\}\times\{-1,0,1\}^m\right)\\
&\qquad\cup
 \left(\{B_m+1,\ldots,A_m\}\times\{\mathbf0_m\}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The scalar \(A_m\) is the height of the new apex, not the predecessor's finite support \(A\).

There is a unique positive real solution \(q_m\) to the exact equation
\[
3^m\sum_{t=1}^{B_m}tq_m^t+
 \sum_{t=B_m+1}^{A_m}tq_m^t
=5^m(3q_m^{-3}+2q_m^{-2}+q_m^{-1}),
\qquad
1-s_m<q_m<1-\frac{s_m}{4}.
\]
Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_m
&=5^m(q_m^{-3}+q_m^{-2}+q_m^{-1})
  +3^m\sum_{t=0}^{B_m}q_m^t
  +\sum_{t=B_m+1}^{A_m}q_m^t,\\
p_m(t,\mathbf y)&=\frac{q_m^t}{Z_m}
 \quad ((t,\mathbf y)\in D_m).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is strictly positive and globally log-affine on the complete support \(D_m=\operatorname{conv}(D_m)\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Its difference lattice is \(\mathbb Z^n\), its rank is \(n\), and its full vector mean is exactly the supported interior integer point \(\mathbf j_m=\mathbf0_n\). It satisfies \(r_{D_m,\mathbf j_m}=p_m\) and \(\mathsf C_{p_m}(\mathbf j_m)=1\).

For every real affine flat \(F\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) of codimension \(k\), including every orientation,
\[
p_m(F)\le2^{-k}.
\]
For the proper closed halfspace \(H_m=\{(t,\mathbf y):t\ge0\}\), which contains the mean, let \(\delta_m=p_m(H_m)\). Then
\[
\tau_{p_m}(\mathbf j_m)
\le\delta_m
\le\frac43s_m(1+3^{-m})
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Also \(H(p_m)-n\log2\ge m\log(5/2)+\log(3/2)\to\infty\), and, for \((T,\mathbf Y)\sim p_m\),
\[
\kappa_m:=\frac{\mathbb E_{p_m}T^4}
 {(\mathbb E_{p_m}T^2)^2}
\ge\frac1{4\delta_m}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently no positive absolute \(\delta\) can bound every mean closed cap below on the class specified in the subgoal.

## Proof

The exact sections of \(\mathcal P_m\) are boxes of radius
\[
R_m(t)=
\begin{cases}
2,&-3\le t\le-1,\\
2(A_m-t)/(A_m+1),&-1\le t\le A_m.
\end{cases}
\]
To verify this, the set given by \(-3\le t\le A_m\), \(|y_i|\le2\), and \((A_m+1)|y_i|\le2(A_m-t)\) for every \(i\) is convex, since these are linear inequalities after replacing each absolute value by its two signs. It contains the plateau cylinder and the apex. Conversely, for \(-3\le t\le-1\) its section is in the cylinder, while for \(-1\le t<A_m\) every point is a convex combination of the apex and a point of \(\{-1\}\times[-2,2]^m\), with apex coefficient \((t+1)/(A_m+1)\). At \(t=A_m\) its section is the apex. This proves both the convex-hull formula and the section formula.

At integral heights \(-3,-2,-1\), the transverse grid is exactly \(\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^m\). At \(0\le t\le B_m\), the radius is less than two and at least one; at \(t=B_m\) it equals one because \(A_m=2B_m+1\). At \(B_m+1\le t\le A_m\), the radius is less than one and nonnegative. Thus the displayed three parts exhaust all integer points, with \(5^m\), \(3^m\), and one point per height, respectively. All vertices of the plateau cylinder and the apex are integral and belong to \(D_m\). Therefore \(\operatorname{conv}(D_m)=\mathcal P_m\), proving completeness. The support contains the origin and all \(n\) standard basis vectors, so its difference lattice is exactly \(\mathbb Z^n\) and its rank is \(n\). At the origin all the displayed polytope inequalities are strict. It is consequently interior, as well as integral and supported.

Define, for \(q>0\),
\[
G_m(q)=3^m\sum_{t=1}^{B_m}tq^t+
 \sum_{t=B_m+1}^{A_m}tq^t
 -5^m(3q^{-3}+2q^{-2}+q^{-1}).
\]
Equivalently, \(G_m(q)\) is the sum of \(tq^t\) over all support points. Its derivative is the sum of \(t^2q^{t-1}\) over those points, and is strictly positive. Also \(G_m(q)\to-\infty\) as \(q\downarrow0\) and \(G_m(q)\to+\infty\) as \(q\to+\infty\). Continuity and strict increase therefore give a unique positive root.

We certify the two bounds on this root. First \(0<s_m\le27/125<1/4\). At \(q=1-s_m\), the infinite geometric first-moment identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_m(1-s_m)
&\le \frac{3^m+1}{s_m^2}-6\cdot5^m\\
&=5^m(1+3^{-m}-6)<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here each positive sum was bounded by its infinite sum \(\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}tq^t=q/(1-q)^2\le1/s_m^2\), and each negative-height factor \(q^{-j}\) is at least one.

For the upper bound put \(q_+=1-s_m/4\), \(d_m=s_m/4\), and \(L_m=B_md_m=60^h/4\). These are local scalars for the root estimate. They satisfy \(q_+>15/16\), \(q_+>3/4\), and \(L_m\ge16\). The negative magnitude is at most \(12\cdot5^m\), because
\[
3q_+^{-3}+2q_+^{-2}+q_+^{-1}
\le3(4/3)^3+2(4/3)^2+4/3=12.
\]
The full infinite middle first moment is \(3^m q_+/d_m^2=16\cdot5^m q_+>15\cdot5^m\). Its omitted tail has the exact form
\[
\begin{aligned}
3^m\sum_{t=B_m+1}^{\infty}tq_+^t
&=3^m q_+^{B_m+1}
 \left(\frac{B_m+1}{d_m}+\frac{q_+}{d_m^2}\right)\\
&=16\cdot5^m q_+^{B_m+1}(L_m+1)\\
&\le16\cdot5^m e^{-L_m}(L_m+1)
 <5^m.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the last strict inequality, \(e^{L_m}\ge L_m^3/6\), \(L_m+1\le17L_m/16\), and \(L_m\ge16\) give
\(16e^{-L_m}(L_m+1)\le102/L_m^2\le102/256<1\).
The positive singleton part may be omitted in a lower bound on \(G_m(q_+)\). Thus \(G_m(q_+)>14\cdot5^m-12\cdot5^m>0\). Strict increase proves \(1-s_m<q_m<1-s_m/4\). In particular \(3/4<q_m<1\), so \(\log q_m\) is a finite real tilt.

The exact support counts now give the stated normalizer \(Z_m\). Conditional on any height, the transverse coordinates have a uniform product law on the corresponding symmetric grid, or are identically zero. Every transverse conditional mean is zero. The root equation is precisely the zero-height-mean numerator, so \(\mathbb E T=G_m(q_m)/Z_m=0\). Hence the full mean is \(\mathbf j_m\). Positivity holds on every support point, and \(\log p_m(t,\mathbf y)=t\log q_m-\log Z_m\) is one affine function on the entire support.

For completeness, the entropy-row identification follows without assuming that the balancing tilt is an entropy optimizer. For any probability law \(\mu\) on \(D_m\) of mean \(\mathbf j_m\), one has \(\mathbb E_\mu T=0\), and therefore
\[
D(\mu\|p_m)=\log Z_m-H(\mu),
\qquad H(p_m)=\log Z_m.
\]
Relative entropy is nonnegative and equals zero only when \(\mu=p_m\); this follows by summing \(z\log z\ge z-1\) against the positive weights \(p_m\), with \(z=\mu/p_m\) and the continuous value at zero. Thus \(p_m\) is the unique maximum-entropy law at this mean, proving \(r_{D_m,\mathbf j_m}=p_m\) in the convention of bf1ff94487296374. Its entropy-capacity identity gives \(\mathsf C_{p_m}(\mathbf j_m)=1\). Since \(q_m<1\), \(Z_m\ge3\cdot5^m\), proving the claimed entropy surplus.

We next certify the small mass of the entire singleton region, which is essential for the high-codimension guards. Put
\(\varepsilon_m=p_m(T\ge B_m+1)\).
The root bracket, \(Z_m\ge3\cdot5^m\), and \(L_m=B_ms_m/4\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_m
&\le\frac{q_m^{B_m+1}}{Z_m(1-q_m)}
 <\frac{4e^{-L_m}}{3\cdot5^m s_m}\\
&\le\frac{8}{3\cdot5^m s_m L_m^2}
 =\frac{128}{3\cdot54000^h}
 <2^{-m-2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second line uses \(e^{L_m}\ge L_m^2/2\), \(5^ms_m=15^h\), and \(L_m^2=3600^h/16\). The last strict inequality is equivalent to \(512<3\cdot13500^h\), which holds for \(h\ge3\).

All individual nonsingleton layer probabilities are less than one half. Among the three plateau layers the largest is the \(t=-3\) layer, and its probability is at most
\[
\frac{q_m^{-3}}{q_m^{-3}+q_m^{-2}+q_m^{-1}}
=\frac1{1+q_m+q_m^2}<\frac12,
\]
because \(q_m>3/4\) implies \(1+q_m+q_m^2>37/16>2\). Every middle layer has probability at most \(3^m/Z_m\le(3/5)^m/3<1/2\).

Here is the grid count needed for arbitrary orientations. In a Cartesian grid with \(q_{\mathrm{grid}}\) values per coordinate, a real affine flat of dimension \(d\) in \(\mathbb R^m\) contains at most \(q_{\mathrm{grid}}^d\) grid points. Indeed, coordinate functionals restricted to its direction space span that space's dual. Choose \(d\) independent restrictions; projection to those coordinates is injective on the flat, since a difference annihilated by that basis must be zero. There are at most \(q_{\mathrm{grid}}^d\) possible projected coordinate tuples. This includes \(d=0\), where at most one grid point occurs. Consequently its conditional probability in a uniform grid layer is at most \(q_{\mathrm{grid}}^{d-m}\). This is valid for every real flat, without any rationality or coordinate-alignment hypothesis.

Fix a proper nonempty affine flat \(F\subset\mathbb R^n\), and let \(k=\operatorname{codim}F\). If height is nonconstant on \(F\), then \(1\le k\le m\). Its intersection with any fixed-height hyperplane is a dimension-\((m-k)\) flat, and projection onto transverse coordinates preserves that dimension. Each nonsingleton conditional grid, having three or five points per coordinate, gives conditional probability at most \(3^{-k}\). The singleton region contributes at most its whole mass. Therefore
\[
p_m(F)\le3^{-k}+\varepsilon_m<2^{-k},
\]
because
\(2^{-k}-3^{-k}=2^{-k}(1-(2/3)^k)\ge2^{-m}/3>2^{-m-2}>\varepsilon_m\).

If height is constant on \(F\), it has zero probability unless its constant height is supported. At a nonsingleton height the transverse dimension is \(n-k=m+1-k\), so the grid count and the layer bound give
\[
p_m(F)<\frac12\,3^{1-k}\le2^{-k}.
\]
At a singleton height, \(p_m(F)\le\varepsilon_m<2^{-m-2}<2^{-n}\le2^{-k}\). This also covers all singleton flats. These cases exhaust the proper nonempty flats. The whole ambient space has codimension zero and mass one; an empty flat has mass zero. Thus every required flat guard is established.

Finally, the cap numerator is bounded using two infinite geometric sums:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_m
&=\frac{3^m\sum_{t=0}^{B_m}q_m^t
       +\sum_{t=B_m+1}^{A_m}q_m^t}{Z_m}\\
&\le\frac{3^m+1}{Z_m(1-q_m)}
 \le\frac{4(3^m+1)}{3\cdot5^m s_m}
 =\frac43s_m(1+3^{-m}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The closed cap includes every atom at \(t=0\), including the mean atom; none was dropped. It is proper and contains \(\mathbf j_m\), so the definition of \(\tau_{p_m}\) in bf1ff94487296374 gives the stated depth upper bound. As \(h\to\infty\), \(s_m=(3/5)^h\to0\). For any proposed positive absolute \(\delta\), a sufficiently large \(h\) gives a mean closed cap of mass less than \(\delta\), while all hypotheses and flat guards continue to hold.

To verify the fourth-moment limitation, \(T\) is centered, has positive variance, and has finite fourth moment. Hölder gives
\((\mathbb E|T|)^2\ge(\mathbb E T^2)^3/\mathbb E T^4\).
Also \(\mathbb E T_+=\mathbb E|T|/2\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\((\mathbb E T_+)^2\le\delta_m\,\mathbb E T^2\).
Combining them yields \(\kappa_m\ge1/(4\delta_m)\), which tends to infinity. Thus the construction refutes exactly the guard-only absolute mean-cap claim, rather than a claim with a fixed directional fourth-moment ceiling or the locked conjecture's existence of some other deep supported point.
