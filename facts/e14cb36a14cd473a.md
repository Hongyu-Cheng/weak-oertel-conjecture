## Statement

Let $r\ge1$ and let $L\subset\mathbb R^r\times[0,3]$ be a convex body. If its intersections with the three unit slabs $i\le t\le i+1$ for $i=0,1,2$ have equal positive $(r+1)$-dimensional volume, there are a convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^r$ and a vector $v\in\mathbb R^r$ such that $L=\{(y+tv,t):y\in K,\ 0\le t\le3\}$.

## Proof

Write $L_i=L\cap\{i\le t\le i+1\}$. Convexity gives $(L_0+L_2)/2\subseteq L_1$. The classical Brunn–Minkowski inequality and its equality case for positive-volume convex bodies imply equality of the volumes and that $L_0,L_2$ are positive homothets. Their equal volumes make them translates. A proper inclusion of compact convex bodies of positive volume strictly increases volume, since a point outside the smaller closed body and an interior ball of the larger one span a positive-volume exterior region. Thus $L_2=L_0+q$ and $L_1=L_0+q/2$.

If $[\alpha,\beta]$ is the projection of $L$ onto the $t$ axis, positive outer slab volumes give $\alpha<1<2<\beta$. The slab projections are $[\alpha,1],[1,2],[2,\beta]$. Translation gives $q_t=2-\alpha$ and $\beta=3-\alpha$. The left endpoint of the midpoint projection is $1+\alpha/2$ and equals $1$, so $\alpha=0$, $\beta=3$ and $q=(2v,2)$ for some $v$.

For every $0\le t\le2$ the sections obey $L_{t+1}^{\mathrm{sec}}=L_t^{\mathrm{sec}}+v$, using the successive translate identities on each of the first two unit intervals. For each direction $u\in\mathbb R^r$, let $f(t)=h_{L_t^{\mathrm{sec}}}(u)-t\,u\cdot v$. Section inclusions from convexity make $f$ concave and the translate identities give $f(t+1)=f(t)$. For $0\le a\le1$, concavity on $[0,1]$ yields $f(a)\ge f(0)$. The expression of $1$ as the convex combination of $0$ and $1+a$ gives $(1+a)f(1)\ge a f(0)+f(1+a)=a f(0)+f(a)$, so $f(a)\le f(0)$. Periodicity gives constancy throughout. Hence all sections have the support function of $L_0^{\mathrm{sec}}+tv$. Their positive integrated volume makes $K=L_0^{\mathrm{sec}}$ full dimensional.

The Brunn–Minkowski equality theorem used here is the classical one for compact convex bodies of positive volume, as in Schneider, *Convex Bodies: The Brunn–Minkowski Theory*, 2nd ed., §7.1.
