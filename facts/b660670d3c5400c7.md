## Statement

Let \(m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m,h>0\), let \(L\ge0\), let \(\theta_i\le0\) for \(1\le i\le m\), and let \(\lambda\ge0\). Empty sums have their usual meanings. Define the full original support, normalizer, and probabilities by
\[
D=\left\{(\mathbf x,y)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}\times\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_i x_i+hy\le L\right\},\qquad
Z=\sum_{(\mathbf x,y)\in D}e^{\sum_i\theta_i x_i+\lambda y},\qquad
p(\mathbf x,y)=\frac{e^{\sum_i\theta_i x_i+\lambda y}}{Z}.
\]
For \(j\in D\), let \(\tau_p(j)\) be the infimum of \(p(H\cap D)\) over all closed halfspaces \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+1}\) containing \(j\), and set \(M_3(p)=\sum_{j\in D}p(j)\tau_p(j)^3\). Define the following absolute constants, with natural logarithms:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=(8/27)^{3/2},&\gamma&=2048,&
b&=20+\log(48\gamma^4),\\
c&=e^{-(20+b)}/128,&B&=\gamma b+144,&
\delta&=\log(8\alpha)>0,\\
T&=\left\lceil\max\left\{1,\left(\frac{B+8}{\delta}\right)^2\right\}\right\rceil,&
\kappa&=\min\left\{\frac{ce^{-24}}{16\cdot100^4},\frac{1}{64\cdot8^T}\right\}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(M_3(p)\ge\kappa\,8^{-(m+1)}\) for every stated input. For \(m\ge1\) the proof also supplies the stronger estimate
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{ce^{-24}}{128\cdot100^4(m+1)^8}
e^{-B\sqrt m}\alpha^m.
\tag{1}
\]
The constants \(\alpha,\gamma,b,c,B,\delta\) are exactly those of accepted fact ac02a0ffd389f816, with its dimension \(n\) specialized to the light dimension \(m\) when that fact is applied. Accepted fact e37608e52c4b1521 is used at the original \(m,a_i,h,L,\theta_i,\lambda\), only when \(L>0\). The full \(D,Z,p\) in the conclusion are never replaced by the auxiliary product law. The resource model and its auxiliary dimension are not identified with the locked problem's \(n,d,C,S\), or \(h_S\).

## Proof

All resource coefficients are positive, so \(D\) is finite and contains the origin; hence the displayed original \(Z\) is positive and finite. We first prove two elementary comparisons and an arbitrary-score estimate, and then combine them with the two declared accepted inputs.

1. Depth under pointwise domination.

Let \(q\) be any probability law on a subset of \(D\), extended by zero on the rest of \(D\), and suppose \(p(j)\ge\eta q(j)\) at every point for some \(\eta>0\). Let \(\tau_q\) use closed halfspaces in the same ambient space. For every root in the support of \(q\), pointwise domination gives \(p(H\cap D)\ge\eta q(H)\) for every closed halfspace containing that root. Taking infima proves \(\tau_p(j)\ge\eta\tau_q(j)\). Consequently, with the original root weights on the left,
\[
M_3(p)\ge\sum_{j:q(j)>0}p(j)\tau_p(j)^3
\ge\eta^4\sum_{j:q(j)>0}q(j)\tau_q(j)^3
=\eta^4M_3(q).
\tag{2}
\]
One factor of \(\eta\) compares root weights and three compare cap probabilities; no conditioning or replacement of \(Z\) occurs.

2. A complete uniform height band.

Let \(Q\) be any probability law on a finite nonempty subset of \(\mathbb R^m\), where \(m\ge1\). Let \(I=\{y_0,\ldots,y_1\}\) be a nonempty consecutive integer interval, let \(u_I\) be uniform on \(I\), and put \(q=Q\otimes u_I\). Write \(\tau_Q\) for closed-halfspace depth in \(\mathbb R^m\) and \(\tau_q\) for such depth in \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\). We prove
\[
M_3(q)\ge\frac1{128}M_3(Q).
\tag{3}
\]
Put \(N_I=|I|\), and let \(I_{\mathrm c}\) consist of the heights \(y\in I\) for which both closed tails \(I\cap(-\infty,y]\) and \(I\cap[y,\infty)\) have uniform mass at least \(1/4\). To count the excluded heights, order the atoms from the lower endpoint. A height whose lower closed tail has mass less than \(1/4\) has a positive integer rank less than \(N_I/4\). There are fewer than \(N_I/4\) such ranks. The identical assertion holds when ordering from the upper endpoint. The union of the two excluded sets therefore contains fewer than \(N_I/2\) atoms, so \(u_I(I_{\mathrm c})\ge1/2\). This includes \(N_I=1\), when both closed tails are the entire interval.

Fix a root \((\mathbf x,y)\) with \(y\in I_{\mathrm c}\), and let \(H\) be any closed halfspace containing it. Choose an orientation so that \(H=\{(\mathbf z,t):u\cdot\mathbf z+v t\ge\beta\}\), where \((u,v)\ne0\). Root containment says \(u\cdot\mathbf x+v y\ge\beta\); hence the closed halfspace through the root defined by \(u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf x)+v(t-y)\ge0\) is contained in \(H\). Let the base set be \(A=\{\mathbf z:u\cdot(\mathbf z-\mathbf x)\ge0\}\), and choose the height tail \(J=I\cap[y,\infty)\) when \(v\ge0\), and \(J=I\cap(-\infty,y]\) when \(v<0\). Every point of \(A\times J\) satisfies the root halfspace inequality, so \(A\times J\subseteq H\).

If \(u\ne0\), \(A\) is a closed halfspace containing \(\mathbf x\), and \(Q(A)\ge\tau_Q(\mathbf x)\). If \(u=0\), \(A=\mathbb R^m\) and the same inequality holds because \(Q(A)=1\ge\tau_Q(\mathbf x)\). The chosen closed tail has mass at least \(1/4\), including the case \(v=0\). Thus \(q(H)\ge Q(A)u_I(J)\ge\tau_Q(\mathbf x)/4\). Taking the infimum over \(H\), cubing, and averaging over the product roots gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_3(q)
&\ge\sum_{\mathbf x}Q(\mathbf x)\sum_{y\in I_{\mathrm c}}u_I(y)
\left(\frac{\tau_Q(\mathbf x)}4\right)^3\\
&=\frac{u_I(I_{\mathrm c})}{64}M_3(Q)
\ge\frac1{128}M_3(Q),
\end{aligned}
\]
as claimed. This proof uses only the complete uniform interval and works when the base law is a singleton.

3. A direct original-law parity bound with arbitrary scores.

We now prove, for the original law in the statement and every \(m\ge0\),
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}\,64^{-(m+1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
No score-sign restriction is needed for this step. Set \(r=m+1\), regard \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z^r\), and write the original score vector as \(\sigma=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m,\lambda)\); thus \(p(z)=e^{\sigma\cdot z}/Z\). For each \(j\in D\), define \(E_j=D\cap(2j-D)\), \(k_j=|E_j|\), and \(\mathsf H_j=k_jp(j)\), with the oriented count including \(z=j\). For \(z\in E_j\), original log-affinity gives the exact identity \(\sqrt{p(z)p(2j-z)}=p(j)\).

Let \(H\) be a closed halfspace containing \(j\), and put \(A=E_j\cap H\). At least one of \(z,2j-z\) belongs to \(H\) for every reflected pair: their midpoint is \(j\), and a halfspace is specified by a linear inequality satisfied at that midpoint. Reflection preserves \(E_j\) and the quantity \(\sqrt{p(z)p(2j-z)}\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf H_j
&=\sum_{z\in E_j}\sqrt{p(z)p(2j-z)}
\le2\sum_{z\in A}\sqrt{p(z)p(2j-z)}\\
&\le2\sqrt{p(A)\,p(2j-A)}
\le2\sqrt{p(H\cap D)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz, and the last uses \(2j-A\subseteq D\) and total probability one. Taking infima gives the original-depth estimate \(\tau_p(j)\ge\mathsf H_j^2/4\), valid for the positive height score as well as for all other real scores.

Partition \(D\) by coordinatewise parity, and let \(\pi_\varepsilon=p(\{z\in D:z\equiv\varepsilon\pmod2\})\) for \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r\). Two points \(z,w\in D\) of the same parity have the integer midpoint \(j=(z+w)/2\in D\). Indeed, its coordinates are nonnegative and its resource value is the average of two values at most \(L\). Conversely every oriented pair \(z,2j-z\in D\) has the same parity. Original log-affinity also gives \(p(z)p(w)=p(j)^2\) for such a midpoint. The resulting exact finite identity and Cauchy-Schwarz are
\[
\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j
=\sum_{j\in D}k_jp(j)^2
=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^r}\pi_\varepsilon^2
\ge2^{-r}.
\]
Cube \(\tau_p(j)\ge\mathsf H_j^2/4\) and use Jensen for the sixth power on the original probability space to obtain
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j^6
\ge\frac1{64}(\mathbb E_p\mathsf H_j)^6
\ge\frac1{64}64^{-r},
\]
which proves (4). In particular, when \(m=0\), \(D=\{0,\ldots,\lfloor L/h\rfloor\}\) and \(p(y)=e^{\lambda y}/Z\). The same proof in \(r=1\) gives \(M_3(p)\ge1/4096\), including \(\lambda=0\) and a singleton height support. This case is established independently of the decreasing-score predecessor.

4. Consume the accepted band and light-law exponential surplus.

Suppose first that \(L>0\) and \(m\ge1\). Accepted fact e37608e52c4b1521, at exactly the statement's original parameters and probabilities, supplies a complete consecutive height interval \(I=\{y_0,\ldots,y_1\}\), the common light budget \(R=L-hy_1\ge0\), and the actual decreasing light law
\[
D_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{m}:\sum_i a_i x_i\le R\},\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{\mathrm{light}}(R)}e^{\sum_i\theta_i x_i},\qquad
Q_R(\mathbf x)=\frac{e^{\sum_i\theta_i x_i}}{Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)}.
\]
The product law \(q=Q_R\otimes u_I\), extended by zero off its support, is supported in the original \(D\), since \(\sum_i a_i x_i+hy\le R+hy_1=L\) at every product point. The accepted pointwise comparison is
\[
p\ge\eta q,\qquad
\eta=\frac{|I|Z_{\mathrm{light}}(R)e^{\lambda y_0}}{Z}
\ge\frac{e^{-6}}{100(m+1)^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
The denominator \(Z\) is the original normalizer. In particular, \(Q_R\) is used as a comparison probability law, not as a new meaning of \(p\).

Accepted fact ac02a0ffd389f816, specialized from its \(n\) to \(m\ge1\), with its \(a_i,R,\theta_i\) equal to those just displayed, gives \(M_3(Q_R)\ge c e^{-B\sqrt m}\alpha^m\). Its hypotheses hold because \(a_i>0\), \(R\ge0\), and \(\theta_i\le0\). Its depth is exactly the ambient \(\mathbb R^m\) closed-halfspace depth used in step 2. If \(R=0\), \(D_{\mathrm{light}}(R)=\{\mathbf0\}\), \(Q_R(\mathbf0)=1\), and \(M_3(Q_R)=1\); this is also explicitly included in that accepted theorem. Thus no nonempty-interior or positive-budget assumption is introduced.

Combining (2), (3), (5), and this accepted strong bound proves
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{\eta^4}{128}M_3(Q_R)
\ge\frac{ce^{-24}}{128\cdot100^4(m+1)^8}
e^{-B\sqrt m}\alpha^m,
\]
which is (1). It holds uniformly in \(\lambda\ge0\), including \(\lambda=0\), because (5) covers that endpoint. If \(L=0\), positivity of all resource coefficients makes \(D\) a singleton and \(M_3(p)=1\). Since \(c<1\), \(e^{-B\sqrt m}\le1\), and \(0<\alpha<1\), the right side of (1) is less than one for every \(m\ge1\); therefore (1) also holds in this case without invoking the band theorem at \(L=0\).

5. Explicit absorption and all dimensions.

The inequality \(\alpha>1/8\) follows from \(\alpha^2=512/19683>1/64\), equivalent to \(32768>19683\); thus \(\delta>0\). For every real \(s\ge0\), \(\log(1+s)\le\sqrt s\): for \(s>0\), integrate \(1/(1+t)\le1/(2\sqrt t)\) from \(0\) to \(s\), using \(1+t\ge2\sqrt t\); at zero the inequality is equality. If \(m\ge T\), the definition of \(T\) gives
\[
\delta m-B\sqrt m-8\log(m+1)
\ge\delta m-(B+8)\sqrt m
\ge0.
\]
Write \(\alpha^m=8^{-m}e^{\delta m}\) in (1). The preceding inequality then gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac{ce^{-24}}{128\cdot100^4}8^{-m}
=\frac{ce^{-24}}{16\cdot100^4}8^{-(m+1)}
\ge\kappa8^{-(m+1)}.
\]
Here \(m\ge T\ge1\), so (1) is available. If \(0\le m<T\), then \(m+1\le T\), and the independently proved arbitrary-score bound (4) gives
\[
M_3(p)\ge\frac1{64}64^{-(m+1)}
=\left(\frac{8^{-(m+1)}}{64}\right)8^{-(m+1)}
\ge\frac1{64\cdot8^T}8^{-(m+1)}
\ge\kappa8^{-(m+1)}.
\]
This covers \(m=0\) and every remaining dimension with the original positive height score. The constant \(\kappa\) is positive and absolute. All supports and resource inequalities are the stated complete ones with weak boundaries, and every root weight and reflected mass in the original-law conclusion and small-dimension argument uses the original \(Z\).
