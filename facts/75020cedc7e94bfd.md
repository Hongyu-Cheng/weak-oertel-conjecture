## Statement

Let $A,C\subseteq\mathbb R^2$ be convex bodies of areas $a,c>0$, and write $q=V(A,C)$ for their mixed area. For $0\le s\le1$, put $B=(A+C)/2$, $A_s=(1-s)A+sC$, and $C_s=sA+(1-s)C$. The lifted hull of $A_s,B,C_s$ at $-1,0,1$ is a convex body and has exactly those integer fibers, with $B=(A_s+C_s)/2$. Write $W=(a+c+2q)/4=|B|$ and $\Delta=2q-a-c$. Its raw mixed mass is $M_s=a+c+W+2\Delta s(1-s)$, with derivatives $M_s'=2\Delta(1-2s)$ and $M_s''=-4\Delta$. Also $|A_s|-|C_s|=(1-2s)(a-c)$. For $a=c=V$, $\Delta=4(W-V)\ge0$, with equality exactly for translates. When $a\ne c$, $\Delta$ can be negative.

## Proof

Minkowski distributivity and convexity give $A_s+C_s=A+C$. In the hull of the two lifted endpoints the fibers at $-1$ and $1$ are their original endpoint bodies, while the fiber at zero uses equal endpoint weights and is $(A_s+C_s)/2=B$. The projection is $[-1,1]$, so there are no other integer sites. The endpoints have interior and their affine hulls are parallel at distinct bases, giving a full-dimensional compact hull. Every parameter in $(0,1)$ permits both signs of small changes.

The planar mixed-area polynomial gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_s&=(1-s)^2a+2s(1-s)q+s^2c,\\
c_s&=s^2a+2s(1-s)q+(1-s)^2c,\\
W&=(a+c+2q)/4.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $V(A,C)$ is the mixed area normalized so that $|xA+yC|=x^2a+2xyq+y^2c$ for $x,y\ge0$. See [Schneider, Convex Bodies, second expanded edition, §5.1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/convex-bodies-the-brunnminkowski-theory/400F6173EE613859F144E9598DDD8BDF). Adding and subtracting the endpoint polynomials gives $M_s=M_0+2\Delta s(1-s)$ and $a_s-c_s=(1-2s)(a-c)$, where $\Delta=4W-2(a+c)$. Differentiation gives the two mass derivatives.

For $a=c=V$, ordinary planar Brunn–Minkowski gives $W\ge V$, with equality exactly for positively homothetic endpoints. Their equal areas then force the homothety ratio to be one, so equality means translates. See [Gardner, The Brunn–Minkowski Inequality, Theorem 4.1 and its equality discussion](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-02-00941-2). When $C=rA$ with $r>0$, $r\ne1$, one instead has $\Delta=-(r-1)^2a<0$.
