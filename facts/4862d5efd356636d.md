## Statement

Use exactly the rate normalization of accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`: for \(s>0\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), let
\[
P_s(j)=(1-e^{-s})e^{-sj},\qquad
F_3(s)=\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj},\qquad
Q_s(j)=\frac{(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}}{F_3(s)}.
\]
Here \(Q_s\) is the predecessor's same auxiliary law, and \(P_s\) is its geometric law with the exact alias \(\rho=e^{-s}\). The modulus \(q\) below is an integer and is not a geometric parameter. Define the absolute constants
\[
c_{\mathrm{mod}}=\frac1{8e^4(1+e^4)},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{mod}}=8e^4(1+e^4).
\]
Let \(m,q\ge1\) be integers, let \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\in\mathbb Z\), and let \(J_1,\ldots,J_m\) be independent integer-valued random variables. Suppose that for some index \(i\), \(\gcd(u_i,q)=1\), and \(J_i\) has law either \(P_{s_i}\) or \(Q_{s_i}\) at a positive rate satisfying \(qs_i\le1\). The other coordinates may have arbitrary probability laws, including deterministic laws; in particular they may be geometric or cubic-reflection-weight coordinates at arbitrary positive rates. Then every residue \(a\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) satisfies
\[
\frac{c_{\mathrm{mod}}}{q}
\le \Pr\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^m u_\ell J_\ell\equiv a\pmod q\right)
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{mod}}}{q}.
\tag{1}
\]

The precise normalizer consequence and the limits of (1) are as follows. Fix positive rates \(s_1,\ldots,s_m\) and the same coprime diffuse-coordinate hypothesis, and write \(s=(s_1,\ldots,s_m)\) and \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_m)\). Let \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^mP_{s_i}\), \(Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^mQ_{s_i}\), and
\[
A_a=\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m:u\cdot j\equiv a\pmod q\},\qquad
G=\prod_{i=1}^m(1-e^{-s_i})^{-1},\qquad
\mathcal F_3=\prod_{i=1}^m F_3(s_i).
\]
Thus both \(P(A_a)\) and \(Q(A_a)\) lie between \(c_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\) and \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\). For any nonempty original support \(D\subseteq A_a\), retain its actual original normalizer and law
\[
Z=\sum_{j\in D}e^{-s\cdot j}=G P(D),\qquad
p_D(j)=\frac{e^{-s\cdot j}}Z\quad(j\in D),
\]
and extend \(p_D\) by zero outside \(D\). All these sums are finite even when \(D\) is infinite, since \(Z\le G<\infty\). Define
\[
N(j)=\prod_{i=1}^m(2j_i+1),\qquad
K_D(j)=\#\{v\in\mathbb Z^m:j-v\in D,\ j+v\in D\}\quad(j\in D),
\]
and the oriented reflected Hellinger sum \(H_D(j)=\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^m}\sqrt{p_D(j-v)p_D(j+v)}\). The exact identities are
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_D(j)&=\frac{e^{-s\cdot j}}Z K_D(j),\\
\sum_{j\in D}p_D(j)H_D(j)^3
&=\frac1{Z^4}\sum_{j\in D}e^{-4s\cdot j}K_D(j)^3\\
&=G^{-4}\mathcal F_3\,P(D)^{-4}
\sum_{j\in D}Q(j)\left(\frac{K_D(j)}{N(j)}\right)^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently, the following implication is conditional algebra: if an event \(E\subseteq D\) and positive constants \(b,d_*\) satisfy
\[
Q(E)\ge\frac bq,\qquad
\frac{K_D(j)}{N(j)}\ge\frac{d_*}{q}\quad(j\in E),
\tag{3}
\]
then the original quantity in (2), with its unchanged \(D,p_D,Z\), obeys
\[
\sum_{j\in D}p_D(j)H_D(j)^3
\ge \frac{b d_*^3}{C_{\mathrm{mod}}^4}
\prod_{i=1}^m(1-e^{-s_i})^4F_3(s_i).
\tag{4}
\]
The factors in this implication have exact powers \(q^4\), \(q^{-1}\), and \(q^{-3}\). Bound (1) proves the auxiliary-root lower bound only for the whole congruence class \(A_a\), and supplies \(P(D)\le P(A_a)\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\). It does not supply (3) for a resource-restricted \(E\) or \(D\). The assertion is a residue-normalizer lemma and a conditional algebraic consequence, not a resource-conditioned probability theorem, an actual mixed halfspace-depth theorem, or a final for the locked Weak Oertel conjecture.

## Proof

The normalization and positivity of \(Q_s\) agree exactly with accepted fact `336979d75ca7a348`. Its defining series is positive and finite for every \(s>0\), since a polynomial times a decaying exponential is summable. We prove the modular estimates directly from these defining weights.

First let \(J\) have law \(P_s\), where \(qs\le1\). For the representative \(r\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) of a residue, summing a geometric series gives
\[
\Pr(J\equiv r\pmod q)
=\frac{(1-e^{-s})e^{-sr}}{1-e^{-sq}}
=\frac{e^{-sr}}{\sum_{t=0}^{q-1}e^{-st}}.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(0\le st<sq\le1\), every term \(e^{-st}\) is between \(e^{-1}\) and 1. The same is true of the numerator of the last expression. Hence (5) lies between \(e^{-1}/q\) and \(e/q\). These bounds imply the claimed bounds with \(c_{\mathrm{mod}},C_{\mathrm{mod}}\). Formula (5) also includes \(q=1\), when the residue probability is exactly 1.

Next let \(J\) have law \(Q_s\), again with \(qs\le1\), and put \(w(j)=(2j+1)^3e^{-4sj}\). Split the nonnegative integers into blocks \(\{qk,\ldots,qk+q-1\}\), \(k\ge0\). Write
\[
B_k=\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}w(qk+r),\qquad
T=\sum_{k\ge1}B_k,
\]
so \(F_3(s)=B_0+T\). For every \(k\ge1\), the ratio of the largest polynomial factor to the smallest in its block is at most
\[
\left(\frac{2qk+2q-1}{2qk+1}\right)^3\le8,
\]
because \(2qk+2q-1<2(2qk+1)\) when \(k\ge1\). The ratio of the largest exponential factor to the smallest is \(e^{4s(q-1)}\le e^4\). Thus any two weights in that block have ratio at most \(8e^4\). It follows for every residue representative \(r\) that
\[
\frac{B_k}{8e^4q}\le w(qk+r)\le\frac{8e^4B_k}{q}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Summing these inequalities over \(k\ge1\), and writing \(T_r=\sum_{k\ge1}w(qk+r)\), gives
\[
\frac{T}{8e^4q}\le T_r\le\frac{8e^4T}{q}.
\tag{6}
\]

The first block cannot be estimated by polynomial comparability alone, since it includes \(j=0\). Instead, for every \(j\ge0\),
\[
\frac{w(j+q)}{w(j)}
=\left(\frac{2j+2q+1}{2j+1}\right)^3e^{-4sq}
\ge e^{-4}.
\tag{7}
\]
Summing (7) over \(j=0,\ldots,q-1\) proves \(B_1\ge e^{-4}B_0\). Therefore \(T\ge e^{-4}B_0\) and
\[
F_3(s)\le(1+e^4)T.
\tag{8}
\]
For the upper residue estimate, (7) at \(j=r\) also gives \(w(r)\le e^4w(q+r)\le e^4T_r\). Consequently the full unnormalized residue mass \(w(r)+T_r\) satisfies
\[
\frac{T}{8e^4q}
\le w(r)+T_r
\le(1+e^4)T_r
\le\frac{8e^4(1+e^4)T}{q}.
\tag{9}
\]
Divide the lower bound in (9) by (8), and the upper bound in (9) by \(F_3(s)\ge T\). This proves the lower bound \(c_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\) and upper bound \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\) for every residue of \(Q_s\), including \(q=1\).

Now return to the independent coordinates. Since \(\gcd(u_i,q)=1\), multiplication by \(u_i\) permutes the residues modulo \(q\). Thus the same bounds hold for the law of \(u_iJ_i\) modulo \(q\). Let \(R\) be the residue of \(\sum_{\ell\ne i}u_\ell J_\ell\). Independence gives, for each \(a\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^m u_\ell J_\ell\equiv a\pmod q\right)
=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z}\Pr(R=r)\Pr(u_iJ_i\equiv a-r\pmod q).
\]
This is a convex combination of numbers between \(c_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\) and \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\), proving (1). No property of the other coordinate laws except independence is needed.

For the product normalizer calculation, the geometric and auxiliary masses are exactly
\[
P(j)=G^{-1}e^{-s\cdot j},\qquad
Q(j)=\mathcal F_3^{-1}N(j)^3e^{-4s\cdot j}\quad(j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m).
\tag{10}
\]
The product identity for \(\mathcal F_3\) follows by summing the nonnegative product weights, or by repeated summation of the convergent one-coordinate series from `336979d75ca7a348`. Applying (1) to \(P\) and to \(Q\) proves the two whole-coset estimates. Summing the first equality in (10) over \(D\) gives \(Z=GP(D)\), with the actual original support retained, and \(D\subseteq A_a\) gives \(P(D)\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}/q\).

If \(j-v,j+v\in D\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^m\), then \(-j_i\le v_i\le j_i\) in each coordinate. Since \(j,j+v\in A_a\), every such displacement also satisfies \(u\cdot v\equiv0\pmod q\). Thus \(K_D(j)\) counts compatible displacements and \(K_D(j)\le N(j)<\infty\). Each admissible summand in \(H_D(j)\) equals
\[
\sqrt{\frac{e^{-s\cdot(j-v)}}Z\frac{e^{-s\cdot(j+v)}}Z}
=\frac{e^{-s\cdot j}}Z.
\]
Every inadmissible summand is zero, proving the first identity in (2). Cubing it and multiplying by \(p_D(j)\) proves the second identity. The last identity follows from \(Z=GP(D)\) and the second equality of (10). The sum is finite because \(K_D(j)\le N(j)\) and \(\sum_j N(j)^3e^{-4s\cdot j}=\mathcal F_3<\infty\).

Finally, under precisely the additional hypotheses (3), restriction of the nonnegative sum in (2) to \(E\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j\in D}p_D(j)H_D(j)^3
&\ge G^{-4}\mathcal F_3\,P(D)^{-4}Q(E)\left(\frac{d_*}{q}\right)^3\\
&\ge G^{-4}\mathcal F_3\left(\frac q{C_{\mathrm{mod}}}\right)^4
\frac bq\left(\frac{d_*}{q}\right)^3\\
&=\frac{b d_*^3}{C_{\mathrm{mod}}^4}G^{-4}\mathcal F_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is (4), since \(G^{-4}\mathcal F_3=\prod_i(1-e^{-s_i})^4F_3(s_i)\). There is one \(q^{-1}\) from the hypothesized auxiliary-root mass and three \(q^{-1}\) factors from the cubed compatible-displacement count, cancelling the \(q^4\) obtained from the original denominator. Neither (1) nor this algebra establishes (3) for any smaller event; those hypotheses must be proved separately for any proposed resource-support application.
