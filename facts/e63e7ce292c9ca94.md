## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), put \(m:=n+d\), and let \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body. Suppose that
\[
K:=\mathbf a+\rho\mathbb B_\infty^n
\subseteq \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(D),
\qquad \rho\geq0.
\]
Let \(A\subseteq D\) be a compact convex set, possibly empty or lower-dimensional. For \(E\in\{A,D\}\) and \(\mathbf z\in\mathbb R^n\), write
\[
E_{\mathbf z}:=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^d:(\mathbf z,\mathbf x)\in E\},
\qquad
g_E(\mathbf z):=\operatorname{vol}_d(E_{\mathbf z})^{1/d},
\]
where the volume of the empty fiber is zero. Define
\[
L_s^E:=\{\mathbf z:g_E(\mathbf z)\geq s\}\quad(s>0),
\qquad
L_0^E:=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbb R^n}(E).
\]
Then, for every \(s\geq0\) and \(\lambda\in[0,1]\),
\[
(1-\lambda)L_s^A+\lambda K
\subseteq L_{(1-\lambda)s}^D. \tag{1}
\]

For \(q=0,\ldots,n\), set
\[
\beta_{m,n,q}
:=
\frac{n!}{(n-q)!}\frac{(m-q)!}{(m+1)!}
\]
and, for \(s>0\), set
\[
\Psi_q(s):=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)^{\,1-q/n},&0\leq q<n,\\[2mm]
\mathbf 1_{\{L_s^A\neq\varnothing\}},&q=n.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\geq
\sum_{q=0}^n
\beta_{m,n,q}(2\rho)^q
d\int_0^\infty s^{d-1}\Psi_q(s)\,ds. \tag{2}
\]
Thus the formal zero exponent in the \(q=n\) summand means “one exactly on nonempty levels,” not \(0^0=1\) on empty levels.

## Proof

For a compact set \(E\), the map \(\mathbf z\mapsto\operatorname{vol}_d(E_{\mathbf z})\) is upper semicontinuous. Indeed, if \(\mathbf z_j\to\mathbf z\), compactness gives
\[
E_{\mathbf z_j}\subseteq E_{\mathbf z}+\varepsilon\mathbb B_2^d
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\); otherwise a convergent subsequence of violating points contradicts the definition of \(E_{\mathbf z}\). Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves upper semicontinuity. Hence \(g_E\) is measurable and every positive level \(L_s^E\) is compact. When \(E\) is convex, fiberwise convexity and Brunn-Minkowski in \(\mathbb R^d\) show that \(g_E\) is concave on its projection, so every \(L_s^E\) is convex.

Fix \(s>0\), \(\lambda<1\), \(\mathbf y\in L_s^A\), and \(\mathbf p\in K\). Choose \(\mathbf x_{\mathbf p}\in D_{\mathbf p}\). Convexity of \(D\) and \(A\subseteq D\) give
\[
(1-\lambda)A_{\mathbf y}+\lambda\mathbf x_{\mathbf p}
\subseteq
D_{(1-\lambda)\mathbf y+\lambda\mathbf p}.
\]
The set on the left has \(d\)-volume
\((1-\lambda)^d\operatorname{vol}_d(A_{\mathbf y})\), so
\[
g_D((1-\lambda)\mathbf y+\lambda\mathbf p)
\geq(1-\lambda)g_A(\mathbf y)
\geq(1-\lambda)s.
\]
This proves (1) for \(s>0\) and \(\lambda<1\). For \(s=0\), the same argument without volumes proves the projection inclusion. For \(\lambda=1\), the left side is empty if \(L_s^A\) is empty and otherwise equals \(K\), while the right side is \(L_0^D=\operatorname{proj}(D)\); hence (1) also holds at \(\lambda=1\).

Layer cake and Fubini give
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
=d\int_0^\infty t^{d-1}\operatorname{vol}_n(L_t^D)\,dt. \tag{3}
\]
Fix \(\lambda\in[0,1)\) and substitute \(t=(1-\lambda)s\) in (3). For each nonempty \(L_s^A\), (1) and Brunn-Minkowski in \(\mathbb R^n\), using
\(\operatorname{vol}_n(K)^{1/n}=2\rho\), give
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(L_{(1-\lambda)s}^D)
\geq
\left[
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)^{1/n}
+2\rho\lambda
\right]^n. \tag{4}
\]
If \(L_s^A=\varnothing\), (1) gives no copy of \(K\), so the correct lower bound at that level is zero. Consequently, (3) and (4) yield
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\geq
d\int_0^\infty s^{d-1}(1-\lambda)^d
\mathbf 1_{\{L_s^A\neq\varnothing\}}
\left[
(1-\lambda)\operatorname{vol}_n(L_s^A)^{1/n}
+2\rho\lambda
\right]^n ds. \tag{5}
\]
Brunn-Minkowski remains valid when a nonempty \(L_s^A\) has zero \(n\)-volume. Expanding the \(n\)-th power in (5), with precisely the convention encoded by \(\Psi_n\), gives
\[
\operatorname{vol}_m(D)
\geq
\sum_{q=0}^n
\binom nq(2\rho)^q
(1-\lambda)^{m-q}\lambda^q
d\int_0^\infty s^{d-1}\Psi_q(s)\,ds. \tag{6}
\]
All terms are nonnegative. Integrate (6) over \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) and use Tonelli. The substitution leading to (5) was used only for \(\lambda<1\); at \(\lambda=1\), every right-hand term in (6) is zero because \(m-q\geq d>0\), so adjoining that endpoint is harmless. Finally,
\[
\binom nq\int_0^1(1-\lambda)^{m-q}\lambda^q\,d\lambda
=
\binom nq\frac{(m-q)!\,q!}{(m+1)!}
=
\frac{n!}{(n-q)!}\frac{(m-q)!}{(m+1)!}
=\beta_{m,n,q},
\]
which proves (2).

If \(L_s^A\) is empty, every \(\Psi_q(s)\) is zero. Since \(A\) is compact, all levels above \(\max g_A\) are empty. At the top level itself, (4) is still valid even if that level has zero \(n\)-volume; the \(q=n\) convention records its nonemptiness, and the value at this single \(s\) does not alter the integrals. These observations also cover \(A=\varnothing\).
