## Statement

Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a compact convex body, \(n,d\ge1\), and let \(\ell_i^\pm:T\to[0,\infty)\) be bounded upper-semicontinuous concave functions. Define
\[
C=\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y):
\mathbf y\in T,\ -\ell_i^-(\mathbf y)\le x_i\le1+\ell_i^+(\mathbf y)\},
\quad S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),
\]
\[
a_i=\lfloor\ell_i^-\rfloor,\quad b_i=\lfloor\ell_i^+\rfloor,\quad
N_i=a_i+b_i+2,\quad B=\prod_i(N_i-1),\quad M=\int_TB.
\]
Let \(H_*=\max_{\mathbf p\in S}h_S(\mathbf p)\), with original unnormalized mixed measure and containing closed halfspaces.

(a) Put
\[
\beta=M^{-1}\int_TB(\mathbf y)\#\{i:N_i(\mathbf y)=2\}\,d\mathbf y.
\]
Then
\[
H_*\ge10^{-58}(1+\beta)^{-8}M. \tag{1}
\]
Coordinates with \(N_i=2\) almost everywhere may first be removed and need not be counted in \(\beta\).

(b) Fix \(L\ge3\). Suppose the coordinates partition into \(\mathcal O,\mathcal G\), where \(N_i\le L\) almost everywhere on \(\mathcal O\), and each such coordinate has either \(b_i\ge a_i\) almost everywhere or the reverse inequality almost everywhere. Suppose \(N_i\ge3\) almost everywhere on \(\mathcal G\), with no upper width bound or orientation restriction. Then
\[
H_*\ge\frac{10^{-34}}{L^6(1+\log L)^5}M. \tag{2}
\]

The shared estimates below are also asserted.

For
\[
f(N)=\frac{N(N^2-1)}{N^2+2},
\]
every halfspace containing a point within coordinatewise distance \(1/2\) of the center of an integer box with side counts \(N_i\ge2\) contains at least \(\prod_i f(N_i)/144\) points.

For a measure whose conditional integer sections are uniform product boxes of side counts \(N_i\), total mass \(Z\), and base probability law \(\nu\), adding independent uniform \([-1/2,1/2]\) noise and subsequently rounding a real center to nearest integers loses at most
\[
\sqrt6\,Z\sqrt{\sum_i(\mathbb E_\nu N_i^{-1})^2}
\]
from any containing halfspace.

For positive concave widths \(w_i\ge2\), let
\[
G=\prod_iw_i,\quad d\gamma=G\,d\mathbf y/\int_TG,\quad
\Psi=\sum_iw_i^{-1},\quad s=\mathbb E_\gamma\Psi.
\]
Then
\[
-\log\mathbb E_\gamma e^{-q\Psi}
\ge2s(\sqrt{1+q}-1)\quad(q\ge0), \tag{3}
\]
\[
\log\mathbb E_\gamma e^{\Psi/2}\le s. \tag{4}
\]

## Proof

First prove the box estimate. Direct algebra gives
\[
f(2)=1,\quad N-1\le f(N)\le N,\quad
\log\frac{f(N)}{N-1}\ge\frac1{4N}\quad(N\ge3). \tag{5}
\]
For the last inequality use
\(f(N)/(N-1)=1+(N-2)/(N^2+2)\) and
\(\log(1+t)\ge t/(1+t)\).

For a two-site coordinate, fix the favorable endpoint for the chosen normal. It contributes nonnegatively relative to any permitted shifted center and contributes the factor \(f(2)=1\).

For \(N\ge3\), let \(X\) be uniform on the centered integer interval, with \(v=(N^2-1)/12\), and tilt by the nonnegative likelihood
\(L_e(X)=1+eX/v\), where \(|e|\le1/2\). The tilted mean is \(e\), and its variance is \(v-e^2\). The original kurtosis is
\[
\kappa=\frac{3(3N^2-7)}{5(N^2-1)}\in[3/2,9/5).
\]
Writing \(t=e^2/v\), expansion gives
\[
\mathbb E_{L_e}(X-e)^4/v^2
=\kappa+(6-4\kappa)t-3t^2\le3(1-t)^2.
\]
Indeed, the difference is
\((3-\kappa)(1-4t)+6t^2\), whose minimum is nonnegative for this range of \(\kappa\). Independent sums under the product tilted law have kurtosis at most three. Interpolation and Cauchy-Schwarz therefore give probability at least \(1/12\) on either side of their mean. Also
\[
\mathbb EL_e^2\le1+3/(N^2-1).
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz transfers the halfspace probability back to the original uniform box, giving the claimed factor \(1/144\). Degenerate normal directions cause no difficulty.

Next prove the smoothing estimate. Conditional on the base, the jittered coordinates are independent uniform intervals of lengths \(N_i\). A nonconstant linear combination has a symmetric log-concave density, by preservation of log-concavity under marginalization. If such a density has height \(H\), its survival function on the positive half-line is at most \(\frac12e^{-2Ht}\). To see this, log-concavity makes the hazard rate nondecreasing, and its value at zero is \(2H\). Integration of the survival bound gives variance at most \(1/(2H^2)\).

The variance of the conditional linear combination with coefficients \(a_i\) is \(\sum_i a_i^2N_i^2/12\). Its height is therefore at most
\[
\sqrt6/(\sum_i a_i^2N_i^2)^{1/2}.
\]
Rounding the center and removing the noise changes the linear functional by at most \(\|\mathbf a\|_1\). The lost strip has mass at most
\[
\sqrt6\,Z\,
\mathbb E_\nu\frac{\|\mathbf a\|_1}
{(\sum_i a_i^2N_i^2)^{1/2}}.
\]
The function \(F(\mathbf t)=(\sum_i a_i^2/t_i^2)^{-1/2}\) is concave on the positive orthant. Along any direction \(\mathbf v\), its second derivative has the sign of
\[
(\sum_i a_i^2v_i/t_i^3)^2
-(\sum_i a_i^2/t_i^2)(\sum_i a_i^2v_i^2/t_i^4),
\]
which is nonpositive by Cauchy-Schwarz. Jensen, followed by Cauchy-Schwarz, proves the smoothing estimate. If all \(a_i=0\), the loss is zero.

We now prove (3)-(4). For smooth positive concave widths on a smooth convex domain, let \(\gamma_q\) have density proportional to \(Ge^{-q\Psi}\). Its negative log-density \(U_q\) satisfies
\[
\nabla^2U_q\succeq
\sum_i\frac{w_i+2q}{w_i^3}
\nabla w_i\nabla w_i^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The Brascamp-Lieb variance inequality and matrix Cauchy-Schwarz give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\gamma_q}(\Psi)
\le\mathbb E_{\gamma_q}\sum_i\frac1{w_i(w_i+2q)}
\le\frac{\mathbb E_{\gamma_q}\Psi}{2(1+q)}.
\]
Since \(\frac d{dq}\mathbb E_{\gamma_q}\Psi
=-\operatorname{Var}_{\gamma_q}(\Psi)\),
one gets \(\mathbb E_{\gamma_q}\Psi\ge s/\sqrt{1+q}\). Integration gives (3).

For density proportional to \(Ge^{\theta\Psi}\), \(0\le\theta<1\), the analogous Hessian uses coefficients \((w_i-2\theta)/w_i^3>0\), and gives variance at most
\(\mathbb E_\theta\Psi/[2(1-\theta)]\).
Thus \(\mathbb E_\theta\Psi\le s/\sqrt{1-\theta}\).
Integrating to \(1/2\) gives
\(2s(1-1/\sqrt2)\le s\), proving (4).

The convex-domain variance inequality used here follows from the weighted Neumann Bochner identity: for a mean-zero test function, solve the weighted Poisson equation, integrate by parts, and discard the nonnegative boundary second-fundamental-form term. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz then gives the inverse-Hessian bound. Add a positive quadratic regularizer before inversion and pass to its zero limit.

For the stated nonsmooth roofs, exhaust the interior of \(T\) by smooth convex subdomains. On compact subsets of the interior, concave widths are continuous and can be mollified in a slightly larger interior neighborhood; mollification preserves concavity and the lower bound \(2\). Apply the smooth argument, pass to the mollification limit on each subdomain, and then exhaust \(T\). All functions in (3)-(4) are bounded for each fixed instance, and the boundary of \(T\) is Lebesgue-null. Dominated convergence proves the asserted extension. This step concerns the real widths only; no convergence of floor functions is required.

We shall use two further tools, recording their proofs.

First, accepted fact 9ee12600178ce4dd gives, for a density of mass \(Z\) whose normalized law has relative entropy \(D\) from a log-concave probability reference, maximum continuous depth at least
\[
Z/[300(1+D)]. \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, take \(\theta=1/(1+D)\), \(\alpha=1/(1+\theta)\), and use Jensen to lower-bound its fractional moment by \(Ze^{-\theta D}\). If \(c=\log(3/2)\), then
\[
\kappa_{1/(1+\theta)}
\ge[\theta c/(1+\theta)]^{1+\theta}\ge\theta/25
\quad(0<\theta\le1).
\]
For the second inequality, the logarithmic derivative of
\(\theta^\theta[c/(1+\theta)]^{1+\theta}\) is
\(\log(c\theta/(1+\theta))<0\), so its minimum is \(c^2/4>1/25\).
The weaker constant in (6) follows.

Second, deleting \(r\) real coordinates permits depth lifting with factor \(r+1\), while preserving a compact convex expansion \(E\) in the remaining coordinates. For a source measure \(\sigma\), use expanded caps with threshold
\(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x+\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf z-h_E(\mathbf v)\).
For every source halfspace of mass greater than \(Z-\delta/(r+1)\), impose its expanded inequality on the fiber over the prescribed projected point. If these are infeasible, the finite linear-inequality alternative gives nonnegative multipliers supported on at most \(r+1\) constraints and eliminates the deleted coordinates. Support-function subadditivity produces a projected expanded cap of mass below \(\delta\), a contradiction. Coordinate bounds and compactness give a common point in
\(\operatorname{conv}(\operatorname{supp}\sigma)+(\{0\}\times E)\).
A smaller-mass cap would have a heavy closed complement excluding this point. This proves the lifting assertion. The sparse alternative follows by normalizing the nonnegative Farkas multipliers and taking an extreme point, whose support has size at most \(r+1\).

We prove (a). Define
\[
w_i=2+\ell_i^-+\ell_i^+,\quad
G=\prod_iw_i,\quad V_G=\int_TG,\quad
d\gamma=G\,d\mathbf y/V_G,
\]
\[
\Psi=\sum_i1/w_i,\quad s=\mathbb E_\gamma\Psi,\quad L_0=1+s.
\]
One has \(N_i\le w_i<N_i+2\). For \(I\subseteq[n]\), put
\[
\Phi_I=\prod_{i\in I}f(N_i)\prod_{j\notin I}N_j,\quad
Z_I=\int_T\Phi_I,\quad d\nu_I=\Phi_I\,d\mathbf y/Z_I.
\]
The inequalities
\[
e^{-12\Psi}\le\Phi_I/G\le1
\]
follow from \(w_i/f(N_i)\le(N_i+2)/(N_i-1)\) and
\(\log((N_i+2)/(N_i-1))\le12/(N_i+2)<12/w_i\).
Thus
\[
\log(V_G/Z_I)\le12s,\qquad D(\nu_I\|\gamma)\le12s.
\]
The entropy variational inequality and (4) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_I}\Psi\le26s,\qquad
\sum_i\mathbb E_{\nu_I}(1/N_i)\le52s. \tag{7}
\]

Set \(R=10^{10}L_0^3\). Start at \(I=\varnothing\), adding a coordinate whenever
\(\mathbb E_{\nu_I}(1/N_i)>1/R\). Each addition decreases \(\log Z_I\) by at least
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_I}\frac3{N_i^2+2}
\ge2(\mathbb E_{\nu_I}1/N_i)^2>2/R^2.
\]
The total decrease is at most \(12s\), so the final set has
\[
r=|I|\le6sR^2.
\]
For its complement \(K\), (7) and the stopping condition give
\[
\sum_{j\in K}(\mathbb E_{\nu_I}1/N_j)^2\le52s/R. \tag{8}
\]

Assign weight \(F_I=\prod_{i\in I}f(N_i)\) to each original \(K\)-section point and jitter those coordinates. The resulting density has mass \(Z=Z_I\) and is dominated by
\[
g=(\prod_{i\in I}w_i)\mathbf1_{Q_K},
\]
where \(Q_K\) has intervals
\([-\ell_j^--1/2,\,3/2+\ell_j^+]\) in \(K\). This is a log-concave density of mass \(V_G\). Applying accepted fact 9ee12600178ce4dd with
\(\alpha=(1+12s)/(2+12s)\), the above bound on \(\kappa_\alpha\), and
\(\log(V_G/Z)\le12s\), gives continuous depth at least \(Z/(1000L_0)\).

Nearest-integer rounding and (8) lose at most
\[
\sqrt{312}Z\sqrt{s/R}\le Z/(2000L_0).
\]
Thus the discrete projected measure has depth at least \(Z/(2000L_0)\) at one point \((\mathbf p_K,\mathbf y_0)\).

For the retained coordinates use the original section centers
\(m_i=(1+b_i-a_i)/2\). Put the projected mass on the graph
\((\mathbf m_I(\mathbf y),\mathbf k_K,\mathbf y)\).
Lift through the \(r\) retained real coordinates, then round them to nearest integers. The box estimate gives
\[
H_*\ge\frac{Z}{144\cdot2000L_0(r+1)}
\ge10^{-27}Z/(1+s)^8. \tag{9}
\]
Feasibility is part of this construction: every center-graph point lies in the convex body obtained by moving the selected lower and upper endpoints inward by \(1/2\); its convex hull remains there, and rounding returns to \(C\).

Let \(q(\mathbf y)=\#\{i:N_i=2\}\). From (5),
\[
\Phi_I/B\ge\exp(\Psi/4-q/8),\qquad Z\ge M. \tag{10}
\]
Use (3) at \(2703\), for which \(\sqrt{2704}=52\). Chernoff gives
\(\gamma\{\Psi\le s/52\}\le e^{-(50+1/52)s}\).
Since \(d\nu_I/d\gamma\le e^{12s}\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_I}e^{-\Psi/4}\le2e^{-s/208}.
\]
The set \(q\le2\beta\) has \(B\)-mass at least \(M/2\); when \(\beta=0\), it has full mass. Therefore (10) yields
\[
Z\ge(M/4)e^{s/208-\beta/4}. \tag{11}
\]

If \(s\le104(1+\beta)\), use \(Z\ge M\) in (9). Otherwise
\(s/208-\beta/4\ge s/416\), and use (11).
The minimum of \(e^{s/416}/(1+s)^8\) occurs at \(s=3327\) and exceeds \(4000^{-8}\). Both cases give (1).

A permanently two-site coordinate has integer section \(\{0,1\}\) almost everywhere and contributes one to \(B\). After deleting such coordinates, fix any one core vertex in them. The original mixed measure restricted to that vertex is the reduced measure, so its depth lifts without loss. This justifies their removal.

We now prove (b). Reflect coordinates so that \(b_i\ge a_i\) on \(\mathcal O\). Put
\[
F=\prod_if(N_i),\quad Q=F/B,\quad
\rho_\lambda=BQ^\lambda,\quad Z_\lambda=\int_T\rho_\lambda,
\quad0\le\lambda\le1.
\]
Fix \(\lambda\), and let \(d\nu=\rho_\lambda d\mathbf y/Z_\lambda\). Define
\[
t=\mathbb E_\nu\#\{i\in\mathcal O:N_i\ge3\}.
\]
Use only \(\mathcal G\) in
\[
G=\prod_{i\in\mathcal G}w_i,\quad
d\gamma=G\,d\mathbf y/\int_TG,\quad
\Psi=\sum_{i\in\mathcal G}1/w_i,\quad s=\mathbb E_\gamma\Psi,
\]
and set \(T_\lambda=1+s+(1+\log L)t\).

Each scalar factor of \(\rho_\lambda\) is
\((N_i-1)^{1-\lambda}f(N_i)^\lambda\). The \(\mathcal G\)-factor divided by \(G\) lies between \(e^{-12\Psi}\) and one; the \(\mathcal O\)-factor is at most \(L^{\#\{N_i\ge3\}}\) and at least one. Hence
\[
D(\nu\|\gamma)\le12s+(\log L)t,
\]
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal G}\mathbb E_\nu(1/N_i)
\le52s+4(\log L)t. \tag{12}
\]

We claim
\[
H_*\ge Z_\lambda/[10^{22}L\,T_\lambda^5]. \tag{13}
\]
Set \(R=10^{12}T_\lambda^3\), and retain
\(I_{\mathcal G}=\{i\in\mathcal G:\mathbb E_\nu(1/N_i)>1/R\}\).
For \(U=\mathcal G\setminus I_{\mathcal G}\), (12) gives
\[
|I_{\mathcal G}|\le52RT_\lambda,\qquad
\sum_{i\in U}(\mathbb E_\nu1/N_i)^2\le52T_\lambda/R.
\]

Assign weight \(\rho_\lambda/\prod_{i\in U}N_i\) to each original \(U\)-section point and jitter \(U\). Compare its density \(h\) with
\[
g=(\prod_{i\in I_{\mathcal G}}w_i)\mathbf1_{Q_U}.
\]
The reference has mass \(\int_TG\), and
\[
D(h/Z_\lambda\|g/\int_TG)
\le12s+(\log L)t\le12(T_\lambda-1).
\]
By (6), continuous depth is at least \(Z_\lambda/(4000T_\lambda)\). The smoothing estimate loses at most half this amount. Thus at one integer point in \(U\), the projected discrete depth is at least
\[
\delta_1Z_\lambda,\qquad \delta_1=1/(8000T_\lambda).
\]

For \(i\in\mathcal O\), put \(s_i=b_i-a_i\ge0\), and retain
\(I_{\mathcal O}=\{i:\mathbb E_\nu s_i>\delta_1\}\).
Since \(s_i\le L\mathbf1_{\{N_i\ge3\}}\),
\[
|I_{\mathcal O}|\le8000LT_\lambda^2.
\]
Let \(I=I_{\mathcal G}\cup I_{\mathcal O}\) and \(K=\mathcal O\setminus I_{\mathcal O}\).
Form the graph measure with original centers \((1+b_i-a_i)/2\) in \(I\), anchor centers \((b_i-a_i)/2\) in \(K\), and original integer points in \(U\), with the same weight as the projected measure. Preserve the cube expansion \([0,1]^K\).

At the target \(\mathbf1_K\), choose the favorable cube vertex for a normal in \(K\). On a favorable projected halfspace in \((U,\mathbf y)\), its \(K\)-contribution is
\[
\sum_{u_i\ge0}u_is_i/2
+\sum_{u_i<0}|u_i|(1-s_i/2).
\]
Failure requires a convex combination of the \(s_i/2\) to exceed one. Markov bounds lost mass by
\((Z_\lambda/2)\max_{i\in K}\mathbb E_\nu s_i
\le\delta_1Z_\lambda/2\).
Thus projected expanded depth is at least \(\delta_1Z_\lambda/2\).

Expanded projection through \(|I|\) coordinates, nearest rounding there, and the box estimate give
\[
H_*\ge\delta_1Z_\lambda/[288(|I|+1)].
\]
Indeed,
\(\rho_\lambda/\prod_{i\in U}N_i
\le\prod_{i\in I\cup K}f(N_i)\);
the favorable \(K\)-cube vertex differs from the original section center by at most \(1/2\). The graph plus that cube lies in the original convex body with only the \(I\)-endpoints moved inward by \(1/2\), so the rounded point is feasible.
Since \(|I|+1\le10^{15}LT_\lambda^4\), this proves (13).

Finally put \(u=\mathbb E_\nu\Psi\) and \(g(\lambda)=\log(Z_\lambda/M)\).
The entropy variational inequality applied to \(-168\Psi\), together with (3), gives
\[
D(\nu\|\gamma)\ge24s-168u,
\quad\text{hence}\quad
s\le14u+(\log L)t/12.
\]
Also
\[
g'(\lambda)=\mathbb E_\nu\log Q\ge t/(4L)+u/4.
\]
Therefore
\[
T_\lambda\le1+25L(1+\log L)g'(\lambda).
\]
Set \(A_L=10^{22}L\), \(B_L=25L(1+\log L)\). Equation (13) gives
\[
H_*/M\ge e^{g(\lambda)}/[A_L(1+B_Lg'(\lambda))^5].
\]
If \(A_LH_*/M<(10B_L)^{-5}\), set
\(K=(A_LH_*/M)^{-1/5}>10B_L\). Then
\[
g'\ge K e^{g/5}/(2B_L),\qquad
\frac d{d\lambda}e^{-g/5}\le-K/(10B_L)<-1.
\]
Integration from zero to one contradicts positivity and \(g(0)=0\).
Thus
\[
H_*\ge M/[A_L(10B_L)^5]
\ge10^{-34}M/[L^6(1+\log L)^5],
\]
proving (2).

Eric A. Carlen, Dario Cordero-Erausquin, and Elliott H. Lieb,
"Asymmetric Covariance Estimates of Brascamp-Lieb Type and Related Inequalities for Log-concave Measures," arXiv:1106.0709v2:
https://arxiv.org/html/1106.0709v2

Equation (1.3) is the inverse-Hessian variance inequality
\[
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)
\le\int\nabla f^{\mathsf T}(\nabla^2U)^{-1}\nabla f\,d\mu
\]
for a sufficiently smooth log-concave density proportional to \(e^{-U}\).
The proof above specifies the convex-domain Neumann version, regularization, and approximation needed for the present widths. It is applied only after the relevant Hessian lower bounds are checked.

The same paper explicitly records preservation of log-concavity under marginalization in Section 4. Here that property is used only for linear projections of uniform product-interval measures; symmetry and positive variance are checked separately. No log-concavity is asserted for the floor-count or jittered roof density itself.
