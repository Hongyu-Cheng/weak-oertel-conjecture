## Statement

For $d\ge1$, let $m=d+1$, $r=d/m$, $\kappa=(1-r^d)^{1/d}$, $\psi(q)=\min\{1,\max\{0,1-q\}\}$ and $\phi(q)=(1-\psi(q)^d)^{1/d}$.

Let $T\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a full-dimensional simplex of centroid zero and volume $V>0$, with barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i=1/m+\ell_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. The convex hull of the lifts of $A=aT+\delta$ at $-1$ and $D=bT-\delta$ at $1$, with $a,b>0$, has actual midpoint fiber $K=((a+b)/2)T$. Take ambient halfspaces $H_i=\{(z,x):\ell_i(x)+\beta_i z\ge0\}$, with $B=\sum_i\beta_i$. Their endpoint barycentric cutoffs on the reference $T$ are
$$
q_i^A=1/m-\ell_i(\delta)/a+\beta_i/a,\qquad
q_i^D=1/m+\ell_i(\delta)/b-\beta_i/b.
$$
They satisfy
$$
\sum_iq_i^A=1+B/a,\qquad\sum_iq_i^D=1-B/b,\qquad
a q_i^A+b q_i^D=(a+b)/m.
$$
The endpoint capture fractions are $\psi(q)^d$. The actual volume roots of the residuals are $aV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^A)$ and $bV^{1/d}\phi(q_i^D)$. The closed residual is empty for $q<0$ and tangent for $q=0$, while the capture is tangent for $q=1$ and empty for $q>1$. For $0<q<1$ both have positive volume.

The source residual root is $V^{1/d}\kappa(a+b)/2$ in each direction. The normalized root overshoots are $\Delta_i=[a\phi(q_i^A)+b\phi(q_i^D)-\kappa(a+b)]/2$, with actual root overshoots $V^{1/d}\Delta_i$.

For every $h\in\mathbb R$ the attained maximum of $\sum_{i=1}^m\phi(q_i)$ over all real vectors with $\sum_i q_i=h$ is
$$
E_d(h)=\max\{d,m\phi(h/m)\}.
$$
The branches meet at $h_*=m(1-\kappa)$. At $h=1$ the deficit $J(q)=m\kappa-\sum_i\phi(q_i)$ is nonnegative. Equality holds precisely at the uniform vector if $d\ge2$ and at every such vector if $d=1$.

## Proof

The linear forms $\ell_i$ sum to zero. Their map to $\mathbb R^m$ is injective since barycentric coordinates determine a point, so it is an isomorphism onto the sum-zero subspace. At any given base, the convex hull of the two endpoint lifts has exactly the section given by the corresponding Minkowski interpolation. At zero this is the asserted $K$ because the two translations cancel and $aT+bT=(a+b)T$. Substituting $x=aX+\delta$ or $x=bX-\delta$ with $X\in T$ gives the cutoffs and their shared identities. Conversely, every pair of vectors satisfying the displayed identities for a specified $B$ is actual: take $\delta=0$ and $\beta_i=a(q_i^A-1/m)$, whose sum is $B$; the paired identity then gives the other vector. On $T$, $\lambda_i\ge q$ has relative volume $(1-q)^d$ for $0\le q\le1$ by homothety at vertex $i$. The extremes and tangent sets follow from the range $[0,1]$ of $\lambda_i$ on $T$. Hyperplane levels have zero $d$-volume, so either open or closed residual gives the same root. The middle trace has cutoff $1/m$ and its simplex is scaled by $(a+b)/2$, giving the source root and stated overshoots.

On $[0,\infty)$, $\phi$ is nondecreasing and concave, with $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(q)=1$ for $q\ge1$. For $d=1$ it is the capped linear function there. For $d\ge2$, on $(0,1)$ its derivative is $(1-q)^{d-1}[1-(1-q)^d]^{1/d-1}$ and its second derivative is
$$
-(d-1)(1-q)^{d-2}[1-(1-q)^d]^{1/d-2}<0.
$$
The derivative tends to zero at the upper endpoint and $\phi$ is continuous at both ends, so adjoining the constant part preserves concavity. If all $q_i\ge0$, Jensen bounds their sum of values by $m\phi(h/m)$. If at least one is negative, it contributes zero, and the others contribute at most $d$. The value $d$ is realized for every $h$ by taking $d$ entries at a common sufficiently large value $L\ge1$ and the last entry $h-dL<0$. When the other branch dominates, its equal coordinates are nonnegative and realize that value. The branch equation follows from $\phi(1-\kappa)=r$.

For $d\ge2$, $(1+1/d)^d>2$ gives $r^d<1/2$ and $\kappa>r$, so $d<m\kappa$. The negative-threshold branch then cannot attain the maximum at $h=1$. For the nonnegative vectors, the mean $1/m$ is strictly inside $(0,1)$, and strict concavity there makes Jensen strict for a nonconstant vector: its range contains a nonempty subinterval of $(0,1)$ and $\phi$ is not affine on the range. In $d=1$, clipping gives $\phi(q)+\phi(1-q)=1$ for all real $q$ and $m\kappa=1$.
