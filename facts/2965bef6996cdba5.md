## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ be an integer and let $A,K,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact convex bodies with nonempty interiors and $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Put $S=(\lbrace-1\rbrace\times A)\cup(\lbrace0\rbrace\times K)\cup(\lbrace1\rbrace\times B)$, measured by $d$-dimensional Hausdorff measure $\mu$ on $S$. Fix $y\in K$ and let $Q$ be the infimum of $\mu(S\cap H)$ over all closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(0,y)$.

Suppose $H_\alpha=\lbrace(z,x):u\cdot(x-y)+\alpha z\ge0\rbrace$, with $u\ne0$, attains $Q$. Let $U,q,V$ be the respective $d$-volumes of its traces on $A,K,B$. Suppose $q>0$ and $q^{1/d}=(U^{1/d}+V^{1/d})/2$. Then $U=q=V=Q/3$. All three cuts are proper. The captured endpoint bodies are translates, their Minkowski midpoint equals the entire captured middle body, and the endpoint cutting sections have equal positive $(d-1)$-dimensional measure.

## Proof

Apply the finite-tilt equalization lemma to these three full-dimensional bodies, the specified mark and its depth-attaining cap with nonzero continuous normal. The stated root equality, $d\ge2$ and $q>0$ give $U=V=q=Q/3$ and proper endpoint cuts. Let $A',K',B'$ be the three captured bodies. They are compact, convex and full-dimensional because their volumes are positive. For $a'\in A'$ and $b'\in B'$, their midpoint belongs to $K$ and has $u\cdot((a'+b')/2-y)\ge0$, by the endpoint thresholds $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$. Hence $(A'+B')/2\subseteq K'$.

Ordinary Brunn–Minkowski gives $|(A'+B')/2|^{1/d}\ge(|A'|^{1/d}+|B'|^{1/d})/2=|K'|^{1/d}$. Both inequalities in the volume comparison are equalities. A strict inclusion of two compact full-dimensional convex bodies would give a positive volume difference. Indeed, joining a point outside the smaller body to an interior point of the larger yields interior points still outside the smaller closed body. Thus $(A'+B')/2=K'$ as sets. The positive-volume equality case of Brunn–Minkowski makes $A'$ and $B'$ positively homothetic, with ratio one because their volumes agree. This translation takes the face minimizing $u\cdot x$ on $A'$ to the face minimizing $u\cdot x$ on $B'$. These faces are the respective cutting sections since the cuts are proper. Each contains a relative interior ball in its cutting plane, so their common $(d-1)$-dimensional measure is positive.

For each of the two strict sides, take an interior point of each endpoint on that side of its own cut. Their midpoint belongs to $K$ and is strictly on that side of the middle cut. Averages of interior balls in $A$ and $B$ yield an open ball about each such midpoint in $K$, so the middle cut is proper.
