## Statement

Let the base coordinate be $z\in\mathbb R$, the continuous coordinate be $x\in\mathbb R^d$, and let two finite groups of affine cap margins have respective contacts $(r_0,y_0)$ and $(r_1,y_1)$ with $r_0<r_1$. Every margin is nonpositive at both contacts, and each group's own margins vanish at its contact. Assume the continuous normals within each group form a minimal positive dependence.

One affine shear, fixing $z$ and translating the continuous fiber at each base, puts the two groups' margins respectively in the forms $u_{0j}\cdot x+c_j(r_0-z)$ and $u_{1j}\cdot x+b_j(z-r_1)$ with every $c_j,b_j\ge0$. If both circuits have rank $d$ and nonzero projected coefficient (the positive weighted sum of their base coefficients), set
$$
B_0=\{x:u_{0j}\cdot x\le c_j\text{ for every }j\},\qquad
B_1=\{x:u_{1j}\cdot x\le b_j\text{ for every }j\}.
$$
These are full-dimensional bounded simplices containing zero. In the sheared coordinates the common intersection of the reversed weak inequalities has fiber $(z-r_0)B_0\cap(r_1-z)B_1$ for $r_0\le z\le r_1$ and is empty otherwise. At an endpoint the corresponding zero-right-side system gives $\{0\}$ in full rank; for a rank-deficient circuit that individual system gives the common kernel of its continuous normals.

## Proof

Subtract $y_0+(z-r_0)(y_1-y_0)/(r_1-r_0)$ from the continuous coordinate. This bijection fixes $z$ and preserves volume and the mixed lattice fiberwise. With both contacts at continuous coordinate zero, each margin of group $i$ becomes $u_{ij}\cdot x+\alpha_{ij}(z-r_i)$. Nonpositivity at the opposite contact forces $\alpha_{0j}\le0$ and $\alpha_{1j}\ge0$, giving the displayed forms.

For either group let $\lambda_j>0$ be its normalized dependence with sum one and write $t_j=c_j$ or $t_j=b_j$ as appropriate. If the kernel of the normals as a column matrix contained a direction independent of the strictly positive vector $\lambda$, one could move along that direction to a first zero coordinate without making any coordinate negative. The resulting nonzero dependence of smaller support contradicts minimality. Thus in rank $d$ there are $d+1$ normals, and the image of $x\mapsto (u_{ij}\cdot x)_j$ is the hyperplane $\lambda\cdot v=0$. The shear does not change the weighted base coefficient, as $\sum_j\lambda_j u_{ij}=0$. Hence the assumed nonzero projected coefficient implies $\bar t=\sum_j\lambda_j t_j>0$. Under the affine map $x\mapsto (t_j-u_{ij}\cdot x)_j$, the body $B_i$ corresponds bijectively to the weighted simplex $\{s\ge0:\lambda\cdot s=\bar t\}$. Since the $t_j$ are nonnegative, $0\in B_i$.

Summing the reversed inequalities with each group's positive weights gives $z\ge r_0$ and $z\le r_1$. At an interior base, dividing the two groups by the respective positive scalars $z-r_0$ and $r_1-z$ gives the claimed intersection, which contains zero. If one scalar vanishes, the corresponding group reduces to $u_{ij}\cdot x\le0$ for all $j$. Its positively weighted sum is zero, so all these inequalities are equalities. This gives the common kernel in general and the singleton in full rank, as claimed.
