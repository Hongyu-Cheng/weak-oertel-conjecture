## Statement

Let $C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$ be bounded and convex with compact nonempty integer fibers, where $n\ge0$ and $d\ge1$. Write $P$ for its positive-$d$-volume integer bases, $V_z=|C_z|_d$ and $r_z=V_z^{1/d}$ on $P$. Fix $p\in P$ and $y\in C_p$. Write $D$ for the raw ambient depth at $(p,y)$ under the sum of $d$-volumes of the integer fibers, and $f$ for the ordinary raw depth of $y$ in $C_p$. For $k\ge0$ define
$$
\Phi_k(b)=\sum_{z\in P}\min\{V_z,[k+b\cdot(z-p)]_+^d\},\qquad b\in\mathbb R^n,
$$
where $[t]_+=\max(t,0)$.

Suppose $u\ne0$ gives a proper through-$y$ cap in $C_p$ with raw mass $q\in(0,V_p)$ and $k=q^{1/d}$. For every $b$ there is a closed ambient through-$(p,y)$ halfspace with continuous normal $u$ whose capture on each positive fiber is at most its summand in $\Phi_k(b)$. In particular $D\le\min_b\Phi_k(b)$. Also $D\le\min_b\Phi_{f^{1/d}}(b)\le |P|f$. Both minima are attained at finite slopes, including the zero-budget case at $b=0$ when $f=0$.

If an actual depth-attaining cap through $(p,y)$ with nonzero continuous normal has captured volume roots $a_z=a_p+\ell\cdot(z-p)$ satisfying $0<a_z<r_z$ on all of $P$, then $\ell$ minimizes $\Phi_{a_p}$ globally with minimum $Q=D=\sum_z a_z^d$. One has $\sum_z (z-p)a_z^{d-1}=0$. For $d\ge2$ this gives
$$
|P|a_p^d-Q=\sum_z(a_p-a_z)^2\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}(j+1)a_p^{d-2-j}a_z^j.
$$
For $d=1$ it gives $\sum_z(z-p)=0$ and $Q=|P|a_p$.

## Proof

Fix $b$ and put $q_z=[k+b\cdot(z-p)]_+^d$. Constrain only the bases with $q_z<V_z$. For such a base with positive $q_z$, let $h_z$ be the upper-tail quantile with mass $q_z$ in direction $u$. The tail is continuous because hyperplanes have zero $d$-volume, and is strictly decreasing between its support levels because every intermediate open slab meets the interior. If $q_z=0$ choose the maximum support level. The cap $E_z=C_z\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge h_z\}$ is in all cases nonempty and compact with volume $q_z$. At $p$ this threshold is $s=u\cdot y$.

For any convex combination of constrained bases with barycenter $p$, convexity gives
$$
\sum_z\lambda_z E_z\subseteq C_p\cap\{x:u\cdot x\ge\sum_z\lambda_z h_z\},\qquad
\Big|\sum_z\lambda_z E_z\Big|_d^{1/d}
\ge \sum_z\lambda_z q_z^{1/d}\ge k.
$$
The first lower inequality is Brunn–Minkowski, including nonempty zero-volume summands by applying it after adding an $\varepsilon$ ball to each set and taking decreasing compact outer parallel sets to their volume limits. The last inequality uses $[t]_+\ge t$ and the barycenter $p$. Thus the source tail at the averaged threshold has volume at least $q$. Because $q$ is proper and the source tail is strictly decreasing between support levels, $\sum_z\lambda_z h_z\le s$.

On the constrained bases put auxiliary intervals $[L,h_z]$ for one $L$ smaller than all $h_z$. The preceding inequality is precisely the anchored Minkowski inclusion into $[L,s]$ at $p$. Apply 155691fc3768c06f with the positive unit normal in the auxiliary dimension one. It gives a finite $a$ with $h_z\le s+a\cdot(z-p)$ on every constrained base. The ambient halfspace $u\cdot x\ge s+a\cdot(z-p)$ through $(p,y)$ thus captures at most $q_z$ on those fibers and at most $V_z$ on every other positive fiber. Its continuous normal is $u$, and its tilt $a$ comes from the quantile constraints. This proves the pointwise cap realization and budget bound. Apply faf0509574789c00 to the same $P,p,k,d,V_z$ for attainment.

Ordinary depth is attained over through-point unit normals by compactness and dominated convergence, as hyperplane boundaries have zero mass. Opposite caps have volumes summing to $V_p$, so an attaining normal for $f>0$ gives a proper cap and the preceding bound. Its comparison with $b=0$ gives $|P|f$. If $f=0$, take the compact convex hull of the finitely many compact nonempty lifted integer fibers. It is contained in $C$ and preserves every sampled fiber. Apply 39f796c5a2993690 with intrinsic dimension $d$ to give $D=0$. The nonnegative objective $\Phi_0$ equals zero at $b=0$.

For an actual critical cap as in the statement, the through-mark budget bound with $k=a_p$ and its own continuous normal gives $Q=D\le\min_b\Phi_{a_p}(b)\le\Phi_{a_p}(\ell)=Q$. Every sampled affine value at $\ell$ lies strictly between its clipping levels, so differentiating there gives $\sum_z (z-p)a_z^{d-1}=0$. In particular $\sum_z(a_z-a_p)a_z^{d-1}=0$. For $d\ge2$, apply the identity
$$
x^d-y^d-dy^{d-1}(x-y)=(x-y)^2\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}(j+1)x^{d-2-j}y^j
$$
with $x=a_p$ and $y=a_z$, and sum over $z$. The linear term cancels by the balance. For $d=1$ the balance becomes $\sum_z(z-p)=0$, and summing the affine expression for $a_z$ gives $Q=|P|a_p$.
