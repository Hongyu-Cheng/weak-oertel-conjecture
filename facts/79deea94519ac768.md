## Statement

Let $A,K,B$ be full-dimensional polygons at bases $-1,0,1$ with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Fix $y_0\in\operatorname{int}K$ and three spanning normals $v_i$ summing to zero. Suppose the caps with thresholds $2v_i\cdot y_0-h_i$, $v_i\cdot y_0$, $h_i=h_B(v_i)$ on $A,K,B$ have common raw mass $Q$ and proper $A,K$ cuts. Define $p_i$ as the normalized reciprocals of $2\sigma_{A,i}+\sigma_{K,i}$, where each $\sigma$ is its cutting chord length divided by $\lVert v_i\rVert$, and let $W_A,W_K$ be the weighted sums of the three old cap indicators on the respective polygons. Set $M=|A|+|K|+|B|$ and $r=Q/M$.

Suppose an outward unit normal $n$ has $h_A(n)+h_B(n)=2h_K(n)$ and $F_A$ is a nondegenerate exposed edge. For an apex $p\in\operatorname{relint}F_A$ use the actual enlarged $A_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}(A\cup\{p+\varepsilon n\})$. In a common unit tangential coordinate write $F_K=[a,b]$, $I_0=(F_A+F_B)/2=[a_0,b_0]$ and $J=(p+F_B)/2=[c,d]$, with $a\le a_0<c\le d<b_0\le b$. For every $(q_L,q_R)\in[a,a_0]\times[b_0,b]$, keep all other $K$ facets and replace its old height constraint $t=n\cdot x-h_K(n)\le0$ by
$$
t\le\frac{\varepsilon}{2}G(s),\qquad
G(s)=\min\left(1,\frac{s-q_L}{c-q_L},\frac{q_R-s}{q_R-d}\right).
$$
For small positive $\varepsilon$ the resulting polygons with $B$ unchanged are actual compatible fibers. With $T_p$ the unit tent on $F_A$ with apex $p$, the common moving middle-cap budget divided by the total mass has right derivative
$$
\frac1M\left[\int_{F_A}T_p(W_A-r)\,ds+\frac12\int_a^b G(s)(W_K(s)-r)\,ds\right].
$$
For every real integrable $f$ on $[a,b]$, the minimum of $\frac12\int_a^b Gf$ over the parameter rectangle is attained at one of $\{a,a_0\}\times\{b_0,b\}$. This minimum concerns the middle cost for the fixed apex and the stated roof family.

## Proof

The midpoint of $A_\varepsilon$ and $B$ is the convex hull of the old midpoint polygon and the new seed $(p+\varepsilon n+B)/2$. The old exposed face is $I_0$, where $G\ge0$, and the new seed has exposed face $J$ at height $\varepsilon/2$, where $G=1$. The other vertices of these two polygons have fixed negative limiting $t$. For every other $K$ facet normal $u$, the apex $p$ strictly satisfies $u\cdot p<h_A(u)$ because the outward normal cone at the relative interior of the edge is the ray generated by $n$. Consequently the entire limiting seed satisfies $u\cdot (p+B)/2<h_K(u)$, using old compatibility. All these strict inequalities persist for the finitely many vertices and facets at sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. The midpoint inclusion follows. The labeled convex hull then realizes exactly these three integer fibers.

The new middle polygon is uniformly bounded by the old other facets and $t\le\varepsilon/2$. Every fixed compact subset of the old interior remains interior for small $\varepsilon$, and every limit point belongs to the old polygon by the defining inequalities. The symmetric difference lies in a strip $|t|=O(\varepsilon)$ since $G$ is bounded on the common bounded domain. Outside tangential intervals of length $O(\varepsilon)$ about the two face endpoints the other facet slacks on the old edge are sufficient for the entire signed strip from $0$ to $\varepsilon G(s)/2$. The two adjacent nonparallel facets also confine this strip's possible extra tangential extent to $O(\varepsilon)$ past the endpoints. Thus the difference from the signed face-strip area formula is $O(\varepsilon^2)$. On $A$ the addition is the exact triangle over $F_A$ since its apex has strict slack in every other old facet. A proper cap parallel to an old edge has fixed positive distance from the strip. For a transverse cut only a tangential interval of length $O(\varepsilon)$ around the old crossing can disagree with the old face signature, including for threshold shifts of size $O(\varepsilon)$. Hence the cap increment formula for the strips is uniform at those shifts.

The scalar inverse-level argument in 4c54e089191137d9 applies to these same old caps, proper section slopes, uniformly bounded area changes of order $\varepsilon$, and convergent polygons with a fixed interior disk about $y_0$. In each row the old threshold slope at the middle level is $-(2\sigma_{A,i}+\sigma_{K,i})$, because the $A$ threshold shifts twice the middle shift and the $B$ support stays fixed. Equalizing the row masses with the sum-zero level constraint gives the response weighted by the $p_i$. The three spanning normals summing to zero give an actual nearby meeting point with these levels. The common raw budget derivative is therefore $\int_{F_A}T_pW_A\,ds+\frac12\int_a^b GW_K\,ds$, and the total mass derivative is $\int_{F_A}T_p\,ds+\frac12\int_a^b G\,ds$. The quotient rule gives the displayed coefficient. If applying this coefficient to global depth, the endpoint gaps still have to be checked. For example, with the old $Q$ global, a strict $A$ gap and a tied unchanged $B$ require positive total mass derivative and negative displayed numerator for a strictly negative first-order upper certificate: its numerator is the maximum of the displayed numerator and minus $r$ times the mass derivative. The middle caps cover the whole plane by the balanced normals and the unchanged endpoint depth is $Q$.

On $[a,c]$ the left ramp is the minimum because it is at most one while the right ramp is at least one. On $[d,b]$ the right ramp is the minimum by the corresponding comparison. The plateau on $[c,d]$ is fixed, and the two remaining integrals depend independently on $q_L,q_R$. Differentiating the two affine quotients gives
$$
\frac{d}{dq_L}\int_a^c \frac{s-q_L}{c-q_L}f(s)\,ds
=\frac{\int_a^c (s-c)f(s)\,ds}{(c-q_L)^2},
\qquad
\frac{d}{dq_R}\int_d^b \frac{q_R-s}{q_R-d}f(s)\,ds
=\frac{\int_d^b (s-d)f(s)\,ds}{(q_R-d)^2}.
$$
Each sign is constant over its compact parameter interval. Each integral thus has a minimum at an endpoint, independently of the other. This includes a point plateau and degenerate parameter intervals.
