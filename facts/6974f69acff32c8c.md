## Statement

Let \(n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\) and \(d\in\mathbb Z_{\geq2}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and use the notation of accepted fact `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`:
\[
 C_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z),\qquad
 Z:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\]
\[
 W:=\sum_{z\in Z}a_z,\qquad
 G:=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C),\qquad
 \gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d.
\]
For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\) and
\(0<b\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), write
\[
 \mathsf M_b(K):=
 \left\{
 \frac1b\int_Kx f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_Kf(x)\,dx=b
 \right\}.
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and
\(y\in\operatorname{conv}E\), write
\[
 \Lambda_y(E):=
 \left\{\lambda\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_z=1,\ \sum_{z\in E}\lambda_zz=y\right\}.
\]
Assume
\[
 0<G<\frac W2.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(p\) be any saturated maximum free matching satisfying conclusion 1 of `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`. Thus
\[
 t_z:=2d_z(p)\quad(z\in Z),\qquad
 T:=\sum_{z\in Z}t_z,
\]
where \(t_z\) is extended by zero away from the positive-degree support, and
\[
 U:=\{z\in Z:t_z<a_z\},\qquad
 t_z=a_z\ (z\in Z\setminus U),\qquad
 \sum_{z\in U}a_z\leq G,\qquad
 W-T\leq G.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(m\) be any admissible integral centerpoint for this saturated profile. Choose any support-minimal balanced negative circuit and any associated valid exact affine minorants furnished by accepted fact `07009c5047a9f7bb`, and fix one circuit index. Write its nonzero direction, affine minorant, and individual cap as
\[
 w\in\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\},\qquad
 \ell:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R,\qquad
 A:=C\cap\{(z,x):\langle w,x\rangle\geq\ell(z)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(z\in Z\), put
\[
 q_z:=a_z-\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z),\qquad
 s_z:=\min\left\{t_z,\frac{q_z}{1-\gamma_d}\right\},\qquad
 S:=\sum_{z\in Z}s_z.
\tag{4}
\]
Then the following assertions hold.

1. For every \(z\in Z\) with \(s_z>0\), the compact convex boundary section
\[
 F_z:=\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)
 \cap\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\langle w,x\rangle=\ell(z)\}
\tag{5}
\]
is nonempty.

2. The individual cap obeys the exact selected-mass loss charge
\[
 W-W(A)\leq G+(1-\gamma_d)S.
\tag{6}
\]

3. Fix \(\eta\in(0,1]\) and assume additionally that
\[
 R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\leq(1-\eta)W,
 \qquad
 G\leq\frac{\eta}{6}W,
\tag{7}
\]
where \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}\) is precisely the descendant-retention capacity defined in `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`. For \(z\in Z\setminus U\), let \(c_z\) be the centroid of \(C_z\) and put
\[
 \sigma_z:=\langle w,c_z\rangle-\ell(z),
 \qquad
 r_z:=h_{\mathsf M_{a_z/2}(C_z)-c_z}(w).
\tag{8}
\]
The exact-minorant inequality and saturation give \(\sigma_z\geq0\) on
\(Z\setminus U\). Define the near-contact halo and its selected support by
\[
 H:=\left\{z\in Z\setminus U:
 \sigma_z\leq\frac{2}{\eta}r_z\right\},
 \qquad
 E_H:=\{z\in H:s_z>0\},
 \qquad
 S_H:=\sum_{z\in E_H}s_z.
\tag{9}
\]
Then
\[
 S_H\geq\frac{\eta}{3}W>G.
\tag{10}
\]
There exists \(\widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E_H\) such that every proper closed base halfspace \(J\ni\widehat m\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_H\cap J}s_z\geq2^{-n}S_H.
\tag{11}
\]
For every
\[
 \widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E_H
\tag{12}
\]
satisfying the centerpoint inequalities (11), the halo metronoid complete core is empty:
\[
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_H)}
 \sum_{z\in E_H}\lambda_z\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)
 =\varnothing.
\tag{13}
\]
Moreover, choose any \(u\in\mathbb R^d\) with \(\langle w,u\rangle=1\) and define
\[
 \widehat F_z:=F_z-\ell(z)u\subseteq w^\perp
 \qquad(z\in E_H).
\tag{14}
\]
For every integral \(\widehat m\) in (12) satisfying (11), the translated boundary sections have empty complete core in \(w^\perp\):
\[
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_H)}
 \sum_{z\in E_H}\lambda_z\widehat F_z
 =\varnothing.
\tag{15}
\]
The construction and conclusions above concern the individual cap \(A\) only; they do not assert an analogous boundary construction for any positive same-base circuit reweighting.

## Proof

We first prove the one-fiber boundary assertion used in part 1. Let \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body of volume \(a\), let \(0<t\leq a\), let \(w\ne0\), and suppose
\[
 L\leq h_{\mathsf M_t(K)}(w).
\tag{16}
\]
Put
\[
 q:=a-\operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(K\cap\{x:\langle w,x\rangle\geq L\}\bigr),
 \qquad
 s:=\min\left\{t,\frac q{1-\gamma_d}\right\},
\tag{17}
\]
and assume \(s>0\). We show that \(\mathsf M_s(K)\) meets the level \(\{\langle w,x\rangle=L\}\).

Let \(K_s^-\) be the closed bottom \(w\)-cap of \(K\) having volume \(s\), and let \(c_s^-\) be its centroid. The same threshold-selection and bathtub comparison used in accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives
\[
 \langle w,c_s^-\rangle
 =\min_{y\in\mathsf M_s(K)}\langle w,y\rangle.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed, for an admissible density \(f\), subtracting its \(w\)-moment from that of the bottom-cap indicator and then subtracting the threshold times the common mass makes the integrand pointwise nonpositive. Reversing the direction gives the analogous exact top-cap support formula.

If \(q\geq s\), the bottom cap of mass \(s\) is contained, up to its null boundary hyperplane, in \(\{x:\langle w,x\rangle\leq L\}\), so (18) is at most \(L\). Suppose instead that \(q<s\). Every level hyperplane has zero \(d\)-volume, and the sublevel-volume function is continuous; hence the threshold of \(K_s^-\) is strictly above \(L\). Thus the bottom cap contains all of the part of \(K\) strictly below level \(L\), and
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(K_s^-\cap\{x:\langle w,x\rangle\geq L\}\bigr)
 =s-q\leq\gamma_ds,
\tag{19}
\]
where the last inequality follows from \((1-\gamma_d)s\leq q\), a consequence of (17). If \(\langle w,c_s^-\rangle>L\), apply the full-mass case of `6769574fc1bd9f8d` to the convex body \(K_s^-\). Its centroid halfspace gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_d\bigl(K_s^-\cap
 \{x:\langle w,x\rangle\geq\langle w,c_s^-\rangle\}\bigr)
 \geq\gamma_ds.
\tag{20}
\]
The centroid of a full-dimensional convex body is an interior point. Since its \(w\)-coordinate is strictly above \(L\), an interior neighborhood supplies positive volume in the slab between these two levels. Therefore the left side of (19) is strictly larger than the left side of (20), contradicting (19)-(20). Thus in every case
\[
 \min_{y\in\mathsf M_s(K)}\langle w,y\rangle\leq L.
\tag{21}
\]

Because \(s\leq t\), the exact top-cap support formula also gives
\[
 h_{\mathsf M_s(K)}(w)\geq h_{\mathsf M_t(K)}(w)\geq L.
\tag{22}
\]
For completeness, the first inequality follows by writing the top cap of mass \(t\) as the top cap of mass \(s\) together with a lower layer of mass \(t-s\): the average \(w\)-coordinate on the top \(s\) mass is at least the average on their union. Accepted fact `6769574fc1bd9f8d` gives that \(\mathsf M_s(K)\) is nonempty, compact, and convex. Its image under \(y\mapsto\langle w,y\rangle\) is therefore the interval between the extrema in (21)-(22), so it contains \(L\). This proves the one-fiber assertion.

Return to the saturated circuit. For every \(z\) with \(s_z>0\), one has \(t_z>0\), hence \(z\) lies in the selected matching support. The exact-minorant property from `07009c5047a9f7bb` gives
\[
 \ell(z)\leq h_{\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z)}(w).
\tag{23}
\]
Apply the one-fiber assertion with \((K,t,L,q,s)=(C_z,t_z,\ell(z),q_z,s_z)\). It proves (5).

We next prove the loss charge. If \(t_z=0\), then \(s_z=0\) and trivially
\[
 q_z\leq a_z=(a_z-t_z)+(1-\gamma_d)s_z.
\tag{24}
\]
Suppose \(t_z>0\). If the second entry realizes the minimum in (4), then
\(q_z=(1-\gamma_d)s_z\). If the first entry realizes the minimum, then \(s_z=t_z\), while the individual-cap estimate in `07009c5047a9f7bb` gives
\(\operatorname{vol}_d(A_z)\geq\gamma_dt_z\). Consequently, in either case,
\[
 q_z\leq(a_z-t_z)+(1-\gamma_d)s_z.
\tag{25}
\]
Summing (24)-(25) and using (2) yields
\[
 W-W(A)=\sum_{z\in Z}q_z
 \leq W-T+(1-\gamma_d)S
 \leq G+(1-\gamma_d)S,
\]
which is (6).

Assume now (7). The individual cap \(A\) belongs to the descendant collection defining \(R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)\) in `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`. Hence
\[
 \sum_{z\in Z}q_z=W-W(A)
 \geq W-R_{\mathrm{desc}}(C)
 \geq\eta W.
\tag{26}
\]
On the exceptional set, (2) gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in U}q_z\leq\sum_{z\in U}a_z\leq G.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(z\in Z\setminus(U\cup H)\), one has
\(\sigma_z>(2/\eta)r_z\). The dimension-free one-fiber estimate in conclusion 2 of `f23ff6dd60fbbc37`, applied exactly as in its optimized near-contact proof, gives
\[
 q_z\leq a_z\frac{r_z}{\sigma_z}
 <\frac{\eta}{2}a_z.
\tag{28}
\]
Its zero-slack convention causes no exception: a site outside \(H\) has \(\sigma_z>0\). Equations (7) and (26)-(28) imply
\[
 \sum_{z\in H}q_z
 \geq\eta W-G-\frac{\eta}{2}W
 \geq\frac{\eta}{3}W.
\tag{29}
\]
Every \(z\in H\) is saturated, so \(t_z=a_z\). Since \(0\leq q_z\leq a_z\) and \(q_z/(1-\gamma_d)\geq q_z\), equation (4) gives \(s_z\geq q_z\) on \(H\). Therefore (29) proves
\[
 S_H\geq\sum_{z\in H}q_z\geq\frac{\eta}{3}W>G,
\]
which is (10), and in particular \(E_H\ne\varnothing\).

Apply accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c` to the finite atomic measure
\(\sum_{z\in E_H}s_z\delta_z\) on \(\mathbb Z^n\), using its \(d=0\) specialization. This supplies an integral lattice point \(\widehat m\) satisfying (11); positive depth also forces \(\widehat m\in\operatorname{conv}E_H\). Now fix any
\[
 \widehat m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E_H
\]
satisfying (11). If the metronoid core in (13) were nonempty, the arbitrary-selected-mass nonempty-core branch of `07009c5047a9f7bb`, whose center hypothesis is now met by this integral \(\widehat m\), applied to the profile \((s_z)_{z\in E_H}\) would give \(G\geq S_H\). This contradicts (10), proving (13) for every integral centerpoint quantified in (12).

Part 1 gives nonempty \(F_z\subseteq\mathsf M_{s_z}(C_z)\) for every \(z\in E_H\). Hence, for each such integral \(\widehat m\),
\[
 \bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_H)}
 \sum_{z\in E_H}\lambda_zF_z=\varnothing
\tag{30}
\]
by (13). Affinity of \(\ell\) and the barycentric identities defining \(\Lambda_{\widehat m}(E_H)\) give, for every such \(\lambda\),
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_H}\lambda_zF_z
 =\ell(\widehat m)u+
 \sum_{z\in E_H}\lambda_z\widehat F_z.
\tag{31}
\]
Also \(\widehat F_z\subseteq w^\perp\) by (5) and \(\langle w,u\rangle=1\). The translation in (31) is independent of \(\lambda\), so (30) is equivalent to (15) for the same integral \(\widehat m\). This proves all assertions.
