## Statement

Let \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge3\) be integers. Define the convex function \(g:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) and the finite lattice support by

\[
g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n\max\{-x_i,0,x_i-1\},
\qquad D=\{\mathbf I\in\mathbb Z^n:g(\mathbf I)\le1\}.
\]

Define the complete table, including all its zeros, by

\[
p_{\mathbf I}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k2^n},&\mathbf I\in\{0,1\}^n,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{kn2^n},&\mathbf I\in D\setminus\{0,1\}^n,\\
0,&\mathbf I\notin D.
\end{cases}
\]

This is a positive probability table on \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\), is invariant under every reflection \(I_i\mapsto1-I_i\), and has a finite-valued globally log-concave extension agreeing with every lattice value. Retain the original normalization of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`, namely \(w=p\), \(W=1\), \(w_{\max}=\max_Dp\), \(\phi_{\mathbf I}=\log(w_{\max}/p_{\mathbf I})\), and \(B=\max_D\phi\). Here

\[
B=\log\bigl(n(k-1)\bigr)>0,
\qquad
\phi_{\mathbf I}=B g(\mathbf I)\quad(\mathbf I\in D),
\qquad
\mathbb E_p\phi=\frac Bk.
\]

Use exactly the variance-minimal entropy optimizer \(r^\star\) of accepted fact `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`: the nonnegative arrays \(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\), indexed by \(D\times D\), satisfy \(\sum_{\mathbf J}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}=p_{\mathbf I}\) and \(\sum_{\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}(\mathbf I-\mathbf J)=\mathbf0\); first minimize \(\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\|\mathbf J\|^2\), and then maximize \(-\sum_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}\log(r_{\mathbf J,\mathbf I}/p_{\mathbf I})\), with \(0\log0=0\). Write \(H_\star=H(r^\star)\) for this exact conditional assignment entropy. Then

\[
\frac{1}{2k}\log(kn)
\le H_\star
\le\frac1k\log(kn)+\frac2k.
\]

For the original integral hitting quantity of `488f0a583886b5fc`, at the original integer target \(\mathbf0\), the exact low-cutoff mass is

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;p)=p_{\mathbf0}
=\frac{k-1}{k}2^{-n}
\qquad\left(0\le L<\frac B2\right).
\]

Explicitly, this \(m_{\log}\) minimizes \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in F}p_{\mathbf I}\) over subsets \(F\subseteq D\) that hit every nonempty affinely independent \(E\subseteq D\) having \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}E\) and \(\sum_{\mathbf I\in E}\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\phi_{\mathbf I}\le L\), where the \(\lambda_{E,\mathbf I}\) are its positive barycentric coefficients. Every support uses the same original capacities \(p_{\mathbf I}\); no row capacities or per-orbit restriction is imposed.

In particular, for every \(C,c>0\), choose any integer \(k\ge3\) with \(k>4C\), and any integer \(n\ge1\) with

\[
\log(kn)>\max\left\{4C+2+2\log2,\frac{2k}{c}\right\}.
\]

The displayed table satisfies \(C(1+H_\star)<B/2\) and

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,C(1+H_\star);p)
=\frac{k-1}{k}2^{-n}
<c(1+H_\star)2^{-n}.
\]

Thus there do not exist universal constants \(C,c>0\) satisfying the proposed fixed-cutoff gain for the reflection class. Nevertheless every table in this family satisfies

\[
\max_{L\ge0}\frac{m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;p)}{1+L}
\ge\frac{k-1}{k}2^{-n}
\ge\frac23\,2^{-n}.
\]

## Proof

Each function \(x_i\mapsto\max\{-x_i,0,x_i-1\}\) is convex, nonnegative, and invariant under \(x_i\mapsto1-x_i\). Thus \(g\) is convex and invariant under all the required reflections. Its sublevel set \(\{g\le1\}\) is convex and is contained in \([-1,2]^n\). This proves finiteness of \(D\) and symmetry of its complete indicator. Since \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\subseteq\{g\le1\}\), every integer point in \(\operatorname{conv}(D)\) belongs to \(D\); the reverse inclusion is automatic. Hence \(D=\operatorname{conv}(D)\cap\mathbb Z^n\).

At a lattice point, every summand of \(g\) is a nonnegative integer. The sites with \(g=0\) are exactly the \(2^n\) cube vertices. A site with \(g=1\) has exactly one coordinate in \(\{-1,2\}\) and every other coordinate in \(\{0,1\}\). There are exactly \(n2^n\) such sites. Therefore the total mass of the displayed table is \((k-1)/k+1/k=1\). Its positive entries are rational, and its maximum is the cube value because \(n(k-1)>1\).

The required extension is the explicit finite-valued function

\[
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k2^n}\exp(-B g(x)),&g(x)\le1,\\
0,&g(x)>1.
\end{cases}
\]

On its convex positive support, convexity of \(g\) proves log-concavity of \(f\). If either endpoint in the global log-concavity inequality has value zero, its right side is zero, so the inequality also holds in that case. At every lattice point of \(D\), its value agrees with \(p\), because \(\exp(-B)=1/[n(k-1)]\); at every other lattice point both values are zero. This proves all global extendability, support, and reflection hypotheses. It also proves the stated normalized potential and \(\mathbb E_p\phi=B/k\).

To compute the original hitting mass, use the exact closed lower-cap description (15) of accepted fact `488f0a583886b5fc`. Since \(\phi_{\mathbf0}=0\), it reads

\[
m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;p)=
\min_{\substack{(\mathbf b,t)\ne(\mathbf0,0)\\t\ge0}}
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf I\in D\\
\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf I+t(L-\phi_{\mathbf I})\ge0}}
p_{\mathbf I}.
\]

Every one of these caps contains \(\mathbf0\): its score there is \(tL\ge0\). This includes every normal with \(t=0\) and every equality case. Consequently every admissible cap has original weight at least \(p_{\mathbf0}\). Equivalently, the zero-cost singleton \(\{\mathbf0\}\) belongs to the full support family and forces every hitting set to contain the target.

Now fix \(0\le L<B/2\), and take the actual finite normal \(\mathbf b=-(B/2)\mathbf1\), \(t=1\). At any cube vertex other than \(\mathbf0\), the sum of its coordinates is at least one, so its score is at most \(L-B/2<0\). At any site of \(D\setminus\{0,1\}^n\), the potential is \(B\) and the sum of its coordinates is at least \(-1\). Its score is therefore also at most
\(B/2+L-B=L-B/2<0\). At \(\mathbf0\) the score is \(L\ge0\). This closed cap contains exactly the target atom and proves the upper bound \(m_{\log}(\mathbf0,L;p)\le p_{\mathbf0}\). Together with the preceding lower bound it proves the asserted equality for the full original hitting quantity. At cutoff zero the target itself is an equality atom and is retained. The proof makes no assertion extending the formula to \(L=B/2\), where other equality atoms can enter.

It remains to bound the exact assignment entropy. Retain the original excess radii \(\mathbf Z=\mathbf R-\tfrac12\mathbf1\) and their joint folded law \(\nu\), with precisely the normalization in accepted fact `ff205d92ef0102e0`: \(\nu_{\mathbf z}=2^np_{\mathbf1+\mathbf z}\). For this table their entire joint law is

\[
\nu_{\mathbf0}=\frac{k-1}{k},
\qquad
\nu_{\mathbf e_i}=\frac1{kn}\quad(1\le i\le n),
\qquad
\nu_{\mathbf z}=0\quad
\bigl(\mathbf z\notin\{\mathbf0,\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_n\}\bigr).
\]

In particular, two different excess coordinates are never simultaneously positive. These correlations are retained throughout; \(\nu\) is not replaced by a product distribution. Put \(\delta=1/(kn)\). Every coordinate is expanded and has the exact marginal law \(\mathbb P_p(R_i=1/2)=1-\delta\), \(\mathbb P_p(R_i=3/2)=\delta\).

The parity probabilities in `ff205d92ef0102e0` are \(b_i=\delta\). Its accepted inequality \(\sum_i\mathsf h(b_i)\le2H_\star\), with \(\mathsf h(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)\), therefore gives

\[
H_\star\ge\frac n2\mathsf h(\delta)
\ge\frac{n\delta}{2}\log(1/\delta)
=\frac{1}{2k}\log(kn).
\]

For the upper bound, the exact formula in `eb9a8fb910b2faf3` applies to this correlated law. All its multipliers \(\theta_i\) equal a common \(\theta>0\), since all the original radius marginals are equal and the defining equation has a unique solution. Define the auxiliary scalar function

\[
F_\delta(t)=(1-\delta)\log(2\cosh(t/2))
+\delta\log(2\cosh(3t/2))-\frac t2
\qquad(t\ge0).
\]

The accepted entropy formula is \(H_\star=nF_\delta(\theta)\). Moreover

\[
F_\delta'(t)=\frac{1-\delta}{2}\tanh(t/2)
+\frac{3\delta}{2}\tanh(3t/2)-\frac12,
\qquad
F_\delta''(t)=\frac{1-\delta}{4\cosh^2(t/2)}
+\frac{9\delta}{4\cosh^2(3t/2)}>0.
\]

The defining multiplier equation says exactly \(F_\delta'(\theta)=0\), so \(F_\delta(\theta)\le F_\delta(t)\) for every \(t\ge0\). Take \(t=\log(1/\delta)>0\). Expanding the two hyperbolic cosines and then using \(\log(1+u)\le u\) for \(u\ge0\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_\delta(\theta)
&\le\delta\log(1/\delta)
+(1-\delta)\log(1+\delta)+\delta\log(1+\delta^3)\\
&\le\delta\log(1/\delta)+\delta+\delta^4\\
&\le\delta\log(1/\delta)+2\delta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Multiplication by \(n\) proves \(H_\star\le k^{-1}\log(kn)+2/k\). Use of a one-coordinate marginal in this formula is authorized by the exact entropy identity of `eb9a8fb910b2faf3`; no independence of the original radii or conditional row barycenter at a fixed radius is assumed.

Finally fix arbitrary proposed constants \(C,c>0\), and choose \(k,n\) as in the statement. Since \(k>4C\), the upper entropy bound implies

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(1+H_\star)
&\le C+\frac Ck\log(kn)+\frac{2C}{k}\\
&<C+\frac14\log(kn)+\frac12\\
&<\frac12\log(kn)-\frac12\log2\\
&\le\frac12\log\bigl(n(k-1)\bigr)=\frac B2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second strict inequality uses \(\log(kn)>4C+2+2\log2\); the last inequality uses \((k-1)/k\ge1/2\). The exact hitting calculation therefore applies at the proposed cutoff. The lower entropy bound and \(\log(kn)>2k/c\) give \(cH_\star>1\). Hence
\(c(1+H_\star)>1>(k-1)/k\), proving the strict violation in the statement. An integer \(n\) satisfying the two displayed strict bounds exists for every fixed \(C,c,k\), so this negates the asserted existence of universal constants rather than merely refuting one choice of constants.

At the same time, \(B>0\) makes cutoff zero available in the exact hitting formula. It gives the optimized-score lower bound \((k-1)2^{-n}/k\ge(2/3)2^{-n}\). This verifies the stated limitation of the counterexample: it obstructs the growing entropy factor at the prescribed cutoff, and does not obstruct a universal optimized original hitting score.
