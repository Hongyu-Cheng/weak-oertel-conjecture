## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ and let $A,B,K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be full-dimensional compact convex bodies with $(A+B)/2\subseteq K$. Let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$ and $H_i=\{x:u_i\cdot(x-y)\ge0\}$ for nonzero normals forming a minimal positive circuit. Suppose every endpoint cut is proper, every captured-side midpoint Brunn–Minkowski comparison is an equality, and every source cap $K\cap H_i$ has volume fraction $\Gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d$. Then $K=(A+B)/2$ is a $d$-simplex, $y$ is its centroid, and there are $d+1$ directions, one apex-side orientation for each opposite facet. The endpoints $A,B$ are positively homothetic simplices, and their full-body Brunn–Minkowski comparison is an equality.

## Proof

Positive balance makes the through-$y$ caps cover $K$. Properness gives positive endpoint cap volumes, so the equality cap cover lemma identifies $K=(A+B)/2$ and gives the cap-set equalities. For any point in $K$, a containing member of the cover bounds its normalized ordinary depth by $\Gamma_d$. By the ordinary maximal-depth rigidity theorem 03bc771bddd39a8a, $K$ is a simplex.

Let $g$ be the ordinary centroid. If $u_i\cdot(g-y)>0$, the parallel through-$g$ cap removes a slab of positive volume from $K\cap H_i$, since $g$ has an interior ball and the thresholds are distinct. The centroid cap bound then makes $|K\cap H_i|>\Gamma_d|K|$, a contradiction. Thus every $u_i\cdot(g-y)\le0$, and their positive balance forces all values to be zero. These are centroid equality caps. The single-cap equality characterization recorded in 03bc771bddd39a8a identifies each direction as the apex-side normal of a pyramid presentation of $K$. Since $K$ is a simplex, its base in any such presentation is the opposite facet of that apex. The corresponding direction is a positive multiple of the gradient of the apex's barycentric coordinate. Minimality excludes repeated positive directions, because their coefficients could be merged. The only dependence among the $d+1$ barycentric gradients is a scalar multiple of their sum, since the gradients have rank $d$. Therefore every one of the $d+1$ orientations must occur, and the orthogonality of $g-y$ to all of them gives $g=y$.

Apply e5152cdadf65331d with weights $1/2,1/2$ and enclosing simplex $S=K=(A+B)/2$. Its zero-deficit conclusion makes $A$ and $B$ positive homothets of $K$. Writing them as $v+\lambda K$ and $w+\mu K$, equality of their midpoint with $K$ forces $\lambda+\mu=2$ by volume, then $v+w=0$ by support addition. Thus $A=v+\lambda K$ and $B=-v+(2-\lambda)K$ for $0<\lambda<2$. Their volume roots are $\lambda|K|^{1/d}$ and $(2-\lambda)|K|^{1/d}$, so their full midpoint comparison is an equality.
