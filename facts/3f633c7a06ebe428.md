## Statement

Let $k\ge1$ be an integer. Consider the laws
$$
P_k(b)=\Pr\!\left(X\ge E_0+\sum_{i=1}^q b_iE_i\right),
\qquad 0\le b_i\le1,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^q b_i=k-1,
$$
where $X$ has the $\operatorname{Gamma}(k,1)$ law and $E_0,\ldots,E_q$ are unit-mean exponentials, with all variables independent. Here $q$ ranges over the finite nonnegative integers for which the coefficient constraints are feasible. For $k\ge2$, the fixed-$q$ minimum is
$$
A_{k,q}=\Pr\!\left(X\ge E_0+\frac{k-1}{q}\,G\right),
\qquad G\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(q,1)
$$
with $G$ independent. These minima strictly decrease as $q$ increases and converge to
$$
G_k=\Pr(X\ge E_0+k-1)
=e^{-(k-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-1)^j}{j!}\left(1-2^{-(k-j)}\right).
$$
Thus the infimum over all finite $q$ and all feasible $b$ is $G_k$, and $P_k(b)>G_k$ for every such $b$ when $k\ge2$. In particular $G_2=5/(4e)$ and $G_3=27/(8e^2)$. When $k=1$ every residual coefficient is zero, and $P_1(b)=G_1=1/2$, including the empty list.

## Proof

Fix $k\ge2$. The fixed-count equalization theorem gives the displayed $A_{k,q}$ for every feasible $q\ge k-1$. In dimension $q+1$, pad the equal $q$-vector by one zero. Its probability is $A_{k,q}$, but it is unequal because its first $q$ coordinates are positive. The strict equality criterion in the equalization theorem gives $A_{k,q+1}<A_{k,q}$.

For the independent variable $G$ of shape $q$, the threshold $(k-1)G/q$ has mean $k-1$ and variance $(k-1)^2/q$. For any $\epsilon>0$, it is within $\epsilon$ of $k-1$ except on an event whose probability tends to zero by Chebyshev's inequality. Outside this event, the sign indicator with threshold $(k-1)G/q$ lies between those with deterministic thresholds $k-1+\epsilon$ and $k-1-\epsilon$, for the variable $X-E_0$. This variable has no atoms, by conditioning on $E_0$ and using the continuous law of $X$. Taking $q\to\infty$ and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ gives the stated limit. Strict decrease gives $A_{k,q}>G_k$ for every finite $q$, and equal vectors approach $G_k$.

For $s\ge0$, conditioning on $X$ gives
$$
\Pr(X\ge E_0+s)=\Pr(X\ge s)-e^s\mathbb E[e^{-X}\mathbf 1_{\{X\ge s\}}].
$$
The gamma density and the substitution $u=2x$ give $\mathbb E[e^{-X}\mathbf 1_{\{X\ge s\}}]=2^{-k}\Pr(X\ge2s)$. Repeated integration by parts gives $\Pr(X\ge t)=e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}t^j/j!$ for $t\ge0$. Substitution and collection of the two sums gives
$$
\Pr(X\ge E_0+s)=e^{-s}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{s^j}{j!}(1-2^{j-k}),
$$
where the constant term uses $s^0=1$. Taking $s=k-1$ proves the formula and the stated values. If $k=1$, the nonnegative coefficients of total zero all vanish, and symmetry of the two independent continuous unit exponentials gives probability $1/2$.
