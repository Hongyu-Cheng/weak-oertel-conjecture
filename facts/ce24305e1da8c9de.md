## Statement

Fix $\mathbb R^m=\mathbb R^q\times\mathbb R^r$ orthogonally, with $0\le q<m$, $r=m-q$, and let $\pi$ be the first projection. For nonempty compact convex sets $K_z$ and a single $c>0$, define $K_z^\#$ by replacing every nonempty section over $x\in\pi(K_z)$ by the centered $r$-ball of $r$-volume $c$ times its original $r$-volume, retaining a singleton when that volume is zero. These are compact convex sets; each anchored finite inclusion $\sum_i\lambda_i K_{z_i}\subseteq K_j$ with $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$, is preserved. Moreover $|K_z^\#|=c|K_z|$ and $(\pi)_\#(\mathbf1_{K_z^\#}\,dx\,dy)=c(\pi)_\#(\mathbf1_{K_z}\,dx\,dy)$. Thus the corresponding labeled sums of fiber volume preserve every ambient closed-cap mass up to the factor $c$ when the hidden continuous normal vanishes, with arbitrary base tilt.

## Proof

Write $a_z(x)$ for the original $r$-volume over $x$ and $\kappa_r$ for the unit-ball volume. The replacement radius is $(c\,a_z(x)/\kappa_r)^{1/r}$. The volume function is upper semicontinuous: over $x_k\to x$, compactness puts all sufficiently late hidden sections in every fixed closed neighborhood of the section over $x$, and continuity from above of these neighborhood volumes gives the bound. Thus each new set is closed and bounded. Brunn–Minkowski for nonempty compact convex sections gives concavity of the radius for one body, and then convexity follows by the triangle inequality.

For an anchored inclusion and arbitrary projected points $x_i$, the Minkowski average of their original sections is in the target section at $x=\sum_i\lambda_i x_i$. Brunn–Minkowski in the common hidden dimension shows that the radius over $x$ in the target replacement is at least the weighted sum of the source replacement radii. This holds for nonempty sections of volume zero as well. The triangle inequality now proves the full anchored inclusion. Fubini gives the displayed projection and volume identities. An ambient cap with zero hidden normal restricts on each base to a set determined by $x$ alone, including full and empty traces; hence the identities apply for any base tilt.
