## Statement

At integer bases $0,1,2,3,4$ take total weights $w(z)=1$ and five scalar cap profiles $v_i(z)=\mathbf1_{\{i\}}(z)$, $0\le i\le4$. The total root $w^{1/2}$ is concave, each cap root extends continuously and concavely on its own nondegenerate positive-support interval in $[0,4]$, the profiles cover pointwise, and each cap budget is one fifth of the total, strictly below $2/9$. These profiles cannot be the actual closed ambient cap areas of a compact convex source with planar integer-fiber areas $w(z)$.

## Proof

The constant total root and the extension $v_i(t)^{1/2}=\max(1-10|t-i|,0)$ on $[0,4]$ have the claimed concavities on their respective positive domains, with the exact integer samples. Pointwise coverage and the $1/5$ normalized budgets follow by summation.

Suppose such a source and caps exist. Choose the cap for the interior site $i=2$. If it is pure-base, its zero area at $0$ and $4$ forces both bases strictly outside the cap and hence also excludes $2$. Otherwise its trace normal is nonzero. Zero area in each full-dimensional endpoint fiber implies both fibers lie in the closed complementary halfspace: a point strictly on the cap side and an interior ball in the same fiber would give positive cap area by convexity. The midpoint of the two lifted fibers lies in the same closed complementary halfspace and in the actual fiber at $2$. By Brunn–Minkowski it has area at least one, the whole area of that fiber. The nonvertical cap boundary has zero planar area there, so this cap cannot capture area one at $2$.
