## Statement

Let $D\subset\mathbb R^m$ be a convex body centrally symmetric about $0$, $m\ge1$, and let $r$ be positive and concave on $[a,b]$, with $a<0<b$. Assume $r$ is $C^2$ or continuous piecewise affine with finitely many pieces. Set $d=m+1$ and $K=\{(X,z):a\le z\le b,\ X\in r(z)D\}$. In coordinates $X=r(z)q$, $u=z/r(z)$, put $D_r=r-zr'$, $I=u([a,b])$ and $R(u)=1/r(z(u))$. Then $u$ is strictly increasing and $R$ is convex, with
$$
D_r\ge r(0)>0,\quad u'=\frac{D_r}{r^2},\quad \frac{dz}{du}=\frac{r^2}{D_r},\quad
R'=-\frac{r'}{D_r},\quad R''=-\frac{r^3r''}{D_r^3}\ge0,\quad R-uR'=\frac1{D_r}
$$
on smooth pieces. In the piecewise affine case $R$ is continuous piecewise affine. Volume in these coordinates is $dq\,w(u)du$ for $w(u)=(R-uR')R^{-d-1}$ on $I$ and zero off $I$.

For $c\in\mathbb R^m$, the through-origin cap with raw normal $(c,1)$ has condition $u+c\cdot q\ge0$. Its section with measure $d\mathcal H^m/\|(c,1)\|_2$ pulls back to $w(s)R(s)\,dq$ on $\{q\in D:s=-c\cdot q\in I\}$. Its horizontal first moment for this measure is $\int_D q\,w(-c\cdot q)dq$. The section centroid is $0$ exactly when this vector vanishes.

## Proof

At every differentiability point the supporting tangent at $z$ bounds $r(0)$ by $r(z)-zr'(z)$. The formulas for $u'$ and $dz/du$ follow by differentiation and show strict increase on the entire interval. Differentiating $R$ with respect to $u$ gives the stated formulas for $R'$, $R''$ and $R-uR'$. At a piece boundary the one-sided slopes of $r$ decrease. For fixed $z,r(z)$ the expression $-h/(r(z)-zh)$ is decreasing in $h$ on positive denominators, so the slopes of $R$ increase across the boundary. On each affine piece of $r$, $R'$ is constant. This proves the asserted convexity in both cases.

The volume Jacobian in $(q,z)$ is $r^m$, and the change to $u$ makes it $r^{m+2}/D_r=(R-uR')R^{-d-1}$. The cap condition follows by dividing $z+c\cdot X$ by $r(z)>0$. On the section $z=-c\cdot X$ its measure $d\mathcal H^m/\|(c,1)\|_2$ is precisely Lebesgue measure in $X$ coordinates, by the graph Jacobian. Its parametrization in the $q$ coordinates is $X=q/R(s)$ with $s=-c\cdot q$. It is injective, since $X$ fixes $z=-c\cdot X$ and then $q=X/r(z)$. The absolute Jacobian is
$$
R(s)^{-m}\bigl(1-sR'(s)/R(s)\bigr)=w(s)R(s)>0.
$$
For $c\ne0$ the finitely many piece boundaries and interval ends have preimages of $m$-measure zero. For $c=0$ the section is simply $r(0)D$ and $w(0)R(0)=r(0)^m$ for either one-sided slope. Multiplication by $X=q/R(s)$ gives the horizontal first moment. The vertical first moment is its pairing with $-c$, and the section measure is positive since $0$ is interior to $K$. Normalizing by a constant does not change the centroid.
