## Statement

Let $P=\operatorname{conv}(P)\cap\mathbb Z^n$ be finite and nonempty, and let the positive convex $d$-bodies $K_z$, $z\in P$, obey all anchored Minkowski inclusions. Mark any finite set of fibers for perturbation. Suppose every affected nonself extreme anchored inclusion has compact containment in the interior of its target. If $\Delta$ is the least of their support margins, simultaneous Hausdorff displacements smaller than $\Delta/3$ of the marked fibers preserve all anchored inclusions. If no nonself extreme inclusion is affected, there is no displacement restriction from compatibility. Any such compatible perturbed positive family has a full ambient convex realization with precisely the designated integer fibers, including when $\operatorname{aff}(P)$ has lower rank.

## Proof

For each target $p$ the polytope of probability vectors $\lambda$ on $P$ with barycenter $p$ has finitely many vertices. The identity vector at $p$ is tautological. For an affected nonself vertex, interior containment gives $h_{K_p}(u)-\sum_z\lambda_z h_{K_z}(u)>0$ for each unit $u$. These gaps are continuous on the compact unit sphere. The finite list has positive minimum $\Delta$ if it is nonempty. Hausdorff displacement below $\Delta/3$ changes each support by less than $\Delta/3$, and each weighted input sum changes by at most the maximum individual change. Thus the new gaps are positive; unaffected and self rows still hold. By linearity in $\lambda$ this checks every anchored probability vector.

Take the convex hull of the lifted perturbed fibers. Its projection is $\operatorname{conv}(P)$, and every point at $p\in P$ belongs to the designated fiber by grouping its convex representation by base and applying the anchored inclusion. If the affine rank is full, the hull is already a full-dimensional convex body. Otherwise fix $p_0\in P$ and let $E$ be the span of $P-p_0$. The orthogonal projection onto $E^\perp$ has rational matrix entries, since $E$ is spanned by integer vectors and projection onto their span is given by the inverse Gram matrix of a linearly independent sublist. In rank zero the transverse projection is the identity. Some positive integer $N$ clears the denominators. Every integer offset from $p_0$ with nonzero projection therefore has projected length at least $1/N$. Adding a closed ball of radius less than $1/N$ in $E^\perp$ in the base coordinates gives full dimension. Any integer base of the enlarged body must have zero transverse projection, so its added transverse vector is zero and its fiber is unchanged.
