## Statement

Let $d\ge2$ be an integer, $t=1/(d+1)$, and $\Gamma=(dt)^d$. For $a>0$ and $0\le\beta\le at$, put $r_h=(1-ah)_+$, $q_h=t-\beta h$, and $p_h=t+\beta(h+1)$ for integers $h\ge0$, where $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For $r,q>0$ set $E_d(q,r)=d^dq^{d-1}(r-dq)_+$. The diagonal-envelope deficit is
$$
D_d(a,\beta)=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge0} r_h^d-
\sum_{\substack{h\ge0\\r_h>0}}\bigl[E_d(q_h,r_h)+E_d(p_h,r_h)\bigr].
$$
Every term in the second sum has positive thresholds. For $1\le H\le d$ put
$$
a_H=\frac{H+1}{H(d+1)},\qquad
W_{d,H}=\sum_{h\ge0}(1-a_Hh)_+^d,\qquad
K_{d,H}=\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}(1-h/d)^{d-1}(1-h/H).
$$
The unrestricted maximum of $D_d(a,\beta)$ on the specified domain is attained and satisfies
$$
0<\max_{\substack{a>0\\0\le\beta\le at}}D_d(a,\beta)
=\Gamma\max_{1\le H\le d}(W_{d,H}-K_{d,H})
<\frac{\Gamma}{6}.
$$
Equivalently, the envelope sum for every admissible $(a,\beta)$ is strictly larger than $\Gamma\sum_h r_h^d-\Gamma/6$.

## Proof

For each positive radius we have $q_h\ge t(1-ah)=tr_h>0$ and $p_h>0$. When $a\ge1$ the only positive radius is $r_0=1$. Its left envelope is $E_d(t,1)=\Gamma$, and its right envelope is nonnegative. Thus $D_d(a,\beta)\le0$ for $a\ge1$.

At the admissible pair $a=2t$, $\beta=t/d$, the right threshold satisfies $p_h\ge p_0=1/d$, so every right envelope vanishes. At each positive radius with $h\ge1$, the left factor $r_h-dq_h=t(1-h)$ is nonpositive. Therefore the only nonzero envelope is $E_d(t,1)=\Gamma$, and
$$
D_d(2t,t/d)=\Gamma\sum_{h\ge1}(1-2th)_+^d>0
$$
since $2t<1$.

Choose $0<\varepsilon<\min\{1/(2d),\varepsilon_d\}$, where $\varepsilon_d$ is the positive cutoff of 8712af98d6b2c23e. That theorem gives $D_d(a,\beta)\le-\Gamma/4$ whenever $0<a\le\varepsilon$ and $0\le\beta\le at$. All pairs $(a_H,t/d)$ for $1\le H\le d$ belong to $\varepsilon\le a\le1$, $0\le\beta\le at$, since $1/d\le a_H\le2t<1$. This includes the positive witness with $H=1$. The compact reduction 4c65d2a05d1be51c gives an attained maximum on that truncated domain. Its value is positive, and the excluded small-slope region and $a\ge1$ cannot exceed it. It is therefore the unrestricted maximum.

The compact reduction bounds this value by the largest of zero, the maximum at $a=\varepsilon$, and the values at the double knots in the truncated domain. The edge value is negative by the choice of $\varepsilon$. Each positive-right-index knot has negative deficit by 3da9e3745e7c7879. The admissible knots of right index zero are exactly $(a_H,t/d)$ for $1\le H\le d$. Thus the unrestricted maximum is at most the largest of their values. Each such knot is admissible, so equality holds.

The zero-right-index theorem 9fa02d395df8157e evaluates each of these values as $\Gamma(W_{d,H}-K_{d,H})$ and bounds it strictly by $\Gamma/6$. There are finitely many of them, so the maximum is strictly below $\Gamma/6$. This proves the finite representation and the equivalent envelope bound.
