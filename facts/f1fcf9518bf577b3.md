## Statement

For each integer \(k\ge8\), fix the complete support of the log-affine specialization of 604889294a17d7df, as also used in 163ec41d3cfa86a9. Explicitly, set
$$
b_\ell=0\ (1\le\ell<8),\qquad
b_\ell=\left\lfloor\frac{2^\ell}{8\ell}\right\rfloor\ (8\le\ell\le k),\qquad
Q_0=0,\quad Q_\ell=\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}b_h,\quad Q=Q_k,
$$
$$
a_0=0,\qquad a_s=\ell\quad(Q_{\ell-1}<s\le Q_\ell),\qquad
m=2(Q+1)^4,\qquad n=\sum_{r=1}^{Q}m^r,\qquad N=n+1.
$$
Coordinates of the spatial tree are indexed by \(\mathcal T_{m,Q}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{Q}[m]^r\). Allow any symmetric saturated-chain partition \(\mathcal C\) of \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^m\), and use its injective partial successor \(F_m\) in the original tree construction. The symbol \(\mathcal C\) is the chain partition denoted by \(\Pi\) in 163ec41d3cfa86a9. A Boolean vector \(b\) has its original forward path \(u_1,\ldots,u_{h(b)}\), and its level-\(s\) point is \(z_s(b)=b+2\sum_{r=1}^{s}e_{u_r}\) for \(0\le s\le h(b)\). Thus the spatial support and full lifted support are exactly
$$
A_{m,Q}=\bigcup_b\lbrace z_s(b):0\le s\le h(b)\rbrace,\qquad
\widehat A_k=\lbrace(z,t):z\in A_{m,Q},\ a_{\operatorname{lev}(z)}\le t\le k,\ t\in\mathbb Z\rbrace.
$$
Here \(\operatorname{lev}(z)\) is the number of coordinates equal to two. These are the same \(m,Q,n,N,a_s\) and complete supports as in the cited predecessors, with every original height atom retained.

For a real spatial field \(\gamma\), retain exactly the path variation of dbbd63823b5f4870:
$$
\Pi(\gamma)=
\max_{\substack{u_0=\varnothing,\ u_r\in[m]^r\\u_r\text{ child of }u_{r-1}\ (1\le r\le Q)}}
\sum_{r=0}^{Q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\gamma_{u_ri}|.
$$
In particular this sums whole child blocks along a full rooted path. Assume \(\Pi(\gamma)\le1\). For arbitrary \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), give every point of \(\widehat A_k\) its positive original weight and full normalization
$$
w_{(z,t)}=e^{\gamma\cdot z+\beta t},\qquad
Z=\sum_{(z,t)\in\widehat A_k}e^{\gamma\cdot z+\beta t},\qquad
p_{(z,t)}=w_{(z,t)}/Z.
$$
The support and its parameters were fixed before choosing \(\gamma,\beta\).

Use the mixed-row convention of 8ab782832d1c7da2. At each supported center \(J\), a row is partitioned into the separately fixed target singleton \(\lbrace J\rbrace\) and any pairwise disjoint pairs or triples of distinct other supported points, each having arithmetic mean \(J\). Rows at different centers may overlap. Cycle each pair or triple and fix the target. For a source \(I\) in a cycle \(\mathcal O\), define
$$
\begin{aligned}
g_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)&=\left(\prod_{I'\in\mathcal O}p_{I'}\right)^{1/|\mathcal O|},
&H^{(\mathrm m)}(J)&=\sum_{I\in R_J^{(\mathrm m)}}g_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I),\\
\kappa_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)&=g_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)/H^{(\mathrm m)}(J),
&Y_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)&=\log\frac{p_I}{g_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)},\\
V_J^{(\mathrm m)}&=\sum_{I\in R_J^{(\mathrm m)}}\kappa_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)\bigl(Y_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)\bigr)^2,
&b_{\mathrm m}&=\sum_Jp_J\bigl(H^{(\mathrm m)}(J)\bigr)^2,\\
q_J^{(\mathrm m)}&=p_J\bigl(H^{(\mathrm m)}(J)\bigr)^2/b_{\mathrm m},
&\nu_{\mathrm m}&=\sum_Jq_J^{(\mathrm m)}V_J^{(\mathrm m)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are exactly its \(b^{(\mathrm m)},\nu^{(\mathrm m)}\), abbreviated by subscripts. Put \(A_0=4^N\sum_Jp_J^3\) and \(B=4^Nb_{\mathrm m}\).

There exists one finite absolute constant \(C\ge4\), independent of \(k,\mathcal C,\gamma,\beta\), such that every active law, meaning \(A_0<1\), admits these rows with
$$
B\ge1,\qquad \nu_{\mathrm m}\le C\log B.
\tag{1}
$$
For \(k\ge32\), the rows can be chosen using only target singletons and triples at height \(k\), with \(B>e\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\le4\). For \(8\le k<32\), the full reflected rows satisfy (1).

Consequently take any positive integer \(L\), arbitrary parameters \(k_i\ge8\), arbitrary allowed partitions \(\mathcal C_i\), arbitrary spatial fields \(\Pi(\gamma_i)\le1\), and arbitrary height fields \(\beta_i\). Let \(p_i\) be their full original laws just defined, \(P=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{L}p_i\), and \(\mathcal N=\sum_{i=1}^{L}N_{k_i}\). For actual closed-halfspace depth in the full original ambient space and its original-law moments,
$$
\tau_P(J)=\inf\lbrace P(H):H\subseteq\mathbb R^{\mathcal N}\text{ a closed halfspace containing }J\rbrace,
\qquad M_r(P)=\sum_JP_J\tau_P(J)^r,
$$

$$
M_2(P)\ge\frac{e^{-3C}}9\,4^{-\mathcal N},\qquad
M_r(P)\ge\left(\frac{e^{-3C/2}}3\right)^r2^{-r\mathcal N}\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{2}
$$
The same \(C\) works for every number of heterogeneous factors.

## Proof

We first verify the adaptive comparison on which the large-\(k\) construction depends. Let \(\mu_\theta\) be the Boolean product law on \(\lbrace0,1\rbrace^n\) assigning probability proportional to \(e^{\theta\cdot j}\), and write \(Z_{\mathrm B}(\theta)=\prod_u(1+e^{\theta_u})\). In particular \(\mu_0\) is the uniform Boolean law. These are auxiliary Boolean laws, not replacements for \(p\).

Use the exact center set \(G_Q\) and inverse-path algorithm of 3e23587245b6989f. At the root its Boolean block \(c\) must have two predecessors, written \(F_m^{-1}(c)=c-e_{v_1}\) and \(F_m^{-2}(c)=c-e_{v_1}-e_{u_1}\), with \(u_1\ne v_1\). In the two distinct root subtrees, query the child block at the current node, require it to have a predecessor, and descend along its removed coordinate, until both inverse paths reach depth \(Q\). A successful transcript specifies the root block and the \(2Q-2\) subsequently queried blocks in full. Its queried coordinate set \(\mathcal V\) is the union of the child blocks along the two resulting rooted paths, counting their common root block once.

The successful transcripts are disjoint Boolean cylinders. Indeed, their specified values determine each next query and both path choices, inductively. Every extension of those values gives the same successful transcript, with no condition on unqueried coordinates. Conversely a successful Boolean point gives a unique transcript. If two cylinder assignments admitted a common extension, this deterministic algorithm would give that extension two transcripts, which is impossible. For any one such cylinder with assigned values \(c_u\), its exact tilted-to-fair probability ratio is
$$
\frac{\mu_\theta(\text{cylinder})}{\mu_0(\text{cylinder})}
=\prod_{u\in\mathcal V}\frac{2e^{\theta_uc_u}}{1+e^{\theta_u}}
\ge\exp\left(-\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}|\theta_u|\right)
\ge e^{-2\Pi(\theta)}.
\tag{3}
$$
The coordinate inequality holds for either Boolean value, since \(\log((1+e^a)/2)\) lies between \(\min(0,a)\) and \(\max(0,a)\). The last inequality holds because each of the two full paths has block budget at most \(\Pi(\theta)\). All unqueried coordinates integrate to one under each product law; no global density comparison is being used. Summing (3) over the disjoint successful cylinders is therefore valid despite their adaptive, differing query locations.

For the prescribed \(m\), 3e23587245b6989f proves the exact fair count \(\mu_0(G_Q)=\alpha\eta^{2Q-2}\ge1/2\), where \(\eta=1-2^{-m}\binom m{m/2}\) and \(\alpha\) is its root two-predecessor fraction. Its estimate is uniform over all the allowed partitions. Substitution of \(\theta=3\gamma\) in (3) gives the required comparison
$$
\mu_{3\gamma}(G_Q)\ge\tfrac12e^{-6},
\tag{4}
$$
because \(\Pi(3\gamma)=3\Pi(\gamma)\le3\).

We next show that activity imposes one uniform lower bound on the height parameter. Set \(x=e^\beta\). In the source-to-level parametrization of 604889294a17d7df and dbbd63823b5f4870, each selected coordinate lies on one rooted path and in one of that path's child blocks. Thus
$$
\left|\gamma\cdot(z_s(b)-b)\right|
=\left|2\sum_{r=1}^{s}\gamma_{u_r}\right|\le2.
\tag{5}
$$
For \(s\ge1\), injectivity of this parametrization therefore gives
\(Z_s(\gamma):=\sum_{z:\operatorname{lev}(z)=s}e^{\gamma\cdot z}\le e^2Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)\), while \(Z_0(\gamma)=Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)\). This only bounds each level sum and does not assert that the spatial field has bounded total absolute value.

If \(0<x\le1/4\), the original full normalizer consequently obeys
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Z}{Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)}
&\le\frac1{1-x}\left(1+e^2\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell x^\ell\right),\\
\sum_{\ell=8}^{k}b_\ell x^\ell
&\le\frac1{64}\sum_{\ell=8}^{\infty}(2x)^\ell
\le\frac1{8192}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$
Here the level sums are grouped by their original entrance heights, so that exactly \(b_\ell\) positive levels enter at height \(\ell\). Since \(e^2<9\), (6) gives \(Z/Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)<\frac43(1+9/8192)<3/2\). On the other hand, \((1+y)^3\le4(1+y^3)\) for every \(y>0\), by convexity of the cube. Multiplying this coordinate inequality yields
\(4^nZ_{\mathrm B}(3\gamma)/Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)^3\ge1\).
Retaining all height-zero Boolean atoms in the original third-power sum now gives
$$
A_0\ge\frac{4^{n+1}Z_{\mathrm B}(3\gamma)}{Z^3}
\ge\frac4{(Z/Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma))^3}>\frac{32}{27}>1.
\tag{7}
$$
Thus every active law has \(x>1/4\). For any \(x>0\), the same level estimate and the original column ranges also give
$$
Z\le e^2Z_{\mathrm B}(\gamma)(Q+1)(k+1)\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace.
\tag{8}
$$

At each target \((j,k)\) with \(j\in G_Q\), take its singleton and all the \(2^{Q-1}\) depth-\(Q\) triples in (14) of 3e23587245b6989f, all lifted to height \(k\). At every other target take just the singleton. The cited construction gives three distinct sources in each triple, pairwise disjoint over the whole family and avoiding \(j\), with arithmetic mean \(j\). Their spatial levels are \(Q,Q,0\). All lifted sources are present at the original top height. Thus these are admissible mixed rows, and every nontrivial row has size \(c_Q=1+3\cdot2^{Q-1}\).

More explicitly, those triples change only coordinates on the two inverse paths \(u_1,\ldots,u_Q\) and \(v_1,\ldots,v_Q\). At each changed coordinate the source displacement from \(j\) has absolute value at most one, as is visible directly in the cited triple's coordinate table. Consequently every such source satisfies \(|\gamma\cdot(z-j)|\le2\Pi(\gamma)\le2\). Since all source heights equal the target height, the affine identities of 8ab782832d1c7da2 give
$$
g_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)=p_J,\qquad
H^{(\mathrm m)}(J)=|R_J^{(\mathrm m)}|p_J,\qquad
Y_J^{(\mathrm m)}(I)=\gamma\cdot(z_I-z_J),\qquad
V_J^{(\mathrm m)}\le4.
\tag{9}
$$
Singletons have zero score. Hence \(\nu_{\mathrm m}\le4\), without requiring that the original probabilities within a triple be equal.

Using only the selected top targets, then (4), (8), and the Boolean third-ratio inequality used in (7), yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
B
&\ge\frac{4^{n+1}c_Q^2x^{3k}}{Z^3}
Z_{\mathrm B}(3\gamma)\mu_{3\gamma}(G_Q)\\
&\ge2e^{-12}c_Q^2
\frac{x^{3k}}{(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3\max\lbrace1,x^k\rbrace^3}\\
&\ge\frac92e^{-12}
\frac{4^{Q-3k}}{(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3}
\qquad\text{for every active law.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$
For the last line, \(c_Q^2\ge(9/4)4^Q\) and \(x>1/4\). If \(x\le1\), the remaining height ratio is \(x^{3k}>4^{-3k}\); if \(x\ge1\), it is one.

Here is a fixed cutoff absorbing all losses in (10). For integers \(k\ge32\), \(2^k\ge192k^2\): it holds at 32, and \(2k^2>(k+1)^2\) makes the ratio increase thereafter. Also \(2^k/(8k)\ge2\), so taking its floor loses at most a factor two. Therefore
\(Q\ge b_k\ge2^k/(16k)\ge12k\ge384\).
The elementary estimate \((q+1)^6\le2^q\) for integers \(q\ge64\) is established in 163ec41d3cfa86a9. It can also be checked at 64 using \(65^6<2^{42}\), then propagated because \(((q+2)/(q+1))^6\le(66/65)^6<(10/9)^6<2\). Since \(k+1\le Q+1\), it follows that
$$
\frac{4^{Q-3k}}{(Q+1)^3(k+1)^3}
\ge\frac{2^{3Q/2}}{(Q+1)^6}
\ge2^{Q/2}\ge2^{192}.
\tag{11}
$$
In particular (10) is greater than \(e\), since \(e^{13}<4^{13}=2^{26}<2^{192}\). Equations (9)-(11) prove (1) for \(k\ge32\) with any \(C\ge4\).

We handle the finite remaining range uniformly over all its spatial fields and all its chain partitions. Fix \(8\le k<32\) and one allowed \(\mathcal C\). Let
\(\mathcal K_k=\lbrace\gamma\in\mathbb R^n:\Pi(\gamma)\le1\rbrace\).
This set is compact. There are finitely many full paths, so \(\Pi\) is continuous and its unit sublevel set is closed. Every coordinate belongs to a child block along at least one full path, so \(|\gamma_u|\le1\) on \(\mathcal K_k\); hence it is bounded in the fixed finite dimension \(n\). Compactness here is a consequence for each fixed \(k\), not an additional assumption of a global absolute-value bound independent of \(k\).

Choose at every target the full reflected row
\(R_J=\lbrace I\in\widehat A_k:2J-I\in\widehat A_k\rbrace\), and set \(\mathsf r_J=|R_J|\). This is an admissible target singleton and a family of disjoint pairs. Under the exact pure-pair identification in 8ab782832d1c7da2, its quantities are \(b_{\mathrm m}=b(p)\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm m}=\nu(p)\) of 445a9e668ee1a705. In particular affine weights give \(H_p(J)=\mathsf r_Jp_J\).

For every finite \(\beta\) and every \(\gamma\), this choice has
\(B^{\mathrm{full}}(\gamma,\beta)>1\).
Indeed completeness and convexity of the original support, supplied by 604889294a17d7df, and affineness give the exact parity identity from 445a9e668ee1a705:
$$
\chi(p):=\sum_Jp_JH_p(J)
=\sum_{c\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^N}p(c+2\mathbb Z^N)^2\ge2^{-N}.
\tag{12}
$$
To check the equality, ordered same-parity endpoints have their supported integer midpoint \(J\); their contribution \(p_J\sqrt{p_Ip_{2J-I}}\) is exactly \(p_Ip_{2J-I}\). Moreover \(H_p\) is not constant. At any Boolean spatial point \(j\), the rows of the targets \((j,0),(j,1),(j,2)\) have respectively one, three, and five sources, all in the same spatial column. Both source heights sum to at most four. Thus both sources have Boolean spatial bases, because every positive spatial level enters at height at least eight. A Boolean point is a vertex of the Boolean cube, so two Boolean bases with midpoint \(j\) must both equal \(j\). Conversely all the stated vertical sources are present, since \(k\ge8\). It follows that
$$
\bigl(H_p((j,0)),H_p((j,1)),H_p((j,2))\bigr)
=\frac{e^{\gamma\cdot j}}Z(1,3x,5x^2).
\tag{13}
$$
The three entries cannot all be equal: \(1=3x\) would give \(5x^2=5/9\). Since every original target has positive probability, strict Jensen and (12) imply \(b(p)>\chi(p)^2\ge4^{-N}\), proving the strict claim.

For completeness we explicitly compactify the unbounded positive-height-field endpoint, including its nonzero limiting spatial variance. Put \(y=e^{-\beta}\), so the active range \(\beta>\log(1/4)\) is contained in \(0<y\le4\). Write \(J=(z_J,t_J)\), and define the finite sums
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf S(\gamma,y)&=\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}e^{\gamma\cdot z_J}y^{k-t_J},\\
\mathsf T(\gamma,y)&=\sum_{J\in\widehat A_k}\mathsf r_J^2e^{3\gamma\cdot z_J}y^{3(k-t_J)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
They are polynomials in \(y\) with continuous positive coefficient functions of \(\gamma\). For \(y>0\), the unchanged original normalizer is \(Z=e^{\beta k}\mathsf S(\gamma,y)\), and
$$
\mathsf B(\gamma,y):=4^N\frac{\mathsf T(\gamma,y)}{\mathsf S(\gamma,y)^3}
=B^{\mathrm{full}}(\gamma,-\log y).
\tag{15}
$$
For \(I\in R_J\), put \(h_{J,I}=t_I-t_J\). The exact affine full-row score gives
$$
\mathsf V(\gamma,y):=\nu^{\mathrm{full}}(\gamma,-\log y)=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_J\mathsf r_Je^{3\gamma\cdot z_J}y^{3(k-t_J)}
\sum_{I\in R_J}\bigl(\gamma\cdot(z_I-z_J)-h_{J,I}\log y\bigr)^2}
{\mathsf T(\gamma,y)}.
\tag{16}
$$
These formulas follow directly from the uniform geometric law within each affine row and the center weights \(q_J=\mathsf r_J^2p_J^3/b(p)\). In particular they do not discard lower-height atoms from \(Z\).

Let \(M_{\mathrm{top}}\) be the positive number of targets of height \(k\). Since \(|\gamma_u|\le1\), \(z_{J,u}\in\lbrace0,1,2\rbrace\), and \(|z_{I,u}-z_{J,u}|\le2\), uniformly on \(\mathcal K_k\) we have \(|\gamma\cdot z_J|\le2n\) and \(|\gamma\cdot(z_I-z_J)|\le2n\). Therefore
\(\mathsf S(\gamma,0)\ge M_{\mathrm{top}}e^{-2n}>0\) and \(\mathsf T(\gamma,0)\ge M_{\mathrm{top}}e^{-6n}>0\).
If \(t_J=k\), reflection and the height ceiling force every \(I\in R_J\) to have height \(k\), so \(h_{J,I}=0\). Thus every top-target term in the numerator of (16) is independent of \(\log y\). For \(t_J<k\), \(k-t_J\ge1\) and \(|h_{J,I}|\le k\), so for \(0<y\le1\) the total lower-target numerator is bounded above, uniformly on \(\mathcal K_k\), by
$$
e^{6n}\left(\sum_{J:t_J<k}\mathsf r_J^2\right)
y^3\bigl(2n+k|\log y|\bigr)^2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
$$
The lower-target contributions to \(\mathsf S\) and \(\mathsf T\) tend uniformly to zero as well, respectively bounded by finite constants times \(y\) and \(y^3\). Equations (14)-(17) prove that \(\mathsf B\) and \(\mathsf V\) extend continuously to the whole compact set \(\mathcal K_k\times[0,4]\). The extended variance at zero is the finite spatial-score expression of the top rows; it need not vanish. This explicitly handles all factors of \(\beta^2\) in disappearing lower rows and proves uniformity in the spatial field.

At \(y=0\), the original law becomes the positive affine law proportional to \(e^{\gamma\cdot z}\) on the complete top slice \(A_{m,Q}\times\lbrace k\rbrace\). For top targets the original reflected row is exactly the reflected row in that slice. Its occupied ambient parity classes number at most \(2^{N-1}\). The parity identity and Jensen from (12), now applied in the top slice (or in spatial dimension \(n=N-1\)), therefore give
$$
\mathsf B(\gamma,0)=4^N b(p^{\mathrm{top}}_\gamma)\ge4.
\tag{18}
$$
Completeness of the slice guarantees that every same-parity midpoint used here remains supported. No positivity is required at the disappearing lower-height atoms, since this calculation is entirely on the positive top slice.

The continuous extended \(\log\mathsf B\) is strictly positive at every point of \(\mathcal K_k\times[0,4]\), by (12)-(13) for \(y>0\) and (18) for \(y=0\). It therefore has a positive minimum, and the extended \(\mathsf V/\log\mathsf B\) has a finite maximum there. For each fixed \(k\), only finitely many allowed chain partitions exist, since they are partitions of the fixed finite Boolean cube, with their saturated chain orders fixed by inclusion. Finally only the 24 integers \(8\le k<32\) occur in this part. Taking the maximum of these finitely many compact-set maxima and 4 defines one finite numerical constant \(C\). Thus it is independent of the chosen \(k,\mathcal C,\gamma,\beta\), even though its definition is not intended to yield a manageable numerical size. Equations (7), (12)-(18) give (1) in this finite range. Together with (9)-(11), they prove the active-factor assertion in full.

For the heterogeneous product, choose the rows just proved in every active factor, and singleton rows in every inactive factor.
