## Statement

Use the retained-area envelope and tail function of accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{\mathbf v,j}(s)
&=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}
\min\{v_i^2,(\sqrt s+\lambda(i-j))_+^2\},
\qquad 0\le s\le v_j^2,\\
\Phi(a)&=\sum_{k\ge1}(1-k/a)_+^2,
\qquad z_+=\max\{z,0\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(\lambda\) is the cap slope denoted by \(\beta\) in that accepted fact. Let \(1<a\le b\), and define the asymmetric profile by

\[
g_0=1,\qquad
g_{-k}=(1-k/a)_+,\qquad
g_k=(1-k/b)_+\quad(k\ge1),
\qquad
U=\Phi(a),\quad V=\Phi(b),\quad T=1+U+V.
\tag{2}
\]

Both lengths in (2) are measured from the same anchor zero. In the one-anchor replacement notation in the proof of accepted fact `82092cd50249b044`, the exact aliases are \(a=a_-\) and \(b=a\) of that predecessor, before its lighter-tail raising. Thus \(T\) in (2) is the preserved original normalized total, not the total of a symmetrically enlarged profile.

Assume \(U\le V\le U+1\) and \(a\ge9/2\), equivalently \(U\ge28/27\). Put \(x=\sqrt2/3\) and

\[
M=V+\frac29+(2x-1+1/b)_+^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Then

\[
M\le \mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(2/9)
\le\max\{5T/9,M\}.
\tag{4}
\]

The lower bound in (4) is attained by a finite cap slope. Consequently the small-sector condition \(\mathrm S(\mathbf g,0)\), meaning \(\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(2/9)\le5T/9\), holds if and only if

\[
\frac{5+5U-4V}{9}
\ge\frac29+(2\sqrt2/3-1+1/b)_+^2.
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, (5) always holds if \(U\ge6/5\).

For the actual-body consequence, let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a convex body, \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), \(S=C\cap\Lambda\), \(C_i=\{\mathbf y:(i,\mathbf y)\in C\}\), \(a_i=\mathcal L^2(C_i)\), \(r_i=\sqrt{a_i}\), and \(W=\mathcal H_2(S)=\sum_i a_i>0\). If an area-weighted median index \(j\) satisfies

\[
\min\left\{\sum_{i<j}a_i,\sum_{i>j}a_i\right\}
\ge\frac65a_j,
\tag{6}
\]

then there is one \(\mathbf p\in\{j\}\times C_j\) such that every closed ambient halfspace \(H\) containing \(\mathbf p\) satisfies \(\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge2W/9\). More generally, the same conclusion holds at this median when its normalized tails satisfy the hypotheses of (4) and the explicit inequality (5). Independently of (6), every such actual body with \(N=\#\{i:a_i>0\}\ge13\) has a point with this depth. A failure of (5) characterizes a sufficient scalar test for the auxiliary profile; it asserts no depth upper bound for an actual body.

## Proof

We use accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` for root concavity, variable-length tail comparison, normalization, and the fixed-fiber small-sector and heavy-fiber consumers. From accepted fact `82092cd50249b044` we use only its quadrature inequality: if \(h\) is nonnegative, continuous, piecewise affine, compactly supported, and concave on its positive support interval, then

\[
\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}h(i)^2
\le\int_{\mathbb R}h(t)^2\,dt
+\frac14\int_{\mathbb R}h'(t)^2\,dt.
\tag{7}
\]

From accepted fact `d773b98105d1ae6c` we use its neighboring-pair inequality: at cap intercept \(x\), neighboring root caps \(m\le M_1\) have retained square sum at most \(M_1^2+\min\{m^2,(2x-M_1)_+^2\}\). These three accepted inputs have exactly their stated area normalizations. All remaining inequalities are proved below.

We first record consequences of the median constraint without changing either tail. For integers \(m\ge1\), on \([m,m+1]\) the finite-sum identity for \(\Phi\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi'(z)&=\frac{m(m+1)}{z^2}
-\frac{m(m+1)(2m+1)}{3z^3},\\
\Phi''(z)&=\frac{m(m+1)(2m+1-2z)}{z^4},\\
\Phi'(m)&=\frac{m^2-1}{3m^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]

The derivative is continuous at integers, because the newly appearing summand and its derivative vanish there. On each full unit interval it first increases and then decreases. Its minimum is therefore at an endpoint. On \([9/2,5]\) it decreases to \(\Phi'(5)=8/25\); on every subsequent full unit interval both endpoint derivatives are at least \(8/25\). Hence \(\Phi'(z)\ge8/25\) for every \(z\ge9/2\). Since \(\Phi(b)-\Phi(a)\le1\), this proves

\[
b-a\le\frac{25}{8},\qquad
1\le\frac ba\le\frac{61}{36}<\frac74.
\tag{9}
\]

Also \(\Phi(9/2)=28/27\) and \(\Phi(5)=6/5\), directly from the finite-sum formula. If \(\theta=z-\lfloor z\rfloor\), expansion of the same formula gives

\[
\Phi(z)=\frac z3-\frac12+\frac1{6z}
-\frac{\theta(1-\theta)(1-2\theta)}{6z^2}.
\tag{10}
\]

Since \(\theta(1-\theta)\le1/4\) and \(|1-2\theta|\le1\), we have \(\Phi(z)\ge z/3-1/2\) for \(z\ge1\). Thus the original total in (2) satisfies

\[
T\ge\frac{a+b}{3}.
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(g(t)\) be the continuous asymmetric tent equal to \(1+t/a\) on \([-a,0]\), \(1-t/b\) on \([0,b]\), and zero outside, so \(g(i)=g_i\). For a cap slope \(\lambda\), put \(h_\lambda(t)=\min\{g(t),(x+\lambda t)_+\}\). Its integer square sum is the quantity inside the supremum in (1) at \(s=2/9\). On the intersection of the positive support intervals, the minimum of the concave tent and the affine cap is concave. It vanishes continuously at the support endpoints. Therefore (7) applies, including when a crossing or a support endpoint is an integer.

It is enough to bound slopes \(\lambda\ge0\). Indeed, at every positive integer distance \(k\), the right root cap is at least the left root cap. If \(0\le L\le R\) and \(0\le p\le q\), then
\(\min\{L,p\}+\min\{R,q\}\ge\min\{L,q\}+\min\{R,p\}\).
To see this, \(\min\{R,z\}-\min\{L,z\}\) is nondecreasing in \(z\). Apply the inequality to the two squared root caps and the squared positive cap values at \(k\) and \(-k\). The retained sum at \(\lambda\ge0\) is at least its value at \(-\lambda\).

We use \(7/15<x<1/2\); both inequalities follow by squaring, using \(x^2=2/9\). First suppose \(0\le\lambda\le x/a\). Define

\[
G(y)=\frac{(1-x)^2((1+2x)+(2+x)y)}{3(1+y)^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

The cap crosses the left and right tent edges at distances \(a(1-x)/(1-a\lambda)\) and \(b(1-x)/(1+b\lambda)\) from zero. These distances lie in their corresponding tails in this slope range. The right loss from the full tent is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{b(1-x)/(1+b\lambda)}
\bigl((1-t/b)^2-(x+\lambda t)^2\bigr)\,dt
&=bG(b\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

For a direct verification, an antiderivative of the integrand is
\((1-x^2)t-(1/b+x\lambda)t^2+(1/b^2-\lambda^2)t^3/3\); substitution of the displayed endpoint gives (13). Reflecting the left coordinate gives left loss \(aG(-a\lambda)\). Thus
\(\int h_\lambda^2=(a+b)/3-aG(-a\lambda)-bG(b\lambda)\).

Write \(G_0=G(0)=1/9+4x/27\). Direct subtraction in (12) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
G(-z)&=G_0+(1-x)^2xz
+\frac{(1-x)^2z^2}{3}\frac{4x-1-3xz}{(1-z)^2},\\
G(y)&=G_0-(1-x)^2xy
+\frac{(1-x)^2y^2}{3}\frac{4x-1+3xy}{(1+y)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

For \(0\le z\le x\), the last coefficient ratio in the first line is at least \(3/5\). Its derivative has the sign of \(5x-2-3xz\), so its minimum is at zero or at \(x\). The value at zero is \(4x-1>3/5\). The assertion at \(x\), after multiplication by \(5(1-x)^2>0\), is \(26x-12\ge0\), which follows from \(x>7/15\). For \(0\le y\le7x/4\), the ratio in the second line decreases, because its derivative has the sign of \(2-5x-3xy<0\). At \(y=7x/4\), its lower bound by \(3/5\) is equivalent to \((19/2)x-101/24\ge0\), again a consequence of \(x>7/15\).

Here \(z=a\lambda\le x\) and \(y=b\lambda\le7x/4\), by (9). Therefore (14), followed by completing the square in \(\lambda\), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
aG(-a\lambda)+bG(b\lambda)
&\ge G_0(a+b)-(1-x)^2x(b^2-a^2)\lambda
+\frac{(1-x)^2}{5}(a^3+b^3)\lambda^2\\
&\ge(a+b)\left(
G_0-\frac54(1-x)^2x^2\frac{(b-a)^2}{a^2-ab+b^2}
\right)\\
&\ge(a+b)\left(G_0-\frac54(1-x)^2x^2\frac9{37}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

For the last step, \((\rho-1)^2/(\rho^2-\rho+1)\) increases on \(1\le\rho\le7/4\): its derivative is \((\rho-1)(\rho+1)/(\rho^2-\rho+1)^2\). Its right endpoint value is \(9/37\). The loss in (15) exceeds \(4(a+b)/27\) by at least \((a+b)/72\), because

\[
G_0-\frac4{27}-\frac54(1-x)^2x^2\frac9{37}
=\frac{566x-239}{1998}>\frac1{72}.
\tag{16}
\]

The last inequality is equivalent to \(x>1067/2264\). Both sides are positive, and squaring proves it since \(2\cdot2264^2-9\cdot1067^2=4991>0\).

In this slope range the successive slopes of \(h_\lambda\) are \(1/a,\lambda,-1/b\), allowing a piece of zero length. Integration by parts on its affine pieces gives \(\int h_\lambda'^2=-\sum h_\lambda\Delta h_\lambda'\). Its height is at most one, and the total downward slope jump is \(1/a+1/b\). Hence \(\int h_\lambda'^2\le1/a+1/b\). Combining (7), (11), and (15)--(16) gives

\[
\sum_i h_\lambda(i)^2
\le\frac{5(a+b)}{27}-\frac{a+b}{72}
+\frac{a+b}{4ab}
\le\frac{5T}{9},
\tag{17}
\]

since \(ab\ge(9/2)^2>18\). This includes \(\lambda=0\) and \(\lambda=x/a\).

Next suppose \(x/a\le\lambda\le x/2\), and put \(B=b\lambda\). The cap vanishes at \(-x/\lambda\) within the left support. The retained root is one affine segment of slope \(\lambda\) from that point to its right crossing, then the right tent edge of slope \(-1/b\). Its height at the crossing is \((x+B)/(1+B)\). Its Dirichlet energy is consequently exactly

\[
\int h_\lambda'^2
=\frac{x+B}{1+B}\left(\lambda+\frac1b\right)
=\lambda+\frac xb.
\tag{18}
\]

Its left integral is \(x^3/(3\lambda)\), and its right loss is (13). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int h_\lambda^2&=\frac b3-bD(B),\\
D(B)&=\frac{(2-3x)B^2+B/3-x^3}{3B(1+B)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

We prove that (7) and (18)--(19) imply the desired bound throughout this whole slope interval. Put \(A_0=2-3x>0\) and \(\mathscr F_b(B)=bD(B)-B/(4b)\). A positive multiple of \(\mathscr F_b'(B)\) has numerator

\[
-A_0B^3+(A_0-2/3)B^2+3x^3B+x^3
-\frac{3}{4b^2}B^2(1+B)^3.
\tag{20}
\]

After division by \(B>0\), this is strictly decreasing from a positive to a negative value: \(A_0-2/3=4/3-3x<0\), and differentiation of every nonconstant term after division gives a negative contribution. Therefore \(\mathscr F_b\) first increases and then decreases. Its minimum on \([bx/a,bx/2]\) occurs at an endpoint. The bound

\[
bD(b\lambda)
\ge\frac{4b-5a}{27}+\frac\lambda4+\frac{x}{4b}
\tag{21}
\]

is thus proved for the entire interval once proved at its two endpoints. Indeed, (21) is precisely \(\mathscr F_b(B)\ge(4b-5a)/27+x/(4b)\), with a constant right side.

At the left endpoint, \(B=bx/a\in[x,7x/4]\). For \(B\ge x\), (19) gives

\[
D(B)\ge\frac{A_0B+1/9}{3(1+B)^2}.
\tag{22}
\]

This follows from \(x^3/B\le x^2=2/9\). On \([x,7x/4]\), the right side of (22) is at least \(1/18\). To verify this, the required numerator inequality is
\(-B^2+(10-18x)B-1/3\ge0\).
This is a concave quadratic in \(B\). Its endpoint values are \(10x-41/9>0\) and \((35/2)x-577/72>0\); substitution of the strict bound \(x>7/15\) proves both. Hence, at \(\lambda=x/a\), the right side of (21) minus its left side is at most

\[
\frac{5b-10a}{54}+\frac{x}{4a}+\frac{x}{4b}
\le-\frac{5a}{216}+\frac1{4a}<0,
\tag{23}
\]

using (9), \(b\ge a\), \(x<1/2\), and \(a\ge9/2\).

For the right endpoint define \(\mathscr H(b)=bD(bx/2)\). With \(B=bx/2\), it has the explicit form

\[
\mathscr H(b)=\frac{2}{3x}\frac{A_0B^2+B/3-x^3}{(1+B)^2}.
\tag{24}
\]

It is increasing and concave on \(b>0\). Differentiation with respect to \(B\) shows that the first derivative has positive numerator \((2A_0-1/3)B+1/3+2x^3\); the second derivative has numerator
\((2A_0-4/3-6x^3)-2(2A_0-1/3)B<0\).
Here \(2A_0-1/3>0\) and \(2A_0-4/3-6x^3=(8-22x)/3<0\).

For \(9/2\le b\le61/8\), it suffices to prove

\[
\mathscr H(b)-\frac x8-\frac{x}{4b}-\frac{4b-45/2}{27}\ge0,
\tag{25}
\]

because \(a\ge9/2\). The left side is concave in \(b\). At \(b=9/2\), it is greater than \(1/6-13x/72>0\), because \(\mathscr H(9/2)>0\) follows from (22). It remains to check \(b=61/8\).

The rational bounds \(8/17<x<17/36\) follow by squaring from \(578>576\) and \(289>288\), respectively. Applying (22) at \(B=61x/16\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr H(61/8)
&\ge\frac{(61/8)((61/8)x-175/72)}{3(1+61x/16)^2}\\
&>\frac{16595904}{44230073}>\frac38.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

The middle bound uses \(x>8/17\) in the positive numerator and \(x<17/36\) in the denominator. Its final comparison has positive cross-product difference \(8\cdot16595904-3\cdot44230073=77013\). Meanwhile

\[
\frac8{27}+\frac x8+\frac{2x}{61}
<\frac8{27}+\frac{17}{288}+\frac{17}{1098}
=\frac38-\frac{221}{52704}<\frac38.
\tag{27}
\]

Equations (26)--(27) prove (25) at \(61/8\), and concavity proves it throughout the interval. For \(b\ge61/8\), use \(a\ge b-25/8\) from (9). It suffices to prove
\(\mathscr H(b)-x/8-x/(4b)-(125/8-b)/27\ge0\).
This expression is increasing, since \(\mathscr H\) is increasing, \(-x/(4b)\) is increasing, and \(b/27\) is increasing. At \(61/8\) it is the already verified expression. Thus (21) holds also at \(\lambda=x/2\), for every allowed \(a,b\). The endpoint argument based on (20) now proves (21) throughout the intermediate slope interval.

Finally (7), (11), and (18)--(21) give

\[
\sum_i h_\lambda(i)^2
\le\frac b3-bD(b\lambda)+\frac\lambda4+\frac{x}{4b}
\le\frac{5(a+b)}{27}\le\frac{5T}{9}.
\tag{28}
\]

This verifies all intermediate slopes, including both clipping boundaries.

It remains to consider \(\lambda\ge x/2\). Every negative integer index at most \(-2\) has nonpositive cap value. Put \(b_1=g_1=1-1/b\); the other neighboring root cap is \(g_{-1}=1-1/a\le b_1\). The neighboring-pair bound from accepted fact `d773b98105d1ae6c`, followed by retaining all right indices at least two in full, gives

\[
\sum_i h_\lambda(i)^2
\le V+\frac29+(2x-b_1)_+^2=M.
\tag{29}
\]

This upper bound is attained. Choose \(\lambda=b_1-x\). Since \(b\ge a\ge9/2\), \(b_1\ge7/9>3x/2\), so this slope lies in the present interval. At every integer \(k\ge1\), the cap line minus the affine right root before its zero endpoint is \((k-1)(1-x)\ge0\); every right entry is therefore retained in full. At \(-1\) the retained root is \((2x-b_1)_+\), which is smaller than \(2/9\), whereas \(g_{-1}\ge7/9\). Thus its root cap does not bind. At every integer at most \(-2\), the cap is nonpositive: already \(x-2\lambda=3x-2b_1<0\). The central area is \(x^2=2/9\). These are exactly the terms of (3).

The gentle, intermediate, and steep ranges exhaust every nonnegative slope. The reflection comparison covers negative slopes. Equations (17), (28), and (29), together with the attained value, prove (4). Rearranging \(M\le5T/9\) gives (5), so its asserted equivalence follows as well.

If \(U\ge6/5\), monotonicity of \(\Phi\) and \(\Phi(5)=6/5\) give \(b\ge a\ge5\). The median constraint implies

\[
\frac{5T}{9}-V
=\frac{5+5U-4V}{9}
\ge\frac{1+U}{9}\ge\frac{11}{45}.
\tag{30}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
M-V\le\frac29+(2x-4/5)^2<\frac{11}{45}.
\tag{31}
\]

Indeed, the right side of (31) minus its middle expression is \((16/5)(x-113/240)>0\), and \(x^2-(113/240)^2=31/57600>0\). This proves the claimed lighter-tail theorem.

We transfer it to the actual body without changing the original total. At a positive-area actual weighted median \(j\), put \(A=a_j\), translate the anchor to zero, and reflect the integer coordinate if necessary to put the heavier tail on the right. Define the normalized roots by \(\widehat r_i=r_{j+i}/\sqrt A\), or by \(\widehat r_i=r_{j-i}/\sqrt A\) after reflection. Then
\(U=\sum_{i<0}\widehat r_i^2\), \(V=\sum_{i>0}\widehat r_i^2\), \(U\le V\le U+1\), and \(T=W/A=1+U+V\).
When \(U\ge28/27\), both tails have positive mass. Accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d` replaces them separately by (2), with \(\Phi(a)=U\) and \(\Phi(b)=V\), and increases every clipped sum at this anchor. The replacement preserves \(U,V,A\), and \(T\). Thus (5), whenever satisfied, implies

\[
\mathcal E_{\mathbf r,j}(2A/9)
=A\,\mathcal E_{\widehat{\mathbf r},0}(2/9)
\le A\,\mathcal E_{\mathbf g,0}(2/9)
\le\frac{5AT}{9}=\frac{5W}{9}.
\tag{32}
\]

The equality in (32) is the exact area and slope normalization of that accepted fact; reflection reverses the slope's sign. Its fixed-fiber small-sector consumer chooses one point in the actual fiber with depth at least \(2W/9\), with that point fixed before all closed containing halfspaces are quantified. Equation (6) gives \(U\ge6/5\), so (30)--(31) establish this consequence in particular.

To prove the assertion for \(N\ge13\), a fiber with area at least \(W/2\) already gives the required point by the heavy-fiber clause of accepted fact `4d628efe7673ce2d`. Otherwise choose an area-weighted median. It is strictly inside the consecutive positive-area support, because an endpoint median would itself be heavy. After the normalization just used, let \(m_-\) and \(m_+\) be the two positive tail lengths. They are positive integers with \(m_-+m_+=N-1\ge12\). Concavity and nonnegative endpoint roots give
\(\widehat r_{-k}\ge1-k/m_-\) and \(\widehat r_k\ge1-k/m_+\) on the respective tails. Consequently

\[
U\ge\Phi(m_-),\qquad
V\ge\Phi(m_+),\qquad
V\ge\Phi(\max\{m_-,m_+\}).
\tag{33}
\]

If both tail lengths are at least five, (33) gives \(U\ge\Phi(5)=6/5\). If the larger tail length is at least nine, it gives \(V\ge\Phi(9)=68/27\), and hence \(U\ge V-1\ge41/27>6/5\). These two cases are already proved. The only other possibility is that the tail lengths are four and eight: their sum is at least twelve, the smaller is at most four, and the larger is at most eight, forcing those exact values.

In this last case \(V\ge\Phi(8)=35/16\), so \(U\ge19/16>28/27\). The lengths in the asymmetric replacement therefore satisfy \(a\ge9/2\) and \(b\ge8\). Since \(2x<1\), (3) and the median inequality \(T\ge2V\) imply

\[
M-V\le\frac29+\frac1{64}=\frac{137}{576}
<\frac{140}{576}=\frac{35}{144}
\le\frac V9\le\frac{5T}{9}-V.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus (5) holds here as well, and (32) proves the required actual depth. The values of \(\Phi(8)\) and \(\Phi(9)\) used above are direct finite sums of the squared roots \(1-k/8\) and \(1-k/9\). No support-length cases were removed by an approximate test.

All original zero-area integer fibers remain in \(S\); their intersections with any halfspace have area zero. The fixed-fiber consumer applies to the locked closed-halfspace convention, including a boundary containing an entire integer fiber. Undoing the initial integer translation and possible reflection preserves the mixed measure and that convention. This proves all stated actual-body consequences and completes the proof.
