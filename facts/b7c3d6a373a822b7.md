## Statement

Retain the complete positive support \(D\), its hull \(P\), convex-admissibility, \(w_{\mathbf I}=e^{-\phi_{\mathbf I}}\), and \(W=\sum_{\mathbf I\in D}w_{\mathbf I}\) from accepted fact 488f0a583886b5fc. Potentials may be real, as in that fact's stability assertion; subtracting their minimum gives its normalized potential. Retain \(\boldsymbol\theta(u)\), \(p^u\), and \(\mathcal G(u)=\mathbb E_{p^u}u-u_{\mathbf0}\) exactly from accepted fact 3ee88fc83ab3b533. In particular, the tilt is chosen to make the original law of the effective potential \(u-\boldsymbol\theta(u)\cdot\mathbf I\) have mean zero. The scalar maxima below concern \(\beta>0\), with this tilt recomputed at every \(\beta\).

Let
\[
f_t(x)=\max\{|x|,2|x|-2+2t\},\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
This is exactly the seven-site fan of accepted fact baf0d06113557f53. Denote its complete one-dimensional support, called \(D\) in that predecessor, by \(D_7=\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}\). On \(D_7\), its complete globally optimized endpoint energy strictly exceeds its complete globally optimized starting energy. Thus that accepted example, despite its negative optimized right derivative at zero, is not itself an endpoint counterexample.

For a global endpoint counterexample, take rank \(r=2\) and
\[
P=[-3,3]\times[-1,1],\qquad
D=P\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad u_t(x,y)=f_t(x)+|y|.
\]
These are restrictions of finite convex functions on \(\mathbb R^2\), \(\mathbf0\in\operatorname{int}P\), and \(D\) is the same complete 21-site support throughout. The starting function is its lattice-value lower convex roof on \(P\). Its eight supporting affine branches have intercepts zero or minus two; replacing each intercept \(b\) by \((1-t)b\) gives exactly \(u_t\). Each starting branch is active on a nonempty open subset of \(P\). The endpoint is the finite convex positively homogeneous function
\[
u_1(x,y)=2|x|+|y|.
\]
For every finite \(\beta>0\) and every \(t\in[0,1]\), all 21 sites have strictly positive original weights, their weighted mean is zero, and \(\boldsymbol\theta(\beta u_t|_D)=\mathbf0\).

Write
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}=\max_{\beta>0}\mathcal G(\beta u_0|_D),\qquad
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{end}}=\max_{\beta>0}\mathcal G(\beta u_1|_D).
\]
These maxima exist and satisfy
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{end}}<\frac{11}{10}
<\frac{32}{31}\log3
\le\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}<\frac{19}{16}.
\]
Hence \(\max_{\beta>0}\mathcal G(\beta u_1|_D)\ge\max_{\beta>0}\mathcal G(\beta u_0|_D)\) fails.

The symbol \(h\) below denotes a homogeneous potential, not the value variation direction called \(h\) in 3ee88fc83ab3b533. Nevertheless the different finite convex positively homogeneous potential
\[
h(x,y)=(\log3)|x|+(\log4)|y|
\]
on this exact same complete \(D\) has centering tilt zero and satisfies
\[
\mathcal G(h|_D)
=\frac{36}{53}\log3+\frac23\log2
>\frac{19}{16}>\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}.
\]
In particular, if \(\beta_{\mathrm{src}}\) is any maximizing starting temperature, then \(\phi=\beta_{\mathrm{src}}u_0|_D\) is an originally centered convex-admissible potential and \(h\) strictly dominates its original energy gap. Indeed \(h\) dominates every positive-temperature starting potential in this example. The arbitrary homogeneous domination statement for all other complete supports and all other originally centered potentials remains open. No integral hitting or final depth assertion is claimed.

## Proof

All logarithms are natural. To avoid a normalization ambiguity, the original potential \(\phi=\beta u_t|_D\) used here has minimum zero, so the canonical \(w_{\max}\) of 488f0a583886b5fc is one. Its canonical \(B=\max_D\phi\) is \(\beta(5+2t)\). Thus the original positive source weights are exactly \(e^{-\beta u_t(\mathbf I)}\) on every site of \(D\), with zero table values outside \(D\). Nothing deletes or replaces the support. The notation \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}\) and \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{end}}\) denotes only optimized scalar energies, not any predecessor normalization or packing capacity.

The function \(u_t\) is even separately in its two coordinates. Therefore its original law has mean zero in each coordinate, at every temperature and parameter. Its weights are positive on the full-span support \(D\), so uniqueness of the centering tilt in 3ee88fc83ab3b533 gives \(\boldsymbol\theta=\mathbf0\). This verifies the centering condition for the actual original weights rather than substituting a different mean condition. Also \(u_t(0,0)=0\).

The eight affine branches are obtained by adding either \(y\) or \(-y\) to each member of
\[
x,\quad -x,\quad 2x-2+2t,\quad -2x-2+2t.
\]
Thus the deformation is precisely the supporting-fan intercept deformation of baf0d06113557f53. At \(t=0\), each branch is active on an open rectangle inside \(P\): use \(0<x<2\), \(-2<x<0\), \(2<x<3\), or \(-3<x<-2\), respectively, and the indicated sign of \(y\). No redundant boundary-only branch is needed. The initial lower-roof assertion uses the convention of 488f0a583886b5fc and can also be checked directly. On every unit grid rectangle in \(P\), \(f_0(x)+|y|\) is affine. The point in that rectangle has a convex representation using its four integer corners whose average prescribed value equals \(u_0(x,y)\). Convexity gives the opposite inequality for every convex representation of the point. The lower roof therefore equals \(u_0\) everywhere on \(P\).

We first bound the accepted seven-site starting maximum exactly. For the auxiliary scalar \(\zeta=e^{-\beta}\), introduce
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr Z_7(\zeta)&=1+2\zeta+2\zeta^2+2\zeta^4,\\
\mathscr N_7(\zeta)&=2\zeta+4\zeta^2+8\zeta^4,\\
\mathscr A_7(\zeta)&=2\zeta+8\zeta^2+32\zeta^4,\\
\mu_7(\beta)&=\mathscr N_7/\mathscr Z_7,\\
\mathscr V_7(\beta)&=\mathscr A_7/\mathscr Z_7-\mu_7^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\mathscr Z_7\) is the original total weight \(W\) for the seven-site potential \(\beta f_0\); \(\mathscr N_7\) and \(\mathscr A_7\) are unnormalized height moments. The scalar \(\mathscr V_7\) is height variance and is not the convex roof. The scalar \(\zeta\) is unrelated to the integer continuous dimension in 488f0a583886b5fc.

Let \(\mathscr E_7(\beta)=\beta\mu_7(\beta)\). Accepted fact baf0d06113557f53 proves that this function has a unique stationary point, which is its strict global maximum, and that
\[
\mathscr E_7'(\beta)=\mu_7-\beta\mathscr V_7,
\qquad \mu_7'=-\mathscr V_7<0.
\]
The following exact rational substitutions in the displayed moment polynomials locate that maximum:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\zeta=369/1000 &
10\mathscr N_7<7\mathscr Z_7,
\quad \mathscr A_7\mathscr Z_7-\mathscr N_7^2
<\mathscr N_7\mathscr Z_7,\\[2pt]
\zeta=9/25 &
50\mathscr N_7\mathscr Z_7
<51(\mathscr A_7\mathscr Z_7-\mathscr N_7^2).
\end{array}
\]
Each inequality is an integer inequality after multiplying by the positive power of 1000 or 25 in its denominator. Define
\[
b_{\mathrm{left}}=\log(1000/369),\qquad
b_{\mathrm{right}}=\log(25/9).
\]
The exponential series gives
\[
e>\sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac1{j!}=\frac{163}{60}>\frac{1000}{369},
\]
so \(b_{\mathrm{left}}<1\). At this temperature the rational inequalities give \(\mu_7/\mathscr V_7>1\), hence \(\mathscr E_7'(b_{\mathrm{left}})>0\), and also \(\mu_7(b_{\mathrm{left}})<7/10\).

For \(0<z<1\), integrating the geometric series for \(1/(1-z^2)\) gives
\[
\log\frac{1+z}{1-z}
=2\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}.
\]
With \(z=8/17\), direct rational arithmetic gives
\[
2\left(\frac8{17}+\frac{(8/17)^3}{3}
+\frac{(8/17)^5}{5}+\frac{(8/17)^7}{7}\right)
>\frac{51}{50}.
\]
Consequently \(b_{\mathrm{right}}>51/50\), while the moment inequality at \(\zeta=9/25\) gives \(\mu_7/\mathscr V_7<51/50\). Hence \(\mathscr E_7'(b_{\mathrm{right}})<0\). Another rational exponential-series comparison gives
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{6}\frac{(41/40)^j}{j!}>\frac{25}{9},
\]
so \(b_{\mathrm{right}}<41/40\). The unique seven-site maximizer \(\beta_7\) therefore lies strictly between these two temperatures, and strict decrease of \(\mu_7\) yields
\[
\mathcal M_7:=\max_{\beta>0}\mathscr E_7(\beta)
=\beta_7\mu_7(\beta_7)
<\frac{41}{40}\frac7{10}=\frac{287}{400}.
\]
For its homogeneous endpoint \(f_1(x)=2|x|\) on \(D_7\), let \(\mathscr E_{7,\mathrm{end}}(\beta)\) denote its original energy, equivalently the centered energy of 3ee88fc83ab3b533 computed on \(D_7\). Use temperature \(\beta=(\log3)/2\). On the same complete seven sites, the height \(|I|\) has weights \(3^{-|I|}\), total weight \(53/27\), and unnormalized first moment \(36/27\). Its energy is \((36/53)\log3\). The logarithm series with \(z=1/2\) gives \(\log3>1+1/12=13/12\), so
\[
\max_{\beta>0}\mathscr E_{7,\mathrm{end}}(\beta)
\ge\frac{36}{53}\log3
>\frac{39}{53}>\frac{287}{400}>\mathcal M_7.
\]
This establishes the asserted global comparison for the accepted example, before passing to the two-dimensional support.

For the three-site coordinate \(\{-1,0,1\}\), write
\[
\mathscr E_{\mathrm{one}}(b)=\frac{2b}{e^b+2}.
\]
Its derivative has the sign of \(2+(1-b)e^b\), a strictly decreasing function for \(b>0\). Its unique maximum is the positive number \(m_2\) satisfying
\[
m_2e^{m_2+1}=2.
\]
Indeed its maximizing temperature is \(1+m_2\) and substitution gives energy \(m_2\). The function \(m\mapsto me^{m+1}\) is strictly increasing for \(m\ge0\). The exact rational comparison
\[
\frac{47}{100}\sum_{j=0}^{6}\frac{(147/100)^j}{j!}>2
\]
and the positive exponential-series remainder therefore show \(m_2<47/100\). This scalar maximum is not a capacity or an integral hitting mass.

Return now to the complete two-dimensional \(D\). Product factorization of the original weights gives
\[
\mathcal G(\beta u_0|_D)
=\mathscr E_7(\beta)+\mathscr E_{\mathrm{one}}(\beta).
\]
Both summands are positive for positive temperature and tend to zero at both ends of that interval. The sum consequently attains a global maximum. The bounds just proved give
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}<\frac{287}{400}+\frac{47}{100}
=\frac{19}{16}.
\]
At \(\beta=\log3\), the seven-site starting polynomial has
\(\mathscr Z_7=155/81\), \(\mathscr N_7=98/81\). The other coordinate contributes \((2/5)\log3\). Thus
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{src}}
\ge\left(\frac{98}{155}+\frac25\right)\log3
=\frac{32}{31}\log3
>\frac{104}{93}>\frac{11}{10}.
\]
The final strict rational comparison is \(1040>1023\).

We next bound the entire globally optimized endpoint, not merely its value or derivative at any specified temperature. For \(u_1=2|x|+|y|\), its original partition polynomial is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}(\zeta)
&=(1+2\zeta)(1+2\zeta^2+2\zeta^4+2\zeta^6)\\
&=1+2\zeta+2\zeta^2+4\zeta^3+2\zeta^4
+4\zeta^5+2\zeta^6+4\zeta^7.
\end{aligned}
\]
This counts all 21 sites, including all corners and every equality site. Define its successive unnormalized height moments and normalized moments by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}&=\zeta\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}',
&\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}&=\zeta\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}',
&\mathscr T_{\mathrm e}&=\zeta\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}',\\
\mu_{\mathrm e}&=\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}/\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e},
&\mathscr V_{\mathrm e}&=\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}/\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}-\mu_{\mathrm e}^2,\\
\kappa_{3,\mathrm e}
&=\mathscr T_{\mathrm e}/\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}
-3\mu_{\mathrm e}\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}/\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}
+2\mu_{\mathrm e}^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Polynomial primes here mean derivatives in \(\zeta\); derivatives of the energies below mean derivatives in \(\beta\), with \(\zeta=e^{-\beta}\). Every finite-temperature law assigns positive mass to different heights, so \(\mu_{\mathrm e}>0\) and \(\mathscr V_{\mathrm e}>0\). Direct differentiation of these finite sums gives
\[
\mathscr E_{\mathrm e}(\beta)=\beta\mu_{\mathrm e},\qquad
\mathscr E_{\mathrm e}'=\mu_{\mathrm e}-\beta\mathscr V_{\mathrm e},\qquad
\mathscr E_{\mathrm e}''=\beta\kappa_{3,\mathrm e}-2\mathscr V_{\mathrm e},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm e}'=-\mathscr V_{\mathrm e}.
\]
An exact polynomial multiplication gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}(\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}\mathscr T_{\mathrm e}
-2\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}^2)+\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}^2\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}\\
&\quad=-4\zeta^2\bigl(
1+6\zeta+26\zeta^2+100\zeta^3+330\zeta^4
+594\zeta^5+1394\zeta^6+2160\zeta^7\\
&\qquad\qquad+5308\zeta^8+7592\zeta^9+14088\zeta^{10}
+17192\zeta^{11}+21972\zeta^{12}\\
&\qquad\qquad+18288\zeta^{13}+10352\zeta^{14}
+7136\zeta^{15}+1560\zeta^{16}+872\zeta^{17}
+56\zeta^{18}\bigr)<0.
\end{aligned}
\]
All coefficients inside the parentheses are positive. As can also be verified by substituting the normalized moments,
\[
\mu_{\mathrm e}\kappa_{3,\mathrm e}-2\mathscr V_{\mathrm e}^2
=\frac{\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}(\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}\mathscr T_{\mathrm e}
-2\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}^2)+\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}^2\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}}
{\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}^3}<0.
\]
At any stationary temperature, \(\beta=\mu_{\mathrm e}/\mathscr V_{\mathrm e}\), so its second derivative is strictly negative. The endpoint energy is positive on \((0,\infty)\) and tends to zero as \(\beta\downarrow0\) and \(\beta\to\infty\); the latter follows directly from its finitely many positive integer heights and its mass-one origin. Hence it has an interior global maximum. Every stationary point is a strict downward crossing of the derivative. There can be only one: two downward crossings would require another zero of the derivative between them at which the sign crosses upward, contrary to strict negative second derivative at every stationary point. Thus the endpoint has one stationary temperature \(\beta_{\mathrm e}\), and it is its unique global maximizer.

Here are exact rational bounds for that temperature. Substitution in the endpoint moment polynomials gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\zeta=21/40 &
20\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}
>13(\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}-\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}^2),
\quad20\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}<33\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e},\\[2pt]
\zeta=103/200 &
50\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}
<33(\mathscr A_{\mathrm e}\mathscr Z_{\mathrm e}-\mathscr N_{\mathrm e}^2).
\end{array}
\]
Again these are exact finite integer comparisons after clearing the indicated rational denominators. The following rational series inequalities bound the accompanying logarithms:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{(13/20)^j}{j!}&>\frac{40}{21},\\
2\left(\frac{97}{303}+\frac{(97/303)^3}{3}\right)&>\frac{33}{50},\\
\sum_{j=0}^{4}\frac{(2/3)^j}{j!}&>\frac{200}{103}.
\end{aligned}
\]
They imply, respectively,
\[
\log(40/21)<13/20,
\qquad33/50<\log(200/103)<2/3.
\]
The first endpoint moment inequality therefore gives positive energy derivative at \(\log(40/21)\); the last moment inequality gives negative derivative at \(\log(200/103)\). Uniqueness of the global stationary point yields
\[
\log(40/21)<\beta_{\mathrm e}<\log(200/103)<2/3.
\]
Since \(\mu_{\mathrm e}\) is strictly decreasing and its value at the left temperature is less than \(33/20\),
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{end}}
=\beta_{\mathrm e}\mu_{\mathrm e}(\beta_{\mathrm e})
<\frac23\frac{33}{20}=\frac{11}{10}.
\]
This bound covers the complete positive-temperature axis by the proved global uniqueness. It does not use a numerical optimizer, local derivative extrapolation, or a finite-temperature truncation as a maximum certificate.

Finally consider the different homogeneous potential \(h\) in the statement on all 21 sites. Its original weights are
\[
w_{(x,y)}=3^{-|x|}4^{-|y|},
\]
and again each coordinate has original weighted mean zero by symmetry, so its unique centering tilt is zero. The seven-site \(x\)-coordinate has mean absolute height \(36/53\). The three-site \(y\)-coordinate has weights \(1,1/4,1/4\) and mean absolute height \(1/3\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal G(h|_D)=\frac{36}{53}\log3+\frac13\log4
=\frac{36}{53}\log3+\frac23\log2.
\]
The logarithm series already used gives \(\log3>13/12\) and
\(\log2>2/3+2/81=56/81\). Hence
\[
\mathcal G(h|_D)
>\frac{39}{53}+\frac{112}{243}
=\frac{15413}{12879}>\frac{19}{16},
\]
where the final exact comparison is \(246608>244701\). The function \(h\) is finite, convex, and positively homogeneous on all of \(\mathbb R^2\), and the complete support remains exactly \(D\). This proves that the demonstrated failure is a failure of the particular radial endpoint, even after scalar optimization, and not a failure of every same-support positively homogeneous potential for the starting law.
