## Statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be compact, let $F_j$ be finitely many jointly continuous functions on $K$ times a neighborhood of zero, and assume
$$
F_j(y,t)=Q-g_j\cdot(y-y_0)+b_jt+o(\lVert y-y_0\rVert +|t|)
$$
near $(y_0,0)$, with $F_j(y_0,0)=Q$. Suppose $y_0$ uniquely maximizes $\min_jF_j(y,0)$ on $K$, zero is interior to $\operatorname{conv}\lbrace g_j\rbrace $ in $\mathbb R^d$, and $\theta_j\ge0$, $\sum_j\theta_j=1$, $\sum_j\theta_j g_j=0$. Then
$$
\max_{y\in K}\min_j F_j(y,t)\le Q+t\sum_j\theta_j b_j+o(t)
\quad(t\downarrow0).
$$

## Proof

Let $y_t$ be a maximizer, which exists by compactness. Joint continuity gives uniform convergence on $K$ as $t\to0$, so uniqueness forces $y_t\to y_0$. Positive spanning gives $c>0$ such that $\max_j g_j\cdot v\ge c\lVert v\rVert $ for every $v$. The finite first-order expansions now bound the perturbed minimum near $y_0$ by $Q-(c/2)\lVert y-y_0\rVert +C|t|$, for a common $C$. Its value at $y_0$ is at least $Q-C'|t|$. Thus $\lVert y_t-y_0\rVert =O(t)$ without a global time Lipschitz bound. At $y_t$ the minimum is no greater than the $\theta$-weighted average, whose mark term vanishes by balance and whose remainder is $o(t)$.
