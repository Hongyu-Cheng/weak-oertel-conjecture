## Statement

Fix $n,d\ge 1$, a finite nonempty $P\subset\mathbb R^n$, and nonempty compact convex sets $K_z\subset\mathbb R^d$ for $z\in P$. Write $S=\bigcup_{z\in P}(\{z\}\times K_z)$, $\mu=\sum_{z\in P}\delta_z\otimes(\mathcal L^d|_{K_z})$, and $M=\mu(S)$. Let $h_\mu(z,x)$ be the infimum of $\mu(H)$ over closed ambient halfspaces $H$ containing $(z,x)$. Assume $M>0$ and put $Q=\max_{(z,x)\in S}h_\mu(z,x)$. Suppose for every $\varepsilon>0$ the interiors of at most $N$ nonvertical closed ambient halfspaces cover $S$, each of mass less than $Q+\varepsilon M$. Then $S$ is covered by at most $N$ nonvertical closed ambient halfspaces of mass at most $Q$, with at least one of mass $Q$.

If $S=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)$ for a compact convex set $C\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}$, the hypothesis holds with $N=2^n(d+1)$. The conclusion at this cardinality covers $S$.

## Proof

Assume first the strict cover hypothesis. Take a sequence $\varepsilon_j\downarrow0$ and pass to a subsequence of covers with a constant number $k\le N$ of caps, with an indexing from $1$ to $k$ at each step. Apply the affine-threshold compactification successively to the $k$ sequences on a ball containing all fibers. All limiting traces are realized there by actual nonvertical closed halfspaces, each of mass at most $Q$ by mass convergence. For any fixed point of $S$, some cap index contains it infinitely often, so the closed membership property puts it in the corresponding realizing cap. This applies to every point of $S$, including boundary points and null fibers. If each of these finitely many caps had mass strictly below $Q$, their largest mass would bound $h_\mu$ at every point of $S$ by a number strictly below $Q$, a contradiction.

Now let $S=C\cap\Lambda$ with $\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$. At each point of $S$, the depth formula and finite attainment for the anchored capacity give a nonvertical closed halfspace through it of mass at most $Q$. Decreasing its threshold slightly puts the point in the interior and keeps the mass less than $Q+\varepsilon M$, by dominated convergence on the fibers with fixed unit continuous normal. Compactness gives a finite interior cover. Its complementary closed halfspaces, together with $C$, have empty common intersection on $\Lambda$. Use the finite mixed-integer Helly theorem documented in accepted fact `6d6fcf377e68c79c`, namely $h(\Lambda)=2^n(d+1)$, from [Averkov and Weismantel, Theorem 1.1(3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0948). This theorem applies to the listed ambient convex sets, with the coordinate blocks permuted from the reference convention. Some at most $2^n(d+1)$ members already have empty intersection on $\Lambda$. The complementary caps corresponding to the halfspaces in this subfamily have interiors covering $S$, whether or not $C$ was selected. This proves the strict hypothesis at the asserted cardinality.
