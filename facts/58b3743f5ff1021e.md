## Statement

Set \(n=1\), \(d=2\), and \(\Delta_2=\{u\in\mathbb R^2:u_1,u_2\ge0,\ u_1+u_2\le1\}\). On the base \([-1,1]\), define the canonical facet functions and their sum by
\[
\psi_0(x)=\min\{7,7-6x\},\qquad
\psi_1(x)=\psi_2(x)=0,\qquad
\psi(x)=(\psi_1(x),\psi_2(x)),\qquad
L(x)=\sum_{i=0}^2\psi_i(x)=\psi_0(x).
\tag{1}
\]
Let
\[
C=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2:
-1\le x\le1,\ y_1,y_2\ge0,\ y_1+y_2\le7,\ y_1+y_2\le7-6x\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(C_x=-\psi(x)+L(x)\Delta_2\) for \(x\in[-1,1]\), and the other fibers are empty. For an integer \(z\), let \(\mu_z=\mathcal L^2|_{C_z}\), let \(w_z=\mu_z(\mathbb R^2)\), and retain the locked definitions \(S=C\cap(\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2)\), \(W=\sum_z w_z=\mathcal H_2(S)\), and \(h_S\). The complete positive integer support is \(D=\{-1,0,1\}\), and
\[
C_{-1}=C_0=7\Delta_2,\qquad C_1=\Delta_2,\qquad
w_{-1}=w_0=\frac{49}{2},\qquad w_1=\frac12,\qquad W=\frac{99}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(Q\) be uniform probability on \(\Delta_2\), so \(Q=2\mathcal L^2|_{\Delta_2}\), and define \(F_z(u)=-\psi(z)+L(z)u\). At target zero the permitted outputs are
\[
\kappa_0=\sigma+B_\#\eta,\qquad
B(u)=\frac{F_{-1}(u)+F_1(u)}2,
\qquad
0\le\sigma\le\mu_0,\quad
(F_{-1})_\#\eta\le\mu_{-1},\quad (F_1)_\#\eta\le\mu_1,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(\eta\) is any finite nonnegative Borel measure on \(\Delta_2\). Repeated copies of the outer pair are permitted provided their measures are summed in the source capacities; they merge into (4). At \(J\in\{-1,1\}\), the permitted output satisfies \(0\le\kappa_J\le\mu_J\). For every such target and output, and every nonnegative measurable log-concave Lebesgue density \(f\) satisfying \(f\,d\mathcal L^2\le\kappa_J\) as Borel measures, one has
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}f(y)\,dy\le\frac{49}{2}<\frac{99}{4}=\frac W2.
\tag{5}
\]
The value \(49/2\) is attained by \(J=0\), \(\eta=0\), \(\sigma=\mu_0\), and \(f=\mathbf1_{7\Delta_2}\).

The notation and source normalization in (3)-(4) are those of accepted facts `793210f8a4be3647` and `b996315081af6e10`: their source indices \(\mathbf I\) or \(z\) are the present \(z\), their target \(\mathbf J\) is the present \(J\), and \(U_z=(F_z)_\#Q=\mu_z/w_z\). Each occurrence of the outer pair uses the entire mass \(|\eta|\) at each source, with no factor \(1/2\). The predecessor's scalar central mass is \(|\sigma|\), and its output mass is \(|\kappa_J|\). The canonical fiber length \(L(x)\) in (1) is distinct from the standalone root-volume cutoff called \(L\) in `793210f8a4be3647`, which would be denoted \(L_{\mathrm{cut}}\) here; no cutoff restriction is imposed. The root volumes satisfy \(w_z^{1/2}=L(z)/\sqrt2\); the outer pair has the predecessor's root-mean volume \(V_\lambda=((w_{-1}^{1/2}+w_1^{1/2})/2)^2=8\) and logarithmic cost \(L_\lambda=\log(w_0/V_\lambda)=\log(49/16)\). In particular neither a root volume nor a logarithmic cutoff is substituted for the length \(L(x)\).

## Proof

The functions in (1) are continuous concave rational piecewise-affine functions. The minimum of the two displayed affine functions is concave, and \(1\le L(x)\le7\) throughout \([-1,1]\). Thus the bundle is strict-positive. Description (2) makes \(C\) a closed bounded rational convex polytope. The point \((-1/2,1,1)\) satisfies every defining inequality strictly, so \(C\) has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^3\). Its fibers follow directly from (1), and \(\operatorname{vol}_2(t\Delta_2)=t^2/2\) proves every entry of (3). The base contains exactly the three stated integers.

We first determine a measure upper bound on all central outputs, using actual source capacities. The maps are \(F_{-1}(u)=7u\), \(F_0(u)=7u\), \(F_1(u)=u\), and \(B(u)=4u\). The pullback of \(\mu_z\) under \(F_z\) equals \(w_zQ\). Consequently both inequalities in (4), including any repeated-pair usages, imply \(\eta\le\min(w_{-1},w_1)Q=Q/2\). Pushing this measure through \(B\) and using its Jacobian \(16\) gives
\[
\kappa_0\le g\,d\mathcal L^2,
\qquad
g(y)=\mathbf1_{7\Delta_2}(y)+\frac1{16}\mathbf1_{4\Delta_2}(y).
\tag{6}
\]
Equality in (6) is feasible by taking \(\sigma=\mu_0\) and \(\eta=Q/2\). Its mass is \(25\), which exceeds \(W/2\); the issue is extraction of a log-concave subdensity, not shortage of unretained output mass. Formula (6) bounds every choice in (4), without requiring those choices to maximize their mass.

For clarity about aggregate retention, if \(\rho\le\kappa_0\), let \(r=d\rho/d\kappa_0\) take values in \([0,1]\). Replacing \(\sigma\) by \(r\sigma\) and \(\eta\) by \((r\circ B)\eta\) realizes exactly \(\rho\) and decreases both source marginals. This is the arbitrary-retention convention of accepted fact `b996315081af6e10`, applied with its actual common-coordinate maps. Thus an upper bound for all log-concave \(f\le g\) almost everywhere covers every permitted source and aggregate retention.

We prove the elementary one-dimensional estimate needed for that upper bound. Let \(s=4/7\). Suppose \(\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)\) is measurable and log-concave, with
\(\varphi(t)\le17/16\) for almost every \(0<t<s\), and \(\varphi(t)\le1\) for almost every \(s<t<1\). Then
\[
2\int_0^1t\varphi(t)\,dt\le1.
\tag{7}
\]
All assertions in this estimate concern integrals, so values on null sets and at support endpoints do not matter.

We shall use three elementary exponential bounds. For \(0\le v\le1\), the inequality \(j!\ge2\cdot3^{j-2}\) for integers \(j\ge2\), followed by a geometric-series sum, gives
\[
e^v\le1+v+\frac{v^2}{2(1-v/3)}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular \(e\le11/4\), \(e^{4/7}\le211/119<9/5\), and \(e^{3/7}\le43/28<8/5\). Thus
\[
2e^{4/7}(1-2/e)<\frac{54}{55}<1,
\qquad e^{-3/7}>\frac58.
\tag{9}
\]
The factor \(1-2/e\) is positive, since the exponential series gives \(e>2\).

If \(\varphi\le1\) almost everywhere, (7) follows from \(2\int_0^1t\,dt=1\). Otherwise \(\varphi>1\) on a set of positive measure in \((0,s)\). If \(\varphi=0\) almost everywhere in \((s,1)\), then \(2\int_0^1t\varphi(t)\,dt\le(17/16)s^2=17/49<1\). It remains to consider the case where \(\varphi\) is also positive on a set of positive measure to the right of \(s\).

The positive support of a log-concave function is an interval. In the remaining case, \(s\) lies in the interior of that interval, and \(h=\log\varphi\) is a finite concave function there. Such a function is continuous in the interior and has a supporting affine upper bound at every interior point; this follows from the monotonicity of its secant slopes. The almost-everywhere bound to the right of \(s\) therefore implies \(h(s)\le0\). Choose an interior point \(t_0<s\) with \(h(t_0)>0\), and a supporting slope \(m\) at \(s\). The inequality \(h(t_0)\le h(s)+m(t_0-s)\) forces \(m<0\). Put \(k=-m>0\). On the entire positive support, the supporting-line inequality gives \(h(t)\le h(s)+k(s-t)\le k(s-t)\). Outside that support the density is zero. Hence, almost everywhere,
\[
\varphi(t)\le e^{k(s-t)}\quad(0<t<1),
\qquad
\varphi(t)\le\frac{17}{16}\quad(0<t<s).
\tag{10}
\]
This step imposes no affine or uniform form on \(\varphi\); the exponential is only an upper bound derived from its concavity.

If \(0<k\le1\), convexity of the exponential as a function of \(k\) gives \(e^{k(s-t)}\le1-k+k e^{s-t}\). Direct integration and (9) yield
\[
2\int_0^1t\varphi(t)\,dt
\le1-k+2k e^s\int_0^1t e^{-t}\,dt
=1-k+2k e^{4/7}(1-2/e)<1.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(k\ge1\), use the second bound in (10) on \((0,s)\), and \(e^{k(s-t)}\le e^{s-t}\) on \((s,1)\). Again integrating explicitly and applying (9) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\int_0^1t\varphi(t)\,dt
&\le\frac{17}{16}s^2+2e^s\int_s^1t e^{-t}\,dt\\
&=\frac{17}{49}+\frac{22}{7}-4e^{-3/7}
<\frac{17}{49}+\frac{22}{7}-\frac52
=\frac{97}{98}<1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
These cases prove (7) for all of the stated profiles, including those with a proper convex support or with zero density on part of the interval.

Now let \(f\) be any log-concave density dominated by the central bound (6). It vanishes almost everywhere outside \(7\Delta_2\). Parameterize that simplex, up to a null set, by
\(y=7t(\theta,1-\theta)\) with \(0<t<1\) and \(0<\theta<1\). This change of variables has absolute Jacobian \(49t\). By Fubini's theorem, for almost every fixed \(\theta\) the ray profile \(\varphi_\theta(t)=f(7t(\theta,1-\theta))\) has precisely the almost-everywhere bounds in (7), since membership in \(4\Delta_2\) is equivalent to \(t\le4/7\). Restriction to an affine line preserves log-concavity. Thus (7) applies to almost every ray, with no symmetry assumption on \(f\), and
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2} f(y)\,dy
=49\int_0^1\int_0^1t\varphi_\theta(t)\,dt\,d\theta
\le\frac{49}{2}.
\tag{13}
\]
If log-concavity is specified through a representative of a Lebesgue density, use that representative in the ray restriction; its null-set modifications do not change either the domination or (13). A ray with zero integral already satisfies (7).

At target \(-1\), the allowed output is at most \(\mu_{-1}\), so every retained density has mass at most \(49/2\). At target \(1\), the corresponding upper bound is \(1/2\). These statements also follow directly from the pair rule: two distinct indices in \(\{-1,0,1\}\) cannot have midpoint \(-1\) or \(1\). Together with (13) they establish (5) for every target. The central choice \(f=\mathbf1_{7\Delta_2}\) is log-concave because its support is convex, is feasible using only \(\sigma=\mu_0\), and has mass \(49/2\). This proves the claimed exact optimum.

Finally, the capacities used here retain their physical meaning for the locked problem. For every closed ambient halfspace \(H\), with sections \(H_z=\{y:(z,y)\in H\}\), the midpoint relation gives \(\mathbf1_{H_0}(B(u))\le\mathbf1_{H_{-1}}(F_{-1}(u))+\mathbf1_{H_1}(F_1(u))\). If both source scores were strictly outside \(H\), their average would be strictly outside. Integrating this inequality and using the full marginal capacities in (4) gives \(\kappa_0(H_0)\le\sum_{z\in D}\mu_z(H_z)=\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\), including closed boundaries and vertical halfspaces. This is the transfer convention of accepted fact `793210f8a4be3647`. The counterexample restricts the mass that can be made log-concave under these capacities; it does not infer any upper bound on the actual depth \(h_S\).
