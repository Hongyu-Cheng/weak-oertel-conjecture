## Statement

For a finite complete support \(D\subset\mathbb Z^N\), a probability law \(p\) on \(D\), and \(\mathbf z\in D\), define its actual closed-halfspace depth and cubic depth moment by
\[
\tau_p(\mathbf z)
=\min\left\{
p(H): H\subseteq\mathbb R^N\text{ is a closed halfspace and }\mathbf z\in H
\right\},
\quad
p(H)=\sum_{\mathbf x\in D\cap H}p(\mathbf x),
\quad
M_3(p)=\sum_{\mathbf z\in D}p(\mathbf z)\tau_p(\mathbf z)^3.
\]
Use
\[
p_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf z)=e^{\bm{\theta}\cdot\mathbf z}/Z_D(\bm{\theta}),
\quad
F_D(\bm{\theta})=M_3(p_{\bm{\theta}}).
\]
Put \(\Sigma_p=\operatorname{Cov}_p(\mathbf X)\).

Put \(e_{\mathbf z}=-\log p(\mathbf z)\). For each occupied parity class
\(\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z^N/2\mathbb Z^N\), meaning
\(D\cap(\mathbf c+2\mathbb Z^N)\ne\varnothing\), list its distinct energies as
\(e_{\mathbf c,1}<\cdots<e_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}}\) and define the canonical parity-energy capacity
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z^N/2\mathbb Z^N\\
D\cap(\mathbf c+2\mathbb Z^N)\ne\varnothing}}
\left[1+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\mathbf c}-1}
\tanh\frac{e_{\mathbf c,i+1}-e_{\mathbf c,i}}4\right].
\]
Equivalently, if the distinct log-probabilities in class \(\mathbf c\) are listed as
\(y_{\mathbf c,1}<\cdots<y_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}}\), then
\(e_{\mathbf c,i}=-y_{\mathbf c,k_{\mathbf c}+1-i}\); reversing their order preserves the adjacent gaps. Thus the local notation is the exact alias
\[
\mathcal L(p):=\kappa_{\mathrm{par}}(p)=
\sum_{\substack{\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z^N/2\mathbb Z^N\\
D\cap(\mathbf c+2\mathbb Z^N)\ne\varnothing}}
\left[1+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\mathbf c}-1}
\tanh\frac{y_{\mathbf c,i+1}-y_{\mathbf c,i}}4\right].
\]
An empty parity class contributes zero.

The following three families exist.

(a) For every fixed \(N\ge2\), there are finite strict local minima \(p_m\) with unique minimizing traces and
\[
8^NM_3(p_m)\to\frac14,\qquad
8^N\sum p_m^4\to0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{p_m}(\log p_m)\sim\frac14(\log m)^2\to\infty.
\]
They satisfy \(\nabla^2\log F_D\succ8\Sigma_p\).

(b) There are such strict minima \(p_k\) in dimension \(k+1\) satisfying
\[
8^{k+1}M_3(p_k)\to\frac14,\qquad
8^{k+1}\sum p_k^4\to0,
\]
\[
\sup_a p_k\{\log p_k\in[a,a+L]\}\to0
\quad\text{for every fixed }L>0,
\qquad
\mathcal L(p_k)=2^{k+1}.
\]

(c) For every \(\eta>0\), there is a fixed dimension \(N\) and a sequence of such strict minima \(p_L\) satisfying
\[
\frac14<8^NM_3(p_L)<\frac14+\eta,\qquad
8^N\sum p_L^4<\eta,\qquad
\mathcal L(p_L)/2^N\to\infty.
\]

None of these assertions gives a vanishing normalized cubic moment.

## Proof

We first record the common interval calculation. Let
\(I_m=\{1-m,\ldots,m\}\), and let \(f_m(t)\) be the cubic moment of its exponential tilt. At \(t=0\), the endpoint-cap cardinalities at each point are unequal because \(2m\) is even. Thus all minimizing traces are unique and locally fixed. Reflection about \(1/2\) gives \(f_m'(0)=0\), and
\[
c_m=f_m(0)=\frac{(m+1)^2}{32m^2},
\]
\[
f_m''(0)
=\frac{(m+1)(18m^4+8m^3+5m^2+3m+2)}{192m^3}. \tag{1}
\]
To verify the second formula, center the interval at \(1/2\). A left cap of size \(i\) contributes weight \(i^3/(2m)^4\) to the quartic event. Conditional on this contribution, the sum of the four centered observations has mean
\((5i-8m-1)/2\) and variance \((i^2-1)/4\).
Differentiating the numerator divided by the fourth power of the partition function gives
\[
f_m''(0)=\frac2{(2m)^4}\sum_{i=1}^m i^3
\left[\frac{(5i-8m-1)^2+i^2-1}{4}
-4\sigma_m^2\right],
\quad
\sigma_m^2=\frac{4m^2-1}{12}.
\]
Power-sum identities give (1). Moreover,
\[
f_m''(0)/c_m>8\sigma_m^2, \tag{2}
\]
because the numerator after clearing denominators is
\(4m^2(m-2)^2+2m^2+14m+4>0\).

Whenever a finite support is a union of exposed fibers, a point in a fiber of mass \(\alpha\) and conditional law \(q\) has actual depth exactly \(\alpha\tau_q\). Restriction gives the lower bound. Adding a sufficiently steep exposing functional to a conditional minimizing cap excludes all other fibers and proves equality. Positive weights then force every minimizing trace to stay in its own fiber.

For (a), take
\[
D_{m,M}=(\{0\}\times I_m)\cup(\{1\}\times I_M).
\]
Its convex hull is a trapezoid with integer rows only at \(0,1\), so it is complete and full-dimensional. Use all parameters \((s,t)\) with weights \(e^{sr+t(x-1/2)}\). If the row masses are \(a,b\), exposed-fiber depth gives
\[
F(s,t)=a(s,t)^4f_m(t)+b(s,t)^4f_M(t).
\]
Let \(r_m=c_m^{1/3}\), and set
\[
a_*=\frac{r_M}{r_m+r_M},\quad
b_*=\frac{r_m}{r_m+r_M},\quad
s_*=\log\left(\frac mM\frac{r_m}{r_M}\right).
\]
At \((s_*,0)\),
\[
a_*^3c_m=b_*^3c_M=F_*,
\qquad
F_*=(c_m^{-1/3}+c_M^{-1/3})^{-3}.
\]
Both derivatives vanish. Centered row partition functions are even in \(t\); the contribution from their second derivatives cancels because the objective is stationary in the row mass. Thus the full Hessian is
\[
\nabla^2F=
\begin{pmatrix}
12a_*b_*F_*&0\\
0&a_*^4f_m''(0)+b_*^4f_M''(0)
\end{pmatrix}\succ0. \tag{3}
\]
The covariance is
\[
\Sigma_p=\operatorname{diag}
(a_*b_*,\,a_*\sigma_m^2+b_*\sigma_M^2).
\]
Equation (2), together with \(a_*^4c_m=a_*F_*\) and its second-row analogue, gives \(\nabla^2\log F\succ8\Sigma_p\).

Take \(M=m^2\). Then \(a_*,b_*\to1/2\), \(s_*=-\log m+O(m^{-1})\), and
\[
64F_*\to1/4,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p)=a_*b_*s_*^2,
\]
\[
\sum p^4=\frac{a_*^4}{(2m)^3}+\frac{b_*^4}{(2m^2)^3}.
\]
These prove (a) for \(N=2\). Adjoining \(N-2\) independent fair Boolean coordinates multiplies \(M_3\) and the atomic fourth sum by \(8^{-(N-2)}\), leaves the log-weight variance unchanged, and adds strictly positive Hessian blocks. Indeed, the Boolean factor is \(p^4+(1-p)^4\), whose log-Hessian at the fair tilt is \(12\operatorname{Var}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2))\). This proves (a).

For (b), put
\[
m_0=2^{16}k^2,\quad R_k=2^k,\quad
j(\bm{\epsilon})=\sum_{i=1}^k2^{i-1}\epsilon_i,\quad
m_{\bm{\epsilon}}=m_0R_k^{j(\bm{\epsilon})},
\]
and
\[
D_k=\bigcup_{\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^k}
\{\bm{\epsilon}\}\times I_{m_{\bm{\epsilon}}}.
\]
Completeness follows because every lattice point of the Boolean projection is a vertex and can only use its own interval fiber.

Use parameters \((\mathbf s,t)\) with weights
\(e^{\mathbf s\cdot\bm{\epsilon}+t(x-1/2)}\).
At \(t=0\), set \(y_i=s_i+2^{i-1}\log R_k\). The fiber probabilities become the product-Bernoulli law
\[
\alpha_{\bm{\epsilon}}(\mathbf y)
=\frac{e^{\mathbf y\cdot\bm{\epsilon}}}{\prod_i(1+e^{y_i})},
\]
and
\[
G_k(\mathbf y)=\sum_{\bm{\epsilon}}
c_{m_{\bm{\epsilon}}}\alpha_{\bm{\epsilon}}(\mathbf y)^4.
\]
Write \(c_\infty=1/32\) and \(r=(1+1/m_0)^2\). Every coefficient lies in
\([c_\infty,rc_\infty]\). A boundary product law has at most \(2^{k-1}\) atoms, so its value is at least \(8c_\infty8^{-k}\), while \(G_k(0)\le rc_\infty8^{-k}\). Thus a global minimizer \(\mathbf y_*\) is finite.

Let \(u(y)=\cosh(2y)/\cosh^4(y/2)\). Comparison with zero gives
\[
\sum_i\log u(y_{*,i})\le\log r\le2/m_0.
\]
The function \(\log u\) is even and increasing in \(|y|\), and its second derivative exceeds \(2\) on \([-1/4,1/4]\). Therefore
\[
|y_{*,i}|<1/4,\quad
\|\mathbf y_*\|_2^2\le2/m_0,\quad
\|\mathbf y_*\|_1<1/128. \tag{4}
\]

Define
\(\nu_{\bm{\epsilon}}
=c_{m_{\bm{\epsilon}}}\alpha_{\bm{\epsilon}}^4/G_k\).
Then
\[
\nabla^2\log G_k
=16\operatorname{Cov}_\nu(\bm{\epsilon})
-4\operatorname{Cov}_\alpha(\bm{\epsilon}).
\]
The density of \(\nu\) relative to the product law at parameter \(4\mathbf y\) is at least \(1/r\). Hence on the displayed cube,
\[
\nabla^2\log G_k
\succeq\left(\frac4{r\cosh^2(1/2)}-1\right)I\succ2I.
\]
At the corresponding \((\mathbf s_*,0)\), reflection makes the \(t\)-derivative and every mixed derivative vanish. Direct differentiation gives
\[
F_{tt}/F
=\mathbb E_\nu[f_m''(0)/c_m+4\sigma_m^2]
-4\mathbb E_\alpha\sigma_m^2.
\]
Equation (4) implies \(\nu_{\bm{\epsilon}}\ge(3/4)\alpha_{\bm{\epsilon}}\). Equation (2) therefore makes the last display strictly greater than
\(5\mathbb E_\alpha\sigma_m^2>0\). The full Hessian is positive definite.

Coefficient comparison gives
\[
\frac14\le8^{k+1}F\le r/4,\qquad
8^{k+1}\sum p^4\le r/m_0^3.
\]
The log-weight is constant in each fiber, and distinct fibers have energy gaps at least
\(k\log2-\|\mathbf y_*\|_1\), while their masses are at most
\(e^{1/128}2^{-k}\). This proves the anti-concentration claim. Every parity class fixes the Boolean label, so it has one energy level; both interval parities occur. Hence \(\mathcal L(p_k)=2^{k+1}\).

For (c), use the two-row support \(B_m=D_{m,m^2}\) from (a), with strict minimum \((s_m,0)\). Choose \(m\) large enough that \(s_m<-\log2\). For an integer \(r\ge2\), set
\(S_r=\{0,1\}^r\setminus\{\mathbf1\}\), \(K=2^r-1\), and define
\[
D_{R,L}=(S_r\times B_m)
\cup\left(\{\mathbf1\}\times\{(R+j,0):0\le j\le L\}\right).
\]
The Boolean-fiber argument proves completeness and full dimensionality.

Use parameters \((\bm{\beta},s,t)\) with weights
\(e^{\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}+sa+tx}\).
Let \(Z_S(\bm{\beta})=\sum_{\bm{\epsilon}\in S_r}e^{\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}}\), and let \(Z_B\) be the base partition function. For \(s<-\log2\), the tail's first atom has singleton minimizing trace and every later atom has its suffix as unique minimizing trace. Put
\[
z_L(s)=\sum_{j=0}^Le^{sj},\qquad
q_L(s)=1+\sum_{j=1}^Le^{sj}\left(\sum_{\ell=j}^Le^{s\ell}\right)^3.
\]
Exact exposed-fiber depth gives
\[
F_{D_{R,L}}
=
\frac{
Z_S(4\bm{\beta})Z_B(s,t)^4F_{B_m}(s,t)
+e^{4\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf1+4Rs}q_L(s)}
{[Z_S(\bm{\beta})Z_B(s,t)
+e^{\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf1+Rs}z_L(s)]^4}. \tag{5}
\]
On a fixed small neighborhood of \((0,s_m,0)\), \(z_L,q_L\) and their first two derivatives are bounded uniformly in \(L\), by convergent geometric series with polynomial factors. Thus (5) converges in \(C^2\), uniformly over all \(L\), as \(R\to\infty\), to
\[
F_0(\bm{\beta},s,t)
=\frac{Z_S(4\bm{\beta})}{Z_S(\bm{\beta})^4}F_{B_m}(s,t).
\]
At zero label tilt its label log-Hessian is
\(12\operatorname{Cov}_{\mathrm{Unif}(S_r)}\succ0\).
Together with (3), this gives a nondegenerate strict minimum of \(F_0\).

Choose a sufficiently small closed ball where the Hessian remains positive definite and the center value is strictly below the boundary values. Uniform \(C^2\) convergence gives an interior strict minimum of (5) in this ball for all sufficiently large \(R\), uniformly in \(L\). All conditional traces remain fixed there, so full traces are unique.

The normalized reference value is
\[
\left(\frac{2^r}{2^r-1}\right)^3
64F_{B_m}(s_m,0).
\]
First choose \(r,m\), then the ball and \(R\), so that all finite minima lie strictly between \(1/4\) and \(1/4+\eta\), and their normalized atomic fourth sums are below \(\eta\).

Fix these choices and let \(L\to\infty\). The tail occupies two parity classes not shared with the main fibers. Its log-weights have step \(s_{R,L}\), so its capacity contribution is
\[
2+(L-1)\tanh(|s_{R,L}|/2).
\]
The parameters remain in a neighborhood with \(s_{R,L}\le-c<0\), proving divergence in fixed dimension.

For the strengthened covariance interpretation in (a), at fixed minimizing traces the objective is a quartic exponential sum divided by \(Z^4\). Its log-Hessian is
\(\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf U\mid E)-4\Sigma_p\).
Thus the asserted Hessian slack also gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf U\mid E)\succ12\Sigma_p\).
