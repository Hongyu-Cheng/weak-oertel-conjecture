## Statement

Fix integers \(k\ge1\) and even \(B\ge2\), and put \(n=k+1\) and \(m=2^k\). For \(\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k\), define \(h(\boldsymbol\eta)=1+\sum_{i=1}^k2^{i-1}\eta_i\) and \(L_h=B^h\). In coordinates \((\mathbf x,t)\in\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R\), let

\[
P_{k,B}=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k}
\bigl(\{\boldsymbol\eta\}\times[0,L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}-1]\bigr),
\qquad
D=\{(\boldsymbol\eta,j):\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k,\ 0\le j<L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)},\ j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Then \(P_{k,B}\) is a full-dimensional polytope, \(D=P_{k,B}\cap\mathbb Z^n\), and the positive weights \(w_{(\boldsymbol\eta,j)}=B^{-h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\), extended by zero to the other integer indices, are the restriction of a globally log-concave function whose logarithm is affine on \(P_{k,B}\). Their total weight is \(W=m\). Write \(p_{\mathbf J}=w_{\mathbf J}/W\), and use the canonical normalized depth and second moment

\[
\tau_p(\mathbf J)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf J}\sum_{\mathbf I\in D\cap H}p_{\mathbf I},
\qquad
M_2(p)=\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}p_{\mathbf J}\tau_p(\mathbf J)^2.
\tag{2}
\]

All halfspaces in this packet are closed proper halfspaces in the displayed ambient Euclidean space. The unnormalized aliases are \(T_w(\mathbf J)=W\tau_p(\mathbf J)\) and \(F_2(w)=W^3M_2(p)\). At every \(\mathbf J=(\boldsymbol\eta,j)\in D\), with \(h=h(\boldsymbol\eta)\),

\[
T_w(\boldsymbol\eta,j)=B^{-h}\min(j+1,L_h-j),
\qquad
\tau_p(\boldsymbol\eta,j)=\frac{\min(j+1,L_h-j)}{mL_h}.
\tag{3}
\]

For even \(L\ge2\), put \(\mu_2(L)=(L+1)(L+2)/(12L^2)\). The exact second-moment identities are

\[
M_2(p)=\frac1{m^3}\sum_{h=1}^m\mu_2(B^h),
\qquad
4^nM_2(p)=\frac13+\frac1m\sum_{h=1}^m B^{-h}
+\frac{2}{3m}\sum_{h=1}^m B^{-2h}.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(s>0\), define \(A_s=\{\mathbf J\in D:w_{\mathbf J}\ge s\}\), \(N(s)=|A_s|\), and \(d_s(\mathbf J)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf J}|A_s\cap H|\) for every \(\mathbf J\in D\), including those outside \(A_s\). Define \(w_h=B^{-h}\) for \(1\le h\le m\), \(w_{m+1}=0\), \(\Delta_h=w_h^3-w_{h+1}^3\), and \(S_h=\sum_{r=1}^hB^r\). On \(w_{h+1}<s\le w_h\), the set \(A_s\) consists exactly of the first \(h\) labelled intervals; it is complete lattice-convex, has cardinality \(S_h\), and has affine rank
\(r_h=1+\lceil\log_2 h\rceil\). For all \(s>0\) and \((\boldsymbol\eta,j)\in D\),

\[
d_s(\boldsymbol\eta,j)=
\begin{cases}
\min(j+1,L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}-j),&s\le B^{-h(\boldsymbol\eta)},\\
0,&s>B^{-h(\boldsymbol\eta)}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Define the actual-depth and scalar comparison functionals by

\[
\mathcal L(w)=\frac3{W^3}\int_0^\infty s^2
\sum_{\mathbf J\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf J)^2\,ds,
\qquad
\mathcal R(w)=\frac3{W^3}\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)^3\,ds.
\tag{6}
\]

Define the two cardinality comparison functionals, retaining their diagonal terms, by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_n(w)
&=\frac3{4W^3}\int_0^\infty
s^2N(s)(2^{-n}N(s)+1)^2\,ds\\
&=\frac{3\,4^{-n}}{4W^3}\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)^3\,ds
+\frac{3\,2^{-n}}{2W^3}\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)^2\,ds
+\frac1{4W^3}\sum_{\mathbf J\in D}w_{\mathbf J}^3,\\
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)
&=\frac1{4m^3}\sum_{h=1}^m
\Delta_h S_h(2^{-r_h}S_h+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

For this family these quantities satisfy the exact identities and comparisons

\[
\mathcal L(w)=M_2(p),
\qquad
\mathcal R(w)=\frac1{m^3}\sum_{h=1}^m\Delta_h S_h^3,
\qquad
\mathcal S_n(w)=\frac1{4m^3}\sum_{h=1}^m
\Delta_h S_h(2^{-n}S_h+1)^2
\le\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)\le M_2(p).
\tag{8}
\]

In all the following inner limits, \(k\), and therefore \(m,n\), is fixed and \(B\) tends to infinity through even integers:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_B\mathcal R(w)&=m^{-2}=4^{1-n},&
\lim_B\mathcal S_n(w)&=16^{-n},&
\lim_B M_2(p)&=\tfrac13\,4^{-n},\\
\lim_B4^n\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)&=\frac{3m-1}{8m^2},&
\lim_B\frac{\mathcal S_n(w)}{M_2(p)}&=3\,4^{-n},&
\lim_B\frac{\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)}{M_2(p)}&=\frac{3(3m-1)}{8m^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular, both \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_B4^n\mathcal S_n(w)\) and \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_B4^n\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)\) are zero, whereas \(\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_B4^nM_2(p)=1/3\). More explicitly, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is \(k_0\) such that for every \(k\ge k_0\) there is \(B_0(k,\varepsilon)\) for which every even \(B\ge B_0(k,\varepsilon)\) satisfies
\(4^n\mathcal S_n(w)<\varepsilon\), \(4^n\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}(w)<\varepsilon\), and \(|4^nM_2(p)-1/3|<\varepsilon\). No interchange of these limits or dimension-independent choice of \(B_0\) is asserted.

If \(\mathbf J\) has law \(p\), then

\[
2^n\max_{\mathbf J\in D}p_{\mathbf J}=\frac2B,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_p(-\log p_{\mathbf J})
=\frac{m^2-1}{12}(\log B)^2,
\qquad
p(F\cap D)\le\frac12
\tag{10}
\]

for every proper face \(F\) of \(P_{k,B}\). Thus in each fixed integer dimension \(n\ge2\), the variance is unbounded as the scaled largest atom tends to zero, with the face bound holding throughout.

For every integer \(d\ge1\), define \(K_h=B^{-h/d}[-1/2,1/2]^d\) and the convex body

\[
C_{k,B,d}=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\{0,1\}^k}
\bigl(\{\boldsymbol\eta\}\times[0,L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}-1]\times K_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\bigr)
\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}.
\tag{11}
\]

Its complete fiber at every original integer index \((\boldsymbol\eta,j)\in D\) is exactly \(K_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\), and its fibers at all other integer indices are empty. Hence \(W=\mathcal H_d(C_{k,B,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d))=m\) is exactly the original mixed-integer volume, where \(\mathcal H_d\) sums ordinary \(d\)-dimensional volumes over the original integer fibers. Write \(S=C_{k,B,d}\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d)\) and define its original unnormalized depth by \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_d(S\cap H)\), with ambient halfspaces \(H\subset\mathbb R^{n+d}\). For \((\boldsymbol\eta,j)\in D\), put \(a=\min(j,L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}-1-j)\). Then, for every \(\mathbf u\in K_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}\),

\[
h_S(\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf u)
\le\frac{a+1/2}{L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}},
\qquad
h_S(\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf0)
=\frac{a+1/2}{L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}}.
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently the maximum is attained and has the exact value

\[
\frac{\max_{\mathbf y\in S}h_S(\mathbf y)}{W}
=\frac{1-B^{-m}}{2m}
=(1-B^{-m})2^{-n}.
\tag{13}
\]

## Proof

The projection of \(P_{k,B}\) onto its first \(k\) coordinates is the Boolean cube. An integer point in this projection has a Boolean label. If a convex combination of Boolean labels equals \(\boldsymbol\eta\), each coordinate equal to zero forces that coordinate of every positively weighted label to be zero, and each coordinate equal to one forces it to be one. Thus all positively weighted labels equal \(\boldsymbol\eta\). The section of \(P_{k,B}\) at that label is exactly \([0,L_{h(\boldsymbol\eta)}-1]\). This proves the complete support statement, including both endpoints and the absence of other integer points. The points \((\mathbf0,0)\), \((\mathbf e_i,0)\) for \(1\le i\le k\), and \((\mathbf0,1)\) belong to \(P_{k,B}\); their differences span \(\mathbb R^{k+1}\). This proves full dimensionality.

The real function
\(f(\mathbf x,t)=\exp[-(\log B)(1+\sum_{i=1}^k2^{i-1}x_i)]\mathbf1_{P_{k,B}}(\mathbf x,t)\)
restricts to the asserted lattice weights and zeros. Its logarithm is affine wherever it is positive, and its positive domain is convex. The log-concavity inequality follows with equality when both endpoints and their segment lie in the positive domain, and with a zero right-hand side if an endpoint lies outside. Each labelled interval has \(L_h\) points of weight \(1/L_h\), hence total weight one and \(W=m\). This extension is only an extension of the lattice table; it is not claimed to be the fiber-volume function of (11) at noninteger indices.

We compute the base depths directly. Restrict a halfspace containing \((\boldsymbol\eta,j)\) to the line with this label. Its intersection is the whole line or a closed ray containing \(j\); the empty intersection is impossible. The whole line contains all \(L_h\) supported indices, and either ray contains at least \(\min(j+1,L_h-j)\) of them. This proves the lower bound in (3).

For a finite explicit upper cut, set
\(a_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf x)=\sum_{i=1}^k(2\eta_i-1)(x_i-\eta_i)\).
It is zero at \(\boldsymbol\eta\) and equals minus the number of differing coordinates at any other Boolean label, hence is at most \(-1\) there. The two halfspaces
\(L_m a_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf x)+(t-j)\ge0\) and
\(L_m a_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf x)-(t-j)\ge0\)
contain the target on their boundaries. They exclude all supported indices with another label because \(|t-j|\le L_m-1\) on \(D\). At the selected label they retain, respectively, exactly \(L_h-j\) and \(j+1\) indices. Choosing the smaller proves (3), with the closed-boundary counts as stated.

For even \(L=2v\), the sequence \(\min(j+1,L-j)\), \(0\le j<L\), is \(1,2,\ldots,v,v,\ldots,2,1\). Therefore

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\min(j+1,L-j)^2
=2\sum_{i=1}^v i^2
=\frac{L(L+1)(L+2)}{12}
=L^3\mu_2(L).
\tag{14}
\]

Substituting (3) into (2) gives \(M_2(p)=m^{-3}\sum_h\mu_2(B^h)\). Expanding \(\mu_2(L)=1/12+1/(4L)+1/(6L^2)\), and using \(4^n=4m^2\), gives (4). In particular every even endpoint case, including \(B=2\) and \(L=2\), is included in this computation.

For superlevels, \(w_1>\cdots>w_m>0\) gives the asserted interval decomposition for every \(s>0\), with \(A_s=\varnothing\) for \(s>w_1\). Completeness follows also from
\(A_s=Q_s\cap\mathbb Z^n\), where
\(Q_s=P_{k,B}\cap\{(\mathbf x,t):1+\sum_i2^{i-1}x_i\le-\log s/\log B\}\).
Because \(A_s\subset Q_s\) and \(Q_s\) is convex, this implies \(\operatorname{conv}(A_s)\cap\mathbb Z^n=A_s\). This assertion is used only for nonempty superlevels.

The first \(h\) labels represent the binary integers \(0,\ldots,h-1\), with the first coordinate being the least significant bit. If \(h=1\), the only label is \(\mathbf0\) and its affine rank is zero. If \(h\ge2\) and \(r=\lceil\log_2h\rceil\), then \(2^{r-1}<h\le2^r\). All these labels have zero coordinates above \(r\). They contain \(\mathbf0\) and all \(\mathbf e_i\) for \(1\le i\le r\), since \(2^{i-1}\le h-1\). Their affine rank is therefore exactly \(r\). The interval direction supplies one further independent vector, since every interval contains both integer positions zero and one. This proves \(r_h=1+\lceil\log_2 h\rceil\).

For a target belonging to \(A_s\), the same line restriction and the same two exposed-ray cuts used for (3) give (5). If its label is absent from \(A_s\), the halfspace \(a_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf x)\ge0\) contains the target and excludes \(A_s\), giving depth zero. This includes the empty superlevel and proves (5) at every target in \(D\).

Integrating the explicit depth formula, each label contributes exactly until its own weight threshold. There are finitely many labels and indices, so the integrations may be interchanged with their sums without a convergence issue. Equation (14) yields

\[
3\int_0^\infty s^2\sum_{\mathbf J\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf J)^2\,ds
=\sum_{h=1}^m L_h^3\mu_2(L_h)\left(3\int_0^{w_h}s^2\,ds\right)
=\sum_{h=1}^m\mu_2(L_h)
=F_2(w).
\tag{15}
\]

This proves the actual-depth equality by direct computation; no general layer-cake inequality is assumed or reproved. The interval formula \(3\int_{w_{h+1}}^{w_h}s^2\,ds=\Delta_h\) gives the exact expressions for \(\mathcal R\) and \(\mathcal S_n\) in (8). Expanding the square in (7) and using \(3\int_0^\infty s^2N(s)\,ds=\sum_{\mathbf J}w_{\mathbf J}^3\) proves its displayed diagonal expansion. The rank expression in (7) is the corresponding integral with the value \(r_h\) on each nonempty superlevel interval.

For completeness, the comparisons in (8) can also be checked just for this family, without using a general cardinality theorem. On a superlevel containing \(h\) intervals, let \(N=S_h\). The sum of counting depths on an even interval is \(L(L+2)/4\), as follows by summing the sequence used in (14). Thus

\[
\sum_{\mathbf J\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf J)
=\frac14\sum_{r=1}^h L_r^2+\frac N2
\ge\frac{N^2}{4h}+\frac N2
\ge\frac N2\left(\frac N{2^{r_h}}+1\right).
\tag{16}
\]

The first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz, and the second uses \(2^{r_h}\ge2h\). Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the depth values now gives
\(\sum_{\mathbf J\in A_s}d_s(\mathbf J)^2\ge N(2^{-r_h}N+1)^2/4\).
Integrating and using (15) proves \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}\le M_2(p)\). Since \(r_h\le n\), the remaining comparison follows termwise.

For the limits, fix \(k\) and hence all \(1\le h\le m\). Then
\(S_hB^{-h}=1+B^{-1}+\cdots+B^{1-h}\to1\).
For \(h<m\), \(\Delta_h=B^{-3h}(1-B^{-3})\), while \(\Delta_m=B^{-3m}\). Consequently
\(\Delta_hS_h^3\to1\), \(\Delta_hS_h^2\to0\), and \(\Delta_hS_h\to0\), separately for every \(h\). There are finitely many such terms. This proves \(\lim_B\mathcal R=m^{-2}\) and
\(\lim_B\mathcal S_n=4^{-n}/(4m^2)=16^{-n}\); the second-moment limit follows from (4). The diagonal term tends to zero explicitly because \(\sum_{\mathbf J}w_{\mathbf J}^3=\sum_{h=1}^mB^{-2h}\to0\).

The rank values equal one for \(h=1\), and equal \(r+1\) at the \(2^{r-1}\) indices \(2^{r-1}<h\le2^r\), for \(1\le r\le k\). Therefore

\[
\sum_{h=1}^m4^{-r_h}
=\frac14+\sum_{r=1}^k2^{r-1}4^{-(r+1)}
=\frac14+\frac18(1-2^{-k})
=\frac38-\frac1{8m}.
\tag{17}
\]

The finite expression in (7) then gives
\(\lim_B4^n\mathcal S_{\mathrm{rank}}=m^{-1}\sum_h4^{-r_h}=(3m-1)/(8m^2)\).
Dividing by the strictly positive limit of \(M_2(p)\) gives both ratio limits in (9). These formulas prove the iterated limits. To obtain the explicitly quantified version, choose \(k_0\) so the limits of each scaled comparison functional are below \(\varepsilon/2\) for every \(k\ge k_0\). For each such fixed \(k\), the three convergences supply a threshold \(B_0(k,\varepsilon)\) so that the comparison limits have error below \(\varepsilon/2\) and the scaled moment has error below \(\varepsilon\). Restricting to even integers above this threshold proves the assertion. In the inner-limit ratios the ambient loss has order \(4^{-n}\), and the intrinsic-rank loss has order \(2^{-n}\).

Under \(p\), each label has total probability \(1/m\). Therefore \(h(\boldsymbol\eta)\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and at every point of that label \(-\log p_{\mathbf J}=\log m+h\log B\). The uniform integer law has mean \((m+1)/2\) and variance \((m^2-1)/12\), obtained from \(\sum_{h=1}^m h=m(m+1)/2\) and \(\sum_{h=1}^m h^2=m(m+1)(2m+1)/6\). This proves the variance formula. The largest atom is \(1/(mB)\), and \(2^n=2m\), proving the first formula in (10).

Every nonempty proper face of a polytope is exposed by a nonconstant linear functional. For \(P_{k,B}\), write that functional as \(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf x+\beta t\). If \(\beta=0\), then \(\boldsymbol\alpha\ne\mathbf0\), and its maximizing Boolean labels are a proper face of the Boolean cube. Every coordinate where \(\alpha_i\ne0\) is fixed in these maximizers, so at most \(2^{k-1}=m/2\) labels are selected. Each has probability \(1/m\), proving the required bound. If \(\beta\ne0\), the maximizing set intersects each labelled interval in at most one endpoint. Its probability is consequently at most
\(m^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^mB^{-h}=(1-B^{-m})/[m(B-1)]\le1/2\), since \(m,B\ge2\). The empty face has probability zero. This proves the face assertion and the fixed-dimension consequences of (10).

It remains to verify the actual body and its original depth. Each set in (11) is compact and convex, and there are finitely many; their convex hull is compact and convex. Every one of its label blocks contains \([0,B-1]\times K_m\). Taking convex combinations at fixed points of that common set shows that \(C_{k,B,d}\) contains \([0,1]^k\times[0,B-1]\times K_m\), which has nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^{k+1+d}\). Thus this is a convex body for every \(d\ge1\).

The Boolean extreme-point argument from (1) applies unchanged: fixing an integer Boolean label permits convex combinations only inside its own block. That entire block is the product \([0,L_h-1]\times K_h\), which is already convex. The projection in \(t\) gives exactly the original integer indices of \(D\), and the fiber at each of them is precisely \(K_h\). Its volume is \((B^{-h/d})^d=B^{-h}=1/L_h\). This establishes the full actual fiber table and original total mixed volume \(W=m\), with no uncounted boundary or exterior fibers.

Fix a label, an integer \(j\) on it, and \(a=\min(j,L_h-1-j)\). The subcollection of \(S\) consisting of its \(2a+1\) columns at positions \(j-a,\ldots,j+a\) is centrally symmetric about \((\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf0)\). Its mixed volume is \((2a+1)/L_h\), and reflection about that center preserves its measure. For any halfspace containing the center, each point or its reflection belongs to the halfspace: writing the halfspace as \(\ell(\mathbf z)\ge c\), the inequality \(\ell(\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf0)\ge c\) prevents both reflected points from having \(\ell<c\). Integrating their two indicators gives mass at least half of the subcollection. Hence
\(h_S(\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf0)\ge(a+1/2)/L_h\).

We supply finite upper halfspaces at every continuous point, not only at the center. Fix \(\mathbf u\in K_h\). Let \(\mathbf b=\mathbf e_1\) if \(u_1\ge0\), and \(\mathbf b=-\mathbf e_1\) otherwise, using the first continuous coordinate, which exists because \(d\ge1\). Then \(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf u=|u_1|\ge0\), and the cut \(\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)\ge0\) captures at most half of the centered cube \(K_h\). At \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\) it captures exactly half; its continuous boundary has \(d\)-dimensional measure zero.

Choose \(\sigma=-1\) if \(j\le L_h-1-j\), and \(\sigma=1\) otherwise, so that \(\sigma(t-j)>0\) selects the shorter integer tail of exactly \(a\) columns. Consider the ambient closed halfspace

\[
(2L_m+2)a_{\boldsymbol\eta}(\mathbf x)
+2\sigma(t-j)+\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]

Its normal is nonzero because \(\mathbf b\ne\mathbf0\), and its boundary contains the target \((\boldsymbol\eta,j,\mathbf u)\). For every point in every supported fiber, \(\mathbf v\in K_r\subset K_1\) and \(\mathbf u\in K_h\subset K_1\). Therefore
\(|\mathbf b\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)|\le B^{-1/d}\le1\).
At the selected label, any nonzero integer displacement \(t-j\) has absolute value at least one, so the coefficient two makes (18) include every point of precisely the shorter tail and exclude every point of the opposite tail. At \(t=j\), it reduces exactly to the continuous cut just chosen. At any other Boolean label, \(a_{\boldsymbol\eta}\le-1\) and \(|t-j|\le L_m-1\), so the left side of (18) is at most
\(-(2L_m+2)+2(L_m-1)+1=-3<0\).
Thus no other label contributes. This halfspace has mixed volume at most \((a+1/2)/L_h\), with equality when \(\mathbf u=\mathbf0\). Combined with the preceding lower bound, this proves (12) for all \(d\ge1\), all supported indices, and all continuous points, including boundary points.

Since \(L_h\) is even, \(a\) is maximized at \(j=L_h/2-1\) and \(j=L_h/2\), where \(a=L_h/2-1\). Equation (12) shows that the maximum over this entire labelled block is \((L_h-1)/(2L_h)\), attained at either of these indices with continuous coordinate zero. This value increases with \(L_h\), so the largest block \(L_m=B^m\) attains the global maximum \((1-B^{-m})/2\). Dividing by the original \(W=m\) proves (13). In particular \(1-B^{-m}\ge3/4\) for all parameters here, and tends to one in the fixed-\(k\), even-\(B\) limit. Thus the family satisfies the desired depth scale while exhibiting all the comparison losses proved above.
