## Statement

Let $q(z)=Bz+c$ be affine and let finitely many separately movable cap occurrences have margins $u_i\cdot(x-q(z))$ with $0\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}\{u_i\}$ in $\mathbb R^d$. They cover all of $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d$, and they still do after independent sufficiently small changes of their continuous normals and any change to one common affine meeting section. Keeping only a common ambient point does not give this conclusion.

On finitely many actual positive compact convex fibers, suppose the cuts of one such cap are proper or strictly full/empty. Let $R_i=\sum_z\int(x-q(z))\,d\tau_{iz}$, using full proper-section coarea $d\tau_{iz}=d\mathcal H^{d-1}/\|u_i\|$ and counting measure when $d=1$. A normal change with velocity $v_i$ at fixed $q$ has derivative $v_i\cdot R_i$, and $u_i\cdot R_i=0$. If $R_i\ne0$, there is a two-sided admissible angular motion of that cap alone that strictly decreases its mass to first order. Stationarity of a positively weighted cap sum under these independent motions forces $R_i=0$ for every positive multiplier.

## Proof

The quantity $\delta=\min_{\|w\|=1}\max_i u_i\cdot w$ is positive by the interior hypothesis and compactness. Perturbing the normals by less than $\delta$ in maximum norm preserves positivity. Off the meeting section take $w=(x-q(z))/\|x-q(z)\|$ to find a cap with positive margin. On the section every margin vanishes, so the closed caps cover there as well. The argument applies with any common new section. It requires separate movable occurrences; an original cap shared across different group sections has extra identities.

In mixed coordinates $(z,x)$ the two caps $x\ge0$ and $-x\ge0$ cover the plane. Changing only the first to $x+\varepsilon z\ge0$ keeps their common point $(0,0)$ and the same continuous normals. For $\varepsilon>0$ both caps miss $(-1,\varepsilon/2)$. This example isolates the need to keep an affine section or otherwise control projected coverage.

For the fixed-section motion $u_i(t)=u_i+tv_i$, the ambient base coefficient velocity is $-B^{\mathsf T}v_i$ and the threshold velocity is $v_i\cdot c$. Apply 1269c554ca9a41b8 §2, since all nonproper cuts here are strict and contribute zero. The margin derivative on the original section is $v_i\cdot(x-q(z))$. Its integral gives the displayed derivative, and on each section $u_i\cdot(x-q(z))=0$. Take $v_i=-R_i$ for a derivative $-\|R_i\|^2$. The cover remains valid on both sides for small $t$ by the first part. As the velocity is perpendicular to $u_i$, positive normalization to its original norm realizes the same halfspace path as a genuine angular motion. When the cap sum is stationary, test each cap independently with this velocity and its reverse. The result is a condition on the summed force over all actual fibers.
