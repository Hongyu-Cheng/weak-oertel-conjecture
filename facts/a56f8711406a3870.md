## Statement

Let $d\ge1$, let $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a convex body, and let $y\in\operatorname{int}K$. Define $E_K(y)=\min_a\int_K[1+a\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-1}\,dx$ over $a$ for which the denominator is positive on $K$, and let $a_y$ be its unique minimizer. The maps $y\mapsto a_y$ and $y\mapsto E_K(y)$ are smooth on $\operatorname{int}K$ and
$$
\nabla_y E_K(y)=(d+1)E_K(y)a_y,\qquad
\nabla_y E_K(y)^{1/d}=\frac{d+1}{d}E_K(y)^{1/d}a_y.
$$
The optimal denominator $1+a_y\cdot(x-y)$ is at least $1/(d+1)$ on $K$ and strictly greater than $1/(d+1)$ on $\operatorname{int}K$.

## Proof

Write $V(a,y)=\int_K[1+a\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-1}\,dx$. Around any admissible pair $(a,y)$ the denominator has a uniform positive lower bound on $K$, so $V$ is smooth by differentiation under the integral. The positive definite parameter Hessian from the attainment and escort lemma lets the implicit function theorem solve $\nabla_a V=0$ smoothly for $a$ near each optimal pair. The continued parameter remains admissible and is the unique minimizer by strict convexity. This proves smoothness. In differentiating $E_K(y)=V(a_y,y)$ the parameter derivative vanishes by stationarity, so
$$
\nabla_y E_K(y)=(d+1)a_y\int_K [1+a_y\cdot(x-y)]^{-d-2}\,dx
=(d+1)a_y E_K(y)
$$
by the escort normalization. The derivative of the root follows by the chain rule.

The joint energy concavity theorem gives concavity of $E_K^{1/d}$ with $K$ fixed. For $x\in\operatorname{int}K$, positivity and the tangent inequality give
$$
0<E_K(x)^{1/d}\le E_K(y)^{1/d}\left[1+\frac{d+1}{d}a_y\cdot(x-y)\right].
$$
The bracket is positive, so $1+a_y\cdot(x-y)>1/(d+1)$. Continuity of this affine denominator and density of $\operatorname{int}K$ in $K$ give the weak bound on all of $K$.
