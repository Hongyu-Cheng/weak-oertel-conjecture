## Statement

For each integer $d\ge1$ there is a family of convex bodies with exactly four positive integer fibers in one integer coordinate and with a unique volume maximizer in each parity. Put $T=\sum_z w_z$, $R=T-\sum_c m_c$ for the residual after removal of the two class maxima, $f_z=w_z^{1/d}$, and $F(z)=(z+v_{[z]})/2$. Every forest edge has zero root slack $2f_{F(z)}-f_z-f_{v_{[z]}}$ and exact midpoint fiber inclusion. Yet along the family $R/T\to1/2$ and $\sum_{z:F(z)\ne z}(w_{F(z)}-w_z)/R\to0$. Therefore neither total root slack nor total absolute edge growth has a positive dimension-only lower bound proportional to this residual, even with unique maxima.

## Proof

Fix a unit-volume $d$-body $K$. For $0<\epsilon<1/3$ use the real fibers $(1-\epsilon z)K$ on $[0,3]$. The family is convex because Minkowski combinations of positive homothets of one convex set have exactly the corresponding averaged scale and this scale is affine in $z$. The family is compact with positive-dimensional base and full continuous interiors, so is a full-dimensional convex body. Its four integer fiber roots and volumes are $f_i=1-i\epsilon$ and $w_i=f_i^d$. They strictly decrease. Thus the even root is $0$, the odd root is $1$, and the only nonfixed edges are $2\to1$ and $3\to2$. The affine scale gives equality in every midpoint fiber inclusion and makes every root slack zero. Meanwhile $R=w_2+w_3$, with $R/T\to1/2$, and the total absolute growth telescopes to $w_1-w_3\to0$. For the rational sequence $\epsilon=1/q$ with $q\ge4$ integral, the growth-to-residual ratio is exactly
$$
\frac{(q-1)^d-(q-3)^d}{(q-2)^d+(q-3)^d}\longrightarrow0.
$$
