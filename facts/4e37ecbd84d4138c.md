## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and write
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\}.
\]
For a bounded measurable \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(s>0\), define
\[
\mathsf M_s(K):=
\left\{
 \frac1s\int_{\mathbb R^d}x f(x)\,dx:
 0\leq f\leq\mathbf1_K\ \text{a.e.},\quad
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}f=s
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) be finite and nonempty, and suppose that
\[
b_z>0,
\qquad q_z\in\mathsf M_{b_z}(C_z)
\qquad(z\in I).
\tag{2}
\]
Extend \(b_z\) by zero away from \(I\). For each parity class
\(r\in(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n\), put
\[
I_r:=\{z\in I:z\equiv r\pmod2\},
\qquad W_r:=\sum_{z\in I_r}b_z.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(W_r>0\), define, for \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\),
\[
S_{r,m}:=\sum_{z\in I_r}b_zb_{2m-z},
\qquad b_{r,m}:=\frac{S_{r,m}}{W_r},
\tag{4}
\]
and, when \(S_{r,m}>0\),
\[
q_{r,m}:=
\frac1{S_{r,m}}
\sum_{z\in I_r}b_zb_{2m-z}
\frac{q_z+q_{2m-z}}2.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(W_r=0\), set \(b_{r,m}:=0\) for every \(m\), and never define or use
\(q_{r,m}\). Likewise, if \(S_{r,m}=0\), then \(b_{r,m}=0\) and no
\(q_{r,m}\) is assigned. Accepted fact fc828b039898bc41 gives, whenever
\(b_{r,m}>0\),
\[
q_{r,m}\in\mathsf M_{b_{r,m}}(C_m),
\tag{6}
\]
as well as, for every \(r\),
\[
\sum_m b_{r,m}=W_r,
\qquad
\sum_m b_{r,m}m=\sum_{z\in I_r}b_zz,
\qquad
\sum_{m:b_{r,m}>0}b_{r,m}q_{r,m}
=\sum_{z\in I_r}b_zq_z,
\tag{7}
\]
where all three identities are \(0=0\) for an empty color.

Choose arbitrary coefficients
\[
\alpha_r\geq0,
\qquad \sum_r\alpha_r\leq1,
\tag{8}
\]
and define
\[
t_m:=\sum_r\alpha_rb_{r,m}.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(t_m>0\), define
\[
q_m:=\frac1{t_m}
\sum_{r:\alpha_rb_{r,m}>0}
\alpha_rb_{r,m}q_{r,m}.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
q_m\in\mathsf M_{t_m}(C_m)
\qquad(t_m>0),
\tag{11}
\]
and in particular \(0<t_m\leq |C_m|_d\). The pooled profile has the exact
mass and full first moment
\[
\sum_m t_m=\sum_r\alpha_rW_r,
\tag{12}
\]
\[
\sum_{m:t_m>0}t_m(m,q_m)
=\sum_r\alpha_r\sum_{z\in I_r}b_z(z,q_z).
\tag{13}
\]
With
\[
\beta_{\alpha}:=
\sum_{m:t_m>0}t_m\,\delta_{(m,q_m)},
\qquad
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,
\tag{14}
\]
every closed ambient halfspace \(H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\) satisfies
\[
\mu_C(H):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}|C_z\cap H_z|_d
\geq\gamma_d\,\beta_{\alpha}(H),
\qquad
H_z:=\{x:(z,x)\in H\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The same assertion holds for the whole space if it is admitted as an
improper halfspace; an admitted empty halfspace has both relevant sides zero.

There is a canonical specialization with an exact covariance contraction.
Let
\[
a_z:=|C_z|_d,
\qquad E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
\qquad W:=\sum_z a_z>0,
\qquad
c_z:=\frac1{a_z}\int_{C_z}x\,dx.
\tag{16}
\]
Apply (3)--(10) to \(I=E\), \(b_z=a_z\), \(q_z=c_z\), and
\(\alpha_r=2^{-n}\) for every one of the \(2^n\) parity classes. Denote the
resulting graph measure by
\[
\mathsf B_C:=\sum_{m:t_m>0}t_m\delta_{(m,q_m)}.
\tag{17}
\]
Then
\[
|\mathsf B_C|=2^{-n}W,
\qquad
\frac1{|\mathsf B_C|}\int p\,d\mathsf B_C(p)
=\frac1W\sum_z a_z(z,c_z),
\tag{18}
\]
and (15) holds with \(\beta_{\alpha}=\mathsf B_C\).

Let \(P=(Z,c_Z)\) have law
\[
\mathbb P(Z=z)=\frac{a_z}{W},
\qquad R:=Z\bmod2.
\tag{19}
\]
Conditionally on \(R\), let \(P_1=(Z_1,c_{Z_1})\) and
\(P_2=(Z_2,c_{Z_2})\) be independent copies of \(P\mid R\), and put
\[
Y:=\frac{P_1+P_2}{2},
\qquad M:=\frac{Z_1+Z_2}{2}\in\mathbb Z^n,
\qquad Q:=\mathbb E[Y\mid M].
\tag{20}
\]
Then \(Q=(M,q_M)\), its law is the probability measure
\(\mathsf B_C/(2^{-n}W)\), and the following exact matrix identity holds:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(P)-\operatorname{Cov}(Q)
=\frac12\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R)]
+\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(Y\mid M)].
}
\tag{21}
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Q)
\preceq
\operatorname{Cov}(P)-\frac12\,
\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R)]
\preceq\operatorname{Cov}(P).
\tag{22}
\]
If
\[
\kappa:=
\sum_{r:W_r>0}\frac{W_r}{W}
\sum_{z\in E\cap r}
\left(\frac{a_z}{W_r}\right)^2,
\qquad
E\cap r:=\{z\in E:z\equiv r\pmod2\},
\tag{23}
\]
where here \(W_r=\sum_{z\in E\cap r}a_z\), then
\[
\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(P)
-\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(Q)
\geq1-\kappa.
\tag{24}
\]

Finally, the condition in (8) cannot in general be deleted and replaced by
unscaled addition of all colliding color outputs. For
\[
C=[0,2]\times[0,1/2]\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R,
\quad I=\{0,1,2\},
\quad (b_0,b_1,b_2)=(1/4,1/2,1/4),
\quad q_0=q_1=q_2=1/4,
\tag{25}
\]
the even-class repacking places mass \(1/4\) at target \(1\), while the
odd-class repacking places mass \(1/2\) there. Their unscaled merge
\(\alpha_{\rm even}=\alpha_{\rm odd}=1\) would require a point of
\(\mathsf M_{3/4}(C_1)\), but \(C_1=[0,1/2]\) has volume \(1/2\), so
\[
\mathsf M_{3/4}(C_1)=\varnothing.
\tag{26}
\]
This is the color-collision calibration already contained in accepted fact
fc828b039898bc41; it does not contradict the scaled pooling theorem because
the two coefficients in this failed merge sum to \(2\).

## Proof

Fix a parity class \(r\) with \(W_r>0\). Its support \(I_r\) is finite,
nonempty, and contained in one parity class. Applying accepted fact
fc828b039898bc41 to the data in (2) gives (6) and (7). If \(W_r=0\), all
quantities in (7) were defined as zero, so no assertion involving an undefined
metronoid point occurs.

For every pair \((r,m)\) with \(b_{r,m}>0\), choose a density
\[
0\leq f_{r,m}\leq\mathbf1_{C_m},
\qquad
\int f_{r,m}=b_{r,m},
\qquad
\int x f_{r,m}(x)\,dx=b_{r,m}q_{r,m}
\tag{27}
\]
representing (6). Put \(f_{r,m}=0\) whenever \(b_{r,m}=0\), including all
outputs of an empty color, and define
\[
f_m:=\sum_r\alpha_rf_{r,m}.
\tag{28}
\]
There are only \(2^n\) summands, and (8) gives pointwise almost everywhere
\[
0\leq f_m
\leq\left(\sum_r\alpha_r\right)\mathbf1_{C_m}
\leq\mathbf1_{C_m}.
\tag{29}
\]
Equations (9), (10), and (27) give, whenever \(t_m>0\),
\[
\int f_m=t_m,
\qquad
\int x f_m(x)\,dx=t_mq_m.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(f_m\) represents \(q_m\in\mathsf M_{t_m}(C_m)\), proving (11).
It also gives \(t_m\leq|C_m|_d\). When \(t_m=0\), nonnegativity in (9)
implies \(\alpha_rb_{r,m}=0\) for every \(r\); no \(q_m\), density of positive
mass, or graph atom is asserted. This proves the pooled capacity claim with
all zero-output cases included.

Summing (9) and using the first identity in (7) proves (12). For (13), first
use (9) for its base component and (10) for its continuous component, then
interchange finite sums and invoke the last two identities in (7):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m:t_m>0}t_m(m,q_m)
&=\sum_r\alpha_r
  \sum_{m:b_{r,m}>0}b_{r,m}(m,q_{r,m})\\
&=\sum_r\alpha_r\sum_{z\in I_r}b_z(z,q_z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]

We next prove (15). Fix an atom \((m,q_m)\in H\). By (11) and accepted
fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d, if the fiber section \(H_m\) is a proper closed
halfspace of \(\mathbb R^d\), then
\[
|C_m\cap H_m|_d\geq\gamma_dt_m.
\tag{32}
\]
If \(H\) is proper but vertical in the continuous variables, then \(H_m\) is
all of \(\mathbb R^d\) for every graph atom lying in \(H\), so instead
\[
|C_m\cap H_m|_d=|C_m|_d\geq t_m\geq\gamma_dt_m.
\tag{33}
\]
The same estimate applies to the whole space. Summing (32) or (33) over all
distinct graph atoms in \(H\), and retaining only those nonnegative terms from
the definition of \(\mu_C(H)\), gives (15). An empty halfspace contains no
atom and has \(\beta_{\alpha}(H)=0\).

For the canonical specialization, every \(c_z\) is the full-mass metronoid
point represented by \(\mathbf1_{C_z}\). Thus the general theorem applies.
Equations (12) and (13), with every \(\alpha_r=2^{-n}\), give
\[
\sum_m t_m=2^{-n}\sum_rW_r=2^{-n}W
\tag{34}
\]
and
\[
\sum_m t_m(m,q_m)=2^{-n}\sum_z a_z(z,c_z),
\tag{35}
\]
which prove (18).

We identify the canonical probability law. Conditional on \(R=r\), an
ordered pair \((Z_1,Z_2)=(z,w)\) has probability
\[
\frac{a_z}{W_r}\frac{a_w}{W_r},
\tag{36}
\]
whereas \(\mathbb P(R=r)=W_r/W\). Hence, for every color with \(W_r>0\),
\[
\mathbb P(R=r,M=m)=\frac{S_{r,m}}{WW_r}=\frac{b_{r,m}}W.
\tag{37}
\]
Summing over \(r\) gives
\[
\mathbb P(M=m)=\frac1W\sum_r b_{r,m}
=\frac{t_m}{2^{-n}W}.
\tag{38}
\]
Moreover, (5) and the same conditional-pair calculation give
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{c_{Z_1}+c_{Z_2}}2\,\middle|\,M=m\right]
=\frac{\sum_r b_{r,m}q_{r,m}}{\sum_r b_{r,m}}
=q_m
\tag{39}
\]
whenever \(t_m>0\). The base coordinate of \(Y\) is already \(M\), so
\(Q=\mathbb E[Y\mid M]=(M,q_M)\). Equations (38)--(39) prove that its law is
\(\mathsf B_C/(2^{-n}W)\).

All random variables have finite support. Conditional independence gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Y\mid R)
=\frac12\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R),
\qquad
\mathbb E[Y\mid R]=\mathbb E[P\mid R].
\tag{40}
\]
Twice applying the law of total covariance yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(Y)
&=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[P\mid R])
  +\frac12\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R)]\\
&=\operatorname{Cov}(P)
  -\frac12\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R)]
\tag{41}
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Y)
=\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[Y\mid M])
 +\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(Y\mid M)]
=\operatorname{Cov}(Q)
 +\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(Y\mid M)].
\tag{42}
\]
Subtracting (42) from (41) proves the exact identity (21); both terms on its
right are positive semidefinite, which proves (22).

For (24), the number \(\kappa\) in (23) is exactly
\(\mathbb P(Z_1=Z_2)\) under the conditionally independent construction.
Distinct lattice points of one parity class differ by a nonzero vector in
\((2\mathbb Z)^n\), and therefore have squared Euclidean distance at least
\(4\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(Z\mid R)]
&=\mathbb E\|Z_1-Z_2\|_2^2\\
&\geq4\,\mathbb P(Z_1\neq Z_2)=4(1-\kappa).
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
Since the base covariance is a principal block of the full graph covariance,
\[
\mathbb E[\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(P\mid R)]
\geq2(1-\kappa).
\tag{44}
\]
Taking traces in (21) and discarding the nonnegative last term proves (24).

It remains only to verify the stated boundary of the pooling rule. In (25),
the input metronoid conditions hold: use the constant densities \(1/2,1,1/2\)
on the three copies of \([0,1/2]\), respectively. The even input class has
total mass \(1/2\), and its two ordered cross-pairs contribute
\(S_{{\rm even},1}=2(1/4)^2=1/8\), hence
\(b_{{\rm even},1}=1/4\). The odd class is the singleton of mass \(1/2\), so
\(S_{{\rm odd},1}=1/4\) and \(b_{{\rm odd},1}=1/2\). All corresponding
continuous points equal \(1/4\). Adding both outputs with coefficient one
would require mass \(3/4\) in \(C_1\), while every admissible density on that
interval has integral at most \(1/2\). This proves (26) and precisely
distinguishes the failed unscaled collision from (8)--(11).
