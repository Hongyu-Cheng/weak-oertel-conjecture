## Statement

Let $n,L\ge1$ be integers and set $P=\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{n-1}\times\lbrace1,\ldots,2L\rbrace$. Give each $(v,j)\in P$ weight $w_j>0$, where $w_j=w_{2L+1-j}$. For the resulting probability measure on $P$, the maximum over sites in $P$ of the minimum mass of a containing closed halfspace in $\mathbb R^n$ is exactly $2^{-n}$.

For $d\ge1$, suppose the weights are proportional to $r(j)^d$ for a positive concave profile $r$ on $[1,2L]$ symmetric about $L+1/2$. They are proportional to the $d$-volumes of the actual integer fibers of the compact convex body with base $[0,1]^{n-1}\times[1,2L]$ and continuous fibers $\tilde r(s)K$, where $\tilde r$ is the piecewise linear interpolant of the integer samples and $K\subset\mathbb R^d$ is a convex body containing zero. With equal weights one may use the product with $K$ itself. In that case there are $2^nL$ positive fibers of normalized mass $1/(2^nL)$ each, and the last base coordinate has span $2L-1$.

## Proof

Let $W=\sum_{j=1}^{2L}w_j$. Each closed tail of the $v$-line that contains $L$ or $L+1$ contains at least $W/2$ of line weight: the two disjoint halves $\lbrace1,\ldots,L\rbrace$ and $\lbrace L+1,\ldots,2L\rbrace$ each weigh $W/2$. The trace of any ambient containing halfspace on that line is a containing closed tail or the full line. Thus at either middle site the mass is at least $(W/2)/(2^{n-1}W)$.

For any $j$, the lower tail ending at $j$ when $j\le L$, or the upper tail starting at $j$ otherwise, weighs at most $W/2$. Write its inequality as $as\ge b$ with $a\in\lbrace-1,1\rbrace$. For $n>1$ let $\ell_v(z)=\sum_i(2v_i-1)(z_i-v_i)$ on the first $n-1$ coordinates. This is zero at $v$ and at most $-1$ at the other binary vertices. Then $as+A\ell_v(z)\ge b$ has precisely the chosen tail on the $v$-line and misses every other line at the sampled sites for sufficiently large finite $A$. For $n=1$ use the tail directly. These upper bounds agree with the lower bound.

The integer samples have nonincreasing successive differences by concavity. Their piecewise linear interpolant $\tilde r$ is positive, continuous, concave and symmetric. At heights $s,s'$, convex combinations of points in the two dilates belong to the dilate with radius $(1-\theta)\tilde r(s)+\theta\tilde r(s')$, by convexity of $K$. Since $0\in K$ the dilates are nested, and concavity places that dilate in the fiber at $(1-\theta)s+\theta s'$. Thus the body is convex, and continuity and positivity on the closed interval give compactness and full dimension. Its sampled volumes are $r(j)^d|K|$. Constant $r$ gives the product, the stated counts and the coordinate span.
