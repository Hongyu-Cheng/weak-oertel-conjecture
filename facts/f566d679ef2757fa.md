## Statement

Let \(A,h,\Delta\) be integers with \(A\ge2\), \(h\ge2\), \(1\le\Delta<A\), \(\gcd(A,\Delta)=1\), and \(1/3\le\Delta/A\le2/3\). Put \(B=Ah-\Delta\), assume \(B-h>0\), and define
\[
D_{A,B}=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(A,0),(0,B)\}\cap\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad \mathbf j=(1,B-h).
\]
Let \(\nu_{\mathbf j}\) be the maximum-entropy probability law on this original support with full vector mean \(\mathbf j\), and let \((X,Y)\) have this law. It exists uniquely and is positive on the entire support. Define the original affine coordinate \(T=B-hX-Y\); thus \(\mathbb EX=1\) and \(\mathbb ET=0\).

There is an absolute finite \(K\ge3\) such that every such row and every real \(a,b\) satisfy
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}[a(X-1)+bT]^4
\le K\left(\mathbb E_{\nu_{\mathbf j}}[a(X-1)+bT]^2\right)^2.
\]
Consequently, for every \(k\ge1\), every independently chosen collection of these parameter triples, the original product \(Q=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k\nu_{\mathbf j_i}\), and every choice of real block normals \((u_i,v_i)\),
\[
Q\left\{\sum_{i=1}^k
 [u_i(X_i-1)+v_i(Y_i-(B_i-h_i))]\ge0\right\}
\ge\delta,\qquad \delta:=\frac1{48K}>0.
\]
The all-zero direction is included. Every proper closed halfspace containing \(J=(\mathbf j_1,\ldots,\mathbf j_k)\) therefore has original product mass at least \(\delta\). In the vector consumer of accepted fact ee1f822184669d64, the aliases are \(D_i=D_{A_i,B_i}\), \(m_i=2\), \(\nu_i=\nu_{\mathbf j_i}\), and the same \(Q,J\); the scalar representation supplied here has \(K_W=K\), \(K_N=3\), and \(N_i=0\).

## Proof

Write \(c=\Delta/A\) and, for \(0\le x\le A\), put \(k_x=\lfloor cx\rfloor\), \(M_x=B-hx\), and \(n_x=M_x+k_x\). These are exact integer column quantities. Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\lfloor B\left(1-\frac xA\right)\right\rfloor
 &=B-hx+\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta x}{A}\right\rfloor=n_x,\\
D_{A,B}
 &=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:0\le x\le A,\ 0\le y\le n_x\},\\
\operatorname{supp}(X,T)
 &=\{(x,r)\in\mathbb Z^2:0\le x\le A,\ -k_x\le r\le M_x\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
At the apex, \(k_A=\Delta\), \(M_A=-\Delta\), and \(n_A=0\), so (1) includes precisely its original value \(T=-\Delta\). Since \(k_1=0\), the root is in (1). Its two coordinates are positive, and its strict facet gap is \(AB-B-A(B-h)=\Delta>0\). Thus \(\mathbf j\) is interior to the convex hull of the full support.

For completeness, this verifies the entropy-row identification for the present family. The function \(F(\boldsymbol\theta)=\log\sum_{\mathbf x\in D_{A,B}}e^{\boldsymbol\theta\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf j)}\) is coercive because that convex hull contains a ball about \(\mathbf j\): \(F(\boldsymbol\theta)\ge\varepsilon\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert\) for some \(\varepsilon>0\). Its Hessian is the covariance of its positive exponential law and is positive definite, since the support contains the three noncollinear vertices. Its unique minimizer therefore gives the unique positive exponential law with full mean \(\mathbf j\). For any other law \(\eta\) with this mean, direct substitution gives \(D(\eta\Vert\nu_{\mathbf j})=H(\nu_{\mathbf j})-H(\eta)\ge0\), with equality only for \(\eta=\nu_{\mathbf j}\). Nonnegativity and this equality condition follow by summing \(-\log z\ge1-z\) over the positive masses of \(\eta\). Hence this is exactly the unique maximum-entropy row.

Write its exponential parameter as \((\lambda_x,\beta_{\mathrm v})\), and set \(q=e^{-\beta_{\mathrm v}}>0\), \(t=e^{\lambda_x-h\beta_{\mathrm v}}>0\). Define \(P_n(q)=\sum_{r=0}^nq^r\), \(w_x=t^xq^{-k_x}P_{n_x}(q)\), and \(Z=\sum_{x=0}^Aw_x\). The exact normalization is \(e^{\lambda_xx+\beta_{\mathrm v}y}=q^{-B}t^xq^{B-hx-y}\), so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_{\mathbf j}(x,y)&=\frac{t^xq^{B-hx-y}}Z,\\
\sum_{x=0}^A(x-1)w_x&=0,\\
\sum_{x=0}^At^x\sum_{r=-k_x}^{M_x}r q^r&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, the horizontal balance in (2) is \(w_0=\sum_{x=2}^A(x-1)w_x\). No column law or full mean has been replaced.

We prove absolute bounds on \(q,t\). Let \(V=T+k_X\). Conditional on \(X=x\), this nonnegative integer variable has probabilities proportional to \(q^r\) on \(\{0,\ldots,n_x\}\). Let \(m_n(q)\) denote the mean of this geometric law. Its mean is increasing in \(q\), since differentiation with respect to \(\log q\) gives its variance. Also, (2) gives \(\mathbb EV=\mathbb Ek_X\le c\mathbb EX=c\le2/3\).

If \(q\ge1\), then \(m_n(q)\ge n/2\). Since \(n_X=B-hX+k_X\), this would imply
\[
\mathbb Ek_X=\mathbb EV\ge\frac12\mathbb En_X
 =\frac12(B-h+\mathbb Ek_X),
\qquad \mathbb Ek_X\ge B-h\ge1,
\]
contradicting \(\mathbb Ek_X\le2/3\). Therefore \(q<1\).

In fact \(q<9/10\). Exact evaluation gives \(m_1(9/10)=9/19>4/9\), \(m_2(9/10)=252/271>8/9\), and \(m_3(9/10)=4707/3439>4/3\). Increasing the upper endpoint cannot decrease the mean, so \(m_n(9/10)\ge(4/9)\min(n,3)\) for every integer \(n\ge0\). Moreover \(B/A=h-c>1\), so \(n_x\ge A-x\). If \(A\ge3\), then \(\min(n_x,3)\ge3(A-x)/A\). Thus \(q\ge9/10\) would give \(\mathbb EV\ge(4/9)3(A-1)/A\ge8/9>2/3\), a contradiction.

For \(A=2\) necessarily \(\Delta=1\), \(c=1/2\). Put \(p_x=\Pr(X=x)\). The horizontal mean gives \(p_0=p_2\le1/2\). If \(h\ge3\), then \(n_0\ge5\), \(n_1\ge2\), and \(n_2=0\). Hence \(\mathbb E\min(n_X,3)\ge3p_0+2p_1=2-p_0\ge3/2\), and \(q\ge9/10\) would give \(\mathbb EV\ge2/3>c\). If \(h=2\), then \(n_0=3\), \(n_1=1\). The equality \(w_0=w_2\) gives \(t^2=qP_3(q)\), whence \(p_1/p_0=\sqrt q P_1(q)/\sqrt{P_3(q)}\le2\) and \(p_0\ge1/4\). For \(q\ge9/10\), the same exact mean estimates give \(\mathbb EV\ge(4/3)p_0+(4/9)p_1=4/9+(4/9)p_0\ge5/9>c\). This finishes the upper bound in all cases.

Consequently \(1\le P_{n_x}(q)<10\), and \(w_0<10\). Retaining the \(x=2\) term of the horizontal balance gives \(t^2\le10q^{k_2}\), so \(t<4\). If \(A\ge3\), then \(k_3\ge1\), and retaining \(x=3\) gives \(t^3\le5q^{k_3}\le5q\). If \(A=2\), then \(k_2=1\) gives \(t\le\sqrt{10q}\). Both cases yield \(t\le4q^{1/3}\).

The truncated geometric law of \(V\) is the infinite geometric law conditioned to be at most \(n_x\); this conditioning cannot increase its mean. Thus \(\mathbb Ek_X=\mathbb EV\le q/(1-q)\). For every supported integer \(x\),
\[
x\le\mathbf1_{\{x=1\}}
 +2\mathbf1_{\{x=2,\ k_2=0\}}+5k_x.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(x\ge3\), this follows from \(k_x\ge\lfloor x/3\rfloor\) and \(x\le5\lfloor x/3\rfloor\); the remaining values are explicit. Since \(Z\ge1\), \(\Pr(X=1)\le10t\), and, when \(k_2=0\), \(\Pr(X=2)\le10t^2\). Taking expectations in (3) gives \(1\le10t+20t^2+5q/(1-q)\). If \(q\le10^{-6}\), the bound on \(t\) makes its right side at most \(54/125+5/999999<1\), a contradiction. Put \(q_*=10^{-6}\); we have proved \(q_*<q<9/10\).

Set \(s=tq^{-c}\) and \(f_x=cx-k_x\in[0,1)\). Then \(w_x=s^xq^{f_x}P_{n_x}(q)\), so
\[
q_*s^x\le w_x\le10s^x.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular \(s\le10000t<40000\). If \(t\le q_*\), then \(s\le1/100\), and (4) would give \(\mathbb EX\le10\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}xs^x\le1000/9801<1\). Hence \(t>q_*\).

Let \(M_*=10000\) and \(\rho_*=1-1/(2M_*)=19999/20000\). If \(A\ge M_*\) and \(s\ge\rho_*\), the horizontal balance and (4), together with Bernoulli's inequality, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
10>w_0
&\ge q_*\sum_{x=2}^{M_*}(x-1)\rho_*^x\\
&\ge q_*\rho_*^{M_*}\frac{M_*(M_*-1)}2
\ge\frac{9999}{400}>10,
\end{aligned}
\]
a contradiction. Thus \(s<\rho_*\) whenever \(A\ge M_*\). In this case \(Z\le10/(1-\rho_*)=200000\). If \(A<M_*\), then (4) and \(s<40000\) give \(Z\le10\sum_{x=0}^{M_*-1}40000^x<40000^{M_*}\). Consequently, with \(Z_*=40000^{M_*}\), we have the uniform bounds
\[
q_*<q<\frac9{10},\qquad q_*<t<4,\qquad Z\le Z_*.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the finite absolute constants
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_*&=M_*^4+10\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}x^4\rho_*^x,\\
D_*&=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}r^4(9/10)^r,\\
K&=\max\left\{3,\frac{64(L_*+D_*+1)Z_*^4}{q_*^2}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Both series converge by the ratio test. When \(A<M_*\), \(X<M_*\); otherwise \(\Pr(X=x)=w_x/Z\le10\rho_*^x\). Thus \(\mathbb EX^4\le L_*\). Conditional on every column, direct summation using \(P_{n_x}(q)\ge1\) gives \(\mathbb E[V^4\mid X=x]\le D_*\). Since \(0\le k_X\le X\) and \(T=V-k_X\), we obtain \(\mathbb ET^4\le8(D_*+L_*)\), and \(\mathbb E(X-1)^4\le8(L_*+1)\). Therefore, for \(S_{a,b}=a(X-1)+bT\),
\[
\mathbb ES_{a,b}^4
\le64(L_*+D_*+1)(a^4+b^4).
\tag{7}
\]

The original support contains the three atoms \((X,T)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)\). Their original masses are respectively \(1/Z,t/Z,q/Z\). For any scalar function on a finite probability space, its variance equals the sum, over unordered pairs of atoms, of the product of their masses times their squared difference. Applying this identity to the two pairs joining \((0,0)\) to the other displayed atoms, and using (5), gives
\[
\mathbb ES_{a,b}^2=\operatorname{Var}S_{a,b}
\ge\frac{t}{Z^2}a^2+\frac q{Z^2}b^2
\ge\frac{q_*}{Z_*^2}(a^2+b^2).
\tag{8}
\]
The first equality uses the two full mean equations in (2). Since \(a^4+b^4\le(a^2+b^2)^2\), (6)--(8) prove the asserted fourth-moment inequality. When \(a=b=0\), both sides are zero, so its zero-variance case is also checked.

Finally, fix the independently chosen rows and the original normals \((u_i,v_i)\). The exact coefficient map is \(a_i=u_i-h_iv_i\), \(b_i=-v_i\), because \(Y_i-(B_i-h_i)=-h_i(X_i-1)-T_i\). Let \(W_i=a_i(X_i-1)+b_iT_i\), \(N_i=0\), and \(\mathcal G_i=\sigma(W_i)\), on independent original block experiments. These jointly finite scalar representations have \(\mathbb EW_i=0\), regularity constant \(K_W=K\), and conditional noise constant \(K_N=3\). Their sum is exactly the original full-vector product projection, atom by atom. Applying accepted fact ee1f822184669d64 with these aliases gives the cap \(1/(16K\cdot3)=\delta\), independently of \(k\) and the normals. Its zero-variance provisions include the all-zero direction. Every containing halfspace contains its parallel translate with boundary through \(J\), so the same bound holds for every proper closed halfspace containing \(J\).
