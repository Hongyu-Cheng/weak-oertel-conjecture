## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), and let \(C\subset\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a compact full-dimensional convex body. Retain from accepted fact 88b755e99b7eeb7c the canonical notation
\[
C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},\qquad
w_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)\quad(z\in\mathbb R^n),
\]
where empty and lower-dimensional fibers have volume zero, and
\[
S:=C\cap(\mathbb Z^n\times\mathbb R^d),\qquad
W:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}w_z>0,
\]
\[
h_S(p):=\inf\{\mathcal H_d(S\cap H):
H\subseteq\mathbb R^{n+d}\text{ is a closed halfspace and }p\in H\}.
\]
For \(q\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_q>0\), define
\[
M(q):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\min\{w_z,w_{2q-z}\}.
\]
Under the exact alias \(j=q\), \(v=z-q\), this \(M(q)\) is the scalar \(S_j\) of accepted fact 88b755e99b7eeb7c; it is not the mixed set \(S\).
Then there is \(x_q\in C_q\) such that
\[
h_S((q,x_q))\ge \frac{M(q)}{80n}.
\]
The point \(x_q\) is chosen before the arbitrary containing closed halfspace; the conclusion includes vertical halfspaces and halfspaces whose boundaries carry mixed-integer mass.

Consequently, if some \(q\in\mathbb Z^n\) satisfies
\[
w_{q+z}=w_{q-z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb Z^n),
\]
then \(w_q>0\), \(M(q)=W\), and
\[
h_{\max}(C):=\max_{p\in S}h_S(p)
\ge \frac{W}{80n}
\ge \frac{W}{40\,2^n}.
\]
Only the lattice fiber volumes are assumed symmetric; the sets \(C_z\) need not be symmetric or mutually homothetic.

## Proof

Fix \(q\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_q>0\), and abbreviate \(M=M(q)\). Compactness of the base projection of \(C\) makes every lattice sum below finite. In particular,
\[
M\ge \min\{w_q,w_q\}=w_q>0.
\tag{1}
\]

We first extract a large, symmetric, bounded-ratio part of the reflected profile. Accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2 proves that the actual real-base volume profile
\[
u\longmapsto w(u):=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_u)
\]
is globally log-concave, with zero allowed outside its positive domain, and is upper semicontinuous. Reflection about \(q\) preserves these properties. Hence
\[
g(u):=\min\{w(u),w(2q-u)\}
\tag{2}
\]
is log-concave: with \(\log 0=-\infty\), its logarithm is the minimum of the two concave functions \(\log w(u)\) and \(\log w(2q-u)\), whose hypographs have convex intersection. The function \(g\) is symmetric about \(q\). Log-concavity at the midpoint of \(u\) and \(2q-u\) gives
\[
g(q)\ge \sqrt{g(u)g(2q-u)}=g(u),
\]
so \(g(q)=w_q\) is its maximum.

Translate the base by the integer vector \(q\), solely for the next counting argument, and set
\[
\phi(v):=
\begin{cases}
\log\!\bigl(w_q/g(q+v)\bigr),&g(q+v)>0,\\
+\infty,&g(q+v)=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
K_a:=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:\phi(v)\le a\},
\qquad
N(a):=\#(K_a\cap\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(\phi\) is even, convex, and nonnegative. Each \(K_a\) is a centrally symmetric convex set containing \(0\); it is compact because it is a positive superlevel set of the upper-semicontinuous \(g\) inside the compact base projection of \(C\) and its reflection.

For every centrally symmetric convex \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) containing \(0\),
\[
\#(2K\cap\mathbb Z^n)\le 5^n\#(K\cap\mathbb Z^n).
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, partition \(2K\cap\mathbb Z^n\) into residue classes modulo \(5\). Choose one \(u\) in each nonempty class. Within that class the map \(z\mapsto(z-u)/5\) is injective and takes values in
\[
\mathbb Z^n\cap\frac{2K-2K}{5}
=\mathbb Z^n\cap\frac45K
\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\cap K.
\]
There are \(5^n\) residue classes, proving (4), including lower-dimensional \(K\) and lattice points on its boundary.

Convexity of \(\phi\), together with \(\phi(0)=0\), gives \(K_{2a}\subseteq2K_a\). Thus (4) implies
\[
N(2a)\le5^nN(a)\qquad(a\ge0).
\tag{5}
\]
Tonelli's theorem applies to the following finite nonnegative lattice sums and gives the exact layer-cake identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}
\left(\frac{g(z)}{w_q}\right)^{1/2}
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-a}N(2a)\,da\\
&\le5^n\int_0^\infty e^{-a}N(a)\,da
=5^n\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\frac{g(z)}{w_q}
=5^n\frac{M}{w_q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Here and below \(g(z)\) means the unshifted value in (2), and a zero term contributes zero. Therefore, for every \(a\ge0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\
\log(w_q/g(z))>a}}g(z)
&\le e^{-a/2}w_q
\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^n}\left(\frac{g(z)}{w_q}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le5^ne^{-a/2}M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The displayed condition is understood to include \(g(z)=0\); those terms vanish.

Choose
\[
s:=2n\log5+\log16,\qquad
E:=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:g(z)\ge w_qe^{-s}\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (7) gives
\[
T:=\sum_{z\in E}g(z)\ge\frac{3M}{4}.
\tag{9}
\]
The finite set \(E\) and its weights are symmetric about \(q\). For a closed base halfspace through \(q\), reflection about \(q\) exchanges it with the opposite closed halfspace. The two have equal \(g\)-mass, their union contains all of \(E\), and their intersection is their common boundary. Hence each has \(g\)-mass at least \(T/2\). Any closed base halfspace merely containing \(q\) contains its parallel translate through \(q\). Consequently \(q\) has closed-halfspace depth at least
\[
\beta:=\frac{3M}{8}
\tag{10}
\]
for the finite base measure \(\sum_{z\in E}g(z)\delta_z\). This argument keeps all mass on a base cutting hyperplane.

We now connect (10) to the accepted repeated-cut theorem rather than repeating its cut construction. Use accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893 with its target \(\mathbf J=q\), its original weights \(w_{\mathbf I}=w_I\), and cutoff \(L=s\). Denote its integral hitting mass by \(m_d(q,s)\). We claim
\[
m_d(q,s)\ge\beta.
\tag{11}
\]
Let \(F\) be any subset of the positive lattice support \(D=\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:w_z>0\}\) with
\[
\sum_{z\in F}w_z<\beta.
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(g(z)\le w_z\), the \(g\)-mass of \(F\cap E\) is strictly less than \(\beta\). If \(q\notin\operatorname{conv}(E\setminus F)\), strict separation of a point from the convex hull of a finite set would give a closed base halfspace through \(q\), containing no point of \(E\setminus F\), whose \(g\)-mass is strictly less than \(\beta\). This contradicts (10). Thus
\[
q\in\operatorname{conv}(E\setminus F).
\tag{13}
\]
Choose an inclusion-minimal subset \(A\subseteq E\setminus F\) whose convex hull contains \(q\). It is affinely independent and \(q\in\operatorname{relint}\operatorname{conv}A\): a minimal convex representation has every coefficient positive, and an affine dependence would permit a signed perturbation eliminating one coefficient while preserving the represented point. Write its positive barycentric coefficients as \((\lambda_z)_{z\in A}\). Every \(z\in A\) has \(g(z)>0\), hence belongs to \(D\), and (8) gives
\[
\left(\sum_{z\in A}\lambda_z w_z^{1/d}\right)^d
\ge
\left(\sum_{z\in A}\lambda_z(w_qe^{-s})^{1/d}\right)^d
=w_qe^{-s}.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore the root-volume cost \(L_A\) of accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893 satisfies \(L_A\le s\). The set \(A\) is one of that fact's eligible low-cost supports and is disjoint from \(F\). Thus no \(F\) satisfying (12) is a hitting set for all eligible supports. This proves (11).

Put
\[
\gamma_d:=\left(\frac d{d+1}\right)^d,\qquad
\rho_d:=1-\gamma_d,\qquad
\rho_\star:=1-e^{-1},\qquad
\eta:=-\log\rho_\star.
\tag{15}
\]
The elementary inequality \(\log(1+1/d)\le1/d\) gives \(\gamma_d\ge e^{-1}\), hence \(\rho_d\le\rho_\star\). Choose
\[
k:=\left\lceil\frac{s+\log2}{\eta}\right\rceil
=\left\lceil\frac{2n\log5+\log32}{\eta}\right\rceil.
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
e^s\rho_d^k\le e^s\rho_\star^k
=e^{s-\eta k}\le\frac12.
\tag{17}
\]

For completeness, the numerical estimate needed below is \(k\le15n\), with no decimal approximation. First,
\[
e<\frac83+\frac1{24}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}5^{-j}
=\frac{87}{32}<\frac{11}{4}.
\]
Thus \(e^{-1}>4/11\), and the power series for \(-\log(1-x)\) gives
\[
\eta>
\sum_{j=1}^{4}\frac1j\left(\frac4{11}\right)^j
=\frac{19772}{43923}
>\frac9{20},
\tag{18}
\]
where the last difference is \(133/878460>0\). Also,
\[
e^{13/8}>
\sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac{(13/8)^j}{j!}
=\frac{19839493}{3932160}>5,
\qquad
e^{7/10}>
\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{(7/10)^j}{j!}
=\frac{12013}{6000}>2.
\]
Hence \(\log5<13/8\) and \(\log2<7/10\). Since \(n\ge1\),
\[
s+\log2
=2n\log5+5\log2
<\frac{13n}{4}+\frac72
\le\frac{27n}{4}
<15n\eta.
\tag{19}
\]
The number \(15n\) is an integer, so (16) and (19) imply \(k\le15n\).

The first repeated-cut inequality (4) of accepted fact 7fa5a28fe1ac6893, applied exactly at \((q,s,k)\), now supplies a point \(x_q\in C_q\) such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_S((q,x_q))
&\ge
\frac{1-e^s(1-\gamma_d)^k}{k}\,m_d(q,s)\\
&\ge\frac{m_d(q,s)}{2k}
\ge\frac{3M}{16k}
\ge\frac{M}{80n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
That accepted inequality takes the maximum over the original target fiber before the arbitrary halfspace and was proved for closed ambient halfspaces with the original mixed measure. It includes vertical halfspaces and retains all boundary mass, so (20) proves the first assertion without any general-position or null-boundary assumption.

Finally suppose the lattice volume profile is symmetric about an integer \(q\). Choose \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) with \(w_z>0\), which exists because \(W>0\). Symmetry gives \(w_{2q-z}=w_z>0\), and the global log-concavity supplied by accepted fact b563a1eb51a20cd2 gives
\[
w_q\ge\sqrt{w_zw_{2q-z}}=w_z>0.
\]
Thus the first assertion applies. Moreover \(w_z=w_{2q-z}\) for every lattice \(z\), so
\[
M(q)=\sum_z w_z=W.
\]
Equation (20) gives \(h_{\max}(C)\ge W/(80n)\). The elementary induction \(2^n\ge2n\) for \(n\ge1\) gives the final inequality \(W/(80n)\ge W/(40\,2^n)\).

No zero reflected weight is normalized: it never enters \(E\), its value of \(\phi\) in (3) is \(+\infty\), and its lattice summand vanishes. Under the stated hypotheses \(W>0\). If the locked zero-mass case \(W=0\) is considered separately, then \(M(q)=0\) and every mixed-integer halfspace mass is nonnegative, so its required zero lower bound is automatic without invoking any normalization or positive-fiber point.
