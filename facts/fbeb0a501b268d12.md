## Statement

Let \(n,d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\), let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^d\) be a convex body, and put
\[
 C_z:=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:(z,x)\in C\},
 \qquad
 a_z:=\operatorname{vol}_d(C_z)
 \quad(z\in\mathbb Z^n).
\]
For \((z,x)\in C\), define the absolute fiber depth
\[
 \delta_C(z,x)
 :=
 \inf\left\{
 \operatorname{vol}_d(C_z\cap H):
 H\subseteq\mathbb R^d\text{ is a closed halfspace and }x\in H
 \right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Allowing or excluding the whole space in (1) does not change any assertion below.

For a \(d\)-dimensional convex body \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) and \(0<t\leq\operatorname{vol}_d(K)\), let
\[
 \mathsf M_t(K)
 :=
 \left\{
 \frac1t\int_K x f(x)\,dx:
 f\in L^\infty(K),\ 0\leq f\leq1\text{ a.e.},\ \int_K f\,dx=t
 \right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a finite nonempty \(E\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\) and \(m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E\), write
\[
 \Lambda_m(E)
 :=
 \left\{
 \alpha\in\mathbb R_+^E:
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z=1,
 \quad
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z z=m
 \right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Given positive masses \(t_z\leq a_z\) on \(E\), define
\[
 \mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)
 :=
 \bigcap_{\alpha\in\Lambda_m(E)}
 \sum_{z\in E}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\tag{4}
\]
The complete metronoid-core capacity from accepted fact ae0161dd5d4ea672 is
\[
 \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)
 :=
 \sup\left\{
 \sum_{z\in E}t_z:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \varnothing\ne E\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:a_z>0\},
 \quad 0<t_z\leq a_z,\\
 \text{there is }m\in\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{conv}E
 \text{ with }\mathcal K_m(\mathsf M_t)\ne\varnothing,\\
 \displaystyle
 \sum_{z\in E\cap J}t_z
 \geq2^{-n}\sum_{z\in E}t_z
 \text{ for every proper closed halfspace }J\subseteq\mathbb R^n
 \text{ containing }m
 \end{array}
 \right\},
\tag{5}
\]
where the supremum of the empty family is zero.

The following three conclusions hold.

1. Set-valued simplex-core principle. Let
\[
 \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{v_0,\ldots,v_q\}\subseteq\mathbb R^q
\]
be a simplex, let \(F\subseteq\Delta\) be finite and contain all its vertices, and let \(D_u\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) be a nonempty convex set for every \(u\in F\). Write \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_q\) for the barycentric coordinates on \(\Delta\). If
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(u)D_{v_i}\subseteq D_u
 \qquad(u\in F),
\tag{6}
\]
then, for every \(c\in\operatorname{conv}F\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(c)D_{v_i}
 \subseteq
 \bigcap_{\alpha\in\Lambda_c(F)}
 \sum_{u\in F}\alpha_uD_u,
 }
\tag{7}
\]
where
\[
 \Lambda_c(F)
 :=
 \left\{
 \alpha\in\mathbb R_+^F:
 \sum_{u\in F}\alpha_u=1,
 \quad
 \sum_{u\in F}\alpha_u u=c
 \right\}.
\]

2. One-factor packets are dominated by the complete core. Consider an admissible factorized lattice-polysimplex packet in the sense of accepted fact 5b6523682250e7a5. Thus \(k\geq1\), the factor ranks satisfy \(r_i\geq1\),
\[
 q:=\sum_{i=1}^k r_i\leq n,
\]
each
\[
 \Delta_i=\operatorname{conv}\{v_{i,0},\ldots,v_{i,r_i}\}
 \subseteq\mathbb R^{r_i}
\]
is a full-dimensional lattice simplex, \(D_i:=\Delta_i\cap\mathbb Z^{r_i}\), and
\[
 \Phi(t_1,\ldots,t_k)
 :=b+\sum_{i=1}^kU_it_i,
 \qquad
 b\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \quad
 U_i\in\mathbb Z^{n\times r_i},
\tag{8}
\]
has concatenated matrix
\[
 U=[U_1\ \cdots\ U_k]\in\mathbb Z^{n\times q}
\]
of rank \(q\). For every product vertex \(j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\), suppose the packet gives
\[
 x_j\in C_{\Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k})}
\]
and numbers \(a_{i,j_i}\geq0\) satisfying
\[
 \delta_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j
 \right)^{1/d}
 \geq
 \prod_{i=1}^k a_{i,j_i}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(\lambda_{i,j}\) be the barycentric coordinate of \(v_{i,j}\) on \(\Delta_i\), and put
\[
 g_i(t_i):=\sum_{j=0}^{r_i}\lambda_{i,j}(t_i)a_{i,j},
 \qquad
 A_i:=\sum_{u_i\in D_i}g_i(u_i)^d,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E(\Pi):=\prod_{i=1}^kA_i.
\tag{10}
\]
If the packet has one factor, so \(k=1\), \(r_1=q\), and \(A:=A_1=\mathcal E(\Pi)\), then
\[
 \boxed{A\leq\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C).}
\tag{11}
\]
This includes \(A=0\), zero vertex values, zero values of \(g_1\) at individual lattice points, every \(1\leq q\leq n\), and nonsaturated integral embeddings.

3. Every proper-rank packet is dominated by its primitive slice. For an arbitrary number \(k\geq1\) of factors, define the ambiently normalized packet score
\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi)
 :=2^{n-q}\mathcal E(\Pi).
\tag{12}
\]
This is the exact normalization because
\[
 2^{-q}\mathcal E(\Pi)
 =2^{-n}\operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi).
\tag{13}
\]
Suppose \(1\leq q<n\), and set
\[
 V:=\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb R}U,
 \qquad
 \Gamma:=V\cap\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 L:=b+V.
\tag{14}
\]
Then \(\Gamma\) is a saturated rank-\(q\) lattice,
\[
 L\cap\mathbb Z^n=b+\Gamma,
\]
and \(L\) is a proper primitive affine lattice subspace of codimension \(n-q\). With
\[
 W_L:=\sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\tag{15}
\]
one has the packet-specific domination
\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi)
 =2^{n-q}\mathcal E(\Pi)
 \leq2^{n-q}W_L
 =2^{\operatorname{codim}L}W_L.
 }
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, if
\[
P_n(C)
:=
\sup_{L'\text{ proper primitive affine lattice subspace}}
 2^{\operatorname{codim}L'}W_{L'},
 \qquad
 W_{L'}:=\sum_{z\in L'\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z,
\tag{17}
\]
then every rank-\(q<n\) packet satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_n(\Pi)\leq P_n(C).
\tag{18}
\]

Combining (11) and (16), a one-factor packet is already dominated by the complete core even when \(q=n\), and every packet with \(q<n\) is already dominated after exact ambient normalization by its proper primitive slice. Therefore only packets satisfying
\[
 \boxed{q=n\quad\text{and}\quad k\geq2}
\tag{19}
\]
remain as a potentially independent raw factorized-polysimplex certificate.

## Proof

We first prove the set-valued simplex-core principle. Fix \(c\in\operatorname{conv}F\), a point
\[
 y\in\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(c)D_{v_i},
\]
and an arbitrary representation \(\alpha\in\Lambda_c(F)\). Choose \(y_i\in D_{v_i}\) such that
\[
 y=\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(c)y_i.
\tag{20}
\]
For every \(u\in F\), define
\[
 y_u:=\sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(u)y_i.
\tag{21}
\]
By (6), \(y_u\in D_u\). Each barycentric coordinate is affine, and \(c=\sum_u\alpha_u u\), so
\[
 \lambda_i(c)=\sum_{u\in F}\alpha_u\lambda_i(u)
 \qquad(0\leq i\leq q).
\tag{22}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{u\in F}\alpha_u y_u
 =
 \sum_{i=0}^q
 \left(\sum_{u\in F}\alpha_u\lambda_i(u)\right)y_i
 =
 \sum_{i=0}^q\lambda_i(c)y_i
 =y.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(y\in\sum_u\alpha_uD_u\). Since the representation \(\alpha\) was arbitrary, \(y\) belongs to the intersection in (7). Since \(y\) was arbitrary, (7) follows. No preferred representation was selected.

We next record the interpolation estimate shared by both packet-pruning arguments. For \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\in\prod_i\Delta_i\) and a product vertex \(j=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)\), put
\[
 \Lambda_j(t):=\prod_{i=1}^k\lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i),
 \qquad
 x(t):=\sum_j\Lambda_j(t)x_j.
\tag{24}
\]
All coefficients in (24) are nonnegative and sum to one. The barycentric identities give
\[
 \sum_j\Lambda_j(t)
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k})
 =\Phi(t),
\]
so convexity of \(C\) gives
\[
 (\Phi(t),x(t))\in C.
\tag{25}
\]
Accepted fact 4e868f774de77608 gives joint concavity of \((z,x)\mapsto\delta_C(z,x)^{1/d}\), including at zero-depth and lower-dimensional fibers. Hence (9) and (25) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_C(\Phi(t),x(t))^{1/d}
 &\geq
 \sum_j\Lambda_j(t)
 \delta_C\!\left(
 \Phi(v_{1,j_1},\ldots,v_{k,j_k}),x_j
 \right)^{1/d}\\
 &\geq
 \sum_j\prod_{i=1}^k
 \lambda_{i,j_i}(t_i)a_{i,j_i}\\
 &=\prod_{i=1}^kg_i(t_i).
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Every \(g_i\) is nonnegative on \(\Delta_i\), even if some of its vertex values vanish. Raising (26) to the \(d\)-th power gives
\[
 \delta_C(\Phi(t),x(t))
 \geq\prod_{i=1}^kg_i(t_i)^d.
\tag{27}
\]

We prove the one-factor core domination. Write
\[
 \Delta=\Delta_1,
 \quad
 D=D_1,
 \quad
 g=g_1,
 \quad
 \Phi(u)=b+Uu,
 \quad
 x(u)=\sum_{j=0}^q\lambda_j(u)x_j.
\]
Here \(x(u)\) is affine in the intrinsic coordinate \(u\in\mathbb R^q\). If \(A=0\), then (11) follows immediately from \(\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq0\). This case includes the possibility that every factor value is zero, and no zero-mass metronoid or zero measure is introduced.

Assume \(A>0\), and let
\[
 D_+:=\{u\in D:g(u)>0\},
 \qquad
 t_u:=g(u)^d\quad(u\in D_+).
\tag{28}
\]
Then \(D_+\) is nonempty and \(\sum_{u\in D_+}t_u=A\). Equation (27) gives
\[
 \delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u))\geq t_u>0
 \qquad(u\in D_+).
\tag{29}
\]
For every compact fiber, its absolute depth is at most its total volume: if only proper halfspaces are admitted in (1), choose a proper closed halfspace containing that entire compact fiber. Thus
\[
 0<t_u\leq a_{\Phi(u)}.
\tag{30}
\]
In particular, every fiber in (30) is \(d\)-dimensional. Accepted fact 6769574fc1bd9f8d proves the depth-to-metronoid inclusion, so (29) yields
\[
 x(u)\in\mathsf M_{t_u}(C_{\Phi(u)})
 \qquad(u\in D_+).
\tag{31}
\]

Apply accepted fact 6d6fcf377e68c79c in the intrinsic lattice \(\mathbb Z^q\) to the nonzero finite measure
\[
 \nu:=\sum_{u\in D_+}t_u\delta_u.
\]
It gives \(c\in\mathbb Z^q\) such that every proper closed halfspace \(K\subseteq\mathbb R^q\) containing \(c\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{u\in D_+\cap K}t_u\geq2^{-q}A.
\tag{32}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 c\in\operatorname{conv}D_+.
\tag{33}
\]
Indeed, otherwise strict separation from the finite convex hull would give a proper closed halfspace containing \(c\) and no point of \(D_+\), contradicting (32). In particular \(c\in\Delta\).

Set
\[
 m_*:=\Phi(c)\in\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 E_*:=\Phi(D_+)\subseteq\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad
 t_{\Phi(u)}:=t_u.
\tag{34}
\]
The matrix \(U\) has rank \(q\), so \(\Phi\) is injective. Thus the masses in (34) are well-defined, have total mass \(A\), and (33) gives
\[
 m_*\in\operatorname{conv}E_*.
\tag{35}
\]

Let \(J\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) be any proper closed halfspace containing \(m_*\). Its inverse image \(\Phi^{-1}(J)\) is either the whole of \(\mathbb R^q\) or a proper closed halfspace containing \(c\). In the first case it contains all of \(D_+\); in the second case (32) applies. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_*\cap J}t_z
 =
 \sum_{u\in D_+\cap\Phi^{-1}(J)}t_u
 \geq2^{-q}A
 \geq2^{-n}A.
\tag{36}
\]
This is exactly the ambient balance condition in (5). It was obtained by pulling ambient halfspaces back to intrinsic coordinates; no determinant or lattice index appears.

It remains to prove complete-core nonemptiness, including the intersection over every barycentric representation. Fix arbitrary \(\alpha\in\Lambda_{m_*}(E_*)\). For each \(z\in E_*\), let \(u_z\in D_+\) be its unique preimage under \(\Phi\). Since \(\sum_z\alpha_z=1\), affineness of \(\Phi\) gives
\[
 \Phi\left(\sum_{z\in E_*}\alpha_zu_z\right)
 =
 \sum_{z\in E_*}\alpha_z\Phi(u_z)
 =m_*
 =\Phi(c).
\]
Injectivity of \(\Phi\) therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{z\in E_*}\alpha_zu_z=c.
\tag{37}
\]
Since the one-factor selector \(x(u)\) is affine, (31) and (37) imply
\[
 x(c)
 =\sum_{z\in E_*}\alpha_zx(u_z)
 \in
 \sum_{z\in E_*}\alpha_z\mathsf M_{t_z}(C_z).
\tag{38}
\]
The representation \(\alpha\) was arbitrary, so
\[
 x(c)\in\mathcal K_{m_*}(\mathsf M_t).
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (30), (35), (36), and (39) make the configuration (34) admissible in the complete supremum (5), with total mass \(A\). Hence
\[
 \mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\geq A,
\]
which proves (11). Notice that a nonsaturated lattice image causes no issue: \(c\in\mathbb Z^q\) ensures \(m_*=b+Uc\in b+U\mathbb Z^q\), and all balance tests are ambient halfspaces pulled back through the injective affine map.

We finally prove the primitive-slice domination. Assume \(1\leq q<n\), and use (14). The \(q\) columns of \(U\) are linearly independent integral vectors, so \(U\mathbb Z^q\subseteq\Gamma\) has rank \(q\). Since \(\Gamma\subseteq V\) and \(\dim V=q\), the lattice \(\Gamma\) also has rank exactly \(q\). It is saturated: if \(z\in\mathbb Z^n\) and \(rz\in\Gamma\) for some nonzero integer \(r\), then \(rz\in V\), hence \(z\in V\), and therefore \(z\in\Gamma\). Thus
\[
 L\cap\mathbb Z^n=b+(V\cap\mathbb Z^n)=b+\Gamma,
\]
and \(L\) is a primitive affine lattice subspace. Its dimension is \(q<n\), so it is proper and has codimension \(n-q\).

For each product lattice point \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_k)\in\prod_iD_i\), equation (27) and the elementary upper bound on fiber depth give
\[
 0\leq
 \prod_{i=1}^kg_i(u_i)^d
 \leq
 \delta_C(\Phi(u),x(u))
 \leq
 a_{\Phi(u)}.
\tag{40}
\]
This includes zero factor values and zero-volume or lower-dimensional fibers; in the latter cases (40) forces the product on the left to be zero. Expanding the product of the finite sums in (10) gives the exact identity
\[
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 =
 \sum_{u\in\prod_iD_i}\prod_{i=1}^kg_i(u_i)^d.
\tag{41}
\]
Because \(U\) has full column rank, \(\Phi\) is injective on all of \(\mathbb R^q\), not merely on the product lattice set. Hence the terms in (41) belong to distinct lattice fibers. Also every image point lies in \(b+U\mathbb Z^q\subseteq b+\Gamma=L\cap\mathbb Z^n\). Summing (40) and using nonnegativity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E(\Pi)
 &\leq
 \sum_{u\in\prod_iD_i}a_{\Phi(u)}\\
 &\leq
 \sum_{z\in L\cap\mathbb Z^n}a_z
 =W_L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
The possibly strict containment
\[
 b+U\mathbb Z^q\subsetneq b+\Gamma
\]
only adds nonnegative fiber masses in the second line of (42), so a nonsaturated embedding produces no multiplicative index loss. Multiplying (42) by \(2^{n-q}=2^{\operatorname{codim}L}\) proves (16). Equations (18) and (19) follow immediately from (11), (16), and the definition (17).
