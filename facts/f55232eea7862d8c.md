## Statement

Let \(s,h\ge1\) be integers, let \(a\ge0\), and let \(Q=[-h,h]^s\). For every \(v\in\mathbb R^s\), define

\[
Z_Q(a;v)=\sum_{y\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^s}\exp\bigl(-a\|y-v\|_\infty\bigr).
\]

Writing \(\gamma=2/\sqrt3<2\), one has

\[
Z_Q(a;v)\le (1+\gamma^s)Z_Q(a;0)
\le 2\gamma^s Z_Q(a;0),
\qquad
\frac{2^{s-3}}{2\gamma^s(1+4s)}\ge\frac1{48}.
\]

The last inequality is an equality at \(s=2\). The exponential base \(\gamma\) is optimal up to an absolute prefactor: for every \(s\ge1\), taking \(h=1\), \(a=s\log3\), and \(v=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)\) gives \(Z_Q(a;v)/Z_Q(a;0)>\gamma^s/2\). Thus there is no finite dimension-independent comparison factor, and no bound \(Z_Q(a;v)\le K\beta^sZ_Q(a;0)\) with constants \(K<\infty\) and \(0<\beta<\gamma\) holds uniformly.

## Proof

If \(a=0\), then \(Z_Q(0;v)=(2h+1)^s\) for every \(v\), so the claimed comparison holds. For its remaining proof assume \(a>0\), and put \(r=e^{-a/2}\) and \(q=e^{-a}=r^2\), so \(0<q,r<1\).

For \(t\ge0\), let \(N_v(t)\) count the points of \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) at distance at most \(t\) from \(v\) in the infinity norm. An interval of length \(2t\) contains at most \(\lfloor2t\rfloor+1\) integers: if it contains \(m\ge1\) integers, the difference between the largest and smallest is at least \(m-1\). Each coordinate also has at most \(2h+1\) choices in \(Q\). The coordinate conditions are independent, and hence, for every \(t\ge0\),

\[
N_v(t)\le \min(2h+1,\lfloor2t\rfloor+1)^s.
\]

For every nonnegative distance \(b\), the identity \(e^{-ab}=\int_b^\infty ae^{-at}\,dt\) holds. Summing this identity over the finite set \(Q\cap\mathbb Z^s\) gives \(Z_Q(a;v)=\int_0^\infty ae^{-at}N_v(t)\,dt\).

For integers \(k\ge0\), define

\[
A_k=\min(2k+1,2h+1)^s,
\qquad
B_k=\min(2k+2,2h+1)^s,
\qquad
T=\sum_{k=0}^\infty A_kq^k.
\]

Both sequences are bounded by \((2h+1)^s\), so all series below converge. The exact central count is \(N_0(t)=A_k\) for \(k\le t<k+1\), giving

\[
Z_Q(a;0)=(1-q)\sum_{k=0}^\infty A_kq^k=(1-q)T.
\]

The count envelope equals \(A_k\) on \([k,k+1/2)\) and \(B_k\) on \([k+1/2,k+1)\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_Q(a;v)
&\le \sum_{k=0}^\infty\left(
A_k\int_k^{k+1/2}ae^{-at}\,dt
+B_k\int_{k+1/2}^{k+1}ae^{-at}\,dt\right)\\
&=(1-r)\left(T+r\sum_{k=0}^\infty B_kq^k\right)\\
&=\frac{1-q}{1+r}\left(T+r\sum_{k=0}^\infty B_kq^k\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

The count bound holds also at the interval endpoints. Any assignment of an endpoint to an adjacent integration interval has no effect on these integrals, so no radius boundary has been omitted.

For \(0\le k<h\), the three integer bases in \(A_k,B_k,A_{k+1}\) are \(2k+1,2k+2,2k+3\), respectively, including \(k=h-1\), where \(2k+3=2h+1\). Since

\[
\frac{(2k+2)^2}{(2k+1)(2k+3)}
=1+\frac1{(2k+1)(2k+3)}\le\frac43,
\]

we obtain \(B_k\le\gamma^s\sqrt{A_kA_{k+1}}\). For \(k\ge h\), all three terms equal \((2h+1)^s\), so the same inequality holds. The elementary Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, first for finite partial sums and then for their convergent limits, now yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
r\sum_{k=0}^\infty B_kq^k
&\le \gamma^s\sum_{k=0}^\infty
\sqrt{A_kq^k}\sqrt{A_{k+1}q^{k+1}}\\
&\le \gamma^s
\sqrt{\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty A_kq^k\right)
\left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty A_{k+1}q^{k+1}\right)}\\
&=\gamma^s\sqrt{T(T-1)}\le\gamma^s T.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(A_0=1\). Substituting into the envelope integral and using the central identity gives

\[
Z_Q(a;v)\le\frac{1+\gamma^s}{1+r}Z_Q(a;0)
\le(1+\gamma^s)Z_Q(a;0)
\le2\gamma^s Z_Q(a;0),
\]

because \(\gamma\ge1\). This proves the comparison for every finite cube, including \(h=1\), and every real shift.

For the absorption inequality, direct cancellation gives

\[
\frac{2^{s-3}}{2\gamma^s(1+4s)}
=\frac{3^{s/2}}{16(1+4s)}.
\]

It therefore suffices to prove \(3^{s/2}\ge(1+4s)/3\). At \(s=1\), this follows from \(3>25/9\); at \(s=2\), it is an equality. If it holds at an integer \(s\ge2\), then

\[
3^{(s+1)/2}\ge\frac{\sqrt3(1+4s)}3
\ge\frac{(3/2)(1+4s)}3
\ge\frac{1+4(s+1)}3.
\]

The second inequality uses \(\sqrt3>3/2\); the last is equivalent to \(4s\ge7\). Induction proves the assertion for all \(s\ge1\).

Finally, take \(h=1\), \(v=(1/2,\ldots,1/2)\), and \(a=s\log3\). Among the points of \(\{-1,0,1\}^s\), the origin has central distance \(0\) and all other points have central distance \(1\). Consequently, \(Z_Q(a;0)=1+(3^s-1)3^{-s}=2-3^{-s}<2\). Exactly the \(2^s\) points in \(\{0,1\}^s\) have distance \(1/2\) from \(v\); all remaining points have distance \(3/2\). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_Q(a;v)
&=2^s3^{-s/2}+(3^s-2^s)3^{-3s/2}\\
&\ge\gamma^s,\\
\frac{Z_Q(a;v)}{Z_Q(a;0)}
&>\frac12\gamma^s.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(\gamma>1\), the ratios are unbounded. More generally, if \(0<\beta<\gamma\), then \((\gamma/\beta)^s/2\) is unbounded, contradicting every proposed uniform bound with factor \(K\beta^s\). For an explicit finite failure of coefficient \(1\), already \(s=6\) gives a ratio greater than \(\gamma^6/2=32/27>1\).
