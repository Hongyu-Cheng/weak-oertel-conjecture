\left(\sum_{I\in D\cap(c+2\mathbb Z^N)}p_I\right)^2
\ge2^{-N}.
\tag{24}
$$
At every fixed Boolean spatial point \(j\), the full reflected rows at heights zero, one, and two have respectively \(1,3,5\) sources. Indeed their endpoint heights sum to at most four, whereas positive spatial levels enter only at height at least eight. Both spatial endpoints are consequently Boolean, and their Boolean midpoint \(j\) forces both to equal \(j\). All the indicated vertical pairs are present since \(k\ge8\). The corresponding values of \(H_J=\mathsf r_Jp_J\) are proportional to \(1,3x,5x^2\), where \(x=e^{\ell_N}>0\). They cannot all be equal. Strict Jensen under the positive law \(p\), followed by (24), gives
\(b_{\mathrm f}=\mathbb E_p H_J^2>(\mathbb E_p H_J)^2=\chi(p)^2\ge4^{-N}\), and hence \(B_{\mathrm f}(p)>1\).

Let \(\overline{\mathcal P}\) be the closure of the entire affine family in the finite probability simplex. Thus \(B_{\mathrm f}>1\) at every such point.

We check every proper boundary law \(p^*\in\overline{\mathcal P}\). Let \(S\subsetneq D\) be its nonempty support, take positive affine laws \(p^{(j)}\to p^*\) with parameters \(\ell_j\), choose \(J_0\in S\), and put

On a basis of these differences, the scalar products with \(\ell_j\) are logarithmic ratios converging to finite numbers. Hence the projections of \(\ell_j\) onto \(V\) are bounded. For every \(I\notin S\), \(\ell_j\cdot(I-J_0)=\log(p_I^{(j)}/p_{J_0}^{(j)})\to-\infty\). The projections onto \(V^\perp\) consequently have negative scalar product with every such \(I-J_0\) for all sufficiently large \(j\), and zero scalar product with differences from \(S\). One such vector exposes exactly \(S\); in particular \(V\) is proper.

The subspace \(V^\perp\) is defined by integer equations, so rational vectors are dense in it. Replace the exposing vector by a rational vector preserving all finitely many strict inequalities, multiply to make it integral, and divide by the gcd of its coordinates. This yields a nonzero primitive integral normal \(a\), with \(a\cdot J=c\in\mathbb Z\) for all \(J\in S\). Some coordinate of \(a\) is odd. Hence \(S\) meets at most \(2^{N-1}\) ambient parity classes, since \(a\cdot J\equiv c\pmod2\) on \(S\).

\tag{25}
$$
Thus the polynomial \(B_{\mathrm f}\) is greater than one on this whole compact closure.
