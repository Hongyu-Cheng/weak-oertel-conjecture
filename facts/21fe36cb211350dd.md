## Statement

Let $V$ be convex and $C^1$ on $\mathbb R^N$ with locally Lipschitz gradient, and let $q:\Omega\to\mathbb R^N$ be $C^2$ on an open subset $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$. Put $F=\nabla V\circ q$. Then $F$ is locally Lipschitz, and $(Dq(y))^{\mathsf T}DF(y)$ is symmetric positive semidefinite for Lebesgue-almost every $y\in\Omega$.

## Proof

The composite $F$ is locally Lipschitz. The $C^1$ function $g=V\circ q$ has locally Lipschitz gradient $(Dq)^{\mathsf T}F$. The weak Hessian of $g$ is symmetric by commutation of distributional derivatives. By Rademacher's theorem and the identification of weak and almost-everywhere ordinary derivatives of locally Lipschitz functions, the product rule gives almost everywhere
$$
D^2g=(Dq)^{\mathsf T}DF+\sum_{a=1}^N F_aD^2q_a.
$$
This proves symmetry. Monotonicity of $\nabla V$ gives $(F(y+tv)-F(y))\cdot(q(y+tv)-q(y))\ge0$ whenever $y+tv\in\Omega$. At a point of differentiability of $F$, divide by $t^2$ and let $t\to0$ to get $v^{\mathsf T}(Dq(y))^{\mathsf T}DF(y)v\ge0$ for every $v\in\mathbb R^d$.
