## Statement

Let \(C\subseteq\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^2\) be a compact convex set with nonempty interior, let \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb R^2\), and assume \(S=C\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing\). For \(i\in\mathbb Z\), put

\[
C_i=\{\mathbf x\in\mathbb R^2:(i,\mathbf x)\in C\},\qquad
a_i=\mathcal L^2(C_i),\qquad
W=\mathcal H_2(S)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}a_i.
\]

Use the locked depth \(h_S(\mathbf y)=\inf_{H\ni\mathbf y}\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\), where \(H\) ranges over closed ambient halfspaces in \(\mathbb R^3\). There exists one \(\mathbf y^\star\in S\) such that

\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)\ge\frac29W
\qquad
\text{for every closed ambient halfspace }H\text{ containing }\mathbf y^\star.
\]

Thus \(h_S(\mathbf y^\star)\ge2W/9\). This includes \(W=0\), every number of positive-area integer fibers, and all zero-area fibers in the original set \(S\).

The fraction \(2/9\) is optimal for this entire class: for every \(c>2/9\), there is such a convex body with \(W>0\) for which \(h_S(\mathbf y)<cW\) for every \(\mathbf y\in S\).

## Proof

Compactness gives only finitely many nonempty integer fibers. Every zero-area fiber has zero-area intersection with every halfspace, so it contributes zero to both \(W\) and every halfspace mass. These fibers remain in \(S\). If \(W=0\), any point of the nonempty set \(S\) proves the assertion.

Assume \(W>0\), put \(I=\{i:a_i>0\}\), and let \(N=\#I\). Use the canonical roots \(r_i=\sqrt{a_i}\). Accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d proves that \(I\) is a consecutive integer interval and that these roots are concave on \(I\); concavity of their zero extension is not needed.

If \(1\le N\le3\), the actual-body corollary of accepted fact 2a8c2bc6d7992c5e gives the required point. In the three-fiber application, its total \(T\) is precisely \(W\), since every other fiber has area zero. If \(N=4\), apply the actual-body conclusion of accepted fact d773b98105d1ae6c, with exactly the present \(C,S,a_i,r_i,W\). Henceforth \(N\ge5\). If any \(a_i\ge W/2\), the heavy-fiber conclusion of accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d gives the required point at that fiber's centroid. In particular, equality \(a_i=W/2\) is covered.

It remains to treat \(N\ge5\) and \(a_i<W/2\) for every \(i\). Choose \(j\) to be the first element of \(I\) for which \(\sum_{i\le j}a_i\ge W/2\), and put \(A=a_j>0\). This choice exists because the final cumulative sum is \(W\). Its minimality gives

\[
\sum_{i<j}a_i<\frac W2,\qquad
\sum_{i>j}a_i=W-\sum_{i\le j}a_i\le\frac W2.
\]

Thus \(j\) is an area-weighted median. If either tail were zero, the other would equal \(W-A>W/2\), contradicting these bounds. Both tails are therefore positive, and \(j\) is strictly inside \(I\). In particular, the adjacent fibers on both sides have positive area.

Choose \(\tau\in\{-1,1\}\) so that the lighter tail is on the left after the integer isometry \(\mathcal T(t,\mathbf x)=(\tau(t-j),\mathbf x)\); either sign is allowed when the tails are equal. Define the normalized root profile \(\widehat{\mathbf r}\) and its tail sums by

\[
\widehat r_k=\frac{r_{j+\tau k}}{\sqrt A},\qquad
U=\sum_{k<0}\widehat r_k^2,\qquad
V=\sum_{k>0}\widehat r_k^2,\qquad
T_{\widehat{\mathbf r}}=\frac WA=1+U+V.
\]

This is exactly the root normalization of accepted fact 4d628efe7673ce2d, with the median translated to zero and \(\widehat r_0=1\). The two original tail areas become \(AU,AV\). The median inequalities give \(AU,AV\le W/2\), and the orientation gives \(U\le V\). Consequently

\[
0<U\le V\le U+1,\qquad
1+U+V>2,\qquad
V>1-U.
\]

The strict inequalities follow from the positive tails and \(A<W/2\). Every normalized entry also satisfies \(\widehat r_k^2<T_{\widehat{\mathbf r}}/2\), by the original no-heavy assumption. The isometry \(\mathcal T\) preserves \(\Lambda\), closed ambient halfspaces, and mixed area. In particular, the body \(\mathcal T(C)\) still has total mixed area \(W\); only its scalar root profile has been divided by \(\sqrt A\).

Apply the following accepted actual-body conclusions to \(\mathcal T(C)\).

- If \(U\ge6/5\), its lighter median tail has area \(AU\ge6A/5\). This is exactly the median-tail hypothesis of accepted fact 239b75c53977cde9, which gives a point in the median fiber with depth at least \(2W/9\).
- If \(1/4\le U<6/5\), accepted fact 195c9c11f5096e9f applies with its median area \(A\) and normalized tails \(U,V\) exactly as above. Its actual-body hypothesis allows the full closed interval \([1/4,6/5]\), and \(A<W/2\) has already been checked. It gives a point in the median fiber with depth at least \(2W/9\).
- If \(0<U<1/4\), all hypotheses of accepted fact fd5758bb129f2e08 hold: there are at least five positive-area fibers, no fiber is heavy, and its median at zero and normalized profile \(\widehat{\mathbf r}\) are precisely the ones just defined. Its actual-body conclusion gives a point in the median fiber or its adjacent fiber toward the heavier tail with depth at least \(2W/9\).

These ranges exhaust \(U>0\). The boundary \(U=1/4\) is in the second case and \(U=6/5\) is in the first; equality between the tails or in a median inequality is allowed. Each conclusion supplies one point \(\mathbf p\in\mathcal T(S)\) before quantifying over halfspaces. Set \(\mathbf y^\star=\mathcal T^{-1}(\mathbf p)\). For every closed halfspace \(H\ni\mathbf y^\star\), its image contains \(\mathbf p\) and

\[
\mathcal H_2(S\cap H)
=\mathcal H_2\bigl(\mathcal T(S)\cap\mathcal T(H)\bigr)
\ge\frac29W.
\]

All invoked conclusions concern closed ambient halfspaces, including those whose boundary contains an entire fiber. They already concern the actual body and its actual total \(W\); no depth comparison with a body realizing an auxiliary root profile is used. This proves the asserted quantifier order for the full original \(S\).

For optimality, use exactly the family established in accepted fact 2a8c2bc6d7992c5e. Let \(\Delta\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be a nondegenerate triangle with centroid \(\mathbf0\), let \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and set

\[
C_\varepsilon=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(
(\{0\}\times\varepsilon\Delta)\cup(\{1,2\}\times\Delta)\bigr),
\qquad
S_\varepsilon=C_\varepsilon\cap\Lambda.
\]

That accepted fact proves that \(C_\varepsilon\) is a convex body with exactly these three integer fibers, each of positive area, and proves the upper bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_\varepsilon&=\mathcal H_2(S_\varepsilon)
=(2+\varepsilon^2)\mathcal L^2(\Delta)>0,\\
\frac{\sup_{\mathbf y\in S_\varepsilon}h_{S_\varepsilon}(\mathbf y)}
{W_\varepsilon}
&\le
\frac{4/9+\varepsilon^2}{2+\varepsilon^2}
\longrightarrow\frac29
\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here the predecessor's total \(T\) is \(W_\varepsilon\), and its halfspace mass is exactly \(\mathcal H_2(S_\varepsilon\cap H)\). For any \(c>2/9\), choose a sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\) so that the displayed upper bound is less than \(c\). Then every point has depth less than \(cW_\varepsilon\). Together with the universal lower bound already proved, this establishes optimality of \(2/9\).
