## Statement

Let \(m\ge0\) be an integer, and let \(G:\{-m,\ldots,m\}\to[0,\infty)\) be a finite real-valued even function with \(G(0)=0\). Assume that the successive increments \(G(k)-G(k-1)\), for \(1\le k\le m\), are nondecreasing. Define \(Z_G:=\sum_{v=-m}^m e^{-G(v)}\), let \(V\) have distribution \(Q(v):=e^{-G(v)}/Z_G\), and put \(r:=\Pr_Q(V\ne0)=1-Q(0)\). All logarithms below are natural.

For \(r>0\), the following explicit bounds hold:

\[
\mathbb E_Q[G(V)^2]
\le r\left[\log\!\left(\frac{2-r}{r}\right)+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{e}\right]^2
\le 16r\bigl(1+\log(1/r)\bigr)^2.
\]

For \(r=0\), \(\mathbb E_Q[G(V)^2]=0\), and the final right-hand side is defined to be zero. In all cases, \(\mathbb E_QG(V)\le\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q[G(V)^2]}\).

## Proof

If \(r=0\), then \(V=0\) almost surely, so both energy moments vanish. This includes \(m=0\). Hence assume \(r>0\). Then \(m\ge1\), and finiteness of \(Z_G\) gives \(0<r<1\).

Write \(\delta_k:=G(k)-G(k-1)\) for \(1\le k\le m\). Since \(\delta_1=G(1)\ge0\) and these increments are nondecreasing, all \(\delta_k\) are nonnegative. In particular, \(G\) is nondecreasing on the nonnegative integers, and \(G(k)=\sum_{i=1}^k\delta_i\ge kG(1)\).

Evenness and \(G(0)=0\) give \(Z_G=1+2\sum_{k=1}^m e^{-G(k)}\). Since \(r=(Z_G-1)/Z_G\), it follows that \(\sum_{k=1}^m e^{-G(k)}=r/[2(1-r)]\). If \(G(1)>0\), the preceding lower bound on \(G(k)\) yields

\[
\frac{r}{2(1-r)}
=\sum_{k=1}^m e^{-G(k)}
\le\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-kG(1)}
=\frac{1}{e^{G(1)}-1}.
\]

Rearranging gives \(G(1)\le\log((2-r)/r)\). The same inequality holds when \(G(1)=0\), because \((2-r)/r>1\). Thus no division by zero or positive initial slope assumption is needed.

To control the energy above \(G(1)\), define \(F(j):=G(j+1)-G(1)\), for \(0\le j\le m-1\), and \(Z_F:=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{-F(j)}\). The function \(F\) is nonnegative and nondecreasing, satisfies \(F(0)=0\), and has nondecreasing successive increments.

We first prove a two-to-one comparison for this finite sequence. For \(k\ge1\) with \(2k\le m-1\), monotonicity of the increments gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(2k)-F(k)
&=\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl(F(k+i)-F(k+i-1)\bigr)\\
&\ge\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl(F(i)-F(i-1)\bigr)
=F(k).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence \(F(2k)\ge2F(k)\); the inequality is also true for \(k=0\). Nondecreasingness gives \(F(2k+1)\ge2F(k)\) whenever \(2k+1\) lies in the domain. Consequently, \(F(\lfloor j/2\rfloor)\le F(j)/2\) for every \(0\le j\le m-1\). Each integer has at most two preimages under \(j\mapsto\lfloor j/2\rfloor\), so

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{-F(j)/2}
\le\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{-F(\lfloor j/2\rfloor)}
\le2\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{-F(j)}
=2Z_F.
\]

For \(t\ge0\), the function \(t^2e^{-t/2}\) has derivative \(te^{-t/2}(2-t/2)\), so its maximum is \(16/e^2\), attained at \(t=4\). Therefore

\[
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}F(j)^2e^{-F(j)}}{Z_F}
\le\frac{16}{e^2}\,
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e^{-F(j)/2}}{Z_F}
\le\frac{32}{e^2}.
\]

Conditional on \(V\ne0\), evenness shows that \(|V|-1\) takes the value \(j\) with probability \(e^{-F(j)}/Z_F\). The last inequality is therefore exactly \(\mathbb E_Q[(G(V)-G(1))^2\mid V\ne0]\le32/e^2\).

For any finite-law real random variable, nonnegativity of its variance gives \((\mathbb EX)^2\le\mathbb E[X^2]\). Apply this under the conditional law to \(X=G(V)-G(1)\). Expanding \(G(V)^2=(G(1)+X)^2\) and using \(G(1)\ge0\) gives

\[
\mathbb E_Q[G(V)^2\mid V\ne0]
\le\left(G(1)+\sqrt{\mathbb E_Q[X^2\mid V\ne0]}\right)^2
\le\left(G(1)+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{e}\right)^2.
\]

The energy is zero at \(V=0\). Multiplication by \(r\), followed by the bound on \(G(1)\), proves the first asserted inequality.

Finally, \(\log((2-r)/r)\le\log(1/r)+\log2\). Since \(e>2\), we have \(\log2<1\) and \(4\sqrt{2}/e<3\). Also \(\log(1/r)\ge0\). It follows that

\[
\log\!\left(\frac{2-r}{r}\right)+\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{e}
\le\log(1/r)+4
\le4\bigl(1+\log(1/r)\bigr).
\]

This proves the second inequality with the absolute constant \(16\). Applying the same variance inequality to \(G(V)\) under \(Q\), and using \(G(V)\ge0\), proves the stated first-moment consequence. Every comparison used finite sums except the explicitly convergent geometric series for \(G(1)>0\); in particular, no bound depends on the support length or on a positive lower bound for the increments.
