## Statement

Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a positive-area triangle and let \(L\subseteq T\) be any planar convex body, meaning a compact convex set with nonempty interior. No rationality assumption is required. Here \(|T|\) is the ordinary planar area of the larger triangle. Set \(n=d=2\), \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^2\times\mathbb R^2\), and

\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\bigl([0,1]^2\times T,[-1,2]^2\times L\bigr),
\qquad S=C\cap\Lambda,
\qquad \mu(E)=\mathcal H_2(S\cap E).
\]

For real bases \(\mathbf t\), let \(A_{\mathbf t}=\{\mathbf x:(\mathbf t,\mathbf x)\in C\}\); for integer bases let \(a_{\mathbf z}=|A_{\mathbf z}|\), and let \(W=\sum_{\mathbf z}a_{\mathbf z}\). Use the prescribed affine functions

\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_0(\mathbf x)&=1-x_1-x_2,&
\beta_1(\mathbf x)&=x_1,&
\beta_2(\mathbf x)&=x_2,\\
d_1(\mathbf t)&=t_1+t_2,&
d_2(\mathbf t)&=1-t_1+t_2,\\
d_3(\mathbf t)&=1+t_1-t_2,&
d_4(\mathbf t)&=2-t_1-t_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\in\mathbb R\) and \(b_0,b_1,b_2>0\), with \(\sum_s\alpha_s<1\). The twelve ORIGINAL open caps are
\(H_{j,s}=\{(\mathbf t,\mathbf x):\beta_s(\mathbf x)>\alpha_s+b_s d_j(\mathbf t)\}\), for \(1\le j\le4\) and \(0\le s\le2\). For \(K=T,L\), put \(F_{K,s}(h)=|K\cap\{\beta_s>h\}|\). Then \(\mu(H_{j,s})=M_s\), independently of \(j\), where

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_s={}&F_{T,s}(\alpha_s)+2F_{T,s}(\alpha_s+b_s)
+F_{T,s}(\alpha_s+2b_s)\\
&+F_{L,s}(\alpha_s-2b_s)
+2\sum_{q=-1}^{3}F_{L,s}(\alpha_s+q b_s)
+F_{L,s}(\alpha_s+4b_s).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Every actual integer fiber is included in this formula. One has \(W=4|T|+12|L|\) and

\[
\max_{0\le s\le2}M_s\ge\frac49|T|+\frac43|L|=\frac W9.
\tag{2}
\]

More specifically, for each \(s\), replacing \(\alpha_s\) by \(\beta_s(\mathbf g_T)\) in (1), where \(\mathbf g_T\) is the centroid of \(T\), gives a mass at least \(W/9\) for every \(b_s>0\). At least one original \(\alpha_s\) is smaller than this centroid threshold, which gives (2). Consequently, if all twelve original masses are less than \(\eta\), then \(W<9\eta\). In particular, this conclusion applies whenever the original real residual is nonempty and full-dimensional and the original-normal expansion ends at the prescribed diamond \(P=\{\mathbf t:d_j(\mathbf t)\ge0\text{ for every }j\}\).

## Proof

First determine all actual fibers. The body \(C\) is compact and convex and contains \([0,1]^2\times T\), a set with nonempty interior in \(\mathbb R^4\). Since \(L\subseteq T\), it is contained in \([-1,2]^2\times T\). Write \(\mathcal V=\{(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}\). At every base in \(\mathcal V\), the actual section is exactly \(T\). Any other integer base in \([-1,2]^2\) has a coordinate equal to \(-1\) or \(2\). A convex combination attaining that extreme coordinate cannot put positive weight on the inner product \([0,1]^2\times T\). Its continuous coordinate therefore lies in \(L\); the reverse inclusion follows from \([-1,2]^2\times L\subset C\). Hence

\[
A_{\mathbf z}=
\begin{cases}
T,&\mathbf z\in\mathcal V,\\
L,&\mathbf z\in\{-1,0,1,2\}^2\setminus\mathcal V,\\
\varnothing,&\mathbf z\notin\{-1,0,1,2\}^2.
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

All sixteen listed fibers have positive area. In particular, the eight outer edge fibers are the full convex body \(L\), even though the outer product is already generated by the four corner copies of \(L\). This proves the formula for \(W\). For each fixed \(j\), the exact counts of the values \(q=d_j(\mathbf z)\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
q&-2&-1&0&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
A_{\mathbf z}=T&0&0&1&2&1&0&0\\
A_{\mathbf z}=L&1&2&2&2&2&2&1.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

For \(j=1\), this is direct addition of the two coordinates on the sixteen-site square. Each other \(d_j\) is obtained by replacing one or both coordinates \(t_i\) by \(1-t_i\), transformations preserving that square and \(\mathcal V\). Thus (4) holds for all four groups and proves (1) for all twelve original caps. Each \(\beta_s\) is a nonconstant affine function on the plane, so its level lines have planar area zero on both \(T\) and \(L\). Thus the areas of open and closed cap traces agree; the open caps themselves are retained throughout.

The original cap family also covers all of \(S\). If \(d_j(\mathbf z)\le0\), a point outside the three caps of group \(j\) would satisfy
\(1=\sum_s\beta_s(\mathbf x)\le\sum_s\alpha_s+d_j(\mathbf z)\sum_s b_s<1\), a contradiction. Every integer base has some \(d_j\le0\): otherwise \(u=z_1+z_2\) and \(v=z_1-z_2\) would satisfy \(u=1\), \(v=0\), contradicting their equal parity. This checks coverage of all fiber boundaries as well as their interiors. The three complementary rows in each group have the prescribed minimal positive dependence of their continuous normals, and their sum gives \(d_j\ge(1-\sum_s\alpha_s)/(\sum_s b_s)>0\). The quantitative argument below needs no further condition on the residual or the subsequent expansion, so those requirements restrict the proved class further.

We next prove the one-dimensional triangle estimate needed for the mass bound. Normalize the values of a nonconstant affine function at the three vertices of a triangle to \(0,\xi,1\), with \(0\le\xi\le1\). For its uniform area distribution, let \(p_\xi(v)\) be the fraction with value at most \(v\), and put \(f_\xi(v)=1-p_\xi(v)\). When \(0<\xi<1\), direct similar-triangle areas give

\[
p_\xi(v)=
\begin{cases}
0,&v\le0,\\
v^2/\xi,&0\le v\le\xi,\\
1-(1-v)^2/(1-\xi),&\xi\le v\le1,\\
1,&v\ge1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed, below the middle vertex the two edge fractions are \(v/\xi\) and \(v\); above it the complementary cap has edge fractions \((1-v)/(1-\xi)\) and \(1-v\). At \(\xi=0\), the nontrivial formula is \(p_0(v)=1-(1-v)^2\) for \(0\le v\le1\); at \(\xi=1\), it is \(p_1(v)=v^2\). These also follow by the same edge calculation. All values at the stated endpoints agree.

The normalized centroid value is \(m=(1+\xi)/3\). We claim that for every \(\gamma\ge0\),

\[
f_\xi(m)+2f_\xi(m+\gamma)+f_\xi(m+2\gamma)
-p_\xi(m-2\gamma)-\frac13p_\xi(m-\gamma)
\ge\frac49.
\tag{6}
\]

Suppose first that \(0\le\xi\le1/2\). Then \(m\ge\xi\), and (5) gives
\(f_\xi(m)=(2-\xi)^2/[9(1-\xi)]=4/9+\xi^2/[9(1-\xi)]\ge4/9\). We prove the reflected-tail bound

\[
p_\xi(m-h)\le2f_\xi(m+h)
\qquad(h\ge0).
\tag{7}
\]

For \(h\ge m\), its left side is zero. Otherwise put \(v=m-h\in(0,m]\) and \(e=1-2m=(1-2\xi)/3\in[0,1/3]\). Since \(m+h=2m-v\ge m\ge\xi\), the upper-cap formula gives \((1-\xi)f_\xi(m+h)=(v+e)^2\). If \(0<v\le\xi\), necessarily \(\xi>0\), and

\[
\frac{p_\xi(v)}{f_\xi(2m-v)}
=\frac{1-\xi}{\xi}\left(\frac{v}{v+e}\right)^2
\le\frac{\xi(1-\xi)}{(\xi+e)^2}
=\frac{9\xi(1-\xi)}{(1+\xi)^2}
\le\frac98<2.
\]

The first inequality uses \(e\ge0\); the last uses \((1+\xi)^2-8\xi(1-\xi)=(1-3\xi)^2\ge0\). If \(v\ge\xi\), formula (5), including its \(\xi=0\) version, instead gives the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
2(v+e)^2-(1-\xi)p_\xi(v)
&=3v^2+(4e-2)v+2e^2+\frac{1-3e}{2}\\
&=3\left(v+\frac{2e-1}{3}\right)^2
+\frac{4e^2-e+1}{6}>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(4e^2-e+1\ge1-e\ge2/3\). This proves (7), including the shared branch boundary; its zero endpoint was already handled by \(h\ge m\). Apply (7) with \(h=\gamma\) and \(h=2\gamma\). Since \(f_\xi(m+2\gamma)\le f_\xi(m+\gamma)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&2f_\xi(m+\gamma)+f_\xi(m+2\gamma)
-p_\xi(m-2\gamma)-\tfrac13p_\xi(m-\gamma)\\
&\qquad\ge\tfrac43f_\xi(m+\gamma)-f_\xi(m+2\gamma)
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Together with \(f_\xi(m)\ge4/9\), this proves (6) in the first range.

Now suppose \(1/2\le\xi\le1\). In this range \(m\le\xi\), and
\(p_\xi(m)=m^2/\xi\le1/2\), because
\(2(1+\xi)^2-9\xi=(2\xi-1)(\xi-2)\le0\). Thus \(f_\xi(m)\ge1/2\). Put \(c=1-m\); then \(c\ge m/2\), since \(m\le2/3\).

If \(\gamma\ge m/2\), then \(p_\xi(m-2\gamma)=0\) and
\(p_\xi(m-\gamma)\le p_\xi(m/2)=m^2/(4\xi)\le1/8\). The left side of (6) is consequently at least \(1/2-1/24=4/9+1/72\).

It remains to consider \(0\le\gamma\le m/2\). Let \(q=\gamma/m\in[0,1/2]\). A homothety toward a vertex where the normalized affine function equals one, with ratio \((c-\gamma)/c\), sends the portion above \(m\) into the portion above \(m+\gamma\) and stays inside the triangle. Therefore

\[
f_\xi(m+\gamma)
\ge f_\xi(m)\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{c}\right)^2
\ge\frac12(1-2q)^2.
\tag{8}
\]

When the homothety ratio is zero, the area inequality is automatic. Otherwise the strict upper-cap inclusion follows by applying the affine function to the homothety. The last inequality uses \(c\ge m/2\), \(\gamma\le m/2\), and \(f_\xi(m)\ge1/2\). Both lower thresholds lie between zero and \(m\le\xi\), so

\[
p_\xi(m-2\gamma)+\frac13p_\xi(m-\gamma)
=\frac{m^2}{\xi}\left((1-2q)^2+\frac{(1-q)^2}{3}\right)
\le\frac12(1-2q)^2+\frac16(1-q)^2.
\]

Use these inequalities and discard the nonnegative term \(f_\xi(m+2\gamma)\). The left side of (6), minus \(4/9\), is at least

\[
\frac1{18}+\frac12(1-2q)^2-\frac16(1-q)^2
=\frac{11}{6}\left(q-\frac5{11}\right)^2+\frac1{99}>0.
\tag{9}
\]

This proves (6) for the second range, including \(\xi=1\). Thus every triangle shape and every nonnegative normalized slope have been covered, with the repeated-vertex projection endpoints checked explicitly.

Return to the actual body and fix \(s\). Put \(h=\beta_s(\mathbf g_T)\) and \(b=b_s\). The affine function \(\beta_s\) is nonconstant on \(T\), so its vertex values have positive range. Dividing its values minus their minimum by that range gives exactly (5); the normalized value of \(h\) is \(m\), and the normalized shift is \(\gamma=b/(\max_T\beta_s-\min_T\beta_s)>0\). Multiplying (6) by \(|T|\) consequently yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
&F_{T,s}(h)+2F_{T,s}(h+b)+F_{T,s}(h+2b)\\
&\qquad-\bigl(|T|-F_{T,s}(h-2b)\bigr)
-\frac13\bigl(|T|-F_{T,s}(h-b)\bigr)
\ge\frac49|T|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(N\) denote the complete contribution of the twelve \(L\) fibers to (1) after this centroid-threshold replacement. By (4), it includes the nonnegative contributions \(F_{L,s}(h-2b)+2F_{L,s}(h-b)\). For every real number \(u\), one has the pointwise inequality

\[
\frac43-\mathbf 1_{\{u>h-2b\}}-2\mathbf 1_{\{u>h-b\}}
\le\mathbf 1_{\{u\le h-2b\}}+\frac13\mathbf 1_{\{u\le h-b\}}.
\]

Indeed, the two sides agree when \(u\le h-b\), and the left side is negative while the right side is zero when \(u>h-b\). Integrate with \(u=\beta_s(\mathbf x)\) over \(L\). The right-side integrands are nonnegative and \(L\subseteq T\), so

\[
N\ge\frac43|L|
-\bigl(|T|-F_{T,s}(h-2b)\bigr)
-\frac13\bigl(|T|-F_{T,s}(h-b)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]

All other outer-fiber terms have merely been discarded as nonnegative after retaining them in the exact mass formula (1). Adding the four \(T\)-fiber terms to (11) and invoking (10) proves that the full centroid-threshold cap mass is at least \(4|T|/9+4|L|/3\), for this arbitrary \(s\) and arbitrary \(b_s>0\).

Finally, \(\sum_s\beta_s(\mathbf g_T)=1>\sum_s\alpha_s\), so some index \(s\) satisfies \(\alpha_s<\beta_s(\mathbf g_T)\). Every summand of (1) is nonincreasing in its threshold. Its ORIGINAL mass \(M_s\) is therefore at least the corresponding centroid-threshold mass just bounded. This proves (2). If every original \(\mu(H_{j,s})<\eta\), then \(W/9\le\max_s M_s<\eta\), proving \(W<9\eta\). No unaccepted handoff, heterogeneous square-root estimate, or asserted positive cross-group overlap was used.
