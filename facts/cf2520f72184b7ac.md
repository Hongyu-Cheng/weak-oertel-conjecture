## Statement

Retain the complete canonical setting, notation, and physical ledgers of accepted facts `3024a86da358e4ea` and `4f7a1ed043c380e1`. Thus \(\Lambda\ne\varnothing\) is the good-label family generated by one normalized affine family, \(o_+\) is any maximum-tag selector, and
\[
 y_\sigma:=y_{\sigma,o_+(\sigma)},
 \qquad
 c_\sigma^+:=c_{y_\sigma},
 \qquad
 r_\sigma:=r_\sigma^c,
 \qquad
 M_\Lambda=\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
 \Lambda_t:=\{\sigma\in\Lambda:t(\sigma)=t\},
 \qquad
 \Lambda_0:=\{\sigma\in\Lambda:y_\sigma=t(\sigma)\},
\tag{2}
\]
and define the selected loop mass and the target-collision maximum mass by
\[
 M_0:=\frac14\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda_0}\theta_\sigma,
 \qquad
 H_{\mathrm{tar}}
 :=\sum_{t:\Lambda_t\ne\varnothing}
 \max_{\sigma\in\Lambda_t}\theta_\sigma.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\sigma\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_0\), let \(g_\sigma\ge1\) be the lattice length of the selected source-target segment from accepted fact `4f7a1ed043c380e1`, namely
\[
 g_\sigma
 :=\gcd\{|(y_\sigma-t(\sigma))_i|:
 (y_\sigma-t(\sigma))_i\ne0\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
 E_{\mathrm{seg}}
 :=\max_{\sigma\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_0}
 (g_\sigma+1)\theta_\sigma,
\tag{5}
\]
with \(E_{\mathrm{seg}}:=0\) when \(\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_0=\varnothing\). Let \(\mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+\) be the collision-safe full-credit forward-ray capacity of accepted fact `3024a86da358e4ea`, let \(G=\mathfrak C_{\mathrm{core}}(C)\), let \(\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o_+}\) be its rank-at-most-two selected-source slice score, and put \(\gamma_d=(d/(d+1))^d\).

Then the following simultaneous screens hold:
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+
 \ge \max\{M_0,H_{\mathrm{tar}}\},
 \qquad
 G\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4}E_{\mathrm{seg}}.}
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently MTP+ is proved, with absolute constants
\[
 \varepsilon_+=\frac1{32},
 \qquad
 c_+=\frac1{256},
\tag{7}
\]
on every retained canonical hard zero-fresh square instance satisfying the quantitatively sufficient special-case condition
\[
 \boxed{
 \max\{M_0,H_{\mathrm{tar}},E_{\mathrm{seg}}\}
 \ge\frac1{16}M_\Lambda.}
\tag{8}
\]
Explicitly, under (8),
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+
 \le\frac1{32}M_\Lambda
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 G+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o_+}
 \ge\frac1{256}M_\Lambda.}
\tag{9}
\]

Moreover, if both inequalities in (9) fail in the difficult directions, namely
\[
 \mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+
 \le\frac1{32}M_\Lambda,
 \qquad
 G+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o_+}
 <\frac1{256}M_\Lambda,
\tag{10}
\]
then the remaining instance has the exact normal form
\[
 \boxed{
 M_0\le\frac1{32}M_\Lambda,
 \qquad
 H_{\mathrm{tar}}\le\frac1{32}M_\Lambda,
 \qquad
 E_{\mathrm{seg}}<\frac1{16}M_\Lambda.}
\tag{11}
\]
In particular, at least \(31M_\Lambda/32\) of the selected mass comes from nonzero source-target segments, and the effective target collision number obeys
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\theta_\sigma}
 {H_{\mathrm{tar}}}
 =\frac{4M_\Lambda}{H_{\mathrm{tar}}}
 \ge128.}
\tag{12}
\]
No conclusion beyond the special case (8) is asserted.

## Proof

We first pack the labels whose selected physical source is already their physical target. For every \(\sigma\in\Lambda_0\), accepted fact `4f7a1ed043c380e1` supplies the literal selected-source density
\[
 g_\sigma^{\mathrm{sel}}(x)
 =\frac{\theta_\sigma}{4a_{y_\sigma}}
 \mathbf1_{C_{y_\sigma}}(x).
\tag{13}
\]
It has mass \(q_\sigma=\theta_\sigma/4\) and barycenter \(c_\sigma^+\). Since \(y_\sigma=t(\sigma)\), it is a density on the correct target fiber. The accepted one-source ledger bounds the sum of all selected-source densities at a physical source, even before the nonnegative replacement densities are discarded, by \(17/32<1\). Hence the subfamily (13), summed over \(\Lambda_0\), satisfies the common pointwise target capacity at every physical target.

If \(c_\sigma^+\ne r_\sigma\), set \(b_\sigma=q_\sigma\). Then
\[
 q_\sigma r_\sigma
 +b_\sigma(c_\sigma^+-r_\sigma)
 =q_\sigma c_\sigma^+
 =\int_{C_{t(\sigma)}}xg_\sigma^{\mathrm{sel}}(x)\,dx.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(c_\sigma^+=r_\sigma\), use the required convention \(b_\sigma=0\), and the same moment identity holds. Give zero density to every label outside \(\Lambda_0\). This is one feasible point of the full-credit program, of objective value \(M_0\). Therefore
\[
 \mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+\ge M_0.
\tag{15}
\]
This construction uses the selected packets once on the target fibers on which they already live; it does not move a packet between different base fibers.

For the second target screen, choose for every nonempty \(\Lambda_t\) one label
\[
 \sigma_t\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\sigma\in\Lambda_t}
 \theta_\sigma.
\tag{16}
\]
Accepted fact `4f7a1ed043c380e1` retains the individual target realization
\[
 r_{\sigma_t}\in
 \mathsf M_{\theta_{\sigma_t}}(C_t).
\tag{17}
\]
Choose one representing density in (17), set
\(q_{\sigma_t}=\theta_{\sigma_t}\) and \(b_{\sigma_t}=0\), and give zero density to every other label. There is exactly one nonzero label at each physical target, so the common target capacity is automatic. This feasible point has objective \(H_{\mathrm{tar}}\), proving
\[
 \mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+\ge H_{\mathrm{tar}}.
\tag{18}
\]
Equations (15) and (18) prove the first part of (6). Notice that they are lower bounds from two separate feasible target families; the proof takes their maximum and does not add the two families.

For every \(\sigma\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_0\), the individual one-factor lattice-segment theorem in accepted fact `4f7a1ed043c380e1` gives
\[
 G\ge
 \frac{\gamma_d}{4}(g_\sigma+1)\theta_\sigma.
\tag{19}
\]
Taking the maximum over these individually valid lower bounds proves the second part of (6). No two complete-core witnesses are summed.

We record the dimension-free elementary estimate
\[
 \gamma_d=\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{-d}>\frac14.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed,
\[
 \left(1+\frac1d\right)^d
 =\sum_{k=0}^d\binom dk d^{-k}
 \le\sum_{k=0}^d\frac1{k!}
 <4,
\tag{21}
\]
where \(\binom dk d^{-k}\le1/k!\), and the last inequality follows, for example, from \(k!\ge2^{k-1}\) for \(k\ge2\).

Assume (8) and the antecedent in (9). Equations (15) and (18) rule out
\(M_0\ge M_\Lambda/16\) and
\(H_{\mathrm{tar}}\ge M_\Lambda/16\), because either inequality would give
\(\mathsf R_{\Lambda,o_+}^+\ge M_\Lambda/16>M_\Lambda/32\). Hence (8) forces
\(E_{\mathrm{seg}}\ge M_\Lambda/16\). Equations (6) and (20) then give
\[
 G\ge\frac{\gamma_d}{4}E_{\mathrm{seg}}
 >\frac1{16}E_{\mathrm{seg}}
 \ge\frac1{256}M_\Lambda.
\tag{22}
\]
Since \(\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o_+}\ge0\), equation (22) proves (9).

Finally assume (10). Equations (15) and (18) immediately give the first two inequalities in (11). Hence
\[
 M_\Lambda-M_0\ge\frac{31}{32}M_\Lambda.
\tag{23}
\]
Also \(H_{\mathrm{tar}}>0\), because \(\Lambda\ne\varnothing\) and every label mass is positive. Using
\(\sum_{\sigma\in\Lambda}\theta_\sigma=4M_\Lambda\), the second inequality in (11) proves (12).

If \(E_{\mathrm{seg}}>0\), equations (6), (10), and (20) give
\[
 \frac1{16}E_{\mathrm{seg}}
 <G
 \le G+\mathsf S_{\Lambda,o_+}
 <\frac1{256}M_\Lambda,
\tag{24}
\]
so \(E_{\mathrm{seg}}<M_\Lambda/16\). If
\(E_{\mathrm{seg}}=0\), the same inequality is immediate. This proves the third part of (11), and completes the collision-safe special-case MTP+ theorem and its residual normal form.
